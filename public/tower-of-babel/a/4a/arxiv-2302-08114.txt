Energy decay for wave equations with a potential and a localized damping Thanks: Corresponding author: xiaoyanli@hust.edu.cn
Ryo Ikehata

Abstract
We consider the total energy decay together with L 2 L^{2} -bound of the solution itself of the Cauchy problem for wave equations with a localized damping and a short-range potential. We treat it in the one dimensional Euclidean space 𝐑 {\bf R} . We adopt a simple multiplier method to study them. In this case, it is essential that the compactness of support of the initial data is not assumed. Since this problem is treated in the whole space, the Poincaré and Hardy inequalities are not available as is developed in the exterior domain case for n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . For compensating such a lack of useful tools, the potential plays an effective role. As an application, the global existence of small data solution for a semilinear problem is provided.

\displaystyle\leq CI_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+CM^{p}I_{0}^{p}\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+s)^{-\frac{p-1}{4}}ds

\displaystyle~~~~+CV_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}M^{p}I_{0}^{p}\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big((R+s)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}+L^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\big)(1+s)^{-\frac{p-1}{4}}ds

\displaystyle\leq CI_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+CM^{p}I_{0}^{p}(V_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}+1)\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+s)^{-\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{\beta}{2}}ds

(3.13)

for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Let

\gamma:=\frac{p-1}{4}-\frac{\beta}{2}.

By assumption
(\bf B.2)
, we see
\gamma>1
. Using (
3.3
) one has

\displaystyle\|U(t)\|_{E}\leq

\displaystyle\big(CI_{0}+CM^{p}I_{0}^{p}(V_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}+1)\big)(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle CI_{0}\big(1+M^{p}I_{0}^{p-1}(V_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}+1)\big)(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{0}Q_{0}(I_{0},M,R,V_{0})(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(3.14)

for all
t\in[0,T)
, where

Q_{0}(I_{0},M,R,V_{0})=C\big(1+M^{p}I_{0}^{p-1}(V_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}+1)\big).

Next, we derive
L^{2}
-bound for the local solution to problem (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).

In fact, one has from Theorem 1.1 and (
3.6
) that

\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq CI_{0}+C\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|u{(s,\cdot)}\|^{p}_{2p}+\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{V(\cdot)}}|u{(s,\cdot)}|^{p}\|\big)ds.

(3.15)

Substituting (
3
) and (
3
) to (
3.15
) and proceeding similar arguments to the estimates for
\|U(t)\|_{E}
, one has

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq I_{0}Q_{0}(I_{0},M,R,V_{0})

for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Take
M>0
further to satisfy
M>C
, and
I_{0}
small enough such that

\displaystyle CM^{p}I_{0}^{p-1}(V_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}+1)<M-C.

(3.16)

By choosing

\delta:=\left(\frac{M-C}{CM^{p}(V_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2}}+1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

and if
I_{0}<\delta
, then it follows from (
3.16
) that

Q_{0}(I_{0},M,R,V_{0})<M.

Therefore, we see that

\|U(t)\|_{E}<MI_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,T),\\

(3.17)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|<MI_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,T).

(3.18)

This contradicts (
3.8
)-(
3.9
), and so (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) are true in
[0,T)
. This shows that the local solution can be extended globally in time and the estimates (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) for the solution
u(t,x)
hold true for all
t\geq 0
, which completes the proof.

Explicit formulas for matrices associated to ladder, circular ladder, and Möbius ladder graphs
Ali Azimi, M. Farrokhi D. G

Abstract
. We give explicit formulas for resistance distance matrices and Moore-Penrose inverses of incidence and Laplacian matrices of ladder, circular ladder, and Möbius ladder graphs. As a result, we compute the Kirchhoff index of these graphs and give new combinatorial formulas for the number of their spanning trees.

1.
introduction

The incidence matrix
Q(\Gamma)
of an oriented graph
\Gamma
with vertex-set
V(\Gamma)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and edge-set
E(\Gamma)=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
is an
n\times m
matrix defined as follows: The rows and columns of
Q
are indexed by vertices and edges of
\Gamma
, respectively. The
(i,j)
entry of
Q
is
0
if the vertex
v_{i}
and the edge
e_{j}
are not incident, otherwise it is
1
or
-1
depending on whether
e_{j}
starts from or ends at
v_{i}
. The Laplacian matrix
L(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
, that is actually equal to
Q(\Gamma)Q^{\prime}(\Gamma)
, is an
n\times n
matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by vertices of
\Gamma
. The
(i,j)
entry of
L(\Gamma)
is equal to the degree of the vertex
v_{i}
if
i=j
, and it is
-1
or
0
if the vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are adjacent or non-adjacent, respectively.

The Moore-Penrose inverse of an
m\times n
real matrix
A
, denoted by
A^{+}
, is the (unique)
n\times m
real matrix that satisfies the following equations

AA^{+}A=A,\hskip 11.38092ptA^{+}AA^{+}=A^{+},\hskip 11.38092pt(AA^{+})^{\prime}=AA^{+},\hskip 11.38092pt(A^{+}A)^{\prime}=A^{+}A

(see
[
9
,
10
]
). The Moore-Penrose inverse of the incidence matrix of a graph was first studied by Ijira in
[
17
]
. We refer the interested reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
for previous studies on the Moore-Penrose inverses of matrices associated to various classes of graphs.

Incidence matrices and their inverses, such as the Moore-Penrose inverses, are important tools used in analysis of graphs. Let
\Gamma
be a simple connected graph. The resistance distance between two vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
can be computed via the formula

r(v_{i},v_{j})=L^{+}_{v_{i},v_{i}}+L^{+}_{v_{j},v_{j}}-L^{+}_{v_{i},v_{j}}-L^{+}_{v_{j},v_{i}},

where
L^{+}_{v_{i},v_{j}}
denotes the
(i,j)
-entry of the Moore-penrose inverse
L^{+}
of
L=L(\Gamma)
. The notion of resistance distance between vertices of a graph is introduced by Klein and Randic in 1993
[
19
]
as the effective resistance between the given vertices (computed by Ohm’s law) when a battery is attached between them assuming that all edges are unit resistors. Beside many applications in physics, chemistry, and network sciences (see
[
13
,
18
,
22
]
), resistance distances can also be studied from a purely mathematical point of view as they represent new metrics on graph as well as making connections to graph theoretical objects like the Moore-Penrose inverse of Laplacian matrices above, see
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. A strictly related parameter is the so-called Kirchhoff index of the graph, namely

Kf(\Gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r(v_{i},v_{j}).

In this paper, we consider three familiar classes of graphs, namely ladder, circular ladder, and Möbius graphs, and study their resistance distances and the Moore-Penrose inverse of their Laplacian matrices. Recall that the ladder graph
L_{n}
on
2n
vertices is composed of two paths of length
n-1
with spokes/rungs connecting corresponding vertices. The circular ladder graph
CL_{n}
defines analogous to ladder graphs with paths replaced by cycles. Also, the Möbius ladder graph
M_{n}
obtains from the circular ladder graph by switching two non-spoke edges of a square.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

be the resistance distance matrix of the ladder graph

\alpha(i,j)=(s(L_{n}/f_{i})+s(L_{n}/f_{j}))/4s_{n}

Let

be the shortest path between
u_{i}^{+}
and
u_{j}^{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Then

\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}q^{+}_{e_{k}^{+},u_{i}^{+}}=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}c_{k,i}=c_{i,i}+\sum_{k=i+1}^{j-1}c_{k,i},

Thus

\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}q^{+}_{e_{k}^{+},u_{i}^{+}}=\left(1-\frac{i}{2n}-\frac{a_{n-i+1}s_{i}}{2s_{n}}\right)+\sum_{k=i+1}^{j-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k}{2n}+\frac{a_{i}s_{n-k}}{2s_{n}}\right).

By equations (
2
) and (
3
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i}^{j}q^{+}_{e_{k}^{+},u_{i}^{+}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{id}{2n}-\frac{d(d-1)}{4n}+\frac{a_{i}(a_{n-i}-a_{n-j+1})-a_{n-i+1}(a_{i+1}-a_{i})}{4s_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{id}{2n}-\frac{d(d-1)}{4n}+\frac{a_{n}-a_{n+1}+a_{i}a_{n-i+1}-a_{i}a_{n-j+1}}{4s_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{id}{2n}-\frac{d(d-1)}{4n}+\frac{-2s_{n}+s(L_{n}/f_{i})-a_{i}a_{n-j+1}}{4s_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{id}{2n}-\frac{d(d-1)}{4n}-\frac{a_{i}a_{n-j+1}}{4s_{n}}+\frac{s(L_{n}/f_{i})}{4s_{n}}.

The same argument for the vertex

yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}q^{+}_{e_{k}^{+},u_{j}^{+}}=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}c_{k,j}

\displaystyle=-\frac{id}{2n}-\frac{d(d-1)}{4n}+\frac{a_{n-j+1}(a_{i}-a_{d+i})}{4s_{n}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{id}{2n}-\frac{d(d-1)}{4n}+\frac{a_{i}a_{n-j+1}}{4s_{n}}-\frac{s(L_{n}/f_{j})}{4s_{n}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle r(u_{i}^{+},u_{j}^{+})=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{a_{i}a_{n-j+1}}{2s_{n}}+\frac{s(L_{n}/f_{i})+s(L_{n}/f_{j})}{4s_{n}}.

From the block structure of

it follows that

. Finally, from the equality

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}q^{+}_{e_{k}^{+},u_{j}^{-}}=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}d_{k,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\left(-\frac{k}{2n}+\frac{a_{n-j+1}s_{k}}{2s_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{id}{2n}-\frac{d(d-1)}{4n}-\frac{a_{i}a_{n-j+1}}{4s_{n}}+\frac{s(L_{n}/f_{j})}{4s_{n}}

\displaystyle r(u_{j}^{-},u_{i}^{+})=r(u_{i}^{+},u_{j}^{-})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}q^{+}_{e_{k}^{+},u_{i}^{+}}+b_{j,i}-\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}q^{+}_{e_{k}^{+},u_{j}^{-}}+b_{j,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}+\frac{a_{i}a_{n-j+1}}{2s_{n}}+\frac{s(L_{n}/f_{i})+s(L_{n}/f_{j})}{4s_{n}},

as required.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
CL_{n}
be the circular ladder graph of order
2n
. If
f
is a spoke, then

s(CL_{n}/f)=ns(L_{n}).

Proof.

Consider a natural embedding of circular ladder
CL_{n}
on a cylinder as in Fig.
3
and assume without loss of generality that
f=u_{1}^{+}u_{1}^{-}
. We have two (equivalent up to reflection) classes of Jordan paths:

(1)

Jordan paths
\mathcal{J}_{i,j}^{1}
through edges
u_{i}^{+}u_{i+1}^{+},u_{i+1}^{+}u_{i+1}^{-},\ldots,u_{j-1}^{+}u_{j-1}^{-},u_{j-1}^{-}u_{j}^{-}
for
1\leq i<j\leq n+1
;

(2)

Jordan paths
\mathcal{J}_{i,j}^{2}
through edges
u_{j}^{+}u_{j-1}^{+},u_{j-1}^{+}u_{j-1}^{-},\ldots,u_{i+1}^{+}u_{i+1}^{-},u_{i}^{-}u_{i+1}^{-}
for
1\leq i<j\leq n+1
.

Note that the Jordan paths in parts (1) and (2) coincide when

. Also

. Utilizing the above Jordan paths in conjunction with equation (

), yields

\displaystyle s(CL_{n}/f)=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}s_{\mathcal{J}_{i,j}^{1}}(CL_{n}/f)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{\mathcal{J}_{i,i+1}^{1}}(CL_{n}/f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}s(L_{n+1+i-j}/f_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}s(L_{n}/f_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}a_{i}a_{n+2-j}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{n+1-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{2\leq j\leq n+1}s_{j-1}a_{n+2-j}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{n+1-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i}s_{n+1-i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{n+1-i},

where
L_{s}/f_{t}
denotes the contraction of the

L_{s}
from right or left. Let

and

for all

. Thus

, which implies that

as required.
∎

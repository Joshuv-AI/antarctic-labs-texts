Worldsheet computation of heavy-light correlators
Davide Bufalini, Sergio Iguri, Nicolas Kovensky, David Turton

Abstract
We compute a large collection of string worldsheet correlators describing light probes interacting with heavy black hole microstates. The heavy states consist of NS5 branes carrying momentum and/or fundamental string charge. In the fivebrane decoupling limit, worldsheet string theory on a family of such backgrounds is given by exactly solvable null-gauged WZW models. We construct physical vertex operators in these cosets, including all massless fluctuations. We first compute a large class of novel heavy-light-light-heavy correlators in the AdS 3 limit, where the light operators include those dual to chiral primaries of the holographically dual CFT. We compare a subset of these correlators to the holographic CFT at the symmetric product orbifold point, and find precise agreement in all cases, including for light operators in twisted sectors of the orbifold CFT. The agreement is highly non-trivial, and includes amplitudes that describe the analogue of Hawking radiation for these microstates. We further derive a formula for worldsheet correlators consisting of n n light insertions on these backgrounds, and discuss which subset of these correlators are likely to be protected. As a test, we compute a heavy-light five-point function, obtaining precisely the same result both from the worldsheet and the symmetric orbifold CFT. This paper is a companion to and extension of Bufalini:2022wyp .

String Theory provides a microscopic description of black holes as being bound states of strings and branes with an exponentially large number of internal microstates

While supergravity constructions provide valuable insight into the structure of black hole microstates, it is natural to expect that string-theoretic physics beyond supergravity will be necessary to obtain a complete description of black hole microstructure.
A fruitful arena in which to investigate such stringy physics is provided by bound states of NS5 branes carrying fundamental string (F1) and/or momentum charge (P). More specifically, we work in Type IIB compactified on

The supergravity backgrounds we consider are known as NS5-F1 circular supertubes and spectral flows thereof

The associated worldsheet models are null-gauged WZW models, where before gauging one considers a (10+2)-dimensional target space AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}_{y}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}

3

These coset models can also be thought of as marginal current-current deformations of the worldsheet theory for strings in AdS
3
. These are instances of a larger class of deformations that undo the decoupling limit with respect to the F1 harmonic function, i.e. they
“add back the 1+”
in that function, leading to linear dilaton asymptotics; see e.g.

;
Giveon:2017nie
, flowing towards a non-local Little String Theory.

In this paper we study string correlators in these highly excited backgrounds. To do so, we first compute a large set of physical vertex operators, in both NSNS and RR sectors, building on
Martinec:2018nco
;
Martinec:2020gkv
. These describe linearized perturbations of the background configurations.
We focus primarily on coset states in discrete series representations, including worldsheet spectral flow, that are dual to chiral primary operator excitations in the HCFT.
When the background is BPS, a subset of these are BPS fluctuations.

The currents being gauged in these cosets are linear combinations of the Cartan generators of the symmetry algebra. Therefore the “
m
-basis” for vertex operators, in which the actions of these currents are diagonalized, is the natural framework to use. In the IR AdS
3
limit, we describe how these operators are related to their global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
counterparts.

We then compute a large set of correlators in the AdS
3
limit. It is well known that in worldsheet models of global AdS
3
, one can define an “
x
” variable that corresponds to the local coordinate of the holographic CFT
Kutasov:1999xu
.
One of the main novelties of our approach is the identification of the analogous
x
variable in the coset models we study. This identification requires some care due to the gauging. Indeed, the construction of
Kutasov:1999xu
breaks down, because the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) raising and lowering operators do not commute with the BRST charge. A considerable amount of interesting physics follows from this step. It leads, for instance, to the combination of seemingly simple
m
-basis two-point functions into spacetime-local
x
-basis correlators with highly non-trivial
x
-dependence.

Our first main result is of a family of HLLH correlators, for which we obtain fully explicit expressions. In doing so, we show that these correlators assume a remarkably simple structure when written in terms of a covering space related only to the heavy states.
From this observation, we obtain our second main result: a closed-form expression for a set of HL worldsheet correlators with an arbitrary number
n
of massless insertions, in terms of a correlator consisting of
n
light insertions in global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
.
For
n=3
this result can be made completely explicit, and we present a particular example in full detail. This constitutes the first correlator in the literature involving three light worldsheet vertices on a black hole microstate background, dual to a heavy-light five-point function of the holographic CFT.

A priori
, our worldsheet correlators give predictions for correlators of the dual holographic CFT at strong coupling. Generically, four-point correlators are not protected across moduli space, however, a specific set of HLLH correlators have been shown to precisely agree between supergravity and the symmetric product orbifold CFT
Galliani:2016cai
. Similarly, the
emission spectrum and rate for the unitary
analog of Hawking radiation from the JMaRT solutions agrees between supergravity and symmetric product orbifold CFT
Chowdhury:2007jx
;
Avery:2009tu
;
Avery:2009xr
;
Chakrabarty:2015foa
. Thus it is natural to investigate more generally which HL correlators are protected (at large
N
) between worldsheet and symmetric product orbifold CFT, and which are not.

We carry out this comparison for three sub-families of our worldsheet correlators. Firstly, we compare various sets of HLLH correlators to the symmetric product orbifold CFT, finding exact agreement in all cases for which the orbifold CFT correlator is available in the literature. Importantly, this matching holds at leading order in large
N
, but exactly in
\alpha^{\prime}
.
This comparison includes a substantial generalization of the supergravity and holographic CFT correlators computed in
Galliani:2016cai
. Our comparisons notably include an example in which the light operators in the symmetric orbifold CFT are twist-two. In this case, and as shown recently in
Lima:2021wrz
;
AlvesLima:2022elo
, the Lunin-Mathur covering map used in the symmetric orbifold computation is different to the one appearing in the worldsheet computation, making the comparison highly non-trivial. Remarkably, both results agree exactly in the large
N
limit.

Secondly, we compute the five-point HLLLH symmetric orbifold CFT correlator corresponding to the three-point worldsheet correlator mentioned above, and also find exact agreement. While most of our main results were announced in the short paper
Bufalini:2022wyp
, this five-point correlator is completely new.

Finally, we compute the analogue of the Hawking radiation rate for the JMaRT solutions. Once again, we find perfect agreement with the dual symmetric product orbifold CFT, extending the supergravity and holographic CFT results of
Chowdhury:2007jx
;
Avery:2009tu
;
Avery:2009xr
;
Chakrabarty:2015foa
.

A likely explanation for this remarkable agreement is that the heavy states we consider are quite special. Specifically, the heavy backgrounds are related to the global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
vacuum via orbifolding and fractional spectral flow
Giusto:2012yz
;
Chakrabarty:2015foa
. This fact also underlies our general formula for the HL correlators with
n
light insertions. When
n>3
, we do not expect these HL correlators to be generically protected across moduli space; we shall discuss this in detail in due course.

In Section

We then examine their AdS

In Sections
5
and
6

We identify the “

2.3
Holographic description and boundary spectral flow

As mentioned in the Introduction, the holographic CFT that corresponds to the AdS
3
limit of the system in which we work is an
\mathcal{N}=(4,4)
symmetric product orbifold CFT with target space
\left(\mathbb{T}^{4}\right)^{N}/S_{N}
, where
N=n_{1}n_{5}
. To make the presentation self-contained, we now review some aspects of this theory.

Recall that we work in Type IIB compactified on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
, with
n_{5}
NS5 branes wrapped on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
,
n_{1}
units of F1 winding on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and
n_{P}
units of momentum charge along
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
The moduli space is
20
-dimensional and the symmetric product orbifold CFT lies at a particular locus of this moduli space
Larsen:1999uk
, see also
Bena:2017xbt
.
The configuration
breaks the
\text{SO}(1,9)
Lorentz group to
\text{SO}(1,1)\times\text{SO}(4)_{E}\times\text{U}(1)^{4}
, where the external
R-symmetry

\text{SO}(4)_{E}\simeq\text{SU}(2)_{L}\times\text{SU}(2)_{R}
corresponds to rotations in the spatial
\mathbb{R}^{4}
transverse to the branes (in the IR limit, rotations of the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
).
It is customary to introduce an approximate internal
\text{SO}(4)_{I}\simeq\text{SU}(2)_{1}\times\text{SU}(2)_{2}
, which is broken to
\text{U}(1)^{4}
by the compactification, but which is useful for classifying states and organizing fields
David:2002wn
;
Avery:2010qw
.

In the symmetric product orbifold theory, for each copy of
\mathbb{T}^{4}
there are four free bosons, together with their left and right-moving fermionic superpartners.
Indices
\alpha,\dot{\alpha},A,\dot{A}
correspond respectively to
\text{SU}(2)_{L}\>\!,\text{SU}(2)_{R}\>\!,\text{SU}(2)_{1}\>\!,\text{SU}(2)_{2}
.
The free fields are denoted as (we use the conventions of
Avery:2009tu
)

X_{A\dot{A}\,(r)}(z,\bar{z})\;,\quad\qquad\psi^{\alpha\dot{A}}_{(r)}(z)\;,\quad\qquad\bar{\psi}^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{A}}_{(r)}(\bar{z})\,,

(25)

where the subscript
(r)
denotes the
r
-th copy of the seed
\mathbb{T}^{4}
theory. Omitting this copy subscript and focusing on the holomorphic sector, the energy-momentum tensor
\mathbb{T}(z)
, the supercurrents
\mathbb{G}^{\alpha A}(z)
and the SU
(2)_{L}
currents
\mathbb{J}^{a}
generate the small (4,4) supersymmetric algebra. We denote holographic CFT conformal weights by
h
and R-symmetry quantum numbers by
(\mathbbl{j},m^{\prime})
and
(\bar{\mathbbl{j}},\bar{m}^{\prime})
, respectively.

The heavy states we are interested in are obtained by fractional spectral flow
Chakrabarty:2015foa
, see also
Martinec:2001cf
;
Giusto:2012yz
. We start from the NSNS vacuum in the
{\mathsf{k}}
-twisted sector,
\ket{0_{{\mathsf{k}}}}_{\text{NS}}
.
In order to have a gauge invariant state we consider
n_{1}n_{5}/{\mathsf{k}}
identical strands of length
{\mathsf{k}}
. Its dimension is

h=\bar{h}=\frac{c}{24}\left[1-\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2}}\right],

(26)

where the central charge
c=6N
. The R-charges of this state are zero. Because all strands are of length
{\mathsf{k}}
there is an enhancement of the usual spectral flow, such that one can perform spectral flow with fractional parameters,

\alpha=\frac{{\mathsf{m}}+{\mathsf{n}}}{{\mathsf{k}}}=\frac{2s+1}{{\mathsf{k}}}\;,\quad\qquad\bar{\alpha}=\frac{{\mathsf{m}}-{\mathsf{n}}}{{\mathsf{k}}}=\frac{2\bar{s}+1}{{\mathsf{k}}}\,,

(27)

where
s,\bar{s}\in\mathbb{Z}
and the range
{\mathsf{m}}>{\mathsf{n}}\geq 0
is the range
s\geq\bar{s}\geq 0
. This generates a new state with quantum numbers

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{24}\left[1-\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\right]\;,\quad\qquad m^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha c}{12}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{24}\left[1-\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2}}+\bar{\alpha}^{2}\right]\;,\quad\qquad\bar{m}^{\prime}=\frac{\bar{\alpha}c}{12}\,.

(28)

These states are “heavy” in the sense that their conformal dimensions and charges scale linearly with the large central charge
c=6N
. In the dual theory they correspond to the classical configurations presented in Eqs. (
21
)–(
23
).
By constrast, the “light” perturbative string states probing these backgrounds will correspond to holographic CFT states with conformal dimensions that are independent of
c
.

We conclude this section by summarizing how the bulk, the worldsheet, and the dual CFT encode in different ways the same information about the heavy state.

•

In the worldsheet model, the heavy state defines the theory itself by means of the gauging parameters
l_{i},r_{i}
appearing in Eq. (
4
) and the radius
R_{y}
.

•

In supergravity, the information about the heavy state is contained in the integers
{\mathsf{m}},{\mathsf{n}},{\mathsf{k}}
parameterizing the fields in Eq. (
2.2
), together with
R_{y}
, which gets scaled out in the AdS limit.

•

In the symmetric orbifold CFT, the information about the heavy states is contained in the spectral flow parameters
\alpha
and
\bar{\alpha}
, and the twist index
{\mathsf{k}}
.

The map between the three descriptions, in the AdS limit, is then

-R_{y}\,\frac{l_{2}}{l_{4}}~\xrightarrow{R_{y}\to\infty}~\frac{{\mathsf{m}}+{\mathsf{n}}}{{\mathsf{k}}}\,=\,\alpha\;,\quad\qquad R_{y}\,\frac{r_{2}}{r_{4}}~\xrightarrow{R_{y}\to\infty}~\frac{{\mathsf{m}}-{\mathsf{n}}}{{\mathsf{k}}}\,=\,\bar{\alpha}\;.

(29)

3
The light probe states

We now introduce the light states that we will study. These correspond to chiral primary operators of the boundary theory.
We focus first on fluctuations around the global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
vacuum. We review the dictionary between holographic CFT operators and their counterparts in the worldsheet theory, following
Dabholkar:2007ey
;
Giribet:2007wp
.

3.1
Chiral primaries in the D1D5 CFT

We first briefly review the construction of chiral primary operators in the symmetric orbifold CFT
Lunin:2001pw
. We focus primarily on the holomorphic sector in the following; the antiholomorphic sector is entirely analogous.
In the untwisted sector, on each copy of the seed
\mathbb{T}^{4}
theory, the chiral primary operators correspond to the states (suppressing the copy
(r)
label)

|0_{\mathrm{NS}}\rangle\;,\quad\qquad\psi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{+\dot{A}}|0_{\mathrm{NS}}\rangle\;,\quad\qquad\mathbb{J}_{-1}^{+}|0_{\mathrm{NS}}\rangle\,=\,\psi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{+\dot{1}}\psi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{+\dot{2}}|0_{\mathrm{NS}}\rangle\,,

(30)

where
|0_{\mathrm{NS}}\rangle
is the NS vacuum. The corresponding weights and R-charges are
h=m^{\prime}=0,\frac{1}{2},1
, respectively. Physical configurations in the orbifold theory are obtained by symmetrizing the states in (
30
) over the different copies of the seed theory.

By including the antiholomorphic sector we can obtain, for instance, the dimension
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
operator
(see e.g.
Moscato:2017usq
)

O^{++}=\sum_{r=1}^{N}O^{++}_{(r)}=\frac{-i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{N}\psi^{+\dot{A}}_{(r)}\varepsilon_{\dot{A}\dot{B}}\bar{\psi}^{+\dot{B}}_{(r)}\;,\quad\qquad(O^{++})^{\dagger}=O^{--}\,.

(31)

We will use this operator in an explicit example later in the paper.

In order to construct more general chiral primaries one needs to consider the twisted sectors of the theory. Consider the ‘bare’ twist operators
\sigma_{n}
, defined on the cylinder, that impose the following boundary conditions corresponding to a single-cycle permutation,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X_{(1)}\to X_{(2)}\to\cdots\to X_{(n)}\to X_{(1)}\,,\\
\psi_{(1)}\to\psi_{(2)}\to\cdots\to\psi_{(n)}\to-\psi_{(1)}\,,\end{aligned}

(32)

and likewise for the antiholomorphic fermions.
The bare twist operators are defined to be the lowest-dimension twist operators that impose the above boundary conditions; they have dimension
h=\bar{h}=\frac{1}{4}\left(n-\frac{1}{n}\right)
and zero R-charge. Chiral operators are obtained by exciting the bare twist operators operators to add R-charge. The lowest-dimension chiral operators have
h=m^{\prime}=\frac{n-1}{2}
. For
n
odd, these operators are obtained by acting with modes of the SU(2) currents, which are bilinears in the free fermions. Due to the twist operator, the SU(2) currents are fractional-moded in units of
1/n
. The relation between these modes and those of free fermions on the
n
copies of the seed theory can be found in
Lunin:2001pw
.
To construct the chiral operators, one acts with the currents
\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-l/n}
for which
l
is odd and
l<n
,

n~\mathrm{odd}:\qquad\quad\sigma_{n}^{-}\,=\,\prod_{p=1}^{(n-1)/2}\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-\frac{2p-1}{n}}{\sigma}_{n}~=~\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-\frac{n-2}{n}}\cdots\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-\frac{3}{n}}\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-\frac{1}{n}}{\sigma}_{n}\,.

(33)

For
n
even, one first acts with a spin field
S^{+}_{n}
, which has weight
\frac{1}{4n}
and charge
\frac{1}{2}
, putting the fermions into the Ramond sector (i.e. their boundary conditions are similar to Eq. (
32
) but with the final sign being
+\psi_{(1)}
). One then acts with the currents
\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-l/n}
for which
l
is even and
l<n
,

n~\mathrm{even}:\qquad\quad\sigma_{n}^{-}\,=\,\prod_{p=1}^{(n-2)/2}\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-\frac{2p}{n}}S^{+}_{n}{\sigma}_{n}~=~\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-\frac{n-2}{n}}\cdots\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-\frac{4}{n}}\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-\frac{2}{n}}S^{+}_{n}{\sigma}_{n}\,.

(34)

As in the untwisted case, for both odd and even
n
we can act with
\psi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{+\dot{A}}\equiv\sum\limits_{r=1}^{n}\psi_{-\frac{1}{2}(r)}^{+\dot{A}}
to obtain a chiral operator

\psi_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{+\dot{A}}\sigma_{n}^{-}

which has
h=m^{\prime}=\frac{n}{2}
. Similarly we can act with
\mathbb{J}_{-1}^{+}
to obtain a chiral operator

\mathbb{J}^{+}_{-1}\sigma_{n}^{-}

which has
h=m^{\prime}=\frac{n+1}{2}
.
Together with the analogous antiholomorphic operators, this exhausts the single-cycle chiral operators. Indeed, by making use of anti-commutators of the supercurrent modes
\mathbb{G}_{-m/n}^{\pm A}
in the corresponding twisted sectors, one can show that chiral weights are bounded by
Avery:2010qw

\frac{n-1}{2}\,\leq\,h\,\leq\,\frac{n+1}{2}\,.

(35)

In the twisted sectors, it is often convenient to work in a basis that diagonalizes the twisted boundary conditions. We shall make use of this basis in Section
6.4
. One defines

\tilde{\psi}_{\rho}^{\alpha\dot{A}}\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{r=1}^{n}e^{\alpha\frac{2\pi ir\rho}{n}}\psi_{(r)}^{\alpha\dot{A}}\;,\quad\qquad\rho=0,\dots,n-1\,,

(36)

where
\alpha=\pm
should not be confused with the spectral flow parameter in Eq. (
27
). These are mutually orthogonal, and diagonalize the twisted boundary conditions as

\tilde{\psi}_{\rho}^{\alpha\dot{A}}(e^{2\pi i}z)\,=\,e^{-\alpha\frac{2\pi i\rho}{n}}\tilde{\psi}_{\rho}^{\alpha\dot{A}}(z)\,.

(37)

These fermions can be bosonized to construct an explicit expression for the spin fields mentioned above. Note that the fields
\tilde{\psi}_{\rho=0}^{\alpha\dot{A}}
are invariant under the twisting. For further discussion, see
Dabholkar:2007ey
.

We now combine the above holomorphic construction with its antiholomorphic counterpart and define the complete list of scalar left-right chiral primaries we will be interested in:

O_{n}^{--}=\sigma_{n}^{--}\;,\quad\qquad O_{n}^{\dot{A}\dot{B}}=\tilde{\psi}_{\!\!\;\rho=0}^{+\dot{A}}\;\!\bar{\tilde{\psi}}_{\!\!\;\rho=0}^{+\dot{B}}\;\!\sigma_{n}^{--}\;,\quad\qquad O_{n}^{++}=\tilde{\psi}_{\!\!\;\rho=0}^{+\dot{1}}\;\!\tilde{\psi}_{\rho=0}^{+\dot{2}}\;\!\bar{\tilde{\psi}}_{\!\!\;\rho=0}^{+\dot{1}}\;\!\bar{\tilde{\psi}}_{\!\!\;\rho=0}^{+\dot{2}}\;\!\sigma_{n}^{--}\,,

(38)

where
\sigma_{n}^{--}
is defined similarly to Eqs. (
33
), (
34
) but now also with the same construction in the antiholomorphic sector. The operators in (
38
) are normalized such that they have unit two-point functions.

For later reference, we note that in each case the respective weights and twist numbers can be written in terms of
j=\frac{n+1}{2}
as

h\left[O_{n}^{--}\right]=j-1\;,\quad\qquad h\left[O_{n}^{\dot{A}\dot{B}}\right]=j-\frac{1}{2}\;,\quad\qquad h\left[O_{n}^{++}\right]=j\,.

(39)

An analogous list of anti-chiral primaries (which have
h=-m^{\prime}
) is obtained by acting on the bare twist fields with current and fermion modes with opposite charge, i.e.
\mathbb{J}_{-l/n}^{-}
and
\psi^{-\dot{A}}
. As we will shortly review, and up to a shift related to spectral flow, this
j
will be identified with the principal quantum number of the bosonic (global) SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) algebra of the worldsheet theory, to which we now turn.

3.2
Superstring theory on AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}

We now review the basics of superstring theory on AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
using the RNS formalism with BRST quantization. We first discuss the bosonic SL(
2,\mathbb{R}
) and SU
(2)
WZW models and then present their supersymmetric counterparts.
We present the current algebra and review the spectrum, including states arising from worldsheet spectral flow.

3.2.1
Bosonic WZW model for SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
)

The SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) WZW model was studied in detail in
Maldacena:2000hw
;
Maldacena:2000kv
;
Maldacena:2001km
. In what follows we will mostly follow the notation of
Dabholkar:2007ey
;
Giribet:2007wp
;
Dei:2021xgh
, and normal ordering will be implicitly assumed. The holomorphic SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) currents will be denoted
j^{a}(z)
. They satisfy the OPEs

j^{a}(z)j^{b}(w)\,\sim\,\frac{k}{2}\frac{\eta^{ab}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{f^{ab}_{\phantom{ab}c}\;\!j^{c}(w)}{z-w}\,,

(40)

where
k
is the level of the affine algebra, and where

-2\eta^{33}=\eta^{+-}=2\;,\quad\qquad f^{+-}_{\phantom{+-}3}=-2\,,\quad f^{3+}_{\phantom{3+}+}=-f^{3-}_{\phantom{3-}-}=1\,.

(41)

The holomorphic stress tensor and the central charge follow from the Sugawara construction, and are given by (likewise for the antiholomorphic sector)

T_{\mathrm{sl}}(z)=\frac{1}{k-2}\left[-j^{3}(z)j^{3}(z)+\frac{1}{2}\,j^{+}(z)j^{-}(z)+\frac{1}{2}\,j^{-}(z)j^{+}(z)\right]\,,\qquad c_{\mathrm{sl}}=\frac{3k}{k-2}\,.

(42)

We denote bosonic SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
primary vertex operators by
V_{j,m,\bar{m}}(z,\bar{z})
. Their zero-mode wavefunctions do not factorize between holomorphic and antiholomorphic sectors, however as is often done we shall work primarily with the holomorphic sector, and suppress the
\bar{m}
and
\bar{z}
dependence.
The relevant representations of the holomorphic zero-mode algebra are as follows.
The principal series discrete representations of lowest (highest) weight are spanned by

{\cal{D}}_{j}^{\pm}=\{|j,m\rangle\ ,\ m=\pm j,\pm j\pm 1,\pm j\pm 2,\cdots\}\,,

(43)

respectively, where
j_{0}^{3}|j,m\rangle=m|j,m\rangle
.
These are unitary representations for any positive real
j
, and one is the charge conjugate of the other (we will restrict the range of
j
momentarily). There are also the principal continuous series representations, spanned by

{\cal{C}}_{j}^{\hat{\alpha}}=\{|j,\hat{\alpha},m\rangle\ ,\ 0\leq\hat{\alpha}<1\ ,\ j=\frac{1}{2}+is\ ,\ s\in\mathbb{R}\ ,\ m=\hat{\alpha},\hat{\alpha}\pm 1,\hat{\alpha}\pm 2,\cdots\}\,.

(44)

The particular case
\hat{\alpha}=1/2=j
is actually reducible. It was shown in
Maldacena:2000hw
that the spectrum of the model is built out of continuous and lowest weight representations with

\frac{1}{2}<j<\frac{k-1}{2}\,,

(45)

together with their spectrally flowed images, to be introduced below. The allowed range (
45
) follows from
L^{2}
normalization conditions, no-ghost theorems and spectral flow considerations.

Before considering worldsheet spectral flow (we refer to this as the “unflowed” sector), the action of the currents on the primary states is given by

\displaystyle j^{3}_{0}|j,m\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m|j,m\rangle\;,

(46a)

\displaystyle j^{\pm}_{0}|j,m\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\,(m\mp(j-1))|j,m\pm 1\rangle&\text{if}~~m\neq\mp j\\
~0&\text{if}~~m=\mp j\;,\end{cases}

(46b)

\displaystyle j^{a}_{n}|j,m\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ \qquad\forall n>0\,.

(46c)

These vertex operators
can be obtained from those of the Euclidean counterpart of the model, namely the
H_{3}^{+}
WZW model
Teschner:1997ft
;
Teschner:1997fv
;
Teschner:1999ug
(see also
McElgin:2015eho
), as follows.
One introduces a set of operators depending on a complex label
x
, written as
V_{j}(x|z)
, and having conformal weight

\Delta\,=\,-\frac{j(j-1)}{k-2}\,.

(47)

The action of the currents on
V_{j}(x,z)
is given by

j^{a}(z)V_{j}(x,w)\,\sim\,\frac{D_{j}^{a}V_{j}(x,w)}{(z-w)}\,,

(48)

where

D_{j}^{+}=\partial_{x}\;,\quad\qquad D_{j}^{3}=x\partial_{x}+j\;,\quad\qquad D_{j}^{-}=x^{2}\partial_{x}+2jx\,.

(49)

The two-point function is given by
Teschner:1999ug

\langle V_{j_{1}}(x_{1},z_{1})V_{j_{2}}(x_{2},z_{2})\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4\Delta_{1}}}\left[\delta^{2}(x_{1}-x_{2})\delta(j_{1}+j_{2}-1)+\frac{B(j_{1})}{|x_{12}|^{4j_{1}}}\delta(j_{1}-j_{2})\right],

(50)

with

B(j)=\frac{2j-1}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma[1-b^{2}(2j-1)]}{\Gamma[1+b^{2}(2j-1)]}\,\nu^{1-2j}\;,\quad\qquad\nu=\frac{\Gamma[1-b^{2}]}{\Gamma[1+b^{2}]}\ ,\ b^{2}=(k-2)^{-1}\,.

(51)

The operators
V_{j,m}(z)
are related to
V_{j}(x|z)
by means of the following Mellin-like transform:

V_{j,m}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}d^{2}x\,x^{j-m-1}\bar{x}^{j-\bar{m}-1}V_{j}(x,z)\,.

(52)

In the Euclidean
H_{3}^{+}
model,
j
takes values
j=1/2+is
. To obtain the unflowed
V_{j,m}
for Lorentzian AdS
3
, one assumes a well-defined analytically continuation to real values of
j
. This procedure was discussed in
Maldacena:2001km
, which identified the physical origin of the different divergences arising in correlation functions. For related work, see
Harlow:2011ny
.
The two-point functions in the
m
-basis then follow from (
50
), (
52
).
Using the shorthand
V_{i}\equiv V_{j_{i},m_{i}}
, one finds

\langle V_{1}V_{2}\rangle\,=\,\frac{\delta^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})}{|z_{12}|^{4\Delta_{1}}}\left[\delta(j_{1}+j_{2}-1)+\delta(j_{1}-j_{2})\frac{\pi B(j_{1})}{\gamma(2j_{1})}\frac{\gamma(j_{1}+m_{1})}{\gamma(1-j_{1}+m_{1})}\right],

(53)

where
\gamma(x)=\Gamma(x)/\Gamma(1-\bar{x})
, and where
\delta^{2}(m)
is a Dirac delta in
m+\bar{m}
times a Kroenecker delta in
m-\bar{m}
.

At first sight, the complex variable
x
may appear simply as an SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) version of the isospin variables defined for SU(2) in
Zamolodchikov:1986bd
. However, given that the integrated zero modes of the currents realize the spacetime Virasoro modes
L_{0}
and
L_{\pm 1}
, and by examining the expressions of the associated differential operators (
49
), one is led to interpret
x
as the local coordinate on the boundary theory
Giveon:1998ns
. According to (
50
), in the bosonic theory a
z
-integrated vertex operator
V_{j}(x)
is identified with a local operator on the boundary theory with weight
j
. Conversely, the corresponding boundary modes are given by the
m
-basis operators. Indeed, for states in the discrete sector, the transform in Eq. (
52
) can be inverted, giving

V_{j}(x,z)\;=\sum_{m=j+n,\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}x^{m-j}\>\!\bar{x}^{\bar{m}-j}\,V_{j,m\bar{m}}(z)\,.

(54)

The vertex
V_{j}(x,z)
is realized via Eq. (
54
) as
V_{j,j}(z)
translated from the origin to
x
. Poles in the integrand of (
52
) coming from the expansion around
x=0
(
x=\infty
) are associated to states in the
{\cal D}_{j}^{+}
(
{\cal D}_{j}^{-}
) representations
Eberhardt:2019ywk
;
Dei:2021xgh
.

Spectral flow automorphisms of the current algebra (
40
) are defined as

j^{\pm}(z)\to\tilde{j}^{\pm}(z)=z^{\pm w}j^{\pm}(z)\;,\quad\qquad j^{3}(z)\to\tilde{j}^{3}(z)=j^{3}(z)-\frac{k\,\omega}{2}\frac{1}{z}\,,

(55)

where the so-called spectral flow charge
\omega
is an integer.
Analogous formulas hold for the antiholomorphic sector. We work with the universal cover of SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
, which imposes that the holomorphic and antiholomorphic spectral flow parameters must be equal,
\bar{\omega}=\omega
.
The action of (
55
) on the above representations defines in general inequivalent representations that must be considered in order to generate a consistent spectrum. This holds up to the so-called series identifications due to the fact that the affine modules
\hat{{\cal{D}}}_{j}^{+,w}
and
\hat{{\cal{D}}}_{k/2-j}^{-,w+1}
are isomorphic. Thus, as mentioned above, the discrete series spectrum is constructed solely upon lowest weight representations with
j
restricted to the range (
45
).

At the level of vertex operators and for
w>0
, the spectral flow operation introduced in (
55
) defines the so-called flowed primaries, whose OPEs with the currents take the form

\displaystyle j^{+}(z)V_{j,m}^{\omega}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m+1-j)V_{j,m+1}^{\omega}(w)}{(z-w)^{\omega+1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\omega}\frac{(j^{+}_{n-1}V_{j,m}^{\omega})(w)}{(z-w)^{n}}+\dots\,,

(56a)

\displaystyle j^{3}(z)V_{j,m}^{\omega}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(m+\frac{k}{2}\omega\right)V_{j,m}^{\omega}(w)}{(z-w)}+\dots\,,

(56b)

\displaystyle j^{-}(z)V_{j,m}^{\omega}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(z-w)^{\omega-1}(m-1+j)V_{j,m-1}^{\omega}(w)+\dots\,,

(56c)

where the ellipses indicate higher-order terms. Similar expressions hold for
\omega<0
, with the roles of
j^{+}
and
j^{-}
inverted.
The operators
V_{j,m}^{\omega}(z)
are not affine primaries. They are, however, Virasoro primaries, with worldsheet conformal weight

\hat{\Delta}\,=\,-\frac{j(j-1)}{k-2}-m\omega-\frac{k}{4}\omega^{2}\,.

(57)

Note that for
\omega>0
(
\omega<0
), independently of the characteristics of the original state, these correspond to lowest (highest) weight states, with SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) spin

h=m+\frac{k}{2}\omega\,,

(58)

(
h=-m-k\omega/2
, respectively).
The notation
h
anticipates that the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) spin is identified with the holographic CFT conformal weight
Kutasov:1999xu
(see also e.g.
Dabholkar:2007ey
), as we shall see in Eq. (
60
).

The flowed affine modules alluded above are built by acting freely with the currents on flowed primary states. In particular, the remaining states in the zero-mode algebra, which are obtained by acting with
j_{0}^{-}
, are
not
flowed primaries.
Nevertheless, one can proceed as done for the unflowed states, and combine them into a local operator, defined initially for
\omega>0
as

V^{w}_{j,h}(x,z)\,=\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}x^{n}\bar{x}^{\bar{n}}\,V_{j,h+n,h+\bar{n}}^{w}(z)\,.

(59)

Moreover, by inverting
x\to 1/x
in the expansion, one also obtains the states in the highest-weight representation with the same spin and opposite
\omega
and
m
. This shows that the resulting
x
-basis states are actually defined in terms of the absolute value of
\omega
, its sign being irrelevant. A direct
x
-basis definition for spectrally flowed vertex operators was recently derived in
Iguri:2022eat
, extending the original proposal of
Maldacena:2001km
valid only for the singly flowed case.

The classical analog of the spectral flow operation (
55
) maps space-like geodesics of point-like strings into solutions in which a long string wound around the AdS
3
angular direction at large radius comes in to the centre of global AdS
3
, collapses to a point, and then re-expands to large radial distance
Maldacena:2000hw
. The spectral flow parameter
\omega
is thus sometimes referred to in the literature as a “winding” number. Note that since the AdS
3
angular direction is contractible in the interior of global AdS
3
, the parameter
\omega
is not a conserved quantity. However, the
m
-basis two-point functions are diagonal in
\omega
:
it was shown in
Maldacena:2001km
that the
m
-basis two-point function of flowed primaries is as in (
53
) with an extra factor of
\delta_{\omega_{1},-\omega_{2}}
and the worldsheet conformal weight
\Delta_{1}
replaced by
\hat{\Delta}_{1}
given in Eq. (
57
). On the other hand, in the
x
-basis one finds

\langle V_{j_{1},h_{1}}^{\omega_{1}}(x_{1},z_{1})V_{j_{2},h_{2}}^{\omega_{2}}(x_{2},z_{2})\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{|x_{12}|^{4h_{1}}}\frac{\langle V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1},m_{1}}V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2},m_{2}}\rangle}{V_{\mathrm{conf}}}\,.

(60)

Thus, as mentioned above, the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) spin
h
is identified with the holographic CFT conformal weight
Kutasov:1999xu
, even though in the flowed sectors the spin is independent of the value of
j
of the corresponding unflowed operator. The factor
V_{\mathrm{conf}}
stands for the divergent volume of the conformal group; it reflects the fact we are picking up the contribution from a pole, and it will cancel in the relevant computations that follow.

3.2.2
Bosonic WZW model for SU(2)

The bosonic WZW model based on the SU
(2)
group manifold was studied in
Fateev:1985mm
;
Zamolodchikov:1986bd
. We denote the generators of the current algebra by
k^{a}
, and for most quantities we use primes to distinguish them from their SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
counterparts. The currents satisfy the OPEs

k^{a}(z)k^{b}(w)\,\sim\,\frac{k^{\prime}}{2}\frac{\delta^{ab}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{f^{\prime ab}_{\phantom{ab}c}\;\!k^{c}(w)}{z-w}\;,

(61)

where
k^{\prime}
is the level of the affine Lie algebra,
\delta^{ab}
is the Killing form, and
f^{\prime abc}
are the corresponding structure constants,

2\delta^{33}=\delta^{+-}=2\;,\quad\qquad f^{\prime+-}_{\phantom{+-}3}=2\ ,\quad f^{\prime 3+}_{\phantom{3+}+}=-f^{\prime 3-}_{\phantom{3-}-}=1\,.

(62)

The energy-momentum tensor and central charge are

T_{\mathrm{su}}(z)=\frac{1}{k^{\prime}+2}\left[k^{3}(z)k^{3}(z)+\frac{1}{2}\,k^{+}(z)k^{-}(z)+\frac{1}{2}\,k^{-}(z)k^{+}(z)\right]\;,\qquad c_{\text{su}}=\frac{3k^{\prime}}{k^{\prime}+2}.

(63)

We denote SU
(2)
vertex operators by
V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}(z,\bar{z})
. Again, their zero-mode wavefunctions do not factorize into holomorphic and antiholomorphic parts, however we shall mostly work holomorphically and suppress antiholomorphic quantities (
\bar{m}^{\prime},\bar{z}
).

For SU
(2)
, the unitary representations of the zero-mode algebra are labeled by

\ 0\leq j^{\prime}\leq\frac{k^{\prime}}{2}\;,\quad\qquad j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}/2\ ,

(64)

and their states are
|j^{\prime},m^{\prime}\rangle
with
m^{\prime}=-j^{\prime},-j^{\prime}+1,\dots,j^{\prime}-1,j^{\prime}
. Using conventions that mimic those used above for SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle k_{0}^{3}|j^{\prime},m^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{\prime}|j^{\prime},m^{\prime}\rangle\,,

(65a)

\displaystyle k^{\pm}_{0}|j^{\prime},m^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\,(j^{\prime}+1\pm m^{\prime})|j^{\prime},m^{\prime}\pm 1\rangle&\text{if}~~m\neq\pm j\\
~0&\text{if}~~m=\pm j\;,\end{cases}

(65b)

\displaystyle k_{n}^{a}|j^{\prime},m^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ \qquad\forall n>0\,,

(65c)

and

\Delta^{\prime}\,=\,\frac{j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}+1)}{k^{\prime}+2}\,.

(66)

Unlike SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
, in the SU
(2)
WZW model spectral flow is not necessary for constructing a consistent spectrum due to the compactness of the group manifold. Indeed, the spectral flow automorphisms merely reshuffle primary and descendant fields, and they do not introduce new inequivalent representations. Nevertheless, for superstring theory applications it is of practical use to include it in the discussion
Giribet:2007wp
;
Martinec:2018nco
;
Bufalini:2021ndn
. We will discuss this in more detail shortly.

For SU
(2)
, spectral flow is defined as

k^{\pm}(z)\to\tilde{k}^{\pm}(z)=z^{\mp w^{\prime}}k^{\pm}(z)\;,\quad\qquad k^{3}(z)\to\tilde{k}^{3}(z)=k^{3}(z)-\frac{k^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}}{2}\frac{1}{z}\,.

(67)

In this case, however,
it is possible to have
\bar{\omega}^{\prime}\neq\omega^{\prime}
.
As before, spectrally flowed primaries
V_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{\omega^{\prime}}(z)
are Virasoro primaries, with weight

\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}\,=\,\frac{j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}+1)}{k^{\prime}-2}+m^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}+\frac{k^{\prime}}{4}\omega^{\prime 2}\,,

(68)

but they are not affine primaries, and for
\omega^{\prime}>0
they are defined in terms of the OPEs

\displaystyle k^{+}(z)V_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{\omega^{\prime}}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(z-w)^{\omega^{\prime}-1}(j^{\prime}-m^{\prime})V_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}+1}^{\omega^{\prime}}(w)+\dots\,,

(69a)

\displaystyle k^{3}(z)V_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{\omega^{\prime}}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(m^{\prime}+\frac{k}{2}\omega^{\prime}\right)V_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{\omega^{\prime}}(w)}{(z-w)}+\dots\,,

(69b)

\displaystyle k^{-}(z)V_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{\omega^{\prime}}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(j^{\prime}+m^{\prime})V_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}-1}^{\omega^{\prime}}(w)}{(z-w)^{\omega^{\prime}+1}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\omega^{\prime}}\frac{(k^{-}_{n-1}V_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{\omega^{\prime}})(w)}{(z-w)^{n}}+\dots\,.

(69c)

The corresponding two-point functions are, again, the unflowed ones times
\delta_{\omega_{1},-\omega_{2}}
, with the appropriate powers of
z_{12}
.

3.2.3
Superstrings in AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}

We now review supersymmetric generalizations of the bosonic WZW models discussed above.
We introduce fermions
\psi^{a}
and
\chi^{a}
which are superpartners of the SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
and SU
(2)
currents
J^{a}
and
K^{a}
respectively.
The appropriate
{\cal{N}}=1
supersymmetric extensions of the affine sl(2,
\mathbb{R}
)
k
and su(2)
{}_{k^{\prime}}
algebras are generated by the supercurrents
\psi^{a}+\theta\>\!J^{a}
and
\chi^{a}+\theta K^{a}
, where
\theta
is a Grassmann variable. The currents
J^{a}
and
K^{a}
satisfy the OPEs (
40
) and (
61
) respectively, with level
n_{5}
in both cases, and the OPEs involving the fermions
\psi^{a}
and
\chi^{a}
are

\displaystyle J^{a}(z)\psi^{b}(w)\,\sim\,\frac{f^{ab}_{\phantom{ab}c}\;\!\psi^{c}(w)}{(z-w)}\;,\quad\qquad

\displaystyle K^{a}(z)\chi^{b}(w)\,\sim\,\frac{f^{\prime ab}_{\phantom{lab}c}\;\!\chi^{c}(w)}{(z-w)},

(70a)

\displaystyle\psi^{a}(z)\psi^{b}(w)\,\sim\,\frac{n_{5}}{2}\frac{\eta^{ab}}{(z-w)}\;,\quad\qquad

\displaystyle\chi^{a}(z)\chi^{b}(w)\,\sim\,\frac{n_{5}}{2}\frac{\delta^{ab}}{(z-w)}\,.

(70b)

One can split the currents into two independent contributions via

J^{a}=j^{a}-\frac{1}{n_{5}}f^{a}_{\phantom{a}bc}\>\!\psi^{b}\psi^{c}\;,\quad\qquad K^{a}=k^{a}-\frac{1}{n_{5}}f^{\prime a}_{\phantom{la}bc}\;\!\chi^{b}\chi^{c}\,.

(71)

The “bosonic” currents
j^{a}
and
k^{a}
commute with the free fermions, and are currents of bosonic WZW models as described in Section
3.2
, with levels
k=n_{5}+2
and
k^{\prime}=n_{5}-2
respectively. In the fermionic sector, the spectral flow automorphisms act as

\tilde{\psi}^{\pm}(z)=z^{\mp}\psi^{\pm}(z)\,,\quad\tilde{\psi}^{3}(z)=\psi^{3}(z)\;,\quad\qquad\tilde{\chi}^{\pm}(z)=z^{\mp}\chi^{\pm}(z)\,,\quad\tilde{\chi}^{3}(z)=\chi^{3}(z)\,.

(72)

The remaining flat compact directions are treated as usual. For the
\mathbb{T}^{4}
, we simply have four (canonically normalized) free bosons
Y^{i}
and their fermionic partners
\lambda^{i}

(i=6,\dots,9)
, with OPEs

Y^{i}(z)Y^{j}(w)\,\sim\,-\delta^{ij}\log(z-w)\;,\quad\qquad\lambda^{i}(z)\lambda^{j}(w)\,\sim\,\frac{\delta^{ij}}{(z-w)}\,.

(73)

We can now write down the energy-momentum tensor
T
and the supercurrent
G
of the worldsheet theory for type II superstrings in AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
. The matter contributions read

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{5}}\Big(j^{a}j_{a}-\psi^{a}\partial\psi_{a}+k^{a}k_{a}-\chi^{a}\partial\chi_{a}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial Y^{i}\partial Y_{i}-\lambda^{i}\partial\lambda_{i}\right),

(74)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n_{5}}\left(\psi^{a}j_{a}-\frac{1}{3n_{5}}f_{abc}\psi^{a}\psi^{b}\psi^{c}+\chi^{a}k_{a}-\frac{1}{3n_{5}}f^{\prime}_{abc}\chi^{a}\chi^{b}\chi^{c}\right)+i\>\lambda^{j}\partial Y_{j}\,,

(75)

and the resulting central charge is compensated by the usual
bc
and
\beta\gamma
ghost systems, leading to the BRST charge

{\cal{Q}}=\oint dz\left(c\left(T+T_{\beta\gamma}\right)-\gamma\,G+c(\partial c)b-\frac{1}{4}b\gamma^{2}\right)\,.

(76)

Here
T_{\beta\gamma}
is the energy-momentum tensor of the
\beta\gamma
system, which is bosonized as

\displaystyle\beta=e^{-\varphi}\partial\xi\;,\quad\qquad\gamma=\eta\>\!e^{\varphi}\,,

(77)

where
\varphi(z)\varphi(w)\simeq-\ln(z-w)
has background charge
2
, and
\xi(z)\eta(w)\sim(z-w)^{-1}
.
For computational purposes it is useful to also bosonize the rest of the fermions
Giveon:1998ns
;
Dabholkar:2007ey
. We thus define (canonically normalized) bosonic fields
H_{I}
with
I=1,\dots 5
, and write

\hat{H}_{I}=H_{I}+\pi\sum_{J<I}N_{J}\;,\quad\qquad N_{J}\equiv\oint i\partial H_{J}\,,

(78)

where the number operators
N_{I}
are introduced in order to keep track of the cocycle factors, namely

e^{ia\hat{H}_{I}}e^{ib\hat{H}_{J}}=e^{ib\hat{H}_{J}}e^{ia\hat{H}_{I}}\,e^{i\pi ab}\;,\quad\qquad\text{if}\quad I>J\,.

(79)

We bosonize as

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}=\sqrt{n_{5}}\,e^{\pm i\hat{H}_{1}}\,,\quad\chi^{\pm}=\sqrt{n_{5}}\,e^{\pm i\hat{H}_{2}}\,,\quad\lambda^{6}\pm i\lambda^{7}=e^{\pm i\hat{H}_{4}}\,,\quad\lambda^{8}\pm i\lambda^{9}=e^{\pm i\hat{H}_{5}}\,,

(80a)

\displaystyle\qquad\psi^{3}=\frac{\sqrt{n_{5}}}{2}\,\left(e^{i\hat{H}_{3}}-e^{-i\hat{H}_{3}}\right)\;,\quad\qquad\chi^{3}=\frac{\sqrt{n_{5}}}{2}\,\left(e^{i\hat{H}_{3}}+e^{-i\hat{H}_{3}}\right)\,,

(80b)

where
\hat{H}_{I}^{\dagger}=\hat{H}_{I}
for
I\neq 3
and
\hat{H}_{3}^{\dagger}=-\hat{H}_{3}
. Then we have

\displaystyle i\partial\hat{H}_{1}=\frac{1}{n_{5}}\,\psi^{+}\psi^{-}\;,\quad\qquad i\partial\hat{H}_{2}=\frac{1}{n_{5}}\,\chi^{+}\chi^{-}\;,\quad\qquad i\partial\hat{H}_{3}=\frac{2}{n_{5}}\,\psi^{3}\chi^{3}\,,

(81a)

\displaystyle i\partial\hat{H}_{4}=i\lambda^{6}\lambda^{7}\;,\quad\qquad i\partial\hat{H}_{5}=i\lambda^{8}\lambda^{9}\,.

(81b)

The phases in (
79
) ensure that bosonized fermions anticommute, and will be important when working with states in the Ramond sector. From now on we will simply omit the hats, and explicitly include the phase factors when they are needed.

The spacetime supercharges can be written as:

Q_{\varepsilon}=\oint dz\,e^{-\varphi/2}S_{\varepsilon}\;,\quad\qquad S_{\varepsilon}=\exp\left(\frac{i}{2}\sum_{I=1}^{5}\varepsilon_{I}H_{I}\right),

(82)

where
S_{\varepsilon}
are spin fields and
\varepsilon_{I}=\pm 1
.
Imposing BRST invariance – where the relevant contributions come from the
f_{abc}\psi^{a}\psi^{b}\psi^{c}
and
f_{abc}^{\prime}\chi^{a}\chi^{b}\chi^{c}
pieces of
G
in (
75
) – and mutual locality (chiral GSO) leads to the conditions

\prod_{I=1}^{3}\varepsilon_{I}=\prod_{I=1}^{5}\varepsilon_{I}=1\,.

(83)

In the holomorphic sector this gives the expected four ‘ordinary’ supercharges and four ‘superconformal’ supercharges. The same applies in the antiholomorphic sector, giving the total 16 real supercharges of global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}

Giveon:1998ns
.

For later use, let us also recall that the R-symmetry of the boundary theory is generated on the worldsheet by the SU(2) currents. More precisely, the zero modes of the spacetime R-currents are given by the integrated worldsheet currents
Giveon:1998ns
, i.e.

\mathbb{J}^{a}_{0}=\oint dz\,K^{a}(z)\,.

(84)

Consequently, the holomorphic R-charge in the holographic CFT is identified with
m^{\prime}
, the eigenvalue of
K^{3}
.
It is for this reason that we used the notation
m^{\prime}
in Sections
2.3
and
3.1
.

3.3
Vertex operators and two-point functions

We now discuss physical vertex operators and their two-point functions, both in NS and R sectors. This section is largely review, though we also give explicit expressions for some R sector operators that to our knowledge have not appeared before in the literature.

Our main interest is in worldsheet operators that correspond to chiral primaries of the holographic CFT. We thus focus on states belonging to the discrete representations of SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
). We also discuss the role of SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) and SU
(2)
spectral flows in the string theoretical construction.
These vertex operators and their two-point functions will be used as building blocks for constructing the vertex operators and two-point functions of the null-gauged models in Section
4
.

3.3.1
NS sector

The unflowed NS-NS sector was considered in
Kutasov:1998zh
, see also
Dabholkar:2007ey
.
We continue to suppress antiholomorphic parts of the SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
and SU
(2)
vertex operators
V_{j,m,\bar{m}}
and
V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}
. The complete NSNS vertex is obtained by including the antiholomorphic fermions and ghosts.

We work in the canonical “
-1
” ghost picture, and consider only states with vanishing momentum in the
\mathbb{T}^{4}
directions.
Then the (holomorphic part of the) BRST invariant states with up to a single fermionic excitation include the
tachyon (which is projected out by GSO),

\mathcal{T}_{j,m,j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}=e^{-\varphi}V_{j,m}V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\,,

(85)

and the spacetime vectors (
\varepsilon=\pm 1
, and recall
i=6,\dots,9
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{i}_{j,m,j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\varphi}\lambda^{i}V_{j,m}V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\,,

(86a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\varepsilon}_{j,m,j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\varphi}\left(\psi V_{j}\right)_{j+\varepsilon,m}V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\,,

(86b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}_{j,m,j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\varphi}V_{j,m}(\chi V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}})_{j^{\prime}+\varepsilon,m^{\prime}}\,,

(86c)

where we have introduced the linear combinations

(\psi V_{j})_{j+\varepsilon,m}=c_{\varepsilon}^{r}\psi^{r}V_{j,m-r}\;,\quad\qquad(\chi V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}})_{j^{\prime}+\varepsilon,m^{\prime}}=d_{\varepsilon}^{r}\chi^{r}V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}-r}\,,

(87)

where a summation over
r=+1,-1,0
is implicit, “
0
” corresponding to the “
3
” direction of the respective algebras. These combine the products of bosonic primaries and free fermions into fields of total spins
J=j+\varepsilon
and
J^{\prime}=j^{\prime}+\varepsilon
under the action of the supersymmetric currents
J^{a}
and
K^{a}

Kutasov:1998zh
. The Clebsh-Gordan coefficients are given in our conventions by

\displaystyle c^{r}_{-}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},-1\right),\quad d^{r}_{+}=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\right),

\displaystyle c_{+}^{r}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}(j+m)(j+m-1),\;\frac{1}{2}(j-m)(j-m-1),\;(j+m)(j-m)\right),

(88)

\displaystyle d^{r}_{-}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}(j^{\prime}-m^{\prime})(j^{\prime}-m^{\prime}+1),\;-\frac{1}{2}(j^{\prime}+m^{\prime})(j^{\prime}+m^{\prime}+1),\;(j^{\prime}-m^{\prime})(j^{\prime}+m^{\prime})\right)\,.

The Virasoro condition associated to all vertex operators in Eq. (
86
) reads

\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j(j-1)}{n_{5}}+\frac{j^{\prime}(j^{\prime}+1)}{n_{5}}=1\,,

(89)

and is solved by
j=j^{\prime}+1
(or its reflection under

), thus implying that we are dealing with bosonic primaries in the discrete representations of SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
)
k
.

Let us briefly discuss the worldsheet two-point functions involving these operators. The different bosonic sectors factorize and the fermions are free, so we can express the results directly in terms of the non-trivial contributions coming from the bosonic SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) WZW model, namely Eq. (
53
). By construction, the only non-vanishing two-point functions are the diagonal ones. For the 6D scalars coming from the NS-NS sector polarizations on the
\mathbb{T}^{4}
, (
86a
), we have

\langle\mathcal{V}^{i\bar{i}}_{1}\mathcal{V}^{j\bar{j}}_{2}\rangle\,=\,\langle V_{1}V_{2}\rangle\langle V_{1}^{\prime}V_{2}^{\prime}\rangle\left[\langle e^{-\varphi_{1}}e^{-\varphi_{2}}\rangle\langle\lambda^{i}_{1}\lambda^{j}_{2}\rangle\times\mathrm{c.c.}\right]\,=\,\langle V_{1}V_{2}\rangle\langle V_{1}^{\prime}V_{2}^{\prime}\rangle\times\frac{\delta^{ij}\delta^{\bar{i}\bar{j}}}{|z_{12}|^{4}}\,.

(90)

Since we are dealing with discrete representations, the contact term in (

) vanishes, thus imposing
j_{1}=j_{2}\equiv j
.
As discussed above Eq. (
54
), the conformal weight in the holographic CFT is to be identified with the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) spin, i.e.

. On the other hand, the R-charge is given by
m^{\prime}
, with
|m^{\prime}|\leq j^{\prime}=j-1

, so
\mathcal{V}^{i}
cannot correspond to a chiral primary of the HCFT.

We now turn to the operators introduced in the second and third line of (

\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle\langle\left(\psi_{1}V_{j_{1}}\right)_{j_{1}+\varepsilon_{1},m_{1}}\left(\psi_{2}V_{j_{2}}\right)_{j_{2}+\varepsilon_{2},m_{2}}\rangle=\sum_{r_{1,2}}c^{r_{1}}_{\varepsilon_{1}}c^{r_{2}}_{\varepsilon_{2}}\braket{\psi^{r_{1}}\psi^{r_{2}}}\braket{V_{j_{1},m_{1}-r_{1}}V_{j_{2},m_{2}-r_{2}}}.

(91)

We use the action of the bosonic currents (
46
) to express
\braket{V_{j_{1},m_{1}-r_{1}}V_{j_{2},m_{2}-r_{2}}}
in terms of
\braket{V_{j_{1},m_{1}}V_{j_{2},m_{2}}}
, insert the coefficients (
88
), and perform the sum. Recalling the shorthand
V_{i}\equiv V_{j_{i},m_{i}}
,
V^{\prime}_{i}\equiv V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}_{i},m^{\prime}_{i}}

So far, we have constructed chiral operators whose boundary weights

In the supersymmetric theory, spectrally flowed primary operators are built by combining the bosonic flowed primaries introduced in Eqs. (

In principle, one could simply ignore the possibility of including spectral flow in SU(2) since it does not give any new representations. However, for discrete series states it is useful to do so in order to solve the modified Virasoro condition, as discussed in

We use the spectral flow operator with equal amount of spectral flow in SL(2,

\displaystyle{\cal{V}}\text{-type operators:}~~

\displaystyle\ \ |m|\geq j\ ,\ \quad~~|m^{\prime}|\leq j^{\prime}=j-1\,,

\displaystyle{\cal{W}}\text{-type operators:}~

\displaystyle\ \ |m|\geq j-1\ ,\ ~~|m^{\prime}|\leq j^{\prime}=j-1\,,

\displaystyle{\cal{X}}\text{-type operators:}~~

\displaystyle\ \ |m|\geq j\ ,\ \quad~~|m^{\prime}|\leq j^{\prime}+1=j\,.

(97a)

\displaystyle{\cal{X}}^{\omega}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\varphi}e^{-i\omega H_{1}}\chi^{+}e^{i\omega H_{2}}V^{\omega}_{j,j}V^{\prime\omega}_{j-1,j-1}\,.

\mathbb{R}

from their values for the unflowed vertex operators.

4
Null-gauged description and worldsheet spectrum

We now proceed to describe massless excitations in the worldsheet theories associated with the heavy backgrounds we study. We describe in detail how to obtain the low-lying physical states via BRST quantization in these null-gauged models, both in the NSNS and RR sectors.
In the subset of the backgrounds that preserve some supersymmetry,
we discuss the BPS light excitations.

In the full null-gauged models, these massless vertex operators describe linearized fluctuations around the full asymptotically linear dilaton solutions describing the heavy states, and so can be thought of as worldsheet representatives of light states belonging to the Little String Theory living on the NS5 branes.

Our main interest in this work will be computing correlators in the IR AdS
3
limit, in which we have reviewed the fact that the backgrounds are related to orbifolded AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
via a spacetime spectral flow large coordinate transformation. We describe how the AdS
3
limit can be taken on the worldsheet vertex operators. This leads to states that can be understood holographically, in the spacetime (fractionally) spectrally flowed frame defining the heavy background, as discussed around Eq. (
27
).

Picture changing in the R sector

In order to compute two-point functions of operators
\mathcal{Y}
in the Ramond sector of the gauged model, we need to define their picture-changed versions. Propagators will be non-vanishing only if the total ghost charges add up to
\;\!-Q_{\varphi}=-2\;\!
and
\;\!-Q_{\tilde{\varphi}}=0\>\!
. The picture-changing operators are given by
P_{+1}\sim e^{\varphi}G
and
\tilde{P}_{+1}\sim e^{\tilde{\varphi}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. One possible natural choice would be to compute the two-point function (superscripts denote
(q_{\varphi},q_{\tilde{\varphi}})
charges)

\braket{\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right)}(z)\;\!\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2},+\frac{1}{2}\right)}(w)}\,.

(159)

However, it turns out that looking for an explicit expression for the state
\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right)}
is not the simplest way to go. This is due to the fact that such a state is automatically BRST closed, so that it must be determined by the somewhat cumbersome procedure of removing all the BRST-exact contributions. To avoid this issue, one can distribute the ghost charges in a different way inside the correlator, and consider instead the equivalent two-point function

\braket{\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{3}{2},+\frac{1}{2}\right)}(z)\;\!\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right)}(w)}\,.

(160)

Here
\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{3}{2},+\frac{1}{2}\right)}
is in the canonical
\tilde{\varphi}
-picture, while
\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right)}
is in the canonical
\varphi
-picture. Thus, although this forces us to compute two additional R-sector operators instead of only one, these are constrained by the
\>\!\tilde{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\>\!
and
\>\!\gamma G\>\!
BRST constraints, respectively. The procedure is then similar to that employed above to construct
\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2},+\frac{1}{2}\right)}
. We thus make the Ansätze

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{3}{2},+\frac{1}{2}\right),\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{5}}\,=\,e^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varphi}\right)}\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{5}}S_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}}\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon_{6}\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{5}}V_{j,m-\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}}V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}-\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{2}}e^{i(-E\>\!t+P_{y}\>\!y)}\,,\\
&\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right),\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{5}}\,=\,e^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varphi}\right)}\sum_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}\mathcal{G}^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{5}}S_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}}\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon_{6}\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{5}}V_{j,m-\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}}V^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}-\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{2}}e^{i(-E\>\!t+P_{y}\>\!y)}\,.\end{aligned}

where again

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \oint dz:e^{\tilde{\varphi}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}:\!(z)\>\mathcal{Y}^{\left(-\frac{3}{2},+\frac{1}{2}\right),\varepsilon_{4}\varepsilon_{5}}(w)&\,=\,0\,,\\

R_{y}
, namely
\delta E=-{\mathsf{k}}R_{y}+{\cal{O}}(1/R_{y})
. The interpretation of the factor
{\mathsf{k}}
relating
\omega
and
\omega_{y}
can be traced back, for instance, to the
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
orbifold appearing in the IR, see Eq. (
21
). It reflects the fact that the CFT state associated with the background lives in the
{\mathsf{k}}
-twisted sector of the D1D5 CFT.

The operators discussed in this section do not exhaust the spectrum of the worldsheet model; for instance we have not discussed operators that do not not satisfy
\omega_{y}\equiv 0
mod
{\mathsf{k}}
, which were analyzed in
Martinec:2018nco
.
However, the operators described above
comprise a large set of light operators in spectral flowed sectors, in parallel to the analysis of global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
, which will be general enough for our purposes in the present work.

5
Novel heavy-light correlators from the worldsheet

In this section we describe the computation of two-point correlators in the null gauged models, corresponding to HLLH correlators of the holographic CFT. To do so, we take a set of physical coset operators derived in the previous section and flow them to the IR, in which the geometry is locally an orbifold of AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
.
We develop a proposal to define coset operators in an appropriate
x
-basis corresponding to local operators of the holographic CFT.
We then use this definition to compute a large set of HLLH correlators. We observe precise agreement between a subset of these and known results computed in supergravity and holographic CFT, and significantly extend these results.

5.1
Light states in the AdS
3
regime

We begin by describing in more detail the vertex operators of the null-gauged model in the AdS
3
limit. As discussed around Eq. (
20
), we send
R_{y}\to\infty
, keeping
\tilde{t}=t/R_{y}
and
\tilde{y}=y/R_{y}
fixed. After choosing the gauge
\tau=\sigma=0
, this leads to a geometry described by the six-dimensional metric (
21
), which is related to
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathsf{k}}
-orbifolded AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
by the large coordinate transformation Eq. (
24
).

We focus initially on light states with no winding or worldsheet spectral flow. As we have argued in the previous section, the different polarizations and the associated coefficients simply reduce to those described in Section
3.2
in the AdS
3
limit. Here we further describe what happens to their quantum numbers in the regime of interest. In general, the Virasoro condition (
138
) determines
j
via the solution

j=\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\left(j^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{n_{5}}{4}\left(P_{y}^{2}-E^{2}\right)}\,,

(169)

where we have fixed the sign in order to have
j
in the range (
45
).
As
R_{y}\to\infty
, we hold fixed the rescaled energy and momentum

{\cal{E}}=ER_{y}\;,\quad\qquad n_{y}=P_{y}R_{y}\,.

(170)

Hence, the second term inside the square root in (
169
) is
{\cal{O}}\left(1/R_{y}^{2}\right)
, and at large
R_{y}
the solution becomes
j=j^{\prime}+1+{\cal O}\left(1/R_{y}^{2}\right)
, which to leading order is the usual AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
relation.
The
{\cal{O}}\left(1/R_{y}^{2}\right)
corrections to
j
are non-zero when
|{\cal{E}}|\neq|n_{y}|
, which is generically the case.
To see this, note that at large
R_{y}

the gauging parameters associated to the
t
and
y
directions become

l_{3}=r_{3}=l_{4}=-r_{4}=-{\mathsf{k}}R_{y}+{\cal O}\left(1/R_{y}\right).

(171)

On the other hand, those associated to the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
angular directions do not scale with
R_{y}
, and remain
l_{2}=2s+1
and
r_{2}=2\bar{s}+1
. Hence, at leading order at large
R_{y}
, Eqs. (
139
) take the form

0=m+(2s+1)\,m^{\prime}-\frac{{\mathsf{k}}}{2}\left({\cal{E}}+n_{y}\right)\;,\quad\qquad 0=\bar{m}+(2\bar{s}+1)\,\bar{m}^{\prime}-\frac{{\mathsf{k}}}{2}\left({\cal{E}}-n_{y}\right)\,,

(172)

which fix
{\cal{E}}
and
n_{y}
in terms of
m
,
m^{\prime}
,
\bar{m}
,
\bar{m}^{\prime}
, such that indeed generically
{\cal{E}}\neq\pm n_{y}
.

Although for simplicity we restricted to light states with no winding or worldsheet spectral flow in Eqs. (
169
) and (
172
), the present discussion and the computations in the rest of this section are analogous for winding states after replacing the projections
m\to m_{\omega}
, etc, where
m_{\omega}
was defined above Eq. (
167
). Our results will be valid for the full set of chiral primaries that can be described within the usual AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
worldsheet theory, as well as their descendants under the global part of the chiral algebra, that fill out the short multiplet.
We shall comment further on states with non-trivial winding and/or worldsheet spectral flow in due course.

5.2
Identifying the spacetime modes

Let us discuss the identification of the spacetime modes. We shall work in a gauge in which the upstairs SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) time
\tau
and angular direction
\sigma
are fixed. Then, importantly, the asymptotic boundary of the physical downstairs AdS
3
is parameterized by
t/R_{y}
and
y/R_{y}
, at a fixed point on the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. We therefore define

m_{y}=\frac{1}{2}\left({\cal{E}}+n_{y}\right)\;,\quad\qquad\bar{m}_{y}=\frac{1}{2}\left({\cal{E}}-n_{y}\right)\,,

(173)

and we interpret these as the asymptotic mode labels.
We will see that this gives rise to a rich set of correlators that agree with and extend previous results.

Let us first consider
{\mathsf{k}}=1
, and continue to focus mainly on the holomorphic sector. Given a holographic CFT chiral primary with spacetime weight
h
and definite (left) R-charge
m^{\prime}
, we wish to construct the dual worldsheet operator by summing over the corresponding modes. As reviewed in Section (
3.2
), in global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
this leads to
x
-basis operators of the form
{\cal{O}}_{h,m^{\prime}}(x)=\sum_{m}x^{m-h}{\cal{O}}_{h,m,m^{\prime}}
, where the modes
{\cal{O}}_{h,m,m^{\prime}}
are identified as either
\mathcal{W}_{j,m,j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
,
\mathcal{X}_{j,m,j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
or
\mathcal{Y}_{j,m,j^{\prime},m^{\prime}}
, where
j
and
h
are related by Eq. (
118
).
For simplicity, we collect all of these modes under the notation
\mathcal{V}_{j,m,\bar{m}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}
.
In the null-gauged models, we replace
m\to m_{y}
in the exponent of
x
in the sum, defining the worldsheet operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{h,m^{\prime}}(x,\bar{x})

\displaystyle\,\equiv\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{h+\bar{h}}}\sum_{m_{y},\bar{m}_{y}}x^{m_{y}-h}\bar{x}^{\bar{m}_{y}-\bar{h}}\mathcal{V}_{j,m,\bar{m}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}e^{-im_{y}(\tilde{t}-\tilde{y})}e^{-i\bar{m}_{y}(\tilde{t}+\tilde{y})}\,,

(174)

where we have temporarily included the antiholomorphic dependence to emphasize the coupling between the left- and right-moving sectors due to the null gauge constraints, Eq. (
139
).
The normalization factors of
{\mathsf{k}}
have been introduced for later convenience and will be discussed below Eq. (
208
).
We emphasize that, from the point of view of the worldsheet theory,
x
is an auxiliary complex variable, while
\tilde{t}
and
\tilde{y}
are scalar fields.

Note that combining the bosonic null constraint (
172
) and the definition of the modes (
173
), we obtain
m_{y}=m+(2s+1)m^{\prime}
. This relation parallels the supergravity spectral flow large gauge transformation (
24
), and we will make use of this observation when discussing the relation to the holographic CFT in due course.

For
{\mathsf{k}}>1
we follow the same logic, and make the same definition (
174
). This time however, combining Eqs. (
172
) and (
173
) we obtain

m_{y}=\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}}\left(m+(2s+1)m^{\prime}\right)\,,\qquad\bar{m}_{y}=\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}}\left(\bar{m}+(2\bar{s}+1)\bar{m}^{\prime}\right)\,.

(175)

We shall shortly see that this gives rise to an important technical complication relating the holomorphic and antiholomorphic sectors.

Before computing our first example of a HLLH correlator, let us briefly return to operators with non-zero spectral flow and/or winding charge.
In Section
4.4
we analyzed a set of coset vertex operators in sectors with non-zero worldsheet spectral flow, corresponding to chiral primaries of higher twists, similar to those in global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
.
In that section we worked with
\omega_{y}=0
, and reviewed that the large gauge spectral flow transformation (
168
) relates these to operators with
\omega_{y}\in{\mathsf{k}}\mathbb{Z}
.

When
\omega_{y}\neq 0
, in general one should be careful both when examining whether the states survive the AdS
3
limit, and also when defining the AdS
3
energy
\mathcal{E}
and angular momentum
n_{y}
, since fundamental string
y
-winding charge can be exchanged for background flux
Martinec:2018nco
.
However, for operators which have
\omega_{y}\in{\mathsf{k}}\mathbb{Z}
, the situation is straightforward: we shall simply use gauge spectral flow to always work in a frame in which
\omega_{y}=0
, and then use the definitions of
\mathcal{E}
and
n_{y}
in (
170
) and the modes
m_{y}
,
\bar{m}_{y}
in (
173
).

The discussion becomes more complicated when considering global SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
)
\times
SU(2) descendants of the spectrally flowed primaries. The isomorphism between the affine modules gives a simple identification for the highest/lowest weight states, but this structure becomes more complicated for rest of the of the multiplet. Indeed, global descendants of the spectrally flowed affine primary state are identified with affine/Virasoro descendants of the corresponding unflowed equivalent state with non-trivial
y
-winding, such that one needs to include string oscillator excitations. The situation is similar to what happens for the usual series identification
V_{j,j}^{\omega=0}\sim V_{\frac{k}{2}-j,j-\frac{k}{2}}^{\omega=1}
in bosonic SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
), where, for instance, one has
V_{j,m}^{\omega=0}\sim(j^{+}_{0})^{m-j}V_{j,j}^{\omega=0}\sim(j^{+}_{-1})^{m-j}V_{\frac{k}{2}-j,j-\frac{k}{2}}^{\omega=1}
, see
Martinec:2020gkv
.
We leave a more detailed exploration of these features in the coset models for future work. In the reminder of this paper we will work with operators that have
\omega_{y}=0
.

5.3
HLLH correlators: first example

We now compute a first explicit example of a worldsheet two-point function in the cosets corresponding to the heavy backgrounds under consideration. For this purpose, we focus on a particular light operator probing the backgrounds with
\bar{s}=0
(hence
r_{2}=1
), but general
s
. These describe supersymmetric spectral flowed supertubes
Lunin:2004uu
;
Giusto:2004id
;
Giusto:2004ip
;
Jejjala:2005yu
;
Giusto:2012yz
. We shall demonstrate that this worldsheet correlator agrees with both the supergravity and symmetric product orbifold CFT HLLH four-point functions computed in
Galliani:2016cai
. We will also significantly extend beyond the set of correlators computed in
Galliani:2016cai
.

The light operator in this first example is a massless RR operator of
\mathcal{Y}_{[A]}
type with
h=m^{\prime}=1/2
and hence
j=1
, see Eq. (
110
). We shall denote this operator by
\mathcal{Y}_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Together with the analogous antiholomorphic part, this vertex operator is
dual to a particular
(h,\bar{h})=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
chiral primary of the HCFT denoted by
O^{++}
, which we introduced in Eq. (
31
).
In the six-dimensional supergravity arising from reduction on
\mathbb{T}^{4}
, this corresponds to a
particular combination of fluctuations of a scalar and anti-self-dual two-form potential, in a tensor multiplet that is not turned on in the backgrounds we consider.
In type IIB supergravity, in the present NS5-F1-P duality frame, these fields correspond to a particular combination of supergravity fluctuations of the RR axion and certain components of the RR two-form and four-form potentials.
(In the D1-D5-P frame, the fluctuations are of the RR axion and certain components of the RR four-form and NS-NS two-form potentials).
The light operator corresponds to a particular scalar spherical harmonic of these fields
Galliani:2016cai
;
Giusto:2019qig
;
Rawash:2021pik
.

In the large
R_{y}
limit, we have shown that the
\mathcal{Y}
operators simply reduce to their AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
cousins of Section
3.2
, times the additional exponentials in
t
and
y
. These exponentials give trivial contributions to the two-point functions
\langle\mathcal{Y}_{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{Y}_{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle
once the charge conservation conditions
m_{y,1}=-m_{y,2}
and
\bar{m}_{y,1}=-\bar{m}_{y,2}
are imposed. So do the SU(2) parts of the vertex operators for
m^{\prime}_{1}=-m^{\prime}_{2}
, upon using the appropriate normalization. Hence, at the level of the two-point function of
m
-basis operators, i.e. the mode correlators from the spacetime point of view, the only non-trivial contribution comes from the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) part of the upstairs theory.

To illustrate this more explicitly and also more generally, we introduce the following notation:
\mathcal{O}_{h,m^{\prime}}
will denote a generic massless worldsheet vertex operator in the AdS limit of the null-gauged model with spacetime weight
h
and spacetime R-charge
m^{\prime}
. When
m^{\prime}=h
, we shall suppress the label
m^{\prime}
and write
\mathcal{O}_{h}
. The corresponding holographic CFT chiral primaries will be denoted by
O_{h}
.
Let us then consider the following worldsheet correlator,

\displaystyle\bra{s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{h_{1},m^{\prime}_{1}}(x_{1},z_{1})\mathcal{O}_{h_{2},m^{\prime}_{2}}(x_{2},z_{2})\ket{s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}}

\displaystyle\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2h_{1}+2h_{2}}}\sum_{m_{y,i},\bar{m}_{y,i}}\prod_{i=1,2}x_{i}^{m_{y,i}-h_{i}}\bar{x}_{i}^{\bar{m}_{y,i}-h_{i}}\lim_{R_{y}\to\infty}\langle\mathcal{V}_{1}(z_{1})\mathcal{V}_{2}(z_{2})\rangle\,,

(176)

where
\mathcal{V}
denotes a generic
m
-basis massless vertex operator of the full coset model.
The spacetime correlator corresponds to the worldsheet-integrated version of (
176
).

As discussed below (
123
), the normalization of the vertex operators is chosen so that the overall factors coming from the worldsheet integration procedure cancel out.
After setting
h_{1}=h_{2}=h
,
x_{1}=1
and
x_{2}=x
, computing the free-field correlators,
and imposing the various charge conservations, the integrated correlator becomes

\bra{s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}}\mathcal{O}_{h,m^{\prime}}(1)\mathcal{O}_{h,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(x)\ket{s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}}\,=\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{4h}}\sum_{m_{y},\bar{m}_{y}}x^{m_{y}-h}\bar{x}^{\bar{m}_{y}-h}\lim_{R_{y}\to\infty}\langle\mathcal{V}_{1}\>\!\mathcal{V}_{2}\rangle\,,

(177)

where
\langle\mathcal{V}_{1}\;\!\mathcal{V}_{2}\rangle
stands for the
m
-basis two-point function with the
z
-dependence stripped out, c.f. Eqs. (
92
), (
109
). Note that on the right-hand side, the sum over
m_{y}
involves the R-charge and other quantum numbers of the operator located at
x
.

In turn, the remaining correlator is particularly simple in our context.
As argued around Eq. (
109a
), the
m
-basis two-point functions of worldsheet chiral primary operators reduce to the Gamma functions expression in the bulk term of (
53
) with the replacement
j\mapsto J=h
. These are simply the coefficients obtained by Mellin-transforming the usual propagator
|1-x|^{-4h}
, which further all become equal to one when
h=\frac{1}{2}
.
Thus, for
\mathcal{Y}_{\frac{1}{2}}

in the
\bar{s}=0
backgrounds,
we obtain

\bra{s,{\mathsf{k}}}\mathcal{Y}_{\frac{1}{2}}(1)\mathcal{Y}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\dagger}(x)\ket{s,{\mathsf{k}}}\,=\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2}}\sum_{m_{y},\bar{m}_{y}}x^{m_{y}-\frac{1}{2}}\bar{x}^{\bar{m}_{y}-\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(178)

To be fully explicit, we take the operator at
x_{2}=x
to be a discrete series state
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{j}
corresponding to an anti-chiral primary which, having set
J=h=\bar{h}
, has
m=h+n
,
\bar{m}=h+\bar{n}
and
m^{\prime}=\bar{m}^{\prime}=-h
, where
n,\bar{n}
are non-negative integers. For the supersymmetric backgrounds with

\bar{s}=0
and setting
h=\frac{1}{2}
, the relation (
175
) becomes

m_{y}=\frac{n-s}{{\mathsf{k}}}\,,\qquad\bar{m}_{y}=\frac{\bar{n}}{{\mathsf{k}}}\,.

(179)

Hence, the correlator takes the form

\bra{s,{\mathsf{k}}}\mathcal{Y}_{\frac{1}{2}}(1)\mathcal{Y}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\dagger}(x)\ket{s,{\mathsf{k}}}\,=\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2}}\sum_{n,\bar{n}}\phantom{}^{{}^{\prime}}x^{\frac{n-s}{{\mathsf{k}}}-\frac{1}{2}}\,\bar{x}^{\frac{\bar{n}}{{\mathsf{k}}}-\frac{1}{2}}\,,

(180)

where we have denoted the sum with a prime because the range of summation over
n,\bar{n}
is constrained. We now determine this constraint. Subtracting the two equations in (
179
) and comparing with (
173
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} m_{y}-\bar{m_{y}}\,=\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}}(n-\bar{n}-s)&\,=\,n_{y}\cr\Rightarrow n-\bar{n}&\,=\,{\mathsf{k}}n_{y}+s\,.\end{aligned}

(181)

Thus the allowed values of
n-\bar{n}
are constrained by
n_{y}\in\mathbb{Z}
. To see this in detail, let us first write
s={\mathsf{k}}p+\hat{s}
with
0\leq\hat{s}<{\mathsf{k}}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
. For convenience here and later, we define
a\equiv{\mathsf{k}}-\hat{s}
, so that
s={\mathsf{k}}p-a
, and
1\leq a\leq{\mathsf{k}}
. Then the sum in (
180
) is restricted to be over non-negative integers
n,\bar{n}
satisfying

\bar{n}-n\;\equiv\;a\quad\mathrm{mod}~{\mathsf{k}}.

(182)

We now demonstrate that the correlator (
180
), (
182
) agrees precisely with the supergravity and orbifold CFT expressions derived in
Galliani:2016cai
. In the holographic CFT, we have a HLLH four-point function
\langle O_{H}(x_{3})O_{L}(x_{1})O_{L}^{\dagger}(x_{2})O_{H}^{\dagger}(x_{4})\rangle
. Using Möbius symmetry we set
x_{3}=0
and
x_{4}\to\infty
, in which case the heavy operators are interpreted as in/out states which we similarly denote as

|s,{\mathsf{k}}\rangle
, such that the four-point function becomes a two-point function in the heavy background. We further set
x_{1}=1
, while
x_{2}=x
parametrizes the usual cross-ratio. Then the supergravity result of
(
Galliani:2016cai
, Eq. (4.25))
is

\langle s,{\mathsf{k}}|O_{\frac{1}{2}}(1)O_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\dagger}(x)|s,{\mathsf{k}}\rangle\;=\;\frac{x^{(\hat{s}-s)/{\mathsf{k}}}}{|x||1-x|^{2}}\frac{1-|x|^{2(1-\hat{s}/{\mathsf{k}})}+\bar{x}\left(|x|^{-2\hat{s}/{\mathsf{k}}}-1\right)}{1-|x|^{2/{\mathsf{k}}}}\;,

(183)

where the overall normalization of the supergravity amplitude was not fixed in
Galliani:2016cai
.
Eq (
183
) was further shown to coincide with the corresponding symmetric orbifold CFT calculation for the particular cases
\hat{s}=0
and
\hat{s}={\mathsf{k}}-1
.

To demonstrate agreement between (
180
) and (
183
), it is easier to work from (
183
) towards our expression (
180
).
We start by rewriting (
183
) in terms of sums akin to those involved in the definition of the
x
-basis, i.e. the mode expansion of local operators in spacetime as seen from the worldsheet theory. Recalling that
\hat{s}={\mathsf{k}}-a
, the correlator becomes

\displaystyle\langle s,{\mathsf{k}}|O_{\frac{1}{2}}(1)O_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\dagger}(x)|s,{\mathsf{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{1-p}}{|x||1-x|^{2}}\frac{1-|x|^{2a/{\mathsf{k}}}+\bar{x}\left(|x|^{2a/{\mathsf{k}}-2}-1\right)}{1-|x|^{2/{\mathsf{k}}}}

(184)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{-p}}{|x|}\left[\frac{1}{|1-x|^{2}}\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{1-|x|^{2/{\mathsf{k}}}}-\frac{1}{1-\bar{x}}\frac{1-|x|^{2a/{\mathsf{k}}}}{1-|x|^{2/{\mathsf{k}}}}\right].

Assuming
|x|<1
, the RHS can then be expressed as

\sum_{\hat{\bar{n}}=0}^{\infty}\left[\,\sum_{\hat{n}=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{\delta=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}\,-\,\delta_{\hat{n},0}\,\sum_{\delta=0}^{a-1}\,\right]x^{\hat{n}+\frac{\delta}{{\mathsf{k}}}-p-\frac{1}{2}}\,\bar{x}^{\hat{\bar{n}}+\frac{\delta}{{\mathsf{k}}}-\frac{1}{2}}\;.

(185)

The second term in (
185
) is understood as subtracting the
\hat{n}=0
and
\delta=0,\dots,a-1
coming from the first term. As a consequence, we can further rewrite the sum over
\hat{n},\hat{\bar{n}}
and
\delta
in Eq. (
185
) as a restricted double sum,

\sum_{n,\bar{n}}\phantom{}^{{}^{\prime}}x^{\frac{n-s}{{\mathsf{k}}}-\frac{1}{2}}\,\bar{x}^{\frac{\bar{n}}{{\mathsf{k}}}-\frac{1}{2}}\ ,

(186)

over pairs of non-negative integers
(n,\bar{n})
satisfying the following restrictions

\bar{n}-n\;\equiv\;a\,\mathrm{mod}\,{\mathsf{k}}\;,\quad\qquad n,\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,.

(187)

Indeed, for
a={\mathsf{k}}
we simply parametrize
n={\mathsf{k}}\hat{n}+\delta
and
\bar{n}={\mathsf{k}}\hat{\bar{n}}+\delta
, which enforces the mod
~{\mathsf{k}}
condition (
187
), so that the sum gives the first term in (
185
). On the other hand, for
a<{\mathsf{k}}
we can take
n={\mathsf{k}}\hat{n}+\delta-a
and
\bar{n}={\mathsf{k}}\hat{\bar{n}}+\delta
, so that the factors of
a
coming from
s
and
n
cancel each other out in the exponent. However, in this case we need to explicitly subtract the contribution of all pairs (
\hat{n},\delta
) which lead to
n<0
, thus again giving (
185
).
Therefore we observe agreement between (
180
), (
182
) and (
183
), up to the overall normalization that was not fixed in the supergravity calculation of
Galliani:2016cai
. Here and throughout the paper, we shall not keep track of overall normalization factors.

For
\hat{s}=0
and
\hat{s}={\mathsf{k}}-1
, since Eq. (
183
) matches the corresponding symmetric orbifold CFT correlator, we have demonstrated an explicit match between the worldsheet and symmetric orbifold CFT correlators. This is striking, as it is an agreement across moduli space for a correlator that a priori is not covered by an existing non-renormalization theorem.

This agreement is almost certainly due to the special nature of the heavy states we consider. Indeed, let us compare the worldsheet
x
-basis operator Eq. (
174
) with the discussion of holographic CFT spectral flow in
(
Avery:2009tu
, App. A)
.
Spectral flow in the holographic CFT is an automorphism of the small
(4,4)
superconformal algebra, that is a useful tool to relate different states and operators.
For instance, the heavy backgrounds we consider are related by fractional spectral flow to the
{\mathsf{k}}
-orbifolded NSNS vacuum, as discussed around Eq. (
27
).

Given a symmetric orbifold CFT correlator, one can perform spectral flow on both the operators and the background states. The value of the correlator is invariant under this operation. One can use this to map the correlator in one of our heavy backgrounds to a correlator in the
{\mathsf{k}}
-orbifolded NSNS vacuum. Of course, for
{\mathsf{k}}=1
, after undoing the spectral flows one obtains a vacuum correlator.

Taking for simplicity
{\mathsf{k}}=1
and
\bar{s}=0
, the transformation of a chiral primary operator under this operation is
(
Avery:2009tu
, App. A)

\tilde{{{O}}}_{h}(x)\,=\,x^{(2s+1)m^{\prime}}{O}_{h}(x)\,.

(188)

The exponent of
x
directly parallels the
x
factors appearing in Eq. (
174
).
This observation generalizes straightforwardly to
\bar{s}\neq 0
and to
{\mathsf{k}}>1
, whereupon operators have fractional modes taking values in
\mathbb{Z}/{\mathsf{k}}
. We will comment further on the relation between worldsheet and symmetric product orbifold CFT correlators in due course.

5.4
Non-BPS HLLH correlators for
h=\frac{1}{2}

The correlator presented in the previous subsection can be readily generalized to compute a set of novel HLLH correlators involving the same light operators, but probing the more general class of non-supersymmetric backgrounds given by the JMaRT solutions, in which both spacetime (fractional) spectral flow parameters
s
and
\bar{s}
are non-trivial.
Note that the parameters
s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}

6
More general heavy-light correlators

In this section we present HLLH correlators for generic chiral primaries of conformal weights
h>1/2

As an application, we compute the analogue of the Hawking radiation process from the backgrounds under consideration. We also compute a five-point HLLLH correlator of the symmetric product orbifold CFT, and demonstrate precise agreement with the corresponding worldsheet correlator.

6.1
HLLH correlators for general

We now consider HLLH correlators where the light operators are chiral primaries with

(2,\mathbb{R})

By following the method outlined in the previous sections, the worldsheet computation leads to restricted sums of the form

\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{4h}}\sum_{n,\bar{n}}\phantom{}^{{}^{\prime}}x^{\frac{n-2hs}{{\mathsf{k}}}-h}\bar{x}^{\frac{\bar{n}-2h\bar{s}}{{\mathsf{k}}}-h}\frac{\Gamma(2h+n)\Gamma(2h+\bar{n})}{\Gamma(2h)^{2}\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\bar{n}+1)}\ .

On the other hand, for

Our iterative construction operates by expressing the additional coefficients in the sum in Eq. (

\displaystyle\langle s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}|\mathcal{O}_{h=1}(1)\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}_{h=1}(x,\bar{x})|s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{4}}\sum_{n,\bar{n}}\phantom{}^{{}^{\prime}}x^{\frac{n-2s}{{\mathsf{k}}}-1}\bar{x}^{\frac{\bar{n}-2\bar{s}}{{\mathsf{k}}}-1}(n+1)(\bar{n}+1)

213

{\mathsf{k}}x\partial_{x}\,=\,u\partial_{u}+(2s+1)m^{\prime}+h(1-{\mathsf{k}})-\beta\,.

(214)

In order that this condition is solved by
u^{\mathsf{k}}=x
, we choose

\beta=h(1-{\mathsf{k}})+(2s+1)m^{\prime}\,.

(215)

Thus the role of
\beta
is two-fold. On the one hand, the first term in (
215
) effectively replaces the weight by
h\to h_{u}\equiv{\mathsf{k}}h
, which further supports the discussion above about
u
corresponding to a covering space coordinate in the holographic CFT. It also generates the Jacobian factor obtained in Eq. (
208
). On the other hand, the second term in (
215
) takes into account the shift arising from spacetime spectral flow.

We now use this to obtain an improved construction of gauge-invariant operators directly in the
x
-basis, built upon
u
-basis operators of the upstairs SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
), i.e. without relying on their spacetime Virasoro mode expansion as in (
174
). Although such a construction gives equivalent results at the level of worldsheet two-point functions (
208
), its importance for higher-point functions was highlighted recently in
Dei:2021xgh
. The construction proceeds as follows:

1.

We consider an operator whose upstairs SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) part is expressed in the usual local SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) basis,
\mathcal{V}_{h}(u,\bar{u})
, where for simplicity we set
h=\bar{h}
. We multiply this by an SU(2) vertex operator
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}
. We suppress the exponentials of
t
and
y
, since they have weight zero in the AdS
3
limit, and their only effect is taken into account through (
213
) and its antiholomorphic counterpart. We introduce the notation

\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h,m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}(u,\bar{u})\,\equiv\,\mathcal{V}_{h}(u,\bar{u})\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}\,.

(216)

2.

We introduce the above
\beta
-shift by multiplying by an extra factor
u^{\beta}\bar{u}^{\bar{\beta}}
.

3.

We sum the resulting operator over all insertion points
u
such that
u^{\mathsf{k}}=x
.

Explicitly, we define

\mathcal{O}_{h,m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}(x,\bar{x})\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2h+1}}\sum_{u^{\mathsf{k}}=x}u^{\beta}\bar{u}^{\bar{\beta}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h,m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}(u,\bar{u})\,.

(217)

Comparing with Eq. (
174
), using the Kronecker comb (
200
) to impose the constraints as above, we indeed have

\displaystyle{{\cal{O}}}_{h,m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}(x,\bar{x})

\displaystyle\;\!\equiv\;\!\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{h+\bar{h}}}\sum_{m_{y},\bar{m}_{y}}x^{m_{y}-h}\bar{x}^{\bar{m}_{y}-h}\;\!\mathcal{V}_{j,m,\bar{m}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\;\!=\;\!\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2h}}\sum_{m,\bar{m}}\phantom{}^{{}^{\prime}}x^{\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}}\left[m+(2s+1)m^{\prime}\right]-h}\bar{x}^{\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}}\left[\bar{m}+(2\bar{s}+1)\bar{m}^{\prime}\right]-h}\;\!\mathcal{V}_{j,m,\bar{m}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\;\!=\;\!\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2h+1}}\!\!\>\sum_{u^{\mathsf{k}}\,=\,x}\sum_{m,\bar{m}}u^{m-h+(2s+1)m^{\prime}+h(1-{\mathsf{k}})}\bar{u}^{\bar{m}-h+(2\bar{s}+1)\bar{m}^{\prime}+h(1-{\mathsf{k}})}\;\!\mathcal{V}_{j,m,\bar{m}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\;\!=\;\!\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{2h+1}}\!\!\>\sum_{u^{{\mathsf{k}}}\,=\,x}u^{\beta}\bar{u}^{\bar{\beta}}\;\!\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h,m^{\prime},\bar{m}^{\prime}}(u,\bar{u})\,.

(218)

We note that in the symmetric product orbifold CFT, when mapping the
S_{{\mathsf{k}}}
-invariant untwisted operators
O(x)=\sum_{r=1}^{{\mathsf{k}}}O_{(r)}(x)
to the
{\mathsf{k}}
-fold covering space, using the
inverse relation to (
209
),

O_{(r)}(x)\,=\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}}\sum_{m}O_{\frac{m}{{\mathsf{k}}}}x^{-\frac{m}{{\mathsf{k}}}-h}e^{-\frac{2\pi im}{{\mathsf{k}}}(r-1)},

(219)

one obtains an expression closely analogous to Eq. (
217
).

We now exploit the expression (
218
) to study higher-point functions. We first rewrite the HLLH correlator (
208
) in the simple form

\langle s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}|\mathcal{O}_{h,m^{\prime}}(x_{1},\bar{x}_{1})\mathcal{O}_{h,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(x_{2},\bar{x}_{2})|s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{4h+2}}\sum_{u_{i}^{\mathsf{k}}=x_{i}}\dfrac{u_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\bar{u}_{1}^{\bar{\beta}_{1}}u_{2}^{\beta_{2}}\bar{u}_{2}^{\bar{\beta}_{2}}}{|u_{1}-u_{2}|^{4h}}\,,

(220)

with
\beta_{i}=h_{i}(1-{\mathsf{k}})+(2s+1)m_{i}^{\prime},\,\bar{\beta}_{i}=h_{i}(1-{\mathsf{k}})+(2\bar{s}+1)\bar{m}_{i}^{\prime}
, and where the charge conservation
m_{1}^{\prime}+m_{2}^{\prime}=0
is understood.
We then observe that the worldsheet correlator with
n
light insertions with weights
h_{i}
and charges
m_{i}^{\prime},\bar{m}_{i}^{\prime}
is given by the following straightforward generalization of (
220
) (in which we partially suppress antiholomorphic quantities):

\displaystyle\langle s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}|\mathcal{O}_{h_{1},m^{\prime}_{1}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\dots\mathcal{O}_{h_{n},m^{\prime}_{n}}(x_{n})|s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{H+\bar{H}+n}}\sum_{u_{i}^{\mathsf{k}}=x_{i}}\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}u_{\ell}^{\beta_{\ell}}\bar{u}_{\ell}^{\bar{\beta}_{\ell}}\right)\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h_{1},m^{\prime}_{1}}(u_{1})\dots\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h_{n},m^{\prime}_{n}}(u_{n})\rangle\,,

(221)

where
H=h_{1}+\dots+h_{n}
, and
\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h_{1},m^{\prime}_{1}}(u_{1})\dots\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h_{n},m^{\prime}_{n}}(u_{n})\rangle
stands for the global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
vacuum
n
-point function evaluated at the roots of the original insertion points.

The expression (
6.3
), which holds for generic values of
s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}
and generic light weights and charges
h_{i},m_{i}^{\prime},\bar{m}_{i}
, constitutes one of the main results of this paper. In Eq. (
6.3
), the
n=3
case can be made quite explicit, as we shall do so in the next subsection.

The above result can straightforwardly seen to include spectrally-flowed vertex operators, as follows. Setting
\omega^{\prime}=\bar{\omega}^{\prime}=\omega
for simplicity, the bosonic null-gauge condition Eq. (
167
) in the AdS limit becomes

0=m_{\omega}+(2s+1)m^{\prime}_{\omega}-{\mathsf{k}}m_{y}\;,\quad\qquad 0=\bar{m}_{\omega}+(2\bar{s}+1)\bar{m}^{\prime}_{\omega}-{\mathsf{k}}\bar{m}_{y}\,,

(222)

where, for discrete states in the lowest weight representation,
m_{\omega}=h_{\omega}+n
,
h_{\omega}=J+n_{5}\,\omega/2
and
m^{\prime}_{\omega}=h_{\omega}^{\prime}+n_{5}\,\omega/2-n^{\prime}
.
As a consequence, the exponent
\beta
of the covering space coordinate
u
gets replaced by
\beta\mapsto\beta_{\omega}=h_{\omega}(1-{\mathsf{k}})+(2s+1)m^{\prime}_{\omega}
, and the power of
{\mathsf{k}}
in the normalisation factor is modified accordingly. Thus, for the vertex operators in the coset models, the net effects of the spectral flow procedure are the replacements
h\mapsto h_{\omega},m\mapsto m^{\prime}_{\omega}
. This is understood by the fact that when a boundary light operator has the spacetime dimension
h=J
that renders the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) spin above the unitary bound Eq. (
45
), it corresponds holographically to a spectrally-flowed worldsheet vertex operator
Maldacena:2000hw
. This implies that the structure of the correlator in Eq. (
6.3
) is not drastically modified when
\omega\neq 0
.

It is important to note, however, that the entire computational complication due to worldsheet spectral flow remains present in the resulting
vacuum
correlator. Indeed, we see that the
n
-point function on the heavy state is now written in terms of a vacuum
n
-point function of spectrally-flowed states. It is thus natural to expect that the AdS
3
selection rules carry over to
n
-point functions in the JMaRT microstates. We conclude that the generalisation of Eq. (
6.3
) to the case of worldsheet spectrally-flowed states reads

\displaystyle\langle s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}|\mathcal{O}_{h_{1},m^{\prime}_{1}}^{\omega_{1}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\dots\mathcal{O}_{h_{n},m^{\prime}_{n}}^{\omega_{n}}(x_{n})|s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}\rangle=

(223)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{H_{\omega}+\bar{H}_{\omega}+n}}\sum_{u_{i}^{\mathsf{k}}=x_{i}}\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}u_{\ell}^{\beta_{\omega,\ell}}\bar{u}_{\ell}^{\bar{\beta}_{\omega,\ell}}\right)\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h_{1},m^{\prime}_{1}}^{\omega_{1}}(u_{1})\dots\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{h_{n},m^{\prime}_{n}}^{\omega_{n}}(u_{n})\rangle\,,

where
H_{\omega}=\sum_{i}h_{\omega,i}
and the light operators
\mathcal{O}_{h_{i},m^{\prime}_{i}}^{\omega_{i}}
are
x
-basis spectrally-flowed worldsheet vertex operators.

We emphasize that the construction we have outlined in this section only holds in the IR AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
limit. In the full asymptotically linear dilaton geometry, the identification of the modes
m_{y}
and
\bar{m}_{y}
as defined in (
173
) breaks down, and the
t
and
y
exponentials can no longer be ignored. This is consistent with the fact that in the UV the dual holographic theory is not a CFT, but is instead a little string theory. Since little string theories are non-local, it is correct that the above definition of local operators does not apply. Note, however, that the
mode
correlators computed in the
m
-basis still make perfect sense, and carry information about string perturbation theory in the full geometry.

Let us speculate on which subset of the above correlators can be expected to agree with those of the symmetric product orbifold theory.
Since our expressions for the general correlators (
6.3
), (
223
) involve vacuum correlators, it is natural to conjecture that for these particular heavy backgrounds, the heavy-light correlator is protected whenever the global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
vacuum correlator appearing in (
6.3
), (
223
) is protected.
Recall that, in the global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
vacuum, two-point and three-point correlation functions of chiral primaries are protected
Baggio:2012rr
, while four-point and higher-point functions are generically renormalized.
So heavy-light correlators with two or three light insertions on these backgrounds may be protected between worldsheet and symmetric product orbifold CFT.
It may even be possible to prove a non-renormalization theorem generalizing
Baggio:2012rr
; work in this direction is in progress. For now however, we next compute a heavy-light correlator with three light insertions in both worldsheet and holographic CFT.

6.4
An HLLLH correlator in worldsheet and holographic CFT

We now investigate the general expression for our worldsheet correlator (
6.3
), in a particular example with three light insertions, and compare it to the symmetric product orbifold CFT. We shall observe another highly non-trivial agreement.

We consider three light insertions living in the untwisted sector of the holographic CFT, with weights
(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},1)
. In the dual CFT notation, we are then interested in computing the correlator
\langle O_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1})O_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2})O^{\dagger}_{1}(x_{3})\rangle_{H}
. We further focus on heavy backgrounds with
s={\mathsf{k}}p
with
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\bar{s}=0
.

We start by evaluating the general expression (
6.3
) for this particular worldsheet correlator. In the worldsheet theory associated to the global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
, the
O_{\frac{1}{2}}
correspond to two RR states, while
O_{1}
is an NSNS state polarized on the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
directions. The (integrated) vacuum three-point functions for these chiral primaries were studied in
Dabholkar:2007ey
. In our notation, they take the form

\langle\mathcal{O}_{h_{1}}^{\mathrm{RR}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{h_{2}}^{\mathrm{RR}}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}_{h_{3}}^{\dagger\,\mathrm{NSNS}}(x_{3})\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\frac{\sqrt{(2j_{1}-1)(2j_{2}-1)(2j_{3}-1)^{-1}}}{|x_{12}|^{2(h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{3})}|x_{13}|^{2(h_{1}+h_{3}-h_{2})}|x_{23}|^{2(h_{2}+h_{3}-h_{1})}}\,,

(224)

where
j_{1}=h_{1}+\frac{1}{2}
,
j_{2}=h_{2}+\frac{1}{2}
, and
j_{3}=h_{3}
. The relevant values for us are simply
j_{i}=1
, and upon a global SL(2,
\mathbb{C}
) transformation to set
x_{3}=1
, we have

\langle\mathcal{O}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}_{1}(x_{3})\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\frac{1}{|1-x_{1}|^{2}|1-x_{2}|^{2}}\,.

(225)

In order to compute the HLLLH correlator in the worldsheet coset models corresponding to the JMaRT backgrounds, we must sum (
225
) evaluated at all
{\mathsf{k}}^{\mathrm{th}}
-roots of the insertion points. An explicit expression can be obtained following the arguments of Sec.
5.3
, using the following generalisation of Eq. (
204
) and Eq. (
206
) for the case of three insertions,

\frac{{\mathsf{k}}}{|x_{3}|^{\frac{2(\alpha+\beta)}{{\mathsf{k}}}}}\sum_{r_{1,2}=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}\frac{1}{|1-u_{13,r}|^{2\alpha}|1-u_{23,r}|^{2\beta}}\,=\sum_{r_{1,2,3}=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}\frac{1}{|u_{3,r_{3}}-u_{1,r_{1}}|^{2\alpha}|u_{3,r_{3}}-u_{2,r_{2}}|^{2\beta}}\,,

(226)

where
u_{i,r_{i}}=x_{i}^{1/{\mathsf{k}}}e^{2\pi i\,r_{i}/{\mathsf{k}}}
and
u_{j\ell,r}=(x_{j}/x_{\ell})^{1/{\mathsf{k}}}e^{2\pi i\,r/{\mathsf{k}}}
with
\alpha=\beta=1
, one obtains

\langle\mathcal{O}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}_{1}(x_{3})\rangle_{H}\,=\,\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}^{7}}\frac{(x_{1}x_{2})^{p}\,|x_{1}x_{2}|^{\frac{2}{{\mathsf{k}}}-1}}{|1-x_{1}|^{2}|1-x_{2}|^{2}}\,\frac{1-|x_{1}|^{2}}{1-|x_{1}|^{\frac{2}{{\mathsf{k}}}}}\,\frac{1-|x_{2}|^{2}}{1-|x_{2}|^{\frac{2}{{\mathsf{k}}}}}\,.

(227)

The result (
227
)
constitutes the first computation of a
heavy-light worldsheet correlator with three light insertions probing a black hole microstate.

We now show that the same result can be obtained from the HCFT at the symmetric orbifold point. We follow the method used in
(
Galliani:2016cai
, App. A)
for the HLLH correlator reviewed in Section
5.3
.
The heavy states we use indicate that we should work in the
{\mathsf{k}}
-twisted sector of the theory.
The operators can be written in terms of the fermions introduced in Eq. (
36
). For the
h=\frac{1}{2}
chiral primaries, and for each strand of
{\mathsf{k}}
copies of the theory, this simply reads

O_{\frac{1}{2}}\,=\,\sum_{r=1}^{\mathsf{k}}O_{\frac{1}{2},(r)}\,=\,-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{r=1}^{{\mathsf{k}}}\psi_{(r)}^{+\dot{A}}\tilde{\psi}_{(r)}^{+\dot{B}}\epsilon_{\dot{A}\dot{B}}\,=\,-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\rho=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}\psi_{\rho}^{+\dot{A}}\tilde{\psi}_{\rho}^{+\dot{B}}\epsilon_{\dot{A}\dot{B}}\;,

(228)

while for the
h=1
operator we find

\displaystyle O^{\dagger}_{1}\,=\,\sum_{r=1}^{\mathsf{k}}O_{1,(r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r=1}^{{\mathsf{k}}}\psi_{(r)}^{-\dot{A}}\psi_{(r)}^{-\dot{B}}\tilde{\psi}_{(r)}^{-\dot{C}}\tilde{\psi}_{(r)}^{-\dot{D}}\epsilon_{\dot{A}\dot{B}}\epsilon_{\dot{C}\dot{D}}

(229)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4{\mathsf{k}}}\sum_{\rho_{i}=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}\delta_{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2},\rho_{3}+\rho_{4}}\,\psi_{\rho_{1}}^{-\dot{A}}\psi_{\rho_{2}}^{-\dot{B}}\tilde{\psi}_{\rho_{3}}^{-\dot{C}}\tilde{\psi}_{\rho_{4}}^{-\dot{D}}\epsilon_{\dot{A}\dot{B}}\epsilon_{\dot{C}\dot{D}}.

We will work in the bosonized language, in which

\psi_{\rho}^{+\dot{1}}=ie^{iH_{\rho}}\,,\qquad\psi_{\rho}^{-\dot{2}}=ie^{-iH_{\rho}}\,,\qquad\psi_{\rho}^{+\dot{2}}=e^{iK_{\rho}}\,,\qquad\psi_{\rho}^{-\dot{1}}=e^{-iK_{\rho}}\,,

(230)

Here
H_{\rho}
and
K_{\rho}
are canonically normalized bosonic fields, in terms of which the (unit normalized) heavy states take the form
Galliani:2016cai

|H\rangle=|s={\mathsf{k}}p,{\mathsf{k}}\rangle=\left[\Sigma_{{\mathsf{k}}}\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathsf{k}}\prod_{\rho=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}e^{i\left(p+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho}{{\mathsf{k}}}\right)(H_{\rho}+K_{\rho})}e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho}{{\mathsf{k}}}\right)(\tilde{H}_{\rho}+\tilde{K}_{\rho})}\right]^{\frac{N}{{\mathsf{k}}}}|0\rangle\,,

(231)

where
\Sigma_{{\mathsf{k}}}
and
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathsf{k}}
are the twist operators. Note that the contribution of
\Sigma_{{\mathsf{k}}}
and
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathsf{k}}
to the correlators will simply factorize, since the
\psi_{\rho}
fermions diagonalize the twisted boundary conditions. Choosing the labelling of the insertion points for later convenience, the correlator to be computed is then

\displaystyle\langle O_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1})O_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{2})O^{\dagger}_{1}(x_{3})O_{H}(x_{4})O^{\dagger}_{H}(x_{5})\rangle=\frac{1}{{\mathsf{k}}}\prod_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}\sum_{\rho_{i}=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}\delta_{\rho_{3}+\rho_{4},\rho_{5}+\rho_{6}}

(232)

\displaystyle\Big\langle\left[\psi_{\rho_{1}}^{+\dot{A}_{1}}\tilde{\psi}_{\rho_{1}}^{+\dot{B}_{1}}\epsilon_{\dot{A}_{1}\dot{B}_{1}}\right](x_{1})\left[\psi_{\rho_{2}}^{+\dot{A}_{2}}\tilde{\psi}_{\rho_{2}}^{+\dot{B}_{2}}\epsilon_{\dot{A}_{2}\dot{B}_{2}}\right](x_{2})\left[\psi_{\rho_{3}}^{-\dot{A}_{3}}\psi_{\rho_{4}}^{-\dot{B}_{3}}\tilde{\psi}_{\rho_{5}}^{-\dot{C}_{3}}\tilde{\psi}_{\rho_{6}}^{-\dot{D}_{3}}\epsilon_{\dot{A}_{3}\dot{B}_{3}}\epsilon_{\dot{C}_{3}\dot{D}_{3}}\right](x_{3})

\displaystyle\,e^{i\left(p+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho}{{\mathsf{k}}}\right)(H_{\rho}+K_{\rho})}(x_{4})e^{-i\left(p+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{{\mathsf{k}}}\right)(H_{\rho^{\prime}}+K_{\rho^{\prime}})}(x_{5})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho}{{\mathsf{k}}}\right)(\tilde{H}_{\rho}+\tilde{K}_{\rho})}(x_{4})e^{-i\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{{\mathsf{k}}}\right)(\tilde{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}+\tilde{K}_{\rho^{\prime}})}(x_{5})\Big\rangle.

Clearly, charge conservation implies
\rho=\rho^{\prime}
. For the same reason, the correlator vanishes unless
\rho_{1}=\rho_{3}
and
\rho_{2}=\rho_{4}
, or

\rho_{1}=\rho_{4}
and
\rho_{2}=\rho_{3}
, or both. An analogous statement holds with
\rho_{3},\rho_{4}
replaced by
\rho_{5},\rho_{6}
, hence all contributions trivially satisfy the
\rho_{3}+\rho_{4}=\rho_{5}+\rho_{6}
constraint. Consequently, we only really need to sum over all possible values of, say,
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
, and also compute the product over
\rho
.
In this way, up to an irrelevant numerical factor, the holomorphic free field contractions give

\sum_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}=0}^{{\mathsf{k}}-1}\frac{1}{|x_{45}^{2h_{H}}x_{13}x_{23}|^{2}}\left(\frac{x_{41}x_{35}}{x_{51}x_{34}}\right)^{p+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho_{1}}{{\mathsf{k}}}}\left(\frac{x_{42}x_{35}}{x_{52}x_{34}}\right)^{p+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho_{2}}{{\mathsf{k}}}}\left(\frac{\bar{x}_{41}\bar{x}_{35}}{\bar{x}_{51}\bar{x}_{34}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho_{1}}{{\mathsf{k}}}}\left(\frac{\bar{x}_{42}\bar{x}_{35}}{\bar{x}_{52}\bar{x}_{34}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\rho_{2}}{{\mathsf{k}}}}

(233)

where
h_{H}
is the weight of the heavy state.

We can now take
x_{3}\to 1
,
x_{4}\to 0
and
x_{5}\to\infty
, and perform the sums over
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
explicitly. Upon doing so, we find that the structure of this orbifold CFT correlator Eq. (
233
) precisely matches the worldsheet correlator (
227
).

6.5
Hawking radiation from the worldsheet

As a final application of our results, we now use the HLLH correlator (
208
) to compute the amplitude that describes the analogue of the Hawking radiation process for the JMaRT backgrounds
Chowdhury:2007jx
;
Avery:2009xr
;
Avery:2009tu
;
Chakrabarty:2015foa
. In the bulk, this process is ergoregion radiation, which is a feature of the full asymptotically flat JMaRT solutions
Cardoso:2005gj
.
The ergoregion does not survive the fivebrane decoupling limit
Martinec:2018nco
or AdS
3
decoupling limit, however aspects of the process can still be studied quantitatively in those limits.
This process has been interpreted as an enhanced analogue of Hawking radiation, since both are described by the same microscopic process in the holographic CFT
Chowdhury:2007jx
.
Indeed, acting on a thermal state, this vertex operator gives precisely the spectrum and rate of Hawking radiation of the corresponding black hole, while acting on the states dual to the JMaRT solutions yields their characteristic spectrum and rate of emission
Chowdhury:2007jx
;
Avery:2009xr
;
Avery:2009tu
;
Chakrabarty:2015foa
.

The emission spectrum and rate for general
{\mathsf{k}},s,\bar{s}
was computed in supergravity and symmetric product orbifold CFT in
Chakrabarty:2015foa
, building on the results of
Chowdhury:2007jx
;
Avery:2009xr
;
Avery:2009tu
.
We will reproduce these results from the worldsheet CFT.

We start with a specific HLLH correlator in which the light operators are given by minimally coupled scalars in six dimensions, after reducing on the
\mathbb{T}^{4}
. The corresponding vertex operators were defined in Eq. (
140
). These are not chiral primaries of the boundary theory, but are their superdescendants within the short multiplet, so the holographic correlator arising in the AdS
3
limit is easily computed by using the techniques outlined in the previous sections.
The amplitude of interest involves an initial state consisting of a probe excitation on top of the JMaRT background, a vertex operator
\mathcal{V}
associated to a light insertion, and a final state given by the black hole microstate. Schematically we have

\bra{s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}}\mathcal{O}(x)\ket{s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}+\text{probe}}=\bra{s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}}\mathcal{O}(x)\,\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}(0)\ket{s,\bar{s},{\mathsf{k}}}\,.

(234)

To begin with, we work with
{\mathsf{k}}=1
.
Up to an overall sign, and considering the lowest energy state, the holomorphic part of the amplitude for the Hawking emission of a single quanta of dimension
h=\frac{l}{2}+1
and whose corresponding vertex operator has charge
m^{\prime}=k-\frac{l}{2}
reads
Avery:2009tu

\mathcal{A}_{L}(x)=\frac{1}{x^{(1+\alpha)\frac{l}{2}-\alpha\;\!k+1}}=\frac{1}{x^{\frac{l}{2}+1-\alpha(k-\frac{l}{2})}}\,.

(235)

Here
\frac{l}{2}
denotes the total angular momentum of the probe on the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
part of the geometry, while
k
is the number of
\mathbb{J}_{0}^{+}
operators acting on the state with the lowest projection, appearing in the definition of the vertex operator. To compare their computation with our worldsheet result, one uses the following (notation) map:

\displaystyle k-\frac{l}{2}\mapsto m^{\prime}\;,\quad\qquad\frac{l}{2}+1\mapsto h\;,\quad\qquad\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto l_{2}={\mathsf{m}}+{\mathsf{n}}=2s+1\,.

(236)

Taking care of the cylinder-to-plane conversion factor
x^{-\frac{l}{2}-1}
, one obtains

\mathcal{A}_{L}(x)=\frac{1}{x^{2h-m^{\prime}(2s+1)}}\,.

(237)

We now perform the analogous computation in the worldsheet cosets. From Eq. (
234
), in the worldsheet formalism all we need to do is to insert the second operator at the boundary origin, i.e. to take the
x_{2}\to 0
limit in Eq. (
208
) (with
{\mathsf{k}}=1
for now).

7
Discussion and outlook

In this paper we have computed a large set of worldsheet correlators describing the dynamics of light modes probing a class of highly-excited supergravity backgrounds, the JMaRT solutions, in the fivebrane decoupling limit.
The results are exact in
\alpha^{\prime}
and were obtained by exploiting the solvability of the null-gauged WZW models corresponding to these backgrounds.

These coset models provide a powerful method to calculate HLLH correlators, since the heavy states are already taken into account in the worldsheet CFT itself.
Thus spacetime HLLH correlators are two-point functions on the worldsheet, which can be computed once the vertex operators have been constructed.

We constructed physical vertex operators in both NS and R sectors, and then computed several families of correlators in the full coset models.
We primarily focused on short strings belonging to discrete representations of the affine SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) algebra, as well as a tower of modes generated by worldsheet spectral flow.
Our main techniques can also be employed in more general sectors of the theory.

In the IR AdS
3
limit, due to the non-trivial gauging, the identification of the
x
variable dual to the local coordinate of the holographic CFT requires some care.
Once we made this identification, we computed several non-trivial HLLH correlators explicitly, and analyzed them in the context of AdS
3
/CFT
2
.

Vertex operators that are local on the AdS
3
boundary are constructed by summing over all allowed values of the spacetime modes.
An important step in our analysis consists of identifying these modes.
We chose a gauge in which the IR AdS
3
boundary coordinates are
(t,y)
of the timelike
\mathbb{R}
and spacelike
\mathbb{S}^{1}
directions of the (10+2)-dimensional model before gauging.
We therefore identified the spacetime mode indices with the quantum numbers
m_{y}
and
\bar{m}_{y}
defined in (
173
).
Then the gauge constraints (
139
) satisfied by the physical states imply that the
m_{y}
mode numbers take values in
\mathbb{Z}/{\mathsf{k}}
.
This is how the worldsheet coset models capture the fact that when
{\mathsf{k}}>1
, the heavy background states of the symmetric product orbifold CFT are in the
{\mathsf{k}}
-twisted sector

Giusto:2012yz
;
Chakrabarty:2015foa
.

We observed that, at large
N
, several correlators agree exactly between worldsheet and symmetric product orbifold CFT.
The fact that our correlators are exact in
\alpha^{\prime}
significantly strengthens previous results that compared HLLH correlators between the separate supergravity and symmetric product orbifold CFT regimes.

To demonstrate our method, we presented a detailed example with an
(h,\bar{h})=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
chiral primary.
The worldsheet correlator involves a non-trivial structure in terms of the boundary coordinate
x
, Eq. (
183
).
When the background is BPS, the correlator agrees precisely with the supergravity and symmetric product orbifold CFT correlators computed in
Galliani:2016cai
.
The non-BPS JMaRT backgrounds were not considered in
Galliani:2016cai
, however we demonstrated that the agreement extends also to those backgrounds.

Similarly to correlators on the background of the global AdS
3
vacuum, the holomorphic and antiholomorphic sectors are related through the constraint
m_{y}-\bar{m}_{y}=n_{y}
, where
n_{y}
is the quantized momentum on the
y
circle.
Thus, while the spacetime modes
m_{y}
,
\bar{m}_{y}
are fractional, their difference must be an integer.
This mirrors the
m-\bar{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
condition in the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
)/U(1) cigar coset and in global AdS
3
, which ensures that the wavefunctions are single-valued.
In our models, the difference of the left and right gauge constraints leads to the mod
{\mathsf{k}}
condition in Eq. (
195
), constraining which of the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) modes can contribute.
The HLLH correlator is then obtained by summing over a specific linear combination of
m
-basis worldsheet two-point functions.

Our analysis of these correlators involving the
h=1/2
light operator indicated a way to obtain similar expressions for more general correlators.
We considered general massless vertex operators, which correspond to symmetric product orbifold CFT operators in short multiplets whose top component is a chiral primary of arbitrary weight
h
, including those that live in twisted sectors.
We computed all HLLH correlators where the light operators are massless, and where the heavy states correspond to any of the general family of orbifolded JMaRT configurations, including their BPS limits.
The result assumes a remarkably simple form, presented in Eq. (
208
).
It is built from three distinct factors: (1) the global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}

vacuum
two-point function of the light operators inserted at the
{\mathsf{k}}
-th roots of the original insertion points
x_{i}
, (2) the Jacobian factor associated with the corresponding change of coordinates, and (3) an additional factor coming from the way in which operators of definite R-charge transform under spectral flow. The product of these factors is then summed over all such roots.
This structure reflects that one can formulate the computation in a
{\mathsf{k}}
-fold covering space of the target space.

We then obtained a similar expression for all higher-point functions of the schematic form
\langle H|\mathcal{O}_{1}(x_{1},\bar{x}_{1})\dots\mathcal{O}_{n}(x_{n},\bar{x}_{n})|H\rangle
, with heavy JMaRT states, and
n
massless insertions. This is presented in Eq. (
6.3
). We expect this to be valid for an arbitrary number of massless insertions of weights
h_{i}
and charges
m^{\prime}_{i}
and
\bar{m}^{\prime}_{i}
, and also arbitrary parameters
({\mathsf{k}},s,\bar{s})
for which a consistent background exists. In this way, we have provided a recipe for computing such
(n+2)
-point heavy-light correlators in terms of
n
-point global AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}

vacuum
correlation functions of the corresponding light insertions.

It is known that vacuum two- and three-point functions of chiral primary operators are protected
Baggio:2012rr
. We therefore conjectured that heavy-light correlators in JMaRT heavy states are protected whenever the corresponding vacuum correlator in our general formula (
6.3
) is protected. We have investigated a particular HLLLH five-point function—the first of its kind in the literature—finding that worldsheet and symmetric product results agree. We leave a more general investigation of this proposal to future work.

As an application, we have shown that our results describe the analog of the Hawking radiation process for the general family of non-BPS JMaRT black hole microstates, generalizing the analysis in
Avery:2009tu
;
Avery:2010qw
;
Chakrabarty:2015foa
.

In addition to these main results, our work has clarified some important technical details. For instance, the full asymptotically linear dilaton JMaRT backgrounds do not have AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
isometries.
Correspondingly, in the worldsheet cosets, the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) and SU(2) raising and lowering operators
J^{\pm}
,
K^{\pm}
of the (10+2)-dimensional ungauged model do not commute with the gauging.
Thus the NS sector vertex operators of the cosets do not have well-defined SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) or SU(2) spins, see for instance Eq. (
146
). The same holds for the chirality quantum number
\varepsilon
in the R sector, as discussed around Eq. (
151
).

The absence of the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) spin has important implications also from the holographic point of view. It underlines the fact that the construction of
x
-basis operators is only appropriate in the AdS
3
limit, and breaks down otherwise. The breakdown of the
x
coordinate is a signal of the non-locality of the non-gravitational little string theory that lives on the worldvolume of the NS5 branes, dual to the full asymptotically linear-dilaton models. Thus the states we have constructed contain valuable information about the dual LST and, more generally, about non-AdS holography
Giveon:2017myj
;
Giveon:2017nie
;
Asrat:2017tzd
.
We have nevertheless demonstrated how, in the AdS
3
limit, our vertex operators acquire definite spins and reduce to the appropriate expressions.

Our results suggest several directions for future investigations. First, it would be interesting to compute more general worldsheet correlators, both in the AdS
3
limit and in the full models.
Our correlators are likely to generalize to a larger set of worldsheet vertex operators that correspond to operators in the symmetric product orbifold CFT that transform nicely under spacetime spectral flow
Guo:2022ifr
.
In global AdS
3
, correlators are known to involve a highly non-trivial structure related to the non-conservation of the spectral flow number
Maldacena:2001km
.
More generally, one would like to describe the physics of long/winding strings and their correlators in these backgrounds.
A number of interesting techniques recently developed in
Dei:2021xgh
;
Dei:2021yom
(for the bosonic case) are likely to have interesting implications for computations in the coset theories, for which SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) constitutes a crucial building block.

It would also be interesting to study such correlators by using conformal perturbation theory on top of a putative dual CFT explicitly associated to the NSNS singular point
Seiberg:1999xz
of the moduli space, defined along the lines of
Balthazar:2021xeh
;
Eberhardt:2021vsx
. Doing so would require an understanding of how to define the JMaRT heavy states in such a theory. Separately, it would be interesting to investigate the case
n_{5}=1
, which would require going beyond the RNS formalism, as done in related recent developments
Gaberdiel:2018rqv
;
Eberhardt:2018ouy
;
Gaberdiel:2022oeu
. Here one should go though the coset construction starting with the supergroup PSU(1,1|2).

In the full asymptotically linear dilaton models, more general correlators can be computed by using the vertex operators constructed in Section
4
. However, a shift in perspective will be needed, since the
x
coordinate seems unlikely to be of any use in this regime. Although
a priori
in our case it is more natural to work in the
m
-basis, it seems plausible to relate our results to the momentum-space correlators studied in
Giveon:2017nie
;
Giveon:2017myj
, see also
Giribet:2017imm
. In those papers, the authors work with a related null-gauged model, and further interpret their holographic LST correlators in terms of an irrelevant (single-trace)
T\bar{T}
-deformation of the IR CFT
2
.

Separately, it will be interesting to investigate our proposal for the subset of heavy-light correlators that we expect to be protected by considering the dual computations in the symmetric product orbifold CFT.

Last, but not least, one would like to explore further how these correlation functions encode more detailed information about the physics of the microstate backgrounds we are working with. For instance, two-point functions are expected to probe the multipole ratios of the geometry
Bena:2020see
;
Bianchi:2020bxa
, while certain worldsheet three-point functions should be related to the Penrose process in the JMaRT backgrounds
Bianchi:2019lmi
.

Although the JMaRT backgrounds are atypical microstates,
the HLLH correlators we have computed approach black-hole-like behaviour at large
{\mathsf{k}}
, reflecting the properties of the backgrounds in this limit. We expect that the techniques developed in this work will help further the study of more typical black hole microstates in string theory.

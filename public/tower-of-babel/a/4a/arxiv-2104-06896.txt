Abstract
. In this study, we examine a double nonlinear porous medium equation subject to a novel nonlinearity condition within a bounded domain. First, we introduce the blow-up solution for the problem under consideration for the negative initial energy. By introducing a set of potential wells, we construct invariant sets of solutions for the double nonlinear porous medium equation. For subcritical and critical initial energy scenarios, we derive the global existence and asymptotic behavior of weak solutions, as well as blow-up phenomena occurring within a finite time for the positive solution to the double nonlinear porous medium equation.

Introduction

1.1.
Setting problem

The aim of this paper is to study the global existence of a weak solution and the blow-up phenomena in a finite time frame for a double nonlinear porous medium equation in the context of initial energy states. We are interested in the initial-boundary value problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)-\nabla\cdot(|\nabla u^{m}|^{p-2}\nabla u^{m})=f(u(x,t)),\,\,\,&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(x,t)=0,\,\,\,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,+\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\,\,\,&x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
,
m\geq 1
and
2\leq p<n
.

The nonlinearity
f(u)
satisfies the following assumptions:

(i)

f\in C^{1}
,
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
;

(ii)

f(u)
is convex for
u>0
;

(iii)

For
p<\alpha\leq\gamma<\frac{pn}{n-p}
, we have

u^{m}f(u)\leq\gamma(F(u)-\sigma),

(1.2)

and

\alpha F(u)\leq u^{m}f(u)+\beta u^{pm}+\alpha\sigma,

(1.3)

where
F(u)=m\int_{0}^{u}s^{m-1}f(s)ds
,
\sigma>0
, and
0<\beta\leq\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(\alpha-p)}{p}
with
m\geq 1
and
\lambda_{1,p}
is the first eigenvalue of
p
-Laplacian.

For our convenience, these assumptions on the nonlinearity
f(u)
denoted as
(H)

1.3.
Main objectives of the paper

In this paper, we aim to address two key questions pertaining to the double nonlinear porous medium equation (

For the case of

Question 2:

) for the subcritical initial energy scenario

d:=\inf\{J(u):u^{m}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\},I(u)=0\},

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}||\nabla u^{m}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}[F(u)-\sigma]dx,

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the potential wells method has not yet been employed to examine porous medium equations featuring a nonlinear source term.

We prove several results on the initial-boundary value problem (

•

2.
Blow-up results for

In this section, we discuss the blow-up of the positive solution to the double nonlinear porous medium equation (
1.1
) in the case
J(u_{0})<0

Below we give a main result of this section.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let a function
f
satisfy

\alpha F(u)\leq u^{m}f(u)+\beta u^{pm}+\alpha\sigma,\,\,\,u>0,

(2.1)

where
m\geq 1
and
\sigma>0
,

F(u)=m\int_{0}^{u}s^{m-1}f(s)ds,

(2.2)

and
0<\beta\leq\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(\alpha-p)}{p}
. If
u_{0}^{m}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
satisfies the inequality

J(u_{0})=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}^{m}(x)|^{p}dx-\int_{\Omega}\left(F(u_{0}(x))-\sigma\right)dx<0,

(2.3)

then there cannot exist a positive solution
u
of (
1.1
) existing for all times
T^{*}
and a positive constant
M_{0}
such that

0<T^{*}\leq\frac{M_{0}}{\sigma\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx},

(2.4)

in the sense that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau=+\infty.

(2.5)

We introduce a lemma that will be helpful in our analysis.

Lemma 2.2

(Theorem 1.1,
[
12
]
)
.

There exists
\lambda_{1,p}>0
and
0<w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(|\nabla w(x)|^{p-2}\nabla w(x))+\lambda_{1,p}w(x)=0,\,\,\,&x\in\Omega,\\
w(x)=0,\,\,\,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.6)

where
\lambda_{1,p}
is given by

\lambda_{1,p}:=\inf_{v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}dx}{\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}dx}>0.

Recall that
\lambda_{1,p}
is the first eigenvalue of
p
-Laplace operator and
w
is a corresponding eigenfunction.

Denote

\displaystyle J(u):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx-\int_{\Omega}(F(u(x,t))-\sigma)dx,

and

\displaystyle J(u_{0}):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}_{0}(x)|^{p}dx-\int_{\Omega}(F(u_{0}(x))-\sigma)dx.

We know that

J(u)=J(u_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}J^{\prime}(u)d\tau,

(2.7)

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}J^{\prime}(u)d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{d}{d\tau}|\nabla u^{m}(x,\tau)|^{p}dxd\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{d}{d\tau}(F(u(x,\tau))+\sigma)dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}(x,\tau)|^{p-2}\nabla u^{m}(x,\tau)\cdot\nabla(u^{m}(x,\tau))_{\tau}dxd\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}F_{u}(u(x,\tau))u_{\tau}(x,\tau)dxd\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}[\Delta_{p}u^{m}(x,\tau)+f(u)](u^{m}(x,\tau))_{\tau}dxd\tau

\displaystyle=-m\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau.

This yields

J(u)+m\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau=J(u_{0}).

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let

E_{p}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau+M_{0},\,\,t\geq 0,

(2.8)

with the positive constant
M_{0}
. Then we have

E^{\prime}_{p}(t)=\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}(x,t)dx=(m+1)\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{t}u^{m}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}(x,\tau)d\tau dx+\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}_{0}(x)dx.

By using the condition (
2.1
), Lemma
2.2
and
0<\beta\leq\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(\alpha-p)}{p}
, we estimate the second derivative of
E_{p}(t)
with respect to time

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=(m+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{m}(x,t)u_{t}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=(m+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{m}(x,t)\Delta_{p}u^{m}(x,t)+(m+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{m}(x,t)f(u(x,t))dx

\displaystyle=-(m+1)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+(m+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{m}(x,t)f(u(x,t))dx

\displaystyle\geq-(m+1)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+(m+1)\int_{\Omega}\left[\alpha F(u(x,t))-\beta u^{pm}(x,t)-\alpha\sigma\right]dx

\displaystyle=-\alpha(m+1)\left[\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx-\int_{\Omega}(F(u(x,t))-\sigma)dx\right]

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha(m+1)}{p}-m-1\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx-\beta(m+1)\int_{\Omega}u^{pm}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\geq-\alpha(m+1)J(u)+(m+1)\left[\frac{\lambda_{1,p}(\alpha-p)}{p}\ -\beta\right]\int_{\Omega}u^{pm}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\geq-\alpha(m+1)J(u).

Therefore,
E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)
can be rewritten in the following form

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)\geq-\alpha(m+1)J(u_{0})+\alpha m(m+1)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau,

and

\displaystyle(E^{\prime}_{p}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(m+1)^{2}(1+\delta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}.

Then by taking
\varepsilon=\delta=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha m(m+1)}}{m+1}-1>0
, we discover

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)E_{p}(t)-(1+\varepsilon)(E^{\prime}_{p}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\alpha M_{0}(m+1)J(u_{0})

\displaystyle+\alpha m(m+1)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)u^{m-1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)

\displaystyle-(m+1)^{2}(1+\varepsilon)(1+\delta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)

\displaystyle-(1+\varepsilon)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\alpha M_{0}(m+1)J(u_{0})-(1+\varepsilon)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}.

By assumption
J(u_{0})<0
, thus if we select
M
sufficiently large we have

E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)E_{p}(t)-(1+\varepsilon)(E^{\prime}_{p}(t))^{2}>0.

(2.9)

We can see that the above expression for
t\geq 0
implies

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{E^{\prime}_{p}(t)}{E^{\varepsilon+1}_{p}(t)}\right]>0,

hence

\begin{cases}E^{\prime}_{p}\geq\left[\frac{E^{\prime}_{p}(0)}{E^{\varepsilon+1}_{p}(0)}\right]E^{1+\varepsilon}_{p}(t),\\
E_{p}(0)=M_{0}.\end{cases}

Then for
\varepsilon=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha m(m+1)}}{m+1}-1>0
, we arrive at

E_{p}(t)\geq\left(\frac{1}{M^{\sigma}_{0}}-\frac{\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}_{0}(x)dx}{M^{\varepsilon+1}_{0}}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}.

Then the blow-up time

satisfies

0<T^{*}\leq\frac{M_{0}}{\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{m+1}dx}.

That completes the proof.
∎

3.
Family of Potential Wells

Define

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}||\nabla u^{m}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}[F(u)-\sigma]dx,

where
u^{m}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)

The depth of potential well is defined by

d:=\inf\{J(u):u^{m}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\},I(u)=0\}.

Also the quantity
d

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f(u)
satisfy
(H)
, then we have

(a)

wf^{\prime}(w)-m(p-1)f(w)\geq 0
holds for
w\geq 0,

w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)

(b)

|F(u)-\sigma|\leq A|u|^{\gamma}
for some

(c)

F(u)-\sigma\geq B|u|^{\lambda}
for

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

(a)
is proved by the variational method. We follow the proof of Lemma 2.1 in Payne-Sattinger’s paper
[
20
]
. It is known that a critical point of functional

J(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}|^{p}dx-\int_{\Omega}F(u)dx,

is a solution of Euler-Lagrange equation (

-\Delta_{p}w^{m}=f(w),

where the existence and uniqueness of the solution is presented in Chapter 2 of
[
16
]
. Given the functional
J(u)
exhibits concavity as a consequence of the convex nature of
f(u)
for
u>0
, we can analyze its behavior at non-trivial critical points. In particular, at a non-trivial critical point
w
, the second variation of
J(u)
, denoted as
i^{\prime\prime}(0)
, will not be positive definite. Using the fact
i^{\prime\prime}(0)\leq 0
, we intend to demonstrate that
wf^{\prime}(w)-m(p-1)f(w)\geq 0
. Define

i(\tau)=J(w+\tau v)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(w+\tau v)^{m}|^{p}dx-\int_{\Omega}F(w+\tau v)dx.

Let us compute the second variation of
i(\tau)
. The first derivative yields

\displaystyle i^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=m\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(w+\tau v)^{m}|^{p-2}\nabla(w+\tau v)^{m}\cdot\nabla[(w+\tau v)^{m-1}v]dx

\displaystyle-m\int_{\Omega}(w+\tau v)^{m-1}f(w+\tau v)vdx,

and

\displaystyle i^{\prime\prime}(\tau)=

\displaystyle m^{2}(p-2)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(w+\tau v)^{m}|^{p-4}|\nabla(w+\tau v)^{m}\cdot\nabla[(w+\tau v)^{m-1}v]|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+m^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(w+\tau v)^{m}|^{p-2}|\nabla[(w+\tau v)^{m-1}v]|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+m(m-1)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(w+\tau v)^{m}|^{p-2}\nabla(w+\tau v)^{m}\cdot\nabla[(w+\tau v)^{m-2}v^{2}]dx

\displaystyle-m(m-1)\int_{\Omega}(w+\tau v)^{m-2}v^{2}f(w+\tau v)dx-m\int_{\Omega}(w+\tau v)^{m-1}v^{2}f^{\prime}(w+\tau v)dx.

Then the second variation of
J
at a critical point

is

\displaystyle i^{\prime\prime}(0)=

\displaystyle m^{2}(p-2)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{m}|^{p-4}|\nabla w^{m}\cdot\nabla[w^{m-1}v]|^{2}dx+m^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{m}|^{p-2}|\nabla[w^{m-1}v]|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+m(m-1)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{m}|^{p-2}\nabla w^{m}\cdot\nabla[w^{m-2}v^{2}]dx

\displaystyle-m(m-1)\int_{\Omega}w^{m-2}v^{2}f(w)dx-m\int_{\Omega}w^{m-1}v^{2}f^{\prime}(w)dx.

Let us choose the test function
v
to be

, then we compute

\displaystyle i^{\prime\prime}(0)=

\displaystyle m(mp-1)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{m}|^{p}dx-m(m-1)\int_{\Omega}w^{m}f(w)dx-m\int_{\Omega}w^{m+1}f^{\prime}(w)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\int_{\Omega}w^{m}\left[(mp-1)(-\Delta_{p}w^{m})-(m-1)f(w)-wf^{\prime}(w)\right]dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m\int_{\Omega}w^{m}\left[wf^{\prime}(w)-m(p-1)f(w)\right]dx.

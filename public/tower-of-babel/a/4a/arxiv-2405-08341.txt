On approximation to a real number by algebraic numbers of bounded degree
Anthony Poëls

Abstract
In his seminal 1961 paper, Wirsing studied how well a given transcendental real number ξ \xi can be approximated by algebraic numbers α \alpha of degree at most n n for a given positive integer n n , in terms of the so-called naive height H ⁡ ( α ) H(\alpha) of α \alpha . He showed that the infimum ω n ∗ ​ ( ξ ) \omega^{*}_{n}(\xi) of all ω \omega for which infinitely many such α \alpha have | ξ − α | ≤ H ​ ( α ) − ω − 1 |\xi-\alpha|\leq H(\alpha)^{-\omega-1} is at least ( n + 1 ) / 2 (n+1)/2 . He also asked if we could even have ω n ∗ ​ ( ξ ) ≥ n \omega^{*}_{n}(\xi)\geq n as it is generally expected. Since then, all improvements on Wirsing’s lower bound were of the form n / 2 + 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) n/2+\mathcal{O}(1) until Badziahin and Schleischitz showed in 2021 that ω n ∗ ​ ( ξ ) ≥ a ​ n \omega^{*}_{n}(\xi)\geq an for each n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , with a = 1 / 3 ≃ 0.577 a=1/\sqrt{3}\simeq 0.577 . In this paper, we use a different approach partly inspired by parametric geometry of numbers and show that ω n ∗ ​ ( ξ ) ≥ a ​ n \omega^{*}_{n}(\xi)\geq an for each n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , with a = 1 / ( 2 − log ⁡ 2 ) ≃ 0.765 a=1/(2-\log 2)\simeq 0.765 .

1
Introduction

One of the fundamental questions in Diophantine approximation is the following. Given an irrational real number
\xi
, how well can it be approximated by rational numbers? A simple application of Dirichlet’s box principle ensures that there exist infinitely many rational numbers
p/q
with
q\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\left|\xi-\frac{p}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q^{2}}.

(1.1)

A result which goes back to Khintchine
[
11
]
ensures that the above property is optimal in the following sense. For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the set of real number
\xi
for which there exist infinitely many
p/q
with
|\xi-p/q|\leq 1/q^{2+\varepsilon}
has Lebesgue measure zero. If we think of rational numbers as algebraic numbers of degree one, then it is natural to generalize the previous question in the following way: given a positive integer
n
, how well can
\xi
be approximated by algebraic numbers of degree at most
n
? In
[
12
]
Koksma introduced a classification of real numbers in terms of the behaviour of
the sequence
(\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi))_{n\geq 1}
, where the classical exponent
\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)
is defined as the supremum of the real numbers
\omega^{*}>0
for which the inequalities

\displaystyle 0<|\xi-\alpha|\leq H(\alpha)^{-\omega^{*}-1}

(1.2)

admit infinitely many solutions in algebraic numbers
\alpha
of degree at most
n
. Here,
H(\alpha)
denote the (naive)
height
of
\alpha
, that is the largest absolute value of the coefficients of its irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{Z}
. See
[
15
, Chapter VIII, Section 9]
and
[
5
, Section 2]
for a motivation of the summand
-1
appearing in the exponent in (
1.2
). By a result of Sprindžuk
[
18
]
combined with classical transference inequalities (see
[
15
, Chapter VIII, Section 9]
and
[
5
, Theorem 2.8]
), we have

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)=n

(1.3)

for almost all real numbers
\xi
with respect to Lebesgue measure. Schmidt’s Subspace theorem implies that (
1.3
) also holds if
\xi
is algebraic of degree
\geq n+1
(see
[
15
, Chapter 6, Corollary 1E]
). However, given a specific transcendental real number
\xi
, it is usually extremely difficult to determine
\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)
. We can find in Wirsing’s original 1961 paper
[
21
]
the following famous problem, which is the main motivation for the present work.

Wirsing’s problem.
Do we have
\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)\geq n
for any integer
n\geq 1
and any transcendental real number
\xi
?

So far, and despite a lot of effort, it has been confirmed only for
n=1
(this is a consequence of (
1.1
)) and for
n=2
(by Davenport and Schmidt
[
7
]
, also see
[
8
]
). In his 1961 paper, Wirsing also established the following lower bound

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)\geq\frac{n+1}{2},

(1.4)

valid for each transcendental real number
\xi
. Until very recently, the best lower bounds due to Bernik and Tishchenko
[
2
]
and
[
19
,
20
]
were of the form
n/2+\mathcal{O}(1)
. In 2021, Badziahin and Schleischitz made a important breakthrough
[
1
]
by improving on the factor
1/2
for the first time. For precisely, they showed that for each
n\geq 4
and each transcendental real number
\xi
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)\geq an,\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{where }a=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}=0.577\cdots

Our main result improves the above result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n
be an integer
\geq 2
. For any transcendental real number
\xi
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)\geq an,\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{where }a=\frac{1}{2-\log 2}=0.765\cdots.

Note that our bounds are better than those obtained in
[
20
]
starting with
n=7
. We believe that the constant
a
in Theorem
1.1
is not optimal and could be improved by refining our method.

Given a transcendental real number
\xi
, Wirsing’s approach to showing his lower bound (
1.4
) is to construct coprime polynomials
P
and
Q
of degree at most
n
, which have integer coefficients and have very small absolute values at
\xi
. Considering their resultant, he then proves that a root of
P
or
Q
must be very close to
\xi
. For the proof of Theorem
1.1
, the key-point is to consider simultaneously
n+1
linearly independent polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
, instead of just two. This idea has its origins in
[
13
]
, where we improve the upper bound for the uniform exponent of polynomial approximation.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sections
3
and
4
, we construct the aforementioned polynomials
P_{i}
, which roughly realize the successive minima of a certain symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq n}
(with respect to the lattice of integer polynomials
\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
). We are able to control rather precisely their size and their absolute value at
\xi
. In Section
5
, by evaluating some kind of non-zero generalized resultant, we prove that for each
k=2,\dots,n+1
, at least one of the polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}
has a root very close to
\xi
. Taking into account all these approximations, we then conclude in Section
6
that
\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)
is bounded below by the minimum of an explicit function of
n+1
variables. In the last two Sections
7
and
8
, which are independent from the previous ones, we deal with the optimization problem of finding this minimum. We show that it is at least equal to
n/(2-\log(2))
.

2
Notation

Given a ring
A
(typically
A=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{Z}
) and an integer
n\geq 0
, we denote by
A[X]
the ring of polynomials in
X
with coefficients in
A
, and by
A[X]_{\leq n}\subseteq A[X]
the subgroup of polynomials of degree at most
n
. We say that
P\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
is
primitive
if it is non-zero and the greatest common divisor of its coefficients is
1
. Given
P(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}X^{k}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
, we set

\displaystyle\|P\|=\max_{0\leq k\leq n}|a_{k}|.

For
k=0,\dots,n
, we define

\displaystyle P^{[k]}=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k\,}P}{dX^{k}}\in\mathbb{R}[X].

Then, for each real number
\xi
, we have

\displaystyle P(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}P^{[k]}(\xi)(X-\xi)^{k}.

For short, we say that polynomials of
\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq n}
or
\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
are
linearly independent
to mean that they are linearly independent over
\mathbb{R}
. We identify
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
to
\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq n}
via the isomorphism

(a_{0},\dots,a_{n})\longmapsto a_{0}+a_{1}X+\cdots+a_{n}X^{n}.

Then, the volume
\mathrm{vol}(C)
of a closed set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq n}
is simply the Lebesgue measure of the corresponding set in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
be a transcendental number and
n
a positive integer. The following two classical Diophantine exponents will play an important role in our study. We denote by
\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)
(resp.
\omega_{n}(\xi)
), the supremum of the real numbers
\omega>0
such that the system

\displaystyle\|P\|\leq H\and 0<|P(\xi)|\leq H^{-\omega}

admits a non-zero solution
P\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
for each large enough
H
(resp. for arbitrarily large
H
). Dirichlet’s Theorem implies that

\displaystyle n\leq\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)\leq\omega_{n}(\xi)

(see for example
[
15
, Chapter 2, Theorem 1C]
). The exponent
\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)
, defined as in the introduction, is the supremum of the real numbers
\omega^{*}>0
for which there are infinitely many algebraic numbers
\alpha
of degree at most
n
satisfying

\displaystyle 0<|\xi-\alpha|\leq H(\alpha)^{-\omega^{*}-1}.

Here,
H(\alpha)=\|P_{\alpha}\|
, where
P_{\alpha}
is the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
irreducible over
\mathbb{Z}
(with positive leading coefficient). The reader may consult
[
6
]
for an interesting survey presenting, among others, several transference inequalities between these exponents.

Remark 2.1
.

According to
[
5
, Theorems 2.6 and 3.1]
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)\geq\omega_{n}(\xi)-n+1,

so that if
\omega_{n}(\xi)=\infty
, then
\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)=\infty\geq n
. Consequently, in the following we will often assume that
\omega_{n}(\xi)<\infty
.

By Gelfond’s Lemma (see
e.g.
[
4
, Lemma A.3]
as well as
[
3
]
), for each non-zero
P,Q\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
, if
P
divides
Q
, then

e^{-n}\|P\|<\|Q\|.

(2.1)

In particular, if
\|Q\|\leq e^{-n}\|P\|
, then
P
cannot be a factor of
Q
.

Finally, given two functions
f,g:I\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
on a set
I
, we write
f=\mathcal{O}(g)
or
f\ll g
or
g\gg f
to mean that there is a positive constant
c
such that
f(x)\leq cg(x)
for each
x\in I
. We write
f\asymp g
when both
f\ll g
and
g\ll f
hold.

3
Parametric geometry of numbers

Let
\xi
be a transcendental real number and
n
be an integer
\geq 2
. Schmidt and Summerer’s parametric geometry of numbers
[
16
,
17
]
,
[
14
]
is a powerful tool for studying Diophantine exponents. Although we do not need much of this theory, it provides a convenient framework to state the results we need. In this section we first recall some elementary results from parametric geometry of numbers, then we establish several lemmas which form the basis of our future polynomial constructions.

Following the approach of Roy
[
14
]
(with the maximum norm instead of the Euclidean norm), we consider for any parameter
q\geq 0
the symmetric convex body

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(q)=\Big\{P\in\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq n}\,;\,\|P\|\leq 1\and|P(\xi)|\leq e^{-q}\Big\}.

For
i=1,\dots,n+1
, we define
L_{i}(q)
as the smallest real number
L
such that
e^{L}\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(q)\cap\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
contains at least
i
linearly independent polynomials. Thus,
e^{L_{1}(q)},\dots,e^{L_{n+1}(q)}
are the successive minima of
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(q)
with respect to the lattice
\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
. We group these minima in a map
\mathrm{L}_{\xi}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{\xi}(q)=\big(L_{1}(q),\cdots,L_{n+1}(q)\big).

Recall that the functions
L_{i}
are continuous, piecewise linear with slopes
0
and
1
(they are therefore non-decreasing). Furthermore, since
\mathrm{vol}\big(\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(q)\big)\asymp e^{-q}
, Minkowski’s second theorem implies that

\displaystyle L_{1}(q)+\cdots+L_{n+1}(q)=q+\mathcal{O}(1),\hskip 20.00003ptq\in[0,\infty),

where the implicit constant depends on
n
and
\xi
only. To any non-zero polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
we associate a function
L(P,\cdot)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
by setting

\displaystyle L(P,q)=\max\big\{\log\|P\|,q+\log|P(\xi)|\big\}\hskip 20.00003pt(q\in[0,+\infty)).

Following Roy’s terminology
[
14
, §2.2]
, the
trajectory
of a non-zero polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
is the graph of the function
L(P,\cdot)
. Note that
L(P,\cdot)
is continuous, piecewise linear, constant on
[0,q_{P}]
and increasing with slope
1
on
[q_{P},\infty)
, where the slope change point
q_{P}
is

\displaystyle q_{P}=\log\|P\|-\log|P(\xi)|.

Thus, for each
q\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle L(P,q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\log\|P\|&\textrm{if $q\leq q_{P}$},\\
\\
q+\log|P(\xi)|&\textrm{if $q\geq q_{P}$}.\end{array}\right.

Since, for each
q\geq 0
, the smallest
L\geq 0
such that
P\in e^{L}\mathcal{C}(q)
is precisely
L(P,q)
, we have

\displaystyle L_{1}(q)=\min_{P\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}\setminus\{0\}}L(P,q).

(3.1)

Moreover, since
\xi
is transcendental, we have
\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}L_{1}(q)=\infty
. Although we will not need them, we have the classical formulas (arguing as in
[
16
, Theorem 1.4]
)

\displaystyle\underline{\varphi}=\liminf_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{L_{1}(q)}{q}=\frac{1}{1+\omega_{n}(\xi)}\and\overline{\varphi}=\limsup_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{L_{1}(q)}{q}=\frac{1}{1+\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)}.

The exponents
\underline{\varphi}
and
\overline{\varphi}
are parametric versions of the exponents
\omega_{n}(\xi)
and
\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
\widehat{\omega}<\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)
. There exists
q_{0}=q_{0}(\widehat{\omega})\geq 0
with the following property. Let
q\in[q_{0},\infty)
and
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
be such that
L(Q,\cdot)
has slope
1
on
[q,\infty)
and coincides with
L_{1}
at
q
. Then

\displaystyle|Q(\xi)|\leq e^{-\widehat{\omega}L_{1}(q)}.

Proof.

Choose
q\geq 0
and
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
such that
L(Q,\cdot)
has slope
1
on
[q,\infty)
. This means that
L(Q,q)=q+\log|Q(\xi)|
. We also assume that
L(Q,q)=L_{1}(q)
and set
H=e^{L_{1}(q)}
. By definition of
\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)
, if
q
is large enough, there exists a non-zero
P\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
such that

\displaystyle\|P\|<H=e^{L_{1}(q)}\and|P(\xi)|\leq H^{-\widehat{\omega}}.

Since
L_{1}(q)\leq L(P,q)=\max\{\log\|P\|,q+\log|P(\xi)|\}
, this yields
L(P,q)=q+\log|P(\xi)|
, and

\displaystyle q+\log|Q(\xi)|=L_{1}(q)\leq q+\log|P(\xi)|\leq q-\widehat{\omega}L_{1}(q).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
c>0
which depends on
n
and
\xi
only such that, for any linearly independent polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
, we have

\displaystyle 1\leq c\|P_{1}\|\cdots\|P_{n+1}\|\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{|P_{i}(\xi)|}{\|P_{i}\|}.

Proof.

Since
\det(P_{1},\dots,P_{n+1})
is a non-zero integer, we have

\displaystyle 1\leq\left|\det(P_{1},\dots,P_{n+1})\right|=\left|\det\left(P^{[i-1]}_{j}(0)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n+1}\right|=\left|\det\left(P^{[i-1]}_{j}(\xi)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n+1}\right|.

We conclude by expanding the last determinant and by noting that for
j=1,\dots,n+1
, we have
P^{[0]}_{j}(\xi)=P_{j}(\xi)
and

|P^{[i-1]}_{j}(\xi)|\ll\|P_{j}\|
(
i=2,\dots,n+1
).
∎

The following result is crucial for our approach. Under some condition, it provides
n+1
linearly independent polynomials with integer coefficients which have “good” properties: their absolute values are small at
\xi
and their height are under control. In some way, it is reminiscent of
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
. The idea is to start with a family of polynomials which realize the successive minima of
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(q)
, and then to correct these polynomials to make their absolute values small at
\xi
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
q\in[0,\infty)
and
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
such that
L_{1}(q)=L(Q,q)
. We suppose that
L(Q,\cdot)
has slope
1
on
[q,+\infty)
. Then, there exist linearly independent polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
such that
P_{1}=Q
and

1.

|P_{i}(\xi)|<|P_{1}(\xi)|
and
e^{L_{i}(q)}\leq\|P_{i}\|\leq 2e^{L_{i}(q)}
for
i=2,\dots,n+1
;

2.

\|P_{1}\|\leq\cdots\leq\|P_{n+1}\|
;

3.

|P_{1}(\xi)|\cdot\|P_{2}\|\cdots\|P_{n+1}\|\asymp 1
, with implicit constants depending only on
n
and
\xi
.

Proof.

Let
Q_{1}=Q,Q_{2},\dots,Q_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
be linearly independent polynomials which realize
L_{1}(q),\cdots,L_{n+1}(q)
,
i.e.
such that

L(Q_{i},q)=L_{i}(q)\hskip 20.00003pt(i=1,\dots,n+1).

By hypothesis on
Q=Q_{1}
, we have
q\geq q_{1}
, where
q_{1}=\log\|Q_{1}\|-\log|Q_{1}(\xi)|
is the abscissa where
L(Q_{1},\cdot)
changes slope. We obtain

\displaystyle L_{1}(q)=L(Q_{1},q)=\log\|Q_{1}\|+q-q_{1}\and\log|Q_{1}(\xi)|=L_{1}(q)-q.

Then, Minkowski’s second theorem yields

\displaystyle|Q_{1}(\xi)|e^{L_{2}(q)+\cdots+L_{n+1}(q)}=e^{-q+L_{1}(q)+\cdots+L_{n+1}(q)}\ll 1.

(3.2)

Set
P_{1}=Q_{1}
, and for
i=2,\dots,n+1
set

\displaystyle R_{i}=Q_{i}-\left\lfloor\frac{Q_{i}(\xi)}{P_{1}(\xi)}\right\rfloor P_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}.

We have
|R_{i}(\xi)|<|P_{1}(\xi)|
, as well as

\displaystyle\|R_{i}\|\leq 2\max\left\{\|Q_{i}\|,\frac{|Q_{i}(\xi)|}{|P_{1}(\xi)|}\cdot\|P_{1}\|\right\}=2e^{L(Q_{i},q_{1})}\leq 2e^{L(Q_{i},q)}=2e^{L_{i}(q)}.

Denote by
P_{2},\dots,P_{n+1}
the polynomials
R_{2},\dots,R_{n+1}
reordered by increasing norm. By the above, for
i=2,\dots,n+1
, we have

\displaystyle|P_{i}(\xi)|<|P_{1}(\xi)|\and\|P_{i}\|\leq 2e^{L_{i}(q)}.

(3.3)

On the other hand, since
\log|P_{i}(\xi)|+q<\log|P_{1}(\xi)|+q=L_{1}(q)\leq L(P_{i},q)
(the last inequality coming from the minimality property of (
3.1
)), we must have
L(P_{i},q)=\log\|P_{i}\|
, thus

\displaystyle\log\|P_{1}\|\leq L(P_{1},q)=L_{1}(q)\leq L(P_{i},q)=\log\|P_{i}\|.

So, we have

\displaystyle L(P_{1},q)\leq L(P_{2},q)=\log\|P_{2}\|\leq\cdots\leq\log\|P_{n+1}\|=L(P_{n+1},q).

Since the polynomials
P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
are linearly independent, we deduce that

\displaystyle L_{i}(q)\leq L(P_{i},q)=\log\|P_{i}\|\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{for }i=2,\dots,n+1.

(3.4)

So the conditions
1
and
2
are fulfilled. Finally, Lemma
3.2
together with (
3.3
) and (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle 1\ll\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\|P_{i}\|\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{|P_{i}(\xi)|}{\|P_{i}\|}\ll|P_{1}(\xi)|\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}\|P_{i}\|\ll|P_{1}(\xi)|e^{L_{2}(q)+\dots+L_{n+1}(q)}\ll 1.

Note that (
3.4
) together with (
3.3
) show that
L_{i}(q)\leq L(P_{i},q)\leq L_{i}(q)+\log 2
for
i=1,\dots,n+1
, while
L_{1}(q)=L(P_{1},q)
. Thus, roughly speaking, the polynomials
P_{i}
realize the successive minima of
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}(q)
up to a factor
\leq 2
.

∎

4
Families of polynomials

Let
\xi
be a transcendental real number and
n
be an integer
\geq 2
. In this section, we suppose that

\displaystyle\omega_{n}(\xi)<\infty

(see Remark
2.1
). In this section, we start to relate our polynomial constructions to the exponents
\omega_{n}(\xi)
and
\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)
. Fix a small
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and set

\displaystyle\widehat{\omega}=\widehat{\omega}(\varepsilon)=\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\and\omega=\omega(\varepsilon)=\omega_{n}(\xi)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(4.1)

It follows from the definition of
\omega_{n}(\xi)
and
\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)
that there exists
H_{0}\geq 1
such that for each
H>H_{0}
, the system

\|Q\|\leq H\and|Q(\xi)|\leq H^{-\widehat{\omega}}

(4.2)

has a non-zero solution
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
, and that any such
Q
satisfies

\displaystyle|Q(\xi)|\geq\|Q\|^{-\omega_{n}(\xi)-\varepsilon/2}

(4.3)

(because when
H
goes to infinity, the quantity
|Q(\xi)|
tends to
0
, and thus
\|Q\|
also goes to infinity). Define

\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)=\left\{P\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}\textrm{ irreducible }\,;\,e^{-n}\|P\|\geq H_{0}\and|P(\xi)|\leq\|P\|^{-\omega}\right\}.

Note that any element of
\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)
has norm at least
e^{n}H_{0}>1
. A classical argument of Wirsing
[
21
, Hilfssatz 4]
ensures that the set
\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)
is infinite (see also
[
9
, Section 6]
).
Now, write
\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)
as a disjoint union

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{P}_{0}(\varepsilon)\bigsqcup\mathcal{P}_{1}(\varepsilon),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}(\varepsilon)=\left\{P\in\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)\,;\,\log\big(e^{-n}\|P\|\big)<L_{1}(q_{P})\right\}\and\mathcal{P}_{1}(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)\setminus\mathcal{P}_{0}(\varepsilon),

and
q_{P}=\log\|P\|-\log|P(\xi)|
as in Section
3
. The set
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\varepsilon)
is the set of polynomials
P\in\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)
which almost realize
L_{1}
at
q=q_{P}
, since
L(P,q_{P})<L_{1}(q_{P})+n
. There is however no guarantee that this set is infinite.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let
P\in\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)
. There are linearly independent polynomials
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
satisfying the following properties. Write
H_{i}=\|Q_{i}\|
for
i=1,\dots,n+1
.

1.

The polynomials
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are coprime and
Q_{2}=P
.

2.

We have
H_{1}\leq\dots\leq H_{n+1}
, and there exists
x\geq n
such that
H_{2}\cdots H_{n+1}=H_{2}^{x}
.

3.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\varepsilon)
and
\|P\|
is large enough, then
x\in[\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)-\varepsilon,\omega_{n}(\xi)+\varepsilon]
and

\displaystyle\max\left\{|Q_{1}(\xi)|,\dots,|Q_{n+1}(\xi)|\right\}\ll H_{2}^{-x}.

(4.4)

4.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\varepsilon)
and
\|P\|
is large enough, then
x\in[\omega_{n}(\xi)-\varepsilon,\omega_{n}(\xi)+\varepsilon]
and

\displaystyle\max\left\{|Q_{1}(\xi)|,\dots,|Q_{n+1}(\xi)|\right\}\ll H_{2}^{-x+\varepsilon}.

(4.5)

The implicit constants depend on
n
and
\xi
only.

Proof.

Recall from (
4.1
) that
\widehat{\omega}=\widehat{\omega}(\varepsilon)
and
\omega=\omega(\varepsilon)
. Let
P\in\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)
and let
q\geq 0
be maximal such that
L_{1}(q)=\log(e^{-n}\|P\|)
. The point
q
tends to infinity as
\|P\|
goes to infinity. Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
be such that

\displaystyle L(Q,q)=L_{1}(q).

By maximality of
q
, there exists
\eta>0
such that
L_{1}
has slope
1
on
[q,q+\eta]
. Since
L_{1}\leq L(Q,\cdot)
, the function
L(Q,\cdot)
has slope
1
on
[q,\infty)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\log\|Q\|\leq L(Q,q)=\log|Q(\xi)|+q=L_{1}(q)=\log\big(e^{-n}\|P\|\big).

(4.6)

Thus
\|Q\|\leq e^{-n}\|P\|
, and, by (
2.1
), the irreducible polynomial
P
cannot be a factor of
Q
. They are therefore coprime. Moreover, Lemma
3.1
implies that if
q
(or equivalently
\|P\|
) is large enough, then
Q
is solution of (
4.2
), namely

\|Q\|\leq H\and|Q(\xi)|\leq H^{-\widehat{\omega}},

with
H=e^{-n}\|P\|\geq H_{0}
. Combined with (
4.3
), this gives

\displaystyle\|Q\|^{-\omega_{n}(\xi)-\varepsilon/2}\leq|Q(\xi)|\leq\big(e^{-n}\|P\|\big)^{-\widehat{\omega}}\ll\|P\|^{-\widehat{\omega}}.

(4.7)

On the other hand, there exist
P_{1},\dots,P_{n+1}
in
\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
, with
P_{1}=Q
satisfying assertions
1
–
3
of Lemma
3.3
. In particular, for
i=2,\dots,n+1
, we have

\|P_{i}\|\geq e^{L_{1}(q)}=e^{-n}\|P\|\and|P_{i}(\xi)|<|Q(\xi)|.

For these indices
i
, set
\widetilde{P}_{i}=P_{i}+\lambda_{i}P
with
\lambda_{i}=0
if
\|P_{i}\|>\|P\|
, and
\lambda_{i}=3
otherwise, so that

\|P_{i}\|\asymp\|\widetilde{P}_{i}\|>\|P\|\and|\widetilde{P}_{i}(\xi)|\leq 4\max\left\{|Q(\xi)|,|P(\xi)|\right\},

as well as

\displaystyle|P_{1}(\xi)|\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}\|\widetilde{P}_{i}\|\asymp|P_{1}(\xi)|\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}\|P_{i}\|\asymp 1.

(4.8)

Since the family
P,P_{1},\widetilde{P}_{2},\dots,\widetilde{P}_{n+1}
spans
\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq n}
and since
P
and
Q=P_{1}
are linearly independent, there exists an index
j\in\{2,\dots,n+1\}
such that
P,P_{1},\widetilde{P}_{2},\dots,\widehat{\widetilde{P}_{j}},\dots,\widetilde{P}_{n+1}
are linearly independent (where
\widetilde{P}_{j}
is omitted from the list). We denote by
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n+1}
this family reordered by increasing norm. By construction of the polynomials
\widetilde{P}_{k}
, we have
(Q_{1},Q_{2})=(Q,P)
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

\|Q_{2}\|\cdots\|Q_{n+1}\|=\|Q_{2}\|^{x}.

The inequalities
\|Q_{2}\|\leq\cdots\leq\|Q_{n+1}\|
imply that
x\geq n
. The two first assertions of the lemma are thus satisfied. We also have

\displaystyle\max\left\{|Q_{1}(\xi)|,\dots,|Q_{n+1}(\xi)|\right\}\ll\max\{|Q(\xi)|,|P(\xi)|\}.

(4.9)

Since
\|P\|\leq\|\widetilde{P}_{j}\|
, we deduce from (
4.8
) that

\displaystyle 1\asymp|P_{1}(\xi)|\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}\|\widetilde{P}_{i}\|\geq|Q(\xi)|\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}\|Q_{i}\|=|Q(\xi)|\cdot\|P\|^{x}.

Therefore

\displaystyle|Q(\xi)|\ll\|P\|^{-x}.

(4.10)

According to the first inequality of (
4.7
) (and since
\|Q\|<\|P\|
), we have

\displaystyle\|P\|^{x}\ll\|P\|^{\omega_{n}(\xi)+\varepsilon/2}.

(4.11)

Consequently, as soon as
\|P\|
is large enough, we have
x\in[n,\omega_{n}(\xi)+\varepsilon]
. It remains to prove the last two assertions of our Lemma.

Proof of assertion
3
.

Assume that
P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\varepsilon)
. If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\varepsilon)
, then
L_{1}(q_{P})>\log(e^{-n}\|P\|)
and we find

q<q_{P}=\log H(P)-\log|P(\xi)|.

It follows that

\displaystyle\log\big(e^{-n}\|P\|\big)=L_{1}(q)=\log|Q(\xi)|+q\leq\log|Q(\xi)|+\log\|P\|-\log|P(\xi)|,

which implies
e^{-n}|P(\xi)|\leq|Q(\xi)|
, and so (
4.9
) becomes

\displaystyle\max\left\{|Q_{1}(\xi)|,\dots,|Q_{n+1}(\xi)|\right\}\ll|Q(\xi)|\ll\|P\|^{-x},

hence (
4.4
). On the other hand, Lemma
3.2
applied to the family
(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n+1})
ensures that

\displaystyle 1\ll|Q(\xi)|\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}\|Q_{i}\|=|Q(\xi)|\cdot\|P\|^{x}.

Combined with (
4.10
), this yields
|Q(\xi)|\asymp\|P\|^{-x}
. Then (
4.7
) gives
\|P\|^{\widehat{\omega}}\ll\|P\|^{x}
, and we conclude that
x\geq\widehat{\omega}_{n}(\xi)-\varepsilon
as soon as
\|P\|
is large enough.

Proof of assertion
4
.
Assume that
P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\varepsilon)
. We now have
L_{1}(q_{P})\leq\log(e^{-n}\|P\|)
, and thus
q\geq q_{P}
. Combined with

\displaystyle e^{L(Q,q)}=e^{L_{1}(q)}=e^{-n}H(P)<H(P)

this implies
|Q(\xi)|=e^{L_{1}(q)-q}<H(P)e^{-q_{P}}=|P(\xi)|\leq\|P\|^{-\omega}
, and (
4.9
) becomes

\displaystyle\max\left\{|Q_{1}(\xi)|,\dots,|Q_{n+1}(\xi)|\right\}\ll|P(\xi)|\leq\|P\|^{-\omega}.

(4.12)

Together with (
4.11
) it yields (
4.5
). Finally, Lemma
3.2
yields

\displaystyle 1\ll|P(\xi)|\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}\|Q_{i}\|\leq\|P\|^{x-\omega},

and so
x\geq\omega-\varepsilon/2=\omega_{n}(\xi)-\varepsilon
as soon as
\|P\|
is large enough.
∎

5
Generalized resultants

Let
\xi
be a transcendental real number and
n
be an integer
\geq 2
.
The main result of the section is the following one, which, combined with Lemma
4.1
, will allow us to construct algebraic numbers of degree at most
n
very close to
\xi
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
k
be an integer with
2\leq k\leq n+1
and set
N=2n-k+1\geq n
. Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
be linearly independent polynomials, and write
H_{i}=\|P_{i}\|
for
i=1,\dots,k
. Suppose that
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are coprime, and that

H_{1}\leq\cdots\leq H_{k}\and\max_{1\leq i\leq k}|P_{i}(\xi)|\leq\delta,

for some
\delta>0
. Then, there exists an algebraic number
\alpha
of degree
\leq n
and an index
m\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that

\displaystyle H(\alpha)\ll H_{m}\and|\xi-\alpha|\ll\delta^{2}H_{1}^{n-k+1}H_{2}^{n-k+2}H_{3}\cdots H_{k}H_{m}^{-1},

(5.1)

where the implicit constants depend on
n
and
\xi
only.

To prove this result we will use generalized resultants. Let us recall the results from
[
13
, §6]
. We say that a function
g:\{n,n+1,n+2\cdots\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
concave
if

g(i)-g(i-1)\geq g(i+1)-g(i)

for any
i>n
. Let
N\geq n
be an integer and let
\mathcal{A}\neq\{0\}
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq n}
containing a non-zero element. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{N}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\big\{Q,XQ,\dots,X^{N-\deg(Q)}Q\,;\,Q\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\{0\}\big\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq N},

\displaystyle V_{N}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{B}_{N}(\mathcal{A})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}},

\displaystyle g_{\mathcal{A}}(N)

\displaystyle=\dim V_{N}(\mathcal{A}).

We call
generalized resultant
any determinant of
N+1
elements chosen in
\mathcal{B}_{N}(\mathcal{A})
, for some
\mathcal{A}
as above. According to
[
13
, Lemma 6.3]
, the function
g_{\mathcal{A}}
is (strictly) increasing and concave on
\{n,n+1,\dots\}
. If we assume furthermore that the
\gcd
of the elements of
\mathcal{A}
is
1
(in other words the ideal spanned by
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathbb{R}[X]
), then

\displaystyle V_{2n-1}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq 2n-1}

(5.2)

(it is a direct consequence of
[
13
, Proposition 6.2]
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a linearly independent subset of
\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq n}
of cardinality
j
with
2\leq j\leq n+1
. We also suppose that the
\gcd
of the elements of
\mathcal{A}
is
1
. Then, for
k=0,\dots,n-j+1
, we have

\displaystyle\dim V_{n+k}(\mathcal{A})\geq 2k+j.

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose that there exists
k\in\{0,\dots,n-j+1\}
such that

g_{\mathcal{A}}(n+k)<2k+j.

Since
g_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\geq\textrm{card}(\mathcal{A})=j
, we have
k\geq 1
.

Case 1.
Suppose that
g_{\mathcal{A}}(n+k)\geq g(n+k-1)+2
. By concavity, we have
g_{\mathcal{A}}(i)\geq g_{\mathcal{A}}(i-1)+2
for
i=n+1,\dots,n+k
, and we deduce that

\displaystyle g_{\mathcal{A}}(n+k)\geq 2k+g_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\geq 2k+j

which is a contradiction.

Case 2.
So
g_{\mathcal{A}}(n+k)\leq g(n+k-1)+1
. By concavity (and since
g_{\mathcal{A}}
is increasing), we have
g_{\mathcal{A}}(i+1)=g_{\mathcal{A}}(i)+1
for
i=n+k,\dots,2n
. Combined with (
5.2
), we get

\displaystyle 2n=g_{\mathcal{A}}(2n-1)=g_{\mathcal{A}}(n+k)+2n-1-(n+k)

\displaystyle<n+k+j-1\leq 2n

(the last inequality coming from
k\leq n-j+1
), which is, once again, a contradiction.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain the following useful result.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
k,N
be as in Proposition
5.1
. Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
be linearly independent polynomials such that
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are coprime. Then, for each
j=2,\dots,k
we have

\displaystyle\dim V_{N}(P_{1},\dots,P_{j})\geq 2(n-k+1)+j.

In particular,

\displaystyle V_{N}(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})=\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq N}.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

First, note that there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\{0,\dots,n\}
such that the polynomials

Q_{i}=(X-\lambda_{i})^{n-\deg(P_{i})}P_{i}\hskip 20.00003pt(i=1,2)

are coprime and of degree exactly
n
. By Gel’fond’s Lemma, they also satisfy
\|Q_{i}\|\asymp\|P_{i}\|=H_{i}
and
|Q_{i}(\xi)|\asymp|P_{i}(\xi)|\leq\delta
(
i=1,2
), and the space

F=V_{N}(Q_{1},Q_{2})

spanned by
Q_{1},XQ_{1},\dots,X^{n-k+1}Q_{1},Q_{2},XQ_{2},\dots,X^{n-k+1}Q_{2}
has dimension
2(n-k+2)
. We can choose a subsequence
(Q_{3},\dots,Q_{k})
of
(P_{1},\dots,P_{k})
such that
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{k}
are linearly independent. For each
j=3,\dots,k
there is some
i\in\{1,\dots,j\}
such that
\|Q_{j}\|=H_{i}\leq H_{j}
. According to Corollary
5.3
, for
j=2,\dots,k
, we have

\dim\big(F+V_{N}(Q_{3},\dots,Q_{j})\big)=\dim V_{N}(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{j})\geq\dim F+j-2.

For
j=k
we obtain
V_{N}(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{k})=\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq N}
. By recurrence, for
j=3,\dots,k
, we can choose
R_{j}\in\mathcal{B}_{N}(Q_{3},\dots,Q_{j})
such that

\displaystyle\dim\left(F+\left\langle R_{3},\dots,R_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\right)=\dim F+j-2,

in particular
F+\left\langle R_{3},\dots,R_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}[X]_{\leq N}
. Note that for each
j=3,\dots,k
, there is some index
i\in\{1,\dots,j\}
such that,

\displaystyle\|R_{j}\|=H_{i}\leq H_{j}\and|R_{j}(\xi)|\ll\delta.

(5.3)

Moreover, the roots of
R_{j}
are algebraic numbers of degree at most
n
, since they are either
0
or a root of one of the polynomials
Q_{3},\dots,Q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
. For simplicity, write

\displaystyle(S_{0},\dots,S_{N})=(Q_{1},XQ_{1},\cdots,X^{n-k+1}Q_{1},Q_{2},XQ_{2},\cdots,X^{n-k+1}Q_{2},R_{3},\dots,R_{k}).

The first
n-k+2
polynomials
S_{i}
have norm
\asymp H_{1}
, while the following
n-k+2
ones have norm
\asymp H_{2}
. We control the norms of the last
k-2
polynomials
S_{i}
, which are equal to the polynomials
R_{j}
, by using (
5.3
). The non-zero generalized resultant
\det(S_{0},\dots,S_{N})
satisfies

\displaystyle 1\leq\left|\det(S_{0},\dots,S_{N})\right|=\left|\det\left(S^{[i]}_{j}(0)\right)_{0\leq i,j\leq N}\right|=\left|\det\left(S^{[i]}_{j}(\xi)\right)_{0\leq i,j\leq N}\right|.

For
j=0,\dots,N
, we have

|S^{[0]}_{j}(\xi)|=|S_{j}(\xi)|\ll\delta\and S^{[1]}_{j}(\xi)=S_{j}^{\prime}(\xi).

For
i=2,\dots,N
we will use the crude estimate
|S^{[i]}_{j}(\xi)|\ll\|S_{j}\|
. Expanding the last determinant, we obtain

\displaystyle 1\leq\left|\det\left(S^{[i]}_{j}(\xi)\right)_{0\leq i,j\leq N}\right|

\displaystyle\ll\delta H_{1}^{n-k+1}H_{2}^{n-k+2}H_{3}\cdots H_{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}\frac{|S_{\ell}^{\prime}(\xi)|}{\|S_{\ell}\|}.

(5.4)

Let
\ell\in\{0,\dots,N\}
be such that
|S_{\ell}^{\prime}(\xi)|/\|S_{\ell}\|^{-1}
is maximal, and let
\alpha
be a root of
S_{\ell}
such that
|\xi-\alpha|
is minimal. Recall that
\alpha
is algebraic of degree at most
n
and that there exists
m\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that
\|S_{\ell}\|\asymp H_{m}
. Then, the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
divides
S_{\ell}
, and Gel’fond’s lemma yields
H(\alpha)\ll\|S_{\ell}\|\ll H_{m}
. On the other hand

\displaystyle|\xi-\alpha||S_{\ell}^{\prime}(\xi)|\ll|S_{\ell}(\xi)|\ll\delta

(it is a classical argument, see for example
[
6
, Section 2]
). Multiplying both sides of (
5.4
) by
|\xi-\alpha|
, this yields (
5.1
).
∎

Proposition
5.1
has the following Corollary.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
k
be an integer with
2\leq k\leq n+1
and let
C,y>0
. Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
be linearly independent polynomials and write
H_{i}=\|P_{i}\|
for
i=1,\dots,k
. Assume that

1.

P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are coprime and
H_{2}\geq 2
;

2.

H_{1}\leq\cdots\leq H_{k}
;

3.

|P_{i}(\xi)|\leq CH_{2}^{-y}
for
i=1,\dots,k
.

For
i=1,\dots,k
write
H_{i}=H_{2}^{a_{i}}
, and suppose furthermore that

A_{k}:=2y-2(n+1-k)-a_{2}-\cdots-a_{k}\geq 0.

Then, there exists an algebraic number
\alpha
of degree
\leq n
and a constant
c
which depends on
n
,
\xi
only, such that

\displaystyle|\xi-\alpha|\ll C^{2}\min\left\{\big(cH(\alpha)\big)^{-A_{k}/a_{k}-1},H_{2}^{-A_{k}-1}\right\}.

(5.5)

The implicit constant depends on
n
and
\xi
only.

Remark 5.5
.

Since
A_{k}\geq 0
, equation (
5.5
) implies that
|\xi-\alpha|\ll 1/H_{2}
tends to
0
as
H_{2}
tends to infinity. Consequently
H(\alpha)
tends to infinity as
H_{2}
tends to infinity.

Proof.

Set
\delta=CH_{2}^{-y}
. By Proposition
5.1
, there exists an algebraic number
\alpha
of degree at most
n
and
m\in\{2,\dots,k\}
such that

\displaystyle cH(\alpha)\leq H_{m}\and|\xi-\alpha|\ll\delta^{2}H_{2}^{2n-2k+3}H_{3}\cdots H_{k}H_{m}^{-1}=C^{2}H_{2}^{-A_{k}-a_{m}},

(5.6)

where
c>0
depends on
\xi
and
n
only. Since
a_{m}\geq 1
, we have
|\xi-\alpha|\ll C^{2}H_{2}^{-A_{k}-1}
. Furthermore, using
a_{k}\geq a_{m}
and
A_{k}\geq 0
, Estimates (
5.6
) also yield

\displaystyle|\xi-\alpha|\ll C^{2}H_{m}^{-A_{k}/a_{m}-1}\leq C^{2}H_{m}^{-A_{k}/a_{k}-1}\leq C^{2}(cH(\alpha))^{-A_{k}/a_{k}-1}.

∎

6
A step toward Wirsing’s conjecture

Let
\xi
be a transcendental real number and
n\geq 2
be an integer. In this section, we merge the main results of the preceding two sections to provide a lower bound for
\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)
. This uses the following notation. Given
x\geq n
we define

\mathcal{A}(x)=\Big\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,;\,1=a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n+1}\and a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n+1}=x\Big\}.

For each
\mathbf{a}=(a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})\in\mathcal{A}(x)
and each integer
k
with
2\leq k\leq n+1
, we set

\displaystyle A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=2x-2(n-k+1)-\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}=2(x-n)+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(2-a_{i}),

and

\displaystyle F(x,\mathbf{a})=\max_{2\leq k\leq n+1}\frac{A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{k}}.

Since
\mathbf{a}\mapsto F(x,\mathbf{a})
is continuous on the compact set
\mathcal{A}(x)
, we may also define

F(x)=\min_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}(x)}F(x,\mathbf{a}).

Note that the condition
a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n+1}=x
in the definition of
\mathcal{A}(x)
is equivalent to
x=A_{n+1}(x,\mathbf{a})
. Furthermore, for each
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})\geq 1\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{for }k=2,\dots,n+1,

(6.1)

since
2(n-k+1)+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}\leq(n-k+1)+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n+1}\leq 2x-1
.

Theorem 6.1
.

We have

\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)\geq F_{n}:=\inf_{x\geq n}F(x).

Proof.

If
\omega_{n}(\xi)=\infty
, then
\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)=\infty
(see Remark
2.1
) and we are done. We may therefore suppose that
\omega_{n}(\xi)<\infty
. Fix a small
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
and let
P
be an element of the infinite set
\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)
defined as in Section
4
. According to Lemma
4.1
, if
\|P\|
is large enough, then there exist linearly independent polynomials
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]_{\leq n}
and
x\geq n
such that, writing
H_{i}=\|Q_{i}\|
for
i=1,\dots,n+1
, we have

1.

Q_{1}
et
Q_{2}
are coprime, with
Q_{2}=P
;

2.

H_{1}\leq\dots\leq H_{n+1}
and
H_{2}\cdots H_{n+1}=H_{2}^{x}
;

3.

|Q_{1}(\xi)|,\dots,|Q_{n+1}(\xi)|\ll H_{2}^{-x+\varepsilon}
.

For
i=2,\dots,n+1
, define
a_{i}\geq 1
by
H_{i}=H_{2}^{a_{i}}
. Condition
2
means that the point
\mathbf{a}=(a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})
belongs to

\mathcal{A}(x)
.
Set
y=x-\varepsilon
. By (
6.1
), for each
k\in\{2,\dots,n+1\}
, we have

\displaystyle 2y-2(n+1-k)-a_{2}-\cdots-a_{k}=A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})-2\varepsilon\geq 0.

By Corollary
5.4
applied successively with
k=2,\dots,n+1
,
there exists an algebraic number
\alpha
of degree at most
n
, such that

\displaystyle|\xi-\alpha|\ll H(\alpha)^{-F(x,\mathbf{a})-1+2\varepsilon}\leq H(\alpha)^{-F(x)-1+2\varepsilon}\leq H(\alpha)^{-F_{n}-1+2\varepsilon}.

Recall that
H(\alpha)
tends to infinity with
\|P\|
, because we have
|\xi-\alpha|\ll H_{2}^{-1}=\|P\|^{-1}
in view of the last estimate of Corollary
5.4
. Since Since
\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon)
is infinite, we deduce that

\displaystyle\omega_{n}^{*}(\xi)\geq F_{n}-2\varepsilon,

and we get the result by letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0
.
∎

7
A minimization problem

Let the notation be as in Section
6
. Theorem
6.1
calls for a lower bound estimate for
F_{n}
. We first prove that there exists a point
(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
x\geq n
and
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
satisfying
F(x,\mathbf{a})=F_{n}
. Then, we give a complete description of
\mathbf{a}
as a function of
F_{n}
and
x
and some integer
\ell
with
2\leq\ell\leq n
. In the final Section
8
, we use these properties to give an explicit lower bound for
F_{n}
and deduce Theorem
1.1
. Our approach is inspired by the remarkable strategy described by de La Vallée-Poussin in
[
10
, Chapter VI]
to construct polynomials of best approximation to a continuous real valued function on a closed interval on
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 7.1
.

There exists a point
(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, with
\mathbf{a}=(a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})
, such that

\displaystyle x\geq n,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}(x)\and F_{n}=F(x,\mathbf{a}).

(7.1)

Any such point has the following properties.

1.

There exists
\ell\in\{2,\dots,n\}
such that
F_{n}=2(x-n)+\ell-1
and

\displaystyle x=(2-\theta)F_{n},\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{where }\theta=\left(\frac{F_{n}}{F_{n}+1}\right)^{n+1-\ell}.

2.

The point
\mathbf{a}=(a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})
is given by
a_{2}=\cdots=a_{\ell}=1
, and

\displaystyle a_{k}=2-\left(\frac{F_{n}}{F_{n}+1}\right)^{k-\ell},\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{for }k=\ell,\dots,n+1.

3.

We have

\displaystyle F_{n}=\frac{A_{\ell+1}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{\ell+1}}=\cdots=\frac{A_{n+1}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{n+1}}.

Theorem
7.1
implies that there are at most
n-1
points satisfying (
7.1
) (for such a point is entirely determined by the integer
\ell
). Note that the first part of
1
combined with
a_{\ell}=1
ensures that the formula in
3
is also valid for the index
\ell
. In order to prove the above theorem, we first prove that the infimum
F_{n}
is actually a minimum.

Lemma 7.1
.

We have
F_{n}<n
, and the set
\mathcal{M}_{n}
of points
(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying (
7.1
) is non-empty.
Furthermore, any
(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
has
n<x<(3n-1)/2
.

Proof.

For a fixed
\varepsilon\in[0,1/2)
, the point
\mathbf{a}=(1,\dots,1,1+\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}(x)
with
x=n+\varepsilon
. It follows from the definition that
A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})=2\varepsilon+k-1<n
for
k=2,\dots,n
and

\displaystyle A_{n+1}(x,\mathbf{a})=\frac{n+\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\leq n,

with equality if and only if
\varepsilon=0
. Taking
0<\varepsilon<1/2
, we deduce that
F_{n}\leq F(x,\mathbf{a})<n
. Note that for
x=n
, the set

\mathcal{A}(n)
reduces to
\{(1,\dots,1)\}
and
F(n)=n
. On the other hand, for any
x\geq n
, each
\mathbf{a}=(a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})\in\mathcal{A}(x)
has
a_{2}=1
, thus

\displaystyle F(x,\mathbf{a})\geq\frac{A_{2}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{2}}=2(x-n)+1.

If follows that
F(x)\geq 2(x-n)+1
. Consequently, if
x\geq(3n-1)/2
, then
F(x)\geq n>F_{n}
. Consider the compact subset
\mathcal{K}_{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{n}=\left\{(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\;|\;x\in\left[n,\frac{3n-1}{2}\right]\and\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}(x)\right\}.

By the above, we have
F_{n}=\inf_{(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}F(x,\mathbf{a})
. Since the function
F
is continuous on the compact set
\mathcal{K}_{n}
, this infimum is actually a minimum. Furthermore, since
F_{n}<n
, any point
(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
realizing this minimum satisfies
n<x<(3n-1)/2
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be as in Lemma
7.1
, let
(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
and
write
\mathbf{a}=(a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})
. There exists an integer
\ell\in\{2,\dots,n\}
such that

1.

1=a_{2}=\cdots=a_{\ell}<a_{\ell+1}<\cdots<a_{n+1}<2
;

2.

A_{\ell+1}(x,\mathbf{a})/a_{\ell+1}=\cdots=A_{n+1}(x,\mathbf{a})/a_{n+1}=F_{n}
;

3.

2(x-n)+\ell-1\leq F_{n}<2(x-n)+\ell
.

Proof.

Step
1
.
Suppose that
a_{j}<a_{j+1}
for an integer
j
with
2\leq j\leq n
. We claim that

\displaystyle\frac{A_{j+1}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{j+1}}=F_{n}.

(7.2)

Indeed, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,a_{j+1}-a_{j}]
, the point

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}=(b_{2},\dots,b_{n+1})=(a_{2},\dots,a_{j},a_{j+1}-\varepsilon,a_{j+2},\dots,a_{n+1})

belongs to
\mathcal{A}(y)
, where
y=x-\varepsilon
. Since
b_{2}=1
, we have
y=b_{2}+\cdots+b_{n+1}\geq n
. By definition of the functions
A_{k}

we have

\displaystyle A_{k}(y,\mathbf{b})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})-2\varepsilon&\textrm{for }k=2,\dots,j,\\
A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})-\varepsilon&\textrm{for }k=j+1,\dots,n+1.\end{array}\right.

So, for each
k\neq j+1
, we find

\displaystyle\frac{A_{k}(y,\mathbf{b})}{b_{k}}<\frac{A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{k}}\leq F(x,\mathbf{a})=F_{n}.

However, by minimality of
F_{n}
, we have
F(y,\mathbf{b})\geq F_{n}
, thus

\displaystyle F(y,\mathbf{b})=\frac{A_{j+1}(y,\mathbf{b})}{b_{j+1}}=\frac{A_{j+1}(x,\mathbf{a})-\varepsilon}{a_{j+1}-\varepsilon}\geq F_{n}.

Letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0
, we obtain
F_{n}\leq A_{j+1}(x,\mathbf{a})/a_{j+1}\leq F(x,\mathbf{a})
, hence our claim.

Step
2
.
Suppose that
a_{n+1}\geq 2
. Since
a_{2}=1<2
, there exists an integer
j
with
2\leq j\leq n
such that
a_{j}<2\leq a_{j+1}
. Using Step
1
, we get

\displaystyle F_{n}=F(x,\mathbf{a})\geq\frac{A_{j}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{j}}=\frac{A_{j+1}(x,\mathbf{a})+a_{j+1}-2}{a_{j}}>\frac{A_{j+1}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{j+1}}=F_{n},

a contradiction. Hence
1=a_{2}\leq a_{3}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n+1}<2
.

Step
3
.
Let
\ell
be the largest integer in
\{2,\dots,n+1\}
such that
a_{\ell}=1
. As
1=a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n+1}
, we have
a_{2}=\cdots=a_{\ell}=1
. If
\ell=n+1
, then
x=n
, which contradicts Lemma
7.1
, so
\ell\leq n
. By contradiction, suppose that assertion
1
is false. Then
\ell<n
and by Step
2
there exists an integer
j
with
\ell\leq j\leq n-1
such that
a_{j}<a_{j+1}=a_{j+2}<2
. Using Step
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{n}=F(x,\mathbf{a})\geq\frac{A_{j+2}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{j+2}}=\frac{A_{j+1}(x,\mathbf{a})+2-a_{j+2}}{a_{j+1}}>\frac{A_{j+1}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{j+1}}=F_{n},

a contradiction. Thus
1
holds, and by Step
1
, it yields
2
. Finally,
assertion
3
follows from
a_{\ell+1}>1
and

\displaystyle 2(x-n)+\ell-1=\frac{A_{\ell}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{\ell}}\leq F_{n}=\frac{A_{\ell+1}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{\ell+1}}<A_{\ell+1}(x,\mathbf{a})<2(x-n)+\ell.

∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
x\in[n,(3n-1)/2]
, let
\ell\in\{2,\dots,n\}
, let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
a_{2}=\cdots=a_{\ell}=1
and let
y,F\in\mathbb{R}
with

y=2(x-n)+\ell-1\leq F<y+1.

The following assertions are equivalent

1.

For
k=\ell+1,\dots,n+1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})}{a_{k}}=F.

(7.3)

2.

For
k=\ell+1,\dots,n+1
, we have

\displaystyle a_{k}=2-\frac{2F-y}{F+1}\left(\frac{F}{F+1}\right)^{k-\ell-1}.

(7.4)

If they hold, then
1<a_{\ell+1}<\cdots<a_{n+1}<2
.

Proof.

1

\Leftrightarrow

2
. As
A_{\ell+1}=y+2-a_{\ell+1}
, we first observe that (
7.3
)
holds for
k=\ell+1
if and only if
a_{\ell+1}=(y+2)/(F+1)
. Suppose that (
7.3
) holds for an index
k
with
\ell+1\leq k\leq n
. Then, since
A_{k+1}(x,\mathbf{a})=A_{k}(x,\mathbf{a})+2-a_{k+1}
, the equality (
7.3
) holds for
k+1
if and only if

\displaystyle a_{k+1}=\frac{F}{F+1}a_{k}+\frac{2}{F+1}.

(7.5)

By the above remark,
1
holds if and only if
a_{\ell+1}=(y+2)/(F+1)
and (
7.5
) is satisfied for
k=\ell+1,\dots,n
. This is precisely the arithmetico-geometric sequence of
2
.

Finally, the hypothesis
y\leq F<y+1
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{F}{F+1}\leq\frac{2F-y}{F+1}<1,

so that if (
7.4
) holds, then

a_{2}=\cdots=a_{\ell}<a_{\ell+1}<\cdots<a_{n+1}
.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

With the notation and hypotheses of Lemma
7.3
set

\displaystyle\theta=\left(\frac{F}{F+1}\right)^{n+1-\ell},

and suppose that
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies the two equivalent conditions
1
and
2

of Lemma
7.3
, as well as
1=a_{2}=\cdots=a_{\ell}
. Then,
(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathcal{A}(x)
if and only if

\displaystyle(1-2\theta)x=2F(1-\theta)-\theta(2n-\ell+1).

(7.6)

Moreover, if (
7.6
) holds, then
\theta\neq 1/2
.

Proof.

Since
y=2(x-n)+\ell-1
, Equation (
7.6
) is equivalent to
x=2F-(2F-y)\theta
.
Recall that
a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n+1}=x
if and only if
x=A_{n+1}(\mathbf{a},x)
. Since the coordinates of
\mathbf{a}
are increasing, the point
(x,\mathbf{a})
belongs to
\mathcal{A}(x)
if, and only if,

\displaystyle x=A_{n+1}(x,\mathbf{a})\mathop{=}_{\eqref{eq Lem : second prop du point type point-min : eq 1}}a_{n+1}F\mathop{=}_{\eqref{eq Lem : second prop du point type point-min : eq 3}}2F-\frac{F(2F-y)}{F+1}\left(\frac{F}{F+1}\right)^{n-\ell}=2F-(2F-y)\theta.

It remains to prove the last part of the Lemma. By contradiction, suppose now that (
7.6
) holds with
\theta=1/2
. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle F=\frac{2n-\ell+1}{2}\in\mathbb{Q}\and\frac{1}{2}=\theta=\left(\frac{F}{F+1}\right)^{n+1-\ell}.

This implies that the exponent
n+1-\ell
is equal to
1
, thus
\ell=n
and
F=(n+1)/2=1
, which is impossible since
n\geq 2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

By Lemma
7.1
there exists
(x,\mathbf{a})=(x,a_{2},\dots,a_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying (
7.1
), and any such point has
n<x<(3n-1)/2
. Fix such a point. Then Lemma
7.2
provides an integer
\ell\in\{2,\dots,n\}
for which
a_{2}=\cdots=a_{\ell}=1
,

\displaystyle y:=2(x-n)+\ell-1\leq F_{n}<y+1,

(7.7)

and assertion
3
of Theorem
7.1
holds. It only remains to prove that
F_{n}=y
and that
x=(2-\theta)F_{n}
, for then Lemma
7.3
implies assertion
2
of the theorem. According to Lemma
7.4
, we have

\displaystyle(1-2\theta)x=2F_{n}(1-\theta)-\theta(2n-\ell+1),\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{where }\theta=\left(\frac{F_{n}}{F_{n}+1}\right)^{n+1-\ell}\neq\frac{1}{2}.

Fix
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
and set
F^{\prime}=F_{n}-\varepsilon
. If
\varepsilon
is small enough, then

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}:=\left(\frac{F^{\prime}}{F^{\prime}+1}\right)^{n+1-\ell}\neq\frac{1}{2},

and there exists
x^{\prime}=x^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(x^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},F^{\prime})
satisfy (
7.6
). By contradiction, suppose that
y<F_{n}<y+1
. We note that for
\varepsilon=0
, we have
(x^{\prime},F^{\prime})=(x,F_{n})
. So, if
\varepsilon
is small enough, we also have
n<x^{\prime}<(3n-1)/2
and
y^{\prime}<F^{\prime}<y^{\prime}+1
, where

\displaystyle y^{\prime}=y^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=2(x^{\prime}-n)+\ell-1.

Set
a_{2}^{\prime}=\cdots=a_{\ell}^{\prime}=1
and define
a_{k}^{\prime}
by (
7.4
) (with
F=F^{\prime}
) for
k=\ell+1,\dots,n+1
. We denote by
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
the point
(a_{2}^{\prime},\dots,a_{n+1}^{\prime})
. Then
x^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
,
\ell
,
y^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}
satisfy the hypotheses of Lemmas
7.3
and
7.4
.
According to Lemma
7.4
, and since
x^{\prime}
,
F^{\prime}
satisfy (
7.6
), we have
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(x^{\prime})
. Moreover,
A_{k}(x^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime})/a_{k}^{\prime}=2(x^{\prime}-n)+k-1\leq y^{\prime}
for
k=2,\dots,\ell
, and our choice of
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{A_{k}(x^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime})}{a_{k}^{\prime}}=F^{\prime}\geq y^{\prime}\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{for }k=\ell+1,\dots,n+1.

Thus
F(x^{\prime},\mathbf{a}^{\prime})=F^{\prime}<F_{n}
, a contradiction. It follows that
F_{n}=y
, as expected, hence assertion
2
of Theorem
7.1
holds. In particular, the last coordinate of
\mathbf{a}
multiplied by
F_{n}
is equal to
(2-\theta)F_{n}
by assertion
2
, and is also equal to
A_{n+1}(x,\mathbf{a})=x
by assertion
3
. Hence the identity
(2-\theta)F_{n}=x
.
∎

8
Proof of the main result

Let
n
be an integer
\geq 2
. We keep the notation of Section
6
for the function
F
and its minimum
F_{n}
. We now have all the tools we need to give an explicit lower bound for
F_{n}
. Together with Theorem
6.1
, the next estimate implies Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 8.1
.

We have
F_{n}\geq n/(2-\log 2)
.

Proof.

Fix
(x,\mathbf{a})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying the condition (
7.1
) of Theorem
7.1
, and let
\ell\in\{2,\dots,n\}
such that

\displaystyle F_{n}=2(x-n)+\ell-1.

(8.1)

Set
\theta=\big(F_{n}/(F_{n}+1)\big)^{n+1-\ell}
. The formula
x=(2-\theta)F_{n}
combined with (
8.1
)
leads to

\displaystyle(3-2\theta)F_{n}=2n+1-\ell.

(8.2)

Since
t\log(1+1/t)\leq 1
for each
t>0
, we find

\displaystyle F_{n}\log\theta=-(n+1-\ell)F_{n}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{F_{n}}\right)\geq-(n+1-\ell).

Together with (
8.2
), this yields
(3-2\theta+\log\theta)F_{n}\geq n
. Finally, the function
t\mapsto 3-2t+\log t
has a global maximum on
(0,\infty)
at
t=1/2
, which is equal to
2-\log 2
, hence
(2-\log 2)F_{n}\geq n
.
∎

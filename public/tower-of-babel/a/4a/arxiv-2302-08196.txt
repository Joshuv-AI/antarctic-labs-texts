Effective generic freeness and applications to local cohomology
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Ilya Smirnov

Abstract
. Let A A be a Noetherian domain and R R be a finitely generated A A -algebra. We study several features regarding the generic freeness over A A of an R R -module. For an ideal I ⊂ R I\subset R , we show that the local cohomology modules H I i ​ ( R ) \text{H}_{I}^{i}(R) are generically free over A A under certain settings where R R is a smooth A A -algebra. By utilizing the theory of Gröbner bases over arbitrary Noetherian rings, we provide an effective method to make explicit the generic freeness over A A of a finitely generated R R -module.

1.
Introduction

Our starting point is the following classical result of Grothendieck:

Generic Freeness Lemma
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian domain,
R
be a finitely generated
A
-algebra, and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.
There is a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
M\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.

The main goal of this paper is to extend the above result in the following two directions:

(a)

We extend the above result when
M=\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)
is a local cohomology module with support on an ideal
I\subset R
,
A
contains a field, and
R
is a smooth
A
-algebra (recall that local cohomology modules are typically not finitely generated).

(b)

We give an effective and computable method to choose a specific element
a\in A
in the above result.

The generic freeness of local cohomology modules is an important problem that has been addressed in various contexts by Hochster and Roberts
[
26
, Theorem 3.4]
, by Kollár
[
31
, Theorem 78]
, and by Smith
[
48
]
.
Also, see the recent papers
[
9
,
13
]
.

We divide the Introduction into two subsections addressing these two goals.

Effective generic freeness

Our primary tool to address
Goal (b)
is the theory of Gröbner bases, where we use
A
as the coefficient ring.
The study of Gröbner bases over an arbitrary Noetherian coefficient ring (and not just a field) is a well-established and useful technique (see
[
1
, Chapter 4]
,
[
51
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
22
]
).

Suppose that
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
is a polynomial ring over
A
and
>
is a monomial order on
R
(see
Section 2
for more details).
Vasconcelos
[
51
]
noticed that an effective method of determining generic freeness is to compute an initial ideal and then to make invertible the leading coefficients obtained in
A
. A similar idea was later used by Derksen and Kemper in
[
17
]
.

Theorem

(Vasconcelos
[
51
]
)
.

Let
I\subset R
be an ideal and
{\text{in}}_{>}(I)=\big(a_{1}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{1}},\,a_{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{2}},\,\ldots\,,a_{m}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{m}}\big)
be its corresponding initial ideal, where
0\neq a_{i}\in A
and
\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
.
Choose
0\neq a\in(a_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(a_{b})\subset A
.
Then
R/I\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.

In
Theorem 2.5
, we extend the above result to the case of modules, and we provide
generic versions
(extended to our setting over the coefficient ring
A
) of two important results:
Macaulay’s theorem
and
flat degenerations to the initial ideal/module
.
As a consequence of this result, we obtain new effective proofs for Grothendieck’s generic freeness lemma (see
Section 2
) and for a stronger version by Hochster and Roberts (see
Section 2
).

Suppose now that
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
is a positively graded polynomial ring over
A
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
is the graded irrelevant ideal.
We also provide an effective method of determining generic freeness for the local cohomology modules
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I)
, under the assumption that the corresponding initial ideal is “square-free” over the coefficient ring
A
.
This generic freeness result is inspired by the work of Conca and Varbaro
[
14
]
on square-free Gröbner degenerations.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 3.2
)
.

Assume that
A
contains a field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal and

\text{in}_{>}(I)=\big(a_{1}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{1}},\,a_{2}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{2}},\,\ldots\,,a_{b}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{b}}\big)
be its corresponding initial ideal, where
0\neq a_{i}\in A
and
\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
.
Suppose that each monomial
{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{i}}
is square-free
(
i.e.,
\beta_{i}=(\beta_{i,1},\ldots,\beta_{i,r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
\beta_{i,j}\leq 1
)
.
Choose
0\neq a\in(a_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(a_{b})\subset A
.
Then
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is
A_{a}
-free for all
i\geq 0
.

The assumption that
A
contains a field cannot be dropped in
Theorem A
(see
Section 3
).
In
Section 7
, we apply these results to study certain specializations of determinantal ideals.

Applications to local cohomology

We now describe our contributions towards
Goal (a)
.
To the best of our knowledge, the previous most comprehensive result regarding the generic freeness of local cohomology modules is the following:

Theorem

(Smith
[
48
]
)
.

Let
I\subset R
be an ideal such that
R/I
is a finitely generated
A
-module
.
Then there is a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module for all
i\geq 0
.

A typical example where the above result applies is when
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
is a polynomial ring and
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
(e.g., as in
Theorem A
).
If we drop the assumption that
\text{Spec}(R/I)\rightarrow\text{Spec}(A)
is a finite morphism, then there are known examples where one does
not
have generic freeness for local cohomology modules (see
Section 6
).

In the following theorem, for any ideal
I\subset R
, we settle the generic freeness of
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)
under certain assumptions that include the smoothness of the morphism
\text{Spec}(R)\rightarrow\text{Spec}(A)
.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 6.1
)
.

Assume that
A
contains a field
\mathbb{k}
and
R
is a smooth
A
-algebra.
Suppose one of the following two conditions:

(a)

\mathbb{k}
is a field of characteristic zero, or

(b)

\mathbb{k}
is a field of positive characteristic and the regular locus
{\rm Reg}(A)\subset\text{Spec}(A)
contains a nonempty open subset.

Then, for any ideal
I\subset R
, there is a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module for all
i\geq 0
.

Notice that
Theorem B
is applicable in the particular case when
A
is an algebra finitely generated over any field
\mathbb{k}
(see
§6.2
).
In light of
Theorem B
, it is natural to ask about generic freeness of local cohomology in more general settings.
Due to
[
5
, Example 3.3]
, there are smooth algebras over
\mathbb{Z}
where the local cohomology
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)
is generically flat but not generically free over
\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore we should ask the following question:

Question 1.1
.

Suppose that
R
is a smooth
A
-algebra.
Let
I\subset R
be an ideal and
i\geq 0
.
Does there exist a nonzero element
0\neq a\in A
such that
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a flat
A_{a}
-module?

We have a partial answer when
A=\mathbb{Z}
: due to
[
5
]
we know that
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)
has finitely many associated primes, and hence by inverting a suitable element
0\neq a\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can make
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)
torsion-free and thus
\mathbb{Z}
-flat.
Furthermore, the argument of
[
5
]
allows to deduce finiteness of associated primes from generic flatness.
Thus, we settle a new case of Lyubeznik’s conjecture:
Theorem 6.10
gives finiteness of associated primes of local cohomology in a smooth algebra over a Dedekind domain of characteristic
0
.

Our proof of
Theorem B
is inspired by the works of Lyubeznik
[
34
,
35
]
on the finiteness of associated primes of local cohomology.
For the positive characteristic case of
Theorem B
, the theory of
F
-modules (as developed by Lyubeznik
[
35
]
) is our main tool (see
§6.2
).

For the characteristic zero case of
Theorem B
, we obtain several results regarding the ring of differential operators
D_{R/A}
.
For instance, under the assumption that
R
is a smooth
A
-algebra, we show that
D_{R/A}
equals the derivation ring
\Delta(R/A)
, and that for any
f\in R
the localization
R_{f}
is
generically
a finitely generated left module over
D_{R/A}
.
These type of results are classical when
A
is a field (see
[
40
, Chapter 15]
).
Our main result in this direction is the following theorem.

Theorem C

(
Theorem 5.15
)
.

Assume that
A
contains the field
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers and
R
is a smooth
A
-algebra.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

D_{R/A}=\Delta(R/A)
.
In particular,

\gr(D_{R/A})=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}D^{m}_{R/A}/D^{m-1}_{R/A}

is a Noetherian commutative ring and
D_{R/A}
is a Noetherian ring.

(ii)

D_{R/A}
is strongly right Noetherian.

(iii)

For any
f\in R
, there exists a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
R_{f}\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a finitely generated left module over
D_{R/A}\otimes_{A}A_{a}
.

Our proof of
Theorem C
is based on the following ideas.
From the smooth morphism
\text{Spec}(R)\rightarrow\text{Spec}(A)
, we obtain an affine open covering
\text{Spec}(R_{g_{i}})
of
\text{Spec}(R)
with étale morphisms
\text{Spec}(R_{g_{i}})\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{A}^{n_{i}}
(see
Section 5
).
The ring differential operators
D_{P/A}
of a polynomial ring
P
over
A
is well-understood (it is a relative Weyl algebra, see
Section 5
), and then we can determine the ring of differential operators
D_{R_{g_{i}}/A}
by studying the behavior of differential operators under an étale ring map (see
Theorem 5.11
).
This study of differential operators under étale ring maps follows from the work of Másson
[
38
]
.
The finiteness result in part (iii) of
Theorem C
is a consequence of the rationality of the roots of Bernstein–Sato polynomials (see
Section 5
,
Theorem 5.22
).

Outline

The basic outline of this paper is as follows.
In

, we give our approach to Gröbner bases over a Noetherian commutative coefficient ring.
We provide the proof of

in

.

2.
Gröbner bases & Grothendieck’s generic freeness lemma

In this section, we quickly review the basic theory of Gröbner bases over Noetherian rings with the aim of obtaining an effective proof of Grothendieck’s generic freeness lemma.
For more details regarding the theory of Gröbner bases over arbitrary Noetherian rings, the reader is referred to
[
1
, Chapter 4]
.
The following setup and definitions are used throughout this section.

Setup-Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian domain and
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a polynomial ring over
A
.
Let
F
be a free
R
-module
F=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}Re_{i}
and
>
be a
monomial order on
F
.
A
monomial in
F
is an element of the form
m={\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{i}=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}e_{i}\in F
and a
term in
F
is an element of the form
m^{\prime}=am\in F
, where
a\in A
,
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
A
monomial submodule of
F
is a submodule generated by monomials.
The monomial order
>
is characterized by the following two conditions:

(i)

>
is a total order on the set of all monomials in
F
.

(ii)

If
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
are monomials in
F
and
n\neq 1
is a monomial in
R
, then

m_{1}>m_{2}\qquad\text{ implies that }\qquad nm_{1}>nm_{2}>m_{2}.

We will always assume that the monomial order on
F
is
compatible
with a monomial order on
R
(that we also denote as
>
) in the following sense:
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{i}>{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}e_{j}
if and only if
i<j
, or
i=j
and
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}>{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}
.
An element
w\in F
can be written
uniquely
as
w=\sum_{i=1}^{b}a_{i}m_{i}
where the
m_{i}
’s are different monomials, and its
initial term

{\text{in}}_{>}(w)
is given by the term with largest corresponding monomial.
For a given
R
-submodule
M\subset F
, the corresponding
initial module
is given by

{\text{in}}_{>}(M)\,\coloneqq\,\left({\text{in}}_{>}(w)\mid w\in M\right).

Notice that, contrary to the case where
A
is a field, the initial submodule
{\text{in}}_{>}(M)
is
not
necessarily a monomial submodule as it is generated by terms.
A set of elements
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\}
in an
R
-submodule
M\subset F
is said to be a
Gröbner basis for
M
if
{\text{in}}_{>}(M)=\left({\text{in}}_{>}(w_{1}),\ldots,{\text{in}}_{>}(w_{b})\right)
.
Whenever the monomial order used is clear from the context we will drop reference to it.
By abusing notation, we compare terms according to their respective monomials.

Remark 2.2
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

Let
m\in F
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{b}\in F
be terms.
If

m\in(m_{1},\ldots,m_{b})
, then we can write
m=\sum_{i=1}^{b}h_{i}m_{i}
where the
h_{i}
’s are terms in
R
.

(ii)

If
w\in F
and
f\in R
, then
\text{in}(fw)=\text{in}(f)\text{in}(w)
.

Proof.

(i) By the assumption we can write
m=\sum_{i=1}^{b}f_{i}m_{i}
, where
f_{i}
is a polynomial in
R
.
However, after rewriting
m
as a sum of terms and grouping
the terms whose monomial is equal to the one of
m
, we will obtain an expression that will have (at most) one term
h_{i}\in R
from each
f_{i}
.

(ii) Write decompositions
w=am+w^{\prime}
and
f=bh+f^{\prime}
where
a,b\in A
are nonzero elements, and
m\in F
and
h\in R
are the maximal monomials of
w
and
f
, respectively.
Since
A
is a domain,
ab\neq 0
and so we get
\text{in}(fw)=am\cdot bh=\text{in}(f)\text{in}(w)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M\subset F
be an
R
-submodule.
If
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\}\in M
is a Gröbner basis for
M
, then
M
is generated by the elements
w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}
.

Proof.

Let
w\in M
be a nonzero element.
The assumption and
Section 2
(i) yield the existence of terms
h_{1},\ldots,h_{b}\in R
such that
{\text{in}}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{b}h_{i}{\text{in}}(w_{i})
.
We have that
h_{i}\text{in}(w_{i})=\text{in}(h_{i}w_{i})
(see
Section 2
(ii)).
Therefore, it follows that
w^{\prime}=w-\sum_{i=1}^{b}h_{i}w_{i}
satisfies
\text{in}(w^{\prime})<\text{in}(w)
.
Since a monomial order satisfies the descending chain condition (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Lemma 15.2]
,
[
23
, §2.1]
), we can show that
w\in\left(w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\right)
by repeating the above procedure finitely many times. ∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
M\subset F
be an
R
-submodule.
If an element
f\in R
is such that
\text{in}(f)
is a non-zero-divisor over
F/\text{in}(M)
, then
f
is a non-zero-divisor over
F/M
.

Proof.

Suppose there is an element
w\in F
such that
fw\in M
.
By
Section 2
(ii),
\text{in}(fw)=\text{in}(f)\text{in}(w)
and so the hypothesis implies that
\text{in}(w)\in\text{in}(M)
.
Let
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\}
be a Gröbner basis for
M
.
We can find terms
h_{i}\in R
such that
\text{in}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{b}h_{i}\text{in}(w_{i})
.
Let
w^{\prime}=w-\sum_{i=1}^{b}h_{i}w_{i}
.
It is then clear that
fw^{\prime}\in M
and that
\text{in}(w)>\text{in}(w^{\prime})
.
Again, since a monomial order satisfies a descending chain condition, by repeating the above process we obtain that
w\in M
.
∎

We now discuss a process of homogenization for an
R
-submodule
M\subset F
.
Let
\omega=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{r}
and
\mathbb{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\ell}
be two weight vectors.
The corresponding
(\omega,\mathbb{d})
-degree of the monomial
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k}=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}e_{k}\in F
is given by

\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k})\,\coloneqq\,\omega\cdot{\mathbf{n}}+d_{k}\;=\;n_{1}\omega_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}\omega_{r}+d_{k}.

For an element
w\in F
,
\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(w)
is the maximum
(\omega,\mathbb{d})
-degree of the terms of
w
and
\text{in}_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(w)
is the sum of all the terms of
w
of maximal
(\omega,\mathbb{d})
-degree.
Consider the polynomial ring
S=R[t]
and the corresponding free
S
-module
F[t]=F\otimes_{R}S
.
For an element
w=\sum_{j}a_{j}\,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{n}}_{j}}\,e_{k_{j}}\in F
, the corresponding
(\omega,\mathbb{d})
-homogenization is given by

\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(w)\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{j}a_{j}\,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{n}}_{j}}\,t^{{\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(w)}-{\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{n}}_{j}}e_{k_{j}})}}\,e_{k_{j}}\;\in F[t].

For an
R
-submodule
M\subset F
, we define
\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(M)\subset F[t]
as the
S
-submodule generated by
\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(w)
for all
w\in M
.
After considering
S
as a graded polynomial ring with
[S]_{0}=A
,
\deg(x_{i})=\omega_{i}
and
\deg(t)=1
, we obtain that
\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(M)
is a graded
S
-submodule of the graded free
S
-module
F[t]=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}Se_{i}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}S(-d_{i})
.

The following theorem shows that, in our current relative setting over a Noetherian domain
A
, the theory of Gröbner degenerations
generically over A
behaves just as the classical setting over a field.
As a direct consequence, we obtain an effective proof of Grothendieck’s generic freeness lemma which is suitable for computations.

Theorem 2.5

(Generic deformation to the initial module)
.

Assume
Section 2
.
Let
M\subset F
be an
R
-submodule and suppose that
\text{in}(M)=\left(a_{1}m_{1},\ldots,a_{b}m_{b}\right)
where
0\neq a_{i}\in A
and
m_{i}\in F
is a monomial.
Choose
0\neq a\in(a_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(a_{b})\subset A
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

(
Generic Macaulay’s theorem;
cf.
[
18
, Theorem 15.3]
)

F/M\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module with a basis given by the set of monomials in
\left(F\otimes_{A}A_{a}\right)\setminus\left(m_{1},\ldots,m_{b}\right)
.

(ii)

(
Generic flat degenerations;
cf.
[
18
, Theorem 15.17]
)

There exist weight vectors
\omega=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{r}
and
\mathbb{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\ell}
such that, over the graded polynomial ring
S=R[t]
with
[S]_{0}=A
,
\deg(x_{i})=\omega_{i}
and
\deg(t)=1
, the graded
S
-submodule
E=\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(M)\subset F[t]\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}S(-d_{i})
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

F[t]/E\otimes_{A[t]}A[t]/(t)\cong F/\text{in}(M)
.

(b)

F[t]/E\otimes_{A[t]}A[t,t^{-1}]\cong F/M\otimes_{A}A[t,t^{-1}]
.

(c)

F[t]/E\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}[t]
-module.

Proof.

Throughout the proof we substitute
A
by its localization
A_{a}
.
Therefore we may assume that
\text{in}(M)=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{b})
and that
F/\text{in}(M)
is a free
A
-module with basis
B
, where
B
is the set of monomials not in the initial module
\text{in}(M)
.
We choose a Gröbner basis
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\}
for
M
such that
m_{i}=\text{in}(w_{i})
.

(i) Let
w\in F\setminus M
.
Choose the maximal term
m\in F
of
w
such that
m\in\text{in}(M)
.
Since
\text{in}(M)
is assumed to be an actual monomial submodule, we have
m=hm_{i}
for some term
h\in R
(cf.
Section 2
(i)).
Let
w^{\prime}=w-hw_{i}
and
m^{\prime}\in F
be the maximal term of
w^{\prime}
such that
m^{\prime}\in\text{in}(M)
.
Notice that
[w]=[w^{\prime}]\in F/M
and that
m>m^{\prime}
.
Once again, by the descending chain condition of a monomial order, we can repeat this process finitely many times and obtain an element
z\in F
whose terms only involve monomials in
B
and such that
[w]=[z]\in F/M
.
This shows that
B
is a generating set of
F/M
as an
A
-module.

It is straightforward to check that the monomials in
B
are
A
-linearly independent inside
F/M
.
Indeed, if there is a dependence relation
w=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\mu_{i}\in M
with
0\neq c_{i}\in A
and
\mu_{i}\in B
, then we would obtain the contradiction
c_{i}\mu_{i}=\text{in}(w)\in\text{in}(M)
for some
i
.

(ii)
Notice that there exists a weight vector
\omega\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{r}
such that
\omega\cdot{\mathbf{n}}>\omega\cdot{\mathbf{m}}
for any pair
({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k},{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}e_{k})
in the following finite set

\big\{({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k},{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}e_{k})\,\mid\,\text{${\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k}$ and ${\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}e_{k}$ are monomials of the same $w_{i}$ and ${\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k}>{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}e_{k}$}\big\}.

Indeed, by utilizing Farkas’ lemma, the proof follows as for the case of ideals (see
[
50
, Proposition 1.11]
,
[
23
, Lemma 3.1.1]
).
Then, we can choose
\mathbb{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})
with differences
d_{k}-d_{k+1}>0
as large as needed so that we obtain
\text{in}_{>}(w_{i})=\text{in}_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(w_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq b
.

Let
E=\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(M)\subset F[t]
.
We shall prove that
E
satisfies all the claimed properties.
It is clear that property (a) holds.
Consider the automorphism
\varphi
on
F[t,t^{-1}]
given by
\varphi({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k})=t^{\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k})}{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k}
, and notice that it takes
E\otimes_{A[t]}A[t,t^{-1}]
into
M\otimes_{A}A[t,t^{-1}]
.
Therefore,
\varphi
induces the isomorphism of property (b).
Notice that for both properties (a) and (b) we do not need to substitute
A
by its localization
A_{a}
.
It remains to check property (c).

We now define a monomial order
>^{\prime}
on
F[t]
by setting that
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}t^{n}e_{k}>^{\prime}{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}t^{m}e_{l}
if and only if

–

\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k})+n>\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}e_{l})+m
, or

–

\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}e_{k})+n=\deg_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{m}}e_{l})+m
and
{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha}e_{k}>{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta}e_{l}
.

By construction, for any
z\in E=\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(M)
, we obtain that

\text{in}_{>^{\prime}}(z)\;=\;t^{m}\text{in}_{>}(w)

for some
w\in M
and
m\geq 0
.
Therefore, since
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\}
is a Gröbner basis for
M\subset F
with respect to
>
, it follows that
\{\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(w_{1}),\ldots,\hom_{\omega,\mathbb{d}}(w_{b})\}
is a Gröbner basis for
E\subset F[t]
with respect to
>^{\prime}
.
Hence, we have
\text{in}_{>^{\prime}}(E)=\left(m_{1},\ldots,m_{b}\right)\subset F[t]
.
By utilizing part (i) applied to
E\subset F[t]
, the set of monomials in
F[t]\setminus\text{in}_{>^{\prime}}(E)
provides an
A
-basis for
F[t]/E
, and so it follows that
F[t]/E
is a free
A[t]
-module with a basis given by the monomials in
B
. This settles property (c).
∎

A direct consequence of the above theorem is the following celebrated result of Grothendieck
[

, Lemme 6.9.2]
.

Corollary 2.6

(Grothendieck’s generic freeness lemma)
.

Let

be a Noetherian domain,
B
be a finitely generated
A
-algebra, and
M
be a finitely generated

-module.
Then there exists a nonzero element

such that

is a free
A_{a}
-module.

Proof.

The result follows from

after choosing a surjection

from a polynomial ring and presenting

as a quotient

with

R
-module of finite rank.
∎

We also have the following graded version that provides generic freeness for all the graded parts of a graded module.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let

be a Noetherian domain,
B=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}B_{i}
be a positively graded finitely generated

-algebra with

acting on

, and

-module for all
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We may choose a graded surjection

with

, and a graded presentation

with

a graded free

-module.
Putting aside the grading,

(i) already implies that, after localizing at a nonzero element

The same argument easily recovers a stronger result due to Hochster and Roberts
[
25
, Lemma 8.1]
.

Proposition 2.8

(Hochster – Roberts)
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian domain,
B
be a finitely generated
A
-algebra, and

C
be a finitely generated
B
-algebra.
Let
E
be a finitely generated
C
-module,
N\subset E
be a finitely generated
A
-submodule, and
M\subset E
be a finitely generated
B
-submodule.
Set
D=E/(N+M)
.
Then there exists a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
D\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.

Proof.

We can choose polynomial rings
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},x_{s+1},\ldots,x_{r}]
and
R^{\prime}=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]\subset R

so that we have surjections
R\twoheadrightarrow C
and
R^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow B
.
We can also find a free
R
-module
F
and finitely generated submodules
L_{1}\subset F
,
L_{2}\subset F
and
L_{3}\subset F
over
A
,
R^{\prime}
and
R
, respectively, such that we have an isomorphism

D=E/\left(N+M\right)\;\cong\;F/\left(L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}\right).

We continue using a monomial
>
order on
F
as in
Section 2
.
Let
H=\text{in}(L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3})\subset F
be the
A
-module generated by
\text{in}(w)
for all
w\in L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}
.
Similarly, we define
\text{in}(L_{1})
,
\text{in}(L_{2})
and
\text{in}(L_{3})
as modules over
A
,
R^{\prime}
and
R
, respectively.

Let
G_{1}\subset F
be the
A
-submodule generated by the elements

w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}\in F\;\text{ such that }\;w_{1}\in L_{1},w_{2}\in L_{2},w_{3}\in L_{3}\;\text{ and }\;\text{in}(w_{1})\geq\text{in}(w_{2}+w_{3});

notice that

is a finitely generated

are smaller or equal that some monomial appearing in an element

, and the list of monomials appearing in elements of

is finite.
Similarly, we define the

-submodule

generated by the elements

w_{2}+w_{3}\in F\;\text{ such that }\;w_{2}\in L_{2},w_{3}\in L_{3}\;\text{ and }\;\text{in}(w_{2})\geq\text{in}(w_{3});

by a similar argument,
G_{2}
is finitely generated
R^{\prime}
-module.

By construction, we can see that
H=\text{in}(G_{1})+\text{in}(G_{2})+\text{in}(L_{3})
.
We have that
\text{in}(G_{1})
,
\text{in}(G_{2})
and
\text{in}(L_{3})
are finitely generated modules over
A
,
R^{\prime}
and
R
, respectively, and thus we may write

\text{in}(G_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{h}A\cdot c_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{\alpha_{i}},\quad\text{in}(G_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}R^{\prime}\cdot d_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{in}(L_{3})=\sum_{i=1}^{l}R\cdot f_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{\gamma_{i}},

where

and
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
.
Take a nonzero element

.
Finally, we can utilize the argument in the proof of
Theorem 2.5
(i) to show that

D\otimes_{A}A_{a}\;\cong\;F/\left(L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}\right)\otimes_{A}A_{a}

is a free

-module with a basis given by the set of monomials not in

.
∎

We now briefly discuss the extension of the Buchberger criterion into our current setting.
As long as linear equations are solvable in
A
(e.g., when
A
is a PID or a polynomial ring over a field) this leads to an algorithm for computing Gröbner bases (see
[
1
, §4.2]
).

Definition 2.9
.

Let
w\in F
and
\mathcal{G}=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}\in F
.
We say that
w
reduces to
z\in F
modulo
\mathcal{G}
if we can write

w=f_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+f_{k}w_{b}+z

for some elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{b}\in S
such that
\text{in}(f_{i}w_{i})\leq\text{in}(w)
and no nonzero term of
z
belongs to the submodule
\left(\text{in}(w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(w_{b})\right)\subset F
.

Theorem 2.10

(cf.
[
18
, Theorem 15.8]
,
[
1
, Theorem 4.2.3]
)
.

Let
M\subset F
be an
R
-submodule, and
\mathcal{G}=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\}\subset M
be a generating set of
M
.
Then
\mathcal{G}
is a Gröbner basis for
M
if and only if for all
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{b})\in R^{b}
such that

h_{1}\text{in}(w_{1})+\cdots+h_{b}\text{in}(w_{b})\,=\,0\quad\text{\rm(i.e., $(h_{1},\ldots,h_{b})\in\text{Syz}(\text{in}(w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(w_{b}))$)}

and each
h_{i}
is a term, we have that
h_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+h_{b}w_{b}
reduces to zero modulo
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a Gröbner basis, then it is clear that any such linear combination, as it is an element in
M
, reduces to zero modulo
\mathcal{G}
.
Hence we only need to prove the reverse implication, and we assume that
h_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+h_{b}w_{b}
reduces to zero modulo
\mathcal{G}
for any vector of terms
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{b})\in\text{Syz}(\text{in}(w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(w_{b}))
.

Choose a nonzero
w\in M
and write
w=f_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+f_{b}w_{b}
.
Let
m=\max\{\text{in}(f_{1}w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(f_{b}w_{b})\}
(i.e., the maximal respective monomial appearing among
\text{in}(f_{1}w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(f_{b}w_{b})
).
Notice that
m\geq\text{in}(w)
, and that
\text{in}(w)\in(\text{in}(w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(w_{b}))
when
m
and
\text{in}(w)
have the same monomial.
Therefore we assume that
m>\text{in}(w)
.

Let
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{i\mid\text{ $m$ and $\text{in}(f_{i}w_{i})$ have the same monomial}\}
.
We consider the vector
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{b})\in R^{b}
such that
h_{i}=\text{in}(f_{i})
if
i\in{\mathfrak{J}}
and
h_{i}=0
otherwise.
Since
m>\text{in}(w)
, we necessarily have that
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{b})\in\text{Syz}(\text{in}(w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(w_{b}))
.
Then the hypothesis yields the existence of elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{b}\in R
such that

h_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+h_{b}w_{b}\;=\;g_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+g_{b}w_{b}

and
\text{in}(h_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+h_{b}w_{b})\geq\text{in}(g_{i}w_{i})
.
Set
f_{i}^{\prime}=f_{i}-h_{i}+g_{i}
and
m^{\prime}=\max\{\text{in}(f_{1}^{\prime}w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(f_{b}^{\prime}w_{b})\}
.
By combining everything we obtain the equality

w\;=\;f_{1}^{\prime}w_{1}+\cdots+f_{b}^{\prime}w_{b}

and the strict inequality
m>m^{\prime}
.
After finitely many reductions, we must obtain an expression
w=\gamma_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{b}w_{b}
with
\gamma_{i}\in R
and such that
\text{in}(w)
and
\max\{\text{in}(\gamma_{1}w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(\gamma_{b}w_{b})\}
have the same monomial.
This concludes the proof that
\mathcal{G}
is a Gröbner basis for
M
.
∎

We now apply the above theorem to study the behavior of initial submodules under taking Frobenius powers in a positive characteristic setting.
If
A
contains a field of characteristic
p>0
and
M\subset F
is an
R
-submodule, we denote by
M^{[p]}\subset F
the
R
-submodule generated by the
p
-th power

for all
w=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\in M
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Suppose that
A
contains a field of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
M\subset F
be an
R
-submodule and suppose that
\text{in}(M)=\left(a_{1}m_{1},\ldots,a_{b}m_{b}\right)
where
0\neq a_{i}\in A
and
m_{i}\in F
is a monomial.
Choose
0\neq a\in(a_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(a_{b})\subset A
.
Then, for all
e\geq 0
, we have the equality
\text{in}(M^{[p^{e}]}\otimes_{A}A_{a})=\text{in}(M)^{[p^{e}]}\otimes_{A}A_{a}
and that
F/M^{[p^{e}]}\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.

Proof.

Since taking
p
-th powers commutes with localization, we may substitute
A
by
A_{a}
.
Thus we assume that
\text{in}(M)=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{b})\subset F
is a monomial submodule.
Choose
w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\in M
with
m_{i}=\text{in}(w_{i})
, and set
\mathcal{G}=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\}
.
We have that
\text{Syz}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{b})
is generated by the divided Koszul relations of the
m_{i}
’s; the same proof of
[
18
, Lemma 15.1]
applies in our case.
As these relations commute with taking
p
-th powers, we have that
\text{Syz}(m_{1}^{p},\ldots,m_{b}^{p})=\text{Syz}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{b})^{[p]}
.
By
Theorem 2.10
, for any vector of terms
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{b})\in\text{Syz}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{b})
we have that
h_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+h_{b}w_{b}
reduces to zero modulo
\mathcal{G}
, and in particular this implies that
h_{1}^{p}w_{1}^{p}+\cdots+h_{b}^{p}w_{b}^{p}
reduces to zero modulo
\mathcal{G}^{p}=\{w_{1}^{p},\ldots,w_{b}^{p}\}
.
But then
Theorem 2.10
implies that
\mathcal{G}^{p}
is a Gröbner basis for

, and this gives the claimed equality
\text{in}(M^{[p]})=\text{in}(M)^{[p]}
.

By induction, we obtain that
\text{in}(M^{[p^{e}]})=\text{in}(M)^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e\geq 0
.
Therefore
Theorem 2.5
yields that
F/M^{[p^{e}]}
is a free
A
-module for all
e\geq 0
.
∎

3.
Generic square-free Gröbner degenerations

In this section, we also provide a specific generic freeness result for local cohomology modules under the existence of a square-free Gröbner degeneration.
Throughout this section we use the following specialization of
Section 2
.

Setup 3.1
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian domain,
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a positively graded polynomial ring over
A
, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=[R]_{+}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
be the graded irrelevant ideal.
As in
Section 2
, let
>
be a monomial order on

.

The main goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.
It is inspired by the work of Conca and Varbaro
[
14
]
on square-free Gröbner degenerations.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
Section 3
and suppose that
A
contains a field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal and

\text{in}(I)=\big(a_{1}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{1}},\,\ldots\,,a_{b}{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{b}}\big)
be its corresponding initial ideal, where

and
\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
.
Suppose that each monomial
{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{i}}
is square-free.
Choose
0\neq a\in(a_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(a_{b})\subset A
.
Then
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is

-free for all

.

We point out that the theorem is sharp in the following sense.

Remark 3.3

(
[
47
, Remark 3]
)
.

The condition that

contains a field cannot be avoided in

.
Let

and take the square-free monomial ideal

corresponding to the minimal triangulation of the projective plane.
By
[

, for a field

is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

.
Therefore, it cannot happen that
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I)
is
\mathbb{Z}
-flat for all

.

We state the following result that will be useful for us.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
13
, Theorem A]
)
.

Let
B
be a Noetherian ring and
S
be a positively graded finitely generated

-algebra.
Let
{\mathfrak{M}}=[S]_{+}
be the graded irrelevant ideal of

Let

be a finitely generated graded

is flat over

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{M}}}^{i}(M)
is a flat

-module for all

.

(ii)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{M}}}^{i}\left(M\otimes_{B}B_{\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{q}B_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
is
(B_{\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{q}B_{\mathfrak{p}})
-flat for all

,

and
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(B)
.

The natural map

,

and

Moreover, when any of the above equivalent conditions is satisfied, we have the following base change isomorphism

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{M}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{B}C\;\xrightarrow{\cong}\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{M}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}C)

for all

and any

-algebra

.

Definition 3.5
.

Under the notation of

, we say that a finitely generated graded

-module is

is
B
-flat and

-flat for all

Condition (iii) of the above theorem is a relaxation of the closely related notions of

introduced by Kollár and Kovács
[

and

introduced by Dao, De Stefani and Ma
[

]

In order to state the following lemma, we recall that

is

if for any equicharateristic local ring

\phi\colon(T,\mathfrak{n})\twoheadrightarrow R_{\mathfrak{M}}

\text{H}_{\mathfrak{n}}^{i}(T)\twoheadrightarrow\text{H}^{i}_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(R)
for all

.

Lemma 3.6

(
[
14
, Proposition 2.3, Proposition 3.3]
)

Let

be a positively graded polynomial ring over a field and

be a monomial order on

.
Let

If

is a square-free monomial ideal, then both

After recalling these needed results we are ready for the proof

.

Proof of
Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\delta_{i}=\deg(x_{i})>0

To simplify the notation, we substitute

by

(where

\text{in}(I)=({\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{1}},\,\ldots\,,{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta_{b}})\subset R

By

, (b)

is a free

-module.
Furthermore, we can assume that

Let

.
We first show that

is fiber-full over

Let

Set

Proof.

(i)

By utilizing

Section 5

5.1.
Classical case over a field of characteristic zero

In this subsection, we briefly cover the classical case of a smooth algebra over a field of characteristic zero.
Here the main goal is to show that Bernstein–Sato polynomials have rational roots in any smooth algebra over a field of characteristic zero.
Our approach is to utilize Kashiwara’s result
[
28
]
on local Bernstein–Sato polynomials and the techniques of Mebkhout and Narváez-Macarro
[
41
]
that allow us to globalize.
While the rationality result is usually stated for a polynomial ring, the aforementioned approach can be used more generally.
We point out that this rationality result (in the polynomial ring case) was our main tool in the proof of
Section 5
(i) and this more general result can be similarly used to give a different proof of
Theorem 5.15
(iii).

We first recall some fundamental results about Bernstein–Sato polynomials. Our treatment follows the survey paper
[

First, notice
that the Bernstein–Sato polynomial

of

-module

(i) By

\mathbb{C}
and consider
S\coloneqq R_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}\mathbb{C}
.
Due to the
§5.1
(iii), the Bernstein–Sato polynomial of
f
in all three rings is the same one.
Thus, we reduced the problem to a smooth algebra
S
over
\mathbb{C}
.
The result of Kashiwara
[
28
]
shows that the
local
Bernstein–Sato polynomial (that is, the Bernstein–Sato polynomial for
f
in the localization
S_{\mathfrak{m}}
with
{\mathfrak{m}}\in\text{MaxSpec}(S)
) can be factored completely in
\mathbb{Q}[s]
.
The assertion now follows from
§5.1
(ii).
∎

6.
Applications to local cohomology

In this section, we prove the following theorem regarding the generic freeness of local cohomology modules.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
A
be Noetherian domain containing a field
\mathbb{k}
and
R
be a smooth
A
-algebra.
Suppose one of the following two conditions:

(a)

\mathbb{k}
is a field of characteristic zero, or

(b)

\mathbb{k}
is a field of positive characteristic and the regular locus
{\rm Reg}(A)\subset\text{Spec}(A)
contains a nonempty open subset.

Then, for any ideal
I\subset R
, there is a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module for all

.

Proof.

The result follows from
Theorem 6.3
and
Theorem 6.8
below.
∎

As shown by the following example of Katzman
[

]
, one cannot hope for a generic freeness result of local cohomology that does not involve additional assumptions.

Remark 6.2
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and

be the
\mathbb{k}
-algebra

S=\frac{\mathbb{k}[s,t,x,y,u,v]}{\left(sx^{2}v^{2}-(t+s)xyuv+ty^{2}u^{2}\right)}.

Consider

as the coefficient ring.
In
[

, Example 4.6]
it was proved that,
one cannot find an element

-module.

6.1.
Characteristic zero

Here we prove the generic freeness of local cohomology modules in a characteristic zero setting.
The proof follows straightforwardly from the techniques and results we developed in
Section 5
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian domain containing the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
and
R
be a smooth
A
-algebra.
Then, for any ideal
I\subset R
and any
i\geq 0
, there exists a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.

Proof.

Let
D=D_{R/A}
.
Fix a set of generators
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
of
I
.
Notice that the Čech complex

{C}^{\bullet}\colon\quad 0\rightarrow R\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i}R_{f_{i}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i<j}R_{f_{i}f_{j}}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow R_{f_{1}\cdots f_{n}}\rightarrow 0

is naturally a complex of left
D
-modules (see
Section 5
).
Due to
Theorem 5.15
(iii), after localizing at a suitable nonzero element in
A
, we may assume that
C^{\bullet}
is a complex of finitely generated left
D
-modules.
Therefore,
Section 5
implies that
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)\cong\text{H}^{i}(C^{\bullet})
becomes
A
-free after localizing at another suitable nonzero element in
A
.
∎

6.2.
Positive characteristic

In positive characteristic, Lyubeznik introduced in
[
35
]
the theory of
F
-modules in order to show finiteness of associated primes of local cohomology.
We use the following setup throughout this subsection.

Setup 6.4
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
A
be a Noetherian domain containing
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
R
be a smooth algebra over
A
.
Define
R^{\prime}
to be an
R
-bimodule with usual structure on the left and the Frobenius endomorphism on the right:
r^{\prime}r=r^{p}r^{\prime}
for all
r\in R
and
r^{\prime}\in R^{\prime}
.
The Peskine–Szpiro functor is
F_{R}(M)\coloneqq R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}M
.

For example,
F_{R}(R)=R
and
F_{R}(R/I)=R/{I^{[p]}}
.
In general, if
M
has a presentation
F_{2}\xrightarrow{\phi}F_{1}\to M\to 0
, then
F_{2}\xrightarrow{\phi^{[p]}}F_{1}\to F_{R}(M)\to 0

where
\phi^{[p]}
is obtained by taking all entries of the matrix
\phi
to
p
th power.

Remark 6.5
.

Notice that, if we assume that
A
is regular, then it follows that
R
is also regular.
Let
P\in\text{Spec}(R)
and
{\mathfrak{p}}=P\cap A\in\text{Spec}(A)
.
Since
A\rightarrow R
is smooth, the local homomorphism
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow R_{P}
is flat and the fiber
R_{P}\otimes_{{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})
is a regular ring.
Hence
[
39
, Theorem 23.7]
implies that
R_{P}
is a regular ring.

Our main tool in a positive characteristic setting is the following result.

Theorem 6.6

(Lyubeznik,
[
35
, Propositions 2.3, 2.10]
)
.

Suppose that
R
is a regular ring, and let
I\subset R
be an ideal.
Then
\lc_{I}^{i}(R)
has a
root
, i.e., a finitely generated
R
-module
M
with an injective homomorphism
M\to F_{R}(M)
such that

\lc_{I}^{i}(R)\;=\;\lim_{\to}\left\{M\to F_{R}(M)\to F^{2}_{R}(M)\to\cdots\right\}.

By utilizing the above theorem and
Section 2
, we obtain the following generic freeness result for local cohomology modules.

Corollary 6.7
.

Suppose that
R
is a regular ring, and let
I\subset R
be an ideal.
Then there exists a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
\lc_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.

Proof.

From
Theorem 6.6
, we can find a root
M
of
\lc_{I}^{i}(R)
.
Consider the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow F_{R}(M)\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0
, with
Q
some
R
-module.
Write
Q=F/N
with
F
a free
R
-module of finite rank.
Since
R
is regular, the functor
F_{R}
is exact by Kunz’s theorem
[
32
]
, and so we get a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow F_{R}^{e}(M)\rightarrow F_{R}^{e+1}(M)\rightarrow F_{R}^{e}(Q)\cong F/N^{[p^{e}]}\rightarrow 0

for all
e\geq 0
.
By utilizing
Theorem 2.5
and
Section 2
, we can find
0\neq a\in A
such that
M\otimes_{A}A_{a}
and
F/N^{[p^{e}]}\otimes A_{a}
(for all
e\geq 0
) are free
A_{a}
-modules.
Therefore, for all
e\geq 0
,

0\rightarrow F_{R}^{e}(M)\otimes_{A}A_{a}\rightarrow F_{R}^{e+1}(M)\otimes_{A}A_{a}\rightarrow F_{R}^{e}(Q)\otimes_{A}A_{a}\rightarrow 0

is a short exact sequence of free
A_{a}
-modules, and in particular, is
A_{a}
-split.
As a consequence, we obtain that
\lc_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a}=\lim_{\to}\left(F_{R}^{e}(M)\otimes_{A}A_{a}\right)
is a free
A_{a}
-module.
∎

Theorem 6.8
.

Assume
§6.2
and suppose that
{\rm Reg}(A)
contains a nonempty open subset of
\text{Spec}(A)
.
Let
I\subset R
be an ideal.
Then there exists a nonzero element
a\in A
such that
\lc_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.

Proof.

The assumption yields a nonzero element
a^{\prime}\in A
such that
D(a^{\prime})\subset{\rm Reg}(A)
, hence
A_{a^{\prime}}
is regular.
By
§6.2
,
R\otimes_{A}A_{a^{\prime}}
is also regular.
Then
§6.2
gives a nonzero element
a^{\prime\prime}\in A
such that
\text{H}_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}}
is a free
A_{a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}}
-module, and so result holds for
a=a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime}\in A
. ∎

Remark 6.9
.

If we assume that
A
is a finitely generated algebra over
\mathbb{k}
, from
[
49
,
Tag 07PJ
]
and
[

,
Tag 07P7
]
, we obtain that

\text{Reg}(A)=\big\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(A)\mid A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is a regular local ring}\big\}

is an open subset of
\text{Spec}(A)
.
Moreover, as

\text{Reg}(A)

6.3.
Finiteness of associated primes

We apply our result to derive a finiteness property of associated primes
for smooth algebras over a regular ring of dimension one. As far as we know this result is new, its analogues hold in mixed characteristic by
[
5
, Theorem 4.3]
and in positive characteristic by
[
35
]
.

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain of characteristic
0
and
R
be a smooth
A
-algebra. Then for any ideal
I
of
R
and any nonnegative integer
i\geq 0
the module
\lc_{I}^{i}(R)
has finitely many associated primes.

Proof.

By
Theorem 6.3
there is
0\neq a\in A
such that
\lc_{I}^{k}(R)_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.
It follows that
{\text{Ass}}_{A}(\lc_{I}^{i}(R))
is finite.
Therefore it suffices to show that there are only finitely many
R
-associated primes contracting to a given
A
-associated prime. We proceed as in
[
5
, Theorem 4.3]
.

First, there are only finitely many associated primes that contract to
(0)\subset A
since

\lc_{I}^{i}(R)\otimes_{A}\text{Quot}(A)\cong\lc_{I}^{i}(R\otimes_{A}\text{Quot}(A))
and

R\otimes_{A}\text{Quot}(A)
is a regular finitely generated algebra over a field of characteristic
0
(
[
34
, Remark 3.7 (i)]
). Second, for any maximal ideal

{\mathfrak{p}}
of
A
the localization
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a DVR, let
\pi
be a uniformizer of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
The exact sequence

0\to A_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\pi}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})\to 0

induces the exact sequence

\lc_{I}^{i-1}(R\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}))\xrightarrow{d}\lc_{I}^{i}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\xrightarrow{\pi}\lc_{I}^{i}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}).

So any associated prime of
\lc_{I}^{i}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
that contracts to
{\mathfrak{p}}

must be an associated prime of
\IM(d)
.
By smoothness,
d
is a map of modules over
D_{R/A}\otimes_{A}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})\cong D_{(R\otimes_{A}k({\mathfrak{p}}))/\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}
. Thus, because
\lc_{I}^{i}(R\otimes_{A}k({\mathfrak{p}}))
has finite length by
[
34
]
,

\IM(d)
has finite length as a
D_{(R\otimes_{A}k({\mathfrak{p}}))/\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}
-module, hence it can only have finitely many associated primes.
∎

7.
Examples: specializations of determinantal ideals

By utilizing the methods developed in this paper, we show how to obtain specializations of determinantal ideals that keep many of the original invariants of determinantal ideals.
We chose this example because a description of a Gröbner basis is well-known in this case.
One could use these techniques with any family of ideals for which we have an explicit computation of Gröbner bases (e.g., for Schubert determinantal ideals
[
42
, §16.4]
).
We use the following setup throughout.

Setup 7.1
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian domain containing a field
\mathbb{k}
and
R=A\left[x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\text{ and }1\leq j\leq n\right]
be a polynomial ring with
m\leq n
.
For each
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
, let
a_{i,j}\in A
be a nonzero element.
Consider the matrix

M\;=\;\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1,1}x_{1,1}&a_{1,2}x_{1,2}&\cdots&a_{1,n}x_{1,n}\\
a_{2,1}x_{2,1}&a_{2,2}x_{2,2}&\cdots&a_{2,n}x_{2,n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{m,1}x_{m,1}&a_{m,2}x_{m,2}&\cdots&a_{m,n}x_{m,n}\end{array}\right]\in R^{m\times n}

and let
I_{t}=I_{t}(M)\subset R
be the corresponding ideal of
t
-minors, where
1\leq t\leq m
.
Let
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the set

\mathcal{H}\;=\;\big\{(i,j)\in[m]\times[n]\mid i+j\leq t-1\big\}\;\;\bigcup\;\;\big\{(i,j)\in[m]\times[n]\mid i+j\geq n+m-t+2\big\}

of positions not in the main antidiagonal of any
t
-minor of the matrix
M
.

We have the following specific generic freeness result.

Theorem 7.2
.

Assume
Section 7
.
Choose
0\neq a\in\bigcap_{(i,j)\notin\mathcal{H}}\left(a_{i,j}\right)\subset A
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

R/I_{t}\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module.

(ii)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{t})\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a free
A_{a}
-module for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

Consider the polynomial ring
T=\mathbb{k}\left[y_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\text{ and }1\leq j\leq n\right]
and the generic
n\times m
matrix
Y=\left(y_{i,j}\right)
.
We have an injective
\mathbb{k}
-algebra map
\varphi:T\hookrightarrow R,\,y_{i,j}\mapsto a_{i,j}x_{i,j}
such that
I_{t}=\varphi\left(I_{t}(Y)\right)R
.

Let
>
be an antidiagonal monomial order on
Y
(see
[
8
, Chapter 4]
,
[
42
, §16.4]
).
We extend the same monomial order to
R
(in the sense of
Section 2
) by saying that
x_{i,j}<x_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
if and only if
y_{i,j}<y_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
.
A Gröbner basis of
I_{t}(Y)
is given by the
t
-minors of
Y
(see
[
8
, Chapter 4]
,
[
42
, §16.4]
).
We substitute
A
by
A_{a}
.
Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{b}
be the
t
-minors of
Y
and
w_{1}=\varphi(v_{1}),\ldots,w_{b}=\varphi(v_{b})
be the
t
-minors of
M
.
The elements
\text{in}(v_{i})
and
\text{in}(w_{i})
are generated by the product of the elements in the main antidiagonal of a
t
-minor of the matrices
Y
and
M
, respectively.
The syzygies
\text{Syz}(\text{in}(v_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(v_{b}))
are generated by the divided Koszul relations (see
[
18
, Lemma 15.1]
).
Since we are assuming that
a_{i,j}
is invertible when
(i,j)\notin\mathcal{H}
, it follows that the syzygies
\text{Syz}\left(\text{in}(w_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(w_{b})\right)
are generated by image of
\text{Syz}(\text{in}(v_{1}),\ldots,\text{in}(v_{b}))
under the induced map
\varphi\colon T^{b}\rightarrow R^{b}
.
Therefore, as
v_{1},\ldots,v_{b}
is a Gröbner basis for
I_{t}(Y)
, by utilizing
Theorem 2.10
we obtain that
w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}
is also a Gröbner basis for
I_{t}
.

Part (i) now follows from
Theorem 2.5
.
Since each
\text{in}(w_{i})
is square-free,
Theorem 3.2
yields the result of part (ii).
∎

The next corollary gives a specific locus where a natural specialization behaves just as a generic matrix.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
S=\mathbb{k}\left[x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\text{ and }1\leq j\leq n\right]
and
X=(x_{i,j})
be the
m\times n
generic matrix.
Consider the matrix

N\;=\;\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\beta_{1,1}x_{1,1}&\beta_{1,2}x_{1,2}&\cdots&\beta_{1,n}x_{1,n}\\
\beta_{2,1}x_{2,1}&\beta_{2,2}x_{2,2}&\cdots&\beta_{2,n}x_{2,n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta_{m,1}x_{m,1}&\beta_{m,2}x_{m,2}&\cdots&\beta_{m,n}x_{m,n}\end{array}\right]\in S^{m\times n}

where
\beta_{i,j}\in\mathbb{k}
.
If
\beta_{i,j}\neq 0
for all
(i,j)\notin\mathcal{H}
, then the following statements hold:

(i)

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[S/I_{t}(N)\right]_{\mu}\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[S/I_{t}(X)\right]_{\mu}\right)
for all

(ii)

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I_{t}(N))\right]_{\mu}\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I_{t}(X))\right]_{\mu}\right)

Here we specify

is a polynomial ring over

.
Then

becomes the specialization of

, where

.

∎

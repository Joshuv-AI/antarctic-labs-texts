Refinements of Katz–Sarnak theory for the number of points on curves over finite fields
Jonas Bergström, Everett W. Howe, Elisa Lorenzo García, Christophe Ritzenthaler

Abstract
. This paper goes beyond Katz–Sarnak theory on the distribution of curves over finite fields according to their number of rational points, theoretically, experimentally and conjecturally. In particular, we give a formula for the limits of the moments measuring the asymmetry of this distribution for (non-hyperelliptic) curves of genus g ≥ 3 g\geq 3 . The experiments point to a stronger notion of convergence than the one provided by the Katz–Sarnak framework for all curves of genus ≥ 3 \geq 3 . However, for elliptic curves and for hyperelliptic curves of every genus we prove that this stronger convergence cannot occur.

Proposition 2.5
.

For fixed

one has

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathfrak{b}_{2n+1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})^{1/(2n+1)}=2g.

Proof.

Consider the functions
w_{1}
and

f\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{6}w_{1}^{3}-\frac{1}{2}w_{1}w_{2}+\frac{1}{3}w_{3}-w_{1}

on
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[0,\pi]^{g}
. The maximum value of
\lvert w_{1}\rvert
is attained at exactly two points in
X
,
namely the points
x\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(0,\ldots,0)
and
y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\pi,\ldots,\pi)
.
We have
w_{1}(x)=2g
and
w_{1}(y)=-2g
, and we also have

f(x)=(2/3)(2g^{3}-3g^{2}-2g)>0
and
f(y)=(-2/3)(2g^{3}-3g^{2}-2g)<0
.

Let
V
be the (open) subset of
X
where
w_{1}f>0
,
so that
x
and
y
both lie in
V
, and let

W=X\setminus V
. Let
M
be the supremum of
\lvert w_{1}\rvert
on
W
, so that
M<2g
.
For
\varepsilon\in(0,2g-M)
let
U_{\varepsilon}
be the subset of
X
where

\lvert w_{1}\rvert>2g-\varepsilon
, so that
U_{\varepsilon}\subset V
, and
let
V_{\varepsilon}=V\setminus U_{\varepsilon}
.

Then, for every
n
, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{2n+1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}w_{1}^{2n+1}f\,dm_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{U_{\varepsilon}}w_{1}^{2n+1}f\,dm_{g}+\int_{V_{\varepsilon}}w_{1}^{2n+1}f\,dm_{g}+\int_{W}w_{1}^{2n+1}f\,dm_{g}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{U_{\varepsilon}}w_{1}^{2n+1}f\,dm_{g}+\int_{W}w_{1}^{2n+1}f\,dm_{g}

\displaystyle\geq(2g-\varepsilon)^{2n+1}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}}\lvert f\rvert\,dm_{g}-M^{2n+1}\int_{W}\lvert f\rvert\,dm_{g},

where the third line follows from the fact that

in

and

and

and the rightmost factor tends to

as

\liminf\mathfrak{b}_{2n+1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})^{1/(2n+1)}\geq 2g.

We also have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{2n+1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})

\displaystyle=\int_{U_{\varepsilon}}w_{1}^{2n+1}f\,dm_{g}+\int_{X\setminus U_{\varepsilon}}w_{1}^{2n+1}f\,dm_{g}

\displaystyle\leq(2g)^{2n+1}\int_{U_{\varepsilon}}\lvert f\rvert\,dm_{g}+(2g-\varepsilon)^{2n+1}\int_{X\setminus U_{\varepsilon}}\lvert f\rvert\,dm_{g},

so if we let

Once again the rightmost factor tends to

as

, so

\limsup\mathfrak{b}_{2n+1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})^{1/(2n+1)}\leq 2g,

and the proposition is proven.

4.
The elliptic and hyperelliptic cases: results and experiments

Katz–Sarnak results show that for every interval
J\subseteq I_{g}
, the probability that a
random curve of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(or a random hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
) has
normalized trace in
J
tends towards a fixed value as
q\to\infty
, this value being

\int_{J}\Fd_{g}(\tau)\,d\tau
, where
\Fd_{g}
is the density function for the measure
\mu_{g}

defined at the beginning of Section
3
. Here the interval
J
is fixed, and
we let
q
tend to infinity. One can wonder how rapid this convergence is.
For instance, suppose the interval
J
has length
x
. How large must
q
become in order for
the actual probability that a normalized trace lies in
J
is well-approximated by the Katz–Sarnak prediction? Could it even be the case that the approximation is reasonably good when
q
is as large as
1/x^{2}
,
so that
x\approx 1/\sqrt{q}
and there is exactly one integer
t
with
t/\sqrt{q}\in J
?
In other words, can we use the Katz–Sarnak distribution to estimate the number of curves over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
with a given trace? Since the measures
\mu_{q,g}
converge weakly to
\mu_{g}
,
one might hope that for every
\tau\in I_{g}
,
the integral of
\mu_{q,g}
over an interval of length
1/\sqrt{q}
containing
\tau

would be close to the integral of
\mu_{g}
over this interval. If we let
t
be the unique integer
such that
t/\sqrt{q}
is contained in this interval, this optimistic approximation then translates to

\sqrt{q}\,\mathcal{N}_{q,g}\biggl(\frac{t}{\sqrt{q}}\biggr)\approx\Fd_{g}\biggl(\frac{t}{\sqrt{q}}\biggr).

Since
\mathcal{N}_{q,g}(t/\sqrt{q})

gives us the weighted number of curves with trace
t
, if this approximation is close to the truth
we would have a good estimate for the number of such curves.

Remark 4.1
.

We do not know how to prove that this estimate holds, and indeed we will
see below that it does
not
hold, without modification, for
hyperelliptic curves. One consequence of this estimate, however, is the
much weaker statement that for every fixed value of
t
, the value of

\mathcal{N}_{q,g}(t)
converges to
0
as
q
increases. It is at least easy to
show that this weaker statement holds for
t=0
, by the following argument.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
f\colon I_{g}\to[0,1]
be a continuous function
with
f(0)=1
and with
f(\tau)=0
when
\lvert\tau\rvert\geq\varepsilon
.
From (
3.1
) we find that for
q
large enough we have

\biggl\lvert\,\int_{I_{g}}f\,d\mu_{q,g}-\int_{I_{g}}f(\tau)\Fd_{g}(\tau)\,d\mu_{g}\biggr\rvert\leq\varepsilon.

Hence

As we intimated in the preceding remark, for hyperelliptic curves we can prove
that the naïve approximation for
\mathcal{N}_{q,g}
described above cannot hold.
To state our result precisely, we introduce a function
\mathcal{N}^{\textup{hyp}}_{q,g}(\tau)

which we define analogously to how we defined

:

\mathcal{N}^{\textup{hyp}}_{q,g}(\tau)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\tau(C)=\tau\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\#\Aut(C)}.

Here by
\mathcal{H}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we mean the set of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-isomorphism classes of hyperelliptic curves
of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and by
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
we mean the set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism classes
of such curves. Note that for an integer

in

, the value

is
then the weighted number of genus-

hyperelliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with trace

Proposition 4.2
.

Fix
g>1
and
\varepsilon\in[0,2g)
, let
r_{g}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i=0}^{2g+2}(-2)^{i}/i!
,
and let
v=\int_{2g-\varepsilon}^{2g}\Fd_{g}(\tau)\,d\tau
.
Suppose there are constants
b_{g}\leq c_{g}
such that for
every sufficiently large prime power
q
and for every integer
t
in

[-(2g-\varepsilon)\sqrt{q},(2g-\varepsilon)\sqrt{q}\,]
, we have

\frac{b_{g}}{\sqrt{q}}\Fd_{g}\biggl(\frac{t}{\sqrt{q}}\biggr)\leq\mathcal{N}^{\textup{hyp}}_{q,g}\biggl(\frac{t}{\sqrt{q}}\biggr)\leq\frac{c_{g}}{\sqrt{q}}\Fd_{g}\biggl(\frac{t}{\sqrt{q}}\biggr).

Then
b_{g}\leq(1-r_{g})/(1-2v)
and
c_{g}\geq(1+r_{g}-4v)/(1-2v)
.

The proof is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix
g>1
, and let
r_{g}
be as in Proposition
4.2
. If
q
is
an odd prime power then

\sum_{t\,\textup{even}}\mathcal{N}^{\textup{hyp}}_{q,g}\biggl(\frac{t}{\sqrt{q}}\biggr)=\frac{1+r_{g}}{2}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)\text{\hskip 10.22217ptand\hskip 10.22217pt}\sum_{t\,\textup{odd}}\mathcal{N}^{\textup{hyp}}_{q,g}\biggl(\frac{t}{\sqrt{q}}\biggr)=\frac{1-r_{g}}{2}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}\Bigr).

Proof.

Fix an odd prime power
q
, fix a nonsquare
n\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and consider the set
H

consisting of all pairs
(c,f)
, where
c\in\{1,n\}
and
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is a
monic separable polynomial of degree
2g+1
or
2g+2
. A result of
Carlitz
[
15
, §6]
shows that
\#H=2q^{2g+2}-2q^{2g}.

The group
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
acts on
H
: Given a matrix

[\begin{smallmatrix}r&s\\
t&u\end{smallmatrix}]
and an element
(c,f)
of
H
,
let
(d,g)
be the unique element of
H
such that

dg(x)=ce^{2}(tx+u)^{2g+2}f\Bigl(\frac{rx+s}{tx+u}\Bigr)

for some
e\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.
Note that the stabilizer of
(c,f)
is isomorphic to
the reduced automorphism group
\RedAut(C)
of the hyperelliptic curve
C\colon y^{2}=cf
, that is,
the quotient of the full automorphism group of
C
by the subgroup
generated by the hyperelliptic involution.

The map
\gamma
that sends
(c,f)\in H
to the hyperelliptic curve

y^{2}=cf
takes
H
onto
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Given a curve
C\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
let
(c,f)\in H
be such that
\gamma((c,f))=C
. Then

\#(\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cdot(c,f))=\frac{\#\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}{\#\RedAut(C)},

so that

(4.1)

\frac{\#\gamma^{-1}(C)}{\#\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}=\frac{1}{\#\RedAut(C)}=\frac{2}{\#\Aut(C)}.

Let
H_{\textup{even}}
be the subset of
H
consisting of the pairs
(c,f)
such that
the curve
\gamma(c,f)
has even trace. Let
H^{\prime}_{\textup{even}}
be the subset of
H

consisting of the pairs
(c,f)
such that
f
has degree
2g+2
and has an even
number of roots. Then
H^{\prime}_{\textup{even}}\subseteq H_{\textup{even}}
, and

H_{\textup{even}}\setminus H^{\prime}_{\textup{even}}
consists of pairs
(c,f)\in H_{\textup{even}}
such that
f

has degree
2g+1
. Therefore

\bigl|\#H_{\textup{even}}-\#H^{\prime}_{\textup{even}}\bigr|\leq 2q^{2g+1}.

Leont
′
ev
[
33
, Lem. 4, p. 302]
gives the generating
function for the number of (not necessarily separable) monic polynomials of a
fixed degree over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that have a given number of roots. To find the number
of such polynomials with an even number of roots, we simply need to take the
average of the values of this generating function evaluated at
-1
and at
1
.
We find that

\#\left\{\vbox{\noindent monic polynomials of degree $2g+2$\\
over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$ with an even number of roots}\right\}=\frac{1+r_{g}}{2}q^{2g+2}+O(q^{2g+1}).

The result of Carlitz mentioned earlier shows that

\#\left\{\vbox{\noindent non-separable monic polynomials\\
of degree $2g+2$ over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$}\right\}=q^{2g+1}.

Therefore
\#H^{\prime}_{\textup{even}}=(1+r_{g})q^{2g+2}+O(q^{2g+1})
, so that

\#H_{\textup{even}}=(1+r_{g})q^{2g+2}+O(q^{2g+1})
as well.

Using (
4.1
) we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{t\,\textup{even}}\mathcal{N}^{\textup{hyp}}_{q,g}\biggl(\frac{t}{\sqrt{q}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\Tr(C)\,\textup{even}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(C)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{H}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\Tr(C)\,\textup{even}\end{subarray}}\frac{\#\gamma^{-1}(C)}{2\#\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\#\mathcal{H}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\#\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\#H_{\textup{even}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2q^{2g-1}(q^{3}-q)}\bigl((1+r_{g})q^{2g+2}+O(q^{2g+1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+r_{g}}{2}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{q}\Bigr).

This gives us the first equality in the conclusion of the lemma. The second
follows analogously.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

For every
c>0
, there are infinitely many values of

and

such that

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{0}
be a fundamental quadratic discriminant with
\Delta_{0}<-4

and let
\chi
be the quadratic character modulo
\Delta_{0}
. For a
given value of
n

f
be the product of the first
n

p

that are inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta_{0}})
.
Since the product over all inert primes of

diverges
(see
[

[

Choose

be a prime of the form

, where

and

, viewed as

q_{0}
,

\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}

be the argument of

and let
m
be the smallest integer

.

, and

. Then

. We have

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let

, for all

and for all large enough

, there exists

Another way to phrase this conjecture is to replace the measure

by a measure with density given by the histogram with height

. This is stronger than weak convergence of the measures

]
.

We now conclude by looking at the symmetry breaking for the trace distribution of (non-hyperelliptic) genus 3 curves. In general, if
C
is a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with trace
t
, then its quadratic twist for the hyperelliptic involution has trace
-t
and therefore the distribution of the number of hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
as a function of their trace is symmetric. For non-hyperelliptic curves, the distribution has no reason to be symmetric anymore. Actually, if a principally polarized abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the Jacobian (over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
) of a non-hyperelliptic curve, then its quadratic twist is never a Jacobian. This obstruction, known as
Serre’s obstruction
, is a huge obstacle to finding a closed formula for the maximal number of rational points for
g=3

[
32
]
, whereas such formulas are known for
g=1

[
21
]
and
g=2

[
43
]
. Although we cannot improve on the state-of-art of this question, we can study this asymmetry with the probabilistic angle and the results we got before.

To visualize this asymmetry, let us consider the signed measure
\nu_{q,g}=\mu_{q,g}-(-1)^{*}\mu_{q,g}
where
(-1)^{*}\mu_{q,g}
is the discrete image signed measure defined by

(-1)^{*}\mu_{q,g}=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{M}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\frac{\delta_{-\tau(C)}}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(C)}.

We get the following consequence of Theorem
2.3
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The sequence of signed measures
(\nu_{q,g})
weakly converges to the
0
measure.

Proof.

By definition, the even moments of
\nu_{q,g}
are zero. By Theorem
2.3
the odd moments of
\sqrt{q}\,\nu_{q,g}
are equal to

2\frac{S_{n}(q,\mathcal{M}_{g})}{q^{3g-3+(n-1)/2}}=-2_{n}(\mathcal{M}_{g})+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\right).

Hence all moments of
\nu_{q,g}
are
0
.
Now if
f
is any continuous function on the compact interval
I_{g}=[-2g,2g]
, then by the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, for every
\varepsilon>0
we can find a polynomial
P
such that
\lvert f(\tau)-P(\tau)\rvert\leq\varepsilon
for all
\tau\in I_{g}
. Therefore we have

\biggl\lvert\int_{I_{g}}f\,d\nu_{q,g}\biggr\rvert\leq\biggl\lvert\int_{I_{g}}(f-P)\,d\nu_{q,g}+\int_{I_{g}}P\,d\nu_{q,g}\biggr\rvert\leq\varepsilon\|\nu_{q,g}\|+\biggl\lvert\int_{I_{g}}P\,d\nu_{q,g}\biggr\rvert.

The last term is a sum of moments which converges to
0
when
q
goes to infinity. The variation of
\nu_{g,q}
is also uniformly bounded since

\|\nu_{q,g}\|=\lvert\nu_{q,g}\rvert(I_{g})=\sum_{\tau}\,\Bigl\lvert\mathcal{N}_{q,g}(\tau)-\mathcal{N}_{q,g}(-\tau)\Bigr\rvert\leq 2\sum_{\tau}\mathcal{N}_{q,g}(\tau)=2\mu_{q,g}(I_{g})=2.

∎

Having a
0
measure is not very interesting and the proof of Proposition
5.2
shows that it would be much more interesting to study the weak convergence of the sequence of signed measures
(\sqrt{q}\,\nu_{q,g})
. We have from the previous proof the following corollary.

Corollary 5.3
.

The even moments of
\sqrt{q}\,\nu_{q,g}
are zero and the odd
n
th moments of the sequence
(\sqrt{q}\,\nu_{q,g})
converge to
-2_{n}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
.

Unfortunately we cannot prove weak convergence: the rest of the proof fails as we do not know if one can bound
\sqrt{q}\,\|\nu_{q,g}\|
uniformly in
q
(which is a necessary condition for weak convergence). Moreover, one cannot expect a general result from the convergence of moments alone as in the case of (positive) measures as the following counterexample shows.

Example 5.4
.

Consider the sequence of signed measures
(\mu_{i})
with density
i\sin ix
on the interval
[0,2\pi]
. The sequence of
n
th moments converges to
-(2\pi)^{n}
which is the
n
th moment of the signed measure
\mu=-\delta_{2\pi}
. But
\|\mu_{i}\|=4i
which is not bounded and therefore the sequence
(\mu_{i})
does not weakly converge (to
\mu
); see for instance
[
14
, Prop. 1.4.7]
.

Recall from (
3.3
) that the
n
th moment of the function

\Hd_{g}(\tau)=\int_{A_{\tau}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{6}w_{1}^{3}-\frac{1}{2}w_{1}w_{2}+\frac{1}{3}w_{3}-w_{1}\Bigr)\,dm_{g},

with
A_{\tau}=\{(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{g})\in[0,\pi]^{g}:\,\sum_{j}2\cos\theta_{j}=\tau\}
, is
equal to
{}_{n}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
.
Because of the convergence of the moments above, we conjecture the following.

Conjecture 5.5
.

For
g\geq 3
, the sequence of signed measures
(\sqrt{q}\,\nu_{q,g})
weakly converges to the continuous signed measure with density
-2\Hd_{g}
.

Such a result would for instance imply that
\sqrt{q}\,\|\nu_{q,g}\|
is uniformly bounded, hence there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
q
and all
\tau=t/\sqrt{q}
, we have
\lvert\mathcal{N}_{q,g}(\tau)-\mathcal{N}_{q,g}(-\tau)\rvert\leq C/\sqrt{q}
.

In genus
3

4

-2\Hd_{3}(t/\sqrt{q})

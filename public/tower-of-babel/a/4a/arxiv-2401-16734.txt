Besov and Paley-Wiener spaces, Moduli of continuity and Hardy-Steklov operators associated with the group " ​ a ​ x + b ​ " "ax+b"
Isaac Z. Pesenson

Abstract
. We introduce and describe relations between Sobolev, Besov and Paley-Wiener spaces associated with three representations of the Lie group G G of affine transformations of the line, also known as the " ​ a ​ x + b ​ ” "ax+b” group. These representations are: left and right regular representations and a representation in a space of functions defined on the half-line. The Besov spaces are described as interpolation spaces between respective Sobolev spaces in terms of the K K -functional and in terms of a relevant moduli of continuity. By using a Laplace operators associated with these representations a scales of relevant Paley-Wiener spaces are developed and a corresponding L 2 L_{2} -approximation theory is constructed in which our Besov spaces appear as approximation spaces. Another description of our Besov spaces is given in terms of a frequency-localized Hilbert frames. A Jackson-type inequalities are also proven.

2.1.
The group
"ax+b"

The group
G
of all linear transformations of
\mathbb{R}
preserving orientation (also known as the ”
ax+b,\>a>0
, group”) is isomorphic to the group of matrices of the form

g(a,b)=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr a&b\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right),\>\>\>a>0,\>\>g(a,b)\in G.

Every such matrix can be considered as a linear transformation of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by the formula
y=ax+b
and if one has a transformation
y=a_{1}x+b_{1}
followed by a transformation
z=a_{2}y+b_{2}
then the resulting transformation is
x=a_{1}a_{2}x+a_{2}b_{1}+b_{2}
. In other words,

g(a_{2},b_{2})g(a_{1},b_{1})=g(a_{1}a_{2},\>a_{2}b_{1}+b_{2}).

Consider one-parameter subgroups

(2.1)

\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr e^{x}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right)\right\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}},\>\>\>\>\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 1&x\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right)\right\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}},

The matrices

(2.2)

X_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 1&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right),\>\>\>X_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr 0&0\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right),

represent Lie algebra elements which are tangent to the above subgroups at the identity
e\in G
. It means that for
x\in\mathbb{R}

\exp xX_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr e^{x}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right),\>\>\>\>\exp xX_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 1&x\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right).

In general, by using the following properties

\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 1&\alpha\\
\hline\cr 0&0\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right)^{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 1&\alpha\\
\hline\cr 0&0\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right),\>\>\>\>n\in\mathbb{N},

\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr 0&0\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right)^{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right),\>\>\>\>n>1,

one obtains for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}

\exp(x_{1}X_{1}+x_{2}X_{2})=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr e^{x_{1}}&\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}(e^{x_{1}}-1)\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right)

In other words, the map

Exp:(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto\exp(x_{1}X_{1}+x_{2}X_{2}),\>\>(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

is a coordinate system in a neighborhood of
e\in G
.
One can also consider another coordinate system around identity
e\in G

\varphi:(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto\exp(x_{1}X_{1})\exp(x_{2}X_{2}),\>\>(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Indeed, since

(2.3)

\exp(x_{1}X_{1})\exp(x_{2}X_{2})=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr e^{x_{1}}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr 1&x_{2}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr e^{x_{1}}&x_{2}e^{x_{1}}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right),

one can see that any given element of the group
G

\left(\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr a&b\\
\hline\cr 0&1\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\right)\in G,\>\>\>a>0,\>b\in\mathbb{R},

can be written as

\exp\left(\ln a\>X_{1}\right)\exp\left(\frac{b}{a}\>X_{2}\right).

We also notice the following obvious relation

(2.4)

\left[X_{1},X_{2}\right]=X_{1}X_{2}-X_{2}X_{1}=X_{2}.

The group
G
can be identified with the right half-plane

(2.5)

\left\{(a,b)\>|\>a\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\>b\in\mathbb{R}\right\}=(0,\>\infty)\times\mathbb{R},

equipped with the group operation

(2.6)

(a,b)(c,d)=(ac,\>ad+b).

In a such realization of

where

The group

2.2.
Representations

Let us remind that a strongly continuous representation of a Lie group

\mathbf{E}

g\mapsto T(g),\>\>\>g\in\Gamma,\>\>\>T(g)\in GL(\mathbf{E})

is continuous with respect to

\|f\|^{{}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{E}}=\sup_{g\in G}\|T(g)f\|_{\mathbf{E}},\>\>f\in\mathbf{E},
in which
\|T(g)f\|^{{}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{E}}\leq\|f\|^{{}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{E}}
. Thus, without any restriction we will assume that the last inequality is satisfied in the original norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{E}}
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2}
be the matrices in (
2.2
) which form a basis of the Lie algebra
\mathbf{g}
of the group
G
.
With each

one associates a strongly continuous one-parameter group of isometries

which will be also denoted as

The generator of the group

and the

of operators

that the common domain of these operators contains the so-called Garding space

\int_{G}\varphi(g)T(g)fd\mu_{l},\>\>f\in{\bf L}^{p}(G,d\mu_{l}),\>\>\>\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(G).

The set

is invariant with respect to operators

for all
g\in G
, and with respect to all polynomials in

and
A_{2}
.

It will be convenient identify the
"ax+b"

(a_{1},b_{2})(a_{2},b_{2})=(a_{1}a_{2},a_{1}b_{2}+b_{1}),\>\>\>(a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2})\in G.

In these notations the subgroups in (

Proof.

7.1.
Partitions of unity on the frequency side

We keep the notations from the previous sections and assume that
T
is a unitary representation of the
"ax+b"
group
G
in a Hilbert space
{\bf H}
. As it was mentioned above, the corresponding Laplace operator

\Delta=-\mathbb{A}_{1}^{2}-\mathbb{A}_{2}^{2}

is self-adjoint and non-negative.

The construction of frequency-localized frames is achieved via spectral calculus. The idea is to start from a partition of unity on the positive real axis. In the following, we will be considering two different types of such partitions, whose construction we now describe in some detail. The construction below was described in
[
7
]
.

Let
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be a non-increasing
function such that
supp(g)\subset[0,\>2],
and
g(\lambda)=1
for
\lambda\in[0,\>1],\>0\leq g(\lambda)\leq 1,\>\lambda>0.

We now let

h(\lambda)=g(\lambda)-g(2\lambda)~,
which entails
supp(h)\subset[2^{-1},2]
,
and use this to define

F_{0}(\lambda)=\sqrt{g(\lambda)}~,F_{j}(\lambda)=\sqrt{h(2^{-j}\lambda)}~,j\geq 1~,

as well as
Q_{j}(\lambda)=\left[F_{j}(\lambda)\right]^{2}=F_{j}^{2}(\lambda)~,j\geq 0~.

As a result of the definitions, we get for all
\lambda\geq 0
the equations

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{j}(\lambda)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}F_{j}^{2}(\lambda)=g(2^{-n}\lambda),

and as a consequence

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{j}(\lambda)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}F_{j}^{2}(\lambda)=1~,\>\>\>\lambda\geq 0,

with finitely many nonzero terms occurring in the sums for each
fixed
\lambda
. We call the sequence
(Q_{j})_{j\geq 0}
a
(dyadic) partition of unity
, and
(F_{j})_{j\geq 0}
a
quadratic (dyadic) partition of unity
. As will become soon apparent, quadratic partitions are useful for the construction of frames.
Using the spectral theorem one has

F_{j}^{2}(\Delta)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(F_{j}^{2}(\lambda)\mathcal{F}f(\lambda)\right),\>\>\>j\geq 1,

and thus

(7.1)

f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathcal{F}f(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}F_{j}^{2}(\lambda)\mathcal{F}f(\lambda)\right)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}F_{j}^{2}(\Delta)f

Taking inner product with
f

\|F_{j}(\Delta)f\|^{2}_{{\bf H}}=\langle F_{j}^{2}(\Delta)f,f\rangle,

and

(7.2)

\|f\|_{{\bf H}}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\langle F_{j}^{2}(\Delta)f,f\rangle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|F_{j}(\Delta)f\|_{{\bf H}}^{2}.

Similarly, we get the identity
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{j}(\Delta)f=f~.

Moreover, since the functions
Q_{j},F_{j}
, have their supports in
[2^{j-1},\>\>2^{j+1}]
, the elements

and

are bandlimited to
[2^{j-1},\>\>2^{j+1}]

∎

Theorem 8.3
.

For
\alpha>0,\>\>\>1\leq q\leq\infty,
the norm of

\mathbf{B}_{q}^{\alpha}

is equivalent to

(8.4)

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{j\alpha q}\left(\sum_{k}\left|\left<f,\Phi^{j}_{k}\right>\right|^{2}\right)^{q/2}\right)^{1/q}\asymp\|f\|_{\mathbf{B}_{q}^{\alpha}},

with the standard modifications for

.

Proof.

For
f\in{\bf H}
and the operator
F_{j}(\Delta)

we have

(8.5)

a\left\|F_{j}(\Delta)f\right\|_{{\bf H}}^{2}\leq\sum_{k}\left|\left<F_{j}(\Delta)f,\phi^{j}_{k}\right>\right|^{2}\leq b\left\|F_{j}(\Delta)f\right\|_{\bf H}^{2},

and then
we obtain the following inequality

\sum_{k}\left|\left<f,\Phi^{j}_{k}\right>\right|^{2}\leq\left\|F_{j}(\Delta)f\right\|_{{\bf H}}^{2}\leq a^{-1}\sum_{k}\left|\left<f,\Phi^{j}_{k}\right>\right|^{2},\>\>f\in{\bf H}.

Theorem is proven.

The goal of the section is to introduce basic notions of the theory of interpolation spaces
[
5
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
10
]
,
[

]
, and approximation spaces
[
2
]
,
[
11
]
,
[

]
.
It is important to realize that the relations between
interpolation and approximation spaces cannot be described
in the language of normed spaces. We have to make use of
quasi-normed linear spaces in order to treat them
simultaneously.

A quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\bf A}
on a linear space
\bf A
is
a real-valued function on
\bf E
such that for any

f,f_{1},f_{2}\in{\bf A}
the following holds true:

(1)

\|f\|_{\bf A}\geq 0;\>\>\>

(2)

\|f\|_{\bf A}=0\Longleftrightarrow f=0;\>\>\>

(3)

\|-f\|_{\bf A}=\|f\|_{\bf A};\>\>\>

(4)

there exists some
C_{{\bf A}}\geq 1
such that

\|f_{1}+f_{2}\|_{{\bf A}}\leq C_{{\bf A}}(\|f_{1}\|_{{\bf A}}+\|f_{2}\|_{{\bf A}}).\>\>

Two quasi-normed linear spaces
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
form a
pair if they are linear subspaces of a common linear space

\mathcal{A}
and the conditions

\|f_{k}-g\|_{{\bf A}}\rightarrow 0,
and

\|f_{k}-h\|_{{\bf B}}\rightarrow 0

imply equality
g=h
(in
\mathcal{A}
).
For any such pair
{\bf A},{\bf B}
one can construct the
space
{\bf A}\cap{\bf B}
with quasi-norm

\|f\|_{{\bf A}\cap{\bf B}}=\max\left(\|f\|_{{\bf A}},\|f\|_{{\bf B}}\right)

and the sum of the spaces,
{\bf A}+{\bf B}
consisting of all sums
f_{0}+f_{1}
with
f_{0}\in{\bf A},f_{1}\in{\bf B}
, and endowed with the quasi-norm

\|f\|_{{\bf A}+{\bf B}}=\inf_{f=f_{0}+f_{1},f_{0}\in{\bf A},f_{1}\in{\bf B}}\left(\|f_{0}\|_{{\bf A}}+\|f_{1}\|_{{\bf B}}\right).

Quasi-normed spaces
{\bf J}
with

{\bf A}\cap{\bf B}\subset{\bf J}\subset{\bf A}+{\bf B}

are called intermediate between
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
.
If both
E
and
F
are complete the
inclusion mappings are automatically continuous. An additive homomorphism
T:{\bf A}\rightarrow{\bf B}

is called bounded if

\|T\|=\sup_{f\in{\bf A},f\neq 0}\|Tf\|_{{\bf B}}/\|f\|_{{\bf A}}<\infty.

An intermediate quasi-normed linear space
{\bf J}

interpolates between
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
if every bounded homomorphism
T:{\bf A}+{\bf B}\rightarrow{\bf A}+{\bf B}

which is a bounded homomorphism of
{\bf A}
into

{\bf A}
and a bounded homomorphism of
{\bf B}
into
{\bf B}

is also a bounded homomorphism of
{\bf J}
into
{\bf J}
.
On
{\bf A}+{\bf B}
one considers the so-called Peetre’s
K
-functional

K(f,t)=K(f,t,{\bf A},{\bf B})=\inf_{f=f_{0}+f_{1},f_{0}\in{\bf A},f_{1}\in{\bf B}}\left(\|f_{0}\|_{{\bf A}}+t\|f_{1}\|_{{\bf B}}\right).

The quasi-normed linear space
({\bf A},{\bf B})^{K}_{\theta,q}
,
with parameters
0<\theta<1,\,0<q\leq\infty
, or
0\leq\theta\leq 1,\,q=\infty
,
is introduced as the set of elements
f
in
{\bf A}+{\bf B}
for which

(9.1)

\|f\|_{\theta,q}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{-}K(f,t)\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

It turns out that
({\bf A},{\bf B})^{K}_{\theta,q}

with the quasi-norm
(
9.1
) interpolates between
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
.

Let us introduce another functional on
{\bf A}+{\bf B}
,
where
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
form a pair of quasi-normed linear spaces

\mathcal{E}(f,t)=\mathcal{E}(f,t,\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F})=\inf_{g\in{\bf B},\|g\|_{{\bf B}}\leq t}\|f-g\|_{{\bf A}}.

Definition 8
.

The approximation space
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,q}({\bf A},{\bf B}),0<\alpha<\infty,0<q\leq\infty
is the quasi-normed linear spaces
of all
f\in{\bf A}+{\bf B}
for which the quasi-norm

(9.2)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,q}({\bf A},{\bf B})}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(f,t)\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q}

is finite.

The next two theorems represent a very abstract version of what is known as a Direct and an Inverse Approximation Theorems
[
17
,
3
]
. In the form it is stated below they were proved in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}\subset{\bf B}\subset{\bf A}
are quasi-normed
linear spaces and
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
are complete.
If there exist
C>0
and
\beta>0
such that
the following Jackson-type inequality is satisfied

t^{\beta}\mathcal{E}(t,f,\mathcal{T},{\bf A})\leq C\|f\|_{{\bf B}},\>\>t>0,\>\>f\in{\bf B},

then the following embedding holds true

(9.3)

({\bf A},{\bf B})^{K}_{\theta,q}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\theta\beta,q}({\bf A},\mathcal{T}),\quad\>0<\theta<1,\>0<q\leq\infty.

Theorem 9.2
.

If there exist
C>0
and
\beta>0

such that
the following Bernstein-type inequality holds

\|f\|_{{\bf B}}\leq C\|f\|^{\beta}_{\mathcal{T}}\|f\|_{{\bf A}},\>\>f\in\mathcal{T},

then the following embedding holds true

(9.4)

\mathcal{E}_{\theta\beta,q}({\bf A},\mathcal{T})\subset({\bf A},{\bf B})^{K}_{\theta,q},\quad 0<\theta<1,\>0<q\leq\infty.

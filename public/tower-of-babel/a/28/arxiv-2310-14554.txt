Making RL with Preference-based Feedback Efficient via Randomization
Runzhe Wu, Wen Sun

Abstract
Reinforcement Learning algorithms that learn from human feedback (RLHF) need to be efficient in terms of statistical complexity, computational complexity, and query complexity . In this work, we consider the RLHF setting where the feedback is given in the format of preferences over pairs of trajectories. In the linear MDP model, using randomization in algorithm design, we present an algorithm that is sample efficient (i.e., has near-optimal worst-case regret bounds) and has polynomial running time (i.e., computational complexity is polynomial with respect to relevant parameters). Our algorithm further minimizes the query complexity through a novel randomized active learning procedure. In particular, our algorithm demonstrates a near-optimal tradeoff between the regret bound and the query complexity. To extend the results to more general nonlinear function approximation, we design a model-based randomized algorithm inspired by the idea of Thompson sampling. Our algorithm minimizes Bayesian regret bound and query complexity, again achieving a near-optimal tradeoff between these two quantities. Computation-wise, similar to the prior Thompson sampling algorithms under the regular RL setting, the main computation primitives of our algorithm are Bayesian supervised learning oracles which have been heavily investigated on the empirical side when applying Thompson sampling algorithms to RL benchmark problems.



1
Introduction

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) has been widely used across various domains, including robotics
(
Jain et al. 2013
;
Jain et al. 2015
)
and natural language processing
(
Stiennon et al. 2020
;
Ouyang et al. 2022
)
. Unlike standard RL, RLHF requires the agent to learn from feedback in the format of preferences between pairs of trajectories instead of per-step reward since assigning a dense reward function for each state is challenging in many tasks. For instance, in natural language generation, rating each generated token individually is challenging. Hence, it is more realistic to ask humans to compare two pieces of text and indicate their preference. Recent works have shown that, by integrating preference-based feedback into the training process, we can align models with human intention and enable high-quality human-machine interaction.

Despite the existing empirical applications of RLHF, its theoretical foundation remains far from satisfactory. Empirically, researchers first learn reward models from preference-based feedback and then optimize the reward models via policy gradient-based algorithms such as PPO
(
Schulman et al. 2017
)
. Questions such as
whether or not the learned reward model is accurate, whether PPO is sufficient for deep exploration, and how to strategically collect more feedback on the fly are often ignored. Theoretically, prior works study the regret bound for RL with preference-based feedback
(
Saha et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2022
)
. Despite achieving sublinear worst-case regret, these algorithms are computationally intractable even for simplified models such as tabular Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). This means that we cannot easily leverage the algorithmic ideas in prior work to guide or improve how we perform RLHF in practice.

In addition to maximizing reward, another important metric in RLHF is the query complexity since human feedback is expensive to collect. To illustrate, we note that InstructGPT’s training data comprises a mere 30K instances of human feedback
(
Ouyang et al. 2022
)
, which is significantly fewer than the internet-scale dataset for training the GPT-3 base model. This underscores the challenge of scaling up the size of human feedback datasets.
Ross et al. 2013
;
Laskey et al. 2016
also pointed out that
extensively querying for feedback puts too much burden on human experts. Empirically,
Lightman et al. 2023
observes that active learning reduces query complexity and improves the learned reward model. In theory, query complexity is mostly studied in the settings of active learning, online learning, and bandits
(
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2005
;
Dekel et al. 2012
;
Agarwal 2013
;
Hanneke & Yang 2021
;
Zhu & Nowak 2022
;
Sekhari et al. 2023a
;
Sekhari et al. 2023b
)
, but overlooked in RL.

In this work,
we aim to design new RL algorithms that can learn from preference-based feedback and can be efficient in statistical complexity (i.e., regret), computational complexity, and query complexity
. In particular, we strike a near-optimal balance between regret minimization and query complexity minimization. To achieve this goal, our key idea is to use
randomization
in algorithm design. We summarize our new algorithmic ideas and key contributions as follows.

1.

For MDPs with linear structure (i.e., linear MDP
(
Jin et al. 2020
)
), we propose the first RL algorithm that achieves sublinear worst-case regret and computational efficiency simultaneously with preference-based feedback. Even when reduced to tabular MDPs, it is still the first to achieve a no-regret guarantee and computational efficiency. Moreover, it has an active learning procedure and attains a near-optimal tradeoff between the regret and the query complexity. Our algorithm adds
random Gaussian noises
to the learned state-action-wise reward model and the least-squares value iteration (LSVI) procedure. Using random noise instead of the UCB-style technique
(
Azar et al. 2017
)
preserves the Markovian property in the reward model and allows one to use dynamic programming to achieve computation efficiency.

2.

For function approximation beyond linear, we present a model-based Thompson-sampling (TS) algorithm that forms posterior distributions over the transitions and reward models. Assuming the transition and the reward model class both have small
\ell_{1}
-norm eluder dimension
– a structural condition introduced in
Liu et al. 2022a
that is more general than the common
\ell_{2}
-norm eluder dimension
(
Russo & Van Roy 2013
)
, we show that our algorithm again achieves a near-optimal tradeoff between the Bayesian regret and the Bayesian query complexity. Computation-wise, similar to previous TS algorithms for regular RL (e.g.,
Osband et al. 2013
), the primary computation primitives are Bayesian supervised learning oracles for transition and reward learning.

3.

Our query conditions for both algorithms are based on variance-style uncertainty quantification of the preference induced by the randomness of the reward model. We query for preference feedback only when the uncertainty of the preference on a pair of trajectories is large. Approximately computing the uncertainty can be easily done using i.i.d. random reward models drawn from the reward model distribution, which makes the active query procedure computationally tractable.

Overall, while our main contribution is on the theoretical side, our theoretical investigation provides several new practical insights. For instance, for regret minimization, our algorithms propose to draw a pair of trajectories with one from the latest policy and the other from an older policy instead of drawing two trajectories from the same policy (e.g.,
Christiano et al. 2017
), avoiding the situation of drawing two similar trajectories when the policy becomes more and more deterministic.
Our theory shows that drawing two trajectories from a combination of new and older policies balances exploration and exploitation better. Another practical insight is the variance-style uncertainty measure for designing the query condition. Compared to more standard active learning procedure that relies on constructing version space and confidence intervals
(
Dekel et al. 2012
;
Puchkin & Zhivotovskiy 2021
;
Zhu & Nowak 2022
;
Sekhari et al. 2023a
;
Sekhari et al. 2023b
)
, our new approach comes with strong theoretical guarantees and is more computationally tractable. It is also amenable to existing implementations of Thompson sampling RL algorithms (e.g., using bootstrapping to approximate the posterior sampling
(
Osband et al. 2016a
;
Osband et al. 2023
)
).

Comparison to prior work

RL with preference-based feedback.

Many recent works have obtained statistically efficient algorithms but are computationally inefficient even for tabular MDPs due to intractable policy search and version space construction
(
Chen et al. 2022
;
Zhan et al. 2023a
;
Zhan et al. 2023b
;
Saha et al. 2023
)
. For example,

Zhan et al. 2023b
;
Saha et al. 2023
use the idea from optimal design and rely on the computation oracle:
\mathop{\arg\max}_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi}\|\mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim\pi}\phi(s,a)-\mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim\pi^{\prime}}\phi(s,a)\|_{A}
with some positive definite matrix
A
. Here
\|x\|^{2}_{A}:=x^{\top}Ax
, and
\phi
is some state-action feature.

1

These works typically assume trajectory-wise feature
\phi(\tau)
for a trajectory
\tau
. However, even when specified to state-action-wise features, these algorithms are still computationally intractable, even in tabular MDPs.
It is unclear how to implement this oracle since standard planning approaches based on dynamic programming cannot be applied here. In addition, these methods also actively maintain a policy space by eliminating potentially sub-optimal policies. The policy class can be exponentially large even in tabular settings, so how to maintain it computationally efficiently is unclear. We provide a more detailed discussion on the challenges in achieving computational efficiency in RLHF in
Appendix

A
.

While the work mentioned above is intractable even for tabular MDPs, there are some other works that could be computationally efficient but have weaker statistical results. For instance, very recently,
Wang et al. 2023
proposed a reduction framework that can be computationally efficient (depending on the base algorithm used in the reduction). However, their algorithms have PAC bounds while we focus on regret minimization.
Moreover, we achieve a near-optimal balance between regret and query complexity.

Novoseller et al. 2020
proposed a posterior sampling algorithm for tabular MDP but their analysis is asymptotic (i.e., they do not address exploration, exploitation, and query complexity tradeoff).

Xu et al. 2020
proposed efficient algorithms that do reward-free exploration. However, it is limited to tabular MDPs and PAC bounds.

In contrast to the above works, our algorithms aim to achieve efficiency in statistical, computational, and query complexities simultaneously.
Our algorithms leverage
randomization
to balance exploration, exploitation, and feedback query. Randomization allows us to avoid non-standard computational oracles and only use standard Dynamic Programming (DP) based oracles (e.g., value iteration), which makes our algorithm computationally more tractable. Prior works that simultaneously achieve efficiency in all three aspects are often restricted in the bandit and imitation learning settings where the exploration problem is much easier
(
Sekhari et al. 2023a
)
.

RL via randomization.
There are two lines of work that study RL via randomization. The first injects random noise into the learning object to encourage exploration. A typical example is the randomized least-squares value iteration (RLSVI)
(
Osband et al. 2016b
)
, which adds Gaussian noise into the least-squares estimation and achieves near-optimal worst-case regret
(
Zanette et al. 2020
;
Agrawal et al. 2021
)
for linear MDPs. The other line of work is Bayesian RL and uses Thompson sampling (TS)
(
Osband et al. 2013
;
Osband & Van Roy 2014b
;
Osband & Van Roy 2014a
;
Gopalan & Mannor 2015
;
Agrawal & Jia 2017
;
Efroni et al. 2021
;
Zhong et al. 2022
;
Agarwal & Zhang 2022
)
. They achieve provable Bayesian regret upper bound by maintaining posterior distributions over models.

Active learning.

Numerous studies have studied active learning across various settings
(
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2005
;
Dekel et al. 2012
;
Agarwal 2013
;
Hanneke & Yang 2015
;
Hanneke & Yang 2021
;
Zhu & Nowak 2022
;
Sekhari et al. 2023b
;
Sekhari et al. 2023a
)
. However, most of them focus on the bandits and online learning settings, and their active learning procedures are usually computationally intractable due to computing version spaces or upper and lower confidence bounds. In contrast,
we design a variance-style uncertainty quantification for our query condition, which can be easily estimated by random samples of reward model. This makes our active learning procedure more computationally tractable.

3
Preliminary

Notations.
For two real numbers
a
and
b
, we denote
[a,b]\coloneqq\{x:a\leq x\leq b\}
. For an integer
N
, we denote
[N]:=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
. For a set
\mathcal{S}
, we denote
\Delta(\mathcal{S})
as the set of distributions over
\mathcal{S}
. Let
d_{{\rm{TV}}}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the total variation distance.

We consider a finite-horizon Markov decision process (MDP), which is a tuple
M(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},r^{\star},P^{\star},H)
where
\mathcal{S}
is the state space,
\mathcal{A}
is the action space,
P^{\star}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\Delta(\mathcal{S})
is the transition kernel,
r^{\star}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow[0,1]
is the reward function, and
H
is the length of the episode.
The interaction proceeds for
T
rounds. At each round
t\in[T]
, we need to select two policies
\pi_{t}^{0}
and
\pi_{t}^{1}
and execute them separately, which generates two trajectories
\tau_{t}^{0}
and
\tau_{t}^{1}
where
\tau_{t}^{i}=(s_{t,1}^{i},a_{t,1}^{i},\dots,s_{t,H}^{i},a_{t,H}^{i})
for
i\in\{0,1\}
. For the ease of notation, we assume a fixed initial state
s_{1}
. Then, we need to decide whether to make a query for the preference between
\tau_{t}^{0}
and
\tau_{t}^{1}
. If making a query, we obtain a preference feedback
o_{t}\in\{0,1\}
that is sampled from the Bernoulli distribution:

\displaystyle\Pr(o_{t}=1\,|\,\tau_{t}^{1},\tau_{t}^{0},r^{\star})=\Pr(\tau_{t}^{1}\text{ is preferred to }\tau_{t}^{0}\,|\,r^{\star})=\Phi\big(r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0})\big)

where
r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{i})=\sum_{h=1}^{H}r^{\star}(s_{t,h}^{i},a_{t,h}^{i})
for
i\in\{0,1\}
is the trajectory reward, and
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
is a monotonically increasing link function. We note that, by symmetry,
we have
\Phi(r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1}))+\Phi(r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0}))=1
. If not making a query, we receive no feedback.

This feedback model is weaker than the standard RL where the per-step reward signal is revealed. We impose the following assumption on the link function
\Phi
, which has appeared in many existing works of RLHF
(
Saha et al. 2023
;
Zhu et al. 2023
;
Zhan et al. 2023a
)
.

Assumption 3.1
.

We assume
\Phi
is differentiable and there exists constants
\kappa,\overline{\kappa}>0
such that
\kappa^{-1}\leq\Phi^{\prime}(x)\leq\overline{\kappa}^{-1}
for any
x\in[-H,H]
.

The constants
\kappa
and
\overline{\kappa}
characterize the non-linearity of
\Phi
and determine the difficulty of estimating the reward from preference feedback.
It is noteworthy that, in the theoretical results of our algorithms, the bounds depend polynomially on
\kappa
but logarithmically on
\overline{\kappa}
.
Some typical examples of the link functions are provided below.

Example 3.2

(Link functions)
.

It is common to have
\Phi(x)=1/(1+\exp(-x))
, which recovers the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) model
(
Bradley & Terry 1952
)
, and we have
\kappa=2+\exp(-H)+\exp(H)
and
\overline{\kappa}=4
.
Additionally, if the trajectory-wise reward is scaled within the interval of
[0,1]
, then the difference in reward will be within the range of
[-1,1]
. In this case, another common choice of the link function is
\Phi(x)=(x+1)/2
, which results in
\kappa=\overline{\kappa}=2
.

The goal is to minimize the worst-case regret and the query complexity simultaneously:

\displaystyle{\rm{Regret}}_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Big(2V^{\star}(s_{1})-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}(s_{1})-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}(s_{1})\Big),\quad{\rm{Queries}}_{T}\coloneqq\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}.

Here
V^{\pi}(s_{1})\coloneqq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\sum_{h=1}^{H}r^{\star}(s_{h},a_{h})]
denotes the state-value function of policy
\pi
, and we define
V^{\star}(s_{1})\coloneqq V^{\pi^{\star}}(s_{1})
where
\pi^{\star}
is the optimal policy that maximizes the state-value function. The variable
Z_{t}\in\{0,1\}
indicates whether a query is made at round
t
. Note that the regret looks at the sum of the performance gaps between two pairs of policies:
(\pi^{\star},\pi^{0}_{t})
and
(\pi^{\star},\pi^{1}_{t})
. This is standard in dueling bandits
(
Yue & Joachims 2011
;
Yue et al. 2012
;
Dudík et al. 2015
;
Bengs et al. 2022
;
Wu et al. 2023b
)
and RL with preference-based feedback
(
Saha et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2022
)
.

Bayesian RL.

We also consider Bayesian RL in this work when learning with general function approximation. In the Bayesian setting,
P^{\star}
and
r^{\star}
are sampled from some known prior distributions
\rho_{\rm{P}}
and
\rho_{\rm{r}}
. The goal is to minimize the Bayesian regret and the Bayesian query complexity:

\displaystyle{\rm{BayesRegret}}_{T}\coloneqq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Big(2V^{\star}(s_{1})-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}(s_{1})-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}(s_{1})\Big)\right],\,{\rm{BayesQueries}}_{T}\coloneqq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\right].

Here the expectation is taken with respect to the prior distribution over
P^{\star}
and
r^{\star}
. We will use Bayesian supervised learning oracles to compute posteriors over the transition and reward model.

A model-free randomized algorithm for linear MDPs

In this section, we present a model-free algorithm for linear MDPs which is defined as follows.

Assumption 4.1

(Linear MDP

(
Jin et al. 2020
)
)
.

We assume a known feature map
\phi:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, an unknown (signed) measure
\mu:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and an unknown vector
\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}
such that for any
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
, we have

P^{\star}(s^{\prime}\,|\,s,a)=\phi^{\top}(s,a)\cdot\mu(s^{\prime})
and

r^{\star}(s,a)=\phi^{\top}(s,a)\cdot\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}
.
We assume
\|\phi(s,a)\|_{2}\leq 1
for all
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
,
\int_{\mathcal{S}}\|\mu(s)\|_{2}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s\leq\sqrt{d}
, and
\|\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}\|_{2}\leq B
for some
B>0
. For a trajectory
\tau=(s_{1},a_{1},\dots,s_{H},a_{H})
, we define
\phi(\tau)=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})
and assume
\|\phi(\tau)\|_{2}\leq 1
.

Linear MDPs can capture tabular MDPs by setting

and
\phi(s,a)
to be the one-hot encoding of
(s,a)
. In this case, we have
\|\phi(\tau)\|_{2}\leq H
. However, we can scale it down to get
\|\phi(\tau)\|_{2}\leq 1
at the expense of scaling

H
.
We define
\Theta_{B}=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\theta\|_{2}\leq B\}
, which contains
\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}
.

\displaystyle\omega_{t,h}(s,a)={\begin{cases}\phi(s,a)^{\top}\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}&\text{if }\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}}\leq\alpha_{\rm{L}}\\

Moreover, we note that the usual “value clipping” trick (i.e., simply constraining the value function within the range of

Then, the algorithm computes the greedy policy
\pi_{t}^{0}

\overline{Q}_{t,h}

\pi_{t}^{1}

Conclusion

We use randomization to design algorithms for RL with preference-based feedback. Randomization allows us to minimize regret and query complexity while at the same time maintaining computation efficiency. For linear models, our algorithms achieve a near-optimal balance between the worst-case reward regret and query complexity with computational efficiency. For models beyond linear, using eluder dimension, we present a TS-inspired algorithm that balances Bayesian regret and Bayesian query complexity nearly optimally.

Appendix A
Computational challenges in RL with preference-based feedback

For RL with preference-based feedback, there is currently no algorithm that achieves sublinear worst-case regret and computational efficiency simultaneously, even for tabular MDPs. In this section, we discuss the challenges that hindered previous works from being computationally efficient. Specifically, the reasons are twofold:

(1) Trajectory-wise information.
The feedback is trajectory-wise, meaning that we only receive information about cumulative rewards instead of per-step rewards. In this case, there is no longer a ground truth per-step reward signal. To see this, consider an MDP with two steps and a trajectory with a cumulative reward of 1. Then, we cannot decide the respective per-step reward for the two steps — it could be that the first step has reward 1 and the second has reward 0, or both have reward 0.5. The preference-based feedback is strictly harder than trajectory-wise reward feedback, and thus the same issue persists. This invalidates all algorithms relying on per-step reward information (e.g., UCBVI
(
Azar et al. 2017
)
) and necessitates leveraging feedback signal at a trajectory level. However, trajectory-level approaches typically entail maintaining a version space via trajectory constraints
(
Saha et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2022
;
Zhan et al. 2023a
)
, and the computational complexity of searching within this version space is at least exponential in the length of the episode.
Some works circumvent this computational obstacle by additional assumptions (e.g., explorability
(
Chatterji et al. 2021
)
), which are restrictive and do not generally hold even in tabular MDPs.

(2) Preference-based information.
The feedback relies on a pair of policies. Standard algorithms based on a single policy become computationally intractable when adapting to this setting since optimizing over a pair of policies simultaneously is qualitatively different. For example,

Zhan et al. 2023b
;
Saha et al. 2023
use the idea from optimal design and need the computation oracle:
\mathop{\arg\max}_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi}\|\mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim\pi}\phi(s,a)-\mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim\pi^{\prime}}\phi(s,a)\|_{A}
for some positive definite matrix
A
. Here
\|x\|^{2}_{A}:=x^{\top}Ax
, and
\phi
is some state-action wise feature.
3
3

3

These prior work typically assume trajectory wise feature
\phi(\tau)
for a state-action wise trajectory
\tau
. However, even when specializing to state-action-wise features, these algorithms are still not computationally tractable even in tabular MDPs.
It is unclear how to implement this oracle since standard planning approaches relying on dynamic programming cannot be applied here. Additionally, these methods also require actively maintaining a policy space
\Pi
by eliminating potentially sub-optimal policies. The policy class can be exponentially large even in tabular settings, so it is unclear how to maintain a valid policy space in a computationally tractable manner.

These challenges motivate us to devise a novel algorithm using a technique distinct from previous approaches. Our solution centers around the concept of
randomization
, which allows us to balance exploration and exploitation
and thus enable standard efficient computation oracles (e.g., DP-style planning oracle like value iteration).

The concept of covering number is defined below, which will be used to bound the statistical error of our algorithm.

Definition B.1

(Covering number)
.

Consider a function class
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The
\omega
-cover of a
function
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{F}
is defined as the set of functions
f\in\mathcal{F}
for which
\|f-\widehat{f}\|\leq\omega
. The covering number of
\mathcal{F}
w.r.t. the metric
\|\cdot\|
denoted by
N(\omega,\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|)
is the minimum number of
\omega
-covers needed to cover
\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma B.2

(Covering number of Euclidean balls)
.

(
Pollard 1990
)

Let
\Theta_{B}\coloneqq\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\theta\|_{2}\leq B\}
. Then we have

N(\omega,\Theta_{B},\|\cdot\|_{2})\leq(3B/\omega)^{d}.

.

Proof of

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\prime}_{{\rm{P}},\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+16dV_{\max}\sqrt{\iota_{\epsilon}-\log\big((\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}})\delta\lambda\big)}

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+16dV_{\max}\sqrt{\iota_{\epsilon}-\log(\delta\lambda)-\log(\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}})}

Lemma B.5

(One-step decomposition)
.

For any

, and policy

\displaystyle\phi(s,a)^{\top}\left(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\right)-Q^{\pi}_{h}(s,a)

We have

\displaystyle\phi(s,a)^{\top}\left(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\right)-Q^{\pi}_{h}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\phi(s,a)^{\top}\left(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\right)-\phi(s,a)^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s^{\prime}}[V^{\pi}_{h+1}(s^{\prime})\,|\,s,a]

\displaystyle\qquad+\phi(s,a)^{\top}\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s^{\prime}}[V^{\pi}_{h+1}(s^{\prime})\,|\,s,a].

For the last but one term, we note that

\displaystyle\phi(s,a)^{\top}\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}

\displaystyle=\phi(s,a)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})\right)

\displaystyle=\phi(s,a)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h+1}}\left[\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})\,\middle|\,s_{i,h},a_{i,h}\right]\right)+\phi(s,a)^{\top}\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}

\displaystyle=\phi(s,a)^{\top}\int_{s^{\prime}}\mu^{\star}_{h}(s^{\prime})\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}-\lambda\phi(s,a)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}\int_{s^{\prime}}\mu^{\star}_{h}(s^{\prime})\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}+\phi(s,a)^{\top}\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s^{\prime}}\left[\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s,a\right]-\phi(s,a)^{\top}\lambda_{t,h}+\phi(s,a)^{\top}\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}.

\displaystyle\phi(s,a)^{\top}\left(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\right)-Q^{\pi}_{h}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t})+\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h})+\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s^{\prime}}\left[\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})-V^{\pi}_{h+1}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s,a\right]-\phi(s,a)^{\top}\lambda_{t,h}+\phi(s,a)^{\top}\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}

\displaystyle=\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h})+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s^{\prime}}\left[\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})-V^{\pi}_{h+1}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s,a\right].

This completes the proof.

\displaystyle\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\left(\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{i,h+1}}\left[\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})\,\middle|\,s_{i,h},a_{i,h}\right]\right)\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}.

Towards an upper bound of the above, we will conduct some covering arguments. To that end, we first derive the upper bounds of norms of
\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}
and
\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}
.

Bounding the
\ell_{2}
-norms of
\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}
and
\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}
.

For
\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}
, applying triangle inequality, we have
\|\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}\leq\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}+\|\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}\leq\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}+B
. For
\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}=\sqrt{\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{\top}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{t-1}^{1/2}\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}}\leq\sqrt{\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}}\leq\sqrt{\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}\cdot\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}/\sqrt{\lambda}}

where in the last step we applied
Lemma

B.7
and the fact that
\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}\leq\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}\|\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\|_{2}\leq\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}/\sqrt{\lambda}
. This implies that
\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda}
, and thus,
\|\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda}+B
.

For
\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}
, we have
\|\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}\leq\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}+\|\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}
. For
\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}
, following a similar argument as above, we have

\displaystyle\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}}\leq\sqrt{\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}\cdot\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}/\sqrt{\lambda}},

which implies that
\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda}
. For
\|\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1/2}\|_{2}\cdot\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda^{-1}}\cdot\sqrt{t}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\left\|\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})\right\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda^{-1}}\cdot\sqrt{t}\cdot V_{\max}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\left\|\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\right\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{V_{\max}\sqrt{dt}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}

where the second inequality is by the Jensen’s inequality, the third inequality is by the condition that
\max_{s}|\overline{V}_{t,h}(s)|\leq V_{\max}
, and the last inequality is by
Lemma

D.7
. Putting these together, we get
\|\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda}+V_{\max}\sqrt{dt}/\sqrt{\lambda}
.

Covering construction.

With these bounds in hand, we can now proceed with a covering construction.
First define

\displaystyle Q_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s,a)

\displaystyle\quad\coloneqq\phi(s,a)^{\top}\theta_{{\rm{r}}}+\begin{cases}\phi(s,a)^{\top}\theta_{{\rm{P}}}&\text{if }\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}\leq\alpha_{\rm{L}}\\
\rho(s,a)\Big(\phi(s,a)^{\top}\theta_{{\rm{P}}}\Big)+(1-\rho(s,a))(H-h)&\text{if }\alpha_{\rm{L}}<\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}\leq\alpha_{\rm{U}}\\
H-h&\text{if }\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}>\alpha_{\rm{U}}\end{cases},

\displaystyle V_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s)\coloneqq\max_{a}Q_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s,a).

(9)

where
\rho(s,a):=\frac{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}
.
This definition aims to mimic the behavior of
Algorithm

1
. Then, we define the space of parameters as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\coloneqq\Big\{(\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma)\>:\>

\displaystyle\|\theta_{{\rm{r}}}\|_{2}\leq B+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda},\,\|\theta_{{\rm{P}}}\|_{2}\leq(V_{\max}\sqrt{dt}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi})/\sqrt{\lambda},

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|_{\rm{F}}\leq\sqrt{d}/\lambda,\,\Sigma=\Sigma^{\top},\,\Sigma\succeq 0,\,{\|V_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\|_{\infty}\leq V_{\max}}\Big\}.

Clearly,
\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}
and
\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}
satisfy the constraints of
\theta_{{\rm{r}}}
and
\theta_{{\rm{P}}}
. Moreover,
\|\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}\|_{\rm{F}}\leq\sqrt{d}\|\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{d}/\lambda
, and thus
\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}
satisfies the constraint of
\Sigma
. Hence, we have
(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t},\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h},\Sigma_{t-1})\in\mathcal{U}
.

By
Lemma

B.2
, the size of an
\omega
-covering of
\mathcal{U}
by treating it as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2d+d^{2}}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle N(\omega,\mathcal{U},\|\cdot\|_{2})\leq\left(\frac{3\Big((B+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda})+(V_{\max}\sqrt{dt}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi})/\sqrt{\lambda}+{\sqrt{d}}/\lambda\Big)}{\omega}\right)^{2d+d^{2}}

We denote the covering set by
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
. We will write
N\coloneqq N(\omega,\mathcal{U},\|\cdot\|_{2})
for simplicity when there is no ambiguity. We note that it is possible that not all points in
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
belong to
\mathcal{U}
. However, we can project all points to
\mathcal{U}
so it becomes a
2\omega
-covering of
\mathcal{U}
. We will omit this subtlety.

Covering argument.

For any
(\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma)\in\mathcal{U}
, we define

\displaystyle x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}:=V_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s_{i,h+1})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s^{\prime}}\left[V_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s_{i,h},a_{i,h}\right].

By construction, we have
|x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}|\leq 2V_{\max}
, so it is
4V_{\max}
-subgaussian conditioning on
(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})
. Hence, for all
(\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq\sqrt{32V_{\max}^{2}\left(d\log\left(1+\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)+\log(N/\delta)\right)}

(10)

with probability at least
1-\delta
by
Lemma

D.5
and the union bound over
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
. Now consider an arbitrary tuple
(\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma)\in\mathcal{U}
and the nearest point
(\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}+\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})(x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}})\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\left\|\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\cdot\max_{i}|x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}+\sqrt{\lambda^{-1}}\cdot t\cdot\max_{i}|x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}|

where the last step is by the fact that
\|\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda^{-1}}
.
Observing the derived upper bound above, the first term is already bounded by (
10
) since
(\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
. To bound the second term, we note that, by the definiton of
x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}
and
x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}
,

\displaystyle|x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}|\leq

\displaystyle 2\max_{s}\left|V_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s)-V_{\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}(s)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\max_{s}\left|\max_{a}Q_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s,a)-\max_{a}Q_{\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}(s,a)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\max_{s,a}\left|Q_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s,a)-Q_{\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}(s,a)\right|

We assume
\omega\leq(\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}})^{2}
(and this will be satisfied later when we specify the value of
\omega
). Recall that we have three cases in the definition of
Q_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s,a)
(see (
9
)), and we will refer to them as Case L, Case M, and Case U. There are in total 6 possible combinations of cases for the pair
Q_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s,a),Q_{\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}(s,a)
, and we will discuss them one by one below. For the ease of notation, we denote
Q(s,a)
and
\widetilde{Q}(s,a)
as shorthand for
Q_{\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}(s,a)
and
Q_{\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}(s,a)
.

(1) Case L + Case U.

This is impossible, because, in this case, we have
|\|\phi\|_{\Sigma}-\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}|>\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}
, but we also have
|\|\phi\|_{\Sigma}-\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}|=|\sqrt{\phi^{\top}\Sigma\phi}-\sqrt{\phi^{\top}\widetilde{\Sigma}\phi}|\leq\sqrt{|\phi^{\top}(\Sigma-\widetilde{\Sigma})\phi|}\leq\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{2}\|\Sigma-\widetilde{\Sigma}\|_{\rm{F}}\|\phi\|_{2}}\leq\sqrt{\omega}<\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}
.

(2) Both are Case L.

Then we immediately have

\displaystyle|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)|=

\displaystyle\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\theta_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta_{{\rm{P}}}-\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}-\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}})\right|\leq\|\phi(s,a)\|_{2}\Big(\|\theta_{{\rm{r}}}-\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}\|_{2}+\|\theta_{{\rm{P}}}-\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}}\|_{2}\Big)\leq 2\omega.

(3) Both are Case U.

Then we immediately have

\displaystyle|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)|=

\displaystyle\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\theta_{{\rm{r}}}-\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}})\right|\leq|\phi(s,a)\|_{2}\cdot\|\theta_{{\rm{r}}}-\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}\|_{2}\leq\omega.

(4) Case L + Case M.

Without loss of generality, we assume
Q(s,a)
is in Case M and
\widetilde{Q}(s,a)
is in Case L. Then we have

\displaystyle|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)|\leq

\displaystyle\rho(s,a)\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\theta_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta_{{\rm{P}}}-\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}-\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}})\right|

\displaystyle+(1-\rho(s,a))\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}+\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}})-(H-h)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho(s,a)\cdot 2\omega+(1-\rho(s,a))\cdot 2V_{\max}

Recall that
\rho(s,a):=\frac{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}
, so
1-\rho(s,a):=\frac{\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}
. Futhermore,

\displaystyle 1-\rho(s,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}=\frac{\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}+\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}=\frac{\sqrt{\phi^{\top}\Sigma\phi}-\sqrt{\phi^{\top}\widetilde{\Sigma}\phi}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{|\phi^{\top}(\Sigma-\widetilde{\Sigma})\phi|}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{2}\|\Sigma-\widetilde{\Sigma}\|_{2}\|\phi\|_{2}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}

where the first inequality is by the condition that
\widetilde{Q}
is in Case L. Inserting this back, we get
|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)|\leq 2\omega+\frac{2V_{\max}\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}
.

(5) Case M + Case U.

Without loss of generality, we assume
Q(s,a)
is in Case M and
\widetilde{Q}(s,a)
is in Case U. Then we have

\displaystyle|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)|\leq

\displaystyle\rho(s,a)\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\theta_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta_{{\rm{P}}}-(H-h))\right|+(1-\rho(s,a))\left|(H-h)-(H-h)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(s,a)\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\theta_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta_{{\rm{P}}}-(H-h))\right|\leq\rho(s,a)\cdot 2V_{\max}\leq\frac{2V_{\max}\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}

where the last inequality is from

\displaystyle\rho(s,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}+\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}=\frac{\sqrt{\phi^{\top}\widetilde{\Sigma}\phi}-\sqrt{\phi^{\top}\Sigma\phi}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{|\phi^{\top}(\Sigma-\widetilde{\Sigma})\phi|}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{2}\|\Sigma-\widetilde{\Sigma}\|_{2}\|\phi\|_{2}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}.

where the first inequality is by the condition that
\widetilde{Q}
is in Case U.

(6) Both are Case M.

Denote
\rho(s,a)=\frac{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}
and
\widetilde{\rho}(s,a)=\frac{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle|\rho(s,a)-\widetilde{\rho}(s,a)|=\frac{\Big|\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}-\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma}\Big|}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\|\phi\|_{2}\|\widetilde{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{\rm{F}}\|\phi\|_{2}}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}},

which also implies
|(1-\rho(s,a))-(1-\widetilde{\rho}(s,a))|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\rho(s,a)\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\theta_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta_{{\rm{P}}})-\widetilde{\rho}(s,a)\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}+\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}})\right|

\displaystyle+\left|(1-\rho(s,a))(H-h)-(1-\widetilde{\rho}(s,a))(H-h)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\rho(s,a)\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\theta_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta_{{\rm{P}}})-\rho(s,a)\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}+\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}})\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\rho(s,a)\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}+\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}})-\widetilde{\rho}(s,a)\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}}+\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}})\right|

\displaystyle+\left|(1-\rho(s,a))(H-h)-(1-\widetilde{\rho}(s,a))(H-h)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\omega+\frac{V_{\max}\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}+\frac{H\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}

Putting Everything Together.

Taking the maximum of the six cases, we conclude that

\displaystyle|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)|\leq 2\omega+\frac{2V_{\max}\sqrt{\omega}}{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}

We set
\omega=\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}{2tV_{\max}}\right)^{2}
and obtain

\displaystyle|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)|\leq 2\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}}}{2tV_{\max}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\leq\frac{3}{t}

where we leverage the condition that
\alpha_{\rm{L}},\alpha_{\rm{U}}\leq 1
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle|x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\widetilde{\Sigma}}|\leq 2\max_{s,a}\left|Q(s,a)-\widetilde{Q}(s,a)\right|\leq\frac{6}{t}.

Final Bound.

The covering argument is now complete, and we can use it to derive an upper bound for
\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}
. To that end, we note that, for
\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}
, there must exists
(\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma)\in\mathcal{U}
and its closest element
(\widetilde{\theta_{{\rm{r}}}},\widetilde{\theta_{{\rm{P}}}},\widetilde{\Sigma})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
such that

\displaystyle\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\left(\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{i,h+1}}[\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s_{i,h+1})\,|\,s_{i,h},a_{i,h}]\right)\right\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}+\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\big(x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\big)\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

where the second term is bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\big(x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\big)\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\left\|\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})\big(x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\big)\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda^{-1}}\cdot t\cdot\max_{i}|x_{i,\theta_{{\rm{r}}},\theta_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}-x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 6/\sqrt{\lambda},

and, applying (
10
), the first term is bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{32V_{\max}^{2}\left(d\log\left(1+\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)+\log(N/\delta)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{32V_{\max}^{2}\left(d\log\left(1+\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)+(2d+d^{2})\log\left(\frac{3\Big((B+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda})+(V_{\max}\sqrt{dt}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi})/\sqrt{\lambda}+\sqrt{d}/\lambda\Big)}{\omega\delta}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\max}\sqrt{32d\log\left(1+\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)+32(2d+d^{2})\log\left(\frac{12V_{\max}^{2}t^{2}\Big((B+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda})+(V_{\max}\sqrt{dt}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi})/\sqrt{\lambda}+\sqrt{d}/\lambda\Big)}{(\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}})^{2}\delta}\right)}

where the second inequality is by inserting the upper bound of the covering number
N
, and the equality is by inserting the value of
\omega
. The last line looks complicated and we can further simplify it and get

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\phi(s_{i,h},a_{i,h})x_{i,\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{r}}},\tilde{\theta}_{{\rm{P}}},\Sigma}\right\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 16dV_{\max}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{12V_{\max}t(t+\lambda)\Big((B+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda})+(V_{\max}\sqrt{dt}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi})/\sqrt{\lambda}+\sqrt{d}/\lambda\Big)}{(\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}})\delta\lambda}\right)}.

Plugging these upper bounds back, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+16dV_{\max}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{12V_{\max}t(t+\lambda)\Big((B+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}/\sqrt{\lambda})+(V_{\max}\sqrt{dt}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi})/\sqrt{\lambda}+\sqrt{d}/\lambda\Big)}{(\alpha_{\rm{U}}-\alpha_{\rm{L}})\delta\lambda}\right)}.

Applying union bound over all
t\in[T]
and
h\in[H]
, the upper bound exactly becomes
\epsilon^{\prime}_{{\rm{P}},\eta}
. Further invoking
Lemma

B.3
finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma B.11

(Boundness of value functions and transition estimation Error)
.

Assume the events define in
Lemmas

B.7
and
B.9
hold.
Then, the following holds with probability at least
1-\delta
:

1.

\max_{s,a}|\overline{Q}_{t,h}(s,a)|\leq V_{\max}
for all
t\in[T]
and
h\in[H]
.

2.

\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}
for all
t\in[T]
and
h\in[H]
.

3.

\|\lambda_{t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}\leq\epsilon_{\lambda}
for all
t\in[T]
and
h\in[H]
.

The first statement implies
\max_{s}|\overline{V}_{t,h}(s)|\leq V_{\max}
for all
t\in[T]
and
h\in[H]
.

Proof.

We prove the three statements together by induction.

For the first statement, we define
V_{\max,h}\coloneqq(H-h+1)(1+(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda})
, and we will actually show that
\max_{s,a}|\overline{Q}_{t,h}(s,a)-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)|\leq V_{\max,h}
in the induction, which immediately leads to
\max_{s,a}|\overline{Q}_{t,h}(s,a)|\leq V_{\max,h}+(H-h+1)\leq V_{\max}
.

Base.

For
h=H
, we can directly apply
Lemma

B.10
without the transition argument, which leads to the desired upper bound of
\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,H}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}
. Moreover, the upper bound of
\|\lambda_{t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}
also trivially holds. For an upper bound on the value function, we immediately have

\displaystyle\big|\overline{Q}_{t,H}(s,a)-Q^{\star}_{H}(s,a)\big|=\big|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}})\big|\leq\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\|\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}

by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. We note that
\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda^{-1}}
and
\|\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}\leq\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}\leq\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}+\|\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\big|\overline{Q}_{t,H}(s,a)-Q^{\star}_{H}(s,a)\big|\leq(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}\leq V_{\max,H}.

Inductive Steps.

Now consider
h<H
. By induction hypothesis, we have
\max_{t,s}|\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s)|\leq V_{\max}
. Thus, we can apply
Lemma

B.10
and get
\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}
. For the upper bound of
\|\lambda_{t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}
, by definition, we have

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}=

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda\int_{s^{\prime}}\mu^{\star}_{h}(s^{\prime})\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\right\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda}\left\|\int_{s^{\prime}}\mu^{\star}_{h}(s^{\prime})\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})\mathop{}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle V_{\max}\sqrt{\lambda d}=\epsilon_{\lambda}

where the second inequality is by
\|\Sigma^{-1/2}_{t-1,h}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\lambda}
, and the last inequality is by
4.1
.

For the upper bound on the value function, consider the following three cases.

Case 1:
\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq\alpha_{\rm{L}}
.

We apply
Lemma

B.5
and get

\displaystyle|\phi(s,a)^{\top}\left(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\right)-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h})+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s^{\prime}}\left[\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})-V^{\star}_{h+1}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s,a\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})\right|+\left|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h})\right|+\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s^{\prime}}\left[\overline{V}_{t,h+1}(s^{\prime})-V^{\star}_{h+1}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s,a\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}+\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})+\Big((H-h)(1+\big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}+1+\Big((H-h)(1+\big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H-h+1)(1+\big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\max,h}

where the second inequality is by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
Lemmas

B.7
and
B.9
, and the induction hypothesis. The third inequality is by the condition that
\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq\alpha_{\rm{L}}
and the definition of
\alpha_{\rm{L}}
.

Case 2:
\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}>\alpha_{\rm{U}}
.

We have

\displaystyle|\overline{Q}_{t,h}(s,a)-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)\Big|\leq

\displaystyle|\phi(s,a)^{\top}\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+H-h-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})+H-h+\phi(s,a)^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}+(H-h+1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle V_{\max,h}

where we used the fact that
0\leq\phi(s,a)^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}\leq 1
and
0\leq Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)\leq H-h+1
, which leads to
-(H-h+1)\leq\phi(s,a)^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)\leq 1
.

Case 3:
\alpha_{\rm{L}}<\|\phi(s,a)\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq\alpha_{\rm{U}}
.

Denoting
\rho\coloneqq\rho(s,a)
for simplicity, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\overline{Q}_{t,h}(s,a)-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho\Big|\phi(s,a)^{\top}\left(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\right)-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)\Big|+(1-\rho)\Big|\phi(s,a)^{\top}\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+H-h-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho(H-h+1)\Big(1+(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}\Big)+(1-\rho)\Big|\phi(s,a)^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})+H-h+\phi(s,a)^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho(H-h+1)\Big(1+(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}\Big)+(1-\rho)\Big((\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})/\sqrt{\lambda}+(H-h+1)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle V_{\max,h}

where we have used the similar arguments from Case 1 and 2.

Taking the maximum over the three cases, we conclude that

\displaystyle|\overline{Q}_{t,h}(s,a)-Q^{\star}_{h}(s,a)|\leq V_{\max,h}

which implies
\max_{s,a}|\overline{Q}_{t,h}(s,a)|\leq V_{\max}
.
∎

B.4
Bounding regret

We define

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{t}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\left[\sum_{h=1}^{H}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}\right].

Lemma B.13
.

Assume all events listed in
Lemma

B.12
hold. Fix
t\in[T]
. Then, we have
(V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t})+(\widetilde{V}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})\leq 0
with probability at least
{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)
where
{\rm{F}}
denotes the CDF of a standard normal distribution (i.e.,
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
).

Proof.

Define the indicators

\displaystyle\bm{L}^{\star}_{t,h}(s)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathds{1}\left\{\|\phi(s,\pi^{\star}(s))\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq\alpha_{\rm{L}}\right\},

\displaystyle\bm{M}^{\star}_{t,h}(s)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathds{1}\left\{\alpha_{\rm{L}}<\|\phi(s,\pi^{\star}(s))\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq\alpha_{\rm{U}}\right\},

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{\star}_{t,h}(s)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathds{1}\left\{\|\phi(s,\pi^{\star}(s))\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}>\alpha_{\rm{U}}\right\}.

We note that they are independent of the random noises at episode
t
and only depends on the information up to episode
t-1
. Then, we can decompose
V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t}
:

\displaystyle V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t}=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[V^{\star}_{1}(s_{1})-\overline{V}_{t,1}(s_{1})\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},\pi_{t,1}^{0}(s_{1}))\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{L}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{M}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{U}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right)\right]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{\rm{L}}+\mathtt{T}_{\rm{M}},+\mathtt{T}_{\rm{U}}.

Now we establish upper bounds for each term. For
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{U}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{U}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{U}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-(H-1)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{U}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}(\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{r}},t})-(H-1)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\left(-\bm{U}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right)^{\top}(\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{r}},t})\right]

where the inequality holds since

\displaystyle Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}-(H-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{2}}[V^{\star}_{2}(s_{2})\,|\,s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1})]-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}-(H-1)\leq 0.

For
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{L}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{L}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{L}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{L}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,1}-\lambda_{t,1})+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{2}}\left[V^{\star}_{2}(s_{2})-\overline{V}_{t,2}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1})\right]\right)\right]

where the last equality is by
Lemma

B.5
.

For
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{M}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\left[\bm{M}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\left(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\bigg[\bm{M}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\bigg(\rho(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\Big(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,1})\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\big(1-\rho(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\big)\Big(Q^{\star}_{1}(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-(H-h)\Big)\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\bigg[\bm{M}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\bigg(\rho(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\bigg(-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,1}-\lambda_{t,1})

\displaystyle+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{2}}\left[V^{\star}_{2}(s_{2})-\overline{V}_{t,2}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1})\right]\bigg)+\big(1-\rho(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\big)\left(-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\right)^{\top}(\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{r}},t})\bigg)\bigg]

where the inequality is by similar arguments as in the case of
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{U}}
and
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{L}}
.

So putting
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{L}},\mathtt{T}_{\rm{M}},\mathtt{T}_{\rm{U}}
together, we have

\displaystyle V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1}}\bigg[\underbrace{\Big(\bm{U}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})+\bm{M}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})(1-\rho(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1})))\Big)}_{=:\bm{UM}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})}(-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1})))^{\top}(\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{r}},t})

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\Big(\bm{L}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})+\bm{M}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})\rho(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))\Big)}_{=:\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,1}(s_{1})}\Big(-\phi(s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1}))^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,1}-\lambda_{t,1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{2}}\left[V^{\star}_{2}(s_{2})-\overline{V}_{t,2}(s^{\prime})\,\middle|\,s_{1},\pi^{\star}(s_{1})\right]\Big)\bigg]

Keeping expanding the last term, we arrive at

\displaystyle V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t}\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi^{\star}}\Bigg[\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\bm{UM}^{\star}_{t,h}(s_{h})\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,i}(s_{i})\right)\left(-\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{h}\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,i}(s_{i})\right)\left(-\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h})\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi^{\star}}\Bigg[\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\bm{UM}^{\star}_{t,h}(s_{h})\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,i}(s_{i})+\prod_{i=1}^{h}\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,i}(s_{i})\right)\left(-\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{h}\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,i}(s_{i})\right)\left(-\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h})\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle(-\phi^{\star}_{\rm{r}})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})+\sum_{h=1}^{H}(-\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h})

where in the last step we defined
\phi^{\star}_{\rm{r}}
and
\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}
as

\displaystyle\phi^{\star}_{\rm{r}}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi^{\star}}\left[\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\bm{UM}^{\star}_{t,h}(s_{h})\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,i}(s_{i})+\prod_{i=1}^{h}\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,i}(s_{i})\right)\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\right],

\displaystyle\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi^{\star}}\left[\left(\prod_{i=1}^{h}\bm{LM}^{\star}_{t,i}(s_{i})\right)\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\right].

Now we also decompose
\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}
and get

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\left[\sum_{h=1}^{H}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\right]^{\top}\left(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\left[\sum_{h=1}^{H}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\right]^{\top}\left(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\right)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\phi}_{t})^{\top}\left(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\right).

Combining the decomposition together, we obtain

\displaystyle(V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t})+(\widetilde{V}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left((-\phi^{\star}_{\rm{r}})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})+\sum_{h=1}^{H}(-\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h})\right)+\widetilde{\phi}_{t}^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star}\right)^{\top}\left(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\right)+\sum_{h=1}^{H}(-\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h})^{\top}\left(\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star}\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}\|\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}+(\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star})^{\top}\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\|\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}}\left(\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}+\|\lambda_{t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}\right)-\sum_{h=1}^{H}{\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}}^{\top}\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star}\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}-(\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star})^{\top}\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\sqrt{\sum_{h=1}^{H}\|\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}\|^{2}_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}}}\cdot\sqrt{H(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})^{2}}-\sum_{h=1}^{H}{\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}}^{\top}\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\|\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star}\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}-(\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star})^{\top}\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}}_{(a)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sqrt{\sum_{h=1}^{H}\min\left\{1,\|\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}\|^{2}_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}}\right\}}\cdot\sqrt{H(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})^{2}}-\sum_{h=1}^{H}{\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}}^{\top}\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}}_{(b)}

where the second inequality is by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the third inequality is
Lemma

B.12
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality again, and the last step is by the fact that

\|\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}\|^{2}_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1,h}}\leq 1/\lambda\leq 1.

For (a),

we note that
(\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star})^{\top}\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}^{2}\|\widetilde{\phi}_{t}-\phi^{\star}\|^{2}_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}})
. So by setting
\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\geq\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}
, we have
(a)\leq 0
with probability at least
{\rm{F}}(-1)
.

For (b),

similarly, we note that
\sum_{h=1}^{H}{\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}}^{\top}\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{{\rm{P}}}^{2}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\|\phi^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},h}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}})
. So by setting
\sigma_{{\rm{P}}}\geq\sqrt{H(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})^{2}}
, we have
(b)\leq 0
with probability at least
{\rm{F}}(-1)
.

Conclusion.

Finally, we note that (a) and (b) are independent, so the probability that both (a) and (b) hold is at least
{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)
.
∎

Lemma B.14
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\leq L_{\max}.

Proof.

Denote
a_{t,h}=\pi_{t}(s_{t,h})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathds{1}\left\{\|\phi(s_{t,h},a_{t,h})\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}>\alpha^{2}_{L}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\min\left\{\frac{\|\phi(s_{t,h},a_{t,h})\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}}{\alpha_{L}^{2}},1\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{L}^{2}}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\min\left\{\|\phi(s_{t,h},a_{t,h})\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}},1\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{H}{\alpha_{L}^{2}}\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+T}{\lambda}\right)=L_{\max}

where the second inequality uses the fact that
\alpha_{\rm{L}}<1
, and the last inequality is by
Lemma

D.6
.
∎

Lemma B.15
.

Assume all events listed in
Lemma

B.12
hold. Then, the following holds

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t}\Big((\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}})-(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})\Big)\right|=

\displaystyle\widetilde{O}\bigg(V_{\max}L_{\max}+V_{\max}\sqrt{HT}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})\left(\sqrt{dT}+L_{\max}+\sqrt{HT}+\frac{\epsilon T\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})\left(H\sqrt{dT}+L_{\max}+\sqrt{HT}\right).\bigg)

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Big((\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}})-(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})\Big)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},a_{1})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{1}(s_{1},a_{1})\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\left(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}-\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}\right)\right|

By triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}_{t,1}(s_{t,1})\left(\overline{Q}_{t,1}(s_{1},a_{1})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{1}(s_{1},a_{1})\right)\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\left(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle+2V_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}_{t,1}^{\complement}(s_{t,1})\right]

For the first term, conditioning on
\bm{L}_{t,1}(s_{t,1})
, we have

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}_{t,1}(s_{t,1})\left(\phi(s_{1},a_{1})^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,1})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{1}(s_{1},a_{1})\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\left(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\right)\Bigg|+2V_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}_{t,1}^{\complement}(s_{t,1})\right]

Applying triangle inequality again, we get

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\left(\phi(s_{1},a_{1})^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,1})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{1}(s_{1},a_{1})\right)\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\left(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle+2V_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}_{t,1}^{\complement}(s_{t,1})\right]

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,1}(s_{t,1})\left(\phi(s_{1},a_{1})^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,1})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{1}(s_{1},a_{1})\right)\right]\right|

Applying
Lemma

B.5
and the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{2}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\overline{V}_{t,2}(s_{2})-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{2}(s_{2})\right]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=2}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\left(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\phi^{\top}(s_{1},a_{1})(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,1}-\lambda_{t,1})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\phi^{\top}(s_{1},a_{1})(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})\right)\Bigg|

\displaystyle+2V_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}_{t,1}^{\complement}(s_{t,1})\right]

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{1},a_{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,1}(s_{t,1})\left(\phi(s_{1},a_{1})^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,1})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{1}(s_{1},a_{1})\right)\right]\right|

Keep expanding the first term, we get

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Big((\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}})-(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})\Big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2V_{\max}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}_{t,h}^{\complement}(s_{t,h})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\left[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\left(\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t}+\overline{\theta}_{{\rm{P}},t,h})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{h}(s_{h},a_{h})\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\widetilde{s}_{h},\widetilde{a}_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\Big(\phi(s_{h},a_{h})-\phi(\widetilde{s}_{h},\widetilde{a}_{h})\Big)^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\phi^{\top}(s_{h},a_{h})\Big(\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}-\lambda_{t,h}\Big)\right|

\displaystyle=:\mathtt{T}_{1}+\mathtt{T}_{2}+\mathtt{T}_{3}+\mathtt{T}_{4}.

We bound each term separately.

Bounding
\mathtt{T}_{1}
.

By Hoeffding’s inequality and
Lemma

B.14
, we have

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2V_{\max}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\bm{L}_{t,h}^{\complement}(s_{t,h})\right]+2V_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{HT}{2}\log(1/\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq 2V_{\max}\left(L_{\max}+\sqrt{\frac{HT}{2}\log(1/\delta)}\right).

with probability at least
1-\delta
.

Bounding
\mathtt{T}_{2}
.

By definition and
Lemma

B.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\Bigg|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\Big(\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}+\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}+\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\lambda_{t,h}+\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},t,h})

\displaystyle\qquad-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{h}(s_{h},a_{h})\Big)\bigg]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\Bigg|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\bigg(\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},t,h})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{h}(s_{h},a_{h})

\displaystyle\qquad+\phi^{\top}(s_{h},a_{h})(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})+\phi^{\top}(s_{h},a_{h})(\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}+\lambda_{t,h})\bigg)\bigg]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\Bigg|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\bigg(\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},t,h})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{h}(s_{h},a_{h})

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}(\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}+\|\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}(\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}+\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}+\|\lambda_{t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}})\bigg)\bigg]\Bigg|.

We note that
|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},t,h})|\leq V_{\max}
and
|Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{h}(s_{h},a_{h})|\leq V_{\max}
. Moreover, we have
\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\leq 1/\sqrt{\lambda}
and
\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq 1/\sqrt{\lambda}
. Apply triangle inequality and inserting these upper bounds back, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\Bigg|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\bigg(\phi(s_{h},a_{h})^{\top}(\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{r}}}+\theta^{\star}_{{\rm{P}},t,h})-Q^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{h}(s_{h},a_{h})\bigg)\bigg]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}(\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}+\|\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}})\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}(\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}+\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}+\|\lambda_{t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}})\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2V_{\max}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\sqrt{\lambda^{-1}}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})\bigg]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg[\bm{L}^{\complement}_{t,h}(s_{t,h})\bigg]}_{(*)}\left(2V_{\max}+\frac{\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right).

We notice that
(*)
can be bounded by Hoeffding’s inequality and
Lemma

B.14
, as we did similarly for
\mathtt{T}_{1}
:

\displaystyle(*)\leq L_{\max}+\sqrt{\frac{HT}{2}\log(1/\delta)}

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{2}\leq\left(L_{\max}+\sqrt{\frac{HT}{2}\log(1/\delta)}\right)\left(2V_{\max}+\frac{\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)

with probability at least
1-\delta
.

Bounding
\mathtt{T}_{3}
.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{3}=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\widetilde{\tau}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\Big(\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big)(\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}+\eta_{{\rm{r}},t})\right|

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\widetilde{\tau}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\Big(\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}+\|\eta_{{\rm{r}},t}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}\Big)\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\widetilde{\tau}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}

Since
\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\leq 2/\sqrt{\lambda}
, by Hoeffding’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}\Big)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}+\sqrt{\frac{2T\log(1/\delta)}{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}\Big)\Bigg(\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}}_{\rm(i)}+\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}(1-Z_{t})\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}}_{\rm(ii)}+\sqrt{\frac{2T\log(1/\delta)}{\lambda}}\Bigg).

To bound (i), we have

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\min\left\{2/\sqrt{\lambda},\,\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{T\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\min\left\{4/\lambda,\,\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{T\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\min\left\{1,\,\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{T\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+4T}{\lambda}\right)}

where the last inequality is
Lemma

D.6
. To bound (ii), we have

\displaystyle{\rm(ii)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}(1-Z_{t})\Big\|\phi(\tau)-\phi(\widetilde{\tau})\Big\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}(1-Z_{t})\cdot\epsilon\sqrt{\pi}/(2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon T\sqrt{\pi}/(2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}})

where the first inequality is by
Lemma

B.6
. Putting the two upper bounds together, we have

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{3}\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}\Big)\Bigg(2\sqrt{T\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+4T}{\lambda}\right)}+\epsilon T\sqrt{\pi}/(2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}})+\sqrt{\frac{2T\log(1/\delta)}{\lambda}}\Bigg).

Bounding
\mathtt{T}_{4}
.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{4}\leq

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\Big(\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}+\|\eta_{{\rm{P}},t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}+\|\lambda_{t,h}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}\Big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}\Big)\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s_{h},a_{h}\sim\pi_{t}^{0}}\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}

Since
\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\leq 1/\sqrt{\lambda}
, by Hoeffding’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}\Big)\left(\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}+\sqrt{\frac{TH\log(1/\delta)}{2\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}\Big)\left(\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\min\left\{1/\sqrt{\lambda},\,\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\right\}+\sqrt{\frac{TH\log(1/\delta)}{2\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}\Big)\left(\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sqrt{T\sum_{t=1}^{T}\min\left\{1/\lambda,\,\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\right\}}+\sqrt{\frac{TH\log(1/\delta)}{2\lambda}}\right)

Since
\lambda\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}\Big)\left(\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sqrt{T\sum_{t=1}^{T}\min\left\{1,\,\|\phi(s_{h},a_{h})\|^{2}_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}^{-1}}\right\}}+\sqrt{\frac{TH\log(1/\delta)}{2\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}\Big)\left(H\sqrt{T\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+T}{\lambda}\right)}+\sqrt{\frac{TH\log(1/\delta)}{2\lambda}}\right)

where the last inequality is
Lemma

D.6
.

Conclusion.

Combining the above bounds, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Big((\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}})-(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})\Big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2V_{\max}\left(L_{\max}+\sqrt{\frac{HT}{2}\log(1/\delta)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(L_{\max}+\sqrt{\frac{HT}{2}\log(1/\delta)}\right)\left(2V_{\max}+\frac{\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}\Big)\left(2\sqrt{T\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+4T}{\lambda}\right)}+\frac{\epsilon T\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}}+\sqrt{\frac{2T\log(1/\delta)}{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}\Big)\left(H\sqrt{T\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+T}{\lambda}\right)}+\sqrt{\frac{TH\log(1/\delta)}{2\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4V_{\max}\left(L_{\max}+\sqrt{\frac{HT}{2}\log(1/\delta)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}\Big)\left(2\sqrt{T\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+4T}{\lambda}\right)}+\frac{\epsilon T\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}}+\frac{L_{\max}+2\sqrt{TH\log(1/\delta)/2}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}\Big)\left(H\sqrt{T\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+T}{\lambda}\right)}+\frac{L_{\max}+2\sqrt{TH\log(1/\delta)/2}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{O}\bigg(V_{\max}L_{\max}+V_{\max}\sqrt{HT}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})\left(\sqrt{dT}+L_{\max}+\sqrt{HT}+\frac{\epsilon T\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})\left(H\sqrt{dT}+L_{\max}+\sqrt{HT}\right)\bigg).

∎

Lemma B.16

(Regret decomposition)
.

Assume all events listed in
Lemma

B.12
hold. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}(V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t})+(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left((\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}})-(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})+(V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}-\overline{V}_{t}^{-})-(V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2V_{\max}}{{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{T\log(1/\delta)}{2}}+4\delta\right)

with probability at least
1-\delta
.

Proof.

We note that, at any round
t\in[T]
, conditioning on all information collected up to round
t-1
, the randomness of
\overline{V}_{t}
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}
only comes from the randomness of Gaussian noise variables
\xi_{{\rm{r}},t},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1},\dots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}
. In other words, the values of
\overline{V}_{t}
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}
are determined once given these Gaussian noise variables.
In light of this, we write out the dependence on the Gaussian noise variables explicitly: we treat
\overline{V}_{t}
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}
as functions of
\xi_{{\rm{r}},t},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}
and define
\overline{V}_{t}[\xi_{{\rm{r}},t},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}]
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}[\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}^{-},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}^{-}]
as the values of
\overline{V}_{t}
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}
obtained at round
t
with the Gaussian noise variables
\xi_{{\rm{r}},t},\xi_{{\rm{P}},1},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},H}
. Then, we define a notion of “worst-case” Gaussian noise variables as follows:

\displaystyle\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}^{-},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}^{-}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathop{\arg\min}_{\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}^{-},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}^{-}}\overline{V}_{t}[\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}^{-},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}^{-}]-\widetilde{V}_{t}[\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}^{-},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}^{-}]

\displaystyle\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\|\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}\quad\text{and}\quad\forall h\in[H]:\>\|\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,h}^{-}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1,h}}\leq\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}.

And we denote
\overline{V}_{t}^{-}
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}
as the value functions specified by
\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}^{-},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}^{-}
:

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{t}^{-}\coloneqq\overline{V}_{t}[\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}^{-},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}^{-}],\qquad\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\coloneqq\widetilde{V}_{t}[\xi_{{\rm{r}},t}^{-},\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,1}^{-},\cdots,\xi_{{\rm{P}},t,H}^{-}].

In other words,
\overline{V}_{t}^{-}
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}
are counterparts of
\overline{V}_{t}
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}
that attain the smallest difference,
\overline{V}_{t}-\widetilde{V}_{t}
, while the noise variables still satisfy the high probability bounds. By
Lemma

B.12
, we immediately have

\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}})\coloneqq\Pr\left(\overline{V}_{t}^{-}-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\leq\overline{V}_{t}-\widetilde{V}_{t}\right)\geq 1-\delta/T.

(11)

We note that here we have
\delta/T
instead of
\delta
because we are considering a fixed
t
and the results of
Lemma

B.12
are derived by the union bound over all
t\in[T]
— thus, the probability of the event
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}
is at least
1-\delta/T
for a single
t
.

Then, we denote
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}
as the event that
\overline{V}_{t}-\widetilde{V}_{t}\geq V^{\star}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}
. Thus, by
Lemma

B.13
, we have
\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}})\geq{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)
. Moreover, we denote
p_{{\rm{alg}}}
as the randomness of the algorithm. Then, we define the joint distribution
p_{\rm{opt}}
of
\overline{V}_{t}
and
\widetilde{V}_{t}
by restricting
p_{{\rm{alg}}}
to the event
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}
. Specifically, it is defined as

p_{\rm{opt}}(\overline{V}_{t},\widetilde{V}_{t})\coloneqq\begin{cases}p_{\rm{alg}}(\overline{V}_{t},\widetilde{V}_{t})/\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}})&\text{if }\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Let
z:=\overline{V}_{t}-\widetilde{V}_{t}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t})+(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})\leq

\displaystyle V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{opt}}}\left[V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}-z+z+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{opt}}}\left[z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{z}\frac{\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\}\cdot p_{\rm{alg}}(z)}{\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}})}\cdot(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-})\mathop{}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{alg}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\}\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]/\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}})

where the second inequality is by the definition of
p_{\rm{opt}}
, which rules out the case that
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}
does not hold. Considering the event
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}
, we have

\displaystyle(V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t})+(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{alg}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\}\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}\}\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{alg}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\}\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}^{\complement}\}\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]\right)/\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{alg}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\}\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}\}\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{alg}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\}\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}^{\complement}\}4V_{\max}\right]\right)/\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{alg}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\}\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}\}\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]+4V_{\max}\delta/T\right)/\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}})

where the last inequality is by (
11
). For the first term, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{\rm{alg}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}}\}\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}\}\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{{\rm{alg}}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}\}\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{{\rm{alg}}}}\left[\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{{\rm{alg}}}}\left[\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}^{\complement}\}\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim p_{{\rm{alg}}}}\left[\left(z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right)\right]+4V_{\max}\delta/T

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\overline{V}_{t}-\widetilde{V}_{t}-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\right]+4V_{\max}\delta/T

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[(\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}})-(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})\right]+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[(V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}-\overline{V}_{t}^{-})-(V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-})\right]+4V_{\max}\delta/T.

where the first inequality holds since
z-\overline{V}_{t}^{-}+\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-}\geq 0
conditioning on
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{low}}
, and the second inequality is by (
11
) again.

Recall that
\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{\rm{opt}})\geq{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)
. Inserting all of these back, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}(V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t})+(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm{F}}^{-2}(-1)\left(8V_{\max}\delta+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[(\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}})-(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})\right]+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[(V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}-\overline{V}_{t}^{-})-(V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-})\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm{F}}^{-2}(-1)\Bigg(8V_{\max}\delta+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left((\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}})-(\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}})+(V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}-\overline{V}_{t}^{-})-(V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}-\widetilde{V}_{t}^{-})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+2V_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{T\log(1/\delta)}{2}}\Bigg).

where the last inequality is the Hoeffding’s inequality.
∎

Given all these lemmas, we are ready to establish an upper bound of regret. We first note that, since
\pi_{t}^{1}=\pi_{t-1}^{0}
for all
t
, the regret incurred by
\pi_{t}^{1}
for all
t
is equivalent to that incurred by
\pi_{t}^{0}
for all
t
. Hence, it suffices to compute the regret incurred by
\pi_{t}^{0}
for
t\in[T]
and multiply it by two to get the total regret.

We start with the following regret decomposition:

\displaystyle{\rm{Regret}}_{T}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigg(V^{\star}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}\bigg)=\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigg(V^{\star}-\overline{V}_{t}+\widetilde{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}\bigg)}_{(*)}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigg(\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}+V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}-\widetilde{V}_{t}\bigg).

By
Lemma

B.16
, we can further decompose
(*)
and obtain

\displaystyle{\rm{Regret}}_{T}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)}\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigg(V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}-\overline{V}^{-}_{t}+\widetilde{V}^{-}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}_{t}\bigg)}_{\rm(i)}+\left(1+\frac{1}{{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)}\right)\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigg(\overline{V}_{t}-V^{\pi_{t}^{0}}+V^{\pi_{t}^{1}}-\widetilde{V}_{t}\bigg)}_{\rm(ii)}

\displaystyle+\frac{2V_{\max}}{{\rm{F}}^{2}(-1)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{T\log(1/\delta)}{2}}+4\delta\right).

We note that both (i) and (ii) can be bounded by
Lemma

B.15
:

\displaystyle{\rm(i),(ii)}\leq

\displaystyle\ \widetilde{O}\bigg(V_{\max}L_{\max}+V_{\max}\sqrt{HT}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})\left(\sqrt{dT}+L_{\max}+\sqrt{HT}+\frac{\epsilon T\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})\left(H\sqrt{dT}+L_{\max}+\sqrt{HT}\right)\bigg)

Inserting this back, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm{Regret}}_{T}=

\displaystyle\ \widetilde{O}\bigg(V_{\max}L_{\max}+V_{\max}\sqrt{HT}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta})\left(\sqrt{dT}+L_{\max}+\sqrt{HT}+\frac{\epsilon T\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+(\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda})\left(H\sqrt{dT}+L_{\max}+\sqrt{HT}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+V_{\max}\sqrt{T}\bigg)

To compute this quantity, we note the following asymptotic rate:

•

\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{r}},\eta}=\widetilde{O}\left(d\sqrt{\kappa+B^{2}}\right)

•

\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\xi}+\epsilon_{{\rm{P}},\eta}+\epsilon_{\lambda}=\widetilde{O}\left(d^{5/2}H^{3/2}\sqrt{\kappa+B^{2}}\right)

•

L_{\max}=\widetilde{O}\left(d^{6}H^{4}(\kappa+B^{2})\right)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{Regret}}_{T}=

\displaystyle\ \widetilde{O}\bigg(\epsilon T\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{T}\cdot d^{3}H^{5/2}\sqrt{\kappa+B^{2}}+d^{17/2}H^{11/2}(\kappa+B^{2})^{3/2}\bigg).

B.5
Bounding number of queries

Recall that the number of queries are computed via

\displaystyle{\rm{Queries}}_{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\mathds{1}\left\{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{\theta}_{{\rm{r}},t},\sigma_{\rm{r}}^{2}\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1})}\left[\Big|(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1}))^{\top}(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})\Big|\right]>\epsilon\right\}.

Notice that Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\Big|(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1}))^{\top}(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})\Big|\leq\|\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\|\theta_{0}-\theta_{1}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\|\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\|_{2}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\|\theta_{0}-\theta_{1}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\|\theta_{0}-\theta_{1}\|_{\Sigma_{t-1}}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}^{2}d}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}

where the second inequality is by
\|\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1}\|_{2}=1/\lambda
, and the last step is by the fact that
\theta_{0}-\theta_{1}
follows
\mathcal{N}(0,2\sigma_{\rm{r}}^{2}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1})
and
Lemma

D.3
. We denote
\zeta\coloneqq 2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\sqrt{2d/\lambda}
for the ease of notation. Then, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{Queries}}_{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\mathds{1}\left\{\min\left\{\zeta,\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\left[\Big|(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1}))^{\top}(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})\Big|\right]\right\}>\epsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\mathds{1}\left\{\min\left\{1,\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\left[\Big|(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1}))^{\top}(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})\Big|\right]\right\}>\epsilon/\zeta\right\}

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{Queries}}_{T}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\mathds{1}\left\{\min\left\{1,\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\left[\Big((\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1}))^{\top}(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})\Big)^{2}\right]\right\}>\epsilon^{2}/\zeta^{2}\right\}.

Let
u_{0},u_{1}
denotes two independent standard Gaussian variables with zero mean and identity covariance matrix. Then,
\theta_{0}-\theta_{1}
has the same joint distribution as
\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}(u_{0}-u_{1})
. Hence, we can rewrite the expectation in the indicator as

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\left[\Big((\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1}))^{\top}(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})\Big)^{2}\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u_{0},u_{1}}\left[\left(\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right)^{\top}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\left(u_{0}-u_{1}\right)\right)^{2}\right]

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u_{0},u_{1}}\left[\left(\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right)^{\top}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\left(u_{0}-u_{1}\right)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u_{0},u_{1}}\left[\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right)^{\top}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\left(u_{0}-u_{1}\right)\left(u_{0}-u_{1}\right)^{\top}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right)^{\top}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u_{0},u_{1}}\left[\left(u_{0}-u_{1}\right)\left(u_{0}-u_{1}\right)^{\top}\right]\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right).

For the expectation in the middle, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u_{0},u_{1}}\left[\left(u_{0}-u_{1}\right)\left(u_{0}-u_{1}\right)^{\top}\right]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}[u_{0}u_{0}^{\top}]+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}[u_{1}u_{1}^{\top}]=2I

where we have used the fact that
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}[u_{0}u_{1}^{\top}]=0
by independence.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}}\left[\left(\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right)^{\top}\left(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1}\right)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right)^{\top}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}(2I)\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}\Sigma_{t-1}^{-1/2}\left(\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}^{2}\left\|\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}^{2}.

Inserting this back, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm{Queries}}_{T}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\mathds{1}\left\{\min\left\{1,2\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}^{2}\left\|\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}^{2}\right\}>\epsilon^{2}/\zeta^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\mathds{1}\left\{\min\left\{1,\left\|\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}^{2}\right\}>\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2\zeta^{2}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\zeta^{2}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}\min\left\{1,\left\|\phi(\tau_{t}^{0})-\phi(\tau_{t}^{1})\right\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_{t-1}}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\zeta^{2}\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\cdot 2d\log\left(\frac{\lambda+4T}{\lambda}\right)

where the last step is
Lemma

D.6
.
Plugging the value of
\zeta
and all other variables, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm{Queries}}_{T}\leq\frac{32\sigma_{{\rm{r}}}^{4}d^{2}}{\lambda\epsilon^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\lambda+4T}{\lambda}\right)=\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{d^{4}(\kappa+B^{2})^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\right).

C.1.1
Supporting results from frequentist setting

Consider a conditional probability estimation problem. Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be the instance space and the target space, respectively. Let
\mathcal{F}:(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function class. We are given a dataset
D:=\left\{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
where
x_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}
and
y_{i}\sim f^{\star}(x,\cdot)
. We assume
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}
. Regarding the data generation process, we assume the data distribution
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is history-dependent, i.e.,
x_{i}
can depend on the previous samples:
x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},y_{i-1}
for any
i\in[n]
. Our goal is to estimate the true conditional probability
f^{\star}
using the dataset
D
.

At a high level, this problem is designed to capture both the reward learning and the model learning problems in the RL setting. Specifically, in the reward learning problem, we will instantiate
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{Y}=\{0,1\}
, where we recall that
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
are the state space and the action space, respectively, and the preference feedback is binary. In the model learning problem, we can instantiate
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{S}
. We abstract these two problems into this conditional probability estimation problem to make the analysis more concise. However, one caveat is that all we derived in this section are
frequentist
results, while we are considering
Bayesian
RL. Thus, the results are not directly applicable. In
Section

C.1.2
, we will adapt these frequentist results to the Bayesian setting so that they can be applied.

Now we establish some important results for this problem. First, we have the following lemma, which is a consequence of
Lemma

D.10
.

Lemma C.1

(MLE generalization bound)
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
. Follow the setting stated above.
Let
\widehat{f}
be the maximum likelihood estimator:

\widehat{f}=\mathop{\arg\max}_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f(x_{i},y_{i}).

Define the version space:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{F}}=

\displaystyle\left\{f\in\mathcal{F}\>:\>\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(\widehat{f}(x_{i},\cdot),f(x_{i},\cdot)\right)\leq\beta_{\mathcal{F}}(n)\right\}

for any

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\Big(f(x_{i},\cdot),f^{\prime}(x_{i},\cdot)\Big)\leq 4\beta_{\mathcal{F}}(n).

.

.

\mathcal{F}_{[]}
denote an
(n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1}

\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(f_{[]}(x_{i},\cdot),f^{\star}(x_{i},\cdot)\right)

(12)

Now for any

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\big(f(x_{i},\cdot),f^{\star}(x_{i},\cdot)\big)

\displaystyle\ 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(f_{[]}(x_{i},\cdot),f^{\star}(x_{i},\cdot)\right)+2/n

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 4\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(f_{[]}(x,\cdot),f^{\star}(x,\cdot)\right)+8\log\left(N_{[]}\Big((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)/\delta\right)+2/n

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 8\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(f(x,\cdot),f^{\star}(x,\cdot)\right)+8\log\left(N_{[]}\Big((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)/\delta\right)+10/n.

with probability at least
1-\delta
. Here the second inequality uses (
13
), the third uses (
12
), and the last uses (
14
). Therefore, for any possible value of the estimator
\widehat{f}
, conditioning on
f^{\star}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{F}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\big(\widehat{f}(x_{i},\cdot),f^{\star}(x_{i},\cdot)\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(\widehat{f}(x,\cdot),f^{\star}(x,\cdot)\right)+8\log\left(N_{[]}\Big((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)/\delta\right)+10/n

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 88\log\left(N_{[]}\Big((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)/\delta\right)+10/n

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 98\log\left(N_{[]}\Big((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)/\delta\right)

with probability at least
1-\delta
. Here the second inequality is by the definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{F}}
. The last inequality holds since it is reasonable to assume that the bracketing number is at least some constant so that
\log(N_{[]}((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})/\delta)>1\geq 1/n
.

The above means whenever
f^{\star}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{F}}
, we have
f^{\star}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{F}}
as well with probability at least
1-\delta
. Since
f^{\star}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{F}}
with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have
f^{\star}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{F}}
with probability at least
1-2\delta
. Adjusting
\delta
completes the proof of (i).

For (ii), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\Big(f(x,\cdot),f^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\Big)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(d_{{\rm{TV}}}\Big(f(x,\cdot),\widehat{f}(x,\cdot)\Big)+d_{{\rm{TV}}}\Big(\widehat{f}(x,\cdot),f^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\Big)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}2d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\Big(f(x,\cdot),\widehat{f}(x,\cdot)\Big)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}2d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\Big(\widehat{f}(x,\cdot),f^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\beta_{\mathcal{F}}(n)

where the second inequality is by the fact that
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
for any
a
and
b
, and the third inequality is by the definition of
{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathcal{F}}
.
∎

Next, we further assume that the function class
\mathcal{F}
is parameterized by a function class
\mathcal{G}
via a link function
\Phi
. The reason for this assumption is that we want to capture the structure of the reward learning problem, where the feedback generating distribution is parameterized by the reward function via a link function
\Phi
.

Assumption C.2

(Binary label and function parameterization.)
.

Assume
\mathcal{Y}=\{0,1\}
is binary, and there is a function class
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[0,G]
that parameterizes
\mathcal{F}
via a link function
\Phi
. Specifically, we assume

\mathcal{F}=\Big\{f(x,0)=\Phi\big(g(x)\big),\,f(x,1)=1-\Phi(g(x))\;:\;g\in\mathcal{G}\Big\},

where we further assume
\Phi
satisfies
3.1
. For any
f\in\mathcal{F}
, let
g_{f}
denote the function
g
that parameterizes
f
. We define
\widehat{g}\coloneqq g_{\widehat{f}}
and
g^{\star}\coloneqq g_{f^{\star}}
.

As a preliminary note, the function class
\mathcal{G}
will actually correspond to the function class
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
(
3
) later in the proof.

We should emphasize again that, although the function class
\mathcal{G}
aims to capture the structure of the reward learning problem, the function class
\mathcal{F}
will be used to capture both the reward learning and the model learning problems. When it is applied to the model learning problem, we can simply ignore the function class
\mathcal{G}
and any results that are related to
\mathcal{G}
. On the other hand, when it is applied to the reward learning problem, such a function class
\mathcal{G}
will be helpful to derive some results in the reward function itself.

Before diving into the analysis of the function class
\mathcal{G}
, we first introduce the following lemma which shows that the bracketing numbers of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are bounded by each other.

Lemma C.3
.

Under
C.2
, for any
\omega>0
, we have

\displaystyle N_{[]}\Big(\omega,\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)\leq N_{[]}\Big(\overline{\kappa}\omega,\mathcal{G},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad N_{[]}\Big(\kappa\omega,\mathcal{G},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)\leq N_{[]}\Big(\omega,\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big).

Proof.

For any
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
, we assume they are parameterized by
g\coloneqq g_{f}
and
g^{\prime}\coloneqq g_{f^{\prime}}
, respectively. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y}\left|f(x,y)-f^{\prime}(x,y))\right|=\sup_{x}\left|\Phi\big(g(x)\big)-\Phi\big(g^{\prime}(x)\big)\right|\leq\overline{\kappa}^{-1}\sup_{x}\left|g(x)-g^{\prime}(x)\right|

where the inequality is
Lemma

D.1
. Hence, if we have a
\overline{\kappa}\omega
-bracket of
\mathcal{G}
in the infinite norm, then we have an
\omega
-bracket of
\mathcal{F}
in the infinite norm. This proves the first claim. For the second claim, by
Lemma

D.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\left|g(x)-g^{\prime}(x)\right|\leq\kappa\sup_{x}\left|\Phi\big(g(x)\big)-\Phi\big(g^{\prime}(x)\big)\right|=\kappa\sup_{x,y}\left|f(x,y)-f^{\prime}(x,y)\right|.

This proves the second claim.
∎

Then,
Lemma

C.1
leads to similar results with a version space constructed on
\mathcal{G}
.

Corollary C.4
.

Under
C.2
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{G}}=

\displaystyle\left\{g\in\mathcal{G}\>:\>\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\widehat{g}(x_{i})-g(x_{i})\Big)^{2}\leq\beta_{\mathcal{G}}(n)\right\}

where
\beta_{\mathcal{G}}(n)\coloneqq 98\kappa^{2}\log(2N_{[]}(\overline{\kappa}(n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{G},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})/\delta)
and we denote
\widehat{g}\coloneqq g_{\widehat{f}}
as the function that parameterizes the maximum likelihood estimator
\widehat{f}
. Then, the following holds

(1)

g^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{G}}
with probability at least
1-\delta

(2)

for any
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{G}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(g(x_{i})-g^{\prime}(x_{i})\Big)^{2}\leq 4\beta_{\mathcal{G}}(n).

Proof of
Corollary

C.4
.

To prove (i), we claim that for any
f\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{F}}
(defined in
Lemma

C.1
), we have
g_{f}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{G}}
as well. To see this, we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}\Big(\widehat{g}(x)-g_{f}(x)\Big)^{2}\leq\kappa^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}\left|\Phi\big(\widehat{g}(x)\big)-\Phi\big(g_{f}(x)\big)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}\left|\widehat{f}(x,0)-f(x,0)\right|^{2}=\kappa^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(\widehat{f}(x,\cdot),f(x,\cdot)\right)

where the inequality is
Lemma

D.1
, and the last equality holds since we assume
\mathcal{Y}=\{0,1\}
is binary. Hence, for any
f\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{F}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}\Big(\widehat{g}(x)-g_{f}(x)\Big)^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(\widehat{f}(x,\cdot),f(x,\cdot)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\beta_{\mathcal{F}}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\cdot 98\log(2N_{[]}((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})/\delta)

By
Lemma

C.3
, we have

\displaystyle\log(2N_{[]}((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})/\delta)\leq\log(2N_{[]}(\overline{\kappa}(n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{G},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})/\delta).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}\Big(\widehat{g}(x)-g_{f}(x)\Big)^{2}\leq\beta_{\mathcal{G}}(n)

which implies
g_{f}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{G}}
. Therefore, whenever
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{F}}
, we have
g_{f^{\star}}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{G}}
as well. Since
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{F}}
with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have
g_{f^{\star}}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{G}}
with probability at least
1-\delta
. This completes the proof of (i).

Now we prove (2). For any
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{G}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(g(x_{i})-g^{\prime}(x_{i})\Big)^{2}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\widehat{g}(x_{i})-g(x_{i})\Big)^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\widehat{g}(x_{i})-g^{\prime}(x_{i})\Big)^{2}\leq 4\beta_{\mathcal{G}}(n)

where the first inequality is by the fact that
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
for any
a
and
b
.
∎

C.1.2
Adapting results into Bayesian setting

Now we change the setting defined in
Section

C.1.1
into a Bayesian online setting. The reason for this adaptation is to make these results applicable to the Bayesian RL setting. We formally define the Bayesian online conditional probability estimation problem below.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be the instance space and the target space, respectively. Let
\mathcal{F}:(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function class. The interaction proceeds for
T
rounds. At each round
t\in[T]
, we observe an instance
x_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
and need to outputs a function
f_{t}\in\mathcal{F}
as our prediction. Then, the label
y_{t}\in\mathcal{Y}
is revealed. We assume that
x_{i}\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}
for some distribution
\mathcal{D}_{i}
and
y_{i}\sim f^{\star}(x,\cdot)
. We assume the true conditional distribution
f^{\star}
is sampled from some known prior distribution
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{F})
at the beginning. Regarding the data generation process, we assume the data distribution
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is history-dependent, i.e.,
x_{i}
can depend on the previous samples:
x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},y_{i-1}
for any
i\in[n]
.

Denote
\mathcal{H}_{t}
as the history up to round
t
, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\{x_{1},f_{1},y_{1},x_{2},f_{2},y_{2},\dots,x_{t},f_{t},y_{t}\}
. Define the maximum likelihood estimator of
f^{\star}
on the dataset
\{(x_{s},y_{s})\}_{s=1}^{t-1}
as
\widehat{f}_{t}
, i.e.,

\widehat{f}_{t}=\mathop{\arg\max}_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\log f(x_{s},y_{s}).

Similar to the previous section, we will also consider the presence of a function class
\mathcal{G}
(
C.2
) in the analysis presented in this section. It’s worth noting that although
C.2
is initially introduced in the frequentist setting, when we mention that
C.2
holds in this section, we are actually considering the Bayesian setting.

Lemma C.5
.

It holds that

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{H}_{t-1}}\left[\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{f,f^{\prime}}\Big[d_{{\rm{TV}}}\big(f(x_{t}),f^{\prime}(x_{t})\big)\,\Big|\,\mathcal{H}_{t-1}\Big]\right]\leq 6\sqrt{T\beta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}(T)}\cdot{\rm{dim}}_{1}\Big(\mathcal{F},1/T\Big)\cdot\log(T)

(15)

and

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{H}_{t-1}}\left[\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{f,f^{\prime}}\Big[d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\big(f(x_{t}),f^{\prime}(x_{t})\big)\,\Big|\,\mathcal{H}_{t-1}\Big]\right]\leq 4\beta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}(T)+2

(16)

where
\beta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}(t)\coloneqq 98\log(2TN_{[]}((t|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}))
, and
f,f^{\prime}
are sampled from the posterior of
f^{\star}
conditioning on
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
in the inner conditional expectation. Moreover, if
\mathcal{F}
is parameterized by
\mathcal{G}
(i.e., if
C.2
holds), we have

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{H}_{t-1}}\left[\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{g,g^{\prime}}\Big[\big|g(x_{t})-g^{\prime}(x_{t})\big|\,\Big|\,\mathcal{H}_{t-1}\Big]\right]\leq 6\sqrt{T\beta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{G}}(T)}\cdot{\rm{dim}}_{1}\Big(\mathcal{G},1/T\Big)\cdot\log(GT)

and

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathcal{H}_{t-1}}\left[\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{g,g^{\prime}}\Big[\big(g(x_{t})-g^{\prime}(x_{t})\big)^{2}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{H}_{t-1}\Big]\right]\leq 4\beta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{G}}(T)+2

(18)

where
\beta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\coloneqq 98\kappa^{2}\log(2GTN_{[]}(\overline{\kappa}(t|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{G},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}))

g,g^{\prime}
are sampled from the posterior of
g^{\star}
conditioning on
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
in the inner conditional expectation.

C.4
Generalizing Theorem

through SEC

In this section, we show that the dependence on the eluder dimension in Theorem

can be generalized to the
Sequential Extrapolation Coefficient (SEC)

(
Xie et al. 2022
)
. It have been shown by
Xie et al. 2022
that the SEC can be upper bounded by the eluder dimenion, the Bellman-eluder dimension
(
Jin et al. 2021
)
, and the bilinear rank
(
Du et al. 2021
)
. Thus, SEC is a more general measure of complexity.

We start by introducing the Bayesian Sequential Extrapolation Coefficient (Bayesian SEC) in the preference-based feedback, which is a variant of the original SEC to accommodate the Bayesian and preference-based learning setting. We define the Bayesian SEC for a model class
\mathcal{P}
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{BayesSEC_{P}}(\mathcal{P})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{r^{\star},P^{\star}}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1},\dots,P_{T}\in\mathcal{P}\\
(\pi_{1}^{0},\pi_{1}^{1}),\dots,(\pi_{T}^{0},\pi_{T}^{1})\end{subarray}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\left(\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s,a\sim d^{\pi_{t}}_{h}}\left[d_{{\rm{TV}}}\left(P_{t}(\cdot\,|\,s,a),P^{\star}(\cdot\,|\,s,a)\right)\right]\right)^{2}}{1\lor\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{s,a\sim d^{\pi^{i}}_{h}}\left[d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(P_{t}(\cdot\,|\,s,a),P^{\star}(\cdot\,|\,s,a)\right)\right]}\right]

and the Bayesian SEC for a reward function class
\mathcal{R}
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{BayesSEC_{R}}(\mathcal{R})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{r^{\star},P^{\star}}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},\dots,r_{T}\in\mathcal{R}\\
(\pi_{1}^{0},\pi_{1}^{1}),\dots,(\pi_{T}^{0},\pi_{T}^{1})\end{subarray}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau_{t}^{0}\sim\pi_{t}^{0},\tau_{t}^{1}\sim\pi_{t}^{1}}\Big[\left(r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0})\right)^{2}\Big]}{1\lor\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau_{i}^{0}\sim\pi_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1}\sim\pi_{i}^{1}}\Big[\big(r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{0})\big)^{2}\Big]}\right]

We note that the Bayesian SEC can be easily reduced to the frequentist SEC by specifying the prior on
r^{\star}
and
P^{\star}
to be the Dirac delta function. We note that our definition of the reward function SEC involves squaring within the expectation in the numerator rather than externally, potentially making it larger.

\displaystyle H\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(s_{h},a_{h})\sim d^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{h}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}\Big(P_{t}(s_{h},a_{h}),P^{\star}(s_{h},a_{h})\Big)}{\sqrt{1\lor\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(s_{h},a_{h})\sim d^{\pi_{i}^{0}}_{h}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\Big(P_{t}(s_{h},a_{h}),P^{\star}(s_{h},a_{h})\Big)}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\sqrt{1\lor\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(s_{h},a_{h})\sim d^{\pi_{i}^{0}}_{h}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\Big(P_{t}(s_{h},a_{h}),P^{\star}(s_{h},a_{h})\Big)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle H\underbrace{\sqrt{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\frac{\left(\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(s_{h},a_{h})\sim d^{\pi_{t}^{0}}_{h}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}\Big(P_{t}(s_{h},a_{h}),P^{\star}(s_{h},a_{h})\Big)\right)^{2}}{1\lor\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(s_{h},a_{h})\sim d^{\pi_{i}^{0}}_{h}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\Big(P_{t}(s_{h},a_{h}),P^{\star}(s_{h},a_{h})\Big)}\right]}}_{\text{(i)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\underbrace{\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[1\lor\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(s_{h},a_{h})\sim d^{\pi_{i}^{0}}_{h}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\Big(P_{t}(s_{h},a_{h}),P^{\star}(s_{h},a_{h})\Big)\right]}}_{\text{(ii)}}

Here we note that
a\lor b\coloneqq\max(a,b)\leq a+b
for
a,b\geq 0
. The last step above is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. We observe that term (i) is exactly bounded by the Bayesian SEC of models. For term (ii), we can invoke (
16
) in
Lemma

C.5
since
P_{t}
has an identical posterior distribution as
P^{\star}
given the historical data up to round
t-1
. This gives
\text{(ii)}\leq\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{TH\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{P}}})
.
Plugging these back, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{\rm{model}}\leq\widetilde{O}\left(H\sqrt{\mathsf{BayesSEC_{P}}(\mathcal{P})\cdot TH\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{P}}}\right)

Bounding
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{reward}}
.

The reward part can be bounded similarly, except that we need to additionally consider the query conditions. To begin with, by the definition of state-value function, we have

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{\rm{reward}}=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0})\bigg]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[(1-Z_{t})\bigg(r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0})\bigg)\bigg]}_{\text{(i)}}+\underbrace{\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[Z_{t}\bigg(r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0})\bigg)\bigg]}_{\text{(ii)}}.

Following the same argument as in the proof of
Section

C.2
, we can bound (i) as

\text{(i)}\leq T\epsilon
.
Now we proceed to bound (ii), which is the regret incurred when making queries. By multiplying and dividing by the same term, we obtain the following.

\displaystyle\text{(ii)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi_{t}^{0},\pi_{t}^{1},r_{t},Z_{t}}\left[\frac{\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z_{t}\left(r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0})\right)\,\middle|\,\pi_{t}^{0},\pi_{t}^{1},r_{t},Z_{t}\right]}{\sqrt{1\lor\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z_{t}\left(r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{0})\right)^{2}\,\middle|\,\pi_{t}^{0},\pi_{t}^{1},r_{t},Z_{t}\right]}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\sqrt{1\lor\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tau_{t}^{0},\tau_{t}^{1}}\left[Z_{t}\bigg(r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{0})\bigg)^{2}\,\middle|\,\pi_{t}^{0},\pi_{t}^{1},r_{t},Z_{t}\right]}\;\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi_{t}^{0},\pi_{t}^{1},r_{t},Z_{t}}\left[\frac{\Big(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z_{t}\left(r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{t}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{t}^{0})\right)\,\middle|\,\pi_{t}^{0},\pi_{t}^{1},r_{t},Z_{t}\right]\Big)^{2}}{1\lor\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z_{t}\left(r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{0})\right)^{2}\,\middle|\,\pi_{t}^{0},\pi_{t}^{1},r_{t},Z_{t}\right]}\right]}}_{\text{(iii)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\underbrace{\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T}\left(1\lor\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z_{t}\bigg(r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{0})-r_{t}(\tau_{i}^{1})+r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{1})-r^{\star}(\tau_{i}^{0})\bigg)^{2}\right]\right)}}_{\text{(iv)}}

where the last inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. We observe that term (iii) is exactly bounded by the Bayesian SEC of reward functions via Jensen’s inequality. For term (iv), we invoke (
18
) in
Lemma

C.5
and get

\text{(iv)}\leq\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{\kappa^{2}T\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{R}}})
.
Now plugging these upper bounds back, we get

\displaystyle\mathtt{T}_{\rm{reward}}\leq T\epsilon+\widetilde{O}\left(\sqrt{\mathsf{BayesSEC_{R}}(\mathcal{R})\cdot\kappa^{2}T\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{R}}}\right).

Conclusion.

Given the upper bounds on
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{model}}
and
\mathtt{T}_{\rm{reward}}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle{\rm{BayesRegret}}_{T}\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{O}\Bigg(H\sqrt{\mathsf{BayesSEC_{P}}(\mathcal{P})\cdot TH\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{P}}}+T\epsilon+\sqrt{\mathsf{BayesSEC_{R}}(\mathcal{R})\cdot\kappa^{2}T\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{R}}}\Bigg).

Lemma D.3

(Gaussian concentration)
.

Let

denotes the

\displaystyle\Pr\Big(\|\eta\|_{2}>\alpha\sqrt{d}\Big)\leq\Pr\Big(\exists i:|\eta_{i}|>\alpha\Big)\leq d\Pr\Big(|\eta_{1}|>\alpha\Big)\leq d\cdot 2e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}

The following lemma converts the TV distance between Gaussian distributions into the distance between their means.

Lemma D.8
.

Assume
p_{1}=\mathcal{N}(\mu_{1},\sigma^{2}I)
and
p_{2}=\mathcal{N}(\mu_{2},\sigma^{2}I)
are two Gaussian distributions over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume
\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{2}\leq m
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{2}\cdot\underbrace{\min\left\{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2e\pi\sigma^{2}}},\frac{1}{2m}\right\}}_{=:\ C_{1}}\leq d_{{\rm{TV}}}(p_{1},p_{2})\leq\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{2}\cdot\underbrace{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}}_{=:\ C_{2}}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can rotate and translate the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
space and assume that
p_{1}=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
and
p_{2}=\mathcal{N}(\alpha\cdot e_{1},\sigma^{2}I)
where
\alpha=\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{2}
and
e_{1}=[1,0,\dots,0]\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is clear that the total variation distance is preserved. Then, by definition,

\displaystyle d_{{\rm{TV}}}(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p_{1}(x)-p_{2}(x)|\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{x\cdot e_{1}\leq\alpha/2}p_{1}(x)-p_{2}(x)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x

where the last step holds since
p_{1}(x)-p_{2}(x)\geq 0
if and only if
x\cdot e_{1}\leq\alpha/2
. Note that the above formulation is independent of any dimension except for first dimension, so we can marginize other dimensions and reduce into a one-dimensional problem:

\displaystyle d_{{\rm{TV}}}(p_{1},p_{2})=

\displaystyle\int_{x\leq\alpha/2}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,\sigma^{2})-\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,\alpha,\sigma^{2})\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(F(\alpha/(2\sigma))-F(-\alpha/(2\sigma))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\alpha/(2\sigma)}^{\alpha/(2\sigma)}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,1)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x

where we denote
{\rm{F}}
as the CDF of standard Gaussian
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
in the middle step. Below we show that the above integral is upper bounded by
C_{2}\alpha
and lower bounded by
C_{1}\alpha
.

Upper bound.

For an upper bound, we note that for any
v>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-v}^{v}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,1)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2v\cdot\max_{x}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,1)\leq v\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}.

Inserting
v=\alpha/(2\sigma)
, we obtain the desired upper bound.

Lower bound.

For a lower bound, we note that for any
0<v\leq m/(2\sigma)
, if
v<1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-v}^{v}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,1)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\geq 2v\max_{x\in[-v,v]}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,1)\geq 2v\mathcal{N}(1\,|\,0,1)=2v\cdot\frac{e^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=v\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2}{e\pi}}.

When
v\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-v}^{v}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,1)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{-\sigma}^{\sigma}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,1)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\approx 0.6827\geq v\cdot\frac{1}{2v}\geq v\cdot\frac{\sigma}{m}.

Combining the two cases together, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-v}^{v}\mathcal{N}(x\,|\,0,1)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x\geq v\cdot\min\left\{\sqrt{\frac{2}{e\pi}},\,\frac{\sigma}{m}\right\}.

Inserting
v=\alpha/(2\sigma)
, we obtain the desired lower bound.
∎

Lemma D.9
.

Let
x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
and
A
be a positive semi-definite matrix. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\|x\|_{A}\geq\sqrt{\frac{2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A)}{\pi}}.

Proof.

Let
P
denote the orthogonal matrix diagonalizing
A
, i.e.,
A=P\Lambda P^{\top}
where
\Lambda={\rm diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})
. Then, we have
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\|x\|_{A}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\sqrt{x^{\top}Ax}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{y}\sqrt{y^{\top}\Lambda y}
where
y=P^{\top}x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
. Now we consider the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{M\in\mathcal{M}}f(M)\coloneqq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{y}\sqrt{y^{\top}My}\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\{M\succeq 0:M\text{ is diagonal and }\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Lambda)\}.

Then, we have
\Lambda\in\mathcal{M}
and
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\|x\|_{A}=f(\Lambda)
. Thus, the solution to the above optimization problem is a lower bound of
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\|x\|_{A}
. Since
f(M)
is concave in
M
, it must attain the minimum on the bounary of
\mathcal{M}
. By symmetry of entries in the diagonal, we know that the minimizer must have
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Lambda)
at one entry and 0 at all other entries. Hence, the minimum value is

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{y}\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Lambda)y_{1}^{2}}=\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Lambda)}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{y}|y_{1}|=\sqrt{\frac{2\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Lambda)}{\pi}}.

We finish the proof by noticing that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\Lambda)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A)
.
∎

Lemma D.10

(Maximum likelihood estimation)
.

(

Consider a sequential conditional probability estimation problem. Let

Then, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}d_{{\rm{TV}}}^{2}\left(\widehat{f}(x,\cdot),f^{\star}(x,\cdot)\right)\leq 10\log\left(N_{[]}\Big((n|\mathcal{Y}|)^{-1},\mathcal{F},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Big)/\delta\right)

(21)

with probability at least
1-\delta
. Here
|\mathcal{Y}|
denotes the total measure of space
\mathcal{Y}
, i.e.,
|\mathcal{Y}|\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{Y}}1\mathop{}\mathrm{d}y
.

D.1
Eluder dimension

The following lemmas are adapted from
Russo & Van Roy 2013
and
Liu et al. 2022a
.

Lemma D.11
.

Following the notation of
Definition

5.2
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{F}\>:\>\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}d^{2}\big(f(x_{s}),\widehat{f}_{t}(x_{s})\big)\leq\beta\right\}

where
\widehat{f}_{t}\in\mathcal{F}
is an arbitrary function. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathds{1}\left\{\sup_{f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}d\left(f(x_{t}),f^{\prime}(x_{t})\right)\geq\omega\right\}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\sqrt{T\beta}}{\omega}+1\right){\rm{dim}}_{1}(\mathcal{F},\omega),

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathds{1}\left\{\sup_{f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}d\left(f(x_{t}),f^{\prime}(x_{t})\right)\geq\omega\right\}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4\beta}{\omega^{2}}+1\right){\rm{dim}}_{2}(\mathcal{F},\omega)

(23)

for any constant
\omega>0
.

Proof of
Lemma

D.11
.

We first prove (
22
) and then (
23
).

Proof of (
22
).

We begin by showing that if

\displaystyle\sup_{f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}d\left(f(x_{t}),f^{\prime}(x_{t})\right)\geq\omega

for some
t\in[T]
, then
x_{t}
is
\omega
-dependent on at most
2\beta/\omega
disjoint subsequence of its predecessors. To see this, we note that, if
x_{t}
is
\omega
-dependent on a subsequence
(x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{n}})
of its predecessors, we must have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{n}d\big(f(x_{i_{s}})-f^{\prime}(x_{i_{s}})\big)>\omega.

Hence, if
x_{t}
is
\omega
-dependent on
l
disjoint subsequences, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}d\big(f(x_{s}),f^{\prime}(x_{s})\big)>l\omega.

(24)

For the left-hand side, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}d\big(f(x_{s}),f^{\prime}(x_{s})\big)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}d\big(f(x_{s}),\widehat{f}_{t}(x_{s})\big)+\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}d\big(\widehat{f}_{t}(x_{s}),f^{\prime}(x_{s})\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{T}\sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}d^{2}\big(f(x_{s}),\widehat{f}_{t}(x_{s})\big)}+\sqrt{T}\sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}d^{2}\big(\widehat{f}_{t}(x_{s}),f^{\prime}(x_{s})\big)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{T\beta}

(25)

where the first inequality is the triangle inequality, and the second inequality holds by the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Combining (
24
) and (
25
), we get that
l\leq 2\sqrt{T\beta}/\omega
.

Next, we show that for any sequence
(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{\tau})
, there is at least one element that is
\omega
-dependent on at least
\tau/d-1
disjoint subsequence of its predecessors, where
d:={\rm{dim}}_{1}(\mathcal{F},\omega)
. To show this, let
m
be the integer satisfying
md+1\leq\tau\leq md+d
. We will construct
m
disjoint subsequences,
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}
. At the beginning, let
B_{i}=(x^{\prime}_{i})
for
i\in[m]
. If
x^{\prime}_{m+1}
is
\omega
-dependent on each subsequence
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}
, then we are done. Otherwise, we select a subsequence
B_{i}
which
x^{\prime}_{m+1}
is
\omega
-independent of and append
x^{\prime}_{m+1}
to
B_{i}
. We repeat this process for all elements with indices
j>m+1
until either
x^{\prime}_{j}
is
\omega
-dependent on each subsequence or
j=\tau
. For the latter, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|B_{i}|\geq md
, and since each element of a subsequence
B_{i}
is
\omega
-independent of its predecesors, we must have
|B_{i}|=d
for all
i
. Then,
x_{\tau}
must be
\omega
-dependent on each subsequence by the definition of eluder dimension.

Finally, let’s set the sequence
(x^{\prime}_{1},\dots x^{\prime}_{\tau})
to be the subsequence of
(x_{1},\dots,x_{T})
consisting of elements
x_{t}
for which
d\left(f(x_{t}),f^{\prime}(x_{t})\right)>\omega
. As we have established, we have

1.

each
x^{\prime}_{i}
is
\ell_{1}
-norm
\omega
-dependent on at most
2\sqrt{T\beta}/\omega
disjoint subsequences, and

2.

some
x^{\prime}_{i}
is
\ell_{1}
-norm
\omega
-dependent on at least
\tau/d-1
disjoint subsequences.

Therefore, we must have
\tau/d-1\leq 2\sqrt{T\beta}/\omega
, implying that
\tau\leq(2\sqrt{T\beta}/\omega+1)d
.

Proof of (
23
).

The proof is quite similar to the proof of (
22
). For
\ell_{2}
-norm, following the same argument, we can show the following:

1.

each
x^{\prime}_{i}
is
\ell_{2}
-norm
\omega
-dependent on at most
4\beta/\omega^{2}
disjoint subsequences, and

2.

some
x^{\prime}_{i}
is
\ell_{2}
-norm
\omega
-dependent on at least
\tau/d-1
disjoint subsequences.

Therefore, we must have
\tau/d-1\leq 4\beta/\omega^{2}
, implying that
\tau\leq(4\beta/\omega^{2}+1)d
.
∎

Lemma D.12
.

Following the setting of
Lemma

D.11
, assume
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is upper bounded by
D\leq\beta
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sup_{f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}d\big(f(x_{t}),f^{\prime}(x_{t})\big)\leq 4\sqrt{T\beta}\cdot{\rm{dim}}_{1}(\mathcal{F},1/T)\cdot\log(DT)

Proof of
Lemma

D.12
.

For notational simplicity, we denote

\displaystyle w_{t}\coloneqq\sup_{f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}d\big(f(x_{t}),f^{\prime}(x_{t})\big)

Now we fix a constant
\Delta>0
, and then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{t}=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\int_{0}^{D}\mathds{1}\{w_{t}\geq\delta\}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{D}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathds{1}\{w_{t}\geq\delta\}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta T+\int_{\Delta}^{D}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathds{1}\{w_{t}\geq\delta\}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta T+\int_{\Delta}^{D}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{T\beta}}{\delta}+1\right){\rm{dim}}_{1}(\mathcal{F},\delta)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta T+\int_{\Delta}^{D}\frac{3\sqrt{T\beta}}{\delta}\cdot{\rm{dim}}_{1}(\mathcal{F},\delta)\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta T+3\sqrt{T\beta}\cdot{\rm{dim}}_{1}(\mathcal{F},\Delta)\cdot\log(D/\Delta)

where the second inequality is
Lemma

D.11
, and the last inequality uses the fact that
{\rm{dim}}_{1}(\mathcal{F},\delta)
is non-increasing in
\delta
. We can conclude the proof by setting
\Delta=1/T
.
∎

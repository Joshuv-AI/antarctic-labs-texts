1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a bounded symmetric domain and
X:=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{H}
be a quotient of
\mathcal{H}
by a torsion-free lattice
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{H})
. Various hyperbolicity properties of a toroidal compactification
\overline{X}
of
X
and curves in
\overline{X}
have been proved by studying the positivity of
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles
K_{\overline{X}}(D)-rD
, where
D
is the boundary divisor,
K_{\overline{X}}(D)
is the log-canonical bundle and
r
is a number varying on an interval, the size of which depends on
X
. For instance, see
[
Nad89
,
SB05
]
for the case of moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties
\mathcal{A}_{g},

[
BT18a
]
for the case of Hilbert modular varieties
and
[
BT18b
,
DCDC15
]

for the case of ball quotients.

Motivated by these results for the twists of
K_{\overline{X}}(D)
and their implications for the hyperbolicity properties, we study the positivity of the twists of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)
and
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
in the form of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundles
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\langle-rD\rangle
and
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\langle-rD\rangle
. As the vector bundles
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)
and
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
restrict well to subvarieties (of arbitrary dimensions), the positivity of these bundles reveals various hyperbolicity properties of subvarieties of
\overline{X}
.

In this paper, we focus on this problem for a complex ball quotient
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n},
where
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is a torsion-free lattice. We refer to
X
as a complex hyperbolic manifold with cusps, where cusps correspond to parabolic fixed points of
\Gamma
. For cusps of
X
we used a uniform depth
d
to measure the size of the largest embedded cusp neighborhoods which are disjoint from each other (see
2.3
).
We know that the uniform depth of cusps is at least
2
and it tends to infinity in the cofinal towers of normal covering (see Lemma
2.6
).

Our first main result is the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a complex hyperbolic manifold with cusps whose toroidal compactification
\overline{X}
has no orbifold points, and
d
be the uniform depth of cusps. Then, the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle

\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\langle-rD\rangle

is ample for all rational
r\in(0,d/4\pi)
.

It was known that for small enough
\epsilon>0,
the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\langle-\epsilon D\rangle

is big. Combining this with the observation of Mumford
[
Mum77
, section 4]
, Brunebarbe(
[
Bru20b
]
) and Cadorel(
[
Cad21b
]
) proved that for a cover
X^{\prime}\to X
such that the ramification index of each cusp is at least
l
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X^{\prime}}}\big(\log(D^{\prime})\big)\langle-l\epsilon D^{\prime}\rangle

is big, where
\overline{X^{\prime}}
is the toroidal compactification of
X^{\prime}
, and
D^{\prime}
is the boundary divisor. By assuming that the ramification index
l
is sufficiently large, they conclude the bigness of the cotangent bundle
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X^{\prime}}}
on the cover
X^{\prime}
(see
[
Cad21b
, Theorem 4]
and
[
Bru20b
, sec 6.2]
).

Theorem
A
, however, not only has stronger positivity conclusion for the twisted bundle, but also it has an advantage that it implies the positivity result for
X
itself rather than some cover of
X.
Notably, in Theorem
A
, the quantity
r
can be chosen to be a rational number close to
1/2\pi
for all
X,
since
d
is always greater than or equal to
2.
In particular, Theorem
A
can be applied when
X
is not a cover of other locally symmetric domains, i.e, in the case that
\Gamma
is a maximal lattice in
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
(for every even
n,
there exits infinitely many such lattices, see for example
[
ES14
]
).

Assuming that the uniform depth of cusps is sufficiently large, we showed that
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
is semi-ample and is ample modulo
D:

Theorem B
.

With
X
and
\overline{X}
as in Theorem
A
, suppose that the uniform depth of cusps is greater than
4\pi
. Then, the cotangent bundle
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
is semi-ample and is ample modulo
D
.

It is remarkable that for some towers of coverings, the uniform depth of cusps can increase while the ramification index remains unchanged. For instance, the uniform depth of cusps of the classical modular curve
X_{0}(p)
tends to infinity as
p
approaches infinity; however,
X_{0}(p)\to X(1)
has a cusp with a ramification index of
1
for every
p
(see Remark
2.7
for details). Such towers play a crucial role in number theory, as seen in Wiles’s proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem (see
[
Wil95
]
) and Mazur’s torsion theorem (see
[
MG78
]
). Higher-dimensional analogues of these towers are natural objects to study for generalizing these theorems (see
[
BT18a
]
). Proving the hyperbolicity properties of these towers, which have a cusp with ramification index
1
, is not within reach with the methods used in
[
Bru20b
,
Bru20a
,
Cad21b
]
.

Semi-ampleness of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
in Theorem
B
implies that symmetric differentials over
\overline{X}
forms a finitely generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra(see
[
Laz17
, Example 2.1.29]
):

Corollary C
.

With the same assumptions as Theorem
B
, the graded ring

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H^{0}(\overline{X},S^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}})

is finitely generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

In Theorem
A
,
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)
can not be replaced by
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
without adding any extra assumptions about
X
because there are examples due to Hirzebruch
[
Hir84
]
of the toroidal compactification of a 2-dimensional ball quotient whose canonical bundle is not even nef. We showed that assuming the uniform depth is sufficiently large the replacement is possible as long as
r
varies on a smaller interval.

Theorem D
.

With
X
and
\overline{X}
as in Theorem
A
, suppose that the uniform depth of cusps
d
is greater than
4\pi
. Then, the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle

\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\langle-rD\rangle

is ample for all rational
r\in(0,-1+d/4\pi)
.

As the cotangent bundle is well-behaved under the restriction to subvarieties, by virtue of Theorem
D
we get hyperbolicity of subvarieties of an arbitrary dimension in the following sense:

Corollary E
.

With the same notations as in Theorem
A
, suppose
V
is a smooth subvariety of
\overline{X}
intersecting
X
with dimension
m>0.
Then,

K_{V}-(r-1)D_{|V}

is ample for all rational
r\in(0,\frac{dm}{4\pi}).
In particular, if
m>6
or
d>4\pi
, then
K_{V}
is ample.

Corollary F
.

With the same
X
as Theorem
A
, all subvarieties of
X
are of general type provided that the uniform depth of cusps is greater than
4\pi.

As explained after Theorem
A
, Brunebarbe and Cadorel proved the bigness of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X^{\prime}}},
with the assumption that
X^{\prime}
is a highly ramified cover of
X.
Then, they applied the difficult theorem of Campana-Păun’s
[
CP15
]
to conclude that all subvarieties of
X^{\prime}
are of general type. Beside the fact that Corollary
F
tells us strong hyperbolicity of
X
itself, rather than cover of
X,
our approach has another advantage that it bypass the theorem of Campana-Păun to prove all subvarities of
X
are of general type because of nice restriction properties of ampleness.

Bakker and Tsimerman
[
BT18b
]
proved that
K_{\overline{X}}(D)-rD
is ample for
r\in(0,(n+1)/2)
, where
n=dim\ X
and concluded that if
n\geq 6
,
K_{\overline{X}}
is ample. Similarly, Cadorel
[
Cad21a
]
showed that
V\subset\overline{X}
with
dim\ V\geq 6
is of general type if
V\not\subset D
.

In general, for a smooth variety with dimension greater than 1, the log-cotangent bundle is never ample as its restriction to the boundary is an extension of the trivial bundle.(
[
ADT22
]
,
[
BD18
]
). For a toroidal compactification of a locally symmetric domain, Brunebarbe
[
Bru18
]
proved that
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)
is big. In the case of ball quotients,
Theorem
A
demonstrates that
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)
is a limit of ample
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundles. Moreover, we can deduce from Theorem
A
the following Corollary:

Corollary G
.

With
X
and
\overline{X}
as in Theorem
A
,

\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)
is ample modulo
D
and nef.

In particular, Corollary
G
implies that

\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)
is big and nef, which Cadorel also recently proved (
[
Cad21b
]
). He used the positivity of the Bergman metric on the open part together with a result of
[
Bou02
]
to show that the volume of the line bundle
O(1)
over
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}})
must be positive which implies that
O(1)
is big. Similarly, he proved that
O(1)
over
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}})
is nef by showing that the volume of the pull back of
O(1)

over
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}})
to every subvariety of
\overline{X}
not contained in
D
is positive.

The volume of a line bundle
L
on a projective variety
V
of dimension
m
is defined to the non-negative real number

\operatorname{vol}_{V}(L):=\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{h^{0}(V,L^{\otimes k})}{k^{m}/m!}

which measures the asymptotic growth rate of the pluri-canonical linear series
|kL|.

Brunebarbe
[
Bru20a
]
used the volume of the canonical bundle of a variety to measure its hyperbolicity. In the setting of Shimura varieties (actually in more general setting, see
[
Bru20a
, Theorem 1.1.]
), he proved that the minimum volume of subvarieties of
Y(p),

Y
with full level structure, tends to infinity. As a generalization of Brunebarbe’s work
[
Bru20a
]
, for cofinal towers of normal coverings of ball quotients we prove the following Corollary:

Corollary H
.

Let
X
be a complex hyperbolic manifold with cusps and
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a cofinal tower of normal coverings of
X=X_{1}.

Then, given a positive number
v
, for all but finitely many
i,
every subvariety
V
of
X_{i}
containing a cusp of
X_{i}
have
vol(K_{V})>v.

Indeed, we show that the volume of the canonical bundle (and the log-canonical bundle) of subvarieties of
X
containing a cusp of
X,
is controlled by the uniform depth of cusps (see Corollary
7.3
and Corollary
7.5
).

Using results of
L^{2}
-estimates for a Kähler manifold, Wong
[
Won18
]
proved that there exist a constant
r(n)
and
d(n)
depending only on the dimension of
X
, such that if the injectivity radius is larger than
r(n)
and the uniform depth of cusps is larger than
d(n)
, then
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
is ample modulo
D
. His method relies on the existence theorem of
L^{2}
-estimation (
[
AV65
,
H6̈5
]
).

Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
substantially improve the previous results of Cadorel and Wong for the positivity of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)
and
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
, and our proofs are independent from their proofs.

Our method to prove Theorem
A
is to construct a singular hermitian metric on
L:=O(1)
over
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big))
with the Bergman metric
h
induced on
L
and a carefully chosen weight function. To obtain the positivity of the twisted bundle, there has to be a trade-off between the positivity of
h
and the weight function. This trade-off comes from the inequality we proved between
c_{1}(L_{|\pi^{-1}(X)},h)
and a positive
(1,1)
-form
(see proofs of Proposition
5.5
and Theorem
5.6
). The strength of our method is that it gives a criteria for the positivity of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
explicitly and intrinsically depending on
X.
This criteria can be checked for a given
X.

We prove the semi-ampleness of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
in Theorem
B
by a hybrid
technique using both the results coming from the differential geometry of the toroidal compactification—namely, the positivity of the twisted cotangent bundle—and the results coming from the algebraic geometry of the toroidal compactification.
(see proof of Proposition
5.2
and Theorem
6.2
for more details).

2.
Background

2.1.
Complex Unit Ball

The complex unit ball

\mathbb{B}^{n}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\ |\ |z|^{2}<1\}

has holomorphic automorphism group
G:=\operatorname{PU}(n,1)
. Let
\Gamma\subset G
be a cofinite-volume discrete group which does not have any elliptic element. Therefore,
X:=\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma
is a complex manifold with cusps, where cusps correspond to conjugacy class of maximal parabolic subgroups of
\Gamma
.

The Baily-Borel compactification
[
BB66
,
SY16
]
of
X
is obtained by adding a finite set of points in corresponding to the equivalences
classes of the rational boundary components of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
under the action of
\Gamma.

This compactification is a normal projective variety and we will refer to the the points added to
X
as cusps of
X
.

The Bergman metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is the
\operatorname{PU}(n,1)-
invariant hermitian metric with holomorphic sectional curvature of
-1
given by

h=4.\frac{(1-|z|^{2}).\sum_{i}dz_{i}\otimes d\bar{z_{i}}+\sum_{i}\bar{z_{i}}dz_{i}\otimes\sum_{i}z_{i}d\bar{z}_{i}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}.

2.2.
Siegel Domain

To understand the behaviour of Bergman metric around cusps, we found it more convenient to pass to the Siegel model
\mathbb{S}

\mathbb{B}^{n}

of
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}

\mathbb{S}:=\{(\zeta,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}\ |\ \operatorname{Im}(z)>|\zeta|^{2}\}.

2.3.
Depth of cusps

For
u_{i}>0,
the horoball centered at the cusp at infinity with height
u_{i}
is the open set

B(u_{i}):=\{(\zeta,z)\in\mathbb{S}\ |\ u>u_{i}\}.

Note that the height coordinate
u
on
\mathbb{S}
is invariant under the action of Heisenberg rotations
\operatorname{U}(n-1)
and Heisenberg translations
\mathfrak{N}
because
\operatorname{U}(n-1)
fixes
|\zeta|
and
z,
and

\operatorname{Im}(z^{\prime})-|\zeta^{\prime}|^{2}=\operatorname{Im}(z)-|\zeta|^{2}.

It follows that the horoball is invariant with respect to the action of Heisenberg rotations and Heisenberg translations and the one-dimensional torus
T
scales
B(u)
to
B(au)
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a cusp
c_{i}
, there exists a
g\in G
translating
c_{i}
to
\infty
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}:=(g\cdot\Gamma\cdot g^{-1})_{\infty}
be the parabolic stabilizer of
\infty.
The smallest
u_{i}
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}\backslash B(u_{i})
injects into
X
will be called the height of the
c_{i}
. We will refer to
H_{i}(u)=\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}\backslash B(u)
for
u>u_{i}
as a horoball neighborhood of
c_{i}
.

Thanks to the Shimuzu’s lemma,
H_{i}(u_{i})
must injects into
X
for a large enough
u_{i}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
u_{i}
is the height of cups
c_{i},
and
t_{i}
is the shortest vertical translation in the parabolic stabilizer of
c_{i}
. The number
d_{i}=t_{i}/u_{i}
is called the depth of
c_{i}
which is invariant under conjugating the lattice
\Gamma.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
BT18b
, Definition 3.7.]
) The uniform depth of cusps of
X
is the largest
d
satisfying the following properties, setting
v_{i}=t_{i}/d
:

(1)

for every
i
,
v_{i}\leq u_{i}
(this gives that
H_{i}(v_{i})
injects into
X
).

(2)

all
H_{i}(v_{i})
are disjoint.

Thanks to Parker’s generalization of Shimizu’s lemma
[
Par98
, Proposition 2.4.]
, the uniform depth of cups is at least
2
for torsion free.

Remark 2.4
.

By passing to a cover of
X
, i.e., passing to a subgroup of
\Gamma
, the shortest vertical translations of parabolic subgroups can not decrease and the heights can not increase. Therefore, the canonical depth can not decrease by passing to a cover of
X
.

Definition 2.5
.

(see
[
Yeu94
, Section 2]
) A cofinal normal tower of
X
is
a sequence
\displaystyle\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of étale Galois coverings of
X=X_{1}
given by a sequence of lattices
\displaystyle\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that for each subsequence
\displaystyle\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},
one has

\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{j}=\{1\}.

Similar to
[
Yeu94
, Lemma 1.2.2.]
and
[
HT06
, Proposition 2.1.]
, we can prove that the uniform depth of cusps tends to infinity in cofinal normal towers:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a cofinal normal tower of
X
and

be the uniform depth of cusps of
X_{i}.
Then,

\lim_{i\to\infty}d(X_{i})=\infty.

Proof.

Let

of

is a normal cover of

the group

that get sent to

and therefore the uniform depth can be computed by taking only one cusp over each cusp of

and

be the shortest vertical translation in the stabilizer of

On the other hand, for any subsequence

we have

Putting these together, we conclude that the length of the shortest element except

tends to infinity.

∎

Remark 2.7

In general, the uniform depth of cusps tends to infinity in most of the towers of covering, even in the case that ramification index of some cusps are fixed in the tower. The group

, and

Proposition 3.4

4.
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundles and Base loci

The goal of this section is to prove formal properties of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundles which will be used in §
5
and §
6
to study the twists of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
and
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big).
To this end, we use the definitions and properties of the base loci of a vector bundle which has been systematically studied in
[
BKK+15
]
. We also use the definitions and properties of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundles from
[
Laz04
, section 6]
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle over a projective variety
\overline{Y},
and
D
be an integral divisor on
\overline{Y}.
The base locus of
E
is defined to be the subset

\textbf{Bs}(E)=\{y\in\overline{Y}|H^{0}(\overline{Y},E)\rightarrow E(y)\mbox{ is not surjective}\},

and the stable base loci of
E
is defined to be the algebraic subset

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(E)=\bigcap_{m\geq 0}\textbf{Bs}(S^{m}E).

Let
r
be a rational number.

Definition 4.1
.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
E\langle rD\rangle
on
\overline{Y}
is a pair consisting of a vector bundle
E
on
\overline{Y}
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
rD\in\mathrm{Div}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\overline{Y}).
A
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundles is the equivalence relation generated by considering
E\langle D^{\prime}+rD\rangle
to be equivalent to

E\otimes O_{\overline{Y}}(D^{\prime})\langle rD\rangle,

where
D^{\prime}
is an integral divisor.

The formal symbol
E\langle rD\rangle
is intended to say that we are twisting the vector bundle
E
by a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
rD.
The symmetric power
S^{m}\big(E\langle rD\rangle\big)
denotes the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
S^{m}(E)\langle mrD\rangle.
It is easy to observe that every
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle has a symmetric power which is
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic to a vector bundle.
Let
a
and
b
be integers such that
b
is positive. We will define the base loci of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
E\langle\frac{a}{b}D\rangle
to be the following set

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(E\langle\frac{a}{b}D\rangle)=\bigcap_{m>0}\textbf{Bs}\big(S^{mb}E\otimes O_{\overline{Y}}(maD)\big),

which does not depend on the choice of
a,b.
Let
A
be an ample divisor on
\overline{Y}
. The augmented base locus of
E
is the algebraic subset

\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)=\bigcap_{r\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}}\mathbb{B}(E\langle-rA\rangle),

which does not depend on the choice of the ample divisor
A.

Definition 4.2
.

We say that
E\langle rD\rangle
is ample (nef) if one of the following equivalent properties holds:

(i)

The
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)+\pi^{*}(rD)
is ample (nef) on
\mathbb{P}(E).

(ii)

E\langle rD\rangle
has a symmetric power which is
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic to an ample (a nef) vector bundle.

(iii)

If
r=a/b
and
b
is positive, then the vector bundle
S^{b}E\otimes O_{\overline{Y}}(aD)
is ample (nef).

The equivalency of these definitions is checked in
[
Laz17
, Lemma 6.2.8]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
E
is a nef vector bundle and
rD
is an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, then
E\langle rD\rangle
is an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle.

Proof.

Take
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
r=a/b
and
b
is positive. As
rD
is ample, the integral divisor
aD
is ample.
Since
E
is nef,
S^{b}E
is nef. Combining these two we get that
S^{b}E\otimes O_{\overline{Y}}(aD)
is an ample vector bundle. Hence,
E\langle rD\rangle
is ample.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
0\to G\to E\to F\to 0
is an exact sequence of vector bundles on a projective variety
\overline{Y}
. The following hold:

(i)

If
E\langle rD\rangle
is ample (nef), then
F\langle rD\rangle
is ample (nef).

(ii)

If
G\langle rD\rangle
and
F\langle rD\rangle
are ample, then
E\langle rD\rangle
is ample.

Proof.

(i) Since
\mathbb{P}(E)
parametrizes
one-dimensional quotients, the surjection
E\to F
corresponds to an inclusion
\mathbb{P}(E)\subset\mathbb{P}(F)
, such that the restriction of
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
is
O_{\mathbb{P}(F)}(1)
. Hence, the ampleness (nefness) of
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)+\pi^{*}(rD)
implies the ampleness (nefness) of
O_{\mathbb{P}(F)}(1)+\pi^{*}(rD)
.

(ii) See
[
Laz04
, Lemma 6.2.8]
.
∎

Note that the above mentioned definitions for a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
E\langle rD\rangle
agree with the usual definitions for a vector bundle when a vector bundle
E
is considered as the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
E\langle 0\rangle.

Definition 4.5
.

A vector bundle
E
over
\overline{Y}
is said to be ample modulo
D
if for every coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
over
\overline{Y}
, there exists an
m_{0}>0
such that if
m>m_{0}
, then for every
y\in\overline{Y}\setminus{D}
, the fiber
\mathscr{F}\otimes S^{m}(E)_{|y}
is generated by
H^{0}\big(Y,\mathscr{F}\otimes S^{m}(E)\big)
.

There is an obvious relation between the notion of ampleness modulo a divisor and the augmented base loci:

Proposition 4.6
.

If the vector bundle
E
is ample modulo
D
, then the augmented base loci
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)
contained in
D
and
\mathbb{B}_{+}\big(O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)\big)
is contained in
\pi^{*}(D).

Proof.

Pick an arbitrary point
y\in\overline{Y}\setminus D
. To show
\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)\subset D
, it is enough to show that
y\notin\mathbb{B}_{+}(E).
Let
A
be an ample line bundle. By Definition
4.5
, there exist
n
such that
(S^{n}E-A)_{|y}
is generated by
H^{0}(Y,S^{n}E-A)
and therefore
y\notin\mathbb{B}(E\langle-\frac{1}{n}A\rangle).
Consequently,
y\notin\mathbb{B}_{+}(E)
.

[
BKK+15
, Proposition 3.2]
tells us that
\mathbb{B}_{+}\big(O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)\big)\subset\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{B}_{+}(E))
and therefore the previous part gives that
\mathbb{B}_{+}(O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1))\subset\pi^{*}(D).

∎

Suppose
L
is a line bundle on a projective variety
\overline{Y}
. It is well-known that
L
is big if and only if
\mathbb{B}_{+}(L)\neq X
. This follows that if
E
is ample modulo
D
, then
E
is big in the sense that

is big. Another well-know fact is that
L
is ample if and only if
\mathbb{B}_{+}(L)=\emptyset.
For vector bundles, we will use the following lemma to go from the ampleness modulo
D
to the ampleness:

Lemma 4.7
.

If
E
is ample modulo
D
and
E_{|D}
is ample, then
E
is ample.

Proof.

Let
L
be the line bundle
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
on
\mathbb{P}(E).
To conclude ampleness it is sufficient to prove that for any subvariety
V\subset\mathbb{P}(E)
with dimension
m,
one has
c_{1}(L)^{\operatorname{dim}V}\cdot V>0.
We prove it by induction on
m:

First, we show that for every irreducible curve
C
on
\mathbb{P}(E),
we have
L\cdot C>0.
If
C\subset\pi^{*}(D),
then

because
L_{|\pi^{*}(D)}
is ample. If
C
is not contained in
D,
then there is a point
x\in C
such that
x\not\in\pi^{*}(D).
Since
E
is ample modulo
D
, Proposition
4.6
gives that
\mathbb{B}_{+}(L)\subset\pi^{*}(D)
and therefore

It means that there exist

such that

where

is an ample divisor on
\mathbb{P}(E).
Consequently, the zero locus of

is not contained in

and it follows that
C
intersects transversely with

Hence.

which gives that

Similarly, when

there are two cases to consider. If

, then the result comes from the fact that

is ample. If

, then

Therefore, there exists

and a section

such that

. Let

be the
(m-1)-

associated with the schematic locus

or a formal sum of

dimensional subvarieties with positive coefficients. Therefore, one has

(bL)\cdot V\sim_{rat}W+(aA)\cdot V.
As A is ample, the class

is provided by an

(m-1)-
cycle with only positive coefficients. Since

and the second factor on the right hand side is a
(m-1)-

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
0\to G\to E\to F\to 0
is an exact sequence of vector bundles on a projective variety
\overline{Y}
such that
H^{1}(\overline{Y},G)=0.
If
S^{m}G
and
F
are globally generated, then
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m)
is globally generated.

Proof.

H^{1}(\overline{Y},G)=0
gives the exact sequence of global sections:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow H^{0}(\overline{Y},G)\longrightarrow H^{0}(\overline{Y},E)\longrightarrow H^{0}(\overline{Y},F)\longrightarrow 0.

This implies that
H^{0}(\overline{Y},S^{m}G)
injects into
H^{0}(\overline{Y},S^{m}F)
and
H^{0}(\overline{Y},E)
surjects onto
H^{0}(\overline{Y},F).

To prove that
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m)
is globally generated, we need to show that for every
p\in\mathbb{P}(E),
the fiber
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m)_{|p}
is generated by global sections of
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m).

Suppose
p\in\mathbb{P}(E)
is determined by
y\in\overline{Y}
and an one-dimensional quotient
E_{p}\to L_{p}.

Consider the map
\eta_{y}:G_{y}\to L_{y}.
We may have two cases:

(1)

\eta_{y}:G_{y}\to L_{y}
is the zero map. In this case,
\eta_{y}
factors through
F_{y}.
On the other hand,
H^{0}(\overline{Y},E)
surjects onto
H^{0}(\overline{Y},F),
and
F
is globally generated. Since

H^{0}(\overline{Y},E)\cong H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(E),O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)),

the fiber
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)_{|p}
is generated by the global sections and therefore
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m)_{|p}
is generated by global sections.

(2)

\eta_{y}:G_{y}\to L_{y}
is a non-zero map. Since
H^{0}(\overline{Y},S^{m}G)
injects into
H^{0}(\overline{Y},S^{m}E)
and
S^{m}G
is globally generated, the global sections
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(E),O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m))\cong H^{0}(\overline{Y},S^{m}E)
generates the fiber
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m)_{|p}.

Hence,
O_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(m)
is globally generated, as desired.

∎

Another ingredient we need in order to construct a singular hermitian metric is an appropriate weight function. Roughly speaking, this weight function will be a plurisubharmonic function, supported on a horoball with the largest possible Lelong number on the boundary. The desired wight function on the hoorball around the cusp
c_{i}
will be constructed using the following Lemma:

Lemma 5.4
.

For a positive real number
u_{i}
and sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, there exists a

C^{2}
function
\rho_{i}:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}
and a constant number
c
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\rho_{i}(u)=-\log(u)+c
on
(0,u_{i}+\epsilon^{\prime}]
, where
\epsilon^{\prime}
is a positive number depending on
\epsilon
.

(2)

i\partial\bar{\partial}\rho_{i}\geq 0

Let
u=-\frac{t_{i}}{2\pi}\cdot\log|q|-|\zeta|^{2}
, where
t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and
(\zeta,q)\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}
.

(3)

When
(\zeta,q)
varies on a compact set with non-empty intersection with
q=0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{\rho_{i}(u)}{\log|q|}=\frac{1}{2\pi}(d_{i}-\epsilon),

where
d_{i}=\frac{t_{i}}{u_{i}}.

(4)

The forms
\bar{\partial}\big(\rho(u)-\frac{1}{2\pi}(d_{i}-\epsilon)\log|q|\big)
and
\partial\bar{\partial}\big(\rho(u)\big)
are locally integrable on the set
\{(\zeta,q)\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}\ |\ u>u_{0}+\epsilon^{\prime}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
be small enough such that there exists
\epsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\frac{t_{i}}{4\pi})
satisfying

\frac{t_{i}}{u_{i}}-\frac{t_{i}}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}}=\epsilon
and define
\rho_{i}:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function given by

\rho_{i}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\log(u)+c_{0}&\quad 0<u<u_{i}+\epsilon^{\prime},\\
\\
\dfrac{-u}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}}+c_{1}e^{-a(u-u_{i}-\epsilon^{\prime})}&\quad u\geq u_{i}+\epsilon^{\prime},\\
\end{array}\right.

where

a=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{u_{i}+\epsilon^{\prime}},c_{1}=\big(\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}})^{2},

and

c_{0}=-\log(u_{i}+\epsilon^{\prime})-c_{1}-\dfrac{u_{i}+\epsilon^{\prime}}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}}
. Since
a\cdot c_{1}=\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{(u_{i}+\epsilon^{\prime})(u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime})},
and

a^{2}c_{1}=\frac{1}{(u_{i}+\epsilon^{\prime})^{2}}

its straightforward to see that the function
\rho_{i}
is
C^{2}
.

As
-u
is a plurisubharmonic function, and
c_{1}
and
a
are positive,
-\log(u)+c_{0}
,
\frac{-u}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}}+c_{1}e^{-(u-u_{i}-\epsilon^{\prime})}
are plurisubharmonic because the function
-\log(-x)
when
x<0
and the function
e^{bx}
for every
b>0
are monotonically increasing and convex. Hence,
\rho_{i}(u)
satisfies the second properties.

\rho_{i}(u)
satisfies the third condition since

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\dfrac{\rho_{i}(u)}{\log|q|}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{t_{i}}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\frac{t_{i}}{u_{i}}-\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(d_{i}-\epsilon).

To check the forth condition note that

\displaystyle e^{-a(u-u_{i}-\epsilon^{\prime})}=c^{\prime}\cdot e^{-a|\zeta|^{2}}\cdot|q|^{l},

where
l=\dfrac{a\cdot t_{i}}{2\pi}
and
c^{\prime}
is a constant. The inequality
a>\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\prime}}>\frac{4\pi}{t_{i}}
gives that
l>2
, the function
|q|^{l}
, and the forms
\bar{\partial}(|q|^{l}),\partial(|q|^{l})
and
\partial\bar{\partial}(|q|^{l})
are locally integrable and therefore letting
c^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{u_{0}+2\epsilon^{\prime}}
,

\partial\bar{\partial}\big(\rho_{i}(u)\big)=\partial\bar{\partial}(c^{\prime\prime}\cdot|\zeta|^{2})+\partial\bar{\partial}(c^{\prime}e^{-a|\zeta|^{2}}|q|^{l})

is a locally integrable form.
On the other hand,
\frac{-u}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}}=(d_{i}-\epsilon)\log|q|+\frac{|\zeta|^{2}}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}},
in other words,

\bar{\partial}\Big(\frac{-u}{u_{i}+2\epsilon^{\prime}}-(d-\epsilon)\log|q|\Big)=\bar{\partial}(c^{\prime\prime}\cdot|\zeta|^{2}),

which is a locally integrable form. Hence,

\bar{\partial}\big(\rho(u)-(d_{i}-\epsilon)\log|q|\big)
is locally integrable on
\{(\zeta,q)\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}\ |\ u>u_{0}+\epsilon^{\prime}\}
as desired.
∎

.

(Theorem
D
)
Suppose that the uniform depth of cusps
d
is greater than
4\pi
. Then,

\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\langle-rD\rangle

is ample for all rational
r\in(0,-1+d/4\pi).

Proof.

Let
r=a/b
with a positive
b
be a rational number in
(0,-1+d/4\pi)
. Since
d>4\pi,
Theorem
5.6
gives that the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\big\langle(-1-r)D\big\rangle
is ample. In particular,
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\big\langle(-1-r)D\big\rangle
is ample modulo
D.
Note that the exact sequence
20
gives that the vector bundle
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\otimes O_{\overline{X}}(-D)
is a subbundle of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}.
Therefore, the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\big\langle(-1-r)D\big\rangle
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-subsheaf of
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\langle-rD\rangle.
It follows from Lemma
4.10
that
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\langle-rD\rangle
is ample modulo
D,
that is, the vector bundle
S^{b}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\otimes O_{\overline{X}}(-aD)
is ample modulo
D.
On the other hand, the restricted bundle
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}|D}\langle-rD_{|D}\rangle
is ample thanks to Proposition
5.2
. This means that
\big(S^{b}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\otimes O_{\overline{X}}(-aD)\big)_{|D}
is ample. Hence, it follows from Lemma
4.7
that
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\langle-rD\rangle
is ample.

∎

Theorem 6.2
.

(Theorem
B
)
Suppose that the uniform depth of cusps
d
is greater than
4\pi
. Then,
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
is ample modulo
D
and semi-ample.

Proof.

Set
E=\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}
. Since the uniform depth of all cusps is greater then
4\pi
, Theorem
5.6
implies that
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\otimes O_{\overline{X}}(-D)
is ample. It therefore follows from
20
and Lemma
4.10
that
E
is ample modulo
D
.

Let
Y=\mathbb{P}(E),

\pi:\mathbb{P}(E)\to\overline{X}
be the natural projection and
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}=\pi^{*}(D)
.
Given that
E
is ample modulo
D
and
E_{|D}
is semi-ample by Proposition
5.2
,
to show that
E
is semi-ample, we only need to show that there is a large enough
n
such that setting
L=O_{Y}(n)

H^{0}(Y,L)\overset{\phi}{\longrightarrow}H^{0}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},L_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})

is surjective. Choose
r\in(0,-1+d/4\pi).
By Theorem
6.1
,
\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\langle-rD\rangle
is ample, i.e.,
O_{Y}(1)-r\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}
is ample. We can choose
n
large enough so that
H^{1}(Y,L-\tilde{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})=0,
where
\tilde{n}=nr\in\mathbb{Z}.
Therefore, denoting the restriction of
L
to the
\tilde{n}
th order thickening of
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}
by
L_{|\tilde{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}}
, we obtain

H^{0}(Y,L)\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}H^{0}(Y,L_{|\tilde{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})

is surjective. Considering the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},L_{|\tilde{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(Y,L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},L_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

to conclude that
\phi
is surjective, it is enough to show that
\eta
is surjective. To this end, consider the following exact sequence on
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}}
:

0\longrightarrow L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}}\longrightarrow L_{|(m+1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}}\longrightarrow L_{|m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}}\longrightarrow 0,

where
m
is a positive integer. We prove that for every
m>0
,

H^{0}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},L_{|(m+1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\longrightarrow H^{0}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},L_{|m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})

is surjective by showing that
H^{1}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})=0.
This implies that
\eta
is surjective and therefore
\phi
is surjective which finishes the proof.

Let
\varphi:D\longrightarrow Spec(\mathbb{C}).
Applying the exact sequence of low degrees to the composition of the push-forward functor

\pi_{*}:Sh(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})\longrightarrow Sh(D)

and the global section functor
\varphi_{*}
yields

0\longrightarrow H^{1}\big(D,\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\big)\longrightarrow R^{1}(\varphi\circ\pi)_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\longrightarrow H^{0}\big(D,R^{1}\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\big).

Note that
(\varphi\circ\pi)_{*}
is the global section functor
H^{0}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},-)
and therefore

R^{1}(\varphi\circ\pi)_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})=H^{1}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}},L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}}).

Hence, it is sufficient to prove that

H^{1}\big(D,\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\big)=H^{0}\big(D,R^{1}\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\big)=0.

To prove
H^{0}\big(D,R^{1}\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\big)=0,
note that
\pi
is a flat morphism because the fibers of
\pi
are projective spaces of the same dimension. Since for every
x\in D
, the dimension
h^{1}(x,\pi_{*}(L-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{x})=h^{1}\big(\pi^{-1}(x),O_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(n)\big)
is constant, Grauert’s theorem (
[
Har13
, Corollary 12.9]
) gives the isomorphism

R^{1}\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\cong H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},O_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(n))=0.

Hence
H^{0}\big(D,R^{1}\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\big)=0.

To prove
H^{1}\big(D,\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\big)=0,
note that
\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})=S^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}|D}(-mD_{|D})
.
Consider the filtration of
S^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}|D}
obtained by the exact sequence
18
:

S^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}|D}=F^{0}\supseteq F^{1}\supseteq...\supseteq F^{m}\supseteq F^{m+1},

with quotients

F^{i}/F^{i+1}\cong S^{m-i}\Omega_{D}^{1}(-iD_{|D})

for each
i.

Tensoring the filtration by
-mD_{|D},
we get a filtration for
S^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}|D}(-mD_{|D})
whose successive quotients are
S^{m-i}\Omega_{D}^{1}(-jD_{|D})
for some
j>0.
As
D
is an étale quotient of abelian variety, there exists a finite étale map
f:D^{\prime}\to D,
where
D^{\prime}
is an abelian variety. Since
f^{*}\Omega^{1}_{D}\cong\Omega^{1}_{D^{\prime}},
and
\Omega^{1}_{D^{\prime}}
is trivial, we get that
f^{*}S^{m-i}\Omega_{D}^{1}(-jD_{|D})
is a power of
f^{*}(-jD_{|D}).
Since the canonical bundle
K_{D^{\prime}}
is trivial,
f
is finite and
-D_{|D}
is ample, Kodaira vanishing theorem gives that
H^{1}(D^{\prime},f^{*}(-jD_{|D}))=0
for every positive integer
j.

It follows that the successive quotients of the filtration of
S^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}|D}(-mD_{|D})
have vanishing first cohomology. Hence,
H^{1}\big(D,S^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}|D}(-mD_{|D})\big)=0,
i.e.,

H^{1}\big(D,\pi_{*}(L(-m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{4.73611pt}}})_{|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{D}\rule{0.0pt}{3.31529pt}}}})\big)=0.

∎

\overline{X}
forms a finitely generated

Corollary 6.3
.

(Corollary
C
)
With the same assumption as Theorem

, the graded ring

\bigoplus_{n>0}H^{0}(\overline{X},S^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}})

7.
Application to Hyperbolicity of subvarieties

We follow the same notations as previous section and denote a ball quotient
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
with a torsion-free lattice
\Gamma
by
X
, the boundary divisor
\overline{X}\setminus X
by
D
, the toroidal compactification of
X
by
\overline{X}
and the uniform depth of cusps by
d
. In addition, we
assume that
\overline{X}
does not have any orbifold point. Let
V
be an irreducible subvariety of
\overline{X}
intersecting
X.

The purpose of this section is to prove the result related to hyperbolicity of
V.
We first prove Corollary
E
and Corollary
F
. Further, we prove Corollary
H
which tells us that the hyperbolicity increases in towers of normal covering in the sense that the minimum volume of subvarieties of
\overline{X}
intersecting both
X
and
D
tends to infinity. Note that, as the components of
D
are étale quotient of abelian varieties, we can not expect that the hyperbolic volume of a variety entirely contained in
D
tends to infinity.

Corollary 7.1
.

(Corollary
E
) Suppose
V
is smooth with dimension
m>0.
Then,
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle

K_{V}-(r-1)D_{|V}

is ample for all rational
r\in(0,\frac{md}{4\pi}).
Moreover, if
d>4\pi
or
m>6
, then
K_{V}
is ample.

Proof.

Let
a
and
b
be positive integers such that
a/b<d/4\pi
and let
D^{\prime}
be
D_{|V}.
Since the vector bundle

S^{b}\Omega^{1}_{V}(\log(D^{\prime}))\otimes O_{V}(-aD^{\prime})
is a quotient of

\Big(S^{b}\Omega^{1}_{\overline{X}}\big(\log(D)\big)\otimes O_{\overline{X}}(-aD)\Big)_{|V},

Theorem
5.6
gives that
S^{a}\Omega^{1}_{V}(\log(D^{\prime}))\otimes O_{V}(-bD^{\prime})
is ample and therefore its determinant

\frac{b}{m}\binom{m+b-1}{b}\big(K_{V}(D^{\prime})\big)-\binom{m+b-1}{b}aD^{\prime}

is ample. Scaling yields the first part of the claimed result. The rest follows from the fact that the uniform depth of cusps
d
is at least
2.

∎

Corollary 7.2
.

(Corollary
F
)
All subvarieties of
X
are of general type provided that the uniform depth of cusps is greater than
4\pi.

Proof.

Let
V_{0}
be an
m
-dimensional (possibly non-smooth) subvariety of
\overline{X}
not entirely contained in
D
and let
\mu:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V_{0}
be a resolution of singularities. There is a generically
surjective homomorphism
\mu^{*}\Omega^{m}_{\overline{X}}\rightarrow\Omega^{m}_{V^{\prime}}=K_{V^{\prime}}.
Thanks to Theorem
6.2
,
\Omega^{m}_{\overline{X}}
is ample modulo
D
and therefore, the pull back
\mu^{*}\Omega^{m}_{\overline{X}}
is ample modulo
\mu^{*}D
. This implies that
K_{V^{\prime}}
is in particular big, i.e.,
V_{0}
is of general type.
∎

The volume of a line bundle is a birational invariant and it is positive if and only if the line bundle is big. It turns out that if
L
is nef, then
\operatorname{vol}(L)=L^{n}.

In the setting of the compactification of a locally symmetric domain, a natural quantity reflecting the hyperbolicity behaviour is the volume of the log-canonical bundle. In particular,
vol_{V}(K_{V}+D_{|V})>0
if and only if
V
is of log-general type. In the case of ball quotients, Corollary
5.7
in particular implies
\Omega^{1}_{V}(\log D_{|V})
is nef and therefore
K_{V}+D_{|V}
is nef on
V.
Hence,

\operatorname{vol}_{V}(K_{V}+D_{|V})=(K_{V}+D_{|V})^{m}.

We show that the minimum volume of log-canonical bundle of subvarieties of
\overline{X}
is controlled by the uniform depth of cusps:

Corollary 7.3
.

Suppose
V
is a
m
-dimensional smooth subvariety of
\overline{X}
not entirely contained in
D
. If
l
is the number of component of
D
intersecting with
V
, then

\operatorname{vol}_{V}(K_{V}+D_{|V})>m^{m}\Big(\frac{d}{4\pi}\Big)^{m}l(m-1)!.

Proof.

Let
r=\frac{d}{4\pi}
and
D^{\prime}
be
D_{|V}.

Since
D
is a union of étale quotient of abelian varieties with ample conormal bundle
O_{D}(-D)
, we get

\displaystyle l\leq\frac{D^{\prime}\cdot(-D^{\prime})^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}=\dfrac{-(-D^{\prime})^{m}}{(m-1)!}.

(21)

On the other hand, Corollary
7.1
implies that

K_{V}-(mr-1)D^{\prime}

is in particular big and nef. Thus,
(K_{V}-(mr-1)D^{\prime}\big)^{m}>0.

On the other hand,
(K_{V}+D^{\prime})_{|D^{\prime}}
is nef and
-D^{\prime}_{|D^{\prime}}
is ample. Consequently, for every
0<i<m,

(K_{V}+D^{\prime})^{m-i}(-D^{\prime})^{i}=-\big((K_{V}+D^{\prime})_{|D^{\prime}}\big)^{m-i}(-D^{\prime}_{|D^{\prime}})^{i-1}

is non-positive. Hence,
(K_{V}-(mr-1)D^{\prime}\big)^{m}>0
yields that

(K_{V}+D^{\prime})^{m}>-(rm)^{m}(-D^{\prime})^{m}.

Combining this with the inequality
21
gives the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

Parker’s generalization of Shimizu’s lemma
[
Par98
, Proposition 2.4.]
gives that the uniform depth of cusps is at least
2
. Plugging in this result to Corollary
7.3
gives
that if
V
intersects
D
, then
V
is of log general type. This can be concluded from the recent result of Guenancia
[
Gue22
, Theorem B]
as well.

We also get a uniform lower bound for the volume of the canonical bundles of subvarieties:

Corollary 7.5
.

If the canonical depth of cusps d is greater than
4\pi
, then

\operatorname{vol}_{V}(K_{V})\geq\big(\frac{md}{4\pi}-1\big)^{m}l(m-1)!

Proof.

Since
d>4\pi,
by Corollary
7.1
we get that
K_{V}
is ample and therefore
\operatorname{vol}_{V}(K_{V})=K_{V}^{m}.
Let
r=\frac{d}{4\pi}
and
D^{\prime}
be
D_{|V}.
As sated in the proof of Corollary
7.3
,
(K_{V}-(mr-1)D^{\prime})^{m}>0
. Since
K_{V|D^{\prime}}
and
-D^{\prime}_{D^{\prime}}
are ample, for every
0<i<m,

K_{V}^{m-i}\cdot(-D^{\prime})^{i}\cong-(K_{V|D^{\prime}})^{m-i}\cdot(-D^{\prime}_{|D^{\prime}})^{i-1}

is negative. Hence,
(K_{V}-(mr-1)D^{\prime})^{m}>0
together with the inequality
21
yields that

K_{V}^{m}\geq-(rm-1)^{m}(-D^{\prime})^{m}\geq(rm-1)^{m}l(m-1)!

∎

As a generalization of Brunebarbe’s work
[
Bru20a
]
, for towers of ball quotients we show that:

Corollary 7.6
.

(Corollary
H
)
Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a tower of
X=X_{1}.

Suppose that toroidal compactification of
X_{i}
does not have any orbifold points. Then, given a positive number
v
, for all but finitely many
i,
every subvariety
V
of
X_{i}
containing a cusp of
X_{i}
have
vol(K_{V})>v.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
2.6
and Corollary
7.5
gives the result.

∎

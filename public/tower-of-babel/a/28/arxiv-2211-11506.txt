Long time dynamics for the focusing inhomogeneous fractional Schrödinger equation
Mohamed Majdoub and Tarek Saanouni

Abstract
. We consider the following fractional NLS with focusing inhomogeneous power-type nonlinearity i ​ ∂ t u − ( − Δ ) s ​ u + | x | − b ​ | u | p − 1 ​ u = 0 , ( t , x ) ∈ ℝ × ℝ N , i\partial_{t}u-(-\Delta)^{s}u+|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}, where N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , 1 / 2 < s < 1 1/2<s<1 , 0 < b < 2 ​ s 0<b<2s and 1 + 2 ​ ( 2 ​ s − b ) N < p < 1 + 2 ​ ( 2 ​ s − b ) N − 2 ​ s 1+\frac{2(2s-b)}{N}<p<1+\frac{2(2s-b)}{N-2s} . We prove the ground state threshold of global existence and scattering versus finite time blow-up of energy solutions in the inter-critical regime with spherically symmetric initial data. The scattering is proved by the new approach of Dodson-Murphy (Proc. Am. Math. Soc. 145: 4859–4867, 2017). This method is based on Tao’s scattering criteria and Morawetz estimates. One describes the threshold using some non-conserved quantities in the spirit of the recent paper by Dinh (Discr. Cont. Dyn. Syst. 40: 6441–6471, 2020). The radial assumption avoids a loss of regularity in Strichartz estimates. The challenge here is to overcome two main difficulties. The first one is the presence of the non-local fractional Laplacian operator. The second one is the presence of a singular weight in the non-linearity. The greater part of this paper is devoted to prove the scattering of global solutions in H s ​ ( ℝ N ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) .

1.
Introduction

We consider the following fractional NLS with focusing inhomogeneous power nonlinearity

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}i\partial_{t}u-(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&-|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u(0,x)&=&u_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
N\geq 2
,
s\in(0,1)
,
p>1
and
b>0
. The fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}u:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{2s}\mathcal{F}(u)\right)
where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are the Fourier transform and inverse Fourier transform, respectively.

The fractional Schrödinger equation arises for instance as an effective equation in the
continuum limit of discrete models with long-range interactions. In
[
50
]
Kirkpatrick, Lenzmann and Staffilani refer to models of mathematical biology, specifically
for the charge transport in biopolymers like the DNA. Numerous applications of fractional NLS-type equations in the physical sciences
could be mentioned, ranging from the description of Boson stars
[
37
]
to water
wave dynamics. The fractional Laplacian also appears as a natural operator when
considering jump processes
[
61
]
, which makes it valuable for Lévy processes in
probability theory with applications in financial mathematics.

From the mathematical point of view, there is a quite large literature devoted to (
1
) and its variants. We emphasize that the case
s=1,\,b=0
corresponds to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
NLS
). There have been tremendous amount of researches on (
NLS
), and the monographs
[
12
,
36
,
57
,
59
]
cover all known results in great mathematical detail.

The case
s=1,b\neq 0
corresponds to the inhomegeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
INLS
). The Cauchy problem for (
INLS
) with
b>0
has received a lot of interested in the mathematical community; see, among others,

Let us turn now to the fractional homogeneous case, that is,
0<s<1,\,b=0
, which we call for short (
FNLS
). A first analysis of the well-posedness in Sobolev spaces has been done by Hong and Sire
[

. It is worth noticing that the results obtained in
[

require some extra assumptions due to the loss of

derivatives in the dispersion
[

[

[

To the best authors knowledge, there exist few works dealing with the inhomogeneous fractional Schrödinger equation (
1
), with
b\neq 0
and
0<s<1
. Sufficient conditions for global existence in

were derived in
[

[

[

for the bi-harmonic Schrödinger equation.

The main goal of this paper is to fill in a gap in the literature. Indeed, the ground state threshold of global existence and scattering versus finite time blow-up of focusing radial solutions seems not to be treated before. One needs to deal with two combined difficulties. The first one is the presence of the non-local fractional Laplacian operator, which gives a loss of derivatives in the dispersive estimate (
2.16
). The second one is the singular weight
|x|^{-b}
in the non-linearity. The main part of this work is devoted to proving the scattering. We avoid here the Kenig-Merle road-map, based on the concentration-compactness method
[
47
]
. Instead, we use the Dodson-Murphy method
[
31
]
based on Tao’s scattering criteria
[
60
]
and Morawetz estimates. Moreover, we expresse the threshold using some non-conserved quantities in the spirit of the recent paper by Dinh
[
28
]
. This gives as a consequence the classical mass-energy threshold obtained first by Roudenko et al.
[
32
]
.

The non-linear Schrödinger equation (
1
) satisfies the scaling invariance

0<\lambda\longmapsto u_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{\frac{2s-b}{p-1}}u(\lambda^{2s}t,\lambda x).

The only one homogeneous Sobolev norm invariant under the above scaling is relevant in this study. Indeed, the next identity

\|u_{\lambda}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\mu}}=\lambda^{\mu-(\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2s-b}{p-1})}\|u(\lambda^{2s}t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\mu}},

gives rise to the critical Sobolev index
s_{c}:=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2s-b}{p-1}
. The energy-critical regime corresponds to
s_{c}=s
or
p=p^{*}:=1+\frac{2(2s-b)}{N-2s}
and is related to the energy conservation law

E[u(t))]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\mathbf{D}^{s}u(t)|^{2}\,dx-\frac{2}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{1+p}\,dx=E[u_{0}],

(1.3)

where
\mathbf{D}^{\sigma}
stands for the operator with Fourier multiplier

\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbf{D}^{\sigma}\phi\right)(\xi)=|\xi|^{\sigma}\mathcal{F}({\phi})(\xi).

(1.4)

In particular,
(-\Delta)^{s}=\mathbf{D}^{2s}.

The mass-critical regime corresponds to
s_{c}=0
, or
p=p_{*}:=1+\frac{2(2s-b)}{N}
and is related to the mass conservation law

M[u(t)]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx=M[u_{0}].

(1.5)

Unless otherwise specified, we restrict ourselves to the inter-critical regime
0<s_{c}<s
. The last condition can be written in terms of
p
as
p_{*}<p<p^{*}
.

Define the positive real number
\gamma_{c}:=\frac{s-s_{c}}{s_{c}}
and the quantities

\displaystyle P[\phi]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx,

(1.6)

\displaystyle I[\phi]:=\|\mathbf{D}^{s}\,\phi\|^{2}-\frac{B}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|\phi(x)|^{1+p}\,dx,

(1.7)

where
B
is given by (
2.11
) below and
\|\cdot\|
denotes the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm.
Define also the scale invariant quantities

\displaystyle\mathcal{ME}[\phi]

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{M[\phi]}{M[Q]}\Big)^{\gamma_{c}}\Big(\frac{E[\phi]}{E[Q]}\Big),

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{MG}[\phi]

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{\|\phi\|}{\|Q\|}\Big)^{\gamma_{c}}\Big(\frac{\|\nabla\phi\|}{\|\nabla\,Q\|}\Big),

(1.9)

where
Q\in H^{s}
is the unique non-negative radially symmetric decreasing solution of (
2.13
) given by Lemma
2.5
below.

From now on, we hide the variable
t
for simplicity, spreading it out only when necessary. Let
H_{rad}^{s}
denote the space of functions in
H^{s}
which are radially symmetric.
Our main contribution reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
N\geq 2
,
s\in(\frac{N}{2N-1},1)
,
0<b<2s
and
p_{*}<p<p^{*}
. Let
u_{0}\in H_{rad}^{s}
and
{u}\in C([0,T^{*});H_{rad}^{s})
be the corresponding maximal solution of (
1
) given by Proposition
2.8
.

(i)

Suppose that

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{\ast})}P[u(t)][M[u(t)]^{\gamma_{c}}<P[Q][M[Q]]^{\gamma_{c}}.

(1.10)

Then,
u
is global. Moreover, if
N\geq 3
,
s>\frac{N}{1+N}
,
p>2(1-\frac{b}{N})

(
p<\frac{N-2b}{N-2s}
if
N=3
)
, then
u
scatters in
H^{s}
.

(ii)

Suppose that

\sup_{0\leq t<T^{*}}\,I[u(t)]<0.

(1.11)

Then,
u
blows-up in finite or infinite time in the sense that

or

and there is

such that

as

goes to infinity.

Useful tools & Auxiliary results

2.1.
Useful tools

For future convenience, we recall some known and useful tools which will play an important role in the proof of our main results.

The fractional radial Sobolev inequality reads
[
18
, Proposition 1]

\sup_{x\neq 0}|x|^{\frac{N}{2}-\alpha}|u(x)|\leq C(N,\alpha)\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}u\|,\quad\forall u\in\dot{H}^{\alpha}_{rad},

(2.1)

provided that
N\geq 2
and
1/2<\alpha<N/2
.

We also recall the homogeneous Sobolev embedding
[
3
, Theorem 1.38]

\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(N,\alpha)\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}u\|,\quad\forall\,u\in\dot{H}^{\alpha},

(2.2)

provided that
0\leq\alpha<N/2
.

The classical Sobolev embedding
[
2
]
states that

H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad 2\leq\,q\leq\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha},

(2.3)

provided that
0\leq\alpha<N/2
.

We know from
[
3
, Theorem 1.62, p. 41]
that multiplication by a function of Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a continuous map
from
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
into itself for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Precisely we have

\|\varphi\,u\|_{H^{s}}\leq 2^{\frac{|s|}{2}}\left\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{|s|}{2}}\widehat{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{1}}\,\|u\|_{H^{s}},

where
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

The next fractional chain rule
[
20
]
will be useful.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{i}}+\frac{1}{q_{i}}
,
i=1,2
and
F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C})
. Then,

\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}F(u)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|F^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{p_{1}}},

(2.4)

and

\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}(uv)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|v\|_{L^{q_{1}}}+\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|u\|_{L^{q_{2}}}.

(2.5)

The next result gives a Leibniz rule for fractional derivatives
[
48
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
s_{1}+s_{2}:=s\in(0,1)
, such that
s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,1)
and
1<p,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}.

Then,

\|\mathbf{D}^{s}(uv)-u\mathbf{D}^{s_{1}}v-v\mathbf{D}^{s_{2}}u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{D}^{s_{1}}v\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\mathbf{D}^{s_{2}}u\|_{L^{p_{2}}}.

(2.6)

Moreover, for
s_{1}=0
, the value
p_{1}=\infty
is allowed.

A useful Sobolev’s embedding reads as follows (see
[
7
, Theorem 6.1.6]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p_{1}\leq p_{2}<\infty
such that

s_{1}-\frac{N}{p_{1}}=s_{2}-\frac{N}{p_{2}}.

Then

W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{s_{2},p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The following continuity argument (or bootstrap argument) will also be useful for our purpose. See
[
29
, Lemma 2.11]
for a similar statement.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
58
, Lemma 3.7, p. 437]

Let
\mathbf{I}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a time interval, and
\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{I}\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous function satisfying, for every
t\in\mathbf{I}
,

\mathbf{X}(t)\leq a+b[\mathbf{X}(t)]^{\theta},

(2.7)

where
a,b>0
and
\theta>1
are constants. Assume that, for some
t_{0}\in\mathbf{I}
,

\mathbf{X}(t_{0})\leq a,\quad a\,b^{\frac{1}{\theta-1}}<(\theta-1)\,\theta^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}.

(2.8)

Then, for every
t\in\mathbf{I}
, we have

\mathbf{X}(t)<\frac{\theta\,a}{\theta-1}.

(2.9)

Proof.

We give here a simpler proof than done in
[
58
]
.
We argue by contradiction. Suppose that
\mathbf{X}(t_{1})\geq\frac{\theta\,a}{\theta-1}
for some
t_{1}\in\mathbf{I}
. Then
\mathbf{X}(t_{0})\leq a<\frac{\theta\,a}{\theta-1}\leq\mathbf{X}(t_{1}).
By continuity, there exists
t_{2}\in\mathbf{I}
such that
\mathbf{X}(t_{2})=\frac{\theta\,a}{\theta-1}
. This contradicts the second assumption in (
2.8
) since
\frac{\theta\,a}{\theta-1}\leq a+b\bigg(\frac{\theta\,a}{\theta-1}\bigg)^{\theta}
imply that
a\,b^{\frac{1}{\theta-1}}\geq(\theta-1)\,\theta^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}.
This leads to (
2.9
) as desired.
∎

The next Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality
[
53
, Theorem 2.2]
is crucial in our proofs.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
s\in(0,1)
,
0<b<2s
and
1<p<1+\frac{4s-2b}{N-2s}
. Then the following sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx\leq\,K_{opt}\,\|u\|^{A}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}\,u\|^{B},

(2.10)

where

B:=\frac{1}{2s}\bigg(N(p-1)+2b\bigg)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad A:=p+1-B.

(2.11)

Moreover, the sharp constant is given by

K_{opt}=\frac{p+1}{A}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{B/2}\,\|Q\|^{1-p},

(2.12)

where
Q\in H^{s}
is the unique non-negative radially symmetric decreasing solution of

-(-\Delta)^{s}Q-Q+|x|^{-b}|Q|^{p-1}Q=0.

(2.13)

Furthermore,

\|\mathbf{D}^{s}\,Q\|=\sqrt{\frac{B}{A}}\,\|Q\|\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{-b}\,|Q(x)|^{p+1}\,dx=\frac{p+1}{A}\,\|Q\|^{2}.

(2.14)

Now, let us collect some standard estimates related to the Schrödinger equation. The free operator associated to the fractional Schrödinger equation is given by

e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}\phi:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{-it|\xi|^{2s}})*\phi.

(2.15)

It is classical that (
1
) has the following integral formulation

u(t)=e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-\tau)\mathbf{D}^{2s}}[|x|^{-b}|u(\tau)|^{p-1}u(\tau)]\,d\tau.

The following dispersive estimate can be found in
[
19
]
for instance.

\|e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}\,\phi\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{C}{|t|^{N(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}\|\mathbf{D}^{2N(1-s)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}\,\phi\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\forall\;r\geq 2,\;\;\forall\;t\neq 0.

(2.16)

Definition 2.6
.

1)

A pair
(q,r)
is said admissible if
q,r\geq 2
and

\frac{4N+2}{2N-1}\leq q\leq\infty,\quad\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2N-1}{r}\leq N-\frac{1}{2},

or

2\leq q\leq\frac{4N+2}{2N-1},\quad\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2N-1}{r}<N-\frac{1}{2}.

2)

(q,r)\in\Gamma_{\gamma}
if it is an admissible pair such that
(N,q,r)\neq(2,2,\infty)
and

N(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})=\frac{2s}{q}+\gamma.

Moreover,
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{0}
.

3)

let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval, one denotes the Strichartz spaces

S^{\gamma}(I):=\displaystyle\bigcap_{(q,r)\in\Gamma_{\gamma}}L^{q}(I,L^{r}),\quad S(I):=S^{0}(I).

Let us now state some Strichartz estimates
[

,
17
]
.

.

Let
N\geq 2
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and

. Then,

1)

\|e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}\|_{S^{\gamma}(I)}\lesssim\|\mathbf{D}^{\gamma}u_{0}\|;

2)

\|u-e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}\|_{S^{\gamma}(I)}\lesssim\displaystyle\inf_{(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})\in\Gamma_{-\gamma}(I)}\|i\partial_{t}u-\mathbf{D}^{2s}u\|_{L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(I,L^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}})};

\frac{N}{2N-1}<s\leq 1

1<p<p^{*}
and
u_{0}\in H_{rad}^{s}
. Then, there exist
0<T^{*}\leq\infty
and a unique maximal solution
{u}\in C([0,T^{*}),H_{rad}^{s})
to (

).
Moreover, the solution
u
satisfies the conservation of mass and energy (
1.5
) and (
1.3
).

In trying to apply the arguments in
[
8
]
and obtain a localized Morawetz estimate, one encounters serious difficulties due the nonlocal operator
(-\Delta)^{s}=\mathbf{D}^{2s}
. To handle this difficulty, we use the following representation known as Balakrishnan’s formula

\mathbf{D}^{2s}=(-\Delta)^{s}=\frac{\sin\pi\,s}{s}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\,m^{s-1}\,\bigg(\frac{-\Delta}{-\Delta+m}\bigg)\,dm.

(2.17)

We define the auxiliary function
u_{m}
, for
m>0
, by

u_{m}:=c_{s}\,R_{m}\,u:=c_{s}(m-\Delta)^{-1}\,u:=c_{s}\,{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{\widehat{u}(\xi)}{m+|\xi|^{2}}\bigg),

(2.18)

where

c_{s}=\sqrt{\frac{\sin\pi\,s}{\pi}}.

The following formula can be easily derived by using Plancherel’s and Fubini’s theorem as in
[
8
, (2.12)]

s\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm.

(2.19)

Here and hereafter, we denote by
B(R):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N};\;|x|\leq R\}
the ball of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at the origin and with radius
R>0
. Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a radial bump function such that

\psi=1\quad\mbox{on}\quad B\left(\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad\psi=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad|x|\geq 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0\leq\psi\leq 1.

(2.20)

For
R>0
, define

\psi_{R}(x)=\psi\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right).

(2.21)

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a real-valued function and
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\nabla(\varphi\,v)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,\varphi^{2}|\nabla\,v|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,\varphi\,\Delta\varphi\,|v|^{2}\,dx,

(2.22)

\displaystyle|\nabla(\varphi\,v)_{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})|^{2}-\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})\cdot\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta,\varphi]\bar{v}_{m})

(2.23)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta,\varphi]v_{m})\cdot\nabla(\varphi\bar{v})_{m}

and

\displaystyle s\|\mathbf{D}^{s}(\varphi\,v)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m^{s}|\nabla(\varphi\,v)_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m^{s}|\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})|^{2}\,dx\,dm}

(2.24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{s}\,\varphi^{2}|\nabla\,v_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm-\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{s}\,\varphi\,\Delta\varphi\,|v_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm.

Proof.

The proof of (
2.22
) is straightforward and uses integration by parts. In particular, we obtain the useful identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m^{s}|\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})|^{2}\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m^{s}\varphi^{2}|\nabla v_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

(2.25)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m^{s}\varphi\Delta\varphi|v_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm.

To prove (
2.23
), let us write using computation done in the proof of
[
56
, Lemma 4.8]
,

\displaystyle\nabla(\varphi\,v)_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})-\nabla[\varphi,R_{m}]v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})-\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta+m,\varphi]R_{m}\,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})-\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta,\varphi]v_{m}).

Hence

\displaystyle|\nabla(\varphi\,v)_{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla(\varphi\bar{v})_{m}\Big(\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})-\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta,\varphi]v_{m})\Big)

(2.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})\Big(\nabla(\varphi\bar{v}_{m})-\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta,\varphi]\bar{v}_{m})\Big)-\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta,\varphi]v_{m})\nabla(\varphi\bar{v})_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})|^{2}-\nabla(\varphi\,v_{m})\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta,\varphi]\bar{v}_{m})-\nabla(R_{m}[-\Delta,\varphi]v_{m})\nabla(\varphi\bar{v})_{m}.

Finally, the proof of (
2.24
) follows easily from (
2.23
) and (
2.25
).
∎

Now, let us give an improvement of
[
56
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then

\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\bigg|\leq C\,R^{-2\theta},

(2.27)

where
u_{m}
given by (
2.18
) and
C=C({N},s,\theta,\psi,\|u\|)
is a positive constant depending only on
N,s,\theta,\psi
and
\|u\|
.

Proof.

Note that, for any
0\leq\alpha<2
, we have

\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}u_{m}\|\lesssim\,m^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\,\|u\|.

(2.28)

Indeed, by (
2.18
), we get

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}^{\alpha}u_{m}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{s}\bigg\|\frac{|\xi|^{\alpha}}{m+|\xi|^{2}}\,\widehat{u}(\xi)\bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{C_{N,s}}\bigg\|\frac{t^{\alpha}}{m+t^{2}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\,\|u\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{C_{N,s}}m^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\,\|u\|.

Next, for
M>0
we write

\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\bigg|{\leq}(I)+(II),

where

\displaystyle(I)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{M}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\bigg|,

\displaystyle(II)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{M}^{\infty}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\bigg|.

As in
[
56
, Lemma 4.2]
, we have

(II)\lesssim R^{-2}\,M^{\theta-1}.

To handle the first term
(I)
let us choose
\alpha=1-\frac{\theta}{2}
and write by Hölder’s inequality, (
2.2
) and (
2.28
),

\displaystyle(I)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{M}\,m^{\theta}\|\psi_{R}\|_{\frac{N}{2\alpha}}\|\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{\infty}\|u_{m}\|^{2}_{\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha}}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{2\alpha-2}\int_{0}^{M}\,m^{\theta}\,m^{\alpha-2}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-\theta}\int_{0}^{M}\,m^{\frac{\theta}{2}-1}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-\theta}\,M^{\frac{\theta}{2}}.

We conclude the proof by choosing
M=R^{-2}
.
∎

We also need the following estimates.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\theta\in(0,s)
. Then,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,|\Delta\psi_{R}|^{2}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}},

(2.29)

\|\psi_{R}\,u\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta}}\leq\,C(s,\psi)\,\|u\|_{H^{s}},\quad R\geq 1,

(2.30)

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,|\nabla\psi_{R}|^{2}|\nabla\,u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}.

(2.31)

Proof.

First, let us prove (
2.29
). For
M>0
we write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,|\Delta\psi_{R}|^{2}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,|\Delta\psi_{R}|^{2}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{M}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,|\Delta\psi_{R}|^{2}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm.

The second term in the RHS can be estimated as follows

\displaystyle\int_{M}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,|\Delta\psi_{R}|^{2}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}^{\infty}\,m^{\theta}\,\|\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u_{m}\|^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-4}\,\int_{M}^{\infty}\,m^{\theta-2}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-4}\,M^{\theta-1}.

To estimate the first term in the RHS, we use Hölder’s inequality together with (
2.2
) and (
2.28
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,|\Delta\psi_{R}|^{2}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{M}\,m^{\theta}\,\|\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{\frac{N}{2\alpha}}\|\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{\infty}\,\|u_{m}\|_{\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha}}^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{2\alpha-4}\,\int_{0}^{M}\,m^{\theta}\,m^{\alpha-2}\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-\theta-2}\,M^{\theta/2},

where
\alpha=1-\theta/2
. Choosing
M=R^{-2}
yields (
2.29
).

Next, we turn to (
2.30
). Since
\theta\in(0,s)
then
\|\psi_{R}u\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta}}\leq\|\psi_{R}u\|_{H^{s}}
. Using (
2.1
), we conclude the proof of (
2.30
).

Finally, let us prove (
2.31
). By (
2.19
) and the fact that
\theta\in(0,s)
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{\theta}\,|\nabla\psi_{R}|^{2}|\nabla\,u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}\,\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}\,\|u\|_{{H}^{s}}^{2}.

∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
u\in H^{s}
with
s\in(1/2,1)
. Then

\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{s}\,\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\bigg|\lesssim\frac{1}{R},

(2.32)

\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{s}\,\nabla\left(R_{m}[-\Delta,\psi_{R}]u_{m}]\right)\cdot\nabla(\psi_{R}\bar{u})_{m}\,dx\,dm\bigg|\lesssim\frac{1}{R},

(2.33)

and

\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{s}\,\nabla(\psi_{R}\,u_{m})\cdot\nabla\left(R_{m}[-\Delta,\psi_{R}]\bar{u}_{m}]\right)\,dx\,dm\bigg|\lesssim\frac{1}{R}.

(2.34)

Proof.

The estimate (
2.32
) follows easily from (
2.27
) and
2s>1
.

The proof of (
2.33
) uses similar arguments as in
[
56
, Lemma 4.3]
together with (
2.29
)-(
2.30
)-(
2.31
).

To prove (
2.34
) let us first define

I(R):=\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{s}\,\nabla(\psi_{R}\,u_{m})\cdot\nabla\left(R_{m}[-\Delta,\psi_{R}]\bar{u}_{m}\right)\,dx\,dm\bigg|.

Note that

[-\Delta,\psi_{R}]\bar{u}_{m}=-(\Delta\psi_{R})\bar{u}_{m}-2\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla\bar{u}_{m},

and

\|\nabla(R_{m}w)\|\lesssim\,m^{-1/2}\,\|w\|.

Taking
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2s},1)
and noticing that

,

, one writes thanks to Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality

\displaystyle I(R)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|m^{s\beta-\frac{1}{2}}(\Delta(\psi_{R})u_{m}+2\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla u_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\,\|m^{s(1-\beta)}\nabla(\psi_{R}u_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\,\|m^{s(1-\beta)}\nabla(\psi_{R}u_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))},

where we have used (
2.29
) and (
2.30
).
Now, applying Lemma
2.10
, one has

\displaystyle\|m^{s(1-\beta)}\nabla(\psi_{R}u_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{2s(1-\beta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla(\psi_{R}u_{m})|^{2}\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{2s(1-\beta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\psi_{R}^{2}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}-\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u_{m}|^{2})\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s(1-\beta)}}+\frac{1}{R^{4s(1-\beta)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+1.

This gives (
2.34
) as desired.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain

. Then, as

\displaystyle s\|\mathbf{D}^{s}(\psi_{R}\,u)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{s}\,\psi^{2}_{R}\,|\nabla\,u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm+\text{O}\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)

(2.35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2}+\text{O}\left(\frac{1}{R}\right).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}m^{s}\,\psi_{R}^{2}\,|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,m^{s}\,\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

∎

3.
Variational analysis

Recall that
Q
stands for the unique nonnegative radially symmetric decreasing solution to (
2.13
). The following inequality will be useful in obtaining a coercivity result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{s}
. Then

P[u]\leq\frac{p+1}{B}\bigg(\frac{M[u]^{\gamma_{c}}P[u]}{M[Q]^{\gamma_{c}}P[Q]}\bigg)^{\frac{B-2}{B}}\;\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2},

(3.1)

where
B
is given by (
2.11
).

Proof.

Thanks to Pohozaev identities (
2.14
) (see also
[
53
, Theorem 2.2]
), one has

P[Q]=\frac{p+1}{A}\,M[Q]=\frac{p+1}{B}\,\|\mathbf{D}^{s}Q\|^{2}.

Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.10
), the expression of
K_{opt}
given by (
2.12
) and the identities
(p-1)s_{c}=s(B-2)
and
\gamma_{c}(B-2)=A
, one writes

\displaystyle[P[u]]^{\frac{B}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{opt}\left(\|u\|^{2\gamma_{c}}P[u]\right)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\,\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{p+1}{A}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{B}{2}}\|Q\|^{-(p-1)}\left(M[u]^{\gamma_{c}}P[u]\right)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1+p}{A}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{B}{2}}M[Q]^{\frac{A-(p-1)}{2}}[P[Q]]^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\bigg(\frac{M[u]^{\gamma_{c}}P[u]}{M[Q]^{\gamma_{c}}P[Q]}\bigg)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{A}{B}\frac{P[Q]}{M[Q]}\bigg)^{\frac{B}{2}}\,\bigg(\frac{M[u]^{\gamma_{c}}P[u]}{M[Q]^{\gamma_{c}}P[Q]}\bigg)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\,\|\mathbf{D}^{2s}u\|^{B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{M[u]^{\gamma_{c}}P[u]}{M[Q]^{\gamma_{c}}P[Q]}\bigg)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\,\bigg(\frac{1+p}{B}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{B}{2}}.

This leads to (
3.1
) as desired.
∎

As a consequence of the above lemma, we obtain the following coercivity result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
u\in H^{s}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
satisfying

P[u][M[u]]^{\gamma_{c}}\leq(1-\varepsilon)P[Q][M[Q]]^{\gamma_{c}}.

(3.2)

Then,

P[u]\leq\frac{p+1}{B}\;(1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{B-2}{B}}\;\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2}\leq\frac{p+1}{B}\;\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2},

(3.3)

and

\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2}-\frac{B}{p+1}P[u]\geq c(\varepsilon,B)\;\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2},

(3.4)

where
c(\varepsilon,B):=1-(1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{B-2}{B}}>0
.
Moreover, for
\varepsilon
small enough, we have

E[u]\geq\frac{B-2}{B}\;\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2}.

(3.5)

Proof.

Inequality (
3.3
) follows immediately from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).
To prove (
3.4
) we use the first inequality in (
3.3
) and the fact that
B>2
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Finally, using the first inequality in (
3.3
), we infer

\displaystyle E[u]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2}-\frac{2}{1+p}P[u]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigg(1-\frac{2}{B}\Big(1-\varepsilon\Big)^{\frac{B-2}{B}}\bigg)\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|^{2}.

Since
1-\frac{2}{B}\Big(1-\varepsilon\Big)^{\frac{B-2}{B}}\to 1-\frac{2}{B}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
B>2
, we get (
3.5
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Since

P[\psi_{R}\,u]\leq P[u]\quad\mbox{and}\quad M[\psi_{R}\,u]\leq M[u],\;\;\;\forall\;\;\;R>0,

inequalities (
3.3
)-(
3.4
) remain true for
\psi_{R}\,u
instead of
u
. Namely, we have

P[\psi_{R}\,u]\leq\frac{p+1}{B}\;\|\mathbf{D}^{s}(\psi_{R}\,u)\|^{2},

(3.6)

and

\|\mathbf{D}^{s}(\psi_{R}\,u)\|^{2}-\frac{B}{p+1}P[\psi_{R}\,u]\geq c(\varepsilon,B)\;\|\mathbf{D}^{s}(\psi_{R}\,u)\|^{2}.

(3.7)

Remark 3.4
.

The solution is global by (
3.5
).

4.
Morawetz estimates

In this section, we assume that
N\geq 2
,
s\in(\frac{N}{2N-1},1)
,
b\in(0,2s)
,
p\in(p_{*},p^{*})
and
u\in C([0,\infty);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
is a global solution of (
1
).

Consider a smooth real-valued function
f
such that

0\leq f^{\prime\prime}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad f(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{r^{2}}{2},\quad\mbox{if}\quad 0\leq r\leq 1;\\
1,\quad\mbox{if}\quad r\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

As a consequence, we see that
f^{\prime}(r)\leq r
.
Define, for
R>0
, the smooth radial function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

f_{R}(x):=R^{2}f\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right).

(4.2)

We easily deduce from (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) the following properties

0\leq f_{R}^{\prime\prime}\leq 1,\quad f_{R}^{\prime}(r)\leq r,\quad N-\Delta f_{R}\geq 0.

(4.3)

Denote the localized virial of
u(t,x)
by

M_{R}[u(t)]:=2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{u}(t)\nabla{f_{R}}\cdot\nabla u(t)\,dx:=2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{u}\partial_{k}{f_{R}}\partial_{k}u\,dx.

(4.4)

The next lemma gives the evolution of
M_{R}[u(t)]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{R}[u(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{|x|<R}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm+4\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{R<|x|<2R}{f^{\prime\prime}}\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right)|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

(4.5)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta^{2}{f_{R}}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm-\frac{4sB}{1+p}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)}{1+p}\int_{|x|>R}(N-\Delta{f}_{R})|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx-\frac{4b}{1+p}\int_{|x|>R}\frac{x\cdot\nabla{f}_{R}}{|x|^{2}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx,

where
u_{m}
is given by (
2.18
).

Proof.

Let us denote the differential operator
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{R}
by

\mathbf{\Gamma}_{R}\,\phi=-i\bigg(\textnormal{div}(\phi\nabla{f_{R}})+\nabla{f_{R}}\cdot\nabla\phi\bigg),

which satisfies

<u(t),\mathbf{\Gamma}_{R}\,u(t)>=M_{R}[u(t)].

Using (
1
), the time derivative of
M_{R}[u(t)]
reads

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{R}[u(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle<u(t),[\mathbf{D}^{2s},i\mathbf{\Gamma}_{R}]u(t)>+<u(t),[-|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1},i\mathbf{\Gamma}_{R}]u(t)>

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(t)+\mathbf{B}(t),

where the commutator of
X
and
Y
is
[X,Y]:=XY-YX
. Thanks to the computations done in
[
8
]
, one writes

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(4\partial_{k}\bar{u}_{m}\partial^{2}_{kl}{f}_{R}\partial_{l}u_{m}-\Delta^{2}{f}_{R}|u_{m}|^{2}\,\Big)dx\,dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{|x|<R}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm+4\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{R<|x|<2R}{f^{\prime\prime}}\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right)|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta^{2}{f_{R}}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm.

Let us denote the source term
\mathcal{N}:=|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u
and compute

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-<u,\frac{\mathcal{N}}{u}\nabla{f}_{R}\cdot\nabla u>-<u,\frac{\mathcal{N}}{u}\textnormal{div}(u\nabla{f}_{R})>

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle<u,\nabla{f}_{R}\nabla\mathcal{N}>+<u,\textnormal{div}[\mathcal{N}\nabla{f}_{R}]>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2<u,\frac{\mathcal{N}}{u}\nabla{f}_{R}\nabla u>+2<u,\nabla{f}_{R}\nabla\mathcal{N}>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\nabla{f}_{R}\nabla[\frac{\mathcal{N}}{u}]\,dx.

Integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\nabla{f}_{R}\nabla[|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}]\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[\nabla(|u|^{2})\nabla{f}_{R}+|u|^{2}\Delta{f}_{R}]|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla(|u|^{2})\nabla{f}_{R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}\,dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta{f}_{R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta{f}_{R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx-\frac{4}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}\nabla{f}_{R}\nabla(|u|^{1+p})\,dx.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2(p-1)}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta{f}_{R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx-\frac{4b}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{x\cdot\nabla{f}_{R}}{|x|^{2}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2N(p-1)}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+\frac{2(p-1)}{1+p}\int_{|x|>R}(N-\Delta{f}_{R})|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4b}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{x\cdot\nabla{f}_{R}}{|x|^{2}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4sB}{1+p}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+\frac{2(p-1)}{1+p}\int_{|x|>R}(N-\Delta{f}_{R})|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4b}{1+p}\int_{|x|>R}\frac{x\cdot\nabla{f}_{R}}{|x|^{2}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx.

Thus, with the above calculus we obtain (
4.5
) as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

We have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{1+p}\,dx\,dt\lesssim\,R+TR^{-b},\quad\mbox{for any}\quad T,R>0.

(4.6)

Proof.

By
[
8
, Lemma A.2]
, one has

\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta^{2}{f_{R}}|u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm\bigg|\lesssim R^{-2s}.

Thus, using the properties of
f_{R}
and the fact that
b<2s
, one writes

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{R}[u(t)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{|x|<R}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm-\frac{4sB}{1+p}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx+O\Big(R^{-b}\Big).

Taking into account Corollary
3.2
and Lemma
2.13
, with the fact that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and
\psi=0
on
B(\frac{1}{2})
, one gets

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{R}[u(t)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{|x|<R}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm-\frac{4sB}{1+p}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx+O\Big(R^{-b}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}^{2}|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm+4\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{R/2<|x|<R}(1-\psi_{R}^{2})|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}\,dx\,dm

\displaystyle-\frac{4sB}{1+p}P[\psi_{R}u]-\frac{4sB}{1+p}\int_{R/2<|x|<R}|x|^{-b}(1-\psi_{R}^{p+1})|u|^{1+p}\,dx+O\Big(R^{-b}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4s\|\mathbf{D}^{s}(\psi_{R}u)\|^{2}-\frac{4sB}{1+p}P[\psi_{R}u]+O\Big(R^{-b}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle cP[\psi_{R}u]+O\Big(R^{-b}\Big).

Hence,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|M_{R}[u(t)]|

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}P[\psi_{R}u]\,ds+O\Big(R^{-b}\Big)T

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|x|<\frac{R}{2}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx\,ds+O\Big(R^{-b}\Big)T.

Thus, with previous computation via
[
8
, Lemma A.1]
and the assumption
s>\frac{1}{2}
, one gets

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|x|<\frac{R}{2}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\sup_{[0,T]}|M_{R}[u(t)]|+TR^{-b}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(R+TR^{-b}\Big).

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

For any sequence
R_{n}\to\infty
, there exists a sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\int_{|x|<R_{n}}|x|^{-b}|u(t_{n},x)|^{1+p}\,dx=0.

(4.7)

Proof.

Let
(R_{n})
be a sequence of positive numbers tending to infinity. By taking
T_{n}=R_{n}^{1+b}
, it follows from (
4.6
)
that

\frac{2}{T_{n}}\int_{T_{n}/2}^{T_{n}}\int_{|x|<{R_{n}}}\,|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{1+p}\,dx\,dt\lesssim R_{n}^{-b}\to 0\;\;\text{as}\;\;n\to\infty.

The proof follows using the integral mean value theorem.
∎

5.
Scattering criterion

Here and hereafter, one denotes the real numbers

\displaystyle a:=\frac{s(1+p-\theta)}{s-s_{c}},\quad d:=\frac{s(1+p-\theta)}{s+(p-\theta)s_{c}};

\displaystyle{q:=\frac{2s(1+p-\theta)}{2(s-s_{c})+s_{c}(1+p-\theta)}};

\displaystyle r:=\frac{2N(1+p-\theta)}{(N-2s_{c})(1+p-\theta)-4(s-s_{c})}.

Clearly, one can choose
\theta>0
small enough so that

(q,r)\in\Gamma,\quad(a,r)\in\Gamma_{s_{c}},\quad(d,r)\in\Gamma_{-s_{c}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(p-\theta)d^{\prime}=a.

This section is devoted to the proof of the following scattering criterion.

Proposition 5.1
.

Take the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
. Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{s}_{rad})
be a global solution to (
1
). Assume that

0<\sup_{t\geq 0}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}:=E<\infty.

(5.1)

Then, there exist
R,\varepsilon>0
depending on
E,N,p,b,s
such that if

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\int_{|x|<R}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx<\varepsilon^{2},

(5.2)

then,
u
scatters for positive time.

Before proving Proposition
5.1
, let us first give a technical result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
I
be a time slab. There exists
\theta>0
small enough such that the global solution
u
to (
1
) satisfies

1)

\|u-e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}\|_{L^{a}(I,L^{r})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,H^{s})}^{\theta}\|u\|^{p-\theta}_{L^{a}(I,L^{r})}
;

2)

\|(1+|\nabla|^{s})(u-e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0})\|_{L^{q}(I,L^{r})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,H^{s})}^{\theta}\|u\|^{p-1-\theta}_{L^{a}(I,L^{r})}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{q}(I,L^{r})}
.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

1)

Using Hölder’s inequality, one writes

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|<1)}=\||x|^{-b}|u|^{p-\theta}|u|^{\theta}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|<1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\mu}(|x|<1)}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-2s}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-\theta},

where

\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{\theta(N-2s)}{2N}+\frac{p-\theta}{r},

provided that

0<\theta<\frac{2N}{N-2s},\;0<\theta<p,\;p-r<\theta,\;\frac{b}{N}<\frac{1}{\mu}.

The last integrability condition reads

\displaystyle\frac{b}{N}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}=1-\frac{\theta(N-2s)}{2N}-\frac{1+p-\theta}{r}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\theta(N-2s)}{2N}-\frac{(N-2s_{c})(1+p-\theta)-4(s-s_{c})}{2N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2N-\theta(N-2s)-(N-2s_{c})(1+p-\theta)+4(s-s_{c})}{2N}.

This is equivalent to

\displaystyle 2b

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2N-\theta(N-2s)-(N-2s_{c})(1+p-\theta)+4(s-s_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2N-\theta(N-2s)-(N-2s_{c})(-1+p+2-\theta)+4(s-s_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\theta(s-s_{c})+2b.

This is obviously satisfied and gives via Sobolev embedding

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u\|_{L^{d^{\prime}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|<1))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s})}^{\theta}\|\|u(t)\|_{L^{r}}^{p-\theta}\|_{L^{d^{\prime}}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{s})}^{\theta}\|u(t)\|^{p-\theta}_{L^{a}(I,L^{r})}.

Let us estimate the same term on the complementary to the unit ball. Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|>1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\mu_{1}}(|x|>1)}\|u\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-\theta}.

Here, the integrability condition reads

\displaystyle\frac{b}{N}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}=1-\frac{\theta}{r_{1}}-\frac{1+p-\theta}{r}

(5.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\theta}{r_{1}}-\frac{(N-2s_{c})(1+p-\theta)-4(s-s_{c})}{2N}.

This is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{r_{1}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 1-\frac{b}{N}-\frac{(N-2s_{c})(1+p-\theta)-4(s-s_{c})}{2N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2N-2b-(N-2s_{c})(-1+p+2-\theta)+4(s-s_{c})}{2N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(N-2s_{c})}{2N}.

Thus, it is sufficient to choose
r_{1}\in[2,\frac{2N}{N-2s_{c}})
. The first point follows with Strichartz estimates arguing as previously.

2)

Using the first point with the equality
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{p-1-\theta}{a}+\frac{1}{q}
, one has by Strichartz estimates

\displaystyle\|u-e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(I,L^{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,H^{s})}^{\theta}\|\|u\|^{p-1-\theta}_{L^{r}}\|u\|_{L^{r}}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,H^{s})}^{\theta}\|u\|^{p-1-\theta}_{L^{a}(I,L^{r})}\|u\|_{L^{q}(I,L^{r})}.

Now, let us estimate the term

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}^{s}[|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b-s}|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|<1))}+\||x|^{-b-s}|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|>1))}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\mathbf{D}^{s}[|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|<1))}+\||x|^{-b}\mathbf{D}^{s}[|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|>1))}.

Using Hölder’s inequality, one writes

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b-s}|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|<1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b-s}\|_{L^{\mu}(|x|<1)}\|\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-2s}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{rN}{N-rs}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{r}}.

Here, one has the same condition (
5.3
) above

\frac{s+b}{N}<\frac{1}{\mu}=1+\frac{s}{N}+\theta(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{s}{N})-\frac{p+1-\theta}{r}.

On the complementary of the unit ball, one has

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b-s}|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|>1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b-s}\|_{L^{\mu_{1}}(|x|>1)}\|u\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{rN}{N-rs}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{r}}.

Here, one has the same condition (
5.4
) above

\frac{s+b}{N}>\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}=1+\frac{s}{N}+\frac{\theta}{r_{1}}-\frac{p+1-\theta}{r}.

Thus, one can choose
r_{2}\in[2,\frac{2N}{N-2s_{c}})
. Now, using the fractional chain rule in Lemma
2.1
, one gets

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\mathbf{D}^{s}[|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|<1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\mu_{2}}(|x|<1)}\|u\|_{L^{r_{2}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{r}}.

Here

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{b}{N}<\frac{1}{\mu_{2}}=1-\frac{\theta}{r_{2}}-\frac{p+1-\theta}{r};\\
2\leq r_{2}\leq\frac{2N}{N-2s}\end{array}\right.

This condition is satisfied as before for
r_{2}=\frac{2N}{N-2s}
. The last term reads

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\mathbf{D}^{s}[|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|>1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\mu_{3}}(|x|>1)}\|u\|_{L^{r_{3}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{r}}.

Here

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{b}{N}>\frac{1}{\mu_{3}}=1-\frac{\theta}{r_{3}}-\frac{p+1-\theta}{r};\\
2\leq r_{3}\leq\frac{2N}{N-2s}\end{array}\right.

This condition is satisfied as before for
r_{3}\in[2,\frac{2N}{N-2s_{c}})
. Now, plugging all the above estimates together and using the equality
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{p-1-\theta}{a}+\frac{1}{q}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}^{s}[|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u]\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{r}}^{p-1-\theta}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{r}}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{a}(I,L^{r})}^{p-1-\theta}\|\mathbf{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{q}(I,L^{r})}.

The proof is finished via Strichartz estimates.

∎

The key of the proof of the scattering criterion is the next result.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Proposition
5.1
are fulfilled. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
T,\mu>0
satisfying

\|e^{-i(t-T)\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u(T)\|_{L^{a}((T,\infty),L^{r})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\mu}.

(5.5)

Proof.

By the integral formula

\displaystyle e^{-i(t-T)\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}+i\int_{0}^{T}e^{-i(t-\tau)\mathbf{D}^{2s}}[|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u]\,d\tau

(5.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}+i\Big(\int_{0}^{T-\varepsilon^{-\beta}}+\int_{T-\varepsilon^{-\beta}}^{T}\Big)e^{-i(t-\tau)\mathbf{D}^{2s}}[|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u]\,d\tau

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle e^{-it\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}+F_{1}+F_{2}.

\bullet

Estimate of the linear term
.

Since
(a,\frac{rN}{N+rs_{c}})\in\Gamma
, by Strichartz estimate and Sobolev embedding, one has

\displaystyle\|e^{-i\cdot\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}\|_{L^{a}((T,\infty),L^{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}e^{-i\cdot\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}\|_{L^{a}((T,\infty),L^{\frac{rN}{N+rs_{c}}})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}.

Thus, one may choose
T_{0}>\varepsilon^{-\beta}>0
, where
\beta>0
, such that

\|e^{-i\cdot\mathbf{D}^{2s}}u_{0}\|_{L^{a}((T_{0},\infty),L^{r})}\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

(5.7)

\bullet

Estimate of the term
F_{2}
.

By the assumption (
5.2
), one has for
T>\varepsilon^{-\beta}
large enough,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}(x)|u(T,x)|^{2}\,dx<\varepsilon^{2}.

Moreover, a computation with the use of (
1
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}(x)\Re[\dot{u}(t,x)\bar{u}(t,x)]\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}(x)\Im[\mathbf{D}^{2s}u(t,x)\bar{u}(t,x)]\,dx.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{D}^{s}(\psi_{R}\bar{u})\mathbf{D}^{s}u\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{u}\mathbf{D}^{s}\psi_{R}\mathbf{D}^{s}u\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{D}^{s}u\Big(\mathbf{D}^{s}(\psi_{R}\bar{u})-\bar{u}\mathbf{D}^{s}(\psi_{R})-\psi_{R}\mathbf{D}^{s}(\bar{u})\Big)\,dx.

By Lemma
2.2
with
\psi_{R}
rather than
v
, one has via Sobolev embedding

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-s}+\|\mathbf{D}^{s_{1}}\psi_{R}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\,\|\mathbf{D}^{s_{2}}\bar{u}\|_{L^{p_{2}}},

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-s}+R^{\frac{N}{p_{1}}-s_{1}}\|u\|_{H^{s}},

provided that
s_{1},s_{2},p_{1},p_{2}
satisfy

s=s_{1}+s_{2},\quad 2\leq p_{2}\leq\frac{2N}{N-2(s-s_{2})},\quad\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}.

Let us choose
s_{1}=\theta\,s
and
p_{1}=\frac{2N}{\theta\,s}
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. Hence

p_{2}=\frac{2N}{N-\theta\,s}
and
\frac{N}{p_{1}}-s_{1}=-\frac{\theta}{2}s
. It follows that

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-s}+R^{-\frac{\theta}{2}s}\|u\|_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(N,s,\psi,\theta)\,R^{-\frac{\theta}{2}s},

where
C(N,s,\psi,\theta)
comes from the sharp constant in the Sobolev embedding
[
22
, Theorem 1.1, p. 226]
and
\|\mathbf{D}^{s_{1}}\psi\|_{p_{1}}
. Since

C(N,s,\psi,\theta)\to C(N,s,\psi)>0\;\;\text{as}\;\;\theta\to 1,

we deduce that

\bigg|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\bigg|\lesssim R^{-\frac{s}{2}}.

Therefore, for any
T-\varepsilon^{-\beta}\leq t\leq T
and
R>\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(2+\beta)}{s}}
, we obtain

\|\psi_{R}u(t)\|\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi_{R}(x)|u(T,x)|^{2}\,dx+C\frac{T-t}{R^{\frac{s}{2}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon.

This gives

\|\psi_{R}u\|_{L^{\infty}([T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T],L^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon.

By Strichartz estimate and Lemma
5.2
, one has

\displaystyle\|\psi_{R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}\|_{L^{d^{\prime}}((T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T),L^{r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T),H^{1})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{a}(J_{1},L^{r})}^{p-1-\theta}\|\psi_{R}u\|_{L^{a}((T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T),L^{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|(T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T)|^{\frac{p-1-\theta}{a}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T),H^{1})}^{p-1}\|\psi_{R}u\|_{L^{a}((T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T),L^{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\beta\frac{p-1-\theta}{a}}\|\psi_{R}u\|_{L^{\infty}((T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T),L^{r})}.

Using Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, one has

\displaystyle\|\psi_{R}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}\|_{L^{d^{\prime}}((T-\varepsilon^{-\beta},T),L^{r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\beta\frac{p-1-\theta}{a}}\|\psi_{R}u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{1},L^{2})}^{1-\frac{N}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\beta\frac{p-1-\theta}{a}}\varepsilon^{1-\frac{N}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{(1-\beta)\frac{(s-s_{c})(p-1-\theta)}{s(1+p-\theta)}}.

Now, let us estimate

\displaystyle\|(1-\psi_{R})|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}u^{p-\theta}u^{\theta}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(|x|>R/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\mu}(|x|>R/2)}\|u\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{\theta}\|u\|^{p-\theta}_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{N-b\mu}\|u\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{\theta}\|u\|^{p-\theta}_{L^{r}}.

Here

\displaystyle b>\frac{N}{\mu}

\displaystyle\|[|x|^{-s}u]^{\frac{b}{s}}u^{p-1-\frac{b}{s}}\mathbf{D}^{N(1-s)(1-\delta)}u\|_{L^{\frac{2}{2-\delta}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\||x|^{-s}u\|^{\frac{b}{s}}\|u\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{p-1-\frac{b}{s}}\|\mathbf{D}^{N(1-s)(1-\delta)}u\|_{L^{r_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\frac{b}{s}}\|u\|_{{L^{r_{1}}}}^{p-1-\frac{b}{s}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{1+\frac{b}{s}}\|u\|_{{L^{r_{1}}}}^{p-1-\frac{b}{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{p}.

Here, on takes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2-\delta}{2}=\frac{b}{2s}+\frac{p-1-\frac{b}{s}}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{2},\\
p\geq 1+\frac{b}{s},\\
0\leq N(1-s)(1-\delta)\leq s,\\
2\leq r_{1}\leq\frac{2N}{N-2s}.\end{array}\right.

So,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(p-1-\frac{b}{s})\frac{N-2s}{2N}\leq\frac{1-\delta}{2}-\frac{b}{2s}=\frac{p-1-\frac{b}{s}}{r_{1}}\leq\frac{p-1-\frac{b}{s}}{2},\\
p\geq 1+\frac{b}{s},\\
0\leq\frac{N(1-\delta)}{1+N(1-\delta)}\leq s.\end{array}\right.

Then,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2\Big(\frac{1-\delta}{2}-\frac{b}{2s}\Big)\leq p-1-\frac{b}{s}\leq\frac{2N}{N-2s}\Big(\frac{1-\delta}{2}-\frac{b}{2s}\Big);\\
p\geq 1+\frac{b}{s};\\
0\leq\frac{N(1-\delta)}{1+N(1-\delta)}\leq s.\end{array}\right.

Therefore,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\{1+\frac{b}{s},2-\delta\}\leq p\leq 1+\frac{b}{s}+\frac{N}{N-2s}\Big(1-\delta-\frac{b}{s}\Big);\\
\frac{N(1-\delta)}{1+N(1-\delta)}\leq s.\end{array}\right.

The proof is achieved by taking
\delta\to 0
.

Appendix B
A remark on the defocusing case

Although the main goal of this paper is concerned with the focusing regime, we want to draw the reader’s attention to the fact that the defocusing case can be studied in a more simpler way. For the sake of completeness, we give the statement and the proof in this appendix.

Consider the defocusing fractional inhomogeneous Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}i\partial_{t}u-(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u(0,x)&=&u_{0}(x).\end{array}\right.

One proves the following scattering result.

Proposition B.1
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
s\in(\frac{N}{2N-1},1)
,
0<b<2s
and
p_{*}<p<p^{*}
. Then, for
u_{0}\in H_{rad}^{s}
, the maximal energy solution of (
B
) is global and scatters in
H^{s}
.

Proof.

The global existence follows by the conservation of the energy and the energy sub-critical regime. Let the Morawetz action

M_{f}[u(t)]:=2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{u}\nabla{f}\cdot\nabla u\,dx:=2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{u}\partial_{k}{f}\partial_{k}u\,dx.

Taking into account the calculus performed in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Big(M_{f}[u(t)]\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(4\partial_{k}\bar{u}_{m}\partial^{2}_{kl}{f}\partial_{l}u_{m}-\Delta^{2}{f}|u_{m}|^{2}\,\Big)dx\,dm

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta{f}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+\frac{4b}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{x\cdot\nabla{f}}{|x|^{2}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx.

One picks
f(x):=|x|
. Then,

\nabla f(x)=\frac{x}{|x|},\quad\Delta f=\frac{N-1}{|x|},

and

-\Delta^{2}f=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}4\pi(N-1)\delta_{0}\quad\mbox{if}\quad N=3,\\
\frac{(N-1)(N-3)}{|\cdot|^{3}}\quad\mbox{if}\quad N\geq 4.\end{array}\right.

Clearly we have

\partial_{l}\partial_{k}f\Re(\partial_{k}u_{m}\partial_{l}\bar{u_{m}})\geq 0,

where
u_{m}
is given by (
2.18
).
Indeed, denoting
r:=|x|
, one computes

\displaystyle\partial_{l}\partial_{k}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}(\delta_{lk}-\frac{x_{l}x_{k}}{r^{2}});

\displaystyle\partial_{l}\partial_{k}f\Re(\partial_{k}u_{m}\partial_{l}\bar{u}_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left(|\nabla u_{m}|^{2}-\frac{|x\cdot\nabla u_{m}|^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\geq 0.

Hence,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Big(M_{f}[u(t)]\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta{f}|x|^{-b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+\frac{4b}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{x\cdot\nabla{f}}{|x|^{2}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)(N-1)}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b-1}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+\frac{4b}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+p}\,dx.

Since by
[
8
, Lemma A.1]
, one has
|M_{f}[u(t)]|\leq C(\|\mathbf{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\|^{2}+\|u(t)\|\|\mathbf{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\|)
, one gets

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b-1}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{1+p}\,dx\lesssim 1.

Therefore, by the fractional radial Sobolev inequality (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b-1}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\,dx\,dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{1+p}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(t,x)|^{1+\frac{2(1+b)}{N-2s}+p}\,dx\,dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}}|u(t,x)|^{1+\frac{2b}{N-2s}+p}\,dx\,dt.

Hence,

u\in L^{1+\frac{2(1+b)}{N-2s}+p}\Big(\mathbb{R},L^{1+\frac{2(1+b)}{N-2s}+p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\Big)\cap L^{1+\frac{2b}{N-2s}+p}\Big(\mathbb{R},L^{1+\frac{2b}{N-2s}+p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\Big).

Now, since
0<s_{c}<1
implies that
0<1+\frac{2b}{N-2s}+p<s
, one has
(1+\frac{2b}{N-2s}+p,1+\frac{2b}{N-2s}+p)\in\Gamma_{\gamma}
for some
0<\gamma<s
. The scattering follows by standard arguments via Stichartz estimates.
∎

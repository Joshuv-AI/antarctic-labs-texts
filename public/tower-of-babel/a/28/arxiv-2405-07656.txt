Non-Rigid Designators in Modal and Temporal Free Description Logics
Alessandro Artale, Roman Kontchakov, Andrea Mazzullo, Frank Wolter

Abstract
Definite descriptions, such as ‘the General Chair of KR 2024,​’ are a semantically transparent device for object identification in knowledge representation. In first-order modal logic, definite descriptions have been widely investigated for their non-rigidity, which allows them to designate different objects (or none at all) at different states. We propose expressive modal description logics with non-rigid definite descriptions and names, and investigate decidability and complexity of the satisfiability problem. We first systematically link satisfiability for the one-variable fragment of first-order modal logic with counting to our modal description logics. Then, we prove a promising NExpTime -completeness result for concept satisfiability for the fundamental epistemic multi-agent logic 𝐒𝟓 n \mathbf{S5}^{n} and its neighbours, and show that some expressive logics that are undecidable with constant domain become decidable (but Ackermann-hard) with expanding domains. Finally, we conduct a fine-grained analysis of decidability of temporal logics.

Introduction

Definite descriptions
,

Definite descriptions and individual names can also

or the individual name
‘KR 2019.​’
In order to admit these

[
2002
,
2002
,
2021
,
2021
]
.
In contrast, classical logic approaches assume that individual names always designate, and that definite descriptions can be paraphrased in terms of
existence and uniqueness conditions, an approach dating back to Russell (

Recently,

include the universal role,

(‘the unique object in

Hence, for instance, using nominal

to designate KR 2024, we can refer to the General Chair of KR 2024 by means of the definite description
\{\smash{\iota}\exists\mathsf{isGenChair}.\{\mathsf{kr24}\}\}
.
Then, in
\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}

\exists u.(\{\mathsf{pierre}\}\sqcap\{\smash{\iota}\exists\mathsf{isGenChair}.\{\mathsf{kr24}\}\}).

The free DLs

and
\smash{\mathcal{ELO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}

ExpTime

Names and descriptions display interesting behaviours also in
modal
(epistemic, temporal) settings. These are indeed
referentially opaque
contexts, where the
intension
(i.e., the meaning) of a term might not coincide with its
extension
(that is, its referent)
[
2004
]
.
Here, referring expressions can behave as
non-rigid designators
, meaning that they can designate different individuals across different states (epistemic alternatives, instants of time, etc.).

For example, in an epistemic scenario, even if everybody is aware that Pierre is the General Chair of KR 2024, not everyone thereby knows that he is also the General Chair of the (only, so far, and excluding virtual location) KR Conference held in Southeast Asia, despite the fact that ‘the KR Conference held in Southeast Asia’ and ‘KR 2024’ refer (to this day) to the same object. Indeed, ‘the KR Conference held in Southeast Asia’ can be conceived to designate another event by someone unaware of its actual reference to KR 2024.
Similarly, in a temporal setting,
‘the General Chair of KR’
refers to
Pierre
in 2024,
but
will designate someone else over the years.
So, for instance, if we assume that Pierre works and will continue working in Europe,
we can conclude that the General Chair of KR currently works in Europe, but we should not infer that it will always remain the case.

Due to this fundamental and challenging interplay between designation and modalities,
non-rigid descriptions and names
have been widely investigated in first-order modal and temporal logics
as individual concepts or flexible terms
capable of taking different values across states
[
1984
,
2001
,
2007
,
2008
,
2012
,
2013
,
2020
,
2021
,
2023
]
.

The aim of this contribution is to introduce modal DLs that have sufficient expressive power to represent the phenomena discussed above, explore their relationship to standard modal DLs without definite descriptions and non-designating names, and investigate decidability and complexity of reasoning in these formalisms.

In detail, we propose the language
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}^{n}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}

\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}

For instance, under an epistemic reading of the modal operator

In a temporal setting, where

In this work, we consider

We

Here,

Remark 1

(Encoding of assertions)
.

Assertions can be introduced as syntactic sugar using the universal role, with

and

\exists u.(\{\tau\}\sqcap C)\text{ and }\exists u.(\{\tau_{1}\}\sqcap\exists r.\{\tau_{2}\}).

The first example in Sec.

To avoid ambiguities, we need to use

Observe that, in an assertion of the form

\Diamond_{i}C
applies to the object, if any, designated by the term
\tau

, and the assertion is false at a world whenever
\tau

\Diamond_{i}[C(\tau)]
,
the diamond plays the role of a

[

, assertion

Spy Points: Eliminating the Universal Role

Our next observation allows us to eliminate occurrences of the universal role from ontologies.

Lemma 6

Non-Rigid Designators and Counting

We prove a strong link between non-rigid designators and the first-order one-variable modal logic enriched with the ‘elsewhere’ quantifier,

[

u

All constructs are interpreted as before,

\displaystyle(\exists^{\neq}u.C)^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{d\in\Delta^{w}\mid C^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}\setminus\{d\}\neq\emptyset\bigr\}.

Discussion and Future Work

We have introduced and investigated novel fragments of first-order modal logic with non-rigid (and possibly non-referring) individual names and definite descriptions. Potential applications that remain to be explored include business process management, where formalisms for representing the dynamic behaviour of data and information are crucial
[
2023
,
2018
]
, and context, knowledge or standpoint-dependent reasoning for which possible worlds semantics is needed
[
2017
,
2023
]
.

I_{i}=-(r_{k},q_{j},q^{\prime})
,
v_{k}>0
,
v^{\prime}_{k}=v_{k}-1
,
and
v^{\prime}_{\overline{k}}=v_{\overline{k}}
;

•

I_{i}=-(r_{k},q^{\prime},q_{j})
,
v_{k}=v^{\prime}_{k}=0
, and

v^{\prime}_{\overline{k}}=v_{\overline{k}}
.

Given an
input

(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, the

computation of
M
on input
(v_{1},v_{2})
is the (unique) longest
sequence of configurations

(q^{0},v^{0}_{1},v^{0}_{2})\Rightarrow_{M}(q^{1},v^{1}_{1},v^{1}_{2})\Rightarrow_{M}\ldots
, such that

q^{0}=q_{0}
,
v^{0}_{1}=v_{1}
and
v^{0}_{2}=v_{2}
.
We say that
M

halts

on input

(0,0)
if the computation of
M
on input
(0,0)
is finite: thus, its initial configuration takes the form
(q_{0},0,0)
, while its last configuration has the form
(q_{L},v_{1},v_{2})
, for some
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
.
The
halting problem for Minsky machines
is the problem of
deciding, given a Minsky machine
M
, whether
M
halts on input

(0,0)
.
This problem is known to be undecidable; see, e.g.,
[
2002
,
2017
]
.

To represent the computation of a Minsky machine
M
on input
(0,0)
, we use the
temporal dimension to model successive configurations in the
computation. We introduce a concept name
Q_{i}
, for each state
q_{i}
of
M
. Concept names
R_{1},R_{2}

are used to represent registers
r_{1},r_{2}
, respectively: the value of register
r_{k}
at
any computation step will coincide with the cardinality of the extension of
R_{k}
at the corresponding moment of time.
The incrementation of the
value of register
r_{k}
is modelled by requiring that the extension of
concept
\lnot R_{k}\sqcap{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{k}
is included in that of nominal

\{a_{k}\}
; dually, the decrementation of register
r_{k}
is modelled by including the extension of

R_{k}\sqcap{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}\lnot R_{k}
in the nominal
\{b_{k}\}
.
Since the individual names
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
are interpreted

non-rigidly
, the (unique) element added or subtracted from the
extension of
R_{k}
will vary over time.

We first construct a
\smash{\mathcal{TL}^{\textstyle\circ}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
ontology
\mathcal{O}_{M}
such that

•

Q_{0}
is satisfiable under
\mathcal{O}_{M}
over
(\mathbb{N},<)
iff machine
M
never halts on
(0,0)
;

•

Q_{0}
is satisfiable under
\mathcal{O}_{M}
over finite flows of time iff machine
M
halts on
(0,0)
;

Then, Lemmas
4
and
6
can be used to replace the ontology
\mathcal{O}_{M}
with a
\smash{\mathcal{TL}^{\textstyle\circ}_{\mathcal{ALCO}}}
ontology
\mathcal{O}_{M}
with the same property, thus establishing the required results.

We use the following CIs to ensure that concepts
Q_{i}
representing states are either empty or coincide with the whole domain at any moment of time:

(\textbf{S1})

\exists u.Q_{i}\sqsubseteq Q_{i}
, for all
0\leq i\leq L
;

(\textbf{S2})

Q_{i}\sqcap Q_{j}\sqsubseteq\bot
, for all
0\leq i<j\leq L
.

Next, we encode instructions. We begin with incrementation. An instruction of the form

I_{i}=+(r_{k},q_{j})
is represented by the following CIs:

(\textbf{I1})

Q_{i}\sqsubseteq\exists u.(\{a_{k}\}\sqcap\neg R_{k})
;

(\textbf{I2})

Q_{i}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{k}\Leftrightarrow(R_{k}\sqcup\{a_{k}\})
;

(\textbf{I3})

Q_{i}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{\overline{k}}\Leftrightarrow R_{\overline{k}}
;

(\textbf{I4})

Q_{i}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}Q_{j}
.

An instructions of the form
I_{i}=-(r_{k},q_{j},q_{h})
is
represented by the following CIs:

(\textbf{D1})

Q_{i}\sqcap\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq\exists u.(\{b_{k}\}\sqcap R_{k})
;

(\textbf{D2})

Q_{i}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{k}\Leftrightarrow(R_{k}\sqcap\neg\{b_{k}\})
;

(\textbf{D3})

Q_{i}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{\overline{k}}\Leftrightarrow R_{\overline{k}}
;

(\textbf{D4})

Q_{i}\sqcap\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}Q_{j}
;

(\textbf{D5})

Q_{i}\sqcap\neg\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}Q_{h}
.

Given
\mathfrak{M}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}})
, we say that an instant
t\in\mathfrak{T}

encodes
a configuration
(q_{i},v_{1},v_{2})
whenever
Q_{i}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}=\Delta
and
|R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}|=v_{k}
, for
k=1,2
. We show the following:

Claim 7
.

For any
\mathfrak{M}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{t})_{t\in\mathfrak{T}})
that satisfies

(
S1
)

–

(
D5
)

, if
t\in\mathfrak{T}
encodes
(q_{i},v_{1},v_{2})
and
(q_{i},v_{1},v_{2})\Rightarrow_{M}(q^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{2})
, then
t+1
encodes
(q^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{2})
.

Proof of Claim.

We consider the following cases.

•

Suppose
I_{i}=+(r_{k},q_{j})
. By

(
I4
)

with

(
S1
)

, we have
Q_{j}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=\Delta
, and thus

q^{\prime}=q_{j}
. Note that, by

(
S2
)

,
Q_{j^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=\emptyset
for any
j^{\prime}\neq j
. Then,
by

(
I1
)

,
a_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}
is defined and
a_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}\notin R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}
. By

(
I2
)

,
R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}\cup\{a_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}\}
, whence
|R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}|=|R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}|+1
, and so
v^{\prime}_{k}=v_{k}+1
. For the other counter,
by

(
I3
)

,
R_{\overline{k}}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=R_{\overline{k}}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}
, whence
v^{\prime}_{\overline{k}}=v_{\overline{k}}
.

•

Suppose
I_{i}=-(r_{k},q_{j},q_{h})
. By

(
D4
)

with

(
S1
)

, if
R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}\neq\emptyset
, then
Q_{j}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=\Delta
, and thus

q^{\prime}=q_{j}
. Otherwise, if
R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}=\emptyset
, then, by

(
D5
)

with

(
S1
)

,
Q_{h}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=\Delta
, and thus

q^{\prime}=q_{h}
. Note that in either case, by

(
S2
)

,
Q_{j^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=\emptyset
for any other
j^{\prime}
. We next deal with the values of counters. If
R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}\neq\emptyset
, then, by

(
D1
)

,
b_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}
is defined and
b_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}\in R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}
. Then, by

(
D2
)

, we have
R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}\setminus\{b_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}\}
. It follows that
|R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}|=|R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}|-1
and so
v^{\prime}_{k}=v_{k}-1
. On the other hand, if
R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}=\emptyset
, then, by

(
D2
)

,
R_{k}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=\emptyset
and so
v^{\prime}_{k}=v_{k}=0
. For the other counter,
by

(
D3
)

,
R_{\overline{k}}^{\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}=R_{\overline{k}}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}
, whence
v^{\prime}_{\overline{k}}=v_{\overline{k}}
.

So, in each case,
t+1
encodes
(q^{\prime},v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime})
.
∎

It remains to encode the initial configuration and the acceptance
condition. For the initial configuration, we two CIs

(\textbf{O1})

Q_{0}\sqcap R_{k}\sqsubseteq\bot
, for
k=1,2
;

Clearly, these two CIs ensure that the value of both counters is

.
For the non-termination condition over
(\mathbb{N},<)
and the termination condition over finite flows of time, we use the following CIs, respectively:

(\textbf{A1})

.

(\textbf{A2})

\neg{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{{$\bigcirc$}}}}\top\sqsubseteq Q_{L}
.

We claim that
Q_{0}
is satisfiable under
\mathcal{O}_{M}

. Indeed, if

is satisfiable under
\mathcal{O}_{M}
, then by

(
O1
)

, moment 0 encodes configuration
(q_{0},0,0)
. By Claim
7
, moments 0, 1, 2, etc. encode configurations
(q^{t},v^{t}_{1},v_{2}^{t})
such that
(q^{t},v_{1}^{t},v_{2}^{t})\Rightarrow_{M}(q^{t+1},v^{t+1}_{1},v^{t+1}_{2})

(
A1
)

, the halting state

Conversely,
(q^{0},v^{0}_{1},v^{0}_{2})\Rightarrow_{M}\ldots\Rightarrow_{M}(q^{t},v^{t}_{1},v^{t}_{2})\Rightarrow_{M}\ldots
be the computation of
M
on input

(0,0)

and

.
Let
\Delta

t\in\mathbb{N}
and

, for some

,
we set
Q_{i}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}=\Delta
and
Q_{j}^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}=\emptyset

Then, for every instant

,

,

•

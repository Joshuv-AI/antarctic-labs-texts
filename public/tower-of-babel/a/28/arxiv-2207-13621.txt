Results on K 1 {\textrm{K}_{1}} of general quadratic groups
Rabeya Basu and Kuntal Chakraborty

Abstract
In the first part of this article, we discuss the relative cases of Quillen–Suslin’s local-global principle for the general quadratic (Bak’s unitary) groups, and its applications for the (relative) stable and unstable K 1 {\textrm{K}_{1}} -groups. The second part is dedicated to the graded version of the local-global principle for the general quadratic groups and its application to deduce a result for Bass’ nil groups.



keywords
Graded rings, Form rings, Bak’s unitary groups, Bass’ nil groups

2
Preliminaries

Let us recall some necessary definitions and the key lemmas.

Definition 2.1

(cf. ​
[
Bak1
]
)
.

Let
R
be an (not necessarily commutative) associative ring with identity, and
with involution
-:R\rightarrow R
,
a\mapsto\overline{a}
. Let
\lambda\in C(R)
= center of
R
be an element
with the property
\lambda\overline{\lambda}=1
. We define additive subgroups of
R
:

\Lambda_{max}=\{a\in R\mid a=-\lambda\overline{a}\}~\text{and }~\Lambda_{min}=\{a-\lambda\overline{a}\mid a\in R\}.

One checks that
\Lambda_{max}
and
\Lambda_{min}
are closed under the conjugation operation

a\mapsto\overline{x}ax
for any
x\in R
. A
\lambda
-form parameter on
R
is an additive subgroup
\Lambda
of
R
such that
\Lambda_{min}\subseteq\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{max}
, and

\overline{x}\Lambda x\subseteq\Lambda
for all
x\in R
. A pair
(R,\Lambda)
is called a form ring.

Remark 2.2
.

For a from ring
(R,\Lambda)
, we can extend the involution
-:R\to R
to an involution
-:R[X]\to R[X]
by setting
\overline{X}=X
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(R,\Lambda,\lambda)
be a form ring. Then
(R[X],\Lambda[X],\lambda)
is also a form ring
with respect to the involution obtained by extending, as in the previous remark.

Proof 2.4
.

Let us consider the subgroups
\Lambda_{min}(R[X])
and
\Lambda_{max}(R[X])
. It can be checked that
\Lambda_{min}(R[X])=\Lambda_{min}(R)[X]
, and
\Lambda_{max}(R[X])=\Lambda_{max}(R)[X]
. Hence it follows

\Lambda_{min}(R[X])\subseteq\Lambda[X]\subseteq\Lambda_{max}(R[X]).

To prove that it is closed under conjugation,
let us consider elements
a(X)\in R[X]
and
b(X)\in\Lambda[X]
. Using double induction on the degrees of
a(X)
and
b(X)
, we will show that

\overline{a(X)}b(X)a(X)\in\Lambda[X].

We first show that the statement is true for
\deg(b(X))=0
. Assume
b(X)=b\in\Lambda
. Hence we need to prove

\overline{a(X)}ba(X)\in\Lambda[X]
for all
a(X)\in R[X]
. Clearly, this is obvious when
\deg(a(X))=0
. For a linear polynomial
a(X)=a_{0}+a_{1}X
,

\displaystyle\overline{a(X)}ba(X)

\displaystyle=(\overline{a}_{0}+\overline{a}_{1}X)b(a_{0}+a_{1}X)

\displaystyle=\overline{a}_{0}ba_{0}+(\overline{a}_{0}ba_{1}+\overline{a}_{1}ba_{0})X+\overline{a}_{1}ba_{1}X^{2}

\displaystyle=\overline{a}_{0}ba_{0}+\overline{(a_{0}+a_{1})}b(a_{0}+a_{1})X-(\overline{a}_{0}ba_{0}+\overline{a}_{1}ba_{1})X+\overline{a}_{1}ba_{1}X^{2}.

Hence the statement is true for
\deg(b(X))=0
and
\deg(a(X))=1
. Assume the statement for
\deg(b(X))=0
is true for all
a(X)
with
\deg(a(X))<m
. Suppose
\deg(a(X))=m
. Then,
a(X)=a_{m-1}(X)+a_{m}X^{m}
, where
a_{m-1}(X)
is a polynomial of degree at most
m-1
, and
a_{m}\in R
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\overline{a(X)}ba(X)=

\displaystyle(\overline{a_{m-1}(X)}+\overline{a}_{m}X^{m})b(a_{m-1}(X)+a_{m}X^{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{a_{m-1}(X)}ba_{m-1}(X)+\overline{a_{m-1}(X)}ba_{m}X^{m}+\overline{a}_{m}ba_{m-1}(X)X^{m}+\overline{a}_{m}ba_{m}X^{2m}.

Now

\displaystyle\overline{a_{m-1}(X)}ba_{m}+\overline{a}_{m}ba_{m-1}(X)=

\displaystyle=\overline{(a_{m-1}(X)+a_{m})}b(a_{m-1}(X)+a_{m})-(\overline{a_{m-1}(X)}ba_{m-1}(X)+\overline{a}_{m}ba_{m}).

Hence by induction it follows that
\overline{a(X)}ba(X)\in\Lambda[X]
.
Assume the statement is true for
\deg(b(X))<n
. Let
b^{\prime}(X)
be a polynomial in
\Lambda[X]
of degree
n
. Then we can write
b^{\prime}(X)=b^{\prime\prime}(X)+b_{n}X^{n}
, where
b^{\prime\prime}(X)
is a polynomial in
\Lambda[X]
of degree at most
n-1
and
b_{n}\in\Lambda
. Hence by induction

\displaystyle\overline{a(X)}b^{\prime}(X)a(X)=

\displaystyle\overline{a(X)}(b^{\prime\prime}(X)+b_{n}X^{n})a(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{a(X)}b^{\prime\prime}(X)(a(X))+(\overline{a(X)}b_{n}a(X))X^{n}\in\Lambda[X].

To define Bak’s unitary group or the general quadratic group, we fix a (non-zero) central element
\lambda\in R
with
\lambda\overline{\lambda}=1
, and
then consider the form

\psi_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&{\I}_{n}\\
\lambda{{\I}}_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Bak’s Unitary or General Quadratic Groups
{\GQ}
:

{\GQ}(2n,R,\lambda)~=~\{\sigma\in{\GL}(2n,R)\,|\,\overline{\sigma}\psi_{n}\sigma=\psi_{n}\}.

Elementary Quadratic Matrices:

Let
\rho:\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\to\{n+1,n+2,\ldots,2n\}
be defined by
\rho(i)=n+i
.
Let
e_{ij}
be the matrix with
1
in the
ij
-th position
and
0
’s elsewhere. For
a\in R
, and
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we define

q\varepsilon_{ij}(a)={\I}_{2n}+ae_{ij}-\overline{a}e_{\rho(j)\rho(i)}
for
i\neq j
,

qr_{ij}(a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\I}_{2n}+ae_{i\rho(j)}-\lambda\overline{a}e_{j\rho(i)}&\text{for}~i\neq j\\
{\I}_{2n}+ae_{\rho(i)j}&\text{for}~i=j,\end{array}\right.

ql_{ij}(a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\I}_{2n}+ae_{\rho(i)j}-\overline{\lambda}\overline{a}e_{\rho(j)i}&\text{for}~i\neq j\\
{\I}_{2n}+ae_{\rho(i)j}&\text{for}~i=j.\end{array}\right.

(Note that for the second and third type of elementary matrices, if
i=j
, then
we get
a=-\lambda\overline{a}
, and hence it forces that
a\in\Lambda_{max}(R)
. One checks that these
above matrices belong to
{\GQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
; cf. ​
[
Bak1
]
.)

n
-th Elementary Quadratic Group

{\EQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
: The subgroup generated
by
q\varepsilon_{ij}(a),qr_{ij}(a)\text{and }ql_{ij}(a)
, for
a\in R
and
1\leq i,j\leq n
. For uniformity we denote the elementary generators of
{\EQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
by
\eta_{ij}(*)
.
It is clear that the stabilization map takes generators of
{\EQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
to the
generators of
{\EQ}(2(n+1),R,\Lambda)
.

Remark 2.5
.

Throughout this article we shall assume that all ideals of
R
are involution-invariant, i.e., if
J
is an ideal, then
\overline{J}=J
. We also assume that
2n\geq 6

Definition 2.6
.

The relative general quadratic subgroup of

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(R,\Lambda)
be a form ring and
J\subset R
be an ideal. The subgroup of
{\GQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
generated by the

\eta_{ij}(x)\eta_{ji}(a)\eta_{ij}(x)^{-1}

Notation 2.7
.

A row

(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n})\in\nolinebreak R^{n}

of

. We will mostly use localizations with respect to two types of multiplicatively
closed subsets of

S=\{1,s,s^{2},\dots\}

s\in R
is a non-nilpotent
non-zero divisor; and

for some

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(R,\Lambda)
be a form ring and
J\subset R
be an ideal of
R
. The excision ring of
R
with respect to the ideal

J
is denoted by
R\oplus J
and is defined by the set
\{(r,i)\mid r\in R,i\in J\}
with the addition defined by

3
Relative L-G Principle for the Transvection Subgroups

It is known that any module finite ring (i.e., finite over its center)
R
can be written as a direct limit of its finitely generated subrings. Also,
{\G}(R,\lambda)=\underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim}\,{\G}(R_{i},\lambda_{i})
,
where the limit is taken over all finitely generated subring of
R
.
Hence, we can assume that
C(R)
is Noetherian.
For the rest of this section, we shall consider
R
to be a module finite ring with identity.
The local-global principle for the transvection subgroups (absolute cases) of the full automorphism groups was established in
[
BBR
]
for the traditional classical groups. Then, in
[
basu2
]
it was generalized for the general quadratic groups.
In this section we deduce the relative L-G principle for the transvection subgroups.
The following results are proved in
[
BRK
]
for traditional classical groups of free modules, and the steps of the proof for the general quadratic groups are identical. Therefore, we state these results without proof.
For any column vector
v\in(R^{2n})^{t}
we consider the row vector
\widetilde{v}=\overline{v}^{t}\psi_{n}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We define a map
M:(R^{2n})^{t}\times(R^{2n})^{t}\rightarrow M(2n,R)
and the inner product
\langle\>,\>\rangle
as follows:

M(v,w)=v.\widetilde{w}-\overline{\lambda}\overline{w}\widetilde{v},~\langle v,w\rangle=\widetilde{v}.w

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(R,\Lambda)
be a form ring and
v\in{\EQ}(2n,R,\Lambda,J)e_{1}
. Let
w\in J^{2n}
be
a column vector such that
\langle v,w\rangle=0
. Then
{\rm I}_{2n}+M(v,w)\in{\EQ}(2n,R,\Lambda,J)
.

Theorem 3.3

(Relative L-G principle)
.

Let
R
be a ring and
J\subset R
be an ideal of
R
. Let
\alpha(X)\in{\GQ}(2n,R[X],\Lambda[X],J[X])
, with
\alpha(0)={\rm I}_{2n}
be such that for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\in{\rm Max}(C(R))
, we have
\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}(X)\in{\EQ}(2n,R_{\mathfrak{m}}[X],\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}[X],J_{\mathfrak{m}}[X])
. In that case,

\alpha(X)\in\nolinebreak{\EQ}(2n,R[X],\Lambda[X],J[X])
.

We recall some definitions and fix notations.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(R,\Lambda)
be a form ring and
P
be a right
R
-module. A map
f:P\times P\to R
is said to be a sesquilinear form if
f(pa,qb)=\overline{a}f(p,q)b
for all
p,q\in P
and
a,b\in R
. A map
q:P\to R/\Lambda
is said to be a quadratic form if
q(p)=f(p,p)+\Lambda
, where
f
is a sesquilinear form on
P
. With respect to a sesquilinear form on
P
, we can define an associated
\lambda
-Hermitian
form

by
h(p,q)=f(p,q)+\lambda\overline{f(q,p)}
. The triplet
(P,h,q)
is called a quadratic module.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(P,q,h)
be a quadratic module and
{\GL}(P)
be the full automorphism group of
P
.
The quadratic module
P
is said to be non-singular if
P
is a projective
R
-module
and the associated
\lambda
-Hermitian form is non-singular.
For a non-singular

{\GQ}(P,\Lambda,q,h)=\{\alpha\in{\GL}(P)\mid h(\alpha u,\alpha v)=h(u,v),q(\alpha u)=q(u)\}

We will denote by
{\GQ}(P,\Lambda,J)

\{\alpha\in{\GQ}(P,\Lambda)\mid\alpha\equiv{\rm Id}\pmod{JP}\}

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(P,h,q)

Lemma 6.10

.

Let

Lemma 6.13
.

.

Definition 6.15
.

Consider the embedding:

{\GQ}^{\lambda}(2n,R,\Lambda)\rightarrow{\GQ}^{\lambda}(2n+2,R,\Lambda),\,\,\,

We use the following notation:

{\GQ}^{\lambda}(R,\Lambda)=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\cup}}{\GQ}^{\lambda}(2n,R,\Lambda),

{\EQ}^{\lambda}(R,\Lambda)=\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\cup}}{\EQ}^{\lambda}(2n,R,\Lambda).

In view of quadratic analog of Whitehead Lemma, we have that the group

coincides with the commutator of

is denoted by

acting from the category of form rings to the category of abelian groups.

Definition 6.16

In the quadratic case, the kernel of the group homomorphism

induced from the form ring homomorphism

is denoted by

{\rm NKQ_{1}}^{\lambda}(R,\Lambda)
.

Remark 6.17
.

Since the class of
\Lambda
-quadratic groups is a subclass of the class of quadratic groups,
the local-global principle holds for
\Lambda
-quadratic groups. We use this fact throughout the next section.

7
Absence of torsion in
{\rm NKQ_{1}}^{\lambda}(R,\lambda)

In
[
STI
]
, J. Stienstra showed that

{\textrm{NK}_{1}}(R)
is a
{\w}(R)
-module, where
{\w}(R)
is the ring of big Witt vectors (cf.
[
BL1
]
and
[
WEL1
]
).
Consequently, in (
[
WEL
]
, §3), C. Weibel
observed that if
k
is a unit in a commutative local ring
R
, then
{\textrm{SK}_{1}}(R[X])
has no

k
-torsion. Note that if
R
is a commutative local ring, then

{\textrm{SK}_{1}}(R[X])
coincides with
{\textrm{NK}_{1}}(R)
; indeed, if
R
is a local ring, then

{\SL}_{n}(R)={\E}_{n}(R)
for all
n>0
. Therefore, we may replace
\alpha(X)
by

\alpha(X)\alpha(0)^{-1}
and assume that
[\alpha(0)]=[{\I}]
. In
[
RB1
]
, the first author
extended Weibel’s result for arbitrary associative rings. In this section we prove the analog
result for
\lambda
-unitary Bass nil-groups,
viz.

{\rm NK_{1}GQ}^{\lambda}(R,\lambda)
,
where
(R,\lambda)
is the form ring as introduced by A. Bak in
[
Bak1
]
. The main
ingredient for our proof is an analog of Higman linearization (for a subclass of
Bak’s unitary group) due to V. Kopeiko; cf.​
[
V
]
.
For the general linear groups, Higman linearization (cf.​
[
RB1
]
) allows us
to show that
{\textrm{NK}_{1}}(R)
has a unipotent representative. The same result is not true in general
for the unitary nil-groups. Kopeiko’s results in
[
V
]
,
[
V1
]
give a complete description
of the elements of
{\rm NK_{1}GQ}^{\lambda}(R,\lambda)
which have (unitary) unipotent representatives.

Definition 7.1
.

For an associative unital ring
R
we consider the truncated polynomial ring

R_{t}=\frac{R[X]}{(X^{t+1})}.

Lemma 7.2

(cf. ​
[
RB1
]
, Lemma 4.1)
.

Let
P(X)\in R[X]
be any polynomial.
Then the following identity holds in the ring
R_{t}
:

(1+X^{r}P(X))=(1+X^{r}P(0))(1+X^{r+1}Q(X)),

where
r>0
and
Q(X)\in R[X]
, with
\deg(Q(X))<t-r
.

Proof 7.3
.

Let us write
P(X)=a_{0}+a_{1}X+\cdots+a_{t}X^{t}
. Then we can
express the polynomial
P(X)
as
P(X)=P(0)+XP^{\prime}(X)
for some
P^{\prime}(X)\in R[X]
. Now, in
R_{t}

\displaystyle(1+X^{r}P(X))(1+X^{r}P(0))^{-1}

\displaystyle=(1+X^{r}P(0)+X^{r+1}P^{\prime}(X))(1+X^{r}P(0))^{-1}

\displaystyle=1+X^{r+1}P^{\prime}(X)(1-X^{r}P(0)+X^{2r}(P(0))^{2}-\cdots)

\displaystyle=1+X^{r+1}Q(X),

​​where
Q(X)\in R[X]
with
\deg(Q(X))<t-r
.
Hence the lemma follows.

Remark 7.4
.

Iterating the above process we can write, for any polynomial

P(X)\in R[X]
,

(1+XP(X))=\Pi_{i=1}^{t}(1+a_{i}X^{i})

in
R_{t}
,
for some
a_{i}\in R
. By ascending induction, it follows that the
a_{i}
’s
are uniquely determined.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
R
be an associative ring, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
kR=R
. Let
P(X)
be a polynomial in
R[X]
. Assume

lies in the center of
R
. Then, if

(1+X^{r}P(X))^{k^{r}}=1

for some

, we have that

(1+X^{r}P(X))=(1+X^{r+1}Q(X))

in the ring
R_{t}
, for some
Q(X)\in R[X]
with
\deg(Q(X))<t-r
.

The following result is due to V. Kopeiko, cf. ​
[
V
]
. This is an analog of Higman linearization for this special case.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
(R,\lambda)
be a form ring. Then,
every element of the group
{\rm NKQ_{1}}^{\lambda}(R,\Lambda)
has a
representative of the form

[a;b,c]_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}{\I}_{r}-aX&bX\\
-cX^{n}&{\I}_{r}+a^{*}X+\cdots+(a^{*})^{n}X^{n}\end{pmatrix}\in{\GQ}^{\lambda}(2r,R[X],\Lambda[X])

for some positive integers

satisfy the following conditions:

the matrices

and

are Hermitian and also

,

2.

the matrices

and

are Hermitian and also

,

3.

bc=a^{n+1}
and

.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let

be an associative ring.
Let

have the representation

for some

Then, if it is true that

Proof 7.8

By Lemma

[a;b,c]_{n}\equiv\mathbb{H}({\I}_{r}-aX)\pmod{{\EQ}^{\lambda}(2r,R[X],\Lambda[X])}
.
Hence
[\alpha]=[\mathbb{H}({\I}_{r}-aX)]
in
{\rm NKQ_{1}}^{\lambda}(R,\Lambda)
.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
(R,\Lambda)
be a form ring, where
R
is an associative ring with
1

and
k
is an invertible integer, i.e.,
kR=R
. Let

[\alpha(X)]=\left[\begin{pmatrix}A(X)&B(X)\\

with

.
Then
[\alpha(X)]
has no

-torsion.

.

By Theorem

,
[\alpha(X)]=[[a;b,c]_{n}]
for some

and for some natural numbers

and

.
Note that in Step

, the invertibility of the top left corner of the matrix

The same is true for the remaining steps of the Proposition

have

-torsion, which implies that also

8
Graded Analog

We recall the
well-known “
Swan–Weibel homotopy trick
”, which is the main ingredient to handle the graded case. Let

R=R_{0}\oplus R_{1}\oplus R_{2}\oplus\cdots
be a graded ring.
An element
a\in R
is denoted by
a=a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots
, where
a_{i}\in R_{i}
for each
i
,
and all but finitely many
a_{i}
’s are zero. Let
R_{+}=R_{1}\oplus R_{2}\oplus\cdots
.
A graded structure of
R
induces a graded structure on
{\rm M}_{n}(R)
(the ring of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
R
).

Definition 8.1
.

Let
a\in R_{0}
be a fixed element. We fix an element
b=b_{0}+b_{1}+\cdots
in
R
and define a ring homomorphism

\epsilon:R\rightarrow R[X]
as follows:

\epsilon(b)=\epsilon(b_{0}+b_{1}+\cdots)\;=\;b_{0}+b_{1}X+b_{2}X^{2}+\cdots+b_{i}X^{i}+\cdots.

Then we evaluate the polynomial
\epsilon(b)(X)
at
X=a
and denote the image by
b^{+}(a)
i.e.,
b^{+}(a)=\epsilon(b)(a)
.
Note that
\big(b^{+}(x)\big)^{+}(y)=b^{+}(xy)
. Observe,
b_{0}=b^{+}(0)
.
We shall use this fact frequently.

The above ring homomorphism
\epsilon
induces a group homomorphism from
{\GL}(n,R)
to
{\GL}(n,R[X])
of rank
n
for every
n\geq 1
, i.e., for

\alpha\in{\GL}(n,R)
we get a map

\epsilon:{\GL}(n,R)\rightarrow{\GL}(n,R[X])\text{, defined by}

\alpha=\alpha_{0}\oplus\alpha_{1}\oplus\alpha_{2}\oplus\cdots\mapsto\alpha_{0}\oplus\alpha_{1}X\oplus\alpha_{2}X^{2}\cdots,

where
\alpha_{i}\in{\rm M}(n,R_{i})
.
As above, for
a\in R_{0}
, we define
\alpha^{+}(a)
as
\alpha^{+}(a)=\epsilon(\alpha)(a).

The graded dilation lemma and graded local-global principle are proved in
[
BS
]
for linear, symplectic and orthogonal groups.
Arguing in similar manner one gets:

Theorem 8.2
.

(Graded Local-Global Principle)

Let
R=R_{0}\oplus R_{1}\oplus R_{2}\oplus\cdots
be an almost commutative graded ring with identity
1
.
Let
\alpha\in{\GQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
be such that
\alpha\equiv{\I}_{2n}\pmod{R_{+}}
. If

\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}\in{\EQ}(2n,R_{\mathfrak{m}},\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})
,
for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(C(R_{0}))
, then
\alpha\in{\EQ}(2n,R,\Lambda).

Moreover, the L-G principle for the elementary subgroups and their normality property are equivalent.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
R=R_{0}\oplus R_{1}\oplus R_{2}\oplus\cdots
be an almost commutative graded ring with identity
1
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent for
2n\geq 6
:

1.

{\EQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
is a normal subgroup of
{\GQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
.

2.

If
\alpha\in{\GQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
with
\alpha^{+}(0)={\rm I}_{2n}
, and
\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}\in{\EQ}(2n,R_{\mathfrak{m}},\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})
for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\in{\rm Max}(C(R_{0}))
, then
\alpha\in{\EQ}(2n,R,\Lambda)
.

As an application of Theorem
8.2
and the Theorem
7.9
, we obtain the following:

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
R=R_{0}\oplus R_{1}\oplus\dots
be an almost commutative graded ring with
1
.
Let
N=N_{0}+N_{1}+\dots+N_{r}\in{\rm M}_{r}(R)
be a nilpotent matrix, and let
{\I}
denote the identity matrix.
Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be a unit in
R_{0}
. If

[({\I}+N)]^{k}=[{\I}]
in
{\rm NKQ}_{1}^{\lambda}(R,\Lambda)
, then
[{\I}+N]=[{\I}+N_{0}]
.

Proof 8.5
.

Consider the ring homomorphism
f:R\rightarrow R[X]
defined by

f(a_{0}+a_{1}+\dots)=a_{0}+a_{1}X+\dots.

Then

\displaystyle[({\I}+N)^{k}]=[{\I}]

\displaystyle\Rightarrow f([{\I}+N]^{k})=[f({\I}+N)]^{k}=[{\I}]

\displaystyle\Rightarrow[({\I}+N_{0}+N_{1}X+\dots+N_{r}X^{r})]^{k}=[{\I}].

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal in
C(R_{0})
. By Theorem
7.9
,
we have

[({\I}+N_{0}+N_{1}X+\dots+N_{r}X^{r})]=[{\I}]

in
{\rm NKQ}_{1}^{\lambda}(R_{\mathfrak{m}},\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})
.
Hence by using the local-global principle we conclude

[({\I}+N)]=[{\I}+N_{0}]

in
{\textrm{NK}_{1}}{\GQ}^{\lambda}(R,\Lambda)
, as required.

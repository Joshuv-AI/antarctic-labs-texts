Quantum mechanics on a circle with a finite number of α \alpha -uniformly distributed points


Abstract
In this paper, quantum mechanics on a circle with finite number of α \alpha -uniformly distributed points is discussed. The angle operator and translation operator are defined. Using discrete angle representation, two types of discrete angular momentum operators and Hermitian Hamiltonian on a circle with d d α \alpha -distributed discrete angles are constructed. The energy levels are computed for a free particle on a circle where the wave function is defined in the d d α \alpha -distributed discrete angles.

Introduction

The quantum mechanics on a circle is characterized by the position operator measuring the angle and angular momentum operator playing a role of translation on a circle [1-8]. The angle is real-valued and it is restricted in
[0,2\pi)
from the periodic properties.

Schwinger [9] presented a most interesting model where the position space
and the momentum space consist of finite number of points and are periodic. This was referred to as the quantum mechanics in a finite Hilbert space [9-12].

The idea of finite Hilbert space was adopted in constructing the quantum mechanics on a circle [1-8] with a finite number of points, where the discrete angle was introduced for describing the position of a particle on a circle and they are uniformly distributed on a circle. Thus, the angular momentum is given by the finite difference with respect to discrete angles.
The finite quantum mechanics on a circle becomes the continuous quantum mechanics on a circle from the continuous limit. Then, the finite difference with respect to discrete angles becomes the derivative with respect to a continuous angle in this limit.

Bonatsos et al. [13] have considered the position and momentum operators for the
q
-deformed oscillator with
q
being a root of unity. They have shown that the phase space of this oscillator has a lattice structure, which is a
non-uniformly distributed grid.

In this paper we consider another type of the non-uniformly distributed grid, where we consider the discrete angles are described in terms of the
\alpha
-addition [14]. Using these discrete angles, we construct the quantum mechanics on a circle with a finite number of
\alpha
-uniformly distributed points.

2
\alpha
-uniformly distributed angles on a circle

Now let us consider the quantum mechanics on a circle with finite points. We
consider the case that
d
points

\{\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{d-1}\}

(1)

on a circle are allowed and they are not uniformly distributed. Here we set
\theta_{0}=0
and

\theta_{d}\equiv\theta_{0}~~~(mod~2\pi)

(2)

and

\theta_{0}<\theta_{1}<\cdots<\theta_{d-1}<2\pi

(3)

In this paper we consider the discrete angles are given in terms of the deformed addition called
\alpha
-addition,

\theta_{n+1}\ominus_{\alpha}\theta_{n}=\sigma,~~~or~~~\theta_{n+1}=\theta_{n}\oplus_{\alpha}\sigma,~~~\sigma>0,

(4)

where
\alpha
-addition and
\alpha
-subtraction [6] are defined as

\displaystyle a\oplus_{\alpha}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a|a|^{\alpha-1}+b|b|^{\alpha-1}|^{1/\alpha-1}(a|a|^{\alpha-1}+b|b|^{\alpha-1})

(5)

\displaystyle a\ominus_{\alpha}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a|a|^{\alpha-1}-b|b|^{\alpha-1}|^{1/\alpha-1}(a|a|^{\alpha-1}-b|b|^{\alpha-1}),

(6)

with

a\otimes_{\alpha}b=a\,b
and

a\oslash_{\alpha}b=\frac{a}{b}
(
\alpha
-product and
\alpha
-division ). Solving Eq.(4), we get

\theta_{n}=n^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sigma,~~~~n=0,1,\cdots,d-1

(7)

If we demand that
d
-th position is the same as the
0
-th position we have

\theta_{d}=d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sigma=2\pi,

(8)

which gives

\sigma=\frac{2\pi}{d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}

(9)

Thus, the relation (4) is replaced with

\theta_{n+1}\ominus_{\alpha}\theta_{n}=\frac{2\pi}{d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}},

(10)

and

\theta_{n}=2\pi\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

(11)

In the limit
d\rightarrow\infty
, the lattice
\{\theta_{n}|n=0,1,\cdots,d-1\}
becomes the semi open interval
0\leq\theta<2\pi
.

3
Angle operator, translation operator and discrete angle representation

Now let us discuss a finite quantum mechanics on a circle
d

\alpha
-distributed discrete angles.
The Hermitian angle operator
\Theta
acts on the angle eigenvectors denoted by
|n\rangle,n=0,1,2,\cdots,d-1
such
that

\Theta|n\rangle=\theta_{n}|n\rangle=2\pi\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|n\rangle

(12)

We know that Eq.(12) is not well-defined because the operator
\Theta
is defined modulo
2\pi
. Thus, we introduce a new operator defined as

V=e_{\alpha}(i\xi\Theta),

(13)

where
\xi>0
is real and will be fixed later, and the
\alpha
-exponential [15] is defined as

e_{\alpha}(z)=e^{|z|^{\alpha-1}z},

(14)

with
-\infty<z<+\infty
. The
\alpha
-exponential obeys [15]

e_{\alpha}(z\oplus_{\alpha}w)=e_{\alpha}(z)e_{\alpha}(w)

(15)

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}e_{\alpha}(z\ominus_{\alpha}w)=e_{\alpha}(z)\oslash_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}(w)}

Acting the operator
V
on
|n\rangle
, we get

V|n\rangle=e_{\alpha}(i\xi\theta_{n})|n\rangle=e^{i\xi^{\alpha}\theta_{n}^{\alpha}}|n\rangle=e^{i\xi^{\alpha}(2\pi)^{\alpha}\frac{n}{d}}|n\rangle

(16)

From
V|0\rangle=|0\rangle
and
|d\rangle\equiv|0\rangle
, we have

e^{i\xi^{\alpha}(2\pi)^{\alpha}}=1,

(17)

which gives

\xi=(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}

(18)

Thus, the operator
V
is written as

V=e_{\alpha}\left[i(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\Theta\right],

(19)

where we have

V^{{\dagger}}=V^{-1}

(20)

and

V^{d}=I

(21)

Now let us introduce a unitary translation operator
U
and its Hermitian adjoint as

U|n\rangle=|n-1\rangle,

(22)

and

U^{{\dagger}}|n\rangle=|n+1\rangle,

(23)

where we assume

|n+d\rangle:=|n\rangle

(24)

Acting
V
on
U^{{\dagger}}|n\rangle
, we get

V(U^{{\dagger}}|n\rangle)=V|n+1\rangle=e_{\alpha}\left[i(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\theta_{n+1}\right]|n+1\rangle

(25)

Acting
U^{{\dagger}}|n\rangle
on
V
, we get

U^{{\dagger}}(V|n\rangle)=e_{\alpha}\left[i(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\theta_{n}\right]U^{{\dagger}}|n\rangle=e_{\alpha}\left[i(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\theta_{n}\right]|n+1\rangle

(26)

Using Eq.(10), we have

VU^{{\dagger}}=qU^{{\dagger}}V,

(27)

where

q=e_{\alpha}\left[i(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\sigma\right]=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{d}}

(28)

Similarly, we have

VU=q^{-1}UV

(29)

and

U^{d}=(U^{{\dagger}})^{d}=1

(30)

The commutation relation between the angle operator and unitary translation operator is given by

\Theta U\ominus_{\alpha}U\Theta=-\sigma U

(31)

and

\Theta U^{{\dagger}}\ominus_{\alpha}U^{{\dagger}}\Theta=\sigma U^{{\dagger}}

(32)

Besides, for
r>0
, we have

\Theta(U^{{\dagger}})^{r}=(U^{{\dagger}})^{r}(X\oplus_{\alpha}r^{1/\alpha}\sigma)

(33)

and

\Theta U^{r}=U^{r}(X\ominus_{\alpha}r^{1/\alpha}\sigma)

(34)

Now let us discrete angle representation of the quantum state
|\psi\rangle
as

\langle n|\psi\rangle=\psi(\theta_{n})

(35)

By considering the Eq. (22) and the unitary of U, we obtain

\langle n|U^{-1}=\langle n-1|

(36)

Multiplying
|\psi\rangle
from the right we get

\langle n|U^{-1}|\psi\rangle=\langle n-1|\psi\rangle,

(37)

which gives

U^{-1}\psi(\theta_{n})=\psi(\theta_{n-1})=\psi(\theta_{n}\ominus_{\alpha}\sigma)

(38)

Similarly we have

U\psi(\theta_{n})=\psi(\theta_{n+1})=\psi(\theta_{n}\oplus_{\alpha}\sigma)

(39)

In general, for
r>0
, we have

(U^{-1})^{r}\psi(\theta_{n})=\psi(\theta_{n-r})=\psi(\theta_{n}\ominus_{\alpha}r^{1/\alpha}\sigma)

(40)

U^{r}\psi(\theta_{n})=\psi(\theta_{n+r})=\psi(\theta_{n}\oplus_{\alpha}r^{1/\alpha}\sigma).

(41)

4
Discrete angular momentum operator and Hermitian Hamiltonian

Now let us introduce the discrete angular momentum operator. In a discrete theory, we have two types of discrete angular momentum operators,

L_{+}=\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{U-I}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)

(42)

and

L_{-}=\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{I-U^{-1}}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)

(43)

Thus, in the discrete angle representation by substituting Eqs. (38) and (39) in Eq. (42), we have

\displaystyle L_{+}\psi(\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{U}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\psi(\theta_{n})-\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{I}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\psi(\theta_{n})

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\psi(\theta_{n+1})-\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\psi(\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i\sigma^{\alpha}}\left(\psi(\theta_{n+1})-\psi(\theta_{n})\right)

and from Eqs. (38-39) and (43) in the discrete angle representation, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{-}\psi(\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{I}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\psi(\theta_{n})-\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{U^{-1}}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\psi(\theta_{n})

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\psi(\theta_{n})-\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\psi(\theta_{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i\sigma^{\alpha}}\left(\psi(\theta_{n})-\psi(\theta_{n-1})\right)

Here, we know that neither
L_{+}
nor
L_{-}
are not Hermitian. Instead, we have

L_{+}^{{\dagger}}=L_{-}

(46)

From the non-Hermitian discrete angular momentum operator and its conjugate, we can construct the Hermitian Hamiltonian of the form

H=\frac{1}{2mR^{2}}L_{+}L_{-}+V(\Theta),

(47)

where
mR^{2}
denotes an inertia of moment with
R
is a radius of a circle. Also, from Eqs. (42) and (43),
L_{+}L_{-}
can be introduced in the following form

\displaystyle L_{+}L_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{U-I}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\right]\left[\frac{\hbar}{i}\left(\frac{I-U^{-1}}{\sigma^{\alpha}}\right)\right]

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{\sigma^{2\alpha}}\left(U+U^{-1}-2I\right)

Now let us consider a free particle on a circle, whose Hamiltonian by using of Eqs. (47) and (48) is given by

H=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}}(U+U^{-1}-2I)

(49)

As for the time independet Schrodinger equation, the wavefunction of Hamiltonian (49) can be found by applying the translation operators
U
and
U^{-1}
in the discrete angle representation:

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}}[\psi(\theta_{n+1})+\psi(\theta_{n-1})-2\psi(\theta_{n})]=E\psi(\theta_{n})

(50)

Therefore, from Eq. (50) we can get the following form for
(n=2,3,4,...)
:

\displaystyle\psi(\theta_{2})=\left(2-\frac{2mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)\psi(\theta_{1})-\psi(\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\psi(\theta_{3})=\left[\left(2-\frac{2mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{2}-1\right]\psi(\theta_{1})-\left(2-\frac{2mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)\psi(\theta_{0})

(51)

\displaystyle\psi(\theta_{4})=\left(2-\frac{2mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)\left[\left(2-\frac{2mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{2}-2\right]\psi(\theta_{1})-\left[\left(2-\frac{2mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{2}-1\right]\psi(\theta_{0})

where
\psi(\theta_{0})
and
\psi(\theta_{1})
are the initial conditions. Then, by choosing of the following change variable

\displaystyle\cos\xi=\left(1-\frac{mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)\quad,\quad\sin\xi=\sqrt{1-\left(1-\frac{mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{2}}

(52)

and substituting in Eqs. (51), we obtain

\psi(\theta_{n})=\frac{1}{\sin\xi}\left[\psi(\theta_{1})\sin(n\xi)-\psi(\theta_{0})\sin((n-1)\xi)\right],

(53)

or

\psi(\theta_{n})=\left(\frac{\psi(\theta_{1})-\psi(\theta_{0})\cos\xi}{\sin\xi}\right)\sin(n\xi)+\psi(\theta_{0})\cos(n\xi),

(54)

where from Eq. (52), we obtain

\xi=\tan^{-1}\left[\frac{\sqrt{1-\left(1-\frac{mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{2}}}{1-\frac{mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}E}{\hbar^{2}}}\right].

(55)

Thus, the energy is given by

E=\frac{(\hbar d)^{2}}{mR^{2}(2\pi)^{2\alpha}}(1-\cos\xi),

(56)

which gives the energy bound,

0\leq E\leq\frac{2\hbar^{2}}{mR^{2}\sigma^{2\alpha}}=\frac{2(\hbar d)^{2}}{mR^{2}(2\pi)^{2\alpha}}

(57)

Thus, the energy is bounded from above in the discrete theory. In the continuum limit, the energy is not bounded from above.
From the cyclic property
\psi(\theta_{0})=\psi(\theta_{d})
, we have

(\psi(\theta_{1})-\psi(\theta_{0})\cos\xi)\sin(d\xi)+\psi(\theta_{0})\sin\xi\cos(d\xi)=\psi(\theta_{0})\sin\xi

(58)

The quantization rule depend on the value of
d\xi
.

4.1
Case of
d\xi=2N\pi,~N\in\mathbb{Z}

In this case we have the energy level

E_{N}=\frac{\hbar^{2}d^{2}}{mR^{2}(2\pi)^{2\alpha}}\left(1-\cos\frac{2N\pi}{d}\right),~~~N\in\mathbb{Z}

(59)

and corresponding wave function is

\psi_{N}(\theta_{n})=\left(\frac{\psi(\theta_{1})-\psi(\theta_{0})\cos\left(\frac{2N\pi}{d}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{2N\pi}{d}\right)}\right)\sin\left(\frac{2Nn\pi}{d}\right)+\psi(\theta_{0})\cos\left(\frac{2Nn\pi}{d}\right).

(60)

4.2
Case of
d\xi=2N\pi+\frac{\pi}{2},~N\in\mathbb{Z}

In this case we have

\psi(\theta_{1})=\psi(\theta_{0})(\cos\xi+\sin\xi)

(61)

Then, we have the energy level

E_{N}=\frac{\hbar^{2}d^{2}}{mR^{2}(2\pi)^{2\alpha}}\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{d}\left(2N+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\right],~N\in\mathbb{Z}

(62)

and corresponding wave function is

\psi_{N}(\theta_{n})=\sqrt{2}\psi(\theta_{0})\sin\left[\frac{n\pi}{d}\left(2N+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{\pi}{4}\right].

(63)

4.3
Case of
d\xi=2N\pi+\pi,~N\in\mathbb{Z}

In this case we have
\psi(\theta_{0})=0
.
Then, we have the energy level

E_{N}=\frac{\hbar^{2}d^{2}}{mR^{2}(2\pi)^{2\alpha}}\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{d}\left(2N+1\right)\right)\right],~N\in\mathbb{Z}

(64)

and corresponding wave function is

\psi_{N}(\theta_{n})=\psi(\theta_{0})\left[\frac{\sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{d}\left(2N+1\right)\right)}{\sin\frac{\pi}{d}(2N+1)}\right].

(65)

4.4
Case of
d\xi=2N\pi+\frac{3}{2}\pi,~N\in\mathbb{Z}

In this case we have

\psi(\theta_{1})=\psi(\theta_{0})(\cos\xi-\sin\xi)

(66)

Then, we have the energy level

E_{N}=\frac{\hbar^{2}d^{2}}{mR^{2}(2\pi)^{2\alpha}}\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{d}\left(2N+\frac{3}{2}\right)\right)\right],~N\in\mathbb{Z}

(67)

and corresponding wave function is

\psi_{N}(\theta_{n})=\sqrt{2}\psi(\theta_{0})\cos\left[\frac{n\pi}{d}\left(2N+\frac{3}{2}\right)+\frac{\pi}{4}\right].

(68)

5
Conclusion

In this paper we
considered the non-uniformly ( more precisely
\alpha
-uniformly) distributed grid where we consider the discrete angles are described in terms of the
\alpha
-addition. Here,
the discrete angles
\theta_{n}
were assumed to obey the condition
that the
\alpha
-difference between two adjacent discrete angles are constant.
With these discrete angles, we defined the angle operator and translation operator. We also discussed the discrete angle representation for the wave function.
Using these, we constructed two types of discrete angular momentum operators and Hermitian Hamiltonian on a circle with
d

\alpha
-distributed discrete angles.
As an example we computed the energy levels
for the free particle on a circle where the wave function is defined in the
d

\alpha
-distributed discrete angles. We found that the energy
is bounded from above in the discrete theory.

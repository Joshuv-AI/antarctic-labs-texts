A note on the eigenvalues of zero divisor graphs associated with commutative rings
Bilal Ahmad Rather

Abstract




Abstract
.
For a commutative ring
\displaystyle R,
with non-zero zero divisors
\displaystyle Z^{\ast}(R)
. The zero divisor graph
\displaystyle\Gamma(R)
is a simple graph with vertex set
\displaystyle Z^{\ast}(R)
, and two distinct vertices
\displaystyle x,y\in V(\Gamma(R))
are adjacent if and only if
\displaystyle x\cdot y=0.
In this note, we provide counter examples to the eigenvalues, the energy and the second Zagreb index related to zero divisor graphs of rings obtained in [Johnson and Sankar, J. Appl. Math. Comp. (2023),
[
16
]
]. We correct the eigenvalues (energy) and the Zagreb index result for the zero divisor graphs of ring
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{4}\rangle.
We show that for any prime
\displaystyle p
,
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{4}\rangle)
is non-hyperenergetic and for prime
\displaystyle p\geq 3
,
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{4}\rangle)
is hypoenergetic. We give a formulae for the topological indices of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{4}\rangle)
and show that its Zagreb indices satisfy Hansen and Vuki
\displaystyle\check{c}
cević conjecture
[
15
]
.

Keywords: Adjacency matrix; Commutative rings; Zero divisor graphs; Energy; Topological indices.

AMS subject classification: 05C50, 05C25, 15A18, 13A70.

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle v.

degree

\displaystyle d_{i}
(or
\displaystyle d_{v_{i}}

\displaystyle v_{i}
. An

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle d_{i}

\displaystyle i.
The

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle v_{i}\sim v_{j}

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(G)
’s is its

The

\displaystyle G

The energy

Eigenvalues and energy of zero divisor graphs

For the sake of completeness and the typos in the construction of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{4}\rangle),
we discuss its structural process.
For a prime
\displaystyle p
, the structure of zero divisor graph
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{4}\rangle)
(see,
[
16
]
) of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]/\langle x^{4}\rangle
can be constructed as given below:

We partition the vertex set
\displaystyle V(G)
of
\displaystyle G
into following sets

\displaystyle\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Big\{kx^{3}|k=1,2,\dots,(p-1)~\text{and}~p\nmid k\Big\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Big\{lx^{3}+lx^{2}|l=1,2,\dots,(p-1)~\text{and}~p\nmid l\Big\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Big\{mx^{3}+lx^{2}+kx|m=1,2,\dots,(p-1)~\text{and}~p\nmid m\Big\}.

The elements of
\displaystyle A
are
\displaystyle\Big\{x^{3},2x^{3},3x^{3},\dots,(p-2)x^{3},(p-1)x^{3}\Big\}
and its cardinality is
\displaystyle|A|=p-1.
Similarly,
\displaystyle|B|=p(p-1)
and that of
\displaystyle C
is
\displaystyle p(p^{2}-p).
Further, for arbitrary
\displaystyle k_{i}x^{3}
and
\displaystyle k_{j}x^{3}
in
\displaystyle A,
we have

(k_{i}x^{3})\cdot(k_{j}x^{3})=k_{i}k_{j}x^{6}\equiv 0\mod x^{4}.

Thus, it follows that each element of
\displaystyle A
is adjacent to the remaining elements contained inside
\displaystyle A.
Again for any two elements say
\displaystyle l_{i}x^{3}+k_{j}x^{2}
and
\displaystyle l_{j}x^{3}+k_{i}x^{2}
in
\displaystyle B,
we have

(l_{i}x^{3}+k_{j}x^{2})\cdot l_{j}x^{3}+k_{i}x^{2}=l_{i}l_{j}x^{6}+(l_{i}k_{i}+l_{j}k_{j})x^{5}+(k_{i}k_{j})x^{4}\equiv 0\mod x^{4}.

It implies that each vertex in
\displaystyle B
is adjacent to every other vertex of
\displaystyle B,
that is, the vertices of
\displaystyle B
form a clique of size
\displaystyle p^{2}-p
. Again for
\displaystyle m_{i}x^{3}+l_{i}x^{2}+k_{i}x\in C
and
\displaystyle m_{j}x^{3}+l_{j}x^{2}+k_{j}x\in C,
then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle(m_{i}x^{3}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+l_{i}x^{2}+k_{i}x)\cdot(m_{j}x^{3}+l_{j}x^{2}+k_{j}x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=m_{i}m_{j}x^{6}+(l_{i}m_{j}+l_{j}m_{i})x^{5}+(k_{i}m_{j}+l_{i}l_{j}+m_{i}k_{j})x^{4}+(k_{i}l_{j}+l_{i}k_{j})x^{3}+k_{i}k_{j}x^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\not\equiv 0\mod x^{4}.

Thus, the vertices in
\displaystyle C
are not adjacent to themselves, so they form an independent set of cardinality
\displaystyle p^{3}-p^{2}.
Again, for
\displaystyle k_{i}x^{3}\in A
and
\displaystyle l_{i}x^{3}+k_{j}x^{2}\in B,
we see that

(k_{i}x^{3})\cdot(l_{i}x^{3}+k_{j}x^{2})=l_{i}k_{i}x^{6}+k_{i}k_{j}x^{5}\equiv 0\mod x^{4},

so it implies that each vertex of
\displaystyle A
is adjacent to every vertex in
\displaystyle B
. Likewise for any
\displaystyle k_{i}x^{3}\in A
and
\displaystyle m_{i}x_{3}+l_{i}x_{2}+k_{j}x\in C,
we see that

(k_{i}x^{3})\cdot(m_{i}x_{3}+l_{i}x_{2}+k_{j}x)=m_{i}k_{i}x^{6}+l_{i}k_{i}x^{5}+k_{i}k_{j}x^{4}\equiv 0\mod x^{4}.

So each vertex in
\displaystyle A
is connected to every vertex in
\displaystyle C.
Lastly, if
\displaystyle l_{i}x^{3}+k_{i}x^{2}\in B
and
\displaystyle m_{j}x_{3}+l_{j}x_{2}+k_{j}x\in C,
then

(l_{i}x^{3}+k_{i}x^{2})\cdot(m_{j}x_{3}+l_{j}x_{2}+k_{j}x)=l_{i}m_{j}x^{6}+(k_{i}m_{j}+l_{i}l_{j})x^{5}+(k_{i}l_{j}+l_{i}k_{j})x^{4}+k_{i}k^{j}x^{3}\not\equiv 0\mod x^{4}.

Thus, t follows that the vertices in
\displaystyle B
are not adjacent to any vertex in
\displaystyle C
(while authors in

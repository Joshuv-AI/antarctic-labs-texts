Decay of quantum conditional mutual information for purely generated finitely correlated states
Pavel Svetlichnyy, T.A.B. Kennedy

Abstract
The connection between quantum state recovery and quantum conditional mutual information (QCMI) is studied for the class of purely generated finitely correlated states (pgFCS) of one-dimensional quantum spin chains. For a tripartition of the chain into two subsystems separated by a buffer region, it is shown that a pgFCS is an approximate quantum Markov chain, and stronger, may be approximated by a quantum Markov chain in trace distance, with an error exponentially small in the buffer size. This implies that, (1) a locally corrupted state can be approximately recovered by action of a quantum channel on the buffer system, and (2) QCMI is exponentially small in the size of the buffer region. Bounds on the exponential decay rate of QCMI and examples of quantum recovery channels are presented.

Proof.

III.3
Finitely correlated states

FCS are a special class of translationally invariant quantum states on a chain of identical finite dimensional quantum systems (spins). The structure of FCS was characterized in Ref.
Fannes et al. 1992
, and is summarized here in the form convenient for our purposes. We refer the reader to the original papers Refs.
Fannes et al. 1992
;
Fannes et al. 1994
for the unabridged treatment of FCS.

A FCS can be described by the pair of a full-rank density operator
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})
and a quantum channel
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})
, satisfying a compatibility condition
\mathrm{tr}_{s}\Phi(\sigma)=\sigma
.
Fannes et al. 1992
Here
\mathcal{H}_{M}
and
\mathcal{H}_{s}
are Hilbert spaces with dimensions
d_{M}
and
d_{s}
, respectively. The space
\mathcal{H}_{M}
is referred to as the
memory space
, and
\mathcal{H}_{s}
is the Hilbert space of a single spin. The FCS reduced density operator for a continuous region
R
with
|R|
spins, is generated by
\Phi
and
\sigma
as

\rho_{R}=\mbox{tr}_{M}(\Phi^{|R|}(\sigma)).

(8)

Here
\mathrm{tr}_{M}
is a partial trace over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{M}
; it should not be confused with the trace over the spins in the chain outside the region
R
. Colloquially, each
\Phi
generates a single spin, and a composition of
\Phi
generates consecutive spins in the chain. Note that the pair of
\Phi
and
\sigma
generating
\rho_{R}
is not necessarily unique, and using the results of Refs.
Fannes et al. 1992
;
Fannes et al. 1994
we may choose the most convenient representation for our purposes. In this subsection we list all the relevant properties of the representation we choose.

For the FCS
(\Phi,\sigma)
, we define the induced quantum channel
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})
by

\mathcal{E}(X)=\mathrm{tr}_{s}\Phi(X),

(9)

for which
\sigma
is a fixed point,
\mathcal{E}(\sigma)=\sigma
.

III.3.1
Ergodic FCS

We consider a subcollection of FCS, called
ergodic
FCS. By definition, an ergodic state is extremal in the convex set of translationally invariant states. However, in the context of this paper, two other equivalent definitions (see Proposition 3.1 of Ref.
Fannes et al. 1992
and Lemma 4.1 of Ref.
Evans and Høegh-Krohn 1978

of
\mathcal{E}
is non-degenerate; (ii) the state for which
\mathcal{E}
is

\tilde{\Pi}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}

(Corollary 3.2 of Ref.
Fannes et al. 1992

Evans and Høegh-Krohn 1978
applied to
\mathcal{E}
and
\sigma
. The items 2 and 3 are obtained using the same reasoning as in the proof of Proposition 3.3 of Ref.
Fannes et al. 1992
. The item 4 follows from Lemma 4.1 of Ref.
Evans and Høegh-Krohn 1978
and item 2.

Proposition 1
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that a FCS is generated by a pair
(\Phi,\sigma)
, with the induced quantum channel
\mathcal{E}
, for which the following properties hold

1.

There exist
J
orthogonal projectors
\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{M}
,
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{1}_{M}
, such that
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})\subseteq\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
. The restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to
\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
is irreducible.

2.

The density operator
\sigma
is block-diagonal with respect to the decomposition
\mathcal{H}_{M}=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{M}
, i.e.,

\sigma=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\sigma\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
.

3.

\Phi(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s})\otimes\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
.

4.

The restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to
\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
has a non-degenerate eigenvalue equal to 1, corresponding to the fixed point
\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\sigma\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
.

The decomposition of FCS into ergodic components follows from items 2 and 3 of Proposition
1
,

\rho_{R}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}\lambda_{\alpha}\rho^{\alpha}_{R},

(10)

where
\rho^{\alpha}_{R}=\mathrm{tr}_{M}\Phi^{|R|}(\sigma_{\alpha})

\sigma_{\alpha}=\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\sigma\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}/\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\sigma\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})
, and
\lambda_{\alpha}=\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\sigma\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})

The structure of ergodic components can be analyzed further. We summarize their properties in the proposition below, which is a restatement of Proposition 3.3 of Ref.
Fannes et al. 1992
in the language of density operators

Proposition 2

(Proposition 3.3, Ref.
Fannes et al. 1992
)
.

For an ergodic FCS, generated by
(\Phi,\sigma)
, with the induced quantum channel
\mathcal{E}
, we may assume that the following properties hold

1.

The peripheral spectrum of

consists of

2.

There exist

The density operator

The restriction of

\Pi_{k}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\Pi_{k}

Quantum Markov chains are defined as those states for which QCMI vanishes,

Let
\rho_{ABC}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{C})

1.

\rho_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov chain,
i.e.
,
I(A:C|B)=0
.

2.

There exists a quantum channel
\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}_{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{C})

\rho_{ABC}=\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(\rho_{AB})

although this choice is not unique.

3.

There is a decomposition
\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{B})\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathcal{K}_{b^{l}_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{b^{r}_{k}}
,
i.e.

\rho_{b^{r}_{k}C}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}_{b^{r}_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{C})

\mathcal{H}_{A}
, and use it to construct the state

\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}=\mbox{tr}_{M}\left(V_{C}\tilde{V}_{B}V_{A}\sigma V_{A}^{\dagger}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\dagger}V_{C}^{\dagger}\right).

(15)

Note that the isometry
\tilde{V}_{B}
generates all the spins in the region
B
, but unlike
V_{B}
, it is not a concatenation of isometric factors generating one spin at a time. It is a key step to choose
\tilde{V}_{B}
to be of a particular form that ensures the state
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
is simultaneously close (in trace norm distance) to
\rho_{ABC}
and is an exact quantum Markov chain.

The trace distance
\|\rho_{ABC}-\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}\|_{1}
is bounded by twice the error in approximating
V_{B}
by
\tilde{V}_{B}
in operator norm,

\displaystyle\|\rho_{ABC}-\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\mbox{tr}_{M}\left(V_{C}V_{B}V_{A}\sigma V_{A}^{\dagger}V_{B}^{\dagger}V_{C}^{\dagger}\right)

(16)

\displaystyle\quad-\mbox{tr}_{M}\left(V_{C}\tilde{V}_{B}V_{A}\sigma V_{A}^{\dagger}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\dagger}V_{C}^{\dagger}\right)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|V_{B}V_{A}\sigma V_{A}^{\dagger}V_{B}^{\dagger}-\tilde{V}_{B}V_{A}\sigma V_{A}^{\dagger}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\dagger}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|,

where in the last line we have used the inequalities
\|XY\|_{1}\leq\|X\|\|Y\|_{1}
,
\|XZX^{\dagger}-YZY^{\dagger}\|_{1}\leq 2\|X-Y\|\|Z\|_{1}
, and the fact that
\|V_{A}\sigma V_{A}^{\dagger}\|_{1}=1
.

For
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
a quantum Markov chain, according to condition 2 of Theorem
III.5
there exists a recovery channel
\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}
, such that
\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(\tilde{\rho}_{AB})=\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
. We may select
\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}
to be the Petz recovery map, defined in statement 2 of Theorem
III.5
, or one of the alternatives that we discuss in Section
V
. If we use such an
\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}
to approximately recover
\rho_{ABC}
from
\rho_{AB}
, then the recovery error is given by

\displaystyle\|\rho_{ABC}-\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)\|_{1}=\|\rho_{ABC}-\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{C}\left(\rho_{ABC}\right)\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\rho_{ABC}-\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}+\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{C}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}-\rho_{ABC}\right)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{ABC}-\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}\|_{1}+\|\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{C}\left(\rho_{ABC}-\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}\right)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\rho_{ABC}-\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}\|_{1}

(17)

where we have used the quantum Markov property, the triangle inequality, and the property that the trace norm is non-increasing under actions of quantum channels.

Combining (
16
) and (
IV
), we obtain a bound on the recovery error,

\|\rho_{ABC}-\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)\|_{1}\leq 4\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|.

(18)

Our goal is to choose the isometry
\tilde{V}_{B}
so that
\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|
is exponentially small in the size of the region
B
. Consider the
|B|
-fold composition of
\mathcal{E}
, defined in (
11
),
\mathcal{E}^{|B|}
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is dilated by the elementary isometry

|B|

, that is

, dilates
\mathcal{E}^{|B|}
.
From
\mathcal{E}
we construct the quantum channel
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
via Definition
1
, and then approximate
\mathcal{E}^{|B|}
by
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}
, the
|B|
-fold composition of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
. As discussed in Section
III.2
, the maximum dimension of the dilating space of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}
is equal to
\dim\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})=d_{M}^{2}
. We will assume that
\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}\geq d_{M}^{2}
, so that the dilation space of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}
can be embedded into
\mathcal{H}_{B}
. Under this condition, we define
\tilde{V}_{B}
to be an isometry dilating
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}
. Note that any isometry of the form
U_{B}\tilde{V}_{B}
, where
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}
is unitary, will also be a dilating isometry for
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}
. We will use this freedom of choice to minimize

.

By the continuity of Stinespring’s dilation,
Kretschmann et al. 2008
we can relate the operator norm distance between

and
\tilde{V}_{B}
to the distance between channels

and

\inf_{U_{B}}\|V_{B}-U_{B}\tilde{V}_{B}\|^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}\|_{\diamond}\leq 2\inf_{U_{B}}\|V_{B}-U_{B}\tilde{V}_{B}\|,

(19)

where the infimum is taken over the set of all unitaries
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}.
(Recall that the diamond norm is defined by
\|\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}\|_{\diamond}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\mbox{id}_{n}\otimes(\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|})\|_{1\rightarrow 1}
.
Kitaev et al. 2002
) As the dimension of

\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|\leq\sqrt{\|\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}\|_{\diamond}}.

(20)

To bound the diamond norm

\|X\|_{2}:=\sqrt{\mbox{tr}(X^{\dagger}X)}

is finite dimensional, the supremum
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\mbox{id}_{n}\otimes(\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|})\|_{1\rightarrow 1}

Kitaev et al. 2002
hence

\|X\|_{1}\leq\mathrm{rank}(X)^{1/2}\|X\|_{2}
and
\|\mbox{id}_{n}\otimes(\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|})\|_{2\rightarrow 2}=\|\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}\|_{2\rightarrow 2}
(tensoring with the identity map does not change the

\|\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}\|_{\diamond}\leq d_{M}\|\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}\|_{2\rightarrow 2}.

(21)

From Lemma

and

\|\mathcal{E}^{|B|}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}\|_{2\rightarrow 2}\leq c\nu^{|B|},

, and

\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|\leq\sqrt{d_{M}c}\;\nu^{|B|/2},

(23)

and, from (

\displaystyle\|\rho_{ABC}-\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{d_{M}c}\ \ \nu^{|B|/2}

(24)

\displaystyle\|\rho_{ABC}-\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{d_{M}c}\ \ \nu^{|B|/2}.

Observe that statements
1
and
2

II.1
hold with the constants
K=4\sqrt{d_{M}c}
and
q=\ln\left(\nu^{-1}\right)/2
. The bound (

IV.1
Ergodic pgFCS of period 1

We first prove that
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov chain for an ergodic pgFCS with
p=1.

Since
\mathcal{E}
has a trivial peripheral spectrum and
\sigma
is its fixed point, then
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
constructed according to Definition
1
sends any input into the state proportional to
\sigma
,
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(X)=\mathrm{tr}(X)\sigma
. It follows that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{n}=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
, in particular
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
. We observe that the isometry
\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{M}}\sqrt{\sigma_{i}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|
is a dilation of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
, where we have introduced an arbitrary orthonormal set of vectors
\{|\xi_{ij}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{B}\;|\;i,j=1\cdot\cdot\cdot d_{M}\}
. The vectors
|i\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{M}
and
\sigma_{i}>0
are the eigenvectors and corresponding eigenvalues of
\sigma
, respectively, i.e.,
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{M}}\sigma_{i}|i\rangle\langle i|
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{B}(\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}X\tilde{V}^{\prime\dagger}_{B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\sqrt{\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i^{\prime}}}\langle\xi_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle\langle j|X|j^{\prime}\rangle\,|i\rangle\langle i^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{M}}\langle j|X|j\rangle\,\sum_{i=1}^{d_{M}}\sigma_{i}|i\rangle\langle i|

\displaystyle=\mbox{tr}(X)\sigma.

Since
\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}
dilates
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
, which is also dilated by
\tilde{V}_{B}
, there is unitary
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}
, such that
\tilde{V}_{B}=U_{B}\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}
, and we observe that this amounts to a unitary change of the orthonormal basis
\{|\xi_{ij}\rangle\}
. Thus, considering the freedom of choice of the basis vectors
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\tilde{V}_{B}=\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}
,

\tilde{V}_{B}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{M}}\sqrt{\sigma_{i}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|.

(25)

Substituting (
25
) into (
15
),
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
may be written explicitly,

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}=\sum_{i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime}}\langle j|V_{A}\sigma V_{A}^{\dagger}|j^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\otimes\sqrt{\sigma_{i}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle\langle\xi_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}|\sqrt{\sigma_{i^{\prime}}}

(26)

\displaystyle\otimes\mbox{tr}_{M}\left(V_{C}|i\rangle\langle i^{\prime}|V_{C}^{\dagger}\right).

We will show by direct computation that
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
satisfies the condition 3 of Theorem
III.5
and hence is a quantum Markov chain. We denote
\mathcal{H}_{b}:=\mathrm{span}\{|\xi_{ij}\rangle\;|\;i,j=1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,d_{M}\rangle\}
, and note that since their dimensions are equal,
\mathcal{H}_{b}
and

\mathcal{H}_{M}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
are isomorphic. We construct the unitary isomorphism
I:\mathcal{H}_{b}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{M}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
, defined by
I|\xi_{ij}\rangle=|j\rangle\otimes|i\rangle
. Under this isomorphism, a straightforward calculation gives,

I\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}I^{\dagger}=V_{A}\sigma V^{\dagger}_{A}\otimes\mbox{tr}_{M}\left(V_{C}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}|+\rangle\langle+|\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}V^{\dagger}_{C}\right),

where

is a non-normalized, maximally entangled state vector, and the partial trace
\mathrm{tr}_{M}
is over the second factor. If we write
\mathcal{H}_{b}=\mathcal{H}_{b^{l}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b^{r}}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{b^{l}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{b^{r}}
are isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{M}
, we observe that
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
satisfies condition 3 of Theorem
III.5
with

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{B})\cong\mathcal{H}_{b}=\mathcal{H}_{b^{l}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b^{r}},

\displaystyle I\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}I^{\dagger}=\tilde{\rho}_{Ab^{l}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}C},

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{Ab^{l}}:=V_{A}\sigma V^{\dagger}_{A},

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}C}:=\mbox{tr}_{M}(V_{C}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}|+\rangle\langle+|\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}V^{\dagger}_{C}),

and is thus a quantum Markov chain.

Lemma IV.1
.

, the isometry

Remarks

1.

Explicitly, each
\tilde{V}_{B}^{k}
,

\tilde{V}_{B}^{k}=\sqrt{p}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{O}_{k}}\sqrt{\sigma_{i}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|,

(28)

where

\langle\xi_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle=\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}

2.

\tilde{V}^{k}_{B}X\tilde{V}^{k\dagger}_{B}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B_{k}})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})
,

and
\tilde{V}^{k}_{B}X\tilde{V}^{k\dagger}_{B}+\tilde{V}^{k^{\prime}}_{B}X\tilde{V}^{k^{\prime}\dagger}_{B}\in\left(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B_{k}})\oplus\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B_{k^{\prime}}})\right)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})

Proof.

As we show in Appendix
I
, the quantum channel
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
, obtained via Definition
1

with the properties listed in Proposition

, has the form

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(X)=p\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\mbox{tr}\left(\Pi_{k}X\Pi_{k}\right)\Pi_{k+1}\sigma\Pi_{k+1}.

(29)

In
\Pi_{k+k^{\prime}}
the addition in the subscript is interpreted modulo
p
. Then,

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}(X)=p\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\mbox{tr}\left(\Pi_{k}X\Pi_{k}\right)\Pi_{k+|B|}\sigma\Pi_{k+|B|}.

Notice that for each

the map,

\tilde{V}_{B}^{k}:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}

It is easy to check that

is a partial isometry,

\tilde{V}_{B}^{k\dagger}\tilde{V}_{B}^{k}=\sum_{|j\rangle\in\Pi_{k}\mathcal{H}_{M}}|j\rangle\langle j|=\Pi_{k}.

We define the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{B_{k}}:=\mathrm{span}\{|\xi_{ij}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{B}\;|\;(i,j)\in\mathcal{O}_{k}\}
that has the dimension
\dim\mathcal{H}_{B_{k}}=\mathrm{rank}(\Pi_{k})\mathrm{rank}(\Pi_{k+|B|})
.

Now we prove that the assumption
\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}\geq d_{M}^{2}
is consistent with mutual orthogonality of the subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{B_{k}}
. For
\mathcal{H}_{B}
to contain
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{p-1}\mathcal{H}_{B_{k}}
, we require
\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\dim\mathcal{H}_{B_{k}}\leq\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\mathrm{rank}(\Pi_{k})\mathrm{rank}(\Pi_{k+|B|})\leq(\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\mathrm{rank}(\Pi_{k}))^{2}=d^{2}_{M}
, this condition is satisfied.

The channel
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}

, since

\tilde{V}_{B}^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{p-1}\tilde{V}^{k_{1}\dagger}_{B}\tilde{V}^{k_{2}}_{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\tilde{V}^{k\dagger}_{B}\tilde{V}^{k}_{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\Pi_{k}=\mathbb{1}_{B},

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{B}(\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}X\tilde{V}^{{}^{\prime}\dagger}_{B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{p-1}\mathrm{tr}_{B}(\tilde{V}^{k_{1}}_{B}X\tilde{V}^{k_{2}\dagger}_{B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\mathrm{tr}_{B}(\tilde{V}^{k}_{B}X\tilde{V}^{k\dagger}_{B})

\displaystyle=p\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\mbox{tr}\left(\Pi_{k}X\Pi_{k}\right)\Pi_{k+|B|}\sigma\Pi_{k+|B|}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}(X).

We have used mutual orthogonality of
\mathcal{H}_{B_{k}}
in both calculations. Since both
\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B},\tilde{V}_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
dilate
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{B}
, there exists unitary
U_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}
, such that
\tilde{V}_{B}=U_{B}\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}
. Using the same reasoning as in Section
IV.1
we can, without loss of generality, take
\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{B}=\tilde{V}_{B}
,

\tilde{V}_{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\tilde{V}_{B}^{k}=\sqrt{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{O}_{k}}\sqrt{\sigma_{i}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|.

(30)

Notice that the case
p=1
corresponds to a single projector
\Pi_{0}=\mathbb{1}_{M}
, so that (
29
) reduces to
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}(X)=\mathrm{tr}(X)\sigma
, and (
30
) reduces to (
25
).

Comparing (
28
) to (
25
), we realize that the we can use the same arguments as in Section
IV.1
to prove that
p\mathrm{tr}_{M}(V_{C}\tilde{V}_{B}^{k}V_{A}\sigma V_{A}\tilde{V}_{B}^{k\dagger}V_{C}^{\dagger})
is a quantum Markov chain. The normalization constant comes from the observation that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(V_{C}\tilde{V}^{k}_{B}V_{A}\sigma V^{\dagger}_{A}\tilde{V}_{B}^{k\dagger}V_{C}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(\Pi_{k}V_{A}\sigma V^{\dagger}_{A}\Pi_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left(\Pi_{k}\mathcal{E}^{|A|}(\sigma)\Pi_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left(\Pi_{k}\mathcal{E}^{|A|}\left(\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}=0}^{p-1}\Pi_{k^{\prime}}\sigma\Pi_{k^{\prime}}\right)\Pi_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(\Pi_{k}\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}=0}^{p-1}\Pi_{k^{\prime}+|A|}\sigma\Pi_{k^{\prime}+|A|}\Pi_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(\Pi_{k}\sigma\Pi_{k})=\frac{1}{p}.

∎

IV.3
The general case of pgFCS

We generalize the proof to the case of a convex sum of ergodic pgFCS considered in the previous subsection. The properties of pgFCS are given in Proposition
3
, whose notation and definitions we employ below.

We will prove that
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
, as defined in (
15
), is a quantum Markov chain by anticipating the Lemma
IV.2
, which states that the isometry
\tilde{V}_{B}
can be decomposed as a sum of partial isometries corresponding to ergodic components,
\tilde{V}_{B}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}
, where
\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
is a partial isometry with
\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{M}
the orthogonal complement of its kernel, and where
\mathrm{Range}(\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha})=\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}\otimes\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{M}
, with subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{B}
being mutually orthogonal. The projectors
\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
are defined in Proposition
3
, and correspond to different ergodic components of
\rho_{ABC}
. Moreover,
\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}
are such that

\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{ABC}:=\mbox{tr}_{M}\left(V^{\alpha}_{C}\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}V^{\alpha}_{A}\sigma_{\alpha}V_{A}^{\alpha\dagger}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha\dagger}V_{C}^{\alpha\dagger}\right)

separately approximates the corresponding
\rho^{\alpha}_{ABC}
, defined in Proposition
3
. Then
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
may be decomposed,

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}

\displaystyle=\mbox{tr}_{M}\left(V_{C}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}\right)V_{A}\sigma V_{A}^{\dagger}\left(\sum_{\beta=1}^{J}\tilde{V}^{\beta\dagger}_{B}\right)V_{C}^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}\lambda_{\alpha}\mbox{tr}_{M_{\alpha}}\left(V^{\alpha}_{C}\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}V^{\alpha}_{A}\sigma_{\alpha}V_{A}^{\alpha\dagger}\tilde{V}^{\alpha\dagger}_{B}V_{C}^{\alpha\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}\lambda_{\alpha}\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{ABC},

where we have used
V_{C}\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}=V_{C}^{\alpha}\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}
and
\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}V_{A}\sigma V^{\dagger}_{A}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}V^{\alpha}_{A}\sigma_{\alpha}V^{\alpha\dagger}_{A}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\beta}
. By the proof in Section
IV.2
, each
\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov chain, and by Corollary
III.5.1

\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov chain. This is sufficient to complete the proof.

The following lemma shows that
\tilde{V}_{B}
indeed possesses the requisite decomposition. We repeatedly use Proposition
3
in the proof.

Lemma IV.2
.

For a pgFCS, the isometry
\tilde{V}_{B}:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
defined in (
15
) can be constructed as the sum
\tilde{V}_{B}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha}
, where
J
is the number of ergodic components, and

1.

\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha}
is a partial isometry,
\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha\dagger}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha}=\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
, and
\tilde{V}^{\alpha}_{B}\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha}
.

2.

\mathrm{Range}(\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha})=\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}\otimes\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{M}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
. The subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}
are mutually orthogonal.

3.

\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{ABC}=\mathrm{tr}_{M}(V^{\alpha}_{C}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha}V^{\alpha}_{A}\sigma V^{\alpha\dagger}_{A}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha\dagger}V_{C}^{\alpha\dagger})
is a quantum Markov chain.

Proof.

We express
\mathcal{E}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{J}\mathcal{E}\left(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}\right)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{J}\mbox{tr}_{s}\left(V_{\alpha}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}V_{\beta}^{\dagger}\right),

(31)

where we have used
V\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}=V_{\alpha}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
. Since the range of
V_{\alpha}
is
\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{M}
, then
\mbox{tr}_{s}\left(V_{\alpha}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}V_{\beta}^{\dagger}\right)\in\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}
.
We decompose
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
similarly,

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(X)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{J}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}\left(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}\right).

We now show that the last equation reduces to the direct sum,
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(X)=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})
. We observe that the map,
X\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta})
, is obtained via Definition
1
from the map
X\mapsto\mbox{tr}_{s}(V_{\alpha}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}V_{\beta}^{\dagger})
. Recall that for
\alpha\neq\beta
there is no unitary
U:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{M}
and
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
V_{\alpha}=e^{i\phi}UV_{\beta}U^{\dagger}
, which implies by Lemma
E.1
that the eigenvalues of
X\mapsto\mbox{tr}_{s}(V_{\alpha}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}V_{\beta}^{\dagger})
have magnitudes less than
1
. Then, following the construction in Definition
1
, the map
X\mapsto\mbox{tr}_{s}(V_{\alpha}\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta}V_{\beta}^{\dagger})
is identically zero,
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\beta})=0
for
\alpha\neq\beta
. Thus,
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(X)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})
. Since
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})\in\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
, from Definition
1
follows that
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})\in\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
. Since
\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
are orthonormal projectors, the sum is direct,
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(X)=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})
, and

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}(X)=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{|B|}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}).

We define
\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha}:\mathcal{H}_{M_{\alpha}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M_{\alpha}}
to be an isometric dilation of
X\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})
. Here
\mathcal{H}_{M_{\alpha}}:=\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{M}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}
is the dilation space embedded in
\mathcal{H}_{B}
of dimension
\dim\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}\leq(\dim\mathcal{H}_{M_{\alpha}})^{2}=d^{2}_{M_{\alpha}}
. It immediately follows that
\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha\dagger}\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha}=\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}
, proving the statement 1. From property 4 of Proposition
3
we know that
(V_{\alpha},\sigma_{\alpha})
generates an ergodic pgFCS with the induced quantum channel
X\mapsto\mathcal{E}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})
. As mentioned before,
X\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})
is exactly the map obtained via Definition
1
from the map
X\mapsto\mathcal{E}(\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha}X\tilde{\Pi}_{\alpha})
. Then, separately for each
\alpha
, we can repeat the reasoning of Section
IV.2
, showing that each
\tilde{V}_{B}^{\alpha}
, the dilating isometry of

, possesses the properties which ensure that
\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov chain, proving the statement 3.

Now we show that we can safely choose the subspaces

\mathcal{H}_{M}=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}\mathcal{H}_{M_{\alpha}}
, and since we assume

, then
\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}\geq(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}d_{M_{\alpha}})^{2}\geq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}d^{2}_{M_{\alpha}}\geq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{J}\dim\mathcal{H}_{B_{\alpha}}

V
Recovery maps

In this section we consider the recovery maps that can be used to restore
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
, defined in (
15
), from
\tilde{\rho}_{AB}
. The obvious choice is the Petz recovery map, defined on
\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{\rho}_{B}))
in (
2
),
\mathcal{P}_{B\rightarrow BC}(X)=\tilde{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{B}X\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{B}\tilde{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and which can be extended by a trivial embedding
\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{\rho}_{B})^{\perp}\rightarrow\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{\rho}_{B})^{\perp}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}
to the map
\mathcal{P}_{B\rightarrow BC}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{C})
.
Petz 1986
;
Petz 1988
;
Hayden et al. 2004
The Petz recovery map can be defined for any state that is a quantum Markov chain.

The Petz recovery map is, however, not the only map that can exactly reconstruct a quantum Markov chain
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
. From Theorem
III.5
, we note that there is another obvious choice of the recovery channel, which relies on the isomorphism
I:\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{\rho}_{B})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{k=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathcal{H}_{b^{l}_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b^{r}_{k}}
defined in Theorem
III.5
, and which provides the decomposition
I\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}I^{\dagger}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\lambda_{k}\tilde{\rho}_{Ab^{l}_{k}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}_{k}C}
. We gave an example of such an isomorphism in Section
IV.1
for the case of an ergodic pgFCS of period
1
.
Using
I
, for a general quantum Markov chain, we can construct the map
\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C})
,

\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(X):=I^{\dagger}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathrm{tr}_{b^{r}_{k}}\left(\mathbb{P}_{k}(IXI^{\dagger})\mathbb{P}_{k}\right)\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}_{k}C}I,

where
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is a projector onto
\mathcal{H}_{b^{l}_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b^{r}_{k}}
. We verify that this map is indeed a recovery channel by direct computation,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(\tilde{\rho}_{AB})=I^{\dagger}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathrm{tr}_{b^{r}_{k}}\left(\mathbb{P}_{k}(I\tilde{\rho}_{AB}I^{\dagger})\mathbb{P}_{k}\right)\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}_{k}C}I

\displaystyle=I^{\dagger}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathrm{tr}_{b^{r}_{k}}(\mathbb{P}_{k}\left(\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\lambda_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\rho}_{Ab^{l}_{k^{\prime}}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}_{k^{\prime}}}\right)\mathbb{P}_{k})\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}_{k}C}I

\displaystyle=I^{\dagger}\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\lambda_{k^{\prime}}\mathrm{tr}_{b^{r}_{k^{\prime}}}(\tilde{\rho}_{Ab^{l}_{k^{\prime}}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}_{k^{\prime}}})\otimes\rho_{b^{r}_{k}C}I

\displaystyle=I^{\dagger}\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k_{\mathrm{max}}}\lambda_{k^{\prime}}\tilde{\rho}_{Ab^{l}_{k^{\prime}}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{b^{r}_{k^{\prime}}C}I

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho}_{ABC},

where we have used
I\tilde{\rho}_{AB}I^{\dagger}=I\mathrm{tr}_{C}\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}I^{\dagger}=\mathrm{tr}_{C}(I\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}I^{\dagger})
(recall that
I
acts non-trivially only on
\mathcal{H}_{B}
), the structure of the quantum Markov chain from Theorem
III.5
in the second line, and
\mathbb{P}_{k}\rho_{Ab^{l}_{k^{\prime}}}\otimes\rho_{b^{r}_{k^{\prime}}C}\mathbb{P}_{k}=\delta_{{k^{\prime}}k}\rho_{Ab^{l}_{k^{\prime}}}\otimes\rho_{b^{r}_{k^{\prime}}C}
in the third line.

Unlike the Petz recovery map that is defined in terms of the reduced density operators
\tilde{\rho}_{BC}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{B}
, the recovery map
\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}
requires knowledge of the isomorphism
I
, the structure of which is not obvious in the case of a general quantum Markov chain. In the case of a quantum Markov chain approximating a pgFCS, and possessing the form (
15
), we can construct the isomorphism
I
. For simplicity of argument, we illustrate this claim using the case of ergodic pgFCS of period
1
. Recall that
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
has the form (
15
), and
\tilde{V}_{B}
has the form (
25
),
\tilde{V}_{B}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{M}}\sqrt{\sigma_{i}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|
, where
|i\rangle
and
|j\rangle
are the eigenvectors of
\sigma
, and
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
is an orthonormal basis set. This allows us to express
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
as in (
26
) and define
I
in terms of the orthonormal vectors
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
,
I=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{M}}(|i\rangle\otimes|j\rangle)\langle\xi_{ij}|
. The required set of orthonormal vectors
\{|\xi_{ij}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{B}\;|\;i,j=1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,d_{M}\}
is the solution of the minimization problem for the norm
\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|
over unitary change of
d_{M}^{2}
basis vectors in
\mathcal{H}_{B}
.

Interestingly, we can construct another recovery channel, that has a slightly less optimal bound in terms of the pre-exponential factor, but for which the vectors
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
can be constructed more explicitly. Again we use the case of ergodic pgFCS of period
1
for illustration.

For
\tilde{V}_{B}
in the form (
25
),
any
choice of the
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
induces an exact quantum Markov chain
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}
of the form (
15
). A particular choice of
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
influences the bound on the recovery error. Expressing

\displaystyle\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{M}^{2}}(\langle i|V_{B}|j\rangle-\sqrt{\sigma_{i}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle)\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|\|.

we observe that any choice of
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
close to
\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{i}\langle i|V_{B}|j\rangle
is expected to be a good one. Let us construct the positive semidefinite matrix

Q_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime};ij}:=\langle j^{\prime}|V^{\dagger}_{B}\left(\mathbb{1}_{B}\otimes|i^{\prime}\rangle\langle i|\right)V_{B}|j\rangle,

(32)

which is a Gramian matrix for the set of vectors
\{\langle i|V_{B}|j\rangle\;|\;i,j=1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,d_{M}\}
. For large
|B|

\|\langle i|V_{B}|j\rangle\|

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(X)=\mathrm{tr}(X)\sigma
in the limit

converges to the adjoint of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
, the map
X\mapsto\mathrm{tr}(\sigma X)\mathbb{1}_{M}
. Thus,
Q_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime};ij}
converges to
\langle j^{\prime}|\mbox{tr}_{M}\left(\sigma|i^{\prime}\rangle\langle i|\right)\mathbb{1}_{M}V_{B}|j\rangle=\sigma_{i}\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}
.

Then we define
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
as

|\xi_{ij}\rangle=\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{M}}(Q^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{mn;ij}\langle m|V_{B}|n\rangle.

(33)

(As we show in Appendix
J

is guaranteed to be invertible, if the region
B
is large enough). We verify that the set of
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
is orthonormal,

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}(Q^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime};m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\langle n^{\prime}|V^{\dagger}_{B}|m^{\prime}\rangle\langle m|V_{B}|n\rangle(Q^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{mn;ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}(Q^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime};m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}Q_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime};mn}(Q^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{mn;ij}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}.

In Appendix
J
we show that for this choice of
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
,

\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|\leq\sqrt{2(1+2^{-\frac{11}{2}})c}\;d_{M}\nu^{\frac{|B|}{2}},

(34)

with
c
and
\nu
defined in Lemma
III.3
, leading to the recovery error

\|\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(\rho_{AB})-\rho_{ABC}\|_{1}\leq 4\sqrt{2(1+2^{-\frac{11}{2}})c}\;d_{M}\nu^{\frac{|B|}{2}},

(35)

which is worse than the recovery error (
24
) by the factor
\sqrt{2(1+2^{-\frac{11}{2}})d_{M}}
.

In Appendix
J
we present a similar construction for the general case of pgFCS, which is based on the same idea, but is more delicate.

VI
Conclusion

Quantum conditional mutual information (QCMI) is non-negative by virtue of the strong subadditivity of von Neumann entropy. We have studied the QCMI for a homogeneous quantum spin chain described by a purely generated finitely correlated state (pgFCS)
\rho_{ABC}
. Explicitly, by separating the chain regions A and C by a domain B, and provided the size
|B|
of region B is large enough, we show there exists a quantum Markov chain which differs from
\rho_{ABC}
in trace distance by an error exponentially small in
|B|
. It follows that
\rho_{ABC}
may be recovered, within the stated error, from the reduced density operator
\rho_{AB}
by a quantum channel acting only on the domain B. As a consequence this implies that the QCMI, denoted
I(A:C|B),
is also exponentially small in
|B|.
We have presented quantum channels that, in principle, can perform the state recovery. Besides the obvious choice of the Petz recovery map, we give two other examples derived from the particular structure of the pgFCS. We have carried out numerical experiments on the decay of QCMI, the results of which are consistent with the bound presented in Theorem
II.1
. The details will be reported elsewhere.

A natural next step would be to prove the exponential decay of QCMI for generic FCS, as conjectured in Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
. Both the results presented here and in Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
point in this direction. As discussed further in Appendix
K
, the pgFCS considered here and the class of states considered in Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
are in general distinct.

Generalization of our result to generic FCS seems to require an approach different from the one we used in this paper. It remains an open question whether a FCS exhibiting an exponential decay of QCMI is necessarily close to a quantum Markov chain in trace distance.

Appendix B

In this appendix we derive the right-hand side of the inequality (
3
). In Refs.
Fawzi and Renner 2015
;
Sutter et al. 2016
;
Junge et al. 2018
;
Kim 2017
, the bound is based on the Alicki-Fannes inequality,
Alicki and Fannes 2004
which is the extension of the Fannes inequality.
Fannes 1973
Here we employ an improved version of the Alicki-Fannes inequality from Ref.
Winter 2016
(we refer the reader to this paper for further references on the topic).

In order to apply the results of Ref.
Winter 2016
, we first express the QCMI in terms of
quantum relative entropy
,
D(\rho||\sigma):=\mathrm{tr}\rho(\ln\rho-\ln\sigma)
,

\displaystyle I(A:C|B)

\displaystyle=S(\rho_{BC})-S(\rho_{ABC})+S(\rho_{AB})-S(\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=D(\rho_{ABC}||\rho_{BC})-D(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{B}).

By the monotonicity of quantum relative entropy under the action of quantum channels,
Lindblad 1975
;
Watrous 2018

D(\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(\rho_{AB})||\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(\rho_{B}))\leq D(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{B})
, thus we can estimate

I(A:C|B)\leq D(\rho_{ABC}||\rho_{BC})-D\left(\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(\rho_{AB})||\mathcal{R}_{B\rightarrow BC}(\rho_{B})\right).

To make the result of Ref.
Winter 2016
directly applicable, we recast the above inequality in terms of
conditional entropy
,
S(R_{1}|R_{2})_{\rho}:=S(\rho_{R_{1}R_{2}})-S(\rho_{R_{2}})
(we adopt the notation of Ref.
Winter 2016
). We notice that

Lemma E.1

(Lemma A.2 of Ref.
Cirac et al. 2017
)
.

Let isometries
V_{1}:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
and
V_{2}:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
be such that full-rank density operators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})
are the only fixed points of the quantum channels
X\mapsto\mathrm{tr}_{s}(V_{1}XV^{\dagger}_{1})
and
X\mapsto\mathrm{tr}_{s}(V_{2}XV^{\dagger}_{2})
,
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})
, respectively, and that these quantum channels have no other eigenvalues of magnitude 1. Then the eigenvalues
\nu_{i}
of the map
X\mapsto\mathrm{tr}_{s}(V_{1}XV^{\dagger}_{2})
are such that
|\nu_{i}|\leq 1.
Furthermore, if there exists the eigenvalue
\nu_{i}
of magnitude
1
, then
V_{1}=e^{-i\phi}(\mathbb{1}_{s}\otimes W^{\dagger})V_{2}W
for some
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
and unitary
W:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{M}
.

Proof.

Let
\nu_{i}
be an eigenvalue, so that for some
X
:

\mbox{tr}_{s}(V_{1}XV^{\dagger}_{2})=\nu_{i}X.

Using the fact that
\sigma_{1}
is invertible, consider:

\displaystyle\left|\nu_{i}\mbox{tr}(X^{\dagger}\sigma_{1}^{-1}X)\right|^{2}=\left|\mbox{tr}(X^{\dagger}\sigma_{1}^{-1}\mbox{tr}_{s}(V_{1}XV^{\dagger}_{2}))\right|^{2}

(41)

\displaystyle=\left|\mbox{tr}((\mathbb{1}_{s}\otimes X^{\dagger}\sigma_{1}^{-1})V_{1}{\sigma_{1}}^{1/2}\sigma_{1}^{-1/2}XV^{\dagger}_{2})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{tr}(X^{\dagger}\sigma_{1}^{-1}\mbox{tr}_{s}(V_{1}{\sigma_{1}}V^{\dagger}_{1})\sigma_{1}^{-1}X)\mbox{tr}(V_{2}X^{\dagger}\sigma_{1}^{-1}XV^{\dagger}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{tr}(X^{\dagger}\sigma_{1}^{-1}X)^{2},

where we have used the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product
|\mathrm{tr}(A^{\dagger}B)|^{2}\leq\mathrm{tr}(A^{\dagger}A)\mathrm{tr}(B^{\dagger}B)
to get from the second line to the third. Thus
|\nu_{i}|\leq 1
. Moreover
|\nu_{i}|=1
only in the case of equality in the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, which implies:

\sigma_{1}^{-1/2}XV^{\dagger}_{2}=e^{i\phi}{\sigma_{1}}^{1/2}V^{\dagger}_{1}(\mathbb{1}_{s}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{-1}X),

where
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
.
Using that
\sigma_{1}^{-1/2}
is invertible and denoting
W=\sigma_{1}^{-1}X
, we get:

WV^{\dagger}_{2}=e^{i\phi}V^{\dagger}_{1}(\mathbb{1}_{s}\otimes W).

Using that
V_{2}^{\dagger}V_{2}=\mathbb{1}_{M}
, we obtain:

WW^{\dagger}=V_{1}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{1}_{s}\otimes WW^{\dagger})V_{1}.

This means that
WW^{\dagger}
is the eigenvector with eigenvalue
1
of the map
X\mapsto V_{1}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{1}_{s}\otimes X)V_{1}
. The latter map is adjoint to the quantum channel
X\mapsto\mathrm{tr}_{s}(V_{1}XV_{1}^{\dagger}),
with respect to the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product, therefore it has the same spectrum. Hence the adjoint map has only one eigenvector with eigenvalue
1
. Since
V_{1}
is an isometry,
V_{1}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{1}_{s}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{M})V_{1}=\mathbb{1}_{M}
, the identity operator
\mathbb{1}_{M}
is this eigenvector, and
W^{\dagger}W=\mathbb{1}_{M}
. Replacing
\mathrm{tr}(X^{\dagger}\sigma_{1}^{-1}X)
with
\mathrm{tr}(X\sigma_{2}^{-1}X^{\dagger})
in (
41

WW^{\dagger}=\mathbb{1}_{M}
. Thus
W
is unitary and

V_{2}=e^{-i\phi}(\mathbb{1}_{s}\otimes W^{\dagger})V_{1}W.

∎

N_{\nu_{i}}^{K_{i}+1}=0
. The Jordan decomposition for
\tilde{E}
can be obtained by setting all
\nu_{i}
, except for the ones of magnitude 1, to 0,

\tilde{E}=\sum_{|\nu_{i}|=1}(\nu_{i}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}}),

From the properties of the operators
P_{\nu_{i}}
and
N_{\nu_{i}}
in the Jordan decomposition,
P_{\nu_{i}}P_{\nu_{j}}=\delta_{ij}P_{\nu_{i}}
,
P_{\nu_{i}}N_{\nu_{j}}=N_{\nu_{j}}P_{\nu_{i}}=\delta_{ij}N_{\nu_{i}}
, and
N_{\nu_{i}}N_{\nu_{k}}=\delta_{ik}N_{\nu_{i}}^{2}
, it follows that we can express

as

For all peripheral eigenvalues
\nu_{i}
,
i.e.
such that
|\nu_{i}|=1
,
N_{\nu_{i}}=0
(see, for example, the proof of Proposition 3.1 in Ref.
Fannes et al. 1992
). Thus, we can write the expression for

as

E^{n}=\sum_{|\nu_{i}|=1}\nu^{n}_{i}P_{\nu_{i}}+\sum_{|{\nu_{i}}|<1}({\nu_{i}}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}})^{n}.

It immediately follows that

\tilde{E}^{n}=\sum_{|\nu_{i}|=1}\nu^{n}_{i}P_{\nu_{i}}.

Then we can estimate

\displaystyle\|E^{n}-\tilde{E}^{n}\|^{2}=\|\sum_{|\nu_{i}|<1}({\nu_{i}}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}})^{n}\|^{2}

(43)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\||\psi\rangle\|=1}\langle\psi|\sum_{|\nu_{i}|<1}(\nu_{i}^{*}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}}^{\dagger})^{n}({\nu_{i}}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}})^{n}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\nu_{i}|<1}\sup_{\||\psi\rangle\|=1}\langle\psi|P_{\nu_{i}}(\nu_{i}^{*}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}}^{\dagger})^{n}({\nu_{i}}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}})^{n}P_{\nu_{i}}|\psi\rangle

\nu_{i}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}}
to get the second line. The third line follows from the identity

Now, using the fact that
N_{\nu_{i}}
is nilpotent with the index

, we estimate

\displaystyle\|(\nu_{i}P_{\nu_{i}}+N_{\nu_{i}})^{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|P_{\nu_{i}}\sum_{k=0}^{K_{i}}\binom{n}{k}\nu_{i}^{n-k}N_{\nu_{i}}^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq|\nu_{i}|^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{K_{i}}\binom{n}{k}\frac{\|N_{\nu_{i}}\|^{k}}{|\nu_{i}|^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq|\nu_{i}|^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{K_{i}}\frac{n^{k}}{k!}\frac{\|N_{\nu_{i}}\|^{k}}{|\nu_{i}|^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq|\nu_{i}|^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{K_{i}}\binom{K_{i}}{k}\left(\frac{n\|N_{\nu_{i}}\|}{|\nu_{i}|}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=|\nu_{i}|^{n}\left(\frac{n\|N_{\nu_{i}}\|}{|\nu_{i}|}+1\right)^{K_{i}},

for

, we bound

(44)

Combining (
42
), (
43
), and (
44
) together,

\|\mathcal{E}^{n}-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{n}\|_{2\rightarrow 2}\leq\nu_{\mathrm{gap}}^{n}\left(c_{1}n+1\right)^{K_{\mathrm{max}}},

manifesting an almost exponential decay. Using a slightly weaker bound, we can make the decay to be strictly exponential. We choose some
\nu_{\mathrm{gap}}<\nu<1
, expand

\nu_{\mathrm{gap}}^{n}\left(c_{1}n+1\right)^{K_{\mathrm{max}}}=\left(\frac{\nu_{\mathrm{gap}}}{\nu}\right)^{n}\left(c_{1}n+1\right)^{K_{\mathrm{max}}}\nu^{n},

and notice that first two factors can be bounded by a constant

equal to the cardinality of the set
\mathcal{O}_{r}
, which is equal to the rank of
\tilde{Q}
, hence we want to force the ranks of
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
to be equal. But first let us rigorously determine how close
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
are.

Matrices
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
are partial transposes of
E
and
\tilde{E}
, respectively, defined in Appendix
E
. The latter are related to the quantum channels
\mathcal{E}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
, respectively, defined in Section
IV
. Then from Ref.
Tomiyama 1983
,

\|Q-\tilde{Q}\|\leq d_{M}\|E-\tilde{E}\|\leq cd_{M}\nu^{|B|},

(55)

where we have used Lemma
III.3
in the second inequality. Now we can represent
Q
in the form

Q=\tilde{Q}+cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}Z,

(56)

where
Z
is a Hermitian matrix with
\|Z\|\leq 1
.

From (
54
) it is clear that the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of
\tilde{Q}
is equal to
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}
, the smallest eigenvalue of
\sigma
. The matrices
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
are close if the region
B
is large enough. By dropping from
Q
the part of the spectrum below
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}
, we obtain from
Q
the matrix
Q^{\prime}
, which approximates to both
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
and has the same rank as the latter. We first make the spectral decomposition,
Q=W\Lambda W^{\dagger}
. We now define
\Pi_{>\epsilon}
, the projector onto the part of the spectrum with eigenvalues greater than
\epsilon=cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}
, and further define
Q^{\prime}:=W\Lambda_{>\epsilon}W^{\dagger}
, where
\Lambda_{>\epsilon}=\Pi_{>\epsilon}\Lambda\Pi_{>\epsilon}
. As follows from (
55
), if
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}>2cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}
, then as
|B|\rightarrow\infty
there is a part of the spectrum of
Q
with eigenvalues converging to
0
that is well separated from the rest of the spectrum. We assume that this condition is satisfied. For
Q^{\prime}
we have the decomposition analogous to (
56
),
Q^{\prime}=\tilde{Q}+c\nu^{|B|}Z^{\prime}
, where
Z^{\prime}
is Hermitian and
\|Z^{\prime}\|\leq 1
.

Since
Q^{\prime}
is positive semidefinite,
Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}
is well-defined. We take the pseudo-inverse of
Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}
, and define
|\xi_{ij}\rangle
,

|\xi_{ij}\rangle:=\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{M}}(Q^{\prime-\frac{1}{2}})_{mn;ij}\langle m|V_{B}|n\rangle.

(57)

Now we determine the bound on
\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|
. By definition of the operator norm,

\displaystyle\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{M}}\left(\langle i|V_{B}|j\rangle-\sqrt{p\sigma_{i}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle\right)\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|\|^{2}=\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{M}}\left(\langle i|V_{B}|j\rangle-\sqrt{p\sigma_{i}}\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{M}}(Q^{\prime-\frac{1}{2}})_{mn;ij}\langle m|V_{B}|n\rangle\right)\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|\|^{2}

(58)

\displaystyle=\max_{\sum_{j}|\eta_{j}|^{2}=1}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}=1}^{d_{M}}\bar{\eta}_{j^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{M}}\left(Q_{ij^{\prime};ij}+\tilde{Q}_{ij^{\prime};ij}-2\sqrt{p\sigma_{i}}Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}_{ij^{\prime};ij}\right)\eta_{j}=\|\mathrm{tr}_{1}Q+\mathrm{tr}_{1}\tilde{Q}-2\mathrm{tr}_{1}((\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{1})Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}})\|,

where we have used the definition of
Q
(
53
), the identity
p\sigma_{i}\langle\xi_{ij^{\prime}}|\xi_{ij}\rangle=\tilde{Q}_{ij^{\prime};ij}
, which follows from (
53
) and (
57
), and the identity
\langle j^{\prime}|V^{\dagger}_{B}|i\rangle\langle m|V_{B}|n\rangle(Q^{\prime-\frac{1}{2}})_{mn;ij}=Q_{ij^{\prime};mn}(Q^{\prime-\frac{1}{2}})_{mn;ij}=Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}_{ij^{\prime};ij}
. In the last line
\mathrm{tr}_{1}
denotes the partial trace over the first pair of indices.

Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d_{M}}\tilde{Q}_{ij^{\prime};ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{M}}p\sigma_{i}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\chi_{\mathcal{O}_{r}}\left((i,j)\right)

\displaystyle=p\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{M}}\chi_{\mathcal{O}_{r}}\left((i,j)\right)\sigma_{i}\right)\delta_{jj^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=p\mathrm{tr}(\Pi_{r+|B|}\sigma\Pi_{r+|B|}))\delta_{jj^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=p\frac{1}{p}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}.

Thus,

\mathrm{tr}_{1}(\tilde{Q})=\mathbb{1}.

(59)

Now we need to bound
\mathrm{tr}_{1}Q
and
\mathrm{tr}_{1}((\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{1})Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}})
. Using the representation (
56
),

\mathrm{tr}_{1}Q=\mathrm{tr}_{1}\tilde{Q}+cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\mathrm{tr}_{1}Z=\mathbb{1}+cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\mathrm{tr}_{1}Z,

where we have used (
59
). Since
\|Z\|\leq 1
, then
\|\mathrm{tr}_{1}Z\|\leq d_{M}
, and then

(1-cd_{M}^{2}\nu^{|B|})\mathbb{1}\leq\mathrm{tr}_{1}Q\leq(1+cd_{M}^{2}\nu^{|B|})\mathbb{1}.

(60)

To bound
\mathrm{tr}_{1}((\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{1})Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}})
, recall that
Q^{\prime}=\tilde{Q}+cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}Z^{\prime}
, hence

\displaystyle Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tilde{Q}+cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\mathbb{1}+c\nu^{|B|}d_{M}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\prime}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{4}},

where
\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
should be regarded as a pseudo-inverse. As
Z^{\prime}
is Hermitian, we can decompose
Z^{\prime}=Z^{\prime}_{+}-Z^{\prime}_{-}
, where both
Z^{\prime}_{+}
and
Z^{\prime}_{-}
are positive semidefinite. Then, by the monotonicity of matrix square root with respect to partial ordering of non-negative operators,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\mathbb{1}-cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\prime}_{-}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\mathbb{1}+cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\prime}_{+}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Using Taylor expansion we can estimate

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{1}-cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\prime}_{-}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{{1}}-\frac{1+2^{-\frac{9}{2}}}{2}cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\prime}_{-}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{1}+cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\prime}_{+}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{1}+\frac{1}{2}cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\prime}_{+}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

The former inequality is based on the previously assumed condition
2cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}
, which leads to
\|cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\prime}_{-}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\leq\frac{cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. We have used the latter bound to estimate the remainder term in the Taylor expansion. We have also used
\|Z^{\prime}_{-}\|\leq\|Z\|\leq 1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1+2^{-\frac{9}{2}}}{2}cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{4}}Z^{\prime}_{-}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{4}}\leq Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{4}}Z^{\prime}_{+}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{4}},

which leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{1}-\frac{1+2^{-\frac{9}{2}}}{2}cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\mathrm{tr}_{1}Z^{\prime}_{-}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{tr}_{1}((\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{1})Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{1}+\frac{1}{2}cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}\mathrm{tr}_{1}Z^{\prime}_{+},

where we have used
\mathrm{tr}_{1}((\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{1})\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{2}})=\mathrm{tr}_{1}\tilde{Q}=\mathbb{1}
, and
\mathrm{tr}_{1}((\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{1})\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{4}}Z^{\prime}_{\pm}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{4}}))=\mathrm{tr}_{1}(\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{4}}\tilde{Q}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{Q}^{-\frac{1}{4}}Z^{\prime}_{\pm})=\mathrm{tr}_{1}Z^{\prime}_{\pm}
. Since
\|\mathrm{tr}_{1}Z^{\prime}_{\pm}\|\leq\|\mathrm{tr}_{1}(\mathbb{1}\otimes\mathbb{1})\|\leq d_{M}
, then

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1+2^{-\frac{9}{2}}}{2}cd_{M}^{2}\nu^{|B|}\right)\mathbb{1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{tr}_{1}((\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{1})Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}})

(61)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{2}cd_{M}^{2}\nu^{|B|}\right)\mathbb{1}.

Combining (
59
), (
60
), and (
61
) together,

\|\mathrm{tr}_{1}Q+\mathrm{tr}_{1}\tilde{Q}-2\mathrm{tr}_{1}((\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{1})Q^{\prime\frac{1}{2}})\|\leq 2(1+2^{-\frac{11}{2}})cd_{M}^{2}\nu^{|B|}.

From (
58
) we obtain

\|V_{B}-\tilde{V}_{B}\|\leq\sqrt{2(1+2^{-\frac{11}{2}})c}d_{M}\nu^{\frac{|B|}{2}}.

(62)

This bound is only a factor
\sqrt{2(1+2^{-\frac{11}{2}})d_{M}}
worse than the bound (
23
), though it requires the condition
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}>2cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}
to be satisfied.

Analogous to the bound (
23
) in Section
IV
, (
62
) remains true for a general pgFCS. In the case
J>1
of the convex decomposition (
13
), the matrices
Q
,
Q^{\prime}
and
\tilde{Q}
decompose into direct sums over ergodic components, i.e.,
Q=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{J}Q_{\alpha}
. The generalization to this case is straightforward. For the ergodic pgFCS of period
1
,
\tilde{Q}
is full-rank, hence we have the simplification
Q^{\prime}=Q
, where
Q
is guaranteed to be invertible provided
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}>cd_{M}\nu^{|B|}
. We have discussed this case in Section
V
.

Appendix K

In this appendix we make some remarks comparing our Theorem
II.1
to Theorems III.1, III.2 (a generalization of III.1), and Proposition III.3 of Ref
Chen et al. 2021
. In particular, we show that the classes of states considered in Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
are in general distinct from pgFCS.

In our notation, the states considered in the mentioned theorems and proposition of Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
have the form

\rho_{ABM}=\Phi^{|B|}\circ\Phi^{|A|}(\sigma),

(63)

where the compatibility condition
\mathrm{tr}_{s}\Phi(\sigma)=\sigma
is not assumed. Note that what is called system
C
in Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
, is the memory system
M
in our article, hence the indexing in (
63
). The states
\rho_{ABM}
of (
63
) are related to the states
\rho_{ABC}
of (
8
) and (
12
) in the current paper through

\rho_{ABC}=\mathrm{tr}_{M}\circ\Phi^{|C|}(\rho_{ABM}).

(64)

In Theorems III.1 and III.2 of Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
, the considered quantum channels
\Phi
satisfy the condition (in our notation)

\Phi(\zeta_{M})=\chi_{s}\otimes\zeta_{M},

(65)

for some states
\chi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{s})
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{M})
, which are maximally mixed states in the setup of Theorem III.1. It is proven that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{ABM}-\rho_{AB}\otimes\zeta_{M}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=O(\eta^{|B|})

(66)

\displaystyle I(A:M|B)

\displaystyle=O(|B|\eta^{|B|}),

(67)

where
\eta<1
under some additional assumptions. We note that, while it is not stated in Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
, (
66
) implies that
\rho_{ABM}
is approximated in trace norm by a manifestly QMC
\rho_{AB}\otimes\zeta_{M}
. Since action of a quantum channel on the system
M
increases neither trace norm, nor QCMI, the state
\rho_{ABC}
in (
64
) inherits the properties (
66
) and (
67
),

\displaystyle\|\rho_{ABC}-\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=O(\eta^{|B|})

(68)

\displaystyle I(A:C|B)

\displaystyle=O(|B|\eta^{|B|}),

(69)

where
\tilde{\rho}_{ABC}=\rho_{AB}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{M}\circ\Phi^{|C|}(\zeta_{M})
is a QMC. In these bounds our result is similar, however the pgFCS that we consider are generally an independent class of states. Indeed, for pgFCS, the generating channel
\Phi
has the form
\Phi(X)=VXV^{\dagger}
, where
V:\mathcal{H}_{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
is an isometry, consistent with the condition (
65
) only under the restrictive choice
\chi=|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|
and
V=|\phi\rangle\otimes\mathbb{1}_{M}
for some normalized vector
|\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{s}
. Hence a pgFCS in general does not belong to the class of states considered in Theorem III.2 (the converse is also true), and never belongs to the class considered in Theorem III.1.

The quantum channels considered in Proposition III.3 of Ref.
Chen et al. 2021
have a forgetful component, i.e., can be decomposed as

\Phi(X)=(1-\eta)\mathrm{tr}(X)\chi+\eta\mathcal{N}(X),

(70)

where
\chi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M})
,
0<\eta<1
and
\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{M})
is some quantum channel. This class of states is also distinct from pgFCS, since no channel of the form
\Phi(X)=VXV^{\dagger}
can be decomposed as (
70
). It is intuitively obvious that a channel constructed as a conjugation by isometries does not have a forgetful component. As proof, consider two orthogonal vectors
|\psi\rangle,|\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{M}
,
\langle\psi|\phi\rangle=0
. Then, on the one hand,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left(\Phi(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\Phi(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)\right)

(71)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathrm{tr}\left(V|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|V^{\dagger}V|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|V^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\left|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=0,

and on the other hand, for quantum channels admitting the decomposition (
70
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left(\Phi(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\Phi(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)\right)

(72)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left(\left((1-\eta)\chi+\eta\mathcal{N}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left.\left((1-\eta)\chi+\eta\mathcal{N}(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)^{2}\mathrm{tr}(\chi^{2})+\eta(1-\eta)\mathrm{tr}\left(\chi\mathcal{N}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\eta^{2}\mathrm{tr}\left({N}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|){N}(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)\right)

\displaystyle>0.

since all the summands are non-negative, and
(1-\eta)^{2}\mathrm{tr}(\chi^{2})>0
. Hence, the intersection between the class of pgFCS and the states generated by a partially forgetful channel (
70
) is empty.

Abstract
. We give a handy way to have a situation that the k ​ G kG -Scott module with vertex P P remains indecomposable under taking the Brauer construction for any subgroup Q Q of P P as k ⁡ [ Q ​ C G ​ ( Q ) ] k[Q\,C_{G}(Q)] -module, where k k is a field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 . The motivation is that the Brauer indecomposability of a p p -permutation bimodule is one of the key steps in order to obtain a splendid stable equivalence of Morita type by making use of the gluing method, that then can possibly lift to a splendid derived equivalence. Further our result explains a hidden reason why the Brauer indecomposability of the Scott module fails in Ishioka’s recent examples.

Introduction and notation

In modular representation theory of finite groups,
the Brauer construction
M(P)
of a
p
-permutation
kG
-module

p
-subgroup

where

It canonically becomes a
p
-permutation module over

(see
[
2

In their paper
[
8

Kessar, Kunugi and Mitsuhashi introduce a notion Brauer indecomposability.
Namely,

is called

if the restriction
module
{\mathrm{Res}}\,^{N_{G}(Q)}_{Q\,C_{G}(Q)}\,M(Q)
is indecomposable or zero
as
k(Q\,C_{G}(Q))
-module for any subgroup
Q
of
P
.
Actually in order to get a kind of equivalence between
the module categories for the principal blocks

and
B

of the group algebras
kG
and
kH
, respectively
(where
H
is another finite group), e.g.
in order to prove Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture,

have to face a situation such that

and

are stably equivalent of Morita type.
In order to do it
we often want to check whether the
k(G\times H)
-Scott module

with a vertex

\Delta P:=\{(u,u)\in P\times P\}
induces a stable
equivalence of Morita type between
A
and
B
, where

P
is a common Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
and
H

by making use of
the gluing method due to Broué (see
[
3
, 6.3.Theorem]
), and also

[
19
, Theorem 5.6]
.
If this is the case, then

has to be Brauer indecomposable
(as done in
[
12
,
8
,
7
,
9
,
10
,

]
).
Therefore it should be very important to know if

is Brauer indecomposable or not.
Even more is that one can understand what happens in interesting Ishioka’s examples
[
5
]

from main result’s point of view.
These motivate us to write this paper.

Actually, our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a finite group and
M:={\mathrm{Sc}}(G,P)
the
Scott

-module with vertex

.
Then the following

(1)

is Brauer indecomposable.

(2)

There exists an

satisfying that

.
Suppose that

(1)

(2)

2.
Lemmas

Throughout this section we fix a finite group
G
and its
p
-subgroup
P
, and further set

M:={\text{Sc}}(G,P)
and
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{F}_{P}(G)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
p\,{\not|}\,\,|N_{G}(P)/P\,C_{G}(P)|
and
P\leq\mathrm{ker}(M)
,
then
M
is Brauer indecomposable.

Proof.

It holds
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N_{G}(P)}\,M=M(P)

by
[
20
, Exercise (27.4)]
. So that
[
8
, Lemma 4.3(i)]
yields that

(1)

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{C_{G}(P)}\,M\text{ is indecomposable.}

Now, take any
Q\leq P
. Clearly
Q\leq\mathrm{ker}(M)
, so that

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N_{G}(Q)}\,M=M(Q)

as above. Thus,

\mathrm{Res}^{N_{G}(Q)}_{C_{G}(Q)}M(Q)=\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{C_{G}(Q)}\,M
,
which implies from (
1
) that

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{C_{G}(Q)}\,M
is indecomposable
since
C_{G}(P)\leq C_{G}(Q)
. Namely
\mathrm{Res}^{N_{G}(Q)}_{C_{G}(Q)}\,M(Q)
is
indecomposable.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

If
P\unlhd G
and
p\,{\not|}\,\,|G/P\,C_{G}(P)|
,
then
M
is Brauer indecomposable.

Proof.

Since
P\unlhd G
we have that

M\ |\ \mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}\,k\cong k(G/P)
so that
P\leq\mathrm{ker}(M)
and
the result follows from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Suppose that
G:=S_{4}
,
p=2
and
P:=O_{2}(G)

(then
P\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
and

G=P\rtimes S_{3}
, so that

Set

, then

by

, Chap.4, Corollary 8.5]
.
Note that

by the definition of Brauer construction (quotient).
So that Green’s indecomposability theorem implies that

M=\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}\,k_{H}\cong\boxed{\begin{matrix}k_{G}\\
k_{G}\end{matrix}}

(a uniserial
kG
-module of length
2

with two composition factors

) that affords the ordinary character

1_{G}+\chi_{1}
where
\chi_{1}
is (so-called) the sign character of degree

Since

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{P}\,M\cong k_{P}\oplus k_{P}.

Thus

Note that although

is not a

-group. See Corollary

.

If

(2)

p\,{\not|}\,|G/P\,C_{G}(P)|

(2)\Rightarrow(3):

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}
be an indecomposable
p
-permutation
kG
-module with vertex
P
, and let
R\unlhd G

be a
p
-subgroup such that
R\leq\mathrm{ker}({\mathfrak{M}})
. Assume that
Q\leq P
.
Then for any
H
such that
R\not\leq H<QR
, we have that
\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{H})=0
.

Proof.

Since
R\not\leq H
, we have
H<HR
. The representatives of the set of left cosets
of
H
in
HR
can be chosen from
R
and the number of the left cosets is divisible by
p
.
So, since
R
acts trivially on
{\mathfrak{M}}

\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{HR}(m)=\sum_{r}rm=\sum_{r}m=0\text{ for every }m\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{H}

where
r
runs over the different left cosets of
H
in
HR
. Then

\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{H})=(\mathrm{tr}_{HR}^{QR}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{HR})({\mathfrak{M}}^{H})=0

as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}
be an indecomposable
p
-permutation
kG
-module with vertex
P
, and let
R\unlhd G

be a
p
-subgroup such that
R\leq\mathrm{ker}({\mathfrak{M}})
. Assume that
Q\leq P
.
Then for any
K
with
(Q\cap R)\not\leq K<Q
, we have that
\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}({\mathfrak{M}}^{K})=0
.

Proof.

Since
(Q\cap R)\not\leq K
, we have
K<(Q\cap R)K\leq Q
. The representatives of the set of left cosets
of
K
in
(Q\cap R)K
can be chosen from
R
and the number of the left cosets is divisible by
p
.
So, since
R
acts trivially on
{\mathfrak{M}}
,

\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{(Q\cap R)K}(m)=\sum_{r}rm=\sum_{r}m=0\text{ for every }m\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{K}

where
r
runs over the different left cosets of
K
in
(Q\cap R)K
. Then

\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}({\mathfrak{M}}^{K})=(\mathrm{tr}_{(Q\cap R)K}^{Q}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{(Q\cap R)K})({\mathfrak{M}}^{K})=0

as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume that
R\unlhd G
and
Q\leq G
.

(i)

For
K
with
(Q\cap R)\leq K\leq Q
,
KR=QR
if and only if
K=Q
.

(ii)

There is a bijective correspondence between the following
sets:
I_{1}:=\{K\ |\ (Q\cap R)\leq K<Q\}
and
I_{2}:=\{H\ |\ R\leq H<QR\}

given by
K\mapsto KR
.

Proof.

(i)
We always have that if
K=Q
, then
KR=QR
.
Now suppose that
KR=QR
. It follows that for any
q\in Q
,
there exist
\kappa\in K
and
r\in R
such
that
q=\kappa r
. Then
r=\kappa^{-1}q
and
K\leq Q
implies that

r\in Q
. Thus
r\in(Q\cap R)
. Since
(Q\cap R)\leq K
,
we have that
r\in K
, so that
q=\kappa r\in K
. Hence
Q\leq K
.

(ii)
First, by (i) the correspondence is well-defined (a map).

Surjectivity:
Take any
H\in I_{2}
. Set
K:=Q\cap H
. Then,
Q\cap R\leq Q\cap H=:K\leq Q
. Further,
if
K=Q
, then
Q\cap H=:K=Q
so
Q\leq H
so that
QR\leq HR=H
since
R\leq H
, that
contradicts the fact that
H\in I_{2}
. Hence
K<Q
, that shows that
K\in I_{1}
.
Now, we claim that
KR=H
. Obviously
KR\leq HH=H
.
Suppose that
KR<H
. Then there exists an element
h\in H\backslash KR
. Write

h=:qr
for elements
q\in Q
and
r\in R
since
H\leq QR
. Hence

q=hr^{-1}
and since
qr\,{\not\in}\,(Q\cap H)R
,
q\,{\not\in}\,H
which means that

r\,{\not\in}\,H
, a contradiction since
H\in I_{2}
. Thus,
KR=H
, that yields that
the correspondence is surjective.

Injectivity: Suppose that
KR=K^{\prime}R
for
K,K^{\prime}\in I_{1}
. Take any
k^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
. Then,
since
k^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}R=KR
, there exist

\kappa\in K
and
r\in R
with
k^{\prime}=\kappa r
.
Hence,
R\ni r={\kappa}^{-1}k^{\prime}\in Q
, that yields that
{\kappa}^{-1}k^{\prime}=r\in Q\cap R\leq K
,
and hence
k^{\prime}\in K
. Thus,
K^{\prime}\leq K
. Similarly we get
K\leq K^{\prime}
.

∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
R\,\unlhd\,G
and
Q,K\leq G
such that
(Q\cap R)\leq K<Q
, and set
n:=|Q:K|
.
Then there exist elements
q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\in Q
such that

Q=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\,q_{i}K
(disjoint union) and

QR=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\,q_{i}KR
(disjoint union).

Proof.

Since
Q=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\,q_{i}K
, we have that
QR=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\,q_{i}KR
.
Suppose there exist
t
and
s
such that
q_{t}KR=q_{s}KR
. Then it follows that

q_{s}^{-1}q_{t}\in KR
and thus
q_{s}^{-1}q_{t}\in(Q\cap KR).
Note that since
K<Q
,
we have that
Q\cap KR=K(Q\cap R)
, hence
q_{s}^{-1}q_{t}\in K(Q\cap R)=K.
Thus

q_{t}K=q_{s}K
. It follows that
QR=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\,q_{i}KR
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}
be an indecomposable
p
-permutation
kG
-module with vertex
P
, and let
R\unlhd G

be a
p
-subgroup such that
R\leq\mathrm{ker}({\mathfrak{M}})
. Assume that
Q\leq P
.

(i)

It holds that
{\mathfrak{M}}(QR)={\mathfrak{M}}(Q)

as
k
-vector spaces and that
\mathrm{Res}_{N_{G}(Q)}^{N_{G}(QR)}\,{\mathfrak{M}}(QR)\cong{\mathfrak{M}}(Q).

(ii)

If
\mathrm{Res}^{N_{G}(QR)}_{C_{G}(Q)}\,\mathfrak{M}(QR)
is indecomposable, then

\mathrm{Res}^{N_{G}(Q)}_{C_{G}(Q)}\,\mathfrak{M}(Q)
is indecomposable.

Proof.

(i)
Let
(Q\cap R)\leq K<Q
, then by Lemma
2.8
(i), we have that

KR<QR
.
Note that
{\mathfrak{M}}^{K}={\mathfrak{M}}^{KR}
since
R\leq\mathrm{ker}({\mathfrak{M}})
.
Let
q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}
be the same as in Lemma
2.9
.

Then for any
m\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{K}
,

\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}(m)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}m=\mathrm{tr}_{KR}^{QR}(m).
Hence,

(2)

\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}({\mathfrak{M}}^{K})=\mathrm{tr}_{KR}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{KR}).

Further by Lemma
2.7
, we have that

(3)

\sum_{K<Q}\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}({\mathfrak{M}}^{K})=\sum_{(Q\cap R)\leq K<Q}\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}({\mathfrak{M}}^{K}).

It follows from Lemma
2.6
that

(4)

\sum_{H<QR}\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{H})=\sum_{R\leq H<QR}\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{H}).

Now, Lemma
2.8
(ii) implies that

(5)

\sum_{R\leq H<QR}\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{H})=\sum_{(Q\cap R)\leq K<Q}\mathrm{tr}_{KR}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{KR}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{K<Q}\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}({\mathfrak{M}}^{K})=

\displaystyle\sum_{(Q\cap R)\leq K<Q}\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}({\mathfrak{M}}^{K})\text{\quad}\text{ by (\ref{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(Q\cap R)\leq K<Q}\,\mathrm{tr}_{KR}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{KR})\quad\text{ by (\ref{1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{R\leq H<QR}\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{H})\quad\text{ by (\ref{4})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{H<QR}\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{H})\quad\text{ by (\ref{3})}.

Therefore, we have that

{\mathfrak{M}}(Q)={\mathfrak{M}}^{Q}\big/\big(\sum_{K<Q}\mathrm{tr}_{K}^{Q}({\mathfrak{M}}^{K})\big)={\mathfrak{M}}^{QR}\big/\big(\sum_{H<QR}\mathrm{tr}_{H}^{QR}({\mathfrak{M}}^{H})\big)={\mathfrak{M}}(QR)

as sets. But the left and right hand sides, respectively, are a
kN_{G}(Q)
-module
and a
kN_{G}(QR)
-module. Then,
the fact that
N_{G}(Q)\leq N_{G}(QR)

implies that the right hand side can also be seen as a
kN_{G}(Q)
-module, which
competes the proof.

(ii)
Suppose that
\mathrm{Res}^{N_{G}(Q)}_{C_{G}(Q)}\,{\mathfrak{M}}(Q)
is not indecomposable.
By (i), we have that
{\mathfrak{M}}(QR)\cong{\mathfrak{M}}(Q)

as
k\,N_{G}(Q)
-modules. So,

\mathrm{Res}^{N_{G}(QR)}_{C_{G}(Q)}\,{\mathfrak{M}}(QR)
is not indecomposable.
But since
C_{G}(QR)\leq C_{G}(Q)
,
it follows that

\mathrm{Res}^{N_{G}(QR)}_{C_{G}(QR)}\,{\mathfrak{M}}(QR)
is not indecomposable,
a contradiction.
∎

The following is an easy application of Corollary

Example 3.3
.

Set
p:=2
,

D:=\langle a,y,z|\ a^{2}=y^{2}=z^{2}=1,[a,z]=[y,z]=1,[a,y]=z\rangle\cong D_{8}\text{ and}

Let

N_{G}(P)/P\,C_{G}(P)=(D\times(C_{2}\times C_{2}))/(D\times(C_{2}\times C_{2}))\cong 1
.
We claim that
M=\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}\,k

Note that for

N:=N_{G}(P)=D\times V_{4}\,\unlhd\,G

kN

We know easily by

\mathrm{Ind}_{C_{2}}^{A_{4}}\,k=\boxed{\begin{matrix}\ \ k\ \ 1_{\omega}\ \ 1_{\omega^{2}}\\
1_{\omega}\ 1_{\omega^{2}}\ k\end{matrix}}\ \ \text{ (Loewy and socle series)}

non-trivial one-dimensional

-modules corresponding to a primitive

This implies that
{\text{Sc}}(A_{4},\langle b\rangle)=\mathrm{Ind}_{C_{2}}^{A_{4}}\,k

Let

Then

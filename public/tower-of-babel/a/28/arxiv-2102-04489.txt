Laplace principle for large population games with control interaction
Peng Luo and Ludovic Tangpi

Abstract
. This work investigates continuous time stochastic differential games with a large number of players whose costs and dynamics interact through the empirical distribution of both their states and their controls. The control processes are assumed to be open-loop. We give regularity conditions guaranteeing that if the finite-player game admits a Nash equilibrium, then both the sequence of equilibria and the corresponding state processes satisfy a Sanov-type large deviation principle. The results require existence of a Lipschitz continuous solution of the master equation of the corresponding mean field game, and they carry over to cooperative (i.e. central planner) games. We study a linear-quadratic case of such games in details.

\displaystyle|Y^{i,i}_{t}-\mathcal{Y}^{i,N}_{t}|^{2}

\varepsilon_{1}>0

\displaystyle|Y^{i,i}_{t}-\mathcal{Y}^{i,N}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[3L_{g}^{2}|X^{i,\underline{\hat{\alpha}}}_{T}-\mathcal{X}^{i,N}_{T}|^{2}+3L_{g}^{2}\Big\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X^{j,\underline{\hat{\alpha}}}_{T}-\mathcal{X}^{j,N}_{T}|^{2}\Big\}+3|\gamma^{i,N}|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{N}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}\bigg((2L_{f}+2L_{\Lambda}L_{f}+3\varepsilon_{1}+3\eta_{1})|Y^{i,i}_{s}-\mathcal{Y}^{i,N}_{s}|^{2}+\frac{(L_{f}+L_{f}L_{\Lambda})^{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}|X^{i,\underline{\hat{\alpha}}}_{s}-\mathcal{X}^{i,N}_{s}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{(L_{\Lambda}L_{f}+L_{f}\sqrt{1+8L_{\Lambda}^{2}})^{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X^{j,\underline{\hat{\alpha}}}_{s}-\mathcal{X}^{j,N}_{s}|^{2}+\frac{4L_{f}^{2}L_{\Lambda}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|Y^{j,j}_{s}-\mathcal{Y}^{j,N}_{s}|^{2}

12

\hat{\alpha}^{i,N}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}|Y^{i,i}_{t}-\mathcal{Y}^{i,N}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\delta(\varepsilon_{1},\eta_{1})(T-t)}\bigg\{6L_{f}^{2}+\Big(\frac{L_{f}(1+L_{\Lambda})^{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}+\frac{L_{\Lambda}(L_{f}+\sqrt{1+8L_{\Lambda}^{2}})^{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\Big)T\bigg\}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i,\underline{\hat{\alpha}}}-\mathcal{X}^{i,N}|^{2}\Big\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}}

Therefore, we have

3.2.
Example: A model of systemic risk

For illustration, consider (as slight modification of) the problem of inter-bank borrowing and lending studied by
Carmona et al. 2015
.
In the systemic risk model proposed by these authors, bank i’s reserve is given by the dynamics

dX^{i}_{t}=a\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{j}_{t}-X^{i}_{t}\Big)+\alpha^{i}_{t}\,dt+\sigma\,dW^{i}_{t}

for a given mean-reverting parameter
a\geq 0
.
Each bank controls its borrowing and lending rate
\alpha^{i}
at time
t
by choosing it so as to minimize the cost function

J^{i}(\underline{\alpha})=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}f(X_{t}^{i},L^{N}(\underline{X}_{t}),\alpha^{i}_{t})\,dt+g(X^{i}_{T})\Big]

with

f(x,\mu,\alpha):=\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}-q\alpha\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(dz)-x\Big)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(dz)-x\Big|\quad\text{and}\quad g(x)=\frac{c}{2}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(dz)-x\Big|

where
q,c
and
\varepsilon
are strictly positive parameters such that
q^{2}\leq\varepsilon
.
With these specifications of the coefficients of the game, the conditions
(A1)
-
(A5)
are clearly satisfied.
To check
(A7)
, notice that the function
\Lambda
therein now takes the form

\Lambda(x,\mu,y)=q\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(dz)-x\Big)-y.

In particular, the functions
B,F
and
G
in
(A7)
are linear and time-independent.
Thus, it follows from
[
16
, Proposition 5.2]
and
[
11
, Lemma 5.6]
that the PDE (
6
) admits a solution
V
which is Lipschitz–continuous in its second and third variables.
Therefore, if for each
N
the finite player game admits a Nash equilibrium, (see e.g.
[
14
, Section 3.1]
for details on the existence)
\underline{\hat{\alpha}}=(\hat{\alpha}^{1,N},\dots\hat{\alpha}^{N,N})
then it follows by Theorem
2
that if
T
is small enough, then the sequence
(L^{N}(\underline{X}^{\underline{\hat{\alpha}}}))
satisfies the LDP with rate function given by (
9
), wherein

\overline{B}(t,x,\mu):=(a+q)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(dz)-x\Big)-V(t,x,\mu).

For LDP with
T
arbitrary, by Theorem
3
, one needs additional monotonicity properties.
These can be guaranteed by appropriate conditions on the constants
\varepsilon,q,c

a
and
T
.
Note that
the large deviation principle for this example was obtained in
[
19
]
for the case of closed-loop controls.

4.
Large deviation principle for cooperative games: a case study

As explained in the introduction, the method developed in this article also applies to cooperative large population games.
Such games are important in several applications, especially when a “societal goal” should be achieved.
We refer the reader for instance to
[
10
]
and
[
27
]
.
In the general setting, and keeping the notation of the previous section, a continuous time differential cooperative game takes the form

(29)

V^{N}:=\inf_{\underline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}^{N}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X^{i,\underline{\alpha}}_{t},{\alpha^{i}_{t}},L^{N}(\underline{X}^{\underline{\alpha}}_{t},\underline{\alpha}_{t}))\,dt+g(X^{i,\underline{\alpha}}_{T},L^{N}(X^{\underline{\alpha}}_{T}))\Big],

where
\mathcal{A}^{N}
is the
N
-fold cartesian product of the set
\mathcal{A}
.
In
[
35
, Theorem 25]
, conditions on the parameters
b,f
and
g
are given to guarantee that, if the problem (
29
) admits a solution
\hat{\alpha}^{N}=(\hat{\alpha}^{1,N},\dots,\hat{\alpha}^{N,N})
, then for each
i
, and each
t
, the sequence
(\hat{\alpha}^{i,N}_{t})
converges to
\hat{\alpha}_{t}
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
, where
\hat{\alpha}
solves the McKean-Vlasov control problem

(30)

\begin{cases}\inf_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}}E\Big[\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t},\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t}))\,dt+g(X^{\alpha}_{T},\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha}_{T}))\Big]\\
dX^{\alpha}_{t}=b(t,X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t},\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha}_{t},\alpha_{t}))\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t},\quad X^{\alpha}_{0}=x.\end{cases}

Since the derivation of the Laplace principle for this game is similar to the non-cooperative case, in order to avoid repetitions we will focus here on the linear quadratic case.
Thus, let us specify the coefficients as follows:

(31)

\begin{cases}f(t,x,\alpha,\xi)&=Q|x|^{2}+\bar{Q}|\overline{x}|^{2}+R|\alpha|^{2}+\bar{R}|\overline{\alpha}|^{2}+\bar{S}x\overline{\alpha},\\
b(t,x,\alpha,\xi)&=Ax+\bar{A}\overline{x}+B\alpha+\bar{B}\overline{\alpha},\\
g(x,\mu)&=Q_{T}x^{2}+\bar{Q}_{T}\overline{x}^{2}\end{cases}

for some real numbers
A,\bar{A},B,\bar{B},R,\bar{R},Q,\bar{Q},Q_{T},\bar{Q}_{T},R
and
\bar{R}
, and where
\bar{x}
and
\bar{\alpha}
are the mean of the first and second marginals of
\xi
, respectively.
We assume that
x
and
\alpha
have the same dimension denoted
m
.
To state the LDP in this case, let us introduce the following notation:
We consider the functions

\ell(y,\bar{x},\bar{y}):=-\frac{1}{2R}\left[By+\bar{S}\left(1-\frac{\bar{R}}{R+\bar{R}}\right)\bar{x}+\left(\bar{B}-\frac{\bar{R}}{R+\bar{R}}(B+\bar{B})\right)\bar{y}\right]

and

\displaystyle\ell_{1}(x,y,\xi)

\displaystyle:=Ax+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{1}}[X]+B\ell\Big(y,\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{1}}[X],\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{2}}[Y]\Big)+\bar{B}\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{2}}\Big[\ell\Big(Y,\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{1}}[X],\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{2}}[Y]\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=Ax+\Big[\bar{A}-\frac{1}{2R}(SB+\bar{S}\bar{B})\Big(1-\frac{\bar{R}}{R+\bar{R}}\Big)\Big]\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{1}}[X]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{B^{2}}{2R}y-\frac{1}{2R}\Big[\bar{B}B+(B+\bar{B})\Big(\bar{B}-\frac{\bar{R}}{R+\bar{R}}(B+\bar{B})\Big)\Big]\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{2}}[Y].

where
\xi_{i}
is the
i^{th}
marginal of
\xi
, and
\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{1}}[X]
and
\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{2}}[Y]
are the means of
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
, respectively.
We also consider

\ell_{2}(x,y,\xi)=2Qx+\Big[2\bar{Q}-\frac{\bar{S}^{2}}{2(R+\bar{R})}\Big]\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{1}}[X]+Ay+\Big[\bar{A}-\frac{\bar{S}(B+\bar{B})}{2(R+\bar{R})}\Big]\mathbb{E}^{\xi_{2}}[Y].

With these notation, consider the PDE

(32)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}V(t,x,\mu)+\partial_{x}V(t,x,\mu)\ell_{1}(x,V(t,x,\mu),\xi)+\ell_{2}(x,V(t,x,\mu),\xi)\\
\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\partial_{\mu}V(t,x,\mu)\cdot\ell_{1}(y,V(t,x,\mu),\xi)d\mu(y)\\
+\frac{1}{2}tr\Big[\partial_{xx}V(t,x,\mu)\sigma(x)\sigma^{\prime}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\partial_{y}\partial_{\mu}V(t,x,\mu)(y)\sigma(x)\sigma^{\prime}(x)\,d\mu(y)\Big]=0\\
\quad V(T,x,\mu)=2Q_{T}x+2\bar{Q}_{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}x\mu(dx)\end{cases}

with
\xi=\text{law}(\chi,V(t,\chi,\mu))
when
\chi\sim\mu
.
The main result of this section is the following:

Theorem 7
.

Assume that
R\neq 0
,
R+\bar{R}\neq 0
.
If the
N
-player problem (
29
) admits an optimal control
(\hat{\alpha}^{i,N})_{i=1,\dots,N}
, then, there is
\delta>0
such that if
T\leq\delta
, the sequence
(L^{N}(\underline{X}^{\hat{}\underline{\alpha}}))
satisfies the LDP with rate function

(33)

\mathcal{J}(\theta):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}:\,\mathrm{law}(X^{u})=\theta}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|u_{t}|^{2}\,dt\bigg],

where
dX^{u}_{t}=\overline{\ell}_{1}(t,X^{u}_{t},\mathcal{L}(X^{u}_{t}))+\sigma u_{t}\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t}
and
\overline{\ell}_{1}
is the function defined by

(34)

\overline{\ell}_{1}(t,x,\xi):=\ell_{1}\Big(x,V(t,x,\xi_{1}),\mathcal{L}(\chi,V(t,\chi,\xi_{1}))\Big)\quad\text{with}\quad\chi\sim\xi_{1}\

and
\xi_{1}
the first marginal of
\xi
.

Proof.

Let us put

\overline{x}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x^{j},\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\alpha}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha^{j}.

It was shown in
[
35
, Section 5.3.2]
that, if there is an optimal control
(\hat{\alpha}^{1,N},\dots,\hat{\alpha}^{N,N})
, then it satisfies

(35)

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}^{i,N}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2R}\left[BY^{i}_{t}+\bar{S}\left(1-\frac{\bar{R}}{R+\bar{R}}\right)\overline{X_{t}}^{N}+\left(\bar{B}-\frac{\bar{R}}{R+\bar{R}}(B+\bar{B})\right)\overline{Y_{t}}^{N}\right]

\displaystyle=\ell(Y_{t}^{i},\overline{X}_{t}^{N},\overline{Y}_{t}^{N})

for every
i=1,\dots,N
,
where the processes
(X^{i,N},Y^{i,N},Z^{i,j,N})
satisfy the FBSDE system

\displaystyle dX^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left[AX^{i}_{t}+\bar{A}\overline{X_{t}}^{N}+B\hat{\alpha}^{i}_{t}+\bar{B}\overline{\hat{\alpha}_{t}}^{N}\right]\,dt+\sigma\,dW^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle dY^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\left\{2QX^{i}_{t}+\left[2\bar{Q}-\frac{\bar{S}^{2}}{2(R+\bar{R})}\right]\overline{X_{t}}^{N}+AY^{i}_{t}+\left[\bar{A}-\frac{\bar{S}(B+\bar{B})}{2(R+\bar{R})}\right]\overline{Y_{t}}^{N}\right\}dt+\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z^{i,k}_{t}dW^{k}_{t}

\displaystyle X^{i}_{0}=x,\quad Y^{i}_{T}=2Q_{T}X_{T}^{i}+2\bar{Q}_{T}\overline{X}^{N}_{T}.

This is essentially a consequence of Pontryagin’s maximum principle.
On the other hand, using again the maximum principle, the optimal control
\hat{\alpha}
of the McKean–Vlasov control problem satisfies

\hat{\alpha}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2R}\left[BY_{t}+\bar{S}\left(1-\frac{\bar{R}}{R+\bar{R}}\right)\mathbb{E}[X_{t}]+\left(\bar{B}-\frac{\bar{R}}{R+\bar{R}}(B+\bar{B})\right)\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]\right]

where
(X,Y,Z)
solves the McKean-Vlasov FBSDE

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=\Big\{AX_{t}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}[X_{t}]+B\hat{\alpha}_{t}+\bar{B}\mathbb{E}[\hat{\alpha}_{t}]\Big\}\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t}

(36)

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle=-\left\{2QX_{t}+\Big[2\bar{Q}-\frac{\bar{S}^{2}}{2(R+\bar{R})}\Big]\mathbb{E}[X_{t}]+AY_{t}+\Big[\bar{A}-\frac{\bar{S}(B+\bar{B})}{2(R+\bar{R})}\Big]\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]\right\}dt+Z_{t}dW_{t}

\displaystyle X_{0}=x,\quad Y_{T}=2Q_{T}X_{T}+2\bar{Q}_{T}\mathbb{E}[X_{T}],

see
[
35
]
.
This is a fully coupled McKean–Vlasov FBSDE system with linear coefficients.
The existence of a unique solution of this equation is guaranteed e.g. by
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
if
T
is small enough.
Furthermore,
it follows by
[
16
, Theorem 2.7, Proposition 5.2]
that if
T
is sufficiently small, then the solution
V
of Equation
32
exists, is Lipschitz–continuous in
(x,\mu)
and it satisfies
V(t,\chi,\mu)=Y_{t}^{t,\chi,\mu}
, which is the solution of (
36
) such that
X_{t}=\chi
and
\mathcal{L}(\chi)=\mu
.
Therefore, the process
X
satisfies the (decoupled) equation

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=\ell_{1}\big(X_{t},V(t,X_{t},\mathcal{L}(X_{t})),\mathcal{L}(X_{t},V(t,X_{t},\mathcal{L}(X_{t})))\big)\,dt+\sigma\,dW_{t}

\displaystyle=\overline{\ell}_{1}(t,X_{t},\mathcal{L}(X_{t}))+\sigma\,dW_{t}.

Now, consider the interacting particle system

d\mathcal{X}^{i,N}_{t}=\overline{\ell}_{1}\big(\mathcal{X}_{t}^{i,N},L^{N}(\underline{\mathcal{X}}_{t}))\big)+\sigma\,dW_{t}^{i}

which is well defined, since
\overline{\ell}_{1}
is Lipschitz–continuous, as the composition of two Lipschitz–continuous functions.
It follows by
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
that the sequence
(L^{N}(\underline{\mathcal{X}}))_{N}
satisfies the LDP with rate function
\mathcal{J}
.
It is checked exactly as in the proof of Theorem
2
that
\mathcal{J}
has compact sublevel sets.
Thus, it remains to show that
X^{i,N}
and
\mathcal{X}^{i,N}
are exponentially close.
It follows from a similar argument as in Lemma
5
that

(37)

\sup_{N}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i,N}-\mathcal{X}^{i,N}|\Big\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})}+\sup_{N}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}|Y^{i,i}-V(\cdot,\mathcal{X}^{i,N},L^{N}(\underline{\mathcal{X}}))|\Big\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})}<\infty.

Subsequently using Chebyshev’s inequality and (
37
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{W}_{2}(L^{N}(\underline{X}_{t}),L^{N}(\underline{\mathcal{X}}_{t}))>\varepsilon\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big\{\frac{1}{N}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i,N}_{t}-\mathcal{X}^{i}_{t}|^{2}\Big\}^{1/2}\geq\varepsilon\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big\{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|X^{\underline{\alpha},i}-\mathcal{X}^{i}\|^{2}_{\infty}\Big)\Big\}\Big]e^{-\varepsilon^{2}N^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon^{2}N^{2}}

from which we deduce that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{W}_{1}(L^{N}(\underline{X}_{t}),L^{N}(\underline{\mathcal{X}}_{t}))\geq\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

Therefore, it follows by
[
20
, Theorem 4.2.13]
that the sequence
(L^{N}(\underline{X}))
satisfies the LDP with rate function
\mathcal{J}
.
∎

Representations of almost regular m m -gonal forms I I
Dayoon Park

Abstract
. It is known that any m m -gonal form of rank ⁡ n ≥ 5 \operatorname{rank}n\geq 5 is almost regular. In this article, we study the sufficiently large integers which are represented by (almost regular) m m -gonal forms of rank ⁡ n ≥ 6 \operatorname{rank}n\geq 6 .

Introduction

The
m
-gonal number

m
-gon.
We especially call the total number of dots to constitute a regular
m
-gon with

-gonal number

The

-gonal number which was designed in 2nd century BC has been an interesting target in number theory for a long time.
A Fermat’s conjecture which states that every positive integer written as a sum of at most
m

m
-gonal numbers was resolved by Lagrange, Gauss, and Cauchy for
m=4
,
m=3
, and for all
m\geq 3
, respectively.

By definition of
m
-gonal number, only positive integer
x
would be admitted in (
1.1
).
But we may naturally generalize the
m
-gonal number by admitting every rational integer
x
in (
1.1
).
Very recently, as a general version of Fermat’s conjecture, the author completed the optimal
\ell_{m}
for which every positive integer is written as a sum of
\ell_{m}
generalized
m
-gonal numbers for all
m\geq 3
in
[
12
]
.
We call a weighted sum of
m
-gonal numbers

(1.2)

-gonal form

(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}

.
If an

When

.
If an
m
-gonal form

If an

.
The author and Kim determined every type of regular

-gonal form of

On the other hand, there are some results on the sufficiently large integers which are represented by

2.
preliminaries

Before we move on, we set our languages and notations which are used throughout this article.
Basically, our languages and notations follow conventional that in the arithmetic theory of quadratic form.
A
quadratic form over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
)
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
)-module
L
equipped with a quadratic form
Q:L\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) and the corresponding symmetric bilinear map
B(x,y):=\frac{1}{2}(Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y))
.
For a quadratic form
L={\mathbb{Z}}v_{1}+\cdots+{\mathbb{Z}}v_{n}
of
\operatorname{rank}n
, we usually identify
L
with its
Gram-matrix
\begin{pmatrix}B(v_{i},v_{j})\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n}
.
When
L
is a binary (i.e.,
n=2
) quadratic form over
{\mathbb{Z}}
, we adopt the notation
[Q(v_{1}),B(v_{1},v_{2}),Q(v_{2})]
instead of
\begin{pmatrix}B(v_{i},v_{j})\end{pmatrix}_{2\times 2}
.
We write an
m
-gonal form

(2.1)

a_{1}P_{m}(x_{1})+\cdots+a_{n}P_{m}(x_{n})

simply as
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>_{m}
.
When
m=4
, we conventionally denote the square form (i.e., diagonal quadratic form) of (
2.1
) by
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>
instead of
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>_{4}
.

Note that an integer
A(m-2)+B
is represented by an
m
-gonal form

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}P_{m}(x_{i})=\frac{m-2}{2}\{(a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2})-(a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n})\}+(a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n})

over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
)
if and only if the diophantine system

(2.2)

\begin{cases}a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}=2A+B+k(m-4)\\
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}=B+k(m-2)\end{cases}

is solvable for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
).
The system (
2.2
) is solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) if and only if

(2.3)

\left(B+k(m-2)-\left(\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\right)\right)^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}a_{1}a_{i}x_{i}^{2}=2Aa_{1}+Ba_{1}+k(m-4)a_{1}

is solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

The left hand side of the equation (
2.3

Q_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}}((x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})-(B+k(m-2))(r_{2},\cdots,r_{n}))+(B+k(m-2))^{2}\cdot\left(1-\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}r_{i}\right)

where
Q_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}}(x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}):=\sum_{i=2}^{n}(a_{1}a_{i}+a_{i}^{2})x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n}2a_{i}a_{j}x_{i}x_{j}
is a quadratic form and
r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
are the solution for

\begin{cases}(a_{1}a_{2}+a_{2}^{2})r_{2}+a_{2}a_{3}r_{3}+\cdots+a_{2}a_{n}r_{n}=a_{2}\\
a_{2}a_{3}r_{2}+(a_{1}a_{3}+a_{3}^{2})r_{3}+\cdots+a_{3}a_{n}r_{n}=a_{3}\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\vdots\\
a_{2}a_{n}r_{2}+a_{3}a_{n}r_{3}+\cdots+(a_{1}a_{n}+a_{n}^{2})r_{n}=a_{n}.\end{cases}

Note that since

(2.4)

Q_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}a_{2}+a_{2}^{2}&a_{2}a_{3}&\cdots&a_{2}a_{n}\\
a_{2}a_{3}&a_{1}a_{3}+a_{3}^{2}&\cdots&a_{3}a_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{2}a_{n}&a_{3}a_{n}&\cdots&a_{1}a_{n}+a_{n}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

is a sub-quadratic form of rank

of the positive definite diagonal quadratic form

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{1}a_{i}x_{i}^{2}
satisfying

we have that the determinant of

is positive.
We denote the non-zero determinant of

as

.

By Theorem 4.9 in
[
1
]
, since every quadratic polynomial of rank greater than or equal to

, we may get an absolute constant

(2.5)

which is dependent on

if the equation (

.

And then we may observe that an integer

) with

-gonal form

A\geq\frac{1}{2a_{1}}\left(C(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})+(B+k(m-2))^{2}\cdot\left(1-\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}r_{i}\right)\right)-B-k(m-2).

Throughout this article, we show that for any integer

with
0\leq B\leq m-3
which is locally represented by
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>_{m}
with

for which

2.2

.

3.
Local representation

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})=a_{1}P_{m}(x_{1})+\cdots+a_{n}P_{m}(x_{n})
be a primitive
m
-gonal form.

(1)

When
p
is an odd prime with
p|m-2
,
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is universal over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

(2)

When
m\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}
,
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is universal over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

(3)

When
p
is an odd prime with
(p,m-2)=1
, an integer
N
is represented by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
if and only if the integer
8(m-2)N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})(m-4)^{2}
is represented by the diagonal quadratic form
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

(4)

When
m\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, an integer
N
is represented by
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
if and only if the integer
2(m-2)N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\left(\frac{m-4}{2}\right)^{2}
is represented by the diagonal quadratic form
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

Proof.

(1) One may easily show that for any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
P_{m}(x)=N
is solovable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
by using Hensel’s Lemma (13:9 in
[
7
]
).
Which yields the claim from the assumption that
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is primitive.

(2) For any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, the equation
P_{m}(x)=N
has a
2
-adic integer solution
\frac{m-4-\sqrt{(m-4)^{2}-8N(m-2)}}{2(m-2)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
Which yields the claim from the assumption that
F_{m}(\mathbf{x})
is primitive.

(3) Note that

N=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}P_{m}(x_{i})

if and only if

8(m-2)N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})(m-4)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(2(m-2)x_{i}-(m-4))^{2}

where
2(m-2)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
.
It completes the proof.

(4) Note that

N=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}P_{m}(x_{i})

if and only if

2(m-2)N+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\left(\frac{m-4}{2}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\left((m-2)x_{i}-\frac{m-4}{2}\right)^{2}

where
m-2\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{\times}
and
\frac{m-4}{2}\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
It completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For an odd prime
p
, if there are more than four units of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
in
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
with
n\geq 6
by admitting a recursion, then

\begin{cases}a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}=2A+B+k(m-4)\\
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}=B+k(m-2)\end{cases}

is solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

for any
A,B,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

First assume that
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
Then by using 82:15 and 91:9 in
[
7
]
, we may obtain that for any
A,B,k
,
the binary quadratic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-lattice

[a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n},B+k(m-2),2A+B+k(m-4)]\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

is represented as

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&a_{2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&a_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{1}\\
1&x_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
1&x_{n}\end{pmatrix}

for some
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
since every quaternary unimodular
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-lattice is universal.
Which yields the claim.

Now assume that
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
Without loss of generality, let
0=\text{ord}_{p}(a_{1})
.
Then through same arguments with the above, one may induce that

\begin{cases}a_{2}x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}=2A+B+k(m-4)\\
a_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}=B+k(m-2)\end{cases}

is solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

for any
A,B,k
since
a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and every ternary unimodular
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-lattice is universal.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime with
(p,m-2)=1
.
If an integer
A(m-2)+B
is represented by
\left<a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\right>_{m}
of
\operatorname{rank}n\geq 6
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
then there is a residue
k_{m;\mathbf{a},A,B}(p)
in
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{2\text{ord}_{p}(d_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}})}{\mathbb{Z}}
for which

\begin{cases}a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}=2A+B+k(m-4)\\
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}=B+k(m-2)\end{cases}

is solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
for any
k\equiv k_{m;\mathbf{a},A,B}(p)\pmod{p^{2\text{ord}_{p}(d_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}})}}
.

Proof.

It would be enough to show that if

\begin{cases}a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}=2A+B+k(m-4)\\
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}=B+k(m-2)\end{cases}

is solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, then

\begin{cases}a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{2}=2A+B+k^{\prime}(m-4)\\
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}=B+k^{\prime}(m-2)\end{cases}

is also solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
for any
k^{\prime}\in k+p^{2\text{ord}_{p}(d_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}})}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
In other ways, we show that if the equation

Q_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x}-(B+k(m-2))\mathbf{r})=a_{1}(2A+B+k(m-4))-(B+k(m-2))^{2}\cdot\left(1-\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}r_{i}\right)

is solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, then the equation

Q_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x}-(B+k^{\prime}(m-2))\mathbf{r})=a_{1}(2A+B+k^{\prime}(m-4))-(B+k^{\prime}(m-2))^{2}\cdot\left(1-\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}r_{i}\right)

is also solvable over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
for any
k^{\prime}\in k+p^{2\text{ord}_{p}(d_{a_{1};\mathbf{a}})}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
In virtue of the
Jordan decomposition
(see 91 in
[
7
]
), we may take
T\in GL_{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
for which

T^{t}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}a_{2}+a_{2}^{2}&a_{2}a_{3}&\cdots&a_{2}a_{n}\\

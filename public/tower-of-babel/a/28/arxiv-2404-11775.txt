Abstract
A multi-species Fokker-Planck model for simulating particle collisions in a plasma is presented. The model includes various parameters that must be tuned. Under reasonable assumptions on these parameters, the model satisfies appropriate conservation laws, dissipates an entropy, and satisfies an ℋ \mathcal{H} -Theorem. In addition, the model parameters provide the additional flexibility that is used to match simultaneously momentum and temperature relaxation formulas derived from the Boltzmann collision operator for a binary mixture with Coulomb potentials. A numerical method for solving the resulting space-homogeneous kinetic equation is presented and two examples are provided to demonstrate the relaxation of species bulk velocities and temperatures to their equilibrium values.



Keywords:
Multi-species kinetic models, Fokker-Planck, Lenard-Bernstein, Dougherty, moment equations, entropy, conservation laws, plasma physics

Introduction

In this paper, we propose and analyze a multi-species version of a kinetic collision model for plasma physics applications, often referred to as the Lenard-Bernstein
[
15
]
or Dougherty model
[
5
]
.
The single-species version of this model (which we refer to as LB) is a nonlinear Fokker-Planck operator that is used as a computationally cheaper surrogate for the full Landau-Fokker-Planck (LFP) operator
[
14
]
.
Roughly speaking, the LB model replaces the nonlocal advection and diffusion terms of the LFP operator by parameters that depend only on the bulk velocity and temperature of the underlying kinetic distribution
[
16
]
, while maintaining the conservation, entropy dissipation, and equilibrium properties of the original LFP operator.
In this way, the approximation of LFP by LB is similar in spirit to the approximation of the Boltzmann collision operator (see e.g,
[
4
]
) by the Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK) model
[
1
]
.

Recently, there have been efforts to extend the LB operator to the multi-species case
[
20
,
7
]
(see
[
12
]
for the well-posedness result of a similar model) in a manner that maintains relevant (global) conservation properties, entropy dissipation, and an
\mathcal{H}
-Theorem that uniquely characterizes equilibrium states.
These multi-species models are written as a sum of Fokker-Planck operators, with coefficients that can be interpreted as tunable collision frequencies.
Each Fokker-Planck term is parameterized by species bulk velocity and temperature in the case of intra-species collisions or by mixture values for the bulk velocity and temperature in the case of inter-species collisions.
The choice of mixture values uses the strategy for the multi-species BGK (M-BGK) model introduced in
[
9
]
, which preserves the aforementioned structural properties.
However, like
[
9
]
, the models in
[
20
,
7
]
, can only match the pairwise relaxation rate (derived from the Boltzmann collision operator for a binary mixture with Coulomb potential)

This limitation is a consequence of the fact that mixture values for the bulk velocity and temperature are assumed to be symmetric with respect to a permutation in indices between two species.

A more general M-BGK model was introduced in
[
13
]
(see also
[
2
]
) that does not make the symmetry assumption in
[

.
Rather, the model in
[

[
9

as a special case, introduces additional parameters in the mixture values to enable matching of pairwise bulk velocity and temperature relaxation rates at the same time.
In this work, we take a similar approach and construct a more general multi-species LB (M-LB) model that satisfies the requisite conservation, entropy dissipation, and equilibrium (
\mathcal{H}
-Theorem) properties, but contains additional parameters for matching of velocity and temperature relaxation rates at the same time.
The parameters however, have constraints; thus a principled approach for matching must be carried out.
We develop a procedure for doing so, matching the rates of the M-LB model with those derived from the Boltzmann collision operator with Coulomb potentials.
We then illustrate the application of the matching procedure with several numerical experiments.

2
Multi-species space homogeneous Lenard-Bernstein equation

Let
f_{i}(t,\boldsymbol{v})
denote the kinetic distribution of particles of species
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
having mass
m_{i}
.
Associated to each
f_{i}
are the species number density
n_{i}
, mass density
\rho_{i}
, bulk velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
, and temperature
T_{i}
, defined by

\rho_{i}=m_{i}n_{i}=m_{i}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f_{i}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v},\;\;\;\boldsymbol{u}_{i}=\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}m_{i}\boldsymbol{v}f_{i}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v},\;\;\;T_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}d}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\left|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\right|^{2}f_{i}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}.

(1)

The species energy densities,
E_{i}
, are given by

E_{i}=\frac{1}{2}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}m_{i}|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}f_{i}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{i}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{d}{2}n_{i}T_{i}.

(2)

Definition 2.1
.

For
n>0
,
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\theta>0
, define the Maxwellian

M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}=\frac{n}{(2\pi\theta)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}}{2\theta}\right).

(3)

Lemma 2.2
.

For a general Maxwellian
M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}
,

\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n,\qquad{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\boldsymbol{v}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n\boldsymbol{u},

(4)

\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n|\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}+dn\theta,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\Delta_{\boldsymbol{v}}\log(M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta})=-\frac{d}{\theta}.

(5)

Definition 2.3
.

The (space homogeneous) multi-species Lenard Bernstein (M-LB) model is

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{i,j}(f_{i},f_{j}),\qquad i=1,\cdots,N,

(6)

where, for
1\leq i,j\leq N
, the pairwise collision operators
\mathcal{C}_{i,j}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{i,j}(f_{i},f_{j})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lambda_{i,j}\theta_{i,j}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}}{\theta_{i,j}}f_{i}+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f_{i}\right)=\lambda_{i,j}\theta_{i,j}\mathcal{L}(f_{i},M_{i,j}),

(7a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f,M)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot\left(M\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\frac{f}{M}\right)\right)=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot\left[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f-f\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}(\log(M))\right].

(7b)

The parameters
\lambda_{i,j}\geq 0
are the frequencies of collisions between species
i
and
j
.
The mixture Maxwellians are given by

M_{i,j}\coloneqq M_{n_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j},\theta_{i,j}},\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad\theta_{i,j}\coloneqq T_{i,j}/m_{i},

(8)

and the mixture Maxwellian parameters are given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha_{i,j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}+(1-\alpha_{i,j})\boldsymbol{u}_{j},

(9a)

\displaystyle T_{i,j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\beta_{i,j}T_{i}+(1-\beta_{i,j})T_{j}+\frac{1}{d}\gamma_{i,j}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{2},

(9b)

where the parameters
\alpha_{i,j}
,
\beta_{i,j}
, and
\gamma_{i,j}
are to be determined.

Remark 2.4
.

When
i=j
, we often drop the second index.
Thus, for example,
M_{i,i}=M_{i}
is the unique Maxwellian associated to
f_{i}
with bulk velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{i,i}=\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
and temperature
T_{i,i}=T_{i}
.

3
Conservation laws and mixture parameter constraints

Throughout the paper, we make the following assumption, which allows us to use the chain rule for time derivatives, apply integration by parts in velocity, and, when doing the latter, set any boundary terms that arise to zero.
While more precise conditions can be formulated, we do not do so here.

Assumption 3.1
.

For each
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,
f_{i}>0
is a smooth function of
\boldsymbol{v}
and continuously differentiable in
t
.
For each
t
,
f_{i}
decays exponentially to zero as
|\boldsymbol{v}|\to\infty
.

The M-LB model satisfies conservation of species mass:
m_{i}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\mathcal{C}_{i,j}(f_{i},f_{j})\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=0
.
We assume further that it satisfies the pairwise conservation of momentum and energy.

Assumption 3.2
.

The collision operators in (
6
) satisfy the following invariance properties, which correspond to pairwise momentum conservation and pairwise energy conservation, respectively.
Specifically, for any
i,j
,

\displaystyle m_{i}\langle\!\;\boldsymbol{v}\mathcal{C}_{i,j}(f_{i},f_{j})\!\;\rangle+m_{j}\langle\!\;\boldsymbol{v}\mathcal{C}_{j,i}(f_{j},f_{i})\!\;\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0},

(10a)

\displaystyle m_{i}\left\langle\frac{|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}}{2}\mathcal{C}_{i,j}(f_{i},f_{j})\right\rangle+m_{j}\left\langle\frac{|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}}{2}\mathcal{C}_{j,i}(f_{j},f_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

(10b)

where brackets are used as a shorthand for velocity integration, i.e.,

\langle\!\;\boldsymbol{g}\!\;\rangle={\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}{\boldsymbol{g}}({\boldsymbol{v}})\,\textnormal{d}{\boldsymbol{v}}
.

Evaluation of these conservation laws relies on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,

\displaystyle\langle\!\;m_{i}\mathcal{L}(f_{i},M_{i,j})\!\;\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

(11a)

\displaystyle\langle\!\;m_{i}\boldsymbol{v}\mathcal{L}(f_{i},M_{i,j})\!\;\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho_{i}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j})}{\theta_{i,j}},

(11b)

\displaystyle\left\langle m_{i}\frac{|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}}{2}\mathcal{L}(f_{i},M_{i,j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j})}{\theta_{i,j}}-\frac{d\rho_{i}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i,j})}{\theta_{i,j}}.

(11c)

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward, but tedious computation, using integration by parts several times.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Given the definitions of
\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}
and
T_{i,j}
in (
9
), the pairwise conservation of momentum and energy in
Assumption

3.2
holds if and only if the following parameter relationships hold:

\displaystyle\alpha_{j,i}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}}{\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}(1-\alpha_{i,j}),

(12a)

\displaystyle\beta_{j,i}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}}{n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}(1-\beta_{i,j}),

(12b)

\displaystyle\gamma_{j,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}(\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(1-\alpha_{i,j})-n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\gamma_{i,j}).

(12c)

Proof.

We show that
Assumption

3.2
implies (
12
).
The converse follows by simply reversing the order of computations.
According to (
11b
), the conservation of momentum (
10a
) implies that

\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}\boldsymbol{u}_{j,i}=\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}\boldsymbol{u}_{j}.

(13)

Rearranging this equation to isolate
\boldsymbol{u}_{j,i}
and applying (
9a
) yields (
12b
).
By (
11c
), the conservation of energy (
10b
) implies that

n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}T_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}T_{j,i}=\frac{1}{d}\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(1-\alpha_{i,j})|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{2}+n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}T_{i}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}T_{j}.

(14)

Rearranging this equation to isolate
T_{j,i}
and using (
9b
) yields (
12b
) and (
12c
).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The relations in (
12
) can be rearranged as follows, in order to highlight the symmetries in the model:

\displaystyle\delta_{i,j}\coloneqq\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(1-\alpha_{i,j})

\displaystyle=\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}(1-\alpha_{j,i})\eqqcolon\delta_{j,i},

(15a)

\displaystyle n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(1-\beta_{i,j})

\displaystyle=n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}(1-\beta_{j,i}),

(15b)

\displaystyle\delta_{i,j}-2n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\gamma_{i,j}

\displaystyle=2n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}\gamma_{j,i}-\delta_{j,i}.

(15c)

Remark 3.6
.

If we constrain the mixture velocity terms to enforce the symmetry
\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}=\boldsymbol{u}_{j,i}
, then
\alpha_{i,j}+\alpha_{j,i}=1
, and

\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}=\alpha_{i,j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}+\alpha_{j,i}\boldsymbol{u}_{j},\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad\alpha_{i,j}=\frac{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}.

(16)

This is the same expression for the mixture velocity as in

]
.
If, in addition, we require

, then
\beta_{i,j}+\beta_{j,i}=1

, and

T_{i,j}=\beta_{i,j}T_{i}+\beta_{j,i}T_{j}+\frac{1}{d}\frac{\delta_{i,j}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{2}}{\lambda_{i,j}n_{i}+\lambda_{j,i}n_{j}},

(17)

where

\beta_{i,j}=\frac{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\delta_{i,j}=\frac{\rho_{i}\rho_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\lambda_{j,i}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}.

(18)

In this case

\displaystyle T_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}T_{i}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}T_{j}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}+\frac{1}{d}\frac{\rho_{i}\rho_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\lambda_{j,i}}{(\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{i,j})(n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i})}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{2},

(19)

which is the mixture temperature in
[
9
]
.

The following moment ODE system, describing the time evolution of the momenta, and energies can be derived (see

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(1-\alpha_{i,j})(\boldsymbol{u}_{j}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}),

(20a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{i}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=d\sum_{j}n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(1-\beta_{i,j})(T_{j}-T_{i})

(20b)

\displaystyle\quad+\!\sum_{j}\bigg[(\delta_{i,j}-n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\gamma_{i,j})\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}_{j}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i})-(\delta_{j,i}-n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}\gamma_{j,i})\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j})\bigg].

), the following ODE describing the temperature can be derived:

\frac{d}{2}\frac{\partial(n_{i}T_{i})}{\partial t}=d\sum_{j}n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(1-\beta_{i,j})(T_{j}-T_{i})+\sum_{j}n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\gamma_{i,j}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{2}.

(21)

Lemma 3.7
.

The ODE system (
20
) satisfies the conservation of total momentum and total energy:

\sum_{i}\frac{\partial(\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})}{\partial t}=\mathbf{0}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{i}\frac{\partial E_{i}}{\partial t}=0,

(22)

and the quantities

\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}=\frac{\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}}{\sum_{i}\rho_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad T^{\infty}=\frac{\sum_{i}n_{i}T_{i}}{\sum_{i}n_{i}}+\frac{\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\left(|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}\right)}{d\sum_{i}n_{i}}>0

are independent of time.

Proof.

To verify the conservation of total momentum, sum (

) over all species

) to obtain

\sum_{i}\frac{\partial(\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})}{\partial t}=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(1-\alpha_{i,j})(\boldsymbol{u}_{j}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i})=\mathbf{0}.

(24)

Next, to verify the conservation of total energy, sum (

) over
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
and use (

).
Finally, the time invariance of

and

follow directly from (

), and the positivity of

follows as in

4
Entropy dissipation and
\mathcal{H}
-Theorem

In this section, we state and prove an entropy dissipation result and an
\mathcal{H}
-Theorem, under the following physically motivated assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

Motivated by physical principles, we assume that for
1\leq i,j\leq N
,

0\leq\alpha_{i,j}\,,\,\beta_{i,j}\leq 1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\gamma_{i,j}\geq 0.

(25)

Definition 4.2
.

The total entropy functional for a particle system with kinetic distribution
\boldsymbol{f}=[f_{1},\cdots f_{N}]^{\top}
is given by

\mathcal{H}[\boldsymbol{f}]=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}(f_{i}\log(f_{i})-f_{i})\,\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\!\;f_{i}\log(f_{i})-f_{i}\!\;\rangle.

(26)

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}=[f_{1},\cdots f_{N}]^{\top}
satisfy the M-LB system
(
6
)-(
7
).
Then under the assumptions in (
25
),
\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial t}\leq 0
, with equality if and only if each
f_{i}
is a Maxwellian with common equilibrium velocity
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and equilibrium temperature
T>0
; that is,

\displaystyle f_{i}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=n_{i}\left(\frac{m_{i}}{2\pi T^{\infty}}\right)^{d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{m_{i}|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}}{2T^{\infty}}\right),\qquad 1\leq i\leq N,

(27)

where
\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}
and
T^{\infty}
are defined in (
23
).

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{H}
in (
26
) and the chain rule,

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial t}&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\!\;\log(f_{i})\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}\!\;\rangle\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\!\;\log(f_{i})\mathcal{C}_{i,i}(f_{i},f_{i})\!\;\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\langle\!\;\log(f_{i})\mathcal{C}_{i,j}(f_{i},f_{j})+\log(f_{j})\mathcal{C}_{j,i}(f_{j},f_{i})\!\;\rangle.\end{split}

(28)

Thus it is sufficient to show that

\langle\!\;\log(f_{i})\mathcal{C}_{i,i}(f_{i},f_{i})\!\;\rangle\leq 0,

(29)

with equality if and only if
f_{i}
takes the form in (
27
), and

\langle\!\;\log(f_{i})\mathcal{C}_{i,j}(f_{i},f_{j})+\log(f_{j})\mathcal{C}_{j,i}(f_{j},f_{i})\!\;\rangle\leq 0,

(30)

with equality if and only if
f_{i}
and
f_{j}
take the Maxwellian form in (
27
) with a common bulk velocity
\boldsymbol{u}
and temperature
T>0
.
We establish these conditions in the remainder of the section.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For the intra-species collisions,

\langle\!\;\log(f_{i})\mathcal{C}_{i,i}(f_{i},f_{i})\!\;\rangle\leq 0,

(31)

with equality if and only if
f_{i}=M_{i}
.

Proof.

Since

\log(M_{i})=\log\left(\frac{n_{i}}{(2\pi\theta_{i})^{d/2}}\right)-\frac{|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}}{2\theta_{i}},

(32)

it follows from
Lemma

3.3
that

\left\langle\log(M_{i})\mathcal{C}(f_{i},f_{i})\right\rangle=0.

(33)

Hence, after integrating by parts,

\begin{split}\left\langle\log(f_{i})\mathcal{C}_{i,i}(f_{i},f_{i})\right\rangle&=\left\langle\log\left(\frac{f_{i}}{M_{i}}\right)\mathcal{C}_{i,i}(f_{i},f_{i})\right\rangle\\
=\lambda_{i,i}\theta_{i}&\left\langle\log\left(\frac{f_{i}}{M_{i}}\right)\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot\left(M_{i}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\frac{f}{M_{i}}\right)\right)\right\rangle=-\lambda_{i,i}\theta_{i}\left\langle\frac{M_{i}^{2}}{f}\left|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\frac{f_{i}}{M_{i}}\right)\right|^{2}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(34)

Thus the inequality in (
31
) holds, with equality if and only if
f_{i}/M_{i}
is constant with respect to
\boldsymbol{v}
.
Since
\langle\!\;f_{i}\!\;\rangle=\langle\!\;M_{i}\!\;\rangle
, this constant must be one, and the result follows.
∎

We now prove (
30
).
Several intermediate steps are required.

Lemma 4.5
.

For a general Maxwellian
M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}
,

\langle\!\;\log(f)\mathcal{L}(f,M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta})\!\;\rangle=-\langle\!\;f\Delta_{\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\log(f)-\log(M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta})\right)\!\;\rangle,

(35)

which implies that

\langle\!\;\log(f_{i})\mathcal{L}(f_{i},M_{i,j})\!\;\rangle-\langle\!\;\log(f_{i})\mathcal{L}(f_{i},M_{i,i})\!\;\rangle=\langle\!\;f_{i}\Delta_{\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\log(M_{i,i})-\log(M_{i,j})\right)\!\;\rangle.

(36)

Proof.

Using integration by parts,

\begin{split}\langle\!\;\log(f)\mathcal{L}(f,M)\!\;\rangle&=\langle\!\;\log(f)\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot\left[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f-f\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}(\log(M))\right]\!\;\rangle\\
&=-\langle\!\;\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\log(f)\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f\!\;\rangle+\langle\!\;f\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\log(f)\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\log(M)\!\;\rangle\\
&=-\langle\!\;\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\log(f)\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f\!\;\rangle+\langle\!\;\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\log(M)\!\;\rangle\\
&=\langle\!\;\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\log(M)-\log(f)\right)\!\;\rangle\\
&=-\langle\!\;f\Delta_{\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\log(f)-\log(M)\right)\!\;\rangle.\end{split}

(37)

Identity (
36
) follows immediately.

\begin{split}\left[\mathcal{L}_{i,j}\right]_{k}&=\left[\,\textnormal{d}_{v}\left(M_{i,j}\,\textnormal{d}_{v}\left(\frac{f_{i}}{M_{i,j}}\right)\right)\right]_{k}\\

(71)

where

a_{k-1}=\frac{[M_{i,j}]_{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{[M_{i,j}]_{k-1}},\qquad b_{k}=\frac{[M_{i,j}]_{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{[M_{i,j}]_{k}}+\frac{[M_{i,j}]_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{[M_{i,j}]_{k}},\qquad c_{k+1}=\frac{[M_{i,j}]_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{[M_{i,j}]_{k+1}},

(72)

and the dependence of

,

, and

(73)

(74)

0&-a_{2}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}&b_{3}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}&-c_{4}^{\mathfrak{n}+1}&0&\cdots&0\\

The values of the Maxwellians

8
Conclusions

In this paper, we have derived a multi-species Lenard-Bernstein (M-LB) model that satisfies (i) conservation of species mass and global conservation of momentum and energy; (ii) dissipates entropy and satisfies an H-Theorem; and (iii) matches the pairwise momentum and temperature relaxation rates of the Boltzmann collision operator with a Coulomb potential.
We have also demonstrated a simple numerical algorithm for implementing the (M-LB) model with implicit time stepping.
In future work, we hope to embed this algorithm into a full kinetic simulation capability, such as
[
6
]
or
[
19
]
.

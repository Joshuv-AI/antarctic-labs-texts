Systemic robustness: a mean-field particle system approach
Erhan Bayraktar, Gaoyue Guo, Wenpin Tang, Yuming Paul Zhang

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the problem of budget control in a large particle system modeled by stochastic differential equations involving hitting times, which arises from considerations of systemic risk in a regional financial network. Motivated by Tang and Tsai [ 32 ] (Ann. Probab., 46 (2018), pp. 1597–1650), we focus on the number or proportion of surviving entities that never default to measure the systemic robustness. First we show that both the mean-field particle system and its limit McKean-Vlasov equation are well-posed by virtue of the notion of minimal solutions. We then establish a connection between the proportion of surviving entities in the large particle system and the probability of default in the McKean-Vlasov equation as the size of the interacting particle system N N tends to infinity. Finally, we study the asymptotic efficiency of budget control in different economy regimes: the expected number of surviving entities is of constant order in a negative economy; it is of order N \sqrt{N} in a neutral economy; and it is of order N N in a positive economy where the budget’s effect is negligible.



Key words:
Budget control, drifted Brownian motion, hitting times, interacting particle systems, large system limits, McKean-Vlasov equation, mean-field interactions, systemic risk.

Given a generic Polish space

\displaystyle d_{u}(f,f^{\prime}):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\big(\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{n}\wedge 1\big)\quad\mbox{with }\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{n}:=\sup_{t\in[0,n]}|f_{t}-f^{\prime}_{t}|.

Denote by

(see e.g. Whitt

Moreover, we introduce the set

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}:=\big\{\ell\in{\cal D}\mbox{ is non-decreasing:}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\ell_{t}=:\ell_{\infty}\leq 1\mbox{ and }\ell_{t}=0,~\forall t\in[-1,0)\big\}

\displaystyle d(\ell,\ell^{\prime}):=\inf\big\{\varepsilon>0:~\ell_{t+\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\geq\ell^{\prime}_{t}\geq\ell_{t-\varepsilon}-\varepsilon,~\forall t\geq 0\big\},\quad\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}.

For each

Lemma

Lemma 1

For every

We have

\mathbb{M}
). For
L\in{\cal S}({\cal D})
, define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N}[L]_{t}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{L}_{i}\leq t\}},

where for
1\leq i\leq N
,

\displaystyle X^{L,i}_{t}:=Z^{i}+\beta t+B^{i}_{t}-G(L_{t})

and

\displaystyle\tau^{L}_{i}:=\inf\big\{t\geq 0:~X^{L,i}_{t}\leq 0\big\}.

For any
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in{\cal D}
(resp.
L,L^{\prime}\in{\cal S}({\cal D})
), we write
\ell\preceq\ell^{\prime}
(resp.
L\preceq L^{\prime}
)
if
\ell_{t}\leq\ell^{\prime}_{t}
for all
t\geq-1
(resp.
L_{t}\leq L^{\prime}_{t}
for all
t\geq-1
almost surely).
It is easy to verify that
\Gamma,\Gamma_{N}
are monotone with respect to
\preceq
, i.e.
\ell\preceq\ell^{\prime}\Longrightarrow\Gamma[\ell]\preceq\Gamma[\ell^{\prime}]
(resp.
L\preceq L^{\prime}\Longrightarrow\Gamma_{N}[L]\preceq\Gamma_{N}[L^{\prime}])
. In particular, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{M}
(resp.
L\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{M})
),
(X^{\ell},\ell)
(resp.
(X^{L,1},\ldots,X^{L,N},L)
) is a solution to (
3
) (resp. (
2
)) if and only if
\Gamma[\ell]=\ell
(resp.
\Gamma_{N}[L]=L
).

Our goal is to prove that
\Gamma
(resp.
\Gamma_{N}
) has a fixed point corresponding to the minimal solution.
To this end, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 7
.

For any
(\ell^{n},\,n\geq 1)\subset\mathbb{M}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{M}
satisfying
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{d}(\ell^{n},\ell)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}\leq\Gamma[\ell]_{t},\quad\mbox{for all }t\geq 0.

Proof.

Denote
g^{n}:=G(\ell^{n})
and
g:=G(\ell)
for all
n\geq 1
. By Lemma
1
, there exist
\theta^{n},\theta\in{\cal P}_{\leq}
such that
\ell^{n}_{t}=\theta^{n}([0,t])
and
\ell_{t}=\theta([0,t])
for all
t\geq 0
.
By the Portmanteau theorem, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\ell^{n}_{t}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\theta^{n}([0,t])\leq\theta([0,t])=\ell_{t},

which yields
\Gamma[\ell]_{t}=\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\ell}\leq t)\geq\mathbb{P}(C)
, where
C:=\{\exists s\in[0,t]:Z+\beta s+B_{s}\leq h_{s}\}
and
h_{t}:=G(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\ell^{n}_{t})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}g^{n}_{t}
. Then it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\ell^{n}}\leq t)\leq\mathbb{P}(C).

For each
t\geq 0
, set
h^{n}_{t}:=\sup_{k\geq n}g^{k}_{t}
so that
g^{n}_{t}\leq h^{n}_{t}\downarrow h_{t}
as
n\to\infty
. Hence,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\ell^{n}}\leq t)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\exists s\in[0,t]:Z+\beta s+B_{s}\leq h^{n}_{s})=\mathbb{P}(A),

where

\displaystyle A:=\{\forall n,~\exists s\in[0,t]:Z+\beta s+B_{s}\leq h^{n}_{s}\}=\{\forall n\geq m,~\exists s\in[0,t]:Z+\beta s+B_{s}\leq h^{n}_{s}\}.

Here
m
can be any positive integer.
Note that
h^{n}
and
h
are non-decreasing by definition, but may not be right-continuous. It is enough to show

\mathbb{P}(A\setminus C)=0
. Introducing an equivalent probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
defined by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\right)_{t}:=\exp(-\beta B_{t}-\beta^{2}t/2).

Then
\mathbb{P}(A\setminus C)=0\Longleftrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(A\setminus C)=0
,
and
(\beta s+B_{s})_{0\leq s\leq t}
is a Brownian motion under
\mathbb{Q}
.
Thus, it suffices to prove for the case
\beta=0
. Define

\displaystyle\tau^{n}:=\inf\{s\in[0,t]:~Z+B_{s}\leq h^{n}_{s}\},\quad\forall n\geq 1,

where we adopt the convention
\inf\emptyset:=\infty
. Clearly,
\tau^{n}\in[0,t]
on
A
.
By definition
h^{n}\downarrow h
as
n\to\infty
,
n\mapsto\tau^{n}
is non-decreasing, which implies the existence of
\tau:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau^{n}
. In particular,
\tau\in[0,t]
also holds on
A
.
In the following, we let the event
A
occur and distinguish four cases.

(i)

Suppose
\tau^{n}<\tau
for all
n\geq 1
.
By definition, there is some
t_{n}\in[\tau^{n},\tau)
such that
x+B_{t_{n}}\leq h^{n}_{t_{n}}\leq h^{n}_{\tau}
.
Hence,
x+B_{\tau}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(x+B_{t_{n}})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{n}_{\tau}=h_{\tau}
.
Thus, we get

\displaystyle A\cap\{\tau^{n}<\tau,~\forall n\geq 1\}\subseteq C.

(7)

(ii)

If
\tau^{n}=\tau=t
for some
n
, then we have
x+B_{t}\leq h^{n}_{t}
, and further
x+B_{t}\leq h_{t}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle A\cap\{\exists n:\tau^{n}=\tau=t\}\subseteq C.

(8)

(iii)

If
\tau^{n}=\tau<t
for some
n
, and
\tau
is a point of discontinuity of
h
,
then we have
x+B_{\tau}=x+B_{\tau^{n}}\leq h^{n}_{\tau^{n}+}=h^{n}_{\tau+}
. Define
\hat{h}_{s}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{n}_{s+}
, then
x+B_{\tau}\leq\hat{h}_{\tau}
. Namely,

\displaystyle x+B_{d}\leq\hat{h}_{d},

(9)

at some point
d\in D
, where
D
is the countable subset of points of discontinuity of
h
.
For all
0\leq u<s\leq t
, we have
h_{s}=\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{n}_{s}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{n}_{u+}=\hat{h}_{u}
. Now suppose that
C
does not occur, which implies that
x+B_{s}>h_{s}
for all
s\in[0,t]
and hence
x+B_{s}>\hat{h}_{d}
for all
d\in D
and
s\in(d,t]
.
By the local behavior of Brownian motion, for every
d\in[0,t)
the set

\{x+B_{d}\leq\hat{h}_{d},~x+B_{s}>\hat{h}_{d},\ \forall s\in(d,t]\}
is negligible. We conclude by (
9
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A\cap\{\exists n:\tau^{n}=\tau<t,~\tau\in D\}\setminus C)\leq\sum_{d\in D}\mathbb{P}(A\cap\{\exists n:\tau^{n}=\tau=d<t\}\setminus C)=0.

(10)

(iv)

Finally, suppose
\tau^{n^{*}}=\tau<t
for some
n^{*}
, and
\tau
is a point of continuity of
h
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
h_{\tau+\delta}\leq h_{\tau}+\varepsilon
.
Hence, for all
n\geq n^{*}
large enough,
\tau^{n^{*}}=\tau^{n}=\tau
and

\displaystyle x+B_{\tau}=x+B_{\tau^{n}}\leq h^{n}_{\tau^{n}+\delta}=h^{n}_{\tau+\delta}\to h_{\tau+\delta}\leq h_{\tau}+\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we get
x+B_{\tau}\leq h_{\tau}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle A\cap\{\exists n:\tau^{n}=\tau<t,~\tau\notin D\}\subseteq C.

(11)

Combining (
7
), (
8
), (
10
) and (
11
),
we conclude that
\mathbb{P}(A\setminus C)=0
and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\ell^{n}}\leq t)\leq\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\ell}\leq t)
.
∎

Next we show that
\Gamma:(\mathbb{M},\hat{d})\to(\mathbb{M},d)
is continuous.

Proposition 8
.

For any
(\ell^{n},\,n\geq 1)\subset\mathbb{M}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{M}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}d(\Gamma[\ell^{n}],\Gamma[\ell])=0
holds when
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{d}(\ell^{n},\ell)=0
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
X^{\ell^{n}}\equiv X^{n}
,
X^{\ell}\equiv X
,
\tau^{\ell^{n}}\equiv\tau^{n}
and
\tau^{\ell}\equiv\tau
.
We first show that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}=\Gamma[\ell]_{t}
for all
t\in J
,
where
J\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
denotes the collection of the points of continuity of
\Gamma[\ell]
.
By Lemma
7
, we have
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}\leq\Gamma[\ell]_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
,
and it remains to prove
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\Gamma[\ell]_{t}-\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}\big)^{+}=0
for
t\in J
.
Write

\displaystyle\big(\Gamma[\ell]_{t}-\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}\big)^{+}\leq\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t,\tau\leq t)=\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t|\tau=s)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]_{s}.

As in the proof of Lemma
7
, we denote
g^{n}:=G(\ell^{n})
and
g:=G(\ell)
.
We split the integrand in its continuous and jump part, writing
\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}
for the
continuous part, which we estimate in the following.

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t|\tau=s)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}(X^{n}_{u}>0,\forall u\leq t|\tau=s)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}(X^{n}_{s}+\beta(u-s)+B_{u}-B_{s}-(g^{n}_{u}-g^{n}_{s})>0,\forall s\leq u\leq t|\tau=s)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}(X^{n}_{s}+\beta(u-s)+B_{u}-B_{s}>0,\forall s\leq u\leq t|\tau=s)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}.

On the event
\{\tau=s\}
, we have
X_{s}\leq 0
, so

X^{n}_{s}\leq X^{n}_{s}-X_{s}=g_{s}-g^{n}_{s}
.
We conclude, using the reflection principle (see e.g.
[
26
, Section 2.8A]
),

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t|\tau=s)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}(\beta(u-s)+B_{u}-B_{s}>g^{n}_{s}-g_{s},\forall s\leq u\leq t|\tau=s)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{s\leq u\leq t}\big(\beta(u-s)+B_{u}-B_{s}\big)>g^{n}_{s}-g_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{0\leq u\leq t-s}\big(\beta u+B_{u}\big)>g^{n}_{s}-g_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\left({\cal N}\left(\frac{g_{s}-g^{n}_{s}+\beta(t-s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}\right)-e^{2\beta(g^{n}_{s}-g_{s})}{\cal N}\left(\frac{g^{n}_{s}-g_{s}+\beta(t-s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s},

where
{\cal N}
denotes the cumulative distribution function of standard normal.
Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}g^{n}_{s}=g_{s}
holds in a co-countable set, the integrand above converges
\Gamma[\ell]^{c}-
almost everywhere to
0
. Consequently, the integral above vanishes
as
n\to\infty
by the dominated convergence theorem.

For the integral with respect to the jump part, we get

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t|\tau=s)\mathrm{d}\big(\Gamma[\ell]_{s}-\Gamma[\ell]^{c}_{s}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s\leq t}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t|\tau=s)\Delta\Gamma[\ell]_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s<t}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t|\tau=s)\mathbb{P}(\tau=s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s<t}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t,\tau=s),

where we may take the sum over
s<t
because
t\in J
. For any
s>0
, we have by the Portmanteau theorem

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\ell^{n}_{s-}\geq\ell_{s-}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}g^{n}_{s-}\geq g_{s-}.

For
s<t
, we have
\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t,\tau=s)\leq\mathbb{P}(\forall\varepsilon\in(0,t-s):Z+\beta(s+\varepsilon)+B_{s+\varepsilon}\geq g^{n}_{(s+\varepsilon)-},\tau=s)
.
Taking the
\limsup
yields, by Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t,\tau=s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\forall\varepsilon\in(0,t-s):Z+\beta(s+\varepsilon)+B_{s+\varepsilon}\geq g_{(s+\varepsilon)-},\tau=s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z+\beta s+B_{s}\geq g_{s},\tau=s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z+\beta s+B_{s}=g_{s})=0.

Again by the dominated convergence theorem,
we get that the sum converges to zero as
n\to\infty
, which proves
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}=\Gamma[\ell]_{t}
. Hence,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{d}(\Gamma[\ell^{n}],\Gamma[\ell])=0
.

To show
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(\Gamma[\ell^{n}],\Gamma[\ell])=0
, it remains to show
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{\infty}=\Gamma[\ell]_{\infty}
.
This is straightforward when
\beta\leq 0
, and we assume
\beta>0
in the remaining of proof.
For each
z\in\mathbb{R}
, denote by
p(z)
the probability that
z+\beta t+B_{t}
never hits
(-\infty,0]
.
We have

p(z)=1-e^{-2\beta z^{+}}
, and further
\lim_{z\to\infty}p(z)=1
.
For each
t\geq 0
, we get

\displaystyle\big|\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{\infty}-\Gamma[\ell]_{\infty}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty)-\mathbb{P}(\tau=\infty)\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty)\big|+\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau>t)\big|+\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau=\infty)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty)\big|+\big|\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}-\Gamma[\ell]_{t}\big|+\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau=\infty)\big|.

Let us estimate the first and third terms.

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{n}>t\}}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{n}>t\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{n}=\infty\}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{n}>t\}}]-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty|\tau^{n}>t)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{n}>t\}}\big].

On the event
\{\tau^{n}>t\}
, we have

\displaystyle\{\tau^{n}=\infty\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{X^{n}_{t}+\beta(s-t)+(B_{s}-B_{t})-(g^{n}_{s}-g^{n}_{t})>0,\forall s\geq t\}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\{X^{n}_{t}-c+\beta(s-t)+(B_{s}-B_{t})>0,\forall s\geq t\},\quad\mbox{with }c:=G(1),

which yields by the Markov property
\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty|\tau^{n}>t)\geq p(X^{n}_{t}-c)
and thus

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty)\big|=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{n}>t\}}\big(1-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty|\tau^{n}>t)\big)\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{n}>t\}}\big(1-p(X^{n}_{t}-c)\big)\big].

Now we couple the process
X_{t}
by
Y_{t}:=Z+\beta t+B_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
,
so
X^{n}_{t}\geq Y_{t}-c
.
Let
\sigma:=\inf\{t\geq 0:Y_{t}\leq 0\}
, and we have
\tau^{n}\leq\sigma
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty)\big|\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{n}>t\}}\big(1-p(X^{n}_{t}-c)\big)\big]\leq\big|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-2c)\big)\big].

Similarly, we can establish

\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau=\infty)\big|\leq\big|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-2c)\big)\big]
, and thus

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{n}=\infty)-\mathbb{P}(\tau=\infty)\big|\leq 2\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-2c)\big)\big]+\big|\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}-\Gamma[\ell]_{t}\big|.

As
\lim_{t\to\infty}Y_{t}=\infty
holds almost surely, we get by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-2c)\big)\big]=0.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
t_{\varepsilon}
such that

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-2c)\big)\big]\leq\varepsilon
for all
t\geq t_{\varepsilon}
. Fix an arbitrary
t\in J
with
t>t_{\varepsilon}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{\infty}-\Gamma[\ell]_{\infty}\big|\leq 2\varepsilon+\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\Gamma[\ell^{n}]_{t}-\Gamma[\ell]_{t}\big|=2\varepsilon,

which yields the desired result.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

(i)  We start with the particle system (
2
).
For each
n\geq 0
, denote by
\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}
the
n
-th iterate of the operator
\Gamma_{N}
. First we show
\underline{L}^{N}:=\Gamma_{N}^{(N)}[{0}]
is the minimal solution to (
2
), where
0\in\mathbb{M}
is the function that is identically equal to zero. The monotonicity of
\Gamma_{N}
, combined with the fact
0\preceq\Gamma_{N}[0]=\Gamma_{N}^{(1)}[0]
, yields
\Gamma_{N}^{(1)}[0]\preceq\Gamma_{N}\big[\Gamma_{N}^{(1)}[0]\big]=\Gamma_{N}^{(2)}[0]
,
and thus by induction
\Gamma_{N}^{(n-1)}[0]\preceq\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[0]
for all
n\geq 1
. Note that
\underline{\Lambda}^{N}\in\mathbb{M}
only takes values in
\{k/N:k=0,\ldots,N\}
. For
0\leq n\leq N
, define the stopping times

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:~\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{t}\geq\frac{n}{N}\right\}\in[0,\infty].

We claim that (
12
) holds for all
1\leq n\leq N
:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N}^{(n-1)}[{0}]_{t}=\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{t},\quad\mbox{for all }t<\sigma_{n}.

(12)

Clearly,
\Gamma_{N}^{(0)}
denotes the identity operator. For
n=1
, observe that the first jump time
\sigma_{1}
of
\Gamma_{N}^{(0)}[0]
coincides with the first time at which any of the drifted Brownian motions
Z^{i}+\beta t+B^{i}_{t}
hits zero, and
\Gamma_{N}^{(1)}[0]
is equal to zero prior to the time
\sigma_{1}
, regardless of
\sigma_{1}<\infty
and
\sigma_{1}=\infty
.
This means that
\Gamma_{N}^{(0)}[0]=0=\Gamma_{N}^{(1)}[0]
holds for all
t<\sigma_{1}
.
For the inductive step, assume that (
12
) is true for all positive integers up to
n
.
Applying
\Gamma_{N}
to both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{t}=\Gamma_{N}^{(n+1)}[{0}]_{t},\quad\mbox{for all }t<\sigma_{n}.

We are done if
\sigma_{n}=\infty
, and it suffices to deal with the case
\sigma_{n}<\infty
.
We distinguish two cases. In the first case, we suppose
\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{\sigma_{n}}>n/N
.
Therefore, we have
\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{\sigma_{n}}\geq(n+1)/N
, and hence
\sigma_{n}=\sigma_{n+1}
, completing the inductive step.
For the second case, we have
\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{\sigma_{n}}=n/N
, and for
t\in(\sigma_{n},\sigma_{n+1})
,

\displaystyle\frac{n}{N}=\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{\sigma_{n}}\leq\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{t}\leq\Gamma_{N}^{(n+1)}[{0}]_{t}<\frac{n+1}{N},

which implies that
\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]
and
\Gamma_{N}^{(n+1)}[{0}]
agree on
(0,\sigma_{n+1})
, completing the inductive
step.
Next we prove that
\underline{L}^{N}=\Gamma_{N}^{(N)}[{0}]
solves the particle system. For
n\geq 0
, repeatedly applying
\Gamma_{N}
, we get

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N}^{(n)}[{0}]_{t}=\Gamma_{N}^{(n+1)}[{0}]_{t}=\cdots=\underline{L}^{N}_{t}=\Gamma_{N}[\underline{L}_{N}]_{t},\quad\mbox{for all }t<\sigma_{n}.

Choosing
n=N
, we obtain
\underline{L}^{N}_{t}=\Gamma_{N}[\underline{L}_{N}]_{t}
on
[0,\sigma_{N})
.
If
\sigma_{N}=\infty
, then
\underline{L}^{N}=\Gamma_{N}[\underline{L}_{N}]
.
Otherwise, recall that
\underline{L}^{N}\preceq\Gamma_{N}[\underline{L}^{N}]
.
So we see that
1\leq\underline{L}^{N}_{\sigma_{N}}=\Gamma_{N}[\underline{L}^{N}]_{\sigma_{N}}\leq 1
, and thus
\underline{L}^{N}=\Gamma_{N}[\underline{L}^{N}]
.
To prove the minimality of
\underline{L}^{N}
, we pick an arbitrary solution
L
.
By definition, we have
0\preceq L
, and thus by iteration,

\displaystyle\underline{L}^{N}=\Gamma_{N}^{(N)}[0]\preceq\Gamma_{N}^{(N)}[L]=L,

which implies that
\underline{L}^{N}
is indeed the minimal solution.

(ii)  Next we turn to the McKean-Vlasov equation (
3
).
Define the sequence
(\Gamma^{(n)}[{0}],\,n\geq 1)\subset\mathbb{M}
,
where
\Gamma^{(n)}
denotes the
n
-th iterate of
\Gamma
.
By construction, we see that the sequence is non-decreasing with respect to
\preceq
.
We show in the following that
\Gamma^{(n)}[{0}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{d}}}{{\to}}\underline{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{M}
exists, and
\underline{\Lambda}
is the minimal solution to (
3
).
By definition, we have
{0}\preceq\Gamma[{0}]
.
For each
t\geq 0
, the sequence
\big(\Gamma^{(n)}[{0}]_{t},\,n\geq 1\big)
is non-decreasing and lying in
[0,1]
, which
implies that we can define
\tilde{\Lambda}
to be its pointwise limit

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Gamma^{(n)}[{0}]_{t},\quad\mbox{for all }t\geq 0.

Clearly,
\tilde{\Lambda}
is non-decreasing with

0\leq\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}\leq 1
, so its modification
\underline{\Lambda}_{t}:=\tilde{\Lambda}_{t+}
lies in
\mathbb{M}
.

p(z):=\mathbb{P}(z+\beta t+B^{1}_{t}>0,\,\forall t\geq 0)
. Then we have
\lim_{z\to\infty}p(z)=1
.
For each
t\geq 0
, we get

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty)-\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}=\infty)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty)\big|+\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}>t)\big|+\big|\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}=\infty)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad=\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty)\big|+\big|\mathbb{E}[\underline{L}_{t}^{N}]-\underline{\Lambda}_{t}\big|+\big|\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}=\infty)\big|,

where
\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}>t)=1-\mathbb{E}[\underline{L}_{t}^{N}]
is by symmetry.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{1}>t\}}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{1}>t\}}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty\}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{1}>t\}}]-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty|\tau^{N}_{1}>t)\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{1}>t\}}\big].

On the event
\{\tau^{N}_{1}>t\}
, we have

\displaystyle\{\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{X^{N,1}_{t}+\beta(s-t)+(B^{1}_{s}-B^{1}_{t})-(G(L^{N}_{s})-G(L^{N}_{t}))>0,\forall s\geq t\}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\{X_{t}^{N,1}-c+\beta(s-t)+(B^{1}_{s}-B^{1}_{t})>0,\forall s\geq t\},

which yields by the Markov property
\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty|\tau^{N}_{1}>t)\geq p(X^{N,1}_{t}-c)
, and thus

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{1}>t\}}\big(1-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty|\tau^{N}_{1}>t)\big)\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{1}>t\}}\big(1-p(X^{N,1}_{t}-c)\big)\big].

Set
Y_{t}:=Z^{N,1}+\beta t+B^{1}_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
and
\sigma:=\inf\{t\geq 0:Y_{t}\leq 0\}
. Then
X^{N,1}_{t}\geq Y_{t}-c
and
\tau^{N}_{1}\leq\sigma
. Hence,

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty)\big|\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{N}_{1}>t\}}\big(1-p(X^{N,1}_{t}-c)\big)\big]\leq\big|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-2c)\big)\big].

Similarly, we can establish

\big|\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}>t)-\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}=\infty)\big|\leq\big|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-\alpha(\gamma_{N})-2c)\big)\big]
,
and thus

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau_{1}=\infty)-\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}=\infty)\big|\leq 2\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-\alpha(1)-2c)\big)\big]+\big|\mathbb{E}[\underline{L}^{N}_{t}]-\underline{\Lambda}_{t}\big|.

As
\lim_{t\to\infty}Y_{t}=\infty
holds almost surely, we have by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y_{t}-\alpha(1)-2c)\big)\big]=0.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

such that

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\big(1-p(Y(t)-\alpha(1)-2c)\big)\big]\leq\varepsilon
for all
t\geq t_{\varepsilon}
.
Fix an arbitrary
t\in J
with
t>t_{\varepsilon}
. Then we get

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\big|\mathbb{P}(\tau^{N}_{1}=\infty)-\mathbb{P}(\hat{\tau}=\infty)\big|\leq 2\varepsilon+\lim_{N\to\infty}\big|\mathbb{E}[\underline{L}_{t}^{N}]-\underline{\Lambda}_{t}\big|=2\varepsilon,

which yields
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}]=1-\underline{\Lambda}_{\infty}
. Take a sequence
(t_{m})_{m\geq 1}\subset J
satisfying
\lim_{m\to\infty}t_{m}=\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}=1-\underline{L}^{N}_{\infty}\leq 1-\underline{L}^{N}_{t_{m}}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}\leq 1-\underline{\Lambda}_{t_{m}}.

Letting
m\to\infty
, we deduce that
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}\leq 1-\underline{\Lambda}_{\infty}
.
On the other hand, we get by Fatou’s lemma that

1-\underline{\Lambda}_{\infty}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\limsup_{N\to\infty}\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}]
,
which implies
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}=1-\underline{\Lambda}_{\infty}
.
We conclude by applying Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}-1+\underline{\Lambda}_{\infty}|]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\limsup_{N\to\infty}|\underline{S}_{\infty}^{N}-1+\underline{\Lambda}_{\infty}|\right]=0,

which yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 13

Let

Denote by

\displaystyle\{\tau<t\mbox{ and }W_{\tau}=f_{\tau}\mbox{ and }W_{s}\geq f_{\tau}\mbox{ for }s\in[\tau,t]\}:=B_{t}^{\prime},

Lemma 14

Let
(f^{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset{\cal D}\cap\mathbb{M}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{0\leq s\leq t}(x+W_{s}-f^{n}_{s})\leq 0\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{0\leq s\leq t}(x+W_{s}-f_{s})\leq 0\right),

(resp.
\tau

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{0\leq s\leq t}(W_{s}-f^{n}_{s})\leq-x\right)=\mathbb{P}(\tau_{n}\leq t)

,
and

\lim_{T\to 0}h_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(T)=-\infty,\quad\lim_{T\to\infty}h_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(T)=-\varepsilon\log\varepsilon-(1-\varepsilon)\log(1-\varepsilon)>0,

so the unique root of
h_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(T)=0
on
(0,\infty)
is
T=T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
defined by (
16
).
Therefore,
h_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(T)<0
for
T<T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
,
and the bound (
17
) follows from (
18
) and (
22
).
∎

The next lemma shows that many (most) of the banks surviving up to time
T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
have suitably large capital levels.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\delta>0
, and let

Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}:=\#\left\{k:X^{N,k}_{T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}\geq\alpha+\delta\mbox{ and }X^{N,k}_{t}>0\mbox{ for }t\leq T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}\right\},

(24)

be the number of surviving banks whose final capital levels are above
\alpha+\delta
at time
T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
.
Let

p_{\alpha,\,\varepsilon,\delta}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\left(\left(\frac{\alpha+\delta}{1-\alpha\varepsilon}-1\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}\right)\right).

(25)

Then we have

\mathbb{E}Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}\geq(1-\varepsilon)p_{\alpha,\varepsilon.\delta}N,

(26)

and for
\lambda<(1-\varepsilon)p_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}
,

\mathbb{P}(Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}>\lambda N)\geq 1-CN^{-k},

(27)

for any
k>0
, as
N\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon}:=\left\{\tau^{N}_{(\varepsilon n)}>T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}\right\}
.
By Lemma
16
, we get
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{c}_{\alpha,\varepsilon})\lesssim N^{-2}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}1_{\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}1_{\Omega^{c}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}\,|\,\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon}\right)\,(1-\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{c}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}))+\mathbb{E}\left(Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}1_{\Omega^{c}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}\,|\,\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon}\right)+\mathcal{O}(N\cdot N^{-2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}\,|\,\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon}\right)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1}),

where we use the fact that
Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}\leq N
and
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{c}_{\alpha,\varepsilon})\lesssim N^{-2}
in the third equation.
On the event
\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
,
there are at least
(1-\varepsilon)N
surviving banks,
the capital levels of which are stochastically larger than
Brownian motion starting at
1-\alpha\varepsilon
(see
[
14
]
).
By an obvious coupling (to Brownian particles without control
\phi
),

(Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}\,|\,\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon})
is stochastically larger than
{\rm Bin}((1-\varepsilon)N,p_{\alpha,\,\varepsilon,\delta})
,
where

p_{\alpha,\,\varepsilon,\delta}:=\mathbb{P}\left(B_{T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}\geq\alpha(1+\varepsilon)+\delta-1\right)=\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\left(\frac{\alpha(1+\varepsilon)+\delta-1}{\sqrt{T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}}\right).

(29)

Recall the definition of
T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
from (
16
),
and the expression (
29
) simplifies to the right side of (
25
).
Combining with (
28
) yields the lower bound (
26
).
The bound (
27
) follows from the stochastic comparison and the Chernorff bound.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
6
(ii)  .

Proof of Theorem
6

(ii)
.

The difference between (
6
) and the Up-the-River model is that
there is an additional term
-\frac{\alpha}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}1_{\{\tau^{N}_{k}\leq t\}}
,
which drags the capital level down.
Under the natural coupling of the model (
6
) and the Up-the-River model,
if a bank survives using a control in the model (
6
),
it will also survive using the same control in the Up-the-River model.
This implies

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\underline{S}^{\phi,N}_{\infty}}{\sqrt{N}}\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{S^{\tiny\mbox{UR}}_{N}}{\sqrt{N}},

where
S^{\tiny\mbox{UR}}_{N}
denotes the maximum number of surviving banks in the Up-the-River model.
By
[
32
, Theorem 1.2]
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{S^{\tiny\mbox{UR}}_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}=4/\sqrt{\pi}
,
and hence the upper bound in (
31
).
Now we prove the lower bound.
Fix
m>0
. We use the following control strategy:

•

For each bank, exercise no control until it reaches the capital level
N/m
.

•

If the bank does reach the capital level
N/m
, and is the one of the first
m
banks achieving so,
then exercise the control
1/m
thereafter.

We write
\underline{S}^{\phi,N}\equiv S_{N}
without any danger of confusion.
For
\lambda<(1-\varepsilon)p_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}
,
let
\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}:=\{Z_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}>\lambda N\}
.
By Lemma
17
, we have
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda})\geq 1-N^{-2}
.
On the event
\Omega_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda}
,
there are (at least)
\lambda N
banks whose capital levels are above
\alpha+\delta
at time
T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
.
The capital levels of these banks after
T_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
are stochastically larger than
Brownian motion starting at
\delta
.
Recall from
[
18
, Theorem 7.5.3]
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\delta+B_{t}\mbox{ hits }\frac{N}{m}\mbox{ before }0\right)=\frac{\delta m}{N}.

As a result, the number of banks whose capital levels reach
K/m
is bounded from below
by

\min\left(m,{\rm Bin}(\lambda N,\,\frac{\delta m}{N})\right).

Also note that (
[
18
, Exercise 7.5.2]
)

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{N}{m}-\alpha+B_{t}+\frac{t}{m}\mbox{ does not hit }0\right)=1-\exp\left(-\frac{2}{m}\left(\frac{N}{m}-\alpha\right)\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}S_{N}

\displaystyle\geq\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{2}{m}\left(\frac{N}{m}-\alpha\right)\right)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\min\left(m,{\rm Bin}\bigg(\lambda N,\,\frac{\delta m}{N}\bigg)\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m=\theta\sqrt{N}}}{{=}}\min(1,\lambda\delta)\,\theta\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\theta^{2}}\right)\right)\sqrt{N}+o(1).

By taking
\lambda\uparrow(1-\varepsilon)p_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}
, we get

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}S_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}\geq\min\left(1,(1-\varepsilon)\delta p_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}\right)\sup_{\theta}\left\{\theta\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\theta^{2}}\right)\right)\right\}.

(30)

Note that
\sup_{\theta}\left\{\theta\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\theta^{2}}\right)\right)\right\}\approx 0.9
, which is attained at
\theta\approx 1.26
.
Further by taking
\varepsilon=\min\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)
and
\delta=\alpha
yields

c_{\alpha}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}S_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}S_{N}}{\sqrt{N}}\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}},

(31)

where
c_{\alpha}\approx 0.9\min(1,\rho_{\alpha})
with

\rho_{\alpha}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\left((4\alpha-1)\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{4\alpha}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)^{2\alpha-1}\right)\right)&\mbox{for}&\alpha>1\\
\frac{1}{2}\alpha\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\left(\frac{5\alpha-2}{2-\alpha}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{-1}(1/8)\right)&\mbox{for}&\alpha\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

(32)

∎

Note that the lower and upper bounds appearing in the proof above may not be optimal.
It is easy to see from the proof that the lower bound can be numerically improved to

c^{\prime}_{\alpha}\approx 0.9\min(1,\rho^{\prime}_{\alpha})
with

\rho^{\prime}_{\alpha}:=\sup_{\varepsilon,\delta}\left\{(1-\varepsilon)\delta p_{\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta}\right\}.

But it does not seem that
\rho^{\prime}_{\alpha}
is easily simplified, and has a closed-form expression.
It is also possible to refine the stochastic comparisons in Lemmas
16
and
17
to further improve
the bounds in (
31
).

5.3
Case of
\beta>0

Finally, we consider the positive economy where the capital level of each bank has a positive drift
\beta>0
:

X^{N,i}_{t}=X^{N,i}_{0}+\beta t+B_{t}^{i}-\frac{\alpha}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}1_{\{\tau^{N}_{k}\leq t\}}.

(33)

The question is whether the budget control is helpful,
and what is the optimal control to maximize the number of surviving banks.
As previously mentioned,
this problem is challenging.

To provide insights, we focus on the setting without interaction, i.e.
\alpha=0
.
In this case, the equations

X_{t}^{\phi,N,i}=X^{N,i}_{0}+\beta t+B_{t}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{s}^{N,i}ds,

(34)

have a unique solution,
and we look for

S_{N}=\max_{\phi}S^{\phi,N}:=\max_{\phi}\sum_{k=1}^{N}1_{\{\tau^{\phi,N}_{k}=\infty\}}.

where
\tau^{\phi,N}_{k}:=\inf\{t>0:X^{\phi,N,i}_{t}\leq 0\}
.
For
\phi=0
, we have
X^{0,N,i}_{t}=X_{0}^{N,i}+\beta t+B^{i}_{t}
, which are i.i.d. Brownian motion with drift
\beta>0
.
By
[
18
, Exercise 7.5.2]
,

\mathbb{P}(X^{N,i}_{0}+\beta t+B^{i}_{t}\mbox{ does not hit }0)=1-\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\beta X^{N,1}_{0}}\right].

(35)

Thus,
\mathbb{E}S^{0,N}=\left(1-\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\beta X^{N,i}_{0}}\right]\right)N
.
We aim to prove that in the supercritical case where
\beta>0
,
the number of surviving banks scales as
N
,
and moreover, the unit control does not help.
That is, for any
\phi\in\Phi_{N}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}S^{\phi,N}}{N}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}S^{0,N}}{N}=1-\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\beta X^{N,1}_{0}}\right].

To this end, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 18
.

Let
Y^{x}_{t}=x+\beta t+B_{t}
, and
\Psi(t,x):=\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{s\leq t}Y^{x}_{s}>0\right)
.
Then

1.

\Psi(t,x)
solves the PDE

\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=\beta\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial t^{2}},\quad\Psi(t,0)=0,\quad\Psi(0,x)=1_{\{x>0\}}.

(36)

2.

We have

\Psi(t,x)=\Phi\left(\frac{x+\beta t}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-e^{-2\beta x}\Phi\left(\frac{-x+\beta t}{\sqrt{t}}\right),

(37)

where
\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}}dz

is the cumulative distribution function of standard normal.

Proof.

(1) Let
T_{x}:=\inf\{t>0:Y^{x}_{t}=0\}
be the first time at which
Y^{x}
hits
0
.
By duality, we have

\Psi(t,x):=\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{s\leq t}Y^{x}_{s}>0\right)=\mathbb{P}(T_{x}>t),

which is the Green function of
Y^{x}
absorbed at
0
.
It follows from the classical Feynman–Kac formula (see
[
20
, Section 5.4]
) that

\Psi
solves the initial-boundary problem (
36
).

(2) It is easy to check that the formula (
37
) satisfies the PDE (
36
).
It can also be read from
[
7
]
,
[
12
, Example 5.5]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6

(iii)
.

First by the natural coupling, we have for any control
\phi\in\Phi_{N}
,

\mathbb{E}S^{\phi,N}\geq\mathbb{E}S^{0,N}=\left(1-\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\beta X^{N,1}_{0}}\right]\right)N.

This implies that
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}S_{N}/N\geq 1-\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\beta X^{N,1}_{0}}\right]
. Recall the definition of
\Psi(t,x)
in Lemma
18
.
Let
Z_{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Psi(t-s,X_{s}^{\phi,N,i})
,
s\leq t
.
Applying Itô’s formula, we get

\displaystyle Z_{t}

\displaystyle=N\Psi(t,X^{N,i}_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\right)\Psi(t-s,X_{s}^{\phi,N,i})ds

(38)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi^{N,i}(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Psi(t-s,X_{s}^{\phi,N,i})ds+\mbox{martingale}

\displaystyle=N\Psi(t,X^{N,i}_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi^{N,i}(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Psi(t-s,X_{s}^{\phi,N,i})ds+\mbox{martingale},

where the second equality follows from (
36
).
Next by (
37
), we get

\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x}(t,x)=p(t,x+\beta t)+e^{-2\beta x}p(t,x-\beta t)+2\beta e^{-2\beta x}\Phi\left(\frac{-x+\beta t}{\sqrt{t}}\right),

(39)

where
p(t,x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}\right)
is the heat kernel.
Observe that for
x\geq 0
,

p(t,x+\beta t)\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\beta^{2}t},\quad e^{-2\beta x}p(t,x-\beta t)\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\beta^{2}t},\quad e^{-2\beta x}\Phi\left(\frac{-x+\beta t}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\leq 1.

(40)

By (
39
) and (
40
), we get

\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi^{N,i}(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Psi(t-s,X_{s}^{\phi,N,i})ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{-\beta^{2}(t-s)}}{\sqrt{t-s}}ds+2\beta t\leq C^{\prime}+2\beta t.

(41)

Combining (
38
) and (
41
) yields

\mathbb{E}S_{N}\leq\mathbb{E}Z_{t}\leq N\mathbb{E}\Psi(t,X^{N,1}_{0})+2\beta t+C^{\prime}
for any
t>0
.
Fixing
\varepsilon>0
and taking
t=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\beta}N
, we get

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}S_{N}}{N}\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Psi\left(\frac{\varepsilon N}{2\beta},X^{N,1}_{0}\right)+\varepsilon=1-\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\beta X^{N,1}_{0}}\right]+\varepsilon.

(42)

Since (
42
) holds for any
\varepsilon>0
, we conclude that
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}S_{N}/N\leq 1-\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\beta X^{N,1}_{0}}\right]
.
∎

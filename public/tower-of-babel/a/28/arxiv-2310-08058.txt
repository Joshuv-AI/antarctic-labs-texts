Viscosity solutions to a Cauchy type problem for timelike Lorentzian eikonal equation
Siyao Zhu, Xiaojun Cui and Tianqi Shi

Abstract
. In this paper, we propose a Cauchy type problem to the timelike Lorentzian eikonal equation on a globally hyperbolic space-time. For this equation, as the value of the solution on a Cauchy surface is known, we prove the existence of viscosity solutions on the past set (future set) of the Cauchy surface. Furthermore, when the time orientation of viscosity solution is consistent, the uniqueness and stability of viscosity solutions are also obtained.

1.
Introduction

In the study of global Lorentzian geometry, timelike Lorentzian eikonal equation

\displaystyle g(\nabla u,\nabla u)=-1

plays a vital role, where
g
is the Lorentzian metric. The integral curves of a gradient field of a smooth solution, if exists, are timelike geodesics. Geometrically, many distance-like functions, such as Busemann functions, are essentially the solutions to timelike eikonal equation in some sense. Owing to this fact, distance-like functions are one of the key points to construct the compactification and completion of a manifold and to prove splitting theorem, even for finding the solutions to Einstein field equations. For more details, readers can refer
[
3
,
21
]
.

It is well known an eikonal equation, as a particular kind of Hamilton-Jacobi equation, has no global smooth solutions in general. Actually, the expectable maximal regularity of these solutions is the local semiconcavity (see
[
5
,
7
,
32
]
for more information).
For the well-posedness of Hamilton-Jacobi equations, a more natural choice in some sense is the notion of viscosity solutions, systematically introduced by M-G. Crandall, L-C. Evans and P-L. Lions in
[
9
,
10
]
during the early 1980s, although the discussion on well-posedness of evolutionary Hamilton-Jacobi equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+H\left(x,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)=0,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times M

had been developed in
[
13
,
23
,
24
]
before the theory of viscosity solutions. Until now, many monographs have done rich work on this topic. Since late 1990s, for Tonelli Hamiltonian
H
which includes the eikonal case, Fathi established weak KAM theory, which made the connection between Aubry-Mather theory (
[
26
,
25
,
27
]
) and the theory of viscosity solution of stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle H(x,Du(x))=c,\,\,x\in M,

on Riemannian manifold (
[
20
,
14
,
16
,
15
,
18
,
17
]
). Cauchy and Dirichlet problems of Hamilton-Jacobi equations are partly mentioned in
[
19
,
6
]
.

To our knowledge, the convexity (positive definiteness), coercivity (even superlinearity) of Hamiltonians are needed essentially in the classical theory of viscosity solutions, Mather theory and weak KAM theory, but these properties cannot apply to the general space-time. Even so, there are also a few research papers on viscosity solutions to eikonal equation of various space-time in recent years. Following from
[
2
]
, the negative regular cosmology time function is the viscosity solution of eikonal equation, which means a kind of viscosity solutions is found in a specific globally hyperbolic space-time (
[
12
]
). Moreover, in the case of class A torus,
[
22
]
and
[
30
]
developed the well-posedness of eikonal equations, weak KAM theory and Aubry-Mather theory for corresponding geodesic flows.
[
31
]
is also a good reference for Aubry-Mather theory for compact space-time. More recently, the work in
[
29
]
dealt the related problems in globally hyperbolic space-time. Authors gave the existence of global-defined viscosity solutions and the local variational representation of a solution which has a consistent time-orientation.

Based on the ideas above, we consider a Cauchy type problem of eikonal equation on a globally hyperbolic space-time. Main results of this paper are as follows.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a globally hyperbolic space-time, then there exists a unique viscosity solution of the following problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g(\nabla u(x),\nabla u(x))=-1,&x\in I^{-}[\Gamma],\\
u|_{\Gamma}=\phi\in\mbox{$\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}$}(\Gamma),&\end{array}\right.

with always past-directed directed time orientation, where
\Gamma
is a Cauchy surface. Moreover, this solution can also be represented by a variational formula as

\displaystyle u_{\phi}(x)=\inf_{y\in J_{\Gamma}^{+}(x)}\{\phi(y)-d(x,y)\}

and it yields that the solution is semiconcave. In the end, we show that (
1
) continuously depends on the initial value function on fixed
\Gamma
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduced some basic materials involving the Lorentzian geometry and causality theory, semiconcave functions and viscosity solutions. In Section 3, we answered affirmatively on the well-posedness of (
1
) (Theorem
3.9
,
3.14
and
3.16

3.6
and Theorem
3.9
.

3.1.
Existence of the viscosity solutions

For brevity, as

For any

\displaystyle T_{\Gamma,x}:U_{h}T_{x}M\cap\mathscr{C}_{x}

\displaystyle\to\Gamma

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\mapsto\gamma_{x,V}\cap\Gamma,

where

is the unit tangent sphere at

is the future-pointing light cone at

is a future-directed inextendible causal curve with

Proposition 3.6
.

is locally semiconcave on

.

Proof.

Since
\gamma_{x}
maximizes the distance between any pair of its points, there is no cut point to
x
on
\gamma_{x}|_{(0,d(x,p))}
for appropriately selected

. Then by
[

, there exists a neighborhood

of

such that
\bar{O}
is compact,
\bar{O}\varsubsetneqq I^{-}(p)
and

is a smooth function on

Combining with Lemma

, we only need to prove that there exists a constant

depending only on

such that

near

in the setting of Euclidean coordinates. By the definition of

, this is equivalent to prove that

near

.

Now for any
z\in O
, we denote
\eta_{z}
the unique speed future-directed maximal geodesic connecting

to

. In the following we will use the standard comparison principle to give an estimation of Hessian of Lorentzian distance function

in terms of upper and lower bounds of timelike sectional curvatures of 2-planes containing

and the length of maximal segment between

and
p
, where the Hessian of

is defined in terms of the Levi-Civita connection with respect to

.

Since
\bar{O}
is a compact subset of

,

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in\bar{O},V\in\partial\mathscr{C}_{z}^{+1}}\left\{\bigg|\frac{\dot{\eta}_{z}(0)}{|\dot{\eta}_{z}(0)|_{h}}-V\bigg|_{h}\right\}>0,

where

, which implies there exists a compact subset

such that
\cup_{z\in O}\{(\eta_{z}(0),\dot{\eta}_{z}(0))\}
(thus
\cup_{z\in O}\{(\eta_{z}(t),\dot{\eta}_{z}(t)):t\in[0,d(z,p)]\}

\cup_{z\in O}\{(z,V)\in TM:g(V,\dot{\eta}_{z}(0))=0,|V|=1\}
are contained in

. Therefore, both the timelike sectional curvatures of 2-planes containing
\dot{\eta}_{z}(0)|_{z\in O}
and the length of
\eta_{z}|_{[0,d(z,p)]}

C>0
(e.g.
[
2
, Proposition 3.1]
). By Lemma
2.1
, there exists a constant

depending only on

(3.5)

\displaystyle D^{2}d(\cdot,p)\geqslant C_{1}(O)I.

For any

, let

be a local orthonormal frame of

. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}d_{p}(z)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial d_{p}(z)}{\partial x_{k}}\geqslant C_{1}(O)I,

and

for any

Lemma 3.7

(
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

Suppose

\displaystyle g(V,W)\geqslant\sqrt{-g(W,W)}

Proposition 3.8
.

If
u_{\phi}
is differentiable at
x
, then
\nabla u_{\phi}(x)
is a past-directed timelike vector. Moreover, in this case,
u_{\phi}
satisfies (
Eg
) at
x
.

Proof.

Assume that
u_{\phi}
is differentiable at
x\in I^{-}[\Gamma]
. Choosing any fixed smooth future-directed causal curve
\gamma:[0,T)\to M
with some small
T>0
and
\gamma(0)=x
, by reverse triangle inequality and the definition of
u_{\phi}
, we have

\displaystyle u_{\phi}(\gamma(t))-d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=\min_{y\in J_{\Gamma}^{+}(\gamma(t))}\{\phi(y)-d(\gamma(t),y)\}-d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))

\displaystyle\geqslant\min_{y\in J_{\Gamma}^{+}(\gamma(t))}\{\phi(y)-d(\gamma(0),y)\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\min_{y\in J_{\Gamma}^{+}(\gamma(0))}\{\phi(y)-d(\gamma(0),y)\}=u_{\phi}(\gamma(0))

since
J_{\Gamma}^{+}(\gamma(t))\subset J_{\Gamma}^{+}(\gamma(0))
for every
t\in[0,T)
. Armed with that information,

\displaystyle u_{\phi}(\gamma(t))-u_{\phi}(\gamma(0))

\displaystyle\geqslant d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))\geqslant\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{-g(\dot{\gamma}(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))}\,ds

for every
t\in[0,T)
. Dividing by
t
on both sides, we get

(3.7)

\frac{u_{\phi}(\gamma(t))-u_{\phi}(x)}{t}\geqslant\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{-g(\dot{\gamma}(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))}\,ds.

As
t\searrow 0
, the differentiability of
u_{\phi}
at
x
and inequality (
3.7
) yield

du_{\phi}(x)(\dot{\gamma}(0))\geqslant\sqrt{-g(\dot{\gamma}(0),\dot{\gamma}(0))}.

Note that
du_{\phi}(x)(\dot{\gamma}(0))=g(\nabla u_{\phi}(x),\dot{\gamma}(0))
, this means

(3.8)

g(\nabla u_{\phi}(x),\dot{\gamma}(0))\geq\sqrt{-g(\dot{\gamma}(0),\dot{\gamma}(0))}

for any future-directed causal vector
\dot{\gamma}(0)
.

We now prove that
\nabla u_{\phi}(x)
is timelike. First, it is easy to see that
\nabla u_{\phi}(x)\neq 0
.
Furthermore, suppose
\nabla u_{\phi}(x)
is spacelike, then there exists a smooth future-directed timelike curve
\bar{\gamma}:[0,\varepsilon)\to M
such that
\bar{\gamma}(0)=p
and
g(\dot{\bar{\gamma}}(0),\nabla u_{\phi}(x))=0
, which means that
\nabla u_{\phi}(x)
and
\dot{\bar{\gamma}}(0)
are orthogonal with respect to the Lorentzian metric
g
. On the other hand, from (
3.8
), we get
g(\nabla u_{\phi}(x),\dot{\bar{\gamma}}(0))>0
, which contradicts the orthogonal hypothesis. Thus,
\nabla u_{\phi}(p)
is a causal vector. In addition,
g(\nabla u_{\phi}(x),V)\geqslant 0
for any future-directed vector
V\in T_{p}M
. This implies that
\nabla u_{\phi}(x)
is past-directed. By Lemma
3.7
, we have
\nabla u_{\phi}(x)
is indeed a past-directed timelike vector.

For the rest of the proof, we can show that
|\nabla u_{\phi}(x)|=1
equivalently. First we choose a future-directed smooth causal curve
\eta:[0,\varepsilon)\rightarrow M
with
\eta(0)=x
,
\dot{\eta}(0)=-\nabla u_{\phi}(x)
. Then by inequality (
3.8
),

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{\phi}(x)|^{2}=-g(\nabla u_{\phi}(x),\nabla u_{\phi}(x))=g(\nabla u_{\phi}(x),\dot{\eta}(0))\geqslant\sqrt{-g(\dot{\eta}(0),\dot{\eta}(0))}=|\nabla u_{\phi}(x)|.

Hence,
|\nabla u_{\phi}(x)|\geqslant 1
since
\nabla u_{\phi}(x)\neq 0
.
Recall that for any
x\in I^{-}[\Gamma]
there exists a
y_{x}\in\Gamma
and a forward calibrated curve
{\gamma}_{x}:[0,d(x,y_{x})]\to M
that starts at
x
and ends at
y_{x}
satisfies

\displaystyle u_{\phi}({\gamma}_{x}(t))-u_{\phi}({\gamma}_{x}(0))=t

for any
t\in[0,d(x,y_{x})]
. Then we get

\displaystyle\frac{u_{\phi}({\gamma}_{x}(t))-u_{\phi}({\gamma}_{x}(0))}{t-0}=1

for every
t\in[0,d(x,y_{x})]
. Let
t\searrow 0
, and by the the reverse Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for causal vectors (
[
28
, Section 2.2.1]
) and
{\gamma}_{x}
is a timelike (unit-speed) curve, we have

(3.9)

1=du_{\phi}(x)(\dot{{\gamma}}_{x}(0))=g(\nabla u_{\phi}(x),\dot{{\gamma}}_{x}(0))\geqslant|\nabla u_{\phi}(x)||\dot{{\gamma}}_{x}(0)|=|\nabla u_{\phi}(x)|.

Combining with
|\nabla u_{\phi}(x)|\geqslant 1
, (
3.9
) implies
|\nabla u_{\phi}(x)|=1
.
∎

.
∎

Theorem 3.16
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.15
,
u_{n}\rightrightarrows u_{\phi}
on any compact set of
I^{-}[\Gamma]
, which means (
2.3
) depends continuously on the initial function defined on
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Invoking Arzela-Ascoli theorem, we can get a subsequence of
\{u_{n_{k}}\}
on
\bar{O}
metioned in proof of Lemma
3.15
which uniformly converges to some
u
on any compact subset
\bar{O}
. Lemma
2.7
claims that
u
is also semiconcave with the same constant, whence it is also Lipschitz. Without loss of generality, this subsequence is still denoted by
\{u_{n_{k}}\}
for the sake of brevity. Also by Lemma
2.7
, for any
y
, the differentiable point of
u
, there exists
\{y_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
y_{k}\to y
and
u_{n_{k}}
is differentiable at
y_{k}
. In this case, Lemma
2.7
implies
\nabla u_{n_{k}}(y_{k})\to\nabla u(y)
. Then by the continuity of Lorentz metric
g
,

(3.15)

\displaystyle g(\nabla u(x),\nabla u(x))=\lim_{k\to\infty}g(\nabla u_{n_{k}}(y_{k}),\nabla u_{n_{k}}(y_{k}))=-1.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.9
, we can show that
u
is a viscosity solution to (
2.3
) from (
3.15
).

For any fixed
x\in\Gamma
, we choose a sequence
\{x_{m}\}_{m\geqslant 1}\subset I^{-}[\Gamma]
with
x_{m}\rightarrow x
. Since
u_{n_{k}}\rightrightarrows u
and
\phi_{n_{k}}\rightrightarrows\phi
, then by the continuous extension of
u
to
\Gamma
,

\displaystyle u(x)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}u(x_{m})=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{n_{k}}(x_{m})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{n_{k}}(x)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\phi_{n_{k}}(x)=\phi(x).

Combining with the arbitrariness of
x\in\Gamma
,
u|_{\Gamma}=\phi
.
Due to the uniqueness of solution to (
2.3
),
u=u_{\phi}
. Note that the argument above does not depend on some fixed
O
and all convergent subsequences of
\{u_{n}\}
will converge to
u_{\phi}
, and it yields
u_{n}\rightrightarrows u_{\phi}
on any compact subset of
I^{-}[\Gamma]
.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

All of results on well-posedness in this paper still hold true, when we consider instead the following Cauchy type problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g(\nabla u(x),\nabla u(x))=-1,&x\in I^{+}[\Gamma],\\
u(x)=\phi(x),&x\in\Gamma.\end{array}\right.

The only main difference is that the unique solution to the equation above is given by

\displaystyle u_{\phi}(x):=-\sup_{y\in J_{\Gamma}^{-}(x)}\{\phi(y)+d(y,x)\},\,\,x\in I^{+}[\Gamma],

the local semiconcavity of which ensures all the proofs go through with slight modification.

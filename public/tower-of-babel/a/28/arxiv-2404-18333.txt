H 2 H^{2} -regularity for stationary and non-stationary Bingham problems with perfect slip boundary condition
Takeshi Fukao, Takahito Kashiwabara

Abstract
. H 2 H^{2} -spatial regularity of stationary and non-stationary problems for Bingham fluids formulated with the pseudo-stress tensor is discussed. The problem is mathematically described by an elliptic or parabolic variational inequality of the second kind, to which weak solvability in the Sobolev space H 1 H^{1} is well known. However, higher regularity up to the boundary in a bounded smooth domain seems to remain open. This paper indeed shows such H 2 H^{2} -regularity if the problems are supplemented with the so-called perfect slip boundary condition and if the yield stress vanishes on the boundary. For the stationary Bingham–Stokes problem, the key of the proof lies in a priori estimates for a regularized problem avoiding investigation of higher pressure regularity, which seems difficult to get in the presence of a singular diffusion term. The H 2 H^{2} -regularity for the stationary case is then directly applied to establish strong solvability of the non-stationary Bingham–Navier–Stokes problem, based on discretization in time and on the truncation of the nonlinear convection term.

Primary: 76D03, 76A05.

1.
Introduction

Bingham fluid, one of non-Newtonian fluids, is a model describing the motion of viscoplastic materials, which behave like viscous fluid if shear stress magnitude exceeds a threshold, called the yield stress, and like rigid bodies otherwise.
Examples of viscoplastic materials are sauces, pastes, crude oils, drilling muds, slurries, lava, etc., understanding of which is important in several fields of industry or physics.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d=2,3)
be a bounded domain with the smooth boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
.
We consider the following boundary value problem:

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(\bm{T}^{D}-p\bm{I})=\bm{f},\quad\operatorname{div}\bm{u}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\quad\begin{cases}\bm{T}^{D}=\nu\nabla\bm{u}+g\dfrac{\nabla\bm{u}}{|\nabla\bm{u}|}&\text{if}\quad\nabla\bm{u}\neq\bm{O},\\
|\bm{T}^{D}|\leq g&\text{if}\quad\nabla\bm{u}=\bm{O},\end{cases}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}=0,\quad(\bm{T}^{D}\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma,

which we refer to as the
stationary Bingham–Stokes
problem (note that it reduces to the standard Stokes equations if
g=0
), and the following initial boundary value problem:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{u}}{\partial t}+(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}-\operatorname{div}(\bm{T}^{D}-p\bm{I})=\bm{f},\quad\operatorname{div}\bm{u}=0\quad\text{in}\quad Q:=(0,T)\times\Omega,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\quad\begin{cases}\bm{T}^{D}=\nu\nabla\bm{u}+g\dfrac{\nabla\bm{u}}{|\nabla\bm{u}|}&\text{if}\quad\nabla\bm{u}\neq\bm{O},\\
|\bm{T}^{D}|\leq g&\text{if}\quad\nabla\bm{u}=\bm{O},\end{cases}

(1.6)

\displaystyle\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}=0,\quad(\bm{T}^{D}\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\Sigma:=(0,T)\times\Gamma,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

which we refer to as the
non-stationary Bingham–Navier–Stokes
problem.
Here, the unknown functions
\bm{T}^{D}
,
\bm{u}
, and
p
mean the deviatoric part of the stress tensor (
\nu>0
is the viscosity constant), the velocity, and the pressure of a material, respectively.
Then
\bm{T}=-p\bm{I}+\bm{T}^{D}
constitutes the total stress tensor with
\bm{I}
the identity matrix.
For matrices
\bm{A},\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, we define the inner product and magnitude by
\bm{A}:\bm{B}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}A_{ij}B_{ij}
and
|\bm{A}|=(\bm{A}:\bm{A})^{1/2}
, respectively.
The external force
\bm{f}
and the yield stress
g\geq 0
are given functions.

Let us discuss the stationary problem for a while.
Compared with the standard Bingham fluid model (see e.g.
[
15
, Section VI.1.3]
), problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) is simplified in the sense that we exploit the
pseudo stress tensor
, i.e, we use
\nabla\bm{u}=(\partial u_{i}/\partial x_{j})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
instead of the rate-of-strain tensor
\bm{D}(\bm{u})=(\nabla\bm{u}+(\nabla\bm{u})^{\top})/2
in the constitutive law.
This facilitates technical arguments in our analysis (especially, the coercivity relation is simpler than the one obtained from Korn’s inequality, cf. (
2.2
) below), still retaining mathematical difficulties arising from the Bingham nonlinearity.

The boundary condition (
1.3
) is the perfect slip boundary condition, where
\bm{n}
is the outer unit normal to
\Gamma
and the subscript
\tau
means the tangential part of a vector (namely,
\bm{v}_{\tau}=\bm{v}-(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n})\bm{n}
).
In view of (
1.2
), it reduces to

(1.8)

\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}=0,\quad(\nabla\bm{u}\,\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma.

By adopting the theory presented in the classical literature
[
15
, Chapter VI]
, we can easily find that (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) is formulated as a variational inequality problem and that it admits a unique weak solution
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
.
However, the higher regularity, e.g.,
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
seems much less understood.
For the scalar Bingham problem without the divergence-free constraint (also known as Bingham flows in cylinders), the
H^{2}
-regularity is established by
[
9
, Theorem 15]
using a sophisticated estimate for a resolvent-like problem for the Laplace operator.
We also refer to recent results
[
1
,
3
]
, which show
H^{2}(\Omega)
-regularity for a scalar pseudo-parabolic problem with a singular diffusion term (similar to the Bingham problem).
The strategy of
[
1
]
has several things in common with ours; e.g., regularity for a stationary problem is obtained first, which is then incorporated to examine an evolution problem via discretization in time.
From another perspective, partial interior
C^{1}
-regularity, as well as interior
H^{2}
-regularity (i.e.
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
), is obtained in
[
16
,
17
]
for the stationary Bingham flow with the convective term.
See also
[
27
]
which mentions some interior
H^{2}
-regularity for the non-stationary problem.
More recently, the
C^{1}(\Omega)
-regularity is established for the scalar case by
[
36
]
.

On the other hand, it seems that there are no results of
H^{2}
-regularity
up to the boundary
when the divergence-free constraint is imposed, that is, the pressure
p
appears as an unknown variable.
A major difficulty consists in obtaining higher regularity than
L^{2}(\Omega)
for
p
.
In fact, as is seen from the linear Stokes case, we usually need to establish higher tangential regularity of
p
before discussing the regularity of
\bm{u}
in the normal direction (see
[
13
, pp. 24–25]
and
[
7
, p. 1104]
).
However, this strategy seems infeasible for the Bingham case, which formally reads

\nabla p=\bm{f}-\nu\Delta\bm{u}-\operatorname{div}(g\nabla\bm{u}/|\nabla\bm{u}|)
, since the last term is too singular to be even a measurable function near the region where
\nabla\bm{u}
vanishes.

If the no-slip boundary condition is imposed, it is not trivial to settle this issue.
Thereby we suggest to replace it by the perfect slip one (
1.3
) or (
1.8
) and indeed prove the regularity
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
up to the boundary if
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, which is the first objective of the present paper (see Corollary
3.1
below).
In the proof, we introduce a regularized problem, transform it into a half-space problem, and estimate tangential and normal second-order derivatives.
The key observation is that for (
1.8
) one can obtain the regularity of velocity in the normal direction, knowing only lower regularity for the pressure (in other words, higher regularity is shown only for
\bm{u}
but not for
p
).

Let us make a comment on the assumption
g=0
on
\Gamma
, which is required for a technical reason (see Remark
3.5
(ii)).
We emphasize that, even with it the issue of
\bm{H}^{2}
-regularity up to the boundary remains non-trivial.
In fact, once we are away from the boundary and
g>0
—no matter how small the distance or
g
is—the effect of singular diffusion becomes present, which cannot be reduced to an interior case or to small perturbation from the standard Stokes equations.
However, it is true that this assumption excludes some important case, e.g.
g=
const., which is left as an open problem.

The latter part of this paper (Section
4
) deals with the non-stationary Bingham–Navier–Stokes problem.
In this regard we notice that preceding studies
[
8
,
15
,
18
,
23
,
26
,
28
,
30
]
considered only one spatial differentiability whereas
H^{2}
-regularity in the two-dimensional whole space was obtained in
[
25
]
.
By directly applying Corollary
3.1
, we will prove that there exists a unique local-in-time solution
\bm{u}
having the
H^{2}
-regularity—more precisely,
\bm{u}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))
in Theorem
4.1
and
\bm{u}\in H^{1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))
in Theorem
4.2
—depending on assumptions for the data
\bm{f},g
, and
\bm{u}_{0}
.
The proof is based on the discretization in time (also known as Rhothe’s method) combined with the truncation technique for the convection term as in
[
5
]
.

Briefly speaking, our well-posedness result tells us that, if the yield stress
g
is first-order differentiable with respect to space and time, then we can construct an
H^{2}
-strong solution.
As one of its possible applications and future studies, we mention non-isothermal Bingham fluids involving heat transfer, in which the yield stress and the viscosity parameter may depend on temperature (cf.
[
22
,
29
]
).

We use the standard Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\Omega)
and Sobolev spaces
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Those spaces on the boundary, in particular the Sobolev–Slobodeckij space
W^{s,p}(\Gamma)
will also be utilized.
Function spaces for vectors are indicated with bold fonts, e.g.,
\bm{L}^{p}(\Omega),\,\bm{W}^{m,p}(\Omega)
.
Those for tensors (matrices) are also indicated by the same symbols; nevertheless, in case confusion may occur, we write
L^{p}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
etc.
Throughout this paper, we let
C
denote a generic positive constant that may differ at each occurrence.
When we need to specify its dependency on parameters, we indicate
C=C(\Omega)
etc.

2.
Weak formulations for the stationary problem

In this and next sections, let us consider the stationary Bingham–Stokes problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
).
For simplicity in the presentation, we assume
\nu=1
.
Let us derive weak formulations and recall unique existence of a weak solution, which is already known.
For that purpose we introduce the following Hilbert spaces:

\displaystyle\bm{V}

\displaystyle=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\mid\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0\;\text{ on }\Gamma\},

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}\mid\operatorname{div}\bm{v}=0\},\quad\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}}=\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}:=\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\mid\operatorname{div}\bm{v}=0,\quad\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0\;\text{ on }\Gamma\},\quad\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}:=\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid\int_{\Omega}q\,d\bm{x}=0\Bigr\}.

We regard
\bm{V}_{\sigma}\subset\bm{H}_{\sigma}=\bm{H}_{\sigma}^{\prime}\subset\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
as a Gelfand triple, where the prime means the dual norm space.
The
L^{2}(\Omega)
-inner products for scalar-, vector-, and matrix-valued functions are denoted by the same symbol
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
The duality pairing between
\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
and
\bm{V}_{\sigma}
is written as
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
.

With this setting, we show that a solution of the Bingham problem satisfies a variational inequality.

Proposition 2.1
.

(i) Let
(\bm{u},p)
be a sufficiently smooth solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.3
).
Then we have

(2.1)

(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla(\bm{v}-\bm{u}))+(g,|\nabla\bm{v}|-|\nabla\bm{u}|)\geq\left<\bm{f},\bm{v}-\bm{u}\right>\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

(ii) Let
\bm{f}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
and
g\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,g\geq 0
.
Then there exists a unique solution
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
of (
2.1
).

Proof.

(i) It follows from integration by parts that
(\bm{T}^{D}(\bm{u}),\nabla\bm{v})=(\bm{f},\bm{v})
for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
, and hence

\displaystyle(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla(\bm{v}-\bm{u}))+(g,|\nabla\bm{v}|-|\nabla\bm{u}|)-(\bm{f},\bm{v}-\bm{u})=(g,|\nabla\bm{v}|-|\nabla\bm{u}|)-(\bm{T}^{D}(\bm{u})-\nabla\bm{u},\nabla(\bm{v}-\bm{u})).

The right-hand side
\geq 0
because
(\bm{T}^{D}(\bm{u})-\nabla\bm{u}):\nabla\bm{v}\leq g|\nabla\bm{v}|
and
(\bm{T}^{D}(\bm{u})-\nabla\bm{u}):\nabla\bm{u}=g|\nabla\bm{u}|
in
\Omega
by (
1.2
).

(ii) Observe that the functional
j
defined by
j(\bm{v})=(g,|\nabla\bm{v}|)
is continuous from
\bm{V}
to
\mathbb{R}
and convex, and that the following coercivity holds:

(2.2)

\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq C\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}.

Then a well-known existence theory of elliptic variational inequalities (see
[
20
, Theorem I.4.1]
) implies the desired result.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

There holds a Poincaré inequality
\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
for
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0
on
\Gamma
, which may be proved in a similar way to
[
7
, Lemma 2.3]
.
In fact, a constant vector field
\bm{v}
satisfying
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0
on
\Gamma
must be zero since the Gauss map
\bm{n}
is surjective from a compact manifold
\Gamma
onto the
(d-1)
-dimensional sphere.

The converse statement of Proposition
2.1
is not trivial to prove.
We achieve it by constructing a multiplier based on a regularized variational inequality introduced for
\epsilon>0
as follows:

(2.3)

(\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon},\nabla(\bm{v}-\bm{u}_{\epsilon}))+(g,\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{v}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}-\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}})\geq\left<\bm{f},\bm{v}-\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\right>\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

Proposition 2.2
.

(i) Let
\bm{f}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
and
g\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,g\geq 0
.
Then there exists a unique solution
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
of (
2.3
).
Moreover, it satisfies the following variational equation:

(2.4)

(\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon},\nabla\bm{v})+\Big(g\frac{\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}},\nabla\bm{v}\Big)=\left<\bm{f},\bm{v}\right>\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

(ii) We have
\|\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}}
.
Moreover, when
\epsilon\to 0
,
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}
converges to the solution
\bm{u}
of (
2.1
) strongly in
\bm{V}_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

(i) Unique existence of
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}
is immediately obtained as in Proposition
2.1
(ii).
Let
j_{\epsilon}:\bm{V}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by
j_{\epsilon}(\bm{v})=(g,\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{v}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}})
.
It is Gâteaux differentiable and we get

j_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\bm{v})[\bm{w}]=\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{j_{\epsilon}(\bm{v}+s\bm{w})-j_{\epsilon}(\bm{v})}{s}=\int_{\Omega}g\frac{\nabla\bm{v}}{\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{v}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}}:\nabla\bm{w}\,d\bm{x}\quad(\bm{v},\bm{w}\in\bm{V}).

Now take
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}+s\bm{v}
with
s\gtrless 0
as a test function in (
2.3
) to find

(\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon},\nabla\bm{v})+\frac{j_{\epsilon}(\bm{u}_{\epsilon}+s\bm{v})-j_{\epsilon}(\bm{u}_{\epsilon})}{s}\gtreqless\left<\bm{f},\bm{v}\right>.

Making
s\to\pm 0
deduces the desired equality.

(ii) The
\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
-estimate for
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}
immediately results from taking
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{\epsilon}
in (
2.4
) together with the coercivity (
2.2
).
Next, taking
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{\epsilon}
in (
2.1
) and
\bm{v}=\bm{u}
in (
2.3
) respectively and adding the resulting inequalities, we obtain

\|\nabla(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{\epsilon})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq j_{\epsilon}(\bm{u})-j(\bm{u})+j(\bm{u}_{\epsilon})-j_{\epsilon}(\bm{u}_{\epsilon})\leq\epsilon\|g\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

where we have used
0\leq\sqrt{|\bm{A}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}-|\bm{A}|=\epsilon^{2}/(\sqrt{|\bm{A}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}+|\bm{A}|)\leq\epsilon
.
This shows
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\to\bm{u}
strongly in
\bm{V}_{\sigma}
as
\epsilon\to 0
.
∎

For the regularized problem, it is easy to recover the associated pressure.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
\bm{f}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, then for the solution
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
constructed in Proposition
2.2
, there exists a unique
p_{\epsilon}\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(2.5)

(\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon},\nabla\bm{v})+\Big(g\frac{\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}},\nabla\bm{v}\Big)-(p_{\epsilon},\operatorname{div}\bm{v})=(\bm{f},\bm{v})\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}.

Moreover,
p_{\epsilon}
satisfies

(2.6)

\|p_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Proof.

Unique existence of
p_{\epsilon}
follows from an abstract theory for variational equations in a mixed form (see
[
19
, Lemma I.4.1(ii)]
), if we notice that the inf-sup condition

C\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{(q,\operatorname{div}\bm{v})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}}\quad\forall q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

holds (see e.g.
[
19
, p. 81]
).
Since
\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\bm{V}
, we also obtain

\displaystyle C\|p_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}}\frac{(p_{\epsilon},\operatorname{div}\bm{v})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}}=\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}}\frac{(\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon},\nabla\bm{v})+\Big(g\frac{\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}},\nabla\bm{v}\Big)-(\bm{f},\bm{v})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

which combined with Proposition
2.2
(ii) proves the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-estimate for the pressure.
∎

With the help of the regularized problem, we are now able to establish a counterpart to the “converse” statement of Proposition
2.1
(cf.
[
20
, Theorem II.6.3]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\bm{f}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,g\geq 0
, and
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
be the solution of (
2.1
).
Then there exist
\bm{\lambda}\in\bm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)=L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
and
p\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle(\nabla\bm{u}+g\bm{\lambda},\nabla\bm{v})-(p,\operatorname{div}\bm{v})=(\bm{f},\bm{v})\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\bm{\lambda}=0,\quad|\bm{\lambda}|\leq 1,\quad g\bm{\lambda}:\nabla\bm{u}=g|\nabla\bm{u}|\quad\text{a.e.\ in}\quad\Omega.

In particular, if we set
\bm{T}^{D}:=\nabla\bm{u}+g\bm{\lambda}
then
(\bm{T}^{D},\bm{u},p)
solves (
1.1
)–(
1.3
).

Proof.

In (
2.5
) we set
\bm{\lambda}_{\epsilon}:=\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}/\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}\in\bm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
to get

(2.8)

\displaystyle(\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}+g\bm{\lambda}_{\epsilon},\nabla\bm{v})-(p_{\epsilon},\operatorname{div}\bm{v})=(\bm{f},\bm{v})\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\bm{\lambda}_{\epsilon}=0,\quad|\bm{\lambda}_{\epsilon}|\leq 1\quad\text{a.e.\ in}\quad\Omega.

In view of this and (
2.6
), we can choose a sub-sequence of
\{\bm{\lambda}_{\epsilon}\},\{p_{\epsilon}\}
(denoted by the same symbols) and some
\bm{\lambda}\in\bm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega),p\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\operatorname{Tr}\bm{\lambda}=0
,
|\bm{\lambda}|\leq 1
a.e. in
\Omega
and

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup\bm{\lambda}\quad\text{weakly-$*$ in}\quad\bm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega),\qquad p_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup p\quad\text{weakly in}\quad L^{2}(\Omega).

Then, letting
\epsilon\to 0
in (
2.8
) we obtain (
2.7
).
Next, taking
\bm{v}=\bm{0},2\bm{u}
in (
2.1
) we have
\|\nabla\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(g,|\nabla\bm{u}|)=(\bm{f},\bm{u})
, which combined with (
2.7
) for
\bm{v}=\bm{u}
leads to
(g,|\nabla\bm{u}|)=(g\bm{\lambda},\nabla\bm{u})
.
Since
|\bm{\lambda}|\leq 1
, this holds if and only if
g\bm{\lambda}:\nabla\bm{u}=g|\nabla\bm{u}|
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Consequently, we have
g\bm{\lambda}=g\nabla\bm{u}/|\nabla\bm{u}|
where
\nabla\bm{u}\neq\bm{O}
.
Now we find from (
2.7
) that (
1.1
) holds in the distributional sense (or in
\bm{H}^{-1}(\Omega)
) and that the second condition in (
1.3
) holds in the sense of
\bm{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Uniqueness of
\bm{\lambda}
(thus that of
p
neither) may not be true; see
[
21
, p. 354]
.

3.
H^{2}
-regularity for the stationary problem

We continue to focus on the stationary problems and to assume
\nu=1
as in the previous section.
The following
\bm{H}^{2}
-regularity theorem on the regularized problem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be of
C^{3,1}
-class,
\bm{f}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be non-negative, and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
.
Then the solution of
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}
of (
2.3
) satisfies
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\in\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
,
(\nabla\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}
a.e. on
\Gamma
, and

\|\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}),

where the constant

is independent of
\epsilon
.

Remark 3.1
.

As for the pressure, although it is possible to show

, we were not able to get an
H^{1}(\Omega)
-estimate uniform in
\epsilon
(see Subsection

).

This theorem allows us to derive an
\bm{H}^{2}
-regularity result for the original variational inequality without regularization.

Corollary 3.1
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
3.1
, the solution
\bm{u}
of (
2.1
) satisfies
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
,
(\nabla\bm{u}\,\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}
a.e. on
\Gamma
, and
\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)})
.

Proof.

There exists a sub-sequence of
\{\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\}
(denoted by the same symbol) and some
\bm{u}^{*}\in\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
such that
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup\bm{u}^{*}
weakly in
\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
as
\epsilon\to 0
.
By the compactness,
\bm{u}_{\epsilon}
converges to
\bm{u}^{*}
strongly in
\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
(\nabla\bm{u}^{*}\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}
a.e. on
\Gamma
.
Now Proposition
2.2
(ii) and the uniqueness of the limit imply
\bm{u}=\bm{u}^{*}
(this also implies the
\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
-weak convergence of the whole sequence
\{\bm{u}_{\epsilon}\}
).
∎

3.1

3.1.
Coordinate transformation to flatten the boundary

The fist step to show Proposition
3.1
(i) is, as usual in a regularity theory for elliptic problems, to localize the PDE using a cut-off function and to transform it into a half-space problem.

3.1.1.
Local coordinates

Since
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
is of
C^{3,1}
-class, each
\bm{x}_{0}\in\Gamma
admits the following graph representation based on a hight function
\rho\in C^{3,1}((-2r,2r)^{d-1})
with some
r>0
after suitable rotation and translation of the
\bm{x}
-coordinate:

•

\bm{x}_{0}=\bm{0}
and
\rho(\bm{0})=0

•

Setting
U(2r,R):=\{\bm{x}=(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{d})\mid\bm{x}^{\prime}\in(-2r,2r)^{d-1},\,\rho(\bm{x}^{\prime})-R<x_{d}<\rho(\bm{x}^{\prime})+R\}
, one has

\begin{aligned} \Omega\cap U(2r,R)&=\{\rho(\bm{x}^{\prime})-R<x_{d}<\rho(\bm{x}^{\prime})\},\\
\Omega^{c}\cap U(2r,R)&=\{\rho(\bm{x}^{\prime})<x_{d}<\rho(\bm{x}^{\prime})+R\},\\
\Gamma\cap U(2r,R)&=\{x_{d}=\rho(\bm{x}^{\prime})\},\end{aligned}\qquad\text{for}\quad\bm{x}^{\prime}\in(-2r,2r)^{d-1}.

Remark 3.2
.

Hereafter we do not explicitly mention the use of rotation and translation because the weak form (
2.5
) is invariant under these congruent transformation, cf.
[
7
, p. 1096]
.
The “sizes”
2r
and
R
, as well as upper bounds for
\|\rho\|_{C^{3,1}((-2r,2r)^{d-1})}
, may be taken uniformly for all
\bm{x}_{0}\in\Gamma
.

In the subsequent discussion, we sometimes require that the last component of a vector be aligned with the normal direction to
\Gamma
.
To this end we introduce the so called “normal transformation”, cf.
[
37
, Theorem I.2.12]
,
[
1
, p. 315]
, defined by the relation

\begin{pmatrix}\bm{x}^{\prime}\\
x_{d}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{y}^{\prime}\\
\rho(\bm{y}^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}+y_{d}\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{\prime}\rho(\bm{y}^{\prime})\\
-1\end{pmatrix}=:\bm{\psi}(\bm{y}),

where
\nabla^{\prime}=\nabla_{\bm{y}^{\prime}}
denotes the gradient operator w.r.t.
\bm{y}^{\prime}
.
If
y_{d}=0
, the Jacobian matrix
\bm{\Psi}:=\nabla_{\bm{y}}\bm{\psi}
is given by

(3.1)

\bm{\Psi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\bm{I}&\nabla^{\prime}\rho\\[2.84526pt]
\hline\cr\\[-11.38109pt]
(\nabla^{\prime}\rho)^{\top}&-1\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\bm{\Psi}^{-1}=\frac{1}{|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}(|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1)\bm{I}-(\nabla^{\prime}\rho)(\nabla^{\prime}\rho)^{\top}&\nabla^{\prime}\rho\\[2.84526pt]
\hline\cr\\[-11.38109pt]
(\nabla^{\prime}\rho)^{\top}&-1\\
\end{array}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad y_{d}=0.

By the implicit function theorem,
\bm{\psi}
is a
C^{2,1}
-diffeomorphism near
\bm{y}=\bm{0}
, which we may suppose is defined for
\bm{y}\in(-r,r)^{d}=:K
.
Setting
\bm{\varphi}:=\bm{\psi}^{-1}
thus
\bm{y}=\bm{\varphi}(\bm{x})\Leftrightarrow\bm{x}=\bm{\psi}(\bm{y})
, we see that the Jacobian matrix of
\bm{\varphi}
is
\bm{\Psi}^{-1}=:\bm{\Phi}
, more precisely,
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\bm{\varphi}(\bm{\psi}(\bm{y}))=\bm{\Phi}(\bm{y})
.
Moreover,
U_{\bm{x}_{0}}:=\bm{\psi}(K)
is a neighborhood of
\bm{x}_{0}
and

\bm{\varphi}(\Omega\cap U_{\bm{x}_{0}})=K_{+}:=\{\bm{y}\in K\mid y_{d}>0\},\quad\bm{\varphi}(\Gamma\cap U_{\bm{x}_{0}})=K_{0}:=\{\bm{y}\in K\mid y_{d}=0\}.

Next we discuss transformation of functions and vectors.
For a scalar function
f
defined in
U_{\bm{x}_{0}}
, its coordinate transformation
\tilde{f}
in
K
is given as
\tilde{f}(\bm{y})=f(\bm{x})=f(\bm{\psi}(\bm{y}))
, which is nothing but a pullback by
\bm{\psi}
.
The chain rule reads
\nabla_{\bm{x}}f=\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{y}}f
, and we have (note that
\|\bm{\Phi}\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}(K)},\|\bm{\Psi}\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}(K)}\leq C
)

|\nabla_{\bm{y}}\tilde{f}|=|\bm{\Psi}^{\top}\,\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{y}}\tilde{f}|\leq C|\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{y}}\tilde{f}|\quad\text{for}\quad\bm{y}\in K.

For a vector-valued function
\bm{v}
defined in
U_{\bm{x}_{0}}
, its pullback to
K
is again denoted by
\tilde{\bm{v}}=\bm{v}\circ\bm{\psi}
.
The chain rule for this case reads
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\bm{v}=(\nabla_{\bm{y}}\tilde{\bm{v}})\bm{\Phi}
, where we recall that the gradient of a vector is defined as

(\nabla_{\bm{x}}\bm{v})_{ij}=\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\quad(i,j=1,\dots,d),\quad\text{that is,}\quad\nabla_{\bm{x}}\bm{v}=(\nabla_{\bm{x}}\otimes\bm{v})^{\top}.

Hereafter we do not indicate the subscripts
\bm{x}
or
\bm{y}
in the gradient operator explicitly when no confusion occurs.

To describe the outer unit normal
\bm{n}
to
\Gamma
in local coordinates, let us define

\bm{N}(\bm{y}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\nabla^{\prime}\rho(\bm{y}^{\prime})|^{2}+1}}\begin{pmatrix}-\nabla^{\prime}\rho(\bm{y}^{\prime})\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\bm{n}(\bm{\psi}(\bm{y}^{\prime},0))\quad\text{for}\quad(\bm{y}^{\prime},0)\in K_{0}.

Considering a pullback
\tilde{\bm{v}}=(\tilde{v}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}
only is not convenient in some cases because its components are not aligned with
\bm{N}
in general.
Thereby we introduce a “transformation of frame” by

\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}=\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}^{-1}\tilde{\bm{v}}\Longleftrightarrow\tilde{\bm{v}}=\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}\quad\text{where}\quad\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}:=\bm{\Psi}|_{y_{d}=0}\quad\text{(cf.\ (\ref{eq: Psi and Phi}))}.

On
K_{0}
, we simply write
\bm{\Psi}
instead of
\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}
.
It is clear that the
\bm{H}^{m}(K)
-norms of
\tilde{\bm{v}}
and
\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}
are equivalent to each other for
m=0,1,\dots
(up to multiplication by a constant depending only on
\Omega
).
Other properties of this new frame are collected as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

With the notation above we have:

(i)
\bm{\Phi}\bm{N}=(|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1)^{-1/2}\underline{\bm{n}}
and
\bm{\Psi}\bm{N}=(|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1)^{1/2}\underline{\bm{n}}
on
K_{0}
, where
\underline{\bm{n}}:=(0,\dots,0,-1)^{\top}
denotes the outer unit normal to
K_{0}
.

(ii)
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0
on
\Gamma\cap U_{\bm{x}_{0}}\Longleftrightarrow\underline{\tilde{v}}_{d}=0
on
K_{0}
.

(iii)
(\nabla\bm{v}\,\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}
on
\Gamma\cap U_{\bm{x}_{0}}\Longleftrightarrow\partial_{d}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}^{\prime}=\bm{0}
on
K_{0}
, where
\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}^{\prime}:=(\underline{\tilde{v}}_{1},\dots,\underline{\tilde{v}}_{d-1})^{\top}
.

(iv) If
\underline{\tilde{v}}_{d}=0
on
K_{0}
, then
\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}^{\prime}=\tilde{\bm{v}}^{\prime}:=(\tilde{v}_{1},\dots,\tilde{v}_{d-1})^{\top}
on
K_{0}
.

Proof.

(i)(iv) These follow from a direct computation using (
3.1
).

(ii) By (i) and symmetry of the matrix
\bm{\Psi}
, the left-hand side in the
\bm{y}
-coordinate reads

\bm{N}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{v}}=0\Longleftrightarrow\bm{N}^{\top}\bm{\Psi}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}=0\Longleftrightarrow-(|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1)^{1/2}\underline{\tilde{v}}_{d}=0.

(iii) By a direct computation in the
\bm{y}
-coordinate and transformed frame (note that
\partial_{d}\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}=\bm{O}
),

\displaystyle(\nabla\bm{v}\,\bm{n})_{\tau}

\displaystyle=(\bm{I}-\bm{N}\bm{N}^{\top})\nabla(\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}})\bm{\Phi}\bm{N}=(|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1)^{-1/2}(\bm{I}-\bm{N}\bm{N}^{\top})\nabla(\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}})\underline{\bm{n}}

\displaystyle=-(|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1)^{-1/2}(\bm{I}-\bm{N}\bm{N}^{\top})\bm{\Psi}\partial_{d}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1}}\left[\bm{\Psi}-\begin{pmatrix}-\nabla^{\prime}\rho\\
1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\end{pmatrix}\right]\partial_{d}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\nabla^{\prime}\rho|^{2}+1}}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{d}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}^{\prime}\\
(\nabla^{\prime}\rho)^{\top}\partial_{d}\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

which yields the desired equality.
∎

3.1.2.
Reduction to a half-space problem

For simplicity in the notation, in the subsequent sections, we agree to omit the subscript
\epsilon
from the solution
(\bm{u}_{\epsilon},p_{\epsilon})
of (
2.5
).
Thus
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{V}\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies
\partial_{i}u_{i}=0
and

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}u_{i}\,\partial_{j}v_{i}\,d\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega}g\frac{\partial_{j}u_{i}\,\partial_{j}v_{i}}{\sqrt{|\nabla\bm{u}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}}\,d\bm{x}-\int_{\Omega}p\,\partial_{i}v_{i}\,d\bm{x}=\int_{\Omega}f_{i}v_{i}\,d\bm{x}\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V},

where we exploit Einstein’s summation convention, omitting the symbols
\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{d}
.

For fixed
\bm{x}_{0}\in\Gamma
, we consider test functions
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}
with
\operatorname{supp}\bm{v}\subset U_{\bm{x}_{0}}
and transform the above weak formulation using
\bm{y}=\bm{\varphi}(\bm{x})
together with

\partial/\partial x_{j}=\Phi_{kj}\partial/\partial y_{k}
(the summation convention is applied to

as well) and denoting the Jacobian determinant by

to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{K_{+}}\partial_{k}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{kj}\,\partial_{l}\tilde{v}_{i}\Phi_{lj}\,|J|\,d\bm{y}+\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{g}\,\frac{\partial_{k}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{kj}\,\partial_{l}\tilde{v}_{i}\Phi_{lj}}{(|(\nabla\tilde{\bm{u}})\bm{\Phi}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}\,|J|\,d\bm{y}-\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{p}\,\partial_{k}\tilde{v}_{i}\Phi_{ki}\,|J|\,d\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{f}_{i}\tilde{v}_{i}\,|J|\,d\bm{y}\qquad\forall\tilde{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+}),\;\tilde{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{N}=0\text{ on }K_{0},\;\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\bm{v}}\subset K,

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{ki}=0\quad\text{in }K_{+}.

Regarding
\tilde{\bm{v}}|J|
as a new test function, we see that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{K_{+}}\partial_{k}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{kj}\,\partial_{l}\tilde{v}_{i}\Phi_{lj}\,d\bm{y}+\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{g}\,\frac{\partial_{k}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{kj}\,\partial_{l}\tilde{v}_{i}\Phi_{lj}}{(|(\nabla\tilde{\bm{u}})\bm{\Phi}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}\,d\bm{y}-\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{p}\,\partial_{k}\tilde{v}_{i}\Phi_{ki}\,d\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{F}_{i}\tilde{v}_{i}\,d\bm{y}\qquad\forall\tilde{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+}),\;\tilde{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{N}=0\text{ on }K_{0},\;\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\bm{v}}\subset K.

Here,

\tilde{F}_{i}:=\tilde{f}_{i}-\partial_{k}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{kj}\,\Phi_{lj}\frac{\partial_{l}|J|}{|J|}-\tilde{g}\,\frac{\partial_{k}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{kj}\,\Phi_{lj}\frac{\partial_{l}|J|}{|J|}}{(|(\nabla\tilde{\bm{u}})\bm{\Phi}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}+\tilde{p}\,\Phi_{ki}\frac{\partial_{k}|J|}{|J|}\quad(i=1,\dots,d)

satisfies, because
\partial_{k}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{kj}/(|(\nabla\tilde{\bm{u}})\bm{\Phi}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2})\leq 1
,

(3.3)

\|\tilde{\bm{F}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(K_{+})}\leq C(\|\tilde{\bm{f}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(K_{+})}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(K_{+})}+\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+})}+\|\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(K_{+})}).

We further transform the frame by
\tilde{u}_{i}=\mathring{\Psi}_{ia}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{a}
and
\tilde{v}_{i}=\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}\underline{\tilde{v}}_{b}
to arrive at

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{K_{+}}\partial_{k}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ia}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{a})\Phi_{kj}\,\partial_{l}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}\underline{\tilde{v}}_{b})\Phi_{lj}\,d\bm{y}+\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{g}\,\frac{\partial_{k}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ia}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{a})\Phi_{kj}\,\partial_{l}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}\underline{\tilde{v}}_{b})\Phi_{lj}}{(|\nabla(\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}\underline{\tilde{\bm{u}}})\bm{\Phi}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}\,d\bm{y}-\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{p}\,\partial_{k}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}\underline{\tilde{v}}_{b})\Phi_{ki}\,d\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{F}_{i}\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}\underline{\tilde{v}}_{b}\,d\bm{y}\qquad\forall\tilde{\bm{v}}\in\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+}),\;\underline{\tilde{v}}_{d}=0\text{ on }K_{0},\;\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\bm{v}}\subset K,

where Lemma
3.1
(ii) has been used.

3.2.
Regularity in the tangential direction

The next step to show Proposition

(i) is to establish regularity w.r.t.
\bm{y}^{\prime}
for the solution
(\tilde{\bm{u}},\tilde{p})
of (

).
If we set
W:=(-r/2,r/2)^{d}
, then
W_{\bm{x}_{0}}:=\bm{\psi}(W)
is an open neighborhood of
\bm{x}_{0}
and
\overline{W_{\bm{x}_{0}}}\subset U_{\bm{x}_{0}}
.
Hence there exists a cut-off function
\theta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K)
such that
\theta\equiv 1
in
\overline{W}
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Tangential regularity of

and that of

are addressed below.

3.2.1.
Estimates for
\nabla\nabla^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{u}}
uniform in
\epsilon

Lemma 3.2
.

We have
\nabla\nabla^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{u}}\in\bm{L}^{2}(K_{+}\cap W)
and

\|\nabla\nabla^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(K_{+}\cap W)}\leq C(\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega\cap U_{\bm{x}_{0}})}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\cap U_{\bm{x}_{0}})}+\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega\cap U_{\bm{x}_{0}})}+\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\cap U_{\bm{x}_{0}})}).

The proof relies on the method of difference quotient, notation of which is introduced as follows.
The standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\bm{e}_{1},\dots,\bm{e}_{d}
.
For
\alpha=1,\dots,d-1
and
0<|h|<(1/2)\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{supp}\theta,\partial K)
, the shift and difference quotient operators in the direction
\bm{e}_{\alpha}
are defined by

(s_{h}^{\alpha}f)(\bm{y})=f(\bm{y}+h\bm{e}_{\alpha}),\qquad D_{h}^{\alpha}f=\frac{s_{h}^{\alpha}f-f}{h}.

Since it is clear that one direction
\alpha
is fixed, we omit the superscript
\alpha
below.
The following formulas are well known (see e.g.
[
11
, Chapter 9]
):

\displaystyle D_{h}(fg)

\displaystyle=f(D_{h}g)+(D_{h}f)(s_{h}g),

\displaystyle\int_{K_{+}}f\,(D_{-h}g)\,d\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{+}}(D_{h}f)\,g\,d\bm{y}\quad\text{if }f,g\in L^{2}(K_{+})\text{ and }\operatorname{supp}f\cup\operatorname{supp}g\subset\operatorname{supp}\theta

\displaystyle\|D_{h}f\|_{L^{p}(K_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(K_{+})}\quad(1\leq p\leq\infty)\quad\text{if }f\in W^{1,p}(K_{+}),\;\operatorname{supp}f\subset\operatorname{supp}\theta.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Take
\underline{\tilde{\bm{v}}}=D_{-h}(\theta^{2}D_{h}\underline{\tilde{\bm{u}}})
in (
3.4
) (note that this is a legitimate test function) to get

\displaystyle\int_{K_{+}}\partial_{k}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ia}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{a})\Phi_{kj}\,\partial_{l}\big(\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}D_{-h}(\theta^{2}D_{h}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{b})\big)\Phi_{lj}\,d\bm{y}+\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{g}\,\frac{\partial_{k}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ia}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{a})\Phi_{kj}\,\partial_{l}\big(\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}D_{-h}(\theta^{2}D_{h}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{b})\big)\Phi_{lj}}{(|\nabla(\mathring{\bm{\Psi}}\underline{\tilde{\bm{u}}})\bm{\Phi}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}\,d\bm{y}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{p}\,\partial_{k}\big(\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}D_{-h}(\theta^{2}D_{h}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{b})\big)\Phi_{ki}\,d\bm{y}=\int_{K_{+}}\tilde{F}_{i}\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}D_{-h}(\theta^{2}D_{h}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{b})\,d\bm{y},

which is rephrased as

J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}=J_{4}.

We divide each of
J_{1}
to
J_{4}
into “principal” and “lower-order” parts in terms of the number of differentiation or difference quotient applied to
\tilde{\bm{u}}
.
For example, for
J_{1}
define
M_{1}
by the following equality:

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{+}}\theta\partial_{k}D_{h}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ia}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{a})\Phi_{kj}\cdot\theta\partial_{l}D_{h}(\mathring{\Psi}_{ib}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{b})\Phi_{lj}\,d\bm{y}+M_{1}.

Then we find that each term of

involves two derivatives and two difference quotients and that at least one of them is applied to
\mathring{\bm{\Psi}},\bm{\Phi},\theta
—namely quantities other than
\tilde{\bm{u}}
.
This observation implies

(3.5)

|M_{1}|\leq C\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+})}(\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+})}+\|\theta\,\nabla D_{h}\tilde{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(K_{+})}),

where we go back from the transformed frame
\underline{\tilde{\bm{u}}}
to the original one
\tilde{\bm{u}}
using an equivalence relation
|\nabla\underline{\tilde{\bm{u}}}|\leq C(|\nabla\tilde{\bm{u}}|+|\tilde{\bm{u}}|)
a.e. on
K_{+}
, etc.
Therefore,

(3.6)

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{+}}\theta^{2}|(\nabla D_{h}\tilde{\bm{u}})\bm{\Phi}|^{2}\,d\bm{y}+M_{1}\geq C\|\theta\,\nabla D_{h}\tilde{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(K_{+})}^{2}+M_{1},

with the lower-order terms

satisfying (
3.5
).
In this way, we agree to omit the detailed expressions of the lower-order terms, only displaying their bounds based on inequalities.
Then, for
J_{2},J_{3},J_{4}

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{K_{0}}\partial_{d}\underline{\tilde{u}}_{d}\,\eta\,d\bm{y}^{\prime}+\int_{K_{0}}\tilde{g}\frac{\partial_{d}\tilde{u}_{i}\Phi_{dj}\Phi_{dj}}{(|(\nabla\tilde{\bm{u}})\bm{\Phi}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}\Psi_{id}\eta\,d\bm{y}^{\prime}.

By (
3.14
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{K_{0}}\tilde{p}\eta\,d\bm{y}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\tilde{\bm{F}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(K_{+})}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(K_{+})}+\|\nabla\tilde{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(K_{+})}+\|\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(K_{+})})\|\tilde{\bm{v}}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+})}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C(\|\tilde{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(K_{0}\cap(-r/2,r/2)^{d})}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(K_{0})}\big)\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(K_{0})},

which proves the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

As shown above, the assumption
g=0
on
\Gamma
is not necessary here.

Setting
\eta:=\partial_{d}(\theta^{2}\,\partial_{d}\tilde{u}_{d}^{(m)})\in H^{1}(K_{+})
, we see from (
3.16
), (
3.20
), and a trace inequality that

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{H^{1/2}(K_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{u}}^{(m)}\|_{\bm{H}^{1/2}(K_{0}\cap(-r/2,r/2)^{d})}+\|\tilde{\bm{u}}^{(m)}\|_{\bm{H}^{1/2}(K_{0})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{u}}^{(m)}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+}\cap(-r/2,r/2)^{d})}+\|\tilde{\bm{u}}^{(m)}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}(K_{+})}).

Applying Lemma
3.8
to this
\eta
and using (
3.3
), we conclude that
I_{32}^{(m)}
also admits a bound as claimed in (

).
This completes the proof of Lemma

.

4.
H^{2}
-regularity for the non-stationary problem

In this section, we focus on the non-stationary Bingham–Navier–Stokes problem (
1.4
)–(
1.7
), retrieving the notation
\nu
to represent the positive viscosity constant.
Similarly to Proposition
2.1
(i), one can interpret (
1.4
)–(
1.6
) as a variational inequality (cf.
[
15
, Section VI.3.2]
) as follows:

(4.1)

(\bm{u}^{\prime}(t),\bm{v}-\bm{u}(t))-\nu(\Delta\bm{u}(t),\bm{v}-\bm{u}(t))+b(\bm{u}(t),\bm{u}(t),\bm{v}-\bm{u}(t)){}+(g(t),|\nabla\bm{v}|-|\nabla\bm{u}(t)|)\geq(\bm{f}(t),\bm{v}-\bm{u}(t))\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}

and for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
with the initial condition
\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0}
in
\bm{H}_{\sigma}
.
Here,
\bm{u}^{\prime}
means the time derivative
\partial\bm{u}/\partial t
, and the trilinear form
b
is given by

b(\bm{u},\bm{v},\bm{w})=\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\frac{\partial v_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}w_{j}dx\qquad\forall\bm{u},\bm{v},\bm{w}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

We also define
B:\bm{V}_{\sigma}\to\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
by
B\bm{u}:=P[(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}]
, where

P:\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\bm{H}_{\sigma}
is the Helmholtz–Leray projection, which can be naturally extended to
\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}\to\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
.
Then we have
\langle B\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle=b(\bm{u},\bm{u},\bm{v})
(recall
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
and
\bm{V}_{\sigma}
) and

(4.2)

\|B\bm{v}-B\bm{w}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}}\leq C_{\rm L}\|\bm{v}-\bm{w}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}\{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}+\|\bm{w}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}\}

for some constant

, which implies the continuity of

(cf.
[
5
, pp. 251–253]
).

Under these settings we obtain the following extension of
[
5
, Theorem 5.9]
to the Bingham–Navier–Stokes problem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be of
C^{3,1}
-class,
\bm{f}\in W^{1,1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))
, and
g\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
,
g\geq 0
.
Assume that there exists
\bm{f}^{*}\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that
\bm{u}_{0}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}\cap\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies

(4.3)

\displaystyle(\nabla\bm{u}_{0}\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}\quad\text{a.e.\ on }\;\Gamma,

(4.4)

\displaystyle(-\nu\Delta\bm{u}_{0}+B\bm{u}_{0}-\bm{f}(0),\bm{v}-\bm{u}_{0})+(g(0),|\nabla\bm{v}|-|\nabla\bm{u}_{0}|)\geq(\bm{f}^{*},\bm{v}-\bm{u}_{0})\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

Then, there exist

T_{*}\in(0,T]
and a unique function
\bm{u}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T_{*};\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap H^{1}(0,T_{*};\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))

such that
\bm{u}
satisfies
\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0}
and (
4.1
) for a.a.
t\in(0,T_{*})
.
If
d=2
,

can be taken as
T
.

Remark 4.1
.

Assumption (

\partial\varphi^{0}(\bm{u}_{0})+B\bm{u}_{0}-P\bm{f}(0)\cap\bm{H}_{\sigma}\neq\emptyset
(
\varphi^{t}
is defined in Subsection
4.3
below).

We can obtain another strong solution with less regular
\bm{f}
,

, and
\bm{u}_{0}
as follows.
Note that the compatibility condition (
4.4
) is not necessary to assume in this case.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be of
C^{3,1}
-class,
\bm{f}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))
,
g\in W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
,

, and
\bm{u}_{0}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
.
Then, there exist
T_{*}\in(0,T]

\bm{u}\in H^{1}(0,T_{*};\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap C([0,T_{*}];\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{*};\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))
such that
\bm{u}
satisfies
\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0}
and (
4.1
) for a.a.
t\in(0,T_{*})
.
If
d=2
,
T_{*}
can be taken as
T
.

Remark 4.2
.

(i) Since the embedding

\bm{u}\in H^{1}(0,T_{*};\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{*};\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))
.

(ii) The well-posedness in this class may be discussed also from the abstract theory of evolution inclusion governed by the subdifferential with nonmonotone perturbation
[
31
]
.
However, in order to apply this abstract theory we need stronger assumption on
g
with respect to
t
, more precisely the Lipschitz continuity (see
[

, p. 273]
).
In this sense, our assumption of Theorem

with respect to

is weaker than

]
.

4.1.
Truncation of
B

The proof of the above theorems is based on the truncation method.
To this end, for
M>0
we introduce a truncated operator
B_{M}:\bm{V}_{\sigma}\to\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
by

B_{M}\bm{v}=\begin{cases}B\bm{v}&{\rm if~}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}\leq M,\\
\displaystyle\frac{M^{2}}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}}B\bm{v}&{\rm if~}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}>M.\\
\end{cases}

The essential idea of this truncation goes back to
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, see also a similar method in
[
12
,
14
]
.
It follows from
[
5
, Chapter 5]
that if
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}\cap\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
then
B_{M}\bm{v}
makes sense in
\bm{H}_{\sigma}
and

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|B_{M}\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm L}M^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|B_{M}\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm L}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad

\displaystyle{\rm if~}d=2,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|B_{M}\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm L}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad

\displaystyle{\rm if~}d=3,

for some constant
C_{\mathrm{L}}=C(\Omega)>0
independent of
M
.
Moreover, similarly to (
4.2
), for all
\bm{v},\bm{w}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
we have

(4.8)

\|B_{M}\bm{v}-B_{M}\bm{w}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}}\leq 2C_{\rm L}\|\bm{v}-\bm{w}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}\{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}+\|\bm{w}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}\}.

Given a nonnegative function
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, define a proper, lower semi-continuous, and convex functional

j_{g}:\bm{H}_{\sigma}\to[0,+\infty]
by

j_{g}(\bm{v})=\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\bm{v}|^{2}\,d\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega}g|\nabla\bm{v}|\,d\bm{x}\quad\text{if }\;\bm{v}\in D(j_{g}):=\bm{V}_{\sigma},\qquad j_{g}(\bm{v})=+\infty\quad\text{if }\;\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{\sigma}\setminus\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

By the definition of a subdifferential, we have
\bm{f}\in\partial j_{g}(\bm{u})
in
\bm{H}_{\sigma}
if and only if
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
satisfies

(4.9)

\nu(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla(\bm{v}-\bm{u}))+(g,|\nabla\bm{v}|-|\nabla\bm{u}|)\geq(\bm{f},\bm{v}-\bm{u})\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

Then Corollary
3.1
implies that
\bm{u}\in\{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}\cap\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)\mid(\nabla\bm{v}\,\bm{n})_{\tau}=\bm{0}\;\text{ a.e.\ on }\Gamma\}=:\bm{W}
and that

(4.10)

\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\rm K}(\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}),

where
C_{\rm K}=C(\Omega,\nu)
is independent of
\bm{f}
and
g
.
In particular, we have
D(\partial j_{g})\subset\bm{W}
.

Under these settings, according to
[
5
]
we define an operator
\Gamma_{g,M}:\bm{H}_{\sigma}\to 2^{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}
by

\Gamma_{g,M}=\partial j_{g}+B_{M},\quad D(\Gamma_{g,M})=D(\partial j_{g}).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
M\in(0,\infty)
.
Then there exists a constant
\alpha_{M}=C(\Omega,\nu,M)>0
independent of
g
such that
\Gamma_{g,M}+\alpha I
is maximal monotone
in
\bm{H}_{\sigma}
for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{M}
, where
I
denotes the identity operator.

Proof.

We closely follow the arguments of
[
5
, Lemma 5.2]
.
We remark that, for operators in a Hilbert space, the concepts of maximal monotonicity (where its dual is identified with itself) and
m
-accretivity coincide.

Step 1.

By
[
5
, pp. 254–255]
there exists a positive constant
c_{M}=C(\Omega,\nu,M)
such that

(4.11)

|\langle B_{M}\bm{w}-B_{M}\bm{z},\bm{w}-\bm{z}\rangle|\leq\frac{\nu}{8}\|\bm{w}-\bm{z}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}+c_{M}\|\bm{w}-\bm{z}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\qquad\forall\bm{w},\bm{z}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

Therefore, we see from (
4.9
), (
4.11
), and (
2.2
) that for all
\bm{w}^{*}\in((1/8)\partial j_{g}+B_{M}+\alpha I)\bm{w}
,
\bm{z}^{*}\in((1/8)\partial j_{g}+B_{M}+\alpha I)\bm{z}
, and
\alpha\geq\alpha_{M}:=c_{M}
, there exist
\bm{f}\in(1/8)\partial j_{g}(\bm{w})
and
\bm{h}\in(1/8)\partial j_{g}(\bm{z})
such that

\displaystyle(\bm{w}^{*}-\bm{z}^{*},\bm{w}-\bm{z})

\displaystyle=(\bm{f}-\bm{h},\bm{w}-\bm{z})+\langle B_{M}\bm{w}-B_{M}\bm{z},\bm{w}-\bm{z}\rangle+\alpha\|\bm{w}-\bm{z}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\nu}{8}(\nabla\bm{w},\nabla(\bm{w}-\bm{z}))+\frac{1}{8}(g,|\nabla\bm{w}|-|\nabla\bm{z}|)+\frac{\nu}{8}(\nabla\bm{z},\nabla(\bm{z}-\bm{w}))+\frac{1}{8}(g,|\nabla\bm{z}|-|\nabla\bm{w}|)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\nu}{8}\|\bm{w}-\bm{z}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}+(\alpha-c_{M})\|\bm{w}-\bm{z}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\alpha-c_{M})\|\bm{w}-\bm{z}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\geq 0.

This implies the monotonicity of
(1/8)\partial j_{g}+B_{M}+\alpha I
, and hence that of
\Gamma_{g,M}+\alpha I
.

Step 2.

Let us consider
(1/4)\partial_{*}j_{g}+B_{M}:\bm{V}_{\sigma}\to 2^{\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}}
where
\partial_{*}
stands for the subdifferential between
\bm{V}_{\sigma}
and
\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
.
Recall that
\partial j_{g}(\bm{u})\subset\partial_{*}j_{g}(\bm{u})
in
\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
and that if
\bm{f}\in\bm{H}_{\sigma}
and
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
satisfy
\bm{f}\in\partial_{*}j_{g}(\bm{u})
then
\partial j_{g}(\bm{u})=\partial_{*}j_{g}(\bm{u})
in
\bm{H}_{\sigma}
.
Now we prove that
(1/4)\partial_{*}j_{g}+B_{M}+\alpha I:\bm{V}_{\sigma}\to 2^{\bm{V}^{\prime}_{\sigma}}
is maximal monotone.
For this we define

j_{1}(\bm{v})=\frac{\nu}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\bm{v}|^{2}\,d\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega}g|\nabla\bm{v}|\,d\bm{x},\quad j_{2}(\bm{v})=\frac{\nu}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\bm{v}|^{2}\,d\bm{x},\quad D(j_{1})=D(j_{2})=\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

Then
(1/4)\partial_{*}j_{1}:\bm{V}_{\sigma}\to 2^{\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}}
is maximal monotone with
D((1/4)\partial_{*}j_{1})=\bm{V}_{\sigma}
.
Moreover,
(1/4)\partial_{*}j_{2}+B_{M}+\alpha I:\bm{V}_{\sigma}\to\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
is single-valued, monotone (by a similar computation to Step 1), and demi-continuous.
Therefore, it follows from the Rockafellar theorem
[
5
, Corollary 2.6]
that the sum

\frac{1}{4}\partial_{*}j_{g}+B_{M}+\alpha I=\frac{1}{4}\partial_{*}j_{1}+\Big(\frac{1}{4}\partial_{*}j_{2}+B_{M}+\alpha I\Big):\bm{V}_{\sigma}\to 2^{\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime}}

is maximal monotone.
Furthermore, again by a similar computation to Step 1, it is coercive.
Hence we can apply the Minty theorem
[
5
, Corollary 2.2]
to find that it is surjective, i.e.,
R((1/4)\partial_{*}j_{g}+B_{M}+\alpha I)=\bm{V}^{\prime}_{\sigma}
.

Step 3.

Let us define
F_{1}:\bm{H}_{\sigma}\to 2^{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}
and
F_{2}:\bm{H}_{\sigma}\to 2^{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}
by

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\partial j_{g},\qquad

\displaystyle D(F_{1})=D(\partial j_{g})\subset\bm{W}\subset\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\partial_{*}j_{g}+B_{M}+\alpha I,\qquad

\displaystyle D(F_{2})=\Big\{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}\,\Big|\,\frac{1}{4}\partial_{*}j_{g}(\bm{v})+B_{M}\bm{v}\subset\bm{H}_{\sigma}\Big\}.

Note that
F_{2}
is the restriction of
(1/4)\partial_{*}j_{g}+B_{M}+\alpha I
to
\bm{H}_{\sigma}
.
It is surjective as a result of Step 2, and so is
F_{2}+I
.
Therefore, again by the Minty theorem, it is maximal monotone (cf.
[
10
, Exemple 2.3.7]
).

Step 4.

It remains to show the maximal monotonicity of
F_{1}+F_{2}
with the domain
D(\partial j_{g})
.
Recall that the minimal section of
F_{2}
is given by

F_{2}^{\circ}\bm{v}:=\left\{\bm{v}^{*}_{\rm min}\in F_{2}\bm{v}\,\middle|\,\|\bm{v}^{*}_{\rm min}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}=\min_{\bm{v}^{*}\in F_{2}\bm{v}}\|\bm{v}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}\right\}.

Let
\bm{u}\in D(\partial j_{g})\subset\bm{W}
and
\bm{f}\in\partial_{*}j_{g}(\bm{u})=\partial j_{g}(\bm{u})
be arbitrary.
Then, from (
4.5
) and (
4.10
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|F_{2}^{\circ}\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+\|B_{M}\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+\alpha\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+C_{\rm L}M^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+C_{\rm L}M^{\frac{3}{2}}C_{\rm K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{\rm L}M^{\frac{3}{2}}C_{\rm K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+C_{\rm L}^{2}M^{3}C_{\rm K}+C_{\rm L}M^{\frac{3}{2}}C_{\rm K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}},

which implies, with some constant
C=C(M,C_{\rm L},C_{\rm K},\alpha,g)>0
,

\|F_{2}^{\circ}\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}\leq\frac{2}{3}\|F_{1}^{\circ}\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+C(\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+1).

Therefore, we conclude from a perturbation result for
m
-accretive operators (see
[
5
, Proposition 3.9]
or
[
10
, Corollaire 2.6]
) that
F_{1}+F_{2}
is maximal monotone in
\bm{H}_{\sigma}
, especially,
D(F_{1}+F_{2})=D(\partial j_{g})
.
∎

4.2.
Discretization in time and a priori estimates

To show Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
, we construct an approximate solution of (
4.1
) by the method of discretization in time.
For this purpose, fix
M>0
, choose a division number
N\in\mathbb{N}
of the time interval
[0,T]
to be sufficiently large (at least
N/T\geq 4\alpha_{M}
), and define the time increment
h_{N}=T/N
.
We introduce discretizations of
\bm{f}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))
and
g\in W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
by

\bm{f}_{k}=\frac{1}{h_{N}}\int_{(k-1)h_{N}}^{kh_{N}}\bm{f}(s)\,ds\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{k}=\frac{1}{h_{N}}\int_{(k-1)h_{N}}^{kh_{N}}g(s)\,ds\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\qquad(k=1,\dots,N),

together with
g_{0}:=g(0)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
We also use
\bm{f}_{0}:=\bm{f}(0)\in\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, which makes sense in the setting of Theorem
4.1
.
Then we set
\varphi_{k}:=j_{g_{k}}
, that is,

\varphi_{k}(\bm{v})=\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\bm{v}|^{2}\,d\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega}g_{k}|\nabla\bm{v}|\,d\bm{x},\quad D(\varphi_{k})=\bm{V}_{\sigma}\qquad(k=1,\dots,N).

Recalling that
\bm{u}_{0}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
is given as an initial datum, we consider the following differential inclusions:

(4.12)

\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}+\partial\varphi_{k}(\bm{u}_{k})+B_{M}\bm{u}_{k}\ni P\bm{f}_{k}\quad\text{in }\;\bm{H}_{\sigma}\qquad(k=1,\dots,N),

which is equivalent to

(4.13)

\Big(\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v}\Big)+\nu(\nabla\bm{u}_{k},\nabla(\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v}))+(B_{M}\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v})+(g_{k},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}|-|\nabla\bm{v}|)\leq(\bm{f}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v})\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

Since
\partial\varphi_{k}+B_{M}+(1/h_{N})I:\bm{H}_{\sigma}\to 2^{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}
is maximal monotone by Lemma
4.1
, there exists
\bm{u}_{k}\in D(\partial\varphi_{k})\subset\bm{W}\subset\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying (
4.12
) for all
k=1,\dots,N
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{\bm{u}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
be defined as above.
Then, for
m=1,2,\ldots,N
we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{m}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\nu\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}(g_{k},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}|)h_{N}\leq C(\|\bm{u}_{0}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}),

where the constant
C=C(\Omega,\nu)
is independent of
m
,
N
, and
M
.

Proof.

Taking
\bm{v}=\bm{0}
in (
4.13
), using the relations (recall (
2.2
) as well)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}},\bm{u}_{k}\right)=\frac{1}{2h_{N}}(\|\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}-\|\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}),

\displaystyle(B_{M}\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k})=0,\quad(\bm{f}_{k},\bm{u}_{k})\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\bm{f}_{k}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and adding the resulting inequalities for
k=1,\dots,m
, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Next we obtain an
\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega))
-a priori estimate for
\{\bm{u}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
.
To this end we rewrite (
4.12
) into the equation

(4.14)

\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}+\bm{u}_{k}^{*}+B_{M}\bm{u}_{k}=P\bm{f}_{k}\quad\text{with some }\bm{u}_{k}^{*}\in\partial\varphi_{k}(\bm{u}_{k})\qquad(k=1,\dots,N).

Lemma 4.3
.

For
m=1,\dots,N
we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\bm{u}_{m}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(g_{m},|\nabla\bm{u}_{m}|)+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\bm{u}^{*}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}h_{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\right\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}(\|\nabla\bm{u}_{0}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}),

where the constant
C_{M}=C(\Omega,\nu,T,M)
is independent of
m
and
N
.

Proof.

By the definition of a subdifferential,
\varphi_{k}(\bm{u}_{k})+(\bm{u}_{k-1}-\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}^{*})\leq\varphi_{k}(\bm{u}_{k-1})
.
Substituting (
4.14
) gives

\varphi_{k}(\bm{u}_{k})+\|\bm{u}^{*}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}+(B_{M}\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{f}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}^{*})h_{N}\leq\varphi_{k}(\bm{u}_{k-1}),

so that, for
k=1,\dots,N
,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}(\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})+(g_{k},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}|)-(g_{k-1},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|)+\|\bm{u}^{*}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\bm{f}_{k}-B_{M}\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}^{*})h_{N}+(g_{k}-g_{k-1},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|\bm{u}^{*}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}+\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}((k-1)h_{N},kh_{N};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}-(B_{M}\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}^{*})h_{N}+(g_{k}-g_{k-1},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|),

where we have used
\|\bm{f}_{k}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq(1/\sqrt{h_{N}})\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}((k-1)h_{N},kh_{N};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}
.
Since
\bm{u}_{k}^{*}\in\partial\varphi_{k}(\bm{u}_{k})
implies
\|\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\bm{u}_{k}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+\|g_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)})
, cf. (
4.10
), we find from (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle|(B_{M}\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}^{*})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\|\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\bm{u}_{k}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}\leq C_{M}(\|\bm{u}_{k}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{3/2}+\|g_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\bm{u}_{k}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\bm{u}^{*}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+C_{M}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{N}}}\|g\|_{L^{2}((k-1)h_{N},kh_{N};H^{1}(\Omega))}+1\Big),

where we have used
\|g_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq(1/\sqrt{h_{N}})\|g\|_{L^{2}((k-1)h_{N},kh_{N};H^{1}(\Omega))}
.
Finally, by direct computation one has

\displaystyle(g_{k}-g_{k-1},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{N}}\int_{(k-1)h_{N}}^{kh_{N}}\int_{\min\{0,s-h_{N}\}}^{s}(g^{\prime}(r),|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|)\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(\min\{(k-2)h_{N},0\},kh_{N};L^{2}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Substituting the two estimates above into (
4.16
) and absorbing
\|\bm{u}^{*}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}
to the left-hand side, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}(\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})+(g_{k},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}|)-(g_{k-1},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|)+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}^{*}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle C_{M}(\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}((k-1)h_{N},kh_{N};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}((k-1)h_{N},kh_{N};H^{1}(\Omega))}h_{N}^{1/2}+h_{N})

\displaystyle\qquad+\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(\min\{(k-2)h_{N},0\},kh_{N};L^{2}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Now, if
m:=\operatorname{argmax}_{k=0,\dots,N}\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 1
, then we add the above estimate for
k=1,\dots,m
to obtain

(4.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}(\|\nabla\bm{u}_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|\nabla\bm{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})+(g_{m},|\nabla\bm{u}_{m}|)-(g_{0},|\nabla\bm{u}_{0}|)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|\bm{u}^{*}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{M}(\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}+T)+\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\bm{u}_{m}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{M}(\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+T)+\frac{\nu}{4}\|\nabla\bm{u}_{m}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

which gives us a bound of
\max_{k=0,\dots,N}\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
independent of
N
(note:
\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega,T)\|g\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}
).
A similar computation to (
4.17
), in which
m
is replaced by an arbitrary integer, proves (
4.15
) for the first 3 terms on the left-hand side.
The fourth and fifth terms can be addressed by

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\bm{u}_{k}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\frac{1}{h_{N}}\|g\|_{L^{2}((k-1)h_{N},kh_{N};H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}

\displaystyle=P\bm{f}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k}^{*}-B_{M}\bm{u}_{k},\quad\|B_{M}\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\leq C_{M}(\|\bm{u}_{k}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+\|g_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}),

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Finally we derive an
H^{1}(0,T;\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega))
-like a priori estimate for
\{\bm{u}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
to show Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, let
M\geq\|\bm{u}_{0}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}
.
Then, for
m=1,\dots,N
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\bm{u}_{m}-\bm{u}_{m-1}}{h_{N}}\right\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|\nabla\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}h_{N}

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{M}(\|\bm{u}_{0}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\bm{f}\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bm{f}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}),

where the constant
C_{M}=C(\Omega,\nu,T,M)
is independent of
m
and
N
.

Proof.

For the functional
j_{2}
defined in the Step 2 of the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we see that
\partial j_{2}=-(\nu/2)P\Delta
and that
F:=(1/4)\partial j_{2}+B_{M}+\alpha_{M}I
is monotone.
In addition, observe that (
4.13
) is rewritten as

(4.18)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v}\Big)+\frac{7\nu}{8}(\nabla\bm{u}_{k},\nabla(\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v}))+(F\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v})+(g_{k},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}|-|\nabla\bm{v}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\bm{f}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v})+\alpha_{M}(\bm{u}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{v})\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma},\quad k=1,\dots,N.

Taking
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{k-1}
in (
4.18
), taking
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{k}
in (
4.18
) with
k
replaced by
k-1
, adding the two inequalities, and applying the monotonicity of
F
, we obtain for
k=2,\dots,N

\displaystyle\frac{\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}-\|\bm{u}_{k-1}-\bm{u}_{k-2}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}}{2h_{N}}+\frac{\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-2}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}}{2h_{N}}+\frac{7\nu}{8}\|\nabla(\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle(\bm{f}_{k}-\bm{f}_{k-1},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1})+\alpha_{M}\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}-(g_{k}-g_{k-1},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}|-|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|)

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle\|\bm{f}_{k}-\bm{f}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+\alpha_{M}\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+C\|g_{k}-g_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{3\nu}{8}\|\nabla(\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Therefore,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}-\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{k-1}-\bm{u}_{k-2}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\nu\Big\|\nabla\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}h_{N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\bm{f}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}((k-2)h_{N},kh_{N};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+2\alpha_{M}\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}+2C\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((k-2)h_{N},kh_{N};L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2},

where
\|\bm{f}_{k}-\bm{f}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{f}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}((k-2)h_{N},kh_{N};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}
and
\|g_{k}-g_{k-1}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{h_{N}}\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}((k-2)h_{N},kh_{N};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}
are used.
Now we add (
4.19
) for
k=2,\dots,m
, argue similarly as in (
4.17
), and then substitute (
4.15
) to arrive at

(4.20)

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{m}-\bm{u}_{m-1}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\nu\sum_{k=2}^{m}\Big\|\nabla\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}h_{N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{0}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+C_{M}(\|\bm{u}_{0}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\|\bm{f}\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2})\qquad(m=2,\dots,N).

On the other hand, recall that (
4.4
) holds, in which we have
B=B_{M}
since
M\geq\|\bm{u}_{0}\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}
.
Then it follows that (recall that
\bm{f}_{0}
means
\bm{f}(0)
)

\frac{7\nu}{8}(\nabla\bm{u}_{0},\nabla(\bm{u}_{0}-\bm{v}))+(F\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{0}-\bm{v})+(g_{0},|\nabla\bm{u}_{0}|-|\nabla\bm{v}|)\leq(\bm{f}_{0}+\bm{f}^{*}+\alpha_{M}\bm{u}_{0},\bm{u}_{0}-\bm{v})\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}.

Taking
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{1}
above, taking
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{0}
in (
4.18
) for
k=1
, adding the two inequalities, and dividing the both sides by
h_{N}
, we derive (recall
N
is chosen so large that
h_{N}\alpha_{M}\leq 1/4
)

(4.21)

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{0}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\frac{7\nu}{8}\Big\|\nabla\frac{\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{0}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}h_{N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\bm{f}_{1}-\bm{f}_{0}-\bm{f}^{*},\frac{\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{0}}{h_{N}}\Big)+h_{N}\alpha_{M}\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{0}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}-\frac{1}{h_{N}}(g_{1}-g_{0},|\nabla\bm{u}_{1}|-|\nabla\bm{u}_{0}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\bm{f}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,h_{N};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|\bm{f}^{*}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{0}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+C\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,h_{N};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\frac{3\nu}{8}\Big\|\nabla\frac{\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{0}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}h_{N}.

Combining (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) concludes the desired estimate.
∎

4.3.
Limiting procedure as
N\to\infty

We continue to deal with
\{\bm{u}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{N}
constructed in the previous subsection.
Let us introduce a piecewise-linear interpolation
\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}:[0,T]\to\bm{H}_{\sigma}
and piecewise-constant interpolations
\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}:(0,T]\to\bm{H}_{\sigma}
and
\bar{\bm{f}}_{N}:(0,T]\to\bm{H}_{\sigma}
as follows: for
k=1,\dots,N
,

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{kh_{N}-t}{h_{N}}\bm{u}_{k-1}+\frac{t-(k-1)h_{N}}{h_{N}}\bm{u}_{k}\qquad

\displaystyle{\rm for~}t\in[(k-1)h_{N},kh_{N}],

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{u}_{k},\quad\bar{\bm{f}}_{N}(t)=\bm{f}_{k}\qquad

\displaystyle{\rm for~}t\in((k-1)h_{N},kh_{N}].

Define
\Phi_{N},\Phi:L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Phi_{N}(\bm{\upsilon})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{(k-1)h_{N}}^{kh_{N}}\varphi_{k}(\bm{\upsilon}(t))\,dt,\qquad\Phi(\bm{\upsilon})=\int_{0}^{T}\varphi^{t}(\bm{\upsilon}(t))\,dt,

where
\varphi^{t}:=j_{g(t)}
, that is,
\varphi^{t}(\bm{v})=(\nu/2)\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(g(t),|\nabla\bm{v}|)
for each
t\in[0,T]
.
By direct computation, we see that
\Phi_{N}(\bm{\upsilon})\to\Phi(\bm{\upsilon})
as
N\to\infty
for
\bm{\upsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})

and that
|\Phi_{N}(\bm{\upsilon}_{1})-\Phi_{N}(\bm{\upsilon}_{2})|\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\|\bm{\upsilon}_{1}-\bm{\upsilon}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}
.

Now we observe that (
4.13
) can be reformulated as the following space-time variational inequality:

(4.22)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}^{\prime}(t),\bm{\upsilon}(t)-\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}(t))dt+\int_{0}^{T}(B_{M}\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}(t),\bm{\upsilon}(t)-\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}(t))dt+\Phi_{N}(\bm{\upsilon})-\Phi_{N}(\bar{\bm{v}}_{N})\geq\int_{0}^{T}(\bar{\bm{f}}_{N}(t),\bm{\upsilon}(t)-\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}(t))dt

for all
\bm{\upsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})
.
From this we construct a solution of (
4.1
) in which
B
is replaced with its truncation
B_{M}
.
The following result is an extension of
[
5
, Proposition 5.15]
to a time-dependent nonlinear multivalued operator
\partial\varphi^{t}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under the assumptions in Theorem
4.1
, there exists at least one
\bm{u}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap H^{1}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{W})
such that
\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0}
and

(4.23)

(\bm{u}^{\prime}(t),\bm{v}-\bm{u}(t))-\nu(\Delta\bm{u}(t),\bm{v}-\bm{u}(t))+(B_{M}\bm{u}(t),\bm{v}-\bm{u}(t))+(g(t),|\nabla\bm{v}|-|\nabla\bm{u}(t)|)\geq(\bm{f}(t),\bm{v}-\bm{u}(t))

for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
and a.a.
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

Collecting the estimates in Lemmas
4.2
,
4.3
, and
4.4
, we deduce that, for sufficiently large
N
,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})}+\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}+\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}+\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M},

(4.25)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}+\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M},

(4.26)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}-\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}^{2}=\frac{h_{N}^{2}}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big\|\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\Big\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}\leq\frac{h_{N}^{2}}{3}C_{M}\to 0\quad\text{as $N\to\infty$},

where
C_{M}=C(\Omega,\nu,T,\bm{f},g,\bm{u}_{0},\bm{f}^{*},M)
is independent of
N
.
As a result of the weak compactness and of the Aubin–Lions compactness theorem
[
34
, Corollary 4]
, we can find some
\bm{u}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap H^{1}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W})
,
\bar{\bm{u}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W})
, and subsequence
\{N_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}^{\prime}\to\bm{u}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad{\rm weakly~star~in~}L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\to\bm{u}

\displaystyle\quad{\rm weakly~star~in~}L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W}),

(4.27)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\to\bm{u}

\displaystyle\quad{\rm strongly~in~}C([0,T];\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\to\bar{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle\quad{\rm weakly~star~in~}L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W}),

as
n\to\infty
.
By virtue of (
4.26
) and (
4.27
), one has

\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}\leq\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}+\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$},

so that
\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\to\bm{u}
strongly in
L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})
and
\bm{u}=\bar{\bm{u}}
.
This implies that
B_{M}\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\to B_{M}\bm{u}
strongly in
L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma}^{\prime})
in view of (
4.8
) and that

|\Phi_{N_{n}}(\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}})-\Phi(\bm{u})|\leq|\Phi_{N_{n}}(\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}})-\Phi_{N_{n}}(\bm{u})|+|\Phi_{N_{n}}(\bm{u})-\Phi(\bm{u})|\\
\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Therefore, setting
N=N_{n}
and letting
n\to+\infty
in (
4.22
), we obtain, for all
\bm{\upsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\bm{u}^{\prime}(t),\bm{\upsilon}(t)-\bm{u}(t))dt+\int_{0}^{T}(B_{M}\bm{u}(t),\bm{\upsilon}(t)-\bm{u}(t))dt+\Phi(\bm{\upsilon})-\Phi(\bm{u})\geq\int_{0}^{T}(\bm{f}(t),\bm{\upsilon}(t)-\bm{u}(t))dt.

In particular,
\bm{u}(t)\in D(\partial\varphi^{t})\subset\bm{W}
for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
and the following evolution equation holds:

(4.28)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\bm{u}^{\prime}(t)+\partial\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))+B_{M}\bm{u}(t)\ni P\bm{f}(t)\quad\mbox{in~}\bm{H}_{\sigma},\quad\mbox{for~a.a.~}t\in(0,T),\\
\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0}\quad\mbox{in~}\bm{H}_{\sigma}.\end{cases}

This
\bm{u}
satisfies the equivalent variational formulation (
4.23
).
Finally, using (
4.5
), (
4.28
), and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|P\bm{f}(t)-\bm{u}^{\prime}(t)-B_{M}\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+\|g(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}(\|\bm{f}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{u}^{\prime}(t)\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}+\|g(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+1)+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for a.a.\ $t\in(0,T)$},

which yields
\bm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{W})
.
This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
4.1

Let
\bm{u}
be a solution obtained by Proposition
4.1
, which means the existence of some
\bm{u}^{*}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})
such that

\bm{u}^{\prime}(t)+\bm{u}^{*}(t)+B_{M}\bm{u}(t)=P\bm{f}(t)\text{ in }\bm{H}_{\sigma}\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{u}^{*}(t)\in\partial\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))\text{ in }\bm{H}_{\sigma}\quad\text{for a.a.\ }t\in(0,T).

Let us recall a chain rule for a time-dependent convex functional, which is essentially due to
[
2
, Lemma 2.10]
,
[
24
, Theorem 2.1.1]
,
[
31
, Proposition 3.4]
,
[
32
, Lemma 2.4]
, and
[
33
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The mapping
t\mapsto\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))
is differentiable a.e. on
(0,T)
, and it satisfies

(4.29)

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))-(\bm{u}^{*}(t),\bm{u}^{\prime}(t))\leq\|g^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{for a.a.\ }t\in(0,T).

Proof.

An argument based on the Moreau–Yosida regularization (see e.g.
[
24
, p. 29]
) shows that
t\mapsto\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))
is absolutely continuous on
[0,T]
, which implies its differentiability.
Since
\bm{u}^{\prime}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})
, we have

\frac{\bm{u}(t+h)-\bm{u}(t)}{h}=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\bm{u}^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau\to\bm{u}^{\prime}(t)\quad{\rm in~}\bm{H}_{\sigma}\text{ as $h\to 0$, for a.a.\ $t\in(0,T)$}.

Next, since
\bm{u}^{*}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})
, for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
there exists
\{h_{n}\}
,
h_{n}\searrow 0
such that

\bm{u}^{*}(t+h_{n})\in\partial\varphi^{t+h_{n}}(\bm{u}(t+h_{n})),\quad\bm{u}^{*}(t+h_{n})\to\bm{u}^{*}(t)\quad{\rm in~}\bm{H}_{\sigma}\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

By the definition of the subdifferential of
\varphi^{t+h_{n}}
,

\displaystyle\varphi^{t+h_{n}}(\bm{u}(t+h_{n}))-\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))-(\bm{u}(t+h_{n})-\bm{u}(t),\bm{u}^{*}(t+h_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\varphi^{t+h_{n}}(\bm{u}(t))-\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{t+h_{n}}\|g^{\prime}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}d\tau\|\nabla\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

which proves (
4.29
) after dividing by
h_{n}
and taking
n\to\infty
.
∎

Let us start the proof of the existence part in Theorem
4.1
.
For this purpose, it suffices to establish an upper bound of
\|\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{V}_{\sigma}}
that is uniform in
t
and independent of
M
.
By Lemma
4.2
, we have
\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}\leq C(\Omega,\nu,\bm{u}_{0},\bm{f})
.
Next we apply Lemma
4.5
to obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))+\|\bm{u}^{*}(t)\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\leq(\bm{u}^{*}(t),\bm{f}(t)-B_{M}\bm{u}(t))+\|g^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for a.a.\ $t\in(0,T)$,}

where we observe from (
4.7
), (
2.2
), (
4.10
), and Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle(\bm{u}^{*}(t),\bm{f}(t)-B_{M}\bm{u}(t))

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{u}^{*}(t)\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}\big[\|\bm{f}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{3/2}(\|\bm{u}^{*}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}+\|g(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2})\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}^{*}(t)\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+C(\|\bm{f}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{6}+\|g(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}).

Consequently, for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
,

\frac{d}{ds}\varphi^{s}(\bm{u}(t))\leq C(\|\bm{f}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|g(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2})+\|g^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{6}.

If we set
y(t):=\varphi^{t}(\bm{u}(t))+1\geq 1
, this yields

(4.30)

y^{\prime}(t)\leq\underbrace{C(\|\bm{f}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|g(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|g^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})}_{=:a(t)}y(t)^{2},\quad C=C(\Omega,\nu),

where
\int_{0}^{T}a(s)\,ds\leq C(\Omega,\nu,\bm{f},g,\bm{u}_{0})
independently of
M
by Lemma
4.2
. Then, for suitably small
t>0
,

y(t)\leq\frac{y(0)}{1-y(0)\int_{0}^{t}a(s)\,ds}.

It is possible to choose
T^{*}=C(\Omega,\nu,\bm{f},g,\bm{u}_{0})
in such a way that
y(0)\int_{0}^{T^{*}}a(t)\,dt\leq 1/2
, which gives us a bound
\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};\bm{V}_{\sigma})}\leq C(\Omega,\nu,\bm{f},g,\bm{u}_{0})
independently of
M
.
We remark that in case
d=2
we need not restrict
T
to
T^{*}
.
In fact, use (
4.6
) in place of (
4.7
) to arrive at a similar estimate to (
4.30
), where the power of
y(t)
in the right-hand side is replaced by 1.
Then Gronwall’s inequality enables us to bound
\bm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})
globally in
(0,T)
.

Now fix
M
to be greater than the
L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};\bm{V}_{\sigma})
-bound above, and consider
\bm{u}
constructed in Proposition
4.1
.
Since
B_{M}\bm{u}(t)=B\bm{u}(t)
for a.a.
t\in(0,T^{*})
, this
\bm{u}
indeed solves (
4.1
) on
(0,T^{*})
, which proves the existence part.

The uniqueness part of Theorem
4.1
is easy.
In fact, let
\bm{v}
and
\bm{u}
be two solutions of (
4.1
).
Then, we take
\bm{v}(t)
(resp.
\bm{u}(t)
) in the variational inequality that
\bm{u}(t)
(resp.
\bm{v}(t)
) satisfies, and add the resulting inequalities to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\bm{v}(t)-\bm{u}(t)\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla(\bm{v}(t)-\bm{u}(t))\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq|b(\bm{v}(t),\bm{v}(t),\bm{v}(t)-\bm{u}(t))-b(\bm{u}(t),\bm{u}(t),\bm{v}(t)-\bm{u}(t))|

\displaystyle=|b(\bm{v}(t)-\bm{u}(t),\bm{v}(t)-\bm{u}(t),\bm{u}(t))|.

Then a standard argument, e.g.
[
35
, Chapter 3]
, leads to
\bm{v}=\bm{u}
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

4.5.
Proof of Theorem
4.2

We exploit the same strategy as in Theorem
4.1
, however without Lemma
4.4
this time (Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
remain to hold in the setting of Theorem
4.2
).
The difficulty is two folded: 1) the strong convergence of
\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}
in
L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})
is not trivial if we do not know the boundedness of
\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}^{\prime}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})
; 2)
\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}
may not belong to
L^{2}(0,h_{N};\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))
because we only assume
\bm{u}_{0}\in\bm{V}_{\sigma}
.

To address issue 1), we devise an auxiliary estimate for Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma 4.6
.

We have

(4.31)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\nabla(\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{M}(\|\nabla\bm{u}_{0}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|g\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}),

where the constant
C_{M}=C(\Omega,\nu,T,M)
is independent of
N
.

Proof.

For
k=1,\dots,N
, we take
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{k-1}
in (
4.13
) to get

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}}{h_{N}}\right\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}h_{N}+\frac{\nu}{2}(\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla(\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})+(g_{k},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k}|)-(g_{k-1},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|)

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle-(B_{M}\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{f}_{k},\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1})+(g_{k}-g_{k-1},|\nabla\bm{u}_{k-1}|).

Adding this for
k=1,\dots,N
and applying the estimates known from Lemma
4.3
, we obtain (
4.31
).
∎

Next, to resolve issue 2), we introduce another piecewise linear interpolation of
\{\bm{u}_{k}\}
by

\bm{w}_{N}(t)=\begin{cases}\bm{u}_{1}&\quad{\rm if~}t\in[0,h_{N}],\\
\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}(t)&\quad{\rm if~}t\in[h_{N},T].\end{cases}

Then we have
\bm{w}_{N}(t)\in\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)
for all
t\in[0,T]
and

\displaystyle\|\bm{w}_{N}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\bm{w}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{h_{N}}{2}\|\bm{u}_{1}\|_{\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2},

which implies
\|\bm{w}_{N}\|_{H^{1}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W})}\leq C(\Omega,\nu,T,\bm{f},g,\bm{u}_{0},M)
by Lemma
4.3
.
Combining this with the boundedness of
\{\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}\}
and
\{\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}\}
already known, we can find some
\bm{u}\in H^{1}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})
,
\bar{\bm{u}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W})
,
\bm{w}\in H^{1}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W})
and subsequence
\{N_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\to\bm{u}

\displaystyle\quad{\rm weakly~star~in~}H^{1}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\to\bm{u}

\displaystyle\quad{\rm strongly~in~}L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\to\bar{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle\quad{\rm weakly~star~in~}L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W}),

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{N_{n}}\to\bm{w}

\displaystyle\quad\text{weakly in }H^{1}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W}),

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{N_{n}}\to\bm{w}

\displaystyle\quad\text{strongly in }C([0,T];\bm{H}_{\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma}),

when
n\to\infty
, as a result of weak compactness and the Aubin–Lions theorem.

Now we claim
\bm{u}=\bar{\bm{u}}=\bm{w}
. In fact, since

\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})}^{2}=\frac{h_{N}}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}\to 0\quad(N\to\infty)

by direct computation and Lemma
4.2
, we have

\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-{\bm{u}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})}\leq\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})}+\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})}\to 0\quad(n\to\infty),

hence
\bm{u}=\bar{\bm{u}}
.
A similar computation based on
\|{\bm{w}}_{N}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{N}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}_{\sigma})}^{2}=(h_{N}/3)\|\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{0}\|_{\bm{H}_{\sigma}}^{2}
shows
\bm{u}=\bm{w}
.

We note that these counterparts for the
\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
-norm also hold, that is,

\|\nabla(\bar{\bm{u}}_{N}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{N})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}=\frac{h_{N}}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\nabla(\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{u}_{k-1})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\to 0,\quad\|\nabla({\bm{w}}_{N}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{N})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\to 0\quad(N\to\infty)

by Lemma
4.3
. Therefore, we conclude

\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}\leq\|\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}+\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}-{\bm{w}}_{N_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}+\|{\bm{w}}_{N_{n}}-\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})}\to 0\quad(n\to\infty),

which establishes the strong convergence of
\{\bar{\bm{u}}_{N_{n}}\}
in
L^{2}(0,T;\bm{V}_{\sigma})
.

Now we can proceed completely in the same way as in Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.1
, finishing the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Abstract
. The paper examines the problems related to the well-posedness of the Cucker-Smale model with communication restricted to the q q -closest neighbors, known also as the Cucker-Dong model. With agents oscillating on the boundary of different clusters, the system becomes difficult to precisely define, which leads to further problems with kinetic limits as the number of agents tends to infinity. We introduce the fuzzy q q -closest system, which circumvents the issues with well-posedness. For such a system we prove a stability estimate for measure-valued solutions and perform the kinetic mean-field limit.

1.
Introduction

The study of interacting particle systems is one of the prolific subjects of
mathematical modelling, with a wide spectrum of applications ranging from aggregation of bacteria and flocking of birds to opinion dynamics, distribution of goods and traffic flows. Our main interest is the Cucker-Smale (CS) flocking model
[
10
]
, which describes the dynamics of an ensemble of autonomous self-propelled agents with a tendency to flock. The inter-particle interactions in the CS model are all-to-all and symmetric, akin to many forces typical for classical physics. However, recently it has been recognized that asymetric interactions with finite range are fundamental from the point of view of applications, for instance, in systems related to human and animal behavior. Among CS-type models with non-standard interactions is the so called
q
-closest model
[
11
]
also known as the Cucker-Dong (CD) model, which describes the motion of
N
particles following the ODE system

(1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i}\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle\dot{v}_{i}=-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{N}}_{i}}(v_{i}-v_{j})\end{cases}

where
(x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t))
denotes the position and velocity of
i
th particle at the time
t\geq 0
. Set
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}=\mathcal{N}_{i}(t)
consists of the indices of
q
-closest neighbors of the
i
th agent at the time
t
. Throughout the paper we identify the indices in
\mathcal{N}_{i}
with their corresponding particles.

Our goal is to tackle the issue of ill-posedness of (
1
) related to the problems with the rigorous definition of
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
. We introduce a fuzzy
q-closest model which is well-posed. Moreover we propose a kinetic variant of the said model and prove a stability estimate for its measure-valued solutions in the
2
-Wasserstein metric. Finally, we use the stability estimate to derive solutions to the kinetic equation as a mean-field limit of the ODE system. We end our considerations with propositions of further developments related to the mean-field limit and asymptotics for the original CD model. The latter point is left unanswered, we have conjectures for large time behavior, but still without the complete proof for
q\leq\frac{N}{2}
.

The main feature of system (
1
) is its combinatorical character.
The interactions depend on the inter-particle distance only as far as definition of the neighbor sets
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
is concerned. This is different to the typical CS and CD setting, where the so-called communication weight is involved; basically each summant on the right-hand side of the second equation of (
1
) is multiplied by a function of the inter-particle distance
\psi(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)
. The main purpose of the communication weight
\psi
is to ensure that the interactions decay with the distance, i.e.
\psi(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)\to 0
as
|x_{i}-x_{j}|\to+\infty
. From the perspective of the CS model, one can differentiate between two types of communication: regular (say bounded and Lipschitz continuous) and singular (e.g.
\psi(r)=r^{-\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
). The case of regular communication weight has been thoroughly investigated over past years leading to results in asymptotics and large-time behavior
[
16
,
29
,
30
]
, influence of additional forces
[
4
,
5
,
15
]
and control
[
1
,
3
,
8
]
. Other directions include stability and mean-field limit
[
16
,
17
]
. The case of the singular communication weight is more delicate resulting in interesting behaviors such as sticking of the trajectories of the particles
[
31
]
, or for
\alpha\geq 1
complete collision-avoidance
[
6
,
19
,
36
]
. The issue of well-posedness is also delicate; the weakly singular case with
\alpha\in(0,1)
has beed studied in
[
32
]
followed by
[
25
]
and more recently in
[
9
]
and
[
34
]
, while the strongly singular case remains completely open on the kinetic level (well posedness for the ODE was proved in
[
6
]
). For more information on the CS models and its variants for both regular and singular communication weight we recommend the surveys
[
7
,
20
]
.

Here, we assume that the communication weight is simply
\psi\equiv 1
, which together with the influence restricted to the neighbor sets introduces an interesting combinatorical challenge. From the perspective of well-posedness regular communication does not introduce any more difficulty than
\psi\equiv 1
, albeit computations would be somewhat more complicated. Singular communication weights, naturally introduce problems, which are mostly unrelated to the essence of the CD model – the rigorous treatment of the neighbor sets. We decided to focus solely on the problem of well-posedness related to the neighbor sets.

Interestingly, without the decay introduced by the communication weight, the interaction between the particles are more chaotic, effectively switching between
0
(off) and
1
(on) depending on how the particles are situated in relation to each other. Such a perspective is uncommon for the standard analysis where almost all quantities are depending on the distance, they decrease and finally vanish for large distances. We elaborate on this issue at the end of the paper in Section
4
.

1.1.
Critical issue of the rigorous definition of
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}

Let us discuss the neighbor set
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
. Its mathematically rigorous definition is not straightforward. The situation is clear if no two agents are situated at the same distance from
x_{i}
. Then the definition of
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
can be stated as follows:

j\in{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
if
i\neq j
and

(2)

\sharp\{x_{k}:{\rm dist}(x_{i},x_{k})<{\rm dist}(x_{i},x_{j})\}<q.

Here
\sharp{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
is the cardinality of
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
. Then of course we find that
\sharp{\mathcal{N}}_{i}=q
.
However the general case is more complex and the above definition does not fit to all cases. Problems occur when exactly
q_{0}<q
particles satisfy the sharp equation in (
2
) and there are
q_{1}>q-q_{0}
particles at the next smallest admissible distance from
x_{i}
(with the same distance from
x_{i}
). Thus, in order to have
\sharp{\mathcal{N}}_{i}=q
, we need to somehow choose
q-q_{0}
particles from
q_{1}
particles. This is impossible without additional rules. There are a couple of ways to circumvent the above issue.

•

Cucker and Dong
[
11
]
prescribe an arbitrary order to the particles and define the set
\mathcal{N}_{i}
by (
2
) provided that
\sharp\mathcal{N}_{i}=q
and if it
\sharp\mathcal{N}_{i}>q
the definition takes
q-q_{0}
particles with the smallest indices from the the group of
q_{1}
particles with the same distance from
x_{i}
.
Then
\sharp{\mathcal{N}}_{i}=q
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
is well defined.

•

Another option would be to simply take all
q_{1}
particles which are at the same distance from
x_{i}
and accept that at times
\sharp{\mathcal{N}}_{i}>q
. Then replace the factor
\frac{1}{q}
in (
1
) with
\frac{1}{\sharp{\mathcal{N}}_{i}}
, which then is ”usually” (but not always) equal to
q
.

Regardless of the chosen strategy we can easily show that the system is ill posed from the viewpoint of stability–uniqueness properties, see Example
1
below.
Our goal is to modify the definition of
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
so that it retains its original features, while simultaneously allowing well-posedness of the system. To this end we introduce the fuzzy
q
-closest alignment system.

Fix
\sigma>0
and a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
\phi\geq 0
and

(3)

{\rm supp}\,\phi\subset B(0,\sigma)\mbox{ \ \ and \ \ }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)dx=1.

The fuzzy variant of (
1
), supplemented by initial data, then reads

(4)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i},&(x_{i}(0),v_{i}(0))=(x_{0i},v_{0i}),\\
\displaystyle\dot{v}_{i}=-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B(x_{i},R)}\phi(z-x_{j})(v_{i}-v_{j})\,{\rm d}z,\end{cases}

where
R=R(x_{i}(t))
is the unique radius such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B(x_{i},R(x_{i}(t)))}\phi(z-x_{j})\,{\rm d}z=q.

Intuitively, the particles’ perception is fuzzy – if a neighbor is situated at the position
x_{i}
, it is perceived to be spread in an area around
x_{i}
with radius
\sigma
. Such a phenomenon seems realistic as noise and other inadequacies related to perception and communication occur routinely in real life. Observe that for any
0\leq q\leq N
existence and uniqueness of
R
follows from smoothness of
\phi
. We emphasise that this definition is valid for all real
q\in[0,N]
. Thanks to this observation system (
4
) can be restated in a more convenient and more general way as a kinetic equation, where the ODE concept of particles looses its meaning. First we fix any

\eta>0,\qquad q=\eta N

which serves as the proportion of agents that should be included in the neighbor sets. Then at each
t\geq 0
let
f=f_{t}(x,v)
be the probabilistic distribution of particles moving through position
x
with velocity
v
. We define the kinetic, fuzzy
q
-closest alignment equation as follows

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}f_{t}+v\cdot\nabla f_{t}+{\rm div}_{v}(F[f_{t}]f_{t})&=0,\\
F[f_{t}](x,v)&=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{B(x,R[f_{t}](x))}\phi(z-y)(v-w)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}f_{t}(y,w),\end{aligned}

where the radius
R
is defined for any probability measure
\mu
by

(6)

R[\mu](x)=\inf\Bigg\{r>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{B(x,r)}\phi(z-y)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}\mu(y,w)\geq\eta\Bigg\}.

Naturally, any solution

t\mapsto(\mathbf{x}(t),\mathbf{v}(t)):=\Big((x_{1}(t),v_{1}(t)),...,(x_{N}(t),v_{N}(t))\Big)

to (
4
) can be identified with the measure-valued atomic function

(7)

t\mapsto f^{N}_{t}(x,v)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}(t)}(x)\otimes\delta_{v_{i}(t)}(v)

solving (
5
) with
R[f^{N}_{t}](x_{i})=R(x_{i})
, see Proposition
3
in the appendix.

1.2.
Main results

The starting point of our analysis of system (
4
) is the above observation that solutions to (
4
) are equivalent to atomic (cf. (
7
)) measure-valued (or simply distributional) solutions to (
5
), at least provided that (
5
) is well-posed. Thus, we perform the analysis of well-posedness of the microscopic system (
4
) on the more robust level of well-posedness of (
5
) in the sense of measure-valued solutions. The main results are expressed in the following two theorems related to well-posedness and stability of (
5
), respectively. We denote the space of probability measures with finite second moment over
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
by
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

Theorem 1

(Well-posedness in the kinetic case)
.

Let
T>0
and
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
with

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{x}:={\rm diam}_{x}({\rm supp}f_{0})<\infty,\quad{\mathcal{D}}_{v}:={\rm diam}_{v}({\rm supp})f_{0}<\infty

be given. Then there exists a unique measure-valued solution to (
5
) in the sense of Definition
1
(see below) issued at
f_{0}
. Furthermore, it is recovered as a mean-field limit of atomic solutions of the form (
7
), which follow trajectories
t\mapsto(\mathbf{x}(t),\mathbf{v}(t))
solving the ODE system (
4
).

Theorem 2

(
W_{2}
-stability)
.

Let
T>0
and let
f
and
\widetilde{f}
be two measure-valued solutions to (
5
) in the sense of Definition
1
issued at the initial data
f_{0},\widetilde{f}_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
with

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{x}:={\rm diam}_{x}({\rm supp}f_{0})<\infty,\quad{\mathcal{D}}_{v}:={\rm diam}_{v}({\rm supp})f_{0}<\infty,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}}}_{x}:={\rm diam}_{x}({\rm supp}\widetilde{f}_{0})<\infty,\quad\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}}}_{v}:={\rm diam}_{v}({\rm supp})\widetilde{f}_{0}<\infty,

Then the following stability estimate holds

(8)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}W_{2}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t})\leq e^{(2c_{2}+c_{1}^{2}e^{2c_{2}T})t}W_{2}^{2}(f_{0},\widetilde{f}_{0}),

where
W_{2}
is the
2
-Wasserstein metric, while
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are positive constants depending on
\phi,d,\eta
and the diameters of supports of the solutions
{\mathcal{D}}_{x},{\mathcal{D}}_{v},\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}}}_{x},\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}}}_{v}
.

Remark 1
.

It is worthwhile to note that the choice of the metric in Theorem
2
is quite arbitrary. Indeed, based on the assumptions of Theorems
1
and
2
, and on Definition
1
, we are dealing with solutions that have uniformly bounded supports (controlled by the initial data). Thus all of the associated families of measures are uniformly tight and have all moments uniformly bounded. In such a scenario all of the standard Wasserstein metrices are equivalent to the narrow topology, wherein convergence is characterised by testing the measure with all bounded-continuous functions (in fact in our case, all of the above topologies coincide with the weak
-*
topology on the space
C_{c}
of all continuous, compactly supported functions). Our choice to use the
W_{2}
framework is mostly arbitrary, but it has certain advantages related to the fact that models of second-order collective dynamics have close relation to gradient-flows with respect to
W_{2}
. As discovered recently in
[
33
]
and
[
34
]
CS-type interactions can be equivalently described as gradient-flows with respect to the so-called fibered Wasserstein distance, which requires a weakly Riemannian structure similar to that of the classical work
[
28
]
, by
F. Otto
which specifically requires the
W_{2}
topology.

The literature on the
q
closest model concerns mostly asymptotics, with the most prominent result obtained by none other than Cucker and Dong in
[
11
]
. It basically says that if
q>\frac{n}{2}
then the ensemble is connected and flocking is likely to occur (depending on the tail of communication weight
\psi
). This result has been extended to other variants of the models such as the time-delayed CS model
[
12
]
. However the story of the CS model with non-standard and asymetric communication does not end (nor does it begin) here. One should mention the Motsch-Tadmor model
[
24
]
that scales the communication with respect to the relative inter-particle distances, and CS model with short-range interactions
[
18
,
23
]
. Another direction are communication protocols, which involve topology that depends on the distribution of the particles, such as the
topological CS model due to Shvydkoy and Tadmor
[
35
]
or the alignment model operating using the so called density induced consensus protocol
[
21
,
22
]
. In some sense many of the techniques and models ultimately circle back to the basic question of linear consensus
[
26
,
27
]
, such as in the case of the CS model with switching topologies
[
13
]
.

What distinguishes our work from the literature is that, to the best of our knowledge, the issue of well-posedness for the CD model has not been solved yet. We propose a way to circumvent the main problems with well-posedness of the particle system and we follow through with a relatively simple derivation of the kinetic
q
closest model by a mean-field argument. It is performed by developing a stability estimate in the
2
-Wasserstein topology.

The reminder of the paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we present the preliminaries: example of ill-posedness and lack of large-time stability for (
1
) as well as the weak formulation for (
5
) and the proof of well-posedness for the ODE system (
4
). Section
3
is dedicated to the proofs of the main results and finally in Section
4
we present conclusions and outlook.

Notation.
Throughout the paper we often denote the time-dependence of a measure-valued function
f=f(t,x,v)
as
f=f_{t}(x,v)
. For a Borel-measurable mapping
T
and a probability measure
\mu
, we define the
pushforward measure
of
\mu
along
T
as
T_{\#}\mu(A):=\mu(T^{-1}(A))
. The crucial property of the pushforward measure is the change of variable formula, that we use throughout the paper

(9)

\int_{A}g\,{\rm d}T_{\#}\mu=\int_{T^{-1}(A)}g\circ T\,{\rm d}\mu

assuming that the above integrals are well-defined.

Since we work with measures with uniformly compact supports weak
-*
convergence is equivalent to narrow convergence (tested by bounded continuous functions) and to convergence in any
p
-Wasserstein metric (
1\leq p<+\infty
). We chose to use the
2
-Wasserstein metric
W_{2}
(cf. Remark
1
). More general
W_{p}
metric,
p\in[1,+\infty]
, is defined on the space of (uniformly) compactly supported probability measures as

\displaystyle W_{p}(\mu,\sigma)=\left(\inf_{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{p}\,{\rm d}\gamma(x,x^{\prime})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\gamma
are all
admissible transference plans
between
\mu
and
\sigma
, i.e. all probabilistic measures on
\mathbb{R}^{4d}
with
x
-marginal
\pi^{x}_{\#}\gamma

equal to
\mu
and
x^{\prime}
-marginal
\pi^{x^{\prime}}_{\#}\gamma
equal to
\sigma
, or equivalently

\gamma(A\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})=\mu(A),\quad\gamma(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times B)=\sigma(B).

Here we also include two relevant properties of the
W_{p}
metric. First,
W_{1}
is recovered as a dual to Lipschitz continuous functions, namely

(10)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g(x,v){\rm d}(\mu-\sigma)\right|\approx[g]_{Lip}W_{1}(\mu,\sigma),

where
[g]_{Lip}
denotes the Lipschitz constant of the function
g
.
This property is known as the Kantorovitch-Rubinstein duality theorem
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
. Second, by the Hölder inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}|x-x^{\prime}|\,{\rm d}\gamma(x,x^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\gamma(x,x^{\prime})},

which ensures that

(11)

W_{1}(\mu,\sigma)\leq W_{2}(\mu,\sigma).

Finally, we shall denote

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},..,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{Nd},\quad\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},...,v_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{Nd}.

2.2.
Weak formulation for the kinetic
q
-closest system

Next, we provide the weak formulation for the kinetic equation (
5
).

Definition 1
.

Let
T>0
and
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{x}:={\rm diam}_{x}({\rm supp}f_{0})<+\infty,\qquad{\mathcal{D}}_{v}:={\rm diam}_{v}({\rm supp}f_{0})<+\infty.

We say that
f
is a measure-valued solution to (
5
) issued in the initial datum
f_{0}
on the time interval
[0,T]
if

(i)

The function
t\mapsto f_{t}
belongs to the space
C([0,T];({\mathcal{P}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),W_{2}))
.

(ii)

The function
f_{t}
is compactly supported uniformly with respect to
t\in[0,T]
with

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{x}(t):={\rm diam}_{x}({\rm supp}f_{t})\leq{\mathcal{D}}_{x}+t{\mathcal{D}}_{v},\qquad{\mathcal{D}}_{v}(t):={\rm diam}_{v}({\rm supp}f_{t})\leq{\mathcal{D}}_{v}.

(iii)

For any function
\psi\in C^{1}_{b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})
compactly supported in
[0,T)
we have

(13)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Big(\partial_{t}\psi(t,x,v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(t,x,v)+\nabla_{v}\psi(t,x,v)\cdot F[f_{t}](x,v)\Big)\,{\rm d}f_{t}(x,v)\,{\rm d}t\\
=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi(0,x,v)\,{\rm d}f_{0}(x,v).\end{split}

Remark 2
.

Hereinafter we shall use an alternative representation of
F[f_{t}]
in (
5
) and of the integral in the definition of
R
in (
6
). Namely, by Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle F[f_{t}](x,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{B(x,R[f_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{1}f_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z,

\displaystyle R[f_{t}](x)

\displaystyle=\inf\Bigg\{r>0:\int_{B(x,r)}(\phi*m_{0}f_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z\geq\eta\Bigg\},

where

(14)

m_{1}f_{t}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\,{\rm d}f_{t}(x,v)\quad\mbox{and}\quad m_{0}f_{t}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,{\rm d}f_{t}(x,v)

are the first and second local velocity moments of
f_{t}
, respectively.

One of the main tools used throughout the paper is the observation that for any
\mu
the radius
R[\mu]
is Lipschitz continuous with constant 1. As a matter of fact this is a geometrical property completely independent of the mollification
\phi
.

Lemma 1

(Lipschitz continuity of
R
)
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure defined over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
R[\mu]
defined by (
6
) is Lipschitz continuous with constant 1, i.e.

(15)

|R[\mu](x)-R[\mu](x^{\prime})|\leq|x-x^{\prime}|.

Furthermore if
\mu
is compactly supported then
R[\mu]
is bounded by
{\rm diam}({\rm supp}\mu)
, i.e.

(16)

|R[\mu](x)|\leq{\rm diam}({\rm supp}\mu)+\sigma\quad\mbox{for }\mu-{a.a.}\ x,

where we recall that
\sigma>0
is the fixed range of the support of the mollification
\phi
(cf. (
3
)).

Proof.

Since
\mu
is fixed we denote
R(x):=R[\mu](x)
. Take any
x
and
x^{\prime}
, and assume without loss of generality that
R(x^{\prime})\leq R(x)
. Note that we always have

B(x^{\prime},R(x^{\prime}))\subset B(x,R(x^{\prime})+|x-x^{\prime}|).

The above inclusion together with (
6
) for
R(x^{\prime})
, lead to

(17)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{B(x,R(x^{\prime})+|x-x^{\prime}|)}\phi(z-y)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}\mu(y,w)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{B(x^{\prime},R(x^{\prime}))}\phi(z-y)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}\mu(y,w)\geq\eta,

since we integrate a positive function over a larger set. But definition (
6
) for
R(x)
implies that
R(x)
is the infimum of all
r
from (
6
), thus by
(
17
) we deduce

R(x)\leq R(x^{\prime})+|x-x^{\prime}|.

Recalling that
R(x^{\prime})\leq R(x)
this finishes the proof of (
15
). Now suppose that
\bar{R}:={\rm diam}({\rm supp}\mu)<+\infty
and fix
x\in{\rm supp}\mu
so that
{\rm supp}\mu\subset B(x,\bar{R})
. Using the representation from Remark
2
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{B(x,\bar{R}+\sigma)}\phi(z-y)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}\mu(y,w)=\int_{B(x,\bar{R}+\sigma)}\phi*m_{0}\mu(z)\,{\rm d}z,

which together with the fact that
{\rm supp}(\phi*m_{0}\mu)={\rm supp}\phi+{\rm supp}\ m_{0}\mu\subset B(x,\bar{R}+\sigma)
implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{B(x,\bar{R}+\sigma)}\phi(z-y)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}\mu(y,w)=1>\eta

and by (
6
) inequality (
16
) necessarily follows.

∎

2.3.
Well-posedness of the fuzzy
q
-closest system (
4
)

In this section we prove the well-posedness of the fuzzy system (
4
) together with some of its basic properties.

Proposition 1
.

Let
(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
q,T>0
.
Then there exists a unique global in time solution
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\in C^{1}([0,T])^{d}
to problem (
4
), such that

(18)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\mathbf{v}(t)|_{\infty}\leq|\mathbf{v}_{0}|_{\infty},\qquad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\mathbf{x}(t)|_{\infty}\leq|\mathbf{x}_{0}|_{\infty}+T|\mathbf{v}_{0}|_{\infty}.

Furthermore for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(19)

{\rm conv}(v_{1}(t),...,v_{N}(t))\subset{\rm conv}(v_{01},...,v_{0N}).

Here and hereinafter
{\rm conv}A
denotes the convex hull of
A
.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
1
we are ensured that the right-hand side of the system is Lipschitz continuous in
x
and
v
, so unique solutions in the
C^{1}
class exists at least locally in time.
In order to show the global existence we need to prove global bounds.

The form of the right-hand side of system (
4
) implies immediately the bound for the velocity.
Indeed, let
\mathcal{V}(t)={\rm conv}(v_{1}(t),...,v_{N}(t))
, then by definition

\mbox{ as \ }w_{max}\mbox{ realizes the maximum}\sup_{w\in\partial\mathcal{V}(t)}|w|,

then

\mbox{ for all }v_{k}\mbox{ \ with \ }k=1,...,N\qquad v_{k}\cdot w_{max}-|w_{max}|^{2}\leq 0

and equality holds only as
w_{max}=v_{k}
for some
k
.
Let us multiply (
4
)
2
by
v_{k}=w_{max}
obtaining

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|v_{k}|^{2}=-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{l}\int_{B(x_{k},R(x_{k}))}\phi(z-x_{l})(v_{k}-v_{l})\cdot v_{k}\leq 0,

which leads to the first part of (
18
), and then to the second assertion.

In order to prove more precise information about the evolution of the set of velocities note that since the right-hand side is smooth enough the system can be effectively approximated by the semi-discretization in time. So for
\Delta t>0

we consider

(20)

v_{k}(t+\Delta t)=v_{k}(t)-{\Delta t}\sum_{l}\theta_{l}(v_{k}-v_{l})

with
\theta_{l}=\frac{1}{q}\int_{B(x_{k},R(x_{k}))}\phi(z-x_{l})dz
. It’s clear
\theta_{l}\geq 0
and

\sum_{l}\theta_{l}=1
. So (
20
) takes the form

v_{k}(t+\Delta t)=(1-\Delta t)v_{k}(t)+{\Delta t}\sum_{l}\theta_{l}v_{l}.

Hence
v_{k}(t+\Delta t)
is a convex combination of the rest of velocities at the previous time step
(for
\Delta t<1
). So for the semi-discretization we get

\mathcal{V}(t)\supset\mathcal{V}(t+\Delta t).

The above relation is preserved for the limit
\Delta t\to 0
. We have (
19
) and the proof is done.
∎

Proof of the main results

The following section is dedicated to the proof of well-posedness of system (
5
) in the class of solutions delivered by Definition
1
i.e. Theorem
1
. We begin with the second result – the
W_{2}
-Wasserstein stability estimate for solutions of (

) (and grants uniqueness).

3.1.
Representation formula

We start with the representation formula for any solution of (
5
) in the sense of Definition
1
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
f
be a measure-valued solution to (
5
) in the time interval
[0,T]
issued at the compactly supported initial datum
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Then there exists a unique mapping
{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}[f](z):=({\mathcal{X}}_{t}(x,v),{\mathcal{V}}_{t}(x,v))
,
z=(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, satisfying the ODE system

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{X}}_{t}(x,v)={\mathcal{V}}_{t}(x,v),&({\mathcal{X}}_{0}(x,v),{\mathcal{V}}_{0}(x,v))=(x,v),\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{V}}_{t}(x,v)=F[f_{t}]({\mathcal{X}}_{t}(x,v),{\mathcal{V}}_{t}(x,v)).\\
\end{cases}

Moreover
f_{t}=({\mathcal{Z}}_{t})_{\#}f_{0}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2
below, the velocity field
F[f_{t}]
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous and bounded for any measure-valued solution
f
of (
5
). Thus the characteristics for (
5
) are well defined and unique. Since (
5
) is ultimately a continuity equation, albeit in the combined variable
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the representation formula in Proposition
2
follows directly from the classical theory
[
2
, Proposition 8.1.8]
. Thus, the issue is the Lipschitz continuity of
F[f_{t}]
which we explore in the following lemma.

That said, for the reader’s convenience we shall at least provide a formal argument based on the change of variables formula (
9
), which supports the claim that
f_{t}=({\mathcal{Z}}_{t})_{\#}f_{0}
satisfies (
5
) in the distributional sense. To such an end let
\psi
be a smooth test function, compactly supported in
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. Using the change of variables formula (
9
) and the chain rule we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Big(\partial_{t}\psi(t,z)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(t,z)+\nabla_{v}\psi(t,z)\cdot F[f_{t}](z)\Big)\,{\rm d}({\mathcal{Z}}_{t})_{\#}f_{0}(z)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Big((\partial_{t}\psi)(t,{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}(z))+v\cdot(\nabla_{x}\psi)(t,{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}(z))+(\nabla_{v}\psi)(t,{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}(z))\cdot F[f_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}_{t}(z))\Big)\,{\rm d}f_{0}(z)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\psi(t,{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}(z))\Big)\,{\rm d}f_{0}(z)\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi(0,z){\rm d}f_{0}(z),

which is the weak formulation of (
5
), cf. (
13
).

∎

Remark 3
.

Observe that Proposition
2
already provides existence and uniqueness for (
5
). This is not a surprise – it is a Vlasov-type equation with a Lipschitz continuous right-hand side after all. However are goal lies beyond that, we aim to show a stability estimate and approximate the solutions by the mean-field limit.

Lemma 2
.

Let
f
and
\widetilde{f}
be a pair of measure-valued solutions to (
5
). Then
F[f_{t}]
is bounded as a function of
(t,x,v)
and for a.a.
t\in[0,T)
we have

\displaystyle|F[f_{t}](x,v)-F[f_{t}](x^{\prime},v^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}(|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|)

\displaystyle|F[f_{t}](x,v)-F[\widetilde{f}_{t}](x,v)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}W_{2}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t}),

where
c_{0},c_{1}
are constants depending on
\phi,\eta,d,{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{v}
.

Proof.

First, we note that since
f
has a uniformly bounded support and
\phi
is bounded, it is clear that the function
(t,x,v)\mapsto F[f_{t}](x,v)
is bounded by its definition in (
5
).

We proceed with the Lipschitz continuity of
F[f_{t}]
. Since it is linear with respect to
v
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}F[f_{t}](x,v)=\frac{1}{\eta}1_{d},

where
1_{d}=(1,..,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which concludes the proof of Lipschitz continuity with respect to
v
.
For the Lipschitz continuity with respect to
x
we denote
R:=R[f_{t}](x)
and
R^{\prime}:=R[f_{t}](x^{\prime})
, and write

\displaystyle F[f_{t}](x,v)-F[f_{t}](x^{\prime},v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}w\left(\int_{B(x,R)}\phi(z-y)\,{\rm d}z-\int_{B(x^{\prime},R^{\prime})}\phi(z-y)\,{\rm d}z\right)\,{\rm d}f_{t}(y,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}w\int_{B(0,1)}\left(R^{d}\phi(Rz+x-y)-R^{\prime d}\phi(R^{\prime}z+x^{\prime}-y)\right)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}f_{t}(y,w)

\displaystyle=(R^{d}-R^{\prime d})\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}w\int_{B(x,R)}\phi(z-y)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}f_{t}(y,w)

\displaystyle+R^{\prime d}\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}w\int_{B(0,1)}\left(\phi(Rz+x-y)-\phi(R^{\prime}z+x^{\prime}-y)\right)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}f_{t}(y,w)

\displaystyle=:I+II.

Then, by Lemma
1

\displaystyle|I|\leq d(R^{d-1}+R^{\prime d-1})D_{v}|R-R^{\prime}|\leq 2dD_{x}^{d-1}D_{v}|x-x^{\prime}|

and

\displaystyle|II|\leq R^{\prime d}[\phi]_{{\rm Lip}}\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}w\int_{B(0,1)}|z|+1\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}f(t,y,w)|x-x^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq 2D_{x}^{d}[\phi]_{{\rm Lip}}\frac{1}{\eta}D_{v}\omega_{d}|x-x^{\prime}|,

where
\omega_{d}
is the volume of a
d
-dimensional unit ball. Combining the above yields the first desired inequality with
c_{0}=\max\{\frac{1}{\eta},2dD_{x}^{d-1}D_{v}+\frac{2}{\eta}D^{d}_{x}[\phi]_{Lip}D_{v}\}
.

Next, we prove the pointwise Lipschitz continuity of
f\mapsto F[f_{t}](x,v)
with respect to the
W_{2}
metric. Using representation of
F[f_{t}]
from Remark
2
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}

\displaystyle:=F[f_{t}](x,v)-F[\widetilde{f}_{t}](x,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{B(x,R[f_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{1}f_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z-\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{B(x,R[\widetilde{f}_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{1}\widetilde{f}_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z,

where we used notation (
14
),
and thus, assuming without a loss of generality that
R[f](t,x)\geq R[\widetilde{f}_{t}](x)
,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|B(x,R[f_{t}](x))|}{\eta}|(\phi*m_{1}f_{t})-(\phi*m_{1}\widetilde{f}_{t})|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{B(x,R[f_{t}](x))\setminus B(x,R[\widetilde{f}_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{1}\widetilde{f}_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z=:III+IV.

By Kantorovitch-Rubinstein duality theorem for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle|(\phi*m_{1}f_{t})(z)-(\phi*m_{1}\widetilde{f}_{t})(z)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\phi(z-y)w\,({\rm d}f_{t}(y,w)-{\rm d}\widetilde{f}_{t}(y,w))\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{k-r}}}{{\leq}}L_{\phi,w}W_{1}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{w12}}}{{\leq}}L_{\phi,w}W_{2}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t}),

where
L_{\phi,w}=([\phi]_{Lip}{\mathcal{D}}_{v}+|\phi|_{\infty})
is the Lipschitz constant of the function

(y,w)\mapsto\phi(z-y)w.

Thus

\displaystyle III\leq\frac{\omega_{d}{\mathcal{D}}_{x}^{d}L_{\phi,w}}{\eta}W_{2}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t}).

On the other hand (recalling (
14
))

\displaystyle IV\leq\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{v}}{\eta}\int_{B(x,R[f_{t}](x))\setminus B(x,R[\widetilde{f}_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{0}\widetilde{f}_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z

and we use the relation

\displaystyle\eta=\int_{B(x,R[f_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{0}f_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z=\int_{B(x,R[\widetilde{f}_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{0}\widetilde{f}_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z

to infer that

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,R[f_{t}](x))\setminus B(x,R[\widetilde{f}_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{0}\widetilde{f}_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z=\int_{B(x,R[f_{t}](x))}(\phi*m_{0}f_{t})(z)-(\phi*m_{0}\widetilde{f}_{t})(z)\,{\rm d}z.

Using Kantorovitch-Rubinstein duality theorem in a similar manner to the estimation of
III
we conclude that

\displaystyle IV\leq\frac{\omega_{d}{\mathcal{D}}_{x}^{d}{\mathcal{D}}_{v}}{\eta}W_{2}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t}).

∎

Stability estimate

Our next goal is to establish the stability estimate for solutions of (

2

Lemma 3
.

Let
f
and
\widetilde{f}
be a pair of measure-valued solutions to (
5
) issued at initial data
f_{0}
and
\widetilde{f_{0}}
. Assume further that
{\mathcal{Z}}_{t}[f]
and

are characteristics defined in Proposition

for

\widetilde{f}
, respectively. Then there exists a positive constant

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{2c_{2}t}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2},

(22)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}^{2}e^{2c_{2}t}\int_{0}^{t}W_{2}^{2}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t})\,{\rm d}t.

Proof.

Fix

\displaystyle\quad\cdot(\mathcal{V}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{V}_{t}[f](z^{\prime}),F[f_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z))-F[f_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z^{\prime})))

\displaystyle\leq(2+2c_{0}^{2})|\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z^{\prime})|^{2},

where we use Lemma
2
and Young’s inequality in the last line. By Gronwall’s inequality we recover (

.

To prove inequality (
22
) we proceed similarly. Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and compute using Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z)|^{2}=2(\mathcal{X}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{X}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z),\mathcal{V}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{V}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z))

\displaystyle\cdot(\mathcal{V}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{V}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z),F[f_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z))-F[\widetilde{f}_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[\widetilde{f}](z)))

\displaystyle\leq 2|\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z)|^{2}+2|F[f_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z))-F[\widetilde{f}_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z))|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|F[\widetilde{f}_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z))-F[\widetilde{f}_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[\widetilde{f}](z))|^{2}.

In the above inequality, we use Lemma
2
to estimate

\displaystyle|F[f_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z))-F[\widetilde{f}_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z))|^{2}\leq c_{1}^{2}W_{2}^{2}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t})

\displaystyle|F[\widetilde{f}_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[f](z))-F[\widetilde{f}_{t}]({\mathcal{Z}}[\widetilde{f}](z))|^{2}\leq 2c_{0}^{2}|\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z)|^{2},

yielding

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z)|^{2}\leq(2+2c_{0}^{2})|\mathcal{Z}_{t}[f](z)-\mathcal{Z}_{t}[\widetilde{f}](z)|^{2}+c_{1}^{2}W_{2}^{2}(f_{t},\widetilde{f}_{t}),

which, by Gronwall’s inequality leads to (

∎

Let

and
{\widetilde{f}}

Mean-field approximation and existence revisited

f^{N}_{t}\to f_{t}
narrowly, all the involved balls are closed, Portmanteau theorem ensures that

\displaystyle f_{t}(B_{\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{x}+t{\mathcal{D}}_{v}}{2}}\times B_{\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{v}}{2}})\geq\limsup_{N\to\infty}f^{N}_{t}(B_{\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{x}+t{\mathcal{D}}_{v}}{2}}\times B_{\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{v}}{2}})=1

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Therefore for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm supp}f_{t}\subset B_{\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{x}+t{\mathcal{D}}_{v}}{2}}\times B_{\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{v}}{2}}

and the bound on the support of
f
required in item (ii) of Definition
1
is proven.

Finally, we aim to show that
f
satisfies the weak formulation (
13
). Taking an arbitrary test function
\psi\in C^{1}_{b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, compactly supported in
[0,T)
we know that for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
the measure-valued function
f^{N}
satisfies (
13
) with initial datum
f_{0}^{N}
. Thus, it suffices to prove the following convergences:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\psi+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\psi+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi\,{\rm d}f_{t}\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot F[f^{N}_{t}]\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot F[f_{t}]\,{\rm d}f_{t}\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi(0,x,v)\,{\rm d}f_{0}^{N}

\displaystyle\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi(0,x,v)\,{\rm d}f_{0}.

The first and the third are trivial, since the integrands are bounded and continuous and
f^{N}_{t}\to f_{t}
in
W_{2}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. As for the middle term involving the force
F[f_{t}]
, we use a classical weak-strong convergence argument. First we write

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot F[f^{N}_{t}]\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot F[f_{t}]\,{\rm d}f_{t}\,{\rm d}t\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot(F[f^{N}_{t}]-F[f_{t}])\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}\,{\rm d}t\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot F[f_{t}]\,({\rm d}f^{N}_{t}-{\rm d}f_{t})\,{\rm d}t\right|.

By Lemma
2
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot(F[f^{N}_{t}]-F[f_{t}])\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}\,{\rm d}t\right|\leq T|\nabla_{v}\psi|_{\infty}|F[f^{N}_{t}]-F[f_{t}]|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}T|\nabla_{v}\psi|_{\infty}W_{2}(f^{N}_{t},f_{t})\longrightarrow 0.

Finally suppose that
F[f_{t}]
is continuous and bounded as a function of
(t,x,v)
. Then by the characterisation of
W_{2}
convergence that includes the narrow convergence tested with bounded continuous functions, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot F[f_{t}]\,({\rm d}f^{N}_{t}-{\rm d}f_{t})\,{\rm d}t\right|\to 0.

Altogether
f
satisfies the weak formulation (
13
) and the proof is finished.

Thus it remains to ensure the continuity of
(t,x,v)\mapsto F[f_{t}](x,v)
. To this end let
(t_{n},x_{n},v_{n})\to(t,x,v)
and observe that

\displaystyle|F[f_{t_{n}}](x_{n},v_{n})-F[f_{t}](x,v)|

\displaystyle\leq|F[f_{t_{n}}](x_{n},v_{n})-F[f_{t}](x_{n},v_{n})|

\displaystyle\qquad+|F[f_{t}](x_{n},v_{n})-F[f_{t}](x,v)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}W_{2}(f_{t_{n}},f_{t})+c_{0}(|x_{n}-x|+|v_{n}-v|)\to 0,

where we used Lemma
2
and the fact that
f_{t_{n}}\to f_{t}
in
W_{2}
.

∎

4.
Concluding remarks

To summarize the idea of the paper we need to return to system (
1
).
Looking formally at the velocity equation, we see a system, which is piece-wise constant in time. The communication is not symmetric, and in fact it is often expected to occur only in one direction (particle being influenced by another, which it does not influence back). Our modification of the system makes it well posed from the mathematical viewpoint; the change of the communications becomes smooth.

A very natural question concerns the asymptotics. It is well known that with
q>(N-1)/2
there is a permanent communication between all particles, which results in global flocking – all elements tend to the same speed at
t=+\infty
. However for smaller
q

there is no such phenomenon. In fact a more natural question is if a consistent-in-time segmentation of the ensemble occurs or not. We shall call such phenomenon of large-time emmergence of isolated clusters
clustering
.

Note that for the fuzzy system, the question is virtually the same. For the large-time behavior, parameter
\sigma

does not play any role. Let us formulate the question as the following conjecture.

Conjecture:

The set of neighbors
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}(t)
to system (
1
) stabilizes in time into separate constant subsets, in particular we observe the velocity stabilisation, i.e.

(25)

\mbox{ for each }i\in\{1,...,N\}\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{v}_{i}(t)=0.

In general we expect a negative answer to the above conjecture. On the torus one can find an example (essentially Example
1
on the 2D torus). On the other hand in the case of the whole Euclidean space, our simulations indicate that the conjecture holds.

Finally we take another look at (
1
) with non-constant communication weight
\psi
. If the communication weight
\psi
vanishes at infinity, we may lose the interesting combinatorical character of the problem. Consider

(26)

\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i},\qquad\dot{v}_{i}=-\frac{1}{q}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\psi(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)(v_{i}-v_{j}),

where
\psi(\cdot)
is a sufficiently smooth scalar function such that

\psi(s)\to 0
as
s\to\infty
. Note that such system is still ill-posed. The analysis of system (
26
) seems to be the first step in proving our conjecture. However to find the suitable approach there is a need of a new viewpoint for such unsymmetrical models.

Appendix A
Atomic solutions

The appendix consists of the following proposition, which states that the solutions of the fuzzy particle system (
4
), treated as the empirical measures (
24
) constitute solutions to the kinetic equation (
5
).

Proposition 3
.

Let
(\mathbf{x}^{N},\mathbf{v}^{N})
be a solution to (
4
). Then
f^{N}
given in (
24
) satisfies (
5
) in the sense of Definition
1
.

Proof.

Given
\psi\in C_{b}^{1}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, by the chain rule, we have

\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\psi(t,x_{i}^{N}(t),v_{i}^{N}(t))\\
&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\partial_{t}\psi)(t,x_{i}^{N}(t),v_{i}^{N}(t))+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}^{N}(t)\cdot\nabla\psi(t,x_{i}^{N}(t),v_{i}^{N}(t))\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{v}\psi(t,x_{i}^{N}(t),v_{i}^{N}(t))\frac{1}{\eta N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Bigg(v_{i}^{N}-\int_{B(x_{i},R(x_{i}(t))}\phi(z-x_{j})\,{\rm d}z\ v_{j}^{N}\Bigg).\end{split}

To finish the proof we integrate the above equation over
[0,T)
and observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\partial_{t}\psi\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\partial_{t}\psi)(t,x_{i}^{N}(t),v_{i}^{N}(t)),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}v\cdot\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}^{N}(t)\cdot\nabla\psi(t,x_{i}^{N}(t),v_{i}^{N}(t)),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{v}\psi F(f_{N})\,{\rm d}f^{N}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\nabla_{v}\psi(t,x^{N}_{i}(t),v_{i}^{N}(t))\Bigg(v_{i}^{N}-\int_{B(x_{i}^{N},R(t,x_{i}^{N}))}\phi(z-x_{j}^{N})\,{\rm d}z\ v_{j}^{N}\Bigg).

∎

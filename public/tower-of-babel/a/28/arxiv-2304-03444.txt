Smoothness of conformal heat flow of harmonic maps
Woongbae Park

Abstract
. The conformal heat flow of harmonic maps is a system of evolution equations combined with harmonic map flow with metric evolution in conformal direction. It is known that global weak solution of the flow exists and smooth except at mostly finitely many singular points. In this paper, we show that no finite time singularity occurs, unlike the usual harmonic map flow. And if the initial energy is small, we can obtain the uniform convergence of the map to a point and the conformal factor of the metric under some time sequence t n → ∞ t_{n}\to\infty . Also, under the assumption that energy concentration is uniform in time, we show that there exists a sequence of time t n → ∞ t_{n}\to\infty such that f ⁡ ( ⋅ , t n ) f(\cdot,t_{n}) converges to a harmonic map in W 1 , 2 W^{1,2} on any compact set away from at most finitely many points.

1.
Introduction

We consider the following conformal heat flow (CHF) for harmonic maps. It is a solution
(f,u)
of a pair of equations

(1)

\begin{cases}f_{t}=&\tau_{g}(f)\\
u_{t}=&b|df|_{g}^{2}-a\end{cases}

where
a,b
are positive constants.
Here
f:(M,g)\times[0,T]\to(N,h)
is a time-dependent map from a Riemann surface
(M,g)
without boundary to a compact Riemannian manifold
(N,h)
,
u:(M,g)\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is a conformal factor of the metric
g
given by
g(x,t)=e^{2u(x,t)}g_{0}(x)
,
\tau_{g}(f)
is the tension field of
f
with respect to
g
and
|df|_{g}^{2}=g^{ij}h_{\alpha\beta}f^{\alpha}_{i}f^{\beta}_{j}
is the energy density in local coordinates.
We put the initial condition
f(0)=f_{0}:(M,g)\to(N,h)
and
u(0)=0
.
If we embed
(N,h)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{L}
isometrically, the equation can be written with respect to
g_{0}
by

(2)

\begin{cases}f_{t}=&e^{-2u}\tau_{g_{0}}(f)=e^{-2u}(\Delta f+A(f)(df,df))\\
u_{t}=&be^{-2u}|df|^{2}-a.\end{cases}

We assume that there is a constant
C_{N}
which only depends on the embedding
(N,h)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{L}
such that
\|A\|_{\infty},\|DA\|_{\infty},\|D^{2}A\|_{\infty}\leq C_{N}
and that
b>2C_{N}^{2}+C_{N}
.

The motivation of CHF for harmonic maps is introduced in
[
11
]
.
It is designed to obtain a variation of harmonic map flow which has a smooth global solution.
For a map
f:(M,g)\to(N,h)
between two Riemannian manifolds, the harmonic map flow is a negative gradient flow of Dirichlet energy

E(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|df|_{g}^{2}dvol_{g}

where
dvol_{g}=\sqrt{|g|}dx
is the volume form of
M
.
It is known that the harmonic map heat flow exists and smooth under non-positive sectional curvature condition of
N
by Eells-Sampson
[
5
]
, and global weak solution was obtained by Struwe
[
17
]
where at most finitely many bubble points can occur.
And such finite time singularity indeed exists, by Chang-Ding-Ye
[
2
]
and recently generalized by Davila-Del Pino-Wei
[
3
]
.
The further results, we refer to, e.g., Qing-Tian
[
14
]
, Chang
[
7
]
, Topping
[
20
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
, Freire
[
6
]
, Ling-Wang
[
8
]
, and many others.

In terms of variations of harmonic map flow together with evolution of the metric, Topping-Rupflin
[
15
]
developed the so-called Teichmüller flow which is the negative
L^{2}
gradient flow of the Dirichlet energy with respect to both the map and the metric.
Müller
[
10
]
considered Ricci-harmonic map flow, a combination of harmonic map flow and Ricci flow of the metric, and Buzano-Rupflin
[
1
]
showed the long time existence under large coupling constant for 2 dimensional domain.

In a previous paper
[
11
]
, the author studied the CHF of harmonic maps (
1
) and established the following analogue of Struwe’s theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Park
[
11
]
)
.

There exists a weak solution
f\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(M\times[0,\infty),N)
,
u\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(M\times[0,\infty))
of (
1
) that is smooth in
M\times[0,\infty)
except at most finitely many points.

This paper improves
Theorem

1.1
by studying the possible finite-time singularities, and showing that, in fact, no such singularities exist.

Theorem 1.2
.

Any solution
(f,u)
of (
1
) is smooth on
M\times[0,\infty)
.

To show this, we assume there is a finite time singularity and get a contradiction.
By
[
11
]
, the only singularity type is due to the energy concentration, unlike the Teichmüller flow where metric degeneration may occur.
For a smooth solution
(f,u)
of (
1
) on
M\times[0,T)
, we say that the flow blows up at
(x_{0},T)
if

(3)

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\nearrow T}\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|df|^{2}(t)\geq\varepsilon_{1}

for some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
.
These singular points are called bubble points.

One can try to build bubbles by usual method which requires sequence of time
t_{n}\nearrow T
and
L^{2}
control of tension field
\|\tau(f)(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
, see Qing
[
13
]
.
However, for all
t_{1}<t_{2}<T
, we only have weaker control

(4)

\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}e^{-2u}|\tau(f)|^{2}=E(t_{1})-E(t_{2})

and
e^{-2u}(x,t)\to 0
if
(x,t)
approaches singularity.
At this point, we even do not have
\|\tau(f)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
.
Thanks to the estimate in
[
11
]
, however, we can overcome this difficulty and obtain that no finite time bubbling occurs.

For infinite time bubbling, the same issue exists.
In the case of usual harmonic map flow, one can have
\|\tau(f)(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
for some time sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
and using it to get bubbles at infinite time and show the energy identity.
Again, this is not the case for CHF.
For more general control of the tension field, Qing-Tian
[
14
]
and Ding-Tian
[
4
]
showed energy identity and no-neck property for
\|\tau(f_{i})\|_{L^{2}}<C
. Wang
[
22
]
considered the case of
\|\tau(f_{i})\|_{M^{1,\delta}}<C
where
M^{1,\delta}
is the Morrey space for some
0\leq\delta<2
, and Wang-Wei-Zhang
[
23
]
showed the same results for
\|\tau(f_{i})\|_{L^{p}}<C
for some
p>1
.
Note that Parker
[
12
]
showed that
\|\tau(f_{i})\|_{L^{1}}<C
is not enough for a no-neck property.

On the other hand, for CHF, we cannot choose a sequence of time
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
f(t_{n})
be a sequence of approximate harmonic maps with
L^{2}
bounded tension fields due to the weaker control of tension field (
4
).
But if the initial energy is small enough, one can recover convergence over a sequence of time
t_{n}\to\infty
similar to usual harmonic map flow, see Wang
[
21
]
or Lin
[
9
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{2}>0
such that if
(f,u)
be a solution of (
1
) with
E(f(0))\leq\varepsilon_{2}
, then

\|df\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
and
u(x,t)\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
for all
x\in M
.

In particular, there exists
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
f(\cdot,t_{n})
converges to a point uniformly.

Without small energy assumption, we can have the following convergence result at time infinity away from finitely many bubble points.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(f,u)
be a solution of (
1
) and assume that all sequential bubble points are uniform bubble points.
Then there exists
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
f(\cdot,t_{n})
converges to a harmonic map
f_{\infty}(\cdot)
in
W^{1,2}
on any compact set
K\subset M\setminus S
where
S=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\}
is the set of uniform bubble points.

For the definition of sequential bubble points and uniform bubble points, see
Definition

6.1
.
The assumption that all sequential bubble points are uniform bubble points says that the energy concentration is uniform in time.
This assumption is needed to avoid the case when a bubble is traveling inside
N
forever and does not converge.

Throughout the paper, all the computations are obtained with respect to the metric
g_{0}
unless the metric is specified.

2.
Local and global estimate

In this section we show some estimates about CHF for harmonic maps.
First we review that how local energy behaves and when we get smoothness locally.
Then we develop parallel global version of the estimates similar to
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Park
[
11
]
, Lemma 2)
.

Let
(f,u)
be a solution of (
2
) on
M\times[0,\infty)
.
Then the volume with respect to the metric
g(t)=e^{2u}g_{0}
is finite for all time.
Also, for any
t_{1}<t_{2}
,

(5)

E(t_{1})-E(t_{2})=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{2}.

Note that the energy over whole manifold
M
is decreasing.
Hence we denote
E(t)\leq E(0)=E_{0}
.

The following lemma is so-called energy gap property of harmonic maps.

Lemma 2.2

(Parker
[
12
]
or Sacks-Uhlenbeck
[
16
]
)
.

There is
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, if
f:M\to N
is a nontrivial harmonic maps, then
E(f)\geq\varepsilon_{0}
.

Even though global energy is decreasing, local energy may be increasing at some regions.
Similar to
[
17
]
, we have the following local energy lemma, saying that local energy can increase by the amount controlled by
\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}}
.

Lemma 2.3

(Park
[
11
]
, Proposition 14)
.

Let
(f,u)
be a solution of (
2
) on
M\times[0,T]
and smooth on
M\times[0,T)
.
Choose
t_{1}<t_{2}<T
and some ball
B_{2r}
with cut-off function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{2r})
such that
|\nabla\varphi|\leq\frac{2}{r}
.
Then

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{2r}}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{2}\varphi^{2}+

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2r}}|df|^{2}\varphi^{2}(t_{2})-\int_{B_{2r}}|df|^{2}\varphi^{2}(t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4^{2}}{ar^{2}}(e^{2at_{2}}-e^{2at_{1}})E_{0}.

In particular, for
r_{1}<r_{2}
and
t_{1}<t_{2}<T
,

(7)

E(B_{r_{1}},t_{2})-E(B_{r_{2}},t_{1})\leq C\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}}

where
C
depends on
E_{0},T
.

Next, we investigate when the finite time singularity occurs.
The author in
[
11
]
showed that if local energy is small and conformal factor is small, we have local smoothness.

Proposition 2.4

(Park
[
11
]
, Lemma 25)
.

There is
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that if
(f,u)
be a smooth solution of (
2
) on
B_{2r}\times[T-\delta r^{2},T)
satisfying, for all
r
small enough,

\sup_{t\in[T-\delta r^{2},T]}E(B_{2r},t)\leq\varepsilon_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{B_{2r}\times\{T-\delta r^{2}\}}e^{18u}\leq\varepsilon_{1},

then Hölder norms of
f
and
u
on
B_{r}\times[T-\delta r^{2},T]
and their derivatives are all bounded by constants only depending on
T
,
\delta
,
r
,
\varepsilon_{1}
and
C_{N}
.

To achieve the assumptions, we first fix
t_{1}
and let
\delta=\delta(t_{1},r,T)
such that
T-\delta r^{2}=t_{1}
.
Then the condition
\int_{B_{2r}\times\{t_{1}\}}e^{18u}\leq\varepsilon_{1}
can be achieved if we choose
r
small enough, because the integral is only computed at initial time
t_{1}
when
u
is assumed to be smooth.
Finally, if
\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\nearrow T}E(B_{2r},t)\leq\varepsilon_{1}
, then we choose
r
small enough to achieve the first assumption as well.
Hence, the only possibility for finite time singularity is when (
3
) holds.
This means that any singularity of CHF is not due to the geometric evolution but only due to the energy concentration as usual harmonic map flow.
So this proposition validates blow up condition (
3
).

We state several important lemmas that are similar to those in
[
11
]
but over the whole domain manifold
M
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(f,u)
be a smooth solution of (
2
) on
M\times[0,T)
.
For
p\geq 0
, we have

(8)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}\leq&\,\,2a(p+1)\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}-\frac{p+2}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p}\\
&+\left((p+2)C_{N}+2(p+2)C_{N}^{2}-2b(p+1)\right)\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+2}.\end{split}

Proof.

Taking time-derivative of the first equation in (
2
) to get

(e^{2u}f_{t})_{t}=\Delta f_{t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A(df,df).

Taking inner product with
f_{t}|f_{t}|^{p}
and integrating over
M
gives

\begin{split}\int_{M}\langle(e^{2u}f_{t})_{t},f_{t}|f_{t}|^{p}\rangle=&\int_{M}\langle\Delta f_{t},f_{t}|f_{t}|^{p}\rangle+\int_{M}\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}A(df,df),f_{t}|f_{t}|^{p}\rangle\\
=&-\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p}-p\int_{M}(\langle\nabla f_{t},f_{t}\rangle)^{2}|f_{t}|^{p-2}\\
&+\int_{M}\langle DA(df,df)\cdot f_{t},f_{t}\rangle|t_{t}|^{p}+2\int_{M}\langle DA(df,df_{t}),f_{t}|f_{t}|^{p}\rangle\\
\leq&-\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p}+C_{N}\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+2}+2C_{N}\int_{M}|df||\nabla f_{t}||f_{t}|^{p+1}\\
\leq&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p}+(C_{N}+2C_{N}^{2})\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+2}.\end{split}

On the other hand, LHS becomes

\begin{split}\int_{M}\langle(e^{2u}f_{t})_{t},f_{t}|f_{t}|^{p}\rangle=&\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}+2\frac{p+1}{p+2}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}u_{t}\\
=&\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}+2b\frac{p+1}{p+2}\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+2}\\
&-2a\frac{p+1}{p+2}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}.\end{split}

Combining the two, we get

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}\leq&2a(p+1)\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}-\frac{p+2}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p}\\
&+\left((p+2)C_{N}+2(p+2)C_{N}^{2}-2b(p+1)\right)\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+2}.\end{split}

∎

Now from the condition of
b>2C_{N}^{2}+C_{N}
,

(9)

-C_{4}(p):=(p+2)C_{N}+2(p+2)C_{N}^{2}-2b(p+1)<0

for all
p\geq 0
.
So,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}\leq 2a(p+1)\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}

and for any
t_{1}<t_{2}
, we get

(10)

\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}(t_{2})-\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}(t_{1})\leq 2a(p+1)\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}.

In particular, for any
t>0
we have

(11)

\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{2}(t)\leq\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{2}(0)+2a\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{2}\leq K(0)+2aE(0)

where we denote
K(0)=\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{2}(0)
.

Integrating (
8
) from
t_{1}
to
t_{2}
gives

(12)

\begin{split}C_{4}(p)\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+2}&+\frac{p+2}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p}+\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}(t_{2})-\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}(t_{1})\\
\leq&\,\,2a(p+1)\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}.\end{split}

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(f,u)
be a smooth solution of (
2
) on
M\times[0,T)
.
Then for any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
, we have

(13)

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{2}\leq C

for some constant
C
only depending on
E(0),K(0),T,C_{4}(0)
.

Proof.

From (
12
) and (
11
), we get the conclusion.
∎

3.
Proof of
Theorem

1.2

Now we prove the first main theorem.
We assume there is a finite time blow up at
(x_{0},T)
.
Since blow up is a local nature, we only focus on some small ball
B
centered at the bubble point
x_{0}
.
Now let
(f,u)
be a smooth solution of CHF on
B\times[0,T)
which blows up at
(x_{0},T)
.
The blow up assumption implies

(14)

\lim_{t\nearrow T}\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}|df|^{2}(t)=K+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}|df|^{2}(T)

where
K>0
is the energy loss at
(x_{0},T)
.
Equivalently,
K
can be obtained by

K=\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\nearrow T}\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|df|^{2}(t).

From (
3
),
K\geq\varepsilon_{1}
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(B)
be a cut-off function supported in
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
on
B_{r}
,
\varphi\equiv 1
on
B_{r/2}
and
|\nabla\varphi|\leq\frac{C}{r}
.
For each
t\in[0,T)
, as in
[
19
]
, define the local energy

(15)

\Theta_{r}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}}\varphi^{2}|df(t)|^{2}(y)dy.

Then

\begin{split}\frac{d\Theta}{dt}=&\int_{B_{r}}\varphi^{2}\langle df,df_{t}\rangle=-\int_{B_{r}}\varphi^{2}\langle\Delta f,f_{t}\rangle-2\int_{B_{r}}\varphi\langle df,\nabla\varphi\cdot f_{t}\rangle\\
=&-\int_{B_{r}}\varphi^{2}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{2}-2\int_{B_{r}}\varphi\langle df,\nabla\varphi\cdot f_{t}\rangle\\
\leq&\frac{C}{r}\int_{B_{r}}|df||f_{t}|\\
\leq&C\left(\int_{B_{r}}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

So

(16)

\begin{split}\Theta_{r}(t)-\Theta_{r}(s)\leq&C\int_{s}^{t}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{B_{r}}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

by
2.6
.
Hence
\Theta_{r}(t)
has a limit as
t\nearrow T
and we have

K=\lim_{t\nearrow T}\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}|df|^{2}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}|df|^{2}(T)=\lim_{t\nearrow T}\Theta_{r}(t)-\Theta_{r}(T)

for any
r
.
So, take
t\nearrow T
in (
16
) to get

(17)

\left|K+\Theta_{r}(T)-\Theta_{r}(s)\right|\leq C(T-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for any
r
and
s<T
.

Now fix
\delta>0
such that

(18)

\delta<\max\{\frac{1}{C},\frac{K}{2}\}

where
C,K
are from (
17
).
Fix
s=T-\delta^{4}
and choose
\varepsilon
small enough such that
|\Theta_{\varepsilon r}(T)|<\frac{K}{10}
and
|\Theta_{\varepsilon r}(s)|<\frac{K}{10}
.
Then we can replace
r
by
\varepsilon r
in (
17
) to get

\frac{8K}{10}<C(T-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}=C\delta^{2}<\delta<\frac{K}{2}

which is a contradiction.
Hence there is no finite time bubbling and the
Theorem

1.2
is proved.

4.
Infinite time analysis

Harmonic map flows may develop finite time bubbling or infinite time bubbling.
And for infinite time bubbling, the limit map becomes harmonic and there is a sequence of time
t_{n}\to\infty
such that the tension fields at
t_{n}
tends to zero in
L^{2}
.
Hence, unlike finite time bubbling, no-neck properties can be obtained in infinite time bubbling.
(Note that energy identity can be obtained in both cases.)
This observation leads to the finite time bubbling example, if the initial map is in the homotopy class that contains harmonic spheres far from each other.

Now if we run the CHF for the same initial condition, since no finite time bubbling occurs, there should be infinite time bubbling.
But we even do not know whether the limit map is harmonic.
In fact, we can find sequences
t_{n}\to\infty
such that

t_{n}\int_{M}e^{-2u}|\tau(f)|^{2}(t_{n})\to 0

which is not strong enough so that we cannot guarantee
\|\tau(f)(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
.

In this section, we try to overcome this difficulties by using extra regularity of CHF.

We first need the following higher estimate.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(f,u)
be a smooth solution of (
2
) on
M\times[0,\infty)
.
For
p\geq 1
and
t_{1}<t_{2}
, we have

(19)

\begin{split}\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}\leq C\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p-1}+C\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+1}\end{split}

where
C
only depends on
p
.

Proof.

From the first equation in (
2
), taking inner product with
f_{t}|f_{t}|^{p}
and integrating over
M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]
gives

\begin{split}\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}=&\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\langle f_{t},\Delta f\rangle|f_{t}|^{p}\\
=&-\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\langle\nabla f_{t},\nabla f\rangle|f_{t}|^{p}\\
&-p\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\langle f_{t},\nabla f\rangle|f_{t}|^{p-2}\langle f_{t},\nabla f_{t}\rangle\\
\leq&C\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p-1}+C\iint_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+1}.\end{split}

∎

Using above lemma and step-by-step increasing of regularity, we can get the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(f,u)
be a smooth solution of (

) on

.
For any

, we have

(
A_{p}
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+2},\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p}\leq

\displaystyle C

(
B_{p}
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}\leq

\displaystyle C

(

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}(t)=0

where the constant

depends on

Proof.

The proof consists of two parts.
First, we show that

A_{p}\Longrightarrow B_{p+1},\quad B_{p}\Longrightarrow C_{p},\quad B_{p}\Longrightarrow A_{p}.

Then we show the initial step, where we only need to show

.

By

, clearly

implies

.
Also, from (

),

implies

.
To see this, suppose

Then for any

, we can choose sequences

such that

,

, and that

But this contradicts to (

.

To show

implies

, we take

and

in (

).

For the initial step

, the result directly comes from

.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(f,u)
be a smooth solution of (
2
) on

.
For any

, we have

(

)

\displaystyle\lim_{t_{1}\to\infty}\lim_{t_{2}\to\infty}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}|df|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p+2},\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}|f_{t}|^{p}=

\displaystyle 0

(

)

\displaystyle\lim_{t_{1}\to\infty}\lim_{t_{2}\to\infty}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}=

\displaystyle 0.

Proof.

As above,

implies

.
From (

), together with

, we conclude that

implies

.
Finally, the initial step

is achieved from

.
∎

In fact, by taking a time sequence, we can show a better control than

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(f,u)
be a smooth solution of (
2
) on
M\times[0,\infty)
.
For any

, there exists

depending on

)

\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}\int_{M}e^{2u}|f_{t}|^{p+2}(t_{n})=0.

Proposition 5.4
.

There exists

(27)

On the other hand, by

with

Now choose

6.
Large energy case - Proof of
Theorem

1.4

In this section we deal with the case where
E(0)>\varepsilon_{2}
.
Note that if

for some

, then we can consider
f(T)
as the initial map and

be the initial time and apply
Theorem

1.3
to get the convergence.
So, in this section we essentially consider the case
E(t)>\varepsilon_{2}
for all

.

We consider a small ball
B_{2r}
where the energy over the ball is less than
\varepsilon_{2}
for all
t\geq T
for some
T
.
Due to the fact that
e^{2u}
involves time integral, this assumption for all

is needed.
Then we get the local
L^{2}
bound for the tension field, but due to the extra term from cut-off function, this bound is only under the time sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
defined on (

C
p

′

) with

.
By classical bubbling analysis, we can get a strong

convergence of

.

Let us describe the situation more precisely.

Definition 6.1
.

We say
x_{0}\in M
be a sequential bubble point (at time infinite) if there exists
s_{n}\to\infty
such that

(33)

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|df|^{2}(s_{n})>\varepsilon_{2}.

We say
x_{0}\in M
be a uniform bubble point (at time infinite) if

(34)

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|df|^{2}(t)>\varepsilon_{2}.

We say
x_{0}\in M
be a regular point (at time infinite) if

(35)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|df|^{2}(t)\leq\varepsilon_{2}

for all
r>0
small enough.

Now in each ball centered at a regular point, we will get
W^{1,2}
convergence of
f(t_{n})
.
Since the total energy is finite, the number of uniform bubble point is at most finite.
But we do not know how large the set of sequential bubble points is.
For example, we can consider a bubble moving around
N
and winding forever within a circular trajectory, in which case all points on the trajectory are sequential bubble points.

The following

convergence is similar to
5.4

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
(f,u)
be a solution of (
2
) and

be as in (

p

′

From the equation

By Sobolev embedding, for all

\begin{split}X(t)=&\left(\int_{B_{2r}}|\nabla^{2}(f\varphi)|^{2}(t)\right)^{2}\\

p

) and (

′

) with

Proof of

.

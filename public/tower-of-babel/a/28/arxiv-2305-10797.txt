A remark on Fremlin–Miller theorem concerning the Menger property and Michael concentrated sets
J. Chaber, R. Pol

Abstract
The theorem we prove is a slight strengthening of some results by Just, Miller, Scheepers and Szeptycki [ JMSS ] . We use the Michael technique instead of the combinatorial approach in the literature.



We consider only separable metrizable spaces. A space
M
has the
Menger property if for any sequence
{\mathcal{U}}_{1},{\mathcal{U}}_{2},\ldots

of open covers of
M
there are finite collections
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
with
\bigcup_{i}{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
covering
M
.
Let
{\rm{\bf d}}
be the minimal cardinality of a dominating set in
{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}

(we recall the definition below).

Fremlin and Miller
[
MF
]
proved that any space of cardinality
less than
{\rm{\bf d}}
has the Menger property. In particular, upon an
identification of
{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
with the irrationals, if
S
is a scale in

{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
of cardinality
{\rm{\bf d}}
, and
{\mathbb{Q}}
are the rationals, the union

S\cup{\mathbb{Q}}
has the Menger property (notice also that
S
is
concentrated about
{\mathbb{Q}}
). In effect, no matter where
{\rm{\bf d}}
is
situated on the alephs scale, the Fremlin–Miller theorem yields a
non-
\sigma
-compact space with the Menger property.

We shall add to this important result an observation that the
Michael technique of constructing concentrated sets in product
spaces, introduced in
[
Mi
]
, allows one to use the
Fremlin–Miller theorem to the following effect.

Theorem.

In every completely metrizable, separable
space
X
without any compact set with nonempty interior there is
an
M\subset X
whose all finite powers have the Menger property,
but
M
is not contained in any
\sigma
-compact subset of
X
.

Some closely related results were proved by Just, Miller,
Scheepers and Szeptycki with the use of a different, combinatorial
approach, cf. Theorems 3.9 and 5.1 in
[
JMSS
]
.

Before passing to a proof of the theorem, let us recall some facts
about the order
\leq^{*}
in
{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
, where
s\leq^{*}t
means that

s(n)\leq t(n)
for all but finitely many
n
. The minimal
cardinality of a set in
{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
unbounded with respect to
\leq^{*}
is
denoted by
{\rm{\bf b}}
, and
{\rm{\bf d}}
is the minimal cardinality of a set
dominating in
{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
, i.e., a set
D\subset{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
such that for any

s\in{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
there is a
t\in D
with
s\leq^{*}t
, cf.
[
vD
]
.
Always
{\rm{\bf b}}\leq{\rm{\bf d}}
, and the equality
{\rm{\bf b}}={\rm{\bf d}}
yields a dominating
set of cardinality
{\rm{\bf d}}
well-ordered by
\leq^{*}
, i.e., a scale in

{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
.

Proof of Theorem.
To begin with, let us recall that each

t\in{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
determines a
\sigma
-compact set

F(t)=\{s\in{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}:s\leq^{*}t\}
and each
\sigma
-compact set in
{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
is
contained in some
F(t)
.

Let
X
satisfy the assumptions and let us fix a countable set
Q

dense in
X
.

Let

f:{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}\rightarrow X

(1)

be a perfect
surjection.

Assume
{\rm{\bf b}}<{\rm{\bf d}}
, let
B\subset{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
be an unbounded set of
cardinality
{\rm{\bf b}}
and let
M=f(B)
. Since the cardinality of
M
is
less than
{\rm{\bf d}}
, any finite power of
M
has the Menger property by
Fremlin–Miller theorem and
M
is not contained in any

\sigma
-compact set in
X
.

From now on we assume
{\rm{\bf b}}={\rm{\bf d}}
. Then we have a scale

\{t_{\alpha}:\alpha<{\rm{\bf d}}\}
in
{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}
,
t_{\alpha}\leq^{*}t_{\beta}
for

\alpha<\beta
. Let, cf. (1),

F_{\alpha}=f(F(t_{\alpha})).

(2)

Each
\sigma
-compact set in
X
is contained in some
F_{\alpha}
.
Let
Z
be a compactification of
X
and let

g_{m}:{{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathbb{N}}}\rightarrow Z^{m}\setminus Q^{m}

be perfect surjections,
H_{\alpha}=X^{m}\setminus g_{m}(F(t_{\alpha}))
. Then
H_{\alpha}\supset Q^{m}
and, for any
collection
{\mathcal{H}}
of
G_{\delta}
sets in
X^{m}
containing
Q^{m}
,
if
|{\mathcal{H}}|<{\rm{\bf d}}
then there is
\alpha
with

H_{\alpha}\subset\bigcap{\mathcal{H}}
.

In effect, changing the enumeration of the sets
H_{\alpha}
, we get

G_{\delta}
-sets
G_{\xi}
with

Q^{m(\xi)}\subset G_{\xi}\subset X^{m(\xi)},\quad\xi<{\rm{\bf d}},

(3)

such that for any collection
{\mathcal{H}}
of
G_{\delta}
-sets in
X^{m}

containing
Q^{m}
,

\mbox{ if
}|{\mathcal{H}}|<{\rm{\bf d}}\mbox{ then }G_{\xi}\subset\bigcap{\mathcal{H}}\mbox{ for some }\xi\mbox{ with }m(\xi)=m.

(4)

We shall define

B=\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha<{\rm{\bf d}}\}\subset X\setminus Q,\quad\mbox{and}\quad M=Q\cup B.

(5)

such that the following concentration property holds true (cf.
[
PPR
, Lemma 2.2.2]
): for any
m
and each collection
{\mathcal{H}}
of
G_{\delta}
sets in
X^{m}
containing
Q^{m}
, if
|{\mathcal{H}}|<{\rm{\bf d}}
then
M^{m}\setminus\bigcap{\mathcal{H}}
is contained in the
union of less than
{\rm{\bf d}}
hyperplanes obtained by fixing one of the
coordinates in
M^{m}
.

A straightforward induction on
m
shows that for such
M
every
product
L\times M^{m}
, where
|L|<{\rm{\bf d}}
, has the Menger property
(for
m=0
use the Fremlin–Miller theorem). Therefore it is
enough to define the set
B
in (5), ensuring also that
B
is not
contained in any
\sigma
-compact set in
X
.

To this end, we shall modify slightly a reasoning in
[
PPR
, Comment
9.5]
, which is a variation of the celebrated Michael technique

[
Mi
]
.

We shall start with some notation. Let
S\subset\{1,2,...,m\}
,

u\in X^{S}
,
v\in X^{\{1,2,...,m\}\setminus S}
. Then
(u,v)
is
the point
w\in X^{m}
with
w(i)=u(i)
for
i\in S
and
w(i)=v(i)

for
i\notin S
. Further, given a
G_{\delta}
-set
G
in
X^{m}

containing
Q^{m}
, we let

G(S)=\{u\in X^{S}:(u,v)\in G\mbox{ for
all }v\in Q^{\{1,2,...,m\}\setminus S}\},

and, for
u\in G(S)
,

j\in{\{1,2,...,m\}\setminus S}
, we let

G(u,j)=\{x\in X:(u,x)\in G(S\cup\{j\})\}.

Then
G(S)
,
G(u,j)
are

G_{\delta}
-sets with

Q^{S}\subset G(S)\subset X^{S},\quad Q\subset G(u,j)\subset X

(6)

We shall pick distinct points
x_{\alpha}
of the set
B
in (5) by
transfinite induction, such that, with
\Delta(S)=\{u\in X^{S}:\mbox{ at least two coordinates of }u\mbox{ are equal}\},
we
shall have for any
\alpha<{\rm{\bf d}}
,
\xi<\alpha
,
S\subset\{1,2,...,m(\xi)\}
,

\{x_{\eta}:\xi<\eta\leq\alpha\}^{S}\setminus\Delta(S)\subset G_{\xi}(S)

We begin with an arbitrary
x_{0}\notin Q
. At each stage

\alpha>0
,
x_{\alpha}
must be chosen from the intersection of a
collection
{\mathcal{H}}
consisting of sets of the form
G_{\xi}(u,j)
,

\xi<\alpha
,
u\in G_{\xi}(S)\cap\{x_{\eta}:\xi<\eta<\alpha\}^{S}\setminus\Delta(S)
,
S\cup\{j\}\subset\{1,2,...,m(\xi)\}
, and

j\notin S
. But these are
G_{\delta}
-sets in
X
containing
Q
,
cf. (6), (3), and
|{\mathcal{H}}|<{\rm{\bf d}}
, hence by (4), the intersection

\bigcap{\mathcal{H}}
contains a
G_{\delta}
-set in
X
containing
Q
.
This shows that a choice of
x_{\alpha}
is always possible and,
moreover, one can pick
x_{\alpha}
outside of the set
F_{\alpha}
,
meager in
X
, cf. (2), which will also guarantee that the
resulting set
B
is not contained in any
\sigma
-compact set in

X
. A verification that the space
M=Q\cup B
has the
concentration property described below (5) is similar to a simple
reasoning in
[
PPR
, Comment 9.5]
.

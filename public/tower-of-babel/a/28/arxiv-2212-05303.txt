Dynamical optical conductivity for gapped α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} materials with a curved ”flat” band
Andrii Iurov 1 , Liubov Zhemchuzhna 1,2 , Godfrey Gumbs 2,3 , and Danhong Huang 4

Abstract
We have calculated the dynamical optical conductivity for α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} materials in the presence of a finite bandgap in their energy bandstructure. This is a special type of energy dispersions because for all α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} materials with a bandgap, except graphene and a dice lattice limits, the flat band receives a non-zero dispersion and assumes a curved shape. The infinite 𝐤 {\bf k} -degeneracy of the flat energy band is also lifted. Such a low-energy bandstructure could be obtained if an α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} material is irradiated off-resonant with circularly polarized light. We have calculated the optical conductivity for the zero and finite temperatures, as well as for the cases of a finite and nearly-zero doping. We have demonstrated that analytical expressions could be in principle obtained for all types of gapped α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} materials and provided the closed-form analytical expressions for a gapped dice lattice. Our numerical results reveal some well-known signatures of the optical conductivity in α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} and silicene with two non-equivalent bandgaps, as well as demonstrate some very specific features which have not been previously found in any existing Dirac materials.

III
Optical conductivity

Once the electronic states and their dispersions are known, we are at position to calculate the dynamical optical conductivity of our lattice. According to the Kubo formalism, the real (absorptive) part of the longitudinal conductivity
\sigma_{T=0}^{\,(D)}(\omega\,|\,\Delta_{0})
is given by

\sigma_{T=0}^{\,(D)}(\omega\,|\,\Delta_{0})=\frac{\pi e^{2}}{\hbar}\,\int\frac{d\xi}{\omega}\,\mathbb{T}(\xi,\phi)\,\left[f_{0}(\xi+\omega\,|\,\mu(T),T)-f_{0}(\xi+\omega\,|\,\mu(T),T)\right]\,

(11)

where where
g_{s}g_{e}=4
are the spin and valley degeneracy factors,
n_{F}(\xi\,|\,\mu,T)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function and
\mu(T)

is the chemical potential such that
\mu(T)\longrightarrow E_{F}
and
n_{F}(\xi\,|\,\mu,T)\longrightarrow\Theta(E_{F}-\xi)
for
T\rightarrow 0
.

The most crucial part of Eq. (
11
) is the trace of the product of four matrices obtained as

\mathbb{T}(\xi,\phi)=\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\text{Tr}\left\{\left[\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)},x\right]\,\delta\left(\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}-\xi-\omega\right)\,\left[\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)},x\right]\,\delta\left(\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}-\xi\right)\right\}\,.

(12)

Operators
\left[\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)},x\right]
could be rewritten using the definition of a current:

\hat{j}_{x}=-ie\left[\hat{H}_{\tau}^{(\phi)}({\bf k}),x\right]=-e\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}({\bf k})}{\partial k_{x}}\,,

(13)

and a similar expression for current
\hat{j}_{y}
. Since we only consider isotropic materials and circularly polarized irradiation, we will only
calculate the
xx-
component of the optical conductivity.

Therefore, we also need to to evaluate

\mathbb{T}(\xi,\phi)=\text{Tr}\left\{\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}({\bf k})}{\partial k_{x}}\,\delta\left(\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}-\xi-\omega\right)\,\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}({\bf k})}{\partial k_{x}}\,\delta\left(\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}-\xi\right)\right\}\,,

(14)

which is only possible in the representation in which Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}({\bf k})
is a diagonal matrix. In order to do that, we need to apply the following transformation

\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}({\bf k})}{\partial k_{x}}\longrightarrow\left\langle\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(-1)}\Big|\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}({\bf k})}{\partial k_{x}}\Big|\hat{P}_{\psi}\right\rangle

(15)

and

\displaystyle\delta\left(\hat{H}_{0}^{\,(\phi)}({\bf k},\Delta_{0})-\xi\right)\longrightarrow\left\langle\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(-1)}\Big|\delta\left(\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}-\varepsilon\right)\Big|\hat{P}_{\psi}\right\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\delta\left[\epsilon^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=-1}(k)-\xi\right]&0&0\\
0&\delta\left[\epsilon^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=0}(k)-\xi\right]&0\\
0&0&\delta\left[\epsilon^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=1}(k)-\xi\right]\end{array}\right\}\,,

and

\displaystyle\delta\left(\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}-\varepsilon-\omega\right)\longrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\delta\left[\epsilon^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=-1}(k,\Delta_{0})-\xi-\omega\right]&0&0\\
0&\delta\left[\epsilon^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=0}(k,\Delta_{0})-\xi-\omega\right]&0\\
0&0&\delta\left[\epsilon^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=1}(k,\Delta_{0})-\xi-\omega\right]\end{array}\right\}\,,

The diagonalizing transformation matrix
\hat{P}_{\psi}
is build from the component of the eigenfunction

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{\,(\phi)}_{\psi}({\bf k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=-1}({\bf k})_{[1]}&\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=0}({\bf k})_{[1]}&\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=1}({\bf k})_{[1]}\\
\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=-1}({\bf k})_{[2]}&\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=0}({\bf k})_{[2]}&\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=1}({\bf k})_{[2]}\\
\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=-1}({\bf k})_{[3]}&\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=0}({\bf k})_{[3]}&\Psi^{\,(\phi)}_{\gamma=1}({\bf k})_{[3]}\end{array}\right\}

This transformation obviously does not change a trace of the product of four matrices
\hat{A}^{\,(\phi)}({\bf k})\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}^{\,(\phi)}_{\epsilon}({\bf k})\times\hat{A}^{\,(\phi)}({\bf k})\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}^{\,(\phi)}_{\xi+\omega}({\bf k})
since

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{\left[\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(-1)}\times\hat{A}\times\hat{P}_{\psi}\right]\times\left[\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(-1)}\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi}\times\hat{P}_{\psi}\right]\times\left[\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(-1)}\times\hat{A}\times\hat{P}_{\psi}\right]\times\left[\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(-1)}\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi+\omega}\times\hat{P}_{\psi}\right]\right\}=

(28)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\left\{\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(-1)}\times\hat{A}\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi}\times\hat{A}\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi+\omega}\times\hat{P}_{\psi}\right\}=

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\left\{\hat{A}\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi}\times\hat{A}\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi+\omega}\times\hat{P}_{\psi}\times\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(-1)}\right\}=\text{Tr}\left\{\hat{A}\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi}\times\hat{A}\times\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi+\omega}\right\}\,.

where
\hat{A}^{\,(\phi)}({\bf k})=\partial\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}({\bf k})/(\partial k_{x})
,
\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\epsilon}({\bf k})=\delta\left[\hat{H}_{0}^{(\phi)}-\varepsilon\right]
and
\hat{\mathbb{O}}_{\xi+\omega}({\bf k})=\delta\left[\hat{H}^{\,(\phi)}_{0}({\bf k})-\xi-\omega\right]
. Here, we also used a relation
\text{Tr}\left\{\hat{A}\times\hat{B}\right\}=\text{Tr}\left\{\hat{B}\times\hat{A}\right\}
.

For a gapless
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\psi}^{\,(\phi)}({\bf k})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\texttt{e}^{-i\theta_{\bf k}}\,\cos\phi&\sqrt{2}\,\texttt{e}^{-i\theta_{\bf k}}\,\sin\phi&\texttt{e}^{-i\theta_{\bf k}}\,\cos\phi\\
1&0&-1\\
\texttt{e}^{i\theta_{\bf k}}\,\sin\phi&\sqrt{2}\,\texttt{e}^{i\theta_{\bf k}}\,\cos\phi&\texttt{e}^{i\theta_{\bf k}}\,\sin\phi\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\psi}^{(0)}({\bf k})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
\texttt{e}^{i\theta_{\bf k}}&-\texttt{e}^{i\theta_{\bf k}}\end{array}\right\}

IV
Summary and Remarks

We have calculated the dynamical optical conductivity for various kinds of
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
materials in the presence of a finite energy bandgap for different values of electron doping, including the final temperatures. The bandstructure of a gapped
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
lattice demonstrates some very unusual properties since except for the two limiting cases of graphene
\phi=0
and a dice lattice
\phi=\pi/4
, the middle (initially, flat) band receives a finite dispersion. Therefore, it could be partially or completely doped. Also, it is important that the infinite degeneracy of the flat band is lifted and the Fermi level could be placed within its energy range. Such energy dispersions and the electronic states could be obtained as a result of the interaction between Dirac electrons in an
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
material with off-resonance dressing field.

We have found that the dynamical, or frequency-dependent, optical conductivity shares all the known features of that in a zero-gap
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
materials and silicene with two inequivalent bandgaps. At zero temperature, the optical conductivity in graphene for
\omega<2\mu
is equal to zero because of the Pauli blocking, meaning that the carriers cannot transfer between the two occupied states.

For
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
, the conductivity shows two separate steps corresponding to the transitions between the valence and conduction bands, and the flat and conduction bands, correspondingly. For a finite Fermi level, we also observe an infinite Drude conductivity peak
\backsim\delta(\omega)/\omega
at
\omega\longrightarrow 0
due to the intraband electron transitions with vanishing energy transfer between the initial and final electron states.

In a specific case of gapped
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
materials, only one of the two peaks of the optical conductivity shows a
\backsim\left(1-\Delta_{0}^{2}/\omega^{2}\right)
decrease which is not observed for a dice lattice with only one such jump of optical conductivity for
\omega=\mu
. The latter case is special because the flat band remains unaffected by the external radiation. Thus, our results are simplified and an analytical solution for the Green’s function, spectral function and the optical conductivity have been obtained. We have also derived analytical expressions for the zero-bandgap limit of a dice lattice which also corresponds to
\phi=\pi/4
limit of a gapless
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
material.

Optical conductivity is one of the most important measurable quantities which has been thoroughly studied for any new low-dimensional material. Its unique properties and, specifically, its frequency dependence allows for an experimental verification of the bandstructure of a material for various doping levels.
Grazianetti et al. 2018
We are confident that our unusual results for the optical conductivity of a gapped
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
could be undoubtedly employed in a number of promising technical applications.

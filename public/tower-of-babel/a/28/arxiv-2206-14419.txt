Fractal polynomials On the Sierpiński gasket and some dimensional results
V. Agrawal, S. Verma, T. Som

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore some significant properties associated with a fractal operator on the space of all continuous functions defined on the Sierpiński Gasket (SG). We also provide some results related to constrained approximation with fractal polynomials and study the best approximation properties of fractal polynomials defined on the SG. Further we discuss some remarks on the class of polynomials defined on the SG and try to estimate the fractal dimensions of the graph of α \displaystyle\alpha - fractal function defined on the SG by using the oscillation of functions.

2.1.
Attractors and measures

One of the most fundamental concepts that emerge from IFS is the concept of an invariant set. IFS is composed of a finite family of contraction maps.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(X)
be a collection of all non-empty compact subsets of
\displaystyle X
, where
\displaystyle(X,d)
is a metric space.
For all
\displaystyle k=1,\ldots,K
, let
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}:X\rightarrow X
be a finite family of contraction maps with a contraction ratio
\displaystyle\alpha_{k}
respectively. The family
\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{S}_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{K}
is called an IFS. The function
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
:
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(X)
defined by
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(B)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{S}_{k}(B)
is called the associated Hutchinson operator. The metric space
\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}(X),h)
is complete with respect to the Hausdorff metric
\displaystyle h
if and only if
\displaystyle(X,d)
is complete for the contraction ratio
\displaystyle\alpha
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
is
\displaystyle\max\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
. A set
\displaystyle A\in\mathcal{K}(X)
is said to be an attractor of the IFS whenever
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(A)=A
.

For more details, the reader may refer to
[
5
]
. Let us denote the Hausdorff dimension, lower box dimension and upper box dimension of
\displaystyle A
by
\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{H}(A)
,
\displaystyle\underline{\operatorname{dim}}_{B}(A)
and
\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{dim}}_{B}(A)
, respectively and the graph of
\displaystyle f
by
\displaystyle Gr(f)
, i.e.,
\displaystyle Gr(f)=

\displaystyle\{(t,f(t)):t\in\mathrm{SG}\}
.

2.2.
Fractal interpolation function on SG

Let
\displaystyle A
be the notation of an equilateral triangle. Let
\displaystyle V_{0}=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
be the vertices of
\displaystyle A
. For the vertices
\displaystyle V_{0}
, the contraction mappings
\displaystyle L_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
are defined by

L_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{2}(p_{i}+t),

where
\displaystyle p_{i}\in V_{0}
and
\displaystyle\{\mathbb{R}^{2}:L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\}
be the IFS and produces
\displaystyle SG
as an attractor, that is,
\displaystyle SG=\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}(SG).

For
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\displaystyle I^{N}
represents the set of all the words of length
\displaystyle N
, where
\displaystyle I=\{1,2,3\}
, that is, if
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}
, then
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}=\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}
, where
\displaystyle\omega_{i}\in\{1,2,3\}.
For
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}
,
\displaystyle L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
is defined by

L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=L_{\omega_{1}}\circ\dots\circ L_{\omega_{N}}.

In
[
13
]

\displaystyle\mu_{p}
supported on SG associated with the IFS
\displaystyle\{\mathbb{R}^{2},L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\}
and probability vector
\displaystyle p=\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3}\right)
satisfying:

\displaystyle\mu_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mu_{p}\circ L_{i}^{-1}.

Set
\displaystyle V_{N}=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(p_{2}),L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(p_{3}),L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(p_{1}):\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}\}.
Let
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}|=N
and if we take the union of images of
\displaystyle V_{0}
for the iterations
\displaystyle L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
, then we get the set of N-th stage vertices
\displaystyle V_{N}
of
\displaystyle SG
. Now define
\displaystyle V_{*}=\bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty}V_{N}.

Let
\displaystyle f:SG\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function on SG
and
\displaystyle K=SG\times\mathbb{R}
. Define maps
\displaystyle W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}:K\rightarrow K
by

W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t,x)=\Big(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t,x)\Big),~~{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\{1,2,3\}^{N},

where
\displaystyle F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t,x):SG\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
must satisfy the following condition:

|F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(.,x)-F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(.,x^{\prime})|\leq c|x-x^{\prime}|,

with
\displaystyle c<1
and

\displaystyle F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(p_{j},f(p_{j}))=f(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(p_{j})).

In particular,

F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t,x)=\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t)x+f(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t))-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t)b(t),

where
\displaystyle b:SG\to\mathbb{R}
is a map satisfying
\displaystyle b(p_{j})=f(p_{j}),~j=1,2,3
and for all
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}
,
\displaystyle\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function with
\displaystyle\|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{\infty}<1.

Finally, the system
\displaystyle\{K;W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}\}
is an IFS. From
[

f^{\alpha}(t)=f(t)+\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{-1}(t))~f^{\alpha}\big(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{-1}(t)\big)-\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{-1}(t))~b\big(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{-1}(t)\big),

(1)

for all
\displaystyle t\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG),~\boldsymbol{\omega}~~\in I^{N},
and the
\displaystyle Gr(f^{\alpha})
is the attractor of IFS
\displaystyle\{K;W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}\}.
This type of fractal functions are widely known as

2.4.
Energy

We begin with the construction of a complete graph
\displaystyle{\Gamma_{0}}
via vertex set
\displaystyle V_{0}
. The following procedure is used to recursively construct the graph
\displaystyle{\Gamma_{m}}
. First we construct the graph
\displaystyle\Gamma_{m-1}
using the vertex set
\displaystyle V_{m-1}
for some
\displaystyle m\geq 1
. The graph
\displaystyle{\Gamma_{m}}
on
\displaystyle V_{m}
is defined as follows: The edge relation
\displaystyle t\sim_{m}z
exists for any
\displaystyle t,z\in V_{m}

if and only if
\displaystyle t=L_{i}(t^{{}^{\prime}}),~z=L_{i}(z^{{}^{\prime}})
with

\displaystyle t^{{}^{\prime}}\sim_{m-1}z^{{}^{\prime}}
and
\displaystyle i\in I
. Equivalently,
\displaystyle t\sim_{m}z
if and only if there exists
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}
such that
\displaystyle t,z\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(V_{0})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the graph energy at the
\displaystyle m^{th}
level on
\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}
is denoted by
\displaystyle E_{m}
and defined as follows:

E_{m}(f)=\bigg(\frac{5}{3}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{t\sim_{m}z}\big(f{(t)}-f(z)\big)^{2},

which satisfy
\displaystyle E_{m-1}(f)=\min E_{m}(\tilde{f})
, where the minimum is computed through every
\displaystyle\tilde{f}
such that
\displaystyle\tilde{f}|_{V_{m-1}}=f
for each
\displaystyle f:V^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle m\geq 1
. It is worth noting that
\displaystyle\big(E_{m}(f)\big)_{m=0}^{\infty}
is increasing sequence for all
\displaystyle f
.

The limit

E(f):=\lim_{m\to\infty}E_{m}(f)

is referred to as the energy of
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle V_{*}
. Note that if
\displaystyle f
has finite energy, then

\displaystyle E(f)<\infty.

Note that every
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle V_{*}
whose energy is finite are uniformly continuous. Since
\displaystyle V_{*}
is dense in
\displaystyle SG
, which immediately implies that
\displaystyle f
has a unique extension as a continuous function on
\displaystyle SG
. We shall denote
\displaystyle f\in\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
as a continuous function
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle SG
with
\displaystyle E(f)<+\infty
. We now define for each
\displaystyle f,g\in\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})

\mathcal{E}(f,g)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\bigg(\frac{5}{3}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{x\sim_{m}y}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y)).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}(SG)
such that
\displaystyle E_{m-1}(f)=E_{m}(f)
for all
\displaystyle m\geq 1
, then
\displaystyle f
is
a harmonic function on
\displaystyle SG
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\displaystyle f\in\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
and
\displaystyle g
be continuous. Then
\displaystyle f\in\text{dom}\Delta
with
\displaystyle\Delta f=g
if

\mathcal{E}(f,h)=-\int_{SG}gh~d\mu_{p}~~\text{for all}~~h\in\text{dom}_{0}(\mathcal{E}),

where
\displaystyle\text{dom}_{0}(\mathcal{E})=\{f\in\text{dom}(\mathcal{E}):f|_{V_{0}}=0\}.

The Laplacian of
\displaystyle f
can also be determined using a pointwise formula.
Define a graph Laplacian
\displaystyle\Delta_{m}
on
\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}
by

\Delta_{m}f(x):=\sum_{y\sim_{m}x}\big(f(y)-f(x)\big),~x\in V_{m}\backslash V_{0}.

Using Theorems
\displaystyle(2.2.1)
and
\displaystyle(2.2.12)
in
[
27
]
, we get the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\displaystyle f\in\text{dom}(\Delta)
, then the pointwise formula

\Delta f(x)=\frac{3}{2}\lim_{m\to\infty}5^{m}\Delta_{m}f(x)

(2)

holds with the limit uniform across
\displaystyle V_{*}\backslash V_{0}
. Conversely, suppose
\displaystyle f
is a continuous function and the right side of equation (
2
) converges uniformly to a continuous function on
\displaystyle V_{*}\backslash V_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle f\in\text{dom}(\Delta)
and equation (
2
) holds.

It can be seen that the Laplacian is a linear operator from the above lemma.
Let
\displaystyle h
be a harmonic function. Now, using the well-known rule
\displaystyle``\frac{1}{5}-\frac{2}{5}"
, we have
\displaystyle\Delta_{m}h(x)=0
for any
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle x\in V_{m}\backslash V_{0}
so that
\displaystyle\Delta h=0
.

2.5.
Polynomials on SG

The space of polynomials on the unit interval may be represented as the space of solutions to
\displaystyle\Delta^{k}=0
for some
\displaystyle k
. So one can define a polynomial on
\displaystyle SG
with standard Laplacian discussed in
[
27
]
as the solution of the same equation. We define

\mathcal{H}_{k}(SG)=\{f:\Delta^{k+1}f=0\}

. These functions are referred as a multi-harmonic. In the case of
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}
, the class of harmonic functions is three-dimensional and a function in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}
is obtained uniquely by boundary values. For
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}
, the class of bi-harmonic functions is six- dimensional. For
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2}
, the class of tri-harmonic functions is nine-dimensional. For
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}
, the class of multi-harmonic functions is
\displaystyle 3(k+1)
dimensional.

The Weierstrass Approximation Theorem shows that the continuous real-valued functions on a compact interval can be uniformly approximated by polynomials.
Whereas the polynomials defined on
\displaystyle SG
, which are obtained from the class of multi-harmonic functions, do not follow the Weierstrass Approximation Theorem.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\displaystyle f:SG\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function defined on SG. Then
\displaystyle f
is called lower Hölder continuous function if there exists
\displaystyle\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\displaystyle x\in SG
and
\displaystyle\delta<\delta_{0}
, there exists
\displaystyle y\in SG
with
\displaystyle\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}\leq\delta
such that

\lvert f(x)-f(x)\rvert\geq K\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}^{\sigma},

where
\displaystyle K\geq 0
is a constant and
\displaystyle 0<\sigma\leq 1
is Hölder exponent.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\displaystyle f:SG\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a lower Hölder continuous function. Then the energy of
\displaystyle f
is infinite.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle f
is a lower Hölder continuous function, then using Definition
2.6
, for every
\displaystyle x\in SG
there exists
\displaystyle y\in SG
such that
\displaystyle\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}\leq 1
. Since
\displaystyle 0<\sigma\leq 1
. Then

\lVert x-y\rVert_{2}^{\sigma}\geq\lvert x-y\rVert_{2}.

(3)

For
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
\displaystyle x,y\in V_{n}
, the
\displaystyle n^{th}
level energy of
\displaystyle f
,
\displaystyle E_{n}(f)
is

E_{n}(f)=\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{n}\sum_{x\sim_{n}y}\|f(x)-f(y)\|^{2}.

Using Definition
2.6
, we get

E_{n}(f)\geq\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{n}K\sum_{x\sim_{n}y}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2\sigma}.

(4)

Now, from (
3
), we have

E_{n}(f)\geq\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{n}K\sum_{x\sim_{n}y}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}.

(5)

For
\displaystyle x,y\in V_{0}
, we get

\sum_{x\sim_{0}y}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}=1+2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}.

At
\displaystyle n^{th}
level of
\displaystyle SG
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\sim_{n}y}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=3^{n}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\right)^{2}\right]

(6)

\displaystyle=\frac{3^{n+1}}{2^{2n+1}}.

Putting the above value in (
5
), we get

E_{n}(f)\geq 3K\frac{5^{n}}{2^{2n+1}}

Finally,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}(f)=\infty\text{. }

Therefore, energy of the lower Hölder continuous function
\displaystyle f
defined on
\displaystyle SG
is infinite, that is,

E(f)=\infty\text{. }

This completes the proof.
∎

Taking
\displaystyle t,u\in SG
, one obtains

\displaystyle|F_{\omega_{1}}(t,f^{\alpha}(t))-F_{\omega_{1}}(u,f^{\alpha}(u))|=

\displaystyle\big|\alpha_{\omega_{1}}(t)f^{\alpha}(t)+f(L_{\omega_{1}}(t))-\alpha_{\omega_{1}}(t)b(t)

(21)

\displaystyle-\alpha_{\omega_{1}}(u)f^{\alpha}(u)-f(L_{\omega_{1}}(u))+\alpha_{\omega_{1}}(u)b(u)\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|\alpha_{\omega_{1}}(t)\big|~\big|f^{\alpha}(t)-f^{\alpha}(u)\big|+\big|\alpha_{\omega_{1}}(t)\big|~\big|b(t)-b(u)\big|

\displaystyle+(\big|b(u)|+|f^{\alpha}(u)\big|)~\big|\big(\alpha_{\omega_{1}}(t)-\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}(u)\big)\big|

\displaystyle+\big|f(L_{{\omega}_{1}}(t))-f(L_{{\omega}_{1}}(u))\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\big\|_{\infty}\big|f^{\alpha}(t)-f^{\alpha}(u)\big|+K_{b}\big\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\big\|_{\infty}\big\|t-u\big\|_{2}^{\sigma_{b}}

\displaystyle+K_{f}\big\|L_{{\omega}_{1}}(t)-L_{{\omega}_{1}}(u)\big\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\big\|_{\infty}\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}(SG)+\big\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\big\|_{\infty}K_{b}+K_{\alpha}\ \big(\big\|b\big\|_{\infty}+\big\|f^{\alpha}\big\|_{\infty}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{K_{f}}{2^{N\sigma}}.

Performing iteration, we have

\displaystyle\text{OSC}_{F_{{\omega}_{m}}}\big(G_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m-1}}\big)\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\big\|_{\infty}~\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}\big(L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m-1}}\big)

(22)

\displaystyle+\frac{K_{b}\big\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\big\|_{\infty}+K_{\alpha}\ \big(\big\|b\big\|_{\infty}+\big\|f^{\alpha}\big\|_{\infty}\big)}{2^{N\sigma(m-1)}}+\frac{K_{f}}{2^{N\sigma}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}
be
\displaystyle\alpha
- fractal function on
\displaystyle SG
. Then we have the following:

(1).

If
\displaystyle\alpha_{\min}>\frac{1}{2^{nN}}
, then
\displaystyle\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}\leq\dim_{H}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))\leq~\underline{\dim_{B}}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))\\
\leq\overline{\dim_{B}}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))\leq~1+\frac{\log(\tilde{\gamma})}{N\log 2}.

(2).

If
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{nN}}<\alpha_{max}<1
, then
\displaystyle\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}\leq\dim_{H}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))\leq~\underline{\dim_{B}}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))\\
\leq\overline{\dim_{B}}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))\leq~1+\frac{\log(3\alpha_{\max})}{N\log 2}.

Proof.

Note that
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}
holds the following self-referential equation:

f^{\alpha}(t)=F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\big(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{-1}(t),f^{\alpha}(t)\big)~~\forall~t\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG)~\text{~}{and}~\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}.

Let
\displaystyle t,u\in L_{{\omega}_{1}}(SG),
we have

\displaystyle|f^{\alpha}(t)-f^{\alpha}(u)|

\displaystyle=|F_{{\omega}_{1}}\big(L_{{\omega}_{1}}^{-1}(t),f^{\alpha}(t)\big)-F_{{\omega}_{1}}\big(L_{{\omega}_{1}}^{-1}(u),f^{\alpha}(u)\big)|

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\tilde{t},\tilde{u}\in L_{{\omega}_{1}}(SG)}|F_{{\omega}_{1}}\big(L_{{\omega}_{1}}^{-1}(\tilde{t}),f^{\alpha}(\tilde{t})\big)-F_{{\omega}_{1}}\big(L_{{\omega}_{1}}^{-1}(\tilde{u}),f^{\alpha}(\tilde{u})\big)|

\displaystyle=\text{OSC}_{F_{{\omega}_{1}}}\big[Gr(f^{\alpha})\big].

Hence, for any
\displaystyle t,u\in L_{{\omega}_{1}}(SG)
,

\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}\big[L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m}}\big(SG)]\leq\text{OSC}_{F_{{\omega}_{m}}}\big[G_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m-1}}\big].

Using the previous lemma, one obtains

\displaystyle\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\big[L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\cdots{\omega}_{m}}\big(SG)]

(24)

\displaystyle~\leq

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\|_{\infty}\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}[SG]

\displaystyle+K_{b}\bigg[\frac{\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}}{2^{(m-1)N\sigma}}+\frac{\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}}{2^{(m-2)N\sigma}}+\cdots+\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\|_{\infty}\bigg]

\displaystyle+K_{f}\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{mN\sigma}}+\frac{\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}}{2^{(m-1)N\sigma}}+\frac{\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}}{2^{(m-2)N\sigma}}

\displaystyle+\dots+\frac{\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{2}}\|_{\infty}}{2^{N\sigma}}\bigg]

\displaystyle+K_{\alpha}\big(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\big)\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{(m-1)N\sigma}}+\frac{\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}}{2^{(m-2)N\sigma}}+\frac{\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}}{2^{(m-3)N\sigma}}

\displaystyle+\dots+\big(\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{2}}\|_{\infty}\big)\bigg].

Now, we are well-equipped to estimate the fractal dimension of
\displaystyle Gr(f^{\alpha})
.

(1).

If
\displaystyle\alpha_{min}>\frac{1}{2^{N\sigma}}
, then we get

\displaystyle\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\big[L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m}}\big(SG)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\|_{\infty}\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}[SG]

\displaystyle+K_{b}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\|_{\infty}\bigg[1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\min}2^{N\sigma}}+\dots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\min}^{m}2^{mN\sigma}}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{K_{f}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{2}}\|_{\infty}}{2^{N\sigma}}\bigg[1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\min}2^{N\sigma}}+\dots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\min}^{m-1}2^{(m-1)N\sigma}}\bigg]

\displaystyle+K_{\alpha}\big(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\big)\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{2}}\|_{\infty}\bigg[1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\min}2^{N\sigma}}+\dots

\displaystyle\dots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\min}^{m-1}2^{(m-1)N\sigma}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\|_{\infty}\bigg[\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}[SG]

\displaystyle+\bigg(K_{b}+\frac{K_{f}}{\alpha_{\min}2^{N\sigma}}+\frac{K_{\alpha}\big(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\big)}{\alpha_{\min}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{\min}2^{N\sigma}}}\bigg)\bigg].

Finally, we have

\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}\big[L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m}}\big(SG)]\leq R_{1}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\|_{\infty},

where
\displaystyle R_{1}=\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}[SG]+\bigg(K_{b}+\frac{K_{f}}{\alpha_{\min}2^{N\sigma}}+\frac{K_{\alpha}\big(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\big)}{\alpha_{\min}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{\min}2^{N\sigma}}}\bigg).

Lemma (
7.2
) now yields

\displaystyle N_{\delta}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\leq 2.3^{n}+2^{nN}\sum_{\omega\in I^{n}}\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}\big[L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m}}\big(SG)]

(25)

\displaystyle\leq 2.3^{n}+2^{nN}\sum_{\omega\in I^{n}}R_{1}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{m-1}}\|_{\infty}\dots\|\alpha_{{\omega}_{1}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2.3^{n}+2^{nN}R_{1}\bar{\gamma}^{n},

where
\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}=\sum_{\omega\in I^{n}}\|\alpha_{\omega}\|_{\infty}
. Further, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\lim}_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log(N_{\delta}(Gr(f^{\alpha})))}{-\log(\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\lim}_{n\to{\infty}}\frac{\log(2.3^{n}+2^{nN}R_{1}\bar{\gamma}^{n})}{nN\log(2)}

(26)

\displaystyle=\overline{\lim}_{n\to{\infty}}\frac{\log(2^{nN}R_{1}\bar{\gamma}^{n})+\log\bigg(1+\frac{2.3^{n}}{2^{nN}R_{1}\bar{\gamma}^{n}}\bigg)}{nN\log 2}.

Since
\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}=\sum_{\omega\in I^{n}}\|\alpha_{\omega}\|_{\infty}
, we have
\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}\geq 3^{n}\alpha_{\min}
. Combining the inequality
\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}\geq 3^{n}\alpha_{\min}
and
\displaystyle N>N\sigma
with the above inequality (i.e.
\displaystyle\alpha_{min}>\frac{1}{2^{N\sigma}}
). This gives
\displaystyle\frac{3}{\tilde{\gamma}2^{N}}<1
. Therefore, we obtain

\overline{\lim}_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log(N_{\delta}(Gr(f^{\alpha})))}{-\log(\delta)}\leq 1+\frac{\log(\bar{\gamma})}{N\log 2}.

(2).

If
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N\sigma}}<\alpha_{\max}<1
, then we get

\displaystyle\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\big[L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m}}\big(SG)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{\max}^{m}\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}[SG]

\displaystyle+K_{b}\alpha_{\max}^{m}\bigg[1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\max}2^{N\sigma}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\max}^{m}2^{mN\sigma}}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{K_{f}\alpha_{\max}^{m-1}}{2^{N\sigma}}\bigg[1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\max}2^{N\sigma}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\max}^{m-1}2^{(m-1)N\sigma}}\bigg]

\displaystyle+K_{\alpha}\big(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\big)\alpha_{\max}^{m-1}\bigg[1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\max}2^{N\sigma}}+\dots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{\max}^{m-1}2^{(m-1)N\sigma}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{max}^{m}\bigg[\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}[SG]+\bigg(K_{b}+\frac{K_{f}}{\alpha_{\max}2^{N\sigma}}+\frac{K_{\alpha}\big(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\big)}{\alpha_{\max}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{\max}2^{N\sigma}}}\bigg)\bigg].

Hence,

\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}\big[L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m}}\big(SG)]\leq R_{2}\alpha_{max}^{m},

where
\displaystyle R_{2}=\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}[SG]+\bigg(K_{b}+\frac{K_{f}}{\alpha_{\max}2^{N\sigma}}+\frac{K_{\alpha}\big(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\big)}{\alpha_{\max}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{\max}2^{N\sigma}}}\bigg).

Lemma (
7.2
) yields

\displaystyle N_{\delta}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\leq 2.3^{n}+2^{n}\sum_{\omega\in I^{n}}\text{OSC}_{f^{\alpha}}\big[L_{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{m}}\big(SG)]

(27)

\displaystyle\leq 2.3^{n}+2^{n}.3^{n}R_{2}\alpha_{\max}^{n}.

Further, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\lim}_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log(N_{\delta}(Gr(f^{\alpha})))}{-\log(\delta)}\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\lim}_{n\to{\infty}}\frac{\log(2.3^{n}+2^{nN}.3^{n}R_{2}\alpha_{\max}^{n})}{nN\log(2)}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\lim}_{n\to{\infty}}\frac{\log(2^{nN}.3^{n}R_{2}\alpha_{\max}^{n})+\log\bigg(1+\frac{2.3^{n}}{2^{nN}.3^{n}R_{2}\alpha_{\max}^{n}}\bigg)}{nN\log 2}.

Plugging the inequality
\displaystyle 2^{N}>2^{N\sigma}
with the aforementioned inequality (i.e.
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N\sigma}}<\alpha_{\max}<1)
), we get

\overline{\lim}_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log(N_{\delta}(Gr(f^{\alpha})))}{-\log(\delta)}\leq 1+\frac{\log(3\alpha_{\max})}{N\log 2}.

Hence, we complete the proof.
∎

Conclusion and future remarks

In this article, by providing sufficient conditions to the parameters, we can ensure that the perturbation function
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}
exhibits certain properties of the original function
\displaystyle f
. Furthermore, we have addressed the existence of a fractal piecewise harmonic function of the best one-sided approximant from below to
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle SG
. Under certain conditions, we have also computed the bounds for
\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{B}(Gr(f^{\alpha}))
. Moreover, we have presented interesting results distinguishing the fundamental properties of the polynomials defined on

Evaluating GPT-4’s Vision Capabilities on Brazilian University Admission Exams
Ramon Pires, Thales Sales Almeida, Hugo Abonizio, and Rodrigo Nogueira

Abstract
Recent advancements in language models have showcased human-comparable performance in academic entrance exams. However, existing studies often overlook questions that require the integration of visual comprehension, thus compromising the full spectrum and complexity inherent in real-world scenarios. To address this gap, we present a comprehensive framework to evaluate language models on entrance exams, which incorporates both textual and visual elements. We evaluate the two most recent editions of Exame Nacional do Ensino Médio (ENEM), the main standardized entrance examination adopted by Brazilian universities. Our study not only reaffirms the capabilities of GPT-4 as the state of the art for handling complex multidisciplinary questions, but also pioneers in offering a realistic assessment of multimodal language models on Portuguese examinations. One of the highlights is that text captions transcribing visual content outperform the direct use of images, suggesting that the vision model has room for improvement. Yet, despite improvements afforded by images or captions, mathematical questions remain a challenge for these state-of-the-art models. The code and data used on experiments are available at https://github.com/piresramon/gpt-4-enem .

III
Methodology

III-A

Datasets

We evaluate the LMs using two datasets: ENEM 2022 and ENEM 2023. These datasets encompass all multiple-choice questions from the last two editions
1
1

1

Except the annulled questions. Each edition had one question of Mathematics annulled.
. The datasets have been created to allow the evaluation of both textual-only and textual-visual language models.
To evaluate textual-only models, we incorporated into the datasets the textual descriptions of the images that appear in the questions’ statements from the orange ENEM exam booklet
2
2

2

A particular booklet that offers accessibility to people with visual impairments. The transcription of images, tables, and chemical equations are read by a reader during the exam.
.

The datasets were created through a multi-step process. After downloading the booklets and respective answer sheets, both in PDF format, we use the Adobe PDFServices API
[
23
]
to extract text, tables, and images. The extraction process is followed by subsequent parsing procedures, and thorough manual verification and fixings. The last step was taken to remove accessibility patterns (e.g., replacing
open quotation marks, sentence, close quotation marks
with
"sentence"
) and converting mathematical expressions from natural language to standardized symbolic form (e.g., replacing
v is equal to zero and a is different from zero
with
v
=
0 and a
\neq
0
).

Different from the well-known datasets of ENEM exams
[
20
,
9
]
, our datasets address several key differences. First and foremost, our datasets keeps all the questions, including the ones that necessitate image understanding, table interpretation, mathematical reasoning, or familiarity with chemical elements. We also did not exclude the five questions related to the foreign language (English or Spanish, chosen at the time of registration) and kept the English ones. This comprehensive approach enables a more rigorous and nuanced evaluation process of language models across diverse questions.

In addition, a distinctive characteristic of our datasets lies in the inclusion of textual descriptions for visual elements, such as images and complex chemical equations. This method enables the evaluation of both multimodal and textual language models on the entrance exams.

Furthermore, our datasets incorporate table contents in Markdown formatting. This standardized structure is easily understandable by the language models, and because of this, we ignore the reader descriptions related to tables.

GPT-3.5 Turbo

We use the most recent

III-B
2
GPT-4

We use the latest version of GPT-4,
gpt-4-0613

III-B
3
GPT-4 Turbo

For comparative analysis, we employed the most recent
gpt-4-1106-preview
, trained on data up to April 2023, that can accommodate contexts with 128,000 tokens. This preview model is not currently recommended for production traffic.

III-B
4
GPT-4 Turbo with Vision

We also extended the investigation to multimodal models, evaluating the latest version of GPT-4 with vision, the
gpt-4-vision-preview
. The model was trained on data up to April 2023, with support for contexts of 128,000 tokens. Similar to the GPT-4 Turbo, this preview model is not intended for production traffic at present. We will refer to this model as GPT-4V.

For all models, we used the following parameters: temperature of 0, which defaults to greedy decoding, maximum output of 512 tokens, frequency penalty of 0, and presence penalty of 0.

Since the last two models were trained with data up to April 2023 and the ENEM 2022 exam was applied on November 12-20 2022, we do not discard the possibility of data contamination. However, the results on the ENEM 2023 test are trustworthy, given that the models could not have access to the examination questions and answers during their training phase.

III-C

Experiments

We adopted the prompt design that performed best in
[
9
]
: 3-shot with Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
[
24
]
. Those prompts include three few-shot examples from different knowledge areas of ENEM 2022, and include the solutions in explanatory steps, decomposing complex problems into smaller ones before compiling the final response. The Appendix
A-A
presents the instruction, the few-shot examples and respective CoT explanations applied in the experiments.

We performed three experiments to analyze multiple-choice questions: without images, with images, and with captions. Each experiment is described hereafter.

III-C
1
Without Images

In this experiment, we analyzed multiple-choice questions excluding any visual elements. This approach involved disregarding all images that could be present in both the question statement (and even in the answer alternatives). The primary aim was to lower-bound the language models’ performance in the absence of visual contextual cues.

III-C
2
With Images

For this experiment, we aim to evaluate multimodal LMs by embedding the images directly into the question body. Unlike the previous experiment, the inclusion of visual elements aimed to test the language models’ ability to comprehend and process information from both textual and visual modalities.

III-C
3
With Captions

In this experiment, our focus was on assessing textual LMs by replacing images with textual descriptions containing all essential information necessary for understanding the question. The objective was to measure the language models’ proficiency in solving the exam when equipped with all the necessary information. This approach resembles LENS
[
25
]
, a modular approach that fed LMs with image captions generated by vision models. The main difference herein is that we used human-generated captions.

We evaluate the model in the three experiments: without images, with images, and with captions.
Both for ENEM 2022 and 2023, even without images, the GPT-4V model performed well in all the knowledge areas except in Mathematics (both reached 54.55%). The substantial improvement in Mathematics was expected given the model’s initial difficulty in the without-images scenario. This enhancement is particularly noteworthy because Mathematics comprises a higher proportion of questions demanding image understanding (43%).

Tables

Upon initial analysis of the results, intriguing patterns emerge. Firstly, even with the inclusion of captions, the GPT-3.5 Turbo had a performance decline in two areas for ENEM 2022 and one area for ENEM 2023, deviating from anticipated outcomes. Secondly, across all evaluated models, considerably superior results were achieved for ENEM 2023 compared to ENEM 2022, which raises the possibility that the most recent test had a lower level of difficulty.

Conversely, even though the GPT-4 Turbo was trained with more recent data (April 2023), it exhibited the smallest discrepancy in accuracy between ENEM 2023 (never seen during training) and ENEM 2022 (potentially seen during training). This observation challenges the contamination hypothesis, given that the model’s performance was inferior to that of GPT-4.

Nevertheless, as expected, all models had a substantial improvement after including captions. The most notable advancement is evident in GPT-4, which had a 7-point enhancement, reaching 90.50% with ENEM 2023, and surpassing the multimodal GPT-4V model.

V
Conclusion

This study extends the applicability of language models (LMs) by incorporating image understanding to address the diverse challenges posed by entrance exams.
Our evaluation reaffirms the GPT-4 as the state-of-the-art for tackling the ENEM, reinforcing findings from a previous study.
The multimodal GPT-4V LM exhibits notable performance across multiple experiments, but the superiority of the use of captions instead of images shows that there is room for improvement.
Through our analyses, we demonstrate the potential of LMs to contribute significantly to the educational landscape, which positions them as potential tools for enhancing educational assessments, offering perspectives on question difficulty prediction, generating new exam items, and ultimately helping the students in the preparation for admission exams

VI
Future Work

For future work, we intend to include the essay in the evaluation and ask experts to correct the generated text using the same ENEM criteria. This would provide a more complete benchmark.

Additionally, we would like to adopt the same evaluation criteria currently adopted for the ENEM: the Item Response Theory (IRT). Different from the Classical Test Theory (CTC), which computes the total sum of correct answers regardless of successful guesses, the IRT is a mathematical model developed to make the test correction process fairer and more accurate.
This will allow a fair comparison of performance between LMs and students, checking the position of the models on the same ruler that measures the student’s level of knowledge.

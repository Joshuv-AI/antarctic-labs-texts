Full Γ \Gamma -expansion of reversible Markov chains level two large deviations rate functionals
Claudio Landim, Ricardo Misturini, Federico Sau

Abstract
. Let Ξ n ⊂ ℝ d \Xi_{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , be a sequence of finite sets and consider a Ξ n \Xi_{n} -valued, irreducible, reversible, continuous-time Markov chain ( X t ( n ) : t ≥ 0 ) (X^{(n)}_{t}:t\geq 0) . Denote by 𝒫 ⁡ ( ℝ d ) {\mathscr{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}) the set of probability measures on ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} and by ℐ n : 𝒫 ( ℝ d ) → [ 0 , + ∞ ) {\mathscr{I}}_{n}\colon{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to[0,+\infty) the level two large deviations rate functional for X t ( n ) X^{(n)}_{t} as t → ∞ t\to\infty . We present a general method, based on tools used to prove the metastable behaviour of Markov chains, to derive a full expansion of ℐ n {\mathscr{I}}_{n} expressing it as ℐ n = ℐ ( 0 ) + ∑ 1 ≤ p ≤ 𝔮 ( 1 / θ n ( p ) ) ​ ℐ ( p ) {\mathscr{I}}_{n}={\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}\,+\,\sum_{1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}}(1/\theta^{(p)}_{n})\,{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)} , where ℐ ( p ) : 𝒫 ⁡ ( ℝ d ) → [ 0 , + ∞ ] {\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}\colon{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to[0,+\infty] represent rate functionals independent of n n and θ n ( p ) \theta^{(p)}_{n} sequences such that θ n ( 1 ) → ∞ \theta^{(1)}_{n}\to\infty , θ n ( p ) / θ n ( p + 1 ) → 0 \theta^{(p)}_{n}/\theta^{(p+1)}_{n}\to 0 for 1 ≤ p < 𝔮 1\leq p<{\mathfrak{q}} . The speed θ n ( p ) \theta^{(p)}_{n} corresponds to the time-scale at which the Markov chains X t ( n ) X^{(n)}_{t} exhibits a metastable behaviour, and the ℐ ( p − 1 ) {\mathscr{I}}^{(p-1)} zero-level sets to the metastable states. To illustrate the theory we apply the method to random walks in potential fields.

1.
Introduction

With the aim of rigorously describing the metastable behaviours
emerging in all sorts of natural phenomena, the research on
metastability of Markov processes registered a considerable
development over the past decades. See, e.g., the books
[
28
,
10
]
and the review article
[
20
]
and references therein
for recent accounts on the subject and the diverse approaches
developed. The common feature of all these mathematical theories rests
on the identification of two or more locally stable states, together
with a clear separation of scales: persistence in a state and
transitions from state to state may be observed over time spans of
different order. Furthermore, when the system comprises a complex
energetic landscape, several metastable states and scales, naturally
ordered, arise (cf.
[
6
]
and references therein).

The goal of this article is that of developing an analytical framework
to describe the hierarchical metastability structure in the context of
continuous-time Markov chains
X_{t}^{(n)}
on a finite state space

\Xi_{n}
of growing size, satisfying natural assumptions. In the spirit
of the recent works
[
12
,
6
,
21
]
, we express the
metastable structure of
X_{t}^{(n)}
through an expansion, in the limit

n\to\infty
, of the corresponding level two large deviations rate
functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

[
11
]
, which describes the deviations of
the empirical measure of
X_{t}^{(n)}
as
t\to\infty
(see
(
2.2
)–(
2.3
)). This expansion is informally represented as

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\;=\;{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}\;+\;\sum_{p=1}^{{\mathfrak{q}}}\frac{1}{\theta^{(p)}_{n}}\,{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}\;,

(1.1)

where
\theta_{n}^{(p)}\in(0,\infty)
are diverging constants referred to as
speeds
, and
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}
are functionals.
Since the topology employed to perform (
1.1
) is that induced by the notion of
\Gamma
-convergence, we refer to (
1.1
) as a
full
\Gamma
-expansion of the functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
(Definition
2.1
).

Our main results, Theorems
2.7
and
2.11
, establish that the speeds
\theta^{(p)}_{n}
appearing in (
1.1
) correspond to the time-scales at which the chain exhibits a
metastable behaviour. Furthermore, the functionals
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}

coincide with the level two large deviations rate functionals of the
reduced Markov chain, which describes the evolution of the process

X^{(n)}_{t}
among the wells in the time-scale
\theta^{(p)}_{n}
. In
particular, the
0
-level sets of the functionals
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}
is
formed by convex combinations of the metastable states of the Markov
chain in the time-scale
\theta^{(p+1)}_{n}
.

Our original motivating example, discussed in detail in Section
6
, is that of random walks in potential fields evolving on the discrete
d
-dimensional torus of mesh size
1/n
, whose metastable
behaviour has already been considered in
[
24
,
25
]
. However, instead of proving directly the full
\Gamma
-expansion for this specific example, we develop a more general method primarily based on two assumptions, namely, conditions (H0) and (H1) in Section
2.2
below. Roughly speaking, (H0) is what allows to start a metastability analysis, by defining a reduced model with a finite number of states, whereas (H1) on the convergence of mean rates (see (
2.10
)) for the trace process encodes the Markov behaviour of the reduced model. These two conditions lie at the core of the so-called martingale approach to metastability and have been verified for several metastable dynamics of interest, including zero-range processes and spin systems at low temperature; for more details, we refer to Remark
2.2
.
The other conditions (H2) – (H5) which we assume for our first main result Theorem
2.7
are either structural/natural (e.g., (H2) on the nested structure of the support of the metastable states, or (H3) on the finiteness of the number of scales) or technical (e.g., (H4b) on the size of the wells, or (H5) on fast relaxation within the well), to be checked in each example. Theorem
2.11
replaces part of (H4) with condition (H6), more natural in the setting of condensing particle systems.

The key analytical tool in our analysis is the
\Gamma
-convergence of rate functionals. More precisely, since we consider the underlying state space
\Xi_{n}
to be a discrete approximation of a limiting base space
\Xi
, we state our results in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence of non-negative functionals
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
on the space
{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)
of probability measures on
\Xi
.
For precise definitions and more details on the role of
\Gamma
-convergence in large deviations theory, we refer to the next section. Let us here emphasize that condition (H5), which is not present in
[
6
,
21
]
, is crucial in our proof of the
\Gamma
-liminf
inequality to infer some quantitative features for weakly converging sequences with finite energy.

As already mentioned, our results relate to those obtained in
[
12
,
6
,
21
]
. More specifically, the expansion in (
1.1
) has been proved in
[
12
]
for overdamped Langevin
dynamics in which the potential has a finite number of local minima
and each local minimum is separated from the others by a unique
optimal saddle point. In
[
6
,
21
]
, this expansion has been derived for finite
state Markov chains with a fixed state space using tools developed in
[
2
,
4
]
to prove the metastable behaviour of Markov chains.

We emphasize that, while our example of random walks in potential fields may be thought of as the discrete analogue of the model considered in
[
12
]
, our assumptions and strategy differ considerably from those in
[
12
]
. Indeed,
[
12
]
, which is based on semi-classical analysis techniques, strongly relies on the non-overlap of low-lying eigenvalues of the associated generator, a condition ensured by the assumption of having at most one minimum on each level. Our analysis based on capacity estimates does not prevent us to consider a setting with multiple wells at the same height.

Moreover, compared to the works
[
6
,
21
]
concerned with Markov chains on a fixed state space
\Xi_{n}=\Xi
, our setting of growing state space covers a much broader list of examples. As a drawback, our analysis and assumptions are more involved, compensating for the substantial simplification that, in case of
\Xi
finite, all topologies on
{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)
are equivalent. For instance,
[
6
]
merely requires the jump rates to satisfy a ratio assumption, a request which may be thought of as analogous to our condition (H1) (see
[
6
, Eqs. (2.4) & (2.10)]
). In particular,
[
6
]
requires none of our technical conditions.

Before presenting in full detail our setting, assumptions and main results in the next section, we conclude this introduction by mentioning that, whereas we work in a reversible setting, we expect our results to carry over to the non-reversible setting as well, via an approach similar to that in
[
21
]
and with an analogous set of assumptions. Along the same lines, we believe a similar analysis based on capacities to yield a full
\Gamma
-expansion for non-reversible diffusions in potential fields, extending the results in
[
12
]
obtained in the reversible context.

2.
Setting and main results

2.1.
Setting

Let
\Xi
be the closure of an open and bounded subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
or the
d
-dimensional torus. Denote by
\Xi_{n}
the discretization of

\Xi
:
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Xi_{n}=\Xi\cap(n^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
,
n\geq 1
,
where
n^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}=\{k/n:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\}
. The elements of

\Xi_{n}
are represented by the symbols
x
,
y
and
z
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X^{(n)}_{t}
,
t\geq 0
, be a
\Xi_{n}
-valued,
reversible, irreducible, continuous-time Markov chain. Denote the
jump rates by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}R_{n}\colon\Xi_{n}\times\Xi_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
,
and the generator by
{\mathscr{L}}_{n}
, so that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}({\mathscr{L}}_{n}f)(x)}\;=\;\sum_{y\in\Xi_{n}}R_{n}(x,y)\,\{\,f(y)\,-\,f(x)\,\}\;,\quad f\colon\Xi_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}\;.

(2.1)

The holding times are represented by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda_{n}(x)
and
the jump probabilities by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}p_{n}(x,y)
so that

R_{n}(x,y)=\lambda_{n}(x)\,p_{n}(x,y)
. Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\pi_{n}
be the
unique stationary state, assumed to satisfy the detailed-balance
conditions.

Large deviations

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{P}}(\Omega)
,
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the
set of probability measures on
\Omega
endowed with the weak
topology, and by
L^{(n)}_{t}
the empirical measure of the chain

X^{(n)}_{t}
defined as:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L^{(n)}_{t}}\;:=\;\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{X^{(n)}_{s}}\;ds\;,

(2.2)

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\delta_{x}
,
x\in\Xi
, represents the Dirac
measure concentrated at
x
. Thus,
L^{(n)}_{t}
is a random element of

{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi_{n})
and
L^{(n)}_{t}({\mathscr{A}})
,
{\mathscr{A}}\subset\Xi_{n}
, stands for the
proportion of time the process
X^{(n)}_{t}
stays at
{\mathscr{A}}
in the time
interval
[0,t]
.

As the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
is irreducible, by the ergodic
theorem, for any starting point
x\in\Xi_{n}
, as
t\to\infty
, the
empirical measure
L^{(n)}_{t}
converges in probability to the
stationary state
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\pi_{n}
.

Donsker and Varadhan
[
11
]
proved the associated large
deviations principle. More precisely, they showed that for any subset

A
of
{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi_{n})
,

\displaystyle-\,\inf_{\mu\in A^{o}}{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\;\liminf_{t\to\infty}\min_{x\in\Xi_{n}}\,\frac{1}{t}\,\log{\mathbf{P}}^{(n)}_{x}\big[\,L^{(n)}_{t}\in A\,\big]

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq\;\limsup_{t\to\infty}\max_{x\in\Xi_{n}}\,\frac{1}{t}\,\log{\mathbf{P}}^{(n)}_{x}\big[\,L^{(n)}_{t}\in A\,\big]\;\leq\;-\,\inf_{\mu\in\overline{A}}{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)\;.

In this formula,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbf{P}}^{(n)}_{\!x}
,

x\in\Xi_{n}
, stands for the distribution of the process
X^{(n)}_{t}

starting from
x
,
A^{o}
,
\overline{A}
represent the
interior, closure of
A
, respectively, and

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi_{n})\to[0,+\infty)
is the level two large
deviations rate functional given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)}\;:=\;\sup_{u}\,-\,\sum_{x\in\Xi_{n}}\frac{({\mathscr{L}}_{n}u)(x)}{u(x)}\;\mu(x)\;,

(2.4)

where the supremum is performed over all functions

u:\Xi_{n}\to(0,\infty)
. Since we assumed reversibility and

\pi_{n}(x)>0
for all
x\in\Xi_{n}
, by
[
11
, Theorem 5]
,

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)\;=\;\langle\,\sqrt{f_{n}}\,,\,(-\,{\mathscr{L}}_{n})\sqrt{f_{n}}\,\rangle_{\pi_{n}}\;,

(2.5)

for all measures
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi_{n})
, where

f_{n}(x)=\mu(x)/\pi_{n}(x)
.

\Gamma
-convergence

We investigate in this article the
\Gamma
-convergence of the action
functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
. We first recall its definition.

Fix a Polish space
{\mathcal{X}}
and a sequence
(U_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}})
of
functionals on
{\mathcal{X}}
,
U_{n}\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to[0,+\infty]
. The
sequence
U_{n}

\Gamma
-converges
to the functional

U\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to[0,+\infty]
if and only if the two following
conditions are met:

(i)

\Gamma
-liminf.
The functional
U
is a

\Gamma
-liminf for the sequence
U_{n}
: For each
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
and each
sequence
x_{n}\to x
, we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}U_{n}(x_{n})\;\geq\;U(x)\;.

(ii)

\Gamma
-limsup.
The functional
U
is a

\Gamma
-limsup for the sequence
U_{n}
: For each
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
there
exists a sequence
x_{n}\to x
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}(x_{n})\;\leq\;U(x)\;.

(2.6)

The role of the
\Gamma
-convergence of rate functionals in large
deviations theory has long been established
[
27
]
. In our context,
\Gamma
-convergence of the rate functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
to

{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
implies that, for every closed subset
F
and open subset
G
of

{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \limsup_{n\to\infty}\,\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{t}\,\max_{x\in\Xi_{n}}\,\log\,{\mathbf{P}}_{\!x}^{(n)}\Big[\,L_{t}^{(n)}\,\in\,F\,\Big]\;&\leq\;-\,\inf_{\mu\in F}{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}(\mu)\;,\\
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,\liminf_{t\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{t}\,\min_{x\in\Xi_{n}}\,\log\,{\mathbf{P}}^{(n)}_{\!x}\Big[\,L_{t}^{(n)}\,\in\,G\,\Big]\;&\geq\;-\,\inf_{\mu\in G}{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}(\mu)\;,\end{aligned}

where we recall that
L_{t}^{(n)}
are the empirical measures defined in (
2.2
).

\Gamma
-convergence was a key tool in a number of problems involving large deviations principles. Among these,
[
26
]
deals with a
large deviations principle for conservation laws as the viscosity and
the noise vanish simultaneously. Here, since the large deviations rate
functional vanishes at all weak solutions of the conservation law, the large deviations
have to be examined at a smaller speed in order to
distinguish entropic solutions from non-entropic ones. This last step requires tools from the
theory of
\Gamma
-convergence.

A related problem involving a double-limit procedure allows to recover the large
deviations rate functional of one-dimensional asymmetric exclusion
processes, previously obtained in
[
13
,
31
,
32
,
29
]
. In this setting, one first derives a large deviations principle for
one-dimensional weakly asymmetric exclusion processes, and then prove
the
\Gamma
-convergence of the rate functional as the diffusivity
vanishes (or as the asymmetry diverges). In a slightly
different context, the
\Gamma
-convergence of the rate functional for
a fast-slow dynamics of
N
interacting diffusions is proven in

[
1
]
.

The
\Gamma
-convergence of the large deviations rate functional

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
introduced in (
2.4
) has been examined recently in

[
5
,
9
]
in the context of interacting particles systems to
show that in the large deviations principle one can interchange the
limits
n\to\infty
and
t\to\infty
, where
1/n
represents the
interdistance between particles. A similar result has been obtained in

[
7
]
for diffusions in a potential field.

\Gamma
-expansion

In view of the previous results, assume that the functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to a functional denoted by
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
. If the

0
-level set of
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
is a singleton there is nothing to
add. Otherwise, it is natural to consider the
\Gamma
-convergence of

\theta^{(1)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
, for some sequence

\theta^{(1)}_{n}\to+\infty
.

By (
2.4
), the rate functional
\theta^{(1)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

corresponds to the level two large deviations rate functional of the
Markov chain induced by the generator
\theta^{(1)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{L}}_{n}
,
that is, to the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
speeded-up by

\theta^{(1)}_{n}
.

Assume that there exists a sequence
\theta^{(1)}_{n}\to+\infty
for
which
\theta^{(1)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to a functional
denoted by
{\mathscr{I}}^{(1)}
. Clearly,
{\mathscr{I}}^{(1)}(\mu)
is finite only
if
\mu
belongs to the
0
-level set of
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
. We say that
we obtained the correct speed
\theta^{(1)}_{n}
whenever

{\mathscr{I}}^{(1)}(\mu)
is finite if, and only if,
\mu
belongs to the

0
-level set of
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
. In other words,

{\mathscr{I}}^{(1)}(\mu)<\infty
if, and only if,
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}(\mu)=0
. If this is not the case, it means that there is an
intermediate scale
\theta^{\prime}_{n}
,
\theta^{\prime}_{n}\to\infty
,

\theta^{\prime}_{n}/\theta^{(1)}_{n}\to 0
, which has been missed.

If the
0
-level set of
{\mathscr{I}}^{(1)}
is a singleton we end the
analysis of
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
. Otherwise, we may iterate the procedure. We
summarize these considerations in the next definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Consider a functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)\to[0,+\infty)
. A

full
\Gamma
-expansion of
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
is given by the speeds

(\theta^{(p)}_{n},n\geq 1)
,
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
, and the functionals

{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)\to[0,+\infty]
,
0\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
, if:

(a)

The speeds
\theta^{(1)}_{n},\dots,\theta^{({\mathfrak{q}})}_{n}
are
sequences such that
\theta^{(1)}_{n}\to\infty
,

\theta^{(p)}_{n}/\theta^{(p+1)}_{n}\to 0
,
0\leq p<{\mathfrak{q}}
;

(b)

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
, and for
each
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
,
\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges
to
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}
;

(c)

For
0\leq p<{\mathfrak{q}}
,
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p+1)}(\mu)
is finite if,
and only if,
\mu
belongs to the
0
-level set of
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}
;

(d)

The
0
-level set of
{\mathscr{I}}^{({\mathfrak{q}})}
is a singleton.

In the next section, we present a set of conditions on the Markov chain

X^{(n)}_{t}
which guarantee that the rate functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
can be
expanded as in (
1.1
), as defined in Definition
2.1
.

2.2.
Hypotheses

We start extending the definition of
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
to
{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)
by setting
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)=+\infty
for
\mu\not\in{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi_{n})
. Similarly, elements of
{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi_{n})
are
considered as measures on
\Xi
.

Recall the notation introduced in the previous section. The starting
point of the analysis of the large deviations rate functional

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
is the assumption that
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to a
functional
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)\to[0,+\infty]
whose

0
-level set consists of convex combinations of a finite number of
Dirac measures:

(H0)

The sequence of functionals

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)\to[0,+\infty]

\Gamma
-converges to a
functional
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi)\to[0,+\infty]
. Moreover,
there exists a finite set

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{M}}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{1}}\}
such that

\{\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi):{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}(\mu)=0\}=\{\sum_{j\in S_{1}}\omega_{j}\,\delta_{x_{j}}:\omega\in{\mathscr{P}}(S_{1})\}
, where

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{1}=\{1,\dots,{\mathfrak{n}}_{1}\}
.

This hypothesis is analysed in Remark
2.8
.

Trace process

Fix a non-empty subset
W
of
\Xi_{n}
. Denote by
T^{W}_{n}(t)
the
total time the process
X^{(n)}_{t}
spends in
W
in the time-interval

[0,t]
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}T^{W}_{n}(t)}\;:=\;\int_{0}^{t}\,\chi_{{}_{W}}(X^{(n)}_{s})\;ds\;,

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\chi_{{}_{W}}
represents the indicator function of the
set
W
. Denote by
S^{W}_{n}(t)
the generalized inverse of
T^{W}_{n}(t)
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S^{W}_{n}(t)}\;=\;\sup\{\,s\geq 0\,:\,T^{W}_{n}(s)\leq t\,\}\;.

The trace of
X
on
W
, denoted by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(Y^{W}_{t}:t\geq 0)
, is defined by

Y^{W}_{t}\;=\;X^{(n)}_{S^{W}_{n}(t)}\;;\;\;\;t\geq 0\;.

(2.7)

By Propositions 6.1 and 6.3 in
[
2
]
, the trace process is an
irreducible,
W
-valued continuous-time Markov chain, obtained by
turning off the clock when the process
X^{(n)}_{t}
visits the set

W^{c}
, that is, by deleting all excursions to
W^{c}
. For this
reason, it is called the trace process of
X^{(n)}_{t}
on
W
.

Metastable structure

We next assume that the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
exhibits a metastable
behaviour at different time-scales. The formulation of this condition
requires some notation.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}D({\mathbb{R}}_{+},A)
,
A
a finite set, the space of
right-continuous functions
{\mathfrak{x}}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to A
with left-limits
endowed with the Skorohod topology and the associated Borel

\sigma
-algebra. Let
{\mathbf{P}}^{(n)}_{\!x}
,
x\in\Xi_{n}
, be the
probability measure on the path space
D({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\Xi_{n})
induced by
the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
starting from
x
. Expectation with
respect to
{\mathbf{P}}^{(n)}_{\!x}
is represented by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbf{E}}^{(n)}_{x}
.

Consider a partition

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathfrak{W}}=\{{\mathscr{W}}^{(1)}_{n},\dots,{\mathscr{W}}^{({\mathfrak{m}})}_{n},\Delta_{n}\}
of
\Xi_{n}
, and let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{W}}_{n}\,:=\,\cup_{j=1}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}{\mathscr{W}}^{(j)}_{n}
.
Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\{Y^{{\mathscr{W}}_{n}}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
the trace of

\{X^{(n)}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
on
{\mathscr{W}}_{n}
. By equation (2.5) in

[
20
]
, the jump rates of the trace process
Y^{{\mathscr{W}}_{n}}_{t}
,
represented by

R^{{\mathfrak{W}}}_{n}\colon{\mathscr{W}}_{n}\times{\mathscr{W}}_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, are given by

(H1)

Remark 2.10

The proof of the

Fix

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be disjoint subsets of

\Xi_{n}
,
n\geq 1
. Under assumption (

),

By definition,

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that condition
(H4b)

Assume that conditions

Lemma 4.6

For all

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that conditions
(H1), (H2), (H4b) and (H5)

\mu_{n}\in{\mathscr{P}}(\Xi_{n})

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu_{n})\;\geq\;{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}(\mu)\;.

Proof.

Consider a sequence
\mu_{n}\to\mu
. We may assume, without loss of
generality, that (
2.20
) holds. Fix a function

{\mathbf{h}}\colon S_{p}\to(0,\infty)

be given by

h_{n}=\sum_{j\in S_{p}}{\mathbf{h}}(j)\,\chi_{{\mathscr{V}}^{p,j}_{n}}
. Let

u_{n}\colon\Xi_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the solution of the Poisson equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathscr{L}}_{n}u_{n}\,=\,0\;,\quad\Xi_{n}\setminus{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}\;,\\
&u_{n}\,=\,h_{n}\;,\quad{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}\;.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

By the standard stochastic representation of the solution of the
Poisson equation
u_{n}(x)={\mathbf{E}}_{x}^{(n)}\big[\,h_{n}(X_{\tau_{n}}^{(n)})\,\big]
, with
\tau_{n}\geq 0
being the first hitting time of
{\mathscr{V}}_{n}^{(p)}
, it is clear that
u_{n}(x)\in(0,\infty)
for all

x\in\Xi_{n}
.

By definition of
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
,

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu_{n})\;\geq\;-\,\int_{\Xi_{n}}\frac{{\mathscr{L}}_{n}u_{n}}{u_{n}}\,d\mu_{n}\;.

Since
u_{n}
is harmonic on
\Xi_{n}\setminus{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}
and

u_{n}=h_{n}
on
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}

[
6
, Lemma A.1]
, the
right-hand side is equal to

-\,\int_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}}\frac{{\mathscr{L}}_{n}u_{n}}{u_{n}}\,d\mu_{n}\;=\;-\,\int_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}}\frac{{\mathscr{L}}_{n}u_{n}}{h_{n}}\,d\mu_{n}\;=\;-\,\int_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}}\frac{({\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}}{\mathscr{L}}_{n})\,h_{n}}{h_{n}}\,d\mu_{n}\;,

where
{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}}{\mathscr{L}}_{n}
has been introduced in
(
3.1
) and is the generator of the trace process

Y^{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{n}}_{t}
. Since
h_{n}
is constant on each set

{\mathscr{V}}^{p,j}_{n}
(and equal to
{\mathbf{h}}(j)
), the last integral is equal
to

-\,\sum_{j,k\in S_{p}}\frac{[\,{\mathbf{h}}(k)-{\mathbf{h}}(j)\,]}{{\mathbf{h}}(j)}\,\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{p,j}_{n}}\mu_{n}(x)\,R^{(p)}_{n}(x,{\mathscr{V}}^{p,k}_{n})\;.

By Proposition
4.4
, this expression multiplied by

converges to

-\,\sum_{j\in S_{p}}\omega_{j}\,\frac{1}{{\mathbf{h}}(j)}\,\sum_{k\in S_{p}}r^{(p)}(j,k)\,[\,{\mathbf{h}}(k)-{\mathbf{h}}(j)\,]\;=\;-\,\sum_{j\in S_{p}}\omega_{j}\,\frac{{\mathbb{L}}^{(p)}{\mathbf{h}}}{{\mathbf{h}}}\;.

Summarising, we proved that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu_{n})\;\geq\;\sup_{{\mathbf{h}}}\,-\,\sum_{j\in S_{p}}\omega_{j}\,\frac{{\mathbb{L}}^{(p)}{\mathbf{h}}}{{\mathbf{h}}}\;,

where the supremum is carried over all functions

{\mathbf{h}}:S_{p}\to(0,\infty)
. By (
2.17
), (
2.18
), the
right-hand side is precisely
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}(\mu)
, which completes the
proof of the
\Gamma
-liminf
.
∎

Lemma 5.4

6.
Random walks in potential fields

In this section, based on the theory developed in Section
2
,
we derive the full
\Gamma
-expansion of the large deviations rate
functional of a random walk in a potential field. Let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Xi={\mathbb{T}}^{d}
with
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
denote the vectors of the canonical
basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and fix a potential function

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}F\colon\Xi\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying the following
assumptions:

(F1)

F\in{\mathscr{C}}^{2,1}(\Xi)
, i.e.,

F\in{\mathscr{C}}^{2}(\Xi)
and all second-order partial derivatives of
F

are Lipschitz continuous.

(F2)

F
has finitely-many critical
points
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathcal{P}}:=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\}
.

(F3
)

All the eigenvalues of the Hessian of

F
at the critical points are non-zero.

(F4)

Let

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathcal{S}}}\subset{\mathcal{P}}
be the set of saddle points of
F
.
The Hessian of
F
at saddle points
z\in{\mathcal{S}}
has only one negative
eigenvalue.

(F5)

All saddle points
z\in{\mathcal{S}}
are at
the same height: there exists
h\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

F(z)={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}h}
for all
z\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

(F6)

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathcal{M}}=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{{\mathfrak{n}}}\}
be the set of
local minima of
F
. The set
\{x\in\Xi:F(x)\leq h\}
is connected
and it contains
{\mathcal{M}}
. Each connected component of

\{x\in\Xi:F(x)<h\}
contains a unique critical point of
F
(one of
the local minima). Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{C}}_{k}
the connected
component which contains
m_{k}
.

Remark 6.1
.

The asymptotic behaviour of the mean jump rates (
2.10
) for random walks in
potential fields stated in condition (H1) has been studied in

[
24
]
under condition (F5). It should be possible to remove
this condition, but this has not been done yet and there are serious
technical problems to compute some capacities without this assumption.

Let
\Xi_{n}
denote the discretisation of
\Xi
, and
\pi_{n}
the
probability measure on
\Xi_{n}
defined by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\pi_{n}(x)}\;:=\;\frac{1}{Z_{F,n}}\,e^{-nF(x)}\;,\quad x\in\Xi_{n}\;,

where
Z_{F,n}
is the partition function

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}Z_{F,n}:=\sum_{x\in\Xi_{n}}\exp\left(-nF(x)\right)
. Let

be the continuous-time Markov chain on

with
transition rates

given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}R_{n}(x,y)}\>:=\>\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{n}{2}\left(F(y)-F(x)\right)}&\text{if}\ |y-x|=1/n\\

the corresponding generator
{\mathscr{L}}_{n}
and level two large deviations

are then defined as in (
2.1
) and
(
2.4
), respectively. With these definitions, the Markov chain

).

]

Valleys.

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{1}}=\{1,\ldots,{\mathfrak{n}}\}

Lemma 6.3

\Gamma

Hence, plugging such a

\Gamma

It suffices to prove

Therefore, fix

, and

.
Further, it is not difficult to check that

\displaystyle\mu_{n}:=\frac{e^{-nV}}{\sum e^{-nV}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\mu=\delta_{x}\,.

for every

and

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mu_{n},\phi\rangle-\langle\delta_{x},\phi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\Xi\\

which, by the continuity of

vanishes taking first

, and then
\delta\to 0
. As for

, (

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\Xi_{n}}\mu_{n}(y)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\{e^{-\frac{n}{2}\left(F(y+\frac{e_{i}}{n})-F(y)\right)}+e^{-\frac{n}{2}\left(F(y-\frac{e_{i}}{n})-F(y)\right)}-2\right\}

Since

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu_{n})={\mathscr{G}}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{d}2\left\{\cosh\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}V(x)\right)-1\right\}={\mathscr{G}}(x)\,,

Ground state of the Gross-Pitaevskii equation with a harmonic potential in the energy-critical case
Dmitry E. Pelinovsky, Szymon Sobieszek

Abstract
. Ground state of the energy-critical Gross–Pitaevskii equation with a harmonic potential can be constructed variationally. It exists in a finite interval of the eigenvalue parameter. The supremum norm of the ground state vanishes at one end of this interval and diverges to infinity at the other end. We explore the shooting method in the limit of large norm to prove that the ground state is pointwise close to the Aubin–Talenti solution of the energy-critical wave equation in near field and to the confluent hypergeometric function in far field. The shooting method gives the precise dependence of the eigenvalue parameter versus the supremum norm.



1.
Introduction

We consider the stationary Gross–Pitaevskii equation with a harmonic potential,

(-\Delta+|x|^{2})u-|u|^{2p}u=\lambda u,

(1.1)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and
u\in\mathbb{R}
. Existence of its ground state (a positive and radially decreasing solution) has been addressed by using variational methods in the energy subcritical
[
11
,
19
]
and critical
[
25
,
26
]
cases, where the critical exponent is
p=\frac{2}{d-2}
if
d\geq 3
. Uniqueness of the ground state
was proven in the energy subcritical
[
16
,
17
]
and critical

[
28
]
cases.

Variational methods are not applicable in the energy supercritical case
p(d-2)>2
if
d\geq 3
for which a more efficient shooting method was developed in
our previous work
[
3
,
22
]
(see also
[
8
]
for study of the Schrödinger–Newton–Hooke model). The shooting method is based on the reformulation of the existence problem after the Emden–Fowler transformation
[
9
,
18
]
and construction of two analytic families
of solutions, one family gives a bounded solution near
r=0
with parameter

b:=u(0)\equiv\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
and the other family
gives a decaying solution as
r\to\infty
with parameter

c:=\lim_{r\to\infty}u(r)e^{\frac{1}{2}r^{2}}r^{\frac{d-\lambda}{2}}.

(1.2)

The shooting method gives robust results in the large-norm limit as
b\to\infty
. In an asymptotic region, where both solution families coexist, the matching condition gives a condition on
(c,\lambda)
as a function of
b

which determines the solution curve
\lambda=\lambda(b)
. The
c
-family of solutions is defined in a local neighborhood of the limiting singular solution constructed in
[
27
]
for
\lambda=\lambda_{\infty}\in(0,d)
such that
\lambda(b)\to\lambda_{\infty}
as
b\to\infty
along the solution curve. As follows from the shooting method
[
3
]
under some non-degeneracy assumptions, the convergence is oscillatory for
2+\frac{2}{p}<d<d_{*}(p)
and monotone for
d>d_{*}(p)
, where

d_{*}(p):=6+\frac{2}{p}+2\sqrt{4+\frac{2}{p}}.

The same shooting method was extended in
[
22
]
to compute the Morse index of the ground state in the monotone case from the Morse index of the limiting singular solution. It was recently proven in
[
23
]
by using comparison with the stationary Schrödinger equation solvable in terms of the confluent hypergeometric functions (see
[
1
]
for review) that the Morse index of the limiting singular solution is infinite
in the oscillatory case and is equal to one in the monotone case
for large values of
d>d_{*}(p)
.

Properties of the energy-supercritical Gross–Pitaevskii equation with a harmonic potential are very similar to those for the energy-supercritical nonlinear Schrödinger equation in a ball. See
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
for the developments in the shooting method,
[
21
]
for convergence to the limiting singular solution,
and
[
12
,
20
]
for computation of the Morse index.

The purpose of this work is to extend the shooting method to the energy-critical case and to obtain the asymptotic representation of
\lambda(b)
as
b\to\infty
. As far as we are aware, the shooting method has not been previously developed in the context of the energy-critical case, for which the variational approximations are more common.

For the shooting method in the energy-critical case with
d=2+\frac{2}{p}
, we introduce the same two analytic families of solutions,
the
b
-family is defined by parameter
b:=u(0)
and the
c
-family is defined by parameter
c
in the asymptotic behavior (
1.2
). Contrary to the energy-supercritical case, the
c
-family exists in a local neighborhood of a spatially decaying solution to the stationary Schrödinger equation

V^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{d-1}{r}V^{\prime}(r)-r^{2}V(r)+\lambda V(r)=0,

which is satisfied by
V(r)=ce^{-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}}\mathfrak{U}(r^{2};\alpha,\beta)
, where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary and
\mathfrak{U}(z;\alpha,\beta)
is the Tricomi function (see
[
1
]
) with

\alpha:=\frac{p+1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4},\qquad\beta:=1+\frac{1}{p}.

(1.3)

Furthermore, contrary to the energy-supercritical case, the
b
-family exists in a local neighborhood of the algebraic soliton

\displaystyle U_{b}(r)=\frac{b}{(1+\alpha_{p}b^{2p}r^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}},\qquad\alpha_{p}:=\frac{p^{2}}{4(1+p)},

(1.4)

where the parameter
b
has been introduced from the condition
b=U_{b}(0)
.
The algebraic soliton (also called the Aubin-Talenti solution
[
2
,
29
]
) satisfies the nonlinear wave equation
-\Delta U_{b}=U_{b}^{2p+1}
for every
b>0
. It has been used in many studies of the energy-critical wave equations in bounded domains as in the pioneering work
[
4
]
and in follow-up works
[
10
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
24
]
.
In the context of the stationary Gross–Pitaevskii equation (
1.1
), it was used in
[
25
]
in order to obtain the lower bound
on the dependence
\lambda(b)
from a variational method. Recently in
[
23
]
, the variational methods and the elliptic estimates were extended in order to get the upper bound on the dependence
\lambda(b)
. We will use the shooting method to justify the relevance of the algebraic soliton (
1.4
) for the asymptotic behavior of
\lambda(b)
as
b\to\infty
.

The following theorem presents outcomes of the shooting method, which is the main result of this work. We use the following notations:

•

\lambda(b)\sim\lambda_{0}(b)
denotes the asymptotic equivalence
in the sense
\lim\limits_{b\to\infty}\lambda_{0}(b)^{-1}\lambda(b)=1
,

•

\lambda(b)=\mathcal{O}(b^{q})
denotes the order of magnitude in the sense that

|\lambda(b)|\leq Cb^{q}
for some
C>0
and all sufficiently large
b
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
p=\frac{2}{d-2}\in(0,1)
for
d>4
and let
\lambda=\lambda(b)
be the solution curve for the ground state
u=u_{b}
of the stationary Gross–Pitaevskii equation (
1.1
) satisfying
u_{b}(0)=b
,
u_{b}^{\prime}(r)<0
for
r>0
, and
u_{b}(r)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
. Then,

\lambda(b)\sim C_{p}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle b^{-2(1-p)},&\quad\frac{1}{2}<p<1,\\
\displaystyle b^{-1}\log b,&\quad p=\frac{1}{2},\\
\displaystyle b^{-2p},&\quad 0<p<\frac{1}{2},\\
\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{\rm as}\quad b\to\infty,

(1.5)

with

C_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)}{(1+p)\Gamma\left(\frac{p-1}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}+1\right)}\left[\frac{4(1+p)}{p^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}},&\quad\frac{1}{2}<p<1,\\
\displaystyle 144,&\quad p=\frac{1}{2},\\
\displaystyle\frac{8(1+p)^{2}}{p^{2}(1-2p)},&\quad 0<p<\frac{1}{2}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Moreover, for every
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
, there exist
B_{a},C_{a}>0

such that for every
b\geq B_{a}
, we have

\sup_{r\in[0,b^{-p(1-a)}]}b^{-1}\left|u_{b}(r)-U_{b}(r)\right|\leq C_{a}b^{-2p(1-a)},

(1.6)

\sup_{r\in[b^{-p(1-a)},1]}r^{\frac{2}{p}}\left|u_{b}(r)-c(b)e^{-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}}\mathfrak{U}(r^{2};\alpha,\beta)\right|\leq C_{a}|c(b)|^{2p+1}b^{2p(1-a)}

(1.7)

and

\sup_{r\in[1,\infty)}\left|e^{\frac{1}{2}r^{2}}r^{\frac{d-\lambda(b)}{2}}u_{b}(r)-c(b)r^{\frac{d-\lambda(b)}{2}}\mathfrak{U}(r^{2};\alpha,\beta)\right|\leq C_{a}|c(b)|^{2p+1},

(1.8)

where
c=c(b)\sim A_{p}b^{-1}
as
b\to\infty
for some
A_{p}>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

The asymptotic result (
1.5
) coincides with Theorem 1.1 in
[
23
]
obtained by the variational theory and elliptic estimates. It follows from Remark 1.2 in
[
23
]
that
there exists
C_{d}
such that

\lambda(\varepsilon)\sim C_{d}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon&\quad d=5,\\
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|&\quad d=6,\\
\varepsilon^{2}&\quad d\geq 7,\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

where
\varepsilon=b^{-p}
is defined from the algebraic soliton (
1.4
). The case
d\geq 7
corresponds to

0<p<\frac{1}{2}
as in (
1.5
). For
d=6
, we have
p=\frac{1}{2}
so that
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|\sim b^{-1}\log b
as in (
1.5
). For
d=5
, we have
p=\frac{2}{3}
so that
\varepsilon\sim b^{-2/3}=b^{-2(1-p)}
as in (
1.5
).

Remark 1.2
.

It also follows from Remark 1.2 in
[
23
]
that
\lambda(\varepsilon)\sim C|\log\varepsilon|^{-1}
for
d=4
and
\lambda(\varepsilon)-1\sim C\varepsilon
for
d=3
. In our notations with
\varepsilon=b^{-p}
, this would correspond to
\lambda(b)\sim C(\log b)^{-1}
for
p=1

and
\lambda(b)-1\sim Cb^{-2}
for
p=2
. However, we have found that the shooting method based on the
b
-family and the
c
-family can be applied for
p\in(0,1)
but needs some further modifications for
p\geq 1
.

Remark 1.3
.

Since
\mathfrak{U}(r^{2};\alpha,\beta)=\mathcal{O}(r^{-\frac{2}{p}})
as
r\to b^{-p(1-a)}
, bound (
1.7
) shows that
u_{b}(r)=\mathcal{O}(b^{1-2a})
as
r\to b^{-p(1-a)}
. This is smaller than
u_{b}(r)=\mathcal{O}(b)
as
r\to 0
in the bound (
1.6
). Since
\mathfrak{U}(r^{2};\alpha,\beta)=\mathcal{O}(r^{-\frac{d-\lambda(b)}{p}})
as
r\to\infty
, bound (
1.8
) shows that
u_{b}(r)
satisfies the asymptotic behavior (
1.2
) with
c=c(b)=\mathcal{O}(b^{-1})
as
b\to\infty
.

.

Our strategy to prove Theorem

is as follows.
Section
2

Section

), and (

2.
Preliminary results

As in our previous works
[
3
,
22
]
, we reformulate the existence problem for the ground state of the stationary Gross–Pitaevskii equation (
1.1
) as the following initial-value problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{d-1}{r}f^{\prime}(r)-r^{2}f(r)+\lambda f(r)+|f(r)|^{2p}f(r)=0,&\quad r>0,\\
f(0)=b,\quad f^{\prime}(0)=0,&\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

where
b>0
is the free parameter and
d=2+\frac{2}{p}
is defined in terms of
p>0
in the energy-critical case. We say that the solution
of the initial-value problem (
2.1
) is a ground state
if
f^{\prime}(r)<0
for
r>0
and
f(r)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.
Similarly to Lemmas 3.2 and 3.4 in
[
3
]
obtained
in the particular case
p=1
, the existence of a unique classical
solution to the initial-value problem (
2.1
) can be concluded
by using the integral equation formulation and the Lyapunov function method.
We skip the proof since it is standard and state that for every
p>0
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and
b>0
, there exists a unique classical solution
f\in C^{2}(0,\infty)
to the initial-value problem (
2.1
) satisfying the asymptotic behavior:

f(r)=b-\frac{p(\lambda+b^{2p})}{4(p+1)}br^{2}+\mathcal{O}(r^{4}),\quad\text{as }\;\;r\to 0.

(2.2)

The singularity of the stationary equation (
2.1
) at
r=0
is unfolded by introducing the Emden-Fowler transformation:

r=e^{t},\quad\Psi(t)=e^{\frac{t}{p}}f(e^{t}).

(2.3)

After the transformation of variables,
\Psi
satisfies the second-order nonautonomous equation

\Psi^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\Psi(t)+|\Psi(t)|^{2p}\Psi(t)=-\lambda e^{2t}\Psi(t)+e^{4t}\Psi(t).

(2.4)

We say that the
b
-family of solutions to Eq. (
2.4
) is defined
by applying the transformation (
2.3
) to the unique solution of the initial-value problem (
2.1
). The corresponding
b
-solution, denoted as
\Psi_{b}(t)
, satisfies the asymptotic behaviour

\Psi_{b}(t)=be^{\frac{t}{p}}\left[1-\frac{p(\lambda+b^{2p})}{4(p+1)}e^{2t}+\mathcal{O}(e^{4t})\right],\quad\text{ as }\;\;t\to-\infty,

(2.5)

which follows from (
2.2
). Thus, the
b
-family of solutions decays to zero as
t\to-\infty
. We will show in Section
3
that the
b
-family stays close to the positive homoclinic orbit of the truncated version of Eq. (
2.4
) given by the second-order autonomous equation

\Theta^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\Theta(t)+|\Theta(t)|^{2p}\Theta(t)=0.

(2.6)

The second-order equation (
2.6
) is integrable with the first-order invariant

\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{1}{2p^{2}}\Theta^{2}+\frac{1}{2(p+1)}\Theta^{2(p+1)}=E,

(2.7)

where
E
is constant along the classical solutions of Eq. (
2.6
). The origin in the
(\Theta,\Theta^{\prime})
-plane is a saddle point. The unique (up to translation) positive homoclinic orbit exists at the energy level
E=0
for every
p>0
. The homoclinic orbit can be found explicitly in the form

\Theta_{h}(t+t_{0})=\frac{e^{\frac{t+t_{0}}{p}}}{(1+\alpha_{p}e^{2(t+t_{0})})^{\frac{1}{p}}},\qquad\alpha_{p}:=\frac{p^{2}}{4\left(1+p\right)},

(2.8)

where
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is an arbitrary parameter of translation. Since

\Theta_{h}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{\frac{t}{p}}[1+\mathcal{O}(e^{2t})]&\text{ as }\;\;t\to-\infty,\\
\alpha_{p}^{-\frac{1}{p}}e^{-\frac{t}{p}}[1+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2t})]&\text{ as }\;\;t\to+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

it follows by comparison with (
2.5
) that

\Psi_{b}(t)\sim b\Theta_{h}(t)
as
t\to-\infty
for which
the translation parameter
t_{0}
in (
2.8
) is uniquely selected
as
t_{0}=p\log b
. With this choice for
t_{0}
,
we observe that
\Theta_{h}(t+p\log b)
after transformation (
2.3
) coincides with the algebraic soliton
U_{b}(r)
given by (
1.4
).

Next, we introduce another analytical family of solutions to Eq. (
2.4
) that decay to zero as
t\to+\infty
, which we call
the
c
-family and denote as
\Psi_{c}(t)
. We show in Section
4

that the
c
-family stays close to the decaying solutions
of the linearized version of Eq. (
2.4
) given by the linear
second-order nonautonomous equation

\Upsilon^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\Upsilon(t)+\lambda e^{2t}\Upsilon(t)-e^{4t}\Upsilon(t)=0.

(2.10)

By using the change of variables

z=e^{2t},\quad\Upsilon(t)=z^{\frac{1}{2p}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}z}u(z),

(2.11)

the second-order equation (
2.10
) becomes the confluent hypergeometric equation (also known as the Kummer equation):

zu^{\prime\prime}(z)+\left(\beta-z\right)u^{\prime}(z)-\alpha u(z)=0,

(2.12)

with parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
given by (
1.3
).
Two special solutions of the Kummer equation (
2.12
)
are given by the Kummer function
\mathfrak{M}(z;\alpha,\beta)
and the Tricomi function

\mathfrak{U}(z;\alpha,\beta)
, which are defined as follows
[
1
]
. The Kummer function is defined by the power series

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(z;\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{k}}{(\beta)_{k}}\;\frac{z^{k}}{k!}

(2.13)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{z}{1!}+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{\beta(\beta+1)}\frac{z^{2}}{2!}+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)}{\beta(\beta+1)(\beta+2)}\frac{z^{3}}{3!}+\ldots,

hence it is bounded as
z\to 0
. The Tricomi function satisfies
the asymptotic behavior

\mathfrak{U}(z;\alpha,\beta)\sim z^{-\alpha}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1})\right]\quad\mbox{\rm as}\;\;z\to+\infty,

(2.14)

hence it is decaying as
z\to+\infty
if
\alpha>0
. In fact, for every
\lambda\in(-\infty,d)
, we have

\alpha=\frac{p+1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}>\frac{p+1}{2p}-\frac{d}{4}=\frac{p+1}{2p}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}=0,

(2.15)

so that
\alpha>0
is satisfied in the energy-critical case.
By
[
1
, 13.1.3]
, the Tricomi function can be represented in the superposition form

\mathfrak{U}(z;\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi\beta}\left[\frac{\mathfrak{M}(z;\alpha,\beta)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha-\beta)\Gamma(\beta)}-z^{1-\beta}\frac{\mathfrak{M}(z;1+\alpha-\beta,2-\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(2-\beta)}\right],

(2.16)

which is true for
\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}
but can also be used in the limit

\beta\to\mathbb{Z}
. By using the identity

\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi z}=\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z),\quad z\notin\mathbb{Z}.

(2.17)

we can rewrite (
2.16
) for
\beta\notin\mathbb{N}
as

\mathfrak{U}(z;\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\Gamma(1-\beta)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha-\beta)}\mathfrak{M}(z;\alpha,\beta)+z^{1-\beta}\frac{\Gamma(\beta-1)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathfrak{M}(z;1+\alpha-\beta,2-\beta).

(2.18)

By
[
1
, 13.1.6]
, if
\beta=n+1\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}(z;\alpha,n+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n!\Gamma(\alpha-n)}(\mathfrak{M}(z;\alpha,n+1)\log z

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{k}}{(n+1)_{k}}\;\frac{z^{k}}{k!}[\psi(\alpha+k)-\psi(1+k)-\psi(1+n+k)])

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(k-1)!(1-\alpha+k)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}z^{-k},

(2.19)

where
\psi(z)=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
.

By means of the transformation (
2.11
), Tricomi function
determines a suitable solution of the linear equation (
2.10
):

\Upsilon_{h}(t)=e^{\frac{t}{p}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}e^{2t}}\mathfrak{U}(e^{2t};\alpha,\beta).

(2.20)

This solution is considered to be the leading-order approximation of the
c
-family such that

\Psi_{c}(t)\sim c\Upsilon_{h}(t)
as
t\to+\infty
satisfies the asymptotic behavior

\Psi_{c}(t)\sim ce^{-\frac{(2-\lambda)t}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}e^{2t}}\quad\mbox{\rm as}\;\;t\to+\infty.

(2.21)

The ground state of Theorem
1.1

is the connection of the unique solution of the initial-value problem (
2.1
) satisfying (
2.2
) with the unique
solution satisfying the decay behavior

f(r)\sim cr^{-(1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\lambda}{2})}e^{-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}}\quad\mbox{\rm as}\;\;r\to\infty,

(2.22)

which follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.21
).
The connection between (
2.2
) and (
2.22
) only exists for some specific values of
c=c(b)
and
\lambda=\lambda(b)
. Thus, the main question is to find and to justify the analytical expressions
for
c(b)
and
\lambda(b)
in the asymptotic limit
b\to\infty
.

3.
Persistence of the
b
-family of solutions

The
b
-family of solutions
\Psi_{b}
of the differential equation (
2.4
) satisfying (
2.5
) is considered in
a neighborhood of the homoclinic orbit
\Theta_{h}
of the differential
equation (
2.6
) satisfying (
2.9
).
Since the comparison gives
\Psi_{b}(t)\sim b\Theta_{h}(t)\sim\Theta_{h}(t+p\log b)
as
t\to-\infty
, we translate
\Psi_{b}(t)
by
-p\log b
and introduce the perturbation term

\gamma(t):=\Psi_{b}(t-p\log b)-\Theta_{h}(t),

which satisfies

\mathcal{L}\gamma=f_{b}(\Theta_{h}+\gamma)-N(\Theta_{h},\gamma),

(3.1)

where
f_{b}(t):=-\lambda b^{-2p}e^{2t}+b^{-4p}e^{4t}
,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}\gamma)(t):=\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\gamma(t)+(2p+1)|\Theta_{h}(t)|^{2p}\gamma(t),

and

\displaystyle N(\Theta_{h},\gamma):=|\Theta_{h}+\gamma|^{2p}(\Theta_{h}+\gamma)-|\Theta_{h}|^{2p}\Theta_{h}-(2p+1)|\Theta_{h}|^{2p}\gamma.

Remark 3.1
.

Since
\Theta_{h}(t)
is positive for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

and
\Theta_{h}(t)+\gamma(t)
is shown to be positive in the region of
t
where
we analyze persistence of the
b
-family of solutions, we can neglect
writing modulus signs in
\mathcal{L}\gamma
and
N(\Theta_{h},\gamma)
.

The nonlinear term
N(\Theta_{h},\gamma)
is superlinear in
\gamma
if
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and quadratic if
p\geq\frac{1}{2}
, according to the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix
p>0
and
a>0
. If
F:[-a,a]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

F(x):=(a+x)^{2p+1}-a^{2p+1}-(2p+1)a^{2p}x,

then there exists a positive constant
C>0
, such that for all
x\in[-a,a]

|F(x)|\leq\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C|x|^{2p+1},&\text{if }p\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),\\
C|x|^{2},&\text{if }p\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty).\end{array}

(3.2)

Proof.

Without the loss of generality, we assume that
a=1

due to the scaling transformation:

F(x)=a^{2p+1}\left[\left(1+\frac{x}{a}\right)^{2p+1}-1-\left(2p+1\right)\frac{x}{a}\right].

Note that
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=2p(2p+1)(1+x)^{2p-1}
, so that if
2p-1\geq 0
, then
F^{\prime\prime}
is bounded on
[-1,1]
, and the second line of (
3.2
) follows from Taylor’s theorem. If
2p-1<0
, then
F^{\prime\prime}
is bounded on
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
so that

|F(x)|\leq C|x|^{2}\leq C|x|^{2p+1},\quad x\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right],

for some positive constant
C
. For
|x|\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, we have
\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2p+1}\leq|x|^{2p+1}
so that

|F(x)|=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2p+1}2^{2p+1}|F(x)|\leq C|x|^{2p+1},\quad|x|\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right],

for another positive constant
C
. The two estimates above give the second line of (
3.2
).
∎

The homogeneous equation
\mathcal{L}\gamma=0
admits two linearly independent solutions. The first one is given by
\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)
due to
the translation symmetry of the autonomous equation (
2.6
). The other solution denoted by
\Sigma(t)
can be obtained from the Wronskian relation

\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Sigma^{\prime}(t)-\Theta_{h}^{\prime\prime}(t)\Sigma(t)=\Sigma_{0},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

(3.3)

where
\Sigma_{0}\neq 0
is constant. We take
\Sigma_{0}=1
for normalizing
\Sigma(t)
. Using
(
2.9
) in (
3.3
), we obtain that

\Sigma(t)=-\frac{p^{2}}{2}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{-\frac{t}{p}}[1+\mathcal{O}(e^{2t})]&\text{ as }\;\;t\to-\infty,\\
\alpha_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}e^{\frac{t}{p}}[1+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2t})]&\text{ as }\;\;t\to+\infty.\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Using the two linearly independent solutions
\Theta_{h}^{\prime}
and
\Sigma
of the homogeneous equation
\mathcal{L}\gamma=0
, we rewrite (
3.1
) as an integral equation for
\gamma
:

\gamma(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\Sigma(t)-\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Sigma(t^{\prime})\right)\left[f_{b}(t^{\prime})\left(\Theta_{h}(t^{\prime})+\gamma(t^{\prime})\right)-N\left(\Theta_{h}(t^{\prime}),\gamma(t^{\prime})\right)\right]dt^{\prime},

(3.5)

where the free solution
c_{1}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)+c_{2}\Sigma(t)
has been set to zero from the requirement that
\gamma(t)
decays to zero as
t\to-\infty
faster than
\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)
.

The perturbation term
\gamma
can be estimated to be small in the
L^{\infty}
norm on the semi-infinite interval
(-\infty,T+ap\log b]
with fixed
T>0
and
a>0
, where the right end point diverges asymptotically to
+\infty
as
b\to\infty
. The following lemma gives the persistence result
for the solution
\Psi_{b}(t-p\log b)
to stay close to the leading-order
term
\Theta_{h}(t)
for
t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
p\in(0,1]
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. For any fixed
T>0
and
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
there exist
b_{T,a}>0
and
C_{T,a}>0
such that the unique solution
\Psi_{b}(t)
to the second-order equation (
2.4
) with asymptotic behaviour (
2.5
) satisfies for
t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b]
and all
b\geq b_{T,a}
:

|\Psi_{b}(t-p\log b)-\Theta_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}b^{-2p(1-a)}e^{\frac{t}{p}},

(3.6)

where the bound can be differentiated in
t
.

Remark 3.2
.

The remainder term in the bound (
3.6
) is small
on
[ap\log b,T+ap\log b]
for every
a\in(0,\frac{2p}{1+2p})
for which
b^{-2p+a(2p+1)}\to 0
as
b\to\infty
. However,
\Theta_{h}(t)=\mathcal{O}(b^{-a})
on the same interval so that the remainder term
\gamma(t)
is smaller than the leading-order term
\Theta_{h}(t)
for
t\in[ap\log b,T+ap\log b]
if
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
for which
b^{-2p+a(2p+1)}\ll b^{-a}
for sufficiently large
b
.

Proof.

In order to eliminate the divergence of the integral kernel in the integral equation (
3.5
) as
t\to-\infty
, we introduce a change of variables:
\tilde{\Theta}_{h}(t):=e^{-\frac{t}{p}}\Theta_{h}(t)
and
\tilde{\gamma}(t):=e^{-\frac{t}{p}}\gamma(t)
. The new integral equation for
\tilde{\gamma}
can be considered as the fixed-point equation
\tilde{\gamma}=A\tilde{\gamma}
, where

(A\tilde{\gamma})(t):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}K(t,t^{\prime})\left[f_{b}(t^{\prime})(\tilde{\Theta}_{h}(t^{\prime})+\tilde{\gamma}(t^{\prime}))-e^{2t^{\prime}}N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h}(t^{\prime}),\tilde{\gamma}(t^{\prime}))\right]dt^{\prime},

(3.7)

where the integral kernel is defined as

K(t,t^{\prime}):=e^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{p}}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})e^{-\frac{t}{p}}\Sigma(t)-e^{-\frac{t}{p}}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)e^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{p}}\Sigma(t^{\prime}),\quad t^{\prime}\leq t.

It follows from the asymptotic behaviours (
2.9
) and (
3.4
) for
\Theta_{h}
and
\Sigma
that
the integral kernel is bounded for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
by

|K(t,t^{\prime})|\leq C\left(1+e^{-\frac{2}{p}(t-t^{\prime})}\right),\quad t^{\prime}\leq t,

for some positive constant
C
. Thus, as the integration in (
3.7
) is done in
t^{\prime}
from
-\infty
to
t
, the kernel
K(t,t^{\prime})
is bounded. In addition, the lower bound on
\tilde{\Theta}_{h}
follows from (
2.9
):

\tilde{\Theta}_{h}(t)\geq C_{T,a}b^{-2a},\quad t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b),

for some positive constant
C_{T,a}
that depends on
T
and
a
for all large
b
. We shall prove that the integral operator
A
is a contraction in a small closed ball in the Banach space
L^{\infty}(-\infty,T+ap\log b)

equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.

Case
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
Since
\tilde{\Theta}_{h}(t)
is bounded from below for
t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b)
, it follows by Proposition
3.1
if
\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\ll b^{-2a}
for all large
b
, then

\|N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma})\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2p+1},

(3.8)

for some positive constant
C
. We use

f_{b}(t):=b^{-2p}e^{2t}[-\lambda+b^{-2p}e^{2t}]
and estimate

\displaystyle\|A\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\left(1+\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}|f_{b}(t^{\prime})|dt^{\prime}+\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2p+1}\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{2t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\left(1+\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\right)b^{-2p(1-a)}+b^{2ap}\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2p+1}\right],

where the positive constant
C
can change from one line to the other line. If
\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq 2Cb^{-2p(1-a)}
, then

\displaystyle\|A\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[b^{-2p(1-a)}+2Cb^{-4p(1-a)}+(2C)^{2p+1}b^{-2p(1-2a(p+1)+2p)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2Cb^{-2p(1-a)},

where we have used
2p(1-a)<2p(1-2a(p+1)+2p)
if
a\in(0,\frac{2p}{1+2p})
.
Since
2a<2p(1-a)
if
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
with
\frac{p}{1+p}<\frac{2p}{1+2p}
, the bound
\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq 2Cb^{-2p(1-a)}

ensures validity of the bound
\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\ll b^{-2a}
for which the bound (
3.8
) can be used. Similar calculations show that for two functions
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}
in the same small closed ball in
L^{\infty}(-\infty,T+ap\log b)
, we have

\|A\tilde{\gamma}-A\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq Cb^{-2p(1-a)}\|\tilde{\gamma}-\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\|_{\infty},

so that
A
is a contraction for sufficiently large
b
. By the Banach fixed-point theorem, there exists a unique fixed point
\tilde{\gamma}
of
A
such that

\sup_{t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b)}|\tilde{\gamma}(t)|\leq 2Cb^{-2p(1-a)}.

Since
\gamma(t)=e^{\frac{t}{p}}\tilde{\gamma}(t)
, we obtain the bound (
3.6
) for the unique solution
\gamma(t)
to the integral equation (
3.5
).

Case
p\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
.
The only difference in the proof is that, by Proposition
3.1
, the bound (
3.8
) is replaced by the bound

\|N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma})\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2},

(3.9)

if
\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\ll b^{-2a}
for all large
b
. In this case, we get the estimate

\displaystyle\|A\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\left(1+\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\right)b^{-2p(1-a)}+b^{2ap}\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\leq C\left[b^{-2p(1-a)}+2Cb^{-4p(1-a)}+(2C)^{2}b^{-2p(2-3a)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2Cb^{-2p(1-a)},

where we have used
2p(1-a)<2p(2-3a)
if
a\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
Since
2a<2p(1-a)
if
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
with
\frac{p}{1+p}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, the bound
\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq 2Cb^{-2p(1-a)}

ensures validity of the bound
\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\ll b^{-2a}
for which the bound (
3.9
) can be used. The rest of the proof is verbatim to the case of
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The bound (
3.6
) can be extended for every
p\geq 1
if the values of
a
are restricted to
a\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
as follows
from the proof of Lemma
3.1
in the case of
p\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
.

The result of Lemma
3.1
allows us to justify the validity
of

\Psi_{b}(t-p\log b)\sim\Theta_{h}(t)\sim\alpha_{p}^{-\frac{1}{p}}e^{-\frac{t}{p}},\quad t\in[ap\log b,T+ap\log b]

due to (
2.9
) and (
3.6
). In order to obtain
the correction term which behaves like
e^{\frac{t}{p}}
in the same asymptotic region, we need to analyze
\gamma
in more details
and obtain the leading-order part of
\gamma
. To do so, we write

\gamma=\gamma_{h}+\delta
, where the leading-order term
\gamma_{h}
satisfies
\mathcal{L}\gamma_{h}=f_{b}\Theta_{h}
and is given explicitly by

\gamma_{h}(t)=\Sigma(t)\int_{-\infty}^{t}f_{b}(t^{\prime})\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\Theta_{h}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}-\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\int_{-\infty}^{t}f_{b}(t^{\prime})\Sigma(t^{\prime})\Theta_{h}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(3.10)

whereas the correction term
\delta
satisfies

\mathcal{L}\delta=f_{b}(\gamma_{h}+\delta)-N(\Theta_{h},\gamma_{h}+\delta).

(3.11)

The following lemma gives a sharper bound on
\gamma_{h}
compared to the bound (
3.6
). The sharper bound holds on
[ap\log b,T+ap\log b]
, where the asymptotic behavior of
\Theta_{h}(t)
and
\Sigma(t)
as
t\to+\infty
is relevant.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
p\in(0,1)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. For any fixed
T>0
and
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
there exist
b_{T,a}>0
and
C_{T,a}>0
such that

\gamma_{h}
in (
3.10
) satisfies for
t\in[ap\log b,T+ap\log b]
and all
b\geq b_{T,a}
:

•

if
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, then

|\gamma_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}\left[(|\lambda|b^{-2p(1-a)}+b^{-4p(1-a)})e^{-\frac{t}{p}}+(|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p})e^{\frac{t}{p}}\right],

(3.12)

•

if
p\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, then

|\gamma_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}\left[(|\lambda|b^{-2p(1-a)}+b^{-4p(1-a)})e^{-\frac{t}{p}}+(|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p(1-a)})e^{\frac{t}{p}}\right],

(3.13)

where the bounds can be differentiated in
t
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma(t)\Theta_{h}(t)
is bounded for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
independently of
b
, the second integral term in (
3.10
) is controlled by

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}f_{b}(t)\Sigma(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}\left(|\lambda|b^{-2p}e^{2t}+b^{-4p}e^{4t}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,a}(|\lambda|b^{-2p(1-a)}+b^{-4p(1-a)}).

This estimate yields the first term in the bounds (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) due to
the asymptotic behavior (
2.9
). On the other hand,
since
\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}=\mathcal{O}(e^{-\frac{2t}{p}})
as
t\to+\infty
,
the first integral term in (
3.10
) is controlled by

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}f_{b}(t)\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}\left(|\lambda|b^{-2p}e^{-\frac{2(1-p)}{p}t}+b^{-4p}e^{-\frac{2(1-2p)}{p}t}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,a}(|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p+2a\nu_{p}}),

where
\nu_{p}=0
for
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\nu_{p}=2p-1
for
p\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
. This yields the second term in the bounds (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) due to
the asymptotic behavior (
3.4
), where we have also used that

4p(1-a)<4p-2a(2p-1)
.
∎

The sharper bounds (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) are compatible with the bound
(
3.6
) on the semi-infinite interval
(-\infty,T+ap\log b]
, which can be rewritten in the form:

\displaystyle|\gamma_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}b^{-2p(1-a)}e^{\frac{t}{p}},\qquad t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b].

(3.14)

The correction term
\delta
is estimated to be smaller than
\gamma_{h}
according to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
p\in(0,1]
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. For any fixed
T>0
,
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
, there exist
b_{T,a}>0
and
C_{T,a}>0
such that for
t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b]
and all
b\geq b_{T,a}
:

•

if
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, then

|\Psi_{b}(t-p\log b)-\Theta_{h}(t)-\gamma_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}b^{-2p\left[(2p+1)(1-a)-a\right]}e^{\frac{t}{p}},

(3.15)

•

if
p\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, then

|\Psi_{b}(t-p\log b)-\Theta_{h}(t)-\gamma_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}b^{-2p(2-3a)}e^{\frac{t}{p}},

(3.16)

where the bounds can be differentiated in
t
.

Remark 3.4
.

If
a\in(0,\frac{2p}{1+2p})
for
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have

2p(1-a)<2p\left[(2p+1)(1-a)-a\right],

so that comparison (
3.14
) and (
3.15
)
shows that
\delta
is smaller than
\gamma_{h}

for sufficiently large
b
. Similarly, if
a\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
for
p\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

2p(1-a)<2p(2-3a),

so that the comparison of (
3.14
) and (
3.16
) shows that
\delta
is smaller than
\gamma_{h}

for sufficiently large
b
. In both cases, by Lemma
3.1
, we also have

\gamma_{h}
being smaller than
\Theta_{h}
if
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
, where
\frac{p}{1+p}\leq\min\{\frac{2p}{1+2p},\frac{1}{2}\}
if
p\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Equation (
3.11
) for
\delta
can be written similarly to (
3.5
) as the integral equation

\delta(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\Sigma(t)-\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Sigma(t^{\prime})\right)\left[f_{b}(t^{\prime})\left(\gamma_{h}(t^{\prime})+\delta(t^{\prime})\right)-N\left(\Theta_{h}(t^{\prime}),\gamma_{h}(t^{\prime})+\delta(t^{\prime})\right)\right]dt^{\prime}.

(3.17)

We proceed in a similar way to the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
Using the change of variables

\tilde{\Theta}_{h}(t):=e^{-\frac{t}{p}}\Theta_{h}(t),\quad\tilde{\gamma}_{h}(t):=e^{-\frac{t}{p}}\gamma_{h}(t),\quad\tilde{\delta}(t):=e^{-\frac{t}{p}}\delta(t),

we rewrite the integral equation for
\delta
as
the fixed-point equation
\tilde{\delta}=B\tilde{\delta}
,
where

(B\tilde{\delta})(t):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}K(t,t^{\prime})\left[f_{b}(t^{\prime})(\tilde{\gamma}_{h}(t^{\prime})+\tilde{\delta}(t^{\prime}))-e^{2t^{\prime}}N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h}(t^{\prime}),\tilde{\gamma}_{h}(t^{\prime})+\tilde{\delta}(t^{\prime}))\right]dt^{\prime}.

(3.18)

The only essential difference between
A
in (
3.7
) and
B
in (
3.18
) is the source term which dictates the size of the closed ball in
L^{\infty}(-\infty,T+ap\log b]
, where the fixed-point iterations are closed. In (
3.18
), it consists of the linear term
f_{b}\tilde{\gamma}_{h}
and the contribution from nonlinearity term
N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h})
. The linear term is estimated from (
3.14
) as

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}f_{b}(t)\tilde{\gamma}_{h}(t)dt\right|\leq C\|\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\|_{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}|f_{b}(t)|dt\leq Cb^{-4p(1-a)}.

(3.19)

Estimates for the nonlinear term depend on the value of
p
. To proceed with the estimates, we decompose

N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h}+\tilde{\delta})=N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h})+\tilde{N}(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h},\tilde{\delta}),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{N}(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h},\tilde{\delta})

\displaystyle:=N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h}+\tilde{\delta})-N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h})

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\Theta}_{h}+\tilde{\gamma}_{h}+\tilde{\delta})^{2p+1}-(\tilde{\Theta}_{h}+\tilde{\gamma}_{h})^{2p+1}-(2p+1)(\tilde{\Theta}_{h})^{2p}\tilde{\delta}.

Case
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
By Proposition
3.1
, we have

\|N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h})\|_{\infty}\leq C^{\prime}\|\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\|_{\infty}^{2p+1}.

(3.20)

Since
\tilde{N}|_{\delta=0}=0
and

\frac{\partial\tilde{N}}{\partial\tilde{\delta}}\bigg|_{\tilde{\delta}=0}=(2p+1)(\tilde{\Theta}_{h}+\tilde{\gamma}_{h})^{2p}-(2p+1)(\tilde{\Theta}_{h})^{2p},

we obtain by a minor modification of the proof of Proposition
3.1
that

\|\tilde{N}(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h},\tilde{\delta})\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\|_{\infty}^{2p}\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}.

(3.21)

Putting together estimates (
3.19
), (
3.20
), and (
3.21
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|B\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq C\left(b^{-4p(1-a)}+b^{-2p(1-a)}\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}+b^{-2p[(2p+1)(1-a)-a]}+b^{-2p\left[2p(1-a)-a\right]}\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}\right).

Since
4p(1-a)>2p[(2p+1)(1-a)-a]
for every
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have
b^{-4p(1-a)}\ll b^{-2p[(2p+1)(1-a)-a]}
for sufficiently large
b
, hence the source term coming from the nonlinearity
N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h})
is much larger than the source
term coming from
f_{b}\tilde{\gamma}_{h}
as
b\to\infty
.
As a result, if
\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq 2Cb^{-2p[2p(1-a)-a]}
,
then
\|B\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq 2Cb^{-2p[2p(1-a)-a]}
.
Moreover,
B
is a contraction in the same small closed ball in
L^{\infty}(-\infty,T+ap\log b)
for sufficiently large
b
. Hence, there exists a unique fixed point
\tilde{\delta}
of
B
satisfying

\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq 2Cb^{-2p[(2p+1)(1-a)-a]}
,
which yields (
3.15
) for
\delta(t)=e^{\frac{t}{p}}\tilde{\delta}(t)
.

Case
p\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
.
By Proposition
3.1
, we have

\|N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h})\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\|_{\infty}^{2},

and similarly,

\|\tilde{N}(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h},\tilde{\delta})\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\|_{\infty}\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}.

Proceeding similarly to the previous computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\|B\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq C^{\prime}\left(b^{-4p(1-a)}+b^{-2p(1-a)}\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}+b^{-2p(2-3a)}+b^{-2p(1-2a)}\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}\right).

Since
4p(1-a)>2p(2-3a)
, we have
b^{-4p(1-a)}\ll b^{-2p(2-3a)}
for sufficiently large
b
, hence again
the source term coming from the nonlinearity

N(\tilde{\Theta}_{h},\tilde{\gamma}_{h})
is much larger than the source
term coming from
f_{b}\tilde{\gamma}_{h}
as
b\to\infty
.
Proceeding similarly, for sufficiently large
b
, there exists a unique fixed point
\tilde{\delta}
of
B
satifying

\|\tilde{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq 2Cb^{-2p(2-3a)}
,
which yields (
3.16
) for
\delta(t)=e^{\frac{t}{p}}\tilde{\delta}(t)
.
∎

Similarly to Lemma
3.2
, we can find a sharper bound on
\delta
compared to the bounds (
3.15
) and (
3.16
).
This is given by the following lemma, the proof of which follows from
the estimates obtained in Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix
p\in(0,1)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. For any fixed
T>0
and
a\in(0,\frac{p}{1+p})
there exist
b_{T,a}>0
and
C_{T,a}>0
such that

\delta
in (
3.17
) satisfies for

and all

•

if

, then

|\delta(t)|\leq C_{T,a}(|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p})^{2p+1}b^{2ap}e^{\frac{t}{p}},

(3.22)

•

if

, then

|\gamma_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}(|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p(1-a)})^{2}b^{2ap}e^{\frac{t}{p}},

(3.23)

where the bounds can be differentiated in

.

Proof.

For

, the first term in the bound (
3.12
) with

is much smaller than the second term with

on

. As a result, it can be neglected. On the other hand, the second term in the bound (
3.12
) can be extended for the semi-infinite interval

such that
the sharper bound compared to (
3.6
) can be written in the form

|\gamma_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}(|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p})e^{\frac{t}{p}},\quad t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b].

The bound (
3.22
) follows from
analysis of the integral equation (
3.17
) by using the transformation to the tilde variables
in the proof of Lemma
3.3
and the estimate (
3.20
) on the nonlinear term
N
which is much larger than the source term from
f_{b}
.

For

, the proof is analogous but we use

|\gamma_{h}(t)|\leq C_{T,a}(|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p(1-a)})e^{\frac{t}{p}},\quad t\in(-\infty,T+ap\log b].

and the estimate (
3.21
) on the nonlinear term
N
which is still much larger than the source term from

.
∎

4.
Persistence of the
c
-family of solutions

The
c
-family of solutions
\Psi_{c}
of the differential equation (
2.4
) satisfying (
2.21
) is considered near the solution
\Upsilon_{h}
of the linear equation (
2.10
) given by (
2.20
). The comparison gives
\Psi_{c}(t)\sim c\Upsilon_{h}(t)
as
t\to+\infty
. The correction term
\eta(t):=\Psi_{c}(t)-c\Upsilon_{h}(t)
satisfies

\mathcal{M}\eta=-\left|c\Upsilon_{h}+\eta\right|^{2p}(c\Upsilon_{h}+\eta),

(4.1)

where

\left(\mathcal{M}\eta\right)(t):=\eta^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\eta(t)+\lambda e^{2t}\eta(t)-e^{4t}\eta(t).

The homogeneous equation
\mathcal{M}\eta=0
has two linearly independent solutions. One solution is
\Upsilon_{h}
given by (
2.20
). The other solution, denoted as
\Upsilon_{g}
, can be obtained from the normalized Wronskian relation

\Upsilon_{h}(t)\Upsilon_{g}^{\prime}(t)-\Upsilon_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Upsilon_{g}(t)=1.

(4.2)

Since it follows from (
2.21
) that

\Upsilon_{h}(t)\sim e^{-\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)t}e^{-\frac{1}{2}e^{2t}},\quad\text{ as }t\to+\infty,

(4.3)

integrating the Wronskian relation (
4.2
) yields

\Upsilon_{g}(t)\sim\frac{1}{2}e^{-\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)t}e^{\frac{1}{2}e^{2t}},\quad\text{ as }t\to+\infty.

(4.4)

With two linearly independent solutions
\Upsilon_{h}
and
\Upsilon_{g}
, we rewrite (
4.1
) as an integral equation for
\eta
:

\eta(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(\Upsilon_{h}(t^{\prime})\Upsilon_{g}(t)-\Upsilon_{h}(t)\Upsilon_{g}(t^{\prime})\right)|c\Upsilon_{h}(t^{\prime})+\eta(t^{\prime})|^{2p}(c\Upsilon_{h}(t^{\prime})+\eta(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime},

(4.5)

where the free solution
c_{1}\Upsilon_{h}+c_{2}\Upsilon_{g}
has been set to zero in order to guarantee that
\eta(t)
decays to zero as
t\to+\infty
faster than
\Upsilon_{h}(t)
.

The following lemma describes the size of
\eta(t)
for
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
\lambda\in(-\infty,2]
and
p>0
. Then, there exist some constants
C>0
and
c_{0}>0
, such that for
t\in[0,\infty)
and

c\in(-c_{0},c_{0})
, we have

|\Psi_{c}(t)-c\Upsilon_{h}(t)|\leq C|c|^{2p+1}e^{-(1-\frac{\lambda}{2})t}e^{-\frac{1}{2}e^{2t}},

(4.6)

where the bound can be differentiated term by term.

Proof.

In order to obtain a bounded kernel in the integral equation (
4.5
), we first introduce the change of variables

\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t):=e^{(1-\frac{\lambda}{2})t}e^{\frac{1}{2}e^{2t}}\Upsilon_{h}(t),\qquad\tilde{\eta}(t):=e^{(1-\frac{\lambda}{2})t}e^{\frac{1}{2}e^{2t}}\eta(t),

which applied to (
4.5
) results in the integral equation
\tilde{\eta}=E\tilde{\eta}
, where

(E\tilde{\eta})(t):=\int_{t}^{\infty}\hat{K}(t,t^{\prime})e^{-p(2-\lambda)t^{\prime}-pe^{2t^{\prime}}-2t^{\prime}}\hat{N}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t^{\prime}),\tilde{\eta}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime},

(4.7)

and where the kernel
\hat{K}
and the nonlinearity
\hat{N}
are given by

\hat{K}(t,t^{\prime}):=e^{-(1-\frac{\lambda}{2})(t^{\prime}-t)+2t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(e^{2t}-e^{2t^{\prime}})}(\Upsilon_{h}(t^{\prime})\Upsilon_{g}(t)-\Upsilon_{h}(t)\Upsilon_{g}(t^{\prime})),

and

\hat{N}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h},\tilde{\eta}):=|c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}+\tilde{\eta}|^{2p}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}+\tilde{\eta}).

Using asymptotic behaviours (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we get that there exists
C>0

such that

|\hat{K}(t,t^{\prime})|\leq C(1+e^{\phi(t,t^{\prime})}),\qquad 0\leq t\leq t^{\prime}.

(4.8)

where
\phi(t,t^{\prime}):=\lambda(t^{\prime}-t)-e^{2t^{\prime}}(1-e^{-2(t^{\prime}-t)})
. Since

\phi(t,t^{\prime})\to-\infty
as
t^{\prime}\to+\infty
and
\phi(t,t^{\prime})
has an extremum in
t^{\prime}
at
\lambda-2e^{2t^{\prime}}=0
, which does not belong to
\mathbb{R}
if
\lambda\in(-\infty,0]
and is located on
\mathbb{R}_{-}
if
\lambda\in(0,2)
, we conclude that
\max\limits_{t^{\prime}\in[t,\infty)}e^{\phi(t,t^{\prime})}=e^{\phi(t,t)}=1
for
t\geq 0
. Hence, the kernel
\hat{K}(t,t^{\prime})
is bounded for every
0\leq t\leq t^{\prime}<\infty
. On the other hand, since
\hat{N}(c\Upsilon_{h},\tilde{\eta})
is a
C^{1}
function for every
p>0
, the nonlinear term satisfies the following bound:

|\hat{N}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h},\tilde{\eta})|\leq C|c|^{2p}(|c|+|\tilde{\eta}|),\quad\mbox{\rm as long as }\;|\tilde{\eta}|\leq C,

(4.9)

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
c
.

In order to use the Banach fixed-point theorem, we first estimate the size of
E\tilde{\eta}
for
\tilde{\eta}
in a small closed ball in
L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
. Since
e^{-p(2-\lambda)t-pe^{2t}-2t}
in (
4.7
) is absolutely integrable on
[0,\infty)
,
we obtain by using (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) that

\|E\tilde{\eta}\|_{\infty}\leq C|c|^{2p}(|c|+\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{\infty}),

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
c
. Thus, the operator
E
maps a closed ball of radius
2C|c|^{2p+1}
into itself as long as
|c|
is chosen sufficiently small.

Similarly, by Proposition
3.1
, we get
for every
\tilde{\eta}_{1}
and
\tilde{\eta}_{2}
in the same small closed ball
in
L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
that
\hat{N}
is a Lipschitz function satisfying

\|\hat{N}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h},\tilde{\eta}_{1})-\hat{N}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h},\tilde{\eta}_{2})\|_{\infty}\leq C|c|^{2p}\|\tilde{\eta}_{1}-\tilde{\eta}_{2}\|_{\infty},

(4.10)

which yields

\|E\tilde{\eta}_{1}-E\tilde{\eta}_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq C|c|^{2p}\|\tilde{\eta}_{1}-\tilde{\eta}_{2}\|_{\infty},

so that the operator
E
is a contraction for sufficiently small values of
|c|
. By the Banach fixed-point theorem, there exists a unique solution
\tilde{\eta}(t)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
of the integral equation
\tilde{\eta}=E\tilde{\eta}
satisfying
\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{\infty}\leq 2C|c|^{2p+1}
. This estimate yields (
4.6
) after unfolding the transformation for
\eta(t)
.
∎

Using the result of Lemma
4.1
, we can now extend the estimates for
\eta(t)
for large negative values of
t
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
\lambda\in(-\infty,2]
,
p>0
, and
a\in(0,1)
. Then, there exist
b_{0}>0
and
C>0
, such that for every
b\geq b_{0}
there exists
c_{0}>0
, such that for
t\in[-(1-a)p\log b,0]
and

c\in(-c_{0}b^{-(1-a)},c_{0}b^{-(1-a)})
, we have

|\Psi_{c}(t)-c\Upsilon_{h}(t)|\leq C|c|^{2p+1}b^{2p(1-a)}e^{-\frac{t}{p}},

(4.11)

where the bound can be differentiated term by term.

Proof.

We rewrite equation (
4.1
) as an integral equation for
t\in[-(1-a)p\log b,0]
:

\eta(t)=\left(\Upsilon_{g}^{\prime}(0)\Upsilon_{h}(t)-\Upsilon_{h}^{\prime}(0)\Upsilon_{g}(t)\right)\eta(0)+\left(\Upsilon_{h}(0)\Upsilon_{g}(t)-\Upsilon_{g}(0)\Upsilon_{h}(t)\right)\eta^{\prime}(0)\\
+\int_{t}^{0}\left(\Upsilon_{h}(t^{\prime})\Upsilon_{g}(t)-\Upsilon_{h}(t)\Upsilon_{g}(t^{\prime})\right)\left(c\Upsilon_{h}(t^{\prime})+\eta(t^{\prime})\right)\left|c\Upsilon_{h}(t^{\prime})+\eta(t^{\prime})\right|^{2p}dt^{\prime},

(4.12)

where
|\eta(0)|+|\eta^{\prime}(0)|\leq C|c|^{2p+1}
by Lemma
4.1
.
By using the scattering relation (
2.18
) and the transformation (
2.20
), we obtain the following asymptotic behavior for
\Upsilon_{h}(t)
:

\Upsilon_{h}(t)\sim\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-\frac{t}{p}},\quad\text{ as }t\to-\infty,

(4.13)

where
\alpha>0
by (
2.15
). Wronskian relation (
4.2
) implies the following asymptotic behavior for
\Upsilon_{g}(t)
:

\Upsilon_{g}(t)\sim\frac{p\Gamma(\alpha)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}e^{\frac{t}{p}},\quad\text{ as }t\to-\infty.

(4.14)

The divergent behaviour of
\Upsilon_{h}(t)
as
t\to-\infty
dictates the correct form of the transformation to use, which in this case is given by:

\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t):=e^{\frac{t}{p}}\Upsilon_{h}(t),\quad\tilde{\Upsilon}_{g}(t):=e^{\frac{t}{p}}\Upsilon_{g}(t),\quad\tilde{\eta}(t):=e^{\frac{t}{p}}\eta(t).

Applying it to the integral equation (
4.12
) results in the fixed point equation
\tilde{\eta}=\mathcal{F}\tilde{\eta}
, where

(\mathcal{F}\tilde{\eta})(t):=\left(\Upsilon_{g}^{\prime}(0)\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t)-\Upsilon_{h}^{\prime}(0)\tilde{\Upsilon}_{g}(t)\right)\eta(0)+\left(\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(0)\Upsilon_{g}(t)-\Upsilon_{g}(0)\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t)\right)\eta^{\prime}(0)\\
+\int_{t}^{0}e^{-2t^{\prime}}\tilde{K}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{N}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t^{\prime}),\tilde{\eta}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime},

(4.15)

where

\tilde{K}(t,t^{\prime}):=e^{\frac{t-t^{\prime}}{p}}\left(\Upsilon_{h}(t^{\prime})\Upsilon_{g}(t)-\Upsilon_{h}(t)\Upsilon_{g}(t^{\prime})\right)

and
\hat{N}
is the same as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

We proceed by estimating each term of
\mathcal{F}\tilde{\eta}
in the space
L^{\infty}(-(1-a)p\log b,0)
. Since
\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}
and
\tilde{\Upsilon}_{g}
are bounded for
t\in(-\infty,0]
and

|\eta(0)|+|\eta^{\prime}(0)|\leq C|c|^{2p+1}
, we obtain

\left|\left(\Upsilon_{g}^{\prime}(0)\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t)-\Upsilon_{h}^{\prime}(0)\tilde{\Upsilon}_{g}(t)\right)\eta(0)+\left(\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(0)\Upsilon_{g}(t)-\Upsilon_{g}(0)\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t)\right)\eta^{\prime}(0)\right|\leq C|c|^{2p+1}.

Furthermore, if
t\ll-1
, asymptotics (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) allow us to estimate size of the last term in (
4.15
) as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t}^{0}e^{-2t^{\prime}}\tilde{K}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{N}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h}(t^{\prime}),\tilde{\eta}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C|c|^{2p}\int_{t}^{0}e^{-2t^{\prime}}(1+e^{-\frac{2}{p}(t^{\prime}-t)})(|c|+|\tilde{\eta}(t^{\prime})|)dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C|c|^{2p}b^{2p(1-a)}(|c|+\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{\infty}),

where we have used the
C^{1}
property of
\hat{N}(c\tilde{\Upsilon}_{h},\tilde{\eta})
satisfying (
4.9
).
These two estimates yield

\|\mathcal{F}\tilde{\eta}\|_{\infty}\leq C|c|^{2p}b^{2p(1-a)}\left(|c|+\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{\infty}\right),

for sufficiently large values of
b
. The divergent behaviour of
b^{2p(1-a)}
for large
b
is controlled by appropriately reducing the value of
|c|
satisfying
|c|<c_{0}b^{-(1-a)}
for sufficiently small
c_{0}>0
. Thus, we see that the operator
\mathcal{F}
maps the closed ball of the radius
2C|c|^{2p+1}b^{2p(1-a)}
in
L^{\infty}(-(1-a)p\log b,0)
into itself. Moreover, since
\hat{N}
is a Lipschitz function satisfying (
4.10
), we get that if
\tilde{\eta}_{1},\tilde{\eta}_{2}
belong to the same ball, then

\|\mathcal{F}\eta_{1}-\mathcal{F}\eta_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq C|c|^{2p}b^{2p(1-a)}\|\tilde{\eta}_{1}-\tilde{\eta}_{2}\|_{\infty},

so that the operator
\mathcal{F}
is a contraction as long as
|c|<c_{0}b^{-(1-a)}
for sufficiently small
c_{0}>0
. By the Banach fixed-point theorem, there exists a unique
\tilde{\eta}\in L^{\infty}(-(1-a)p\log b,0)
satisfying

\sup_{t\in[-(1-a)p\log b,0]}|\tilde{\eta}(t)|\leq C|c|^{2p+1}b^{2p(1-a)},

which yields the bound (
4.11
) due to the transformation
\eta(t)=e^{-\frac{t}{p}}\tilde{\eta}(t)
.
∎

5.
Matching solutions: the proof of Theorem
1.1

We are now equipped with all the necessary estimates to prove Theorem
1.1
. The ground state
u=u_{b}
of the stationary Gross–Pitaevskii equation (
1.1
) in the Emden-Fowler variables (
2.3

and as

for every

if

\Psi_{b}(t)=\Psi_{c(b)}(t),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}

(5.1)

for some

and

) and (
3.16
) for
\Psi_{b}(t)

4.11
) for
\Psi_{c}(t)
at the times

with varying
T>0
and sufficiently large values of

. Equaling the asymptotic behaviors due to (
5.1
) yields two implicit equations for parameters
\lambda
and
c
.

Bound (
1.6
) follows from the bound (
3.6
) with
T=0
after the transformation (
2.3
). Bounds
(
1.7
) and (
1.8
) follow

) and (
4.11
)

2.3

is completed after obtaining the asymptotic representation for

and

We fix
p\in(0,1)
,
T>0
, and

in the rectangle

for which both Lemmas

By Lemma
3.4

\Psi_{b}(t-p\log b)=\Theta_{h}(t)+\gamma_{h}(t)+\mathcal{O}(F(\lambda,b)b^{2ap}e^{\frac{t}{p}}),

.
Using (

\displaystyle\Psi_{b}(t-p\log b)=

\displaystyle-\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\int_{-\infty}^{t}f_{b}(t^{\prime})\Sigma(t^{\prime})\Theta_{h}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}+\mathcal{O}(F(\lambda,b)b^{2ap}e^{\frac{t}{p}}).

Evaluating these expressions at

as

\displaystyle\Psi_{b}(T-(1-a)p\log b)=

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}^{-1/p}e^{-\frac{T}{p}}b^{-a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\alpha_{p}^{1/p}e^{\frac{T}{p}}b^{a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}f_{b}(t)\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{p}\alpha_{p}^{-1/p}e^{-\frac{T}{p}}b^{-a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}f_{b}(t)\Sigma(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(F(\lambda,b)b^{2ap+a}e^{\frac{T}{p}}),

.
Since

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}f_{b}(t)\Sigma(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt\right|\leq C_{T,a}b^{-2p(1-a)}

, we finally obtain
the asymptotic formula:

\displaystyle\Psi_{b}(T-(1-a)p\log b)=\alpha_{p}^{-1/p}e^{-\frac{T}{p}}b^{-a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap},b^{-2p(1-a)})]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\alpha_{p}^{1/p}e^{\frac{T}{p}}b^{a}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}f_{b}(t)\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]+\mathcal{O}(F(\lambda,b)b^{2ap})\right].

(5.3)

By Lemma
4.2
, we have for
t\in(-(1-a)p\log b,0]
,

\Psi_{c}(t)=c\Upsilon_{h}(t)+\mathcal{O}(|c|^{2p+1}b^{2p(1-a)}e^{-\frac{t}{p}}),

(5.4)

where
\Upsilon_{h}
is given by (
2.20
) and the asymptotic expansion can be differentiated in
t
. Since the expansion (
5.4
)
is used for

, we can use either (
2.18
)
or (
2.19
) for asymptotic expansions of the Tricomi
function
\mathfrak{U}(e^{2t};\alpha,\beta)
in (

), where

due to (
2.15
) and

.
If
p\neq\frac{1}{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
the asymptotic formula for the solution
\Psi_{c}
evaluated at

is obtained with the help of (

), (

), and (

) in the form:

\displaystyle\Psi_{c}(T-(1-a)p\log b)=

\displaystyle ce^{\frac{T}{p}}\frac{\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p-1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)}b^{-(1-a)}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)})]

\displaystyle+ce^{-\frac{T}{p}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)}b^{1-a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)},|c|^{2p}b^{2p(1-a)})].

(5.5)

If
p=\frac{1}{n}
with

, then the asymptotic formula for the solution

evaluated at

5.4
) in the form:

\displaystyle\Psi_{c}(T-(1-a)p\log b)=

\displaystyle ce^{nT}\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n!\Gamma\left(\frac{1-n}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)}b^{-(1-a)}[(T-(1-a)p\log b)[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)})]+\mathcal{O}(1)]

\displaystyle+ce^{-nT}\frac{(n-1)!}{\Gamma(\alpha)}b^{1-a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)},|c|^{2p}b^{2p(1-a)})],

(5.6)

where
\alpha=\frac{1+n}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}>0
.

When we use the connection equation (
5.1
), it sets up the system of two equations for two unknowns
\lambda
and
c
. These two equations can be obtained by equaling
\Psi_{b}(t)
and
\Psi_{c}(t)
as well as their first derivatives at the time

. Alternatively, since the asymptotic expansions are differentiable in
t
term by term, we can set up the system by equaling coefficients in front of the exponential functions

e^{\frac{T}{p}}
and
e^{-\frac{T}{p}}
. Equaling the coefficients for
the

terms in (

(
5.5
) or (

) yields the following equation:

\alpha_{p}^{-1/p}b^{-a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap},b^{-2p(1-a)})]=c\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}b^{1-a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)},|c|^{2p}b^{2p(1-a)})].

(5.7)

The nonlinear equation (
5.7
) is defined for
(\lambda,c)\in[0,2]\times[0,c_{0}b^{-(1-a)}]
and the remainder terms are
C^{1}
functions
with respect to
(\lambda,c)
. Since the leading-order part of the nonlinear equation (
5.7
) is linear in
c
and suggests
the solution
c=\mathcal{O}(b^{-1})
, which clearly exists inside

|c|\leq c_{0}b^{-(1-a)}
, we have by an application of the implicit function
theorem the existence of a
C^{1}
function
c=c(\lambda,b)
for

\lambda\in[0,2]
and sufficiently large
b\geq b_{T,a}
, which is given asymptotically as

c(\lambda,b)=\alpha_{p}^{-1/p}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}b^{-1}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap},b^{-2p(1-a)})].

(5.8)

Equaling the coefficients for
the
e^{\frac{T}{p}}
terms in (
5.3
) with either
(
5.5
) or (
5.6
) and substituting
the expression (
5.8
) for

yields a nonlinear equation
for
\lambda

.

Case

. If
p\neq\frac{1}{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we use (
5.3
) and
(
5.5
) in (
5.1
), equal the coefficients for the

e^{\frac{T}{p}}
terms, and substitute
the expression (
5.8
) for

. This yields the nonlinear equation for
\lambda
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\alpha_{p}^{1/p}b^{a}\big[\lambda b^{-2p}\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]

\displaystyle-b^{-4p}\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]+\mathcal{O}((|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p})^{2p+1}b^{2ap})\big]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{p}^{-1/p}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p-1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}b^{-2+a}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)})].

(5.9)

If
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, both integrals in the left-hand-side of (
5.9
) converge due to the asymptotic expansion
(
2.9
) so that they can be expanded as

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2a(1-p)}),

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt

\displaystyle=-2\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2a(1-2p)}),

which implies that the nonlinear equation (
5.9
) for
\lambda
can be rewritten
in the equivalent form:

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap},b^{-2a(1-p)})]

\displaystyle-2b^{-2p}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap},b^{-2a(1-2p)})]+\mathcal{O}((|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p})^{2p+1}b^{2p(1+a)})

\displaystyle=-2p^{-2}\alpha_{p}^{-2/p}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p-1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}b^{-2(1-p)}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)})].

(5.10)

If
p\neq\frac{1}{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\frac{p-1}{2p},-\frac{1}{p}\neq-m
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
so that the arguments of the Gamma functions at
\lambda=0
are away from their pole singularities. Hence, all terms of the nonlinear equation (
5.10
) are
C^{1}
functions of
\lambda
at
\lambda=0
.
For
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
b^{-2(1-p)}\ll b^{-2p}
for sufficiently large
b
. Since the leading-order part of the nonlinear equation (
5.10
)
is linear in
\lambda
and suggests the solution
\lambda=\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p})
, we have by an application of the implicit function
theorem the existence of a
C^{1}
function
\lambda=\lambda(b)
for sufficiently large
b
which is given asymptotically by

\lambda(b)=2\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt}{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt}b^{-2p}+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2(1-p)},b^{-2p(1+a)},b^{-2(p+a(1-2p))},b^{-2p(4p+1-a)}).

(5.11)

The integrals in (
5.11
) can be computed by using
the explicit expression for
\Theta_{h}
given by
(
2.8
) with
t_{0}=0
. Using the substitution
s=(1+\alpha_{p}e^{2t})^{-1}
we express the integrals in terms of the
Beta function

B(z_{1},z_{2}):=\int_{0}^{1}s^{z_{1}-1}(1-s)^{z_{2}-1}ds=\frac{\Gamma(z_{1})\Gamma(z_{2})}{\Gamma(z_{1}+z_{2})},\quad z_{1},z_{2}>0

and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha_{p}^{1+1/p}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\frac{1}{p}-2}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}ds=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}+1\right)}{2\alpha_{p}^{1+1/p}\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)},

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha_{p}^{2+1/p}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\frac{1}{p}-3}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}+1}ds=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}-2\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}+2\right)}{2\alpha_{p}^{2+1/p}\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)}.

Substituting these expressions into (
5.11
) yields
the final formula for
p\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
p\neq\frac{1}{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\lambda(b)=\frac{2(1+p)}{\alpha_{p}(1-2p)}b^{-2p}+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2(1-p)},b^{-2p(1+a)},b^{-2(p+a(1-2p))},b^{-2p(4p+1-a)}),

(5.12)

where we have used the property
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
.

If
p=\frac{1}{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{1,2\}
, we use (
5.3
) and
(
5.6
) in (
5.1
), equal the coefficients for the

e^{\frac{T}{p}}
terms, and substitute
the expression (
5.8
) for
c=c(\lambda,b)
. This yields the nonlinear equation for

:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\alpha_{p}^{1/p}b^{a}\big[\lambda b^{-2p}\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]

\displaystyle-b^{-4p}\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]+\mathcal{O}((|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p})^{2p+1}b^{2ap})\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+n}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)}{\alpha_{p}^{n}n!(n-1)!\Gamma\left(\frac{1-n}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)}b^{-2+a}[(T-(1-a)p\log b)[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)})]+\mathcal{O}(1)].

(5.13)

After dividing it by
b^{a-2p}
, this equation can be rewritten in the form
(
5.10
), where the right-hand side has the order of

\frac{\log b\;b^{-2(1-p)}}{\Gamma(\frac{1-n}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4})},

which is much smaller than the leading-order term of the order of

for

. For even

5.11
) for

\lambda(b)=\frac{2(1+p)}{\alpha_{p}(1-2p)}b^{-2p}+\mathcal{O}(\log bb^{-2(1-p)},b^{-2p(1+a)},b^{-2(p+a(1-2p))},b^{-2p(4p+1-a)}).

which modifies the final formula (

, we have

with either even or odd

. In all cases,

for suffiently large values of

.

Case
p\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Since
p\neq\frac{1}{n}
for every

if

5.9

\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt

as

. Consequently, there is a positive constant

such that for all
b\geq b_{T,a}
, we have

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt\right|\leq C_{T,a}b^{2a(2p-1)}.

The nonlinear equation (
5.9
) can be rewritten in the equivalent form:

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap},b^{-2a(1-p)})]

\displaystyle+b^{-2p}\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})]+\mathcal{O}((|\lambda|b^{-2p}+b^{-4p(1-a)})^{2}b^{2p(1+a)})

\displaystyle=-2p^{-2}\alpha_{p}^{-2/p}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p-1}{2p}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}b^{-2(1-p)}[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p(1-a)})],

(5.14)

where the second term on the left-hand side is of the order of

as

for

, which is satisfied automatically, since
\frac{p}{2p-1}>1
for

. Moreover, since

for

and

, the right-hand side dominates in the nonlinear equation (
5.14
). Solving the nonlinear equation (
5.14
) by an application of the implicit function theorem, we have the existence of a
C^{1}
function
\lambda=\lambda(b)
for sufficiently large
b
which is given asymptotically by

\displaystyle\lambda(b)=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\alpha_{p}^{1-1/p}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)}{p^{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{p-1}{2p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}+1\right)}b^{-2(1-p)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2p+2a(2p-1)},b^{-2(1-p)-2ap},b^{-2(1+a)(1-p)},b^{-2(1-pa)},b^{-2p(3-5a)}).

(5.15)

Since

\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{p}\right)>0
and
\Gamma\left(\frac{p-1}{2p}\right)<0
. Hence,
\lambda(b)>0
for sufficiently large values of
b
.

Case
p=\frac{1}{2}
. This case corresponds to

in the nonlinear equation (
5.13

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-a})]

\displaystyle+b^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-a})]+\mathcal{O}((|\lambda|b^{-1}+b^{-2}\log b)^{2}b^{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{8\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)}{\alpha_{p}^{4}\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}\right)}b^{-1}[(T-(1-a)p\log b)[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-(1-a)})]+\mathcal{O}(1)],

(5.16)

where
\alpha_{p=\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{4!}
.
The second integral
\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt
diverges linearly in the upper limit since
\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}=\mathcal{O}(e^{-4t})
as

. The exact computations
with the help of the explicit formula (
2.8

following asymptotic expression:

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}^{\prime}(t)\Theta_{h}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}\biggr|^{t=T+ap\log b}_{t\to-\infty}-2\int_{-\infty}^{T+ap\log b}e^{4t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha_{p}^{4}}-\frac{1}{\alpha_{p}^{4}}\left[2(T+ap\log b)+\log(\alpha_{p})-\frac{11}{6}+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap})\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\alpha_{p}^{4}}(T+ap\log b)+\frac{7}{3\alpha_{p}^{4}}-\frac{\log(\alpha_{p})}{\alpha_{p}^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(b^{-2ap}).

On the other hand, we use (
2.17

\frac{8\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)}=-\frac{8}{\pi}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)=-2,

so that the leading-order terms of the nonlinear equation (
5.16

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{2t}\Theta_{h}(t)^{2}dt[1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-a})]+\mathcal{O}(b^{-1},\log b\;b^{-1-a})+\mathcal{O}((|\lambda|b^{-1}+b^{-2}\log b)^{2}b^{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha_{p}^{4}}\log b\;b^{-1}[1+\mathcal{O}(\lambda)][1+\mathcal{O}(b^{-(1-a)})]+\mathcal{O}(b^{-1}).

By using the implicit function theorem, we have the existence of a
C^{1}
function
\lambda=\lambda(b)
for sufficiently large

which is given asymptotically by

\lambda(b)=144\log b\;b^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(b^{-1},\log b\;b^{-1-a},(\log b)^{2}\;b^{-2}),

(5.17)

where we have used

Hence,

for sufficiently large values of

.

Case

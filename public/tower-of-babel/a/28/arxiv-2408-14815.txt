The fourth moment of truncated Eisenstein series Thanks: The second author was supported by the National Science Foundation grants DMS-2344044 and DMS-2341239.
Goran Djanković, Rizwanur Khan

Abstract
. We obtain an asymptotic for the fourth moment of truncated Eisenstein series of large Laplacian eigenvalue, verifying for the first time that the main term corresponds to Gaussian random behavior. This is a manifestation of the Random Wave Conjecture, which for Eisenstein series was formulated by Hejhal and Rackner over thirty years ago. Our innovation is to tackle the problem after introducing, at no cost, an extra averaging over the truncation parameter.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 11F12, 11M99; Secondary: 81Q50, 58J51.

1.
Introduction

In arithmetic settings, good progress has been made in recent years on the Quantum Unique Ergodicity (QUE) problem. However towards the more general Random Wave Conjecture (RWC), there has been more limited success. The RWC refers to the principle of Berry
[
1
]
that highly excited eigenfunctions of a classically ergodic system should manifest Gaussian random behavior. Hejhal and Rackner
[
8
]
and Hejhal and Strömbergsson
[
9
]
have supported this conjecture numerically for Laplacian eigenfunctions on the modular surface
\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}
. Further, in
[
8
, section 7.3]
, the conjecture was formulated for the continuous spectrum of the Laplacian, the Eisenstein series, even though they are not
L^{2}
-integrable.

We focus on the modular surface, where one can consider joint eigenfunctions of the Laplacian and Hecke operators. A desciption of the RWC in terms of moments of even or odd Hecke-Maass cusp forms
f
for
\Gamma=SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with Laplacian eigenvalue
\frac{1}{4}+t_{f}^{2}
can be given as follows. For any integer
p\geq 1
and any fixed, compact regular set
\Omega\subset\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
, it is expected that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{f(z)}{\|f\|_{2}}\right)^{p}d\mu z\sim c_{p}

as
f
traverses any sequence with
t_{f}\to\infty
, where we use the usual hyperbolic measure,
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denotes the
L^{p}
-norm, and
c_{p}
is the
p
-th moment of a standard normal random variable. For the Eisenstein series, the conjecture is that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\tilde{E}(z,\frac{1}{2}+iT)}{\sqrt{2\log T}}\right)^{p}d\mu z\sim c_{p}

as
T\to\infty
, where
\tilde{E}(z,s)
can be taken to be either
\frac{\xi(1+2iT)}{|\xi(1+2iT)|}E(z,\frac{1}{2}+iT)
or
\frac{\xi(1+2iT)}{|\xi(1+2iT)|}E_{A}(z,\frac{1}{2}+iT)
. The notation will be defined below but note that
E_{A}(z,s)
is the truncated Eisenstein series, which equals
E(z,s)
on the fundamental domain but with the constant term of its Fourier series subtracted off for
\Im(z)>A
. Multiplication by
\frac{\xi(1+2iT)}{|\xi(1+2iT)|}
serves to ensure that
\tilde{E}(z,\frac{1}{2}+iT)
is real valued. And finally, note that
\sqrt{2\log T}
roughly equals
\|E_{A}(\cdot,\frac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{2}
. One can expand these conjectures to include the set
\Omega=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
, which is not compact, as long as
p
is not so large that the moments diverge (in the limit). This situation is likely easier than the case of an arbitrary compact set
\Omega
. Note that for (
1.1
), when working with
\Omega=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
one must use the definition of
\tilde{E}(z,s)
in terms of
E_{A}(z,s)
instead of
E(z,s)
since the latter is not square-integrable.

The first nontrivial case of the RWC is
p=2
, better known as QUE. This was established for the Eisenstein series by Luo and Sarnak
[
15
]
well before the Hecke-Maass cusp forms by Lindenstrauss
[
14
]
and Soundararajan
[
16
]
. The case
p=3
has no main term (since
c_{p}=0
for odd
p
) and was settled for both Eisenstein series and Hecke-Maass cusp forms, by Watson
[
18
]
in the case
\Omega=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
even before QUE was resolved, and recently by Huang
[
10
]
in the case of compact
\Omega
. This brings us to
p=4
, which is the largest value for which the RWC has any sort of resolution. For dihedral Maass newforms, the case
p=4
and
\Omega=\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}
was proven by Humphries and the second author
[
12
]
. Until now, this was the only resolved fourth moment instance of the RWC, a fact that seems surprising since one might have naively expected the problem for Eisenstein series to be easier and to be solved first. However this is not the case as the fourth moment of Eisenstein series has a different set of challenges, which in this paper we are able to overcome. For general Hecke-Maass cusp forms, the fourth moment is only known conditionally on the Generalized Lindelöf Hypothesis by the work of Buttcane and the second author
[
4
]
.

We state again Hejhal and Rackner’s conjecture for the fourth moment of Eisenstein series. The following is equivalent to (
1.1
) with
p=4
, for which
c_{4}=3
, and
\Omega=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
8
]
Fix a constant
A>1
. We have

\|E_{A}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{4}^{4}\sim\frac{36}{\pi}\log^{2}T

as
T\rightarrow\infty
.

Until now, the best result directly towards this conjecture was the upper bound

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|E_{A}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{4}^{4}\ll\log^{2}T

due to Spinu
[
17
]
and Humphries
[
11
]
. There is also an alternative version of the above conjecture, using Zagier’s
[
19
]
regularized integral, which was formulated and solved by the authors in
[
5
,
6
]
. However, the regularized version is a weaker result in the sense that it is implied by but does not imply Conjecture
1.1
. Nevertheless, this paper builds upon
[
6
]
.

In Conjecture
1.1
and all previous works on it, the truncation parameter
A
is a fixed constant. Our main novelty is to give a more fluid role to
A
. We will introduce an extra averaging over
A
and establish Conjecture
1.1
on average. This may seem like we are proving something weaker, but we will show that this averaged result actually returns the conjecture for fixed
A
. In this way, we will fully resolve Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix a constant
A>1
. We have

\|E_{A}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{4}^{4}\sim\frac{36}{\pi}\log^{2}T

as
T\rightarrow\infty
.

2.
Discussion of the proof

Part of the difficulty is the unique set-up of the problem in the Eisenstein series case due to convergence issues. If we ignore for a moment all convergence issues then the usual starting point would be to use Parseval’s identity and spectral decomposition to express the fourth moment as

\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{=}}\sum_{j\geq 1}|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle|^{2},

where the sum is over an orthonormal basis of even or odd Hecke-Maass cusp forms, and we use the symbol
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{=}}
to mean that the equality is only true in spirit (for example, in this case to have a true equality we would need to also write down the contribution from the rest of the spectrum). Next by ‘unfolding’ we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{=}}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{L(\tfrac{1}{2},u_{j})^{2}|L(\tfrac{1}{2}+2iT,u_{j})|^{2}}{|\zeta(1+2iT)|^{4}}|\mathcal{H}(t_{j})|^{2},

where
t_{j}
is the spectral parameter of
u_{j}
and
\mathcal{H}(t_{j})
is a ratio of gamma functions which dictates that the sum is essentially supported on
|t_{j}|<2T
. At this point the problem is reduced to that of evaluating an average of central
L
-values, which was accomplished by the authors in
[
6
]
. Before we continue the discussion, recall that for
F(z)
automorphic with rapid decay at the cusp, the technique of unfolding refers to the manipulation

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}}E(z,s)F(z)d\mu z=\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma}\Im(\gamma z)^{s}\Big)F(z)d\mu z=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\mathbb{H}}\Im(z)^{s}F(z)d\mu z,

where the final integral, over the rectangle
[0,1]\times[0,\infty]
, may be evaluated by Mellin transformation after taking a Fourier series expansion of
F(z)
.

Unfortunately the above strategy does not actually work because the starting object
\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\rangle
is not convergent. If we replace
E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)
with
E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)
, then we do have a convergent object. Then after applying Parseval’s identity, we want to pass from
\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle
to
\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle
since we can evaluate (
2.1
). We get (with details to follow) that

\sum_{j\geq 1}|\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{=}}\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\langle y^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle_{A}\right|^{2}\\
-2\Re\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}\overline{\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle}\langle y^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle_{A}\right),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A}
denotes an inner product only over the region of the fundamental domain with
\Im(z)>A
. This region is already a rectangle, so we can skip the unfolding process for these inner products. Consider the third sum on the right hand side, for which we find

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}\overline{\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle}\langle y^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle_{A}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{=}}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{L(\tfrac{1}{2},u_{j})L(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT,u_{j})}{\zeta^{2}(1-2iT)}\overline{\mathcal{H}(t_{j})}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}\frac{L(\tfrac{1}{2}+s,u_{j})L(\tfrac{1}{2}+s+2iT,u_{j})}{\zeta(1+2iT)\zeta(1+2s+2iT)}\mathcal{H}(s,t_{j})A^{-s}\frac{ds}{s},

where
\mathcal{H}(s,t_{j})
is a ratio of gamma functions that equals
\mathcal{H}(t_{j})
for
s=0
. It is interesting that the problem boils down to a mean value of
L
-functions that resembles (
2.1
), but one that is more difficult. If we shift contours left then we can recover (
2.1
) from the pole at
s=0
, but it is not clear what to do with the shifted integral. In any case we cannot shift far without the Riemann Hypothesis due to the
\zeta(1+2s+2iT)
factor in the denominator and the fact that the function
\mathcal{H}(s,t_{j})
does not preclude
|s|
from being of size
T
. Thus essentially the difference between the mean values (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) is that the former involves only central values while in the latter we must treat the
L
-functions along the critical line (or close to it), which seems very difficult to do.

We are able to break through with a new approach. We ‘smoothen out’ the sharp truncation of
E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)
by introducing in (
2.2
) an integral with respect to
A
over a shrinking interval. We show that to prove our main theorem, it suffices to prove this smooth version. The introduction of the
A
-integral enables us to use integration by parts with respect to
A
to restrict to
|s|<T^{\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
which we can fix as small as we like. Thus the problem becomes much closer to (
2.1
).

We use an approximate functional equation to get a handle on the central values
L(\tfrac{1}{2},u_{j})L(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT,u_{j})
. But for
L(\tfrac{1}{2}+s,u_{j})L(\tfrac{1}{2}+s+2iT,u_{j})
we can already expand this into a Dirichlet series. Then using properties of
\mathcal{H}(s,t_{j})
we find surprisingly that this Dirichlet series is actually shorter than that of the approximate functional equation of
L(\tfrac{1}{2},u_{j})L(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT,u_{j})
. Thus since (
2.1
) can be evaluated, it is plausible we should be able to evaluate (
2.2
) too. This is precisely what we do, using Kuznetsov’s formula. Our success in treating the off-diagonal is due to the observation of the short Dirichlet series together with the smoothing device which enforces
|s|<T^{\alpha}
. Altogether we find several main terms from various pieces, which nicely combine to give the conjectured main term of
\frac{36}{\pi}\log^{2}T
.

It is also worth remarking that at some point in the argument (see section
8.2
), we must make use of a sub-Weyl strength subconvexity bound for the Riemann Zeta function. We find it quite surprising that we need such a deep result, which was not required in the treatment of (
2.1
) given in
[
6
]
, nor in the fourth moment of dihedral Maass newforms established in
[
12
]
. This input may be indicative of the difficulty of Conjecture
1.1
.

3.
Notation and preliminaries

The constant term of Eisenstein series
E(z,\frac{1}{2}+iT)
is given by

e(y,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)=y^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}+y^{\frac{1}{2}-iT}\frac{\xi(1-2iT)}{\xi(1+2iT)}=y^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}+{\bf c}\cdot y^{\frac{1}{2}-iT},

where we denote

{\bf c}:={\bf c}(T)=\frac{\xi(1-2iT)}{\xi(1+2iT)},

and
\xi(s)=\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma(\tfrac{s}{2})\zeta(s)
is the completed Riemann zeta function.

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the usual fundamental domain for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
, and for
A>1
, let

\mathcal{F}_{A}=\{z\in\mathcal{F}:\Im(z)\leq A\},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{A}=\{z\in\mathcal{F}:\Im(z)>A\}

denote the compact and the cuspidal part of the fundamental domain, respectively, which lie below and above the line
\Im(z)=A
, respectively.
Then
the truncated Eisenstein series is defined (following
[
17
]
) by

E_{A}(z,s)=\begin{cases}E(z,s),&\text{if }z\in\mathcal{F}_{A}\\
E(z,s)-e(y,s),&\text{if }z\in\mathcal{C}_{A}.\end{cases}

This definition may be extended to
\mathbb{H}
by
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
translates, but anyway when taking the fourth moment we integrate only over
\mathcal{F}
.

For
z\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
we have the Fourier expansion

(3.1)

E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)=\frac{2}{\xi(1+2iT)}\sum_{n\neq 0}\tau(|n|,T)\sqrt{y}K_{iT}(2\pi|n|y)e(nx),

where
we denote for positive integers
m
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}

\tau(m,\gamma)=\sum_{ab=m}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{i\gamma}=m^{-i\gamma}\sum_{a|m}a^{2i\gamma}.

This generalized divisor function
is real valued,
\tau(m,\gamma)=\tau(m,-\gamma)
, and
|\tau(m,\gamma)|\leq\tau(m)
. We will also be using that
E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\xi(1+2iT)
is real valued, a fact that was noted in
[
5
, equation (5.2)]
.

Let
\{u_{j}\;:j\geq 1\}
denote an orthonormal basis of even or odd Hecke-Maass cusp forms for the modular group
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\frac{1}{4}+t_{j}^{2}
, with
t_{j}>0
denote the corresponding Laplacian eigenvalues and
let
\lambda_{j}(m)
denote the (real) eigenvalues of the
m
-th Hecke operator corresponding to
u_{j}
:
T_{m}u_{j}=\lambda_{j}(m)u_{j}
for all
m\geq 1
.
Moreover we write
\lambda_{j}(-m)=\lambda_{j}(m)
for
u_{j}
even and
\lambda_{j}(-m)=-\lambda_{j}(m)
for
u_{j}
odd. Each such Hecke-Maass cusp form
u_{j}
has
the Fourier expansion

u_{j}(z)=\rho_{j}(1)\sum_{m\neq 0}\lambda_{j}(m)\sqrt{y}K_{it_{j}}(2\pi|m|y)e(mx),

where we have the following
formula relating the normalizing factor
\rho_{j}(1)
with the symmetric square
L
-function associated to
u_{j}
:

(3.2)

|\rho_{j}(1)|^{2}=\frac{2\cosh(\pi t_{j})}{L(1,\text{sym}^{2}u_{j})}=\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+it_{j})\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-it_{j})L(1,\text{sym}^{2}u_{j})}.

In both cases, even or odd,
u_{j}
is real-valued on
\mathbb{H}
.

The
L
-function attached to the Hecke-Maass cusp form
u_{j}
is defined
for
\Re(s)>1
by

L(s,u_{j})=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{j}(m)}{m^{s}}.

The completed
L
-function for
even

u_{j}
is

\Lambda(s,u_{j})=\pi^{-s}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+it_{j}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-it_{j}}{2}\right)L(s,u_{j})

and it satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,u_{j})=\Lambda(1-s,u_{j})
.
For
odd

u_{j}
, the completed
L
-function is

\Lambda(s,u_{j})=\pi^{-1-s}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s+it_{j}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s-it_{j}}{2}\right)L(s,u_{j})

and satisfies
\Lambda(s,u_{j})=-\Lambda(1-s,u_{j})
.

3.1.
Stirling’s approximation

Let
\delta>0
be fixed. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(z)>\delta
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
with
|t|>2|z+1|^{2}
, we will frequently appeal to Stirling’s approximation

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma(z+it)=\sqrt{2\pi}|t|^{(z+it-\frac{1}{2})}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}|t|-it+i\mathrm{sgn}(t)\frac{\pi}{2}\left(z-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\left(1+O\left(\frac{|z+1|^{2}}{|t|}\right)\right),

which can be refined to arbitrary precision by replacing the factor
1+O\Big(\frac{|z+1|^{2}}{|t|}\Big)
with

(3.4)

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{c_{2k}(z)}{t^{k}}+O_{N}\bigg(\left(\frac{|z+1|^{2}}{|t|}\right)^{N+1}\bigg),

where
c_{2k}(z)
are polynomials in
z
of degree at most
2k
and
N\geq 1
is any integer.

To see the statements above, recall that Stirling’s approximation gives

(3.5)

\displaystyle\log\Gamma(z+it)=(z+it-\tfrac{1}{2})\log(z+it)-(z+it)+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{c_{j}}{(it)^{j}(1+\frac{z}{it})^{j}}+O_{M}\left(\frac{1}{|z+it|^{M+1}}\right)

for some constants
c_{j}
and any integer
M\geq 1
. We write

(3.6)

\displaystyle\log(z+it)=\log(it)+\log\Big(1+\frac{z}{it}\Big)=\log|t|+i\frac{\pi}{2}\mathrm{sgn}(t)+\frac{z}{it}-\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}\Big(\frac{z}{it}\Big)^{j}.

Now inserting (
3.6
) into (
3.5
), taking a power series expansion of
(1+\frac{z}{it})^{-j}
, taking
M
as large as we like, and finally taking the exponential of both sides of (
3.5
), gives (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).

4.
A smooth version

We will prove a version of Theorem
1.2
with an extra averaging over
A
. One may interpret the effect of this averaging as a way to smoothen out the sharp truncation of
E_{A}(z,\frac{1}{2}+iT)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
B>1
and
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{100}
. Let
h(A)
be a smooth, non-negative real function supported on
B-T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}<A<B+T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
for
T>0
, satisfying

\displaystyle T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\ll\hat{h}(0)\ll T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},

(4.1)

\displaystyle h^{(k)}(A)\ll_{k}\left(T^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{k},

where
\hat{h}(0)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)dA
and
k
is any nonnegative integer.
Then for
\alpha
small enough, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\|E_{A}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{4}^{4}\ dA\;\;\sim\;\hat{h}(0)\frac{36}{\pi}\log^{2}T

as
T\rightarrow\infty
.

The rest of the paper will focus on proving Proposition
4.1
, because that is enough to imply our main theorem.

Lemma 4.2
.

Proposition
4.1
implies Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

Suppose that Proposition
4.1
is true. We need to prove that

\hat{h}(0)\|E_{B}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{4}^{4}\sim\hat{h}(0)\frac{36}{\pi}\log^{2}T.

The left hand side equals

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\ \|E_{B}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{4}^{4}\ dA=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\int\limits_{\mathcal{F}}\ |E_{B}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|^{4}d\mu z\ dA=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where
d\mu(z)=\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}
for
z=x+iy
, and

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{F}\\
\Im(z)<B-T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{subarray}}\left|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)+(E_{B}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)-E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT))\right|^{4}d\mu z\ dA,

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{F}\\
B-T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\Im(z)\leq B+T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{subarray}}\left|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)+(E_{B}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)-E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT))\right|^{4}d\mu z\ dA,

\displaystyle I_{3}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{F}\\
\Im(z)>B+T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{subarray}}\left|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)+(E_{B}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)-E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT))\right|^{4}d\mu z\ dA.

Since
A
is restricted to
B-T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}<A<B+T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
by the support of
h(A)
, we have

E_{B}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)-E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)=0

in
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
. In
I_{2}
, we have

|E_{B}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)-E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|\ll y^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll 1.

Thus

(4.2)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{F}\\
B-T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\Im(z)\leq B+T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{subarray}}|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|^{4}d\mu z\ dA

\displaystyle+O\Bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{F}\\
B-T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\Im(z)\leq B+T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{subarray}}1\ d\mu z\ dA\Bigg)

\displaystyle+O\Bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{F}\\
B-T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\Im(z)\leq B+T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{subarray}}|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|^{3}d\mu z\ dA\Bigg).

We have shown that the main term of
I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}
equals

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\int_{\mathcal{F}}|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT))|^{4}d\mu z\ dA\sim\hat{h}(0)\frac{36}{\pi}\log^{2}T,

since we are assuming Proposition
4.1
. Thus is remains to show that the error term in (
4.2
) is
o(\hat{h}(0)\log^{2}T)
. We will show that it is actually bounded by
\hat{h}(0)
times a negative power of
T
.

Write
\Omega=\{z\in\mathcal{F}:B-T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\Im(z)\leq B+T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\}
and note that
\mu(\Omega)\ll T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
. The first error term in (
4.2
) is bounded by
\hat{h}(0)T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
. By Hölder’s inequality and (
1.2
), the second error term is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}1\ d\mu z\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\mathcal{F}}|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|^{4}d\mu z\Bigg)^{\frac{3}{4}}dA\ll\hat{h}(0)(T^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}})^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log^{2}T)^{\frac{3}{4}}\ll\hat{h}(0)T^{-\frac{\alpha}{9}}.

∎

5.
Spectral decomposition of the fourth moment

Let
\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{F}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}d\mu(z)
denote Petersson’s inner product. The starting point is Parseval’s identity

\|E_{A}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{4}^{4}=\int_{\mathcal{F}}|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|^{4}d\mu z

=|\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),(\tfrac{3}{\pi})^{1/2}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 1}|\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),E(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\rangle|^{2}dt.

For a Hecke-Maass cusp form
u_{j}(z)
we have

\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle=\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{A}}2e(y,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)u_{j}(z)d\mu z.

Thus if we denote

H_{A}(z)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\;z\in\mathcal{F}_{A},\\
2e(y,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),&\text{if}\;z\in\mathcal{C}_{A},\\
\end{cases}

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}|\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle-\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}-2\Re\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}\overline{\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle}\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle\right).

We rewrite the middle sum by employing Parseval’s identity again:

\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}=\langle H_{A},H_{A}\rangle-\left|\langle H_{A},(\tfrac{3}{\pi})^{1/2}\rangle\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\langle H_{A},E(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\rangle\right|^{2}dt.

From the Fourier expansion (
3.1
) we see that the function
H_{A}(z)
is orthogonal to constants, and thus we arrive at the following expression:

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
A>1
, we have

\displaystyle\|E_{A}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\|_{4}^{4}=

\displaystyle|\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),(\tfrac{3}{\pi})^{1/2}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\langle H_{A},H_{A}\rangle-2\Re\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}\overline{\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle}\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),E(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\rangle|^{2}dt-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\langle H_{A},E(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\rangle\right|^{2}dt.

We denote the ‘cross-term’ in the parentheses in the second line by

\Xi(A):=\sum_{j\geq 1}\overline{\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle}\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle.

For the first term we have

Proposition 5.2
.

For any
A>0
, as
T\rightarrow\infty
, we have

|\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),(\tfrac{3}{\pi})^{1/2}\rangle|^{2}\sim\frac{12}{\pi}(\log T)^{2}.

Proof.

We start with the Maass-Selberg relation:
for
s_{1}\neq s_{2}
,
s_{1}+s_{2}\neq 1
, we have that

\int_{\mathcal{F}}E_{A}(z,s_{1})E_{A}(z,s_{2})d\mu z=\frac{A^{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}-\varphi(s_{1})\varphi(s_{2})A^{1-s_{1}-s_{2}}}{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}+\frac{\varphi(s_{2})A^{s_{1}-s_{2}}-\varphi(s_{1})A^{s_{2}-s_{1}}}{s_{1}-s_{2}},

where
\varphi(s)=\frac{\xi(2s-1)}{\xi(2s)}
. We apply it to
s_{1}=\frac{1}{2}+\delta+iT\neq s_{2}=\frac{1}{2}+iT
and take the limit
\delta\rightarrow 0
, getting

\varphi(\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\left[2\log A-\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{\varphi}(\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\right]+\frac{A^{2iT}-\varphi(\frac{1}{2}+iT)^{2}A^{-2iT}}{2iT}.

Using Stirling’s approximation
\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(\frac{1}{2}+iT)=\log T+O(1)
and Vinogradov’s estimate
\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\frac{1}{2}+iT)\ll(\log T)^{\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}
, we obtain

-\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{\varphi}(\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)=2\log T+O((\log T)^{\frac{2}{3}+\epsilon}).

Hence as
T\rightarrow\infty
,

\langle E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),1\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{F}}E_{A}^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)d\mu z\sim 2\,\varphi(\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\log T.

The result follows since
|\varphi(\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|=1
.

∎

For
u_{j}
even we have by Rankin-Selberg integration (also called unfolding), cf.
[
5
, Lemma 4.1]
, that

(5.1)

\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle=\frac{{\rho_{j}(1)}}{2}\frac{\Lambda(\frac{1}{2},u_{j})\Lambda(\frac{1}{2}+2iT,u_{j})}{\xi^{2}(1+2iT)},

while for
u_{j}
odd the triple product vanishes. The following asymptotic formula is the main result of
[

]
:

Proposition 5.3
.

[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
As
T\rightarrow\infty
, we have

(5.2)

\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle\right|^{2}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{{|\rho_{j}(1)|^{2}}}{4}\frac{\Lambda^{2}(\frac{1}{2},u_{j})|\Lambda(\frac{1}{2}+2iT,u_{j})|^{2}}{|\xi(1+2iT)|^{4}}\sim\frac{48}{\pi}\log^{2}T.

Next we need the asymptotic evaluation as
T\rightarrow\infty
of the integral

\displaystyle\langle H_{A},H_{A}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{A}}4|e(y,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|^{2}d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{A}}4\left(2y+{\bf c}y^{1-2iT}+\overline{{\bf c}}y^{1+2iT}\right)|E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)|^{2}d\mu(z).

Since
\xi(1+2iT)E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)
is real-valued, this integral is
exactly
equal
\frac{2}{3}
times the integral at the bottom of page 251 in
[
5
]
. The asymptotic formula for that integral was calculated there, in

. Therefore, we get the following proposition:

.

For any

, as

, we have

\langle H_{A},H_{A}\rangle\sim\frac{24}{\pi}(\log T)^{2}.

Finally, the two continuous spectrum contributions in Lemma

are asymptotically negligible. Namely,

proves that there exists a positive number

such that, as

In particular, this bound is

.
Moreover, Spinu proves in

6.1.
Reduction of the cross-term to the bulk range

Starting with (

) and analyzing the size of the corresponding gamma functions, one can see
[
17
, Section 5.1.1]
that the sum on the left hand side of (

) is supported on

for any fixed

, up to an error of

say. Moreover, it has been shown that

\left(\sum_{|t_{j}|<T^{1-\epsilon}}+\sum_{2T-T^{1-\epsilon}<|t_{j}|<2T+T^{\epsilon}}\right)|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\ll T^{-\delta}

for some
\delta>0
.
For this see
[
11
, Sections 3.6 and 3.7]
. For brevity, we denote the spectral sum on the left hand side by
\sum^{\flat}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz, we have that the corresponding part of the cross-term
\Xi(A)
is also negligible:

\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}\overline{\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle}\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle

\ll\left(\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}|\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}|\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\ll T^{-\delta/2}\langle H_{A},H_{A}\rangle^{1/2}\ll T^{-\delta/3},

since
\langle H_{A},H_{A}\rangle^{1/2}\ll\log T
by Proposition
5.4
. Therefore it suffices to restrict the cross-term to the range

(6.1)

\displaystyle T^{1-\alpha}\leq t_{j}\leq 2T-T^{1-\alpha},

for any fixed
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{100}
. We refer to this interval of
t_{j}
as the ‘bulk range’, and we can essentially pick it out with the smooth function
W(t)
constructed in
[
4
, Lemma 5.1]
:

(6.2)

\Xi(A)\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 1\end{subarray}}W(t_{j})\overline{\langle E^{2}(\cdot,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT),u_{j}\rangle}\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle.

Recall that the function
W
is explicitly given by

(6.3)

\displaystyle W(t)=W_{\alpha}(t)=\left(1-\exp\left(-\left(\frac{t}{(2T)^{1-\alpha/2}}\right)^{2\lceil 1000/\alpha\rceil}\right)\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\left(\frac{4T^{2}-t^{2}}{4T^{2-\alpha/2}}\right)^{2\lceil 1000/\alpha\rceil}\right)\right).

We have that
W(t)
is
O(T^{-100})
unless
T^{1-\alpha}\leq|t|\leq 2T-T^{1-\alpha}
, while
W(t)=1+O(T^{-100})
for

is the same parameter as in Proposition
4.1
.

6.2.
The projection onto a cusp form: a formula for
\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle

Let
u_{j}(z)
be an even or odd Hecke-Maass cusp form for the group
\Gamma
.
Using Fourier expansions in the cuspidal region
\mathcal{C}_{A}
, we get for any
A>1

\displaystyle\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{A}}2e(y,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)E_{A}(z,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)u_{j}(z)d\mu z

\displaystyle=\int_{A}^{\infty}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}2e(y,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)\frac{2}{\xi(1+2iT)}\sum_{n\neq 0}\tau(|n|,T)\sqrt{y}K_{iT}(2\pi|n|y)e(nx)

\displaystyle\quad\times\rho_{j}(1)\sum_{m\neq 0}\lambda_{j}(m)\sqrt{y}K_{it_{j}}(2\pi|m|y)e(mx)dx\frac{dy}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\rho_{j}(1)}{\xi(1+2iT)}\sum_{n\neq 0}\tau(|n|,T)\lambda_{j}(-n)\int_{A}^{\infty}e(y,\tfrac{1}{2}+iT)K_{iT}(2\pi|n|y)K_{it_{j}}(2\pi|n|y)\frac{dy}{y}.

From here we see that
\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle

vanishes for
odd

u_{j}
(since then
\lambda_{j}(-n)=-\lambda_{j}(n)
), while for
u_{j}

even
, we have

\displaystyle\langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle=\frac{8\rho_{j}(1)}{\xi(1+2iT)}\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau(n,T)\lambda_{j}(n)\int_{A}^{\infty}(y^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}+{\bf c}\cdot y^{\frac{1}{2}-iT})K_{iT}(2\pi ny)K_{it_{j}}(2\pi ny)\frac{dy}{y}.

The integrals can be expressed as follows, where we follow the type of argument given in
[
17
, equations (2.26)–(2.28)]
. Denote for
x>0
,

g(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}K_{iT}(y)K_{it_{j}}(y)\frac{dy}{y},

and note that this converges absolutely since
K_{ir}(y)\ll_{r}e^{-y}
for
r\geq 1
and
y>0
(see for example
[
2
, equations (14) and (25)]
). Thus
g(x)\ll_{T}e^{-x}
for
x>0
, and so the Mellin transform

G(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)x^{s-1}dx

converges absolutely for
\Re(s)>0
. Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)x^{s-1}dx=-\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{\prime}(x)x^{s}dx=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s+\frac{1}{2}+iT}K_{iT}(x)K_{it_{j}}(x)\frac{dx}{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{s-\frac{5}{2}+iT}}{s}\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}+2iT\pm it_{j}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}\pm it_{j}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2}+iT)},

where we used
the Mellin-Barnes formula
[
7
, 6.576.4]
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s}K_{\mu}(x)K_{\nu}(x)\frac{dx}{x}=\frac{2^{s-3}}{\Gamma(s)}\prod_{\pm,\pm}\Gamma\left(\frac{s\pm\mu\pm\nu}{2}\right).

Then by the inverse Mellin transform we have for
\sigma>0
that

g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}G(s)x^{-s}ds
, which converges absolutely since
G(s)
decays exponentially as
|\Im(s)|\to\infty
, by Stirling’s approximation. Hence we get that for
u_{j}
even and
\sigma>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\ \langle H_{A},u_{j}\rangle\ dA

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{j}(1)}{\xi(1+2iT)}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)\lambda_{j}(n)}{(\pi n)^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{h(A)}{(A\pi n)^{s}}dA\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}+2iT\pm it_{j}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}\pm it_{j}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2}+iT)}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle+{\bf c}\cdot\frac{\rho_{j}(1)}{\xi(1+2iT)}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)\lambda_{j}(n)}{(\pi n)^{\frac{1}{2}-iT}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{h(A)}{(A\pi n)^{s}}dA\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}-2iT\pm it_{j}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}\pm it_{j}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2}-iT)}\frac{ds}{s}.

We introduce the following notation

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{+}(s,t)=\tilde{h}(1-s)\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}+2iT\pm it}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}\pm it}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2}+iT)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+iT)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+it)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{-}(s,t)=\tilde{h}(1-s)\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}-2iT\pm it}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\frac{1}{2}\pm it}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{1}{2}-iT)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+iT)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+it)},

where

for any

Then we denote for

The following result, afforded by the averaging over

\displaystyle\sim\frac{24}{\pi^{3}}\hat{h}(0)\zeta(1+2iT)\zeta(1-2iT)^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)}{(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\log^{2}\left(\frac{t(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T}\right)dt

\displaystyle\sim\frac{24}{\pi^{3}}\hat{h}(0)\zeta(1+2iT)\zeta(1-2iT)^{2}\int_{T^{1-\frac{\alpha}{4}}}^{2T-T^{1-\frac{\alpha}{4}}}\frac{\log^{2}(t)}{(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}dt

\displaystyle\sim\frac{24}{\pi^{3}}\hat{h}(0)\zeta(1+2iT)\zeta(1-2iT)^{2}\left(\int_{T^{1-\frac{\alpha}{4}}}^{T^{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log T}}}}+\int_{T^{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log T}}}}^{2T-T^{1-\frac{\alpha}{4}}}\right)\frac{\log^{2}(t)}{(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}dt.

The contribution of the first integral in the last line is

\ll T^{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log T}}}\hat{h}(0)|\zeta(1+2iT)|^{3}\log^{2}T\ll\hat{h}(0).

In the second integral, we write
\log t=\log T+O(\sqrt{\log T})
. The integral can then be asymptotically evaluated using the arcsine function, as in
[
4
, Section 6]
, to give

(8.5)

\mathcal{D}_{++}\sim\hat{h}(0)\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}\zeta(1+2iT)\zeta(1-2iT)^{2}\log^{2}T.

8.2.
Diagonal
\mathcal{D}_{-+}

The treatment of
\mathcal{D}_{-+}
has a crucial difference to that of
\mathcal{D}_{++}
. Whereas in the previous subsection we needed Weyl strength subconvexity bounds for the Riemann zeta function, here we will need the much deeper sub-Weyl subconvexity.

The diagonal contribution arising by applying (
7.1
) to

\Xi_{-+}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}k^{1+2iT}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\tau(m,T)}{m^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{j}(n)\lambda_{j}(m)}{L(1,\text{sym}^{2}u_{j})}W(t_{j})\mathcal{H}(t_{j})V_{-}(k^{2}n,t_{j})\mathcal{V}_{+}(m,t_{j})

is, for any
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{-+}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)^{2}}{n^{1+2iT}k^{1+2iT}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W(t)\mathcal{H}(t)V_{-}(k^{2}n,t)\mathcal{V}_{+}(n,t)\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi^{2}}.

By (
6.14
) and (
6.19
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{-+}\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)^{2}}{n^{1+2iT}k^{1+2iT}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)V_{-}(t)}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{1})}e^{w^{2}+i\frac{\pi}{2}w}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4\pi^{2}k^{2}n}\right)^{w}\frac{dw}{w}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{2})}8\pi\tilde{h}(1-s)\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4T}\right)^{s}n^{-s}\frac{ds}{s}\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)V_{-}(t)}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{1})}e^{w^{2}+i\frac{\pi}{2}w}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{w}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k^{1+2iT+2w}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{2})}8\pi\tilde{h}(1-s)\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4T}\right)^{s}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)^{2}}{n^{1+2iT+w+s}}\frac{ds}{s}\frac{dw}{w}\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)V_{-}(t)}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{1})}e^{w^{2}+i\frac{\pi}{2}w}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{2})}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4}\right)^{w+s}8\pi\tilde{h}(1-s)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\zeta(1+2iT+2w)\zeta(1+w+s)\zeta(1+2iT+w+s)^{2}\zeta(1+4iT+w+s)}{\zeta(2+4iT+2w+2s)}\frac{ds}{s}\frac{dw}{w}\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi^{2}}.

Let

\sigma_{1}=\frac{1}{101}\;<\;\sigma_{2}=\frac{1}{100}
. We first move the
w
-integral to the left, to the line
\Re(w)=-\sigma_{1}
. In doing so, we cross simple poles at
w=0
and
w=-iT
. By the same argument as given for
\mathcal{D}_{-+}
in the previous subsection, and keeping in mind that
|V_{-}(t)|=1
by (
6.15
), we have that the shifted integral and residue at
w=-iT
contribute
O(T^{-\alpha})
if
\alpha
is chosen small enough. Thus we need only consider the contribution of the simple pole at
w=0
, which equals

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)V_{-}(t)}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{2})}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{s}8\pi\tilde{h}(1-s)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\zeta(1+2iT)\zeta(1+s)\zeta(1+2iT+s)^{2}\zeta(1+4iT+s)}{\zeta(2+4iT+2s)}\frac{ds}{s}\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi^{2}}.

Next, we move the contour to the left to
\Re(s)=-\sigma_{2}<0
, crossing a double pole at
s=0
, a double pole at
s=-2iT
and a simple pole at
s=-4iT
. We can immediately say that the poles at at
s=-2iT
and
s=-4iT
contribute a negligible amount, by Lemma
6.1
. Thus we only need to address the contributions of the double pole at
s=0
and of the new integral at
\Re(s)=-\sigma_{2}<0
.

For the pole at
s=0
we follow the same type of calculations as we had for (
8.5
), but this time we have a double pole instead of a triple pole, so get that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)V_{-}(t)}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\zeta(1+2iT)

\displaystyle\times\underset{s=0}{\mathrm{Res}}\left[\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{s}\frac{8\pi\tilde{h}(1-s)}{s}\frac{\zeta(1+s)\zeta(1+2iT+s)^{2}\zeta(1+4iT+s)}{\zeta(2+4iT+2s)}\right]\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\hat{h}(0)|\zeta(1+4iT)||\zeta(1+2iT)|^{3}\log T\ll\hat{h}(0)|\zeta(1+2iT)|^{3}(\log T)^{\frac{5}{3}+\epsilon}

and this is smaller in size than the main term (
8.5
) that we saw for
\mathcal{D}_{++}
.

It remains to consider the contribution of the shifted integral,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)|V_{-}(t)|}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\mathcal{IR}(t)|d^{*}t\ll T^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W(t)|\mathcal{IR}(t)|dt,

where, following the same type of notation as in the previous subsection, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{IR}(t):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(-\sigma_{2})}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4T}\right)^{s}8\pi\tilde{h}(1-s)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\zeta(1+2iT)\zeta(1+s)\zeta(1+2iT+s)^{2}\zeta(1+4iT+s)}{\zeta(2+4iT+2s)}\frac{ds}{s}.

The subconvexity estimate
\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+it)\ll|t|^{\frac{13}{84}+\epsilon}
from
[
3
]
(of sub-Weyl quality!) and the Phragmén-Lindelöf principle together give that
\zeta(1-\sigma+it)\ll|t|^{\frac{13}{42}\sigma+\epsilon}
, for
0\leq\sigma\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
We apply this estimate to three (of the four) zeta factors in the numerator to get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IR}(t)|

\displaystyle\ll T^{\frac{39}{42}\sigma_{2}+\epsilon}\int_{-T^{-\alpha}}^{T^{\alpha}}|\tilde{h}(1-\sigma_{2}-iy)||\zeta(1-\sigma_{2}+iy)|\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4T}\right)^{-\sigma_{2}}\frac{dy}{1+|y|}

\displaystyle\ll T^{\frac{39}{42}\sigma_{2}-(1-\alpha)\sigma_{2}+\epsilon}\int_{-T^{-\alpha}}^{T^{\alpha}}\frac{|\zeta(1-\sigma_{2}+iy)|}{1+|y|}dy

\displaystyle\ll T^{\frac{39}{42}\sigma_{2}-(1-\alpha)\sigma_{2}+\epsilon}\ll T^{-\frac{1}{1400}+\frac{\alpha}{100}+\epsilon},

so that

T^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W(t)|\mathcal{IR}(t)|dt\ll T^{-1-\frac{1}{1400}+\frac{\alpha}{100}+\epsilon}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W(t)dt\ll T^{-\frac{1}{1400}+\frac{\alpha}{100}+\epsilon},

which is
O(T^{-\alpha})
for
\alpha
small enough.

8.3.
Diagonal
\mathcal{D}_{+-}

The argument is the same as that given in the previous subsection for
\mathcal{D}_{-+}
, and we get

\mathcal{D}_{+-}\ll\hat{h}(0)|\zeta(1+2iT)|^{3}(\log T)^{\frac{5}{3}+\epsilon}.

8.4.
Diagonal
\mathcal{D}_{--}

The argument here is analogous to the one given previously for
\mathcal{D}_{++}
and as in that case, we get a main term.
Using (
6.20
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{--}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)T^{2iT}\pi^{-4iT}e^{-2iT}}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{1})}e^{w^{2}-i\frac{\pi}{2}w-i\pi s}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{2})}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4}\right)^{w+s}\pi^{-2w}T^{-s}

\displaystyle\times 8\pi\tilde{h}(1-s)\frac{\zeta(1+2iT+2w)\zeta(1+w+s)^{2}\zeta(1+w+s+2iT)\zeta(1+w+s-2iT)}{\zeta(2+2w+2s)}\frac{ds}{s}\frac{dw}{w}\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi^{2}}.

As before, we let
\sigma_{1}=\frac{1}{101}\;<\;\sigma_{2}=\frac{1}{100}
and then shift the contours left. The main contribution arises from the simple pole at
w=0
and triple pole at
s=0
. This is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{--}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)T^{2iT}\pi^{-4iT}e^{-2iT}}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}4\pi\hat{h}(0)\frac{\zeta(1+2iT)^{2}\zeta(1-2iT)}{\zeta(2)}\log^{2}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{T}\right)\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\hat{h}(0)\pi^{-4iT}e^{-2iT}T^{2iT}\frac{12}{\pi^{2}}\zeta(1+2iT)^{2}\zeta(1-2iT)\log^{2}T,

by the same final calculation leading to (
8.5
).

8.5.
The total diagonal contribution

We collect all diagonal contributions from the sum (
6.12
):

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\zeta^{2}(1-2iT)\zeta(1+2iT)}

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{D}_{++}+\mathcal{D}_{-+}+{\bf c}\pi^{2iT}\mathcal{D}_{+-}+{\bf c}\pi^{2iT}\mathcal{D}_{--}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\pi}{\zeta^{2}(1-2iT)\zeta(1+2iT)}\left(\mathcal{D}_{++}+{\bf c}\pi^{2iT}\mathcal{D}_{--}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\hat{h}(0)\frac{12}{\pi}(\log T)^{2}\left(1+\frac{\xi(1-2iT)}{\xi(1+2iT)}\pi^{-2iT}e^{-2iT}T^{2iT}\frac{\zeta(1+2iT)}{\zeta(1-2iT)}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\hat{h}(0)\frac{12}{\pi}(\log T)^{2}\left(1+\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-iT)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+iT)}e^{-2iT}T^{2iT}\right)

(8.6)

\displaystyle\sim\hat{h}(0)\frac{24}{\pi}(\log T)^{2},

where the last line follows
by Stirling’s approximation (
3.3
).

9.
The Eisenstein series contribution

We recombine the Eisenstein series contributions from
\Xi_{++}
and
\Xi_{-+}
(while for
\Xi_{+-}
and
\Xi_{--}
, one proceeds analogously) to get that

\mathcal{E}_{++}+\mathcal{E}_{-+}
is equal to

-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W(t)\mathcal{H}(t)}{|\zeta(1+2it)|^{2}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)\tau(n,t)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}-iT}k^{1-2iT}}V_{+}(k^{2}n,t)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)\tau(n,t)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}k^{1+2iT}}V_{-}(k^{2}n,t)\right)

\times\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\tau(m,T)\tau(m,-t)}{m^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}}\mathcal{V}_{+}(m,t)\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

The sum of double sums over
n
and
k
is an approximate functional equation (see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 5.3]
) of
|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT+it)\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT-it)
, and by Ramanujan’s identity (
8.1
),

\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\tau(m,T)\tau(m,-t)}{m^{\frac{1}{2}+iT+s}}=\frac{\zeta(\frac{1}{2}-it+s)\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+it+s)\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+2iT-it+s)\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+2iT+it+s)}{\zeta(1+2iT+2s)}.

Therefore for
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\zeta^{2}(1-2iT)\zeta(1+2iT)}\left(\mathcal{E}_{++}+\mathcal{E}_{-+}\right)

\displaystyle=-\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W(t)\frac{|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT+it)\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT-it)}{|\zeta(1+2it)|^{2}\zeta^{2}(1-2iT)\zeta(1+2iT)}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}\mathcal{H}(t)\mathcal{H}_{+}(s,t)

(9.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\zeta(\frac{1}{2}-it+s)\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+it+s)\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+2iT-it+s)\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+2iT+it+s)}{\zeta(1+2iT+2s)}\frac{ds}{s}\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

Since
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
, all the zeta factors in the numerator in the third line are absolutely bounded. So by (
6.19
), the
s
-integral is
O(T^{-2+\sigma+\epsilon})
uniformly for any
t
in the bulk range. The zeta values
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT+it)
,
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}-2iT-it)
are each
O(T^{\frac{1}{6}+\epsilon})
by Weyl’s bound (although here, any subconvexity bound would suffice). Thus

\frac{\mathcal{E}_{++}+\mathcal{E}_{-+}}{\zeta^{2}(1-2iT)\zeta(1+2iT)}\ll T^{-2+\sigma+\frac{1}{3}+\epsilon}\int_{-2T}^{2T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}dt\ll T^{-\frac{2}{3}+\sigma+\epsilon},

which is admissible if we choose
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{100}
, say.

10.
The off-diagonal contribution

We treat only the sum
\mathcal{O}_{++}
as the other three cases are similar. We will show that

(10.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{++}\ll T^{-1},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{++}=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\sum_{n,m,k,c\geq 1}\frac{\tau(n,T)\tau(m,T)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}-iT}k^{1-2iT}m^{\frac{1}{2}+iT}}\frac{S(n,m;c)}{c}

(10.2)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{J}\left(\frac{\sqrt{nm}}{c},t\right)W(t)\mathcal{H}(t)V_{+}(k^{2}n,t)\mathcal{V}_{+}(m,t)\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi}\ dA.

By the properties of the weight functions
V_{+}(nk^{2},t)
and
\mathcal{V}_{+}(m,t)
given in Section
6.6
, we may restrict the summation in (
10.2
) to
nk^{2}<T^{2+\epsilon}
and
m<T^{1+\epsilon}
, up to an error of size
O(T^{-100})
. By a standard method we may also restrict to
c<T^{3}
, see e.g. the paragraph at the top of page 1495 in
[
4
]
.

Recall that by Lemma
6.1
, we may assume that
|s|<T^{\alpha}
in the definition (
6.6
) of
\mathcal{V}_{+}(m,t)
, up to negligible error. Also recall that in the definition (
6.10
) of
V_{+}(k^{2}n,t)
we may assume that
|w|<T^{\epsilon}
, by the rapid decay of
e^{w^{2}}
in vertical lines.

Let
Z
be a function as described in Lemma
10.1
below, so that
Z(\frac{t}{T})
is supported on
T^{1-2\alpha}<|t|<T^{1+2\alpha}
. Since
W(t)
is already supported on
T^{1-\alpha}\leq|t|\leq 2T-T^{1-\alpha}
up to an error of
O(T^{-100})
, we may insert
Z(\frac{t}{T})
into (
10.2
) up to
O(T^{-100})
error. Thus to establish (
10.1
), it suffices to show that for any
s,w\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s),\Re(w)>0
fixed,
|w|<T^{\epsilon}
,
|s|<T^{\alpha}
, we have

(10.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}nk^{2}<T^{2+\epsilon}\\
m<T^{1+\epsilon}\\
c<T^{3}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau(n,T)\tau(m,T)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}-iT+w}k^{1-2iT+2w}m^{\frac{1}{2}+iT+s}}\frac{S(n,m;c)}{c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{J}\left(\frac{\sqrt{nm}}{c},t\right)Z\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)W(t)\mathcal{H}(t)\mathcal{H}_{+}(s,t)\\
\times e^{w^{2}}\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}+w\pm it)\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}-2iT+w\pm it)}{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}\pm it)\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}-2iT\pm it)}\frac{d^{*}t}{2\pi}\ \ll T^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Next we recall the following information about the
J
-Bessel function transform:

Lemma 10.1
.

[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
Let
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{100}
. For any
x>0
and any smooth, even function
Z
compactly
supported on
(T^{-2\alpha},T^{2\alpha})\cup(-T^{2\alpha},-T^{-2\alpha})
with derivatives satisfying
\|Z^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\ll_{k}(T^{2\alpha})^{k}
for
k\geq 0
, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{J_{2it}(2\pi x)}{\cosh(\pi t)}Z\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)t\,dt

=\frac{-i\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\frac{T^{2}}{\sqrt{x}}\Re\left((1+i)e(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}tZ(t)e\left(\frac{-t^{2}T^{2}}{2\pi^{2}x}\right)dt\right)+O\left(\frac{x}{T^{3-24\alpha}}\right)+O(T^{-100}).

The main term is
O(T^{-100})
if
x<T^{2-6\alpha}
.

First, we need to show that Lemma
10.1
applies to (
10.3
), and for this we need to show that the functions

W(t),\ \ \ e^{w^{2}}\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}+w\pm it)\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}-2iT+w\pm it)}{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}\pm it)\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}-2iT\pm it)},\ \ \ \mathcal{H}(t)\mathcal{H}_{+}(s,t)

can be ‘absorbed’ into
Z(\frac{t}{T})
, which is to say that they satisfy the same bounds as

\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}Z\Big(\frac{t}{T}\Big)\ll_{k}T^{k(-1+2\alpha)}.

For
W(t)
, this was already observed in
[
4
, Lemma 5.1]
. For the remaining functions, this can easily be checked after using Stirling’s expansion. The leading terms are, by (
6.14
) and (
6.19
),

e^{w^{2}}\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}+w\pm it)\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}-2iT+w\pm it)}{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}\pm it)\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{1}{2}-2iT\pm it)}=e^{w^{2}+i\frac{\pi}{2}w}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{w}+\ldots

and

\mathcal{H}(t)\mathcal{H}_{+}(s,t)=\frac{8\pi\tilde{h}(1-s)}{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{|t|(4T^{2}-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{4T}\right)^{s}+\ldots

for
t
in the bulk range. Keep in mind that
|s|<T^{\alpha}
, which is crucial to control the size of derivatives with respect to
t
.

Since
\frac{\sqrt{nm}}{c}<T^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}
for all pairs
n,m
in our summation (
10.3
), we see that the main term from Lemma
10.1
gives a total contribution of
O(T^{-50})
and the error term
O(\frac{\sqrt{nm}}{cT^{3-24\alpha}})
gives a contribution of

O\left(\frac{T^{\epsilon}}{T^{2-\alpha}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}nk^{2}<T^{2+\epsilon}\\
m<T^{1+\epsilon}\\
c<T^{3}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\tau(n,T)\tau(m,T)|}{(nm)^{\frac{1}{2}}k}\frac{|S(n,m;c)|}{c}\frac{\sqrt{nm}}{cT^{3-24\alpha}}\right)=O(T^{-2+25\alpha+\epsilon}),

using Weil’s bound for Kloosterman sums. The bound is admissible since
\alpha<\frac{1}{100}
.

11.
Conclusion

Taking everything together,
by (
8.6
) and the upper bounds for the Eisenstein series and off-diagonal contributions, we obtain the following proposition:

Proposition 11.1
.

For
h
as in Proposition
4.1
,
as
T\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(A)\ \Xi(A)\ dA\sim\hat{h}(0)\frac{24}{\pi}(\log T)^{2}.

Now we are ready to prove the main Proposition.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

The proposition follows by combining Lemma
5.1
with (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and Propositions
5.2
,
5.3
,
5.4
and
11.1
.

Inertial Newton Algorithms Avoiding Strict Saddle Points
Camille Castera CNRS - IRITUniversité de ToulouseFrance

Abstract
We study the asymptotic behavior of second-order algorithms mixing Newton’s method and inertial gradient descent in non-convex landscapes. We show that, despite the Newtonian behavior of these methods, they almost always escape strict saddle points. We also evidence the role played by the hyper-parameters of these methods in their qualitative behavior near critical points. The theoretical results are supported by numerical illustrations.

Introduction

Designing algorithms for large-scale optimization remains a major challenge and is crucial for modern machine learning problems. Many of these problems amount to the unconstrained minimization of a so-called
loss function

\min_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{P}}\mathcal{J}(\theta).

(1)

Lots of efforts are put into building algorithms exploiting second-order derivatives of

As mentioned in the introduction, a powerful property of (

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}(t)&=-(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta})\theta(t)-\frac{1}{\beta}\psi(t)-\beta\nabla\mathcal{J}(\theta(t))\\

Since

3.1
DIN is Likely to Avoid Strict Saddle Points

We start with our main result regarding the limit of the solutions of DIN.

(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})\in\Phi(\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}\cap\mathsf{W}^{s}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})},-t)
, or more generally,

(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})\in\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\Phi\left(\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}\cap\mathsf{W}^{s}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})},-k\right),

(9)

where the right-hand side is the union over
k\in\mathbb{N}
of initial conditions such that the corresponding solution has reached
\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}\cap\mathsf{W}^{s}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}
at time
k
.

The set defined in the right-hand side of (
9
) has zero measure. Indeed, we showed that
(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathsf{S}_{<0}
implies that
DG(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})
has one eigenvalue with positive real part, so by Theorem
3.3
,
\mathsf{W}^{s}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}
has dimension strictly less than
2P
(the dimension of
\mathbb{R}^{P}\times\mathbb{R}^{P}
), hence this manifold has zero measure. Due to the uniqueness of the solution of (
3
), for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Phi(\cdot,-k)
is a local diffeomorphism, so
\Phi\left(\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}\cap\mathsf{W}^{s}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})},-k\right)
is also a zero-measure set. Finally, a countable union of zero-measure sets also has zero measure. So we proved that the set

\mathsf{I}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}=\left\{(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{P}\times\mathbb{R}^{P}\middle|\Phi((\theta_{0},\psi_{0}),t)\xrightarrow[t\to+\infty]{}(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\right\},

of initial conditions such that the associated solutions converge to
(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})
has measure zero.

Finally, since
\mathcal{J}
is a Morse function it has isolated critical points so the set
\mathsf{S}_{<0}
is countable and applying the reasoning above to any
(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathsf{S}_{<0}
, we get that
\bigcup_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathsf{S}_{<0}}\mathsf{I}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}
is a countable union of zero-measure sets, so it has zero measure. This set is the set of initializations whose associated solutions converge to a strict saddle point, which proves the theorem.
∎

We keep the same notations as in the proof of Theorem

5
Conclusion

We showed that DIN and INNA are likely to avoid strict saddle points despite their second-order nature, and even for functions with non-isolated critical points in the case of INNA. This makes them relevant to tackle non-convex minimization problems. We also provided new convergence results for INNA with fixed step-size and a qualitative analysis of the influence of
\alpha
and
\beta
on the solutions of DIN around minimizers.
As for future work, we could extend the results to INNA with varying step-sizes
\gamma_{k}
. We may do so in two cases where recent progress was made for GD: when
\gamma_{k}
is chosen via line-search
[
46
]
, and when
\gamma_{k}
asymptotically vanishes
[
38
]
. Finally, one may tackle the stochastic case where we intuitively expect that stochasticity actually helps INNA escaping saddle points as it is the case for stochastic GD
[
30
]
.

DG(\theta,\psi)=\begin{pmatrix}(1-\gamma(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta}))I_{P}-\gamma\beta\nabla^{2}\mathcal{J}(\theta)&&-\frac{\gamma}{\beta}I_{P}\\
-\gamma(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta})I_{P}&&(1-\frac{\gamma}{\beta})I_{P}\end{pmatrix}.

We can again block-diagonalize
DG(\theta,\psi)
(see (
7
)), in blocks of the form (up to symmetric permutations):
M_{p}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\gamma(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta})-\gamma\beta\lambda_{p}&&-\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\\
-\gamma(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta})&&1-\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\end{pmatrix}
, where
\lambda_{p}
is an eigenvalue of
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{J}(\theta)
.
To use the Theorem
4.2
, we need
G
to be a local diffeomorphism.

Theorem B.2
.

Under the same assumptions as that of Theorem
4.1
, the mapping
G
defined in (
17
) is a local diffeomorphism from
\mathbb{R}^{P}\times\mathbb{R}^{P}
to
\mathbb{R}^{P}\times\mathbb{R}^{P}
.

This result is proved later in Section
B.1
. We can now prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
such that the assumptions of the theorem hold and let
G
defined in (
17
) with these parameters. By Theorem
B.2
,
G
is a local diffeomorphism.
Let
(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathsf{S}_{<0}
, to use Theorem
4.2
we study the magnitude of the eigenvalues of
DG(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})
. Let
\lambda_{p}<0
be a negative eigenvalue of
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{J}(\theta^{\star})
, using the notations and elements stated in the beginning of this section, the eigenvalues of
M_{p}
are the roots of

\chi_{M_{p}}(X)=X^{2}-\mathrm{trace}(M_{p})X+\det(M_{p})=X^{2}-(2-\gamma(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p}))X+1-\gamma(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p})+\gamma^{2}\lambda_{p}.

The discriminant of
\chi_{M_{p}}
is:

\Delta_{M_{p}}=(2-\gamma(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p}))^{2}-4(1-\gamma(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p})+\gamma^{2}\lambda_{p})=\gamma^{2}\left((\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p})^{2}-4\lambda_{p}\right).

Remark that Lemma
3.5
gives again the sign of
\Delta_{M_{p}}
. Thus since
\lambda_{p}<0
, we necessarily have
\Delta_{M_{p}}\geq 0
, and can ignore the case
\Delta_{M_{p}}<0
.
So
M_{p}
has two real eigenvalues,

\begin{cases}\sigma_{p,+}&=1-\frac{1}{2}\gamma(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p})+\frac{1}{2}\gamma\sqrt{(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p})^{2}-4\lambda_{p}}\\
\sigma_{p,-}&=1-\frac{1}{2}\gamma(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p})-\frac{1}{2}\gamma\sqrt{(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p})^{2}-4\lambda_{p}}\end{cases}.

Since
\lambda_{p}<0
, then
|\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p}|<\sqrt{(\alpha+\beta\lambda_{p})^{2}-4\lambda_{p}}
, so observe that
\sigma_{p,+}>1
and
\sigma_{p,-}<1
, so
DG(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})
has at least one eigenvalue with magnitude larger than one.

We can now use the stable manifold theorem, we omit some details since the arguments are the same as for the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
By Theorem
4.2
, around each
(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathsf{S}_{<0}
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}
on which the stable manifold theorem holds.
Denote by
\mathsf{A}

the possibly uncountable union of all these neighborhoods:
\mathsf{A}=\bigcup_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathsf{S}_{<0}}\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})}
. By Lemma
B.1
, there exists a countable sub-cover of this set,
i.e.
, there exists a sequence
(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathsf{S}_{<0}
such that

\mathsf{A}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}.

(18)

Let
(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathsf{S}_{<0}
, it need not be an element of
(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, but according to (
18
), there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})\in\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}
. Let
an initialization
(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})
such that the associated realization
(\theta_{k},\psi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of INNA converges to
(\theta^{\star},\psi^{\star})
. This means that there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\forall k\geq k_{0}
,
G^{k}(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}
and thus
G^{k}(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathsf{W}^{sc}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}
, where
\mathsf{W}^{sc}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}
is the stable manifold around
(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})
as defined in Theorem
4.2
.
By Theorem
B.2
,
G
is a local diffeomorphism, so we can reverse the iterations and obtain,

(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})\in\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}G^{-j}\left(\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}\cap\mathsf{W}^{sc}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}\right)
.
Since
(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})\in\mathsf{S}_{<0}

, so by Theorem
4.2
,

B.2
for all

,

is a local diffeomorphism, so the union above has zero measure. Using (
18
), the rest of the proof is then similar to the end of that of Theorem
3.1
since
\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}G^{-j}\left(\mathsf{\Omega}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}\cap\mathsf{W}^{sc}_{(\theta_{i}^{\star},\psi_{i}^{\star})}\right)\right]
is a countable union of zero-measure sets, so it has again measure zero.

\gamma

Appendix C
Proof of Convergence of INNA

To prove Theorem
4.3
, we will use the following lemma.

Lemma C.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

If a bounded sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{P}
satisfies,

\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\|u_{k+1}-u_{k}\|=0,

then the set of accumulation points of
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is connected. If this set is finite then it reduces to a singleton and
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Assume that Assumption
1
holds and
\alpha>0
. Let
(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{P}\times\mathbb{R}^{P}
, and let
\gamma>0
such that (
15
) holds.
Let
(\theta_{k},\psi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence generated by INNA initialized at
(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})
. We first show that the sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
defined
\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
by

\mathcal{E}_{k}=(1+\alpha\beta-\gamma\alpha)\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})+\frac{1}{2}\|(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta})\theta_{k}+\frac{1}{\beta}\psi_{k}\|^{2}

(21)

converges. The sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
represents an “energy” that decreases along the iterations, where the first and second terms in (
21
) represent “potential” and “kinetic” energies respectively. This sequence resembles the Lyapunov function of DIN
[
3
,
19
]
but is more involved to derive, as often for algorithms compared to ODEs.
We use the notations
a=\alpha-1/\beta
,
b=1/\beta
,
\Delta\theta_{k}=\theta_{k+1}-\theta_{k}
and
\Delta\psi_{k}=\psi_{k+1}-\psi_{k}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, so that INNA is rewritten as:

\begin{cases}\Delta\psi_{k}&=-\gamma a\theta_{k}-\gamma b\psi_{k}\\
\Delta\theta_{k}&=\Delta\psi_{k}-\gamma\beta\nabla\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})\end{cases}.

(22)

Also denote
\mu=1+\alpha\beta-\gamma\alpha
, where
\mu>0
since
\gamma<1/\alpha+\beta
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we will prove
\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}\leq 0
. From Assumption
1
follows a
descent lemma
(see
[
16
, Proposition A.24]
):

\mu\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k+1})-\mu\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})\leq\mu\langle\nabla\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k}),\Delta\theta_{k}\rangle+\frac{\mu L}{2}\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2},

which according to (
22
), can equivalently be rewritten as,

\mu\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k+1})-\mu\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})\leq-\mu\langle\frac{\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}}{\gamma\beta},\Delta\theta_{k}\rangle+\frac{\mu L}{2}\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}.

(23)

We save this for later and now turn our attention to the other term in
\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}
,

\frac{1}{2}\|a\theta_{k+1}+b\psi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|a\theta_{k}+b\psi_{k}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|a\theta_{k}+a\Delta\theta_{k}+b\psi_{k}+b\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|a\theta_{k}+b\psi_{k}\|^{2}.

Expanding this and using the fact that
a\theta_{k}+b\psi_{k}=-\Delta\psi_{k}/\gamma
, we can show that,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\|a\theta_{k}+a\Delta\theta_{k}+b\psi_{k}+b\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|a\theta_{k}+b\psi_{k}\|^{2}\\
=&\frac{a^{2}}{2}\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}\|\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}+ab\langle\Delta\theta_{k},\Delta\psi_{k}\rangle-\frac{a}{\gamma}\langle\Delta\theta_{k},\Delta\psi_{k}\rangle-\frac{b}{\gamma}\|\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(24)

We then use
\|\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}=\|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}+\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}-2\langle\Delta\theta_{k},\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\rangle
and
\langle\Delta\theta_{k},\Delta\psi_{k}\rangle=\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}-\langle\Delta\theta_{k},\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\rangle
in
(
24
) to obtain:

\frac{1}{2}\|a\theta_{k+1}+b\psi_{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|a\theta_{k}+b\psi_{k}\|^{2}=\left(\frac{a^{2}}{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}+ab-\frac{a}{\gamma}-\frac{b}{\gamma}\right)\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}\\
+\left(\frac{b^{2}}{2}-\frac{b}{\gamma}\right)\|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}+\left(-b^{2}-ab+\frac{a}{\gamma}+\frac{2b}{\gamma}\right)\langle\Delta\theta_{k},\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\rangle.

(25)

We then simplify the factors using the identity
a+b=\alpha
, as well as
\frac{a^{2}}{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}+ab=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)^{2}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}
, and
-b^{2}-ab=-\alpha/\beta
to deduce that (
25
) is equal to

\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\right)\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma-2\beta}{2\gamma\beta^{2}}\|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{-\gamma\alpha+\alpha\beta+1}{\gamma\beta}\langle\Delta\theta_{k},\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\rangle.

(26)

We can finally combine (
23
) and (
26
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}\leq&\left(\frac{\mu L}{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\right)\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma-2\beta}{2\gamma\beta^{2}}\|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}\\
&+\left(-\frac{\mu}{\gamma\beta}+\frac{1+\alpha\beta-\gamma\alpha}{\gamma\beta}\right)\langle\Delta\theta_{k},\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\rangle.\end{split}

(27)

Notice that,
\mu=1+\alpha\beta-\gamma\alpha
is specifically chosen so that the last term in (
27
) vanishes, so,

\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}\leq\frac{\gamma\mu L+\gamma\alpha^{2}-2\alpha}{2\gamma}\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma-2\beta}{2\gamma\beta^{2}}\|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}.

(28)

To prove the decrease of
(\mathcal{E}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, it remains to justify that both terms in (
28
) are negative. First, the condition
\gamma<2\beta
in (
15
) makes the second term negative. Then,

\gamma\mu L+\gamma\alpha^{2}-2\alpha<0\iff-\alpha L\gamma^{2}+\left(\alpha^{2}+(1+\alpha\beta)L\right)\gamma-2\alpha<0.

A simpler sufficient condition for this to hold is
\left(\alpha^{2}+(1+\alpha\beta)L\right)\gamma-2\alpha<0
or equivalently
\gamma<2\alpha/\left(\alpha^{2}+(1+\alpha\beta)L\right)
, which holds from (
15
).
So the sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a decreasing. It is also lower-bounded since
\mathcal{J}
is lower-bounded, so it converges.

The rest of the proof then relies on exploiting (
28
). Let
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we sum (
28
):

\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}\leq\frac{\gamma\mu L+\gamma\alpha^{2}-2\alpha}{2\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma-2\beta}{2\gamma\beta^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}\|^{2}.

The left-hand side is a telescopic series, and it follows from (
22
) that
\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\psi_{k}=-\gamma\beta\nabla\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})
, so denoting
C_{1}=-(\gamma\mu L+\gamma\alpha^{2}-2\alpha)/2\gamma>0
and
C_{2}=-(\gamma^{2}-2\gamma\beta)/2>0
,

\mathcal{E}_{0}-\mathcal{E}_{K+1}\geq C_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|\Delta\theta_{k}\|^{2}C_{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})\|^{2}.

Then,
\mathcal{E}_{0}-\mathcal{E}_{K+1}
is upper bounded since
(\mathcal{E}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges,
so
\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})\|^{2}<+\infty
. This implies that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})\|^{2}=0
and we deduce similarly that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\theta_{k+1}-\theta_{k}\|^{2}=0
.
Using (
14
), we also have,

\|(\alpha-\frac{1}{\beta})\theta_{k}+\frac{1}{\beta}\psi_{k}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\|\psi_{k+1}-\psi_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}\|\theta_{k+1}-\theta_{k}\|^{2}+2\beta^{2}\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k})\|^{2}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0.

(29)

The convergence of
(\mathcal{E}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and (
29
) imply that
(\mathcal{J}(\theta_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges, which proves the first part of the theorem.
Assume that the critical points are isolated and that the sequence
(\theta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{P}
.
According to Lemma
C.1
, since
(\theta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded and
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|\theta_{k+1}-\theta_{k}\|=0
, the set of accumulation points of
(\theta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is connected.
By continuity of
\nabla\mathcal{J}
, accumulation points of
(\theta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are critical points of
\mathcal{J}
, which are assumed to be isolated. So the set of accumulation points is a singleton and
(\theta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to it.
∎

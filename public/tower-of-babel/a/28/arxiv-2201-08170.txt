Unified Explanation of the Anomalies in Semi-Leptonic B B decays and the W W Mass Preprint: PSI-PR-22-02, ZU-TH 02/22
Marcel Algueró, Andreas Crivellin, Claudio Andrea Manzari, Joaquim Matias

Abstract
The discrepancies between the measurements of rare (semi-)leptonic B B decays and the corresponding Standard Model predictions point convincingly towards the existence of new physics for which a heavy neutral gauge boson ( Z ′ Z^{\prime} ) is a prime candidate. However, the effect of the mixing of the Z ′ Z^{\prime} with the SM Z Z , even though it cannot be avoided by any symmetry, is usually assumed to be small and thus neglected in phenomenological analyses. In this letter we point out that a mixing of the naturally expected size leads to lepton flavour universal contributions, providing a very good fit to B B data. Furthermore, the global electroweak fit is affected by Z − Z ′ Z-Z^{\prime} mixing where the tension in the W W mass, recently confirmed and strengthened by the CDF measurement, prefers a non-zero value of it. We find that a Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson with a mass between ≈ 1 − 5 ​ TeV \approx 1-5\rm{TeV} can provide a unified explanations of the B B anomalies and the W W mass. This strongly suggests that the breaking of the new gauge symmetry giving raise to the Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson is linked to electroweak symmetry breaking with intriguing consequences for model building.

II
Setup

We extend the SM by adding a heavy neutral
SU(2)_{L}
singlet gauge boson. Following the notation of Ref.
del Aguila et al. 2010
;
de Blas et al. 2013
the kinetic term and the mass term of this new boson, before EW symmetry breaking, are

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{Z^{\prime}_{0}}=&-\frac{1}{4}Z^{\prime}_{0,\mu\nu}Z_{0}^{\prime\mu\nu}+\frac{\mu_{Z}^{\prime 2}}{2}Z^{\prime}_{0\mu}Z^{\prime\mu}_{0}\,\\
&+g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{0\mu}Z^{\prime\mu}_{0}\phi^{\dagger}\phi-ig_{Z^{\prime}}^{\phi}Z^{\prime\mu}_{0}\phi^{\dagger}\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\phi\,,\end{aligned}

(1)

where
Z^{\prime}_{0,\mu\nu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}Z^{\prime}_{0\nu}-\partial_{\nu}Z^{\prime}_{0\mu}
is the field strength tensor,
\overset{\leftrightarrow}{D}_{\mu}\;=\overset{\rightarrow}{D}_{\mu}-(\overset{\leftarrow}{D}_{\mu})^{\dagger}
,
\phi
is the SM Higgs
SU(2)_{L}
doublet and we use

D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ig_{2}W^{a}_{\mu}T^{a}+ig_{1}YB_{\mu}

as the definition of the SM part of the covariant derivative and
g_{Z^{\prime}}^{\phi}
is real by hermicity. The physical
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
masses are obtained from diagonalizing the mass matrix

\mathcal{M}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}m_{Z_{0}}^{2}&-\frac{y}{c_{W}}\\
-\frac{y}{c_{W}}&m_{Z^{\prime}_{0}}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad y\equiv\frac{v^{2}}{2}\,g_{2}\,g_{Z^{\prime}}^{\phi}\,,

(2)

in the
Z_{0},\,Z_{0}^{\prime}
basis, where
Z_{0}
coincides with the the SM
Z
for
g_{Z^{\prime}}^{\phi}=0
with
m_{Z_{0}}^{2}=\frac{v^{2}}{4}\left(g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}\right)
,
\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}\approx 174\,
GeV and
c_{W}
is the cosine of the Weinberg angle. At leading order in
v/m_{Z_{0}^{\prime}}
we have

\displaystyle m_{Z}^{2}\simeq

\displaystyle\;m_{Z_{0}}^{2}-\frac{y^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}m_{Z_{0}^{\prime}}^{2}}\equiv m_{Z_{0}}^{2}\left(1+\delta m_{Z}^{2}\right)\,.

(3)

Note that the corrections to the mass of the
Z
with respect to the SM value
m_{Z_{0}}
can only be negative. The mass eigenstates
Z^{(\prime)}
can then be expressed as

\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}Z\\
{Z^{\prime}}\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{Z_{0}^{\prime}\sin\xi+Z_{0}\cos\xi}\\
{Z_{0}^{\prime}\cos\xi\;-Z_{0}\sin\xi}\end{array}}\right)\,,

(4)

where
\sin\xi\simeq\frac{y}{c_{W}m_{Z_{0}^{\prime}}^{2}}

describes the
Z-Z^{\prime}
mixing.

The interactions with the SM fields are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{Z^{\prime}_{0}}^{\rm fermions}=&{\bar{u}}_{j}{\gamma_{\mu}}(g_{ji}^{uL}{P_{L}}+g_{ji}^{uR}{P_{R}}){u_{i}}\,Z^{\prime\mu}_{0}\\
&+{\bar{d}}_{j}{\gamma_{\mu}}(g_{ji}^{dL}{P_{L}}+g_{ji}^{dR}{P_{R}}){d_{i}}\,Z^{\prime\mu}_{0}\\
&+g_{ji}^{\ell L}({\bar{\nu}}_{j}{\gamma_{\mu}}{P_{L}}{\nu_{i}})\,Z^{\prime\mu}_{0}\\
&+\bar{\ell}_{j}{\gamma_{\mu}}(g_{ji}^{\ell L}{P_{L}}+g_{ji}^{\ell R}{P_{R}}){\ell_{i}}\,Z^{\prime\mu}_{0}\,,\end{split}

(5)

where, in the down basis,
g_{ji}^{uL}=V_{jk}g_{kk^{\prime}}^{dL}V_{ik^{\prime}}^{*}
. Note that the couplings to left-handed charged leptons and neutrinos (up and down quarks) are the same (up to a CKM rotation), due to
SU(2)_{L}
invariance and that only the relative phase between
\sin\xi
and
g^{L,R}_{ij}
is physical, such that one can assume
\sin\xi
to be positive without loss of generality. In the following, we will assume flavour diagonal coupling to leptons and in the quark sector disregard all couplings except left-handed
b-s
couplings.

V
Conclusions and Outlook

In this article we systematically studied the impact of
Z-Z^{\prime}
mixing on the global fit to
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
data and EW precision observables. Concerning the former, we observe that a LFU effect is generated while in the latter the mixing leads to modified
Z
couplings and to an enhancement in the predicted
W
mass w.r.t. the SM, which accommodates the new experimental average (including the recent one from CDF). Therefore, while in previous analyses in the literature the effect of
Z-Z^{\prime}
mixing was usually assumed to be small and was therefore mostly neglected, we stress that both
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
data and the EW fit even prefers a small but non-zero value of the order of
10^{-3}
for
m_{Z^{\prime}}\approx 1{\rm TeV}-5{\rm TeV}
. Note that this is in agreement with the expectation
\sin\xi\approx g_{2}g^{\prime}m_{Z}^{2}/m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
for a TeV scale
Z^{\prime}
with order one couplings in case
U(1)^{\prime}
and EW symmetry breaking are related.

If
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
data is in fact explained by a
Z^{\prime}
with non-vanishing
Z-Z^{\prime}
mixing, one predicts a pattern for the main observables driving the anomaly as shown in Table
2
. We observe that all tensions with experiment reduce significantly below the 1.5
\,\sigma
level in the scenario analyzed. Because
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
ratios testing LFUV depend naturally (and almost entirely) on
{\cal C}_{9\mu}^{\rm V}
and thus do not carry information on
\sin\xi
, angular observables are necessary for a distinctive study of
Z^{\prime}
models. It will therefore be important to verify with more precise LHCb data together with future Belle II analysis if this scenario gets reinforced.

Furthermore, forthcoming LHC measurements of the
W
mass may reinforce the current tension and any improvement in the global EW fit (e.g. in the top mass or in
Z
decays) would lead to a more precise
W
mass predictions which could be very precisely measured with future lepton colliders such as FCC-ee
Abada et al. 2019
, ILC
Bae 2013
, CEPC
An et al. 2019
or CLIC
Aic 2012
. Importantly, if a non-zero
Z-Z^{\prime}
mixing is established in the future, like e.g. predicted in the model of Ref.
Crivellin et al. 2015b
, this would imply that
SU(2)_{L}
and
U(1)^{\prime}
are broken by a field charged under both symmetries with important consequences for model building.

On subadditivity of Okounkov bodies for algebraic fiber spaces
Sung Rak Choi, Jinhyung Park

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to establish a subadditivity theorem of Okounkov bodies for algebraic fiber spaces. As applications, we obtain a product formula of the restricted canonical volumes for algebraic fiber spaces and a sufficient condition for an algebraic fiber space to be birationally isotrivial in terms of Okounkov bodies when a general fiber is of general type. Furthermore, we also prove the subadditivity of the numerical Iitaka dimensions for algebraic fiber spaces, and this confirms some numerical variants of the Iitaka conjecture. We hope that our results would provide a new approach toward the Iitaka conjecture.



1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. In this paper, every divisor is assumed to be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor unless otherwise stated, and an
algebraic fiber space
is a surjective morphism
f\colon X\to Y
between smooth projective varieties
X,Y
with connected fibers. We denote by
F
a general fiber of
f
. The intertwined connection among
X,Y,F
is still mysterious and has yet to be studied further.

An
Okounkov body
of a divisor is a convex subset in Euclidean space introduced by Kaveh–Khovanskii
[
KK
]
and Lazarsfeld–Mustaţă
[
LM
]
independently. It is well known that the Okounkov bodies encode various asymptotic invariants of divisors (see Section
2
for the relevant definitions). Our experience indicates that the Okounkov bodies are extremely useful in studying the subadditivity properties of the Iitaka dimensions and the volumes of divisors. The aim of this paper is to unveil some relations on the positivity properties of the canonical divisors
K_{X},K_{Y},K_{F}
in a fixed framework provided by the Okounkov bodies.

First of all, we recall one of the most important problems in birational geometry about algebraic fiber spaces, which still remains open since 1972 (see
[
F3
]
for the comprehensive account for the Iitaka conjecture).

Iitaka Conjecture.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
. Then the following inequality for the Kodaira dimensions holds:

\kappa(K_{X})\geq\kappa(K_{Y})+\kappa(K_{F}).

Along with the Kodaira dimension, the volume function also provides a refined measurement of the positivity of canonical divisors. Regarding the relation on the volumes of canonical divisors
K_{X},K_{Y},K_{F}
, the following product formula of volumes was obtained by Kawamata
[
Kaw2
]
under the assumption that
X,Y,F
are of general type:

(1.1)

\frac{\vol_{X}(K_{X})}{\dim X!}\geq\frac{\vol_{Y}(K_{Y})}{\dim Y!}\cdot\frac{\vol_{F}(K_{F})}{\dim F!}.

In
[
CJPW
]
, the product formula (
1.1
) was interpreted and reproved from an alternative perspective in terms of Okounkov bodies of canonical divisors. More precisely, the formula (
1.1
) can be obtained as an immediate consequence of the following “subadditivity” of the Okounkov bodies of canonical divisors:

(1.2)

\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})\supseteq\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})+\Delta_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})

where
X_{\bullet}
is a
fiber-type
admissible flag on
X
associated to admissible flags
Y_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}
on
Y,F
, respectively with
F=f^{-1}(Y_{\dim Y})
, i.e.,

X_{i}=\begin{cases}f^{-1}(Y_{i})&\text{if $0\leq i\leq\dim Y$}\\
F_{i-\dim Y}&\text{if $\dim Y<i\leq\dim X$}.\end{cases}

By identifying
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})
with
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})\times\{0\}^{\dim F}
and
\Delta_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})
with
\{0\}^{\dim Y}\times\Delta_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})
in an obvious way, we may regard
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y}),\Delta_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\dim X}
. Then “
+
” in (
1.2
) is the Minkowski sum of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{\dim X}
, and we will use this convention if no confusion is likely to occur.
As
K_{X},K_{Y},K_{F}
are big by assumption, the Okounkov bodies

\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\dim X},\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\dim Y},\Delta_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\dim F}

have full dimensions in the respective spaces. Thus their Euclidean volumes can be expressed as the volumes of divisors up to some constants depending on the dimensions of varieties (see
[
LM
, Theorem A]
). This leads to a new proof of Kawamata’s product formula (
1.1
) via (
1.2
). It is worth recalling from
[
CJPW
, Theorem 1.3]
(see also
[
T
, Corollary 4.7]
) that the equality holds in (
1.1
) (or equivalently, in (
1.2
)) if and only if
f
is birationally isotrivial.

The principal aim of this paper is to generalize the main results of
[
CJPW
]
, and to provide a new approach toward the Iitaka conjecture. The following theorem illustrates how the Okounkov bodies can be used effectively in the study of Iitaka conjecture.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
over a point
\eta\in Y
. Assume that
K_{Y}
and
K_{F}
are pseudoeffective. Let
X_{\bullet}
be a fiber-type admissible flag on
X
associated to
Y_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}
, where
Y_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
Y
centered at
\eta
containing a positive volume subvariety
Y^{\prime}
of
K_{Y}
and
F_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
F
containing a positive volume subvariety
F^{\prime}
of
K_{F}
. Then the following hold:

(1)
We have

\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})\supseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})+\Delta^{\lim}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F}).

In particular,

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{X})\geq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{Y})+\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{F}).

(2)
Assume that
K_{F}
is big.
If the equality
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{X})=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{Y})+\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{F})
holds, then the following canonical volume formula holds:

\frac{\vol_{X|X^{\prime}}^{+}(K_{X})}{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{X})!}\geq\frac{\vol_{Y|Y^{\prime}}^{+}(K_{Y})}{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{Y})!}\cdot\frac{\vol_{F|F^{\prime}}^{+}(K_{F})}{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{F})!}

where
X^{\prime}=f^{-1}(Y^{\prime})
. If the equality
\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})+\Delta^{\lim}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})
holds, then
f
is birationally isotrivial.

We refer to Section
2
for the definitions of positive volume subvariety, Nakayama subvariety, and the numerical Iitaka dimensions
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D),\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
of a pseudoeffective divisor
D
.
The
limiting Okounkov body

\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(D)
of a pseudoeffective divisor
D
was introduced and studied in
[
CHPW
]
. Recall that
\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)\leq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
in general.
If
X_{\bullet}
contains a positive volume subvariety of
D
, then the equality holds and the Euclidean volume of
\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)
in
\mathbb{R}^{\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}
coincides with
\vol_{X|X^{\prime}}^{+}(D)/\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)!
.

In contrast to
[
CJPW
, Theorem 1.3]
, the birational isotriviality of
f
does not necessarily imply
the equality
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{X})=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{Y})+\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{F})
in Theorem
1.1
(1). This means that the inclusion

\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})\supseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})+\Delta^{\lim}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})
in Theorem
1.1
(1) can be strict even when
f
is birationally isotrivial and
K_{F}
is big. See Example
4.1
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.1
, Nakayama
[
N
, Theorem V.4.1]
and Fujino
[
F2
, Theorem 2.1]
(see also the discussion in
[
F2
, Section 3]
) proved the following inequalities

\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X})\geq\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{Y})+\kappa(K_{F})\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X})\geq\kappa(K_{Y})+\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{F}).

However, we still do not know whether the following inequality holds:

\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X})\geq\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{Y})+\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{F}).

We remark that
\kappa_{\sigma}(D)\geq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
holds for every pseudoeffective divisor
D
and the inequality can be strict for some
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
(see
[
CP
, Theorem 1.2]
).
Notice that
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
is the smallest one among the mostly used numerical Iitaka dimensions in the literature (see e.g.,
[
BDPP
]
,
[
CP
]
,
[
E
]
,
[
Leh
]
,
[
N
]
) while
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\geq\kappa(D)
. Thus the inequality in Theorem
1.1
is in some sense closer to what is expected by the Iitaka conjecture than the previously known results. Note also that through Theorem
1.1
, we can also confirm that the
abundance conjecture

[
BDPP
, Conjecture 3.8]
, which predicts that
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{X})=\kappa(K_{X})
for every smooth projective variety
X
, implies the Iitaka conjecture. See Remark
3.4
for another numerical variant of the Iitaka conjecture.

The
valuative Okounkov body

\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)
of an effective divisor
D
is the Okounkov body constructed in the usual way without taking the limit process as we did for
\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)
. It is introduced and studied in
[
CHPW
]
. It is known that
\dim\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)=\kappa(D)
and if
X_{\bullet}
contains a Nakayama subvariety
X^{\prime}
of
D
and
X_{\dim X}
is a general point, then the Euclidean volume of
\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)
in
\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(D)}
is
\vol_{X|X^{\prime}}(D)/\kappa(D)!
. Theorem
1.1
naturally leads us to ask the following intriguing question:

Question.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
over a point
\eta\in Y
. Assume that
K_{Y}
and
K_{F}
are effective. Let
X_{\bullet}
be a fiber-type admissible flag on
X
associated to
Y_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}
, where
Y_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
Y
centered at
\eta
containing a Nakayama subvariety
Y^{\prime}
of
K_{Y}
and
F_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
F
centered at a general point of
F
containing a Nakayama subvariety
F^{\prime}
of
K_{F}
.
Then does the following inclusion

\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})\supseteq\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})+\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})

hold?

An affirmative answer to this question would solve the Iitaka conjecture because the dimensions of the valuative Okounkov bodies
\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X}),\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y}),\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})
coincide with the Kodaira dimensions
\kappa(K_{X}),\kappa(K_{Y}),\kappa(K_{F})
, respectively. However, this naïve expectation fails to hold due to Example
4.2
for which the following reverse strict inclusion holds:

\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})\subsetneq\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})+\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F}).

We note that
K_{F}
is big in this example. However, when
K_{Y}
is big, we do have an affirmative answer to the question.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
over a point
\eta\in Y
. Assume that
K_{Y}
is big and
K_{F}
is effective. Let
X_{\bullet}
be a fiber-type admissible flag on
X
associated to
Y_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}
, where
Y_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
Y
centered at
\eta
containing a Nakayama subvariety
Y^{\prime}
of
K_{Y}
and
F_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
F
centered at a general point of
F
containing a Nakayama subvariety
F^{\prime}
of
K_{F}
. Then we have

\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})\supseteq\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})+\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F}).

In particular,

\kappa(K_{X})=\kappa(K_{Y})+\kappa(K_{F}).

Recall that the Iitaka conjecture for the case where
Y
is of general type has been settled previously by Viehweg
[
V1
, Corollary IV]
. Note that the first statement in Theorem
1.2
only implies that
\kappa(K_{X})\geq\kappa(K_{Y})+\kappa(K_{F})
, but then the easy addition lemma shows the desired equality.
When
K_{F}
is also big in Theorem
1.2
, the similar statements to Theorem
1.1
(2) hold, but this case is already treated in
[
CJPW
]
.

Both Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
easily follow from a more general statement about the subadditivity of Okounkov bodies of divisors on algebraic fiber spaces stated below. The following subadditivity theorem is a generalization of
[
CJPW
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
over a point
\eta\in Y
. Let
D,R
be divisors on
X
, and
D_{Y},A_{Y}
be divisors on
Y
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}D_{Y}+R
.

(2)

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mR)
is weakly positive for every sufficiently divisible integer
m>0
.

(3)

R|_{F}
is effective.

(4)

D_{Y}
is effective.

(5)

A_{Y}
is ample.

Let
X_{\bullet}
be a fiber-type admissible flag on
X
associated to
Y_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}
, where
Y_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
Y
centered at
\eta
containing a Nakayama subvariety of
D_{Y}
, and
F_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
F
centered at a general point of
F
containing a Nakayama subvariety of
R|_{F}
. Then we have

\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(D+f^{*}A_{Y})\supseteq\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D_{Y})+\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F}).

As consequences of Theorem
1.3
, we obtain two Corollaries
3.3
and
3.5
, which in turn imply Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
by the weak positivity theorem of Viehweg
[
V1
, Theorem III]
and its variants. See Section
2
for the definition of weakly positive sheaves.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. We begin in Section
2
with recalling basic definitions and facts. Section
3
is devoted to the proofs of the theorems stated in the introduction. In Section
4
, we present some relevant examples, and we formulate a refinement of the question in the introduction.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Algebraic fiber spaces and weak positivity

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
. Recall that the
variation
\operatorname{Var}(f)
of
f
is defined as the minimum of the transcendental degrees
\text{tr.}\deg_{\mathbb{C}}L
of algebraically closed subfields
L\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{C}(Y)}
such that
F\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(L)}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{\mathbb{C}(Y)})
is birationally equivalent to
X\times_{Y}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{\mathbb{C}(Y)})
for some smooth projective variety
F
over
L
(see
[
V1
]
). We say that
f
is
birationally isotrivial
if there exists a generically finite cover
\tau\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
such that the fiber product
X\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}
is birationally equivalent to
F\times Y^{\prime}
. It is well known that
f
is birationally isotrivial if and only if
\operatorname{Var}(f)=0
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Kaw1
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
V2
, Theorem 1.20]
; see also
[
F3
, Theorem 3.4.7]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
. Suppose that
F
is a variety of general type. If
D_{Y}
is any divisor on
Y
with
\kappa(D_{Y})\geq 0
, then we have

\kappa(K_{X/Y}+f^{*}D_{Y})\geq\kappa(K_{F})+\max\{\kappa(D_{Y}),\operatorname{Var}(f)\}.

The
Iitaka dimension
of a divisor
D
on a smooth projective variety is defined as

\kappa(D):=\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mD\rfloor))}{m^{k}}>0\right.\right\}

if
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mD\rfloor))>0
for some integer
m>0
and
\kappa(D):=-\infty
otherwise.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent torsion-free sheaf on a smooth projective variety
X
.
We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
weakly positive
if there exists a nonempty open subvariety
U\subseteq X
such that for every ample Cartier divisor
H
on
X
and every integer
m>0
, the map

H^{0}(X,\hat{S}^{mk}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(kH))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\hat{S}^{mk}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(kH)

is surjective at each point in
U
for some integer
k>0
, where
\hat{S}^{mk}(\mathcal{F}):=\big(S^{mk}(\mathcal{F})\big)^{\vee\vee}
is the double dual of the sheaf
S^{mk}(\mathcal{F})
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
DM
, Theorem D]
,
[
F3
, Theorem 3.4.7]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
, and
\Delta
be an effective divisor on
X
such that
(X,\Delta)
is an lc pair and
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Then
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(k(K_{X/Y}+\Delta))
is weakly positive for every positive integer
k
such that
k(K_{X}+\Delta)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly equivalent to a Cartier divisor.

We refer to
[
KM
]
for the basic definitions of the singularities of pairs. When
\Delta=0
, Theorem
2.2
is nothing but Viehweg’s weak positivity theorem
[
V1
, Theorem III]
.

2.2.
Okounkov bodies

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
, and fix an
admissible flag

X_{\bullet}
on
X

X_{\bullet}:X=X_{0}\supseteq X_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq X_{n}\supseteq X_{n}=\{x\}

where each
X_{i}
is an irreducible closed subvariety of
X
having codimension
i
and smooth at the point
x
.
Let
D
be an effective divisor on
X
. We consider a valuation-like function

\nu_{X_{\bullet}}\colon|D|_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0},\penalty\ \penalty\ D^{\prime}\mapsto\nu_{X_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n})

where the
\nu_{i}
are defined inductively as follows:

(1)

let
\nu_{1}:=\operatorname{mult}_{X_{1}}D^{\prime}
and
D^{\prime}_{1}:=D^{\prime}-a_{1}X_{1}
, and then,

(2)

assuming that we have defined
\nu_{i}
and
D^{\prime}_{i}
, define
\nu_{i+1}:=\operatorname{mult}_{X_{i+1}}(D^{\prime}_{i}|_{X_{i}})
and
D^{\prime}_{i+1}=D^{\prime}_{i}|_{X_{i}}-a_{i+1}X_{i+1}
.

The
Okounkov body
of
D
with respect to
X_{\bullet}
is defined as

\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(D):=\text{the convex closure of }\nu_{X_{\bullet}}(|D|_{\mathbb{Q}})\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}.

By
[
B
, Proposition 3.3]
, we have
\dim\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(D)=\kappa(D)
. When
D
is not big, we use the notation
\Delta^{\val}_{\bullet}(D)
, which is called the
valuative Okounkov body
of
D
with respect to
X_{\bullet}
, for the Okounkov body
\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(D)
. By
[
LM
, Theorem A]
, we have

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(D)=\frac{1}{n!}\vol_{X}(D)\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for every admissible flag $X_{\bullet}$ on $X$}.

Recall that the
restricted volume
of a divisor
D
along an irreducible closed subvariety
V
of dimension
v
is defined as

\vol_{X|V}(D):=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim\im\big(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mD\rfloor))\to H^{0}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}(\lfloor mD\rfloor|_{V}))\big)}{m^{v}/v!}.

When
V=X
, we simply set
\vol_{X}(D):=\vol_{X|X}(D)
, and we call it the
volume
of
D
. The
augmented restricted volume
of
D
along
V
is defined as

\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\vol_{X|V}(D+\varepsilon A),

where
A
is an ample divisor on
X
. It is easy to check that the definition is independent of the choice of
A
and
\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D)
depends only on the numerical class of
D
. If
D
is big, then
\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D)=\vol_{X|V}(D)
. For more details on the restricted volumes, see
[
CHPW
]
,
[
ELMNP
]
.

Now, let
D
be a pseudoeffective divisor on
X
. The
limiting Okounkov body
of
D
with respect to
X_{\bullet}
is defined as

\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(D):=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(D+\varepsilon A)

for any fixed ample divisor
A
on
X
. This definition is independent of the choice of
A
. The limiting Okounkov body
\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)
depends only on the numerical class of
D
(see
[
CHPW
, Theorem C]
). If
D
is big, then
\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)
. By
[
CP
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have
\kappa(D)\leq\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)\leq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
for every admissible flag
X_{\bullet}
.

The following numerical Iitaka dimension was introduced by Boucksom–Demailly–Păun–Peternell
[
BDPP
]
:

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D):=\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\langle D^{k}\rangle\neq 0\right.\right\}

where
\langle D^{k}\rangle
is the positive intersection product (see
[
Leh
, Section 4]
for the definition and basic properties).
By
[
Leh
, Theorem 6.2]
(see also
[
CP
, Theorem 1.1]
), we have

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=\max\left\{\dim W\left|\vol^{+}_{X|W}(L)>0\right.\right\}

where the
W
range over all irreducible closed subvarieties of
X
. We now recall some other numerical Iitaka dimensions introduced by Nakayama
[
N
]
and Lehmann
[
Leh
]
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\kappa_{\sigma}(D)&:=\displaystyle\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)}{m^{k}}>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\vol}(D)&:=\displaystyle\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0\right.\right\}\end{array}

where
A
is a sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
. It is well known that the numerical Iitaka dimensions
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D),\kappa_{\sigma}(D),\kappa_{\vol}(D)
depend only on the numerical class of
D
. Furthermore,
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D),\kappa_{\sigma}(D),\kappa_{\vol}(D)
are nonnegative integers at most
n
when
D
is pseudoeffective, and
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D),\kappa_{\sigma}(D),\kappa_{\vol}(D)=n
if and only if
D
is big. By
[
CP
, Proposition 3.1]
,
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\sigma}(D),\kappa_{\vol}(D)
, and by
[
CP
, Theorem 1.2]
, the inequality can be strict (see also
[
Les
]
). We refer to
[
CP
]
for more basic properties of numerical Iitaka dimensions.

For an effective divisor
D
, an irreducible closed subvariety
U\subseteq X
is called a
Nakayama subvariety
of
D
if
\dim U=\kappa(D)
and the natural restriction map

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

First, we write

D+f^{*}A_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}D_{Y}+(R+f^{*}A_{Y}).

By the usual subadditivity property of Okounkov bodies, we have

(3.4)

\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(D+f^{*}A_{Y})\supseteq\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(f^{*}D_{Y})+\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(R+f^{*}A_{Y}).

It is clear that

\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(f^{*}D_{Y})=\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D_{Y})\times\{0\}^{\dim F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\dim Y}\times\{0\}^{\dim F}.\\

Thus we may identify
\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D_{Y})
with
\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(f^{*}D_{Y})
.
We can also regard

\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F})\subseteq\{0\}^{\dim Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\dim F}.

To prove the theorem, by considering (
3.4
), it is sufficient to show that
\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F})
is a subset of
\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(R+f^{*}A_{Y})
.
To this end, let
N
be the Nakayama subvariety of
R|_{F}
contained in the admissible flag
F_{\bullet}
so that
\dim N=\kappa(R|_{F})
.
By Theorem
2.3
, we have

(3.5)

\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(R|_{F})}\text{ and }\kappa(R|_{F})!\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(R|_{F})}}\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F})=\vol_{F|N}(R|_{F}).

Now, consider the graded linear series
W_{\bullet}
on
N
associated to
(R+f^{*}A_{Y})|_{N}=R|_{N}
consisting of subspaces

W_{i}=\text{Image}\big(H^{0}(X,\lfloor iR+if^{*}A_{Y}\rfloor)\to H^{0}(N,\lfloor iR+if^{*}A_{Y}\rfloor|_{N})\big)

for each integer
i\geq 0
. Then Lemma
3.1
and (
3.5
) imply that

\vol_{N}(W_{\bullet})=\vol_{X|N}(R+f^{*}A_{Y})=\vol_{F|N}(R|_{F})=\kappa(R|_{F})!\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(R|_{F})}}\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F}).

Let
N_{\bullet}
be an admissible flag on
N
such that
N_{i}=X_{i+\dim X-\dim N}
for all
0\leq i\leq\dim N
.
Since
N_{\dim N}=X_{\dim X}
is a general point, it follows from
[
LM
, Remark 2.8 and Theorem 2.13]
that

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(R|_{F})}}(\Delta_{N_{\bullet}}(W_{\bullet}))=\frac{1}{\kappa(R|_{F})!}\vol_{N}(W_{\bullet})=\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{\kappa(R|_{F})}}\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F}).

As we have

\Delta_{N_{\bullet}}(W_{\bullet})\subseteq\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(R+f^{*}A_{Y})_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\dim Y}=0}\subseteq\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F}),

we see that
\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(R+f^{*}A_{Y})_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\dim Y}=0}=\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F})
.
Hence we may regard
\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(R|_{F})
as a subset of
\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(R+f^{*}A_{Y})
, and we complete the proof.
∎

Now, by Theorem

, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\geq\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{X_{\bullet}}(D)

.

3.5
. We also have
\kappa(K_{X})\geq\kappa(K_{Y})+\kappa(K_{F})
. Recall that
K_{Y}
is big. The easy addition lemma shows that
\kappa(K_{X})\leq\dim Y+\kappa(K_{F})=\kappa(K_{Y})+\kappa(K_{F})
. Thus
\kappa(K_{X})=\kappa(K_{Y})+\kappa(K_{F})
.
∎

4.
Examples

In this section, we exhibit some relevant examples. First, we give a counterexample to the converse of Theorem
1.1
(2).

Example 4.1
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective minimal surface of general type. Suppose that there is a genus two fibration
f\colon S\to C
over an elliptic curve
C
such that
f
is birationally isotrivial. Let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. Then
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{C})=\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{C_{\bullet}}(K_{C})=0
for every admissible flag
C_{\bullet}
on
C
and
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{F})=\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})=1
for every admissible flag on
F
. However,
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{S})=\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{S_{\bullet}}(K_{S})=\dim\Delta_{S_{\bullet}}(K_{S})=2
for every admissible flag
S_{\bullet}
on
S
. Therefore, we have

\Delta^{\lim}_{S_{\bullet}}(K_{S})\supsetneq\Delta^{\lim}_{C_{\bullet}}(K_{C})+\Delta^{\lim}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})

for every fiber-type admissible flag
S_{\bullet}
associated to admissible flags
C_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}
on
C,F
, respectively. Note also that the strict inequality

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{S})>\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{C})+\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{F})

holds even though
f
is birationally isotrivial.

Next, we give a counterexample to the question stated in the introduction.

Example 4.2
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective minimal surface with
\kappa(K_{S})=1
. Suppose that
q(S)=2
and there is a smooth genus two fibration
f\colon S\to C
over an elliptic curve
C
. Then
f
is birationally isotrivial, and the Iitaka fibration
g\colon S\to C^{\prime}
of
S
has two double fibers (see
[
Kar
, Lemma 2.1]
). Notice that
S
is not biratioinal to
C\times F
, where
F
is a general fiber of
f
.
Let
x
be a general point on
F
, and consider the admissible flag on
S
:

S_{\bullet}:S\supseteq F\supseteq\{x\},

which is a fiber-type admissible flag associated to
C_{\bullet}:C\supseteq\{f(F)\}
and
F_{\bullet}:F\supseteq\{x\}
. Note that
\Delta^{\val}_{C_{\bullet}}(K_{C})=\{0\}
. We have

\Delta^{\val}_{S_{\bullet}}(K_{S})=\frac{1}{\deg g|_{F}}\Delta^{\lim}_{S_{\bullet}}(K_{S})=\frac{1}{\deg g|_{F}}\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F}).

As
\deg g|_{F}\geq 2
, we get

\Delta^{\val}_{S_{\bullet}}(K_{S})\subsetneq\Delta^{\val}_{C_{\bullet}}(K_{C})+\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F}).

This shows that the expected inclusion in the question of the introduction does not hold in general.

In the above example, we observe that

\deg g|_{F}\cdot\Delta^{\val}_{S_{\bullet}}(K_{S})\supseteq\Delta^{\val}_{C_{\bullet}}(K_{C})\times\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F}).

Although our expectation in the introduction may fail, we wonder whether the following question would have an affirmative answer, which would imply the Iitaka conjecture.

Question 4.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space with general fiber
F
over a point
\eta\in Y
. Assume that
K_{Y}
and
K_{F}
are effective. Let
X_{\bullet}
be a fiber-type admissible flag on
X
associated to
Y_{\bullet},F_{\bullet}
, where
Y_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
Y
centered at
\eta\in Y
containing a Nakayama subvariety
Y^{\prime}
of
K_{Y}
and
F_{\bullet}
is an admissible flag on
F
centered at a general point of
F
containing a Nakayama subvariety
F^{\prime}
of
K_{F}
.
Then do there exist some positive numbers
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
such that

\alpha\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})\supseteq\beta\Delta^{\val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(K_{Y})+\gamma\Delta^{\val}_{F_{\bullet}}(K_{F})

hold?

In Example
4.2
, we can take
\alpha=\deg g|_{F}
,
\beta=\gamma=1
. By
[
CPW2
, Theorem C]
, using the abundance of the canonical divisors, we can find a positive integer
\alpha>0
such that
\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}^{\lim}(K_{X})=\alpha\cdot\Delta^{\val}_{X_{\bullet}}(K_{X})
. Such an integer
\alpha
is given by the degree of the canonical contraction on a minimal model restricted on the image of the Nakayama subvariety. See
[
CPW2
]
for more details. Assuming the abundance conjecture
[
BDPP
, Conjecture 3.8]
, we have an affirmative answer to Question
4.3
by Theorem
1.1
.

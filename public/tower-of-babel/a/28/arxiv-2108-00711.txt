Existence of ground state solutions to some Nonlinear Schrödinger equations on lattice graphs
Bobo Hua, Wendi Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the nonlinear Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u = f ⁡ ( x , u ) -\Delta u+V(x)u=f(x,u) on the lattice graph ℤ N \mathbb{Z}^{N} . Using the Nehari method, we prove that when f f satisfies some growth conditions and the potential function V V is periodic or bounded, the above equation admits a ground state solution. Moreover, we extend our results from ℤ N \mathbb{Z}^{N} to quasi-transitive graphs.



1.
introduction

The nonlinear Schrödinger type equations of the form

(1)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=f(x,u),\quad u\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or the whole Euclidean space, have been extensively studied during the past several decades. See for examples
[
Rab92
,
DN86
,
JT05
,
KS98
,
AL92
,
Cao92
,
Str08
,
LW04
]
and the references therein. Since the above equations have variational structures, one can use the critical point theory to study them. The corresponding variational functional is

\Phi(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,u)\,dx,

where
F(x,u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,t)dt
. The solutions corresponding to the least positive critical value of
\Phi
are called ground state solutions of (
1
).

In
[
LWZ06
]
, Y.Q. Li, Z.Q. Wang and J. Zeng considered two cases of potential: one is periodic and the other is bounded, namely
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V<+\infty
.
In both cases, without compact embedding for
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, they established, under a Nehari type condition (see
[
Neh60
,
Neh61
]
) and a super-quadratic condition on the nonlinearity
f
, the existence of ground state solutions to (
1
). In their note
[
SW10
]
, S. Andrzej and T. Weth presented a unified approach to the Nehari method and proved results similar to Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 3.1 in
[
LWZ06
]
.

Recently, many researchers paid attention to various differential equations on graphs.
In
[
GLY16a
,
GLY16b
,
GLY17
]
, A. Grigor’yan, Y. Lin and Y.Y. Yang applied variational methods on graphs to various types of nonlinear elliptic equations. In particular, in
[
GLY17
]
, they proved the existence of a strictly positive solution to
-\Delta u+Vu=f(x,u)
if
V(x)\to\infty
as
{\rm d}(x,x_{0})\to\infty
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{V}
, where d
(x,x_{0})
is the combinatorial distance, i.e., the minimal number of edges contained in paths connecting
x
and
x_{0}
.
Via the Nehari method, Zhang, N. and Zhao, L., see
[
ZZ18
]
, proved that, if
a(x)\to\infty
as d
(x,x_{0})\to\infty
, then, for any
\lambda>1
, the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
-\Delta u+(\lambda a(x)+1)u=|u|^{p-1}u
admits a ground state solution on locally finite graphs. Both
[
GLY17
]
and
[
ZZ18
]
assumed that the potential functions tend to infinity when
{\rm d}(x,x_{0})\to\infty
, while we prove the results without such assumptions for special graphs.

In this paper, we consider the Schrödinger type equation

-\Delta u+V(x)u=f(x,u)

on graphs. We first introduce the basic setting on graphs. Let
G=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
be a simple, undirected, locally finite graph, where
\mathbb{V}
is the set of vertices and
\mathbb{E}
is the set of edges. Two vertices
x
and
y
are called neighbours, denoted by
x\sim y
, if there is an edge connecting them, i.e.,
\{x,y\}\in\mathbb{E}
. A graph is called locally finite if each vertex has finitely many neighbours.

Denote the set of functions on
G
by
C(V)
.
The Laplacian on
G=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
is defined as, for any function
u\in C(V)
and
x\in\mathbb{V}
,

\Delta u(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{V},y\sim x}\big(u(y)-u(x)\big).

Let
C_{c}(\mathbb{V})
be the set of all functions of finite support, and
H^{1}(\mathbb{V})
be the completion of
C_{c}(\mathbb{V})
under the norm

\|u\|_{H^{1}}:=\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\big(u(y)-u(x)\big)^{2}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}u^{2}(x)\Big)^{1/2}.

We denote by
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
the standard lattice graph with the set of vertices

\{x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}):x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq N\}

and the set of edges

\Big\{\{x,y\}:x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}-y_{i}|=1\Big\}.

For
T\in\mathbb{N}
, a function
g
on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
is called T-periodic if
g(x+Te_{i})=g(x),\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},1\leq i\leq N
, where
e_{i}
is the unit vector in the i-th coordinate.

We are concerned with the existence of ground state solutions of the following Schrödinger equation:

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u=f(x,u),&x\in\mathbb{V}\\
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V})&\end{array}\right.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the Schrödinger equation (
2
) on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Suppose that the nonlinearity
f:\mathbb{Z}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous with respect to
u
and satisfies the growth condition

(3)

|f(x,u)|\leq a(|u|+|u|^{q-1})\quad\textrm{ for some }a>0\textrm{ and }q>2.

Moreover, we assume the following conditons hold:

(i)
V(x)
and
f(x,u)
are T-periodic in
x
for any
u\in\mathbb{R}

and
V(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

(ii)
f(x,u)=o(u)
uniformly in
x
as
u\to 0
.

(iii)
u\mapsto\frac{f(x,u)}{|u|}
is strictly increasing on
(-\infty,0)
and
(0,+\infty)
respectively.

(iv)
\frac{F(x,u)}{u^{2}}\to\infty
uniformly in
x
as
|u|\to\infty
with
F(x,u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,s)\,ds
.

Then the equation (
2
) has a ground state solution.

We also consider the case that
V
is not periodic but bounded in the sense that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=\sup_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}V<+\infty
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider the Schrödinger equation (
2
) on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Suppose that the nonlinearity
f=f(u)
is a continuous function satisfying the growth condition (
3
) and the following conditions:

(i)
f(u)=o(u)
as
u\to 0
.

(ii)
u\mapsto\frac{f(u)}{|u|}
is strictly increasing on
(-\infty,0)
and
(0,+\infty)
respectively.

(iii)
\frac{F(u)}{u^{2}}\to\infty
as
|u|\to\infty
.

Moreover, we assume that
V
is a bounded potential well, i.e.,

0<V_{0}\leq\inf_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}V(x)\leq\sup_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}V(x)=V_{\infty}<\infty\quad\text{with }\quad V_{\infty}=\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x).

Then the equation (
2
) has a ground state solution.

Remark 1.3
.

(1) The potential function
V(x)\to\infty
as
{\rm d}(x,x_{0})\to\infty
is assumed in
[
GLY17
,
ZZ18
]
to prove the existence of ground state solutions to
-\Delta u+V(x)u=f(x,u)
on locally finite graphs. We don’t need this assumption on lattice graphs.

(2) The above two theorems are discrete analogs of the results in
[
SW10
]
. However, the Sobolev embedding in our setting is different from that in the Euclidean space, which allows us to remove the constraint that
q<\frac{2N}{N-2}
(subcritical) in the continuous case.

We follow the proofs in the continuous setting
[
SW10
]
to prove the theorems. In particular, we set up the framework of Nehari methods on graphs and adopt it to prove the existence of ground state solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
2
).

Moreover, we extend the results from lattice graphs to quasi-transitive graphs (see
[
Woe00
]
).
G
is called a quasi-transitive graph if there are finitely many orbits for the action of
Aut(G)
on
G
where
Aut(G)
is the set of automorphisms of
G
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
be a quasi-transitive graph,
\Gamma\leq Aut(G)
. Suppose that the action of
\Gamma
on
G
has finitely many orbits. Then Theorem
1.2
holds on
G
. By replacing T-periodicity of
f
and
V
with
\Gamma
-invariance, Theorem
1.1
holds on
G
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the setting of function spaces on graphs. Then we clarify the functional framework for Nehari manifold in Section 3. The proofs of Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
are given in Section 4 and Section 5 respectively. Since the proofs of theorems on quasi-transitive graphs are similar to that on lattice graphs, we give a brief proof at the end of Section 5. Finally, the detailed proofs of theorems in Section 3 are contained in the Appendix.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
G=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
be a locally finite graph. In this paper, we always assume that

(4)

\#\{y\in\mathbb{V}:y\sim x\}\leq C,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V},

where
C
is a uniform constant. For any function
u,v\in C(\mathbb{V})
, define the associated gradient form as

\Gamma(u,v)(x):=\sum_{y\sim x}\dfrac{1}{2}\big(u(y)-u(x)\big)\big(v(y)-v(x)\big).

Write
\Gamma(u)=\Gamma(u,u)
and

|\nabla u|(x):=\sqrt{\Gamma(u)(x)}=\Big(\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{1}{2}\big(u(y)-u(x)\big)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Let
\mu
be the counting measure on
\mathbb{V}
, i.e., for any subset
A\subset\mathbb{V}
,
\mu(A):=\#\{x:x\in A\}
. For any function
f
on
\mathbb{V}
, we write

\int_{\mathbb{V}}fd\mu:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}f(x)

whenever it makes sense.

The corresponding energy is defined as

\mathcal{E}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{V}}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)^{2}.

By definition,
H^{1}(\mathbb{V})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product given by

\langle u,v\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{V}}\Gamma(u,v)+uv\,d\mu.

Let
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V},\mu),p\in[1,\infty],
be the Hilbert space of
\ell^{p}
summable functions on
\mathbb{V}
w.r.t. the measure
\mu
. We write
\|\cdot\|_{p}
as the
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V},\mu)
norm, i.e.,

\|u\|_{p}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|u(x)|^{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,\\
&\sup_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|u(x)|,\quad\quad p=\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

By definition,

\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{V}}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\big)d\mu=\mathcal{E}(u)+\|u\|_{2}^{2},\\

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\big(u(y)-u(x)\big)^{2}\leq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\big(u^{2}(x)+u^{2}(y)\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence, there exists a constant
C^{{}^{\prime}}>0
such that

(5)

1/C^{{}^{\prime}}\|u\|_{2}\leq\|u\|_{H^{1}}\leq C^{{}^{\prime}}\|u\|_{2}.

As usual, we write

\|u\|_{2}\simeq\|u\|_{H^{1}}

to indicate (
5
).
For a bounded uniformly positive function
V:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e. there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, such that

(6)

C_{1}\leq V(x)\leq C_{2},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V},

we can define an equivalent norm on
H^{1}(\mathbb{V})
as

\|u\|:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{V}}|\nabla u|^{2}+Vu^{2}d\mu\Big)^{1/2}.

One can show that

\|u\|\simeq\|u\|_{H^{1}}\simeq\|u\|_{2}.

We say that
u\in C(\mathbb{V})
is a weak solution of (
2
) if

\int_{\mathbb{V}}\big(\Gamma(u,\phi)+Vu\phi\big)d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{V}}f(x,u)\phi\,d\mu,\quad\forall\,\phi\in H^{1}.

Since
C_{c}(\mathbb{V})
is dense in
H^{1}
, the integeral by parts gives

\int_{\mathbb{V}}\big(-\Delta u+Vu\big)\phi\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{V}}f(x,u)\phi\,d\mu,\quad\forall\,\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{V}).

For any given
y\in\mathbb{V}
, one can take the test function
\phi=\delta_{y}(x)
and obtain

-\Delta u(y)+V(y)u(y)-f(y,u(y))=0.

Now that
y\in\mathbb{V}
is arbitrarily chosen, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
u
is a weak solution of (
2
), then
u
is a pointwise solution.

3.
Nehari Manifolds

In this section, we introduce the functional framework of Nehari manifolds on graphs following the continuous setting in e.g.
[
SW10
,
AM07
,
DM07
,
Wil96
]
and the references therein. The proofs of the results will be given in the Appendix.

Let
G=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
be a locally finite graph satisfying (
4
),
V:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
be the potential function satisfying (
6
) and
f(x,u)
satisfy (i)-(iv) in Theorem
1.1
without assuming the T-periodicity of
f
and
V
. We write
H^{1}=H^{1}(\mathbb{V})
. Define the variational functional as

\displaystyle\Phi(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{V}}\big(|\nabla{u}|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}\big)d\mu-\int_{\mathbb{V}}F(x,u)d\mu

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-I(u)

Lemma 3.1
.

Each solution
u\in H^{1}
of (
2
) corresponds to a critical point of
\Phi
, i.e.,
\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(u)=0
.

Denote the unit sphere in
H^{1}
by
S
. Set
\mathcal{N}=\{u\in H^{1}\backslash\{0\}:\Phi^{\prime}(u)u=0\}
. We call it Nehari manifold which is crucial for searching nontrivial critical points of
\Phi
. In the following Propositions
3.2
-
3.4
, we show that, for each
w\in S
, the ray
w\mapsto sw\,(s>0)
has a unique intersection with
\mathcal{N}
. Besides,
\mathcal{N}
is uniformly bounded away from zero. This allows us to establish a homeomorphic mapping between
\mathcal{N}
and
S
, where
S
is a Banach submanifold of
H^{1}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
I(u)=\int_{\mathbb{V}}F(x,u)\,d\mu
and
F(x,u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,s)\,ds
. Then

(i)
I^{{}^{\prime}}(u)=o(\|u\|)
as
u\to 0
,

(ii)
s\mapsto\frac{I^{{}^{\prime}}(su)u}{s}
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
for any
u\neq 0
, and

(iii)
\frac{I(su)}{s^{2}}\to\infty
uniformly on weakly compact subsets of
H^{1}\backslash\{0\}
as
s\to\infty
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
w\in H^{1}\backslash\{0\}
, define a function
\alpha_{w}(s):=\Phi(sw),s\in(0,\infty)
. We have the following:

(i) For any given
w
, there exists
s_{w}>0
such that
\alpha^{\prime}_{w}(s)>0
for
0<s<s_{w}
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{w}(s)<0
for
s>s_{w}
.

(ii) There exists
\delta>0
such that
s_{w}\geq\delta
for all
w\in S
.

(iii) For each compact subset
\mathcal{K}\subset S,
there exists a constant
C_{\mathcal{K}}
such that
s_{w}\leq C_{\mathcal{K}}
for all
w\in\mathcal{K}.

Define

\displaystyle\widehat{m}:H^{1}\backslash\{0\}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\mapsto\widehat{m}(w)=s_{w}w

and

\quad m:=\widehat{m}|_{S}:S\to\mathcal{N}.

Based on the geometric features of
\Phi
in the radial direction, we have the following observation.

Proposition 3.4
.

The mapping
\,\widehat{m}
is continuous and
m
is a homeomorphism between
S
and
\mathcal{N},
with the inverse of
m
given by
m^{-1}(u)=\frac{u}{\|u\|}
.

Define the functional
\widehat{\Psi}:H^{1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
as
\widehat{\Psi}(w):=\Phi(\widehat{m}(w))

and
\Psi:=\widehat{\Psi}|_{S}=\Phi\circ m
. One can prove that
w\in S
is a critical point of
\Psi
if and only if
u=s_{w}w\in\mathcal{N}
is a critical point of
\Phi
.

Proposition 3.5
.

We have

\widehat{\Psi}=\Phi\circ\widehat{m}\in C^{1}\big(H^{1}\backslash\{0\},\mathbb{R}\big)

and

\widehat{\Psi}^{\prime}(w)z=\ \frac{\|\widehat{m}(w)\|}{\|w\|}\widehat{\Phi}^{\prime}(\widehat{m}(w))z,\quad\quad\forall w,z\in H^{1}\backslash\{0\}.

Corollary 3.6
.

When restricted on
S
,

\Psi=\widehat{\Psi}\big|_{S}\in C^{1}(S,\mathbb{R})

and

\Psi^{\prime}(w)z=\|m(w)\|\Phi^{\prime}\big(m(w)\big)z,\quad\forall z\in T_{w}S.

Recall that a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}
is called a
Palais-Smale sequence
if
\{\Phi(u_{n})\}
is bounded and
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
. If
\Phi(u_{n})\to c
and
\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{n})\to 0
, we call
\{u_{n}\}
a
(PS)_{c}-sequence
.

Proposition 3.7
.

(i) If
\{w_{n}\}\subset S
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
\Psi
, then
\{m(w_{n})\}
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
\Phi
. If
\{u_{n}\}
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
\Phi
, then
\{m^{-1}(u_{n})\}
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
\Psi
.

(ii)
w
is a critical point of
\Psi
if and only if
m(w)
is a nontrivial critical point of
\Phi
.

4.
The proofs of Theorem 1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. Firstly, we are concerned with the properties of the lattice graph
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. It’s worth pointing out that we haven’t used the periodicity of
f
and
V
yet.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Set
c:=\inf_{S}\Psi
and take a minimizing sequence
\{w_{n}\}\subset S
, i.e.,
\Psi(w_{n})\to c
. By Ekeland’s Variational Principle, see
[
Str08
,
LW17
,
Tal87
]
, we may assume that
\Psi^{\prime}(w_{n})\to 0
, then
\{w_{n}\}
is a
(PS)_{c}-sequence
for
\Psi
.

Let
u_{n}=m(w_{n})\in\mathcal{N},
we claim that
\{\|u_{n}\|\}
is uniformly bounded. By Proposition
3.7
, we know that

\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0

and

\Phi(u_{n})=\Psi(w_{n})\to c\equiv\inf_{S}\Psi=\inf_{\mathcal{N}}\Phi,\quad\text{where }u_{n}=m(w_{n}).

Suppose that
\{\|u_{n}\|\}
is unbounded, then, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume
that
\|u_{n}\|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty.
Set
v_{n}:=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}\in S
, then it has a weakly convergent subsequence

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, for
S
is weakly compact.

For any
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}),

\langle v_{n},\phi\rangle\,\to\,\langle v,\phi\rangle.

By taking
\phi=\delta_{x}
, one can see that
\{v_{n}\}
is pointwise convergent, i.e.,
v_{n}(x)\to v(x),\,\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

We claim that
\{v_{n}\}
doesn’t converge to 0 in
\ell^{q}

(q>2)
. Suppose that
v_{n}\to 0
in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that, for all
s>0,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}F(x,sv_{n})d\mu\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\big|\int_{0}^{sv_{n}}f(x,t)dt\big|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|sv_{n}|\Big(\epsilon\big|sv_{n}\big|+C_{\epsilon}\big|sv_{n}\big|^{q-1}\Big)\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon s^{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2}+C_{\epsilon}s^{q}\|v_{n}\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\to\epsilon s^{2}\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we get

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}F(x,sv_{n})d\mu\Big|\to 0\quad\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Hence,

\Phi(sv_{n})=\frac{1}{2}s^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}F(x,sv_{n})d\mu\,\to\,\frac{1}{2}s^{2},\quad\forall s>0.

By definition,
\Phi(sv_{n})\leq\Phi(u_{n})
for all
s>0
. Besides,
\Phi(u_{n})\to c
as
n\to\infty.
Hence,

c+1\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi(sv_{n})=\frac{1}{2}s^{2},\quad\forall s>0.

Choosing
s>\sqrt{2(c+1)}
, we get a contradition. Therefore,
v_{n}\nrightarrow 0
in
\ell^{q}
, i.e.,

\varlimsup_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{q}=c>0

for some constant
c>0
.
By interpolation inequality, we have

\|v_{n}\|_{q}\leq\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{q}}\cdot\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{q-2}{q}}.

Taking the limit from both sides, one can see

\varlimsup_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{q}\leq\varlimsup_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{q}}\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}.

Since

\|v_{n}\|_{2}\leq\|v_{n}\|=1\quad\text{and}\quad\varlimsup_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{q}=c>0,

we have

\varlimsup_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\geq\varlimsup_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{q}}\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\geq c>0.

Hence, there exists a subsequence
\{v_{n}\}
and a sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
|v_{n}(y_{n})|\geq c
for each
n
. By translations, we define
\widetilde{v}_{n}:=v_{n}(y+k_{n}T)
with
k_{n}=(k^{1}_{n},\dots k^{N}_{n})
to ensure that
\{y_{n}-k_{n}T\}\subset\Omega
where
\Omega=[0,T)^{N}\bigcap\mathbb{Z}^{N}
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Then, for each
\widetilde{v}_{n},

\|\widetilde{v}_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq|v_{n}(y_{n})|\geq c>0.

Since
V(x)
and
f(x,u)
are both T-periodic in
x
,
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{N}
are invariant under the translation. Moreover, we have

\Psi(\widetilde{v}_{n})\to c,\quad\Psi^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{n})\to 0,

\Phi(\widetilde{u}_{n})\to c,\quad\Phi^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}_{n})\to 0.

We may assume that
\|v_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq c>0.

Since
\Omega
is bounded, there exists at least one point, say
x_{0}\in\Omega
, such that
v_{n}(x_{0})\to v(x_{0})\geq c>0
. Therefore,
|u_{n}(x_{0})|=\|u_{n}\||v_{n}(x_{0})|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty.
We have

\displaystyle 0<\dfrac{\Phi(u_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}-\int_{\mathbb{V}}\dfrac{F(x,u_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|^{2}}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{F(x_{0},u_{n})}{u_{n}^{2}(x_{0})}v_{n}^{2}(x_{0})\,\to-\infty\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

It is a contradiction, so that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded.
For bounded
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, one has a subsequence
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. By above discussions, it is pointwise convergent. For any
w\in C_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})w

\displaystyle=\,\langle u_{n},w\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})w\,d\mu

\displaystyle\to\,\langle u,w\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(x,u)w\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\Phi^{\prime}(u)w.

We have
\Phi^{\prime}(u)=0
, for
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
.
If
u\neq 0
, then
u
is a critical point of
\Phi
; otherwise, one can get a nontrivial critical point of
\Phi
by translation.
In the case of
u=0
, suppose
u_{n}\to 0
in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\big|\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})u_{n}d\mu\big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|f(x,u_{n})||u_{n}|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\big(\epsilon|u_{n}|+C_{\epsilon}|u_{n}|^{q-1}\big)|u_{n}|d\mu

\displaystyle=\epsilon\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\|u_{n}\|^{q}_{q}

Since
\|u_{n}\|_{2}\simeq\|u_{n}\|
, one can choose
\epsilon
sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})u_{n}

\displaystyle=\|u_{n}\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})u_{n}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{n}\|^{2}-C_{\epsilon}\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}.

Letting
n\to\infty
, then
\|u_{n}\|_{q}\to 0
and
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
imply
\|u_{n}\|\to 0.
This contradicts the fact that
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{N}
is bounded away from
0
. Hence,

u_{n}\nrightarrow 0
in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}).

By translation again, one can get a sequence
\{\widehat{u}_{n}\}
such that

\|\widehat{u}_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq c>0,\quad,\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

for some constant
c>0
.
Since
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{N}
are invariant under translation, then
\{\widehat{u}_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{N}
and

\Phi(\widehat{u}_{n})\to c,\quad\Phi^{\prime}(\widehat{u}_{n})\to 0.

By passing to a subsequence, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{n}\rightharpoonup\widehat{u}\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}),

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{n}(x)\to\widehat{u}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},

and
\widehat{u}\neq 0.
By the same argument, one has

\Phi^{\prime}(\widehat{u})=0,
i.e.,
\widehat{u}
is a nontrivial critical point of
\Phi
and
\widehat{u}\in\mathcal{N}
.

Finally, we prove that the nontrivial critical point
u
(or
\widehat{u}
) satisfies

\Phi(u)=\inf_{\mathcal{N}}\Phi\equiv c.

We have an elementary estimate that, for any
u\neq 0
,

F(x,u)\leq\frac{1}{2}f(x,u)u.

Combined with Fatou’s Lemma, we have

\displaystyle c+o(1)

\displaystyle=\Phi(u_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})u_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\big(\frac{1}{2}f(x,u_{n})u_{n}-F(x,u_{n})\big)d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\big(\frac{1}{2}f(x,u)u-F(x,u)\big)d\mu+o(1)

\displaystyle=\Phi(u)-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime}(u)u+o(1)

\displaystyle=\Phi(u)+o(1),\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Hence,
\Phi(u)\leq c\equiv\inf_{\mathcal{N}}\Phi.

So that
\Phi(u)=c\equiv\inf_{\mathcal{N}}\Phi.

∎

Remark 4.1
.

Once we know
v_{n}\nrightarrow 0
in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, one can use Concentration-Compactness Principle in the Euclidean space by P.L.Lions
[
Wil96
,
Lio85
,
Str08
]
to get a sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\int_{B_{r}(y_{n})}v_{n}^{2}\geq\delta

for some
\delta>0
.
Inspired by this, we prove a similar result on lattice graphs to overcome the loss of compactness. In fact, we can find a sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z^{N}}
such that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

|u_{n}(y_{n})|\geq c

for some constant
c>0
. This is sufficient for our proof.

(7)

\displaystyle\big|\varphi^{\prime}(w)v\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}w(x)v(x)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\big(w(y)-w(x)\big)\big(v(y)-v(x)\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\big|w(y)-w(x)\big|\big(|v(y)|+|v(x)|\big).

For the second term in the last line of (
7
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\big|w(y)-w(x)\big|\big(|v(y)|+|v(x)|\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}|w(y)||v(x)|+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}|w(x)||v(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}|w(y)|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}|v(x)|^{2}\big)^{1/2}+C\|w\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\|w\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}.

Therefore,

\|z\|\leq\|z+tw\|+|t|\|w\|\leq(1+C^{\prime})\|z+tw\|,

where
C^{\prime}
is a positive constant.
We show that

(8)

\|\Psi^{\prime}(w)\|\leq\|u\|\|\Phi^{\prime}(u)\|\leq(C^{\prime}+1)\|\Psi^{\prime}(w)\|,

where
w\in S,\,u=m(w)\in\mathcal{N}\text{ and }\|u\|\geq\delta>0.

On one hand,

\displaystyle\|\Psi^{\prime}(w)\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}z\in T_{w}S\\
\|z\|=1\end{array}$}}|\Psi^{\prime}(w)z|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}z\in T_{w}S\\
\|z\|=1\end{array}$}}\big|\Phi^{\prime}(m(w))z\big|\cdot\|m(w)\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}z\in T_{w}S\\
\|z\|=1\end{array}$}}\big|\Phi^{\prime}(u)z\big|\cdot\|u\|

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|\cdot\|\Phi^{\prime}(u)\|

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|u\|\|\Phi^{\prime}(u)\|

\displaystyle=\|u\|\sup_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}z+tw\neq 0\\
z\in T_{w}S\end{array}$}}\dfrac{\big|\Phi^{\prime}(u)(z+tw)\big|}{\|z+tw\|}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|(1+C^{\prime})\sup_{z\in T_{w}S}\dfrac{\big|\Phi^{\prime}(u)z\big|}{\|z\|}.

Since
\Phi^{\prime}(u)w=\frac{1}{\|u\|}\Phi^{\prime}(u)u=0
and
u=m(w),
we have

\displaystyle\|u\|\|\Phi^{\prime}(u)\|

\displaystyle=(1+C^{\prime})\sup_{z\in T_{w}S}\dfrac{\|m(w)\|\big|\Phi^{\prime}(m(w))z\big|}{\|z\|}

\displaystyle=(1+C^{\prime})\sup_{z\in T_{w}S}\dfrac{\big|\Psi^{\prime}(w)z\big|}{\|z\|}

\displaystyle=(1+C^{\prime})\|\Psi^{\prime}(w)\|.

If
\{u_{n}\}
is a bounded
Palais-Smale sequence
for
\Phi
, that is,
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
and
\delta\leq\|u_{n}\|\leq C
for some constant
0<\delta<C,
then
\Psi(w_{n})\equiv\Psi\big(\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}\big)
is bounded as well. Moreover,

\|\Psi^{\prime}(w_{n})\|\leq\|u_{n}\|\|\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\|\leq C\|\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\|\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

We conclude that
\{w_{n}\}
is a bounded
Palais-Smale sequence
for
\Psi
.
By (
8
) one can see that
\|\Psi^{\prime}(w_{0})\|=0
if and only if
\|\Phi^{\prime}(u_{0})\|=0
with
u_{0}=m(w_{0}).
By definition,
\Phi(u_{0})=\Psi(w_{0})
. Since
\Phi\big|_{\mathcal{N}}>0
is bounded away from below, it has an infimum and
\inf_{\mathcal{N}}\Phi=\inf_{S}\Psi
.
∎

The generalized 4-connectivity of godan graphs
*

Abstract




Abstract
The generalized
k
-connectivity of a graph
G
, denoted by
\kappa_{k}(G)
, is the minimum number of internally edge disjoint
S
-trees for any
S\subseteq V(G)
and
|S|=k
. The generalized
k
-connectivity is a natural extension of the classical connectivity and plays a key role in applications related to the modern interconnection networks. The godan graph
EA_{n}
is a kind of Cayley graphs which posses many desirable properties. In this paper, we shall study the generalized 4-connectivity of
EA_{n}
and show that
\kappa_{4}(EA_{n})=n-1
for
n\geq 3
.

Keywords
interconnection network, godan graph, generalized
k
-connectivity, tree

MR(2000) Subject Classification
05C40, 05C05

1
Introduction

A network is usually represented by a connected graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
, where
V(G)
is the set of processors and
E(G)
is the set of communication links between processors. Fault tolerance has become increasingly significant nowadays since multiprocessor systems failure is inevitable. It is important to consider how the network performs in the event of a certain number of nodes of failure and/or links failure in the network topology. The connectivity is an important parameter for measuring the fault tolerance of the network. A subset
S\subseteq V(G)
of a connected graph
G
is called a
vertex-cut
if
G\setminus S
is disconnected or trivial. The
connectivity

\kappa(G)
of
G
is defined as the minimum cardinality over all vertex-cuts of
G
. Note that the larger
\kappa(G)
is, the more reliable the network is.

Let
P
be a path in
G
with
x
and
y
as its two terminal vertices, then
P
is called an
(x,y)
-
path
. Two (
x,y
)-paths
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are
internally disjoint
if they have no common internal vertices, that is,
V(P_{1})\cap V(P_{2})=\{x,y\}
. A well known theorem of Whitney
[
20
]
provides an equivalent definition of connectivity. For each 2-subset
S=\{x,y\}\subseteq V(G)
, let
\kappa(S)
denote the maximum number of internally disjoint (
x,y
)-paths in
G
. Then

\kappa(G)=\min\{\kappa(S)|S\subseteq V(G)\;{\rm and}\;|S|=2\}.

The generalized
k
-connectivity, which was introduced by Chartrand et al.
[
2
]
, is a strengthening of connectivity and can be served as an essential parameter for measuring reliability and fault tolerance of the network. Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a simple graph,
S
be a subset of
V(G)
. A tree
T
in
G
is called an
S
-
tree
, if
S\subseteq V(T)
. The trees
T_{1},T_{2},\cdots,T_{r}
are called
internally edge disjoint
S
-trees
if
V(T_{i})\cap V(T_{j})=S
and
E(T_{i})\cap E(T_{j})=\emptyset
for any integers
1\leq i<j\leq r
. Throughout this paper, we will use IDSTs to denote internally edge disjoint
S
-trees for short. Let
\kappa_{G}(S)
denote the maximum number of IDSTs.

For an integer
k
with
2\leq k\leq|V(G)|
, the
generalized
k
-connectivity
of
G
, denoted by
\kappa_{k}(G)
, is defined as

\kappa_{k}(G)=\min\{\kappa_{G}(S)|S\subseteq V(G)\;{\rm and}\;|S|=k\}.

The generalized 2-connectivity is exactly the classical connectivity. Over the past few years, research on the generalized connectivity has received meaningful progress. Li et al.
[
12
]
derived that it is NP-complete for a general graph
G
to decide whether there are
l
internally edge disjoint trees connecting
S
, where
l
is a fixed integer and
S\subseteq V(G)
. Authors in
[
7
,
11
]
investigated the upper and lower bounds of the generalized connectivity of a general graph
G
.

For
3\leq k\leq|V(G)|
, Li gave an upper bound of
\kappa_{k}(G)
for a general graph
G
in her Ph.D. thesis
[
9
]
.

Lemma 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
Let
G
be a connected graph with minimum degree
\delta(G)
. If there are two adjacent vertices of degree
\delta(G)
, then
\kappa_{k}(G)\leq\delta(G)-1
for
3\leq k\leq|V(G)|
.

The following result is about the relationship between
\kappa_{k}(G)
and
\kappa_{k-1}(G)
of a regular graph
G
.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
15
]
)
Let
G
be an
r
-regular graph. If
\kappa_{k}(G)=r-1
, then
\kappa_{k-1}(G)=r-1
, where
k\geq 4
.

Many authors tried to study exact values of the generalized connectivity of graphs. The generalized
k
-connectivity of the complete graphs,
\kappa_{k}(K_{n})
, was determined in
[
3
]
for every pair
k,n
of integers with
2\leq k\leq n
. The generalized
k
-connectivity of the complete bipartite graphs
K_{a,b}
was obtained in
[
10
]
for all
2\leq k\leq a+b
. Apart from these results, the generalized
k
-connectivity of hypercubes
[
8
,
15
]
, the generalized hypercubes
[
17
]
, balanced hypercubes
[
19
]
, exchanged hypercubes
[
22
]
, dual cubes
[
26
]
, star graphs and bubble-sort graphs
[
14
]
, (
n,k
)-star graphs
[
6
,
24
]
, bubble-sort star graphs
[
23
]
, pancake graphs
[
25
]
, Cayley graphs generated by trees and cycles
[
13
]
, et al, have drawn many scholars’ attention. So far, as we can see, the results on the generalized
k
-connectivity of networks were almost about
k\leq 4
.

Ren and Wang
[
16
]
introduced the
n
-dimensional godan graph
EA_{n}
as a topology structure of interconnection networks, they proved that
EA_{n}
poses many desirable properties, such as regularity, high connectivity and vertex-transitivity. The authors in
[
16
]
also gave various connectivity, such as nature connectivity, 2-good-neighbour connectivity, 2-extra connectivity, of
EA_{n}
. The component connectivity of
EA_{n}
was studied in
[
21
]
. In this paper, we try to calculate the generalized 4-connectivity of
EA_{n}
and obtain the following main result.

Theorem 1.1

Let
EA_{n}
be the
n
-dimensional godan graph for
n\geq 3
. Then
\kappa_{4}(EA_{n})=n-1
.

We end this section with some necessary preliminaries. We only consider a simple, connected graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. For
x\in V(G)
, the
degree
of
x
in
G
, denoted by
{\rm deg}_{G}(x)
, is the number of edges of
G
incident with
x
. Denote
\delta(G)
the
minimum degree
of vertices of
G
. A graph is
d
-
regular
if
{\rm deg}_{G}(x)=d
for every
x\in V(G)
. For
x\in V(G)
, we use
N_{G}(x)
to denote the set of neighbours of
x
in
G
. Let
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
, denote by
G\backslash V^{\prime}
the graph obtained from
G
by deleting all the vertices in
V^{\prime}
together with their incident edges. Denote by
G[V^{\prime}]
the subgraph of
G
induced on
V^{\prime}
.

The following Lemma
1.3
, Lemma
1.4
and Lemma
1.5
are results on the connectivity of a graph that are well-known in the literature.

Lemma 1.3

(
[
1
]
)
Let
G
be a
k
-connected graph, and let
x
and
y
be a pair of distinct vertices of
G
. Then there exist
k
internally disjoint (

)-paths in
G
.

Lemma 1.4

(
[
1
]
)
Let
G
be a
k
-connected graph, let
x
be a vertex of
G
and let
Y\subseteq V(G)\backslash\{x\}
be a set of at least
k
vertices of
G
. Then there exists a
k
-fan in
G
from
x
to
Y
, that is, there exists a family of
k
internally disjoint (
x,Y
)-paths whose terminal vertices are distinct in
Y
.

Lemma 1.5

(
[
1
]
)
Let
G
be a
k
-connected graph, and let
X
and
Y
be subsets of
V(G)
of cardinality at least
k
. Then there exists a family of
k
pairwise disjoint (

)-paths in
G
.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents the definition and properties of godan graphs. We note that the lower bound of
\kappa_{4}(EA_{n})
is critical to obtain the main result. Therefore, some lemmas which will be used to obtain the lower bound of

are given in Section

.

2
Definition and properties of godan graph

Let
[n]=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
. For convenience, we denote the permutation
\scriptsize{\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&n\\
p_{1}&p_{2}&\cdots&p_{n}\end{pmatrix}}
by
p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{n}
and the permutation
\scriptsize{\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&i&\cdots&j\cdots&n\\
1&\cdots&j&\cdots&i\cdots&n\end{pmatrix}}
by
(ij)
, while the latter is called a transposition. The composition
\sigma\circ\tau
of two permutations
\sigma
and
\tau
is the function that maps any element
i
to
\sigma(\tau(i))
.

Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group on
[n]
. The alternating group
A_{n}
(
n\geq 3
) is the subgroup of
S_{n}
containing all even permutations. Let
\Omega=\{(123),(132)\}\cup\{(12)(3i)|4\leq i\leq n\}
. For
n\geq 3
, the
n
-dimensional alternating group network
AN_{n}
(
[
5
]
) is a graph with vertex set
V(AN_{n})=A_{n}
and edge set
E(AN_{n})=\{uv|u=v\circ s,\;{\rm where}\;\{u,v\}\subseteq V(AN_{n})\;{\rm and}\;s\in\Omega\}
.

The alternating group network
AN_{3}
is depicted in Figure
2
. It is worth mentioning that
AN_{n}
is in fact isomorphic to the
(n,n-2)
-star
S_{n,n-2}

[
4
]
, whose generalized 4-connectivity is proved to be (
n-2
) in
[
6
]
.

The alternating group network
AN_{3}

Figure 2:
The godan graph
EA_{3}

Lemma 2.1

(
[
6
,
27
]
)
For the
n
-dimensional alternating group network
AN_{n}
(
n\geq 3
), the following properties hold:

(1)
AN_{n}
is (
n-1
)-regular with
\frac{n!}{2}
vertices and
\frac{n!(n-1)}{4}
edges;

(2)
\kappa(AN_{n})=n-1
;

(3)
\kappa_{4}(AN_{n})=\kappa_{4}(S_{n,n-2})=n-2
.

In
[
16
]
, Ren and Wang introduced the
n
-dimensional godan graph
EA_{n}
as a topology structure of interconnection networks.

Definition 2.1

(
[
16
]
)
For
n\geq 3
, let
\Omega^{*}=\{(12),(123),(132)\}\cup\{(12)(3i)|4\leq i\leq n\}
. The
n
-dimensional godan graph
EA_{n}
is the graph with vertex set
V(EA_{n})=S_{n}
in which two vertices
u
and
v
are adjacent in
EA_{n}
if and only if
u=v\circ s^{*}
, where
s^{*}\in\Omega^{*}
.

The godan graphs
EA_{3}
and
EA_{4}
are depicted in Figure
2
and Figure
3
, respectively. Some properties of
EA_{n}
are listed in Lemma
2.2
.

Figure 3:
The godan graph
EA_{4}

Lemma 2.2

(
[
16
,
21
]
)
For
n\geq 3
, the
n
-dimensional godan graph
EA_{n}
has the following properties:

(1)
EA_{n}
is
n
-regular with
n!
vertices and
\frac{n\times n!}{2}
edges;

(2)
EA_{n}[A_{n}]\cong AN_{n}
,
EA_{n}[S_{n}\backslash A_{n}]\cong AN_{n}
;

(3)
E(A_{n},S_{n}\backslash A_{n})
is a perfect matching of
EA_{n}
, where
E(A_{n},S_{n}\backslash A_{n})
denotes all cross edges between
A_{n}
and
S_{n}\backslash A_{n}
;

(4)
\kappa(EA_{n})=n
.

It is seen that both
EA_{n}[A_{n}]
and
EA_{n}[S_{n}\backslash A_{n}]
are isomorphic to
AN_{n}
. For simplicity, we denote
EA_{n}[A_{n}]
by
AN_{n}^{1}
and denote
EA_{n}[S_{n}\backslash A_{n}]
by
AN_{n}^{2}
, respectively. Then we can write the construction of
EA_{n}
symbolically as

EA_{n}=AN_{n}^{1}\otimes AN_{n}^{2}.

For
1\leq i\leq 2
, let
x
be a vertex in
AN_{n}^{i}
, it is clear that
x
has a unique neighbor
x\circ(12)
in
V(AN_{n}^{3-i})
. Denote
x\circ(12)
by
\widetilde{x}
. We say that
\widetilde{x}
is the
parity neighbour
of
x
. Due to Definition
2.1
, the following result is an easy observation.

Lemma 2.3

For
n\geq 3
, let
EA_{n}=AN_{n}^{1}\otimes AN_{n}^{2}
and
x
be a vertex of
AN_{n}^{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 2
. If
N_{AN_{n}^{i}}(x)=\{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n-1}\}
. Then
N_{AN_{n}^{3-i}}(\widetilde{x})=\{\widetilde{b}_{1},\widetilde{b}_{2},\cdots,\widetilde{b}_{n-1}\}
.

Lemma 2.4

For
n\geq 4
, let
x
be a vertex of
EA_{n}
with
N_{EA_{n}}(x)=\{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n-1},\widetilde{x}\}
. Set
B=\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n-2},\widetilde{x},v\}
, where
v
is a neighbour of
\widetilde{x}
with
v\neq x
. Then
EA_{n}\backslash B
is connected.

Proof
Remind that
EA_{n}=AN_{n}^{1}\otimes AN_{n}^{2}
. By symmetry, assume that
x\in V(AN_{n}^{1})
. Let
B_{1}=\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n-2}\}
and
B_{2}=\{\widetilde{x},v\}
.

By Definition
2.1
, for
1\leq i\leq 2
,
B_{i}=B\cap V(AN_{n}^{i})
because
v\neq x
.

By Lemma
2.1
(2), for
1\leq i\leq 2
,
AN_{n}^{i}\backslash B_{i}
is connected since
|B_{1}|=n-2
and
|B_{2}|=2
. Since
\frac{n!}{2}>n
for
n\geq 4
and
E(A_{n},S_{n}\backslash A_{n})
is a perfect matching of
EA_{n}
,
EA_{n}\backslash B
is still connected.
\Box

Now, we shall investigate the structure of
EA_{n}
from another perspective. Let
EA_{n}^{m:i}
, called a
cluster
, be a subgraph of
EA_{n}
induced on vertices with the
m
th symbol is
i
, where
4\leq m\leq n
and
i\in[n]
. Note that each cluster
EA_{n}^{m:i}
is isomorphic to
EA_{n-1}
,
4\leq m\leq n
and
i\in[n]
. Thus, the construction of
EA_{n}
may be described as

EA_{n}=EA_{n}^{m:1}\oplus EA_{n}^{m:2}\oplus\cdots\oplus EA_{n}^{m:n},\;\;4\leq m\leq n.

For simplicity, we use
EA_{n}^{i}
to denote
EA_{n}^{n:i}
in the rest of this paper,
i\in[n]
. Thus, we may write the construction of
EA_{n}
symbolically as

EA_{n}=EA_{n}^{1}\oplus EA_{n}^{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus EA_{n}^{n}.

For
i\in[n]
, let
x
be a vertex in
EA_{n}^{i}
. Clearly,
x
has (
n-1
) neighbours in
EA_{n}^{i}
and exactly one neighbor
x^{\prime}=x\circ(12)(3n)
out of
EA_{n}^{i}
. We say that
x^{\prime}
is the
out neighbour
of
x
.

By Definition
2.1
, some properties of
EA_{n}
can be derived directly, which are presented in Lemma
2.5
.

Lemma 2.5

For
n\geq 3
, let
EA_{n}=EA_{n}^{1}\oplus EA_{n}^{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus EA_{n}^{n}.
The following properties hold:

(1)
|E(EA_{n}^{i},EA_{n}^{j})|=(n-2)!
, where
i
and
j
are distinct integers in
[n]
.

(2) Let
x\in V(EA_{n}^{i})
,
i\in[n]
. Then
|N_{EA_{n}^{i}}(x)|=n-1
. Furthermore, the vertices in
N_{EA_{n}^{i}}(x)
can be ordered in an unique way
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{i-1}
,
x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{n}
such that
x_{j}^{\prime}\in V(EA_{n}^{j})
for
j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}
. We use
P[x,x_{j}^{\prime}]
to denote the path
\{xx_{j},x_{j}x_{j}^{\prime}\}
, where
j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}
.

(3) Let
x\in V(EA_{n}^{i})
and
x_{j}
be a neighbour of
x
in
EA_{n}^{i}
,
\{i,j\}\subseteq[n]
and
j\neq i
. Then
x^{\prime}
and
x_{j}^{\prime}
belong to a same cluster of
EA_{n}
if and only if
x_{j}=x\circ(12)
. Moreover,
x_{j}^{\prime}=x^{\prime}\circ(12)
.

(4) Let
x,y
and
z
be three distinct vertices of
EA_{n}
. The subgraph induced on
\{x,y,z\}
is a 3-cycle if and only if
\{y,z\}=\{x\circ(123),x\circ(132)\}
.

We end this section with Lemma
2.6
which determines the lower bound of the connectivity of a subgraph of
EA_{n}
.

Lemma 2.6

For
n\geq 4
, let
EA_{n}=EA_{n}^{1}\oplus EA_{n}^{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus EA_{n}^{n}.
Denote
H=\bigcup_{i=1}^{h}EA_{n}^{k_{i}}
be a subgraph of
EA_{n}
, where
k_{i}\in[n]
and
h\geq 2
. Then
\kappa(H)\geq n-2
.

Proof
Let
x
and
y
be any two distinct vertices in
H
. It suffices to prove that there are (
n-2
) internally disjoint (
x,y
)-paths in
H
.

Case 1
. Both
x
and
y
belong to a same cluster, say
EA_{n}^{k_{1}}
.

By Lemma
2.2
(4), there are (
n-1
) internally disjoint (
x,y
)-paths in
EA_{n}^{k_{1}}
since
\kappa(EA_{n}^{k_{1}})=\kappa(EA_{n-1})=n-1
.

Case 2
.
x
and
y
belong to different clusters of
EA_{n}
.

W.l.o.g., assume that
x\in V(EA_{n}^{k_{1}})
and
y\in V(EA_{n}^{k_{2}})
. Based on Lemma
2.5
(1),
|E(EA_{n}^{k_{1}},EA_{n}^{k_{2}})|=(n-2)!\geq n-2
for
n\geq 4
. We may choose (
n-2
) different vertices
u_{i}\in V(EA_{n}^{k_{1}})
such that
u_{i}^{\prime}\in V(EA_{n}^{k_{2}})
,
i\in[n-2]
. Combined with Lemma
1.4
and Lemma
2.2
(4), there is an (
n-2
)-fan
L_{1},L_{2},\cdots,L_{n-2}
in
EA_{n}^{k_{1}}
from
x
to
\{u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{n-2}\}
such that
u_{i}\in V(L_{i})
, where
i\in[n-2]
. Likewise, there is an (
n-2
)-fan
Q_{1},Q_{2},\cdots,Q_{n-2}
in
EA_{n}^{k_{2}}
from
y
to
\{u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,u_{n-2}^{\prime}\}
such that
u_{i}^{\prime}\in V(Q_{i})
,
i\in[n-2]
.

For
i\in[n-2]
, let
P_{i}=L_{i}\cup Q_{i}\cup\{u_{i}u_{i}^{\prime}\}
. Then
P_{1},\cdots,P_{n-2}
are (
n-2
) internally disjoint (
x,y
)-paths in
H
.
\Box

3
Some lemmas

The lower bound of
\kappa_{4}(EA_{n})
is the key to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We shall make great efforts to provide several lemmas to obtain the lower bound of
\kappa_{4}(EA_{n})
for
n\geq 4
.

Lemma 3.1

For
n\geq 4
, let
EA_{n}=AN_{n}^{1}\otimes AN_{n}^{2}
and
S=\{x,y,z,w\}
be any 4-subset of
V(EA_{n})
. If there exists an integer
i
(
1\leq i\leq 2
) such that
S\subseteq V(AN_{n}^{i})
. Then there are (
n-1
) IDSTs in
EA_{n}
.

Proof
W.l.o.g., assume that
S\subseteq V(AN_{n}^{1})
. Then there are (
n-2
) IDSTs
T_{1},\cdots,T_{n-2}
in
AN_{n}^{1}
by Lemma
2.1
(3) and Lemma
2.2
(2).

Note that
\{\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y},\widetilde{z},\widetilde{w}\}\subseteq V(AN_{n}^{2})
. There is a
\{\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y},\widetilde{z},\widetilde{w}\}
-tree
T_{n-1}^{\prime}
in
AN_{n}^{2}
. Let
T_{n-1}=T_{n-1}^{\prime}\cup\{x\widetilde{x},y\widetilde{y},z\widetilde{z},w\widetilde{w}\}
. Clearly,
T_{1},\cdots,T_{n-1}
are (
n-1
) IDSTs in
EA_{n}
.
\Box

Lemma 3.2

For
n\geq 4
, let
EA_{n}=AN_{n}^{1}\otimes AN_{n}^{2}
and
S=\{x,y,z,w\}
be any 4-subset of
V(EA_{n})
satisfying the following three conditions:

(1)
\{x,y,z\}\subseteq V(AN_{n}^{1})
and
w\in V(AN_{n}^{2})
;

(2)
\widetilde{w}\notin\{x,y,z\}
;

(3)
|E(\widetilde{w},\{x,y,z\})|\leq 1
.

Then there are (
n-1
) IDSTs in
EA_{n}
.

Proof
Based on Lemma
2.1
(3), there are (
n-2
) internally edge disjoint
\{x,y,z,\widetilde{w}\}
-trees
T_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,T_{n-2}^{\prime}
in
AN_{n}^{1}
. Furthermore, it has
1\leq{\rm deg}_{T_{i}^{\prime}}(\widetilde{w})\leq 2
for each
i\in[n-2]
since
{\rm deg}_{AN_{n}^{1}}(\widetilde{w})=n-1
. For convenience, we may assume that
{\rm deg}_{T_{i}^{\prime}}(\widetilde{w})=1
and
\widetilde{u}_{i}
be a neighbour of
\widetilde{w}
in
T_{i}^{\prime}
, where
i\in[n-3]
.

Let
\widetilde{U}=\{\widetilde{u}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{u}_{n-3}\}
and
U=\{u_{i}|~\widetilde{u}_{i}\in\widetilde{U},~i\in[n-3]\}.

Note that
U\subseteq V(AN_{n}^{2})
and
|U|=n-3
. Thus
AN_{n}^{2}\backslash U
is still connected since
\kappa(AN_{n}^{2})=n-1
. The following two cases are considered.

5
Conclusion

The generalized
k
-connectivity is a natural generalization of the classical connectivity and can serve for measuring the capability of a network
G
to connect any
k

G
. In this paper, we focus on the generalized 4-connectivity of the
n
-dimensional godan graph
EA_{n}
. Together with Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
1.2
, the generalized 3-connectivity of
EA_{n}
can be derived immediately. This result was also investigated in
[
18
]
.

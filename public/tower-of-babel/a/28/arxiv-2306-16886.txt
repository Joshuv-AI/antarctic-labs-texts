2.
Notation and preliminary results

For prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{8}
, the Dirichlet
L
-function with respect to
\chi_{p}(\cdot)
is

(2.1)

L(s,\chi_{p})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi_{p}(n)n^{-s}.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
28
]
, Theorem 5.3)
.

For prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{8}
, we have

L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{p})=\frac{2}{(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{2}}\sum_{\textrm{odd }n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{p}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}V(n\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{p}}),

where
V(y)
is defined by

(2.2)

V(y):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(u)}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})}(1-\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{2}-s}})y^{-s}e^{s^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}

with
u>0
. Here, and in sequel,
\int_{(u)}
stands for
\int_{u-i\infty}^{u+i\infty}
.
The function
V(y)
is smooth in
y>0
and satisfies
y^{h}V^{(h)}(y)\ll_{A,N}y^{-A}
for non-negative integers
h
and large positive constant
A
, and we have
V(y)=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+O(y^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon})
as
y\to 0
.

Let
\Phi
be a smooth and nonnegative function in Schwarz class supported on
[1,2]
with
\Phi(x)=1
for
x\in[\frac{7}{6},\frac{11}{6}]
, and for any complex number
s
we define

(2.3)

\Phi_{s}(x):=\Phi(x)x^{\frac{s}{2}}\textrm{, and }\check{\Phi}(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(x)x^{s}\mathrm{d}x.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
39
]
, Proposition 1)
.

Writing
\sum\nolimits^{\flat}
as the summation over square-free numbers.
We have

(2.4)

\sum_{d\equiv 1\pmod{8}}\nolimits^{\flat}\chi_{d}(n)\Phi(\frac{d}{X})=\check{\Phi}(0)\frac{X}{\pi^{2}}\prod_{p\mid n}(1+\frac{1}{p})^{-1}\delta_{n=\square}+O(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\sqrt{n}),

where the symbol
\delta_{n=\square}
equals to
1
for
n
is square and 0 otherwise.

3.
Soundararajan’s resonance method

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We introduce
M=X^{\theta}
with some
\theta<\frac{1}{4}
.
Set
\mathcal{L}=\sqrt{\log M\log\log M}
.
Following Soundararajan’s resonance method
[
47
]
, we choose the resonator coefficient
b(\cdot)
to be a multiplicative function supported on square-free numbers with
b(p)=\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{p}\log p}
for
\mathcal{L}^{2}\leq p\leq\exp((\log\mathcal{L})^{2})
and
b(p)=0
otherwise.
Let

(3.1)

R(p)=\sum_{\textrm{odd }m\leq M}b(m)\chi_{p}(m).

In the following we will omit the odd condition of the variables due to the definition of
b(\cdot)
.
Let

\displaystyle M_{1}(R,X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\equiv 1\pmod{8}}\nolimits^{\flat}R(d)^{2}\Phi(\frac{d}{X})

\displaystyle=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2}\leq M\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}b(m_{1})b(m_{2})\sum_{d\equiv 1\pmod{8}}\nolimits^{\flat}\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})\Phi(\frac{d}{X})=\mathcal{M}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{1},

where , by Lemma
2.2
,
m\leq M<\frac{X}{2}\leq p
and trivial estimates,

\mathcal{M}_{1}=\sum_{m\leq M}b(m)^{2}\sum_{d\equiv 1\pmod{8}}\nolimits^{\flat}\Phi(\frac{d}{X})=\check{\Phi}(0)\frac{X}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{m\leq M}b(m)^{2}\prod_{p\mid m}(1+\frac{1}{p})^{-1}+O(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}M)

is the diaogonal part of it and

\mathcal{R}_{1}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2}\leq M\\
m_{1}\neq m_{2}\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}b(m_{1})b(m_{2})\sum_{d\equiv 1\pmod{8}}\nolimits^{\flat}\chi_{d}(m_{1}m_{2})\Phi(\frac{d}{X})=O(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}M^{3})

is the off-diaogonal part.

Let

\displaystyle M_{2}(R,X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\textrm{prime }p\equiv 1\pmod{8}}(\log p)L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{p})R(p)^{2}\Phi(\frac{p}{X})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{2}}\sum_{\textrm{prime }p\equiv 1\pmod{8}}(\log p)\Phi(\frac{p}{X})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2}\leq M\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}b(m_{1})b(m_{2})\frac{\chi_{p}(nm_{1}m_{2})}{\sqrt{n}}V(n\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{p}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{2}+\mathcal{R}_{2},

where,
after removing the coprime restrictions for
m_{1},m_{2}
with respect to
p
since
m_{1},m_{2}\leq M<\frac{X}{2}\leq p
,

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}_{2}=\frac{2}{(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{2}}\sum_{\textrm{prime }p\equiv 1\pmod{8}}(\log p)\Phi(\frac{p}{X})\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(u)}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})}(1-\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{2}-s}})\\
&\hskip 30.0pt\times\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}nm_{1}m_{2}=\square\\
m_{1},m_{2}\leq M\\
(n,p)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum\sum}\frac{b(m_{1})b(m_{2})}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+s}}(\frac{p}{\pi})^{\frac{s}{2}}e^{s^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}_{2}=\frac{2}{(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{2}}\sum_{\textrm{prime }p\equiv 1\pmod{8}}(\log p)\Phi(\frac{p}{X})\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(u)}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})}(1-\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{2}-s}})\\
&\hskip 30.0pt\times\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}nm_{1}m_{2}\neq\square\\
m_{1},m_{2}\leq M\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum\sum}\frac{b(m_{1})b(m_{2})}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+s}}\chi_{p}(nm_{1}m_{2})(\frac{p}{\pi})^{\frac{s}{2}}e^{s^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}.\end{split}

We have the following two propositions which will be proved in later sections.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
\theta<1/4
,

(3.2)

\mathcal{M}_{1}\ll X\prod_{\mathcal{L}^{2}\leq p\leq\exp((\log\mathcal{L})^{2})}(1+b(p)^{2})

and

(3.3)

\mathcal{M}_{2}\gg X\log X\prod_{\mathcal{L}^{2}\leq p\leq\exp((\log\mathcal{L})^{2})}(1+2\frac{b(p)}{\sqrt{p}}+b(p)^{2}).

Proposition 3.2
.

For
\theta\leq\frac{1}{19}-\varepsilon
,

(3.4)

\mathcal{R}_{2}=O(\mathcal{M}_{2}X^{-\varepsilon}).

Choose
\theta=\frac{1}{19}-\varepsilon
,
then we end the proof of Theorem
1.1
by using

(3.5)

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<p\leq 2X\\
\textrm{prime }p\equiv 1\pmod{8}\end{subarray}}L(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{p})\geq\frac{M_{2}(R,X)}{M_{1}(R,X)\log 2X}\gg\prod_{\mathcal{L}^{2}\leq p\leq\exp((\log\mathcal{L})^{2})}(1+2\frac{b(p)}{\sqrt{p}})\\
\gg\exp(2\mathcal{L}\sum_{\mathcal{L}^{2}\leq p\leq\exp((\log\mathcal{L})^{2})}\frac{1}{p\log p})=\exp\Big((2\sqrt{\theta}+o(1))\frac{\sqrt{\log X}}{\sqrt{\log\log X}}\Big).

∎

4.

3.1
.

For
m_{1},m_{2}
square-free, we write

Thus

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{2}&=\frac{2}{(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})^{2}}\underset{m_{1},m_{2}\leq M}{\sum\sum}b(m_{1})b(m_{2})\sum_{\textrm{prime }p\equiv 1\pmod{8}}(\log p)\Phi(\frac{p}{X})\\

We have the Laurent series expansions

\begin{gathered}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})}=1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4})}s+\dots=1-\frac{1691}{800}s+\dots;\\

where

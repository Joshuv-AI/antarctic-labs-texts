Abstract
. We characterise the model-theoretic algebraic closure in Zilber’s exponential field. A key step involves showing that certain algebraic varieties have finite intersections with certain finite-rank subgroups of the graph of exponentiation. Mordell-Lang for algebraic tori (a theorem of Laurent) plays a central role in our proof.

1.
Introduction

Given a complex number
a
and a subfield
K
of the complex field, we can ask if
a
is
algebraic
over
K
, that is, satisfies a non-trivial polynomial equation with coefficients in
K
. If we look purely at the field structure then this is essentially all we can say about
a
given
K
; in model-theoretic terms, the minimal polynomial of
a
over
K
(or the fact that
a
is transcendental over
K
) completely determines the
type
of
a
over
K
.
When
a
is algebraic over
K
, this type has only finitely many realisations: the conjugates of
a
over
K
.

If we now add the exponential function to the field then this single notion of algebraicity generalises into a number of distinct concepts.
One could ask for
a
to satisfy some exponential polynomial equation with coefficients from
K
, but this is not particularly useful, as it neither pins down
a
to a finite set, nor does this concept even give a closure operator.

The generalisation of algebraicity central to this paper is the one called
model-theoretic algebraicity
: an element
a
of a structure
M
is algebraic over a subset
B\subseteq M
if it lies in a finite set which is definable by a first-order formula with parameters from
B
. Equivalently, the type of
a
over
B
in
M
has only finitely many realisations, so is said to be an algebraic type. The
(model-theoretic) algebraic closure

\acl_{M}(B)
of
B
in
M
is the union of all the finite subsets of
M
which are definable over
B
. We consider this concept in the case of exponential fields.

An
exponential field
or
E-field
is a field
F
of characteristic 0 equipped with a homomorphism
\exp
from its additive to its multiplicative group. We often write
e^{z}
instead of
\exp(z)
. If
F
is algebraically closed as a field, we call it an
EA-field
. If, in addition, the exponential map is surjective (every non-zero element has a logarithm), we call it an
ELA-field
.
Given any exponential field
F
and a subset
B\subseteq F
, we have the exponential subfield
\left\langle B\right\rangle_{F}^{\mathrm{E}}
generated by
B
, and also write
\left\langle B\right\rangle_{F}^{\mathrm{EA}}
for the smallest exponential subfield containing
B
which is relatively algebraically closed in
F
in the field-theoretic sense. We also write
\left\langle B\right\rangle_{F}^{\mathrm{ELA}}
for the smallest E-subfield
K
containing B which is relatively algebraically closed in
F
and also closed under logarithms, that is, if
\exp(b)\in K
then
b\in K
.

In any exponential field
F
, it is immediate that
\acl_{F}(B)\supseteq\left\langle B\right\rangle_{F}^{\mathrm{EA}}
.

The main classical examples of exponential fields are
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{exp}}
and
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{exp}}
, the real and complex fields equipped with the usual exponentials.
In the celebrated paper
[
Wil96
]
, Wilkie showed that
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{exp}}
is o-minimal. Every o-minimal field has definable Skolem functions, and so for any
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
the algebraic closure
\acl(B)
is equal to the definable closure
\dcl(B)
(the union of all singleton sets definable over
B
), and is the smallest elementary substructure of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{exp}}
containing
B
.

A different generalisation of algebraic closure which is also important for exponential fields is that known as
exponential algebraic closure
, denoted by
\ecl
. For any exponential field
F
and subset
B
,
\ecl_{F}(B)
consists of the components of zeros of functions defined implicitly from exponentials and polynomials (see
[
Kir10a
, Definition 3.2]
). In
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{exp}}
,
\acl
coincides with this
\ecl
(see
[
JW08
, Theorem 4.2]
) but this is not true for most exponential fields.

In the complex exponential field, the characterisation of
\acl
is, like many model-theoretic questions, completely open. We discuss this further at the end of the paper. One observation we can make is that
\pi
is in
\acl(\emptyset)
.
To see this, we observe that the kernel
2\pi i\mathbb{Z}
of the exponential map is defined by

x\in\ker\text{ iff }e^{x}=1

and the integers
\mathbb{Z}
are definable as the multiplicative stabiliser of the kernel, that is, by

x\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ iff }\forall y[y\in\ker\to xy\in\ker].

The set
\{\pm 2\pi i\}
is definable as the set of generators of the kernel, via

x\in\ker\wedge(\forall y\in\ker)(\exists z\in\mathbb{Z})[y=zx]

and the result follows since
\pi
is of course field-theoretically algebraic over
2\pi i
.
In
[
KMO12
]
this argument is extended to show that
\pi
is actually pointwise definable. We give the extended argument at the end of this paper.

Following his philosophy that
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{exp}}
should be as model-theoretically tame as possible given that the ring
\mathbb{Z}
is definable, Zilber
[
Zil05
]
introduced a new exponential field
\mathbb{B}
, satisfying many known and desirable properties of
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{exp}}
, and conjectured that it is in fact isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{exp}}
. In particular, the kernel of the exponential map in
\mathbb{B}
is of the form
\tau\mathbb{Z}
, with
\tau
transcendental, and an exponential field of this form is said to have
standard kernel
. Exactly the same argument as above shows that in any exponential field with standard kernel we have
\tau\in\acl(\emptyset)
.

The exponential field
\mathbb{B}
is characterised by three further properties: the Schanuel Property, Strong Exponential-Algebraic Closedness, and the Countable Closure Property, all of which we explain in the next section. The Schanuel property is the conclusion of Schanuel’s conjecture of transcendental number theory. It can be formulated as saying that for any finite tuple
{\bar{a}}
from an exponential field
F
, a certain predimension function
\delta({\bar{a}})
is non-negative. We can relativise this predimension function over a subset
B
of
F
, and we say that
B
is
strong
in
F
if for all finite tuples
{\bar{a}}
from
F
, the predimension
\delta({\bar{a}}/B)
is non-negative, and
B
also contains the kernel of
F
.
It turns out that for any
B\subseteq F
, there is a smallest strong subset of
F
containing it, called the
hull
of
B
, denoted
\lceil B\rceil_{F}
. We write
\lceil B\rceil_{F}^{\mathrm{EA}}
for
\left\langle\lceil B\rceil_{F}\right\rangle_{F}^{\mathrm{EA}}
. In the case
F=\mathbb{B}
, we will drop the subscript
F
from the above notation and just write
\lceil B\rceil
,
\left\langle B\right\rangle^{\mathrm{E}}
,
\left\langle B\right\rangle^{\mathrm{EA}}
, and
\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
.
Our main theorem characterises the model-theoretic algebraic closure in
\mathbb{B}
as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every subset
B\subseteq\mathbb{B}
we have

\acl(B)=\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
.

We view this theorem as the first part of a broader project aiming to characterise all the finite rank types in
\mathbb{B}
. The algebraic types correspond to the rank-0 types.

1.1.
Outline of the paper

In Section
2
we explain the other properties defining
\mathbb{B}
and the hull, and we set up our notation. In Section
3
we characterise the types of elements of the hull of a finite tuple over that tuple and show that they are isolated. This plays a crucial role in the rest of the paper and relies on several geometric properties of
\mathbb{B}
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we show first that
\acl(B)\supseteq\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
. This reduces to showing that
\acl(B)\supseteq\lceil B\rceil
, which is done in Section
4
, and comprises the bulk of the work of the paper. This statement can be seen as a “Diophantine” statement about some algebraic varieties containing only finitely many points from a certain finite rank subgroup of the graph of the exponential map. This resembles the Mordell-Lang conjecture for tori, a particular case of which states that an algebraic curve living inside an algebraic torus contains only finitely many points from a finite rank subgroup of the torus unless the curve is a coset of a subtorus. Hence it should not come as a surprise that Mordell-Lang plays a crucial role in the proof of the theorem.

We then show that
\acl(B)\seq\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
in Section
5
.
This inclusion follows from the Hrushovski style amalgamation-with-predimension construction of a countable submodel of
\mathbb{B}
, combined with the first inclusion. The reader familiar with Hrushovski constructions may be aware that in structures obtained by such a construction in certain settings (e.g. Hrushovski’s ab initio example) the model-theoretic algebraic closure can be described in terms of the hull. In our setting this yields only a weak version of the second inclusion; some non-trivial work and the first inclusion are required to prove the full case.

In the final Section
6
we compare the algebraic closure with other closure operators in
\mathbb{B}
, including the definable closure and various bounded closure operators, discuss what we can say about algebraic closure in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{exp}}
, and leave some open questions.

2.
Zilber’s exponential fields

Zilber’s exponential field
\mathbb{B}
is the unique (up to isomorphism) ELA-field of cardinality continuum with standard kernel which satisfies three further properties: the Schanuel Property, Strong Exponential-Algebraic Closedness, and the Countable Closure Property. We explain these properties and the relevant surrounding concepts, roughly following the presentation in
[
Kir13b
]
.

2.1.
The Schanuel Property, predimension, and strong hull

We begin by introducing the notion of
relative predimension
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space over another. We work in an ambient exponential field
F
.

Given two
\mathbb{Q}
-vector subspaces
L_{1}\seq L_{2}
of
F
such that the quotient
L_{2}/L_{1}
is finite dimensional, we define the
relative predimension
of
L_{2}
over
L_{1}
as

\delta(L_{2}/L_{1}):=\td(L_{2},\exp(L_{2})/L_{1},\exp(L_{1}))-\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(L_{2}/L_{1})

where
\td
and
\ldim
stand for transcendence degree and linear dimension respectively.

For an arbitrary
\mathbb{Q}
-vector subspace
L_{1}
of
F
and a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector subspace
L
of
F
we define

\delta(L/L_{1}):=\delta(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(L_{1}\cup L)/L_{1}).

For two finite tuples
{\bar{a}},{\bar{b}}
we let
\delta({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}}):=\delta(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{a}})/\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}))
.

Given
\mathbb{Q}
-vector subspaces
L_{1}\seq L_{2}
of an exponential field
F
, we say
L_{1}
is
strong
in
L_{2}
and write
L_{1}\lhd L_{2}
if
\ker(\exp)\cap L_{2}\subseteq L_{1}
and for all finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspaces
L\seq L_{2}
we have
\delta(L/L_{1})\geqslant 0
.

For a tuple
{\bar{b}}
from a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace
L
we say
{\bar{b}}
is strong in
L
, and write
{\bar{b}}\lhd L
, if
\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}})\lhd L
.

For a subset
B\seq F
we define the
strong hull
or just
hull
of
B
as follows:

\lceil B\rceil_{F}:=\bigcap\{L\seq F:L\mbox{ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector subspace},B\cup\ker(\exp)\seq L\mbox{ and }L\lhd F\}.

One can show that the intersection of strong
\mathbb{Q}
-subspaces is again strong, and hence
\lceil B\rceil_{F}
is the smallest strong subspace of
F
containing
B
. As mentioned in the introduction, we write
\lceil B\rceil_{F}^{\mathrm{EA}}
for
\left\langle\lceil B\rceil\right\rangle_{F}^{\mathrm{EA}}
, the smallest EA-subfield of
F
which contains
B
and is strong in
F
.

For a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
L
we define its
predimension
as

\delta(L):=\delta(L/\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(\ker(\exp)))

and likewise for a finite tuple
{\bar{a}}
we define
\delta({\bar{a}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\delta(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{a}}))
.

An exponential field
F
satisfies the
Schanuel Property
if the conclusion of Schanuel’s conjecture holds in
F
, that is, for any
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in F

\td_{\mathbb{Q}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},e^{z_{1}},\ldots,e^{z_{n}})-\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\geqslant 0.

For exponential fields
F
with standard kernel (or trivial kernel), it is easy to see that
F
satisfies the Schanuel Property if and only if for every finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector subspace
L\seq F
we have
\delta(L)\geqslant 0
.
In this case, it easily follows that if
\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}B
is finite then
\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}\lceil B\rceil_{F}
is finite and
\delta(\lceil B\rceil_{F})
is the smallest possible predimension of an extension (as a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space) of
B
in
F
.

2.2.
Strong Exponential-Algebraic Closedness and the Countable Closure Property

For an
l\times n
matrix
M
of integers we define
M:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{l}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{l}
to be the map given by
M:(\bar{x},\bar{y})\mapsto(M{\bar{x}},{\bar{y}}^{M})
where
(M{\bar{x}})_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{ij}x_{j}\mbox{ and }({\bar{y}}^{M})_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{m_{ij}}.

An irreducible subvariety
V\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
is
rotund
if for any
1\leqslant l\leqslant n
and any
l\times n
matrix
M
of integers
\dim M(V)\geqslant\rk M
.

A subvariety
V
as above is called
free
if for any
M
as above with
l=\rk(M)=1
, both the additive and multiplicative projections of
M(V)
have dimension 1.

Given an exponential field
F
and a subvariety

V\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
, we define

V^{\dagger}_{{\bar{b}}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{{\bar{a}}:({\bar{a}},e^{{\bar{a}}})\in V\text{and the tuple ${\bar{a}}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent over ${\bar{b}}$}\}.

When
F
has standard kernel,
\mathbb{Q}
is
\emptyset
-definable in
F
, hence if
V
is defined (in the field-theoretic sense) over
\left\langle{\bar{b}}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{E}}_{F}
then
V^{\dagger}_{{\bar{b}}}
is a
{\bar{b}}
-definable set.

We say an exponential field
F
is

Strongly Exponentially-Algebraically Closed
if it is an ELA field and for every
free
and
rotund
subvariety
V\seq\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{n}(F)\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}(F)
of dimension
n
defined over
F
and for every finite tuple
{\bar{b}}
from
F
, the set
V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}
is non-empty.

An exponential field
F
is said to have the
Countable Closure Property
if for every countable subset
B
, the exponential-algebraic closure
\ecl_{F}(B)
is countable. We have not carefully defined
\ecl
, and in fact we will not make any use of this property (save for some comments in section
6
) but for those exponential fields with standard kernel which satisfy the Schanuel Property there is a simpler equivalent formulation:

For every free and rotund subvariety
V\seq\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{n}(F)\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}(F)
of dimension
n
and for every finite tuple
{\bar{b}}
, the set
V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}
is countable.

For each uncountable cardinal
\kappa
there is a unique (up to isomorphism) ELA-field of cardinality
\kappa
with standard kernel satisfying the Schanuel Property, Strong Exponential-Algebraic Closedness, and the Countable Closure Property
[
Zil05
,
BK18
]
. The one of cardinality
2^{\aleph_{0}}
is denoted by
\mathbb{B}
as already pointed out above.

3.
Isolation of types

To understand when
a\in\acl({\bar{b}})
in
\mathbb{B}
, we need to know something about the types of finite tuples which are realised in
\mathbb{B}
. We are interested in syntactic complete types, that is,
\tp(a/{\bar{b}})=\left\{\varphi(x,{\bar{b}})\,\left|\,\mathbb{B}\models\varphi(a,{\bar{b}})\right.\right\}
where
\varphi(x,{\bar{y}})
runs over first-order formulas. The construction of
\mathbb{B}
is done in a semantic way, so the natural notion of type which arises is a Galois type, that is, an automorphism orbit. As
\mathbb{B}
is not a saturated, or even homogeneous, model of its first-order theory, Galois types give in general a slightly finer equivalence relation than syntactic types. However,
\mathbb{B}
is quasiminimal excellent and hence
\aleph_{0}
-homogeneous over the empty set
[
Kir10b
, Definition 1.1]
and it follows that for those types of finite tuples (over a finite tuple of parameters) which are realised in
\mathbb{B}
, the two do coincide. Thus we have the following statement.

Fact 3.1
.

If
{\bar{a}},{\bar{a}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
{\bar{b}}\in\mathbb{B}^{k}
then
\tp({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})=\tp({\bar{a}}^{\prime}/{\bar{b}})
if and only if there is an automorphism of
\mathbb{B}
fixing
{\bar{b}}
pointwise and mapping
{\bar{a}}
to
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}
.

Since
\tau\in\acl(\emptyset)
, it is convenient to work over tuples containing
\tau
, so from now on we focus on types over such tuples.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field. For a subfield
K
of
F
and a tuple
\bar{u}
from
F
the
locus
of
\bar{u}
over
K
, denoted by
\loc(\bar{u}/K)
, is the Zariski closure of
\bar{u}
over
K
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
{\bar{b}}
be a finite tuple from
\mathbb{B}
containing
\tau
, such that
{\bar{b}}\lhd\mathbb{B}
.
Suppose
{\bar{a}}
is a finite tuple from
\mathbb{B}
which is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent over
{\bar{b}}
and that
\delta({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})=0
.

Then there is a positive integer
m
such that, after replacing
{\bar{a}}
by
{\bar{a}}/m
, we get that

\tp({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})
is isolated by the formula
{\bar{x}}\in V^{\dagger}_{\bar{b}}
, where
V=\loc({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}})/B)
with
B:=\mathbb{Q}\left(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}),\exp(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}))\right)
.

Proof.

We give a proof referring to the paper
[
BK18
]
, which however uses a somewhat different and more general setup, of what are there called
\Gamma
-fields. In our situation, a
\Gamma
-field is a subfield
F
of
\mathbb{B}
with a distinguished
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace
D=D(F)
containing
\tau
, and the restriction of
\exp
to
D
. Furthermore,
F
should be generated as a subfield of
\mathbb{B}
by
D\cup\exp(D)
. It is finitely generated in the sense of that paper if and only if
D
is finite-dimensional as a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. In
[
Kir13a
]
and elsewhere, this concept is called a partial exponential field.

With these definitions and notation,
B
is the
\Gamma
-field with
D(B)
spanned by
{\bar{b}}
. Take
A
to be its extension with
D(A)
spanned by
{\bar{a}},{\bar{b}}
. Then
[
BK18
, Proposition 3.22]
says that, after replacing
{\bar{a}}
by
{\bar{a}}/m
for a suitable
m
, we get that
{\bar{a}}
is a
good basis
, which by
[
BK18
, Definition 3.19]
means that the extension
A
is determined up to isomorphism by the locus
V
of
({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}}))
over
B
for a basis
{\bar{a}}
, that is,
V
together with the information that the basis
{\bar{a}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent over
{\bar{b}}
.

Now we note that since
{\bar{b}}\lhd\mathbb{B}
, the extension
B\subseteq A
is necessarily strong. Further, since
\delta({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})=0
, we conclude that
A\lhd\mathbb{B}
.

The exponential field
\mathbb{B}
is constructed as an elementary extension of a countable model
\mathbb{F}

[
Kir10b
, Lemma 5.4]
, and this
\mathbb{F}
is the Fraïssé limit of the category of finitely generated
\Gamma
-fields with strong extensions
[
BK18
, Theorem 5.9]
. So from the standard homogeneity of Fraïssé limits, we see that if
A
and
A^{\prime}
are isomorphic strong finitely generated extensions of
B
within
\mathbb{F}
then the isomorphism extends to an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}
, and hence the corresponding generators of
A
and
A^{\prime}
over
B
have the same Galois type over
B
, and then in particular they have the same syntactic type over
{\bar{b}}
. The proposition follows.
∎

Now consider the situation where
{\bar{b}}
is a finite tuple from
\mathbb{B}
which is not strong in
\mathbb{B}
, and
{\bar{a}}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear basis for
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
over
{\bar{b}}
.
We can assume that
{\bar{b}}
contains
\tau
and is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent. Then
{\bar{a}}{\bar{b}}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear basis for
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
, and replacing
{\bar{a}}
by
{\bar{a}}/m
and
{\bar{b}}
by
{\bar{b}}/m
for a suitable
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we may assume it is a good basis.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
{\bar{b}}
be a finite tuple from
\mathbb{B}
containing
\tau
and suppose
{\bar{a}}{\bar{b}}
is a good basis of
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
. Then
\tp({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})
is isolated by the formula
{\bar{x}}\in V^{\dagger}_{\bar{b}}
, where
V=\loc({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}})/B)
with
B:=\mathbb{Q}\left(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}),\exp(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}))\right)
. Furthermore, all realisations
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}
of this type are such that the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear span of
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}{\bar{b}}
is equal to that of
{\bar{a}}{\bar{b}}
, namely
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
.

Proof.

If
{\bar{a}}
is non-empty then
\delta({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})<0
, so we are not in the situation of Proposition
3.3
. To get to that situation, we take a subtuple
{\bar{b}}^{\prime}
of
{\bar{b}}
which contains
\tau
and is otherwise exponentially algebraically independent in
\mathbb{B}
, of maximal length, say
d+1
, and let
{\bar{c}}={\bar{b}}\setminus{\bar{b}}^{\prime}
, the rest of the tuple. Then
{\bar{b}}^{\prime}\lhd\mathbb{B}
with
\delta({\bar{b}}^{\prime})=\delta({\bar{b}}^{\prime}{\bar{c}}{\bar{a}})=d
, and
\delta({\bar{c}}{\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}}^{\prime})=0
.

Let
U:=\loc({\bar{c}}{\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{c}}{\bar{a}})/{\bar{b}}^{\prime},\exp({\bar{b}}^{\prime}))
. Then by Proposition
3.3
,
\tp({\bar{c}}{\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}}^{\prime})
is isolated by the formula
{\bar{y}}{\bar{x}}\in U^{\dagger}_{{\bar{b}}^{\prime}}
. Evaluating at
{\bar{y}}={\bar{c}}
we see that
\tp({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}}^{\prime}{\bar{c}})
is isolated by
{\bar{x}}\in V^{\dagger}_{{\bar{b}}^{\prime}{\bar{c}}}
, that is,
{\bar{x}}\in V^{\dagger}_{\bar{b}}
, as required.

Now suppose that
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}\in V^{\dagger}_{\bar{b}}
. Then
\tp({\bar{a}}^{\prime}/{\bar{b}})=\tp({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})
so
V=\loc({\bar{a}}^{\prime},\exp({\bar{a}}^{\prime})/{\bar{b}},\exp({\bar{b}}))
, and
\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{a}}^{\prime}/{\bar{b}})=\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})
. Thus
\delta({\bar{a}}^{\prime}/{\bar{b}})=\delta({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})
, and by the minimality of this predimension and the uniqueness of the hull,
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}
must also be a basis for
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
over
{\bar{b}}
.
∎

From the
\aleph_{0}
-homogeneity of
\mathbb{B}
over
\emptyset
as mentioned above, we get:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
{\bar{a}},{\bar{b}}
, and
B
be as in Proposition
3.4
. Let
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}
be another tuple such that
\loc({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}})/B)=\loc({\bar{a}}^{\prime},\exp({\bar{a}}^{\prime})/B).

Then there is an automorphism of

fixing

{\bar{a}}
to
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}
. ∎

4.
The strong hull is contained in the algebraic closure

The main technical part of the proof is done in the following proposition where we consider a locus of a special form.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\bar{b}}
be a finite tuple from
\mathbb{B}
containing
\tau
, and let
{\bar{a}}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
be a good basis of the strong hull
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
. Let also
V\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\loc\left({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}})/B\right)\seq\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{n}(\mathbb{B})\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}(\mathbb{B})
where
B:=\mathbb{Q}\left(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}),\exp(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}))\right)
. Assume that
W:=\pr_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}}V
is a coset of an algebraic subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
. Then
{\bar{a}}\seq\acl({\bar{b}})
.

Proof.

We proceed to the proof by induction on
n
, the length of
{\bar{a}}
. The base case
n=0
holds vacuously. If
n\geqslant 1
then
\delta({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})<0
which means
\dim V<n
. In particular, if
n=1
then
\dim V=0
and so
V
is finite, and the conclusion of the theorem holds.

So let
n\geqslant 2
and assume now that for any tuple
{\bar{b}}^{\prime}
for which

•

l:=\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\lceil{\bar{b}}^{\prime}\rceil/{\bar{b}}^{\prime})<n
, and

•

\pr_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{l}}\loc\left({\bar{a}}^{\prime},\exp({\bar{a}}^{\prime})/\mathbb{Q}({\bar{b}}^{\prime},\exp({\bar{b}}^{\prime}))^{\mathrm{alg}}\right)
is a coset of a subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{l}
where
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}^{l}
is a basis of
\lceil{\bar{b}}^{\prime}\rceil
over
{\bar{b}}
,

we have
{\bar{a}}^{\prime}\seq\acl({\bar{b}}^{\prime})
.

We need to show that the conclusion holds for
{\bar{a}}
and
{\bar{b}}
as in the statement of the proposition. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
denote the coordinates on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{n}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
. Observe that by a simple change of coordinates we may reduce to the case where
W
is defined by equations of the form
y_{k}=c_{k},~k=1,\ldots,s
for some
c_{k}\in B
and some
s\leqslant n
.

Claim 4.2
.

There is
a^{\prime}\in\Span({\bar{a}},{\bar{b}})\setminus\Span({\bar{b}})
such that
a^{\prime}\in\acl({\bar{b}})
.

We now explain why the claim is enough to prove the induction step. If the claim holds, then we may assume
a^{\prime}
is one of the
a_{i}
, say
a_{t}
. Then the rest of the
a_{i}
, denoted
{\bar{a}}_{*}
as a tuple, form a basis of
\lceil{\bar{b}},a^{\prime}\rceil
over
{\bar{b}},a^{\prime}
. Let
B^{\prime}:=B(a^{\prime},\exp(\mathbb{Q}a^{\prime}))
. Since by our assumption
e^{a_{1}},\ldots,e^{a_{s}}\in B
and
e^{a_{s+1}},\ldots,e^{a_{n}}
are algebraically independent over
B
, we see that the multiplicative projection of
\loc\left({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}}))/B^{\prime}\right)
is defined by the equations
y_{k}=c_{k},~k=1,\ldots,s
, and
y_{t}=e^{a^{\prime}}
and is a coset of a subtorus of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
. Therefore, the multiplicative projection of
\loc\left({\bar{a}}_{*},\exp({\bar{a}}_{*}))/B^{\prime}\right)
is a coset of a subtorus of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n-1}
. Also,
\dim(\lceil{\bar{b}},a^{\prime}\rceil/{\bar{b}},a^{\prime})=n-1
, hence by the induction hypothesis we have
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil=\lceil a^{\prime},{\bar{b}}\rceil\seq\acl(a^{\prime},{\bar{b}})=\acl({\bar{b}})
.

Now we prove the claim considering two cases.

Case 1.

\dim W<n-1
. This means
W
has at least two constant coordinates,
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
.
Let
U\seq\mathbb{B}^{2}
be the projection of
V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}
to the first two coordinates. Clearly
U\seq(a_{1}+\tau\mathbb{Z})\times(a_{2}+\tau\mathbb{Z})
. Suppose for some
m_{1},m_{2},l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
(a_{1}+m_{1}\tau,a_{2}+m_{2}\tau),(a_{1}+l_{1}\tau,a_{2}+l_{2}\tau)\in U
. We may assume
m_{1}\geqslant 0,~l_{1}\geqslant 0
. By Corollary
3.5
, the maps

\mu:(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto(a_{1}+m_{1}\tau,a_{2}+m_{2}\tau),~\lambda:(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto(a_{1}+l_{1}\tau,a_{2}+l_{2}\tau)

extend to automorphisms over
B
.

Now, the automorphism
\mu^{l_{1}}\lambda^{-m_{1}}
maps
(a_{1},a_{2})
to
(a_{1},a_{2}+(l_{1}m_{2}-l_{2}m_{1})\tau)
. If
l_{1}m_{2}-l_{2}m_{1}\neq 0
then the orbit of
a_{2}
is infinite under the powers of
\mu^{l_{1}}\lambda^{-m_{1}}
while
a_{1}
is fixed. However, by the induction hypotheses,
a_{2}
is model-theoretically algebraic over
{\bar{b}}a_{1}
, so this is not possible. Therefore, we deduce that
l_{1}m_{2}-l_{2}m_{1}=0
.

Let
d_{i}:=\gcd(l_{i},m_{i})
. Then
l_{i}=d_{i}l_{i}^{\prime},~m_{i}=d_{i}m_{i}^{\prime}
with
\gcd(l_{i}^{\prime},m_{i}^{\prime})=1
. Substituting this in the above equality we see that

l_{1}^{\prime}m_{2}^{\prime}=l_{2}^{\prime}m_{1}^{\prime}
which then implies that
l_{1}^{\prime}=l_{2}^{\prime},m_{1}^{\prime}=m_{2}^{\prime}
or
l_{1}^{\prime}=-l_{2}^{\prime},m_{1}^{\prime}=-m_{2}^{\prime}
.

Since
\gcd(l_{1}^{\prime},m_{1}^{\prime})=1
, there are integers
u,v
such that
m_{1}^{\prime}u+l_{1}^{\prime}v=1
. Then
m_{2}^{\prime}u+l_{2}^{\prime}v=\pm 1
. Thus, we have

m_{1}u+l_{1}v=d_{1},~m_{2}u+l_{2}v=\pm d_{2}.

Consider the automorphism
\sigma:=\mu^{u}\lambda^{v}:(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto(a_{1}+d_{1}\tau,a_{2}\pm d_{2}\tau)
. Then on
(a_{1},a_{2})
the automorphism
\mu
(respectively
\lambda
) agrees with
\sigma^{m_{1}^{\prime}}
(respectively
\sigma^{l_{1}^{\prime}}
), regardless of the sign in the second coordinate above. This shows that on
(a_{1},a_{2})
all automorphisms over
B
agree with powers of an automorphism
\sigma:(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto(a_{1}+d_{1}\tau,a_{2}+d_{2}\tau)
where
|d_{1}|,|d_{2}|
are minimal. Since
\sigma^{r}
maps
(a_{1},a_{2})
to
(a_{1}+rd_{1}\tau,a_{2}+rd_{2}\tau)
, we conclude that any point in
U
must be of the latter form for some integer
r
. Then the formula

\varphi(x):=\exists x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\left((x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}\wedge x=d_{1}x_{2}-d_{2}x_{1}\right)

defines
a^{\prime}:=d_{1}a_{2}-d_{2}a_{1}
over
{\bar{b}}
. Since
{\bar{a}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent over
{\bar{b}}
, if
a^{\prime}\in\Span({\bar{b}})
, then
d_{1}=d_{2}=0
, and
U
is a singleton and so
a_{1},a_{2}\in\acl({\bar{b}})
. Otherwise
a^{\prime}\in\Span({\bar{a}},{\bar{b}})\setminus\Span({\bar{b}})
and
a^{\prime}\in\acl({\bar{b}})
which proves Claim
4.2
.

Case 2.

\dim W=\dim V=n-1
. In this case
V
projects dominantly to the coordinates
{\bar{y}}_{*}:=(y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})
, the coordinate
y_{1}
is constant (equal to
c_{1}
), and every coordinate
x_{k}
is algebraic over
{\bar{y}}_{*}
on
V
.
Let
M\in\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{Q})
and
{\bar{\beta}}_{*}\in\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(a_{1},{\bar{b}})^{n-1}
and
\beta_{1}\in\tau\mathbb{Z}
be such that
(a_{1}+\beta_{1},M{\bar{a}}_{*}+{\bar{\beta}}_{*})\in V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}
, where
{\bar{a}}_{*}:=(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})
. Observe that an arbitrary element of
V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}
is of the form
(a_{1}+\beta_{1},M{\bar{a}}_{*}+{\bar{\beta}}_{*})
, and we aim to show that
\beta_{1}
must necessarily be
0
.

Recall that
B=\mathbb{Q}\left(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}),\exp(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}))\right)
. Write
{\bar{c}}_{*}
for
\exp({\bar{a}}_{*})
. Since
\dim W=n-1
, the elements
c_{2},\ldots,c_{n}
are algebraically independent over
B
. Consider the minimal polynomial of
a_{1}
over

C:=B\left(c_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{l}}}:l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},2\leqslant k\leqslant n\right)=B\left(\exp\left(\frac{a_{k}+r\tau}{l}\right):l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},r\in\mathbb{Z},2\leqslant k\leqslant n\right).

Only finitely many values of
c_{k}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{l}}}
occur in that polynomial, so by a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear change of variables we may assume it has coefficients in
B({\bar{c}}_{*})
.
Thus, there is an irreducible polynomial
p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*})\in B[X_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n}]
such that
p(X_{1},{\bar{c}}_{*})
is the minimal polynomial of
a_{1}
over
C
.

Since the map
(a_{1},{\bar{a}}_{*})\mapsto(a_{1}+\beta_{1},M{\bar{a}}_{*}+{\bar{\beta}}_{*})
extends to an automorphism
\sigma
over
B
, we have
p(a_{1}+\beta_{1},\bar{c}_{*}^{M}\cdot{\bar{\gamma}}_{*})=0
where
{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}:=e^{{\bar{\beta}}_{*}}\in\exp\left(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(a_{1},{\bar{b}})\right)^{n-1}\seq B^{n-1}
for
e^{a_{1}}=c_{1}\in B
. As
M
is a rational matrix, there is some ambiguity when we compute
{\bar{c}}_{*}^{M}
, so the last sentence means that for some choice of roots the equality holds. In fact, for any choice of roots there is a
{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}
for which the equality holds because different roots differ just by a root of unity and these factors can be included in
{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}
.

Thus, the polynomial
p\left(X_{1}+\beta_{1},\bar{c}_{*}^{M}\cdot{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}\right)
vanishes at
a_{1}
and is defined over
C
. Therefore, it must be divisible by the minimal polynomial
p(X_{1},{\bar{c}}_{*})
. Since these two have the same degree, they must agree up to a constant factor, that is,

p\left(X_{1}+\beta_{1},\bar{c}_{*}^{M}\cdot{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}\right)=\frac{f({\bar{c}}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})}{g({\bar{c}}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})}\cdot p(X_{1},{\bar{c}}_{*})

for some polynomials
f,g\in B[\bar{Y}_{*}]
and some positive integer
t
. Since
{\bar{c}}_{*}
is generic over
\beta_{1},{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}
, the following equality holds:

g(\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})p\left(X_{1}+\beta_{1},\bar{Y}_{*}^{M}\cdot{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}\right)=f(\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*}).

Here all terms are regarded as Puiseux polynomials, more precisely, Laurent polynomials in
\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{m}}}
for a suitable
m
(any integer that is divisible by all denominators appearing in exponents of
\bar{Y}_{*}
). The ring of Laurent polynomials is a unique factorisation domain (as it is a localisation of the ring of polynomials), where we may assume that
f
and
g
are coprime. We claim that
f(\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})
and
g(\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})
must be units in that ring (monomials). Indeed,
g(\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})
must divide
p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*})
which implies that
p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*})
is divisible by a polynomial in
\bar{Y}_{*}
, namely, any irreducible factor of the product of all Galois conjugates of
g(\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})
over
B(\bar{Y}_{*})
. Since
p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*})
is irreducible,
g(\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})
must be a unit, i.e. a monomial in
\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{m}}}
. Similarly,
f(\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}})
is a monomial in
\bar{Y}_{*}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{m}}}
.

Thus, we have

(1)

p\left(X_{1}+\beta_{1},\bar{Y}_{*}^{M}\cdot{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}\right)=\xi\cdot\bar{Y}_{*}^{\bar{u}}\cdot p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*})\mbox{ for some }\xi\in B,~\bar{u}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n-1}.

Let
S:=\{\bar{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}:\bar{Y}_{*}^{\bar{s}}\mbox{ occurs in }p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*})\}\seq\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
be the support of
p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*})
in
\bar{Y}_{*}
and let
h:=|S|
(the size of
S
). If
N
is the transpose of
M
then equality (
1
) implies that
N(S)=S+\bar{u}
(equality as sets), that is,
N
permutes
S
and translates by
\bar{u}
. So there is a permutation
\mu
on
h
elements such that

N\bar{s}=\mu(\bar{s})+\bar{u}\mbox{ for all }\bar{s}\in S.

So for every
r\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

N^{r}\bar{s}=\mu^{r}(\bar{s})+(I+\ldots+N^{r-1})\bar{u}\mbox{ for all }\bar{s}\in S.

In particular, for
r=h!
we have
\mu^{h!}=\mathrm{Id}
hence

N^{h!}\bar{s}=\bar{s}+\bar{v}\mbox{ for all }\bar{s}\in S,\mbox{ where }\bar{v}=(I+\ldots+N^{h!-1})\bar{u}.

Now, from (
1
) we deduce that for all
r

p\left(X_{1}+r\beta_{1},\bar{Y}_{*}^{N^{r}}\cdot{\bar{\gamma}}_{*}^{I+\ldots+N^{r-1}}\right)=\xi^{r}\cdot\bar{Y}_{*}^{(I+\ldots+N^{r-1})\bar{u}}\cdot p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*}).

In particular,

p\left(X_{1}+h!\beta_{1},\bar{Y}_{*}^{N^{h!}}\cdot\bar{\delta}\right)=\xi^{h!}\cdot\bar{Y}_{*}^{\bar{v}}\cdot p(X_{1},\bar{Y}_{*})\mbox{ where }\bar{\delta}:={\bar{\gamma}}_{*}^{I+\ldots+N^{h!-1}}.

Now pick
\bar{s}\in S
such that the coefficient of
\bar{Y}_{*}^{\bar{s}}
in
p(X_{1},\bar{Y}^{*})
is a non-constant polynomial
q(X_{1})\in B[X_{1}]
. Comparing the coefficients of
\bar{Y}_{*}^{\bar{s}+\bar{v}}
in both sides in the above equality we get

q(X_{1}+h!\beta_{1})\cdot\bar{\delta}^{\bar{s}}=\xi^{h!}\cdot q(X_{1}).

The comparison of the leading coefficients yields
\bar{\delta}^{\bar{s}}=\xi^{h!}
. Therefore,
q(X_{1}+h!\beta_{1})=q(X_{1})
. Since
q
is non-constant and therefore cannot be periodic, we conclude that
\beta_{1}=0
.

Thus, we showed that for any element in
V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}
the first coordinate is
a_{1}
, hence
a_{1}\in\acl({\bar{b}})
, and the claim follows. This finishes the proof.
∎

We now show that in the above proposition the condition on
W
can be dropped by proving that the general case can always be reduced to that situation. To that end we will need the Mordell-Lang conjecture for algebraic tori:

Fact 4.3

(Mordell-Lang for algebraic tori,
[
Lau84
]
)
.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
. Let also
W\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}(K)
be an irreducible proper algebraic subvariety, and
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}(K)
a subgroup of finite rank.

Then there is a finite number
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there are
\bar{\gamma}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\gamma}_{N}\in\Gamma
, and algebraic subgroups
H_{1},\ldots,H_{N}
of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
, such that for each
i
we have
\bar{\gamma}_{i}H_{i}\subseteq W
and

W\cap\Gamma=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\bar{\gamma}_{i}H_{i}\cap\Gamma.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{B}
be any subset. Then
\lceil D\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}\seq\acl(D)
.

Proof.

By finite character and the definition of the EA-closure it suffices to show that if
{\bar{b}}\seq\mathbb{B}
is any finite tuple containing
\tau
and
{\bar{a}}
is a good basis of the strong hull
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
over
{\bar{b}}
then
{\bar{a}}\seq\acl({\bar{b}})
.

Suppose for a contradiction there is a tuple
{\bar{b}}
for which this is not true, and let
V\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\loc\left({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}})/B\right)
where, as above,
B:=\mathbb{Q}\left(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}),\exp(\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}))\right)
. Let
{\bar{b}}^{*}
be a tuple from
\acl({\bar{b}})
extending
{\bar{b}}
over which
\loc({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}})/\acl({\bar{b}}))
is defined. Then
\lceil{\bar{b}}^{*}\rceil\nsubseteq\acl({\bar{b}})=\acl({\bar{b}}^{*})
. So we replace
{\bar{b}}
with
{\bar{b}}^{*}
and assume
V=\loc({\bar{a}},\exp({\bar{a}})/\acl({\bar{b}}))
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}:=\exp(\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil)\leqslant\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mathbb{B})
. Since
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space,
\Gamma_{0}
is a finite rank subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mathbb{B})
. It is also definable over
{\bar{b}}
, for the formula
{\bar{x}}\in V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}
isolates
\tp({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})
. More precisely,
\Gamma_{0}
is defined by the formula

\left(\exists{\bar{x}}\in V_{{\bar{b}}}^{\dagger}\right)\left(\exists\bar{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|{\bar{a}}|}\right)\left(\exists\bar{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|{\bar{b}}|}\right)\left[y=\exp\left(\sum_{i}m_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{j}k_{j}b_{j}\right)\right].

Hence
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{0}^{n}\leqslant\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
, where
n:=|{\bar{a}}|
, is also definable over
{\bar{b}}
.

Let
W:=\pr_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}}V
. Clearly, we have

\exp({\bar{a}})\in{\exp(V^{\dagger}_{{\bar{b}}})}\subseteq W\cap\Gamma.

By Mordell-Lang, there are a positive integer
N
and
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{N}\seq W
such that

(2)

W\cap\Gamma=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}(\kappa_{j}\cap\Gamma),

where each
\kappa_{j}
is a coset of a connected algebraic subgroup
T_{j}
of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}
. We may assume
\kappa_{j}
’s are maximal with respect to inclusion. This also means that each set
\kappa_{j}\cap\Gamma
is maximal, for it is Zariski dense in
\kappa_{j}
.

Claim 4.5
.

Each
\kappa_{j}
is field-theoretically definable over
\acl({\bar{b}})
.

Proof.

We will prove that
\kappa_{1}
is definable over
\acl({\bar{b}})
. Suppose
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{M}
are all the cosets of
T_{1}
among
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{N}
, i.e.
T_{1}=\ldots=T_{M}\neq T_{j}
for all
j>M
. Consider the set

\Gamma_{1}:=\left\{{\bar{\gamma}}\in\Gamma:{\bar{\gamma}}T_{1}\seq W\wedge\bigwedge_{M<j\leqslant N}{\bar{\gamma}}T_{j}\nsubseteq W\right\}.

We claim that
\Gamma_{1}\subseteq\bigcup_{j\leqslant M}\kappa_{j}
. Indeed, if there exists
{\bar{\gamma}}\in\Gamma_{1}\setminus\bigcup_{j\leqslant M}\kappa_{j}
then
{\bar{\gamma}}T_{1}\seq W
hence
{\bar{\gamma}}T_{1}\seq\kappa_{j}
for some
j>M
, which then implies
{\bar{\gamma}}T_{j}\seq W
, a contradiction.

Since the
\kappa_{j}
are maximal,
\Gamma_{1}
contains at least one point from each
\kappa_{j}
for
j\leqslant M
.

The set
\Gamma
is definable over
{\bar{b}}
, hence so is
\Gamma_{1}
. Now if
T_{1}
satisfies an equation of the form
y_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{m_{n}}=1
, then
y_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{m_{n}}
is constant on
\kappa_{j}
for all

. The finite set of all these constants is definable over
{\bar{b}}
as the set of the values of the definable function
y_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{m_{n}}
on
\Gamma_{1}
. Thus, each of these constants is in
\acl({\bar{b}})
. This means that
\kappa_{1}
is field-theoretically definable over
\acl({\bar{b}})
.

A similar argument shows that every
\kappa_{j}
is definable over
\acl({\bar{b}})
. That concludes the proof of Claim
4.5
.
∎

To conclude the proof of Proposition
4.4
we observe that by (
2
),
\exp({\bar{a}})\in\kappa_{j}
for some
j
. But we also know that
W=\loc(\exp({\bar{a}})/\acl({\bar{b}}))
, and so
W\subseteq\kappa_{j}
. On the other hand,
\kappa_{j}\seq W
hence

.

We can now apply Proposition
4.1
and deduce that
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil\seq\acl({\bar{b}})
.
∎

5.
Upper bound for the algebraic closure

Recall that an ELA-subfield of
\mathbb{B}
is an exponential subfield on which
\exp
is surjective (i.e. every element has a logarithm) and which is field-theoretically algebraically closed.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
B\lhd\mathbb{B}
and
B
is an ELA-subfield then
B=\acl_{\mathbb{B}}(B)
.

Proof.

Let
a_{1}\in\mathbb{B}\setminus B
. We will show that
a_{1}\notin\acl(B)
. By
[
Kir13b
, Proposition 3.2]
the (unique) type of an element which is exponentially transcendental over
B
is not isolated, so in in particular not algebraic. Hence we may assume that
a_{1}
is exponentially algebraic over
B
.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
be a good basis for
\lceil Ba_{1}\rceil
over
B
(replacing
a_{1}
by some
\frac{a_{1}}{m}
for an integer
m
if necessary) and let
{\bar{a}}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. Define
V:=\mathrm{Loc}({\bar{a}},e^{\bar{a}}/B)
. Since
B
is an ELA-subfield,
V
is free. Furthermore,
V
is rotund, for
B\lhd\mathbb{B}
. Since
a_{1}
is exponentially algebraic over
B
and
{\bar{a}}
is a basis for
\lceil Ba_{1}\rceil
over
B
, we have
\delta({\bar{a}}/B)=0
, and so
\dim V=n
.

It is enough to show that
a_{1}\notin\acl({\bar{b}})
for any finite tuple
{\bar{b}}
from
B
. Extending
{\bar{b}}
, we may assume
V
is defined over
{\bar{b}}
and
{\bar{b}}\lhd B
, so also
{\bar{b}}\lhd\mathbb{B}
.

By Proposition
3.3
,
\tp({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})
is isolated by the formula
{\bar{x}}\in V^{\dagger}_{{\bar{b}}}
. Suppose we have
N
distinct realisations of this type, say
{\bar{c}}_{1},\ldots,{\bar{c}}_{N}
. Then by Strong Exponential-Algebraic Closedness there is
{\bar{c}}_{N+1}\in V^{\dagger}_{{\bar{b}},{\bar{c}}_{1},\ldots,{\bar{c}}_{N}}
.

So inductively we can find infinitely many realisations of
\tp({\bar{a}}/{\bar{b}})
, with all components of the tuples
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent, so in particular distinct. Hence,
\tp(a_{1}/{\bar{b}})
is not algebraic, and so
a_{1}\notin\acl(B)
.
∎

In order to deal with the case when
B
is not an ELA-subfield of
\mathbb{B}
, we need to establish several auxiliary facts.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{B}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace and
a\in\left\langle X\right\rangle^{\mathrm{ELA}}
. Then it follows from the construction of the ELA-closure (see
[
Kir13a
]
) that there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{B}
such that for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, at least one of
a_{i}
and
e^{a_{i}}
is algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}(X,\exp(X),a_{1},e^{a_{1}},\ldots,a_{i-1},e^{a_{i-1}})
, and
a\in\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(X,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. In fact without loss of generality,
a_{n}=a
.

If we take
n
to be least possible for
X
and
a
then it follows that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent over
X
and (unless
a\in X
) that
n\geqslant 1
. In this case we say that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is a witnessing sequence that
a\in\left\langle X\right\rangle^{\mathrm{ELA}}
. If
X\lhd\mathbb{B}
then in addition we have that for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, exactly one of
a_{i}
and
e^{a_{i}}
is algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}(X,\exp(X),a_{1},e^{a_{1}},\ldots,a_{i-1},e^{a_{i-1}})
. It also follows that
\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(X,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\lhd\mathbb{B}
(by induction on
n
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
X\lhd\mathbb{B}
and that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is a witnessing sequence that
a\in\left\langle X\right\rangle^{\mathrm{ELA}}
. Then
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is also a basis for
\lceil Xa\rceil
over
X
.

Proof.

Suppose not, and take a counterexample with minimal possible value of
n
. Then
H\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lceil Xa\rceil
is a proper
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace of
\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(X,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, for the latter is strong in
\mathbb{B}
and contains
X\cup\{a\}
. Then
a_{1}\notin H
, since otherwise we can replace
X
by
\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(Xa_{1})
to reduce
n
.

We have
a_{n}\in H
, so we can take
j
least possible such that
a_{j}\in H
(even allowing the sequence of
a_{i}
’s to change, but fixing
X
and
a
). So
a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent over
H
, but
H\lhd\mathbb{B}
and hence

\displaystyle\td(a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1},e^{a_{1}},\ldots,e^{a_{j-1}}/H,\exp(H))

\displaystyle=j-1

\displaystyle=\td(a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1},e^{a_{1}},\ldots,e^{a_{j-1}}/X,\exp(X)).

So, considering forking independence in the field we have

a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1},e^{a_{1}},\ldots,e^{a_{j-1}}\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}_{X,\exp(X)}^{\mathrm{ACF}}H,\exp(H)

and in particular, as
a_{j}\in H
, by monotonicity and symmetry we have

a_{j},e^{a_{j}}\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}_{X,\exp(X)}^{\mathrm{ACF}}a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1},e^{a_{1}},\ldots,e^{a_{j-1}}.

Hence
\td(a_{j},e^{a_{j}}/X,\exp(X))=1
and so
\delta(a_{j}/X)=0
. Therefore, we can reorder the witnessing sequence by moving
a_{j}
to the start. But now we have
a_{1}\in H
which gives the same contradiction as above to the minimality of
n
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If
{\bar{b}}\lhd\mathbb{B}
with
\tau\in{\bar{b}}
and
e^{a}\in\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}})
but
a\notin\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}})
then
a\notin\acl({\bar{b}})
.

Proof.

We have

0\leqslant\delta(a/{\bar{b}})=\td(a,e^{a}/{\bar{b}},e^{{\bar{b}}})-\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(a/{\bar{b}})=\td(a/{\bar{b}},e^{{\bar{b}}})-1.

So
a
is transcendental over
{\bar{b}},e^{{\bar{b}}}
. Without loss of generality assume that
e^{a}=b_{1}
. Hence the variety
V:=\mathrm{Loc}(a,e^{a}/{\bar{b}},e^{{\bar{b}}})
is defined by a single equation
y=b_{1}
. So by Proposition
3.3
,
\tp(a/{\bar{b}})
is isolated by the formula

e^{x}=b_{1}\wedge x\notin\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}({\bar{b}}).

The realisations of this formula are
a+m\tau
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, hence the type is not algebraic.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

If
B\lhd\mathbb{B}
and
B
is an EA-subfield containing
\tau
then
B=\acl(B)
.

Proof.

Pick an arbitrary
a\in\mathbb{B}\setminus B
. We will show that
a\notin\acl(B)
. By Proposition
5.1
, it suffices to consider
a\in\left\langle B\right\rangle^{\mathrm{ELA}}\setminus B
. Take a witnessing sequence
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}=a
as above.
Then by Lemma
5.2
we have
\lceil Ba\rceil=\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(B,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

Since
B
is an EA-subfield, we must have
e^{a_{1}}\in B
and
a_{1}
transcendental over
B
. So, by Lemma
5.3
,
a_{1}\notin\acl(B)
.

However, by Proposition
4.4
,
a_{1}\in\acl(Ba)
. If
a\in\acl(B)
then
\acl(Ba)=\acl(B)
, which gives a contradiction. So
a\notin\acl(B)
as required.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
B\seq\mathbb{B}
. By Proposition
4.4
, we have
\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}\seq\acl(B)
, hence
\acl(B)=\acl(\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}})
. Then by Proposition
5.4
,
\acl(\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}})=\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
.
∎

6.
Discussion of closure operators

6.1.
Closure operators on
\mathbb{B}

Having shown that
\acl(B)=\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
for
B\subseteq\mathbb{B}
, an obvious question is whether
\acl
generally admits a simpler description. For example, if
a=\log\log 2
(any determination of this multivalued function) then it is easy to see that
\lceil a\rceil=\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(a,e^{a})
, so
\lceil a\rceil\subseteq\left\langle a\right\rangle^{E}
. Might it generally be the case that
\lceil B\rceil\subseteq\left\langle B\right\rangle^{\mathrm{EA}}
?

In fact it is not generally true. For example,
let
b=\exp(a^{2})
, where
\exp(\exp(a))=2
as above. Then
\lceil b\rceil=\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(b,a^{2},a,e^{a})
, but
a,a^{2},e^{a}\notin\left\langle b\right\rangle^{\mathrm{EA}}
, although we do have
\lceil b\rceil\subseteq\left\langle b\right\rangle^{\mathrm{ELA}}
.

In fact, given a subset
B\subseteq\mathbb{B}
, we have various closures of
B
forming the following diagram:

\begin{diagram}

One can make any of the inclusions strict or non-strict by choosing a suitable
B
. Indeed, it is evident that the inclusions on the two horizontal rows are in general strict. The example above shows that the left three vertical inclusions can be made strict as well.
The proof of Proposition 7.5 of
[
Kir13a
]
gives an example of a point
a
where
\lceil a\rceil\nsubseteq\left\langle a\right\rangle^{\mathrm{ELA}}
, which then implies that the rightmost vertical inclusion can be made strict too. This example is somewhat technical, involving a point
({\bar{a}},e^{\bar{a}})
on the intersection of three generic hyperplanes in
\mathbb{B}^{6}
, so we do not give the details here.

Model theorists are particularly interested in those closure operators which are pregeometries, but of those in the diagram, it is easy to see that none but
\ecl
satisfies the exchange property. The fact that
\ecl
is a pregeometry on any exponential field is
[
Kir10a
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Given the description of
\acl
in
\mathbb{B}
, it is natural to ask for a characterisation of the definable closure
\dcl
.
It is immediate that
\left\langle B\right\rangle^{\mathrm{E}}\subseteq\dcl(B)\subseteq\acl(B)=\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
but these bounds are not particularly good.

While neither
i
nor
\tau
is in
\dcl(\emptyset)
, being indistinguishable from
-i
and
-\tau
respectively, we can define the sine function
x\mapsto\sin(x)=\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}
without the parameter
i
, due to the symmetry with
-i
.
So
\dcl(B)
is closed under the sine function and when
i\notin B
this may take us outside
\left\langle B\right\rangle^{E}
.
Furthermore, we saw in the introduction that in the complex exponential field
\{\pi,-\pi\}
is definable, but we have
\sin(\pi/2)=1
and
\sin(-\pi/2)=-1
, so we can use this property to see that
\pi\in\dcl(\emptyset)
in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{exp}}
. We can then use this to define
\pi
in
\mathbb{B}
. Thus,
\pi\in\dcl(\emptyset)
in
\mathbb{B}
too.

Observe that
\lceil\pi\rceil=\Span_{\mathbb{Q}}(\pi,\tau)
and
\tau\notin\dcl(\pi)
, so we do not generally have
\lceil B\rceil\subseteq\dcl(B)
.

Apart from sine, there are many more definable functions such as
x\mapsto e^{e^{\sqrt{x}}}+e^{e^{-\sqrt{x}}}
, suggesting there is no simple description of
\dcl
in
\mathbb{B}
.

6.2.
Non-standard kernel

We have described
\acl
on
\mathbb{B}
itself, so an obvious question to ask is “what about other models of its first-order theory?” If
M
is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{B}
and also has standard kernel, then from §2 of
[
Kir13b
]
it follows that
M
satisfies all the axioms we gave for
\mathbb{B}
except perhaps the countable closure property, and the same proof works to show that
\acl(B)=\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
.
On the other hand if
M
has non-standard kernel then the Schanuel property may fail and then our methods would break down. If the multiplicative Zilber-Pink conjecture is true, then one can work with the Schanuel property “over the kernel”, and combining the methods of this paper with those of
[
KZ14
]
may answer the question. Without assuming that conjecture the question seems much more difficult.

6.3.
Bounded closures

One important role that
\acl
plays in the model theory of complete first-order theories is to characterise those types which cannot have unboundedly many realisations in elementary extensions. We also call this the
bounded closure

\bcl
. The natural axiomatisation of
\mathbb{B}
we gave is in the logic
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}(Q)
, and without the countable closure property we get an axiomatisation in the logic
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
. In these logics, the compactness theorem does not hold, and
\acl
and
\bcl
can differ. It is known that for the
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
-theory of
\mathbb{B}
, the bounded closure
\bcl(B)=\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{ELA}}
, and for the
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}(Q)
-theory we have
\bcl(B)=\ecl(B)
.

6.4.
The complex case

It is an open question whether or not
\mathbb{R}
is definable in
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{exp}}
. If it is, then
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{exp}}
is bi-interpretable with second order arithmetic, which has definable Skolem functions, and
\acl(B)=\dcl(B\cup\{i\})
and is (bi-interpretable with) the smallest elementary submodel of second-order arithmetic containing
B
. This is the best upper bound we can get for
\acl(B)
without something like the homogeneity obtained from Strong Exponential-Algebraic Closedness.

There is the obvious lower bound
\acl(B)\supseteq\left\langle B\cup\{\pi\}\right\rangle^{\mathrm{EA}}
as discussed in the introduction. However, without the Schanuel Property, one does not have any control over
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil
for a finite tuple
{\bar{b}}
, and so the arguments of this paper do not show that
\lceil{\bar{b}}\rceil\subseteq\acl({\bar{b}})
. Things are better if we work over
\lceil\emptyset\rceil
, and we expect that the methods of this paper would show that if
B\supseteq\lceil\emptyset\rceil
then
\acl(B)\supseteq\lceil B\rceil^{\mathrm{EA}}
. If Schanuel’s conjecture is true then
\lceil\emptyset\rceil=2\pi i\mathbb{Q}
and we recover this lower bound for all
B\subseteq\mathbb{C}
.

Parity of ranks of Jacobians of curves
Vladimir Dokchitser, Holly Green, Alexandros Konstantinou, Adam Morgan

Abstract
. We investigate Selmer groups of Jacobians of curves that admit an action of a non-trivial group of automorphisms, and give applications to the study of the parity of Selmer ranks. Under the Shafarevich–Tate conjecture, we give an expression for the parity of the Mordell–Weil rank of an arbitrary Jacobian in terms of purely local invariants; the latter can be seen as an arithmetic analogue of local root numbers, which, under the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, similarly control parities of ranks of abelian varieties. As an application, we give a new proof of the parity conjecture for elliptic curves. The core of the paper is devoted to developing the arithmetic theory of Jacobians for Galois covers of curves, including decomposition of their L L -functions, and the interplay between Brauer relations and Selmer groups.

The core of this paper relies on the study of Galois covers of curves. One subtlety is that we adhere to the following convention:

Convention 1.1
.

Throughout this paper, curves are assumed to be smooth and proper, but are not assumed to be connected, nor are their connected components assumed to be geometrically connected.

The reason for considering this broader notion of a ‘curve’ is that they arise naturally in the context of Galois covers. For example, considering Example
1.9
with
a=0
and

\mathbb{Q}_{7}

fails to be connected. For a thorough treatment of the arithmetic of curves and Jacobians in the context of Convention
1.1
, see
[
29
]
.

In §

-functions

and local root numbers

and show that they satisfy the Artin formalism (

2.
Motivic pieces of curves

In the introduction we factorised the
L
-function of a curve
X
with a group of automorphisms
G
as a product of the
L
-function “pieces”
L(X^{\tau},s)
.
Decompositions of this kind were already mentioned by Serre
[
45
]
as coming from “Artin
L
-functions in the setting of schemes”.
In the present section we formalise this construction and justify the generalisation of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for these
L
-functions given in Conjecture
1.4
. The reader who is willing to take the formulation of the latter conjecture on trust (and its Selmer group analogue given in Conjecture
2.23
) can fairly safely skip this section; the only other results that will be used later are the existence and basic properties of the local and global root numbers
w(X^{\tau}/\mathcal{K})
and
w(X^{\tau})
(Propositions
2.21
,
2.22
) in §§
7
–
8
.

Formally,
X^{\tau}
should be constructed as a motive — it comes from the 1
st
cohomology groups of the variety
X
and the idempotent corresponding to
\tau
in
\mathbb{C}[G]
. We will not set up the entire motivic machinery here and instead will just concentrate of the system of
\ell
-adic representations attached to
X^{\tau}
.
We will show that these are independent of
\ell
and form a compatible system and satisfy other desired properties (Theorem
2.13
, Corollary
2.14
).

Throughout this section we will phrase everything in terms of principally polarised abelian varieties
A
with an action of a finite group
G
by automorphisms. The case of interest for the rest of the paper is
A=\Jac_{X}
for a curve
X/K
with
G
coming from
K
-automorphisms of
X
. Thus we will simply write

L(X^{\tau},s)=L(A^{\tau},s)\qquad\text{and}\qquad w(X^{\tau})=w(A^{\tau}).

While much of what follows remains true without assuming the existence of a principal polarisation, several statements are cleaner in the presence of this assumption. Since our main application is to Jacobians of curves, we elect to work in the principally polarised setting throughout.

In this section we will adopt the following conventions and notation.

Convention.

For the purpose of working with
\ell
-adic representations, we fix embeddings
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{C}
for all
\ell
(with the resulting embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{C}
independent of
\ell
).

Notation 2.1
.

When a prime
\ell
is fixed, we write
\chi_{\rm{cyc}}
for the
\ell
-adic cyclotomic character.

Notation 2.2
.

We take all
d

\tau
of finite groups to be valued in
\GL_{d}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, and implicitly extend scalars to
\GL_{d}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
or

whenever necessary.

For a finite group
G
and a
G

, we write

for the (abelian) number field generated by the values of the character of
\tau
, i.e. by
\Tr\tau(g)

. For

\tau^{\alpha}
for the representation that is
\alpha

\tau
, that is
\Tr\tau^{\alpha}(g)=\alpha(\Tr\tau(g))
for all

.

Convention.

By an automorphism

of a principally polarised abelian variety, we always mean one that respects the polarisation. That is, such that

, where

Definition 2.3
.

For an abelian variety

We caution the reader that this notation depends on the underlying group

H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\mathds{1}})
is the subspace of
G

.

The following abelian variety

plays the role of the (Jacobian of the) quotient curve

.

Definition 2.4

Let

The abelian variety

Remark 2.6

(see

Let

The Jacobian

2.1.
Galois representation of
A^{\tau}

Before discussing the
\ell
-adic representation for
A^{\tau}
we first record the following property of
H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{G})
, analogous to Theorem
1.14
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
A
be a principally polarised abelian variety over a field
L
and
G
a finite group acting on
A
by
L
-automorphisms.
For
H\leq G
,

H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{H})\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{H}

as
\Gal(\overline{L}/L)
-representations, and as
G/H\times\Gal(\overline{L}/L)
-representations if
H
is normal in
G
.

Proof.

Write
N_{H}=\sum_{h\in H}h
, which we view as an endomorphism of
A
. Note that
N_{H}(A)\subseteq A_{H}
. Denoting by
i_{H}
the inclusion of
A_{H}
into
A
, we thus have maps

N_{H}:A\rightarrow A_{H}
and
i_{H}:A_{H}\rightarrow A.

Noting that the image of
N_{H}^{*}:H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{H})\rightarrow H^{1}_{\ell}(A)
is contained in
H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{H}
, we obtain maps

N_{H}^{*}:H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{H})\rightarrow H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{H}\quad\textup{ and }\quad i_{H}^{*}:H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{H}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{H}).

The composition
N_{H}\circ i_{H}
is multiplication by
|H|
on
A_{H}
, hence
i_{H}^{*}\circ N_{H}^{*}
is multiplication by
|H|
on
H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{H})
. Similarly,
N_{H}^{*}\circ i_{H}^{*}
is multiplication by
|H|
on
H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{H}
.
We conclude that both
N_{H}^{*}
and
i_{H}^{*}
are isomorphisms as in the statement.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
A
be a principally polarised
abelian variety over a field
L
of characteristic 0, let
G
be a finite group acting on
A
by
L
-automorphisms, and let
\ell
be a prime.
There are isomorphisms of
\Gal(\overline{L}/L)
-representations as follows:

(1)

For
G
-representations
\tau,\tau^{\prime}
,
H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\tau\oplus\tau^{\prime}})\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\tau})\oplus H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\tau^{\prime}})
;

(2)

If
H\leq G
and
\rho
is a representation of
H
, then
H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\Ind_{H}^{G}\rho})\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\rho})
;

(3)

If
\sigma
factors through
G/N
for
N\triangleleft G
, then
H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\sigma})\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}((A_{N})^{\sigma})
;

(4)

For
H\leq G
,
H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathds{1}})\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{H})
;

(5)

There is a non-degenerate
\Gal(\overline{L}/L)
-equivariant bilinear pairing

H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\tau})\times H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\tau^{*}})\to\chi_{\rm{cyc}}^{*}.

If
\tau
is self-dual and orthogonal, this pairing is alternating and
\dim H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\tau})
is even;

(6)

H_{\ell}^{1}(A^{\tau})^{*}\simeq H_{\ell}^{1}(A^{\tau^{*}})\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}\chi_{\rm{cyc}}
;

(7)

If
\tau
is self-dual, then
(\det H_{\ell}^{1}(A^{\tau}))^{\otimes 2}\simeq\chi_{\rm{cyc}}^{*\otimes\dim H_{\ell}^{1}(A^{\tau})}
. If
\tau
is orthogonal, then
\det H_{\ell}^{1}(A^{\tau})\simeq\chi_{\rm{cyc}}^{*\otimes\frac{1}{2}\dim H_{\ell}^{1}(A^{\tau})}
.

Proof.

(1) Follows from the fact that
\textup{Hom}_{G}
commutes with finite direct sums in the first variable.

(2) Since induction is left adjoint to restriction, for any
G
-representation
V
there is an isomorphism

\Hom_{G}(\Ind_{H}^{G}\rho,V)\simeq\Hom_{H}(\rho,V)

which is functorial in
V
. Taking
V=H^{1}_{\ell}(X)
gives the result.

(3) By Proposition
2.7

H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{N})\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{N}
. Since
\sigma
factors through
G/N
, the natural map

\Hom_{G}\big(\sigma,H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{N}\big)\rightarrow\Hom_{G}\big(\sigma,H^{1}_{\ell}(A)\big)

is an isomorphism, from which the claim follows.

(4) By (2) and Proposition
2.7
,
H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathds{1}})\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{H}\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A_{H})
.

(5) It suffices to construct a
G
-invariant pairing

(
*
)

\textup{Hom}(\tau,H^{1}_{\ell}(A))\times\textup{Hom}(\tau^{*},H^{1}_{\ell}(A))\longrightarrow\chi_{\rm{cyc}}^{*}

with the same properties. Restricting this to
G
-invariants then gives the sought pairing on
H^{1}_{\ell}(A^{\tau})
(the restriction remains non-degenerate by
[
13
, Lemma 2.15]
). Note that the standard isomorphisms of vector spaces

\textup{Hom}(\tau,H^{1}_{\ell}(A))\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A)\otimes\tau^{*}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\textup{Hom}(\tau^{*},H^{1}_{\ell}(A))\simeq H^{1}_{\ell}(A)\otimes\tau

are
G\times\textup{Gal}(\overline{L}/L)
-equivariant, where
G
acts diagonally on the tensor products and
\textup{Gal}(\overline{L}/L)
acts via the first factors. Having made these identifications, the tensor product of the Weil pairing on

with the natural ‘evaluation’ pairing between
\tau
and
\tau^{*}
gives the pairing (
*
).
That this is alternating when
\tau
is self-dual and orthogonal follows from the fact that the tensor product of an antisymmetric pairing with a symmetric pairing is antisymmetric.

(6) Follows from (5).

(7) The first claim follows from (6) on taking determinants. The second claim follows from the orthogonal case of (5) and the Pfaffian identity
\textup{pf}(MTM^{t})=\det(M)\textup{pf}(T),
where

[

).

We now turn to the properties of

for semistable abelian varieties, except that we additionally keep track of the action of a finite group of automorphisms.

Notation 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a non-archimedean local field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}
and residue field
k
.
Let

be a principally polarised abelian variety, and let

be a finite group acting on

Recall (e.g. from

where

is the identity component of the special fibre of the Néron model

of

,

is a torus and

an abelian variety.

We denote by
\mathfrak{X}_{A}
the character lattice of

.

The action of

extends uniquely to

, inducing actions on

,

and

Proposition 2.10
.

Continuing with the setup of Notation

, let

be a prime distinct from the characteristic of

(1)

We have

H^{1}_{\ell}(A)/H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{I_{\mathcal{K}}}\simeq(\mathfrak{X}_{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\otimes\chi_{\rm{cyc}}^{*}

as
G\times\Gal(\bar{k}/k)
-modules.

(2)

Let

, where
W_{\mathcal{K}}
is the Weil group of
\mathcal{K}
. Then
\Tr(g|H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{I_{\mathcal{K}}})
is a rational number independent of
\ell
. If
\sigma
acts on
\overline{k}
as a non-negative power of the geometric Frobenius, then
\Tr(g|H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{I_{\mathcal{K}}})\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The Weil pairing
T_{\ell}(A)\otimes T_{\ell}(A)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
induces an isomorphism

(
*
)

H^{1}_{\ell}(A)\simeq T_{\ell}(A)(-1)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

of

-modules, where the

The description of the
\ell
-adic Tate module of a semistable abelian variety given in
[
25
]
(see also

whose graded pieces are unramified and are, respectively,

\mathfrak{X}_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\quad T_{\ell}(B)\quad\textup{ and }\quad\mathfrak{X}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}.

Here

is the cocharacter lattice of

. Twisting by
(-1)
and using (

) we deduce part (1).

For part (2), the above discussion gives

\textup{Tr}\big(g\mid H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{I_{\mathcal{K}}}\big)=\textup{Tr}\big(g\mid T_{\ell}(B)(-1)\big)+\textup{Tr}\big(g\mid\mathfrak{X}_{*}(T)\big).

Consequently, it suffices to consider the trace of

on

. The Weil pairing on

gives an isomorphism of

-modules
T_{\ell}(B)(-1)\simeq\textup{Hom}\big(T_{\ell}(B^{\vee}),\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\big),
where

as

Proposition 2.11

Let

be a principally polarised abelian variety over a non-archimedean local field
\mathcal{K}

\mathcal{K}
-automorphisms, and let

(1)

\Tr(\Frob_{\mathcal{K}}\>|\>H_{\ell}^{1}(A^{\tau})^{I_{\mathcal{K}}})=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\Tr(g^{-1}|\tau)\Tr(\Frob_{\mathcal{K}}\cdot g|H^{1}_{\ell}(A)^{I_{\mathcal{K}}}).

(2)

For

Proof.

(1) For irreducible

(2) below, applied to

,

(2) The coefficients of the characteristic polynomial are symmetric functions in the eigenvalues of Frobenius, and hence can be expressed as universal

(3) This follows from Proposition

(1).

Lemma 2.12

Let

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
A
be a principally polarised abelian variety over a local field
\mathcal{K}
of characteristic 0 with finite residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let

be a finite group acting on

by
\mathcal{K}
-automorphisms, and let

be a representation of

.
The local Weil–Deligne representation associated to

\ell

where
\rho_{i}
are continuous complex representations of the Weil group that are independent of the choice of
\ell\nmid q
, Frobenius acts semisimply on the

with eigenvalues of absolute value

, and

denotes the 2-dimensional special representation.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the result for irreducible

(1)).
As

is an abelian variety, the Weil-Deligne representation associated to

admits as decomposition of the above form (see e.g.
[

]
Proposition 1.10).
By Lemma

(1),

.

It remains to prove independence of

.
For a fixed integer

and finite extension of the coefficient field

,
[
18
]
Theorem 7 and Corollary 8 show that there is a finite list of finite extensions

with the property that

(1)

with

has semistable reduction over each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, and

(2)

-adic representation

for

and

is determined by the traces of
\Frob_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
acting on

(2) and Proposition

(1).
∎

Proposition 2.22

Let

be a principally polarised abelian variety over a local field
\mathcal{K}
,

a finite group acting on
A
by
\mathcal{K}
-automorphisms, and
\tau
an orthogonal

(1)

(2)

Proof.

(1) This follows from Definition
2.15
and
[
11
]

\mathparagraph
5.3.

(2)
By Proposition

H^{1}(A^{\tau})/H^{1}(A^{\tau})^{I_{\mathcal{K}}}\simeq\Hom_{G}(\tau,\mathfrak{X}_{A}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\otimes\chi_{\rm{cyc}}^{-1}.

By
[

]
(4.2.4), the root number of an

-adic representation

is related to that of its semisimplification

by
w(V/\mathcal{K})=w(V_{ss}/\mathcal{K})\frac{\sgn\det(-\Frob^{-1}_{\mathcal{K}}|V_{ss}^{I_{\mathcal{K}}})}{\sgn\det(-\Frob^{-1}_{\mathcal{K}}|V^{I_{\mathcal{K}}})}

,

We now define pseudo Brauer relations and their regulator constants, extending the notion of regulator constants for Brauer relations considered in

Throughout this section,

,

is a set of representatives of the subgroups of

3.1.
Pseudo Brauer relations

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an

-representation.
An element
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{H}]

and

\rho_{1}\oplus\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]\simeq\rho_{2}\oplus\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{j}^{\prime}].

Remark 3.2
.

When
\mathcal{V}=L[G]
, we necessarily have
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=0
. In this case, Definition
3.1
coincides with the existing notion of a Brauer relation.

Remark 3.3
.

The choice of representatives in
\mathcal{H}
will be immaterial in practice (see Remark
3.23
for a thorough discussion). When specific choices are required (for instance in §
6
), these will be explicitly stated.

The set of all pseudo Brauer relations relative to
\mathcal{V}
forms an abelian subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{H}]
. The following result describes the rank of this subgroup.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an
L[G]
representation and let
\textup{Irr}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
be the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of
G
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then,

\rk_{\mathbb{Z}}\textup{PBR}(\mathcal{V})=\#\{\textup{conj. classes of non-cyclic }H\leq G\}+\#\{\rho\in\textup{Irr}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G):\langle\rho,{\mathcal{V}}\rangle=0\},

where
\textup{PBR}(\mathcal{V})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{H}]
denotes the subgroup of pseudo Brauer relations relative to
\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

Denote by
BR\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{H}]
the subgroup of Brauer relations. It’s well known that the rank of
BR
is equal to the number of conjugacy classes of non-cyclic subgroups of
G
. Indeed, denoting by
R(G)
the rational representation ring, we have a natural linear map
\alpha:\mathbb{Q}[\mathcal{H}]\rightarrow R(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
sending
\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}
to
\sum_{i}n_{i}\textup{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}\mathds{1}
. The kernel of
\alpha
is
BR\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, the dimension of
R(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is equal to the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of
G
by
[
46
, §13.1, Cor. 1]
, and
\alpha
is surjective by the induction theorem
[
46
, §13.1, Theorem 30]
.

The restriction of
\alpha
to
PBR(\mathcal{V})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is readily seen to have image contained in the subspace of
R(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
spanned by irreducible rational representations
\rho
with
\left\langle\rho,\mathcal{V}\right\rangle=0
. To complete the proof we wish to show that, conversely, any such
\rho
lies in the image of
\alpha
. This, again, is a consequence of the induction theorem
[
46
, §13.1, Theorem 30]
.
∎

3.2.
Regulator constants

Notation 3.5
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{H}]
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to a self-dual
L[G]
-representation
\mathcal{V}
. Given a non-degenerate,
G
-invariant,
L
-bilinear pairing
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
on
\mathcal{V}
, we denote by
\langle,\rangle_{1}
the pairing

\langle,\rangle_{1}=\bigoplus_{i}\frac{1}{|H_{i}|}\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle\quad\textup{on the vector space}\quad\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{H_{i}},

and define the pairing
\left\langle,\right\rangle_{2}
on
\bigoplus_{j}\mathcal{V}^{H_{j}^{\prime}}
similarly. Given a basis
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{i}\}
for
\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{H_{i}}
, we denote by
\langle\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}\rangle_{1}
the matrix with
(i,j)
th entry
\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle_{1}
, and define
\langle\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\rangle_{2}
for a basis
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
of
\bigoplus_{j}\mathcal{V}^{H_{j}^{\prime}}
similarly. By
[
13
, Lemma 2.15]
, both
\langle,\rangle_{1}
and
\langle,\rangle_{2}
are non-degenerate.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{H}]
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to a self-dual
L[G]
-representation
\mathcal{V}
, and let
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
be a non-degenerate,
G
-invariant,
L
-bilinear pairing on
\mathcal{V}
taking values in some field extension
L^{\prime}
of
L
. Given bases
\mathcal{B}
for
\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{H_{i}}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
for
\bigoplus_{j}\mathcal{V}^{H_{j}^{\prime}}
, we define

\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})=\frac{\text{det}\langle\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}\rangle_{1}}{\text{det}\langle\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\rangle_{2}}\in L^{\prime\times}.

We then define the
regulator constant of
\mathcal{V}
relative to
\Theta
, denoted
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})
, to be the class of
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})
in
L^{\prime\times}/L^{\times 2}
for any choice of bases
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
(the result being independent of this choice).

Many properties of regulator constants associated to Brauer relations
[
13
, §2.ii]
continue to hold for the pseudo Brauer relations of Definition
3.6
. Specifically, we have the following:

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
L
be a field of characteristic
0
,
G
be a finite group and
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2}
be finite dimensional self-dual
L[G]
-representations. Then,

(1)

given a pseudo Brauer relation
\Theta
relative to
\mathcal{V}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})
is independent of the choice of pairing
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
, and takes values in
L^{\times}/L^{\times 2}
,

(2)

given pseudo Brauer relations
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
relative to
\mathcal{V}
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{C}_{\Theta_{1}}(\mathcal{V})\,\mathcal{C}_{\Theta_{2}}(\mathcal{V}),

(3)

if
\Theta
is a pseudo Brauer relation relative to both
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
, then

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{2})=\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V}_{1})\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V}_{2}).

In particular, if
\mathcal{V}\simeq\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}_{i}^{n_{i}}
is a decomposition into self-dual
L[G]
-representations, then

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})=\prod_{i}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V}_{i})^{n_{i}}.

We will prove Theorem
3.7
after introducing an alternative description of regulator constants in §
3.4
. We note that an analogue of (1) also holds for
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{V})
, see Remark
3.22
.

Remark 3.8
.

We note that
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}
is compatible with extension of scalars: if
K/L
is a field extension, then
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V}\otimes_{L}K)
in
K^{\times}/K^{\times 2}
. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{V}
descends to a
K[G]
-representation
\mathcal{W}
for a subfield
K\subseteq L
, then
\mathcal{W}
is unique up to
K[G]
-isomorphism. In particular, we can associate to
\mathcal{V}
a well-defined regulator constant
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{W})\in K^{\times}/K^{\times 2}
. We will often omit
\mathcal{W}
from the notation and simply view
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})
as an element of
K^{\times}/K^{\times 2}
without comment.

Theorem
3.7
represents a generalisation of
[
13
, Theorem 2.17 & Corollary 2.18]
. In addition, the following result generalises
[
13
, Corollary 2.25 & Lemma 2.26]
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to
\mathcal{V}
. If either

(1)

\mathcal{V}
is symplectic, or

(2)

\langle\mathcal{V},L[G/H_{i}]\rangle=\langle\mathcal{V},L[G/H_{j}^{\prime}]\rangle=0
for all
i,j
,

then
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})\equiv 1\textup{ mod }L^{\times 2}
.

Proof.

See
[
13
, Corollary 2.25, Lemma 2.26]
.
∎

We use this to show that
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})
can be taken to be a positive real number.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\Theta
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to a self-dual
L[G]
-representation
\mathcal{V}
. Then, there exists a positive real number in the same class as
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})
in
L^{\times}/L^{\times 2}
. (Recall that we fixed an embedding
L\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
at the start of the section.)

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{V}\simeq\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{2}
where
\psi_{1}
(resp.
\psi_{2}
) is an orthogonal (resp. symplectic) respresentation.
Then
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\psi_{1})\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\psi_{2})=\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\psi_{1})
by Theorem
3.7
(3) and Lemma
3.9
. Now
\psi_{1}
is realisable over
\mathbb{R}
, say on a real vector space
\mathcal{W}
, which necessarily admits a positive definite
G
-invariant pairing (take the average, over
g\in G
, of any inner product). The associated pairings
\langle,\rangle_{1},\langle,\rangle_{2}
(as in Notation
3.5
) are then positive definite. Computing
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{W})
with respect to these pairings we obtain a positive real number. By Remark
3.8
, this gives the result.
∎

3.3.
The representations
\tau_{\Theta,p}

We now introduce a special class of representations
\tau_{\Theta,p}
in the case
L=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, which feature throughout the paper.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\Theta
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to a self-dual
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-representation
\mathcal{V}
.
Consider the set
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{V}}
of all self-dual
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-representations
\tau
all of whose irreducible constituents appear in
\mathcal{V}
, that is
\tau
for which
\langle\rho,\mathcal{V}\rangle=0
implies
\langle\rho,\tau\rangle=0
for all
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G]
-representations
\rho
.
We define
\tau_{\Theta,p}
to be (any choice of) self-dual
\mathbb{C}[G]
-representation, all of whose complex irreducible constituents are orthogonal, that satisfies

\langle\tau_{\Theta,p},\rho\rangle\equiv\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\rho)\quad\textup{mod }2,\qquad\qquad\forall\rho\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{V}}.

(For the purposes of this paper, the choice of
\tau_{\Theta,p}
will be immaterial.)

Remark 3.12
.

A choice for
\tau_{\Theta,p}
always exists by Lemma
3.9
(1). For instance, let
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i}
be the set of all self-dual and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-irreducible representations of
G
with
\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau_{i})
odd. Then we can take

\tau_{\Theta,p}=\bigoplus_{i}\big(\textup{any }\mathbb{C}\textup{-irreducible constituent of }\tau_{i}\big).

In particular, when all irreducible representations of
G
are realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
, we can take

\tau_{\Theta,p}=\bigoplus_{\tau}(\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau))\tau,

where the sum ranges over all irreducible representations of
G
, and
\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)
is taken in
\{0,1\}
.

Remark 3.13
.

If
\Theta
is a Brauer relation (see Remark
3.2
), then Definition
3.11
coincides with
[

, Definition 2.50]
.

]

3.4.
Alternative description of regulator constants

The following reinterpretation of regulator constants is based on expositions, in the case of Brauer relations, given in
[
3
, §3]
and
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
. We begin with some notation.

Notation 3.15
.

Let
M
be a
\mathbb{Z}[G]
-module (below we will take
M=\mathcal{V}
to be a self-dual
L[G]
-representation, but this greater generality will be useful in later sections). For each subgroup
H
of
G
we have an isomorphism

(
*
)

\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[G/H],M)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}M^{H}

given by evaluating homomorphisms at the trivial coset. Given subgroups
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
and
H_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,H_{m}^{\prime}
of
G
, define
G
-sets

S=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}G/H_{i}
and
S^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}G/H_{j}^{\prime}.

Taking
\mathbb{Z}[S]
and
\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]
to be the corresponding permutation modules,
(
*
)
induces isomorphisms

\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],M)\simeq\bigoplus_{i}M^{H_{i}}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}],M)\simeq\bigoplus_{j}M^{H_{j}^{\prime}}.

Consequently, given
\Phi\in\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}])
, the map from
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}],M)
to
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],M)
sending
f
to
f\circ\Phi
induces a homomorphism

\Phi^{*}:\bigoplus_{j}M^{H_{j}^{\prime}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i}M^{H_{i}}.

The
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}[S]
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]
) is canonically self-dual, via the pairing making the elements of
S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
) an orthonormal basis. Given
\Phi\in\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}])
we denote by
\Phi^{\vee}
the corresponding dual homomorphism
\Phi^{\vee}:\mathbb{Z}[S^{\prime}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[S]
.

Definition 3.16
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{H}]
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to a self-dual
L[G]
-representation
\mathcal{V}
.
We say that a
\mathbb{Z}[G]
-module homomorphism
\Phi:\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{i}]\to\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{j}^{\prime}]

realises

\Theta
if the induced map

\Phi^{*}:\bigoplus_{j}\mathcal{V}^{H_{j}^{\prime}}\to\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{H_{i}}

is an isomorphism.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to a self-dual
L[G]
-representation
\mathcal{V}
. Then, there exists a
G
-module homomorphism
\Phi
realising
\Theta
.

Proof.

By Definition
3.1
there are
\mathbb{C}G
-representations
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
such that

\rho_{1}\oplus\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]
is isomorphic to
\rho_{2}\oplus\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{j}^{\prime}]
.
We may assume that
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
have no common irreducible constituents, so that, in particular, they are both realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
.
We can then find free
\mathbb{Z}[G]
-modules
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
such that
V_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\rho_{1}
and
V_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\rho_{2}
, and a
G
-module homomorphism

\phi:V_{1}\oplus\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{i}]\to V_{2}\oplus\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{j}^{\prime}]\

with finite kernel and cokernel. Denoting by
\iota
and
\pi
the inclusion/projection in/out of the permutation modules, one checks that
\Phi=\pi\circ\phi\circ\iota
realises the pseudo Brauer relation
\Theta
. ∎

Remark 3.18
.

If
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
is a Brauer relation, then a
G
-map realising it is precisely a
G
-injection
\Phi:\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{i}]\to\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{j}^{\prime}]
with finite cokernel.

Notation 3.19
.

Given finite dimensional
L
-vector spaces
V,W
, with bases
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\{v_{i}\}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{2}=\{w_{j}\}_{j}
respectively, and given an
L
-linear map
T:V\to W
, we write
[T]_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}^{\mathcal{B}_{2}}
to denote the matrix of
T
relative to
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
. Similarly to Notation
3.5
, given a pairing
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
between
V
and
W
, we denote by
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
the matrix with
(i,j)
th entry
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle v_{i},w_{j}\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
.

The following proposition (along with Corollary
3.21
) gives the promised alternative description of regulator constants. Analogues for Brauer relations appear as
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
,
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
.

We highlight that, while we have not yet shown that
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{V})
is independent of the choice of pairing
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
made in its definition, the proof of the proposition applies regardless of the choice made. As a by-product, this will prove the sought independence.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to a self-dual
L[G]
-representation
\mathcal{V}
.
For any
G
-module homomorphism
\Phi
realising
\Theta
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{V})=\frac{\textup{det}[(\Phi^{\vee})^{*}]_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}}{\textup{det}[\Phi^{*}]_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{B}}}

where
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
are bases for
\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{H_{i}}
,
\bigoplus_{j}\mathcal{V}^{H_{j}^{\prime}}
respectively.

Proof.

Fix a non-degenerate
G
-invariant pairing
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
on
\mathcal{V}
. We follow the proof of
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
. For a finite
G
-set
T
we define the pairing
(,)_{T}
on
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[T],\mathcal{V})
by setting

(f_{1},f_{2})_{T}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{t\in T}\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle.

Take
S=\bigsqcup_{i}G/H_{i}
and
S^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{j}G/H_{j}^{\prime}
. After identifying
\textup{Hom}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}[S],\mathcal{V})
with
\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{H_{i}}
as in Notation
3.15
, the pairing
(,)_{S}
identifies with the pairing
\langle,\rangle_{1}
of Notation
3.5
. Similarly, the pairing
(,)_{S^{\prime}}
identifies with the pairing
\langle,\rangle_{2}
. An easy computation then shows that
\Phi^{*}
and
(\Phi^{\vee})^{*}
are adjoint for the pairings
\langle,\rangle_{1}
and
\langle,\rangle_{2}
. We now compute

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{V})=\frac{\text{det}\langle\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}\rangle_{1}}{\text{det}\langle\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\rangle_{2}}=\frac{\text{det}\langle\mathcal{B},\Phi^{*}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\rangle_{1}}{\textup{det}[\Phi^{*}]_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{B}}}\cdot\frac{\textup{det}[(\Phi^{\vee})^{*}]_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}}{\text{det}\langle(\Phi^{\vee})^{*}\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\rangle_{2}}.\qed

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to a self-dual
L[G]
-representation
\mathcal{V}
. Let
\Phi
be a
G
-module homomorphism realising
\Theta
and
\mathcal{B}
a basis for
\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{H_{i}}
.
Then

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B},\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{V})=\frac{1}{\textup{det}(\Phi^{\vee}\Phi)^{*}}.

In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B},\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{V})
is independent of
\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Immediate from Proposition
3.20
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Parts (2) and (3) follow readily from part (1). To prove (1), pick some
\Phi
realising the pseudo Brauer relation
\Theta
, and pick a pairing
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
as in the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})
. The proof of Proposition
3.20
then shows that

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{V})\equiv\textup{det}(\Phi^{\vee}\Phi)^{*}\quad\textup{mod }L^{\times 2}.

Since the right hand side is an element of
L^{\times}
which is independent of
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
, the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.22
.

By the same argument, for any bases
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
as in Definition
3.6
, the quantity
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{V})
lies in
L^{\times}
, and is independent of the choice of pairing
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle,\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle
used to define it.

Remark 3.23
.

The choice of representatives in
\mathcal{H}
made at the start of this section does not affect the above results in any meaningful way. For instance, if
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{H}
is a pseudo Brauer relation for
\mathcal{V}
, and we set
M_{i}=g_{i}H_{i}g_{i}^{-1}
,
M_{i}^{\prime}=g_{i}^{\prime}H_{i}^{\prime}g_{i}^{\prime-1}
for some
g_{i},g_{i}^{\prime}\in G
, then we have canonical isomorphisms
\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{H_{i}}\simeq\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}^{M_{i}}
and
\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}[G/M_{i}]\simeq\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{i}]
given by
(x_{i})\mapsto(g_{i}x_{i})
and
(y_{i}M_{i})\mapsto(y_{i}g_{i}H_{i})
respectively. Similarly for
H_{j}^{\prime}
.
We conclude that the calculation of a regulator constant from Definition
3.6
and the notion of realising a pseudo Brauer relation from Definition
3.16
are consistent even after a change from
\Theta
to
\Theta^{\prime}=\sum_{i}M_{i}-\sum_{j}M_{j}^{\prime}
. In addition, a change from
\Theta
to
\Theta^{\prime}
does not affect Proposition
3.20
or Corollary
3.21
.

4.
Isogenies induced from pseudo Brauer relations

Let
X
be a curve defined over a field
K
of characteristic
0
and let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{K}(X)
. In what follows, we will exploit a consequence of
[
29
, Theorem 4.14]
, presented as Theorem
4.3
below, which allows us to associate certain isogenies to pseudo Brauer relations relative to the
\ell
-adic Tate module
V_{\ell}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\ell
denote any prime. We say that
\Theta
is a pseudo Brauer relation for
G
and
X
if
\Theta
is a pseudo Brauer relation relative to
V_{\ell}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
in the sense of Definition
3.1
. When there is no ambiguity, we call these pseudo Brauer relations for
X
.

Remark 4.2
.

Having fixed an embedding
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we have a
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[G]
-isomorphism

H_{1}(\textup{Jac}_{X}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\simeq V_{\ell}(\textup{Jac}_{X})

for every prime
\ell
. Thus the notion of a pseudo Brauer relation for
G
and
X
is independent of
\ell
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be a curve over a field
K
of characteristic
0
, and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{K}(X)
. Let
\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation for
X
realised by
\Phi
(in the sense of Definition
3.16
). Then, the following hold.

(1)

The
G
-map
\Phi
induces a
K
-isogeny

f_{\Phi}:\prod_{j}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}}\to\prod_{i}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}}.

(2)

If
\Phi^{\prime}
realises a pseudo Brauer relation
\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}-\sum_{k}{H}_{k}^{\prime\prime}
for
X
, then the composition
\Phi^{\prime}\Phi
realises
\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{k}{H}_{k}^{\prime\prime}
and
f_{\Phi^{\prime}\Phi}=f_{\Phi}f_{\Phi^{\prime}}.

(3)

The dual homomorphism
\Phi^{\vee}
(as in Notation
3.15
) realises the pseudo Brauer relation
\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}-\sum_{i}H_{i}
for
X
, and we have
f_{\Phi^{\vee}}=(f_{\Phi})^{\vee}
where
(f_{\Phi})^{\vee}
denotes the dual of
f_{\Phi}
with respect to the canonical principal polarisations.

Proof.

(1) follows from
[
29
, Theorem 4.14(1,3)]
. For (2), it is clear that
\Phi^{\prime}\Phi
realises the pseudo Brauer relation
\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{k}{H}_{k}^{\prime\prime}
, while the equailty
f_{\Phi^{\prime}\Phi}=f_{\Phi}f_{\Phi^{\prime}}
follows from
[
29
, Theorem 4.14(2)]
. (3) follows from
[
29
, Theorem 4.14(1)]
.
∎

The next result concerns the degree of the isogeny
f_{\Phi}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
is a self-dual
G
-representation. Fix any basis
\mathcal{B}_{1}
for
\Omega^{1}(\prod_{i}\Jac_{X/H_{i}})
and let
\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}
be the basis for
\Omega^{1}(\prod_{j}\Jac_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}})
obtained by applying
(\Phi^{\vee})^{*}
to the basis vectors in
\mathcal{B}_{1}.

(1)

We have

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))^{-1}=\pm{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi})}.

(2)

If
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
is orthogonal, then

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))^{-1}={\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi})}.

(3)

For any
G
-maps
\Phi_{1}
,
\Phi_{2}
realising
\Theta
,
{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi_{1}})}/{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi_{2}})}
lies in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
.

Proof.

For (1), we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))^{-2}

\displaystyle\overset{\begin{subarray}{c}\textup{Cor. \ref{Regulator Constants and pullback basis} \&}\\
\textup{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Etale_paper}{}{}, Lem. 5.10(1)]}}\end{subarray}}{=}

\displaystyle\textup{det}\big((\Phi^{\vee}\Phi)^{*}\ |\ V_{\ell}(\prod\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}})\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\textup{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Etale_paper}{}{}, Lem. 4.28]}}}{=}

\displaystyle\textup{det}\big(f_{\Phi^{\vee}\Phi}\ |\ V_{\ell}(\prod\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}})\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\textup{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{MR861974}{}{}, Prop. 12.9]}}}{=}

\displaystyle\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi^{\vee}\Phi})

\displaystyle\overset{\textup{Thm. \ref{isogeny_from_pseudo_brauer} (2),(3)}}{=}

\displaystyle\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi})^{2}.

For (2), the
G
-representation
\Omega^{1}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
is realisable over
\mathbb{R}
, say on an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
W_{\mathbb{R}}
. By evaluating
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))
with respect to an
\mathbb{R}
-basis
\mathcal{B}_{1}
for
\prod_{i}(W_{\mathbb{R}})^{H_{i}}
, Lemma
3.10
combined with Theorem
3.7
(1) tell us that
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))
is a positive real number. Since the equality in
(1)
holds for any
\mathcal{B}_{1}
, we obtain the result.
For (3), note that
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})^{\oplus 2}
is realisable over
\mathbb{Q}
by
[
29
, Lemma 5.10(2)]
. If follows from
[
41
, Theorem 32.15]
that there exist infinitely many distinct quadratic extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\Omega^{1}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
is realisable over
L
. For each such, we deduce from part (1) and Theorem
3.7
(1) that

\pm\tfrac{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi_{1}})}{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi_{2}})}\in L^{\times 2}.

This is only possible if
\tfrac{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi_{1}})}{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi_{2}})}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
.
∎

5.
Local formulae for Selmer rank parities

Let
X/K
be a curve defined over a number field
K
, and let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{K}(X)
. The main result of this section expresses the valuation of the regulator constant of the dual
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group,
{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\Jac_{X})
, in terms of explicit local invariants.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be a curve over a number field
K
, and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{K}(X)
. Let
\Theta
be a pseudo Brauer relation for
X
, and suppose that
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
is a self-dual
K[G]
-representation. Then

\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X}))\equiv\sum_{v\textup{ place of }K}\ord_{p}\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2,

where the local invariant
\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})
is as in Definition
5.16
. In particular, for any choice of
\tau_{\Theta,p}
as in Definition
3.11
,

\langle\tau_{\Theta,p},{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\Jac_{X})\rangle\equiv\sum_{v\textup{ place of }K}\ord_{p}\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2.

To prove this theorem, we begin by defining the following local invariant

\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/K_{v})=\frac{\#\textup{coker}f_{\Phi}(K_{v})}{\#\textup{ker}f_{\Phi}(K_{v})}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i}\mu_{v}({X/H_{i}})}{\prod_{j}\mu_{v}({X/H_{j}^{\prime}})},

where
\Phi
is a
G
-map realising
\Theta
,
\textup{coker}f_{\Phi}(K_{v})
and
\textup{ker}f_{\Phi}(K_{v})
are the induced maps on
K_{v}
-points and
\mu_{v}
encodes whether a curve is deficient at a place
v
(see §
5.1
below). We prove an analogue of the above formula (see Theorem
5.8
) with
\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}
in place of
\Lambda_{\Theta}
. Unfortunately,
\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}
depends on the choice of
\Phi
. To remove this dependence (see Lemma
5.11
(1)), we introduce a revised invariant

\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})=\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/K_{v})\cdot\bigl|\sqrt{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi})}\bigr|_{v}.

The drawback now is that
\ord_{p}\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}\equiv 0,\,\frac{1}{2},\,1\textup{ or }\,\frac{3}{2}\bmod 2
. We finally fix this (see Theorem
5.17
(1)) by defining

{\Lambda}_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})=\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})\cdot\Big|\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\textup{sf}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))}\Big|_{v},

where
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\textup{sf}}\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the square-free part of
\deg(f_{\Phi})
, calculated with respect to any
\Phi
. We caution that whilst
\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}
is multiplicative in
\Theta
, in general
\Lambda_{\Theta}
is not.

Remark 5.2
.

When
K_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
is a finite extension and
p\neq 2\textup{ or }\ell
, we will additionally show (see Theorem
5.17
(3)) that

\textup{ord}_{p}\,\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})=\textup{ord}_{p}\,\frac{\prod_{i}c_{v}(\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}})}{\prod_{j}c_{v}(\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}})}

where
c_{v}(A)
denotes the Tamagawa number of an abelian variety
A/K_{v}
.
Lemma
5.19
details the analogue of this formula for an arbitrary prime
p
.

Remark 5.3
.

By Theorem
1.14
(5),(7),
\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
is a self-dual
G
-representation for all
p
, and any pseudo Brauer relation for
X
is a pseudo Brauer relation relative to
\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X})
. Thus, the left-hand side of the formula in Theorem
5.1
is well-defined.

5.1.
Deficiency

Here we remind the reader of the notion of deficiency for curves over local fields. It is well-known that when the curve arises via base-change from a number field, this concept controls the size, modulo rational squares, of the
2
-primary part of the Shafarevich–Tate group of its Jacobian (see
[
40
, §8]
for geometrically connected curves, and
[
29
, §5.5]
for an extension to the curves of Convention
1.1
).

Definition 5.4

(
[
29
]
, Definition 5.13)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a local field of characteristic 0.

A geometrically connected curve
X/\mathcal{K}
of genus
g
is called
deficient
if it has no
\mathcal{K}
-rational divisor of degree
g-1
. For such
X
, we define

\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $X$ is deficient,}\\
1&\textup{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Suppose that
X/\mathcal{K}
is connected (but not necessarily geometrically connected). We write
\mathcal{L}
for the minimal field of definition of one of its geometric components,
Y
say, and define
\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X)=\mu_{\mathcal{L}}(Y)
.

Finally, writing
X=\bigsqcup_{i}X_{i}
as a disjoint union of connected components, we define

\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X)=\prod_{i}\mu_{\mathcal{K}}(X_{i}).

If the field
\mathcal{K}
is clear from context, we will often omit it from the notation.
Further, when
\mathcal{K}=K_{v}
is the completion of a number field
K
at a place
v
, we write
\mu_{v}(X)
in place of
\mu_{K_{v}}(X)
.

5.2.
A local formula in
\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/\mathcal{K})

Here we prove the analogue of Theorem
5.1
obtained by replacing
\Lambda_{\Theta}
with the local invariant
\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}
defined below.

Notation 5.5
.

For an abelian variety
A
over a local field
\mathcal{K}
of characteristic
0
, and a choice of a non-zero exterior form
\omega
on
A
, we write

C(A,\omega)=\begin{cases}c(A)\cdot|\omega/\omega^{0}|&\textup{when $\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ is finite},\\
\int_{A(\mathcal{K})}|\omega|&\textup{when ${\mathcal{K}}=\mathbb{R}$},\\
2^{\text{dim}(A)}\int_{A(\mathcal{K})}|\omega\wedge\bar{\omega}|&\textup{when ${\mathcal{K}}=\mathbb{C}$}.\end{cases}

Here
\omega/\omega^{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
is such that
\omega=(\omega/\omega^{0})\cdot\omega^{0}
, where
\omega^{0}
is a Néron exterior form on
A
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a local field of characteristic
0
,
X/\mathcal{K}
be a curve,
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{\mathcal{K}}(X)
and
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation for
X
. Fix bases
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
for
\Omega^{1}(\prod_{i}\Jac_{X/H_{i}})
,
\Omega^{1}(\prod_{j}\Jac_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}})
and write
\omega(\mathcal{B}_{1})
,
\omega(\mathcal{B}_{2})
for the exterior forms given by the wedge product of the elements in
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
respectively. We define

\lambda_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}}(X/\mathcal{K})=\frac{C(\prod_{i}\Jac_{X/H_{i}},\omega(\mathcal{B}_{1}))\ }{C(\prod_{j}\Jac_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}},\omega(\mathcal{B}_{2}))}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i}\mu({X/H_{i}})}{\prod_{j}\mu({X/H_{j}^{\prime}})}

where
C
is given in Notation
5.5
and
\mu
is as in Definition
5.4
.

Given a
G
-map
\Phi
realising
\Theta
, write
\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}
for the basis obtained by applying
(\Phi^{\vee})^{*}
to the elements of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
(cf. Notation
3.15
). We additionally define

\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/\mathcal{K})=\lambda_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}}(X/\mathcal{K}).

We write
f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})
for the map on
\mathcal{K}
-points induced by the isogeny
f_{\Phi}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

We have

\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/\mathcal{K})=\frac{\#\textup{coker}f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})}{\#\textup{ker}f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i}\mu({X/H_{i}})}{\prod_{j}\mu({X/H_{j}^{\prime}})}.

In particular,
\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/\mathcal{K})\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
.

Proof.

By applying
[
29
, Remark 4.29]
with
F=\Omega^{1}(-)
, and taking exterior powers, we deduce that
f_{\Phi}^{*}\omega(\mathcal{B}_{1})=\omega(\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1})
.
As in the proof of
[
33
, Theorem 7.3]
, we have

\frac{C(\prod_{i}\Jac_{X/H_{i}},\omega(\mathcal{B}_{1}))}{C(\prod_{j}\Jac_{X/{H_{j}^{\prime}}},\omega(\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{1}))}=\frac{\#\textup{coker}f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})}{\#\textup{ker}f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})},

giving the required result. ∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
X
be a curve over a number field
K
and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{K}(X)
. Let
\Theta
be a pseudo Brauer relation for
X
,
\Phi
a
G
-map realising
\Theta
and
p
a prime. Then,

\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X}))\equiv\sum_{v\textup{ place of }K}\ord_{p}\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2.

Proof.

For a
K
-isogeny
f:A\to B
, we write

\begin{split}Q(f)=\#\text{coker}(f:A(K)/A(K)_{\text{tors}}\to B(K)/B(K)_{\text{tors}})\cdot\#\text{ker}(f:\Sha(A)_{\text{div}}\to\Sha(B)_{\text{div}}),\end{split}

where
\Sha_{\textup{div}}
denotes the divisible part of
\Sha
. Thus, if
f
is a self-isogeny of
A
, then
\ord_{p}Q(f)=\ord_{p}\textup{det}(f|\mathcal{X}_{p}(A))
. For each place
v
of
K
, let
\mathcal{B}_{v}
be a basis for
\Omega^{1}(\prod_{i}\textup{Jac}_{X/{H_{i}}}/K_{v})
and write

\omega(\mathcal{B}_{v})
for the exterior form on
\prod_{i}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}}/K_{v}
obtained by taking the wedge product of elements in
\mathcal{B}_{v}
, and similarly for
\omega(\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{v})
. As in the proof of Lemma
5.7
,
\omega(\Phi^{\vee}\mathcal{B}_{v})=f_{\Phi}^{*}\omega(\mathcal{B}_{v})
and so

\displaystyle\frac{{Q(f_{\Phi})}}{{Q(f_{\Phi}^{\vee})}}

\displaystyle\overset{\textup{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{MR2680426}{}{}, Thm. 4.3]}}}{\equiv}

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{v}C_{v}(\prod_{i}\textup{Jac}_{X/{H_{i}}},\omega(\mathcal{B}_{v}))}{\prod_{v}C_{v}(\prod_{j}\textup{Jac}_{X/{H_{j}^{\prime}}},f_{\Phi}^{*}\omega(\mathcal{B}_{v}))}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i}\#\Sha_{0}(\Jac_{X/H_{i}})[2^{\infty}]}{\prod_{j}\#\Sha_{0}(\Jac_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}})[2^{\infty}]}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Etale_paper}{}{}, Prop. 5.14]}}}{\equiv}

\displaystyle\prod_{v}\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}

where
C_{v}(A,\omega)
denotes
C(A,\omega)
for
A/K_{v}
. On the other hand, by parts (2) and (3) of Theorem
4.3
, we have

\frac{{Q(f_{\Phi})}}{{Q(f_{\Phi}^{\vee})}}~\equiv~{{Q(f_{\Phi})}}{{Q(f_{\Phi}^{\vee})}}~=~Q(f_{\Phi}f_{\Phi}^{\vee})~=~Q(f_{\Phi}f_{\Phi^{\vee}})~=~Q(f_{\Phi^{\vee}\Phi})\quad\textup{mod }\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

Putting everything together, we see that

\displaystyle\ord_{p}\displaystyle\prod_{v}\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/K_{v})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\ord_{p}\det\big(f_{\Phi^{\vee}\Phi}\ |\ \mathcal{X}_{p}\big(\prod_{i}\Jac_{X/H_{i}}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\textup{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Etale_paper}{}{}, Lem. 4.28]}}}{\equiv}

\displaystyle\ord_{p}\det\big((\Phi^{\vee}\Phi)^{*}\ |\ \mathcal{X}_{p}\big(\prod_{i}\Jac_{X/H_{i}}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\textup{Cor. \ref{Regulator Constants and pullback basis}}}{\equiv}

\displaystyle\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X}))\quad\textup{mod }2

as claimed.
∎

5.3.
The local invariant
\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})

Assuming that
\Omega^{1}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
is a self-dual
\mathcal{K}[G]
-representation, we now define a revised version of the local invariant
\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}
.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a local field of characteristic
0
,
X/\mathcal{K}
be a curve,
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{\mathcal{K}}(X)
and
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation for
X
.
Fix bases
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
for
\Omega^{1}(\prod_{i}\Jac_{X/H_{i}})
,
\Omega^{1}(\prod_{j}\Jac_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}})
. Assuming that
\Omega^{1}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
is self-dual as a
\mathcal{K}[G]
-representation, we define

\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})=\frac{\lambda_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}}(X/\mathcal{K})}{\Big|\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))}\Big|_{\mathcal{K}}},

where
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}}
is the regulator constant evaluated with respect to
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
as in Definition
3.6
.

Remark 5.10
.

By Theorem
1.14
(7), any pseudo Brauer relation
\Theta
for
X
is also a pseudo Brauer relation for
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))
is well-defined.

Lemma 5.11
.

Suppose that
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
is a self-dual
\mathcal{K}[G]
-representation.

(1)

\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})
is independent of the bases
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
.

(2)

\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})
is independent of how
\Theta
is expressed as a formal linear combination of conjugacy classes of subgroups of
G
. That is, for all subgroups
H
of
G
,

\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})=\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta+(H-H)}(X/\mathcal{K}).

(3)

Given pseudo Brauer relations
\Theta_{1}
,
\Theta_{2}
for
X
,

\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}}(X/\mathcal{K})=\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta_{1}}(X/\mathcal{K})\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta_{2}}(X/\mathcal{K}).

(4)

For every
G
-map
\Phi
realising
\Theta
,

\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})=\frac{\#\textup{coker}f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})}{\#\textup{ker}f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i}\mu({X/H_{i}})}{\prod_{j}\mu({X/H_{j}^{\prime}})}\cdot\bigl|\sqrt{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi})}\bigr|_{\mathcal{K}}.

Proof.

(1) holds since, using Notation
3.19
,

\frac{\lambda_{\Theta}^{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}(X/\mathcal{K})}{\lambda_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}}(X/\mathcal{K})}=\frac{|\textup{det}\big([\textup{Id}]_{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}\big)|_{\mathcal{K}}}{|\textup{det}\big([\textup{Id}]_{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}^{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}\big)|_{\mathcal{K}}}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))}{\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))}=\frac{\textup{det}\big([\textup{Id}]_{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}^{{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}\big)^{2}}{\textup{det}\big([\textup{Id}]_{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}^{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\big)^{2}}.

For (2), given a basis
\mathcal{B}_{H}
for
\Omega^{1}(\textup{Jac}_{X/H})
, we write
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{B}_{H}
for the corresponding basis for
\Omega^{1}(\prod_{i}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}}\times\textup{Jac}_{X/H})
. Similarly, we write
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}_{2}\sqcup\mathcal{B}_{H}
. Evaluating
\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta+(H-H)}(X/\mathcal{K})
with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}^{\prime}
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})
with respect to
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
, gives the desired equality, which
holds for any choice of bases by (1). (3) follows in the same way as (2). (4) follows from part (1), Lemma
5.7
and Proposition
4.4
(1).
∎

Theorem 5.12
.

With the same set up as in Theorem
5.8
, and supposing that
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
is a self-dual
{K}[G]
-representation, we have

\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X}))\equiv\sum_{v\textup{ place of }K}\ord_{p}\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2\mathbb{Z},

where we extend

to
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
so that

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be any
G
-map realising
\Theta
.
By Lemma

\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})={\lambda_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/\mathcal{K})}\cdot{\left|\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi})\right|^{1/2}_{v}}
for all

\ord_{p}\prod_{v}\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})=\ord_{p}\prod_{v}{\lambda}_{\Theta,\Phi}(X/K_{v})\overset{\textup{Thm. \ref{Theorem: Local_Formula_dependent_on_differnetials}}}{\equiv}\ord_{p}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X}))\quad\textup{mod }2.\qed

Although
\tilde{\lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})
is independent of the underlying bases of differentials, it is difficult to work with in practice since it may not be rational.

Definition 5.16

Let

For the definitions of

Theorem 5.17
.

Let
X
be a curve over a local field
\mathcal{K}
of characteristic
0
, and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\textup{Aut}_{\mathcal{K}}(X)
. Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation for

\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
is a self-dual
\mathcal{K}[G]

(1)

\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})
is a rational number independent of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}.

(2)

{\Lambda}_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})

(3)

For any

\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})=\frac{\#\textup{coker}f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})}{\#\textup{ker}f_{\Phi}(\mathcal{K})}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i}\mu(X/{H_{i}})}{\prod_{j}\mu(X/{H_{j}^{\prime}})}\cdot\Bigl|\sqrt{\textup{deg}(f_{\Phi})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\textup{sf}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))}\Bigr|_{\mathcal{K}}.

(4)

If
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}
, then
\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\textup{sf}}(\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X}))
.

Proof.

Independence of the bases
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
follows from Lemma
5.11
(1). (2) follows from Lemma
5.11
(2), provided we show that

where

. Let
\Phi
be any

-map realising
\Theta

, where

and so they must be equal. (3) follows from Lemma

is trivial when

. It remains to show that

.

.

∎

(3). For (2), let

Let
\Phi
be any
G
-map realising
\Theta
. Then

6.
Parity conjecture for elliptic curves

Here we compare the local terms appearing in Example
1.9
to the corresponding root numbers. This gives a new proof of the parity conjecture for elliptic curves over number fields, assuming finiteness of Tate–Shafarevich groups.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
E:y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
with
a\neq 0
. Define the elliptic curve
E^{\prime}:y^{2}=x^{3}-27bx^{2}-27a^{3}x
. If
\Sha(E)
has finite
3
-primary part and
\Sha(E^{\prime})
has finite
2
- and
3
-primary parts, then the parity conjecture holds for
E
.

Remark 6.2
.

An elliptic curve

admits a
3
-isogeny and so, assuming that the
3
-primary part of
\Sha(E)
is finite, the parity conjecture is known to hold by
[
16
, Theorem 1.8]
.

The proof of Theorem
6.1
uses the theory of covers of curves developed in §§
4
-
5
, as opposed to the proof given in
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
which comes from studying elliptic curves over extensions of number fields. We adhere to the following notation.

Notation 6.3
.

Let

with

is

-Galois extension (c.f. Example

appearing in the statement of Theorem

, the existence of an isogeny involving

,

and

Theorem 6.4
.

Let

with

, let

be its discriminant curve and let

be its bihyperelliptic cover. Then,

where
\Theta=2C_{2}+C_{3}-2S_{3}-\{1\}

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
1.14
(4) and Theorem

, via an identical argument to that given in Example

∎

To deduce the

-parity conjecture for

it remains to suitably relate

to the product of local root numbers

Before proving this relationship, we note some immediate consequences. For the following result only, we allow for the possibility that

Let

be an elliptic curve over a number field

and

If

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

As above,
E^{\prime}=\textup{Jac}_{D}
. By Theorem

, the

.

Let

be the Brauer relation for

.

Notation 6.8
.

Let
\sigma
,
\tau\in S_{3}
have orders
3
and
2
respectively, with
K(E)=K(B)^{\langle\tau\rangle}
and
K(D)=K(B)^{\langle\sigma\rangle}
. Let
\Phi
denote the injective
S_{3}
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[S_{3}/\langle\tau\rangle]y_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[S_{3}/\langle\tau\rangle]y_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[S_{3}/\langle\sigma\rangle]y_{3}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}x_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}x_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}[S_{3}]x_{3}

given by
y_{1}\mapsto x_{1}+(1+\tau)x_{3}
,
y_{2}\mapsto x_{1}+x_{2}+(\sigma+\tau\sigma)x_{3}
and
y_{3}\mapsto x_{1}+x_{2}+(1+\sigma+\sigma^{2})x_{3}
.
By Theorem
4.3
, there are isogenies

f_{\Phi}:\Jac_{B}\to E\times E\times\Jac_{D}\quad\textup{ and }\quad f_{\Phi^{\vee}}=f_{\Phi}^{\vee}:E\times E\times\Jac_{D}\to\Jac_{B}.

From the explicit construction of these isogenies given in
[
29
, §4.3]

f_{\Phi}=(\pi_{E*},\pi_{E*}\circ\sigma_{*},\pi_{D*})\quad\textup{ and }\quad f_{\Phi}^{\vee}=\pi_{E}^{*}+\sigma^{*}\circ\pi_{E}^{*}+\pi_{D}^{*},

where
\pi_{E}:B\to E
,
\pi_{D}:B\to D
denote the quotient maps.

Lemma 6.9
.

We have
\deg f_{\Phi}=\deg f_{\Phi}^{\vee}=9
and

\ker(f_{\Phi})=\pi_{D}^{*}(\textup{Jac}_{D}[3])
. The first projection
E\times E\times\textup{Jac}_{B}\rightarrow E
induces an isomorphism of Galois modules
\ker(f_{\Phi}^{\vee})\simeq E[3]
.

Proof.

Let
P,Q\in E
and
R\in\Jac_{D}
. Then
(f_{\Phi}\circ f_{\Phi}^{\vee})(P,Q,R)=\big(2P-Q,2Q-P,3R\big)
. For the first entry, this follows using that
{\pi_{E}}_{*}\circ\pi_{E}^{*}=[2]
,
{\pi_{E}}_{*}\circ\sigma^{*}\circ\pi_{E}^{*}=[-1]
and
{\pi_{E}}_{*}\circ\pi_{D}^{*}=0
, and similarly for the second entry. For the third entry, we additionally use that
{\pi_{D}}_{*}\circ\pi_{D}^{*}=[3]
. We therefore deduce that

\ker(f_{\Phi}\circ f_{\Phi}^{\vee})=\{(P,-P,R):P\in E[3],\,R\in\Jac_{D}[3]\}

and

\deg f_{\Phi}=\deg f_{\Phi}^{\vee}=9
. Further, the identities above show that
\pi_{D}^{*}(\textup{Jac}_{D}[3])
is contained in
\ker(f_{\Phi}).
By
[
2
, Lemma 6]
, since
B\rightarrow D
has no non-trivial unramified subcover,
\pi_{D}^{*}:\textup{Jac}_{D}\rightarrow\textup{Jac}_{B}
is injective. Since
\deg f_{\Phi}=9
this gives
\ker(f_{\Phi})=\pi_{D}^{*}(\textup{Jac}_{D}[3])
. Finally, the description of
\ker(f_{\Phi}\circ f_{\Phi}^{\vee})
given above shows that the first projection induces a map
\alpha:\ker(f_{\Phi}^{\vee})\rightarrow E[3]
whose kernel is contained in
0\times 0\times\textup{Jac}_{D}[3]
. Since
\pi_{D}^{*}
is injective,
0\times 0\times\textup{Jac}_{D}[3]
and
\ker(f_{\Phi}^{\vee})
have trivial intersection. Thus
\alpha
is injective. Since
\deg(f_{\Phi}^{\vee})=9
,
\alpha
is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 6.10
.

The proof of Lemma
6.9
shows that
\pi_{D}^{*}
is injective. We conclude that
\ker(f_{\Phi})\simeq\textup{Jac}_{D}[3]
and
\ker(f_{\Phi}^{\vee})\simeq E[3]
as Galois modules. Since

is its own Cartier dual, we have an isomorphism of Galois modules
\textup{Jac}_{D}[3]\simeq E[3]
.

Remark 6.11
.

Since

, the ‘correction term’

Parity of ranks of Jacobians

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a cover of degree
n
over a number field
K
. Let
X_{Y}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be its
S_{n}
closure and
\Delta_{Y}\mskip-3.0mu=\mskip-3.0muX_{Y}/A_{n}
its associated discriminant curve.

(i) If the
p
-primary part of
\text{\tencyr Sh}(\Jac_{X_{Y}})
is finite for all primes
p\leq n
, then

\rk\Jac_{Y}+\rk\Jac_{\Delta_{Y}}\equiv\sum_{v\textup{ place of }K}\alpha(Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2.

(ii) If Conjecture
1.6
holds, then

w(\Jac_{Y})w(\Jac_{\Delta_{Y}})=\prod_{v\textup{ place of }K}(-1)^{\alpha(Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}/K_{v})}.

Proof.

Write
\rho
for the irreducible
n\mskip-3.0mu-\mskip-3.0mu1
dimensional character of
G
that is the natural permutation character on
n
points minus the trivial character.

(i) We have

\langle\Jac_{X_{Y}}(K),\mathds{1}\rangle=\rk\Jac_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}=0,

\langle\Jac_{X_{Y}}(K),\rho\rangle=\rk\Jac_{X_{Y}/S_{n-1}}=\rk\Jac_{Y},

\langle\Jac_{X_{Y}}(K),\det\rho\rangle=\rk\Jac_{X_{Y}/A_{n}}=\rk\Jac_{\Delta_{Y}}.

By Theorem
5.1
and the assumed finiteness of the
p_{i}^{(n)}
-primary part of
Sh
,

\langle\Jac_{X_{Y}}(K),\tau_{\Theta_{i}^{(n)},p_{i}^{(n)}}\rangle\equiv\sum_{v}\ord_{p_{i}^{(n)}}\Lambda_{\Theta_{i}^{(n)}}(Y/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2.

Taking the sum over
i
, we get

\rk\Jac_{\Delta_{Y}}+\rk\Jac_{Y}=\langle\Jac_{X_{Y}}(K),-n\mathds{1}+\rho+\det\rho\rangle\equiv\sum_{v}\alpha(Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2.

(ii)
The proof is analogous to (i). Using Proposition
2.21
we see that

w(X_{Y}^{\mathds{1}})\mskip-3.0mu=\mskip-3.0mu1,\qquad w(X_{Y}^{\rho})\mskip-3.0mu=\mskip-3.0muw(Y)\qquad\text{and}\qquad w(X_{Y}^{\det\rho})=w(\Delta_{Y}).

By Conjecture
1.6
,
w(X_{Y}^{\tau_{\Theta_{i}^{(n)},p_{i}^{(n)}}})=\prod_{v}(-1)^{\ord_{p_{i}^{(n)}}\Lambda_{\Theta_{i}^{(n)}}(Y/K_{v})}
. Taking the product over
i
gives the result.
∎

8.
On the parity conjecture for Jacobians

We end with a discussion on what one would need in order to prove the parity conjecture for general Jacobians, assuming finiteness of Tate–Shafarevich groups.
Recall from Corollary
7.14
that the supply of Brauer relations is sufficiently large as to control the parity of ranks of all Jacobians using the local invariants
\Lambda
.
What is required is the connection to local root numbers given by Conjecture
1.6
. Recall that this asserts that

\prod_{v\textup{ place of }K}w(X^{\tau_{\Theta,p}}/K_{v})(-1)^{\ord_{p}\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})}=1

for every curve
X
with a finite group
G
acting by automorphisms, every prime
p
and every Brauer relation
\Theta
for
X
.

As the following theorem illustrates, the local terms
w(X^{\tau_{\Theta,p}}/K_{v})
and
(-1)^{\ord_{p}\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})}
often match. Part (1) of the theorem is a special case of the root number formula given in Proposition
2.22
. We will not prove part (2) here; it will appear in forthcoming work
[
37
]
.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X
be a curve over a local field
\mathcal{K}
of characteristic
0
and
G
a finite group of
\mathcal{K}
-automorphisms of
X
, such that
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
is self-dual as a
G
-representation.
For an orthogonal representation
\tau
of
G
:

(1a)

w(X^{\tau}/\mathcal{K})=(-1)^{\langle\tau,\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})\rangle}
if
\mathcal{K}
is archimedean;

(1b)

w(X^{\tau}/\mathcal{K})=(-1)^{\langle\tau,(\mathfrak{X}_{\Jac_{X}}\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\Frob_{\mathcal{K}}}\rangle}
if
\mathcal{K}
is non-archimedean,
X/\mathcal{K}
is semistable.

For every odd prime
p
and pseudo Brauer relation
\Theta
for
X
:

(2a)

\ord_{p}\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})=\langle\tau_{\Theta,p},\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})\rangle
if
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}
;

(2b)

\ord_{p}\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})=\langle\tau_{\Theta,p},(\mathfrak{X}_{\Jac_{X}}\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\Frob_{\mathcal{K}}}\rangle
if
X/\mathcal{K}
is semistable and either
\mathcal{K}
non-archimedean of residue characteristic different from
p
or if
\mathcal{K}
is an extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of even degree.

Since
\tau_{\Theta,p}
is always orthogonal, under the assumptions of the theorem,

w(X^{\tau_{\Theta,p}}/\mathcal{K})=(-1)^{\ord_{p}\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/\mathcal{K})}.

In particular, Conjecture
1.6
holds for curves over number fields that satisfy these local constraints at all places. In view of Theorem
5.1
, we deduce the following case of the
p
-parity conjecture for
X^{\tau_{\Theta,p}}
(see Conjecture
2.23
):

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
X
be a curve over a number field
K
,
p
an odd prime and
G
a finite group of
K
-automorphisms of
X
such that
\Omega^{1}(\Jac_{X})
is self-dual as a
G
-representation.
Suppose moreover that
X
is semistable,
K
is totally complex and that
[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
is even for all
v\mid p
.
Then for all pseudo Brauer relations
\Theta
for
X

(-1)^{\langle\tau_{\Theta,p},\mathcal{X}_{p}(\Jac_{X})\rangle}=w(X^{\tau_{\Theta,p}}).

A more general version is proved in
[
37
]
, which removes some of the assumptions at
\ell=p
and allows real places. The local terms no longer agree in such generality. However, the discrepancy can be expressed in terms of a certain Artin symbol, which, when ones takes the product over all places of a number field, vanishes by the product formula. The case
p\mskip-3.0mu=\mskip-3.0mu2
is more difficult, although not without progress. In particular, the parity conjecture for hyperelliptic curves with suitably good local behaviour should be within reach (see
[
23
, §1.3.3]
).

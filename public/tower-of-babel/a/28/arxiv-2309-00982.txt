Densities for sets of natural numbers vanishing on a given family Thanks: The second author has been supported by the grant MNiD-539-5100-B348-19.
Rafał Filipów, Jacek Tryba

Abstract
. Abstract upper densities are monotone and subadditive functions from the power set of positive integers into the unit real interval that generalize the upper densities used in number theory, including the upper asymptotic density, the upper Banach density, and the upper logarithmic density. At the open problem session of the Workshop “Densities and their application”, held at St. Étienne in July 2013, G. Grekos asked a question whether there is a “nice” abstract upper density, whose the family of null sets is precisely a given ideal of subsets of ℕ \mathbb{N} , where “nice” would mean the properties of the familiar densities consider in number theory. In 2018, M. Di Nasso and R. Jin (Acta Arith. 185 (2018), no. 4) showed that the answer is positive for the summable ideals (for instance, the family of finite sets and the family of sequences whose series of reciprocals converge) when “nice” density means translation invariant and rich density (i.e. density which is onto the unit interval). In this paper we extend their result to all ideals with the Baire property.

1.
Abstract densities and ideals

Definition 1.1
.

An
abstract upper density
on
\mathbb{N}
is a function
\delta:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})\to[0,1]
that satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\delta(\mathbb{N})=1
,

(2)

if
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is finite then
\delta(F)=0
,

(3)

if
A\subseteq B
then
\delta(A)\leq\delta(B)
,

(4)

\delta(A\cup B)\leq\delta(A)+\delta(B)
.

Notation
.

For
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we write
A+k
to denote the set
(A+k)\cap\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 1.2
.

An abstract upper density
\delta
is

(1)

translation invariant
if
\delta(A+k)=\delta(A)
for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
;

(2)

rich
if for every
r\in[0,1]
there is
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with
\delta(A)=r
.

Definition 1.3
.

An
ideal on
\mathbb{N}
(in short
ideal
) is a family
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
that satisfies the following properties:

(1)

if
A,B\in\mathcal{I}
then
A\cup B\in\mathcal{I}
,

(2)

if
A\subseteq B
and
B\in\mathcal{I}
then
A\in\mathcal{I}
,

(3)

\mathcal{I}
contains all finite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
,

(4)

\mathbb{N}\notin\mathcal{I}
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is
translation invariant
if
A+k\in\mathcal{I}
for every
A\in\mathcal{I}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
,
we write
\mathcal{I}^{+}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A\notin\mathcal{I}\}
and call it the
coideal of
\mathcal{I}
,
and we write
\mathcal{I}^{*}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:\mathbb{N}\setminus A\in\mathcal{I}\}
and call it the
dual filter of
\mathcal{I}
.

Remark
.

We also consider ideals on any infinite countable set by identifying this set with
\mathbb{N}
via a fixed bijection.

Example 1.4
.

(1)

The family
\mathrm{Fin}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A\text{ is finite}\}
is a translation invariant ideal.

(2)

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)
be a non-increasing function such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n)=\infty
.
The family
\mathcal{I}_{f}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:\sum_{n\in A}f(n)<\infty\}

is a translation invariant ideal. We call it the
summable ideal
determined by
f
.

(3)

The family
\mathcal{I}_{d}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:\limsup_{n\to\infty}|A\cap\{1,\dots,n\}|/n=0\}
of all sets of asymptotic density zero
is a translation invariant ideal. We call it the
asymptotic density zero ideal
.

Example 1.5
.

(1)

Let
\delta
be an abstract upper density. The family

\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:\delta(A)=0\}
is an ideal.
If
\delta
is translation invariant, so is the ideal
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal.
The function
\delta:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})\to[0,1]
given by

\delta(A)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $A\in\mathcal{I}$},\\
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is an abstract upper density and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}
.
If
\mathcal{I}
is translation invariant, so is
\delta
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is
maximal
if
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
implies
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{J}
for every ideal
\mathcal{J}
(i.e.
\mathcal{I}
is a maximal element with respect to inclusion among all ideals).
It is known that an ideal
\mathcal{I}
is maximal
if and only if
A\in\mathcal{I}
or
\mathbb{N}\setminus A\in\mathcal{I}
for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}

(see e.g.
[
6
, Lemma 7.4, p. 74]
).

A translation invariant ideal
\mathcal{I}
is
T-maximal
if
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
implies
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{J}
for every translation invariant ideal
\mathcal{J}
(i.e.
\mathcal{I}
is a maximal element with respect to inclusion among all translation invariant ideals).

Remark
.

By identifying sets of natural numbers with their characteristic functions,
we equip
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
with the topology of the Cantor space
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
and therefore
we can assign topological complexity to ideals on
\mathbb{N}
.
In particular, an ideal
\mathcal{I}
has the Baire property if
\mathcal{I}
has the Baire property as a subset of the Cantor space. (Recall that a subset
B
of a topological space
X
has the
Baire property
if there is an open set
U\subseteq X
such that the set
(B\setminus U)\cup(U\setminus B)
is of first category.)

Theorem 1.6

(Sierpiński, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
.
Then
\mathcal{I}
is of first category or
\mathcal{I}
does not have the Baire property.

Theorem 1.7

(Talagrand
[
11
, Théorème 21]
(see also
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.2]
))
.

An ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\mathbb{N}
has the Baire property if and only if there is an increasing sequence
k_{1}<k_{2}<\dots
such that
if there are infinitely many
n
with
[k_{n},k_{n+1})\cap\mathbb{N}\subseteq A
, then
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.

Example 1.8
.

(1)

It is known that the ideals
\mathrm{Fin}
,
\mathcal{I}_{f}
and
\mathcal{I}_{d}
have the Baire property, in fact they are Borel ideals (see e.g.
[
4
]
).

(2)

Maximal ideals do not have the Baire property (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.1]
).

Theorem 1.9

(Plewik
[
10
, Theorem 1]
)
.

The intersection of a countable family of ideals without the Baire property is an ideal without the Baire property.
In particular, the intersection of countable family of maximal ideals is an ideal without the Baire property.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
. We say that a family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
is

(1)

\mathcal{I}
almost disjoint family

(in short
\mathcal{I}
-AD family
) if

(a)

\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{+}
,

(b)

A\cap B\in\mathcal{I}
for any distinct
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
.

(2)

\mathcal{I}
translation almost disjoint family

(in short
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family
) if

(a)

\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{+}
,

(b)

A\cap(B+k)\in\mathcal{I}
for any distinct
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.
Rich densities

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
.
If there exists an
\mathcal{I}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, then there is a rich abstract upper density
\delta
such that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Using Zorn’s lemma, it is not difficult to extend
\mathcal{I}
-AD family to a maximal (with respect to the inclusion)
\mathcal{I}
-AD family.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a maximal
\mathcal{I}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\}
and
(0,1)=\{r_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\}
.
We define
\delta:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})\to[0,1]
by

\delta(A)=\sup\{r_{\alpha}:A_{\alpha}\cap A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}\}

with the convention that
\sup\emptyset=0
.

First, we show that
\delta
is an abstract upper density.

For every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
we have
A_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{N}=A_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, so
\delta(\mathbb{N})=\sup(0,1)=1
.

For every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
and a finite set
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we have
A_{\alpha}\cap F\subseteq F\in\mathcal{I}
, so
\delta(F)=\sup\emptyset=0
.

If
A\subseteq B
, then
A_{\alpha}\cap A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
implies
A_{\alpha}\cap B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Thus
\delta(A)\leq\delta(B)
.

If
A_{\alpha}\cap(A\cup B)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, then

A_{\alpha}\cap A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}

or

A_{\alpha}\cap B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.
Thus

\delta(A\cup B)\leq\delta(A)+\delta(B)
.

Now we show that
\delta
is rich.

It is enough to show that
\delta(A_{\alpha})=r_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is an
\mathcal{I}
-AD family,
A_{\beta}\cap A_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}
for every
\alpha\neq\beta
. On the other hand,
A_{\alpha}\cap A_{\alpha}=A_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.
Thus
\delta(A_{\alpha})=\sup\{r_{\alpha}\}=r_{\alpha}
.

Finally, we show that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

If
A\in\mathcal{I}
, then
A_{\alpha}\cap A\subseteq A\in\mathcal{I}
for every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
. Thus
\delta(A)=\sup\emptyset=0
.

If
A\notin\mathcal{I}
, then, by maximality of
\mathcal{A}
, there is
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
with
A_{\alpha}\cap A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Thus
\delta(A)\geq r_{\alpha}>0
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
with the Baire property.
There is a rich abstract upper density
\delta
such that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Since it is known (see
[
5
]
or
[
7
]
) that there is an
\mathcal{I}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
for every ideal with the Baire property, Theorem
2.1
finishes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.3

(Oliver
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
.
We say that a partition
\{P_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
is an
\omega
-partition of
\mathbb{N}
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
if

(1)

P_{n}\not\in\mathcal{I}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, if
A\cap P_{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
A\in\mathcal{I}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

If there exists an
\omega
-partition
\{P_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
, then there is a rich abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\mathcal{I}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A\cap P_{n}\in\mathcal{I}\text{ for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\}

and

\delta(A)=\sum_{A\cap P_{n}\notin\mathcal{I}}\frac{1}{2^{n}}

is an abstract upper density with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

To see that
\delta
is rich it is enough to notice that for every
r\in(0,1)
there is
B\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with
\sum_{n\in B}1/2^{n}=r
and then
\delta(\bigcup\{P_{n}:n\in B\})=r
.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

There exists an ideal
\mathcal{I}
for which there is a rich abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
, but there is no
\mathcal{I}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Let
\{P_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a partition of
\mathbb{N}
such that
P_{n}
is infinite for all
n
.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{n}
be a maximal ideal on
P_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It is easy to see that

\mathcal{I}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A\cap P_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\text{ for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\}

is an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
.
Since
\{P_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an
\omega
-partition of
\mathbb{N}
with respect to
\mathcal{I}
, there is a rich abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
(by Theorem
2.4
).

Lastly, we show that there is no
\mathcal{I}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
(in fact we show that there is no uncountable
\mathcal{I}
-AD family).
Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{+}
be uncountable. For every
A\in\mathcal{A}
there is at least one
n_{A}\in\mathbb{N}
with
A\cap P_{n_{A}}\in\mathcal{I}_{n_{A}}^{+}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is uncountable, there are distinct
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
with
n_{A}=n_{B}
.
Let
n=n_{A}
.
Since
\mathcal{I}_{n}
is a maximal ideal on
P_{n}
,

\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}=\mathcal{I}_{n}^{*}
.
Thus,
(A\cap P_{n})\cap(B\cap P_{n})\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{*}
, so
(A\cap B)\cap P_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}^{*}=\mathcal{I}_{n}^{+}
.
Hence
A\cap B\notin\mathcal{I}
, so
\mathcal{A}
is not an
\mathcal{I}
-AD family.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

There exists an ideal
\mathcal{I}
without the Baire property for which there is
\mathcal{I}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Let
\{P_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a partition of
\mathbb{N}
such that
P_{n}
is infinite for all
n
.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{n}
be a maximal ideal on
P_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It is easy to see that

\mathcal{I}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A\cap P_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\text{ for all but finitely many $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\}

is an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be
\mathrm{Fin}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
(there is one, see e.g.
[
6
, Lemma 9.21]
).
For
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we define
C_{A}=\bigcup\{P_{n}:n\in A\}
.
Then
C_{A}\notin\mathcal{I}
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
, and for distinct
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
we have
C_{A}\cap C_{B}=\bigcup\{P_{n}:n\in A\cap B\}\in\mathcal{I}
.
Thus,
\{C_{A}:A\in\mathcal{A}\}
is
\mathcal{I}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.

Lastly, we show that
\mathcal{I}
does not have the Baire property.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define
\mathcal{J}_{n}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A\cap P_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{J}_{n}
is a maximal ideal on
\mathbb{N}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\mathcal{I}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcap_{k\geq n}\mathcal{J}_{k}.

By Theorem
1.9
,
the ideal
\bigcap_{k\geq n}\mathcal{J}_{k}
does not have the Baire property for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular,
\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{J}_{k}
does not have the Baire property, so it is of second category. Thus,
\mathcal{I}
is of second category as a superset of
\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{J}_{k}
.
Then, by Theorem
1.6
,
\mathcal{I}
does not have the Baire property.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a maximal ideal.
If
\delta
is an abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
then
\delta
takes only two values: zero and one. In particular, there is no rich abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
.
If
A\in\mathcal{I}
then
\delta(A)=0
.
If
A\notin\mathcal{I}
, then
\mathbb{N}\setminus A\in\mathcal{I}
, hence

1=\delta(\mathbb{N})\leq\delta(A)+\delta(\mathbb{N}\setminus A)=\delta(A)+0=\delta(A)\leq 1
, thus
\delta(A)=1
.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

There exists a non-maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}

for which there is no rich abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Let
N\geq 2
.
Let
\{P_{n}:n\leq N\}
be a partition of
\mathbb{N}
such that
P_{n}
is infinite for all
n\leq N
.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{n}
be a maximal ideal on
P_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It is easy to see that

\mathcal{I}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A\cap P_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\text{ for every $n\leq N$}\}

is an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
. Since both
P_{1}\notin\mathcal{I}
and
\mathbb{N}\setminus P_{1}\notin\mathcal{I}
,
\mathcal{I}
is not a maximal ideal.

Let
\delta
be any abstract upper density such that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Let
n\leq N
and
A\subseteq P_{n}
.
If
A\in\mathcal{I}_{n}
then
\delta(A)=0
.
If
A\notin\mathcal{I}_{n}
, then
P_{n}\setminus A\in\mathcal{I}_{n}
, hence

\delta(P_{n})\leq\delta(A)+\delta(P_{n}\setminus A)=\delta(A)+0=\delta(A)\leq\delta(P_{n})
, hence
\delta(A)=\delta(P_{n})
.

Let
A\notin\mathcal{I}
. There is at least one
n\leq N
with
A\cap P_{n}\notin\mathcal{I}_{n}
, so
\delta(A)\geq\delta(A\cap P_{n})=\delta(P_{n})
.

Thus,
\delta(A)\geq\min\{\delta(P_{n}):n\leq N\}>0
for every
A\notin\mathcal{I}
, hence
\delta
is not rich.
∎

3.
Rich and translation invariant densities

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a translation invariant ideal on
\mathbb{N}
.
If there exists an
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, then there is a translation invariant and rich abstract upper density
\delta
such that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Using Zorn’s lemma, it is not difficult to extend
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family to a maximal (with respect to the inclusion)
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a maximal
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\}
and
(0,1)=\{r_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\}
.
We define
\delta:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})\to[0,1]
by

\delta(A)=\sup\{r_{\alpha}:A_{\alpha}\cap(A+k)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}\text{ for some $k\in\mathbb{Z}$}\}

with the convention that
\sup\emptyset=0
.

First, we show that
\delta
is an abstract upper density.

For every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
we have
A_{\alpha}\cap(\mathbb{N}+0)=A_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, so
\delta(\mathbb{N})=\sup(0,1)=1
.

For every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and a finite set
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we have
A_{\alpha}\cap(F+k)\subseteq F+k\in\mathcal{I}
, so
\delta(F)=\sup\emptyset=0
.

If
A\subseteq B
, then
A_{\alpha}\cap(A+k)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
implies
A_{\alpha}\cap(B+k)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Thus
\delta(A)\leq\delta(B)
.

If
A_{\alpha}\cap((A\cup B)+k)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, then

A_{\alpha}\cap(A+k)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}

or

A_{\alpha}\cap(B+k)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.
Thus

\delta(A\cup B)\leq\delta(A)+\delta(B)
.

Second, we show that
\delta
is translation invariant.

If
A_{\alpha}\cap((A+m)+k)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
then

A_{\alpha}\cap(A+(m+k))\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
and vice versa. Thus
\delta(A+m)=\delta(A)
.

Now we show that
\delta
is rich.

It is enough to show that
\delta(A_{\alpha})=r_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is an
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family,
A_{\beta}\cap(A_{\alpha}+k)\in\mathcal{I}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha\neq\beta
. On the other hand,
A_{\alpha}\cap(A_{\alpha}+0)=A_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.
Thus
\delta(A_{\alpha})=\sup\{r_{\alpha}\}=r_{\alpha}
.

Finally, we show that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

If
A\in\mathcal{I}
, then
A_{\alpha}\cap(A+k)\subseteq A+k\in\mathcal{I}
for every
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus
\delta(A)=\sup\emptyset=0
.

If
A\notin\mathcal{I}
, then, by maximality of
\mathcal{A}
, there is
\alpha<\mathfrak{c}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
A_{\alpha}\cap(A+k)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
. Thus
\delta(A)\geq r_{\alpha}>0
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
with the Baire property.
There is an
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.7
there is
an increasing sequence
k_{1}<k_{2}<\dots
such that
if there are infinitely many
n
with
[k_{n},k_{n+1})\cap\mathbb{N}\subseteq A
, then
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.
Without loss of generality we can assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}(k_{n+1}-k_{n})=\infty
.

Let us denote
I_{n}=[k_{n},k_{n+1})\cap\mathbb{N}
and
|I_{n}|=k_{n+1}-k_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
be a
\mathrm{Fin}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.
For every
A\in\mathcal{A}
we define

C_{A}=\bigcup_{n\in A}I_{2n}.

We claim that
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{A}:A\in\mathcal{A}\}
is the required family.

First, we show that
C_{A}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

If
A\in\mathcal{A}
, then
A
is infinite, so
C_{A}
contains infinitely many intervals
[k_{n},k_{n+1})\cap\mathbb{N}
, thus
C_{A}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.

Second, we show that
\mathcal{C}
is of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.

If
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
are distinct, then there is
n\in(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)
, so
I_{2n}\subseteq(C_{A}\setminus C_{B})\cup(C_{B}\setminus C_{A})
.
Thus
|\mathcal{C}|=|\mathcal{A}|=\mathfrak{c}
.

Finally, we show that
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathcal{I}
-TAD.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
be distinct and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We have to show that
C_{A}\cap(C_{B}+k)\in\mathcal{I}
.

Let
N
be such that
|I_{n}|>|k|
for every
n>N
.
Then
I_{2n}+k\subseteq I_{2n-1}\cup I_{2n}\cup I_{2n+1}
for every
n>N
.
Hence
(I_{2n}+k)\cap I_{2m}=\emptyset
for any
n>N
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\neq m
.

Since

C_{A}\cap(C_{B}+k)=\bigcup_{m\in A}I_{2m}\cap\left(\bigcup_{n\in B}I_{2n}+k\right)=\bigcup_{m\in A}\bigcup_{n\in B}(I_{2m}\cap(I_{2n}+k))

and
A\cap B
is finite, we obtain that
C_{A}\cap(C_{B}+k)
is contained in finitely many intervals
I_{2m}
. Thus
C_{A}\cap(C_{B}+k)\in\mathrm{Fin}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
.
∎

.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a translation invariant ideal on

with the Baire property.
There is a translation invariant and rich abstract upper density
\delta
such that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Follows from Theorems
3.1
and

.

(Di Nasso and Jin
[

)
.

Let

It is not difficult to check that summable ideals are translation invariant, and it is known that summable ideals have the Baire property (see e.g.

Question 1
.

Does there exist a translation invariant ideal
\mathcal{I}
without the Baire property such that
there is an
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
?

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
. We say that a sequence of reals
\langle x_{n}\rangle
is
\mathcal{I}
-convergent to
L
if

\{n\in\mathbb{N}:|x_{n}-L|\geq\varepsilon\}\in\mathcal{I}

for every
\varepsilon>0
. The number
L
is called
an
\mathcal{I}
-limit of
\langle x_{n}\rangle

and we denote it by
\lim^{\mathcal{I}}x_{n}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

There exists a translation invariant ideal
\mathcal{I}
for which there is a rich translation invariant abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
, but there is no
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a maximal ideal on
\mathbb{N}
.
It is known (see e.g.
[
2
, p. 3314]
)
that

\delta(A)={\lim}^{\mathcal{J}}\frac{|A\cap\{1,\dots,n\}|}{n}

is a translation invariant finitely additive measure (in particular,
\delta
is a translation invariant abstract upper density).

Moreover,
\delta
is rich. Indeed, it is enough to observe (see e.g.
[
8
, p. 216]
) that for every
r\in[0,1]
there is a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that the asymptotic density of
A
equals
r
i.e.
r=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\{1,\dots,n\}|}{n}
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}
. Since
\delta
is a translation invariant abstract upper density,
\mathcal{I}
is a translation invariant ideal.
To finish the proof we have to show that there is no
\mathcal{I}
-AD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
(in fact we show that there is no uncountable
\mathcal{I}
-AD family).
Suppose
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{+}
is an uncountable
\mathcal{I}
-AD family.
For every
A\in\mathcal{A}
, let
n_{A}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\delta(A)\geq 1/n_{A}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is uncountable, there are infinitely many sets
A_{1},A_{2},\dots\in\mathcal{A}
with
n_{A_{i}}=n_{A_{j}}
for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
n=n_{A_{1}}
.
Since
A_{j}\cap A_{j}\in\mathcal{I}
for distinct
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta(A_{i}\cap A_{j})=0
.
Since
\delta
is a finitely additive measure,

\delta(A_{i}\cup A_{j})=\delta(A_{i})+\delta(A_{j})-\delta(A_{i}\cap A_{j})=\delta(A_{i})+\delta(A_{j})
for distinct
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Finally,

1\geq\delta(A_{1}\cup\dots\cup A_{n+1})=\delta(A_{1})+\dots+\delta(A_{n+1})\geq(n+1)/n>1
, a contradiction.
∎

We do not know if there is any translation invariant ideal such that there is no rich translation invariant abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
.

Question 2
.

Does there exist a translation invariant ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
there is no rich translation invariant abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
?

Keeping Theorem
2.7
in mind, it is natural to examine T-maximal ideals in the quest for a solution of the above question.

Question 3
.

Does there exist a T-maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
such there is a rich translation invariant abstract upper density
\delta
with
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=\mathcal{I}
?

In Theorems
3.7
and
3.11
,
we show that if the above question had positive answer, then the required density would be different from densities constructed in the proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
3.6
.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
\mathcal{I}
is a T-maximal ideal, then for any
A,B\notin\mathcal{I}
there is
k
with
(A+k)\cap B\notin\mathcal{I}
.
In particular, there is no
\mathcal{I}
-TAD family of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Let
A,B\notin\mathcal{I}
. Since
\mathcal{I}
is maximal among all translation invariant ideals, there are
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(A+k_{1})\cup\dots\cup(A+k_{n})\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
.
Thus,
B\cap[(A+k_{1})\cup\dots\cup(A+k_{n})]\notin\mathcal{I}
.
Hence, there is
i\leq n
with
B\cap(A+k_{i})\notin\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\mathbb{N}
.

(1)

The family

t(\mathcal{I})=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A+k\in\mathcal{I}\text{ for every $k\in\mathbb{Z}$}\}

is a translation invariant ideal.

(2)

If
\mathcal{I}
does not have the Baire property then
t(\mathcal{I})
does not have the Baire property.

Proof.

(1)
Straightforward.
(
2
)
It follows from Theorem
1.9
, because

\mathcal{I}+k=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:A-k\in\mathcal{I}\}

is the ideal without the Baire property for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}

and
t(\mathcal{I})=\bigcap\{\mathcal{I}+k:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\delta
be defined as in the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
Then
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}\neq t(\mathcal{I})
for any maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=t(\mathcal{I})
for some maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
. Since
t(\mathcal{I})\subseteq\mathcal{I}
and one could easily notice that
\mathcal{I}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}
, we get
\mathcal{I}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
.
Let
A=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{2n}
, where
I_{n}=(2^{n},2^{n+1}]\cap\mathbb{N}
. Then
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\{1,\dots,n\}|}{n}=\frac{2}{3}
and
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\{1,\dots,n\}|}{n}=\frac{1}{3}
as
\frac{|I_{2n}|}{|I_{2n-1}\cup I_{2n}|}=\frac{2}{3}
and
\frac{|I_{2n}|}{|I_{2n}\cup I_{2n+1}|}=\frac{1}{3}
.
Therefore,
\delta(A)\in\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\right]
, thus
A\not\in\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}
.

Now, since
\mathcal{I}
is a maximal ideal, either
A\in\mathcal{I}
or
\mathbb{N}\setminus A\in\mathcal{I}
. Assume that
A\in\mathcal{I}
(the other case is identical since
\mathbb{N}\setminus A\not\in\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}
too). By the supposition that
\mathcal{Z}_{\delta}=t(\mathcal{I})
, we know that
A\not\in t(\mathcal{I})
, so there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
A+k\not\in\mathcal{I}
. It follows that
B=A+k\setminus A\not\in\mathcal{I}
. However, for all but finitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\frac{|B\cap I_{n}|}{|I_{n}|}\leq\frac{k}{2^{n}},

which tends to
0
as
n
tends to infinity. Thus,
B\in\mathcal{I}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
, a contradiction with
B\not\in\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{J}
is a T-maximal ideal then
\mathcal{J}=t(\mathcal{I})
for any maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}\supseteq\mathcal{J}
.

(2)

There is a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
t(\mathcal{I})
is not a T-maximal ideal.

(3)

Any T-maximal ideal does not have the Baire property.

Proof.

(1)
(
\subseteq
)
If
A\in\mathcal{J}
, then
A+k\in\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,
A\in t(\mathcal{I})
.

(
\supseteq
)
Since
\mathcal{J}\subseteq t(\mathcal{I})
,
t(\mathcal{I})
is translation invariant and
\mathcal{J}
is maximal among all translation invariant ideals,
\mathcal{J}=t(\mathcal{I})
.

(2)
Let
B=\{b_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be such that the sequence
\langle b_{n+1}-b_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\rangle
is increasing and diverging to infinity.
Let
\mathcal{I}
be a maximal ideal with
\mathbb{N}\setminus B\in\mathcal{I}
.
If we show that
t(\mathcal{I})\cup\{B\}
can be extended to a translation invariant ideal,

t(\mathcal{I})
will not be a T-maximal ideal.

To prove that
t(\mathcal{I})\cup\{B\}
can be extended to a translation invariant ideal, we have to show that

\mathbb{N}\setminus[(B+k_{1})\cup\dots\cup(B+k_{n})]\notin t(\mathcal{I})

for any
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{-k_{1},\dots,-k_{n}\}
.
Since
|(B+l)\cap B|\leq 1
for every
l\neq 0
,

\left([(B+k_{1})\cup\dots\cup(B+k_{n})]+k\right)\cap B
is finite.
Since
\mathbb{N}\setminus B\in\mathcal{I}
,

[(B+k_{1})\cup\dots\cup(B+k_{n})]+k\in\mathcal{I}
.
Then

\mathbb{N}\setminus([(B+k_{1})\cup\dots\cup(B+k_{n})]+k)\notin\mathcal{I}
,
and consequently

(\mathbb{N}\setminus[(B+k_{1})\cup\dots\cup(B+k_{n})])+k\notin\mathcal{I}
which means that

\mathbb{N}\setminus[(B+k_{1})\cup\dots\cup(B+k_{n})]\notin t(\mathcal{I})
.

(3)
Follows from (1) and Proposition
3.8
(
2
).
∎

Gapped boundaries of fermionic topological orders and higher central charges
Minyoung You

Abstract
We develop a test for the vanishing of higher central charges of a fermionic topological order, which is a necessary condition for the existence of a gapped boundary, purely in terms of the modular data of the super-modular tensor category. More precisely, we test whether a given super-MTC has c = 0 c=0 mod 1 2 \frac{1}{2} , and, if so, whether the modular extension with c = 0 c=0 mod 8 8 has vanishing higher central charges. The test itself does not require an explicit computation of the modular extensions and is easily carried out. We apply this test to known examples of super-modular tensor categories. Since our test allows us to obtain information about the chiral central charge of a super-modular tensor category in terms of its modular data without direct knowledge of its modular extensions, this can also be thought of as the first step towards a fermionic analogue of the Gauss-Milgram formula.

VI
Discussion

In this article, we have developed a method which tests whether a given super-MTC
\mathcal{B}

(1) has chiral central charge
c=0
mod
\frac{1}{2}
and (2) if so, whether the
c=0
modular extension
(\breve{\mathcal{B}},0)
has vanishing higher central charges. The test only makes use of the modular data
(\hat{S},\hat{T}^{2})
of
\mathcal{B}
as input. This gives a set of obstructions to the existence of a gapped boundary for a super-MTC
\mathcal{B}
which can be efficiently computed, and we apply this to known examples of super-MTCs to rule out gapped boundaries for many of them.

In addition to providing necessary conditions for the existence of a gapped boundary, our results are significant because it represents a step toward computing the chiral central charge
c
of a super-MTC
\mathcal{B}
in terms of its modular data. Thus far, the the computation of
c
mod
\frac{1}{2}
for a super-MTC relied on finding the modular extensions, which is a highly nontrivial process
Lan et al. 2016a
(see also Ref.
Cano et al. 2014
for a discussion of the abelian case). Ref.
Kobayashi 2022
has developed a Gauss-Milgram-like formula giving obstructions to the existence of a gapped boundary of fermionic topoloigcal orders with
U(1)_{f}
-symmetry, but a formula for the general case is lacking. The first part of our test, on the other hand, tests if a given
\mathcal{B}
has
c=0
mod
\frac{1}{2}
using congruence representation theory without any extra assumptions on
\mathcal{B}
and without needing to compute the modular extensions. While this only tests if
c=0
mod
\frac{1}{2}
and does not in general determine
c
mod
\frac{1}{2}
itself (it is not straightforward to use the congruence representation relations in general, because the relations depend on the level
N
and
N
in turns depends on
c
), we believe that further investigation of the matter from the direction of congruence representation could lead to an algorithm which computes
c
(and possibly all higher central charges as well) in terms of the modular data of
\mathcal{B}.

Appendix A
Gapped boundaries of spin-TQFTs and gapped boundaries of modular extensions

Recall that a spin-MTC
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
(which can be thought of as a modular extension of a super-MTC
\mathcal{B}
) gives rise to a
2+1
d spin-TQFT
{\mathcal{T}}_{f}(\breve{\mathcal{B}})

Bhardwaj et al. 2017
;
Delmastro et al. 2021
.
In this appendix, we show that, on the level of the torus, a gapped boundary for
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
gives rise to a gapped boundary for
{\mathcal{T}}_{f}
and vice versa.

Consider a spin-MTC
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
which has a Lagrangian algebra
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{a}Z_{a}a
,
a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}
(for convenience, we will abuse notation and write
a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}
, which is understood to mean that
a
belongs to the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
). The Lagrangian algebra has dimension
\dim\mathcal{L}=\sum_{a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}Z_{a}d_{a}=D
where
D=\sqrt{\sum_{a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}d_{a}^{2}}
is the total quantum dimension of
\breve{\mathcal{B}}.

Following Ref.
Kobayashi 2022
, we can think of the boundary condition on a torus as a state in the torus Hilbert space, by placing the
2+1
d TQFT on
T^{2}\times[0,1]
with the gapped boundary condition
\mathcal{L}
on
T^{2}\times\{1\}
; then the state on the Hilbert space at
T^{2}\times\{0\}
is given by
|\mathcal{L}\rangle=\sum\limits_{a}Z_{a}|a\rangle
(up to normalization) where
|a\rangle
are basis states on the torus.

Let us first derive some properties of
\mathcal{L}
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is a spin-MTC, it has a distinguished fermion
f
. We can decompose the set of simple objects of
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
into
\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm NS}\oplus\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm R}
, where
a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm NS}
if
a
has trivial mutual braiding with
f
,
S_{af}=+\frac{d_{a}}{D}
, and
a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm R}
if
a
has nontrivial mutual braiding with
f
,
S_{af}=-\frac{d_{a}}{D}.
Likewise, we can decompose
\mathcal{L}
into
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}^{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}^{1}
, where each condensing boson belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}
(
\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}
) if it braids trivially (nontrivially) with
f.

Since the boundary is topological, it is invariant under modular transformations; this leads to
Z_{a}
being invariant under
S
- and
T
-transformations
Ji and Wen 2019
;
Kaidi et al. 2022
. Invariance under
T
simply means the anyons
a
for which
Z_{a}
are nonzero are all bosons. Invariance under
S
,
\sum_{b}S_{ab}Z_{b}=Z_{a}
, puts additional constraints. Let us specialize to
a=f
, the simple object representing the fundamental fermion. Then we see

\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{fb}Z_{b}=\frac{1}{D}\left(\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm NS}}d_{b}Z_{b}-\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm R}}d_{b}Z_{b}\right)=Z_{f}=0

(29)

since
f
, as a fermion, does not belong to the Lagrangian algebra (enters with coefficient
Z_{f}=0
). This leads to

\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm NS}}d_{b}Z_{b}=\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm R}}d_{b}Z_{b}

(30)

which means that
\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}^{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}^{0}
have the same quantum dimensions; each has
\frac{1}{2}D
.

We can then define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}^{f}=f\times\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}^{1}

(31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}^{f}=f\times\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}^{1}

(32)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}^{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}^{f}

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}^{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}^{f}.

(34)

This gives us the NS and R sector Lagrangian algebras of Ref.
Kobayashi 2022
, but generalized to the case where
\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm R}
possibly contains “q-type” anyons which absorb the fermion,
q\times f=q.
Note that
\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}^{f}
is always distinct from
\mathcal{L}_{\rm NS}^{1}
, as none of the anyons in
\mathcal{B}_{\rm NS}
absorb
f
. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}^{1}
contains a
q
-type anyon, it will also appear in
\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}^{f}
; in such cases, it will appear in
\mathcal{L}_{\rm R}
with multiplicity.

To consider the spin-TQFT, we change the basis into the “sector basis,” as in Eq. (
13
). This amounts to considering the states

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{\text{NS-NS}}\rangle=\sum_{a}(Z_{\rm NS,a}^{1}+Z_{\rm NS,a}^{f})|a\rangle

(35)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{\text{NS-R}}\rangle=\sum_{a}(Z_{\rm NS,a}^{1}-Z_{\rm NS,a}^{f})|a\rangle

(36)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{\text{R-NS}}\rangle=\sum_{a}(Z_{\rm R,a}^{1}+Z_{\rm R,a}^{f})|a\rangle

(37)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{\text{R-R}}\rangle=\sum_{a}(Z_{\rm R,a}^{1}-Z_{\rm R,a}^{f})|a\rangle.

(38)

The R-R sector of the
2+1d
spin-TQFT also has puncture states
|q;f\rangle
for each
q
which absorbs a fermion
Delmastro et al. 2021
. However, to describe a gapped boundary of a
2+1
d spin-TQFT on the torus, i.e. a
1+1
d spin-TQFT, it is sufficient to give its partition functions (more precisely, these are partition functions on tori with specified spin structures and with “background field” specified by the anyon
a

Ji and Wen 2019

\displaystyle Z_{\text{NS-NS},a}=Z_{\rm NS,a}^{1}+Z_{\rm NS,a}^{f}

(39)

\displaystyle Z_{\text{NS-R},a}=Z_{\rm NS,a}^{1}-Z_{\rm NS,a}^{f}

(40)

\displaystyle Z_{\text{R-NS},a}=Z_{\rm R,a}^{1}+Z_{\rm R,a}^{f}

(41)

\displaystyle Z_{\text{R-R},a}=Z_{\rm R,a}^{1}-Z_{\rm R,a}^{f}.

(42)

How do these transform under modular transformations? Using the invariance of
Z_{a}=Z_{{\rm NS},a}^{1}+Z_{{\rm R},a}^{1}
under
S
, together with the following facts:
Z_{{\rm NS},a}^{1}=0
for
a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm R}
and
Z_{{\rm R},a}^{1}=0
for

;
S_{a,b\times f}=\epsilon_{a}S_{ab}
where
\epsilon_{a}=+1
if
a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm NS}
and
-1
if
a\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}_{\rm R}

Z_{{\rm NS},a}^{f}=Z_{{\rm NS},a\times f}^{1}
and
Z_{{\rm R},a}^{f}=Z_{{\rm R},a\times f}^{1}
, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{ab}\left(Z_{{\rm NS},b}^{1}+Z_{{\rm R},b}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{{\rm NS},a}^{1}+Z_{{\rm R},a}^{1}

(43)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{a\times f,b}\left(Z_{{\rm NS},b}^{1}+Z_{{\rm R},b}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{ab}\left(Z_{{\rm NS},b}^{1}-Z_{{\rm R},b}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{{\rm NS},a}^{f}+Z_{{\rm R},a}^{f}

(44)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{a,b\times f}\left(Z_{{\rm NS},b}^{1}+Z_{{\rm R},b}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{ab}\left(Z_{{\rm NS},b}^{f}+Z_{{\rm R},b}^{f}\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{{\rm NS},a}^{1}-Z_{{\rm R},a}^{1}

(45)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{a\times f,b\times f}\left(Z_{{\rm NS},b}^{1}+Z_{{\rm R},b}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{ab}\left(Z_{{\rm NS},b}^{f}-Z_{{\rm R},b}^{f}\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{{\rm NS},a}^{f}-Z_{{\rm R},a}^{f}

(46)

From this we can see that

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{ab}Z_{\text{NS-NS},b}=Z_{\text{NS-NS},a}

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{ab}Z_{\text{NS-R},b}=Z_{\text{R-NS},a}

(48)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{ab}Z_{\text{R-NS},b}=Z_{\text{NS-R},a}

(49)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}S_{ab}Z_{\text{R-R},b}=Z_{\text{R-R},a}.

(50)

Moreover, from the fact that
Z_{\text{NS},a}^{f}
is nonzero only for
a
such that
\theta_{a}=-1
, while
Z_{\text{NS},a}^{1}
,
Z_{\text{R},a}^{1}
and
Z_{\text{R},a}^{f}
nonzero only for

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}T_{ab}Z_{\text{NS-NS},b}=Z_{\text{NS-R},a}

(51)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}T_{ab}Z_{\text{NS-R},b}=Z_{\text{NS-NS},a}

(52)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}T_{ab}Z_{\text{R-NS},b}=Z_{\text{R-NS},a}

(53)

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\breve{\mathcal{B}}}T_{ab}Z_{\text{R-R},b}=Z_{\text{R-R},a}.

(54)

•

\Gamma^{0}(2)=\langle tst,t^{2}\rangle

•

•

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
itself.

Note that invariance under

Appendix B
Higher central charges for super-MTC

Going beyond the
c=0

\xi_{n}
for odd
n
are well-defined up to factors of
e^{2\pi i/16}
for super-MTCs (similar to how the chiral central charge
c
is well-defined modulo
\frac{1}{2}
). Note that the presence of a fermion with
\theta_{f}=-1
means that
N_{FS}
is always even for a super-MTC; hence any
n
coprime to
N_{FS}
is odd, and restricting to odd
n
loses no value as far as the relevance to gapped boundaries is concerned.

First, we note that if
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
is a modular extension of a super-MTC
\mathcal{B}
, other modular extensions of
\mathcal{B}
can be constructed by stacking
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
with
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
, one of the Kitaev 16-fold way phases
Kitaev 2006

(f,\psi)
where

is the distinguished fermion of
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
and
\psi
is the distinguished fermion of
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}.

We denote the resulting theory as
\left(\breve{\mathcal{B}}\boxtimes\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\right)_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}.

Higher central charges simply multiply under stacking. If we are given two MTCs
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle(\xi_{n})_{\mathcal{B}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}}=\frac{\sum\limits_{b\in\mathcal{D}}\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{B}}(d_{a}d_{b})^{2}(\theta_{a}\theta_{b})^{n}}{|\sum\limits_{b\in\mathcal{D}}\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{B}}(d_{a}d_{b})^{2}(\theta_{a}\theta_{b})^{n}|}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sum_{a}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}}{|\sum_{a}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}|}\right)\left(\frac{\sum_{b}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{b}^{n}}{|\sum_{b}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{b}^{n}|}\right)=(\xi_{n})_{\mathcal{B}}(\xi_{n})_{\mathcal{D}}.

(57)

This means that, under stacking with
\mathcal{S}
, the higher central charges
(\xi_{n})_{\breve{\mathcal{B}}}
of
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
change by the higher central charges
(\xi_{n})_{\mathcal{S}_{\nu}}
of
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
.
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
are well-known, and we can easily compute their higher central charges. We do not list them explicitly, but simply note that they are all 16th roots of unity.

Now, we show that under condensation of a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
boson,
\xi_{n}
remains invariant for odd
n.
Suppose an MTC
\mathcal{C}
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-boson
e
(i.e. a boson such that
e\times e=1
and
\theta_{e}=1)
). Then, every anyon
a\in\mathcal{C}
has mutual braiding phase
\pm 1
with
e
, and we can divide
\mathcal{C}
in to two sectors
\mathcal{C}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{C}_{1}
where anyons in
\mathcal{C}_{0}
have trivial braiding with
e
and anyons in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
have
-1
braiding with
e
. Because of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-fusion rule of
e
, a given anyon
a
may either absorb
e
or not under fusion:
a\times e=a
(“short orbit”) or
a\times e=a^{e}\neq a
(“long orbit”). If
a
absorbs
e
, it has to belong to the
\mathcal{C}_{0}
sector (see Appendix A.1 of Ref.
Delmastro et al. 2021
). Hence every anyon
a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
comes in pairs
a,a^{e}
, and moreover they satisfy
\theta_{a^{e}}=-\theta_{a}.
This means that

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{0}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{1}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{e}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{0}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{1}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}-\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{1}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{0}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n}

(58)

for
n
odd.

Condensation corresponds to throwing away the anyons in
\mathcal{C}_{1}
(which are confined), and then identifying anyons related by fusion with
e
, and splitting anyons which absorb
e
. We already see from Eq. (
58
) that confined anyons do not affect
\xi_{n}
for odd
n
. Identifying
a\sim a\times e
means that long orbits now contribute half as much to the sum; splitting the short orbit means that instead of
d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}
, it now contributes
2(\frac{1}{2}d_{a})^{2}\theta_{a}
so again half as much. So, after condensation, we simply get an overall factor of
\frac{1}{2}
in front of the sum
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{C}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}
. Since
\xi_{n}
is the phase of this, this factor does not affect it.

Thus,
\left(\breve{\mathcal{B}}\boxtimes\mathcal{S}_{\nu}\right)_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}
has higher central charges which differ from those of
\breve{\mathcal{B}}
by at most factors of 16th roots of unity, and higher central charges of
\mathcal{B}
are well-defined up to factors of 16th roots of unity.

Appendix D
Proof that

is a signed permutation matrix

From Sec.

After a basis change,

, where

\displaystyle U=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}&\mathds{1}\\
\mathds{1}&-\mathds{1}\end{pmatrix}\oplus\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}&\mathds{1}\\
\mathds{1}&-\mathds{1}\end{pmatrix}\oplus\mathds{1}.

(61)

(This

\hat{H}(n):=\hat{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}n&0\\
0&\bar{n}\end{pmatrix}
.

Let us write

\displaystyle H(n)=\begin{pmatrix}A&B&\cdots\\
C&D&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}

where

0&\ddots&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}=UH(n)U^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}A+B+C+D&A+C-(B+D)&\cdots\\
A-C+B-D&A-C-(B-D)&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}

(63)

from which we get

\displaystyle B=C

(64)

and

\displaystyle A-D=0.

Then,

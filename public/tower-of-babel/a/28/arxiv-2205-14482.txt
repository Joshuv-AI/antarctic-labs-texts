Doubling the equatorial for the prescribed scalar curvature problem on 𝕊 N {\mathbb{S}}^{N}
Lipeng Duan, Monica Musso, Suting Wei

Abstract
. We consider the prescribed scalar curvature problem on 𝕊 N {\mathbb{S}}^{N} Δ 𝕊 N ​ v − N ⁡ ( N − 2 ) 2 ​ v + K ~ ​ ( y ) ​ v N + 2 N − 2 = 0 on ​ 𝕊 N , v > 0 in ​ 𝕊 N , \displaystyle\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{N}}v-\frac{N(N-2)}{2}v+\tilde{K}(y)v^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}=0\quad\mbox{on}\ {\mathbb{S}}^{N},\qquad v>0\quad{\quad\hbox{in }}{\mathbb{S}}^{N}, under the assumptions that the scalar curvature K ~ \tilde{K} is rotationally symmetric, and has a positive local maximum point between the poles. We prove the existence of infinitely many non-radial positive solutions, whose energy can be made arbitrarily large. These solutions are invariant under some non-trivial sub-group of O ⁡ ( 3 ) O(3) obtained doubling the equatorial. We use the finite dimensional Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method. 1.
Introduction

Given the
N
-th sphere
({\mathbb{S}}^{N},g)
equipped with the standard metric
g
and a fixed smooth function
\tilde{K}
, the prescribed scalar curvature problem on
{\mathbb{S}}^{N}
consists in understanding whether it is possible to find another metric
\tilde{g}
in the conformal class of
g
, such that the scalar curvature of
\tilde{g}
is
\tilde{K}
.
For a conformal change of the metric

\tilde{g}=v^{4\over N-2}\,g,

for some positive function
v:{\mathbb{S}}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, the scalar curvature with respect to
\tilde{g}
is given by

v^{-{N+2\over N-2}}\,\left(\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{N}}v-\frac{N(N-2)}{2}v\right)

where
\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{N}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
{\mathbb{S}}^{N}
.
Thus the prescribed scalar curvature problem on
{\mathbb{S}}^{N}

can be addressed by studying the solvability of the problem

\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{N}}v-\frac{N(N-2)}{2}v+\tilde{K}(y)v^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}=0\quad\mbox{on}\ {\mathbb{S}}^{N},\qquad v>0\quad{\quad\hbox{in }}{\mathbb{S}}^{N}.

(1.1)

Testing the equation against
v
and integrating on
{\mathbb{S}}^{N}
, we get that
a necessary condition for the solvability of this problem is that
\tilde{K}(y)
must be positive somewhere. There are other obstructions for the existence of solutions in the energy space, which are said to be of topological type. For instance, a solution
v
must satisfy the following Kazdan-Warner type condition (see
[
15
]
):

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{N}}\nabla\tilde{K}(y)\cdot\nabla y\,v^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}\,d\sigma=0.

(1.2)

This condition is a direct consequence of Theorem 5.17 in
[
16
]
: Kazdan and Warner proved that given a positive solution
v
to

\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{N}}v-\frac{N(N-2)}{2}v+H(y)v^{a}=0

on the standard sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{N}}v^{a+1}\nabla H\cdot\nabla F={1\over 2}(N-2)\left({N+2\over N-2}-a\right)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{N}}v^{a+1}H\,F\,

for any spherical harmonics
F
of degree
1
. Taking
a={N+2\over N-2}
,
H=\tilde{K}
and
F=y
, we obtain (
1.2
).

The problem of determining which
\tilde{K}(y)
admits a solution has been the object of several studies in the past years. We refer the readers to
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
14
,
15
,
30
]
, and the references therein.

The prescribed scalar curvature problem on
{\mathbb{S}}^{N}
(
1.1
) can be transformed into a semi-linear elliptic equation in the flat space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
via the stereo-graphic projection. Letting
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denote the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}
, solving (
1.1
) in the energy space is equivalent to solving

\displaystyle\Delta v+K(y)v^{2^{*}-1}=0,\quad v>0,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{N},\,v\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.3)

Because of its geometry roots, it is of interest to establish under what kind of assumptions on
K
problem (
1.3
) admits one or multiple solutions.

For
N=3
, Y.Y. Li
[
17
]
showed problem (
1.3
) has infinitely many solutions provided that
K(y)
is bounded below, and periodic in one of its variables, and the set
\{x\,|\,K(x)=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(y)\}
is not empty and contains at least one bounded connected component.

If
K
has the form
K(y)=1+\epsilon h(y)
, namely it is a perturbation of the constant
1
, D. Cao, E. Noussair and S. Yan
[
5
]
proved the existence of multiple solutions.

If
K(y)
has a sequence of strictly local maximum points moving to infinity, S. Yan
[
32
]
constructed infinitely many solutions.

In
[
31
]
, J. Wei and S. Yan showed that problem (
1.3
) has infinitely many solutions provided
K
is radially symmetric
K(y)=K(r)
,
r=|y|
, and has a local maximum around a given
r_{0}>0
. More precisely, they ask that there are
r_{0}
,
c_{0}>0
and
m\in[2,N-2)
such that

K(s)=K(r_{0})-c_{0}|s-r_{0}|^{m}+O\big({|s-r_{0}|^{m+\sigma}}\big),\quad s\in(r_{0}-\delta,r_{0}+\delta),

for some
\sigma,\delta
small positive constants. These solutions are obtained by gluing together a large number of Aubin-Talenti bubbles (see
[
29
]
)

\displaystyle U_{x,\Lambda}(y)=c_{N}\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{1+\Lambda^{2}|y-x|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}},\,c_{N}=[N(N-2)]^{\frac{N-2}{4}}.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, these functions solve

\Delta u+u^{N+2\over N-2}=0\quad~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.4)

In fact they are the only positive solutions to (
1.4
). At main order their solution looks like

\tilde{u}_{k}\sim\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{x_{j},\bar{\Lambda}},

where
\bar{\Lambda}
is a positive constant and the points
x_{j}
are distributed along the vertices of a regular polygon of
k
edges in the
(y_{1},y_{2})
-plane, with
|x_{j}|\to r_{0}
as
k\to\infty
:

x_{j}=\Big(\tilde{r}\cos\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k},\tilde{r}\sin\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k},0,\cdots,0\Big),\quad j=1,\cdots,k,

with
\tilde{r}\rightarrow r_{0}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Under the weaker symmetry conditions for
K(y)=K(|y^{\prime}|,y^{\prime\prime})
with
y=(|y^{\prime}|,y^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
, S. Peng, C. Wang and S. Wei
[
27
]
constructed infinitely many bubbling solutions, which concentrate at the saddle points of the potential
K(y)
. Their method uses local Pohozaev identities adapted to problem (
1.3
). Y. Guo and B. Li
[
11
]
generalized these results for problems (
1.3
) with polyharmonic operators
[
31
]
. For the fractional scalar field equation, we refer to
[
13
,
23
]
.

The study of other aspects of problem (
1.3
), such as radial symmetry of their solutions, uniqueness of solutions, Liouville type theorem, a prior estimates, and bubbling analysis, have been the object of investigation of several researchers. We refer the readers to the papers
[

,

,

,

and the references therein.

Recently, Y. Guo, M. Musso, S. Peng and S. Yan
[
12
]
investigated the spectral property of the linearized problem associated to (
1.3
) around the solution
\tilde{u}_{k}
found in
[
31
]
. They proved a non-degeneracy result for such operator using a refined version of local Pohozaev identities. As an application of this non-degeneracy result, they built new type of solutions by gluing another large number of bubbles, whose centers lie near the circle
|y|=r_{0}
in the
(y_{3},y_{4})
-plane.

All these results concern solutions made by gluing together Aubin-Talenti bubbles with centres distributed along the vertices of one or more planar polygons, thus of two-dimensional nature.
The purpose of this paper is to present a different type of solution to (
1.3
) with a more complex concentration structure, which cannot be reduced to a two-dimensional one.

To present our result, we made the following assumptions on
K
: it is radially symmetric and

{\bf H}
: There are
r_{0}
and
c_{0}>0
such that

K(s)=K(r_{0})-c_{0}|s-r_{0}|^{m}+O\big({|s-r_{0}|^{m+\sigma}}\big),\quad s\in(r_{0}-\delta,r_{0}+\delta),

where
\sigma,\delta>0
are small constants,

\displaystyle m\in\begin{cases}[2,N-2)\quad&\text{if}\quad N=5~\text{or}~6,\\[5.69054pt]
\big(\frac{N-2}{2},N-2\big)\quad&\text{if}\quad N\geq 7.\end{cases}

(1.5)

There is a slight difference between our assumptions on
K(s)
and the ones in
[
31
]
. We will comment on this issue later in the introduction.

Without loss of generality, we assume
r_{0}=1,\,K(1)=1
. For any integer
k
, we define

{\bf r}=k^{\frac{N-2}{N-2-m}}.

We scale
u(y)={\bf r}^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}v\big(\frac{|y|}{{\bf r}}\big)
, so that problem (
1.3
) becomes

\displaystyle-\Delta u=K\Big(\frac{|y|}{{\bf r}}\Big)u^{2^{*}-1},\quad u>0,\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.6)

where

2^{*}={2N\over N-2}.

Consider the points

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\overline{x}_{j}=r\Big(\sqrt{1-h^{2}}\cos{\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k}},\sqrt{1-h^{2}}\sin{\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k}},h,{\bf{0}}\Big),\quad j=1,\cdots,k,\\[11.38109pt]
\underline{x}_{j}=r\Big(\sqrt{1-h^{2}}\cos{\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k}},\sqrt{1-h^{2}}\sin{\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k}},-h,{\bf{0}}\Big),\quad j=1,\cdots,k,\end{cases}

where
{\bf{0}}
is the zero vector in
\mathbb{R}^{N-3}
and
h,r
are positive parameters.

We define

\displaystyle W_{r,h,\Lambda}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{\overline{x}_{j},\Lambda}(y)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{\underline{x}_{j},\Lambda}(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.7)

We will produce a family of solutions to (
1.6
) which are small perturbations of
W_{r,h,\Lambda}
, for any integer
k
sufficiently large. The Aubin-Talenti bubbles are now centred at points lying on the top and the bottom circles of a cylinder and this configuration is now invariant under a non-trivial sub-group of
O(3)
rather than
O(2)
.

Throughout of the present paper, we assume
N\geq 5
and
(r,h,\Lambda)\in{{\mathscr{S}}_{k}}
, where

\displaystyle{{\mathscr{S}}_{k}}=\Bigg\{

\displaystyle(r,h,\Lambda)\big|\,r\in\Big[k^{\frac{N-2}{N-2-m}}-\hat{\sigma},k^{\frac{N-2}{N-2-m}}+\hat{\sigma}\Big],\quad\Lambda\in\Big[\Lambda_{0}-\hat{\sigma},\Lambda_{0}+\hat{\sigma}\Big],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad h\in\Big[\frac{B^{\prime}}{k^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}}\Big(1-\hat{\sigma}\Big),\frac{B^{\prime}}{k^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}}\Big(1+\hat{\sigma}\Big)\Big]\Bigg\},

(1.8)

with
\Lambda_{0},B^{\prime}
are the constants in (
3.7
), (
3.10
) and
\hat{\sigma}
is a small fixed number, independent of
k
. For convenience, we denote

{\bf r}=k^{\frac{N-2}{N-2-m}},\quad{\bf h}=\frac{B^{\prime}}{k^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}}.

Since
h\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, the two circles where the points
\overline{x}_{j}
and
\underline{x}_{j}
are distributed become closer to each other as
k
increases.

In this paper, we prove that for any
k
large enough problem (
1.6
) has a family of solutions
u_{k}
with the approximate form

u_{k}(y)\sim W_{r,h,\Lambda}.

(1.9)

These solutions have polygonal symmetry in the
(y_{1},y_{2})
-plane, are even in the
y_{3}
direction and radially symmetric in the variables
y_{4},\cdots,y_{N}
. Our solutions are thus different from the ones obtained in
[
31
]
and have strong analogies with the doubling construction of the entire finite energy sign-changing solutions for the Yamabe equation in
[
24
]
.

Define the symmetric Sobolev space:

\displaystyle H_{s}=\Bigg\{

\displaystyle u:u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\text{ $u$ is even in $y_{\ell},\,\ell=2,3,4,\cdots,N,$}\quad

\displaystyle\qquad u\Big(\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}\cos\theta,\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}\sin\theta,y_{3},y^{\prime\prime}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad=u\Big(\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}\cos{\big(\theta+\frac{2j\pi}{k}\Big)},\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}\sin{\big(\theta+\frac{2j\pi}{k}\Big)},y_{3},y^{\prime\prime}\Big)\Bigg\},

where
\theta=\arctan{\frac{y_{2}}{y_{1}}}
.

Let us define the following norms which capture the decay property of functions

\|u\|_{*}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big[\frac{1}{(1+|y-\overline{x}_{j}|)^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\tau}}+\frac{1}{(1+|y-\underline{x}_{j}|)^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\tau}}\Big]\Bigr)^{-1}|u(y)|,

(1.10)

and

\|f\|_{**}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big[\frac{1}{(1+|y-\overline{x}_{j}|)^{\frac{N+2}{2}+\tau}}+\frac{1}{(1+|y-\underline{x}_{j}|)^{\frac{N+2}{2}+\tau}}\Big]\Bigr)^{-1}|f(y)|,

(1.11)

where

\displaystyle\tau=(\frac{N-2-m}{N-2},\frac{N-2-m}{N-2}+\epsilon_{1}),

(1.12)

for some
\epsilon_{1}
small.
The main results of this paper are the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
K(|y|)
satisfies
{\bf H}
and
N\geq 5
. Then there exists a large integer
k_{0}
, such that for each integer
k\geq k_{0}
, problem (
1.6
) has a solution
u_{k}
of the form

\displaystyle u_{k}(y)=W_{r_{k},h_{k},\Lambda_{k}}(y)+\phi_{k}(y),

(1.13)

where
\phi_{k}\in H_{s},\,(r_{k},s_{k},\Lambda_{k})\in{{\mathscr{S}}_{k}}
and
\phi_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\phi_{k}\|_{*}=o_{k}(1),\quad{\mbox{as}}\quad k\to\infty.

Equivalently, problem (
1.3
) has solution
v_{k}(y)
of the form

\displaystyle v_{k}(y)={\bf r}^{\frac{2-N}{2}}\Big[W_{r_{k},h_{k},\Lambda_{k}}({\bf r}y)+\phi_{k}({\bf r}y)\Big].

Let us outline the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The first step in our argument is to find
\phi
so that

u=W_{r,h,\Lambda}+\phi
solves the auxiliary problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta\bigl(W_{r,h,\Lambda}+\phi\bigr)=K\bigl(\frac{|y|}{{\bf r}}\bigr)\bigl(W_{r,h,\Lambda}+\phi\bigr)^{2^{*}-1}\\[5.69054pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{3}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}{c_{\ell}}\Big(\,U_{\overline{x}_{j},\Lambda}^{2^{*}-2}\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell j}+U_{\underline{x}_{j},\Lambda}^{2^{*}-2}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell j}\,\Big)\,\;\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\phi\in\mathbb{E},\end{cases}

(1.14)

for some scalars
c_{\ell}
for
\ell=1,2,3
. In (
1.14
), the functions
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell j}
and
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell j}
are given by

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{1j}=\frac{\partial U_{\overline{x}_{j},\Lambda}}{\partial r},\qquad\qquad\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{2j}=\frac{\partial U_{\overline{x}_{j},\Lambda}}{\partial h},\qquad\qquad\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{3j}=\frac{\partial U_{\overline{x}_{j},\Lambda}}{\partial\Lambda},

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{1j}=\frac{\partial U_{\underline{x}_{j},\Lambda}}{\partial r},\qquad\qquad\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{2j}=\frac{\partial U_{\underline{x}_{j},\Lambda}}{\partial h},\qquad\qquad\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{3j}=\frac{\partial U_{\underline{x}_{j},\Lambda}}{\partial\Lambda},

for
j=1,\cdots,k
. Moreover the function
\phi
belongs to the set
\mathbb{E}
given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}=\Big\{v:v\in H_{s},\quad

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}U_{\overline{x}_{j},\Lambda}^{2^{*}-2}\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell j}v=0\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}U_{\underline{x}_{j},\Lambda}^{2^{*}-2}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell j}v=0,\quad j=1,\cdots,k,\quad\ell=1,2,3\Big\}.

(1.15)

From the linear theory developed in Section
2
, (
1.14
) can be solved by means of the contraction mapping theorem. More precisely, we prove that, for any
(r,h,\Lambda)\in{{\mathscr{S}}_{k}}
there exist
\phi=\phi_{r,h,\Lambda}\in\mathbb{E}
and constants
c_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,2,3
which solve the auxiliary problem (
1.14
).

The results in Theorem
1.1
can be then proved if there exists a choice of
(r,h,\Lambda)\in{{\mathscr{S}}_{k}}
so that the multipliers
c_{\ell}\,(\ell=1,2,3)
in (
1.14
) can be made equal zero.
We now make the following observation: take the functional corresponding to the problem (
1.6
)

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dy-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K\Big(\frac{|y|}{{\bf r}}\Big)|u|^{2^{*}}dy.

(1.16)

If we can choose
(r,h,\Lambda)
to be a critical point of function

F(r,h,\Lambda):=I(W_{r,h,\Lambda}+\phi_{r,h,\Lambda})\quad\text{for}~\phi\in{\mathbb{E}},

then the constants
c_{\ell},\,\ell=1,2,3
would be zero. Thus finding solutions of problem (
1.6
) would be reduced to find a critical point of
F(r,h,\Lambda)
. This is the result in Proposition
3.1
.

An important work of this paper is to give an accurate expression of
F(r,h,\Lambda)
(see Proposition
3.2
).
Under the assumptions
r\sim k^{\frac{N-2}{N-2-m}},h\rightarrow 0,\frac{1}{hk}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we first get the expansion of energy functional
I(W_{r,h,\Lambda})

\displaystyle F_{1}(r,h,\Lambda):=I(W_{r,h,\Lambda})

\displaystyle\,=\,kA_{1}-\frac{k}{\Lambda^{N-2}}\Big[\frac{B_{4}k^{N-2}}{(r\sqrt{1-h^{2}})^{N-2}}\,+\,\frac{B_{5}k}{r^{N-2}h^{N-3}\sqrt{1-h^{2}}}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+k\Big[\frac{A_{2}}{\Lambda^{m}k^{\frac{(N-2)m}{N-2-m}}}+\frac{A_{3}}{\Lambda^{m-2}k^{\frac{(N-2)m}{N-2-m}}}({\bf r}-r)^{2}\Big]+kO\Big(\frac{1}{k^{\frac{(N-2)m}{N-2-m}+\sigma}}\Big),

where
A_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
and
B_{j}
for
j=4,5
are constants.
Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(h)

\displaystyle:=\frac{B_{4}k^{N-2}}{(\sqrt{1-h^{2}})^{N-2}}\,+\,\frac{B_{5}k}{h^{N-3}\sqrt{1-h^{2}}}.

Let
h
be the solution of
\partial_{h}\mathcal{G}(h)=0
, then

h=\frac{B^{\prime}}{k^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}}\big(1+o(1)\big),\quad{\mbox{as}}\quad k\to\infty

for some
B^{\prime}>0
.
If
r\sim k^{\frac{N-2}{N-2-m}},h\sim\frac{B^{\prime}}{k^{\frac{N-3}{N-1}}}
, then

\frac{B_{5}k}{r^{N-2}h^{N-3}\sqrt{1-h^{2}}}=\frac{\tilde{B}}{k^{\frac{(N-2)m}{N-2-m}+\frac{2(N-3)}{N-1}}}(1+o(1)),\quad{\mbox{as}}\quad k\to\infty

for some constant
\tilde{B}
.

However, we now find that the term
O\Big(\frac{1}{k^{\frac{(N-2)m}{N-2-m}+\sigma}}\Big)
in the expansion of
F_{1}(r,h,\Lambda)
competes with the term
\frac{B_{5}k}{r^{N-2}h^{N-3}\sqrt{1-h^{2}}}
, making it impossible to identify a critical point for
F_{1}(r,h,\Lambda)
. In reality though the remainder
O\Big(\frac{1}{k^{\frac{(N-2)m}{N-2-m}+\sigma}}\Big)
can be estimated in a more accurate way (see Proposition
A.4
) under our assumptions
{\bf H}
.

We need to expand the full energy
F(r,h,\Lambda)=I(W_{r,h,\Lambda}+\phi_{r,h,\Lambda})
. We need a strong control on the size of
\phi_{r,h,\Lambda}
in order not to destroy the critical point structure of
F_{1}(r,h,\Lambda)
and to ensure the qualitative properties of the solutions as stated in Theorem
1.1
. This is another delicate step of our construction, where we make full use of the assumption
{\bf H}
on
K
.

2.
Finite dimensional reduction

For
j=1,\cdots,k
, we divide
\mathbb{R}^{N}
into
k
parts:

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-3}:

\displaystyle\qquad\Big\langle\frac{(y_{1},y_{2})}{|(y_{1},y_{2})|},\Big(\cos{\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k}},\sin{\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k}}\Big)\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\geq\cos{\frac{\pi}{k}}\Big\}.

where
\langle,\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
denote the dot product in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For
\Omega_{j}
, we further divide it into two parts:

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}^{+}=

\displaystyle\Big\{y:y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y^{\prime\prime})\in\Omega_{j},y_{3}\geq 0\Big\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}^{-}=

\displaystyle\Big\{y:y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y^{\prime\prime})\in\Omega_{j},y_{3}<0\Big\}.

We can know that

\mathbb{R}^{N}=\cup_{j=1}^{k}\Omega_{j},\quad\Omega_{j}=\Omega_{j}^{+}\cup\Omega_{j}^{-}

and

(2.1)

for some constants

Lemma 2.1

\begin{split}\phi(y)\,=\,&(2^{*}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|z-y|^{N-2}}K\Big(\frac{|z|}{{\bf r}}\Big)W_{r,h,\Lambda}^{2^{*}-2}\phi(z)\,{\mathrm{d}}z+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|z-y|^{N-2}}\,f(z){\mathrm{d}}z\\[5.69054pt]

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(\,U_{\overline{x}_{j},\Lambda}^{2^{*}-2}\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell j}+U_{\underline{x}_{j},\Lambda}^{2^{*}-2}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell j}\,\Big)\,\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{q1}=\bar{c}_{\ell}\delta_{\ell q}(1+\delta_{q2}r^{2})+o(1),\quad{\mbox{as}}\quad k\to\infty.

&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{(1+|y-\underline{x}_{j}|)^{\frac{N-2}{2}+\tau+\sigma}}.\end{split}

Proof.

The proof of Lemma
B.5
is similar to Lemma B.3 in
[
31
]
. Here we omit it.
∎

Lemma B.6
.

Suppose that
N\geq 5
and
m
satisfies (
1.5
). We have

\displaystyle{\bf r}\max\Big\{\frac{1}{k^{(\frac{m}{N-2-m})(N+2-2\frac{N-2-m}{N-2}-2\epsilon_{1})}},\frac{1}{k^{(\frac{N-2}{N-2-m})\min\{2m,m+3\}}}\Big\}\leq\frac{C}{k^{\big(\frac{m(N-2)}{N-2-m}+\frac{(N-3)}{N-1}+\sigma\big)}},

(B.1)

provided with
\sigma,\epsilon_{1}
small enough.

Proof.

It’s easy to show that

\displaystyle\frac{\bf r}{k^{(\frac{N-2}{N-2-m})\min\{2m,m+3\}}}\leq\frac{C}{k^{\big(\frac{m(N-2)}{N-2-m}+\frac{(N-3)}{N-1}+\sigma\big)}},

for
m\geq 2
. In order to get (
B.1
), we just need to show

\displaystyle\frac{\bf r}{k^{(\frac{m}{N-2-m})(N+2-2\frac{N-2-m}{N-2}-2\epsilon_{1})}}=\frac{k^{\frac{N-2}{N-2-m}}}{k^{(\frac{m}{N-2-m})(N+2-2\frac{N-2-m}{N-2}-2\epsilon_{1})}}\leq\frac{C}{k^{\big(\frac{m(N-2)}{N-2-m}+\frac{(N-3)}{N-1}+\sigma\big)}},

(B.2)

for some
\sigma,\epsilon_{1}
small. The problem to show (
B.2
) can be reduced to show that
~6+\frac{(N-3)}{N-1}<3(\frac{N-2}{N-2-m})+2\frac{N-2-m}{N-2}
, for
m
satisfying (
1.5
). This inequality follows by simple computations. This fact concludes the proof.
∎

First-order elliptic boundary value problems on manifolds with non-compact boundary
Christian Bär, Lashi Bandara

Abstract
. We consider first-order elliptic differential operators acting on vector bundles over smooth manifolds with smooth boundary, which is permitted to be noncompact. Under very mild assumptions, we obtain a regularity theory for sections in the maximal domain. Under additional geometric assumptions, and assumptions on an adapted boundary operator, we obtain a trace theorem on the maximal domain. This allows us to systematically study both local and nonlocal boundary conditions. In particular, the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary condition occurs as a special case. Furthermore, we study contexts which induce semi-Fredholm and Fredholm extensions.

Introduction

Boundary value problems are fundamental to engineering and physics and have been studied extensively in that context.
Historically, the study of boundary value problems has been carried out on Euclidean domains (or more generally in the setting of smooth manifolds with smooth compact boundary) with a focus on
local
boundary conditions.
There have been many significant generalisations - nonsmooth boundary and nonsmooth coefficients for second-order operators in divergence form as well as in the nondivergence form setting.

In the last fifty years, boundary value problems for first-order elliptic operators have become important in geometry, exemplified through the study of scalar curvature via the use of the Dirac operator on spin manifolds.
A description of the maximal domain was obtained by Seeley
[
14
]
in the 1960s via the use of Calderón projectors.
While this was useful in the study of PDEs in geometric settings, it was the identification of the APS boundary condition by Atiyah, Patodi and Singer which made a profound impact on index theory.
This is highlighted by their celebrated index theorem for Dirac-type operators on manifolds with smooth, compact boundary in in
[
1
,
3
,
2
,
4
]
.

The identification of the APS boundary condition was not only useful from an index theory perspective, but it also accounted for “half” of all possible boundary conditions.
This was understood much later, whereby on taking a sum of the APS boundary condition with its “anti-APS” counterpart, a trace theorem on the entire maximal domain of the operator was obtained.
Such a description allows for the study of
all
boundary conditions and is useful in index theory as it permits for the study of continuous deformations of boundary conditions.

The ability to describe all boundary conditions requires the study of spectral projectors associated with an
adapted boundary operator
on the boundary.
This is a perspective that matured and culminated in the work of Bär-Ballmann
[
6
]
and later Bär-Bandara
[
7
]
.
The former paper assumes that the bounded adapted operator can be chosen selfadjoint, essentially restricting the setting to Dirac-type operators.
In the latter paper, this restriction is removed so that there are no additional assumptions on the operator other than requiring it to be first-order elliptic.
An alternative approach is taken in
[
5
]
allowing for general-order elliptic differential operators on compact manifolds.

The analysis in
[
6
]
and earlier in
[
3
]
utilised Fourier circle methods that are possible due to selfadjointness of the adapted boundary operator and the presence of discrete spectrum.
In contrast, the analysis carried out in
[
7
]
differs significantly as adapted boundary operators may no longer be selfadjoint.
The Fourier circle perspective here was replaced by obtaining an
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus for the adapted boundary operator.

In this paper, we dispense with the requirement that the boundary is compact, only assuming that it is smooth.
In this direction, there have been a number of developments, which either assume strong conditions on the underlying geometry or modify the operator through adding a potential to gain a handle on its spectral theory.
In
[
11
]
Große and Nakad consider boundary value problems for noncompact boundary, including applications to index theory.
These results are obtained for local boundary conditions under the assumption of bounded geometry.
In
[
8
,
10
,
9
]
, Braverman and Shi investigate both local and nonlocal boundary value problems for Callias-type operators for noncompact boundary.

In our work, under very minimal assumptions which we call the standard setup (cf. Subsection
1.2
), we obtain regularity theory for sections in the maximal domain (Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
).
We then move on to describe the maximal domain of the operator.
To account for the lack of compactness of the boundary, we impose geometric conditions which are natural and are much weaker than bounded geometry.
However, we are forced to consider selfadjoint adapted boundary operators, mirroring the assumption made in
[
6
]
.
This still allows for a very large class of operators including all Dirac-type operators.
These assumptions are what we call the geometric setup and are listed in Subsection
1.3
.

Due to the fact that the boundary is allowed to be noncompact, this operator may have the entire real line as spectrum.
Therefore, Fourier circle methods cannot be used in the analysis.
Instead, we perform the analysis in the same vein as
[
7
]
, alluding to the
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus of the adapted boundary operator.
This posits the methods and ideas utilised in this paper closer to those arising from real-variable harmonic analysis.

Mirroring the trace results for the maximal domain in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, Theorem
1.4
establishes a trace theorem on the maximal domain.
As in the compact boundary case, this allows for an understanding of
all
boundary conditions, including those that are nonlocal.
Moreover, this allows us to define the APS boundary condition in the noncompact boundary setting.

In Section
7
, we study semi-Fredholm and Fredholm extensions, despite the noncompactness of the boundary.
We identify an appropriate notion of coercivity (cf. Definition
7.1
) with respect to a boundary condition.
When such a boundary condition is sufficiently regular, we show that operators satisfying this notion of coercivity are semi-Fredholm.
In particular, the APS boundary condition is a Fredholm one when the operator is coercive.

Finally, in Section
8
, we study applications to Dirac-type operators.
In particular, we show how the minimal and geometric setup can be satisfied under natural curvature assumptions.
Together with the results obtained in Section
7
, we show Fredholm extensions for Dirac-type operators for the APS boundary condition as well as chiral boundary conditions.
In particular, this includes the setup considered by Große and Nakad in
[
11
]
.
We also consider Callias-type perturbations of Dirac-type operators in the spirit of Braverman and Shi in
[
8
,
10
,
9
]
, extending some of their results.

1.
Setup and statement of main results

1.1.
Notation

Throughout, we use the analysts’ inequality
a\lesssim b
to mean that there exists a constant
C<\infty
such that
a\leq Cb
.
The objects
a
and
b
will be quantified and the dependency of
C
will often be clear from context.
Otherwise, it will be spelt out.
By
a\simeq b
, we mean that
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
.

On Banach spaces
\mathscr{X}_{1},\mathscr{X}_{2}
, we consider
T:\mathscr{X}_{1}\to\mathscr{X}_{2}
to typically be an unbounded operator.
The domain on which it acts will be written as
\mathrm{dom}(T)\subset\mathscr{X}_{1}
, with its range denoted by
\mathrm{ran}(T)\subset\mathscr{X}_{2}
.
Its kernel is given by
\mathrm{ker}(T)
.
When
\mathrm{ran}(T)=\mathscr{X}_{2}
, we say that the operator
T
is
invertible
if it is injective and has a bounded inverse.
An operator
T
is
densely-defined
if
\mathrm{dom}(T)
is dense in
\mathscr{X}_{1}
and it is
closed
if its graph is closed in
\mathscr{X}_{1}\times\mathscr{X}_{2}
.
When
\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{X}_{1}=\mathscr{X}_{2}
, let
\mathrm{res}(T)
be the
resolvent set
consisting of
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
such that
(\zeta-T)
is invertible.
The
spectrum
is
\mathrm{spec}(T)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathrm{res}(T)
.

For
M
a smooth manifold which is not necessarily compact, we do not obtain canonical Sobolev spaces as Banach spaces.
Nevertheless, we obtain local versions as locally convex linear spaces which are independent of measure and metric.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, let
{\rm H}^{\rm\alpha}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
be the set of all distributional sections
u
of
E
such that
u{{\lvert}}_{\Omega}\in{\rm H}^{\rm\alpha}(\Omega;E)
for every precompact
\Omega\subset M
.

Typically, we will be in the setting of
(M,\mu)
, where
M
is a smooth manifold with smooth measure
\mu
.
The spaces
{\rm H}^{\rm\alpha}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
are locally convex linear spaces with the topology induced by the family of semi-norms
\left\{\rho_{\Omega}(u):=\|u{{\lvert}}_{\Omega}\|_{{\rm H}^{\rm\alpha}(\Omega)}:\Omega\text{ precompact}\right\}.

If
(E,h^{E})\to M
is a Hermitian vector bundle, then for
p\in[1,\infty)
, we obtain Banach spaces
{\rm L}^{p}(M;E)
as the space of equivalence classes of measurable sections of
E
with

\int_{M}h^{E}(u,u)^{\frac{p}{2}}\ d\mu<\infty.

This is, by definition, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\rm L}^{p}}^{p}
, in
{\rm L}^{p}(M;E)
raised to the power
p
.
Two sections are considered equivalent if they coincide outside a null set.
For
p=\infty
, the space
{\rm L}^{\infty}(M;E)
is the space (of equivalence classes) of essentially bounded measurable sections of
E
.
The special case
p=2
is a Hilbert space, with inner product

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}}=\int_{M}h^{E}(u,v)\ d\mu.

For a first-order differential operator
D:{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;E)\to{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;F)
, where
(E,h^{E}),(F,h^{F})\to M
are two hermitian bundles, there exists a unique formal adjoint
D^{\dagger}:{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;F)\to{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;E)
.
Then, the maximal and minimal extensions of
D
are given by

D_{\max}=(D^{\dagger})^{\ast},\quad D_{\min}=\overline{D_{cc}},

where
D_{cc}=D
with
\mathrm{dom}(D_{cc})={\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm cc}(M;E)
, the space of smooth sections with compact support contained in the interior of
M
.
Similarly, we define
D^{\dagger}_{\max}
an
D^{\dagger}_{\min}
by interchanging the roles of
D
and
D^{\dagger}
.
The principal symbol of
D
is denoted by
\upsigma_{D}(x,\xi)
, which in the first-order case is given by the commutator
[D,f_{\xi}]
, where
f_{\xi}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M)
with
df_{\xi}(x)=\xi
.

1.2.
Minimal setup and regularity

The most general setup, the background setup, under which we obtain results, is the following.

(M1)

M
is a smooth manifold with smooth boundary
\partial M
, equipped with a smooth measure
\mu
;

(M2)

\vec{T}
is an interior pointing vector field along
\partial M
and
\tau
the associated covector field;

(M3)

(E,h^{E}),\ (F,h^{F})\to M
are Hermitian vector bundles over
M
;

(M4)

D
is a first-order elliptic differential operator from
E
to
F
;

(M5)

D
and
D^{\dagger}
are complete: i.e., compactly supported sections in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and
\mathrm{dom}(D^{\dagger}_{\max})
are dense in the respective graph norms.

From
(M2)
, combining with
(M1)
, the induced measure on
\partial M
is given by

\mu_{\tau}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n-1}):=\mu(\tau,\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n-1})

for
\xi_{i}\in T^{\ast}\partial M
and by viewing
\mu
as a density.

Under these assumptions, we first note we can make sense of the boundary trace map as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Under the assumptions
(M1)
–
(M5)
, the space
{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M;E)
is dense in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
with respect to the corresponding graph norm.
Moreover, the restriction map to the boundary

u\mapsto u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}:{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M;E)\to{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E)

has a unique bounded extension

u\mapsto u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}:\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})\to{\rm H}^{\rm-\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E).

Recall the Green’s formula

\left\langle Du,v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(M;F)}-\left\langle u,D^{\dagger}v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(M;E)}=-\left\langle\upsigma_{0}u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M},v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;F)},

(1)

for
u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M;E)
and
v\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M;F)
.
Here
\upsigma_{0}=\upsigma_{D}(\tau)
.
We will generalise this formula to
u
and
v
in the respective maximal domains in Theorem
1.4
.

By making sense of the trace, we can then proceed to prove the following regularity result under the minimal setup.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the minimal setup
(M1)
–
(M5)
, we have that:

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})\cap

\displaystyle{\rm H}^{\rm k}_{\rm loc}(M;E)

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max}):Du\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-1}_{\rm loc}(M;E)\text{ and }u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)\big\}.

(2)

In particular, by the Sobolev embedding theorem,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})\cap{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;E)=\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max}):Du\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;E)\text{ and }u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\partial M;E)\right\}.

Obviously, on interchanging the roles of
D
and
D^{\dagger}
, we obtain the same conclusions of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
for
D^{\dagger}
.

1.3.
Geometric setup and existence

To understand the maximal domain of the operator and study boundary value problems, we impose an additional set of assumptions which we call the geometric setup.
In what is to follow, we will use the notation
Y_{[0,r)}:=[0,r)\times\partial M
for
r>0
to denote the
r
-cylinder over
\partial M
.

The assumptions below are automatically satisfied when the boundary
\partial M
is compact, and they are identified here as a sufficiently general and geometrically verifiable set of assumptions beyond compact boundary.

(G1)

Let
\tau
be as in
(M2)
and let
A
and
\tilde{A}=-(\upsigma_{D}(\cdot,\tau(\cdot))^{-1})^{\ast}A\upsigma_{D}(\cdot,\tau(\cdot))^{\ast}
be essentially selfadjoint first-order differential operators on
E{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
and
F{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
respectively, adapted to the operators
D
and
D^{\dagger}
in the sense that for all

\displaystyle\upsigma_{A}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\upsigma_{D}(x,\tau(x))^{-1}\circ\upsigma_{D}(x,\xi)\text{ and}

\displaystyle\upsigma_{\tilde{A}}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\upsigma_{D^{\dagger}}(x,\tau(x))^{-1}\circ\upsigma_{D^{\dagger}}(x,\xi).

(G2)

Let
U\supset\partial M
in

be an open neighbourhood and let
\Phi=(t,\phi)\colon U\to Y_{[0,T_{0})}
be a diffeomorphism with
T_{0}>0
such that

(a)

\partial M=t^{-1}(0)

(b)

\phi{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=\mathrm{id}_{\partial M}

(c)

\tau=dt
along
\partial M
,

(d)

d\Phi(\vec{T})=\partial/\partial t
on
\partial M
where
\vec{T}
is the associated vector field to
\tau
, and

(e)

\Phi_{\ast}\mu=|dt|\otimes\mu_{\tau}

(G3)

There exists a

such that on
\Phi^{-1}Y_{[0,T)}
:

D=\upsigma_{t}(\partial_{t}+A+R_{t})\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\dagger}=-\upsigma_{t}^{\ast}(\partial_{t}+\tilde{A}+\tilde{R}_{t}),

where
\upsigma_{t}(x):=\upsigma_{D}(t,x,dt)
and for which there exists
C\geq 1
such that for all
(t,x)\in\Phi^{-1}Y_{[0,T)}
and

,

\displaystyle C^{-1}|u(x)|_{h^{E}}\leq|\upsigma_{t}(x)u(x)|_{h^{F}}\leq C|u(x)|_{h^{E}},

\displaystyle\|R_{t}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\leq Ct\|Au\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}+C\|u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)},

\displaystyle\|\tilde{R}_{t}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\leq Ct\|\tilde{A}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}+C\|u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}.

As a consequence of
(G2)
, for a given
\rho\leq T
, we write
Z_{[0,\rho)}:=\Phi^{-1}Y_{[0,\rho)}
.
If
\rho<T
, then define
Z_{[0,\rho]}:=\Phi^{-1}Y_{[0,\rho]}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that the
\tau
in
(G1)
is a particular choice of
\tau
, which is related to the operators
A
and
\tilde{A}
through the alluded principal symbol condition.
Contrast this to the
\tau
appearing in
(M2)

By the fact that

\chi^{+}(A)=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)

\chi^{-}(A)=\chi_{(-\infty,0)}(A)
are bounded selfadjoint projections on
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})
as well as
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
for all

.
Therefore, define

\check{\mathrm{H}}(A):=\chi^{-}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\oplus\chi^{+}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast},

with norm

\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}:=\|\chi^{-}(A)u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|\chi^{+}(A)u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}.

For

the norm
\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}
is equivalent to
\|(1+|A|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}}
and, if

\||A|^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}}
.
After defining
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):=\check{\mathrm{H}}(-A)
, the
{\rm L}^{2}
-inner product extends to a perfect pairing
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\to\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Under the setup
(M1)
–
(M5)
and
(G1)
–
(G3)

(i)

the trace maps

Remark 1.5
.

Assertions
(i)
and
(ii)
in Theorem

2.
Sufficient criterion for completeness

By definition, completeness of
D
and
D^{\dagger}
holds if
M
is compact.
In the noncompact case, completeness can often be checked using the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
D\colon{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;E)\to{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;F)
be a first-order differential operator.
Suppose that
M
carries a complete Riemannian metric with respect to which the principal symbol of
D
satisfies an estimate

|\upsigma_{D}(\xi)|\leq C(\mathrm{dist}(x,p))\cdot|\xi|

(4)

for all
x\in M
and
\xi\in T_{x}^{*}M
, where
p\in M
is a fixed point and
C:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a positive monotonically increasing continuous function with

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{C(r)}=\infty.

(5)

Then
D
and
D^{\dagger}
are complete.

This theorem applies, for instance, if
C
is a constant.
For instance, when the operator arises geometrically, this is often the case.
Note that we do not assume that
\mu
is the volume element induced by the Riemannian metric.
The proof is essentially identical with the discussion in Section 3 of
[
6
]
.
We give it for the sake of completeness.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We first prove the theorem under the assumption that
C>0
is constant.
Let
r:M\to\mathbb{R}
be the distance function from the point
p
,
r(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial M)
.
Then
r
is a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant
1
.
Choose
\rho\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
so that
0\leq\rho\leq 1
,
\rho(t)=0

,

\rho(t)=1

For

is a Lipschitz function and we have almost everywhere

Moreover,

is a uniformly bounded sequence of functions converging pointwise to

Now let

\|\chi_{m}u-u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}\to 0
as

by Lebesgue’s theorem.
Furthermore,
\chi_{m}u
has compact support and
\chi_{m}u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
, see Lemma 3.1 in
[
6

Since

\displaystyle\|D_{\max}(\chi_{m}u)

\displaystyle-D_{\max}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-\chi_{m})D_{\max}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}+\|\sigma_{D}(d\chi_{m})u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-\chi_{m})D_{\max}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}+\frac{2C}{m}\,\|u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}\to 0

as

, we conclude that
\chi_{m}u\to u
in the graph norm of
D_{\max}
.

The same discussion applies to

because
|\sigma_{D^{\dagger}}(\xi)|=|-\sigma_{D}(\xi)^{*}|=|\sigma_{D}(\xi)|
.
This proves the theorem in case

is constant.

Now we only assume that

satisfies (
5
).
Choose a smooth function

with

C(\mathrm{dist}(x,p))\leq f(x)\leq 2\,C(\mathrm{dist}(x,p))

for all

Let

be the complete Riemannian metric for which (

Then

is also complete because the

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|c^{\prime}(t)|_{g}}{f(c(t))}\,dt\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|c^{\prime}(t)|_{g}}{C(\mathrm{dist}(c(t),p))}\,dt\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{C(t)}\,dt=\infty.

With respect to

is uniformly bounded and we have reduced the discussion to the case that

∎

3.
The first trace theorem and regularity

In the compact boundary case, the regularity assertions for when the operator has higher regularity were given in terms of the
{\rm H}^{\rm k}

Here, the compactness of these spaces meant that
{\rm H}^{\rm k}

In our case, the assertions have to be rephrased to be local, and therefore, we require a localisation argument.
One issue with localisation at the boundary is that this introduces boundary to a subset that is already on the boundary.

.
To prove that
u\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
, we need to show that for each
\Omega\subset M
which is compact with nonempty interior, we have that

\|u\|_{{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega)}<\infty.

Therefore, fix
\Omega\subset M
compact with nonempty interior, and let
\Omega^{\prime}
be another compact subset with nonempty interior and with smooth boundary with
\Omega\subsetneqq\Omega^{\prime}
.
Let
\chi\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M)
be such that
\chi=1
on
\Omega
and
\chi=0
outside of
\Omega^{\prime}
.
Set
u^{\prime}=\chi u
.
It is easy to see that
u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
,
Du^{\prime}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-1}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
and that
u^{\prime}{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
.
Let
\left\{V_{i}\right\}
compact subsets of
\partial M
with nonempty interior such that
\left\{\mathring{V}_{i}\right\}
cover
\Omega^{\prime}\cap\partial M
as guaranteed from Lemma
3.1
and let
\delta=\min\left\{\delta_{i}\right\}
.
Now, let
\xi=1
on
\cup_{i=1}^{k}\Phi_{i}([0,\frac{1}{2}\delta]\times V_{i})
and
0
outside of
\cup_{i=1}^{k}\Phi_{i}([0,\delta]\times V_{i})
.
Let
u^{\prime\prime}:=(1-\xi)u^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
with
Du^{\prime\prime}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-1}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
.
Moreover,
\spt u^{\prime\prime}\cap\partial M=\emptyset
and therefore, from interior regularity theory, we have that
u^{\prime\prime}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
.
In particular,
u^{\prime\prime}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega^{\prime})
.

Now consider
v:=\xi u^{\prime}
.
We have that
u^{\prime}=u^{\prime\prime}+v
and we show that
v\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega^{\prime})
.
This would then yield that
u^{\prime}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega^{\prime})
.
First let
\left\{\eta_{i}\right\}
be a smooth partition of unity, subordinate to
\Phi_{i}([0,\frac{3}{4}\delta)\times\mathring{V}_{i})
.
Since
\spt v\subset\cup_{i=1}^{k}\Phi_{i}([0,\frac{1}{2}]\times\mathring{V}_{i})
, we have that
v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\eta_{i}v
.
Let
v_{i}\in{\rm L}^{2}(N_{i};E)
be defined by

v_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}\eta_{i}(x)v(x)&x\in\Phi_{i}([0,\frac{3}{4}\delta]\times V_{i})\\
0&x\not\in\Phi_{i}([0,\frac{3}{4}\delta]\times V_{i})\\
\end{cases}.

Note first that
\tilde{\tilde{D}}_{i,\max}v_{i}=Dv_{i}
and so
v_{i}\in\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\tilde{D}}_{i,\max})
.
Furthermore,
Dv\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-1}(\Omega^{\prime})
yields that
\tilde{\tilde{D}}_{i,\max}v_{i}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-1}(N_{i};E)
.
Moreover, since
v\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-\frac{1}{2}}(\cup_{i=1}^{k}\left\{0\right\}\times\partial V_{i};E)
, we have that
v_{i}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial N_{i};E)
.
Therefore, by applying Theorem 2.4 in
[
7
]
, we obtain that
v_{i}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}(N_{i};E)
.
However, since
v_{i}=0
outside of
[0,\frac{3}{4}\delta]\times V_{i}
, we obtain
\|\eta_{i}v\|_{{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega^{\prime})}<\infty
.
Now
v\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega^{\prime})
since

\|v\|_{{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega^{\prime})}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\eta_{i}v\|_{{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega^{\prime})}.

This yields
u^{\prime}\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega^{\prime};E)
and since
u^{\prime}=u
on
\Omega
, we conclude
u\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}(\Omega;E)
.
Since
\Omega
was an arbitrary compact set with nonempty interior, this proves
u\in{\rm H}^{\rm k}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
.
∎

4.
The model operator

We first consider the “model operator” associated to our problem, which is denoted by
D_{0}=\upsigma_{0}(\partial_{t}+A)
.

Throughout this section, due to the assumptions we make in
1.3
(in particular
(G1)
), we identify the unique closed extension
\overline{A}
with
A
, and similarly for
\tilde{A}
.
In
[
7
]
the boundary was compact and therefore, the boundary operators exhibited pure point spectrum.
However, they were merely bi-sectorial and not, in general, selfadjoint.
In particular, this meant that their generalised eigenspaces were in general nonorthogonal and possibly of higher algebraic multiplicity.
However, bi-sectorial operators are well suited to analysis via the
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus, which was afforded to us through pseudo-differential methods.
In our situation, despite the fact that the boundary defining operators are selfadjoint, we may have continuous spectrum, and therefore, we are again forced to abandon resorting to reasoning through eigenspaces.
However, motivated by the “global” approach we develop in
[
7
]
, we treat this case operator more in the spirit of the bi-sectorial situation via functional calculus.
Unfortunately, due to noncompactness, it is unlikely we can resort to pseudo-differential methods to obtain access to the
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus as we did in the compact case.
However, since we are in the selfadjoint context, we can instead allude to the Borel-functional calculus as a suitable replacement.

Recall that by the selfadjointness of
A
, we obtain a Borel functional calculus via the spectral theorem.
In particular, this means we are able to construct bounded projectors
\chi_{I}(A)
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval and
\chi_{I}
its characteristic function.
Let us write
\chi^{+}(A)=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)
and
\chi^{-}(A)=\chi_{(-\infty,0)}(A)
and define
|A|:=A\sgn(A)
, where
\sgn(A)=\chi^{+}(A)-\chi^{-}(A)
.

In
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, we were able to use the fact that
\chi^{\pm}(A)
are pseudo-differential operators of order
0
in order to see their boundedness properties on
{\rm H}^{\rm\alpha}(\partial M;E)
, which coincided with
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})
as a consequence of the assumed compactness of
\partial M
.
Since this assumption does not hold in our setting, we cannot reason in this manner and instead, we are forced to directly work with
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})
instead.

For
\varepsilon>0
put
|A|_{\varepsilon}:=(|A|+\varepsilon I)
.
In light of Lemma
A.3
and Remark
A.4
, we see that
|A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\alpha}
is extended boundedly to
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
and, in fact,
\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})^{\ast})}\simeq\||A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\alpha}u\|
.
By the same lemma, we obtain that
\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha}}\simeq\||A|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}u\|
.
Also, from Lemma
A.5
, we obtain that
\chi_{I}(A):\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\to\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
extend as bounded projections.

Define

\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)=\chi^{-}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\oplus\chi^{+}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}

(10)

with norm

\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}^{2}:=\|\chi^{-}(A)u\|^{2}_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|\chi^{+}(A)u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}.

(11)

Also, define
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):=\check{\mathrm{H}}(-A)
.
Proposition
A.11
tells us that
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A-rI)=\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
and
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A-rI)=\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
.
In applications, we will be precise about the norm which is used in computations.

Fix
T>0
from
(G3)
and for
\rho\in(0,T]
let
\eta_{\rho}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}([0,T))
such that
\eta_{\rho}(t)=1
whenever
x\in[0,1/2\rho]
and
\eta_{\rho}(t)=0
for
[3/4\rho,T)
.
Using this, and fixing some
\varepsilon>0
, define the extension operator

(\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u)(t,x)=\eta_{\rho}(t)(\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u)(x)

(12)

on the cylinder
Y_{[0,T)}
.
Using
\Phi
from
(G2)
, we regard
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
as an operator on
Z_{[0,T)}\subset M
.
Recall the notation
{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)=\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{k})
, which is guaranteed to be a dense subset in
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{k})
by Corollary
A.8
.
Moreover, it is a dense subset in
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
and
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
by Corollary
A.9
.
By the Sobolev embedding theorem,
{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)\subset{\rm C}^{\infty}(\partial M;E)
and if
\partial M
is compact we even have equality.
In general, however, sections in
{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)
enjoy a certain decay that is crucial for analysis in our noncompact setting.

Lemma 4.1
.

The operator
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
extends to a linear map
\mathrm{dom}(A)^{\ast}\to{\rm C}^{\infty}(Z_{(0,T)};E)\cap{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T)};E)
.
Furthermore,

\mathrm{ran}(\mathcal{E}_{\rho}{{\lvert}}_{{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)})\subset{\rm C}^{\infty}([0,T);{\rm D}^{\infty}(A))\subset{\rm C}^{\infty}(Z_{[0,T)};E).

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
A.8
, along with the fact that
{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)\subset{\rm C}^{\infty}(\partial M;E)
.
∎

This lemma ensures in particular that
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
is defined on
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
and
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
which are densely embedded in
\mathrm{dom}(A)^{\ast}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
u\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
, we have that
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{0,\max})
and

\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\|_{D_{0}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)},

where the implicit constants depend on
T
,
\eta_{\rho}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Similarly, for
v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
, we have that
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}v\in\mathrm{dom}((\sigma_{0}^{-1}D)^{\dagger}_{0,\max})
and

\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\|_{(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})^{\dagger}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}.

Proof.

We restrict our considerations here to
u\in{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)
because, by Corollary
A.9
,
{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)
is dense in
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.
That
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
maps
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
to
{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T)};E)
follows directly from Lemma
4.1
.

First, since
|\upsigma_{0}(x)|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C
by
(G3)
, we note that

\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\|_{D_{0}}\leq C(\|(\partial_{t}+A)\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\|+\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\|),

so to establish the claim, it suffices to estimate the right hand side.

Let
u=u^{-}+u^{+}
, where
u^{\pm}=\chi^{\pm}(A)
.
Then,
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u=\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u^{-}+\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u^{+}
, and a calculation mimicking the proof of Proposition 5.4 in
[
7
]
then yields

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+A)u^{-}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{t}\eta_{\rho}(t)+\varepsilon\eta_{\rho}(t))\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{-}-2\eta_{\rho}(t)|A|_{\varepsilon}\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{-}

(13)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+A)u^{+}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{t}\eta_{\rho}(t)+\varepsilon\eta_{\rho}(t))\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{+}.

Also,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta_{\rho}(t)^{2}\left(\|\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{-}\|^{2}+\|\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{+}\|^{2}\right)\ dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{\rho}(t)^{2}\left(\|\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{-}\|^{2}\ dt+\|\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{+}\|^{2}\ dt\right).

From the selfadjointness and positivity of
|A|_{\varepsilon}
, we have that that
\|\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})\|_{{\rm L}^{2}\to{\rm L}^{2}}\leq 1
, and therefore,

\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{\rho}(t)^{2}\|\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{-}\|^{2}\ dt\leq T\|u^{-}\|\lesssim\|u^{-}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}.

For the second term,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{+}\|^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\|t^{\frac{1}{2}}|A|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})|A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u^{+}\|^{2}\ \frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u^{+}\|\simeq\|u^{+}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}},

where the first inequality follows from Lemma
A.3
since
|A|_{\varepsilon}
has a
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus.
To finish the proof,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|2\eta_{\rho}(t)|A|_{\varepsilon}\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u^{-}\|^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\|t^{\frac{1}{2}}|A|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})|A|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{-}\|^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\||A|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{-}\|\simeq\|u^{-}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})},

where again, we have alluded to the
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus of
|A|_{\varepsilon}
.
Combining these estimates proves the first claim.

For the second claim, note that
(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})^{\dagger}=-(\partial_{t}-A)
.
Therefore, we repeat this argument with
-A
in place of
A
.
Note that
|-A|=\sqrt{(-A)^{2}}=\sqrt{A^{2}}=|A|
, so
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}^{\ast}=\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.
A repetition of the above estimates with
(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})
in place of
D_{0}
then yields that, for
v\in\mathrm{dom}(A)
,

\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}v\|\lesssim\|v\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(-A)}.

By definition
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)=\check{\mathrm{H}}(-A)
and again, by density, we obtain this estimate for all
v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.
∎

The following is then argued similarly to Lemma 6.5 in
[
7
]
, but with some minor modifications to account for the fact that
\partial M
is noncompact.

Corollary 4.3
.

For all
u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(Z_{[0,T)};E)

\|u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\lesssim\|u\|_{D_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\lesssim\|u\|_{(\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})^{\dagger}}.

Proof.

Since
\spt u
is compact, Greens formula yields

\left\langle(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})u,v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}-\left\langle u,(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})^{\dagger}v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}=-\left\langle u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M},v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}

for all
v\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(Z_{[0,T)};E)
.
Take
v_{0}\in{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)
, and let
v:=\mathcal{E}_{\rho}v_{0}
.
Clearly,
v\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(Z_{[0,T)};E)
by Lemma
4.1
.
Since
v_{0}=v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
,

\displaystyle|\left\langle u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M},v_{0}\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}|

\displaystyle\leq|\left\langle(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})u,\mathcal{E}_{\rho}v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}|+|\left\langle u,(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})^{\dagger}\mathcal{E}_{\rho}v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}+\|u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}\|(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})^{\dagger}\mathcal{E}_{\rho}v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{D_{0}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}v\|_{(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{D_{0}}\|v_{0}\|_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)},

where the ultimate inequality follows from Proposition
4.2
.
Therefore,

\|u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\simeq\sup_{0\neq v_{0}\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\frac{|\left\langle u,v_{0}\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}|}{\|v_{0}\|_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}}\lesssim\|u\|_{D_{0}},

where we have used that
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}=\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
on
\mathrm{dom}(A)
by Lemma
A.10
and the fact that
\mathrm{dom}(A)
is dense in
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.
∎

Also, the following holds as in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(Z_{[0,T)};E)
, the following equation holds.

\|(\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})u\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T)})}=\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T)})}+\|Au\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T)})}-\left\langle|A|\sgn(A)u_{0},u_{0}\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}.

Proof.

Writing
\|(\upsigma^{-1}_{0}D_{0})u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T)})}^{2}=\left\langle(\partial_{t}+A)u,(\partial_{t}+A)u\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T)})}
, the conclusion follows from the selfadjointness of
A
.
∎

Lastly we note the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

Over the boundary
\partial M
, the homomorphism field
(\upsigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}:E_{\partial M}\to F_{\partial M}
induces an isomorphism
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)\to\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})
, where
\tilde{A}
is the adapted boundary operator for
D^{\dagger}
.
Also, we have that
(\upsigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}:{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)\to{\rm D}^{\infty}(\tilde{A})
.
Moreover, the pairing
\beta:\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})\to\mathbb{C}
, obtained by extending
\beta(u,v)=-\left\langle\upsigma_{0}u,v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}
for
u\in{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)\cap\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
and
v\in{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;F)\cap\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})
, is perfect.

Proof.

Throughout, note by Assumption
(G3)
, the map
\sigma_{0}
is uniformly bounded.
By Assumption
(G1)
, we note that
\tilde{A}
is also the induced adapted operator for
D_{0}^{\dagger}
from
A
, given by
\tilde{A}=-(\sigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}A\sigma_{0}^{\ast}
.
Let
u\in{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)
and since

\tilde{A}(\sigma_{0}^{-1})u=-(\sigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}A\sigma_{0}^{-1}(\sigma_{0}^{-1})u=-(\sigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}Au,

we have that
(\sigma_{0}^{-1})u\in\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{A})
.
By repeated application of this calculation, we conclude that
(\sigma_{0}^{-1})u\in{\rm D}^{\infty}(\tilde{A})
.

Now, note that
u=(\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u){{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
and therefore,

\displaystyle\|(\sigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})}\lesssim\|(\sigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\|_{D_{0}^{\dagger}}\simeq\|\mathcal{E}_{\rho}u\|_{(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0})^{\dagger}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)},

where the first inequality follows from applying Corollary
4.3
to
D_{0}^{\dagger}
and the last inequality from applying Proposition
4.2
.
The space
\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{A})
is dense in
\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})
and
{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)
is dense in
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
by Lemma
4.1
.
Therefore, this inequality holds for all
u\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.

Using a similar argument, interchanging
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
to
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}
, where the latter is the extension map with respect to
\tilde{A}
, we obtain by a similar calculation as before that

\|\sigma_{0}^{\ast}v\|_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\lesssim\|\sigma_{0}^{\ast}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}v\|_{D_{0}^{\dagger}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})},

for all
v\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})
.

Together, this shows that
\sigma_{0}^{\ast}
induced the isomorphism between
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
and
\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})
and also proves that the pairing given in the conclusion is perfect.
∎

5.
The maximal domain

Let
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max},Z_{[0,r]})
be the maximal domain of
D_{\max}
, considered as an operator in
Z_{[0,r]}
as defined in the geometric setup in Section
1.3
.
The following result is immediate for compact boundary, but in the noncompact case requires a little work.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
T
be as in
(G3)
.
There exists
C_{T}>0
and
T_{c}\in(0,T)
such that:

(i)

for all
u\in{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)
, we have
\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and

\|\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|_{D}\lesssim\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)};

(ii)

for all
u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(Z_{[0,T_{c})};E)
, we have

\|u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\lesssim\|u\|_{D}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\lesssim\|u\|_{D^{\dagger}}.

Proof.

To prove
(i)
, write
D=\upsigma_{t}(\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}+R_{t})
where
R_{t}
is at most a differential operator of order
1
.
Therefore,
R_{t}
is closable and Assumption
(G3)
then yields that
\mathrm{dom}(A)\subset\mathrm{dom}(\overline{R_{t}})
since
A
is essentially selfadjoint and with
\overline{R_{t}}
the closure of
R_{t}
on
{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E)
.
Since
\upsigma_{t}
is uniformly bounded by Assumption
(G3)
,

\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})\supset\mathrm{dom}(D_{0,\max})\cap\mathrm{dom}(\overline{R_{t}};Z_{[0,T_{c}]}).

By Proposition
4.2
we have that
\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{0,\max})
with the required estimate, and therefore, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\in\mathrm{dom}(\overline{R_{t}};Z_{[0,T_{c}]})
with the corresponding estimate.

Now, by a limiting argument, we note that the estimate in Assumption
(G3)
still holds on
\mathrm{dom}(A)
.
In particular, for each
t\geq 0
,
\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u(t)\in\mathrm{dom}(\overline{R_{t}})
.
Therefore, to show that
\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\in\mathrm{dom}(\overline{R_{t}};Z_{[0,T_{c}]})
, with the required trace bound, we use Assumption
(G3)
to write

\displaystyle\|R_{t}\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{c}]})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\|R_{t}\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}t^{2}\|A\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ dt+\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}\ dt.

We note

A\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u=A\eta_{T_{c}}\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u=\eta_{T_{c}}A\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u

and therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}t^{2}\|A\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\|t|A|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})|A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\|t^{\frac{3}{2}}|A|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})|A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ \frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\simeq\||A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}

\displaystyle\simeq\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}.

For the remaining term,

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\||A|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\varepsilon}\exp(-t|A|_{\varepsilon})|A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\simeq\||A|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}

Combining these estimates,

\|R_{t}\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{c}]})}^{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)},

and from Proposition
4.2
, we have that

\|\sigma_{t}\sigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|\simeq\|D_{0}\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|\lesssim\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}.

On extension by
0
, we have that
\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and

\|\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|_{D}=\|\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max};Z_{[0,T_{c}]})}\lesssim\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}.

For Assertion
(ii)
, we first note that, by using
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{T_{c}}

(i)
for the operator

.
Let

-\left\langle u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M},v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=-\left\langle u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M},\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}w\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}{A}}=\left\langle Du,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{T_{c}}w\right\rangle-\left\langle u,D^{\dagger}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{T_{c}}w\right\rangle.

6.
Boundary value problems

6.1.
Boundary conditions

As a consequence of Theorem
1.4
, given a subspace
B\subset\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
, we can define an associated operator
D_{B,\max}
with domain

\mathrm{dom}(D_{B,\max})=\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max}):u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in B\right\}.

Conversely, given an operator
D^{\prime}
satisfying
D_{\min}\subset D^{\prime}\subset D_{\max}
, define

B^{\prime}=\left\{u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}:u\in\mathrm{dom}(D^{\prime})\right\}.

With this notation, we obtain the following important lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

(i)

If
D^{\prime}
is an operator such that
D_{\min}\subset D^{\prime}\subset D_{\max}
, we have that

\mathrm{dom}(D^{\prime})=\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max}):u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in B^{\prime}\right\}.

(ii)

The operator
D_{B,\max}
is closed if and only if
B
is closed.

Proof.

The lemma follows immediately from the trace map inducing an isomorphism

\frac{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})}{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\min})}\to\check{\mathrm{H}}(A),

see Remark
1.5
.
∎

Definition 6.2
.

A boundary condition for
D
is a
closed
subspace
B\subset\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.
The associated operators are then given by
D_{B,\max}
and
D_{B}
, with domains

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}(D_{B,\max})=\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max}):u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in B\right\}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})=\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})\cap{\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm loc}(M;E):u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in B\right\}.

As a consequence of Lemma
6.1
, for any boundary condition
B
, the operator
D_{B,\max}
is closed.

Proposition 6.3
.

A boundary condition
B
satisfies
B\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
if and only if
D_{B}=D_{B,\max}
.

Proof.

It is clear that we always have
D_{B}\subset D_{B,\max}
.
Assuming that
B\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
, we prove that
D_{B,\max}\subset D_{B}
.
For that, let
u\in D_{B,\max}
, and so we have that
u\in{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
.
By Theorem
1.2
, we then obtain that
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})\cap{\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
.
This shows that
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
.

For
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
, we have that
u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)\cap B
.
Given that we assume
D_{B}=D_{B,\max}
, we have by Lemma
6.1

(i)
that
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B,\max})
if and only if
u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in B
which can only happen if
B\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
.
∎

Given a boundary condition
B
, operator theory implies that the adjoint operator
D_{B,\max}^{\ast}
satisfies:

D_{B,\max}^{\ast}\subset(D_{\min})^{\ast}=D^{\dagger}_{\max}.

Therefore, as a consequence of Theorem
1.4

(iv)
, we define

B^{\ast}:=\left\{v\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A}):\left\langle\upsigma_{0}u,v\right\rangle_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})\times\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})}=0\ \quad\forall u\in B\right\}.

(14)

Proposition 6.4
.

The space
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\ast}=B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
and
D_{B,\max}^{\ast}
satisfies:

\mathrm{dom}(D_{B,\max}^{\ast})=\left\{v\in\mathrm{dom}(D^{\dagger}_{\max}):v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in B^{\ast}\right\}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
A.10
and calculating:

\displaystyle B^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A}):\left\langle\upsigma_{0}u,v\right\rangle_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})\times\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A})}=0\ \quad\forall u\in B\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A}):\left\langle u,\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\ \quad\forall u\in B\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(\upsigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}w\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(\tilde{A}):\left\langle u,w\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\ \quad\forall u\in B\right\}

\displaystyle=(\upsigma_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}\left\{w\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,w\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\ \quad\forall u\in B\right\}.

The remaining assertion follows directly from Theorem

, noting that

\left\langle D_{B,\max}u,v\right\rangle=\left\langle u,D_{B,\max}^{\ast}v\right\rangle

for all

and
v\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B,\max}^{\ast})
, along with Lemma
6.1
.
∎

Definition 6.5
.

We say that a boundary condition
B
is
elliptically semi-regular
if
B\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
and
elliptically regular
if
B
is elliptically semi-regular and
B^{\ast}
is also elliptically semi-regular.

Remark 6.6
.

Note that since
\upsigma_{0}:{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;F)\to{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;F)
, the elliptic semi-regularity of
B^{\ast}
is equivalent to the assertion that
B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
.

Then we immediately obtain the following.

Corollary 6.7
.

A boundary condition
B
is elliptically regular if and only if
\mathrm{dom}(D_{B,\max})\subset{\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
and
\mathrm{dom}(D_{B,\max}^{\ast})\subset{\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm loc}(M;F)
. ∎

Note that in the compact case, the assertion of a elliptically (semi-)regular boundary condition can be formulated in terms of the adapted operator
A
.
Namely, since
{\rm H}^{\rm\alpha}(\partial M;E)=\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})
, we can see from Definition
6.5
and Corollary
6.7
that the spaces
{\rm H}^{\rm\alpha}(\partial M;E)
for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}
can be described in terms of domains
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}
.
Clearly, in the noncompact setting, it is a futile effort to attempt to identify the spaces
{\rm H}^{\rm\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
, which are locally convex linear spaces, with domains of powers of
A
, which are Banach spaces.
That being said, it is important in applications to consider the following class of boundary conditions whose regularity is captured in terms of
A
.

Definition 6.8
.

We say a boundary condition
B
is
A
-elliptically semi-regular
if
B\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
If further
\sigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\ast}\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
, then we say that it is
A
-elliptically regular
.

Remark 6.9
.

If
B
is
A
-elliptically regular, then
B^{*}
is
\tilde{A}
-elliptically regular.

As the following corollary demonstrates, this is a more restricted class of elliptically (semi-)regular boundary conditions.

Corollary 6.10
.

Every
A
-elliptically (semi-)regular boundary condition is elliptically (semi-)regular.

Proof.

From elliptic regularity,
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
.
Therefore, if
B
is elliptically semi-regular, then
B\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and so in particular,
B\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
.
Similarly for
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\ast}
.
∎

An important way in which the
A
-elliptic regularity can be detected in practice is captured in the following.

Proposition 6.11
.

Suppose that
\upsigma_{0}B=B
,
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
and that
\upsigma_{0}A=-A\upsigma_{0}
.
Then
B
is
A
-elliptically regular.

Proof.

Since
B\subset\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
, for
u\in B
, we have that
\chi^{-}(A)u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
We also have that
\upsigma_{0}u\in B
,

\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\ni\chi^{-}(A)\upsigma_{0}u=\upsigma_{0}\chi^{+}(A)u,

where in the equality, we have used that
\upsigma_{0}A=-A\upsigma_{0}
.

To prove that
\chi^{+}(A)u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
, it suffices to show
\upsigma_{0}:\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\to\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
is a Banach space isomorphism.
For that, first note that from
(G3)
, we have that
\upsigma_{0}:{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)\to{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)
is an isomorphism.
Next,

\||A|\upsigma_{0}u\|=\|A\sgn(A)\upsigma_{0}u\|=\|A(-\upsigma_{0}\sgn(A)u)\|=\|\upsigma_{0}|A|u\|\simeq\||A|u\|.

This yields
\upsigma_{0}:\mathrm{dom}(|A|)\to\mathrm{dom}(|A|)
is a Banach space isomorphism.
Therefore, through interpolation, we obtain that
\upsigma_{0}:\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\to\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
is a Banach space isomorphism.
Hence,
\chi^{+}(A)u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
which shows that
B\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

It remains to show that
B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
For that, we first note that by taking adjoints and using selfadjointness of
A
,
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}A=-A\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}
.
As before, a direct interpolation argument allows us to assert that
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\to\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
is a Banach space isomorphism.

Fix
u\in B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
.
By construction, we have that
\chi^{+}(A)u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Using the condition
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
, we have a
v\in B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
such that
u=\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}v
.
Then,
\chi^{-}(A)u=\chi^{-}(A)\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}v=\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}\chi^{+}(A)v\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
since
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}
preserves
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and
\chi^{+}(A)v\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
as
v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.

Since we have shown that
B\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and
B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
, the boundary condition
B
is
A
-elliptically regular.
∎

Remark 6.12
.

Note that both properties
\upsigma_{0}B=B
and
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
are required and this is what contributes to the nontriviality of this statement.
For instance, if
B=0
then

.
The condition
\upsigma_{0}A=-A\upsigma_{0}
means that
\upsigma_{0}:\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\to\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
but
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)\neq\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.
From this, it is easy to see that

is not an
A
-elliptically regular boundary condition.

In the context of “geometric” first-order operators,
A
-elliptically regular boundary conditions imply important approximation properties.
These properties are important in calculations, particularly in determining Fredholm extensions.
Before we present the main approximation result, we note the following lemma.

Lemma 6.13
.

For
\chi\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;\mathbb{R})

\|\chi y\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C^{\prime}\|\chi\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\chi\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}(\partial M)}+C^{2}\|\upsigma_{A}(d\chi)\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}(\partial M;TM)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|y\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})},

(15)

where
C^{\prime}<\infty
is a universal constant.

We obtain this through interpolation, noting that, due to the fact that

is nonnegative selfadjoint,
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})=[{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E),\mathrm{dom}(|A|)]_{\theta=\frac{1}{2}}
.
First,

\|\chi y\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\leq\|\chi\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}(\partial M)}\|y\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}.

Also, we have a constant

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{C_{0}}\|\chi y\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\||A|(\chi y)\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}+\|\chi y\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}

\displaystyle=\|\sgn(A)A(\chi y)\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}+\|\chi y\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}

where in the third line we used the locality of

By interpolation, the norms also interpolate and hence, we obtain (

∎

Proposition 6.14

\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})

First, note that by Corollary

, we have that
{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)=\cap_{k}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{k})
is dense in
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.
Therefore, Proposition
5.1

(i)
is valid on all of
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
and in particular on
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})

Fix

as in the hypothesis.
In order to obtain smoothness up to the boundary for the approximating sequence, we first note that we can find
u^{0}_{k}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\partial M;E)\cap\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})

\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})

For instance,

Now, let

v^{0}_{k}:=\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}(u^{0}_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad v:=\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}(u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}).

By Proposition

(i)
, we have that both

.
Moreover,

and therefore,

.
Therefore, there exists
w_{n}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm cc}(M;E)
such that
w_{n}\to w
in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
.

Moreover, by the properties of
u_{0}^{k}
, we have in addition that
v^{0}_{k}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(M;E)
.
By design, we have that
v^{0}_{k}\to v
in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and also that
v^{0}_{k}{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\to u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
in
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

Fixing a base point
p\in M
, by the hypothesis of metric completeness of
g
, we can find
\chi_{m}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M;[0,1])
with
\chi_{n}=1
on
B_{m}(p)
(where the radius of the geodesic ball is
n
) and
\chi_{m}=0
outside of
B_{10m}
further satisfying:

|\nabla\chi_{m}|_{g}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{m}\quad\text{and}\quad|\chi_{m}-1|\to 0

.

Let
v_{m,n}=\chi_{m}v_{n}^{0}
.
By construction,

.
Moreover,
v_{m,n}{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=\chi_{m}{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}v_{n}^{0}{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=\chi_{m}u_{n}^{0}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|(u-v_{m,n}){{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq\|(\chi_{m}-1)(u_{n}^{0})\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|(u_{n}^{0}-u){{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}

For a fixed

\displaystyle\|(\chi_{m}-1)(u_{n}^{0}){{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|(\chi_{m}-1)\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}(M)}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\chi_{m}-1\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}(\partial M)}+C^{2}\|\nabla\chi_{m}\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}(\partial M;TM)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{n}^{0}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|(\chi_{m}-1)\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}(M)}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\chi_{m}-1\|_{{\rm L}^{\infty}(\partial M)}+C^{2}C^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{n}^{0}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}\to 0,

by applying (

\chi_{m}-1
in place of
\chi
.
Also,
v_{m,n}\to v^{0}_{n}
as

in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and

in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
.
Therefore, through a diagonal argument, we can find a subsequence
v_{n}:=v_{f(n),n}
(where

in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and

in
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

Define

.
Since
v_{n}\to v
and

\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})

in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
.
Now,

This finishes the proof.
∎

Under this assumption, an
A
-elliptically semi-regular boundary conditions further enjoys the following regularity property at the level of the domain of the operator.

Corollary 6.15
.

Assume the hypothesis of Proposition
6.14
.
Then, there exists
T_{d}\in(0,T)
such that for any
A
-elliptically-semi-regular boundary condition
B
and
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
with
\spt u\subset Z_{[0,T_{d})}
, we have

\|u\|+\|\partial_{t}u\|+\|Au\|\lesssim\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})}

where the implicit constant depends on
B
.

Proof.

Let
T_{d}\in(0,T)
be chosen later.
From Lemma
4.4
, for
v\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}

\displaystyle+\|Av\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}

(16)

\displaystyle\leq|\left\langle|A|\sgn(A)v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M},v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}|+\|\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}

\displaystyle\leq\||A|^{\frac{1}{2}}v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}+\|\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}

\displaystyle\leq\||A|^{\frac{1}{2}}v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}.

Now, since
D=\upsigma_{t}(\partial_{t}+A+R_{t})=\upsigma_{t}(\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}+R_{t})
, we can write
\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}=\upsigma_{t}^{-1}D-R_{t}
.
Then, the last term in Equation
16
is:

\displaystyle\|\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T_{d}}\|(\upsigma_{t}^{-1}D-R_{t})v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}\ dt

(17)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{T_{d}}\|Dv\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ dt+C_{1}\int_{0}^{T_{d}}\|R_{t}v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|Dv\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{T_{d}}t^{2}\|Av\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ dt+C_{2}\int_{0}^{T_{d}}\|v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}\ dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|Dv\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+C_{2}T_{d}^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{d}}\|Av\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ dt+C_{2}\|v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d}))}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{C_{1},C_{2}\right\}\left(\|Dv\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+\|v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d}))}}^{2}\right)+C_{2}T_{d}^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{d}}\|Av\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}\ dt.

The constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
depend only on universal constants and the constant
C
from Assumption
(G3)
.

Now, choose
T_{d}<\min\left\{T,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2C_{2}}}\right\}
, so that
C_{2}T_{d}^{2}<\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Then, we find

\|\upsigma_{0}^{-1}D_{0}v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}\leq\max\left\{C_{1},C_{2}\right\}\left(\|Dv\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+\|v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d}))}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|Av\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})}}.

Putting this into Equation
16
and subtracting the term
\frac{1}{2}\|Av\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})}}
from both sides,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}

\displaystyle+\|Av\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+\|v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\||A|^{\frac{1}{2}}v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+3\max\left\{C_{1},C_{2}\right\}\left(\|Dv\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})}}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})}}\right)

In other words, since we assumed
\spt v\subset Z_{[0,T_{d})}
,

\|\partial_{t}v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+\|Av\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+\|v\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})}+\||A|^{\frac{1}{2}}v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}

(18)

Now, fix
u\in B
with
\spt u_{n}\subset Z_{[0,T_{d})}
and let
u_{n}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M)
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
and
u_{n}{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\to u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
in
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\spt u_{n}\subset Z_{[0,T_{d})}
.
As a consequence of Equation

,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+\|Au\|^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}+\|u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Z_{[0,T_{d})})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||A|^{\frac{1}{2}}u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})}

\displaystyle\simeq\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})}\lesssim\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})}^{2},

where we used the isomorphism
\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}=\|u\|_{B}\simeq\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
since

is closed in both spaces.
∎

6.2.
Regularity revisited

In the situation when
\partial M
is compact, given two spectral cuts
r>q
, we saw that
\chi^{-}(A_{r}){\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)\cap\chi^{+}(A_{q}){\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)
is finite dimensional and by elliptic regularity, a subspace of smooth sections.
For selfadjoint
A
, this space is the same as
\chi_{[q,r)}(A){\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)
.
In the present context, with
\partial M
being noncompact in general, it is unlikely that
\chi_{[q,r)}(A){\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)
is finite dimensional.
However, it is worth considering whether this is a smooth subspace of sections, despite potential infinite dimensionality.

Proposition 6.16
.

For any
\alpha\geq 0
and any Borel set
S\subset\mathbb{R}
that is bounded, the subspace
\chi_{S}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\subset{\rm C}^{\infty}(\partial M;E)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
A.12
we obtain

\chi_{S}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\subset\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{dom}(A^{2k}).

By elliptic regularity,

\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{dom}(A^{2k})\subset\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}{\rm H}^{\rm 2k}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)={\rm C}^{\infty}(\partial M;E).\qed

We now consider regularity questions via adapted boundary operators
A
.

Theorem 6.17
.

The spaces
\chi^{\pm}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}

\displaystyle(D_{\max})\cap{\rm H}^{\rm k}_{\rm loc}(M;E)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max}):Du\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-1}_{\rm loc}(M;E)\ \text{with}\ \chi^{+}(A)(u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M})\in{\rm H}^{\rm k-\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)\right\}.

Proof.

By elliptic regularity we have

\chi^{\pm}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E).

To prove the remaining equality, note that from Theorem
1.2
, we need to consider
u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
.
But we established that
\chi^{-}(A)(u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M})\in{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
and so

u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)\ \iff\ \chi^{+}(A)(u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M})\in{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E).\qed

6.3.
Boundary conditions via projectors

In the context of compact boundary, given a classical pseudo-differential projector
P
of order zero, the resulting pseudo-local boundary condition was written as
B:=\overline{P{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}(\partial M;E)}^{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
.
The fact that renders this notion to be well-defined is that pseudo-differential operators of order zero act boundedly on the Sobolev scale.
Unfortunately, in our present situation, we no longer have this luxury.
Nevertheless, we will define the following general class of boundary conditions which capture pseudo-local boundary conditions of the compact setting.

Definition 6.18

(Projection boundary condition)
.

Let
P:\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\to\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}
be a bounded projector, which restricts to a bounded projection on
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Then, we call
B:=\overline{P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
a
projection boundary condition
.

Remark 6.19
.

By definition,
I-P
,
P^{\ast}
and
I-P^{\ast}
all define projection boundary conditions.
That is, they satisfy the condition listed in Definition
6.18
.

Lemma 6.20
.

For a projection boundary condition
B
obtained via a projector
P
,
P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
is dense in
P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}
, i.e.,
u=Pv
for
v\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}
.
Let
v_{n}\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
such that
v_{n}\to v
in
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}
.
Then, defining
u_{n}=Pv_{n}
,

\|u_{n}-u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}=\|Pv_{n}-Pv\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}\lesssim\|v_{n}-v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}\to 0.

By the hypothesis on
P
in Definition
6.18
, we have that
u_{n}\in P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
∎

The following demonstrates an alternative characterisation of this class of boundary conditions, as well as the related adjoint boundary condition.

Proposition 6.21
.

If
B
is a projection boundary condition, then:

(i)

\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\ast}=(I-P^{\ast})\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
, and

(ii)

B=P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
.

Proof.

From Proposition
6.4
, we have that
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\ast}=B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
.
We compute this explicitly in this setting:

\displaystyle B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\quad\forall u\in B\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\quad\forall u\in\overline{P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\quad\forall u\in P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle Pu^{\prime},v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\quad\forall u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\right\}.

(19)

Now, note that for
w\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
, and all
v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
,

\displaystyle\left\langle w,v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\chi^{-}(A)w,\chi^{-}(A)v\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}

(20)

\displaystyle+\left\langle\chi^{+}(A)w,\chi^{+}(A)v\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}.

If further
w\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\chi^{+}(A)w,\chi^{+}(A)v\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\chi^{+}(A)w,\chi^{+}(A)v\right\rangle_{{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)\times{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\chi^{+}(A)w,\chi^{+}(A)v\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}.

Therefore, substituting this back into equation (
20
), we have that

\left\langle w,v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=\left\langle w,v\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}.

Putting this into equation (
19
), we have that

\displaystyle B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle Pu^{\prime},v\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}=0\quad\forall u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\right\}

\displaystyle=(I-P^{\ast})\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A).

This proves
(i)
.

To prove
(ii)
, we note that

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=((I-P^{\ast})\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A))^{\hat{,}\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}=0\quad\forall v\in(I-P^{\ast})\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\text{ and }v\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,(I-P^{\ast})v^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}=0\quad\forall v^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\text{ and }(I-P^{\ast})v^{\prime}\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)\right\}

Note, however, that as noted in Remark
6.19
,
(I-P^{\ast})
also defines a projection boundary condition.
Therefore, on application of Lemma
6.20
, we have that
(I-P^{\ast})\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
is dense in
(I-P^{\ast})\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}
.
Moreover, for
v^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
, we have automatically that
(I-P^{\ast})v^{\prime}\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
, and hence,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,(I-P^{\ast})v^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}=0\quad\forall v^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\text{ and }(I-P^{\ast})v^{\prime}\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,(I-P^{\ast})w\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}=0\quad\forall w\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle(I-P)u,w\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}=0\quad\forall w\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\right\}

\displaystyle=P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\check{\mathrm{H}}(A).\qed

6.4.
Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary condition

Now we consider perhaps the most fundamental boundary condition, originally formulated in the compact setting by Atiyah-Patodi-Singer.

Definition 6.22

(Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary condition)
.

Given an adapted boundary operator
A
, we define the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer boundary condition with respect to this operator to be

B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A):=\chi_{(-\infty,0)}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}).

We define the associated operator as
D_{\mathrm{APS}(A)}:=D_{B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)}
.

Clearly this is a projection boundary condition as we have defined the in the previous section.
In the context of noncompact boundary, we cannot expect the operator
D_{\mathrm{APS}(A)}
to be Fredholm.
Nevertheless, we obtain the following which demonstrates that it has the expected regularity.

Proposition 6.23
.

The APS boundary condition for an adapted operator
A
is
A
-elliptically regular.
In particular, it is elliptically regular.

Proof.

Since, by definition,
B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
, by Remark
6.6
it suffices to show that
B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\subset{\rm H}^{\rm\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm loc}(\partial M;E)
.
We recall that
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)=\chi_{(-\infty,0)}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\oplus\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and compute:

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\ \quad\forall v\in B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A):\left\langle u,\chi^{-}(A)v\right\rangle_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=0\quad\forall v\in B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)\right\}

\displaystyle=[\chi_{(-\infty,0)}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})]^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

\displaystyle=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}).

Since
B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)^{\ast}
by Proposition
6.4
.
Therefore,
B
is
A
-elliptically regular.
By Corollary
6.10
, it is in particular elliptically regular.
∎

Remark 6.24
.

In case
\upsigma_{0}
anticommutes with
A
, the proof shows that the adjoint boundary condition of
B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)
is given by

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)^{*}

\displaystyle=\upsigma_{0}B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=\upsigma_{0}\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})=B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)\oplus\ker(A).

6.5.
Local boundary conditions

Local boundary conditions are of important class of boundary conditions.
In the compact case, they arise as a special case of projection boundary conditions.
The noncompact situation deviates from this, as the pointwise projectors governing such a boundary condition may not have the required decay to be a projection boundary condition.
Nevertheless, we capture the notion of a local boundary condition here and study when they coincide with projection boundary conditions.
In particular, we are motivated to study chiral boundary conditions which we present towards the end of this subsection.

Definition 6.25
.

Let
E^{\prime}\subset E{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
be a smooth subbundle of
E
.
Then

B_{E^{\prime}}:=\overline{{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E^{\prime})}^{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

is called a
local boundary condition
.

Local boundary conditions, as expected, are preserved under multiplication by compactly supported smooth functions.
To show this, we first establish the following lemma.

Lemma 6.26
.

If
\xi\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M)
, then
\xi\mathrm{id}:\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\to\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
boundedly.

Proof.

Let
u\in{\rm D}^{\infty}(A)=\cap_{\alpha}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\alpha})
.
Then, by Proposition
5.1

(i)
, we have that
v:=\mathcal{E}_{T_{c}}u\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and clearly,
v{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=u
.
Let
\tilde{\xi}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M)
such that
\tilde{\xi}{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=\xi
.
Since
|[D,\tilde{\xi}]|\lesssim 1
,
\tilde{\xi}v\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
.
Moreover,
(\tilde{\xi}v){{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=\xi u
.
Putting this together,

\|\xi u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\lesssim\|\tilde{\xi}v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})}\lesssim\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)},

where the first inequality follows from Theorem
1.4

(i)
and the ultimate inequality from Proposition
5.1

(i)
.
∎

Remark 6.27
.

Note that it is unclear how to perform the estimate in Lemma
6.26
directly, since it is not known about the boundedness of the commutator of
\chi^{\pm}(A)
and
\xi
.

Note that local boundary conditions “localise” in the sense that multiplication by compactly supported functions preserve the boundary condition.

Proposition 6.28
.

Let
B
be a local boundary condition.

(i)

If
\xi\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E)
, then
\xi B\subset B
.

(ii)

If
\chi\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M;E)
, then
\chi\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})\subset\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in B
.
By construction, there exists
u_{n}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E^{\prime})
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
and
u_{n}(x)\in E^{\prime}_{x}
for all
x\in\partial M
.
Now,

\|\xi u_{n}-\xi u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=\|\xi(u_{n}-u)\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\lesssim\|u_{n}-u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)},

where the inequality follows from Lemma
6.26
.
Clearly,
\xi u_{n}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E^{\prime})
and
(\xi u_{n})(x)\in E^{\prime}_{x}
for all
x\in\partial M
.
Therefore,
\xi u\in B
.
This proves
(i)
.

To prove
(ii)
, let
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
.
That is,
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and
u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in B
.
Clearly,
\chi u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\max})
and from what we have proved, setting
\xi=\chi{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}
, we have that
(\chi u){{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=\xi u{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}\in B
.
∎

As in the compact boundary case, we obtain the following which provides elliptically regular boundary conditions.

Theorem 6.29
.

Suppose that

Moreover, assume that
\sigma_{A}(x,\xi)
interchanges

.
Furthermore, let

be the manifold given by Corollary

Let

on

By the fact that

is a local boundary condition, using Proposition

\chi u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
when
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
.
Note that if

is an adapted operator for
\tilde{D}
from Corollary

, then
\upsigma_{0}(x,A^{N})=\upsigma_{0}(x,A)
for

.
Letting

Proposition
6.21
yields that

is also a local boundary condition and applying this argument to

The argument for
\overline{(I-P)P\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

In the compact case, local boundary conditions can be seen as a particular case of pseudo-local boundary conditions.
This is due to the fact that a projection defining local boundary conditions are always pseudo-differential operators of order zero and hence bounded on Sobolev scales.
In this present context, that may no longer be the case.
However, the following proposition says that when the bundle projection defines a projection boundary condition, then it agrees with the local boundary condition.

Proposition 6.30
.

Let
E{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=E_{-}\oplus E_{+}
be a bundle splitting and let
P_{\pm}
be the associated bundle projection to
E_{\pm}
along
E_{\mp}
.
Suppose that
P_{\pm}
defines a projection boundary condition.
Then, the projection boundary condition coincides with the local boundary condition with respect to
E_{\pm}
.

Proof.

Let

\tilde{B}_{E_{\pm}}=\overline{\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}:u(x)\in(E_{\pm})_{x}\ \text{almost-everywhere}\right\}}^{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}.

Since
P_{\pm}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
as it defines a projection boundary condition, it is readily verified that

\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}):u(x)\in(E_{\pm})_{x}\right\}=P_{\pm}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}).

Therefore
\tilde{B}_{E_{\pm}}=\overline{P_{\pm}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}^{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
.

It remains to prove that
B_{E_{\pm}}=\tilde{B}_{E_{\pm}}
.
Since
{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E_{\pm})\subset\tilde{B}_{E_{\pm}}
, it is immediate that
B_{E_{\pm}}\subset\tilde{B}_{E_{\pm}}
.

We prove the reverse containment.
Given
u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
with
u(x)\in(E_{\pm})_{x}
, by the density of
{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E)
in
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
, we have
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Since
P_{\pm}
is a smooth bundle projection,
P_{\pm}u_{n}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E_{\pm})
, but also
P_{\pm}u_{n}\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Therefore,

\|P_{\pm}u_{n}-u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}=\|P_{\pm}u_{n}-P_{\pm}u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}=\|P_{\pm}(u_{n}-u)\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}\lesssim\|u_{n}-u\|\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.
Therefore,
{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(\partial M;E_{\pm})\subset\left\{u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}}):u(x)\in{E_{\pm}}_{x}\right\}
is dense in
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and
\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
embeds densely into
\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.
This yields that
\tilde{B}_{E_{\pm}}\subset B_{E_{\pm}}
.
∎

A particular class of local boundary conditions which are of interest are
chiral
boundary conditions.

Definition 6.31
.

A
chirality operator
for
A
is a bundle homomorphism
\Xi\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\End(E{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}))
which satisfies
\Xi^{2}=I
and anticommutes with
A
, i.e.
\Xi A=-A\Xi
.

Since
\Xi
anticommutes with
A
, it also anticommutes with the principal symbol
\sigma_{A}(x,\xi)
for every
\xi\in T_{x}^{*}\partial M
and
x\in\partial M
.
Therefore, the eigenspaces
E_{\pm,x}
of
\Xi_{x}
for the eigenvalues
\pm 1
have the same dimension for all
x\in\partial M
and we obtain a bundle splitting
E|_{\partial M}=E_{+}\oplus E_{-}
.
If
\Xi
is selfadjoint, this splitting is orthogonal.

Proposition 6.32

(Chiral boundary conditions)
.

Let
\Xi
be a chirality operator for
A
and let
E{{\lvert}}_{\partial M}=E_{+}\oplus E_{-}
be the corresponding splitting into eigenbundles of
\Xi
.
Then, the local boundary conditions
B_{E_{\pm}}
are
A
-elliptically regular.

Proof.

Since
\Xi:{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)\to{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)
and
\Xi A=-A\Xi
, by Corollary
A.17
, the fibrewise projectors
P_{\pm}
and
P_{\pm}^{\ast}
define projection boundary conditions.
Using Proposition
6.30
and Proposition
6.21
, we obtain

B_{E_{\pm}}=P_{\pm}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\ \text{ and }\ B_{E_{\pm}}^{\perp}=P_{\mp}^{\ast}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A),

(21)

where we used
(I-P_{\pm}^{\ast})=P_{\mp}^{\ast}
in the second equality.

First, we prove that
B_{E_{\pm}}\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
We automatically have that
\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
whenever
u\in B_{E_{\pm}}
.
For such
u\in B_{E_{\pm}}
, from equation
21
, we have that
u=P_{\pm}u
.
Then,

\displaystyle\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u

\displaystyle=\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)P_{\pm}u

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)(I+\Xi)u

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u\pm\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)\Xi u\right).

Since
\Xi A=-A\Xi
, we have that

\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)\Xi=\Xi\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(-A)=\pm\Xi\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A).

Therefore,

2\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u=\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u\pm\Xi\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)u\iff\Xi\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)u=\pm\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u.

This allows us to conclude that
\Xi\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Now we invoke Proposition
A.16
with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
to obtain that
\Xi:\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\to\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Therefore,

\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\ni\Xi(\Xi\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)u)=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)u,

and hence
B_{E_{\pm}}\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

It remains to show that
B_{E_{\pm}}^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
In this case, choosing an equivalent norm for
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
appropriately, we obtain that
\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)v\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})

for
v\in B_{E_{\pm}}^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
.
Using equation
21
, we have that
v=P_{\mp}^{\ast}v
, and using the fact that
\Xi^{\ast}A=-\Xi^{\ast}A
, we get

\chi_{[0,\infty)(A)}\Xi^{\ast}=\Xi^{\ast}\chi_{[0,\infty)(-A)}=\Xi^{\ast}\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)
.
Mirroring the argument we have just made, we obtain that
\Xi^{\ast}\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Again, by Proposition
A.16
, we have that
\Xi^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\to\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and therefore,

\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\ni\Xi^{\ast}(\Xi^{\ast}\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u)=\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(A)u.

Together, this proves that
B_{E_{\pm}}
are
A
-elliptically regular boundary conditions.
∎

Remark 6.33
.

Note that in Proposition
6.32
, we do not ask for
\Xi
to be selfadjoint.
Since
A
is selfadjoint, the adjoint involution
\Xi^{*}
also anticommutes with

.
Hence,
\Xi^{*}
is a chirality operator for

as well, and Proposition
6.32
applies accordingly.

Remark 6.34
.

There are two distinct assumptions on
\upsigma_{0}
which allow us to identify the adjoint boundary condition.

(1)

If
\upsigma_{0}
commutes with
\Xi
, then the adjoint boundary condition for
B_{E_{\pm}}
are given by

\displaystyle B_{E_{\pm}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\upsigma_{0}(P_{\mp}^{\ast}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A))=P_{\mp}^{\ast}\upsigma_{0}(\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast})\cap\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)

\displaystyle=P_{\mp}^{\ast}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)=B_{\tilde{E}_{\mp}},

where
\tilde{E}_{\pm}
are the
\pm 1
-eigenbundles of
\Xi^{*}
.

(2)

If instead we assume that
\upsigma_{0}
anticommutes with

, then
\tilde{\Xi}:=\upsigma_{0}\Xi^{*}\upsigma_{0}^{-1}
is a chirality operator.
We find

\displaystyle B_{E_{\pm}}^{*}

\displaystyle=\upsigma_{0}(P_{\mp}^{\ast}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A))=\upsigma_{0}P_{\mp}^{\ast}\upsigma_{0}^{-1}\upsigma_{0}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)

\displaystyle=\upsigma_{0}P_{\mp}^{\ast}\upsigma_{0}^{-1}\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\cap\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)=B_{\tilde{E}_{\mp}}

are the

-eigenbundles of

.

6.6.
Matching conditions

In the case of compact boundary, an important boundary condition for the purpose of topological editing and relative index theory is the notion of “matching boundary conditions”.
We phrase this in our noncompact situation also.

The underlying geometric setup to phrase this condition is under the assumption that
\partial M
splits into two components that can be identified with each other, but opposite orientations.
In this case, if
A_{0}
is an adapted boundary operator on the component
(\partial M)_{1}
, then
-A_{0}
is an adapted boundary operator on
(\partial M)_{2}
and
A=A_{0}\oplus(-A_{0})
is an adapted boundary operator on
\partial M
.
Note in this situation,
u=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)=\check{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})
and
v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)=\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})\times\check{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})
.
Therefore,

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=\left\langle u_{1},v_{1}\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})}+\overline{\left\langle v_{2},u_{2}\right\rangle}^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})\times\check{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})}.

(22)

This, along with the matching boundary condition and its regularity, is made precise in the following.

Proposition 6.35
.

Suppose that
\partial M=(\partial M)_{1}\sqcup(\partial M)_{2}
where
(\partial M)_{1}=(\partial M)_{2}=:N
with
A=A_{0}\oplus(-A_{0})
, where
A_{0}
is a selfadjoint adapted boundary operator on
(\partial M)_{1}
.
Let

B_{\mathrm{Match}}:=\left\{(u,u):u\in\mathrm{dom}(|A_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}})\right\}.

Then
B_{\mathrm{Match}}
is an
A
-elliptically regular boundary condition.

Proof.

We compute
B_{\mathrm{Match}}^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
.
First, we note that
B_{\mathrm{Match}}^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}=B_{\mathrm{Match}}^{\perp,\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}\cap\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)
.
This follows from a computation mirroring the proof of Proposition
6.21

(i)
, in particular using (
20
).
Therefore, let
v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in B_{\mathrm{Match}}^{\perp,\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}
.
Then, from (
22
),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\langle(u,u),(v_{1},v_{2})\right\rangle_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u,v_{1}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}+\overline{\left\langle v_{2},u\right\rangle}^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u,v_{1}+v_{2}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})\times\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}}

That is
v_{1}=-v_{2}
.
But since
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)=\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})\times\check{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})
, we have that
v_{1}=-v_{2}\in\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})\cap\check{\mathrm{H}}(A_{0})\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
Therefore,
\upsigma_{0}^{\ast}B^{\ast}\subset\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and by a repetition of this argument, it is easy to see that
B=(B^{\perp,\hat{\mathrm{H}}(A)})^{\perp,\check{\mathrm{H}}(A)}
.
∎

Note that the argument here to establishing the
A
-elliptic regularity here differs from that of the compact boundary case, where the equivalence between elliptic regularity and a
“elliptic” graphical decomposition
of a boundary condition was utilised.
The latter approach has the advantage that this boundary condition can be continuously deformed to the boundary condition
B_{\mathrm{APS}}(A)=\chi^{-}(A)\mathrm{dom}(|A|^{\frac{1}{2}})=\mathrm{dom}(|A_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
It is unlikely that such a graphical decomposition would exist in the noncompact case.

7.
Coercivity and Fredholmness

In the compact boundary case, the study of Fredholm boundary conditions were preceded by a notion of coercivity for the operator.
To some extent, this case can be recovered but this requires extra information on the adapted operator as we will see in Subsection
7.2
.
In the absence of such a condition, we need to instead consider a special class of boundary conditions, which are outlined in Subsection
7.1
.

7.1.
Coercivity through special boundary conditions

We formulate a corresponding notion here, but since we are in the noncompact setting, we also need to take boundary condition also into account.
In the remark following this definition, the relation to the compact case is outlined.

Definition 7.1

(Coercive with respect to a compact set)
.

The operator
D
is said to be
B
-coercive with respect to
K
, a compact set, if:

(i)

there exists
\eta_{K}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M)
with
\eta_{K}=1
on
K
with

\eta_{K}\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})\subset\mathrm{dom}(D_{B});\qquad

(ii)

there is a constant
c>0
such that

\|Du\|\geq c\|u\|

for all
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
with
\spt u\cap K=\emptyset
.

Remark 7.2
.

(a)

If
D
is
B
-coercive with respect to
K
, then a similar conclusion holds for all
u\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\min})
since
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\min})\subset\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
.

(b)

Suppose that
K
is contained in the interior of
M
,
K\subset\mathring{M}
, and
D
satisfies
(ii)
.
Then
\eta_{K}
can be chosen to also have support in
\mathring{M}
, hence
(i)
holds automatically.

(c)

If
B
is a local boundary condition, then
(i)
is automatic for any compact
K
including those that intersect the boundary.

(d)

If
\partial M
is compact, then
\partial M\subset K
can be assumed without loss of generality.
Hence,
(i)
is again automatic.
More generally, if
\partial M
contains a finite number of compact connected components, they can be assumed to be contained in
K
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We prove that if
\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subset\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})
is a bounded sequence (with respect to the graph norm) such that
D_{B}u_{n}\to v\in{\rm L}^{2}(M;E)
, then
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
has a convergent subsequence.
By Proposition A.3 in
[
6
]
, this implies that
\ker(D_{B})
is finite dimensional and
\mathrm{ran}(D_{B})
is closed.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be such a sequence.
Since
\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|_{D}\simeq\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|+\|D(u_{n}-u_{m})\|
with the second summand tending to
0
, it suffices to find a subsequence
u_{n_{k}}
that converges in
{\rm L}^{2}(M;E)
.
Letting
\tilde{K}=\spt(\eta_{K})
, we find

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta_{K}(u_{n}-u_{m})\|+\|(1-\eta_{K})(u_{n}-u_{m})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta_{K}(u_{n}-u_{m})\|+c^{-1}\|D((1-\eta_{K})(u_{n}-u_{m}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta_{K}(u_{n}-u_{m})\|+c^{-1}\|\upsigma_{D}(d\eta_{K})(u_{n}-u_{m})\|+c^{-1}\|(1-\eta_{K})D(u_{n}-u_{m})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\tilde{K})}+\|D(u_{n}-u_{m})\|.

(23)

In the second inequality, we used that
D
is
B
-coercive with respect to
K
.

Since
B
is semi-regular, we have that
\mathrm{dom}(D_{B})\subset{\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm loc}(M;E)
.
In particular, using the boundedness of the sequence
\{u_{n}\}

7.2.
Discrete spectrum

In this subsection, we consider the situation that the adapted operator
A
has discrete spectrum.
This, in particular, allows us to more closely mirror the situation encountered for compact
\partial M
.
More precisely, the coercivity requirement can be placed wholly on the operator and the geometry near the boundary, which allows for a larger class of boundary conditions beyond those studied in subsection
7.1
.

To begin with, we note the following.
We recall that by

, we denote the cylinder

Lemma 7.9
.

Suppose that the adapted operator on the boundary

has discrete spectrum.
For
r>0
fixed, the space

{\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm r}(A,\partial_{t}):=\left\{u\in{\rm L}^{2}(Y_{[0,r);E}:\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\partial M}|\partial_{t}u)(t,x)|^{2}+|Au(t,x)|^{2}\ d\mu_{\tau}(x)\ dt<\infty\right\},

equipped with norm

\|u\|_{{\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm r}(A,\partial_{t})}^{2}=\|u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Y_{[0,r);E}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Y_{[0,r);E}}^{2}+\|Au\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Y_{[0,r);E}}^{2}

embeds compactly into
{\rm L}^{2}(Y_{[0,r)};E)
.

Proof.

First, we note that for
\mathrm{dom}(\partial_{t})={\rm H}^{\rm 1}([0,r))
, we have that
\mathrm{dom}(\sqrt{\partial_{t}^{\ast}\partial_{t}})=\mathrm{dom}(\partial_{t})
.
So let
\tau=\sqrt{\partial_{t}^{\ast}\partial_{t}}
, which is a selfadjoint operator.
Since
{\rm H}^{\rm 1}([0,r))\hookrightarrow{\rm L}^{2}([0,r))
compactly, Theorem
A.13
guarantees us that
\tau
has discrete spectrum.
Let
\mathrm{spec}(\tau)=\left\{\theta_{k}\geq 0\right\}
with orthogonalised eigenfunctions
\left\{t_{i}\right\}
.
Similarly, since
\mathrm{dom}(A)\hookrightarrow{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)
is compact, we have that
\mathrm{spec}(A)=\left\{\lambda_{i}\right\}
with orthonormalised eigensections
\left\{a_{i}\right\}
.
Consequently, given

, we can write
u(t,x)=\sum_{j}u_{j}(t)a_{j}=\sum_{i,j}u_{ij}t_{i}(t)a_{i}(x)
since each

and therefore,
\|u\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(Y_{[0,r)})}^{2}=\sum_{ij}|u_{ij}|^{2}
.

Consider the operator
\Gamma u=(\tau u,Au)
with domain
\mathrm{dom}(\Gamma)={\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm r}(A,\partial_{t})
.
Via the expansion,
\Gamma u=(\sum_{ij}\theta_{i}u_{ij}t_{i}a_{j},\sum_{ij}\lambda_{j}u_{ij}t_{i}a_{j})
.
Now, for
v=(v^{1},v^{2})\in\mathrm{dom}(\Gamma^{\ast})
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\Gamma^{\ast}v,u\right\rangle=\left\langle v,\Gamma u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{kl}v^{1}_{kl}t_{k}a_{l},\sum_{ij}\theta_{i}u_{ij}t_{i}a_{j}\right\rangle+\left\langle\sum_{kl}v^{2}_{kl}t_{k}a_{l},\sum_{ij}\lambda_{j}u_{ij}t_{i}a_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}\theta_{i}v^{1}_{ij}u_{ij}+\sum_{ij}v^{2}_{ij}\lambda_{j}u_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}(\theta_{i}v^{1}_{ij}+\lambda_{j}v^{2}_{ij})u_{ij}.

That is,

From operator theory,

Clearly then, the compactness of the embedding of

Now, for

We claim that this is bounded.
Since

and therefore,
\theta_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{j}^{2}\to\infty
.
Hence, we can find

such that for all

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|R_{\Xi}(\zeta)u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j\leq N}\frac{1}{|\zeta-\theta_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2}|}|u_{ij}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j\leq N}\frac{1}{|\zeta-\theta_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2}|}|u_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{|\zeta-\theta_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2}|}:i,j\leq N\right\}\sum_{i,j<N}|u_{ij}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j\geq N}|u_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\max\left\{\frac{1}{|\zeta-\theta_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2}|}:i,j\leq N\right\}\right)\sum_{ij}|u_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\max\left\{\frac{1}{|\zeta-\theta_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2}|}:i,j\leq N\right\}\right)\|u\|^{2}.

Moreover, by definition, it is clear that
I=R_{\Xi}(\zeta-\Xi)=(\zeta-\Xi)R_{\Xi}
and therefore, we conclude that
\zeta\not\in\mathrm{spec}(\Xi)

Taking the contrapositive, we see that
\zeta\in\mathrm{spec}(\Xi)
implies that
\zeta\in\left\{\theta_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{j}^{2}\right\}
which is a discrete set.
Now, suppose that
0=(\theta_{l}^{2}+\lambda_{m}^{2}-\Xi)u
, which occurs if and only if
0=\sum_{ij}(\theta_{l}^{2}+\lambda_{m}^{2}-\theta_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2})u_{ij}t_{i}a_{j}
.
But since
\tau
and

for only finitely many

and

and therefore, each eigenspace is finite dimensional.
That is,
\Xi
has discrete spectrum.
From Theorem

, we deduce that
{\rm H}^{\rm 1}_{\rm r}(A,\partial_{t})\hookrightarrow{\rm L}^{2}(Y_{[0,r)};E)
compactly.
∎

8.
Dirac-type operators

Dirac-type operators are undoubtedly the most important first-order elliptic operators in geometry.
In this section we derive geometric conditions that ensure that the results of the present work can be applied to them.

Let
D
be a Dirac-type operator with respect to a Riemannian metric on
M
, i.e. its principal symbol satisfies the Clifford relation
\sigma_{D}(\xi)^{*}\sigma_{D}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2}\cdot\mathrm{id}
for all covectors
\xi
.
This implies
|\sigma_{D}(\xi)|=|\xi|
and, in particular,
D
is elliptic.

Moreover, if the Riemannian metric is complete, Theorem
2.1
ensures that
D
and
D^{\dagger}
are complete.
Choosing
\vec{T}
as the interior pointing unit normal vector field and
\tau
the associated covector field, we are in the minimal setup.
Thus, the conditions of the minimal setup are satisfied for any Dirac-type operator if the underlying Riemannian metric is complete.
In particular, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
apply.

8.1.
Twisted spinorial Dirac operators

Ensuring conditions
(G1)
–
(G3)
of the geometric setup is more subtle.
We specialise to twisted spinorial Dirac operators.
For this, we assume that
M
carries a spin structure.
Let
SM\to M
be the associated spinor bundle, equipped with its natural Hermitian metric and connection
\nabla^{SM}
.
Let
C\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle with compatible connection
\nabla^{C}
.
The twisted spinorial Dirac operator
D
maps sections of
SM\otimes C
to sections of
SM\otimes C
.
It is a formally selfadjoint Dirac-type operator and, in particular, elliptic.

The Weitzenböck formula for twisted spinorial Dirac operators says

D^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\tfrac{1}{4}S+\mathscr{K}^{C}

(24)

where
S
is the scalar curvature of
M
and
\mathscr{K}^{C}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i}\cdot e_{j}\cdot R^{C}(e_{i},e_{j})
, see Theorem 8.17 in Chapter II of
[
13
]
.
Here
R^{C}
denotes the curvature tensor of
C
, the dot
\cdot
denotes Clifford multiplication, and
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
is a local orthonormal tangent frame.

If
n=\dim(M)
is odd, we put
E=F=SM\otimes C
.
If
n
is even, then the spinor bundle splits into the chirality subbundles,
SM=S^{+}M\oplus S^{-}M
, and the Dirac operator interchanges these bundles.
In this case we put
E=S^{+}M\otimes C
and
F=S^{-}M\otimes C
.

In both cases,
E|_{\partial M}
and
F|_{\partial M}
can be canonically identified with the twisted spinor bundle
S\partial M\otimes C
of
\partial M
.
The intrinsic twisted Dirac operator
D^{\partial M}
of the boundary is one possible choice of adapted boundary operator.
Thus, a general adapted boundary operator will be of the form
A=D^{\partial M}+V
where
V
is a symmetric endomorphism field of
E|_{\partial M}
.
We always choose
\tilde{A}=A
.

We now list geometric assumptions which ensure that we are in the geometric setup so that all the results obtained in the previous sections can be applied.

Assumptions 8.1
.

Given a Hermitian vector bundle
C
with compatible connection over a Riemannian manifold
M
, the following assumptions will be useful:

(A1)

The focal radius of
\partial M
is positive, i.e. there exists an
r_{0}>0
such that the normal exponential map induces a diffeomorphism
[0,r_{0})\times\partial M\to U
where

is a neighbourhood of
\partial M
in
M
.

(A2)

The pointwise norms of the second fundamental forms of
\partial M
and of its parallel hypersurfaces

are uniformly bounded for small

.

(A3)

The pointwise norm of the Ricci curvature tensor of
M
is uniformly bounded on a distance tube of
\partial M
.

(A4)

The curvature tensor

of

is uniformly bounded on a distance tube of
\partial M
.

(A5)

The potential

, i.e. there exists

Note that these assumptions are automatic if

\displaystyle D=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{j}\cdot\nabla_{e_{j}}+\vec{T}\cdot\nabla_{\vec{T}}\quad\text{ and }\quad A=-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\vec{T}\cdot e_{j}\cdot\nabla_{e_{j}}+\tfrac{1}{2}H+V

As to

Since the principal symbol of a Dirac-type operator is given by Clifford multiplication which is an isometry if the covector has length

\exp(-r_{0}C_{1})\leq|\sigma_{t}|\leq\exp(r_{0}C_{1})

.
This identification is a pointwise isometry.
We study how the Dirac operator changes along such an integral curve.
Fix

of

Then

We extend the basis by parallel translation along the integral curve of

The parallel extension of

The Dirac operator then takes the form

Thus the remainder term in

\displaystyle R_{t}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{t}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{j}(t)\cdot\nabla_{e_{j}(t)}-\sigma_{0}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{j}(0)\cdot\nabla_{e_{j}(0)}

The second equation follows from

since Clifford multiplication is parallel, and hence
\sigma_{t}^{-1}=f\sigma_{0}^{-1}
.
Since
\sigma_{0}
is an isometry, we only need to estimate the expression in parentheses.
Using that the

and Clifford multiplication are parallel we compute for

\displaystyle\nabla_{\vec{T}}\bigg(f\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{j}\cdot\nabla_{e_{j}}u\bigg)=fH\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{j}\cdot\nabla_{e_{j}}u+f\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{j}\cdot\nabla_{\vec{T}}\nabla_{e_{j}}u.

Writing

Denote by

We find

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}e_{j}\cdot\nabla_{\vec{T}}\nabla_{e_{j}}u

Now we use Assumption

(29)

on

Combining (

(30)

Recall that the adapted boundary operator is given by

This, together with (

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\vec{T}}R_{t}u|

This gives us

Proposition 8.10
.

Let

(M5)

Proof.

Since the metric is assumed to be complete,

, as as their principal symbols coincide.

It remains to show that the remainder terms are appropriately controlled in terms of

Remark 8.17

In the literature, the condition that is used to call the operator

Appendix A
Auxiliary functional analytic tools

A.1.
Czech spaces for selfadjoint operators

Here we collect some functional analytic tools which were used in the main body of the paper.
Throughout this section let
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space and
T
be a (generally unbounded) selfadjoint operator on
\mathscr{H}
.
By the Borel functional calculus we are able to construct bounded projectors
\chi_{S}(T)
, where
S\subset\mathbb{R}
is a Borel set and
\chi_{S}
its characteristic function.
Let us write
\chi^{+}(T)=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(T)
and
\chi^{-}(T)=\chi_{(-\infty,0)}(T)
and define
|T|:=T\sgn(T)
, where
\sgn(T)=\chi^{+}(T)-\chi^{-}(T)
.
Also, write
S_{\mu+}^{o}
to be the open sector of angle
\mu
centred at the origin which is symmetric about the positive real line.
Let
S_{\mu+}
be the closure of
S_{\mu+}^{o}
.
We say
\psi\in\Psi(S_{\mu+}^{o})
if it is holomorphic on
S_{\mu+}^{o}
and there exists some
\alpha>0
and
C>0
with
|\psi(\zeta)|\leq C\min\left\{|\zeta|^{\alpha},|\zeta|^{-\alpha}\right\}
.

Proposition A.1
.

The following hold:

(i)

\chi_{I}(T)
is a bounded selfadjoint projector,

(ii)

\mathrm{dom}(T)=\mathrm{dom}(|T|)
,

(iii)

|T|\geq 0
and selfadjoint,

(iv)

T=|T|\sgn(T)
,

(v)

there is a splitting
\mathscr{H}=\mathrm{ker}(|T|)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\perp}}{{\oplus}}\overline{\mathrm{ran}(|T|)}=\mathrm{ker}(T)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\perp}}{{\oplus}}\overline{\mathrm{ran}(T)}
,

(vi)

|T|
admits an
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus: for every
\mu>0
and nonzero
\psi\in\Psi(S_{\mu+}^{o})
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\psi(t|T|)u\|^{2}\ \frac{dt}{t}\simeq\|u\|^{2}

for
u\in\overline{\mathrm{ran}(|T|)}
.

Proof.

Assertions
(i)
–
(v)
are immediate consequences from Borel functional calculus.
Using condition
(iii)
, and utilising Corollary 7.1.6 in
[
12
]
we obtain that
|A|
has a
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus for each
\mu>0
.
Since
(v)
gives us that
|A|{{\lvert}}_{\overline{\mathrm{ran}(|A|)}}
is injective, the McIntosh Theorem, Theorem 7.3.1 in
[
12
]
, furnishes us with the required estimate in
(vi)
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

For
\alpha>0
, the spaces
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
are Hilbert, and hence reflexive.
Any dense subset
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{H}
is also dense in
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
, the dual space of
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
.

Proof.

To see that
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
are Hilbert, note that
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}=\left\langle u,v\right\rangle+\left\langle|T|^{\alpha}u,|T|^{\alpha}v\right\rangle
induces the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}
.

For
v\in\mathscr{C}
, let
F_{v}(u)=\left\langle v,u\right\rangle
, where
u\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
, which yields that
F_{v}\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
.
Take
\Lambda=\left\{F_{v}:v\in\mathscr{C}\right\}
and let
\overline{\Lambda}
be the closure of this set in
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
.
Now, suppose that
\xi\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\setminus\overline{\Lambda}
and by the Hahn-Banach theorem, let
l\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast\ast}
such that
l(\xi)\neq 0
and
l{{\lvert}}_{\overline{\Lambda}}\equiv 0
.
By reflexivity of
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
, there exists
f_{l}\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
such that
l(\xi^{\prime})=\xi^{\prime}(f_{l})
for all
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
.
But then,
0=l(F_{v})=F_{v}(f_{l})=\left\langle v,f_{l}\right\rangle
for all
v\in\mathscr{C}
which is a dense subset of
\mathscr{H}
and therefore, we get that
f_{l}=0
.
This yields a contradiction on recalling
0\neq l(\xi)=\xi(f_{l})
.
∎

As we saw in the proof of Lemma
A.2
, the graph norm on
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
carries an inhomogeneous term.
In calculations, it can be cumbersome to carry this inhomogeneity.
Therefore, we instead consider the the operator
|T|_{\varepsilon}=|T|+\varepsilon I
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
In the following lemma, we show that this operator is invertible and the homogeneous norm of
|T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
is equivalent (up to a constant) to the usual graph norm on
|T|^{\alpha}
.
Moreover, we will see that it provides us with a way of computing the norm on
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
.

Lemma A.3
.

The operator
|T|_{\varepsilon}>0
and in particular invertible.
Moreover, it has an
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus.
For
\alpha\geq 0
,
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})=\mathrm{dom}(|T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha})
with estimate

\||T|^{\alpha}\|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}\simeq\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\|^{2}.

The inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
on
\mathscr{H}
extends to a linear pairing between
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
and
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
.
For all
u\in\mathscr{H}\subset\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
, we have that
\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{-\alpha}u\|\simeq\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
|T|_{\varepsilon}>0
and invertible.
This operator has
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}
-functional calculus by Corollary 7.1.6 in
[
12
]
and by Theorem 6.6.9 in
[
12
]
.
Through interpolation theory, we obtain
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})=\mathrm{dom}(|T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha})
with the norm estimate
\||T|^{\alpha}u\|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}\simeq\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\|
.

Next, we show that
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
extends to a perfect pairing between
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
and
\mathrm{dom}(|T|)^{\ast}
.
Let
f\in\mathscr{H}
and note that

\|f\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}=\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}\frac{|f(v)|}{\|v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}}=\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}\frac{|\left\langle f,v\right\rangle|}{\|v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}}.

For
v\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
, letting
F_{v}\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast\ast}
with
\|v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}=\|F_{v}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast\ast}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|F_{v}\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast\ast}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq w\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|)^{\ast}}\frac{|F_{v}(w)|}{\|w\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq w\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|)^{\ast}}\frac{|w(v)|}{\|w\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq f\in\mathscr{H}}\frac{|f(v)|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq f\in\mathscr{H}}\frac{|\left\langle f,v\right\rangle|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}}.

On combining these two calculations, along with the fact that
\mathrm{dom}(|T|)
is dense in both
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
and
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
and contained in
\mathscr{H}
, we see that
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})\times\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\to\mathbb{C}
, extended by the
\mathscr{H}
-inner product, is a perfect pairing.

Lastly, we show that
\||T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u\|\simeq\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}
when
u\in\mathscr{H}
.
Using the fact that
\||T|^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}v\|\simeq\|v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}
, and since
|T|^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}
is invertible, for every
v\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
there exists
w\in\mathscr{H}
with
|T|^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}v=w
, we get

\displaystyle\||T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq w\in\mathscr{H}}\frac{|\left\langle|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u,w\right\rangle|}{\|w\|}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}\frac{|\left\langle|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u,|T|^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}v\right\rangle|}{\||T|^{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}v\|}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}\frac{|\left\langle u,v\right\rangle|}{\|v\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}}

\displaystyle\simeq\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}.\qed

Remark A.4
.

As a consequence of Lemma
A.2
,
\mathscr{H}
itself is a dense subset in
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
, and therefore, we see that

extends boundedly to

.
Therefore, for

\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}}\simeq\||T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\||T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u_{n}\|,

where
\mathrm{dom}(T)\ni u_{n}\to u
.

Lemma A.5
.

For all
\alpha\geq 0
,
\chi_{I}(T):\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
is a bounded projector.
By duality, this extends to a bounded projector

\chi_{I}(T):\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
for

.
In particular, this holds for
\chi^{\pm}(T)
.

Proof.

We have already noted that
\chi_{I}(T):\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
boundedly.
Next, from Lemma
A.3
, we have that
\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}\simeq\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}u\|
, and thus,

\|\chi_{I}(T)u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}\simeq\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\chi_{I}(T)u\|\simeq\|\chi_{I}(T)|T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}u\|\lesssim\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}u\|\simeq\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})}.

Now, by duality, we have that
\chi_{I}(T)^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
but since
\chi_{I}(T)^{\ast}=\chi_{I}(T)
on
\mathscr{H}
and since
\mathscr{H}
is dense in
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}

∎

Define for

,

{\mathcal{H}}^{k}(T)=\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{k})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{H}}^{-k}(T)={\mathcal{H}}^{k}(T)^{\ast}.

Recalling
\chi^{-}=\chi_{(-\infty,0)}
and
\chi^{+}=\chi_{[0,\infty)}
, define the space:

\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)=\chi^{-}(T){\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)\oplus\chi^{+}(T){\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(T).

(38)

with norm

\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)}^{2}:=\|\chi^{-}(T)u\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)}+\|\chi^{+}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(T)}.

(39)

Also, define
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(T):=\check{\mathrm{H}}(-T)
.

Lemma A.6
.

(i)

If
B\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)
is a subspace that is closed in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)
and in
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)
, then
\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)}\simeq\|u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)}
.

(ii)

Let
\mathscr{C}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)
be a subspace such that
\chi^{\pm}(T)\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{C}
.
Suppose that it dense in both
{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)
and in
\mathscr{H}
.
Then,
\mathscr{C}
is dense in
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)
.

Proof.

For Assertion
(i)
, we first show that
\chi^{+}(T){\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)\hookrightarrow\chi^{+}(T){\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(T)
is a dense embedding.
Using Lemma
A.3
,

\|\chi^{+}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(T)}\simeq\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\chi^{+}(T)u\|\simeq\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\chi^{+}(T)|T|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|\lesssim\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi^{+}(T)u\|\simeq\|\chi^{+}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)}.

Therefore, for
u\in B
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)}

\displaystyle\simeq\|\chi^{-}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)}+\|\chi^{+}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi^{-}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)}+\|\chi^{+}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)}\simeq\|u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)}\simeq\|u\|_{B}.

Since
(B,\|\cdot\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)})
and
(B,\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)})
are Banach spaces, we have that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)}
are equivalent on
B
.

Now we prove Assertion
(ii)
.
By Lemma
A.2
,
\mathscr{C}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)
is a dense subspace
{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(T)
.
Therefore, there exist
w_{n}\in\mathscr{C}
such that
v_{n}\to\chi^{-}(T)u
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)
and
w_{n}\to\chi^{+}(T)u
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(T)
.
It is easy to see that
\chi^{-}(T)v_{n}\to\chi^{-}(T)u
and
\chi^{+}(T)w_{n}\to\chi^{+}(T)u
.
Moreover,
\chi^{-}(T)v_{n},\chi^{+}(T)w_{n}\in\mathscr{C}
by assumption and therefore,
u_{n}:=\chi^{-}(T)v_{n}+\chi^{+}(T)w_{n}\in\mathscr{C}
and it is immediate that
u_{n}\to u
in
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{E}:\mathscr{H}\to([0,\infty)\mapsto\mathscr{H})
be defined by

(\mathcal{E}u)(t):=\exp(-t|T|)u.

Moreover, define the following space

{\rm D}^{\infty}(T):=\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{k}).

Lemma A.7
.

For all
l,m\geq 0
, The operator
\mathcal{E}
extends to a map
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{l})^{\ast}\to((0,\infty)\mapsto\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{m}))
linear for each
t>0
with
\mathrm{ran}(\mathcal{E})\subset{\rm C}^{\infty}((0,\infty);{\rm D}^{\infty}(T))
.
If

, then

\mathcal{E}u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}([0,\infty);{\rm D}^{\infty}(T)).

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in(0,\infty)
, consider
f_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f_{k}(x)=(x+\varepsilon)^{k}\exp(-t|x+\varepsilon|)
.
Clearly,
f_{k}\in{\rm C}^{0}\cap{\rm L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and therefore,
\|f_{k}(T)v\|\lesssim\|v\|
for
v\in\mathscr{H}
.
For
u\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{l})
,

\|f_{k}(T)u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{m})}=\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{m+k+l}\exp(-t|T|_{\varepsilon})|T|_{\varepsilon}^{-l}u\|\lesssim\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{-l}u\|_{\mathscr{H}}\simeq\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{l})^{\ast}}.

By Lemma
A.2
, since
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{l})
is dense in
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{l})^{\ast}
, we have that
\mathcal{E}
extends to a mapping on
\mathrm{dom}(T)^{\ast}
.
Moreover, we see that
\exp(-t|T|_{\varepsilon}):\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{l})^{\ast}\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{m})
boundedly.
Since
m
is arbitrary,

\mathrm{ran}(\exp(-t|T|_{\varepsilon}))\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{k})={\rm D}^{\infty}(T).

The map
(0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\exp(-t|T|_{\varepsilon})u

Now, for
u\in{\rm D}^{\infty}(T)
, for each

\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathcal{E}u)(t)=(-1)^{k}|T|^{k}(\mathcal{E}u)(t)=(-1)^{k}(\mathcal{E}|T|^{k}u)(t).

Since

, we obtain that the trace
\lim_{t\to 0}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathcal{E}u)(t)

This shows that
\mathcal{E}u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}([0,\infty);{\rm D}^{\infty}(T))
.
∎

Corollary A.8
.

For every
l\geq 0
, the subspace

.

Proof.

Note that restricting
\mathcal{E}
to

.

∎

Corollary A.9
.

The subspace
{\rm D}^{\infty}(T)\subset\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)

We note that for each
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{k})
, we have that it is dense in
\mathrm{dom}(T)
and also in
\mathscr{H}
.
Moreover,
\chi^{\pm}(T)\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{k})\subset\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{k})
.

3.4
, we obtain the conclusion for
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)
.
Since
\hat{\mathrm{H}}(T)=\check{\mathrm{H}}(-T)
, by application of this to

Lemma A.10
.

The
\mathscr{H}
inner product extends to a perfect pairing
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)\times\hat{\mathrm{H}}(T)\to\mathbb{C}
.
Therefore, this induces an isomorphism
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)^{\ast}\cong\hat{\mathrm{H}}(T)
.

Proof.

An argument similar to Proposition 5.1 in
[
7
]
, using that
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
extends to a linear pairing between
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
and
\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
by Lemma
A.3
, and using the fact that projectors preserve reflexivity, shows that the
\mathscr{H}
inner product extends to a pairing

and that these spaces are dual to each other.
∎

For
r\in\mathbb{R}
, let
T_{r}:=T-r
and note that by construction,
{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(T_{r})={\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(T)
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
In the following, we show that for any two
q,r\in\mathbb{R}
, the spaces
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T_{r})\simeq\check{\mathrm{H}}(T_{q})
with equivalence of norms.
The argument here proceeds differently to that of
[
7
]
, since we may have continuous spectrum than just pure point spectrum.
Nevertheless, as the proof illustrates, we can compensate by resorting to the Borel functional calculus.

Proposition A.11
.

For
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T_{r})=\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)
as sets with equivalence of norms.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
0<r
.
Note first that, via functional calculus,

\chi^{+}(T-r)=\chi_{[r,\infty)]}(T)\quad\text{and}\quad\chi^{-}(T-r)=\chi_{(-\infty,r)}(T).

(40)

Fix
u\in{\rm D}^{\infty}(T)
, which is a dense subset of both
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T_{r})
and
\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)
by Lemma
A.3
.
Then,
u=\chi^{-}(T)u+\chi^{+}(T)
and arguing as in the proof of Proposition 5.2 in
[
7
]
and using (
40
) we have that
\chi^{-}(T_{r})u=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(T)u+\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u.

Also,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(T_{r})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\chi^{-}(T_{r})u\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\chi^{+}(T_{r})u\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\|\chi^{-}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|\chi^{+}(T_{r})\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Moreover, on writing
u=\chi^{-}(T_{r})u+\chi^{+}(T_{r})u
and via a similar calculation, we find that

\|u\|_{\check{\mathrm{H}}(T)}\simeq\|\chi^{-}(T)u\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|\chi^{+}(T_{r})u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

Therefore, we are reduced to showing that
\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\simeq\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Fixing an
\varepsilon>0
and using Lemma
A.3
, we obtain that

\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\simeq\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u\|=\||T|_{\varepsilon}|T|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u\|.

Now, note that
v=|T|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u\in\mathrm{ran}(\chi_{[0,r)}(T))
, and that
f(t)=(|t|+\varepsilon)\chi_{[0,r)}(t)>\varepsilon
is a bounded Borel function.
Therefore,
f(T)=|T|_{\varepsilon}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)
is a bounded self adjoint operator on
\mathscr{H}
.
Also, for
w\in\mathscr{H}
,

\left\langle f(T)w,w\right\rangle=\left\langle|T|_{\varepsilon}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)w,w\right\rangle=\left\langle\chi_{[0,r)}(T)|T|_{\varepsilon}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)w,w\right\rangle\\
=\left\langle|T|_{\varepsilon}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)w,\chi_{[0,r)}(T)\right\rangle\geq\varepsilon\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)w\|^{2}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality on
\left\langle f(T)w,w\right\rangle
and on noting that
f(T)\chi_{[0,r)}(T)w=f(T)w
, we obtain

\varepsilon\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)w\|\leq\|f(T)w\|\leq\|f(T)\|_{\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}}\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)w\|
.
Therefore, on noting that
w=v=\chi_{[0,r)}(T)v
,

\||T|_{\varepsilon}|T|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u\|\simeq\||T|_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u\|\simeq\|\chi_{[0,r)}(T)u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}},

which is the required estimate to establish the claim.
∎

Proposition A.12
.

For any
\alpha\geq 0
and any Borel set
S\subset\mathbb{R}
that is bounded,

\chi_{S}(T)\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\subset\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{dom}(T^{2k}).

Proof.

Drawing inspiration from the proof of Proposition
A.11
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
define
f_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f_{k}(x):=x^{2k}\chi_{S}(x).

Since
S
is bounded, clearly there exists a constant
C_{k,S}<\infty
dependent on
k
and
I
such that
|f_{k}(x)|\leq C_{k,S}
.
Therefore, by the Borel functional calculus, we have that

\|f_{k}(T)u\|\lesssim\|u\|

for all
u\in{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)
.
However,
f_{k}(T)u=T^{2k}\chi_{S}(T)u
, which means that
\chi_{S}(T)u\in\mathrm{dom}(T^{2k})
.

Now, from Remark
A.4
, we have that
|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}:\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\to{\rm L}^{2}(\partial M;E)
is bounded.
Moreover, by functional calculus,
\chi_{S}(T)|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u=|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{S}(T)u
and hence, for
u\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
,

\displaystyle\||T|^{2k-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{S}(T)u\|

\displaystyle=\||T|^{2k}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{S}(T)|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u\|

\displaystyle\simeq\||T|^{2k}\chi_{S}(T)\chi_{S}(T)|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u\|+\|\chi_{S}(T)|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}u\|

\displaystyle=\|f_{k}(T)|T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{S}(T)u\|+\||T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{S}(T)u\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\||T|^{-\alpha}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{S}(T)u\|

\displaystyle\simeq\|\chi_{S}(T)u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}},

where in the second norm equivalence, we have used
\chi_{S}(T)^{2}=\chi_{S}(T)
.
Therefore,

\chi_{S}(T)u\in\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{2k-\alpha})\subset\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{2k-2})=\mathrm{dom}(T^{2(k-1)})

when
k>1
.
∎

A.2.
Abstract Rellich theory

Theorem A.13
.

Let
T
be a selfadjoint operator on a separable Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

T
has discrete spectrum.

(ii)

The embedding
\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{s})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{t})
is compact for some
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
s>t\geq 0
.

(iii)

The embedding
\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{s})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{t})
is compact for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
s>t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
T
unbounded.
Indeed, if
T
is bounded, then each of the assertions
(i)
–
(iii)
is equivalent to
\mathscr{H}
being finite dimensional.

It is immediate that
(iii)
implies
(ii)
.
We show that
(ii)
implies
(i)
.
So, assume
(ii)
and note that

\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{s})\xhookrightarrow{\mathrm{compact}}\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{t})\xhookrightarrow{\mathrm{continuous}}\mathscr{H},

and therefore we have that
(1+T^{2})^{-s}:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
is a compact map.
Hence
(1+T^{2})^{-s}
has discrete spectrum except for the accumulation point
0
.
Since
s>0
it follows that
1+T^{2}
and hence
T
has discrete spectrum.

It remains to show that
(i)
implies
(iii)
.
Let
T
have discrete spectrum.
To show that
\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{s})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{t})
is compact, let
u_{k}\in\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{s})
be a bounded sequence,

\|u_{k}\|_{\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{s})}\leq C.

We need to find a subsequence which converges in
\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{t})
.
Let
\lambda_{j}
be the eigenvalues of
T
and
\phi_{j}
its orthonormalised eigenvectors.
We write
u_{k}=\sum_{j}u_{k,j}\phi_{j}
.
For each fixed
j
we have

(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{2s}|u_{k,j}|^{2}\leq\|u_{k}\|_{\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{s})}^{2}\leq C^{2}

and hence

|u_{k,j}|\leq C.

By a diagonal argument, we can pass to a subsequence, again denoted
(u_{k})
, such that for each
j

u_{k,j}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}w_{j}.

We show that
(u_{k})
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{t})
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Since the spectrum is discrete and
T
is unbounded there exists
j_{0}
such that

|\lambda_{j}|\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}

for all
j>j_{0}
.
For these
j
we then have

(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{t-s}\leq|\lambda_{j}|^{2(t-s)}\leq\varepsilon^{2(s-t)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j>j_{0}}(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{2t}|u_{k,j}-u_{\ell,j}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j>j_{0}}(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{2(t-s)}(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{2s}|u_{k,j}-u_{\ell,j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{4(s-t)}\sum_{j\leq j_{0}}(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{2s}|u_{k,j}-u_{\ell,j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{4(s-t)}\|u_{k}-u_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{s})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{4(s-t)}\cdot 4C^{2}.

Choose
m
so large that such that we have for all (finitely many)
j\leq j_{0}

(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{2t}|u_{k,j}-u_{\ell,j}|^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{j_{0}}

whenever
k,\ell\geq m
.
Then we find for such
k
and
\ell
that

\displaystyle\|u_{k}-u_{\ell}\|_{\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{t})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq j_{0}}(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{2t}|u_{k,j}-u_{\ell,j}|^{2}+\sum_{j>j_{0}}(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})^{2t}|u_{k,j}-u_{\ell,j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{4(s-t)}\cdot 4C^{2}.

This shows that
(u_{k})
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathrm{dom}((1+T^{2})^{\frac{t}{2}})
and hence converges.
∎

.

A.3.
Involutions

Let
\Xi:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
be a bounded
involution
, i.e., a bounded operator with
\Xi^{2}=I
.
We recall some basic facts, in particular when such a
\Xi
interacts with a selfadjoint operator
T
.

Lemma A.15
.

Let
\Xi:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
be a bounded involution. Then:

(i)

\Xi^{\ast}:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
is also a bounded involution.

(ii)

\mathrm{spec}(\Xi)=\left\{\pm 1\right\}
.

(iii)

P_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(I\pm\Xi)
define bounded projectors to eigenspaces corresponding to
\pm 1
so that inducing a splitting
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{+}\oplus\mathscr{H}_{-}
where
\mathscr{H}_{\pm}=P_{\pm}\mathscr{H}
.

Proof.

The first assertion
(i)
is immediate.
For
(ii)
, note that
\Xi^{2}=1\iff(\Xi-1)(\Xi+1)=0
and therefore, we get
\left\{\pm 1\right\}\subset\mathrm{spec}(\Xi)
.
The reverse inclusion follows from noting that
(\Xi-\lambda)^{-1}=(1+\lambda^{2})^{-1}(\Xi+\lambda)
.
The last assertion
(iii)
is readily verified.
∎

Due to
(i)
, the assertions
(i)
and
(ii)
are valid on replacing
\Xi
by
\Xi^{*}
.

Proposition A.16
.

Let
T:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
be a selfadjoint operator and
\Xi:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
a bounded involution satisfying
\Xi T=-T\Xi
.
Then,
\Xi,\Xi^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
boundedly and restrict to bounded operators
\Xi,\Xi^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
and

Proof.

We first prove that
\Xi:\mathrm{dom}(|T|)\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|)
boundedly.
For this, note that

\displaystyle\|\Xi u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|)}

\displaystyle\simeq\||T|\Xi u\|+\|u\|=\|T\Xi u\|+\|u\|

\displaystyle=\|-\Xi Tu\|+\|u\|\lesssim\|Tu\|+\|u\|\simeq\|u\|_{\mathrm{dom}(|T|)}.

By taking adjoints in
\Xi T=-T\Xi
, we obtain that
\Xi^{\ast}T=-T\Xi^{\ast}
.
Therefore, replacing the previous argument with
\Xi^{\ast}
in place of
\Xi
, we obtain that
\Xi^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|T|)\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|)
.
Through run of the mill interpolation for selfadjoint operators, we obtain that
\Xi,\Xi^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})\to\mathrm{dom}{|T|^{\alpha}}
for
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
On dualising this, we obtain the remaining assertion.
∎

As a direct consequence, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary A.17
.

Under the hypothesis of Proposition
A.16
, the projectors
P_{\pm},P_{\pm}^{\ast}:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}
extend to bounded projectors
P_{\pm},P_{\pm}^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})^{\ast}
and restrict to bounded projectors
P_{\pm},P_{\pm}^{\ast}:\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})\to\mathrm{dom}(|T|^{\alpha})
for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

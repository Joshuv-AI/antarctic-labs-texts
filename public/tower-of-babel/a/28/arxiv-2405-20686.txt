From pre-Lie bialgebras to phase spaces of Lie algebras: a categorical correspondence
Qi Wang, Xueyi Wang, Jiefeng Liu

Abstract
. This paper establishes a categorical framework for phase spaces of Lie algebras, pre-Lie bialgebras, Manin triples, classical 𝔰 \mathfrak{s} -matrices, and relative Rota-Baxter operators by introducing the concept of coherent homomorphisms. Starting with endo pre-Lie algebras (pre-Lie algebras equipped with endomorphisms), we extend classical constructions to this enhanced setting, which leads to the notion of coherent endomorphisms for each class of structures. Through polarization, these endomorphisms naturally generalize to coherent homomorphisms, establishing well-defined categories of these algebraic objects. Furthermore, mappings between categories are elevated to functors or equivalences, formalizing interconnections among the constructions. Finally, exploiting the categorical correspondence between s-matrices and relative Rota-Baxter operators, we develop cohomology and deformation of 𝔰 \mathfrak{s} -matrices, thereby bridging algebraic structures with category-theoretic methods.

Introduction

This paper presents a categorical investigation of phase spaces, pre-Lie bialgebras, Manin triples, classical
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices, and relative Rota-Baxter operators through the systematic introduction of their respective morphisms. By demonstrating that these morphisms preserve natural correspondences among these classes, we elevate the interrelations between these structures to the level of categorical correspondences. This work bridges algebraic structure theory with category-theoretic methods, demonstrating how categorical perspectives can systematically organize and deepen understanding of classical algebraic constructions.

1.1.
Phase spaces of Lie algebras, pre-Lie bialgebras and the related structures

Pre-Lie algebras (or left-symmetric algebras) are a class of nonassociative algebras coming from the study of convex homogeneous cones, affine manifolds and affine structures on Lie groups, deformation of associative algebras and then appeared in many fields in mathematics and mathematical physics, such as complex and symplectic structures on Lie groups and Lie algebras

1.2.
Morphisms of bialgebras, Manin triples and matched pairs

To advance the study and applications of these important structures and their interrelations as depicted in the diagram, it is crucial to understand them within the framework of category theory. The key step in this direction involves defining appropriate morphisms for these structures to organize them into well-defined categories. Such a categorical formulation would enable the precise characterization of their relationships through functors and equivalences between these categories. However, this endeavor presents significant challenges. While one can define a notion of homomorphisms for pre-Lie bialgebras and Manin triples for pre-Lie algebras by analogy with the corresponding homomorphisms of Lie bialgebras and Manin triples for Lie algebras (as presented in
[
18
]
), this approach encounters fundamental limitations. Specifically, the homomorphisms of Manin triples fail to align consistently with the homomorphisms of their corresponding pre-Lie bialgebras. This discrepancy highlights the need for a more refined approach to defining morphisms that can properly capture the categorical structure of these mathematical objects.

To address the challenges at the Lie algebra level, the authors in
[
7
]
utilize two key strategies. The first involves a polarization process that shifts the focus from homomorphisms to endomorphisms. The second strategy entails reordering operations to endow Lie algebras with additional structures, specifically bialgebras and endomorphisms. This polarization strategy can be likened to transforming a homogeneous polynomial in a single variable into a multilinear form in multiple variables through linear substitutions and derivations
[
28
]
. Rather than attempting to define morphisms between arbitrary Lie bialgebras, the authors concentrate on the specific case of endomorphisms for a given object. To define these endomorphisms for a Lie bialgebra, they treat it as an additional endomorphism structure layered onto the Lie bialgebra, which itself is derived from imposing a bialgebra structure on a Lie algebra. The crux of their approach lies in reversing the sequence of these two processes: they first augment Lie algebras with endomorphisms, resulting in what they term endo Lie algebras, and subsequently introduce a bialgebra structure to these endo Lie algebras. This sequence is illustrated in the following diagram, specifically for the case of Lie bialgebras.

Guided by this philosophy, we first introduce the notion of an endo pre-Lie algebra, which is a pre-Lie algebra endowed with a pre-Lie algebra endomorphism. We then investigate matched pairs, Manin triples, bialgebras for endo pre-Lie algebras, and endo phase spaces for Lie algebras, which in turn induce endomorphisms of these structures on pre-Lie algebras. Subsequently, a polarization process generalizes the notion of endomorphisms to homomorphisms, endowing each class of objects with a category structure. 1.3.
Morphism of phase spaces, classical
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices and relative Rota-Baxter operators

A phase space of a Lie alebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a natural symplectic structure
\omega_{p}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that both
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are Lie subalgebras. A natural approach to define endomorphisms between phase spaces is to use symplectic endomorphisms. However, this approach fails to establish a categorical correspondence between phase spaces of Lie algebras and Manin triples for pre-Lie algebras. In
[
26
]
, Kupershmidt demonstrated that a symplectic structure on a Lie algebra is equivalent to an invertible skew-symmetric relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the coadjoint representation. To address this, we introduce a coherent homomorphism between symplectic Lie algebras, which establishes an equivalence between the category of symplectic Lie algebras with coherent homomorphisms and the category of invertible skew-symmetric relative Rota-Baxter operators with their natural homomorphisms. Notably, this coherent homomorphism between phase spaces arises as the extended endomorphism from the endo phase spaces.

Similarly, a symmetric classical
\mathfrak{s}
-matrix on a pre-Lie algebra is equivalent to a symmetric relative Rota-Baxter operator on its sub-adjacent Lie algebra with respect to the coregular representation. From a categorical perspective, we introduce another homomorphism between
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices, ensuring that the induced categories are equivalent. Recently, the deformation theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras has been developed in
[
30
]
. The authors provide a cohomology theory for relative Rota-Baxter operators and use it to study their deformations. In this paper, leveraging the close relationship between
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices and relative Rota-Baxter operators, we develop a cohomology theory for
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices on pre-Lie algebras. Furthermore, using this new homomorphism between symmetric classical
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices, we investigate the deformations of
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices.

1.4.
Outline of the paper

The paper is organized as following. In Section
2
, we first recall the representations and cohomology of pre-Lie algebras. Then we recall the graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras and the cohomology for a relative Rota-Baxter operator.

In Section
3
, we introduce the notion of an endo pre-Lie algebra and develop a bialgebra theory for endo pre-Lie algebras together with their equivalences to matched pairs, Manin triples of endo pre-Lie algebras and endo phase phases (Theorem
3.32
), which leads to the notion of coherent endomorphisms for each class of structures.

In Section
4
, we interpret a bialgebra of endo pre-Lie algebras as an endomorphism of a pre-Lie bialgebra, which then naturally generalizes to a homomorphism of pre-Lie bialgebras that is compatible with that of phase spaces of Lie algebras, Manin triples and matched pairs of pre-Lie algebras, showing that the correspondences among pre-Lie bialgebras, phase spaces of Lie algebras, Manin triples for pre-Lie algebras and matched pairs of pre-Lie algebras are equivalences of categories (Theorem
4.18
). In particular, we build the equivalences of categories between symplectic Lie algebras and corresponding relative Rota-Baxter operators (Proposition
4.2
).

In Section
5
, we introduce the weak homomorphism between symmetric classical
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices and show that the equivalences of categories between symmetric classical
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices and corresponding relative Rota-Baxter operators (Proposition
5.8
). We use the graded Lie algebra for relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras to construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements characterize symmetric classical
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices on pre-Lie algebras (Theorem
5.12
). Furthermore, we use the graded Lie algebra for
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices to establish the cohomology of
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices and use the this cohomology and weak homomorphisms to study their deformations.

In this paper, all the vector spaces are over algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
and finite dimensional.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Pre-Lie algebras, representations and cohomologies

Definition 2.1
.

A
pre-Lie algebra
is a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space and
\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a bilinear multiplication
satisfying that for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the associator

(x,y,z)=(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z-x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)
is symmetric in
x,y
,
i.e.

(x,y,z)=(y,x,z),\;\;{\rm or}\;\;{\rm equivalently,}\;\;(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z-x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)=(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z-y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z).

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra. The commutator
[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y-y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x
defines a Lie algebra structure
on
\mathfrak{g}
, which is called the
sub-adjacent Lie algebra
of

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
. Furthermore,

L:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
with
x\rightarrow L_{x}
, where

L_{x}y=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, gives a representation of
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
5
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra and
V
a vector
space. A
representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
consists of a pair

(\rho,\mu)
, where
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a representation
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
on
V
and
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear
map satisfying

(4)

\displaystyle\rho(x)\mu(y)u-\mu(y)\rho(x)u=\mu(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)u-\mu(y)\mu(x)u,\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~u\in V.

Usually, we denote a representation by
(V;\rho,\mu)
. It is
obvious that
(\mathbb{K};\rho=0,\mu=0)
is a representation, which we
call the
trivial representation
. Let
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
be a linear map with
x\longrightarrow R_{x}
, where the
linear map
R_{x}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

R_{x}(y)=y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x,
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then

(\mathfrak{g};\rho=L,\mu=R)
is also a representation, which we call the

regular representation
. Define two linear maps
L^{*},R^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
with
x\longrightarrow L^{*}_{x}
and

x\longrightarrow R^{*}_{x}
respectively (for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
)
by

(5)

\langle L_{x}^{*}(\alpha),y\rangle=-\langle\alpha,x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y\rangle,\;\;\langle R_{x}^{*}(\alpha),y\rangle=-\langle\alpha,y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x\rangle,\;\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\rho={\rm ad}^{*}=L^{*}-R^{*},\mu=-R^{*})
is a
representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
. In fact, it is the dual
representation of the regular representation
(\mathfrak{g};L,R)
and is called
coregular representation
.

The cohomology complex for a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
with a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
is given as follows (
[
14
]
).
The set of
n
-cochains is given by

\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V),\ n\geq 1.
For all
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V)
, the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V)
is given by

(6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\phi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(x_{i})\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\mu(x_{n+1})\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j}\phi([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n+1}),

for all
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},~i=1,\cdots,n+1
.

2.2.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras and their Maurer-Cartan characterizations

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
26
]

Let

Let

to

(

3.
Endo pre-Lie algebras, Manin triples and endo symplectic Lie algebras

In this section we introduce the notion of endo pre-Lie algebras and give the equivalent structures of pre-Lie bialgebras, matched pairs, Manin triples for endo pre-Lie algebras and phase spaces of Lie algebras.

3.1.
Endo pre-Lie algebras and their representations

Definition 3.1
.

An
endo pre-Lie algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi)
, or simply
(\mathfrak{g},\phi)
, where
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a pre-Lie algebra and
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a pre-Lie algebra endomorphism.

As an analogy of a pre-Lie algebra representation, we have

Definition 3.2
.

A
representation
of an endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\phi)
is
(V;\rho,\mu,\alpha)
, where
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of a pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{End}(V)
such that

(16)

\displaystyle\alpha(\rho(x)(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\phi(x))(\alpha(v))

(17)

\displaystyle\alpha(\mu(x)(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(\phi(x))(\alpha(v)),\quad\forall~x\in\mathfrak{g},~v\in V.

Two representations
(V_{1};\rho_{1},\mu_{1},\alpha_{1})
and
(V_{2};\rho_{2},\mu_{2},\alpha_{2})
of an endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\phi)
are called
equvialent

\varphi:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
such that

(18)

\displaystyle\varphi(\rho_{1}(x)(v))=\rho_{2}(x)(\varphi(v)),\varphi(\mu_{1}(x)(v))=\mu_{2}(x)(\varphi(v)),\quad\forall~x\in\mathfrak{g},~v\in V.

For vector spaces
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, and linear maps
\phi_{1}:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{1}
and
\phi_{2}:V_{2}\rightarrow V_{2}
, we denote
\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}
by the linear map

(19)

\displaystyle\phi_{V_{1}\oplus V_{2}}:V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\rightarrow V_{1}\oplus V_{2},\quad\phi_{V_{1}\oplus V_{2}}(v_{1}+v_{2}):=\phi_{1}(v_{1})+\phi_{2}(v_{2}).

For a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
, a linear space
V
, two linear maps
\rho,\mu:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, define a multiplication
\ast

\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
by

(20)

\displaystyle(x_{1}+v_{1})\ast(x_{2}+v_{2})=x_{1}{\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}}x_{2}+\rho(x_{1})v_{2}+\mu(x_{2})v_{1}.\quad\forall~x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},~v_{1},v_{2}\in V.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
be pre-Lie algebras,
(\mathfrak{h};\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a representation of the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
(\mathfrak{g};\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})
be a representation of the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
. Define a multiplication on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
by

(23)

\displaystyle(x+a)\ast(y+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(a)y+\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(b)x

\displaystyle+a\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}b+\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)b+\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)a,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},a,b\in\mathfrak{h}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},\ast)
is a pre-Lie algebra if and only if
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a matched pair of pre-Lie algebras of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
. We denote the resulting pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},\ast)
by
\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{h}
.

Definition 3.11
.

A
matched pair of endo pre-Lie algebras
is
((\mathfrak{g},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}),\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})
, where
(\mathfrak{g},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
are endo pre-Lie algebras.
\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h}),\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
are linear maps, such that

(i)

(\mathfrak{g};\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a representation of endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}),

(ii)

(\mathfrak{h};\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a representation of endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}),

(iii)

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a matched pair of pre-Lie algebras.

The endo pre-Lie algebras have the following characterization.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
be endo pre-Lie algebras and
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})
be a matched pair of pre-Lie algebra of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is an endo pre-Lie algebra if and only if
((\mathfrak{g},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}),\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a matched pair of endo pre-Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.10
,
\mathfrak{g}\bowtie h
is a pre-Lie algebra if and only if
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a matched pair of pre-Lie algebras of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Therefore, in order
to prove that
(\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a endo pre-Lie algebra, we just need to prove
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism. By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})((x+a)\ast(y+b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(a)y+\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(b)x+a\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}b+\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)b+\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(a)y+\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(b)x)+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}b+\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)b+\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)a),

\displaystyle(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})(x+a)\ast(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})(y+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a))\ast(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)+\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)+\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a))\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)+\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(b))\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)

\displaystyle+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(b)+\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(b)+\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a).

If
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism, letting
x=b=0
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(a)y)+\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)a)=\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a))\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)+\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a),

which implies that

\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(a)y)=\rho_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a))\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y),\quad\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)a)=\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(a).

Similarly, for
y=a=0
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(b)x)=\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(b))\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\quad\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)b)=\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(b).

Thus,
(\mathfrak{g};\rho_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a representation of the endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
(\mathfrak{h};\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a representation of the endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
. The converse can be proved similarly.
∎

3.3.

A nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form

(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}
on a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})

is called
invariant
if

(24)

(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)_{-}+(y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})_{-}=0,\quad\forall~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 3.13
.

A
quadratic pre-Lie algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{-})
, where
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a pre-Lie algebra and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}
is a nondegenerate invariant skew-symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 3.14
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{ad}^{*},-R^{*}}\mathfrak{g}^{*},(\cdot,\cdot)_{-})
is a quadratic pre-Lie algebra, where the nondegenerate invariant skew-symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}
is given by

(25)

\displaystyle(x+a,y+b)_{-}=\langle a,y\rangle-\langle x,b\rangle,\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~~a,b\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Recall that a nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is called a
symplectic structure
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
if

\omega([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z)+\omega([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},x)+\omega([z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},y)=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

A symplectic Lie algebra is a Lie algebra equipped with a symplectic structure.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between quadratic pre-Lie algebras and symplectic Lie algebras.

Theorem 3.15
.

(
[
19
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a quadratic pre-Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is a symplectic Lie algebra. Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a symplectic Lie algebra. Then there exists a compatible pre-Lie algebra structure
\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
given by

(26)

\omega(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)=-\omega(y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad~\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},

whose sub-adjacent Lie algebra is exactly
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Moreover,
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is a quadratic pre-Lie algebra.

Definition 3.16
.

A
Manin triple of pre-Lie algebras

is a triple
((\mathfrak{d},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}),\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
, where
(\mathfrak{d},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{-})

\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are pre-Lie subalgebras, both isotropic with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}
, and
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
as vector spaces.

Definition 3.17
.

(
[
24
]
)

\mathfrak{g}

and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}

2

Theorem 3.18
.

(
[
5
]
)

Let

(28)

\displaystyle[x+a,y+b]_{p}=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}+L^{*}_{x}b-L^{*}_{y}a+\mathfrak{L}^{*}_{a}y-\mathfrak{L}^{*}_{b}x+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g},a,b\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Definition 3.19
.

A
quadratic endo pre-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{d},\phi,(\cdot,\cdot)_{-})

of
\phi

(29)

.

Let

Proposition 3.21
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between quadratic endo pre-Lie algebras and endo symplectic Lie algebras.

Proof.

For
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(\phi(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)-\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\phi(y),z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,\widehat{\phi}(z))+\omega(\phi(y),[\phi(x),z]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(y,\widehat{\phi}[\phi(x),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[x,\widehat{\phi}(z)]_{\mathfrak{g}}),

which implies that
\phi
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism if and only if (
30

By Theorem
3.15
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between quadratic pre-Lie algebras and symplectic Lie algebras. The conclusion follows.
∎

Let
(\mathfrak{d},\phi,(\cdot,\cdot)_{-})
be a quadratic endo pre-Lie algebra. We also let
\widehat{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle(\phi(x),y)_{-}=(x,\widehat{\phi}(y))_{-},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 3.22
.

Let
(\mathfrak{d},\phi,(\cdot,\cdot)_{-})
be a quadratic endo pre-Lie algebra and
\widehat{\phi}
be the adjoint of
\phi

\widehat{\phi}
dually represents
(\mathfrak{d},\phi)

(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\mathrm{ad}^{*},-R^{*},\widehat{\phi}^{*})

Proof.

For
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{d}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}y),z)_{-}-(\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}\phi(y),z)_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}y,\widehat{\phi}(z))_{-}+(\phi(y),[\phi(x),z]_{\mathfrak{d}})_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(y,[x,\widehat{\phi}(z)]_{\mathfrak{d}})_{-}+(y,\widehat{\phi}([\phi(x),z]_{\mathfrak{d}}))_{-},

which implies that

[x,\widehat{\phi}(z)]_{\mathfrak{d}}=\widehat{\phi}([\phi(x),z]_{\mathfrak{d}}).

Similarly, by fact
(\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{d}},z)_{-}-([\phi(x),\phi(y)]_{\mathfrak{d}},z)_{-}=0

Thus

Let

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\phi+\psi^{\ast},(\cdot,\cdot)_{-})
be a quadratic endo pre-Lie algebra. Then we have

(a)

The adjoint
\widehat{\phi+\psi^{\ast}}
of
\phi+\psi^{\ast}
with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}
is
\psi+\phi^{\ast}
. Furthermore,
\psi+\phi^{\ast}
dually represent the endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\phi+\psi^{\ast})
.

(b)

\psi
dually represents the endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\phi)
.

(c)

\phi^{\ast}
dually represents the endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\psi^{\ast})

Proof.

For (a), we have

\displaystyle((\phi+\psi^{\ast})(x+a),y+b)_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi(x)+\psi^{\ast}(a),y+b)_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\ast}(a),y\rangle-\langle\phi(x),b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle a,\psi(y)\rangle-\langle x,\phi^{\ast}(b)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+x,(\psi+\phi^{\ast})(y+b))_{-},

which implies that the adjoint
\widehat{\phi+\psi^{\ast}}
of
\phi+\psi^{\ast}
with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}

\psi+\phi^{\ast}

For (b), due to (a), we have

\displaystyle(\psi+\phi^{\ast})([(\phi+\psi^{\ast})(x+a),(y+b)]_{\mathfrak{d}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x+a,(\psi+\phi^{\ast})(y+b)]_{\mathfrak{d}},

\displaystyle(\psi+\phi^{\ast})((\phi+\psi^{\ast})(x+a)\ast(y+b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+a)\ast(\psi+\phi^{\ast})(y+b).

Letting

\displaystyle\psi([\phi(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[x,\psi(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\psi(\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\psi(y),

which implies that
\psi
dually represents the endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\phi)

For (c), letting

Definition 3.25
.

(\mathfrak{g},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})

Theorem 3.26
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\phi)
be an endo pre-Lie algebra. Suppose that there is an endo pre-Lie algebra structure
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\psi^{\ast})
on its dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

((\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\phi+\psi^{\ast},(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}),(\mathfrak{g},\phi),(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\psi^{\ast}))
is a Manin triple of endo pre-Lie algebras;

(ii)

((\mathfrak{g},\phi),(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\psi^{\ast}),\mathrm{ad}^{\ast},-R^{\ast},\mathfrak{ad}^{\ast},-\mathfrak{R}^{\ast}))
is a matched pair of endo pre-Lie algebras;

(iii)

(\omega_{p},\phi,\psi)
is the endo phase space of the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
.

Proof.

We first show that (i) is equivalent to (ii).

Note that
((\mathfrak{d},\phi+\psi^{\ast},(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}),(\mathfrak{g},\phi),(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\psi^{\ast}))
is a Manin pair of endo pre-Lie algebras if and only if

(1)

(\mathfrak{d},\phi+\psi^{\ast},(\cdot,\cdot)_{-})
is a quadratic endo pre-Lie algebra,

(2)

(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\ast})
is a Manin triple of pre-Lie algebras associated to
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
.

Since
(\cdot,\cdot)_{-}
is invariant, the pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{d}
is given by

\displaystyle(x+a)\ast(y+b)=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{\ast}b-R^{*}_{y}a+a\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}b+\mathfrak{ad}^{\ast}_{a}y-\mathfrak{R}^{*}_{b}x,\quad x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~a,b\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}.

So by Theorem
3.18
, we only need to prove that
\phi+\psi^{\ast}
is a homomorphism of
(\mathfrak{d},\ast)
. By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle(\phi+\psi^{\ast})(x+a)\ast(\phi+\psi^{\ast})(y+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi(x)+\psi^{\ast}(a))\ast(\phi(y)+\psi^{\ast}(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\phi(y)+\mathrm{ad}^{\ast}_{\phi(x)}(\psi^{\ast}(b))-R^{\ast}_{\phi(y)}(\psi^{\ast}(a))

\displaystyle+\psi^{\ast}(a)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}\psi^{\ast}(b)+\mathfrak{ad}_{\psi^{\ast}(a)}^{\ast}\phi(y)-\mathfrak{R}_{\psi^{\ast}(b)}^{\ast}\phi(x),

\displaystyle(\phi+\psi^{\ast})((x+a)\ast(y+b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)+\phi(\mathfrak{ad}_{a}^{\ast}y)-\phi(\mathfrak{R}_{b}^{*}x)

\displaystyle+\psi^{\ast}(a\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}}b)+\psi^{\ast}(\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{\ast}b)-\psi^{\ast}(R^{*}_{y}a).

Therefore,
\phi+\psi^{\ast}
is a homomorphism of
(\mathfrak{d},\ast)
if and only if
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast};\mathrm{ad}^{\ast},R^{\ast},\psi^{\ast})
is a representation of the endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\phi)
and
(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{ad}^{\ast},\mathfrak{R}^{\ast},\phi)
is a representation of the endo pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},\psi^{\ast})
.

Next, we show that (i) is equivalent to (iii). Since the adjoint
\widehat{\phi+\psi^{\ast}}
of
\phi+\psi^{\ast}
with respect to
\omega_{p}
is
\psi+\phi^{\ast}
, (
50
) is just (
30
). Furthermore, by
Proposition
3.21
,
(\mathfrak{d},\phi+\psi^{\ast},\omega_{p})
is a quadratic endo pre-Lie algebra. The conclusion follows.

∎

3.4.
Endo pre-Lie bialgebras

Definition 3.27
.

A linear space
\mathfrak{g}
with a linear map
\alpha:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}

(33)

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\alpha)\alpha(x)-(\tau\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes\alpha)\alpha(x)=(\alpha\otimes{\rm id})\alpha(x)-(\tau\otimes{\rm id})(\alpha\otimes{\rm id})\alpha(x),

\tau:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}

For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

(V;\rho)
of
\mathfrak{g}
, recall that a 1-cocycle associated to
\alpha:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
is a linear map from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho(x)\alpha(y)-\rho(y)\alpha(x),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 3.28
.

(
[
5
]

\mathfrak{g}

(i)

\alpha^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is a pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
,

(ii)

\beta^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
,

(iii)

\alpha
is a
1
-cocycle of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
L\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathrm{ad}
with values in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
,

(iv)

\beta

1
-cocycle of
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}

4.
Coherent homomorphisms of pre-Lie bialgebras, Manin triples and phase spaces of Lie algebras

As the main motivation and application of our study
of endo pre-Lie bialgebras, Manin triples, matched pairs and phase space spaces of Lie algebras, we introduce new notions of homomorphisms of these structures ensuring compatibility with structural correspondences and enabling the organization of these objects into categories so that the maps among the classes become functors or category equivalences.

4.1.
Coherent homomorphisms of phase spaces and Manin triples for pre-Lie algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
\omega
be a skew-symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form. Define
\omega^{\natural}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by

\langle\omega^{\natural}(x),y\rangle=\omega(x,y),\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Furthermore, we let
T_{\omega}=(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}
. Then
\omega
is a
2
-cocycle if and only if
T_{\omega}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respective to the coadjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\mathrm{ad}^{*})
.

In order to build the relationships between symplectic Lie algebras and relative Rota-Baxter operators on the level of categories, we introduce the notion of coherent homomorphisms between symplectic Lie algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\omega_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two symplectic Lie algebras. A
coherent homomorphism
from
(\mathfrak{g},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
to
(\mathfrak{h},\omega_{\mathfrak{h}})
consists of a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
and a linear map
\psi:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that the following equalities holds:

(48)

\displaystyle[x,\psi(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi[\phi(x),y]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(49)

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x),y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,\psi(y)),\quad\forall~x\in\mathfrak{g},y\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\omega_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two symplectic Lie algebras. Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
and
\psi:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be linear maps. Then

(\phi,\psi)
is a coherent homomorphism of symplectic Lie algebras from
(\mathfrak{g},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
to
(\mathfrak{h},\omega_{\mathfrak{h}})
if and only if
(\phi,\psi^{*})
is a homomorphism between
the corresponding relative Rota-Baxter operators
T_{\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}}
and
T_{\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}}
.

This correspondence defines an equivalence from the category
{\bf SLA}
of symplectic Lie algebras to the category
{\bf IRB}
of
invertible relative Rota-Baxter operators on the Lie algebras associated to the representations
\mathrm{ad}^{*}
satisfying
\langle T_{\omega}(x),y\rangle=-\langle T_{\omega}^{\sharp}(y),x\rangle
for

or
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

It follows by a direct calculation.
∎

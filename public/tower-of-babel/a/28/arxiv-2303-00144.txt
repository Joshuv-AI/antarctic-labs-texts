cl-prereductions, i-postexpansions, and related structures
Sarah Poiani, Janet Vassilev

Abstract
. Expanding on the work of Kemp, Ratliff and Shah [ KRS20 ] , for any closure cl \rm cl defined on a class of modules over a Noetherian ring, we develop the theory of cl \rm cl -prereductions of submodules. For any interior i \rm i on a class of R R -modules, we also develop the theory of i \rm i -postexpansions. Using the duality of Epstein, R.G. and Vassilev [ ERV23b ] , we show that if i \rm i is the interior dual to cl \rm cl , then these notions are in fact dual to each other. We consider the cl \rm cl -precore ( i \rm i -postcore), the intersection of all cl \rm cl -prereductions ( i \rm i -postexpansions) of a submodule and the cl \rm cl -prehull ( i \rm i -posthull), the sum of all cl \rm cl -prereductions ( i \rm i -postexpansions) of a submodule and give comparisons with the cl \rm cl -core ( i \rm i -hull). We further give a classification of cl \rm cl -prereductions of cl \rm cl -closed ideals of a Noetherian ring where cl \rm cl is a closure with a special part.

All of these closures are defined on the class of finitely generated modules over Noetherian rings.

Definition 2.4

Definition 2.5

{\bf P}_{\rm cl}(N,M)
.
Note that if
{\bf I}^{\prime}_{\rm cl}(N,M)
is the set of all ideals properly contained in
N
, then
N
has no
\rm cl
-reductions. We usually call such a submodule
N
of
M
which only has itself as a
\rm cl
-reduction
\rm cl
-basic
.

Instead of the non-nilpotence assumption as Kemp, Ratliff and Shah do for
[
KRS20
, Proposition 3.7]
, we will require
{\bf I}^{\prime}_{\rm cl}(N,M)
to be nonempty for our generalization.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
cl
a Nakayama closure on
\mathcal{P}
, the class of finitely generated
R
-modules. Suppose
(N,M)\in\mathcal{P}
such that
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)\neq\emptyset
then the following hold

(1)

Suppose
K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
and
L\subseteq K
then
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
.

(2)

Let
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
. There exists a submodule
\mathfrak{A}\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
which is maximal in
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
and
\mathfrak{A}\supseteq L
.

(3)

Suppose that
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{2}
are both maximal submodules in
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
. Then
\mathfrak{A}_{1}+\mathfrak{A}_{2}\notin{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
and
\mathfrak{A}_{1}+\mathfrak{A}_{2}
is a
cl
-reduction of
N
in
M
.

(4)

If
L,K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
then either
L+K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
or
L+K
is a
cl
-reduction of
N
in
M
.

(5)

If
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
then
L+\mathfrak{m}N\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
.

(6)

If
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
then
L_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
.

(7)

If
\mathfrak{A}
is maximal in
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
then
(\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}+(\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl})_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N=\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

(
1
) If
K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
, then
K
is not a cl-reduction of
N
. Since
L\subseteq K
and
L_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq K_{M}^{\rm cl}\subsetneq N_{M}^{\rm cl}
then
L
is also not a cl-reduction of
N
in
M
.

(
2
) Since
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
and
R
Noetherian, then there exists an element
\mathfrak{A}\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
which is maximal in
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
and
\mathfrak{A}\supseteq L
.

(
3
) Since
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{2}
are both maximal in
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
and
\mathfrak{A}_{1},\mathfrak{A}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}_{1}+\mathfrak{A}_{2}\subseteq N
, then
\mathfrak{A}_{1}+\mathfrak{A}_{2}
is a cl-reduction of
N
in
M
.

(
4
) Since
L,K\subseteq N
then
L+K\subseteq N
. If
L+K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
, we are done. If
L+K\notin{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
, then by definition
L+K
is a cl-reduction of
N
in
M
.

(
5
) Suppose
L+\mathfrak{m}N\notin{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
. So
L+\mathfrak{m}N
is a cl-reduction of
N
in
M
. Then
L\subseteq N\subseteq(L+\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl}
. Because cl is a Nakayama closure,
L_{M}^{\rm cl}=N_{M}^{\rm cl}
and
L
is a cl-reduction of
N
in
M
which contradicts our assumption. So
L+\mathfrak{m}N\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
.

(
6
) Suppose that
L_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N\notin{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
. So
L_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N
is a cl-reduction of
N
in
M
. Then
L_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq N_{M}^{\rm cl}=(L_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N_{M}^{\rm cl})_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq(L_{M}^{\rm cl})_{M}^{\rm cl}=L_{M}^{\rm cl}
and
L
is a cl-reduction of
N
in
M
which contradicts
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
.

(
7
) Since
(\mathfrak{A}+\mathfrak{m}N)\subseteq(\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}+(\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl})\subseteq(\mathfrak{A}+\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl}
, we have
(\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}+(\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl})_{M}^{\rm cl}=(\mathfrak{A}+\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl}
. By (5), we know that
\mathfrak{A}+\mathfrak{m}N\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
. Since
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}+\mathfrak{m}N
and
\mathfrak{A}
maximal, then
\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}+\mathfrak{m}N
and hence
\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}=(\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}+(\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl})_{M}^{\rm cl}
. By (6), we know that
(\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}+(\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl})_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
and by the maximality of
\mathfrak{A}
we have
\mathfrak{A}=(\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}+(\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl})_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N
.
∎

The following proposition gives us some nice properties that always hold for
\rm cl
-prereductions.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
\rm cl
a Nakayama closure on
\mathcal{P}
. Then

(1)

Every submodule
L\subseteq N
which is not a
\rm cl
-reduction of
N
in
M
is contained in a
\rm cl
-prereduction of
N
in
M
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a
\rm cl
-prereduction of
N
in
M
. Then

(a)

\mathfrak{m}N\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
.

(b)

\mathfrak{A}
is
\rm cl
-closed in
N
or
\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N=\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

(1)

By Proposition
3.4
(
2
), there is some maximal element
\mathfrak{A}
of
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
which contains
L
. Such an
\mathfrak{A}
must be a
\rm cl
-prereduction since any submodule containing it must be a
\rm cl
-reduction of
N
in
M
.

(2)

(a)

In the proof of Proposition
3.4
(
7
), we saw
\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}+\mathfrak{m}N
. This implies
\mathfrak{m}N\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
.

(b)

We also saw in the proof of Proposition
3.4
(
7
) that
\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N=(\mathfrak{A}+\mathfrak{m}N)_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N=\mathfrak{A}
.

∎

In particular, if a submodule is
\rm cl
-closed in
M
then the
\rm cl
-prereductions will also be
\rm cl
-closed.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
\rm cl
a Nakayama closure on
\mathcal{P}
. For every
\rm cl
-prereduction
\mathfrak{A}
of
N_{M}^{\rm cl}
,
\mathfrak{m}N_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathfrak{A}
is also a
\rm cl
-prereduction of
N_{M}^{\rm cl}
since
\mathfrak{A}\in N_{M}^{\rm cl}
and every submodule
L
with
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq L\subseteq N
,
L
is a
\rm cl
-prereduction of
N
and hence a
\rm cl
-reduction of
N_{M}^{\rm cl}
. By Proposition
3.5
(
2a
),
\mathfrak{m}N_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
and by Proposition
3.5
(
2b
),
\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}=\mathfrak{A}_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap N_{M}^{\rm cl}=\mathfrak{A}
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
\rm cl
a Nakayama closure on the modules of
R
. Let
K\subseteq N\subseteq M
be submodules of
R
with
K
a
\rm cl
-reduction of
N
in
M
. Then

(1)

{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)\subseteq{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
.

(2)

For each
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
,
L\cap K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)
.

(3)

For each maximal element of
\mathfrak{A}
of
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)
there exists a maximal element
\mathfrak{B}
of
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
such that
\mathfrak{B}\cap K=\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

(
1
) Let
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)
. Since
K\subseteq N
, then
L\subseteq K\subseteq N
and
L_{M}^{\rm cl}\subsetneq K_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq N_{M}^{\rm cl}
. Since
L
is not a
\rm cl
-reduction of
K
, it cannot be a
\rm cl
-reduction of the larger module
N
. Thus
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
.

(
2
) If
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
, then
L\subseteq N
and
L
is not a
\rm cl
-reduction of
N
. Note that
L\cap K\subseteq K
. To see that
L\cap K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)
, it is enough to see that
L\cap K
is not a
\rm cl
-reduction of
K
. Suppose that
L\cap K
is a
\rm cl
-reduction of
K
. Then
(L\cap K)_{M}^{\rm cl}=K_{M}^{\rm cl}
. Note that
(L\cap K)_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq L_{M}^{\rm cl}\cap K_{M}^{\rm cl}
. Since
K
is a
\rm cl
-reduction of
N
, then
N_{M}^{\rm cl}=K_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq L_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq N_{M}^{\rm cl}
which gives a contradiction to
L\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
. Hence,
L\cap K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)
.

(
3
) Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a maximal element of
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)
. By (1),
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)\subseteq{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
. Thus
\mathfrak{A}\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
and there must exist a maximal element
\mathfrak{B}\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
with
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}
. By (2),
\mathfrak{B}\cap K\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(K,M)
. Since
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}\cap K
and
\mathfrak{A}
is maximal, we get
\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{B}\cap K
.

∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
\rm cl
a Nakayama closure on
\mathcal{P}
, pairs of finite
R
-modules. Let

be submodules of

with

\rm cl

\mathfrak{A}

\rm cl
-prereduction of

\rm cl
-prereduction
\mathfrak{B}
of

.

This is a direct consequence of Propostion

(

) and the fact that maximal elements of

∎

.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})

\rm cl
a Nakayama closure on the modules of

\rm cl

Since

For submodules which do not have cyclic

-prereductions, we obtain the following which is similar to
[

, Proposition 3.13]
for ideals with no principal prereductions.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
\rm cl
a Nakayama closure on the modules of
R
. Let
N\subseteq M
, be a submodule. Then
N=\cup\{\mathfrak{a}\mid\mathfrak{a}\text{ a }
cl
\text{-prereduction of }N\}
if and only if
N
has no cyclic
\rm cl
-reductions in

.

Proof.

Note that
N=\cup\{xR\mid x\in N\}
as sets. Note that if
N
has no cyclic
\rm cl
-reductions then for any
x\in N
,
xR\in{\bf I}^{\prime}_{\rm cl}(N)
and there is a maximal element
\mathfrak{a}_{x}
of
{\bf I}^{\prime}_{\rm cl}(N)
containing
xR
. Since

N=\cup\{xR\mid x\in N\}\subseteq\cup\{\mathfrak{a}_{x}\mid x\in N\}=\cup\{\mathfrak{a}\mid\mathfrak{a}\text{ a }{\rm cl}\text{-prereduction of }N\}\subseteq N

we see that
N=\cup\{\mathfrak{a}\mid\mathfrak{a}\text{ a }\rm cl\text{-prereduction of }N\}
if
N
has no cyclic
\rm cl
-reductions in
M
.

Suppose now that
N
has a cyclic
\rm cl
-reduction
xR
. Then for any submodule
L
of
M
with
xR\subseteq L\subseteq N
,
L
is a
\rm cl
-reduction of
N
. Thus no
\rm cl
-prereduction of
N
contains

, thus

\cup\{\mathfrak{a}\mid\mathfrak{a}\text{ a }{\rm cl}\text{-prereduction of }N\}\subsetneq N.

∎

4.
\rm i
-expansions and
\rm i
-postexpansions

The second author along with Epstein and R.G. defined the dual notions to
\rm cl
-reduction and
\rm cl
-core,
\rm i
-expansion and
\rm i
-hull in
[
ERV23b
]
which we state below.

Definition 4.1
.

[
ERV23b
, Definition 2.13]
Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
\mathcal{M}
be a class of
R
-modules and
\rm i
be an interior operation defined on
\mathcal{P}
of pairs of modules
(A,B)
with
A\subseteq B
in
\mathcal{M}
. Suppose
(A,B),(C,B)\in\mathcal{P}
.

(1)

We say that
C
is an
\rm i
-expansion of
A
in
B
if
C^{B}_{\rm i}\subseteq A\subseteq C
.

(2)

We say that a submodule
C\subseteq B
is
\rm i
-cobasic
if
C_{\rm i}^{B}=C
.

(3)

If
C
is an
\rm i
-expansion of
A
in
B
and there is no submodule
D\subseteq B
such that
D^{B}_{\rm i}=A^{B}_{\rm i}
then we say that
C
is a
maximal

\rm i
-expansion of
A
in
B
.

(4)

We define the
\rm i
-hull
by
{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}=\sum\{C\mid C^{B}_{\rm i}\subseteq A\subseteq C\text{ and }(C,B)\in\mathcal{P}\}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and
\rm i
an interior operation on Artinian R-modules. We say that
\rm i
is a
Nakayama interior
if for any Artinian R-modules
A\subseteq C\subseteq B
, if
(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})^{B}_{\rm i}\subseteq A
, then
A^{B}_{\rm i}=C^{B}_{\rm i}
.

It is known that maximal
\rm i
-expansions exist for a submodule
A
of
B
in the following cases: if
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a complete local ring,
\mathcal{M}
is the class of Artinian
R
-modules and
\rm cl
is a Nakayama interior
[
ERV23a
, Proposition 6.4]
or when
R
is an associative ring and if there exists an
\rm i
-expansion
C
of
A
such that
B/C
is Noetherian
[
ERV23a
, Proposition 6.5]
. When maximal
\rm i
-expansions of
A
exist in
B
, then

{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}=\sum\{C\mid C\text{ a maximal {\rm i}-expansion of }A\text{ and }(C,B)\in\mathcal{P}\}.

We will now switch our focus to interior operations
\rm i
. As Kemp, Ratliff and Shah defined the set of ideals
{\bf I}^{\prime}(I)
to be the ideals contained in
I
which are not (integral) reductions of
I
, we can dually defined the set

{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)=\{A\subseteq C\subseteq B\mid C\text{ not an {\rm i}-expansion of }A\text{ in }B\}.

Definition 4.3
.

We say
C
is an i
-postexpansion of
A
in
B
if
A\subseteq C\subseteq B
,
C
not an i-expansion of
A
in
B
, and for all submodules
D
such that
A\subseteq D\subseteq C\subseteq B
,
D
is an i-expansion of
A
in
B
.

Note that the maximal elements of
{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
are cl-prereductions and the minimal elements of
{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
are i-postexpansions. The following properties hold for
{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an Noetherian local ring and
\mathcal{P}
be Artinian
R
-modules. Let
i
a Nakayama interior on
\mathcal{P}
. Let
A\subseteq B
be R-modules such that
{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)\neq\emptyset
. Then the following hold:

(1)

Suppose
D\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
and
(D,C)\in\mathcal{P}
submodules of
B
. Then
C\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
.

(2)

Let
C\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
. Then there exists a element
\mathfrak{A}\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
minimal in
{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
with
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq C
.

(3)

Suppose that
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
are both minimal submodules in
{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
. Then
\mathfrak{A}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{A}_{2}\notin{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
and
\mathfrak{A}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{A}_{2}
is an
i
-expansion of
A
in
B
.

(4)

If
C,D\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
then either
C\cap D\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
or
C\cap D
is an
i
-expansion of
A
in
B
.

(5)

If
C\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
then
(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
.

(6)

If
C\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
then
C_{\rm i}^{B}+A\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
.

(7)

If
\mathfrak{A}
is minimal in
{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
then
(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm i}^{B}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})_{\rm i}^{B})_{\rm i}^{B}+A=\mathfrak{A}
for any
C\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
.

Proof.

(
1
) If
D\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
, then
D
is not an i-expansion of
A
. Since
D\subseteq C
and
D_{B}^{\rm i}\subseteq C_{B}^{\rm i}\subsetneq A_{B}^{\rm i}
then
D
is also not an i-expansion of
A
in
B
.

(
2
) Since
C\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
and R Artinian, then there exists an element
\mathfrak{A}\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
which is minimal in

.

(
3
) Since

in

.

(
4
) Since

. If

, we are done. If

, then by definition

in
B
.

(
5
) Suppose

. So
(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})
is an i-expansion of

in

. Then

(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})_{\rm i}^{B}\subseteq A\subseteq(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m}).

and

is an i-expansion of

in

.

(
6
) Suppose that

is an i-expansion of

in

. Then
{C}_{\rm i}^{B}\subseteq({C}_{\rm i}^{B}+A)_{\rm i}^{B}=A_{\rm i}^{B}\subseteq{C}_{\rm i}^{B}
. This is the case because
\rm i
is intensive and order preserving and

and

. Hence,

is an i-expansion of

in

(
7
) Since

, we have

((\mathfrak{A}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m}))_{\rm i}^{B})_{\rm i}^{B}\subseteq(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm i}^{B}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})_{\rm i}^{B})_{\rm i}^{B}\subseteq(\mathfrak{A}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m}))_{\rm i}^{B}.

So
(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm i}^{B}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})_{\rm i}^{B})_{\rm i}^{B}=(\mathfrak{A}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m}))_{\rm i}^{B}
. By (5),
(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
and by (4)
\mathfrak{A}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
. Since
\mathfrak{A}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}
is minimal,
\mathfrak{A}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m})=\mathfrak{A}
and
(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm i}^{B}\cap((A:_{C}\mathfrak{m}))_{\rm i}^{B})_{\rm i}^{B}=(\mathfrak{A}\cap(A:_{C}\mathfrak{m}))_{\rm i}^{B}=\mathfrak{A}_{\rm i}^{B}
. By (6),
(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm i}^{B}\cap((A:_{C}\mathfrak{m}))_{\rm i}^{B})_{\rm i}^{B}+A\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
. Hence, by the minimality of

\mathfrak{A}=(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm i}^{B}\cap((A:_{C}\mathfrak{m}))_{\rm i}^{B})_{\rm i}^{B}+A.

∎

5.
Comparing
\rm cl
-prereductions and
\rm i
-postexpansions for different closures and interiors

There are many different closure and interior operations so it would be beneficial to know when closure operations are comparable and when interior operations are comparable. Knowing when they are comparable allows us to compare
\rm cl
-reductions,
\rm cl
-prereductions,
\rm i
-expansions, and
\rm i
-postexpansions.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be pair operations defined on
\mathcal{P}
a collection of pairs of modules in
R
. We say
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
if
p_{1}(N,M)\subseteq p_{2}(N,M)
for all
(N,M)\in\mathcal{P}
. We say
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are
comparable
if
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
or
p_{2}\leq p_{1}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
\rm cl_{1}
and
\rm cl_{2}
be closure operations defined on the submodules of
R
. We say
\rm cl_{1}\leq\rm cl_{2}
if
N_{M}^{\rm cl_{1}}\subseteq N_{M}^{\rm cl_{2}}
for all
(N,M)\in\mathcal{P}
. So
\rm cl_{1}
and
\rm cl_{2}
are
comparable
if
\rm cl_{1}\leq\rm cl_{2}
or
\rm cl_{2}\leq\rm cl_{1}
.

Let
\rm i_{1}
and
\rm i_{2}
be interior operations defined on the submodules of
R
. We say
\rm i_{1}\leq\rm i_{2}
if
A_{\rm i_{1}}^{B}\subseteq A_{\rm i_{2}}^{B}
for all
(A,B)\in\mathcal{P}
. So
\rm i_{1}
and
\rm i_{2}
are
comparable
if
\rm i_{1}\leq\rm i_{2}
or
\rm i_{2}\leq\rm i_{1}
.
∎

We first explore relationships between
{\rm cl}_{i}
-prereductions for
i=1,2
, when
\rm cl_{1}\leq\rm cl_{2}
.

.

Example 5.8
.

Let

6.

In this section, we extend the work of

]

We will use

is the class of Matlis-dualizable

Definition 6.1
.

[

, Definition 3.1]
Let

-modules

.

[

Let

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian complete local ring. Let
\rm i
be a relative interior operation on pairs
A\subseteq B
of
R
-modules that are Noetherian or Artinian, and let
\rm cl:=\rm i^{\smallsmile}
be its dual closure operation. There exists a one-to-one correspondence between the set of
\rm i
-postexpansions of
A
in
B
and the set of
\rm cl
-prereductions of
(B/A)^{\vee}
in

. Under this correspondence, an
\rm i
-postexpansion

of

-prereduction of

C

\rm i
-postexpansion of

. Furthermore, since
\mathfrak{m}g\subseteq(B/A)^{\vee}
then

A=\left(\displaystyle\frac{(B/C)^{\vee}}{(B/A)^{\vee}}\right)^{\vee}\subseteq\left(\displaystyle\frac{(B/C)^{\vee}}{\mathfrak{m}g}\right)^{\vee}=\left(\displaystyle\frac{B^{\vee}}{\mathfrak{m}(g\circ\pi^{\vee})}\right)^{\vee}=(\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g)):_{B}\mathfrak{m}).

Suppose

for some

and
\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}g:_{B/C}\mathfrak{m})\supseteq A
. Then by Lemma

(B/C)^{\vee}=\left(\frac{B}{A\cap\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g)}\right)^{\vee}\cong(B/A)^{\vee}+(B/\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g)))^{\vee}

and since
(g)\cong((B/C)/({\rm ker}(g)))^{\vee}\cong(B/(\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g)))^{\vee}
, we see that
(B/C)^{\vee}=(B/A)^{\vee}+(g)
.

Since
(\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g)):_{B}\mathfrak{m})\supseteq A
, we have

(B/A)^{\vee}\supseteq\left(\displaystyle\frac{B}{(\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g)):_{B}\mathfrak{m})}\right)=\left(\displaystyle\frac{B/C}{({\rm ker}(g)):_{B/C}\mathfrak{m})}\right)^{\vee}=\mathfrak{m}g

or
\mathfrak{m}g\subseteq(B/A)^{\vee}
and by Remark
7.3

(B/C)^{\vee}
covers
(B/A)^{\vee}
.

(
2
)
Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an
\rm i
-postexpansion of
C
. Since

is
\rm i
-cobasic, then for all

such that
\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g))\not\supseteq\mathfrak{A}
,
\mathfrak{A}\cap\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g))
is an
\rm i
-expansion of

. But this implies that
\mathfrak{A}\cap\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g))=C
for all

such that
\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g))\not\supseteq\mathfrak{A}
. By Proposition
4.5
(
2a
),
(A:_{B}\mathfrak{m})\supseteq\mathfrak{A}
and (
1

(

. Thus

is a non-
\rm i

.

(

\rm i

-expansion of

. By (

there exists a

∎

Proposition 7.17
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian complete local ring and
A
be a strongly
\rm i
-independent submodule in
B
with
\rm i
-cospread equal to
k\geq 1
elements and

is the canonical surjection. Then any
\rm i
-postexpansion of

in

has the form

\bigcap\limits_{r\neq i}\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{r}))\cap(\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{i})):_{B}\mathfrak{m}).

Proof.

Let
g_{1},...,g_{k}
be a minimal cogenerating set for
B/A
and let

\mathfrak{A}=\bigcap\limits_{r\neq i}\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{r}))\cap(\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{i})):_{(B/A)}\mathfrak{m}).

Then
\mathfrak{A}\cap\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{k}))=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{k}\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{i}))=A
and
(\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{k})):_{B/A}\mathfrak{m})\supseteq A
, so
\mathfrak{A}
is a cover of
A
by Proposition
7.16
(
1
). Also, the

are strongly
\rm i
-independent, so
A
is
\rm i
-cobasic and
A
is the only
\rm i
-expansion of itself. Thus
\mathfrak{A}
is a non-
\rm i
-cover of
A
. Hence, Remark
7.16
(
2
) implies that
\mathfrak{A}
is an
\rm i
-postexpansion of
A
in
B
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{A}
is an arbitrary
\rm i
-postexpansion of
A
where
B/A=B/(\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{k}\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{i})))
. Then there exists an

such that
\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{i}))\not\supseteq\mathfrak{A}
. Let us assume

, then
\pi^{-1}({\rm ker}(g_{k}))\not\supseteq\mathfrak{A}
. Note that since
\mathfrak{A}
is an
\rm i
-postexpansion of

in
B
, then for any

is an
\rm i
-expansion of

in

. However, since

is
\rm i
-cobasic, this implies that

for any

. In particular,

.

Thus for

, there exists

and

such that

and

. Thus

. Since

(

-postexpansion of

∎

8.
Pre-core and Post-hull

Because minimal reductions are not unique in a Noetherian local ring, Rees and Sally defined the core of an ideal as
\textrm{core}(I)=\bigcap_{J\subset I}J
, where
J
is an integral reduction of
I

[
RS88
]
. In this section, we will generalize the notions of
\rm cl
-core and
\rm i
-hull, but instead of defining them in terms of
\rm cl
-reductions and
\rm i
-expansions, we will intersect and sum the prereductions or the postexpansions for these new constructs.

Definition 8.1
.

[
ERV23b
, Definition 2.12]
If
(N,M)\in\mathcal{P}
the
\rm cl
-core
of
N
with respect to
M
is the intersection of all
\rm cl
-reductions of
N
in
M
, or

{\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}:=\bigcap\{L\mid L\subseteq N\subseteq L_{M}^{\rm cl}\subseteq M\textrm{ and }(L,M)\in\mathcal{P}\}

Remark 8.2
.

As long as
N
is not
\rm cl
-basic in
M
, then the
\rm cl
-core of
N
in
M
will be contained in some
\rm cl
-prereduction of
N
in
M
.

Proof.

Let
N
be a submodule of
M
that is not
\rm cl
-basic. Then there exists some
L\neq N
such that
L
is a
\rm cl
-reduction of
N
. Thus
{\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}=\bigcap\{L\mid L\subseteq N\subseteq L_{M}^{\rm cl}\text{ and }(L,M)\in\mathcal{P}\}\neq N
and
{\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}\in{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)
. So by Proposition
3.4
(
2
), there exists a
\rm cl
-prereduction of
N
which contains
{\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}
.
∎

Definition 8.3
.

[
ERV23b
, Definition 6.1]
If
(A,B)\in\mathcal{P}
, the
\rm i
-hull
of a submodule

with respect to

is the sum of all
\rm i
-expansions of

in

, or

{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}:=\sum\{C\mid{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{B}\subseteq A\subseteq C\subseteq B\textrm{ and }(C,B)\in\mathcal{P}\}

Remark 8.4
.

As long as
A
is not
\rm i
-cobasic in
B
, then the
\rm i
-hull of
A
in
B
will contain some
\rm i
-postexpansion of
A
in
B
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a submodule of
B
that is not
\rm i
-cobasic. Then there exists some
C\neq A
such that
C
is an
\rm i
-expansion of
A
in
B
. Thus
{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}=\sum\{C\mid{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{B}\subseteq A\subseteq C\subseteq B\textrm{ and }(C,B)\in\mathcal{P}\}\neq A
and
{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}\in{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)
. So by Proposition
4.4
(
2
), there exists an
\rm i
-postexpansion of
A
containing
{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}
.
∎

Theorem 8.5
.

[
ERV23b
, Theorem 6.6]
Let

be a complete Noetherian local ring. Let

be Artinian

-modules and let
\rm i
be a relative Nakayama interior defined on Artinian

in

.

.

The
\rm cl
-prehull of

with respect to

-prereductions of

in

, or

.

{\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm prehull}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}=\sum\{L\mid L\textrm{ maximal in }{\bf I}_{\rm cl}^{\prime}(N,M)\}

{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm postcore}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}=\bigcap\{C\mid C\textrm{ minimal in }{\bf C}_{\rm i}^{\prime}(A,B)\}

The following proposition gives some upper and lower bounds on the
\rm cl
-core in terms of the
\rm cl
-precore and the
\rm cl
-prehull.

Proposition 8.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring,
\rm cl
a Nakayama closure, and
N
a submodule of
M
.

(1)

If
N
is
\rm cl
-basic or every
\rm cl
-prereduction is contained in some minimal reduction, then

\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm precore}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}\subseteq\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}.

(2)

If
N=\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm prehull}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}
or
N
has a unique
\rm cl
-prereduction in
M
, then

\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}\subseteq\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm prehull}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}.

Proof.

(
1
) First, if
N
is
\rm cl
-basic, then the only
\rm cl
-reduction of
N
in
M
is
N
itself. So
{\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}=N
. Since by definition, every
\rm cl
-prereduction is contained in
N
, we have
{\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm precore}\mathnormal{{}_{N}(M)}}\subseteq N
. Thus
{\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm precore}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}\subseteq\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}
.

Next, suppose every
\rm cl
-prereduction is contained in some minimal
\rm cl
-reduction. Then minimal
\rm cl
-reductions exist and we know that

{\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}=\bigcap\{L\mid L\text{ a minimal {\rm cl}-reduction of }N\text{ and }(L,M)\in\mathcal{P}\}.

Since the intersection of
\rm cl
-prereductions is contained in every
\rm cl
-prereduction and every
\rm cl
-prereduction is contained in a minimal
\rm cl
-reduction, we see that
\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm precore}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}\subseteq\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}
.

(
2
) If
N={\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm prehull}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}=\sum\{L\mid L\textrm{ a }{\rm cl}\textrm{-prereduction}\textrm{ of }N\textrm{ in }M\}
. Since by definition, every
\rm cl
-reduction is contained in
N
, we have
{\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{N}(M)}}\subseteq N
. Thus
\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}\subseteq\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm prehull}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}
.

If
L
is the unique
\rm cl
-prereduction of
N
in
M
, by Remark
8.2
we know that

{\rm cl\textrm{-}{{\rm core}}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}\subseteq L={\rm cl\textrm{-}{\rm prehull}\mathnormal{{}_{M}(N)}}.

∎

The following proposition gives some upper and lower bounds on the
\rm i
-hull in terms of the
\rm i
-postcore and the
\rm i
-posthull.

Proposition 8.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring,
\rm i
a Nakayama interior, and
A
a submodule of
B
.

(1)

If
A=\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm postcore}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}
or
A
has a unique
\rm i
-postexpansion in
B
, then

\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm postcore}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}\subseteq\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}.

(2)

If
A
is
\rm i
-cobasic or every
\rm i
-postexpansions contains some maximal expansion, then

\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}\subseteq\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm posthull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}.

Proof.

(
1
) If
A={\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm postcore}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}=\bigcap\{C\mid C\textrm{ a }{\rm i}\textrm{-postexpansion}\textrm{ of }A\textrm{ in }B\}.
Since by definition,
A
is contained in every
\rm i
-expansion and the hull is the sum of all expansions, we have
A\subseteq\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}
. Thus
\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm postcore}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}\subseteq\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}
.

If
C
is the unique
\rm i
-postexpansion of
A
in
B
, then by Remark
8.4
we know that

{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm postcore}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}=C\subseteq{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}.

(
2
) First, if
A
is
\rm i
-cobasic, then the only
\rm i
-expansion of
A
in
B
is
A
itself. So
A=\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{A}(B)}
. Since
A
is contained in every
\rm i
-postexpansion of
A
in
B
, we have
A\subseteq\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm posthull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}
. Thus
\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}\subseteq\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm posthull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}
.

Next, suppose every
\rm i
-postexpansion contains some maximal
\rm i
-expansion. Then maximal
\rm i
-expansions exist and we know that

{\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}}=\sum\{C\mid C\text{ a maximal {\rm i}-expansion of }A\text{ and }(C,B)\in\mathcal{P}\}.

Since every
\rm i
-postexpansion contains a maximal
\rm i
-expansion, the sum of all maximal
\rm i
-expansions is contained in the sum of all
\rm i
-postexpansions. Thus

\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm hull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}\subseteq\rm i\textrm{-}{\rm posthull}\mathnormal{{}^{B}(A)}.

∎

Definition 9.1

We collect a few facts about the special part of the tight closure below:

Let

(1)

. However, since
(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{\ell})^{\rm cl}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
, then it must be the case that
(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{\ell})^{\rm cl}=\mathfrak{a}
as both
(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{\ell})^{\rm cl}
and
\mathfrak{a}
are elements of
{\bf I}^{\prime}_{\rm cl}(I)
and they both must be maximal in
{\bf I}^{\prime}_{\rm cl}(I)
. This contradicts
J
being a minimal
\rm cl
-reduction of
\mathfrak{a}
. Hence
\ell\leq k-1
.

Now we need to show that
I^{{\rm clsp}}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
. Suppose
x\in I^{{\rm clsp}}\setminus\mathfrak{a}
. Since
\mathfrak{a}+(x)
is a
\rm cl
-reduction of
I
by definition of
\rm cl
-prereduction, then for any minimal
\rm cl
-reduction
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1})
of
\mathfrak{a}
, we have that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1},x)^{\rm cl}=(\mathfrak{a}+(x))^{\rm cl}=I
. However, this implies that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1},x)
is a minimal
\rm cl
-reduction of
I
which is a contradiction by Proposition
9.8
.
∎

The following two corollaries follow directly from Theorem
9.12
.

Corollary 9.13
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local excellent normal ring of characteristic
p>0
and
I
be a tightly closed ideal. Suppose that the
*
-spread of
I
is
k
. Then the set of all
*
-prereductions of
I
is

{\bf P}_{*}(I)=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k-1})+I^{*{\rm sp}}\mid(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})\text{ a minimal }*\text{-reduction of }I\}.

Corollary 9.14
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local excellent normal ring of characteristic
p>0
and
I
be a Frobenius closed ideal. Suppose that the
F
-spread of
I
is
k
. Then the set of all
F
-prereductions of
I
is

{\bf P}_{F}(I)=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k-1})+I^{F{\rm sp}}\mid(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})\text{ a minimal }F\text{-reduction of }I\}.

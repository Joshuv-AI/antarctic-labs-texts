Quantum symmetries of the deformation quantization of S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3)
Mitsuru Wilson

Abstract
. We prove a criterion of when a coaction of a compact Lie group on an algebra of continuous functions on a compact manifold extends to a coaction of deformation quantizations of the Lie group on the deformation quantization of the algebra. We compute an explicit example of a compact quantum group S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) θ SU(3)_{\theta} , which arises as a deformation quantization of the Lie group S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) by an action of its maximal torus. Using the criterion, we determine exactly when the action of S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) on S 5 S^{5} extends to a coaction of S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) θ SU(3)_{\theta} on the noncommutative 5-sphere S θ ′ 5 S^{5}_{\theta^{\prime}} . Furthermore, this coaction is shown to be cotransitive. A coaction of S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) θ SU(3)_{\theta} on the product of two noncommutative 5-sphere ( S 5 × S 5 ) θ ′ (S^{5}\times S^{5})_{\theta^{\prime}} is constructed and we derive a nontrivial subalgebra of coinvariant elements.

Noncommutative toric manifolds and deformation quantization of manifolds

In this section we discuss the construction of noncommutative toric manifolds as a special
case of Rieffel’s deformation quantization along the action of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
15
]
. The construction of
noncommutative toric manifolds proceeds as follows
[
7
]
.
Given a compact Riemannian manifold
M
endowed with an action of a
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
,

n\geq 2

C^{\infty}(M)
of smooth functions on

isotypic components
C^{\infty}(M)_{\vec{r}}=\big\{f\in C^{\infty}(M):\alpha_{t}(f)=e^{2\pi it\cdot\vec{r}}f\big\}
indexed by
\vec{r}\in\widehat{\mathbb{T}^{n}}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}

A deformed algebra structure can, then, be given by the linear extension of the product of two functions
f_{\vec{r}}
and
g_{\vec{s}}
in some
isotypic components. A new product
\times_{J}
on these elements is given by

(2.1)

f_{r}\times g_{s}=\chi(r,s)f_{r}g_{s},

where
\chi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is an antisymmetric bicharacter on the Pontryagin dual

\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\widehat{\mathbb{T}}^{n}

\mathbb{T}^{n}

\overline{\chi(s,r)}=\chi(r,s)

\chi(r,s+t)=\chi(r,s)\chi(r,t),\qquad\chi(r+s,t)=\chi(r,t)\chi(s,t),\qquad s,t\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

ensures the associativity of the new product. For instance,

\chi(r,s):=\exp\left(\pi i~r\cdot\theta s\right)

where
\theta=\left(\theta_{jk}\right)
is a real skew symmetric
n\times n
matrix gives a noncommutative
deformation of the algebra structure. The involution for the new product is given by the complex conjugation
of the functions. We denote the algebra with the new multiplication
\times_{\theta}
by
C(M_{\theta}).

The definition of a new product in (

[
15
]

by viewing the action as a periodic action of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. First of all, Rieffel expressed the deformed product of an algebra endowed with an action
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle a\times_{J}b=\int_{V\times V}\alpha_{Js}(a)\alpha_{t}(b)~e^{2\pi is\cdot t}~ds~dt

and

A

J
is a skew symmetric real

matrix
[
15
]
. This integral may be interpreted

\alpha:V\rightarrow

(A)
is a strongly continuous action of a finite dimensional vector space

on

A
. The oscillatory integral (

of the smooth subalgebra
A^{\infty}
(which is
a Fréchet algebra) of
A
under the action
\alpha
i.e.

is smooth.

A^{\infty}
can be given a suitable pre

is
continuous in a way that the completion with respect to this norm obtains

A_{J}
. This justifies that the deformed product can be
extended to the entire algebra
A_{J}

It should be remarked that the smooth subalgebra remains

If the action

The noncommutative toric manifolds

3.
Compact quantum Lie groups associated with
n
-torus

We apply the construction in Section

With the notations above, let

2.1

with

We denote the deformed algebra by

the deformation

[
14
]

In fact, the coproduct defined by

\Delta:C(G_{\theta})\rightarrow C(G_{\theta})\otimes C(G_{\theta})
. Here, the interpretation of

C\left((G\times G)_{\theta\oplus\theta}\right)\cong C(G_{\theta})\otimes C(G_{\theta})

[
15
]

the counit
\epsilon(f)(x)=f(e)
remain a homomorphism and the coinverse

and they satisfy all the compatibility conditions in the deformation. Lastly, since the normalized Haar measure determines a linear functional

\mu
on
C(G)
,
\mu
gives a normalized state on
C(G_{\theta})
. This state extends to a normalized tracial state

[
15
]

Theorem 4.2 of
[
15
]
.

The Haar measure
\mu

determines a Haar state on the quantum group

.
That is, a continuous linear functional
\mu_{\theta}
that is unimodular in the sense that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(id\otimes\mu_{\theta}\right)\circ\Delta=\iota\circ\mu_{\theta}\\
\left(\mu_{\theta}\otimes id\right)\circ\Delta=\iota\circ\mu_{\theta},\end{split}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(id\otimes\mu_{\theta}\right)\left(1\otimes a\right)\left(\Delta b\right)=\left(id\otimes\mu_{\theta}\right)\left(\left(S\otimes id\right)\Delta a\right)\left(1\otimes b\right),\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu_{\theta}\circ S=\mu_{\theta}.\end{split}

Furthermore,

We denote this faithful trace simply by

.

In

5.

As an example, we construct a coaction of

\displaystyle z_{j}z_{k}=e^{2\pi i\theta_{jk}}z_{k}z_{j},\qquad z_{j}z_{k}^{*}=e^{2\pi i\theta_{kj}}z_{k}^{*}z_{j},\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{3}z_{k}z_{k}^{*}=1.

Definition 5.1

Let

\displaystyle\rho:A\rightarrow H\otimes A

\left(\Delta\otimes id\right)\circ\rho=\left(id\otimes\rho\right)\circ\rho

\left(\epsilon\otimes id\right)\circ\rho=id

For instance,

is given by the matrix multiplication

Let

Theorem 5.2

Let

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\sum_{\vec{n},\vec{m}}e^{\pi i\theta(\vec{n,}\vec{m})}a_{\vec{n}}b_{\vec{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{n},\vec{m}}e^{\pi i\theta(\vec{n,}\vec{m})}\rho(a_{\vec{n}})\rho(b_{\vec{m}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{n},\vec{m}}e^{\pi i\theta(\vec{n,}\vec{m})}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho(\alpha_{t}(a))\rho(\alpha_{s}(b))e^{-2\pi i(t\cdot\vec{n}+s\cdot\vec{m})}dtds

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
A
be an algebra and
H
a Hopf algebra both equipped with

-torus

\rho:A\rightarrow H\otimes A

of

. Moreover, the algebra of
coinvariant elements
A_{\theta}^{\mathrm{co}H_{\theta}}\subset A_{\theta}
consists of the same elements as
A^{\mathrm{co}H}
.

Proof.

First, we show that
\rho
remains a homomorphism under the assumptions. Let

a=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{l}
and
b=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}b_{r}
,
a_{l}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\alpha_{t}(a)e^{2\pi il\cdot t}dt
and
b_{r}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\alpha_{t}(b)e^{2\pi ir\cdot t}dt

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho(a\times_{\theta}b)&=\rho(\sum_{l,r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{\pi i\theta(l,r)}a_{l}b_{r})\\
&=\rho\left(\sum_{l,r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{\pi i\theta(l,r)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\alpha_{t}(a)e^{2\pi il\cdot t}dt\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\alpha_{t}(b)e^{2\pi ir\cdot t}dt\right)\right)\\
&=\left(\sum_{l,r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{\pi i\theta(l,r)}\left(\rho(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\alpha_{t}(a)e^{2\pi il\cdot t}dt)\right)\left(\rho(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\alpha_{t}(b)e^{2\pi ir\cdot t}dt)\right)\right)\\
&=\left(\sum_{l,r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{\pi i\theta(l,r)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\alpha_{t}(\rho(a))e^{2\pi il\cdot t}dt\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\alpha_{t}(\rho(b))e^{2\pi ir\cdot t}dt\right)\right)\\
&=\rho(a)\times_{\theta}\rho(b).\end{split}

To check that the other compatibility conditions, since
\rho
already satisfies

\displaystyle(1_{H}\otimes\rho)\circ\rho(a)

\displaystyle=(\Delta\otimes 1_{A})\circ\rho(a)

\displaystyle(\epsilon\otimes 1_{A})\circ\rho(a)

\displaystyle=a

and the coproduct

and the counit

is a coaction with respect to the deformed

We would like to show, now, that
A_{\theta}^{\mathrm{co}H_{\theta}}=A^{\mathrm{co}H}
. Recall that

A^{\mathrm{co}H}=\{a\in A:\rho(a)=1\otimes a\}.

Since
\rho(a)=1\otimes a
for all

and elements of

can be regarded as the elements of
A_{\theta}
, by the very extension of
\rho
,
we have that
a\in A_{\theta}^{\mathrm{co}H_{\theta}}
. Likewise if

, then

\rho(c)=1\otimes c
. Using the same argument by interchanging
A_{\theta}^{\mathrm{co}H_{\theta}}
and
A_{\theta}^{\mathrm{co}H_{\theta}}
, we have that

.
∎

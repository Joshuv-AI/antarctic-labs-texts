Abstract
. We prove -under certain conditions (local-global compatibility and vanishing of modulo p p cohomology), a generalization of a theorem of Galatius and Venkatesh. We consider the case of 𝖦𝖫 ⁡ ( N ) \GL(N) over a CM field; we construct a Hecke-equivariant injection from the divisible group associated to the first fundamental group of a derived deformation ring to the Selmer group of the twisted dual adjoint motive with divisible coefficients and we identify its cokernel as the first Tate-Shafarevich group of this motive. Actually, we also construct similar maps for higher homotopy groups with values in exterior powers of Selmer groups, although with less precise control on their kernel and cokernel. By a result of Y. Cai generalizing previous results by Galatius-Venkatesh on the graded cohomology group of a locally symmetric space, our maps relate the (non-Eisenstein) localization of the graded cohomology group for a locally symmetric space to the exterior algebra of the Selmer group of the Tate dual of the adjoint representation. We generalize this to Hida families as well.

3.1.1.

For
v\in S

1) Note that the proof uses the fact that the matrix

is regular nilpotent.

2) This result is also proven in
[
CHT08

[

3) The functor

(

Proof.

Let
C_{0}
be the centralizer of

in the standard Borel
B\subset G
.
The formal scheme
\mathcal{C}
of centralizers of liftings of

is isomorphic to the principal homogeneous formal scheme
\widehat{\GL_{N}/C_{0}}
,
hence is formally smooth of dimension

.
There is a fibration of functors

Therefore, to study the formal smoothness of

and study its fiber
\mathcal{F}_{v,[C]}^{min,\square}
in
\mathcal{F}_{v}^{min,\square}

.
We therefore consider
\rho_{v}
such that for any

\rho_{v}(\tau)=\exp(t_{v}(\tau)J_{N})

Let
\Phi_{v}=\diag(q_{v}^{N-1},\ldots,1)

\widehat{C}_{0}

.
By the considerations above, we see easily that the map

This proves the formal smoothness of

.
Let
\Lambda_{\mathbf{I}}
be the image of
\Lambda
in
\mathbf{I}
. Let
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}}=\mathcal{R}_{h}\underline{\otimes}_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{\mathbf{I}}

and
R_{\mathbf{I}}=R_{h}\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{\mathbf{I}}
. We have
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})=R_{\mathbf{I}}

and there is a natural
R_{h}
-algebra homomorphism

\pi_{\mathbf{I}}\colon\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{h})\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{h})=0

\pi_{\mathbf{I}}=0

\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
of weight
\lambda^{\prime}

Theorem 15
.

There are natural
\mathbf{I}
-linear injective homomorphisms

GV_{h}\colon\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{h})\otimes_{R_{h}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(F,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{\theta}(1)\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}).

and

GV_{\mathbf{I}}\colon\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})\otimes_{R_{\mathbf{I}}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(F,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{\theta}(1)\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}).

and we have
GV_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\pi_{\mathbf{I}}=GV_{h}
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 1
, let
\mathbf{I}_{n}=\mathbf{I}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}
. For any finite
\mathbf{I}_{n}
-module
M
(which can be viewed as a
\mathbb{T}_{h}
-module),
we consider the simplicial ring
\Theta_{n}=\mathbf{I}_{n}\oplus M[1]
.
Let
L_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{h})
be the set of homotopy equivalence classes of simplicial ring homomorphisms
\Phi\colon\mathcal{R}_{h}\to\Theta_{n}
such that

\pr_{n}\circ\Phi=\phi_{n}
. Note that any such
\Phi
factors through
\mathcal{R}_{h}\to\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}}
into a homomorphism

\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}\colon\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}}\to\Theta_{n}
. So,
L_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{h})
can be reinterpreted as the set
L_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})

of homotopy equivalence classes of

\Hom_{\pr_{n}}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}},\Theta_{n}).

There is a canonical bijection

L_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{h})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{L}}(F,\Ad(\rho_{\theta})\otimes_{\mathbf{I}_{n}}M)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the minimal ordinary condition.
Moreover, as in
[
GV18
, Lemma 15.1]
and in Formula (
5
), for each
n\geq 1
, we have a
\mathbb{T}_{h}
-linear homomorphism

\pi(n,\mathcal{R}_{\ast})\colon L_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast})\to\Hom_{\mathbb{T}_{h}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast}),M)

where
\ast=h,\mathbf{I}
.
As in Proposition
5
, this homomorphism is surjective.
We take
M=\mathbf{I}_{n}
. Its Pontryagin dual is
M^{\vee}=\Hom_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbf{I},K/\mathcal{O})[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}]
.
Let
\widehat{\mathbf{I}}=\Hom_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbf{I},K/\mathcal{O})
. Let
\pi_{1}\mathcal{R}_{\ast,\theta}=\pi_{1}\mathcal{R}_{\ast}\otimes_{\mathbb{T}_{h},\theta}\mathbf{I}
.
We see that we can rewrite the Pontryagin dual of the right hand side of (
8
) as

\Hom_{\mathcal{O}}(\Hom_{\mathbb{T}_{h}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast}),M),K/\mathcal{O})=\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast})_{\theta},\mathbf{I}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}),\widehat{\mathbf{I}})

Since

\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\widehat{\mathbf{I}},\widehat{\mathbf{I}})=\mathbf{I}
, we have

\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\widehat{\mathbf{I}}[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}],\widehat{\mathbf{I}})=\mathbf{I}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}

hence

\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast})_{\theta},\mathbf{I}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n})=\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast})_{\theta},\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\widehat{\mathbf{I}}[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}],\widehat{\mathbf{I}})))=

\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast})_{\theta}\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}],\widehat{\mathbf{I}})),

but for
S=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast})_{\theta}\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}]
, we have
\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(S,\widehat{\mathbf{I}}),\widehat{\mathbf{I}})=S
,
so we conclude

\Hom_{\mathcal{O}}(\Hom_{\mathbb{T}_{h}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast}),M),K/\mathcal{O})=\pi_{1}\mathcal{R}_{\ast,\theta}\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}].

Similarly, the Pontryagin dual of the left hand side of (
8
) is

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(F,\Ad\rho_{\theta}^{\ast}(1)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}M^{\vee})

which can be rewritten as

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(F,\Ad\rho_{\theta}^{\ast}(1)\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}^{n}])

Let
GV_{\ast,n}
be the Pontryagin dual of
\pi(n,\mathcal{R}_{\ast})
. Let
GV_{\ast}=\varinjlim_{n}GV_{\ast,n}
, it provides the desired injection

GV_{\ast}\colon\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\ast})\otimes_{\mathbb{T}_{h}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(F,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{\theta}(1)\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}).

∎

8.1.
Interpolation properties

Let
P\in\Spec\mathbf{I}
be a codimension one prime; assume that
\mathcal{O}_{P}=\mathbf{I}/P
is a dvr. Let
K_{P}=\Frac(\mathcal{O}_{P})
.
We have
\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{P},K/\mathcal{O})=K_{P}/\mathcal{O}_{P})
.
The following exact control theorem holds for the minimal ordinary Selmer group.
We put

{\mathop{\rm Sel}}^{\ast}_{\mathbf{I}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(F,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{\theta}(1)\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}),\quad{\mathop{\rm Sel}}^{\ast}_{P}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(F,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{P}(1)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P}}K_{P}/\mathcal{O}_{P})

Proposition 10
.

For any prime ideal
P\subset\mathbf{I}
,

{\mathop{\rm Sel}}^{\ast}_{\mathbf{I}}[P]={\mathop{\rm Sel}}^{\ast}_{P}.

Proof.

We apply
[
SU14
, Proposition 3.2.8]
: Let
T
be a
\mathbf{I}[G_{F}]
-module, which is finite free over
\mathbf{I}
.
Then, for any ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathbf{I}
,
\mathop{\rm Sel}(T\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}})[{\mathfrak{a}}]=\mathop{\rm Sel}(T\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}/{\mathfrak{a}}})

under the assumption that

T\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}
has no non trivial subquotient with trivial Galois action.

Let us check this condition for
T=\Ad\rho_{\mathbf{I}}^{\ast}(1)
. Let
M
be a
\mathbf{I}[G_{F}]
-submodule of

T\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}

and
N
a
\mathbf{I}[G_{F}]
-quotient of
M
.
By applying
\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(-,\widehat{\mathbf{I}})
, one sees that
\widehat{M}

is a
\mathbf{I}[G_{F}]
-quotient of
\Ad{\rho}_{\mathbf{I}}
. The reduction of this homomorphism modulo

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathbf{I}}
is null by absolute irreducibility of
\Ad\overline{\rho}
. By Nakayama’s lemma,
this implies
\widehat{M}=0
.
The same holds for the
\mathbf{I}[G_{F}]
-submodule
\widehat{N}\subset\widehat{M}
.
∎

Let
M_{\mathbf{I}}
be the Pontryagin dual of
{\mathop{\rm Sel}}^{\ast}_{\mathbf{I}}
.
Let
\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}
be the Pontryagin dual of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})\otimes_{R_{\mathbf{I}}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}
. We have

\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}=\Hom_{R_{\mathbf{I}}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}}),\mathbf{I})=\Hom_{{\mathbf{I}}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})_{\theta},\mathbf{I}).

It is a finitely generated torsion free
\mathbf{I}
-module.
Let
N_{\mathbf{I}}=\Ker(M_{\mathbf{I}}\to\Phi_{\mathbf{I}})
be the Pontryagin dual of
\Coker\,GV_{\mathbf{I}}
.
We have a short exact sequence

0\to N_{\mathbf{I}}\to M_{\mathbf{I}}\to\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}\to 0

Let
{\mathop{\rm Sel}}_{\mathbf{I}}=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Gamma,\Ad\rho_{\mathbf{I}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{I}})
.

Theorem 16
.

The
\mathbf{I}
-modules
M_{\mathbf{I}}
and
\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}
have rank
\ell_{0}
and
\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}
is free.

Proof.

Let
P
be an arithmetic prime of weight
\lambda^{\prime}

congruent to
\lambda
modulo
p
; let
\mathcal{O}_{P}=\mathbf{I}/P
.
Generically, it is a dvr. We compare

GV_{\mathbf{I}}\colon\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})\otimes_{R_{\mathbf{I}}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Gamma,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{\theta}(1)\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}})

to the "classical" map

GV_{P}\colon\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda^{\prime}})\otimes_{R_{\lambda^{\prime}}}K_{P}/\mathcal{O}_{P}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Gamma,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{P}(1)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P}}K_{P}/\mathcal{O}_{P})

We have a commutative diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}GV_{\mathbf{I}}&\colon&\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})\otimes_{R_{\mathbf{I}}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}}&\hookrightarrow&\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Gamma,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{\theta}(1)\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\widehat{\mathbf{I}})\\
&&\uparrow&&\uparrow\\
GV_{P}&\colon&\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda^{\prime}})\otimes_{R_{\lambda^{\prime}}}K_{P}/\mathcal{O}_{P}&\hookrightarrow&\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Gamma,\Ad^{\ast}\rho_{P}(1)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{P}}K_{P}/\mathcal{O}_{P})\end{array}

Let

be the Pontryagin dual of
{\mathop{\rm Sel}}^{\ast}_{P}
.
Similarly, let
\Phi_{P}
denote the Pontryagin dual of

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda^{\prime}})\otimes_{R_{\lambda^{\prime}}}K_{P}/\mathcal{O}_{P}

By Pontryagin duality we obtain a diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}M_{\mathbf{I}}&\to&\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
M_{P}&\to&\Phi_{P}\end{array}

where the two horizontal arrow are surjective and where the left downarrow

M_{\mathbf{I}}/PM_{\mathbf{I}}\cong M_{P}.

It implies that the right downarrow

\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}/P\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}\rightarrow\Phi_{P}

Let us prove it is an isomorphism.
Note that
\Phi_{P}
is free of rank
\ell_{0}

11
.
We have

\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}/P\Phi_{\mathbf{I}}=\Hom_{\mathbf{I}}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})_{\theta},\mathbf{I}/P)=\Hom_{\mathbf{I}/P}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{I}})_{\theta}\otimes_{\mathbf{I}}\mathbf{I}/P,\mathbf{I}/P)

be the integral closure of
\mathbf{I}

For

\mathbf{I}/P=\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}/P\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}

\Phi_{{}_{b}I}

\mathbf{I}

, resp.

\mathbf{I}

∎

For any

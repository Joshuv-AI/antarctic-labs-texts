Internal mode-induced growth in 3 3 d nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations Thanks: F.P. was supported in part by a start-up grant from the University of Toronto, and NSERC grant RGPIN-2018-06487.
Tristan Léger, Fabio Pusateri

Abstract
. This note complements the paper [ 20 ] by proving a scattering statement for solutions of nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations with an internal mode in 3 3 d. We show that small solutions exhibit growth around a one-dimensional set in frequency space and become of order one in L ∞ L^{\infty} after a short transient time. The dynamics are driven by the feedback of the internal mode into the equation for the field (continuous spectral) component. The main part of the proof consists of showing suitable smallness for a “good” component of the radiation field. This is done in two steps: first, using the machinery developed in [ 20 ] , we reduce the problem to bounding a certain quadratic normal form correction. Then we control this latter by establishing some refined estimates for certain bilinear operators with singular kernels.

Introduction

1.1.
Background

The study of the small data regime for dispersive PDEs with quadratic nonlinearities
has attracted a lot of attention in recent years, and is closely related to several open
questions about the asymptotic stability of coherent structures, such as (topological) solitons.
This has turned out to be a challenging problem, since quadratic nonlinearities
are strong enough to influence the long-time behavior of solutions and may lead to blow-up
even in a weakly nonlinear regime.

In recent years, several general methods have been developed to tackle
problems about global existence and long-time asymptotics of nonlinear (and quasilinear) dispersive equations

Set-up and assumptions.

Let
\bf{P_{c}}
denote the projection onto the continuous spectral subspace,
namely, for every
\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}})

(1.5)

\displaystyle{\bf P_{c}}\psi:=\psi-(\phi,\psi)\phi,\qquad(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\psi_{1}\psi_{2}\,dx.

We make the following assumptions:

•

Regularity and decay of
V
: We assume
V\in\mathcal{S}
, but a finite amount of regularity and decay (measured in weighted Sobolev spaces) would suffice.

•

Coupling to continuous spectrum
:
\lambda\in(1/2,1)
so that
\lambda\notin\sigma_{ac}(-\Delta+V+1)

and
2\lambda\in\sigma_{ac}(-\Delta+V+1).

•

Fermi Golden Rule
: The “Fermi Golden Rule” resonance condition holds:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\frac{\pi}{2\lambda}\Big({\bf{P_{c}}}\phi^{2},\delta(L-2\lambda){\bf P_{c}}\phi^{2}\Big)>0,

where
L:=\sqrt{-\Delta+V+1}
.

•

Genericity of
V
: the
0
energy level is regular for
H:=-\Delta+V
,
that is,
0
is not an eigenvalue,
nor a resonance, i.e., there is no
\psi\in\langle x\rangle^{1/2+}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
H\psi=0
.
Such a potential
V
is said to be ‘generic’.

Distorted Fourier transform.

For a regular and decaying
V
(as in our assumptions above)
we can decompose
L^{2}
into absolutely continuous and pure point subspaces:

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=L^{2}_{ac}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\oplus L^{2}_{pp}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
where
L^{2}_{pp}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathrm{span}(\phi)
.
Let
\psi(x,\xi)
denote the generalized eigenfunctions solving for
\xi,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(-\Delta+V)\psi(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{2}\psi(x,\xi),\quad\mbox{with}\quad\big|\psi(x,\xi)-e^{ix\cdot\xi}\big|\longrightarrow 0,\quad\mbox{as}\quad|x|\rightarrow\infty,\end{split}

with the Sommerfeld radiation condition
r(\partial_{r}-i|k|)(\psi(x,k)-e^{ix\cdot k})\rightarrow 0
,
for
r=|x|\rightarrow\infty
.
We can then define a unitary operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, the distorted Fourier Transform (dFT), as

(1.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}f(\xi):=\widetilde{f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\lim_{R\to+\infty}\int_{|x|\leqslant R}f(x)\overline{\psi(x,\xi)}dx,

with inverse

(1.9)

\displaystyle\big(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f\big)(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\lim_{R\to+\infty}\int_{|\xi|\leqslant R}f(\xi)\psi(x,\xi)d\xi.

In particular, for any

we can write

g(x)=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\big(\widetilde{{\bf P}_{c}g}\big)+(g,\phi)\phi
.
Moreover

diagonalizes the Schrödinger operator (on the continuous spectrum),
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}{\bf{P_{c}}}H=|\xi|^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m=m(\xi,\eta)
,
\xi,\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be a bounded multiplier localized at frequencies

|\xi|\approx 2^{K},|\eta|\approx 2^{L}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\big|\nabla_{\xi}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\eta}^{\beta}m(\xi,\eta)\big|\lesssim 2^{-|\alpha|K-|\beta|L},\ \ 0\leqslant|\alpha|+|\beta|\leqslant 4,

and let
b_{0}=b_{0}(\omega,\xi)
,
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2},\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, be such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\big|\nabla_{\xi}^{\beta}b_{0}(\omega,\xi)\big|\lesssim 1+\big(|\xi|/\langle\xi\rangle\big)^{1-|\beta|},\ \ 0\leqslant|\beta|\leqslant 4.

We have (note the frequency localization on the profile
h
)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\widetilde{f}(\xi-\eta)(\varphi_{M}\widetilde{h})(\sigma)m(\xi,\eta)b_{0}(\eta/|\eta|,\sigma)\mathrm{p.v.}\frac{1}{|\eta|-|\sigma|}d\eta d\sigma\Bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\\
\lesssim 2^{L+M}\langle 2^{M}\rangle^{-2}\|m\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi,\eta}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|h\|_{H^{2}},\end{split}

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\widetilde{f}(\eta+\sigma)\widetilde{h}(\sigma)m(\eta,\sigma)\mathrm{p.v.}\frac{1}{|\eta|-|\xi|}b_{0}(\eta/|\eta|,\xi)d\eta d\sigma\Bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\lesssim 2^{2K}\langle 2^{K}\rangle^{-2}\|f\|_{H^{2}}\|h\|_{H^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1).
We stress that the implicit constants in the argument below do not depend on
\epsilon.

Denote
\varphi_{\epsilon}
a radial cut-off function supported on the region
\epsilon<r<\epsilon^{-1}.

We first prove the more challenging inequality (
3.4
).
We start by inserting a cut-off
\varphi_{k}(\xi)
and pass to polar coordinates

\xi=\rho\phi
and
\eta=r\omega
,
\phi,\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and estimate
the left-hand side of (
3.4
) by

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon>0}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{\phi\in\mathbb{S}^{2},\rho>0}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widetilde{f}(r\omega+\sigma)m(r\omega,\sigma)\widetilde{h}(\sigma)d\sigma\,\frac{\varphi_{\epsilon}(r-\rho)}{r-\rho}\varphi_{k}(\rho)b_{0}(\omega,\rho\phi)r^{2}drd\omega\Big|.

Let us define
f_{1}:=L^{2}f
and
h_{1}:=L^{2}h
, and

\displaystyle F(r,\omega):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widetilde{f_{1}}(r\omega+\sigma)m_{1}(r\omega,\sigma)\widetilde{h_{1}}(\sigma)d\sigma,\quad m_{1}(\eta,\sigma):=\frac{m(\eta,\sigma)}{\langle\eta+\sigma\rangle^{2}\langle\sigma\rangle^{2}}.

We then take an inverse Fourier transform in
\rho
in (
3.5
);
using the fact that the Fourier transform of the Hilbert transform kernel is bounded,
we can bound (
3.5
) by a constant times

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon>0}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{\phi\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\rho\rightarrow x}\Bigg\{\Bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}}r^{2}F(r,\omega)\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r)\frac{\varphi_{\epsilon}(r-\rho)}{r-\rho}dr\Bigg]\varphi_{k}(\rho)b_{0}(\omega,\rho\phi)\Bigg\}d\omega\Bigg\|_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\epsilon>0}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{\omega,\phi\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}\bigg\|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\rho\rightarrow x}\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{\epsilon}(r)}{r}\ast\big(r^{2}F(r,\omega)\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r)\big)\bigg)\bigg\|_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}{\big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\rho\rightarrow x}\big(\varphi_{k}(\cdot)b_{0}(\omega,\cdot\phi)\big)\big\|}_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{r\rightarrow x}\big(r^{2}F(r,\omega)\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r)\big)\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{\omega,\phi}L^{1}_{x}}\cdot\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg\|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\rho\rightarrow x}\big(\varphi_{k}(\cdot)b_{0}(\omega,\cdot\phi)\big)\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{\omega,\phi}L^{1}_{x}}.

To bound the second term in (
3.6
) we use the pointwise bound

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\rho\rightarrow x}\big(\varphi_{k}(\cdot)b_{0}(\omega,\cdot\phi)\big)(x)\big|\lesssim\frac{2^{k}}{(1+|x|)(1+2^{k}/\langle 2^{k}\rangle|x|)^{2}},

which follows by integration by parts in
\rho
and our assumptions (
3.2
) on
b_{0}.

This yields

\displaystyle\bigg\|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\rho\rightarrow x}\big(\varphi_{k}(\cdot)b_{0}(\omega,\cdot\phi)\big)\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{\omega,\phi}L^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\langle 2^{k}\rangle.

We then focus on the first term.
We denote the wave operator by
\mathcal{W}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\widehat{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{W}^{*}=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},

and recall that it is bounded on Sobolev spaces under our assumptions.
We change coordinates by denoting
R_{\omega}
the rotation that sends
\omega
to
e_{1}:=(1,0,0)
and denoting

g^{\omega}:=g\circ R_{\omega}^{-1}
,
so that we find (omitting irrelevant constants)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{r\rightarrow x}\big(r^{2}F(r,\omega)\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r)\big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ixr}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\widetilde{f_{1}}(\sigma+r\omega)m_{1}(r\omega,\sigma)\widetilde{h_{1}}(\sigma)d\sigma\,r^{2}\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\Bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{ir(x-y\cdot\omega)}e^{-i\sigma\cdot(y+z)}m_{1}(r\omega,\sigma)r^{2}\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r)drd\sigma\Bigg]\mathcal{W}^{*}f_{1}(y)\mathcal{W}^{*}h_{1}(z)dydz

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}T[m_{1}](x-y_{1},R_{\omega}^{-1}y+R_{\omega}^{-1}z)(\mathcal{W}^{*}f_{1})^{\omega}(y)(\mathcal{W}^{*}h_{1})^{\omega}(z)dydz.

Note that the last identity defines

.
We then observe that, given the localization of

to
|\eta|\approx 2^{K}
and
|\sigma|\approx 2^{L}
,
using integration by parts and (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\big|T[m_{1}](y_{1},z)\big|=\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{iy_{1}r}e^{-i\sigma\cdot z}m_{1}(r\omega,\sigma)r^{2}\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r)drd\sigma\Bigg|\lesssim\frac{2^{3K}\langle 2^{K}\rangle^{-2}}{\big(1+2^{K}|y_{1}|\big)^{2}}\frac{2^{3L}\langle 2^{L}\rangle^{-2}}{\big(1+2^{L}|z|\big)^{4}}.

This yields the bound

\displaystyle\big\|T[m_{1}]\big\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\lesssim 2^{2K}\langle 2^{K}\rangle^{-2}.

Changing variables

and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality as well as the boundedness of wave operators on

, we have

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{r\rightarrow x}\big(r^{2}F(r,\omega)\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(r)\big)\big|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\big|T[m_{1}](y_{1},R_{\omega}^{-1}z)\big|dy_{1}dz

\displaystyle\times\sup_{(y_{1},z)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big|(\mathcal{W}^{*}f_{1})^{\omega}(x-y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\big|\cdot\big|(\mathcal{W}^{*}h_{1})^{\omega}(x-y_{1}-z_{1},y_{2}-z_{2},y_{3}-z_{3})\big|dxdy_{2}dy_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{2K}\langle 2^{K}\rangle^{-2}{\|f_{1}\|}_{L^{2}}{\|h_{1}\|}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{2}}\|h\|_{H^{2}}.

We now prove the easier inequality (
3.3
).
Passing to polar coordinates
\eta=r\omega
and
\sigma=\rho\phi,

\omega,\phi\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
we estimate using the boundedness of the Hilbert transform on
L^{2},
and recalling that
on the support of the integral

,
|\eta|\approx 2^{L}
and
|\sigma|\approx 2^{M}
:

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\widetilde{f}(\xi-r\omega)m(\xi,r\omega)\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}b_{0}(\omega,\rho\phi)\frac{\varphi_{\epsilon}(r-\rho)}{r-\rho}\widetilde{h}(\rho\phi)\varphi_{M}(\rho)\rho^{2}d\rho d\phi\,r^{2}drd\omega\Bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}{\big\|\widetilde{f}(\xi-r\omega)m(\xi,r\omega)\,r\|}_{L^{2}_{r}(r^{2}dr)}d\omega\cdot\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}\bigg\|\frac{\varphi_{\epsilon}(r)}{r}\ast\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}b_{0}(\omega,r\phi)\widetilde{h}(r\phi)\varphi_{M}(r)r^{2}d\phi\bigg)\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\|m\|}_{L^{\infty}_{\xi,\eta}}\cdot 2^{L}{\big\|\varphi_{L}(\cdot)\widetilde{f}(\xi-\cdot)\big\|}_{L^{2}}\cdot 2^{M}{\big\|\varphi_{M}\widetilde{h}\big\|}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

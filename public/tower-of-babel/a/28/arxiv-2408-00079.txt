Metrologically optimal quantum states under noise
Chao Yin, Victor V. Albert, Sisi Zhou

Abstract
We propose a class of metrological resource states whose quantum Fisher information scales optimally in both system size and noise rate. In these states, qubits are partitioned into sensing groups with relatively large correlations within a group but small correlations between groups. The states are obtainable from local Hamiltonian evolution, and we design a metrologically optimal and efficient measurement protocol utilizing time-reversed dynamics and single-qubit on-site measurements. Using quantum domino dynamics, we also present a protocol free of the time-reversal step that has an estimation error roughly twice the best possible value. Finally, we show that spin squeezed states are also optimal for noisy metrology under general conditions.

On-site measurement scheme with time-reversal

For locally generated states, we propose an on-site measurement scheme by assuming the ability to time-reverse the evolution

After sensing, one applies a unitary

Spin-squeezing

We have established in

Theorem
1
a sufficient condition for metrological optimality. Here we show this small-correlation condition (

[

46

do not satisfy it in general, but can be optimal (see Appendix).

Theorem 4
.

Suppose state
\rho
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\langle X\right\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\Theta(N),\quad\left\langle\Delta Y^{2}\right\rangle_{\rho}=N\xi^{2},

(31)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta S^{2}\right\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{O}(N/\xi^{2}),\quad\forall S:=\textstyle\sum_{i}S_{i}\text{ with }\left\lVert S_{i}\right\rVert=1

where
X=\sum_{i}X_{i}
etc and
S_{i}
denotes an operator on
i

(33)

Theorem
32
generalizes special SSSs and noise analyzed previously
[
47
,
31
,
48
]
, where it was shown that SSS could saturate the numerical prefactor in
Eq. (
2
)
as well.
Eq. (
31
)
is a standard definition of SSS:
\rho
is polarized in the

in the

exist in the permutation-symmetric subspace of the

Here we require the “polarization”

may mix with “orthogonal” directions

and thus increase its variance.

ensures its impact is sufficiently weak. The condition
Eq. (
32
)
suggests the general usefulness of correlation bounds for noisy metrology.

We remark that SSSs were known to achieve the optimal QFI (with the optimal prefactor) for parameter estimation in general noisy quantum channels, when concatenated with an inner layer of quantum error correcting codes
[

,

. This endows SSSs with a particular advantage in noisy quantum metrology relative to other state families discussed previously, although achieving the optimal prefactor is not a primary focus of this work.

In general, it is not straightforward to prepare SSSs from natural (i.e., time-independent) local dynamics: The squeezing parameter

in recent proposals

65

66

This is in sharp contrast with the preparable domino state above, which has optimal robustness against noise and on-site measurement schemes. Nevertheless, it is unclear whether the domino dynamics can be generalized to higher dimensions. The small-correlation states and SSSs are thus complementary classes of metrological resources, with each offering advantages depending on the context.

9
Discussion

In this work, we prove sufficient conditions for optimal noisy metrology and illustrate them with several examples, from simple quantum parity states to quantum domino dynamics and spin-squeezed states.
Beyond state optimality, we also address the problem of efficient measurement schemes for metrology. In particular, the domino example suggests that on-site measurements apply to wider sensing scenarios beyond GHZ.

Appendix B
Simplifying single-group QFI for a pure state via Wigner-Yanase correlation functions

The single-group QFI, i.e., the QFI of the RDM in a single group (
Eq. (
34
)
) does not have an explicit expression in general. However, if the entire state
\rho_{\theta}
is pure, we show that it is almost equivalent to certain correlation functions, which is more intuitive and easier to work with. The connection turns out to come from the quantity called Wigner-Yanase skew information

:

\mathcal{I}(\rho,A):=\mathrm{tr}\left(\rho A^{2}\right)-\mathrm{tr}\left(\sqrt{\rho}A\sqrt{\rho}A\right),

\rho

.

Proposition 4
.

For a pure global state

, consider bipartitioning the qubits into

and

(complement of
I
). Then the QFI for group
I
in the noiseless case is equivalent to its Wigner-Yanase skew information up to a factor between

and

\mathcal{I}(\rho_{I,\theta}^{(0)},Z_{I})\leq\mathcal{F}(\rho_{I,\theta}^{(0)})\leq 2\,\mathcal{I}(\rho_{I,\theta}^{(0)},Z_{I}).

(61)

Moreover, the Wigner-Yanase skew information can be expressed as

\mathcal{I}(\rho_{I,\theta}^{(0)},Z_{I})=C_{\psi}(Z_{I},Z_{I})-C_{\psi}(Z_{I},A^{\rm c}),

(62)

for some operator
A^{\rm c}
acting on
I^{\rm c}
with norm
\left\lVert A^{\rm c}\right\rVert\leq 2M
.

Proof.

Eq. (
61
)
is known from
[

]
, given that the generator evolving
\rho_{I,\theta}^{(0)}
is
Eq. (
46
)
. As a result, we only need to show

)
.

Writing

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{\alpha}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}}\ket{\alpha}_{I}\otimes\ket{\alpha}_{I^{\rm c}},

(63)

by Schmidt decomposition, we have

\rho_{I,\theta}^{(0)}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta Z_{I}}\left(\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha}_{I}\bra{\alpha}_{I}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta Z_{I}}.

(64)

Defining

\widetilde{Z}_{I}:=Z_{I}-\bra{\psi}Z_{I}\ket{\psi},

(65)

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\rho_{I,\theta}^{(0)},Z_{I})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}\left(\rho_{I,\theta}^{(0)},\widetilde{Z}_{I}\right)=\mathcal{I}\left(\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha}_{I}\bra{\alpha}_{I},\widetilde{Z}_{I}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\bra{\alpha}\widetilde{Z}_{I}^{2}\ket{\alpha}_{I}-\sum_{\alpha\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}}\bra{\alpha}\widetilde{Z}_{I}\ket{\beta}_{I}\bra{\alpha}\widetilde{Z}_{I}\ket{\beta}^{*}_{I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\bra{\alpha}\widetilde{Z}_{I}^{2}\ket{\alpha}_{I}-\sum_{\alpha\beta}\sqrt{p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}}\bra{\alpha}\widetilde{Z}_{I}\ket{\beta}_{I}\bra{\alpha}A^{\rm c}\ket{\beta}_{I^{\rm c}},

\displaystyle=\bra{\psi}\widetilde{Z}_{I}^{2}\ket{\psi}-\bra{\psi}\widetilde{Z}_{I}A^{\rm c}\ket{\psi}=C_{\psi}(Z_{I},Z_{I})-C_{\psi}(Z_{I},A^{\rm c}),

(66)

where

denotes complex conjugate.
Here the first line comes from the fact that the skew information

is unchanged when adding identity to the operator
Z_{I}
, and that the evolution

commutes through
\widetilde{Z}_{I}
. The second line comes from directly expanding

acting on

(67)

and setting other matrix elements to

The last line of

simplifies the terms by invoking the Schmidt decomposition

, and the definition of the connected correlation function noticing

Finally,

\left\lVert A^{\rm c}\right\rVert=\left\lVert(P\widetilde{Z}_{I}P)^{*}\right\rVert=\left\lVert P\widetilde{Z}_{I}P\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\widetilde{Z}_{I}\right\rVert\leq 2\left\lVert Z_{I}\right\rVert=2M.

(68)

from

We introduce a weak solution concept (called ”rough weak solutions”) for singular SDEs with additive
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise (including the Brownian noise case) and prove its equivalence to martingale solutions from
[
KP22
]
in the Young and rough regularity regime. In the rough regime this leads to the construction of certain rough stochastic sewing integrals involved. For rough weak solutions, we can then prove a generalized Itô formula. Furthermore, we show that canonical weak solutions are wellposed in the Young case (and equivalent to rough weak solutions), while ill-posed in the rough case. For the latter, we construct a counterexample for uniqueness in law.

Keywords: fractional Laplace operator, singular Lévy SDEs, equivalence of weak solution concepts, paracontrolled distributions, rough stochastic integrals

Introduction

We study the weak well-posedness of SDEs

dX_{t}=V(t,X_{t})dt+dL_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.1)

driven by non-degenerate symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy noise
L
for
\alpha\in(1,2]
and with drift
V(t,\cdot)
that is a Besov distribution in the space variable.

The special case where
L
is a Brownian motion has received lots of attention in recent years, since such
singular diffusions
arise as models for stochastic processes in random media. Examples are random directed polymers
[
AKQ14
,
DD16
,
CSZ17
]
, self-attracting Brownian motion in a random medium
[
CC18
]
, or a continuum analogue of Sinai’s random walk in random environment (Brox diffusion,
[
Bro86
]
). Singular diffusions also arise as “stochastic characteristics” of singular SPDEs, for example the KPZ equation, cf.
[
GP17
]
, or the parabolic Anderson model, cf.
[
CC18
]
.

SDEs with distributional drifts were first considered in
[
BC01
,
FRW03
]
in the one-dimensional time-homogeneous setting.
Of course, for distributional
V
the point evaluation
V(t,X_{t})
is not meaningful, so a priori it is not clear how to make sense of (
1.1
). The appropriate perspective is not to consider
V(t,X_{t})
at fixed time
t
, but rather to work with the integral
\int_{0}^{t}V(s,X_{s})ds
. The intuition is that, because of small scale oscillations of
X
induced by the oscillations of
L
, we only “see an averaged version” of
V
and this gives rise to some regularization, at least for a Brownian motion or a sufficiently “wild” Lévy jump process. On the other hand we would not expect any regularization from a Poisson process. In the Brownian case, this intuition can be made rigorous in different ways. For example via a Zvonkin transform which removes the drift, cf.
[
Zvo74
,
Ver81
,
BC01
,
KR05
,
FGP10
,
FIR17
]
, by considering the associated martingale problem and by constructing a domain for the singular generator, cf.
[
FRW03
,
DD16
,
CC18
]
, or by Dirichlet forms as in
[
Mat94
]
. In the one-dimensional case it is also possible to apply an Itô-McKean construction based on space and time transformations, cf.
[
Bro86
]
.

In
[
KP22
]
we followed the martingale problem approach in the spirit of
[
DD16
,
CC18
]
who considered the Brownian case. Formally,
X
solves (
1.1
) if and only if it solves the martingale problem for the generator
\mathcal{G}^{V}=\partial_{t}-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}+V\cdot\nabla
, where the fractional Laplacian
(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})
is the generator of
L
. That is, for all functions
u
in the domain of
\mathcal{G}^{V}
, the process
u(t,X_{t})-u(0,x)-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}^{V}u(s,X_{s})ds
,
t\geqslant 0
, is a martingale. One difficulty is that the domain of
\mathcal{G}^{V}
necessarily has a trivial intersection with the set of smooth functions: If
u
is smooth, then
(\partial_{t}-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})u
is smooth as well, while for non-constant
u
the product
V\cdot\nabla u
is only a distribution, but not a continuous function. If we want
\mathcal{G}^{V}u
to be a continuous function, then
u
has to be non-smooth, so that
(\partial_{t}-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})u
is also a distribution which has appropriate cancellations with
V\cdot\nabla u
.

We can find such
u
by solving the Kolmogorov backward equation for continuous right-hand sides and regular terminal conditions, such that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathscr{V}}u=f
by construction, as carried out in
[
KP22
]
.

There have been several results on singular Lévy SDEs in the Young regime in recent years.
[
ABM20
]
consider the time-homogeneous one-dimensional case and construct weak solutions via a Zvonkin transform. They additionally establish strong uniqueness and existence by a Yamada-Watanabe type argument, which in particular is restricted to
d=1
. Related to that, but for Hölder continuous drift of regularity at least
1-\alpha/2
,
[
Pri12
]
proves pathwise uniqueness in the multidimensional, time-homogeneous case. Two nearly simultaneous articles,
[
LZ19
]
and
[
dRM19
]
, consider the multidimensional case (time-homogeneous, respectively time-inhomogeneous) and prove existence and uniqueness for the martingale problem. They consider
V\in C([0,T],B_{p,q}^{\beta})
for general
p,q
(subject to suitable conditions), where for
p=q=\infty
they require the Young regime,
\beta>(1-\alpha)/2
. Let us also mention
[
HL20
]
, who prove pathwise regularization by noise results for SDEs driven by (very irregular) fractional Lévy noise, based on the methods of
[
CG16
,
HP21
]
.

We treat the multidimensional time-inhomogeneous case with drifts in the rough regime. However, we concentrate on
B_{\infty,\infty}
Besov spaces and do not consider
B_{p,q}
for general
p,q
.

The main contribution of this paper is the derivation of a well-posed rough weak solution concept. In the case of bounded and measurable coefficients the equivalence of probabilitic weak solutions and solutions of the martingale problem is by now classical, cf.
[
SV06
]
(in the Brownian noise case) and
[
KC11
]
(in the Lévy noise case). It has so far been an open problem (in both the Brownian and Lévy noise case), whether these results can be generalized to distributional drifts in the rough regime. In the Young regime with time-independent drift
[
ABM20
]
introduces a canonical weak solution concept, replacing the singular drift by the limit of smooth drift terms.
The same concept can be considered in multiple dimensions and with time-depending drift. A canonical weak solution is then a tuple of stochastic processes
(X,L)
on some probability base space, such that
L
is a symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy process and
X
is given by

\displaystyle X=x+Z+L,

(1.2)

for a continuous drift process
Z
that is given as a limiting object (in probability)

\displaystyle Z=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\cdot}V^{n}(r,X_{r})dr=:\lim_{n\to\infty}Z^{n}

for smooth
(V^{n})
approximating
V
. Furthermore,
Z
shall satisfy the following Hölder-type bound: there exists
C>0
, such that for all
0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\lvert Z_{t}-Z_{s}\rvert^{2}]\leqslant C\lvert t-s\rvert^{2(\alpha+\beta)/\alpha}.

(1.3)

The particular Hölder-regularity in (
1.3
) originates from the time regularity of the solution of the Kolmogorov backward equation. This implies that the solution
X
is a Dirichlet process and Itô formulas are available.

The recent article
[
IR22
]
consideres the multidimensional Brownian noise case and proves equivalence of martingale solutions and so-called
B
-solutions from
[
IR22
, Definition 6.2]
with
B=C_{T}\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
for
\beta\in(-1/2,0)
, that additionally satisfy the “reinforced local time property” (cf.
[
IR22
, Definition 6.7]
).
The reinforced local time property boils down to stability of the integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\nabla u(s,X_{s})dZ_{s},

(1.4)

when approximating the drift term by
(Z^{n})
and
u
solving
\mathcal{G}^{V}u=f
, by
(u^{n})\subset C^{1,2}_{b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In the Young case we show that the regularity of the PDE solution, stability of the PDE solution map and the bound (
1.3
) together yield stability for the stochastic integral (
1.4
) using the stochastic sewing lemma by
[
Lê20
]
. However, this fails in the rough case as the PDE solution is too irregular.
In the setting of general dimension, time-depending drift and
\alpha
-stable noise for
\alpha\in(1,2]
, we prove that canonical weak solutions are equivalent to martingale solutions in the Young regime.
In the rough case, we prove that canonical weak solutions are in general non-unique in law. Heuristically, this is due to the fact that the canonical weak solution does not uniquely determine the enhancement
\mathscr{V}
of
V
. In the one-dimensional Brownian case the situation becomes particularily interesting. While for any smooth approximating sequence
(V^{n})
of the singular drift
V
, the strong solutions
X^{n}
of

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{n}=V^{n}(t,X_{t}^{n})dt+dB_{t}

converge in distribution to the same limit, given by the solution of the
\mathcal{G}^{\cal V}
- martingale problem, it is however not the case that there exists a unique canonical weak solution in the above sense.

Our approach to obtain a well-posed weak solution concept in the rough regime is to impose further assumptions that ensure the uniqueness of the extension of the integral (
1.4
).
To that aim, we use ideas from rough paths theory, specifically the construction of rough stochastic integrals from
[
FHL21
]
. Lifting
Z
to a rough
stochastic
integrator process
(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{V})
enables to extend the integral from smooth integrands
\nabla u^{n}
in a stable manner to (para-)controlled integrands
\nabla u
. The lift is chosen in such a way as to correct for the most irregular terms in the paracontrolled decomposition of
\nabla u
.
We then define rough weak solutions accordingly. That is, we require a rough weak solution
X
to satisfy the conditions of a canonical weak solution and to be furthermore such that the iterated integrals
\mathbb{Z}^{V}

are well-defined and satisfy suitable Hölder-moment-bounds. Here
\mathbb{Z}^{V}
formally corresponds to the resonant product component in the enhancement
\mathscr{V}
.
We say that
(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{V})
is a so-called
rough stochastic integrator
, while the process
(\nabla u(t,X_{t}))
for the solution
u
of the Kolmogorov equation for regular right-hand side, is
stochastically controlled
.

The terms “stochastically controlled” and “rough (stochastic) integrator” are motivated by
[
FHL21
]
, but our definitions and the integral we construct differ. The difficulty here does not arise due to a low regularity integrator, but due to a integrand of low regularity, that is however controlled. Further difficulties arise due to the integrability issue in the pure stable noise case, which means that we can construct the integral in
L^{2}(\mathds{P})
, but possibly not in
L^{p}(\mathds{P})
for
p>2
. Summarizing, for a rough weak solution
X
we obtain a stable rough stochastic integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\nabla u(s,X_{s})d(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{V})_{s}

(1.5)

in
L^{2}(\mathds{P})
. For regular integrands the rough stochastic integral (
1.5
) coincides with the stochastic integral against
Z
in (
1.4
). More generally, we construct the rough stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{\cdot}f_{t}d(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{A})_{t}
, for rough stochastic integrators
(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{A})
for a given stochastic process
A
(formally
\mathbb{Z}^{A}_{st}=\int_{s}^{t}A_{s,r}dZ_{r}
) and an integrand
f
, that is stochastically controlled by
A
.

The stability of the rough stochastic integral (
1.5
) then enables to prove that a rough weak solution is indeed a solution of the
\mathcal{G}^{\mathscr{V}}
-martingale problem, in particular unique in law.
To prove that a martingale solution is a rough weak solution we need to show the existence of the iterated integrals
\mathbb{Z}^{V}
satisfying suitable bounds. We show that the bounds on
\mathbb{Z}^{V}
are implied by regularity properties and fortunate cancellations between the solutions of Kolmogorov backward equations for certain
singular
paracontrolled terminal conditions,
whose existence theory we carried out in
[
KP23
]
.

The paper is structured as follows.

Section

3
introduces the rough weak solution concept and states the main
Theorems

3.10
and
3.11
. In
Section

4
we construct the general rough stochastic integral. In
Section

5
we prove in
Theorems

5.7
and
5.10
equivalence of the weak solution concepts, which together imply
Theorem

3.10
. Furthermore we prove a generalized Itô formula for rough weak solutions in
Theorem

5.11
.
Section

6
investigates the canonical weak solution concept. We prove well-posedness in the Young case and ill-posedness in the rough case.

Preliminaries

Let
(p_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
be a smooth dyadic partition of unity, i.e. a family of functions
p_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
j\geqslant-1
, such that

1.)

p_{-1}
and
p_{0}
are non-negative radial functions (they just depend on the absolute value of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
), such that the support of
p_{-1}
is contained in a ball and the support of
p_{0}
is contained in an annulus;

2.)

p_{j}(x):=p_{0}(2^{-j}x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
j\geqslant 0
;

3.)

\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}p_{j}(x)=1
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; and

4.)

\operatorname{supp}(p_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(p_{j})=\emptyset
for all
\lvert i-j\rvert>1
.

We then define the Besov spaces for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,

\displaystyle B^{\theta}_{p,q}:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\lVert u\rVert_{B^{\theta}_{p,q}}=\big\lVert(2^{j\theta}\lVert\Delta_{j}u\rVert_{L^{p}})_{j\geqslant-1}\big\rVert_{\ell^{q}}<\infty\},

(2.1)

where
\Delta_{j}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(p_{j}\mathcal{F}u)
are the Littlewood-Paley blocks, and the Fourier transform is defined with the normalization
\hat{\varphi}(y):=\mathcal{F}\varphi(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,y\rangle}dx
(and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi(x)=\hat{\varphi}(-x)
); moreover,
\mathcal{S}
are the Schwartz functions and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are the Schwartz distributions.

Let
C^{\infty}_{b}=C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
denote the space of bounded and smooth functions with bounded partial derivatives.
For
q=\infty
, the space
B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}
has the unpleasant property that
C^{\infty}_{b}\subset B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}
is not dense.
Therefore, we rather work with the following space:

\displaystyle B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\mid\lim_{j\to\infty}2^{j\theta}\lVert\Delta_{j}u\rVert_{L^{p}}=0\},

(2.2)

for which
C^{\infty}_{b}
is a dense subset (cf.
[
BCD11
, Remark 2.75]
). We also use the notation
\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:=(\mathscr{C}^{\theta})^{d}=\mathscr{C}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathscr{C}^{\theta-}:=\bigcap_{\gamma<\theta}\mathscr{C}^{\gamma}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\theta+}=\bigcup_{\gamma>\theta}\mathscr{C}^{\gamma}
. Furthermore, we introduce the notation
\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p}:=B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}
for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, where
\mathscr{C}^{\theta}:=\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{\infty}
with norm denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\theta}:=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}}
.

For
1\leqslant p_{1}\leqslant p_{2}\leqslant\infty
,
1\leqslant q_{1}\leqslant q_{2}\leqslant\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the Besov space
B^{s}_{p_{1},q_{1}}
is continuously embedded in
B_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{s-d(1/p_{1}-1/p_{2})}
(cf.
[
BCD11
, Proposition 2.71]
). Furthermore, we will use that for
u\in B^{s}_{p,q}
and a multi-index
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
\lVert\partial^{n}u\rVert_{B^{s-\lvert n\rvert}_{p,q}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{B^{s}_{p,q}}
, which follows from the more general multiplier result from
[
BCD11
, Proposition 2.78]
.

We recall from Bony’s paraproduct theory (cf.
[
BCD11
, Section 2]
) that in general for
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\theta}
and
v\in\mathscr{C}^{\beta}
with
\theta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the product
uv:=u\varolessthan v+u\varogreaterthan v+u\varodot v
, is well defined in
\mathscr{C}^{\min(\theta,\beta,\theta+\beta)}
if and only if
\theta+\beta>0
. Denoting
S_{i}u=\sum_{j=-1}^{i-1}\Delta_{j}u
, the paraproducts are defined as follows

\displaystyle u\varolessthan v:=\sum_{i\geqslant-1}S_{i-1}u\Delta_{i}v,\quad u\varogreaterthan v:=v\varolessthan u,\quad u\varodot v:=\sum_{\lvert i-j\rvert\leqslant 1}\Delta_{i}u\Delta_{j}v.

Here, we use the notation of
[
MP19
,
MW17
]
for the para- and resonant products
\varolessthan,\varogreaterthan
and
\varodot
.

In estimates we often use the notation
a\lesssim b
, which means, that there exists a constant
C>0
, such that
a\leqslant Cb
. In the case that we want to stress the dependence of the constant
C(d)
in the estimate on a parameter
d
, we write
a\lesssim_{d}b
.

The paraproducts satisfy the following estimates for
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\leqslant 1
and
\theta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
PvZ22
, Theorem A.1]
and
[
BCD11
, Theorem 2.82, Theorem 2.85]
)

\displaystyle\lVert u\varodot v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta+\beta}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}},\qquad\text{if }\theta+\beta>0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lVert u\varolessthan v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}},\qquad\text{if }\theta>0,

\displaystyle\lVert u\varolessthan v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta+\theta}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}},\qquad\text{if }\theta<0.

So if
\theta+\beta>0
, we have
\lVert uv\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\gamma}_{p}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}}
for
\gamma:=\min(\theta,\beta,\theta+\beta)
.

Next, we collect some facts about
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes and their generators and semigroups. For
\alpha\in(0,2]
, a symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy process
L
is a Lévy process, that moreover satisfies the scaling property
(L_{kt})_{t\geqslant 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}k^{1/\alpha}(L_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
for any
k>0
and
L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}-L
, where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}
denotes equality in law. These properties determine the jump measure
\mu
of
L
, see
[
Sat99
, Theorem 14.3]
. That is, if
\alpha\in(0,2)
, the Lévy jump measure
\mu
of
L
is given by

\displaystyle\mu(A):=\mathds{E}\bigg[\sum_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\mathbf{1}_{A}(\Delta L_{t})\bigg]=\int_{S}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathbf{1}_{A}(k\xi)\frac{1}{k^{1+\alpha}}dk\tilde{\nu}(d\xi),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}),

(2.4)

where
\tilde{\nu}
is a finite, symmetric, non-zero measure on the unit sphere
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Furthermore, we also define for
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
and
t\geqslant 0
the Poisson random measure

\displaystyle\pi(A\times[0,t])=\sum_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}\mathbf{1}_{A}(\Delta L_{s}),

with intensity measure
dt\mu(dy)
. Denote the compensated Poisson random measure of
L
by
\hat{\pi}(dr,dy):=\pi(dr,dy)-dr\mu(dy)
.
We refer to the book by Peszat and Zabczyk
[
PZ07
]
for the integration theory against Poisson random measures and for the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality
[
PZ07
, Lemma 8.21 and 8.22]
, which we will both use in the sequel.
The generator
A
of
L
satisfies
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\operatorname{dom}(A)
and is given by

\displaystyle A\varphi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\mathbf{1}_{\{\lvert y\rvert\leqslant 1\}}(y)\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot y\big)\mu(dy)\qquad\text{for }\varphi\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.5)

If
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
denotes the semigroup of
L
, the convergence
t^{-1}(P_{t}f(x)-f(x))\to Af(x)
is uniform in

(see
[

, Theorem 5.4]
).

4
A rough stochastic sewing integral

In this section, we construct in
Theorem

4.4
a rough stochastic integral using the stochastic sewing
Lemma

2.14
. The theory is inspired by
[
FHL21
]
.
Nonetheless, the results from
[
FHL21
]
do not apply in our setting and our rough stochastic integral differs from the one constructed there. Instead of considering a
\gamma
-rough path as an integrator, we consider a lifted stochastic process
(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{A})
with bounds with respect to the semi-norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\theta,2}
and
\lVert\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}\rVert_{\theta,\infty}
, cf. (
2.12
). The Hölder exponent
\sigma
of
Z
is assumed to satisfy
\sigma>1/2
. That Hölder-regularity is satisfied in the case of the drift term we are interested in. In this sense the integrator in good. However the integrand is rough and we need to assume that it is stochastically controlled by a given stochastic process
A
.
We construct the integral in
L^{2}(\mathds{P})
, but replacing
2
by
p\geqslant 2
in the bounds below, we could more generally construct the integral in
L^{p}(\mathds{P})
. We will not bother doing so, as we aim for the situation where the integrand is given by a
\vartheta
-Hölder function of the
\alpha
-stable process for
\vartheta\in(0,\alpha/2)
, which lacks arbitrarily high moments, i.e.
\mathds{E}[\lvert L_{1}\rvert^{2\vartheta}]<\infty
as
2\vartheta<\alpha
, but we may not necessarily have
\mathds{E}[\lvert L_{1}\rvert^{p\vartheta}]<\infty
for
p>2
.

Let us start with the definition of stochastically controlled processes. Let here and below,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathds{P})
be a complete filtered probability space.

Definition 4.1

(Stochastically controlled processes)
.

Let
(A_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued adapted stochastic process with
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert A_{t}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{P},\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
and
\lVert A\rVert_{\varsigma,2}<\infty
for
\varsigma\in(0,1)
.
We call an adapted stochastic process
(f_{t},f^{\prime}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
with
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}[\lVert f_{t}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{P},\mathbb{R})}+\lVert f^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{P},\mathbb{R}^{d})}]<\infty
stochastically controlled by

, if for
\varsigma,\varsigma^{\prime}\in(0,1)
the following bounds hold true

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{\varsigma,2}+\lVert f^{\prime}\rVert_{\varsigma^{\prime},2}<\infty

and the remainder
(R^{f}_{st})_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}}
defined by

\displaystyle R^{f}_{st}:=f_{st}-f^{\prime}_{s}\cdot A_{st},

satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert R^{f}\rVert_{\varsigma+\varsigma^{\prime},2}<\infty.

The remainder is then an adapted process indexed by
\Delta_{T}
. We denote the space of all such
(f,f^{\prime})
, that are stochastically controlled by
A
by
D^{\varsigma,\varsigma^{\prime}}_{T}(A)
and we define the complete norm

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{D^{\varsigma,\varsigma^{\prime}}_{T}(A)}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}[\lVert f_{t}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{P})}+\lVert f^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{P},\mathbb{R}^{d})}]

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert f\rVert_{\varsigma,2}+\lVert f^{\prime}\rVert_{\varsigma^{\prime},2}+\lVert R^{f}\rVert_{\varsigma+\varsigma^{\prime},2}.

Definition 4.2

(Rough stochastic integrator)
.

Let
(A_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be an adapted
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued stochastic process, which satisfies
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert A_{t}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{P},\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
and
\lVert A\rVert_{\varsigma,2}<\infty
for
\varsigma\in(0,1)
. Let
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
. Then we call
(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{A})
a rough integrator, if
(Z_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
,
(\mathbb{Z}^{A}_{st})_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{T}}
are adapted stochastic processes with
Z
being
\mathbb{R}
-valued and
\mathbb{Z}^{A}
being
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued, such that
Z_{0}=0
and for all

, the following algebraic relation holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{st}^{A}=\mathbb{Z}^{A}_{sl}+\mathbb{Z}^{A}_{lt}+A_{sl}Z_{lt}.

Furthermore the following Hölder-type moment bounds hold:

\displaystyle\lVert(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{A})\rVert_{\mathcal{R}^{\sigma}_{T}(A)}:=\lVert Z\rVert_{\sigma,2}+\lVert Z\mid\mathcal{F}\rVert_{\sigma,\infty}+\lVert\mathbb{Z}^{A}\rVert_{\sigma,2}+\lVert\mathbb{Z}^{A}\mid\mathcal{F}\rVert_{\sigma+\varsigma,\infty}<\infty.

We call the space of stochastic processes, that are rough integrators
\mathcal{R}^{\sigma}_{T}(A)
. Equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\lVert(Z,\mathbb{Z}^{A})\rVert_{\mathcal{R}_{T}^{\sigma}(A)}

\displaystyle:=\lVert Z\rVert_{\sigma,2}+\lVert Z-W\mid\mathcal{F}\rVert_{\sigma,\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert\mathbb{Z}^{A}\rVert_{\sigma,2}+\lVert\mathbb{Z}^{A}\mid\mathcal{F}\rVert_{\sigma+\varsigma,\infty}

the space becomes a Banach space.

Remark 4.3
.

We refer to
[

]
for the connection to rough paths. The space of rough stochastic integrators is a vector space, because the algebraic relation here is linear. Furthermore, we assume that
Z_{0}=0
to obtain a norm.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(A_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}

\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued stochastic process with

Proof.

Define for

and the definition of the remainder

\displaystyle\delta\Xi_{r,l,s}=\Xi_{rs}-\Xi_{rl}-\Xi_{ls}

To apply the stochastic sewing

We start with the bound on the expectation. As

\displaystyle\mathds{E}[\lvert\mathds{E}_{r}[\delta\Xi_{r,l,s}]\rvert^{2}]

with

,

. The prescribed Lipschitz bound follows from the bound on

.

If

\displaystyle\tilde{I}(f,Z)_{t}=\lim_{\lvert\Pi\rvert\to 0}\sum_{r<l\in\Pi}f_{r}Z_{rl}\in L^{2}(\mathds{P}),

we obtain that

with

∎

6
Ill-posedness of the canonical weak solution concept in the rough regime

Below, we introduce so-called canonical weak solutions. We prove in
Corollary

6.5
, that in the Young case the canonical weak solution concept is well-posed (and equivalent to martingale solutions). We finalize by proving in
Theorem

6.7
that canonical weak solutions are in general non-unique. We construct a counterexample, that justifies the latter.

We start by defining the concept of canonical weak solutions.

Definition 6.1

(Canonical weak solution)
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2]
,
V\in C_{T}\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
for
\beta\in(\frac{2-2\alpha}{3},0)
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We call a tuple
(X,L)
a canonical weak solution starting at

, if there exists a stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},\mathds{P})
, such that
L
is an
\alpha
-stable symmetric non-degenerate
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-Lévy process and almost surely

\displaystyle X=x+Z+L,

where
Z
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted, continuous process with

\displaystyle\lVert Z\rVert_{(\alpha+\beta)/\alpha,2}+\lVert Z\mid\mathcal{F}\rVert_{(\alpha+\beta)/\alpha,\infty}<\infty.

Moreover there exists a sequence
(V^{n})\subset C_{T}C^{\infty}_{b}
with
V^{n}\to V
in
C_{T}\mathscr{C}^{\beta}
, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\cdot}V^{n}(s,X_{s})ds=Z,

(6.1)

with convergence in
L^{2}(\mathds{P})
, uniformly in
[0,T]
.

Remark 6.2
.

The definition of canonical weak solutions is similar to
[
ABM20
, Definition 2.1]
. The difference is the assumption that
\lVert Z\mid\mathcal{F}\rVert_{(\alpha+\beta)/\alpha,\infty}<\infty
. However, boths bounds on

are natural to assume and motivated by
Lemma

2.13
.

Remark 6.3

(One-dimensional, time-homogeneous case with
\alpha=2
)
.

In this case, for any approximation
(V^{n})
of
V
, the weak limit of the strong solutions
(X^{n})
with

\displaystyle dX^{n}_{t}=V^{n}(X_{t}^{n})dt+dB_{t}

is the same and given by the solution of the
\mathcal{G}^{\mathscr{V}}
-martingale problem.
The one-dimensional case is special in this sense. The above is true, because in
d=1
the resonant products
(J^{T}(\partial_{x}V^{n})\varodot V^{n})
converge to the same limit for any approximation
(V^{n})
.
This can be seen with Leibniz rule considering
((-\Delta)^{-1}(\partial_{x}V^{n})\varodot V^{n})_{n}=(J^{\infty}(\partial_{x}V^{n})\varodot V^{n})_{n}
. Indeed, we have with
v^{n}
, such that
V^{n}=\partial_{x}v^{n}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(-\Delta)^{-1}(\partial_{x}V^{n})\varodot V^{n}=-\frac{1}{2}\lim_{n\to\infty}\partial_{x}(v^{n}\varodot v^{n})=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(v\varodot v)

using that
(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}(\partial_{x}V^{n})=-v^{n}
and
v^{n}\varodot v^{n}\to v\varodot v
as products of functions. Notice moreover that this does not imply, that the limit of the mixed resonant products
((-\Delta)^{-1}(\partial_{x}V^{n})\varodot V)_{n}
is uniquely determined. In fact in the rough case the latter limit is in general not unique (cf.
Lemma

6.6
below). This fact will imply that, even in the one-dimensional case, a weak solution in the sense of
Definition

6.1
is in general non-unique in law.

Remark 6.4

(One and all sequences
(V^{n})
)
.

One may ask, if requiring the convergence (
6.1
) to hold for all (instead of one) approximating sequences
(V^{n})
renders the solution concept well-posed. Though, if we require (
6.1
) to hold for all such sequences
(V^{n})
, then in the rough case the solution of the
\mathcal{G}^{\mathscr{V}}
-martingale problem won’t be a solution. This follows also from the fact that the limit of the mixed resonant products is non-unique. Thus, we would expect non-existence of solutions in the rough case.
Hence, requiring (
6.1
) to hold for all such sequences
(V^{n})
makes the definition too restrictive.

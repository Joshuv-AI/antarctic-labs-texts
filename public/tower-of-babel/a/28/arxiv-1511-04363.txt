Dynamics of z n + 1 = α + α ​ z n + β ​ z n − 1 1 + z n \displaystyle{z_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha+\alpha z_{n}+\beta z_{n-1}}{1+z_{n}}} in Complex Plane
Sk. Sarif Hassan

Abstract
The dynamics of the second order rational difference equation z n + 1 = α + α ​ z n + β ​ z n − 1 1 + z n \displaystyle{z_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha+\alpha z_{n}+\beta z_{n-1}}{1+z_{n}}} with complex parameters α \alpha , β \beta and arbitrary complex initial conditions is investigated. The same difference equation is well studied with positive real parameters and initial values and one of the main results on trichotomy of parameters is revisited in the complex set-up and the similar result is found to be true. In addition, the chaotic solutions of the equation is fetched out in the complex set-up which was absent in the real scenario.



Keywords: Rational difference equation, Local asymptotic stability, Chaotic trajectory and Periodicity.

Mathematics Subject Classification: 39A10, 39A11

2.1
Local Asymptotic Stability of
z_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha+\alpha z_{n}+\beta z_{n-1}}{1+z_{n}}

The equilibrium points of Eq.(
1
) are the solutions of the quadratic equation

\bar{z}=\frac{\alpha+\alpha\bar{z}+\beta\bar{z}}{1+\bar{z}}

Note that if
\alpha=0
,
\bar{z}_{\pm}=0
and
\alpha+\beta-1
are two equilibriums of the equation Eq.(
1
).

The Eq.(1) has the two equilibria points

\bar{z}_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{1+2\alpha+\alpha^{2}-2\beta+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)
and
\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{1+2\alpha+\alpha^{2}-2\beta+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)
respectively when
\alpha\neq 0
.
The linearized equation of the rational difference equation Eq.(
1
) with respect to the equilibrium point
\bar{z}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{1+2\alpha+\alpha^{2}-2\beta+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)
is

\displaystyle{z_{n+1}+\frac{2\beta\left(-1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)}{\left(1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)^{2}}z_{n}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)z_{n-1}=0,n=0,1,\ldots}

(2)

with associated characteristic equation

\lambda^{2}+\frac{2\beta\left(-1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)}{\left(1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)^{2}}\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)=0.

(3)

By the
Clark’s theorem
, the equilibrium
\bar{z}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{1+2\alpha+\alpha^{2}-2\beta+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)
is
locally asymptotically stable
if the sum of the modulus of two coefficients of the characteristic equation Eq. (3) is less than
1
. Therefore the condition for local asymptotic stability of the equilibrium would be

\left\lvert\frac{2\beta\left(-1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)}{\left(1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)^{2}}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)\right\rvert<1

It is noted that that the conditional inequality for local asymptotic stability of the equilibrium attains maximum
19.7392
at
\alpha=0.93601--0.293238i
and
\beta=-0.00195532-0.211385i
. Also the minimum value attain by the inequality is
1.28533
at
\alpha=0.86278+0.446302i
and
\beta=-0.0309069+0.749819i
.

This numerical result suggests that there does not exist any
\alpha
and
\beta
so that the inequality holds. Therefore the equilibrium
\bar{z}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\alpha+\beta-\sqrt{1+2\alpha+\alpha^{2}-2\beta+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)
is
unstable
indeed.

The linearized equation of the rational difference equation Eq.(
1
) with respect to the equilibrium point
\bar{z}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{1+2\alpha+\alpha^{2}-2\beta+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)
is

\displaystyle{z_{n+1}+\frac{2\beta\left(-1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)}{\left(1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)^{2}}z_{n}-\frac{2\beta}{1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}}z_{n-1}=0,n=0,1,\ldots}

(4)

with associated characteristic equation

\lambda^{2}+\frac{2\beta\left(-1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)}{\left(1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)^{2}}\lambda-\frac{2\beta}{1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}}=0.

Theorem 2.1
.

The equilibriums
\bar{z}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{1+2\alpha+\alpha^{2}-2\beta+2\alpha\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)

Proof.

Proof the theorem follows from the

of local asymptotic stability of the equilibriums. Hence by

\left\lvert\frac{2\beta\left(-1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)}{\left(1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}\right)^{2}}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\frac{2\beta}{1+\alpha+\beta+\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^{2}+2(-1+\alpha)\beta+\beta^{2}}}\right\rvert<1

Boundedness and Unbounded Solutions

Here problem is to determine an open ball
B(0,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{C}
such that if
z_{n}\in B(0,\epsilon)
and
z_{n-1}\in B(0,\epsilon)
then
z_{n+1}\in B(0,\epsilon)
for all
n\geq 0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For the difference equation Eq.(
1
), for every
\epsilon>0
, if
z_{n}
and
z_{n-1}

\in B(0,\epsilon)
then
z_{n+1}\in B(0,\epsilon)
provided

\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert+\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leq 1-\epsilon-\frac{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}{\epsilon}

Proof.

Let
\{z_{n}\}
be a solution of the equation Eq.(
1
). Let
\epsilon>0
be any arbitrary real number. Consider
z_{n},z_{n-1}\in B(0,\epsilon)
. We need to find out an
\epsilon
such that
z_{n+1}\in B(0,\epsilon)
for all
n
. It is follows from the Eq.(
1
) that for any
\epsilon>0
, using Triangle inequality for

\left\lvert z_{n+1}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\frac{\alpha+\alpha z_{n}+\beta z_{n-1}}{1+z_{n}}\right\rvert\leq\frac{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert+\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\epsilon+\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}

In order to ensure that
\left\lvert z_{n+1}\right\rvert<\epsilon
, it is required to be

\frac{\frac{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}{\epsilon}+\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert+\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert}{1-\epsilon}<1

That is
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert+\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\leq 1-\epsilon-\frac{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}{\epsilon}
. Hence it is proved.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
\beta=\alpha+1
, the solution of the difference equation Eq.(
1
) converges to the periodic solution of period 2.

Proof.

When
\beta=\alpha+1
, then all prime period 2 solutions of Eq.(
1
)
\dots,\phi,\psi,\phi,\psi,\dots
are the solution of the equation

\alpha+\alpha(\phi+\psi)-\phi\psi=0

(7)

Keeping in mind the equation Eq. (
7
) we set

\mathfrak{J}_{n}=\alpha+\alpha(\phi+\psi)-\phi\psi

for
n>0
.

The following identities are holding well for
n>0

\mathfrak{J}_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha+1}{1+z_{n}}\mathfrak{J}_{n}

(8)

z_{n+1}-z_{n-1}=\frac{\mathfrak{J}_{n}}{1+z_{n}}

(9)

and for
n\geq 1

z_{n+1}-z_{n-1}=\frac{\alpha+1}{1+z_{n}}(z_{n}-z_{n-2})

(10)

and therefore, for all
n\geq 1

z_{n+1}-z_{n-1}=(z_{1}-z_{-1})\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha+1}{1+z_{k}}

(11)

From
8
, it is seen that
\mathfrak{J}_{n}
remains constant along each solution and so it is now following from
9
that for each solution
\{z_{n}\}_{n}
of the equation Eq.(
1
), exactly one of the following two conditions would be true.

Either

\left\lvert z_{n+1}-z_{n-1}\right\rvert=0

or

\left\lvert z_{n+1}-z_{n-1}\right\rvert\neq 0

If the the either condition holds then the proof is immediate. If the other condition holds, then solution would converges to the period
2
solutions provided the solution is bounded.

Hence the theorem is proved.

The method of Lyapunov characteristic exponents serves as a useful tool to quantify chaos. Specifically Lyapunav exponents measure the rates of convergence or divergence of nearby trajectories. Negative Lyapunov exponents indicate convergence, while positive Lyapunov exponents demonstrate divergence and chaos. The magnitude of the Lyapunov exponent is an indicator of the time scale on which chaotic behavior can be predicted or transients decay for the positive and negative exponent cases respectively.

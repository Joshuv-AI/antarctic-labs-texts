Constructive stability results in interpolation inequalities and explicit improvements of decay rates of fast diffusion equations ∗ Corresponding author: J. Dolbeault


Abstract
We provide a scheme of a recent stability result for a family of Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev (GNS) inequalities, which is equivalent to an improved entropy – entropy production inequality associated with an appropriate fast diffusion equation (FDE) written in self-similar variables. This result can be rephrased as an improved decay rate of the entropy of the solution of (FDE) for well prepared initial data. There is a family of Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN) inequalities which has a very similar structure. When the exponents are in a range for which the optimal functions for (CKN) are radially symmetric, we investigate how the methods for (GNS) can be extended to (CKN). In particular, we prove that the solutions of the evolution equation associated to (CKN) also satisfy an improved decay rate of the entropy, after an explicit delay. However, the improved rate is obtained without assuming that initial data are well prepared, which is a major difference with the (GNS) case.

Introduction and main results

Let us start with some Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities (without weights) and related flow issues in Section

1.1
Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities and related flows

The
Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality

\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{2}^{\theta}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{p+1}^{1-\theta}\geq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{2\,p}

(1)

holds on the space of the functions
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\nabla f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with exponents given by

\theta=\tfrac{d\,(p-1)}{(d+2-p\,(d-2))\,p}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptp\in(1,+\infty)\;\mbox{if}\;d=1\;\mbox{or}\;2\,,\hskip 10.00002ptp\in(1,p_{\star}]\;\mbox{if}\;d\geq 3\,,\hskip 10.00002ptp_{\star}=\tfrac{d}{d-2}\,.

According to
[
27
,
17
]
, equality in (
1
) is achieved if and only if
f
is equal to

\mathsf{g}(x)=\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,

(2)

up to a multiplication by a constant, a translation and a scaling. We denote by
\mathfrak{M}
the manifold of optimal functions for (
1
). This inequality has a number of interesting limit cases: Sobolev’s inequality if
d\geq 3
and
p=p_{\star}
, the Euclidean Onofri inequality if
d=2
in the limit as
p\to+\infty
, and the scale invariant Euclidean logarithmic Sobolev inequality in the limit as
p\to 1_{+}
. Let us define the
deficit functional

\delta[f]:=(p-1)^{2}\,\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{2}^{2}+4\,\tfrac{d-p\,(d-2)}{p+1}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{p+1}^{p+1}-\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{GNS}}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{2\,p}^{2\,p\,\chi}

with
\chi=\frac{d+2-p\,(d-2)}{d-p\,(d-4)}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{GNS}}
chosen so that
\delta[\mathsf{g}]=0
. Up to a scaling, the inequality
\delta[f]\geq 0
is equivalent to (
1
), as was shown in
[
17
]
.

In the critical case
p=p_{\star}
,
d\geq 3
, optimal functions in (
1
) are known as the
Aubin-Talenti functions
and this result goes back to
[
1
,
33
,
30
]
. Later, in
[
9
]
, H. Brezis and E.H. Lieb asked the next natural question: which distance to
\mathfrak{M}
is controlled by the deficit
\delta
? Soon after, an answer was given in
[
2
]
by G. Bianchi and H. Egnell: there is a positive constant
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BE}}
such that

\tfrac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\,\delta[f]=\|{\nabla f}\|_{2}^{2}-\mathsf{S}_{d}\,\|{f}\|_{2^{*}}^{2}\geq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BE}}\,\inf_{\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}}\,\|{\nabla f-\nabla\varphi}\|_{2}^{2}\,,

where
\mathsf{S}_{d}
is the optimal constant in Sobolev’s inequality. This striking result had anyway an important drawback:
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BE}}
is obtained by a
non-constructive
method. Various extensions and improvements as,
e.g.
, in
[
16
,
26
]
have been obtained, as well as similar results for (
1
) in
[
11
,
31
]
, in the subcritical range: we refer to
[
6
]
for a review of the literature. New
constructive stability results
were recently obtained:

Theorem 1
.

[
6
, Corollary 5.4 and Theorem 6.1]
Let
d\geq 3
and
p\in(1,p_{\star}]
, or
d=1
,
2
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
. For any
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\nabla f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

A:=\sup_{r>0}r^{\frac{d-p\,(d-4)}{p-1}}\int_{|x|>r}|f|^{2p}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\,,

we have the estimate

\delta[f]\geq\mathcal{C}\,\inf_{\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\big|(p-1)\,\nabla f+f^{p}\,\nabla\varphi^{1-p}\big|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

(3)

for some explicit
\mathcal{C}>0
which depends only on
d
,
p
,
\left\|{f}\right\|_{2p}
and
A
.

As a function of
f
or, to be precise, as a function of the mass
\left\|{f}\right\|_{2p}
and
A
,
\mathcal{C}
takes positive values on
\mathfrak{M}
even if it is not uniformly bounded away from
0
on
\mathfrak{M}
. The distance to
\mathfrak{M}
is measured by a
Fisher information
functional and the strategy of the proof involves
entropy
methods. Inequality (
3
) is equivalent to an improved
entropy – entropy production inequality
, which relates the Fisher information with a
relative entropy
, or
free energy
, defined for
m\in[m_{1},1)
with
m_{1}:=1-1/d
by

\mathcal{F}[v]:=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left(v^{m}-\mathcal{B}^{m}-m\,\mathcal{B}^{m-1}\,(v-\mathcal{B})\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x

(4)

where

\mathcal{B}(x):=\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,.

(5)

The functional
\mathcal{F}
enters in the study of nonlinear evolution equations as follows. Let us consider the
fast diffusion equation

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\left(v\,\nabla v^{m-1}\right)=2\,\nabla\cdot(x\,v)\,,\hskip 10.00002ptv(t=0,\cdot)=v_{0}\,.

(6)

By a standard computation, a solution
v
of (
6
) is such that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{F}[v(t,\cdot)]=-\,\mathcal{I}[v(t,\cdot)]\;\mbox{where}\;\mathcal{I}[v]:=\frac{m}{1-m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{v\,\big|\nabla v^{m-1}-\nabla\mathcal{B}^{m-1}\big|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

(7)

where
\mathcal{I}[v]
is the
relative Fisher information
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
. The exponents
m
in (
6
) and
p
in (
1
) are related by the condition
p=1/(2\,m-1)
. In practice, for
m>m_{1}
(subcritical range), the result of Theorem
1
can be rephrased as a result on
decay rates
for the solutions of (
6
).

Corollary 2
.

[
6
, Corollary 5.2]
Let
d\geq 1
and
m\in(m_{1},1)
. If
v
is a solution of (
6
) with nonnegative initial datum
v_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{v_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{d}x
,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{x\,v_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
and

A[v_{0}]:=\sup_{r>0}r^{\frac{2}{1-m}-d}\int_{|x|>r}v_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\,,

then we have

\mathcal{F}[v(t,.)]\leq\mathcal{F}[v_{0}]\,e^{-\,(4+\zeta)\,t}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0

(8)

for some positive constant
\zeta
which depends explicitly only on
m
,
d
, and
A[v_{0}]
.

The integral
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{x\,v_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x
is finite because
A[v_{0}]<+\infty
. Note that if the center of mass of
v_{0}
is finite, then
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{x\,v(t,x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{x\,v_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,e^{-2t}
for any
t\geq 0
.

Let us consider the
optimized free energy functional

\mathcal{F}_{\star}[v]:=\inf_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left(v^{m}-B^{m}-m\,B^{m-1}\,(v-B)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x

where
\mathfrak{B}
is the set of all
Barenblatt
functions obtained from
\mathcal{B}
using a multiplication by a constant, translations and scalings.

Corollary 3
.

Let
m\in[m_{1},1)
if
d\geq 2
,
m\in(1/2,1)
if
d=1
and consider
\zeta
as in Corollary
2
. If
v
is a solution of (
6
) with nonnegative initial datum
v_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
A[v_{0}]
is finite, then

\mathcal{F}_{\star}[v(t,.)]\leq\mathcal{F}_{\star}[v_{0}]\,e^{-\,(4+\zeta)\,t}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

(9)

In the subcritical range, this result is a straightforward consequence of Corollary
2
, but it is new in the critical case
m=m_{1}
corresponding to
p=p_{\star}
.

The
entropy – entropy production inequality
relates rates of convergence for the solutions of (
6
) with (
1
), but (
1
) can also be invoked in the context of the standard
fast diffusion equation

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u^{m}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptu(t=0,\cdot)=u_{0}\,.

(10)

Assume that
m\in[m_{1},1)
if
d\geq 2
and
m\in(1/2,1)
if
d=1
. Using the
Rényi entropy powers
formalism, we learn from
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
that a solution
u
of (
10
) with initial datum
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}_{+}\!\left({\mathbb{R}}^{d},(1+|x|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\right)
such that
u_{0}^{m}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
satisfies

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u^{m}(t,x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\left(\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u_{0}^{m}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{m-m_{c}}{1-m}}+\tfrac{(1-m)\,C_{0}}{m-m_{c}}\,t\right)^{\frac{1-m}{m-m_{c}}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,,

(11)

for some constant
C_{0}
which explicitly involves
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GNS}}
and where
m_{c}:=(d-2)/d
. Equality in (
11
) holds for any
t\geq 0
if and only if
u_{0}\in\mathfrak{B}
. At
t=0
, (
11
) is an equality for any
u_{0}
and we can recover the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality (
1
) written with the optimal constant by differentiating with respect to
t
the growth estimate (
11
) at
t=0
. A more readable estimate is obtained by considering the
optimized free energy functional

\mathcal{F}_{\star}
applied to (
10
).

Corollary 4
.

Assume that
d\geq 1
,
m\in[m_{1},1)
and consider a solution of (
10
) with initial datum
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}_{+}\!\left({\mathbb{R}}^{d},(1+|x|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\right)
such that
u_{0}^{m}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. With
\kappa=\zeta/d
and
\zeta
as in Corollary
3
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\star}[u(t,.)]\leq\mathcal{F}_{\star}[u_{0}]\,\big(1+d\,(m-m_{c})\,t\big)^{-\frac{m+\kappa}{m-m_{c}}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

This new result is remarkable. While the best matching function
B(t,\cdot)\in\mathfrak{B}
is such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{B^{m}(t,x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\sim t^{(1-m)/(m-m_{c})}\to+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
, according to (
11
), it turns out that
\mathcal{F}[u(t,.)]
, which involves
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left(u^{m}(t,x)-B^{m}(t,x)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x
, decays to
0
at an algebraic rate. As a consequence, we have
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left\|{u(t,.)-B(t,.)}\right\|_{1}=0
, with an explicit rate, by the Csiszár-Kullback-Pinsker inequality. The case
\kappa=0
is a consequence of (
1
) and the improvement
\kappa>0
is a consequence of Theorem
1
.

1.2
Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities and related flows

So far the results are simple consequences of the method of
[
6
]
. We are now going to extend them to a larger class of inequalities. On
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
, let us consider the
Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities

\left\|{f}\right\|_{2p,\gamma}\leq{\mathsf{C}}_{\beta,\gamma,p}\,\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{2,\beta}^{\theta}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{p+1,\gamma}^{1-\theta}

(12)

with optimal constant
{\mathsf{C}}_{\beta,\gamma,p}
, parameters
\beta
,
\gamma
and
p
such that

\gamma-2<\beta<\frac{d-2}{d}\,\gamma\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma\in(-\infty,d)\,,\hskip 10.00002ptp\in\left(1,p_{\star}\right]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002ptp_{\star}:=\frac{d-\gamma}{d-\beta-2}\,,

(13)

and an exponent

\theta=\frac{(d-\gamma)\,(p-1)}{p\,\big(d+\beta+2-2\,\gamma-p\,(d-\beta-2)\big)}

which is determined by the scaling invariance. This formula for
\theta
extends to (
12
) the expression for (
1
), which corresponds to the special case
\beta=\gamma=0
. Here
\mathrm{L}^{q,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\mathrm{L}^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
respectively denote the spaces of all measurable functions
f
such that

\left\|{f}\right\|_{q,\gamma}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|f|^{q}\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/q}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|{f}\right\|_{q}:=\left\|{f}\right\|_{q,0}

are finite. Inequality (
12
) holds in the space
\mathrm{H}^{p}_{\beta,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
of functions
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p+1,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\nabla f\in\mathrm{L}^{2,\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, defined as the completion of the space
\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
of the smooth functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with compact support in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, with respect to the norm given by
f\mapsto(p_{\star}-p)\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{p+1,\gamma}^{2}+\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{2,\beta}^{2}
. Since the weights are locally integrable, these spaces can also be defined as the completion of the space
\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. The limitation
p\leq p_{\star}
in (
13
) amounts, for a given
p>1
to a restriction to the admissible set of parameters
(\beta,\gamma)
, namely
\beta\geq d-2+(\gamma-d)/p
. On the other hand, we notice that

\beta<\frac{d-2}{d}\,\gamma\hskip 10.00002pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002ptp_{\star}<\frac{d}{d-2}\,.

Inequality (
12
) belongs to a family of inequalities introduced by L. Caffarelli, R. Kohn and L. Nirenberg in
[
10
]
and also earlier by V.P. Il’in in
[
28
]
. The range of admissible parameters
(\beta,\gamma)
is limited by (
13
) to a cone in the quadrant
\beta<d-2
and
\gamma<d
(see Fig.
1
), but the inequality also holds in a cone in the quadrant
\beta>d-2
and
\gamma>d
using the property of
inversion symmetry
: see
[
4
, Section 2.1]
for details.

A central issue in Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities (
12
) is to decide whether the equality case is achieved among radial functions or not when
d\geq 2
. We summarize this alternative by
symmetry
versus
symmetry breaking
. Symmetry in (
12
) means that the equality case is achieved by the (generalized)
Aubin-Talenti type functions

\mathsf{g}(x)=\big(1+|x|^{\sigma}\big)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma:=2+\beta-\gamma\,.

(14)

This definition of Aubin-Talenti type functions extends the one in (
2
). In the critical case
p=p_{\star}
,
\theta=1
, V. Felli and M. Schneider proved in
[
25
]
that
symmetry breaking
holds if

\gamma<0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\beta_{\rm FS}(\gamma)<\beta<\frac{d-2}{d}\,\gamma\,,

where

\beta_{\rm FS}(\gamma):=d-2-\sqrt{(\gamma-d)^{2}-4\,(d-1)}\,.

Reciprocally, if

\gamma<d\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma-2<\beta<\frac{d-2}{d}\,\gamma\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\beta\leq\beta_{\rm FS}(\gamma)\,,

then
symmetry
holds according to
[
21
]
. The results are exactly the same in the subcritical case
p\in\left(1,p_{\star}\right)
as was shown in
[
4
, Theorem 2]
and in
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
. See Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:

In dimension
d=4
, the critical exponent
p=p_{\star}=d/(d-2)=2
corresponds to the left figure, while
p=6/5
is subcritical and corresponds to the right figure. The half cone of admissible regions of the parameters
(\beta,\gamma)
appear in grey, with symmetry breaking in dark grey and symmetry in light grey (the symmetry region is bounded if and only if
p<p_{\star}
).

More is known. In the limit case
\beta=(d-2)\,\gamma/d
corresponding to
p=p_{\star}=d/(d-2)
, we learn from
[
15
]
that the optimal constant is achieved among radial functions, but that optimal functions exist only for
\beta\geq 0
. The limit case as
\beta\to(\gamma-2)_{+}
, that is,
p\to 1_{+}
, gives rise to a family of logarithmic Hardy inequalities which has been studied in
[
18
,
19
]
. We refer to
[
21
,
23
]
for more details on earlier contributions and to
[
22
]
for a general overview. Stability results
à la
Bianchi-Egnell, with no constructive estimates, appeared in
[
36
]
in the critical case of (
12
), as an extension of the results of
[
2
,
26
]
. So far there are no constructive stability results for (
12
).

Exactly as in the case of Inequalities (
1
), it is interesting to consider on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the nonlinear flow defined by

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+|x|^{\gamma}\,\nabla\left(|x|^{-\beta}\,u\,\nabla u^{m-1}\right)=0\,,\hskip 10.00002ptu(t=0,\cdot)=u_{0}\,,

(15)

which generalizes (
10
) to
(\beta,\gamma)\neq(0,0)
for
m\in[m_{1},1)
with a generalized critical exponent defined by
m_{1}:=1-\frac{2+\beta-\gamma}{2\,(d-\gamma)}
. The computation

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0\,,\,\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u^{m}\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{m^{2}}{1-m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,\left|\nabla u^{m-1}\right|^{2}\,|x|^{-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x

(16)

enters in the formalism of the
generalized Rényi entropy powers
. With
f=u^{m-1/2}
and
p=1/(2\,m-1)
so that
u=f^{2p}
, we notice that

\textstyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\left\|{f}\right\|_{2p,\gamma}^{2p}\,,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u^{m}\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\left\|{f}\right\|_{p+1,\gamma}^{p+1}\\
\textstyle\mbox{and}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,|\nabla u^{m-1/2}|^{2}\,|x|^{-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{4\,(1-m)^{2}}{(2\,m-1)^{2}}\,\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{2,\beta}^{2}\,.

As in the non-weighted case,
m=m_{1}
corresponds to
p=p_{\star}
. Taking into account (
12
) and (
16
), any solution of (
15
) satisfies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u^{m}\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u^{m}\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{-\frac{1-\theta}{\theta(p+1)}}

for some numerical constant
C
involving
{\mathsf{C}}_{\beta,\gamma,p}
. Altogether, this proves a growth estimate similar to (
11
). As for (
1
), this estimate is in fact equivalent to (
12
).

Again, more readable estimates are achieved using self-similar variables and relative entropies. Equation (
15
) can be rewritten in these variables as

|x|^{-\gamma}\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\left(|x|^{-\beta}\,v\,\nabla v^{m-1}\right)=\sigma\,\nabla\cdot\left(x\,|x|^{-\gamma}\,v\right)\,.

(17)

The counterpart of Corollary
2
is an improved decay rate of the free energy
\mathcal{F}
now defined as

\mathcal{F}[v]=\frac{2\,p}{1-p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left(v^{\frac{p+1}{2\,p}}-\mathsf{g}^{p+1}-\frac{p+1}{2\,p}\mathsf{g}^{1-p}\left(v-\mathsf{g}^{2p}\right)\right)|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

(18)

where
\mathsf{g}^{2p}
is now a stationary solution to (
17
) and
m=(p+1)/(2\,p)
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
d\geq 1
. Assume that
(\beta,\gamma)\neq(0,0)
satisfies

\gamma<d\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma-2<\beta<\frac{d-2}{d}\,\gamma\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\beta<\beta_{\rm FS}(\gamma)\,,

(19)

let
\alpha=1+(\beta-\gamma)/2
and assume that
m\in[m_{1},1)
. If
v
solves (
17
) with a nonnegative initial datum
v_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{1,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{v_{0}(x)\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathsf{g}(x)^{2p}\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x
and

A[v_{0}]:=\sup_{R>0}R^{\frac{2+\beta-\gamma}{1-m}-(d-\gamma)}\,\int_{|x|>R}v_{0}(x)\,|x|^{-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\,<\infty\,,

then there are some
\zeta>0
and some
T>0
which depend explicitly only on
m
,

Proof.

The proof is based on Propositions

and

and on the comparison (for

with

The inner estimate

We consider what happens inside a ball as in

Corollary 11
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
7
and for any
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{m}\right)

, there exists a constant
\mathrm{K}>0

\Big|\frac{u(t,x)}{B(t,x)}-1\Big|\leq\frac{\mathrm{K}}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{1-m}}}\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}[u_{0}]}}{R(t)}\right)^{\vartheta}\,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{if}\hskip 10.00002pt|x|\leq 2\,\rho(\varepsilon)\,R(t)

(30)

The constants

and

are numerical constants, which depend only on

,
m
,
\beta
and
\gamma
. Their explicit values follow from the proof as in

Proof.

The proof follows the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 4.1]
and further properties of parabolic regularity which are detailed in
[
6
]
. We have to estimate the term
|u(t,x)/B(t,x)-1|
in the ball of radius

. This can be done by interpolating its

norm between
\mathrm{L}^{p}
and
C^{\mu}
semi-norms, defined on a bounded open domain
\Omega
as

\lfloor u\rfloor_{C^{\mu}\left(\Omega\right)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\,,

using the interpolation inequality (see

6

)

\left\|{u}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(B_{R}(x))}\,\leq\,C\left(\lfloor u\rfloor_{C^{\mu}(B_{2R}(x))}^{\frac{(d-\gamma)}{(d-\gamma)+p\,\mu}}\,\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p,\gamma}(B_{2R}(x))}^{\frac{p\,\mu}{(d-\gamma)+p\,\mu}}+R^{-\frac{d-\gamma}{p}}\,\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p,\gamma}(B_{2R}(x))}\right)\,.

(31)

Here
C
is a positive constant which depends on
d
,
\gamma
,
\mu
and
p
and

\textstyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p,\gamma}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}:=\left(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u|^{p}\,|x|^{-\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}\,.

In order to estimate the
C^{\mu}
semi-norm of

and
B(t,x)
on the domain
|x|\leq\,2\,\rho(\varepsilon)R(t)
with

, we have to deal with the time dependence of those functions and the domain itself. In order to simplify the analysis, we introduce the scaling
\hat{u}_{\tau,\kappa}(t,x):=\kappa^{\sigma/(1-m)}\,\tau^{(d-\gamma)/\xi}\,u\big(\tau\,t,\kappa\,\tau^{1/\xi}\,x\big)
for some positive
\kappa
and
\tau>0
. If
u
is a solution to (
15
), then so is
\hat{u}_{\tau,\kappa}
, for any
\tau,\kappa>0
. The Barenblatt profile is transformed by the previous scaling as
\hat{B}_{\tau,\kappa}(t,x)=B_{\kappa^{\xi/(1-m)}\,\mathcal{M}}\big(t,x\big)
where
B_{M}(t,x)
is as in Proposition
8
. The advantage comes from the identity

\frac{u(t,x)-B(t,x)}{B(t,x)}=\frac{\hat{u}_{t,1}\big(1,t^{-1/\xi}\,x\big)-\hat{B}_{t,1}\big(1,t^{-1/\xi}\,x\big)}{\hat{B}_{t,1}\big(1,t^{-1/\xi}\,x\big)}\,,

(32)

as the domain
|x|\leq 2\,\rho(\varepsilon)\,R(t)
is included in
|y|\leq 2\,Z\,\rho(\varepsilon)
where
y=x\,t^{-1/\xi}
for

large enough. So, to estimate the

semi-norm of the quotient

32
) on a domain which is now independent of the time.

The denominator of the right-hand side of (
32
) can be estimated from below by a direct computation, while we use the parabolic regularity theory developed in
[
7
,
32
]
to bound the numerator
|\hat{u}_{t,1}(1,y)-\hat{B}_{t,1}(1,y)|

-norm of
\hat{B}_{t,1}
can be estimated by a direct computation, while for

we use the fact that it is a solution to a linear equation

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=|x|^{\gamma}\,\nabla\cdot\left(|x|^{-\beta}\,A(t,x)\,\nabla u\right)

(33)

where the coefficient
A(t,x)=m\,u^{m-1}(t,x)

\varepsilon
, we estimate directly the
C^{\mu}
-norm of
\hat{u}_{t,1}
on the whole space

with subdomains of type
B_{k}(0)\setminus B_{k/2}(0)

, we apply to the rescaled function

\lfloor\hat{u}_{t,k}(1,\cdot)\rfloor_{C^{\mu}\left(B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{1/2}(0)\right)}=k^{\frac{\sigma}{1-m}+\mu}\,\lfloor\hat{u}_{t,1}(1,\cdot)\rfloor_{C^{\mu}\left(B_{k}(0)\setminus B_{k/2}(0)\right)}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t>0\,.

. Therefore, by
[
7

, which depends only on

\begin{split}\lfloor\hat{u}_{t,k}(1,\cdot)\rfloor_{C^{\mu}\left(B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{1/2}(0)\right)}&\leq\overline{c}_{1}\,\|\hat{u}_{t,k}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}\left((\frac{1}{2},4)\times B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{1/2}(0)\right)}\\

By setting

and letting

in

,

\beta
and

\left\|{B(t+\overline{\tau},x)-B(t,x)}\right\|_{1,\gamma}\leq\frac{\overline{c}}{t}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq T(\varepsilon)\,.

Combining (

3.1
An artificial dimension

Inequality (
12
) can be recast as an interpolation inequality with same weight in all integrals which, in terms of scaling properties, amounts to introduce an
artificial dimension
. To a function
f\in\mathrm{H}^{p}_{\beta,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, let us associate the function
F\in\mathrm{H}^{p}_{\nu,\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\nu:=d-n<0
such that

f(x)=F\left(|x|^{\alpha-1}\,x\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,

(40)

where

\alpha=1+\frac{\beta-\gamma}{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptn=2\,\frac{d-\gamma}{\beta+2-\gamma}\,.

Notice that
p_{\star}=n/(n-2)
. In spherical coordinates, with
r=|x|
and
\omega=x/r
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, let us define the derivation operator

\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5ptU:=\left(\alpha\,\frac{\partial U}{\partial r},\frac{1}{r}\,\nabla_{\kern-2.0pt\omega}U\right)\,.

With
\alpha>0
and
p\in(1,p_{\star}]
, we can rewrite (
12
) as

\left\|{U}\right\|_{2p,\nu}\leq\mathsf{K}_{\alpha,n,p}\,\left\|{\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5ptU}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{\theta}\,\left\|{U}\right\|_{p+1,\nu}^{1-\theta}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,U\in\mathrm{H}^{p}_{\nu,\nu}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\,,

(41)

for some optimal constant
\mathsf{K}_{\alpha,n,p}
which is explicitly related with the optimal constant in (
12
): see
[
4
, Proposition 6]
. Inequality (
41
) can be interpreted as a Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality in the artificial dimension
n
. As
\alpha\neq 1
unless
\beta=\gamma
, notice that symmetry issues in (
41
) are in no way simpler than in (
12
). A remarkable point is that the Aubin-Talenti type function as defined by (
14
) is transformed into the more standard function

x\mapsto\left(1+|x|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\,.

We refer to
[
4
, Section 2.3]
and
[
21
, Section 3.1]
for further details.

Through the transformation
u(t,x)=U\left(t,|x|^{\alpha-1}\,x\right)
, a solution
u
of (
15
) is transformed into a solution of

\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}={\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\,}U^{m}

(42)

where
\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 0.5pt
denotes the adjoint of
\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},|x|^{-\nu}\,dx)
and
{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\,}=-\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt
is an elliptic self-adjoint differential operator given in spherical coordinates by

{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\,}u=\alpha^{2}\left(u^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n-1}{r}\,u^{\prime}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,\Delta_{\omega}\,u\,.

Functions obtained from

by reduction to the artificial dimension

are self-similar Barenblatt solutions of (
42
). If
\mathcal{U}
is a solution of (
42
) with initial datum
\mathcal{B}
given by (
5
), then
\mathcal{U}(t,x)=R(t)^{-n}\,\mathcal{B}\big(x/R(t)\big)
if and only if

\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\mathrm{d}t}=\alpha^{2}\,R^{\,n\,(1-m)-1}\,.

If we additionally assume that
R(0)=1
, this can be solved as

R(t):=(1+\alpha^{2}\,\xi\,t)^{1/\xi}

and
\xi=n\,(m-m_{c})
and
m_{c}=(n-2)/n
as in (
23
). Up to a time shift, this definition of
R(t)
generalizes the definition of Section
2
to the case
\alpha\neq 1
. Other self-similar Barenblatt solutions of (
42
) have same scaling properties and initial data given by
\mathcal{B}
, up to a multiplication by a constant and a scaling.

3.2
Flow and entropies in self-similar variables

Self-similar solutions suggest to rewrite (
42
) in the corresponding scales using the
self-similar change of variables

U(t,x)=\frac{\lambda^{d}}{R(t)^{d}}\,V\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\log R(t),\frac{\lambda\,x}{R(t)}\right)

(43)

where
\lambda^{n\,(m-m_{c})}=(1-m)/(2\,m)

U
solves (

\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}=\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 0.5pt\cdot\left(V\left(\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5ptV^{m-1}-2\,x\right)\right)\,,\hskip 10.00002ptV(t=0,\cdot)=V_{0}

with nonnegative initial datum

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{V(t,\cdot)\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathcal{M}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0

The
free energy
(or

) and the

(or
relative entropy production

\mathcal{F}[V]:=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left(V^{m}-\mathcal{B}^{m}-m\,\mathcal{B}^{m-1}\,(V-\mathcal{B})\right)|x|^{-\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x

and, as a generalization of definition (

\mathcal{I}[V]:=\frac{m}{1-m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{V\,\left|\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5ptV^{m-1}-\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathcal{B}^{m-1}\right|^{2}\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

With
V=|f|^{2\,p}
,
p=1/(2\,m-1)

) is equivalent to the
entropy – entropy production inequality

\mathcal{I}[V]\geq 4\,\alpha^{2}\,\mathcal{F}[V]

(45)

in the symmetry range
for (

V
solves (
44

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{F}[V(t,\cdot)]=-\,\mathcal{I}[V(t,\cdot)]

(46)

[

\mathcal{F}[V(t,\cdot)]\leq\mathcal{F}[V_{0}]\,e^{-\,4\,\alpha^{2}\,t}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

(47)

3.3
An improved spectral gap

We consider the
linearized free energy
and the
linearized Fisher information
given respectively by

\mathsf{F}[h]:=\frac{m}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|h|^{2}\,\mathcal{B}^{2-m}\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{I}[h]:=m\,(1-m)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pth|^{2}\,\mathcal{B}\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

These quadratic forms are obtained as

\mathsf{F}[h]=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-2}\,\mathcal{F}\big[\mathcal{B}+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{B}^{2-m}\,h\big]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{I}[h]=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-2}\,\mathcal{I}\big[\mathcal{B}+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{B}^{2-m}\,h\big]\,.

The following result is taken from
[
4
, Proposition 4]

Proposition 12
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,+\infty)
,
\nu=d-n<0
and
\delta=1/(1-m)\geq n
. Then the

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pth|^{2}\,\mathcal{B}\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\Lambda\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|h|^{2}\,\mathcal{B}^{2-m}\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x

, with an optimal constant

\Lambda=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}2\,\alpha^{2}\,(2\,\delta-n)&\mbox{if}\quad 0<\alpha^{2}\leq\frac{(d-1)\,\delta^{2}}{n\,(2\,\delta-n)\,(\delta-1)}\,,\\[6.0pt]

A stability result for GNS

In this section, we deal with the non-weighted case
(\beta,\gamma)=(0,0)
. Detailed proof are given in
[
6
]
. Here we simply outline the main steps of the proofs and emphasize the differences with the weighted case studied in Section

4.1
A quotient estimate

For any function
v\neq\mathcal{B}
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{v}\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathcal{M}
, with
\mathcal{M}
defined by (
24
), which is smooth enough and sufficiently decaying at infinity let us consider the quotient

\mathcal{Q}[v]:=\frac{\mathcal{I}[v]}{\mathcal{F}[v]}

where
\mathcal{F}[v]
and
\mathcal{I}[v]
are defined respectively by (
4
) and (
7
).

Lemma 14
.

Assume that
v
solves (
6
). Then we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{Q}[v(t,\cdot)]\leq\mathcal{Q}\,(\mathcal{Q}-4)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

Equation (
6
) corresponds to the fast diffusion equation without weights,
i.e.
,
\beta=\gamma=0
and the result follows from

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{I}[v(t,\cdot)]\leq-\,4\,\mathcal{I}[v(t,\cdot)]\,,

which itself arises from the
carré du champ
method adapted to nonlinear flows. See for instance
[
20
]
and references therein. With
(\beta,\gamma)\neq(0,0)
, such an estimate is so far formal.

4.2
Scheme of the proof of Theorem
1

In the absence of weights the result follows by considering the improved spectral gap obtained for the flow (
6
). We shall only sketch the main steps of the proof and the interested reader may find the whole proof in
[
6
, Chp. 5]
. We first consider the subcritical case
1<p<p^{\star}
.

Step 0: normalization.
We notice that the deficit functional
\delta[\cdot]
defined in (
1.1
) is invariant by translations so that, without loss of generality, we assume that
|f|^{2p}
has zero center of mass. As well, there is no harm to assume
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|f|^{2p}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathsf{g}^{2p}}\,\mathrm{d}x
where
\mathsf{g}
is as in (
2
), since the general case can be recover by scalings, see
[
6
, Chapter 5]
. We learn from
[
6
, Lemma 1.12]
that

\frac{p+1}{p-1}\,\delta[f]=\mathcal{I}[v]-4\,\mathcal{F}[v]

with the notation
v=|f|^{2p}
and
p=1/(2\,m-1)
. Notice that translations are not allowed if
(\beta,\gamma)\neq(0,0)
, but that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{x\,|f|^{2p}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
is not required in that case.

Step 1: improved entropy – entropy production inequality in the asymptotic time layer.
Let
v(t)
be the solution the Cauchy problem (
6
) with initial datum
v(0)=|f|^{2p}
. By Theorem
7
we know that, for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, the relative error satisfies
|(v(t,\cdot)-\mathsf{g}^{2p})/\mathsf{g}^{2p}|<\varepsilon
for any
t>t_{\star}
where
t_{\star}
is as in (
25
). So, we are in the position of using Lemma
13
: under the center of mass condition, the improved entropy – entropy production inequality

\mathcal{I}[v(t,\cdot)]\geq(4+\zeta)\,\mathcal{F}[v(t,\cdot)]\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq t_{\star}

(49)

holds with
\zeta=2\,d\,(m-m_{1})
. The computation of the precise value of
\zeta
can be found in
[
6
, Proposition 2.10]
. The additional constraint
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{x\,v_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
is needed to have
\zeta>0
.

Step 2: improved entropy – entropy production inequality in the initial time layer.

By integrating the differential inequality of Lemma
14
backwards on
[0,t_{\star}]
, under the assumption that
Q(v(t_{\star}))\geq 4+\zeta
, one finds

\mathcal{I}[v(t,\cdot)]\geq(4+\mu)\,\mathcal{F}[v(t,\cdot)]\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\in[0,t_{\star}]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\mu=\frac{4\,\zeta\,e^{-4\,t_{\star}}}{4+\zeta-\zeta\,e^{-4\,t_{\star}}}\,.

As a consequence, the improvement obtained in the asymptotic time layer
[t_{\star},+\infty)
can be transferred to the initial time layer
[0,t_{\star}]
and up to the initial datum, with a smaller improvement of the constant. By multiplying the improved inequality by
4/(4+\mu)
, we obtain

\mathcal{I}[v]-4\,\mathcal{F}[v]\geq\frac{\mu}{4+\mu}\,\mathcal{I}[v]

with
v=v(t,\cdot)
for any
t\geq 0
and, as a special case, for

. Notice that the constant
\mu/(4+\mu)
can be estimated explicitly since the dependence of

is given by (
25

Step 3: rescaling and proof of Inequality (

So far we have proven, with the above notation, that

\frac{p+1}{p-1}\,\delta[f]\geq\mathcal{C}\,\mathcal{I}[v]\,,

To obtain inequality (

\mathcal{I}[v]\geq\inf_{\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\big|(p-1)\,\nabla f+f^{p}\,\nabla\varphi^{1-p}\big|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

Step 4: the critical case

Due to the lack of an improved spectral gap in Step 1 under the previous scheme, with mass and center of mass constraints, a second moment constraint is also needed. This amounts to change the evolution equation to a new one which allows to control the evolution of the second moment as well: in practice we need to rescale Equation (

5
A conjecture on stability for Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities

In Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities (
1
) and in Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities (
12
), the invariance under scalings plays an important role, as well as in the corresponding fast diffusion equations (
10
) and (
15
). This explains why entropy methods are so efficient to get sharp results for the best constants as discussed in
[
20
]
. A key feature is the
carré du champ
method, which has been rigorously implemented for (
10
) in
[
13
]
, in the context of parabolic equations. The regularity and decay estimates needed to justify the
carré du champ
method in the context of the fast diffusion flow associated with (
12
) are so far missing, although some partial estimates are known from
[
20
,
24
]
. This is why the symmetry results in
[
21
]
were based on an elliptic version of the method, which formally also applies to parabolic equation (
15
). Proving that all necessary integrations by parts can be justified would establish the following conjecture:

For some
\zeta>0
, Inequality (
20
) holds for any
t\geq 0
.

In other words, this means that in Theorem
5
, one can take
T=0
. Indeed, the
carré du champ
estimate would allow us to extend the estimate on the asymptotic time layer
(T,+\infty)
to the initial time layer
(0,T)
and find a smaller but still constructive value for
\zeta
depending only on the initial datum, by the same scheme as in
[
6
]
. A straightforward consequence would be an improved entropy – entropy production inequality that would provide us with a
stability result with a constructive estimate
for Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities (
12
) similar to the result of Theorem
1
. With this method, the stability would be measured by a relative Fisher information as for (
1
). Such a result is to be expected as, in the critical case of (
12
), a stability result
without
constructive estimate has already been established by F. Seuffert in
[
31
]
.

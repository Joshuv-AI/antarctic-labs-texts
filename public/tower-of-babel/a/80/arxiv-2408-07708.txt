Abstract
. Using properties of harmonic functions in multidimensional space, we transform the Hartree–Fock eigenvalue problem into a more tractable eigenvalue problem in which the Laplacian is eliminated. This new formulation may facilitate the development of novel ways of finding approximate solutions of the electronic problem.

2.
Preliminaries

Here we review our basic assumptions, and state and prove some preliminary results. In stating the problem we follow the formulation of Szabo and Ostlund
[
7
]
, but the method can be adapted to more general situations, such as those discussed in, e.g.,
[
6
]
.

The functions
\psi_{a},a=1,\dots,n
, are spatial molecular orbitals; therefore if
Z_{j}
is the atomic number of nucleus
N_{j}
, which is located at point
\sigma_{j}\in\text{${\mathbb{R}}$}^{3}
, they satisfy the Hartree–Fock equations

(2)

\left[-\nabla^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{Z_{j}}{|r-\sigma_{j}|}\right]\psi_{a}(r)+2\sum_{c=1}^{n}\int|\psi_{c}(s)|^{2}\frac{1}{|r-s|}\,ds\,\psi_{a}(r)\\
-\sum_{c=1}^{n}\int\psi_{c}^{\ast}(s)\psi_{a}(s)\frac{1}{|r-s|}\,ds\,\psi_{c}(r)=\varepsilon_{a}\psi_{a}(r),\qquad a=1,\dots,n.

This eigenvalue problem is of the type studied by Kato
[
5
]
, who showed that the orbitals
\psi_{a}
must be continuous on
\text{${\mathbb{R}}$}^{3}
, that they have partial derivatives of order 1, except at the points
\sigma_{j}
, and that these derivatives are bounded. We also know that
||\psi_{a}||_{\infty}\leq 1
, and that the sequence
\{\psi_{a};a=1,\dots,n\}
is orthonormal in
L_{2}
.

Equation (
2
) implies that the functions
\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}
are defined everywhere on
\text{${\mathbb{R}}$}^{3}
except at the points
\sigma_{j}
. Since the kinetic energy of the electrons must be finite, we also assume that the functions
\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}
are in
L_{\infty}
.

We begin with some preliminary statements.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f,g\in L_{1}\cap L_{\infty}
and
r\in\text{${\mathbb{R}}$}^{3}
; then

(a)
\displaystyle||f\,g\,h_{r}||_{1}\leq 2\pi||fg||_{\infty}+||fg||_{1}
.

(b)
\displaystyle||fh_{r}||_{1}\leq 2\pi||f||_{\infty}+||f||_{1}
.

(c)
\displaystyle||fh_{r}||_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{\pi}||f||_{\infty}+||f||_{2}
.

In particular these assertions hold if
f
and
g
are molecular spin orbitals.

Proof.

(a)\ \int|f(s)g(s)||r-s|^{-1}\,ds=\int|f(r-s)g(r-s)|s|^{-1}|\,ds\\
=\int_{|s|\leq 1}|f(r-s)g(r-s)|s|^{-1}|\,ds+\int_{|s|>1}|f(r-s)g(r-s)|s|^{-1}|\,dr=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Switching to spherical coordinates we get

I_{1}\leq||fg||_{\infty}\int_{|s|\leq 1}|s|^{-1}\,ds=2\pi||fg||_{\infty}.

Clearly

I_{2}\leq||fg||_{1},

and the assertion follows.

The proof of (b) is similar to that of (a) and will be omitted.

(c) We have

\int|r-s|^{-2}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds=\\
\int_{|r-s|\leq 1}|r-s|^{-2}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds+\int_{|r-s|>1}|r-s|^{-2}|f(s)|^{2}\,ds=I_{3}+I_{4}.

Switching to spherical coordinates we get

I_{3}\leq||f||^{2}_{\infty}\int_{|r-s|\leq 1}|r-s|^{-2}\,ds=||f||^{2}_{\infty}\int_{|s\leq 1}|s|^{-2}\,ds=4\pi||f||^{2}_{\infty}.

Since

I_{4}\leq\int_{|r-s|>1}|f(r)|^{2}\,dr\leq||f||^{2}_{2},

the assertion follows.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let

\begin{split}f(r)=&\sum_{j=1}^{n}\quad\frac{Z_{j}}{|r-\sigma_{j}|},\\
g(r)=&2\sum_{c=1}^{n}\int|\psi_{c}(s)|^{2}\frac{1}{|r-s|}\,ds,\\
q_{a,c}(r)=&\int\psi_{c}^{\ast}(s)\psi_{a}(s)\frac{1}{|r-s|}ds.\\
\end{split}

Then

(a)
||g||_{\infty}\leq(4\pi+2)n
, and
||q_{a,c}||_{\infty}\leq 2\sqrt{\pi}+1
.

(b) If
\psi_{a}\in L_{1}
, then
||f\psi_{a}||_{1}\leq(2\pi+||\psi_{a}||_{1})\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}
.

(c)
||f\psi_{a}||_{1}\leq(2\sqrt{\pi}+1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\psi_{a}
be a molecular orbital. Then

(a)
\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}\in L^{2}
.

(b) For any
t>0
and
r\neq\sigma_{j},j=1,\dots,n
,

(3)

\nabla^{2}(\psi_{a}\ast P_{t})(r)=(\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}\ast P_{t})(r).

(c)

\mbox{$\partial$}^{2}_{t}(\psi_{a}\ast P_{t})(r)=(\psi_{a}\ast\mbox{$\partial$}^{2}_{t}P_{t})(r)

Proof.

(a) Corollary
1
(a) implies that
g(r)
and
q_{a,c}(r)
are bounded and Corollary
1
(c) implies that
f(r)\psi_{a}
(r) is in
L_{2}
.
Since (
2
) is equivalent to

(4)

\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}(r)=\left(g(r)-f(r)-\varepsilon_{a}\right)\psi_{a}(r)-\sum_{c=1}^{n}q_{a,c}(r)\psi_{c}(r),

the assertion follows.

(b) Since
\psi
is not necessarily in
L_{1}
we cannot use standard theorems such as
[
3
, 2.27]
or
[
3
, 8.10]
directly; instead, we proceed as follows:
let
e=(1,1,1)
and
\Gamma(\xi,r)=\psi_{a}(r+\xi e)
,
\xi\geq 0
, and let
\Gamma[0,\xi,2\xi,r]
denote the divided difference of order 2 of
\Gamma(\cdot,r)
at
0,\xi,2\xi
; if

\xi
is small enough so that none of the points
\sigma_{j}
(where the nuclei are located) lie on the line segment from
r
to
r+\xi e
, there is a number
\theta
between
0
and
2\xi
such that

\Gamma[0,\xi,2\xi,r]=\frac{\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}{2};

but

\frac{\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\theta)}{2}=\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}(r+\theta e),

thus

\psi_{a}[r,r+\xi e,r+2\xi e]=\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}(r+\theta e).

Since (
4
) implies that
\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}
is continuous at
r
, we conclude that

\lim_{\xi\rightarrow 0+}\psi_{a}[r,r+\xi e,r+2\xi e]=\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}(r).

Proceeding as above we similarly see that

\lim_{\xi\rightarrow 0+}(\psi_{a}*P_{t})[r,r+\xi e,r+2\xi e]=\nabla^{2}(\psi_{a}*P_{t})(r),

where the existence of
\nabla^{2}(\psi_{a}\ast P_{t})
is implied by Theorem
1
(a).

A straightforward computation shows that

(\psi_{a}*P_{t})[r,r+\xi e,r+2\xi e]=\int\psi_{a}[s,s+\xi e,s+2\xi e]P_{t}(r-s)\,ds.

Since
\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}
is bounded, we see that for every
\xi
,

\big|\psi_{a}[r,r+\xi e,r+2\xi e]\big|\leq||\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}||_{\infty}.

But, for large,
r

P_{t}(r)\leq K|r|^{-4}
; thus, applying the
bounded convergence theorem we conclude that

\lim_{\xi\rightarrow 0+}\int\big|\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}(s)P_{t}(r-s)-\psi_{a}[s,s+\xi e,s+2\xi e]P_{t}(r-s)\big|\,ds=0,

whence the assertion follows.

(c) This identity is known, it is implicitly used in the proof of
[
4
, 2.44]
; we include a proof for the sake of completeness.

Let
\xi>0
and let
P_{t}[0,\xi,2\xi,r]
denote the divided difference of order 2 of
P_{t}
at
r
,
r+\xi
, and

such that

. But for large

Singular Soergel bimodules for realizations
Noriyuki Abe

Abstract
. Williamson defined the category of singular Soergel bimodules attached to a reflection faithful representation of a Coxeter group. We generalize this construction to more general realizations of Coxeter groups.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
20F55

1.
Introduction

A Hecke category, by which we mean a good categorification of the Hecke algebra, is an important object in representation theory.
The first Hecke category that appeared in representation theory is the category of Borel subgroup equivariant semisimple complexes on the flag variety and it plays an important role in the proof of the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture.
Soergel
[
Soe07
]
introduced another Hecke category.
His category is not geometric, it is algebraic.
Soergel also proved that his category is equivalent to the category of semisimple complexes when the field is of characteristic zero.
This equivalence is used to prove the Koszul duality of category
\mathcal{O}
for reductive Lie algebras
[
BGS96
]
.
There are also singular versions.
Instead of considering Borel equivariant semisimple complexes on the flag variety, we consider semisimple complexes on a generalized flag variety which is equivariant under the action of a parabolic subgroup.
A singular version of Soergel’s category is introduced by Williamson
[
Wil11
]
.

To prove that these categories are Hecke categories, we need some assumptions.
For the category of equivariant semisimple complexes, the characteristic of the coefficient field has to be zero.
For Soergel’s category, Soergel needed a representation of the Weyl group and he assumed that this is reflection faithful.
After those theories appeared, some new constructions which are the generalizations of these categories appeared.
As a generalization of the category of semisimple complexes, Juteau-Mauter-Williamson
[
JMW14
]
introduced the category of parity complexes and this gives the Hecke category for the coefficient field of any characteristic.
Note that this can be used to define the singular version.
A generalization of Soergel’s category was introduced in
[
Abe21
]
for the regular case and with
[
Abe20
]
it was proved that this category gives a Hecke category if it is attached to the root system.
There is also another Hecke category for the regular case defined by Elias-Williamson
[
EW16
]
and this also works well if it is attached to the root system
[
EW23
,
Haz23
]
.

So the natural question is to construct a category that generalizes the category of singular Soergel bimodules introduced by Williamson.
A partial answer was given in
[
Abe23
]
.
In
[
Abe23
]
we defined a category that generalizes the category of Soergel bimodules which is singular only from the left.
The aim of this paper is to give a category that generalizes Williamson’s theory, namely the category of Soergel bimodules which are singular from both sides.

To give the theorem more precisely, let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter system and
\mathcal{H}
the Hecke algebra.
This is a
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-algebra with a basis
\{H_{w}\mid w\in W\}
where
v
is an indeterminate.
Fix subsets
S_{1},S_{2}\subset S
such that the group
W_{S_{i}}
generated by
S_{i}
is finite for
i=1,2
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{S_{i}}
be the Hecke algebra of
(W_{S_{i}},S_{i})
.
Then this is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\mathrm{triv}_{S_{i}}\colon\mathcal{H}_{S_{i}}\to\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
be the trivial representation and put
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{2}}=\Hom_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathrm{triv}_{S_{2}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{S_{2}}}\mathcal{H},\mathrm{triv}_{S_{1}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{S_{1}}}\mathcal{H})
.
If
S_{3}\subset S
is another subset such that
W_{S_{3}}
is finite, then we have a
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-linear bilinear form
*_{S_{2}}\colon{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{2}}\times{}_{S_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}}\to{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}}
defined by the composition.
Williamson defined a category which categorifies modules
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{2}}
and the map
*_{S_{2}}
.

Soergel and Williamson needed a certain representation of
W
.
For simplicity, let
\mathbb{K}
be a field (in the main body we will work with a complete local Noetherian ring) and
V
a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-representation of
W
.
Let
V^{*}
be the dual space of
V
.
Following Elias-Williamson
[
EW16
]
, we assume that for each
s\in S
non-zero elements
\alpha_{s}\in V
and
\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\in V^{*}
which satisfy
s(v)=v-\langle v,\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\rangle\alpha_{s}
for any
v\in V
and
s(\alpha_{s})=-\alpha_{s}
are given.
Soergel and Williamson assumed that
V
is reflection faithful and under this assumption, Williamson constructed categories
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
and bifunctors
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}\times{}_{S_{2}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{3}}\to{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{3}}
and proved that these categories and functors form a categorification of the above data.
(To be more precise, he assumed that the torsion primes for
(W_{S_{i}},S_{i})
are non-zero in
\mathbb{K}
. We also need this assumption for our theorem.)

1.1.
The category of singular Soergel bimodules

Now we state our main theorem.
We do not assume that
V
is reflection faithful.
However, we assume that
V
satisfies
[
Abe20
, Assumption 1.1]
which enables us to use a theory in
[
Abe21
]
, in particular, we have a Hecke category for the regular case.
Let
R
be the symmetric algebra of
V
and set
Q=R[w(\alpha_{s})^{-1}\mid w\in W,s\in S]
.
Let us recall the construction in
[
Abe21
]
briefly.
We define the category
\mathcal{C}
as follows: an object in
\mathcal{C}
is
M=(M,(M_{x}^{Q})_{x\in W})
where
M
is a graded
R
-bimodule and
M_{x}^{Q}
is a graded
Q
-bimodule such that
mf=x(f)m
for
m\in M_{Q}^{x}
,
f\in Q
and
M\otimes_{R}Q=\bigoplus_{x\in W}M_{Q}^{x}
.
We often write only
M
for the object
(M,(M_{x}^{Q})_{x\in W})
.
The morphism
M\to N
is an
R
-module homomorphism
\varphi\colon M\to N
of degree zero such that
\varphi(M_{x}^{Q})\subset N_{x}^{Q}
for any
x\in W
.
It is easy to see that this category is additive.
Moreover, it has a structure of a monoidal category as follows.
Let
M,N\in\mathcal{C}
.
Then
M\otimes N\in\mathcal{C}
is defined as
(M\otimes_{R}N,(M\otimes N)_{x}^{Q})
where
(M\otimes N)_{x}^{Q}=\bigoplus_{y,z\in W,yz=x}M_{y}^{Q}\otimes_{Q}N_{z}^{Q}
.
The regular Hecke category (the category of Soergel bimodules)
\mathcal{S}
is defined as the full-subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
generated by Bott-Samelson bimodules.
(Here we omit the precise definition, because it is not important for explaining the category for singular cases.)

To define the categories
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
, we need the following assumptions on
S_{i}
.

(1)

The representation
V
is faithful as a
W_{S_{i}}
-representation.

(2)

Any torsion prime of
(W_{S_{i}},S_{i})
in
V
is non-zero.

For the precise meaning of the second assumption, see Assumption
2.8
.

Fix such
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
.
Let
R^{S_{i}}
(resp.
Q^{S_{i}}
) be the ring of
W_{S_{i}}
-fixed points in
R
(resp.
Q
).
Let
x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
and we regard this as a subset of
W
.
We consider the algebra
\prod_{a\in x}Q
and an action of
W_{S_{1}}\times W_{S_{2}}
defined by
w_{1}(f_{a})w_{2}=(w_{1}(f_{w_{1}^{-1}aw_{2}^{-1}}))_{a\in x}
.
Then we can consider the algebra
{}^{S_{1}}Q^{S_{2}}_{x}=(\prod_{a\in x}Q)^{W_{S_{1}}\times W_{S_{2}}}
.
We define a structure map
Q^{S_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Q^{S_{2}}\to{}^{S_{1}}Q^{S_{2}}_{x}
by
f\otimes g\mapsto(fa(g))_{a\in x}
.
Then
{}^{S_{1}}Q^{S_{2}}_{x}
is a
Q^{S_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Q^{S_{2}}
-algebra.
Using this algebra, we define the category
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
.
The category
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
is the category of
(M,(M_{Q}^{x})_{x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}})
which satisfy the following conditions.

•

M
is a graded
(R^{S_{1}},R^{S_{2}})
-bimodule.

•

M_{x}^{Q}
is an
{}^{S_{1}}Q^{S_{2}}_{x}
-module.

•

We have
Q^{S_{1}}\otimes_{R^{S_{1}}}M\otimes_{R^{S_{2}}}Q^{S_{2}}\simeq\bigoplus_{x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}}M_{x}^{Q}
as
(Q^{S_{1}},Q^{S_{2}})
-bimodules where
Q^{S_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Q^{S_{2}}
acts on the right through the structure map.

•

Some other conditions (see Definition
2.2
).

We only write
M
for
(M,(M_{Q}^{x}))
.
For
M\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
we have a grading shift
M(k)\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear graded category.

Let
M_{1}\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
and
M_{2}\in{}_{S_{2}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{3}}
, we define
M_{1}\otimes_{S_{2}}M_{2}\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{3}}
.
As an
(R^{S_{1}},R^{S_{3}})
-bimodule, we set
M_{1}\otimes_{S_{2}}M_{2}=M_{1}\otimes_{R^{S_{2}}}M_{2}
.
To define
(M_{1}\otimes_{S_{2}}M_{2})_{Q}^{x}
for
x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{3}}
, we need to analyze an
{}^{S_{1}}Q^{S_{2}}_{y}\otimes_{Q^{S_{2}}}{}^{S_{2}}Q^{S_{3}}_{z}
-module
(M_{1})_{Q}^{y}\otimes_{Q^{S_{2}}}(M_{2})_{Q}^{z}
.
Since the definition needs some preparation, we skip the definition in the introduction.
See subsection
2.4
for the definition.

The category
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
is defined as a full-subcategory of
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
.
Let
M\in\mathcal{C}
.
Then by restriction,
M
is an
(R^{S_{1}},R^{S_{2}})
-bimodule.
For
x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
, we consider
\bigoplus_{a\in x}M_{Q}^{x}
.
Then this is
\prod_{a\in x}Q
-module, hence
{}^{S_{1}}Q^{S_{2}}_{x}
-module.
This data gives an object in
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
and define a functor
{}_{S_{1},\emptyset}\pi_{S_{2},\emptyset,*}\colon\mathcal{C}\to{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
.
Now for
M\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
, we declare
M\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
if
M
is a direct summand of
{}_{S_{1},\emptyset}\pi_{S_{2},\emptyset,*}(N)
for some
N\in\mathcal{S}
.
We can also prove that if
M_{1}\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
and
M_{2}\in{}_{S_{2}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{3}}
then
M_{1}\otimes_{S_{2}}M_{2}\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{3}}
.

Let
[{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}]
be the split Grothendieck group of
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
.
Since
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
is graded, this is a
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-module and the functor
\otimes_{S_{2}}
gives a
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-bilinear map
[{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}]\times[{}_{S_{2}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{3}}]\to[{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{3}}]
.
We also note that we have a natural
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-basis
\{{}^{S_{1}}H_{x}^{S_{2}}\mid x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}\}
of
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{2}}
.
The following is the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have the following.

(1)

There exists a
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
-linear isomorphism
{}_{S_{1}}\mathrm{ch}_{S_{2}}\colon[{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}]\xrightarrow{\sim}{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{2}}
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}]\times[{}_{S_{2}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{3}}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{3}}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{2}}\times{}_{S_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{H}_{S_{3}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes_{S_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}_{S_{1}}\mathrm{ch}_{S_{2}}\times{}_{S_{2}}\mathrm{ch}_{S_{3}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}_{S_{1}}\mathrm{ch}_{S_{3}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*_{S_{2}}}

is commutative.

(2)

For each
x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
there exists an indecomposable object
{}_{S_{1}}B_{S_{2}}(x)\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
such that
{}_{S_{1}}\mathrm{ch}_{S_{2}}({}_{S_{1}}B_{S_{2}}(x))\in{}^{S_{1}}H^{S_{2}}_{x}+\sum_{y<x}\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]{}^{S_{1}}H^{S_{2}}_{y}
.
Moreover, this is unique up to isomorphism.

(3)

For any indecomposable object
B\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
there exists
x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
B\simeq{}_{S_{1}}B_{S_{2}}(x)(n)
.

The proof of this theorem is different from that of Williamson.
We will construct the left adjoint functor of
{}_{S_{1}}\pi_{S_{2},*}
and using this functor we reduce problems to those for
\mathcal{S}
.
Finally, we use results in
[
Abe21
]
.

1.2.
Relation with parity complexes

Let
G
be a Kac-Moody group,
W
the Weyl group of
G
and
S
the set of simple reflections in
S
.
For
S_{1},S_{2}\subset S
, let
{}_{S_{1}}X
be the generalized flag variety corresponding to
S_{1}
(here
G
acts from the right) and
P_{S_{2}}
the parabolic subgroup corresponding to
S_{2}
.
We consider the
W
-representation
V=X^{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}
where
T\subset G
is the maximal torus and
X^{*}(T)
the group of characters of
T
.
Assume that
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
satisfy the above assumptions and let
\Parity_{P_{S_{2}}}({}_{S_{1}}X)
be the category of
P_{S_{2}}
-equivariant parity complexes on
{}_{S_{1}}X
.
Let
S_{3}
be another subset which satisfies the above assumptions.
Then we have the convolution functor
*_{S_{2}}\colon\Parity_{P_{S_{2}}}({}_{S_{1}}X)\times\Parity_{P_{S_{3}}}({}_{S_{2}}X)\to\Parity_{P_{S_{3}}}({}_{S_{1}}X)
.
(The functor is first defined as a functor between derived categories and by a result in
[
JMW14
]
this induces a functor between parity complexes.)
Let
\mathcal{F}\in\Parity_{P_{S_{2}}}({}_{S_{1}}X)
and put
{}_{S_{1}}\mathbb{H}_{S_{2}}(\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{k}_{P_{S_{2}}}({}_{S_{1}}X,\mathcal{F})
.
This is an
(R^{S_{1}},R^{S_{2}})
-bimodule and we can endow it with a structure of an object of
{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{C}_{S_{2}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\mathcal{F}\in\Parity_{P_{S_{2}}}({}_{S_{1}}X)
we have
{}_{S_{1}}\mathbb{H}_{S_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\in{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
and this gives an equivalence of categories
{}_{S_{1}}\mathbb{H}_{S_{2}}\colon\Parity_{P_{S_{2}}}({}_{S_{1}}X)\to{}_{S_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{S_{2}}
.
Moreover we have
{}_{S_{1}}\mathbb{H}_{S_{3}}(\mathcal{F}*_{S_{2}}\mathcal{G})\simeq{}_{S_{1}}\mathbb{H}_{S_{2}}(\mathcal{F})\otimes_{S_{2}}{}_{S_{2}}\mathbb{H}_{S_{3}}(\mathcal{G})
for
\mathcal{F}\in\Parity_{P_{S_{2}}}({}_{S_{1}}X)
and
\mathcal{G}\in\Parity_{P_{S_{3}}}({}_{S_{2}}X)
.

When
G
is a finite dimensional reductive group,
S_{1}=S_{2}=\emptyset
and if the characteristic of
\mathbb{K}
is zero, then this is a theorem of Soergel
[
BGS96
]
.
The author thinks this theorem is known to experts at least when the characteristic of
\mathbb{K}
is zero, but it does not exist in the literature as far as the author looked for.

2.
Singular Soergel bimodules

2.1.
Notation

In this paper, we use the following notation.
Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter system,
\mathbb{K}
a complete local noetherian integral domain.
The unit element of
W
is denoted by
1
and the length function is denoted by
\ell
.
We fix a realization
(V,\{(\alpha_{s},\alpha_{s}^{\vee})\}_{s\in S})
over
\mathbb{K}
, namely
V
is a free
\mathbb{K}
-module of finite rank with an action of
W
,
\alpha_{s}\in V,\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\in\Hom_{\mathbb{K}}(V,\mathbb{K})
such that
s(v)=v-\langle\alpha_{s}^{\vee},v\rangle\alpha_{s}
for
s\in S,v\in V
and
\langle\alpha_{s}^{\vee},\alpha_{s}\rangle=2

[
EW16
, Definition 3.1]
.
We assume that
\alpha_{s}\neq 0
and
\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\colon V\to\mathbb{K}
is surjective.
We also assume the vanishing of the two-colored quantum binomial coefficients
[
Abe20
, Assumption 1.1]
.
Since the last assumption is technical and is not important for the arguments in this paper, we do not recall the precise statement.
We remark that this assumption enable us to use results in
[
Abe21
]
by the main theorem of
[
Abe20
]
.
We also remark that
[
Abe20
, Assumption 1.1]
is automatically satisfied when
(W,S,V)
comes from a Kac-Moody root datum
[
Abe20
, Proposition 3.7]
.

We call
t\in W
a reflection if it is conjugate to an element in
S
.
For each reflection
t=wsw^{-1}
with
s\in S
and
w\in W
, we put
\alpha_{t}=w(\alpha_{s})
.
This depends on a choice of the pair
(w,s)
but
\mathbb{K}^{\times}\alpha_{t}
does not depend on
(w,s)

[
Abe21
, Lemma 2.1]
.
We fix such
(w,s)
for each
t
to define
\alpha_{t}
.
Let
R
be the symmetric algebra of
V
over
\mathbb{K}
and let
Q=R[\alpha_{t}^{-1}\mid\text{$t$ is a reflection}]
.
For a subset
S_{1}\subset S
, let
W_{S_{1}}
be the group generated by
S_{1}
.
We say that
S_{1}
is finitary if
W_{S_{1}}
is finite.
If
S_{1}
is finitary then let
w_{S_{1}}
be the longest element in
W_{S_{1}}
.
Let
S_{1},S_{2}\subset S
be finitary subsets.
Then each
w\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
has the minimal (resp. the maximal) length representative.
Let
w_{-}
(resp.
w_{+}
) be this representative.

The Bruhat order on
W
is denoted by
\leq
.
We also define the order
\leq
on
W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
by
x\leq y
if and only if
x_{-}\leq y_{-}
.
We define a topology on
W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
as follows: a subset
I\subset W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
is open if
y\in I
,
x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
,
x\geq y
implies
x\in I
.

The algebra
R
is a graded algebra with
\deg(V)=2
.
Here, by graded we always mean
\mathbb{Z}
-graded.
In general, let
A
be a commutative graded algebra.
For a graded
A
-module
M=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M^{i}
, we define a graded
A
-module
M(k)
by
M(k)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M(k)^{i}
,
M(k)^{i}=M^{i+k}
.
A graded
A
-module
M
is called graded free if it is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}A(n_{i})
for some
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Note that in this paper graded free means graded free of finite rank.
If
M\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}A(n_{i})
is graded free, the graded rank
\grk_{A}(M)\in\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
of
M
is defined by
\grk_{A}(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v^{n_{i}}
where
v
is an indeterminate.

2.2.
A category

In general, for a subset
S_{1}\subset S
, let
Q^{S_{1}}=Q^{W_{S_{1}}}
be the subring of
W_{S_{1}}
-fixed elements in
Q
and put
R^{S_{1}}=R^{W_{S_{1}}}=R\cap Q^{W_{S_{1}}}
.
Let
S_{1}\subset S
be a subset such that
\#W_{S_{1}}<\infty
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a multiplicative subset of
R
generated by
\{\alpha_{t}\mid\text{$t$ is a reflection}\}
and
\Sigma^{S_{1}}
the subset of
\Sigma
consisting of
W_{S_{1}}
-fixed elements.
Then
Q^{S_{1}}=(\Sigma^{W_{S_{1}}})^{-1}R^{S_{1}}
.

Proof.

We have
Q=\Sigma^{-1}R
.
Obviously we have
(\Sigma^{S_{1}})^{-1}R^{S_{1}}\subset Q^{S_{1}}
.
Let
f_{0}\in Q^{S_{1}}
and take
s\in\Sigma
and
f\in R
such that
f_{0}=s^{-1}f
.
Set
s^{\prime}=\prod_{w\in W_{S_{1}}}w(s)
and
f^{\prime}=f\prod_{w\in W_{S_{1}}\setminus\{1\}}w(s)
.
Then
s^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{S_{1}}
and
f_{0}=(s^{\prime})^{-1}f^{\prime}
.
Since
f_{0}
and
s^{\prime}
are fixed by
W_{S_{1}}
,
f^{\prime}=f_{0}s^{\prime}
is also fixed by
W_{S_{1}}
.
Hence
f_{0}\in(\Sigma^{S_{1}})^{-1}R^{S_{1}}
.
∎

Let
x\in W_{S_{1}}\backslash W/W_{S_{2}}
and we define a left
W_{S_{1}}
-action and a right
W_{S_{2}}
-action on
\prod_{a\in x}Q
by
w_{1}(f_{a})w_{2}=(w_{1}(f_{w_{1}^{-1}aw_{2}^{-1}}))
.
We define

{}^{S_{1}}Q^{S_{2}}_{x}=\left(\prod_{a\in x}Q\right)^{W_{S_{1}}\times W_{S_{2}}}.

Note that the map
(f_{a})\mapsto f_{x_{-}}
gives an isomorphism

{}^{S_{1}}Q^{S_{2}}_{x}\simeq Q^{W_{S_{1}}\cap x_{-}W_{S_{2}}x_{-}^{-1}}

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

(1)

(2)

(1)

(2)
We have an embedding

and we prove that this is surjective.
It is sufficient to prove that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F\otimes_{F^{A}}F^{A}F^{B}\otimes_{F^{B}}F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F\otimes_{F^{A}}F^{A\cap B}\otimes_{F^{B}}F}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

By (1) the composition

We have

Abstract
. Introduction

An earlier article
[
Harp–88
]
was found to contain several mistakes.
A list of local corrections would have been confusing,
and we rather repeat with complete proofs
Propositions I and II below (II and III in
[
Harp–88
]
), about maximal subgroups.
Theorem I of
[
Harp–88
]
, about automorphisms,
is replaced here by a conjectural statement only.

Let
X
be a closed smooth manifold
and let
G
be a simple connected compact real Lie group,
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
M(G)
be the group of all smooth maps from
X
to
G
,
and let
M_{0}(G)
be its connected component
for the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-compact open topology
(the topology of uniform convergence of the maps and all their partial derivatives of all orders).
Then
M_{0}(G)
is an important example of a well behaved
infinite dimensional Lie group; see
[
Miln–84
,
PrSe–86
,
Neeb–06
,
KhWe–09
]
.
However,
M_{0}(G)
is viewed here as an abstract group.

Proposition I provides the classification
of all maximal normal subgroups of
M_{0}(G)
.
For
a\in X
, let
\varepsilon_{a}\,\colon M_{0}(G)\to G

denote the evaluation map
\gamma\mapsto\gamma(a)
,
and let

N_{a}M_{0}(G)=\varepsilon_{a}^{-1}(Z(G))

be the inverse image by
\varepsilon_{a}

of the centre
Z(G)
of
G
.
Since
G/Z(G)
is simple,

N_{a}M_{0}(G)
is a maximal normal subgroup of
M_{0}(G)
.
Conversely:

Proposition I.

Any proper normal subgroup of
M_{0}(G)

is contained in
N_{a}M_{0}(G)
for some
a\in X
.

One may think of Proposition I as a global result,
of which the proof uses Proposition II
which is a related local result.
Denote by
n
the dimension of
X
,
by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}

the set of germs at the origin
O
of
\mathbf{R}^{n}

of smooth maps from
\mathbf{R}^{n}
to
G
,
and by
\varepsilon\,\colon\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}\to G

the evaluation map
\underline{\gamma}\mapsto\underline{\gamma}(O)
.
Let

N_{O}\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}=\varepsilon^{-1}(Z(G))

be the inverse image of
Z(G)

by
\varepsilon
.
Then
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}
has a unique maximal normal subgroup;
in other words:

Proposition II.

Any proper normal subgroup of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}

is contained in
N_{O}\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}
.

Everything here works equally well for maps and germs
which are of class
\mathcal{C}^{k}
for some
k\geq 0
.
The proof of Proposition II works also in the real analytic setting.
Though a result analogous to Proposition I
for real analytic maps looks plausible,
it is not covered by our proof, which uses partitions of unity.
Also, I guess that Proposition II holds for a simple connected Lie group
which is not necessarily compact.

Let
M(\rm Aut(G))
be the group of smooth maps from
X

to the group
\rm Aut(G)
of automorphisms of
G

(recall that any automorphism in
\rm Aut(G)
is automatically continuous
[
Cart–30
]
,
indeed analytic).
Let
\mathcal{D}(X)
be the group of smooth diffeomorphisms of
X
.
Consider the natural action of
\mathcal{D}(X)
on
M(\rm Aut(G))
, defined by

\varphi(\beta)\,=\,\beta\circ\varphi^{-1}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for}\hskip 5.69046pt\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(X)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\beta\in M(\rm Aut(G)),

and the associated semi-direct product

M(\rm Aut(G))\rtimes\mathcal{D}(X),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{with multiplication}\hskip 5.69046pt(\alpha,\varphi)(\beta,\psi)\,=\,(\alpha\varphi(\beta),\varphi\psi).

This acts on
M_{0}(G)
by automorphisms:

(\alpha,\varphi)(\gamma)(x)\,=\,\alpha(x)\big(\gamma\left(\varphi^{-1}(x)\right)\big)

for
\alpha\in M(\rm Aut(G))
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(X)
,

\gamma\in M_{0}(G)
, and

.
We believe that any automorphism of the abstract group
M_{0}(G)
is of this form,
hence in particular that any automorphism of
M_{0}(G)
is continuous
for the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-compact open topology.
In other terms, we can formulate the following statement,
which is the alleged Theorem I in
[
Harp–88
]
:

Conjectural Theorem.

With the notation above, the group of all abstract group automorphisms of
M_{0}(G)

coincides with the group
M(\rm Aut(G))\rtimes\mathcal{D}(X)
.

In Proposition 3.4.2 of
[

]

Pressley and Segal claim that the group

is the group of all
bicontinuous

of the topological group

1.
Proof of Proposition II when
G=\rm SU(2)

Note that the result of Section
1

for this particular case
G=\rm SU(2)

will be used in the proof of the result for the general case,
see the proof of Lemma
15
in Section
2
.

We adopt the following notation.
The multiplicative group of complex numbers of modulus
1
is denoted by
\mathbf{U}
.
The algebra of linear endomorphisms of
\mathbf{C}^{2}

is the algebra
\rm M_{2}(\mathbf{C})
of 2-by-2 matrices with complex coefficients;

x^{*}
is the conjugate transpose of a matrix
x\in\rm M_{2}(\mathbf{C})
,
and
\rm id_{2}
is the unit matrix.
The space of orthogonal projections from
\mathbf{C}^{2}
onto lines is the projective line

{\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}\,=\,\{p\in\rm M_{2}(\mathbf{C})\mid p^{*}=p=p^{2}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\rm trace(p)=1\}.

In this section, until Proposition
7
included,
we use
G
for the simple connected compact Lie group

\displaystyle\rm SU(2)

\displaystyle=\,\{g\in\rm M_{2}(\mathbf{C})\mid g^{*}g=\rm id_{2}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\det g=1\}

\displaystyle=\,\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\rho&\sigma\\
-\overline{\sigma}&\overline{\rho}\end{pmatrix}\in\rm M_{2}(\mathbf{C})\hskip 2.84544pt\bigg|\hskip 2.84544pt\rho,\sigma\in\mathbf{C},\hskip 5.69046pt|\rho|^{2}+|\sigma|^{2}=1\right\}.

An element
g\in G
is
regular
if
g\notin\{\rm id_{2},-\rm id_{2}\}
;
we denote by
G_{\rm reg}
the open subset of
G
of regular elements.

Any
g\in G_{\rm reg}
has two eigenvalues
z_{g},\overline{z_{g}}\in\mathbf{U}
;
the notation is such that
{\Im(z_{g})>0}
.
Set

A:=\mathopen{]}0,\pi\mathclose{[}.

Let
s_{g}\in A
be the number such that
z_{g}=\exp(is_{g})
.
Let
p_{g}\in{\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}
be the projection of
\mathbf{C}^{2}
onto the eigenspace
\ker(z_{g}\rm id_{2}-g)

and
p^{\prime}_{G}\in{\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}
the projection of
\mathbf{C}^{2}
onto
\ker(\overline{z_{g}}\rm id_{2}-g)
.
We have

g\,=\,\exp(is_{g})p_{g}+\exp(-is_{g})p^{\prime}_{g}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for all}\hskip 5.69046ptg\in G_{\rm reg}.

The group
G
acts by conjugation on both
G_{\rm reg}
and
{\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}
,
trivially on
A
, and by the product action on
{\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}\times A
.

Lemma 1
.

The map

\psi_{A}\,\colon\left\{\begin{aligned} G_{\rm reg}&\to{\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}\times A\\
g\hskip 5.69046pt&\mapsto(p_{g},s_{g})\end{aligned}\right.

is a smooth diffeomorphism,
and it is equivariant for the action of
G
.

Proof.

Let
h\in G_{\rm reg}
.
Let
D_{r}
be a closed disc of centre
\exp(is_{h})
and some radius
r>0
,
contained in the half-plane
\{z\in\mathbf{C}\mid\Im z>0\}
,
and let
\Gamma_{r}
be the circle
\partial D_{r}
, with the positive orientation.
Since eigenvalues depend continuously on matrices,
there exists a neighbourhood
\mathcal{V}
of
h
in
G_{\rm reg}

such that
\exp(is_{g})
is in the interior of
D_{r}
for all
g\in\mathcal{V}
.
For
g\in\mathcal{V}
, we have the explicit formula

p_{g}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{r}}(z-g)^{-1}dz

by holomorphic functional calculus.
It follows that
p_{g}
depends smoothly on
g
.
We have also

[formula]

s_{g}\,=\,\arccos\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rm trace(g)\Big),

hence
s_{g}
depends smoothly on
g
.

For the continuity of eigenvalues and for holomorphic functional calculus,
see for example

[
DuSc–58
, Chapter VII, § 3, no. 19, Corollary 21]
,
or
[
BoTS
, Chap. 1, § 4, no 11, Proposition 16]
,
or

[
Serr–10
, 5.2.3 & 5.5]
,
or
[
Texi–18
]
.

The equivariance of
\psi_{A}
is straightforward.
∎

Lemma 2
.

There exists a smooth map

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{U}&\to G\\
u&\mapsto g_{u}\end{aligned}\right.

such that
g_{u}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}g_{u}^{-1}=u

for all

.

Proof.

Given a rank one projection

\overline{b}&1-a\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{U}

set
\rho=\sqrt{a}
,
\sigma=-b/\rho

g_{u}=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\rho&\sigma\\

Then

g_{u}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

by a simple computation.

Remark 3
.

The diffeomorphism of Lemma
1

and the map of Lemma
2

are not only smooth, they are indeed real analytic.
Similarly, the map of Lemma
10
below is real analytic.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a neighbourhood of the origin in
\mathbf{R}^{n}

and let
\gamma\,\colon\mathcal{O}\to G

be a smooth map with values in

.
Denote by
\underline{\gamma}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}
the germ defined by
\gamma
.
Then

depends smoothly on

, by Lemma

,
and defines at the origin a germ
\underline{s}_{\underline{\gamma}}

of
A
-valued smooth map.
Similarly
p_{\gamma(x)}
defines at the origin a germ
\underline{p}_{\underline{\gamma}}

of
{\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}
-valued smooth map.
We write simply
\underline{s}
and
\underline{p}

when the reference to
\underline{\gamma}
is clear.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\underline{\gamma}
and
\underline{\delta}

be two germs in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}

such that
\underline{\gamma}(O),\underline{\delta}(O)\in G_{\rm reg}
.
Let
(\underline{p},\underline{s})
and
(\underline{q},\underline{t})

be the associated germs at
O
of
({\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}\times A)
-valued smooth maps.

Then
\underline{\gamma}
and
\underline{\delta}

are conjugate in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}

if and only if
\underline{s}=\underline{t}
.

Proof.

Consider
\underline{\zeta}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}
.
We may choose representatives
\gamma,p,s,\delta,q,t,\zeta
of

\underline{\gamma},\underline{p},\underline{s},\underline{\delta},\underline{q},\underline{t},\underline{\zeta}

defined in a common neighbourhood
\mathcal{O}
of the origin in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
and such that
\gamma(x),\delta(x)\in G_{\rm reg}
for all
x\in\mathcal{O}
.

Suppose first that

\underline{\zeta}\hskip 2.84544pt\underline{\gamma}\hskip 2.84544pt\underline{\zeta}^{-1}=\underline{\delta}
.
Upon replacing
\mathcal{O}
by a smaller neighbourhood,
we may assume that
\zeta(x)\gamma(x)\zeta(x)^{-1}=\delta(x)

for all
x\in\mathcal{O}
,
hence that
s(x)=t(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{O}
.
It follows that
\underline{s}=\underline{t}
.

Suppose now that
\underline{s}=\underline{t}
.
We may again assume that

for all
x\in\mathcal{O}
.

Suppose first that, moreover, the range of

and the range of
q(O)

are not orthogonal in
\mathbf{C}^{2}
.
In appropriate orthogonal coordinates:

p(O)\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 8.5359ptq(O)\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}c&d\\
\overline{d}&1-c\end{pmatrix}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{with}\hskip 8.5359ptc>0,\hskip 2.84544ptd\in\mathbf{C},\hskip 2.84544ptc^{2}+|d|^{2}=c.

Upon shrinking
\mathcal{O}
once more if necessary,
we may assume that

and

are of the form

\displaystyle p(x)

\displaystyle=\,\begin{pmatrix}a(x)&b(x)\\
\overline{b(x)}&1-a(x)\end{pmatrix}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{where}\hskip 5.69046pta(x)>0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all}\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\mathcal{O},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{with}\hskip 5.69046pta(O)=1,

\displaystyle=\,\begin{pmatrix}c(x)&d(x)\\
\overline{d(x)}&1-c(x)\end{pmatrix}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{where}\hskip 5.69046ptc(x)>0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all}\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\mathcal{O},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{with}\hskip 5.69046ptc(O)=c.

, there exists a smooth map
\zeta\,\colon\mathcal{O}\to G

such that
\zeta(x)p(x)\zeta(x)^{-1}=q(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{O}
.
Since
s=t
, it follows that
\zeta\gamma\zeta^{-1}=\delta
, hence

\underline{\zeta}\hskip 2.84544pt\underline{\gamma}\hskip 2.84544pt\underline{\zeta}^{-1}=\underline{\delta}

Suppose now that the range of

and the range of

are orthogonal in
{\rm P}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}

Define a third germ
\underline{\eta}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n,G}

with eigenvalue germ equal to
\underline{s}
(hence also equal to
\underline{t}
),

\underline{\eta}(O)

\underline{\gamma}(O)
nor to
\underline{\delta}(O)
.
The previous argument shows that
\underline{\gamma}
and
\underline{\delta}

are both conjugate to
\underline{\eta}

hence are conjugate one to the other.

Lemma 5

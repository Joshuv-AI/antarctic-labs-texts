Introduction

All groups in this paper are implicitly assumed to be finite. We use standard notation and terminology, see for example
[
10
]
or
[
11
]
. Throughout,
p
denotes an arbitrary but fixed prime.

Recall that a group
G
is said to be
p
-nilpotent
if
G
has a normal Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup. This concept plays an important role in local finite group theory, and many criteria for
p
-nilpotence of finite groups can be found in the literature. For example, a well-known theorem of Burnside asserts that a group
G
with Sylow
p
-subgroup

is
p
-nilpotent if
P\leqslant Z(N_{G}(P))
, or equivalently if
P
is abelian and
N_{G}(P)
is
p
-nilpotent (see
[
11
, Theorem 5.13]
). This result has been generalized in many directions, and we shall now consider some of these generalizations.

First let us introduce some notation. Let
P
be a
p
-group and

be a positive integer. Then the subgroup of

generated by all elements

of
P
with

is denoted by
\Omega_{i}(P)
. We set
\Omega(P):=\Omega_{1}(P)
if

is odd and

if

In 1974, Laffey
[

published the following generalization of Burnside’s

-nilpotency criterion: If

is a group and

is a Sylow

-subgroup of
G
, then

is
p
-nilpotent if
\Omega(P)\leqslant Z(P)
and

is
p
-nilpotent (see
[
12
, p. 136]
).

In 2000, Ballester-Bolinches and Guo
[
3
]
published the following more general result: If
G
is a group and

is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, then
G
is
p
-nilpotent if
\Omega(P\cap G^{\prime})\leqslant Z(N_{G}(P))
(see
[
3
, Theorem 1]
). Moreover, they proved that a group

with Sylow
2
-subgroup

is

-nilpotent if
\Omega_{1}(P\cap G^{\prime})\leqslant Z(P)
,

is quaternion-free and

is
2
-nilpotent (see
[
3
, Theorem 2]

8
.

For a non-empty formation
\mathfrak{F}
and a group

to denote the

-residual
of

In 2004, Asaad
[

published the following result, which extends the above-mentioned results of Ballester-Bolinches and Guo.

Theorem 1
.

(
[
1

\Leftrightarrow
(b)]
)
Let

be a Sylow

-subgroup of a group

(1)

is
p
-nilpotent.

(2)

is
p
-nilpotent and
\Omega_{1}(G^{\mathfrak{N}}\cap P\cap P^{x})\leqslant Z(P)
for all

Recall that a group

is said to be
p
-solvable
if any chief factor of

is either a
p
-group or a
p^{\prime}
-group. A

-solvable group

is called

-supersolvable
if every

-chief factor of

has order

-supersolvable groups is denoted by

-nilpotent group is

-supersolvable. In particular, we have

for any group

By

is a Sylow

-subgroup of a group

denotes the formation of all

-nilpotent groups. Therefore, the nilpotent residual

can be replaced by the

-nilpotent residual

in Theorem

still remains true when the nilpotent residual

is replaced by the

-supersolvable residual

cannot only be replaced by

, but that it is enough to require in (2) that the normalizer

is

-nilpotent and that every minimal subgroup of

is central in

or complemented in

remains true if we additionally replace ”

-nilpotent” by ”

-supersolvable” in both statements of the theorem and furthermore assume

-solvable.

In fact, our results are slightly more general than just stated, and they also deal with the case that

is not quaternion-free when

Definition 2

Let

. Then

-embedded

Having introduced the concept of

Theorem A
.

Let

-subgroup of a

is

-supersolvable if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

is

-supersolvable.

(2)

is

-embedded in

The

-embedded in

Lemma 3

Let

-solvable minimal non-

Lemma 4
.

Let

be a

-solvable minimal non-

-supersolvable group,

be a proper subgroup of

[
Proof]Assume for the sake of contradiction that

.

We have

. By Lemma

G^{\mathfrak{U}_{p}}
is a

.

Abstract
. We address the maximum size of binary codes and binary constant weight codes with few distances. Previous works established a number of bounds for these quantities as well as the exact values for a range of small code lengths. As our main results, we determine the exact size of maximal binary codes with two distances for all lengths n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 as well as the exact size of maximal binary constant weight codes with 2,3, and 4 distances for several values of the weight and for all but small lengths.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a metric space with metric
d
that defines the distance between any pair of points in
M
.
For a finite set
X\subset M
, the set of distinct distances in
X
, defined as
\mathscr{D}(X)=\{d(x,y):x\neq y\in X\},

is called a
distance set
of
X
.
We say that
X
is an
s
-distance set
in
M
if
|\mathscr{D}(X)|=s
.
For instance, the eight vertices of a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
form a three-distance set.

Denote by
A(M,s)
the maximum cardinality of an
s
-distance set in
M
.
If
\mathscr{D}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\}
is a set of allowed distances, then we may also use a more detailed notation
A(M,\mathscr{D}).
When the distances are specified explicitly, e.g.,
\mathscr{D}=\{a,b\},
we also write
A(M,\{a,b\})
for the maximum size of s-distance sets in the space
M
with distances
a
and
b
.

The maximum size of
s
-distance sets has been studied for a range of metric spaces. The first to study it were Einhorn and Schoenberg
[
ES66a
]
,
[
ES66b
]
who addressed the case of the

n
-dimensional Euclidean space
M={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. This problem is still far from being resolved. As an example, it was proved
only recently that the only maximum three-distance set in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is formed by the 12 vertices of a regular icosahedron, and thus
A(\mathbb{R}^{3},3)=12
; similarly
A(\mathbb{R}^{3},5)=20,
attained by the vertex set of a dodecahedron (see
[
NS21
]
, which also contains other examples).

The problem of bounding the maximum cardinality of
s
-distance sets has been actively studied for the case of
M=S^{n-1}({\mathbb{R}}),
the
unit sphere in the
n
-dimensional real space. Delsarte, Goethals, and Seidel
[
DGS77
]
proved a general upper estimate
on the size of spherical
s
-distance sets called the
harmonic bound
. Their results were improved in a series of recent papers

[
Mus09
,
Noz11
,
BM11
,
MN11
,
BY13b
,
GY18
,
JTY+20
,
LY20
]
and other works that focused in particular on the case of
s=2,3,4
. The case of two-distance
spherical codes is the most studied one. Apart from being of interest in their own right, spherical 2-distance
sets exhibit deep connections with a number of well-known objects in algebraic combinatorics, notably strongly regular graphs, tight frames, and spherical designs
[
DGS77
,
BB05
,
BGOY15
]
. Recently the problem of bounding the maximum size of such codes was resolved for most values of the dimension
n

[
GY18
]
. Bounds obtained in this paper form a starting point of one of our theorems, and we provide more details on it in the main text below. Further narrowing down the problem, many papers
have addressed the maximum size of spherical 2-distance sets in which every pair of vectors have inner product
\alpha
or
-\alpha
for some value
\alpha\in(0,1),
also known as sets of equiangular lines. This problem also enjoys long history in the literature with a
number of links in algebraic combinatorics, and we refer the reader to
[
BY13a
,
GY18
,
BDKS18
,
JTY+21
]
for recent advances and a general overview.

The maximum cardinality problem for
s
-distance sets has also been studied for metric spaces commonly considered in coding theory,
starting with the Hamming space
\mathcal{H}_{q}^{n}
of
n
-dimensional vectors over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.
Linear two-distance codes in particular
form a classic object in coding theory because of their links to finite geometries, see for instance an early survey
[
CK86
]
as well as recent works
[
SHS+21
]
;
[
BM22
, Ch.7]
. Linear codes with few weights are also often
constructed in the framework of cyclic codes and Boolean functions, in particular, bent functions
[
Mes16
]
.
This line of research is a subject of vast literature, starting with classical families such as duals of 2-error-correcting BCH codes, Kerdock codes and their relatives
[
MS77
]
and many other families. Some recent additions to the literature are
[
WZHZ15
,
Din16
,
LLHQ21
]
(the reader is cautioned that this sample is far from complete in many respects).

At the same time, most of the code families studied in the context of coding theory limit not just on the number of distances but also
aim at constructing codes with large minimum distance. As a result, the codes thus obtained are of cardinality that is much smaller than
the maximum possible if the latter constraint is removed. It is this problem that we seek to resolve in this work: what is the maximum size
of a binary code with two distances (linear or not) when the minimum separation between distinct codewords is unrestricted?
In some cases, notably for binary codes with two distances we give a complete answer, where previously the exact values were
known only for a range of small code lengths
n
.

In addition to
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}
we study codes in the binary Johnson space
\mathcal{J}^{n,w},
formed of all binary vectors of length
n
and Hamming weight
w
such that
2w\leq n
. For two vectors
x,y\in\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
we define the
Johnson distance

d_{J}(x,y)=(1/2)d_{H}(x,y),
where
d_{H}(x,y)=|\{i:x_{i}\neq y_{i}\}|
is the Hamming distance. The problem of determining the maximum size of codes in
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
with few distances recently was the subject of several papers in coding theory literature
[
BM11
,
MN11
]
. This problem can also be phrased in different terms that highlight connections in extremal combinatorics. A code
C\subset\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
can be thought of as a family of
w
-subsets of an
n
-set, and
|x\cap y|=w-d_{J}(x,y),
so disallowing some values of the distance is tantamount to forbidding some intersections of the subsets in the family. For instance, the celebrated Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem
[
EKR61
]
;
[
FT18
,
GM16
]
is a statement about the maximum size of a code in
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
with
\mathscr{D}=\{1,2,\dots,w-t\},
where
t\geq 1
is some fixed number. Generally, the problem of estimating the maximum size of families of finite sets with a prescribed set of intersections has been the subject of a long line of works in combinatorics; we refer to recent overviews in
[
FT18
,
Ell22
]
.

Following the convention of coding theory, we call an
s
-distance set in the Hamming and Johnson spaces an
s
-code
. The maximum cardinality of an
s
-code can be bounded above using Delsarte’s linear programming (LP) method
[
Del73a
]
; however this approach
typically does not yield closed-form results. The same applies to Schrijver’s semidefinite programming (SDP) method
[
Sch05
,
GMS12
]
which represents a
far-reaching extension of the LP bound. Delsarte
[
Del73a
,
Del73b
]
also proved a general upper bound on the size of
s
-codes, called the
harmonic bound
; see also the paper
[
NS10
]
for a recent improvement. Relying on the harmonic bound, the authors of
[
BM11
]
determined the maximum cardinality
A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},2)
and
A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},2)
in a number of cases, and paper
[
MN11
]
extended their results to
s=3
and
4
.

Despite the described advances, the exact values of
A(M,s)
for
M=\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}
or
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
were known only for a limited range
of values of
n
. For instance, it was shown in
[
BM11
]
that
A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},2)=1+\binom{n}{2}
for
6\leq n\leq 78
with a number of
exceptions in that range left unresolved, and
[
MN11
]
showed that
A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},3)=n+\binom{n}{3}
for
8\leq n\leq 37
and
n=44
,
with the exception of
n=23,34,35
(while
n=23
constitutes a true exception due to the fact that the dual Golay code
{\mathcal{G}}_{23}^{\bot}
yields
A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{23},3)\geq 2048
, the other two values are not as we will observe below). As one of our main results we prove

Theorem 1.1
.

A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},2)=1+\binom{n}{2}
for all
n\geq 6.

The idea of the proof is to embed a binary code into a sphere
S^{n-1}
and relate its size to spherical two-distance sets and
spherical equiangular sets, relying on bounds for them proved recently in
[
GY18
]
.

.

2.
The harmonic bound, its improvements and implications

2.1.
The Hamming space

Recall that the set of polynomials

(1)

\phi_{k}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{x}{j}\binom{n-x}{k-j},k=0,1,\dots,n

called the
binary Krawtchouk polynomials
, forms an orthogonal basis in the space of real functions on
\{0,1,\dots,n\}
with weight
\binom{n}{i},i=0,\dots,n

[
Del73a
]
,
[
MS77
, Ch.5]
. Any such function has a unique expansion as a sum of Krawtchouk polynomials, namely
f(x)=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}\phi_{i}(x),
where
f_{i}=2^{-n}\sum_{j\geq 0}f(j)\phi_{i}(j)\binom{n}{j}.

Delsarte
[
Del73a
,
Del73b
]
proved an upper bound on
A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},s)
, called the
harmonic bound

(it is derived relying on the dimension of the space of spherical harmonics).
In the next theorem we quote this bound accounting for an improvement found in
[
NS10
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
NS10
]

Let
C\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}
be an
s
-code with distances
\mathscr{D}=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
. Consider the polynomial
f(t)=\prod_{i}\frac{d_{i}-t}{d_{i}}=\sum_{k=0}^{s}f_{k}\phi_{k}(t)
. Then

A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},\mathscr{D})\leq\sum_{k:f_{k}>0}\binom{n}{k}.

In
[
NS10
]
this theorem is proved for spherical codes, but it can be extended to other polynomial metric spaces including

\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}
and
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
using standard tools.

A concrete form of Theorem
2.1
under the condition that the sum of
the code’s distances does not exceed
\frac{1}{2}sn
is as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
BM11
, Theorem
11
]

Suppose that
\mathscr{D}=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
is such that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}d_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}sn.
Then

A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},\mathscr{D})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s-2}\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n}{s}.

This inequality is proved by showing that the assumption
\sum_{i=1}^{s}d_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}sn

forces the coefficient
f_{s-1}
of the Krawtchouk expansion to be nonpositive, and thus the term
\binom{n}{s-1}

is missing from the sum.

A lower bound on
A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},s)
follows by an easy construction of
s
-codes in
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}
which sometimes
enables one to prove tight results.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
MN11
, Eq.
(3.1)
]

For
2s\leq n
, we have

A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},s)\geq\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{s-2i}.

Proof.

For
2s\leq n
, the set
C
of all binary vectors of Hamming weight
k\leq s
such that

k=s\penalty\ (\text{mod}\,2)
is an
s
-code with distance set
\mathscr{D}(C)=\{2,4,\dots,2s\}
.
∎

2.2.
The Johnson space

The results in the previous section have their analogs for the
Johnson space
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}.

In this case the relevant family of orthogonal polynomials are the
Hahn polynomials,
defined as

(2)

\psi_{k}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\frac{\binom{k}{j}\binom{n+1-k}{j}}{\binom{w}{j}\binom{n-w}{j}}\binom{x}{j},\quad k=0,1,\dots,w.

The Hahn polynomials form an orthogonal basis in the function space
f:\{0,1,\dots,w\}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with weight

\binom{w}{i}\binom{n-w}{i},i=0,1,\dots,w.

The (improved) harmonic bound for the Johnson space has the following form.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
NS10
]

Let
C\subset\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
be a constant weight
s
-code with distances
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
. Consider the polynomial
f(t)=\prod_{i}\frac{d_{i}-t}{d_{i}}
. Suppose that the Hahn expansion of
f(t)
is
f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{s}f_{k}\psi_{k}(t)
. Then

|C|\leq\sum_{k:f_{k}>0}\binom{n}{k-1}\frac{n-2k+1}{k}.

As remarked, this result appears in
[
NS10
]
for the case of spherical codes. Starting with this theorem, one can prove
the following upper bound on
s
-codes in the Johnson space.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
BM11
, Theorem
8
]

Let
C\subset\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
be an
s
-code with distances
\mathscr{D}=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
. Suppose that

\sum_{i=1}^{s}(w-d_{i})\geq\frac{s(w^{2}-(s-1)(2w-\frac{n}{2}))}{n-2(s-1)}.

Then

|C|\leq\binom{n}{s}-\binom{n}{s-1}\frac{n-2s+3}{n-s+2}.

Similarly to Proposition
2.3
, there is a general construction of
s
-codes in the Johnson space that implies a lower bound on
A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},s)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
EKR61
]

For
s\leq n-w
we have

A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},s)\geq\binom{n-w+s}{s}.

Proof.

For
s\leq n-w
, consider the set of binary vectors in
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
with ones in the first
w-s
coordinates.
Clearly, it forms an
s
-code of size
\binom{n-w+s}{s}
with distances
\{1,\dots,s\}
.
∎

3.
The maximum size of
2
-codes in the Hamming space: A proof of Theorem
1.1

We will prove Theorem
1.1
by reduction to spherical codes.
Let
C\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}
be a binary code of length
n.
Our plan is to map
C
on the sphere
S^{n-1}
and view the result as a spherical
2
-distance code.
For that let
f:\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}\to S^{n-1}
be a map given by
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto f(x)=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
, where
v_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(-1)^{x_{i}}
for all
i
. Note that if
d_{H}(x,y)=d
then for the Euclidean inner product we obtain

\langle f(x),f(y)\rangle=1-\frac{2d}{n}
. Thus, if
C
is a binary 2-code with distances
a
and
b
, the set
f(C)
forms
a spherical
2
-code with inner products
\alpha=1-\frac{2a}{n}
,
\beta=1-\frac{2b}{n}
.

In the following subsection we recall some known bounds for equiangular lines. The proof itself is formed of two parts presented in Sections
3.1
,
3.2
. The first part reformulates the problem for spherical codes,
connects it to families of equiangular lines, and then narrows down the possible parameters by using the known results about them. This leaves behind a set of exceptional values of the distances, which we handle in the second part.

3.1.
Bounds from spherical equiangular sets

We will need a few known results concerning equiangular line sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.
Any such set is a finite collection of points
on
S^{n-1}
with pairwise inner products
\{\alpha,-\alpha\}
,
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Let
M(n)
be the size of the largest possible equiangular line set in
n
dimensions and let
M_{\alpha}(n)
be the size of the largest equiangular set with inner products
\{\alpha,-\alpha\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

\displaystyle\hskip-14.45377pt(a)\;

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}(n)\leq 2n\text{ unless $1/\alpha$ is an odd integer}\text{ (Neumann, see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{LS73}{}{}]})};

\displaystyle(b)\;

\displaystyle M_{\frac{1}{3}}(n)=28\text{ for }7\leq n\leq 15,\text{ and }M_{\frac{1}{3}}(n)=2n-2\text{ for }n\geq 15.\text{ (\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{LS73}{}{}]})};

\displaystyle(c)\;

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}(n)\leq\frac{n(1-\alpha^{2})}{1-n\alpha^{2}}\text{ for all $\alpha$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $n\alpha^{2}<1$}\text{ (Relative bound, \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{LS73}{}{}]})};

\displaystyle(d)\;

\displaystyle M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)\leq\frac{(a^{2}-2)(a^{2}-1)}{2}\text{ for all $n$ and $a$ such that $n\leq 3a^{2}-16$ and $a\geq 3$ }\text{(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Yu17}{}{}]})};

\displaystyle(e)\;

\displaystyle M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)\leq n\left(\frac{2}{3}a^{2}+\frac{4}{7}\right)+2\text{ for all $a\geq 3$ and for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$ (\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{GY18}{}{}, Theorem 3]}).}

Parts (a)-(c) of this theorem form classical results while parts (d) and (e) represent recent results on bounds for equiangular lines.

Lemma 3.2
.

M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)\leq\binom{n-1}{2}
for
n\geq 7
unless
a
is an odd integer and
n\in\{a^{2}-1,a^{2}-2\}
.

Proof.

The case-by-case proof below essentially follows the proof of
[
GY18
, Theorem 2]
.

(1)

If
a
is not an odd integer, by Theorem
3.1
(a),
M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)\leq 2n\leq\binom{n-1}{2}
for all
n\geq 7
. Therefore,
below we
will assume that
a
is an odd integer.

(2)

Let
a^{2}\leq n\leq 3a^{2}-16.
By Theorem
3.1
(d),

M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)\leq\frac{(a^{2}-1)(a^{2}-2)}{2}\leq\binom{n-1}{2}.

(3)

For
n\leq a^{2}-3
, by Theorem
3.1
(c),

M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)\leq\frac{n(a^{2}-1)}{a^{2}-n}=n+\frac{n^{2}-n}{a^{2}-n}\leq n+\frac{n^{2}-n}{3}\leq\binom{n-1}{2}

for all
n\geq 13
. If
7\leq n\leq 12
, then
a\geq 5
(recall that
a
is odd) and, again using Theorem
3.1
(c), we obtain

M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)\leq n+\frac{n^{2}-n}{a^{2}-n}\leq n+\frac{n^{2}-n}{13}\leq\binom{n-1}{2}.

(4)

For the last remaining case, consider
n\geq 3a^{2}-15
. By Theorem
3.1
(e),

M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)\leq n\left(\frac{2}{3}a^{2}+\frac{4}{7}\right)+2\leq n\left(\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{n+15}{3}+\frac{4}{7}\right)+2\leq\binom{n-1}{2}

for all
n\geq 20
. For
12\leq n\leq 19
we have
a=3
, and by Theorem
3.1
(b)

M_{\frac{1}{a}}(n)=28\leq\binom{n-1}{2}.

∎

Recall that
A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},\{a,b\})
is the maximum size of
2
-codes in the Hamming space with distance set
\{a,b\}
and
a>b
, and let
g(n,\alpha,\beta)
be the maximum size of a spherical
2
-code with inner products
\alpha,\beta\in[-1,1)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
-1\leq\alpha<\beta<1
and
\alpha+\beta<0
. Let
\gamma=\frac{2-(\alpha+\beta)}{\beta-\alpha}
. Then for
n\geq 6
,

g(n,\alpha,\beta)\leq\binom{n}{2}

unless
\gamma
is an odd integer and
n\in\{\gamma^{2}-2,\gamma^{2}-3\}
.

Proof.

Consider a
2
-code
C=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}\subset S^{n-1}
with inner products
\alpha,\beta
. Let
t=\sqrt{\frac{2}{2-(\alpha+\beta)}}
and let
y\in S^{n}
be the unit vector orthogonal to all
x_{i}\in C
. Then the set

\{tx_{i}+\sqrt{1-t^{2}}y:1\leq i\leq N\}

is an equiangular set in
S^{n}
with inner product
\frac{1}{\gamma}=\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2-(\alpha+\beta)}
. Thus,

g(n,\alpha,\beta)\leq M_{\frac{1}{\gamma}}(n+1)\leq\binom{n}{2}

unless
\gamma
is one of the exceptional values in the statement of the Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

For
n\geq 6
,

A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},\{a,b\})\leq 1+\binom{n}{2}

unless there is a positive integer
m
such that
a=(m+1)(2m+1)
,
b=m(2m+1)
and
n\in\{(2m+1)^{2}-2,(2m+1)^{2}-3\}
.

Proof.

If
a+b\leq n
, by Theorem
2.2
,
A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},a,b)\leq 1+\binom{n}{2}
. Now we assume
a+b>n
and consider the corresponding spherical
2
-code with scalar products
\alpha=1-\frac{2a}{n}
,
\beta=1-\frac{2b}{n}
. Then
\alpha+\beta<0
. By Lemma
3.3
,

A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},a,b)\leq g(n,\alpha,\beta)\leq\binom{n}{2}

unless
\gamma=\frac{2-(\alpha+\beta)}{\beta-\alpha}
is an odd integer and
n\in\{\gamma^{2}-2,\gamma^{2}-3\}
. Write
\gamma=2m+1
for some integer
m
and observe that

\gamma=\frac{a+b}{a-b}=2m+1

implies
\frac{a}{b}=\frac{m+1}{m}
. Denoting
k=\text{gcd}(a,b)
, we get
a=(m+1)k
and
b=mk
.

By a result of Rankin
[
Ran55
]
, the size of a spherical code whose scalar products are all negative cannot be larger than
n+1
. Since
n+1\leq\binom{n}{2}
, it is sufficient to consider the case when
\beta\geq 0
, that is, when
b\leq n/2
. Note that
\alpha+\beta<0
so
\alpha
must be negative and
a>n/2
. Using
n\in\{(2m+1)^{2}-2,(2m+1)^{2}-3\}
, we see that
b\leq 2m^{2}+2m-1
and
a\geq 2m^{2}+2m
. Since
b=mk
,
k<2m+2
. Since
a=(m+1)k
,
k\geq 2m
. The only possible choices are
k=2m
and
k=2m+1
.

Note that
\alpha+\beta<0
is equivalent to
a+b>n
. If
k=2m
,
a+b=2m(m+1)+2m^{2}=4m^{2}+2m\leq(2m+1)^{2}-3\leq n
so this condition is not satisfied. Therefore, the only remaining choice for
k
is
2m+1
and
a=(m+1)(2m+1)
,
b=m(2m+1)
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

[
GY18

Let

if the right-hand side is positive.

Lemma 3.6
.

For all

,

A(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},\{a,b\})\leq\binom{n}{2},

and

Using the same mapping to the sphere as above, we consider spherical

4.1.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
C\subset\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
be an
s
-code with distance set
\mathscr{D}=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
, where
w\geq s+1
and

(3)

From (

\frac{sw(n-2(s-1))-s(w^{2}-(s-1)(2w-\frac{n}{2}))}{n-2(s-1)}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}(w-i)-1.

\sum_{i=1}^{s}d_{i}\leq\frac{sw(n-2(s-1))-s(w^{2}-(s-1)(2w-\frac{n}{2})))}{n-2(s-1)},

]

implies that

∎

.

(4)

\displaystyle A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},2)

\displaystyle A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},3)

4.2.
EKR and forbidden intersections

As already mentioned, constant weight codes with a specified set of distances can be equivalently described as families of
finite sets with restricted intersections. Switching to this language, in this subsection we prove one simple corollary of the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem that implies bounds for
s
-codes with
w=s+1.
We will use the following notation.
A family
\mathcal{F}
of
w
-subsets of
[n]
is called
intersecting
if
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\neq\emptyset
and it is called
t
-intersecting
if
|F_{1}\cap F_{2}|\geq t
for any
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}.
Let
m(n,w,t)
be the maximum size of a
t
-intersecting family.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
EKR61
]

For
1\leq t<w
and
n\geq n_{0}(w,t)

(10)

m(n,w,t)=\binom{n-t}{w-t}.

If
t=1
and
w\leq n/2,
then
m(n,w)\leq\binom{n-1}{w-1}.
If
w<n/2
then equality holds if and only if there is an
i\in[n]
such that

\mathcal{F}=\{F\subset\binom{[n]}{w}:i\in F\}.

Clearly the family
\mathcal{F}=\{F\in\binom{[n]}{w}:\{1,2,\dots,t\}\subset F\}
has cardinality
\binom{n-t}{w-t}
and therefore is
optimal for
n\geq n_{0}(w,t)
. The value of
n_{0}(w,t)
was found in a series of papers culminating with Wilson’s theorem
[
Wil84
]

stating that for all
t

n_{0}(w,t)=(w-t+1)(t+1).

Rephrasing this for s-codes in
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
, the largest size of a code with distances
\mathscr{D}=\{1,2,\dots,s=w-t\}
is

(11)

A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},\mathscr{D})=\binom{n-w+s}{s}\text{ for all }s\leq w-1,\;n\geq(s+1)(w-s+1).

We will now prove a related result for
s=w-1
(any single missing distance) through a connection with families with forbidden intersections. Denote by
m(n,w,\bar{l})
the maximum size of a family
\mathcal{F}
of
w
-subsets of
[n]
such that
|F\cap F^{\prime}|\neq l
for
any
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
.
The well-known Frankl-Füredi theorem
[
FF85
]
asserts that for
w\geq 2l+2
and sufficiently large
n
,

m(n,w,\bar{l})\leq\binom{n-l-1}{w-l-1}.

We first establish a simple (weaker) upper bound for
m(n,w,\bar{l})
that applies for all
n\geq 2w-l

1
1

1

In all likelihood, it is available in the literature, however we could not find an immediate reference.
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
1\leq l\leq w-1
and
n\geq 2w-l,
then
m(n,w,\bar{l})\leq\frac{w-l}{n-l}\binom{n}{w}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
w
-subsets of
[n]
no two of which intersect on
l
elements. Every set
F\in\mathcal{F}
contains
\binom{w}{l}

l
-subsets, so the total number of
l
-subsets in all sets
F
is
|\mathcal{F}|\binom{w}{l}.
Thus on average every
l
-subset is contained
in
E:=\frac{|\mathcal{F}|\binom{w}{l}}{\binom{n}{l}}
sets
F
. Now fix an
l
-subset
S
that is contained in at least
E
subsets of
\mathcal{F}
. By
\mathcal{F}^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
denote the set of all subsets in
\mathcal{F}
containing
S
. For any
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}
,
|F\cap F^{\prime}|\neq l
and thus

(F\backslash S)\cap(F^{\prime}\backslash S)\neq\emptyset.
This means that the collection

\{F\backslash S:F\in\mathcal{F}^{*}\}
forms an intersecting family in
[n]\backslash S
, and so

E\leq\binom{n-l-1}{w-l-1}
by the EKR theorem. The last inequality implies that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leq\frac{\binom{n}{l}}{\binom{w}{l}}\binom{n-l-1}{w-l-1}=\frac{1}{\binom{w}{l}}\frac{w-l}{n-l}\binom{n}{l}\binom{n-l}{w-l}=\frac{w-l}{n-l}\binom{n}{w}.

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},w-1)=\binom{n-1}{w-1}
for
n\geq 2w
.

Proof.

We have

A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},w-1)\leq\max_{l}\frac{w-l}{n-l}\binom{n}{w}\leq\binom{n-1}{w-1}.

At the same time, a
(w-1)
-code all of whose vectors contain a fixed point is of size
\binom{n-1}{w-1},
hence the equality.
∎

-ary Hamming space,
q\geq 3
, was recently
resolved in
[
KLLM21

5.
Using the Delsarte inequalities: Bounds for small
w

5.1.
Preliminaries

A powerful tool to estimate the maximum size of a code is provided by Delsarte’s linear programming bound. We state this bound for

\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}
and
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
in the following theorem, adapting the statements to
s
-codes.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
Del73a
]

(a) Let
C\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n}
be an
s
-code with distances
\mathscr{D}=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
. Then
|C|
is bounded above
by the value of the linear program
\text{LP}_{H}(\mathscr{D})
given by

(12)

1+\sum_{j=1}^{s}f_{j}\to\max,

where
f_{j}\geq 0
for
j=1,\dots,s
and

(13)

\sum_{j=1}^{s}f_{j}\phi_{k}(d_{j})\geq-\binom{n}{k},\;k=0,\dots,n,

and
\phi_{k}
are the Krawtchouk polynomials (
1
).

(b) Let
C\subset\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
be a constant weight
s
-code with distances
\mathscr{D}=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
. Then

|C|
is bounded above by the value of linear program
\text{{LP}${}_{J}$}(\mathscr{D})
given by

(14)

1+\sum_{j=1}^{s}f_{j}\to\max,

where
f_{j}\geq 0
for
j=1,\dots,s
and

(15)

\sum_{j=1}^{s}f_{j}\psi_{k}(d_{j})\geq-1,\;k=0,1,\dots,w,

and
\psi_{k}
are the Hahn polynomials (
2
).

Relations (
13
) and (
15
) are called the

.

The linear programming bounds have been one of the main tools in estimating the value of
A(M,s)
for
M=\mathcal{H}_{2}^{n},\,\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
as well as for spherical codes. Direct application of these bounds is difficult because of the need to exhaust
all possible subsets
\mathscr{D}
; however it is possible to restrict the search by using the integrality
conditions quoted in the next theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

[
MN11
]

Let
C\subset\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
be an
s
-code with distances
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
. Let
N(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},s)=\binom{n}{s-1}
and

K_{i}=\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{d_{j}}{d_{j}-d_{i}}\quad\text{for $i=1,\dots,s$.}

If
|C|\geq 2N(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},s)
, then

K_{i}
is an integer and

|K_{i}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{N(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},s)^{2}}{2N(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},s)-2}+\frac{1}{4}}\right\rfloor\quad\text{for $i=1,\dots,s$.}

This statement forms an analog of a much earlier result for few-distance sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, namely the Larman-Rogers-Seidel theorem

[
LRS77
]
. A similar set of necessary conditions is also known for the Hamming space
[
MN11
]
.

5.2.
Bounds for

-codes with small weight

The following theorem is proved by considering a subset of inequalities in the LP bound. It allows us to calculate an upper bound of an

Theorem 5.3

\displaystyle 0

Corollary 5.4

5.3.
Upper bounds on
2
-codes for large
n

From the preceding results it is clear that for large
n
the size of maximum 2-codes in the Johnson space
\mathcal{J}^{n,w}
is proportional
to
n^{2},
namely
A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},2)\leq cn^{2}+o(n^{2}).
In this section we make the first term of the asymptotics more precise by
evaluating the behavior of the bound in Theorem
5.3
. As a result, we will compute the coefficient
c=c(w,d_{1},d_{2})
.

We need some asymptotic estimates for the Hahn polynomials. More precise results are abundant in the literature (e.g.,

[
LW13
]
); however the simple bound that stated below is easier to use to compute bounds on codes.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
k,x\in\{1,2,\dots,w\}.
As
n\to\infty
, the Hahn polynomial has the order
\psi_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\binom{w}{x}}(A+Bn^{-1}+Cn^{-2})+O(n^{-3})
, where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\binom{w-k}{x},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=-kx\binom{w-k+1}{x},

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=-kxw\binom{w-k+1}{x}+\frac{k(k-1)x(x-1)}{2}\binom{w-k+2}{x}.

The proof of this proposition appears in the end of this section.

Corollary 5.10
.

As
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)\sim\frac{\binom{w-k}{x}}{\binom{w}{x}}\text{ if }x\leq w-k

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)\sim\frac{-kx}{\binom{w}{x}}\cdot n^{-1}\text{ if }x=w-k+1

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)\sim\frac{k(k-1)x(x-1)}{2\binom{w}{x}}\cdot n^{-2}\text{ if }x=w-k+2

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)=O(n^{-3})\text{ if }x\geq w-k+3.

The main result of this section is stated next.

Theorem 5.11
.

As
n\to\infty
,
A(\mathcal{J}^{n,w},\{d_{1},d_{2}\})\leq c(w,d_{1},d_{2})n^{2}(1+o(1)),
where

(23)

c(w,d_{1},d_{2})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2\binom{w}{d_{2}}}{d_{1}d_{2}(w+1-d_{1})(w+1-d_{2})}&\text{if }d_{1}=d_{2}-1\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\binom{d_{2}-1}{d_{1}}\binom{w}{d_{2}}}{d_{1}d_{2}(w+1-d_{1})(w+1-d_{2})}&\text{if }d_{1}<d_{2}-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

First we analyze the case of
d_{1}+1=d_{2}.
We have
\psi_{k_{1}}(d_{1})\sim\frac{-k_{1}d_{1}}{\binom{w}{d_{1}}}\cdot n^{-1}
,
\psi_{k_{1}}(d_{2})\sim\frac{k_{1}k_{2}d_{1}d_{2}}{2\binom{w}{d_{2}}}\cdot n^{-2}
,
\psi_{k_{2}}(d_{1})\sim\frac{1}{\binom{w}{d_{1}}}
,
\psi_{k_{2}}(d_{2})\sim\frac{-k_{2}d_{2}}{\binom{w}{d_{2}}}\cdot n^{-1}
. For sufficiently large
n
, assumptions (
16
) of Theorem
5.3
holds. The order of the
2
by
2
determinant is

\begin{vmatrix}\psi_{k_{1}}(d_{1})&\psi_{k_{2}}(d_{1})\\
\psi_{k_{1}}(d_{2})&\psi_{k_{2}}(d_{2})\end{vmatrix}\sim\begin{vmatrix}\frac{-k_{1}d_{1}}{\binom{w}{d_{1}}}\cdot n^{-1}&\frac{1}{\binom{w}{d_{1}}}\\
\frac{k_{1}k_{2}d_{1}d_{2}}{2\binom{w}{d_{2}}}\cdot n^{-2}&\frac{-k_{2}d_{2}}{\binom{w}{d_{2}}}\cdot n^{-1}\end{vmatrix}=\frac{k_{1}k_{2}d_{1}d_{2}}{2\binom{w}{d_{1}}\binom{w}{d_{2}}}\cdot n^{-2},

which is positive for sufficiently large
n
, so we can apply the bound (
18
). The order of the
3
by
3
determinant is

\begin{vmatrix}1&1&1\\
1&\psi_{k_{1}}(d_{1})&\psi_{k_{2}}(d_{1})\\
1&\psi_{k_{1}}(d_{2})&\psi_{k_{2}}(d_{2})\end{vmatrix}\sim\begin{vmatrix}1&1&1\\
1&\Theta(n^{-1})&\frac{1}{\binom{w}{d_{1}}}\\
1&\Theta(n^{-2})&\Theta(n^{-1})\end{vmatrix}=\frac{1}{\binom{w}{d_{1}}}(1+o(1)),

so the order of the upper bound is
\frac{2\binom{w}{d_{2}}}{d_{1}d_{2}(w+1-d_{1})(w+1-d_{2})}\cdot n^{2}
.

Now suppose that
d_{1}+1<d_{2},
then the order of
\psi_{k_{1}}(d_{1})
and
\psi_{k_{2}}(d_{2})
are the same as above,
\psi_{k_{2}}(d_{1})\sim\frac{\binom{d_{2}-1}{d_{1}}}{\binom{w}{d_{1}}}
, and
\psi_{k_{1}}(d_{2})=O(n^{-3})
. Similar calculations show that assumptions (
16
) and (
17
) of Theorem
5.3
hold, and the bound (
18
) gives the order
\frac{\binom{d_{2}-1}{d_{1}}\binom{w}{d_{2}}}{d_{1}d_{2}(w+1-d_{1})(w+1-d_{2})}\cdot n^{2}
.
∎

\displaystyle\log\frac{\binom{n+1-k}{j}}{\binom{n-w}{j}}

\displaystyle=\log\frac{(n+1-k)\dots(n+2-k-j)}{(n-w)\dots(n-w-j+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}\left(\log\left(1-\frac{k+r-1}{n}\right)-\log\left(1-\frac{w+r}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}\left(\frac{(w+r)-(k+r-1)}{n}+\frac{(w+r)^{2}-(k+r-1)^{2}}{2n^{2}}\right)+O(n^{-3})

\displaystyle=j(w-k+1)\cdot n^{-1}+\frac{j(w-k+1)(j+w+k-2)}{2}\cdot n^{-2}+O(n^{-3}).

Expanding
\exp\Big({\log\frac{\binom{n+1-k}{j}}{\binom{n-w}{j}}}\Big)
into a power series, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{n+1-k}{j}}{\binom{n-w}{j}}

\displaystyle=1+j(w-k+1)\cdot n^{-1}+\frac{j(w-k+1)(j+w+k-2)+j^{2}(w-k+1)^{2}}{2}\cdot n^{-2}

\displaystyle\quad+O(n^{-3})

\displaystyle=1+j(w-k+1)\cdot n^{-1}+jw(w-k+1)n^{-2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{j(j-1)(w-k+1)(w-k+2)}{2}\cdot n^{-2}+O(n^{-3}),

and

(24)

\psi_{k}(x)=\alpha+(w-k+1)\beta n^{-1}+w(w-k+1)\beta n^{-2}+\binom{w-k+2}{2}\gamma n^{-2}+O(n^{-3}),

where

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\binom{x}{j}}{\binom{w}{j}}=\frac{1}{\binom{w}{x}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\binom{w-j}{w-x}=\frac{\binom{w-k}{x}}{\binom{w}{x}}

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}j\binom{k}{j}\frac{\binom{x}{j}}{\binom{w}{j}}=\frac{k}{\binom{w}{x}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}\binom{w-j}{w-x}=-k\frac{\binom{w-k}{x-1}}{\binom{w}{x}}

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}j(j-1)\binom{k}{j}\frac{\binom{x}{j}}{\binom{w}{j}}=\frac{k(k-1)}{\binom{w}{x}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k-2}{j-2}\binom{w-j}{w-x}

\displaystyle=k(k-1)\frac{\binom{w-k}{x-2}}{\binom{w}{x}},

where the last step in each of these three equalities is a version of the Vandermonde convolution.

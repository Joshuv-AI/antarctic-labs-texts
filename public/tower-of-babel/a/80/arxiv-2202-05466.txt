Characterization of Rational Solutions of a KdV-Like Equation
Brian D. Vasquez

Abstract
We characterize the rational solutions to a KdV-like equation which are generated from polynomial solutions to the corresponding generalized bilinear equation. We use a particular class of polynomials satisfying a quadratic difference equation to obtain that there are no solutions of the bilinear equation with degree (in the spatial variable) greater than 5 5 . As a byproduct, we answer positively a conjecture of Yi Zhang and Wen-Xiu Ma about these solutions. Key words : rational solution, generalized bilinear equation, KdV equation.



Introduction

Rational solutions of nonlinear partial differential equations have been considered as a simple way to understand their associated flows. Two remarkable cases are the KdV and the KP equations. In the KdV case, it is known after the work of Airault, McKean, and Moser
Airault et al. 1977
that the rational solutions
u(x,t)
which decay to zero are of the form

u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(x-x_{i}(t))^{2}}.

On the other hand, the method developed by Krichever
Krichever 1978
for constructing rational solutions of the Zakharov-Shabat equations allows to understand the motion of the poles
x_{1}(t),...,x_{n}(t)
of rational solutions of the KP equation. This method uses the Calogero-Moser theory for a system of
n
particles on a line with the Hamiltonian

H(p,x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i<j}2(x_{i}-x_{j})^{-2}.

Furthermore, rational solutions of nonlinear partial differential equations yield bispectral AKNS/ZS operators
Zubelli 1992
;
Sakhnovich and Zubelli 2001
.
In fact, if
q(x,t_{2},...,t_{m})
and
r(x,t_{2},...,t_{m})
are certain rational solutions of the AKNS hierarchy then the matrix differential operator

L=\left(\begin{matrix}\partial_{x}&-q\\
r&-\partial_{x}\end{matrix}\right),

is bispectral, i.e., there exist eigenfunctions
\psi(x,z)
of
L
such that
B(z,\partial_{z})\psi=\theta(x)\psi
for some matrix differential operator
B=B(z,\partial_{z})
which is independent of the variable
x
and
\theta
is a nonconstant function of
x
.
In
Zubelli and Valerio Silva 2000
rational solutions which decay at infinity and are bispectral potentials for the Schrödinger operator were characterized. Those functions are known as master symmetries for KdV.

As an application of the rational solutions of nonlinear partial differential equations, we have the rogue wave solutions as a model to be aware of the unexpected nonlinear wave phenomena in the ocean called rogue waves
Kharif et al. 2008
,
Müller et al. 2005
.

We consider the generalized bilinear differential equation of KdV type

2f_{xt}f-2f_{t}f_{x}+6(f_{xx})^{2}=0.

(1)

By taking the logarithmic derivative transformation

u=2\partial_{x}log(f),

(2)

we obtain the KdV-like equation

u_{t}+\frac{3}{2}(u_{x})^{2}+\frac{3}{2}u^{2}u_{x}+\frac{3}{8}u^{4}=0.

(3)

Note that a polynomial
f
solves Equation (
1
) if and only if
u
is a rational solution of the KdV-like Equation (
3
).
In
Ma 2011
generalizations of the bilinear differential operator of Hirota form (see
Hirota 2004
) were first studied.

To define the generalized operators we fix two integers
p,k\in\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}
and consider

D_{p,x_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot D_{p,x_{k}}^{n_{k}}f\cdot g=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+\alpha_{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)^{n_{i}}f(x_{1},...,x_{k})g(x_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},...,x_{k}^{{}^{\prime}})\mid_{x_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=x_{1},...,x_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}=x_{k}},

where
n_{1},...,n_{k}
are arbitrary nonnegative integers and the powers of
\alpha_{p}
are defined for an integer
m
as

\alpha_{p}^{m}=(-1)^{r_{p}(m)},\hskip 2.84544pt\text{if}\hskip 2.84544ptm\equiv r_{p}(m)\hskip 2.84544pt(mod\hskip 2.84544ptp)\hskip 2.84544pt\text{with}\hskip 2.84544pt0\leq r_{p}(m)<p.

In particular, if we consider the case
p=3
,
k=2
, then

D_{3,x}D_{3,t}f\cdot f=2f_{xt}f-2f_{t}f_{x},\hskip 2.84544ptD_{3,x}^{4}f\cdot f=6(f_{xx})^{2}.

Therefore,

\left(D_{3,x}D_{3,t}+D_{3,x}^{4}\right)f\cdot f=2f_{xt}f-2f_{t}f_{x}+6(f_{xx})^{2},

which is exactly the operator defining the Equation (
1
).

Furthermore, if we consider the Hirota case, i.e.,
p=k=2
, we obtain

D_{2,x}D_{2,t}f\cdot f=2f_{xt}f-2f_{t}f_{x},\hskip 2.84544ptD_{2,x}^{4}f\cdot f=2f_{xxxx}f-8f_{xxx}f_{x}+6(f_{xx})^{2}.

Therefore,

\left(D_{2,x}D_{2,t}+D_{2,x}^{4}\right)f\cdot f=2f_{xt}f-2f_{t}f_{x}+2f_{xxxx}f-8f_{xxx}f_{x}+6(f_{xx})^{2},

which is exactly the bilinear equation associated to the KdV equation. See
Hirota 2004
.

In the present work we characterize the polynomial solutions of Equation (
1
).
We show that the condition
\deg_{x}(f)\leq 4
is sufficient to obtain polynomial solutions of Equation (
1
) by giving their explicit expressions.

In order to characterize the algebro-geometric structure of the set of solutions we start by defining a classical notation used in algebraic geometry.

be an arbitrary set,

.

Definition 3

Let

.

We consider the nonlinear operator

. Note that the polynomial solutions of the Equation (

The plan of this article is as follows: In Section
1
, we fix

This give a positive answer to the conjecture proposed in

=f_{m}(t)^{2}\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{2m-1}\left\{\sum\limits_{l=0}^{m}(m-l)W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))\right.

\left.+3\sum\limits_{l=0}^{m}(m-l)(m-l-1)(m+l-k+3)(m+l-k+2)Q_{l}(t)Q_{k-l-3}(t)\right\}x^{2m-k-1}

=f_{m}(t)^{2}\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{2m-1}R_{k}(m,t)x^{2m-k-1}

with

R_{k}(t)=\sum\limits_{l=k-m}^{m}(m-l)W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))

+3\sum\limits_{l=k-m-1}^{m-2}(m-l)(m-l-1)(m+l-k+3)(m+l-k+2)Q_{l}(t)Q_{k-l-3}(t),

for every
m+1\leq k\leq 2m-1
.

However, we can symmetrize the first term

\sum\limits_{l=k-m}^{m}(m-l)W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))=\sum\limits_{l=k-m}^{\left[\frac{k-1}{2}\right]}(m-l)W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))+\sum\limits_{l=\left[\frac{k-1}{2}\right]+1}^{m}(m-l)W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))

=\sum\limits_{l=k-m}^{\left[\frac{k-1}{2}\right]}(m-l)W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))-\sum\limits_{j=k-m}^{\left[\frac{k-1}{2}\right]}(m-(k-j))W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-j}(t))

=\sum\limits_{l=k-m}^{\left[\frac{k-1}{2}\right]}(k-2l)W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))=\sum\limits_{l=k-m}^{m}\frac{(k-2l)}{2}W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))

Therefore,

R_{k}(t)=\sum\limits_{l=k-m}^{m}\frac{(k-2l)}{2}W_{t}(Q_{l}(t),Q_{k-l}(t))

+3\sum\limits_{l=k-m-1}^{m-2}(m-l)(m-l-1)(m+l-k+3)(m+l-k+2)Q_{l}(t)Q_{k-l-3}(t).

Theorem 4
.

The only fundamental solutions of the bilinear differential equation

are

.

By simple calculations we have that

are solutions. We don’t have solutions of degree
m=2

If we consider the case

The only restriction is

Therefore, the fundamental solution is

Conclusions and Comments

We considered a generalized bilinear equation and its associated KdV-like equation. Those types of equations are linked to the bilinear operators defined and studied in
Ma 2011
and we characterized the solution based on a prime number
p=3
. We gave a positive answer to the conjecture proposed in
Zhang and Ma 2015
about two classes of rational solutions of the nonlinear KdV-like equation (
3
) generated from the polynomial solutions to the generalized bilinear equation (
1
).

The most important fact is that the bilinear equation for
(p,k)=(2,2)
i.e., the bilinear KdV equation
\left(D_{2,x}D_{2,t}+D_{2,x}^{4}\right)f\cdot f=0
has polynomial solutions of degree arbitrarily large in the variable
x

Ma and You 2005
while, we just saw, in the case
(p,k)=(3,2)
the bilinear KdV-like equation
(D_{3,x}D_{3,t}+D_{3,x}^{4})f\cdot f=0
only has polynomial solutions of degree
1,3,4
in the variable
x
.

The previous facts suggest that the next question in this direction is to characterize the polynomial solutions of the bilinear KdV-like equation in the case
(p,2)
for arbitrary
p
and to look for generalizations for several variables
k
greater than
2
.

It is natural to think about the existence of generalized
d-
linear differential operators such as the trilinear studied in
Ma 2013
. We think that studying their rational solutions is an interesting topic for future research. It may be possible that in the general
d-
linear case, the set of rational solutions of the KdV-like equation obtained applying the transformation (
2
) to polynomial solutions of the generalized
d-
linear equation is finite as in the bilinear case studied in this paper for the prime number
p=3
.

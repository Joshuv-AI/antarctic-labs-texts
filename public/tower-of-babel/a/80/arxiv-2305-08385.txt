Matrix quadratic risk of orthogonally invariant estimators for a normal mean matrix
Takeru Matsuda

Abstract
In estimation of a normal mean matrix under the matrix quadratic loss, we develop a general formula for the matrix quadratic risk of orthogonally invariant estimators. The derivation is based on several formulas for matrix derivatives of orthogonally invariant functions of matrices. As an application, we calculate the matrix quadratic risk of a singular value shrinkage estimator motivated by Stein’s proposal for improving on the Efron–Morris estimator 50 years ago.

Introduction

Suppose that we have a matrix observation
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
whose entries are independent normal random variables
X_{ij}\sim{\rm N}(M_{ij},1)
, where
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is an unknown mean matrix. In this setting, we consider estimation of
M
under the matrix quadratic loss
[
1
,
6
]
:

\displaystyle L(M,\hat{M})=(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M),

(1)

which takes a value in the set of
p\times p
positive semidefinite matrices.
The risk function of an estimator
\hat{M}=\hat{M}(X)
is defined as
R(M,\hat{M})={\rm E}_{M}[L(M,\hat{M}(X))]
, and an estimator
\hat{M}_{1}
is said to dominate another estimator
\hat{M}_{2}
if
R(M,\hat{M}_{1})\preceq R(M,\hat{M}_{2})
for every
M
, where
\preceq
is the Löwner order:
A\preceq B
means that
B-A
is positive semidefinite.
Thus, if
\hat{M}_{1}
dominates
\hat{M}_{2}
under the matrix quadratic loss, then

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}[\|(\hat{M}_{1}-M)c\|^{2}]\leq{\rm E}_{M}[\|(\hat{M}_{2}-M)c\|^{2}]

for every
M
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
In particular, each column of
\hat{M}_{1}
dominates that of
\hat{M}_{2}
as an estimator of the corresponding column of
M
under quadratic loss.
Recently,
[
6
]
investigated shrinkage estimation in this setting by introducing a concept called matrix superharmonicity, which can be viewed as a generalization of the theory by Stein
[
7
]
for a normal mean vector.
Note that shrinkage estimation of a normal mean matrix under the Frobenius loss, which is the trace of the matrix quadratic loss, has been well studied, e.g.
[
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

Many common estimators of a normal mean matrix are orthogonally invariant.
Namely, they satisfy
\hat{M}(PXQ)=P\hat{M}(X)Q
for any orthogonal matrices
P\in O(n)
and
Q\in O(p)
.
It can be viewed as a generalization of the rotationally invariance of estimators for a normal mean vector, which is satisfied by many minimax shrinkage estimators
[
3
]
.
We focus on orthogonally invariant estimators given by

\displaystyle\hat{M}=X+\widetilde{\nabla}h(X),

(2)

where
h(X)
is an orthogonally invariant function satisfying
h(PXQ)=h(X)
for any orthogonal matrices
P\in O(n)
and
Q\in O(p)
and
\widetilde{\nabla}
is the matrix gradient operator defined by (
3
).
For example, the maximum likelihood estimator
\hat{M}=X
corresponds to
h(X)=0
.
The Efron–Morris estimator
[
2
]
defined by
\hat{M}=X(I-(n-p-1)(X^{\top}X)^{-1})
when
n-p-1>0
corresponds to
h(X)={-(n-p-1)/2}\cdot\log\det(X^{\top}X)
.
This estimator can be viewed as a matrix generalization of the James–Stein estimator and it is minimax under the Frobenius loss
[
2
]
as well as matrix quadratic loss
[
6
]
.
We will provide another example of an orthogonally invariant estimator of the form (
2
) in Section
4
.
Note that an estimator of the form (
2
) is called a pseudo-Bayes estimator
[
3
]
, because it coincides with the (generalized) Bayes estimator when
h
is given by the logarithm of the marginal distirbution of
X
with respect to some prior on
M
(Tweedie’s formula).

In this study, to further the theory of shrinkage estimation under the matrix quadratic loss, we develop a general formula for the matrix quadratic risk of orthogonally invariant estimators of the form (
2
).
First, we prepare several matrix derivative formulas in Section
2
.
Then, we derive the formula for the matrix quadratic risk in Section
3
.
Finally, we present an example in Section
4
, which is motivated by Stein’s proposal for improving on the Efron–Morris estimator 50 years ago
[
7
]
.

2
Matrix derivative formulas

Here, we develop matrix derivative formulas based on
[
7
]
.
Note that, whereas
[
7
]
considered a setting where
X
is a
p\times n
matrix, here we take
X
to be a
n\times p
matrix.
In the following, the subscripts
a
,
b
,
\ldots
run from
1
to
n
and the subscripts
i
,
j
,
\ldots
run from
1
to
p
.
We denote the Kronecker delta by
\delta_{ij}
.

We employ the following notations for matrix derivatives introduced in
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\to\mathbb{R}
, its matrix gradient
\widetilde{\nabla}f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is defined as

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\nabla}f(X))_{ai}=\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{ai}}f(X).

(3)

Definition 2.2
.

For a
C^{2}
function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\to\mathbb{R}
, its matrix Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
is defined as

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\Delta}f(X))_{ij}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial X_{ai}\partial X_{aj}}f(X).

(4)

Let

\displaystyle X^{\top}X=V\Lambda V^{\top}

(5)

be a spectral decomposition of
X^{\top}X
, where
V=(v_{1},\dots,v_{p})
is an orthogonal matrix and
\Lambda={\rm diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p})
is a diagonal matrix.
Then, the derivatives of
\lambda
and
V
are obtained as follows.

Lemma 2.3
.

The derivative of
\lambda_{i}
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\lambda_{i}}{\partial X_{aj}}=2V_{ji}\sum_{k}X_{ak}V_{ki}.

(6)

Thus,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}\lambda_{i}=2Xv_{i}v_{i}^{\top},

(7)

where
v_{i}
is the
i
-th column vector of
V
.

Proof.

By differentiating
V^{\top}V=I_{p}
and using
({\rm d}V)^{\top}V=(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)^{\top}
, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{\top}{\rm d}V+(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)^{\top}=O,

(8)

which means the antisymmetricity of
V^{\top}{\rm d}V
.

Taking the differential of (
5
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)=({\rm d}V)\Lambda V^{\top}+V({\rm d}\Lambda)V^{\top}+V\Lambda({\rm d}V)^{\top}.

(9)

Then, multiplying (
9
) on the left by
V^{\top}
and on the right by
V
, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{\top}\cdot{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)\cdot V=V^{\top}({\rm d}V)\Lambda+{\rm d}\Lambda+\Lambda({\rm d}V)^{\top}V.

(10)

Since
\Lambda
and
{\rm d}\Lambda
are diagonal and
({\rm d}V)^{\top}V=(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)^{\top}
, the
(i,j)
-th entry of (
10
) yields

\displaystyle(V^{\top}\cdot{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)\cdot V)_{ij}

\displaystyle=(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ij}\lambda_{j}+\delta_{ij}{\rm d}\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i}(({\rm d}V)^{\top}V)_{ij}

\displaystyle=(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ij}\lambda_{j}+\delta_{ij}{\rm d}\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{i}(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ji}.

Since
(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ji}=-(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ij}
from (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle(V^{\top}\cdot{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)\cdot V)_{ij}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ij}+\delta_{ij}{\rm d}\lambda_{i}.

(11)

On the other hand, from
{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)=({\rm d}X)^{\top}X+X^{\top}{\rm d}X
,

\displaystyle{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)_{ij}=\sum_{a}(({\rm d}X)_{ai}X_{aj}+X_{ai}({\rm d}X)_{aj}).

(12)

By taking
i=j
in (
11
),

\displaystyle{\rm d}\lambda_{i}=(V^{\top}\cdot{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)\cdot V)_{ii}=\sum_{j,k}(V^{\top})_{ij}({\rm d}(X^{\top}X))_{jk}V_{ki}.

Then, by using (
12
),

\displaystyle{\rm d}\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k}V_{ji}V_{ki}\sum_{a}(({\rm d}X)_{aj}X_{ak}+X_{aj}({\rm d}X)_{ak})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j,k}V_{ji}V_{ki}\sum_{a}X_{ak}({\rm d}X)_{aj}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{a,j}V_{ji}\sum_{k}X_{ak}V_{ki}({\rm d}X)_{aj}.

Thus, we obtain (
6
) and it leads to (
7
).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

The derivative of
V_{ij}
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V_{ij}}{\partial X_{ak}}=\sum_{l\neq j}\frac{V_{il}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}((XV)_{aj}V_{kl}+(XV)_{al}V_{kj}).

(13)

Proof.

From (
8
), we have
(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ii}=0
.
Also, from (
11
),

\displaystyle(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ij}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}(V^{\top}\cdot{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)\cdot V)_{ij}

for
i\neq j
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm d}V_{ij}

\displaystyle=(VV^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}V_{ik}(V^{\top}{\rm d}V)_{kj}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq j}V_{ik}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}}(V^{\top}\cdot{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)\cdot V)_{kj}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}}V_{ik}\sum_{l,m}V_{lk}{\rm d}(X^{\top}X)_{lm}V_{mj}.

Then, by using (
12
),

\displaystyle{\rm d}V_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}}V_{ik}\sum_{l,m}V_{lk}\sum_{a}(({\rm d}X)_{al}X_{am}+X_{al}({\rm d}X)_{am})V_{mj}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}}V_{ik}\sum_{a}\left((XV)_{aj}\sum_{l}V_{lk}({\rm d}X)_{al}+(XV)_{ak}\sum_{m}V_{mj}({\rm d}X)_{am}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{V_{ik}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}}\sum_{a}((XV)_{aj}V_{lk}+(XV)_{ak}V_{lj})({\rm d}X)_{al}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,k}\sum_{l\neq j}\frac{V_{il}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}((XV)_{aj}V_{kl}+(XV)_{al}V_{kj})({\rm d}X)_{ak},

where we switched
k
and
l
in the last step.
Thus, we obtain (
13
).
∎

A function
h
is said to be orthogonally invariant if it satisfies
h(PXQ)=h(X)
for any orthogonal matrices
P\in O(n)
and
Q\in O(p)
.
Such a function can be written as
h(X)=H(\lambda)
, where
\lambda
is the eigenvalues of
X^{\top}X
as given by (
5
), and its derivatives are calculated as follows.

Lemma 2.5
.

The matrix gradient (
3
) of an orthogonally invariant function
h(X)=H(\lambda)
is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}h=2\sum_{i}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{i}}Xv_{i}v_{i}^{\top}.

(14)

Thus,

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\nabla}h)^{\top}(\widetilde{\nabla}h)=4\sum_{i}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\right)^{2}\lambda_{i}v_{i}v_{i}^{\top}=VDV^{\top},

(15)

where
D
is the
p\times p
diagonal matrix given by

\displaystyle D_{kk}=4\lambda_{k}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

From (
7
),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}h=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\widetilde{\nabla}\lambda_{i}=2\sum_{i}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{i}}Xv_{i}v_{i}^{\top},

which yields (
14
).
Then, by using
X^{\top}X=V\Lambda V^{\top}
and
V^{\top}V=I_{p}
,

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\nabla}h)^{\top}(\widetilde{\nabla}h)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{j}}v_{i}v_{i}^{\top}X^{\top}Xv_{j}v_{j}^{\top}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{j}}v_{i}\Lambda_{ij}v_{j}^{\top}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\right)^{2}\lambda_{i}v_{i}v_{i}^{\top},

which yields (
15
).
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

The matrix Laplacian (
4
) of an orthogonally invariant function
h(X)=H(\lambda)
is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}h=VDV^{\top},

(16)

where
D
is the
p\times p
diagonal matrix given by

\displaystyle D_{kk}=4\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+2n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}+2\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{l}}\right).

Proof.

From (
14
),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial X_{ai}\partial X_{aj}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{ai}}\left(2\sum_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}Xv_{k}v_{k}^{\top}\right)_{aj}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k}\left(\left(\widetilde{\nabla}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)_{ai}(Xv_{k}v_{k}^{\top})_{aj}+\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}(v_{k}v_{k}^{\top})_{ij}+\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{l}X_{al}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{ai}}(v_{k}v_{k}^{\top})_{lj}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k}\left(2\sum_{l}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}\partial\lambda_{l}}(Xv_{l}v_{l}^{\top})_{ai}(Xv_{k}v_{k}^{\top})_{aj}+\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}V_{ik}V_{jk}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{l}X_{al}\left(\frac{\partial V_{lk}}{\partial X_{ai}}V_{jk}+V_{lk}\frac{\partial V_{jk}}{\partial X_{ai}}\right)\right).

(17)

Also, from (
13
),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V_{lk}}{\partial X_{ai}}V_{jk}+V_{lk}\frac{\partial V_{jk}}{\partial X_{ai}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{V_{lm}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}((XV)_{ak}V_{im}+(XV)_{am}V_{ik})V_{jk}

\displaystyle\qquad+V_{lk}\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{V_{jm}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}((XV)_{ak}V_{im}+(XV)_{am}V_{ik})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{(V_{lm}V_{jk}+V_{lk}V_{jm})((XV)_{ak}V_{im}+(XV)_{am}V_{ik})}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}.

(18)

By substituting (
18
) into (
17
) and taking the sum,

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\Delta}h)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial X_{ai}\partial X_{aj}}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{k,l}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}\partial\lambda_{l}}(v_{l}v_{l}^{\top}X^{\top}Xv_{k}v_{k}^{\top})_{ij}+2n\sum_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}V_{ik}V_{jk}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{k,l}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{(V_{lm}V_{jk}+V_{lk}V_{jm})((X^{\top}XV)_{lk}V_{im}+(X^{\top}XV)_{lm}V_{ik})}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(4\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+2n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)V_{ik}V_{jk}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{k,l}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{(V_{lm}V_{jk}+V_{lk}V_{jm})(\lambda_{k}V_{lk}V_{im}+\lambda_{m}V_{lm}V_{ik})}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(4\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+2n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)V_{ik}V_{jk}+2\sum_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{k}V_{jm}V_{im}+\lambda_{m}V_{jk}V_{ik}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}

where we used
X^{\top}X=V\Lambda V^{\top}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{l}{(V_{lm}V_{jk}+V_{lk}V_{jm})(\lambda_{k}V_{lk}V_{im}+\lambda_{m}V_{lm}V_{ik})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(\delta_{km}V_{jk}+V_{jm})V_{im}+\lambda_{m}(V_{jk}+\delta_{km}V_{jm})V_{ik}.

Then,

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\Delta}h)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(4\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+2n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}+2\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}\right)V_{ik}V_{jk}+2\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{V_{im}V_{jm}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(4\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+2n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}+2\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{m}}\right)\right)V_{ik}V_{jk},

where we used

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{V_{im}V_{jm}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}=\sum_{m}V_{im}V_{jm}\sum_{k\neq m}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}=\sum_{k}V_{ik}V_{jk}\sum_{m\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{k}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{m}}.

Thus, by rewriting
m
to
l
, we obtain (
16
).
∎

By taking the trace of the matrix Laplacian (
16
), we have

\displaystyle{\Delta}h

\displaystyle={\rm tr}(\widetilde{\Delta}h)={\rm tr}(D)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(4\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+2n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}+2\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{l}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(4\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+2n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)+2\sum_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+2\sum_{k}\left(n-p+1+2\lambda_{k}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\right)\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}},

where we used

\displaystyle\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}+\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}+\sum_{l\neq k}\left(\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{k}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}-p+1.

(19)

This coincides with the Laplacian formula in
[
7
]
.

3
Risk formula

Now, we derive a general formula for the matrix quadratic risk of orthogonally invariant estimators of the form (
2
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
h(X)=H(\lambda)
be an orthogonally invariant function.
Then, the matrix quadratic risk of an estimator
\hat{M}=X+\widetilde{\nabla}h(X)
is given by

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M)

\displaystyle=nI_{p}+{\rm E}_{M}[VDV^{\top}],

(20)

where
D
is the
p\times p
diagonal matrix given by

\displaystyle D_{kk}

\displaystyle=4\left(2\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}+\lambda_{k}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{l}}\right)\right).

Proof.

From
[
6
]
, the matrix quadratic risk of an estimator
\hat{M}=X+g(X)
with a weakly differentiable function
g
is

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M)=nI_{p}+{\rm E}_{M}[\widetilde{\mathrm{div}}\ g(X)+(\widetilde{\mathrm{div}}\ g(X))^{\top}+g(X)^{\top}g(X)],

(21)

where the matrix divergence
\widetilde{\mathrm{div}}\ g:\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
of a function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is defined as

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\mathrm{div}}\ g(X))_{ij}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{ai}}g_{aj}(X).

Therefore, by substituting
g(X)=\widetilde{\nabla}h(X)
and using
\widetilde{\mathrm{div}}\circ\widetilde{\nabla}=\widetilde{\Delta}
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M)=nI_{p}+E_{M}[2\widetilde{\Delta}h(X)+\widetilde{\nabla}h(X)^{\top}\widetilde{\nabla}h(X)].

Thus, by using (
15
) and (
16
), we obtain (
20
).
∎

By taking the trace of (
20
) and using (
19
), we obtain the following formula for the Frobenius risk of orthogonally invariant estimators, which coincides with the one given by
[
7
]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
h(X)=H(\lambda)
be an orthogonally invariant function.
Then, the Frobenius risk of an estimator
\hat{M}=X+\widetilde{\nabla}h(X)
is given by

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}\|\hat{M}-M\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle np+4{\rm E}_{M}\left[\sum_{k}\left(2\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}+\lambda_{k}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)^{2}\right)+\sum_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle np+4{\rm E}_{M}\left[\sum_{k}\left(2\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}+\lambda_{k}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)^{2}\right)+\sum_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\left(2\lambda_{k}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}-p+1\right)\right].

We derived the risk formula for orthogonally invariant estimators of the form (
2
), which are called pseudo-Bayes estimators
[
3
]
.
The class of pseudo-Bayes estimators includes all Bayes and generalized Bayes estimators.
It is an interesting future work to extend the current result to general orthogonally invariant estimators.
Also, extension to unknown covariance case is an important future problem. Note that Section 6.6.2 of
[
9
]
derived a risk formula for a class of estimators in the unknown covariance setting.

4
Example

We provide an example of the application of Theorem
3.1
.
Let
X=U\Sigma V^{\top}
with
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
,
\Sigma={\rm diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{p})
and
V\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
be a singular value decomposition of
X
, where
U^{\top}U=V^{\top}V=I_{p}
and
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{p}\geq 0
are the singular values of
X
.
We consider an orthogonally invariant estimator given by

\displaystyle\hat{M}=U\cdot{\rm diag}\left(\sigma_{1}-\frac{c_{1}}{\sigma_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{p}-\frac{c_{p}}{\sigma_{p}}\right)\cdot V^{\top},

(22)

where
c_{1},\dots,c_{p}\geq 0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The estimator (
22
) can be written in the form (
2
) with

\displaystyle h(X)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{p}{c_{k}}\log\lambda_{k}=-\sum_{k=1}^{p}{c_{k}}\log\sigma_{k},

where
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p}
are the eigenvalues of
X^{\top}X
as shown in (
5
).

Proof.

From (
7
),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}\log\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\widetilde{\nabla}\lambda_{k}=\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}Xv_{k}v_{k}^{\top}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}h(X)=-X\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}v_{k}v_{k}^{\top}=-U\cdot{\rm diag}\left(\frac{c_{1}}{\sigma_{1}},\dots,\frac{c_{p}}{\sigma_{p}}\right)\cdot V^{\top},

where we used
X=U\Sigma V^{\top}
and
\lambda_{k}=\sigma_{k}^{2}
.
Therefore, the estimator (
22
) is written as
\hat{M}=X+\widetilde{\nabla}h(X)
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

The matrix quadratic risk of the estimator (
22
) is given by

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M)

\displaystyle=nI_{p}+{\rm E}_{M}[VDV^{\top}],

(23)

where
D
is the
p\times p
diagonal matrix given by

\displaystyle D_{kk}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}c_{k}(c_{k}-2n+4)-\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{c_{k}\lambda_{l}-c_{l}\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}.

Proof.

To apply Theorem
3.1
, let

\displaystyle H(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}c_{k}\log\lambda_{k}.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}=-\frac{c_{k}}{2\lambda_{k}},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}=\frac{c_{k}}{2\lambda_{k}^{2}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle D_{kk}

\displaystyle=4\left(2\lambda_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\lambda_{k}^{2}}+n\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}+\lambda_{k}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{k}}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda_{l}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}c_{k}(c_{k}-2n+4)-2\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}-\frac{c_{l}}{\lambda_{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}c_{k}(c_{k}-2n+4)-\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{c_{k}\lambda_{l}-c_{l}\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}.

Therefore, we obtain (
23
) from Theorem
3.1
.
∎

The Efron–Morris estimator
[
2
]
corresponds to (
22
) with
c_{k}\equiv n-p-1
.
In this case,

\displaystyle D_{kk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(n-p-1)(-n-p+3)-\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}(n-p-1)\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(n-p-1)(-n-p+3)+\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}(n-p-1)(p-1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(n-p-1)^{2}.

Thus, its matrix quadratic risk (
23
) is

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M)

\displaystyle=nI_{p}-(n-p-1)^{2}{\rm E}_{M}\left[(X^{\top}X)^{-1}\right].

(24)

This coincides with the result in
[
6
]
.

Motivated by Stein’s proposal
[
7
]
for improving on the Efron–Morris estimator, we consider the estimator (
22
) with
c_{k}=n+p-2k-1
.
In the following, we call it “Stein’s estimator” for convenience.
Stein
[
7
]
stated that the positive-part of Stein’s estimator dominates the positive-part of the Efron--Morris estimator under the Frobenius loss

, where “positive-part” means the modification of (
22
) given by

\displaystyle\hat{M}=U\cdot{\rm diag}\left(\left(\sigma_{1}-\frac{c_{1}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)_{+},\dots,\left(\sigma_{p}-\frac{c_{p}}{\sigma_{p}}\right)_{+}\right)\cdot V^{\top},

(25)

where
(a)_{+}=\max(0,a)
.
It is known that the estimator (
22
) is dominated by its positive-part (
25
) under the Frobenius loss

]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

The matrix quadratic risk of Stein’s estimator (estimator (
22
) with
c_{k}=n+p-2k-1
) is given by

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M)

\displaystyle=nI_{p}+{\rm E}_{M}[VDV^{\top}],

where
D
is the
p\times p
diagonal matrix given by

\displaystyle D_{kk}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(n+p-2k-1)(n-3p+2k-1)+4\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{k-l}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}.

Thus, Stein’s estimator dominates the maximum likelihood estimator under the matrix quadratic loss when
n\geq 3p-1
.

Proof.

By substituting
c_{k}=n+p-2k-1
into Theorem
4.2
,

\displaystyle D_{kk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(n+p-2k-1)(-n+p-2k+3)-\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{(n+p-2k-1)\lambda_{l}-(n+p-2l-1)\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(n+p-2k-1)(-n+p-2k+3)-\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}\sum_{l\neq k}\left(-\frac{2(k-l)\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}-(n+p-2k-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(n+p-2k-1)(-n+p-2k+3)+4\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{k-l}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}+\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}(p-1)(n+p-2k-1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(n+p-2k-1)(n-3p+2k-1)+4\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{k-l}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}.

The second term is nonpositive since
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{p}
.
When
n\geq 3p-1
, the first term is also nonpositive and thus

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{M}(\hat{M}-M)^{\top}(\hat{M}-M)\preceq nI_{p}.

∎

Numerical results indicate that the bound of
n
in Proposition
4.3
may be relaxed to
n\geq p+2
, which is the same bound with the Efron–Morris estimator.
See Appendix.

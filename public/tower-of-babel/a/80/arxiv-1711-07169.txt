A characterization of finite vector bundles on Gauduchon astheno-Kähler manifolds
Indranil Biswas, Vamsi Pritham Pingali

Abstract
A vector bundle on a projective variety X X is called finite if it satisfies a nontrivial polynomial equation with integral coefficients. Nori proved that a vector bundle E E on X X is finite if and only if there is a finite étale Galois covering q : X ~ ⟶ X q\,:\,\widetilde{X}\,\longrightarrow\,X and a Gal ​ ( q ) \text{Gal}(q) –module V V , such that E E is isomorphic to the quotient of X ~ × V \widetilde{X}\times V by the twisted diagonal action of Gal ​ ( q ) \text{Gal}(q) [ No1 ] , [ No2 ] . Therefore, E E is finite if and only if the pullback of E E to some finite étale Galois covering of X X is trivial. We prove the same statement when X X is a compact complex manifold admitting a Gauduchon astheno-Kähler metric.



Keywords.
Finite bundles; astheno-Kähler manifolds; numerically flat bundles; Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem

1.
Introduction

For a polynomial
f(x)\,=\,\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}
, where

are nonnegative integers,
and a holomorphic vector bundle

on

(the vector bundle

is defined to be the trivial line

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
). An algebraic (respectively, holomorphic) vector bundle

is isomorphic to

. It
is known that a vector bundle

for all

[
No1
, p. 35, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
No2
, p. 80, Lemma 3.1]
. When

In a foundational work, Nori proved that a vector bundle

and a

–module

[

]
,
[

]
. Therefore, the monodromy homomorphism of the flat connection on

Let

This pair

Our aim here is to prove the following:

Suppose

that is both Gauduchon and astheno-Kähler. Then a holomorphic vector

fundamental group of

Note that a flat connection with finite monodromy is a unitary connection for a suitable
Hermitian structure.

Theorem

[

[

]
crucially used a

[

]

only in the Kähler context. Here we have been able to avoid using the
Corlette–Simpson theorem.

For higher dimensional examples of Gauduchon astheno-Kähler manifolds,

2.
Vanishing of Chern classes

In whatever follows, for a compact complex manifold
X
we use the Bott-Chern cohomology
defined as

H^{p,q}_{BC}(X)\,=\,\frac{ker(\partial:A^{p,q}(X)\rightarrow A^{p+1,q}(X))\cap ker(\bar{\partial}:A^{p,q}(X)\rightarrow A^{p,q+1}(X))}{Im(\mathcal{\partial\overline{\partial}}(A^{p-1,q-1}(X)))}\,

where
A^{p,q}(X)
consists of smooth
(p,q)
-forms on
X
. These groups are finite
dimensional. They coincide with the Dolbeault cohomology
groups for Kähler manifolds, as a consequence of the
\mathcal{\partial\overline{\partial}}
lemma. If
h
is a
Hermitian metric on a holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
X
, and
\Theta_{h}
is the
curvature of its
Chern connection, then for any characteristic class
\Phi
, its Chern-Weil form

\Phi(\Theta_{h})
is closed, and
\Phi(\Theta_{h_{2}})-\Phi(\Theta_{h_{1}})\,=\,\mathcal{\partial\overline{\partial}}BC(h_{1},h_{2})
, where
BC
is the Bott-Chern form. Therefore, we can think of
\Phi
as
an element of the Bott-Chern cohomology (with representatives furnished by using
Chern connections). Moreover, if
E\,=\,S\bigoplusop\displaylimits V
, then

[ch(E)]_{BC}\,=\,[ch(S)]_{BC}+[ch(V)]_{BC}
(proven by taking a direct sum metric).
Likewise, we have
[ch(E\otimes F)]_{BC}\,=\,[ch(E)]_{BC}[ch(F)]_{BC}
.

From now onwards, we denote a vector bundle
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}(F_{j})^{\oplus a_{ij}}

as
\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}a_{ij}F_{j}
.

Suppose
E
is a finite holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
on a compact complex manifold

X
, i.e., there exists integers
m\geq 1
,
a_{i,j}\,\geq\,0
,
r_{j}\,\geq\,1
with

1\leq\,j\,\leq\,m
,
i\,\in\,{\mathbb N}^{>0}
,
and holomorphic vector bundles
V_{j}
of ranks
r_{j}
such that
E^{\otimes i}\,=\,\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}a_{i,j}V_{j}
. Then
[\mathrm{ch}(E)]_{BC}\,=\,r
.

We induct on
i
to show that
[ch_{i}(E)]_{BC}\,=\,0
for all
i\,\geq\,1
.

For
i\,=\,1
, note that
\frac{n}{r}[c_{1}(E)]_{BC}\,=\,\frac{[ch_{1}(E^{\otimes n})]_{BC}}{r^{n}}
. On
the other hand,
E^{\otimes n}\,=\,\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}a_{n,j}V_{j}
.
So,

\frac{[ch_{1}(E^{\otimes n})]_{BC}}{r^{n}}\,=\,\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}a_{n,j}\frac{[c_{1}(V_{j})]_{BC}}{r^{n}}\,=\,\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}a_{n,j}\frac{[c_{1}(V_{j})]_{BC}/r_{j}}{r^{n}}\,.

Since
r^{n}\,=\,\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}a_{n,j}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}a_{n,j}\frac{[c_{1}(V_{j})]_{BC}}{r^{n}}
lies in a bounded subset of

H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})
; indeed it lies in the convex subset generated by

\{[c_{1}(V_{j})/r_{j}]_{BC}\}_{j=1}^{k}
. However,
\frac{n}{r}[c_{1}(E)]_{BC}
is unbounded as
n

varies if
[c_{1}(E)]_{BC}
is nonzero. Therefore, it follows that
[c_{1}(E)]_{BC}\,=\,0
.

Suppose
[ch_{i}(E)]_{BC}\,=\,0
for all
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,k-1
. Then,

\frac{[ch_{k}(E^{\otimes n})]_{BC}}{r^{n}}\,=\,\frac{n}{r}[ch_{k}(E)]_{BC}\,,

and hence

\frac{[ch_{k}(E^{\otimes n})]_{BC}}{r^{n}}
is unbounded as
n
varies if
[ch_{k}(E)]_{BC}
is nonzero.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}a_{n,j}\frac{[ch_{k}(V_{j})]_{BC}}{r^{n}}\,=\,\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}a_{n,j}\frac{[ch_{k}(V_{j})]_{BC}/r_{j}}{r^{n}}\,,

so
\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}a_{n,j}\frac{[ch_{k}(V_{j})]_{BC}}{r^{n}}
lies in the convex subset
of
H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})
generated by
\{[ch_{k}(V_{j})/r_{j}]_{BC}\}_{j=1}^{k}
and is hence bounded. Thus, as before,
[ch_{k}(E)]_{BC}=0
.

\square

\square

3.
A characterization of numerically flat bundles on Gauduchon astheno-Kähler
manifolds

We recall the definition of a
nef
vector bundle as in
[
DPS
]
. A line bundle
L
over
a compact complex manifold
M
, equipped with a Hermitian metric
\omega
, is called nef if
given any real number
\epsilon\,>\,0
, there is a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
L
such that

\Theta(h_{\epsilon})+\epsilon\omega
is a nonnegative Hermitian form, where

\Theta(h_{\epsilon})
is the Chern curvature form. This definition actually does not depend on
the choice of
\omega
. A holomorphic vector bundle
V
on
M
is called nef if the
tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(1)
on
\mathbb{P}(V)
is nef. A
holomorphic vector bundle
V
is called
numerically flat
if both
V
and
V^{*}
are nef.

We note that Lemma 2.4 of
[
BHS
]
, which was obtained as a corollary of Theorem 1.12
of
[
DPS
]
, is stated in the Kähler set-up, because
[
BHS
]
is entirely
in the Kähler set-up. However, Theorem 1.12
of
[
DPS
]
is proved for compact complex manifolds. Hence Lemma 2.4 of
[
BHS
]

remains valid for compact complex manifolds.

If
E
is finite, then
E
is numerically flat.

Since
E
is finite, there are finitely many indecomposable bundles
V_{1},\,\ldots,\,V_{k}
such that

E^{\otimes i}\,=\,\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{k}a_{i,j}V_{j}\ \forall\ i\geq 1
. The symmetric
product
\text{Sym}^{i}(E)
is a direct summand of the vector bundle
E^{\otimes i}
. Hence from

[
At
, p. 315, Theorem 3]
(quoted in Lemma
2
) it follows that

\text{Sym}^{i}(E)\,=\,\,\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{k}a^{\prime}_{i,j}V_{j}\,,

where
a^{\prime}_{i,j}\,\leq\,a_{i,j}
are nonnegative integers. For each
i\,\geq\,1
, fix
an isomorphism of
\text{Sym}^{i}(E)
with
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{k}a^{\prime}_{i,j}V_{j}
.

Now fix smooth Hermitian structures
g_{j}
on
V_{j}
. This produces a Hermitian structure
on any direct sum of copies of
V_{j}
such that the direct sum decomposition is orthogonal.
Consequently, we get a Hermitian structure
{h}_{i}
on
\text{Sym}^{i}(E)
using the above
isomorphism.

Let
\Theta_{j}
be the curvature of the Chern connection on the Hermitian
vector bundle
(V,\,g_{j})
. So the curvature
\Theta_{i}
of the Chern connection for

(\text{Sym}^{i}(E),\,h_{i})
satisfies the equation

\Theta_{i}\,=\,\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{k}a^{\prime}_{i,j}\Theta_{i}

(coefficients correspond to direct sum).

According to Theorem 1.12 of
[
DPS
]
, the vector bundle
E
is nef if
for every
\epsilon\,>\,0
and
m\,\geq\,m_{0}(\epsilon)
we have

\displaystyle\Theta_{m}\,\geq\,-m\epsilon\omega\otimes{\rm Id}_{{\rm Sym}^{m}(E)}

(3.2)

in the sense of Griffiths. It is straight-forward to check that this condition is
satisfied. Hence the finite vector bundle
E
is nef.

\square

We now prove an analogue of Theorem 1.18 of Demailly-Peternell-Schneider
[
DPS
]
in our
special case of astheno-Kähler manifolds.

Suppose
(X,\,\omega)
is a compact complex manifold equipped with a
Hermitian metric
\omega
that is both, Gauduchon (
\mathcal{\partial\overline{\partial}}\omega^{n-1}\,=\,0
), as well as
astheno-Kähler
(\mathcal{\partial\overline{\partial}}\omega^{n-2}\,=\,0
). Suppose a holomorphic vector bundle
E

of rank
r
over
X
satisfies
[c_{2}(E)]_{BC}=0
.
Then it is numerically flat if and only if the following three conditions hold:

(1)

E
is
\omega
-semi-stable,

(2)

there is a Jordan-Hölder filtration

\{0\}\,=\,E_{0}\,\subset\,E_{1}\,\subset\,\ldots\,\subset\,E_{p-1}\,\subset\,E_{p}\,=\,E

consisting of subbundles, and

(3)

for all
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,p
, the
\omega
-stable bundle
E_{i}/E_{i-1}

is Hermitian flat, i.e., given by an irreducible unitary representation of
\pi_{1}(X)
in

{\rm U}({\rm rank}(E_{i}/E_{i-1}))
.

It can be shown that
[c_{1}(E)]_{BC}\,=\,[c_{1}(\det(E))]_{BC}\,=\,0
whenever
E
is numerically
flat. Indeed, applying Proposition 1.14 (ii) of
[
DPS
]
to the determinant representation of

E
and
E^{*}

we see that
\det(E)
is numerically flat. Now Corollary 1.5 of
[
DPS
]
implies that

\det(E)
is Hermitian flat and hence
[c_{1}(\det(E))]_{BC}\,=\,0
. We are grateful to the
anonymous referee for pointing this out.

A Hermitian flat bundle is clearly numerically flat. Now from

[
DPS
, Proposition 1.15(ii)]
it follows that any holomorphic vector bundle
admitting a filtration of holomorphic subbundles such that each successive quotient
admits a flat Hermitian structure is also numerically flat. Consequently,
E
is numerically
flat if the three conditions in the theorem hold.

We prove the converse below. Assume that
E
is numerically flat.

Our proof inducts on the rank of
E
. Note that if
S\,\subset\,E
is a holomorphic subbundle then the vector bundle
E/S
is nef because
E
is
nef
[
DPS
, p. 308, Proposition 1.15(i)]
. If
S
is nef, then applying

[
DPS
, p. 308, Proposition 1.15(iii)]
to the exact sequence

0\,\longrightarrow\,(E/S)^{*}\,\longrightarrow\,E^{*}\,\longrightarrow\,S^{*}\,\longrightarrow\,0

we conclude that
(E/S)^{*}
is nef. Therefore,
E/S
is numerically flat if
S
is so.
These observations are crucial for the induction step.

Since
\omega
is assumed to be Gauduchon, we see that for every holomorphic line
bundle
L
,

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits c_{1}(L)\wedge\omega^{n-1}\,\in\,\mathbb R

is independent
of the choice of Chern connection on
L
. Moreover, because
\omega
is
astheno-Kähler, for any
(2,2)
Chern-Weil form
\Phi
, the integral

\intop\nolimits\Phi\wedge\omega^{n-2}\,\in\,\mathbb R

is independent of the choice of
Chern connection, i.e., independent of the Bott-Chern representative.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a coherent analytic subsheaf of
\mathcal{O}(E)
such that
the quotient
\mathcal{O}(E)/\mathcal{W}
is torsion-free. This implies
that
\mathcal{W}
is reflexive
[
Ko
, p. 153, Proposition 4.13]
.
The rank of
\mathcal{W}
will be denoted by
q_{w}
. Then the arguments of
Step
1
in the proof of Theorem 1.18 in
[
DPS
]
go through to show that

\intop\nolimits_{X}[c_{1}(\mathcal{W})]_{BC}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\,\leq\,0
. Now assume that

\intop\nolimits_{X}[c_{1}(\mathcal{W})]_{BC}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\,=\,0\,.

Then we have
the following:

•

\det(\mathcal{W})
, which is a holomorphic line bundle, is Hermitian flat,

•

[c_{1}(\mathcal{W})]_{BC}\,=\,0
, and

•

the homomorphism
\det(\mathcal{W})\,\longrightarrow\,\Lambda^{q_{w}}E
has no nontrivial
zeroes (that is,
\det(\mathcal{W})
is a line subbundle of
\Lambda^{q_{w}}E
).

Since
\det(\mathcal{W})

is a line subbundle of
\Lambda^{q_{w}}E
, it follows that
\mathcal{W}
is a subbundle
of
E
(recall that
\mathcal{O}(E)/\mathcal{W}
is torsion-free); see

[
DPS
, Lemma 1.20]
.

Now suppose
\mathcal{F}\,\subset\,\mathcal{O}(E)
is a minimal rank
q\,>\,0
subsheaf such that
\mathcal{O}(E)/\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free and

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{X}[c_{1}(\mathcal{F})]_{BC}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\,=\,0\,.

(3.3)

As noted before,
\mathcal{F}
is a holomorphic subbundle of
E
satisfying

[c_{1}(\mathcal{F})]_{BC}=0.

(3.4)

We also note that

\mathcal{F}
is stable, because it is of minimal rank satisfying (
3.3
). Therefore, the Kobayashi-Lübke inequality says that

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{X}\left(2q\cdot[c_{2}(\mathcal{F})]_{BC}-(q-1)[c_{1}(\mathcal{F})^{2}]_{BC}\right)\wedge\omega^{n-2}\,\geq\,0\,;

(3.5)

see
[
LYZ
, p. 107]
. We will now show that in (
3.5
) the equality holds.

It was noted earlier that
\mathcal{W}
is a subbundle of
E
if it satisfies
the condition in (
3.3
). Consequently, we get a filtration of holomorphic subbundles

0\,=\,\mathcal{F}_{0}\,\subset\,\mathcal{F}\,=:\,\mathcal{F}_{1}\,\subset\,\mathcal{F}_{2}\,\subset\,\cdots\,\subset\,\mathcal{F}_{m-1}\,\subset\,\mathcal{F}_{m}\,=\,E

such that each successive quotient
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
,
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,m
, satisfies
the condition

\displaystyle[c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})]_{BC}\,=\,0\,,

(3.6)

and
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
is stable. Indeed,
E/\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a bundle satisfying condition (
3.6
) (by the cup product formula for the Chern classes in Bott-Chern cohomology, equation (
3.4
), and the fact that
[c_{1}(E)]_{BC}=0
by remark
3
). The arguments in step 2 of the proof of Theorem 1.18 in
[
DPS
]
show that it is numerically flat. Therefore, we may induct on the rank of
E
to get the above filtration.

Let
q_{i}
be the rank of
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
.
We again have the Kobayashi-Lübke inequality

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{X}\left(2q_{i}\cdot[c_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})]_{BC}-(q_{i}-1)[c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})^{2}]_{BC}\right)\wedge\omega^{n-2}\,\geq\,0

(3.7)

for every
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,m
. Since
[c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})]_{BC}=0
, we see that

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{X}[c_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})]_{BC}\wedge\omega^{n-2}\geq 0.

By the cup product formula (and
[c_{1}(E)^{2}]_{BC}=0
) we see that

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{X}[c_{2}(E)]_{BC}\wedge\omega^{n-2}\geq 0.

Recall that
[c_{2}(E)]_{BC}\,=\,0
by assumption; therefore, we see that equality holds in all
of the above inequalities.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is
\omega
-stable, by Li-Yau and Lübke-Teleman’s generalization
(
[
LY
]
,
[
LT
]
) of the
Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem
[
UY
]
, it follows that
\mathcal{F}
admits a Hermite-Einstein
metric. Since the inequality in (
3.5
) is an equality, the
Hermite-Einstein metric on
\mathcal{F}
is flat and hence has vanishing Chern classes in the Bott-Chern cohomology. Now we take the quotient
E/\mathcal{F}
and
use the induction hypothesis. Note that the remarks made earlier show that the quotient is numerically flat, and the cup product formula shows that it has vanishing
[c_{2}]_{BC}
.

\square

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

Any representation
W
of a finite group
G
has the property that there are two distinct
polynomials
f
and
g
with nonnegative integral coefficients such that the two

G
-modules
f(W)
and
g(W)
are isomorphic. Indeed, this follows from the
following two facts

•

any finite dimension
G
-module is a direct sum of irreducible

G
-modules, and

The following lemma shows that the holomorphic vector bundle
\pi^{*}E
in Lemma

4
is trivial.

If
E
is a finite holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
on a compact complex manifold

Y
such that there is a filtration of holomorphic subbundles

0\,=\,E_{0}\,\subset\,E_{1}\,\subset\,\cdots\,\subset\,E_{r-1}\,\subset\,E_{r}\,=\,E

(4.15)

satisfying the conditions that
{\rm rank}(E_{i})\,=\,i
, and
the holomorphic line bundle
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
is trivial
for all
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,r
. Then
E
is holomorphically trivial.

Since
E
is finite, there are finitely many indecomposable holomorphic vector bundles

V_{1},\,\cdots,\,V_{m}
such that

E^{\otimes i}\,=\,\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m}c_{i,j}V_{j}

(4.16)

for all
i\,\geq\,1
, where
c_{i,j}
are nonnegative integers. By Lemma
2
the
V_{i}
can be chosen to be finite bundles themselves. Given that

E_{i}/E_{i-1}
is trivial for all
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,r
, each vector bundle

E^{\otimes i}
admits a filtration of holomorphic subbundles such that
each successive quotient of it is the trivial line bundle. Akin to the proof of
Lemma
4
, by the (weak) uniqueness of the graded object of a Jordan-Hölder
filtration in the sense of Proposition 2.1 of
[
BTT
]
and from Theorem 3 of
[
At
]
we see that the
V_{i}
also admit such filtrations.

When
r\,=\,1
, there is nothing to prove. Next we consider the case of rank two.
Assume that

(1)

r\,=\,\text{rank}(E)\,=\,2
, and

(2)

E
is not trivial.

Therefore,
E
fits in a short exact sequence of holomorphic vector bundles

0\,\longrightarrow\,E_{1}\,=\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\,\longrightarrow\,E\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\,=\,E/E_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,0\,,

(4.17)

which does not split holomorphically, because
E
is not trivial.

For any integer
i\,\geq\,1
, let
\text{Sym}^{i}(E)
denote the
i
-th symmetric
power of
E
; it is a direct summand of
E^{\otimes i}
, meaning
\text{Sym}^{i}(E)\,\subset\,E^{\otimes i}
, and there is a holomorphic subbundle
S_{i}\,\subset\,E^{\otimes i}
such that the natural homomorphism

\text{Sym}^{i}(E)\oplus S_{i}\,\longrightarrow\,E^{\otimes i}

is an isomorphism. From this it follows that there are
finitely many vector bundles
\{V^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}
from the collection
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
such that

\text{Sym}^{i}(E)\,=\,\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}c^{\prime}_{i,j}V^{\prime}_{j}

(4.18)

for all
i\,\geq\,1
, where
c^{\prime}_{i,j}
are nonnegative integers. Indeed, since the
V_{j}

are indecomposable, this follows from Theorem 3 of
[
At
]
. Recall that each
V^{\prime}_{j}

admits a filtration of holomorphic subbundles such that each
successive quotient of it is the trivial line bundle. In particular, we have

\dim H^{0}(Y,\,V^{\prime}_{j})\,\geq\,1\,,\ \ \forall\ 1\leq\,j\,\leq\,m^{\prime}\,.

(4.19)

We will now show that

\dim H^{0}(Y,\,\text{Sym}^{n}(E))\,=\,1\,,\ \ \forall\ n\,\geq\,1\,.

(4.20)

Let

\tau\,\in\,H^{1}(Y,\,{\rm Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{Y},\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}))\,=\,H^{1}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})

be the extension class for the short exact sequence in (
4.17
). We have

\tau\,\neq\,0

because (
4.17
) does not split holomorphically. Let

0\,\longrightarrow\,H^{0}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,H^{0}(Y,\,E)\,\longrightarrow\,H^{0}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,H^{1}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})

(4.21)

be the long exact sequence of cohomologies for the short exact sequence in (
4.17
).
Since the homomorphism
\varphi
in (
4.21
) sends
1\,\in\,H^{0}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,=\,\mathbb C
to
\tau\,\in\,H^{1}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})
(this follows from the construction
of the extension class), we conclude that
\alpha
in (
4.21
) is
an isomorphism. Therefore, (
4.20
) holds for
m\,=\,1
.

Now we will prove (
4.20
) using induction on
m
.

Suppose (
4.20
) holds for all
n\,\leq\,n_{0}-1
, with
n_{0}\,\geq\,2
. To
prove (
4.20
) for
n\,=\,n_{0}
, consider
the short exact sequence of holomorphic vector bundles

0\,\longrightarrow\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}-1}(E)\,\longrightarrow\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}}(E)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma^{\otimes n_{0}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}}({\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,=\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\,\longrightarrow\,0\,,

where
\gamma
is the projection in (
4.17
).
The above inclusion of
\text{Sym}^{n_{0}-1}(E)
in
\text{Sym}^{n_{0}}(E)
is given by the
natural inclusion
E^{\otimes(n_{0}-1)}\otimes E_{1}\,\hookrightarrow\,E^{\otimes(n_{0}-1)}\otimes E\,=\,E^{\otimes n_{0}}
(see (
4.17
)). Let

0\,\longrightarrow\,H^{0}(Y,\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}-1}(E))\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,H^{0}(Y,\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}}(E))\,\longrightarrow\,H^{0}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,H^{1}(Y,\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}-1}(E))

(4.22)

be the long exact sequence of cohomologies associated to the above short
exact sequence of vector bundles. Now consider the composition

(\gamma^{\otimes(n_{0}-1)})_{*}\circ\varphi^{\prime}\,:\,H^{0}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,\longrightarrow\,H^{1}(Y,\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}-1}({\mathcal{O}}_{Y}))\,=\,H^{1}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,,

where
\varphi^{\prime}
is the homomorphism in (
4.22
), and

\gamma^{\otimes(n_{0}-1)}\,:\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}-1}(U)\,\longrightarrow\,\text{Sym}^{n_{0}-1}({\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,=\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}

is the homomorphism given by
\gamma
in (
4.17
). This

(\gamma^{\otimes(n_{0}-1)})_{*}\circ\varphi^{\prime}
sends
1\,\in\,H^{0}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\,=\,\mathbb C
to
(n_{0}-1)\tau\,\in\,H^{1}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})
, which is nonzero because

\tau\,\neq\,0
. So, the homomorphism
(\gamma^{\otimes(n_{0}-1)})_{*}\circ\varphi^{\prime}
is injective,
implying that
\varphi^{\prime}
is injective. Consequently, the homomorphism
\beta
in (
4.22
)
is an isomorphism. This completes the proof of (
4.20
).

From (
4.18
), (
4.20
) and (
4.19
) it follows that

\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}c^{\prime}_{i,j}\,\leq\,1
for all
i\,\geq\,0
. This implies that

i+1\,=\,\text{rank}(\text{Sym}^{i}(E))\,\leq\,\max_{1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}\ \text{rank}(V^{\prime}_{j})\,,\ \ \forall\ i\,\geq\,1\,.

In view of this contradiction we conclude that the lemma holds for
r\,=\,2
.

We will now prove the general case.

Assume that

(1)

r\,=\,\text{rank}(E)\,>\,2
, and

(2)

E
is not trivial.

We will show that there is a subbundle
F\,\subset\,E

with
2\,\leq\,\text{rank}(F)\,=\,s\,\leq\,r\,=\,\text{rank}(E)
, and a filtration of
holomorphic subbundles

0\,=\,F_{0}\,\subset\,F_{1}\,\subset\,\cdots\,\subset\,F_{s-1}\,\subset\,F_{s}\,=\,F\,,

(4.23)

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\text{rank}(F_{i})\,=\,i
for all
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,s
,

(2)

the holomorphic line bundles
F_{i}/F_{i-1}
,
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,s
, are
trivial, and

(3)

the rank two holomorphic vector bundle
F/F_{s-2}
is not trivial.

To construct a filtration as in (
4.23
), take
s
to be the smallest positive integer
such that the vector bundle
E_{s}
in (
4.15
) is nontrivial. So
s\,\geq\,2
,
and the vector bundle
E_{s-1}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus(s-1)}_{Y}
.
Moreover,
E_{s}
fits in a short exact sequence of holomorphic vector bundles

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus(s-1)}_{Y}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{s}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\,=\,E_{s}/E_{s-1}\,\longrightarrow\,0\,.

The corresponding extension class

\delta\,:=\,(\delta_{1},\,\cdots,\,\delta_{s-1})\,\in\,H^{1}(Y,\,\text{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{Y},\,{\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus(s-1)}_{Y}))\,=\,H^{1}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})^{\oplus(s-1)}

is nonzero, where
\delta_{i}\,\in\,H^{1}(Y,\,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})
,
because
E_{s}
is nontrivial, implying that the above short exact sequence of vector
bundles does not split holomorphically. Choose an integer
j_{0}\,\in\,[1,\,s-1]
such that

\delta_{j_{0}}\,\neq\,0
. Now in (
4.23
), take

•

F_{i}\,=\,E_{i}
for all
0\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,j_{0}-1
(see (
4.15
)),

•

F_{i}\,=\,E_{i+1}
for all
j_{0}\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,s-2
,

•

F_{s-1}\,=\,E_{j_{0}}
, and

•

F\,=\,F_{s}\,=\,E_{s}
.

This filtration clearly satisfies all the three conditions.

Note that for all
i\,\geq\,1
, the symmetric product
\text{Sym}^{i}(F)
is a
subbundle of
\text{Sym}^{i}(E)
, and
\text{Sym}^{i}(F/F_{s-2})
is a quotient of

\text{Sym}^{i}(F)
. Since
F/F_{s-2}
is a nontrivial extension of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
by

{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
, we have

\dim H^{0}(Y,\,\text{Sym}^{i}(F/F_{s-2}))\,=\,1\,,\ \ \forall\ i\,\geq\,1\,,

(4.24)

as proved in (

) only uses that the

Since

is indecomposable for all

. Consequently, the

\text{Sym}^{i}(E)
are of arbitrarily large rank

goes to infinity. Since
\text{Sym}^{i}(E)
is a direct summand of

, the

are also of arbitrarily large rank as
i
goes to

4.16
) it follows that these ranks are bounded by
\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\ \text{rank}(V_{j})
.

In view of the above contradiction, the lemma is proved.

The vector bundle
\pi^{*}E
in Lemma
4
is finite because
E
is finite.
So from Lemma
4
we know that
\pi^{*}E
is holomorphically
trivial. Let
D
be a connection on
\pi^{*}E
given by a trivialization of it (this
connection does not depend on the choice of the trivialization). Let

\Gamma\,:=\,\text{Gal}(\pi)
be the Galois group for the Galois covering
\pi
. For
any
g\,\in\,\Gamma
, the vector bundle
g^{*}\pi^{*}E
is trivial, and any isomorphism
between
\pi^{*}E
and
g^{*}\pi^{*}E
takes the connection
D
on
\pi^{*}E
to the
connection
g^{*}D
on
g^{*}\pi^{*}E
. Therefore,
D
descends to a connection on
E
;
this descended connection will be denoted by

. We note that
D_{E}
is flat
because
D
is so. The monodromy representation for

clearly factors through the

\pi_{1}(X)\,\longrightarrow\,\Gamma
. Therefore,
E
admits a flat
connection with finite monodromy group. This completes the proof of Theorem

.

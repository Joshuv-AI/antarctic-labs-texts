Dual Bounded Generation: Polynomial, Second-order Cone and Positive Semidefinite Matrix Inequalities
Khaled Elbassioni

Abstract
In the monotone integer dualization problem, we are given two sets of vectors in an integer box such that no vector in the first set is dominated by a vector in the second. The question is to check if the two sets of vectors cover the entire integer box by upward and downward domination, respectively. It is known that the problem is (quasi-)polynomially equivalent to that of enumerating all maximal feasible solutions of a given monotone system of linear/separable/supermodular inequalities over integer vectors. The equivalence is established via showing that the dual family of minimal infeasible vectors has size bounded by a (quasi-)polynomial in the sizes of the family to be generated and the input description. Continuing in this line of work, in this paper, we consider systems of polynomial, second-order cone, and semidefinite inequalities. We give sufficient conditions under which such bounds can be established and highlight some applications.

\displaystyle f_{i}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{1}^{\sigma_{i}(k)}-\mathbf{1}^{\sigma_{i}(j)})-f(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\quad\qquad a^{i}_{\sigma_{i}(k)}-a^{i}_{\sigma_{i}(j)}+\frac{\Phi^{-1}(\alpha_{i})\left[(d^{i}_{\sigma_{i}(k)})^{2}-(d^{i}_{\sigma_{i}(j)})^{2}\right]}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\sqrt{\sum_{j^{\prime}}(d^{i}_{j^{\prime}})^{2}x_{j^{\prime}}+(d^{i}_{\sigma_{i}(k)})^{2}-(d^{i}_{\sigma_{i}(j)})^{2}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\sqrt{\sum_{j^{\prime}}(d^{i}_{j^{\prime}})^{2}x_{j^{\prime}}}}}}\geq 0.

Thus, we can use Lemma

for any subset
{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
of the maximal feasible solutions for (

Chance-constrained covering binary programs.

In a similar setting as in the previous example, we are given
n
-dimensional normally distributed random vectors
\mathbf{w}^{i}\sim N(\mathbf{a}^{i},\Sigma^{i})
, for
i\in[r]
, and demands
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Here,
w^{i}_{j}
can be interpreted as the coverage value for the
j
th item with respect to the
i
th demand. The requirement is to select a subset of the items, such that the
i
th demand constraint is satisfied with probability at least
\alpha_{i}\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Pr[(\mathbf{w}^{i})^{\top}\mathbf{x}\geq~t_{i}]\geq\alpha_{i},\quad\text{ for }i\in[r],

(12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}.

As before, we can reformulate the constraints (
8
) as a second-order cone program:

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\mathbf{a}^{i})^{\top}\mathbf{x}+\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha_{i})\sqrt{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\Sigma^{i}\mathbf{x}}\geq t_{i},\quad\text{ for }i\in[r]:=\{1,\ldots,r\},

(13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}.

In the special case when the random variables are independent, that is, when
\Sigma^{i}:=(D^{i})^{2}:=\operatorname{Diag}((d^{i}_{1})^{2},\ldots,

(d^{i}_{n})^{2})
is a full-rank
diagonal
matrix, and
\alpha_{i}\leq 0.5
and hence
\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha_{i})\geq 0
, we can enumerate the family
\mathcal{G}
of minimal feasible solutions for the system (
12
) by using the result in (B4). Indeed, let

f_{i}(\mathbf{x}):=R_{i}-(\mathbf{a}^{i})^{\top}(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{x})-\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha_{i})\|D^{i}(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{x})\|,

where
R_{i}:=(\mathbf{a}^{i})^{\top}\mathbf{1}+\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha_{i})\sqrt{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\Sigma^{i}\mathbf{1}}
, and
\mathbf{1}
is
n
-dimensional the vector of all ones. Then, for any
\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
s.t.
x_{j}=0
,

\displaystyle f_{i}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{1}^{j})-f_{i}(\mathbf{x})=a^{i}_{j}+\frac{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha_{i})(d^{i}_{j})^{2}}{\sqrt{\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}(d^{i}_{j^{\prime}})^{2}(1-x_{j^{\prime}})+(d^{i}_{j})^{2}}+\sqrt{\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}(d^{i}_{j^{\prime}})^{2}(1-x_{j^{\prime}})}},

(14)

which is monotone increasing
\mathbf{x}
. Supermodularity follows by Proposition
1
below. Next we bound the traction
\tau_{i}
and maximum range
R_{i}
of each
f_{i}
. From (

), we get
\tau_{i}\geq\min\left\{a_{\min},\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha_{i})\frac{d_{\min}^{2}}{2\sqrt{n}d_{\max}}\right\}
,
where
a_{\min}=\min_{i,j}\{a^{i}_{j}~|~a^{i}_{j}>0\}
,
d_{\min}=\min_{i,j}\{d^{i}_{j}~|~d^{i}_{j}>0\}
and
d_{\max}=\max_{i,j}d^{i}_{j}
. Similarly, we have

,
\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha_{i})
and

are bounded by quasi-polynomials
in

Quantum hypergraph covers.

A quantum hypergraph
[
AW02
,
WX08
]
is a pair
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space and each element
j\in\mathcal{E}:=[n]
is identified with a Hermitian operator
A_{j}
over
\mathcal{V}
satisfying
0\preceq A_{j}\preceq\mathbf{I}_{d}
, with
\mathbf{I}_{d}
denoting the
d
-dimensional identity operator over
\mathcal{V}
and ”
\succeq
“ denoting the
Löwner
(partial) order on Hermitian operators:
A\succeq B
if and only if
A-B
is positive semidefinite. A
cover
of
\mathcal{H}
is a subset
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}A_{j}\succeq\mathbf{I}_{d}
.
This notion arises in the area of quantum information theory
[
AW02
]
. Note that a minimal quantum hypergraph cover is a minimal feasible solution for the inequality:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}x_{j}\succeq~\mathbf{I}_{d},

(15)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}.

Assuming feasibility of (
15
), we must have
T:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}-\mathbf{I}_{d}\succeq 0
. It follows then that the minimal quantum hypergraph covers are in one-to-one correspondence with the maximal feasible binary solutions for the inequality
\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}x_{j}\preceq T
, which is of the form considered in (B8). It is not difficult to see that the result in (B8) can be extended to the case when the matrices

are Hermitian positive semidefinite, while only increasing the dual bound by a factor of at most
2
in the exponent (that is, we get
q(\mathcal{L},|{\mathcal{Y}}|)\leq O(n)^{4d+1}r|{\mathcal{Y}}|
). We conclude that, if the dimension
d
is fixed, then all minimal covers for a quantum hypergraph can be enumerated in quasi-polynomial time.

Supermodular Inequalities

Given a function
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, one can check if
f
is supermodular using the following statement, generalizing the well-known characterization of supermodular functions over the Boolean cube
[
Lov83
]
.

Proposition 1
.

A function
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{R}
is supermodular if and only if, for any
j\in[n]
, for any
z\in\mathcal{C}_{j}\setminus\{c_{j}\}
, and
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{C}_{j-1}\times\{z\}\times\mathcal{C}_{j+1}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{C}_{k}
,
the difference

\displaystyle\partial_{f}(\mathbf{x},j,z):=f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{1}^{j})-f(\mathbf{x}),

is monotone in
\mathbf{x}
.

We include the proof in the appendix for completeness.
Define the
“traction”
of
f
, denoted by
\tau(f)
, to be the minimum possible positive increase in
f
corresponding to a minimal change in the variables
along one coordinate
:

\displaystyle\tau(f):=\min_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j\in[n],~\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C},~x_{j}<c_{j}}}{{f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{1}^{j})>f(\mathbf{x})}}}f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{1}^{j})-f(\mathbf{x}).

(16)

Consider a monotone inequality (
5
), where the function
f
is supermodular, and let
\mathcal{F}
denote the family of all its maximal feasible vectors.
We can extend the result in
[
BEGK03
]
as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Consider the inequality (
5
) and suppose that
f:\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a monotone supermodular function with traction
\tau
and maximum range
R:=f(\mathbf{c})
. Then for any subset
{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
of size
|{\mathcal{Y}}|\geq 2
, it holds that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{Y}})\cap{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{F})|\leq|{\mathcal{Y}}|^{o(\log\frac{R-t}{\tau})}.

(17)

Before we prove Theorem
1
, we need some preliminaries.
Given
{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{X}:={\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{Y}})\cap{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{F})
,
we follow the proof in
[
BEGK03
]
by constructing a binary tree
\mathbf{T}
, in which each leaf
l\in L(\mathbf{T})
is mapped to an element
\mathbf{x}^{l}\in\mathcal{X}
, and each internal node
v
is associated with the element
\mathbf{x}^{v}=\bigvee_{l\in L(\mathbf{T}(v))}\mathbf{x}^{l}
; here,
\mathbf{T}(v)
denotes the binary sub-tree of
\mathbf{T}
rooted at node
v
, and
L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime})
denotes the set of leaves of the subtree
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
. Such a mapping
\phi:L(\mathbf{T})\to\mathcal{X}
is said to be
proper
if it assigns different elements to different leaves, and if
f(\mathbf{x}^{u}\wedge\mathbf{x}^{v})\leq t
whenever
u
and
v
are incomparable
nodes of
\mathbf{T}
(that is, when the sub-trees
\mathbf{T}(u)
and
\mathbf{T}(v)
are disjoint). As shown in
[
BEGK03
]
, a sufficiently large binary tree admitting a proper mapping can always be constructed:

Lemma 2

(Lemma 11 in
[
BEGK03
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be an antichain of size
|\mathcal{A}|\geq 2
in an integral box
\mathcal{C}
and let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{A})
. Then there exists a binary tree
\mathbf{T}
and a proper mapping
\phi:L(\mathbf{T})\to\mathcal{B}
such that
|L(\mathbf{T})|\geq|\mathcal{B}|^{1/o(\log|\mathcal{A}|)}.

To prove the theorem, we consider a partition of
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}
, where
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}:~f(\mathbf{x})\geq t+\frac{\tau}{2}\}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{Y}})
and
\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}:=\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{Y}})
, and proper mappings
\phi^{\prime}:L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime})\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}:L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime})\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}
, defined respectively on two binary trees
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime}
, as guaranteed by Lemma
2
.
Consequently, the theorem
will follow
from the following extension of Lemma 1 in
[
BEGK03
]
.

Lemma 3
.

Given binary trees
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime}
and proper mappings
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
as above, we have

|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime})|\leq\frac{2(R-t)}{\tau}\text{ and }|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime})|\leq\frac{2(R-t)}{\tau}+1.

(18)

Proof.

Consider first the tree
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
and the mapping
\phi^{\prime}
. We show by induction that

f(\mathbf{x}^{w})\geq t+\frac{\tau}{2}|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(w))|.

(19)

holds for every node
w
of the binary tree
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
. Since
f(\mathbf{x}^{w})\leq R
, it follows that

|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(w))|\leq\frac{2(R-t)}{\tau}

which, when applied to the root of
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
, proves the first part of the lemma. To see
(
19
), let us apply (backward) induction on the level of the node
w
in
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
.
Clearly, if
w=l
is a leaf of
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
, then
|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(l))|=1
, and (
19
) follows by the assumption that
\mathbf{x}^{l}\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.
Let us assume now that
w
is a node of
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
with
u
and
v
as its immediate successors.
Then
|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(w))|=|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(u))|+|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime}(v))|
, and
\mathbf{x}^{w}=\mathbf{x}^{u}\vee\mathbf{x}^{v}
.
By our inductive hypothesis, and since
f
is supermodular and
f(\mathbf{x}^{u}\wedge\mathbf{x}^{v})\leq t
, we have
the inequalities

\begin{array}[]{rl}f(\mathbf{x}^{u}\vee\mathbf{x}^{v})&\geq f(\mathbf{x}^{u})+f(\mathbf{x}^{v})-f(\mathbf{x}^{u}\wedge\mathbf{x}^{v})\\
&\geq t+\frac{\tau}{2}|L(\mathbf{T}(u))|+t+\frac{\tau}{2}|L(\mathbf{T}(v))|-t\\
&=t+\frac{\tau}{2}|L(\mathbf{T}(w))|.\end{array}

Consider next the tree
\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime}
and the mapping
\phi^{\prime\prime}
.
We prove by induction that

f(\mathbf{x}^{w})\geq t-\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime}(w))|.

(20)

holds for every node
w
of the binary tree
\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime}
. If
w=l
is a leaf node, then (
20
) holds as
|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime}(l))|=1
, and
f(\mathbf{x}^{l})>t
. If
w
is a node of
\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime}
with as immediate successors
u
and
v
, then (as
f(\mathbf{x}^{u})>t
and
f(\mathbf{x}^{v})>t

f(\mathbf{x}^{u}\wedge\mathbf{x}^{v})\leq t
), there must exist a
j\in[n]
such that
\mathbf{x}^{u}\wedge\mathbf{x}^{v}\leq\mathbf{x}^{v}-\mathbf{1}^{j}
. The definition of
\tau
and the fact that
\mathbf{x}^{v}\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}
imply that
f(\mathbf{x}^{v}-\mathbf{1}^{j})\leq f(\mathbf{x}^{v})-\tau<t+\frac{\tau}{2}-\tau=t-\frac{\tau}{2}
. It follows from this and the inductive hypothesis that

\begin{array}[]{rl}f(\mathbf{x}^{u}\vee\mathbf{x}^{v})&\geq f(\mathbf{x}^{u})+f(\mathbf{x}^{v})-f(\mathbf{x}^{u}\wedge\mathbf{x}^{v})\\
&\geq f(\mathbf{x}^{u})+f(\mathbf{x}^{v})-f(\mathbf{x}^{v}-\mathbf{1}^{j})\\
&\geq t-\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}|L(\mathbf{T}(u))|+t-\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}|L(\mathbf{T}(v))|-(t{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\frac{\tau}{2})\\
&=t-\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}|L(\mathbf{T}(w))|.\end{array}

Applying (
20
) to the root of
\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime}
establishes the second part of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

It follows from the above two lemmas that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{Y}})\cap{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{F})|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{X}^{\prime}|+|\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}|\leq|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime})|^{o(\log|{\mathcal{Y}}|)}+|L(\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime})|^{o(\log|{\mathcal{Y}}|)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{2(R-t)}{\tau}+1\right)^{o(\log|{\mathcal{Y}}|)}.

∎

Products of Affine Functions

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
be affine functions given in the form:
p_{k}(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{a}^{k})^{\top}\mathbf{x}+a_{0}^{k}
, where
\mathbf{a}^{k}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{n}
are given vectors and
a_{k}^{0}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
are given numbers. Given
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
, we are interested in enumerating the family
\mathcal{G}
of all
minimal feasible
vectors for the inequality
g(\mathbf{x})\geq t
over
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
g(\mathbf{x}):=\prod_{k=1}^{m}p_{k}(\mathbf{x})
.
We may assume without loss of generality that
\mathbf{a}^{k}_{j},t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
for all
k,j
.
Although
g(\mathbf{x})
defines a polynomial, we cannot (directly) apply the result in (B6) since the number of terms
s
(and possibly also the maximum degree
d
) is exponential in
m
. Instead, we consider the function

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}):=R-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\log\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x}),

(21)

where
R:=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\log\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{c})
,
\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{x}):=p_{k}(\mathbf{x})+\epsilon
, and

\displaystyle\epsilon:=\frac{1}{2m(1+\max_{k}p_{k}(\mathbf{c}))^{m-1}}

(22)

is a sufficiently small perturbation parameter that is needed to ensure that the range of the function
f
remains bounded (which might fail to hold without perturbation when
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{c}
).
It is not difficult to see that the family
\mathcal{F}
of maximal feasible solutions for the inequality
f(\mathbf{x})\leq t^{\prime}:=R-\log t
is in one-to-one correspondence with
\mathcal{G}
.
Indeed, given
\mathbf{x}
such
g(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})\geq t
, it is immediate that
R-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\log\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})\leq t^{\prime}
, as
\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})\geq p_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})
. Conversely, given
\mathbf{x}
such that
f(\mathbf{x})\leq t^{\prime}
, it holds that
\prod_{k=1}^{m}\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})\geq t
. As

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{m}\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{m}p_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})+\epsilon\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq[m]~:~|S|<m}\epsilon^{m-1-|S|}\prod_{k\in S}p_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=1}^{m}p_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})+\epsilon\cdot m(\epsilon+\max_{k}p_{k}(\mathbf{c}))^{m-1},

we get by our choice (
22
) of
\epsilon
that
\prod_{k=1}^{m}p_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})\geq t-\frac{1}{2}
, which in turn implies that
g(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})\geq t
by the integrality assumption.

To enumerate the elements of
\mathcal{F}
in quasi-polynomial time, it would be enough by (B4) to show that the function
f
is supermodular and to bound both the inverse of the traction
\tau
of
f
and the difference
f(\mathbf{c})-t^{\prime}
by polynomials in the input size. To see that
f
is supermodular, we apply Proposition
1
. For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}
with
x_{j}<c_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{1}^{j})-f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{k}\log\frac{\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x})}{\overline{p}_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{1}^{j})}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\sum_{k}}}\log\left(1+\frac{a_{j}^{k}}{p_{k}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{1}^{j})+\epsilon}\right),

(23)

which is monotone increasing in
\mathbf{x}
.

It follows also from (
23
) that the traction of
f
can be bounded from below by

\displaystyle\min_{k,~j~|~a_{j}^{k}>0}\log\left(\frac{a_{j}^{k}}{(\mathbf{a}^{k})^{\top}\mathbf{c}+a_{0}^{k}+\epsilon}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}}\right)}},

where
\mathcal{L}
is the total encoding length of the coefficients of the given functions. It remains to bound the difference
f(\mathbf{c})-t^{\prime}
, which can be done as follows:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{c})-t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\log t-\sum_{k}\log(p_{k}(\mathbf{0})+\epsilon)\leq\log{t}+m\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log t+m^{2}\log(2m(1+\max_{k}p_{k}(\mathbf{c})))=O(\mathcal{L}^{2}).

Thus, we arrive at the following result.

Corollary 1
.

Consider an inequality
\prod_{k=1}^{m}p_{k}(\mathbf{x})\geq t
where each
p_{k}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
is an affine function. Then for any subset
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
of the family
\mathcal{G}
of minimal feasible solutions of size
|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\geq 2
, it holds that
5
5

5

Recall that, for any
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
,
{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\mathcal{B})
denotes the family of all maximal non-dominating elements of
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathcal{C}
.

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\mathcal{X})\cap{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(\mathcal{G})|\leq|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|^{o(\log\mathcal{L})},

(24)

where
\mathcal{L}
is the total encoding length of the coefficients of the given functions.

Note that the introduction of the function (

) is merely for the purpose of proving dual-boundedness of the family

, but is not actually used it the enumeration algorithm.

It is worth noting that the enumeration of minimal feasible solutions for a single inequality involving a product of linear functions over binary vectors is as hard as (and hence polynomially equivalent to) the well known

Separable Monotone Functions and Polynomial Inequalities

A monotone function
f:\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
is said to be
separable
if
f(\mathbf{x})
can be written as the product of single-variable non-negative monotone functions. For instance, a polynomial function of the form (
2
) is the sum of separable monotone functions. A single-variable function
g:{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\{0,1,\ldots,c\}}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is said to be
discretely convex
if for all
x,y\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\{0,1,\ldots,c\}}}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\lambda g(x)+(1-\lambda)g(y)\geq\min_{u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\{0,1,\ldots,c\}}}:~|\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y-u|\leq 1}g(u).

A sufficient and necessary condition for discrete convexity is that (see, e.g.,
[
Yüc02
]
)
the difference
\partial_{g}(x)=g(x+1)-g(x)
is monotone in
x{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\in\{0,1,\ldots,c-1\}}}
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the sum of separable monotone functions, that is,

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}a_{H}\prod_{j\in H}f_{j}^{H}(x_{j}),\quad\text{ for $\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}$},

(25)

where
\mathcal{H}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is a given multiset family (repetitions allowed),
f_{j}^{H}:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
are non-negative monotone functions and
a_{H}>0
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}
.

Applying Proposition
1
to the function
f
defined in (
25
), we see that a sufficient condition for
f
to be supermodular is that the difference function
\partial_{f_{j}^{H}}(x_{j})
is monotone in
x_{j}
, or equivalently,
f_{j}^{H}
is discretely convex, for all
j\in[n]
, as

\displaystyle\partial_{f}(\mathbf{x},j,z)=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}~|~j\in H}a_{H}\big(f_{j}^{H}(x_{j}+1)-f_{j}^{H}(x_{j})\big)\prod_{j^{\prime}\in H\setminus\{j\}}f_{j^{\prime}}^{H}(x_{j^{\prime}})

is monotone whenever
\partial_{f_{j}^{H}}(x_{j})=f_{j}(x_{j}+1)-f_{j}(x_{j})
is monotone in
x_{j}
. As a corollary of this and the bound mentioned in (B1) above, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2
.

Consider the inequality (
5
) and suppose that the function
f:\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
has traction

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}):=\|A\mathbf{x}\|+\mathbf{b}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\leq~t,

Proposition 5

Let

be the set of maximal feasible vectors for (

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{I}}({\mathcal{Y}})\cap{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{F})|\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{n(n+1)}}(2|{\mathcal{Y}}|+1)^{2}.

Proof.

When
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0}

, where

When

57
) by
w(u)
and summing up the resulting inequalities over
u\in U_{2}
, we get by the threshold separability of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
,

\displaystyle t_{2}|\mathcal{S}|\leq\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}w(S)

\displaystyle\leq w(U_{1})+\sum_{u\in U_{2}}w(u)|\mathcal{S}(u)|\leq w(U_{1})+\sum_{u\in U_{2}}w(u)\frac{w(U)-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}|\mathcal{T}(u)|

\displaystyle=w(U_{1})+\frac{w(U)-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}w(T)\leq w(U_{1})+\frac{w(U)-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\alpha|\mathcal{T}|

\displaystyle\leq w(U)-\alpha+\frac{w(U)-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\alpha|\mathcal{T}|,

(58)

where
\alpha\leq t_{1}
. Note that the right hand side of (
58
) is monotone
non-decreasing
in

and hence is maximized at

. It follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}|\leq\frac{w(U)-t_{1}}{t_{2}}+\frac{w(U)-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\cdot\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|\mathcal{T}|.

(59)

Using

Proof of Proposition
4
.

The result follows from the following reduction from the so-called

relay cuts
enumeration problem in a relay circuit with two terminals.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with vertex set
V
and edge set
E
, and two distinguished vertices
s,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{g}}\in V
.
To each edge in
e\in E
, is assigned a relay
j(e)\in[n]
from a given set
of relays
[n]
(two or more distinct edges may be assigned identical relays).
Let
\mathcal{G}
be the family of all minimal
s
-
g
relay cuts
, i.e., minimal subsets of relays
that disconnect
s
and
g
. It is known that the problem of incrementally generating
\mathcal{G}
is NP-hard, see
[
GK99
]
. We define a polynomial
f:\{0,1\}\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}}
as follows. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of
walks
between
s
and
g
of length
|V|
in
G
. We associate a variable
x_{j}
to each relay
j\in[n]
, and for
\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we let

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\prod_{e\in P}x_{j(e)}.

(60)

Given
\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, we can compute
f(\mathbf{x})

in polynomial time (this requires only computing the
|V|
-th
power of the adjacency
matrix of the graph
G(\mathbf{x})
obtained from
G
by deleting all edges
e\in E
with

.
In particular
, checking
if
f(\mathbf{x})\leq 0
is equivalent to checking if there is
no

s
-
g
path in

).
This gives a polynomial time evaluation oracle for
f
. Finally, note
that minimal
s
-
g
relay
cuts are exactly the complements of the maximal feasible solutions
\mathcal{F}
of the polynomial
inequality

.
∎

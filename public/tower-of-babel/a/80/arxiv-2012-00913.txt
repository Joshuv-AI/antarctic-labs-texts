Chromatic symmetric functions of Dyck paths and q q -rook theory
Laura Colmenarejo \addressmark 1, Alejandro H. Morales \addressmark 1, Greta Panova \addressmark 2

Abstract
The chromatic symmetric function (CSF) of Dyck paths of Stanley and its Shareshian–Wachs q q -analogue have important connections to Hessenberg varieties, diagonal harmonics and LLT polynomials. In the case of, so called, abelian Dyck paths they are also curiously related to placements of non-attacking rooks by results of Stanley–Stembridge (1993) and Guay-Paquet (2013). For the q q -analogue, these results have been generalized by Abreu–Nigro (2020) and Guay-Paquet (private communication), using q q -hit numbers, which are a variant of the ones introduced by Garsia and Remmel. Among our main results is a new proof of Guay-Paquet’s elegant identity expressing the q q -CSFs in a CSF basis with q q -hit coefficients. We further show its equivalence to the Abreu–Nigro identity expanding the q q -CSF in the elementary symmetric functions.

Introduction

Let

(1)

where
\mathbb{P}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
,
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
, and

Stanley and Stembridge
[

conjectured that the chromatic symmetric
functions expand with positive coefficients in the basis
\{e_{\lambda}\}

of elementary symmetric functions for the following graphs. Given a Dyck path

of

with

[

]
.

Let

2
Background on
q
-rook theory

For the rest of the paper, we assume
m
and
n
are non-negative integers with
m\geq n
.

2.1
q
-rook numbers

Definition 2.1

(
q
-rook numbers
[
8
]
)
.

Given a partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
inside an
n\times m
board, the
Garsia-Remmel
q
-rook numbers

are defined as
R_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{p}q^{\inv(p)}
,
where the sum is over all placements
p
of
k
non-attacking rooks on
\lambda
and

\inv(p)
is the number of cells of
\lambda
left after each rook cancels its cell, the cells North in its column and the cells West in its row (see Figure
1
).

Proposition 2.2

(Garsia-Remmel
[
8
]
)
.

Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
we have that

F(x;\lambda):=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}R_{k}(\lambda)[x]_{\ell-k}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}[x+\lambda_{\ell-i+1}-i+1].

(2)

Lemma 2.3
.

Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
we have that

q^{\lambda_{1}}[x]\frac{F(x-1;\lambda)}{F(x;\lambda)}=[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}]-\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{\lambda_{1}-j}\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}.

Proof.

We use induction on
\ell(\lambda)
and apply Proposition
2.2
. For
\ell(\lambda)=1
, we have

[x]\frac{F(x-1;\lambda)}{F(x;\lambda)}=[x-1]+\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{-j}-q^{-j}\frac{[x+\lambda_{1}-1]}{[x+\lambda_{1}]}=q^{-\lambda_{1}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{1}-1]}{[x+\lambda_{1}]}([x+\lambda_{1}]-[\lambda_{1}]).

Next, expanding the RHS of the above identity and doing standard manipulations gives

[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}]-\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}q^{\lambda_{1}-j}\prod_{t=1}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}=\\
=q^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{1}-\ell]}{[x+\lambda_{1}-\ell+1]}\left([x+\lambda_{2}-(\ell-1)]-\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{2}}q^{\lambda_{2}-j}\prod_{t=2}^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{[x+\lambda_{t}-1-\ell+t]}{[x+\lambda_{t}-\ell+t]}\right).

By induction hypothesis the parenthetical on the RHS above is
q^{\lambda_{2}}[x]F(x-1;\tilde{\lambda})/F(x;\tilde{\lambda})
where
\tilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
. Using
\tilde{\lambda}_{t}=\lambda_{t+1}
for the reindexing we obtain the result.
∎

2.2
q
-hit numbers

The
q
-hit numbers are defined in terms of the
q
-rook numbers by a change of basis. Let
(a;q)_{k}=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1-aq^{i})
denote the
q
-Pochhammer symbol
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
13
, Def. 3.1, Prop. 3.5]
)
.

For
\lambda
inside an
n\times m
board, we define the
q
-hit polynomial
of
\lambda
by

\sum_{i=0}^{n}H_{i}^{m,n}(\lambda)x^{i}:=\frac{q^{-|\lambda|}}{\left[m-n\right]!}\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}(\lambda)\left[m-i\right]!(-1)^{i}q^{mi-\binom{i}{2}}(x;q)_{i},

(3)

where the coefficients
H_{i}^{m,n}(\lambda)
are the
q
-hit numbers
associated to
\lambda
.

Notation 2.5
.

For square boards with
m=n
, we denote the
q
-hit number by
H_{j}^{m}(\lambda)
.

Remark 2.6
.

For the case
n=m
, Garsia–Remmel defined
q
-hit numbers
\widetilde{H}_{k}^{n}(\lambda)
by the relation

\sum_{i=0}^{n}\widetilde{H}_{i}^{n}(\lambda)x^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}R_{i}(\lambda)[n-i]!\prod_{k=n-i+1}^{n}(x-q^{k}).

(4)

One can show that the Garsia–Remmel
q
-hit numbers and our
q
-hit numbers differ by a power of
q
, namely
\widetilde{H}^{n}_{k}(\lambda)=q^{|\lambda|-kn}H^{n}_{k}(\lambda)
(see
[
6
, Appendix]
).

For the case of square boards, Garsia and Remmel showed that
\widetilde{H}^{n}_{k}(\lambda)
are in
\mathbb{N}[q]
. Later, Dworkin
[
7
]
and Haglund
[
11
]
found different Mahonian statistics on rook placements that realize the polynomials
\widetilde{H}_{j}(\lambda)
. Guay-Paquet
[
10
]
defined the rectangular
q
-hit numbers using a statistic similar to Dworkin’s statistic in
[
7
]
that we define next.

Definition 2.7

(Statistic for the
q
-hit numbers)
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition inside an
n\times m
board. Given a placements
p
of
n
nonattacking rooks on a
n\times m
board, with exactly
j
inside
\lambda
, let
stat(p)
be the number of cells
c
in the
n\times m
board such that (i) there is no rook in
c
,
(ii) there is no rook above
c
on the same column, and either,
(iii) if
c
is in
\lambda
then the rook on the same row of
c
is in
\lambda
and to the right of
c
or (iv) if
c
is not in
\lambda
then the rook on same row of
c
is either in
\lambda
or to the right of
c
.

Remark 2.8
.

Intuitively, Dworkin’s statistic
\stat(p)
is the number of remaining cells in the
n\times m
board after: wrapping this board on a vertical cylinder and each rook of
p
cancels the cells South in its column and the cells East in its row until the border of
\lambda
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition inside an
n\times m
board and
j=0,\ldots,n
then

H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)=\sum_{p}q^{stat(p)}
,
where the sum is over all placements
p
of
n
non-attacking rooks on a
n\times m
board, with exactly
j
rooks inside
\lambda
.

The proof of this result is included in
[
6
, Appendix]
. Moreover, for each partition
\lambda
, the statistic
\stat(\cdot)
is
Mahonian
. This results follows readily from Definition
2.4
.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition inside an
n\times m
board, then

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)=[m]_{n}}
.

.

Consider the partition

-hit numbers.
First, we give a deletion/contraction relation. Given a shape
\lambda
and a corner cell

in
\lambda

\lambda\backslash e

\lambda

Lemma 2.12
.

We have the following deletion/contraction relation:

H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)=H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda\backslash e)+q^{|\lambda/e|-|\lambda|+j+m-1}\left(H_{j-1}^{m-1,n-1}(\lambda/e)-qH_{j}^{m-1,n-1}(\lambda/e)\right).

(5)

The next results show the relation between the

-hit numbers when we change the dimensions of the board.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition inside an

board. Then,

H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{[m-n]!}H_{j}^{m,m}(\lambda)
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition inside an

H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)=\left[m+1-n\right]H_{j}^{m,n-1}(\lambda).

(6)

Finally, we give formulas for

-hit numbers for rectangular shapes and omit their standard proofs.

Proposition 2.15
.

H_{k}^{N}(m^{j})=q^{(N-j-m+k)k}\left[m\right]_{k}\left[N-j\right]!\dfrac{\left[N-m\right]_{j-k}\left[j\right]_{j-k}}{\left[j-k\right]!}.

Proposition 2.16
.

H_{r}^{m,n}((m-1)^{k})=0
for
0\leq r\leq k-2
,
H_{k-1}^{m,n}((m-1)^{k})=[k][m-1]_{n-1}
, and
H_{k}^{m,n}((m-1)^{k})=q^{k}[m-k][m-1]_{n-1}

The Guay-Paquet

In this section we sketch our main result, a proof of Theorem

Example 3.1
.

For
\lambda=(2,1)
inside a
2\times 3
board, looking at Figure

, we see that

H_{0}^{3,2}(\lambda)=q^{0}=1
,
H_{1}^{3,2}(\lambda)=2q+2q^{2}
,

H_{2}^{3,2}(\lambda)=q^{3}

X_{21}({\bf x},q)=\dfrac{1}{[3][2]}\left(X_{3^{0}}({\bf x},q)+(2q^{2}+2q)X_{3^{1}}({\bf x},q)+q^{3}X_{3^{2}}({\bf x},q)\right),

Left: an abelian Dyck path
\lambda
inside an
n\times m
board. Top right: the paths for

3^{0},3^{1},3^{2}

We consider the chromatic symmetric functions in variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}
and each monomial appearing as a particular assignment of the variables (i.e. colors) to the vertices. That is, the vertices
1,\ldots,N=m+n
are colored
\{1,\ldots,M\}
. For simplicity, we denote by
X_{\lambda}^{N}(M)
the chromatic symmetric polynomial
X_{G(\lambda)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M};q)
where the graph
G(\lambda)
has
N
vertices. We will use induction on both
M
and
n,\ m
when necessary, driven by the following recursion.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\lambda\subset m\times n
we have the following recursion

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}^{m+n}(M)=

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}^{m+n-1}(M-1)+x_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m+n}q^{m+n-i-\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}X^{m+n-1}_{\lambda/i}(M-1)

\displaystyle+x_{M}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i-1+(m+n-j-\lambda_{j}^{\prime})}X^{m+n-2}_{\lambda/(i,j)}(M-1),

where
\lambda/(i,j)
is the partition obtained by removing row
i
and column
j
from
\lambda
, and
\lambda/i
means we remove from
\lambda
column
i
, for
i=1,\ldots,m
or row
m+n-i+1
, for
i=m+1,\ldots,m+n
.

Proof.

In the abelian case, the graph
G(\lambda)
consists of a clique with vertices
\{1,\ldots m\}
, a clique with vertices
\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
and a bipartite graph in between with edges
(i,m+j)
for each
(i,j)
in
\overline{\lambda}
. So a coloring of this graph has at most two vertices of the same color. If the colors used are in
\{1,\ldots,M\}
, there are three cases for the color
M
:

1.

No vertex is colored
M
, this term contributes
X_{\lambda}^{m+n-1}(M-1)
to
X_{\lambda}^{m+n}(M)
.

2.

Only one vertex is colored
M
. Suppose this vertex is in column
j
(from left) and row
i=N-j
(from top to bottom). It creates ascents with all vertices above it but not in
\lambda
, giving
N-j-\lambda_{j}^{\prime}
ascents. Deleting this vertex corresponds to deleting its row and column (only one would be a row/column of
\lambda
) and we get a graph on
N-1
vertices with shape
\lambda/j
(deleting row
N-j
, column
j
or row
j
, column
N-j
). These terms contribute

x_{M}\sum_{j}q^{N-j-\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}X_{\lambda/j}(M-1)
.

3.

Two vertices are colored
M
. Suppose that one is in column
j
and the other one is in row
i
, necessarily with
(i,j)\in\lambda
. The ascents they contribute are
N-j-\lambda_{j}^{\prime}+i-1
. We can remove these two vertices, by removing row
i
and column
j
from
\lambda
and decreasing
N
by 2.
These terms contribute

x_{M}^{2}\sum_{(i,j)}q^{N-j-\lambda^{\prime}_{j}+i-1}X^{N-2}_{\lambda/(i,j)}(M-1)
.

∎

For rectangular shapes
\lambda=(m^{k})
, Lemma
3.2
gives the following recursive expansion.

Lemma 3.3
.

\displaystyle X^{m+n}_{m^{k}}(M)

\displaystyle=X^{m+n}_{m^{k}}(M-1)+x_{M}\left(q^{n-k}[m]X^{m+n-1}_{(m-1)^{k}}(M-1)+[k]X^{m+n-1}_{m^{k-1}}(M-1)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+q^{k}[n-k]X^{m+n-1}_{m^{k}}(M-1)\right)+x_{M}^{2}q^{n-k}[k][m]X^{m+n-2}_{(m-1)^{k-1}}(M-1).

Proof.

This follows by carefully applying Lemma
3.2
to the shape
\lambda=m^{k}
, since
\lambda/i
is either
(m-1)^{k}
or
m^{k-1}
and
\lambda/(i,j)=(m-1)^{k-1}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Translating Theorem
1.3
into chromatic symmetric polynomials, we want to prove that for every
M
we have

\displaystyle X_{\lambda}(M)=\dfrac{1}{\left[m\right]_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)\cdot X_{m^{j}}(M).

(7)

We apply Lemma
3.3
to each term
X_{m^{j}}(M)
appearing in the right hand side of the formula in (
7
). We also apply Lemma
3.2
and the induction hypothesis to the left hand side of the formula in (
7
), i.e. to
X_{\lambda}(M)
. Then, we obtain an expression where both sides are written in terms of
X_{m^{k}}^{m+n}(M-1)
,
x_{M}X^{m+n-1}_{m^{k}}(M-1)
,
x_{M}X^{m+n-1}_{(m-1)^{k}}(M-1)
and
x_{M}^{2}X^{m+n-2}_{(m-1)^{k}}(M-1)
. The term at
x_{M}^{0}
corresponds to the coefficient of
X^{m+n}_{m^{k}}(M-1)
, which is
\frac{1}{[m]_{n}}H^{m,n}_{k}(\lambda)
on both sides by induction on
M
. For the linear term, matching the coefficients of
x_{M}X^{m+n-1}_{(m)^{k}}(M-1)
and
x_{M}X^{m+n-1}_{(m-1)^{k}}(M-1)
separately, is equivalent to two
q
-hit identities we need to prove:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{m+n-i-\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}H_{k}^{m-1,n}(\lambda/(m+n-i,i))

\displaystyle=[m-n]H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)q^{n-k},

(8)

\displaystyle[m-n+1]\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+n}q^{m+n-i-\lambda_{i}^{\prime}}H_{k}^{m,n-1}(\lambda/(m+n-i,i))

\displaystyle=H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)q^{k}[n-k]+H_{k+1}^{m,n}(\lambda)[k+1].

(9)

Finally, the quadratic term
x_{M}^{2}X^{m+n-2}_{(m-1)^{k}}(M-1)
corresponds to the
q
-hit identity:

\displaystyle q^{k}\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i+(m-j-\lambda^{\prime}_{j})}H^{m-1,n-1}_{k}(\lambda/(i,j))=[k+1]H^{m,n}_{k+1}(\lambda).

(10)

By Definition
2.4
, we translate these three identities involving
q
-hit numbers into three identities involving
q
-rook numbers that are in Lemma
3.4
. These identities complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The
q
-hit identities (
8
), (
9
), and (
10
) are equivalent, in that order, to:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m-j}R_{k}(\lambda/j)

\displaystyle=R_{k}(\lambda)[m-k]-R_{k+1}(\lambda)(q^{m}-q^{m-k-1}),

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}q^{i-1+\lambda_{i}}R_{k}(\lambda/i)

\displaystyle=([n]-[k])R_{k}(\lambda),

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{i-j+\lambda_{i}}R_{k}(\lambda/(i,j))

\displaystyle=q[k+1]R_{k+1}(\lambda).

(13)

We give the proof of the first relation (
11
). The arguments for the other two relations have a similar flavor.

Proof of (
11
).

Let
\ell=\ell(\lambda)
.
Multiplying on both sides by
[x]_{\ell-i}
and summing over
i=0,\ldots,\ell
, the claimed relation is equivalent to the generating function identity:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m-j}F(x;\lambda/j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}R_{i}(\lambda)\left([m-i][x]_{\ell-i}-(q^{m}-q^{m-i})[x]_{\ell-i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=[m+x-\ell]F(x;\lambda)-q^{m}[x]F(x-1;\lambda),

where
F(x;\lambda)
is as in (
2
) and we used the observation that

[m-i][x]_{\ell-i}-(q^{m}-q^{m-i})[x]_{\ell-i+1}=[x]_{\ell-i}[x+m-\ell]-q^{m}[x]_{\ell-i+1}.

We have that

F(x;\lambda/j)=\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}[x+\lambda_{i}-1-\ell+i]\prod_{i=\lambda^{\prime}_{j}+1}^{\ell}[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]=F(x;\lambda)\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{[x-1+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}{[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}.

Using Lemma
2.3
, we have that since
\lambda/j=\lambda
if
j>\lambda_{1}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{m-j}F(x;\lambda/j)

\displaystyle=F(x;\lambda)\bigg([m-\lambda_{1}]+q^{m-\lambda_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{\lambda_{1}-j}\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{[x-1+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}{[x+\lambda_{i}-\ell+i]}\bigg)

\displaystyle=F(x;\lambda)\left([m-\lambda_{1}]+q^{m-\lambda_{1}}[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}]-q^{m-\lambda_{1}}q^{\lambda_{1}}[x]\frac{F(x-1;\lambda)}{F(x;\lambda)}\right)

\displaystyle=F(x;\lambda)[x-\ell+\lambda_{1}+m-\lambda_{1}]-q^{m}[x]F(x-1;\lambda),

as desired.
∎

4
The Abreu–Nigro expansion in the elementary basis

In this section we show that Guay-Paquet’s identity (Theorem
1.3
) is equivalent to Abreu–Nigro’s identity presented (Theorem
1.2
).
We start by giving a proof of Abreu–Nigro’s identity for rectangular shapes.

Lemma 4.1

(Abreu–Nigro’s formula for rectangles)
.

\displaystyle X_{m^{k}}({\bf x},q)

\displaystyle=\left[k\right]!H_{k}^{m+n-k}(m^{k})\cdot e_{m+n-k,k}+\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}q^{r}\left[r\right]!\left[n+m-2r\right]H_{r}^{m+n-r-1}(m^{k})\cdot e_{m+n-r,r}.

In order to prove this case of the Abreu–Nigro identity we need the following result.

Lemma 4.2

(Guay-Paquet formula for rectangles)
.

[m]X_{(m-1)^{k}}^{m+n-1}=q^{k}[m-k]X_{m^{k}}+[k]X_{m^{k-1}}.

(14)

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
for the shape
\lambda=(m-1)^{k}\subset n\times m
and the formula for the
q
-hit numbers
H^{m,n}_{r}((m-1)^{k})
from Proposition
2.16
we obtain

\displaystyle X_{(m-1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[m]_{n}}q^{k}[m-1]_{k}[m-k]_{n-k}X_{m^{k}}+\frac{1}{[m]_{n}}[k][m-1]_{k-1}[m-k]_{n-k}X_{m^{k-1}},

\displaystyle[m]X_{(m-1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=q^{k}[m-k]X_{m^{k}}+[k]X_{m^{k-1}}.

∎

Proof sketch of Lemma
4.1
.

We use induction on
m
and
k
. For the base case, note that
X_{m^{0}}=[m+n]!e_{m+n}=H_{0}^{m+n}(m^{0})e_{m+n}
. By Lemma
4.2
we have that

X_{m^{k}}=\frac{1}{q^{k}[m-k]}\left([m]X_{(m-1)^{k}}-[k]X_{m^{k-1}}\right).

Next, we use the induction hypothesis on
X_{(m-1)^{k}}
and
X_{m^{k-1}}
, Proposition
2.15
for the hit numbers of rectangles, and routine simplifications to verify the desired formula for
X_{m^{k}}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove that the Guay-Paquet’s identity and Abreu–Nigro’s follow from each other. As a corollary, we obtain a new proof of the latter.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Applying Lemma
4.1
to the RHS of the formula in Theorem
1.3
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\left[m\right]_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)\cdot X_{m^{j}}({\bf x},q)=\dfrac{1}{\left[m\right]_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)\left(\left[j\right]!H_{j}^{n+m-j}(m^{j})\cdot e_{m+n-j,j}\right)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{\left[m\right]_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)\left(q^{r}\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}\left[r\right]!\left[n+m-2r\right]H_{r}^{m+n-r-1}(m^{j})\cdot e_{m+n-r,r}\right).

Now, switching the summation order, we have that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\left[m\right]_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)\cdot X_{m^{j}}({\bf x},q)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}e_{m+n-r,r}\dfrac{1}{\left[m\right]_{n}}\left[r\right]!H_{r}^{m+n-r}(m^{r})H_{r}^{m,n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}e_{m+n-r,r}\dfrac{1}{\left[m\right]_{n}}\left(q^{r}\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}\left[r\right]!\left[n+m-2r\right]H_{r}^{m+n-r-1}(m^{j})H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda)\right).

Thus, we need to show that for
r=k=\ell(\lambda)
,

\displaystyle\left[m\right]_{n}H_{k}^{m+n-k}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=H_{k}^{m+n-k}(m^{k})H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda)+q^{k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\left[n+m-2k\right]H_{k}^{m+n-k-1}(m^{j})H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda),

and for
r<k=\ell(\lambda)
,

\displaystyle\left[m\right]_{n}q^{r}\left[n+m-2r\right]H_{r}^{m+n-r-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=H_{r}^{m+n-r}(m^{r})H_{r}^{m,n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle+q^{r}\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}\left[n+m-2r\right]H_{r}^{m+n-r-1}(m^{j})H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda).

After using Proposition
2.15
, these two relations are equivalent to the following identities relating
q
-hit numbers of
\lambda
in square boards and rectangular boards. Finally, the Abreu-Nigro expansion for
X_{\lambda}({\bf x},q)
follows now from Lemma
4.3
. ∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition inside an
n\times m
board and
k=\ell(\lambda)
, then

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}m-k\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}H_{k}^{m+n-k}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=q^{k(n-k)}\left[m+n-2k\right]_{m-k}H_{k}^{m,n}(\lambda).

(15)

For
0\leq r<k
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}m-r\\
n-r\end{bmatrix}H_{r}^{m+n-r-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=q^{r(n-r-1)}\left[m+n-2r-1\right]_{m-r-1}H_{r}^{m,n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}q^{r(n-1-j)}\begin{bmatrix}j\\
r\end{bmatrix}\dfrac{\left[m+n-r-j-1\right]_{m-r}}{\left[n-r\right]}H_{j}^{m,n}(\lambda).

(16)

Proof sketch of Lemma
4.3
.

Each identity follows by using Definition
2.4
to rewrite both the LHS and RHS in terms of
q
-rook numbers
R_{j}(\lambda)
and showing the resulting expressions are equal via routine
q
-factorial manipulations.

We remark that there is a more interesting proof using the deletion/contraction formula in Lemma
2.12
on both sides of each relation, the rectangle-resizing identity in Lemma
2.14
, and induction.
∎

5
Open problems

Since our proof of Theorem
1.3
uses
q
-rook theory, it would be interesting to find a bijective proof of this result relating colorings with rook placements.

There are other rules for the elementary basis expansion of
X_{\lambda}({\bf x},q)
. In particular, Cho–Huh
[
5
]
give an expansion in terms of
P
-tableaux
of shape
2^{j}1^{m+n-2j}
such that there is no
s\geq j+2
such that
(a_{i,1},a_{s,1})\in\lambda
for all
i\in\{\ell+1,\ldots,s-1\}
. Let
c_{j}^{m,n}(q):=\sum_{T}q^{\inv_{G(\lambda)}(T)}
, where the sum is over such tableaux (see
[
14
, Sec. 6]
). It would be interesting to find a weight-preserving bijection that shows that

c_{j}^{m,n}(q)=\begin{cases}[j]!H_{j}^{m+n-j}(\lambda)&\text{ if }j=\ell(\lambda),\\
q^{j}[j]![m+n-2j]H_{j}^{m+n-j-1}(\lambda)&\text{ if }j<\ell(\lambda).\end{cases}

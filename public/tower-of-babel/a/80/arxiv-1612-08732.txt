On Asymptotic Regimes of Orthogonal Polynomials with Complex Varying Quartic Exponential Weight This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Orthogonal Polynomials, Special Functions and Applications. The full collection is available at http://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/OPSFA2015.html
Marco BERTOLA † and Alexander TOVBIS ‡

Abstract
We study the asymptotics of recurrence coefficients for monic orthogonal polynomials π n ​ ( z ) \pi_{n}(z) with the quartic exponential weight exp ⁡ [ − N ⁡ ( 1 2 ​ z 2 + 1 4 ​ t ​ z 4 ) ] \exp[-N(\frac{1}{2}z^{2}+\frac{1}{4}tz^{4})] , where t ∈ ℂ t\in{\mathbb{C}} and N ∈ ℕ N\in{\mathbb{N}} , N → ∞ N\to\infty . Our goal is to describe these asymptotic behaviors globally for t ∈ ℂ t\in{\mathbb{C}} in different regions. We also describe the “breaking” curves separating these regions, and discuss their special (critical) points. All these pieces of information combined provide the global asymptotic “phase portrait” of the recurrence coefficients of π n ​ ( z ) \pi_{n}(z) , which was studied numerically in [ Constr. Approx. 41 (2015), 529–587, arXiv:1108.0321]. The main goal of the present paper is to provide a rigorous framework for the global asymptotic portrait through the nonlinear steepest descent analysis (with the g g -function mechanism) of the corresponding Riemann–Hilbert problem (RHP) and the continuation in the parameter space principle. The latter allows to extend the nonlinear steepest descent analysis from some parts of the complex t t -plane to all noncritical values of t t . We also provide explicit solutions for recurrence coefficients in terms of the Riemann theta functions. The leading order behaviour of the recurrence coefficients in the full scaling neighbourhoods the critical points (double and triple scaling limits) was obtained in [ Constr. Approx. 41 (2015), 529–587, arXiv:1108.0321] and [Asymptotics of complex orthogonal polynomials on the cross with varying quartic weight: critical point behaviour and the second Painlevé transcendents, in preparation].

As

To obtain the asymptotics of the recurrence coefficients (and of the pseudonorm), associated with the polynomials
\pi_{n}
, we use the nonlinear steepest descent analysis of the associated Riemann–Hilbert problem (RHP), see Section
2.1
. Our goal is to show that this method is applicable at any
t\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\setminus\{t_{0},t_{2}\}
. (In this paper, we restrict our attention to the first sheet of the Riemann surface of
t^{\frac{1}{4}}
only; the remaining sheets can be analyzed similarly.) As it was mentioned earlier, our approach is based on the method of continuation in the (external) parameter space: once the validity of the nonlinear steepest descent method is established for some (regular) point
t=t_{*}
, say, for some
t_{*}\in(-\frac{1}{12},0)
, it will remain in place as
t

is continuously deformed in
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\setminus\{t_{0},t_{2}\}
.

We now introduce the recurrence coefficients and the pseudonorms. The orthogonality condition for the monic polynomials
\pi_{n}(z)
can be written as

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{n},z^{k}\rangle_{\vec{\nu}}={\mathbf{h}}_{n}\delta_{nk},\qquad k=0,1,2,\dots,n,\qquad\vec{\nu}=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}),

(1.6)

where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta. The coefficient
{\mathbf{h}}_{n}
can also be written as
{\mathbf{h}}_{n}=\langle\pi_{n},\pi_{n}\rangle_{\vec{\nu}}
and hence is the equivalent of the “square norm” of
\pi_{n}
(in general, it is a complex number). We call
{\mathbf{h}}_{n}
the pseudonorms. The existence of orthogonal polynomials
\pi_{n}(z)
is not
a priori
clear. However, if three consecutive monic polynomials exists, then they are related by a three-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle\pi_{n+1}(z)=(z-\beta_{n})\pi_{n}(z)-\alpha_{n}\pi_{n-1}(z),

(1.7)

where
\alpha_{n}
,
\beta_{n}
are called recurrence coefficients. These recurrence coefficients depend on
t
,
N
.

If the bilinear pairing is invariant under the map
z\mapsto-z
then it follows immediately that the orthogonal polynomials are even or odd according to their degree and thus
\beta_{n}=0
,
\forall\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The remaining recurrence coefficients
\alpha_{n}
satisfy

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}[1+t(\alpha_{n+1}+\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n-1})]=\frac{n}{N},

which is known in literature as the string equation or the Freud equation
[
16
]
. We are interested in the asymptotic limit of
\alpha_{n}
,
\beta_{n}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
and
\frac{n}{N}=1
, so we will use notations
\alpha_{n}=\alpha_{n}(t)
,
\beta_{n}=\beta_{n}(t)
.

In the case of
t\in(-\frac{1}{12},0)
, the asymptotics of
\alpha_{n}
,
\beta_{n}
was obtained in
[
13
]
as

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{1+12t}-1}{6t}+O\big(n^{-1}\big)

and
\beta_{n}
decaying exponentially as
n\rightarrow\infty
. In fact, Theorem 1.1 from
[
13
]
states that there exists some
n_{0}=n_{0}(t)
, such that
\alpha_{n}(t)
,
\beta_{n}(t)
exist for all
n\geq n_{0}
and have the above mentioned asymptotics.

Proposition 2.1

\displaystyle Y_{+}(z)=Y_{-}(z)\left(\begin{matrix}1&\nu(z){e}^{-Nf(z,t)}\\

\displaystyle C_{\Omega}[\phi]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi(\zeta)d\zeta}{\zeta-z}

\displaystyle Y^{(n)}(z)=\left({\bf 1}+\frac{Y^{(n)}_{1}}{z}+\frac{Y^{(n)}_{2}}{z^{2}}+O(z^{-3})\right)\left(\begin{matrix}z^{n}&0\\

(2.2)

The Deift–Zhou steepest descent analysis is a powerful tool in finding the asymptotic behavior of a matrix RHP with respect to a given asymptotic parameter. In the case of the RHP (

\bullet
One starts with the RHP (

) for

\bullet

\displaystyle T(z):={\rm e}^{-N\ell\frac{\sigma_{3}}{2}}Y(z){\rm e}^{-N(g(z,t)-\frac{\ell}{2}){\sigma_{3}}},

If all these requirements are fulfilled, the leading order asymptotics for the problem (the solution to the model problem) can be obtained in terms of Riemann theta functions on a suitable hyperelliptic Riemann surface of a positive genus (with the case of zero genus not requiring the Riemann theta function).

We construct the

1)

Remark 2.5
.

The assumption that all the main and complementary arcs are simple smooth curves that can intersect each other only at the endpoints would have simplified the following discussion. In reality, though, main (complementary) arcs could intersect each other in a finite number of interior points forming, for example, crosses, “Y”-shaped contours (whiskers), etc., see, for example, various panels on Fig.
2

is in the interior of the neighboring main arc

that connects these two main arc. Thus, the number of the complementary arcs in (

\varpi_{j}
from (

\varpi_{j}

\gamma_{m,j}

that connects one of the endpoints with the point of intersection. Thus, we can assume that there are always

in (

2.2.2
Inequality (sign) requirements (or sign distribution requirements)

for
\boldsymbol{h}
and the modulation equation

The following sign distribution requirements must be satisfied:

1)

in the interior of every bounded complementary arc
\gamma_{c,j}\subset\mathfrak{C}
,
j=1,\dots,L
, as well as in the interior of
every unbounded complementary arc, we have

;

2)

is an interior point of a main arc

.

Remark 2.9
.

The second requirement together with (

) imply that
\Re h(z)\equiv 0
on
\mathfrak{M}
. Thus, the jump conditions (
2.15
) imply that
\Re h(z)
is continuous everywhere in
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Remark 2.10
.

We shall call the above mentioned case

, with the same connotation as in
[
10

. Violation of the above mentioned strict inequalities is allowed in exceptional (non-regular) cases at no more than a finite number of interior points of the corresponding main and/or complementary arcs
\Omega=\mathfrak{C}\cup\mathfrak{M}
. Let
z_{*}\in\Omega
denote one of such points. Then
\Re h(z_{*})=0
. Indeed, this is true when
z_{*}\in\mathfrak{M}
, see Remark
2.9
; otherwise, when
z_{*}\in\mathfrak{C}
, this is implied by the continuity of
\Re h(z)
. Moreover, the above requirements imply that there are at least four zero level curves of
\Re h(z)=0
, emanating from

(pinching).

2.3
Modulation equations

The sign distribution requirements of Section
2.2.2
imply that
\Re h(z)
should attain both positive and negative values in a vicinity of any branchpoint
\lambda
, where a main and a complementary arcs meet. On the other hand, if an endpoint

\Re h(z)
emanating from
\lambda
. Combined with the requirement

, we see that

\displaystyle\Re h(z)=\mathcal{O}(z-\lambda_{k})^{\frac{3}{2}},\qquad z\to\lambda_{k},

at every branchpoint
\lambda_{k}
,

(or faster) as

Consider equation (

to a vicinity of a branchpoint
\lambda_{k}

\hat{\gamma}_{m,j}
,
\hat{\gamma}_{c,l}
, which yield terms

and
\pm 2\pi i\eta_{l}
respectively,

. It now follows from (
2.17
) that
\Re h(z)=\mathcal{O}(z-\lambda_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
k=0,1,\dots,2L+1
. Then, the conditions (
2.18
) can be equivalently restated as

\displaystyle\oint_{\hat{\mathfrak{M}}}{{f(\zeta)+\ell}\over{(\zeta-\lambda_{k})R(\zeta)_{+}}}d\zeta+\sum_{j=0}^{L}\oint_{\hat{\gamma}_{m,j}}{{2\pi i\varpi_{j}d\zeta}\over{(\zeta-\lambda_{k})R(\zeta)_{+}}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\sum_{j=1}^{L}\oint_{\hat{\gamma}_{c,j}}{{2\pi i\eta_{j}d\zeta}\over{(\zeta-\lambda_{k})R(\zeta)}}+\int_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}}{{2\pi id\zeta}\over{(\zeta-\lambda_{k})R(\zeta)}}=0,

where
k=0,1,\dots,2L+1
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}
is a positively oriented loop around
\gamma_{0}
. Assuming that all the constants
\ell
,
\eta_{j}
,
\omega_{j}
,

, are known (see Section

that governs the location of the endpoints

provided the genus

is given.

The modulation equations (

three zero level curves of
\Re h
emanating from each branch-point
\lambda_{k}
,
k=0,1,\dots,2L+1
. They do not guarantee, however, that the sign requirements for

are satisfied. The latter requires a particular choice of the genus

of
\mathfrak{R}(t)
depending on

The modulation equations (
2.18
) imply that solutions of the RHPs for

and for

commute with differentiation, since the limiting values of
g_{\pm}
,
h_{\pm}
and
g^{\prime}_{\pm}
,

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(z)=\mathcal{O}\big(\sqrt{z-\lambda_{k}}\big),\qquad z\to\lambda_{k},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,2L+1

(note that
h^{\prime}(z)
is not continuous on the main arcs). The function

can be obtained as the solution of the RHP for

with the following jump conditions and the asymptotics at infinity

\displaystyle h^{\prime}_{+}(z)+h^{\prime}_{-}(z)=0,\qquad z\in\gamma_{m,j},\qquad j=0,1,\dots,L,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{+}(z)+g^{\prime}_{-}(z)=f^{\prime}(z),\qquad z\in\gamma_{m,j},\qquad j=0,1,\dots,L,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{z}+\mathcal{O}\big(z^{-2}\big),\qquad z\rightarrow\infty.

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(z)={{R(z)}\over{4\pi i}}\oint_{\hat{\mathfrak{M}}}{{f^{\prime}(\zeta)}\over{(\zeta-z)R(\zeta)}}d\zeta,

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(z)={{R(z)}\over{2\pi i}}\oint_{\hat{\mathfrak{M}}}{{f^{\prime}(\zeta)}\over{(\zeta-z)R(\zeta)}}d\zeta=R(z)M(z),

\displaystyle y^{2}(z)=h^{\prime 2}(z)=M^{2}(z)\prod_{j=0}^{2L+1}(z-\lambda_{j})=P(z),

∎

Now, according to (

\displaystyle\left.\frac{h^{\prime}(z;t_{b})}{R(z)}\right|_{z=\pm z_{0}}=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad h^{\prime}(z_{0};t_{b})=h^{\prime\prime}(z_{0};t_{b})=0,

4.3
Continuation principle for Boutroux deformations

In Section
3.2
we have derived the explicit formula (
3.21
), (
3.2
) for the
h
function in the genus
L
region, where
L=1,2,\dots
. For quartic exponential weight considered here (
L\leq 2
) there is no guarantee yet that the function
h
, given by (
3.21
), (
3.2
), satisfies the sign requirements of Section
2.2.2
. In Section
4.1
, we showed that the sign requirements are satisfied in the genus zero region. To prove that the correct signs of
\Re h
persist beyond the genus zero region, we use the continuation principle for Boutroux deformations. Let
\vartheta
be a smooth bounded curve in the external parameters space (complex
t
-plane in our case). According to (
2.24
), the deformation of the vector of (all the) branchpoints
\vec{\lambda}(t)
along
\vartheta
, governed by the modulation equations, preserves the Boutroux condition (Boutroux deformation). The idea of the continuation principle is that moving along
\vartheta
, we can show that the sign conditions for
\Re h(z;t)
will be satisfied
for all
t
along the curve, provided that:

•

the sign conditions for
\Re h(z;t)
were satisfied at the beginning point of
\vartheta
;

•

the curve
\vartheta
does not contain any critical point or any singular point (
t=0
), and;

•

every time a breaking curve is crossed (at a regular breaking point), the genus is properly
adjusted.

Let
t
be a regular point in a genus
L
region, that is, all the branchpoints in
\vec{\lambda}(t)
are distinct and
\Re h(z;t)
satisfies the strict inequalities from Section
2.2.2
at every point of the contour
\Omega=\Omega(t)
except the branchpoints. Take another regular point
t^{*}
in the same open connected component
\mathfrak{G}
of the genus
L
region, that is,
t,t^{*}\in\mathfrak{G}
, and let
\nu\subset\mathfrak{G}
be a simple smooth contour, connecting
t
and
t^{*}
. Using Lemma
4.2
, we can continue
\vec{\lambda}(t)
from
t
to
t^{*}
along
\nu
preserving the condition
\lambda_{j}(t)\neq\lambda_{k}(t)
,
j\neq k
. Therefore, we can also continue
h(z;t)
from
t
to
t^{*}
along
\nu
. The only possibility for the inequalities for
h(z;t)
to fail at some point
t\in\nu
is if
t
is a breaking point, which is excluded by construction of
\nu
. Thus, we proved the existence of
h
-function satisfying the strict inequalities at any point of
\mathfrak{G}
.

In view of the above results, it remains only to prove that the strict inequalities can be preserved after a transversal crossing (along
\vartheta
) of the breaking curve at a regular breaking point
t_{b}
. Since
L\leq 2
, at any regular breaking point
t_{b}
, there can be either one or two symmetrical double points
z_{0}=0
or
\pm z_{0}
respectively. Because of the symmetry of
h_{z}(z;t)
, it is sufficient to consider only one double point
z_{0}
in any case.

At a regular breaking point
t_{b}
the topology of zero level curves of
\Re h(z;t)
undergoes a change according to one of the following four scenarios:

•

two endpoints of a main arc collide and the arc becomes a double point
z_{0}
;

•

two endpoints of neighboring main arcs collide and the corresponding bounded complementary arc turns into a double point
z_{0}
;

•

a complementary arc is pinched by zero level curves of
\Re h(z;t)
and a new double point
z_{0}
appears;

•

a main arc collides with (another) zero level curve of
\Re h(z;t)
and a new double point
z_{0}
appears.

The first two scenarios lead to decrease of the genus whereas the last two lead to its increase.

Let
h^{(n)}(z;t)
denote the
h
function (that means that all the strict inequalities of Section
2.2.2
are satisfied) in the genus
n
region.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
t_{b}
be a regular breaking point on a breaking curve, separating regions of genus
n
and
m
, where
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
. Then
h_{z}^{(m)}(z;t_{b})\equiv h_{z}^{(n)}(z;t_{b})
and
h_{zt}^{(m)}(z;t_{b})\equiv h_{zt}^{(n)}(z;t_{b})
for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

For the RHP, describing the NLS evolution, and with
|m-n|=2
, the first identity of the theorem was proved in
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
, and the second identity – in
[
24
, Theorem 3.1]
. In the context of orthogonal polynomials, this theorem was proved in
[
1
]
. Finally, in the most general case which include both regular breaking points and the critical points, the theorem follows from the continuity of Boutroux-normalized meromorphic differentials, proven in
[
5
]
.

Corollary 4.7
.

In the conditions of Theorem
4.6
,
h^{(m)}(z;t_{b})\equiv h^{(n)}(z;t_{b})
and
h_{t}^{(m)}(z;t_{b})\equiv h_{t}^{(n)}(z;t_{b})
for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

We can now return to the continuation principle for Boutroux deformations. Let
t(s)
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, be a parametrization of the smooth curve
\vartheta
that is transversal to the breaking curve at a regular breaking point
t_{b}=t(0)
. Then, by Lemma
6.1
,
h_{t}(z_{0},t_{b})\neq 0
, where
z(s)
is the corresponding saddle point and
z_{0}=z(0)
. Since
\Re[h_{t}(z_{0};t_{b})\Delta t]=0
when

is tangential to the breaking curve at
t_{b}
, we conclude that
\Re\frac{dh}{ds}=\Re[h_{t}\frac{dt}{ds}]\neq 0
at
z_{0}
,
t_{b}
.

Let us assume, for example, that
\Re\frac{dh}{ds}(z_{0};t_{b})<0
, and that the sign conditions for
h(z,t)=h^{(L)}(z,t)
hold when
t=t(s)
with
s<0
. Then, as
s\rightarrow 0^{-}
, either a main arc
\gamma_{mj}
collapsed into the double point
z=z_{0}
, or a main arc collides with another zero level curve of
\Re h(z;t)
at
z_{0}
. We now need to prove the sign requirements on the other side of the breaking curve in a small vicinity of
z_{0}
, that is, when
t=t(s)
with a small

.

Consider first the case of a collapsing simple (without whiskers) main arc
\gamma_{mj}
. This case can be illustrated by Fig.
3
as transition from the second to the first (counting from the left) panel, caused by collapse of the two symmetrical small main arcs (on the right and on the left). We will now consider only a vicinity of
z_{0}
and disregard the symmetrical to
z_{0}
double point. If
s<0
is sufficiently close to zero then
\gamma_{mj}
is the only main arc in a vicinity of
z_{0}
. Moreover,
z_{0}
is a saddle point of
\Re h^{(L)}(z,t_{b})
with

. Let us desingularize the hyperelliptic Riemann surface

of the genus

by removing a pair of branchpoints that collided into

Consider the case when

is not a simple main arc, that is,

is a disappearing “whisker”, see the center top panel in Fig.

and the other panel right below it (see also transition from the third to the second panel (counting from the left) panel on Fig.

. However, after a proper rescaling of

in
[

The remaining case of a main arc colliding with another zero level curve can be considered similarly. Finally, the remaining two cases of different topological change at the breaking curve correspond to

, i.e., by replacing

Thus, we have essentially proved the following continuation principle: if

,

satisfies all the conditions for

-functions. Since the curve

and the breaking curve are smooth and their intersections are transversal, there can be no more than a finite values of

for which the inequalities are not strict; for these values of

are regular breaking points.

The next step in the proof of the sign requirements for

.

5
Boundedness of the branchpoints

Continuation principle allows us to prove that if the
g

-function) exists for some

and

,

, provided that

4.3
, as well as general theorem about continuation principle from
[
1
]
, does not exclude the possibility that some of the branchpoints

may approach infinity as

under the Boutroux evolution, which implies boundedness of the branchpoints for general polynomial potentials.

We start our analysis by considering polynomials

of the form

\displaystyle P(z)=M^{2}(z)S(z),\qquad M(z):=t^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(z-\mu_{j}),\qquad S(z):=R^{2}(z)=\prod_{j=0}^{2L+1}(z-\lambda_{j}),

2.22
), where

. Let us assume that

\displaystyle y(z)=f^{\prime}(z)+\frac{T}{z}+\mathcal{O}\big(z^{-2}\big),

(5.2)

where

is a residue of

at infinity and the polynomial potential

\displaystyle f(z)=f(z,\vec{t})=\sum_{j=1}^{m+L+2}\frac{t_{j}}{j}z^{j}

Note that

defines a nodal hyperelliptic curve

of the (geometric) genus

. We further assume that the meromorphic differential

satisfies the Boutroux condition:

\displaystyle\Re\oint_{\gamma}y(z)dz=0

Remark 5.1
.

A simple counting of the parameters shows that the

and

Remark 5.2
.

It is clear that

satisfies the jump conditions (

are the branchcuts of

is a collection of complementary loops on

and

is a contour connecting

and

\displaystyle h(z)=f(z)+\ell+T\ln z+\mathcal{O}\big(z^{-1}\big),\qquad z\rightarrow\infty,

\vec{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m+L+2})

.

Our goal is to study finite deformations of the parameters
\vec{t}
(we consider

) that preserve the Boutroux condition. Such deformations will be called Boutroux deformations. Let
\vec{t}(s)
be a smooth finite curve in

is the arclength parameter. We assume the residue

5.1
A general theorem on ODE’s for polynomials

Let

\displaystyle P(z,s)=p_{0}(s)z^{n}+p_{1}(s)z^{n-1}+\cdots+p_{n-1}(s)z+p_{n}(s),

(5.5)

where the coefficients
\vec{p}(s)=(p_{1}(s),\dots,p_{n}(s))
of
P
satisfy an
autonomous
system of differential equations of the form

\displaystyle{\dot{\vec{p}}(s)}=\vec{g}(\vec{p}(s)),\qquad s\in[0,1],

(5.6)

where
\vec{g}(\vec{p})=(g_{1}(\vec{p}),\dots,g_{n}(\vec{p}))
. To prove that the roots of the polynomial
P
are bounded (in
s
), it would be sufficient to show that
p_{0}(s)
is separated from zero and all the coefficients
\vec{p}(s)
are bounded on
[0,1]
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\vec{g}(\vec{p})
in (
5.6
) be continuous and suppose that there exists
K>0
such that the functions
g_{j}(\vec{p})
satisfy the bound

\displaystyle|g_{j}(\vec{p})|\leq K\rho^{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,n,

(5.7)

where

\displaystyle\rho=\rho(\vec{p}):=\max_{\ell\geq 1}|p_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}.

(5.8)

Then for any solution of the system (
5.6
) the function
\rho(s):=\rho(\vec{p}(s))
remains bounded for all
s\in[0,1]
.

To prove Theorem
5.3
, we need to establish the following statements from nonsmooth analysis.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\vec{p}(s)\in C^{1}[0,1]
. Then
\rho(s)
, defined by (
5.8
), admits Dini’s derivative

\displaystyle D^{+}\rho(s):=\limsup_{\widetilde{s}\to s_{+}}\frac{\rho(\widetilde{s})-\rho(s)}{\widetilde{s}-s},\qquad s\in[0,1].

(5.9)

Moreover,

\displaystyle D^{+}\rho(s)=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\{\frac{d}{ds}|p_{j}(s)|^{\frac{1}{j}}\colon\rho(s)=|p_{j}(s)|^{\frac{1}{j}}\right\}.

(5.10)

In particular, the derivative is finite at all points where
\rho(s)>0
.

Proof.

Denote
f_{j}(s):=|p_{j}(s)|^{\frac{1}{j}}
. Let
s
be such that
\rho(s)>0
. Fix a sequence
s_{k}\rightarrow s
from above and let
j_{k}
be such that
f_{j_{k}}(s_{k})=\rho(s_{k})
. Since the set of indices is finite, there is a subsequence of
j_{k}
that eventually becomes constant, say
j_{0}
. Then, along any such subsequence
\{j_{k_{\ell}}\}
,

\displaystyle f_{j_{0}}(s)=\lim_{\ell}f_{j_{k_{\ell}}}(s)=\lim_{\ell}f_{j_{k_{\ell}}}(s_{k_{\ell}})=\lim_{\ell}\rho(s_{k_{\ell}})=\rho(s).

Then

\displaystyle D^{+}\rho(s)\geq\lim_{\ell}\frac{\rho(s_{k_{\ell}})-\rho(s)}{s_{k_{\ell}}-s}=\lim_{\ell}\frac{f_{j_{k_{\ell}}}(s_{k_{\ell}})-\rho(s)}{s_{k_{\ell}}-s}=\lim_{\ell}\frac{f_{j_{0}}(s_{k_{\ell}})-f_{j_{0}}(s)}{s_{k_{\ell}}-s}=f_{j_{0}}^{\prime}(s),

(5.11)

where we used the fact that the subsequence
j_{k_{\ell}}
is eventually constant with limiting value
j_{0}
. Since
f_{j_{0}}^{\prime}(s)
belongs to the set in (
5.10
) we prove the inequality.

Now let us prove (
\leq
). Let the limsup in (
5.9
) is attained along the sequence
\{s_{k}\}
, introduced above. We have

\displaystyle D^{+}\rho(s)=\lim_{k}\frac{\rho(s_{k})-\rho(s)}{s_{k}-s}=\lim_{k}\frac{f_{j_{k}}(s_{k})-\rho(s)}{s_{k}-s}\leq\lim_{k}\frac{f_{j_{k}}(s_{k})-f_{j_{k}}(s)}{s_{k}-s}.

The limit set of the sequence
j_{k}
necessarily belongs to the set
\{\widetilde{j}\colon f_{\widetilde{j}}(s)=\rho(s)\}
and hence

\displaystyle\lim_{k}\frac{f_{j_{k}}(s_{k})-f_{j_{k}}(s)}{s_{k}-s}\in\left\{\frac{d}{ds}f_{j}(s)\colon f_{j}(s)=\rho(s)\right\}.

Thus the inequality
\leq
holds. The latter equation and (
5.11
) prove (
5.10
).
∎

Next we state the following well known fact that will be used in the proof of Theorem

Proposition 5.5

(

)

If

(

\displaystyle M(b)-M(a)\leq\overline{\int_{a}^{b}}D^{+}M(t)\delta t

Proof of Theorem
5.3

Since
{(\ell\geq 1)}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}|p_{\ell}(s)|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}=\frac{1}{\ell}|p_{\ell}(s)|^{\frac{1}{\ell}-1}\frac{d}{ds}|p_{\ell}(s)|{\leq}|p_{\ell}(s)|^{\frac{1}{\ell}-1}\frac{d}{ds}|p_{\ell}(s)|\leq|p_{\ell}(s)|^{\frac{1}{\ell}-1}\left|\dot{p}_{\ell}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{d}{d s} |p_{\ell}(s)|^{\frac{1}{\ell}} }{}=|p_{\ell}(s)|^{\frac{1}{\ell}-1}\left|g_{\ell}(\vec{p}(s))\right|{\leq}K\rho^{\ell}|p_{\ell}(s)|^{\frac{1}{\ell}-1},

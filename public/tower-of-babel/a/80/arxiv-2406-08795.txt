Hölder Continuity for Fully Fractional Parabolic Equations with Space-time Nonlocal Operators
Lingwei Ma, Qi Xiong, Zhenqiu Zhang

Abstract
We study the local Hölder regularity of weak solutions to the fully fractional parabolic equations involving spatial fractional diffusion and fractional time derivatives of the Marchaud type. It is worth noting that we do not impose boundedness assumptions on the weak solutions and nonhomogeneous terms. Within the space-time nonlocal framework, it is crucial to consider both space-dependent nonlocal tail terms and the first introduced time-dependent nonlocal tail term. By adapting a nonlocal variant of the parabolic De Giorgi iterative technique, we initially establish a priori local boundedness with tail terms for weak solutions and then prove the local Hölder continuity. Mathematics Subject classification (2010): 35R11; 26A33; 35B65; 39A22. Keywords: Space-time nonlocal parabolic equation; Local boundedness; Hölder regularity; Nonlocal tails; De Giorgi technique.

Introduction.

In this paper, we investigate the space-time nonlocal parabolic equation involving the fractional time derivative and the fractional diffusion in space as follows

\partial^{s}_{t}u(t,x)+\mathcal{L}u(t,x)=f(t,x)~~\text{in}~~I\times\Omega,

(1.1)

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is a finite open interval,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

is a bounded domain, and the dimension
d\geq 2
.
The fractional time derivative
\partial_{t}^{s}
we consider here is the Marchaud fractional derivative of order
s\in(0,1)
, defined by

\partial_{t}^{s}u(t,x):=C_{s}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{u(t,x)-u(\tau,x)}{(t-\tau)^{1+s}}\operatorname{d}\!\tau,

(1.2)

which was first introduced by Marchaud in
[
24
]
. The normalization positive constant
C_{s}=\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}
, and
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function.
The nolocal operator
\mathcal{L}
is of the form

\mathcal{L}u(t,x)=\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(t,x)-u(t,y)){K}(t,x,y)\operatorname{d}\!y,

(1.3)

where
{K}
is a nonnegative measurable kernel satisfying the symmetry assumption

{K}(t,x,y)={K}(t,y,x)

(1.4)

and the ellipticity condition

\Lambda^{-1}|x-y|^{-d-2s}\leq{K}(t,x,y)\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{-d-2s}

(1.5)

for a.e.
(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with some
\Lambda\geq 1
. This integral operator obviously includes the fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
when the kernel
K(t,x,y)=C_{n,s}|x-y|^{-d-2s}
. The space-time nonlocal equation in (
1.1
) serves as a prototypical model in the continuous time random walks
[
27
]
, which has diverse applications across physical and biological phenomena, refer to
[
7
]
for detailed descriptions.
Observe that the definition of the fractional time derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
given in (
1.2
) looks similar to the one-dimensional fractional Laplacian except that such integral only takes account of the interactions in the past.
This characteristic is attributed to the fact that the transport is irreversible in time and possesses a memory effect.

In the context of the nonlinear partial differential equations, numerous significant regularity results are derived from Hölder estimates for linear equations with only measurable coefficients.
The pioneering work on this subject dates back to
De Giorgi
[
8
]
, who proved a local
C^{\gamma}
estimate of solutions for second-order homogeneous uniformly elliptic equations in divergence form with measurable coefficients. Alternatively, Nash
[
28
]
independently obtained this Hölder continuity and established for the parabolic case.
Subsequently, Moser
[
25
]
provided a completely different proof of their results. Nowadays such fundamental regularity methods are known as De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory, which has been extensively applied to various second-order nonlinear elliptic and parabolic equations, as detailed in the survey by Mingione
[
26
]
.

During the past two decades, the regularity theory for integro-differential equations with merely measurable kernels has attracted widespread attention not only in practical applications, but also from the perspective of pure mathematics. For the linear fractional elliptic equation, Silvestre
[
30
]
and Kassmann
[
16
]
proved local Hölder continuity of bounded weak solutions by extending the De Giorgi approach and the Moser iteration scheme, respectively.
To remove the priori boundedness assumption of weak solutions, Di Castro, Kuusi, and Palatucci
[
10
]
first introduced a nonlocal tail and derived the local Hölder continuity for the fractional
p
-Laplace elliptic equation based on the De Giorgi theory. For more Hölder regularity results for diverse types of nonlocal elliptic problems, please refer to
[
4
,
5
,
17
,
18
]
and references therein.
With respect to the Hölder regularity results for fractional parabolic equations with the local time derivative
\partial_{t}u(x,t)
, comprehensive investigations have been carried out utilizing the modified De Giorgi method (cf.
[
3
,
6
,
12
,
15
,
22
,
23
,
29
]
). On the other hand, the relevant Hölder estimates were obtained in
[
13
,
20
]
via the Moser technique.

In the pure time fractional case, Zacher et al.
[
19
,
31
]
established the Hölder continuity of bounded solutions to local diffusion equations involving the Caputo derivative, with the results depending on the initial and boundary conditions. Regarding the space-time nonlocal parabolic equation analogous to type (
1.1
), Allen, Caffarelli and Vasseur
[
2
]
showed a Hölder continuity of global weak solutions when the nonhomogeneous term
f
is bounded.
In contrast, the local Hölder estimate for weak solutions to (
1.1
) seems to be rather incomplete, particularly in the absence of a priori boundedness assumption on weak solutions or without initial and boundary data.

Based on the De Giorgi approach modified to the space-time nonlocal setting, this paper aims to establish the local boundedness
and the local Hölder regularity of weak solutions for the nonhomogeneous fractional parabolic equations (
1.1
) with merely measurable kernel.
The main difficulty arises in the nonlocal nature of both the fractional time derivative and the fractional diffusion in the equation (
1.1
), so it is necessary to take into account long range interactions both in time and space.
In order to obtain the desired local estimates, we highlight two quantities that will play crucial roles in the present extended local theory. One of them is the nonlocal tail term related to space, given by

\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},r)=r^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(t,y)|}{|x_{0}-y|^{d+2s}}\operatorname{d}\!y.

(1.6)

This quantity was originally detailed in
[
10
]
.
Furthermore,
the memory strongly affects the estimates,
so we also need to introduce a novel nonlocal tail term associated with time, defined as

\operatorname{tail}(u(x);t_{0},r)=r^{2s}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{0}-r^{2}}\frac{|u(\tau,x)|}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1+s}}\operatorname{d}\!\tau.

(1.7)

To illustrate the main results of this paper, we start by presenting the definition of local weak solutions to the equation (
1.1
),
which utilizes the integration by parts formula for the Marchaud fractional derivative mentioned in
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f\in L_{\rm loc}^{2}(I;L_{\rm loc}^{2}(\Omega))
. A function

u\in L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(I;L_{\rm loc}^{2}(\Omega))\cap H_{\rm loc}^{\frac{s}{2}}(I;L_{\rm loc}^{2}(\Omega))\cap{\mathcal{L}}^{-}_{s}(\mathbb{R};L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L_{\rm loc}^{2}(I;H_{\rm loc}^{s}(\Omega))\cap L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(I;{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

is called a local weak subsolution to (
1.1
), if for any nonnegative test functions
\varphi\in L^{2}(I;H^{s}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
with compact support

\varphi\equiv 0
in
\left((t_{1},t_{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega^{\prime}\right)\cup\left((-\infty,t_{1}]\times\Omega^{\prime}\right)
, where
(t_{1},t_{2})\times\Omega^{\prime}\Subset I\times\Omega
,
we have

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}u(t,x)\partial^{s}_{t}\varphi(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)}{(t_{2}-t)^{s}}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle+C_{s}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{(u(t,x)-u(\tau,x))(\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(\tau,x))}{(t-\tau)^{1+s}}\operatorname{d}\!\tau\operatorname{d}\!t\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(t,x)-u(t,y))(\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(t,y))K(t,x,y)\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}f\varphi\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t.

Here,
H^{s}
and
H^{\frac{s}{2}}
denote the fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,2}
and
W^{\frac{s}{2},2}
respectively.

{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
represents the nonlocal tail space defined as

{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mathrel{\Big|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|v(y)|}{1+|y|^{d+2s}}\operatorname{d}\!y<\infty\right\},

and
{\mathcal{L}}^{-}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
is a class of slowly increasing functions given by

{\mathcal{L}}^{-}_{s}(\mathbb{R}):=\{v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})\mid\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{|v(\tau)|}{1+|\tau|^{1+s}}\operatorname{d}\!\tau<+\infty\,\,\mbox{for any}\,\,t\in I\}.

A function
u
is called a local weak supersolution if the previous formula holds for every nonpositive test function
\varphi
. If a function
u
is both a local weak subsolution and a local weak supersolution, then it is referred to as a local weak solution.

In what follows, we denote the parabolic cylinder by

Q_{r}(t_{0},x_{0}):=I_{r}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\times B_{r}(x_{0})
, where

I_{r}^{\ominus}(t_{0}):=(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0})
and
B_{r}(x_{0})
represents an open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with center
x_{0}
and radius
r>0
. Now we are in a position to present the main results of this paper. The first one describes the local boundedness estimate of weak solutions to (
1.1
) in the a priori way.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u
be a local weak solution to (
1.1
) in
I\times\Omega
with

f\in L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(I;L_{\rm loc}^{\frac{d}{s}}(\Omega))
,
then for any
R>0
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
I^{\ominus}_{2R}(t_{0})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{\frac{R}{2}}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u(t,x)|\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t+\sup_{t\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},R)+\sup_{x\in B_{R}(x_{0})}\operatorname{tail}(u(x);t_{0},R)\right.

(1.8)

\displaystyle\left.+R^{s}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0});L^{\frac{d}{s}}(B_{R}(x_{0})))}\right).

By virtue of the aforementioned
L^{\infty}-L^{1}
estimate with tails, and together with a growth lemma developed below, we can further derive the local parabolic Hölder continuity of weak solutions to (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d>2
and
u
be a local weak solution to (
1.1
) in
I\times\Omega
with

f\in L_{\rm loc}^{\infty}(I;L_{\rm loc}^{\frac{d}{s}}(\Omega))
,
then
u\in C^{\frac{\gamma}{2},\gamma}_{\rm loc}(I\times\Omega)
for some
\gamma=\gamma(d,s,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
, and satisfies

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{\frac{\gamma}{2},\gamma}(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{-\gamma}\fint_{Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0})}|u(t,x)|\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle+CR^{-\gamma}\left(\sup_{t\in I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},2R)+\sup_{x\in B_{2R}(x_{0})}\operatorname{tail}(u(x);t_{0},2R)\right)

\displaystyle+CR^{s-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0});L^{\frac{d}{s}}(B_{2R}(x_{0})))}

for any
R>0
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
(t_{0}-(2R)^{2},t_{0}+(2R)^{2})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, where

[u]_{C^{\frac{\gamma}{2},\gamma}(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x),(\tau,y)\in Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0})\\
(t,x)\neq(\tau,y)\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(t,x)-u(\tau,y)|}{\left(|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|x-y|\right)^{\gamma}},

and the positive constant
C
depends only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda
.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that the
L^{\infty}
tails

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I_{r}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},r)\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\displaystyle\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}\operatorname{tail}(u(x);t_{0},r)

are finite whenever
u
belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}^{-}_{s}(\mathbb{R};L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0})))
and
L^{\infty}(I_{r}^{\ominus}(t_{0});{\mathcal{L}}^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
respectively.
Analogous outcomes with more natural
L^{2}
-Tail have recently been proved in
[
22
,
29
]
for parabolic equations involving the standard local time derivative, the treatment of local time derivatives in the aforementioned studies is not applicable to the parabolic equations considered in this paper, which possess derivatives with respect to time of fractional order.
Although we do not provide more natural assumptions on the tail terms, our results are still novel for the fully fractional parabolic equation.

Remark 1.5
.

The previous results are new even for the fully fractional parabolic equation (
1.1
) with
f\equiv 0
or a bounded nonhomogeneous term. In both scenarios, the condition
d>2
in Theorem
1.3
can be relaxed to
d\geq 2
. Furthermore,
with only marginal modifications to the proof, the main results remain valid for the local weak solution

of

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u(t,x)+\mathcal{L}u(t,x)=f(t,x)~~\text{in}~~I\times\Omega,

where the orders are decoupled in time
\alpha\in(0,1)
and in space
s\in(0,1)
.
More precisely, at this point, the parabolic cylinder given by

Q_{r}(t_{0},x_{0}):=(t_{0}-r^{\frac{2s}{\alpha}},t_{0})\times B_{r}(x_{0}),

the constants
C
and
\gamma
in Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
also depend on
\alpha
, and the definition of the tail term associated with time is replaced by

\operatorname{tail}(u(x);t_{0},r)=r^{2s}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{0}-r^{\frac{2s}{\alpha}}}\frac{|u(\tau,x)|}{(t_{0}-\tau)^{1+\alpha}}\operatorname{d}\!\tau.

(1.9)

Here we assume the orders
\alpha=s
to make the proof more concise and clear.

2.2
Caccioppoli inequality

In this subsection, we focus on establishing a Caccioppoli-type inequality with tails for the space-time nonlocal parabolic equation (
1.1
).
When there is no confusion, we usually omit the vertex

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

) in

,

(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega

The proof goes by testing the weak formula with the test function

4.1
) implies that

A_{1}+B_{1}^{1+\kappa}=A_{1}+A_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\leq 2A_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\leq 2\nu^{\frac{q-1}{q}},

where
q=\frac{d}{s}
. From this, combining (
4.6
) with (
4.7
), and applying a classical iteration Lemma
2.5
to derive that there exists
\nu>0
sufficiently small, depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda
, and
\delta
, such that
the sequences
\{A_{i}\}
and
\{B_{i}\}
tend to zero as
i\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence, we conclude that

u(t,x)\geq\frac{H}{2}~~\text{ in }I^{\ominus}_{\frac{\delta R}{2}}\times B_{\frac{R}{2}},

which completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

In the sequel, we establish a measure shrinking lemma, where the third term of the Caccioppoli inequality (
2.1
) plays an important role in the proof.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
q=\frac{d}{s}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and let

u
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.1
) in
I\times\Omega
.
For every
R>0
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
I^{\ominus}_{2R}(t_{0})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, and any
\delta,\,\sigma\in(0,1]
and
H>0
, if
u\geq 0
in
Q_{2R}(t_{0},x_{0})

and

\left|\left\{u(t,\cdot)\geq H\right\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\geq\alpha|B_{R}(x_{0})|\,\,\mbox{for a.e.}\,\,t\in I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{0}),

(4.8)

then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda
, such that either

\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(u_{-}(t);x_{0},2R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}>\sigma H,\,\,\left(\fint_{B_{2R}^{\ominus}(x_{0})}\operatorname{tail}(u_{-}(x);t_{0},2\delta R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}>\sigma H,

(4.9)

and

R^{s}\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|f(t,x)|^{q}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}>\sigma H,

(4.10)

or

\left|\left\{u\leq\frac{\sigma H}{4}\right\}\cap I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\leq\frac{C\sigma}{\delta^{2}\alpha}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})|.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) do not hold true. Let
w(t,x)=u(t,x)-\frac{\sigma H}{2}
, then it is easy to verify that
w_{-}(t,x)\leq u_{-}(t,x)+\frac{\sigma H}{2}
.
Applying the Caccioppoli inequality (
2.1
) with
l=\frac{\sigma H}{2}
and utilizing Hölder’s inequality to the tail terms both in time and space, we deduce that

\displaystyle R^{-d-2s}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}w_{-}(t,x)w_{+}(t,y)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\delta R)^{-2s}\int_{I_{2\delta R}^{\ominus}}\int_{B_{2R}}w^{2}_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle+C(\delta R)^{-2s}\int_{B_{2R}}\left(\int_{I_{2\delta R}^{\ominus}}w_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!t\right)\operatorname{tail}(w_{-}(x);t_{0},2\delta R)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle+CR^{-2s}\int_{I_{2\delta R}^{\ominus}}\left(\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)\operatorname{Tail}(w_{-}(t);x_{0},2R)\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{2\delta R}^{\ominus}}\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(t,x)|f(t,x)|\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\sigma H)^{2}\left(\delta^{-2s}+\delta^{-2}+\delta^{-2+\frac{2}{q}}\right)R^{-2s}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}\times B_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\sigma H)^{2}\delta^{-2}R^{-2s}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}\times B_{R}|.

Finally, combining the condition (
4.8
) with the aforementioned estimates, we arrive at the conclusion that

\displaystyle\left|\left\{u\leq\frac{\sigma H}{4}\right\}\cap I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}\times B_{R}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(\sigma H)^{-1}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}}\int_{B_{R}}w_{-}(t,x)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(\sigma\alpha)^{-1}H^{-2}R^{-d}\int_{I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}w_{-}(t,x)w_{+}(t,y)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\sigma}{\delta^{2}\alpha}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}\times B_{R}|.

Thus, we complete the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

An immediate consequence of Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
is the derivation of the following growth lemma, which is an essential
ingredient in proving Hölder continuity.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
d>2
,
q=\frac{d}{s}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and let
u
be a local weak supersolution to (
1.1
) in
I\times\Omega
.
For every
R>0
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times\Omega
with
I^{\ominus}_{2R}(t_{0})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
, any
H>0
, if

\left|\left\{u(t,x)\geq H\right\}\cap I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\geq\alpha|I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\times B_{R}(x_{0})|,

(4.11)

then there exist

\delta,\,\theta\in(0,1)
depending only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,\alpha
, and
t_{1}\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})
with
I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{1})\subset I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})
, such that

u(t,x)\geq\frac{\theta H}{8}~~\text{ in }I_{\frac{\delta R}{2}}^{\ominus}(t_{1})\times B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})

holds, provided
u\geq 0
in
I^{\ominus}_{2R}(t_{1})\times B_{2R}(x_{0})\Subset I\times\Omega
,

\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{1})}\operatorname{Tail}(u_{-}(t);x_{0},2R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{\theta H}{4},\,\,\left(\fint_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}\operatorname{tail}(u_{-}(x);t_{1},2\delta R)^{q}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{\theta H}{4},

(4.12)

and

R^{s}\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{1})}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|f(t,x)|^{q}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\frac{\theta H}{4}.

(4.13)

Proof.

Let

\Sigma:=\big\{t\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})\mid|\{u(\cdot,x)\geq H\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|>\frac{\alpha}{2}|B_{R}(x_{0})|\big\},

using the proof by contradiction in conjunction with (
4.11
), it is easy to deduce that
|\Sigma|\geq\alpha|I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})|
. Then there exist
t_{1}\in I_{R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})
and
\delta
depending on
\alpha
such that the time slice
I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{1})\subset\Sigma
and

\left|\left\{u(t,\cdot)\geq H\right\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}|B_{R}(x_{0})|\,\,\mbox{for a.e.}\,\,t\in I_{\delta R}^{\ominus}(t_{1}).

From this, Lemma
4.2
implies that

\left|\left\{u\leq\frac{\theta H}{4}\right\}\cap I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{1})\times B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\leq\frac{C_{5}\theta}{\delta^{2}\alpha}|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{1})\times B_{R}(x_{0})|,

where the constant
C_{5}=C_{5}(d,s,\Lambda)>1
. We further
choose
\theta\leq C_{5}^{-1}\delta^{2}\alpha\nu
such that

\left|\left\{u\leq\frac{\theta H}{4}\right\}\cap I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{1})\times B_{R}(x_{0})\right|\leq\nu|I^{\ominus}_{\delta R}(t_{1})\times B_{R}(x_{0})|,

where
\nu
appears in (
4.1
), then it follows from the dependence of
\nu
that
\theta
depends only on
d,\,s,\,\Lambda,\,\alpha
.
Hence, by virtue of Lemma
4.1
, we conclude that

u(t,x)\geq\frac{\theta H}{8}~~\text{ in }I_{\frac{\delta R}{2}}^{\ominus}(t_{1})\times B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0}),

which completes the proof of Corollary
4.3
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the Hölder regularity established in Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The proof goes by constructing two sequences
\{M_{j}\}
and
\{m_{j}\}
that are non-increasing and non-decreasing, respectively, and to select a small
\gamma\in(0,1)
and a large
\nu>1
such that

m_{j}\leq u\leq M_{j}~~\text{ in }Q_{\nu^{-j}R}(t_{0},x_{0}),~~\text{ and }~~M_{j}-m_{j}=L\nu^{-\gamma j},

(4.14)

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
where

\displaystyle L:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{6}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(t_{0},x_{0}))}+\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}(u(t);x_{0},2R)^{\frac{d}{s}}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{s}{d}}+\left(\fint_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}\operatorname{tail}(u(x);t_{0},2R)^{\frac{d}{s}}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{s}{d}}

\displaystyle R^{s}\left(\fint_{I_{2R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|f(t,x)|^{\frac{d}{s}}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{s}{d}}

for some positive constant
C_{6}
to be determined later. Once (
4.14
) is achieved, we immediately obtain the desired result by using the definition of
L
and Lemma
1.2
.

For any fixed
j_{0}
, if we set
M_{j}=\frac{\nu^{-\gamma j}L}{2}
and
m_{j}=-\frac{\nu^{-\gamma j}L}{2}
, and choose
C_{6}\geq 2\nu^{\gamma j_{0}}
, then it is not difficult to verify that (
4.14
) is valid for every
j\leq j_{0}
.
In order to show that (
4.14
) also holds true for
j>j_{0}
, we need to construct appropriate
M_{j+1}
and
m_{j+1}
, and proceed by induction. Assuming that (
4.14
) is valid up to some
j\geq j_{0}
, we will show that (
4.14
) must hold true for
j+1
. To proceed,
without loss of generality, we suppose that

\big|\{u(t,x)\geq m_{j}+\frac{M_{j}-m_{j}}{2}\}\cap Q_{\nu^{-j}R}(\bar{t},x_{0})\big|\geq\frac{1}{2}|Q_{\nu^{-j}R}(\bar{t},x_{0})|

(4.15)

for some
\bar{t}\in(t_{0},t_{0}+(\nu^{-j}R)^{2})
, such that the above
t_{0}
acts as
t_{1}
in Corollary
4.3
.
Through a straightforward calculation, it becomes evident that

\left(\fint_{I_{2\nu^{-j}R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\operatorname{Tail}((u-m_{j})_{-}(t);x_{0},2\nu^{-j}R)^{\frac{d}{s}}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{s}{d}}\leq\frac{\theta(M_{j}-m_{j})}{8},

\left(\fint_{B_{2\nu^{-j}R}(x_{0})}\operatorname{tail}((u-m_{j})_{-}(x);t_{0},2\delta\nu^{-j}R)^{\frac{d}{s}}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{s}{d}}\leq\frac{\theta(M_{j}-m_{j})}{8},

and

(2\nu^{-j}R)^{s}\left(\fint_{I_{2\nu^{-j}R}^{\ominus}(t_{0})}\int_{B_{2\nu^{-j}R}(x_{0})}|f(t,x)|^{\frac{d}{s}}\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\frac{s}{d}}\leq\frac{\theta(M_{j}-m_{j})}{8}

can fulfill by first choosing a sufficiently small
\gamma<\min\big\{\frac{4s}{d+2},\frac{(d-2)s}{d}\big\}
that depends only on
d,\,s,\,\theta
, and then selecting a sufficiently large
\nu>\frac{2}{\delta}
that is dependent on
d,\,s,\,\theta
.
Thus, applying Corollary
4.3
with
u:=u-m_{j}
,
R:=\nu^{-j}R
,
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, and
H:=\frac{M_{j}-m_{j}}{2}=\frac{L\nu^{-\gamma j}}{2}
, we deduce that

u(t,x)\geq m_{j}+\frac{\theta L\nu^{-\gamma j}}{16}~~\text{ in }Q_{\nu^{-(j+1)}R}(t_{0},x_{0}),

(4.16)

where
\theta=\theta(d,s,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
. Let

M_{j+1}=M_{j}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,m_{j+1}=M_{j}-L\nu^{-\gamma(j+1)},

we further select
\gamma
to be sufficiently small such that
\gamma\leq\log_{\nu}(\frac{16}{16-\theta})
to ensure that the chosen
M_{j+1}
and
m_{j+1}
satisfy (
4.14
).

On the other hand, if (
4.15
) fails, we proceed analogously but instead apply Corollary
4.3
with
u:=M_{j}-u
. In this case, setting
M_{j+1}=L\nu^{-\gamma(j+1)}+m_{j}
and
m_{j+1}=m_{j}
as desired, then we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Conformal blocks attached to twisted groups
Chiara Damiolini

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to generalize the notion of conformal blocks to the situation in which the Lie algebra they are attached to is replaced with a sheaf of Lie algebras depending on covering data of curves. The result is a vector bundle of finite rank on the stack ℋ ​ ur ¯ ​ ( Γ , ξ ) g , n {\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}} parametrizing Γ \Gamma -coverings of curves. Many features of the classical sheaves of conformal blocks are proved to hold in this more general setting, in particular the factorization rules, the propagation of vacua and the WZW connection.



1.
Introduction

In conformal field theory
[
25
]
there is a way to associate to a natural number
\ell
and to a simple and simply connected group
G
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero, a vector bundle
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(0)
, called the
sheaf of covacua
, on
{\mathcal{M}_{g}}
, the stack parametrizing smooth curves of genus
g
. The goal of this paper is to generalize the construction and properties of these bundles to the case in which the group
G
they are attached to is replaces by a
parahoric Bruhat-Tits group

\mathcal{H}
which depends on cyclic coverings of curves. For this reason, this new sheaf of covacua will not be defined on
{\mathcal{M}_{g}}
, but on the Hurwitz stack parametrizing coverings of curves.

One of the reasons that lead algebraic geometers to study the sheaves of covacua, and generalization thereof, is their relation to the stack parametrizing principal bundles on curves. In the classical setting, the fibres of the dual of
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(0)
over the curve
X
, called space of
conformal blocks
, have been identified as global sections of well determined line bundles on
\textrm{Bun}_{G,X}
, obtaining insights on the geometry of this moduli space
[
4
]

[
12
]
[
19
]

[
24
]
. The key point to prove this isomorphism is the
uniformization theorem
which describes
\textrm{Bun}_{G,X}
as a quotient of the the affine Grassmannian
\textsf{Gr}(G)
, whose Picard group and the space of global sections of line bundles have been described in terms of representations of
{\mathfrak{g}}
by Kumar
[
18
]
and Mathieu
[
21
]
. The uniformization theorem, which was proved initially by Beauville and Laszlo in
[
4
]
for
G=\text{SL}_{n}
, has been shown to hold for any simple group
G
by Drinfeld and Simpson
[
9
]
. The identification of global sections of line bundles and conformal blocks was then extended to the case of parabolic bundles by Pauly in
[
23
]
and by Laszlo and Sorger in
[
19
]
. Finally Heinloth, answering questions posed by Pappas and Rapoport in
[

]
, proved the uniformization theorem for
\textrm{Bun}_{\mathcal{H},X}
, for connected parahoric Bruhat-Tits groups
\mathcal{H}
in
[
14

, where he also gave a description of the Picard group of
\textrm{Bun}_{\mathcal{H},X}
.

This motivates out interest in developing the theory of conformal blocks attached to twisted groups
\mathcal{H}
. Inspired by
[
2
]
we restrict ourselves to consider only those groups
arising from cyclic Galois coverings
, so that
{\mathcal{M}_{g}}
will be replaced by the Hurwitz stack
{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g}}

As in the classical case, also in this new setting the sheaf of conformal blocks arises as dual of the
sheaf of covacua
. Classically, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, given

dominant weights

of level at most

\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
on
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}
, called sheaves of covacua
[
25
]
. Similarly, in Section

on
{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}
which generalizes the definition and the properties of the classical covacua.

Besides the relation with
\textrm{Bun}_{G,X}
, the classical sheaves of conformal blocks have been used to investigate the geometry of
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}
and provide examples of divisors satisfying interesting combinatorial relations. In particular, in the case

, the sheaves of covacua are globally generated, hence their first Chern classes, called

, lie in the nef cone, where they generate a full dimensional sub-cone

11
]
. The combinatorial nature of these divisors, inherited from the structure of the sheaf of covacua, made possible to establish vanishing and non-vanishing criteria for conformal blocks divisors which gave insights in the geometry of
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}

[

]

[

which can be used to study

itself and, and forgetting about the covering data, the geometry of moduli of curves.

The main results of this paper (Theorem

Theorem

The sheaf

In Section

The bundle

It is important to remark that after the first draft of this paper was completed, several authors worked towards a definition and properties of conformal blocks attached to twisted groups arising from coverings. In particular Zelaci in
[
26
]
constructs special cases of twisted conformal blocks and relates them to global sections of line bundles on appropriate

[

It is worth to mention that their construction is compatible with the one presented in
[
26
]
and in this paper.

We now give an overview of how the twisted conformal blocks are defined, generalizing the methods used in
[
16
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
20
]
. To start with, let us briefly explain how a Galois covering determines a twisted group. We fix the cyclic group
\Gamma:=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
of prime order
p
and a group homomorphism
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\text{Aut}(G)
. To every
\Gamma
-covering
q\colon{\widetilde{X}}\to X
of curves, we associate the sheaf of groups
\mathcal{H}
defined as the group of
\Gamma
-invariants of the Weil restriction of
{\widetilde{X}}\times_{k}G
along
q
, i.e.
\mathcal{H}=q_{*}({\widetilde{X}}\times_{k}G)^{\Gamma}
. The Lie algebra of
\mathcal{H}
is denoted
{\mathfrak{h}}
. The preliminaries on coverings, on the stack parametrizing them and on the construction of
\mathcal{H}
are collected in Section
2
.

To construct the sheaf of covacua, we explain how to associate to each Galois covering
(q\colon{\widetilde{X}}\to X,{\mathfrak{p}})\in{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,1}}(\textrm{Spec}(k))
and representation
\mathcal{V}
of
{\mathfrak{h}}|_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
a finite dimensional vector space. We want to remark that in our construction, we assume the point
{\mathfrak{p}}
marking
X
is disjoint from the branch locus of
q
: this implies that
{\mathfrak{h}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}
is isomorphic, although non canonically, to
{\mathfrak{g}}
. It follows that once we choose such an isomorphism, we can use the classical construction
[
16
, Chapter 7]
to associate to each representation
\mathcal{V}\in{\text{IrRep}_{\ell}}({\mathfrak{h}}|_{\mathfrak{p}})
the integrable highest weight representation
{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({\mathcal{V}})}
of
{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}}
, a central extension of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}={\mathfrak{h}}|_{k(\!(t)\!)}
defined in terms of Killing form and residue pairing. The key point is to see that this construction is actually independent of the isomorphism chosen between
{\mathfrak{h}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Thanks to the residue theorem, the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}:={\mathfrak{h}}|_{X\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}}
is a Lie subalgebra of
{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}}
and we set
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V})_{X}
to be the quotient
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}{\,\big\backslash}{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({\mathcal{V}})}
. The construction of the sheaf of covacua runs similarly for any family of coverings
({\widetilde{X}}\to X,\sigma)\in{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,1}}(S)
, being careful that the isomorphism between
{\mathfrak{h}}|_{\sigma(S)}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{k}S
exists only locally on
S
. The construction of the sheaf is the content of Section
3
.

Although it is easy to show that
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V})
is coherent (Proposition
3.2.13
), it is not immediate from its construction that it is also locally free. Following the approach of Looijenga in
[
20
]
, the first step to achieve this result is to generalize to this twisted setting the
Wess-Zumino-Witten connection
defined in terms of conformal field theory. After recalling how the connection arises using the Virasoro algebra of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
, in Section
4
we show:

Corollary

(Corollary
4.5.2
)
.

The sheaf
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})
on
{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}
is equipped with a projectively flat connection with logarithmic singularities along the boundary
{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}\setminus{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}
.

This shows in particular that
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})
is a locally free module over
{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}
. Combining this with a refined version of the factorization rules (Proposition
6.2.6
), we are able to prove the local freeness of the sheaf on the whole stack
{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}
. Also in this twisted setting then, the factorization rules play a double role in the theory of conformal blocks. On one side they contribute to show that
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})
is locally free on the whole
{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}
, and on the other side they are a useful tool to reduce the computation to lower genera curves.

2.
Preliminaries on groups arising from coverings and Hurwitz stacks

In this section we introduce the group schemes associated with coverings as indicated in the introduction. Since we need to work with these groups in families, we will formulate the definition for families of coverings of curves. We obtain in this way the family
\mathcal{H}_{{\text{univ}}}
over the universal curve
{X_{univ}}
over the Hurwitz stack parametrizing coverings of curves.

Definition 2.0.1
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a possibly nodal curve over a
k
-scheme
S
. A
Galois covering
of
X
with group
\Gamma
, called also
\Gamma
-covering
, is the data of

a)

a finite, faithfully flat and generically étale map

between curves;

b)

an isomorphism
\phi\colon\Gamma\cong\text{Aut}_{X}({\widetilde{X}})
;

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

each fibre of
{\widetilde{X}}
is a generically étale
\Gamma
-torsor over
X
;

(2)

the singular locus of
\pi q
, i.e. the set of nodes of
{\widetilde{X}}
, is contained in the étale locus of
q
.

We want to attach to any
\Gamma
-covering
({\widetilde{X}}\overset{q}{\to}X\overset{\pi}{\to}S)
and to the homomorphism
\rho\colon\Gamma\to\text{Aut}(G)
a group scheme
\mathcal{H}
over
X
in the same fashion as in
[
2
, Section 4]
.

Remark 2.0.2
.

We remark that Balaji and Seshadri consider
\rho
to map to the inner automorphisms of
G
only, i.e. arising from a morphism
\Gamma\to G
. Without imposing that restriction we allow also groups
\mathcal{H}
which are non-split over the generic point of
X
.

First of all we consider the scheme
\widetilde{G}:={\widetilde{X}}\times_{k}G
and let
q_{*}(\widetilde{G})
be its Weil restriction along

, i.e.
q_{*}\widetilde{G}(T):=\textrm{Hom}_{\widetilde{X}}(T\times_{X}{\widetilde{X}},\widetilde{G})
for every
T
over
X
. It follows from
[
8
, Theorem 4 and Proposition 5, Section 7.6]
that

is representable by a smooth group scheme over
X
. The actions of
\Gamma
on
G
and on
{\widetilde{X}}
induce the action of
\Gamma
on

given by

(\gamma\cdot f)(t,{\widetilde{x}}):=\rho(\gamma)^{-1}f(\gamma(t,{\widetilde{x}}))=\rho(\gamma)^{-1}f(t,\gamma^{*}({\widetilde{x}}))

for all

and
{\widetilde{x}}\in{\widetilde{X}}
.

We define
\mathcal{H}
to be the subgroup of
\Gamma
-invariants of
q_{*}(\widetilde{G})
, i.e.

\mathcal{H}:=(q_{*}\widetilde{G})^{\Gamma}.

We denote by
{\mathfrak{h}}
the sheaf of Lie algebras of

. Since

is smooth, as shown in
[

, Proposition 3.4]
,

is a vector bundle on

which is moreover equipped with a structure of Lie algebra.

Example 2.0.3
.

Let
\rho\colon\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to\text{Aut}(\text{SL}_{r})
be given by
\rho(\gamma)M=(M^{t})^{-1}
and

-covering of smooth curves. The group

Remark 2.0.4
.

The action of
\Gamma
on

via

induces an action on

. We equivalently could have defined
{\mathfrak{h}}
as the Lie algebra of
\Gamma
-invariants of

2.1.
Properties of
\Gamma
-coverings

In this section we recall the definitions and properties of coverings of curves. The main refernce is
[
7
]

2.1.1.
Ramification and branch divisors

Consider a
\Gamma
-covering
(f\colon{\widetilde{X}}\overset{q}{\to}X\overset{\pi}{\to}S)
. We define the
ramification divisor

{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}
to be the effective Cartier divisor
(p-1){\widetilde{X}}^{\Gamma}
, where
{\widetilde{X}}^{\Gamma}
is the subscheme of
{\widetilde{X}}
fixed by
\Gamma
. Equivalently, since
\Gamma
does not have proper subgroups,
{\widetilde{X}}^{\Gamma}
is the complement of the étale locus of

, which is either empty or an effective Cartier divisor of
{\widetilde{X}}
. The
reduced branch divisor

\mathcal{R}
is the effective divisor given by the image of
{\widetilde{X}}^{\Gamma}
in
X
. One can moreover observe that
q_{*}(\mathscr{O}(-{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}))^{\Gamma}
is isomorphic to
\mathscr{O}(-\mathcal{R})
.

Remark 2.1.1
.

If the map
q
is not étale both divisors
{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}
and
\mathcal{R}
are finite and étale over

. This is a proved in
[
7
, Proposition 3.1.1]
for the smooth case only and in
[
7
, Proposition 4.1.8]
for the general situation.

The ramification divisors are naturally related to tangent sheaves of
X
and
{\widetilde{X}}
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{{\widetilde{X}}/S}
be the tangent sheaf of
{\widetilde{X}}
relative to
S
, so that its sections are

-linear derivations of
\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}
. Consider its pushforward to
X
along
q
and notice that the action of
\Gamma
on

induces an action on
q_{*}\mathcal{T}_{{\widetilde{X}}/S}
by sending a derivation

to
\gamma D\gamma^{-1}
. The following statement, which describes the
\Gamma
-invariants of

[

, Proposition 4.1.11]
.

Proposition 2.1.2
.

The sheaf
(q_{*}\mathcal{T}_{{\widetilde{X}}/S})^{\Gamma}
over

is isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}_{X/S}(-\mathcal{R})
. ∎

Hurwitz data

The Hurwitz data provide a description of the action of
\Gamma
at the ramification points. Before working with families we consider
q\colon{\widetilde{X}}\to X
, a
\Gamma
-covering of curves over
k
. Let
{\widetilde{x}}\in{\widetilde{X}}(k)^{\Gamma}
be a ramification point and up to the choice of a local parameter

the formal disc around
{\widetilde{x}}
is isomorphic to
\textrm{Spec}(k[\![t]\!])
. Since
\Gamma
fixes
{\widetilde{x}}
, one of its generators acts on
k[\![t]\!]
by sending
t
to
\zeta t
for a primitive
p
-th root unity
\zeta
. It follows that the action of
\Gamma
on
\textrm{Spec}(k[\![t]\!])
is uniquely determined by non trivial characters
\chi_{{\widetilde{x}}}\colon\Gamma\to k^{*}
. Let
\textrm{Char}(\Gamma)^{*}
be the set of all non trivial characters of
\Gamma
and set
R_{+}(\Gamma):=\oplus_{\chi\in\textrm{Char}(\Gamma)^{*}}\mathbb{Z}\chi
. The
ramification data
or
Hurwitz data
of a
\Gamma
-covering

is the element

\xi:=\sum_{{\widetilde{x}}\in{\widetilde{X}}^{\Gamma}}\chi_{{\widetilde{x}}}\in R_{+}(\Gamma).

The
degree
of
\xi=\sum b_{i}\chi_{i}
is
\deg(\xi):=\sum b_{i}
. Note that
\deg(\xi)=\deg({\widetilde{X}}^{\Gamma})=\deg(\mathcal{R})
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

Let
{\widetilde{X}}\to X\to S
be a
\Gamma
-covering with
S
connected. We say that it has Hurwitz data
\xi\in R_{+}(\Gamma)
if
\xi
is the Hurwitz data of one, hence all (see
[
7
, Lemme 3.1.3]
), of its fibres.

We fix for the next two lemmas, a generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
and
\zeta\in k
a primitive
p
-th root of
1
. This identifies the set of characters of
\Gamma
with
\{0,\dots,p-1\}
.

Lemma 2.1.4
.

Denote by
\mathscr{E}_{i}
the
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-submodule of
q_{*}\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}
where
\gamma
acts by multiplication by
\zeta^{i}
. Then

q_{*}\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-1}\mathscr{E}_{i}\and\mathscr{E}_{i}\otimes\mathscr{E}_{p-i}\cong\mathscr{O}(-\mathcal{R})\quad\text{for }i\neq 0.

Proof.

The action of
\Gamma
on
q_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}
provides the decomposition with
\mathscr{E}_{0}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
. Observe furthermore that since
\mathscr{O}_{X}
is
\Gamma
-invariant, each eigenspace
\mathscr{E}_{i}
is naturally an
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module. Since the action of
\Gamma
is compatible with the product in
q_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}
, the tensor product
\mathscr{E}_{i}\otimes\mathscr{E}_{p-i}
is a submodule of
\mathscr{E}_{0}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X}
. Outside the branch divisor
\mathcal{R}
this is an isomorphism so we only need to check what is the image along
\mathcal{R}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{R}
and call
{\widetilde{x}}\in{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}
the point above
x
so that
\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{{\widetilde{X}},{\widetilde{x}}}\cong k[\![t]\!]
, with
\gamma(t)=\zeta^{n}t
with
n\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
. If follows that
(\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{i})_{x}\cong t^{[i/n]}R[\![t^{p}]\!]
and
(\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{p-i})_{x}\cong t^{p-[i/n]}k[\![t^{p}]\!]
, where
[i/n]\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
denotes the product of
i
with the multiplicative inverse of
n
in
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
(\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{i})_{x}\otimes(\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{p-i})_{x}\cong t^{p}k[\![t^{p}]\!]
which is isomorphic to the completion of
\mathscr{O}(-\mathcal{R})
at the point
x
.
∎

Lemma 2.1.5
.

Denote by
{\mathfrak{g}}^{(i)}
the submodule of
{\mathfrak{g}}
where
\gamma
acts by multiplication by
\zeta^{i}
. The sheaf
{\mathfrak{h}}
decomposes as

{\mathfrak{h}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-1}{\mathfrak{g}}^{(-i)}\otimes_{k}\mathscr{E}_{i}.

Proof.

As the action of
\gamma
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is diagonalizable with eigenvalues belonging to
\{1,\zeta,\cdots,\zeta^{p-1}\}
, we can decompose
{\mathfrak{g}}
as
\bigoplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{(-i)}
. As
{\mathfrak{h}}
is the Lie algebra of
\Gamma
-invariants of
q_{*}(\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}\otimes_{k}{\mathfrak{g}})=q_{*}\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}\otimes_{k}{\mathfrak{g}}
, we can combine this with the description of
q_{*}\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}
provided by Lemma
2.1.4
to obtain the wanted decomposition of
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
∎

Remark 2.1.6
.

In view of the conclusions of Remark

, hold for any family of coverings or curves over an arbitrary scheme

Example 2.1.7

The previous Lemma shows in particular that when the group

Definition 3.1.3

Remark 3.1.4

As

Proposition 3.1.5

We can also prove the previous proposition as a consequence of the following two lemmas, which we present as they are going to be useful in Section

. Let denote by

Lemma 3.1.6

The image of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}

\omega_{{\mathcal{A}}/S}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}^{\vee}

Proof.

We can restrict to the case of family of smooth curves, as on the singular points the result follows from

by identifying

with

{\mathfrak{h}}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-1}{\mathfrak{g}}^{(-i)}\otimes_{k}\mathscr{E}_{i}

(\,|\,)

\displaystyle=({\mathfrak{g}}^{(-i)}\otimes_{k}\mathscr{E}_{i}(\mathcal{R}),|-)_{{\mathfrak{h}}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\Omega_{X/S}

\displaystyle=({\mathfrak{g}}^{(-i)}|-)\otimes_{k}(\mathscr{E}_{i}(\mathcal{R}))^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\Omega_{X/S}

∎

Lemma 3.1.7
.

The annihilator of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}
with respect to the pairing
\textrm{Res}_{\mathfrak{h}}
, denoted
\text{Ann}_{\textrm{Res}_{\mathfrak{h}}}({\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}})
, is
\omega_{{\mathcal{A}}/S}\otimes{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}^{\vee}
.

Proof.

Before starting with the proof, we remark that this lemma holds if we replace
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}
with any vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
on
X
as it is essentially a consequence of Serre duality. We start by giving a description of the quotient
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}{\,\big\backslash}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
, as the annihilator of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}
will be the dual of that quotient with respect to the residue pairing. The double quotient
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}{\,\big\backslash}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}{\,\big/}F^{n}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
computes
R^{1}\pi_{*}({\mathfrak{h}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}^{n})
. It follows that the projective limit
\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}R^{1}\pi_{*}({\mathfrak{h}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}^{n}))
equals
\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}{\,\big\backslash}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}{\,\big/}F^{n}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
which is
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}{\,\big\backslash}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
.
As the residue pairing gives rise to Serre duality, we know that
R^{1}\pi_{*}({\mathfrak{h}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}^{n})
is isomorphic to the dual of
\pi_{*}(\omega_{X/S}\otimes({\mathfrak{h}}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}^{n})^{\vee})
. It follows that

\text{Ann}_{\textrm{Res}_{\mathfrak{h}}}({\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}})=\varinjlim_{n\geq 1}\pi_{*}\left(\omega_{X/S}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}({\mathfrak{h}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}^{n})^{\vee}\right).

which equals
\omega_{{\mathcal{A}}/S}\otimes_{{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}^{\vee}
. ∎

3.2.
Conformal blocks attached to integrable representations

We have all the ingredients to define the sheaf of conformal blocks. Let
U{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}}

Definition 3.2.1
.

For any

Definition 3.2.2
.

An irreducible finite dimensional representation

Let

be an irreducible finite dimensional representation of the Lie algebra

In view of this, once we fix

For every

Remark 3.2.4

Note that when

In the constant case

3.2.1.

The morphism

We recall here some facts about representation theory of

We note that the action of

The properties of

are well known and described for example in

]
,
[

]

For

(1)

It follows that to every

Let

The inclusion

Integrable representations of level

3.2.3.

We extend the notion of covacua, and hence of conformal blocks, to the case in which more points of

Let

{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}

In the same way as we defined

We extend centrally
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}_{i}}}

Let

which then is a

Definition 3.2.11
.

The
sheaf of covacua attached to

-module

\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})_{X}:={\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}\circ{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n}})}{\,\big\backslash}{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n}})}.

Theorem 3.2.12

The sheaf of covacua
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})
on

As a first step in the direction of the proof, and inspired by
[

, Section 2.5]
we prove the following statement.

Proposition 3.2.13

The sheaf
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})
is a coherent module on
{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,1}}

Proof.

It is enough to show that the
\mathscr{O}_{S}
-module
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})_{X}
is coherent and we show it in the case

. We furthermore observe that this is essentially a consequence of
[
24
, Lemma 2.5.2]
. As this is a local statement, we can assume that
{\mathcal{L}}\cong R(\!(t)\!)
and we can fix an isomorphism
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}{\mathcal{L}}}
. Observe that the quotient
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}{\,\big\backslash}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}{\,\big/}F^{0}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
is a finitely generated

and

is locally free over

. This implies that
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}{\,\big\backslash}{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}}{\,\big/}F^{1}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
is finitely generated too over

so that we can write

{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}}=F^{1}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}+{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}Re_{i}.

which in terms of enveloping algebras becomes

U{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}}=\sum_{(N_{1},\dots,N_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}^{n}}U({\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}})\circ{e_{1}}^{\circ N_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ{e_{n}}^{\circ N_{n}}\circ U(F^{1}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}})

thing that can be proven using induction on the length of elements of

We can furthermore assume that the elements
e_{i}
acts locally nilpotently on

, meaning that there exists

such that

and the Cartan decomposition of

. The algebras

, so that

is generated by

are generated by elements of

so that, up to replace

with a choice of nilpotent generators, we can ensure that all the

and so using Corollary

It follows that

{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}({\mathcal{V}})}=\sum_{(N_{1},\dots,N_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}^{n}}U({\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{A}}})\circ{e_{1}}^{\circ N_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ{e_{n}}^{\circ N_{n}}\underset{c=\ell}{\otimes}\mathcal{V}

and that

4.
The projective connection on
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V})

We want to prove that the sheaf of covacua
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})
is a vector bundle on the Hurwitz stack
{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,n}}
. This will in particular imply that also its dual
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})^{\dagger}
is a vector bundle, and that its rank is constant on

\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})
is coherent, one method to exhibit local freeness is to provide a projectively flat connection on it. In this section we provide a projective action of
\mathcal{T}_{{\overline{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,1}}/k}(-\log(\Delta))
on
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V})
, showing its freeness when restricted to
{\mathcal{H}\textrm{ur}(\Gamma,\xi)_{g,1}}

4.1.

Let

We now use the assumption that

4.3.
The Virasoro algebra of
{\mathcal{L}}

Now that we can express

In order to achieve this result, we will follow the methods of

of

as a central extension of

-module

does not depend on the covering, and the same holds for its central extension

The Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{l}}
and its central extension
\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}

We denote by
{\mathfrak{l}}
the sheaf of abelian Lie algebras (over
S
) whose underlying module is
{\mathcal{L}}

F^{*}\!{\mathcal{L}}
gives the filtration
F^{*}{\mathfrak{l}}

the universal enveloping algebra, which is isomorphic to

since

\overline{U}{\mathfrak{l}}:=\varprojlim_{n}U{\mathfrak{l}}/U{\mathfrak{l}}\circ F^{n}{\mathfrak{l}}.

Remark 4.3.1
.

Note that in this case the completions on the right

and on the left

coincide because

is abelian. The element

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}t^{-(i)^{m}}\circ t^{i}
for every

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}t^{-i}\circ t
is not an element of
\overline{U}{\mathfrak{l}}
.

We extend centrally
{\mathfrak{l}}
via the residue pairing described in Section
3.1
defining the Lie bracket on

as

[f+\hslash r,g+\hslash s]=\hslash\textrm{Res}(gdf)

for every
f,g\in{\mathfrak{l}}
and
r,s\in\mathscr{O}_{S}
. The filtration of
{\mathfrak{l}}
extends to a filtration of
\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}
by setting
F^{i}\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}=F^{i}{\mathfrak{l}}
for
i\geq 0
and
F^{i}\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}=F^{i}{\mathfrak{l}}\oplus\hslash\mathscr{O}_{S}
for

. The universal enveloping algebra of
\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}
is denoted by
U\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
\overline{U}\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}
denotes its completion on the right with respect to the filtration
F^{*}\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}
. Note that since

is a central element, we have that

is an

instead of
\hslash\circ\hslash
and similarly

for every

Remark 4.3.2
.

Since
\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}
is no longer abelian, completion on the right and on the left differ. Take for example the element
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}t^{i}\circ t^{-i}
which belongs to
\overline{U}{\mathfrak{l}}

\overline{U\widehat{{\mathfrak{l}}}}

on the right side", we should use the equality
t^{i}\circ t^{-i}=t^{-i}\circ t^{i}+\hslash i
infinitely many times, which is not allowed.

4.4.
Sugawara construction

In this section we generalize to our case, i.e. using
{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}}
in place of
{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathcal{L}}}

described by Looijenga in

, which essentially represents the local picture of our situation. In the classical case the idea is to use the Casimir element of

to induce, from
\widehat{C}
the map

which, in turn, will give the map of Lie algebras

{\mathfrak{g}{\mathcal{L}}}

\mathfrak{c}
of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}

As in Section
3.1
, we consider the normalized Killing form defined on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Recall that it provides an isomorphism between
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\vee}
, hence it gives an identification of
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
with
\text{End}_{k}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}^{\vee}
. Moreover, as
\sigma(S)
is disjoint from the ramification locus, we also have that

provides an isomorphism of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
with
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}^{\vee}
, giving in this way an identification of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}\otimes_{\mathcal{L}}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
with
\text{End}_{\mathcal{L}}({\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}})

Casimir element
of
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
with respect to the form
(\,|\,)_{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
is the element in
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}\otimes_{\mathcal{L}}{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
corresponding to the identity
\text{Id}_{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
via the identification provided by
(\,|\,)

Remark 4.4.1
.

We could have defined the Casimir element of

{\mathfrak{g}{\mathcal{L}}}

Nodal degeneration and factorization rules

In this section we want to compare the sheaf of covacua

Setting and notation

We will consider the following objects.

6.
Locally freeness of the sheaf of conformal blocks

In this section we show how to use a refined version of the factorization rules to prove that the sheaves of covacua

6.1.

. This moreover shows the flatness of the family.
∎

This deformation of

induces a deformation of
{\widetilde{X}}
which, in rough terms, is obtained as the trivial deformation outside the points
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{p}
of

lying above

, and for every

, the formal neigbourhood of

will be isomorphic to the formal neighbourhood of
\mathcal{X}_{n}
around
{\mathfrak{p}}
.

To do this, let denote by
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j,+}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j,-}
the two points of
{\widetilde{X}}_{N}
mapping to
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
. We fix local coordinates

at

so that

{\widetilde{X}}_{N}\times_{X_{N}}\textrm{Spec}(k[\![t_{\pm}]\!])\cong\textrm{Spec}\left(\oplus_{j=1}^{p}k[\![t_{j,\pm}]\!]\right)

and let consider

an open subset of
{\widetilde{X}}
. If

is disjoint from

, we set
\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{n}}(U)=\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}(U)[\tau]/\tau^{n+1}
. Let

and if

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}

\textstyle{\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{n}}(U):=\ker\left(\dfrac{k[\![t_{i,+},t_{i,-}\!]\!][\tau]}{t_{i,+}t_{i,-}=\tau,\tau^{n+1}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{n}}(U\setminus\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\right.}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\alpha}_{n}-\widetilde{\beta}_{n}}
\textstyle{\left.\dfrac{k(\!(t_{i,+})\!)[\tau]}{\tau^{n+1}}\oplus\dfrac{k(\!(t_{i,-})\!)[\tau]}{\tau^{n+1}}\right)}

where the maps
\widetilde{\alpha}_{n}
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{n}
are defined as in the case of

.

Remark 6.1.3
.

As was shown for
\mathcal{X}^{n}
, also
{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{n}
is a curve over
\textrm{Spec}(k[\tau]_{n})
deforming
\mathcal{X}
.

Let denote by
\mathcal{R}_{n}
the trivial deformation of the branch locus
\mathcal{R}
inside
\mathcal{X}^{n}
. The natural map
q_{n}\colon{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{n}\to\mathcal{X}^{n}
which extends

realizes

as a
\Gamma
-covering which is étale exactly outside

since the map
q_{n}
is étale on
{\mathfrak{p}}
by construction. Furthermore, as
\sigma_{0}
is disjoint from the singular locus, it follows that for every

we can set

to be the trivial deformation of
\sigma_{0}
.

By taking the direct limit of this family of deformations we obtain the
\Gamma
-covering of formal schemes
{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{\infty}\to\mathcal{X}^{\infty}
over
\text{Spf}(k[\![t]\!])
. To prove that
{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{\infty}\to\mathcal{X}^{\infty}
is algebraizable, i.e. that is comes from an algebraic object

, we can invoke Grothendieck’s existence theorem (
[
13
, Théorème 5.4.5]
) so that we are left to prove that the family
({\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{n}\to\mathcal{X}^{n})_{n}
is equipped with a compatible family of very ample line bundles. This is true because given a smooth point
P
of
X
which is not in
\mathcal{R}
and
m
sufficiently big, we know that
\mathscr{O}(mP)
is a very ample line bundle on

whose pullback to
\mathscr{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}}
is also very ample. Since

lies in the smooth locus of
X
these line bundles extend naturally to very ample line bundles on
\mathcal{X}^{n}
and on
{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}^{n}
, providing the wanted family of very ample line bundles.

We refer to the covering
(q\colon{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathcal{X},\sigma)
that we have just constructed as the
canonical smoothing
of
(q_{0}\colon{\widetilde{X}}\to X,\sigma_{0})
. Observe that the generic fibre of
{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathcal{X}
is a covering of smooth curves over
k(\!(\tau)\!)
because, as one can deduce from Remark

, the formal neighbourhood of
{\mathfrak{p}}
is given by
k[\![t_{+},t_{-}]\!](\!(\tau)\!)/t_{+}t_{-}=\tau
.

Remark 6.2.1

Proof of Theorem
6.2.2

Notation
.

In what follows we denote by

in

. Moreover we will write

at the point

.

Lemma 6.2.3
.

The canonical smoothing identifies
k[\![t_{+},t_{-}]\!]
with the subalgebra of
{\mathcal{L}}_{N}[\![\tau]\!]
consisting of elements

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:=\left\{\sum_{i,j\geq 0}a_{ij}\left(t_{+}^{i-j}\tau^{j}\,,\,t_{-}^{j-i}\tau^{i}\right)\,|\,a_{ij}\in k\right\}

via the map sending

to

and
t_{-}
to

.

Proof.

Taking the limit of the definition of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{X}^{n}}
we identify the formal neighbourhood of
\mathcal{X}^{\infty}
at

with

\ker\left(k[\![t_{+},t_{-}]\!]\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{N}[\![\tau]\!]\overset{\alpha-Id}{\longrightarrow}{\mathcal{L}}_{N}[\![\tau]\!]\right)

where

and

∎

In view of Proposition
5.2.2
we identify
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{0})_{X}[\![\tau]\!]
with

\bigoplus_{W\in{\text{IrRep}_{\ell}}({\mathfrak{h}}|_{\mathfrak{p}})}{\mathfrak{h}}_{A_{N}}{\,\big\backslash}\left({\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({\mathcal{V}_{0}})}\otimes(W\otimes W^{*})\right)[\![\tau]\!]

or equivalently with

Recall that
{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}}
is a filtered Lie algebra, hence this induces a filtration on

.

Remark 6.2.4
.

Once we choose local coordinates and an isomorphism between
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}{\mathcal{L}}}
we observe that the elements of

are

-linear combinations of elements
X_{r}t^{-k_{r}}\circ\cdots\circ X_{1}t^{-k_{1}}\circ e_{0}
with

and

, where

stands for

. We can explicitly write the graded pieces of

as

{\mathcal{F}}^{+}({\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\mathcal{L}}})(-d)=\left\langle X_{r}t^{-k_{r}}\circ\cdots\circ X_{1}t^{-k_{1}}\circ e_{0}\,|\,\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}=d\right\rangle

and in particular

which shows that
{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({W})}(0)=W
.

Proposition 6.2.5
.

Let

and

be the trace morphism. Then there exists an element

\epsilon(W)=\sum_{d\geq 0}\epsilon(W)_{d}\cdot\tau^{d}\in\left({\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({W})}\otimes{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({W^{*}})}\right)[\![\tau]\!]

satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

the constant term
\epsilon(W)_{0}\in W\otimes W^{*}
is the dual of
b_{W}^{0}
and for every

we have
\epsilon(W)_{d}\in{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({W})}(-d)\otimes{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}({W^{*}})}(-d)
;

(b)

\epsilon(W)
is annihilated by the image of
{\mathfrak{h}}_{k[\![t_{+},t_{-}]\!]}
in
\overline{U}\widehat{{\mathfrak{h}}}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{N}}[\![\tau]\!]
.

Proof.

We choose an isomorphism between
{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{L}_{N}}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
, as well as an isomorphism between
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
and

. The construction of

essentially lies in showing that the pairing

extends to a unique pairing

such that for all

for all

and

and that

is identically zero when restricted to

to which we refer. ∎

Proposition 6.2.6

The

of

Remark 6.2.7
.

The argument we used run similarly if instead of starting with a covering of curves over
\textrm{Spec}(k)
, we had considered a family of coverings
({\widetilde{X}}\overset{q}{\to}X\overset{\pi}{\to}S,\sigma)
where the singular locus of

is given by one (or more) sections of
\pi
and whose normalization is a covering of versal pointed smooth curves. Using these assumptions we are able to construct the canonical smoothing

of

over

which is moreover a versal deformation of
({\widetilde{X}}\overset{q}{\to}X\overset{\pi}{\to}S,\sigma)
. Once we have this construction, the analogue of Theorem
6.2.2
follows.

We have now all the ingredients to prove Theorem
3.2.12
which, we recall, stated that
\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{n})

.

Improvements on Hippchen’s Conjecture
Eun-Kyung Cho, Ilkyoo Choi, Boram Park

Abstract
Let G G be a k k -connected graph on n n vertices. Hippchen’s Conjecture [ 6 ] states that two longest paths in G G share at least k k vertices. Gutiérrez [ 5 ] recently proved the conjecture when k ≤ 4 k\leq 4 or k ≥ n − 2 3 k\geq\frac{n-2}{3} . We improve upon both results; namely, we show that two longest paths in G G share at least k k vertices when k = 5 k=5 or k ≥ n + 2 5 k\geq\frac{n+2}{5} . This completely resolves two conjectures in [ 5 ] in the affirmative.

Introduction

It is easy to see that two longest paths in a connected graph must have a common vertex.
In 1966, Gallai
[
1
]
asked if all longest paths in a connected graph must have a common vertex.
This is known to be true for connected series-parallel graphs
[
3
]
, yet, it is not true in general.
The minimum order of a graph answering Gallai’s question in the negative has 12 vertices, found by Walther and Voss
[
8
]
and independently by Zamfirescu
[
9
]
.
There is a rich literature regarding the history of intersections of longest paths.
We direct the readers to the surveys
[
10
,
7
]
for more details and related results.

Instead of ensuring a common vertex in all (or many) longest paths, one could seek sufficient conditions that imply many common vertices in two longest paths.
Hippchen
[
6
]
made the following intriguing conjecture, suggesting that the number of vertices shared by two longest paths in a graph
G
is at least the connectivity of
G
.
Note that the complete bipartite graph where the two parts have
k
and
2k+2
vertices demonstrates that the conjecture, if true, is tight.
There is also an infinite family of graphs for each
k
that demonstrates the tightness of the conjecture
[
5
]
.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

If
G
is a
k
-connected graph, then two longest paths in
G
share at least
k
vertices.

Hippchen
[
6
]
proved the above conjecture for
k\leq 3
, and recently, Gutiérrez
[
5
]
proved the conjecture for
k=4
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
6
,
5
]
)
.

For
k\leq 4
, Conjecture
1.1
is true.

Our first result verifies Hippchen’s Conjecture for the next open case, which was a conjecture in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
k=5
, Conjecture
1.1
is true.

Gutiérrez also proved that the same conclusion holds whenever the connectivity is linear in terms of the number of vertices.
Namely, for a
k
-connected graph
G
on at most
3k+2
vertices, two longest paths in
G
share at least
k
vertices.
He actually proved the following stronger statement: If
G
is a
k
-connected graph on
n
vertices, then two longest paths in
G
share at least
\left\lceil\frac{8k-n+2}{5}\right\rceil
vertices.
We improve upon the aforementioned statement in the following form:

Theorem 1.4
.

If
G
is a
k
-connected graph on
n
vertices, then two longest paths in
G
share at least
\min\left\{k,\left\lceil\frac{8k-n-4}{3}\right\rceil\right\}
vertices.

Since
k\geq\frac{n+2}{5}
implies
\min\left\{k,\left\lceil\frac{8k-n-4}{3}\right\rceil\right\}=k
, we obtain the following corollary as a direct consequence of Theorem
1.4
, thereby extending the class of graphs for which Hippchen’s Conjecture is true.
This resolves a proposed conjecture in
[
5
]
in a stronger form.

Corollary 1.5
.

For a
k
-connected graph on at most
5k-2
vertices, Conjecture
1.1
is true.

In Section

, we prove Theorem
1.4
, and
the proof of Theorem
1.3
is presented in Section

We conclude the paper with some remarks in Section

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a
k
-connected graph, where
P
and

are longest paths in

.
If

q^{\prime}_{0}
, and either

q^{\prime}_{0}q^{\prime}_{i+1}\in E(G)

Proof.

Suppose that

.
Let

.
Since

is
k
-connected,
q_{0}
has at least
k

q_{0}
is an end of
Q
, and

is a longest path.
Also,
q_{0}
must have a neighbor

(

) such that

|V(P)\cap V(Q)|\leq k-1
.
Now, the path
Q^{\prime}:Q_{[q_{i},q_{0}]}+q_{0}q_{i+1}+Q_{[q_{i+1},q_{\ell}]}
is a longest path in
G
since
V(Q^{\prime})=V(Q)
.
Note that
q_{i}
, which is the first vertex of
Q^{\prime}
, is not on
P
.
Applying the same process to
q_{l}
, which is the other end of
Q^{\prime}
,
we can find a longest path in
G
whose vertex set is
V(Q)
where both end vertices are not on
P
; let
Q^{\prime}:q^{\prime}_{0}q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots q^{\prime}_{\ell}
be such a path.
That is,
Q^{\prime}
is a longest path in
G
such that
V(Q)=V(Q^{\prime})
and
q_{0}^{\prime},q^{\prime}_{\ell}\not\in V(P)
.
The following holds:

(1)

If
q^{\prime}_{1}\not\in V(P)
or
q^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\not\in V(P)
, then
Q^{\prime}
is the path we seek, so
q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\in V(P)
.

(2)

If
q^{\prime}_{0}q^{\prime}_{\ell}
is an edge, then
q^{\prime}_{\ell}q^{\prime}_{0}q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots q^{\prime}_{\ell-1}
is the path we seek, so
q^{\prime}_{\ell}\not\in N_{G}(q^{\prime}_{0})
.

(3)

If
q^{\prime}_{0}
has a neighbor
q^{\prime}_{j}

(j\geq 2)
such that
q^{\prime}_{j-1},q^{\prime}_{j-2}\in V(Q)\setminus V(P)
, then
Q^{\prime}_{[q^{\prime}_{j-1},q^{\prime}_{0}]}+q^{\prime}_{0}q^{\prime}_{j}+Q^{\prime}_{[q^{\prime}_{j},q^{\prime}_{\ell}]}
is the path we seek, so for every neighbor
q^{\prime}_{j}
of
q^{\prime}_{0}
with
j\geq 2
,
either
q^{\prime}_{j-1}\in V(P)
or
q^{\prime}_{j-2}\in V(P)
.

In other words, since
q^{\prime}_{\ell}\not\in N_{G}(q^{\prime}_{0})
, we know

N_{G}(q^{\prime}_{0})\setminus\{q^{\prime}_{1}\}
is contained in
X=\{q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}_{i+1}\mid q^{\prime}_{i-1}\in V(P)\cap V(Q),i\neq\ell\}
.
For each
q^{\prime}_{i-1}\in V(P)\cap V(Q)
with
i\neq\ell
,
define

X_{i-1}=\begin{cases}\{q^{\prime}_{i}\}&\text{ if }q^{\prime}_{i}\in V(P)\cap V(Q),\\
\{q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}_{i+1}\}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that the union of the sets
X_{i-1}
’s is
X
and the pairwise intersection of
X_{i-1}
’s is empty, so

\{X_{i-1}\mid q^{\prime}_{i-1}\in V(P)\cap V(Q),i\neq\ell\}
is a partition of

with at most
k-2

Since
|N_{G}(q^{\prime}_{0})\setminus\{q^{\prime}_{1}\}|\geq k-1
, by the Pigeonhole Principle,
there is some
q^{\prime}_{i-1}\in V(P)\cap V(Q)
, with

i\neq\ell
such that both vertices in
X_{i-1}=\{q^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}_{i+1}\}
are neighbors of

.
This also implies that

is the path we seek.

5
Concluding remarks

We extended the results of
[
5
]
to provide further evidence for Conjecture
1.1
.
It is worth mentioning that natural generalizations for Claims
4.1
and
4.2
can be easily verified, so developing similar additional structural claims should be useful for proving Conjecture
1.1
for
k\geq 6
.

In
[
4
]
, it was shown that any two longest cycles in a
k
-connected graph share at least
ck^{3/5}
vertices, where
c=1/(\sqrt[3]{256}+3)^{3/5}\approx 0.2615
. By following the argument in
[
4
]
, one can obtain the same conclusion for two longest paths in a
k
-connected graph; that is, every two longest paths in a
k
-connected graph have at least common
ck^{3/5}
vertices.
As far as we know, this is the best known result so far, so any improvement on the growth rate would be interesting.

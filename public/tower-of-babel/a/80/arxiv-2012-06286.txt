(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

JT_{2,p}
. Then
there exists a subsequence of
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that admits an upper

\ell_{2}
-estimate with constant
4
.

The above result yields that every normalized weakly null sequence
in
JT_{2,p}
with a non-zero
\ell_{p}
-vector has a subsequence that is equivalent to
the unit vector basis of
\ell_{2}
.

Combining the above we get the following.

Corollary I.4
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a normalized weakly null sequence in
JT_{2,p}
. Then

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence which is either equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{p}
or equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{2}
.

The above also yields the following.

Corollary I.5
.

The space
JT_{2,p}
is reflexive.

The main result of the fourth section concerns the complemented
subspaces of
JT_{2,p}
. We prove the following.

Theorem I.6
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
JT_{2,p}
not containing

\ell_{p}
.
Then
Y
is a complemented subspace of
JT_{2,p}
isomorphic to
\ell_{2}
.

To prove this theorem we employ some properties of the
\ell_{p}
-vector of a normalized
weakly null sequence
to achieve the following.

Proposition I.7
.

For every subspace
Y
of
JT_{2,p}
not containing
\ell_{p}
there
exist a finite set of branches
T=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{d}\}
and a finite codimensional
subspace
Y_{0}
of
Y
such that the restriction to
Y_{0}
of the natural projection
on the subspace
Z_{T}=\overline{\langle\{e_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}n\in\cup_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{i}\}\rangle}

is an isomorphism.

Since the subspace
Z_{T}
is
Hilbertian it follows that
Y_{0}
is
Hilbertian as well. This yields
that the subspace
Y
is
also a Hilbertian and moreover complemented in
JT_{2,p}
.

As a last result of Section 4 we prove that the space
JT_{2,p}
is
isomorphic to the space
JT_{2,p}^{\mathcal{D}}
, where
JT_{2,p}^{\mathcal{D}}
is the space whose norm
is defined as the norm of

JT_{2,p}

but using the dyadic tree
\mathcal{D}
instead of the infinitely
brancing
tree
\mathcal{T}
. The reflexivity of the spaces

and

implies that the natural preduals of those spaces are

who proved that the preduals of

and

are not

[

]
.

I.1.
The definition of the norm of the space
JT_{2,p}

In this section we define the norm of the space
JT_{2,p}
and we present
some of its basic properties. We start with the following notation.

Notation I.1.1
.

We denote by
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
the set of all positive integers.
Let
\preceq
be a partial order on
\mathbb{N}
, compatible with the standard order (i.e.,
n\leq m
whenever
n\preceq m
), defining an infinitely branching tree
\mathcal{T}_{0}
without a root. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the tree we obtain by subjoining to
\mathcal{T}_{0}
the empty set as a root.

A segment is any set of the form
[m,n]=\{k\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}m\preceq k\preceq n\}
.
We shall denote the segments by the letters
s,t
.
An initial segment is a segment having as its first element the root.
If
s
is a segment and
I
an interval of
\mathbb{N}
, the restriction of
s
to
I

is the segment
s_{|I}=s\cap I
.

A branch is a maximal segment. We shall denote the branches by the letters
\sigma,\tau
.
If
\sigma
is a branch and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
\sigma_{>n}

the set
\sigma_{>n}=\{k\in\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}k>n\}
.
For a branch
\sigma
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we shall call the set
\sigma_{>n}
a final segment.

Also by
\sigma_{<n}
we shall denote the initial segment

\sigma_{<n}=\{k\in\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}k<n\}
.
In a similar manner, we define the initial segment
\sigma_{\leq n}
and the final segment
\sigma_{\geq n}
.

Two final segments

\sigma_{1,>n_{1}}
,
\sigma_{2,>n_{2}}

are said to be incomparable if their first elements are incomparable.
Let us note that

a) if
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{l}
are distinct branches then there exists

k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the final segments
\sigma_{1,>k},\dots,\sigma_{l,>k}
are incomparable.

b) if
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of initial segments there exist a subsequence
(s_{n})_{n\in L}
and an initial segment or branch
s
such that

s_{n}\xrightarrow[n\in L]{}s
in the pointwise topology.

If
M
is an infinite subset of
\mathbb{N}
we denote by
[M]
the set of the
infinite subsets of
M
.

For
x\in c_{00}
and a segment
s
of the tree we define

x_{|s}
be the restriction of
x
to
s

x_{|s}=\sum_{n\in s}x(n)e_{n}
.

Given a Banach space
X
,
by
B_{X}
we denote the unit ball of
X
and by
S_{X}
its the unit
sphere.
If
X
has a Schauder basis
(e_{n})_{n}
, then for
every
x\in X
with

we denote by
\supp(x)
the
support of
x
, i.e.
\supp(x)=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}a_{n}\neq 0\}
.

For a given sequence
(a_{n})_{n}\in\ell_{p}
, its conjugate sequence
is the sequence
(b_{n})_{n}\in S_{\ell_{q}}
such that
\sum_{n}a_{n}b_{n}=\lVert(a_{n})_{n}\rVert_{\ell_{p}}
.

In general, we follow
[
29
]
for standard notation and terminology concerning Banach space theory.

I.1.1.
The norm of the space
JT_{2,p}

For
x\in c_{00}
and a segment
s
we define

\lVert x\rVert_{s}=\lVert x_{|s}\rVert_{\ell_{2}}=\left(\sum_{i\in s}x(i)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Definition I.1.2
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
. On
c_{00}(\mathbb{N})
we define the following norm

\begin{split}\|x\|&=\sup\bigg\{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lVert x\rVert_{s_{k}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\,\text{are pairwise disjoint segments},n\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}\\
&=\sup\bigg\{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\sum_{i\in s_{k}}x(i)^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\,\text{are pairwise disjoint segments},n\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}.\end{split}

The completion of
c_{00}(\mathbb{N})
with respect to the above norm is
denoted by
JT_{2,p}
.

Let us observe, for later use, that

(e_{n})
is a 1-unconditional and boundendly
complete basis of
JT_{2,p}
. Also it is easy to see that for every segment
s
and
x\in c_{00}(\mathbb{N})

it holds

\lVert x\rVert_{s}=\lVert x_{|s}\rVert
.

I.1.2.
An alternative definition of the norm

For every branch
\sigma
of
\mathcal{T}
we set

B^{*}_{\sigma}=\{\sum_{i\in s}a_{i}e^{*}_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}s\,\,\text{is a segment of }\,\sigma\,\text{and}\,(a_{i})_{i\in s}\in B_{\ell_{2}}\}.

For
\phi\in B_{\sigma}^{*}
we say that
\phi
is supported by the segment

\phi=\sum_{i\in s}a_{i}e^{*}_{i}
.
Let
\mathcal{K}=\cup\{B_{\sigma}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\sigma\,\,\text{branch of}\,\,\mathcal{T}\}
.

We set

\displaystyle W=\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\phi_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{q}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\;\phi_{i}\in\mathcal{K}\,\textnormal{
and
are supported by disjoint segments},n\in\mathbb{N}\Bigg\}.

It follows easily that

(1)

W
is symmetric and closed under projections on intervals.

(2)

W
is a norming set of
JT_{2,p}
i.e.

\lVert x\rVert=\sup\{\phi(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\phi\in W\}.

Remark I.1.3
.

i) For
x\in JT_{2,p}
and disjoint segments
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}

\lVert x\rVert^{p}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert x\rVert_{s_{i}}^{p}.

ii) Let

,

with

and

, be pairwise disjoint segments, with the property that

\lVert x\rVert_{s_{i}}\geq\varepsilon
for every

.
Then i) yields that

.

iii) For every

,

that are supported by pairwise disjoint segments,

it holds that

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\phi_{i}(x)\right\rvert}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\lVert x\rVert.

iv) If
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is a finite block
sequence and
\sigma
is a branch then

\lVert x_{1|\sigma}+\dots+x_{n|\sigma}\rVert=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert x_{i|\sigma}\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Lemma I.1.4
.

Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{d}
be distinct branches of
\mathcal{T}
and
\rho\in\mathbb{N}
such that the final segments
\sigma_{i,>\rho}
are incomparable.
Then for
a finitely supported vector

such that

it holds that

\lVert x\rVert=\lVert x_{|\sigma_{1}}+\dots+x_{|\sigma_{d}}\rVert=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lVert x_{|\sigma_{i}}\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Proof.

The incomparability of the final segments
\sigma_{i,\geq\min\supp x}
,
i\leq d
, yields

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lVert x_{|\sigma_{i}}\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\lVert x_{|\sigma_{1}}+\dots+x_{|\sigma_{d}}\rVert

For the reverse inequality,
let us observe that for any segment
s
with
\rho<\min s
there exists
at most one
i\leq d
such that
s\cap\sigma_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Let
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
be pairwise
disjoint segments.
Since
\min\supp(x)>\rho
we may assume that
s_{i}>\rho
for every
i
.
For
i\leq d
set
J_{i}=\{l\leq k\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x_{|\sigma_{i}\cap s_{l}}\neq 0\}
. Then from the above observation,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{k}\lVert x_{|s_{l}}\rVert^{p}=\sum_{l=1}^{k}\lVert(\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{|\sigma_{i}})_{|s_{l}}\rVert^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in J_{i}}\lVert(x_{|\sigma_{i}})_{|s_{l}}\rVert^{p}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lVert x_{|\sigma_{i}}\rVert^{p}.

The above inequality implies

\lVert x_{|\sigma_{1}}+\dots+x_{|\sigma_{d}}\rVert\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lVert x_{|\sigma_{i}}\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}
.
∎

I.2.
\ell_{p}
-sequences in
JT_{2,p}
.

It follows easily from the definition of the norm that for any
subsequence
(e_{k_{n}})_{n}
of the basis such that
(k_{n})_{n}

are incomparable nodes of the tree, the subspace
[e_{k_{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}n\in\mathbb{N}]
is
isometric to
\ell_{p}
and in particular
(e_{k_{n}})_{n}
is

1
-equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{p}
. In this section we provide a
complete characterization of the normalized block sequences which are
equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{p}
. More precisely we shall prove the
following theorem.

Theorem I.2.1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be normalized block sequence in
JT_{2,p}
such
that
\lim_{n}\lVert x_{n|\sigma}\rVert=0
for every branch
\sigma
of
\mathcal{T}
. Then,
there exists a subsequence
(x_{n})_{n\in N}
of
(x_{n})_{n}
that is
equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{p}
. In particular for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a subsequence of

(x_{n})_{n}
which is
(\sqrt[q]{2}+\epsilon)
-equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{p}
.

The proof of the theorem relies on a refinement
of the well known result due to
I. Amemiya and T. Ito
[
2
]
, that every normalized weakly null
sequence in
JT
contains a subsequence which is
2
-equivalent to the
usual basis of
\ell_{2}
, which appeared in
[
8
]
.
We break up the proof of the theorem into several
lemmas and we start with the following Ramsey-type
result.

Lemma I.2.2
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a normalized block sequence in

JT_{2,p}
such that
\lim_{n}\lVert x_{n|\sigma}\rVert=0
for every
branch
\sigma
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an
L\in[\mathbb{N}]
such that for every segment
s
with
\min s\leq n_{0}
there exists at most one
n\in L
with the property that
\lVert x_{n|s}\rVert\geq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Set
[p_{n,}q_{n}]=\ran(x_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If the conclusion is false,
using Ramsey’s theorem we may assume that there exists
L\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}

such that, for every pair
m<n
in
L
, there exists a segment

s_{m,n}
such that
\min s_{m,n}\leq n_{0}
,

\lVert x_{m|s_{m,n}}\rVert\geq\varepsilon
and
\lVert x_{n|s_{m,n}}\rVert\geq\varepsilon
.
For every segment
s_{m,n}
we denote by
\bar{s}_{m,n}
the unique
initial segment determined by
s_{m,n}
.

Claim 1
: For every
n\in L
it holds
\#\{\bar{s}_{m,n}|_{[1,p_{n})}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}m\in L,m<n\}\leq[\epsilon^{-p}]
.

Proof of Claim 1.

If
\#\{\bar{s}_{m,n}|_{[1,p_{n})}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}m\in L,m<n\}>[\epsilon^{-p}]
for some
n\in L
, it follows
that there exist
at least
[\epsilon^{-p}]+1
pairwise disjoint segments

\bar{s}_{m,n}|_{[p_{n},q_{n}]}
such that
\lVert x_{n|\bar{s}_{m,n|[p_{n},q_{n}]}}\rVert\geq\varepsilon
. This contradicts item (ii) of Remark
I.1.3
.
∎

For every
n\in L
, let
\{\bar{s}_{m,n}|_{[1,p_{n})}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}m\in L,m<n\}=\{s_{n,1},\ldots,s_{n,\mu(n)}\}
with
\mu(n)\leq[\epsilon^{-p}]=\mu
. Set

L_{i}^{n}=\{m\in L\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}m<n\;\text{and }\lVert x_{m|s_{n,i}}\rVert\geq\epsilon\},\,\,\,1\leq i\leq\mu(n)

and
L^{n}_{i}=\emptyset
, for
\mu(n)<i\leq\mu
. Notice that

\{m\in L\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}m<n\}=\cup_{i=1}^{\mu}L_{i}^{n}
for all
n\in L
. Passing to a
further subsequence we may assume that, for every
1\leq i\leq\mu
,

(L_{i}^{n})_{n\in{M}}
converges point-wise and we denote that limit
by
L_{i}
. Then it is easy to see that
L=\cup_{i=1}^{\mu}L_{i}
and
hence some
L_{i_{0}}
is an infinite subset of
L
. Moreover for
every
n\in M
and for all
m<n
,
m\in L_{i_{0}}
,
there exists a segment
s_{n,i_{0}}

such that

\lVert x_{m|s_{n,i_{0}}}\rVert\geq\varepsilon
.
Then
there exists
P\in[M]^{\infty}

such that
(s_{n,i_{0}})_{n\in P}
converges point-wise to a branch or an
initial segment.

Claim 2
:

(s_{n,i_{0}})_{n\in P}
converges to a
branch.

Proof of Claim 2.

On the contrary, assume that there exists an initial segment
s_{0}

such that
s_{n,i_{0}}\xrightarrow[P\ni n]{}s_{0}
. Since

\mathcal{T}
is an infinitely branching tree we get
P_{0}\in[P]^{\infty}
such
that the segments
s_{n,i_{0}|(\max s_{0},+\infty)},n\in P_{0}
are
incomparable.
Then for every
m\in L_{i_{0}}
with
\min\supp(x_{m})>\max(s_{0})
it follows that
\lVert x_{m|(s_{n,i_{0}}-s_{0}})\rVert\geq\epsilon

for infinitely many

.
This contradicts item (ii) of Remark
I.1.3
.
∎

Let

\sigma
be the branch such that
s_{n,i_{0}}\xrightarrow[P\ni n]{}\sigma
. It follows
that
\lVert x_{n|\sigma}\rVert\geq\varepsilon
for infinitely many

. This yields a

∎

I.3.

Definition I.3.1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a normalized block sequence in
JT_{2,p}
.
A decreasing sequence
(c_{i})_{i\in N}

or the set of the natural numbers,
is said to be the

\ell_{p}
-vector of

with associated set of branches

,
if
(c_{i})_{i\in N}\in B_{\ell_{p}}
,

\lim_{n}\lVert x_{n|\sigma_{i}}\rVert=c_{i}>0\,\,\text{for every $i\in N$ and }\lim_{n}\lVert x_{n|\sigma}\rVert=0\,\,\text{for every $\sigma\neq\sigma_{i}$},i\in N.

If
\lim_{n}\lVert x_{n|\sigma}\rVert=0
for every branch
\sigma,
then the

\ell_{p}
-vector of
(x_{n})_{n}
is the zero one and the associated
set of branches
\mathcal{B}
is the empty one.

Proposition I.3.2
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a normalized block sequence in
JT_{2,p}
. Then

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
either has a subsequence equivalent to the unit vector basis of

\ell_{p}
-vector.

The proof relies on the following lemma due to Lindenstrauss-Stegall
[

Lemma I.3.3

(
[
28
]
)
.

Let

be a normalized block sequence in

countable set of distinct branches

Moreover

and the sequence

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
.
From the definition of the norm it follows that for every choice of distinct branches
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}

(5)

\limsup_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lVert x_{n|\sigma_{i}}\rVert^{p}\leq\sup_{n}\lVert x_{n}\rVert^{p}\leq 1.

Combining (

(x_{n})_{n\in L}

there exist at most

distinct branches
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}
such that

\lim_{n\in L}\lVert x_{n|\sigma_{i}}\rVert=c_{i}\geq\epsilon
and for every

\sigma\neq\sigma_{j}

\limsup_{n\in L}\lVert x_{n|\sigma}\rVert<\epsilon
.

Let
\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
. Applying the above for
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\dots

L_{1}\supset L_{2}\supset\dots\supset L_{j}\supset L_{j+1}\supset\dots
and

\sigma_{j,1},\dots,\sigma_{j,k_{j}}
distinct branches,

such that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\in L_{j}}\lVert x_{n|\sigma_{j,k}}\rVert=c_{j,k}\geq\epsilon_{j}\,\text{for

\,\,\limsup_{n\in L_{j}}\lVert x_{n|\sigma}\rVert<\epsilon_{j}\,\,\forall\sigma\neq\sigma_{i,k},k=1,\dots,k_{i},i=1,\dots,j.\end{split}

Reordering

The inductive choice stops in the case we cannot find

and
a branch
\sigma

.
Enumerate the
c_{j,k}\neq 0
and the associated
\sigma_{j,k,}

as

(c_{i})_{i\in N}
and
(\sigma_{i})_{i\in N}
respectively, following the
natural order
of
c_{j,k},k\leq k_{j},j=1,2,\dots
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{\sigma_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}i\in N\}

be a diagonal subsequence of
(x_{n})_{n\in L_{j}}

Once we show that

we have the result for

and
\mathcal{B}_{0}
.
Assume that

Then
\sum_{i\in F}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{i}\right\rvert}^{p}>1

II.1.

In this section we recall the definition of the Schreier families

The Schreier families form an increasing sequence of families of finite subsets of the natural numbers and they first appeared in

\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert x_{n}^{\sigma}\rVert_{s_{i}}e_{t_{s_{i}}}\rVert_{S^{(2)}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert x_{n}^{\sigma}\rVert_{s_{i}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\in s_{i}\cap s_{n}^{\sigma}}\beta_{j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq 1.

Lemma II.3.4

For every

II.4.

-estimates

In this section we will prove the theorem bellow. By slightly abusing language, we will say that a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in a Banach space admits an upper
S^{(2)}
-estimate with constant
C
, if it is
C
-dominated by a subsequence of the unit vector basis of
S^{(2)}
.

Theorem II.4.1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a normalized block sequence in
JT_{G}
such that

\lim_{n}\lVert P_{\sigma}(x_{n})\rVert=0
, for every branch
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})
. Then,
there exists a subsequence
(x_{n})_{n\in L}

-estimate with constant
3
.

We start with the following remark.

Remark II.4.2
.

Note that if for some
f\in W(\sigma)
and an interval
I
we have that

\lvert f_{|I}(x)\rvert\geq\epsilon
it does not follow that

\lVert P_{\sigma}(x)\rVert
is necessarily comparable to

\lvert f_{|I}(x)\rvert

For example, let
x_{n}^{\sigma}=m_{2j_{n}+1}^{2}\sum_{k\in s_{n}^{\sigma}}\beta_{k}e_{k}

On rigidity of conformal submersions
Atreyee Bhattacharya and Sayoojya Prakash

Abstract
. Conformal submersions are generalizations of Riemannian submersions where the differential of the submersion map is a conformal isometry when restricted to the horizontal distribution. Riemannian submersions have been instrumental in the construction of new Riemannian metrics from existing ones. Therefore, being generalizations of Riemannian submersions, conformal submersions are potential tools for constructing new examples of (special) Riemannian manifolds. A conformal submersion is said to be rigid if it reduces to a Riemannian submersion up to homothety. In this paper, we study curvature conditions that force conformal submersions to be rigid. Moreover, under suitable circumstances, using these rigidity criteria for conformal submersions, we conclude the rigidity of certain quasi-Einstein metrics.

1.
Introduction

Based on curvature properties, Riemannian manifolds are broadly classified into following three categories: the
space forms (manifolds with constant sectional curvature), Einstein manifolds (manifolds with constant Ricci
curvature), and locally conformally flat manifolds (manifolds with vanishing Weyl curvature). While the
topology of a space form is completely well known via its curvature properties, conditions under which a
smooth manifold admits an Einstein or a locally conformally flat metric, are yet to be fully understood.
Creating more examples of Einstein and locally conformally flat manifolds is thus a necessary step to
understand more about these spaces. To this end, Riemannian submersions have been used as standard tools
to construct special classes of Einstein manifolds and classify them (
[
2
,
21
,
11
,
22
,
3
]
). Conformal submersions are generalizations of Riemannian submersions where the differential of the
submersion map is a conformal isometry when restricted to its horizontal distribution (see Definition

2.9
for details). The structural equations (connecting the geometry of the total
space, fibres and the base) of a conformal submersion are immediate from the corresponding equations of
Riemannian submersions. Despite this, conformal submersions are far from being well studied as compared
to Riemannian submersions, apart from making sporadic appearances in Riemannian geometry and theoretical
physics (
[
20
,
13
,
14
]
). On the other hand, as generalizations of Riemannian submersions,
conformal submersions are potential tools for the construction of more examples of Riemannian manifolds.
In view of this, one would like to understand geometric conditions that force a conformal submersion to
become a Riemannian submersion up to homothety, and in that case, the conformal submersion under
consideration is called
rigid.
Recently, the rigidity of conformal submersions was proved for a
special class of
contact metric manifolds
(
[
1
]
) using standard contact Riemannian
geometry techniques. However, in general, not much is known about the rigidity of conformal submersions
that the authors are aware of.

It is to be noted that in Riemannian geometry and geometric analysis, analysing
rigidity
of
standard geometric properties (such as curvature properties, existence of special maps, etc.) of
Riemannian manifolds has been a key technique in identifying when a given manifold can admit a special
Riemannian metric. Although there are several interpretations of rigidity of a geometric property, in
this paper, we will restrict to the following. A (geometric) property
\mathcal{P}
on a Riemannian
manifold
(M,g)
is said to be
rigid
if it has one of the following features:

(1)

Certain constraints are imposed on (the underlying topology of)
M
due to the existence of
\mathcal{P}
. In other words,
(M,g)
fails to possess
\mathcal{P}
unless it has the desired underlying structure.

(2)

\mathcal{P}
generalises a property
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, but under some conditions, the existence of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
are equivalent.

The above notions of rigidity will be referred to as Type (1) and Type (2), respectively. While Type (1)
is the strongest form of rigidity (e.g., constant sectional curvature, Ricci curvature bounded below by a
positive constant, etc.) as it restricts the underlying topology of a manifold, rigidity of a conformal
submersion is of Type (2). In fact, in this paper, we discuss two rigidity problems of Type (2), as elaborated below.

We first study curvature conditions that impose rigidity on conformal submersions where the total space, fibres, and base are
all closed orientable Riemannian manifolds, and the total space has at least dimension four. Our analysis mostly uses
classical techniques from Riemannian geometry, such as the weak maximal principle and the divergence theorem. Recall that in
a Riemannian submersion, the fundamental tensors
T
and
A
represent obstructions to the fibres being totally geodesic and
the integrability of the horizontal distribution, respectively, and
N
denotes the trace of
T
. Analogously, there are
fundamental tensors
T
and
A
for a conformal submersion (see Section
2.2
for details). In particular, we prove the
following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion with fibre
F
, where the total space
(M,g)

is the product of closed Einstein manifolds. Let
\lambda_{i}
denote the Einstein constant of the
i^{th}

component of
(M,g)
, and
m_{i}
and
k_{i}
denote the respective dimensions of horizontal and vertical distributions restricted to the same component.
Then
\pi
is rigid if it satisfies one of the following three conditions.

(1)

The horizontal distribution is integrable, and
N
is parallel, together with either of the following properties:

(a)

The fibres are Einstein (with the same Einstein constant) and

dim(B)\geq 2
,

(b)

The base
(B,g_{B})
is Ricci-flat and

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}m_{i}=-|T|^{2}-\frac{|N|^{2}}{\dim(F)}(\dim(M)+\dim(B)-4)
.

(2)

The tensor
N
vanishes together with either of the following conditions:

(a)

The fibres are Einstein (with Einstein constant
\lambda_{F}
),
\dim(B)\geq 2
, and

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}k_{i}=\lambda_{F}dim(F)+|A|^{2}
,

(b)

(B,g_{B})
is Ricci-flat and
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}m_{i}+2|A|^{2}+|T|^{2}=0
,

(3)

The fibres are totally geodesic,
(B,g_{B})
is Ricci-flat with
\dim(B)\geq 2
, and

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}m_{i}+2|A|^{2}=0
.

Using Theorem
1.1
, we make the following interesting observation.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Einstein manifold of dimension at least
4
, which is the total space of a conformal submersion

\pi\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
with integrable horizontal distribution and vanishing
T
. Then up to homothety,

g
is the unique Einstein metric in its conformal class whenever the fibres are one dimensional or
the base is Ricci-flat.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.2
says in particular that a closed Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
that is a Riemannian product, admits a unique Einstein metric in the conformal class of
g
, up to homothety.

The next result deals with the rigidity of conformal submersions with locally conformally flat total space.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\pi\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion with fibre
(F,g_{F})
such that
(M,g)
is locally conformally flat and
dim(B)=dim(F)
. If the scalar curvatures of the total space and fibres have particular signs (not necessarily, the same sign) and
(F,g_{F},h,m,\lambda)
is quasi-Einstein, then
\pi
is rigid if either of the following conditions hold.

(1)

T=0
, the scalar curvature of the base is non-positive, and
\lambda\leq 0
.

(2)

A=0
, parallel
T
, the scalar curvature of the base is non-negative, and
\lambda\geq 0
.

Remark 1.5
.

We also provide criteria for rigidity of conformal submersions similar to Theorem
1.4
by assuming either the total space or the base to be
quasi-Einstein
(see Theorems
4.5
and
4.8
). In the following paragraph,
quasi-Einstein manifolds
are briefly discussed; for more details on the same, we refer to Section
2.3
.

Remark 1.6
.

The condition
dim(F)=dim(B)
in Theorem
1.4
is necessary, and can be verified using Example
4.10
.

It turns out that under suitable circumstances, the rigidity of conformal submersions, is naturally related to the rigidity of a special class of Riemannian manifolds known as
quasi-Einstein manifolds
, a generalization of Einstein manifolds. More precisely, a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be quasi-Einstein if its Ricci curvature
Ric_{g}
satisfies the identity:

\ Ric_{g}-\lambda g=Hess(f)-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df,

for some
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and constants
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<m\leq\infty
. A quasi-Einstein manifold is called rigid if it reduces to being Einstein. Quasi-Einstein manifolds and their rigidity are topics of interest both in Riemannian geometry and theoretical physics (cf.
[
9
,
15
,
8
,
19
,
18
,
17
,
23
,
5
,
4
]
).
In this paper, we obtain rigidity criteria for a closed quasi-Einstein manifold by realizing it as the base or fibre of a suitable conformal submersion and then using the aforementioned rigidity criteria for conformal submersions. This seems to be a natural approach as Riemannian submersions have been used to answer analogous questions in the context of Einstein metrics (cf.
[
22
,
3
]
). Since all closed quasi-Einstein manifolds of dimension two are known to be rigid (
[
4
,
9
]
), we consider quasi-Einstein manifolds of dimensions at least
3
and prove the following result.

Theorem 1.7
.

A closed orientable quasi-Einstein manifold
(M,g,f,m,\lambda)
is rigid if it satisfies one of the following criteria.

(1)

m\geq 1
,
\lambda\geq 0
and
(M,g)
is the fibre (respectively, the base) of a conformal submersion with
integrable horizontal distribution, parallel
T
, locally conformally flat total space having twice the dimension of

M
, and where the base (respectively, the fibre) has non-negative scalar curvature.

(2)

(M,g)
is the fibre of a conformal submersion with integrable horizontal distribution, vanishing
T
and homogeneous Einstein total space, and where the base is Einstein with a non-positive Einstein constant.

Remark 1.8
.

We obtain rigidity results for a quasi-Einstein manifold similar to Theorem
1.7
by realizing it as the total space of a conformal submersion (see Theorem
5.2
) and conclude the following result (see Example
5.3
for details).

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
(M,g,h,m,\lambda)
be a locally conformally flat quasi-Einstein manifold of dimension
\geq 4,
where
m\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 0
. If the universal cover of
(M,g)
is closed and the Riemannian product of two manifolds of equal dimensions, then
(M,g)
is a space form.

Preliminaries

The Riemann curvature tensor of

\displaystyle(h\circ k)(X,Y,Z,W)=

\displaystyle h(X,Z)k(Y,W)+h(Y,W)k(X,Z)-h(X,W)k(Y,Z)

\displaystyle-h(Y,Z)k(X,W),\ \ \ \forall\,h,\,k\,\in\,S^{2}(M).

Definition 2.3
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be

(1)

(2)

(3)

if given any point

Remark 2.4

A Riemannian manifold

2.2.
Conformal submersions.

Definition 2.9
.

Let

(B,g_{B})

Remark 2.10

(1)

Given a conformal submersion
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})

(2)

Conformal submersions generalize Riemannian submersions, and when

This leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.11
.

A conformal submersion

Remark 2.12
.

In this paper, the following conventions will be followed.

(1)

Given a vector field

(2)

Given

.

(3)

Given a conformal submersion

\mathcal{H}(\Delta h):=\sum_{i}g(\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla h,X_{i})

\{X_{i}\}{i\in I}

Remark 2.13
.

In a conformal submersion
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})

are totally geodesic if and only if

vanishes for all vertical vector fields

Definition 2.14

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion. Then fibres of

are said to be

(that is, all fibres are totally umbilical submanifolds of

) if there exists a horizontal vector field
X

\mathcal{H}\nabla_{U}V=g(U,V)X
for all vertical vector fields
U,V
(cf.
[
24
]
).

Remark 2.15
.

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion. It follows that if
T
vanishes, then fibres of
\pi_{f}
are totally umbilical.

2.3.
Quasi-Einstein manifolds.

In this section, we briefly discuss a special class of Riemannian manifolds known as quasi-Einstein manifolds, which are certain generalizations of Einstein manifolds. We refer to
[
4
,
5
,
8
,
23
]
for detailed discussions on quasi-Einstein manifolds.

Definition 2.16
.

A
smooth metric measure space
is a triple
(M,g,f)
, where
(M,g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Given a smooth metric measure space
(M,g,f)
, its
m
-Bakry-Emery Ricci tensor
denoted as
Ric_{f}^{m}
is defined by

Ric_{f}^{m}=Ric+Hess(f)-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df,

where
Hess(f)(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\nabla f,Y)
and
0<m\leq\infty
.

Definition 2.17
.

A smooth metric measure space
(M,g,f,m)
is called a
quasi-Einstein manifold
if
Ric_{f}^{m}=\lambda g
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 2.18
.

It is easy to see that a quasi-Einstein manifold
(M,g,f,m)
becomes Einstein if
f
is constant. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.19
.

A quasi-Einstein manifold
(M,g,f,m,\lambda)
is said to be

if
f
is a constant function.

Remark 2.20
.

From now on, we will restrict ourselves to closed-orientable Riemannian manifolds and conformal submersions with total space of dimension at least
4
.

3.
Conformal submersions with Einstein total space

In this section, we analyse the rigidity of conformal submersions where either the total space or its universal cover is the Riemannian product of closed-orientable Einstein manifolds. In both cases, the Ricci tensor turns out to be parallel. This section is divided into three subsections. The first subsection discusses the rigidity when the horizontal distribution is integrable and
N
is parallel which is a more general assumption than that of
T
being parallel (cf. Lemma
4.6
of
[
12
]
). In the second subsection, we discuss the rigidity when
N
vanishes (a weaker assumption than
T=0
, particularly when the dimension of fibres is at least
2
) and the horizontal distribution is not necessarily integrable. The last subsection deals with the rigidity when fibres are totally geodesic. Throughout this section, we will have the following two assumptions (even when it is not explicitly mentioned): (a). the total space or its universal cover is the Riemannian product of closed Einstein manifolds and (b). whenever fibres are assumed to be Einstein, all the fibres have the same Einstein constant.
For the sake of brevity, proofs of all the results in this section are only showcased when the total space is Einstein. However, the same idea also works when either the total space or its universal cover is the Riemannian product of closed-orientable Einstein manifolds.

3.1.
When the horizontal distribution is integrable and
N
is parallel.

The following theorem addresses the rigidity of conformal submersions with Einstein fibres.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion with integrable horizontal distribution and parallel
N
and
dim(B)\geq 2
. Then
\pi_{f}
is rigid if the fibres are Einstein.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality, that
(M,g)
is Einstein with Einstein constant
\lambda
, and
\lambda_{F}
is the Einstein constant of the fibres. First, we show that
\lambda=\lambda_{F}
. The following equation can be derived by using Equation
2.12
.

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lambda\,dim(F)=

\displaystyle\lambda_{F}\,dim(F)-|N|^{2}+(2dim(F)-dim(B))N(f)

\displaystyle+dim(F)(dim(B)-dim(F))|\mathcal{H}(\nabla f)|^{2}-dim(F)\mathcal{H}(\Delta f)

\displaystyle+dim(B)(dim(F)-1)|\mathcal{V}(\nabla f)|^{2}-dim(B)\mathcal{V}(\Delta f)

Consequently, we observe that
\lambda\leq\lambda_{F}
as
Hess(f)
is non-negative definite and
\nabla f
vanishes at the point where
f
attains its global minimum. The equation below can then be derived from
Equation

3.1
by using the fact that
N
is parallel.

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lambda\,dim(F)=

\displaystyle\lambda_{F}\,dim(F)-dim(F)div(\mathcal{H}(\nabla f))

\displaystyle-dim(B)div(\mathcal{V}(\nabla f))+dim(B)(dim(B)-1)|\mathcal{V}(\nabla f)|^{2}

Further, using the divergence theorem in Equation
3.2
, it can be seen that
\lambda\geq\lambda_{F}
. Thus,
\lambda=\lambda_{F}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{V}(\nabla f)
vanishes by employing the divergence theorem in Equation
3.2
. By substituting
\mathcal{V}(\nabla f)=0
in Equation
3.2
, and applying the weak maximal principle, the function
f
turns out to be constant. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In particular, Theorem
3.1
implies that in a conformal submersion with integrable horizontal distribution and parallel
N
, if the total space and fibres are both Einstein, then their Einstein constants must coincide.

The following rigidity result assumes the base of the given conformal submersion to be Ricci-flat.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
is a conformal submersion with integrable horizontal distribution and parallel
N
, where
(B,g_{B})
is Ricci-flat, then
\pi_{f}
is rigid if and only if

\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}m_{i}=-|T|^{2}-\frac{|N|^{2}}{dim(F)}(dim(M)+dim(B)-4),

where
\lambda_{i}
and
m_{i}
are the Einstein constants and dimensions of the horizontal distribution of the
i^{th}
component of
(M,g)
or its universal cover, respectively.

Proof.

Assuming that
(M,g)
is Einstein with Einstein constant
\lambda
, it follows from Equation
2.14
that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lambda\,dim(B)=

\displaystyle-|T|^{2}-\frac{|N|^{2}}{dim(F)}(dim(M)+dim(B)-4)-(dim(M)-2)

\displaystyle div(\mathcal{H}(\nabla f))+(dim(M)-2)(dim(M)-1)|\mathcal{H}(\nabla f)|^{2}-dim(B)\Delta f.

If
\lambda\,dim(B)=-|T|^{2}-\frac{|N|^{2}}{dim(F)}(dim(M)+dim(B)-4)
, then by the divergence theorem and the weak maximal principle, we conclude that
\pi_{f}
is rigid.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion with integrable horizontal distribution and
parallel
N
. If both
(M,g)
and
(B,g_{B})
are Einstein with Einstein constants
\lambda
and
\lambda_{B}
,
respectively, then by Equation

\lambda\leq c\lambda_{B}
where

(B,g_{B})
is

(M,g)
is a homogeneous Einstein manifold, then
(M,g)
is

of
[

).

Since the Einstein constant of a compact homogeneous Einstein manifold, is always non-negative, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
is a conformal submersion with integrable horizontal distribution and parallel
N
, where either
(M,g)
or its universal cover is the Riemannian product of homogeneous Einstein manifolds and
(B,g_{B})

\pi_{f}
is rigid if and only if

(M,g)
is a symmetric space.

3.2.
When

vanishes identically.

The following result is about the rigidity of conformal submersions with Einstein fibres.

\displaystyle(\lambda-\lambda_{F})dim(F)=

The following theorem addresses the rigidity conditions when the base is Ricci-flat.

Theorem 3.12

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})

Using Equation

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lambda\,dim(B)=

\displaystyle-2|A|^{2}-|T|^{2}-(dim(M)-2)div(\mathcal{H}(\nabla f))

\displaystyle+(dim(M)-2)(dim(M)-1)|\mathcal{H}(\nabla f)|^{2}-dim(B)\Delta f

Remark 3.13
.

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion with vanishing

and

are Einstein with Einstein constants

The following rigidity result considers conformal submersion with a Ricci-flat base.

Corollary 3.14

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion with vanishing

, such that
(B,g_{B})
is Ricci-flat and either

or its universal cover is the Riemannian product of compact homogeneous Einstein manifolds. Then
\pi_{f}
is rigid if and only if its horizontal distribution is integrable and

is a symmetric space.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.12
, together with Equations
2.12
and
2.14

Corollary 3.15
.

If
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
is a conformal submersion with integrable horizontal distribution and vanishing

, where

is Ricci-flat, then
\pi_{f}
is rigid. In particular, if
(M,g)
is Einstein, then
(M,g)

Remark 3.16
.

It is well known that a Kähler manifold
(M,g)
of dimension at least

(see

for the definition of Kähler manifolds) with harmonic Weyl tensor has parallel Ricci tensor (Proposition

Remark 3.17

The rigidity of warped product Einstein metrics is a well-explored problem (

Let

Using Theorem

If

In particular, Theorem

3.3.
When the fibres are totally geodesic.

The following theorem discusses rigidity conditions of a conformal submersion with a Ricci-flat base in this case.

.

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion with totally geodesic fibres and

, where
(B,g_{B})

\pi_{f}
is rigid if and only if
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}m_{i}+2|A|^{2}=0
, where
\lambda_{i}
and
m_{i}
are the Einstein constants and dimensions of the horizontal distribution of the
i^{th}
component of
(M,g)
or its universal cover, respectively.

WE use Equation

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lambda\,dim(B)=

\displaystyle-2|A|^{2}-(dim(B)-2)div(\mathcal{H}(\nabla f))-dim(B)\Delta f

Let

When

Remark 3.21

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})

Using Equation

If
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})

Remark 3.23

It should be noted that unlike Riemannian submersions, having totally geodesic fibres and integrable horizontal distribution, does not necessarily mean that the total space of a conformal submersion is locally a Riemannian product.

Remark 3.24

If

is a Kähler manifold of dimension at least

with harmonic Weyl tensor and a closed universal cover, then by Corollary

, a conformal submersion with integrable horizontal distribution, totally geodesic fibres, Ricci-flat base, and

Remark 3.25

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})

Remark 3.26

Let

4.
Conformal submersions with locally conformally flat total space

In this section, we consider conformal submersions whose total spaces are Riemannian products of
locally conformally flat and Einstein manifolds (where the Einstein components are possibly trivial), and study their rigidity. We further assume that the base and the fibres have the same dimension. In such a case, we provide suitable curvature conditions imposing rigidity.

4.1.
The total space is the product of locally conformally flat and Einstein manifolds.

In conformal submersions
whose total spaces are Riemannian products of locally conformally flat and Einstein manifolds (where the Einstein components are
possibly trivial), we make the following observations about the scalar curvature of the components of the total space.

Proposition 4.1
.

(1)

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion such that
(M,g)
is locally conformally flat and
dim(B)=dim(F)
where
F_{p}
denotes the fibre at
p\in B
. Then the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
satisfies the following equation.

(4.1)

\footnotesize\frac{ns}{2(n-1)}=2(|A|^{2}-|T|^{2}-\hat{\delta}N)-(n-4)N(f)-n\Delta f+\frac{n(n-2)}{2}|\nabla f|^{2},

where
n=dim(M)
.

(2)

More generally, consider a conformal submersion
\pi_{f}\colon(M,g)\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
where
(M,g)
is the Riemannian product of locally conformally flat manifolds each of dimension at least
4
such that the horizontal and the vertical distributions restricted to each component of
(M,g)
have the same dimension. Then,

(4.2)

\footnotesize\sum_{i}\frac{n_{i}s_{i}}{2(n_{i}-1)}=2(|A|^{2}-|T|^{2}-\hat{\delta}N)-(n-4)N(f)-n\Delta f+\frac{n(n-2)}{2}|\nabla f|^{2},

where
n_{i}
and
s_{i}
respectively denote the dimension and the scalar curvature of the
i^{th}
component of
(M,g)
, and
n=dim(M)
.

(3)

Let
\pi_{f}\colon(M\times N,g_{M}+g_{N})\rightarrow(B,g_{B})
be a conformal submersion where
(M,g_{M})
is as in
(2)

4.2.

Example 4.10

Lemma 4.11

If

By Equation

The following result is an immediate consequence of Lemma

5.
An Application: Rigidity of quasi-Einstein manifolds

In this section, we conclude rigidity results for certain quasi-Einstein manifolds that can be realized as the total space, the fibre or the base of suitable conformal submersions. All the quasi-Einstein manifolds mentioned here are closed and orientable. The first result is about a quasi-Einstein manifold realized as the fibre or the base of a conformal submersion.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M,g,f,m,\lambda)
be a quasi-Einstein manifold with
\lambda\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
. Then
(M,g,f)
is rigid if
(M,g)
is the fibre (respectively, the base) of a conformal submersion with an integrable horizontal distribution and parallel
T
satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

The total space of the conformal submersion is locally conformally flat and its dimension is twice the dimension of
M
.

(2)

The base (respectively, the fibre) has non-negative scalar curvature.

Proof.

Proposition
3.6
of
[
4
]
says that‘
If
(M,g,f,m,\lambda)
is quasi-Einstein with
m\geq 1
,
\lambda>0
and
M
is compact, then the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
is bounded below by

\frac{n(n-1)\lambda}{m+n-1}
’. It readily follows from the above result that the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
(as in hypothesis) is non-negative if
\lambda>0
. Using the same idea of Proposition
3.6
of
[
4
]
, it can further be checked that the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
is non-negative whenever
\lambda=0
. Using Theorem
4.8
, we conclude the result.
∎

The next result is about a locally conformally flat quasi-Einstein manifold realized as the total space of a conformal submersion.

Theorem 5.2
.

If
(M,g,f,m,\lambda)
is a locally conformally flat quasi-Einstein manifold of dimension at least 4 with
m\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 0
, then
(M,g,f)
is a flat space form if it is the total space of a conformal submersion with an integrable horizontal distribution and parallel
N
whose base and fibre have the same dimension.

Proof.

Again, by using Proposition
3.6
of
[
4
]
, the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
is non-negative. Finally, Theorem
4.5
concludes the result.
∎

Example 5.3
.

Let
(M_{1}\times M_{2},g)
be a locally conformally flat manifold with

, and

A quasi-Einstein manifold

Multi-Objective Trust-Region Filter Method for Nonlinear Constraints using Inexact Gradients
\firstname Manuel \lastname Berkemeier, \firstname Sebastian \lastname Peitz

Abstract
In this article, we build on previous work to present an optimization algorithm for non-linearly constrained multi-objective optimization problems. The algorithm combines a surrogate-assisted derivative-free trust-region approach with the filter method known from single-objective optimization. Instead of the true objective and constraint functions, so-called fully linear models are employed, and we show how to deal with the gradient inexactness in the composite step setting, adapted from single-objective optimization as well. Under standard assumptions, we prove convergence of a subset of iterates to a quasi-stationary point and, if constraint qualifications hold, then the limit point is also a KKT-point of the multi-objective problem.

Introduction

Multi-objective optimization problems (MOPs) arise naturally in many areas of mathematics, engineering, in the natural sciences or
in economics.
The goal of multi-objective optimization (MOO) is to find acceptable trade-offs between the competing objectives of a
multi-objective optimization problem.
Generally, there are multiple solutions constituting the so-called Pareto Set in variable space
and the Pareto Front in objective space.
In this article, we consider the problem

\min_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})\\
\vdots\\
f_{K}(\boldsymbol{x})\end{bmatrix}=\min_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\boldsymbol{h}(\boldsymbol{x})=[h_{1}(\boldsymbol{x}),\ldots,h_{M}(\boldsymbol{x})]^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}=\boldsymbol{0},\;\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x})=[g_{1}(\boldsymbol{x}),\ldots,g_{P}(\boldsymbol{x})]^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}\leq\boldsymbol{0},

(MOP)

where all functions are twice continuously differentiable.
A global Pareto-optimal point
\boldsymbol{x}^{*}
is feasible and non-dominated, i.e.,
there is no other feasible
\boldsymbol{x}\neq\boldsymbol{x}^{*}
with
\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})
and

f_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{x})<f_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{x}^{*})
for some
{\ell}\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
.
Throughout this article we will refer to the feasible set as

\mathcal{X}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\boldsymbol{h}(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{0},\,\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\boldsymbol{0}\right\}
.

Pareto Set/Front (or a superset thereof)

the demands of the person seeking a solution.

Trust-Region Concepts and Surrogates

As mentioned in the previous section,
we assume (at least some) of the objectives or constraints to be computationally expensive.
To approximate a Pareto-critical point whilst avoiding expensive function evaluations,
a trust-region approach is used :
The true functions
\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{h}
and
\boldsymbol{g}
are modeled by
\boldsymbol{\hat{f}}^{(k)},\boldsymbol{\hat{h}}^{(k)}
and
\boldsymbol{\hat{g}}^{(k)}
respectively.
These models are constructed to be
sufficiently accurate within iteration-dependent trust-regions

B^{(k)}=B(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)};\Delta_{(k)}):=\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}\right\|_{\mathrm{tr,k}}\leq\Delta_{(k)}\right\}.

Using the surrogate models, a step
\boldsymbol{s}^{(k)}
and a step-size
\sigma^{(k)}
are
determined – in such a way as to reduce the constraint violation or
achieve an objective value reduction at the
trial point
\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}+\sigma^{(k)}\boldsymbol{s}^{(k)}
,
which is tested as a candidate for the next iterate.
The step
\sigma^{(k)}\boldsymbol{s}^{(k)}
is computed so that the trial point is contained
in both the trust-region and the approximate linearized feasible set at
\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}
:

\mathcal{L}_{k}=\mathcal{L}_{k}(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})=\left\{\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}+\boldsymbol{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\boldsymbol{\hat{h}}^{(k)}(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})+\boldsymbol{\hat{{H}}}_{k}(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})\cdot\boldsymbol{s}=\boldsymbol{0},\;\boldsymbol{\hat{g}}^{(k)}(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})+\boldsymbol{\hat{{G}}}_{k}(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})\cdot\boldsymbol{s}\leq\boldsymbol{0}\right\},

(3)

where
\boldsymbol{\hat{{H}}}_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{\hat{{G}}}_{k}
are now the full model Jacobians.
This introduces uncertainty and iterates might no longer be feasible.
Hence, we treat the constraints as
relaxable
, and we might have to evaluate
\boldsymbol{f}

(and
\boldsymbol{g}
and
\boldsymbol{h}
) outside of
\mathcal{X}
, which motivates the next assumption.
It states that all functions have to be available in all possible trust-regions.
Note, that the algorithm actually can use two trust-region sizes,
the preliminary size
\bar{\Delta}_{(k)}
at the beginning of an iteration
and
\Delta_{(k)}\leq\bar{\Delta}_{(k)}
after the
Criticality Routine
,
which is described in
Section

3.1
.

Assumption 2
.

There is a constant
0<\Delta_{\text{max}}<\infty
such that
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the trust region sizes conform to

0<\Delta_{(k)}\leq\bar{\Delta}_{(k)}<\Delta_{\text{max}}
.
All functions (the true functions and their models)
are defined in these regions, i.e.,
on the set

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X})=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}B\left(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)};\bar{\Delta}_{(k)}\right)

2.1
Fully Linear Models

In this subsection, we first want to explain what it means for the model functions
to be sufficiently accurate.
Although the true derivative information is not used in any computation,
we will need it for the convergence

1

Assumption 3
.

The objective functions
f_{\ell},\,{\ell}=1,\ldots,K
,
the constraint functions
g_{\ell},\,1,\ldots,P
,
and
h_{\ell},\,{\ell}=1,\ldots,M
,
are twice continuously differentiable in an open domain containing
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X})

and have Lipschitz continuous gradients on
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X})
.

With
3
we can define models satisfying
error bounds w.r.t. to their construction radius :

Definition 1

(Fully Linear Models)
.

Let
\Delta_{\text{max}}>0
be a given constant and let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
scalar-valued function
satisfying
3

A set of model functions
\mathcal{M}=\{\hat{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\}

1.

There are positive constants
\mathtt{e},\dot{\mathtt{e}}

\Delta\in(0,\Delta_{\text{max}}]

\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!{f}(\boldsymbol{\xi})-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!{\boldsymbol{\hat{f}}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right\|_{2}\leq\dot{\mathtt{e}}\Delta\quad\text{and}\quad\left|f(\boldsymbol{\xi})-\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|\leq\mathtt{e}\Delta^{2},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in B\left(\boldsymbol{x};\Delta\right)\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X}).

2.

For this class

B\left(\boldsymbol{x};\Delta\right)

Definition 2
.

Let
\Delta_{\text{max}}>0
be a given constant and let
\boldsymbol{f}=[f_{1},\ldots,f_{K}]^{\mkern-1.5mu\mathsf{T}}

Composite Step Approach and Sub-Problems

Just like in previous articles on multi-objective trust-region

2.3
Filter Mechanism

As mentioned already, iterates can become infeasible.
We employ a so-called
Filter
to drive them back towards
the feasible set.
Thus, the trial point
\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{s}}=\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}+\boldsymbol{s}^{(k)}

is tested not only
for actual decrease of the original functions
but also against previous
iterates stored in the filter
\mathcal{F}
.

\mathcal{F}
is a set of tuples
({\theta}_{j},\Phi_{j})
describing a forbidden area
in image space.
In fact, the tuples in
\mathcal{F}
(w.r.t.
\boldsymbol{f}
) are currently non-dominated for
the bi-objective optimization problem of minimizing both
\theta(\boldsymbol{x})
and
\Phi\bigl[\boldsymbol{f}\bigr]\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)
.
The trial point
\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{s}}
is only acceptable for
\mathcal{F}
if its value
tuple is also (sufficiently) non-dominated.
An acceptable trial point is further tested and if it sufficiently reduces the
true

objectives it is kept as the next iterate.
The current iterate might be added to the filter if the predicted objective decrease
is small compared to the constraint violation, or if the latter is too large.
More formally, we use the following definition :

Definition 4

(Multi-Objective Filter)
.

A filter
\mathcal{F}
with respect to some function

\boldsymbol{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{K}

is a discrete set of tuples
\{({\theta}_{j},\Phi_{j})\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
and a point
\boldsymbol{x}
is
acceptable
for the filter iff

{\theta}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\leq(1-\gamma_{{\theta}}){\theta}_{j}\quad\text{or}\quad\Phi\bigl[\boldsymbol{f}\bigr]\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\leq\Phi_{j}-\gamma_{{\theta}}{\theta}_{j}\quad\forall({\theta}_{j},\Phi_{j})\in\mathcal{F}.

When a point
\boldsymbol{x}
is
added
to the filter the tuple

({\theta}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right),\Phi\bigl[\boldsymbol{f}\bigr]\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right))
is added to the set
\mathcal{F}
, but all
tuples
({\theta}_{j},\Phi_{j})
with

{\theta}_{j}\geq{\theta}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\text{ and }\Phi_{j}-\gamma_{{\theta}}{\theta}_{j}\geq\Phi\bigl[\boldsymbol{f}\bigr]\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)-\gamma_{{\theta}}{\theta}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)

are removed from the set.

As can be seen from
4
a filter strengthens non-dominance testing
by employing a positive offset
\gamma_{{\theta}}{\theta}_{j}
.
Note, that we could also use a stricter,

(K+1)
-dimensional filter instead of the
2
-dimensional filter,
similar to
[
29
]
, by using
\boldsymbol{f}({\bullet})
instead of
\Phi\bigl[\boldsymbol{f}\bigr]\left({\bullet}\right)
.

7
Conclusion and Outlook

In this article, we have presented an algorithm for non-linearly constrained
multi-objective optimization problems.
The method does not necessarily need derivative information of the functions
but can use surrogate models instead.
Additionally, a Filter ensures convergence towards feasibility.
We have proven convergence of an algorithmic sub-sequence to Pareto-criticality
and confirm the theoretical results with numerical experiments.

The experiments have shown both promising features of the algorithm compared to
naive scalarizations and potential drawbacks.

—

Like many gradient-based multi-objective optimization algorithms, there often seems to be bias towards
individual objective minima.
Thus, an important task for future research lies in trying to remedy this
behavior,
maybe by using some gradient scaling.

—

In this regard, it would be desirable to be able to use alternative descent
direction
that allow guidance of the iterates or use momentum to accelerate convergence.

—

Moreover, we believe it possible to transfer the model construction
optimizations from
[
51
,
4
]
into our algorithm.
This would allow for “model-improvement” steps with models that are not fully
linear and could potentially save expensive function evaluations.

—

Finally, our algorithm produces only one critical point.
After a run has finished, can we leverage the surrogates we already have
constructed to warm-start the optimization of a modified multi-objective optimization problem
to find additional critical
points and obtain a covering of (parts) of the critical set ?

A more theoretical question is whether or not our convergence results can be
strengthened. To this end, using a slanted filter like in
[
19
]
might prove
beneficial.
Furthermore, in
Section

4.1
we have already talked about why an approach similar to that
in
[
14
]
could proof beneficial.

Interpretable Deep Learning for Two-Prong Jet Classification with Jet Spectra Preprint: KEK-TH-2117 arXiv: 1904.02092
Amit Chakraborty, Sung Hak Lim, and Mihoko M. Nojiri

Abstract
Classification of jets with deep learning has gained significant attention in recent times. However, the performance of deep neural networks is often achieved at the cost of interpretability. Here we propose an interpretable network trained on the jet spectrum S 2 ​ ( R ) S_{2}(R) which is a two-point correlation function of the jet constituents. The spectrum can be derived from a functional Taylor series of an arbitrary jet classifier function of energy flows. An interpretable network can be obtained by truncating the series. The intermediate feature of the network is an infrared and collinear safe C-correlator which allows us to estimate the importance of an S 2 ​ ( R ) S_{2}(R) deposit at an angular scale R R in the classification. The performance of the architecture is comparable to that of a convolutional neural network (CNN) trained on jet images, although the number of inputs and complexity of the architecture is significantly simpler than the CNN classifier. We consider two examples: one is the classification of two-prong jets which differ in color charge of the mother particle, and the other is a comparison between Pythia 8 and Herwig 7 generated jets.

One of the primary motivations is to extract the maximum information from the complex collision events.
The deep learning in collider physics takes advantage of a large influx of data from experiments, more precise theoretical predictions, significant improvement in computing power, and ongoing progress in the field of machine learning itself.

Two-Point Correlation Spectrum and Two-Prong Jets

2.1
Jet Spectra

In
Lim:2018toa
, we introduced a two-point correlation spectral function
S_{2}(R)
which maps a jet to a function of angular scale
R
,

\displaystyle S_{2}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\vec{R}_{1}\,d\vec{R}_{2}\,P_{T}(\vec{R}_{1})\,P_{T}(\vec{R}_{2})\,\delta(R-R_{12}),

(1)

\displaystyle P_{T}(\vec{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbf{J}\end{subarray}}p_{T,i}\,\delta(\vec{R}-\vec{R}_{i}),

(2)

where
\mathbf{J}
is a set of jet constituents,

\vec{R}_{i}=(\eta_{i},\phi_{i})
is the position of the
i
-th jet
constituent in the pseudorapidity-azimuth plane,

R_{ij}=\sqrt{(\eta_{i}-\eta_{j})^{2}+(\phi_{i}-\phi_{j})^{2}}
is the
angular distance between the two jet constituents
i
and
j
,
and
P_{T}(\vec{R})
is an energy flow functional
Tkachov:1995kk
of
\mathbf{J}
.
For practical purpose,

is discretized as below,

\displaystyle S_{2}(R;\Delta R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta R}\int_{R}^{R+\Delta R}dR\,S_{2}(R)

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta R}\sum_{i,j\in\mathbf{J}}p_{T,i}\,p_{T,j}\,I_{[R,R+\Delta R)}(R_{ij}),

where
I_{A}(R_{ij})
is an indicator function of the angular distance
R_{ij}
of the domain
A
,

\displaystyle I_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1&\mathrm{if}\;x\in A,\\
0&\mathrm{if}\;x\notin A.\end{cases}

The spectral function

is, therefore, the sum of the product of
p_{T}
’s of the two jet constituents with an angular distance
R_{ij}
lying between
R
and
R+\Delta R
.

We obtain IRC safe quantities by multiplying smooth functions

To understand the IRC safety of

The inner product of

\displaystyle\int d\vec{R}\,\delta P_{T}(\vec{R})w(\vec{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{T,i_{1}}w(\vec{R}_{i_{1}})+p_{T,i_{2}}w(\vec{R}_{i_{2}})-p_{T,i_{0}}w(\vec{R}_{i_{0}})

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\delta p_{T,i_{0}}-p_{T,i_{1}}(\delta\vec{R}_{i_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{\vec{R}})-p_{T,i_{2}}(\delta\vec{R}_{i_{2}}\cdot\nabla_{\vec{R}})+\cdots\right]w(\vec{R}_{i_{0}}),

The spectrum

The inner product of the smooth function

\int dR\,\delta S_{2}(R)w(R)=2\sum_{j\in\mathbf{J}}\left[\delta p_{T,i_{0}}+p_{T,i_{1}}(\delta\vec{R}_{i_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{\vec{R}})+p_{T,i_{2}}(\delta\vec{R}_{i_{2}}\cdot\nabla_{\vec{R}})+\cdots\right]p_{T,j}\,w(R_{i_{0}j}).

(5)

Again, this integral vanishes in the IRC limits.

Nevertheless, when the domain is discretized into small sections

\int d\vec{R}_{1}\cdots d\vec{R}_{n}\,P_{T}(\vec{R}_{1})\cdots P_{T}(\vec{R}_{n})\,w(\vec{R}_{1},\cdots,\vec{R}_{n}),

The

We recluster the constituents of a jet of a radius parameter

to subjets with a smaller radius parameter
R_{\mathrm{trim}}
.
A subjet
\mathbf{J}_{a}

-th subjet respectively.
The trimmed jet

is defined as a union of the remaining subjets,

\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{trim}}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\\
\frac{p_{T,\mathbf{J}_{a}}}{p_{T,\mathbf{J}}}\geq f_{\mathrm{trim}}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{J}_{a}~.

(8)

The jet trimming is beneficial because it does not introduce additional angular scale parameters other than
R_{\mathrm{trim}}
.
The trimmed spectrum is then calculated using the constituents of the trimmed jet.
We denote it as
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)
and its binned version
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R;\Delta R)
, which are defined as follows:

\displaystyle S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\vec{R}_{1}\,d\vec{R}_{2}\,P_{T,\mathrm{trim}}(\vec{R}_{1})\,P_{T,\mathrm{trim}}(\vec{R}_{2})\cdot\delta(R-R_{12}),

(9)

\displaystyle P_{T,\mathrm{trim}}(\vec{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{trim}}\end{subarray}}p_{T,i}\,\delta(\vec{R}-\vec{R}_{i}),

(10)

\displaystyle S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R;\Delta R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta R}\sum_{i,j\in\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{trim}}}p_{T,i}~p_{T,j}\cdot I_{[R,R+\Delta R)}(R_{ij}),

(11)

where
P_{T,\mathrm{trim}}(\vec{R})
is the energy flow of
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{trim}}
.

In the limit of the constituents of each subjet
\mathbf{J}_{a}
are localized, the energy flow and the jet spectrum can be approximated in terms of the subjet momenta.
The energy flow of such a jet is decomposed into a sum of energy flows of all the subjets,

P_{T}(\vec{R})=\sum_{a}P_{T,a}(\vec{R}),\quad P_{T,a}(\vec{R})=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{J}_{a}}p_{T,i}\delta(\vec{R}-\vec{R}_{i}).

(12)

The energy flow of each subjet converges weakly to
p_{T,\mathbf{J}_{a}}\delta(\vec{R}-\vec{R}_{\mathbf{J}_{a}})
.
The
S_{2}(R)

\displaystyle S_{2}(R;\Delta R)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\\
\mathbf{J}_{a},\mathbf{J}_{b}\subset\mathbf{J}\end{subarray}}p_{T,\mathbf{J}_{a}}\,p_{T,\mathbf{J}_{b}}\cdot I_{[R,R+\Delta R)}(R_{ab}).

(13)

The jet trimming also introduces a
p_{T}
scale hierarchy among the subjets, and so their pairwise contributions to

can be classified by the scale.
We define a quantity
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R;\Delta R)
where

S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R;\Delta R)=S_{2}(R;\Delta R)-S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R;\Delta R).

(14)

In the r.h.s. of the above equation, the correlations among the constituents of the hard subjets are canceled, and we have

\displaystyle S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R;\Delta R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{T,\mathbf{J}}^{2}\cdot\mathcal{O}\left[1\right],

(15)

\displaystyle S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R;\Delta R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{T,\mathbf{J}}^{2}\cdot\left(\mathcal{O}\left[f_{\mathrm{trim}}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left[f_{\mathrm{trim}}^{2}\right]\right).

(16)

The dominant contributions to

(i.e., the

\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{trim}}
and a constituent in
\mathbf{J}-\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{trim}}

The subleading

h_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\int dR\,S_{2,A}(R)w_{i,A}^{(2)}(R;\vec{x}_{\mathrm{kin}}).

(28)

The above-mentioned linear setup has an advantage on the interpretability and visualization of the network predictions; we discuss more on this network in section
4
.

2.3
Relation between Two-Point Correlation Spectra and Energy Flow Polynomials

Both the two-point correlation spectra and the energy flow polynomials
Komiske:2017aww
with two vertices span the set of bilinear
C
-correlators; therefore, there is a transformation rule between them.
We first extend the definition of the energy flow polynomials to compare them to
S_{2,ab}
.
Since
S_{2,ab}
is a multivariate function of energy flows, we introduce a multivariate energy flow polynomial with two labeled vertices,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{EFP}}_{2,ab}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\vec{R}_{1}d\vec{R}_{2}\,P_{T,a}(\vec{R}_{1})P_{T,b}(\vec{R}_{1})\,R_{12}^{n}=\sum_{i\in{\mathbf{J}_{a}}}\sum_{j\in{\mathbf{J}_{b}}}p_{T,i}p_{T,j}R_{ij}^{n}.

(29)

This expression suggests that
R^{n}
can be considered as an angular weighting function
w_{i,ab}^{(2)}(R)
in eq. (
23
).

The resulting transformation from
S_{2,ab}(R)
to
{\mathrm{EFP}}_{2,ab}^{(n)}
is the Mellin transformation,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{EFP}}_{2,ab}^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dR\,R^{n-1}\cdot S_{2,ab}(R).

(30)

The integral on the right-hand side is finite because
S_{2,ab}(R)
vanishes on
R\gg 2R_{\mathbf{J}}
.
The inverse transform is also well-defined if we allow the exponent
n
in the angular weighting function of
{\mathrm{EFP}}_{2,ab}^{(n)}
to be a complex number.

2.4
Spectra of Two-Prong Jets

The
S_{2}(R;\Delta R)
spectrum is useful to identify the substructures of the jet and also to characterize the jet.
Typically,
S_{2}(R;\Delta R)
of QCD jet has a peak at
R=0
with a long tail towards large
R
.
The peak originates from the autocorrelation term
\sum_{i}p^{2}_{T,i}
in eq. (
3
).
On the other hand, if a jet originates from a Higgs boson decaying into
b\bar{b}
, the
b
-partons create two isolated cores inside the jet.
The spectrum of the Higgs jet has a peak at the angular scale equal to the angle between the two clusters.
In addition,
S_{2}(R;\Delta R)
encodes the fragmentation pattern of
b
-partons.

At the LHC, boosted heavy objects such as top quark, gauge bosons and Higgs boson decaying into quarks can be studied by identifying jet substructures.
Usually, these substructures are characterized by parameters such as
D_{2}
defined as,

\displaystyle D_{2}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{3}^{\beta}/(e^{\beta}_{2})^{3},

(31)

\displaystyle e_{2}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{2}_{T,\mathbf{J}}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathbf{J},i<j}p_{T,i}p_{T,j}R^{\beta}_{ij},

(32)

\displaystyle e_{3}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{3}_{T,\mathbf{J}}}\sum_{i,j,k\in\mathbf{J},i<j<k}p_{T,i}p_{T,j}p_{T,k}R^{\beta}_{ij}R^{\beta}_{jk}R^{\beta}_{ki},

(33)

where
\beta
is the angular exponent.
If a jet has a two-prong substructure,
D_{2}
is much less than one.
The jet spectrum
S_{2}(R;\Delta R)
contains more information than
D_{2}
, and therefore, the analysis with
S_{2}(R;\Delta R)
goes beyond the one using
D_{2}
.
It was shown that a neural network trained on
S_{2}(R;\Delta R)
distinguishes Higgs jet from QCD jet better than the one trained on
D_{2}

Lim:2018toa
.

To study the fragmentation pattern of the
b
-partons and their color connection to the mother particle, we introduce a color-octet scalar, sgluon (
\sigma
).
We assume that the Higgs boson (
h
) and
\sigma
decay into
b\bar{b}
through the interaction,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L_{\mathrm{SM}}}

\displaystyle\ni

\displaystyle y_{hb\bar{b}}\,h\,\bar{b}b+\mathrm{h.c.}

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L_{\mathrm{Sgluon}}}

\displaystyle\ni

\displaystyle y_{\sigma b\bar{b}}\,\sigma^{a}\,\bar{b}T^{a}b+\mathrm{h.c.}.

(35)

The Higgs boson is a color singlet particle, and the decay
h\rightarrow b\bar{b}
is isolated in color flows.
Therefore,
S_{2}(R;\Delta R)
beyond the angle between the b-partons, i.e.,
R_{b\bar{b}}
, is suppressed due to the color coherence.
No such constraint on the angular scale exists for sgluon and QCD jets.
Meanwhile, the Higgs jet and sgluon jet have the same two-prong substructure, unlike the QCD jet, as both are originating from a particle decaying into
b\bar{b}
final states.

To study the spectra of two-prong jets, we simulate events as follows.

3
Classifying Higgs jet, sgluon jet, and QCD jet

In this section, we introduce a neural network trained on the jet spectra for classifying Higgs jet, sgluon jet, and QCD jet.
We first discuss the basic kinematic features of these jets and then outline their dependence on the parton shower simulators.
Afterward, we show the details of the neural network and then present our results in terms of the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.

Interpretable Two-level Architecture

A quantitative understanding of a neural network is not straightforward because the parameters and intermediate outputs of the neural network are less readable.
In this section, we propose an architecture constructed from the truncated series in eq. (

In the case of binary classifications, the discretized architecture is defined as follows,

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}S^{k}_{2,\mathrm{trim}}\,w^{k}_{\mathrm{trim}}+\sum_{k}S^{k}_{2,\mathrm{soft}}\,w^{k}_{\mathrm{soft}},\quad w_{A}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{R_{k}}^{R_{k}+\Delta R_{k}}dR\,w_{A}^{(2)}(R)\;\;(A=\mathrm{trim},\,\mathrm{soft}),

The

\varphi_{\mathrm{ELU}}(x)=\begin{cases}x&x>0,\\

, 2

We do not use
\mathrm{ReLU}

\mathrm{ReLU}

\vec{x}_{\mathrm{kin}}

This is known as the dying
\mathrm{ReLU}

In this case, the architecture is reduced to the logistic classifier.

The vanishing gradient problem arises when the momentum range of the training sample is too small, which generates constant weights.
The characteristic scale of
\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}
is
[0.625,0.833]
for the
p_{T,\mathbf{J}}
range [300, 400] GeV.
The variation in
\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}
is about 0.2, which is not significantly large compared with the calorimeter resolution of 0.1.
Therefore, we extend the
p_{T,\mathbf{J}}
range of all the samples to
[300,\,600]
GeV.
In addition, we avoid vanishing gradient problem by using He uniform initializer
He_2015_ICCV
.
The weights and biases are initialized by uniform random numbers in
[-\sqrt{6/N_{\mathrm{in}}},\sqrt{6/N_{\mathrm{in}}}]
where
N_{\mathrm{in}}
is the number of inputs to the layer. The advantage of using He initializer over Glorot initializer is that it generates random numbers in a wider range so that the neural network can start up from wider initial weights and gradient.
The weight decay parameter
\lambda
of the

weight regularizer is set to 0.001 so that the weights do not vanish too early.

The
w_{\mathrm{trim}}
around
\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}
is positive because the correlation at the scale is a characteristic feature of Higgs jet.
If a Higgs boson is decaying to a pair of bottom quarks perpendicular to the boosted direction in its rest frame, the relative angular separation of the decay products is
\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}
in the lab frame.
Due to the phase space of the decay, most of the events are distributed near
\theta=\pi/2
, where
\theta
is Higgs decay angle relative to the boost direction.
As
p_{T,\mathbf{J}}
increases,
\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}
decreases, and the lower edge of
w_{\mathrm{trim}}>0
moves toward smaller values.
The region with
w_{\mathrm{trim}}>0
also shifts towards smaller values of
R
.
The weight
w_{\mathrm{trim}}
on
R>\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}
is positive for capturing a Higgs jet whose
\theta
is smaller than
\pi/2
.
These events have
p_{T}
asymmetric subjets, and the cross-correlation terms in
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)
are smaller than that of
p_{T}

symmetric case.
These correlations are still useful for the classification because
S_{2}
spectrum of QCD jet reduces much faster than that of Higgs jet.
As a result, the
w_{\mathrm{trim}}
is an increasing function in this region to compensate for the
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
reduction.

For
R\gtrsim R_{\mathbf{J}}
, weight
w_{\mathrm{trim}}
is negative.
The score
\hat{y}_{h}
decreases whenever there are any energy deposits at
R\gtrsim R_{\mathbf{J}}
.
The crossover point from
w_{\mathrm{trim}}>0
to
w_{\mathrm{trim}}<0
shifts towards smaller values of
R
with an increase of
p_{T,\mathbf{J}}
because the Higgs decay products become more asymmetric with respect to the boost direction.
In such a case, one of the subjets tends to be soft so that the two-point correlations are included in
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
, instead of
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
.
These contributions to
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
do not affect
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
, because
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
spectra of QCD jets are overwhelming at large
R
.

The
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
on
R>R_{\mathrm{trim}}
always reduces
\hat{y}_{h}
, and there is no prominent structure around
\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}
.
Moreover,
|w_{\mathrm{soft}}|
decreases as
p_{T,\mathbf{J}}
increases.
The reduction of
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
compensates the increase of
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
, and the prediction is more or less
p_{T,\mathbf{J}}
independent. On
R>R_{\mathbf{J}}
,
|w_{\mathrm{soft}}|
increases with
R
because activity in this region is a sign of QCD jet even though corresponding
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
decreases due to suppressed large angle radiations.

The
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
on
R\lesssim R_{\mathrm{trim}}
is positive and
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
has a break at
R\sim R_{\mathrm{trim}}
.
Correlations between the constituents in a soft subjet contributes to the
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
on
R<R_{\mathrm{trim}}
, i.e.,
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(0;R_{\mathrm{trim}})\propto f^{2}_{\mathrm{trim}}
.
Let us assume
\mathbf{J}_{a}
is a single soft subjet, then
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(0;R_{\mathrm{trim}})\sim p_{T,\mathbf{J}_{a}}^{2}\sim(p_{T,\mathbf{J}}-p_{T,\mathbf{J},\mathrm{trim}})^{2}
.
If there are multiple soft subjets, then
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(0;R_{\mathrm{trim}})\sim\sum_{a}p_{T,\mathbf{J}_{a}}^{2}<(\sum_{a}p_{T,\mathbf{J}_{a}})^{2}\sim(p_{T,\mathbf{J}}-p_{T,\mathbf{J},\mathrm{trim}})^{2}
.
This triangular inequality suggests that the magnitude of
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(0;R_{\mathrm{trim}})
is small for a jet with a given
p_{T,\mathbf{J}}-p_{T,\mathbf{J},\mathrm{trim}}
when there are multiple soft jets.
The positive
w_{2}
on
R<R_{\mathrm{trim}}
means that Higgs jet has less soft subjets than QCD jet.
The
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}^{0}
consists of the autocorrelation of soft subjets, which has different energy scaling behavior compared with the other
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}^{k}
.
The
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
on
R<R_{\mathrm{trim}}
is
S_{2,22}\sim\mathcal{O}[f_{\mathrm{trim}}^{2}]
in eq. (
24
).
On the other hand,
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}^{k}

(k\geq 1)
is dominated by
S_{2,12}
.
The
S_{2,12}
on
R<R_{\mathrm{trim}}
does not contribute to
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}^{0}
because it vanishes.
Therefore, we may rewrite
h
as follows,

\displaystyle h=\int dR\,S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)w_{\mathrm{trim}}(R)+\int_{0}^{R_{\mathrm{trim}}}dR\,S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R)w_{\mathrm{soft}}^{\prime}(R)+\int dR\,S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R)w_{\mathrm{soft}}^{\prime\prime}(R)

(49)

where
w_{\mathrm{soft}}^{\prime}
and
w_{\mathrm{soft}}^{\prime\prime}
are continuous functions with

and

The second term is essentially the same as
\int dR\,S_{2,22}(R)w_{2,22}^{(2)}(R)

\int dR\,S_{2,12}(R)w_{2,12}^{(2)}(R)+\int dR\,S_{2,21}(R)w_{2,21}^{(2)}(R)

The sudden changes of
w_{\mathrm{trim}}
and
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
at

The

and

however, the probability of such a radiation pattern is small.
As a result, the number of events used for training the weights at large

The large weights also do not contribute much to the classification (see figure

The merged dataset is divided into ten subsets and we train a network for each of them.

and

and

For Higgs jet and sgluon jet, the
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
distribution of
PY8
events is significantly large (and positive) for
R\sim R_{\mathrm{trim}}
and it decreases as
R
increases.
The weight
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
is negative for
R>R_{\mathbf{J}}
, which means that
HW7
events have more soft activity in the region
R\gg\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}
.

For the case of QCD jet, the distribution of
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
is always positive and flat for
R<R_{\mathbf{J}}
and negative for
R>1.5
.
It would be interesting to evaluate the weights for the classifiers trained with the experimental data and compare with the simulated results to tune the parameters of the event generators further.

5
Summary and Outlook

The classification of jets with deep learning has gained significant attention in recent times.
Majority of these analyses take advantage of the significant development in computing power.
These deep learning architectures utilize the complete event information in terms of low-level observables.
These deep learning based strategies can be compared with the previous approaches for tagging jets, for example, mass drop tagger,
n
-subjettiness, energy correlation function where each of them has solid physics motivation.

In this paper, we introduce neural networks trained on “jet spectrum”
S_{2}(R)
, which are essentially two-point correlation functions of jet constituents.
We also introduce
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)
, which is
S_{2}(R)
calculated from the trimmed jet, to encode hard substructure of the jet.
The difference
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R)=S_{2}(R)-S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)
encodes the remaining correlations with soft radiations and is less affected by the correlations among the hard constituents.
Our neural networks are trained on
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
integrated over certain bins.
If the
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
spectra are multiplied by smooth functions and integrated over
R
, it forms an IRC safe C-correlator.
This feature assures that the classifiers trained on binned
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
are approximately IRC safe.

The performance of MLP trained on
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
is compared to that of CNN trained on jet images.
The CNN shows better performance than the MLP, but the difference is small.
The key reason is efficient preprocessing of parton shower effects with less number of free parameters.
Parton shower is the multiple splittings of the partons where each splitting is parametrized by the angular scale and momentum fraction of the partons.
The binned
S_{2}(R)
spectra collect the information of the parton splitting successfully.
The spectra provide comparable jet classification performance with a fewer number of inputs.
Furthermore, the MLP is computationally economical than the CNN because the MLP has smaller complexity than the CNN and takes only
\mathcal{O}(40)
inputs.

The
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R)
spectra can be obtained from a functional Taylor series of an arbitrary classifier in energy flows.
The spectra are basis vectors of the second order term in the expansion.
In this context, the MLP trained on
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R)
can be considered as a sum of
2n
-linear
C
-correlators which can be reduced to products of the bilinear
C
-correlators in
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
.
The (mild) difference in the performance of the CNN and MLP comes from the remaining irreducible
n
-linear
C
-correlators.

The terms linear in
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}(R)
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}(R)
provide an opportunity to visualize and interpret the network predictions; therefore, we study a novel two-level architecture that involves an interpretable layer of a single node in the form of a
C
-correlator.
The output is the sum of the product of the trained weights (
w_{\mathrm{trim}}
and
w_{\mathrm{soft}}
) and jet spectra (
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
).
The absolute values of the weights signify the impact of the corresponding
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
and
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
bin values on the jet classification.
In the context of classification between Higgs jet and QCD jet, the distribution of
w_{\mathrm{trim}}
shows that
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
spectrum around
\hat{R}_{b\bar{b}}=2m_{h}/p_{T,\mathbf{J}}
increases the output, and the classifier regards the jet as a Higgs jet.
We have also shown that the dependence of
w_{\mathrm{trim}}
on jet
p_{T}
can be qualitatively understood (at the parton level) from the decay of a boosted Higgs boson.
In short, the network is using
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
inputs to obtain the core substructure information inside the jet.

The soft activity is also useful for Higgs jet vs. QCD jet classification.
The probability for assigning the given jet as a QCD jet increases with increase in
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
on
R>R_{\mathrm{trim}}
.
To study the impact of soft physics in jet classification, we also introduce sgluon, a hypothetical color octet scalar, and compare the classifier performance among Higgs jet, sgluon jet, and QCD jet.
The network predictions for sgluon jet vs. QCD jet classification are primarily determined by the core substructure information as expected.
However, the network uses the difference in the
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
spectra arising from the different color structure of the decaying particle for Higgs jet vs. sgluon jet classification.

The non-trivial role of soft radiations in the predictions of the classifiers implies the results are highly sensitive to the choice of event generators.
The weights associated with the
S_{2,\mathrm{trim}}
are almost insensitive to the choice; however, the weights of the
S_{2,\mathrm{soft}}
are strongly affected.
This behavior is expected as modeling of soft physics is quite different in
Pythia 8
and
Herwig 7
.

The two-point correlation spectra and the architectures introduced in this paper can be applied for solving other interesting problems, thanks to flexibility on designing neural network.
For jets with more complex substructures, e.g., top jet, the higher order terms in the energy flow series expansion may be included.
It would be worthwhile to study the classifier performances when the network is trained with the experimental data and compare with the predictions of event generators to tune their parameters to reduce the uncertainty in modeling the soft physics.
It is also interesting to use this interpretable architecture as a model-agnostic interpreter for black box architectures
2016arXiv160605386T
.
We leave these possibilities for future works.

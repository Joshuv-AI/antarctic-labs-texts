Daoji Huang, Pavlo Pylyavskyy

Abstract
. In our previous work we have introduced an analogue of Robinson-Schensted-Knuth correspondence for Schubert calculus of the complete flag varieties. The objects inserted are certain biwords, the outcomes of insertion are bumpless pipe dreams, and the recording objects are decorated chains in Bruhat order. In this paper we study a class of biwords that have a certain associativity property; we call them plactic biwords. We introduce analogues of Knuth moves on plactic biwords, and prove that any two plactic biwords with the same insertion bumpless pipe dream are connected by those moves.



1.
Introduction

The classical Robinson-Schensted-Knuth (RSK) correspondence
[
Rob38
,
Sch61
,
Knu70
]
, in its most general form, gives a bijection between matrices
A
with nonnegative integers and paris of semistandard Young tableaux
(P,Q)
of the same shape
\lambda
, where the size of
\lambda
is equal to the sum of entries in
A
. This correspondence has numerous consequences and applications; for example it gives a bijective proof of the celebrated Cauchy’s identity of symmetric functions. There are also numerous specializations and variants of the RSK correspondence that are useful in different contexts. In particular, one version gives a bijection between the set of finite-length words on the alphabet
\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
and pairs of Young tableaux
(P,Q)
where
P
is semistandard and
Q
is standard with the same shape. Here
P
is known as the
insertion tableaux
and
Q
as the
recording tableaux
. This gives a combinatorial interpretation of the identity

(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots)^{m}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash m}|\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)|s_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots).

A central property of this RSK correspondence is the following: two words
\mathbf{a}_{1}
and
\mathbf{a}_{2}
have the same insertion tableaux if and only if they are related by the
elementary Knuth relations
:

bac\sim bca\text{ if }a<b\leq c

acb\sim cab\text{ if }a\leq b<c.

This led to the definition of
plactic monoid
by Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
LS81
]
, see also
[
Lot02
, Chapter 5]
, which is a monoid whose generators are elements in a totally ordered alphabet and relations are the elementary Knuth relations.

In our previous work
[
HP22
]
, we generalized Schensted’s left and right insertion algorithms on semistandard Young tableaux, which give a combinatorial model of Schur polynomials (or functions), to bumpless pipe dreams of Lam, Lee, and Shimozono
[
LLS21
]
, which give a combinatorial model for Schubert polynomials
[
LS82
]
. Based on these algorithms we introduced two versions of the RSK correspondence, where we generalize the input from words to certain biwords, the insertion objects from SSYTs to BPDs, and recording objects from SYTs to certain decorated chains in Bruhat order. This generalized RSK correspondence interprets the polynomial identity

\mathfrak{S}_{k_{1}}\mathfrak{S}_{k_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{S}_{k_{m}}=\sum_{\pi\in S_{\infty}:\ell(\pi)=m}|chs_{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})}(\pi)|\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}.

where
k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}
are positive integers,
\mathfrak{S}_{k}=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}
for any
k
,

\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
is the Schubert polynomial for
\pi
, and

chs_{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})}(\pi)=\{(\operatorname{id}\lessdot_{k_{1}}\pi_{1}\lessdot_{k_{2}}\cdots\lessdot_{k_{\ell(\pi)}}\pi_{\ell(\pi)}=\pi)\},

namely the set of complete chains in mixed
k
-Bruhat order where the decoration is
(k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}).
Notice that it’s possible that
chs(\pi)
is empty.

In classical RSK, Schensted’s row (right) and column (left) insertions always commute. Therefore, given a word, we may read it from left to right and iteratively perform right insertion, read it from right to left and iteratively perform left insertion, or more generally start with a letter in the middle and iteratively construct a consecutive subword by adding one more letter, and whenever we read a letter to the left we perform left insertion and whenever we read a letter to the right we perform right insertion. The insertion object (SSYT) is always well-defined regardless of these various choices of insertion order. However, this is not true in general for our generalized RSK insertion. This poses an obstacle
to generalize the classical theory of plactic monoid to the Schubert setting. In this paper, we identify a simple condition on biwords (Definition
1.1
) under which the insertion BPD is well-defined regardless of order of insertion (Proposition
1.2
) and generalize classical Knuth relations to this restricted set of biwords, which we will call
plactic biwords
(Definition
1.3
). We furthermore study the basic properties of these generalized Knuth relations and plactic biwords.

Definition 1.1
.

A biletter is a pair of positive integers
\binom{a}{k}
where
a\leq k
.
A
plactic biword
is a word of biletters
\binom{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{k}}=\binom{a_{1},\cdots,a_{\ell}}{k_{1},\cdots,k_{\ell}}
, where
k_{i}\geq k_{i+1}
for each
i
. If furthermore
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{\ell}=k
for a fixed
k
, we call the plactic biword a
k
-biword
. We may write a
k
-biword
\binom{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{k}}
as
\binom{\mathbf{a}}{k}
.

The following statement shows that the insertion object of a plactic biword is well-defined regardless of choices of order of insertion. Recall that a given biword
Q=\binom{a_{1},\cdots,a_{\ell}}{k_{1},\cdots,k_{\ell}}
,
[
HP22
]
defines a map
\mathcal{L}(Q)=(\varphi_{L}(Q),ch_{L}(Q))
where
\varphi_{L}(Q)
is the BPD obtained by reading
Q
from right to left and successively performing left insertion, and
ch_{L}(Q)
is the recording chain in mixed
k
-Bruhat order with edge labels
k_{\ell},\cdots,k_{1}
,
as well as a map
\mathcal{R}(Q)=(\varphi_{R}(Q),ch_{R}(Q))
where
\varphi_{R}(Q)
is the BPD obtained by reading
Q
from left to right and successively performing right insertion, and
ch_{R}(Q)
is the recording chain in mixed
k
-Bruhat order with edge labels
k_{1},\cdots,k_{\ell}
.
For details of these insertion algorithms see
[
HP22
, Section 3]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose
Q=\binom{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{k}}
is a plactic biword of length
\ell
.
Let
Q_{1},Q_{2},\cdots,Q_{\ell}
be a sequence of biwords such that
\operatorname{length}(Q_{i})=i
,
Q_{i}
is a consecutive subword of
Q
, and for each
1\leq i<\ell

Q_{i+1}=\binom{a}{k}Q_{i}
, or
Q_{i+1}=Q_{i}\binom{a}{k}
for some
a
and
k
. Let
D_{1}:=\varphi_{L}(Q_{1})=\varphi_{R}(Q_{1})
, and for each
1\leq i<\ell
, define

D_{i+1}=\begin{cases}\binom{a}{k}\rightarrow D_{i}&\text{ if }Q_{i+1}=\binom{a}{k}Q_{i}\\
D_{i}\leftarrow\binom{a}{k}&\text{ if }Q_{i+1}=Q_{i}\binom{a}{k}.\end{cases}

Then
D_{\ell}
is independent of the choice of the sequence of biwords. We write
\varphi(Q)
for
D_{\ell}
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
HP22
, Theorem 3.3]
by induction.
∎

Definition 1.3
.

We define the
generalized Knuth relations
on plactic biwords as follows:

(1)

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cdots&b&a&c&\cdots\\
\cdots&k&k&k&\cdots\end{smallmatrix}\right)\sim\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cdots&b&c&a&\cdots\\
\cdots&k&k&k&\cdots\end{smallmatrix}\right)
if
a<b\leq c

(2)

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cdots&a&c&b&\cdots\\
\cdots&k&k&k&\cdots\end{smallmatrix}\right)\sim\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cdots&c&a&b&\cdots\\
\cdots&k&k&k&\cdots\end{smallmatrix}\right)
if
a\leq b<c

(3)

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cdots&a&b&\cdots\\
\cdots&k&k&\cdots\end{smallmatrix}\right)\sim\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cdots&a&b&\cdots\\
\cdots&k+1&k&\cdots\end{smallmatrix}\right)
if
a\leq b

(4)

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cdots&b&a&\cdots\\
\cdots&k+1&k+1&\cdots\end{smallmatrix}\right)\sim\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cdots&b&a&\cdots\\
\cdots&k+1&k&\cdots\end{smallmatrix}\right)
if
a<b
.

Note that these relations are defined on the set of plactic biwords only; namely we do not apply the relation (3) or (4) if the resulting word is no longer plactic. Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
D\in\operatorname{BPD}(\pi)
, the set of plactic biwords

\operatorname{words}(D):=\{Q:\varphi(Q)=D\}

is connected by the generalized Knuth relations.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
1.4
is that the set of plactic biwords that insert into
D
for any
D\in\operatorname{BPD}(\pi)
using
\mathcal{L}
(resp.,
\mathcal{R}
) is in bijection with the set of mixed
k
-chains from
\operatorname{id}
to
\pi
with weakly increasing (resp., weakly decreasing) labels.

Equipped with the formulation of plactic biwords, we may restate our structure constant rule
[
HP22
]
for the separated descent case (see
[
KZJ
,
Hua22
]
, and also
[
Kog00
,
Len10
]
) as counting certain pairs of plactic biwords instead of growth diagrams. Following
[
HP22
]
, given a permutation
\pi
, we let
d_{1}(\pi)
denote the first descent of
\pi
and
d_{2}(\pi)
the last descent of
\pi
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\pi,\rho
,
\sigma
be permutations such that
d_{1}(\pi)\geq d_{2}(\rho)
. Then for any
D\in\operatorname{BPD}(\sigma)
, as well as

ch_{\pi}=(\operatorname{id}\lessdot_{k_{1}}\pi_{1}\lessdot_{k_{2}}\cdots\lessdot_{k_{\ell(\pi)}})

where
d_{1}(\pi)\leq k_{1}\leq\cdots\leq k_{\ell(\pi)}
, and

ch_{\rho}=(\operatorname{id}\lessdot_{m_{1}}\rho_{1}\lessdot_{m_{2}}\cdots\lessdot_{m_{\ell(\rho)}}\rho)

where
d_{2}(\rho)\geq m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{\ell(\rho)}
,

c_{\pi,\rho}^{\sigma}=\#\{(Q_{\pi},Q_{\rho}):ch_{L}(Q_{\pi})=ch_{\pi},ch_{R}(Q_{\rho})=ch_{\rho},\varphi(Q_{\pi}Q_{\rho})=D\}.

Notice that by assumptions on descents,
Q_{\pi}Q_{\rho}
is a plactic biword whose second row is
k_{\ell(\pi)},\cdots,k_{1},m_{1},\cdots,m_{\ell(\rho)}
.

In Section
2
we establish basic properties of plactic biwords and in Section
3
we prove our main theorem. In particular, we assume the knowledge of
[
HP22
, Section 3]
for these developments. We remark that in the case of plactic biwords, the left and right insertion algorithms simplify, and the details are stated precisely in
[
HP22
, Lemma 5.1 and Lemma 5.2]
.

2.
Properties of plactic biwords

Proposition 2.1
.

If
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are plactic biwords such that
Q_{1}\sim Q_{2}
, then
\varphi(Q_{1})=\varphi(Q_{2})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show when
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
differ by a single generalized Knuth move, and by Proposition
1.2
it suffices to consider the case when they are length 3 or 2 and all biletters participate in the application of the relation. If
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
differ by relations (1) and (2) in Definition
1.3
, which corresponds to the ordinary Knuth moves, the claim follows from the observation that our insertion algorithms on
k
-biwords correspond to classical Schensted’s insertion algorithms. For relation (3), we consider first the insertion of a single biletter
\binom{b}{k}
. The resulting BPD contains a single blank on the diagonal at
(b,b)
, a single
at
(k+1,k+1)
, and a
at each
(i,i)
for
b<i\leq k
. If we then left insert
\binom{a}{k}
for
a\leq b
, the final
min-droop
is always at
(k,k+1)
which causes the pipes that exit from row
k
and row
k+2
to cross. Therefore we see that left-inserting
\binom{a}{k+1}
would have the same effect. For relation (4), we consider first the insertion of a single biletter
\binom{b}{k+1}
. The resulting BPD contains a single blank on the diagonal at
(b,b)
, a single
at
(k+2,k+2)
, and a
at each
(i,i)
for
b<i\leq k+1
. If we then right insert
\binom{a}{k+1}
for
a<b
, during the algorithm there must be a
min-droop
at
(b-1,b-1)
followed by a
min-droop
at
(b,b-1)
, by definition of right insertion. The final
min-droop
must be at
(k+1,k)
, causing the pipes that exit from row
k
and
k+2
to swap. We can then see that right inserting
\binom{a}{k}
instead of
\binom{a}{k+1}
has the same effect.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation. Define
\operatorname{code}(\pi):\mathbb{Z}^{>0}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
such that

\operatorname{code}(\pi)(i)=\#\{j>i:\pi(j)<\pi(i)\}.

In other words,
\operatorname{code}(\pi)(i)
counts the number of blank boxes in the
i
th row of the Rothe diagram of
w
. This permutation statistics is known as the
inversion code
or
Lehmer code
of
\pi
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation. Define
\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi):\mathbb{Z}^{>0}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
such that

\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi)(i)=\#\{j<i:\pi(j)>\pi(i)\}.

In other words,
\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi)(i)
counts the number of crosses in the
i
th row of the Rothe diagram of
\pi
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\pi=13574862
. Then
\operatorname{code}(\pi)=(0,1,2,3,1,2,1,0,0,\cdots)
and

\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi)=(0,0,0,0,2,0,2,6,0,0,\cdots)
.

The following lemma is straightforward from the definition of
\operatorname{code}
and
\operatorname{maxcode}
. We omit the proof.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation and suppose
\pi t_{\alpha\beta}\gtrdot\pi
. Let

m_{\alpha}:=\#\{i>\beta:\pi(\alpha)<\pi(i)<\pi(\beta)\}

and

m_{\beta}:=\#\{i<\alpha:\pi(\alpha)<\pi(i)<\pi(\beta)\}.

Then

(a)

For
i\neq\alpha
and
i\neq\beta
,

\operatorname{code}(\pi)(i)=\operatorname{code}(\pi t_{\alpha\beta})(i)

and

\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi)(i)=\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi t_{\alpha\beta})(i).

(b)

\operatorname{code}(\pi t_{\alpha\beta})(\alpha)=\operatorname{code}(\pi)(\alpha)+m_{\alpha}+1

and

\operatorname{code}(\pi t_{\alpha\beta})(\beta)=\operatorname{code}(\pi)(\beta)-m_{\alpha}.

(c)

\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi t_{\alpha\beta})(\alpha)=\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi)(\alpha)-m_{\beta}

and

\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi t_{\alpha\beta})(\beta)=\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi)(\beta)+m_{\beta}+1.

From now on, for a word
\mathbf{a}
, we let
S(\mathbf{a})
denote the insertion semistandard tableau obtained from performing Schensted’s insertion. Given a semistandard tableau
S
, we let
\operatorname{sh}(S)
denote its shape and
\operatorname{colread}(S)
denote its column reading word; that is, the word obtained by reading
S
by columns from left to right, and within each column from bottom to top.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
Q=\binom{\mathbf{a}}{k}
be a
k
-biword, then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\binom{a}{k+1}Q\sim Q^{\prime}
where
Q^{\prime}
is a
k
-biword,

(b)

left inserting
a
into
S(\mathbf{a})
does not add a new row,

(c)

\binom{a}{k+1}\rightarrow\varphi(Q)
does not create a descent at
k+1
.

Proof.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a): Since
\mathbf{a}\sim\operatorname{colread}(S(\mathbf{a}))
we can without loss of generality assume
\mathbf{a}=\operatorname{colread}(S(\mathbf{a}))
. Let
c
denote the bottom-most entry in the first column of
S(\mathbf{a})
. Suppose left inserting
a
into
S(\mathbf{a})
does not add a new row, then it must be the case that

. Since
\binom{c}{k}
is the first biletter of

, we can apply (3) of the generalized Knuth relations and the result follows.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(c): This is straightforward since
\sim
preserves the insertion BPD.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(b):
Suppose left inserting
a
into
S(\mathbf{a})
does add a new row. Since a bumping step of Schensted left insertion on SSYTs corresponds to drooping into a blank tile of our left insertion on Grassmannian BPDs, the left insertion of
\binom{a}{k}
into
D:=\varphi(Q)
starts from the leftmost
in row
a>c
, never droops into a blank tile, and the last
min-droop

must create a
with the only
in row
k+1
, so inserting
\binom{a}{k+1}
must create a descent at
k+1
. See the illustration below.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
Q=\binom{\mathbf{a}}{k}
be a
k
-biword. Suppose
\lambda:=\operatorname{sh}(S(\mathbf{a}))
where
\lambda^{t}=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m})
with

. Namely, the column heights of

is a

-biword with

Let

Q_{D}^{\max}\binom{a}{k}\sim Q_{D^{\prime}}^{\max}

Case 2 (

).

Since
\beta<k_{1}+1
,
m:=\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi t_{\alpha\beta})(k_{1}+1)=\operatorname{maxcode}(\pi)(k_{1}+1)
. Therefore by Lemma
2.13
,
Q_{D^{\prime}}^{\max}=\binom{\mathbf{a}}{k_{1}}Q_{1}
and
Q_{D}^{\max}\binom{a}{k}=\binom{\mathbf{a}}{k_{1}}Q_{2}

\ell(\mathbf{a})=m
, each biletter

Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}

k_{j}<k_{1}
, and
\varphi(Q_{1})=\varphi(Q_{2})

Q_{1}\sim Q_{2}
by induction.

Case 3 (

).
In this case

\pi
.

Case 4 (

In this case

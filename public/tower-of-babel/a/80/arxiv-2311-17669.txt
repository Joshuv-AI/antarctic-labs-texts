Static and LRS spacetimes of type II in f ⁡ ( 𝒬 ) f(\mathcal{Q}) gravity
Fabrizio Esposito, Sante Carloni, Stefano Vignolo

Abstract
We investigate the 1 + 1 + 2 1+1+2 covariant formalism in the presence of nonmetricity. Focusing on static and Locally Rotationally Symmetric spacetimes, we show how nonmetricity affects all the kinematic quantities involved in the covariant 1 + 1 + 2 1+1+2 decomposition. We apply the resulting geometrical framework to study spherically symmetric solutions in the context of f ⁡ ( 𝒬 ) f(\mathcal{Q}) gravity in vacuum. We obtain explicit solutions and sufficient conditions for the existence of Schwarzschild-de Sitter type solutions.



I
introduction

General Relativity (GR) is a geometric theory of gravity that describes the gravitational field in terms of the metric tensor
g_{ab}
and relates the matter distribution to the curvature of the spacetime,
R^{a}{}_{bcd}
. In addition to GR, and since its very first formulation, other metric theories of gravity were developed to extend the geometrization of fundamental forces and, later, to address the limitations of GR on quantum, astrophysical, and cosmological scales. These theories can contain additional fields linked to the gravitational interaction or even have field equations containing higher-than-second derivatives (of the metric tensor). Well-known examples are
f(R)
gravity, a fourth-order theory
Clifton et al. 2012
;
Capozziello and De Laurentis 2011
;
Sotiriou and Faraoni 2010
;
Nojiri and Odintsov 2011
;
Nojiri et al. 2017
, or Hordenski theories (see e.g.
Kobayashi 2019
).

Further alternative theories are the ones based on torsion
T_{ab}{}^{c}
and nonmetricity
Q_{abc}
tensors, which, together with the curvature, express the properties of the affine connection defined on the spacetime. Among them, undoubtedly, the most studied are the so-called teleparallel theories of gravity, a.k.a Teleparallel Gravity (TG). TG represents a class of gravitational gauge theory that assumes spacetime is flat and describes gravitation through the torsion
Krššák et al. 2019
. Recently, much attention has been devoted to another class of teleparallel theories of gravity: Symmetric Teleparallel Gravity (STG). STG is a class of theories in which one assumes a torsion-free and curvature-free connection, but with nonmetricity that differs from zero
Nester and Yo 1999
;
Adak et al. 2006
;
Adak et al. 2013
;
Conroy and Koivisto 2018
. Although teleparallel theories are geometric descriptions of gravity different from GR, in some remarkable cases, they have field equations equivalent to those of GR. These are called Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR) and Symmetric Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (STEGR). TG and STG have also been modified generating what are now commonly known as
f(T)
-gravity and
f(\mathcal{Q})
-gravity
Jiménez et al. 2018
;
Bahamonde et al. 2023a
;
Cai et al. 2016
;
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2020
;
Vignolo et al. 2022
;
Esposito et al. 2022a
;
Esposito et al. 2022b
;
D’Ambrosio et al. 2022a
;
Gadbail et al. 2022
;
Albuquerque and Frusciante 2022
;
Capozziello and D’Agostino 2022
;
Khyllep et al. 2023
;
Agrawal et al. 2023
;
Narawade et al. 2022
;
Anagnostopoulos et al. 2023
;
Mustafa et al. 2022
;
Xu et al. 2019
;
Yang et al. 2021
;
Iosifidis et al. 2018
;
Dimakis et al. 2022
;
Capozziello et al. 2022
;
Dimakis et al. 2023
;
Paliathanasis 2023a
;
Heisenberg 2023
.

This last class of theories, in particular, has been recently studied in great detail in cosmological settings. The calculations in this context are simplified by the choice of the so-called “coincident gauge”. In this specific gauge, the full connection is assumed to be identically zero so that the nonmetricity tensor can be thought to be expressed by a partial derivative of the metric tensor
Jiménez et al. 2018
. Unfortunately, in the context of astrophysically relevant spacetimes, like the spherically symmetric ones, the coincident gauge has been shown to be incompatible with any non-linear form of the function
f(\mathcal{Q})
. Because of this limitation, various other gauges have been proposed. For example, the total connection is made to coincide with the Levi-Civita one in the gravity-free case
Zhao 2022
;
Lin and Zhai 2021
;
Calzá and Sebastiani 2023
, or, alternatively, via the imposition of connection invariance under
SO(3)
group transformations, restrictions are found on the connection itself
Hohmann 2021
;
D’Ambrosio et al. 2022b
;
Paliathanasis 2023b
;
Bahamonde et al. 2022
;
Bahamonde et al. 2023b
.

In this paper, we aim to provide a new framework to study astrophysically relevant metrics, including the static and spherically symmetric ones, in
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity, using a formalism that precludes the assumptions on the gauge and, therefore, treats the nonmetricity as a variable. More specifically, our analysis will be performed on static and Locally Rotationally Symmetric (LRS) spacetimes
Stewart and Ellis 1968
using the
1+1+2
covariant formalism, a natural extension of
1+3
formalism
van Elst and Ellis 1996
;
Clarkson and Barrett 2003
;
Clarkson 2007
;
Luz and Carloni 2019
;
Naidu et al. 2022
that we have used in
Esposito et al. 2022b
. In this approach, in addition to splitting spacetime in a preferred time direction and spatial hypersurfaces, the space itself is split into a preferred spatial direction and surfaces orthogonal to it. Therefore, it is well adapted to describe LRS spacetimes, which have a local preferred spatial direction corresponding to the local axis of symmetry.
We will see, in particular, that the combination of the formalism and the symmetries of LRS spacetimes will allow us to build a complete set of scalar quantities able to describe the structure of static and spherically symmetric spacetimes and to deduce the physical consequences of nonmetricity in this framework.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the geometric framework performing a detailed analysis of the
1+1+2
formalism when both curvature and nonmetricity tensors are present. Sec.
III
is dedicated to the description of constraints induced by the assumptions of local rotational symmetry and staticity. We also show how these constraints allow the decomposition of the nonmetricity tensor in a set of scalars. Using these results, in Sec.
IV
, we derive the 1+1+2 equations for
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity in LRS spacetimes. In Sec.
V
, we restrict to the static and spherical case and consider some solutions. Finally, we discuss the results in Sec.
VI
.

Throughout the paper natural units (
c=8\pi G=1
) and metric signature
(-,+,+,+)

II
Geometric framework

We consider a
4
-dimensional manifold endowed with a metric tensor
g_{ab}
and an affine connection
\Gamma_{ab}{}^{c}
, which is assumed torsionless but non-metric compatible. We denote by
\nabla
the covariant derivative induced by
\Gamma_{ab}{}^{c}
,
and by

Q_{cab}=\nabla_{c}g_{ab}.

(1)

the corresponding nonmetricity tensor.

According to the above assumptions, the connection
\Gamma_{ab}{}^{c}
can be decomposed as

\Gamma_{ab}{}^{c}=\tilde{\Gamma}_{ab}{}^{c}+L_{ab}{}^{c},

(2)

where

\tilde{\Gamma}_{ab}{}^{c}=\frac{1}{2}g^{cd}\left(\partial_{a}g_{bd}+\partial_{b}g_{ad}-\partial_{d}g_{ab}\right),

(3)

is the Levi-Civita connection induced by the metric tensor
g_{ab}
, and

L_{ab}{}^{c}=\frac{1}{2}\left(Q^{c}{}_{ab}-Q_{a}{}^{c}{}_{b}-Q_{b}{}^{c}{}_{a}\right)

(4)

is the disformation tensor. Throughout the paper, we will denote by a tilde all quantities related to the Levi-Civita connection (
3
).

The Riemann tensor
R^{a}{}_{bcd}
induced by the connection
\Gamma_{ab}{}^{c}
can be expressed as

\begin{split}R^{a}{}_{bcd}=&\partial_{c}\Gamma_{db}{}^{a}-\partial_{d}\Gamma_{cb}{}^{a}+\Gamma_{cp}{}^{a}\Gamma_{db}{}^{p}-\Gamma_{dp}{}^{a}\Gamma_{cb}{}^{p}=\\
=&\tilde{R}^{a}{}_{bcd}+\tilde{\nabla}_{c}L_{db}{}^{a}-\tilde{\nabla}_{d}L_{cb}{}^{a}+L_{cp}{}^{a}L_{db}{}^{p}+\\
&-L_{dp}{}^{a}L_{cb}{}^{p},\end{split}

(5)

and it satisfies the following identities:

•

Antisymmetry in the last two indices,

R^{a}{}_{bcd}=-R^{a}{}_{bdc};

(6)

•

First Bianchi identity,

R^{a}{}_{[bcd]}=0;

(7)

•

Second Bianchi identity,

\nabla_{[e|}R^{a}{}_{b|cd]}=0;

(8)

Moreover, the Riemann tensor (
5
) has three independent traces:

\begin{gathered}R_{bd}=R^{a}{}_{bad},\qquad\bar{R}_{ad}=R_{a}{}^{c}{}_{cd},\\
\check{R}_{cd}=R^{a}{}_{acd}=-\partial_{[c}Q_{d]a}{}^{a}.\end{gathered}

(9)

The contraction of Eq. (
9
) with the metric gives the Ricci scalar,

R=g^{ab}R_{ab}=-g^{ab}\bar{R}_{ab}.

(10)

From Eq. (
5
), it is easily seen that the Ricci scalar can be expressed as

R=\tilde{R}+\tilde{\nabla}_{a}L_{b}{}^{ba}-\tilde{\nabla}_{b}L_{a}{}^{ba}+\mathcal{Q},

(11)

where the quantity

\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}&=L_{ap}{}^{a}L_{b}{}^{bp}-L_{bp}{}^{a}L_{a}{}^{bp}=\\
=&\frac{1}{4}Q_{cab}Q^{cab}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{cab}Q^{abc}-\frac{1}{4}Q_{ab}{}^{b}Q^{a}{}_{b}{}^{b}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{ab}{}^{b}Q_{ba}{}^{b},\end{split}

(12)

is the so-called nonmetricity scalar. The Riemann tensor can also be written as

R_{abcd}=C_{abcd}+\sum_{n=2}^{6}W^{(n)}_{abcd}+\sum_{n=2}^{5}Z^{(n)}_{abcd},

(13)

where

C_{abcd}=W^{(1)}_{abcd}+Z^{(1)}_{abcd}

(14)

is the Weyl tensor. Further details are provided in Appendix
A
.

II.1
1+1+2
decomposition

Let us consider a congruence of timelike curves
x^{a}=x^{a}(\lambda)
, expressed in terms of an affine parameter
\lambda
and filling all spacetime. The 4-vector field tangent to the congruence is

u^{a}=\frac{dx^{a}}{d\lambda},\qquad u_{a}=g_{ab}u^{b},\qquad u^{a}u_{a}=-1.

(15)

At any point, we can identify a
3
-dimensional subspace of the tangent bundle orthogonal to
u^{a}
. The projector onto this spatial subspace is defined as

h_{ab}=g_{ab}+u_{a}u_{b},

(16)

and satisfies the properties

h^{a}{}_{c}h^{c}{}_{b}=h^{a}{}_{b},\qquad h_{ab}u^{b}=0.

(17)

The
1+1+2
decomposition is implemented by introducing a congruence of spacelike curves with tangent 4-vector field
e
everywhere orthogonal to
u
:
g_{ab}u^{a}e^{b}=0
. This allows us to split the spatial subspace into a preferred direction parallel to
e
and a
2
-dimensional subspace, called “sheet”, orthogonal to
e
. The projection tensor onto the sheet is given by

N_{ab}=h_{ab}-e_{a}e_{b},

(18)

with

N^{a}{}_{c}N^{c}{}_{b}=N^{a}{}_{b},\qquad N_{ab}u^{b}=N_{ab}e^{b}=0.

(19)

Norm conservation of vectors
u
and
e
along both the congruences, and with respect to the covariant derivative induced by the full connection
\Gamma_{ab}{}^{c}
, as well as the preservation of their orthogonality impose restrictions on the nonmetricity tensor
Q_{cab}
:

\begin{gathered}u^{c}\nabla_{c}\left(g_{ab}u^{a}u^{b}\right)=Q_{cab}u^{c}u^{a}u^{b}+2u_{b}u^{c}\nabla_{c}u^{b}=0\\
\longrightarrow\quad Q_{cab}u^{c}u^{a}u^{b}=2u^{b}u^{c}\nabla_{c}u_{b},\end{gathered}

(20)

\begin{gathered}e^{c}\nabla_{c}\left(g_{ab}e^{a}e^{b}\right)=Q_{cab}e^{c}e^{a}e^{b}+2e_{b}e^{c}\nabla_{c}e^{b}=0\\
\longrightarrow\quad Q_{cab}e^{c}e^{a}e^{b}=2e^{b}e^{c}\nabla_{c}e_{b},\end{gathered}

(21)

\begin{gathered}e^{c}\nabla_{c}\left(g_{ab}u^{a}u^{b}\right)=Q_{cab}e^{c}u^{a}u^{b}+2u_{b}e^{c}\nabla_{c}u^{b}=0\\
\longrightarrow\quad Q_{cab}e^{c}u^{a}u^{b}=2u^{b}e^{c}\nabla_{c}u_{b},\end{gathered}

(22)

\begin{gathered}u^{c}\nabla_{c}\left(g_{ab}e^{a}e^{b}\right)=Q_{cab}u^{c}e^{a}e^{b}+2e_{b}u^{c}\nabla_{c}e^{b}=0\\
\longrightarrow\quad Q_{cab}u^{c}e^{a}e^{b}=2e^{b}u^{c}\nabla_{c}e_{b},\end{gathered}

(23)

\begin{gathered}u^{c}\nabla_{c}\left(g_{ab}u^{a}e^{b}\right)=Q_{cab}u^{c}u^{a}e^{b}+g_{ab}u^{c}\nabla_{c}\left(u^{a}e^{b}\right)=0\\
\longrightarrow\quad Q_{cab}u^{c}u^{a}e^{b}=u^{b}u^{c}\nabla_{c}e_{b}+e^{b}u^{c}\nabla_{c}u_{b},\end{gathered}

(24)

\begin{gathered}e^{c}\nabla_{c}\left(g_{ab}u^{a}e^{b}\right)=Q_{cab}e^{c}u^{a}e^{b}+g_{ab}e^{c}\nabla_{c}\left(u^{a}e^{b}\right)=0\\
\longrightarrow\quad Q_{cab}e^{c}u^{a}e^{b}=u^{b}e^{c}\nabla_{c}e_{b}+e^{b}e^{c}\nabla_{c}u_{b}.\end{gathered}

(25)

The normalization of
u^{a}
(
g_{ab}u^{a}u^{b}=-1
) and
e^{a}
(
g_{ab}e^{a}e^{b}=1
) implies the following properties for the projector tensors:

h_{a}{}^{a}=3,\qquad\text{and}\qquad N_{a}{}^{a}=2.

(26)

Making use of
h_{ab}
and
N_{ab}
, we can define the projected symmetric trace-free (PSTF) part of a tensor with respect to
u^{a}
and
e^{a}
respectively: given a second rank covariant tensor
\psi_{ab}
we have,

\psi_{\langle ab\rangle}=\left[h_{(a}{}^{c}h_{b)}{}^{d}-\frac{1}{3}h_{ab}h^{cd}\right]\psi_{cd},

(27)

and

\psi_{\{ab\}}=\left[N_{(a}{}^{c}N_{b)}{}^{d}-\frac{1}{2}N_{ab}N^{cd}\right]\psi_{cd}.

(28)

It is also useful to introduce the volume elements derived from the Levi-Civita tensor
\epsilon_{abcd}
,

\epsilon_{abc}=\epsilon_{abcd}u^{d}\qquad\qquad\epsilon_{ab}=\epsilon_{abc}e^{c},

(29)

which are characterized by the following properties:

\epsilon_{abc}=\epsilon_{[abc]},\qquad\qquad\epsilon_{ab}=\epsilon_{[ab]},

(30)

\epsilon_{abc}u^{c}=0,\quad\epsilon_{abc}\epsilon^{dec}=2h_{[a}{}^{d}h_{b]}{}^{e},\quad\epsilon_{abc}\epsilon^{abd}=2h_{c}{}^{d}

(31)

\begin{gathered}\epsilon_{ab}e^{b}=\epsilon_{ab}u^{b}=0,\qquad\qquad\epsilon_{a}{}^{c}\epsilon_{cb}=N_{ab},\\
\epsilon_{abc}=e_{a}\epsilon_{bc}-e_{b}\epsilon_{ac}+e_{c}\epsilon_{ab}.\end{gathered}

(32)

Given a generic tensor
\psi^{a\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{b\cdot\cdot\cdot}

1+1+2
formalism. In particular:

•

the time derivative: the covariant derivative along
u^{a}
,

\dot{\psi}^{a\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{b\cdot\cdot\cdot}=u^{c}\nabla_{c}\psi^{a\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{b\cdot\cdot\cdot};

(33)

•

the spatial derivative: the covariant derivative projected onto the
3
-dimensional subspace orthogonal to
u^{a}
,

D_{c}\psi^{a\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{b\cdot\cdot\cdot}=h_{c}{}^{d}h^{a}{}_{e}\cdot\cdot\cdot h_{b}{}^{f}\cdot\cdot\cdot\nabla_{d}\psi^{e\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{f\cdot\cdot\cdot};

(34)

•

the hat derivative: the covariant spatial derivative along
e^{a}
,

\hat{\psi}^{a\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{b\cdot\cdot\cdot}=e^{c}D_{c}\psi^{a\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{b\cdot\cdot\cdot};

(35)

•

the
\delta
-derivative: the covariant spatial derivative projected onto the sheet orthogonal to
e^{a}
,

\delta_{c}\psi^{a\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{b\cdot\cdot\cdot}=N_{c}{}^{d}N^{a}{}_{e}\cdot\cdot\cdot N_{b}{}^{f}\cdot\cdot\cdot D_{d}\psi^{e\cdot\cdot\cdot}{}_{f\cdot\cdot\cdot}.

(36)

We can now decompose the covariant derivative of the
4
-velocity,

\begin{split}\nabla_{a}u_{b}=&D_{a}u_{b}-u_{a}\left(\mathcal{A}_{b}+e_{b}\mathcal{A}^{(u)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}u_{a}u_{b}Q_{cde}u^{c}u^{d}u^{e}+\\
&-\frac{1}{2}u_{b}e_{a}Q_{cde}e^{c}u^{d}u^{e}-\frac{1}{2}u_{b}N_{a}{}^{c}Q_{cde}u^{d}u^{e},\end{split}

(37)

with

\mathcal{A}^{(u)}=e^{a}u^{b}\nabla_{b}u_{a},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{A}_{b}=N_{b}{}^{a}u^{c}\nabla_{c}u_{a}.

(38)

Moreover, the spatial derivative of
u_{a}
can be expressed as

D_{a}u_{b}=\frac{1}{3}h_{ab}\theta+\sigma_{ab}+\omega_{ab},

(39)

where

\theta=h^{ab}D_{a}u_{b}

(40)

represents the expansion of the time congruence,

\begin{split}\sigma_{ab}=&\left[h_{(a}{}^{c}h_{b)}{}^{d}-\frac{1}{3}h_{ab}h^{cd}\right]D_{c}u_{d}=\\
=&\Sigma_{ab}+2\Sigma_{(a}e_{b)}+\Sigma\left(e_{a}e_{b}-\frac{1}{2}N_{ab}\right)\end{split}

(41)

represents the distortion, and it is called “shear” tensor, whereas

\begin{gathered}\Sigma_{ab}=\sigma_{\{ab\}},\qquad\qquad\Sigma_{a}=N_{a}{}^{c}e^{b}\sigma_{cb},\\
\Sigma=e^{a}e^{b}\sigma_{ab}=-N^{ab}\sigma_{ab}\end{gathered}

(42)

are the tensor, vector, and scalar parts of the shear, respectively. Finally, the quantity

\omega_{ab}=D_{[a}u_{b]}=\epsilon_{ab}\Omega+\epsilon_{abc}\Omega^{c}

(43)

is the “vorticity” tensor of the time congruence, with

\Omega_{c}=\frac{1}{2}N_{cd}\epsilon^{abd}D_{a}u_{b},\qquad\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ab}N_{a}{}^{c}N_{b}{}^{d}D_{c}u_{d}.

(44)

In an analogous way, the covariant derivative of
e_{a}
can be expressed as,

\begin{split}\nabla_{a}e_{b}=&D_{a}e_{b}-u_{a}\alpha_{b}-\mathcal{A}^{(e)}u_{a}u_{b}+\left(\frac{1}{3}\theta+\Sigma\right)e_{a}u_{b}+\\
&+\Sigma_{a}u_{b}-\epsilon_{ab}\Omega^{d}u_{b}-\frac{1}{2}u_{a}e_{b}Q_{cdf}u^{c}e^{d}e^{f}+\\
&-N_{a}{}^{c}u_{b}Q_{cdf}u^{d}e^{f}-e_{a}u_{b}Q_{cdf}e^{c}u^{d}e^{f}.\end{split}

(45)

The spatial derivative of
e_{a}
is given by

\begin{split}D_{a}e_{b}=&\frac{1}{2}N_{ab}\phi+\zeta_{ab}+\epsilon_{ab}\xi+e_{a}\mathrm{a}_{b}+\\
&-N_{a}{}^{c}e_{b}L_{cd}^{f}e^{d}e_{f}+\frac{1}{2}e_{a}e_{b}Q_{cdf}e^{c}e^{d}e^{f},\end{split}

(46)

where

\phi=N^{ab}\delta_{a}e_{b}\qquad\zeta_{ab}=\delta_{\{a}e_{b\}}\qquad\xi=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ab}\delta_{a}e_{b}

(47)

are the analogous of expansion, shear, and vorticity for the spatial vector
e_{a}
. The scalar

\mathcal{A}^{(e)}=-u^{b}u^{a}\nabla_{a}e_{b}

(48)

e^{a}
along

\alpha_{b}=u^{c}N_{b}{}^{d}\nabla_{c}e_{d}

(49)

is the projection of the temporal acceleration of
e^{a}
onto the sheet, and

\mathrm{a}_{b}=e^{c}N_{b}{}^{d}\nabla_{c}e_{d}

In addition to this, making use of the Weyl tensor (

\begin{gathered}H_{cd}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{c}{}^{ab}u^{e}C_{abde},\\
\bar{H}_{ab}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{a}{}^{cd}u^{e}C_{becd}=H_{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{a}{}^{cd}u^{e}Z^{(1)}{}_{becd},\\

\check{E}_{cd}=C_{abcd}u^{a}u^{b},\end{gathered}

which are the “magnetic” and “electric” parts of the Weyl tensor, respectively. These tensors satisfy the following properties:

\begin{gathered}H_{cd}=h_{c}{}^{a}h_{d}{}^{b}H_{ab},\quad H_{cd}=H_{dc},\\
\quad H^{a}{}_{a}=\bar{H}^{a}{}_{a}=\check{H}^{a}{}_{a}=0,\end{gathered}

(53)

\check{E}_{cd}=-\check{E}_{dc},\qquad E^{a}{}_{a}=\bar{E}^{a}{}_{a}=\check{E}^{a}{}_{a}=0.

(57)

(58)

(59)

\check{E}_{ab}=\mathfrak{E}_{ab}+2\mathfrak{E}_{[a}e_{b]}-2\check{\mathfrak{E}}_{[a}u_{b]}.

III
Static and Locally Rotationally Symmetric spacetime

A spacetime is said to be Locally Rotationally Symmetric in a neighborhood
N(p^{*})
of a point
p^{*}
,
if at each point
p
in
N(p^{*})
there exists a non-discrete subgroup G of the Lorentz group in the tangent space
T_{p}
which leaves invariant
u^{a}
, the curvature tensor and their derivatives up to third order

Stewart and Ellis 1968
. If
G
is one-dimensional, there is a single preferred spatial direction that constitutes a local axis of symmetry. Therefore, the LRS definition implies that all the spacelike projections of tensors in spatial directions different from the axis of symmetry must be zero.

In the
1+1+2
formalism, the natural choice for the local axis of symmetry is the above-defined spatial vector
e^{a}
. The non-zero kinematic and thermodynamic quantities are the scalars

III.2
Static spacetime

The spacetime is said “stationary” if it admits a timelike Killing vector field
\mathbf{X}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}g_{ab}=0,

(71)

with
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}
denoting Lie derivative. In our geometrical setting in which torsion is zero, we also require the further condition

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}Q_{abc}=0,

(72)

i.e., both metric and nonmetricity tensors are invariant under the action of the
1
-parameter group of local diffeomorphisms generated by
\mathbf{X}
.

If the congruence associated with
\mathbf{X}
is hypersurface orthogonal, the spacetime is said “static”. In the presence of nonmetricity, the Killing equations assume the form

\begin{split}0=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{X}}g_{ab}=&2\nabla_{(a}X_{b)}+2X^{c}L_{abc}=\\
=&2\tilde{\nabla}_{(a}X_{b)}.\end{split}

(73)

In the following analysis, the form involving the total covariant derivative will be used.

We choose the Killing vector expressed as

\mathbf{X}=C(x)u,

(74)

where
C(x)
is a generic smooth function of the coordinates. Contracting Eq. (
73
) twice with
u^{a}
, we obtain

\dot{C}=0,

(75)

that is, the function
C
is independent of time. On the other hand, the contraction of Eq. (
73
) with
u^{a}
and
e^{b}
yields the relation,

\hat{C}=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}C.

(76)

Finally, from the contraction with
h_{c}{}^{a}h_{d}{}^{b}
we find

\begin{split}C&\left[N_{ab}\left(\frac{2}{3}\theta-\Sigma+\frac{1}{2}Q_{6}-Q_{7}\right)+\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.+e_{a}e_{b}\left(\frac{2}{3}\theta+2\Sigma+Q_{2}-2Q_{3}\right)\right]=0,\end{split}

(77)

from which we derive

\begin{gathered}\theta=-\frac{1}{2}Q_{2}+Q_{3}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{6}+Q_{7},\\
\Sigma=-\frac{1}{3}Q_{2}+\frac{2}{3}Q_{3}+\frac{1}{6}Q_{6}-\frac{1}{3}Q_{7}.\end{gathered}

(78)

The condition (
72
) and Eq. (
78
) ensure that the time derivative of the scalar components of nonmetricity tensor is zero. Hence, because of the LRS symmetries and the stationary conditions, the covariant derivatives of the scalars (
63
) are zero except those along the radial direction
e^{a}
.

Given condition (
64
), we have that
\mathbf{X}
is hypersurface orthogonal since it is proportional to the
4
-velocity. Thus, our spacetime is static.

By a direct comparison of Eq. (
70
) with Eq. (
78
), we obtain the identities

\tilde{\theta}=0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{\Sigma}=0.

(79)

This is not a surprising result since
\tilde{\theta}
and
\tilde{\Sigma}
are, respectively, the expansion and shear we would have in GR where both quantities are known to be null in a stationary LRS spacetime of class II. Moreover, it is a direct consequence of the Killing equations (
73
) when using the form with the Levi-Civita covariant derivative.

Equation (
78
) means that in the presence of a non-metric connection, expansion and shear can manifest
exclusively due to the nonmetricity tensor.

IV
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity

f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity is a generalization of Symmetric Teleparallel Gravity in which both curvature and torsion are zero. The action is given by

\begin{split}A=&\int\hbox{d}^{4}x\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}f(\mathcal{Q})+\lambda_{a}{}^{bcd}R^{a}{}_{bcd}+\right.\\
&\left.+\lambda_{a}{}^{cd}T_{cd}{}^{a}+\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{m}\right],\end{split}

(80)

where
g
is the determinant of metric,
f(\mathcal{Q})
is a generic function of the nonmetricity scalar,
\mathcal{L}_{m}
is the matter Lagrangian density,
\lambda_{a}{}^{bij}
and
\lambda_{a}{}^{ij}
are Lagrange multipliers introduced to impose the vanishing of curvature and torsion.
Variations with respect to Lagrange multipliers, metric, and connection yield the field equations,

R^{a}{}_{bcd}=0,\qquad T_{ab}{}^{c}=0,

(81)

\begin{split}&\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{c}\left(\sqrt{-g}f^{\prime}P^{c}{}_{ab}\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}f+\\
&\qquad\qquad+f^{\prime}\left(P_{acd}Q_{b}{}^{cd}-2Q^{cd}{}_{a}P_{cdb}\right)=\Psi_{ij},\end{split}

(82)

and

\nabla_{d}\lambda_{c}{}^{bad}+\lambda_{c}{}^{ab}-\sqrt{-g}f^{\prime}P^{ab}{}_{c}=\Delta^{ab}{}_{c},

(83)

with

P^{c}{}_{ab}=-\frac{1}{4}Q^{c}{}_{ab}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{(ab)}{}^{c}+\frac{1}{4}q^{c}g_{ab}-\frac{1}{4}Q^{c}g_{ab}-\frac{1}{4}q_{(a}\delta^{c}_{b)},

(84)

and

\begin{gathered}\Psi_{ab}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\left(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)}{\delta g^{ab}},\\
\Phi^{ab}{}_{c}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\left(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)}{\delta{\Gamma_{ab}{}^{c}}}.\end{gathered}

(85)

In the above equations, the prime denotes the derivative with respect to the argument of the function. Since we will consider perfect fluids, in our case
\Phi^{ab}{}_{c}
is identically zero, and the energy-momentum conservation law is equal to the standard one,

\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\Psi^{a}{}_{b}=0.

(86)

The metric field equation Eq. (
82
) can be recast in a more suitable form for the
1+1+2
formalism,

\begin{split}\tilde{R}_{ab}=&\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}\left(\Psi_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\Psi\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\left(\frac{f}{f^{\prime}}-\mathcal{Q}\right)+\\
&-2\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\left(P^{c}{}_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}P^{cd}{}_{d}\right)\partial_{c}\mathcal{Q},\end{split}

(87)

with
\Psi=g^{ab}\Psi_{ab}
. By replacing
f(\mathcal{Q})=\mathcal{Q}
into Eq. (
87

\tilde{R}_{ab}=\Psi_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\Psi.

(88)

Some preliminary considerations are in order before writing the final system of equations to be solved.

V.2
\mathcal{Q}
-Gravastars

Among the exotic objects proposed as alternatives for black holes, there are the so-called gravitational vacuum condensate stars or Gravastars
Mazur and Mottola 2023
;
Visser and Wiltshire 2004

f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity can admit gravastar solutions without invoking explicitly the presence of a cosmological constant. We dub these solutions “Q-gravastars” and give their most straightforward realization in the following.

First, let us show that the
\mathcal{Q}
dependent terms in (
87
) can generate an effective cosmological constant. Rewriting these equations in the form

\tilde{R}_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\tilde{R}=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}T_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\left(\frac{f}{f^{\prime}}-\mathcal{Q}\right)-2\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}P^{c}{}_{ab}\partial_{c}\mathcal{Q},

(140)

we have that, in the case
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{*}=const.
, the effective energy-momentum tensor,

T^{eff}_{ab}=-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\left(\frac{f(\mathcal{Q}_{*})}{f^{\prime}(\mathcal{Q}_{*})}-\mathcal{Q}_{*}\right)=-g_{ab}\Lambda_{*},

(141)

can be thought of as a fluid characterized by a negative pressure,

\frac{1}{3}T^{eff}_{ab}h^{ab}=p_{eff}=-\rho_{eff}=-T^{eff}_{ab}u^{a}u^{b},

(142)

\Lambda_{*}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f(\mathcal{Q}_{*})}{f^{\prime}(\mathcal{Q}_{*})}-\mathcal{Q}_{*}\right)>0,

(143)

\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{B}^{-1}=1-\frac{1}{3}\Lambda_{*}r^{2}.

(144)

A critical aspect of the Gravastar models is their compatibility with a vacuum (Schwarzschild) exterior. Such compatibility is obtained by the well-known Israel-Darmois junction conditions

VI
Discussion and conclusions

We have developed the
1+1+2
covariant formalism for static and LRS spacetimes of class II in the presence of nonmetricity. The resulting geometrical setting has been applied for studying the features of vacuum solutions arising in
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity, including the Birkhoff theorem and the presence of Gravastar solutions.

We found that also in the case of
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity, the
1+1+2
covariant formalism is very efficient in describing LRS spacetime and allows us to understand in a very detailed way the effect of nonmetricity precluding from a specific choice of gauge. Special attention has been devoted to a complete decomposition of the Weyl tensor, which has revealed itself to be fundamental to properly analyzing spacetime dynamics.

Our analysis shows that nonmetricity gives rise to significant kinematic and dynamical differences with the purely metric case. For example, the timelike and spacelike congruences can be associated with two different types of acceleration, provided by Eqs. (
38
) and (
48
), respectively. The difference between the accelerations is related to the geometric properties of the congruences themselves, like, e.g., autoparallelism.

As a first application of the general formalism, we have analyzed the case of static and spherically symmetric spacetimes. In particular, using some simplifying assumptions on the nonmetricity tensor connected with the requirement of autoparallelism for the timelike congruence, we have deduced a self-consistent system of algebraic/differential equations for the investigation of vacuum solutions. These equations allow the identification of the conditions under which the theory produces the same Einstein-like field equations as GR. In addition, we have derived sufficient conditions that ensure the existence of a Schwarzschild-de Sitter solution in vacuum, extending the ones presented so far in the literature.

A second study case concerns the presence of gravastars in the context of
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity. We have shown that the simplest possible gravastar solution can be achieved by setting the nonmetricity scalar
\mathcal{Q}
to zero. In this hypothesis, we have an effective cosmological constant, and the solution requires the presence of a shell that can be composed of standard matter. However, the equations show that this toy model is only one possible realization of this scenario. Other realizations, based, for example, on the case of non-constant
\mathcal{Q}
, might lead to gravastars that do not require shells. In this respect, these solutions would be akin to Brill–Hartle Geons (see, e.g.,
Brill and Hartle 1964
;
Anderson and Brill 1997
). Future studies will investigate the existence and, above all, the dynamics of such solutions in
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity.

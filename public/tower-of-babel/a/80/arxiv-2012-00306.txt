Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a family of generalized Donaldson’s functional on holomorphic vector bundles, whose Euler-Lagrange equations are a vector bundle version of the complex k k -Hessian equations. We also discuss the uniqueness of solutions to these equations.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\omega)
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. For any
B\in\Omega^{2}(\End(E))
, that is any
\End(E)
-valued 2-form, we denote

(1.1)

B^{k}:=\underbrace{B\wedge B\cdots\wedge B}_{k},

with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\leq n
. For any given Hermitian metric
H
on
E
, we use
D_{H}
to denote the Chern connection with respect to the metric
H
and the holomorphic structure, and
F_{H}
is the curvature tensor of the Chern connection
D_{H}
.

Let
H_{0}
be a fixed Hermitian metric on
E
and
H(s)
a path connecting metrics
H_{0}
and another Hermitian metric
H
. For any integer
0<k\leq n
, we introduce the following generalized Donaldson’s functionals

(1.2)

\mathcal{M}_{E,k}^{0}(H_{0},H)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mbox{\rm tr\,}\left[\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(s)}\right)^{k}\,H(s)^{-1}\,\frac{\partial H(s)}{\partial s}\right]\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}\,ds

and

(1.3)

\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},H)=\mathcal{M}_{E,k}^{0}(H_{0},H)-\int_{M}\lambda_{E,k}\cdot\log\det(H_{0}^{-1}H)\,\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!},

where the constant
\lambda_{E,k}=(2\pi)^{k}\,(k!)\,\frac{ch_{k}(E)\cdot\left[\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}\right]}{{\rm rank}(E)\cdot{\rm vol}(M,\omega)}
.

As it will be shown in §2, the functional
\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},H)
is well defined, i.e. it is independent of the choice of path
H(s)
. Indeed, when
k=1
, the above functional
\mathcal{M}_{E,1}(H_{0},H)
is just the well-known
Donaldson’s functional
, which was introduced by Donaldson (
[
12
]
,
[
24
]
) in his groundbreaking work on the study of the Hermitian-Yang-Mills connection. In
[
12
]
, it is an important observation that the Euler-Lagrange equation of the Donaldson’s functional is just the Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation

(1.4)

\sqrt{-1}\,\Lambda_{\omega}F_{H}=\lambda_{E,1}{\rm Id}_{E}.

The solvability of the above equation has many interesting geometric applications. Motivated by the study of the inequalities between Chern numbers, we introduce the generalized Donaldson’s functional by increasing the power for the curvature tensor
F_{H}
, which corresponds to higher order Chern classes.

In §2, we will show that the Euler-Lagrange equation of the generalized Donaldson’s functional
\mathcal{M}_{E,k}
is given by

(1.5)

\frac{\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H}\right)^{k}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}}{\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}}=\lambda_{E,k}\,{\rm Id}_{E}.

It is easy to see that, when
k=1
, the above equation is just the standard Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation (
1.4
). On the other hand, if
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is a line bundle, the equation (
1.5
) reduces to the complex
k
-Hessian equation. Given this connection, we will simply call the above equation as the
vector bundle valued
k
-Hessian equation
. In fact, such type of equations were considered in various settings. When
k=n
, the equation (
1.5
) is a vector bundle version of the complex Monge-Ampère equation introduced by Pingali (
[
40
]
) from the viewpoint of moment map, which generalizes the dHYM equations studied in
[
10
,
11
]
to vector bundles. When
k=2
, equations as (
1.5
) also arise naturally in the Hull-Strominger system from theoretical physics. One crucial equation in the system is the so-called anomaly cancellation equation, which can be viewed as a constraint on the second Chern class and it has similar form as (
1.5
). The system has been studied extensively in recent years, see for example
[
14
,
15
,
21
,
41
,
42
,
43
,
44
,
47
]
.

For the Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation (
1.4
), the classical Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem (
[
39
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
56
]
) states that a holomorphic bundle admits the Hermitian-Einstein metric if and only if it is poly-stable in the sense of Mumford-Takemoto. There are many interesting and important works related (
[

,

,

,

,

,

,
34
,

,
46
]
, etc.). The complex Monge-Ampère equation has been the subject of intensive studies in the past forty years, since the groundbreaking work by Yau (
[
55
]
) on the proof of the Calabi conjecture (
[
7
]
). Many interesting works were carried out by many people in the past several decades (see e.g.

Given all the above nice connections mentioned above, we believe that it will be very interesting to study the vector bundle version of the complex Hessian equation (
1.5
). In this paper, we are going to study the uniqueness problem of the solution. Before doing that, one has to address the issue about the natural set for the solutions as the Gärding cone for the standard Hessian equation for functions (see for example
[
50
]
). However, the situation here is much more complicated than the scalar case.
In the following, the space of Hermitian metrics on
E
will be denoted by
\emph{Herm}(E)
. For any Hermitian metric
H\in\emph{Herm}(E)
, we denote

(1.6)

S_{H}(E)=\left\{\eta\in\Omega^{0}(M,End(E))\,|\,\eta^{\ast H}=\eta\right\},

(1.7)

S_{H,\epsilon}(E)=\left\{\eta\in S_{H}(E)\,|\ \sup_{M}|\eta|_{H}<\epsilon,\ \sup_{M}\left(|\partial_{H}\eta|_{H}+|\overline{\partial}\partial_{H}\eta|_{H}\right)<\epsilon\right\}

and

(1.8)

B_{H,\epsilon}(E)=\left\{K\in\emph{Herm}(E)\,|\ \log H^{-1}K\in S_{H,\epsilon}(E)\right\}.

Now, we introduce a
positivity condition
, which is a natural generalization for the
\Gamma_{k}
cone for scalar Hessian equation.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
H
be a Hermitian metric on the holomorphic vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega)
. The curvature tensor
\sqrt{-1}F_{H}
is said to be
\sigma_{k}
-
\omega
-positive on a point
p\in M
if, for any non-zero
End(E)
-valued
(0,1)
-form
\xi
, we have

(1.9)

\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}\wedge\sqrt{-1}\,\mbox{\rm tr\,}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H}\right)^{i}\wedge\xi^{\ast H}\wedge\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H}\right)^{k-1-i}\wedge\xi\right]\bigg|_{p}>0.

We denote the set of Hermitian metrics whose curvature are
\sigma_{k}
-
\omega
-positive on
M
by
\mathcal{H}_{\omega,k}
.

It is a natural question whether the set
\mathcal{H}_{\omega,k}
is indeed convex or not. That is, for any two metrics
K,H\in\mathcal{H}_{\omega,k}
, let
H(t)
be the geodesic connecting
K
and
H
, do we have
H(t)\in\mathcal{H}_{\omega,k}
? Indeed, if one can give a positive answer to the above question, by the second variation formula (
3.8
) for the generalized Donaldson’s functional (
1.3
), one can conclude that a solution
H\in\mathcal{H}_{\omega,k}
of the bundle-valued complex
k
-Hessian equation (
1.5
) attains an absolute minimum of the generalized Donaldson’s functional (
1.3
), and then obtain the uniqueness of the solution. At present we can not prove the convexity of
\mathcal{H}_{\omega,k}
. Even if we can’t give a positive answer to the above question, we can still prove local uniqueness of the solution of bundle version complex
k
-Hessian equation (
1.5
) and the local minimum in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a holomorphic vector bundle over an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega)
,
H_{0}
be a Hermitian metric on
E
. If
H
is a solution of equation (
1.5
) and its curvature is
\sigma_{k}
-
\omega
-positive on
M
, then there exists a small number
\epsilon
such that, for any
\tilde{H}\in B_{H,\epsilon}(E)
, we have

(1.10)

\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},H)\leq\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},\tilde{H}).

Moreover, if the holomorphic bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is simple and
K\in B_{H,\epsilon}(E)
is another solution of (
1.5
), then there exists a constant
a
such that
K=e^{a}H
.

For the special case
k=2
, under the strongly
\sigma_{2}
-
\omega
-positive hypothesis (see the definition in §3), we obtain the following uniqueness theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a simple holomorphic vector bundle over an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega)
,
H
and
K
be two Hermitian metrics on
E
whose curvature are strongly
\sigma_{2}
-
\omega
-positive on the whole
M
. If both
H
and
K
are solutions of (
1.5
) for
k=2
, then there must exist a constant
a
such that
K=e^{a}H
.

In the following, we want to discuss the
k
-stability of holomorphic vector bundles. First, let us recall the definition of the Chern classes (Chern character class) of coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
.
If the base manifold
M
is a projective manifold, there exists a resolution

(1.11)

0\rightarrow E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow E_{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\rightarrow 0

for each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
by locally free sheaves. The total Chern class
c(\mathcal{F})
is defined by

(1.12)

c(\mathcal{F})=c(E_{N})c(E_{N-1})^{-1}\cdots c(E_{N-i})^{(-1)^{i}}\cdots c(E_{1})^{(-1)^{N-1}}.

The definition is indeed independent of the chosen resolution as proved by Borel and Serre
[
5
]
. Over a non-projective compact complex manifold, the Chern classes of a coherent sheaf can be defined by the classes of Atiyah-Hirzenbruch (
[
2
]
, see
[
17
]
for details). Note that on a non-projective compact complex manifold, a coherent sheaf does not necessarily have a global resolution via locally free coherent sheaves. Atiyah and Hirzebruch
[
2
]
circumvent this problem by using real-analytic functions. Using Grauert’s results
[
16
]
, they prove that, by taking tensor with the sheaf
\mathcal{A}_{M}
of germs of real analytic functions on
M
, there is a resolution by real analytic vector bundles

(1.13)

0\rightarrow E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow E_{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{M}}\mathcal{A}_{M}\rightarrow 0,

where
E_{i}^{\prime}s
are real analytic complex vector bundles. Then we can define the total Chern class
c(\mathcal{F})
as that in (
1.12
). The definition is also shown to be independent of the choice of resolution. By the way, we can obtain the definition of the Chern character classes
ch_{k}(\mathcal{F})
. We define the
k
-
\omega
-degree of
\mathcal{F}
by

(1.14)

deg_{k,\omega}(\mathcal{F})=\int_{M}ch_{k}(\mathcal{F})\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold, and
\mathcal{E}
be a torsion-free coherent sheaf
over
M
.

\mathcal{E}
is
called
k
-
\omega
-stable (semi-stable), if for every coherent sub-sheaf

\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}
of lower rank, it holds:

(1.15)

\mu_{k,\omega}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{deg_{k,\omega}(\mathcal{F})}{rank\mathcal{F}}<(\leq)\mu_{k,\omega}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{deg_{k,\omega}(\mathcal{E})}{rank\mathcal{E}}.

When
k=1
,
k
-
\omega
-stability is just the stability in the sense of Mumford-Takemoto, and is MA-stability (
[
40
]
) when
k=n
. Following the classical Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem, it is natural to conjecture:
Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a simple holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold, and the set
\mathcal{H}_{\omega,k}
is non empty. Then
E
admits a Hermitian metric
H
satisfying the bundle valued complex
k
-Hessian equations (
1.5
) if and only if it is
k
-
\omega
-stable
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove that the generalized Donaldson’s functional is well defined, and give the first variation formula of this functional. In section 3, we give the proof of theorem
1.2
and theorem
1.3
.

2.
Generalized Donaldson’s functionals

Let
(M,\omega)
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
.
Given any two Hermitian metrics
H_{0}
and
H
on bundle
E
, let

(2.1)

\displaystyle h=H_{0}^{-1}H\in\Gamma(End(E)).

It is easy to check that

(2.2)

h^{\ast H_{0}}=h^{\ast H}=h,

(2.3)

D_{H}=D_{H_{0}}+h^{-1}\partial_{H_{0}}h

and

(2.4)

F_{H}=F_{H_{0}}+\overline{\partial}(h^{-1}\partial_{H_{0}}h).

Moreover, we can prove the following lemma

Lemma 2.1
.

The functional
\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},H)
is well defined, i.e. it is independent of the choice of path
H(s)
.

Proof.

Let
H(\tau,s)
be a two-parameter family of metrics such that

H(\tau,0)=H_{0}\ \ \textit{\rm and}\ \ H(\tau,1)=H,

where
\tau\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
,
s\in[0,1]
. Then,
H(0,s)
and
H(1,s)
are two paths connecting
H_{0}
and
H
. Let

h(\tau,s)=H_{0}^{-1}H(\tau,s).

It is easy to check that

(2.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}F_{H(\tau,s)}=\overline{\partial}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}\left(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}\right)

and

(2.6)

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F_{H(\tau,s)}=\overline{\partial}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}\left(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right).

Claim:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\mbox{\rm tr\,}\left\{\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)}\right)^{k}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right\}-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mbox{\rm tr\,}\left\{\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)}\right)^{k}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial\mbox{\rm tr\,}\left\{\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}\eta_{k}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right\}-\overline{\partial}\mbox{\rm tr\,}\left\{\sqrt{-1}\partial_{H}\phi_{k}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}\right\}

where

\eta_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)}\right)^{i}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}\wedge\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)}\right)^{k-1-i}

and

\phi_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)}\right)^{i}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\wedge\left(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)}\right)^{k-1-i}.

By the equality (
2.7
), we have:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}H^{-1}(\tau,s)\frac{\partial H(\tau,s)}{\partial s}]\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\{\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}H^{-1}(\tau,s)\frac{\partial H(\tau,s)}{\partial s}]\}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\{\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}H^{-1}(\tau,s)\frac{\partial H(\tau,s)}{\partial\tau}]\}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\{\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}H^{-1}(\tau,s)\frac{\partial H(\tau,s)}{\partial\tau}]\}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}|_{s=0}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

This shows that the functional
\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},H)
is independent of the choice of path
H(s)
. Therefore, we only need to prove the claim (
2.7
).

By applying the second Bianchi identity, we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}H^{-1}(\tau,s)\frac{\partial H(\tau,s)}{\partial s}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{i}\wedge\overline{\partial}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau})\wedge(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k-1-i}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}]

\displaystyle+\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}(-h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}+h^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial\tau\partial s})],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(\eta_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}]+\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}(-h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}+h^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial\tau\partial s})]

and similarly

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}H^{-1}(\tau,s)\frac{\partial H(\tau,s)}{\partial\tau}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(\phi_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}]+\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}(-h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}+h^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial s\partial\tau})].

Using (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\mbox{\rm tr\,}\{(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\}-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mbox{\rm tr\,}\{(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}\overline{\partial}(\eta_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}]-\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(\phi_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}]

\displaystyle+\mbox{\rm tr\,}\{(\sqrt{-1}F_{H}\wedge\eta_{k}-\eta_{k}\wedge\sqrt{-1}F_{H})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\}

\displaystyle-\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s})]+\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau})].

By direct calculation, one can check that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm tr\,}\{(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)}\wedge\eta_{k}-\eta_{k}\wedge\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\}

\displaystyle-\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s})]+\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(\tau,s)})^{k}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle-\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}\overline{\partial}(\eta_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}]-\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(\phi_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}(\eta_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}]-\overline{\partial}\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(\phi_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}]

\displaystyle-\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}(\eta_{k})\wedge\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s})]-\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(\phi_{k})\wedge\overline{\partial}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}(\eta_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}]-\overline{\partial}\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(\phi_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}]

\displaystyle-\mbox{\rm tr\,}\{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(\sqrt{-1}F_{H})^{i}\wedge\overline{\partial}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau})\wedge(\sqrt{-1}F_{H})^{k-1-i}\wedge\partial_{H}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s})\}

\displaystyle-\mbox{\rm tr\,}\{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(\sqrt{-1}F_{H})^{j}\wedge\partial_{H}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s})\wedge(\sqrt{-1}F_{H})^{k-1-j}\wedge\overline{\partial}(h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\overline{\partial}(\eta_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}]-\overline{\partial}\mbox{\rm tr\,}[\sqrt{-1}\partial_{H(\tau,s)}(\phi_{k})h^{-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\tau}].

So (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) imply the claim (
2.7
).

In the following, we denote

(2.14)

\Psi_{k}(H)=\frac{(\sqrt{-1}F_{H})^{k}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}}{\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}}.

Let
H(t)
be a family of metrics on
E
. We can consider two parameter family of metrics
H(t,s)
such that
H(t,0)=H_{0}
and
H(t,1)=H(t)
. By (
2.7
), we have the following first variation formula:

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},H(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\{\mbox{\rm tr\,}[(\sqrt{-1}F_{H(t,s)})^{k}H^{-1}(t,s)\frac{\partial H(t,s)}{\partial s}]\}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}ds

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\lambda_{E,k}\log\det(h(t))\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mbox{\rm tr\,}\{[\Psi_{k}(H(t))-\lambda_{E,k}Id_{E}]H^{-1}(t)\frac{\partial H(t)}{\partial t}\}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

The Hermitian metric
H
is a critical of the functional
\mathcal{M}_{E,k}
if and only if it satisfies the equation

(2.16)

\Psi_{k}(H)-\lambda_{E,k}Id_{E}=0.

By the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{E,k}
and choosing a special path, it is easy to obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

(1) For any metrics
H_{i}
,
i=0,1,2
, we have:

(2.17)

\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},H_{1})+\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{1},H_{2})=\mathcal{M}_{E,k}(H_{0},H_{2}).

(2)

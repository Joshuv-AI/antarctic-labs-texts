Clausius Equation for Horizons in F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) Gravity
Bohuslav Matouš

Abstract
Covariant Hamiltonian formulation of F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) gravity is used to find variation of surface Hamiltonian which leads to formulation of temperature and entropy of a horizon. These thermodynamic quantities are then used to establish an analogy between Einstein equation and Clausius equation. The obtained results are compared to those from classical General Relativity.

Introduction and Summary

The idea of interpreting Einstein equations as a thermodynamic equation originated in work of Jacobson
[
1
]
where he found analogy between Einstein equation and Clausius equation using Rindler horizons and Raychadhauri equation. His method was generalised for
F(R)
gravitational theory in later works
[
2
]
[
3
]
[
22

where this gravitational theory was seen as non-equilibrium thermodynamics and the contribution from new gravity term was interpreted as an addition to entropy which caused a dissipative nature of gravity.

Other approach to study thermodynamic aspects of gravity was introduced in works of Padmanabhan and his colleagues
[
4
]
[
5
]
[
6
]
. Their approach focuses strongly on Lagrangian and variation principle. It is known that Lagrangian for general relativity can be separated into two parts, the quadratic bulk term and a surface term. When deriving the equations of motion with fixed variation of metric tensor and its conjugated momentum, the surface term vanishes. But in general it can be shown that there is a relationship between the two parts of Lagrangian
[
7
]
[
8
]
and that both of them contain information about space-time configuration. This sparked interest to the surface Lagrangian which was until then eclipsed by the bulk Lagrangian. It was found in
[
9
]
that action of the surface Lagrangian on a horizon leads directly to the temperature and the entropy of such horizon. This has put the surface Lagrangian into the spotlight of thermodynamic research of gravity and this surface Lagrangian has been since then thoroughly examined.

The description of gravity is mostly done in Lagrangian formalism that is caused by the principle of covariance. If one wants to switch from the Lagrangian formalism to Hamiltonian formalism using classical Hamiltonian theory the covariance gets broken because the momentum is defined by partial derivative of a Lagrangian with respect to time derivative of metric. To preserve the covariance, a covariant Hamiltonian formulation of field theory has to be used. Weyl-De Donder theory
[
10
]
[
11
]
[
12
]
is such theory. The Weyl-De Donder theory defines the momentum as a partial derivative of a Lagrangian with respect to each partial derivative of each coordinate. That basically means that the momenta have dimensions one higher than the coordinates.

Covariant Hamiltonian formulation of General Relativity was presented by Hořava
[
13
]
and Parattu
[
14
]
. In the latter, it was shown that the situation simplifies significantly when the role of coordinate is not acted by simple metric but a slightly different coordinate
f^{ik}=\sqrt{-g}g^{ik}
. This coordinate is not a new one but it has historical significance
[
15
]
.

This article works with the
F(R)
theory and aims to find Clausius equations on horizons. It is following the Padmanabhan’s approach and examines the surface Hamiltonians of horizons using method presented in
[
14
]
. To do so, covariant formulation of
F(R)
gravity presented in
[
16
]
is used. Albeit the surface Lagrangians in General Relativity and
F(R)
-Gravity are similar when written with connection, they are different when using canonical coordinates. The surface Lagrangian is used to formulate surface Hamiltonian. From the surface Hamiltonian it is visible, that the only thermodynamic difference to General Relativity is the extra factor
F^{\prime}
in entropy. This result is expected, but it validates the used approach.

In the second part, two metrics — spherically symmetric and general static — are studied to calculate
T\delta S
from the surface Hamiltonian. Equations of motion allow to express this relation in a form, which can be then interpreted in a form of Clausius equation. Obtained Clausius equations contain a new member. This member is traditionally interpreted as internal entropy of gravity. However in the used approach, it seems more natural to explain it as a new pressure induced by higher curvature terms of
F(R)
. Interesting is also the fact that the terms containing second derivatives of
F^{\prime}
vanish on horizon for both metrics. It remains an open question, whether such behaviour is general or they can contribute to a new member of Clausius equation for some metrics.

3
Surface Hamiltonian

Let us now examine the surface Lagrangian and its relations to boundaries. The basic relationship for us is the Hamilton’s principle of least action which tell us

\mathcal{A}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{L}\>\text{d}^{4}x\>,

(12)

where
\mathcal{M}
is a manifold and
\mathcal{A}
is the action. Since the action is functional, which is linear in Lagrangian, it can be separated in the very same manner as the Lagrangian and through it we obtain two actions — bulk and surface

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{bulk}+\mathcal{A}_{sur\!f}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{L}_{bulk}\>\text{d}^{4}x+\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{L}_{sur\!f}\>\text{d}^{4}x\>.

(13)

The surface Lagrangian, as its name advises, is significant only on boundaries, because it has a form of divergence of some vector potential

\mathcal{L}_{sur\!f}=\partial_{i}\left(\sqrt{-g}V^{i}\right)\>.

(14)

Thus, the surface action is

\mathcal{A}_{sur\!f}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{L}_{sur\!f}\>\text{d}^{4}x=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\partial_{i}\left(\sqrt{-g}V^{i}\right)\>\text{d}^{4}x=\oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{-g}V^{i}\>\text{d}B_{i}\>.

(15)

where
B_{i}
is the unit covector normal to the manifold’s border
\partial M
.
This is the point, where we put in a bit of thermodynamics and use formula, which defines surface Hamiltonian and relates surface action to entropy
[
14
]
[
17
]

\mathcal{H}_{sur\!f}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal{A}_{sur\!f}}{\partial\tau}=TS\>,

(16)

where
\tau
is the proper time,

is temperature of the horizon and
S
its entropy, the minus sign is a matter of convention, which we will follow.

Note, that the total Hamiltonian can be also separated same way as Lagrangian and action and thus

\mathcal{H}_{total}=\mathcal{H}_{sur\!f}+\mathcal{H}=TS+\mathcal{H}\>,

(17)

which reminds us the Legendre transformation of thermodynamic potentials. Therefore if say that the total Hamiltonian

is an analogy of inner energy then the bulk Hamiltonian can be seen as analogy of free energy
[
9
]
.

However the proper time in (
16

\mathcal{H}_{sur\!f}=-\oint_{H}\sqrt{-g}V^{n}\>\text{d}H_{n}\>,

(18)

H
is the two dimensional surface of a horizon and

Vector potential

(20)

the only difference is the factor
F^{\prime}
. Since the surface Hamiltonian is linear in

-Gravity will also be factor of

.

4
Spherical case

Geometry of Nonequilibrium Chemical Reaction Networks and Generalized Entropy Production Decompositions Preprint: APS/123-QED
Tetsuya J. Kobayashi, Dimitri Loutchko, Atsushi Kamimura, Yuki Sughiyama

Abstract
We derive the Hessian geometric structure of nonequilibrium chemical reaction networks (CRN) on the flux and force spaces induced by the Legendre duality of convex dissipation functions and characterize their dynamics as a generalized flow. With this structure, we can extend theories of nonequilibrium systems with quadratic dissipation functions to more general ones with nonquadratic ones, which are pivotal for studying chemical reaction networks. By applying generalized notions of orthogonality in Hessian geometry to chemical reaction networks, we obtain two generalized decompositions of the entropy production rate, each of which captures gradient-flow and minimum-dissipation aspects in nonequilibrium dynamics.



I
Introduction

Thermodynamics aims at establishing the general description of thermal systems.
Although such a description was obtained for equilibrium thermodynamics
Callen and Callen 1985
;
Sughiyama et al. 2021
, its extension to nonequilibrium systems has been limited to specific situations and models.
For near-equilibrium situations, Onsager and Machlup evaluated the entropy production rate using the linear approximation known as the force-flux relation
Onsager 1931a
;
Onsager 1931b
;
Onsager and Machlup 1953
;
Machlup and Onsager 1953
, which corresponds to a quadratic dissipation function.
With the recent development of macroscopic fluctuation theory and stochastic thermodynamics
Bertini et al. 2015
;
Taniguchi and Cohen 2007
;
Schmiedl and Seifert 2007
, this result was extended to far-from equilibrium situations in fluid dynamics and diffusion processes in a continuous space, but the dissipation functions are still quadratic even though they are generalized to be state dependent.
Those systems are characterized geometrically via the inner product structure induced by the quadratic functions and associated formal Riemannian geometry.
However, the knowledge obtained from such situations and models is not directly applicable to other systems with a discrete state space or with nonlinearities in the governing equations, where the dissipation functions are no longer quadratic.
In such cases, the inner product structure is no longer an adequate mathematical basis.
Although Wasserstein geometry has recently been introduced into the thermodynamics of diffusion processes as a new geometric approach
Aurell et al. 2011
;
Nakazato and Ito 2021
, it also employs the formal Riemannian geometric structure associated with the Wasserstein distance via the Benamou-Brenier formula and thereby is not directly applicable to systems with nonquadratic dissipation functions
Jordan et al. 1997
;
Benamou and Brenier 2000
;
Ambrosio et al. 2006
.

In this work, we clarify that this problem can be resolved by using the Hessian (information) geometric structure of the flux and force spaces induced by their nonlinear Legendre duality via convex (not necessarily quadratic) dissipation functions.
For systems with quadratic convex functions, the force and the corresponding flux are related by a linear transformation induced by the Riemannian metric.
Hessian geometry or information geometry
Shima 2007
;
Amari 2016
;
Nielsen 2020
enables us to relate them by a nonlinear Legendre transformation induced by the convex dissipation functions.
Because the Riemannian metric is regarded as the Legendre transformation with quadratic dissipation functions, Hessian geometry is a nonlinear extension of the inner product structure in Riemannian geometry.
Even with being a nonlinear extension, Hessian geometry still preserves many important aspects of the inner product structure in the form of generalized orthogonalities, Pythagorean theorem, among others
Amari 2016
;
Nielsen 2020
.
Therefore, we can naturally and consistently extend various previous results for systems with quadratic dissipation functions to those with nonquadratic ones.

We derive and demonstrate the structure by focusing mainly on chemical reaction networks (CRNs) because they are representative thermodynamic systems with both discrete state space and nonlinearity in the governing rate equations
Hill 2005
;
Schnakenberg 1976
;
Ge and Qian 2016
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
.
CRNs also include Markov jump processes (MJPs) on a graph, which is an important class of systems in stochastic thermodynamics with a discrete state space but with a linear governing equation
1
1

1

It should be noted that a linear governing equation does not necessarily mean that its dissipation functions are quadratic.
.
In addition, CRNs are also important in light of their biological and engineering applications
Beard and Qian 2008
;
Mikhailov and Ertl 2017
;
Alon 2019
.
The dissipation functions of these systems are not always quadratic, but are more general convex functions.

The nonquadratic property makes it difficult to dissect the equilibrium-like aspects from their nonequilibrium ones by the notion of orthogonality from the usual inner product structure, as was done for overdamped diffusion processes
Dechant et al. 2022
.
As a result, some entropy decomposition formulas have not yet been generalized to MJP and CNR.
By employing the generalized notions of orthogonality in Hessian geometry
Shima 2007
;
Amari 2016
;
Nielsen 2020
,
we demonstrate that different aspects of nonequilibrium CRNs can be dissected as generalized decompositions of the entropy production rate (EPR).
In particular, we derive a CRN version of the Maes-Netočnỳ relation by generalizing the Helmholtz decomposition.
If the dissipation functions are quadratic, these results are automatically and formally reduced to the original results obtained for diffusion processes.
Thus, Hessian geometry provides a natural generalization for resolving the problem.
Finally, we also discuss how Hessian geometry can potentially fill the gap between quadratic and nonquadratic cases by geometrically capturing the nonlinear dual relation between force and flux, unify the description of those systems, and thereby extend the applicability of nonequilibrium thermodynamics.
As such an example, we mention a relation of our results with the thermodynamic uncertainty relation, and describe how our formulation can contribute to network thermodynamics and variational characterizations of nonequilibrium systems.

This work is organized as follows. In sec.

In sec.

In sec.

In sec.

In sec.

In sec.

A reversible Markov jump process describes random jumps of noninteracting particles on a graph

\displaystyle j^{+}_{e}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle=k_{e}^{+}p_{\mathbb{v}^{+}(\mathbb{e}_{e})},

\displaystyle j^{-}_{e}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle=k_{e}^{-}p_{\mathbb{v}^{-}(\mathbb{e}_{e})},

(1)

where
\mathbb{v}^{+}(\mathbb{e}_{e})

\mathbb{e}_{e}

2

Then, the dynamics of the state vector

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{p}}=-B\bm{j}(\bm{p})=-\mathrm{div}\bm{j}(\bm{p}),

(2)

More specifically, for

\displaystyle b_{i,e}

\displaystyle:=+1

\displaystyle b_{i,e}

\displaystyle:=-1

\displaystyle b_{i,e}

\displaystyle:=0

Eq. (

.
We also define the head and tail incidence matrices, respectively, as

and

. Thus,

.

Then, the flux functions are compactly described in a vector form as

\displaystyle\bm{j}^{\pm}(\bm{p})=\bm{k}^{\pm}\circ(B^{\pm})^{T}\bm{p},

(3)

where

is the component-wise product of two vectors.
In this work, we assume that all edges describe reversible transitions, i.e.,

for all

While the representation of the master equation by Eq. (

II.2
Chemical Reaction Networks

Deterministic chemical reaction networks (CRN) are an important class of macroscopic thermodynamic models in light of their historical role played in thermodynamics since Gibbs
Gibbs 1878
and of their wide range of applications in engineering and biology

;
Mikhailov and Ertl 2017
;
Feinberg 2019
;
Alon 2019

A reversible CRN is composed of a collection of forward and reverse reaction pairs(Fig.
1
(A)), the

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,e}\mathbb{X}_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{N_{\mathbb{X}},e}\mathbb{X}_{N_{\mathbb{X}}}\rightarrow\beta_{1,e}\mathbb{X}_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{N_{\mathbb{X}},e}\mathbb{X}_{N_{\mathbb{X}}},

(4)

i

involved as the reactants and products of the
e
th reaction, respectively.
The stoichiometric vector of the
e
th forward reaction is defined as
\bm{s}_{e}:=(\beta_{1,e}-\alpha_{1,e},\cdots,\beta_{N,e}-\alpha_{N,e})^{T}
.
The stoichiometric matrix is

.
The stoichiometric vector of the
e

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{x}}=S\bm{j}(\bm{x})=-\mathrm{div}\bm{j}(\bm{x}).

The intrinsic graph structure of CRN can be manifested by considering the sets of reactants and products as vertices of the graph
\mathbb{G}
connected by the reaction edges (Fig.
1

Specifically, the set of reactants

Reflecting this hypergraph nature,

\displaystyle S=-\Gamma B=-\Gamma(B^{+}-B^{-}),

(6)

In vector form, we can compactly represent it as

\displaystyle\bm{j}^{\pm}(\bm{x})=\bm{k}^{\pm}\circ(B^{\pm})^{T}\bm{x}^{\Gamma^{T}},

where

IV.3
Generalized Hilbert orthogonality

The two generalized distances naturally lead to two generalized notions of orthogonality.

For any two force vectors
\bm{f}_{S},\bm{f}_{A}\in\mathcal{F}
, the first orthogonality is defined to satisfy

\displaystyle\Psi^{*}(\bm{f}_{S}+\bm{f}_{A})=\Psi^{*}(\bm{f}_{S}-\bm{f}_{A}).

(32)

This definition comes from the fact that, if
\bm{f}_{S}
and
\bm{f}_{A}
are orthogonal in an inner product space, then
\|\bm{f}_{S}+\bm{f}_{A}\|_{{M}^{*}}=\|\bm{f}_{S}-\bm{f}_{A}\|_{{M}^{*}}
holds.
We call this orthogonality a generalized Hilbert orthogonality or simply Hilbert orthogonality
Renger 2021
.
Then an orthogonal decomposition of a given force
\bm{f}
into
\bm{f}=\bm{f}_{S}+\bm{f}_{A}
is obtained by finding an iso-dissipation force
\bm{f}_{iso}
satisfying

\Psi^{*}(\bm{f})=\Psi^{*}(\bm{f}_{iso})
and by computing
\bm{f}_{S}
and
\bm{f}_{A}
as

\displaystyle\bm{f}_{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{f}+\bm{f}_{iso}}{2},

\displaystyle\bm{f}_{A}=\frac{\bm{f}-\bm{f}_{iso}}{2}.

(33)

Note that
\bm{f}_{iso}=\bm{f}_{S}-\bm{f}_{A}
and also that we can have different decompositions of
\bm{f}
by choosing different iso-dissipation forces
\bm{f}^{\prime}_{iso}
.
In addition, from the symmetry of
\Psi^{*}
, we have

\Psi^{*}(\bm{f})=\Psi^{*}(\bm{f}_{iso})=\Psi^{*}(-\bm{f}_{iso})=\Psi^{*}(-\bm{f})
.
As a consequence of the orthogonality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|\bm{j}_{iso}]=

\displaystyle 2\langle\bm{j},\bm{f}_{A}\rangle\geq 0,

(34)

where we use
\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|\bm{j}_{iso}]\geq 0
(see Appendix for a proof).
Similarly, by considering the symmetry of
\Psi^{*}(\bm{f})
, we also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\bm{\omega}}[\bm{j}\|-\bm{j}_{iso}]=2\langle\bm{j},\bm{f}_{S}\rangle\geq 0.

(35)

From these relations, we have a decomposition of
\langle\bm{j},\bm{f}\rangle
into two nonnegative terms:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{j},\bm{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{j},\bm{f}_{S}\rangle+\langle\bm{j},\bm{f}_{A}\rangle

(36)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|-\bm{j}_{iso}]}{2}+\frac{\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|\bm{j}_{iso}]}{2}.

(37)

Because
\langle\bm{j},\bm{f}\rangle
is proportional to the EPR (Eq. (
10
)), this decomposition provides a way to obtain nonnegative decompositions of the EPR.
It should be noted that we have infinitely many decompositions of this type.
In addition, by choosing
\bm{j}_{iso}=\bm{j}
, the decomposition reduces to the fluctuation relation
\dot{\Sigma}=\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|-\bm{j}]
, where we used
\bm{j}=\bm{j}_{iso}
.
This orthogonality has been employed in the MFT
Renger 2018
;
Renger and Zimmer 2021
;
Patterson et al. 2021
to characterize the quasi-potential and the gradient flow aspects of equilibrium and nonequilibrium systems as we see in the next section.
The iso-dissipation hypersurface is known as a level surface of the convex function in Hessian geometry
Shima 2007
and as a central affine surface in affine differential geometry
Nomizu 1994
.
In both cases, it works as a central geometric object.
Moreover, this central affine surface also plays an important role in isobaric thermodynamics, where volume can change in conjunction with reactions
Sughiyama et al. 2022

The Hilbert orthogonality is also defined in the flux space using
\Psi(\bm{j})
similarly to
\Psi(\bm{j}_{A}+\bm{j}_{S})=\Psi(\bm{j}_{A}-\bm{j}_{S})
while we do not use it in this work.
It should be noted that the orthogonality of
\bm{f}_{A}
and
\bm{f}_{S}

\bm{j}_{A}
and
\bm{j}_{S}

IV.4
Information geometric orthogonality

The second orthogonality comes from the information geometry
Shima 2007
;
Amari 2016
;
Nielsen 2020
.
For three flux vectors
\bm{j},\bm{j}^{\prime},\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{J}
satisfying
\bm{j}=\bm{j}^{\prime}+\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}
, the Bregman divergence
\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|\bm{0}]
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|\bm{0}]=\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}]+\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}\|\bm{0}]+\langle\bm{j},\bm{f}^{\prime\prime}\rangle,

(38)

where
\bm{f}^{\prime\prime}
is the Legendre dual of
\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}
.
The second orthogonality of
\bm{j}^{\prime}
and
\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}
is defined by
\langle\bm{j}^{\prime},\bm{f}^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0
.
Then the following generalized Pythagorean theorem (GPT) holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|\bm{0}]=\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}\|\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}]+\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}\|\bm{0}].

(39)

For quadratic dissipation functions, the GPT is reduced to the conventional Pythagorean theorem:

\displaystyle\|\bm{j}\|^{2}_{{M}}=\|\bm{j}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{{M}}+\|\bm{j}^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{{M}}.

(40)

Thus, Eq. (
39
) is a generalization of the usual orthogonality.
We call this orthogonality the information geometric orthogonality.
Similarly, for
\bm{f}=\bm{f}^{\dagger}+\bm{f}^{\ddagger}
, the dual orthogonality between
\bm{f}^{\dagger},\bm{f}^{\ddagger}\in\mathcal{F}
is also defined by
\langle\bm{j}^{\dagger},\bm{f}^{\ddagger}\rangle=0
, leading to the dual version of GPT:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{*}[\bm{f}\|\bm{0}]=\mathcal{D}^{*}[\bm{f}\|\bm{f}^{\dagger}]+\mathcal{D}^{*}[\bm{f}^{\dagger}\|\bm{0}].

(41)

The Bregman divergence and the information geometric orthogonality are the central geometric tools in information geometry for analyzing manifolds of statistical models and for conducting statistically meaningful projections onto submanifolds
Amari 2016
;
Nielsen 2020
.
In addition, they have also been recently employed in the thermodynamics of diffusion processes, MJP, and CRN
Ito 2018
;
Kolchinsky and Wolpert 2021
;
Ohga and Ito 2021
;
Kobayashi et al. 2021
;
Sughiyama et al. 2022
;
Sughiyama et al. 2022
.
However, these works investigate the information and Hessian geometric structures of state space, i.e., the space of the probability vector
\bm{p}
or the concentration vector
\bm{x}
, in which the thermodynamic potential functions such as the Gibbs free energy work as the convex potential function inducing the Hessian structure.
As a result, the Hessian structure of the state space captures the energetic or equilibrium aspects of the systems.
In contrast, the Hessian structure of the flux-force space in this work captures the kinetic and nonequilibrium aspects of the systems.

It should be noted that the Hilbert and information geometric orthogonalities are reduced to usual orthogonality when the dissipation functions are quadratic (see also Appendix ).

and the stoichiometric compatibility class
\mathcal{P}(\bm{x}_{0})
as
\bm{x}_{cb}=\mathcal{M}_{cb}(\bm{K}_{cb},\bm{\kappa}_{cb})\cap\mathcal{P}(\bm{x}_{0})

Craciun et al. 2009
;
Kobayashi et al. 2021
.

The CB state inherits important properties of the equilibrium state such as the uniqueness, global stability, and the gradient-flow-like aspect
Craciun et al. 2009
;
Ge and Qian 2016
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
;
Kobayashi et al. 2021
.
By definition, an EQ state is a CB state, but not vice versa.
Additionally, the steady states of MJP always satisfy the CB condition because
B\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x};\bm{K},\bm{\kappa})=\bm{0}
is nothing but the steady state condition for MJP (Eq. (
2
)).
Thus, MJP are unconditionally complex balanced.
In contrast, CRN are not always complex balanced depending on the parameter value.

Using
\bm{x}_{cb}
, the force
\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})
is represented as

\displaystyle\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}S^{T}\partial_{\bm{x}}\varphi_{cb}(\bm{x})+\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\bm{K}_{cb}}{\tilde{\bm{K}}_{eq}},

(54)

where
\tilde{\bm{K}}_{eq}:=S^{T}\ln\bm{x}_{cb}
and
\varphi_{cb}(\bm{x}):=\mathcal{D}_{KL}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}_{cb}]
.
Let
\bm{f}_{S}(\bm{x}):=-\frac{1}{2}S^{T}\partial_{\bm{x}}\varphi_{cb}(\bm{x})
and
\bm{f}_{A}:=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\bm{K}_{cb}}{\tilde{\bm{K}}_{eq}}
.
Then, for all
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, the generalized Hilbert orthogonality holds (Fig.
4
):

\displaystyle\Psi^{*}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{S}(\bm{x})+\bm{f}_{A})=\Psi^{*}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{S}(\bm{x})-\bm{f}_{A}),

(55)

because of the CB condition
B\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}_{cb})=\bm{0}

Renger 2018
;
Renger and Zimmer 2021
;
Patterson et al. 2021
(see Appendix for a proof).
Thus, Eq. (
36
) admits a nonnegative decomposition of the EPR:

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}(\bm{x})=\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}^{GF}(\bm{x})+\dot{\Sigma}_{hk}^{GF}(\bm{x}),

(56)

where
\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}^{GF}(\bm{x}):=2\langle\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}),\bm{f}_{S}(\bm{x})\rangle
and
\dot{\Sigma}_{hk}^{GF}(\bm{x}):=2\langle\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}),\bm{f}_{A}\rangle
.
Owing to this nonnegative decomposition,
\varphi_{cb}(\bm{x})
works as a Lyapunov function (quasi-potential):

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi_{cb}(\bm{x}_{t})}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}^{GF}(\bm{x}_{t})=\mathcal{D}[\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}_{t})\|\bm{f}_{iso}(\bm{x}_{t})]\geq 0,

and
\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}^{GF}(\bm{x})
behaves like an excess EPR, i.e.,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}^{GF}(\bm{x}_{t})=0
.
Thus,
\varphi_{cb}(\bm{x})
as a Lyapunov function guarantees the stability of the CB state
\bm{x}_{cb}
and the gradient-flow-like property of the CB dynamics.

This decomposition is equivalent to those proposed for CRN in
Ge and Qian 2016
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
as a generalization of the Hatano-Sasa decomposition
Hatano and Sasa 2001
.
Therefore, this result is just a reinterpretation of the previous ones.
However, owing to the geometric perspective endowed by Hessian geometry, the non-negativity of
\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}^{GF}(\bm{x})
and
\dot{\Sigma}_{hk}^{GF}(\bm{x})
becomes transparent, the proof of which in previous works required more technical and less transparent computations
Ge and Qian 2016
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
.

Information geometric orthogonality and Maes-Netočnỳ decomposition

While the gradient flow aspect and associated EPR decomposition by the quasi-potential have been investigated in CRN theory, MFT, and applied mathematics
Ge and Qian 2016
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
;
Renger and Zimmer 2021
;
Craciun et al. 2009
, the decomposition does not capture other important thermodynamic aspects such as minimum dissipation or minimum entropy production principles.
In addition, the Hilbert orthogonality for
\varphi_{cb}(\bm{x})
(Eq. (
55
)) holds only when the CB condition is satisfied.
In CRN, we can have non-complex-balanced steady states depending on the parameter values.

As another type of the EPR decomposition, we have Maes-Netočnỳ decomposition, which generalizes the Komatsu-Nakagawa-Sasa-Tasaki approach
Maes and Netočný 2014
;
Komatsu et al. 2008
.
Moreover, its geometric interpretation for overdamped diffusion processes was recently provided in terms of the formal Riemannian geometry of the flux-force space
Dechant et al. 2022
.
Its extension to MJP and CRN has yet to be achieved because of their nonquadratic dissipation functions.
We show that the information geometric orthogonality is central to this extension.

In the Maes-Netočnỳ decomposition, for a given
\bm{x}_{t}
14
14

14

\bm{x}_{t}
is not necessarily a steady state here.
, the non-equilibrium flux
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})
is decomposed into the quasi-steady flux
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
and the remaining part as
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})=\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})+(\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})-\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x}))
.
The quasi-steady flux
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
is the flux that makes a given state
\bm{x}
steady obtained by modulating the conserved force, i.e., being generated by the gradient of potential force in the case of overdamped diffusion.
This means that
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})\in\mathrm{Ker}[\mathrm{div}]
and
\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})-\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})\in\mathrm{Im}[\mathrm{grad}]
hold.
Furthermore,
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
is also characterized as the minimum EPR flux under the constraint of
\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})-\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})\in\mathrm{Im}[\mathrm{grad}]

Maes and Netočný 2014
.
From the viewpoint of vector calculus,
the decomposition is a special case of the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition
Bhatia et al. 2013
.
With the the Riemannian inner product structure between
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{F}
for overdamped diffusion, we can also regard
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
as the orthogonal projection of
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})
onto
\mathrm{Ker}[\mathrm{div}]
along
\mathrm{Im}[\mathrm{grad}]
.
Because the Maes-Netočnỳ decomposition is tightly linked to the orthogonality in the inner product space, it is not trivial how to extend the decomposition to other systems such as CRN (and MJP) with nonquadratic dissipation functions.
We show how information-geometric orthogonality can resolve this problem.

First, we define a linear affine subspace
\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j})
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j})=\left\{\bm{j}^{\prime}|\bm{j}-\bm{j}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Ker}S\right\}\subset\mathcal{J}.

(57)

Because
\bm{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j})
satisfies
S\bm{j}=S\bm{j}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j})
is the set of the fluxes that induces the same instantaneous velocity
\dot{\bm{x}}
as
\bm{j}
.
Thus, we call
\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j})
an iso-velocity subspace in
\mathcal{J}
.
Because
\dot{\bm{x}}=0
for the steady state flux, we also denote
\mathcal{P}^{v}_{st}:=\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{0})
as the steady state (zero-velocity) subspace.

As the complementary subspace of
\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j})
, we also define a linear affine subspace
\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{f})\subset\mathcal{F}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{f}):=\left\{\bm{f}^{\prime}|\bm{f}-\bm{f}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Im}S^{T}\right\}.

(58)

Because
S^{T}
is the gradient of a CRN, i.e.,
\mathrm{grad}=S^{T}
,
\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{f})
is the subspace obtained by shifting
\mathrm{Im}[\mathrm{grad}]=\mathrm{Im}[S^{T}]
by
\bm{f}
.
We call
\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{f})
an nonequilibrium force (NEF) subspace in
\mathcal{F}
if
\bm{f}\notin\mathrm{Im}S^{T}
and the equilibrium force (EF) subspace if
\bm{f}\in\mathrm{Im}S^{T}
because, if the equilibrium condition is satisfied,
\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})
is always in
\mathrm{Im}[S^{T}]
as in Eq. (
48
).
We denote the EF subspace by
\mathcal{P}^{f}_{eq}:=\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{0})
.

To obtain the generalized version of
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
, we transform
\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{f})
and
\mathcal{P}^{f}_{eq}
to
\mathcal{J}
via the Legendre transformation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f})

\displaystyle:=\partial\Psi^{*}_{\bm{x}}\left[\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{f})\right]\subset\mathcal{J},

(59)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x},eq}

\displaystyle:=\partial\Psi^{*}_{\bm{x}}\left[\mathcal{P}^{f}_{eq}\right]\subset\mathcal{J}.

(60)

It should be noted that
\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f})
depends on the current state
\bm{x}
of the system even though
\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{f})
does not because the Legendre transformation is dependent on
\bm{x}
via the activity
\bm{\omega}(\bm{x})
.
From the definition,
\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f})
and
\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x},eq}
are the set of nonequilibrium and equilibrium fluxes obtained by modulating the gradient force.

Then the generalized quasi-steady flux
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
is obtained by the intersection of
\mathcal{P}^{v}_{st}
and
\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}))
:

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{P}^{v}_{st}\cap\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})).

(61)

For the case of overdamped diffusion processes, the Legendre transformation of the corresponding quadratic dissipation function is linear and, therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}))
becomes a flat subspace.
The existence and uniqueness of
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
follow from the linear algebra.
However, for the generalized
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
, the existence and uniqueness of
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
are not guaranteed
a priori
, because
\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}))
is a curved manifold as shown in Fig.
5
(A).
Nonetheless,
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
in Eq. (
61
) exists uniquely.
More generally, for any
\bm{j}^{\prime}
and
\bm{f}^{\prime\prime}
, the following intersection is unique and transversal (see Appendix for proof):

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{int}(\bm{j}^{\prime},\bm{f}^{\prime\prime})=\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j}^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}^{\prime\prime}).

(62)

This result generalizes the uniqueness of the intersection of complementary subspaces under an inner product structure.
This is one of the notable properties of Hessian geometry.
In information geometry and Hessian geometry,
\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j})
and
\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f})
are called dual flat spaces
Amari 2016
;
Nielsen 2020
;
Shima 2007
.

By virtue of the uniqueness, we obtain a unique decomposition of the flux
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})
:

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})=\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})+(\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})-\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})).

(63)

Because
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})\in\mathrm{Ker}S
and
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}))
where
\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}))=\partial_{\bm{j}}\Psi^{*}_{\bm{x}}[\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})+\mathrm{Im}[S^{T}]]
,

\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
is the steady flux that is obtained by modulating the nonequilibrium force
\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})
by adding the gradient equilibrium force.
We also have
\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})-\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})\in\mathrm{Im}S^{T}
.
Thus, Eq. (
63
) is a generalization of Maes-Netočnỳ decomposition and also of Helmholtz decomposition.
Moreover, from
\langle\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x}),\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})-\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})\rangle=0
, we have the generalized Pythagorean relation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}^{*}[\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})\|\bm{0}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}^{*}[\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})\|\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})]+\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}^{*}[\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})\|\bm{0}],

(64)

which is further reduced to a decomposition of the dual dissipation function:

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{x}}^{*}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}^{*}[\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})\|\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})]+\Psi_{\bm{x}}^{*}(\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})).

(65)

Then,
\bm{f}_{st}
is characterized variationally as (see Appendix ):

\displaystyle\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bm{f}\in\mathcal{P}^{f}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}))}\Psi_{\bm{x}}^{*}(\bm{f}).

(66)

This variational formula indicates that
\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})
is the minimum dissipation force that can be obtained by tilting the nonequilibrium force
\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})
with the equilibrium gradient force.
Thus,
\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x})
is the minimum dissipation (MD) steady state flux.
For quadratic cases where the equivalence of the dissipation functions and the EPR holds (Eq. (
63
)), the result reduces to the minimum entropy production principle.

However, for nonquadratic cases, Eq. (
65
) alone does not provide a relevant EPR decomposition because EPR is not determined only by
\Psi_{\bm{x}}^{*}(\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}))
.
To resolve the problem, we consider the dual decomposition of
\bm{j}_{0}
as shown in Fig.
5
(A):

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})=\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})+(\bm{j}(\bm{x})-\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})),

(67)

where we define
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})
as

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x}):=\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}))\cap\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x},eq}.

(68)

Because
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}))
, the flux
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})
induces the same instantaneous velocity
\dot{\bm{x}}
as
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})
.
Moreover, because
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x},eq}
, it is the flux induced by an equilibrium force, i.e., a pure gradient force.
Thus,
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})
is the equilibrium flux that induces the same dynamics as
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})
.
It should be noted that
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})
is generally induced by a nonequilibrium force.
In addition, we have
\bm{f}_{eq}(\bm{x})\in\mathrm{Im}S^{T}
and

\bm{j}(\bm{x})-\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})\in\mathrm{Ker}S
from
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{M}^{f}_{\bm{x},eq}
and
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}))
, respectively.
Thus, Eq. (
67
) is another generalization of Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition of the flux
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})
.
Moreover, owing to
\langle\bm{j}(\bm{x})-\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x}),\bm{f}_{eq}(\bm{x})\rangle=0
, we have the Pythagorean relation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}[\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})\|\bm{0}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}[\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})\|\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})]+\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}[\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x});\bm{0}],

(69)

which is reduced to a decomposition of the primal dissipation function:

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}[\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})\|\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})]+\Psi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})).

(70)

From this,
\bm{j}_{eq}
can be characterized variationally (see Appendix for proof):

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bm{j}\in\mathcal{P}^{v}(\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}))}\Psi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{j}).

(71)

This variational formula means that, among all fluxes that induce the same velocity as
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})
, the equilibrium flux
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})
is the flux that minimizes the primal dissipation function. Thus,
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})
is the MD flux.

By combining Eq. (
70
) and Eq. (
65
),
we have an EPR decomposition (see Appendix for proof):

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}(\bm{x})=\dot{\Sigma}_{hk}^{MD}(\bm{x})+\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}^{MD}(\bm{x})

(72)

where
we define housekeeping and excess EPR as

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{hk}^{MD}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle:=2\left[\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}[\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x})\|\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x})]+\Psi^{*}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x}))\right]\geq 0,

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}^{MD}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle:=2\left[\mathcal{D}_{\bm{x}}^{*}[\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x})\|\bm{f}_{st}(\bm{x})]+\Psi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x}))\right]\geq 0.

If the state
\bm{x}(t)
converges to a steady state
\bm{x}_{st}
15
15

15

\bm{x}_{st}
is not necessarily a CB state but can be a general steady state
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{\Sigma}_{hk}(\bm{x}(t))

\displaystyle=\dot{\Sigma}_{hk}(\bm{x}_{st})=2\langle\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}_{st}),\bm{f}_{0}(\bm{x}_{st})\rangle

(73)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}(\bm{x}(t))

\displaystyle=\dot{\Sigma}_{ex}(\bm{x}_{st})=0,

(74)

where we used
\bm{j}_{0}(\bm{x}_{st})=\bm{j}_{st}(\bm{x}_{st})
and
\bm{j}_{eq}(\bm{x}_{st})=\bm{0}

Thus, this EPR decomposition geometrically generalizes the Maes-Netočnỳ one to MJP and CRN and clarifies its dualistic minimum dissipation principles.
If the state
\bm{x}(t)
converges to a CB state
\bm{x}_{cb}

VII
Summary and Discussion

We have clarified that Hessian geometry is the natural geometric structure of nonequilibrium and nonlinear CRNs as well as MJPs.
More generally, it can capture the geometry induced by nonquadratic convex dissipation functions.
By employing generalized notions of orthogonality, various aspects of nonequilibrium dynamics are dissected as decompositions of EPRs, which generalize the well-established ones
Maes and Netočný 2014
;
Komatsu et al. 2008
;
Dechant et al. 2022
to CRNs and MJPs.

VII.1
Thermodynamic uncertainty relation and Fisher information

The nonquadratic property of the dissipation functions of CRN and MJP also appears implicitly in different problems of thermodynamics.
One such example is thermodynamic uncertainty relation (TUR) and its extension to MJP.
TUR is a relation that bounds the fluctuation of a generalized current in a nonequilibrium steady state by the entropy production
Shiraishi 2021
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{J}_{d}^{ss}]^{2}}{\mathbb{Var}[\mathcal{J}_{d}^{ss}]}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\dot{\Sigma}(t^{\prime})\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

(78)

where
\mathcal{J}_{d}^{ss}
is a time-integrated generalized current at the steady state, and
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{J}_{d}^{ss}]
and
\mathbb{Var}[\mathcal{J}_{d}^{ss}]
are the mean and variance of
\mathcal{J}_{d}^{ss}
.
Since the proposal of the TUR conjecture and its first proof
Gingrich et al. 2016
;
Barato and Seifert 2015
, the TUR has been extended to various models and situations, including MJP
Shiraishi 2021
and CRN
Yoshimura and Ito 2021
.
However, even though Eq. (
78
) is tight for overdamped diffusion processes, it is not tight for MJP.
Instead, for MJP, the fluctuation of the current is bounded tightly by the pseudo entropy production(pEPR)
Yoshimura and Ito 2021
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{J}_{d}^{ss}]^{2}}{\mathbb{Var}[\mathcal{J}_{d}^{ss}]}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\dot{\Pi}(t^{\prime})\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

(79)

where the pEPR is defined as

\displaystyle\dot{\Pi}(t):=\frac{(\bm{j}^{+}(\bm{x}_{t})-\bm{j}^{-}(\bm{x}_{t}))^{2}}{\bm{j}^{+}(\bm{x}_{t})+\bm{j}^{+}(\bm{x}_{t})}.

(80)

and
\dot{\Pi}(t)\leq\dot{\Sigma}(t)
holds
Shiraishi 2021
.
We here mention that this gap between the EPR and pEPR is linked to the Fisher information of the dissipation function.
The Fisher information matrix (or metric) for a strictly convex function
\phi^{*}(\bm{f})
is defined by its Hessian as

\displaystyle G_{e,e^{\prime}}^{*}(\bm{f}):=\frac{\partial^{2}\phi^{*}(\bm{f})}{\partial f_{e}\partial f_{e^{\prime}}}.

(81)

For
\phi^{*}(\bm{f})=\Psi_{\bm{\omega}}^{*}(\bm{f})
defined by Eq. (
12
), we have

\displaystyle G^{*}(\bm{\omega},\bm{f})=\mathrm{diag}[\bm{\omega}\circ\cosh(\bm{f})]=(\bm{j}^{+}+\bm{j}^{-}),

(82)

where we used Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
11
). For
\phi(\bm{j})=\Psi_{\bm{\omega}}(\bm{j})
, we have the Fisher information matrix:

\displaystyle G(\bm{\omega},\bm{j})=G^{*}(\bm{\omega},\bm{f})^{-1}=\frac{1}{\bm{j}^{+}+\bm{j}^{-}}.

(83)

Then, the pEPR is represented as

\displaystyle\dot{\Pi}(\bm{j})=\langle\bm{j},G(\bm{\omega},\bm{j})\bm{j}\rangle,

(84)

which allows us to regard
\dot{\Pi}(t)
as an approximation of the EPR by replacing the actual force
\bm{f}
associated with
\bm{j}
with a pseudo-force
\bm{f}^{p}:=G(\bm{\omega},\bm{j})\bm{j}
.
If the dissipation function is quadratic, the actual force
\bm{f}
and the pseudo-force
\bm{f}^{p}
coincide.
Thus, the non-tightness of the TUR for MJP is a manifestation of the nonquadratic nature of the dissipation functions.

VII.2
Network thermodynamics

The information geometric decomposition that we introduced is tightly related to the network thermodynamics
Oster et al. 1971
;
Hill 2005
;
Schnakenberg 1976
;
Schnakenberg 2012
;
van der Schaft et al. 2013
.
The network thermodynamics is an attempt to extend the methodology of network theory developed mainly for linear electric circuits to other physical systems
Belevitch 1962
;
Chen 2012
.
While the linear network theory successfully works for linear electric circuits, which have a linear force and flux relation (Ohm’s law) and resulting quadratic dissipation functions, its application to MJP and CRN has encountered difficulties due to the nonlinear relation between force and flux, especially when we evaluate the EPR decomposition for a transient and far from equilibrium state.
The Hessian geometric structure and the generalized decompositions in this work are extensions of those in network thermodynamics. Specifically, the information geometric decomposition is a nonlinear generalization of the cycle-cocycle decomposition
Wachtel et al. 2015
.
However, network thermodynamics and network theory accommodate a wide variety of methods that are not yet exploited in this work
Altaner et al. 2012
;
Wachtel et al. 2015
.
For example, the algebraic structure underlying the network is explored by using the integral basis defined by cycles, cords, and spanning trees in the network
Schnakenberg 1976
;
Polettini 2015
.
As an application, the steady EPR is related to cyclic fluxes and affinities in the network.
We may incorporate and exploit the algebraic structure of MJP and CRN more explicitly into our framework.

VII.3
Duality and variational characterizations in thermodynamics

The notion of duality is the core of thermodynamics.
In equilibrium thermodynamics, the duality of extensive and intensive variables induced by thermodynamic potential functions characterizes the energetic aspect of physical systems.
The roles and differences of dual pairs of thermodynamic potential functions are well recognized.
In addition, Hessian geometry is central to comprehend the geometric properties of equilibrium thermodynamics
Kobayashi et al. 2021
;
Sughiyama et al. 2021
.
For the duality between the force and flux explored in this work,
the dual dissipation functions have been recognized for a while in MFT
Bertini et al. 2015
and metric gradient flow theory
Ambrosio et al. 2006
.
Nevertheless, nonquadratic dissipation functions have been investigated only very recently even in MFT
Kaiser et al. 2018
;
Renger 2018
;
Renger and Zimmer 2021
;
Patterson et al. 2021
and also in other physics communities
Suzuki 2012
.
As in the case of equilibrium thermodynamics, grasping the roles played by the dual functions should be essential for understanding nonequilibrium and kinetic aspects of thermodynamics.
Hence, Hessian geometry becomes an indispensable tool for investigating the geometry induced by the duality and also the variational aspects of nonequilibrium phenomena.
In thermodynamics, there exist continued attempts to characterize nonequilibrium states and relations variationally, which go by the names of minimum entropy production principle
Prigogine 1968
;
Klein and Meijer 1954
;
Polettini 2011
, maximum entropy production principle
Županović et al. 2004
;
Martyushev 2021
, the least dissipation principle
Onsager and Machlup 1953
;
Gyarmati 2013
, and others.
However, all these principles still have limitations in their applicability for nonlinear and far from equilibrium situations
Polettini 2013
.
Hessian geometry and also information geometry, which has an ability to handle nonlinearity induced by convex functions, may contribute to resolving a part of problems in such principles.
Moreover, they can serve as the natural language to integrate the equilibrium (energetic) and nonequilibrium (kinetic) descriptions, and thereby provide us a more universal understanding of thermodynamic systems and extend the applicability of nonequilibrium thermodynamics.

Introduction

In 2019, Peter B. Denton, Stephen J. Parke, Terence Tao and Xining Zhang
[
8
]
found the legendary eigenvector-eigenvalue identity and showed how this identity can also be used to extract the relative phases between the components of any given eigenvector. In this paper, we will prove several conjectural identities of Zhi-Wei Sun on determinants and permanents of matrices involving roots of unity. We will extract the eigenvalues of the minors
M_{j}(1\leq j\leq n)
of the Hermitian matrix
A
from the eigenvectors of
A
by virtue of the eigenvector-eigenvalue identity. So it can been seen as extracting eigenvalues from eigenvectors.

Conjecture 1.1

(Zhi-Wei Sun, Conjecture 4.3 of
[
6
]
)
.

Let
n>1
be a integer and
\zeta
a primitive
n
-th root of unity.

(1)

If
n
is a even number, then

\sum_{\tau\in D(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{1-\zeta^{j-\tau(j)}}=\dfrac{\left(\left(n-1\right)!!\right)^{2}}{2^{n}}=\dfrac{n!}{4^{n}}\binom{n}{n/2},

(1.1)

(2)

if
n
is an odd number, then

\sum_{\tau\in D(n-1)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{1-\zeta^{j-\tau(j)}}=\dfrac{1}{n}\left(\dfrac{n-1}{2}!\right)^{2},

(1.2)

and

\sum_{\tau\in D(n-1)}\operatorname{sign}(\tau)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{1-\zeta^{j-\tau(j)}}=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{n}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}!\right)^{2},

(1.3)

where
D(n-1)
is the set of all derangements
\tau
of indices
j=1,\ldots,n-1
such that
\tau(j)\neq j
for all
j=1,\ldots,n-1
.

2.
Eigenvalues from eigenvectors

In this section, we will prove the equation (
1.3
).
The left hand side of (
1.3
) is the determinant of an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
sub-matrix of the
n\times n
matrix
A=(a_{jk})
such that

a_{jk}=\left(1-\delta_{jk}\right)\left\{1+i\cot\frac{(j-k)\pi}{n}\right\}
, where
\delta_{jk}=1
if
j=k
; and
0
if
j\neq k
. The following theorem gives the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
A
.

Theorem 2.1

(F. Calogero and A.M. Perelomov, Theorem 1 of
[
1
]
)
.

The off-diagonal hermitian matrix of order
n
whose elements are defined by the formula

a_{jk}=\left(1-\delta_{jk}\right)\left\{1+i\cot\frac{(j-k)\pi}{n}\right\}

has the integer eigenvalues

\lambda_{i}=2i-n-1,\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n

and the corresponding eigenvectors
v_{i}
with norm
1
have components

v_{i,j}=\exp\left(-\frac{2\pi{\rm{i}}ij}{n}\right)/\sqrt{n},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n.

If
A
is an
n\times n
Hermitian matrix, then we denote its
n
real eigenvalues by
\lambda_{1}(A),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(A)
. We can find an orthonormal basis
v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
v_{i}
is associated to
\lambda_{i}(A),1\leq i\leq n
. For any
i,j=1,\ldots,n
, let
v_{i,j}
denote the
j^{\text{th }}
component of
v_{i}.

If
1\leq j\leq n
, let
M_{j}
denote the
(n-1)\times(n-1)
minor formed from
A
by deleting the
j^{\text{th }}
row and column from
A
. This is also a Hermitian matrix, and thus has
n-1
real eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\left(M_{j}\right),\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}\left(M_{j}\right)
.

Theorem 2.2

(Eigenvector-eigenvalue identity, Theorem 1 of
[
8
]
)
.

With the notation as above, we have

\left|v_{i,j}\right|^{2}\prod_{k=1;k\neq i}^{n}\left(\lambda_{i}(A)-\lambda_{k}(A)\right)=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\lambda_{i}(A)-\lambda_{k}\left(M_{j}\right)\right).

(2.1)

Proof of Equation (
1.3
):

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\zeta^{j-k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1+\zeta^{j-k}}{1-\zeta^{j-k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+{\rm{i}}\cot\frac{\pi(j-k)}{n}\right).

Let
A=(A_{jk})
be the
n\times n
Hermitian matrix defined in Theorem
2.1
. By Theorem
2.1
, the eigenvalues of
A
are

\lambda_{1}=n-1,\lambda_{2}=n-3,\cdots,\lambda_{i}=n+1-2i,\cdots,\lambda_{n}=1-n.

(2.2)

Let
i=\frac{n+1}{2}
and
j=n
. Then
\lambda_{i}=0
. By Theorem
2.2
,

\left|v_{i,n}\right|^{2}\prod_{k=1;k\neq i}^{n}\left(-\lambda_{k}(A)\right)=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(-\lambda_{k}\left(M_{n}\right)\right).

By Theorem
2.1
,
\left|v_{s,n}\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{n}
. Hence

|M_{n}|={\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}\left(M_{n}\right)}=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}((n-1)!!)^{2}}{n}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in D(n-1)}\operatorname{sign}(\tau)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-\zeta^{j-\tau(j)}}

\displaystyle=2^{1-n}|M_{n}|=\frac{2^{1-n}(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}((n-1)!!)^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{n}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}!\right)^{2}.

Hence Equation (
1.3
) is proved.
∎

\operatorname{det}\left(M_{n,1}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}!\right)^{2}.

Let
f(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(x-\lambda_{k}\left(M_{j}\right)\right)

f(\lambda_{i}(A))=f(n+1-2i)=\frac{1}{2n}\prod_{k=1;k\neq i}^{n}\left(k-i\right),\ 1\leq i\leq n.

3.
The permanents of matrices

In this section, we will prove equation (
1.1
) and equation (
1.2
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n
be an integer greater than
2
, and
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
pairwise distinct complex numbers.
Let
L(n)
be the set of all elements
\tau\in S(n)
such that
\tau
is an
n
-cycle. Then

\sum_{\tau\in L(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x_{\tau(j)}-x_{j}}=0.

Proof.

For
\tau_{1}=(1\ a_{2}\ a_{3}\ \cdots\ a_{n}),\tau_{2}=(1\ b_{2}\ b_{3}\ \cdots\ b_{n})\in L(n)
, define
\tau_{1}\sim\tau_{2}
if and only if
(a_{2}\ a_{3}\ \cdots\ a_{n})=(b_{2}\ b_{3}\ \cdots\ b_{n})\in L(n-1)
. One can easily see that
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
L(n)
. Obviously, there are
n-1
elements in each equivalence class.
Take an arbitrary equivalence class

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=(1\ a_{2}\ a_{3}\ \cdots\ a_{n-1}\ a_{n}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=(1\ a_{3}\ a_{4}\ \cdots\ a_{n}\ a_{2}),

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \vdots

\displaystyle\sigma_{n-1}

\displaystyle=(1\ a_{n}\ a_{2}\ \cdots\ a_{n-2}\ a_{n-1}).

Then we just need to show
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x_{\sigma_{i}(j)}-x_{j}}=0
. Let
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x_{\sigma_{i}(j)}-x_{j}}
, obviously,
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
and

\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(x_{1}-x_{a_{2}})(x_{1}-x_{a_{n}})}{x_{a_{2}}-x_{a_{n}}}\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{a_{i+1}}}-\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{a_{i}}}\right),&i\neq 1,\\
1,&i=1.\end{array}\right.

Thus
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}=1+\frac{(x_{1}-x_{a_{2}})(x_{1}-x_{a_{n}})}{x_{a_{2}}-x_{a_{n}}}\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{a_{n}}}-\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{a_{2}}}\right)=0
. Hence we have
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}=0.
This complete the proof of lemma
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and
x
a complex number such that
x^{k}\neq 1
for any positive integer
k<n
. Assume

A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}=\displaystyle\left((1-\delta_{ij})\left(1-\frac{1}{1-x^{i-j}}\right)\right)_{n\times n},

and

B=(b_{ij})\in M_{(n-k)\times(n-k)}(\mathbb{C})

be the sub-matrix obtained from
A
by deleting the
s_{1}th,\cdots,s_{k}th
columns and the

s_{1}th,\cdots,s_{k}th
rows, where
k=0
or
2\leqslant k<n,\ 1\leqslant s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{k}\leqslant n
.

(1)

If
n-k
is an odd number, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(n-k)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-k}b_{j\tau(j)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(n-k)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-k}b_{j\tau(j)}=0,

(2)

If
n-k
is a even number, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(n-k)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=(-1)^{\frac{n-k}{2}+1}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-k}b_{j\tau(j)}=0.

Proof.

First, let’s check this theorem when
\ell=n-k=1,2,3.
If
\ell=1
, then
B=0
and (1) holds; if
\ell=2
, then
B
looks like

\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{1-x^{-1}}\\
\frac{1}{1-x}&0\end{pmatrix},x\neq 0.

Since there is only a odd permutation in
D(2)
, thus (2) holds; if
\ell=3,
then
B
looks like

xy\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{x-1}&\frac{1}{y-1}\\
\frac{1}{1-x}&0&\frac{1}{y-x}\\
\frac{1}{1-y}&\frac{1}{x-y}&0\\
\end{pmatrix},x,y\neq 1\ \text{and}\ x\neq y.

Since there are only two odd permutations
(1\ 2\ 3)
,
(1\ 3\ 2)
in
D(3)
and

\frac{1}{x-1}\cdot\frac{1}{y-x}\cdot\frac{1}{1-y}+\frac{1}{y-1}\cdot\frac{1}{1-x}\cdot\frac{1}{x-y}=0.

This implies that (1) holds

If
\ell\geqslant 4
is odd, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(\ell)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}b_{j\tau(j)}=x_{1}\cdots x_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(\ell)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{x_{\tau(j)}-x_{j}},

where
x_{1},\cdots,x_{\ell}
are different from each other.
For
Y=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\}\subset X=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{\ell}\}
, define

f(Y)=\sum_{\tau\in L(r)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{a_{\tau(r)}-a_{j}}.

By lemma
3.1
we know that
f(X)=0
when
|X|>2
. Note that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(\ell)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{x_{\tau(j)}-x_{j}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{s}X_{i}\\
|X_{i}|\geqslant 2\\
s\ \text{odd}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s}f(X_{i})\right).

(3.1)

Since
\ell
is odd, every disjoint union
X=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{s}X_{i}
in the RHS of (
3.1
) has an
X_{i}
with
|X_{i}|>2
, thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(\ell)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{x_{\tau(j)}-x_{j}}=0.

Similarly, it can be shown that
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(\ell)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{x_{\tau(j)}-x_{j}}=0.

If
\ell
is even, one can prove (2) in the same way by using lemma
3.1
. This complete the proof of this theorem.
∎

Proof of equation (
1.1
) and equation (
1.2
):

In the notations of Theorem
3.2
, let
x=\zeta
. If
k=0
and
n
is a even number, then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(n)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}a_{j\tau(j)}=0,

which implies

\sum_{\tau\in D(n)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}a_{j\tau(j)}=(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sum_{\tau\in D(n)}\mathrm{sign}(\tau)\prod_{j=1}^{n}a_{j\tau(j)}=\dfrac{\left(\left(n-1\right)!!\right)^{2}}{2^{n}}.

If
k=1,s_{k}=1
, and
n
is an odd number, then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in D(n-1)\\
\mathrm{sign}(\tau)=(-1)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j\tau(j)}=0,

which implies that

\sum_{\tau\in D(n-1)}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j\tau(j)}=(-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\sum_{\tau\in D(n)}\mathrm{sign}(\tau)\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}a_{j\tau(j)}=\dfrac{1}{n}\left(\dfrac{n-1}{2}!\right)^{2}.

Thus we complete the proof of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).∎

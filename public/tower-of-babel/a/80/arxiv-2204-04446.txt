Northcott numbers for the weighted Weil heights
Masao Okazaki, Kaoru Sano

Abstract
. We answer the question of Vidaux and Videla about the distribution of the Northcott numbers for the Weil height. We solve the same problem for the weighted Weil heights. These heights generalize both the absolute and relative Weil height. Our results also refine those of Pazuki, Technau, and Widmer.

Primary 11G50

1.
Introduction

For a subset
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, a function
\mathfrak{h}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and a real number
C>0
, we set

\displaystyle B(A,\mathfrak{h},C)

\displaystyle:=\left\{a\in A\mid\mathfrak{h}(a)<C\right\}\text{ and}

\displaystyle Z(A,\mathfrak{h})

\displaystyle:=\left\{a\in A\mid\mathfrak{h}(a)=0\right\}

The following definitions are important concepts when we study finiteness properties in number theory.

Definition 1.1

(
[
BZ01
]
and
[
PTW21
]
)
.

(1)

We say that
A
has the
\mathfrak{h}
-Northcott property
(or
\mathfrak{h}
-(N) for short) if the set
B(A,\mathfrak{h},C)
is finite for all
C>0
.

(2)

We say that
A
has the
\mathfrak{h}
-Bogomolov property
(or
\mathfrak{h}
-(B) for short) if the set
B(A,\mathfrak{h},C)\setminus Z(A,\mathfrak{h})
is finite for some
C>0
.

Definition
1.1
lets us set

\Nor_{\mathfrak{h}}(A):=\inf\left\{C>0\mid\#B(A,\mathfrak{h},C)=\infty\right\}.

The non-negative number
\Nor_{\mathfrak{h}}(A)
is called the
Northcott number of
A
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
, introduced in
[
PTW21
]
and
[
VV16
]
.
By definition, a subset
A
has
\mathfrak{h}
-(N) (resp.
\mathfrak{h}
-(B)) if and only if
\Nor_{\mathfrak{h}}(A)=\infty
(resp.
\Nor_{\mathfrak{h}}(A\setminus Z(A,\mathfrak{h}))>0
).
Here we regard
\inf\emptyset
as
\infty
.
We note that
\mathfrak{h}
-(B) immediately follows from
\mathfrak{h}
-(N).

Let
h:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
In
[
VV16
]
, Vidaux and Videla proposed the following question.

Question 1.2

(
[
VV16
, Question 6]
)
.

Which real numbers can be realized as
\Nor_{h}(L)
for some field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
?

The above question was first dealt with in
[
PTW21
, Theorem 3]
, which reveals that for any given
c>0
there exists a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying that
c/2\leq\Nor_{h}(L)\leq c
.
This result lets us expect that any positive real number can be realized as
\Nor_{h}(L)
for some field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Hence our main result is the following answer to Question
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any given
c>0
, we can construct a field
L
satisfying that
{\rm Nor}_{h}(L)=c
.

We prove a more general result for the weighted Weil heights, which generalize the absolute and the relative Weil height.
For each
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, we set

h_{\gamma}(a):=\deg(a)^{\gamma}h(a),

where
\deg(a):=[\mathbb{Q}(a):\mathbb{Q}]
.
The function
h_{\gamma}
is called the
\gamma
-weighted Weil height
, introduced in
[
PTW21
]
.
Here we should note that
h_{\gamma}
-(B) was studied implicitly in
[
AM16
]
before
[
PTW21
]
.
We first remark that
h_{0}
(resp.
h_{1}
) is the absolute (resp. relative) Weil height.
We also note that the equality
Z(\overline{\mathbb{Q}},h_{\gamma})=\mu_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\cup\{0\}
holds for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\mu_{A}
is the set of roots of unity in a subset
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
(see,
e.g.,
[
BG06
, Theorem 1.5.9]
).
We denote
h_{\gamma}
-(N) (resp.
h_{\gamma}
-(B)) by
\gamma
-(N) (resp.
\gamma
-(B)) for short.
We write
\Nor_{h_{\gamma}}(\cdot)
as
\Nor_{\gamma}(\cdot)
for simplicity.
The property
0
-(N) (resp.
0
-(B)) is usually called the
Northcott property
(resp.
Bogomolov property
).
There are several examples of infinite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
that have
0
-(N) or
0
-(B)
(see,
e.g.,
[
AD00
]
,
[
BZ01
]
,
[
Hab13
]
,
[
Sch73
]
, or
[
Wid11
]
).
On the other hand, the Lehmer conjecture asserts that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
1
-(B) and
\Nor_{1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})\geq\log(s)
, where
s=1.176\ldots
is the smallest known Salem number (see,
e.g.,
[
Sil07
, p.100]
).
In
[
PTW21
, Theorem 4]
, Pazuki, Technau, and Widmer gave fields that have
1
-(B) but not
0
-(B).
More precisely, for each
\gamma\leq 1
and
\epsilon>0
, they constructed a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
that has
\gamma
-(N) but not
(\gamma-\epsilon)
-(B).
As we will see in Lemma
2.1
, the sets

\displaystyle I_{N}(A)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\mid A\text{ has }\gamma\text{-(N)}\right\}\text{ and}

\displaystyle I_{B}(A)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\mid A\text{ has }\gamma\text{-(B)}\right\}

are intervals of the form
(\gamma,\infty)
or
[\gamma,\infty)
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
.
Thus the field
L
in
[
PTW21
, Theorem 4]
satisfies that
(\gamma-\epsilon,\infty)\supset I_{B}(L)\supset I_{N}(L)\supset[\gamma,\infty)
.
However, the values of
\inf I_{B}(L)
and
\inf I_{N}(L)
were not mentioned in
[
PTW21
, Theorem 4]
:

As we will see in Corollary
2.4
and Remark
2.6
, we remark that

\inf I_{N}(A\setminus\mu_{A})=\inf I_{B}(A)\in[-\infty,1]

hold for all subsets
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Hence our generalization of Theorem
1.3
is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\gamma<1
and
c>0
be real numbers.
We can construct a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying the following condition
(1)
,
(2)
, or
(3)
, respectively.

(1)

I_{N}(L)=I_{B}(L)=[\gamma,\infty)
.

(2)

I_{N}(L)=(\gamma,\infty)\subsetneq[\gamma,\infty)=I_{B}(L)
with
{\rm Nor}_{\gamma}(L)=c
.

(3)

I_{N}(L)=I_{B}(L)=(\gamma,\infty)
.

Remark 1.5
.

We note that Theorem
1.4
covers neither the case
\gamma=1
nor
\gamma=-\infty
.
We will discuss fields
L
satisfying each of
I_{N}(L)=I_{B}(L)=[1,\infty)
,
I_{N}(L)=(1,\infty)\subsetneq[1,\infty)=I_{B}(L)
, and
I_{N}(L)=I_{B}(L)=\mathbb{R}
in Section
5
.

Remark 1.6
.

There are some works on calculating Northcott numbers with respect to the house.
The notion of Northcott numbers originally stems from the Julia-Robinson numbers, which are Northcott numbers of sets of totally positive algebraic integers with respect to the house (see,
e.g.,
[
CVV20
]
,
[
GR19
]
,
[
Rob62
]
,
[
VV15
]
, or
[
VV16
]
).
Another remarkable result is
[
PTW21
, Theorem 1]
.
Pazuki, Technau, and Widmer constructed for any given
c\geq 1
a field whose ring of integers has Northcott number (with respect to the house) equal to
c
.
The image under the house of their rings of integers also satisfies additional topological properties motivated by work on the undecidability of rings (see,
e.g.,
[
Rob62
]
and
[
VV15
]
).

The other result concerns the following theorem.

Theorem 1.7

(
e.g.,
[
DZ08
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
be a field with
0
-(N)
.
Then for any
d>0
, the set

L^{(d)}:=\left\{a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\mid[L(a):L]\leq d\right\}

also has
0
-(N)
.

The well-known fact that any number field has
0
-(N) is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.7
.
We show that Theorem
1.7
fails for
h_{\gamma}
whenever
0<\gamma\leq 1
.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}
be the field of all totally real numbers.
Then for all
0<\gamma\leq 1
the field
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}
has
\gamma
-
(N)
but the field
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}(\sqrt{-1})
does not have
\gamma
-
(N)
.
More precisely, for all
0<\gamma\leq 1
the set
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}(\sqrt{-1})\setminus\mu_{\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}(\sqrt{-1})}
does not have
\gamma
-
(N)
.

2.
Intervals associated with
\gamma
-(N) and
\gamma
-(B)

Let
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
In this section, we give some basic properties of
I_{N}(A)
and
I_{B}(A)
defined in Section
1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
\gamma
-
(N)

(
resp.
\gamma
-
(B))
, the set
A
also has
\delta
-
(N)

(
resp.
\delta
-
(B))
for all
\delta>\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\delta>\gamma
and
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Since the inequality
h_{\gamma}(a)\leq h_{\delta}(a)
holds for all
a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, the property
\delta
-(N) of
A
immediately follows from
\gamma
-(N) of
A
.
On the other hand, a set
A
having
\gamma
-(B) means that there exists a constant
D>0
such that
h_{\gamma}(a)\geq D
for all
a\in A\setminus(\mu_{A}\cup\{0\})
.
Thus
\delta
-(B) of
A
also follows from
\gamma
-(B) of
A
since we know that
h_{\delta}(a)\geq h_{\gamma}(a)\geq D
for all
a\in A\setminus(\mu_{A}\cup\{0\})
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For each subset
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, the sets
I_{N}(A)
and
I_{B}(A)
are intervals of forms
(\gamma,\infty)
or
[\gamma,\infty)
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

By the definitions of
\gamma
-(N) and
\gamma
-(B), we know that
I_{N}(A)\subset I_{B}(A)
,
i.e.,
\inf I_{N}(A)\geq\inf I_{B}(A)
.
We actually have
\inf I_{N}(A\setminus\mu_{A})=\inf I_{B}(A)
.
This is an immediate consequence of the following proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
\gamma
-
(B)
, the set
A\setminus\mu_{A}
has
\delta
-
(N)
for all
\delta>\gamma
.

Proof.

Take any
C>0
and
a\in A\setminus(\mu_{A}\cup\{0\})
with
h_{\delta}(a)<C
.
Since
A
has
\gamma
-(B), there exists a constant
D>0
independent of
a
such that
h_{\gamma}(a)\geq D
.
Thus we have

\deg(a)=\left(\frac{h_{\delta}(a)}{h_{\gamma}(a)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta-\gamma}}<\left(\frac{C}{D}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta-\gamma}}.

If
\delta\geq 0
, then we have

h(a)\leq\deg(a)^{\delta}h(a)=h_{\delta}(a)<C.

By Theorem
1.7
, the conditions (
2
) and (
2
) imply that the set
B(A\setminus\mu_{A},h_{\delta},C)
is finite for the case
\delta\geq 0
.

On the other hand, if
\delta<0
, then (
2
) implies that
\deg(a)^{-\delta}<(C/D)^{\frac{-\delta}{\delta-\gamma}}
.
Therefore we have

h(a)=\deg(a)^{-\delta}h_{\delta}(a)<\left(\frac{C}{D}\right)^{\frac{-\delta}{\delta-\gamma}}C.

Again by Theorem
1.7
, the conditions (
2
) and (
2
) also imply that the set
B(A\setminus\mu_{A},h_{\delta},C)
is finite for the case
\delta<0
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

For any subset
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, the equality
\inf I_{N}(A\setminus\mu_{A})=\inf I_{B}(A)
holds.
In addition, if
A
contains only finitely many roots of unity, we have
\inf I_{N}(A)=\inf I_{B}(A)
.

Remark 2.5
.

We can not remove the assumption “excluding
\mu_{A}
” in Proposition
2.3
.
This is because the set
\mu_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
clearly has
\gamma
-(B) but not
\gamma
-(N) for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.6
.

We set

h_{\rm Dob}(a):=\left(\frac{\log^{\prime}(\deg(a))}{\log^{\prime}\log(\deg(a))}\right)^{3}h_{1}(a),

where
\log^{\prime}(\cdot):=\max\{1,\log(\cdot)\}
.
In
[
Dob79
]
, Dobrowolski proved that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
h_{\rm Dob}
-(B).
Hence there exists
D>0
such that
f(a)\geq D
for all
a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus(\mu_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\cup\{0\})
.
It is clear that for any
\varepsilon>0
, if
\deg(a)
is sufficiently large, the inequality

\deg(a)^{\varepsilon}>\left(\frac{\log^{\prime}(\deg(a))}{\log^{\prime}\log(\deg(a))}\right)^{3}

holds.
Thus
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
(1+\varepsilon)
-(B) for any
\varepsilon>0
by Theorem
1.7
.
Therefore, by Corollary
2.4
, we have

I_{N}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\mu_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=(1,\infty)\ \text{ and }\ \inf I_{N}(A\setminus\mu_{A})=\inf I_{B}(A)\in[-\infty,1]

for all subsets
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Note that these immediately follow if we assume the Lehmer conjecture.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.3
, we prove Proposition
1.8
.

Proof of Proposition
1.8
.

It is known that
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}
has
0
-(B) (see
[
Sch73
]
).
Thus
\mathbb{Q}^{\textrm{tr}}
has
\gamma
-(N) for each
\gamma>0
by the equality
\mu_{\mathbb{Q}^{\textrm{tr}}}=\{\pm 1\}
and Proposition
2.3
.
On the other hand, the field
\mathbb{Q}^{\textrm{tr}}(\sqrt{-1})
does not have
\gamma
-(N) for all
0<\gamma\leq 1
.
Indeed, the algebraic number
a_{k}:=((2-\sqrt{-1})/(2+\sqrt{-1}))^{1/k}
is an element of
\mathbb{Q}^{\textrm{tr}}(\sqrt{-1})
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
(see,
e.g.,
[
ADZ14
, Section 5]
).
Since we have the inequalities

h_{\gamma}(a_{k})=\deg(a_{k})^{\gamma}h(a_{1}^{1/k})\leq\frac{2^{\gamma}}{k^{1-\gamma}}h(a_{1})\leq 2h(a_{1}),

the field
\mathbb{Q}^{\textrm{tr}}(\sqrt{-1})
has infinitely many elements with bounded value of
h_{\gamma}
.
∎

3.
Some remarks on lower bounds for heights

Throughout the rest of the paper, we denote the set of positive integers by
\mathbb{N}
.
This section is devoted to giving some technical lemmata to calculate the
\gamma
-Northcott numbers.
Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
allow us to get a lower bound for the Northcott number of our fields.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
K
be a number field.
Assume that
a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies that
[K(a):K]>1
.
We set
M:=K(a)
and
m:=[M:K]
.
Then we have the inequality

h(a)\geq\frac{1}{2(m-1)}\left(\frac{\log(N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{M/K}))}{m[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-\log(m)\right),

where
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
is the usual norm and
D_{M/K}
is the relative discriminant ideal of the extension
M/K
.

Proof.

See
[
Sil84
, Theorem 2]
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We set

\delta_{\gamma}(B):=\inf h_{\gamma}(B)

for each non-empty subset
B\subset A
.
Let
A_{0}\subsetneq A_{1}\subsetneq A_{2}\subsetneq\cdots
be an ascending chain of non-empty subsets of
A
satisfying that

(1)

A_{i}
has
\gamma
-
(N)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and

(2)

A=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}A_{i}
.

Then we have

{\rm Nor}_{\gamma}(A)=\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{\gamma}(A_{i}\setminus A_{i-1}).

Proof.

See
[
PTW21
, Lemma 6]
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\gamma\leq 1
be a real number.
We also let
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(q_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, and
(d_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be strictly increasing sequences of prime numbers.
Assume that there exists
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
i>i_{0}
, the inequality
p_{i}<q_{i}
and

p_{i},q_{i}\notin\{d_{1},p_{1},q_{1},\ldots,d_{i-1},p_{i-1},q_{i-1}\}

hold.
We set

V(i,\gamma):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\log(p_{i})-\frac{\log(d_{i})}{2}&(\gamma=1),\\
\displaystyle\frac{\log(p_{i})}{d_{i}^{1-\gamma}}&(0\leq\gamma<1),\\
\displaystyle\frac{\log(p_{i})}{(d_{1}\cdots d_{i-1})^{-\gamma}d_{i}^{1-\gamma}}&(\gamma<0).\end{cases}

Then the field
L:=\mathbb{Q}((p_{i}/q_{i})^{1/d_{i}}\mid i\in\mathbb{N})
satisfies that

{\rm Nor}_{\gamma}(L)\geq\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}V(i,\gamma).

Especially, we have the following.

(1)

L
has
\gamma
-
(N)
if
\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}V(i,\gamma)=\infty
.

(2)

L
has
\gamma
-
(B)
if
\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}V(i,\gamma)>0
.

Proof.

We set

K_{0}:=\mathbb{Q},\ K_{i}:=K_{i-1}((p_{i}/q_{i})^{1/d_{i}}),\ \text{and}\ F_{i}:=\mathbb{Q}((p_{i}/q_{i})^{1/d_{i}})

for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Lemma
3.2
, it is enough to show that the inequality

\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{\gamma}(K_{i}\setminus K_{i-1})\geq\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}V(i,\gamma)

holds.
Take any
i>i_{0}
and
a\in K_{i}\setminus K_{i-1}
.
Note that the equality
K_{i-1}(a)=K_{i}
holds since
[K_{i}:K_{i-1}]=d_{i}
is a prime number and
a\notin K_{i-1}
.
Thus we have the inequalities

d_{i}\leq\deg(a)\leq d_{1}\cdots d_{i}

and

h_{\gamma}(a)\geq\frac{\deg(a)^{\gamma}}{2(d_{i}-1)}\left(\frac{\log(N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{K_{i}/K_{i-1}}))}{d_{i}[K_{i-1}:\mathbb{Q}]}-\log(d_{i})\right)

by Theorem
3.1
.
To estimate
h_{\gamma}(a)
from below, we give a lower bound for
N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{K_{i}/K_{i-1}})
.
Since
F_{i}=\mathbb{Q}((p_{i}q_{i}^{d_{i}-1})^{1/d_{i}})
and
(p_{i}q_{i}^{d_{i}-1})^{1/d_{i}}
is a root of the
p_{i}
-Eisenstein polynomial
X^{d_{i}}-p_{i}d_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
, we know that
p_{i}
ramifies totally in
F_{i}
(see,
e.g.,
[
FT93
, Theorem 24 (a)]
).
Thus it holds that
p_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\mid D_{F_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}
(see,
e.g.,
[
Neu99
, p.199, (2.6)]
and
[
Neu99
, p.201, (2.9)]
).
Now we note that the equalities

N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{K_{i}/K_{i-1}})D_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}^{[K_{i}:K_{i-1}]}=D_{K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}=N_{F_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{K_{i}/F_{i}})D_{F_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}^{[K_{i}:F_{i}]}

hold (see,
e.g.,
[
Neu99
, p.202, (2.10)]
).
Since
K_{i-1}
is the compositum of
F_{1}
,
\ldots
,
F_{i-1}
and
p_{i}\notin\{d_{1},p_{1},q_{1},\ldots,d_{i-1},p_{i-1},q_{i-1}\}
, we know that
p_{i}
does not ramify in
K_{i-1}
(see,
e.g.,
[
Hil98
, Theorem 85]
and
[
Viv04
, Lemma 4.1]
).
Hence
p_{i}\nmid D_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}
holds.
Therefore (
3
) yields that

p_{i}^{[K_{i}:F_{i}](d_{i}-1)}\mid N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{K_{i}/K_{i-1}}).

Replacing
p_{i}
with
q_{i}
in the above discussion, we also get

q_{i}^{[K_{i}:F_{i}](d_{i}-1)}\mid N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{K_{i}/K_{i-1}}).

Since the equality
[K_{i}:F_{i}]=d_{1}\cdots d_{i-1}=[K_{i-1}:\mathbb{Q}]
holds, the conditions (
3
) and (
3
) yield that

2\log(p_{i})\leq\log(p_{i}q_{i})\leq\frac{\log(N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{K_{i}/K_{i-1}}))}{[K_{i-1}:\mathbb{Q}](d_{i}-1)}.

Thus we conclude that

\displaystyle~h_{\gamma}(a)

\displaystyle\geq

(
[
Amo16
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

If
p\nmid b
and
p^{2}\nmid b^{p-1}-1
, the set
L_{b,p}\setminus\sqrt{\langle b\rangle}
has
0
-
(B)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
c>0
be a real number.
We also let a prime number
b\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy that
b\geq\exp(c)
and
b\in\left\{9n+2\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
.
Then the field
L^{\prime}_{b,3}:=\mathbb{Q}(b^{1/3^{i}}\mid i\in\mathbb{N})
satisfies that
I_{N}(L^{\prime}_{b,3})=(1,\infty)\subsetneq[1,\infty)=I_{B}(L^{\prime}_{b,3})
and
c\leq{\rm Nor}_{1}(L^{\prime}_{b,3})<\infty
.

Remark 5.4
.

By Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, such
b
as in Proposition
5.3
always exists.

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

By Corollary
2.4
and the equality
\mu_{L^{\prime}_{b,3}}=\{\pm 1\}
, it is sufficient only to prove the inequalities
c\leq{\rm Nor}_{1}(L^{\prime}_{b,3})<\infty
.
Note that
3\nmid b=9n+2
and
3^{2}\nmid b^{3-1}-1=(9n+3)(9n+1)
hold.
Thus, by Proposition
2.3
and Theorem
5.2
, the set
L^{\prime}_{b,3}\setminus\sqrt{\langle b\rangle}
has
1
-(N).
Thus there are only finitely many
a\in L^{\prime}_{b,3}\setminus\sqrt{\langle b\rangle}
such that
h_{1}(a)\leq\log(b)
.
On the other hand, we know that
h_{1}(a)\geq\log(b)
for all
a\in\sqrt{\langle b\rangle}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
and that
h_{1}(b^{1/3^{i}})=\log(b)
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence we have
\Nor_{1}(L^{\prime}_{b,3})=\log(b)\geq c
.
∎

Finally, we deal with fields
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
I_{N}(L)=I_{B}(L)=\mathbb{R}
.
We remark that any number field satisfies the condition by Theorem
1.7
.
Here we give such a field of infinite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(d_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, and
(q_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be strictly increasing sequences of prime numbers.
Assume that the inequalities

\exp(d_{i}^{1+i^{2}})\leq p_{i}<q_{i}<p_{i+1}

hold for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the field
L:=\mathbb{Q}((p_{i}/q_{i})^{1/d_{i}}\mid i\in\mathbb{N})
satisfies that
I_{N}(L)=\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
V(i,\gamma)
be the quantity defined in Proposition
3.3
.
For
\gamma<0
, since we have

V(i,\gamma)\geq\frac{d_{i}^{1+i^{2}}}{(d_{1}\cdots d_{i-1})^{-\gamma}d_{i}^{1-\gamma}}\geq\frac{d_{i}^{1+i^{2}}}{d_{i}^{1-i\gamma}}=d_{i}^{i(i+\gamma)}\rightarrow\infty

as
i\rightarrow\infty
, the field
L
has
\gamma
-(N) by Proposition
3.3
.
∎

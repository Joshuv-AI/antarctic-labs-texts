Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative excellent domain and
\Spec(R)
the collection of prime ideals of
R
. If
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
then the
n
th symbolic power of
\mathfrak{p}
is the ideal
\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}:=\mathfrak{p}^{n}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R
. If
0\not=f\in R
then the
order of
f
at
\mathfrak{p}
is
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(f)=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid f\in\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}\}
. Let
e(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/fR_{\mathfrak{p}})
denote the (Hilbert-Samuel) multiplicity of the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}/fR_{\mathfrak{p}}
with respect to the maximal ideal. The values
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(f)
and
e(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/fR_{\mathfrak{p}})
serve as competing notions of vanishing order of the function
f
along the generic point of
V(\mathfrak{p})
, coinciding if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is non-singular. An important distinction between multiplicity and order, at singular primes, is that multiplicity enjoys a semi-continuity property, if
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)
, then
e(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/fR_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq e(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/fR_{\mathfrak{q}})
. Consequently, semi-continuity of multiplicity provides the Local Zariski-Nagata Theorem for primes
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)
belonging to the non-singular locus of
\Spec(R)
; if
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is non-singular then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a containment of ideals
\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}
.

If
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)
and
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is singular, then it is possible that there exists
f\in\mathfrak{p}
so that
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(f)>\ord_{\mathfrak{q}}(f)
, equivalently there exists an
n\in\mathbb{N}
so that
\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}
. The Uniform Chevalley Theorem,
[
HKV09
, Theorem 2.3]
, is an adaptation of the Local Zariski-Nagata Theorem to a singular point
\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)
, and provides a constant
C
, depending on
\mathfrak{q}
, so that for all
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a containment of ideals
\mathfrak{p}^{(Cn)}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a Noetherian ring of arbitrary characteristic.

(a)

[

, Local Zariski-Nagata Theorem, Page 143]
: If

\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}
for all

.

(b)

[

, Uniform Chevalley Theorem]
: Let

a prime with the property that

is analytically unramified. There exists a constant

, depending on

, so that if
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R_{\mathfrak{q}})
, then
\mathfrak{p}^{(Cn)}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{(n)}
for all

.

(c)

[

, Main Result]

Definition 2.1

\circ

2.4.
Multiplicity and Valuations

Let

be an excellent reduced ring. If

is a finite length

-module then

is the length of

. If

is local,

a non-zero and finitely generated

-primary ideal, then the (Hilbert-Samuel) multiplicity of

. If

is local then

is the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of

with respect to the maximal ideal. If

is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded,

a homogeneous ideal so that

is of finite length and an Artin local ring in degree

a finitely generated graded

. In particular, if

is local

an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal, then

If

is excellent and equidimensional then Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity defines an upper semi-continuous function
\Spec(R)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{N}
by
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto e(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
,
[

, Theorem 4]
. We implicitly use this result throughout this article when we assert either of the following consequences of Nagata’s criteria for openness,
[

, Theorem 24.2]
, and quasi-compactness of

:

\circ

If

are prime ideals then

.

\circ

There exists an upper bound

for the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of each localization of

at a prime ideal, i.e., if

.

The following theorem is Rees’
Order Ideal Theorem
, which plays a crucial role in the comparison of divisorial valuations with differing centers in this article. We augment The Order Ideal Theorem statement with insights not explicitly stated in Rees’ original statements. Instead, the additional insights can be derived from Rees’ proof. We provide a streamlined and somewhat novel proof of Rees’ result. Doing so eliminates extensive terminology translation and justifications that exceed the following presentation, resulting in a more concise treatment of the necessary materials.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
Ree61
, Rees’ Order Ideal Theorem]
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an equidimensional local ring and of Krull dimension
d
. Suppose that
R
is analytically reduced and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. For each Rees Valuation
\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}
let
Q_{\nu}
be the corresponding exceptional prime of the normalized extended Rees algebra
\overline{R[IT,T^{-1}]}
. If
f
is an element of
\mathfrak{m}
avoiding all minimal primes of
R
, then

e_{\frac{(I,f)}{(f)}}\left(\frac{R}{fR}\right)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}}\nu(f)e\left(\frac{\overline{R[IT,T^{-1}]}}{Q_{\nu}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}=\bigcap_{\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}}Q_{\nu}^{(\nu(f))}\subseteq\overline{R[IT,T^{-1}]}
. By the associativity formula for multiplicity,
[
Mat89
, Theorem 14.7]
,

e\left(\frac{\overline{R[IT,T^{-1}]}}{\mathfrak{a}}\right)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}}\nu(f)e\left(\frac{\overline{R[IT,T^{-1}]}}{Q_{\nu}}\right).

The degree
n
piece of
\mathfrak{a}
, denoted by
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
, is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{n}

\displaystyle=(T^{-n}\mathfrak{a})\cap R

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcap_{\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}}Q_{\nu}^{(\nu(I)n+\nu(f))}\right)\cap R

\displaystyle=\{x\in R\mid\nu(x)\geq n\nu(I)+\nu(f),\forall\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}\}.

By Rees’ Valuation criteria for containment in integral closure,
\bigcap_{\nu\RR_{I}}I_{\nu\geq n\nu(I)}=\overline{I^{n}}
. Therefore
g\in\overline{I^{n}}
if and only if for all
\nu\in\RR_{I}
,
\nu(gf)=\nu(g)+\nu(f)\geq n\nu(I)+\nu(f)
. Hence,
\overline{I^{n}}=(\mathfrak{a}_{n}:_{R}f)
.

If
h\geq\nu_{i}(f)
for all
1\leq i\leq t
, then
\overline{I^{n+h}}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{n}\subseteq\overline{I^{n}}
for all
n
, i.e. the chains of ideals
\{\mathfrak{a}_{n}\}
,
\{\overline{I^{n}}\}
are cofinal. We are assuming
R
is analytically reduced, therefore
\{\mathfrak{a}_{n}\}
is also cofinal with
\{I^{n}\}
. In conclusion,

\displaystyle e\left(\frac{\overline{R[IT,T^{-1}]}}{\mathfrak{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{\ \ }\infty}\frac{(d-1)!}{n^{d-1}}\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{\overline{I^{n}}}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{\ \ }\infty}\frac{(d-1)!}{n^{d-1}}\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{(\mathfrak{a}_{n}:f)}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{\ \ }\infty}\frac{(d-1)!}{n^{d-1}}\left(\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{R}{(\mathfrak{a}_{n}:f)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{\ \ }\infty}\frac{(d-1)!}{n^{d-1}}\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{R}{(\mathfrak{a}_{n},f)}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{\ \ }\infty}\frac{(d-1)!}{n^{d-1}}\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{R}{(I^{n},f)}\right)

\displaystyle=e_{\frac{(I,f)}{(f)}}\left(\frac{R}{fR}\right).

∎

Corollary 2.9

(Corollary of Rees’ Order Ideal Theorem)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an equidimensional local ring and of Krull dimension
d
. Suppose that
R
is analytically reduced and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. For each Rees Valuation
\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}
let
Q_{\nu}
be the corresponding exceptional prime of the normalized extended Rees algebra
\overline{R[IT,T^{-1}]}
. Then

e_{I}(R)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}}\nu(I)e\left(\frac{\overline{R[IT,T^{-1}]}}{Q_{\nu}}\right).

In particular, if
\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}
then
\nu(I)\leq e_{I}(R)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
, we may assume
R
has an infinite residue field. Then there exists a parameter element
f\in I
with the property that
\nu(f)=\nu(I)
for all
\nu\in\mathcal{R}_{I}
. In particular,
f
is a part of a minimal reduction of
I
, and
e_{I}(R)=e_{\frac{(I,f)}{(f)}}(R/fR)
. The corollary is then an application of Theorem
2.8
.
∎

Theorem
2.8
is a generalization of the observation that if

is a regular local ring then for all

Proposition 2.10
.

Let

Theorem 2.11

(
[

,

Proposition 2.13
.

Let

(b)

and

Proof.

Suppose that

In particular,

\nu_{1}(f)\leq(C-1)\nu_{2}(f).

Conversely, suppose

\displaystyle e_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/fR)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in\RR_{\mathfrak{m}}}\nu(f)e\left(\frac{\overline{R[\mathfrak{m}T,T^{-1}]}}{Q_{\nu}}\right){\footnotesize{(\mbox{by Theorem~\ref{theorem Rees multiplicity formula}}})}

\displaystyle\leq 2BEe_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\ord_{\mathfrak{m}}(f)\left(\sum_{\nu\in\RR_{\mathfrak{m}}}e\left(\frac{\overline{R[\mathfrak{m}T,T^{-1}]}}{Q_{\nu}}\right)\right)

2.6.
Equimultiplicity

In proofs to come, we require a comparison of multiplicities of the form
e(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
e_{\mathfrak{q}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/xR_{\mathfrak{q}})
where
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)
and
\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}=(\mathfrak{p},x)R_{\mathfrak{q}}

is a local ring and

is an ideal, then the

of

is the Krull dimension of the standard graded

-algebra

.

Theorem 2.15

(
[

, Theorem 4]
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a formally equidimensional local ring of Krull dimension

and

an ideal of height

. The following are equivalent.

\circ

\ell(I)=d-1
;

\circ

For some parameter element

of
R/I
,

\circ

For every parameter element
x\in\mathfrak{m}
of

,
e_{(I,x)}(R)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(I)}e_{(x)}(R/\mathfrak{p})e_{I}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Consider the associated graded ring of the normalized extended Rees algebra of

.
Therefore

for all

).

Theorem 2.18

(An Application of Equimultiplicity Theory)
.

Let
R
be an excellent Noetherian normal domain and
\mathfrak{p}\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)
. Assume that
\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}=(\mathfrak{p},x)R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}\not\in\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Ass_{R}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\overline{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}R_{\mathfrak{q}})
;

(b)

\ell(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}})=\height(\mathfrak{p})
;

(c)

e(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=e\left(R_{\mathfrak{q}}\right)
.

Moreover, if there exists an
n_{0}
so that
\mathfrak{q}\not\in\bigcup_{n\geq n_{0}}\Ass_{R}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{p}^{n}R_{\mathfrak{q}})
. Then

e(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=e\left(R_{\mathfrak{q}}\right)=e\left(\frac{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}{xR_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right).

Proof.

Equivalence of
(a)
and
(b)
can be derived from
[
Ree81
, Theorem 2.6]
. For a direct presentation, recall that
\mathfrak{q}\in\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Ass_{R}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\overline{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}R_{\mathfrak{q}})
if and only if
\mathfrak{q}
is a center of a Rees valuation of
\mathfrak{p}
, see Theorem
2.6
(
b
), if and only if
\ell(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}})=\height(\mathfrak{q})
by
[
SH06
, Theorem 10.4.2]
. Moreover,
\height(\mathfrak{q})=\height(\mathfrak{p})+1
as
\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}=(\mathfrak{p},x)R_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Note that
R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a discrete valuation ring whose maximal ideal is generated by the image of

3.2.

The valuation criteria of the Uniform Izumi-Rees Property (see Definition

), Lemma

are utilized to reduce the Uniform Izumi-Rees Property for an algebra essentially of finite type over a field to the Uniform Izumi-Rees Property to an algebra essentially of finite type over an algebraically closed field.

If

) separately along separable and inseparable field extensions. We remark that if

is an algebraic extension,

is not necessarily a domain, but instead decomposes as a product of normal domains (see

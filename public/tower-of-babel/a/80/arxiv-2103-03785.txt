An exact sequence and triviality of Bogomolov multiplier of groups
Sumana Hatui

Abstract
. The Bogomolov multiplier B 0 ​ ( G ) \mathrm{B}_{0}(G) of a finite group G G is the subgroup of the Schur multiplier H 2 ​ ( G , ℚ / ℤ ) {\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}) consisting of the cohomology classes which vanish after restricting to every abelian subgroup of G G . We give a new proof of a Hopf-type formula for B 0 ​ ( G ) \mathrm{B}_{0}(G) and derive an exact sequence for the cohomological version of the Bogomolov multiplier. Using this exact sequence we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for the corresponding inflation homomorphism to be an epimorphism and to be the zero map. Finally, we give a complete list of groups of order p 6 p^{6} , for odd prime p p , having trivial Bogomolov multiplier, so completing the 2020 investigation of Chen and Ma.

1.
Introduction

The Bogomolov multiplier
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
of a finite group
G
is the subgroup of
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
containing all the cohomology classes whose restriction to every abelian subgroup of
G
is zero.
It is a group theoretical invariant isomorphic to the unramified Brauer group of a given quotient space, and it has a connection with Noether’s problem on rationality of fields of invariants. Let
G
be a finite group, and let
V
be a faithful representation of
G
over the field
\mathbb{C}
. Then
G
acts naturally on the field of rational functions
\mathbb{C}(V)
.
Noether’s problem
[
20
]
asks for which groups
G
the field of
G
-invariant functions
\mathbb{C}(V)^{G}
is rational over
\mathbb{C}
?
A question related to the above is whether
\mathbb{C}(V)^{G}
is stably rational over
\mathbb{C}
, i.e., are there independent variables
y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{s}
such that
\mathbb{C}(V)^{G}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{s})
is a purely transcendental extension over
\mathbb{C}
?
This problem has close connection with Lüroth’s problem
[
24
]
and the inverse Galois problem
[
23
,
25
]
. By Hilbert’s Theorem
90
the stable rationality does not depend upon the choice of
V
, it depends only on the group
G
.
Saltman
[
23
]
introduced the unramified Brauer group
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{nr}(\mathbb{C}(V)^{G},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
and using it he found examples of groups
G
of order
p^{9}
such that
\mathbb{C}(V)^{G}
is not stably rational over
\mathbb{C}
.
Later Bogomolov
[
3
]
explored the group
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}_{nr}(\mathbb{C}(V)^{G},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
and proved that it is canonically isomorphic to

\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)=\bigcap_{A\leq G;A\text{ finite abelian}}\mathrm{Ker}~\big(\mathrm{res}^{G}_{A}:{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\big),

which is a subgroup of
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Kunyavskiĭ
[
13
]
called
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
the
Bogomolov multiplier
of
G
.
Bogomolov
[
3
]
used this description to give examples of groups
G
of order
p^{6}
having
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)\neq 0
and this non-vanishing property of
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
answers the classical Noether’s problem, i.e., the invariant field
\mathbb{C}(V)^{G}
is not rational over
\mathbb{C}.
So the triviality of
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
is an obstruction to the Noether’s problem.

Another description of
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
was given by Moravec
[
17
]
.
Let
G\wedge G
denote the
non-abelian exterior square
of group
G
(see Section
2.1
for details). For
x,y\in G
, we write a commutator
[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy
.
Let
M(G)
be the kernel of the commutator map
G\wedge G\to[G,G]
.
Define
M_{0}(G)=\langle x\wedge y\mid[x,y]=1\rangle
.
Moravec
[
17
]
defined

\tilde{\mathrm{B}}_{0}(G)=M(G)/M_{0}(G)

and
proved that, for a finite group
G
,

\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}(\tilde{\mathrm{B}}_{0}(G),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

Hence for finite groups
G
,
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)\cong\tilde{\mathrm{B}}_{0}(G).

This description helps us to compute
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
explicitly; see for example
[
16
,
17
,
9
]
. We also use this description in Section
5
to compute
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
for groups
G
of order
p^{6}
, where
p
is an odd prime.

We establish some notation before proceeding further.
We denote the commutator subgroup and center of
G
by
G^{\prime}
and
Z(G)
respectively. The set of commutators
\{[x,y]\mid x,y\in G\}
is denoted by
K(G)
.
For subgroups
H
and
K
of
G
, the group generated by

\{[h,k],h\in H,k\in K\}
is denoted by
[H,K]
.

Let
G
be a quotient of the free group
F
by the normal closure
R
of a given set of relations of
F
. In this context we say that
F/R

determines
a presentation for
G
.
Moravec
[
17
]
proved a Hopf-type formula for
\tilde{\mathrm{B}}_{0}(G)
by showing that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{B}}_{0}(G)\cong\frac{F^{\prime}\cap R}{\langle K(F)\cap R\rangle}.

To define this isomorphism, he uses the notion of covering group and exterior square of the group
G
.
Now we are ready to state our first main result which provide an alternative proof of a Hopf-type formula.
Our defined map is more explicit in nature and simplifies the proof of
[
17
]
.
This isomorphism will also be used to prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group with presentation determined by
F/R
. If
A=\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, then the transgression map

{\mathrm{tra}}:{\mathrm{Hom}}(R/\langle K(F)\cap R\rangle,A)\to\mathrm{B}_{0}(G),

corresponding to the exact sequence

1\to R/\langle K(F)\cap R\rangle\to F/\langle K(F)\cap R\rangle\to G\to 1,

is surjective. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}\Big(\frac{F^{\prime}\cap R}{\langle K(F)\cap R\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\Big).

We prove this result in Section
3
.

In the next result, we provide an exact sequence for
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
.
Let
G=F/R
and let
N=S/R
be a normal subgroup of
G
. Our aim is to define a homomorphism

\theta:\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)\to{\mathrm{Hom}}\big(\frac{R\cap\langle K(F)\cap S\rangle}{\langle K(F)\cap R\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\big)

and prove the exactness of the following sequence which is the dual of the exact sequence given in
[
17
, Proposition 3.12]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group with presentation determined by
F/R
, and let
N=S/R
be a normal subgroup of
G
. Suppose
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is a trivial
G
-module. For a suitable map
\theta
, the following sequence is exact:

(1.1)

\displaystyle 0\to{\mathrm{Hom}}\big(\frac{N\cap G^{\prime}}{\langle N\cap K(G)\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\big)\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{tra}}}\mathrm{B}_{0}(G/N)\xrightarrow{\inf}\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)\xrightarrow{\theta}

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}\big(\frac{R\cap\langle K(F)\cap S\rangle}{\langle K(F)\cap R\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\big)\to 0.

In
[
26
]
, Vermani gave an exact sequence for the second cohomology group and using it he provided necessary and sufficient conditions for the inflation homomorphism to be an epimorphism or the zero map (for the second cohomology group). We prove an analogous result
for the Bogomolov multiplier.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group with presentation determined by
F/R
, and let
N=S/R
be a normal subgroup of
G
.

(i)

The map
\inf:\mathrm{B}_{0}(G/N)\to\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
is an epimorphism if and only if

R\cap\langle K(F)\cap S\rangle=\langle K(F)\cap R\rangle.

(ii)

The map
\inf:\mathrm{B}_{0}(G/N)\to\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
is the zero map if and only if

R\cap\langle K(F)\cap S\rangle=F^{\prime}\cap R.

As an application of this result, we show that, for extra-special
p
-groups
G
,
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)=0
; see Proposition
3.3
, which leads to another proof of
[
12
, Proposition 2.1]
.

In Theorem
4.1
we provide some necessary and sufficient conditions for the central product of groups to have trivial Bogomolov multiplier. In Theorem
4.4
we show that the Bogomolov multiplier of every generalized discrete Heisenberg groups is trivial.

Our final aim is to list all non-abelian groups
G
of order
p^{6}
for odd prime
p
, having
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
trivial. Groups of order
p^{6}
were classified by James
[
8
]
. These groups are divided into isoclinism classes
\Phi_{i},1\leq i\leq 43
.
The isoclinism class
\Phi_{1}
contains abelian groups and for finite abelian groups

,
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
is trivial; see
[
25
]
.
For groups

of order
\leq p^{4}
,
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)=0
; see
[

]
.
The groups of order

having trivial Bogomolov multiplier were found in
[

.
In
[

, the Bogomolov multipliers of some groups of order

were recently studied, and with the help of a computer calculation, it has been conjectured that

In the next result we confirm this conjecture and provide a complete list of non-abelian groups of order

for odd prime

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

be a non-abelian group of order

where

if and only if

Let
D
be a divisible abelian group regarded as a trivial

1\rightarrow N\rightarrow G\rightarrow G/N\rightarrow 1.

Then the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence
[

0\to{\mathrm{Hom}}(G/N,D)\xrightarrow{\inf}{\mathrm{Hom}}(G,D)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}}{\mathrm{Hom}}(N,D)\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{tra}}}{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G/N,D)\xrightarrow{\inf}{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,D),

where
{\mathrm{tra}}:{\mathrm{Hom}}(N,D)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G/N,D)
is the transgression homomorphism
given by
f\mapsto{\mathrm{tra}}(f)=[\alpha]
, with

\alpha(\overline{x},\overline{y})=f(\mu(\overline{x})\mu(\bar{y})\mu(\bar{xy})^{-1}),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\overline{x},\overline{y}\in G/A,

for a section
\mu:G/N\rightarrow G
.
The inflation homomorphism,
\inf:{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G/N,D)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,D)
is given by
[\alpha]\mapsto\inf([\alpha])=[\beta]
, where
\beta(x,y)=\alpha(xN,yN)
, for all
x,y\in G
.
The map
\mathrm{res}
denotes restriction homomorphism.

Now we check that these above maps can be defined for
\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)
also.
First we define

\mathrm{res}^{\prime}:{\mathrm{Hom}}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to{\mathrm{Hom}}(\frac{N}{\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

by
\mathrm{res}^{\prime}(f)(\bar{n})=f(n)
for
f\in{\mathrm{Hom}}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
and
\bar{n}\in\frac{N}{\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathrm{res}^{\prime}
is a well defined homomorphism.

Next we define the transgression map

{\mathrm{tra}}:{\mathrm{Hom}}\big(\frac{N}{\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\big)\to\mathrm{B}_{0}(G/N)

given by
\chi\mapsto{\mathrm{tra}}(\chi)=[\alpha]
, with

\alpha(\overline{x},\overline{y})=\chi(\overline{\mu(\overline{x})\mu(\bar{y})\mu(\bar{xy})^{-1}}),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\overline{x},\overline{y}\in G/N,

for a section
\mu:G/N\rightarrow G
.
If
\chi\in{\mathrm{Hom}}(\frac{N}{\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle},{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
, then we show that
{\mathrm{tra}}(\chi)\in\mathrm{B}_{0}(G/N)
.
Let
A/N
be a finite abelian subgroup of
G/N
and let
\mu:G/N\to G
be a section. For
\bar{a},\bar{b}\in A/N
,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tra}}(\chi)(\bar{a},\bar{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(\overline{\mu(\bar{a})\mu(\bar{b})\mu(\bar{a}\bar{b})^{-1}})=\chi\big(\overline{[\mu(\bar{a})^{-1},\mu(\bar{b})^{-1}].\mu(\bar{b})\mu(\bar{a})\mu(\bar{b}\bar{a})^{-1}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(\overline{[\mu(\bar{a})^{-1},\mu(\bar{b})^{-1}]})\chi(\overline{\mu(\bar{b})\mu(\bar{a})\mu(\bar{b}\bar{a})^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(\overline{\mu(\bar{b})\mu(\bar{a})\mu(\bar{b}\bar{a})^{-1}})\text{ as }[\mu(\bar{a})^{-1},\mu(\bar{b})^{-1}]\in\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tra}}(\chi)(\bar{b},\bar{a}).

Thus,

is a symmetric

is a divisible abelian group,
[{\mathrm{tra}}(\chi)|_{A/N\times A/N}]=1

Thus

is a well-defined homomorphism.
It is also easy to see that the inflation map
\inf:\mathrm{B}_{0}(G/N)\to\mathrm{B}_{0}(G)

Lemma 3.1

Let

be an exact sequence. The image of the corresponding transgression map

{\mathrm{tra}}:{\mathrm{Hom}}(N/\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{B}_{0}(G/N)

{\mathrm{Hom}}\big(\frac{G^{\prime}\cap N}{\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\big)

Proof.

We have a surjective homomorphism

\eta:{\mathrm{Hom}}\big(\frac{N}{\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\big)\to{\mathrm{Hom}}\big(\frac{N\cap[G,G]}{\langle K(G)\cap N\rangle},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\big)

Now we consider generalized discrete Heisenberg groups.

.

\mathrm{B}_{0}(H^{d_{1}|d_{2}|\cdots|d_{n}|r}_{2n+1}({\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}}))=0
.

Proof.

Let

It is easy to observe that

By Lemma
2.4
,

and
[z,y^{\phi}]

For

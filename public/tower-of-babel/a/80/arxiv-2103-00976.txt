T-Singular Values and T-Sketching for Third Order Tensors
Liqun Qi, Gaohang Yu

Abstract
Based upon the T-SVD (tensor SVD) of third order tensors, introduced by Kilmer and her collaborators, we define T-singular values of third order tensors. T-singular values of third order tensors are nonnegative scalars. The number of nonzero T-singular values is the tensor tubal rank of the tensor. We then use T-singular values to define the tail energy of a third order tensor, and apply it to the error estimation of a tensor sketching algorithm for low rank tensor approximation. Numerical experiments on real world data show that our algorithm is efficient. Key words. T-singular value, tensor sketching, single-pass algorithm, low rank tensor approximation, tensor tubal rank AMS subject classifications. 15A69, 15A18

(1.1)

The tensor rank theory is still not matured yet. Two main tensor ranks are the CP rank and the Tucker rank. For them, the Eckart-Young theorem is unknown. Thus, it is not easy to analyze the properties of the best rank-

,
23

{\mathcal{Y}}:={\mathcal{A}}*\mathcal{B}\ {\rm and}\ \mathcal{W}=\mathcal{C}*{\mathcal{A}},

(1.2)

3. Construct the tensor tubal rank-

approximation

\hat{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathcal{Q}*{\mathcal{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times p}.

(1.4)

We now need to consider several problems:

1. Is such an approach useful?

2. Is such an approach workable?

3. What is the cost of this approach?

4. What are its merits and shortcomings compared with the other approaches.

In Subsection 1.3 of
[
16
]
, three situations are listed for the necessity to apply matrix sketching. The same situations can be drawn here for tensor sketching.

To make error estimation of the proposed tensor sketching algorithm, we need to define the tail energy of a third order tensor. For a matrix

i
th largest singular values of

for

. Based upon the T-SVD (tensor SVD) factorization of third order tensors, introduced by Kilmer and her collaborators, we
define T-singular values of third order tensors. T-singular values of third order tensors are nonnegative scalars. The number of nonzero T-singular values is the tensor tubal rank of the tensor. We then use T-singular values to define the tail energy of a third order tensor, and apply it to the error estimation of the tensor sketching algorithm proposed above.

In the next section, we review some preliminary knowledge on T-product and T-SVD factorization. T-singular values of third order tensors are introduced in Section 3. In Section 4, we propose a tensor sketching algorithm. A probabilistic error bound is established in Section 5. Numerical experiments are presented in Section 6. They show that our algorithm is efficient, in particular for decay spectrum problems.

The relation between nonzero T-singular values and the tensor tubal rank is the same as the relation between nonzero singular values and the rank in the matrix case. This is a very good property.

4.3

Recently, in

such that

{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}={\rm bcirc}^{-1}(({\rm bcirc}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\dagger}).

(4.12)

Further, they point out that for the following least squares problem based on T-product,

\min_{{\mathcal{X}}}\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathcal{A}}*{\mathcal{X}}-\mathcal{B}\|_{F}^{2},

(4.13)

its least squares solution is
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}*\mathcal{B}

2.1

\min_{{\mathcal{X}}}\frac{1}{2p}\|\bar{A}\bar{X}-\bar{B}\|_{F}^{2},

then,
\bar{X}=\bar{A}^{\dagger}\bar{B}

We first prove a proposition.

Proposition 5.1

Let
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times s\times p}
be a Gaussian random tensor defined by Definition
2.2
. Suppose that
\mathcal{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times n\times p}
,
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k\times p}
and
\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times\times p}
. Then

\mathbb{E}\|\mathcal{C}*(\mathcal{G}*\mathcal{K})*\mathcal{B}\|_{F}^{2}=\|\mathcal{C}\|_{F}^{2}\|\mathcal{B}\|_{F}^{2}

(5.15)

Furthermore, if
m>n+1
, then

\mathbb{E}\|(\mathcal{G}*\mathcal{K})^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}={n\over m-n-1}.

(5.16)

Proof
Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{C}*(\mathcal{G}*\mathcal{K})*\mathcal{B}
. By Lemma
2.1
, this is equivalent to
\bar{F}=\bar{C}\bar{G}\bar{K}\bar{B}
, and

\|\mathcal{C}*(\mathcal{G}*\mathcal{K})*\mathcal{B}\|_{F}^{2}={1\over p}\|\bar{C}(\bar{G}\bar{K})\bar{B}\|_{F}^{2}.

Then
\bar{G}
is a standard Gaussian random matrix, and thus,
\bar{G}\bar{K}
is also a standard Gaussian random matrix. By Proposition A.1 of
[
5
]
, we have

\mathbb{E}\|\bar{C}(\bar{G}\bar{K})\bar{B}\|_{F}^{2}=\|\bar{C}\|_{F}^{2}\|\bar{B}\|_{F}^{2}.

Then by Lemma
2.1
, we have (
5.15
).

Expression (
5.16
) can be proved similarly.

.

\Box

Let
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
be the rank

-
k

{\mathcal{A}}
, obtained by Algorithm 3. We now split the error
\|{\mathcal{A}}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}

Proposition 5.2

Let
{\mathcal{A}}\in\Re^{m\times n\times p}
, and
\hat{A}
be the rank
t
-
k
approximation of
{\mathcal{A}}
, obtained by Algorithm 3, with
{\mathcal{X}}
and
\mathcal{Q}
be the intermediate tensors obtained from Algorithm 3, with
\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{I}_{kkp}

\|{\mathcal{A}}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}=\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|{\mathcal{X}}-\mathcal{Q}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}.

(5.17)

Proof
As
\hat{A}=\mathcal{Q}*{\mathcal{X}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*{\mathcal{X}}\|_{F}^{2}+\langle{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*{\mathcal{X}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathcal{Q}*(\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}-{\mathcal{X}})\|_{F}^{2}+\langle(\mathcal{I}_{mmp}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top})*{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{Q}*(\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}-{\mathcal{X}})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}+\|{\mathcal{X}}-\mathcal{Q}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}+{1\over p}{\rm trace}\left[\bar{A}^{H}(I-\bar{Q}\bar{Q}^{H})\bar{Q}(\bar{Q}^{H}\bar{A}-\bar{X})\right].

The first part of the last equality is based upon Lemma 3.19 of
[
7
]

\mathcal{Q}
is only partially orthogonal, noting that the proof of Lemma 3.19 of
[
7

Since

\bar{A}^{H}(I-\bar{Q}\bar{Q}^{H})\bar{Q}(\bar{Q}^{H}\bar{A}-\bar{X})=\bar{A}^{H}(\bar{Q}-\bar{Q}\bar{Q}^{H}\bar{Q})(\bar{Q}^{H}\bar{A}-\bar{X})=O.

We then have (
5.17

\Box

For the first term on the right hand side of (
5.17
), we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.3

Suppose that
{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times p}
. Let
s
be a nonnegative integer such that
s<k-1
. Let
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k\times p}
be a Gaussian random tensor defined by Definition
2.2
,
{\mathcal{Y}}
and
\mathcal{Q}
being calculated by (

) and (
1.3

\mathcal{Q}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}\leq(1+f(s,k))\cdot\tau_{s+1}^{2}({\mathcal{A}}),

where
f(s,t):={s\over t-s-1}
, and
\tau_{s+1}
is the tail energy defined by (
3.11
).

Proof
Denote
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}
. By Lemma
2.1
and linearity of the expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{P}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{A}^{(i)}-\bar{P}^{(i)}\bar{A}^{(i)}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{A}^{(i)}-\bar{Q}^{(i)}\left(\bar{Q}^{(i)}\right)^{H}\bar{A}^{(i)}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right).

By Theorem 10.5 of
[
5
]
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left\|\bar{A}^{(i)}-\bar{Q}^{(i)}\left(\bar{Q}^{(i)}\right)^{H}\bar{A}^{(i)}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq(1+f(s,k))\cdot\tau_{s+1}^{2}\left(\bar{A}^{(i)}\right).

Thus,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{B}}\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}\leq{1\over p}(1+f(s,k))\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\tau_{s+1}^{2}\left(\bar{A}^{(i)}\right)\right)=(1+f(s,k))\cdot\tau_{s+1}^{2}({\mathcal{A}}).

The equality here follows from Proposition
3.6
.

.

\Box

We now consider the second term on the right hand side of (
5.17
). Let
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k\times p}
be the partially orthogonal tensor in Algorithm 3. Then

\left(\overline{\mathcal{I}_{mmp}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}}\right)^{(i)}=I_{m\times m}-\bar{Q}^{(i)}\left(\bar{Q}^{(i)}\right)^{H},

for
i=1,\cdots,p

\bar{P}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times(n-k)}
such that

\bar{P}^{(i)}\left(\bar{P}^{(i)}\right)^{H}=I_{m\times m}-\bar{Q}^{(i)}\left(\bar{Q}^{(i)}\right)^{H},

for
i=1,\cdots,p
. Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(n-k)\times p}
be the tensor in the real domain by inverse DFT on diag
\left(\bar{P}^{(1)},\cdots,\bar{P}^{(p)}\right)
. Then by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\mathcal{P}*\mathcal{P}^{\top}=\mathcal{I}_{mmp}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}_{1}:=\mathcal{C}*\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times(n-k)\times p}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}:=\mathcal{C}*\mathcal{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times k\times p}
. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.4

Assume that the tensor tubal rank of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is
k
. Then

{\mathcal{X}}-\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}*\mathcal{C}_{1}*(\mathcal{P}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}).

(5.18)

Proof
By the algorithm design,
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{C}*{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
\mathcal{P}*\mathcal{P}^{\top}+\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}=\mathcal{I}_{mmp}
, we have

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{C}*{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{C}*\mathcal{P}*\mathcal{P}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}+\mathcal{C}*\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{C}_{1}*(\mathcal{P}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}})+\mathcal{C}_{2}*(\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}).

Since the tensor tubal rank of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is
k
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}
exists. Right-multiplying the last display in t-product by
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}

\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}*\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}*\mathcal{C}_{1}*(\mathcal{P}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}})+\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}.

By the algorithm design, we have
{\mathcal{X}}=\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}*\mathcal{W}
. The conclusion follows.

We further have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.5

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times m\times p}
is a Gaussian random tensor, independent from
\mathcal{B}
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}}[{\mathcal{X}}-\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}]={\mathcal{O}},

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\|{\mathcal{X}}-\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}=f(k,l)\cdot\|{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{Q}*\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2},

where
f(s,t):={s\over t-s-1}

Proof
Observe tensors
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are partial isometries with orthogonal ranges. Because of the marginal property of the standard normal distribution, the random tensors
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
are statistically independent standard normal tensors. The tensor tubal rank of
\mathcal{C}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times k\times p}
almost surely is equal to

Then, taking the expectation of (
5.18

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}}[{\mathcal{X}}-\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}[\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}*\mathcal{C}_{1}*\mathcal{P}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}]={\mathcal{O}}.

For the first equality, we use the statistical independence of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
to write the expectation as an iterated expectation. Then we observe that
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a tensor with zero mean.

Now, taking the expected squared Frobenius norm of (
5.18

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\|{\mathcal{X}}-\mathcal{Q}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\|\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}*\mathcal{C}_{1}*(\mathcal{P}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}})\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}[\|\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}\cdot\|\mathcal{P}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle f(k,l)\cdot\|\mathcal{P}^{\top}*{\mathcal{A}}\|_{F}^{2}.

The two equalities follow from Proposition 5.1.

In this section, numerical experiments are presented to verify the performance of the proposed T-Sketching algorithms (Algorithm 2 and Algorithm 3). We use the following relative error as a metric of the low rank approximation to the input tensor data:

\mbox{Relative Error}:=\frac{\|{\mathcal{A}}-\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{out}\|^{2}_{F}}{\|{\mathcal{A}}\|^{2}_{F}},

where
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{out}

We also employ the peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) defined as

\mbox{PSNR}:==10\log_{10}\frac{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}\|{\mathcal{A}}\|^{2}_{\infty}}{\|{\mathcal{A}}-\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{out}\|^{2}_{F}},

and the algorithm running time to evaluate the test methods.

T-Sketching on real-world data

In this subsection, we first test the T-Sketching method for color image data representation and compression via low-rank approximation. The color image, referred to as HDU picture, is of size 1200x1800x3. As shown in Figure 5, the tensor has decaying spectrum. We can see from Figures 6-8 that T-Sketching method can obtain better low rank approximation with the increase of sketch size
k
. In the case of
k=200
, the relative error of low rank approximation generated by T-Sketching method is less than
10^{-5}
, and the PSNR is 26.27. When
k=600
, the PSNR increased to 38.72 and the the relative error is less than
10^{-8}
. The curve in Figure 8 shows that T-Sketching method and rt-SVD method have similar performance, but T-Sketching method is more effective in terms of running time.

Weak convergence of adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo
Austin Brown, Jeffrey S. Rosenthal

Abstract
This article develops general conditions for weak convergence of adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo processes and is shown to imply a weak law of large numbers for bounded Lipschitz continuous functions. This allows an estimation theory for adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo where previously developed theory in total variation may fail or be difficult to establish. Extensions of weak convergence to general Wasserstein distances are established along with a weak law of large numbers for possibly unbounded Lipschitz functions. Applications are applied to auto-regressive processes in various settings, unadjusted Langevin processes, and adaptive Metropolis-Hastings.



Keywords:

adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo;
Birkhoff ergodic theorem;
weak convergence of adaptive MCMC;

Introduction

Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) provides a mean to estimate integrals with respect to a target probability measure from the empirical average of a Markov chain.
Many Markov chains require a delicate choice of tuning parameters to explore the state space properly such as Metropolis-Hastings
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2001
]
and discretized Langevin diffusions
[
Roberts and Tweedie, 1996
,
Roberts and Rosenthal, 1998
]
.
The optimal tuning parameter choice often depends on properties of the target probability measure which may be challenging to compute precisely.
At the same time, a poor tuning parameter choice may lead to unreliable estimation and diagnostics from the Markov chain. This motivates adaptive MCMC processes which automatically learn or adapt the tuning parameters of the Markov chain as the process progresses in time
[
Haario et al., 2001
,
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
]
.

The general theory for adaptive MCMC processes is accomplished through a convergence guarantee on the non-adapted Markov chain in the total variation distance combined with diminishing conditions on the adaptation of the tuning parameters
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
]
.
Numerous adaptation strategies are possible such as stochastic approximation
[
Robbins and Monro, 1951
]
or specifically designed strategies to rapidly decrease the adaptation as time progresses
[
Chimisov et al., 2018
]
.
The existing general theory results in the ability to approximate arbitrary bounded functions through a weak law of large numbers.
However, there has been increasing evidence that convergence in total variation is inadequate for many high dimensional target probability measures compared to convergence in Wasserstein distances from optimal transportation
[
Hairer et al., 2011
,
Durmus and Moulines, 2015
,
Qin and Hobert, 2021
,
Qin and Hobert, 2022
]
.
The issues with analyzing convergence with total variation are not limited to high dimensions and may appear for certain diffusion processes in any dimension
[
Hairer et al., 2011
]
and even toy examples
[
Tweedie, 1977
,
Butkovsky, 2014
]
.

Since the introduction of adaptive MCMC
[
Haario et al., 2001
]
, many advancements have been made based upon convergence in total variation
[
Atchadé and Rosenthal, 2005
,
Haario et al., 2005
,
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2009
]
but weak convergence appears less explored.
For example, convergence theory for adaptive MCMC has been extended to handle augmented target distributions that may depend on the adaptation to target multi-modal distributions
[
Pompe et al., 2020
]
.
Under specific adaptation strategies based on stochastic approximation, convergence theory under stronger assumptions can lead to a central limit theorem
[
Andrieu and Moulines, 2006
]
.
However, each of these theoretical results and guarantees are based on convergence of the non-adapted Markov chain in total variation.

This article’s main contribution is the weak convergence of adaptive MCMC processes under general conditions using Wasserstein distances that metrize the weak convergence of probability measures
[
Villani, 2009
,
Gibbs, 2004
]
.
Section
2
introduces the general adaptive MCMC regime, and Section
3
reviews the existing theory and some motivating examples that emphasize the inadequacy of the existing convergence theory.
Section
5
extends the traditional convergence framework in total variation for adaptive MCMC
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
]
to a framework based on weak convergence.
While the convergence result is weaker than total variation, it provides theoretical guarantees for approximations of bounded Lipschitz functions and arbitrary closed sets via Strassen’s theorem
[
Strassen, 1965
]
.
Section
6
develops general conditions for a weak law of large numbers applied to bounded Lipschitz functions based on weak convergence.

Some examples and applications are explored in Section
5
with adapted auto-regressive processes, adaptive unadjusted Langevin processes, adaptive Langevin diffusions, and adaptive Metropolis-Hastings.
Beyond the examples studied here, the weak convergence theory for adaptive MCMC can be used to develop new adaptive algorithms for Bayesian inverse problems popular in physics that involve sampling posterior distributions on infinite-dimensional spaces where total variation can be problematic
[
Cotter et al., 2013
]
.
Another potentially useful application of the theory developed here is demonstrated in the adaptive Langevin diffusion example where using Wasserstein distances to show weak convergence can yield simpler proofs of the required conditions in comparison to proofs needed to show convergence in total variation.

Weak convergence and the law of large numbers are further extended to general Wasserstein distances under stronger conditions.
The main application of this extension is a law of large numbers for unbounded Lipschitz functions in Section
6
and is of practical relevance in statistics.
In particular, this extends the weak law of large numbers for Lipschitz functions for adaptive MCMC processes
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
]
and for Markov chains
[
Sandrić, 2017
]
.
Recently, a law of large numbers for bounded Lipschitz functions has been developed under strong contraction conditions in Wasserstein distances combined with strong limitations on the adaptation
[
Hofstadler et al., 2024
]
.
The law of large numbers developed here holds under more general conditions and can apply to unbounded Lipschitz functions under suitable conditions.
Section
9
discusses our theoretical results along with limitations and potential extensions of the newly developed theory.

Background: adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo processes

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
denote the positive integers and denote the minimum and maximum of
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
by
a\wedge b
and
a\vee b
respectively.
For a Borel measurable space
S
, let
\mathcal{B}(S)
denote its Borel sigma field.
The Euclidean norm is denoted by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
and for a measure
\mu
, Lebesgue spaces are denoted by
L^{p}(\mu)
.
For a real-valued function
f
, denote the optimal Lipschitz constant with respect to a metric
d
by
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\text{Lip}(d)}=\sup_{x\not=y}|f(y)-f(x)|/d(x,y)

We follow closely to the adaptive MCMC process framework of
[

]
.
Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\Omega))
be a Borel measurable space and let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be complete separable metric spaces with respect to some metrics and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Y})
be their respective Borel sigma fields.
Let
\pi
be a target Borel probability measure on
\mathcal{X}
.
For a discrete time index
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the adaptive process updates a random tuning parameter
\Gamma_{t}:\Omega\mapsto\mathcal{Y}
as the process progresses using the entire history to improve the distribution of
X_{t}:\Omega\mapsto\mathcal{X}
.
The result is for the marginal distribution of
X_{t}
to approximate the target distribution
\pi
.

We define generalized Borel measurable probability transition kernels
(\mathcal{Q}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\mathcal{Q}_{t}:(\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X})^{t}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Y})\mapsto[0,1]

\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})\mapsto[0,1]
to prescribe the adaptive process by the relations

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{t}\in d\gamma|H_{t-1})=\mathcal{Q}_{t}(H_{t-1},d\gamma)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in dx|\Gamma_{t},X_{s},H_{t-1})=\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(X_{s},dx)

where
H_{t}=(\Gamma_{0},X_{0},\ldots,\Gamma_{t},X_{t})
denotes the history at time
t
.
This prescribes the finite-dimensional distributions so that
(\Gamma_{0},X_{0},\ldots,\Gamma_{t},X_{t})
for fixed
\Gamma_{0},X_{0}
has joint distribution

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(0,\ldots,t)}((\gamma_{0},x_{0}),d\gamma_{1},dx_{1},\ldots,d\gamma_{t},dx_{t})=\prod_{k=1}^{t}\mathcal{Q}_{k}(h_{k-1},d\gamma_{k})\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{k}}(x_{k-1},dx_{k})

with history
h_{k}=(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k},x_{k})
.
This defines an
adaptive process

adapted to the filtration
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{B}(\Gamma_{s},X_{s}:0\leq s\leq t)

\mu

Tulcea, 1949
]
.

We will mostly be concerned with the marginal distribution
X_{t}
from fixed initialization points
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
and general initializations

(\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}))

\displaystyle X_{t}|\Gamma_{0},X_{0}=\gamma,x\sim\mathcal{A}^{(t)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),

Background: Wasserstein distances

Let
d:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
be a lower-semi-continuous metric. Define the Wasserstein distance or transportation distance of order
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
between two arbitrary Borel probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}))
by

\mathcal{W}_{d,p}(\mu,\nu)=\left(\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}d(x,y)^{p}\xi(dx,dy)\right)^{1/p}

where
\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)
is the set of all joint probability measures
\xi
such that
\xi(\cdot\times\mathcal{X})=\mu(\cdot)
and
\xi(\mathcal{X}\times\cdot)=\nu(\cdot)
.
We generally suppress the
1
in the
L^{1}
metric and denote
\mathcal{W}_{d}(\mu,\nu):=\mathcal{W}_{d,1}(\mu,\nu)
.

The total variation distance denoted by
\mathcal{W}_{\text{TV}}(\cdot,\cdot)
between probability measures can be seen as a special case of a Wasserstein distance when the metric is defined by
I_{D^{c}}(\cdot,\cdot)
with the off-diagonal set
D^{c}=\{x,y\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}:x\not=y\}
.
If
(\mathcal{X},d)
is a complete separable metric space, then the standard bounded metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)\wedge 1
defines a Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{W}_{d\wedge 1}(\cdot,\cdot)
. This Wasserstein distance metrizes the weak convergence of probability measures through the bounded Lipschitz metric
[
Dudley, 2018
, Theorem 11.3.3]
and is equivalent up to a constant to the bounded Lipschitz metric by Kantovorich-Rubinstein duality
[
Villani, 2003
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Traditional theory of adaptive MCMC considers an adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
initialized at
\Gamma_{0},X_{0}=\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
satisfying a
(strong) diminishing adaptation
condition

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{W}_{\text{TV}}(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(x,\cdot))=0

(2)

in probability with the supremum assumed Borel measurable and a
(strong) containment
condition which is to show for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, the sequence

\displaystyle M_{\epsilon}(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})=\inf\left\{N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:\mathcal{W}_{\text{TV}}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}^{n}(X_{t},\cdot),\pi\right)\leq\epsilon\text{ for all }n\geq N\right\}

(3)

is bounded in probability, that is,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(M_{\epsilon}(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})>N\right)=0.

The (strong) diminishing adaptation restricts the adaptation plan for
(\Gamma_{t})_{t}
and (strong) containment is a uniform convergence requirement on the non-adapted Markov chain.

Under these two conditions, one has the guarantee
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
]
that for every fixed initialization
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
and for every bounded Borel measurable function
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\text{TV}}\left(\mathcal{A}^{(t)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi\right)=0

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\varphi(X_{s})\to\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varphi d\pi

in probability as
t\to\infty
.
Both of these guarantees have many practical applications in the reliability of Monte Carlo simulations in Bayesian statistics.
General conditions for (strong) containment (
3
) to hold have also been developed
[
Bai et al., 2009
,
Łatuszynski and Rosenthal, 2014
]
.

The (strong) containment condition (
3
) is often established via simultaneous drift and minorization conditions
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
,
Bai et al., 2009
]
.
This requires a drift function
V:\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
and identification of a small set
S=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:V(x)\leq R\}
so that there is are constants
\lambda,\alpha\in(0,1)
and
L\in(0,\infty)
where is it required
R>2L/(1-\lambda)
and for every
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
, there is a Borel probability measure
\nu_{\gamma}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in S}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot)\geq\alpha\nu_{\gamma}(\cdot)

and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V)(x)\leq\lambda V(x)+L.

(4)

These techniques yield (strong) containment (
3
) through a geometric rate
r\in(0,1)
and constant
M_{0}>0
so that for
t,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\text{TV}}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}^{n}(X_{t},\cdot),\pi\right)\leq M_{0}r^{n}V(X_{t})

and
(V(X_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
is bounded in probability
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
, Theorem 18]
.

The identification of such a small set
S
and drift function
V
as in (
4
) often becomes problematic in large dimensions as probability measures often tend towards mutual singularity. Even in low dimensions, a small set may not exist as a non-adapted Markov kernel may fail to be irreducible meaning for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
, there is no Borel probability measure
\phi_{\gamma}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\phi_{\gamma}(\cdot)>0
implies
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot)>0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
In this case, it is not possible to find such a small set regardless of the dimension
[
Meyn and Tweedie, 2012
, Theorem 5.2.2]
.

4
Motivating examples

The following running examples illustrate problematic points with analysis in total variation for adapting the tuning parameters of Markov chains compared to their alternative weak convergence properties.
In particular, (strong) containment (
3
) may fail.
Stark differences in the convergence characteristics may even appear when adapting a discrete Markov chain as the following example illustrates.

Example 1
.

(Discrete auto-regressive process)
Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
\gamma\geq 2
and
(\xi^{\gamma}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be independent uniformly distributed discrete random variables on
\{0,1/\gamma,2/\gamma,\ldots,(\gamma-1)/\gamma\}
.
With
X_{0}=x\in[0,1)
, define the auto-regressive process for
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
by

X^{\gamma}_{t}=\frac{1}{\gamma}X^{\gamma}_{t-1}+\xi^{\gamma}_{t}.

For each fixed
\gamma\geq 2
, this defines a Markov chain with Markov transition kernel denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
.
It can be shown the invariant probability measure
\pi\equiv\text{Unif}(0,1)
is Lebesgue measure on
[0,1)
.

For any adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
using these Markov kernels
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma}
, traditional convergence theory in total variation
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
, Theorem 13]
is inadequate.
Indeed, it can be shown that

\mathcal{W}_{\text{TV}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{t}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\text{Unif}(0,1)\right)=1

and (strong) containment (
3
) fails under any adaptive strategy.
On the other hand, weak convergence is exponentially fast.
For
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and any fixed
\gamma
, starting from
X_{0}=x\in[0,1)
and
Y_{0}=y\in[0,1)
, define
X^{\gamma}_{t}=\frac{1}{\gamma}X^{\gamma}_{t-1}+\xi^{\gamma}_{t}
and
Y^{\gamma}_{t}=\frac{1}{\gamma}Y^{\gamma}_{t-1}+\xi^{\gamma}_{t}
with shared discrete uniformly distributed random variable
\xi^{\gamma}_{t}
.
These random variables
(X^{\gamma}_{t},Y^{\gamma}_{t})
define a coupling so that for any
x\in[0,1)
and
\gamma\geq 2

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{|\cdot|}\left(\mathcal{P}^{t}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\text{Unif}(0,1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X^{\gamma}_{t}-Y^{\gamma}_{t}\right|X_{0}=x,Y_{0}=y\right]dy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-t}.

In particular, we will show later that under a suitable adaptation strategy, the adaptive process converges weakly using this Wasserstein distance.

The next example shows how problems appear in infinite-dimensions. Although the example is somewhat abstract, poor scaling properties in infinite-dimensions can also appear in practical high-dimensional scenarios in statistics.

For any fixed

, if

, then

and the invariant probability measure is

For an adaptive auto-regressive process

defined by (

fails to provide a convergence guarantee.
For each

Main results

This section extends previous results on convergence in total variation of adaptive MCMC processes to weak convergence and general Wasserstein distances
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
, Theorem 5]
and
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
, Theorem 13]
.
Let
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
be a lower semicontinuous metric on
\mathcal{X}
so
\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\cdot,\cdot)
defines a Wasserstein distance.
If
(X,\rho)
, is a complete separable metric space, then
\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\cdot,\cdot)
metrizes weak convergence
[
Dudley, 2018
, Theorem 11.3.3]
.
A motivation for this convergence is Strassen’s theorem which gives approximations to arbitrary closed sets
[
Villani, 2003
, Corollary 1.28]
.
However,
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
need only satisfy the axioms of a metric and
\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined more generally.

The first simple situation is to introduce a stopping time
T
such that the adaptation terminates and
\Gamma_{T}=\Gamma_{t}
for all
t\geq T
.
For any
T\geq 1
determining a stopping point of adaptation, we can construct a finite adaptation process
(Y_{t},\Gamma_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
adapted to the filtration
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{t}=\mathcal{B}(Y_{s},\Gamma_{s}:0\leq s\leq t)
initialized at
\Gamma_{0},Y_{0},=\gamma,x
such that
Y_{t}=X_{t}
for
0\leq t\leq T
and is a Markov chain further out, that is,
Y_{t+1}|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{t},Y_{t}=y\sim\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{T}}(y,\cdot)
for
t\geq T
.
Denote the marginal distribution
B^{(T,t-T)}((\gamma,x),\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(Y_{t}\in\cdot|\Gamma_{0},Y_{0}=\gamma,x)
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
be a lower semicontinuous metric on
\mathcal{X}
and let
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a finite adaptive process with initialization probability measure
\mu
as in (
1
).
If for every initialization
x\in\mathcal{X}
and every
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{t}(x,\cdot),\pi)=0,

then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\mu B^{(T,t-T)},\pi)=0.

Proof.

Since the optimal coupling exists
[
Villani, 2009
, Theorem 4.1]
and is Borel measurable
[
Villani, 2009
, Corollary 5.22]
,

\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\mu B^{(T,t-T)},\pi)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{T}}^{t-T}(X_{T},\cdot),\pi)\right].

The conclusion follows by dominated convergence.
∎

While finite adaptation may be a safe strategy, infinite adaptation where the process continually learns tuning parameters is often of greater interest in applications
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
]
.
Consider now the following two weakened assumptions both generalized from
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
]
.
The first assumption is a weak restriction on the adaptation and the second is a weak containment condition on convergence of the non-adapted Markov chain.

.

Let
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}

).

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(M_{\epsilon,\rho}(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})\geq T\right)=0.

(6)

2.

(Weak diminishing adaptation)
Suppose there is a conditional coupling

Note that

Proposition 5
.

Let
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an adaptive process with initialization probability measure
\mu

The following result shows weak convergence of the adaptive MCMC process.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an adaptive process with initialization probability measure
\mu
as in (
1
).
If weak containment (
6
) holds and weak diminishing adaptation (
7
) holds, then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi)=0.

We will prove Thereom
6
through subsequent lemmas by comparing the adaptive process to an adaptive process where adaptation stops at a finite time.
The first result shows that weak containment ensures the convergence of the finite adaptation process to the target measure uniformly in the finite adaptation stopping time.

Lemma 7
.

If weak containment holds (
6
), then for any
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{T\geq n}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[B^{(T,n-T)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi\right]=0.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
For any
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
and each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the infimum is attained at an optimal coupling
\xi_{x,\gamma}^{(n)}\in\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{n}(x,\cdot),\pi]

[
Villani, 2009
, Theorem 4.1]
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{n}(x,\cdot),\pi\right]=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\rho(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\wedge 1\xi^{(n)}_{\gamma,x}(dx^{\prime},dy^{\prime}).

(8)

The coupling is Borel measurable due to
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)\wedge 1
being lower semi-continuous and can be approximated by a non-decreasing sequence of bounded Lipschitz functions so we can choose a measurable selection
[
Villani, 2009
, Corollary 5.22]
such that the limit is Borel measurable using approximation techniques in
[
Villani, 2003
, Theorem 1.3]
.
Define the set
A_{\epsilon}=\{\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}:M_{\epsilon,\rho}(\gamma,x)\leq N\}
.
For all
\gamma,x\in A_{\epsilon}
and for all
n\geq N
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{n}(x,\cdot),\pi\right]

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon.

(9)

Let
\nu_{\gamma,x}^{(T)}
denote the probability measure for
(X_{T},\Gamma_{T})
given
\Gamma_{0},X_{0}=\gamma,x
.
Then

\hat{\xi}^{(T+n)}_{\gamma,x}(dx_{T+n},dy)=\int_{\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}}\xi_{\gamma_{T},y_{T}}^{(n)}(dx^{\prime},dy^{\prime})\nu^{(T)}_{\gamma,x}(\gamma_{T},y_{T})

defines a coupling for the finite adaptation process
Y_{T+n}\sim B^{(T,n)}((\gamma,x),\cdot)
and
Y\sim\pi

[
Villani, 2009
, Theorem 4.8]
.
By the weak containment assumption (
6
), there is an
N
depending on
\epsilon
such that uniformly in
T\geq 0
,

\nu^{(T)}_{\gamma,x}(A_{\epsilon}^{c})=\mathbb{P}\left(M_{\epsilon,\rho}(\Gamma_{T},X_{T})>N\right)\leq\epsilon.

Using (
9
), then uniformly in
T\geq n
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[B^{(T,n-T)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi\right]\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\rho(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\wedge 1\hat{\xi}_{\gamma,x}^{(T+n)}(dx^{\prime},dy^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\int_{A_{\epsilon}}\rho(y_{T+n},y)\wedge 1\xi_{\gamma_{T},y_{T}}^{(n)}(dx^{\prime},dy^{\prime})\nu^{(T)}_{\gamma,x}(\gamma_{T},y_{T})+\sup_{T^{\prime}\geq 0}\nu^{(T^{\prime})}_{\gamma,x}(A_{\epsilon}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon.

∎

The weak diminishing adaptation condition will ensure our next goal which is to have the adaptive MCMC process converge to the finite adaptation process.

Lemma 8
.

If weak diminishing adaptation (
7
) holds, then for any
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
and any
N\geq 0

\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\tilde{\rho}\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{A}^{(T+N)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),B^{(T,N)}((\gamma,x),\cdot)\right)=0.

Proof.

It will suffice to assume
\tilde{\rho}=\rho
and the optimal coupling in the weak diminishing adaptation assumption (
7
).
Fix
N\geq 1
and fix
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
For each
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
and each

, there exists a Borel measurable optimal coupling
\xi_{x,y,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma}^{*}
so that

\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma^{\prime}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot)\right]=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\rho(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\wedge 1\xi_{x,y,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma}^{*}(dx^{\prime},dy^{\prime}).

Using these conditional couplings, we define a joint probability measure
\zeta_{\gamma_{0},x_{0}}
by

\displaystyle\zeta_{\gamma_{0},x_{0}}(dx_{1},d\gamma_{1},dy_{1},\ldots,dx_{T+N},d\gamma_{T+N},dy_{T+N})

\displaystyle=\prod_{s=1}^{T}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{s}}(x_{s-1},dx_{s})\mathcal{Q}_{s}(h_{s-1},d\gamma_{s})\delta_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}}(dy_{1},\ldots,dy_{s})

The marginal is a coupling
\zeta_{\gamma_{0},x_{0}}(dx_{t},dy_{t})
for the adaptive process
X_{t}|\Gamma_{0},X_{0}=\gamma,x
and the finite adaptation process
Y_{t}|\Gamma_{0},Y_{0}=\gamma,x
initialized so that they are identical up to time
T
and use conditional couplings thereafter.

For
\gamma^{\prime},\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
and

D_{\rho,\delta}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)=\sup_{\{x,y:\rho(x,y)\leq\delta\}}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma^{\prime}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot)\right).

For any

and

\delta_{N}=r\in(0,1)

7

T
large enough depending on
\epsilon,N,\delta_{k}
and we can choose
\delta_{k-1}
sufficiently small so that for all

\mathbb{P}\left(D_{\rho,\delta_{k-1}}(\Gamma_{t+1},\Gamma_{t})>\delta_{k}\epsilon/N^{2}\right)\leq\epsilon/N^{2}.

This constructs

so that using a union probability bound, we can choose
T
sufficiently large depending on
\epsilon,N,\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{N}
so that for each

\mathbb{P}\left(E^{(T,N)}_{\epsilon\delta_{k},\delta_{k-1}}\right)\geq 1-\epsilon/N.

\mathbb{P}(E)=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{N}E^{(T,N)}_{\delta_{k}\epsilon,\delta_{k-1}}\right)\geq 1-\epsilon.

The triangle inequality for the Wasserstein distance
[
Villani, 2003
, Lemma 7.6]
holds for every

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{T+k}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{T}}(y,\cdot)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{T+1}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{T}}(y,\cdot)\right]+\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{T+s+1}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{T}+s}(x,\cdot)\right].

(11)

For each
k
, if
\gamma_{T+1},\ldots,\gamma_{T+k}\in E^{(T,N)}_{\epsilon\delta_{k},\delta_{k-1}}
, then by (
11
) and Markov’s inequality,

\inf_{\{\rho(x,y)\leq\delta_{k-1}\}}\mathcal{W}_{\{\rho(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\leq\delta_{k}\}}\left[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{T+k}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma_{T}}(y,\cdot)\right]\geq 1-\frac{\epsilon}{N}.

By construction of the the distribution
\zeta
,
\rho(x_{T},y_{T})\leq\delta_{0}
regardless of how small
\delta_{0}
is and we have the lower bound

\displaystyle\zeta\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{N}\{\rho(x_{T+k},y_{T+k})\leq\delta_{k}\}\bigm|E\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{N}\right)^{N}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\epsilon.

Combining these results, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{I_{\{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}:\rho(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})>r\}}}\left(\mathcal{A}^{(T+N)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),B^{(T,N)}((\gamma,x),\cdot)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta\left(\{\rho(x_{T+N},y_{T+N})>r\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta\left(\{\rho(x_{T+N},y_{T+N})>\delta_{N}\}\bigm|E\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(E\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon.

Since this holds for any

,

\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{A}^{(T+N)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),B^{(T,N)}((\gamma,x),\cdot)\right)\leq r+2\epsilon

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Fix
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
From Lemma
7
, we can choose
N_{\epsilon}
sufficiently large so that for all

with a particular adaptation stopping time

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[B^{(T_{n},N_{\epsilon})}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi)\right]\leq\epsilon/2.

Given this

sufficiently large so that for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{A}^{(T_{n}+N_{\epsilon})}((\gamma,x),\cdot),B^{(T_{n},N_{\epsilon})}((\gamma,x),\cdot)\right]\leq\epsilon/2.

The triangle inequality holds by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{A}^{(n)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi\right]

Since the conditional optimal coupling is attained and is Borel measurable

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mu\mathcal{A}^{(n)},\pi\right]\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left[\mathcal{A}^{(n)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi\right]\mu(d\gamma,dx)=0.

Interestingly we do not assume that
\pi
is invariant.
Denote the distance to a closed set

by

and the

Theorem

also ensures that for every bounded

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varphi d\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}\to\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varphi d\pi.

The following extends Theorem

to

-Wasserstein distances with unbounded metrics.

Proposition 9
.

Suppose an adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with initialization probability measure
\mu
(
1
) satisfies weak containment (
6
) and weak diminishing adaptation (
7
).
Suppose further for some
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\int\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\pi(dx)<\infty
.
Then the following are equivalent:

1.

Convergence in the
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance holds:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\rho,p}\left(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi\right)=0.

2.

The sequence
(\rho(X_{t},x_{0})^{p})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly integrable:

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\rho(x,x_{0})>R}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}(dx)=0.

If
(\mathcal{X},\rho)
is a complete separable metric space, then the following are also equivalent to (
1
):

3.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}(dx)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\pi(dx)

4.

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}(dx)\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\pi(dx)

Proof.

By Theorem
6
and Markov’s inequality, for any
\epsilon>0

\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi)}\xi(\{\rho(u,v)>\epsilon\})\to 0.

Let
\xi^{(t)}
be the attained optimal coupling for each
t

[
Villani, 2009
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Assume (1) holds.
For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\xi^{(t)}(|\rho(x,x_{0})-\rho(y,x_{0})|\geq\epsilon)=0
.
By Young’s inequality, for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there is a constant
C_{\epsilon,p}
depending on
p,\epsilon
such that

\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)\rho(y,x_{0})^{p}+C_{\epsilon,p}\rho(x,y)^{p}

Integrating with the coupling implies that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}(dx)\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(y,x_{0})^{p}\pi(dy).

By
[
Kallenberg, 2021
, Theorem 5.11]
, (2) holds.

Now assume (2) holds. By convexity,

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{t}\int_{\rho(x,y)>R}\rho(x,y)^{p}\xi^{(t)}(dx,dy)

(12)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\lim\sup_{t}\int_{\rho(x,y)>R}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}(dx)+2^{p-1}\lim\sup_{t}\int_{\rho(x,y)>R}\rho(y,x_{0})^{p}\xi^{(t)}(dx,dy)

(13)

By the characterization of uniform integrability
[
Kallenberg, 2021
, Theorem 5.11]
and dominated convergence, (

) tends to

as
R\to 0
.
This implies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\rho,p}^{p}\left[\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi\right]=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\rho(x_{t},y)^{p}\xi^{(t)}(dx^{\prime},dy^{\prime})=0.

The remaining equivalences follow from
[
Kallenberg, 2021
, Lemma 5.11]
.
∎

Corollary 10
.

Suppose an adaptive process

Lemma 12
.

Let
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an adaptive process with initialization probability measure
\mu
as in (
1
).
Suppose
\pi\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}=\pi
for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
. Assume for some
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and some
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
L=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}\pi(dx)<\infty
and for every
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
,
\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(y,x_{0})^{p}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,dy)<\infty
.
Suppose there is an
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
,

\mathcal{W}_{\rho,p}(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot))\leq(1-\alpha)\rho(x,y).

Then for every
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\mathcal{W}_{\rho,p}(\mathcal{P}^{t}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\pi)\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}[\rho(x,x_{0})+L].

Further,
\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho(X_{t},x_{0})^{p}\right]<\infty
and
(\rho(X_{t},x_{0}))_{t\geq 0}
is bounded in probability.

For each
t\in Z_{+}
and each
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\mathcal{W}_{\rho,p}(\mathcal{P}^{t}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}^{t}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot))^{p}\leq(1-\alpha)^{tp}\rho(x,y)^{p}.

The optimal coupling is attained
[
Villani, 2009
, Theorem 4.1]
at some conditional coupling
\xi_{x,y}
and is Borel measurable
[
Villani, 2009
, Corollary 5.22]
.
Since
\int_{\mathcal{X}}\xi_{x,y}(\cdot,\cdot)\pi(dy)\in\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{t},\pi]
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho,p}(\mathcal{P}^{t}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\pi)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}\rho(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})^{p}\xi_{x,y}(dx^{\prime},dy^{\prime})\pi(dy)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)^{tp}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,y)^{p}\pi(dy).

By Young’s inequality, for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a constant

such that for any

(a+b)^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon)a^{p}+C_{\epsilon}b^{p}

x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, we can choose

and a constant

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x^{\prime},x_{0})^{p}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,dx^{\prime})\leq\left[(1-\alpha)\rho(x,x_{0})+2L^{1/p}\right]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)(1-\alpha)^{p}\rho(x,x_{0})^{p}+C_{\epsilon,p}L.

By
[

(\rho(X_{t},x_{0}))_{t\geq 0}
is bounded in probability.

6
A weak law of large numbers

The point of this section is to develop convergence in probability of the empirical average of the adaptive MCMC process or weak law of large numbers.
The convergence theory developed so far in Wasserstein distances provides estimation accuracy for the marginal distribution of
X_{t}
but this generally has a large variability.
Estimation from the entire adaptive process
X_{s}\sim\mu\mathcal{A}^{(s)}
for
s\leq t
requires theory for the empirical average.
It is then of interest for reliable estimation to develop conditions so that for bounded
\rho
-Lipschitz functions

\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\varphi(X_{s})\to\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varphi d\pi

in probability.

The law of large numbers for non-adapted Markov chains is well-studied under convergence in total variation.
On the other hand, convergence in Wasserstein distances and its connection to the law of large numbers is less understood
[
Sandrić, 2017
, Theorem 1.2]
.
The first result is general and relies on the convergence of the adaptive process but may even apply the law of large numbers to unbounded functions if the conditions are satisfied.

Theorem 13
.

Let
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an adaptive process with initialization probability measure
\mu
so that
X_{t}\sim\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}
(
1
).
Let
d(\cdot,\cdot)
be a lower semicontinuous metric and suppose for some
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and for each
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\int d(x,x_{0})^{2}\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}(dx)<\infty

and

\int d(x,x_{0})^{2}\pi(dx)<\infty.

If

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{d,2}\left(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi\right)=0,

then for every Borel measurable
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\text{Lip}(d)}<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\varphi(X_{s})-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varphi d\pi\right)^{2}\right]=0.

(14)

In particular, the weak law of large numbers holds, that is,
\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\varphi(X_{s})\to\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varphi d\pi
in probability.

Proof.

We can assume
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\text{Lip}(d)}\leq 1
since we may normalize
\varphi
.
We may also assume
\int\varphi d\pi=0
since
\psi=\varphi-\int\varphi d\pi
is also
d
-Lipschitz.
We can assume there is an
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\varphi(x_{0})=0
.
Let
\Gamma
be a coupling of
X_{t}\sim\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}
and
Y\sim\pi
.
By disintegration
[
Kallenberg, 2021
, Theorem 3.4]
, there is a Borel measurable conditional probability measure
\Gamma_{h_{s}}(dx_{t},dy)
with
h_{s}=(\gamma_{1},x_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{s},x_{s})
such that

\displaystyle\Gamma(dx_{t},dy)=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{s}}\Gamma_{h_{s}}(dx_{t},dy)\mu\mathcal{A}^{(1,\ldots,s)}(dh_{s}).

With the history
H_{s}=(\Gamma_{k},X_{k})_{k=1}^{s}
and since
\varphi
is
d
-Lipschitz, for
t\geq s

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(X_{t})|\mathcal{H}_{s}\right]-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varphi d\pi\right|\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}d(x_{t},y)\Gamma_{H_{s}}(dx_{t},dy).

For
T\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have the upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varphi(X_{t})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(X_{t})\varphi(X_{s})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(X_{t})^{2}\right]+2\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(X_{t})|\mathcal{H}_{s}\right]\varphi(X_{s})\right]

\displaystyle\leq T\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathcal{X}}d(x,x_{0})^{2}\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}(dx)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}d(x_{t},y)\Gamma_{H_{s}}(dx_{t},dy)d(X_{s},x_{0})\right].

Using Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}d(x_{t},y)\Gamma_{H_{s}}(dx_{t},dy)d(X_{s},x_{0})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left[\int_{\mathcal{X}^{2}}d(x_{t},y)\Gamma_{H_{s}}(dx_{t},dy)\right]^{2}\right]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[d(X_{s},x_{0})^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}d(x_{t},y)^{2}\Gamma(dx_{t},dy)}\sqrt{\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathcal{X}}d(x,x_{0})^{2}\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)}(dx)}.

By assumption, we can choose a
T_{\epsilon}
depending on
\epsilon
such that for all
t\geq T_{\epsilon}
,

\mathcal{W}_{d,2}\left(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi\right)\leq\epsilon.

By assumption,
\max_{0\leq t\leq T_{\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}\left[d(X_{t},x_{0})^{2}\right]<\infty
and it follows by the triangle inequality
[
Villani, 2003
, Lemma 7.6]
that there is an
R\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{W}_{d,2}\left(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi\right)\leq\sqrt{\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left(d(X_{t},x_{0})^{2}\right)}+\sqrt{\int d(x,x_{0})^{2}\pi(dx)}\leq R.

Since the coupling
\Gamma
is arbitrary, we have the upper bound for every
T\geq T_{\epsilon}+1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\varphi(X_{t})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{2}}{T}+\frac{2R}{T^{2}}\sum_{t=2}^{T}(t-1)\mathcal{W}_{d,2}\left(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{2}}{T}+\frac{2R^{2}}{T^{2}}\sum_{t=2}^{T_{\epsilon}}(t-1)+\frac{2R}{T^{2}}\sum_{t=T_{\epsilon}+1}^{T}(t-1)\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{2}}{T}+\frac{R^{2}T_{\epsilon}(T_{\epsilon}-1)}{T^{2}}+\frac{R\epsilon(T-T_{\epsilon})(T+T_{\epsilon}-1)}{T^{2}}.

The conclusion follows since we can choose
T
sufficiently large and
\epsilon
sufficiently small.
∎

Theorem
13
also provides general conditions for weakly converging Markov chains
[
Sandrić, 2017
, Theorem 1.2]
.
We may now show that the conditions of Theorem
6
are sufficient for a weak law of large numbers for bounded
\rho-
Lipschitz functions.

Corollary 14
.

Suppose an adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with initialization probability measure
\mu
(
1
) satisfies weak containment (
6
) and weak diminishing adaptation (
7
).
Then for every bounded Borel measurable
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\text{Lip}(\rho)}<\infty
and any
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\varphi(X_{s})-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varphi d\pi\right|^{p}\right]=0.

(15)

If in addition, for some
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
,
(\rho(X_{t},x_{0})^{2})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly integrable and
\int\rho(x,x_{0})\pi(dx)<\infty
, then (
15
) holds with
p=2
and for all
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\text{Lip}(\rho)}<\infty
.

Proof.

For Borel measurable
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\text{Lip}(\rho)}<\infty
, apply Theorem
6
and Theorem
13
and it follows (
14
) holds. Since
\varphi
is bounded and (
14
) holds,
L^{p}
convergence follows for
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
To remove the bounded assumption on
\varphi
, since it is assumed
(\rho(X_{t},x_{0})^{2})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly integrable,
\mathbb{E}(\rho(X_{t},x_{0})^{2})<\infty
for each
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\int\rho(x,x_{0})\pi(dx)<\infty
then we can apply Proposition
9
.
∎

7
Examples and applications

Let us now revisit constructing adaptive processes for the running examples of Markov chains
1
and
2
where (strong) containment (
3
) fails to hold.

7.1
Example: discrete adaptive auto-regressive process

Consider an adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
using the Markov kernels
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma}
for the discrete auto-regressive process defined in Example
1
which fails to satisfy (strong) containment (
3
).
Assume the adaptation satisfies
|\Gamma_{t+1}-\Gamma_{t}|\to 0
in probability as
t\to\infty
.
For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
\gamma\geq 2
and
x\in[0,1)
, we showed previously in Example
1
that for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\mathcal{W}_{|\cdot|}\left(\mathcal{P}^{t}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\text{Unif}(0,1)\right)\leq 2^{-t}

where
\text{Unif}([0,1)
is Lebesgue measure on
[0,1)
and so weak containment holds (
6
).
For every
x,y\in[0,1)
define
X^{\Gamma_{t}}_{1}=x/\Gamma_{t}+\xi^{\Gamma_{t}}_{1}
and
Y^{\Gamma_{t}}_{1}=y/\Gamma_{t}+\xi^{\Gamma_{t}}_{1}
with common discrete random variable
\xi^{\Gamma_{t}}_{1}
defined previously in Example
1
.
The random variables
(X^{\Gamma_{t+1}}_{1},Y^{\Gamma_{t}}_{1})
define a coupling and since for sufficiently large
t
,
\Gamma_{t+1}=\Gamma_{t}
with high probability, then

\displaystyle\sup_{|x-y|\leq\delta}\mathcal{W}_{|\cdot|}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(y,\cdot)\right)\leq\sup_{|x-y|\leq\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X^{\Gamma_{t+1}}_{1}-Y^{\Gamma_{t}}_{1}\right||X_{0}=x,Y_{0}=y\right]\leq\frac{\delta}{2}

(16)

holds with high probability.
Then (
16
) tends to
0
as
\delta\to 0
and we conclude weak diminishing adaptation (
7
) holds.
By Theorem
6
, for every
\gamma\geq 2
and
x\in[0,1)
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{|\cdot|}[\mathcal{A}^{(t)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\text{Unif}(0,1)]=0

and this discrete auto-regressive adaptive process converges weakly.
Corollary
14
then implies a weak law of large numbers for all bounded Lipschitz continuous functions.

7.2
Example: infinite-dimensional adaptive auto-regressive process

Consider an adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
using Markov kernels
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma}
for the infinite-dimensional auto-regressive process (
2
) which cannot satisfy (strong) containment (
3
).
Assume the adaptation is restricted to a bounded set, that is, for some
R\in(0,\infty)
, if
\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert>R
then
\Gamma_{t+1}=\Gamma_{t}
and
|\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{t+1}|\to 0
in probability as
t\to\infty
.

We showed previously (
2
) that for any
\gamma\in(0,\gamma^{*})
and any
x,y\in H
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert,2}(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot))\leq\gamma^{*}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert

(17)

and combined with Lemma
12
implies weak containment (
6
) holds.
For
x,y\in H
, let
Y_{t}=\Gamma_{t+1}x+\sqrt{1-{\Gamma_{t+1}}^{2}}\xi_{t}
and
Y^{\prime}_{t}=\Gamma_{t}y+\sqrt{1-{\Gamma_{t}}^{2}}\xi_{t}
with common independent random variable
\xi_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,C)
.
We have the upper bound for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert,2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(y,\cdot))

\displaystyle\leq\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert Y_{t}-\Gamma_{t}x+\Gamma_{t}x-Y^{\prime}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq|\Gamma_{t+1}-\Gamma_{t}|\left\lVert x\right\rVert+{\gamma^{*}}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 11.99998pt+\left|\sqrt{1-\Gamma_{t+1}^{2}}-\sqrt{1-\Gamma_{t}^{2}}\right|\sqrt{\text{tr}(C)}.

If
\left\lVert x\right\rVert>R
and
\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\leq\delta
, then

\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert,2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(y,\cdot))\leq\gamma^{*}\delta.

Otherwise if
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq R
and
\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\leq\delta
,

\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert,2}(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(y,\cdot))\leq|\Gamma_{t+1}-\Gamma_{t}|R+\gamma^{*}\delta+\left|\sqrt{1-\Gamma_{t+1}^{2}}-\sqrt{1-\Gamma_{t}^{2}}\right|\sqrt{\text{tr}(C)}.

In either case, weak diminishing adaptation (
7
) holds.

For any
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, Young’s inequality and Fernique’s theorem
[
Bogachev, 1998
, Theorem 2.8.5]
imply we can choose
\epsilon>0
such that
(1+\epsilon){\gamma^{*}}^{p}<1
and a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
depending on
\epsilon
such that for every
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})(\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{p})\leq(1+\epsilon){\gamma^{*}}^{p}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{p}+C_{\epsilon}.

This implies
(\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert^{p})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly integrable.
From Theorem
6
and Proposition
9
, then for every
\gamma,x\in(0,\gamma^{*}]\times H
and every
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert,p}[\mathcal{A}^{(t)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\mathcal{N}(0,\mathcal{C})]=0

and Corollary
14
implies a weak law of large numbers for all Lipschitz continuous functions.

7.3
Example: adaptive random-walk Metropolis-Hastings

We look at a discrete version of the adaptive random-walk Metropolis-Hastings algorithm
[
Haario et al., 2001
]
which adapts the covariance of the proposal towards the covariance of the target probability measure.
This concrete example illustrates an issue in practical applications since current computers only produce floating-point approximations to real numbers. As a result, convergence theory in total variation corresponding to an adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo simulation targeting a continuous target probability measure is infeasible and has other issues that have been studied previously through perturbation theory
[
Roberts et al., 1998
,
Breyer et al., 2001
]
.
This is not necessarily the case in alternative distances which metrize weak convergence.

Let
\pi
be a target Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and Lebesgue density
f
.
Let
D=(x_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a dense subset in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\Sigma
be a symmetric, positive-definite matrix, and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
\mathcal{N}_{D}(\mu,\Sigma)
denote the discrete Gaussian with probability mass function

g_{\Sigma}(\mu,x)=\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(x-\mu)^{T}\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu)\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(x_{j}-\mu)^{T}\Sigma^{-1}(x_{j}-\mu)\right)}.

When
\mu\in D
, the
g_{\Sigma}(\mu,x)=g_{\Sigma}(x,\mu)
and it is symmetric.
Let
(M(\gamma))_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
be a family of symmetric, positive-definite matrices on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We define a discrete Markov chain
(X^{\gamma}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
using a discrete random-walk Metropolis-Hastings kernel
\mathcal{P}_{\Sigma}
with discrete Gaussian proposal where the proposal
X^{\prime}
given the previous state
x
is
X^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}_{D}(x,M(\gamma))
.
The Markov kernel is defined for each
x_{l},x_{k}\in D
by

\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x_{l},x_{k})=\left[1\wedge\frac{f(x_{k})}{f(x_{l})}\right]g_{M(\gamma)}(x_{l},x_{k})+\delta_{x_{l}}(\{x_{k}\})\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left[1\wedge\frac{f(x_{j})}{f(x_{l})}\right]g_{M(\gamma)}(x_{l},x_{j})\right).

We will assume
f
is continuous with compact support
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Further, we will assume the set of
\Sigma
is compact and so the eigenvalues of
\Sigma
are uniformly bounded so that there are constants
\lambda_{*},\lambda^{*}\in(0,\infty)
such that
\lambda_{*}\leq\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)\leq\lambda^{*}
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
.
It follows by a a minorization argument over
K
there is an
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
, any
x_{i},x_{j}\in D
, and any bounded Lipschitz continuous function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\left|\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{t}\varphi(x_{i})-\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{t}\varphi(x_{j})\right|\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}.

By the density of
D
and continuity of
\mathcal{P}\varphi(\cdot)
, we have that

\left|\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{t}\varphi(x_{i})-\int_{K}\varphi d\pi\right|\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}.

Weak containment (
6
) holds since it follows by Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality
[
Villani, 2003
, Theorem 1.14]

\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}^{t}(x_{i},\cdot),\pi\right)\leq(1-\alpha)^{t}.

Now let
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times D
and let
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
where
X_{t}\sim\mathcal{A}^{(t)}((\gamma,x),\cdot)
be the adaptive process using these Metropolis-Hastings kernels.
Under any valid weak diminishing adaptation strategy satisfying (
7
), we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{A}^{(t)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi\right)=0.

Corollary
14
then implies a weak law of large numbers for bounded Lipschitz continuous functions.
On the other hand, it can be shown that

\mathcal{W}_{\text{TV}}(\mathcal{A}^{(t)}\left((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi\right)=1

and fails to converge in total variation under any adaptation plan.

7.4
Example: adaptive unadjusted Langevin process

In certain cases, it has been observed
[
Villani, 2009
, page 21]
that Wasserstein distances can be simpler to prove convergence results than total variation and the following example provides a concrete illustration.
Consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
where
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with Euclidean norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
.
Let the potential
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
have gradient
\nabla V(\cdot)
with constants
\alpha,\beta>0
such that for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla V(y)-\nabla V(x)\right\rVert\leq\beta\left\lVert y-x\right\rVert

(18)

\displaystyle V(y)\geq V(x)+\left\langle\nabla V(x),y-x\right\rangle+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\lVert y-x\right\rVert^{2}.

(19)

Let
(\mathcal{Y},d)
be a complete separable metric space and for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
, let
M(\gamma)
define a symmetric positive definite matrix.
Let
h\in(0,1)
be a fixed discretization size and consider the unadjusted Langevin process

X^{\gamma,h}_{t+1}=X^{\gamma,h}_{t}-hM(\gamma)\nabla V(M(\gamma)X^{\gamma,h}_{t})+\sqrt{2h}Z_{t+1}

where
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are i.i.d
\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
.
We can define a family of Markov kernels
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,h})_{\gamma,h}
prescribing the conditional distributions
X^{\gamma,h}_{t+1}|X^{\gamma,h}_{t}=x\sim\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,h}(x,\cdot)
.
For an adaptive strategy
(\Gamma_{t},h_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we define the adaptive process
X_{t}:=M(\Gamma_{t})X^{\Gamma_{t},h_{t}}_{t}
for
t\geq 0
.
For example,
M(\Gamma_{t})
can estimate the inverse covariance matrix using the entire history of the process as in adaptive Metropolis-Hastings
[
Haario et al., 2001
]
and adaptive Piecewise-deterministic Markov processes
[
Bertazzi and Bierkens, 2022
]
.
We make the following assumption on the adaptation of the matrix
M(\gamma)
.

Assumption 15
.

Assume
M(\cdot)
is continuous and for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there is a constant
\lambda_{*}\in(0,\infty)
such that for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
with
\left\lVert v\right\rVert=1
,

\lambda_{*}\leq\left\langle vM(\gamma),v\right\rangle\leq 1.

Assume
d(\Gamma_{t+1},\Gamma_{t})\to 0
in probability and
\Gamma_{t+1}=\Gamma_{t}
if
\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert>R
for some
R>0
.

We have the following convergence result.

Proposition 16
.

Assume the adaptation plan satisfies assumption
15
and for some
h_{*}\in(0,1)
, let
H=[h_{*},1/(\alpha+\beta)]
and assume
(h_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a deterministic sequence with
h_{t}\in H
. Further, assume there is a limit
h^{*}\in H
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}|h_{t}-h^{*}|=0
. Then the adaptive unadjusted Langevin process converges in the
L^{1}
-Wasserstein distance for every
\gamma,h,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times H\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
to some probability measure
\pi_{h^{*}}
, that is,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}[\mathcal{A}^{(t)}((\gamma,h,x),\cdot),\pi_{h^{*}}]=0

and a weak law of large numbers holds for all bounded Lipschitz continuous functions.

Proof.

It can be shown that
M(\gamma)\nabla V(M(\gamma)\cdot)
is
\beta
Lipschitz and
\alpha
strongly convex.
For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}
, let

\displaystyle X^{\gamma}_{1}=x-hM(\gamma)\nabla V(M(\gamma)x)+\sqrt{2h}Z_{1}

\displaystyle Y^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{1}=y-hM(\gamma^{\prime})\nabla V(M(\gamma^{\prime})y)+\sqrt{2h}Z_{1}

with shared Gaussian random variable
Z_{1}\sim N(0,I)
.
By
[
Nesterov, 2018
, Theorem 2.1.12]
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert X^{\gamma,h}_{1}-Y^{\gamma,h}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{2h\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\right)\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{2}+h\left(h-\frac{1}{\alpha+\beta}\right)\left\lVert\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(y)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{2h_{*}\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\right)\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{2}.

For each adapted discretization size
h,h^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M(\gamma)X^{\gamma,h^{\prime}}_{1}-M(\gamma)X^{\gamma,h}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq|h^{\prime}-h|\beta\left\lVert x\right\rVert.

These imply along with the assumed adaptation strategy that there exists an invariant measure
\pi_{h^{*}}
with finite second moment.
By Lemma
12
, weak containment (
6
) holds.

For each adapted discretization size
h,h^{\prime}
and each adaptation parameter
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M(\gamma^{\prime})X^{\gamma^{\prime},h^{\prime}}_{1}-M(\gamma)Y^{\gamma,h}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M(\gamma^{\prime})X^{\gamma^{\prime},h^{\prime}}_{1}-M(\gamma^{\prime})X^{\gamma^{\prime},h}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M(\gamma^{\prime})X^{\gamma^{\prime},h}_{1}-M(\gamma)X^{\gamma,h}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 15.00002pt+\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X^{\gamma,h}_{1}-Y^{\gamma,h}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\beta|h^{\prime}-h|\left\lVert x\right\rVert+\left\lVert M(\gamma^{\prime})\nabla V(M(\gamma^{\prime})x)-M(\gamma)\nabla V(M(\gamma)x)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 15.00002pt+\left\lVert\nabla V(M(\gamma)x)-\nabla V(M(\gamma)y)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\beta|h^{\prime}-h|\left\lVert x\right\rVert+2\beta\left\lVert M(\gamma^{\prime})-M(\gamma)\right\rVert\left\lVert x\right\rVert+\left(1-\frac{2h_{*}\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\right)\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert.

This upper bound implies weak diminishing adaptation (
7
) under this adaptation strategy.
Therefore, this adaptive process converges weakly by Theorem
6
.
Lemma
12
implies
(\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert)_{n\geq 0}
is uniformly integrable and then by Proposition
9
, the Wasserstein convergence follows.
Corollary
14
implies a weak law of large numbers for all bounded Lipschitz functions.
∎

7.5
Example: adaptive diffusion process

Let the potential
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy (
18
).
Let
(M(\gamma))_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
be defined as in Example
7.4
and consider adapting a stochastic differential equation with
M(\gamma)
defined for
t\in[0,1]
by

dX^{\gamma}_{t}=-M(\gamma)\nabla V\left(M(\gamma)X^{\gamma}_{t}\right)dt+\sqrt{2}dW_{t}

where
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is standard Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a strong solution
(X^{\gamma}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
that is a Markov process with kernel
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}^{t}_{\gamma}
.
Using the solution at
t=1
, we can define a new Markov chain
X^{\gamma}_{n}|X_{0}=x
with Markov transition kernel
\tilde{P}^{1}_{\gamma}
.
We can define an adaptive process
(\Gamma_{n},X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
X_{n}=M(\Gamma_{n})X_{n}^{\Gamma_{n}}
so that
X_{n}|\Gamma_{n}=\gamma,X_{n-1}=x
has Markov transition
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot)
with invariant measure
\pi(dx)=Z^{-1}\exp(-V(x))dx
where
Z=\int\exp(-V(x))dx
.
This type of adaptive scheme using matrices has been successful in Piecewise-deterministic Markov processes
[
Bertazzi and Bierkens, 2022
]
.

Proposition 17
.

Assume the adaptation plan satisfies Assumption
15
and assume the potential
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies (
18
) and (
19
).
Then for every
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the adaptive diffusion process converges in the
L^{1}
-Wasserstein distance

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}[\mathcal{A}^{(t)}((\gamma,x),\cdot),\pi]=0

and a weak law of large numbers for all bounded Lipschitz continuous functions.

Proof.

For
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
X^{\gamma}_{t}|X_{0}=x
and
Y^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{t}|Y_{0}=y
and
Y_{0}=y
share the same Brownian motion so that these random variables define a coupling.
By strong convexity, for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left\langle\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(y),y-x\right\rangle\geq\alpha\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{2}
.
Define
f_{\gamma}(t)=\left\lVert X^{\gamma}_{t}-Y^{\gamma}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}
and it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}f_{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert x-y-M(\gamma)\int_{0}^{t}\left[\nabla V(M(\gamma)X^{\gamma}_{s})-\nabla V(M(\gamma)Y^{\gamma}_{s})\right]ds\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\nabla V(M(\gamma)X^{\gamma}_{t})-\nabla V(M(\gamma)Y^{\gamma}_{t}),M(\gamma)(X^{\gamma}_{t}-Y^{\gamma}_{t})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-2\alpha f_{\gamma}(t).

By Gronwall’s inequality,

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X^{\gamma}_{1}-Y^{\gamma}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}}\leq\exp(-\alpha)\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert.

We have the upper bound

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert,2}\left[\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot)\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left\lVert M(\gamma)X^{\gamma}_{1}-M(\gamma)Y^{\gamma}_{1}\right\rVert^{2}}\leq\exp(-\alpha)\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert.

(20)

By
[
Durmus and Moulines, 2019
, Propositon 1]
,
\int\left\lVert y\right\rVert^{2}\pi(dy)
is finite and by Lemma
12
, weak containment (
6
) holds.
Lemma
12
then implies weak containment (
6
) and
(\left\lVert X_{n}\right\rVert)_{n\geq 0}
is uniformly integrable.
A similar argument as in Example
7.4
shows weak diminishing adaptation (
7
).
By Proposition
9
, the convergence in Wasserstein follows and Corollary
14
implies a weak law of large numbers for all bounded Lipschitz functions.
∎

8
Connections to geometric drift and coupling conditions

A general approach to satisfy the containment condition (
6
) is through a simultaneous version of the Weak Harris’ Theorem
[
Hairer et al., 2011
, Theorem 4.8]
.
Other similar convergence bounds for non-adapted Markov chains could be modified to simultaneous versions as well
[
Durmus and Moulines, 2015
,
Qin and Hobert, 2022
]
.

Theorem 18
.

(Simultaneous Weak Harris’ Theorem)

Let
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
be a family of Markov kernels on
\mathcal{X}
with invariant probability measure
\pi
.

•

(Simultaneous geometric drift) Suppose there is a Borel drift function
V:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
and constants
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
K\in(0,\infty)
such that for every
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
,

\int_{\mathcal{X}}V(\gamma,x^{\prime})\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,dx^{\prime})\leq\lambda V(\gamma,x)+K.

•

(
\rho
-contracting) Suppose there is a
\kappa\in(0,1)
such that for every
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
with
\rho(x,y)<1
and every
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
,

\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot))\leq(1-\kappa)\rho(x,y).

•

(
\rho-
small) Suppose for some constants
\alpha,\delta\in(0,1)
and every
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
,

\sup_{x,y\in C_{\gamma}}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot))\leq 1-\alpha

where
C_{\gamma}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:V(\gamma,x)\leq(1+\delta)2K/(1-\lambda)\}
.

Then there is an explicit
\alpha^{*}\in(0,1)
depending on
\alpha,\kappa,\lambda
such that for every
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and every
\gamma,x\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
,

\mathcal{W}_{\rho_{\gamma}}(\mathcal{P}^{t}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\pi)\leq(1-\alpha^{*})^{t}\sqrt{(1+\beta^{*}V(\gamma,x)+(\alpha\wedge\kappa)/[4(1-\lambda))]}

where
\rho_{\gamma}(u,v)=\sqrt{(\rho(u,v)\wedge 1)\left[1+\beta^{*}V(\gamma,u)+\beta^{*}V(\gamma,v)\right]}
and
\beta^{*}=(\alpha\wedge\kappa)/(4K)
.

Proof.

The argument is inspired by
[
Hairer et al., 2011
, Theorem 4.8]
.
Fix
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
.
For
\beta>0
, define
\rho_{\beta,\gamma}(x,y)=\sqrt{(\rho(x,y)\wedge 1)(1+\beta(V(\gamma,x)+V(\gamma,y))}
.
First assume for
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,
\rho(x,y)\geq 1
and
V(\gamma,x)+V(\gamma,y)>R
.
Now for
\delta>0
, choose
R=(1+\delta)2K/(1-\lambda)
.
Then using the simultaneous drift condition

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho_{\beta,\gamma}}(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\beta\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V(\gamma,x)+\beta\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V(\gamma,y)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\lambda+\lambda(1+\beta V(\gamma,x)+\beta V(\gamma,y))+\beta 2K

\displaystyle\leq\left[(1-\lambda)\frac{1+\beta 2K/(1-\lambda)}{1+\beta R}+\lambda\right]\rho_{\beta,\gamma}(x,y)^{2}.

Now assume for
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,
\rho(x,y)\geq 1
and
V(\gamma,x)+V(\gamma,y)\leq R
.
Then using that
C_{\gamma}
is
\rho-
small and the simultaneous drift condition and choosing
\beta\leq\alpha/(4K)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho_{\beta,\gamma}}(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)\left[1+\beta\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V(\gamma,x)+\beta\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V(\gamma,y))\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)\rho(x,y)\wedge 1\left[1+\lambda\beta\left(V(x)+V(y)\right)+\beta 2K\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha/2)\rho_{\beta,\gamma}(x,y)^{2}.

Next assume for
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,
\rho(x,y)<1
.
Then using
\rho
-contracting and the simultaneous drift condition with and
\beta\leq\kappa/(4K)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\rho_{\beta,\gamma}}(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\kappa)\rho(x,y)\left[1+\beta\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V(\gamma,x)+\beta\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V(\gamma,y))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\rho(x,y)\wedge 1\left[1-\kappa+\beta 2K+(1-\kappa)\lambda\beta(V(\gamma,x)+V(\gamma,y))\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\kappa/2)\rho_{\beta,\gamma}(x,y)^{2}.

∎

Theorem
18
can be seen as an extension of simultaneous geometric drift and minorization conditions
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
, Theorem 18]
.
We allow the drift function to depend on on the adapted tuning parameter and the metric
\rho
is not restricted to the Hamming metric.
For drift functions
V
constant in
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
so that we can write
V:\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
, we have the following result for the adaptive process if the conditions of Theroem
18
hold true.

Theorem 19
.

Suppose the adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with initialization probability measure
\mu
as in (
1
) constructed with Markov kernels
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
satisfies weak diminishing adaptation (
7
).
Suppose the conditions of Theorem
18
are satisfied for
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
with a drift function
V
constant in
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
.
Then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi\right)=0.

Proof.

Since
\rho\wedge 1
is bounded, it will suffice to assume the adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t}
is initialized at
\gamma_{0},x_{0}\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
.
The conclusion follows from Theorem
6
if we verify weak containment (
6
).
The conditions of Theorem
18
imply in order to satisfy weak containment, it suffices to show
(V(X_{t}))_{t}
is bounded in probability.
The geometric drift condition implies
(V(X_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
is bounded in probability by
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
, Lemma 15]
since it is constant in
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
.
∎

To satisfy weak containment (
6
), Theorem
19
can be weakened to subgeometric rates of convergence.
If there is a constant
M_{0}>0
, a Borel measurable function
V:\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
such that
(V(X_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
is bounded in probability and a rate function
r:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to[0,1]
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}r(n)=0
such that for all
n,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}^{n}(X_{t},\cdot),\pi\right)\leq M_{0}r(n)V(X_{t})

then it follows weak containment (
6
) holds.
For example, a polynomial drift condition is sufficient for
(V(X_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
to be bounded in probability
[
Bai et al., 2009
]
and existing subgeometric rates of convergence for non-adapted Markov chains in Wasserstein distances can be modified to simultaneous versions
[
Butkovsky, 2014
,
Durmus et al., 2016
]
.

In certain cases, it can be difficult to find a drift function that does not change with
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
and alternative techniques can be used to show (strong) containment.
One successful strategy here is to only apply adaptation on a compact or bounded set of the state space
[
Craiu et al., 2015
, Theorem 21]
.
We will say adaptation is restricted to a Borel set
S\subset\mathcal{X}
for the adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
X_{t-1}\not\in S
,
\Gamma_{t}=\Gamma_{t-1}
.
Our next result shows the benefit of Theorem
18
with drift functions depending on the adapted tuning parameter if adaptation is restricted to a set.

Proposition 20
.

Suppose the adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with initialization probability measure
\mu
as in (
1
) constructed with Markov kernels
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
satisfies weak diminishing adaptation (
7
).
Suppose the conditions of Theorem
18
are satisfied with Markov kernels
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
and drift function
V(\cdot,\cdot)
.
Additionally, assume
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has adaptation restricted to the Borel set
S\subset\mathcal{X}
and

\sup_{x\in S}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathbb{E}\left[V(\Gamma_{t+1},X_{t+1})\mid\mathcal{H}_{t},X_{t}=x\right]<\infty
.
Then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{\rho\wedge 1}\left(\mu\mathcal{A}^{(t)},\pi\right)=0.

Proof.

It will suffice to assume the adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t}
is initialized at
\gamma_{0},x_{0}\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
, and by Theorem
6
, we may show that
(V(\Gamma_{t},X_{t}))_{t}
is bounded in probability.
By the assumptions, we can assume
\mathbb{E}\left[V(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})I_{S}(X_{t-1})\right]\leq K
.
We have the upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[V(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})I_{S}(X_{t-1})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[V(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})I_{S^{c}}(X_{t-1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq K+\mathbb{E}\left[V(\Gamma_{t-1},X_{t})I_{S^{c}}(X_{t-1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2K+\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[V(\Gamma_{t-1},X_{t-1})\right].

\displaystyle\leq 2K/(1-\lambda)+V(\gamma_{0},x_{0}).

Therefore,
(V(\Gamma_{t},X_{t}))_{t}
is bounded in probability by Markov’s inequality.
∎

9
Concluding remarks

This article developed weak convergence of adaptive MCMC processes under general conditions which is suited to situations where convergence in total variation is inadequate.
One motivation is adapting the tuning parameters of reducible Markov chains where the traditional theory of adaptive MCMC may not be applied.
Another application of the developed theory can be used to analyze adaptive MCMC processes where (strong) containment is difficult to show but weak containment may be more tractable.
The weak law of large numbers developed here can be seen as an extension of
[
Roberts and Rosenthal, 2007
, Theorem 23]
and appears of practical interest for the reliability and stability of adaptive MCMC simulations widely used in statistics and machine learning.

There are many future research directions worthy of pursuit.
While the developed theory for weak convergence allows a Markov process in continuous time to be adapted at discrete times, the proof techniques do not appear limited to discrete-time adaptive processes. In particular, a precise formulation of an adaptive MCMC process in continuous-time with similar convergence results appears feasible.
It also appears some techniques for the convergence results developed here might be extended to develop quantitative convergence rates in Wasserstein distances or mixing times for adaptive MCMC, but would require stronger assumptions on the adaptation plan.
Another interesting direction is to try to generalize Theorem
6
to hold for general possibly unbounded Wassserstein distances by adapting weak containment and weak diminishing adaptation to hold using general Wasserstein distances.

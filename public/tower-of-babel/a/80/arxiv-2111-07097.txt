Sarth Chavan, Masato Kobayashi, Jorge Layja

Abstract
. We construct an analytic approach to evaluate odd Euler sums, multiple zeta value ζ ⁡ ( 3 , 2 , … , 2 ) \zeta(3,2,\ldots,2) and multiple t t -value t ⁡ ( 3 , 2 , … , 2 ) t\left(3,2,\ldots,2\right) . 1.
Introduction and Main Results

1.1.
Euler sums

The classical
Euler sums
are infinite series

(1.1)

H\left(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k},q\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p_{1}\right)}H_{n}^{\left(p_{2}\right)}\ldots H_{n}^{(p_{k})}}{n^{q}}

where
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,
q\geqslant 2
and

H_{n}^{\left(p\right)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{-p}
is the generalized harmonic number.

This theory indeed dates back to Euler and Goldbach, while recent research on this topic has been quite active. For an early
introduction and study on the evaluations of classical Euler sums, the readers may consult in Flajolet and Salvy’s paper
[
13
]
, in which they have developed a contour integral representation approach to the evaluation of Euler sums. For some recent progress, the readers are referred to
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
21
]

and references therein.

In particular,
linear Euler sums

H(p,q)
with
p,q\geqslant 2
satisfy the
reflection formula

H(p,q)+H(q,p)=\zeta(p)\,\zeta(q)+\zeta(p+q)

as Vălean has discussed in
[
18
]
.
As a result of which, in the evaluation of
H(p,q)
, Riemann zeta values
\zeta(p)
with
p\geqslant 2
often show up with rational coefficients.

1.2.
Multiple zeta values and multiple
t
-values

Multiple zeta values
(MZVs) are real numbers, originally defined by Euler, that
have been much studied in recent years because of their many surprising properties and the many places they appear in mathematics and mathematical
physics, ranging from periods of mixed Tate motives
[
4
]
to values of Feynman
integrals in perturbative quantum field theory.

In fact, multiple zeta values (MZVs) are simply a variant of the Euler sums. For positive integers
i_{1},i_{2}\ldots,i_{k}
where
i_{1}\geqslant 2
, the infinite series

(1.2)

\zeta\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{i_{1}}n_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots n_{k}^{i_{k}}}

is usually called a
multiple zeta value
.
Weight of the index
\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\right)

is
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{k}
and its depth is
k
.
The study of multiple zeta values began in the early 1990s with the works of Hoffman
[
9
]

and Zagier
[
24
]
, and has attracted a lot of research in the last two decades. For detailed history
and applications, the readers may consult the book of Zhao
[
25
]
.

One of the main problems in this topic is to understand the

\mathbf{Q}
-linear dependence of MZVs.
Some MZVs are a combination of single zeta values
while according to Brown, there certainly seems to exist exceptions such as
\zeta(5,3)

[
4
]
. We usually call the MZVs
\zeta(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})

with
i_{j}\in\{2,3\}

Hoffman elements
. In particular, F. Brown
[
4
]
proved that every multiple zeta value is a
\mathbf{Q}
-linear combination of Hoffman elements. Moreover, Zagier
[
23
]
showed explicit relations among Hoffman elements in the form
\zeta(2,\ldots,2,3,2,\ldots,2)
.

In a recent paper
[
10
]
, Hoffman introduced and studied an
odd
variant of the MZVs:

(1.3)

t\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geqslant 1\\
n_{j}\,\mathrm{odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{i_{1}}n_{2}^{i_{2}}\ldots n_{k}^{i_{k}}}=\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{k}\geqslant 1}\dfrac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(2n_{j}-1\right)^{i_{j}}}

which are called the
multiple t-values
(MtVs). As showed in
[
10
, Corollaries 4.1 and 4.2]
, the MtVs are
reducible to a linear combinations of alternating (or colored) MZVs.

There are indeed many conjectures concerning the arithmetic nature of these numbers.
Let
(d_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
be the
Padovan sequence
defined by

d_{1}=0,d_{2}=1,d_{3}=1
and
d_{n}=d_{n-2}+d_{n-3}
for
n\geqslant 4
and let
(f_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
be the Fibonacci sequence with
f_{1}=f_{2}=1
,
f_{n}=f_{n-1}+f_{n-2}
for
n\geqslant 3
. Let
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
and
\mathcal{T}_{n}
denote the
\mathbf{Q}
-span of all MZVs and MtVs of weight
n
respectively. Then, we have the following conjectures by M. Hoffmann
[
10
]
, B. Saha
[
16
]
and D. Zagier
[
23
]
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(1)

(Zagier
[
23
]
)
\dim\mathcal{Z}_{n}=d_{n}
.

(2)

(Hoffman
[
10
]
)
\dim\mathcal{T}_{n}=f_{n}
.

(3)

(Hoffman
[
10
]
)
The following set of weight
n
MZVs forms a
\mathbf{Q}
-basis of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
.

\{\zeta(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})\mid i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{k}=n,i_{j}\in\{2,3\}\}.

(4)

(Saha
[
16
]
)
The following set of weight
n
MtVs forms a
\mathbf{Q}
-basis of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
for
n\geqslant 2
.

\{t\left(a_{1}+1,a_{2},\dots,a_{r}\right)\mid a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{r}=n-1,a_{i}\in\{1,2\},1\leqslant r\leqslant n-1\}.

We often discuss MZVs and MtVs independently since the algebra of multiple
t
-values is quite different
in some ways from the algebra of multiple zeta values. Both the duality
theorem and the
double shuffle relations

[
11
]
of MZVs are
missing for MtVs.
However, there should be rich connections between these two theories.
In this article, we show that
we can compute

\zeta(3,2,\ldots,2)
and

t\left(3,2,\ldots,2\right)
using the same method.

1.3.
Central binomial sums

Central binomial sums
play an important role in number theory.
Informally speaking, it is an infinite series involving
\binom{2n}{n}
.
Lehmer
[
14
]
discussed the following two types of such sums:

\textrm{I}.\,\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}a_{n}\binom{2n}{n},\quad\textrm{II}.\,\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}a_{n}\binom{2n}{n}^{-1}.

Moreover, he also presented connections between such series and
Maclaurin series of
\arcsin\left(z\right)
and
\arcsin^{2}\left(z\right)
.
Some other examples include

\sum_{n=1}^{{+\infty}}\frac{1}{n^{2}\binom{2n}{n}}=\frac{1}{3}\,\zeta(2),\quad\sum_{n=1}^{{+\infty}}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^{3}\binom{2n}{n}}=\frac{2}{5}\,\zeta(3),\quad\sum_{n=1}^{{+\infty}}\frac{1}{n^{4}\binom{2n}{n}}=\frac{17}{36}\,\zeta(4)

as they arise in the work of Apèry
[
1
]
and van der Poorten
[
17
]
to prove irrationality of
\zeta(3)
.
Along this line, it is natural to consider an application of such sums for MZVs and MtVs.

1.4.
Main results

Our main results are at the crossroads of these three topics. Moreover, our idea throughout is more analytic; we seek Euler sums, MZVs and MtVs by
integral evaluation
. This is indeed a powerful method as seen in Doelder
[
6
]
and Vălean
[
18
]
.

In Section
2
and Section
3
,
t\left(3,2,\ldots,2\right)
and
\zeta(3,2,\ldots,2)
are evaluated using the inverse sine integral (
2.1
) and the nested sum representations for integer powers of the inverse sine function
[
2
, Equations (1) and (2)]
.
In particular, it is shown that

\zeta(3,2,\ldots,2)
and

t\left(3,2,\ldots,2\right)
are rational polynomials of

\pi
and odd zeta values
\zeta(2k+1)
with
k\geqslant 1
.

In Section
4
,
multiple mixed value

\mu\left(i_{k},i_{k-1},\ldots,i_{1}\right)
is defined and the Maclaurin series expansion for integer powers of inverse hyperbolic tangent function
[
8
, (6.3)]
is used to evaluate
\mu\left(2,1,\ldots,1\right)
.
Finally, in Section
5
,
odd Euler sums

\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
and
\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)
are defined and a reflection formula for
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
and
\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)
is established to evaluate
\mathcal{O}\left(q,q\right)
and
\mathcal{B}\left(q,q\right)
. Later, an analytic approach is implemented to compute
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
and
\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)
for small values of
p
and
q
. Moreover, integral reduction of
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
and
\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)
are also obtained.

Beyond these specific cases, we hope that the tools used in this study will be useful to the
readers in their endeavour of evaluation of more interesting series and integrals.

2.
Evaluation of Multiple
t
–value
t\left(3,2,\ldots,2\right)

Let us first begin with several Definitions and Propositions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{R}[[z]]
denote the set of
convergent power series with real coefficients.
For
f(z)\in\mathbf{R}[[z]]
, define
\mathbb{W}:\mathbf{R}[[z]]\to\mathbf{R}[[z]]
to be

\mathbb{W}f(z)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(xz)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

It is now helpful to understand the function
\mathbb{W}
coefficientwise.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f(z)\in\mathbf{R}[[z]]
.
Moreover, suppose it can be expressed in the form

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{{+\infty}}\frac{\left(2k-1\right)!!}{\left(2k\right)!!}\,\mathcal{C}_{2k+1}\,z^{2k+1}.

Then, the following identity holds.

\mathbb{W}f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{{+\infty}}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2k+1}}{2k+1}\,z^{2k+1}.

Proof.

From Definition
2.1
, it follows that

\mathbb{W}f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(2k-1\right)!!}{\left(2k\right)!!}\,\mathcal{C}_{2k+1}\,z^{2k+1}{\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{2n+1}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x}

=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(2k-1\right)!!}{\left(2k\right)!!}\frac{\left(2k\right)!!}{\left(2k-1\right)!!}\,\mathcal{C}_{2k+1}\,z^{2k+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{{+\infty}}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2k+1}}{2k+1}\,z^{2k+1}

which is the desired result.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f(z)\in\mathbf{R}[[z]]
and
\alpha\in\mathbf{R}
.
If
f(0)=0
, then we have

\mathbb{W}\left({\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\frac{f(z)}{z}}\,\mathrm{d}z}\right)=\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{f(\alpha x)\arccos{\left(x\right)}}{x}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Notice that, if
\alpha=0
, then both sides are 0.
Otherwise, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}\left({\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\frac{f(z)}{z}}\,\mathrm{d}z}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}{\int_{0}^{\alpha u}{\frac{f(z)}{z}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}u

=\int_{0}^{\alpha}{{\frac{f(z)}{z}}\int_{z/\alpha}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^{2}}}}\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}z=\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\frac{f(z)}{z}}\arccos\left({\frac{z}{\alpha}}\right)\mathrm{d}z=\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{f(\alpha x)\arccos{\left(x\right)}}{x}}\,\mathrm{d}x

as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(J.M. Borwein–Chamberland
[
2
, (1.3), (1.4)]
)
.

For
|x|\leqslant 2
and
N\geqslant 0

\frac{1}{\left(2N+1\right)!}\left[\arcsin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right]^{2N+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{{+\infty}}\frac{G_{N}(k)}{2^{2k}\left(2k+1\right)}\binom{2k}{k}\left({\frac{x}{2}}\right)^{2k+1}

where
G_{0}(k)=1
and

G_{N}(k)=\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\left(2n_{1}+1\right)^{2}}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{n_{1}-1}\frac{1}{\left(2n_{2}+1\right)^{2}}\,\,\,\cdots\sum_{n_{N}=0}^{n_{N-1}-1}\frac{1}{\left(2n_{N}+1\right)^{2}}.

Remark 2.5
.

Notice that the boundary
|x|=\pm 2
is inclusive here.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

(2.1)

I(N)=\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\arcsin^{N}(z)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z\quad\left(N\in\mathbb{N}\right)

Then, the following identities hold.

(2.2)

I(2N+1)=\dfrac{\left(2N+1\right)!}{2^{2N+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{j}\pi^{2N+1-2j}}{\left(2N+1-2j\right)!}\,\eta\left(2j+1\right)

(2.3)

I(2N)=\dfrac{\left(2N\right)!}{2^{2N}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{j}\pi^{2N-2j}}{\left(2N-2j\right)!}\,\eta\left(2j+1\right)+\left(-1\right)^{N}2\left(1-2^{-2N-1}\right)\zeta(2N+1)\right)

where
\eta\left(m\right)=\left(1-2^{1-m}\right)\zeta(m)
represents the Dirichlet eta function with
\eta\left(1\right)=\log(2)
.

Proof.

Buhler and Crandall in their very recent article
[
5
, page 280]
proved that

J(n)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/2}{z^{n}\cot\left(\pi z\right)}\,\mathrm{d}z

=\frac{n!}{2^{n}}\left({\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n,\,k\,\textrm{odd}}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{(k-1)/2}\eta\left(k\right)}{\pi^{k}\left(n-k+1\right)!}}\right)+\cos\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)\frac{\left(4n!\right)\left(1-2^{-n-1}\right)}{\left(2\pi\right)^{n+1}}\,\zeta(n+1).

Note that we corrected the sign
\frac{1+\left(-1\right)^{n}}{2}
to
\cos\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)
. Substituting
x=\sin\left(\pi z\right)
produces

J(n)=\int_{0}^{1/2}{z^{n}\cot\left(\pi z\right)}\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{\pi^{n+1}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{\arcsin^{n}(x)}{x}}\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{I(n)}{\pi^{n+1}}.

Writing down the cases for
n=2N+1
and
n=2N
with
k=2j+1
yields

I(2N+1)=\dfrac{\left(2N+1\right)!}{2^{2N+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{j}\pi^{2N+1-2j}}{\left(2N+1-2j\right)!}\,\eta\left(2j+1\right),

I(2N)=\dfrac{\left(2N\right)!}{2^{2N}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{j}\pi^{2N-2j}}{\left(2N-2j\right)!}\,\eta\left(2j+1\right)+\left(-1\right)^{N}2\left(1-2^{-2N-1}\right)\zeta(2N+1)\right)

which is the desired result.
∎

.

For
N\geqslant 1
, we have

t\left(3,\{2\}^{N}\right)

=\frac{1}{2^{2N+2}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+1}\left(2j\right)\pi^{2N+2-2j}}{\left(2N+2-2j\right)!}\,\eta(2j+1)+\left(-1\right)^{N}2\left(2N+2\right)\left(1-2^{-2N-3}\right)\zeta(2N+3)\right].

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\frac{\arcsin^{2N+1}(z)}{z\left(2N+1\right)!}}\,\mathrm{d}z=\sum_{k>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{N}\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{\left(2k+1\right)^{2}\left(2n_{1}+1\right)^{2}\ldots\left(2n_{N}+1\right)^{2}}\frac{\left(2k-1\right)!!}{\left(2k\right)!!}\,\alpha^{2k+1}.

2.2

\mathbb{W}\left({\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\frac{\arcsin^{2N+1}(z)}{z\left(2N+1\right)!}}\,\mathrm{d}z}\right)=\sum_{k>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{N}\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{\left(2k+1\right)^{3}\left(2n_{1}+1\right)^{2}\ldots\left(2n_{N}+1\right)^{2}}\,\alpha^{2k}

.

3.
Evaluation of Multiple Zeta Value
\zeta(3,2,\ldots,2)

It turns out that we can use the same method as in the previous section to evaluate multiple zeta value
\zeta(3,2,\ldots,2)
. Although this is merely a special case of Zagier’s work
[
23
, p.981, Theorem 1]
, we present our proof since our approach is quite different.

Proposition 3.1

(Borwein–Chamberland
[
2
, (1.1), (1.2)]
)
.

For
|x|\leqslant 2
and
N\geqslant 1
, we have

\frac{1}{\left(2N\right)!}\left[\arcsin{\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}\right]^{2N}=\sum_{k=1}^{{+\infty}}\frac{H_{N}(k)}{\binom{2k}{k}}\frac{x^{2k}}{k^{2}}

where
H_{1}(k)=1/4
and

H_{N+1}(k)=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\left(2n_{1}\right)^{2}}\sum_{n_{2}=1}^{n_{1}-1}\frac{1}{\left(2n_{2}\right)^{2}}\,\,\,\cdots\sum_{n_{N}=1}^{n_{N-1}-1}\frac{1}{\left(2n_{N}\right)^{2}}.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f(z)\in\mathbf{R}[[z]]
. Moreover, suppose it can be expressed in the form

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{{+\infty}}{\frac{\left(2k\right)!!}{\left(2k-1\right)!!}}\,\mathcal{C}_{2k}\,z^{2k}.

Then, the following identity holds.

\mathbb{W}f(z)=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{{+\infty}}\mathcal{C}_{2k}\,z^{2k}.

Proof.

The proof is quite similar to that of Proposition
2.2
. Notice that

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{z^{2k}}{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\left(2k-1\right)!!}{\left(2k\right)!!}

which explains the appearance of
\pi/2
in
\mathbb{W}f(z)
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For
N\geqslant 0
, we have

\zeta\left(3,\{2\}^{N}\right)

=2\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+1}\left(2j\right)\pi^{2N+2-2j}}{\left(2N+3-2j\right)!}\,\eta\left(2j+1\right)-\left(-1\right)^{N}\left[1-\left(1-2^{-2N-2}\right)\left(2N+2\right)\right]\zeta(2N+3)\right].

Proof.

From Proposition
3.1
, it follows that

\frac{1}{\left(2N+2\right)!}\left[\arcsin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right]^{2N+2}=\frac{1}{2^{2N+2}}\sum_{k>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{N}}\frac{1}{k^{2}\,n_{1}^{2}\,n_{2}^{2}\ldots n_{N}^{2}}\frac{\left(2k\right)!!}{\left(2k-1\right)!!}\left({\frac{x}{2}}\right)^{2k}.

For notational convenience, let
z=x/2
, therefore we have

\frac{\arcsin^{2N+2}(z)}{\left(2N+2\right)!}=\frac{1}{2^{2N+2}}\sum_{k>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{N}}\frac{1}{k^{2}\,n_{1}^{2}\,n_{2}^{2}\ldots n_{N}^{2}}\frac{\left(2k\right)!!}{\left(2k-1\right)!!}\,z^{2k}.

Next, we divide both sides by
z
and integrate them termwise from 0 to
\alpha
to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\frac{\arcsin^{2N+2}(z)}{z\left(2N+2\right)!}}\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{2^{2N+3}}\sum_{k>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{N}}\frac{1}{k^{3}\,n_{1}^{2}\,n_{2}^{2}\ldots n_{N}^{2}}\frac{\left(2k\right)!!}{\left(2k-1\right)!!}\,\alpha^{2k}.

Next, we use Proposition
3.2
to deduce that

\mathbb{W}\left({\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}{\frac{\arcsin^{2N+2}(z)}{z\left(2N+2\right)!}}\,\mathrm{d}z}\right)=\frac{\pi}{2^{2N+4}}\sum_{k>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{N}}\frac{1}{k^{3}\,n_{1}^{2}\,n_{2}^{2}\ldots n_{N}^{2}}\,\alpha^{2k}.

Substituting
\alpha=1
and combining Proposition
2.3
, Proposition
2.6
finally produces

\zeta\left(3,\{2\}^{N}\right)=\frac{2^{2N+4}}{\left(2N+2\right)!}\left[\dfrac{I(2N+2)}{2}-\dfrac{I(2N+3)}{\pi}\right]

=2\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+1}\left(2j\right)\pi^{2N+2-2j}}{\left(2N+3-2j\right)!}\,\eta\left(2j+1\right)-\left(-1\right)^{N}\left[1-\left(1-2^{-2N-2}\right)\left(2N+2\right)\right]\zeta(2N+3)\right]

as desired. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.3
.
∎

4.
Evaluation of Multiple Mixed Value
\mu\left(2,1,\ldots,1\right)

Definition 4.1
.

For positive integers
i_{1},i_{2}\ldots,i_{k}
, define

\mu\left(i_{k},i_{k-1},\ldots,i_{1}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{k}>n_{k-1}>\cdots>n_{1}\\
n_{j}\,\,\equiv\,\,j\,\,\textrm{(mod $2$)}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{n_{k}^{i_{k}}n_{k-1}^{i_{k-1}}\ldots n_{1}^{i_{1}}}.

Notice the little reversal of indices. If
i_{k}\geqslant 2
, this infinite sum is certainly convergent
since it is a partial sum of
\zeta(i_{k},i_{k-1},\ldots,i_{1})
.
Let us call this sum a
multiple mixed value
.

It is not so immediate to evaluate such sums.
However, quite recently, Guo–Lim–Qi in their preprint
[
8
]
, announced that they found Maclaurin series expansion for integer powers of inverse hyperbolic tangent function which is helpful in the evaluation
\mu\left(2,1,\ldots,1\right)
.

Proposition 4.2

(Guo–Lim–Qi
[
8
, (6.3)]
)
.

For
N\geqslant 0
,
\ell_{N}=k
and
|z|<1
, we have

(4.1)

\frac{\textnormal{arctanh}^{N}(z)}{N!}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\prod_{m=1}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell_{m}=0}^{\ell_{m+1}}\dfrac{1}{2\ell_{m}+m}\right)\dfrac{z^{2k+N}}{2k+N}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{N}>n_{N-1}>\cdots>n_{1}\\
n_{j}\,\,\equiv\,\,j\,\,\textnormal{(mod $2$)}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{z^{n_{N}}}{n_{N}n_{N-1}\ldots n_{1}}.

Theorem 4.3
.

For
N\geqslant 1
, the following identity holds

\mu\left(2,\{1\}^{N-1}\right)=\dfrac{2^{N+1}-1}{2^{2N}}\,\zeta(N+1).

Proof.

Integrating equation (
4.1
) with respect to
z
from
0
to
1
produces

\mu\left(2,\{1\}^{N-1}\right)=\dfrac{1}{N!}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\textrm{arctanh}^{N}(z)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Let us call this integral
K(N)
.
We can indeed evaluate
K(N)
by simply using the identity

\mathrm{arctanh}\left(z\right)=-\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1-z}{1+z}\right).

We have

K(N)=\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\mathrm{arctanh}^{N}(z)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{N}}{2^{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{1}{z}\log^{N}\left(\dfrac{1-z}{1+z}\right)\mathrm{d}z=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{N}}{2^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{N}(z)}{1-z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{N}}{2^{N-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2k}\log^{N}(z)\mathrm{d}z=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{N}}{2^{N-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{N}N!}{\left(2k+1\right)^{N+1}}=\dfrac{N!\left[2^{N+1}-1\right]}{2^{2N}}\,\zeta(N+1).

Therefore, putting all things together produces the desired result. ∎

5.
Odd Euler Sums

5.1.
Definitions

Let us begin with several definitions and notation.
Set

\mathcal{O}_{n}\left(p\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{p}}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\mathcal{B}_{n}\left(p\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2k-1)^{p}}.

Notice that

\mathcal{O}\left(p\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\infty}\left(p\right)=\left(1-2^{-p}\right)\zeta(p)\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\mathcal{B}\left(p\right)=\mathcal{B}_{\infty}\left(p\right)=\beta\left(p\right)

where
\beta
denotes the
Dirichlet beta function
. In particular,

\beta\left(2\right)=\mathcal{G}
(the Catalan constant).

Definition 5.1
.

Call each of

\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}\left(p\right)}{(2n-1)^{q}}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\,\mathcal{B}_{n}\left(p\right)}{(2n-1)^{q}}

an
odd Euler sum
.

5.2.
Reflection formula for Odd Euler Sums and Evaluation of
\mathcal{O}\left(q,q\right)
and
\mathcal{B}\left(q,q\right)

Euler sums
\{H(p,q)\}
satisfy reflection formula. Here is a natural analog for odd Euler sums.

Proposition 5.2
.

The following identities hold.

(5.1)

\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(q,p\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(p\right)\mathcal{O}\left(q\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(p+q\right),

(5.2)

\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)+\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)=\beta\left(p\right)\beta\left(q\right)+\mathcal{B}\left(p+q\right).

Proof.

Since the double sum

\sum_{k}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)^{p}\left(2n-1\right)^{q}}

is absolutely convergent, we can switch the order of summation to get

\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{{+\infty}}\left({\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)^{p}}}\right)\frac{1}{(2n-1)^{q}}=\sum_{k=1}^{{+\infty}}\sum_{n=k}^{{+\infty}}\frac{1}{\left(2n-1\right)^{q}}\frac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)^{p}}

=\sum_{k=1}^{{+\infty}}\left({\sum_{n=1}^{{+\infty}}-\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}}\right)\frac{1}{\left(2n-1\right)^{q}}\frac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)^{p}}=\sum_{k=1}^{{+\infty}}\frac{\left[\mathcal{O}\left(q\right)-\mathcal{O}_{k-1}\left(q\right)\right]}{\left(2k-1\right)^{p}}

=\mathcal{O}\left(p\right)\mathcal{O}\left(q\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{{+\infty}}\left({\mathcal{O}_{k}\left(q\right)-\frac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)^{q}}}\right)\frac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)^{p}}=\mathcal{O}\left(p\right)\mathcal{O}\left(q\right)-\mathcal{O}\left(q,p\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right).

Inserting the signs
(-1)^{k-1}
and
(-1)^{n-1}
appropriately, we can show the other one (
5.2
).
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The reflection formula enables us to evaluate odd Euler sums for
p=q
.

(5.3)

\mathcal{O}\left(q,q\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[\left(1-2^{-2q}\right)\zeta(2q)+\left(1-2^{-q}\right)^{2}\zeta(q)\,\zeta(q)\right],

(5.4)

\mathcal{B}\left(q,q\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[\left(1-2^{-2q}\right)\zeta(2q)+\beta\left(q\right)\beta\left(q\right)\right].

Example 5.4
.

The following identities hold.

\mathcal{O}\left(2,2\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left({\frac{15}{16}\,\zeta(4)+\frac{9}{16}\,\zeta^{2}(2)}\right)=\frac{5\pi^{4}}{384},\,\,\,\mathcal{B}\left(3,3\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left({\frac{31}{32}\,\zeta(6)+\beta^{2}\left(3\right)}\right)=\dfrac{1937\pi^{6}}{1935360}.

It is thus natural to ask whether it is possible to evaluate
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
or
\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)
for
p\neq q
. We spend some time discussing this question in the next two subsections.

5.3.
Evaluation of
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)

We begin by evaluating
\mathcal{O}\left(2,3\right)
by transforming
\mathcal{O}_{n}\left(p\right)
to an integral and then transforming the initial double sum
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
to a double integral followed by its reduction to single integrals involving the logarithm and polylogarithm function.

Next, we show that our approach can be generalized to obtain an integral reduction for
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
. Let’s start with a variant of
\mathcal{O}\left(2,2\right)
:

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{\left(2k+1\right)^{2}}=-\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}z^{2k}\right]\log\left(z\right)\mathrm{d}z

=-\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{1-z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z+\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{z^{2n}}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}\right]\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{1-z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=\dfrac{3}{4}\,\zeta(2)\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}-\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left(xz\right)^{2n}\log\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x\right]\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{1-z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=\dfrac{3}{4}\,\zeta(2)\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}-\int_{0}^{1}x^{2}\log\left(x\right)\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{z^{2}\log\left(z\right)}{\left(1-x^{2}z^{2}\right)\left(1-z^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x

=\dfrac{3}{4}\zeta(2)\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}-\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x\log(x)\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x+\dfrac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x\log(x)\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x^{2})}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x+\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{8}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x^{2}\log(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x

=\dfrac{3}{4}\zeta(2)\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}-\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x\log(x)\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x+\dfrac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x\log(x)\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x^{2})}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x-\dfrac{3}{4}\zeta(2)\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n+1\right)^{2}}

=\dfrac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x\log(x)\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x^{2})}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{x\log(x)\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log(x)\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x)}{1+x}\mathrm{d}x-\dfrac{7}{16}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log(x)\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x)}{1-x}\mathrm{d}x

(5.5)

=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[-\dfrac{3}{16}\,\zeta(4)\right]-\dfrac{7}{16}\left[-\dfrac{3}{4}\,\zeta(4)\right]=-\dfrac{3}{32}\,\zeta(4)+\dfrac{21}{64}\,\zeta(4)=\dfrac{15}{64}\,\zeta(4)=\frac{\pi^{4}}{384}.

Similarly, we have

\mathcal{O}\left(2,3\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n-1\right)^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)^{2}}=-\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n-1\right)^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}z^{2k}\right]\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}(x)}{x^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}x^{2n}\left(1-z^{2n}\right)\right]\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{1-z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x

=-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{1-x^{2}z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1-x^{2}\right)}\mathrm{d}x-\dfrac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(-x\right)}{x\left(1-x^{2}\right)}\mathrm{d}x

(5.6)

=\dfrac{1}{4}\left(\dfrac{11}{16}\,\zeta(5)+\dfrac{3}{4}\,\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3)\right)-\dfrac{1}{4}\left(-\dfrac{5}{4}\,\zeta(5)-\dfrac{3}{8}\,\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3)\right)=\dfrac{31}{64}\,\zeta(5)+\dfrac{9}{32}\,\zeta(3)\,\zeta(2).

This approach can be easily generalized to the following.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)
represent the polylogarithm, then we have

\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{2\left(q-1\right)!}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(-x\right)}{x\left(1-x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1-x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right].

Proof.

As earlier, we begin by transforming
\mathcal{O}_{n}\left(p\right)
to an integral

\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n-1\right)^{q}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{\left(2k-1\right)^{p}}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\left(2n-1\right)^{q}}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{p-1}}{\left(p-1\right)!}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}z^{2k}\right]\dfrac{\log^{p-1}(z)}{z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q-1}}{\left(q-1\right)!}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{p-1}}{\left(p-1\right)!}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}(x)}{x^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}x^{2n}\left(1-z^{2n}\right)\right]\dfrac{\log^{p-1}(z)}{1-z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q-1}}{\left(q-1\right)!}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{p-1}}{\left(p-1\right)!}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{p-1}(z)}{1-x^{2}z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q-1}}{2\left(q-1\right)!}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}(x)}{1-x^{2}}\left[\dfrac{\left[\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(x\right)-\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(-x\right)\right]}{x}\right]\mathrm{d}x

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{2\left(q-1\right)!}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(-x\right)}{x\left(1-x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1-x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]

as desired.
∎

\mathcal{O}\left(3,4\right)=\dfrac{\pi^{4}}{128}\,\zeta(3)-\dfrac{5\pi^{2}}{128}\,\zeta(5)+\dfrac{127}{256}\,\zeta(7),

\mathcal{O}\left(4,5\right)=\dfrac{5\pi^{4}}{3072}\,\zeta(5)+\dfrac{105\pi^{2}}{3072}\,\zeta(7)+\dfrac{511}{1024}\,\zeta(9),

\mathcal{O}\left(5,6\right)=\dfrac{\pi^{6}}{1024}\,\zeta(5)-\dfrac{7\pi^{4}}{4096}\,\zeta(7)-\dfrac{63\pi^{2}}{2048}\,\zeta(9)+\dfrac{2047}{4096}\,\zeta(11),

\mathcal{O}\left(6,7\right)=\dfrac{7\pi^{6}}{122880}\,\zeta(7)+\dfrac{7\pi^{4}}{4096}\,\zeta(9)+\dfrac{231\pi^{2}}{8192}\,\zeta(11)+\dfrac{8191}{16384}\,\zeta(13).

Consequently, using the reflection formula we have

\mathcal{O}\left(4,3\right)=\dfrac{\pi^{4}}{728}\,\zeta(3)+\dfrac{5\pi^{2}}{128}\,\zeta(5)+\dfrac{127}{256}\,\zeta(7),

\mathcal{O}\left(5,4\right)=\dfrac{13\pi^{4}}{1536}\,\zeta(5)-\dfrac{105\pi^{2}}{3072}\,\zeta(7)+\dfrac{511}{1024}\,\zeta(9),

\mathcal{O}\left(6,5\right)=\dfrac{\pi^{6}}{30720}\,\zeta(5)+\dfrac{7\pi^{4}}{4096}\,\zeta(7)+\dfrac{63\pi^{2}}{2048}\,\zeta(9)+\dfrac{2047}{4096}\,\zeta(11),

\mathcal{O}\left(7,6\right)=\dfrac{\pi^{6}}{1024}\,\zeta(7)-\dfrac{7\pi^{4}}{4096}\,\zeta(9)-\dfrac{231\pi^{2}}{8192}\,\zeta(11)+\dfrac{8191}{16384}\,\zeta(13).

However, note that in these specific values of
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
, no simple pattern can be discerned.

5.4.
Evaluation of
\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)

We begin by evaluating
\mathcal{B}\left(2,3\right)
the same way we evaluated
\mathcal{O}\left(2,3\right)
in the previous subsection. Next, we show that our approach can be generalized to obtain an integral reduction for
\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)
. Let’s begin with
\mathcal{B}\left(2,3\right)
:

\mathcal{B}\left(2,3\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n-1}}{\left(2n-1\right)^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{k-1}}{\left(2k-1\right)^{2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n-1}}{\left(2n-1\right)^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(-z^{2}\right)^{k}\right]\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n-1}}{\left(2n-1\right)^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\left(-z^{2}\right)^{n}\log\left(z\right)}{1+z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z-\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n-1}}{\left(2n-1\right)^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{1+z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)}{x^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left(-x^{2}\right)^{n}\left(-z^{2}\right)^{n}\right]\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{1+z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x+\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{32}

=-\dfrac{\mathcal{G}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)}{1+x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\dfrac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x^{2}\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{32}

=\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{32}-\dfrac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x^{2}\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{32}=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x

-\dfrac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x^{2}\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x-\dfrac{1}{64}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x

Some routine manipulations produce

(5.7)

\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{83}{8}\,\zeta(5)-\dfrac{9}{2}\,\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3).

Next, we have

\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{2}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)\log^{2}\left(x\right)}{\left(1-xz\right)\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}z

=-2\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{z\log\left(z\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(z\right)}{1+z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z-\dfrac{3}{16}\,\zeta(3)\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{z\log\left(z\right)}{1+z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z-\dfrac{\pi^{3}}{16}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)}{1+z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log\left(1+z^{2}\right)\mathrm{Li}_{3}\left(z\right)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z-\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log\left(z\right)\log\left(1+z^{2}\right)\mathrm{Li}_{2}\left(z\right)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z+\dfrac{3}{128}\,\zeta(3)\,\zeta(2)+\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{16}

=\dfrac{1}{8}\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n}H_{2n}}{n^{4}}+\dfrac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n}H^{\left(2\right)}_{2n}}{n^{3}}+\dfrac{1}{4}\,\zeta(2)\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n+1}}{n^{3}}+\dfrac{1}{2}\,\zeta(3)\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n+1}}{n^{2}}+\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{16}

(5.8)

+\dfrac{3}{128}\,\zeta(3)\,\zeta(2)=\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{16}-\dfrac{9}{16}\,\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3)+\dfrac{331}{256}\,\zeta(5)=\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{16}-\dfrac{3\pi^{2}}{32}\,\zeta(3)+\dfrac{331}{256}\,\zeta(5)

where the arising non-linear alternating harmonic series are evaluated by C. Vălean in
[
15
]
:

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n-1}H_{2n}^{(2)}}{n^{3}}=\frac{61\pi^{2}}{192}\,\zeta(3)+\frac{1973}{128}\zeta(5)+\frac{\pi^{5}}{16}-\frac{\pi}{128}\,\psi^{(3)}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n-1}H_{2n}}{n^{4}}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\,\zeta(3)-\frac{437}{64}\,\zeta(5)-\frac{\pi^{5}}{24}+\frac{\pi}{192}\,\psi^{(3)}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)

Putting all things together produces

\mathcal{B}\left(2,3\right)=\dfrac{331}{512}\,\zeta(5)-\dfrac{83}{512}\,\zeta(5)+\dfrac{3\pi^{2}}{256}\,\zeta(3)-\dfrac{3\pi^{2}}{64}\,\zeta(3)+\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{32}=\dfrac{31}{64}\,\zeta(5)-\dfrac{9\pi^{2}}{256}\,\zeta(3)+\dfrac{\mathcal{G}\pi^{3}}{32}

where
\mathcal{G}
is the Catalan constant. This approach can be easily generalized to the following.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathrm{Li}_{n}(z)
represent the polylogarithm, then we have

\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{2\left(q-1\right)!}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(-x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right].

Proof.

The proof is quite similar to that of Theorem
5.5
, we have

\mathcal{B}\left(p,q\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{\left(2n-1\right)^{q}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{\left(2k-1\right)^{p}}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{\left(2n-1\right)^{q}}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{p-1}}{\left(p-1\right)!}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(-z^{2}\right)^{k}\right]\dfrac{\log^{p-1}\left(z\right)}{z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{\left(q-1\right)!}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{p}}{\left(p-1\right)!}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)}{x^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left(-x^{2}\right)^{n}\left(\left(-z^{2}\right)^{n}-1\right)\right]\dfrac{\log^{p-1}\left(z\right)}{1+z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{\left(q-1\right)!}\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{p}}{\left(p-1\right)!}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)}{1+x^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{p-1}\left(z\right)}{1-x^{2}z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q-1}}{2\left(q-1\right)!}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}(x)}{1+x^{2}}\left[\dfrac{\left[\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(x\right)-\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(-x\right)\right]}{x}\right]\mathrm{d}x

=\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{2\left(q-1\right)!}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(-x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(x\right)}{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]

as desired.
∎

6.
Further Research and Acknowledgements

Several paths have not been explored yet and will be the subject of future work. Here, we record some ideas for our future research.

(1) We can regard our inverse sine integral as a
log-sine integral
:

I(n)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\arcsin^{n}(z)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z=-n\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(z)\arcsin^{n-1}(z)}{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}z=-n\int_{0}^{\pi/2}{z^{n-1}\log(\sin(z))}\,\mathrm{d}z.

We refer the reader to Borwein–Broadhurst–Kamnitzer
[
3
]
and Williams-Yue
[
22
]
for relations of such log-sine integrals with central binomial series. Notice that they discussed

\int_{0}^{\pi/3}\log^{\alpha}\left({2\sin\left(\frac{\vartheta}{2}\right)}\right)\vartheta^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta\quad\left(\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0\right)

which is not quite same to ours but it should be possible to relate
I(n)
with other central binomial series. This may serve as a subject of future work.

(2) Together with multiple zeta values and Euler sums evaluated in this article, we can compute other MZVs. For example, we may show that

(6.1)

\zeta(3,1,1)=2\,\zeta(5)-\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3).

Indeed,
Vălean
[
18
, page 303, problem 4.44]
shows that

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{H_{n}}{n}\left(\zeta(3)-1-\dfrac{1}{2^{3}}-\dfrac{1}{3^{3}}-\cdots-\dfrac{1}{n^{3}}\right)=2\,\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3)-\dfrac{7}{2}\,\zeta(5)

where
H_{n}
is the
n
-th Harmonic number. Notice that the left–hand side is equal to

\sum_{m>n\geqslant k}\dfrac{1}{m^{3}nk}=\sum_{m>n>k}\dfrac{1}{m^{3}nk}+\sum_{m>n=k}\dfrac{1}{m^{3}nk}=\zeta(3,1,1)+\zeta(3,2)

Hence, with
\zeta(3,2)
we just found above (
3.4
), we deduce that

\zeta(3,1,1)=2\,\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3)-3\,\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3)-\dfrac{7}{2}\,\zeta(5)+\dfrac{11}{2}\,\zeta(5)=2\thinspace\zeta(5)-\zeta(2)\,\zeta(3).

(3) For a multi-index

\mathbf{i}=(a_{1}+1,\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{b_{1}-1},a_{2}+1,\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{b_{2}-1},\dots,a_{k}+1,\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{b_{k}-1}),

with
a_{i},b_{i}\geqslant 1
, we define its
dual
to be

\mathbf{i}^{\dagger}=(b_{k}+1,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{a_{k}-1},b_{k-1}+1,\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{a_{k-1}-1},\dots,b_{1}+1,\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{a_{1}-1}).

Duality formula for MZVs claims that

\zeta(\mathbf{i})=\zeta(\mathbf{i}^{\dagger})
for all such indices.

In particular,
\zeta(2,\{1\}^{n-1})=\zeta(n+1)
.
After the preparation of this manuscript, we found out that
Kaneko and Tsumura
[
12
]
introduced
a
multiple
T
-value

T\left(i_{k},i_{k-1},\ldots,i_{1}\right)=2^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{k}>n_{k-1}>\cdots>n_{1}\\
n_{j}\,\,\equiv\,\,j\,\,\textrm{(mod $2$)}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{n_{k}^{i_{k}}n_{k-1}^{i_{k-1}}\ldots n_{1}^{i_{1}}}

with the factor
2^{k}
for normalization (we changed the sum convention to ours). They further showed that exactly the same duality formula hold for multiple
T
-values.
In particular,
T(2,\{1\}^{n-1})=T(n+1)
.
This is equivalent to Theorem
4.3
, thus, we gave another proof of a special case of
T
-duality by integration of powers of inverse hyperbolic tangent function.

(4) These duality formulas come from
iterated integral expressions
for multiple zeta values with two kinds of integrals:

\int\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{1-z}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\int\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}.

For example, the famous Euler-Goldbach relation
\zeta(2,1)=\zeta(3)
is nothing but

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{3}}{x_{3}}\int_{0}^{x_{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{1-x_{2}}\int_{0}^{x_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{1-x_{1}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{3}}{z_{3}}\int_{0}^{z_{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{2}}{z_{2}}\int_{0}^{z_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{1}}{1-z_{1}}.

Indeed, it should be possible to bring this idea of iterated integrals into the
study of multiple
t
-values, multiple zeta values and central binomial series with more integrals in the form

\int\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{1-z^{2}},\int\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z\left(1-z^{2}\right)}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\int\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}.

Definition
2.1
, Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
3.2
implicitly highlight this little idea.

Notice that there exist iterated integral expressions for integer powers of inverse sine function and inverse hyperbolic tangent function:

\frac{\arcsin^{n}(z)}{n!}=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{1}}{\sqrt{1-z_{1}^{2}}}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{2}}{\sqrt{1-z_{2}^{2}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{z_{n-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{n}}{\sqrt{1-z_{n}^{2}}}

as remarked in
[
2
, (4.1)]
. Quite similarly, we can show that

\frac{\text{arctanh}^{n}(z)}{n!}=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{1}}{{1-z_{1}^{2}}}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{2}}{{1-z_{2}^{2}}}\cdots\int_{0}^{z_{n-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{n}}{{1-z_{n}^{2}}}.

Thus, as observed above, the equality

t\left(2,1\right)=\frac{I(2)}{2!}=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\arcsin^{2}(z)}{z}\,\mathrm{d}z

implies the relation of iterated integrals

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{3}}{x_{3}}\int_{0}^{x_{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}\left(1-x_{2}^{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{x_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{1-x_{1}^{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{3}}{z_{3}}\int_{0}^{z_{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{2}}{\sqrt{1-z_{2}^{2}}}\int_{0}^{z_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{1}}{\sqrt{1-z_{1}^{2}}}

which is quite remarkable and not so obvious at a first glance. This can be indeed extended and we wish to study more about such iterated integral relations at some another opportunity.

(5) It remains an open problem to compute a closed expression for the integrals

\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(z\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(z\right)}{z\left(1+z^{2}\right)}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\log^{q-1}\left(z\right)\mathrm{Li}_{p}\left(z\right)}{z\left(1-z^{2}\right)}

as it would allow us to compute
\mathcal{O}\left(p,q\right)
(Theorem
5.5
).

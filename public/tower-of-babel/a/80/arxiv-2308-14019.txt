An upper bound on stability of powers of matroidal ideals
Mozhgan Koolani, Amir Mafi* and Parasto Soufivand

Abstract
. Let R = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] be a polynomial ring in n n variables over a field K K and I I be a matroidal ideal of degree d d . Let astab ⁡ ( I ) \operatorname{astab}(I) and dstab ⁡ ( I ) \operatorname{dstab}(I) be the smallest integers l l and k k , for which Ass ⁡ ( I l ) \operatorname{Ass}(I^{l}) and depth ⁡ ( R / I k ) \operatorname{depth}(R/I^{k}) stabilize, respectively. In this paper, we show that astab ⁡ ( I ) , dstab ⁡ ( I ) ≤ min ⁡ { d , ℓ ⁡ ( I ) } \operatorname{astab}(I),\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq\operatorname{min}\{d,\ell(I)\} , where ℓ ⁡ ( I ) \ell(I) is the analytic spread of I I . Furthermore, by a counterexample we give a negative answer to the conjecture of Herzog and Qureshi [ 11 ] about stability of matroidal ideals.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
I
is a monomial ideal of
R
such that
G(I)
is the unique minimal monomial generators set of
I
.
Let
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
be the set of associated prime ideals of
R/I
. Brodmann
[
2
]
showed that there exists an integer
l
such that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{t})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{l})
for all
t\geq l
. The smallest such integer
l
is
called the
index of Ass-stability
of
I
, and denoted by
\operatorname{astab}(I)
. Moreover,
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{l})
is called the stable set of associated
prime ideals of
I
and it is denoted by
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Brodmann
[
3
]
also showed that there exists an integer
k
such that
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{s}=\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k}
for all
s\geq k
. The smallest such integer
k
is called the
index of depth stability
of
I
and denoted by
\operatorname{dstab}(I)
.
Herzog and the second author
[
10
]
proved that if
n=3
then, for any graded ideal
I
of
R
,
\operatorname{astab}(I)=\operatorname{dstab}(I)
. Also, they showed that for
n=4
the indices
\operatorname{astab}(I)
and
\operatorname{dstab}(I)
, in general, are unrelated.

A monomial ideal
I
generated in a single degree is
polymatroidal ideal
when it is satisfying in the following exchange property: for all monomials
u,v\in G(I)
with
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i}}(u)>\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i}}(v)
, there exists an index

such that
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{j}}(v)>\operatorname{deg}_{x_{j}}(u)
and
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
(see
[
7
]
or
[
9
]
). A square-free polymatroidal ideal is called a

.

The polymatroidal ideals have interesting properties, we recall some of useful properties which is needed in this paper:
(1) The product of polymatroidal ideals is again polymatroidal (see
[
5
, Theorem 5.3]
), in particular each power of a polymatroidal ideal is polymatroidal; (2)
I
is a polymatroidal ideal if and only if
(I:u)
is a polymatroidal ideal for all monomials

(see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
); (3) if
I
is a matroidal ideal of degree
d
, then
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=d-1
and
\operatorname{pd}(R/I)=n-d+1
(see
[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
or
[
18
, Theorem 2.3]
); (4) according to
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
, every polymatroidal ideal satisfying the persistence property and non-increasing depth functions, that is, if
I
is a polymatroidal ideal then, for all
l,k
, there is the following sequences:
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{l})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I^{l+1})
and
\operatorname{depth}(R/I^{k+1})\leq\operatorname{depth}(R/I^{k})
, these properties, in general, for all square-free monomial ideals has a negative answer (see
[
17
, Example 2.9]

I
, we have
\operatorname{astab}(I),\operatorname{dstab}(I)<\ell(I)
, where
\ell(I)
is the analytic spread of

(see
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]

(6) if

[
12
, Corollary 3.5]

\operatorname{astab}(I)=1
if and only if
\operatorname{dstab}(I)=1
(see
[
16

).

Herzog and Qureshi
[
11
]
raised the following conjectured:

Conjecture 0.1

Let

be any polymatroidal ideal. Does

This conjecture in the following cases has a positive answer: (1) if

[

The following question asked by Karimi and the second author
[
15
]
:

Question 0.2

Let
I
be any matroidal ideal. Then can we have
\operatorname{astab}(I),\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq d

If

[

In this paper, by a counterexample, we show that the Conjecture

\operatorname{astab}(I),\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq d

Definition 1.1
.

[

Let

. Then
linear relation graph

E(\Gamma)=\{\{i,j\}:
there exist

Following
[
11
]
, let

be a finite simple graph with vertex set

and edge set

. We denote by

the number of connected components of

, and for a subset

we denote by

the graph restricted to the vertex set

. In particular, for each vertex

we set

. A vertex

of

is called a

if

. A connected graph with no cutpoints is called

. We have the following fact from graph theory:

A maximal biconnected subgraph of

is called a biconnected component of
G
. Let
G_{1},\dots,G_{t}
be the biconnected components of

. Then
G=\cup_{i=1}^{t}G_{i}
and each two distinct biconnected components intersect at most in a cutpoint. If

is a biconnected, then each two distinct edges belong to a cycle.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
I
be a matroidal ideal attached to the graph matroid of
G
. Then
m\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
if and only if the linear relation graph
\Gamma
of
I
is a complete graph.

Proof.

(\Longleftarrow)
. Suppose that the linear relation graph
\Gamma
of
I
is a complete graph. Then by
[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
we have
\ell(I)=\mid E(G)\mid
. Since
I
is a matroidal ideal attached to the graph matroid of
G
, it follows
\mid E(G)\mid=n
. Therefore by
[
11
, Corollary 1.6]
we conclude
m\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
.

(\Longrightarrow)
. Let
G
be a graph and
I\subset K[x_{i}|e_{i}\in E(G)]
be a matroidal ideal attached to the graph matroid of
G
such that
m\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then
G
is a biconnected graph and

is generated by monomials
u_{F}=\prod_{e_{i}\in E(F)}x_{i}
, where
F
is a spanning forest of
G
, i.e., a subgraph of

which is a forest and for which
V(F)=V(G)
. From the definition of
\Gamma
we have
\{i,j\}
is an edge of
\Gamma
if there exists a spanning forest

with
e_{i}\in E(F)
such that
(E(F)\setminus\{e_{i}\})\cup\{e_{j}\}
is also a spanning forest of
G
. If
e_{i},e_{j}\in E(G)
, then there is a cycle

in
G
which is
e_{i},e_{j}\in E(C)
. Therefore we can construct a spanning forest

of

such that

is contained in

and
(E(F)\setminus\{e_{i}\})\cup\{e_{j}\}
is again a spanning tree of

and so

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
I
be a matroidal ideal of degree
d
and
A_{i}=\{u\in G(I)|\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i}}(u)=1\}
. Then

Proof.

By
[

Now, we use induction on

is a variable such that

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
I
be a matroidal ideal of degree
d
. If
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, then
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{d}=0
. In particular,
\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq d
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Therefore, by Lemma
1.2
, it follows
\Gamma_{I}
is a connected complete graph. Since for every
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, we have
\{i,j\}\in\Gamma_{I}
and also by Lemma
1.3
we may assume that
|A_{i_{1}}|\geq|A_{i_{2}}|\geq\ldots\geq|A_{i_{2d-1}}|\geq|A_{i_{j}}|\geq d
for all
2d-1<j\leq n
. We may set
u=(x_{i_{1}}\ldots x_{i_{d}})^{d-1}x_{i_{d+1}}\ldots x_{i_{2d-1}}
. If
d+t=n
for some
1\leq t\leq d-1
, then we consider
x_{i_{d+t}}=\ldots=x_{i_{2d-1}}=x_{n}
. Since
I
is the matroidal ideal attached to the graphic matroid of
G
is generated by the monomials
u_{F}=\prod_{e_{i}\in E(F)}x_{i}
, where
F
is a spanning forest of
G
, it follows that
x_{i_{1}}\ldots x_{i_{d}}\in I
. Again, by definition of
\Gamma_{I}
, we have
\{i,j\}\in\Gamma_{I}
for every
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
and so for the spanning forest
F
with
e_{i_{t}}\in E(F)
for all
1\leq t\leq d
we have
(E(F)\setminus\{e_{i_{t}}\})\cup\{e_{i_{j}}\}
is also a spanning forest of
G
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Thus
(x_{i_{1}}\ldots\widehat{x_{i_{t}}}\ldots x_{i_{d}})x_{i_{j}}\in I
for all

I^{d}:u=\mathfrak{m}
and so
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{d})
and
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{d}=0

\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq d

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
I
be a matroidal ideal of degree
d
. Then
\operatorname{astab}(I)\leq d
. In particular, if
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, then
\operatorname{astab}(I)\leq d-1
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I(\mathfrak{p}))
.

Hence by Theorem
1.4

\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}/I(\mathfrak{p})^{\operatorname{deg}(I(\mathfrak{p}))}=0
. Since
\operatorname{deg}(I(\mathfrak{p}))\leq\operatorname{deg}(I)=d
, it follows that

\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}/I(\mathfrak{p})^{d}=0
. Therefore
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{d}(\mathfrak{p}))
and so
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{d})
. Thus
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{d})
and so
\operatorname{astab}(I)\leq d
.
Suppose
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then we have
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{d})=\cup_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Ass}(I[i]^{d})
, (see
[
19
, Remark]
or
[
15
, Remark 2.6]
).
From the first proof we conclude that
\operatorname{astab}(I[i])\leq d-1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. It therefore follows
\operatorname{astab}(I)\leq d-1
, as required.

∎

One of the most distinguished polymatroidal ideals is the ideal of Veronese type. For the fixed positive integers

and

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
I
be a matroidal ideal of degree

. Then
I\subseteq I_{1}\cap\ldots\cap I_{s}
, where
I_{1},\ldots,I_{s}
are Veronese type matroidal ideals with
\operatorname{deg}(I_{i})=d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s

d=d_{1}+\ldots+d_{s}
and also generated by the set of product of vertices of the connected components
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{s}
of
\Gamma_{I}
.

Proof.

By
[
13
, Proposition 2.3]
, we may assume that
s\leq d
and
V(\Gamma_{I})=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. If
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
then, by Lemma
1.2
,
\Gamma_{I}
is a complete graph and it is clear that
I
is a subset of the Veronese type matroidal ideal of degree
d
. Hence we may assume that
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
and so

by
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
. Now, suppose that there exists

such that
I\not\subset I_{i}
. For simplicity, we may assume that

and
\operatorname{supp}(I_{1})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\}
, where

. Thus there are monomials

such that

and
\operatorname{supp}(v)\cap\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\}=\emptyset

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
I
be a matroidal ideal. Then we we have the following:

(1)

\operatorname{min}\{\operatorname{depth}R/I^{t}\mid t\geq 1\}=s-1
, where
s
is the number of connected component of
\Gamma_{I}
.

(2)

\operatorname{dstab}(I)=\operatorname{min}\{t\geq 1\mid\operatorname{depth}R/I^{t}=s-1\}
.

Proof.

By
[
13
, Proposition 2.3]
and
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
, we have
V(\Gamma_{I})=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\ell(I)=n-s+1
. Now by using
[
12
, Corollary 3.5]
we have
\operatorname{min}\{\operatorname{depth}R/I^{t}\mid t\geq 1\}=s-1
.
By definition and
[
12
, Corollary 3.5]
we have
\operatorname{dstab}(I)=\operatorname{min}\{t\geq 1\mid\operatorname{depth}R/I^{t}=n-\ell(I)\}
. Again by
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
we conclude that
\operatorname{dstab}(I)=\operatorname{min}\{t\geq 1\mid\operatorname{depth}R/I^{t}=s-1\}
, as required.

∎

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
I
be matroidal ideal of degree
d
such that
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then
\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq d-1
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, by Lemma
1.7
we have
I\subseteq I_{1}\cap\ldots\cap I_{s}
such that
I_{1},\ldots,I_{s}
are Veronese type matroidal ideals with
\operatorname{deg}(I_{i})=d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s
and
d=d_{1}+\ldots+d_{s}
also generated by the set of product of vertices of the connected components
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{s}
of
\Gamma_{I}
. It is clear that
I\subseteq\prod_{j=1}^{s}I_{j}
and so by exchange property for each
u\in G(I)
we have
u=\prod_{j=1}^{s}u_{j}
such that
u_{j}\in G(I_{j})
. Therefore we may consider that
I=\prod_{j=1}^{s}J_{j}
such that
J_{j}\subseteq I_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq s
. Thus by using
[
14
, Lemma 2.2]
and Theorem
1.4
we have
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{l}=s-1
for some
l<d
. Therefore
\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq d-1
, as required.

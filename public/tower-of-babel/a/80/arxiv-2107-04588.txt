Super Topological Recursion and Gaiotto Vectors For Superconformal Blocks
Kento Osuga 1,2

Abstract
. We investigate a relation between the super topological recursion and Gaiotto vectors for 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 superconformal blocks. Concretely, we introduce the notion of the untwisted and μ \mu -twisted super topological recursion, and construct a dual algebraic description in terms of super Airy structures. We then show that the partition function of an appropriate super Airy structure coincides with the Gaiotto vector for 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 superconformal blocks in the Neveu-Schwarz or Ramond sector. Equivalently, the Gaiotto vector can be computed by the untwisted or μ \mu -twisted super topological recursion. This implies that the framework of the super topological recursion– equivalently super Airy structures – can be applied to compute the Nekrasov partition function of 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 pure U ⁡ ( 2 ) U(2) supersymmetric gauge theory on ℂ 2 / ℤ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2} via a conjectural extension of the Alday-Gaiotto-Tachikawa correspondence.

2.1.
Untwisted/
\mu
-twisted Super Spectral Curves

There are three symplectic vector spaces underlying untwisted/
\mu
-twisted super spectral curves, namely, the vector space for

(1)

bosons
V_{z}^{B}
,

(2)

untwisted fermions
V_{z,\theta}^{NS}
,

(3)

\mu
-twisted fermions
V_{z,\theta}^{R}
.

Since untwisted/
\mu
-twisted fermions correspond to fermions in the Neveu-Schwarz/Ramond sector respectively, we often denote them by NS/R instead.

We start with the vector space
V_{z}^{B}
for bosons defined in the previous section:

2.1.1.
Vector Space for Bosons

Let us define
V_{z}^{B}
by

(2.1)

V_{z}^{B}:=\{\;\omega\in\mathbb{C}[z^{-1},z\rrbracket dz\;\;|\;\;\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{\text{Res}}\,\omega(z)=0\;\},

equipped with the following symplectic pairing
\Omega^{B}:V_{z}^{B}\times V_{z}^{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
:

(2.2)

df_{1},df_{2}\in V_{z}^{B},\;\;\;\;\Omega^{B}(df_{1},df_{2})=\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{\text{Res}}f_{1}(z)df_{2}(z).

Note that
\Omega^{B}
makes sense because no vector in
V_{z}^{B}
has residues. We then define a Lagrangian subspace
V_{z}^{B+}=\mathbb{C}\llbracket z\rrbracket dz\subset V_{z}^{B}
, with a choice of a basis
(d\xi_{l})_{l>0}
as

(2.3)

d\xi_{l}(z):=z^{l-1}dz,\;\;\;\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

We consider another Lagrangian subspace
V_{z}^{B-}\subset V_{z}^{B}
complementary to
V_{z}^{B+}
, but this is not unique, and there is a choice of so-called “bosonic polarization parameters”
\phi_{lm}=\phi_{ml}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If we denote by
(d\xi_{-l})_{l>0}
a basis of
V_{z}^{B-}
, bosonic polarization parameters
\phi_{lm}
appear in
(d\xi_{-l})_{l>0}
as:

(2.4)

d\xi_{-l}(z)=\frac{dz}{z^{l+1}}+\sum_{m>0}\frac{\phi_{lm}}{l}d\xi_{m}(z),\;\;\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

One can easily check

(2.5)

\forall k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0},\;\;\;\;\Omega^{B}(d\xi_{k},d\xi_{l})=\frac{\delta_{k+l,0}}{k},

hence
V_{z}^{B-}
is indeed complementary to
V_{z}^{B+}
. One may find (
2.5
) similar to commutation relations in the Heisenberg algebra.

The choice of polarization can be encoded into a formal symmetry bidifferential
\omega_{0,2|0}
as follows,

(2.6)

\omega_{0,2|0}(z_{1},z_{2}|)=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}+\sum_{k,l>0}\phi_{kl}\;d\xi_{k}(z_{1})d\xi_{l}(z_{2}).

In the domain
|z_{1}|>|z_{2}|
, the basis of
V_{z}^{B\pm}
appears in the expansion of
\omega_{0,2|0}(z_{1},z_{2}|)
as

(2.7)

\omega_{0,2|0}(z_{1},z_{2}|)=\sum_{l\geq 1}ld\xi_{-l}(z_{1})d\xi_{l}(z_{2}).

In order to define a spectral curve without branch covers, we further consider a one-dimensional vector space
V_{z}^{B\,0}
and chose its basis
d\xi_{0}
by

(2.8)

d\xi_{0}(z)=\frac{dz}{z}.

Note that
d\xi_{0}\not\in V_{z}^{B}
. It does not make sense to apply the definition of the symplectic form (
2.2
) to
\tilde{V}_{z}^{B}=V_{z}^{B}\oplus V^{B\,0}_{z}
because
\int d\xi_{0}=\log z
. We can still formally define a degenerate symplectic form
\tilde{\Omega}^{B}:\tilde{V}_{z}^{B}\times\tilde{V}_{z}^{B}\to\mathbb{C}
such that if
k,l\neq 0
then
\tilde{\Omega}^{B}(d\xi_{k},d\xi_{l})
follows (
2.2
) and
\tilde{\Omega}^{B}(d\xi_{0},d\xi_{k})=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, one can interpret that
d\xi_{0}\in V_{z}^{B\,0}
plays the same role as the Heisenberg zero mode.

We now define a spectral curve without branch covers:

Definition 2.1
.

A
spectral curve with one component without branch covers
consists of a degenerate symplectic vector space
\tilde{V}_{z}^{B}
with a maximal isotropic subspace
V_{z}^{B+}
, and the following data:

•

a choice of “dilaton shift parameters”
(\tau_{l})_{l\geq-(N-1)}
with
\tau_{N-1}\neq 0
and

which can be encoded in a choice of a one-form
\omega_{0,1|0}\in\tilde{V}^{B}_{z}
:

(2.9)

\omega_{0,1|0}(z)=\sum_{l\geq-(N-1)}\tau_{l}d\xi_{l}(z),

•

a choice of “bosonic polarization parameters”, which can be encoded in a choice of a symmetric bidifferential
\omega_{0,2|0}
:

(2.10)

\omega_{0,2|0}(z_{1},z_{2}|)=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}+\sum_{k,l>0}\phi_{kl}\;d\xi_{k}(z_{1})d\xi_{l}(z_{2}).

•

a choice of “
D
-terms”
D_{k}
for

which can be encoded in a choice of a one-form
\omega_{1,1}\in\tilde{V}^{B-}_{z}

•

a choice of “crosscap parameters”
(Q_{l})_{l\geq-(N-1)}
with

which can be encoded in a choice of a one-form
\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1|0}\in V^{B}_{z}

(2.12)

\mathbb{C}^{1|1}_{z|\theta}

For untwisted fermions, i.e., fermions in the Neveu-Schwarz sector, it turns out that such a naive exchange of spin-statistics works for untwisted fermions, and we will construct a vector space that admits these properties.

The vector space

(2.13)

V^{NS}_{z,\theta}:=\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}[z^{-1},z\rrbracket\;\Theta^{NS}(z,\theta)\},

(2.14)

\Theta^{NS}(z,\theta):=\left(\theta+dz\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right),

\Omega^{F}:V^{F}_{z,\theta}\times V^{F}_{z,\theta}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

(2.15)

\Omega^{NS}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}):=\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{\text{Res}}\;\eta_{1}(z,\theta)\eta_{2}(z,\theta),

We often denote
\Theta^{NS}(z,\theta)
and
\Theta^{NS}(z_{i},\theta_{i})

\Theta^{NS}_{z}
and
\Theta^{NS}_{i}

, hence,

of rank
0|1

and

V_{z}^{NS+}=\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}\llbracket z\rrbracket\,\Theta_{z}^{NS}\}
, and we fix its basis

(2.16)

\eta_{l+\frac{1}{2}}(z,\theta):=z^{l}\,\Theta_{z},\;\;\;\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Let

be another Lagrangian subspace complementary to

, then its basis

\eta_{-l-\frac{1}{2}}(z,\theta):=\left(\frac{1}{z^{l+1}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}\psi^{NS}_{lk}z^{k}\right)\,\Theta,\;\;\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Note that we have

(2.18)

\forall k,l\in\mathbb{Z},\;\;\;\;\Omega^{NS}(\eta_{k+\frac{1}{2}},\eta_{l-\frac{1}{2}})=\delta_{k+l,0},

which resembles commutation relations in the Clifford algebra. Analogous to

\omega^{NS}_{0,0|2}(|z_{1},z_{2})=-\frac{\Theta^{NS}_{1}\Theta^{NS}_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})}+\sum_{k,l\geq 0}\psi^{NS}_{kl}\;d\eta_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(z_{1})d\eta_{l+\frac{1}{2}}(z_{2}).

, the basis of
V_{z}^{NS\pm}
appears in the expansion of
\omega_{0,0|2}^{NS}(|z_{1},z_{2})

\omega^{NS}_{0,0|2}(|z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{l\geq 0}\eta_{l+\frac{1}{2}}(z_{1})\eta_{-l-\frac{1}{2}}(z_{2}).

•

a choice of “crosscap parameters”
(Q_{l})_{l\geq-(N-1)}
with

which can be encoded in a choice of a one-form

\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1|0}(z|)=\sum_{l\geq-(N-1)}Q_{l}d\xi_{l}(z),

•

a choice of “untwisted polarization parameters”, which can be encoded in a choice of an antisymmetric bidifferential

2.1.3.
Vector Spaces for
\mu
-twisted Fermions

At last we construct the vector space

\mu
-twisted fermions, which already appeared in

]
. Although some aspects of

For

\Omega^{R}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}):=\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{\text{Res}}\;\eta_{1}(z,\theta)\eta_{2}(z,\theta),

We again denote
\Theta^{R}(z,\theta)
and

and

As explained in

\eta_{l}(z,\theta):=z^{l-1}\,\Theta^{R}_{z},\;\;\;\;\;l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

Next, the zero mode space

(\eta_{0})

\eta_{0}(z,\theta):=\left(\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{k>0}\psi^{R}_{0k}z^{k-1}\right)\,\Theta_{z}^{R},

where

\psi_{00}^{R}=0,\;\;\;\;\psi^{R}_{kl}+\psi^{R}_{lk}+\psi^{R}_{0k}\psi^{R}_{0l}=0,\;\;\;\;\forall k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

One can easily check that

\forall k,l\in\mathbb{Z},\;\;\;\;\Omega^{R}(\eta_{k},\eta_{l})=\delta_{k+l,0},

\omega_{0,0|2}^{R}(|z_{1},z_{2}):=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{z_{1}+z_{2}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\frac{\Theta^{R}_{1}\Theta^{R}_{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}}-\sum_{k,l\geq 1}\frac{\psi^{R}_{k-1\;l-1}-\psi^{R}_{l-1\;k-1}}{1+\delta_{(k-1)(l-1),0}}\frac{\eta_{l}(z_{1})\eta_{k}(z_{2})}{2z_{1}z_{2}}.

Then, in the domain
|z_{1}|<|z_{2}|
, we have

(2.35)

\omega^{R}_{0,0|2}(|z_{1},z_{2})\rightarrow\sum_{l>0}\eta_{l}(z_{1})\eta_{-l}(z_{2})+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{0}(z_{1})\eta_{0}(z_{2}).

\mu

•

Untwisted and
\mu
-twisted Abstract Super Loop Equations

It turns out that the forms of untwisted/

\mu
-twisted … with one component”, and we simply call

\displaystyle\text{for }F=NS,\;\;\;\;f=\frac{1}{2},\;\;\;\;\text{for }F=R,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;f=0

Remark 2.5
.

A super spectral curve

is completely fixed by the following seven parameters:

(2.42)

(F,N,\tau_{l},\phi_{kl},\psi_{mn}^{F},D_{k},Q_{l})

2.2.1.
Abstract Super Loop Equations

Given a super spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{F}
, we consider an infinite sequence of multilinear differentials
\omega_{g,n|2m}
for
2g,n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
2g+n+2m\geq 3
as

(2.43)

\omega_{g,n|2m}\in\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}V_{z_{j}}^{B-}\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{k=1}^{2m}V_{u_{k},\theta_{k}}^{F\,0,-}\right),

where we should drop
V_{u_{k},\theta_{k}}^{F\,0}
when
F=NS
. Note that
g
can be a
half-integer
. We say that
\omega_{g,n|2m}
“respect the polarization”, because they are in the subspaces
V_{z_{j}}^{B-}
and
V_{u_{k},\theta_{k}}^{F\,0,-}
defined by the choice of polarization in the super spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{F}
. We impose that the
\omega_{g,n|2m}
are symmetric under permutations of the first
n
entries, and anti-symmetric under permutations of the last
2m
entries. We assume no symmetry under permutations of some of the first
n
entries with some of the last
2m
entries. Note that the
\omega_{g,n|2m}
always have an even number of elements in
\bigotimes V_{u,\theta}^{F\,0,-}

Let us denote by
I,J
a set of variables
I=(z_{1},...)
and

. The number of variables in

and
J
should be read off from multilinear differentials of interests, e.g., for

we have
|I|=n
and
|J|=2m-1
. For
2g+n+2m\geq 3
we define:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{g,n|2m}^{BF}(I|z,J)=\;

\displaystyle\omega_{g-1,n+1|2m}(z,I|z,J)

\displaystyle+\left(\underset{\tilde{z}\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1|0}(\tilde{z})\right)\left(\mathcal{D}_{z}\cdot\omega_{g-\frac{1}{2},n|2m}(I|z,J)+\frac{1-2f}{2}d\xi_{0}(z)\omega_{g-\frac{1}{2},n|2m}(I|z,J)\right)

(2.44)

\displaystyle+\sum_{g_{1}+g_{2}=g}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1}\cup I_{2}=I\\
J_{1}\cup J_{2}=J\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\rho}\omega_{g_{1},n_{1}+1|2m_{1}}(z,I_{1}|J_{1})\,\omega_{g_{2},n_{2}|2m_{2}}(I_{2}|z,J_{2}),

where we dropped the
\theta
-dependence for brevity.
(-1)^{\rho}=1
if

is an even permutation of
J
and
(-1)^{\rho}=-1
otherwise. Similarly, for
2g+n+2m\geq 2
and
(g,n,m)\neq(1,0,0)
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{g,n+1|2m}^{BB}(z,I|J)=\;

\displaystyle\omega_{g-1,n+2|2m}(z,z,I|J)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\underset{\tilde{z}\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1|0}(\tilde{z})\right)\mathcal{D}_{z}\cdot\omega_{g-\frac{1}{2},n+1|2m}(z,I|J)

(2.45)

\displaystyle+\sum_{g_{1}+g_{2}=g}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1}\cup I_{2}=I\\
J_{1}\cup J_{2}=J\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\rho}\omega_{g_{1},n_{1}+1|2m_{1}}(z,I_{1}|J_{1})\,\omega_{g_{2},n_{2}+1|2m_{2}}(z,I_{2}|J_{2}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{g,n+1|2m}^{FF}(z,I|J)=\;

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{z}\cdot\omega_{g-1,n|2m+2}(I|z,u,J)\Bigr|_{u=z}

(2.46)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{g_{1}+g_{2}=g}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{1}\cup I_{2}=I\\
J_{1}\cup J_{2}=J\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\rho}\mathcal{D}_{z}\cdot\omega_{g_{1},n_{1}|2m_{1}}(I_{1}|z,J_{1})\,\omega_{g_{2},n_{2}|2m_{2}}(I_{2}|z,J_{2}),

where for
\omega(z)=f(z)dz\in V_{z}^{B}
and
\eta(z,\theta)=g(z)\Theta^{F}(z,\theta)\in V_{z,\theta}^{F}
, the derivative operator
\mathcal{D}_{z}
is defined to act as

(2.47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{z}\cdot\omega(z)

\displaystyle=df(z)dz\in V_{z}^{B}\otimes V_{z}^{B},

(2.48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{z}\cdot\eta(z,\theta)

\displaystyle=dg(z)\Theta^{F}(z,\theta)\in V_{z}^{B}\otimes V_{z,\theta}^{F}.

We remark that

(2.49)

\underset{\tilde{z}\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,\omega_{\frac{1}{2},1|0}(\tilde{z})=Q_{0}.

In physics, this
Q_{0}
-dependence can be thought of as a consequence of the so-called “background charge”. When

, (

)-(
2.46
) are similar to Eq. (2.36)-(2.38) in
[

]
but they are rather simpler because there is no the involution operator

.

Following

2.3.
Untwisted/
\mu
-twisted Super Topological Recursion

We would like to prove that there exists a unique solution of the abstract super loop equations. Similar to the argument in
[
12
]
, however, existence is easier to prove once we describe them in terms of super Airy structures. Therefore, in this section we assume the existence of a solution, and present a way of computing a unique solution. We call this computational method the “untwisted/
\mu
-twisted super topological recursion”.

Important objects towards the construction of the super topological recursion are the recursion kernels
K^{BB},K^{BF}
. Given a super spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{F}
, we define
K^{BB}
by

(2.52)

K^{BB}(z_{0},z)=-\frac{\int^{z}_{0}\omega_{0,2|0}(z_{0},\cdot|)}{\omega_{0,1|0}(z|)}.\\

On the other hand,
K^{BF}
is defined by

(2.53)

K^{BF}(z_{0},z)=-\frac{\omega^{F}_{0,0|2}(|z,z_{0})-(f+\frac{1}{2})\eta_{f}(z)\eta_{-f}(z_{0})}{2\omega_{0,1|0}(z|)}.

One should find them similar to the recursion kernels in
[
12
, Section 3]
.

With these recursion kernels, we can obtain a unique solution of the abstract super loop equations by the following recursion.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n+1|2m}^{BB,FF,BF}
denote respectively all the terms on the right hand side of (
2.44
)-(
2.46
)
except
the terms involving
\omega_{0,1|0}
. If there exists a solution to the untwisted/
\mu
-twisted abstract super loop equations that respects the polarization, then it is uniquely constructed recursively by the following formulae:

•

For
2g+n+2m\geq 2
with
(g,n,m)\neq(1,0,0)
,

(2.54)

\omega_{g,n+1|2m}(z_{0},I|J)=\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,K^{BB}(z_{0},z)\left(\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n+1|2m}^{BB}(z,I|J)+\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n+1|2m}^{FF}(z,I|J)\right),

•

For
2g+n+2m\geq 3
,

(2.55)

\omega_{g,n|2m+2}(I|u_{1},u_{2},J)=\hat{\omega}_{g,n|2m+2}(I|u_{1},u_{2},J)-\eta_{-f}(u_{1})\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,\hat{\omega}_{g,n|2m+2}(I|u_{2},z,J)\eta_{f}(z),

where

(2.56)

\hat{\omega}_{g,n|2m+2}(I|u_{1},u_{2},J)=\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,K^{FB}(u_{1},z)\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{g,n|2m+2}^{FB}(I|z,u_{2},J).

Proof.

The proof closely follows how
[
12
]
proves the super topological recursion, but our case is simpler because there is no involution operator
\sigma
. Given a super spectral curve
\mathcal{S}_{F}
, let us assume the existence of a solution of the abstract super loop equations. Since
K^{BB}(z_{0},z)
has a zero of order
N+1
at
z=0
, the quadratic bosonic loop equations imply that for
2g+n+2m\geq 2
and
(g,n,m)\neq(1,0,0)
,

(2.57)

\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,K^{BB}(z_{0})\biggl(\mathcal{Q}_{g,n+1|2m}^{BB}(z,I|J)+\mathcal{Q}_{g,n+1|2m}^{FF}(z,I|J)\biggr)=0.

Let us focus on terms involving
\omega_{0,1|0}
on the left hand side. They appear in the form:

\displaystyle\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,K^{BB}(z_{0})\biggl(\omega_{0,1|0}(z|)\omega_{g,n+1|2m}(z,I|J)\biggr)

\displaystyle=-\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,\int^{z}_{0}\omega_{0,2|0}(z_{0},\cdot|)\omega_{g,n+1|2m}(z,I|J)

(2.58)

\displaystyle=-\omega_{g,n+1|2m}(z,I|J),

where we used (
2.7
) in the equality. This proves (
2.54
).

Similarly, the quadratic fermionic loop equations imply that for
2g+n+2m\geq 1
,

(2.59)

\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\,K^{FB}(u_{1},z)\mathcal{Q}_{g,n|2m+2}^{FB}(I|z,u_{2},J)=0.

One can repeat the same procedure as we did in (
2.58
). That is, terms involving
\omega_{0,1|0}
on the left hand side of (
2.59
) become

\displaystyle-\underset{z\rightarrow 0}{{\rm Res}}\left(\omega^{F}_{0,0|2}(|z,u_{1})-\left(f+\frac{1}{2}\right)\eta_{f}(z)\eta_{-f}(u_{1})\right)\omega_{g,n|2m+2}(I|z,u_{2},J)

(2.60)

\displaystyle=:-\hat{\omega}_{g,n|2m+2}(I|u_{1},u_{2},,J),

where this should be taken as the definition of
\hat{\omega}_{g,n|2m+2}
. Notice that the only differences between
\hat{\omega}_{g,n|2m+2}(I|u_{1},u_{2},J)
and
\omega_{g,n|2m+2}(I|u_{1},u_{2},J)
are terms that depend on
\eta_{-f}(u_{1})
thanks to (
2.20
) and (
2.35
). Since fermionic entries are antisymmetric under their permutations by definition, however, one can indeed supplement this missing
\eta_{-f}(u_{1})
-dependence precisely by the second term in (
2.55
). It is clear that (
2.54
) together with (
2.55
) are recursive for
\omega_{g,n|2m}
in
2g+n+2m
, hence we have constructed all
\omega_{g,n|2m}
starting with a super spectral curve, subject to the assumption of the existence of a solution. This completes the proof.

3.
Super Airy Structures

In this section, we will reformulate the super topological recursion from an algebraic point of view. A key notion is super Airy structures introduced in
[
9
]
. It has already been shown in
[
12
]
that the super topological recursion on a
\rho
-
twisted
super spectral curve is dual to a super Airy structure derived from a
\rho
-twisted
module of the
\mathcal{N}=1
super Virasoro algebra. Here, our focus is on super Airy structures related to untwisted/
\mu
-twisted modules instead.

3.1.
Review of Super Airy Structures

We first review the definition and properties of super Airy structures. This subsection very closely follows
[
12
, Section 4.1]
.

Let
U=U_{0}\oplus U_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{0|1}
be a super vector space of dimension
d+1
over
\mathbb{C}
(the super vector space could be infinite-dimensional, but for simplicity of presentation we will assume here that it has finite dimension). We define
\{x^{i}\}_{i\in I}
to be linear coordinates on
U_{0}\oplus U_{1}
where
I=\{1,...,d\}
with
x^{0}
to be the coordinate of the extra
\mathbb{C}^{0|1}
, and their parity is defined such that
|x^{i}|=0
if
x^{i}\in U_{0}
,
|x^{i}|=1
if
x^{i}\in U_{1}
, and
|x^{0}|=1
. Furthermore, let us denote by

(3.1)

\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(U)=\mathbb{C}\llbracket\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}},x^{0},\hbar\partial_{x^{0}},\{x^{i}\}_{i\in I},\{\hbar\partial_{x^{i}}\}_{i\in I}\rrbracket

the completed algebra of differential operators acting on
U
where
\hbar
is a formal variable. We then introduce a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading by

(3.2)

\deg(x^{0})=\deg(x^{i})=1,\;\;\;\deg(\hbar\partial_{x^{0}})=\deg(\hbar\partial_{x^{i}})=1,\;\;\;\;\deg(\hbar)=2.

Definition 3.1

(
[
9
, Definition 2.3]
)
.

A
super Airy structure
is a set of differential operators
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(U)
such that:

(1)

for each
i\in I
,
H_{i}
is of the form

(3.3)

H_{i}=\hbar\partial_{x^{i}}-P_{i},

where
P_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(U)
has degree greater than 1 with
|P_{i}|=|x^{i}|
,

(2)

there exists
f_{ij}^{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{\hbar}(U)
such that

(3.4)

[H_{i},H_{j}]_{s}=\hbar\sum_{k\in I}f_{ij}^{k}\,H_{k},

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{s}
is a super-commutator.

It is crucial that the
x^{0}
-dependence appears only in the
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, but not in the degree 1 term (there is no
H_{0}
). In other words, the dimension of the super vector space
U
is one more than the number of differential operators
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. We thus call
x^{0}
the
extra variable

5.
Conclusion

We have proposed the notion of untwisted/
\mu
-twisted super spectral curves as well as abstract super loop equations. Then, we showed that new recursive formalisms, which we call the “untwisted/
\mu
-twisted super topological recursion”, uniquely solve the untwisted/
\mu
-twisted super loop equations. We note that these new recursions are variants of the
\mathcal{N}=1
super topological recursion of
[
12
]
, which would be called the
\rho
-twisted super topological recursion in our notation. As noted in Section
2
, the difference between untwisted and
\mu
-twisted super spectral curves resembles the difference between Neveu-Schwarz punctures and Ramond punctures in the context of super Riemann surfaces. We further showed an alternative way of solving abstract super loop equations in terms of super Airy structures as untwisted/
\mu
-twisted modules of the
\mathcal{N}=1
super Virasoro algebra. We then proved an equivalence between these super Airy structures and the untwisted/
\mu
-twisted super topological recursion, which is summarised in Theorem
3.4
. Therefore, we have mathematically formalised the flowchart in Figure
2
.

We then applied these new recursions to computations of Gaiotto vectors for superconformal blocks. We showed in Proposition
4.6
that the partition function of an appropriate super Airy structure coincides with the corresponding Gaiotto vector for superconformal blocks. Importantly, since the uniqueness and existence of the partition function of a super Airy structure is mathematically proven, Proposition
4.6
serves as a proof of the uniqueness and existence of the Gaiotto vectors for superconformal blocks. Thanks to a conjectural extension of the AGT correspondence
[
4
,
19
]
, we notice that the super topological recursion have access to
\mathcal{N}=2
pure
U(2)
gauge theory on
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In addition to applications discussed in
[
12
]
such as supereigenvalue models
[
11
,
14
,
36
]
, our results provide another piece of evidence about how useful super Airy structures and the super topological recursion are.

There are a number of future directions one can take to generalise results of the present paper. For example, it is interesting to consider super Airy structures as modules of higher rank supersymmetric algebras such as
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{osp}(n|m))
-algebras. We expect that corresponding two-dimensional conformal field theory would be parafermion theory, and gauge theory counterpart would be
U(N)
theory on
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with an appropriate choice of
m,n,N,p
. See
[
23
]
and references therein for details about parafermion theory. Another aspect is to consider the Gaiotto vector in the Neveu-Schwarz sector with mass parameters. Unlike the Ramond sector discussed in
[
19
]
, it is expected that the generalisation of
[
17
]
is possible in the Neveu-Schwarz sector because both
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
can be shifted by constant terms without changing commutation relations. Furthermore, all the applications discussed in the present paper are when
N=1
as in (
4.29
). Since the super topological recursion stands for any higher
N
, it is interesting to see if there are any examples in physics or mathematics with
N>1
. Finally, as shown in Table
1
there are four types of super Airy structures as modules of the
\mathcal{N}=1
super Virasoro algebra. On the other hand, the geometric counterpart, i.e., the super topological recursion is reported only for three of them (
\rho
-twisted one by
[
12
]
, and untwisted/
\mu
-twisted ones in the present paper), and we hope to introduce the last
\sigma
-twisted super topological recursion in the future with some interesting applications.

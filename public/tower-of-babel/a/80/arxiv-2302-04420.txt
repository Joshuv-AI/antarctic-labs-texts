On a vanishing theorem for birational morphisms of threefolds in positive and mixed characteristics Thanks: The author was supported by SNF #P500PT_203083
Emelie Arvidsson

Abstract
. In this short note, we prove a relative Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing type theorem for birational morphisms. We use this to prove a Grauert–Riemenschneider theorem over log canonical threefolds without zero-dimensional lc-centers, in residue characteristic p > 5 p>5 . In large enough residue characteristics, we prove a Grauert–Riemenschneider theorem over threefold log canonical singularities with standard coefficients. These vanishing theorems can also be used to study the depth of log canonical singularities at non log canonical centers, as well as the singularities of the log canonical centers themselves. The former simplifies joint work with F. Bernasconi and Zs. Patakfalvi, and the latter appears in joint work with Q. Posva.

1.
Introduction

Over the complex numbers, Kawamata log terminal singularities are Cohen–Macaulay and rational
[
Elk81
]
. It is therefore natural to ask if Kawamata log terminal singularities also have such good properties over algebraically closed fields of positive characteristics? S. Kovács showed that these two questions are essentially equivalent to the single question; if klt singularities are Cohen–Macaulay or not over fields of positive characteristics? In particular, he showed that Cohen–Macaulay klt singularities are pseudo–rational, and when resolution exists, rational in the usual sense
[
Kov17
]
. In dimension three, it was recently showed that klt singularities are indeed Cohen–Macaulay in characteristic
p>5

[
ABL22
,
HW19
]
, and counter–examples where constructed in low characteristics
[
CT18
,
Ber17
,
ABL22
]
.

With the positive results for threefold klt singularities at hand, it is natural to ask if Kollár’s theorem on the depth of log canonical singularities at non log canonical centers
[
Kol11
, Theorem 3]
also hold true for threefolds in (possibly large enough) positive characteristics? Let us first recall what Kollár’s theorem says over the Complex numbers.

Theorem 1

(Kollár)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a three dimensional log canonical pair over
\mathbb{C}
and
x\in X
a point which is not a log-canonical center then

\depth_{x}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\codim_{X}(x)

It turns out that Theorem
1
fails to be true in every positive characteristic (see Example
20
). In this note we use the previous results on dlt threefold singularities in characteristic
p>5
to study the depth of log canonical singularities at non lc-centers via a dlt modification
f\colon Y\to X
. Our main result is that
f
can be chosen in such a way that it satisfies a birational Kawamata–Viehweg type vanishing theorem (see Prop.
14
). As a direct consequence of this birational vanishing theorem, we obtain the following, which can be considered as a positive characteristics analog of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2

(Corollary
18
)
.

Assume that
X
is an excellent scheme whose closed points have perfect residue field of characteristic
p>5
. Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a log–canonical threefold singularity which is not a log–canonical center. Suppose

(X,\Delta)
. Then there exists a crepant dlt modification

, where

denotes the restriction of

to the surface

.

If

is not contained in a one dimensional minimal log canonical center of

then

is either klt or plt and

,
[
ABL22
,
BK21
]
. The above theorem therefore says that the failure of the theorem of Kollár (Theorem

) in positive characteristic is completely explained by the possible failure of the higher direct images of certain surface fibrations to be torsion-free in positive characteristics, see remark

The birational vanishing theorems established in this note also implies that we can extend the

theorem
[

We will be working over an excellent local domain

By a variety we shall mean a separated reduced, equidimensional, excellent scheme over

The dimension of a variety

A

By a log pair

-divisor

We say that

A normal surface

For a

Definition 5
.

We will say that a proper morphism

•

Remark 6
.

Note that if
(Y,\Delta)

If

5.
Existence of dlt models with exceptional ample divisors

In order to apply Proposition
8

f
-ample and exceptional divisor. If we reduce our attention to
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial schemes this can easily be assured.

Lemma 9
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a proper morphism between normal quasi–projective varieties. If
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial then there exists an anti–effective,
f
-ample and exceptional divisor

.

Proof.

Let
A
be an ample divisor on
X
. Then

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
A-f^{*}(A^{\prime})
is exceptional, anti–effective and

-ample.

Lemma 11
.

There exists an integer
p_{0}
with the following property. Let
\Delta
be an effective divisor with standard coefficients. Suppose that

is a component of

-divisor

the pair

[

Remark 19

Corollary

says that Kollár’s Theorem

fails to be true at a non lc-center

of

precisely

Example 20
.

Kollár’s Theorem
1
can fail in every positive characteristic. Let
f\colon S\to C
be a minimal wild elliptic surface in characteristic
p>0
. I.e.,
f
is of relative Picard rank one,
S
and
C
are smooth,
K_{S}\sim_{f,\mathbb{Q}}0
and
R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}
has torsion supported at some
x\in C
, i.e.,
H^{0}_{x}(C,R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S})\neq 0
. See
[
KU85
]
for examples of such elliptic fibrations. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a relatively ample line bundle and consider the relative cone
\Spec_{C}(\bigoplus_{i}f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{i})
. This is a log canonical threefold singularity with unique minimal log-canonical center the relative vertex, which is isomorphic to
C
. The point

is a non log canonical center, for which Kollár’s Theorem fails. Indeed, a dlt-modification as in Corollary
18
is given by

. Therefore, Theorem

Corollary 23
.

Let
(X,S+\Delta)
be a log canonical threefold singularity with closed points of perfect residue field of characteristic

.

Proof.

The following proof follows the lines of
[
ABP
, Theorem 1.4]
, except that we use Proposition
14
instead of
[
ABP
, Theorem 1.5]
.
Let
x\in S
be a closed point, we want to show that

. By the long exact sequence of local cohomology associated to the standard short exact sequence of

, it is sufficient to prove that
H^{1}_{x}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
and
H_{x}^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-S))=0
. Since
S
is Cartier,
X
is
S_{2}
, and local cohomology can be computed locally around
x

[
Har67
, Prop. 1.3]
, we only need to show
H^{2}_{x}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
. Since

we can not have that
x
is a log canonical center of
(X,\Delta)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x\in C
where
C
is a one dimensional minimal log canonical center (since otherwise
H^{2}_{x}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
by
[
ABL22
,
BK21
]
). By Corollary
18
there exists a dlt–modification
f\colon(Y,\Delta_{Y}+E)\to(X,\Delta)

with reduced exceptional divisor
E
and such that
H^{2}_{x}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong H^{0}_{x}(C,R^{1}{f_{|E}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E})
. By Theorem
21
, the curve
C
is normal and therefore
{f_{|E}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
, and by miracle flatness
[
Sta20
, Tag 00R4]
,

is flat. By
[
BK21
, Theorem 3.1]
, the divisor
E
is
S_{2}
and each component

is normal. Assume in order to arrive at a contradiction that

[

implies that each component of the fiber over

[
ABP

Abstract
. Given a non-invertible dynamical system with a transfer operator, we show there is a minimal cover with a transfer operator that preserves continuous functions. We also introduce an essential cover with even stronger continuity properties. For one-sided sofic subshifts, this generalizes the Krieger and Fischer covers, respectively. Our construction is functorial in the sense that certain equivariant maps between dynamical systems lift to equivariant maps between their covers, and these maps also satisfy better regularity properties. As applications, we identify finiteness conditions which ensure that the thermodynamic formalism is valid for the covers. This establishes the thermodynamic formalism for a large class of non-invertible dynamical systems, e.g. certain piecewise invertible maps. When applied to semi-étale groupoids, our minimal covers produce étale groupoids which are models for C ∗ C^{*} -algebras constructed by Thomsen. The dynamical covers and groupoid covers are unified under the common framework of topological graphs.

1.
Introduction

Single transformations, i.e., continuous maps
T\colon X\to X
on a topological space
X
, are classical objects of study in topological dynamics.
Such transformations can be viewed as discrete-time dynamical systems, where an application of
T
describes how the system evolves after one discrete time step.
If
T
is invertible, then the inverse of
T
allows us to go back in time, but, in general, transfer operators allow us to at least go back in time on average.
Roughly speaking, the idea is to attach to every point
x\in X
a measure
\lambda_{x}
on
X
that is supported on the pre-image
T^{-1}(x)
of
x
under
T
.
Given a function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
, the transfer operator
\Lambda
attached to
(\lambda_{x})_{x\in X}
produces (under mild assumptions on
f
such as integrability)
a new function
\Lambda(f)
by averaging over pre-images, i.e., by sending
x\in X
to
\Lambda(f)(x)\coloneq\int_{T^{-1}(x)}f{\rm d}\lambda_{x}
.
If
T
is invertible with inverse
T^{-1}
, then pre-images of points
x\in X
are given by singletons
\left\{T^{-1}(x)\right\}
, and the canonical choice for
\lambda_{x}
is the point mass at
T^{-1}(x)
.
In this case, the transfer operator is simply given by pre-composition with
T^{-1}
(i.e.,
\Lambda(f)=f\circ T^{-1}
).
The reader may consult
[
2
]
, for example, for more information about transfer operators.

In this paper, we are interested in the property of transfer operators — sometimes called the Feller property — that they preserve continuity,
i.e., for every continuous (and bounded) function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
,
\Lambda(f)
is again continuous.
In general, transfer operators need not have this property.
However, it turns out that in various different contexts, this property is desirable for several reasons.

For example, in the context of the thermodynamic formalism (see for instance
[
40
,
32
]
), if the transfer operator preserves continuous functions, then this is the starting point for restricting the transfer operator to subspaces of more regular (such as Hölder continuous) functions where properties of the transfer operator such as quasi-compactness allow a further detailed analysis of its spectrum. In addition, for compact Hausdorff spaces
X
, positive linear functionals on the space
C(X)
of continuous functions
X\to\mathbb{C}
are in one-to-one correspondence with Radon measures on
X
. Hence, if the transfer operator
\Lambda
preserves
C(X)
, its dual operator
\Lambda^{*}
acts on Radon measures on
X
. This is the starting point for studying invariant measures on
X
by analyzing eigenmeasures of
\Lambda^{*}
, for example equilibrium measures or Gibbs measures.

The property that transfer operators preserve continuous functions is also relevant in operator algebras.
There is a long history of fruitful interactions between topological dynamics and
C^{*}
-algebras,
and many example classes of
C^{*}
-algebras arising from dynamics (such as crossed products or Cuntz–Krieger algebras
[
10
]
) have played an important role in the development of
C^{*}
-algebra theory.
At the same time, a
C^{*}
-algebraic perspective was instrumental in the classification of Cantor minimal systems
[
14
]
.
Given a continuous map
T\colon X\to X
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
, a transfer operator
\Lambda
for
X
that preserves continuous functions gives rise to a
C_{0}(X)
-
C_{0}(X)
-correspondence,
which in turn allows one to apply Pimsner’s machinery
[
33
]
(see also
[
21
]
) to construct a
C^{*}
-algebra encoding dynamical properties of the original transformation
T
.
Related to this, a very general class of
C^{*}
-algebras of dynamical origin is given by groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras
[
36
]
.
Given a locally compact Hausdorff groupoid
G
, its groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra is constructed by defining a convolution product on
C_{c}(G)
, the space of continuous functions
G\to\mathbb{C}
with compact support.
This works particularly well for étale groupoids, where the range and source maps are local homeomorphisms.
That property ensures that the transfer operator given by summation over pre-images of the range map preserves continuous functions.
This in turn is the key reason why
C_{c}(G)
is closed under convolution.
For general locally compact Hausdorff groupoids, the construction of the convolution product requires the existence of a Haar system,
which by its very definition again ensures that the corresponding transfer operator preserves continuous functions on our groupoid.
This means that the property of preserving continuity is at the heart of many constructions of
C^{*}
-algebras from dynamical data.

The goal of the present paper is to describe a systematic and explicit way of constructing transfer operators with the desirable property of preserving continuity out of general transfer operators which might lack this property.
More precisely, given a continuous map
T\colon X\to X
, where
X
is locally compact and Hausdorff, and a transfer operator
\Lambda
for
T
, we construct a cover (i.e., an extension)
T_{\min}\colon X_{\min}\to X_{\min}
of
T
together with a transfer operator
\bm{\lambda}_{\min}
for
T_{\min}
which extends
\Lambda
and satisfies
\bm{\lambda}_{\min}(C_{c}(X_{\min}))\subseteq C_{c}(X_{\min})
. Moreover,
T_{\min}
is the minimal cover with these properties, in the sense that it has a universal property as we will make precise. In addition, our cover construction is functorial, and most importantly, it is accessible, in the sense that it is possible to describe it explicitly for concrete examples, as we demonstrate in Sections
5
and
6
.
We also construct a modification called the minimal essential cover that often has better properties because it is closer to the original map.

When applied to one-sided subshifts, our constructions reproduce and generalize the covers considered by Fischer
[
12
]
, Krieger
[
24
]
, Matsumoto
[
28
]
and Carlsen
[
6
]
(see also
[
26
]
and the references therein).
We are therefore able to prove these classical covers have (previously unknown) functorial and universal properties, as well as expand the class of subshifts they may be considered for (especially for the Fischer covers).

We identify finiteness conditions which ensure that our minimal cover
T_{\min}
is positively expansive if our original map
T
is. Since the thermodynamic formalism has been established for factors of open positively expansive maps that are topologically transitive, our cover construction (which is always open) allows us to establish the thermodynamic formalism for the original transformation whose transfer operator might be badly behaved. In addition, the analysis of our minimal cover for particular examples such as the tent map has led us to the construction of another, related cover for general piecewise invertible maps, and — again under a finiteness assumption — produces positively expansive covers which again allow us to establish a thermodynamic formalism for piecewise invertible maps.

In a different setting, for locally compact Hausdorff groupoids which are not étale but whose range and source maps are only locally injective (Thomsen calls these semi-étale), our construction produces a cover groupoid which is étale and whose reduced groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra coincides with Thomsen’s construction in
[
42
]
. This provides a conceptual explanation for the work in
[
42
,
43
]
. Whenever given an étale groupoid
G
and an arbitrary closed (but not necessarily invariant) subspace
C
of the unit space of
G
, the restricted groupoid
G_{C}^{C}
has locally injective range and source maps (but it will not be étale in general because
C
is not assumed to be invariant). Hence our construction applies to all these examples and produces étale groupoids for each of them.

Let us now explain our construction and main results in more detail.
We work in the general setting of
topological graphs
, which encompasses single transformations as well as topological groupoids.
A topological graph
Z
is a pair of locally compact Hausdorff spaces
Y
and
X
with continuous maps
r,s\colon Y\to X
.
We regard
Y
as the set of edges,
X
as the set of vertices, and
r
,
s
are the range and source maps.
However, we may also think of a topological graph as defining a generalized discrete-time dynamical system on
X
, where
s(y)
is sent to
r(y)
for each
y\in Y
.

In its most abstract form, a transfer operator for a topological graph
Z
is a positive linear operator
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{R}

defined on a sub-algebra
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
of compactly supported
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on the edges
Y
into a sub-algebra
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\ell^{\infty}_{c}(X)

(subject to compatibility conditions)
such that each linear functional
f\mapsto\Lambda(f)(x)
,
x\in X
, is supported on
r^{-1}(x)
.
When the fibres
r^{-1}(x)
are discrete, we can induce a transfer operator from a weight function
w\colon Y\to[0,\infty)
by the formula
\Lambda(f)(x)\coloneq\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}w(y)f(y)
,
where
f\in\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
.
If the fibres are not discrete, then a typical example of a transfer operator comes from Borel measures
\lambda=(\lambda)_{x\in X}
with
\text{supp}(\lambda_{x})\subseteq r^{-1}(x)
for
x\in X

such that the function
x\mapsto\int f{\rm d}\lambda_{x}
is Borel for every Borel function
f\colon Y\to\mathbb{C}
with compact support.

Given a topological graph
Z=(r,s\colon Y\to X)
and a transfer operator
\Lambda
,
its
minimal cover
is a new topological graph
\hat{Z}=(\hat{r},\hat{s}\colon\hat{Y}\to\hat{X})
equipped with a transfer operator
\hat{\bm{\lambda}}
that is continuous
in the sense that
\hat{\bm{\lambda}}(C_{c}(\hat{Y}))\subseteq C_{c}(\hat{X})
.
There are continuous proper surjections
\pi_{\hat{Y}}\colon\hat{Y}\to Y
,
\pi_{\hat{X}}\colon\hat{X}\to X
,
injective (in general non-continuous) maps
\iota_{Y}\colon Y\to\hat{Y}
,
\iota_{X}\colon X\to\hat{X}
with dense images such that
(\pi_{\hat{Y}},\pi_{\hat{X}})
and
(\iota_{Y},\iota_{X})
are graph morphisms
with
(\pi_{\hat{Y}},\pi_{\hat{X}})\circ(\iota_{Y},\iota_{X})=({\rm id}_{Y},{\rm id}_{X})
, and in addition,
we have
\Lambda(f\circ\iota_{Y})=\hat{\bm{\lambda}}(f)\circ\iota_{X}
for all
f\in C_{c}(\hat{Y})
.
The minimal cover is universal for such maps (see Section
3.3
for details).
We also construct a modification we call the
minimal essential cover
.
It is given by a sub-graph
\tilde{Z}=(\tilde{r},\tilde{s}\colon\tilde{Y}\to\tilde{X})
of
\hat{Z}
with transfer operator

\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}\colon C_{c}(\tilde{Y})\to C_{c}(\tilde{X})
given by the restriction of
\hat{\bm{\lambda}}
to
C_{c}(\tilde{Y})
,
and it remedies the discontinuity of the embeddings
\iota_{Y}
,
\iota_{X}
(at the expense that they are only defined on a dense sub-graph).
The essential cover is in fact closer to the original topological graph and is therefore often a better tool for the analysis of the original system.
This construction does however require a mild condition we call post-critical sparsity (see Section
3.7
).

By viewing a single transformation with a transfer operator as a topological graph (every continuous map
T\colon X\to X
gives rise to the topological graph
Y=X
,
r=T
,
s={\rm id}_{X}
),
we obtain minimal dynamical covers
(T_{\min}\colon X_{\min}\to X_{\min},\bm{\lambda}_{\min})
and minimal essential covers
(T_{{\rm ess}}\colon X_{{\rm ess}}\to X_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})
of single transformations.
However, to ensure that the cover is again a single transformation, we insist that
\hat{Y}=\hat{X}
and
\hat{s}={\rm id}_{\hat{X}}
.
It turns out that the minimal dynamical cover is the shift on the (one-sided) infinite path space of the minimal topological graph cover (see Section
4
).

Let us now present our main results. In this paper, we especially focus on graphs whose range maps
r\colon Y\to X
are locally injective (every edge
y\in Y
has an open neighbourhood
U
such that
r|_{U}
is injective).
The natural transfer operator is induced from the weight function
w=1
, i.e.,
\Lambda(f)(x)=\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}f(y)
.
In this case, the range map of the minimal cover
\hat{r}\colon\hat{Y}\to\hat{X}
is a local homeomorphism (open and locally injective), and the transfer operator is also induced from the weight
w=1
.
Similar statements hold for the minimal dynamical cover as well as for the minimal essential cover.

A map
T
is
open at

if
T(x)
is an interior point of
T(U)
for every open neighbourhood
U
of
x
.
The
critical point set of

is given as
C_{T}=\left\{x\in X\colon T\text{ is not open at }x\right\}
.
Recall also that a subset
C\subseteq X
is
meagre
if it is a countable union of sets whose closures have empty interior.

Theorem A

(See Theorem
4.20
)
.

Suppose
T\colon X\to X
is locally injective and
X
is a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space. If the critical point set
C_{T}
is meagre, then the minimal essential cover
T_{{\rm ess}}\colon X_{{\rm ess}}\to X_{{\rm ess}}
exists and is a local homeomorphism defined on a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space. The factor map
\pi_{{\rm ess}}\colon(T_{{\rm ess}},X_{{\rm ess}})\to(T,X)
is continuous, almost one-to-one and proper.

If
(\dot{T},\dot{X})
is another local homeomorphism defined on a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space with continuous, almost one-to-one and proper factor map
\dot{\pi}\colon(\dot{T},\dot{X})\to(T,X)

\chi\colon(\dot{T},\dot{X})\to(T_{{\rm ess}},X_{{\rm ess}})
such that
\dot{\pi}=\pi_{{\rm ess}}\circ\chi
. Moreover,
\chi
is proper, almost one-to-one and
\chi
restricts to bijections

For a subshift

Theorem B

(See Theorem

)
.

Suppose
(T,X)
and
(T^{\prime},X^{\prime})

The construction of the minimal topological graph cover proceeds in steps.
We first construct a sequence

of topological graphs with a system of maps between the underlying spaces, and then the minimal cover
\hat{Z}
arises as the projective limit of these

(see Section

With applications to the thermodynamic formalism in mind, we identify a finiteness condition which ensures that positive expansivity is inherited by the minimal dynamical cover
T_{\min}
from the original transformation

. First of all, a continuous map

on a compact metric space

is

if there is a constant

such that for every two distinct

such that

. Note that a positively expansive map must be locally injective, and we assume throughout that the transfer operator is induced from the function

.

Theorem C

(see Theorem
4.27
)
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous and positively expansive map on a compact metric space

. If our construction of the minimal topological graph cover terminates after finitely many steps, i.e.
\hat{Z}=Z_{n}
for some

, then the minimal dynamical cover

is continuous, open, and positively expansive.

Among subshifts, it is precisely the sofic systems for which our construction terminates after finitely many steps.
Interestingly, the minimal

The above theorem leads to the following result, which has applications for the thermodynamic formalism.

Corollary D

(see Corollary

.

Let

The point is that the original transformation

Moreover, the analysis of our minimal cover leads to the following application to piecewise invertible maps (which we introduce in §

Theorem E

)

Let

Let

)

K\subseteq Y
, there is
M_{K}>0
such that
\|\Lambda(f)\|\leq M_{K}\|f\|

for every positive
f\in\mathcal{D}
with
\text{supp}(f)\subseteq K
.

Let us consider some other properties of
\Lambda
that will be important to us later. We will say
\Lambda
is

(C):

continuous
if
\mathcal{D}=C_{c}(Y)
and
\mathcal{R}=C_{c}(X)
,

(BB):

bounded below
if for every compact
K\subseteq Y
, there is
N_{K}>0
such that
\Lambda(f)\circ r\geq N_{K}f
for every positive
f\in C_{c}(Y)
with
\text{supp}(f)\subseteq K
, and

(F):

full
if for every
y\in Y
, we have
\Lambda(f)(r(y))>0
for every positive
f\in C_{c}(Y)
with
f(y)>0
.

We now study these conditions.

2.1.
Continuity

We describe some consequences of continuity of an
r
-operator and an equivalent formulation in terms of Radon measures.

The support of a measure
\mu
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
Y
is defined as follows:
first let
N
be the biggest open subset of
Y
such that
\mu(N)=0
.
The support
{\rm supp}(\mu)
of
\mu
is defined as the complement of
N
in
Y
,
{\rm supp}(\mu)\coloneq Y\setminus N
.
We will need the following characterization of points in the support:
a point
y\in Y
lies in
{\rm supp}(\mu)
if and only if
\int f{\rm d}\mu>0
for every
f\in C_{c}(Y)
with
0\leq f\leq 1
and
f(y)>0
(see for instance
[
13
, Exercise 7.2]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

A map
\Lambda\colon C_{c}(Y)\to C_{c}(X)
is an
r
-operator if and only if
\Lambda(f(g\circ r))=\Lambda(f)g
for all
f\in C_{c}(Y)
and
g\in C_{c}(X)
.

Proof.

The “only if” part is trivial. If
f|_{r^{-1}(x)}=0
, then by compactness of the support of
f
and continuity there is a positive function
\psi\in C_{c}(Y)
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is
0\leq\phi\leq 1
in
C_{c}(X)
with
\phi(x)=1
and
f(\phi\circ r)\leq\varepsilon\psi
. Hence,
\Lambda(f)(x)=\Lambda(f(\phi\circ r))(x)\leq\varepsilon\Lambda(\psi)(x)
. As
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, it follows that
\Lambda(f)(x)=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\Lambda
is full, then for every positive
f\in C_{c}(Y)
, we have
{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\Lambda(f))=r({\rm supp}^{\circ}(f))
.

Proof.

\Lambda
being full is equivalent to
r({\rm supp}^{\circ}(f))\subseteq{\rm supp}^{\circ}(\Lambda(f))
. The reverse direction follows from
\Lambda(f)(x)=0
whenever
f|_{r^{-1}(x)}=0.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\Lambda
is full and continuous, then
r
is open.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2.2
, continuity of
\Lambda(f)
, for
f\geq 0
in
C_{c}(Y)
, and the fact that a basis for the topology on
Y
is given by
\left\{{\rm supp}^{\circ}(f)\colon f\geq 0\text{ and }f\in C_{c}(Y)\right\}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Conversely, if
Y
is second countable and
r\colon Y\to X
is a continuous open surjection, then Blanchard showed that there exists a continuous and full
r
-operator (see e.g.
[
47
, Corollary B.18]
).

Recall from
[
47
, Section 3.2]
that an
r
-system
is a family of Radon measures
(\lambda_{x})_{x\in X}
on
Y
such that
\text{supp}(\lambda_{x})\subseteq r^{-1}(x)
for all
x\in X
, and the function
x\mapsto\int f{\rm d}\lambda_{x}
is in
C_{c}(X)
for all
f\in C_{c}(Y)
.
We may define a continuous
r
-operator
\Lambda\colon C_{c}(Y)\to C_{c}(X)
via
\Lambda(f)(x)=\int f{\rm d}\lambda_{x}
.
Conversely, if
\Lambda\colon C_{c}(Y)\to C_{c}(X)
is a continuous
r
-operator, then the positive linear functional
f\mapsto\Lambda(f)(x)
induces, by the Riesz-representation theorem, a Radon measure
\lambda_{x}
, and
(\lambda_{x})_{x\in X}
is an
r
-system.

Using Gelfand duality, there is a way to view every
r
-operator in our sense as continuous (and therefore induced from an
r
-system) on a
covering
of the original space, as we will explain in §
3.3
. The following lemma is the first ingredient.
Below,
{\rm Spec\,}(A)
denotes the space of characters (i.e., non-zero homomorphisms
A\to\mathbb{C}
) endowed with the topology of pointwise convergence.
There is a canonical homeomorphism
{\rm Spec\,}(A)\cong{\rm Spec\,}(\overline{A})
, where
\overline{A}
is the closure of
A
in the uniform norm.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
W
be a locally compact Hausdorff space and
A\subseteq\ell_{c}^{\infty}(W)
be a
∗
-sub-algebra that is closed in the inductive limit topology and contains
C_{c}(W)
. Then
\hat{A}=C_{c}({\rm Spec\,}(A))
, where
\hat{(-)}\colon A\to C_{0}({\rm Spec\,}(A))
is the Gelfand transform. Moreover,
\hat{(-)}\colon A\to C_{c}({\rm Spec\,}(A))
is a homeomorphism relative to the inductive limit topologies on
A
and
C_{c}({\rm Spec\,}(A))
.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{W}\coloneq{\rm Spec\,}(A)
and let
\overline{A}
be the closure of
A
as above. Let
\iota\colon W\to\mathcal{W}
be defined as
\iota(z)=\text{ev}_{z}\colon A\to\mathbb{C}
. The dual of the inclusion
C_{c}(W)\subseteq A
is a proper and continuous map
\pi\colon\mathcal{W}\to W
. It follows from
\pi\circ\iota=\text{id}_{W}
and
\iota^{*}\circ\hat{(-)}=\text{id}_{A}
(i.e.,
\hat{a}\circ\iota=a
for all
a\in\overline{A}
) that for
f\in\overline{A}
, we have
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\text{supp}(f))
and
\text{supp}(f)=\text{supp}(\hat{f}\circ\iota)\subseteq\pi(\text{supp}(\hat{f}))
. Therefore, the image of
\overline{A}\cap\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Z)
under
\hat{(-)}
is
C_{c}(\mathcal{W})
.

To finish proving
\hat{A}=C_{c}({\rm Spec\,}(A))
, it suffices to prove
\overline{A}\cap\ell^{\infty}_{c}(W)=A
. Since
A
is dense in
\overline{A}
, for every
a\in\overline{A}\cap\ell^{\infty}_{c}(W)
, we can find a sequence
a_{n}\in A
such that
a_{n}\to a
uniformly. Let
\phi\in C_{c}(W)
be a function such that
a\phi=a
. Then
a_{n}\phi\in A
and
a\phi_{n}\to a
in the inductive limit topology. Hence,
a\in A
.
From
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\pi^{-1}(\text{supp}(f))
and
\text{supp}(f)=\text{supp}(\hat{f}\circ\iota)\subseteq\pi(\text{supp}(\hat{f}))
for
f\in A
, it follows that
\hat{(-)}
is a homeomorphism relative to the inductive limit topologies.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

If
A
is an algebra satisfying the hypothesis of Lemma
2.5
, then
M(A)
is canonically isomorphic to the algebra of all functions
f\colon W\to\mathbb{C}
which satisfy the condition that
f\phi\in A
for all
\phi\in C_{c}(W)
.

2.2.
The bounded below condition

Let us first consider some examples satisfying the bounded below condition.

Definition 2.7
.

We will say that
r\colon Y\to X
is
locally finite degree
if for every compact set
K\subseteq Y
,
\text{deg}_{K}(r)\coloneq\sup_{x\in X}|r^{-1}(x)\cap K|<\infty
. We shall call a function
w\colon Y\to(0,\infty)
a
strictly positive weight
if for every compact set
K\subseteq Y
, there are
C_{K},D_{K}>0
such that
C_{K}\geq w(y)\geq D_{K}
for all
y\in K
.

Note that a function
w
is a strictly positive weight if and only if
(w^{1/n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an approximate unit for
\ell_{c}^{\infty}(Y)
equipped with the inductive limit topology, and this property is where the name is derived from.

The following observation is easy to see.

Lemma 2.8
.

When
r
is locally finite degree, a function
w\colon Y\to(0,\infty)
determines a transfer operator
\Lambda\colon\ell_{c}^{\infty}(Y)\mapsto\ell^{\infty}_{c}(X)
by the formula

\Lambda(f)(x)=\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}w(y)f(y)

which is bounded above. If
w
is a strictly positive weight, then
\Lambda
is also bounded below.

Moreover, the converse holds, as we now explain.

Proposition 2.9
.

If
\Lambda
is bounded below, then
r
is locally finite degree. Furthermore, there is a strictly positive weight
w
such that
\Lambda(f)(x)=\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}w(y)f(y)
, for all
f\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq Y
be a compact set. Choose an open set
U
such that
K\subseteq U
and
\overline{U}\eqcolon L
is compact, and let
M_{L},N_{L}>0
be the numbers in the bounded above and bounded below conditions. If
\{y_{i}\}^{m}_{i=1}\subseteq r^{-1}(x)\cap K
is a collection of distinct points, then let
\{\phi_{i}\}^{m}_{i=1}\subseteq C_{c}(U)
be a collection of positive functions such that
0\leq\phi_{i}\leq 1
,
\phi_{i}(y_{i})=1
and
\phi_{i}\phi_{j}=0
, for all
i\neq j\leq m
. Hence,
0\leq\phi=\sum^{m}_{i=1}\phi_{i}\leq 1
. By the bounded above condition, we have
M_{L}\geq\Lambda(\phi)
and by the bounded below condition, we have
\Lambda(\phi_{i})(x)\geq N_{L}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Hence,
M_{L}\geq\Lambda(\phi)(x)\geq mN_{L}
. Therefore,
\frac{M_{L}}{N_{L}}\geq m
. It follows that
\text{deg}_{K}(r)\leq\frac{M_{L}}{N_{L}}<\infty
.

Since
r^{-1}(x)
is discrete, for every
y\in r^{-1}(x)
we may choose some positive
\phi_{y}\in C_{c}(Y)
such that
{\rm supp}(\phi_{y})\cap r^{-1}(x)=\{y\}
and
\phi_{y}(y)=1
. Note that
w(y)\coloneq\Lambda(\phi_{y})
does not depend on the choice of
\phi_{y}
. For every
f\in\mathcal{D}
, we have
\text{supp}(f)\cap r^{-1}(x)
is finite. Hence,
g=f-\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}\phi_{y}f(y)
is in
\mathcal{D}
and satisfies
g|_{r^{-1}(x)}=0
. Therefore,

\Lambda(f)(x)=\Lambda\Big(\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}\phi_{y}f(y)\Big)(x)=\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}w(y)f(y).

Since for
y\in K
, we may choose
\phi_{y}\in C_{c}(U)
, it follows from the bounded above and bounded below conditions that
M_{L}\geq w(y)=\Lambda(\phi_{y})(x)\geq N_{L}\phi_{y}(y)=N_{L}
so we can choose
C_{K}=M_{L}
and
D_{K}=N_{L}.

∎

Hence every
r
-operator that is bounded below has a unique extension to an
r
-operator that is bounded below with
\mathcal{D}=\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
and
\mathcal{R}=\ell_{c}^{\infty}(X)
, and is completely determined by a strictly positive weight
w
.

Example 2.10
.

The
r
-operators that are bounded below are not the only examples determined by a function
w\colon Y\to[0,\infty)
. Indeed,
\Lambda(f)(x)=\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)}w(y)f(y)
,
f\in\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
,
x\in X
, defines an
r
-operator
\Lambda\colon\ell_{c}^{\infty}(Y)\to\ell_{c}^{\infty}(X)
if and only if for every compact set
K\subseteq Y
, there is
M_{K}>0
such that

M_{K}\geq\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)\cap K}w(y)
for all
x\in X
. We shall call a function
w
satisfying this property a
weight
.

Remark 2.11
.

The last example of
r
-operators were of a special type with
\mathcal{D}=\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
and
\mathcal{R}=\ell^{\infty}_{c}(X)
. An
r
-operator with maximal domain and range can always be thought of as an extension of the Baire measures
(\lambda_{x})_{x\in X}
determined by the functionals
(\Lambda(-)(x)|_{C_{c}(Y)})_{x\in X}
. Conversely, suppose
(\lambda_{x})_{x\in X}
is a system of Baire measures such that
\text{sup}_{x\in X}\lambda_{x}(K)<\infty
for every compact
G_{\delta}
set
K
and suppose, for every
x\in X
, we choose an extension of
\int(-){\rm d}\lambda_{x}
to a positive linear functional
\Lambda(-)(x)\colon\ell^{\infty}_{c}(r^{-1}(x))\to\mathbb{C}
. Such a choice always exists by the Hahn–Banach theorem and the Axiom of Choice. By positivity of the extensions
\Lambda(-)(x)
and the fact that every element in
\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
is dominated by a function in
C_{c}(Y)
with the same norm and the support depending only on the support of the element, it follows that
\Lambda\colon\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)\to\ell^{\infty}_{c}(X)
is an
r
-operator.

3.
Topological graphs and their covers

3.1.
Topological graphs

A
topological graph
is a pair of locally compact Hausdorff spaces
Y
and
X
(edges and vertices) with continuous maps
r,s\colon Y\to X

that assign the range and source, respectively, to each edge. We shall use the notation
Z=(r,s\colon Y\to X)
to refer to a topological graph. When we refer to
Z
satisfying a topological property, it shall always mean
both

Y
and
X
satisfy it, unless otherwise stated.

Remark 3.1
.

A topological graph equipped with a full and continuous
r
-operator is a topological quiver in the sense of Muhly and Tomforde
[
29
]
.
If
r
is a local homeomorphism, then
r,s\colon Y\to X
is a topological graph in the sense of Katsura
[
21
]
.

Given two topological graphs
Z=(r,s\colon Y\to X)
and
Z^{\prime}=(r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\to X^{\prime})
, a
graph morphism
from
Z
to
Z^{\prime}
is given by a pair
\zeta=(\zeta_{Y},\zeta_{X})
of maps
\zeta_{Y}\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
,
\zeta_{X}\colon X\to X^{\prime}
such that
r^{\prime}\circ\zeta_{Y}=\zeta_{X}\circ r
and
s^{\prime}\circ\zeta_{Y}=\zeta_{X}\circ s
. We will use the notation
\zeta\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
to refer to a graph morphism. Graph morphisms are composed component-wise. If we refer to
\zeta
satisfying some mapping property (e.g. continuous), it shall always mean
both

\zeta_{Y}
and
\zeta_{X}
satisfy it, unless otherwise stated.

3.2.
Measured topological graphs

A
measured topological graph
is a topological graph
Z=(r,s\colon Y\to X)
together with an
r
-operator
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{R}
satisfying the additional property
\mathcal{D}s^{*}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. We shall denote a measured topological graph by
(Z,\Lambda)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
, there always exist the smallest domain and range satisfying
\Lambda(\mathcal{D})\subseteq\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{D}r^{*}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}s^{*}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. We denote them by
\mathcal{D}_{\min}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\min}
and call them the domain and range of minimal definition of
(Z,\Lambda)
. We write
\Lambda_{\min}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\min}\to\mathcal{R}_{\min}
for the restriction of
\Lambda
to
\mathcal{D}_{\min}
.

Proof.

We construct
\mathcal{D}_{\min}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\min}
recursively. Let
\mathcal{F}_{0}\coloneq C_{c}(Y)
and
\mathcal{E}_{0}\coloneq C_{c}(X)
, and for
n\geq 1
we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\overline{{}^{*}\text{-}{\rm alg}}\big(C_{c}(Y),C_{c}(Y)r^{*}\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{n-1}),C_{c}(Y)s^{*}\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{n-1})\big)\subseteq\mathcal{D};

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\overline{{}^{*}\text{-}{\rm alg}}\big(C_{c}(X),\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{n-1})\big)\subseteq\mathcal{R},

where the closures are in the inductive limit topology. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\text{min}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\text{min}}
are the closures of
\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{F}_{n}
and
\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{E}_{n}
in the inductive limit topologies.
∎

We will say a measured topological graph is

(1)

minimal
if
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{\text{min}}
and
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\text{min}}
,

(2)

maximal
if
\mathcal{D}=\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
and
\mathcal{R}=\ell_{c}^{\infty}(X)
,

(3)

continuous
if
\mathcal{D}=C_{c}(Y)
and
\mathcal{R}=C_{c}(X)
,

(4)

Baire
if
\mathcal{D}=B_{c}(Y)
,
\mathcal{R}=B_{c}(X)
and the
r
-operator is defined by a system of Baire measures
\{\lambda_{x}\}_{x\in X}
on
Y
such that
\text{supp}(\lambda_{x})\subseteq r^{-1}(x)
and
\Lambda(f)(x)=\int f{\rm d}\lambda_{x}
for all
x\in X
,

(5)

weighted
if
\mathcal{D}=\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
,
\mathcal{R}=\ell_{c}^{\infty}(X)
and the
r
-operator is defined by a weight
w\colon Y\to[0,\infty)
as in Example
2.10
, and

(6)

strictly positively weighted
if it is weighted and the corresponding weight is strictly positive.

We shall denote a weighted topological graph by
(Z,w)
. We will often consider the case where
w
takes the constant value
1
, i.e.,
w=1
.

Let
(Z,\Lambda)
and
(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
be measured topological graphs. A
measured
graph morphism from
(Z,\Lambda)
to
(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
is a graph morphism
\zeta\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
such that
\zeta^{*}_{Y}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
,
\zeta_{X}^{*}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
and
\Lambda\circ\zeta^{*}_{Y}=\zeta^{*}_{X}\circ\Lambda^{\prime}
. The left hand side here is defined as the limit, in
M(\mathcal{R})
, of
(\Lambda((f\circ\zeta_{Y})e_{i}))_{i}
for positive
f\in\mathcal{R}
, where
(e_{i})\subseteq C_{c}(Y)
is an approximate unit (i.e., a net such that
\lim_{i}e_{i}g=g
holds in
C_{c}(Y)
for all
g\in C_{c}(Y)
). We shall denote a measured graph morphism by
\zeta\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
. To aid in brevity, we shall refer to a measured graph morphism as simply a “morphism” if the notation
\zeta\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
is displayed. Let us see what it means to be a measured graph morphism in some special cases.

Let
(Z,\Lambda)
and
(Z^{\prime},\Lambda)
be two
\sigma
-compact Baire measured topological graphs, and suppose
\zeta\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
is a graph morphism. Then
\zeta
is a morphism
\zeta\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
if and only if
\zeta
is Baire measureable, and
(\zeta_{Y})_{*}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{\prime}_{\zeta_{X}(x)}
, for all
x\in X
. This characterization of morphisms holds true if, instead of assuming
\sigma
-compactness of the graphs, we assume that the pre-images of pre-compact sets under
\zeta_{Y}
and
\zeta_{X}
are pre-compact.

We consider the general case. A set
B\subseteq Y
is
locally
Baire measurable if
B\cap K
is Baire measurable, for every compact
G_{\delta}
set
K
. For a measure
\mu
, and
B
a measurable set, denote by
\mu|_{B}
the measure
A\mapsto\mu(A\cap B)
. In general, a graph morphism
\zeta\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
between two Baire measured topological graphs
(Z,\Lambda)
and
(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
is measured if and only if it is locally Baire measurable and
\lim_{K}(\zeta_{Y})_{*}(\lambda_{x}|_{K})=\lambda^{\prime}_{\zeta_{X}(x)}
, for all
x\in X
, where
K
ranges over all compact
G_{\delta}
sets.

A graph morphism
\zeta\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
between two weighted topological graphs
(Z,w)
and
(Z^{\prime},w^{\prime})
is measured if and only if

\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)\colon\zeta_{Y}(y)=y^{\prime}}w(y)=w^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\text{ for all }y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}\text{ and }x\in X\text{ satisfying }\zeta_{X}(x)=r(y^{\prime}).

In the special case where
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are weighted by
w=1
, the above equation simply says that
\zeta_{Y}\colon r^{-1}(x)\to r^{-1}(\zeta_{X}(x))
is a bijection, for all
x\in X
.

If we take any measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
, then
\text{id}_{Z}\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(Z,\Lambda_{\text{min}})
is a morphism.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
(Z,\Lambda)
,
(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
are measured topological graphs and
\zeta\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
is a morphism. If
\zeta
is continuous, then the morphism we denote by
\zeta_{\min}\colon(Z,\Lambda_{\min})\to(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}_{\min})
induced by
\zeta
is a continuous morphism.

Proof.

This follows from induction on the
∗
-sub-algebras
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{n}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{n}
. For the induction step, observe that we have
\Lambda(f\circ\zeta_{Y})=\Lambda^{\prime}(f)\circ\zeta_{X}\in M(\mathcal{R})
for all
0\leq f\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{n}
. Recall that the left hand side here is defined as the limit, in
M(\mathcal{R})
, of
(\Lambda((f\circ\zeta_{Y})e_{i}))_{i}
, where
(e_{i})\subseteq C_{c}(Y)
is an approximate unit. Now Dini’s lemma (for nets) implies that, for an approximate unit
(e_{i})
as before, the net
\Lambda((f\circ\zeta_{Y})e_{i})\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{n+1}
converges uniformly to
(\Lambda^{\prime}(f)\circ\zeta_{X})\phi
for every
\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{n+1}
and hence
(\Lambda^{\prime}(f)\circ\zeta_{X})\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{n+1}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The assignment
(Z,\Lambda)\mapsto(Z,\Lambda_{\text{min}})
,
\zeta\mapsto\zeta_{\min}
is a functor.
In fact, it is a retraction of the category of measured topological graphs with continuous morphisms onto the sub-category of minimally measured topological graphs with continuous morphisms.

3.3.
Covers of topological graphs

Let us now explain how to describe a measured topological graph equivalently in terms of continuous data, by which we mean the notion of covers as in the following definition.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
Z
be a topological graph.
A
continuously measured cover
(or simply a
cover
) of a topological graph
Z
is a continuously measured topological graph
(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})
, say
\mathcal{Z}=(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X})
and
\bm{\lambda}
is given by a system of Radon measures
\left\{\lambda_{x}\right\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
on
\mathcal{Y}
, together with a continuous and proper morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{Z}\to Z
and a (not necessarily continuous) morphism
\iota\colon Z\to\mathcal{Z}
with dense image such that
\pi\circ\iota={\rm id}_{Z}
and
\text{supp}(\lambda_{\iota_{X}(x)})\subseteq\overline{\iota_{Y}(r^{-1}(x))}
for all
x\in X
. It follows that
\iota
is an injection and
\pi
is a surjection. We will denote a cover by
(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})_{Z}
.

Remark 3.6
.

We denote the
r
-operator in the cover with a lower script Greek letter to emphasize its connections with the associated system of Radon measures. Also, we think of it as having a reduced role in the structure compared to a measured topological graph.

A
cover morphism

\mathfrak{z}_{\zeta}\colon(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})_{Z}\to(\mathcal{Z}^{\prime},\bm{\lambda}^{\prime})_{Z^{\prime}}
is a continuous morphism
\mathfrak{z}\colon(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})\to(\mathcal{Z}^{\prime},\bm{\lambda}^{\prime})
together with a (not necessarily continuous) morphism
\zeta\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
satisfying
\mathfrak{z}\circ\iota=\iota^{\prime}\circ\zeta
.
If
\zeta\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
is continuous, we additionally have
\pi^{\prime}\circ\mathfrak{z}=\zeta\circ\pi
.
In this case, we shall call
\mathfrak{z}\colon(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})_{Z}\to(\mathcal{Z}^{\prime},\bm{\lambda}^{\prime})_{Z^{\prime}}

continuous
.

We now set out to describe an equivalence of categories between measured topological graphs and covers in the sense above.
Given a cover
(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})_{Z}
, let
\mathcal{D}\coloneq\iota_{Y}^{*}(C_{c}(Y))
,
\mathcal{R}\coloneq\iota_{X}^{*}(C_{c}(X))
and set
\Lambda\coloneq(\iota_{X})^{*}\circ\lambda\circ(\iota^{*}_{Y})^{-1}
. Conversely, given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
, let
\mathcal{Y}\coloneq{\rm Spec\,}(\mathcal{D})
,
\mathcal{X}\coloneq{\rm Spec\,}(\mathcal{R})
, let the Gelfand dual of
r^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}\to M(\mathcal{R})
be
\mathfrak{r}\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
, the Gelfand dual of
s^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}\to M(\mathcal{R})
be
\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
and set
\bm{\lambda}\coloneq\Lambda\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{Y})\to C_{c}(\mathcal{X})
. The Gelfand duals of the inclusions
C_{c}(Y)\subseteq D
and
C_{c}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{R}
yield
\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}\colon\mathcal{Y}\to Y
and
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
. Moreover, there are (not necessarily continuous) mappings
\iota_{X}\colon X\to\mathcal{X}
,
\iota_{Y}\colon Y\to\mathcal{Y}
given by sending
x\in X
or
y\in Y
to the character
\text{ev}_{x}\colon D\to\mathbb{C}
or
\text{ev}_{y}\colon R\to\mathbb{C}
. Note that we are using Lemma
2.5
to identify
\mathcal{D}
with
C_{c}(\mathcal{Y})
and
\mathcal{R}
with
C_{c}(\mathcal{X})
.

At the level of morphisms, a morphism
\zeta\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
induces a morphism
\mathfrak{z}_{\zeta}\colon(\mathcal{Z},\lambda)_{Z}\to(\mathcal{Z}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})_{Z^{\prime}}
by Gelfand duality applied to
\zeta_{Y}^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\to M(\bar{D})
and
\zeta^{*}_{X}\colon\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\to M(\bar{R})
. If
\mathfrak{z}_{\zeta}\colon(\mathcal{Z},\lambda)_{Z}\to(\mathcal{Z}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})_{Z^{\prime}}
is a morphism of covers, then
\zeta\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
is a morphism.

Theorem 3.7
.

The correspondence
(Z,\Lambda)\leftrightarrow(\mathcal{Z},\lambda)_{Z}
and
\zeta\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{z}_{\zeta}
is an equivalence of categories. Continuous morphisms are sent to continuous morphisms. Moreover,
(Z,\Lambda)
is bounded below if and only if
(\mathcal{Z},\lambda)
is bounded below.

Proof.

Given a cover
(\mathcal{Z},\lambda)_{Z}
, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{D}
as defined above is a domain,
\mathcal{R}
as defined above is a range, and that
\mathcal{D}r^{*}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{D}s^{*}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. From
\text{supp}(\lambda_{\iota_{X}(x)})\subseteq\overline{\iota_{Y}(r^{-1}(x))}
for all
x\in X
, it follows that the operator
\Lambda=(\iota_{X})^{*}\circ\lambda\circ(\iota^{*}_{Y})^{-1}
is an
r
-operator
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{R}
and hence
(Z,\Lambda)
is a measured topological graph.

Conversely, given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
, it is easy to see that
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}
as defined above are continuous surjections satisfying
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathfrak{r}=r\circ\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}
as well as
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathfrak{s}=s\circ\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}
. Moreover,
\bm{\lambda}
as defined above is a positive linear operator
C_{c}(\mathcal{Y})\to C_{c}(\mathcal{X})
satisfying
\bm{\lambda}(f(g\circ\tilde{r}))=\bm{\lambda}(f)g
for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{Y})
and
g\in C_{c}(\mathcal{X})
. It thus follows from Lemma
2.1
that
\bm{\lambda}
is an
\mathfrak{r}
-system. In addition, it is easy to see that
\iota_{X}
and
\iota_{Y}
as constructed above have dense images and satisfy the relations
\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\iota_{Y}=\text{id}_{Y}
,
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\iota_{X}=\text{id}_{X}
and
\iota_{X}^{*}\circ\bm{\lambda}=\Lambda\circ\iota_{Y}^{*}
.

Lemma
2.5
implies that, up to canonical isomorphisms, our constructions are inverse to each other.
∎

3.4.
Applications

The forgetful functor
(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})_{Z}\mapsto(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})
,
\mathfrak{z}_{\zeta}\mapsto\zeta
, goes from the category of measured topological graphs (with continuous morphisms) to the category of continuously measured topological graphs (also with continuous morphisms).
We will specifically be interested in the functor

(Z,\Lambda)\mapsto(Z,\Lambda_{\min})\mapsto(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})_{Z}\mapsto(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})\eqcolon(\hat{Z},\hat{\bm{\lambda}}),

where the first arrow is given by restriction to domain and range of minimal definition (see Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
as well as Remark
3.4
), the second arrow is given by the correspondence in Theorem
3.7
and the last arrow is the forgetful functor.

Definition 3.8
.

We call
(\hat{Z},\hat{\bm{\lambda}})
the
minimal cover
of
(Z,\Lambda)
. We write
\hat{Z}=(\hat{r},\hat{s}\colon\hat{Y}\to\hat{X})
and denote the structure maps by
\pi\colon\hat{Z}\to Z
the continuous, proper morphism and by
\iota\colon Z\to\hat{Z}
the (not necessarily continuous) morphism.

This cover satisfies a universal property which we explain now. First, observe that by (the proof of) the category correspondence in Theorem
3.7
,
\iota\colon Z\to\hat{Z}
is actually a morphism
\iota\colon(Z,\Lambda_{\text{min}})\to(\hat{Z},\hat{\bm{\lambda}})
. Therefore,
\iota\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(\hat{Z},\hat{\bm{\lambda}})
is a morphism.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that
(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
is another continuously measured topological graph that covers
(Z,\Lambda)
in the sense that there is a continuous proper morphism
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{Z}\to Z
and a morphism
\dot{\iota}\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
with dense image such that
\dot{\pi}\circ\dot{\iota}=\text{id}_{Z}.
Then there is a unique continuous proper morphism
\zeta\colon(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})\to(\hat{Z},\hat{\bm{\lambda}})
such that
\pi\circ\zeta=\dot{\pi}
and
\zeta\circ\dot{\iota}=\iota
.

Proof.

Since
\dot{\iota}\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
is a morphism, it follows that
(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})_{Z}
is a cover of
Z
. Since
\dot{\iota}^{*}_{X}\circ\dot{\bm{\lambda}}=\Lambda\circ\dot{\iota}^{*}_{Y}
, its corresponding measured topological graph is of the form
\Lambda^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
given by the restriction of
\Lambda
, where
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Hence
\mathcal{D}_{\text{min}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\text{min}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
, so that there is a morphism
\text{id}_{Z}\colon(Z,\Lambda^{\prime})\to(Z,\Lambda_{\text{min}})
. Apply the cover functor from Theorem
3.7
and the forgetful functor to this morphism to obtain a morphism
\zeta\colon(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})\to(\hat{Z},\hat{\bm{\lambda}})
satisfying
\pi\circ\zeta=\text{id}_{Z}\circ\dot{\pi}=\dot{\pi}
and
\zeta\circ\dot{\iota}=\text{id}_{Z}\circ\iota=\iota
.
∎

Let us consider some special cases of this theorem.

We first consider Baire measured topological graphs. Suppose
\dot{\iota}\colon Z\to\dot{Z}
is a morphism which satisfies
\dot{\pi}\circ\dot{\iota}=\text{id}_{Z}
for some continuous morphism
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{Z}\to Z
. Then the inverse image of pre-compact sets by
\dot{\iota}
are pre-compact. It follows that
\dot{\iota}\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
from a Baire measured graph into a continuously measured topological graph is a morphism if and only if
\dot{\iota}
is Baire measurable and the measures
(\dot{\iota}_{Y})_{*}\lambda_{x}
,
\dot{\lambda}_{\iota_{X}(x)}
agree on
C_{c}(\dot{Y})
, for all
x\in X
. Since every characteristic function of a compact
G_{\delta}
set is a sequential limit of continuous and compactly supported functions, it follows that
(\dot{\iota}_{Y})_{*}\lambda_{x}=\dot{\lambda}_{\iota_{X}(x)}
for all
x\in X
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Suppose
(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
is a continuously measured topological graph that covers the Baire measured graph
(Z,\Lambda)
in the sense that there is a continuous and proper morphism
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{Z}\to Z
and a Baire measurable morphism
\dot{\iota}\colon Z\to\dot{Z}
with dense image satisfying
\dot{\pi}\circ\dot{\iota}=\text{id}_{Z}
and
(\dot{\iota}_{Y})_{*}\lambda_{x}=\dot{\lambda}_{\dot{\iota}_{X}(x)}
for all
x\in X
. Then there is a unique continuous and proper morphism
\zeta\colon\dot{Z}\to\hat{Z}
satisfying
\pi\circ\zeta=\dot{\pi}
,
\zeta\circ\dot{\iota}=\iota
and
(\zeta_{Y})_{*}\dot{\lambda}_{\dot{x}}=\hat{\lambda}_{\zeta_{X}(\dot{x})}
for all
\dot{x}\in\dot{X}
.

Let us see what the universal property corresponds to for strictly positively weighted topological graphs. Note that by Theorem
3.7
, any cover of a strictly positively weighted topological graph is also strictly positively weighted.

Corollary 3.11
.

Suppose
(\dot{Z},\dot{w})
is a continuously weighted topological graph that covers the strictly positively weighted graph
(Z,w)
in the sense that there is a continuous and proper morphism
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{Z}\to Z
and a morphism
\dot{\iota}\colon Z\to\dot{Z}
with dense image satisfying
\dot{\pi}\circ\dot{\iota}=\text{id}_{Z}
and

\sum_{y\in r^{-1}(x)\colon\dot{\iota}_{Y}(y)=\dot{y}}w(y)=\dot{w}(\dot{y})\text{ for all }\dot{y}\in\dot{Y}\text{ and }x\in X\text{ satisfying }\dot{\iota}_{X}(x)=r(\dot{y}).

Then there is a unique continuous and proper morphism
\zeta\colon\dot{Z}\to\hat{Z}
satisfying
\pi\circ\zeta=\dot{\pi}
,
\zeta\circ\dot{\iota}=\iota
and

\sum_{\dot{y}\in r^{-1}(\dot{x})\colon\zeta_{\dot{Y}}(\dot{y})=\hat{y}}\dot{w}(y)=\hat{w}(\hat{y})\text{ for all }\hat{y}\in\hat{Y}\text{ and }x\in\dot{X}\text{ satisfying }\zeta_{X}(\dot{x})=r(\hat{y}).

Example 3.12
.

The range map of a strictly positively weighted topological graph is locally injective and is weighted by
w=1
if and only if the
r
-operator satisfies the following property:
for every
f\in C_{c}(Y)
, there is a closed ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
f\cdot\mathcal{I}\neq 0
and
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{I}\to\ell^{\infty}_{c}(X)
is a
∗
-homomorphism. It follows from this characterization, Lemma
2.5
, and Lemma
2.3
that the range map of the cover associated to such a topological graph is a local homeomorphism, and is also weighted by
w=1
. Such graphs and their cover form an important example class for this paper.

If
(Z,w)
is a weighted topological graph that is not strictly positively weighted, then the
r
-system from the minimal cover is not necessarily induced by a weight.
Therefore, we consider them in the category of maximally measured topological graphs. Let us investigate the universal property in this category. For
x\in X
, let
\Lambda_{x}\coloneq\Lambda(-)(x)
.

Corollary 3.13
.

Suppose
(\dot{Z},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
is a continuously measured topological graph that covers the maximally measured graph
(Z,\Lambda)
in the sense that there is a continuous and proper morphism
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{Z}\to Z
and a morphism
\dot{\iota}\colon Z\to\dot{Z}
with dense image such that
\dot{\pi}\circ\dot{\iota}=\text{id}_{Z}
and
(\dot{\iota}_{Y})_{*}\Lambda_{x}
is an extension of
\dot{\lambda}_{\dot{\iota}_{X}(x)}
, for all
x\in X
. Then there is a unique continuous and proper morphism
\zeta\colon\dot{Z}\to\hat{Z}
satisfying
\pi\circ\zeta=\dot{\pi}
,
\zeta\circ\dot{\iota}=\iota
and
(\zeta_{Y})_{*}\dot{\lambda}_{\dot{x}}=\hat{\lambda}_{\zeta_{X}(\dot{x})}
for all
\dot{x}\in\dot{X}
.

Remark 3.14
.

The difference in these three corollaries highlights the importance of the choice of ambient domain and range
∗
-sub-algebras.

3.5.
Description of the covers

Recall the terminology from the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
Given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
, let
X_{n}={\rm Spec\,}(\mathcal{E}_{n})
and
Y_{n}={\rm Spec\,}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
. By Gelfand duality, there are maps
X_{n}\to X_{n-1}
,
Y_{n}\to Y_{n-1}
such that
\hat{X}
and
\hat{Y}
are naturally identified with the projective limits
\varprojlim_{n}X_{n}
and
\varprojlim_{n}Y_{n}
. Let us now describe
X_{n}
and
Y_{n}
in terms of
X_{n-1}
and
Y_{n-1}
. Let
\mathcal{M}(Y_{n-1})
be the space of Radon measures on
Y_{n-1}
, which we identify with the positive linear functionals on
C_{c}(Y_{n-1})
equipped with the weak* topology.

Let
\iota_{n,X}\colon X\to X_{n}
and
\iota_{n,Y}\colon Y\to Y_{n}
be the canonical inclusions as characters of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. For
x\in X
, let
x_{n}\coloneq\iota_{n,X}(x)
, and
\lambda_{n-1,x_{n}}\coloneq\Lambda(-)(x)\colon\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\to\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 3.15
.

X_{n}
is (homeomorphic to) the closure of the image of the map

X\to X\times\mathcal{M}(Y_{n-1}),\,x\mapsto(x,\lambda_{n-1,x_{n}}).

Proof.

For the purpose of the proof, we call the closure of the image
\bar{X}
. We start by defining a map
\bar{X}\to X_{n}
: given
\bar{x}=(x,\bar{\lambda})=\lim_{i}(x_{i},\lambda_{n-1,(x_{i})_{n}})
, define a character
\chi\colon\mathcal{E}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
by sending
f\in C_{c}(X)
to
f(x)=\lim f(x_{i})
and
g=\Lambda(h)\in\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{n-1})
, for
h\in\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
, to
\lim g((x_{i})_{n})=\lim\lambda_{n-1,(x_{i})_{n}}(h)=\bar{\lambda}(h)
. By construction,
\chi=\lim\operatorname{ev}_{\iota_{n,X}(x_{i})}
, so that our character extends continuously to a character (again denoted by
\chi
) on
\mathcal{E}_{n}
. By construction, the map sending
\bar{x}\mapsto\chi(g)
is continuous for any generator

. Therefore, our map
\bar{x}\mapsto\chi
is continuous.

Now we construct a map
X_{n}\to\bar{X}
: given
\chi\in X_{n}={\rm Spec\,}(\mathcal{E}_{n})
, the restriction
\chi|_{C_{c}(X)}
is non-zero as
\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{n-1})\subseteq C_{c}(X)\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{n-1})
. Hence
\chi|_{C_{c}(X)}=\operatorname{ev}_{x}
for some

. Moreover, the mapping
\chi\mapsto(x,\bar{\lambda})
, where
\bar{\lambda}\coloneq\chi\circ\Lambda|_{\mathcal{F}_{n-1}}\in\mathcal{M}(Y_{n-1})
is continuous, and its image lies in
\bar{X}
because
X
is dense in
X_{n}
and our map sends
x\in X
to
(x,\lambda_{n-1,x_{n}})
.

It is easy to see these two maps are inverse to each other.
∎

As before, in the following, we use the notation
y_{n}\coloneq\iota_{n,Y}(y)
for
y\in Y
.

Proposition 3.16
.

Y_{n}
is (homeomorphic to) the closure of the image of the map

Y\to Y\times\mathcal{M}(Y_{n-1})\times\mathcal{M}(Y_{n-1}),\,y\mapsto(y,\lambda_{r(y)_{n}},\lambda_{s(y)_{n}}).

Proof.

For the purpose of the proof, let us call the closure of the image
\bar{Y}
.
We start by defining a map
\bar{Y}\to Y_{n}
: given
\bar{y}=(y,\bar{\lambda}_{r},\bar{\lambda}_{s})=\lim_{i}(y_{i},\lambda_{r(y_{i})_{n}},\lambda_{s(y_{i})_{n}})
, define a character
\chi\colon\mathcal{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
by sending
f\in C_{c}(Y)
to
f(y)=\lim_{i}f(y_{i})
,
g=ar^{*}\Lambda(b)\in C_{c}(Y)r^{*}\Lambda(\mathcal{F}_{n-1})
, for

and

\text{lim}_{i}g((y_{i})_{n})=\text{lim}_{i}a(y_{i})\lambda_{r(y_{i})_{n}}(b)=a(y)\bar{\lambda}_{r}(b)

By construction,
\chi=\lim\operatorname{ev}_{\iota_{n,Y}(y_{i})}
, so that our character extends continuously to a character (again denoted by
\chi
) on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. It is easy to see our map sending
\bar{y}
to
\chi
is continuous.

Now we construct a map
Y_{n}\to\bar{Y}
: given
\chi\in Y_{n}={\rm Spec\,}(\mathcal{F}_{n})

3.6.
Essential covers

We now define covers of topological graphs with better regularity properties and that are easier to compute.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
Z=(r,s\colon Y\to X)
be a topological graph. We say a subset
\mathscr{X}\subseteq X
is
backwards invariant
if
s(y)\in\mathscr{X}
whenever
r(y)\in\mathscr{X}
for all
y\in Y
, i.e.,
s(r^{-1}(\mathscr{X}))\subseteq\mathscr{X}
. Then
\mathscr{Y}=r^{-1}(\mathscr{X})
and
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}\coloneq(r,s\colon\mathscr{Y}\to\mathscr{X})
defines a sub-graph of
Z
. We call such a sub-graph arising in this way
invariant
.

Recall that a subset
\mathscr{X}
of a topological space
X
is
co-meagre
if its complement is meagre, i.e., a countable union of sets whose closure have empty interior. In case
X
is locally compact and Hausdorff (more generally a Baire space),
\mathscr{X}\subseteq X
is co-meagre if and only if it contains a dense
G_{\delta}
set.

Definition 3.18
.

An
essential cover
of
Z
is a continuously measured graph
(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})
together with a proper and continuous surjection
\pi\colon\mathcal{Z}\to Z
with the property that there is an invariant sub-graph
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}
of
Z
such that
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}
and the restriction
\mathcal{Z}|_{\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{X})}
to the (automatically invariant) set
\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{X})
are co-meagre, and the restriction of
\pi
gives rise to a bijection
\mathcal{Z}|_{\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{X})}\to Z|_{\mathscr{X}}
.

Our first example of essential covers comes from a restriction of the cover
(\mathcal{Z},\bm{\lambda})_{Z}
of a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
from the correspondence of Theorem
3.7
.

Definition 3.19
.

We call a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)

essentially continuous
if there is a co-meagre invariant sub-graph
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}\subseteq Z
such that
\iota\colon Z|_{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathcal{Z}
is continuous. In that case, let
\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}}\coloneq\overline{\iota(Z|_{\mathscr{X}})}
(with edge space
\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}}
and vertex space
\mathcal{X}_{{\rm ess}}
), let
\pi_{{\rm ess}}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}}\to Z
be the restriction of
\pi
to
\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}}
, and let
\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}}\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}})\to C_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{{\rm ess}})
be the restriction of
\bm{\lambda}
to
C_{c}(\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}})
.

We need the following characterisation of the continuity points of
\iota\colon Z\to\mathcal{Z}
.

Proposition 3.20
.

Suppose that
\pi\colon\mathcal{W}\to W
is a continuous, proper surjection between locally compact Hausdorff spaces,
C
is a subset of
W
,
\iota\colon C\to\mathcal{W}
is a map satisfying
\pi\circ\iota=\text{id}_{C}
and that
\iota(C)
is dense in
\mathcal{W}
. Then
\iota\colon C\to\mathcal{W}
is continuous at
w\in C
if and only if
\pi^{-1}(w)=\{\iota(w)\}
.

Proof.

Suppose
|\pi^{-1}(w)|=1
. By properness of
\pi
, for every open neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
of
\pi^{-1}(w)=\{\iota(w)\}
, there is an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq W
of
w
such that
\iota(U\cap C)\subseteq\pi^{-1}(U)\subseteq\mathcal{U}
. Hence,
\iota
is continuous at
w
.

If
|\pi^{-1}(w)|>1
, then let
\iota(w)\neq\omega\in\pi^{-1}(w)
. By density of
\iota(C)
, there is a net
(w_{i})
in
C
such that
(\iota(w_{i}))
converges to
\omega
. Therefore,
(w_{i}=\pi(\iota(w_{i})))
converges to
w
but
\iota(w)\neq\omega
. Hence,
\iota
is not continuous at
w
.
∎

Corollary 3.21
.

Given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
which is essentially continuous,
(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})
is an essential cover.

Proof.

Since
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}
is an invariant sub-graph and
\iota^{*}_{X}\circ\bm{\lambda}=\Lambda\circ\iota^{*}_{Y}
,
\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}}
is a closed sub-graph of
\mathcal{Z}
such that
{\rm supp}(\lambda_{\chi})\subseteq r^{-1}(\chi)\cap\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}}
, for all
\chi\in\mathcal{X}_{{\rm ess}}
. Hence the restriction
\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}}
of
\bm{\lambda}
is an
r
-operator
C_{c}(\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}})\to C_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{{\rm ess}})
. Moreover, density of
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}
implies that the restriction
\pi_{{\rm ess}}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}}\to Z
of
\pi
remains a surjection. Since
\iota\colon Z\to\mathcal{Z}
is a section of
\pi
, Proposition
3.20
implies that
\iota(Z|_{\mathscr{X}})=\pi_{{\rm ess}}^{-1}(Z|_{\mathscr{X}})
. This proves that
(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})
is an essential cover.
∎

Definition 3.22
.

Given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
which is essentially continuous, we call
(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})

the essential cover of

(Z,\Lambda)
.

We consider an alternative construction of the essential cover through the Gelfand correspondence which explains the name and shows that the essential cover is independent of the choice of invariant sub-graph
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}
.

A topological measured graph
(Z,\Lambda)
is essentially continuous if and only if there is a co-meagre invariant sub-graph
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}=(r,s\colon\mathscr{Y}\to\mathscr{X})
such that
f|_{\mathscr{X}}
is continuous for all
f\in\mathcal{R}
and and
g|_{\mathscr{Y}}
is continuous for all
g\in\mathcal{D}
. Given an essentially continuous measured graph
(Z,\Lambda)
, we define the
essential ideals

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
to be the collection of functions that are zero on a co-meagre set. These ideals are closed by the Baire category theorem. We call the quotients
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm ess}}\coloneq\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{{\rm ess}}\coloneq\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}
the
essential domain
and the
essential range
.

Using the Baire category theorem and continuity of functions in
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathscr{Y}
, it follows that
f\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}
if and only if
f|_{\mathscr{Y}}=0
. Similarly, we obtain that
g\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{R}}
if and only if
g|_{\mathscr{X}}=0
. So, for
f\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}
, by invariance
(r^{-1}(\mathscr{X})=\mathscr{Y})
, we have
\Lambda(f)|_{\mathscr{X}}=\Lambda(f|_{\mathscr{Y}})=0.
Hence,
\Lambda(\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{R}}
. Therefore,
\Lambda
induces a positive operator
\Lambda_{{\rm ess}}\colon\mathcal{D}_{{\rm ess}}\to\mathcal{R}_{{\rm ess}}
with the property that
\Lambda_{{\rm ess}}(f)(x)=0
whenever
x\in\mathscr{X}
and
f\in\mathcal{D}_{{\rm ess}}
satisfy
f|_{r^{-1}(x)}=0
. The properties
g\circ r|_{\mathscr{Y}}=0
and
g\circ s|_{\mathscr{Y}}=0
for any
g\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{R}}
and the ideal characterization above implies the
∗
-homomorphisms
r^{*},s^{*}\colon\mathcal{R}\to M(\mathcal{D})
descend to
∗
-homomorphisms
r^{*},s^{*}\colon\mathcal{R}_{{\rm ess}}\to M(\mathcal{D}_{{\rm ess}})
.

Using the facts that, by Lemma
2.5
, the Gelfand transforms
\hat{(-)}\colon\mathcal{D}\to C_{c}(\mathcal{Y})
and
\hat{(-)}\colon\mathcal{R}\to C_{c}(\mathcal{X})
are homeomorphisms (see also Theorem
3.7
, where
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{X}
are defined), every closed ideal in
C_{c}(W)
— for

a locally compact Hausdorff space — is of the form

for some open subspace

in
W
, and the above descriptions of the ideals
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{R}}
, it follows that
\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{D}}=C_{c}(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{{\rm ess}})
and
\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{R}}=C_{c}(\mathcal{Y}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}})
, where
\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}}=\overline{\iota_{Y}(\mathscr{Y})}
and
\mathcal{X}_{{\rm ess}}=\overline{\iota_{X}(\mathscr{X})}
as above, and we may identify
\Lambda_{{\rm ess}}
with the restriction
\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}}\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}})\to C_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{{\rm ess}})
of

.

Since the construction of the ideals

and

does not involve a choice of invariant sub-graph

Remark 3.23
.

Since essential covers are only defined from the essential domain and range, a morphism of covers is not necessarily uniquely determined by a morphism of the associated measured graphs.

We introduce a property of functions that is relevant for essential covers.

Definition 3.24
.

A continuous map
f\colon Y\to X
is called
weakly open
if
f^{-1}(U)
is dense whenever
U
is open and dense.

Note that this is equivalent to the statement that
{\rm int}(f(V))\neq\emptyset
for every open set
V\subseteq Y
when
X
and

are locally compact and Hausdorff. This last property is sometimes called
quasi-open
in the literature (see for instance
[
3
]
). For Baire spaces

and
Y
,

is weakly open if and only if

is co-meagre whenever
U
is co-meagre.

Proposition 3.25
.

Suppose
\pi\colon\mathcal{W}\to W
is a continuous and proper surjection between locally compact Hausdorff spaces,
C
is a co-meagre subset of
W
and
\iota\colon C\to\mathcal{W}
is a continuous section (in the sense that
\pi\circ\iota=\text{id}_{C}
) such that
\iota(C)
is dense in
\mathcal{W}
. Then
\pi
and
\iota
are weakly open.

Proof.

If
U
is a dense open subset of
W
, then by continuity of
\iota
and Proposition
3.20
, we have
\pi^{-1}(C\cap U)=\iota(C\cap U)
. By the Baire category theorem,
C\cap U
is dense in
W
, so continuity of
\iota
and density of
C\cap U
,
\iota(C)
imply that
\iota(C\cap U)
is dense in
\mathcal{W}
. Therefore,
\pi^{-1}(U)
is dense.

Note that
\iota(C)=\pi^{-1}(C)
, and therefore
\iota(C)
is a co-meagre subset of
\mathcal{W}
. So, given a dense open set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{W}
,
\mathcal{U}\cap\iota(C)
is dense in
\mathcal{W}
by the Baire category theorem. Since
\pi
is a continuous surjection,
\pi(\mathcal{U}\cap\iota(C))=\iota^{-1}(\mathcal{U})
is dense in

and therefore dense in
C
.
∎

Just as in the case for continuously measured topological graphs, there is a topological obstruction for a graph
Z
to be essentially continuously measured.

Corollary 3.26
.

If
(Z,\Lambda)
is full and essentially continuously measured, where
Z=(r,s\colon Y\to X)
, then
r\colon Y\to X
is weakly open.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})
be the associated essential cover and
\iota\colon Z|_{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}}
a continuous section defined on the invariant co-meagre sub-graph
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}
.
Then, by Proposition
3.20
and invariance of
Z|_{\mathscr{X}}
, for all
x\in\mathscr{X}
, we have
r^{-1}(\iota_{X}(x))=r^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(x))=\pi^{-1}(r^{-1}(x))=\iota_{Y}(r^{-1}(x))
.
Hence, by fullness of
\Lambda
, it follows that
(\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})_{\chi}\colon C_{c}(r^{-1}(\chi))\to\mathbb{C}
is faithful for all
\chi
in the co-meagre set
\iota_{X}(\mathscr{X})
. It then follows by continuity of
\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}}
and an argument similar to Lemma
2.3
(using Lemma

) that
r\colon\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm ess}}\to\mathcal{X}_{{\rm ess}}
satisfies
\emptyset\neq r(\mathcal{U}\cap\iota_{Y}(\mathscr{Y}))\subseteq{\rm int}(r(\mathcal{U}))
for
\mathcal{U}
of the form
\mathcal{U}=\left\{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\rm ess}\colon f(y)>0\right\}
, where

. Hence

is weakly open. Since

can be written as

, it follows from Proposition

that

is weakly open and therefore (by the Baire category theorem) so is

.
∎

3.7.
Minimal essential cover

Given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
, when is its minimal restriction
(Z,\Lambda_{\text{min}})
essentially continuous?
Let us provide a sufficient condition called post-critical sparsity.

Given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
, we consider the set of critial vertices

C_{\Lambda}\coloneq\left\{x\in X\colon\Lambda(f)\text{ is not continuous at }x,\text{ for some }f\in C_{c}(Y)\right\}.

We also consider the collection of all forward orbits of
C_{\Lambda}
, which is the set

P_{\Lambda}\coloneq\left\{x\in X\colon\exists\ y_{n},\dotsc,y_{1}\in Y,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ with }s(y_{i+1})=r(y_{i})\ \forall\ 1\leq i<n\text{ and }r(y_{n})=x,\ s(y_{1})\in C_{\Lambda}\right\}.

We call
x\in P_{\Lambda}
a
post-critical vertex
for
(Z,\Lambda)
.
Notice that
r(s^{-1}(P_{\Lambda}))\subseteq P_{\Lambda}
, so the set
X\setminus P_{\Lambda}
satisfies
s(r^{-1}(X\setminus P_{\Lambda}))\subseteq X\setminus P_{\Lambda}
.
It follows that
R_{\Lambda}\coloneq(r,s\colon Y\setminus r^{-1}(P_{\Lambda})\to X\setminus P_{\Lambda})
is an invariant sub-graph of
Z
. We will call
R_{\Lambda}
the
regular
sub-graph of
(Z,\Lambda)
. The following proposition justifies the name.

Proposition 3.27
.

Let
(Z,\Lambda)
be a measured topological graph and
(\mathcal{Z},\lambda)
its associated cover. Then
\iota\colon Z\to\mathcal{Z}
is continuous at
z\in R_{\Lambda}
.

Note that the conclusion we obtain is actually stronger than continuity of the restriction of
\iota
to
R_{\Lambda}
, viewed as a map
R_{\Lambda}\to\mathcal{Z}
. For the proof, we show that the map
\iota_{X}
is continuous at
x\in X
if and only if every
f\in\mathcal{R}
is continuous at
x
, and a similar statement is true for
\iota_{Y}
. The following lemma is the key step.

Lemma 3.28
.

If
x\in X
is not a critical vertex and
f\in\mathcal{D}
is continuous at every point
y\in r^{-1}(x)
, then
\Lambda(f)
is continuous at
x
.

Proof.

By the Tietze extension theorem and the hypothesis, we may choose
\tilde{f}\in C_{c}(Y)
such that
f|_{r^{-1}(x)}=\tilde{f}|_{r^{-1}(x)}
. Let
g(\tilde{x})\coloneq\sup_{\tilde{y}\in r^{-1}(\tilde{x})}|f(\tilde{y})-\tilde{f}(\tilde{y})|
. By continuity at points
y\in r^{-1}(x)
and compactness of the supports of
\tilde{f}
and
f
, we have that
g
is continuous at
x
. Let
0\leq\phi\in C_{c}(Y)
be such that
|f-\tilde{f}|\leq(g\circ r)\phi
. Positivity of
\Lambda
implies
\Lambda(|f-\tilde{f}|)\leq g\Lambda(\phi)
. So, by continuity of
g
and
\Lambda(\phi)
at
x
, it follows that
\Lambda(|f-\tilde{f}|)
is continuous at
x
. In addition, our choice of
\tilde{f}
ensures that
\Lambda(|f-\tilde{f}|)(x)=0
. Therefore, continuity of
\Lambda(\tilde{f})
at
x
and
|\Lambda(f-\tilde{f})|\leq\Lambda(|f-\tilde{f}|)
imply that
\Lambda(f)
is continuous at
x
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.27
.

The argument is by induction on the
∗
-sub-algebras
\mathcal{E}_{n}
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
appearing in Section
3.2
, using Lemma
3.28
and the fact that
s^{-1}(P_{\Lambda})\subseteq r^{-1}(P_{\Lambda})
.
∎

Definition 3.29
.

We call a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)

post-critically sparse
if
P_{\Lambda}
and
r^{-1}(P_{\Lambda})
are contained in meagre sets.

If
(Z,\Lambda)
is post-critically sparse, then Proposition
3.27
implies that
(Z,\Lambda_{\text{min}})
is essentially continuous, with continuous section
\iota\colon R_{\Lambda}\to\mathcal{Z}
of the minimal cover.

Definition 3.30
.

Given a measured topological graph
(Z,\Lambda)
which is post-critically sparse, we define
\tilde{Z}\coloneq\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm ess}}
,
\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}\coloneq\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}}
and call the continuously measured graph
(\tilde{Z},\tilde{\bm{\lambda}})

the minimal essential cover of

(Z,\Lambda)
. We write
\tilde{\pi}
for the canonical projection
\tilde{Z}\to Z
.

Below, we describe the minimality property of
(\tilde{Z},\tilde{\bm{\lambda}})
.
If
\Lambda
is an
r
-operator, we let
\lambda_{x}
denote the restriction of the linear functional
\Lambda(-)(x)
to
C_{c}(Y)

Note that
\tilde{\pi}
satisfies
(\tilde{\pi}_{\tilde{Y}})_{*}(\tilde{\lambda}_{x})=\lambda_{\tilde{\pi}_{\tilde{X}}(x)}
on a dense subset of

.

Theorem 3.31
.

Let
(Z,\Lambda)
be a post-critically sparse measured topological graph. Suppose that
(\dot{Z},\dot{\Lambda})
along with
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{Z}\to Z

(\dot{\pi}_{\dot{Y}})_{*}(\dot{\lambda}_{x})=\lambda_{\dot{\pi}_{\dot{X}}(x)}
for all

.

Before proving this, we require a lemma.

Lemma 3.32
.

Let
(Z,\Lambda)
and
(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
be post-critically sparse topological graphs. If
\zeta\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
is a continuous and proper morphism and
(\zeta_{Y})_{*}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{\prime}_{\zeta_{X}(x)}
for all

in a dense subset of

(\zeta_{Y})_{*}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{\prime}_{\zeta_{X}(x)}

. Moreover,

(2)

\Lambda(f\circ\zeta_{Y})(x)=\Lambda^{\prime}(f)(\zeta_{X}(x)),

for all
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\min}^{\prime}
and
x\in(X\setminus P_{\Lambda})\cap\zeta_{X}^{-1}(X^{\prime}\setminus P_{\Lambda^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a dense subset of
X
such that
(\zeta_{Y})_{*}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{\prime}_{\zeta_{X}(x)}
for all
x\in\mathscr{X}
.
By density of
\mathscr{X}
and continuity of
\Lambda(f\circ\zeta_{Y})
and
\Lambda^{\prime}(f)\circ\zeta_{X}
on

(which is true by construction),
it follows that this equation holds for all
x\in(X\setminus C_{\Lambda})\cap\zeta_{X}^{-1}(X^{\prime}\setminus C_{\Lambda^{\prime}})

For

and

Therefore

(3)

\Lambda(f\circ\zeta_{Y})(x)=\lambda_{x}(\tilde{f}\circ\zeta_{Y})=\lambda^{\prime}_{\zeta_{X}(x)}(\tilde{f})=\Lambda^{\prime}(f)(\zeta_{X}(x)),

Proof of Theorem
3.31
.

Let us prove uniqueness, assuming existence. Let
\mathscr{Z}=(r,s\colon\mathscr{Y}\to\mathscr{X})\subseteq Z

\dot{\pi}^{-1}(\mathscr{Z})\eqcolon\dot{\mathscr{Z}}=(r,s\colon\dot{\mathscr{Y}}\to\dot{\mathscr{X}})\subseteq\dot{Z}

\zeta

\dot{\Lambda}(f\circ\dot{\pi}_{\dot{Y}})(x)=\Lambda(f)(\dot{\pi}_{\dot{X}}(x)),

4.
The case of a single transformation

In this section, we study our constructions in the special case of dynamical systems, i.e, topological graphs with
Y=X
,
s=\text{id}_{X}
and

continuous, where
X
is a locally compact Hausdorff space. From the minimal cover as a topological graph, we construct the minimal cover as a dynamical system.

Remark 4.1
.

As we explain, the minimal cover of
T\colon X\to X
as a dynamical system can also be constructed directly, and this direct construction would not even require continuity of
T
.

4.1.
Measured topological dynamical systems

We say a topological dynamical system
T\colon X\to X
is
measured
if it is measured as a topological graph, with
\mathcal{B}\coloneq\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{R}
and a
T
-operator
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
. We denote a measured dynamical system by
(T,\Lambda)
and call
\Lambda
its transfer operator.

Definition 4.2
.

We let
\mathcal{B}_{\min}
be the smallest
∗
-sub-algebra of
\ell^{\infty}_{c}(X)
which is closed in the inductive limit topology, contains
C_{c}(X)
and satisfies
C_{c}(X)T^{*}\mathcal{B}_{\min}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\min}
and
\Lambda(\mathcal{B}_{\min})\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\min}
. We write
\Lambda_{\min}
for the restriction of
\Lambda
to
\mathcal{B}_{\min}
and call
(T,\Lambda_{\min})
the
minimal dynamical restriction
.

Just like the minimal restriction for measured topological graphs,
\mathcal{B}_{\text{min}}
can be sequentially generated by
∗
-sub-algebras defined as
\mathcal{B}_{0}=C_{c}(X)
and for all
n\geq 1
,

\mathcal{B}_{n}\coloneq\overline{{}^{*}\text{-}{\rm alg}}\big(C_{c}(X),\Lambda(\mathcal{B}_{n-1}),C_{c}(X)T^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{n-1})\big).

Then
\mathcal{B}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by construction and it is easy to see that
\mathcal{B}_{\min}
is given by the closure of
\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}
in the inductive limit topology.

By an argument similar to the proof of Lemma
3.3
, if
\chi\colon(T,\Lambda)\to(T^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
is a continuous morphism between measured dynamical systems, then so is the morphism
\chi_{\min}\colon(T,\Lambda_{\text{min}})\to(T^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}_{\text{min}})
induced by
\chi
.

If we view
(T,\Lambda)
as a measured topological graph, then its minimal restriction may not necessarily be another measured dynamical system. We have
\mathcal{D}_{\min}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\min}
but equality does not necessarily hold. However, it turns out that
\mathcal{B}_{\min}
is generated by
C_{c}(X)(T^{n})^{*}\mathcal{R}_{\text{min}}
,
n\geq 0
.

From a topological graph
Z
, we can construct a dynamical system.
Assuming
s\colon Y\to X
is proper, the
left infinite path space

Z^{-\infty}\coloneq\left\{(\ldots,y_{-2},y_{-1})\in\prod_{i<0}Y\colon s(y_{i})=r(y_{i+1})\text{ }\forall i<0\right\}
,
equipped with the relative topology from the product topology on
\prod_{i<0}Y
, is locally compact,
and there is a naturally defined dynamical system on
Z^{-\infty}
given by
\sigma(\ldots,y_{-2},y_{-1})=(\ldots,y_{-3},y_{-2})
, called the
shift
.

If
(Z,\Lambda)
is a measured topological graph with
s\colon Y\to X
proper, we can induce a measured structure on
\sigma\colon Z^{-\infty}\to Z^{-\infty}
as follows.
Let
\mathcal{B}^{-\infty}\subseteq\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Z^{-\infty})
be the closed
∗
-algebra generated by functions of the form
\underline{g}=g_{-n}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{-1}
for
g_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
,
where
\underline{g}(\underline{y})=g_{-n}(y_{-n})\cdots g_{-1}(y_{-1})
for all
\underline{y}=(...,y_{-2},y_{-1})\in Z^{-\infty}
.
Then
\underline{g}\circ\sigma=\underline{g}\otimes 1
, and from this it is easy to see
\sigma^{*}(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})\subseteq M(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})
.
For
\underline{g}
as above, let
\Lambda_{-\infty}(\underline{g})=g_{-n}\otimes\cdots\otimes(g_{-2}(\Lambda(g_{-1})\circ s))
.
This is well-defined and extends continuously to a
\sigma
-operator
\Lambda_{-\infty}\colon\mathcal{B}^{-\infty}\to\mathcal{B}^{-\infty}
. Furthermore, the operation
(-)^{-\infty}
is a functor, sending a morphism
\upsilon\colon(Z,\Lambda)\to(Z^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
to the morphism
\upsilon^{-\infty}\colon(\sigma,\Lambda_{-\infty})\to(\sigma^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}_{-\infty})
defined as
\upsilon^{-\infty}(\ldots,y_{-2},y_{-1})=(\ldots,\upsilon_{Y}(y_{-2}),\upsilon_{Y}(y_{-1}))
.
We call this the
paths functor
.
Note that if
(T,\Lambda)
is a measured dynamical system, then
(T,\Lambda)^{-\infty}
is naturally conjugate to
(T,\Lambda)
.
We will always make this identification.

Let us see how the paths functor interacts with the minimal restriction functors.

Proposition 4.3
.

(-)_{\text{min}}\circ(-)^{-\infty}=(-)^{-\infty}\circ(-)_{\text{min}}
as functors defined on the category of measured topological graphs with proper source maps. The
(-)_{\text{min}}
functor on the left hand side is the minimal dynamical restriction, while the
(-)_{\text{min}}
functor on the right hand side is the minimal restriction as a topological graph.

Proof.

Let
(Z,\Lambda)
be a topological graph with
s\colon Y\to X
proper. Then
(\mathcal{B}_{\text{min}})^{-\infty}
and
(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}}
are both closed
∗
-sub-algebras of
\mathcal{B}^{-\infty}\subseteq\ell^{\infty}_{c}(Z^{-\infty})
with
\sigma
-operator the restriction of
\Lambda_{-\infty}
. Hence,
(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}}\subseteq(\mathcal{B}_{\text{min}})^{-\infty}
. We need to show the reverse inclusion holds. Functions
\underline{g}=g_{-k}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{-1}
, for
g_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
,
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
generate
(\mathcal{B}_{\text{min}})^{-\infty}
and
\underline{g}=h(g_{-k}\circ\sigma^{k-1}\cdot g_{-k+1}\circ\sigma^{k-2}\cdots g_{-1})
for some
h\in C_{c}(Z^{-\infty})
, so by
\sigma^{*}((\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}})\subseteq M((\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}})
it suffices to show
\mathcal{F}_{n}\subseteq(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We proceed by induction on
n
, noting that
n=0
is trivial. Suppose
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\subseteq(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}}
for some
n-1\geq 0
.
Then, for every
g,h\in\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
, we have
g(\Lambda(h)\circ s)=g\Lambda_{-\infty}(h)
in
(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}}
and
g(\Lambda(h)\circ r)=g(\Lambda_{-\infty}(h)\circ\sigma)
in
(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}}
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{F}_{n}\subseteq(\mathcal{B}^{-\infty})_{\text{min}}
.
∎

4.2.
Dynamical covers

By Theorem
3.7
, we can understand the minimal dynamical restriction functor in terms of continuous data.

Definition 4.4
.

We denote by
(T_{\min},\bm{\lambda}_{\min})
the associated cover of
(T,\Lambda_{\min})
, which is a continuously measured dynamical system. We call this the
minimal dynamical cover
of
(T,\Lambda)
and denote its underlying topological space by
X_{\min}
and its structure maps by
\pi\colon X_{\min}\to X
and
\iota\colon X\to X_{\min}
.

The minimal dynamical cover has the following universal property (the proof is analogous to the one of Theorem
3.9
).

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose that
(\dot{T},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
is another continuously measured dynamical system that covers
(T,\Lambda)
in the sense that there is a continuous proper surjection
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{X}\to X
and a morphism
\dot{\iota}\colon(T,\Lambda)\to(T,\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
with dense image such that
\dot{\pi}\circ\dot{T}=T\circ\dot{\pi}
and
\dot{\pi}\circ\dot{\iota}={\rm id}_{Z}.
Then there is a unique continuous proper morphism
\chi\colon(\dot{T},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})\to(T_{\min},\bm{\lambda}_{\min})
such that
\pi\circ\chi=\dot{\pi}
and
\chi\circ\dot{\iota}=\iota
.

We have the following corollary of Proposition
4.3
.

Corollary 4.6
.

If
(Z,\Lambda)
is a measured topological graph with
s\colon Y\to X
proper, then the minimal dynamical cover of
(\sigma,\Lambda_{-\infty})
is isomorphic to
(\sigma,(\hat{\Lambda})_{-\infty})
as covers, with structure maps given by
\pi^{-\infty}\colon(\hat{Z})^{-\infty}\to Z^{-\infty}
and
\iota^{-\infty}\colon Z^{-\infty}\to\hat{Z}^{-\infty}
.
In particular, the minimal dynamical cover of a measured dynamical system
(T,\Lambda)
is isomorphic as covers to the shift on the infinite path space of its minimal graph cover.

4.3.
Essential dynamical covers

We now briefly introduce minimal essential dynamical covers in analogy to §
3.6
.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a topological dynamical system. We say a subset
\mathscr{X}\subseteq X
is
fully invariant
if
T^{-1}(\mathscr{X})=\mathscr{X}
.

An
essential dynamical cover
of
T\colon X\to X
is a continuously measured dynamical system
(\mathcal{T},\bm{\lambda})
together with a continuous proper factor map
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
satisfying the property that there is a fully invariant set
\mathscr{X}\subseteq X
such that
\mathscr{X}
and the (automatically fully invariant) set
\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{X})
are co-meagre and
\pi\colon\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{X})\to\mathscr{X}
is a bijection.

Note that every essential dynamical cover is an essential cover in the sense of Section
3.6
.

Let us now outline the construction of the minimal essential dynamical cover of
T\colon X\to X
, which is analogous to the construction of minimal essential covers in Section
3.6
.

Let
(T,\Lambda)
be a measured topological dynamical system. Recall the critical and post-critical sets
C_{\Lambda}
and
P_{\Lambda}
from Section
3.7
. In the setting of dynamical systems, we have
P_{\Lambda}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}T^{n}(C_{\Lambda})
.

Definition 4.8
.

We define
G_{\Lambda}=\bigcup_{n\leq 0}T^{n}(P_{\Lambda})
and call it the
saturated critical set
of
(T,\Lambda)
.

We have the following analogue of Proposition
3.27
(the proof is similar).

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(T,\Lambda)
be a measured topological dynamical system. Then
\iota\colon X\to X_{\min}
is continuous at
x\in X\setminus G_{\Lambda}
.

Definition 4.10
.

We call
(T,\Lambda)

sparsely critically saturated
if
G_{\Lambda}
is a meagre set.
In that case, we let
X_{{\rm ess}}\coloneq\overline{\iota(X\setminus G_{\Lambda})}
and define
T_{{\rm ess}}
as the restriction of
T_{\min}
to
X_{{\rm ess}}
.
Now,
T_{{\rm ess}}\colon X_{{\rm ess}}\to X_{{\rm ess}}
together with the restriction
\pi_{{\rm ess}}\colon X_{{\rm ess}}\to X
of
\pi
and the restriction
\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}}\colon C_{c}(X_{{\rm ess}})\to C_{c}(X_{{\rm ess}})
of
\bm{\lambda}_{\min}
form an essential cover, which we call the
minimal essential dynamical cover
.

Note that if
T
is weakly open, then
(T,\Lambda)
is sparsely critically saturated if and only if it is post-critically sparse.

Just as in Section
3.7
, there is an alternative construction of the minimal essential dynamical cover as the Gelfand spectrum of the topological
∗
-algebra
(\mathcal{B}_{\text{min}})_{{\rm ess}}
.
Using this alternate description we can describe the minimal essential dynamical cover in terms of the minimal essential graph cover.

Corollary 4.11
.

The minimal essential dynamical cover of a sparsely critically saturated measured dynamical system
(T,\Lambda)
is isomorphic (as covers) to the shift on the infinite path space of the minimal essential graph cover.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the domain of the minimal graph cover of
(T,\Lambda)
. By Corollary
4.6
,
\mathcal{B}_{\text{min}}
is generated by
C_{c}(X)(T^{n})^{*}\mathcal{D}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We have (by invariance)
\big((T^{n})^{*}\mathcal{D}\big)|_{X\setminus G_{\Lambda}}=(T^{n})^{*}(\mathcal{D}|_{X\setminus G_{\Lambda}})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
(\mathcal{B}_{\text{min}})_{{\rm ess}}=(\mathcal{B}_{\text{min}})|_{X\setminus G_{\Lambda}}
is generated by
(T^{n})^{*}(\mathcal{D}|_{X\setminus G_{\Lambda}})
for
n\geq 0
. Applying the Gelfand correspondence finishes the proof.
∎

We state the analogous universal property and lifting properties of morphisms for sparsely critically saturated dynamical systems. They are proved in a similar way to the results of Section
3.7
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Suppose
(\dot{T},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})
along with
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{X}\to X
is an essential dynamical cover of
T
such that
\dot{\pi}_{*}\dot{\lambda}_{\dot{x}}=\lambda_{\dot{\pi}(\dot{x})}
for all
\dot{x}
in a dense subset of
\dot{X}
. Then there is a unique continuous proper morphism
\chi\colon(\dot{T},\dot{\bm{\lambda}})\to(T_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})
such that
\pi\circ\chi=\dot{\pi}
.

Remark 4.13
.

As in Remark
3.33
, the measure condition in Theorem
4.12
is redundant if the essential cover and the original dynamical system are weighted by
w=1
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
(T,\Lambda)
and
(T^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
be sparsely critically saturated topological dynamical systems. Suppose that
\chi\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is a continuous proper and equivariant map such that
\chi_{*}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{\prime}_{\chi(x)}
for all
x
in a dense subset and
(X\setminus G_{\Lambda})\cap\chi^{-1}(X^{\prime}\setminus G_{\Lambda^{\prime}})
is dense. Then there is a unique morphism
\chi_{{\rm ess}}\colon(T_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})\to(T^{\prime}_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}^{\prime}_{{\rm ess}})
such that
\pi_{{\rm ess}}^{\prime}\circ\chi_{{\rm ess}}=\chi\circ\pi_{{\rm ess}}
.

Corollary 4.15
.

The mapping sending
(T,\Lambda)
to
(T_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})
and
\chi\colon X\to X^{\prime}
to
\chi_{{\rm ess}}\colon(T_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}_{{\rm ess}})\to(T^{\prime}_{{\rm ess}},\bm{\lambda}^{\prime}_{{\rm ess}})
is a functor from the category of sparsely critically saturated topological dynamical systems with continuous, proper, weakly open and equivariant maps
\chi\colon X\to X^{\prime}
satisfying
\chi_{*}\lambda_{x}=\lambda^{\prime}_{\chi(x)}
for all
x
in a dense subset of
X
to the category of continuously measured topological dynamical systems with morphisms given by continuous, proper, weakly open morphisms.

Remark 4.16
.

Let
(T,\Lambda)
be a sparsely critically saturated dynamical system. Assume that
X
is a compact metric space and
T
is topologically transitive (i.e., there is a dense forward orbit under
T
). Then any essential cover
(\tilde{T},\tilde{\bm{\lambda}})
of
T
is topologically transitive. Indeed, by
[
45
, Theorem 5.9 (iv)]
, the set of points in
X
with dense forward orbit under
T
forms a dense
G_{\delta}
subset. As the surjection
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
restricts to a homeomorphism on a fully invariant co-meagre subset of the domain and co-domain, it follows that there exists a point
x
in this restricted co-domain whose forward orbit under
T
is dense in
X
. Hence the forward orbit under
\tilde{T}
of the unique element in
\pi^{-1}(x)
is dense in
\tilde{X}
, as desired.

4.4.
Essential dynamical covers of local injections

Let us apply our results in the locally injective case, where several reductions on the hypotheses can be made.
We will assume throughout this section that our dynamical systems are weighted by
w=1
.
Recall from Example
3.12
that in this case, the minimal graph cover is a local homeomorphism weighted also by
w=1
, and therefore the same is true for the minimal dynamical cover.

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous and locally injective map defined on a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space
X
. If
C\subseteq X
is meagre, then so is
T(C)
.

Proof.

By second countability and local compactness, it suffices to prove the lemma for
C\subseteq X
compact with empty interior, contained in some open neighbourhood
U
such that
T|_{U}
is injective. Suppose
T(C)
does not have empty interior and let
T(x)=y\in\text{int}(T(C))
for some
x\in U
. Let
(x_{i})
be a net in
X\setminus C
converging to
x
. Since
y\in\text{int}(T(C))
, there is
i^{\prime}
such that
T(x_{i})=T(c_{i})
for some
c_{i}\in C
and
x_{i}\in U
, for all
i\geq i^{\prime}
. By injectivity of
T|_{U}
it follows that
x_{i}=c_{i}
for all
i\geq i^{\prime}
, a contradiction to
x_{i}\in X\setminus C
.
∎

We will say
T\colon X\to X
is
open at
x\in X
if for every neighbourhood
U
of
x
, we have
T(x)\in{\rm int}(T(U))
. For a locally injective map
T\colon X\to X
, we will call
C=\left\{x\in X\colon T\text{ is not open at }x\right\}
the
critical point set
.

Corollary 4.18
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous and locally injective map on a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space
X
. Then
T
is sparsely critically saturated if and only if the critical point set
C
is meagre.

Proof.

Let us first assume
C
is meagre.
It is easy to see that
T(C)=C_{\Lambda}
, so if
C
is meagre, then
P_{\Lambda}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}T^{n}(C)
is meagre by Lemma
4.17
and the Baire category theorem.
Since
C
is meagre, we have
U\setminus C\neq\emptyset
for every open set
U
. Therefore, we have
\emptyset\neq T(U\setminus C)\subseteq\text{int}(T(U))
, so that
T
is weakly open. Hence,
G_{\Lambda}=\bigcup_{n\leq 0}T^{n}(P_{\Lambda})
is meagre, proving that
T
is sparsely critically saturated.

For the converse assume
T
is sparsely critically saturated.
Then the minimal essential cover exists and
C_{\Lambda}
is meagre.
Hence, by Corollary
3.26
,
T
is weakly open. So
T(C)=C_{\Lambda}
implies
C\subseteq T^{-1}(C_{\Lambda})
is meagre.
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be second countable locally compact Hausdorff spaces,
T^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
a local homeomorphism,
T\colon X\to X
a continuous and locally injective map and
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
a proper and continuous equivariant map. Then
\pi
is an essential cover if and only if
X_{0}^{\prime}\coloneq\left\{x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}\colon|\pi^{-1}(\pi(x^{\prime}))|=1\right\}
is dense.

Proof.

The “only if” direction is trivial, so we prove the “if” direction.
If
X^{\prime}_{0}
is dense, then
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
implies that
X^{\prime}_{0}
and
X_{0}\coloneq\pi(X^{\prime}_{0})
are co-meagre. Set
C^{\prime}_{0}=X^{\prime}\setminus X^{\prime}_{0}
. By Lemma
4.17
, weak openness of
T^{\prime}
and the Baire category theorem, it follows that
G^{\prime}_{0}=\bigcup_{n,m\geq 0}(T^{\prime})^{-n}((T^{\prime})^{m}(C^{\prime}_{0}))
is meagre. Therefore
X^{\prime}_{00}\coloneq X\setminus G^{\prime}_{0}\subseteq X^{\prime}_{0}
is a co-meagre and fully
T^{\prime}
-invariant subset of
X^{\prime}
. Since
\pi\colon X^{\prime}_{0}\to X_{0}
is a homeomorphism (Proposition
3.20
), it follows that
X_{00}\coloneq\pi(X^{\prime}_{00})
is co-meagre in
X_{0}
and therefore co-meagre in
X
. By
T^{\prime}
-invariance of
X^{\prime}_{00}
we have
T(X_{00})\subseteq X_{00}
. Hence
T\colon X_{00}\to X_{00}
is weakly open, so that
T\colon X\to X
is weakly open. The same procedure as above (now using weak openness of
T
and Lemma
4.17
) produces a fully
T
-invariant co-meagre subspace
X_{000}\subseteq X_{00}
. Then
X^{\prime}_{000}\coloneq\pi^{-1}(X_{000})
is a fully
T^{\prime}
-invariant and co-meagre (by weak openness of
\pi
) subset of
X^{\prime}
. It follows that
\pi
is an essential cover.
∎

Now we can state our theorem for locally injective maps. For a map
f\colon X\to Y
between topological spaces, we will say
f
is
almost one-to-one
if
\left\{x\in X\colon|f^{-1}(f(x))|=1\right\}
is dense in
X
.

Theorem 4.20
.

Suppose
T\colon X\to X
is locally injective and
X
is a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space. If the critical point set of
T
is meagre, then the minimal essential cover
T_{{\rm ess}}\colon X_{{\rm ess}}\to X_{{\rm ess}}
exists and is a local homeomorphism defined on a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space. The factor map
\pi_{{\rm ess}}\colon(T_{{\rm ess}},X_{{\rm ess}})\to(T,X)
is continuous, almost one-to-one and proper.

If
(T^{\prime},X^{\prime})
is another local homeomorphism defined on a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space with continuous, almost one-to-one and proper factor map
\pi^{\prime}\colon(T^{\prime},X^{\prime})\to(T,X)
, then there is a unique continuous factor map
\chi\colon(T^{\prime},X^{\prime})\to(T_{{\rm ess}},X_{{\rm ess}})
such that
\pi^{\prime}=\pi_{{\rm ess}}\circ\chi
. Moreover,
\chi
is proper, almost one-to-one and the restriction
(T^{\prime})^{-1}(x^{\prime})\to T_{{\rm ess}}^{-1}(\chi(x^{\prime}))
of
\chi
is a bijection, for all
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from our description of sparsely critically saturated dynamical systems (Corollary
4.18
), our description of essential covers (Corollary
4.19
) and the universal property of the minimal essential cover Theorem
4.12
. The bijection property is equivalent to
\chi
being a weighted morphism (relative to weights
w=1
). Using the fact that a locally compact Hausdorff space is second countable if and only if
C_{c}(X)
is separable (contains a countable dense set), it is easy to see that if
X
is second countable, then so is
X_{{\rm ess}}
.
∎

By the discussion in the introduction, we also obtain a lifting result that can be seen as a non-invertible analogue of a lifting result of Putnam for Smale spaces
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 4.21
.

Suppose
(T,X)
and
(T^{\prime},X^{\prime})
are locally injective dynamical systems defined on second countable locally compact Hausdorff spaces with meagre critical point sets, and suppose
\chi\colon(T,X)\to(T^{\prime},X^{\prime})
is a continuous, almost one-to-one and proper factor map. Then there is a unique continuous factor map
\chi_{{\rm ess}}\colon(T_{{\rm ess}},X_{{\rm ess}})\to(T^{\prime}_{{\rm ess}},X^{\prime}_{{\rm ess}})
such that
\pi_{{\rm ess}}\circ\chi_{{\rm ess}}=\chi\circ\pi^{\prime}_{{\rm ess}}
.
Moreover,
\chi_{{\rm ess}}
is almost one-to-one, proper and
\chi_{{\rm ess}}
restricts to bijections
T_{{\rm ess}}^{-1}(\tilde{x})\to(T^{\prime}_{{\rm ess}})^{-1}(\chi_{{\rm ess}}(\tilde{x}))
, for all
\tilde{x}\in X_{{\rm ess}}
.

Proof.

Using the weak open property of
T
,
T^{\prime}
and Lemma
4.17
, we can find (as in Corollary
4.19
) a co-meagre and fully
T^{\prime}
-invariant subset
X^{\prime}_{0}\subseteq X^{\prime}
such that
X_{0}\coloneq\chi^{-1}(X^{\prime}_{0})
is co-meagre (as well as fully
T
-invariant) and
\chi
restricts to a bijection
X_{0}\to X^{\prime}_{0}
. Therefore
\chi
restricts to bijections
T^{-1}(x_{0})\to(T^{\prime})^{-1}(\chi(x_{0}))
, for all
x_{0}\in X_{0}
, and
\chi
is weakly open. We may therefore apply the lifting result of Theorem
4.14
.
∎

4.5.
Positive expansivity

Expansivity is a fundamental concept in dynamics with applications to symbolic dynamics and thermodynamic formalism (see for example
[
32
]
). Recall that a continuous map
T\colon X\to X
on a compact metrizable space
X
is
positively expansive
if there is a metric
d
and a constant
c>0
such that for every two distinct
x,z\in X
there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d(T^{n}x,T^{n}z)\geq c
. We shall need the following reformulation of positive expansivity. Recall that a compact Hausdorff space is metrizable if and only if it is second countable.

Lemma 4.22
.

A continuous map
T\colon X\to X
on a metrizable compact Hausdorff space
X
is positively expansive if and only if there is a finite open cover
\{U_{i}\}^{m}_{i=1}
of
X
such that
T^{n}(x),T^{n}(x^{\prime})\in U_{i_{n}}
for some
i_{n}\leq m
, for all
n\geq 0
implies

Remark 4.23

∎

Our goal is to show that if

is positively expansive and the minimal graph cover associated with weight

terminates after finitely many steps,

Let

As in the

Lemma 4.25

Alternatively, there is the following link between our finiteness condition and the condition that the minimal dynamical cover terminates after finitely many steps:
for

Remark 4.30

In general, we cannot expect

to be positively expansive even if

A one-sided subshift (not necessarily of finite type) is always positively expansive, and the cover constructed in

5.
Examples from subshifts

In this section, we apply our construction of minimal and minimal essential covers to subshifts and compare with existing constructions such as Krieger and Fischer covers. We refer the reader to
[
26
]
for background on symbolic dynamics.
Let
\mathfrak{a}=\{1,\ldots,m\}
be a finite set (viewed as a discrete space) of symbols.
The space
\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{N}}
is a Cantor space in the product topology and the shift
\sigma(x_{1},x_{2},...)=(x_{2},x_{3},...)
, defined for all
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...)\in\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{N}}
, is a local homeomorphism.
The pair
(\sigma,\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{N}})
is the
full one-sided
m
-shift
.
A subspace
X
of
\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{N}}
is
shift-invariant
if
\sigma(X)\subseteq X
(we do not assume equality),
and if
X
is closed and shift-invariant, then the restricted shift
\sigma_{X}\coloneqq\sigma|_{X}
on
X
is locally injective but not open in general.
The pair
(\sigma_{X},X)
is called a
one-sided subshift
. We denote by
L(X)
the set of finite words in
\mathfrak{a}
which appear as subwords of elements in
X
. Moreover,
L_{k}(X)
denotes the set of words in
L(X)
of length
k
, and
L_{\leq k}(X)
stands for the set of words in
L(X)
of length at most
k
.

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be a one-sided subshift.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
k
-past
of an element
x\in X
is the set
P_{k}(x)\coloneq\left\{\nu\in L_{k}(X)\colon\nu x\in X\right\}
.
Let
P_{\leq k}(x)\coloneqq\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}P_{j}(x)
and
P_{<\infty}(x)\coloneqq\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}P_{j}(x)
.
For two finite words
\alpha
and
\beta
, we shall consider the closed (and generally not open) set
C(\beta,\alpha)\coloneq\left\{x\in X\colon x\in Z(\alpha),\beta\sigma_{X}^{|\alpha|}(x)\in X\right\}
where
|\alpha|
denotes the length (i.e. the number of symbols) of
\alpha
.

5.1.
The topological Krieger graph

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be a one-sided subshift.
If
\sigma_{X}
is of finite type (i.e. a local homeomorphism), then it is a standard result that
\sigma_{X}
is (conjugate to) the shift on the one-sided infinite path space of a finite directed graph
[
26
, Section 2.2]
.
We now construct a labeled compact topological graph
K
such that
(\sigma_{X},X)
is conjugate to the shift on the one-sided infinite path space of labels on
K
,
and another compact topological graph
\mathcal{K}
which we identify with the minimal cover of
\sigma_{X}
viewed as a topological graph with weight
w=1
.

For a subset
F\subseteq L(X)
, we use the notation
F|_{\leq k}\coloneqq F\cap L_{\leq k}(X)
.
The vertices of
K
are given as

I\coloneqq\left\{F\subseteq L(X)\colon\forall\,k>0\,,\exists\,x^{(k)}\in X\text{ such that }F|_{\leq k}=P_{\leq k}(x^{(k)})\right\}

and we equip this with the following topology:
A net
(F_{i})_{i}\subseteq I
converges to
F\in I
if for all
k>0
, we have
F_{i}|_{\leq k}=F|_{\leq k}
eventually.
Note that
I
is equal to the closure of the image of the function
i\colon X\to I
given by
x\mapsto P_{<\infty}(x)
.
The set of edges is given as

J\coloneq\left\{(F,a)\in P\times\mathfrak{a}\colon a\in F\right\}

in the subspace topology of the product topology, and
J
is equal to the closure of the image of the function
i\colon X\to J
defined as
x\mapsto(P_{<\infty}(\sigma_{X}(x)),x_{1})
.
The range and source maps are
r(F,a)=F
and
s(F,a)=\left\{\nu\in L(X)\colon\nu a\in F\right\}
.
It is easily verified that this is well-defined, and that both range and source are continuous.
Moreover, the range map
r
is a local homeomorphism, so
K
is continuously measured if weighted by
w=1
.
We shall refer to
K=(r,s\colon J\to I)
as the
topological Krieger graph
of
\sigma_{X}
.
Note also that the map
\mathcal{L}\colon J\to\mathfrak{a}
given by
\mathcal{L}(F,a)=a
defines a continuous labeling map.
The subshift
(\sigma_{X},X)
is conjugate to the shift on the infinite paths of labels on
K^{-\infty}
, meaning that
\mathcal{L}^{-\infty}\colon(\sigma,K^{-\infty})\to(\sigma_{X},X)
is a factor map.
In this sense, the topological graph
K
together with its labeling
\mathcal{L}
represents the subshift
(\sigma_{X},X)
.

Remark 5.1
.

The pair of mappings
i\colon X\to J
,
i\colon X\to I
define a graph morphism
i\colon(\sigma,X)\to K
.
We refer to
i
as the
Krieger graph morphism
, and the image is usually known as the Krieger graph.
As explained above, this is a countable and dense sub-graph of our topological Krieger graph
K
.
If
\sigma_{X}
is sofic, then
K
is a finite labeled graph and it coincides with the Krieger graph, see
[
26
, Section 3.2]
or
[
24
]
where it is called the past set cover.
We will see soon that
\mathcal{L}^{-\infty}\colon(\sigma,K^{-\infty})\to(\sigma_{X},X)
together with the section
i^{-\infty}\colon(\sigma_{X},X)\to(\sigma,K^{-\infty})

is isomorphic (as a cover) to the minimal dynamical cover of
\sigma_{X}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Matsumoto
[
27
]
(cf.
[
8
]
) studied the space
I
(called
\Omega
in
[
27
]
) in relation to
C^{*}
-algebras attached to general one-sided subshifts.
Based on this, Carlsen and Eilers
[

show that

can be used to compute the

-theory for certain subshift

-algebras.
It would be interesting to see if the topological Krieger graph can be used to compute

-theory for

-algebras of our covers.

5.2.
The augmented topological Krieger graph

Let us construct an augmented graph
\mathcal{K}
that we prove is a model for the minimal cover for
\sigma_{X}
viewed as a topological graph. For each integer
k>1
, we first construct a graph
\mathcal{K}_{k}
.
The vertices and edges are given as

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneq\left\{(P_{\leq k-1}(\alpha x),\alpha)\colon\alpha\in L_{k-1}(X),\alpha x\in X\right\}

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{k}

\displaystyle\coloneq\left\{(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),a\alpha)\colon\alpha\in L_{k-1}(X),a\in L_{1}(X),a\alpha x\in X\right\}

and the range map is
r(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),a\alpha)=(P_{\leq k-1}(\alpha x),\alpha)
while the source map is defined as

s(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),a\alpha)=(P_{\leq k-1}(a\alpha x),(a\alpha)_{[1,k-1]})
.
Now
\mathcal{K}_{k}=(\mathcal{I}_{k},\mathcal{J}_{k},r,s)
is a finite discrete graph.
There are surjective graph morphisms
\upsilon_{k+1}\colon\mathcal{K}_{k+1}\to\mathcal{K}_{k}
given by

\upsilon_{\mathcal{I}_{k+1}}(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),\alpha)=(P_{\leq k-1}(\alpha x),\alpha_{[1,k-1]})
and
\upsilon_{\mathcal{J}_{k+1}}(P_{\leq k+1}(\alpha x),a\alpha)=(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),(a\alpha)_{[1,k]})
.

We define the graph
\mathcal{K}
to be the projective limit of
\mathcal{K}_{k}
along the morphisms
(\upsilon_{k})_{k}
.
The vertices and edges may be identified with

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\left\{(F,x)\in I\times X\colon\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}\ \exists\,x^{(k)}\in Z(x_{[1,k]})\text{ such that }F|_{\leq k}=P_{\leq k}(x^{(k)})\right\}

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle=\left\{(G,by)\in I\times X\colon(G,y)\in\mathcal{I},b\in G\right\}

in the subspace topology, and
r(G,by)=(G,y)
,
s(G,by)=(\left\{\nu\in L(X)\colon\nu b\in G\right\},by)
are the range and source maps, respectively.
Then
\mathcal{K}
is a compact topological graph whose range map is a local homeomorphism, so we weight
\mathcal{K}
by
w=1
.
We call
\mathcal{K}
the
augmented topological Krieger graph
.

There is a graph morphism
j\colon X\to\mathcal{K}
given by
j_{\mathcal{I}}(x)=(P_{<\infty}(x),x)
and
j_{\mathcal{J}}(x)=(P_{<\infty}(\sigma_{X}(x)),x)
, for all
x\in X
,
and this is injective with dense image.
It is a section of the continuous graph morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{K}\to X
given by
\pi_{\mathcal{I}}(F,x)=x
and
\pi_{\mathcal{J}}(G,x)=x
.
Note that
j_{\mathcal{J}}\colon\sigma_{X}^{-1}(x)\to r^{-1}(j_{\mathcal{I}}(x))
is a bijection, for all
x\in X
, so
j
is a weighted graph morphism.
Therefore,
\mathcal{K}
together with
\pi
and
j
is a graph cover of
(\sigma_{X},X)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be a one-sided subshift.
The minimal graph cover of
\sigma_{X}
is isomorphic (as covers) to the graph
\mathcal{K}
together with
\pi
and
j
.

Proof.

Let
r,s\colon\hat{Y}\to\hat{X}
denote the minimal graph cover of
\sigma_{X}
we constructed in Section
3.4
,
and let
\mathcal{D}_{\text{min}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\text{min}}
denote the domain and range for the minimal restriction of
(\sigma_{X},X)
, weighted by
w=1
.
By the universal property (see Theorem
3.9
) it suffices to show that

(j_{\mathcal{I}})^{*}(C(\mathcal{I}))\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\text{min}}\simeq C(\hat{X})
and
(j_{\mathcal{J}})^{*}(C(\mathcal{J}))\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\text{min}}\simeq C(\hat{Y})
.
We treat the cases of vertices and edges separately.
Note that
C(\mathcal{I})
and
C(\mathcal{J})
are the limits of
C(\mathcal{I}_{k})
and
C(\mathcal{J}_{k})
, and each
\mathcal{K}_{k}
is a finite discrete graph.

For the case of vertices, let
(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),\alpha)\in\mathcal{I}_{k}
and
z\in X
, and observe that

\chi_{(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),\alpha)}(j_{\mathcal{I}}(z))=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if }P_{\leq k}(\alpha x)=P_{\leq k}(z)\textrm{ and }\alpha=z_{[1,k-1]}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This equals the indicator over the set
Z(\alpha)\cap\bigcap_{\beta\in P_{\leq k}(\alpha x)}C(\beta,\varnothing)

which is in
\mathcal{R}_{\text{min}}
since
\Lambda^{|\beta|}(\chi_{Z(\beta)})=\chi_{C(\beta,\varnothing)}
.

For edges, we take
(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),a\alpha)\in\mathcal{J}_{k}
and
z\in X
and observe that

\chi_{(P_{\leq k}(\alpha x),a\alpha)}(j_{\mathcal{J}}(z))=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if }P_{\leq k}(\alpha x)=P_{\leq k}(\sigma_{X}(z))\textrm{ and }a\alpha=z_{[1,k]}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This is equal to the indicator of
Z(a\alpha)\cap\sigma_{X}^{-1}(\bigcap_{\beta\in P_{\leq k}(\alpha x)}C(\beta,\varnothing))
which is in
\mathcal{D}_{\text{min}}
.
∎

There is a natural graph morphism
\mu\colon\mathcal{K}\to K
given by
\mu_{\mathcal{I}}(F,x)=F
for all
(F,x)\in\mathcal{I}
, and
\mu_{\mathcal{J}}(G,by)=(G,b)
for all
(G,by)\in\mathcal{J}
.
Applying the paths functor yields an equivariant continuous map
\mu^{-\infty}\colon\mathcal{K}^{-\infty}\to K^{-\infty}
given by applying
\mu
to each edge in an infinite path.

Lemma 5.4
.

The map
\mu^{-\infty}\colon\mathcal{K}^{-\infty}\to K^{-\infty}
is a topological conjugacy and an isomorphism of dynamical covers of
(\sigma_{X},X)
.

Proof.

Note that
\mu\circ j=i
and so functorality implies
\mu^{-\infty}\circ j^{-\infty}=i^{-\infty}
.
It suffices to show the image of
i^{-\infty}
is dense in
K^{-\infty}
and construct a continuous function
S\colon K^{-\infty}\to\prod_{n<0}(P\times X)
such that
S\circ i^{-\infty}=j^{-\infty}
.
We first construct
S
.
Take
(\ldots,(F_{2},a_{2}),(F_{1},a_{1}))\in K^{-\infty}
and observe that
a\coloneqq a_{1}a_{2}\ldots
is in
X
.
Define
S(\ldots,(F_{2},a_{2}),(F_{1},a_{1}))=(\ldots,(F_{2},\sigma_{X}(a)),(F_{1},a))\in\prod_{n<0}(I\times X)
.
Then,
S\circ i^{-\infty}=j^{-\infty}
and

is continuous.

We prove that the image of
i^{-\infty}
is dense.
Let
\underline{q}=(...(F_{2},a_{2}),(F_{1},a_{1}))\in K^{-\infty}
.
For every
n
in
\mathbb{N}
, we have
\left\{b\in L(X)\colon ba_{n+1}\in F_{n+1}\right\}=F_{n}
.
Therefore,
\left\{b\in L(X)\colon ba_{k+1}a_{k}...a_{n+1}\in F_{n+1}\right\}=F_{k}
for all
k\leq n
.
In particular, for every
n
in
\mathbb{N}
, there is
x_{n}
in
X
such that
F_{n+1}|_{\leq 2n+1}=P_{\leq 2n+1}(x_{n})

and hence
y_{n}=a_{1}...a_{n+1}x_{n}
is an element in
X
such that

P_{\leq n}(\sigma^{k}(y_{n}))=\left\{b\in L_{\leq n}(X)\colon ba_{k+1}...a_{n+1}x_{n}\in X\right\}=\left\{b\in L_{\leq n}(X)\colon ba_{k+1}...a_{n+1}\in F_{n+1}\right\}=(F_{k})_{\leq n},
for all
k\leq n
.
Therefore,
\{i^{-\infty}(y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\underline{q}
. This proves density of

.
∎

We can now identify the minimal dynamical cover of a subshift in terms of the topological Krieger graph.

5.3.
The essential dynamical cover of a subshift

In this section, we identify the minimal essential dynamical cover of a subshift
(\sigma_{X},X)
.
We say a subshift
\sigma_{X}
is
regular
if
\sigma_{X}(X)
is regular in
X
, in the sense that the interior of
\sigma_{X}(X)
is dense in
\sigma_{X}(X)
.
Most subshifts of interest are regular (in particular, when
\sigma_{X}
is surjective), but not all subshifts are regular.
Below we see that this is exactly the condition we need to define the essential cover of
\sigma_{X}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be a subshift. Then the post-critical set (when
\sigma_{X}
is weighted by
w=1
) is meagre. Moreover,
\sigma_{X}
is weakly open if and only if it is regular.

Proof.

For a function
f\colon X\to Y
, we let
D(f)\coloneq\left\{x\in X\colon f\text{ is not continuous at }x\right\}
.
Recall the functions
P_{k}\colon X\to L_{k}(X)
from Section
5.1
.
We regard
L_{k}(X)
as a discrete topological space.
Then
x
is a critical point of
\sigma_{X}
if and only if
x\in D(P_{1})
, and
x\in D(P_{k})
implies
\sigma_{X}(x)\in D(P_{k+1})
.
Therefore, to show the post-critical set is meagre, it suffices to show
D(P_{k})
is meagre, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
(by the Baire category theorem).

Choose a sequence
(U_{n})_{n}
of nested open and closed subset of
\mathfrak{a}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
X\subseteq U_{n}\subseteq U_{n+1}
for all
n
and
X=\bigcap_{n}U_{n}
.
As the full shift
\sigma
is a local homeomorphism,
the mappings
P_{k}^{n}\colon X\to\mathfrak{a}^{k}\subseteq L(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}})
given by
P_{k}^{n}(x)=(\sigma^{-1}(x)\cap U_{n})_{[1,k]}
for all
x\in X
are continuous.
Since
P_{k}
is the pointwise limit of the continuous functions
P_{k}^{n}
, it follows from the Baire category theorem that
D(P_{k})
is meagre.

We prove the second statement now. If
\text{int}(\sigma_{X}(X))
is not dense in
\sigma_{X}(X)
, then the open dense set
U=\text{int}(\sigma_{X}(X))\cup X\setminus\sigma_{X}(X)
does not have a dense pre-image
\sigma_{X}^{-1}(U)
. Hence,
\sigma_{X}
is not weakly open.

To prove the converse, consider
X_{0}\coloneq\left\{x\in X\colon\sigma_{X}(x)\notin\text{int}(\sigma_{X}(U)),\text{ for some neighbourhood }U\text{ of }x\right\}
, where the interior is taken as a subset of
\sigma_{X}(X)
.
Since
\sigma_{X}\colon X\to\sigma_{X}(X)
is a quotient map, we may write

X_{0}=\left\{x\in X\colon\Lambda(f)\circ\sigma\text{ is not continuous at }x\text{ for some positive function }f\in C(Z(x_{1}))\right\}.

Therefore,
X_{0}\subseteq D(j_{\mathcal{J}})
.
Moreover,
D(j_{\mathcal{J}})\subseteq\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D(P_{k}\circ\sigma)
. Each set
D(P_{k}\circ\sigma)
is meagre
because
P_{k}\circ\sigma
is the pointwise limit of the continuous maps
P_{k}^{n}\circ\sigma
.
Therefore,
X_{0}
is meagre, and it follows that
\sigma_{X}\colon X\to\sigma_{X}(X)
is weakly open.
Note that an inclusion
X\subseteq Y
of a subspace
X
is weakly open if and only if its interior is dense in
X
, so by the hypothesis, it follows that
\sigma_{X}\colon X\to X
is weakly open.
∎

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be a regular subshift and recall the Krieger graph morphism
i\colon X\to K
.
We define the
topological Fischer graph
of
(\sigma_{X},X)
to be
K_{\rm ess}\coloneqq\overline{i(R_{\Lambda})}
, where
R_{\Lambda}
is the regular sub-graph of
\sigma_{X}
weighted by
1
.
In particular, if
P_{\Lambda}
is the postcritical set of
\sigma_{X}
,
then
i_{I}\colon X\setminus P_{\Lambda}\to I_{\rm ess}
and
i_{J}\colon X\setminus\sigma_{X}^{-1}(P_{\Lambda})\to J_{\rm ess}
are continuous inclusions with dense image.

Remark 5.8
.

As in the case of the Krieger graph, we could define the augmented Fischer graph and show that it coincides with the essential graph cover of
\sigma_{X}
.

The fact that the left-infinite path space of our topological Fischer graph is (isomorphic to) the essential dynamical cover of a regular subshift
now follows from our construction and the identification of
K^{-\infty}
with the minimal dynamical cover.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be a regular subshift.
Then the minimal essential dynamical cover is isomorphic (as covers) to the shift on the left-infinite path space
K_{\rm ess}^{-\infty}
.

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be a subshift.
It is
irreducible
if whenever
\lambda,\nu\in L(X)
, there is
\mu\in L(X)
such that
\lambda\mu\nu\in L(X)
.
Recall that a word
\mu
in
L(X)
is
synchronizing
if whenever
\nu
and
\lambda
are in
L(X)
and
\nu\mu
and
\mu\lambda
are in
L(X)
, then
\nu\mu\lambda
is in
L(X)
.
A point
x\in X
is synchronizing if it contains a synchronizing word, and we let
X_{\rm sync}
be the subset of
X
of points that contain synchronizing words.
If
\sigma_{X}
is irreducible and
X_{\rm sync}
is nonempty, then it is open and dense.

Recall that a subshift is
sofic
if it is a homomorphic image of a shift of finite type.
Sofic shifts always contain synchronizing words, and the (topological) Krieger graph
K
is finite and discrete.
In particular,
I_{{\rm ess}}=i_{I}(X\setminus P_{\Lambda})
and
J_{{\rm ess}}=i_{J}(X\setminus\sigma_{X}^{-1}(P_{\Lambda}))
, where
P_{\Lambda}
is the post-critical set of
\sigma_{X}
(weighted by
w=1
).
The
Fischer graph

F
of an irreducible sofic subshift
(\sigma_{X},X)
is the unique minimal right-resolving graph presentation of
\sigma_{X}

[
26
, Section 3.4]
,
and it may be concretely defined as the sub-graph of
K
whose vertices are represented by synchronizing points,
i.e.,
F^{0}=i_{I}(X_{\rm sync})\subseteq I
and
F^{1}=r^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{\rm sync}))\cap s^{-1}(i_{J}(X_{\rm sync}))\subseteq J
.

Let us relate synchronizing words to continuity points.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\sigma_{X}\colon X\to X
be a subshift and suppose
x
is synchronizing. Then there is a neighbourhood
U
of
x
such that, for every
k
in
\mathbb{N}
,
P_{k}|_{U}
is continuous.
Therefore,
X_{{\rm sync}}\subseteq X\setminus P_{\Lambda}
,
i_{I}
is continuous at every
x\in X_{\rm sync}
and
i_{J}
is continuous at every
y\in\sigma^{-1}_{X}(X_{\rm sync})
.

Proof.

We may write
x=\alpha mx^{\prime}
, where
m
is a synchronizing word.
Then, for every
y
in
Z(\alpha m)
, we have
P_{k}(y)=P_{k}(\alpha m)\coloneq\left\{f\in L_{k}(X)\colon f\alpha m\in L(X)\right\}
.
The other conclusions follow from
P_{\Lambda}\subseteq\bigcup_{k}D(P_{k})
(see the proof of Lemma
5.7
) and
\mu\circ j=i
.
∎

For an irreducible sofic subshift, our topological Fischer graph coincides with the classical Fischer graph.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be an irreducible sofic subshift.
Then
K_{\rm ess}
is isomorphic to the Fischer graph of
\sigma_{X}
.

Proof.

The edges of the Fischer graph are
r^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}}))\cap s^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}}))
.
Note that
s\circ i_{J}=i_{I}
implies
i_{J}(X_{{\rm sync}})\subseteq s^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}}))
and
j
being a (weighted) morphism implies
i_{J}(\sigma_{X}^{-1}(X_{{\rm sync}}))=r^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}}))
.
Therefore,

(9)

i_{J}(X_{{\rm sync}}\cap\sigma_{X}^{-1}(X_{{\rm sync}}))\subseteq s^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}}))\cap r^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}}))\subseteq i_{J}(\sigma_{X}^{-1}(X_{{\rm sync}})).

The map
\sigma_{X}
is irreducible, and hence surjective, so Lemma
5.7
implies
\sigma_{X}
is weakly open.
As
X_{\rm sync}
is open and dense, Lemma
5.10
implies that
\sigma_{X}^{-1}(X_{{\rm sync}})\cap X_{{\rm sync}}
is an open dense set contained in
X\setminus\sigma_{X}^{-1}(P_{\Lambda})
.
It follows from density of
X\setminus\sigma_{X}^{-1}(P_{\Lambda})
and continuity of
i_{J}
that each containment

i_{J}(X_{{\rm sync}}\cap\sigma_{X}^{-1}(X_{{\rm sync}}))\subseteq i_{Q}(\sigma_{X}^{-1}(X_{{\rm sync}}))\subseteq\overline{i_{J}(X\setminus\sigma_{X}^{-1}(P_{\Lambda}))}=J_{{\rm ess}}

is dense in the following set.
Since
K
is finite, we must have
J_{{\rm ess}}=i_{J}(\sigma_{X}^{-1}(X_{{\rm sync}}))=i_{J}(X_{{\rm sync}}\cap\sigma_{X}^{-1}(X_{{\rm sync}}))
.
Therefore, (
9
) implies
J_{{\rm ess}}\subseteq r^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}}))\cap s^{-1}(i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}}))\subseteq J_{{\rm ess}}
.
A similar argument shows
I_{{\rm ess}}=i_{I}(X_{{\rm sync}})
.
Therefore,
K_{{\rm ess}}
is the Fischer graph.
∎

In Section
4.5
we gave a condition on the minimal graph cover construction that guaranteed expansivity of the minimal dynamical cover. Let us prove that it is necessary in the case of subshifts.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\sigma_{X}\colon X\to X
be a subshift. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

The minimal graph cover terminates after finitely many steps.

(2)

The minimal dynamical cover is expanding.

(3)

\sigma_{X}
is sofic.

Moreover, if
\sigma_{X}
is regular then we may replace “minimal graph cover” with “minimal essential graph cover” in the above statements.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
follows from Theorem
4.27
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: the dynamical cover is an expanding local homeomorphism (hence conjugate to a shift of finite type), so
\sigma_{X}
is sofic.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: note that the spectrum
X_{n}
of
\mathcal{E}_{n}
can be represented as the closure of the image of the map
x\mapsto(P_{\leq n}(x),x)
,
while the spectrum
Y_{n}
of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is the closure of the image of
x\mapsto(P_{\leq n}(\sigma(x)),x,P_{\leq n}(x))
,
with the projections
Y_{n+1}\to Y_{n}
,
X_{n+1}\to X_{n}
given by the projections
F\subseteq L_{\leq k+1}(X)\to F|_{\leq k}\subseteq L_{\leq k}(X)
and the identity
X\to X
on the relevant factors.
As soficity is equivalent to the existence of
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
P_{\leq N}(x)=P_{\leq N}(y)
implies
P_{k}(x)=P_{k}(y)
for any
k\geq N
,
x,y\in X
, it follows from this and the above description of the cover that the maps
X_{k+1}\to X_{k}
and
Y_{k+1}\to Y_{k}
are bijections, for all
k\geq N
.
∎

Example 5.13
.

Let
(\sigma_{X},X)
be the even shift (only an even number of zeros are allowed between any two ones).
This is irreducible, and
0^{\infty}
is the unique critical point and it is not synchronizing.
The (topological) Fischer graph has two vertices, unique edges between them both labeled with zero, and a single loop labeled with one.

Example 5.14
.

If
(\sigma_{X},X)
is a one-sided Sturmian subshift, then
\sigma_{X}
admits a unique branching element
x\in X
for which
|\sigma_{X}^{-1}(x)|=2
.
The infinite path space on the topological Fischer cover is conjugate to the two-sided (i.e. bi-infinite) Sturmian subshift,
and the infinite path space on the topological Krieger cover is conjugate to
K_{\rm ess}^{-\infty}
and a discrete copy of the orbit of the branch point
x
which is dense (see
[
4
]
).

Remark 5.15
.

In
[
20
]
(see also
[
26
, Example 3.3.21]
),
Jonoska exhibits an example of two non-isomorphic labelled graphs that are both minimal and right-resolving and that present the same (reducible) shift of finite type.
This explains why the Fischer graph defined as
the
minimal right-resolving graph presentation is only defined for
irreducible
sofic subshifts.
Our topological Fischer graph is uniquely determined, it is discrete for sofic subshifts, and it is trivially labeled (every edge has a unique label) for shifts of finite type,
so it cannot in general be minimal (in the sense of having the fewest number of vertices) when the subshift is reducible.

5.4.
Natural extensions as minimal essential covers of almost one-to-one dynamical systems

We can generalize Example
5.14
in the following way.
Let
T\colon X\to X
be a proper dynamical system.
Define
X^{\infty}\coloneq\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},...)\in\prod_{i>0}X\colon x_{i}=T(x_{i+1})\text{ }\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
and
\tau(x_{1},x_{2},...)\coloneq(T(x_{1}),x_{1},...)
.
Then
\tau
is a homeomorphism, known as the
natural extension of
T
.
Let us characterize when the essential dynamical cover can be identified with the natural extension in the locally injective case.

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a locally injective, proper and finite degree map defined on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
, weighted by
w=1
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

X_{0}=\left\{x\in X\colon|T^{-n}(T^{m}(x))|=1\text{ }\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is dense in
X
.

(2)

T
is sparsely critically saturated and the minimal essential dynamical cover
T_{{\rm ess}}
is a homeomorphism.

(3)

T
is sparsely critically saturated and
T_{{\rm ess}}
is conjugate to
\tau
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: note that
T
is surjective, as the image of a proper map is closed.
Since
T
is surjective and locally injective, it follows that if
x\in X_{0}
, then there is a neighbourhood
U
of
x
such that
|T^{-1}(\tilde{x})|=1
for all
\tilde{x}\in U
.
Therefore,
\Lambda(f)
is continuous at
x
, for all
f\in C_{c}(X)
, and
X_{0}\cap C_{\Lambda}=\emptyset
, where
C_{\Lambda}
is the critical values of
T
weighted by
w=1
.
Since
X_{0}
is fully invariant, we also have that
X_{0}\cap G_{\Lambda}=\emptyset
, where
G_{\Lambda}
is the saturation of
C_{\Lambda}
.
By a similar argument, we have that
X^{m,n}_{0}=\left\{x\in X\colon|T^{-n}(T^{m}(x))|=1\right\}
is open.
The Baire category theorem now implies that
X_{0}=\bigcap_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}X^{m,n}_{0}
is a dense
G_{\delta}
set contained in
X\setminus G_{\Lambda}
, so
T
is sparsely critically saturated.
In order to prove that
T_{{\rm ess}}
is a homeomorphism, it suffices to show
|T_{{\rm ess}}^{-1}(x)|=1
.
But this follows from the fact that
|T_{{\rm ess}}^{-1}(x)|=1
for all
x\in\iota(X_{0})
, density of
\iota(X_{0})
in
X_{{\rm ess}}
, and continuity of
x\mapsto|T_{{\rm ess}}^{-1}(x)|
.

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
:
consider the factor map
\mu\colon X^{\infty}\to X
given by
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},...)\mapsto\mu(\underline{x})=x_{1}
.
The set
\mathscr{X}=\left\{\underline{x}\in X^{\infty}\colon x_{1}\in X_{0}\right\}
is fully invariant and
G_{\delta}
, and since
X_{0}
is fully invariant, a diagonalization argument shows that it is dense.
Moreover,
\mu
restricted to
\mathscr{X}
is a bijection onto
X_{0}
. It follows that
(\tau,X^{\infty})
and
\mu
form an essential dynamical cover of
T
weighted by
1
. Therefore, by the universal property of essential dynamical covers, there is a continuous equivariant surjection
X^{\infty}\to X_{{\rm ess}}
that is the identity on
X_{0}
(once suitably identified). The map
X_{{\rm ess}}\to X^{\infty}
via
\tilde{x}\mapsto(\pi(\tilde{x}),\pi(T_{{\rm ess}}^{-1}(\tilde{x})),...)
is the inverse, since it is also the identity on
X_{0}
.

(3)\Rightarrow(2)
is trivial, and
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
follows from the fact that
\iota(T^{-n}(T^{m}(x)))=T_{{\rm ess}}^{-n}(T_{{\rm ess}}^{m}(\iota(x)))
for all
x\in X\setminus G_{\Lambda}
.
∎

In particular, we recover the description of Example
5.14
.
This also applies to a large class of minimal subshifts with finitely many branch points (see e.g.
[
16
]
).

Remark 5.17
.

If
X
is a second countable locally compact Hausdorff space, and
T\colon X\to X
is locally injective, then using Lemma
4.17
and the fact that almost one-to-one maps are weakly open, we see that the set
X_{0}
in Theorem
5.16
is dense if and only if

is almost one-to-one.

Remark 5.18
.

If we consider the
C^{*}
-algebra of the Deaconu–Renault groupoid of
T_{\min}
for a dynamical system satisfying the hypothesis and one of the three (equivalent) conditions in Theorem
5.16
,
then, since
X_{{\rm ess}}
is a fully invariant sub-space of

, it will always have a quotient isomorphic to the crossed product of

with the integer action of

. Moreover, the corresponding ideal will be Morita equivalent to a countable direct sum of copies of the compact operators if

6.
Examples from piecewise invertible maps

6.1.
The tent map

Let
0<\mu\leq 2
and consider the tent map
T\colon[0,1]\to[0,1],\,x\mapsto\mu\min(x,1-x)
, i.e.,

T(x)=\begin{cases}\mu x&\text{ if }x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}],\\
\mu(1-x)&\text{ if }x\in[\frac{1}{2},1].\end{cases}

Consider the weight
w=1
. We now set out to describe the minimal cover
T_{\min}\colon X_{\min}\to X_{\min}
and the minimal essential cover
T_{{\rm ess}}\colon X_{{\rm ess}}\to X_{{\rm ess}}
. In the following, we will write
O(x)
for the grand orbit of a point
x\in X=[0,1]
, i.e.,
O(x)\coloneq\bigcup_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\beta=0}^{\infty}T^{-\alpha}(T^{\beta}(x))
.

The case
\mu=2
is special because this is the only case where
T
is already an open map. In that case,
X_{\min}=[0,1]\amalg O(\frac{1}{2})
, where
[0,1]
carries the usual topology,
O(\frac{1}{2})
is equipped with the discrete topology, and
\amalg
stands for topological disjoint union. Moreover, on
[0,1]
,
T_{\min}
is given by
T
, and on
O(\frac{1}{2})
,
T_{\min}
is given by the restriction of
T
. The new weight takes the constant value
1
on
([0,1]\setminus\left\{1\right\})\amalg O(\frac{1}{2})
and the value
2
on
1\in[0,1]
. We obtain the minimal essential cover from the minimal cover by deleting the extra
O(\frac{1}{2})
-part. In other words, the minimal essential cover agrees with the original tent map, the only difference is that the weight changes its value at the point
1\in[0,1]
from its original value
1
to the new value
2
.

Let us now treat the remaining case where
0<\mu<2
. Following the construction described in Section
4.1
, we first describe the spaces
X_{n}={\rm Spec\,}(\mathcal{B}_{n})
. Each of these spaces is constructed from the unit interval
[0,1]
by introducing a set of break points
B_{n}\subseteq[0,1]
and a set of isolated points
D_{n}\subseteq[0,1]
. This means that

X_{n}=\Big(([0,1]\setminus B_{n})\cup\left\{x_{-},x_{+}\colon x\in B_{n}\right\}\Big)\amalg D_{n},

where
D_{n}
is given the discrete topology and
\amalg
stands for topological disjoint union. To explain the topology on
X_{n}\setminus D_{n}
, first let
\pi_{n}\colon X_{n}\to[0,1]
be the canonical projection map (which sends both
x_{-}
and
x_{+}
to
x
and is the identity on
[0,1]\setminus B_{n}
and
D_{n}
). Given a sequence
(x_{i})
in
X_{n}\setminus D_{n}
,
(x_{i})
converges to
x\in[0,1]\setminus B_{n}
if and only if
(\pi_{n}(x_{i}))
converges to
\pi_{n}(x)
;
(x_{i})
converges to
x_{-}
for
x\in B_{n}
if and only if
(\pi_{n}(x_{i}))
converges to
\pi_{n}(x)
and
\pi_{n}(x_{i})<\pi_{n}(x)
eventually; and
(x_{i})
converges to
x_{+}
for
x\in B_{n}
if and only if
(\pi_{n}(x_{i}))
converges to
\pi_{n}(x)
and
\pi_{n}(x_{i})>\pi_{n}(x)
eventually. Now the sets
B_{n}
and
D_{n}
are given recursively as follows:
B_{1}=\left\{\frac{\mu}{2}\right\}=D_{1}
,
B_{n+1}=B_{n}\cup T(B_{n})\cup T^{-1}(B_{n}\setminus\left\{\frac{\mu}{2}\right\})
and
D_{n+1}=D_{n}\cup T(D_{n})\cup T^{-1}(D_{n})
.

The space
X_{\min}
is given by
\varprojlim X_{n}
, which is constructed in a similar way as the
X_{n}
from the unit interval
[0,1]
by introducing a set of break points
B
and a set of isolated points
D
. Here
B=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}
and
D=O(\frac{1}{2})
, i.e.,

\hat{X}=\Big(([0,1]\setminus B)\cup\left\{x_{-},x_{+}\colon x\in B\right\}\Big)\amalg D.

The map
T_{\min}
coincides with (the restriction of)
T
on
D
. For
x\in[0,1]\setminus B
,
T_{\min}(x)=T(x)\in[0,1]\setminus B
unless
x=\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{2}\notin B
, in which case
T_{\min}(\frac{1}{2})=(\frac{\mu}{2})_{-}
. On
\left\{x_{-},x_{+}\colon x\in B\right\}
,
T_{\min}
is given by

T_{\min}(x_{-})=\begin{cases}(T(x))_{-}&\text{ if }x\in B,\,x<\frac{1}{2},\\
(T(x))_{+}&\text{ if }x\in B,\,x>\frac{1}{2};\end{cases}

T_{\min}(x_{+})=\begin{cases}(T(x))_{+}&\text{ if }x\in B,\,x<\frac{1}{2},\\
(T(x))_{-}&\text{ if }x\in B,\,x>\frac{1}{2};\end{cases}

and, if
\frac{1}{2}\in B
,
T_{\min}((\frac{1}{2})_{-})=T_{\min}((\frac{1}{2})_{+})=(\frac{\mu}{2})_{-}
.
Moreover, the new weight
\hat{w}
takes the constant value
1
on all points of
X_{\min}
except at
(\frac{\mu}{2})_{-}
, where
\hat{w}((\frac{\mu}{2})_{-})=2
.

The minimal essential cover is constructed from the minimal cover by deleting
D
, i.e.,
X_{{\rm ess}}=X_{\min}\setminus D
and
T_{{\rm ess}}=T_{\min}|_{X_{{\rm ess}}}
.

With these descriptions of our covers, we can collect some immediate observations.

Corollary 6.1
.

The cover construction terminates after finitely many steps in the sense of Remark
4.29
if and only if
\mu=1
, in which case
X_{\min}=X_{1}
.
In that case,
T_{\min}
is a local homeomorphism.
We have
B=O(\frac{1}{2})
if and only if
\frac{1}{2}
lies in its forward orbit
{\rm O}^{+}(\frac{1}{2})\coloneq\left\{T^{\beta}(\frac{1}{2})\colon\beta\geq 1\right\}
, and this statement is also equivalent to
\frac{1}{2}\in B
.

Remark 6.2
.

The analysis of the example of the tent map motivates the construction of another cover by introducing break points at all points in
O(\frac{1}{2})
. Indeed, let

\tilde{X}\coloneq([0,1]\setminus O(\frac{1}{2}))\cup\{x_{-},x_{+}\colon x\in O(\frac{1}{2})\},

with topology defined similarly as for (the non-discrete parts of)
X_{n}
and
X_{\min}
.
Moreover, let us introduce the map
\tilde{T}\colon\tilde{X}\to\tilde{X}
as follows:
for
x\in[0,1]\setminus O(\frac{1}{2})
,
\tilde{T}(x)\coloneq T(x)\in[0,1]\setminus O(\frac{1}{2})
. On
\left\{x_{-},x_{+}\colon x\in O(\frac{1}{2})\right\}
,
\tilde{T}
is given by

\tilde{T}(x_{-})\coloneq\begin{cases}(T(x))_{-}&\text{ if }x\in O(\frac{1}{2}),\,x<\frac{1}{2},\\
(T(x))_{+}&\text{ if }x\in O(\frac{1}{2}),\,x>\frac{1}{2};\end{cases}

\tilde{T}(x_{+})\coloneq\begin{cases}(T(x))_{+}&\text{ if }x\in O(\frac{1}{2}),\,x<\frac{1}{2},\\
(T(x))_{-}&\text{ if }x\in O(\frac{1}{2}),\,x>\frac{1}{2};\end{cases}

and
\tilde{T}((\frac{1}{2})_{-})\coloneq\tilde{T}((\frac{1}{2})_{+})\coloneq(\frac{\mu}{2})_{-}
.
It is straightforward to see that
\tilde{T}\colon\tilde{X}\to\tilde{X}
is an essential cover of
T\colon X\to X
, and that
\tilde{T}
is always a local homeomorphism.
Moreover, if
1<\mu\leq 2
, then
\tilde{T}
is positively expansive if the forward orbit
{\rm O}^{+}(\frac{1}{2})
is finite or, more generally, if there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that
{\rm O}^{+}(\frac{1}{2})\subseteq\bigcup_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\beta=0}^{\gamma}T^{-\alpha}(T^{\beta}(\frac{1}{2}))
.

6.2.
General piecewise invertible maps

The construction in Remark
6.2
motivates a cover construction for general piecewise invertible maps, which we set out to explain now.

First, it is convenient to introduce the notions of regular open and regular closed partitions (these have been used before to construct covers, see for example
[
25
,
23
]
). Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space. A subset
C\subseteq X
is called regular closed if
C
is closed and satisfies
C=\overline{{\rm int}(C)}
. A subset
U\subseteq X
is called regular open if
U
is open and
U={\rm int}(\overline{U})
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between regular closed and regular open subsets under which a regular closed subset
C
corresponds to the regular open subset
{\rm int}(C)
(and the inverse sends
U
to
\overline{U}
). A finite collection
\left\{C_{i}\right\}
of subsets is called a regular closed partition of
X
if all
C_{i}
are non-empty and regular closed,
X=\bigcup_{i}C_{i}
and for all
C,C^{\prime}\in\left\{C_{i}\right\}
with
C\neq C^{\prime}
, we have
C\cap C^{\prime}\subseteq{\rm bd}(C)\cap{\rm bd}(C^{\prime})
. The latter condition is equivalent to asking that
\left\{{\rm int}(C_{i})\right\}
are pairwise disjoint. A finite collection
\left\{U_{i}\right\}
of subsets is called a regular open partition of
X
if the
U_{i}
are non-empty and regular open,
X=\bigcup_{i}\overline{U_{i}}
and
\left\{U_{i}\right\}
are pairwise disjoint. There is a one-to-one correspondence between regular closed and regular open partitions sending a regular closed partition
\left\{C_{i}\right\}
to
\left\{{\rm int}(C_{i})\right\}
and a regular open partition
\left\{U_{i}\right\}
to
\{\overline{U_{i}}\}
.

Given regular open partitions
\mathcal{U}=\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
and
\mathcal{V}=\left\{V_{j}\right\}_{j\in J}
, the common refinement
\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}
is given by
\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}\coloneq\left\{U_{i}\cap V_{j}\colon i\in I,\,j\in J,\,U_{i}\cap V_{j}\neq\emptyset\right\}
. Moreover, we say that a regular open partition
\mathcal{V}=\left\{V_{j}\right\}_{j\in J}
is finer than (or refines) another regular open partition
\mathcal{U}=\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
if for all
V_{j}\in\mathcal{V}
there exists (a necessarily unique)
U_{i}\in\mathcal{U}
with
V_{j}\subseteq U_{i}
. In that case, we write
\mathcal{U}\preceq\mathcal{V}
. We define a similar refinement relation for regular closed partitions. In other words, given two regular closed partitions
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
with corresponding regular open partitions
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
, we have
\mathcal{C}\preceq\mathcal{D}
if and only if
\mathcal{U}\preceq\mathcal{V}
.

With these preparations, let us now introduce the transformations of interest. Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space. A continuous map
T\colon X\to X
is called
piecewise invertible
if there exists a regular open partition
\mathcal{U}=\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
with corresponding regular closed partition
\mathcal{C}=\left\{C_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
such that
T
restricts to a homeomorphism
T_{i}\colon C_{i}\to T(C_{i}),\,x\mapsto T(x)
. Moreover, we require that
T(U_{i})={\rm int}(T(C_{i}))
. In particular,
T(C_{i})
is regular closed for all
i\in I
. In addition, we call such a piecewise invertible map
T

expansive
if there exists a metric
d
on
X
and a constant
c>1
such that for all
i\in I
and
x,x^{\prime}\in C_{i}
, we have
d(T(x),T(x^{\prime}))\geq cd(x,x^{\prime})
. Note that in general, a piecewise invertible map need not be open, and even if it is expansive in our sense, it might not be positively expansive in the usual sense. The tent map from §
6.1
is an example of a piecewise invertible map, and it is actually expansive in our sense. For similar notions of (expansive) piecewise invertible maps, the reader may consult
[
18
,
44
]
and the references therein.

Let us now construct covers for piecewise invertible maps, motivated by the construction in Remark
6.2
. Let
T\colon X\to X
be a piecewise invertible map with a fixed regular open partition
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. We first need to introduce image and pre-image of regular open partitions under
T
. Let
\mathcal{V}=\left\{V_{j}\right\}_{j\in J}
be a regular open partition of
X
. Fix
i\in I
and let
\mathcal{T}_{i}\coloneq\left\{T(U_{i}\cap V_{j})\colon j\in J,\,U_{i}\cap V_{j}\neq\emptyset\right\}\cup\left\{X\setminus T(C_{i})\right\}
. It is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is a regular open partition of
X
. Now we define
T(\mathcal{V})\coloneq\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathcal{T}_{i}
. Note that
T(\mathcal{U})=\bigvee_{i\in I}\left\{T(U_{i}),\,X\setminus T(C_{i})\right\}
. Moreover, set
T^{-1}(\mathcal{V})\coloneq\left\{T_{i}^{-1}(O)\colon O\in\mathcal{V}\vee T(\mathcal{U}),\,i\in I,\,O\subseteq T(U_{i})\right\}
. Note that for every
W\in T^{-1}(\mathcal{V})
, there exists a unique
V_{j}\in\mathcal{V}
with
T(W)\subseteq V_{j}
.

Now define the regular open partition
\mathcal{U}_{m,n}\coloneq\bigvee_{0\leq\alpha\leq m,\,0\leq\beta\leq n}T^{-\alpha}(T^{\beta}(\mathcal{U}))
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{m,n}
be the corresponding regular closed partition. If
m^{\prime}\geq m
and
n^{\prime}\geq n
, then
\mathcal{U}_{m,n}\preceq\mathcal{U}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
, so that we obtain a map
\mathcal{U}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{U}_{m,n}
sending
U^{\prime}
the the unique
U\in\mathcal{U}_{m,n}
with
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
. With the partial order
(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\geq(m,n)
if
m^{\prime}\geq m
and
n^{\prime}\geq n
, we get an inverse system and hence can form the limit
\bar{X}\coloneq\varprojlim\,\mathcal{U}_{m,n}
. Elements
\bm{U}\in\bar{X}
are of the form
\bm{U}=(U_{m,n})
with
U_{m,n}\in\mathcal{U}_{m,n}
. Define

X^{\prime}\coloneq\left\{(x,\bm{U})\in X\times\bar{X}\colon\bm{U}=(U_{m,n}),\,x\in\overline{U_{m,n}}\ \forall\ m,n\right\}.

Denote by
\pi
the canonical projection
X^{\prime}\to X,\,(x,\bm{U})\mapsto x
. Moreover, define
\bar{T}\colon\bar{X}\to\bar{X}
by
\bar{T}((U_{m,n}))=\bm{V}=(V_{m,n})
, where
V_{m,n}
is the unique element of
\mathcal{U}_{m,n}
with
T(U_{m+1,n})\subseteq V_{m,n}
. In addition, we define
T^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime},\,(x,\bm{U})\mapsto(Tx,\bar{T}(\bm{U}))
.

It is straightforward to see that
T^{\prime}
is a cover of
T
(a cover map is given by
\pi
).

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
T
be a piecewise invertible map. Then the map
T^{\prime}
is a local homeomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{N}
with
\mu\geq 1
. Take
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\mu,\nu}
. Let

\mathbb{V}\coloneq\left\{V\in\mathcal{U}_{\mu-1,\nu+1}\colon\exists\ W\in\mathcal{U}_{\mu,\nu+1}\text{ with }W\subseteq U,\,T(W)\subseteq V\right\}.

Set
Z_{U}\coloneq\left\{(x,(U_{m,n}))\in X^{\prime}\colon U_{\mu,\nu}=U\right\}
and
Z_{\mathbb{V}}\coloneq\left\{(y,(V_{m,n}))\in X^{\prime}\colon V_{\mu-1,\nu+1}\in\mathbb{V}\right\}
. From the definition of
T^{\prime}
and
Z_{\mathbb{V}}
, it is clear that
T^{\prime}(Z_{U})\subseteq Z_{\mathbb{V}}
. We construct a continuous inverse to
T^{\prime}\colon Z_{U}\to Z_{\mathbb{V}}
. Let
i\in I
be the unique index such that
U_{\mu,\nu}\subseteq U_{i}
. Then, by definition of
\mathbb{V}
, we have
T(U_{i})\cap V_{\mu-1,\nu+1}\neq\emptyset
for
(y,(V_{m,n}))\in Z_{\mathbb{V}}
. Since each
V_{m,n}
for
m\geq 0,n\geq 1
lies in a refinement of
T(\mathcal{U})
, we have that
V_{m,n}\subseteq T(U_{i})
. Now it is straightforward to check that the following map

Z_{\mathbb{V}}\to X^{\prime},\,(y,(V_{m,n}))\mapsto(T_{i}^{-1}(y),(W_{m,n})),

where
W_{m,n}\in\mathcal{U}_{m,n}
is uniquely determined by the requirement that
T_{i}^{-1}(V_{m-1,n+1})\subseteq W_{m,n}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 1
, is continuous, maps
Z_{\mathbb{V}}
into
Z_{U}
and is the inverse of the restriction of
T^{\prime}
to
Z_{U}
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

If
T
is an expansive piecewise invertible map, then the canonical projection
X^{\prime}\to\bar{X},\,(x,\bm{U})\mapsto\bm{U}
is a homeomorphism. In particular,
T^{\prime}
and
\bar{T}
are conjugate.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that the map is injective. So assume that
(x,\bm{U})
and
(x^{\prime},\bm{U})
are elements of
X^{\prime}
. That means that for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
i_{\beta}\in I
such that
T^{\beta}(x)
and
T^{\beta}(x^{\prime})
both lie in
\overline{U_{i_{\beta}}}
. Then it follows that
d(T^{\beta}(x),T^{\beta}(x^{\prime}))\geq c^{\beta}d(x,x^{\prime})
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
. But
X
is compact, so that
\sup_{\beta}c^{\beta}d(x,x^{\prime})\leq\sup_{\beta}d(T^{\beta}(x),T^{\beta}(x^{\prime}))<\infty
. It follows that
d(x,x^{\prime})=0
, i.e.,
x=x^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
T
be an expansive piecewise invertible map. Assume that there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\alpha_{m,n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{U}_{m,n}\preceq\bigvee_{0\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{m,n},\,0\leq\beta\leq\gamma}T^{-\alpha}(T^{\beta}(\mathcal{U}))
. Then
\bar{T}
is conjugate to a subshift. Thus
T^{\prime}
is conjugate to a shift of finite type, which is at the same time a cover of
T
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{V}\coloneq\bigvee_{0\leq\beta\leq\gamma}T^{\beta}(\mathcal{U})
and
\pi_{\mathcal{V}}
the canonical projection
\bar{X}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{V}
. Then the map

\bar{X}\to\prod_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{V},\,\bm{U}\mapsto(\pi_{\mathcal{V}}(\bar{T}^{\alpha}(\bm{U}))_{\alpha}

is a continuous map which is injective by our assumption. Therefore, we obtain an identification of
\bar{X}
with the image of this map. Under this identification, it is clear that the map
\bar{T}
corresponds to the one-sided shift. In other words, this shows that
\bar{T}
is conjugate to a subshift.

Now Lemma
6.4
implies that
T^{\prime}
is conjugate to
\bar{T}
, so that
T^{\prime}
is conjugate to a subshift, too. Since it is also a local homeomorphism by Proposition
6.3
, it follows that
T^{\prime}
is conjugate to a shift of finite type.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

If
T
is an expansive piecewise invertible map, then
T^{\prime}
is an essential cover of
T
(weighted by
w=1
).

Proof.

Recall that the covering map
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is defined by
(x,\bm{U})\mapsto x
. By expansivity, the diameters of the elements in
\mathcal{U}_{m,n}
shrink to zero uniformly as
m,n
tend to infinity. Hence, for
x
in the dense
G_{\delta}
set
G^{\prime}\coloneq\bigcap_{m,n}\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{m,n}}U
, there is a unique pre-image element
\iota(x)\coloneq(x,\bm{U})\in\pi^{-1}(x)
, and it is straightforward to see that
\pi^{-1}(G^{\prime})
is dense. Let
P^{\prime}\coloneq X\setminus G^{\prime}
. Since
T
is piecewise invertible, it maps meagre sets to meagre sets, and is weakly open. Therefore,
P\coloneq\bigcup_{k\geq 0,l\geq 0}T^{-k}(T^{l}(P^{\prime}))
is a meagre and fully invariant subset containing
P^{\prime}
. Hence
G\coloneq X\setminus P
is a co-meagre and fully invariant subset contained in
G^{\prime}
, so by density of
\pi^{-1}(G^{\prime})
and continuity of
\iota\colon G^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
(Lemma
3.20
), it follows that
\pi^{-1}(G)
is also co-meagre and fully invariant. Therefore,
T^{\prime}
is an essential cover for
T
.
∎

By the universal property of the minimal essential cover (Theorem
4.12
) and Remark
4.13
, it follows that the minimal essential cover of
T
is a factor of
T^{\prime}
.
The corollary below follows immediately from Lemma
6.6
and Remark
4.16
.

Corollary 6.7
.

If
T
is an expansive piecewise invertible map that is topologically transitive, then
T^{\prime}
is topologically transitive.

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
T
be an expansive piecewise invertible map which is topologically transitive. Assume that there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\alpha_{m,n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{U}_{m,n}\preceq\bigvee_{0\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{m,n},\,0\leq\beta\leq\gamma}T^{-\alpha}(T^{\beta}(\mathcal{U}))
. Then our cover
T^{\prime}
of
T
is (conjugate to) a topologically transitive shift of finite type.

Remark 6.9
.

As in Remark
4.32
, we conclude that for piecewise invertible maps (and their minimal essential covers) satisfying the assumptions in Corollary
6.8
, the thermodynamic formalism holds.

6.3.
Williams pre-solenoids

We present an example class of dynamical systems constructed from substitutions that are used to define the solenoids of
[
48
]
.
We call them
Williams pre-solenoids
and show that the minimal essential dynamical cover (in the sense of Section
4.3
, for the weight
w=1
) of a Williams pre-solenoid is equal to a naturally defined shift of finite type from the associated substitution. As we will explain, Williams pre-solenoids are particular examples of expansive piecewise invertible maps in the sense of Section
6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite set and denote by
\mathcal{A}^{*}=\coprod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{A}^{n}
the collection of words in
\mathcal{A}
. Given a word
\alpha=\alpha_{1}...\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
, we denote by
|\alpha|=n
its length and
\alpha_{i}
its
i^{th}
entry,
i\leq n
. A
substitution
is a function
S\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
. We can extend the definition of the substitution to a function
S\colon\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
by the formula
S(\alpha_{1}...\alpha_{n})=S(\alpha_{1})...S(\alpha_{n})
. The Williams pre-solenoid of a substitution
S
is constructed as follows.

Consider the wedge sum
X_{\mathcal{A}}=\bigvee_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\{\alpha\}
of
\mathcal{A}
copies of the circle, glued together at the origins
(0,\alpha)
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
. Define a function
T_{S}\colon X_{\mathcal{A}}\to X_{\mathcal{A}}
by

T_{S}(t,\alpha)\coloneq(|S(\alpha)|t,S(\alpha)_{i})\text{ whenever }t\in\big[\frac{i-1}{|S(\alpha)|},\frac{i}{|S(\alpha)|}\big].

Since sufficiently small sub-intervals of each individual circle are mapped homeomorphically onto their images under
T_{S}
, it follows that
T_{S}
is a piecewise invertible map in the sense of Section
6.2
. Such a system has a natural cover by a shift of finite type. Let
E^{1}_{S}\coloneq\{(\alpha,i)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathbb{N}\colon i\leq|S(\alpha)|)

and
E^{0}_{S}=\mathcal{A}
. We define range and source maps
r,s\colon E^{1}_{S}\to E^{0}_{S}
by
r(\alpha,i)=\alpha
and
s(\alpha,i)=S(\alpha)_{i}
. The adjacency matrix of this graph is the well-known
substitution matrix
of a substitution. Let
(\sigma,E_{S}^{\infty})
be the corresponding shift of finite type. Let
\pi\colon E_{S}^{\infty}\to X_{\mathcal{A}}
be defined for
x=((\alpha_{1},i_{1})(\alpha_{2},i_{2})...)\in E^{\infty}_{S}
as

\pi(x)\coloneq\big(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{i_{n}-1}{|S(\alpha_{1}...\alpha_{k})|},\alpha_{1}\big).

It is easy to see that
\sigma\circ\pi=\pi\circ T_{S}
. Since
|\pi^{-1}(x)|=1
except possibly at the (invariant set of) rational points and
\pi
is weakly open, it follows that
\pi
is an essential cover of
T_{S}
. Moreover, it can be seen as the cover constructed from the regular closed partition consisting of intervals
\{(\big[\frac{i-1}{|S(\alpha)},\frac{i}{|S(\alpha)}\big],\alpha)\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A},i\leq|S(\alpha)|}
. Let us sketch the argument showing
\pi
is in fact the minimal essential cover of
T_{S}
when the substitution satisfies the following (mild) condition:
for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, there are
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
,
y_{1}\neq y_{2}
and the words
x_{1}\alpha
,
x_{2}\alpha

Remark 6.10
.

These examples show that a dynamical system need not be positively expansive, even if its minimal or minimal essential cover is positively expansive. Non-sofic subshifts provide examples where the original system is positively expansive but our covers are not. So in general there is no relationship between positive expansivity of the minimal or minimal essential cover and positive expansivity of the original system.

7.
Examples from semi-étale groupoids

In this section, we consider a locally compact Hausdorff groupoid
G
with unit space
G^{(0)}=X
and range and source maps
r,s\colon G\to X
.
We assume that
G
is semi-étale in the sense that
r
(and hence also
s
) is locally injective.
We view
G
as a topological graph
r,s\colon G\to X
with the canonical weight
w=1
, system of measures
\lambda_{x}=\sum_{g\in r^{-1}(x)}\delta_{g}
and transfer operator
\Lambda
.
We study its minimal (graph) cover
\hat{r},\hat{s}\colon\hat{G}\to\hat{X}
as introduced in Section
3.4
and observe that the process of constructing this minimal cover as explained in §
3.5
terminates after a single step, and moreover admits a canonical étale groupoid structure.

First we need the following terminology:
every element
g\in G
induces a bijection
G^{s(g)}=r^{-1}(s(g))\to G^{r(g)}=r^{-1}(r(g)),\,\gamma\mapsto g\gamma
.
Now, given a measure
\mu
with
{\rm supp}(\mu)\subseteq G^{s(g)}
, we let
g.\mu
denote the pushforward of
\mu
with respect to this bijection (so
{\rm supp}(g.\mu)\subseteq G^{r(g)}
).

Lemma 7.1
.

If
g_{i}
converges to
g
in
G
and
\lambda_{r(g_{i})}
converges to
\mu
in
\mathcal{M}(G)
, then
\lambda_{s(g_{i})}
converges to
g^{-1}.\mu
.

Proof.

Since
r
is locally injective, the limit points of the measures
\lambda_{x}
,
x\in X
, are of the form
\mu=\sum_{y\in S\subseteq r^{-1}(x)}\delta_{y}
. Therefore, to prove the lemma it suffices to show
g^{-1}\left\{\text{limit points of }r^{-1}(r(g_{i}))\right\}=\left\{\text{limit points of }r^{-1}(s(g_{i}))\right\}
. If
y
is a limit point of
r^{-1}(r(g_{i}))
then there exists a net
(y_{i})
with
r(y_{i})=r(g_{i})
such that
y=\lim y_{i}
. This implies that
g^{-1}y=\lim g_{i}^{-1}y_{i}
is the limit point of
g_{i}^{-1}r^{-1}(r(g_{i}))=r^{-1}(s(g_{i}))
. Conversely, if
y
is a limit point of
r^{-1}(s(g_{i}))
, then there exists a net
(y_{i})
such that
y=\lim y_{i}
and
r(y_{i})=s(g_{i})
. Hence
gy=\lim g_{i}y_{i}
is a limit point of
g_{i}r^{-1}(s(g_{i}))=r^{-1}(r(g_{i}))
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

The cover construction of
r,s\colon G\to X
terminates after a single step, i.e.
\hat{G}=G_{1}
and
\hat{X}=X_{1}
.

Proof.

Recall the terminology from Section
3.2
.
Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the closure of
{}^{*}\text{-}{\rm alg}\big(C_{c}(G),C_{c}(G)r^{*}\Lambda(C_{c}(G))\big)\subseteq\ell^{\infty}_{c}(G)
in the inductive limit topology of
\ell_{c}^{\infty}(G)
and let
G^{\prime}
be the spectrum of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{1}
.
Let
X_{1}
be the spectrum of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
.
From the description in Section
3.5
, we identify
G_{1}
with the closure of
\left\{(g,\lambda_{r(g)},\lambda_{s(g)})\colon g\in G\right\}
in
G\times M(G)\times M(G)
,
and a similar method shows

can be identified with the closure of
\left\{(g,\lambda_{r(g)})\colon g\in G\right\}
in

.
The range and source maps extend to

, and by construction it is clear that

factors through to a continuous map

.
Similarly,

factors through to a continuous map

given by

(Lemma

ensures that this is well-defined).
It follows from Gelfand duality that

, so
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
It follows that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and

and
\hat{X}=X_{1}
.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Hausdorff and semi-étale groupoid.
The cover
\hat{r},\hat{s}\colon\hat{G}\to\hat{X}
admits an étale groupoid structure which extends the given groupoid structure on
G
.

Proof.

We saw in the proof of Lemma
7.2
that
\hat{G}
is (homeomorphic to) the closure of
\left\{(g,\lambda_{r(g)})\colon g\in G\right\}
in
G\times M(G)

and
\hat{X}
is (homeomorphic to) the closure of
\left\{(x,\lambda_{x})\colon x\in X\right\}
in

For

we have

and for
(x,\mu)\in\hat{X}
we have
{\rm supp}(\mu)\subseteq r^{-1}(x)
.
The range and source maps are
\hat{r}(g,\mu)=(r(g),\mu)
and
\hat{s}(g,\mu)=(s(g),g^{-1}\mu)
for all
(g,\mu)\in\hat{G}
.
The product of
(g,\mu)
and
(h,\nu)
is defined precisely if

and

are composable and

in which case

,
and inversion is given by

As explained in Example

,

is an étale groupoid.
∎

Remark 7.4

(Universality and lifting properties)
.

Recall that a groupoid homomorphism
\pi\colon G\to H
is called
r
-bijective
if
\pi\colon G^{x}\to H^{\pi(x)}
is a bijection, for all
x\in G^{(0)}
. This is equivalent to
\pi
being a morphism as a weighted topological graph when weighted by
w=1
. It is easy to see
\iota\colon G\to\hat{G}
is a groupoid homomorphism; under our identifications it is given by
g\mapsto(g,\lambda_{r(g)})
. Moreover, since it is a measured morphism, it is also
r
-bijective.

The minimal cover of a semi-étale groupoid has the following universal property: given an étale groupoid
\dot{G}
that covers
G
in the sense that there is a continuous homomorphism
\dot{\pi}\colon\dot{G}\to G
and an
r
-bijective homomorphism
\dot{\iota}\colon G\to\dot{G}
with dense image such that
\dot{\pi}\circ\dot{\iota}=\text{id}_{G}
, there is a unique continuous and
r
-bijective homomorphism
\phi\colon\dot{G}\to\hat{G}
such that
\phi\circ\dot{\iota}=\iota
and
\pi\circ\phi=\dot{\pi}
.

Moreover, the cover satisfies the following lifting property: If
\varphi\colon G\to H
is a continuous and
r
-bijective homomorphism between semi-étale groupoids, then there is a unique continuous and
r
-bijective groupoid homomorphism
\hat{\varphi}\colon\hat{G}\to\hat{H}
satisfying
\hat{\varphi}\circ\iota_{G}=\iota_{H}\circ\varphi
and hence
\varphi\circ\pi_{\hat{G}}=\pi_{\hat{H}}\circ\varphi
.

These properties follow from the usual universal property and lifting property of the minimal topological graph cover since the identification of compactly supported functions on the covers as functions in
\ell^{\infty}_{c}(G)
preserves convolution.

Next, we identify the étale cover groupoid
\hat{G}
as the transformation groupoid of a canonical action of
G
on
\hat{X}
.
We refer the reader to
[
47
, Chapter 2.1]
for details about groupoid actions.

Proposition 7.5
.

There is an action of
G
on
\hat{X}
given by
g.(x,\mu)\coloneqq(r(g),g.\mu)
, for all
g\in G
and
(x,\mu)\in\hat{X}
with
s(g)=x
.
Moveover, there is an isomorphism of topological groupoids
\hat{G}\to G\ltimes\hat{X}
given by
(g,\mu)\mapsto(g,(s(g),g^{-1}.\mu))
, for all
(g,\mu)\in\hat{G}
.

Proof.

We verify that
G
acts on
\hat{X}
.
The map
\rho\colon\hat{X}\to X
given by
\rho(x,\mu)=x
defines a continuous anchor map.
Let
G\ast\hat{X}\coloneq\left\{(g,(x,\mu))\in G\times\hat{X}\colon s(g)=\rho(x,\mu)=x\right\}
and define
g.(x,\mu)\coloneq(r(g),g.\mu)
for all
(g,(x,\mu))\in G\ast\hat{X}
.
It is straightforward to verify that
\rho(x,\mu).(x,\mu)=(x,\mu)
, for all
(x,\mu)\in\hat{X}
,
and that when
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are composable in
G
and
(g_{2},(x,\mu))\in G\ast\hat{X}
,
then
(g_{1},g_{2}.(x,\mu))\in G\ast\hat{X}
and
(g_{1}g_{2}).(x,\mu)=g_{1}.(g_{2}.(x,\mu))
.

The transformation groupoid of the action of
G
on
\hat{X}
is of the form

G\ltimes\hat{X}=\left\{(g,(x,\nu))\in G\times\hat{X}\colon s(g)=x\right\}

with unit space
\{(x,(x,\mu))\in X\times\hat{X}\}
that we identify with
\hat{X}
.
The range and source maps are
r(g,(s(g),\nu))=(r(g),g.\nu)
and
s(g,(s(g),\nu))=(s(g),\nu)
,
and
(g_{1},(s(g_{1}),\nu))
and
(g_{2},(s(g_{2}),\mu))
are composable precisely if
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are composable and
g_{2}.\mu=\nu
in which case

(g_{1},(s(g_{1}),g_{2}\mu))(g_{2},(s(g_{2}),\mu))=(g_{1}g_{2},(s(g_{2}),\mu))
.
It is straighforward to verify that the map
\hat{G}\to G\ltimes\hat{X}
given by
(g,\mu)\mapsto(g,(s(g),g^{-1}.\mu))
for all

defines an isomorphism of topological groupoids.
The inverse is given by
(g,(s(g),\nu))\mapsto(g,g.\nu)
, for all
(g,(s(g),\nu))\in G\ltimes\hat{X}
.
∎

Remark 7.6
.

The minimal essential cover of
G
(when
G
is post-critically sparse) is also an étale groupoid since
\tilde{X}=\overline{\iota(X\setminus P_{\Lambda})}
satisfies
s(r^{-1}(\tilde{X}))\subseteq\tilde{X}
. Moreover,
\tilde{G}
is isomorphic to
G\ltimes\tilde{X}
.

We derive some structural similarities between the groupoid
G
and its minimal cover.
Recall that a topological groupoid
G
is
effective
if the unit space is the interior of the isotropy of the groupoid. By
[
22
, Theorem 15.1]
, every second countable semi-étale groupoid
G
has a Borel Haar system given by the counting measure
\lambda^{x}
on
G^{x}
,
x\in X
. In this case, it follows from
[
37
, Proposition 2.4]
that
G
is
Borel amenable
if and only if there is a
Borel approximate invariant density
, which is a sequence of Borel functions
(g_{n})
satisfying the conditions in
[
37
, Definition 2.3]
. If
G
is additionally étale, then
(g_{n})
can be chosen to be continuous with additional convergence properties (see
[
37
, Theorem 2.14]
). With these properties,
(g_{n})
is called a
topological approximate invariant density

[
37
, Definition 2.7]
.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
G
be a locally compact Hausdorff and semi-étale groupoid, and let
\hat{G}
be the étale cover groupoid.

(1)

If
G
is second countable, then
G
is Borel amenable if and only if
\hat{G}
is Borel amenable.

(2)

If
\hat{G}
is topologically transitive (or minimal), then
G
is topologically transitive (or minimal).

(3)

G
is principal if and only if
\hat{G}
is principal.

(4)

If
\hat{G}
is effective, then
G
is effective.

Proof.

(1)
: The proof is similar to the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
.
Note that
\pi\colon\hat{G}^{x}\to G^{\pi(x)}
is a bijection for all
x\in\hat{X}
.
If
G
is Borel amenable, then there exists a Borel approximate invariant density
(h_{n})
on
G
, and it follows that
(h_{n}\circ\pi)
is a Borel approximate invariant density on
\hat{G}
. Conversely, since
G
is second countable, so is
\hat{G}
, so that if
\hat{G}
is Borel amenable, then there is a topological approximate invariant density
(g_{n})\subseteq C_{c}(\hat{G})
. Since
\iota\colon G^{x}\to\hat{G}^{\iota(x)}
is a bijection for all
x\in X
, it follows that
(g_{n}\circ\iota)
is a Borel approximate invariant density on
G
, because our transfer operator preserves bounded Borel functions with compact support.

(2)
: Suppose
\hat{G}
is topologically transitive and choose
(x,\mu)\in\hat{X}
such that the orbit
G.(x,\mu)
is dense in
\hat{X}
.
Then
G.x
is dense in
X
, so
G
is topologically transitive.
If
\hat{G}
is minimal, then this argument works for every unit in
\hat{X}
, so it follows that
G
is minimal.

(3)
: Suppose first that
G
is principal and take an isotropy element
(g,(x,\mu))\in\hat{G}
.
Then
g
is an isotropy element in
G
, so that
g=x
, and therefore
(g,(x,\mu))
is a unit in
\hat{G}
.
Conversely, suppose
\hat{G}
is principal and take an isotropy element
g\in G
.
Then
(g,(s(g),\lambda_{s(g)}))
is an isotropy element in
\hat{G}
, hence the element is a unit, so
g=s(g)
.

(4)
:
Suppose
\hat{G}
is effective, and take an element
g
in
G
that is in the interior of the isotropy. This means that there is an open set
U
of
G
which is pure isotropy and contains
g
.
The canonical map
\pi\colon\hat{G}\to G
given by
(g,(s(g),\mu))\mapsto g
is continuous,
and the preimage
\pi^{-1}(U)
is open and pure isotropy and it contains
(g,(s(g),\lambda_{s(g)}))
.
As
\hat{G}
is effective, we see that
(g,(s(g),\lambda_{s(g)}))
is a unit, so it follows that
g
is a unit.
Therefore,
G
is effective.
∎

For the second equality, observe that the value is zero unless

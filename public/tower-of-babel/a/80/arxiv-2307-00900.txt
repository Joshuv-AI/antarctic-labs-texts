Abstract
. In this article, we are interested in modular forms with non-vanishing central critical values and linear independence of Fourier coefficients of modular forms. The main ingredient is a generalization of a theorem due to VanderKam to modular symbols of higher weights. We prove that for sufficiently large primes p p , Hecke operators T 1 , T 2 , … , T D T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{D} act linearly independently on the winding elements inside the space of weight 2 ​ k 2k cuspidal modular symbol 𝕊 2 ​ k ​ ( Γ 0 ​ ( p ) ) \mathbb{S}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p)) with k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 for D 2 ≪ p D^{2}\ll p . This gives a bound on the number of newforms with non-vanishing arithmetic L L -functions at their central critical points and linear independence on the reductions of these modular forms for prime modulo l ≠ p l\not=p .

1.
Introduction

There is a considerable interest to study Hecke algebras generated by Hecke operators acting on the space of classical elliptic modular forms or dually the space of modular symbols. Hecke operators act by correspondences on the modular curves, and hence on the modular forms and modular symbols. These are important for the study of integral or rational structures. These algebras play a crucial rule in some important discoveries of number theory, including Merel’s proof of uniform boundedness of torsion of elliptic curves. Studying linear independence of Hecke operators acting on arithmetic cycles play an important role in Merel’s proof of uniform boundedness conjecture

The group

\displaystyle g\{\alpha,\beta\}=\{g(\alpha),g(\beta)\},

2.3.
Modular symbols and modular forms

For any cusp form

, the integration pairing (see
[
19
]

\displaystyle\big\langle z^{m}\{0,\infty\},f\big\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(z)z^{m}dz\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq m\leq 2k-2.

Let the Fourier expansion of a newform

be of the form
\displaystyle f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)q^{n}
. Then the

\displaystyle L^{ar}(f,s):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},

which is absolutely convergent for

The
L^{ar}
-function has an Euler product expansion of the form

\displaystyle L^{ar}(f,s)=\left(1-a_{f}(p)\,p^{-s}\right)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\not=p\\
q\,\mathrm{prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-a_{f}(q)\,q^{-s}+q^{2k-1}q^{-2s}\right)^{-1}.

The completed
L^{ar}
-function is defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda(f,s):=\left(\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Gamma(s)\,L^{ar}(f,s)

which satisfies the functional equation
\Lambda(f,s)=i^{-2k}\,\epsilon_{f}\,\Lambda(f,2k-s)
, where
\epsilon_{f}=\pm 1
.

Also, for a newform
f\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
we have the following relation (see
[
4
]
, equation(14), p. 1385)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\big\langle z^{m}\{0,\infty\},f\big\rangle=\frac{m!\,i^{m+1}}{(2\pi)^{m+1}}\,L^{ar}(f,m+1)\,\ \text{for}\,\ 0\leq m\leq 2k-2,

where the
L^{ar}
-function has critical values at the integers
m+1
.

2.4.
Different normalization of
L
-function

Two different normalizations of
L
-function exist in the literature. Let
f(z)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{2k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz}
be a Hecke eigenform in
S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
. We define:

L^{an}(f,s):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}

which is absolutely convergent for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
. By our definition we also have

L^{ar}(f,s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{2k-1}{2}}}{n^{s}}.

Then we get
L^{ar}(f,s)=L^{an}(f,s-(2k-1)/2)
. Since it is well known that the critical strip for
L^{an}(f,s)
is
0\leq\operatorname{Re}(s)\leq 1
, we get
\frac{2k-1}{2}\leq\operatorname{Re}(s)\leq\frac{2k+1}{2}
as the critical strip for
L^{ar}(f,s)
.
The completed analytic
L
-function is defined by

\Lambda^{an}(f,s):=p^{\frac{s}{2}}L_{\infty}(f,s)L^{an}(f,s),

where
L_{\infty}(f,s)
is given by

L_{\infty}(f,s)=\pi^{-s}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{2k-1}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}+\frac{2k+1}{4}\right).

The completed analytic
L
-function satisfies the function equation

\Lambda^{an}(f,s)=\epsilon_{f}\Lambda^{an}(f,1-s),

where
\epsilon_{f}=\pm 1
.

3.
Linear dependence of Hecke operators

In this section we are interested in the linear dependence of Hecke operators inside the space of cuspidal symbols
\mathbb{S}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
with a prime
p
. Suppose that the Hecke operators
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{D}
are linearly dependent on
z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}
.
Since the winding element
\mathbf{e}
is the image of
\{0,\infty\}
, there exist
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{D}
(at least one of them non-zero) with

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\,T_{i}(z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\})=0.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
denote the Hecke basis of
S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
. In other words,
\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
consists of Hecke eigenforms.
Let
f
be a Hecke eigenform, then by the self-adjoint property (see
[
19
, Theorem 8.21]
) of the Hecke operator, we have

\displaystyle\langle T_{i}(z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}),f\rangle=\langle(z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}),\,T_{i}f\rangle\quad\text{for}\,\ i=1,\ldots,D.

Let
\lambda_{f}(i)
be the Hecke eigenvalue, i.e.,
T_{i}f=\lambda_{f}(i)f
. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle T_{i}(z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}),f\rangle=\lambda_{f}(i)\,\langle(z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}),\,f\rangle\quad\text{for}\,\ i=1,\ldots,D.

Now, the linear dependence of the Hecke operators is equivalent to the existence of non-trivial solutions
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{D}
to the following system of equations:

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\,\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\,\langle T_{i}(z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}),f\rangle\bigg|^{2},

where
\omega_{f}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\langle f,f\rangle_{\mathrm{pet}}
. The equation (
3.1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(i)\lambda_{f}(j)\big|\langle(z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}),f\rangle\big|^{2}.

Then, using (
2.2
), we get

(3.2)

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(i)\lambda_{f}(j)\big|L^{ar}(f,k)\big|^{2}.

Recall the following Petersson formula (cf.
[
20
, Lemma 1.2]
) that works for arbitrary weight
k\geq 1
(see also
[
3
, §3, p. 1022]
). In the following lemma, a basis of
S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))

is a Hecke basis if it is an eigenform of Hecke operators
T_{n}
for all
n
with
(n,p)=1
.
Recall that Hecke eigenvalues satisfy the following recurssion formula:

Lemma 3.1

(Petersson formula)
.

If
\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
is a Hecke basis of
S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(r)\lambda_{f}(s)=\delta_{rs}+2\pi i^{2k}\sum_{p|c}\frac{\mathcal{S}(r,s,c)}{c}\,J_{2k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{rs}}{c}\right),

where
\mathcal{S}(r,s,c)
denotes the Kloosterman sum and
J_{\nu}(x)
denotes the
J
-Bessel function of order
\nu
.

Note that, we use the Petersson formula (i.e., Lemma
3.1
) many times in this paper. For notational convenience, whenever we use the Petersson formula by
S_{\mathrm{main}}
we denote the term coming from the
\delta_{rs}
-part of the Petersson formula and
S_{\mathrm{off}}
denote the remaining terms coming from the Kloosterman sum in the Petersson formula.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
be a cusp form of weight
2k
and let
L^{ar}(f,s)
be the
L^{ar}
-function associated with the cusp form
f
. Then at the central critical point
s=k
, the
L^{ar}
-function has the following expression:

\displaystyle\big|L^{ar}(f,k)\big|^{2}=\frac{2}{\left((k-1)!\right)^{2}}\sum_{l,\,m\geq 1}G_{k}\left(\frac{lm}{p}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}(l)\lambda_{f}(m)}{\sqrt{lm}},

where
G_{k}(x)
has the following integral representation

\displaystyle G_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(t)=3/\penalty 4}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}x^{t}}\frac{dt}{t}.

Proof.

Note that, although the proof of this lemma is closely resembles VanderKam
[
20
, Lemma 1.1]
, we include the complete proof here in order to provide a comprehensive explanation in our paper.

For values of
s
outside the critical strip, the completed
L^{ar}
-function satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(f,s)=i^{-2k}\epsilon_{f}\Lambda(f,2k-s),

where we have the expression for
\Lambda(f,s)
as given in equation (
2.1
), and
\epsilon_{f}
represents a constant that can take values
\pm 1
.
For
s=k+t
with
t>\frac{1}{2}
the
L
-function
L^{ar}(f,k+t)
satisfies this functional equation, i.e., we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2\pi}\right)^{k+t}\Gamma(k+t)\,L^{ar}(f,k+t)=i^{-2k}\epsilon_{f}\left(\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2\pi}\right)^{k-t}\Gamma(k-t)\,L^{ar}(f,k-t)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{t}\Gamma(k+t)^{2}L^{ar}(f,k+t)^{2}=\left(\frac{p}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{-t}\Gamma(k-t)^{2}L^{ar}(f,k-t)^{.}

This implies
\left(\frac{p}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{t}\Gamma(k+t)^{2}L^{ar}(f,k+t)^{2}
is an even function. Hence, we can integrate this function against
\frac{dt}{t}
at
\mathrm{Re}(t)=\frac{3}{4}
and shift to
\mathrm{Re}(t)=-\frac{3}{4}
by picking up the residue at
t=0
.
From the defintion we have

\displaystyle L^{ar}(f,k+t)^{2}=\sum_{l,\,m\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(l)\lambda_{f}(m)}{\sqrt{lm}}(lm)^{-t}.

Now, since
L^{ar}(f,k)^{2}=|L^{ar}(f,k)|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma(k)^{2}\cdot|L^{ar}(f,k)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{t=0}\left(\frac{p}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{t}\Gamma(k+t)^{2}L^{ar}(f,k+t)^{2}\,\frac{1}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(t)=\frac{3}{4}}\left(\frac{p}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{t}\Gamma(k+t)^{2}L^{ar}(f,k+t)^{2}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{l,\,m\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(l)\lambda_{f}(m)}{\sqrt{lm}}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(t)=\frac{3}{4}}\left(\frac{p}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{t}\frac{\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(lm)^{t}}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{l,\,m\geq 1}G_{k}\left(\frac{lm}{p}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}(l)\lambda_{f}(m)}{\sqrt{lm}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
x\geq 1
we have
G_{k}(x)\ll e^{-c\sqrt{x}}
with a fixed constant
c>0
.

Proof.

We shift the contour of the integral to
\mathrm{Re}(t)=\sqrt{x}
and by using the Stirling formula from
[
9
]
, i.e.,

\Gamma(s)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\ s^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{-s}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{|s|}\right)\right)
, in the integral expression of
G_{k}(x)
, we get

\displaystyle G_{k}(x)\ll\int_{\mathrm{Re}(t)=\sqrt{x}}\frac{(k+t)^{2k+2t-1}}{(2\pi)^{2t}x^{t}}\,e^{-2(k+t)}\,\frac{dt}{t}\ll e^{-c\sqrt{x}}

with a fixed constant
c>0
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

By shifting the contour to the left of the origin, one can easily show that the function
G_{k}(x)
is bounded for
0<x<1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

The Hecke operators
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{D}
are linearly dependent on
z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}
, is equivalent to the existence of the non-trivial solutions to the equation

\displaystyle\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}<D}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}\sum_{Id_{1},Jd_{2}<D}\alpha_{Id_{1}}\bar{\alpha}_{Jd_{2}}\sum_{L,M\geq 1}\frac{G_{k}(LMd_{1}d_{2}/\penalty p)}{\sqrt{LM}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(IL)\lambda_{f}(JM)=0.

Proof.

We have already seen that the linear dependency of
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{D}
on
z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}
is equivalent to the existence of a non-trivial solution of the following equation

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(i)\lambda_{f}(j)\big|L^{ar}(f,k)\big|^{2}.

Using the value of
\big|L^{ar}(f,k)\big|^{2}
from Lemma
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\sum_{l,\,m\geq 1}G_{k}\left(\frac{lm}{p}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{lm}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(i)\lambda_{f}(j)\lambda_{f}(l)\lambda_{f}(m).

Substitute
i=Id_{1},\,j=Jd_{2}
where
d_{1}=(i,l),\,d_{2}=(j,m)
and we also substitute
Ld_{1}=l,\,Md_{2}=m
. Then we get

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}<D}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}\sum_{Id_{1},Jd_{2}<D}\alpha_{Id_{1}}\bar{\alpha}_{Jd_{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{L,M\geq 1}\frac{G_{k}(LMd_{1}d_{2}/\penalty p)}{\sqrt{LM}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(Id_{1})\lambda_{f}(Jd_{2})\lambda_{f}(Ld_{1})\lambda_{f}(Md_{2}).

We use the following multiplicative relation of the eigenvalues of Hecke operatos

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(r)\lambda_{f}(s)=\sum_{d|(r,s)}\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{rs}{d}\right),

where
(r,s)
denotes the gcd of
r
and
s
, and it has no common factor with the level. Since in our case, we have the prime level
p
, we can avoid this restriction. Hence we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}<D}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}\sum_{Id_{1},Jd_{2}<D}\alpha_{Id_{1}}\bar{\alpha}_{Jd_{2}}\sum_{L,M\geq 1}\frac{G_{k}(LMd_{1}d_{2}/\penalty p)}{\sqrt{LM}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(IL)\lambda_{f}(JM).

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we use Lemma
3.1
in the equation (
3.3
). Then by
S_{\mathrm{main}}
we denote the term involving the Kronecker delta function and by
S_{\mathrm{off}}
we denote the remaining terms involving the Kloosterman sum, i.e.,

S_{\mathrm{main}}:=\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}<D}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}\sum_{Id_{1},Jd_{2}<D}\alpha_{Id_{1}}\bar{\alpha}_{Jd_{2}}\sum_{L,M\geq 1}\frac{G_{k}(LMd_{1}d_{2}/\penalty p)}{\sqrt{LM}}\,\ \delta_{IL\,JM}

and

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{off}}:=\sum_{d_{1},\,d_{2}<D}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}

\displaystyle\sum_{Id_{1},\,Jd_{2}<D}\alpha_{Id_{1}}\bar{\alpha}_{Jd_{2}}\sum_{L,M\geq 1}\frac{G_{k}(LMd_{1}d_{2}/\penalty p)}{\sqrt{LM}}

\displaystyle\times\left(2\pi i^{2k}\sum_{p|c}\frac{\mathcal{S}(IL,JM,c)}{c}\,J_{2k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{ILJM}}{c}\right)\right).

4.
Lower bound for the main term

In this section, we establish a lower bound for
S_{\mathrm{main}}
. From the previous section, we get the following:

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}=\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}<D}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}}}\sum_{Id_{1},Jd_{2}<D}\alpha_{Id_{1}}\bar{\alpha}_{Jd_{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{IL=JM}\frac{G_{k}(LMd_{1}d_{2}/\penalty p)}{\sqrt{LM}}.

By employing the same change of variables as VanderKam
[
20
, p. 352]
, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}=\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}L\sum_{U,\,V<\frac{D}{L}}\tau(U)\tau(V)\,x_{UL}\bar{x}_{VL}\sum_{T|L}\frac{\mu(T)}{T}\sum_{A=1}^{\infty}\frac{G_{k}(A^{2}T^{2}UV/\penalty p)}{A},

where
x_{c}=\alpha_{c}/\penalty\sqrt{c}
and
\tau(u)
is the divisor function, i.e.,
\tau(u):=\displaystyle\sum_{d|u}1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

With the above notations, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{A=1}^{\infty}\frac{G_{k}(A^{2}/\penalty X)}{A}=\frac{\left((k-1)!\right)^{2}}{2}\log X+c_{0}+O\left(\frac{1}{X}\right),

where
c_{0}
is a fixed constant.

Proof.

From the integral representation of the function
G_{k}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{A=1}^{\infty}\frac{G_{k}(A^{2}/\penalty X)}{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{A=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(t)=\frac{3}{4}}\frac{\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}}\frac{X^{t}}{A^{2t+1}}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(t)=\frac{3}{4}}\frac{\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}}X^{t}\,\zeta(2t+1)\frac{dt}{t}.

Now by the contour shift, for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(t)=\frac{3}{4}}\frac{\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}}X^{t}\,\zeta(2t+1)\frac{dt}{t}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(t)=-k-\epsilon}\frac{\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}}X^{t}\zeta(2t+1)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Res}_{t=-k}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}}\frac{X^{t}\zeta(2t+1)}{t}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Res}_{t=0}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}}\frac{X^{t}\zeta(2t+1)}{t}\right).

Using the residue formula, we compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{t=0}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}}\frac{X^{t}\zeta(2t+1)}{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{t^{2}\Gamma(k+t)^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2t}}\frac{X^{t}\zeta(2t+1)}{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left((k-1)!\right)^{2}}{2}\log X+c_{0};

with a fixed
c_{0}
, and this completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\phi(L)
be the Euler function and
y_{L}=\displaystyle\sum_{UL<D}\tau(U)\,x_{UL}
.
For given
\delta>0
there exists a constant
c_{\delta}
such that

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}\gg\log p\sum_{L\geq 1}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad c_{\delta}D^{2}<p^{1-\delta}.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}=\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}L\sum_{U,\,V<\frac{D}{L}}\tau(U)\tau(V)\,x_{UL}\bar{x}_{VL}\sum_{T|L}\frac{\mu(T)}{T}\sum_{A=1}^{\infty}\frac{G_{k}(A^{2}T^{2}UV/\penalty p)}{A},

Then by using Lemma
4.1
and the computations from VanderKam
[
20
, pp. 353 – 354]
, we get the following:

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}\geq\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\left(\log\left(\frac{p^{\frac{((k-1)!)^{2}}{2}}}{D}\right)+c_{0}-c_{1}\right),

where
y_{L}=\displaystyle\sum_{UL<D}\tau(U)\,x_{UL}
.
Recall the function
\Lambda
as in VanderKam
[
20
, p. 353]
and
c_{1}
is a fixed quantity obtained by prime number theorem :

\sum\limits_{w<\frac{D}{L}}\frac{\Lambda(w)}{w}<\log\bigg(\frac{D}{L}\bigg)+c_{1}.

For
D^{2}<c_{\delta}p^{1-\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}\geq\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\left(\bigg(\frac{((k-1)!)^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\bigg)\log(p)+c_{0}-c_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\log(c_{\delta})\right).

We then have

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}\geq\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\bigg({\frac{((k-1)!)^{2}}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\bigg)\log p+\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\log(c_{\delta})+(c_{0}-c_{1})\bigg).

Choose
c_{\delta}
such that
-\frac{1}{2}\log(c_{\delta})+(c_{0}-c_{1})>0
.

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}\geq\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\bigg({\frac{((k-1)!)^{2}}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\bigg)\log p.

Since
c_{\delta}
is a fixed quantity, it is clear that the first term dominates the second term.
Since
C_{k,\delta}:=\frac{((k-1)!)^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}>1
, we get

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{main}}>C_{k,\delta}\log p\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}.

We deduce that
S_{\mathrm{main}}\gg\log p(\sum\limits_{L=1}^{\infty}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2})
;
where the implied constant depends on
k
and
\delta
.
∎

\displaystyle\times(d_{1}d_{2})^{-t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|c\\
c<p^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c^{2k+2\ell}}\left(\frac{p^{1+\epsilon}}{d_{1}d_{2}}\right)^{k+\ell-1-\mathrm{Re}(t)}\left(\frac{p^{1+\epsilon}}{d_{1}d_{2}}+c\right)c^{\epsilon}\left(1+|k+\ell-1-t|\right)^{2}dt.

\Gamma(k+t)^{2}\left(1+|k+\ell-1-t|\right)^{2}
times the term

\displaystyle p^{-k-\ell+\epsilon}D^{\epsilon}\left(\sum_{L<D}|y_{L}|L^{\ell+k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll p^{-k-\ell+\epsilon}D^{\epsilon}\left(\sum_{L<D}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{L<D}\frac{L^{2\ell+2k}}{\phi(L)}\right)

(5.8)

\displaystyle\ll(pD)^{\epsilon}\left(\frac{D^{2}}{p}\right)^{k+\ell}\left(\sum_{L<D}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\right).

For
D^{2}<c_{\delta}p^{1-\delta}
, the right hand side of (
5
) is bounded by
p^{\frac{3\epsilon}{2}}p^{-\delta(\ell+k+\frac{\epsilon}{2})}\left(\sum_{L<D}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}\right)
, and by taking the sum over
\ell
and integration over
\mathrm{Re}(t)=3/4
, we see that the term
S_{\mathrm{off}}^{c<p^{2}}
is bounded by
p^{\epsilon}\displaystyle\sum_{L}\phi(L)|y_{L}|^{2}
for any
\epsilon>0
.
∎

6.
Generalization of VanderKam’s theorem for higher weights

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
be a fixed positive integer. For any integer
D\geq 4
. For given
\delta>0
there exists a constant
c_{\delta}
such that the Hecke operators
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{D}
act linearly independently on the cycle
z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}
within the space of cuspidal symbols
\mathbb{S}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))\otimes\mathbb{R}
for all primes
D^{2}<c_{\delta}p^{1-\delta}
.

Proof.

The linear dependency of the Hecke operators
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{D}
is equivalent to saying there exists a non-trivial solution
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{D}
of

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(i)\lambda_{f}(j)\big|L^{ar}(f,k)\big|^{2}=0.

This means that the Hecke operators
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{D}
act linearly independently on
z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}
if and only if there does not exist any non-trivial solution of the equation (
6.1
). With our notations the we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}\omega_{f}\lambda_{f}(i)\lambda_{f}(j)\big|L^{ar}(f,k)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\alpha_{i}\bar{\alpha}_{j}a_{ij}

\displaystyle=Q(\alpha_{i},\bar{\alpha_{j}})

\displaystyle=S_{\mathrm{off}}+S_{\mathrm{main}},

where
a_{ij}:=\sum\limits_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)}^{h}\lambda_{f}(i)\lambda_{f}(j)\big|L^{ar}(f,k)\big|^{2}
with
\sum\limits^{h}
denoting the harmonic average.
We form the quadratic form
Q(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}x_{i}x_{j}a_{ij}
with
\underline{x}=(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq D}
and
\underline{y}=(y_{i})_{1\leq i\leq D}
.

Now, note that the off-diagonal term
S_{\mathrm{off}}
is not large enough to cancel the main diagonal term
S_{\mathrm{main}}
. This implies, for
c_{\delta}D^{2}<p^{1-\delta}
there is no non-trivial solutions
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{D}
of the equation (
6.1
). Hence, the Hecke operators
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{D}
are linearly independent on the cycle
z^{k-1}\otimes\{0,\infty\}
.
∎

7.
Main Theorem

We know that the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
acts on
\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
. For
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
, let
[f]
be the set of all orbits of
f
.
Let
K_{[f]}
be the Hecke field associated to an eigenform
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
. Let
\mathrm{Ann}_{[f]}
be the annihilator ideal of
f
in
\mathbb{T}
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{k-1}
be the set of orbits such that
L^{ar}(f,k)\neq 0
.
We start by showing the following lemma:

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
I_{e}:=\mathrm{Ann}(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}):=\{T\in\mathbb{T}\mid T(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e})=0\}
be the annihilator ideal of
z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\bigcap_{[f]\in\mathcal{E}_{k-1}}\mathrm{Ann}({[f]})=\displaystyle\mathrm{Ann}\left(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}\right).

Proof.

Let us consider an element
T
from the left-hand side. Then
for every
f
belonging to the set
\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
such that
L^{ar}(f,k)\neq 0
, we have the following:

(7.1)

\displaystyle\langle T(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}),f\rangle=\langle z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e},Tf\rangle=0.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
be such that
L^{ar}(f,k)=0
then from (
2.2
) we get

\displaystyle\langle z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e},f\rangle=0.

Therefore
\langle T(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}),f\rangle=0
because
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
. Hence, if
T
be an element in left hand side of (
7.1
) then

Tf=0
(since it is in the annihilator of
f
) and
L^{ar}(f,k)\neq 0
as
f\in\mathcal{E}_{k-1}
.
For all
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
, we have by (
7.1
), i.e.,
\langle T(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}),f\rangle=0
. We deduce that

T(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e})=0
. This implies that the Hecke operator
T\in\displaystyle\mathrm{Ann}(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e})
.

Now, for the other inclusion, let us consider an element
T\in\displaystyle\mathrm{Ann}(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e})
and
hence
T(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e})=0
.
Let
f\in\mathcal{E}_{k-1}
and hence
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2k}(p)
(Hecke basis) such that
L^{ar}(f,k)\neq 0
. If possible
Tf\neq 0
. Since it is a Hecke basis so
Tf=\lambda_{f}(T)f
. Since
T(f)\neq 0
, we have
\lambda_{f}(T_{r})\neq 0
. We then have

\displaystyle\langle z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e},Tf\rangle=\langle T(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}),f\rangle=0.

Now by (
2.2
) implies
\lambda_{f}(T)\langle z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e},f\rangle=\lambda_{f}(T)L^{ar}(f,k)=0
. This is a clear contradiction.
Therefore
T
is an element in the left hand side. This completes the proof.
∎

we prove the following lemma which the number field analogue of

Lemma 7.2

N

M_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N);\mathbb{Z})

M_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))

\mathbb{Z}

[

Let
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{T}(N)\subseteq\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N);\mathbb{Z})

be the Hecke algebra generated by the Hecke operators
T_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

5

Proposition 7.4
.

For any
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
S

\mathbb{T}_{S}:=\mathbb{T}\otimes S
. The pairing:
S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N);S)\times\mathbb{T}_{S}\rightarrow S

given by
(f,T_{l})\mapsto a_{1}(T_{l}f)
is a perfect pairing of

S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N);S)\simeq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{S}(\mathbb{T}_{S},S)
.

\mathbb{T}

7.2.
Proof of the main theorem

The following proposition is a generalization to weight
2k
from weight
2
, which is a theorem due to Kamienny (cf.
[
18
, Theorem 5]
):

Proposition 7.5
.

For all prime numbers
l\neq p
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

There exists weight
2k
cusp forms
\bar{f}_{1},\bar{f}_{2},\ldots,\bar{f}_{D}\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{F}_{l})
with
I_{e}\bar{f}_{i}=0
such that
(a_{1}(\bar{f}_{i}),a_{2}(\bar{f}_{i}),\ldots,a_{D}(\bar{f}_{i}))_{i\in\{1,\ldots,D\}}

are linearly independent.

(2)

The images of
T_{1},\ldots,T_{D}
in
\frac{\mathbb{T}}{(l,I_{e})}
are
\mathbb{F}_{l}
-linearly independent.

(3)

The quantities
T_{1}z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e},\ldots,T_{D}z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{l}
.

Proof.

We first prove
(1)
and
(2)
are equivalent.
There is an induced
perfect
pairing between
\mathbb{F}_{l}
vector spaces:

S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{F}_{l})[I_{e}]\times\frac{\mathbb{T}}{(l,I_{e})}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{l}.

We deduce that
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{l}}S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{F}_{l})[I_{e}]=D
if
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{l}}\frac{\mathbb{T}}{(l,I_{e})}=D
.
From the elementary linear algebra,
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{l}}S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{F}_{l})[I_{e}]=D
implies there exist
\mathbb{F}_{l}
linearly independent cusp forms
\{\bar{f}_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,D}
in the sense of Serre such that
T\bar{f}_{i}=0
for all
T\in I_{e}
. We deduce that
\bar{f}_{i}\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{F}_{l})
are
\mathbb{F}_{l}
linearly independent and
I_{e}\bar{f}_{i}=0
for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,D\}
. If we have a
relation

c_{1}\bar{f}_{1}+c_{2}\bar{f}_{2}+\cdots+c_{D}\bar{f}_{D}=0;

this implies all the
c_{i}
’s are zero. For each
\bar{f}_{i}
we have Fourier expansion of the form
f_{i}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(\bar{f}_{i})q^{n}
with
i=1,\ldots,D
. We have a system of equation of the form

A\underline{c}=0
with
\underline{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{D})
where

A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}(\bar{f}_{1})&a_{2}(\bar{f}_{2})&\cdots&a_{1}(\bar{f}_{D})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{D}(\bar{f}_{1})&a_{D}(\bar{f}_{2})&\cdots&a_{D}(\bar{f}_{D})\end{pmatrix}.

Now,
\underline{c}=0
implies vectors

(a_{1}(\bar{f}_{i}),a_{2}(\bar{f}_{i}),\ldots,a_{D}(\bar{f}_{i}))_{i=1,\ldots,D}
are linearly independent.

To verify the condition
(2)
and
(3)
are equivalent we observe that
\frac{\mathbb{T}}{I_{e}}\simeq\mathbb{T}(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e})
. Hence, we have

\frac{\mathbb{T}}{I_{e}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{l}\simeq\mathbb{T}(z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{l}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of
1.1

(b)
.

By Theorem
6.1
, we have
T_{1}z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e},\ldots,T_{D}z^{k-1}\otimes\mathbf{e}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}
for
D<c_{\delta}p^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}
.
We then have the rank of
\frac{\mathbb{T}}{I_{e}}
is
D
. We deduce there are
D

7.2
. The dimension of the
\mathbb{F}_{l}

.

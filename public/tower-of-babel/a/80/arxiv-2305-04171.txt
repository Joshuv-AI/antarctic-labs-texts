Regularity of the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function on compact Kähler manifolds
Ngoc Cuong Nguyen

Abstract
. We prove that the regularity of the extremal function of a compact subset of a compact Kähler manifold is a local property, and that the continuity and Hölder continuity are equivalent to classical notions of the local L L -regularity and the locally Hölder continuous property in pluripotential theory. As a consequence we give an effective characterization of the ( 𝒞 α , 𝒞 α ′ ) (\mathscr{C}^{\alpha},\mathscr{C}^{\alpha^{\prime}}) -regularity of compact sets, the notion introduced by Dinh, Ma and Nguyen. Using this criterion all compact fat subanalytic sets in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} are shown to be regular in this sense.



1.
Introduction

Background.

Let
\mathcal{L}
denote the Lelong class of plurisubharmonic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with logarithmic growth at infinity, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}=\left\{v\in PSH(\mathbb{C}^{n}):u(z)-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|z|^{2})\leq c_{u}\right\}.

The classical extremal function for a bounded subset
E
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is defined by

(1.1)

L_{E}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in\mathcal{L},\,v\leq 0\text{ on }E\}.

This function was introduced by Siciak
[
Si62
,
Si81
]
using the polynomials and characterized by Zaharjuta
[
Za76
]
via plurisubharmonic functions.
Since its introduction the extremal function has found numerous applications in pluripotential theory, approximation theory in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and other areas. It often turns out that geometrical properties of the compact subset
E
can be read from the regularity of its extremal function
L_{E}
. It is well-known that
E
is non-pluripolar if and only if its upper semicontinuous regularization
L_{E}^{*}
is bounded on
E
.

The continuity of the extremal function is a classical topic.
Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a compact subset and
a\in K
. We say that

(
1^{o}
)

K
is
L
-regular at
a
if
L_{K}
is continuous at
a
;

(
2^{o}
)

K
is
L
-regular
if
L_{K}
is continuous at every point of
K
.

Notice that in (
2^{o}
) if
L_{K}
is continuous at every point of
K
, then
L_{K}
is continuous in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by
[
Si81
]
and
[
Za76
]
.
Its local version in
[
Si81
]
will be important for us. The compact set

K
is said to be

(
3^{o}
)

locally
L
-regular at
a
if
L_{K\cap B(a,r)}
is continuous at
a
for every closed ball
B(a,r)
of radius
r>0
;

(
4^{o}
)

locally
L
-regular
if it is locally
L
-regular at every point of
K
.

This notion is stronger than the
L
-regularity as shown in
[
Sa81
]
and in
[
Ce82
]
. It closely relates to the weighed extremal functions. Given
a continuous function
\phi
on
K
, the function
L_{K,\phi}
is defined by

(1.2)

L_{K,\phi}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in\mathcal{L},v\leq\phi\text{ on }K\}.

Siciak proved in
[
Si81
, Propositions 2.3, 2.16]
that the continuity of
L_{K,\phi}
is equivalent to its continuity on
K
; and if
K
is locally
L
-regular, then
L_{K,\phi}
is continuous. Conversely, Dieu showed in
[
Di03
]
that if
L_{K,\psi}
is continuous for every continuous weight
\psi
, then
K
is locally
L
-regular. In Section
4.1
we collected many well-known examples of the locally
L
-regular sets from
[
Pl79
,
Pl84
,
Pl01
]
and
[
Si97
]
. In particular, a compact domain whose boundary is
C^{1}
-smooth is locally
L
-regular.
Furthermore, we refer to the reader to the textbook by Klimek
[
Kl91
, Chapter 5]
for the detailed discussion and results up to 1990, and the surveys of Levenberg
[
Le06
]
and Pleśniak
[
Pl03
]
for more recent results.

Next, the Hölder continuity of the extremal function has been studied extensively in relation to the Markov inequalities
[
BC14
]
,
[
BC17
]
,
[
BiE16
,
BiE19
]
and
[
Pl98
]
.
Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a compact subset and
a\in K
. We say that

(
5^{o}
)

K
has
\mu
-Hölder continuity property
(
\mu
-HCP for short)
at
a
if there exist constants
C>0
and
\mu>0
such that

L_{K}(z)\leq C\delta^{\mu},\quad|z-a|\leq\delta\leq 1;

(
6^{o}
)

K
has
\mu
-HCP if there exist constants
C>0
and
\mu>0
such that

L_{K}(z)\leq C\delta^{\mu},\quad{\rm dist}(z,K)\leq\delta\leq 1.

Notice in (
6^{o}
) that if
L_{K}
has
\mu
-HCP on
K
, then
L_{K}
has
\mu
-Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by
[
Si97
, Proposition 3.5]
.
It was observed in
[
Si97
, Remark 3.7]
that in (
5^{o}
) and (
6^{o}
) the Hölder exponent satisfies
0<\mu\leq 1
. Moreover, in these two items it is sufficient to show the inequality for
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
with some constant
0<\delta_{0}\leq 1
.
The typical compact sets having
\mu
-HCP are convex sets with non-void interior either in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\cdot 0
, or in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. A compact domain whose boundary is Lipschitz continuous has also
\mu
-HCP. These examples were contained in
[
Si85
]
. Next, Pawłucki and Pleśniak
[
PP86
]
showed that a very large class of uniformly polynomially cuspidal (UPC) sets have this property. The boundary of such sets may have cusp. More recently, Sadullaev and Zeriahi
[
SZ16
]
proved this property for a compact generic real submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Results.
Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.
Let
PSH(X,\omega)
denote the space of
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (
\omega
-psh) functions, i.e.,

PSH(X,\omega)=\{v\in L^{1}(X):\omega+dd^{c}v\geq 0\}.

Note that if
c_{2}>c_{1}>0
, then
v\in PSH(X,c_{1}\omega)
implies
v\in PSH(X,c_{2}\omega)
. If
\phi
is quasi-psh, then
dd^{c}\phi+A\omega\geq 0
for some
A>0
.
Our goal is to study the regularity (continuity and Hölder continuity) of
the extremal functions associated to compact sets on the global setting.
The Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function of a Borel subset
E
in
X
is defined by

(1.3)

V_{E}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in PSH(X,\omega):v\leq 0\quad\text{on }E\}.

This function was introduced and studied early by Guedj and Zeriahi
[
GZ17
, Chapter 9.4]
in analogue with the one in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For a real-valued continuous function
\phi
on
X
, the weighted extremal function is given by

(1.4)

V_{E,\phi}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in PSH(X,\omega),\;v_{|_{E}}\leq\phi\}.

(It is enough to assume
\phi
is defined on
E
, however we are mainly interested in a compact set
E
from which we can extend
\phi
as a continuous function on the whole manifold.)
Berman, Boucksom and Witt-Nystrom
[
BBW11
]
showed that for many applications the continuity of
V_{E,\phi}
is important, e.g., the Bernstein-Markov measure with respect to a plurisubharmonic weight
\phi
.
Recently, Sadullaev
[
Sa16
]
proved the equivalence between the global and local continuity of the extremal function for
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
with
\omega=\omega_{FS}
the Fubini-Study metric.
Our first result extends this equivalence on any compact Kähler manifold.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K\subset X
be a non-pluripolar compact subset and
a\in K
. Let
B(a,r)\subset X
denote a closed coordinate ball centered at
a
with small radius
r>0
.

(a)

V_{K}
is continuous at
a
if and only if
V_{K\cap B(a,r)}
is continuous at
a
.

(b)

Let
\phi\in C^{0}(X,\mathbb{R})
. If
V_{K}
is continuous, then
V_{K,\phi}
is continuous.

(c)

V_{K}
is continuous if and only if
K
is locally
L
-regular.

Note that the sufficient condition in (a) follows easily from the monotonicity. Unlike in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
setting above the necessary condition holds on compact manifolds. This item extends the one in
[
Sa16
]
whose proof relied on the facts that
\mathbb{P}^{n}
admits a large coordinate chart
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and on the relation between the extremal functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Here we improve his proof so that it works for a general compact Kähler manifold. The item (b) is a consequence of (a), where it is often the case that the modulus of continuity of the weighted extremal function is weaker than the unweighted one. The local
L
-regularity in (c) is understood in the sense of
(4^{o})
, because we may assume that the compact set is contained in a holomorphic coordinate chart, which is biholomorphic to a ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, thanks to the characterization in (a).

Next, we wish to study the Hölder regularity of the global extremal functions.
When
K=X
, the weighted extremal function (
1.4
) becomes the envelope. It has been well-understood. For example, it is proved by Tosatti
[
To18
]
that if
\phi
is smooth then its envelope has the optimal
C^{1,1}
-regularity (see also Berman
[
B19
]
). More generally, if
\phi
is
C^{0,\alpha}(X)
for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, then the same regularity of the envelope is proved in
[
LPT21
]
(see also
[
ChZ19
]
,
[
GLZ19
]
).
However, the problem becomes very different for a general compact subset, which is important for applications. This is our main focus. It follows from
[
DMN17
]
that the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function associated to a
C^{2}
-smooth compact domain is Lipschitz continuous. Later, this property is obtained for the extremal function associated to a smooth real generic submanifold in
[
Vu18
]
.

We say that a compact subset
K
has
a uniform density in capacity
if there exist constants
q>0
and
\varkappa>0
such that for every
a\in K
,

(1.5)

\inf_{0<r<1}\frac{cap(K\cap B(a,r))}{r^{q}}\geq\varkappa,

where
cap(\bullet)
is the Bedford-Taylor capacity on
X
. This property holds for all compact sets (Lemma
3.3
) that satisfy the local Hölder regularity version of
(5^{o})
and
(6^{o})
(Definition
3.2
).
Furthermore, it is a local property and it holds for most of natural sets as we will see in Sections
4.2
,
4.3
,
4.4
.
Our next result provides a general criterion to study the Hölder regularity.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K\subset X
be a non-pluripolar compact subset and
a\in K
. Let
B(a,r)\subset X
denote a closed coordinate ball centered at
a
with small radius
r>0
. Assume
\phi
is a Hölder continuous function on
X
.

(a)

V_{K}
is
\mu
-Hölder continuous at
a
if and only if
V_{K\cap B(a,r)}
is
\mu
-Hölder continuous at
a
.

(b)

Assume
K
has a uniform density in capacity of (
1.5
). If
V_{K}
is Hölder continuous, then
V_{K,\phi}
is Hölder continuous.

(c)

V_{K}
is Hölder continuous and
K
has a uniform density in capacity if and only if
K
has locally Hölder continuous property of order
q
.

The item (a) is a useful result. This combined with Demailly’s regularization theorem allows us to prove the general sufficient condition in (b). Notice also that as in Theorem
1.1
-(b) the Hölder exponent in (b) of the weighted extremal function is often smaller than the one of the unweighted extremal function.
The item (c) is a new and effective criterion (the locally Hölder continuous property will be given in Definition
3.2
). It can be applied to the previous works
[
DMN17
]
,
[
SZ16
]
and
[
Vu18
]
, where the Hölder continuity of the (weighted) extremal functions were proved directly. The new input here is a precise estimate on the Hölder norm/coefficient, which is the growth of sup-norm of
V_{E\cap B(a,r)}
like
r^{-q}
for
q>0
and
r>0
small in (c).

To state the applications of the characterization of the Hölder continuity, we recall the notion is introduced by Dinh, Ma and Nguyen
[
DMN17
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

A non-pluripolar compact subset
K\subset X
is said to be
(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha},\mathscr{C}^{\alpha^{\prime}})
-regular
, where
0<\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\leq 1
, if for every
\alpha
-Hölder continuous weight
\phi
, its weighted extremal function
V_{K,\phi}
is
\alpha^{\prime}
-Hölder continuous.

Obviously, if
K
is
(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha},\mathscr{C}^{\alpha^{\prime}})
-regular, then
V_{K}
is necessary Hölder continuous for the weight
\phi\equiv 0
. As shown in
[
DMN17
]
one can obtain the speed of convergence of Fekete’s measures associated to such a compact subset. There are more applications of such regular sets recently found in
[
MV22
]
.
Thanks to Theorem
1.2
-(b) and (c) we are able to prove the
(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha},\mathscr{C}^{\alpha^{\prime}})
-regularity for many new examples in Section
4
. Among them the following subclass is contained in the class of UPC sets. This was somehow conjectured by Zeriahi
[
DMN17
, page 562]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

A compact fat subanalytic subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha},\mathscr{C}^{\alpha^{\prime}})
-regular.

Here a compact subanalytic subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is fat if
E=\overline{{\rm int\,}E}
, and in general, these sets admit a cusp singularity. These subsets are fundamental objects in real algebraic geometry
[
BM88
]
. By invoking
[
DMN17
]
we obtain the speed of convergence of sequence of Fekete’s measures associated to a compact fat subanalytic subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(Theorem
5.2
).

2
we first provide basic properties of the extremal functions on a compact Kähler manifold. Then, we prove the characterizations on the continuity in Theorem

1.4
is a consequence of Theorem
1.2
-(c) and Corollary
4.10
. Section
5
contains some applications related to the speed of convergence for sequences of associated measures with Fekete points of fat subanalytic sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Lastly, Section 6 consists of an appendix which justifies the reduction to working on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
for the Hölder continuous results.

2.
Continuity of the extremal functions

2.1.
Basic properties of the global extremal functions

In this section we recall basic results related to the extremal functions and weighted extremal functions on a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
of dimension
n
. These results are the analogues of the classical results in
[
Si81
,
Si82
]
,
[
Kl91
]
on the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The detailed proofs are contained in
[
GZ17
]
.
The first one is
[
GZ17
, Theorem 9.17]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
E\subset X
be a Borel set. Then,

(a)

E
is pluripolar
\Leftrightarrow

\sup_{X}V_{E}^{*}\equiv+\infty

\Leftrightarrow

V_{E}^{*}\equiv+\infty.

(b)

If
E
is not pluripolar, then
V_{E}^{*}\in PSH(X,\omega)
. Moreover,
V_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
in the interior of
E
.

Next, we have
[
GZ17
, Proposition 9.19]
for the basic properties.

Proposition 2.2
.

(a)

If
E\subset F
, then
V_{E}\leq V_{F}
.

(b)

If
E
is an open subset, then
V_{E}=V^{*}_{E}
.

(c)

If
P\subset X
is pluripolar, then
V_{E\cup P}^{*}=V_{E}^{*}
.

(d)

Let
\{E_{j}\}
be an increasing sequence of sets in
X
and
E:=\cup E_{j}
, then

\lim_{j\to\infty}V_{E_{j}}^{*}=V_{E}^{*}.

(e)

Let
\{K_{j}\}
be a decreasing sequence of compact sets in
X
and
K:=\cap K_{j}
, then
\lim_{j\to\infty}V_{K_{j}}=V_{K}
. Furthermore,
\lim_{j\to\infty}V_{K_{j}}^{*}=V_{K}^{*}
a.e.

We will frequently need the following result for compact Kähler manifolds.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
K\subset X
is a compact subset, then
V_{K}
is lower semi-continuous.

Proof.

Let
v\in PSH(X,\omega)
and
v\leq 0
on
K
. By Demailly’s regularization theorem
[
De94
, Proposition 3.8]
(see also
[
BK07
]
) there exists a sequence of smooth function
v_{j}\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap C^{\infty}(X)
decreasing to
v
. Fix
\delta>0
. By Hartogs’ lemma we have for
j\geq j_{0}
,

v_{j}-\delta\leq 0\quad\text{on }K.

Consequently,
V_{K}
is supremum of a family of continuous functions, and the result follows.
∎

This gives a simple criterion to check the continuity of the extremal functions.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
K
is a compact subset and
V_{K}^{*}\equiv 0
on
K
, then
V_{K}
is continuous on
X
.

Remark 2.5
.

The continuity of
V_{K}
is a property of the set itself, i.e., it is independent of reference Kähler metric
\omega
. Indeed, assume
\omega^{\prime}
is another Kähler metric and
K
is regular with respect to
\omega
. There exists
A>0
such that
\omega^{\prime}\leq A\omega
. It follows that

V_{\omega^{\prime};K}^{*}\leq AV_{\omega;K}^{*}.

Hence, the continuity of
V_{\omega^{\prime};K}
follows from Corollary
2.4
.
Similarly, the independency holds also for the Hölder continuity of
V_{K}
as in Lemma
3.6
below, where the Hölder exponent is independent of the reference metric too. For this reason if there is no confusion then we only write
V_{K}
for the extremal function with respect to a fixed Kähler metric.

The ”zero-one” relative extremal function for a Borel set
E\subset X
is given by

(2.1)

h_{E}(z):=\sup\left\{v(z):v\in PSH(X,\omega),\;v\leq 1,\quad v_{|_{E}}\leq 0\right\}.

We know that
0\leq h_{E}^{*}\leq 1
and
h_{E}^{*}\in PSH(X,\omega)
. Furthermore,
E
is pluripolar if and only if
h_{E}^{*}\equiv 1
(see
[
GZ05
, page 620]
).

For non-pluripolar compact set
K
, let us denote
M_{K}:=\sup_{X}V_{K}^{*}<+\infty
. Then, it is easy to see that

(2.2)

V_{K}^{*}\leq M_{K}h_{K}^{*}.

We will need an estimate of
M_{K}
in term of capacity. Recall that for a Borel set
E\subset X
, the Bedford-Taylor capacity
[
Ko05
]
is defined by

(2.3)

cap(E)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}:v\in PSH(X,\omega),\,-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

(Here
cap=cap_{\omega}
, we omit the subscript
\omega
if it is already fixed and there is no confusion arises). Normalizing the metric
\omega
so that

\int_{X}\omega^{n}=1.

Thus, for every compact subset
K\subset X
,

cap(K)\leq 1.

By
[
GZ17
, Lemmas 12.2, 12.3]
we know that there exists a uniform constant
A
such that for every compact subset
K
,

(2.4)

\frac{1}{[cap(K)]^{\frac{1}{n}}}\leq\sup_{X}V_{K}\leq\frac{A}{cap(K)}.

Next, we consider the weighted extremal function

V_{K,\phi}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in PSH(X,\omega),\;v_{|_{K}}\leq\phi\},

where
\phi
is a real-valued continuous function on
X
.
If
E\subset F
be non-pluripolar compact subsets and
\phi\leq\psi
are continuous functions, then we have the following monotonicity

(2.5)

V_{F,\phi}\leq V_{E,\phi}\leq V_{E,\psi}.

Another useful property is

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
K\subset X
be a non-pluripolar compact subset and
\phi
a continuous function on
X
.

(a)

V_{K}^{*}+\inf_{K}\phi\leq V_{K,\phi}^{*}\leq V_{K}^{*}+\sup_{K}\phi.

(b)

Let
\theta=\omega+dd^{c}\phi
and

V_{\theta;K}=\sup\{v\in PSH(X,\theta):v_{|_{K}}\leq 0\}.

Then,

V_{K,\phi}=V_{\theta;K}+\phi,

(c)

V_{K,\phi}
is continuous if and only if
V_{K,\phi}^{*}\leq\phi
on
K
.

Proof.

The items (a) and (b) follow immediately from the definitions of
V_{K}
and
V_{K,\phi}
. To prove (c) let us write
V:=V_{K,\phi}
. Since
K
is compact, Demailly’s regularization theorem
[
De94
, Proposition 3.8]
(see also
[
BK07
]
) implies also that
V
is lower semi-continuous. The conclusion follows easily from the definition as
V=V^{*}
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Notice that there are more general relative extremal functions studied by Di Nezza and Lu
[
DiL15
]
which is the global version of the weighted extremal function introduced by Bedford
[
Be87
]
and used for the subextension problem by Cegrell, Kołodziej and Zeriahi
[
CKZ
]
. The relative weighted extremal functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
was also studied in Alan’ thesis
[
Al09
]
.

In the special case
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
with
\omega=\omega_{FS}
the Fubini-Study metric, there is a 1-1 correspondence between
PSH(\mathbb{P}^{n},\omega)
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
(see e.g.
[
GZ17
]
).
Let us denote
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|z|^{2})
the local potential of
\omega
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
, i.e.,

\omega=dd^{c}\rho\quad\text{on }\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Recall that for a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

L_{E,\rho}(z)=\sup\left\{f(z):f\in\mathcal{L},\;f_{|_{E}}\leq\rho\right\}.

Hence,

(2.6)

L_{E,\rho}(z)=V_{E}(z)+\rho(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Similar to Lemma
2.6
-(a) we have

(2.7)

L_{E}^{*}+\inf_{E}\rho\leq L_{E,\rho}^{*}\leq L_{E}^{*}+\sup_{E}\rho.

2.2.
Characterizations of the continuity

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(a)
Since
V_{F}^{*}\leq V^{*}_{E}
for Borel sets
E\subset F
, the sufficient condition is obvious. Next, we prove the necessary condition.
Assume
V_{K}^{*}(a)=0
, we need to show that
V_{K\cap B}^{*}(a)=0
, where
B:=B(a,r)
is a closed coordinate ball centered at
a
with small radius
r>0
. Indeed, let us
consider the positive relative extremal function defined in (
2.1
),

h_{K\cap B}(z)=\sup\left\{v(z):v\in PSH(X,\omega),\;v_{|_{K\cap B}}\leq 0,v\leq 1\right\}.

Then,
h^{*}_{K\cap B}\in PSH(X,\omega)
with
0\leq h_{K\cap B}^{*}\leq 1
.

Let
0\leq\chi\leq 1
be a smooth function on
X
such that
\chi(a)=0
and
\chi\equiv 1
on
X\setminus B(a,r)
. We have

(2.8)

\|\chi\|_{C^{1}(X)}\leq c_{1}/r,\quad\|\chi\|_{C^{2}(X)}\leq c_{2}/r^{2},

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are uniform constants independent of
a
and
r
, the norms
\|\cdot\|_{C^{1}(X)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{C^{2}(X)}
are the usual
C^{1}
and
C^{2}
norms on
X
.
Hence, there exists
0<\varepsilon\leq 1/2
, which is a small multiple of
r^{2}
, such that
-\varepsilon\chi
belongs to
PSH(X,\omega/2)
.
Given
u\in PSH(X,\omega)
satisfying
u\leq 1
and
u\leq 0
on
K\cap B
, we define

\varphi(z)=\varepsilon u(z)-\varepsilon\chi(z).

Then,
\varphi(z)\leq 0
on
K
and it is
\omega
-psh. It follows from definition of
V_{K}
that

\varphi(z)\leq V_{K}(z).

Taking supremum over all such
u
we get

(2.9)

\varepsilon h^{*}_{K\cap B}(z)-\varepsilon\chi(z)\leq V_{K}^{*}(z).

Since
V_{K}^{*}(a)=0=\chi(a)
, we have by (
2.2
) that
h_{K\cap B}^{*}(a)=0
. In particular,
K\cap B
is non-pluripolar. Denote
M:=\sup_{X}V_{K\cap B}^{*}
. It follows from (
2.4
) that

1\leq M\leq\frac{A}{cap(K\cap B)}<+\infty

for a uniform constant
A
. Using (
2.2
) again we get
V_{K\cap B}^{*}\leq Mh^{*}_{K\cap B}
. So,
V_{K\cap B}^{*}(a)=0
and the proof of (a) follows.

(b) Assume
V_{K}
is continuous and we write
V:=V_{K,\phi}
. Thanks to Lemma
2.6
-(c) we need to show that
V^{*}\leq\phi
on
K
. In fact, let
a\in K
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. We can choose
r>0
so small that
\phi(x)\leq\phi(a)+\varepsilon^{\prime}
in
B(a,r)
. By monotonicity (
2.5
),
we have

V\leq V_{K\cap B(a,r),\phi(a)+\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\phi(a)+\varepsilon^{\prime}+V_{K\cap B(a,r)}\quad\text{on }X.

Since
V_{K}
is continuous,
V_{K\cap B(a,r)}
is continuous at
a
by (a). Hence
V^{*}(a)\leq\phi(a)+\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0
, we get
V^{*}(a)\leq\phi(a)
. The proof is completed.

(c) It follows from (a) that we may assume that
K
is contained in a closed holomorphic chart in
X
which is biholomorphic to the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Since the continuity of the extremal function is a property of the set and it is invariant under biholomorphic maps, without loss of generality, we assume
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
equipped with the Fubini-Study metric.

Assume that
K
is locally
L
-regular at
a\in K
.
The argument in the proof of (b) applied for
L_{K}
shows that
the local
L
-regularity implies that
L_{K,\rho}
is continuous on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
which was first proved in
[
Si81
, Proposition 2.16]
. Hence,
V_{K}
is continuous by the identity (
2.6
) and Corollary
2.4
.

Conversely, assume
V_{K}
is continuous. Let
a\in K
and
B_{r}:=B(a,r)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a closed ball of radius
r>0
. It follows from (a) that
V_{K\cap B_{r}}
is continuous at
a
. The identity (
2.6
) implies that
L_{K\cap B_{r},\rho}
is continuous at
a
for every
r>0
. Hence, by definition of the weighted extremal function in (
1.2
),

L_{K\cap B_{r},\rho}\leq\rho\quad\text{on }E.

Fix a ball
B:=B(a,r_{0})
. Using (
2.7
) we have, for every
0<r<r_{0}
,

L_{K\cap B}^{*}(a)\leq L_{K\cap B_{r}}^{*}(a)\leq L_{K\cap B_{r},\rho}^{*}(a)-\inf_{K\cap B_{r}}\rho\leq\rho(a)-\inf_{K\cap B_{r}}\rho.

Since
\rho
is continuous on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, letting
r\to 0
we conclude that
L_{K\cap B}^{*}(a)=0
. This means that
L_{K\cap B}
is continuous at
a
. Since
a
is arbitrary the proof of (c) is completed.
∎

It would be also interesting to know the equivalence between global and local regularity of weighted extremal functions. Let
\phi\in C^{0}(X,\mathbb{R})
and let
B(a,r)\subset X
be a closed coordinate ball centered at
a
with small radius
r>0
. It is easy to see that
V_{K\cap B(a,r),\phi}
is continuous at
a
, then
V_{K,\phi}
is continuous at
a
.

Question 2.8
.

Assume
V_{K,\phi}
is continuous at
a
. Is
V_{K\cap B(a,r),\phi}
is continuous at
a
?

If
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
with the Fubini-Study metric
\omega
, then for a bounded Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

L_{E,\phi+\rho}(z)=V_{E,\phi}(z)+\rho(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Sadullaev obtained the positive answer to Question
2.8
in
[
Sa16
, Theorem 2.4]
provided that
\psi:=\phi+\rho
is a
C^{2}
-smooth strictly psh function in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. He also asked in
[
Sa16
, Problem 2.8]
whether it is enough to assume
\psi
is strictly psh in a neighborhood of
K
. We have a positive answer for his question in the case
\psi=\phi+\rho
, where
\phi
is either
C^{2}
-smooth or continuous quasi-psh on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. This is also a partial answer for the above question.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\phi\in C^{0}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Let
K\subset X
be compact and
a\in K
. Assume
\phi
is quasi-psh on
X
, i.e.,

\theta=dd^{c}\phi+A\omega\geq\omega

on
X
in the weak sense for some constant
A>0
. Then,
V_{K,\phi}
is continuous at
a
if and only if
V_{K\cap B(a,r),\phi}
is continuous at
a
.

Proof.

Using the relation between the weighted and unweighted extremal functions in Lemma
2.6
, for every Borel set
E
,

V_{E,\phi}=V_{\theta;E}+\phi.

Hence,
V_{E,\phi}^{*}=V_{\theta;E}^{*}+\phi
. Notice that
\theta
may not be smooth, but it is strictly positive whose potential is continuous. Consequently, the Bedford-Taylor capacity
cap_{\omega}
and
cap_{\theta}
(see (
2.3
) above) are equivalent to each other and the inequality (
2.2
) holds true. Furthermore, Lemma
2.3
, Corollary
2.4
are valid for
PSH(X,\theta)
. Hence, the argument in the proof in Theorem
1.1
-(a) still holds in
PSH(X,\theta)
. It shows that
V_{\theta;K}
is continuous at
a
if and only if
V_{\theta;K\cap B(a,r)}
is continuous at
a
. This finished the proof.
∎

As a consequence we have the converse direction of Theorem
1.1
-(b) for special weights.

Corollary 2.10
.

Assume
\phi
is continuous quasi-psh on
X
. If
V_{K,\phi}
is continuous, then
V_{K}
is continuous.

Proof.

By assumption there exists a constant
A>0
such that
\theta=A\omega+dd^{c}\phi\geq\omega
. Then,

V_{K,\phi}=V_{\theta;K}+\phi.

It follows that the continuity of
V_{K,\phi}
is equivalent to the one of
V_{\theta;K}
. By Lemma
2.9
,
V_{\theta;K\cap B(a,r)}
is continuous at
a\in K
for every closed coordinate ball
B(a,r)
. Hence, using the proof of Theorem
1.1
-(b), we get that
V_{\theta;K,-\phi}
is continuous. Moreover, using one more time the relation

V_{\theta;K,-\phi}=V_{A\omega;K}+\phi,

we get the continuity of
V_{A\omega;K}
and that of
V_{K}
.
∎

3.
Hölder continuity of the extremal functions

3.1.
Local uniform density in capacity

Let
K\subset X
be a compact subset in a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
of dimension
n
.
First, we show that this property is equivalent to having the control of sup-norm of the extremal function on coordinate balls.
Assume that
K
has a uniform density in capacity (
1.5
), i.e., there exist constants
q>0,\kappa>0
such that for every
a\in K
,

\inf_{0<r<1}\frac{cap(K\cap B(a,r))}{r^{q}}\geq\varkappa.

By the second inequality in (
2.4
) for the compact set
K\cap B(a,r)

\sup_{X}V_{K\cap B(a,r)}\leq\frac{A}{\varkappa\,r^{q}},\quad 0<r<1.

Conversely, if we have the control
\sup_{X}V_{K\cap B(a,r)}\leq A/r^{q}
for
0<r<1
, where
A,q>0
are uniform constants, then
K
has uniform density in capacity, i.e.,

\frac{cap(K\cap B(a,r))}{r^{q/n}}\geq\frac{1}{A^{n}},\quad 0<r<1.

Secondly, by definition the uniform density with capacity is a local property. We will see that it can be verified by using the Bedford-Taylor capacity
[
BT82
]
in a local coordinate. Without loss of generality we may assume that
K
is contained in a coordinate unit ball
\Omega
. Then, by considering its image in that chart,
we may assume that

K\subset B(0,1/2)\subset\Omega:=B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}.

By equivalence between the global Bedford-Taylor capacity and the local one
[
Ko05
, Eq. (6.2)]
,
K
has a uniform density in capacity if and only if

(3.1)

\frac{cap^{\prime}(K\cap B(a,r),\Omega)}{r^{q}}\geq\varkappa,\quad 0<r<1,

where for a Borel subset
E\subset\Omega
,

cap^{\prime}(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(dd^{c}u)^{n}:u\in PSH(\Omega),-1\leq u\leq 0\right\}.

Then, by applying the comparison between two capacities
[
AT84
]
(see also
[
Ko05
, Theorem 2.7]
), which is the local version of (
2.4
), the condition (
3.1
) is equivalent to the existence of uniform constant
A,q^{\prime}>0
such that for every
a\in K
and
0<r<1
,

\sup_{\Omega}L_{K\cap B(a,r)}\leq\frac{A}{r^{q^{\prime}}},

where
L_{K\cap B(a,r)}
is defined as in (
1.1
).
More precisely, if
\sup_{\Omega}L_{K\cap B(a,r)}\leq A/r^{q}
, then

(3.2)

\frac{cap^{\prime}(K\cap B(a,r),\Omega)}{r^{nq}}\geq\frac{1}{A^{n}}.

Remark 3.1
.

To verify the uniform density in capacity in (
1.5
) or (
3.1
) it is enough to have a uniform
0<r_{0}\leq 1
satisfying

\inf_{0<r<r_{0}}\frac{cap(K\cap B(a,r))}{r^{q}}\geq\varkappa.

Then, by monotonicity of the capacity we get the whole range
0<r<1
.

As in the continuous case it will be shown in Theorem
1.2
-(a) (see the appendix for the detail) that it is no loss of generality to consider compact subsets
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
for the Hölder continuous case. In this setting, the uniform density in capacity can be seen from the Hölder continuity of
L_{K}
. Recall that the Lelong class in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is given by

\mathcal{L}=\left\{f\in PSH(\mathbb{C}^{n}):f(z)-\rho(z)<c_{f}\right\},

where
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|z|^{2})
. For a non-pluripolar compact subset
E
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

L_{E}(z):=\sup\left\{f(z):f\in\mathcal{L},\;f_{|_{E}}\leq 0\right\}.

The modulus of continuity of
L_{E}
at
a\in E
is given by

\varpi_{E}^{\prime}(a,\delta)=\sup_{|z-a|\leq\delta}L_{E}(z)

for
0<\delta\leq 1
. Then,
L_{E}
is continuous at
a
if and only if

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\varpi^{\prime}_{E}(a,\delta)=0
.
Put

\varpi_{E}^{\prime}(\delta)=\sup\{\varpi_{E}^{\prime}(a,\delta):a\in E\}

which is the modulus of continuity of
L_{E}
over
E
.
It is a well-known fact due to Błocki
[
Si97
, Proposition 3.5]
that the modulus of continuity of
L_{E}
on
E
controls the modulus of continuity of
L_{E}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Namely,

(3.3)

|L_{E}(z)-L_{E}(w)|\leq\varpi^{\prime}_{E}(|z-w|),\quad z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\;|z-w|\leq 1.

Inspired from the definition of local
L
-regularity in
[
Si81
]
we introduce

Definition 3.2
.

Let
q\geq 0
be an integer and
0<\mu\leq 1
. Let
B(a,r)
be a closed ball with center at
a
, of radius
r>0
. We say that a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
has

(a)

locally
\mu
-Hölder continuity property (local
\mu
-HCP for short) of order
q
at
a\in K
if there exist constants
C>0
and
0<r_{0}\leq 1
such that

\varpi_{K\cap B(a,r)}^{\prime}(a,\delta)\leq\frac{C\delta^{\mu}}{r^{q}},\quad 0<\delta\leq 1,\,0<r<r_{0};

(b)

local
\mu
-HCP of order
q
if it has local
\mu
-HCP of order
q
at every point
a\in K
for the constants
C,r_{0}
being independent of
a
.

Here it is important to know both the Hölder exponent and the Hölder coefficient.
A weaker property is the HCP, introduced in
(5^{o})
and
(6^{o})
, where it only requires the Hölder continuity of
L_{K}
.
The definition implies an important property

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a non-pluripolar compact subset. If
K
has local
\mu
-HCP
of order
q
, then it has a uniform density in capacity in the sense of (
3.1
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
K\subset\Omega:=B(0,1)
.
By the assumption there exist uniform constants
q>0
and
C,r_{0}>0
such that for every
a\in K
,

\varpi_{K\cap B(a,r)}^{\prime}(a,\delta)\leq\frac{C\delta^{\mu}}{r^{q}},\quad 0<\delta\leq 1,\,0<r<r_{0}.

Let us fix
\delta=1
. It follows that, for
0<r<r_{0}
,

\sup_{\Omega}L_{K\cap B(a,r)}(z)\leq C/r^{q}.

It follows from the inequality (
3.2
) that

\frac{cap^{\prime}(K\cap B(a,r),\Omega)}{r^{nq}}\geq\frac{1}{C}.

This finished the proof.
∎

Noting that these local
\mu
-HCP properties will be used in Section
4
.

3.2.
Demailly’s approximation theorem.

This is the main technical tool for proving the characterizations of the Hölder continuity. In the proof of the continuous case (Lemma
2.3
) an elementary approximation sequence in
[
BK07
]
was enough. However, in the Hölder continuous case we need to know precisely the negative part of complex Hessian matrices that is lost in the regularization sequence. To this end we will need the approximation sequence due to Demailly
[
De94
]
.

Let
u
be a bounded
\omega
-psh function on
X
.
Considering
\Psi_{\delta}u
the regularization of the
\omega
-psh function
u
defined by

(3.4)

\Psi_{\delta}u(z)=\frac{1}{\delta^{2n}}\int_{\zeta\in T_{z}X}u({\exp}_{z}(\zeta))\chi\Big(\frac{|\zeta|^{2}_{\omega}}{\delta^{2}}\Big)\,d\lambda(\zeta),\ \delta>0;

where
\zeta\mapsto{\exp}_{z}(\zeta):T_{z}X\to X
is the Riemannian exponential map,
d\lambda
denote the Lebesgue measure on the Hermitian space
(T_{z}X,\omega(z))
and the smoothing kernel

\chi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is given by

\chi(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\eta}{(1-t)^{2}}\exp(\frac{1}{t-1})&\ {\rm if}\ 0\leq t\leq 1,\\
0&\ {\rm if}\ t>1\end{cases}

with a suitable constant
\eta
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\chi(\|z\|^{2})\,dV_{2n}(z)=1

(
dV_{2n}
being the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
).
By the explanation in
[
DDGHKZ
, page 624]
the proof in
[
KN19
, Lemma 4.1]
holds for the exponential function
\exp_{z}
in the place of its holomorphic part
{\exp}h_{z}
. The proof itself was originated from
[
De94
, Proposition 3.8]
and
[
BD12
, Lemma 1.12]
. We restate the result here.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
.
Define the Kiselman-Legendre transform with level
b>0
by

(3.5)

U_{\delta,b}=\inf_{t\in[0,\delta]}(\Psi_{t}u+c_{1}t^{2}+c_{1}t-b\log\frac{t}{\delta}),

Then, there exist positive constants
c_{0},c_{1}
depending only on
X,\omega
such that

(i)

\Psi_{t}u+c_{1}t^{2}
is increasing in
t
,

(ii)

\omega+dd^{c}U_{\delta,b}\geq-(c_{0}b+2c_{1}\delta)\,\omega.

Notice that this function is different by a constant
c_{1}\delta^{2}+c_{1}\delta
compared with the one considered there, which was was negative. This term is of order
O(\delta)
that will not affect to the proof of the Hölder continuity. The properties
(i)
and
(ii)
tell us that the convolution-like
\Psi_{\delta}u
produces a sequence of quasi-psh functions, or more precisely, they are
[1+O(\delta)]\omega
-psh. Furthermore, they are comparable in the uniform norm with the usual convolution.
By
[
De94
, Remark 4.6]

(3.6)

\Psi_{\delta}u=u*\chi_{\delta}+O(\delta^{2})

for
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
for a small
\delta_{0}>0
in the normal coordinate system center at
z
, where
\{\chi_{\delta}\}_{\delta>0}
is the standard radial smooth kernel family. In this coordinate system
(U,z)
with
z:U\to\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
a biholomorphic, we also consider
the average value over an Euclidean
\delta
-ball of
u

(3.7)

\check{u}_{\delta}(z)=\frac{1}{v_{2n}\delta^{n}}\int_{B_{\delta}(z)}u(x)dV_{2n}(x),

where
B_{\delta}(z)=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z-x|<\delta\}
. Then, it follows from
[
KN23
, Eq. (5.11), (5.12)]
that there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that in
\Omega_{\delta}=\{z\in\Omega:{\rm dist}(z,\partial\Omega)>\delta\}

(3.8)

\displaystyle u*\chi_{\delta}-u

\displaystyle\leq C(\check{u}_{\delta}-u),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\check{u}_{\delta/2}-u

\displaystyle\leq C(u*\chi_{\delta}-u).

Combining estimates (
3.6
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) and the line of argument given in
[
GKZ08
, Lemma 4.2]
(see also
[
Z21
, Theorem 3.2]
) we arrive at

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
u\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Assume there exist uniform constants
C,\delta_{0}>0
and
0<\mu\leq 1
such that

\Psi_{\delta}u-u\leq C\delta^{\mu}\quad\text{on }X,\quad 0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}.

Then
u
is
\mu
-Hölder continuous on
X
.

3.3.
Characterizations of Hölder continuity

First, we will show that the modulus of continuity of
V_{K}
on
X
is equivalent to the one on
K
only. This is a generalization of a well-known result of Błocki
[
Si97
, Proposition 3.5]
. For simplicity we only prove it for the Hölder continuity case.

Let
K\subset X
be a compact subset and
a\in K
. For
0<\delta\leq 1
the modulus of continuity of
V_{K}
at
a
is given by

\varpi_{K}(a,\delta):=\sup_{|z-a|\leq\delta}V_{K}(z).

Then the modulus of continuity of
V_{K}
on
K
is given by

(3.10)

\varpi_{K}(\delta)=\sup\{\varpi_{K}(a,\delta):a\in K\}.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
K\subset X
be a non-pluripolar compact subset and
0<\mu\leq 1
. Then,

V_{K}
is
\mu
-Hölder continuous on
X
if and only if it is
\mu
-Hölder continuous on
K
, i.e., there exists
0<\delta_{0}\leq 1
such that for every
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,

V_{K}(z)\leq C\delta^{\mu},\quad{\rm dist}(z,K)\leq\delta.

Proof.

The necessary condition is obvious. To prove the sufficient condition, we use the regularization theorem of Demailly (Lemma
3.4
). Notice from (
3.10
) that the Hölder continuity of
V_{K}
on
K
is equivalent to

\varpi_{K}(\delta)\leq C\delta^{\mu}

for every
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, where
C
is a uniform constant and
\delta_{0}>0
is a fixed small constant.
In particular,
V_{K}^{*}\equiv 0
on
K
. Therefore,
V:=V_{K}
is continuous (Corollary
2.4
). Consider the regularization
\Psi_{t}V
of
\omega
-psh function
V
as in (
3.4
) and

V_{\delta,b}(z)=\inf_{[0,\delta]}\left(\Psi_{t}V(z)+c_{1}t^{2}+c_{1}t-b\log\frac{t}{\delta}\right).

From Lemma
3.4
we know that
\Psi_{t}V+c_{1}t^{2}
is increasing in
t
and

\omega+dd^{c}V_{\delta,b}\geq-(c_{0}b+2c_{1}\delta)\omega.

Here
c_{0},c_{1}
are uniform constants depending only on
X
and
\omega
.
Consider
b=A\delta^{\mu}
for
A>0
so that
c_{0}b+2c_{1}\delta=\delta^{\mu}
. Then

(3.11)

V_{\delta}=\frac{V_{\delta,b}}{1+\delta^{\mu}}\in PSH(X,\omega).

Moreover, for
a\in K
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle(1+\delta^{\mu})V_{\delta}(a)

\displaystyle\leq\Psi_{\delta}V(z)+c_{1}\delta^{2}+c_{1}\delta

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|z-a|\leq\delta}V(z)+c_{1}\delta+c_{1}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varpi_{K}(\delta)+c_{1}\delta+c_{1}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\delta^{\mu},

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
V
is
\mu
-Hölder continuous on
K
and
c_{2}
is a uniform constant.
Moreover,
V\geq 0
on
X
, so we have
V_{\delta}(z)\geq 0
. Therefore,

V_{\delta}(a)\leq c_{2}\delta^{\mu}\quad\text{for }a\in K.

This combined with the
\omega
-psh property in (
3.11
) and the definition of
V
gives

(3.13)

V_{\delta}(z)\leq V(z)+c_{2}\delta^{\mu}\quad\text{for }z\in X.

At this point we can conclude the Hölder continuity of
V
on

as in the argument in

Let us fix a point

is realized for some

Combining this with

Since

\displaystyle=V_{\delta,b}(z)-V(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{\mu}}{1-\delta^{\mu}}V_{\delta}+(V_{\delta}-V),

where the last equality follows from (

\Psi_{\delta}V(z)-V(z)\leq C\delta^{\mu},\quad 0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}

Corollary 3.9

Let

(a)

Proof of Theorem

-(c).

For the sufficient condition, let us assume

Now, we can choose

4.
Regularity of compact sets

Thanks to the local characterizations in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
for studying the regularity of the extremal functions we may assume that the compact set is contained in a holomorphic coordinate unit ball. Without loss of generality, we may restrict ourself to compact subsets in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}=:X
. We provide a large number of examples of compact sets possessing these properties.

4.1.
Locally
L
-regular sets

In view of Theorem
1.1
-(c) and the applications in
[
BBW11
]
and
[
MV22
]
we make an effort to collect in this section many well-known examples of locally
L
-regular sets. This is a classical topic but the results are scattered in many different places.

Example 4.1

(accessibility criterion)
.

This criterion is due to Pleśinak
[
Pl79
]
. It provides the following typical example.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open bounded subset with
C^{1}
-boundary. Then,
\bar{\Omega}
is locally
L
-regular (see also
[
Kl91
, Corollary 5.3.13]
and
[
Di03
]
). This criterion has been generalized in
[
Pl84
]
for subanalytic subset in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and later in
[
Pl01
]
for more general setting.

Example 4.2

(Siciak)
.

Let us denote
\mathbb{K}
for either
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
. Let
B(x,r)
be a closed ball in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
with center at
x
and radius
r>0
. Let
E\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
be a compact subset.

(a)

Cusps:
let
h:[0,1]\to\mathbb{K}^{n}
and
r:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a continuous function such that
r(0)=0
and
r(t)>0
,
0<t\leq 1
. Let
a=h(0)
.
A cusp with vertex
a
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
given by

C(h,r):=\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq 1}B(h(t),r(t)).

We say that
E
has a cusp
C(h,r)
at
a=h(0)\in E
if
C(h,r)\subset E
.
It is proved
[
Si97
, Proposition 7.6]
that if
E
has a cusp
C(h,r)
at
a\in E
such that
r(t)=Mt^{m}
and
|h(t)-h(0)|\leq At^{q}
,
0\leq t\leq 1
, where
M,m,A
and
q
are positive uniform constants, then
E
is locally
L
-regular at
a
.

(b)

Corkscrew:

E
is said to have a corkscrew of order
s>0
at
a\in E
if there exists
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for every
0<r<r_{0}
we can find
a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}
for which
B(a^{\prime},r^{s})\subset B(a,r)\cap E
. Let
C(h,r)
be a cusp at
a=h(0)
. By the triangle inequality

|x-h(0)|\leq|x-h(t)|+|h(t)-h(0)|,

it follows that if
r(t)=Mt^{m}
and
|h(t)-h(0)|\leq At^{q}
, then this cusp has a corkscrew of order
s=m/\min\{m,q\}\geq 1
.
The conclusion is that if
E
has a corkscrew of order
s>0
at
a\in E
, then it is locally
L
-regular at
a

[
Si97
, Proposition 7.10]
.

.

There is still missing a good characterization of locally

4.2.
Local
\mu
-HCP sets

Here we require on the local HCP with a precise estimate of the Hölder coefficient.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
be a compact subset (
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
) and
a\in E
. Following Siciak
[
Si85
]
we consider the following

Definition 4.4

(Condition
(P)
)
.

For each point
a=(a_{1},...,a_{n})\in E
there exist compact connected subsets
\ell_{1},...,\ell_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}
and an affine non-singular mapping
h:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfying

(i)

a\in h(\ell_{1}\times\cdots\times\ell_{n})\subset E
;

(ii)

\|\ell_{j}\|\geq d>0
,
j=1,...,n
;

(iii)

\|Dh\|\geq m>0
,

where
\|\ell_{j}\|
is the diameter of
\ell_{j}
, the constants
d
and
m
do not depend on
a
.

Note that
Dh
denotes the Fréchet derivative of
h
which is a linear map of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and does not depend on the point
a
. Hence, its norm
\|Dh\|
does not depend on the point.
Roughly speaking the condition (
P
) for
E
means at each point
a\in E
there is an
affine cube
of uniform size with vertex at
a
and it is contained in
E
.

A basic result
[
Si85
, Proposition 5.1]
says that for compact subset
E\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfying the condition (P),

(4.1)

L_{E}(z)\leq\frac{4\sqrt{1+\|E\|}}{md}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}

holds for every
{\rm dist}(z,E)\leq\delta
and
0<\delta\leq 1
, where
\|E\|
is the diameter of
E
.

This precise estimate of both the Hölder coefficient and the Hölder exponent allows us to study the local
\mu
-HCP of many classes of compact sets in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
. Let us describe them again emphasizing on the locality.

Example 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
be a bounded domain. Let
E\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
be a compact set.

(a)

Lipschitz domain
: Assume
\Omega
has Lipschitz boundary and
a^{\prime}\in\overline{\Omega}
. Then for every
r>0
, the compact set
\overline{\Omega}\cap B(a^{\prime},r)
satisfies the condition (
P
) at each point.

(b)

Geometrical condition
: Assume there exists
r>0
such that for every
a\in E
, there is a point
a^{\prime}\in E
for which the convex hull of the set
\{a\}\cup B(a^{\prime},r)
is contained in
E
. Then,
E
has local
\frac{1}{2}
-HCP of order
q=n
.

(c)

Uniform interior sphere condition
: there exists
r>0
such that for each
a\in\partial\Omega
there is
B(a^{\prime},r)\subset\Omega
and
B(a^{\prime},r)\cap(\mathbb{K}^{n}\setminus\Omega)=\{a\}
. Then,
\overline{\Omega}
has local
\mu
-HCP with the exponent
\mu=1
and of order
q=1
if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
; or with exponent
\mu=1/2
and of order
q=2
if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
.

Let us give the explanation of the above examples.

(a) For the bounded domains with Lipschitz boundary it satisfies the so called Property (
{\bf H_{2}}
) (see
[
BG71
, page 166-167]
) which is a local property at a point. Namely there exists a non-empty parallelepiped
\pi_{0}
such that each point
a\in\partial\Omega
is the vertex of a parallelepiped
\pi_{a}
congruent with
\pi_{0}
(with respect to orthogonal transformation and translation) satisfying
\pi_{a}\subset\overline{\Omega}
. This property clearly implies the condition (P) at
a\in\partial\Omega
for
\overline{\Omega}\cap B(a^{\prime},r)
. Together with the estimate (
4.1
) we conclude the local
\frac{1}{2}
-HCP of order
q=1
of such subsets.

(b) Next, by
[
Si85
, Propostion 5.3]
the geometric condition implies the condition (
P
) with
\ell_{i}=[0,1]\subset\mathbb{C}
,
i=1,...,n
, and

(4.2)

m=\left(\frac{r}{n^{3}}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{\|E\|^{n-1}}.

(c) Finally, the uniform interior sphere condition implies the geometric condition. However, we will give a proof of the improvement of the exponent and order in Lemma
4.7
below.

One important case of the geometrical condition is that
it is satisfied for any convex compact set with non-void interior in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
. Hence, by
(
4.1
) and (
4.2
) (see also
[
Si85
, Remark 5.4]
) we have

Corollary 4.6

(Siciak)
.

A convex compact subset in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
satisfying the geometrical condition has local
\mu
-HCP
with the (optimal) exponent
\mu=1/2
and of order
q=n
.

Let us give the proof of the statement in Example
4.5
-(c).
Notice that the uniform interior sphere condition is satisfied for all smooth bounded domains with
C^{1,1}
-boundary.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
be a bounded domain satisfying the uniform interior sphere condition.

(a)

If
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, then
\overline{\Omega}
has local
\mu
-HCP
with the optimal Hölder exponent
\mu=1
and of order 1.

(b)

If
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\cdot 0\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

has local
\mu
-HCP
with the optimal exponent
\mu=1/2
and of order

Proof.

(a) Let

where

. By decreasing

we may assume
r/2\leq r_{0}\leq r
and by dilating this ball we may assume that
B(a^{\prime},r_{0})\subset\Omega\cap B(a,r)
. Hence,

L_{\overline{\Omega}\cap B(a,r)}(z)\leq L_{B(a^{\prime},r_{0})}(z)=\max\{\log(|z-a^{\prime}|/r_{0}),0\},

where the second identity used the explicit formula of the extremal function (see, e.g.
[
Kl91
, Example 5.1.1]
).
For
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
{\rm dist}(w,a)\leq\delta
with small
\delta
, we have

|w-a^{\prime}|\leq|w-a|+|a-a^{\prime}|\leq\delta+r_{0}.

It follows that

L_{\overline{\Omega}\cap B(a,r)}(w)\leq\log(1+\delta/r_{0})\leq\delta/r_{0}\leq 2\delta/r.

This means that
L_{\overline{\Omega}\cap B(a,r)}
is Lipschitz continuous at
a
. Furthermore, the Lipschitz norm at that point is independent of the point. Thus,
\overline{\Omega}
has local
\mu
-HCP with the exponent
\mu=1
and of order
q=1
.

(b) Assume now
\overline{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\cdot 0\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The proof goes along the same lines as above. Noticing that if
a\in\overline{\Omega}
, then
B(a,r)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\cdot 0)
is the real ball
\widetilde{B}(a,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we have an explicit formula (see e.g.
[
Kl91
, Theorem 5.4.6]
) for such a ball via
L_{\widetilde{B}(a,r)}(z)=L_{\widetilde{B}(0,1)}(f(z))
, where

L_{\widetilde{B}(0,1)}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left({\bf h}\left(|z|^{2}+|\left<z,\bar{z}\right>-1|\right)\right),\quad f(z)=(z-a)/r,

and
{\bf h}(x)=x+(x^{2}-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}
for
x\geq 1
.
∎

Notice that there are other kind of examples arising from Example
4.2
for cusp and corksrcew sets, because it is possible to derive precise estimate on the Hölder coefficient there. We refer the reader to
[
Si97
, Proposition 6.5]
for more detail.

4.3.
Uniformly polynomial cuspidal sets

In this section we follow the method of Pawłucki and Pleśniak
[
PP86
, Theorem 4.1]
to study the uniformly polynomial cuspidal sets. The improvement is a precise estimate on the Hölder coefficient. These sets contain, for example, all bounded convex sets with non-void interior in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
(here
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
) and all bounded domains in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary (see Example
4.5
and also
[
PP86
, page 469]
).

Now we are focusing on the compact sets with cusps in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
considered as a natural subset in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
A compact subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
uniformly polynomial cuspidal
(UPC for short) if there exist positive constants
M
,
m
and a positive integer
d
such that for each
x\in E
, one may choose a polynomial map

h_{x}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\deg h_{x}\leq d

satisfying

\displaystyle h_{x}(0)=x\quad\text{and}\quad h_{x}([0,1])\subset E;

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle dist}(h_{x}(t),\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E)\geq Mt^{m}\quad\text{for all }x\in E,\text{ and }t\in[0,1],

An important property of the UPC sets is that if
a\in E
, then

(4.3)

E_{a}:=\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq 1}D(h_{a}(t),Mt^{m})\subset E,

where

D(p,r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x_{1}-p_{1}|\leq r,...,|x_{n}-p_{n}|\leq r\}
denotes the closed cube.
Notice that
E_{a}
is also a closed subset.

Remark 4.8
.

(a)

Without loss of generality we may assume that the exponent
m
is a positive integer. Otherwise we will choose the smallest positive integer larger than
m
instead.

(b)

It is not clear from the definition that the norm of coefficients

\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}||h_{x}^{(\ell)}(0)\|
of
h_{x}
is uniformly bounded on
E
. Fortunately, in many interesting examples of UPC sets this assumption is satisfied.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact
UPC
subset such that
\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}\|h_{x}^{(\ell)}(0)\|
is uniformly bounded on
E
. Then,
E
has local
\mu
-HCP
of some order
q
.

It is worth to emphasize that by
[
PP86
, Corollary 6.6, Remark 6.5]
, we have
all compact fat subanalytic subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the additional assumption. Thus, we obtain

Corollary 4.10
.

A compact fat subanalytic subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has local
\mu
-HCP
of order
q\geq 0
.

This corollary combined with Theorem
1.2
-(c) gives the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Now, to proceed with the proof of Theorem
4.9
we need the following fact

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}
be a compact subset and
h:\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complex valued polynomial mapping of degree
d
. Then, for every
w\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
,

L_{h(E)}(h(w))\leq d\cdot L_{E}(w).

Proof.

Since
E
is compact, so is
h(E)
.
Now, let
v\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be such that
v\leq 0
on
h(E)
.
Since
\deg h\leq d
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{|w|\to+\infty}(v\circ h(w)-d\log|w|)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{|w|\to+\infty}(v\circ h(w)-\log|h(w)|)+c_{h}

\displaystyle\leq c_{v}+c_{h},

where the second inequality used the assumption of
v\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Hence,

v\circ h/d\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
and it is negative on
E
. It follows that
v\circ h\leq d\cdot L_{E}
and this finishes the proof.
∎

Remark
.

The following interesting fact is pointed out by the referee. Even if
E
is not pluripolar in
\mathbb{C}^{k}
,
h(E)
could be pluripolar in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. However, then
h(E)
is an algebraic subvariety of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and there is still content to the statement as in
[
Sa76
,
Sa82
]
.

Proof of Theorem
4.9
.

In what follows
the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is identified with the subset
\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\cdot 0
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
a\in E
be fixed and denote by
D(a,r)
a closed polydisc.
Observe first that the set
E_{a}
defined in (
4.3
) satisfies

E_{a}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x=h(t)+Mt^{m}\left(x_{1}^{m},...,x_{n}^{m}\right),t\in[0,1],|x_{i}|\leq 1,i=1,...,n\}.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the pyramid

S=\{(t,tx_{1},...,tx_{n})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:t\in[0,1],|x_{i}|\leq 1,i=1,...,n\}.

This is a convex set (with non-void interior in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
) which implies that it has HCP. The crucial observation is that
our cusp
E_{a}
is the image of this set under the polynomial projection

p(t,z):\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by

p(t,z)=h(t)+M(z_{1}^{m},...,z_{n}^{m}).

Clearly,
p(S)=E_{a}
and
p(0,0)=h(0)=a
.

To show the
local

\mu
-HCP of some order at
a
we need to shrink a bit that pyramid. We claim that for each
0<r\leq 1
, we can find
0<r^{\prime}\leq r
such that a smaller pyramid

S(r^{\prime}):=\{(t,tx)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:t\in[0,r^{\prime}],|x_{1}|\leq r^{\prime},...,|x_{n}|\leq r^{\prime}\}

satisfies

(4.4)

S(r^{\prime})\subset p^{-1}(E_{a}\cap D(a,r)).

Indeed, for
(t,tv)\in S(r^{\prime})\subset S
, the point
x=h(t)+Mt^{m}\cdot v^{m}\in E_{a}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle|x-a|

\displaystyle=|h(t)+Mt^{m}\cdot v^{m}-h(0)|

\displaystyle\leq|h(t)-h(0)|+Mt^{m}|v|^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}\|h^{(\ell)}(0)\|\right)r^{\prime}+nMr^{\prime},

where we used the fact that
m
is a positive integer.
Thus we can choose

(4.5)

r^{\prime}=\frac{r}{\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}\|h^{(\ell)}(0)\|+nM}.

This is the sole place we need the uniform bound for the sum
\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\|h^{(\ell)}(0)\|
that does not depend on the point
a
. Otherwise, the Hölder norm of
L_{E\cap B(a,r)}
would depend on
a
.

Since
S(r^{\prime})
contains a ball of radius
\tau_{n}r^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with a numerical constant
\tau_{n}
, it follows from Corollary
4.6
that
S(r^{\prime})
has local
\frac{1}{2}
-HCP of order
q=n+1
, i.e.,

(4.6)

L_{S(r^{\prime})}(t,v)\leq\frac{C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}{r^{\prime n+1}}

for every
(t,v)\in S_{\delta}(r^{\prime}):=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}:{\rm dist}(\zeta,S(r^{\prime}))\leq\delta\}
and
C
does not depend on
r^{\prime}
and
\delta
.

Moreover, for all
0<\delta\leq r^{\prime}
, we have

P(\delta)=\{(t,z)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}:|t|\leq\delta,|z_{i}|\leq\delta,i=1,...,n\}\subset S_{\delta}(r^{\prime}).

Then, for such a small
\delta
, the following inclusions hold

(4.7)

B(a,M\delta^{m})\subset p(\{0\}\times D(0,\delta))\subset p(P(\delta))\subset p(S_{\delta}(r^{\prime})).

Now we are ready to conclude the local
\mu
-HCP of
E_{a}
. Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
be such that
{\rm dist}(z,a)\leq M\delta^{m}
. By (
4.7
) we have
z=p(t,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
for some
(t,v)\in S_{\delta}(r^{\prime})
. Furthermore, by (
4.4
) we have
F:=p(S(r^{\prime}))\subset E_{a}\cap D(a,r)
. Combining these facts with (
4.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle L_{E_{a}\cap D(a,r)}(z)

\displaystyle\leq L_{F}(z)

\displaystyle=L_{F}(p(t,v))

\displaystyle\leq\max(d,m)\cdot L_{S(r^{\prime})}(t,v)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}}{r^{\prime n+1}},

where for the third inequality we used Lemma
4.11
, and the last constant
C
does not depend on
r^{\prime}
and
a
.
Rescaling
\delta:=M\delta^{m}\leq r^{\prime}
, we obtain

L_{E_{a}\cap D(a,r)}(z)\leq\frac{C\delta^{\frac{1}{2m}}}{r^{\prime n+1}}

for every
{\rm dist}(z,a)\leq\delta
, where
0<\delta\leq r^{\prime}
. Notice that
r
and
r^{\prime}
are comparable by (
4.5
). Hence,
E_{a}
has local
\mu
-HCP at
a
with the exponent
\mu=1/2m
and of order
q=n+1
and so does
E\supset E_{a}
. This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.4.
Compact sets slided transversally by analytic half-disc

In this section we provide another class of compact subsets (of Lebesgue measure zero but admitting geometric structure) that have local
\mu
-HCP and of some order. We will see later that they contain generic submanifolds as important examples.

Let

{\rm U}_{+}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}:|\tau|\leq 1,\;{\rm Im}\tau\geq 0\}

denote the (closed) upper half of the closed unit disc and we denote for
0<\delta\leq 1

{\rm U}(\delta)=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}:|\tau|\leq\delta\}.

Motivated by
[
SZ16
]
and
[
Vu18
]
we consider the following class of sets.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a compact subset and
a\in E
. Assume that there are uniform constants
C_{0},M,m
and
\delta_{0}>0
(do not depend on
a
) such that for every
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
and
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
{\rm dist}(x,a)\leq M\delta^{m}
we can find a holomorphic map

f_{a}:\overset{\circ}{\rm U}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}

satisfying:

(a)

f_{a}
is continuous on
{\rm U}_{+}
and
\sup_{U_{+}}|f_{a}|\leq C_{0}
;

(b)

f_{a}(0)=a
and
f_{a}([-1,1])\subset E
;

(c)

x\in f_{a}({\rm U}_{+}\cap{\rm U}(\delta)).

For the sets satisfying the definition we say that
E
can be
slid transversally by analytic half-disc at
a\in E
.
Moreover,
E
is said to be slid transversally by analytic half-disc if
E
can be slid transversally at every point
a\in E
.

The condition (b) is to say that the analytic half-disc
f:{\rm U}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
is attached to
E
along the interval
[-1,1]
. The condition (c) means the analytic half-disc
f_{a}:{\rm U}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
meets transversally with
E
so that
f_{a}({\rm U}_{+}\cap{\rm U}(\delta))
contains an arc joining
a
and
x
(with distance
M\delta^{m}
).

Note that the idea of analytic disc attached to a generic submanifold in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is classical in CR-geometry (see
[
BEP99
]
). However, the above definition emphasizes on the quantitive estimates.
Also, in this definition it is important to require that the constants
M,m
and
\delta_{0}
are independent of the point
a
. For the applications later we will need this independency of all point in the compact set. Geometrically it says that at each point we can attach transversally to the set a closed half-disc of uniform radius, consequently the family of analytic half-disc fills a neighborhood of the given point (in the ambient space). This will be clearly seen in the examples below.

In our setting the half-disc is attached along the real axis which is slightly different from
[
SZ16
]
and
[
Vu18
]
. However, we can use a simple conformal map to convert the results obtained there to our setting as we are only interested near the origin. Also our definition seems to be natural as in the following examples show.

Example 4.13
.

(1)

The simplest example

E:=[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}
. Then, we can choose
C_{0}=2
,
M=1
,
m=2
,
\delta_{0}=1
. In fact, for

f(z)=z^{2},

we have
f([-1,1])=[0,1]
. Consider the analytic function
f_{a}:{\rm U}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

f_{a}(z)=\begin{cases}a+z^{2}\quad\text{for }a\in[-1,0],\\
a-z^{2}\quad\text{for }a\in[0,1].\end{cases}

Moreover,
x\in f_{a}({\rm U}_{+}\cap{\rm U}(\delta))
for every
x\in\mathbb{C}
with
|x-a|\leq\delta^{2}
, where
0<\delta<\delta_{0}=1
. This implies that
E
is slid transversally by analytic half-disc. We can also see that

A=\sup_{\tau\in[-1,1]}\frac{|f_{a}(\tau)-f_{a}(0)|}{|\tau|}\leq 1.

(2)

In a similar fashion we can see that a bounded domain with
C^{2}
-boundary or compact cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\cdot 0\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
are the sets satisfies Definition
4.12
. Using this we get another proof of the local
\mu
-HCP with the exponent
\mu=1/2
and of order
q=2
(see Lemma
4.7
).

We will need the following fact about the harmonic measure.

Lemma 4.14
.

Denote
E=[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}
.
Let
h
be the harmonic extension of
1-{\bf 1}_{E}
from
\partial{\rm U}_{+}
into
{\rm U}_{+}
. Then,

h(z)=\sup\left\{v\in SH(\overset{\circ}{\rm U}_{+})\cap C({\rm U}_{+}):v\leq 1-{\bf 1}_{E}\text{ on }\partial{\rm U}_{+}\right\},

and
h
is Lipschitz continuous at
0\in E
(or on a proper compact subinterval).

Proof.

The harmonicity of the envelope is a classical result. Moreover,
h(z)
is given by an explicit formula

h(z)=\frac{2}{\pi}\arg\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right).

This function is clearly Lipschitz near
0
.
∎

It is a classical fact that

L_{[-1,1]}(z)=\log\left|z+\sqrt{z^{2}-1}\right|,

where the branch of the square root is chosen so that
|z+\sqrt{z^{2}-1}|\geq 1
in the whole plane (see. e.g.
[
Kl91
, Lemma 5.4.2]
). This function is (optimal)
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder continuous.
We obtain the following generalization.

Proposition 4.15
.

Assume that
E\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a compact set satisfying the conditions
(a), (b)
and
(c)
in Definition
4.12
at every point
a\in E
. Then,
L_{E}
is
1/m
-Hölder continuous.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
L_{E}
is
\frac{1}{m}
-Hölder continuous on
E
by Błocki’s result (
3.3
), i.e., there exists
C>0
such that for every
a\in E
,

L_{E}(z)\leq C|z-a|^{\frac{1}{m}}

for
|z-a|\leq\delta.

In fact, for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
{\rm dist}(z,a)\leq M\delta^{m}
there exists
\tau\in{\rm U}_{+}\cap{\rm U}(\delta)
such that
z=f_{a}(\tau)
. Therefore,

L_{E}(z)=L_{E}(f_{a}(\tau))

Observe that
v:=L_{E}\circ f_{a}
is subharmonic in the interior of
{\rm U}_{+}
and
v\in C^{0}({\rm U}_{+})
. It satisfies
\sup_{U_{+}}v\leq C
for a constant depending only on
E
, and
v\equiv 0
on
[-1,1]
. In particular,
v/C
is a candidate for the left hand side of the envelope in Lemma
4.14
. Hence,

v(\tau)\leq Ch(\tau)\leq C\delta,

where
h(\tau)
is the harmonic measure with respect to the interval
[-1,1]\subset\partial\Delta_{+}
and we used the fact
|h(\tau)|\leq c_{1}|\tau|\leq c_{1}\delta
.
Combining with the above identity and rescaling we conclude that

L_{E}(z)\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{m}}.

∎

Remark 4.16
.

(a)

Suppose that the
\mu
-Hölder coefficient/norm,
0<\mu\leq 1
, of the analytic half-disc
f_{a}
at
0
is bounded by a constant
A
that is independent of the point
a
, then from the above proof we can easily get the local
\mu
-Hölder regularity at
a\in E
with the exponent
\mu=1/m
and of order
q=1/\mu
, i.e.,

L_{E\cap B(a,r)}(z)\leq\frac{CA^{\frac{1}{\mu}}\delta^{\frac{1}{m}}}{r^{\frac{1}{\mu}}},\quad{\rm dist}(z,a)\leq\delta,

where
C
is independent of
r
and the point
a
.

(b)

Suppose
E
satisfies (a), (b) and (c) in Definition
4.12
at
a\in E
with an analytic half-disc
f_{a}
whose the
\mu
-Hölder coefficient at
0
, where
0<\mu\leq 1
,

A=A(\mu):=\sup_{\tau\in[-1,1]}\frac{|f_{a}(\tau)-f_{a}(0)|}{|\tau|^{\mu}}<C

for a uniform constant
C
which does not depend on the point
a
.
Then, for every small
r>0
, the set
E\cap B(a,r)
can be slid transversally at
a
by an analytic half-disc
g_{a}:{\rm U}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by

g_{a}(\tau)=f_{a}\left((r/A)^{\frac{1}{\mu}}\tau\right).

Moreover, if
C_{0},M,m,\delta_{0}
are uniform constants satisfying
(c)
for
E
at
a
, then the constants

C_{0},M(r/A)^{\frac{m}{\mu}},m,\delta_{0}
satisfy (c) for
E\cap B(a,r)
at
a
.

It is proved by Vu
[
Vu18
, Proposition 2.5]
and by Sadullaev and Zeriahi
[
SZ16
]
that a
C^{2}
-smooth generic submanifold in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(or in a complex manifold) can be slid transversally by analytic half-disc together with uniform control of Lipschitz norm at
0
of
f_{a}
.
As a consequence the extremal functions of these generic submanifolds are Lipschitz continuous. Furthermore, these manifolds have local
\mu
-HCP with the exponent
\mu=1
and of order
q=1
.
It will be interesting to obtain more examples of sets satisfying Definition
4.12
.

5.
Applications in equidistribution speed for Fekete points

In dimension
n=1
, for a non-polar compact set
E\subset\mathbb{C}
, the function
L_{E}
coincides with the Green function of the unbounded component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus E
with the pole at infinity. A classical result saying that the sequence of the probability counting measures for Fekete points of
E
, called
Fekete’s measures
, converges weakly to the equilibrium measure
\mu_{\rm eq}
of
E
as the number of points goes to infinity (see, e.g
[
BBL92
]
). The speed of convergence of the sequence can be also quantified for a compact domain whose boundary is smooth.

The analogous problems regarding the weak convergence and speed of convergence of the Fekete measures of a compact subset in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, had been open for a long time. The weak convergence was obtained in a deep work of Boucksom, Berman and Witt-Nystrom
[
BBW11
]
. The speed of convergence was proved
[
LO16
]
for
K=X
a compact projective manifold and
\phi
a strictly smooth plurisubharmonic weight. Later, Dinh, Ma and Nguyen
[
DMN17
]
obtained the estimate for a large class of compact sets that satisfies the
(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha},\mathscr{C}^{\alpha^{\prime}})
-regularity (Definition
1.3
).

Let
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be the set of complex valued polynomials of degree at most
d
. Then its dimension is
N_{d}=\binom{n+d}{n}
, and let
\{e_{1},...,e_{N_{d}}\}
be an ordered system of all monomials
z^{\alpha}:=z^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\cdots z_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
with
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots\alpha_{n}\leq d
, where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. For each system
x^{(d)}=\{x_{1},...,x_{N_{d}}\}
of
N_{d}
points of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
we define the generalized Vandermonde matrix
{\rm VDM}(x^{(n)})
by

{\rm VDM}(x^{(d)}):=\det[e_{i}(x_{j})]_{i,j=1,...,N_{d}}.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a non-pluripolar compact subset. Following
[
DMN17
]
we say that a
Fekete configuration of order
d
for
K
is a system
\xi^{(d)}=\{\xi_{1},....,\xi_{N_{d}}\}
of
N_{d}
points of
K
that maximizes the function
|{\rm VDM}(x^{(d)})|
on
K
, i.e.,

\left|{\rm VDM}(\xi^{(d)})\right|=\max\left\{\left|{\rm VDM}(x^{(d)})\right|:x^{(d)}\subset K\right\}.

Given a Fekete configuration

\xi^{(d)}
of
K
, we consider the probability measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined by

\mu_{d}:=\frac{1}{N_{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d}}\delta_{\xi_{j}},

where
\delta_{x}
denotes the Dirac measure concentrated at the point
x
. It is called Fekete’s measure of order
d
by
[
DMN17
, Definition 1.4]
.
It is known for
n=1
that
\{\mu_{d}\}
converges weakly to the equilibrium measure
\mu_{\rm eq}
of
K
as
d
goes to infinity. In a fundamental paper Berman, Boucksom and Witt Nystrom
[
BBW11
]
proved the generalization of this result for
n\geq 2
. Namely,

(5.1)

\lim_{d\to\infty}\mu_{d}=\mu_{\rm eq},\qquad\text{where }\mu_{\rm eq}=\frac{(dd^{c}L_{K}^{*})^{n}}{\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(dd^{c}L_{K}^{*})^{n}},

in the weak topology of measures. This result coincides with the classical one for
n=1
and it was listed as an open problem in
[
Si82
, 15.3]
and
[
ST97
, Problem 3.3]
.

In fact this problem is considered as a special case of
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of a very general reformulation in the framework of a big line bundle over a complex manifold in
[
BBW11
]
. It is possible by observing that
the space
\mathcal{P}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is isomorphic to the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree at most
d
in
(n+1)
-variable
\mathcal{H}_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{n+1})
. The latter space can be identified the space of global holomorphic section
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}(d))
, where
\mathcal{O}(d)
is the
d
-th tensor power of the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
over
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. We refer the readers to
[
Le10
]
for a self-contained proof which derived from
[
BB10
]
and
[
BBW11
]
using only (weighted) pluripotential theory in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Note again that the regularity of weighted extremal functions is a local property and it is invariant under biholomorphic maps. Without loss of generality we restrict ourself to the compact subset in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as in Section
4
(see also Appendix
6
). Hence, an immediate consequence of
the characterizations in Theorem
1.2
is

Lemma 5.1
.

All local
\alpha
-
HCP
compact subsets of order
q
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
are
(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha},\mathscr{C}^{\alpha^{\prime}})
-regular, where
\alpha^{\prime}
is explicitly computed in terms of
\alpha
and
q
.

By Remark
3.10
and the proof of Theorem
1.2
we can compute the exponent
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime\prime 2}/(\alpha^{\prime\prime}+2+q)
where
\alpha^{\prime\prime}=\alpha^{2}/(1+q)
. This combined with
[
DMN17
, Theorem 1.5]
shows that we have a large number of new examples from
Sections
4.2

4.3
,
4.4
for which the following estimate for speed of convergence holds.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a local
\alpha
-
HCP
compact subset of order
q
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(K,\alpha)
such that for every test function
v\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}
(the Hölder space on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
) and for every Fekete configuration
\xi^{(d)}
of
K
,

|\left<\mu_{d}-\mu_{\rm eq},v\right>|\leq C\|v\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}}d^{-\alpha^{\prime}}.

Notice that the holomorphic maps which preserve HCP are characterized in
[
Pi21
]
. It is likely that the criterion can be extended to the local
\mu
-HCP case. If it is true, then we will obtain compact sets, via nice holomorphic maps, which are
(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha},\mathscr{C}^{\alpha^{\prime}})
-regular.

Appendix

In this section we give the proof of Theorem
1.2
-(c) without assuming that it is a compact set contained in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.
Let
K\subset X
be a non-pluripolar compact subset and
a\in K
.
Consider a holomorphic coordinate ball
(\Omega,\tau)
in
X
centered at
a
,

(6.1)

\tau:\Omega\to\mathbb{B}:=\mathbb{B}(0,1)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

is a biholomorphic map with
\tau(a)=0
. Suppose that

\omega=dd^{c}\rho

for a strictly psh function
\rho\in PSH(\Omega)\cap C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, where
\rho
attains its minimum
\inf_{\Omega}\rho=\rho(a)=0
. (In general we can do this by shrinking
\Omega
and modifying
\rho
by a pluriharmornic function.)
As the Hölder continuity of
V_{K}
is independent of the metric (Remark
2.5
), by rescaling we may also assume

0\leq\rho\leq 1\quad\text{ on }\overline{\Omega}.

We recall some classical facts in pluripotential theory. Let
E\subset\mathbb{B}(0,\frac{1}{2})
be a compact set. The weighted zero-one relative extremal function is given by

u_{E}(z)=\sup\left\{v(z):v\in PSH(\mathbb{B}),\,v_{|_{E}}\leq 0,v\leq 1\right\}.

The classical result in pluripotential theory
[
Kl91
, Proposition 5.3.3]
tells us that the extremal function
L_{E}
satisfies

(6.2)

C_{1}\,u_{E}\leq L_{E}\leq C_{2}u_{E}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{B},

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are two positive constants depending only on
E
and
\mathbb{B}
.

Now, let
\phi
be a continuous function on
\overline{\mathbb{B}}
.
For simplicity we assume that the weight function satisfies

(6.3)

0\leq\phi\leq 1.

The (positive) weighted relative extremal function is defined by

u_{E,\phi}(z)=\sup\left\{v(z):v\in PSH(\mathbb{B}),\,v_{|_{E}}\leq\phi,v\leq\phi+1\right\}.

(Notice that these functions are different from the ones in
[
CKZ
]
and
[
Al09
]
). It follows from the definitions and the assumption on
\phi
that

(6.4)

u_{E}+\inf_{E}\phi\leq u_{E,\phi}\leq 2u_{E}+\sup_{E}\phi.

Note that this inequality is very similar to (
2.7
) and Lemma
2.6
-(a). Moreover, the extra constant 2 on the right hand side will not cause any harm in study the regularity of the subset
E
.
Furthermore, we also have the monotonicity for
E\subset F
and
0\leq\phi\leq\psi\leq 1
,

(6.5)

u_{F,\phi}\leq u_{E,\phi}\leq u_{E,\psi}.

We are ready to prove the relation between the global and local extremal functions in a coordinate ball
\Omega\subset X
defined in (
6.1
).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
K\subset\subset\Omega
be a non-pluripolar compact set and
\widehat{\rho}:=\rho\circ\tau^{-1}
. Then, there exist two positive constants
m,M
depending only on
K,\Omega
and
\rho
such that

m\,u_{\tau(K),\widehat{\rho}}(z)\leq(V_{K}+\rho)\circ\tau^{-1}(z)\leq M\,u_{\tau(K),\widehat{\rho}}(z)\quad\text{on }\mathbb{B}.

Proof.

We follow closely the argument in
[
Kl91
, Proposition 5.3.3]
. Since
K
is a non-pluripolar, we have
M_{\Omega}=\sup_{\Omega}V_{K}^{*}<+\infty
.
Let
w\in PSH(X,\omega)
such that
w\leq 0
on
K
.
Hence,
v:=(w+\rho)\circ\tau^{-1}
is plurisubharmornic in
\mathbb{B}
,
v\leq\widehat{\rho}
on
\tau(K)
and
v\leq M_{\Omega}+1=:M
. Since
\widehat{\rho}\geq 0
,

v\leq Mu_{\tau(K),\widehat{\rho}}

and therefore the second inequality follows. Before continuing the proof of the first inequality let us state a useful observation (see also
[
BBW11
, Corollary 1.7]
and
[
DMN17
, Theorem 2.7]
for different proofs).

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
D\subset\subset X
be a smooth domain. Then,
V_{\overline{D}}
is continuous on
X
.

Proof.

Since
V_{\overline{D}}=0
on
\overline{D}
, it is continuous in
D
. By Corollary
2.4
it remains to verify
V^{*}_{\overline{D}}(a)=0
at
a\in\partial D
. Let us fix such a point
a
and consider
\Omega
the local coordinate unit ball center at
a
as in (
6.1
). Set
K=\overline{D}\cap\tau^{-1}(\overline{\mathbb{B}}(0,1/2))
. By monotonicity Proposition
2.2
-(a) it is enough to show that
V_{K}^{*}(a)=0
. To this end we note that
\tau(K)\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}(0,1/2)
is a smooth domain near the origin
0=\tau(a)
. So, it is locally
L
-regular at
0
by Example
4.1
. Hence,
u_{\tau(K)\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}(0,\varepsilon)}^{*}(0)=0
for every
\varepsilon>0
small by the inequality (
6.2
). Next, using the second inequality above, the monotonicity (
6.5
) and (
6.4
) we get

\displaystyle V_{K}\circ\tau^{-1}(z)+\widehat{\rho}(z)

\displaystyle\leq Mu_{\tau(K),\widehat{\rho}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq Mu_{\tau(K)\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}(0,\varepsilon),\widehat{\rho}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq M[2u_{\tau(K)\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}(0,\varepsilon)}(z)+\sup_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}(0,\varepsilon)}\widehat{\rho}].

Using the fact
\widehat{\rho}(0)=0
we get

V_{K}^{*}(a)\leq 2Mu_{\tau(K)\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}(0,\varepsilon)}^{*}(0)+Mc_{3}\varepsilon=Mc_{3}\varepsilon,

where
c_{3}
is the Lipschitz norm of
\widehat{\rho}
in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}
. As
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary we have
V_{K}^{*}(a)=0
and the proof of the corollary is completed.
∎

Let us complete now the proof the first inequality in the lemma. By the assumption
2m:=\inf_{\partial\Omega}\rho>0
. Take
0<\varepsilon<m
. For
\delta>0
denote
K_{\delta}=\{x\in X:{\rm dist}(x,K)\leq\delta\}
where
{\rm dist}(\cdot,K)
is the distance induced from the metric
\omega
. If
\delta>0
is small enough, then
K_{\delta}\subset\subset\Omega
is a smooth domain. Hence, by Corollary
6.2
we have
V_{K_{\delta}}
is a continuous
\omega
-psh function. Let
v\in PSH(\mathbb{B})
be such that
v\leq\widehat{\rho}
on
\tau(K)
and
v\leq\widehat{\rho}+1
in
\mathbb{B}
. Define

\widetilde{v}=\begin{cases}\max\{(m-\varepsilon)v\circ\tau-\rho,V_{K_{\delta}}\}\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
V_{K_{\delta}}\quad&\text{in }X\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Since
\limsup_{x\to\partial\Omega}[(m-\varepsilon)v\circ\tau(x)-\rho(x)]\leq 2(m-\varepsilon)-\inf_{\partial\Omega}\rho\leq-2\varepsilon
, we have
(m-\varepsilon)\,v\circ\tau-\rho\leq V_{K_{\delta}}
near
\partial\Omega
. Hence,

\widetilde{v}\in PSH(X,\omega)
and
\widetilde{v}\leq 0
on
K
. Hence,

(m-\varepsilon)v\circ\tau-\rho\leq V_{K}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

By letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we get the first inequality.
∎

For a general compact set
K\subset X
, we know from Theorem
1.2
-(a) that
V_{K}
is
\mu
-Hölder continuous at
a
if and only if
V_{K\cap B(a,r)}
is
\mu
-Hölder continuous for a closed coordinate ball
B(a,r)=\tau^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(0,r))
centered at
a
and small radius

. Therefore, we may assume that
K
is contained in this holomorphic coordinate ball
(\Omega,\tau)
above and
K\subset B(a,1/2):=\tau^{-1}(\overline{\mathbb{B}}(0,1/2))
. Clearly, for
0<r<1/2

(6.6)

\tau(K\cap B(a,r))=\tau(K)\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}(0,r)\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}(0,1/2).

We now state the characterization of the local
\mu
-HCP.

Lemma 6.3
.

\tau(K)
has local
\mu
-HCP of order
q
at

if and only if
K
has a uniform density in capacity (
1.5
) at
a
and

is
\mu^{\prime}
-Hölder continuous at

\mu^{\prime}>0

Proof.

Let us write
E:=\tau(K)
,
\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}:=\overline{\mathbb{B}}(0,r)
and assume that
E
has local
\mu
-HCP at
\tau(a)=0
, i.e.,

(6.7)

L_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}(z)\leq\frac{C\delta^{\mu}}{r^{q}},\quad|z|\leq\delta\leq\delta_{0},\quad 0<r\leq r_{0}.

By the proof of Lemma
3.3

E
satisfies the property (
3.2
) at
\tau(a)=0
. Equivalently,
K
satisfies (
1.5
) at

Lemma

tells us that

(6.8)

V_{K}\circ\tau^{-1}(z)\leq V_{K}\circ\tau^{-1}(z)+\widehat{\rho}(z)\leq Mu_{E,\widehat{\rho}}(z).

Furthermore, the monotonicity (
6.4

\displaystyle u_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r},\widehat{\rho}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq 2u_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}(z)+\sup_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}\widehat{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{C_{1}}L_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}(z)+c_{3}r

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\delta^{\mu}}{r^{q}}+c_{3}r.

where
c_{3}

\widehat{\rho}

It follows from this and (

V_{K}\circ\tau^{-1}(z)\leq MC\delta^{\mu^{\prime}}\quad\text{for }|z|\leq\delta

at

.

Conversely, assume now

\mu

V_{K\cap B(a,r)}(x)\leq\frac{A}{\varkappa}\frac{\delta^{\mu}}{r^{2+q}}.

Since
\inf_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}\widehat{\rho}=\widehat{\rho}(0)=0

\widehat{\rho}(z)-\inf_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}\widehat{\rho}\leq c_{3}\delta.

\displaystyle L_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}u_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}\leq C_{2}\left(u_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r},\widehat{\rho}}-\inf_{E\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{r}}\widehat{\rho}\right)

Therefore, for

∎

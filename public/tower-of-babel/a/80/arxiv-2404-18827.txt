Extreme fragmentation of a Bose gas
Nathan Dupont, Amit Vashisht, Nathan Goldman

Abstract
Fragmentation of an interacting Bose gas refers to the macroscopic occupation of a finite set of single-particle eigenstates. This phenomenon is related to the notion of particle-number squeezing in quantum optics, an exquisite property of quantum states that can offer metrological gain. So far, fragmentation has only been partially achieved in experiments involving a large number N N of bosons in few modes. Here, we introduce a practical and efficient scheme to prepare fragmented states in systems realizing the L L -mode Bose-Hubbard model. We demonstrate how a large energy detuning between the modes can be used as a practical control parameter to successfully fragment a Bose gas over an extremely short preparation time. Applying an optimal-control approach within realistic experimental constraints, we obtain total fragmentation at a high filling factor, realizing | N / L , … , N / L ⟩ \ket{N/L,...,N/L} Fock states with hundreds of bosons in very few modes over a few tunneling times.



Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC) refers to the macroscopic bosonic occupation of a single-particle ground state
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Interestingly, when the single-particle ground state is degenerate, a rich competition occurs between the different states in which to condense.
In such a scenario, if the order of degeneracy
D
is much smaller than the total number of particles
N
, the system can form a
fragmented
BEC
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
which is characterized by a macroscopic occupation
N/D
of the degenerate states.

Ground state degeneracy, stemming from the single-particle Hamiltonian’s invariance under a symmetry transformation, is generally lifted, in practice, by arbitrarily small perturbations (e.g. an external magnetic field) which break the relevant symmetries
[
7
]
. In practice, this strongly complicates the experimental realization of fragmented BECs with a large
N/D
ratio. Nonetheless, strong interactions among the particles, with an energy scale larger than symmetry-breaking perturbations, can stabilize these elusive phases; this was recently observed in the three-fold degenerate ground state of a spin-1 atomic gas
[
8
]

L
-mode system, this entropy is maximized by the
L
-tuple Fock state
\ket{N/L}\equiv\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}\ket{N/L}_{i}
, evenly distributing the bosons in each mode.

A tight-binding lattice model, as realized by cold atoms in optical lattices
[
3
]

L

As a consequence, bosons condense in the lowest-energy Bloch state, forming a superfluid state over the lattice. Ramping up strong interactions can then fragment the superfluid state, reaching the maximally fragmented Fock state
\ket{N/L}
in the strongly-correlated limit
[

,
3
]

The system.

We consider a fixed number
N
of bosons in a two-mode system, such as the double well illustrated in Fig.
1
(a). Under the low-energy single-band approximation, we describe the system by the two-mode Bose-Hubbard model:

\displaystyle\hat{H}(t)=

\displaystyle-J\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{1}\right)+\dfrac{U}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\delta\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}\right),

(1)

where
\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger},\hat{a}_{i}
are the bosonic creation and annihilation operators in mode
i
,
J
is the particle hopping strength,
U
quantifies the onsite two-body interaction and
\delta
is an eventual tilt between the modes. Using the Schwinger (angular-momentum) representation,

\hat{J}_{x}\!=\!(\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{2}+\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{1})/2
,

\hat{J}_{y}\!=\!(\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{2}-\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{1})/2\text{i}
,

\hat{J}_{z}\!=\!(\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{2})/2
,
Hamiltonian (
1
) is rewritten as

\hat{H}(t)=U\hat{J}^{2}_{z}-2J\hat{J}_{x}+2\delta\hat{J}_{z},

(2)

up to the constant term
U\hat{N}(\hat{N}-2)/4
(with
\hat{N}=\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{2}
), describing our system as an effective spin-
1/2
quantum gas on the Bloch sphere. For a sufficiently large
N
, intuition can be built on the quantum dynamics governed by Eq. (
2
) by taking the semiclassical, mean-field (MF) limit
\hat{a}_{i}\rightarrow\sqrt{n_{i}}\text{e}^{-\text{i}\theta_{i}}
, yielding the MF Hamiltonian

H(\varphi,z)=\frac{\Lambda}{2}z^{2}-\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\cos{(\varphi)}+\frac{\delta}{J}z,

(3)

where
\Lambda=NU/2J
, and where we have introduced the two canonically conjugate Bloch-sphere variables: the relative phase
\varphi=\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
and population
z=(n_{1}-n_{2})/N

[
54
,
55
]
.

To quantify the fragmentation of an arbitrary state
\ket{\psi}
, we define the following three metrics: (i) The fragmentation (von Neumann) entropy
S_{\text{F}}=\sum_{i}-\lambda_{i}\log{\lambda_i}

[
56
]
, with
\lambda_{i}
the normalized eigenvalues of the reduced one-body density matrix with elements
\hat{\rho}_{i,j}\!=\!\bra{\psi}\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}\ket{\psi}
. In a
L
-mode system,
S_{\text{F}}
is maximized to
\ln(L)
by the
L
-tuple Fock state
\ket{N/L}
. (ii) The number squeezing parameter
\xi_{N}^{2}=\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\psi}/\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\text{ref}}
, quantifying the suppression of particle-number fluctuations with respect to the uncorrelated, binomial distribution of variance

\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\text{ref}}=N/4
for
L\!=2\!

[
20
,
50
]
.
(iii) The quantum fidelity to the twin Fock state
\mathcal{F}=|\innerproduct{N/2}{\psi}|^{2}
.

Adiabatic approach.

We first consider the preparation of
\ket{N/2}
by adiabatically increasing
U/J
, as traditionally performed to induce a superfluid to Mott-insulator transition
[
10
,
11
]
. In Fig.
1
(b), we show the many-body spectrum of the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
2
) for
N\!=\!32
and
\delta=0
as a function of
U/J
. The physics is that of the standard bosonic Josephson junction
[
57
,
58
]
: Starting from
U/J\ll 1
, the ground state
\ket{\phi_0}
is a superfluid coherent spin state
[
52
,
20
]
, pointing along
(\varphi,z)=(0,0)
on the Bloch sphere [Fig.
1
(c)]; as one increases interactions, this state asymptotically tends towards
\ket{N/2}
in the limit
U/J\rightarrow\infty
[Fig.
1
(d,e)]. Crucially, very large values of
U/J
are needed to achieve high ground-state fragmentation: considering
N\!=\!32
bosons, we find that reaching a fidelity
\mathcal{F}>0.99
requires
U/J\gtrsim 230
, while
S_{\text{F}}/\ln(2)>0.99
is reached for
U/J\gtrsim 285
.

When adiabatically following the states (c) to (e) in Fig.
1
(b), we find that the narrowest energy gap that could trigger Landau-Zener transitions is
\Delta E\!=\!4J
at
U/J\!=\!0
, after which
\Delta E
keeps increasing monotonically; this observation does not depend on the number of bosons
N
. An adiabatic preparation of the target state thus requires a preparation time
t_{\text{f}}\gg\hbar/J

[
50
]
.

As previously noted, total fragmentation requires
U/J\rightarrow\infty
, which, in practice, would correspond to progressively annihilating the hopping strength, e.g. by increasing the distance between the sites. In an experiment, this could be hard to achieve without exciting motional degrees of freedom
[
59
,
38
,
32
,
35
]
. Alternatively,
J
could be effectively suppressed by means of Floquet engineering, using a fast modulation of the tilt
\delta

[
44
,
45
]
. We discuss the adiabatic preparation of the
\ket{N/2}
state using this Floquet approach in
[
50
]
; we demonstrate the applicability of this method deep in the high-frequency regime, which is found to be only accessible for limited number of bosons
N
, due to the interacting nature of this periodically-driven system
[
50
]
.

Adding a tilt.

To overcome the severe limitations of the adiabatic approach, we now add a strong tilt
\delta
to the system [Fig.
1
(f)]; for the sake of illustration, we henceforth fix the ratio
U/J
to a realistic value of 1. In the limit
\delta\gg J,U
, the tilt effectively annihilates the hopping term, as one can deduce by moving to a rotating frame generated by the unitary transformation
\textstyle\hat{R}=\exp\{\text{i}2\delta\hat{J}_{z}t/\hbar\}
; see Refs.
[
26
,
50
]
. In this frame, the eigenstates of the Hamiltonian (
2
) become the Fock states
\ket{n_1}\equiv\ket{n_1,N-n_1}
, i.e. the eigenstates of
\hat{J}_{z}
, with the intuitive lab-frame ground state
\ket{0,N}
due to the large tilt. In Fig.
1
(g), we show the many-body spectrum associated with the Hamiltonian (
2
) as a function of
\delta
, for
N\!=\!32
bosons, identifying the projection of the target twin Fock state
\ket{N/2=16}
. We see that for
\delta/J\!\sim\!1
, the twin Fock state is projected onto many eigenstates of the system, while it indeed becomes an eigenstate for
\delta/J\gg 1
. As
\delta
increases, the MF fixed-point of lowest energy migrates towards the South pole
z=-1
[Fig
1
(h-i)]. The remaining trajectories wraps around the Bloch sphere, as do the eigenstates, turning into Fock states of definite relative population
\langle\hat{J}_{z}\rangle
.

From this brief study, summarized in Fig.
1
, one apprehends how difficult it is to adiabatically connect the eigenstates of panels (h) to (j), hence suggesting the development of a more sophisticated approach.

Optimal control.

We now consider optimal control as a promising alternative to prepare twin Fock states
\ket{N/2}
, through an arbitrary variation of the tilt
\delta(t)
between the wells. To obtain the optimal
\delta(t)
that steers the ground state
\ket{\phi_0}
of (
2
) (taken at
\delta=0
) towards
\ket{N/2}
in a control duration
t_{\text{f}}
(fixed beforehand, see below), we employ a first-order gradient-based algorithm
[
60
,
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
,
48
]
. This approach amounts to performing a gradient ascent on a state-preparation metric, which we choose here to be the fidelity
\mathcal{F}
to the target Fock state
\ket{N/2}

[
50
]
. The quality of the optimal control fields (in terms of e.g. fidelity maximization, robustness to noise and experimental feasibility) yielded by such numerical approaches strongly depends on the initial guess provided as input. For our purposes, we find that a rather large, constant tilt
\delta_{0}/J\sim\Lambda/2=NU/4J
is an efficacious initial guess, as it amounts to a quench into a configuration where both the initial state and the target Fock state are projected into the same restricted subspace consisting of only a few quasi-Fock eigenstates [see the blue states in Fig.
1
(g)], effectively reducing the Hilbert space dimension into which the optimization takes place. In the large
\delta_{0}
limit, the spacing between two successive Fock eigenstates
\ket{n_1},\ket{n_1+1}

is
\Delta E\sim U(2n_{1}-N+1)+2\delta_{0}\sim 2\delta_{0}
for
n_{1}=N/2

[
50
]
. The period
T_{0}
at which a non-steady state evolves in the vicinity of
\ket{N/2}
in Hilbert space is therefore
T_{0}\sim h/2\delta_{0}
; this establishes a lower bound on the time associated with state alteration near the equator of the Bloch sphere in the strongly tilted system. Here, we typically opt for a control duration
t_{\text{f}}\sim~10\,T_{0}\sim 20\,h/NU

2
2

2

Working with finite
\delta_{0}

t_{\text{f}}
and
\delta_{0}
around the asymptotic expressions given in the text in order to maximize the fidelity resulting from the initial guess.

N

\delta_{0}\sim NU/4
used in our approach. In a tight-binding framework, relevant to optical lattices
[
66

The full quantum dynamics depends on the details of the instantaneous many-body spectra, over the entire control duration. A priori, a limitation of this approach, as compared to adiabatic schemes, is that it requires a precise knowledge of the number of bosons
N
. Quite generically, optimal control lacks robustness against parameter fluctuations, especially those associated with the particle number
N

[
49
]
. However, we find that in this large-average-tilt configuration (where the eigenenergy spacing between successive quasi-Fock eigenstates loses its dependence on the average number of particles in said eigenstates), an optimal
\delta(t)
computed for a given
N
remains efficient to fragment the Bose gas for a close but different
N^{\prime}=N+2k
(keeping commensurability with

), and can be used as a solid initial guess for another optimization with the correct
N^{\prime}

[
50
]
.

\ket{N/2}
in this biased two-mode system by drawing a parallel with spin-squeezing performed by one-axis twisting (OAT)
[

,

\vec{n}
on the Bloch sphere’s equator undergoes

through an evolution governed by the interaction Hamiltonian
U\hat{J}^{2}_{z}
, due to MF trajectories flowing in opposite directions on each Bloch hemisphere (
\differential\varphi/\differential t\propto z
). The resulting spin squeezing, occurring along a tangential direction
\vec{s}
to the average spin direction
\vec{n}
, can be quantified by the squeezing parameter
\xi_{\text{R}}^{2}=N(\Delta\hat{J}_{\vec{s}})^{2}/\langle\hat{J}_{\vec{n}}\rangle^{2}

[
70
,
20
]
, assessing the metrological usefulness of the anisotropy of quantum fluctuations around
\vec{n}
. OAT spin-squeezes an equatorial coherent spin state for

, while for longer times, it gets over-squeezed as it wraps around the Bloch sphere, resulting in
\xi_{\text{R}}^{2}>1

[
68
,
20
]
[Fig.
3
(a,c)]. A salient symptom of over-squeezing is the constellation of zeros riddling the Husimi function [Fig.
3
(a)], which are Majorana anti-stars, antipodes of the
N
individual
1/2
-spins into which the state can be factored according to the Majorana stellar representation
[
71
,
72
,
73
,
74
]
. However, OAT never number squeezes, having Fock states as its eigenstates [Fig.
3
(c)]. In the case of optimal control [Fig.
3
(b)], the large tilt yields MF trajectories similar to OAT near the equator, resulting in a slight short-term spin squeezing (panel (c)). The control on
\delta(t)
then constrains the state towards the equator while it wraps around the Bloch sphere, gradually sending the Majorana anti-stars to its poles. Our optimal control approach, operating around a large tilt value, can thus be viewed as number squeezing through optimized axis twisting.

Conclusion.

In this work, we introduced a practical and efficient approach to achieve total fragmentation of a Bose gas in the
L
-mode Bose-Hubbard model, i.e. to prepare the evenly distributed
L
-tuple Fock state
\ket{N/L,...,N/L}
with
N
bosons. We illustrated our results for systems with
L\!=\!2
and 3 modes. We first considered the conventional adiabatic ramping of the ratio between interaction
U
and hopping
J
(either directly varied, or effectively via Floquet engineering), finding strong limitations and constraints: long preparation time, low particle number and high Floquet frequency. We then applied quantum optimal control with the energy bias
\delta
between the modes as our control parameter. We found that by operating around a large tilt
\delta\approx NU/4
, one is able to diabatically prepare the twin Fock state in a duration
t_{\text{f}}\approx 20\,\hbar/NU
, with appreciable robustness against atom number fluctuations
[
50
]
. This work offers realistic approaches to experimentally achieve
L
-tuple Fock state preparation, the most resourceful number state for metrological purposes
[
20
]
.
We emphasize that our scheme relies on modulating the tilt (or detuning between the modes) around a large mean value. This can be advantageous for certain physical settings that cannot operate close to resonance (e.g. schemes using two coupled internal states operating in the large-detuning regime).

S2
B. Floquet control of
U/J

One can introduce a fast periodic modulation of the tilt around
\delta=0
to dynamically control the effective tunneling between the two modes
[
44
]
. In this way, one can drive a superfluid to Mott insulator transition
[
45

by

ramping down the effective tunneling of the particles.

More precisely, we consider a strong sinusoidal modulation of the tilt
\delta(t)=A\cos(\omega t)
and the Hamiltonian of the periodically driven system is written as

\displaystyle\hat{H}(t)=

\displaystyle-J\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell+1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell+1}\hat{a}_{\ell}\right)+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}+A\cos{(\omega t)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\ell\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell},

(S4)

Changing to a frame of reference rotating with the same frequency
\omega

\displaystyle\hat{R}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\text{i}\frac{A}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\cos{(\omega t')}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\ell\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}\right\},

(S5)

we obtain a transformed Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(t)

\displaystyle=-J\left(\exp\left\{\text{i}\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\sin{(\omega t)}\right\}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell+1}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}.

(S6)

[
75

76

66

[
50

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\differential t\,\tilde{H}(t)=\frac{U}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}-J\mathcal{J}_{0}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell+1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell+1}\hat{a}_{\ell}\right).

(S7)

The first order correction is given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2T\text{i}}\int_{0}^{T}\differential t_{1}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\differential t_{2}\,[\tilde{H}(t_{1}),\tilde{H}(t_{2})]

\displaystyle=\frac{UJ\pi}{2\hbar\omega}\mathcal{H}_{0}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{n}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell+1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{n}_{\ell+1}\hat{a}_{\ell+1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell+1}\hat{n}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell+1}\hat{n}_{\ell+1}\hat{a}_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{J^{2}\pi}{\hbar\omega}\mathcal{H}_{0}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)\mathcal{J}_{0}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{L}\hat{a}_{L}\right),

(S9)

In the limit

S3
C. Negating the hopping by a large constant tilt

We show how a strong tilt between the modes of the Bose-Hubbard model effectively annihilates the hopping, detailing the derivation for
L=2
and
L=3
modes.

S3.1
a.
L=2

We consider the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}=U\hat{J}^{2}_{z}-2J\hat{J}_{x}+2\delta\hat{J}_{z},

(S10)

in the rotating frame accessed by the unitary transformation
|\widetilde{\psi}(t)\rangle=\hat{R}|\psi(t)\rangle
with
\hat{R}=\text{exp}\{\text{i}2\delta\hat{J}_{z}t/\hbar\}
. In this frame, the Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\hat{\widetilde{H}}(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{R}\hat{H}\hat{R}^{\dagger}-\text{i}\hbar\hat{R}\dfrac{\differential\hat{R}^{\dagger}}{\differential t}

\displaystyle=U\hat{J}^{2}_{z}-2J\,\text{e}^{\text{i}2\delta\hat{J}_{z}t/\hbar}\hat{J}_{x}\,\text{e}^{-\text{i}2\delta\hat{J}_{z}t/\hbar}

\displaystyle=U\hat{J}^{2}_{z}-2J\cos\left(\dfrac{2\delta t}{\hbar}\right)\hat{J}_{x}+2J\sin\left(\dfrac{2\delta t}{\hbar}\right)\hat{J}_{y},

(S11)

using the algebra
[\hat{J}_{i},\hat{J}_{j}]=\text{i}\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{J}_{k}
. If
\delta\gg U,J
, the Hamiltonian
\hat{\widetilde{H}}
can be approximated by its Magnus expansion at zeroth order, namely its average over one period
\textstyle\hat{\widetilde{H}}\approx\hat{\widetilde{H}}_{\text{eff}}^{(0)}=U\hat{J}^{2}_{z}
. Finally, going back to the initial frame gives

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=\hat{R}^{\dagger}\hat{\widetilde{H}}\hat{R}-\text{i}\hbar\hat{R}^{\dagger}\dfrac{\differential\hat{R}}{\differential t}

\displaystyle\approx U\hat{J}^{2}_{z}+2\delta\hat{J}_{z},

(S12)

whose eigenstates are the Fock states, among which the maximally fragmented state
\ket{N/2}
; see Fig. 1 of the main text.

S3.2
b.
L=3

We consider the titled Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\hat{H}(t)

\displaystyle=-J\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell+1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell+1}\hat{a}_{\ell}\right)+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}+\delta\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{3}\right).

(S13)

Again, moving to a frame rotating with angular frequency
\delta/\hbar
using the unitary operator
\hat{R}=\mathrm{exp}\{\text{i}\delta t(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{3})/\hbar\}
, we get the transformed Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{\widetilde{H}}(t)

\displaystyle=J\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\left(\text{e}^{\text{i}\delta t/\hbar}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell+1}-\text{e}^{-\text{i}\delta t/\hbar}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell+1}\hat{a}_{\ell}\right)+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}.

(S14)

Using a similar procedure as for
L\!=2
for
\delta\gg U,J
, one can write an effective Hamiltonian which till the second order of Magnus expansion is given as

\displaystyle\hat{\widetilde{H}}_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle\approx\left(\frac{U}{2}-\frac{J^{2}U}{\delta^{2}}\right)\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}-\frac{J^{2}U}{\delta^{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}+\frac{4J^{2}U}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\hat{a}_{\ell}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell+1}\hat{a}_{\ell+1}-\frac{J^{2}U}{\delta^{2}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{J^{2}}{\delta}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{3}\right).

(S15)

As a matter of fact, the terms obtained here resemble the Hamiltonian describing rotationally invariant spin-1 gases
[
80
,
8
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{S}=1}}{U_{s}}

\displaystyle=2\hat{N}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{3}\hat{a}_{3}+2\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}_{2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{2}\hat{a}_{3}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}_{3}\right)+2\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),

(S16)

with
U_{s}
the spin interaction energy. However, a one-to-one mapping between the two Hamiltonians does not exist, as
U,J
and
\delta
in Eq. (
S15
) cannot be tuned to match the corresponding terms in Eq. (
S16
); in this sense, the 3-mode tilted Bose-Hubbard model is not a direct analogue of spin-1 Bose gases.

S4
D. Quantum optimal control of
L
-tuple Fock states

Given a dynamical system described by the state vector
\vec{x}(t)
(
0\leq t\leq t_{\text{f}}
), and whose evolution is parameterized by control parameters
\vec{u}(t)
, optimal control
[
81
,
82
,
83
,
84
]
is the mathematical formalism that allows to determine
\vec{u}(t)
in order to
optimally
control
\vec{x}(t)
. In the framework of optimal control,
optimality
is captured by a cost function
\mathcal{C}
to be minimized. By virtue of the Pontryagin Maximum Principle, the optimal control
\vec{u}(t)
is the one that maximizes the Pontryagin Hamiltonian
H_{\text{P}}(t),\;\forall t\in[0,t_{\text{f}}]
(see below, and Refs.
[
81
,
82
,
84
,
48
]
). Quantum optimal control
[
46
,
62
,
47
,
48
]
is the application of optimal control theory to quantum systems, with typically
\vec{x}(t)\rightarrow\ket{\psi(t)}
.

S4.1
a. Implementation

In this work, we use the energy bias
\delta(t)
between the
L
-modes of a Bose-Hubbard system as our control parameter to optimally steer the ground state of the system (taken at
\delta=0
with
U/J
fixed) towards the
L
-tuple Fock state
\ket{N/L}
in a fixed time
t_{\text{f}}
. We do not place any constraint on
\delta(t)
. The cost function that we minimize is
\mathcal{C}=1-\mathcal{F}
, with
\mathcal{F}=|\innerproduct{N/L}{\psi(t_\df)}|^{2}
, leading to the following definition of the Pontryagin Hamiltonian
[
84
,
63
,
85
]
:

H_{\text{P}}(t)=\Re{\bra{\chi(t)}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi(t)}}=\dfrac{1}{\hbar}\Im{\bra{\chi(t)}\hH(\delta(t))\ket{\psi(t)}},

(S17)

using Schrödinger equation with the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
given in Eqs. (
S10
) and (
S13
) for
L\!=2
and 3 respectively. In Eq. (
S17
),
\ket{\psi(t)}
is state during its preparation and
\ket{\chi(t)}
is a non-normalized adjoint state, also solution of Schrödinger equation and defined by the final condition:

\innerproduct{u_n}{\chi(t_\df)}=\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\left(\innerproduct{\psi(t_\df)}{u_n}\right)},

(S18)

in any basis
\left\{\ket{u_n}\right\}
, giving
\ket{\chi(t_\df)}=\innerproduct{N/L}{\psi(t_\df)}\ket{N/L}
.
Given an non-optimal control
\delta(t)
, the first-order correction
\Delta\delta(t)
required to increase
H_{\text{P}}
(and thus
\mathcal{F}
) is proportional to the derivative of

S5
E. Number fluctuations of reference

We derive, for
L\!=\!2
and 3, the uncorrelated number fluctuations
\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\text{ref}}
used as the reference value when computing the number squeezing parameter
\xi^{2}_{N}(\psi)=\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\psi}/\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\text{ref}}
. In each case considered, the reference state that is taken corresponds to the ground state of the hopping Hamiltonian with
N
bosons.

S5.1
a. Uncorrelated number fluctuations for
L\!=\!2

The reference state is the coherent spin state given in Eq. (
S3
) for
n=0
, explicitly

\ket{\phi^{(0)}_0}=\dfrac{\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\right)^{N}}{2^{N/2}\sqrt{N!}}\ket{0,0}.

(S20)

Number fluctuations for that state are given by the variance on
\hat{J}_{z}
:
\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\text{ref}}=\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{\text{ref}}-\langle\hat{J}_{z}\rangle^{2}_{\text{ref}}
, with
\hat{J}_{z}=(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{2})/2
. As the reference state lies symmetrically on the equator of the Bloch sphere, the first moment of
\hat{J}_{z}
is zero:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{z}\rangle_{\text{ref}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{N+1}N!}\bra{0,0}\left(\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}_{2}\right)^{N}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}\right)\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\right)^{N}\ket{0,0}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{N+1}N!}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}\sqrt{(N-n)!n!}\bra{N-n,n}\right)\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}\right)\left(\sum_{m=0}^{N}\binom{N}{m}\sqrt{(N-m)!m!}\ket{N-m,m}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{N+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}(N-2n)=0,

(S21)

where the last equality is evaluated using the following identities on the sum of the binomial coefficients:

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}=2^{N}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{N}n\binom{N}{n}=2^{N-1}N.

(S22)

Number fluctuations are thus given by the second moment of
\hat{J}_{z}
, that is

\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\text{ref}}=\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{\text{ref}}=\dfrac{1}{2^{N+2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}(N-2n)^{2}=\dfrac{N}{4},

(S23)

using Eqs.
S22
and

\sum_{n=0}^{N}n^{2}\binom{N}{n}=2^{N-2}N(N+1).

(S24)

The result in Eq. (
S23
) is well known, see e.g. Refs.
[
68
,
5
]

S5.2
b. Uncorrelated number fluctuations for
L\!=\!3

For
L=3
, we evaluate the number fluctuations
partially
, via the variance of the population imbalance between modes 1 and 3:
\hat{J}_{z}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{3}

[
6
,
20
]
. The hopping Hamiltonian (without coupling between modes 1 and 3) is

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=-J\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{3}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\hat{a}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}J\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{+}\hat{a}_{+}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-}\hat{a}_{-}\right),

(S25)

with
\hat{a}_{\pm}=(\hat{a}_{1}\pm\sqrt{2}\hat{a}_{2}+\hat{a}_{3})/2
. The reference state for number fluctuations is the ground state of Hamiltonian (
S25
), namely

\displaystyle\ket{\phi^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\pm\sqrt{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{3}\right)^{N}}{2^{N}\sqrt{N!}}\ket{0,0,0}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2^{N}\sqrt{N!}}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}2^{(N-n)/2}\sqrt{(N-n)!}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom{n}{r}\sqrt{(n-r)!r!}\ket{n-r,N-n,r},

(S26)

which is not a coherent spin state in the absence of coupling between modes 1 and 3. For that state (having equal populations in modes 1 and 3), the first moment of
\hat{J}_{z}
is zero:

\langle\hat{J}_{z}\rangle_{\text{ref}}=\dfrac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}\dfrac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom{n}{r}(n-2r)=0,

(S27)

as the second sum is null from Eqs. (
S22
). The variance of
\hat{J}_{z}
is thus

\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\text{ref}}=\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle_{\text{ref}}=\dfrac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}\dfrac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom{n}{r}(n-2r)^{2}=\dfrac{N}{2},

(S28)

using Eqs. (
S22
) and (
S24
).

For completeness, we give that the uncorrelated number fluctuations for
L\!=\!3

with
coupling between modes 1 and 3 (i.e. considering a coherent spin state, e.g. the ground state of a ring of three spatial sites) are given by
\Delta\hat{J}^{2}_{z,\text{ref}}=2N/3
.

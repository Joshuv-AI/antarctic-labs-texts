Abstract
. We study time integration schemes for H ˙ 1 \dot{H}^{1} -solutions to the energy-(sub)critical semilinear wave equation on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . We show first-order convergence in L 2 L^{2} for the Lie splitting and convergence order 3 / 2 3/2 for a corrected Lie splitting. To our knowledge this includes the first error analysis performed for scaling-critical dispersive problems. Our approach is based on discrete-time Strichartz estimates, including one (with a logarithmic correction) for the case of the forbidden endpoint. Our schemes and the Strichartz estimates contain frequency cut-offs.

1.
Introduction

The semilinear wave equation
\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u=\pm|u|^{\alpha-1}u
is one of the most important model problems for dispersive behavior. Its analytical properties are well understood, see
[
23
]
for a survey. In view of the energy equality,
H^{1}
(or the homogeneous version
\dot{H}^{1}
) is the most natural regularity level for solutions
u(t)
and data.

On 3D-domains, in the case of powers
\alpha\in(1,3]
one can study wellposedness by means of the standard tools of evolution equations, whereas the treatment of the case
\alpha\in(3,5]
is based on dispersive properties. To our knowledge, in numerical analysis the latter situation has not been studied in this setting so far. (Compare
[
19
]
for the case
\alpha\in(1,3)
.) The strategy of the error analysis for such problems goes back to the seminal paper
[
14
]
in the case of the semilinear Schrödinger equation. In this work we establish the first error bounds for time integration schemes for the semilinear wave equation in the case
\alpha\in[3,5]
with finite-energy data on the full space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

In the present paper we investigate the equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u

\displaystyle=\Delta u-\mu|u|^{\alpha-1}u,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle=u^{0},\qquad\partial_{t}u(0)=v^{0},

with finite-energy initial data
(u^{0},v^{0})\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, as well as parameters
\mu\in\{-1,1\}
and
\alpha\in[3,5]
. In this setting, a complete local wellposedness theory in the space
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
(u,\partial_{t}u)
has been established, where global existence is known for the defocusing case
\mu=1
. If
\alpha>5
, the problem is ill-posed at least in the focusing case
\mu=-1
. The energy-critical case
\alpha=5
is much more challenging than the subcritical range
\alpha<5
. The theory is based on Strichartz estimates for the linear problem, see the monographs
[
20
]
and
[
23
]
.

To establish error bounds for time integration schemes, we show various discrete-time Strichartz estimates in Section
2
. Here one controls discrete-time points
(u(n\tau))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the solutions to the linear problem in spaces like
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z},\ell^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
by
L^{2}
-based norms of the initial data, where
\tau\in(0,1]
is the time-step size. It is easy to see that a naive discrete-time version of results in continuous time fails. Instead, one has to introduce frequency cut-offs
\pi_{K}
at level
K\geq 1
; i.e.,
\pi_{K}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\xi|\leq K\}}\mathcal{F}
with the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
. The estimates then depend on
K\tau
, but are otherwise in complete analogy with the estimates in continuous time. Similar results for the Schrödinger equation have been obtained in
[
9
]
and
[
16
]
, see also
[
17
]
and
[
15
]
for the case of periodic boundary conditions. Moreover, Strichartz estimates for spatially discrete Schrödinger equations were treated in the seminal works
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
. In contrast to the Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in the wave case one
has to work with frequency-localized estimates and the Littlewood–Paley decomposition already for the basic Strichartz inqualities. In Theorem
2.10
we also derive local-in-time estimates at the forbidden endpoint
(p,q)=(2,\infty)
with an additional logarithmic correction depending on
K
and the end-time
T
. Such an inequality was shown in
[
12
]
for continuous time.

The frequency cut-off has then to be introduced in the time integration schemes, too (as in the Schrödinger case, see
[
9
]
,
[
5
]
, and
[
16
]
). We first analyze the frequency-filtered Lie splitting scheme

(1.2)

\displaystyle U_{n+1}

\displaystyle=e^{\tau A}\big[U_{n}-\tau\big(0,\pi_{\tau^{-1}}(\mu|u_{n}|^{\alpha-1}u_{n})\big)\big],

\displaystyle U_{0}

\displaystyle=(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u^{0},\pi_{\tau^{-1}}v^{0})

for
U_{n}=(u_{n},v_{n})
and the wave operator
A(u,v)=(v,\Delta u)
,
see (
3.2
) and (
4.1
). We show first-order convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for data in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. In contrast to previous works on time discretization of semilinear wave equations in low-regularity regime, such as
[
8
]
,
[
3
]
or
[
13
]
, in our setting there is no uniform spacetime
L^{\infty}
-bound on the solution
u
available, since in three dimensions the Sobolev embedding
H^{s}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
requires
s>3/2
, but we only assume
\dot{H}^{1}
regularity of
u
. Instead, our error analysis is based on discrete-time Strichartz estimates. We use ideas from
[
9
]
,
[
5
]
, and
[
16
]
, where a similar analysis was performed in the case of the subcritical semilinear Schrödinger equation. However, because of the change to a second-order problem the analysis differs in many points.

We treat the subcritical and energy-critical cases separately, since the latter requires a much more delicate analysis.
Our convergence result for the subcritical case
\alpha<5
reads as follows. It is proved at the end of Section
4
, see also Remark
3.4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha<5
and
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)\in C([0,T],\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
solve the semilinear wave equation (
1.1
). Then there are a constant
C>0
and a maximum step size
\tau_{0}>0
such that the iterates
U_{n}
of the filtered Lie splitting scheme (
1.2
) or (
4.1
) satisfy the error bound

\|U(n\tau)-U_{n}\|_{L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}}\leq C\tau

for all
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0}]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
n\tau\leq T
. The numbers
C
and
\tau_{0}
only depend on
T
,
\alpha
, and
\|U\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}
.

For the critical case
\alpha=5
, the next result is shown at the end of Section
5
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha=5
and
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)\in C([0,T],\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with
u\in L^{4}([0,T],L^{12}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
solve the semilinear wave equation (
1.1
). Then there are a constant
C>0
and a maximum step size
\tau_{0}>0
such that the iterates
U_{n}
of the filtered Lie splitting scheme (
4.1
) satisfy the error bound

\|U(n\tau)-U_{n}\|_{L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}}\leq C\tau

for all
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0}]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
n\tau\leq T
. The number
C
only depends on
T
,
\|U\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}
, and
\|u\|_{L^{4}([0,T],L^{12}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}
, whereas
\tau_{0}
only depends on
T
,
u^{0}
, and
v^{0}
.

The more sophisticated analysis for
\alpha=5
is reflected by the dependence of the maximum step size
\tau_{0}
on the solution itself, rather than just on its norm. A similar behavior occurs in the wellposedness theory, see
[
23
]
. To our knowledge, this above theorem provides the first error analysis of a time discretization for a scaling-critical problem.

As a first step to higher-order schemes, in the case
\alpha=3
we study a corrected Lie splitting given in (
6.3
), where we now take the cut-off level
K=\tau^{-3/2}
. A version of the scheme without frequency filter was recently proposed and analyzed in
[
13
]
in higher regularity
H^{7/4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. We can show convergence order
\tau^{3/2}
for the error in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, again using data in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Formally, the scheme is of second order due to a well-chosen correction term. This term also leads to an error formula without second-order derivatives. The loss of
\tau^{1/2}
in our result corresponds to the loss in the Strichartz estimates, so that we believe our result is optimal, compare also
[
16
]
. In the proof we need the endpoint estimates from Theorem
2.10
. The convergence result is shown at the end of Section
6
. Here we use the endpoint versions of Strichartz estimates with logarithmic corrections from Theorem
2.10
, which seem to be new tools in the error analysis.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)\in C([0,T],\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
solve the semilinear wave equation (
1.1
)
with
\alpha=3
. Then there are a constant
C>0
and a maximum step size
\tau_{0}>0
such that the iterates
U_{n}
of the corrected Lie splitting scheme (
6.3
) with
K=\tau^{-3/2}
satisfy the error bound

\|U(n\tau)-U_{n}\|_{L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}}\leq C\tau^{\frac{3}{2}}

for all
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0}]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
n\tau\leq T
. The numbers
C
and
\tau_{0}
only depend on
T
and
\|U\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the defocusing case
\mu=1
, energy conservation shows that the solutions to (
1.1
) exist globally in time. Moreover, the numbers
C
and
\tau_{0}
from Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, as well as the number
C
from Theorem
1.2
, then only depend on
T
,
\alpha
,
\|\nabla u^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
, and
\|v^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
. See Remarks
3.2
and
3.4
.

We comment on variants of our results. A straightforward extension is possible to more general nonlinearities with the same growth behavior, and also to the Klein–Gordon case. Furthermore, exploiting the finite speed of propagation for the wave equation, our analysis remains valid if one replaces the spatial domain
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by the torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
(where the Strichartz estimates only hold locally in time). In this setting the above schemes become fully discrete. They are studied in more detail
in on-going work by the first-named author.
In (most of) these cases, one would have to work with inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces instead of homogeneous (dotted) ones.

We do not analyze the Strang splitting (which is of second order formally) since a preliminary analysis indicates that one only obtains convergence order one for
\dot{H}^{1}
-solutions in the critical case
\alpha=5
. For
\alpha=3
, an order
\tau^{4/3}
seems to be feasible using Strichartz estimates. However, this is inferior to the estimate for the corrected Lie splitting in Theorem
1.3
.
In
[
8
]
and
[
3
]
, second-order convergence of the Strang splitting and closely related
trigonometric integrators
has been shown in case of one space dimension or
H^{2}
-solutions.

Our analysis does not distinguish between the focusing and defocusing cases. Better results could be possible in the defocusing case, where the energy dominates the
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm. For example, an error analysis on unbounded time intervals was done in
[
4
]
in case of Schrödinger equations (under the additional assumption that the initial value lies in the
conformal space
).

In Section
2
we establish the needed discrete-time Strichartz estimates, adapting ideas from the continuous time. The local wellposedness theory is recalled in Section
3
. We introduce the Lie splitting with frequency cut-off in Section
4
, and then derive the core error formula (
4.1
). After estimating the error terms in the subcritical case by means of our Strichartz estimates, we show Theorem
1.1
using a double induction, first iterating within a possible small time interval of size
T_{1}
, and then performing a recursion over intervals of length
T_{1}
. The critical case is studied in Section
5
. Here we use in addition convergence results for
H^{2}
-solutions in order to make sure that the discrete approximation stays close to the PDE in
\dot{H}^{1}
. Moreover, in the argument enters how fast the Strichartz norm
\ell^{4}L^{12}
of a time-discrete solution gets small on small time intervals. The last section is devoted to the corrected Lie splitting which has a more sophisticated error formula (
6.1
) and thus requires additional estimates of error terms.

Notation.
We write
A\lesssim B
(or
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
) if
A\leq cB
for a generic constant
c\geq 0
(depending on quantities
\alpha
). Since we always work on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we abbreviate
L^{p}
for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
etc. We write
\mathcal{F}
for the Fourier transform, where we use the convention with the prefactor
(2\pi)^{-3/2}
. We also use the notation
\hat{u}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}u
. In the context of Fourier multipliers, we often just write
\xi
instead of the map
\xi\mapsto\xi
. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we use the inhomogeneous and homogeneous Sobolev norms

\|w\|_{H^{s}}=\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\hat{w}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\||\xi|^{s}\hat{w}\|_{L^{2}},

if
\hat{w}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. The homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}
is defined as

\dot{H}^{s}\coloneqq\{w\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\hat{w}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}\ \text{and}\ \|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}<\infty\}.

This space is a Hilbert space if and only if
s<3/2
due to Proposition 1.34 of
[
1
]
. Moreover, Schwartz functions with compact Fourier support in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
are dense in
\dot{H}^{s}
if
s<3/2
, cf. Proposition 1.35 of
[
1
]
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
J
be a time interval and
X
be a Banach space. We use the Bochner spaces
L^{p}_{J}X\coloneqq L^{p}(J,X)
with norms

\|F\|_{L^{p}_{J}X}=\Big(\int_{J}\|F(t)\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and the usual modification for
p=\infty
. In the case
J=[0,T]
we also write
T
instead of
J
. If a “free” variable
t
appears in such a Bochner norm, the time integration is taken with respect to
t
. Further we denote by
\ell^{p}\coloneqq\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
the sequence spaces over the integers and abbreviate
\ell^{p}X\coloneqq\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z},X)
in case of Banach space valued sequences. In order to simplify notation we often write
\|F_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}X}
instead of
\|(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell^{p}X}
, where again a “free” variable
n
is assumed to be the summation variable. For a stepsize
\tau>0
and a number
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we further introduce scaled norms

\|F\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}X}=\|F_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}X}\coloneqq\Big(\tau\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|F_{n}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and the truncated variant

\|F\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}X}=\|F_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}X}\coloneqq\Big(\tau\sum_{n=0}^{N}\|F_{n}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that in the case
p=\infty
, the norm

\|F\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\tau}X}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|F_{n}\|_{X}

does not depend on
\tau
. For intervals
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
we also use the notation

\|F\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,X)}=\|F_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,X)}\coloneqq\Big(\tau\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\tau\in J\end{subarray}}\|F_{n}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

2.
Strichartz estimates

Our analysis is based on time-discrete Strichartz estimates for the wave equation, which are established in this section.
We start with some standard definitions and results regarding the time-continuous case.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a radial function with
\chi=1
on
B(0,1)
,
\operatorname{supp}\chi\subseteq B(0,2)
and

\psi(\xi)\coloneqq\chi(\xi)-\chi(2\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

For every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we define

\psi_{j}(\xi)\coloneqq\psi\Big(\frac{\xi}{2^{j}}\Big),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad\text{and}\quad P_{j}u\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\psi_{j}\hat{u}),\quad u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}.

These definitions yield
\operatorname{supp}\psi_{j}\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}\}
and the identity

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{j}(\xi)=1,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}.

We recall that the “Littlewood–Paley projections”
P_{j}
are bounded in
L^{p}
uniformly in
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
.

We define the operator
|\nabla|=\sqrt{-\Delta}
via the Fourier multiplier
|\nabla|f\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|\hat{f})
, and analogously for
\cos(t|\nabla|)
etc. To solve the wave equation, we will use the half wave group

e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}e^{\mathrm{i}t|\xi|}\mathcal{F}.

From Proposition III.1.5 of
[
20
]
, we recall the kernel bound

(2.1)

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\mathrm{i}t|\xi|}\psi)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-1},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

The proof of the Strichartz estimates is based on the following known frequency-localized dispersive inequality. For convenience, we show how it follows from (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.2
.

It holds

\|P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim 2^{3j(1-\frac{2}{q})}(1+2^{j}|t|)^{-(1-\frac{2}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
q\in[2,\infty]
, and
f\in L^{q^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let first
f\in L^{1}
. Young’s convolution inequality yields

\|P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\mathrm{i}t|\xi|}\psi_{j})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{L^{1}},

where we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\mathrm{i}t|\xi|}\psi_{j})(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot\xi}e^{\mathrm{i}t|\xi|}\psi(2^{-j}\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=2^{3j}(2\pi)^{-3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\mathrm{i}2^{j}x\cdot\eta}e^{\mathrm{i}2^{j}t|\eta|}\psi(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=2^{3j}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{\mathrm{i}2^{j}t|\xi|}\psi)(2^{j}x).

Estimate (
2.1
) now gives

\|P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{3j}(1+2^{j}|t|)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

The assertion follows by interpolation with the
L^{2}
-bound

\|P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}}
, which is a consequence of Plancherel’s theorem.
∎

We now state some of the Strichartz estimates for the wave equation to provide a background for our results. The next theorem follows from Corollary IV.1.2 in
[
20
]
combined with formula (
3.5
) below. We call a triple
(p,q,\gamma)

wave admissible
(in dimension three) if
p\in(2,\infty]
,
q\in[2,\infty)
, and

(2.2)

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{2},\qquad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{3}{2}-\gamma.

One then has
\gamma\in[0,\frac{3}{2})
, and the equality in (
2.2
) is called
scaling condition
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(p,q,\gamma)
be wave admissible for dimension three.
Then we have the estimates

\displaystyle\|e^{\pm\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{R}}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}},

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\pm\mathrm{i}(t-s)|\nabla|}F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{R}}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\|F\|_{L^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{H}^{\gamma}}

for all
f\in\dot{H}^{\gamma}
and
F\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{H}^{\gamma}
.

Observe that the triple
(\infty,2,0)
corresponds to the usual energy estimate. In the above inequalities one increases the space integrability paying a price in regularity and time integrability. For the last inhomogeneous estimate, there are also variants involving
L^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}
-norms instead of the
L^{1}L^{2}
-norm. Moreover, for triples
(p,\infty,\gamma)
with
p>2
that satisfy (
2.2
), the above estimates remain true if one replaces
L^{\infty}
by
\dot{B}^{0}_{2,\infty}
. Since we will not need these facts, we omit them for simplicity.

2.1.
Time-discrete estimates

Now we turn our attention to the time-discrete setting. In the following
\ell^{p}L^{q}
estimates, the
\ell^{p}
-summation is always taken over the variable
n
. We start with frequency-localized inequalities.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(p,q,\gamma)
be wave admissible. Then the estimates

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k)|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}2^{2j\gamma}(2^{\frac{2j}{p}}+1)\|F\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{j}e^{-\mathrm{i}k|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}2^{j\gamma}(2^{\frac{j}{p}}+1)\|F\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}2^{j\gamma}(2^{\frac{j}{p}}+1)\|P_{j}f\|_{L^{2}}

hold for all
F\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}
,
f\in L^{2}
, and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We first deduce from Lemma
2.2
the estimate

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k)|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k)|\nabla|}F_{k}\|_{L^{q}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{3j(1-\frac{2}{q})}\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\|F_{k}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}}{(1+2^{j}|n-k|)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2j(\frac{1}{p}+\gamma)}\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\|F_{k}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}}{(1+2^{j}|n-k|)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}},

where the last inequality follows from the admissibility conditions (
2.2
). The first assertion for
p=\infty
is now clear. For
p<\infty
we compute

\displaystyle 2

{}^{2j(\frac{1}{p}+\gamma)}\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\|F_{k}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}}{(1+2^{j}|n-k|)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2j(\frac{1}{p}+\gamma)}\Big(\Big\|\|F_{n}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}}+\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}\\
k\neq n\end{subarray}}\frac{\|F_{k}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}}{(1+2^{j}|n-k|)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2j\gamma}\Big(2^{\frac{2j}{p}}\|F\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}+\Big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}\\
k\neq n\end{subarray}}\frac{\|F_{k}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}}{|n-k|^{\frac{2}{p}}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}2^{2j\gamma}(2^{\frac{2j}{p}}+1)\|F\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}

with the help of the discrete Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality (see Proposition (a) in
[
21
]
). We note that in the case
n=k
the factor
2^{2j/p}
does not cancel. This is the main difference to the continuous case, where such a term does not appear in the continuous Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality. This proves (
2.3
).

The other two claims follow by a standard
TT^{*}
argument that we sketch. Using that
P_{j}e^{-\mathrm{i}k|\nabla|}=e^{-\mathrm{i}k|\nabla|}P_{j}
, from (
2.3
) we derive

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{j}e^{-\mathrm{i}k|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\langle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k)|\nabla|}F_{k},P_{j}F_{n}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}2^{2j\gamma}(2^{\frac{2j}{p}}+1)\|F\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}^{2},

implying (
2.4
). Here we write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the
L^{2}
-inner product. By duality, it follows the estimate

(2.6)

\|P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim_{p,q}2^{j\gamma}(2^{\frac{j}{p}}+1)\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

To recover
P_{j}
on the right-hand side, we use the fattened Littlewood–Paley projection
\widetilde{P}_{j}\coloneqq P_{j-1}+P_{j}+P_{j+1}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, noting that
\widetilde{P}_{j}P_{j}=P_{j}
. Clearly, (
2.6
) also holds with
\widetilde{P}_{j}
instead of
P_{j}
. In this inequality, we then replace
f
by
P_{j}f
to obtain the last assertion (
2.5
).
∎

To deal with the additional factor
2^{\frac{j}{p}}+1
, we include a frequency cut-off in the discrete Strichartz estimates. For each
K\geq 1
we define the Fourier multiplier

(2.7)

\pi_{K}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,K)}\mathcal{F}.

By Plancherel’s theorem, the operators
\pi_{K}
are clearly uniformly bounded in
K
on every
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space.

We can now show the desired discrete Strichartz estimates. We stress that these estimates fail without the cut-off if
p<\infty
. For instance, take a function
f\in\dot{H}^{\gamma}\setminus L^{q}
in (
2.8
). On the other hand, for
f\in\dot{H}^{\gamma}
the map
\pi_{K}f
belongs to all
L^{r}
with
r\geq q_{0}
and
3/2-\gamma=3/q_{0}
by Sobolev’s embedding and Bernstein’s inequality.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(p,q,\gamma)
be wave admissible. Then we have the estimates

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Big\|\tau\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n-1}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k)\tau|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|F\|_{\ell_{\tau}^{1}\dot{H}^{\gamma}}

for all
\tau\in(0,1]
,
K\geq\tau^{-1}
,
f\in\dot{H}^{\gamma}
, and
F\in\ell^{1}\dot{H}^{\gamma}
.

Proof.

By approximation, it is enough to take Schwartz functions and finitely supported sequences.
A scaling argument reduces the estimates to the case
\tau=1
. Indeed, we can write

(2.10)

\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}f=D_{\tau^{-1}}\pi_{K\tau}e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{t}{\tau}|\nabla|}D_{\tau}f,

where the spatial dilation operator
D_{a}
is given by
(D_{a}f)(x)\coloneqq f(ax)
. Assuming the case
\tau=1
is shown, we get the general case

\displaystyle\|\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}L^{q}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}\|D_{\tau^{-1}}\pi_{K\tau}e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}D_{\tau}f\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}=\tau^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}}\|\pi_{K\tau}e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}D_{\tau}f\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\tau^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|D_{\tau}f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}=\tau^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}-\frac{3}{2}+\gamma}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}

by the scaling condition in (
2.2
), and similarly for the inhomogeneous estimate.

So let
\tau=1
and
K\geq 1
. By means of the Littlewood–Paley square function estimate, Minkowski’s inequality, and Lemma
2.4
, we compute

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|P_{j}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}f|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}\leq\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|P_{j}e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}\pi_{K}f\|^{2}_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{j\gamma}(2^{\frac{j}{p}}+1)P_{j}\pi_{K}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{j\gamma}P_{j}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}},

also using that
P_{j}\pi_{K}=0
for
K\lesssim 2^{j}
. Thus, the homogeneous estimate (
2.8
) is true. By duality, we infer the dual homogeneous estimate

(2.11)

\Big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{K}e^{-ik|\nabla|}G_{k}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\lesssim_{p,q}K^{\frac{1}{p}}\|G\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}

that is valid for all
G\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}
.

The truncated inhomogeneous estimate (
2.9
) is proven by another duality argument via

\displaystyle\Big\|

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n-1}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k)|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|G\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\langle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n-1}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k)|\nabla|}F_{k},G_{n}\Big\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|G\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\Big|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\langle F_{k},\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(k-n)|\nabla|}G_{n}\Big\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{\ell^{1}\dot{H}^{\gamma}}\sup_{\|G\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(k-n)|\nabla|}G_{n}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}.

Using (
2.11
), the assertion now follows from

\displaystyle\sup_{\|G\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}\leq 1}

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(k-n)|\nabla|}G_{n}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|G\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{K}e^{-\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}\mathbbm{1}_{\{n\geq k+1\}}G_{n}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}K^{\frac{1}{p}}\sup_{\|G\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\{n\geq k+1\}}G_{n}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}L^{q^{\prime}}}=K^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Alternatively, one could also employ the Christ–Kiselev Lemma
2.11
below to deduce the inhomogeneous estimate from the homogeneous one.
∎

We discuss some variants of the above results and proofs.

Remark 2.6
.

a) Using the Bernstein inequality
\|\pi_{K}f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}\lesssim K^{\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{2}}
that holds for every
L^{2}
-function
f
and
\gamma\geq 0
, we can convert the derivative loss from the discrete Strichartz estimates into a multiplicative factor of the form
K^{\gamma}
. This gives e.g.

\displaystyle\|\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}K^{\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

and similarly for the inhomogeneous estimates.

b) In all the previous estimates we can replace the plus sign in the exponential by a minus sign (e.g.,
e^{-\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}
instead of
e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}
) since we can instead of
f
resp.
F
always use their complex conjugates and
\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,K)}(\xi)=\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,K)}(-\xi)
. This modification is employed below without further notice.

c) In our applications, we will always deal with finite sequences, defined on some set
\{0,\dots,N\}
for an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. In that case, the second estimate of Theorem
2.5
takes the form

\Big\|\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k)\tau|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}L^{q}}\lesssim_{p,q}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|F\|_{\ell_{\tau,N-1}^{1}\dot{H}^{\gamma}},

where on the right-hand side we only need to consider the index range
\{0,\dots,N-1\}
.

Remark 2.7
.

There exists an alternative (simpler) approach to time-discrete Strichartz estimates, which uses the well-known continuous estimates just as a “black box”. In the context of Schrödinger equations, it was used in Lemma 2.6 of the recent preprint
[
25
]
, see also Lemma 2.1 of
[
22
]
for a similar technique. But this approach yields a weaker estimate compared to Theorem
2.5
in the case when
K>\tau^{-1}
. We give the details. For technical reasons, here we have to replace the frequency cut-off
\pi_{K}
by a version
\widetilde{\pi}_{K}
with a smooth cut-off function (similar as the Littlewood–Paley projections from Definition
2.1
). Let first
\tau=1
and
K>0
be arbitrary. For a function
f\in\dot{H}^{\gamma}
, we get

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}\widetilde{\pi}_{K}f\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{n-1}^{n}\|e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}\widetilde{\pi}_{K}f\|^{p}_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{n-1}^{n}\|(e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}-e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|})\widetilde{\pi}_{K}f\|^{p}_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{n-1}^{n}\|e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}\widetilde{\pi}_{K}f\|^{p}_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Note that the last term is equal to
\|e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}\widetilde{\pi}_{K}f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}^{p}
, therefore it can be treated directly by the continuous Strichartz estimate from Theorem
2.3
. The first term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{n-1}^{n}\|(e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}-e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|})\widetilde{\pi}_{K}f\|^{p}_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{n-1}^{n}\Big\|\int_{t}^{n}\mathrm{i}|\nabla|e^{\mathrm{i}\sigma|\nabla|}\widetilde{\pi}_{K}f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\Big\|^{p}_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{n-1}^{n}\int_{n-1}^{n}\||\nabla|e^{\mathrm{i}\sigma|\nabla|}\widetilde{\pi}_{K}f\|^{p}_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}K^{p}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{n-1}^{n}\|e^{\mathrm{i}\sigma|\nabla|}f\|^{p}_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where we used Bernstein’s inequality and the frequency cut-off
\widetilde{\pi}_{K}
to get rid of the differential operator. In this step it would be necessary to use
\widetilde{\pi}_{K}
with a smooth cut-off function. Now we are again in a position to apply Theorem
2.3
. Altogether, this gives the estimate

\|\widetilde{\pi}_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim_{p,q}(1+K)\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}.

A scaling argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
then yields the estimate

\|\widetilde{\pi}_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}L^{q}}\lesssim_{p,q}(1+K\tau)\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}},

for general
\tau>0
.
We see that this estimate is inferior to Theorem
2.5
if
K>\tau^{-1}
, but for
K=\tau^{-1}
they are the same.

2.2.
Endpoint estimates with logarithmic loss

The Strichartz estimates from Theorem
2.3
and
2.5
are in general wrong if
(p,q,\gamma)=(2,\infty,1)
, see
[
22
]
or Exercise 2.44 in
[
23
]
for a discussion. In this section we show a local-in-time estimate with logarithmic loss, which will be used to discuss the corrected Lie splitting in Section
6
. We follow the approach from Section 8 in
[
12
]
and transfer it to the time-discrete setting. First, we need two lemmas with basic estimates. The first one is contained in the proof of Lemma 8.1 in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

The function

M\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C},\quad M(\lambda,z)\coloneqq\int_{B(0,1)}|\xi|^{-2}\cos(\lambda|\xi|)e^{\mathrm{i}z\cdot\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

satisfies the decay estimate

|M(\lambda,z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{1+|\lambda|}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

The function

A\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R},\quad A(\beta,n,j)\coloneqq\Big(\frac{1}{1+\beta|n-j|}+\frac{1}{1+\beta(n+j)}\Big)

satisfies the inequality

\max_{j=0,\dots,N}\sum_{n=0}^{N}A(\beta,n,j)\lesssim 1+\beta^{-1}\log(1+N\beta)

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\beta>0
.

Proof.

Let
j\in\{0,\dots,N\}
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{1}{1+\beta(n+j)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{1}{1+\beta n}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+\beta n}\leq 1+\beta^{-1}\int_{0}^{N\beta}\frac{1}{1+t}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=1+\beta^{-1}\log(1+N\beta)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{1}{1+\beta|n-j|}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{1+\beta(j-n)}+\sum_{n=j+1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+\beta(n-j)}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{j}\frac{1}{1+\beta n}+\sum_{n=1}^{N-j}\frac{1}{1+\beta n}\leq 1+2\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+\beta n}

\displaystyle\leq 1+2\beta^{-1}\log(1+N\beta).\qed

Now we show the announced time-discrete endpoint estimates with logarithmic loss.

Theorem 2.10
.

The estimates

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{K\tau+\log(1+KN\tau)}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\Big\|\tau\sum_{k=0}^{N}\pi_{K}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\tau|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{K\tau+\log(1+KN\tau)}\|F\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N}L^{1}}

hold for all
\tau\in(0,1]
,
K\geq\tau^{-1}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
f\in\dot{H}^{1}
, and
F\in\ell^{2}L^{1}
.

Proof.

By duality, the two estimates are equivalent. Due to a scaling argument, for (
2.12
) it is enough to show

(2.14)

\|\pi_{1}e^{\mathrm{i}n\beta|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{1,N}L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sqrt{1+\beta^{-1}\log(1+N\beta)}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

for any
\beta>0
. Indeed, this estimate and (
2.10
) imply

\displaystyle\|\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D_{K}\pi_{1}e^{\mathrm{i}nK\tau|\nabla|}D_{K^{-1}}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{1,N}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\pi_{1}e^{\mathrm{i}nK\tau|\nabla|}D_{K^{-1}}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{1,N}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{1+(K\tau)^{-1}\log(1+NK\tau)}\|D_{K^{-1}}f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle=(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{1+(K\tau)^{-1}\log(1+NK\tau)}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{K\tau+\log(1+NK\tau)}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

We show (
2.14
) via the dual estimate

(2.15)

\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{N}\pi_{1}e^{-\mathrm{i}n\beta|\nabla|}F_{n}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\lesssim\sqrt{1+\beta^{-1}\log(1+N\beta)}\|F\|_{\ell^{2}_{1,N}L^{1}}

for
F\in\ell^{2}L^{1}
. Instead of the exponential, we treat sine and cosine. From the definition of the
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm and Fubini’s theorem, we deduce

\displaystyle\Big\|

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\pi_{1}\sin(n\beta|\nabla|)F_{n}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}=\Big\||\xi|^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}\sin(n\beta|\xi|)\hat{F}_{n}(\xi)\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,1)}|\xi|^{-2}\sum_{n,j=0}^{N}\sin(n\beta|\xi|)\sin(j\beta|\xi|)\hat{F}_{n}(\xi)\overline{\hat{F}_{j}(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{B(0,1)}|\xi|^{-2}\sum_{n,j=0}^{N}\sin(n\beta|\xi|)\sin(j\beta|\xi|)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\mathrm{i}(y-x)\cdot\xi}F_{n}(x)\overline{F_{j}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-3}\sum_{n,j=0}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G_{-}(n\beta,j\beta,y-x)F_{n}(x)\overline{F_{j}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

where

G_{-}(a,b,z)\coloneqq\int_{B(0,1)}|\xi|^{-2}\sin(a|\xi|)\sin(b|\xi|)e^{\mathrm{i}z\cdot\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Analogously, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\pi_{1}\cos(n\beta|\nabla|)F_{n}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-3}\sum_{n,j=0}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G_{+}(n\beta,j\beta,y-x)F_{n}(x)\overline{F_{j}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

with

G_{+}(a,b,z)\coloneqq\int_{B(0,1)}|\xi|^{-2}\cos(a|\xi|)\cos(b|\xi|)e^{\mathrm{i}z\cdot\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Trigonometric identities
yield

G_{\pm}(a,b,z)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(M(a-b,z)\pm M(a+b,z)\Big)

with
M(\lambda,z)
from Lemma
2.8
. Combined with this lemma, the above equations lead to

\displaystyle\Big\|

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\pi_{1}e^{-\mathrm{i}n\beta|\nabla|}F_{n}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n,j=0}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(|M(\beta(n-j),y-x)|+|M(\beta(n+j),y-x)|\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\cdot|F_{n}(x)||F_{j}(y)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n,j=0}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\frac{1}{1+\beta|n-j|}+\frac{1}{1+\beta|n+j|}\Big)|F_{n}(x)||F_{j}(y)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,j=0}^{N}A(\beta,n,j)\|F_{n}\|_{L^{1}}\|F_{j}\|_{L^{1}},

with
A
from Lemma
2.9
. We can now apply Cauchy–Schwarz twice and Lemma
2.9
. Also noting that
A(\beta,n,j)
is symmetric in
n
and
j
, we estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{n,j=0}^{N}A(

\displaystyle\beta,n,j)\|F_{n}\|_{L^{1}}\|F_{j}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{\ell^{2}_{1,N}L^{1}}\Big[\sum_{n=0}^{N}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}A(\beta,j,n)\|F_{j}\|_{L^{1}}\Big)^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{\ell^{2}_{1,N}L^{1}}\Big[\sum_{n=0}^{N}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}A(\beta,j,n)\Big)\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}A(\beta,n,j)\|F_{j}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\max_{n=0,\dots,N}\sum_{j=0}^{N}A(\beta,n,j)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\max_{j=0,\dots,N}\sum_{n=0}^{N}A(\beta,n,j)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|F\|_{\ell^{2}_{1,N}L^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(1+\beta^{-1}\log(1+N\beta)\Big)\|F\|_{\ell^{2}_{1,N}L^{1}}^{2},

which shows (
2.15
).
∎

In Section
6
we also need an inhomogeneous estimate with the forbidden exponents
(\tilde{p}^{\prime},\tilde{q}^{\prime},1)=(2,1,1)

can often be deduced by means of the

from

.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
X
and
Y
be measure spaces, and
E
and
F
be Banach spaces,
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
, and
T\colon L^{p}(X,E)\to L^{q}(Y,F)
be a bounded linear operator. Furthermore, let
(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of measurable subsets of
X
such that
X_{j}\subseteq X_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the
Christ–Kiselev maximal operator

[T^{*}h](y)\coloneqq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|[T(h\mathbbm{1}_{X_{j}})](y)\|_{F}

for all
h\in L^{p}(X,E)
and almost every
y\in Y
. Then we have the estimate

\|T^{*}h\|_{L^{q}(Y)}\leq(1-2^{-(p^{-1}-q^{-1})})^{-1}\|T\|\|h\|_{L^{p}(X,E)}

for all
h\in L^{p}(X,E)
.

We show the estimate to be used in Section
6
. There we need uniformity with respect to shifts
s\in\mathbb{R}
in the wave propagator.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
(p,q,\gamma)
be wave admissible. Then the inequality

\displaystyle\Big\|\tau

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k+s)\tau|\nabla|}|\nabla|^{-1}F_{k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}K^{\gamma}\sqrt{K\tau+\log(KN\tau)}\|F\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N-1}L^{1}}

holds for all
\tau\in(0,1]
,
K\geq\tau^{-1}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
F\in\ell^{2}L^{1}
.

Proof.

We define the sets
X_{0}\coloneqq\emptyset
and
X_{j}\coloneqq\{0,\dots,\min\{j-1,N\}\}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
X\coloneqq\{0,\dots,N\}
. By the Christ–Kiselev Lemma
2.11
, it suffices to prove the estimate for the operator given by

(TF)_{n}\coloneqq\tau\sum_{k=0}^{N}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k+s)\tau|\nabla|}|\nabla|^{-1}F_{k}.

We compute

\displaystyle\Big\|

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{k=0}^{N}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n-k+s)\tau|\nabla|}|\nabla|^{-1}F_{k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|\tau\sum_{k=0}^{N}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(-k+s)\tau|\nabla|}|\nabla|^{-1}F_{k}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}K^{\gamma}\Big\|\tau\sum_{k=0}^{N}\pi_{K}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\tau|\nabla|}F_{k}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}K^{\gamma}\sqrt{K\tau+\log(1+KN\tau)}\|F\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N}L^{1}}.

Here we first use the homogeneous Strichartz estimate (
2.8
). Then we apply Bernstein’s inequality to get rid of the derivative loss of order
\gamma
, at the cost of the factor
K^{\gamma}
. In the end, we employ the endpoint estimate (
2.13
) from Theorem
2.10
.
∎

Furthermore, we need the following “hybrid” estimates to control time-continuous solutions in time-discrete norms. Their proof is only sketched since it does not require new ideas.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
(p,q,\gamma)
be wave admissible. Then the estimates

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{n\tau}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n\tau-s)|\nabla|}F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|F\|_{L^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{H}^{\gamma}},

(2.17)

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{n\tau}\pi_{K}e^{\mathrm{i}(n\tau-s)|\nabla|}F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{K\tau+\log(1+KN\tau)}\|F\|_{L^{1}([0,N\tau],\dot{H}^{1})}

hold for all
\tau\in(0,1]
,
K\geq\tau^{-1}
,
F\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{H}^{\gamma}
, and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

Two proofs are possible. Since we have an
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space in the space variable on the right-hand side of the inequality, we can apply the same technique as in the proof of (
2.9
) in Theorem
2.5
, just replacing one of the two sums by an integral. For (
2.17
) one uses (
2.13
) instead of (
2.11
). An alternative way would be to proceed as in the previous proof and use the Christ–Kiselev Lemma with the sets
X_{j}\coloneqq(-\infty,j\tau]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
for the first, resp.
X_{j}\coloneqq[0,\min\{j,N\}\tau]\subseteq[0,N\tau]
for the second estimate. So (
2.16
) is reduced to (
2.8
), and (
2.17
) to (
2.12
).
∎

3.
Nonlinear Wave equation

We introduce some notation and recall some well-known results from the wellposedness theory of the nonlinear wave equation (
1.1
). For the nonlinearity we write
g(u)\coloneqq-\mu|u|^{\alpha-1}u
. We will often use the elementary pointwise Lipschitz bound

(3.1)

|g(v)-g(w)|\lesssim(|v|^{\alpha-1}+|w|^{\alpha-1})|v-w|

for
g
.
It is convenient to reformulate (
1.1
) as a system having first order in time. Therefore we set

(3.2)

U\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}\mathrel{\widehat{=}}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
\partial_{t}u\end{pmatrix},\ A\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
\Delta&0\end{pmatrix},\ G(U)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0\\
g(u)\end{pmatrix},\ U^{0}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}u^{0}\\
v^{0}\end{pmatrix},

and obtain the equivalent first-order system

(3.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U(t)

\displaystyle=AU(t)+G(U(t)),\quad t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle U(0)

\displaystyle=U^{0}.

We are looking for a mild solution of (
3.3
), i.e., a function
U\in C([0,T],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})
satisfying the Duhamel formula

(3.4)

U(t)=e^{tA}U^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}G(U(s))\,\mathrm{d}s

for all
t\in[0,T]
. The group
e^{tA}
is given by

(3.5)

e^{tA}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(t|\nabla|)&|\nabla|^{-1}\sin(t|\nabla|)\\
-|\nabla|\sin(t|\nabla|)&\cos(t|\nabla|)\end{pmatrix},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Inserting (
3.5
), the first line of (
3.4
) reads

(3.6)

u(t)=\cos(t|\nabla|)u^{0}+|\nabla|^{-1}\sin(t|\nabla|)v^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla|^{-1}\sin((t-s)|\nabla|)g(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s.

For the linear part of the evolution we abbreviate

(3.7)

S(t)(u^{0},v^{0})\coloneqq\cos(t|\nabla|)u^{0}+|\nabla|^{-1}\sin(t|\nabla|)v^{0}.

In this setting we can apply the Strichartz estimates as formulated in Section
2
, since we can decompose

\sin(t|\nabla|)=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}}(e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}-e^{-\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}),\qquad\cos(t|\nabla|)=\frac{1}{2}(e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}+e^{-\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}).

In the case
\alpha=3
, Sobolev embedding shows that the nonlinearity
G
leaves the space
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
invariant. Therefore, local wellposedness can be shown in a standard way using the Duhamel formula and Banach’s fixed point theorem. If
\alpha>3
, a more sophisticated analysis is needed since in that case the nonlinearity loses too much integrability. By means of Strichartz estimates, local wellposedness can be shown up to the critical power
\alpha=5
.

Let
\alpha\in[3,5]
. We define an exponent
p_{\alpha}\in[4,\infty]
such that
(p_{\alpha},3(\alpha-1),1)
are wave admissible, i.e., by the relation

\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}=\frac{1}{2},

see (
2.2
).
The local wellposedness theorem for the nonlinear wave equation (
1.1
) reads as follows. A proof for the critical case
\alpha=5
is given in, e.g., Theorem IV.3.1 of
[
20
]
, and the subcritical case
\alpha<5
can be proven similarly. See also Sections 8.3–8.4 of
[
1
]
for related results and Chapter 3 in
[
23
]
for an overview.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
U^{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
. Then there exists a time
T_{0}>0
and a unique solution
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)
of (
1.1
) such that
U\in C([0,T_{0}],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})
and
u\in L^{p_{\alpha}}([0,T_{0}],L^{3(\alpha-1)})
.
Moreover,
U
is bounded in the above function spaces by a constant depending only on
\|U^{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}
, and in the subcritical case
\alpha<5
, the time
T_{0}
only depends on
\|U^{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

a) Since
g(u)\in C([0,T_{0}],L^{6/\alpha})\hookrightarrow C([0,T_{0}],H^{-1})
and
\Delta u\in C([0,T_{0}],\dot{H}^{-1})\hookrightarrow C([0,T_{0}],H^{-1})
, one can deduce from (
3.6
) that
\partial_{t}^{2}u
belongs to
C([0,T_{0}],H^{-1})
and that the differential equation in (
1.1
) holds in this space.

b) See Proposition
5.2
for a choice of
T_{0}
in the critical case
\alpha=5
.

c) In the defocusing case
\mu=1
, one can show global-in-time wellposedness using energy conservation. In the focusing case
\mu=-1
however, blow-up in finite time can occur. Moreover, if
\alpha>5
, the problem is ill-posed at least if
\mu=-1
. See Chapters 3 and 5 in
[
23
]
.

d) In the subcritical case
\alpha<5
, one has uniqueness of solutions
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)
to (
1.1
) in the energy class
C([0,T_{0}],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})
without the requirement that
u\in L^{p_{\alpha}}([0,T_{0}],L^{3(\alpha-1)})
, cf.
[
18
]
.

From now on we assume the following.

Assumption 3.3
.

There exists a time
T>0
and a solution
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)
of (
3.3
) such that

U\in C([0,T],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})\quad\text{and}\quad u\in L^{p_{\alpha}}([0,T],L^{3(\alpha-1)})

with bound

(3.8)

M\coloneqq\max\{\|U\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})},\|u\|_{L^{p_{\alpha}}_{T}L^{3(\alpha-1)}}\}.

Remark 3.4
.

If
\alpha<5
, the quantity
M
only depends on
\|U\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})}
. Indeed, the “minimal” existence time
T_{0}
and the number
M
for
T=T_{0}
are controlled by
\|U^{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}
in Theorem
3.1
. Hence, we can divide the interval
[0,T]
into a finite number of smaller subintervals such that the Strichartz norm of
u
is bounded on each of them. Moreover, in the defocusing case
\mu=1
, one can use energy conservation to show that the number
M
only depends on
\|U^{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}
. The latter even holds in the critical case
\alpha=5
, see
[
24
]
and the references therein.

As a preparation for later sections, we now want to convert the continuous-time Strichartz estimate into a discrete-time space-time bound for
u
from Assumption
3.3
. To apply the results of Section
2
, we need to include the frequency cut-off
\pi_{K}
defined by (
2.7
).

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
u
,
T
, and
M
be given by Assumption
3.3
and
(p,q,1)
be wave admissible. Then we have the estimates

\displaystyle\|\pi_{K}u(n\tau+\sigma)\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q,M,T}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\|\pi_{K}u(n\tau+\sigma)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}(K\tau+\log K)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
\tau\in(0,1]
,
K\geq\tau^{-1}
,
\sigma\geq 0
, and
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
N\tau+\sigma\leq T
.

Proof.

Owing to (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), the solution is given by

\displaystyle\pi_{K}u(n\tau+\sigma)

\displaystyle=\pi_{K}S(n\tau)(u(\sigma),\partial_{t}u(\sigma))+\int_{0}^{n\tau}\pi_{K}|\nabla|^{-1}\sin((n\tau-s)|\nabla|)g(u(\sigma+s))\,\mathrm{d}s.

We apply the homogeneous and hybrid Strichartz estimates (
2.8
) and (
2.16
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\pi_{K}

\displaystyle u(n\tau+\sigma)\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\|u(\sigma)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}u(\sigma)\|_{L^{2}}+\|g(u(\sigma+\cdot))\|_{L^{1}_{T-\sigma}L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(M+\||u|^{\alpha-1}u\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(M+\||u|^{\alpha-1}\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{3}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{6}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(M+T^{2-\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\|u\|^{\alpha-1}_{L^{p_{\alpha}}_{T}L^{3(\alpha-1)}}M\Big)\lesssim_{M,T}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we also use Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding
\dot{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{6}
to bound the nonlinearity. The estimate in the
\ell^{2}L^{\infty}
-norm follows in the same way, using the logarithmic endpoint estimates (
2.12
) and (
2.17
).
∎

Remark 3.6
.

With a similar calculation we can also show the continuous-time bound

\|\pi_{K}u\|_{L^{p}_{T}L^{q}}\lesssim_{p,q,M,T}1.

Here we use the continuous-time Strichartz estimate from Theorem
2.3
and the uniform boundedness of the operator
\pi_{K}
in all
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces.

4.
Lie splitting

We consider a semi-discretization in time for (
3.3
), i.e., for a stepsize
\tau>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we want to compute approximations
U_{n}=(u_{n},v_{n})\approx U(t_{n})
at discrete time points
t_{n}\coloneqq n\tau
. One can consider the following Lie Splitting scheme (sometimes also called Lawson–Euler scheme)

U_{n+1}=e^{\tau A}[U_{n}+\tau G(U_{n})]

with
A
and
G
from (
3.2
).
In order to perform one step of the Lie splitting scheme, we first apply one step of the exact nonlinear flow, followed by one step of the exact linear flow. One can also derive the Lie Splitting by setting
s=0
in the integral of the Duhamel formula

U(\tau)=e^{\tau A}U^{0}+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)A}G(U(s))\,\mathrm{d}s.

To compensate the “bad” behavior of the nonlinearity
G
in the case
\alpha>3
, we analyze a modified scheme that includes the frequency cut-off
\pi_{K}
defined in (
2.7
), where we choose
K=\tau^{-1}
. (One could also take
K=c\tau^{-1}
for some fixed
c>0
.) Thanks to this choice, the factor
(K\tau)^{1/p}
in the discrete Strichartz estimates vanishes.
We introduce the notation

\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{\tau^{-1}}&0\\
0&\pi_{\tau^{-1}}\end{pmatrix}.

The modified scheme is given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle U_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\tau}(U_{n})\coloneqq e^{\tau A}[U_{n}+\tau\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}G(U_{n})],

\displaystyle U_{0}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U^{0}.

The filter is applied to the initial data, as well as in the iteration after the application of the nonlinearity. Inductively, one checks that
\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U_{n}=U_{n}
for all
n
. Let the solution
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)
be given by Assumption
3.3
. In Lemma
4.2
we control the projection error
U(t_{n})-\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{n})
. Hence, to compare the numerical approximation with
U
, we define the (main) error

(4.2)

E_{n}\coloneqq\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{n})-U_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
n\tau=t_{n}\leq T
. Note that with this definition we have
E_{0}=0
, since the numerical scheme filters the initial data. We write
u_{n}
for the first component of
U_{n}
, as well as
e_{n}
for the first component of
E_{n}
. We first establish a recursion formula for the error.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E_{n}
be given by (
4.2
) and (
4.1
) for the solution
U
from Assumption
3.3
. We then have

\displaystyle E_{m+n}

\displaystyle=e^{n\tau A}E_{m}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n\tau-s)A}B_{m}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

(4.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n-k)\tau A}\Big(\Delta_{m+k}+\tau Q_{m+k}\Big)

for all
\tau\in(0,1]
, and
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
t_{m+n}\leq T
. The appearing terms are given by

\displaystyle B_{m}(s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq G(U(t_{m}+s))-G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{m}+s)),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-sA}G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{n}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s-\tau G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{n})),

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{n}))-G(U_{n}).

Proof.

We use the Duhamel formula

U(t_{m+n})=e^{n\tau A}U(t_{m})+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}e^{(n\tau-s)A}G(U(t_{m}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s

for the solution
U
. For the discrete approximation
U_{n}
defined by (
4.1
) we have the discrete Duhamel formula

(4.5)

U_{m+n}=e^{n\tau A}U_{m}+\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n-k)\tau A}G(U_{m+k}),

which is easily verified via induction. These equalities yields

\displaystyle E_{m+n}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{m+n})-U_{m+n}

\displaystyle=e^{n\tau A}(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{m})-U_{m})+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n\tau-s)A}G(U(t_{m}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n-k)\tau A}G(U_{m+k})

\displaystyle=e^{n\tau A}E_{m}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n\tau-s)A}B_{m}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n\tau-s)A}G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{m}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n-k)\tau A}G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{m+k}))+\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n-k)\tau A}Q_{m+k}.

Furthermore, we can rewrite the last integral as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}

\displaystyle e^{(n\tau-s)A}G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{m}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n\tau-s)A}G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{m}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n-k)\tau A}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-sA}G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{m+k}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s

to obtain the expression including the local error terms
\Delta_{m+k}
.
∎

We now quantify convergence
\pi_{K}\to I
as
K\to\infty
. In view of later applications, the following two results are formulated for general
K\geq 1
. In this section, we will only use the case
K=\tau^{-1}
. We give the proof of the next (known) lemma for convenience.

Lemma 4.2
.

The estimate

\displaystyle\|(I-\pi_{K})f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}

is true for all
K\geq 1
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f\in\dot{H}^{\gamma}
.

Proof.

We simply use the support property of the Fourier multiplier
\pi_{K}
, Plancherel’s theorem and the definition of the Sobolev norm. This gives

\displaystyle\|(I-\pi_{K})f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma-1}}

\displaystyle=\||\xi|^{\gamma-1}(1-\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,K)})\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\Big\|\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\xi|\geq K\}}}{|\xi|}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}}\||\xi|^{\gamma}\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}.\qed

Using this, we show an estimate for the terms
B_{n}(s)
in (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u
,
T
, and
M
be given by Assumption
3.3
. We then have the inequality

\|g(u)-g(\pi_{K}u)\|_{L^{1}_{T}\dot{H}^{-1}}\lesssim_{M,T}\frac{1}{K}

for all
K\geq 1
.

Proof.

We use estimate (
3.1
), the Sobolev embedding
L^{6/5}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{-1}
, Hölder’s inequality, the definition (
3.8
) of
M
, Remark
3.6
, and Lemma
4.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle g(u)-g(\pi_{K}u)\|_{L^{1}_{T}\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(|u|^{\alpha-1}+|\pi_{K}u|^{\alpha-1})|u-\pi_{K}u|\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|u\|^{\alpha-1}_{L_{T}^{\alpha-1}L^{3(\alpha-1)}}+\|\pi_{K}u\|^{\alpha-1}_{L_{T}^{\alpha-1}L^{3(\alpha-1)}})\|u-\pi_{K}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}\frac{1}{K}\|u\|^{\alpha-1}_{L_{T}^{p_{\alpha}}L^{3(\alpha-1)}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim_{M}\frac{1}{K}.\qed

We next bound the local error terms
\Delta_{n}
from (
4.1
). To differentiate
g
, we identify
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}
using the real scalar product
z\cdot w=\mathrm{Re}(z\bar{w})
, where we omit the dot below.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)
,
T
, and
M
be given by Assumption
3.3
. We then have the representation

(4.6)

\Delta_{n}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\sigma A}\begin{pmatrix}-g(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma))\\
g^{\prime}(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma))\pi_{\tau^{-1}}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+\sigma)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}s.

Moreover, the inequality

\|\Delta_{n}\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N}(L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1})}\lesssim_{M,T}\tau^{2}

holds for all
\tau\in(0,1]
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
(N+1)\tau\leq T
.

Proof.

Starting from (
4.1
), we write

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\Big[e^{-sA}G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{n}+s))-G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{n}))\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma}\Big[e^{-\sigma A}G(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U(t_{n}+\sigma))\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\sigma A}\Bigg[-\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
g(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma))\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\sigma}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
g(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma))\end{pmatrix}\Bigg]\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\sigma A}\begin{pmatrix}-g(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma))\\
g^{\prime}(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma))\pi_{\tau^{-1}}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+\sigma)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}s.

Thanks to the regularization
\pi_{\tau^{-1}}
there are no problems taking the derivative. We can now estimate

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N}(L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{2}\sup_{\sigma\in[0,\tau]}\Big\|\begin{pmatrix}g(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma))\\
g^{\prime}(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma))\pi_{\tau^{-1}}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+\sigma)\end{pmatrix}\Big\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N}(L^{2}\times L^{6/5})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{2}\sup_{\sigma\in[0,\tau]}\||\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{n}+\sigma)|^{\alpha-1}\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N}L^{3}}\big(\|\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{6}}+\|\pi_{\tau^{-1}}\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\big)

using Sobolev’s embedding, the estimate

, Hölder’s inequality, and Proposition

.
∎

Now we turn our attention to the terms

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

,

, and

. Then the inequality

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle g(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{m+n}))-g(u_{m+n})\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,j}\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle g(\pi_{\tau^{-1}}u(t_{m+n}))-g(u_{m+n})\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,j}\dot{H}^{-1}}

Due to scaling considerations, the convergence order of

To measure the error simultaneously in two different norms, we define

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\alpha<5
and

Step 1.
Let

be the constant from the discrete Strichartz estimates from Section

(4,3(\alpha-1),\gamma_{\alpha})

,
4.4
, and
4.5

, we can choose a time

Next, we define

and the maximum step size
\tau_{0}>0
by the relations

(4.9)

\tau_{0}\leq T_{1},\qquad\tau_{0}^{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{4C_{2}(2C_{1})^{L+1}},

where we again exploit that

. For

for all

. Moreover, we define the number

[0,t_{N}]=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\ell-1}[t_{N_{m}},t_{N_{m+1}}]\cup[t_{N_{\ell}},t_{N}],

where each subinterval is of length less or equal

To measure the error in each of them, we define the error terms

by

\mathrm{Err}_{m}\coloneqq\|E_{{N_{m}}+n}\|_{\tau,N_{1}},\quad m\in\{0,\dots,\ell-1\},\qquad\mathrm{Err}_{\ell}\coloneqq\|E_{{N_{\ell}}+n}\|_{\tau,N-N_{\ell}}.

Step 2.
Our next goal is to show the recursion formula

(4.10)

\mathrm{Err}_{m}\leq 2C_{1}\mathrm{Err}_{m-1}+4C_{1}C_{2},\quad m\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}.

Note that as soon as (

(4.11)

\mathrm{Err}_{m}\leq 4C_{1}C_{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(2C_{1})^{k}=4C_{1}C_{2}\frac{(2C_{1})^{m+1}-1}{2C_{1}-1}\leq 4C_{2}(2C_{1})^{L+1}.

a) Fix an index
m\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
. If

4.10
) holds for all indices in
\{0,\dots,m-1\}
. We derive (
4.10
) by proving

\|E_{N_{m}+n}\|_{\tau,j}\leq 2C_{1}\mathrm{Err}_{m-1}+4C_{1}C_{2},\quad j\in\{0,\dots,\min\{N_{1},N-N_{m}\}\},

via induction on

.

b) First let

. If
m=0
, there is nothing to prove since

. If

, we directly obtain

\|E_{N_{m}+n}\|_{\tau,0}\leq\|E_{N_{m-1}+n}\|_{\tau,N_{1}}=\mathrm{Err}_{m-1},

which in particular shows (

) for

. Next, let (

) be true for some

4.10
) then yields

\|E_{N_{m}+n}\|_{\tau,j}\leq 4C_{2}(2C_{1})^{L+1}.

We apply the discrete Strichartz estimates from Theorem

and Corollary

to the error formula (

inductively.
We also use Bernstein’s inequality to convert the derivative loss

in this Strichartz estimate, as explained in Remark

\displaystyle\|E_{N_{m}+n}\|_{\tau,j+1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{n\tau A}E_{N_{m}}\|_{\tau,j+1}+\Big\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\!\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}e^{(n\tau-s)A}B_{N_{m}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big\|_{\tau,j+1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\tau^{-1}\Big(\|E_{N_{m}}\|_{L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}}+\|B_{N_{m}}\|_{L^{1}_{t_{j+1}}(L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1})}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}C_{2}T_{1}^{1-\frac{\alpha-1}{4}}\!\Big(1+\tau^{(1-{\gamma_{\alpha}})(\alpha-1)}\|E_{N_{m}+n}\|^{\alpha-1}_{\tau,j}\Big)\|E_{N_{m}+n}\|_{\tau,j},

where we applied Lemma

,
4.4
, and
4.5
to bound the error terms.
We insert (

), the step size restriction

from (

\displaystyle\|E_{N_{m}+n}\|_{\tau,j+1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathrm{Err}_{m-1}+2C_{1}C_{2}+2C_{1}C_{2}T_{1}^{1-\frac{\alpha-1}{4}}\|E_{N_{m}+n}\|_{\tau,j}

5.
The critical case

We now consider the case
\alpha=5
. As a first auxiliary result we establish convergence of the time-discrete Strichartz norm towards the time-continuous Strichartz norm. This will be done for the homogeneous part of the evolution, i.e.,

S(t)(f,v)=\cos(t|\nabla|)f+|\nabla|^{-1}\sin(t|\nabla|)v,

on a bounded interval
J
. We recall the notation

\|F_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,X)}=\Big(\tau\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\tau\in J\end{subarray}}\|F_{n}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(p,q,\gamma)
be wave admissible,
f\in\dot{H}^{\gamma}
,
v\in\dot{H}^{\gamma-1}
, and
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a bounded interval. Then we have the convergence

\displaystyle\|e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}\pi_{\tau^{-1}}f\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,L^{q})}

\displaystyle\to\|e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{p}_{J}L^{q}},

\displaystyle\|S(n\tau)\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}(f,v)\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,L^{q})}

\displaystyle\to\|S(t)(f,v)\|_{L^{p}_{J}L^{q}},

as
\tau\to 0
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We choose a Schwartz function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
such that

\operatorname{supp}\hat{\varphi}
is compact
and
\|f-\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}\leq\varepsilon
. The function
t\mapsto e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}\varphi
is then continuous with values in
L^{q}
, since Hausdorff–Young and dominated convergence yield

\|e^{\mathrm{i}s|\nabla|}\varphi-e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}\varphi\|_{L^{q}}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}((e^{\mathrm{i}s|\xi|}-e^{\mathrm{i}t|\xi|})\hat{\varphi})\|_{L^{q}}\leq\|(e^{\mathrm{i}s|\xi|}-e^{\mathrm{i}t|\xi|})\hat{\varphi}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}\to 0

as
s\to t
. We also have
\pi_{\tau^{-1}}\varphi=\varphi
for
\tau
small enough, because
\operatorname{supp}\hat{\varphi}
is compact. It follows

\|e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}\pi_{\tau^{-1}}\varphi\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,L^{q})}\to\|e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}\varphi\|_{L^{p}_{J}L^{q}}

as
\tau\to 0
, as there are essentially Riemann sums on the left-hand side. Putting things together, we conclude

\displaystyle\Big|\|

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}\pi_{\tau^{-1}}f\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,L^{q})}-\|e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{p}_{J}L^{q}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}\pi_{\tau^{-1}}(f-\varphi)\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,L^{q})}+\Big|\|e^{\mathrm{i}n\tau|\nabla|}\pi_{\tau^{-1}}\varphi\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau}(J,L^{q})}-\|e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}\varphi\|_{L^{p}_{J}L^{q}}\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+\|e^{\mathrm{i}t|\nabla|}(\varphi-f)\|_{L^{p}_{J}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}2\|f-\varphi\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}+\varepsilon\leq 3\varepsilon

for
\tau
small enough, using the reverse triangle inequality and Strichartz estimates from Theorem
2.3
and
2.5
. Thus, the first assertion is shown. The proof of the second one follows the same lines.
∎

To show the error bound in the critical case, we use a regularization argument. For

initial data, it requires first-order convergence of the scheme (

) in the

-norm. To use this fact, we also need the continuous dependence on the initial data, both for the equation (

) and the scheme (

We now show the global error bound for the critical case. We use ideas from the proof of Theorem 1.6 in
[

, where similar arguments were used in the context of nonlinear Schrödinger equations, but only in the energy-subcritical case.
The proof will be divided in three steps. In the first step, we define the needed variables and divide the interval

into a finite number of subintervals, which are so small that we can apply Proposition

on each of them. In the second step, we first prove the convergence of the scheme in the

-norm without any convergence rate. This fact ensures that the discrete approximation stays close to the solution in the
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
-norm if

-norm to obtain the convergence of order one.
In contrast to Theorem
1.1
, the maximum step size

will now not only depend on the size

of the solution

and on the end time

, but also on further properties of the solution

to (

\displaystyle=\|U(t_{n},U(t_{N_{m}}))-\Phi_{\tau}^{n}(U_{N_{m}})\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\tau}([0,b_{m}],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|U(t_{n},U(t_{N_{m}}))-U(t_{n},Y^{m})\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\tau}([0,b_{m}],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|U(t_{n},Y^{m})-\Phi_{\tau}^{n}(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}Y^{m})\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\tau}([0,b_{m}],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\Phi_{\tau}^{n}(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}Y^{m})-\Phi_{\tau}^{n}(\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}U_{N_{m}})\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\tau}([0,b_{m}],\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|U(t_{N_{m}})-Y^{m}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}+C(Y^{m})\tau+2\|Y^{m}-U_{N_{m}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|U(t_{N_{m}})-Y^{m}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}+\varepsilon+2\|Y^{m}-U(t_{N_{m}})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\|U(t_{N_{m}})-U_{N_{m}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\|U(t_{N_{m}})-U(T_{m})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}+4\|U(T_{m})-Y^{m}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}+\varepsilon+2\mathrm{Err}_{m-1}

\displaystyle\leq 9\varepsilon+2\mathrm{Err}_{m-1}.

Therefore, (
5.25
) is true. It follows that

\mathrm{Err}_{m}\leq 9\varepsilon\sum_{k=0}^{m}2^{k}=9\varepsilon(2^{m+1}-1)\leq 9\varepsilon(2^{L+1}-1)

for all
m\in\{0,\dots,L\}
. This also shows the convergence in the
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
-norm as stated in (
5.24
), since
L
is independent of
\varepsilon
, which we could have replaced by any
\tilde{\varepsilon}\in(0,\varepsilon]
. To complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we will actually not need the full statement of (
5.24
). It is enough to know that
\|U(T_{m})-U_{N_{m}}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}\leq\delta
for all
m\in\{0,\dots,L\}
, as noted in (
5
).

Step 3.

6.
Corrected Lie Splitting

In this section we only consider the cubic wave equation, i.e., the case
\alpha=3
. For simplicity, we set
g(u)\coloneqq-\mu u^{3}
, one could similarly deal with
g(u)=-\mu|u|^{2}u
in case of complex-valued functions. We prove an error estimate with order
3/2
for a frequency-filtered variant of the corrected Lie splitting recently proposed in
[
13
]
. The original form of the method reads

U_{n+1}=e^{\tau A}[U_{n}+\tau G(U_{n})+\tau^{2}\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)H(U_{n})],

where the operator
A
and the nonlinearity
G
are defined in (
3.2
). We thus have added a correction term to the Lie splitting. Here we set

(6.1)

H(u,v)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}-g(u)\\
g^{\prime}(u)v\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{2}(tA)w\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}(1-\sigma)e^{\sigma tA}w\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
v
corresponds to
\partial_{t}u
, the nonlinear term
H
now contains a derivative.
The operator
\varphi_{2}(tA)
can also be understood by the functional calculus for
A
and the function
\varphi_{2}(z)=(e^{z}-z-1)/z^{2}
, which is bounded on
\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}
. The expression (
3.5
) for the wave group
e^{tA}
leads to the formula

\varphi_{2}(tA)=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{1-\cos(t|\nabla|)}{t^{2}|\nabla|^{2}}&\dfrac{t|\nabla|-\sin(t|\nabla|)}{t^{2}|\nabla|^{3}}\\[8.61108pt]
\dfrac{\sin(t|\nabla|)-t|\nabla|}{t^{2}|\nabla|}&\dfrac{1-\cos(t|\nabla|)}{t^{2}|\nabla|^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

This implies a smoothing property for the operator
\varphi_{2}(tA)
. Using the functional calculus for
t|\nabla|
, we deduce the smoothing property

(6.2)

\Big\|\varphi_{2}(tA)\begin{pmatrix}z\\
0\end{pmatrix}\Big\|_{L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}}\lesssim|t|^{-1}\|z\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}

of
\varphi_{2}(tA)
that is valid for all
z\in\dot{H}^{-1}
and
t\neq 0
.

As explained in
[
13
]
, the corrected Lie splitting is formally of second order, but, in contrast to classical second-order integrators for the wave equation, only first-order spatial derivatives appear in the local error. In
[
13
]
, a second-order error estimate in
H^{1}\times L^{2}
was shown for solutions
u
with
H^{1+d/4}
regularity, where
d
denotes the spatial dimension. Since here, we only assume
\dot{H}^{1}
regularity of the solution, we have to use the discrete Strichartz estimates from Section
2
to deal with the error terms. Therefore, as in Section
4
, we include the frequency filter
\pi_{K}
from (
2.7
) in the scheme, which gives

(6.3)

\displaystyle U_{n+1}

\displaystyle=e^{\tau A}\Pi_{K}[U_{n}+\tau G(U_{n})+\tau^{2}\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)H(U_{n})],

\displaystyle U_{0}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{K}U^{0}.

Here, we again set

\Pi_{K}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{K}&0\\
0&\pi_{K}\end{pmatrix}.

We write
u_{n}
(resp.
v_{n}
) for the first (resp. second) component of
U_{n}
. In contrast to the previous sections, we do not set
K=\tau^{-1}
, since such a choice could only lead to an estimate of order one in
L^{2}\times H^{-1}
, because the error coming from frequency truncation would dominate (of order
1/K
, see Lemma
4.2
). To get an improved convergence, we therefore have to choose some
K>\tau^{-1}
. As seen in Section
2
, this leads to a multiplicative loss of the form
(K\tau)^{1/p}
in the discrete Strichartz estimates. Therefore, we cannot reach the optimal order two for the scheme (
6.3
). It turns out that with the choice

K\sim\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}}

we can optimize the global error and get an error bound of order
3/2
. See
[
16
]
for a similar discussion in the context of Schrödinger equations.

For the rest of this section, we fix
K=\tau^{-3/2}
. As in case of the Lie splitting, we define the error terms

(6.4)

E_{n}\coloneqq\Pi_{K}U(t_{n})-U_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
t_{n}\leq T
. For the first component of
E_{n}
we write
e_{n}
. We first show an error recursion for (
6.4
) which is similar to (
4.1
).

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
U
and
T
be given by Assumption
3.3
with
\alpha=3
and let
K=\tau^{-3/2}
. The error defined in (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) then satisfies

\displaystyle E_{m+n}

\displaystyle=e^{n\tau A}E_{m}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{K}e^{(n\tau-s)A}B_{m}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

(6.5)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{K}e^{(n-k)\tau A}\Big(\widetilde{\Delta}_{m+k}+\tau\widetilde{Q}_{m+k}\Big)

for all
\tau\in(0,1]
, and
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
t_{m+n}\leq T
. The appearing terms are given by

(6.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Delta_{n}-\tau^{2}\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n})),

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq Q_{n}+\tau\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)\big[H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}))-H(U_{n})\big],

where
B_{m}(s)
,
\Delta_{n}
and
Q_{n}
are defined as in (
4.1
) with
\Pi_{\tau^{-1}}
replaced by
\Pi_{K}
, and
H
and
\varphi_{2}(tA)
are introduced in (
6.1
).

Proof.

For the discrete approximation
U_{n}
defined by (
6.3
), we have the discrete Duhamel formula

U_{m+n}=e^{n\tau A}U_{m}+\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{K}e^{(n-k)\tau A}\Big(G(U_{m+k})+\tau\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)H(U_{m+k})\Big).

Proceeding as in Proposition
4.1
, we derive

\displaystyle E_{m+n}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{K}U(t_{m+n})-U_{m+n}

\displaystyle=e^{n\tau A}(\Pi_{K}U(t_{m})-U_{m})+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{K}e^{(n\tau-s)A}G(U(t_{m}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{K}e^{(n-k)\tau A}\Big(G(U_{m+k})+\tau\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)H(U_{m+k})\Big)

\displaystyle=e^{n\tau A}E_{m}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{K}e^{(n\tau-s)A}B_{m}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{K}e^{(n-k)\tau A}\Big(\Delta_{m+k}+\tau Q_{m+k}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\tau^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{K}e^{(n-k)\tau A}\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)H(U_{m+k})

\displaystyle=e^{n\tau A}E_{m}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Pi_{K}e^{(n\tau-s)A}B_{m}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Pi_{K}e^{(n-k)\tau A}\Big(\widetilde{\Delta}_{m+k}+\tau\widetilde{Q}_{m+k}\Big).\qed

As we see in the next lemma, in the error formula (
6.1
) all second derivatives of
u
cancel, only first-order derivatives remain. This is the advantage of the corrected Lie splitting in comparison with classical second-order integrators, such as the Strang splitting. We first deal with the local error
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
U=(u,\partial_{t}u)
,
T
, and
M
be given by Assumption
3.3
with
\alpha=3
and let
K=\tau^{-3/2}
. We then have the representation

(6.7)

\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}=\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)e^{-2\sigma A}\int_{0}^{\sigma}e^{sA}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
d_{1}(\tau,s,n)+d_{2}(\tau,s,n)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where we abbreviate

\displaystyle d_{1}(\tau,s,n)

\displaystyle\coloneqq g^{\prime\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\big[(\pi_{K}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+s))^{2}-(\nabla\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))^{2}\big],

\displaystyle d_{2}(\tau,s,n)

\displaystyle\coloneqq g^{\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\pi_{K}g(u(t_{n}+s)).

Here we use the notation
(\nabla f)^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\partial_{j}f)^{2}
. Moreover, the inequalities

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Pi_{K}e^{(N-k)\tau A}\widetilde{\Delta}_{m+k}\Big\|_{L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}\tau^{\frac{3}{2}},

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\pi_{K}S((n-k)\tau)\widetilde{\Delta}_{m+k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T,p,q}\tau^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\gamma)-\frac{1}{2p}}

hold for all
\tau\in(0,1]
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
t_{m+N}\leq T
, and wave admissible parameters
(p,q,\gamma)
.

Proof.

The error representation of the Lie splitting (
4.6
) and Fubini’s theorem yield

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\sigma A}H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}+\sigma))\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)e^{-\sigma A}H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}+\sigma))\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Moreover, the definition of
\varphi_{2}
in (
6.1
) and a substitution lead to

\displaystyle\tau^{2}\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}))

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\sigma)e^{-2\tau\sigma A}H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}))\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)e^{-2\sigma A}H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}))\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

From (
6.6
) it thus follows

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{n}-\tau^{2}\varphi_{2}(-2\tau A)H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)e^{-2\sigma A}\Big[e^{\sigma A}H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}+\sigma))-H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}))\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)e^{-2\sigma A}\int_{0}^{\sigma}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big[e^{sA}H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}+s))\Big]\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

We compute the derivative by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big[e^{sA}H(\Pi_{K}U(t_{n}+s))\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Bigg[e^{sA}\begin{pmatrix}-g(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\\
g^{\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\pi_{K}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+s)\end{pmatrix}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=e^{sA}\Bigg[\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-g(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\\
g^{\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\pi_{K}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+s)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\quad+\begin{pmatrix}-g^{\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\pi_{K}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+s)\\
g^{\prime\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))(\pi_{K}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+s))^{2}+g^{\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\pi_{K}\partial_{tt}u(t_{n}+s)\end{pmatrix}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=e^{sA}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
d_{1}(\tau,s,n)+g^{\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\pi_{K}[\partial_{tt}u(t_{n}+s)-\Delta u(t_{n}+s)]\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=e^{sA}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
d_{1}(\tau,s,n)+d_{2}(\tau,s,n)\end{pmatrix},

where we used the formula
\Delta[g(w)]=g^{\prime\prime}(w)(\nabla w)^{2}+g^{\prime}(w)\Delta w
and the fact that the differential equation (
1.1
) holds in
C([0,T],H^{-1})
. Because of
\pi_{K}
there is no problem in justifying the above differentiations. Thus, the error representation formula (
6.1
) is true.

For the error estimates, we first prove the inequalities

(6.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}\|d_{1}(\tau,s,n)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N-1}L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}\tau^{-\frac{1}{4}},

(6.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}\|d_{2}(\tau,s,n)\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N-1}\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}1.

Note that we have set
g(u)=-\mu u^{3}
, hence
g^{\prime}(u)=-3\mu u^{2}
and
g^{\prime\prime}(u)=-6\mu u
. We derive

\displaystyle\|d_{1}(\tau,s,n)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N-1}L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g^{\prime\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}\!+s))\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N-1}L^{\infty}}\|(\pi_{K}\partial_{t}u(t_{n}+s))^{2}-(\nabla\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))^{2}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\tau,N-1}L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N-1}L^{\infty}}\Big(\|\pi_{K}\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}+\||\nabla\pi_{K}u|\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}(K\tau+\log K)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\tau^{-\frac{1}{4}},

uniformly in

, where we used Proposition
3.5
and the relation

. The second term is easier to estimate, we can directly infer

\displaystyle\|d_{2}(\tau,s,n)\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N-1}\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g^{\prime}(\pi_{K}u(t_{n}+s))\pi_{K}g(u(t_{n}+s))\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N-1}L^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\|g^{\prime}(\pi_{K}u)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{3}}\|\pi_{K}g(u)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\pi_{K}u)^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{3}}\|u^{3}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\pi_{K}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{6}}\|u\|^{3}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{6}}\lesssim\|u\|^{5}_{L^{\infty}_{T}\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim_{M}1.

This shows (
6.8
) and (
6.9
).

Now we turn to the error estimates, starting with the
L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}
-norm. Formulas (
6.7
) and (
3.5
), the endpoint estimate (
2.13
) and the inequalities (

) and (

) imply

\displaystyle\Big\|

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Pi_{K}e^{(N-k)\tau A}\widetilde{\Delta}_{m+k}\Big\|_{L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{3}\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Pi_{K}e^{-k\tau A}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
d_{1}(\tau,s,m+k)+d_{2}(\tau,s,m+k)\end{pmatrix}\Big\|_{L^{2}\times\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{2}\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}\Big(\Big\|\tau\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\pi_{K}e^{\pm\mathrm{i}k\tau|\nabla|}d_{1}(\tau,s,m+k)\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|d_{2}(\tau,s,m+n)\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N-1}\dot{H}^{-1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}\tau^{2}\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}\Big((K\tau+\log K)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|d_{1}(\tau,s,m+n)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N-1}L^{1}}+1\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}\tau^{2-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4}}=\tau^{\frac{3}{2}}.

For the estimate in the
\ell^{p}_{\tau}L^{q}
-norm we can proceed similarly, where we now employ Theorem
2.5
for the

-term. Moreover, for the inequality involving

we have to use Corollary

, since we have non-

-based spaces in the spatial variable on both sides of the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimate. This gives

\displaystyle\Big\|

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\pi_{K}S((n-k)\tau)\widetilde{\Delta}_{m+k}\Big\|_{\ell^{p}_{\tau,N}L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2}\sup_{s,\sigma\in[0,\tau]}\Big\|\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\pi_{K}S((n-k)\tau+s-2\sigma)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0\\

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q,T}\tau^{2}(K\tau)^{\frac{1}{p}}K^{\gamma}\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}\Big((K\tau+\log K)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|d_{1}(\tau,s,m+n)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\tau,N-1}L^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\|d_{2}(\tau,s,m+n)\|_{\ell^{1}_{\tau,N-1}\dot{H}^{-1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,T}\tau^{2-\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{3}{2}\gamma}\Big(\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}+1\Big)\lesssim\tau^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\gamma)-\frac{1}{2p}},

as desired.
∎

We further need a product estimate in
\dot{H}^{-1}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

The inequality

\|vw\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}

holds for all
v\in\dot{H}^{1}
and
w\in\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cap L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.

Proof.

We use the duality between
\dot{H}^{s}
and
\dot{H}^{-s}
for
|s|<3/2
. We estimate

\displaystyle\|vw\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\dot{H}^{1}\\
\|z\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\leq 1}\end{subarray}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vwz\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\dot{H}^{1}\\
\|z\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\leq 1}\end{subarray}}\|vz\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Next, we use the fractional product rule

\||\nabla|^{a}(vz)\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{a}v\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|z\|_{L^{q_{2}}}+\|v\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\||\nabla|^{a}z\|_{L^{q_{2}}}

for
a\in(0,1)
and
p_{j}
,
q_{j}
,
r\in(1,\infty)
with
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{r}
, see Proposition 3.3 in
[
6
]
. It follows

\displaystyle\|vz\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{3}}\|z\|_{L^{6}}+\|v\|_{L^{6}}\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}z\|_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|z\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}z\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\|z\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

using also the Sobolev embeddings
\dot{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{6}
and
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hookrightarrow L^{3}
.
∎

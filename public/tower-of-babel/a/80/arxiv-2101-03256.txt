Towards Optimal Transport for Quantum Densities
Emanuele Caglioti, François Golse, Thierry Paul

Abstract
. An analogue of the quadratic Wasserstein (or Monge-Kantorovich) distance between Borel probability measures on 𝐑 d \mathbf{R}^{d} has been defined in [F. Golse, C. Mouhot, T. Paul: Commun. Math. Phys. 343 (2015), 165–205] for density operators on L 2 ​ ( 𝐑 d ) L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}) , and used to estimate the convergence rate of various asymptotic theories in the context of quantum mechanics. The present work proves a Kantorovich type duality theorem for this quantum variant of the Monge-Kantorovich or Wasserstein distance, and discusses the structure of optimal quantum couplings. Specifically, we prove that, under some boundedness and constraint hypothesis on the Kantorovich potentials, optimal quantum couplings involve a gradient type structure similar in the quantum paradigm to the Brenier transport map. On the contrary, when the two quantum densities have finite rank, the structure involved by the optimal coupling has, in general, no classical counterpart.

Recently, an analogue of the Monge-Kantorovich-Wasserstein distance

Main Results

The key argument in deriving the structure (
1

\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi,\psi\in C_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\\
\phi(x)+\psi(y)\leq|x-y|^{2}\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\mu(dx)+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\psi(y)\nu(dy)\right)\,.

\mu,\nu

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist_{MK,2}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}\pi(dxdy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}\pi_{op}(dxdy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in L^{1}(\mu),\,\psi\in L^{1}(\nu)\\
\phi(x)+\psi(y)\leq|x-y|^{2}\\
\mu\otimes\nu\text{\scriptsize-a.e.}\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\mu(dx)+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\psi(y)\nu(dy)\right)

\displaystyle=

2.1.
A Quantum Analogue to the Kantorovich Duality

The statement below is an analogue of the Kantorovich Duality Theorem (Theorem 1.3 in
[

]
, or Theorem 6.1.1 in
[

]
) for the quantum transport cost operator

Theorem 2.2

(Quantum duality)
.

Let
R,S\in\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H})
. Then

(24)

\min_{F\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F^{1/2}CF^{1/2})=\sup_{(A,B)\in\mathfrak{K}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)\,,

where

\mathfrak{K}:=\{(A,B)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\times\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\text{ s.t. }A=A^{*}\,,B=B^{*}\text{ and }A\otimes I+I\otimes B\leq C\}\,.

In the definition of
\mathfrak{K}
, the inequality

means that

\langle\psi|A\otimes I+I\otimes B|\psi\rangle\leq\langle\psi|C|\psi\rangle

for all
\psi\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(C)
.

Notice that the
\inf
on the left hand side of the duality formula is attained — in other words, there always exists an optimal coupling

. On the contrary, the
\sup
in the right hand side of the duality formula is in general not attained
— at least not attained in the class
\mathfrak{K}
in general.

We shall use repeatedly the following construction. Given a separable Hilbert space

Set

. Define

by the formulas

f(T):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\qquad\hbox{ if }T=T^{*}\geq-C\,,\\
&+\infty&&\qquad\hbox{ otherwise,}\end{aligned}\right.

and

g(T):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)&&\qquad\hbox{ if }T=T^{*}=A\otimes I+I\otimes B\,,\\
&+\infty&&\qquad\hbox{ otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

For each
T=T^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
, the constraint
T\geq-C
in the definition of

is to be understood as follows:

\langle\phi|T|\phi\rangle\geq-\langle\phi|C|\phi\rangle\quad\hbox{ for each }\phi\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(C)\,.

On the other hand, the nullspace of the linear map

\Gamma:\,\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\times\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\ni(A,B)\mapsto A\otimes I+I\otimes B\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})

is
\operatorname{Ker}(\Gamma)=\{(tI,-tI)\text{ s.t. }t\in\mathbf{C}\}
. Since
\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(S)=1
, one has

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)=t\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R-S)=0\quad\text{ for all }(A,B)=(tI,-tI)\in\operatorname{Ker}(\Gamma)

so that

A\otimes I+I\otimes B\mapsto\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)

defines a unique linear functional on
\operatorname{ran}(\Gamma)
. Besides

(A\otimes I+I\otimes B)^{*}=A^{*}\otimes I+I\otimes B^{*}\,,

so that, by cyclicity of the trace,

\displaystyle T=A\otimes I+I\otimes B\text{ and }T=T^{*}\implies A=A^{*}\text{ and }B=B^{*}

\displaystyle\implies g(T)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA^{*}+SB^{*})=\overline{\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(AR+BS)}=\overline{g(T)}

\displaystyle.

Therefore, the prescription above defines indeed a unique function
g
on
E
with values in
(-\infty,+\infty]
.

One easily checks that

and

is the indicator function (in the sense of the definition in §4 of

21
]

while

is the extension by

of

. Clearly,

Moreover

T=T^{*}\hbox{ and }\|T\|<d\hbar\implies T\geq-2d\hbar I\geq-C\,.

T=T^{*}\hbox{ and }\|T\|<d\hbar\implies f(T)=0\,,

0
.

By the Fenchel-Rockafellar duality theorem (Theorem 1.12 in
[
5

\inf_{T\in E}(f(T)+g(T))=\max_{{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime}}(-f^{*}(-{\Lambda})-g^{*}({\Lambda}))\,.

Let us compute

. First

f^{*}(-{\Lambda})=\sup_{T\in E}(\langle-{\Lambda},T\rangle-f(T))=\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{T\in E}{T=T^*\ge-C}}\langle-{\Lambda},T\rangle\,.

If

is not

For
{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime}
such that
{\Lambda}\geq 0

\langle{\Lambda},C\rangle:=\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{T\in E}{T=T^*\le C}}\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle\in[0,+\infty]\,.

(That

f^{*}(-{\Lambda})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\langle{\Lambda},C\rangle&&\qquad\hbox{ if }{\Lambda}\geq 0\,,\\
&+\infty&&\qquad\hbox{ otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Next

g^{*}({\Lambda})=\sup_{T\in E}(\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle-g(T))=\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{T=T^*\in E}{T=A\otimes I+I\otimes B}}(\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle-\operatorname{trace}(RA+SB))\,.

Likewise, if

Hence

g^{*}({\Lambda})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&\qquad\hbox{ if }\langle{\Lambda},A\otimes I+I\otimes B\rangle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)\,,\\
&+\infty&&\qquad\hbox{ otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Notice that the prescription

By the Fenchel-Rockafellar duality theorem recalled above,

\displaystyle\inf_{T\in E}(f(T)+g(T))=\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{A=A^*\,,\,\, B=B^*\in\cL(\fH)}{A\otimes I+I\otimes B\ge-C}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

where
\mathscr{H}
is a separable Hilbert space. If
\ell\in V^{\prime}
satisfies
\ell\geq 0

T=T^{*}\in V\implies\langle\ell,T\rangle\in\mathbf{R}\qquad\text{ and }\quad\|\ell\|=\langle\ell,I_{\mathscr{H}}\rangle\,.

Proof.

Indeed, for all

, one has
-\|T\|I_{\mathscr{H}}\leq T\leq\|T\|I_{\mathscr{H}}
, so that

-\|T\|I_{\mathscr{H}}\leq T\leq\|T\|I_{\mathscr{H}}\,,\quad\text{ so that }-\|T\|\langle\ell,I_{\mathscr{H}}\rangle\leq\langle\ell,T\rangle\leq\|T\|\langle\ell,I_{\mathscr{H}}\rangle\,.

In particular, for all
T=T^{*}\in V
, one has
\langle\ell,T\rangle\in\mathbf{R}
. For all
T\in V
(not necessarily self-adjoint), write

\Re(T)=\tfrac{1}{2}(T+T^{*})\text{ and }\Im(T):=\tfrac{1}{2}i(T^{*}-T)\,.

If
\langle\ell,T\rangle\not=0
, there exists
{\alpha}\in\mathbf{C}
s.t.
|{\alpha}|=1
and
\langle\ell,{\alpha}T\rangle=|\langle\ell,T\rangle|
. These considerations show immediately that
\langle\ell,\Im({\alpha}T)\rangle=0
so that

\displaystyle|\langle\ell,T\rangle|=\langle\ell,\Re({\alpha}T)\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\langle\ell,I_{\mathscr{H}}\rangle\|\Re({\alpha}T)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\ell,I_{\mathscr{H}}\rangle(\|{\alpha}T\|+\|({\alpha}T)^{*}\|)=\langle\ell,I_{\mathscr{H}}\rangle\|T\|\,.

Hence
\|\ell\|\leq\langle\ell,I_{\mathscr{H}}\rangle
, while it is obvious that
\langle\ell,I_{\mathscr{H}}\rangle\leq\|\ell\|
. This concludes the proof of Lemma

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

. Then there exists

such that

Q=Q^{*}\geq 0\,,\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\|Q\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq\|{\Lambda}\|\,,

{\Lambda}=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q{\bullet})+L\,.

\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})=\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})^{\prime}
, one has

{\Lambda}{\big|}_{\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})}=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q{\bullet})\,,

for some
Q\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
.

First, observe that

{\Lambda}\geq 0\implies Q=Q^{*}\geq 0\,.

Indeed, since
Q\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
, then
Q
is compact. Writing

\Re(Q)=\tfrac{1}{2}(Q+Q^{*})\quad\text{ and }\quad\Im(Q)=-\tfrac{i}{2}(Q-Q^{*})\,,

one has
\Re(Q)=\Re(Q)^{*}
and
\Im(Q)=\Im(Q)^{*}

\langle{\Lambda},\Im(Q)\rangle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(\Re(Q)\Im(Q))+i\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(\Im(Q)^{2})\in\mathbf{R}\,.

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(\Re(Q)\Im(Q))=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(\Im(Q)\Re(Q))

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}((\Re(Q)\Im(Q))^{*})=\overline{\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(\Re(Q)\Im(Q))}\in\mathbf{R}\,,

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(\Im(Q)^{2})=0\,,\quad\text{ so that }\quad\Im(Q)^{2})=0\,.

Thus
Q=Q^{*}
. Next observe that, for each

|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|=(|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|)^{*}\geq 0\quad\text{ so that }\quad\langle{\Lambda},|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|\rangle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|)=\langle\xi|Q|\xi\rangle\geq 0\,.

be a complete orthonormal sequence of eigenvectors of

in
\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}
, and let
{\lambda}_{n}
be the eigenvalue of
Q
associated to

. Then

\|Q\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q)=\sup_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\lambda}_{n}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\bigg\langle{\Lambda},\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\phi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|\bigg\rangle\leq\langle{\Lambda},I_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}\rangle=\|{\Lambda}\|\,.

so that

by construction. Let

be the orthogonal projection on
\operatorname{span}(\phi_{0},\ldots,\phi_{n})
. Obviously
\Pi_{n}Q=Q\Pi_{n}
. Then, for each

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle{\Lambda},(I_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}-\Pi_{n})T(I_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}-\Pi_{n})\rangle=\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle-\langle{\Lambda},T\Pi_{n}\rangle-\langle{\Lambda},\Pi_{n}T\rangle+\langle{\Lambda},\Pi_{n}T\Pi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q(T\Pi_{n}+\Pi_{n}T-\Pi_{n}T\Pi_{n}))=\langle{\Lambda},T\rangle-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(\Pi_{n}Q\Pi_{n}T)

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0\leq{\Lambda}\in E^{\prime}
satisfy

\langle{\Lambda},A\otimes I+I\otimes B\rangle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)\,,\quad\hbox{ for all }A=A^{*}\hbox{ and }B=B^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})\,.

Then
{\Lambda}
is of the form

{\Lambda}=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q{\bullet})\,,\quad\hbox{ with }Q=Q^{*}\geq 0\hbox{ and }\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q)=1\,.

Q
is a coupling of
R
and
S
.

Proof.

Let
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots)
be a complete orthonormal system in
\mathfrak{H}
, and let

be the orthogonal projection on
\operatorname{span}(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})

Since
P_{n}\otimes P_{n}\geq 0

0\leq T_{n}\leq I_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes P_{n}+P_{n}\otimes I_{\mathfrak{H}}\leq I_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}\,.

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle{\Lambda},T_{n}\rangle\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q((I_{\mathfrak{H}}-P_{n})\otimes I_{\mathfrak{H}}+I_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes(I_{\mathfrak{H}}-P_{n})))+\langle L,T_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\to\langle L,I_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}\rangle=\langle{\Lambda},I_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}\rangle-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q)

as

are the partial traces of

\displaystyle Q_{1}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})\text{ and }\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q_{1}A)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q(A\otimes I_{\mathfrak{H}}))\,,

\displaystyle Q_{2}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})\text{ and }\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(Q_{2}A)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q(I_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes A))\,,

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\langle{\Lambda},T_{n}\rangle\leq\langle{\Lambda},I_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}\rangle-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q)=1-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q)\,.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((R+S)P_{n})-2\langle{\Lambda},P_{n}\otimes P_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}((R+S)P_{n})-2\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q(P_{n}\otimes P_{n}))

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q^{1/2}(A\otimes I_{\mathfrak{H}}+I_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes B)Q^{1/2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(Q^{1/2}CQ^{1/2})\,.

The inequalities (
42
), (
43
) and (
44
) obviously imply (
41
), and this concludes the proof if Theorem
2.2
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
2.4

Let
(A_{k},B_{k})\in\mathfrak{K}
be a maximizing sequence, i.e.

\operatorname{trace}(RA_{k}+SB_{k})\to\sup_{(A,B)\in\mathfrak{K}}\operatorname{trace}(RA+SB)=:\tau\in[0,+\infty)\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\,.

That
\tau<+\infty
comes from the fact that the
\inf
in Theorem
2.2
is attained by some optimal coupling
F\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)
, and that
R
and
S
both belong to
\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H})
. Indeed, using Lemma
C.3
shows that

\displaystyle F\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)\implies 0\leq\operatorname{trace}(F^{1/2}CF^{1/2})\leq

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{trace}(F^{1/2}(H\otimes I+I\otimes H)F^{1/2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{trace}(R^{1/2}HR^{1/2}+S^{1/2}HS^{1/2})<+\infty\,.

4.1.
Step 1: normalizing the maximizing sequence.

For each
k\geq 1
, set

a_{k}:=2H-A_{k}\quad\text{ and }\quad b_{k}=2H-B_{k}\,.

Thus

\displaystyle a_{k}\otimes I+I\otimes b_{k}

\displaystyle\geq 2(H\otimes I+I\otimes H)-C

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}((-i{\hbar}{\partial}_{x_{j}}-i{\hbar}{\partial}_{y_{j}})^{2}+(x_{j}+y_{j})^{2})=:{\Sigma}\geq 0\,.

The operator
{\Sigma}
satisfies the same uncertainty inequality as
C
:

(45)

\displaystyle{\Sigma}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}((x_{j}+y_{j})+i(-i{\hbar}{\partial}_{x_{j}}-i{\hbar}{\partial}_{y_{j}}))((x_{j}+y_{j})-i(-i{\hbar}{\partial}_{x_{j}}-i{\hbar}{\partial}_{y_{j}}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}i([-i{\hbar}{\partial}_{x_{j}},x_{j}]+[-i{\hbar}{\partial}_{y_{j}},y_{j}])\geq 2d\hbar I\otimes I

\displaystyle,

and

(46)

\operatorname{Form-Dom}({\Sigma})=\{\psi\in\mathfrak{H}\!\otimes\!\mathfrak{H}\text{ s.t. }(x_{j}\!\!+\!y_{j})\psi\text{ and }({\partial}_{x_{j}}\!\!\!+\!{\partial}_{y_{j}})\psi\in\mathfrak{H}\!\otimes\!\mathfrak{H}\,,\,\,1\leq j\leq d\}\,.

Set
{\alpha}_{k}:=\sup\{{\alpha}\in\mathbf{R}\hbox{ s.t. }a_{k}\geq{\alpha}I\}
for each
k\geq 1
. Since
H=H^{*}\geq 0
, one has
a_{k}\geq-A_{k}\geq-\|A_{k}\|I
, so that
{\alpha}_{k}\geq-\|A_{k}\|
. On the other hand, let
e_{0}
be a normalized eigenvector of
R
such that
Re_{0}\not=0
.
Since
R\in\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathfrak{H})
, one has
0\leq\langle e_{0}|H|e_{0}\rangle<+\infty
by (
69
) (see Lemma
C.1
in the Appendix), so that

a_{k}\geq{\alpha}I\implies{\alpha}\leq\langle e_{0}|a_{k}|e_{0}\rangle\leq 2\langle e_{0}|H|e_{0}\rangle+\|A_{k}\|\,.

Hence
{\alpha}_{k}\in[-\|A_{k}\|,2\langle e_{0}|H|e_{0}\rangle+\|A_{k}\|]
. By definition of
{\alpha}_{k}
, there exists
\phi_{n}\in\operatorname{Dom}(H)
such that

\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1\quad\hbox{ and }\langle\phi_{n}|a_{k}|\phi_{n}\rangle\to{\alpha}_{k}\hbox{ as }n\to\infty\quad\hbox{ for each }k\geq 1\,.

Thus

\langle\phi_{n}|a_{k}|\phi_{n}\rangle I+b_{k}\geq 2d\hbar I\quad\hbox{ for each }n\geq 1\,,

so that

{\alpha}_{k}I+b_{k}\geq 2d\hbar I\,.

On the other hand, again by definition of
{\alpha}_{k}
, one has

a_{k}-{\alpha}_{k}I\geq 0\,.

Setting

\hat{a}_{k}:=a_{k}-{\alpha}_{k}I+d\hbar I\,,\quad\hat{b}_{k}:=b_{k}+{\alpha}_{k}I-d\hbar I\,,

one has

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{k}\otimes I+I\otimes\hat{b}_{k}=a_{k}\otimes I+I\otimes b_{k}\geq{\Sigma}

\displaystyle,

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{k}=\hat{a}_{k}^{*}\geq d\hbar I\,,\qquad\hat{b}_{k}=\hat{b}_{k}^{*}\geq d\hbar I

\displaystyle.

Finally

\displaystyle 0\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}\hat{a}_{k}R^{1/2}+S^{1/2}\hat{b}_{k}S^{1/2})=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}a_{k}R^{1/2}+S^{1/2}b_{k}S^{1/2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}HR^{1/2}+S^{1/2}HS^{1/2})

\displaystyle-\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA_{k}+SB_{k})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}HR^{1/2}+S^{1/2}HS^{1/2})-\tau

as
k\to\infty
.

4.2.
Step 2: defining the unbounded operators
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
.

With the minimizing sequence
(a_{k},b_{k})
replaced with its normalized variant
(\hat{a}_{k},\hat{b}_{k})
as explained in the previous section, one has

\displaystyle 0\leq\operatorname{trace}(R^{1/2}\hat{a}_{k}R^{1/2})\leq\sup_{k}\operatorname{trace}(R^{1/2}\hat{a}_{k}R^{1/2})<+\infty\,,

\displaystyle 0\leq\operatorname{trace}(S^{1/2}\hat{b}_{k}S^{1/2})\leq\sup_{k}\operatorname{trace}(S^{1/2}\hat{b}_{k}S^{1/2})<+\infty\,,

since both these sequences are converging as

. Therefore, the sequences of operators
R^{1/2}\hat{a}_{k}R^{1/2}
and
S^{1/2}\hat{b}_{k}S^{1/2}
are bounded in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
. Since
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
is the topological dual of
\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})

(the algebra of compact operators on
\mathfrak{H}
), the Banach-Alaoglu theorem implies that there exists a subsequence of
(\hat{a}_{k},\hat{b}_{k})
(abusively denoted
(\hat{a}_{k},\hat{b}_{k})
for simplicity) such that

Since

\hat{a}_{k}=\hat{a}_{k}^{*}\geq d\hbar I\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\hat{b}_{k}=\hat{b}_{k}^{*}\geq d\hbar I\,,

one has

V=V^{*}\geq d\hbar R\quad\hbox{ and }\quad W=W^{*}\geq d\hbar S\,.

In particular

\operatorname{Ker}(V)\subset\operatorname{Ker}(R)\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{Ker}(W)\subset\operatorname{Ker}(S)\,.

On the other hand

\operatorname{ran}(V)\subset\overline{\operatorname{ran}(R^{1/2})}=\overline{\operatorname{ran}(R)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{ran}(W)\subset\overline{\operatorname{ran}(S^{1/2})}=\overline{\operatorname{ran}(S)}\,.

(To check the first inclusion, pick
\xi=Vx
, and observe that

\langle y|R^{1/2}\hat{a}_{k}R^{1/2}|x\rangle=\operatorname{trace}(R^{1/2}\hat{a}_{k}R^{1/2}|x\rangle\langle y|)\to\langle y|V|x\rangle

so that
\xi_{k}=R^{1/2}\hat{a}_{k}R^{1/2}x\in\operatorname{ran}(R^{1/2})
satisfies
\xi_{k}\to\xi
weakly in
\mathfrak{H}
. Hence
\xi
belongs to the weak closure of
\operatorname{ran}(R^{1/2})
, which is equal to its strong closure
\overline{\operatorname{ran}(R^{1/2})}
since
\operatorname{ran}(R^{1/2})

is a convex subset of
\mathfrak{H}
: see Theorem 3.7 in
[
5
]
.) Since

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}(V)^{\perp}=\overline{\operatorname{ran}(V)}\subset\overline{\operatorname{ran}(R)}=\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp}\,,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}(W)^{\perp}=\overline{\operatorname{ran}(W)}\subset\overline{\operatorname{ran}(S)}=\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}\,,

(see Corollary 2.18 (iv) in
[
5
]
) one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}(V)=\operatorname{Ker}(R)\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\operatorname{ran}(V)}=\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp}\,,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}(W)=\operatorname{Ker}(S)\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\operatorname{ran}(W)}=\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}\,.

In particular

V\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp})\quad\text{ and }\quad W\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp})\,.

Let
\mathfrak{v}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{J}[R],\mathcal{J}[R]^{\prime})
and
\mathfrak{w}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{J}[S],\mathcal{J}[S]^{\prime})
be the operators associated to
V
and
W
by (
30

V=V^{*}
and
W=W^{*}
, one has

\mathfrak{v}^{*}=\mathfrak{v}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathfrak{w}^{*}=\mathfrak{w}\,.

Next

\mathcal{J}_{0}[R]\otimes\mathcal{J}_{0}[S]\subset\operatorname{Form-Dom}(H\otimes I+I\otimes H)\subset\operatorname{Form-Dom}({\Sigma})

where the first inclusion comes from (
31
), and the second from (
21
) and (
46
). By construction, the sequences
(\hat{a}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(\hat{b}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfy

\langle\Phi|\hat{a}_{k}\otimes I+I\otimes\hat{b}_{k}-{\Sigma}|\Phi\rangle\geq 0\quad\hbox{ for all }\Phi\in\mathcal{J}_{0}[R]\otimes\mathcal{J}_{0}[S]\,.

For each
\phi\in\mathcal{J}_{0}[R]
, there exists a unique
\widetilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{J}_{0}[R]
such that
R^{1/2}\widetilde{\phi}=\phi
, so that

\langle\phi|\hat{a}_{k}|\phi\rangle=\langle\widetilde{\phi}|R^{1/2}\hat{a}_{k}R^{1/2}|\widetilde{\phi}\rangle\to\langle\widetilde{\phi}|V|\widetilde{\phi}\rangle=\langle\phi|\mathfrak{v}|\phi\rangle\qquad\text{ as }k\to\infty\,.

Likewise
\langle\psi|\hat{b}_{k}|\psi\rangle\to\langle\psi|\mathfrak{w}|\psi\rangle
as
k\to\infty
for each
\psi\in\mathcal{J}_{0}[R]
. Passing to the limit in the last inequality implies that

\langle\Phi|\mathfrak{v}\otimes I+I\otimes\mathfrak{w}-{\Sigma}|\Phi\rangle\geq 0\quad\hbox{ for all }\Phi\in\mathcal{J}_{0}[R]\otimes\mathcal{J}_{0}[S]\,.

Let
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{J}[R],\mathcal{J}[R]^{\prime})
and
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{J}[S],\mathcal{J}[S]^{\prime})
be the operators associated to
2R^{1/2}HR^{1/2}-V\in\mathcal{L}^{1}((\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp}))
and to
2S^{1/2}HS^{1/2}-W\in\mathcal{L}^{1}((\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}))
respectively. The last inequality on

\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{w}
implies that
(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\in\tilde{\mathfrak{K}}(R,S)
.

4.3.
Step 3: relaxing the constraint.

In this step we prove the following: for each
(\bar{a},\bar{b})\in\tilde{\mathfrak{K}}(R,S)
and each
F\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)
, one has

(47)

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F^{1/2}CF^{1/2})\geq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}\bar{a}R^{1/2}+S^{1/2}\bar{b}S^{1/2})\,.

Let
(e^{\prime}_{j})
and
(f^{\prime}_{l})
be orthonormal sequences of eigenvectors of
R
and
S
belonging to
\mathcal{J}_{0}[R]
and
\mathcal{J}_{0}[S]
, and assumed to be complete in
\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp}
and
\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}
respectively. Call
\mathfrak{p}_{m}
and
\mathfrak{q}_{n}

the orthogonal projections on the
m
first elements of
(e^{\prime}_{j})
and on the
n
first elements of
(f^{\prime}_{l})
respectively, so that

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathfrak{p}_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mathfrak{p}_{m}\leq\sup_{m}\mathfrak{p}_{m}=\mathfrak{P}=\text{ orthogonal projection on }\ker(R)^{\perp}\,,

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathfrak{q}_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mathfrak{q}_{n}\leq\sup_{n}\mathfrak{q}_{n}=\mathfrak{Q}=\text{ orthogonal projection on }\ker(S)^{\perp}\,.

We shall argue instead in terms of the operators

\bar{v}=\bar{v}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{J}[R],\mathcal{J}[R]^{\prime})\quad\text{ and }\quad\bar{w}=\bar{w}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{J}[S],\mathcal{J}[S]^{\prime})

associated by (
30
) to the operators

\displaystyle R^{1/2}HR^{1/2}\!-\!R^{1/2}\bar{a}R^{1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp})\,,

\displaystyle S^{1/2}HS^{1/2}-\,\,S^{1/2}\bar{b}S^{1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp})\,.

Since
(\bar{a},\bar{b})\in\mathfrak{K}(R,S)
, one has
\langle\Phi|C-\bar{a}\otimes I-I\otimes\bar{b}|\Phi\rangle\geq 0
for each
\Phi\in\mathcal{J}_{0}[R]\otimes\mathcal{J}_{0}[S]
, so that, for each
m,n
, one has

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}{\Sigma}_{N}\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}\leq\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}{\Sigma}\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}\leq

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}+\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes(\mathfrak{q}_{n}\bar{w}\mathfrak{q}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes\mathfrak{Q}+\mathfrak{P}\otimes(\mathfrak{q}_{n}\bar{w}\mathfrak{q}_{n})\,,

where
{\Sigma}_{N}:=(I_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}+\tfrac{1}{N}{\Sigma})^{-1}{\Sigma}={\Sigma}_{N}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
and
0\leq{\Sigma}_{N}\leq{\Sigma}
for each
N\geq 1
.

That
(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}\leq(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes\mathfrak{Q}
is seen easily, for instance by the following argument. Let
\Phi
be any element of
\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp}\otimes\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}
, which we decompose on the complete orthonormal system
(e^{\prime}_{j}\otimes f^{\prime}_{l})
:

\Phi=\sum_{j,l}\Phi_{jl}e^{\prime}_{j}\otimes f^{\prime}_{l}\,,\quad\sum_{j,l}|\Phi_{jl}|^{2}=\|\Phi\|_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}^{2}<\infty\,.

Then

\displaystyle\langle\Phi|(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}|\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{1\le j,k\le m}{1\le l\le n}}\overline{\Phi_{jl}}\Phi_{kl}\langle\bar{v}e^{\prime}_{j}|e^{\prime}_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l}\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq m}\overline{\Phi_{jl}}\Phi_{kl}\langle\bar{v}e^{\prime}_{j}|e^{\prime}_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}}=\langle\Phi|\mathfrak{p}_{n}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{n}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}|\Phi\rangle\,,

since the matrix
(\langle\bar{v}e^{\prime}_{j}|e^{\prime}_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}})_{1\leq j,k\leq m}
is Hermitian nonnegative. The analogous inequality for
\bar{w}
is proved similarly.

Thus, for each
F\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)
, one has

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}))\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F((\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes\mathfrak{Q}+\mathfrak{P}\otimes(\mathfrak{q}_{n}\bar{w}\mathfrak{q}_{n})))\,.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R,S\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
and let
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{Q}
be the orthogonal projections on
\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp}
and
\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}
repectively. For each
F\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)
, one has

F=(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})F(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})\,.

Taking this lemma for granted, we conclude the proof of (
47
). First

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F((\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes\mathfrak{Q}))=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})((\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes I)(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}((\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})F(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})((\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes I))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F((\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\otimes I))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m}))=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\bar{v}\mathfrak{p}_{m})R^{1/2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(\mathfrak{p}_{m}R^{1/2}\bar{v}R^{1/2}\mathfrak{p}_{m})\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}\bar{v}R^{1/2})

where the first equality comes from the fact that
\mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{p}_{m}=\mathfrak{p}_{m}=\mathfrak{p}_{m}\mathfrak{P}
, the second and the fifth equality follow by cyclicity of the trace, the third equality from the lemma above, the fourth equality from the fact that
F\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)
,
and the sixth equality from the fact that
\mathfrak{p}_{m}
is a spectral projection of
R
and therefore commutes with
R
. The last inequality is obtained by computing the trace of
R^{1/2}\bar{v}R^{1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
on a complete orthonormal
system in
\mathfrak{H}
whose
m
first vectors span
\operatorname{ran}(\mathfrak{p}_{m})
. By the same token,

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F(\mathfrak{P}\otimes(\mathfrak{q}_{n}\bar{w}\mathfrak{q}_{n})))\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(S^{1/2}\bar{w}S^{1/2})\,.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}))\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F{\Sigma}_{N})

passing to the limit in
m,n
for each
N\geq 1
. Indeed

(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n})\to(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})\quad\text{ strongly in }\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H})

for each
N\geq 1
, since for each
\Psi\in\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}

\displaystyle\|(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})\Psi-(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n})\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}-\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})\Psi\|

\displaystyle+\|(\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}-\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n})\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}-\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n}){\Sigma}_{N}(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q})\Psi\|

\displaystyle+\|{\Sigma}_{N}\|\|(\mathfrak{P}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}-\mathfrak{p}_{m}\otimes\mathfrak{q}_{n})\Psi\|\to 0

in
m,n
for each
N\geq 1
. Then, one concludes as in Example 3 of chapter 2 in
[
23
]
.

Thus, we have proved that

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F{\Sigma}_{N})\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}\bar{v}R^{1/2}+S^{1/2}\bar{w}S^{1/2})\,,\quad\text{ for each }N\geq 1\,.

By Corollary

,
\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F{\Sigma}_{N})\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F^{1/2}{\Sigma}F^{1/2})
as

, so that

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}(F^{1/2}{\Sigma}F^{1/2})\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}\bar{v}R^{1/2}+S^{1/2}\bar{w}S^{1/2})\,,

which is equivalent to the sought inequality (

4.4.
Step 4: the squeezing argument

Pick an optimal coupling
F_{opt}\in\mathcal{C}(R,S)
. (We recall that the existence of such a coupling is one of the conclusions of Theorem
2.2
, and follows from the Fenchel-Rockafellar duality theorem.) One has the following chain of
inequalities:

(48)

\displaystyle\sup_{(A,B)\in\mathfrak{K}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)\leq\sup_{(\bar{a},\bar{b})\in\tilde{\mathfrak{K}}(R,S)}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R^{1/2}\bar{a}R^{1/2}+S^{1/2}\bar{b}S^{1/2})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes 2}}(F_{opt}^{1/2}CF_{opt}^{1/2})

\displaystyle.

The second inequality has been proved in Step 3.

As for the first inequality, observe first that

(49)

\sup_{(A,B)\in\mathfrak{K}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)=\sup_{(A,B)\in\hat{\mathfrak{K}}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)\,,

with the notation

\hat{\mathfrak{K}}:=\{(A,B)\in\mathfrak{K}\text{ s.t. }A\leq 2H-d\hbar I\text{ and }B\leq 2H-d\hbar I\}\,.

This is proved by the normalization argument in Step 1: pick

\rho:=\sup\{{\alpha}\in\mathbf{R}\text{ s.t. }2H-A\geq{\alpha}I\}\,.

Then
\rho\in[-\|A\|,2\langle e_{0}|H|e_{0}\rangle+\|A\|]
, where
e_{0}
is a normalized eigenvector of
R
such that
Re_{0}\not=0
, and one has

A+(\rho-d{\hbar})I\leq 2H-d{\hbar}I\quad\text{ and }\quad B-(\rho-d{\hbar})I\leq 2H-d{\hbar}I

by the same argument as in Step 1. (Indeed, by definition of
\rho
, there exists a sequence
\phi_{n}\in\operatorname{Dom}(H)
such that
\|\phi_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1
and
\langle\phi_{n}|2H-A|\phi_{n}\rangle\to\rho
as
n\to\infty
. With the inequality
A\otimes I+I\otimes B\leq C
,
this implies that, for each
\psi\in\operatorname{Dom}(H)
, one has

\rho\|\psi\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{H}}+\langle\psi|2H-B|\psi\rangle\geq\langle\phi_{n}\otimes\psi|2(H\otimes I+I\otimes H)-C|\phi_{n}\otimes\psi\rangle\geq 2d{\hbar}\|\psi\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{H}}\,,

since
2(H\otimes I+I\otimes H)-C\geq 2d{\hbar}I\otimes I
.) Observing that

(A,B)\in\mathfrak{K}\implies(A+(\rho-d{\hbar})I,B-(\rho-d{\hbar})I)\in\hat{\mathfrak{K}}\,,

and that

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(RA+SB)=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}}(R(A+(\rho-d{\hbar})I)+S(B-(\rho-d{\hbar})I))

leads to (
49
).

Let
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{Q}
be the
\mathfrak{H}
-orthogonal projections on
\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp}
and
\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}
respectively, as in the previous section. We claim that

7.
Examples

In this section, we shall study the optimal operators

and

from the Kantorovich duality theorem, together with the structure of optimal couplings, on a few elementary examples. We will also give a necessary and sufficient condition for the optimal coupling of two quantum densities of semiclassical (Töplitz) type to present the same feature.

7.1.
The case where
R
is a rank-one projection

Let
R=|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|
with
\|\phi\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1
be a rank
1
projection, and let
S
be a finite-rank density operator on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
. By Theorem
2.4
in the finite rank case, the optimal operators
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
should be
sought in the form

\mathfrak{a}:={\alpha}|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|\,,\qquad\mathfrak{b}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\beta}_{k}|e_{k}\rangle\langle e_{k}|\,,

(a) the basis

(b) the real numbers
{\alpha}+{\beta}_{j}

are the eigenvalues of the Hermitian (diagonal) matrix with entries
\langle\phi\otimes e_{j}|C|\phi\otimes e_{k}\rangle
for

These conditions do not completely determine the orthonormal basis

and the real numbers

. For instance, if

of the form (

(e_{j},e_{k})
can be
replaced with its image under any rotation in the plane

.

To prove (a)-(b), we begin with an important observation on the set of couplings of
R
and
S
, which is a straightforward consequence of Lemma 4.1.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume that
R\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
is a projection. Then
\operatorname{rank}(R)=1
and for each
S\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
, one has

\mathcal{C}(R,S)=\{R\otimes S\}\,.

This is the quantum analogue of the case where one considers two Borel probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
, one of which, say
\mu
, is a Dirac measures. In that case, it is obvious that
\Pi(\mu,\nu)=\{\mu\otimes\nu\}
(all the mass from

\nu
is transported to the support of the Dirac measure). Indeed, pure states, corresponding to density operators of the form
R=|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|
where
\phi
is a normalized element of
\mathfrak{H}
, are the quantum analogues of phase space points
in classical mechanics.

Taking this lemma for granted,
R\otimes S
is the optimal coupling — in fact the only coupling — of
R
and
S
. Therefore the optimal operators
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} \operatorname{trace}_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}}((R^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes S^{\frac{1}{2}})(C-\mathfrak{a}\otimes I-I\otimes\mathfrak{b})(R^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes S^{\frac{1}{2}}))=0\,,\\
\langle\Psi|C-\mathfrak{a}\otimes I-I\otimes\mathfrak{b}|\Psi\rangle\geq 0\quad\text{ for }\Psi\!\in\!\operatorname{Ker}(R)^{\perp}\!\otimes\!\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence

\langle\phi\otimes\psi|C-\mathfrak{a}\otimes I-I\otimes\mathfrak{b}|\phi\otimes\psi^{\prime}\rangle=0\quad\text{ for all }\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}\,.

This condition can be checked on any basis of
\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}
. For instance, using the orthonormal basis
(e_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
of eigenvectors of
\mathfrak{b}
leads to the identity

\langle\phi\otimes e_{j}|C|\phi\otimes e_{k}\rangle=({\alpha}+{\beta}_{j}){\delta}_{jk}\,,\quad\text{ for all }j,k=1,\ldots,n\,.

This obviously implies the conclusions (a) and (b) on the real numbers
{\alpha},{\beta}_{1},\ldots,{\beta}_{n}
and the orthonormal basis
(e_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
of
\operatorname{Ker}(S)^{\perp}
.

Let

|q,p\rangle(x):=(\pi{\hbar})^{-d/4}e^{-|x-q|^{2}/2{\hbar}}e^{ip\cdot x/{\hbar}}\,.

15

[

[

[
15
]
.

An optimal element of

In the classical setting, the optimal functions

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tfrac{\ell_{j}^{1/2}\ell_{k}^{1/2}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{2d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4d}}\langle e_{j}|z_{1},z_{2}\rangle\langle z_{1},z_{2}|e_{k}\rangle\langle e_{k}|e_{j}\rangle c(z_{1},z_{2})dq_{1}dp_{1}dq_{2}dp_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}\tfrac{\ell_{j}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{2d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4d}}\langle e_{j}|z_{1},z_{2}\rangle\langle z_{1},z_{2}|e_{j}\rangle c(z_{1},z_{2})dq_{1}dp_{1}dq_{2}dp_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4d}}\sum_{j\geq 1}\tfrac{\ell_{j}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{2d}}\langle e_{j}|z_{1},z_{2}\rangle\langle z_{1},z_{2}|e_{j}\rangle c(z_{1},z_{2})dq_{1}dp_{1}dq_{2}dp_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4d}}\tilde{W}[F](z_{1},z_{2})c(z_{1},z_{2})dq_{1}dp_{1}dq_{2}dp_{2}

\displaystyle.

Since
F=\operatorname{OP}^{T}_{\hbar}[(2\pi{\hbar})^{2d}{\lambda}]
, one has
\tilde{W}_{\hbar}[F]=e^{\frac{1}{2}{\hbar}{\Delta}_{q_{1},p_{1},q_{2},p_{2}}}{\lambda}
(see (51) and the formula following (53) in
[
13
]
), so that

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}(F^{1/2}CF^{1/2})+4d{\hbar}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4d}}\tilde{W}[F](z_{1},z_{2})c(z_{1},z_{2})dq_{1}dp_{1}dq_{2}dp_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4d}}e^{\frac{1}{2}{\hbar}{\Delta}_{q_{1},p_{1},q_{2},p_{2}}}c(q_{1},p_{1},q_{2},p_{2}){\lambda}(dq_{1}dp_{1}dq_{2}dp_{2})

since
e^{\frac{1}{2}{\hbar}{\Delta}_{q_{1},p_{1},q_{2},p_{2}}}
is self-adjoint. On the other hand

e^{\frac{1}{2}{\hbar}{\Delta}_{q_{1},p_{1},q_{2},p_{2}}}c(q_{1},p_{1},q_{2},p_{2})=c(q_{1},p_{1},q_{2},p_{2})+4d{\hbar}\,,

so that

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}(F^{1/2}CF^{1/2})+4d{\hbar}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4d}}(c(q_{1},p_{1},q_{2},p_{2})+4d{\hbar}){\lambda}(dq_{1}dp_{1}dq_{2}dp_{2})\,,

which is equivalent to (
65
).

∎

Appendix A
The Quantum Transport Cost

The quantum cost is the differential operator

C:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}((x_{j}-y_{j})-\hbar^{2}({\partial}_{x_{j}}-{\partial}_{y_{j}})^{2})\,.

For each
f\equiv f(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d})\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, one has

\displaystyle Cf(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(Y_{j}^{2}-4\hbar^{2}{\partial}_{Y_{j}}^{2})f(X_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}Y_{1},X_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}Y_{1},\ldots,X_{d}+\tfrac{1}{2}Y_{d},X_{d}-\tfrac{1}{2}Y_{d})

\displaystyle.

The
d
operators
Y_{j}^{2}-4\hbar^{2}{\partial}_{Y_{j}}^{2}
obviously commute pairwise. Since each one of these operators is the quantum Hamiltonian of a harmonic oscillator, we know that a complete orthonormal system of eigenfunctions for

Y_{j}^{2}-4\hbar^{2}{\partial}_{Y_{j}}^{2}
on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R})
is

(2\hbar)^{-1/4}h_{n}(Y_{j}/\sqrt{2\hbar})\,,\quad n\geq 0\,,

where
h_{n}
is the
n
-th Hermite function

h_{n}(z):=\pi^{-1/4}(2^{n}n!)^{-1/2}e^{-z^{2}/2}H_{n}(z)\,,\quad H_{n}(z):=(-1)^{n}e^{z^{2}}(e^{-z^{2}})^{(n)}\,,

h_{n}(Y_{j}):=(\pi 2\hbar)^{-1/4}(2^{n}n!)^{-1/2}e^{-Y_{j}^{2}/4\hbar}H_{n}(Y_{j}/\sqrt{2\hbar})\,\quad n\geq 0\,,

with

(Y_{j}^{2}-4\hbar^{2}{\partial}_{Y_{j}}^{2})h_{n}(Y_{j}/\sqrt{2\hbar})=2\hbar(2n+1)h_{n}(Y_{j}/\sqrt{2\hbar})\,,\quad n\geq 0\,.

Since the linear transformation of
\mathbf{R}^{2d}

(X_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{d})\mapsto(X_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}Y_{1},X_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}Y_{1},\ldots,X_{d}+\tfrac{1}{2}Y_{d},X_{d}-\tfrac{1}{2}Y_{d})

has Jacobian determinant
(-1)^{d}
, it leaves the Lebesgue measure of
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
invariant, so that

\Psi_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}}(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d}):=(2\hbar)^{-d/2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}h_{m_{j}}(\tfrac{x_{j}+y_{j}}{2\sqrt{2\hbar}})h_{n_{j}}(\tfrac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}})

defines a complete orthonormal system of eigenfunctions of
C
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\overline{\Psi_{m^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,m^{\prime}_{d},n^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,n^{\prime}_{d}}}\Psi_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}}(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d})dx_{1}\ldots dy_{d}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\delta}_{m^{\prime}_{j},m_{j}}{\delta}_{n^{\prime}_{j},n_{j}}

\displaystyle,

with

\displaystyle C\Psi_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}}(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d})

\displaystyle=2\hbar(2(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d})+d)\Psi_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}}(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d})

\displaystyle.

Thus

C=2{\hbar}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{m_1,\ldots,m_d\ge 0}{n_1,\ldots,n_d\ge 0}}(2(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d})+d)|\Psi_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}}\rangle\langle\Psi_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}}|\,;

in other words,
C
has the spectral decomposition

E(d{\lambda})\!\!:=\!\!\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{m_1,\ldots,m_d\ge 0}{n_1,\ldots,n_d\ge 0}}{\delta}({\lambda}-2{\hbar}(2(n_{1}\!+\!\ldots\!+\!n_{d})+d))|\Psi_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}}\rangle\langle\Psi_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d},n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}}|\,.

Appendix B
Monotone Convergence for Trace-Class Operators

Here is an analogue of the Beppo Levi monotone convergence theorem for operators in the form convenient for our purpose.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a separable Hilbert space and
0\leq T=T^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathscr{H})
. For each complete orthonormal system
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of
\mathscr{H}
, set

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T)=\|T\|_{1}:=\sum_{j\geq 1}\langle e_{j}|T|e_{j}\rangle\in[0,+\infty]\,.

See Theorem 2.14 in
[
23
]
; in particular the expression on the last right hand side of these equalities is independent of the complete orthonormal system
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
. Then

T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})\iff\|T\|_{1}<\infty\,.

Lemma B.1

(Monotone convergence)
.

Consider a sequence
T_{n}=T_{n}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})
such that

(i)

0\leq T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq\ldots\leq T_{n}\leq\ldots\,,\qquad\text{ and }\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle<+\infty\text{ for all }x\in\mathscr{H}\,,

or

(ii)

0\leq T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq\ldots\leq T_{n}\leq\ldots\,,\qquad\text{ and }\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathcal{H}}(T_{n})<+\infty\,.

Then

(a) there exists
T=T^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathscr{H})
such that
T\geq 0
and
T_{n}\to T
weakly as
n\to\infty
, and

(b)
\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T)
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

First we prove statements (a) and (b) under assumption (i). Since the sequence
\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle\in[0,+\infty)
is nondecreasing for each
x\in\mathscr{H}
,

\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle\to\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle=:q(x)\in[0,+\infty)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathscr{H}

as
n\to\infty
. Hence

\langle x|T_{n}|y\rangle=\langle y|T_{n}|x\rangle\to\tfrac{1}{4}(q(x+y)-q(x-y)+iq(x-iy)-iq(x+iy))=:b(x,y)\in\mathbf{C}

as
n\to+\infty
. By construction,
b
is a nonnegative sesquilinear form on
\mathscr{H}
.

Consider, for each
k\geq 0
,

F_{k}:=\{x\in\mathscr{H}\text{ s.t. }\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle\leq k\text{ for each }n\geq 1\}\,.

The set
F_{k}
is closed for each
k\geq 0
, being the intersection of the closed sets defined by the inequality
\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle\leq k
as
n\geq 1
. Since the sequence
\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle
is bounded for each
x\in\mathscr{H}
,

\bigcup_{k\geq 0}F_{k}=\mathscr{H}\,.

Applying Baire’s theorem shows that there exists
N\geq 0
such that
\mathring{F}_{N}\not=\varnothing
. In other words, there exists
r>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathscr{H}
such that

|x-x_{0}|\leq r\implies|\langle x|T_{n}|x\rangle|\leq N\text{ for all }n\geq 1\,.

By linearity and positivity of
T_{n}
, this implies

|\langle z|T_{n}|z\rangle|\leq\tfrac{2}{r}(M+N)\|z\|^{2}\text{ for all }n\geq 1\,,\quad\text{ with }M:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle x_{0}|T_{n}|x_{0}\rangle\,.

In particular

\sup_{|z|\leq 1}q(z)\leq\tfrac{2}{r}(M+N)\,,\quad\text{ so that }|b(x,y)|\leq\frac{2}{r}(M+N)|\|x\|_{\mathscr{H}}\|y\|_{\mathscr{H}}

for each
x,y\in\mathscr{H}
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathscr{H})
such that

T=T^{*}\geq 0\,,\quad\text{ and }\quad b(x,y)=\langle x|T|y\rangle\,.

This proves (a). Observe that
T\geq T_{n}
for each
n\geq 1
, so that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})\leq\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T)\,.

In particular

\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})=+\infty\implies\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T)=+\infty\,.

Since the sequence
\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})
is nondecreasing,

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})\to\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty\,.

By the noncommutative variant of Fatou’s lemma (Theorem 2.7 (d) in
[
23
]
),

\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})<\infty\implies T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})\text{ and }\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T)\leq\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})\,.

Since the opposite inequality is already known to hold, this proves (b).

Next we prove (a) and (b) under assumption (ii). Since any
x\in\mathscr{H}\setminus\{0\}
can be normalized and completed into a complete orthonormal system of
\mathscr{H}
, one has

\sup_{n\geq 1}\langle x|T|x\rangle\leq\|x\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\sup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T_{n})<\infty\,.

Thus, assumption (ii) implies (i), which implies in turn (a) and (b).
∎

Appendix C
The Finite Energy Condition

Let
A=A^{*}\geq 0
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathscr{H}
with domain
\operatorname{Dom}(A)
, and let
E
be its spectral decomposition.

Throughout this section, we assume that
T\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})
satisfies
T=T^{*}\geq 0
, and let
(e_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a complete orthonormal system of eigenvectors of
T
with
Te_{j}=\tau_{j}e_{j}
and
\tau_{j}\in[0,+\infty)
for each
j\geq 1
,
such that

(66)

\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle<\infty\,.

Lemma C.1
.

Under the assumptions above,

(67)

T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}:=\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\right)|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{k}|

satisfies
0\leq T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}=(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})
and

(68)

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})=\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle\,.

In particular

(69)

e_{j}\in\operatorname{Ker}(T)^{\perp}\implies e_{j}\in\operatorname{Form-Dom}(A)\,.

Proof.

For each Borel
{\omega}\subset\mathbf{R}
and each
x,y\in\mathscr{H}
, one has

|\langle x|E({\omega})|y\rangle|=|\langle E({\omega})x|E({\omega})y\rangle|\leq\|E({\omega})x\|\|E({\omega})y\|=\langle x|E({\omega})|x\rangle^{1/2}\langle y|E({\omega})|y\rangle^{1/2}

since
E({\omega})
is a self-adjoint projection. In particular, for each
{\alpha}>0
, one has

2|\langle x|E({\omega})|y\rangle|\leq{\alpha}\langle x|E({\omega})|x\rangle+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}}\langle y|E({\omega})|y\rangle\,.

Hence, for all
j,k\geq 1

a_{jk}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\in\mathbf{C}

satisfies

2|a_{jk}|^{2}\leq{\alpha}a_{jj}+\tfrac{1}{{\alpha}}a_{kk}\text{ for all }{\alpha}>0\,,\text{ so that }|a_{jk}|^{2}\leq a_{jj}a_{kk}\,.

Since
(\tau_{j}a_{jj})_{j\geq 1}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbf{N}^{*})
by (
66
) and since

\langle e_{j}|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}|e_{k}\rangle=\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}a_{jk}=\overline{\langle e_{k}|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}|e_{j}\rangle}\,,

one concludes that
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}=(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathscr{H})
. Moreover, for each
x\in\mathscr{H}

\displaystyle\langle x|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}|x\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\overline{\langle e_{j}|x\rangle}\langle e_{k}|x\rangle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\bigg\langle\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\langle e_{j}|x\rangle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\langle e_{j}|x\rangle e_{j}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle T^{1/2}x|E(d{\lambda})|T^{1/2}x\rangle\geq 0\,,

so that
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}\geq 0
. Finally

\displaystyle\sum_{l\geq 1}\langle e_{l}|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}|e_{l}\rangle=\sum_{l\geq 1}\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\right)\langle e_{l}|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{k}|e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\geq 1}\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\right){\delta}_{lj}{\delta}_{lk}=\sum_{l\geq 1}\tau_{l}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{l}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle<\infty

so that
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})
, with

\|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}\|_{1}=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})=\sum_{l\geq 1}\tau_{l}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{l}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{l}\rangle<\infty\,.

In particular for each
j\geq 1
, one has

\tau_{j}>0\implies\langle e_{j}|A|e_{j}\rangle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle\leq\tfrac{1}{\tau_{j}}\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})<\infty\,,

and this proves (
69
).
∎

Corollary C.2
.

Let
\Phi_{n}\in C(\mathbf{R}_{+})
satisfy

0\leq\Phi_{1}(r)\leq\Phi_{2}(r)\leq\ldots\leq\Phi_{n}(r)\to r\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty\,.

For each
n\geq 1
, set

\Phi_{n}(A):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}({\lambda})E(d{\lambda})\in\mathcal{L}(\mathscr{H})\,,\text{ so that }\Phi_{n}(A)=\Phi_{n}(A)^{*}\geq 0\,.

In the limit as
n\to\infty
, one has

T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi_{n}(A)T^{\frac{1}{2}}\to T^{\frac{1}{2}}AT^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ weakly, and }\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T\Phi_{n}(A))\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T^{\frac{1}{2}}AT^{\frac{1}{2}})\,.

Proof.

Since
E
is a resolution of the identity on
[0,+\infty)
, and since
\Phi_{n}
is continuous, bounded and with values in
[0,+\infty)
, the operators
\Phi_{n}(A)
satisfy

0\leq\Phi_{1}(A)\leq\Phi_{2}(A)\leq\ldots\leq\Phi_{n}(A)\leq\Phi_{n}(A)^{*}\leq\left(\sup_{z\geq 0}\Phi_{n}(z)\right)I_{\mathscr{H}}\,.

Set
R_{n}:=T^{1/2}\Phi_{n}(A)T^{1/2}
; by definition, one has
R_{n}=R_{n}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
and

0\leq R_{1}\leq R_{2}\leq\ldots\leq R_{n}\leq\ldots

On account of (
66
), one has

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(R_{n})=\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}({\lambda})\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle<\infty\,.

By Lemma
B.1
, one has
R_{n}\to R
weakly, with
R\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})
and
R=R^{*}\geq 0
. Finally

T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}=\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}({\lambda}-\Phi_{n}({\lambda}))\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{k}\rangle\right)|e_{j}\rangle\langle e_{k}|

using the definition (
67
) of
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}
given in Lemma
C.1
, so that

\displaystyle\langle x|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}|x\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}({\lambda}-\Phi_{n}({\lambda}))\bigg\langle\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}^{1/2}\langle e_{j}|x\rangle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|\sum_{k\geq 1}\tau_{k}^{1/2}\langle e_{k}|x\rangle e_{k}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}({\lambda}-\Phi_{n}({\lambda}))\langle T^{1/2}x|E(d{\lambda})|T^{1/2}x\rangle\geq 0\,.

Hence
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}=(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})
with
T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}\geq 0
, and

\displaystyle\|T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n}\|_{1}=

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}-R_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}\tau_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}({\lambda}-\Phi_{n}({\lambda}))\langle e_{j}|E(d{\lambda})|e_{j}\rangle\to 0

as
n\to\infty
by monotone convergence. Therefore
R_{n}\to T^{1/2}AT^{1/2}
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H})
, and

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T\Phi_{n}(A))=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T^{1/2}\Phi_{n}(A)T^{1/2})\to\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty\,.

∎

Lemma C.3
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{H}})
satisfy the partial trace condition
\operatorname{trace}_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}}(S)=T
. Then
0\leq S^{1/2}(A\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2}=(S^{1/2}(A\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathscr{H}\otimes\widetilde{\mathscr{H}})

\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}\otimes\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}}(S^{1/2}(A\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2})=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}}(T^{1/2}AT^{1/2})\,.

Proof.

For all
n\geq 1
, set
A_{n}=\Phi_{n}(A)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
, with
\Phi_{n}(r):=\frac{r}{1+r/n}
for all
r\geq 0
. Thus

A_{n}=A_{n}^{*}\quad\text{ and }\quad 0\leq A_{1}\leq A_{2}\leq\ldots\leq A_{n}\leq\ldots

Hence
S^{1/2}(A_{n}\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2}=(S^{1/2}(A_{n}\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2})^{*}
for all
n\geq 1
, with

0\leq S^{1/2}(A_{1}\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2}\leq S^{1/2}(A_{2}\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2}\leq\ldots\leq S^{1/2}(A_{n}\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2}\leq\ldots

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}\otimes\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}}(S^{1/2}(A_{n}\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2})=\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}\otimes\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}}(S(A_{n}\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}}))

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}_{\mathscr{H}\otimes\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}}(S^{1/2}(A_{n}\otimes I_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}})S^{1/2})

by Lemma

. This implies the announced equality by uniqueness of the limit.
∎

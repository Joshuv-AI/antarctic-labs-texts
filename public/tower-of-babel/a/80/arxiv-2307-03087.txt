Sobolev spaces and trace theorems for time-fractional evolution equations Thanks: D. Kim and K. Woo were supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF) funded by the Korean government (MSIT) (2019R1A2C1084683).
Doyoon Kim, Kwan Woo

Abstract
. We establish trace and extension theorems for evolutionary equations with the Caputo fractional derivatives in (weighted) L p L_{p} spaces. To achieve this, we identify weighted Sobolev and Besov spaces with mixed norms that accommodate solution spaces and their initial values well-suited for equations involving time-fractional derivatives. Our analysis encompasses both time-fractional sub-diffusion and super-diffusion equations. We also provide observations on the initial behavior of solutions to time-fractional equations.

1.
Introduction

This paper focuses on the
L_{p}
theory of the initial value problem for evolution equations involving time-fractional derivatives.
A representative example of the time-fractional equations we consider is given by:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\Delta u=0&\quad\text{in}\quad{(0,T)\times\Omega},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot)&\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Delta
can be replaced with
a^{ij}D_{ij}
(non-divergence form) or
D_{i}(a^{ij}D_{j}u)
(divergence form).
Here,
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
represents the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
, defined as

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left(u\left(s,x\right)-u_{0}\left(x\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Time-fractional equations as in (
1.1
) have been widely recognized as a valuable approach for describing dynamics governed by anomalous diffusion, as discussed, for instance, in
[
23
]
.
Thus, the mathematical analysis of such equations becomes essential.
The main goal of this paper is to establish an appropriate framework that can discuss initial value problems for equations of type (
1.1
) in Sobolev spaces.
In particular, we require trace and extension theorems that identify the optimal function spaces for initial conditions.
To achieve this, we first explore various properties of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
as a function in the weighted mixed norm space
L_{p,q,w}
and
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}
. See Section
2.2
for the definition of
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}
.
Based on these properties, we define solution spaces and prove trace and extension theorems for these spaces.
Trace theorem provides a rigorous background for discussing the initial value of a solution in Sobolev spaces, while extension theorem ensures that the initial data space considered in the trace theorem is optimal.
Notably, it turns out that if the time regularity
\alpha
is too small, we cannot define
u(0,\cdot)
in
L_{p}
sense adequately; see Remark
2.6
.
This issue can be overcome by appropriately choosing
q
(integrability with respect to the time variable) and temporal weights.
This is also why the mixed norm and weight should be considered; see Remark
4.7
for a more detailed description.
Note that if the order of time smoothness of solution
u
is a positive integer (
e.g.

\alpha=1
), then the precise regularity of
u(0,\cdot)
is well-known in the literature, for example, see
[
30
]
.

There has been a huge body of literature on time-fractional equations, and the following is a brief overview of the literature closely related to initial value problems in Sobolev-type spaces with fractional derivatives in the time variable.
In
[
32
]
and
[
33
]
, the authors obtained an unweighted and unmixed
L_{q}
-theory for the Volterra type equations in non-divergence form.
The solution spaces used in their work are the vector-valued Bessel potential spaces
\bar{H}_{q}^{\alpha}
(see Remark
2.10
-(ii) for the definition of
\bar{H}_{q}^{\alpha}
).
This theory is based on the operator theoretical approach and holds under the assumption that
\alpha>1/q
.
For equations in divergence form, discussions on the
L_{2}
theory within the Sobolev-Slobodetskii spaces can be found in
[
20
]
and references therein (also see Remark
2.10
-(i)).
A totally different approach was employed in
[
6
]
to obtain weighted mixed norm estimates for solutions to (
1.1
) under minimal assumptions on the coefficient
a^{ij}=a^{ij}(t,x)
.
The focus in
[
6
]
is the zero initial value problems, but it also addresses non-zero initial values (without trace theorem), which is related to the extension theorem for a specific class of initial values.
Related results can be found in
[
18
]
and
[
8
]
.
Studies on traces of weighted vector-valued Sobolev-Slobodetskii as well as Bessel potential spaces can be found in
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
.
In a recent study
[
1
]
, the authors established trace theorems for weighted Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, using temporal weights
t^{\mu}
with the assumption that
\alpha>(1+\mu)/q
and
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
.
The results presented in
[
1
]
encompass many previous findings on trace theorems.
Additionally, in
[
1
, Section 6]
, the authors applied their results to derive trace estimates for solutions in Bessel potential spaces to various evolution equations, such as time-fractional diffusion equations.
Note that, except for
[
6
]
(and
[
18
,
8
]
), the aforementioned papers use the intersection of two Banach space-valued function spaces as a solution space to evolution equations in the form

\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,p}(J;X_{0})\cap L^{p}(J;X_{1})
,
where
\mathcal{A}^{s,p}
represents the Sobolev-Slobodetskii space, the Bessel
potential space, or the Triebel-Lizorkin space, and
(X_{0},X_{1})
is a pair of Banach spaces.
In particular, when
J=(0,T)
, for instance, the Triebel-Lizorkin space
\mathcal{A}^{\alpha,p}(J)=F_{p,q}^{\alpha}(J)
is given by a restriction of
F_{p,q}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
.
This restriction is unavoidable when defining function spaces on a finite time interval
J=(0,T)
because the definition of
F^{\alpha}_{p,q}
requires the whole space, i.e.,
\mathbb{R}
, especially when
\alpha
is non-integer.

The results presented in this paper exhibit several methodological differences compared to previous studies on traces of Sobolev-type spaces with time-fractional regularity and initial value problems for time-fractional evolution equations.
For example, the literature
[
27
,
32
,
33
,
1
]
makes use of semigroup and operator theory to address maximal
L_{p}
regularity for time-fractional equations, which is suitable when regarding solutions to the equations as Banach space-valued functions.
In this approach, the operator
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
can be interpreted as
(\partial_{t})^{\alpha}
in a semigroup sense, and various properties of the domain of
(\partial_{t})^{\alpha}
, such as embedding and interpolation results, can be utilized.
In contrast, we directly construct solution spaces (see Definition
3.2
) for time-fractional evolution equations on a given time interval
(0,T)
using fractional calculus.
We view our solutions as functions of
(t,x)
defined on the domain
(0,T)\times\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
Our constructed spaces align well with the approach used in
[
16
,
5
,
6
,
15
,
7
,
8
]
for obtaining
L_{p}
estimates of solutions to time-fractional parabolic equations (with rough coefficients).
In particular, if equations in divergence form contain singular drifts as in
[
15
]
, it becomes necessary to consider the case
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\Delta u=D_{i}g_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}f_{k}
with
g_{i}\in L_{p}
and
f_{k}\in L_{p_{k}}
(
p_{k}<p
).
For such cases, to use an operator theoretical approach, one may need to answer if solution spaces can be represented by an intersection of two Banach space-valued function spaces, especially when the spatial boundary is irregular.
Our construction easily applies to this case.
Furthermore, the trace theorems (see Theorems
3.11
and
3.10
) for the constructed spaces are proved using fundamental tools such as integration by parts and Hardy’s inequality.
Thus, our approach can be seen as an alternative for researchers who prefer to work with function spaces for time-fractional equations without relying on semigroup and operator theory (an alternative approach for solution spaces related to those in this paper).
We not only concentrate on trace and extension theorems but also provide comprehensive explanations of the initial behavior of a solution, depending on the relationship between
\alpha
,
q
, and temporal weights.
See remarks in Section
2
.
Furthermore, unlike the Bessel potential spaces
\bar{H}_{q}^{\alpha}(0,T)
, where the initial value of functions cannot be well-defined if
\alpha\leq 1/q
, as illustrated by Hörmander’s example in
[
11
]
, the initial value
u(0,x)
of a solution
u(t,x)
is well-defined even when
\alpha=1/q
(or
\alpha=(1+\mu)/q
for the weighted case) in our setting.
It is worth mentioning that
[
1
]
focuses on trace theorems (without extension theorems) for anisotropic weighted Tribel-Lizorkin spaces, and our trace theorems when
T\to\infty
correspond to a special case of the trace results mentioned in
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Regarding extension theorems (see Theorems
3.14
and
3.15
), if the time regularity is
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there are some results in
[
26
]
, but they differ from Theorem
3.14
in this paper because the function spaces for the spatial variables in
[
26
]
are different from ours.
Note that we also prove Theorem
3.15
for the case where the time-regularity is
1+\alpha\in(1,2)
.

Let us present one special case of the main results in this paper.
Our solution spaces for the time-fractional equations in non-divergence form are
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,2}
introduced in Definition
3.2
.
We prove that the weighted Besov spaces
B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{2\theta}
(Definition
3.8
) are the exact function spaces for initial values for the time-fractional equations in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,2}
.
More precisely, if
\theta=1-(1+\mu)/(q\alpha)\in(0,1)
(in particular,
\alpha>(1+\mu)/q
), we prove that the following trace operator is bounded (trace theorem)

\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,2}\to B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{2\theta},\quad\textrm{where}\quad\mathcal{T}u(t,x):=u(0,x)

and prove that
B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{2\theta}
is the smallest space satisfying the above (extension theorem).
As a key step in proving the trace theorem, we take an appropriate integral representation of
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,2}
with the help of a certain type of mollification
u^{(\varepsilon)}
, which is also used to obtain Sobolev inequality
[
15
,
7
]
and regularity of solutions on the boundary of the spatial domain (i.e., lateral trace)
[
14
]
.
If there is no spatial weight, i.e.,
w_{2}(x)=1
, we also give an alternative proof for the trace theorem (see Remark
4.3
).
For the extension theorem, we solve a homogeneous equation with a non-zero initial value
u_{0}\in B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{2\theta}
.
To this end, we derive precise time decay estimates for the fundamental solution
P_{\alpha}
of the time-fractional heat equation.
We also obtain trace estimates of
\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u
(non-negative integer
k
), and solve the initial value problem for super-diffusion equation
\partial_{t}^{1+\alpha}u-\Delta u=0
.

2.
Function spaces with fractional regularity in time

This section presents preliminary work on defining Sobolev-type solution spaces for time-fractional evolution equations to be discussed in Section
3
. One of the solution spaces defined in Section
3
is:

\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,2}=\{u\in L_{p}:u\in\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,0},Du,D^{2}u\in L_{p}\},

where the norm is given by

\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,2}}=\left\|\left|u\right|+\left|Du\right|+\left|D^{2}u\right|+|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u|\right\|_{L_{p}}.

To construct such spaces, in this section, we focus on studying the fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in the context of
L_{p}
spaces and examining its properties.
Here is a summary of the contents in this section:

•

We start by defining function spaces with fractional regularity in time:
\mathbb{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,0}
and
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,0}
for the non-divergence case (see Definition
2.2
),
\mathcal{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,-1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\alpha,-1}
for the divergence case (see Definition
2.16
).

•

Next, we investigate properties related to the initial values of
\mathbb{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,0}
and
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,0}
(respectively,
\mathcal{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,-1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\alpha,-1}
) that depend on the relationship between
\alpha
and
p
. These properties are discussed in Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
(respectively, Lemmas
2.17
and
2.18
).

•

Finally, we define
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
and the norms of
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\alpha,-1}
using the construction outlined above (see Definitions
2.5
and
2.19
).

•

Additionally, we present essential properties of the fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in the remaining lemmas, propositions, and remarks throughout this section.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we denote

I^{\alpha}f(t,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad I^{1}f(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s,

and
I^{n}f=I^{1}\left(I^{n-1}f\right)
for
n=2,3,\cdots
. We set

J^{\alpha}\varphi(t,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{T}(r-t)^{\alpha-1}\varphi(r,x)\,\mathrm{d}r.

(2.1)

For
p\in(1,\infty)
and a positive integer
d
, we let
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x)=A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the set of all locally integrable non-negative functions
w
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},r>0}\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{r}(x_{0})}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\operatorname{\,\,\text{\bf--}\kern-9.79996pt\intop\nolimits\!\!}_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left(w\left(x\right)\right)^{-1/(p-1)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

where
B_{r}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x-x_{0}|<r\}
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain and
\Omega_{T}:=(0,T)\times\Omega
for
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Note that throughout the paper, we assume that
T
is a positive real number unless otherwise specified (for instance,
T
can be
\infty
in Lemma
2.1
).
For
w(t,x)=w_{1}(t)w_{2}(x)=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)
, where
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
and
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we set
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
to be the set of all measurable functions
f
defined on
\Omega_{T}
such that

\|f\|_{L_{p,q,w}}(\Omega_{T}):=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}|f(t,x)|^{p}w_{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q/p}w_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

We set
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})=L_{p}(\Omega_{T})
if
p=q
and
w=1
, i.e.,
\mu=0
and
w_{2}(x)=1
.
We similarly define
L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
and
L_{q,w_{1}}(0,T)
.
One can use
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\Omega)
instead of
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if the domain
\Omega
is a space of homogeneous type. See
[
4
, Section 2]
for more details.

Before presenting function spaces incorporating fractional derivatives, we introduce a lemma on the inequality of the Hardy type, which serves as a useful tool throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
q\in(1,\infty)
, and
\mu<q-1
. Then for any function
f
defined on
(0,T)
with
T\in(0,\infty]
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}|t^{-\alpha}I^{\alpha}f|^{q}t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq N\int_{0}^{T}|f|^{q}t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t,

(2.2)

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)>0
.

Proof.

Note that when
\alpha=1
, (
2.2
) is a version of classical Hardy’s inequality. If
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it is a direct consequence of
[
12
, Theorem 10]
by replacing
f
with
f1_{(0,T)}
in
[
12
, Theorem 10]
.
∎

Now we introduce function spaces with fractional derivatives.

2.1.
Non-divergence case

We present a set of definitions and properties for time fractional derivatives that are associated with time-fractional evolution equations in non-divergence form.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and
w(t,x)=w_{1}(t)w_{2}(x)=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)
, where
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
and
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We define
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
and
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
as follows.

(i)

By
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, we mean that
u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
and there exists
f\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
such that

\int_{\Omega_{T}}I^{1-\alpha}u\,\varphi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}f\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(2.3)

for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times\Omega\right)
.
Clearly,
\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u=f
.
When
p=q
and
w=1
, we set
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})=\mathbb{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.

(ii)

By
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, we mean that
u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
and there exists
u_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
such that

u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T}).

We again suppress
q
and
w
if
p=q
and
w=1
(i.e.,
\mu=0
with
w_{2}(x)=1
).

In our notation, the superscript
0
in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
and
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
indicates that no regularity of
u
is required with resect to the spatial variables.
On the other hand, the subscript
0
in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
signifies that the initial value of each element of
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
is zero, whenever it is well-defined (see Lemma
2.4
, Theorem
3.11
, and Theorem
3.12
).

The norms for
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
and
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
will be introduced in Definition
2.5
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,(1+\mu)/q)
and
u_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
.
Then,
u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
as a function on
\Omega_{T}
.
Thus,
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})=\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
for
\alpha\in(0,(1+\mu)/q)
.

Proof.

Clearly,

I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}=\frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}u_{0}(x)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}=\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}u_{0}(x),

and we see that
\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
because
\alpha q<1+\mu
.
By integration by parts and the fact that
I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}|_{t=0}=0
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{T}}I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}\,\varphi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times\Omega\right)
.
Therefore, by Definition
2.2
,
u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.

If
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, there exists
u_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
such that
u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
Since we also have
u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, it follows that

u=(u-u_{0})+u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T}).

The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
and
u_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
.
If
u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, then
u_{0}=0
.
Thus, for
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, there exists a unique
u_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
such that
u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
when
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
.

Proof.

If
u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, by Definition
2.2
, the derivative
\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}
exists in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
.
On the other hand,

\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}=\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}u_{0}(x)

in the distribution sense.
Because
t^{-\alpha}w_{1}(t)^{1/q}=t^{-\alpha+\mu/q}\notin L_{q}(0,T)
(recall that
w(t,x)=w_{1}(t)w_{2}(x)=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)
), we have
\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
only when
u_{0}=0
.

To prove the uniqueness of
u_{0}
, for
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, let
u_{0},v_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
satisfy

u-u_{0},\,u-v_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T}).

Then,
u_{0}-v_{0}=(u-u_{0})-(u-v_{0})\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
From this, along with the fact that
u_{0}-v_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
and the first assertion of the lemma proved above, we get
u_{0}=v_{0}
.
∎

Thanks to the above lemmas we are ready to define
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
and the norm of
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and
w(t,x)=w_{1}(t)w_{2}(x)=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)
, where
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
and
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
\alpha\in(0,(1+\mu)/q)
and
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
,
we denote

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u.

For
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
and
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, by Lemma
2.4
, there exists a unique
u_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega_{T})
such that
u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
In this case, we denote

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0}).

Then, the norm of
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
is defined by

\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})}=\|u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}.

For the case
p=q=2
with
\mu=0
, a similar notion of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
to Definition
2.5
can be found in
[
34
]
and
[
20
]
.

Remark 2.6
.

(i) Note that
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
is a subspace
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
with
u_{0}=0
.
Thus, for
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, we have

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(u-0)=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u.

Regarding the norm for
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, we use the same norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})}
(and the same notation).

(ii) In Definition
2.5
, unlike the case where
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
,
u_{0}
is not involved in defining
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
for
\alpha\in(0,(1+\mu)/q)
.
This distinction arises because the initial value
u_{0}
cannot be properly defined when
\alpha\in(0,(1+\mu)/q)
.
See Lemma
2.3
.
One can observe such inadequacy in the following example for the case when
p=q
and
w(t,x)=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)=1
.
For
\phi(x)(\neq 0)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, set
u_{n}(t,x)=\phi(x)
, and
v_{n}(t,x)=t^{1/n}\phi(x)
for
n=1,2,\cdots
.
By direct computation, we see that
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{n}-\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{m}=0
for all
n,m=1,2,\cdots
, and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v_{n}-\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v_{m}\to 0
as
n,m\to\infty
, provided that
\alpha<1/p
.
But,
u_{n}(0,x)=\phi(x)\neq 0=v_{n}(0,x)
for all
n=1,2,\cdots
.
On the other hand, in the case of
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
, Lemma
2.4
guarantees the well-definedness of the initial trace, at least in a framework of Definition
2.2
.
See Theorems
3.11
and
3.12
for initial trace results concerning functions with both fractional time derivatives and spatial derivatives (precisely, those in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
and
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
).

The following lemma is used in the proof of Lemma
2.8
.
Recall the definition of
J^{\alpha}\varphi
in (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.7
.

For
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\left(0,T\right)\times\Omega\right)
, we have
J^{\alpha}\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega)
and

\partial_{t}J^{\alpha}\varphi=J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi.

Proof.

To check
\partial_{t}J^{\alpha}\varphi=J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi
, for
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,T)
, we consider

\int_{0}^{T}\phi^{\prime}(t)J^{\alpha}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}I^{\alpha}\phi^{\prime}(t)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}I^{\alpha}\phi(t)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}t,

where we know that
\partial_{t}I^{\alpha}\phi=I^{\alpha}\phi^{\prime}
because
\phi(0)=0
.
Thus,

\int_{0}^{T}\phi^{\prime}(t)J^{\alpha}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}I^{\alpha}\phi(t)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}I^{\alpha}\phi(t)\,\partial_{t}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\phi(t)J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}t,

where we used the fact that
I^{\alpha}\phi|_{t=0}=0
and
\varphi(T,x)=0
.
This shows that
\partial_{t}J^{\alpha}\varphi=J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
Then,
u=I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u

and

\|u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq NT^{\alpha}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})},

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)>0
.
Thus, if
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
and
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
with
u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
,
then

\|u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq NT^{\alpha}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+NT^{(1+\mu)/q}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)},

(2.4)

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)>0
.

Proof.

For
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, by Lemma
2.7

J^{\alpha}\varphi(t,x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega)
and
\partial_{t}J^{\alpha}\varphi=J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi
.
Hence,
u=I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u
holds because of the following calculation.

\int_{\Omega_{T}}I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}uJ^{\alpha}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}I^{1-\alpha}u\,\partial_{t}J^{\alpha}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}I^{1-\alpha}uJ^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}I^{\alpha}I^{1-\alpha}u\,\partial_{t}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\Omega_{T}}u\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

which we essentially used that (
2.3
) holds for
J^{\alpha}\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega)
.

By the fact that
u=I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u
and (
2.2
) of Lemma
2.1
,

\|u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}=\|I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq NT^{\alpha}\|\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}.

To prove (
2.4
), we simply write

u=u-u_{0}+u_{0}=I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0})+u_{0}=I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+u_{0}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
Then

\|t^{-\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq N\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})},

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)>0
.
Furthermore, if
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
and
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
with
u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, we have

\|t^{-\alpha}(u-u_{0})\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq N(\alpha,q,\mu)\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}.

Proof.

By definition, the second assertion follows from the first one. Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
and denote
f=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
.
Then by Lemma
2.8

u=I^{\alpha}f
.
Since
f\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
, by Minkowski inequality and (
2.2
) of Lemma
2.1
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}|I^{\alpha}f(t,x)|^{p}w_{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q/p}t^{-q\alpha+\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{T}|I^{\alpha}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}|^{q}t^{-q\alpha+\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t

\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}^{q}t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)
.
We obtain the desired inequality by recalling that
I^{\alpha}f=u
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

We here comment on the equivalence between
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,0}
and the function spaces with fractional regularity in time that appear in literature
[
20
]
and
[
32
,
33
]
.

(i) Lemma
2.8
shows that, for
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
,

\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega)}\simeq\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})}.

Let us use the notation
\mathbb{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,0}(0,T)
for functions without spatial variables with
w_{1}(t)=1
.
Then, the above equivalence indicates that
\mathbb{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,0}(0,T)
, when
p=2
, is the same as
H_{\alpha}(0,T)
defined in
[
20
]
, where the authors state that

\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{2}(0,T)}\simeq\|u\|_{H_{\alpha}(0,T)}.

Indeed, in
[
20
]
the authors defines
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
as an inverse operator of the operator
I^{\alpha}
from
L_{2}(0,T)\to H_{\alpha}(0,T)
.
Thus, for any
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,0}(0,T)
, we have
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\in L_{2}(0,T)
and by Lemma
2.8
,
I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=u
, which means that
u\in H_{\alpha}(0,T)
.
On the other hand, if
u\in H_{\alpha}(0,T)
, then there exists
f\in L_{2}(0,T)
such that
u=I^{\alpha}f
.
Then,
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}I^{\alpha}f=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}I^{\alpha}f=f
.
We also see that (
2.3
) is satisfied. Hence, by Definition
2.2

u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,0}^{\alpha,0}(0,T)
.
Also note that Lemma
2.9
shows that, when
\alpha=1/2
,

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|u(t)|^{2}}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty

for
u\in\mathbb{H}_{2,0}^{1/2,0}(\Omega_{T})
,
which is required for
u\in H_{1/2}(0,T)
in
[
20
]
.

(ii) As noted in
[
33
, Example 2.1]
, if
p=q
,
w(t,x)=1
and
\alpha\neq 1/p
,
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
is equivalent to

\bar{H}_{p}^{\alpha}\left(\left(0,T\right),L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\right):=\{u|_{(0,T)}:u\in\bar{H}_{p}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R},L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\right)\},

where
\bar{H}_{p}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R},L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
is an
L_{p}(\Omega)
-valued Bessel potential space.
The norm is given by

\|u\|_{\bar{H}_{p}^{\alpha}\left(\left(0,T\right),L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\right)}=\inf\{\|v\|_{\bar{H}_{p}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R},L_{p}\left(\Omega\right)\right)}:v|_{(0,T)}=u\}.

The author in
[
33
]
implicitly demonstrated the equivalence between
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
and
\bar{H}_{p}^{\alpha}\left(\left(0,T\right),L_{p}(\Omega)\right)
by using results from operator analysis and the Sobolev embedding theorem.
In fact, one can obtain the above equivalence by using
[
29
, (7.4)]
with an extension of

to
\mathbb{R}\times\Omega
.
For the case
\alpha=1/p
, we can show that
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
is equivalent to a proper subset of
\bar{H}_{p}^{\alpha}\left((0,T),L_{p}(\Omega)\right)
.
At least, Lemma
2.12
below implies that they are not equivalent because one cannot define the initial trace of functions in
\bar{H}_{p}^{\alpha}\left((0,T),L_{p}(\Omega)\right)
.
See Remark
3.16
-(ii) for more discussion about the case
\alpha=1/p
.

Proposition 2.11
.

For
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, there is a sequence
\{u_{k}\}
such that
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T]\times\Omega\right)
,
u_{k}(t,x)
vanishes for large
|x|
(if
\Omega
is unbounded),
u_{k}(0,x)=0
, and
u_{k}\to u
in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

We know
I^{1-\alpha}u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
.
By Definition
2.2
,
\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
.
Thus,
I^{1-\alpha}u\in W_{p,q,w}^{1,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
Moreover, the equality (
2.3
) implies that
I^{1-\alpha}u(t,x)|_{t=0}=0
.
Then, there exists a sequence
\{w_{k}\}
such that
w_{k}\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T]\times\Omega\right)
,
w_{k}(t,x)
vanishes for large
|x|
,
w_{k}(0,x)=0
, and
w_{k}\to I^{1-\alpha}u
in
W_{p,q,w}^{1,0}(\Omega_{T})
as
k\to\infty
.
In particular,

w_{k}\to I^{1-\alpha}u,\quad\partial_{t}w_{k}\to\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u

in
L_{p}(\Omega_{T})
.
Set

u_{k}(t,x)=I^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}w_{k})(t,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\partial_{t}w_{k}(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Then,
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
,
u_{k}(t,x)
vanishes for large
|x|
, and
u_{k}(0,x)=0
.
Since
\partial_{t}w_{k}\to\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u
in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
, we have
u_{k}=I^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}w_{k})\to I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u
in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
, where
I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u=u
by Lemma
2.8
.
We also have

\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u_{k}=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}I^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}w_{k})=\partial_{t}w_{k}\to\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u

in
L_{p}(\Omega_{T})
.
Therefore,
\{u_{k}\}
is a desired sequence.
∎

The following Lemmas
2.12
and
2.13
contain the behavior of
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
at
t=0
.
The complete description of initial traces for functions in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
with additional temporal and spatial Sobolev regularities (that is, functions in

.

Lemma 2.12

(Trace inequality)
.

Let
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
and
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
with
u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
Then,

\|u_{0}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}\leq N\left(T^{-(1+\mu)/q}\|u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+T^{\alpha-(1+\mu)/q}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\right),

(2.5)

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)>0
. Furthermore, if
T\geq 1
, we have

\|u_{0}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}\leq N(\alpha,q,\mu)\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})}.

Proof.

Denote
f:=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0})=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
.
Observe that

I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}=I^{1-\alpha}u-I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0})=I^{1-\alpha}u-\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where the second equality is due to the fact that (
2.3
) holds for
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times\Omega\right)
with
u
replaced with
u-u_{0}
.
Since

I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}=\frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}u_{0}(x),

we have

\frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}u_{0}(x)=I^{1-\alpha}u-\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s.

By taking the
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
-norms on both sides of the above equality, (when
T\geq 1
, we take
L_{p,q,w}((0,1)\times\Omega)
-norms), we have

T^{(1-\alpha)+(1+\mu)/q}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}\leq N\left(\|I^{1-\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\right)

\leq N(\alpha,q,\mu)\left(T^{1-\alpha}\|u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+T\|f\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\right),

where the last inequality is due to (
2.2
) of Lemma
2.1
.
Thus, the inequality (
2.5
) is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\alpha\in((1+\mu)/q,1)
if
\mu\in[0,q-1)
and let
\alpha\in(1/q,1)
if
\mu\in(-1,0)
. For
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
with
u_{0}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
and
u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, there is a version of
u
(still denoted by
u
) such that

\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}\leq Nt^{\alpha-(1+\mu)/q}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}

(2.6)

for
t\in(0,T)
, where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)>0
.
Thus,

\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}\to 0

as
t\searrow 0
.
Furthermore, we have

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}\leq N(\alpha,q,\mu,T)\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})}.

(2.7)

Proof.

Note that (
2.7
) follows from (
2.6
) with the help of (
2.5
) in Lemma
2.12
and the fact that
\alpha-(1+\mu)/q>0
under the assumptions on
\alpha,q
and
\mu
.

Since
u-u_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
, by Lemma
2.8
,

u(t,x)-u_{0}(x)=I^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s

for
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
.
By taking
L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
-norms of both sides along with the Minkowski inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}s

\leq N(\alpha)\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{q^{\prime}(\alpha-1)}s^{-(q^{\prime}-1)\mu}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}^{q}s^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/q}

\leq N(\alpha,q,\mu)t^{\alpha-(1+\mu)/q}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)}^{q}s^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/q},

where the last inequality is due to the assumptions on
\alpha
and
\mu

\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)

2.13

2.12
) and (

\langle\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u_{0},\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(t^{-\alpha}f\left(x\right)\varphi-t^{-\alpha}g_{i}\left(x\right)D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
.
Then

\|t^{-\alpha}u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}\leq N\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})},

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)>0
.
Furthermore, if
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
and
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
with
u-u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
, we have

\|t^{-\alpha}(u-u_{0})\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}\leq N(\alpha,q,\mu)\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}.

Proof.

By definition, the second assertion follows from the first one.
Let
g_{i},f\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
satisfy (
2.15
).
Then, by Lemma
2.21
, for
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
,

\langle t^{-\alpha}u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\langle u,t^{-\alpha}\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,J^{\alpha}(t^{-\alpha}\varphi)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}

=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(fJ^{\alpha}(t^{-\alpha}\varphi)-g_{i}J^{\alpha}(t^{-\alpha}D_{i}\varphi)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\Omega_{T}}t^{-\alpha}\left(I^{\alpha}f\,\varphi-I^{\alpha}g_{i}D_{i}\,\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

where as shown in the proof of Lemma
2.9
, by (
2.2
) of Lemma
2.1
we have

\|t^{-\alpha}I^{\alpha}f\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})},\quad\|t^{-\alpha}I^{\alpha}g_{i}\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq N\|g_{i}\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}.

Hence, we see that the desired inequality follows.
∎

Proposition 2.24
.

If
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
, there exists a sequence
\{u_{k}\}
such that
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
,
u_{k}(t,x)
vanishes for large
|x|
(if
\Omega
is unbounded),
u_{k}(0,x)=0
, and
u_{k}\to u
in
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

We prove the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
, let

\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}(F\varphi-G_{i}D_{i}\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

\langle\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}(f\varphi-g_{i}D_{i}\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for
\varphi\in W_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},w^{\prime}}^{0,1}(\Omega_{T})
, where
F,G_{i},f,g_{i}\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
.

For
\xi=\xi(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, using the definition of
I^{1-\alpha}
in (
2.12
), we see that
I^{1-\alpha}(\xi u)=\xi I^{1-\alpha}u
.
Then,
\langle\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(\xi u),\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}
is equal to

\langle\xi\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left((\xi f-g_{i}D_{i}\xi)\varphi-(\xi g_{i})D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, which further holds for all
\varphi\in\mathring{W}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},w^{\prime}}^{0,1}(\Omega_{T})
.
Hence,
\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(\xi u)=\xi\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u

in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
.
This indicates that we can assume that
u
,
F
,
G_{i}
,
f
, and
g_{i}
have compact support.

Let
\eta(t)
and
\phi(x)
be non-negative infinitely differentiable functions with compact support on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively, such that
\eta(t)=0
for
t\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
.
We then set

\varphi(t,x)=\eta(t)\phi(x),\quad\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\varphi(t/\varepsilon^{2},x/\varepsilon).

For each
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
, we see that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t-s,x-y)\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega)
as a function of
(s,y)
.
Thus, for
w\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
,

w^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=\langle w,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t-\cdot,x-\cdot)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}

is well defined.
Then

u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=\int_{\Omega_{T}}F(s,y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t-s,x-y)\,dy\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\Omega_{T}}G_{i}(s,y)(D_{i}\varphi_{\varepsilon})(t-s,x-y)\,dy\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=F^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)+D_{i}G_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x),

(2.18)

and similarily,

(\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u)^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=f^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)+D_{i}g_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x),

where, for a function
h
on
\Omega_{T}=(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote

h^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=\int_{\Omega_{T}}h(s,y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t-s,x-y)\,dy\,\mathrm{d}s

=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}1_{0<s<T}h(s,y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t-s,x-y)\,dy\,\mathrm{d}s.

We now prove

\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=(\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u)^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)

(2.19)

on
\Omega_{T}
.
If this is the case,
\{u^{(\varepsilon)}\}
is a desired sequence.
Indeed,
u^{(\varepsilon)}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
and

u^{(\varepsilon)}(0,x)=F^{(\varepsilon)}(0,x)+D_{i}G_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}(0,x)=0,

because
\eta(t)=0
for
t\leq 0
.
Moreover,
u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)
vanishes for large
|x|
because
F
and
G_{i}
have compact support.
Since
(F^{(\varepsilon)},G_{i}^{(\varepsilon)},f^{(\varepsilon)},g_{i}^{(\varepsilon)})\to(F,G_{i},f,g_{i})
in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
,
we see that

u^{(\varepsilon)}\to u,\quad(\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u)^{(\varepsilon)}\to\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u

in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
, which together with (
2.19
) means that
u^{(\varepsilon)}\to u
in
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
.

To prove (
2.19
), we first see that from (
2.18
)

I^{1-\alpha}u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=I^{1-\alpha}F^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)+I^{1-\alpha}D_{i}G_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)\\
=(I^{1-\alpha}F)^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)+D_{i}(I^{1-\alpha}G_{i})^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)

(2.20)

on
\Omega_{T}
,
where the second equality is due to the choice of
\eta(t)
(see the proof of
[
5
, Proposition 3.2]
) so that, for a function
h
on
\Omega_{T}
, we have

(I^{1-\alpha}h)^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=I^{1-\alpha}h^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x).

By the equalities in (
2.20
) we have

\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=\partial_{t}(I^{1-\alpha}F)^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)+\partial_{t}D_{i}(I^{1-\alpha}G_{i})^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x),

which finally proves (
2.19
) because

(\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u)^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=\langle\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t-\cdot,x-\cdot)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}

=\langle I^{1-\alpha}u,(\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon})(t-\cdot,x-\cdot)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}

=\int_{\Omega_{T}}(I^{1-\alpha}F)(s,y)(\partial_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon})(t-s,x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s

+\int_{\Omega_{T}}(I^{1-\alpha}G_{i})(s,y)(\partial_{t}D_{i}\varphi_{\varepsilon})(t-s,x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s

=\partial_{t}(I^{1-\alpha}F)^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)+\partial_{t}D_{i}(I^{1-\alpha}G_{i})^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x).

For general
\Omega
, the claim is proved using a partition of unity with respect to the spatial variables. In particular, for
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have
\zeta u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{T})
.
The proposition is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.25

(Trace inequality)
.

Let
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
and
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
with
u-u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
.
Then,

\|u_{0}\|_{H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq NT^{-(1+\mu)/q}\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}+NT^{\alpha-(1+\mu)/q}\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})},

(2.21)

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)>0
. Furthermore, if
T\geq 1
, we have

\|u_{0}\|_{H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq N(\alpha,q,\mu)\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})}.

Proof.

Since
u,\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0})\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
, there exist
F,G_{i},f,g_{i}\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
such that, for
\varphi\in\mathring{W}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},w^{\prime}}^{0,1}(\Omega_{T})
,

\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(F\varphi-G_{i}D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

\langle I^{1-\alpha}u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left((I^{1-\alpha}F)\varphi-(I^{1-\alpha}G_{i})D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

and

\langle\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0}),\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(f\varphi-g_{i}D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

For
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left((0,T)\times\Omega\right)
, set

\Phi(t,x)=\int_{t}^{T}\varphi(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times\Omega\right).

Then,
\Phi_{t}(t,x)=-\varphi(t,x)

and by definition, we have

\langle I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0}),\Phi_{t}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=-\langle\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0}),\Phi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}.

This shows that

\langle I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0}),\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(f\Phi-g_{i}D_{i}\Phi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(\tilde{f}\varphi-\tilde{g}_{i}D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

where

\tilde{f}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad\tilde{g}_{i}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{i}(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
I^{1-\alpha}u_{0}=I^{1-\alpha}u-I^{1-\alpha}(u-u_{0})
in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
,
it follows that

\langle I^{1-\alpha}u_{0},\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left((I^{1-\alpha}F-\tilde{f})\varphi-(I^{1-\alpha}G_{i}-\tilde{g}_{i})D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(2.22)

for any
\varphi\in\mathring{W}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},w^{\prime}}^{0,1}(\Omega_{T})
.
If
\varphi=\phi(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by the definition of
I^{1-\alpha}
and (
2.14
)

\langle I^{1-\alpha}u_{0},\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\langle u_{0},\phi\rangle_{H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)}\int_{0}^{T}t^{1-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=\frac{T^{2-\alpha}}{(2-\alpha)(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\langle u_{0},\phi\rangle_{H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)}.

(2.23)

Note that

\|I^{1-\alpha}F-\tilde{f}\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq NT^{1-\alpha}\|F\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+NT\|f\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})},

\|I^{1-\alpha}G_{i}-\tilde{g}_{i}\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq NT^{1-\alpha}\|G_{i}\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+NT\|g_{i}\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})},

where
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)
.
From these inequalities with (
2.22
) and (
2.23
), we get (
2.21
). If
T\geq 1
, we choose
\varphi(t,x)=\phi(x)\eta(t)
, where
\phi(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\eta(t)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,1)
with
\eta(t)\geq 0
,
\eta(t)=1
on
(1/4,3/4)
.
∎

Let
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
satisfy

\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(F\varphi-G_{i}D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
F,G_{i}\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
.
Set

\langle u(t,\cdot),\phi\rangle_{H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\left(F(t,x)\phi(x)-G_{i}(t,x)D_{i}\phi(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

for

, which is a well-defined for a.e.

.
That is,

for a.e.

.

Lemma 2.26
.

\alpha\in((1+\mu)/q,1)

(2.25)

3.
Main result: solution spaces and initial traces

This section introduces solution spaces for time-fractional evolution equations using the function spaces defined in section
2
.
Then, as the main results of this paper, we present initial trace theorems along with extension theorems for the constructed solution spaces.

3.1.
Solution spaces

Let
k
be a positive integer.
Note that if
\partial_{t}^{m}v\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
(respectively,
\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
) for
m=0,1,\ldots,k
, then by Remark
2.15
(respectively, Remark
2.27
),
\partial_{t}^{m}v(0,x)
,
m=0,1,\ldots,k-1
, is well-defined in
L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
(respectively, in
H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)
). Here,
\partial_{t}^{m}v\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
is defined by, for some
g_{m,i}\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
,
i=1,\ldots,d
, and
f\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
,

\langle\partial_{t}^{m}v,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(f\varphi-g_{m,i}D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for any
\varphi\in\mathring{W}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},w^{\prime}}^{0,1}(\Omega_{T})
.
With these observations at hand, we define function spaces whose elements have
(k+\alpha)
-order fractional time derivatives, as well as zero initial values, where
k=1,2,\ldots
.
Recall that we have already addressed the case
k=0
in Definitions
2.2
and
2.16
.
We also define function spaces having both spatial and fractional time regularities with zero initial values.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
k
be a non-negative integer,
1<p,q<\infty
, and
w=w_{1}(t)w_{2}(x)=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)
with
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
and
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(i) By
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
with
k\geq 1
, we mean that
\partial_{t}^{m}u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
with
\partial_{t}^{m}u(0,x)=0
for
m=0,1,\ldots,k-1
, and
\partial_{t}^{k}u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
Similarly, by
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
, we mean that
\partial_{t}^{m}u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
with
\partial_{t}^{m}u(0,x)=0
for
m=0,1,\ldots,k-1
, and
\partial_{t}^{k}u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
.

(ii) By
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
, we mean that
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
and
Du,D^{2}u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
.
Similarly, by
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
,
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
and
u,Du\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
.

Using the above definition, we now introduce solution spaces for time-fractional evolution equations.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
k
be a non-negative integer,
1<p,q<\infty
, and
w=w_{1}(t)w_{2}(x)=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)
with
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
and
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(i)

For
k=0
, we define

\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})=\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T}):=\{u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T}):Du,D^{2}u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})\}

with a norm

\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})}:=\left\|\left|Du\right|+\left|D^{2}u\right|\right\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})}.

Similarly, we define

\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})=\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T}):=\{u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T}):u,Du\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})\}

with a norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})}:=\left\|\left|u\right|+\left|Du\right|\right\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})}.

(ii)

For a positive integer
k
, we define
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
by

\{u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T}):\partial_{t}^{k}u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T}),Du,D^{2}u,\partial_{t}u,\ldots,\partial_{t}^{k-1}u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})\}

with a norm

\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})}:=\left\|\left|u\right|+\left|Du\right|+\left|D^{2}u\right|+\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}u\right|\right\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{k}u\right\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})}.

Similarly, we define
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
by

\{u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T}):\partial_{t}^{k}u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T}),Du\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T}),\partial_{t}u,\ldots,\partial_{t}^{k-1}u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})\}

with a norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})}:=\left\|\left|u\right|+\left|Du\right|\right\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}+\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}u\right\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{k}u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})}.

In both cases, we denote
\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u:=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}^{k}u)=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(\partial_{t}^{k}u-u_{k})
for
u_{k}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
and
u_{k}\in H^{-1}_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
, respectively.
Note that we do not consider the interchange of the integer and fractional order derivatives, that is,

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}^{k}u)=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}\left(\partial_{t}^{k}u-u_{k}\right)\neq\partial_{t}^{k}\left(\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}\left(u-u_{0}\right)\right)=\partial_{t}^{k}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u).

Remark 3.3
.

If
u
is sufficiently smooth, a standard definition for
(k+\alpha)
-times fractional derivative of
u
in the literature is

\partial_{t}^{k+1}I^{1-\alpha}\left(u(t)-\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\partial_{t}^{m}u(0)\right).

(3.1)

It is worth noting that the
(k+\alpha)
-times fractional derivative considered in this paper (
\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u:=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}^{k}u)
in Definition
3.2
) can also be represented as in (
3.1
).
Indeed, for
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
, if
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
, there is a
u_{k}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
such that
\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}\left(\partial_{t}^{k}u-u_{k}\right)
in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
. On the other hand, since
\partial_{t}^{m}u(0,x)\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
,
m=0,1,\ldots,k-1
, exists at least in the trace sense (Remark
2.15
), we also have
\{u_{m}\}_{m=0}^{k}\subset L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
such that

\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}\left(\partial_{t}^{k}u-u_{k}\right)=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}\left(\partial_{t}^{k}\left(u-\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}u_{m}\right)\right)

=\partial_{t}^{k+1}I^{1-\alpha}\left(u-\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}u_{m}\right)\quad\textrm{in}\quad L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T}),

where, for the last equality, one can directly check that
I^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}^{k}v=\partial_{t}^{k}I^{1-\alpha}v
with
v:=u-\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}u_{m}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
.
If
\alpha\in(0,(1+\mu)/q)
, we just set
u_{k}=0
.

Similarly, for
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
with
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
, there exists
\{u_{m}\}_{m=0}^{k}\subset H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)
(Remark
2.27
) such that

\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u=\partial_{t}^{k+1}I^{1-\alpha}\left(u-\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}u_{m}\right)\quad\textrm{in}\quad\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T}),

where
u-\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}u_{m}\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
. If
\alpha\in(0,(1+\mu)/q)
, we just set
u_{k}=0
.

Proposition 3.4
.

\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
and
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
are Banach spaces.

Proof.

We only consider the case
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
since the proof for the case
\alpha\in(0,(1+\mu)/q)
is similar.
Let
\{u^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
. It is clear that there is a
u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
such that
Du,D^{2}u,\partial_{t}u,\ldots,\partial_{t}^{k}u\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
and
u^{n},Du^{n},D^{2}u^{n},\partial_{t}u^{n},\ldots,\partial_{t}^{k}u^{n}
converge to
u,Du,D^{2}u,\partial_{t}u,\ldots,\partial_{t}^{k}u
in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
, respectively.
On the other hand, there is a (unique)
u^{n}_{k}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
such that
\partial_{t}^{k}u^{n}-u^{n}_{k}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
. By Lemma
2.12
,
\{u^{n}_{k}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
, so there is a
u_{k}\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
satisfying
u^{n}_{k}\to u_{k}
in
L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
. Suppose
\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u^{n}\to f
in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
. Then, for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times\Omega\right)
,

\int_{\Omega_{T}}I^{1-\alpha}(\partial_{t}^{k}u-u_{k})\,\varphi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{T}}I^{1-\alpha}(\partial_{t}^{k}u^{n}-u^{n}_{k})\,\varphi_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\Omega_{T}}f\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

where we use the fact that
\|I^{1-\alpha}f\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}\leq N(\alpha,q,T)\|f\|_{L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})}
and
\partial_{t}^{k}u,u_{k}\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
. Hence,
\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u=f
in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
and
u^{n}
converges to
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
.

The proof for
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
is almost the same, except that we use Lemma
2.25
instead of Lemma
2.12
. The proposition is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

If
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
(respectively,
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
), there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
such that
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
,
u_{n}(t,x)
vanishes for large
|x|
(if
\Omega
is unbounded),
\partial_{t}^{m}u_{n}(0,x)=0
for
m=0,1,\ldots,k
, and
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
(respectively,
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
) as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to the proofs of Propositions
2.11
and
2.24
.
For simplicity, we give a sketched proof. In particular, for the case of
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as in the proof of Proposition
2.24
, we set for
w\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
,

w^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)=\langle w,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t-\cdot,x-\cdot)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})},\quad(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},

where
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t-s,x-y)\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega)
as a function of
(s,y)
.
Then, for
m=0,1,\ldots,k-1
, since
\partial_{t}^{m}u(0,x)=0
in the trace sense, one verifies that
\partial_{t}^{m+1}u^{(\varepsilon)}=(\partial_{t}^{m+1}u)^{(\varepsilon)}
.
Indeed, if
v,\partial_{t}v\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
with
v(0,x)=0
, there exists
g_{i},f\in L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
such that

\langle\partial_{t}v,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=-\langle v,\varphi_{t}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})}=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(f\varphi-g_{i}D_{i}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times\Omega\right)
. From this fact and the choice of
\eta
where
\varphi(t,x)=\eta(t)\phi(x)
(see the proof of Proposition
2.24
), we have
\partial_{t}^{m+1}u^{(\varepsilon)}=(\partial_{t}^{m+1}u)^{(\varepsilon)}
for
m=0,1,\ldots,k-1
. Then it follows that
\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}^{k}u^{(\varepsilon)}=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}(\partial_{t}^{k}u)^{(\varepsilon)}=(\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}^{k}u)^{(\varepsilon)}\to\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}^{k}u
in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})
.
Du^{(\varepsilon)}=(Du)^{(\varepsilon)}\to Du
in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
is directly follows from the definition of
u^{(\varepsilon)}
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

If
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
(respectively,
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
), there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
such that
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
,
u_{n}(t,x)
vanishes for large
|x|
(if
\Omega
is unbounded), and
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
(respectively,
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
) as
n\to\infty
.
In particular, if
\alpha\in[(1+\mu)/q,1)
, for
v\in L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
(respectively,
H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)
) satisfying
\partial_{t}^{k}u-v\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,0}(\Omega_{T})
(respectively,
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
), we have
\partial_{t}^{k}u_{n}(0,\cdot)\to v
in
L_{p,w_{2}}(\Omega)
(respectively,
H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)
) as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

We prove only the case when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For a general
\Omega
, one can use a partition of unity argument, for example, in
[
24
]
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
. As mentioned in Remark
3.3
, there exists
\{u_{m}\}_{m=0}^{k}\subset H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)

.
Now for a given
\delta>0

variable (after multiplying cut-off function if necessary) to

and

, we obtain a

.
Here, for

u_{m}^{(\varepsilon)}(x):=\langle u_{m},\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)\rangle_{H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}(\Omega)},

where
\phi(x)

.
Then by Lemma
3.5
, there is a sequence
\{w_{n}\}
such that
w_{n}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
,
w_{n}(t,x)
vanishes for large
|x|
, and
w_{n}\to v-\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}v_{m}

Note that by Lemma
2.25
,\partial_{t}^{k}u_{n}(0,\cdot)\to v
in

as

, where

satisfies
\partial_{t}^{k}u-v\in\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w,0}^{\alpha,-1}(\Omega_{T})
.
The proof for the case

The proposition is proved.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
we use in this paper is of the Caputo type, but there are many other notions of fractional derivatives.
For example, in
[
2
]
, the authors use the Marchaud fractional derivative
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}
to investigate a De Giorgi-Nash-Moser Hölder regularity theory for solutions to equations in divergence form. For sufficiently smooth function
u
defined on
[0,T]
,
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u
is defined by

\Gamma(1-\alpha)\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t):=\frac{u(t)-u(0)}{t^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u(t)-u(s)}{(t-s)^{\alpha+1}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad 0<t<T,

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Note that, by using integration by parts, we verify that
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
and
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u
are equivalent if
u\in C^{1}[0,T]
.
Also, for
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
with
\alpha\geq(1+\mu)/q
,
we have

\Gamma(1-\alpha)\mathcal{D}_{t,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}u:=\frac{u(t,x)-u_{0}(x)}{t^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}\frac{u(t,x)-u(s,x)}{(t-s)^{\alpha+1}}\,\mathrm{d}s\to\Gamma(1-\alpha)\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u

in
L_{p,q,w}(\Omega_{T})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

3.2.
Spaces for initial values

To establish the initial trace spaces for
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
and
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
, we introduce (weighted) Besov and Bessel potential spaces.

Definition 3.8
.

Take a
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\operatorname{supp}\hat{\psi}=\{1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
,
\hat{\psi}\geq 0
, and
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\psi}(2^{-j}\xi)=1
for
\xi\neq 0
where
\hat{\psi}:=\mathcal{F}\psi
.
Denote
\hat{\psi}_{j}(\xi)=\hat{\psi}(2^{-j}\xi)
for
j=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots
and
\hat{\Psi}(\xi)=1-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\hat{\psi_{j}}(\xi)
.

For
1<p,q<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the weighted Besov space as follows:

B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{s}=B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|\Psi*f\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\ 2^{jsq}\|\psi_{j}*f\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

Here,
\varphi*f
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by
\varphi*f(x)=\langle f,\varphi(x-\cdot)\rangle
, where
\langle f,\varphi(x-\cdot)\rangle
means the action of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
on the test function
\varphi(x-\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We also define weighted Bessel potential space as follows.

H_{p,w_{2}}^{s}=H_{p,w_{2}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}L_{p,w_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and
H_{p,w_{2}}^{0}=L_{p,w_{2}}
, where
(1-\Delta)^{s/2}
is defined by

(1-\Delta)^{s/2}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{s/2}\mathcal{F}u\right).

Note that the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}f=\hat{f}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

\langle\hat{f},\varphi\rangle=\langle f,\hat{\varphi}\rangle

for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
In particular, if
u\in H_{p,w_{2}}^{-1}
, there exists
g_{i},f\in L_{p,w_{2}}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,d
, such that
u=D_{i}g_{i}+f
in the distribution sense.

Theorem 3.11

(Trace theorem)

Let

,

,

Remark 3.16
.

(i)
An unweighted version (i.e.,

On the other hand, with respect to the spatial variables,

2

(iii) In

]

4.1.
Proof of Theorems
3.11
and
3.12

In this subsection, we prove the (initial) trace embedding for
\mathbb{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,2}(\Omega_{T})
and
\mathcal{H}_{p,q,w}^{k+\alpha,1}(\Omega_{T})
.
We fix a non-negative integer
k
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Recall that
w(t,x)=w_{1}(t)w_{2}(x)=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)
where
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
and
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

To prove Theorem
3.11
, we present an integral representation of
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times\Omega)
satisfying

\partial_{t}^{k+\alpha}u(t,x)=\partial_{t}^{k+1}I^{1-\alpha}\left(u(t,x)-\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\partial_{t}^{m}u(0,x)\right)=f(t,x)

(4.1)

(see Remark
3.3
).
Take a non-negative
\eta=\eta(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset(-1,-1/2)
and
\|\eta\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}=1
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, set
\eta_{\varepsilon}(t):=\varepsilon^{-1/\beta}\eta(t\varepsilon^{-1/\beta})
where
\beta:=k+\alpha
.
Then for
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\Omega
and

u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(t-s)u(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s,

we write

u(t,x)=u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)-\left(u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)-u(t,x)\right)

=u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(u(s,x)-u(t,x)\right)\eta_{\varepsilon}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s=:u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)-v(t,x;\varepsilon).

Let
\gamma(\lambda)
be a path from
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by

\gamma(\lambda)=(1-\lambda^{1/\beta})t+\lambda^{1/\beta}s,\quad\lambda\in[0,1].

Since

u(s,x)-u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla_{t,x}u)(\gamma(\lambda))\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

we see that

u(s,x)-u(t,x)=u\left(\gamma(1),x\right)-u\left(\gamma(0),x\right)=\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}\left(\gamma\left(\lambda\right),x\right)\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

with

\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left(\lambda^{1/\beta}(s-t)+t\right)=\frac{1}{\beta}\lambda^{1/\beta-1}(s-t).

Then,

v(t,x)=v(t,x;\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}(\gamma(\lambda),x)\lambda^{1/\beta-1}(s-t)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\eta_{\varepsilon}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s

=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u_{s}(s,x)\,\zeta\left(\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/\beta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

where
\zeta(t)=-t\eta(t)
and the last equality is due to the change of variables
\gamma(\lambda)\to s
, and then
\varepsilon\lambda\to\lambda
.
For a non-negative integer
n
such that
n\leq k
, by taking
n
-times differentiation with respect to
t
and by integration by parts with the fact that
\operatorname{supp}\zeta\subset(-1,-1/2)
, we have

\partial_{t}^{n}u(t,x)=\partial_{t}^{n}u^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)-\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\partial_{s}^{n+1}u(s,x)\,\zeta\left(\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/\beta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

for
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\Omega
.
Using the fact that
I^{1-\alpha}\partial_{t}g=\partial_{t}I^{1-\alpha}g
for a sufficiently smooth
g
with
g(0,x)=0
and (
4.1
), we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\partial_{s}^{n+1}u(s,y)\,\zeta\left(\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/\beta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{t+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{1/\beta}}^{t+\lambda^{1/\beta}}\partial_{s}I^{\alpha}I^{1-\alpha}\partial_{s}^{n+1}u(s,x)\zeta\left(\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/\beta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

=\lambda^{-1/\beta}\int_{t+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{1/\beta}}^{t+\lambda^{1/\beta}}I^{\alpha}\partial_{s}^{n+1}I^{1-\alpha}\left(u(s,x)-\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{s^{m}}{m!}\partial_{s}^{m}u(0,x)\right)\zeta^{\prime}\left(\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/\beta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

=\lambda^{-1/\beta}\int_{t+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{1/\beta}}^{t+\lambda^{1/\beta}}\left(I^{\alpha}I^{k-n}f\left(s,x\right)+R_{n}\left(s,x\right)\right)\zeta^{\prime}\left(\frac{t-s}{\lambda^{1/\beta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad(I^{0}f:=f)

where
R_{n}(s,x)=0
for
n=k
and

R_{n}(s,x)=\sum_{m=n+1}^{k}N(m,n)s^{m-n}\partial_{t}^{m}u(0,x)

(4.2)

for
0\leq n<k
.
Therefore, by evaluating
t=0
in the above, we have the following representation of
\partial_{t}^{n}u(0,x)
:

\partial_{t}^{n}u(0,x)=\partial_{t}^{n}u^{(\varepsilon)}(0,x)\\
-\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{-1-1/\beta}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{1/\beta}}^{\lambda^{1/\beta}}\left(I^{\alpha}I^{k-n}f\left(s,x\right)+R_{n}\left(s,x\right)\right)\zeta^{\prime}\left(\frac{-s}{\lambda^{1/\beta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(4.3)

Moreover, (
4.3
) also holds for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
by extending
u
to
\{t>T\}\times\Omega
properly and following the above argument with
\varepsilon<T^{\beta}
.

Proof of Theorem

.

3.6
, it is enough to prove (

) for

\bullet

\displaystyle 0&\displaystyle\textrm{if}\quad\varepsilon\geq T^{\beta}.\end{array}\right.

Then by (

Step 2 – Estimates of

and

\leq N\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{1/\beta}}\left(\int_{s^{\beta}}^{(2s)^{\beta}}\lambda^{-1-1/\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\right)\|I^{\alpha}f(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}s

Then by the change of variable

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
f\in L_{p,w_{2}}
with
p\in(1,\infty)
,
w_{2}\in A_{p}
. Then for any
\kappa\in(0,\alpha)
, we have

\|\left(P_{\alpha}\ast\psi_{j}\right)\ast f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\leq N(d,\alpha,\kappa,[w_{2}]_{A_{p}})\left(2^{-2\kappa j/\alpha}t^{-\kappa}\wedge 1\right)\|f\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}

(4.14)

for
j=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots
, and

\|\left(P_{\alpha}\ast\Psi\right)\ast f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\leq N(d,\alpha,[w_{2}]_{A_{p}})\|f\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}.

(4.15)

Proof.

For simplicity, set
P_{\alpha}=P
and
P_{\alpha}\ast\psi_{j}=P_{j}
for
j=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots
. By using the integral representation (
3.8
) in Remark
3.10
with the fact that
\hat{P}_{j}=\hat{P}\,\hat{\psi_{j}}
and
\operatorname{supp}\hat{\psi}_{j}\subset\{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j}\}
, we verify that

\left|D_{\xi}^{\gamma}\hat{P}_{j}(t,\xi)\right|\leq N(d,\alpha,\kappa,\gamma)2^{-j|\gamma|}\left(2^{-2j/\alpha}t^{-1}\right)^{\kappa}1_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}

(4.16)

for any multi-index
\gamma
and for any
\kappa\in(0,\alpha)
.
Indeed, for
\gamma=0
and for any
\kappa\in(0,\alpha)
, we have

\left|\hat{P}_{j}(t,\xi)\right|\leq N1_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{\alpha-1}}{r^{2\alpha}+2r^{\alpha}\cos{\alpha\pi}+1}e^{-rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}r

\leq N1_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha-1}e^{-rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}}\left(rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}\right)^{\kappa}\left(rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}\right)^{-\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}r

+N1_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha-1}e^{-rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}}\left(rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}\right)^{\kappa}\left(rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}\right)^{-\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}r

\leq N1_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\left(2^{-2j/\alpha}t^{-1}\right)^{\kappa}\left(\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha-\kappa-1}\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha-\kappa-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)

\leq N(d,\alpha,\kappa)1_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\left(2^{-2j/\alpha}t^{-1}\right)^{\kappa},

where we use the fact that
e^{-rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}}\left(rt|\xi|^{2/\alpha}\right)^{\kappa+m}
is uniformly bounded in
(r,t,\xi)
for any
m\geq 0
and the last inequality is due to
\kappa\in(0,\alpha)
.
The same argument holds for
\gamma
such that
|\gamma|\neq 0
, with the help of
|D_{\xi}^{\gamma_{1}}\hat{\psi}_{j}|\leq N(d)2^{-j|\gamma_{1}|}
for any multi-index
\gamma_{1}
.
Then

\sup_{r>0}\left(r^{2|\gamma|-d}\int_{r<|\xi|<2r}\left|D_{\xi}^{\gamma}\hat{P}_{j}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2}

=N2^{-j|\gamma|-2\kappa j/\alpha}t^{-\kappa}\sup_{2^{j-2}<r<2^{j+1}}\left(r^{2|\gamma|-d}\int_{r<|\xi|<2r}1_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2}

\leq N\left(2^{-2j/\alpha}t^{-1}\right)^{\kappa}

for
N=N(d,\alpha,\kappa,\gamma)>0
.

On the other hand, since
|D_{\xi}^{\gamma}\hat{P}(t,\xi)|\leq N\||\cdot|^{|\gamma|}P(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}}
and
P(1,x)
satisfies (
3.6
) in Remark
3.10
, by direct calculation we get

\left|D_{\xi}^{\gamma}\hat{P}_{j}(t,\xi)\right|\leq N\sum_{|\gamma_{1}|\leq|\gamma|}1_{2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}}2^{-j|\gamma_{1}|}t^{\left|\left(\gamma\right|-\left|\gamma_{1}\right|\right)\alpha/2}

for any multi-index
\gamma
, and then,

\sup_{r>0}\left(r^{2|\gamma|-d}\int_{r<|\xi|<2r}\left|D_{\xi}^{\gamma}\hat{P}_{j}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2}\leq N\sum_{|\gamma_{1}|\leq|\gamma|}t^{\left(\left|\gamma\right|-\left|\gamma_{1}\right|\right)\alpha/2}2^{j\left(\left|\gamma\right|-\left|\gamma_{1}\right|\right)},

where
N=N(d,\alpha,\gamma)>0
.
Observe that if
t\leq 2^{-2j/\alpha}
, the last summation in the above inequality is bounded by
\sum_{|\gamma_{1}|\leq|\gamma|}1=N(\gamma)
. Therefore, for any multi-index
\gamma
with
|\gamma|\leq d
, we have (
4.12
) with
\hat{P}_{j}
in place of
m
, where

\mathfrak{D}(\hat{P}_{j})\leq N(d,\alpha,\kappa)\left(2^{-2j/\alpha}t^{-1}\right)^{\kappa}\wedge 1.

This along with Lemma
4.4
proves (
4.14
).

To prove (
4.15
), recall that
\hat{\Psi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\operatorname{supp}\hat{\Psi}\subset\{|\xi|\leq 2\}
and
\hat{\Psi}=1
on
\{|\xi|\leq 1\}
.
It is easily seen that
\hat{\Psi}
satisfies (
4.12
) with
\mathfrak{D}(\hat{\Psi})=N(d)
and then by Lemma
4.4
,

\|\left(P_{\alpha}\ast\Psi\right)\ast f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}=\|\Psi\ast\left(P_{\alpha}\ast f\right)(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\leq N(d)\|P_{\alpha}\ast f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}.

Also, by a suitable dyadic decomposition, we have
P_{\alpha}\ast f\leq N(d,\alpha)\mathcal{M}f
where
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.
Then by Hardy-Littlewood maximal function theorem with
A_{p}
-weights, we obtain (
4.15
). The lemma is proved.
∎

We prove the key estimate of this subsection in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
w=t^{\mu}w_{2}(x)
with
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
,
w_{2}\in A_{p}
. Suppose
\alpha\in\left(\left(1+\mu\right)/q,1\right)
. Then, for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\|P_{\alpha}\ast f\|_{L_{p,q,w}(T)}\leq N\|f\|_{B_{p,q,w_{2}}^{-\frac{2(1+\mu)}{q\alpha}}},

(4.17)

where
N=N(d,\alpha,p,q,[w_{2}]_{A_{p}},\mu,T)>0
.

Proof.

We set
P_{\alpha}=P
for notational simplicity. Also, for
g\in\{f,P\}
, we set
g_{j}=g\ast\psi_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots
, and
g_{0}=g\ast\Psi
(not
g\ast\psi_{0}
).
Observe that
\hat{\Psi}=\hat{\Psi}(\hat{\Psi}+\hat{\psi}_{1})
,
\hat{\psi}_{1}=\hat{\psi}_{1}(\hat{\Psi}+\hat{\psi}_{1}+\hat{\psi}_{2})
, and
\hat{\psi}_{j}=\hat{\psi}_{j}(\hat{\psi}_{j-1}+\hat{\psi}_{j}+\hat{\psi}_{j+1})
for
j=2,3,\ldots
, by the construction of
\Psi
and
\psi_{j}
. Then

P\ast f=P_{0}\ast f_{0}+P_{1}\ast f_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=j-1}^{j+1}P_{i}\ast f_{j},

and by Lemma
4.5
, for some
\kappa\in(0,\alpha)
(
\kappa
is to be specified below.)

\|P(t,\cdot)\ast f(\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\leq\|P_{0}\left(t,\cdot\right)\ast f_{0}\left(\cdot\right)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}+\|P_{1}(t,\cdot)\ast f_{0}\left(\cdot\right)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}

+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=j-1}^{j+1}\|P_{i}(t,\cdot)\ast f_{j}(\cdot)\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}

\leq N\left(\||f_{0}|+|f_{1}|\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-2\kappa j/\alpha}t^{-\kappa}\wedge 1\right)\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\right),

where
N=N(d,\alpha,\kappa,p,[w_{2}]_{A_{p}})>0
.
From the above inequalities, it suffices to estimate
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
where

\mathcal{I}:=\int_{0}^{T}\||f_{0}|+|f_{1}|\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t

and

\mathcal{J}:=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-2\kappa j/\alpha}t^{-\kappa}\wedge 1\right)\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\right)^{q}t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t.

It is clear that

\mathcal{I}\leq N(\alpha,q,\mu,T)\left(\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}+\|f_{1}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}\right),

where
s=-2(1+\mu)/(q\alpha)
.
On the other hand, note that
\mathcal{J}
is bounded by a positive constant times

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}1_{t\leq 2^{-2j/\alpha}}\right)^{q}t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t

+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-2\kappa j/\alpha}t^{-\kappa}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}1_{t>2^{-2j/\alpha}}\right)^{q}t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t:=\mathcal{J}_{1}+\mathcal{J}_{2}.

Estimate of
\mathcal{J}_{1}
:
Observe that, for
t>2^{-2/\alpha}
, the integrand of
\mathcal{J}_{1}
is always zero. For each
t\in(0,2^{-2/\alpha}]
, there exits the largest positive integer
j_{1}=j_{1}(t)
such that
t\leq 2^{-2j_{1}/\alpha}
.
Then by Hölder’s inequality, for some
r<0
,

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}1_{t\leq 2^{-2j/\alpha}}\right)^{q}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{j_{1}(t)}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\right)^{q}1_{t\leq 2^{-2j/\alpha}}

\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{j_{1}(t)}\left(2^{-r\frac{2j}{\alpha}}\right)^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{q/q^{\prime}}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{1}(t)}\left(2^{r\frac{2j}{\alpha}}\right)^{q}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}1_{t\leq 2^{-2j/\alpha}}

\leq N\left(2^{-2j_{1}/\alpha}\right)^{qr}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{1}}\left(2^{r\frac{2j}{\alpha}}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}\right)^{q}1_{t\leq 2^{-2j/\alpha}}

\leq Nt^{qr}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{1}}\left(2^{r\frac{2j}{\alpha}}\right)^{q}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}1_{t\leq 2^{-2j/\alpha}},

where
N=N(r,\alpha,q)
and the last inequality is due to
t\leq 2^{-2j_{1}/\alpha}
and
r<0
.
Then, by taking
r<0
such that
qr+\mu>-1
, i.e.,
-(1+\mu)/q<r<0
(recall that
-(1+\mu)/q<0
since
\mu>-1
),

\mathcal{J}_{1}\leq N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{2^{-2j/\alpha}}t^{qr+\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)2^{\frac{2j}{\alpha}qr}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}\leq N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{jqs}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q},

where
s=-2(1+\mu)/(q\alpha)
and
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)
.

Estimate of
\mathcal{J}_{2}
: By repeating the above process with
t>2^{-2j/\alpha}
,
where
j_{2}=j_{2}(t)
is the smallest positive integer such that
t>2^{-2j_{2}/\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-2\kappa j/\alpha}t^{-\kappa}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}1_{t>2^{-2j/\alpha}}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq t^{-\kappa q}\left(\sum_{j=j_{2}}^{\infty}\left(2^{-\frac{2j}{\alpha}(\kappa+r^{\prime})}\right)^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{q/q^{\prime}}\sum_{j=j_{2}}^{\infty}2^{\frac{2j}{\alpha}qr^{\prime}}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}1_{t>2^{-2j/\alpha}}

\displaystyle=Nt^{-\kappa q}2^{-\frac{2j_{2}}{\alpha}q(\kappa+r^{\prime})}\sum_{j=j_{2}}^{\infty}2^{\frac{2j}{\alpha}qr^{\prime}}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}1_{t>2^{-2j/\alpha}}

for some
r^{\prime}>-\kappa
such that
qr^{\prime}+\mu<-1
and
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)
.
Note that such
r^{\prime}
always exists since
\kappa<\alpha
and
\alpha>(1+\mu)/q
. Then, from the fact that
t>2^{-2j_{2}/\alpha}
,

\mathcal{J}_{2}\leq N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{2^{-2j/\alpha}}^{\infty}t^{qr+\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)2^{\frac{2j}{\alpha}qr}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}=N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{jqs}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q},

where
s=-2(1+\mu)/(q\alpha)
and
N=N(\alpha,q,\mu)
.
Thus, by taking
\kappa\in(0,\alpha)
such that
\kappa>(1+\mu)/q
(for example,
2\kappa=\alpha+(1+\mu)/q
) and combining the estimates for
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
, we have

\|P_{\alpha}\ast f\|_{L_{p,q,w}(T)}^{q}\leq N\left(\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{jqs}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p,w_{2}}}^{q}\right),

that is, (
4.17
) with
N=N(d,\alpha,p,q,[w_{2}]_{A_{p}},\mu,T)
. The proposition is proved.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Recall that in order for the initial trace

]

-theory.

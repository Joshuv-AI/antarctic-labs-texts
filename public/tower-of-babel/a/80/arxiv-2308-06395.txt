On ideals in quantales, II
Amartya Goswami

Abstract
. As a natural extension of the ongoing development of a theory of ideals in commutative quantales with an identity element, this article aims to study into the analysis of certain topological properties exhibited by distinguished classes of ideals. These ideals are equipped with quantale topologies. The primary objectives encompass characterizing quantale spaces exhibiting sobriety, examining several conditions pertaining to quasi-compactness, and demonstrating that quantale spaces comprised of proper ideals adhere to the spectral properties as defined by Hochster. We introduce the notion of a strongly disconnected spaces and show that for a quantale with zero Jacobson radical, strongly disconnected quantale spaces containing all maximal ideals of the quantale imply existence of non-trivial idempotent elements in the quantale. Additionally, a sufficient criterion for establishing the connectedness of a quantale space is presented. Finally, we discuss on continuous maps between quantale spaces.

3.
Quantale topology

Consider a quantale
\mathcal{Q}
and let
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
be a class of ideals in
\mathcal{Q}
. Our objective is to define a topology on
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. We aim to achieve a topology that coincides with the Stone topology when
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
consists of maximal ideals or with the Zariski topology when
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
consists of prime ideals in
\mathcal{Q}
, and so on. In case of rings, however, it is well-known that neither the Zariski topology, the Stone topology, nor any hull-kernel topology can be imposed on an arbitrary class of ideals.

We will now demonstrate that a similar limitation applies to quantales. The following theorem characterizes the “largest” class of ideals in a quantale on which a hull-kernel topology can be imposed. This result, which was initially established in
[
McK53
, §2.2, p.11]
for rings, is adapted to the context of quantales.
For a class
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
of ideals of
\mathcal{Q}
, we consider the collection

\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}:=\left\{X^{\uparrow}\mid X\preccurlyeq\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{Q}}\right\}

of subsets of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
given by:

(3.1)

X^{\uparrow}=\begin{cases}\left\{J\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}\mid\mathcal{D}_{X}:=\wedge X\preccurlyeq J\right\},&X\neq\emptyset;\\
\emptyset,&X=\emptyset.\end{cases}

Whenever
X=\{I\},
we shall write
I^{\uparrow}
for
\{I\}^{\uparrow}.

Theorem 3.1. —

The collection
\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}
induces a closed-set topology on
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
if and only if the class
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
of ideals satisfies the following property:

(3.2)

I\wedge J\preccurlyeq S\;\;\text{implies}\;\;I\preccurlyeq S\;\;\text{or}\;\;J\preccurlyeq S,

for all
I,

J\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{Q}}
and for all
S\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}.

Proof.

We shall show that
(-)^{\uparrow}
is a Kuratowski closure operator on
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
if and only if the class
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
of ideals satisfies the condition (
3.2
). The facts that
\emptyset^{\uparrow}=\emptyset
and
X\preccurlyeq X^{\uparrow}
follow from (
3.2
), whereas the condition
(X^{\uparrow})^{\uparrow}=X^{\uparrow}
follows from the monotone property of
(-)^{\uparrow}
. Finally, what remains is to show that
(X\vee Y)^{\uparrow}=X^{\uparrow}\vee Y^{\uparrow}
holds for any
X,

Y\preccurlyeq\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{Q}}
if and only if
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
satisfies (
3.2
). The fact that
X^{\uparrow}\vee Y^{\uparrow}\preccurlyeq(X\vee Y)^{\uparrow}
follows from monotonicity of
(-)^{\uparrow}
and from the properties proven above. Suppose
J\in(X\vee Y)^{\uparrow}.
Then
(\mathcal{D}_{X})\vee(\mathcal{D}_{Y})\preccurlyeq J,
and hence,
J\in X^{\uparrow}\vee Y^{\uparrow}
if and only if
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
satisfies (
3.2
).
∎

Remark 3.2. —

It is worth noting that the class of ideals in a quantale that satisfies property (
3.2
) defined earlier corresponds exactly to the class of strongly irreducible ideals. Moreover, it can be easily observed that the classes of maximal, prime, and minimal prime ideals in a quantale also satisfy property (
3.2
). Therefore, these classes serve as suitable candidates upon which a hull-kernel topology can be imposed.

Whenever
(-)^{\uparrow}
is a Kuratowski closure operator on a class
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
of ideals in
\mathcal{Q}
, the collection
\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}
of subsets of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
are exactly the closed subsets of the hull-kernel topology induced on
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}
is a closed basis of that topology. In fact, we can obtain even more, as demonstrated by the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3. —

If
\mathcal{Q}
is a quantale, then
(-)^{\uparrow}
is a Kuratowski closure operator on a class of ideals
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
if and only if
\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}
is a closed basis of the corresponding hull-kernel topology.

Proof.

If
(-)^{\uparrow}
is a Kuratowski closure operator on
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
, then obviously
\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}
is a closed basis of the corresponding hull-kernel topology.
To prove the converse, we shall use the following fact: if
\mathcal{B}
is a closed basis on a topological space
X
, then the closure operator induced by the topology is expressible in terms of
\mathcal{B}
, in the sense that

\mathcal{C}(T)={\wedge}\{B\in\mathcal{B}\mid T\preccurlyeq B\},

for any
T\preccurlyeq X
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}
is a closed basis for
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Let
\mathcal{C}(\cdot)
be the desired Kuratowski closure operator. It suffices to show that
(\cdot)^{\uparrow}=\mathcal{C}(\cdot)
. For any
T\preccurlyeq\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
, we obtain

\mathcal{C}(T)=\bigwedge\left\{S^{\uparrow}\in\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}\mid T\preccurlyeq S^{\uparrow}\right\}.

Since
T^{\uparrow}\in\mathcal{B}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}}
and
T\preccurlyeq T^{\uparrow}
, we deduce from the equality
(T^{\uparrow})^{\uparrow}=T^{\uparrow}
that
\mathcal{C}(T)\preccurlyeq T^{\uparrow}
. To have the reverse inclusion, let
J\in T^{\uparrow}
. Consider any
S\preccurlyeq\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
with
T\preccurlyeq S^{\uparrow}
. Then, we have

\mathcal{D}_{S}=\wedge S^{\uparrow}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{D}_{T}.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{T}\preccurlyeq J
, as
J\in T^{\uparrow}
, it follows that
\mathcal{D}_{S}\preccurlyeq J
. Thus,
J\in S^{\uparrow}
. In all then, we have shown that
J
belongs to every set included in the intersection that describes
\mathcal{C}(T)
, and so
J\in\mathcal{C}(T)
, and so
T^{\uparrow}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{C}(T)
. Consequently,
T^{\uparrow}=\mathcal{C}(T)
, and hence,
(\cdot)^{\uparrow}
is a Kuratowski closure operator. ∎

4.
Quasi-compactness

Recall that a topological space is said to be
quasi-compact
if it satisfies the finite intersection property (or, equivalently, every open cover has a finite subcover), without assuming the space to be
T_{2}
. It is well-known that spectrum of prime ideals of a ring endowed with Zariski topology is quasi-compact. We first provide a similar result encompassing more classes of ideals of a quantale. However, the proof of this result relies on the Alexander’s subbasis theorem.

Theorem 4.1. —

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a quantale. If
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
is a class of ideals in
\mathcal{Q}
with the property that
\mathrm{Max}_{\mathcal{Q}}\preccurlyeq\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}},
then the quantale space
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
quasi-compact.

Proof.

Suppose
\{\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a family of subbasic closed sets of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
with
\bigwedge_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}=\emptyset.
This implies that

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}=X_{\lambda}^{\uparrow}
, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
X_{\lambda}\preccurlyeq\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
such that

\emptyset=\bigwedge_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}^{\uparrow}=\left(\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\right)^{\uparrow}.

The second equality indeed holds. For

J\in\left(\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\right)^{\uparrow}\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}}\preccurlyeq J\Leftrightarrow\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,\mathcal{D}_{X_{\lambda}}\preccurlyeq J\Leftrightarrow\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,J\in X_{\lambda}^{\uparrow}\Leftrightarrow J\in\bigwedge_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}^{\uparrow}.

If
\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\neq\mathcal{Q},
then there exists
M\in\mathrm{Max}_{\mathcal{Q}}
such that
\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\preccurlyeq M.
Moreover,

\mathcal{D}_{X_{\lambda}}\preccurlyeq X_{\lambda}\preccurlyeq\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\preccurlyeq M,

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda.
Therefore, by hypothesis,
M\in X_{\lambda}^{\uparrow}=\mathcal{K}_{\lambda},
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, a contradiction. Hence
\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}=\mathcal{Q},
and thus
\top\in\bigvee_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}.
This implies the existence of a finite subset
\{\lambda_{\scriptscriptstyle 1},\ldots,\lambda_{\scriptscriptstyle n}\}
of
\Lambda
such that
\top=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}x_{\lambda_{i}},
for some
x_{\lambda_{i}}\in X_{\lambda_{i}}
. Hence
\mathcal{Q}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}X_{\lambda_{i}},
which establishes the finite intersection property. Therefore,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
is quasi-compact by Alexander’s subbasis theorem.
∎

Corollary 4.2. —

The quantale spaces of proper, maximal, prime, radical, primary, strongly irreducible, and irreducible ideals are all quasi-compact.

While Theorem
4.1
presents a sufficient condition for a quantale space to possess quasi-compactness, the subsequent proposition delivers a characterization of these spaces. However, before proceeding with the proposition, we need to establish a prerequisite result from the domain of topological spaces.

Lemma 4.3. —

If
X
is a quasi-compact, T
0
-space, then every chain in
X
has an upper bound.

Proof.

Let
K\preccurlyeq X
be a chain and let
\Omega:=\left\{\mathcal{C}(k)\mid k\in K\right\}
. Then
\mathcal{G}
is clearly a chain of nonempty closed sets of
X
and thus it has the finite intersection property. Since
X
is quasi-compact, there is a point
z\in\bigwedge\Omega
and, by definition,
k\preccurlyeq z
, for every
k\in K
, and this proves the result.
∎

Proposition 4.4. —

Let
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
be a class of ideals of a quantale
\mathcal{Q}
. Suppose
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
denotes the set of maximal elements of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
is quasi-compact;

(2)

for every
I\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}},
there exists a
J\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
such that
I\preccurlyeq J
, and
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
is quasi-compact.

Proof.

To show (2)
\Rightarrow
(1), let
\mathcal{O}
be an open cover of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then,
\mathcal{O}
also covers
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
, and thus there is a finite subcover
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
. Consider
I\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. By hypothesis, there is
J\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
such that
I\preccurlyeq J
, and
O\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
such that
J\in O
. Then,
I\in O
, and thus
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is also a finite subcover for
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Hence,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
is quasi-compact.
Next we prove that (1)
\Rightarrow
(2). Suppose that
I\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
, and consider

\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}:=I^{\uparrow}\wedge\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}.

Then,
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
is a closed subset of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
, and thus it is quasi-compact; by Lemma
4.3
, every ascending chain in
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
is bounded, and hence by Zorn’s Lemma
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
has maximal elements,
i.e.
,
I\preccurlyeq J,
for some
J\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
.
Now, suppose
\mathcal{O}
is an open cover of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
. Then,
\mathcal{O}
is also an open cover of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
, and thus it admits a finite subcover, which will be also a finite subcover of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
. Thus,
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}})
is quasi-compact.
∎

Remark 4.5. —

While Theorem
4.1
can now be derived from Proposition
4.4
, it is worth noting that utilizing Theorem
4.1
simplifies the verification of quasi-compactness for a quantale space (see Corollary
4.2
).

In Theorem
4.1
, we have observed that the inclusion of all maximal ideals in a class
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
of ideals
is a sufficient condition for the quantale space
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}

to possess quasi-compactness. In the subsequent proposition, we establish that this inclusion is also a necessary condition for the class of finitely generated (proper) ideals.

Proposition 4.6. —

Let
\mathcal{Q}

is not quasi-compact. Let us consider the collection of closed subspaces:

Let us now explore the characterization of Noetherian quantales in terms of quasi-compact quantale spaces.

Theorem 4.7. —

A quantale
\mathcal{Q}

To obtain the claim, it suffices to show that

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{Q}}

since

In the proposition that follows, we present a sufficient condition for the quasi-compactness of the quantale space of regular ideals in

Proposition 4.8. —

If

(2)

5.
Separation axioms

The purpose of this section is to investigate the lower separation axioms of quantale spaces, specifically
T_{0}
,
T_{1}
, and sobriety. Notably, sobriety will hold significant importance as it aids in characterizing quantale spaces that are spectral. We commence by presenting an assertive yet obvious separation property that holds for all quantale spaces.

Proposition 5.1. —

Every quantale space is a
T_{0}
-space.

Proof.

Let
I,

J\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
such that
I^{\uparrow}=J^{\uparrow}.
Since
J\in I^{\uparrow},
we have
I\preccurlyeq J
. Similarly, we obtain
J\preccurlyeq I
. Hence,
I=J
, and we have the claim. ∎

To proceed further with separation axioms, we first need the following lemma. This lemma, however, is also going to be useful in the context of connectedness.

Lemma 5.2. —

Every subbasic closed set of the form
\left\{I^{\uparrow}\mid I\in I^{\uparrow}\right\}
of a quantale space

is irreducible.

In fact, we show more,

for every

such that
I\in I^{\uparrow}
. By definition of
\mathcal{C}(\cdot)
and hypothesis, we have
\mathcal{C}(I)\preccurlyeq I^{\uparrow}
.
We obtain the other inclusion by considering the following two cases:

(1)
\mathcal{C}(I)=\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Then we have the claim from the fact that

\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}=\mathcal{C}(I)\preccurlyeq I^{\uparrow}\preccurlyeq\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}.

(2)
\mathcal{C}(I)\neq\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Since
\mathcal{C}(I)
is a closed set, there exists an index set

such that for each

\mathcal{C}(I)={\bigwedge_{\omega\in\Omega}}\left({\bigvee_{i\,=1}^{n_{\omega}}}I_{\omega i}^{\uparrow}\right).

Since

\mathcal{C}(I)\neq\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}

{\bigvee_{i\,=1}^{n_{\omega}}}I_{\omega i}^{\uparrow}
is non-empty for each
\omega

I\in{\bigvee_{i\,=1}^{n_{\omega}}}I_{\omega i}^{\uparrow}

Corollary 5.3. —

Every non-empty subbasic closed set of the quantale space

is irreducible.

6.
Connectedness and continuity

Like Alexander subbasis theorem, there is no characterization of connectedness in terms of subbasic closed sets. Nevertheless, we wish to present a disconnectivity result of quantale spaces of a quantale that bears resemblance to the fact that if spectrum of prime ideals (of a commutative ring with identity) endowed with Zariski topology is disconnected, then the ring has a proper idempotent element (see
[
Bou72
, §4.3, Corollary 2]
).

We say a closed subbasis
\mathcal{S}
of a topological space
X

strongly disconnects

X
if there exist two non-empty subsets
A,

B
of
\mathcal{S}
such that
X=A\vee B
and
A\wedge B=\emptyset
.
It is clear that if some closed subbasis strongly disconnects a topological space, then the space is disconnected. Also, if a space is disconnected, then some closed subbasis (for instance the collection of all its closed subspaces) strongly disconnects it.

Proposition 6.1. —

Suppose that
\mathcal{Q}
is a quantale with
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{Q})=\theta
. Let
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
be a spectrum of
\mathcal{Q}
containing all maximal ideals in
\mathcal{Q}.
If the subbasis
\mathcal{S}
of the quantale space
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
strongly disconnects
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
, then
\mathcal{Q}
has a non-trivial idempotent element.

Proof.

Let
I
and
J
be ideals in
\mathcal{Q}
such that
(1)
I^{\uparrow}\wedge J^{\uparrow}=\emptyset,

(2)
I^{\uparrow}\vee J^{\uparrow}=\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}},
and
(3)
I^{\uparrow}\neq\emptyset,J^{\uparrow}\neq\emptyset.

Since
I^{\uparrow}\wedge J^{\uparrow}=(I\vee J)^{\uparrow}
, we therefore have
(I\vee J)^{\uparrow}=\emptyset
and hence
I\vee J=\mathcal{Q}
because
\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}
contains all maximal ideals in
\mathcal{Q}
. On the other hand,

\Sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}=I^{\uparrow}\vee J^{\uparrow}\preccurlyeq(I\mbox{{\small{$\&$}}}J)^{\uparrow},

which then implies that
I\mbox{{\small{$\&$}}}J
is contained in every maximal ideal in
\mathcal{Q}
, and is therefore the zero ideal since
\mathcal{Q}
has zero
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{Q})
. Note that the condition (3) implies that neither
I
nor
J
is the entire quantale
\mathcal{Q}
. So the

furnishes non-zero elements

and

such that

. Since

as

showing that

is a non-zero idempotent element in
\mathcal{Q}
. Since

, and
x\neq\top
, and hence

is a non-trivial idempotent element in
\mathcal{Q}
.
∎

Since

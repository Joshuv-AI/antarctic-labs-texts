Hilbert’s Theorem, via moving frames
William D. Dunbar

Abstract
We present a proof that the hyperbolic plane cannot be isometrically immersed in Euclidean 3 3 -space by a C ∞ C^{\infty} map. Ideas from many topics in (essentially) undergraduate mathematics are applied; the use of moving frames and connection forms to express the geometry simplifies the outline of the proof, compared to, say, using coordinate patches and Christoffel symbols. The key transition is from expressions in terms of the principal directions on the immersed surface (which give access to the Gaussian curvature) to expressions in terms of the asymptotic directions (which yield a coordinate system and give access to surface area).

Proposition 2
.

If

Proof.

Preparing to apply Theorem

as a vector field on the open unit disk in

\mathbf{y_{0}}:=(0,0)=O

, and set

, so that

By the definition of the hyperbolic metric on

(see Appendix), we can take

(for any choice of

The partial derivatives of

are continuous on the compact set

Proposition 3
.

There is a

, and

2.

F_{*}(E_{i})=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
,

Proof.

Working in
\mathbb{D}
, with

, define
F(P):=(\int_{O}^{P}\eta^{1},\int_{O}^{P}\eta^{2})
.
F
is well-defined, using Green’s theorem, since
\eta^{1}
and
\eta^{2}
are closed and any two paths from

to

bound a region in
\mathbb{D}
. By definition, the differential of

at

(E_{i})_{P}
to
(\partial/\partial x^{i})_{F(P)}

E_{1}
and

are everywhere linearly independent, the differential is everywhere nonsingular, so

is a local

[

In addition,

for time

With respect to the orthonormal bases

\mathbb{D}

and

\sqrt{2}
. Now let

such that

Lemma 4

(

)
.

Let

(Sketch) Construct a path between any two points in

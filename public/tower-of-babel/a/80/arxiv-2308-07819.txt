Verdier dualizing and the variation map
David B. Massey

Abstract
. We describe how the canonical and variation maps between the shifted nearby and vanishing cycles interact with Verdier dualizing.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32S25, 32S15, 32S55

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a reduced complex analytic space and let
f:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a nowhere locally constant analytic function. Let
j:V(f)\hookrightarrow X
and
i:X-V(f)\hookrightarrow X
denote the inclusions. (We initially learned about the derived category from Goresky and MacPherson’s “Intersection Homology II”,
[
2
]
. The convention of
j
being a closed inclusion, while
i
is an open inclusion – which is reversed from what many people use – is theirs.)

We fix a base ring,
R
, which is a commutative, regular, Noetherian ring, with finite Krull dimension (e.g.,
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
{\mathbb{Q}}
, or
{\mathbb{C}}
). When we write that
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
is complex of sheaves on a topological space,
X
, we mean that
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
is an object in
D^{b}(X)
, the derived category of bounded complexes of sheaves of
R
-modules on
X
. When
X
is complex analytic, we may also require that
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
is (complex) constructible, and write
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}_{c}(X)
. We remind the reader that constructibility includes the assumption that the stalks of all cohomology sheaves are finitely-generated
R
-modules (so that, by our assumption on
R
, each stalk complex
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}_{x}
, for
x\in X
, is perfect, i.e., is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finitely-generated projective
R
-modules).

Let
{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{D}}_{Z}
denote the Verdier dualizing operator on
D^{b}_{c}(Z)
. We will always write simply
{\mathcal{D}}
, since the relevant space will always be clear.

As we will always work in the derived category, we omit the
R
at the beginning of right derived functors; all of our functors are derived. We also write
f^{*}
where some authors would write
f^{-1}
.

We define the vanishing cycles as in Lemma 1.3.2 of
[
5
]
, or following Exercise VIII.13 of
[
3
]
(but reversing the inequality, and using a different shift); this, of course, means that by definition, the stalk cohomology of vanishing cycles is the hypercohomology of a small ball modulo the Milnor fiber.

We use
T_{f}
and
\widetilde{T}_{f}
for the monodromy natural automorphisms on the shifted nearby and vanishing cycles
\psi_{f}[-1]
and
\phi_{f}[-1]
, respectively. We also have the standard
canonical
and
variation
natural transformations,
\mathbf{can}
and
\mathbf{var}
:

\psi_{f}[-1]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{can}}\phi_{f}[-1]\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\phi_{f}[-1]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{var}}\psi_{f}[-1],

where

\big({\operatorname{id}}-T_{f}\big)_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\big({\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\big)_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}.

In
[
4
]
, we produced a natural isomorphism

(1)

\phi_{f}[-1]\circ{\mathcal{D}}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\epsilon}{\mathcal{D}}\circ\phi_{f}[-1].

We then used the natural isomorphism
\epsilon
to produce a natrual isomorphism

(2)

\psi_{f}[-1]\circ{\mathcal{D}}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\delta}{\mathcal{D}}\circ\psi_{f}[-1].

However, something which we did
not
do in
[
4
]
was discuss/prove in what sense
\mathbf{can}
and
\mathbf{var}
are dual to each other. That is the point of this paper.

We prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

The following diagram commutes:

\begin{CD}\psi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\mathbf{can}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}>{}>\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\delta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
{\mathcal{D}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}.\end{CD}

However, one must be careful now. It is tempting to assume that one can also show, in an analogous fashion, that

(3)

\begin{CD}\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}>{}>\psi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\delta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\end{CD}

commutes; however,
this is false
. To make the diagram above commute, one must replace the natural isomorphism
\delta
with a different natural isomorphism
\hat{\delta}
.

This paper is organized as follows:

In Section 2, we prove a short, technical, lemma that we shall need; we suspect that this lemma is known, but we can find no reference. In Section 3, we prove the main theorem, Theorem
3.3
, (as stated above in Theorem
1.1
). In Section 4, we discuss the failure of the commutation of (
3
) and show how to define
\hat{\delta}
to “fix” this issue.

2.
A Basic Lemma

In this section, we will prove a general lemma, and then apply it in later sections to the situation of the introduction.

Let
k:Y\rightarrow Z
be a continuous map of locally compact topological spaces such that
k_{!}
has finite cohomological dimension (e.g.,
k
is the inclusion of a locally closed subset).

For all
\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(Y)
and
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(Z)
(note that we are not assuming constructibility here), there are canonical isomorphisms of morphism groups

\operatorname{Hom}(k^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},\mathbf{B}^{\bullet})\xrightarrow[\cong]{M_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},k_{*}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet})

and

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{B}^{\bullet},k^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet})\xrightarrow[\cong]{N_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}}}\operatorname{Hom}(k_{!}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet},\mathbf{A}^{\bullet});

see
[
3
]
, Proposition II.2.6.4 and Theorem III.3.1.5. Replacing
\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}
with
k^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
in the first isomorphism and
\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}
with
k^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
(which, of course, equals
k^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
), we obtain isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}(k^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},k^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet})\xrightarrow[\cong]{M_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},k_{*}k^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet})

and

\operatorname{Hom}(k^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},k^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet})\xrightarrow[\cong]{N_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}\operatorname{Hom}(k_{!}k^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}).

The images of the identity morphisms yield the standard natural transformations

\operatorname{id}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}k_{*}k^{*}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377ptk_{!}k^{!}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{id},

that is,
\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=M_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}(\operatorname{id}_{k^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})
and
\beta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=N_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}(\operatorname{id}_{k^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})
.

We return to the situation and notation of the introduction. In the lemma below, we need constructibility of the complexes. Note that
i_{*}
and
i_{!}
are applied only to
i^{*}
and
i^{!}
of constructible complexes on
X
and so yield constructible complexes.

Lemma 2.1
.

There is a natural isomorphism

{\mathcal{D}}i_{*}i^{*}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\gamma}i_{!}i^{!}{\mathcal{D}}

such that, for all
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}_{c}(X)
,

\beta_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}={\mathcal{D}}(\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\gamma_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1}.

Proof.

Suppose that we have
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}\in D_{c}^{b}(X)
.
Then, we have natural isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{D}}i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{B}^{\bullet},i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(i^{*}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet},i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet})\cong\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{D}}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},{\mathcal{D}}i^{*}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet})\cong

\operatorname{Hom}(i^{!}{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},i^{!}{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(i_{!}i^{!}{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}),

where the isomorphisms, in order, follow from Proposition III.3.4.6 of
[
3
]
, Proposition II.2.6.4 of
[
3
]
, Proposition III.3.4.6 of
[
3
]
, Theorem V.10.11 of
[
1
]
, and Proposition III.3.1.10 of
[
3
]
. As this is true for all
\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}
, it follows that there is a natural isomorphism

{\mathcal{D}}i_{*}i^{*}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\gamma}i_{!}i^{!}{\mathcal{D}}

inducing the isomorphism from
\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{D}}i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet})
to
\operatorname{Hom}(i_{!}i^{!}{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet})
.

Replacing
\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}
with
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
, tracing through the
\operatorname{Hom}
isomorphisms, and using the definitions of
\alpha
and
\beta
, one obtains immediately that

\beta_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}={\mathcal{D}}(\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\gamma_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1}.

∎

Remark 2.2
.

It is easy to find the existence of a natural isomorphism
{\mathcal{D}}i_{*}i^{*}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\gamma}i_{!}i^{!}{\mathcal{D}}
in the literature. The point of the lemma above is how
\gamma
interacts with Verdier dualizing.

3.
The Main Theorem

In this section, we will prove our main theorem as stated in Theorem
1.1
. It may seem as though we define an excessive number of maps, but we have found no simpler presentation. We continue with our notation from the introduction.

Consider the two standard natural distinguished triangles

\rightarrow j_{*}j^{*}[-1]\rightarrow\psi_{f}[-1]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{can}}\phi_{f}[-1]\xrightarrow{[1]}

and

\rightarrow\phi_{f}[-1]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{var}}\psi_{f}[-1]\rightarrow j_{!}j^{!}[1]\xrightarrow{[1]}.

Recall the natural transformations
\alpha
and
\beta
from the previous section. By naturality, we immediately conclude:

Let
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}_{c}(X)
. As
\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
depends only on
i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
(and
f
), there are trivial natural isomorphisms

\psi_{f}[-1]\xrightarrow[\cong]{\pi}\psi_{f}[-1]i_{!}i^{!}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\psi_{f}[-1]i_{*}i^{*}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\rho}\psi_{f}[-1]

given by
\pi_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=(\psi_{f}[-1](\beta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}))^{-1}
and
\rho_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=(\psi_{f}[-1](\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}))^{-1}
.

Furthermore, as
j_{*}j^{*}[-1]i_{!}i^{!}=0
and
j_{!}j^{!}[1]i_{*}i^{*}=0
, we have natural isomorphisms

\psi_{f}[-1]i_{!}i^{!}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\mathbf{can}_{i_{!}i^{!}}}\phi_{f}[-1]i_{!}i^{!}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\phi_{f}[-1]i_{*}i^{*}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\mathbf{var}_{i_{*}i^{*}}}\psi_{f}[-1]i_{*}i^{*}.

We let

\sigma_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}:=\mathbf{can}_{i_{!}i^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\pi_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\tau_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}:=\rho_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{var}_{i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}},

so that
\sigma
is a natural isomorphism from
\psi_{f}[-1]
to
\phi_{f}[-1]i_{!}i^{!}
and
\tau
is a natural isomorphism from
\phi_{f}[-1]i_{*}i
to
\psi_{f}[-1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following diagrams commute:

\begin{CD}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\phi_{f}[-1](\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>\phi_{f}[-1]i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\mathbf{var}_{i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\rho_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1}=\psi_{f}[-1](\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>\psi_{f}[-1]i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\end{CD}

and

\begin{CD}\psi_{f}[-1]i_{!}i^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\pi_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1}=\psi_{f}[-1](\beta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\mathbf{can}_{i_{!}i^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
\phi_{f}[-1]i_{!}i^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\phi_{f}[-1](\beta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},\end{CD}

where
\mathbf{can}_{i_{!}i^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}
,
\mathbf{var}_{i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}
,
\psi_{f}[-1](\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})
, and
\psi_{f}[-1](\beta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})
are isomorphisms.

Therefore,

\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\rho_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{var}_{i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\phi_{f}[-1](\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})=\tau_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\phi_{f}[-1](\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})

and

\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\phi_{f}[-1](\beta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\mathbf{can}_{i_{!}i^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\pi_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\phi_{f}[-1](\beta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\sigma_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}.

Proof.

These are immediate from the naturality of
\mathbf{can}
and
\mathbf{var}
.
∎

Recall the definition of
\gamma
from Lemma
2.1
. In
[
4
]
, the natural isomorphism
\delta
is defined in terms of
\epsilon
by, for all
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}_{c}(X)
,

\delta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}={\mathcal{D}}(\tau^{-1}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\epsilon_{i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\phi_{f}[-1](\gamma^{-1}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\sigma_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}.

With this definition, it is easy to show:

Lemma 3.2
.

The following diagram commutes:

\begin{CD}\psi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\phi_{f}[-1](\gamma_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1})\circ\sigma_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}>{}>\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\phi_{f}[-1]({\mathcal{D}}(\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}))}>{}>\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\delta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\epsilon_{i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
{\mathcal{D}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}(\tau_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]i_{*}i^{*}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}(\phi_{f}[-1]\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}.\end{CD}

Proof.

The square on the left commutes by the definition of
\delta
and the square on the right commutes by the naturality of
\epsilon
.
∎

Finally, with all of our lemmas, it is simple to prove:

Theorem 3.3
.

The following diagram commutes:

\begin{CD}\psi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\mathbf{can}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}>{}>\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\delta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
{\mathcal{D}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet},\end{CD}

i.e.,
\mathbf{can}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1}\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\delta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}
.

Proof.

This follows from the outside edges of the diagram in Lemma
3.2
. The composition along the bottom row, combined with Lemma
3.1
, yields

{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})={\mathcal{D}}\big(\tau_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\phi_{f}[-1](\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\big)={\mathcal{D}}(\phi_{f}[-1](\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}))\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\tau_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}).

Composition along the top row yields

\phi_{f}[-1]({\mathcal{D}}(\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}))\circ\phi_{f}[-1](\gamma_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1})\circ\sigma_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\phi_{f}[-1]\big({\mathcal{D}}(\alpha_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\gamma^{-1}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\big)\circ\sigma_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}},

which, by Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
3.1
, is equal to

\phi_{f}[-1](\beta_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\sigma_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\mathbf{can}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that the equality in Theorem
3.3
immediately yields a formula for
\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}
(using twice that
{\mathcal{D}}{\mathcal{D}}
is naturally isomorphic to the identity). One applies the equality in Theorem
3.3
to
{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
in place of
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
and then dualizes both sides to obtain:

\mathbf{can}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1}\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\delta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}.

\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\epsilon_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1}\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\delta_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}.

{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})={\mathcal{D}}(\delta_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\epsilon_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}^{-1}).

\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}={\mathcal{D}}(\delta^{-1}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\epsilon_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}).

4.
A non-commuting diagram

In Theorem
3.3
, we proved that the right-hand square of the following diagram commutes:

(4)

\begin{CD}\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}>{}>\psi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\mathbf{can}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}>{}>\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\delta_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\end{CD}

One might hope that, in an analogous way, we could prove that the left-hand side also commutes. This is not the case;
the left-hand side does not commute
. The fact that not both of the squares above can commute was pointed out to us by Jörg Schürmann. We explain this now.

From the construction of
\epsilon
, it is relatively simple to show that there is a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{(\widetilde{T}_{f}^{-1})_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}>{}>\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}((\widetilde{T}_{f})_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}.\end{CD}

From this, it follows that,
if (
4
) were commutative
, then the outside edges would tell us that

\big({\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\big)_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\mathbf{can}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=\epsilon^{-1}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{var}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=

\epsilon^{-1}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ{\mathcal{D}}\big(\big({\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\big)_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\big)\circ\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}={\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}-\epsilon^{-1}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ{\mathcal{D}}\big((\widetilde{T}_{f})_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\big)\circ\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}=

{\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}-(\widetilde{T}_{f}^{-1})_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}

As
\widetilde{T}_{f}^{-1}
is, in general, not equal to
\widetilde{T}_{f}
, we conclude that (
4
) does not commute.

In fact, it is easy to see that it was our choice of the isomorphism
\delta
which made the right side of (
4
) commute and not the left. If, however, we define the natural isomorphism

\hat{\delta}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}:={\mathcal{D}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})\circ\epsilon_{i_{!}i^{!}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}\circ\phi_{f}[-1]({\mathcal{D}}(\gamma^{-1}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}))\circ\tau^{-1}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}},

then the analogous argument to what we used to prove Theorem
3.3
shows that we have a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}>{}>\psi_{f}[-1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\\
@V{}V{\epsilon_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V@V{}V{\hat{\delta}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}}V\\
{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}@>{{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{can}_{\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}})}>{}>{\mathcal{D}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}.\end{CD}

Note that this gives a different formula for
\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}
than what we found at the end of Remark
3.4
. We have not found a direct way of passing from one formula to the other; we just know that they both equal
\mathbf{var}_{{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}}
.

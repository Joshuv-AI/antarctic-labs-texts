Leveraging Large Language Model to Generate a Novel Metaheuristic Algorithm with CRISPE Framework
Rui Zhong, Yuefeng Xu, Chao Zhang, Jun Yu

Abstract
In this paper, we borrow the large language model (LLM) ChatGPT-3.5 to automatically and quickly design a new metaheuristic algorithm (MA) with only a small amount of input. The novel animal-inspired MA named zoological search optimization (ZSO) draws inspiration from the collective behaviors of animals for solving continuous optimization problems. Specifically, the basic ZSO algorithm involves two search operators: the prey-predator interaction operator and the social flocking operator to balance exploration and exploitation well. Besides, the standard prompt engineering framework CRISPE (i.e., C apacity and R ole, I nsight, S tatement, P ersonality, and E xperiment) is responsible for the specific prompt design. Furthermore, we designed four variants of the ZSO algorithm with slight human-interacted adjustment. In numerical experiments, we comprehensively investigate the performance of ZSO-derived algorithms on CEC2014 benchmark functions, CEC2022 benchmark functions, and six engineering optimization problems. 20 popular and state-of-the-art MAs are employed as competitors. The experimental results and statistical analysis confirm the efficiency and effectiveness of ZSO-derived algorithms. At the end of this paper, we explore the prospects for the development of the metaheuristics community under the LLM era.

Evolutionary computation (EC), a sub-field of AI and soft computing, is a family of population-based stochastic optimization techniques inspired by natural phenomena or organism behaviors. A general flowchart of EC techniques is demonstrated in Figure
1
, encompassing five primary steps: (1). Initialization, which involves setting up the population and parameters, (2). Offspring generation, (3). Evaluation, (4). Selection, and (5). Iteration of step (2) to step (4) until the process reaches termination. Within these steps, the design of specialized search operators in step (2) stands as the core element of the EC technique, which can be realized by imitating the social behaviors of animals
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
or by simulating the laws of science
[

,
23
,
24

. Thanks to its scalability, easy implementation, robustness, and applicability, this approach has become one of the most favored optimization methodologies in both academic fields and real-world applications
[
25
,
26
]
.

Figure 1:
The general flowchart of EC techniques.

Additionally, the existence of the No Free Lunch Theorem (NFLT)
[
27
]
suggests that the performance of any given metaheuristic algorithm (MA) cannot be guaranteed across all possible optimization problems. This uncertainty has sparked a surge in the development of the novel MAs. Therefore, the motivation of this research is to design a novel MA by integrating the LLM with the CRISPE framework.

In this paper, we propose an LLM-assisted MA named the zoological search optimization (ZSO) algorithm. This innovative algorithm draws inspiration from the collective behavior and adaptation strategies observed in various animal species. It uniquely combines the concepts of social communication, dynamic collaboration, and individual adaptability to offer a unique metaheuristic approach for tackling black-box optimization problems. In the meantime, we address a critical limitation in the field of EC, as highlighted in
[
28
]
: the inadequate experimental validation often associated with newly proposed MAs. Thus, we implement a set of numerical experiments to investigate the performance of the proposed ZSO comprehensively. Moreover, we manually design four hyperparameter adaptation strategies and embed them into the ZSO. Experimental results and statistical analysis provide a practical verification of the high competitiveness of the proposed ZSO when compared to other expert-designed MAs. The specific contributions of this paper are outlined as follows:

•

Through the CRISPE framework and ChatGPT-3.5, we propose a novel zoological search optimization (ZSO) algorithm, specifically tailored for continuous optimization.

•

We comprehensively investigate the performance of ZSO in CEC2014 benchmark functions, CEC2022 benchmark functions, and six engineering problems. 20 popular MAs are employed as competitors.

•

We introduce four hyperparameter adaptation variations within the ZSO framework.

•

Both experimental and statistical results confirm the efficiency and robustness of the ZSO in various scenarios.

•

This research also explores the future trajectory of the metaheuristics community in the era of the LLM.

Related works

2.1
LLM-assisted metaheuristic algorithm

The emergence of LLM has swiftly created a wave of interest in interdisciplinary research, making ’AI for science’ a prominent term in contemporary discussions
[
29
,
30
]
. However, the application of LLMs within the metaheuristics community remains relatively unexplored. A few notable exceptions include: Michal et al.
[
31
]
used the GPT-4 to generate a novel hybrid optimization approach based on particle swarm optimization (PSO), cuckoo search (CS), artificial bee colony (ABC), grey wolf optimizer (GWO), self-organizing migrating algorithm (SOMA), and whale optimization algorithm (WOA). This research primarily investigated the interactive process and challenges associated with using GPT-4 to fulfill a series of set tasks. Through the proper prompt engineering, Liu et al.
[
32
]
discovered an efficient learning-based search operator by the LLM and proposed a new variant of decomposition-based multiobjective evolutionary algorithms named MOEA/D-LO, which demonstrates competitiveness with the expert-designed MOEAs. In another study, Liu et al.
[
33
]
also proposed a methodology of algorithm evolution using LLM (AEL). Distinct from earlier methods, AEL is preferred to be determined as a paradigm rather than a problem-specific approach. It does not require model training and focuses on algorithm-level evolution, showing promising results in the discrete optimization domain such as the traveling salesman problem (TSP). Similarly, Liu et al.
[
34
]
proposed an LLM-driven evolutionary algorithm (LMEA) for solving TSPs. In each iteration, LMEA instructs the LLM to select parent solutions from the population and performs search operators to generate offspring solutions. Then, these offspring solutions are evaluated and selected for the next generation. Notably, the proposed LMEA has shown competitive performance in TSP instances with up to 20 cities.

2.2
CRISPE framework

As the LLM becomes fashionable in many research fields, the design of prompts to achieve desired outcomes also attracts the attention of scholars, which promotes the development of the terminology of ”prompt engineering”. Some representative methods are zero/one/few-shot prompting, iterative prompting, chain-of-thought prompting, and hybrid prompting. In this study, the hybrid approach CRISPE
[
35
,
36
]
is employed as the framework which contains five components and is demonstrated in Figure
2
.

Figure 2:
Components in the CRISPE framework.

•

CR: Define the capacity and role.

•

I: Provide necessary background or insight.

•

S: State the core of the prompt.

•

P: Define the fashion of the LLM’s response.

•

E: Ask for multiple responses.

Methodology in this study

In this section, we introduce the process flow of designing an MA assisted by LLMs, as depicted in Figure
3
. The procedure commences with prompt engineering, where we carefully craft a suitable prompt guided by the CRISPE framework. Then, we input the prompt to ChatGPT-3.5. The output received from ChatGPT-3.5 forms the basis for programming the MA, followed by a manual fine-tuning of its hyperparameters. In the following context, we sequentially introduce the CRISPE-based prompt design and the generated MA: ZSO.

3.1
CRISPE-based prompt design

Figure
4
demonstrates the designed prompt based on the CRISPE framework for use with ChatGPT-3.5. This prompt is designed to engage ChatGPT-3.5 as though it were an expert in the field of MAs. In the insight component, we convey to ChatGPT-3.5 our intention to develop a novel animal-inspired MA to solve black-box optimization problems. In the statement component, ChatGPT-3.5 is required to provide a feasible and detailed algorithm design, including the inspiration of the algorithm, the equations of search operators, the detailed parameter settings, and the flowchart of the designed algorithm. In the personality part, the focus here is on innovation and distinction. We express our desire for the proposed MA to be distinct from existing algorithms such as genetic algorithm (GA), differential evolution (DE), evolutionary strategy (ES), and particle swarm optimization (PSO). Finally, only one example is requested by ChatGPT-3.5. In summary, the complete prompt can be found in Figure

clearly.

Discussion

This section commences with a theoretical analysis of the computational complexity of ZSO, then, the performance analysis on three benchmarks is summarized, and finally, we project the future trajectory of the EC techniques in the context of the LLM era.

5.1
Computational complexity analysis

Assuming the population size is
N
, the maximum iteration is
T
, and the dimension size is
D
. The computational complexity of the three main operators is delineated as follows:

•

Swarm initialization:
O(N\times D)

•

Prey-predator interaction operator for the current population:
O(N\times D)

•

Social flocking operator for the current population:
O(N\times D+N\times D)
:=
O(N\times D)

Therefore, the overall computational complexity of ZSO can be expressed as
O(N\times D)
+
O(T\times N\times D)
:=
O(T\times N\times D)
totally. Compared with the popular MAs listed in Table
12
, since the fitness-based sort operator is not involved in the proposed ZSO, it has a lighter computational burden than the GWO and is equal to other optimizers.

5.2
Performance analysis on CEC2014 benchmark functions

The benchmark functions in the CEC2014 exhibit various characteristics, including unimodal (
f_{1}
-
f_{3}
), multimodal (
f_{4}
-
f_{16}
), hybrid (
f_{17}
-
f_{22}
), and composite (
f_{23}
-
f_{30}
) functions. This diversity ensures that the optimization challenges presented within this suite comprehensively reflect the performance capabilities of the investigated algorithms. We can observe the convergence status of all MAs on CEC2014 benchmark functions in Figures
10
and
11
, while the statistical results are summarized in Tables
5
and
6
. From these experimental results, the LLM-assisted ZSO is highly competitive with other compared MAs, particularly in unimodal, multimodal, and hybrid functions. In most instances, ZSO and its variants perform better than the compared MAs in most cases, which demonstrates the superiority of the proposed ZSO. Additionally, the summary of the statistical analysis and the computation of the average ranks further corroborate the efficiency and robustness of ZSO-derived algorithms.

However, the observed under-performance of the ZSO in composite functions, when compared with other algorithms like HHO, AO, ArchOA, AOA, GJO, FOX, CDO, and COA is a significant aspect that warrants attention. ZSO_Gauss is significantly worse than the mentioned MAs in composite functions. We infer that the architecture of ZSO and the search operators designed by the LLM may not be suitable for dealing with composite functions. To contextualize this phenomenon, the No Free Lunch Theorem (NFLT) offers a pertinent explanation. NFLT states that if an algorithm excels in solving a particular class of problems, it inherently comes at the cost of reduced performance in other problem classes. This theoretical perspective suggests that, on average, any pair of algorithms will exhibit similar performance across all observable problem domains.

5.5
Prospects of the metaheuristics community under LLM era

In this paper, we propose a novel MA ZSO through the LLM ChatGPT-3.5 and the CRISPE framework. With slight human-interacted modification, the proposed ZSO and its variants have competitive performance with state-of-the-art MAs. Thus, using LLM to design novel metaphor-based MAs is hopeful. Moreover, during the LLM era, what benefits can LLM bring to the metaheuristic community, and what advantages can metaheuristics contribute to the LLM? With these questions, we ask ChatGPT-3.5 again with the CRISPE-based prompt:
Act as an expert in the fields of MAs and large language models. We want to summarize the potential benefits of introducing the large language model to the metaheuristic community and what advantages can MAs contribute to the large language model. Provide some potential prospects.

According to the ChatGPT-3.5, the responses are as follows: Integrating large language models (LLMs) into the realm of MAs holds significant promise and can lead to several mutually beneficial outcomes. Let’s explore the potential benefits and advantages of introducing LLMs to the metaheuristic community and vice versa:

Benefits of Introducing LLMs to Metaheuristics
:

•

Enhanced Problem Representation
: LLMs can improve the representation of problem instances, offering a more nuanced and context-aware understanding of the optimization landscape. Natural Language Processing (NLP) capabilities in LLMs allow for a more expressive and human-like problem description, aiding in problem definition and interpretation.

•

Knowledge Integration
: Pre-trained LLMs can incorporate vast amounts of domain-specific knowledge, providing metaheuristics with a comprehensive understanding of the problem space. Fine-tuning LLMs on specific problem instances can lead to the extraction of relevant features and patterns, aiding metaheuristics in decision-making.

•

Dynamic Adaptation
: LLMs can facilitate adaptive metaheuristics by dynamically adjusting their parameters based on real-time information and evolving problem conditions. Continuous learning capabilities of LLMs can enhance adaptability, allowing metaheuristics to respond effectively to changes in the optimization landscape.

•

Multi-Objective Optimization
: LLMs can assist in handling multi-objective optimization problems by comprehensively understanding and representing the trade-offs among conflicting objectives. The natural language understanding in LLMs can help in formulating and communicating complex objective functions in a more human-readable manner.

Advantages of MAs for LLMs
:

•

Training Data Augmentation
: MAs can be employed to generate diverse and challenging training instances for LLMs, improving their robustness and generalization capabilities. Exploration-exploitation strategies in metaheuristics can contribute to more effective data sampling during training.

•

Hyperparameter Tuning
: Metaheuristics can be utilized to optimize the hyperparameters of large language models, enhancing their performance on specific tasks. Evolutionary algorithms and swarm intelligence can efficiently search the hyperparameter space for optimal configurations.

•

Transfer Learning and Adaptation
: Metaheuristics can aid in the transfer learning process for LLMs by facilitating the adaptation of pre-trained models to new tasks or domains. Adaptive algorithms can optimize the fine-tuning process, ensuring faster convergence and improved task-specific performance.

•

Resource Allocation and Scaling
: Metaheuristics can optimize the allocation of computational resources for training large language models, improving efficiency and reducing training time. Scalability algorithms can ensure that LLMs can handle increased model sizes and datasets effectively.

In summary, the collaboration between LLMs and metaheuristics holds immense potential for advancing the capabilities of optimization techniques and creating more intelligent, adaptive, and user-friendly solutions across a wide range of applications. This interdisciplinary synergy can pave the way for groundbreaking developments in the field of artificial intelligence and optimization.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a groundbreaking animal-inspired MA named ZSO which is founded upon the principles of ChatGPT-3.5 and the CRISPE framework tailored for prompt design. Depending on the basic framework of ZSO, the human-interacted design is embedded into the original ZSO to contribute to the development of ZSO’s variants. Through comprehensive numerical experiments conducted on CEC2014 benchmark functions, CEC2022 benchmark functions, as well as engineering optimization, the effectiveness and robustness of ZSO are confirmed.

In summary, leveraging LLM to generate a novel MA with the CRISPE framework is feasible and efficient. As the paper concludes, we present several prospective avenues for future research, aiming to foster the growth and healthy development of the metaheuristics community in the era of LLMs.

On Exact Estimates of Instability Zones of the Hill’s Equation with Locally Integrable Potential
O.A.Veliev

Abstract
In this paper we consider the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator L ⁡ ( q ) L(q) with a periodic real and locally integrable potential q q . We study the bands and gaps in the spectrum and explicitly write out the first and second terms of the asymptotic formulas for the length of the gaps in the spectrum. Key Words: One-dimensional Schrödinger operator, Spectral bands, Periodic potential. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 34L20, 47E05.



1
Introduction and Preliminary Facts

In this paper we investigate the spectrum of the operator
L(q)
generated in

L_{2}(-\infty,\infty)
by the differential expression

l(y)=-y^{{}^{\prime\prime}}+qy,

(1)

where
q
is
1
-periodic integrable on
[0,1]
and a real-valued potential.
Without loss of generality, it is assumed that

\int_{0}^{1}q(x)dx=0.

(2)

It is well-known that (see for example [2-4, 6, 7, 9, 11]) the spectrum

\sigma(L(q))
of
L(q)
is the union of the spectra
\sigma(L_{t}(q))
of the
operators
L_{t}(q)
for
t\in(-\pi,\pi]
generated in
L_{2}[0,1]
by (1) and
the boundary conditions

y(1)=e^{it}y(0),\text{ }y^{{}^{\prime}}(1)=e^{it}y^{{}^{\prime}}(0).

(3)

Moreover,
\sigma(L(q))
consists of the closed intervals whose end points are
the eigenvalues of
L_{0}(q)
and
L_{\pi}(q).
Therefore, to study the
spectrum of
L(q)
it is enough to investigate the eigenvalues of
L_{0}(q)

and
L_{\pi}(q),
which are called periodic and antiperiodic eigenvalues,
respectively. By the classical investigations (see formulas (47a), (47b) in
page 65 of the monograph [10]) the large eigenvalues of the operators

L_{0}(q)
and
L_{\pi}(q)
consist of the sequences
\{\lambda_{n,j}:n=N,N+1,...\}
and
\{\mu_{n,j}:n=N,N+1,...\}
satisfying

\lambda_{n,j}=(2n\pi)^{2}+O(n^{\frac{1}{2}}),\text{ }

(4)

and

\mu_{n,j}=(2n\pi+\pi)^{2}+O(n^{\frac{1}{2}})

for
j=1,2
, where
N
is a sufficiently large positive integer. Thus, the
gaps in
\sigma(L(q))
in the high energy region are equal to

\Delta_{n}=(\lambda_{n,1},\lambda_{n,2}),~\Omega_{n}=\left(\mu_{n,1},\mu_{n,2}\right)

(5)

for
n=N,N+1,...,
and without loss of generality it is assumed that

\lambda_{n,1}\leq\lambda_{n,2}~
and
\mu_{n,1}\leq\mu_{n,2}.
In this paper
to obtain a sharp and explicit formulas for the lengths of these gaps, we use
some formulas from [1] and [12]. For the independent reading of this paper,
first of all, we list the formulas of those papers that are essentially used here.

In [1] we obtained asymptotic formulas of arbitrary order for the eigenvalues

\lambda_{n,j}
and corresponding normalized eigenfunctions
\Psi_{n,j}(x)
of

L_{0}(q),
for any
q\in L_{1}[0,1].
For this we used (4) and the equality

(\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi n)^{2})(\Psi_{n,j}(x),e^{i2\pi nx})=(q(x)\Psi_{n,j}(x),e^{i2\pi nx})=

(6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}=-\infty,\\
n_{1}\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{\infty}q_{n_{1}}(\Psi_{n,j},e^{i2\pi(n-n_{1})x}),

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the inner product in
L_{2}[0,1]
and
q_{n}=(q(x),e^{i2\pi nx}).
Iterating formula (6) we obtained

(\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi n)^{2}-A_{m}(\lambda_{n,j}))(\Psi_{n,j}(x),e^{i2\pi nx})-

(7)

(q_{2n}+B_{m}(\lambda_{n,j}))(\Psi_{n,j}(x),e^{-i2\pi nx})=R_{m}(\lambda_{n,j}),

where

A_{m}(\lambda_{n,j})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}(\lambda_{n,j}),\text{ }B_{m}(\lambda_{n,j})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}(\lambda_{n,j}),

a_{k}(\lambda_{n,j})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{k}}\frac{q_{n_{1}}q_{n_{2}}...q_{n_{k}}q_{-n_{1}-n_{2}-...-n_{k}}}{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{k}(\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}-n_{2}-...-n_{s}))^{2})},

b_{k}(\lambda_{n,j})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{k}}\frac{q_{n_{1}}q_{n_{2}}...q_{n_{k}}q_{2n-n_{1}-n_{2}-...-n_{k}}}{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{k}(\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}-n_{2}-...-n_{s}))^{2})},

R_{m}(\lambda_{n,j})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{m+1}}\frac{q_{n_{1}}q_{n_{2}}...q_{n_{m}}q_{n_{m+1}}(q(x)\Psi_{n,j}(x),e^{i2\pi(n-n_{1}-...-n_{m+1})x})}{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{m+1}(\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}-n_{2}-...-n_{s}))^{2})}.

The summation in these formulas is taken over the indices satisfying the
conditions

n_{s}\neq 0,\text{ }n_{1}+n_{2}+...,n_{s}\neq 0,2n

(8)

for
s=1,2,...,m+1.

Moreover, in [1] it were proved that

a_{k}(\lambda_{n,j})=O((\frac{\ln\left|n\right|}{n})^{k}),\text{
}b_{k}(\lambda_{n,j})=O((\frac{\ln\left|n\right|}{n})^{k})

(9)

for
k\geq 1
and

R_{m}(\lambda_{n,j})=O((\frac{\ln\left|n\right|}{n})^{m+1}).

(10)

Using these estimations in (7) we obtained

(\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi n)^{2})u_{n,j}=q_{2n}u_{-n,j}+O\left(\frac{\ln\left|n\right|}{n}\right),

(11)

where
u_{n,j}=(\Psi_{n,j}(x),e^{i2\pi nx})
. In the same way we have got

(\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi n)^{2})u_{-n,j}=q_{-2n}u_{n,j}+O\left(\frac{\ln\left|n\right|}{n}\right).

(12)

Besides, we obtained the following obvious and well known formula (see (19)
and (20) of [1] or Theorem 2.3.2 in page 50 of [14])

\Psi_{n,j}(x)=u_{n,j}e^{i2\pi nx}+u_{-n,j}e^{-i2\pi nx}+h(x),

(13)

where
\left\|h(x)\right\|=O(n^{-1}),

(h,e^{\pm i2\pi nx})=0
and

\left|u_{n,j}\right|^{2}+\left|u_{-n,j}\right|^{2}=1+O(n^{-2}).

From (11)-(13) it readily follows that the sequence
\{\lambda_{n,j}\}_{N}^{\infty}\
(see (4)) obey an asymptotic formula

\lambda_{n,j}=(2n\pi)^{2}+o(1)

(14)

as
\ \,n\rightarrow\infty
for
j=1,2.
(If
\left|u_{n,j}\right|\geq\left|u_{-n,j}\right|
then use (11), if
\left|u_{-n,j}\right|\geq\left|u_{n,j}\right|

Similarly, for
\{\mu_{n,1}\}_{N}^{\infty}\
we have a formula

\mu_{n,j}=(2n\pi+\pi)^{2}+o(1).

Finally, note that we use the following equalities from [12]. Let
p\geq 0
be
an arbitrary integer,

q\in W_{1}^{p}[0,1],\,q^{(l)}(0)=q^{(l)}(1)

(15)

for all
\ \,0\leq l\leq s-1,
with some
s\leq p,
where
W_{1}^{p}[0,1]
is
the Sobolev space and
W_{1}^{0}[0,1]=L_{1}[0,1]
. Define the functions

Q(x)=\int_{0}^{x}q(t)\,dt,\quad S(x)=Q^{2}(x),

and denote by
Q_{k}=(Q,\,e^{2\pi ikx}),\ \,S_{k}=(S,\,e^{2\pi ikx})
the
Fourier coefficients of these functions. The following relations are valid:

b_{1}(\lambda_{n,j})=-S_{2n}+2Q_{0}Q_{2n}+o\left(n^{-s-2}\right),\quad

(16)

and

b_{2}(\lambda_{n,j})=o\left(n^{-s-2}\right),\text{ }b_{k}(\lambda_{n})=o\left(\frac{\ln^{k}\,n}{n^{k+s}}\right)

(17)

for all
\ k=3,4,\dots
(see Lemma 6 of [12]).

In this paper we obtain asymptotic formulas

\lambda_{n,j}=(2\pi n)^{2}+(-1)^{j}\left|q_{2n}\right|+o(n^{-1})

(18)

and

\lambda_{n,j}=(2\pi n)^{2}+A_{2}((2\pi n)^{2})+(-1)^{j}\left|q_{2n}-S_{2n}+2Q_{0}Q_{2n}\right|+o(n^{-2})

(19)

for the sequence
\{\lambda_{n,j}\}_{N}^{\infty}.
Moreover, from these
formulas we get the following formulas for the lengths
\left|\Delta_{n}\right|
of the gaps
\Delta_{n}

\left|\Delta_{n}\right|=2\left|q_{2n}\right|+o(n^{-1})

(20)

and

\left|\Delta_{n}\right|=2\left|q_{2n}-S_{2n}+2Q_{0}Q_{2n}\right|+o(n^{-2}).

(21)

The similar formulas for for the sequence
\{\mu_{n,j}\}_{N}^{\infty}
and

\left|\Omega_{n}\right|
are obtained. The superiority of formula
(20) over well-known Titchmarsh formula

\left|\Delta_{n}\right|=2\left|q_{2n}\right|+O(n^{-1})

(22)

(see [13] Chap. 21, [7] Chap. 3, and their references) for any potential
q\in L_{1}[0,1]
can be explained as follows. Generally speaking, some Fourier
coefficient
q_{2n}
of many functions from
L_{1}[0,1]
can be of order

n^{-1}
in the sense that
q_{2n}=O(n^{-1})
and
n^{-1}=O(q_{2n})
when
n

belong to some subset of
\mathbb{N}
(for example if the potential
q
has
some jump discontinuity). Then (20) with the error term
o(n^{-1})
explicitly
separate the term
2\left|q_{2n}\right|
from the error
o(n^{-1}),

while (22) does not do this. In this sense
2\left|q_{2n}\right|

can be seen as first term of the asymptotic formula for the gaps in

\sigma(L(q))
with the potential
q\in L_{1}[0,1].
Similarly, (21) give us
the second term
2\left|q_{2n}-S_{2n}+2Q_{0}Q_{2n}\right|-2\left|q_{2n}\right|
of the asymptotic formula for the gaps in

\sigma(L(q)).

Then we obtain asymptotic formulas with the error term
O\left((\frac{\ln n}{n})^{k}\right)
for the
n
th periodic and antiperiodic eigenvalues and

n
th gaps in the spectrum of the operator
L(q)
with
q\in L_{1}[0,1],

where
k=3,4,...
. Many studies are devoted to these asymptotic formulas for
differentiable potentials (see [8, Chapter 1] and [7, Chapter 3] and their
references). Here we consider only the potential
q
from
L_{1}[0,1]
.
Finally, we take the Kronig-Penney model as an example and illustrate formulas
(20) and (21) with this example.

Main Results

In this section we obtain asymptotic formulas for the eigenvalues and
eigenfunctions of the operators
L_{0}(q)
and
L_{\pi}(q)
and for the gaps
in
\sigma(L(q)).
First, we prove the following obvious corollary of (14).

Proposition 1

If
q\in L_{1}[0,1],
then the following equalities hold

a_{1}(\lambda_{n,j})=a_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})+o(n^{-2}),

a_{2}(\lambda_{n,j})=a_{2}((2\pi n)^{2})+o(n^{-3}\ln n)

and

b_{1}(\lambda_{n,j})=b_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})+o(n^{-2}),

where
a_{1}((2\pi n)^{2}),

a_{2}((2\pi n)^{2})
and
b_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})

are obtained respectively from
a_{1}(\lambda_{n,j}),

a_{2}(\lambda_{n,j})

and
b_{1}(\lambda_{n,j})
by changing
\lambda_{n,j}
to
(2\pi n)^{2}
in
the corresponding formulas.

Proof.
From (14) it follows that

\frac{q_{n_{1}}q_{-n_{1}}}{\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}))^{2}}-\frac{q_{n_{1}}q_{-n_{1}}}{(2\pi n)^{2}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}))^{2}}=

\frac{o(1)}{((2\pi n)^{2}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}))^{2})^{2}}=\frac{o(1)}{(n_{1}(2n-n_{1}))^{2}}.

Now, using this and the obvious equality

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\neq 0,2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n_{1}(2n-n_{1}))^{2}}=O(n^{-2})

we obtain a proof of the first equality of the proposition. In a similar way
we obtain the proof of the third equality.

Now, let’s prove the second equality. Using (14), it is easy to see that

\frac{q_{n_{1}}q_{n_{2}}q_{-n_{1}-n_{2}}}{[\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}))^{2}][\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}-n_{2}))^{2}]}-

\frac{q_{n_{1}}q_{n_{2}}q_{-n_{1}-n_{2}}}{[(2\pi n)^{2}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}))^{2}][(2\pi n)^{2}-(2\pi(n-n_{1}-n_{2}))^{2}]}=

\frac{o(1)}{n_{1}(2n-n_{1})((n_{1}+n_{2})(2n-n_{1}-n_{2}))^{2}}+

\frac{o(1)}{(n_{1}(2n-n_{1}))^{2}(n_{1}+n_{2})(2n-n_{1}-n_{2})},

which provides a proof of the second equality.

Now, let’s estimate
a_{1}(\lambda_{n,j})
in detail

Lemma 1

(a)
If
q\in L_{1}[0,1],
then the following estimation holds

a_{1}(\lambda_{n,j})=o(n^{-1}).

(23)

(b)
For each real-valued
q\in L_{1}[0,1]
the expressions
a_{1}(\lambda_{n,j})
,
a_{2}(\lambda_{n,j}),...
are the real numbers.

Proof.

(a)
By Proposition 1 we need to prove that

a_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})=o(n^{-1}).

Since

q_{-k}=\int\limits_{0}^{1}q(x)e^{i2\pi kx}dx=\overline{q_{k}},\text{
}\left|q_{k}\right|=\left|q_{-k}\right|,q_{0}=0,

grouping the terms with indices
k
and
-k
we obtain

a_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{2nk-k^{2}}=

(24)

\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N},k\neq 2n}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{2nk-k^{2}}-\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{2nk+k^{2}}=

=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N},k\neq 2n}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{\left(2n-k\right)(2n+k)}.

Now to prove (23) we show that the following two equalities hold

\frac{-1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N},k\neq 2n}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{\left(2n-k\right)(2n+k)}=\int_{0}^{1}(Q(x,n)-Q_{0})^{2}e^{-i8\pi nx}\,dx

(25)

and

\int_{0}^{1}(Q(x,n)-Q_{0})^{2}e^{-i8\pi nx}\,dx=o(n^{-1}),

(26)

where

Q(x,n)=\int_{0}^{x}q(t)e^{i4\pi nt}\,dt-q_{-2n}x.

First let us prove (25) by calculation its right-hand side . It is clear that

Q(1,n)=Q(0,n)=0,\text{ }Q^{{}^{\prime}}(x,n)=q(x)e^{i4\pi nx}\,-q_{-2n}.

(27)

Therefore, using the integrations by parts we see that the Fourier

Q_{2n+k}=(Q(x,n),e^{i2\pi(2n+k)x})
of

for
2n+k\neq 0

Q_{2n+k}=\frac{q_{k}}{i2\pi(2n+k)}.

Moreover,
q_{0}=0

Q(x,n)-Q_{0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,-2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{q_{k}e^{i2\pi(2n+k)x}\,}{i2\pi(2n+k)}

of

To prove (26), first we show that

Since

for

From the last two inequalities it follows
that

converges uniformly for

Now, we are ready to prove (26). Using integration by
parts and (27) we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}(Q(x,n)-Q_{0})^{2}e^{-i8\pi nx}\,dx=\frac{1}{{}^{i8\pi nt}}\int_{0}^{1}2(Q(x,n)-Q_{0})(q(x)e^{i4\pi nx}\,-q_{-2n})e^{-i8\pi nx}\,dx=

\frac{1}{{}^{i4\pi nt}}\int_{0}^{1}Q(x,n)(q(x)e^{i4\pi nx}\,-q_{-2n})e^{-i8\pi nt}\,dt-

\frac{Q_{0}(n)}{{}^{i4\pi nt}}\int_{0}^{1}(q(x)e^{i4\pi nx}\,-q_{-2n})e^{-i8\pi nt}\,dt=o(n^{-1}).

Thus (23) follows from (24)-(26).

Theorem 1

If

(a)

(a)

\leq\left|(\lambda_{n,j}-(2\pi n)^{2})-q_{2n}\overline{u_{n,j}}\frac{1}{u_{n,j}}\right|=O(\alpha_{n,j})

which implies the proof of
(a)
, since

(b)

=-1

\left|u_{n,1}\overline{u_{n,2}}-u_{n,2}\overline{u_{n,1}}\right|<\frac{3}{4}.

(33)

On the other hand, if

\displaystyle q(x)

$b\text{ if }x\in(c,1]$\end{tabular}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ ,\ \ \right.

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle<0<b,\text{ }ac+(1-c)b=0.

Theorem 11

Let
c=\frac{p}{m}\in(0,1),
where
p
and
m
are irreducible positive integers.

(a)
If
k=ms
for some
s\in\mathbb{N},
then

\delta_{k}=\frac{-2ba}{(2\pi k)^{2}}+o(\frac{1}{k^{2}})\sim\frac{1}{k^{2}}.

(b)
If
k=ms+l,
where
l=1,2,...,m-1,
then

\delta_{k}=\left|\frac{a-b}{2\pi k}(1-e^{-2\pi kic})\right|+O(\frac{1}{k^{2}})\sim\frac{1}{k}

Proof.

(a)
In this case

e^{-2\pi kic}=e^{-2\pi psi}=1.

Therefore, the proof follows from (57) and (63).

(b)
In this case

e^{-2\pi kic}=e^{-2\pi i\frac{p}{m}(ms+l)}=e^{-2\pi i\frac{pl}{m}}.

Consider
\frac{pl}{m}.
Since
p
and
m
are irreducible positive integers,
none of the factors
m_{1},m_{2},...,m_{j}
of
m
is a factors of
p.

Moreover, all of these factors cannot be factors of
l

since
l<m.
Therefore
\frac{pl}{m}=s+\frac{v}{m},

This implies that

e^{-2\pi i\frac{pl}{m}}-1\sim 1

\frac{a-b}{2\pi ki}(1-e^{-2\pi kic})\sim\frac{1}{k}.

On the other hand, remaining terms in the right side of (63) are equal to

O(\frac{1}{k^{2}}).
Thus, the proof follows from (57) and (63).

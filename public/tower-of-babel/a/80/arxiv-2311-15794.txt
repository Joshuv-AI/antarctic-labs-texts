A note on new weighted geometric inequalities for hypersurfaces in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n}
Jie Wu

Abstract
. In this note, we prove a family of sharp weighed inequality which involves weighted k k -th mean curvature integral and two distinct quermassintegrals for closed hypersurfaces in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . This inequality generalizes the corresponding result of Wei and Zhou [ 15 ] where their proof is based on earlier results of Kwong-Miao [ 10 , 11 ] . Here we present a proof which does not rely on Kwong-Miao’s results.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
53A07, 53C44.

1.
Introduction

The Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for quermassintegrals of convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as a generalization of the isoperimetric inequality, play an important role in classical geometry.
Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with boundary
\partial\Omega=\Sigma
.
The classical isoperimetric inequality is

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Sigma|}{\omega_{n-1}}\geq\bigg(\frac{Vol(\Omega)}{\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}}\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
|\Sigma|
denotes the area of
\Sigma
and
\omega_{n-1}
is the area of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Equality in (
1.1
) holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball.
Let
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1})
be the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
and for any integer
1\leq k\leq n-1
, define the
k
-th mean curvature
\sigma_{k}
and the normalized
k
-th mean curvature
H_{k}
of
\Sigma
by

\sigma_{k}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq{n-1}}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{k}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad H_{k}=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{C^{k}_{n-1}}.

Here we make a convention that
\sigma_{0}=H_{0}=1
and
\sigma_{k}=H_{k}=0
for
k\geq n
.
The celebrated Alexandrov-Fenchel quermassintegral inequalities
[
1
,
2
]
state that

(1.2)

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}d\mu\geq\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma}H_{j}d\mu\right)^{\frac{n-1-k}{n-1-j}},\quad 0\leq j<k\leq n-1,

for any convex hypersurface
\Sigma
and equality holds in (
1.2
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere.
By using the inverse curvature flows, Guan and Li
[
7
]
extended the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities (
1.2
) to star-shaped and
k
-convex hypersurfaces.
Recently, there is of great interest to establish the weighted geometric inequalities comparing the weighted curvature integrals and the quermassintegrals in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Though out the paper, let
r
be the Euclidean distance to any given point
O\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We say that
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called star-shaped, if its support function
u=\langle X,\nu\rangle
is positive everywhere on
\Sigma,
where
X
is the position function of
\Sigma
and
\nu
is the unit outer-normal of
\Sigma
at
X
. Here a hypersurface is called
k
-convex if its principal curvatures satisfy
H_{i}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.
In particular,
1
-convex is also called mean convex.

Applying the Reilly’s formula
[
13
]
, Kwong and Miao
[
10
]
first obtained a sharp inequality relating
r^{2}
-weighted mean curvature and the enclosed volume for a convex hypersurface and then extend it to a star-shaped, mean-convex hypersurface through the inverse mean curvature flow. Precisely, they showed the following inequality

(1.3)

\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}H_{1}\geq nVol(\Omega),

if
\Sigma
is star-shaped and mean convex. Equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere.

By using the inequality (
1.3
) and the inverse mean curvature flow, Girão and Rodrigues
[
4
]
proved for a star-shaped and mean convex hypersurface that

(1.4)

\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}H_{1}\geq\omega_{n-1}\bigg(\frac{|\Sigma|}{\omega_{n-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}.

Inequality (
1.4
) can be seen as an improvement of (
1.3
) by the classic isoperimetric equality (
1.1
).

For higher order mean curvature integrals, Kwong and Miao
[
11
]
also established a family of weighted inequalities as following:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth, closed, star-shaped and k-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 3)
.
Then for all
k=2,\cdots,n-1
, there holds

(1.5)

\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}H_{k}\geq\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere.

The method to prove (
1.5
) is the use of a generalized Hsiung-Minkowski formula
[
9
]
.

More recently, by using (
1.5
) and the inverse curvature flows, Wei and Zhou
[
15
]
established a sharp weighted geometric inequality with the right hand-side of inequality (
1.5
) replaced by
\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}
. These inequalities can be seen as the analogs of (
1.4
) for general
k
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth, closed, star-shaped and strictly k-convex hypersurface in
R^{n}(n\geq 3)
.
Then for all
k=2,\cdots,n-1
, there holds

(1.6)

\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}H_{k}\geq\omega_{n-1}\bigg(\frac{\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}}{\omega_{n-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{n-k+1}{n-k}}.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere.

Inequality(
1.6
) also improves (
1.5
) since (
1.6
) implies (
1.5
) by the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality for star-shaped and
k
-convex hypersurfaces. By the same reason, they acturally obtained a complete family of sharp weighted inequalities following from (
1.6
) with the right hand-side of (
1.5
) replaced by suitable power of
\int_{\Sigma}H_{l}\;(l\leq k-1)
as stated in their paper (see Theorem 1.2 in
[
15
]
).

However, we notice that the two proofs of inequalities (
1.6
) and (
1.4
) both relies on (
1.5
) and (
1.3
) respectively. It is natural to ask if one can give a direct proof of (
1.6
) and (
1.4
) without using Kwong-Miao’s results (
1.5
) and (
1.3
). We give an affirmative answer in this short note. Motivated by
[
7
]
, we observe that it is more convenient to use the following normalized inverse curvature flow

(1.7)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=\bigg(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\bigg)\nu,

rather than the un-normalized one. It’s well-known that flow (
1.7
) is equivalent to
the inverse curvature flow

(1.8)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}\nu,

introduced by Gerhardt
[
5
]
and Urbas
[
14
]
by rescaling.

Furthermore, by using (
1.7
), we are able to establish a new kind of weighed inequality which involves weighted
k
-th mean curvature integral as well as two distinct quermassintegrals. This inequality improves (
1.6
) by the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality (
1.2
) for star-shaped and
k
-convex hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth, closed, star-shaped and
k
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 3)
.
Then for each
k=1,\cdots,n-1,
there holds

(1.9)

\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}H_{k}d\mu+\frac{2(k-1)}{n-k+1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu\geq\frac{n+k-1}{n-k+1}\omega_{n-1}\bigg(\frac{\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}d\mu}{\omega_{n-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{n-k+1}{n-k}}.

Moreover, equality holds in (
1.9
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere.

Remark 1.4
.

(1)
When
k=1
, we make a convention that
\int_{\Sigma}H_{-1}d\mu=nVol(\Omega)
. Thus (
1.9
) reduces to Girão and Rodrigues’s result (
1.4
).

(2)
We remark that quite recently Kwong and Wei
[
12
]
obtained a slightly stronger result than (
1.9
) with a proof also using Kwong-Miao’s results (
1.3
) and (
1.5
), while our proof presented here does not rely on them. As in turn, our proof provides another approach to the proof of Kwong-Miao’s results (
1.3
) and (
1.5
).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(\Sigma^{n-1},g)
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
g
the induced metric and
h
its second fundamental form. Let
\{x^{1},\cdots x^{n-1}\}
be a local coordinate system of
\Sigma
and denote by
g_{ij}=g(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})
and
h_{ij}=h(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})
. The pricipal curvature
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1})
of
\Sigma
are the eigenvalues of shape opertor
h_{i}^{j}
, where
h_{i}^{j}=g^{jk}h_{ik}
and
(g^{ij})
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij})
. The
k
-th Newton operator of
h_{i}^{j}
is defined by

(2.1)

(T_{k})^{i}_{j}:=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k+1}}{\partial h^{j}_{i}}.

The following basic formulas about
\sigma_{k}
and
T
are well-known, see e.g.
[
7
,
13
]
.

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\delta^{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}_{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}h_{i_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots{h_{i_{k}}^{j_{k}}}=\frac{1}{k}{\rm tr}(T_{k-1}(h)h),

(2.3)

\displaystyle(T_{k})^{i}_{j}(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\delta^{ii_{1}\cdots i_{k}}_{jj_{1}\cdots j_{k}}h_{i_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots{h_{i_{k}}^{j_{k}}},

where
\delta^{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{k}}
is the generalized Kronecker delta given by

(2.4)

\delta^{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}_{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{k}}=\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\delta^{i_{1}}_{j_{1}}&\delta^{i_{2}}_{j_{1}}&\cdots&\delta^{i_{k}}_{j_{1}}\\
\delta^{i_{1}}_{j_{2}}&\delta^{i_{2}}_{j_{2}}&\cdots&\delta^{i_{k}}_{j_{2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\delta^{i_{1}}_{j_{k}}&\delta^{i_{2}}_{j_{k}}&\cdots&\delta^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}}\end{array}\right).

We recall the following lemma for smooth hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see for example
[
6
,
8
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\Sigma^{n-1},g)
be a smooth closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there holds

(2.5)

\nabla_{i}(T^{k-1}_{ij}\nabla_{j}r^{2})=2((n-k)\sigma_{k-1}-k\sigma_{k}u)=2kC_{n-1}^{k}(H_{k-1}-uH_{k}),

where
\nabla
is the covariant derivative with respect to the metric
g
.

Integrating above equality, we get the well-known Minkowski formulas in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(2.6)

\int_{\Sigma}uH_{k}d\mu=\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}d\mu.

We also need the following integral equalities which play an important role in our proof.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma^{n-1}
be a closed hypersurface in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, we have

(2.7)

\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}uH_{k}d\mu=\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}H_{k-1}d\mu+\frac{1}{2kC_{n-1}^{k}}\int_{\Sigma}(T_{k-1})^{ij}\nabla_{i}(r^{2})\nabla_{j}(r^{2})d\mu,

and

(2.8)

\int_{\Sigma}{u}^{2}H_{k}d\mu=\int_{\Sigma}{u}H_{k-1}d\mu+\frac{1}{4kC_{n-1}^{k}}\int_{\Sigma}(T_{k-1})^{ij}h_{i}^{l}\nabla_{j}(r^{2})\nabla_{l}(r^{2})d\mu.

Proof.

The proof is quite simple. First multiplying (
2.5
) by the function
r^{2}
and integrating by parts,
(
2.7
) follows immediately. Similarly, multiplying (
2.5
) by the support function
u
and integrating by parts, we obtain
(
2.8
) by the fact (see
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
for instance)

\nabla_{i}u=h_{i}^{l}\nabla_{l}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\bigg).

∎

We recall the following variational formula for the quermassintegral (see e.g.
[
3
,
7
]
):

(2.9)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k-1}d\mu_{t}=(n-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k}Fd\mu_{t},\qquad k=1,\cdots n-1

along the general normal variation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=F\nu.

Finally, we need a general variation equation for the
r^{2}
-weighted curvature integral
\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}H_{k}
as following:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be a smooth family of closed hypersurfaces in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying a general flow

(2.10)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=F\nu,

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal of
\Sigma_{t}
and
F
is a smooth function on
\Sigma_{t}
. For all
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, we have

(2.11)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}H_{k}d\mu_{t}=\int_{\Sigma}\bigg((n-1-k)\,r^{2}H_{k+1}+2(k+1){u}H_{k}-2kH_{k-1}\bigg)Fd\mu_{t}.

Proof.

The variational formula (
2.11
) is established in
[
15
]
. For the convenience of readers, we include the proof here.
First note that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}r^{2}=\langle\bar{\nabla}r^{2},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X\rangle=2\langle r\partial r,F\nu\rangle=2Fu,

where
\bar{\nabla}
is the covariant derivative with respct to the Euclidean metric.

In view of (
2.5
) and the divergence-free property of
T_{k-1}
, we have along the flow (
2.10
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}r^{2}\bigg)\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial t}d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}\sigma_{k}(F\sigma_{1})d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}2Fu\sigma_{k}\!+\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!r^{2}\bigg(\!-\!T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}F\!-\!F\big(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}\!-\!(k\!+\!1)\sigma_{k+1}\big)\bigg)d\mu_{t}\!+\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!r^{2}F\sigma_{k}\sigma_{1}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\bigg(2u\sigma_{k}-\nabla_{i}(T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{j}r^{2})+(k+1)r^{2}\sigma_{k+1}\bigg)Fd\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\big(2(k+1)u\sigma_{k}+(k+1)r^{2}\sigma_{k+1}-2(n-k)\sigma_{k-1}\big)Fd\mu_{t}.

Thus
(
2.11
) follows from the normalization

H_{k}=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{C_{n-1}^{k}}.

∎

3.
Proof of the main results

In this section, we give a direct proof of (
1.9
) without using Kwong-Miao’s results (
1.3
) and (
1.5
). The idea is to choose the normalized flow (
1.7
) of the inverse curvature flow rather than the un-normalized one.

The proof of Theorem 1.3
:

For all
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, first by choosing

F=\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u,

in (
2.10
), we derive from (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}H_{k}d\mu_{t}+\frac{2(k-1)}{n+1-k}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu_{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg((n-1-k)r^{2}H_{k+1}+2(k+1)uH_{k}-2kH_{k-1}+2(k-1)H_{k-1}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\bigg)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg((n-1-k)r^{2}H_{k+1}+2kuH_{k}+2(uH_{k}-H_{k-1})\bigg)\bigg(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\bigg)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}\bigg(\frac{H_{k+1}H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-uH_{k+1}\bigg)d\mu_{t}+2k\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(uH_{k-1}-u^{2}H_{k})d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k}\bigg(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\bigg)^{2}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}\bigg(H_{k}-uH_{k+1}\bigg)d\mu_{t}-\frac{1}{2C_{n-1}^{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(T_{k-1})^{ij}h_{i}^{l}\nabla_{j}(r^{2})\nabla_{l}(r^{2})d\mu_{t},

where in the last inequality we used (
2.8
) and the Newton-MacLaurin inequality

H_{k+1}H_{k-1}\leq H_{k}^{2}.

To deal with the second term in the above inequality, we apply the well-known iteration of the Newton operator

T_{k}=\sigma_{k}I-T_{k-1}h,

to compute that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}H_{k}d\mu_{t}+\frac{2(k-1)}{n+1-k}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu_{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}\bigg(H_{k}-uH_{k+1}\bigg)d\mu_{t}-\frac{1}{2kC_{n-1}^{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\bigg(-(T_{k})^{jl}+\sigma_{k}g^{jl}\bigg)\nabla_{j}(r^{2})\nabla_{l}(r^{2})d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}\bigg(H_{k}-uH_{k+1}\bigg)d\mu_{t}+\frac{2(k+1)C_{n-1}^{k+1}}{2C_{n-1}^{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}(uH_{k+1}-H_{k})d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2C_{n-1}^{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\sigma_{k}g^{jl}\nabla_{j}(r^{2})\nabla_{l}(r^{2})d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k}|\nabla(r^{2})|^{2}d\mu_{t},

where we plugged into (
2.7
) in the second equality.
Since
H_{k}\geq 0
, we derive

(3.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}H_{k}d\mu_{t}+\frac{2(k-1)}{n+1-k}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu_{t}\bigg)\leq 0.

On the other hand, the
k
th quermassintegral is preserved by the Minkowski formula (
2.6
) that

(3.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k-1}d\mu_{t}=(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k}\big(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\big)d\mu_{t}=0.

Combining the (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) together, we have for each
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, the function

Q_{k}(t)=\bigg(\frac{\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}}{\omega_{n-1}}\bigg)^{-\frac{n-k+1}{n-k}}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}H_{k}d\mu_{t}+\frac{2(k-1)}{n+1-k}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu_{t}\bigg)\\

is monotone decreasing along the normalized flow (
1.7
).

If the initial hypersurface
\Sigma
is star-shaped and
k
-convex, Gerhardt
[
5
]
an Urbas
[
14
]
proved that star-shapeness and the
k
-convexity are preserved and the solution
\Sigma_{t}
expands to infinity and properly rescaled solution converges to a round sphere
S_{r_{\infty}}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Note that the quantity
Q_{k}(t)
is a scaling invariant, we have

Q_{k}(0)\geq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}Q_{k}(t)=\frac{n+k-1}{n-k+1}\omega_{n-1}.

Therefore we obtain the inequality (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) for a smooth, star-shaped and
(k+1)
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Moreover if equality holds, then

\frac{d}{dt}Q_{k}(t)=0
holds on the solution of
\Sigma_{t}
of the flow (
1.7
) for all time t which implies that the initial hypersurface
\Sigma
is a coordinate sphere. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

From the above proof, we actually have the following integral formula along the flow

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=\bigg(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\bigg)\nu,

that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}H_{k}d\mu_{t}+\frac{2(k-1)}{n+1-k}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu_{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}r^{2}\bigg(\frac{H_{k+1}H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-H_{k}\bigg)d\mu_{t}-2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k}\bigg(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\bigg)^{2}d\mu_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k}|\nabla(r^{2})|^{2}d\mu_{t},

which may have the independent interest.

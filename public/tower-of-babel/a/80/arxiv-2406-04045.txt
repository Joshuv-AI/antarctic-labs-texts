On the Diameter of Undirected Cayley Graphs of Finite Abelian Groups
Béla Bajnok W. Kyle Beatty

Abstract
Let s s be a positive integer. Our goal is to find all finite abelian groups G G that contain a 2 2 -subset A A for which the undirected Cayley graph Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) \Gamma(G,A) has diameter at most s s . We provide a complete answer when G G is cyclic, and a conjecture and some partial answers when G G is noncyclic. 2020 AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary: 11B13; Secondary: 05B10, 05C35, 11P70, 11B75, 20K01. Key words: Abelian group, Cayley graph, diameter, sumset, signed sumset.

Introduction

Throughout this paper we let
G
be an additively written finite abelian group. When
G
is cyclic and of order
n
, we identify it with
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
; we consider
0,1,\dots,n-1
interchangeably as integers and as elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Below we use standard terminology and notation of additive combinatorics (see
[
1
]
).

Given a finite abelian group
G
and a subset
A\subseteq G
, the
undirected Cayley graph

\Gamma(G,A)
has its vertex set formed by the elements of
G
, and has an edge between two vertices whenever their difference is an element of
A
, that is,
g_{1}\in G
and
g_{2}\in G
are connected when
g_{2}=g_{1}+a
or
g_{1}=g_{2}+a
for some
a\in A
. If
A
is a generating set of
G
, then
\Gamma(G,A)
is connected; in particular, if
A
is an
s
-spanning set
of
G
, meaning that every element of
G
can be written as the signed sum of at most
s
(not-necessarily distinct) elements of
A
, then
\Gamma(G,A)
has diameter at most
s
.

Suppose now that
A
is an
m
-subset of
G
. It is easy to compute that there are at most
\sum_{i\geq 0}{m\choose i}{h-1\choose i-1}2^{i}
vertices of
\Gamma(G,A)
that are of distance
h
away from any given vertex, and thus if
A
is an
s
-spanning set in
G
, then the order of
G
can be at most

\sum_{h=0}^{s}\sum_{i\geq 0}{m\choose i}{h-1\choose i-1}2^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}{m\choose i}{s\choose i}2^{i}.

Therefore, we have the following bound.

Proposition 1.1

For positive integers
m
and
s
, define

\displaystyle a(m,s)=\sum_{i\geq 0}{m\choose i}{s\choose i}2^{i}.

(1)

If an abelian group
G
has an
m
-subset
A
for which the diameter of
\Gamma(G,A)
is
s
, then the order of
G
is at most
a(m,s)
.

We note, in passing, that the values of
a(m,s)
are known as
Delannoy numbers
(see
[
3
]
), and that they satisfy the recursion

a(m,s)=a(m,s-1)+a(m-1,s)+a(m-1,s-1).

It is a natural question to ask for the largest possible order of a group
G
with an
m
-subset
A
for which the diameter of
{\Gamma}(G,A)
is at most
s
. Before we discuss our results on this question, let us review the analogous question in the directed case.

Given a finite abelian group
G
and a subset
A\subseteq G
, the
directed Cayley graph

\vec{\Gamma}(G,A)
has its vertex set formed by the elements of
G
, and has a directed edge from
g_{1}\in G
to
g_{2}\in G
when
g_{2}=g_{1}+a
for some
a\in A
. We say that
A
is an
s
-basis
of
G
if every element of
G
can be written as the sum of at most
s
(not-necessarily distinct) elements of
A
; in this case, the diameter of
\vec{\Gamma}(G,A)
is at most
s
.

For an
m
-subset
A
of
G
, we see that there are at most
{m+h-1\choose h}
vertices of
\vec{\Gamma}(G,A)
that are of distance
h
away from any given vertex, so if
A
is an
s
-basis of
G
, then we must have

|G|\leq\sum_{h=0}^{s}{m+h-1\choose h}={m+s\choose s}.

When equality holds, we say that
A
is a
perfect
s
-basis
of
G
: in this case, every element of
G
arises as the sum of at most
s
(not-necessarily distinct) elements of
A
in a unique way. Trivially, the set of nonzero elements is a perfect
1
-basis in any
group
G
, and the
1
-element set consisting of a generator of
G
is a perfect
s
-basis
in the cyclic group of order
s+1
. It turns out that there are no others:

Theorem 1.2
(Bajnok, Berson, and Just; cf.
[
2
]
)

If a subset
A
of a finite abelian group
G
is a perfect
s
-basis, then
s=1

and
A=G\setminus\{0\}
, or
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{s+1}
and
|A|=1
.

Given the scarcity of perfect bases, it is natural to ask for the largest groups with a given diameter and size of generating set. The paper
[
9
]
asks the following questions:

•

What is the largest possible order of an abelian group
G
with an
m
-subset
A
for which the diameter of
\vec{\Gamma}(G,A)
is at most
s
?

•

What is the largest possible order of a cyclic group
G
with an
m
-subset
A
for which the diameter of
\vec{\Gamma}(G,A)
is at most
s
?

After a series of papers
[
10
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
4
]
, these two questions have been completely solved for the case of
m=2
. We have the following two results.

Theorem 1.3
(Morillo, Fiol, and Fàbrega; cf.
[
10
]
)

Let
s
be a positive integer. The largest possible order of a cyclic group
G
with a
2
-subset for which the diameter of
\vec{\Gamma}(G,A)
is at most
s
is given by
\left\lfloor(s^{2}+4s+3)/3\right\rfloor
.

Theorem 1.4
(Fiol; cf.
[
4
]
)

Let
s
be any positive integer. The largest possible order of an abelian group
G
with a
2
-subset for which the diameter of
\vec{\Gamma}(G,A)
is at most
s
is given by
\left\lfloor(s^{2}+4s+4)/3\right\rfloor
.

Observe that the quantities in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
agree, unless
s\equiv 1
mod 3; for that case one can verify that the group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3k}
has the
s
-basis
\{(0,1),(1,3k-1)\}
, where
k=(s+2)/3
. However, the question specifically for noncyclic groups is still open when
s\not\equiv 1
mod 3.

Let us now return to the undirected case. When equality occurs in Proposition
1.1
, then
A
is called a
perfect
s
-spanning set
in
G
. Perfect spanning sets can be easily characterized for
m=1
and
s=1
.
Note that
a(1,s)=2s+1
and, clearly, for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}
with
\gcd(a,2s+1)=1
, the set
\{a\}
is a perfect
s
-spanning set in
\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}
. Also,
a(m,1)=2m+1
, and for any group
G
of order
2m+1
, every asymmetric subset of size
m
(that is, a set containing exactly one of any nonzero element or its inverse) is a perfect
1
-spanning set in
G
.

From (
1
) we get
a(2,s)=2s^{2}+2s+1
, and below we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.5

Let
s
be a positive integer. The largest possible order of a cyclic group
G
with a
2
-subset for which the diameter of
{\Gamma}(G,A)
is at most
s
is given by
2s^{2}+2s+1
.

For noncyclic groups the situation seems a bit more nuanced. We have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.6

Let
s\geq 3
be an integer.

•

If
2s+1
is prime, then the largest noncyclic group with an
s
-spanning set of size
2
has order

2s^{2}
.

•

If
2s+1
is composite and
p
is its smallest prime divisor, then the largest noncyclic group with an
s
-spanning set of size
2
has order

2s^{2}+2s-(p^{2}-1)/2
.

In particular, for any noncyclic group
G
with an
s
-spanning set of size
2
we have
|G|\leq 2s^{2}+2s-4.

(We add that the largest noncyclic group with a
2
-spanning set of size
2
is
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.)

Below we prove that the quantities in Conjecture
1.6
are lower bounds.

Theorem 1.7

Let
s
be a positive integer.

•

The largest noncyclic group with an
s
-spanning set of size
2
has order at least
2s^{2}
.

•

If
2s+1
is composite and
p
is any prime divisor of
2s+1
, then the largest noncyclic group with an
s
-spanning set of size
2
has order at least
2s^{2}+2s-(p^{2}-1)/2
.

Establishing upper bounds appears to be more difficult, and we only have the partial results that we summarize here. We first note that a noncyclic group that has a spanning set of size
2
must have rank
2
and thus is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{kc}
for some integers
c\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
.

We say that the group
\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{kc}
is
s
-regular
for some positive integer
s
, if it possesses an
s
-spanning set of the form
\{(1,u),(1,v)\}
for some
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{kc}
. While we have no precise count on the number of
s
-regular groups, it appears that most groups of rank
2
that have an
s
-spanning set of size
2
are
s
-regular. For example, for
s=10
, of the
105
groups of rank
2
that have an
s
-spanning set of size
2
,
103
are
s
-regular. (The two exceptions are
\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{16}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{10}\times\mathbb{Z}_{20}
.) In Theorem
3.1
below we prove for every odd prime
c
and positive integer
k
that if
\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{kc}
has an
s
-spanning set at all, then it is
s
-regular.

Our results for upper bounds can be summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.8

Let
s
be a positive integer, and let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{kc}
be an
s
-regular group for some integers
c\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
.

•

If
c=3
and
s+1
is divisible by
3
, then
|G|\leq 2s^{2}+1
.

•

If
c
is odd and
s
is divisible by
c
, then
|G|\leq 2s^{2}+s
, and if
2s+1
is divisible by
c
, then
|G|\leq 2s^{2}+2s-(c^{2}-1)/2
.

•

In all other cases,
|G|\leq 2s^{2}
.

As a consequence of our results, we can prove that for
s\geq 3
, every
s
-regular abelian group of rank 2 has order at most
2s^{2}+2s-4
, with equality if, and only if, it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{(2s^{2}+2s-4)/3}
for some
s\equiv 1
mod
3
(See Theorem
3.3
below).

For
c=2
we have exact results, even without the assumption that the group is
s
-regular:

Theorem 1.9

Let
k
and
s
be positive integers. The largest possible order of a group of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
that has an
s
-spanning set of size
2
is
2s^{2}
when
s
is even and
2s^{2}-2
when
s
is odd.

In Section
4
below we provide some further results on groups of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
.

For
m\geq 3
, the largest order of abelian groups with an
m
-subset for which the diameter of their Cayley graphs is at most
s
is generally unknown, though some small cases for cyclic groups were displayed by Haanpää in
[
7
]
, by Hsu and Jia in
[
8
]
, and by Graham and Sloane in
[
6
]
.

Of particular interest are perfect spanning sets. An
m
-subset
A
of
G
is called a
perfect
s
-spanning set
, if every element of
G
arises as a signed sum of at most
s
terms in a unique way; in this case we have
|G|=a(m,s)
. Above we mentioned perfect spanning sets for
m=1
, for
s=1
, and for
m=2
; we believe that there are no others.

Conjecture 1.10

If an
m
-subset
A
of a finite abelian group
G
is a perfect
s
-spanning set, then one of the following holds:

•

m=1
,
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}
, and
A=\{a\}
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}
with
\gcd(a,2s+1)=1
;

•

s=1
, and

is the disjoint union of
\{0\}
,

, and

; or

•

m=2
,

, and

for some

\gcd(d,2s^{2}+2s+1)=1

In Sections

2
Constructions and lower bounds

In this section we provide some explicit constructions for
s
-spanning sets of size
2
. We denote the
s
-span of a 2-subset
A=\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
of
G
by

\langle A\rangle_{s}=\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}\mid\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s\}.

It will be helpful to have the following facts available.

Lemma 2.1

For a given positive integer
s
, let

I(s)=\{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mid\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s\}.

1.

For a given integer
h
with
|h|\leq s
, the number of ordered pairs
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in I(s)
with
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=h
equals
s+1
when
s
and
h
have the same parity, and
s
otherwise.

2.

There are
(s+1)^{2}
ordered pairs
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in I(s)
where
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
and
s
have the same parity, and
s^{2}
where
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
and
s
have opposite parity.

3.

There are
2s^{2}+2s+1
elements in
I(s)
.

Proof.
By symmetry, we may assume that
h\geq 0
. Observe that the ‘leftmost’ integer point
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
on the line
x+y=h
within
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s
is

\left(-\left\lfloor(s-h)/2\right\rfloor,\;h+\left\lfloor(s-h)/2\right\rfloor\right),

and the ‘rightmost’ such point is

\left(\left\lfloor(s+h)/2\right\rfloor,\;h-\left\lfloor(s+h)/2\right\rfloor\right),

a set of
s+1
points when
s
and
h
have the same parity, and
s
points otherwise. This establishes the first claim, from which our other two claims follow easily.
\Box

Proposition 2.2

For all positive integers
s
and
n
with

2\leq n\leq 2s^{2}+2s+1,

the set
\{s,s+1\}
is an
s
-spanning set in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

Proof.
The fact that
n
cannot be larger than
2s^{2}+2s+1
follows from Proposition
1.1
.
We verify that for every
n
in the range given above, we have
\langle\{s,s+1\}\rangle_{s}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}.

Considering the elements of

S=\{\lambda_{1}s+\lambda_{2}(s+1)\mid\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s\}

in
\mathbb{Z}
(rather than
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
), we see that they
lie in the interval
[-(s^{2}+s),(s^{2}+s)]
. Since the index set

I(s)=\{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mid\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s\}

contains exactly
2s^{2}+2s+1
elements (see Lemma
2.1
), it suffices to prove that no integer in
[-(s^{2}+s),(s^{2}+s)]
can be written as an element of
S
in two different ways.

For that, suppose that

\lambda_{1}s+\lambda_{2}(s+1)=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}s+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}(s+1)

for some
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in I(s)
and
(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime})\in I(s)
; without loss of generality, we can assume that
\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
. Our equation implies that
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
is divisible by
s
and is, therefore, equal to 0,
s
, or
2s
.

If
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=2s,
then
\lambda_{2}=s
and
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=-s
; since
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s
and
|\lambda_{1}^{\prime}|+|\lambda_{2}^{\prime}|\leq s
, this can only happen if
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=0
, but this case leads to a contradiction with our equation.

Assume, next, that
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=s.
Our equation then yields
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=-s-1.
In this case, we have
\lambda_{1}\leq 0
,
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\geq 0
,
\lambda_{2}\geq 0
, and
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}\leq 0
, and we see that

|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|=-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=(s+1-\lambda_{1}^{\prime})+(s+\lambda_{2}^{\prime})=2s+1-(|\lambda_{1}^{\prime}|+|\lambda_{2}^{\prime}|)\geq s+1,

but that is a contradiction.

This leaves us with the case that
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
, which implies
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
, as claimed, and therefore the set
\{s,s+1\}
is
s
-spanning in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

\Box

Proposition 2.3

For every positive integer
s
, the set
\{(0,1),(1,1)\}
is an
s
-spanning set in
\mathbb{Z}_{s}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2s}
.

Proof.
We need to prove that for any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}_{s}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2s}
, we have
(a,b)\in\langle\{(0,1),(1,1)\}\rangle_{s}.

Note that by symmetry we may assume that
0\leq b\leq s
. We consider three subcases.

When
b\geq a
, we let
\lambda_{1}=b-a
and
\lambda_{2}=a
. We then have

\lambda_{1}(0,1)+\lambda_{2}(1,1)=(a,b)

and

|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=b\leq s,

so
(a,b)\in\langle\{(0,1),(1,1)\}\rangle_{s}.

Next, when
2a-s\leq b<a
, we again let
\lambda_{1}=b-a
and
\lambda_{2}=a
. In this case, we have

|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|=-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=2a-b\leq s,

so again
(a,b)\in\langle\{(0,1),(1,1)\}\rangle_{s}.

Finally, when
b<2a-s
, we let
\lambda_{1}=b-a+s
and
\lambda_{2}=a-s
. Here we have

\lambda_{1}(0,1)+\lambda_{2}(1,1)=(a-s,b),

where
a-s\equiv a
mod
s
. Furthermore,

|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}=(b-a+s)+(s-a)<s,

so again
(a,b)\in\langle\{(0,1),(1,1)\}\rangle_{s}.

This completes our proof.

\Box

Theorem 2.4

Let
c
and
s
be positive integers so that the remainder of
s
mod
c
is at least
(c-1)/2
. Let
u=\lfloor s/c\rfloor
,
v=\lceil s/c\rceil
, and
k=2uv
. Then
A=\left\{(1,u),(1,v)\right\}
is an
s
-spanning set in
G=\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{ck}
.

Proof.
Let
H=\langle(1,u)\rangle
be the subgroup of
G
generated by the element
(1,u)
. Since
H
has order
2cv
, it has
cu
cosets in
G
. We claim that these cosets are
\lambda_{2}\cdot(1,v)+H
, where
\lambda_{2}=0,1,\ldots,cu-1
. In order to prove this, we need to show that these
cu
cosets are pairwise distinct.

Suppose, then, that
\mu_{1}(1,v)
and
\mu_{2}(1,v)
are in the same coset of
H
for some
0\leq\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\leq cu-1
. This implies that
(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})\cdot(1,v)
is an element of
H
, so there exists an integer
\mu
for which

(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})\cdot(1,v)=\mu\cdot(1,u).

From the first coordinates, this means that
\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}-\mu
is divisible by
c
, and from the second coordinates we see that

(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})v-\mu u
, which, since
v=u+1
, equals
(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}-\mu)u+(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}),
is divisible by
ck
and thus by
cu
. Combining these, we get that
\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}
must also be divisible by
cu
, but that is only possible if
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.

Let
g\in G
be arbitrary. According to our claim, we have integers
\lambda_{1}\in[-cv+1,cv]
and
\lambda_{2}\in[0,cu-1]
for which

g=\lambda_{1}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}(1,v).

By assumption, we have integers
q
(quotient) and
r
(remainder) with
(c-1)/2\leq r\leq c-1
for which
s=qc+r
. We then have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq cv+(cu-1)=c(q+1)+(cq-1)=2cq+c-1=2s+(c-2r-1)\leq 2s.

(2)

If
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s
, then
g\in\langle A\rangle_{s}
, and we are done.

Assume then that
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\geq s+1
. In this case, let
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}-\epsilon cv
and
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\lambda_{2}-cu
, where
\epsilon
equals
1
when
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
, and
-1
when
\lambda_{1}<0
.
We can compute that

\lambda_{1}^{\prime}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}(1,v)=(\lambda_{1}-\epsilon cv)(1,u)+(\lambda_{2}-cu)(1,v)=\lambda_{1}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}(1,v)-(\epsilon cv+cu,(\epsilon+1)cuv),

and since
\epsilon+1
equals
0
or
2
, in
G
we have

\lambda_{1}^{\prime}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}(1,v)=\lambda_{1}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}(1,v)=g.

Finally, we show that
|\lambda_{1}^{\prime}|+|\lambda_{2}^{\prime}|\leq s
.
Indeed, we have

|\lambda_{1}^{\prime}|+|\lambda_{2}^{\prime}|=|\lambda_{1}-\epsilon cv|+|\lambda_{2}-cu|=cv-|\lambda_{1}|+cu-|\lambda_{2}|.

From (
2
) above we see that
cv+cu\leq 2s+1
, and by our current assumption we have
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\geq s+1
. Therefore,

|\lambda_{1}^{\prime}|+|\lambda_{2}^{\prime}|\leq s
, as claimed, and our proof is now complete.

\Box

Corollary 2.5

Let
s
be a positive integer for which
2s+1
is composite, and let
p
be any prime divisor of
2s+1
. Then the largest noncyclic group with an
s
-spanning set of size
2
has order at least
2s^{2}+2s-(p^{2}-1)/2.

Proof.
Let us write
2s+1=pt
for some integer
t\geq 2
. Note that
p
and
t
must be odd, and we have

s=\frac{t-1}{2}p+\frac{p-1}{2},

\lfloor s/p\rfloor=(t-1)/2
, and
\lceil s/p\rceil=(t+1)/2
. Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{pk}
where
k=(t^{2}-1)/2
. Then
G
has order
2s^{2}+2s-(p^{2}-1)/2
and, according to Theorem
2.4
, it has an
s
-spanning set of size
2
.

\Box

3
s
-regular groups and upper bounds

Recall that we say that the group
\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{kc}
is
s
-regular
for some positive integer
s
, if it possesses an
s
-spanning set of the form
\{(1,u),(1,v)\}
for some
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{kc}
. In this section we provide some upper bounds for the largest possible order of an
s
-regular group.

Theorem 3.1

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{ck}
for some integers
c\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
that has an
s
-spanning set for some positive integer
s
. If
c
is an odd prime, then
G
is
s
-regular.

Proof.

First, we prove that
G
has an
s
-spanning set of the form
A=\{(a,u),(b,v)\}
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{c}
and
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{ck}
with
u\not\equiv v
mod
c
. Note that if
\{(a,u),(b,v)\}
is an
s
-spanning set, then so is
\{(a,u),(-b,-v)\}
, so if our claim were false, then we would have
u\equiv v\equiv-v
mod
c
for each
s
-spanning set
A=\{(a,u),(b,v)\}
of
G
. But since
c
is an odd prime, that would imply that
u\equiv v\equiv 0
mod
c
, and that is a contradiction with
A
being a spanning set.

Suppose then that
A=\{(a,u),(b,v)\}
is an
s
-spanning in
G
with
u\not\equiv v
mod
c
. We will prove that, in this case,
B=\{(1,u),(1,v)\}
is also an
s
-spanning in
G
.

Let

I(s)=\{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mid\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s\},

and consider the function
f:I(s)\rightarrow G
, given by

(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mapsto\lambda_{1}(a,u)+\lambda_{2}(b,v).

Since
A
is an
s
-spanning set,
f
is surjective; we can thus find a subset
I_{0}
of
I(s)
on which the restriction
f_{0}:I_{0}\rightarrow G
of
f
is bijective. We will prove that
g_{0}:I_{0}\rightarrow G
, given by

(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mapsto\lambda_{1}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}(1,v)

is also bijective. It suffices to show that
g_{0}
is injective. In order to do so, let us assume that the equation

\lambda_{1}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}(1,v)=\mu_{1}(1,u)+\mu_{2}(1,v)

holds in
G

for some
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
and
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
of
I_{0}
. We then have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\equiv\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\;\text{mod}\;c,

(3)

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v\equiv\mu_{1}u+\mu_{2}v\;\text{mod}\;ck.

(4)

From (
4
) we find that

(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})u+\lambda_{2}(v-u)\equiv(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})u+\mu_{2}(v-u)\;\text{mod}\;ck;

considering (
3
), this gives us that

\lambda_{2}(v-u)\equiv\mu_{2}(v-u)\;\text{mod}\;c.

Therefore, since
c
is prime and
u\not\equiv v
mod
c
, we get that
\lambda_{2}\equiv\mu_{2}
mod
c.
From (
3
) then we get
\lambda_{1}\equiv\mu_{1}
mod
c
, and thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}a+\lambda_{2}b\equiv\mu_{1}a+\mu_{2}b\;\text{mod}\;c.

(5)

But
f_{0}
is an injection, so (
5
) and (
4
) can hold together only when
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
. This completes our proof.

\Box

Theorem 3.2

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{ck}
for some integers
c\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
, and suppose that
G
is
s
-regular for some positive integer
s
.

1.

If
c
is even, then
|G|\leq 2s^{2},
and equality may occur only when
s\equiv 0
mod
c
.

2.

If
c
is odd and

•

s\equiv 0
mod
c
, then
|G|\leq 2s^{2}+s
;

•

s\equiv(c-1)/2
mod
c
, then
|G|\leq 2s^{2}+2s-(c^{2}-1)/2;

•

c=3
and
s\equiv 2
mod
3
, then
|G|\leq 2s^{2}+1
.

In all other cases,
|G|<2s^{2}
.

Proof.
Let
A=\{(1,u),(1,v)\}
be an
s
-spanning set in
G
, in which case

\{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v)\mid(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s\}=G.

Recall from Lemma
2.1
that, for a given integer
h
with
|h|\leq s
, the number
\Lambda(h)
of ordered pairs
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s
and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=h
equals
s+1
when
s
and
h
have the same parity, and
s
otherwise. For each
i=0,1,\ldots,c-1
, we let
N(i)
denote the sum of all
\Lambda(h)
with
|h|\leq s
and
h\equiv i
mod
c
.
Since
A
is an
s
-spanning set in
G
, we must have

\{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v)\mid(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s\}=\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{ck},

and thus for each
i=0,1,\ldots,c-1
,

\{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v)\mid(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s,\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\equiv i\;\mbox{mod}\;c\}=\{i\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{ck}.

In particular,

\displaystyle|G|\leq cN(i)

(6)

must hold for each
i=0,1,\ldots,c-1
.
We now inspect (
6
) in several cases.

When
c
is even, we consider
i=r+1
, where
r
is the nonnegative remainder of
s
mod
c
(with
r+1
understood to be
0
when
r=c-1
). To compute
N(r+1)
, we need to identify the values of
h
for which
|h|\leq s
and
h\equiv r+1
mod
c
, and these values are
h=s+1-jc
for
j=1,2,\ldots,j_{\mathrm{max}}
, where

j_{\mathrm{max}}=\lfloor(2s+1)/c\rfloor=(2s-2r)/c+\lfloor(2r+1)/c\rfloor.

Note that
\lfloor(2r+1)/c\rfloor
equals
0
when
0\leq r\leq c/2-1
, and
1
when
c/2\leq r\leq c-1
. Since
s
and
h=s+1-jc
have different parity, and thus
\Lambda(h)=s
for each
j
, we have

cN(r+1)=\left(2s-2r+c\lfloor(2r+1)/c\rfloor\right)s,

and we see that this quantity is at most
2s^{2}
, with equality when
r=0
or
r=c/2
. But
|G|
must be divisible by
c^{2}
, so
|G|=2s^{2}
may only occur when
r=0
.

When
c
is odd and
r\leq c-2
, we consider
i=r+2
(where
r+2
is understood to be
0
when
r=c-2
). In this case the values of
h
with
|h|\leq s
and
h\equiv r+2
mod
c
are
h=s+2-tc
for
t=1,2,\ldots,t_{\mathrm{max}}
, where

t_{\mathrm{max}}=\lfloor(2s+2)/c\rfloor=(2s-2r)/c+\lfloor(2r+2)/c\rfloor.

Note that
\lfloor(2r+2)/c\rfloor
equals
0
when
0\leq r\leq(c-3)/2
, and
1
when
(c-1)/2\leq r\leq c-2
. Since
s
and
h=s+2-tc
have the same parity when
t
is even and different parity when
t
is odd, we have

cN(r+2)=\left(s-r+c\lfloor(2r+2)/c\rfloor\right)s+(s-r)(s+1).

We consider four subcases. For
r=0
, this quantity equals
2s^{2}+s
, and when
1\leq r\leq(c-3)/2
, it equals
(s-r)s+(s-r)(s+1)
, which is less than
2s^{2}
. When
(c+1)/2\leq r\leq c-2
, we have

cN(r+2)=\left(s-r+c\right)s+(s-r)(s+1)=(s-r)(2s+1)+cs\leq\left(s-(c+1)/2\right)(2s+1)+cs,

which again is less than
2s^{2}
.
Our last subcase is when
r=(c-1)/2
, when we have

cN(r+2)=\left(s-(c-1)/2\right)(2s+1)+cs=2s^{2}+2s-(c-1)/2.

Observe that when
s\equiv(c-1)/2
mod
c
, then
2s^{2}+2s-(c^{2}-1)/2\equiv 0
mod
c^{2}
, and since
|G|
must be divisible by
c^{2}
, this is the largest it can then be.

Our final case is when
c
is odd and
r=c-1
; we consider
i=0
. In this case the values of
h
with
|h|\leq s
and
h\equiv 0
mod
c
are
h=s+1-zc
for
z=1,2,\ldots,z_{\mathrm{max}}
, where

z_{\mathrm{max}}=\lfloor(2s+1)/c\rfloor=(2s+2-c)/c.

Note that
z_{\mathrm{max}}
is odd. Since
s
and
h=s+1-zc
have the same parity when
z
is odd and different parity when
z
is even, we have

N(0)=(z_{\mathrm{max}}-1)/2\cdot s+(z_{\mathrm{max}}+1)/2\cdot(s+1),

and thus

cN(0)=\left(s+1-c\right)s+\left(s+1\right)(s+1)=2s^{2}-(c-3)s+1,

which is less than
2s^{2}
, unless
c=3
. This completes our proof.

\Box

Theorem 3.3

For
s\geq 3
, every
s
-regular abelian group of rank 2 has order at most
2s^{2}+2s-4
, with equality if, and only if, it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{(2s^{2}+2s-4)/3}
for some
s\equiv 1
mod
3
.

Proof.
Suppose that
G=\mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{ck}
is an
s
-regular group of order at least
2s^{2}+2s-4
for some integers
s\geq 3
,
c\geq 2
, and
k\geq 1
. Then, by Theorem
3.2
,
c
is odd; furthermore,

•

s\equiv 0
mod
c
, and
2s^{2}+2s-4\leq c^{2}k\leq 2s^{2}+s
, or

•

s\equiv(c-1)/2
mod
c
, and
2s^{2}+2s-4\leq c^{2}k\leq 2s^{2}+2s-(c^{2}-1)/2.

Since the first possibility leads to no solutions, our claim is established.

\Box

4
The groups
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}

In this section we provide some results for
s
-spanning sets in groups of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
, including the exact value of the largest possible order of such groups. Our general conjecture is as follows.

Conjecture 4.1

Let
k
and
s
be positive integers. The group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
has an
s
-spanning set of size
2
if, and only if, one of the following holds:

•

s
is odd and
k\leq(s^{2}-1)/2
;

•

s
is even and
k\leq(s^{2}-s)/2
;

•

s\equiv 0
mod
4
,
k
is even, and
(s^{2}-s)/2<k\leq s^{2}/2
; or

•

s\equiv 2
mod
4
,
k\equiv 2
mod
4
, and
(s^{2}-s)/2<k\leq s^{2}/2
.

Theorem 4.2

Let
k
and
s
be positive integers. If the group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
has an
s
-spanning set of size 2, then it has order at most
2s^{2}
.

Proof.
If
G
is
s
-regular, that is, it has an
s
-spanning set of the form
\{(1,u),(1,v)\}
for some
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
, then our claim follows from Theorem
3.2
. Since no set of the form
\{(0,u),(0,v)\}
can be a spanning set, we can suppose that
G
has an
s
-spanning set of the form
A=\{(0,u),(1,v)\}
for some
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
. Therefore, each element in
\langle A\rangle_{s}
has the form
(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v)
for some integers
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
with
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s
.

Clearly, if
u
and
v
are both even, then
A
can only generate elements of
G
with an even second component, and thus it cannot be a spanning set.

Now if
u
and
v
are both odd, then
\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v
has the same parity as
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
does. According to Lemma
2.1
, the parity of
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
differs from the parity of
s
exactly
s^{2}
times (and agrees with it
(s+1)^{2}
times) within
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s
. This means that
A
cannot generate more than
s^{2}
elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
whose second coordinate has different parity than
s
does, so if it is an
s
-spanning set, then we must have
|G|\leq 2s^{2}
.

Next, if
u
is even and
v
is odd, then for each element of
G
that
A
generates, its two components will have the same parity (namely, the parity of
\lambda_{2}
). Therefore,
A
cannot be a spanning set.

Finally, we prove that if
A=\{(0,u),(1,v)\}
is an
s
-spanning set of
G
with
u
odd and
v
even, then
A^{\prime}=\{(1,u),(1,v)\}
is an
s
-spanning set as well, meaning that
G
is
s
-regular and thus has order at most
2s^{2}
by Theorem
3.2
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
. Since
\langle A\rangle_{s}=G
, there are integers
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
with
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\leq s
for which

\lambda_{1}(0,u)+\lambda_{2}(1,v)=(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v)=(a^{\prime},b),

where
a^{\prime}=a
if
b
is even and
a^{\prime}=1+a
when
b
is odd. Observe that when
b
is even, then (since
u
is odd and
v
is even),
\lambda_{1}
must be even, and thus

\lambda_{1}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}(1,v)=(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v)=(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}u+\lambda_{2}v)=(a^{\prime},b)=(a,b).

Similarly, when
b
is odd,
\lambda_{1}
must be odd, and

\lambda_{1}(1,u)+\lambda_{2}(1,v)=(1+a^{\prime},b)=(a,b).

Therefore,
\langle A^{\prime}\rangle_{s}=G
, as claimed, which completes our proof.

\Box

Theorem 4.3

Let
k
and
s
be positive integers with
s
odd. Then the group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
has an
s
-spanning set of size 2 if, and only if,

.

Proof.
The fact that
k\leq(s^{2}-1)/2
is necessary follows from Theorem
4.2
. We may also recall that Theorem
2.4
, applied with
c=2
and odd

and

Our goal below is to show that the same values of

and

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\{0,1,2,\ldots,k\}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and in the second component in

We thus need to establish that for every

there are integers
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(1,(s-1)/2)+\mu_{2}(1,(s+1)/2)=(a,b)

Let us recall from the proof of Theorem

\lambda_{1}\in[-s,s+1]

\displaystyle-(s^{2}-1)/2\leq B<s^{2}-1.

(8)

In case (i), we have

\Box

Theorem 4.4

Let
k
and
s
be positive integers with

even. Then the group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
has an
s
-spanning set of size 2 if
k\leq(s^{2}-s)/2
.

Proof.
Like in the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we will prove that there are integers
u
and
v
for which the

-span of

contains the set
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\{0,1,2,\ldots,k\}
. (Here too we consider the operation in the first component in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and in the second component in

Since

and

,
\langle A\rangle_{s-1}
contains the set

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\{s/2,s/2+1,\ldots,s/2+k\}

.

\Box

Wide-range T 2 T^{2} resistivity and umklapp scattering in moiré graphene
Hiroaki Ishizuka 1 , Leonid Levitov 2

Abstract
We argue that the unusually strong electron-electron interactions in the narrow bands in moiré superlattices originate from compact Wannier orbitals. Enhanced overlaps of electronic wavefunctions, enabled by such orbitals, result in a strong el-el superlattice umklapp scattering. We identify the umklapp scattering processes as a source of the strong temperature-dependent resistivity observed in these systems. In a simple model, the umklapp scattering predicts a T T -dependent resistivity that grows as T 2 T^{2} and is getting bigger as the Wannier orbital radius decreases. We quantify the enhancement in el-el scattering by the Kadowaki-Woods (KW) ratio, a quantity that is sensitive to umklapp scattering but, helpfully, insensitive to the effects due to the high density of electronic states. Our analysis predicts anomalously large KW ratio values that clearly indicate the importance of the umklapp el-el processes and their impact on the T T -dependent resistivity.

Quasiparticle interactions in metals leave unique marks on the temperature (

The two most striking properties of the

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}+{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}-{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2}-{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4}={\boldsymbol{G}}\neq 0,

(1)

where

As is well known, umklapp processes lead to a

The physics of this enhancement is broadly analogous to photon radiation enhancement by mode localization in the Purcell effect in quantum optics.
The enhancement of el-el scattering due to compact Wannier orbitals is a property tunable by the twist angle and other superlattice parameters that control moiré bands
Ishizuka2020
.
As illustrated in Fig.
1

The resulting

We note that a nonlinear temperature dependence that grows without saturation and extends over a wide range of temperatures is not expected within a FL framework that accounts only for normal (umklapp-free) el-el scattering processes

;
Hlubina1995
;
Rosch1999
;
Maslov2011
;
Pal2012

Known as the non-Galilean

Because of non-parabolic dispersion, momentum-conserving el-el scattering can lead to current relaxation and ohmic dissipation. However, the resulting
T^{2}
dependence quickly saturates due to non-Matthiessen effects arising when the
T^{2}
el-el scattering rate exceeds the

;
Pal2012

T^{2}
behavior is predicted by the non-Galilean mechanism.

It is interesting to mention that many different systems feature resistivity with a large nonlinear
T

These include, in particular, the oxide compounds such as SrTiO
3

;
vdMarel2011
;

, high-

;

2

2

Problems with a common FL explanation has led to considering other possibilities such as the scattering by soft phonons
Okuda2001
;
Maslov2017
;
Zhou2018
.
However, similar to TBG, the origin of the large nonlinear temperature dependence remains poorly understood.

Here, focusing on moiré bands, we study how the Wannier functions with small radius
\xi
affect the large-
q
part of the el-el interaction,
\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
, responsible for the el-el umklapp scattering.
As we will see, for

This yields a large

\theta=1.2^{\circ}

The FL

resistivity mechanism discussed here explains the behavior of resistivity seen near band edges in magic-angle TBG

. In addition, while the “strange-metal” linear-
T
resistivity in TBG conceivably arises from mechanisms that are not part of our FL-based picture, these mechanisms may originate from the strong el-el interactions enabled by compact Wannier orbitals.
Furthermore, in TBG with non-magic twist angles, the enhanced el-el scattering persists so long as Wannier orbitals remain compact.
In that regard, the key aspect of the spatial structure of Wannier orbitals that boosts umklapp scattering is a compact core, whereas the behavior in the tails – exponential vs. power-law – is of lesser importance.
As a result, strong umklapp scattering and

Po2018
;
Song2021
.
These predictions can be directly tested in slightly non-magic TBG.

The el-el umklapp scattering mechanism explains two key features — the strong
T^{2}
resistivity and its growth up to temperatures comparable to the bandwidth.
To delineate the interaction enhancement due to umklapps from the effects of the high density of states we consider
the Kadowaki-Woods (KW) ratio

;
Kadowaki1986
, a quantity that shows anomalous enhancement reflecting the large umklapp scattering, a convenient way to quantify the el-el scattering strength.

II
Method

To analyze the effect of compact Wannier orbitals on the transport properties, we consider a honeycomb model with Wannier function

\displaystyle H_{\text{imp}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime},\alpha,\sigma\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}a^{2}}{2V}\sum_{i}v({\boldsymbol{r}}_{i\alpha})e^{-{\rm i}({\boldsymbol{k}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime})\cdot{\boldsymbol{r}}_{i\alpha}}\right)\psi_{{\boldsymbol{k}},\alpha}^{\dagger}\psi_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime},\alpha},

In general, the umklapp scattering does not contribute to the resistivity when Fermi wavenumber is as small as
k_{F}<G/4
.
Under these conditions, the resistivity, rather than vanishing, is exponentially suppressed,

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{int}}(T)\propto e^{-2(\mu_{T=0}-\mu^{\ast})/k_{B}T},

(15)

where
\mu_{T=0}
is the chemical potential at
T=0
and
\mu^{\ast}
is the chemical potential for the Fermi wavenumber
k_{F}=G/4
. This is so because the only electrons contributing to the resistivity are those thermally excited to a large momentum state that feature umklapp scattering.
The exponential suppression makes the umklapp resistivity negligible in conventional semiconductors.
For TBG, in contrast, the narrow bandwidth yields
(\mu_{T=0}-\mu^{\ast})/k_{B}T
values that are relatively small, allowing the umklapp scattering to contribute to resistivity.
In particular, in Fig.
3
,
(\mu_{T=0}-\mu^{\ast})/k_{B}\sim 20
K for
\nu=9/10
.
Accordingly, the umklapp resistivity increases as
\rho_{\text{int}}(T)\propto e^{-2(\mu_{T=0}-\mu^{\ast})/k_{B}T}
as the temperature grows, where
\mu_{T=0}
is the chemical potential at
T=0
[Fig.
3
b].
As shown in Fig.
3
a, however, the temperature dependence at moderate
T
does not look much different from that for fillings away from band edges, i.e. for a large Fermi surface.
We therefore expect a substantial superlinear temperature dependence due to umklapp scattering to be observable close to the band edges and charge neutrality point.

IV
Conclusions

In summary, compact Wannier orbitals in moiré bands can lead to strong superlattice el-el umklapp scattering.
As argued above, the scattering rate grows as
\propto 1/\xi^{2}
upon the Wannier orbital radius
\xi
decreasing.
The enhanced umklapp scattering manifests itself through a large
T^{2}
resistivity and an anomalously large KW ratio.
The essential requirement for this mechanism is a compact spatial structure of Wannier orbitals, however, spatial localization is not required.
Therefore the predicted enhancement of el-el umklapp scattering, the large
T^{2}
resistivity and the anomalous KW ratios are expected to occur for both non-topological bands and the bands that host topologically nontrivial electronic states such as those described in Ref.
Po2018
;
Song2021
.
Narrow bands in TBG is a system where recent electronic state calculations predict compact Wannier orbitals
Laissardiere2010
;
Kang2018
;
Koshino2018
;
Carr2019
.
At the same time, transport measurements in TBG report strong temperature-dependent resistivity,
T
-linear in the strange metal phase and
T^{2}
in the FL phase
Jaoui2021
, pointing to a key role of the el-el interactions enhanced by compact Wannier orbitals.

A.1
The el-el interaction Hamiltonian in the Wannier orbital representation

We consider an interaction term,

\displaystyle H_{ee}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int d^{2}r^{\prime}d^{2}r\,\psi_{\sigma}^{\dagger}({\boldsymbol{r}})\psi_{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{r}})U({\boldsymbol{r}}-{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime})\psi_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}({\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime})\psi_{\sigma^{\prime}}({\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}).

Here,
U({\boldsymbol{r}})
is the electron-electron interaction and
\psi_{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{r}})
[
\psi_{\sigma}^{\dagger}({\boldsymbol{r}})
] are the annihilation (creation) operators of an electron with spin
\sigma
at position
{\boldsymbol{r}}
. To project the interaction to the low-energy states, we approximate the electron operators by

\displaystyle\psi_{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{r}})\sim\sum_{i,a}w({\boldsymbol{r}}-{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}^{a})c_{a\sigma,i},

where
w({\boldsymbol{r}})
is a Wannier function (orbital) centered at
{\boldsymbol{r}}={\boldsymbol{0}}
and
c_{a\sigma,i}
is the annihilation operator for the state
w({\boldsymbol{r}})
centered at
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}^{a}\equiv{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}+{\boldsymbol{r}}^{a}
. Here,
i
and
a
are respectively the indices for the unit cell and the orbital;
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}
is the position of
i
th unit cell and
{\boldsymbol{r}}^{a}
is the relative position of
a
th orbital within a unit cell.
After a Fourier tranform,
c_{a\sigma,i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}e^{{\rm i}{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i}}c_{a\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}}
, where
c_{a\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}}
is the annihilation operator for electron with momentum
{\boldsymbol{k}}
, orbital
a
, and spin
\sigma
, the interaction Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H_{ee}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},a_{j},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}\end{subarray}}\int\frac{dq^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}g^{a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1},a_{2}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2}}_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3},a_{4}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4}}({\boldsymbol{q}})\,c_{a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}}^{\dagger}c_{a_{2}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2}}c_{a_{3}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}}^{\dagger}c_{a_{4}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4}},

\displaystyle g^{a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1},a_{2}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2}}_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3},a_{4}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4}}({\boldsymbol{q}})=\sum_{i_{j}}\frac{U_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}}{N^{2}}\int d^{2}r^{\prime}d^{2}r\,e^{-{\rm i}{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot({\boldsymbol{r}}-{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime})+{\rm i}{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}\cdot{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{1}}-{\rm i}{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2}\cdot{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{2}}+{\rm i}{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}\cdot{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{3}}-{\rm i}{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4}\cdot{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{4}}}

\displaystyle\times w^{\ast}({\boldsymbol{r}}-{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}})w({\boldsymbol{r}}-{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{2}}^{a_{2}})w^{\ast}({\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}-{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{3}}^{a_{3}})w({\boldsymbol{r}}^{\prime}-{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{4}}^{a_{4}}).

The interaction matrix
g^{a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1},a_{2}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2}}_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3},a_{4}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4}}({\boldsymbol{q}})
consists of two parts: diagonal terms
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}={\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{2}}^{a_{2}}
and
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{3}}^{a_{3}}={\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{4}}^{a_{4}}
, and off-diagonal terms
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}\neq{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{2}}^{a_{2}}
or
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{3}}^{a_{3}}\neq{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{4}}^{a_{4}}
.
The diagonal terms corresponds to extended Hubbard interactions
c_{a\sigma i}^{\dagger}c_{a\sigma i}c_{a^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}j}^{\dagger}c_{a^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}j}
, namely a density-density coupling.
On the other hand, the off-diagonal terms are small in the case of a small Wannier orbital radius
\xi\ll|{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}-{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{2}}^{a_{2}}|
or
\xi\ll|{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{3}}^{a_{3}}-{\boldsymbol{R}}_{i_{4}}^{a_{4}}|
because of small overlaps of Wannier orbitals with different centers.

A.2
Hubbard-like terms

As we are interested in the small Wannier orbital radius limit, we only consider the Hubbard-like interactions,
i_{1}=i_{2}
,
a_{1}=a_{2}
and
i_{3}=i_{4}
,
a_{3}=a_{4}
.
The terms omitted in this approximation involve overlaps of different Wannier orbitals.
Hence, in the limit of compact Wannier orbitals
\xi\ll a
, these terms are expected to be much smaller than the Hubbard-like terms.
Our numerical estimates show that they are indeed small and therefore safe to ignore.

Within the Hubbard-like approximation,
g_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}(a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1},a_{2}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2};a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3},a_{4}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4})
reads

\displaystyle g^{a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1},a_{2}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2}}_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3},a_{4}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4}}({\boldsymbol{q}})=U_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}|\eta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}|^{2}\delta_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{k}}_{2}-{\boldsymbol{q}}},{\boldsymbol{0}}}\delta_{\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}-{\boldsymbol{k}}_{4}+{\boldsymbol{q}}},{\boldsymbol{0}}},

where the form factor
\eta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}=\int d^{2}r\,|w({\boldsymbol{r}})|^{2}e^{{\rm i}{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{r}}}
, contains the information of Wannier function.
As in the main text, the overline
\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}+{\boldsymbol{q}}}
denotes wavevectors shifted by a reciprocal lattice vector into the first Brillouin zone.
This notation is used to relate the extended zone scheme and the folded zone scheme used in our analysis.
Using this interaction term, the interaction part
H_{ee}
reads

\displaystyle H_{ee}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},a_{j},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}\in 1BZ\end{subarray}}\int\frac{dq^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}U_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}|\eta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}|^{2}\,c_{a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}}^{\dagger}c_{a_{1}\sigma,\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{q}}}}c_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}}^{\dagger}c_{a_{4}\sigma^{\prime},\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}+{\boldsymbol{q}}}},

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},a_{j},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j}\in 1BZ\end{subarray}}\int_{1BZ}\frac{dq^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}\,c_{a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}}^{\dagger}c_{a_{1}\sigma,\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{q}}}}c_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}}^{\dagger}c_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}+{\boldsymbol{q}}}},

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{G}}}U_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}|\eta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}+{\boldsymbol{G}}}|^{2}.

Here, the sum over
k_{j}
is over momenta in the first Brillouin zone and the sum over
{\boldsymbol{G}}
is over all reciprocal vectors.
The result shows that the Wannier function form factor renormalizes the Hubbard interaction as
U_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}\to\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
.

The discrete version of
H_{ee}
reads

\displaystyle H_{ee}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},a_{j},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{j},{\boldsymbol{q}}\in 1BZ\end{subarray}}\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}\,c_{a_{1}\sigma,{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}}^{\dagger}c_{a_{1}\sigma,\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{q}}}}c_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}}^{\dagger}c_{a_{3}\sigma^{\prime},\overline{{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}+{\boldsymbol{q}}}}.

A.3
Gaussian Wannier functions and Yukawa interaction

As an illustration, we consider Gaussian Wannier orbitals and a Yukawa interaction model.
The Gaussian model is a simple example of compact Wannier orbital and Yukawa potential is the effective interaction between electrons after taking account of Thomas-Fermi screening.
The corresponding direct-space and momentum-space expressions read

\displaystyle w({\boldsymbol{r}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\xi}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2\xi^{2}}},\qquad\eta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}=e^{-(\xi^{2}q^{2}/4)},

\displaystyle U({\boldsymbol{r}})=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon r}e^{-q_{TF}r},\qquad U_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}=\frac{e^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_{TF}^{2}+q^{2}}}.

Using these quantities, the interaction matrix
\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
reads

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{G}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_{TF}^{2}+|{\boldsymbol{q}}+{\boldsymbol{G}}|^{2}}}e^{-\xi^{2}|{\boldsymbol{q}}+{\boldsymbol{G}}|^{2}/2}e^{{\rm i}({\boldsymbol{q}}+{\boldsymbol{G}})\cdot({\boldsymbol{r}}_{a_{3}}-{\boldsymbol{r}}_{a_{1}})}.

This is the interaction matrix considered in the main text, Eq.
9
.

For a small
q

\Gamma

\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}
is dominated by
G=0

\tilde{U}_{{\boldsymbol{q}}}\propto 1/\sqrt{q_{TF}^{2}+q^{2}}
, and depends strongly on

.
On the other hand,

near the Brillouin zone edge is almost independent of
{\boldsymbol{q}}
, as shown in Fig.

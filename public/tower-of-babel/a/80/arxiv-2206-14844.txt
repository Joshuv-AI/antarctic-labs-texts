Minimal Kullback-Leibler Divergence for Constrained Lévy-Itô Processes
Sebastian Jaimungal, Silvana M. Pesenti, Leandro Sánchez-Betancourt

Abstract
Given an n n -dimensional stochastic process 𝑿 \bm{X} driven by ℙ \mathbb{P} -Brownian motions and Poisson random measures, we seek the probability measure ℚ \mathbb{Q} , with minimal relative entropy to ℙ \mathbb{P} , such that the ℚ \mathbb{Q} -expectations of some terminal and running costs are constrained. We prove existence and uniqueness of the optimal probability measure, derive the explicit form of the measure change, and characterise the optimal drift and compensator adjustments under the optimal measure. We provide an analytical solution for Value-at-Risk (quantile) constraints, discuss how to perturb a Brownian motion to have arbitrary variance, and show that pinned measures arise as a limiting case of optimal measures. The results are illustrated in a risk management setting – including an algorithm to simulate under the optimal measure – and explore an example where an agent seeks to answer the question: what dynamics are induced by a perturbation of the Value-at-Risk and the average time spent below a barrier on the reference process?

Introduction

We consider stochastic processes that follow Lévy-Itô dynamics under a reference probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}
over a finite time horizon. The reference measure may arise in a data driven way and / or from modelling assumptions, however, it does not precisely capture all probabilistic beliefs of a modeller. In this work, misspecification under
{\mathbb{P}}
are characterised via expected values of functions of the stochastic process at terminal time and expected running costs of the processes over the entire time horizon. To mitigate model error, we seek over all absolutely continuous probability measures, under which the process satisfies these constraints, the one which is closest to the reference measure
{\mathbb{P}}
in relative entropy, also called Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. Thus, the key contribution of this work is solving the following constrained optimisation problem: Find the probability measure(s) that has minimal KL-divergence subject to constraints that can be written as (i) expected values of functions applied to the stochastic process at terminal time, and (ii) expected running costs of the processes over the entire time horizon.

We proceed to solve the optimisation problem by first considering a related optimisation problem where we seek over a subset of probability measures. Specifically, the subset consists of equivalent probability measures that arise from Doléans-Dade exponentials and we study this related problem using stochastic control techniques for Lévy-Itô processes. That is, we solve the dynamic programming equations and characterise a candidate solution (Proposition
2.5
), prove that the candidate solution is indeed the value function associated with the optimisation problem and that the resulting controls (which induce the optimal measure change) are admissible (Theorems
2.6
and
2.9
).
Furthermore, we show that the optimal measure change can be written as the exponential of a collection of random variables corresponding to the constraints (Corollary
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
). Finally, we prove that if a solution to the sub-problem (seeking over the subset of equivalent measures) exits, then it is unique and, moreover, it is the unique solution to the original optimisation problem, where we seek over all absolutely continuous probability measures (Theorem
2.11
).

We illustrate the dynamics of the stochastic process under the optimal measure using multiple examples. For example, for the case of two Value-at-Risk (also known as quantile) constraints, we provide an analytical expression for the optimal measure change. We further show how to optimally change the dynamics of a Brownian motion to have zero mean and arbitrary variance. We also discuss the connection of the solution to our constrained optimisation problem to pinned measures; probability measures where the terminal value of the process lies almost surely within a Borel measurable set. While such measures are not equivalent with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
and thus do not fall into the set of admissible measures of our related problem, we derive them as a limiting case of solutions to our optimisation problem. We further consider infinitesimal perturbations; that is, we solve the problem where the constraints are equal to their
{\mathbb{P}}
-expectation plus
\varepsilon
multiplied with a direction
\bm{\delta}
. In this setup, we prove that the Lagrange multiplier is, up to order
\varepsilon
, the inverse of the
{\mathbb{P}}
-covariance matrix of the constraint functions multiplied by
\varepsilon
and the direction of the perturbation. Using this result, we define a derivative – termed
entropic derivative
– of a risk functional along constraints in direction of least relative entropy. As examples we show the connection of the entropic derivative to differential sensitivities of risk functionals such as the Tail-Value-at-Risk and distortion risk measures. Finally, we provide an algorithm for solving and simulating from the optimal probability measure and illustrate the numerical results on a running cost constraint in a financial setting on real data.

The KL-divergence has many applications in financial mathematics. Starting with the influential work of
[
21

[
8

In this exposition, we consider a different problem in that we do not restrict to martingale measures but solve for the optimal dynamics of the process such that given constraints are fulfilled. In particular, in contrast to our setup, all of the above mentioned literature work with risk-neutral measures (i.e., martingale measures). Moreover, the running cost constraint considered in this work is novel. A natural interpretation of the running costs constraint in mathematical finance is that of the average time spent below a barrier which we consider in the numerical example section.

, as we send

, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{t,\bm{x}}^{{\mathbb{Q}_{{\bm{\lambda}},\bm{h}}}}\Big[{\bm{\eta}_{1}}\cdot\left(\bm{f}(\bm{X}_{T})-\bm{c}\right)-{\bm{\eta}_{2}}\cdot\bm{d}\Big]\geq

\displaystyle\qquad J^{\dagger}(t,\bm{x})-{\mathbb{E}}_{t,\bm{x}}^{{\mathbb{Q}_{{\bm{\lambda}},\bm{h}}}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bm{\lambda}}_{u}|^{2}+{\bm{\eta}_{2}}\cdot{\bm{g}}(\bm{X}_{u})\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-{\mathbb{E}}_{t,\bm{x}}^{{\mathbb{Q}_{{\bm{\lambda}},\bm{h}}}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{l}}\big[{\text{{log}}}(\bm{1}-{\bm{h}}_{u}(\bm{z}))\odot(\bm{1}-{\bm{h}}_{u}(\bm{z}))+{\bm{h}}_{u}(\bm{z})\big]\,\bm{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\bm{z})\,\mathrm{d}u\right]\,.

After rearranging the above equation we have that

, and as a consequence of the arbitrariness of
{\bm{\lambda}}
and
{\bm{h}}
, we obtain

.

Finally, using a similar localisation technique as above, this time with
({\bm{\lambda}}^{\dagger},{\bm{h}}^{\dagger})
and corresponding measure
{{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\bm{\lambda}}^{\dagger},{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}}}\in{\bm{\mathcal{Q}}}
, and since it holds by construction that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}J^{\dagger}(t,\bm{x})+{\mathcal{L}}^{{\bm{\lambda}}^{\dagger},{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}}J^{\dagger}(t,\bm{x})+\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{\dagger}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{l}}\big[{\text{{log}}}(\bm{1}-{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}_{t}(\bm{z}))\odot(\bm{1}-{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}_{t}(\bm{z}))+{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}_{t}(\bm{z})\big]\,\bm{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\bm{z})+{\bm{\eta}_{2}}\cdot{\bm{g}}(\bm{x})=0\,,

we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{t,\bm{x}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\bm{\lambda}}^{\dagger},{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}}}\big[{\bm{\eta}_{1}}\cdot\left(\bm{f}(\bm{X}_{T})-\bm{c}\right)-{\bm{\eta}_{2}}\cdot\bm{d}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad=J^{\dagger}(t,\bm{x})-{\mathbb{E}}_{t,\bm{x}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\bm{\lambda}}^{\dagger},{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\tfrac{1}{2}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{\dagger}_{u}|^{2}+{\bm{\eta}_{2}}\cdot{\bm{g}}(\bm{X}_{u})\mathrm{d}u\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-{\mathbb{E}}_{t,\bm{x}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\bm{\lambda}}^{\dagger},{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\int_{{\mathds{R}}^{l}}\big[{\text{{log}}}(\bm{1}-{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}_{u}(\bm{z}))\odot(\bm{1}-{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}_{u}(\bm{z}))+{\bm{h}}^{\dagger}_{u}(\bm{z})\big]\,\bm{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\bm{z})\,\mathrm{d}u\right]\,.

Therefore, after rearranging, we have that

. Combining both inequalities we obtain that

which concludes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\exp\bigg(

and the direction of the perturbation. Using the results on infinitesimal perturbations, we define a directional derivative – termed

– of risk functionals in the direction of least relative entropy in Section
4.2
.

4.1
Optimal Lagrange Multiplier

For functions
f_{j},g_{i}:{\mathds{R}}^{n}\to{\mathds{R}}
with
j\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}
and
i\in{\mathcal{R}}_{2}
, we define the random vector

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}=({\mathfrak{f}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{f}}_{r_{1}+r_{2}})\,,\qquad\text{where}\qquad{\mathfrak{f}}_{j}&:=f_{j}(\bm{X}_{T})\,,\;\forall\,j\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\\
{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}&:=\int_{0}^{T}g_{i}(\bm{X}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\;\forall\,i\in{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\,.\end{split}

(28)

We assume throughout this section that
{\mathbb{C}}({\mathfrak{f}}_{i},{\mathfrak{f}}_{j})<\infty
, where
{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the
{\mathbb{P}}
-covariance, for all
i,j\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{R}}_{2}
. Using the above notation, we state the optimisation problem concerning infinitesimal perturbations.

Optimisation 4.1
.

For
\varepsilon\neq 0
,
\bm{\delta}:=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{r_{1}+r_{2}})\in{\mathds{R}}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
, and a random vector
{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}
given in (
28
), we consider the optimisation problem

\inf_{{\mathbb{Q}}\ll{\mathbb{P}}}\;D_{KL}({\mathbb{Q}}~||~{\mathbb{P}})\quad\text{subject to}\quad{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}}\left[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\left[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]+\varepsilon\,\bm{\delta}\,,

(
P_{\varepsilon}
)

where the constraints are understood as a system of equations.

If a solution to optimisation problem (

P
ε

) exists, we denote the probability measure attaining the infimum by
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
. This probability measure may be viewed as arising from small perturbations of
{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}
in direction of
\bm{\delta}
. The next result shows that the optimal Lagrange multiplier
{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}
is, up to
o(\varepsilon)
, equal to the inverse of the
{\mathbb{P}}
-covariance matrix of the constraint functions multiplied by
\varepsilon
and the direction of the perturbation
\bm{\delta}
. We first prove this result for a single constraint and then, using slightly stronger assumptions, for a collection of constraints.

Theorem 4.2
(Single Constraint).

Let
r_{1}+r_{2}=1
, and write
\mathfrak{X}={\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]-\varepsilon\delta
, i.e. we only consider one constraint, and assume that
{\mathfrak{f}}
satisfies
{\mathbb{C}}\big({\mathfrak{f}},\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]\right)^{2}\big)\in{\mathds{R}}/\{0\}
.
Under the Assumptions of Theorem
2.9
,
the optimisation problem (

P
ε

) has a unique solution
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
with Lagrange multiplier
{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\in{\mathds{R}}
satisfying

{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}=-\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\;\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)\,,

where
{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}}):={\mathbb{E}}\left[({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}])^{2}\right]
denotes the
{\mathbb{P}}
-variance of
{\mathfrak{f}}
. Moreover, the KL-divergence of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
is

D_{KL}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}~||~{\mathbb{P}})=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\,\delta^{2}\,\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\,.

By Theorem
2.9
, the solution to problem (

P
ε

) is

\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}}=\frac{\displaystyle\exp\left(-{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]-\varepsilon\delta\right)\right)}{\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]-\varepsilon\delta\right)\right)\right]}=\frac{\displaystyle\exp\left(-{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\,{\mathfrak{f}}\right)}{\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\,{\mathfrak{f}}\right)\right]}\,,

where the Lagrange multiplier
{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}
solves the equation
{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\right]={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]+\varepsilon\,\delta
.
Let
{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}=-\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\varepsilon+{r_{\varepsilon}}
, where
{r_{\varepsilon}}:=r(\varepsilon)
and
r\colon{\mathds{R}}\to{\mathds{R}}
is the error term satisfying
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{r_{\varepsilon}}=0
. Then for
{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}
to bind the constraint, we require that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\frac{\displaystyle\exp\left(-{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathfrak{f}}\right)}{\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathfrak{f}}\right)\right]}\;-1\right){\mathfrak{f}}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(h\left(\eta_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right)-1\right){\mathfrak{f}}\right]=\varepsilon\,\delta\,,

(29)

where we set the random variable
h(z):=e^{-z\,{\mathfrak{f}}}/{\mathbb{E}}[e^{-z\,{\mathfrak{f}}}]
. Next, we show that
{r_{\varepsilon}}
is an error term of order
o(\varepsilon)
. For this we first apply the Taylor theorem to calculate the Taylor approximation of
h
around
\varepsilon=0
, and obtain that for all
\omega\in\Omega

h({\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}})=1-\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]\right)\left(-\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\varepsilon+{r_{\varepsilon}}\right)+{R_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)\,,

where the random variable
{R_{\varepsilon}}
is the error term of the Taylor approximation of
h
and
\xi
(potentially depending on
\omega
) lies between
0
and
-\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\varepsilon+{r_{\varepsilon}}
. Inserting the expansion of
h
into (
29
) we obtain

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]\right)\,{\mathfrak{f}}\right]\left(\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\,\varepsilon-{r_{\varepsilon}}\right)+{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\,{R_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)\right]=\varepsilon\,\delta\,.

Noting that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]\right)\,{\mathfrak{f}}\right]={\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})
, the above becomes

{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})\,{r_{\varepsilon}}={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}{R_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)\right]\,.

(30)

Using the Lagrange form of the error term, we may write

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\,{R_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)\right]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\,h^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\right]\left(-\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\,\varepsilon+{r_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,M(\xi)\left(\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})^{2}}\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}-2\,\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\,\varepsilon\,{r_{\varepsilon}}+{r_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\right)\,,

where we set
M(\xi):={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\,h^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\right]
, where
h^{\prime\prime}
is the second derivative of
h(z)
with respect to
z
. Note that
M
is continuous so that
\lim_{a\to 0}M(a)=M(0)
. Moreover, calculations show that
M(0)={\mathbb{C}}\big({\mathfrak{f}},\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]\right)^{2}\big)
which by assumption implies that
M(0)\neq 0
and
|M(0)|<\infty
.

Therefore, Equation (
30
) admits the quadratic form

\displaystyle r_{\varepsilon}^{2}-2{r_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\,\delta\varepsilon+\frac{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}{M(\xi)}\right)+\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})^{2}}\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}=0\,,

which has solutions

\displaystyle{r_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\,\delta\,\varepsilon+\frac{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}{M(\xi)}\pm\frac{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}{M(\xi)}\sqrt{1+2\,\frac{M(\xi)}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})^{2}}\,\delta\,\varepsilon\;}\,.

Note that the positive root,
r_{\varepsilon}^{+}
, is not a viable solution since is satisfies

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}r_{\varepsilon}^{+}=2\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}{M(\xi)}\neq 0\,,

which contradicts that
{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}
must converge to 0 as
\varepsilon\to 0
. For the negative root,
r_{\varepsilon}^{-}
, we apply Taylor’s theorem for
\ell(z)=\sqrt{1+z}=1+\tfrac{1}{2}z+o(z)
, and obtain, recall that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}|M(\xi)|=\lim_{a\to 0}|M(a)|<\infty
,

r_{\varepsilon}^{-}=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\,\delta\varepsilon+\frac{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}{M(\xi)}-\frac{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}{M(\xi)}\left(1+\frac{M(\xi)}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})^{2}}\delta\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon M(\xi))\right)=o(\varepsilon)\,.

Thus, indeed
{\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon}}=-\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)
.

Next, we calculate the RN-density. For this we first use Taylor’s theorems for
e^{z}=1+z+o(z)
, and then, in the third equation, Taylor’s theorem for
\frac{1}{1-z}=1+z+o(z)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left(\,\varepsilon\,\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\,{\mathfrak{f}}+o(\varepsilon)\right)}{{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\,\varepsilon\,\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta\,{\mathfrak{f}}+o(\varepsilon)\right)\right]}=\frac{1+\varepsilon\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta{\mathfrak{f}}+o(\varepsilon)}{{\mathbb{E}}\left[1+\varepsilon\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta{\mathfrak{f}}+o(\varepsilon)\right]}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\varepsilon\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta{\mathfrak{f}}+o(\varepsilon)\right)\,\left(1-\varepsilon\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\right]+o(\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\varepsilon\,\delta\,\frac{\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\right]\right)}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}+o(\varepsilon)\,.

Finally, we calculate the KL-divergence, using Taylor’s theorem for
\log(1-z)=-z+o(z)
,

\displaystyle D_{KL}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}~||~{\mathbb{P}})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(1+\varepsilon\delta\,\frac{\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\right]\right)}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}+o(\varepsilon)\right)\left(\varepsilon\delta\,\frac{\left({\mathfrak{f}}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}\right]\right)}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}+o(\varepsilon)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{V}}({\mathfrak{f}})}\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\,.

∎

We prove the result for multiple constraints using a different proof which requires slightly stronger assumptions.

Theorem 4.3
(Multiple Constraints).

Let the Assumptions of Theorem
2.9
be fulfilled. Further, assume that the Lagrange multiplier
{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}=(\eta_{\varepsilon,1}^{*},\ldots,\eta_{\varepsilon,r_{1}+r_{2}}^{*})\in{\mathds{R}}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
corresponding to optimisation problem (

P
ε

) is component-wise differentiable in
\varepsilon
and that
{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}{\mathfrak{f}}_{j}{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}{\mathfrak{f}}_{l}]<\infty
for all
i,j,k,l\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{R}}_{2}
. Then, optimisation problem (

P
ε

) has a unique solution
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
with Lagrange multiplier
{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}
satisfying

{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}=-\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\;\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)\,,

where the matrix
\bm{C}
has components
\bm{C}_{ji}:={\mathbb{C}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{j},{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}]
,
i,j\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{R}}_{2}
. Furthermore, the KL-divergence of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
is

D_{KL}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}~||~{\mathbb{P}})=\bm{\delta}^{\intercal}\,\bm{C}^{-1}\,\bm{\delta}\,\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\,.

By Theorem
2.9
, the solution to problem (

P
ε

) is

\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}}=\frac{\displaystyle\exp\left(-{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)}{\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)\right]}\,,

where the Lagrange multipliers
{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}
solve the system of equations
{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}\left[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]={\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}]+\varepsilon\,\bm{\delta}
. The constraints impose that for all
k\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{R}}_{2}

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\frac{\displaystyle\exp\left(-{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)}{\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)\right]}\;-1\right){\mathfrak{f}}_{k}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(h\left(\bm{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{*}\right)-1\right){\mathfrak{f}}_{k}\right]=\varepsilon\,\delta_{k}\,,

(31)

where the random variable
h({\bm{z}}):=e^{-{\bm{z}}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}}/{\mathbb{E}}[e^{-{\bm{z}}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}}]
for
{\bm{z}}\in{\mathds{R}}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
. Let
{\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}=-\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\varepsilon+{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}
, where
{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}:=\bm{r}(\varepsilon)
,
\bm{r}\colon{\mathds{R}}\to{\mathds{R}}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
, is the error term satisfying component-wise
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}=\bm{0}
. Next, we show that
{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}
is of order
o(\varepsilon)
. For this we first apply Taylor’s theorem to calculate the Taylor approximation of
h
. Indeed, for all
\omega\in\Omega

h({\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}})=1-\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}]\right)^{\intercal}\left(-\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\varepsilon+{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}\right)+R({\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}})\,,

(32)

where the random variable
R
is the error term of the Taylor approximation of
h
, i.e. in integral form

R(\bm{x})=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\,x_{i}\,x_{j}\,\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\,h_{i,j}(t\,\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
h_{i,j}(\bm{x}):=\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}h(\bm{x})
.
Inserting (
32
), the expansion of
h
, into (
31
) we obtain for all
k\in{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{R}}_{2}

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}]\right)^{\intercal}{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}\right]\left(\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\varepsilon-{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}\right)+{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}R({\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}})\right]=\varepsilon\delta_{k}\,.

(33)

Note that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}]\right)^{\intercal}{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}\right]=\bm{C}_{k\,\cdot}
, where
\bm{C}_{k\,\cdot}
is the
k
-th row of
\bm{C}
, and
\bm{C}_{k\cdot}\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}=\delta_{k}
. Thus, Equation (
33
) becomes

\displaystyle\bm{C}_{k\,\cdot}\,{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}R({\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}})\right].

(34)

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}R({\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}})=\sum_{l=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\partial_{x_{l}}R(\bm{x})|_{\bm{x}={\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}}\;\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\eta^{*}_{\varepsilon,l}=\sum_{l=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}Q_{l}({\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}})\left(-(\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta})_{l}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}r_{\varepsilon,l}\right),

where

\displaystyle Q_{l}(\bm{x}):=

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}x_{i}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)h_{i,l}(t\,\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}t\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}x_{i}x_{j}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)h_{i,j,l}(t\,\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}t\right\},

and
h_{i,j,l}(\bm{x}):=\partial_{x_{i},x_{j},x_{l}}h(\bm{x})
.
Next, define the matrix
\bm{B}_{\varepsilon}
whose entries are
\bm{B}_{\varepsilon,i,j}={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}Q_{j}({\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}})]
. Taking derivative of (
34
) with respect to
\varepsilon
, then stacking the equations and isolating for
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}
, we have

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}=-(\bm{C}-\bm{B}_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\bm{B}_{\varepsilon}\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}.

Further, after some tedious computations, and defining
\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}:={\mathfrak{f}}_{i}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}h_{i,j,k}(\varepsilon\,\bm{x})=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}\left({\mathfrak{f}}_{j}+{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{j}\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}]]\right)\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{j}\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{j}\right]\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}

\displaystyle+({\mathfrak{f}}_{i}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}])\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{j}+\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}({\mathfrak{f}}_{j}+{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}_{j}\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}])-\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{i}\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{j}\Delta{\mathfrak{f}}_{k}.

Hence, under the assumption of bounded fourth moments,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\bm{B}_{\varepsilon}=0
and applying L’Hôpital’s rule, we obtain (component-wise)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\frac{{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}}{\varepsilon}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\;\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\;{\bm{r}_{\varepsilon}}=\bm{0}\,,

which implies that
\bm{\eta}^{*}_{\varepsilon}=-\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\varepsilon\,+o(\varepsilon)
.

To calculate the KL-divergence, we first calculate the RN-density, using similar steps to the proof of Theorem
4.2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}+o(\varepsilon)}{{\mathbb{E}}\left[1+\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]+o(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}+o(\varepsilon)\right)\left(1-\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]+o(\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]\right)+o(\varepsilon)\,.

Thus, the KL-divergence becomes

\displaystyle D_{KL}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}~||~{\mathbb{P}})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(1+\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]\right)+o(\varepsilon)\right)\left(\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]\right)+o(\varepsilon)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\bm{\delta}^{\intercal}\bm{C}^{-1}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}\right]\right)^{2}\right]\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}+o\left(\varepsilon^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\bm{\delta}^{\intercal}\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

4.2
Entropic Derivative

In this section, we define a derivative of a risk functional along constraints in the direction of least relative entropy. For this we use the same notation as in Section
4.1
and denote by
{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}
a vector of constraints given in (
28
) and by
\bm{\delta}
the direction of the derivative.

Definition 4.4

(Entropic Derivative)
.

Let
{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}
be a random vector with representation as in (
28
) and
\bm{\delta}\in{\mathds{R}}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
. Then the entropic derivative of an
{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
-measureable random variable
\ell
in direction
\bm{\delta}
along the constraint
{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}
at time
{t\in[0,T]}
is

{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[\ell]=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left({\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\varepsilon}}[\ell\;|\;{\mathcal{F}}_{t}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\ell\;|\;{\mathcal{F}}_{t}]\right)\,,

where for all
\varepsilon
,
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
is the solution to optimisation problem (

P
ε

).

The entropic derivative is 1-homogeneous, in that
{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[m\ell]=m{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[\ell]
for all
m\in{\mathds{R}}
, and additive, i.e.
{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}]={{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[\ell_{1}]+{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[\ell_{2}]
, for all
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}

{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
-measureable random variables. Furthermore, the entropic derivative satisfies
{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[\ell]=0
for all
{t\in[0,T]}
, if
\ell
and
{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}
are independent; a result following from the next proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

The entropic derivative of
\ell
in direction of
\bm{\delta}
along the constraint
{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}
at time
{t\in[0,T]}
has representation

{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[\ell]=\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot\;{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}]\right)\,]\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

At time
t=0
, the derivative may be written as

{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{0}}[\ell]=\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}[\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}]\right)\;\left(\ell-{\mathbb{E}}[\ell]\right)]\,.

Using Theorem
4.3
and in particular the approximation of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
, we have for
{t\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[\ell]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left({\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}[\ell\;|\;{\mathcal{F}}_{t}]-{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\ell]\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big\{{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\left[\left(1+\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}])\right)\ell\right]-{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\ell]+o(\varepsilon)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left\{\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}])\;\ell]+O(\varepsilon)\right\}

\displaystyle=\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot\;{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\left({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}]\right)\;\ell]\,.

The representation for
t=0
follows by noting that
{\mathbb{E}}[({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}]){\mathbb{E}}[\ell]\,]=0
.
∎Next, we provide an example of an entropic risk measure and relate it to the sensitivity of the Tail-Value-at-Risk (TVaR) to a sub-portfolio. Recall that for a random variable
Y
and a probability measures
{\mathbb{Q}}
, the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-TVaR at level
\beta\in[0,1)
is defined as

{\text{TVaR}}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{\beta}(Y)=\frac{1}{1-\beta}\int_{\beta}^{1}F_{Y}^{{\mathbb{Q}},-1}(u)\mathrm{d}u\,,

where the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-quantile function of
Y
is given by
F_{Y}^{{\mathbb{Q}},-1}(u):={\text{VaR}}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{u}(Y)
. For simplicity of notation, we write
{\text{TVaR}}_{\beta}(Y):={\text{TVaR}}^{\mathbb{P}}_{\beta}(Y)

Example

(TVaR Sensitivity)
.

Suppose we have the process
(X_{1,t},X_{2,t})_{{t\in[0,T]}}
and consider
{\mathfrak{f}}={\mathds{1}}_{\{X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}<q\}}
, where
q={\text{VaR}}_{\alpha}(X_{1,T}+X_{2,T})
, and
\delta\in{\mathds{R}}
. For simplicitly assume that the quantile function of
X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}
is continuous around
\alpha
, then the constraint corresponds to a small perturbation constraint of

{\mathbb{Q}}\left(X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}<q\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}<q\right)+\varepsilon\delta=\alpha+\varepsilon\delta\,.

Moreover, the entropic derivative of
X_{1,T}
in direction
\delta
along
{\mathfrak{f}}
at time
{t\in[0,T]}
is

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{t}}[X_{1,T}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{{\mathbb{V}}\left({\mathds{1}}_{\{X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}<q\}}\right)}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\left[\left({\mathds{1}}_{\{X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}<q\}}-\alpha\right)X_{1,T}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,\left((1-\alpha){\mathbb{E}}_{t}[X_{1,T}]-{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[{\mathds{1}}_{\{X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}\geq q\}}X_{1,T}]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{E}}_{t}[X_{1,T}]-\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{1-\alpha}\;{\mathbb{E}}[X_{1,T}\,|\,X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}\geq q,\,{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\,]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\,\left({\mathbb{E}}_{t}[X_{1,T}]-\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{1-\alpha}\;\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}{\text{TVaR}}_{\alpha}(X_{1,T}(1+\varepsilon)+X_{2,T}~|~{\mathcal{F}}_{t})\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\right)\,,

where
\alpha_{t}:={\mathbb{P}}(X_{1,T}+X_{2,T}<q~|~{\mathcal{F}}_{t})
.
In particular, if
{\mathbb{E}}[X_{1,T}]=0
and for
\delta=\alpha
, we obtain

{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{0}}[X_{1,T}]=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}{\text{TVaR}}_{\alpha}\left(X_{1,T}(1+\varepsilon)+X_{2,T}\right)\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\,.

We note that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}{\text{TVaR}}_{\alpha}\left(X_{1,T}(1+\varepsilon)+X_{2,T}\right)\big|_{\varepsilon=0}
is the sensitivity of TVaR in direction of sub-portfolio
X_{1,T}
; see e.g.
[
12
]
.

Next, we generalise the entropic derivative to the class of distortion risk measures, which subsumes TVaR. First introduced by
[
24
]
, distortion risk measures include a wide range of risk measures used in financial risk management and behavioural economics.

Definition 4.6

(Distortion Risk Measures)
.

For a function
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
with
\int_{0}^{1}\gamma(u)\,du=1
, the distortion risk measure of a random variable
Y
with weight function
\gamma
under a probability measure
{\mathbb{Q}}
is given by

\rho_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{Q}}(Y):=\int_{0}^{1}F_{Y}^{{\mathbb{Q}},-1}(u)\;\gamma(u)\,du\,.

We set
\rho_{\gamma}(Y):=\rho_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{P}}(Y)
.
A distortion risk measure
\rho_{\gamma}
satisfies the properties of coherence if the distortion weight function
\gamma
is non-decreasing
[
15
]
.

Proposition 4.7
(Distortion Risk Measures).

Let
\rho_{\gamma}
be a distortion risk measure under
{\mathbb{P}}
and
\ell
a absolutely continuous
{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
-measurable random variable with support
B
. Then the entropic derivative of the distortion risk measure of
\ell
in direction
\bm{\delta}
along the constraint
{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}
at time
t=0
is

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{0}}[\ell;\gamma]

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\rho_{\gamma}^{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}(\ell)-\rho_{\gamma}(\ell)\right)

\displaystyle=-\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot\;\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}])\;{\mathds{1}}_{\{\ell\leq y\}}\right]\;\gamma\left(F_{\ell}(y)\right)\;\mathrm{d}y\,,

where
F_{\ell}(y):={\mathbb{P}}(\ell\leq y)
.

Note that the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-distortion risk measures can be written as an expectation under the reference probability

\rho_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\ell)={\mathbb{E}}\,\left[F_{\ell}^{{\mathbb{Q}},-1}(U)\;\gamma(U)\right]\,,

where
U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbb{P}}}}{{\sim}}U(0,1)
is a standard uniform random variable under
{\mathbb{P}}
. Using this representation, the derivative becomes

{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{0}}\;\left[\ell;\gamma\right]=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\rho_{\gamma}^{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}(\ell)-\rho_{\gamma}(\ell)\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\;{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(F_{\ell}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon},-1}(U)-F_{\ell}^{-1}(U)\right)\gamma(U)\right]\,.

Next, we calculate the derivative of the
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
-quantile function of
\ell
with respect to
\varepsilon
. For this, note that for all
u\in(0,1)
, differentiating the equation
F_{\ell}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}\left(F_{\ell}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon},-1}(u)\right)=u
gives

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}F_{\ell}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon},-1}(u)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}=-\left.\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}F_{\ell}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}(x)}{f_{\ell}(x)}\right|_{\varepsilon=0,\;x=F_{\ell}^{-1}(u)}\,,

(35)

where
f_{\ell}(x)
is the
{\mathbb{P}}
-density of
\ell
. To simplify (
35
), we calculate the
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
-distribution function of
\ell
using the approximation of
\frac{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}}
in Theorem
4.3

\displaystyle F_{\ell}^{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\varepsilon}}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathds{1}}_{\{\ell\leq x\}}\;\left(1+\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}])\right)\right]+o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=F_{\ell}(x)+\varepsilon\,\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}])\;{\mathds{1}}_{\{\ell\leq x\}}\right]+o(\varepsilon)\,.

and thus (
35
) becomes

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}F_{\ell}^{{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\varepsilon},-1}(u)\right|_{\varepsilon=0}=-\left.\frac{\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}])\;{\mathds{1}}_{\{\ell\leq x\}}\right]}{f_{\ell}(x)}\right|_{x=F_{\ell}^{-1}(u)}\,.

Collecting, and using a change of variable
y=F_{\ell}^{-1}(u)
in the second equation, we have

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{0}}\;\left[\ell;\gamma\right]

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}])\;{\mathds{1}}_{\{\ell\leq F_{\ell}^{-1}(u)\}}\right]}{f_{\ell}\left(F_{\ell}^{-1}(u)\right)}\;\gamma(u)\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=-\bm{C}^{-1}\bm{\delta}\cdot\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}-{\mathbb{E}}[{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}}])\;{\mathds{1}}_{\{\ell\leq y\}}\right]\;\gamma\left(F_{\ell}(y)\right)\;\mathrm{d}y\,.

∎

Example
.

(Sensitivity for Distortion Risk Measures)
Consider the one-dimensional process
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and a constraint
{\mathfrak{f}}
satisfying
{\mathfrak{f}}>0

{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. and
{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]=1
. Thus, we can write
\tilde{F}_{X_{T}}(x):={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathfrak{f}}\,{\mathds{1}}_{\{X_{T}\leq x\}}]
which is a distorted distribution function of
X_{T}
.

Then the entropic derivative of a distortion risk measure of
X_{T}
in direction
\delta
along the constraint
{\mathfrak{f}}
at time
t=0
is

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{D}}^{{\bm{\mathfrak{f}}},\bm{\delta}}_{0}}\;\left[X_{T};\gamma\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{-\delta}{{\mathbb{V}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\mathfrak{f}}-1)\;{\mathds{1}}_{\{X\leq F_{X_{T}}^{-1}(u)\}}\right]}{f_{X_{T}}\left(F_{X_{T}}^{-1}(u)\right)}\;\gamma(u)\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{{\mathbb{V}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{u-\tilde{F}_{X_{T}}\left(F_{X_{T}}^{-1}(u)\right)}{f_{X_{T}}\left(F_{X_{T}}^{-1}(u)\right)}\;\gamma(u)\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{{\mathbb{V}}[{\mathfrak{f}}]}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{F_{X_{T}}\left(X_{T}\right)-\tilde{F}_{X_{T}}\left(X_{T}\right)}{f_{X_{T}}\left(X_{T}\right)}\;\gamma(F_{X_{T}}\left(X_{T}\right))\;\right]

where the last equality follows from Proposition 4.2 in
[
20
]
and
where
X_{T,\varepsilon}:=F_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(U_{X_{T}})
and

is a
{\mathbb{P}}
-uniform comonontonic to
X_{T}
, i.e.
U_{X_{T}}:=F_{X_{T}}\left(X_{T}\right)
, and
F_{\varepsilon}(x):=(1-\varepsilon)F_{X_{T}}(x)+\varepsilon\tilde{F}_{X_{T}}(x)
,

. We note that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\rho_{\gamma}(X_{T,\varepsilon})\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}
is the sensitivity of a distortion risk measure to
X_{T}
for a perturbation with the mixture distribution
F_{\varepsilon}
to
X_{T}
, see Proposition 4.2 in
[
20
]
. We refer to
[

]
for a discussion on differential sensitivities to distortion risk measures.

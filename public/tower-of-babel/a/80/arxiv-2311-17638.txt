Resurgence of Refined Topological Strings and Dual Partition Functions
Sergey ALEXANDROV a, Marcos MARIÑO b and Boris PIOLINE c

Abstract
We study the resurgent structure of the refined topological string partition function on a non-compact Calabi–Yau threefold, at large orders in the string coupling constant g s g_{s} and fixed refinement parameter 𝖻 \mathsf{b} . For 𝖻 ≠ 1 \mathsf{b}\neq 1 , the Borel transform admits two families of simple poles, corresponding to integral periods rescaled by 𝖻 {\mathsf{b}} and 1 / 𝖻 1/\mathsf{b} . We show that the corresponding Stokes automorphism is expressed in terms of a generalization of the non-compact quantum dilogarithm, and we conjecture that the Stokes constants are determined by the refined Donaldson–Thomas invariants counting spin- j j BPS states. This jump in the refined topological string partition function is a special case (unit five-brane charge) of a more general transformation property of wave functions on quantum twisted tori introduced in earlier work by two of the authors. We show that this property follows from the transformation of a suitable refined dual partition function across BPS rays, defined by extending the Moyal star product to the realm of contact geometry.

Introduction and discussion

As a mathematically well-defined subsector of type II superstring theories, topological string theory
provides a prime arena for exploring the non-perturbative completion of the asymptotic series
predicted by the perturbative genus expansion in string theory. In cases where the target
Calabi–Yau threefold
X
admits a large
N
dual, a non-perturbative formulation is available,
which resums the perturbative series
to all orders in the topological string coupling
g_{s}=1/N
, for fixed values of the moduli
\boldsymbol{t}

(corresponding to ’t Hooft couplings in the dual gauge theory)
at least for integer values of
N
.
Non-perturbative corrections of order
{\rm e}^{-1/g_{s}}
typically arise from tunneling (or instanton)
effects in the dual matrix model.
In general however, one has only access to the genus-
g
free energy
{\cal F}_{g}(\boldsymbol{t})
occurring
at order
g_{s}^{2g-2}
in the perturbative expansion, and non-perturbative corrections are ambiguous,
without further assumptions.

Assuming that the putative,
non-perturbative topological string partition function
{\cal F}(\boldsymbol{t};g_{s})
belongs to a suitable
class of resurgent functions, one can however extract much information about non-perturbative corrections
from the growth of the coefficients
{\cal F}_{g}(\boldsymbol{t})
at large genus. This method was proposed early on in
[
92
]

for general string theory models, and first applied to the topological string in
[
76
,
77
,
80
,
91
]
.
In particular, the instanton action
{\cal A}

controlling the size of the leading instanton effects
{\rm e}^{-{\cal A}/g_{s}}
can be read off from the location
of the singularity closest to the origin in the Borel plane.

While the explicit computation of the free energies
{\cal F}_{g}(\boldsymbol{t})
is usually impractical at large genus,
they are strongly constrained by the holomorphic anomaly equations (HAE)
[
22
]
.
Following
[
33
,
34
]
, much progress has been made recently in constructing the trans-series
solution to the HAE
[
57
,
59
,
69
,
81

actions
{\cal A}
entering in the trans-series are equal (up to overall normalization) to the central
charge
Z(\gamma)
of vectors
\gamma
in the
charge lattice (the Grothendieck lattice
K_{0}(X)
in the
A
-model, or the
homology lattice
H_{3}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
in the B-model).
Moreover, the Stokes automorphism

\mathfrak{S}

controlling the jump in the Borel resummation
when a radial integration contour crosses a sequence of singularities was determined in
[
69
]
:
the jump induces the following transformation in the partition function
{{\cal Z}={\rm e}^{{\cal F}}}
,

{\cal Z}\ \mapsto\ \mathfrak{S}{\cal Z}=\exp\left[\frac{\omega(\gamma)}{2\pi{\rm i}}\big({\rm Li}_{2}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi x}\big)+2\pi x\log\big(1-{\rm e}^{2\pi x}\big)\big)\right]{\cal Z},

(1.1)

where
\omega(\gamma)
is (up to normalization) the so-called Stokes constant,

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{2}(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}z^{k}/k^{2}

is the dilogarithm function and
x
is suitable operator

(
equal to
\frac{{\rm i}g_{s}}{2\pi}c^{a}\partial_{t^{a}}

[
69

which we also restrict to here for simplicity; here

are the components of
\gamma
in

H_{4}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})

. As observed in
[

(
1.1

\tau(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{u};g_{s})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}{\rm e}^{2\pi\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}/g_{s}}{\cal Z}(\boldsymbol{t}-{\rm i}g_{s}\boldsymbol{n};g_{s})

(1.2)

transforms by a simple factor, up to a
\boldsymbol{u}
-dependent shift in
\boldsymbol{t}
,

\mathfrak{S}\tau(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{u};g_{s})={\rm e}^{\frac{\omega(\gamma)}{2\pi{\rm i}}{\rm Li}_{2}({\rm e}^{2\pi{\boldsymbol{c}}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}/g_{s}})}\tau\left(\boldsymbol{t}+\frac{\omega(\gamma)}{2\pi}g_{s}{\boldsymbol{c}}\log\big(1-{\rm e}^{2\pi{\boldsymbol{c}}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}/g_{s}}\big),\boldsymbol{u};g_{s}\right).

(1.3)

functions) have appeared in many different

2
The refined topological string

In this section, we review some
basic facts about the refined topological string and how to calculate its free energy.

As mentioned above, the refined topological string is parametrized by two complex
parameters
\epsilon_{1,2}
. Setting

\epsilon_{1}=g_{s}{\mathsf{b}},\qquad\epsilon_{2}=-g_{s}{\mathsf{b}}^{-1},

(2.1)

the parameter
g_{s}
can be identified as the topological string coupling constant, while
{\mathsf{b}}
can
be regarded as a deformation parameter.
When
{\mathsf{b}}=1
one recovers the standard topological string. We note that many references (like
[
74
]
)
use a parameter
\beta
, which is related to ours simply by
{{\mathsf{b}}=\beta^{1/2}}
.
The refined topological string free energy is a formal power series
[
67
]

{\cal F}({\boldsymbol{t}};\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})^{2i}(-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2})^{j-1}{\cal F}^{(i,j)}(\boldsymbol{t}),

(2.2)

where the coefficients
{\cal F}^{(i,j)}(\boldsymbol{t})
are sections of an appropriate line bundle
on the moduli space of
X
(of Kähler structures in the A-model, or complex structures in the B-model),
parametrized by the flat coordinates
{\boldsymbol{t}}
. (These are complexified Kähler parameters in the A-model,
and periods of the holomorphic 3-form in the B-model. We recall that the coordinates are flat with respect to the Gauss–Manin connection.)
For the purposes of this paper, we regard the free energy as a formal power series in
g_{s}
whose coefficients
depend on the moduli and on the deformation parameter
{\mathsf{b}}
,

{\cal F}({\boldsymbol{t}};g_{s},{\mathsf{b}})=\sum_{g\geq 0}g_{s}^{2g-2}{\cal F}_{g}({\boldsymbol{t}};{\mathsf{b}}),

(2.3)

where

{\cal F}_{g}({\boldsymbol{t}};{\mathsf{b}})=\sum_{k=0}^{g}{\cal F}^{(k,g-k)}({\boldsymbol{t}})\big({\mathsf{b}}-{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}\big)^{2k}.

(2.4)

The deformed free energies
{\cal F}_{g}({\boldsymbol{t}};{\mathsf{b}})
are Laurent polynomials in
{\mathsf{b}}
, of degree
2g
,
and invariant under the exchange
{\mathsf{b}}\leftrightarrow{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}
. When
{\mathsf{b}}=1
,

{\cal F}_{g}({\boldsymbol{t}};1)={\cal F}^{(0,g)}({\boldsymbol{t}})={\cal F}_{g}({\boldsymbol{t}})

(2.5)

is the conventional, unrefined topological string free energy at genus
g
.
The amplitudes
{\cal F}^{(k,g\!-\!k)}\!({\boldsymbol{t}})
are related to the ones defined in
[
67
]
by an overall sign
(-1)^{k}
.

The refined free energies
{\cal F}_{g}({\boldsymbol{t}};{\mathsf{b}})
can be calculated on certain local CY geometries
using instanton calculus
[
86
]
, and more generally
with the refined topological vertex of
[
68
]
. These A-model-like
calculations are intrinsically attached to the large radius of the geometry.
In the refined case,
the B-model approach to the free energies is based on the HAE of
[
22
]
.
5
5

5

In the
unrefined case,
and in local CY geometries, the B-model is described by the topological recursion of
[
42
]
, as proposed in
[
23
,
77
]
.
To our knowledge a refined version of the formalism
for generic local CY geometries is not available yet, see
however
[
70
]
for recent progress.

An extension of the HAE to the refined case was proposed in
[
67
]

(see also
[
74
]
), and tested in detail in, e.g.,
[
28
,
64
]
.
In the framework of the HAE, one extends
the free energies
{\cal F}_{g}({\boldsymbol{t}};{\mathsf{b}})
to non-holomorphic functions, and the HAE control their non-holomorphic dependence.
We will use Roman capital letters for the non-holomorphic free energies obtained from the HAE,
and curly capital letters for their holomorphic limit,
as in
[
57
,
59
]
.

To write the HAE, we need some ingredients from special geometry (see, e.g.,
[
71
]
for more
details). In the local case, the mirror CY is encoded in an algebraic curve
usually called the mirror curve. We recall that the
moduli space
{\cal M}
of complex structures of the mirror CY is a special
Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
. We will denote
by
z^{a}
, with
a=1,\dots,n
a set of algebraic complex coordinates on this moduli space.
The Kähler metric (here
\partial_{a}=\partial_{z^{a}}
)

\displaystyle G_{a\bar{b}}=\partial_{a}\partial_{\bar{b}}K

(2.6)

derives from a Kähler potential
K
, which is determined
from the prepotential
{\cal F}_{0}
, equal to the
genus zero free energy (the latter does not depend on
{\mathsf{b}}
, as it clear from (
2.4
)).
We introduce the covariant
derivative
D_{a}
for the Levi–Civita connection associated to the metric,

\Gamma^{a}_{bc}=G^{a\bar{k}}\partial_{b}G_{c\bar{k}}.

(2.7)

In the case of compact CY threefolds, the covariant derivative involves as well a
connection on the Hodge bundle
{\cal L}
over
{\cal M}
. However, in the local case,
this connection vanishes in the holomorphic limit. Therefore, we can solve the HAE by setting formally

K_{a}=0

[
72
]
. An additional ingredient we will need is the Yukawa coupling, which is a
tensor
C_{abc}
. In the coordinate system given by the flat coordinates
t^{i}
, the Yukawa coupling
is given by the third derivatives of
the genus 0 free energy, so we have in general

C_{abc}={\partial t^{i}\over\partial z^{a}}{\partial t^{j}\over\partial z^{b}}{\partial t^{k}\over\partial z^{c}}{\partial^{3}{\cal F}_{0}\over\partial t^{i}\partial t^{j}\partial t^{k}}.

(2.8)

Finally, we have
to introduce an anti-holomorphic version of the Yukawa coupling, defined by

{\overline{C}}_{\bar{c}}^{ab}={\rm e}^{2K}G^{a\bar{d}}G^{b\bar{e}}{\overline{C}}_{\bar{a}\bar{d}\bar{e}}.

(2.9)

We can now write the refined HAE, following
[
67
]
. In terms of the free energies
F^{(k,g-k)}
, they read

\partial_{\bar{c}}F^{(k,g-k)}={1\over 2}\overline{C}^{ab}_{\bar{c}}\bigg(D_{a}D_{b}F^{(k,g-k-1)}+\sum_{m,h}D_{a}F^{(m,h)}D_{b}F^{(k-m,g-k-h)}\bigg),

(2.10)

where
g\geq 2
and the sum over
m
,
h
is such that
m+h\geq 1
.
However, it is easy to check that, in terms of the combinations
F_{g}({\mathsf{b}})
defined in (
2.4
),
the HAE take the same form as in the unrefined case of
[
22
]
:

\partial_{\bar{c}}F_{g}({\mathsf{b}})={1\over 2}\overline{C}^{ab}_{\bar{c}}\left(D_{a}D_{b}F_{g-1}({\mathsf{b}})+\sum_{h=1}^{g-1}D_{a}F_{h}({\mathsf{b}})D_{b}F_{g-h}({\mathsf{b}})\right),\qquad g\geq 2.

(2.11)

(Here, we only indicate the dependence of the refined free energies on the deformation parameter,
but they of course also depend on
z^{a}
.) Therefore, the refined free energies satisfy
the same HAE as the unrefined ones, but the starting point of the recursion, given by the
free energies at
g=1
, is different and given by

F_{1}({\mathsf{b}})=F_{1}({\mathsf{b}}=1)+\big({\mathsf{b}}-{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}\big)^{2}F^{(1,0)}.

(2.12)

Here,
F_{1}({\mathsf{b}}=1)=F^{(0,1)}
is the conventional, unrefined free energy at genus one.
The free energy
F^{(1,0)}
turns out to be given by a holomorphic function of the moduli. It has the form
[
64
,
67
]

\displaystyle F^{(1,0)}=-{1\over 24}\log(f({\boldsymbol{z}})\Delta({\boldsymbol{z}})),

(2.13)

where
\Delta({\boldsymbol{z}})
is the discriminant of the mirror curve, and
f({\boldsymbol{z}})
is a rational function of the moduli,
explicitly known in many cases. The fact that
F^{(1,0)}
is holomorphic will be important later on.

As in the unrefined local case, it is useful to introduce propagators
S^{ab}
, defined by the condition that
[
22
]

\partial_{\bar{c}}S^{ab}=\overline{C}^{ab}_{\bar{c}}.

(2.14)

As a result, the HAE (
2.11
) can be rewritten in the form

{\partial F_{g}({\mathsf{b}})\over\partial S^{ab}}={1\over 2}\left(D_{a}D_{b}F_{g-1}({\mathsf{b}})+\sum_{h=1}^{g-1}D_{a}F_{h}({\mathsf{b}})D_{b}F_{g-h}({\mathsf{b}})\right),\qquad g\geq 2.

(2.15)

The initial condition (
2.12
) has to be also re-expressed in terms of propagators.
Since
F^{(1,0)}
is purely holomorphic, it does not
depend on the propagators, whereas for
F^{(0,1)}
one has
[
22
]

\partial_{a}F^{(0,1)}={1\over 2}C_{abc}S^{bc}+f_{a}({\boldsymbol{z}}).

(2.16)

In this equation,
f_{a}({\boldsymbol{z}})
is a function of the
moduli only. The equation (
2.15
), together with the
initial conditions (
2.12
), (
2.16
) and (
2.13
),
provides a recursive procedure to obtain the free energies
F_{g}({\mathsf{b}})
as polynomials
in the propagators, which is sometimes called “direct integration”
[
17
,
55
,
66
,
94
]
. At each
genus
g
one has to fix an integration constant,
independent of the propagators, but dependent on the moduli, and called the holomorphic ambiguity
(the functions
f_{a}({\boldsymbol{z}})
in (
2.16
)
can be regarded as examples thereof).

We would like to recall that the holomorphic free energies
{\cal F}_{g}(\boldsymbol{t};{\mathsf{b}})
depend on what is called
a choice of
symplectic frame
. This choice
is determined, in the B-model, by a choice of a symplectic basis of periods, which determines in turn
a choice of flat coordinates
\boldsymbol{t}
and of “dual” coordinates
\partial{\cal F}_{0}/\partial\boldsymbol{t}
. A symplectic transformation of the periods
leads to a change of frame, which is implemented at the level of the partition function
{\cal Z}
by a generalized Fourier
transform
[
1
]
. There are canonical choices of frame associated to special points in moduli space. In particular,
the large radius frame is adapted to the large radius point in moduli space, and in this frame
the holomorphic free energies
have an expansion in terms of Gromov–Witten invariants. There is also a conifold frame adapted to the conifold point of
the moduli space. We also note that the expression of the free energies in terms of the propagators
S^{ab}
is independent of the
frame. However, the holomorphic limit of the propagators
S^{ab}
, which we will
denote by
{\cal S}^{ab}
, does depend on a choice of frame, and different choices of this holomorphic
limit in the solution of the HAE give the different, frame-dependent holomorphic free energies.

In the case of local CY manifolds the holomorphic ambiguity can be fixed in many cases by using the behavior of the free energies at
special points in moduli space
[
61
]
. Let us review this
behavior in the case of the refined topological string.
We first consider the behavior at the conifold locus. Let
t_{c}
be an appropriately normalized
flat coordinate vanishing at this locus, and parametrizing a normal direction to it.
In the conifold frame, the free energy has the following behavior as
t_{c}\to 0

[
67
,
74
]

{\cal F}_{g}(\boldsymbol{t};{\mathsf{b}})={c_{g}({\mathsf{b}})\over t_{c}^{2g-2}}+{\cal O}(1),\qquad g\geq 2,

(2.17)

where the coefficient
c_{g}({\mathsf{b}})
is given by

c_{g}({\mathsf{b}})=-(2g-3)!\sum_{m=0}^{g}\widehat{B}_{2m}\widehat{B}_{2g-2m}{\mathsf{b}}^{2(2m-g)}.

(2.18)

In this formula

\widehat{B}_{m}=\big(2^{1-m}-1\big){B_{m}\over m!}

(2.19)

and
B_{m}
is the Bernoulli number. In the unrefined limit
{\mathsf{b}}=1
one has

c_{g}(1)={B_{2g}\over 2g(2g-2)},\qquad g\geq 2,

(2.20)

and recovers the well-known universal conifold behavior of the standard topological string
[
51
]
.

Another universal result concerns the behavior of the refined free energies at large radius and in the large radius frame.
It is possible to generalize the Gopakumar–Vafa integrality structure
[
52
]
to a refined version
[
63
,
67
]
.
Let us introduce

\epsilon_{L,R}={\epsilon_{1}\mp\epsilon_{2}\over 2}.

(2.21)

In terms of the characters
\chi_{j}(y)
defined in (
1.7
),
the total free energy is then given, up to a cubic polynomial in
\boldsymbol{t}
, by a sum of the form

{\cal F}({\boldsymbol{t}};g_{s},{\mathsf{b}})=\sum_{w,\boldsymbol{d}}\sum_{j_{L},j_{R}}\frac{\chi_{j_{L}}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}w\epsilon_{L}}\big)\chi_{j_{R}}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}w\epsilon_{R}}\big)}{4\sin\big({g_{s}w{\mathsf{b}}\over 2}\big)\sin\big({g_{s}w\over 2{\mathsf{b}}}\big)}N^{j_{L},j_{R}}_{\boldsymbol{d}}Q_{\boldsymbol{d}}^{w},

(2.22)

where
Q_{\boldsymbol{d}}={\rm e}^{-\boldsymbol{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}}
and
N^{j_{L},j_{R}}_{\boldsymbol{d}}
are integers (here,
j_{L},j_{R}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}/2
).
The integers
N^{j_{L},j_{R}}_{\boldsymbol{d}}
count
BPS states with charge
{\boldsymbol{d}}
transforming with spin
(j_{L},j_{R})

under the little group
{\rm SU}(2)_{L}\times{\rm SU}(2)_{R}
in 5 dimensions,
and are sometimes called BPS invariants.
When
{\mathsf{b}}=1
, we recover the integrality structure of the standard topological string free
energy. In particular,
we have the following relation between the genus zero Gopakumar–Vafa invariants
n^{(0)}_{\boldsymbol{d}}
and the BPS invariants

n_{\boldsymbol{d}}^{(0)}=\sum_{j_{L},j_{R}}d_{L}d_{R}N^{j_{L},j_{R}}_{\boldsymbol{d}},\qquad d_{L,R}=2j_{L,R}+1.

(2.23)

The refined (or motivic) Donaldson–Thomas invariants
\Omega({\boldsymbol{d}},y)

in the large volume limit are characters for the diagonal
{\rm SU}(2)
action:

\Omega({\boldsymbol{d}},y)=\sum_{j_{L},j_{R}}\chi_{j_{L}}(y)\chi_{j_{R}}(y)N^{j_{L},j_{R}}_{\boldsymbol{d}}.

(2.24)

In particular, they reduce to (
2.23
) in the unrefined limit
y\to 1
.
Unlike the BPS invariants
N^{j_{L},j_{R}}_{\boldsymbol{d}}
,
which are only defined when
{\boldsymbol{d}}
is a curve class, the refined DT invariants

\Omega(\gamma,y)

are defined for any vector
\gamma\in K_{0}(X)
, i.e., for arbitrary D6-D4-D2-D0 brane charge in type IIA,
or D5-D3-D1-D(-1) charge in type IIB.
For later purposes, it will be convenient to decompose
\Omega(\gamma,y)
as a sum of
{\rm SU}(2)
characters,

\Omega(\gamma,y):=\sum_{j}\chi_{j}(y)\Omega_{[j]}(\gamma),

(2.25)

where the integers
\Omega_{[j]}(\gamma)

count BPS multiplets of angular momentum
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2
,
and
y
is the corresponding fugacity parameter.
We note that the representation (
2.25
) implies
that
\Omega(\gamma,y)
is a Laurent polynomial in
y
, invariant under Poincaré duality
y\mapsto 1/y
.
As emphasized in
[
84
]
, for the standard definition of DT invariants using cohomology
with compact supports, Poincaré duality is broken when
X
is non-compact, ultimately due
to the non-invariance of the attractor indices counting D0-branes. Here we assume that
the DT invariants which are pertinent for the refined topological string are in fact invariant under Poincaré duality.

3
Trans-series solutions and resurgent structure

In order to understand the resurgent structure of the refined topological string, we
follow the strategy of
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
57
,
59
]

and look for trans-series solutions to the HAE. These define non-perturbative sectors of the theory, but they require
boundary conditions, as in the perturbative case. As first pointed out in
[
33
,
34
]
,
we can obtain these boundary conditions
by looking at the large order behavior of the genus expansions near special points in moduli space.
It turns out that the first aspect of this procedure, namely the construction of formal trans-series solutions to the HAE,
is essentially identical to the unrefined case. Therefore, we will be rather succinct
and refer the reader to
[
57
,
59
]
. The second aspect of the procedure, namely the analysis of the boundary
conditions, has some new ingredients, and we will analyze it in more detail.

3.1
Trans-series solutions

As in
[
57
,
59
]
, we want to construct multi-instanton trans-series solutions for the free energy and partition function. Let us
denote by
Z^{\rm NP}
a non-perturbative correction to the perturbative partition function
Z
(i.e., involving an exponentially small
dependence on the string coupling constant). We will also introduce the “reduced” partition function

Z_{\rm r}={Z^{\rm NP}\over Z},

(3.1)

where
Z
denotes the perturbative partition function. Following
[
33
,
34
,
57
,
59
]
, we will
write down an equation satisfied by the reduced partition function
Z_{\rm r}
as a consequence of the HAE. To do that, we
introduce a pair of
{\cal A}
-dependent operators,
\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{D}
.
In the local case these operators are defined as follows.
Let us introduce

T^{a}=g_{s}\partial_{b}{\cal A}\big(S^{ab}-{\cal S}_{{\cal A}}^{ab}\big).

(3.2)

Here,
{\cal S}_{{\cal A}}^{ab}
is the holomorphic limit of the propagator in a so-called
{\cal A}
-frame, i.e., a symplectic frame in
which the action
{\cal A}
is a linear combination of the flat coordinates
t^{a}
. Then

\mathsf{D}=T^{a}{\partial\over\partial z^{a}}.

(3.3)

To define the operator
\mathsf{W}
, we first introduce the derivation
\omega_{S}
defined as

\omega_{S}={1\over g_{s}^{2}}T^{a}T^{b}{\partial\over\partial S^{ab}}.

(3.4)

Then
\mathsf{W}
is given by

\mathsf{W}=\omega_{S}-\sum_{g\geq 1}\big[\mathsf{D}\big(g_{s}^{2g-2}F_{g}\big)\big]\mathsf{D},

(3.5)

where in the refined case the free energies are
{\mathsf{b}}
-dependent. As shown in
[
57
,
59
]
, as a consequence
of the HAE, the reduced partition function satisfies the linear equation

\mathsf{W}Z_{\rm r}={1\over 2}\mathsf{D}^{2}Z_{\rm r}.

(3.6)

We will consider a special class of solutions of the form

Z_{\rm r}=\exp(\Sigma_{\lambda}),

(3.7)

where
g_{s}\Sigma_{\lambda}
is a formal power series in
g_{s}
. More precisely, we will write

\Sigma_{\lambda}=-{\lambda\over g_{s}}{\cal A}+{\cal O}\big(g_{s}^{0}\big),

(3.8)

so that (
3.7
) is a non-perturbative, exponentially small quantity. In this equation,
\lambda
is an
arbitrary constant, and following
[
33
,
34
,
39
,
57
]
we will assume that
{\cal A}
is an integer period of the mirror
CY manifold. By analogy with instanton physics, we will sometimes call
{\cal A}
an instanton action.
We note that, since (
3.6
) is linear, arbitrary linear combinations of the
basic solutions (
3.7
) are also solutions. As we will see, these solutions will be enough to construct the relevant trans-series
for the refined topological string.

Since
\mathsf{D}
is a derivation, the linear equation (
3.6
) leads to the following operator equation for
\Sigma_{\lambda}
:

\mathsf{W}\Sigma_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathsf{D}^{2}\Sigma_{\lambda}+(\mathsf{D}\Sigma_{\lambda})^{2}\big).

(3.9)

In
[
57
,
59
]
, this equation was solved as follows. Consider the formal series

G={{\cal A}\over g_{s}}+\sum_{g\geq 1}\mathsf{D}\big(g_{s}^{2g-2}F_{g}\big),

(3.10)

where
{\cal A}
is the action appearing in (
3.8
). Let us now assume that
G
satisfies the equation

\mathsf{W}G={1\over 2}\mathsf{D}^{2}G.

(3.11)

Then

\Sigma_{\lambda}=\mathsf{O}^{(\lambda)}G,

(3.12)

where

\mathsf{O}^{(\lambda)}=\sum_{k\geq 1}{(-\lambda)^{k}\over k!}\mathsf{D}^{k-1},

(3.13)

satisfies (
3.9
).
Therefore, it suffices to check that (
3.11
) is still true in the
refined case. After using the HAE, one finds that
(
3.11
) holds if and only if

\omega_{S}\left(\mathsf{D}F_{1}\right)-\mathsf{D}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right)\mathsf{D}F_{1}-{1\over 2}\mathsf{D}^{2}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right)=0.

(3.14)

This equation is true in the conventional
topological string, when
F_{1}=F_{1}({\mathsf{b}}=1)
, as one can check by using (
2.16
). In particular,
it holds for any choice of holomorphic ambiguity
f^{(1)}_{i}({\boldsymbol{z}})
. It is easily checked to be true
in the refined case as well: since
F_{1}({\mathsf{b}})
differs from
F_{1}({\mathsf{b}}=1)
in a purely holomorphic function
of the moduli, as noted after (
2.12
), it follows immediately that (
3.14
) must also
be true for
F_{1}({\mathsf{b}})
. This can also be checked by a direct calculation.

Boundary conditions for trans-series solutions to the HAE are obtained by considering their holomorphic
limit in an
{\cal A}
-frame. In this limit, the propagator
S^{ab}
has to be set to
{\cal S}^{ab}_{\cal A}
,
and the
T^{a}
in (
3.2
), as well as the operator
\mathsf{D}
, vanish. It is easy to see from the formulae above
that
\Sigma_{\lambda}
, when evaluated in the
{\cal A}
-frame, is given by

\Sigma_{\lambda,{\cal A}}=-\lambda{{\cal A}\over g_{s}}.

(3.15)

The solution (
3.12
) involves the full, non-holomorphic propagators,
but in practice one wants to understand its holomorphic limit. This goes as follows.
First, we write the action
{\cal A}
as a linear combination of periods
6
6

6

For compact CY,
there is an additional contribution
-\frac{c^{0}}{2\pi}({\cal F}_{0}-2t^{a}\partial_{a}{\cal F}_{0})
, corresponding at large radius to the D5-brane
charge in IIB, or D6-brane charge in IIA, but this term
is absent when
X
is non-compact.

{\cal A}=-{\rm i}c^{a}{\partial{\cal F}_{0}\over\partial t^{a}}+2\pi d_{a}t^{a}+4\pi^{2}{\rm i}d_{0}.

(3.16)

For example, in the large radius frame, we have that
t^{a}=-\log(z^{a})+\cdots
are the complexified Kähler parameters,
and the genus zero free energy
{\cal F}_{0}
behaves as
7
7

7

Here
\kappa_{abc}
is a rational number,
which plays the role of the triple intersection number for a non-compact CY threefold. Note
that in our conventions, monodromies around the large radius point induce shifts
t^{a}\mapsto t^{a}+2\pi{\rm i}\epsilon^{a}
with
\epsilon^{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

{\cal F}_{0}={\kappa_{abc}\over 3!}t^{a}t^{b}t^{c}+{\cal O}\big({\rm e}^{-t^{a}}\big),

(3.17)

as
t^{a}\to+\infty
. In (
3.16
),
(c^{a},d_{a},d_{0})
are integers, and in the large radius frame they correspond
to D3-D1-D(-1) charges
in type IIB set-up, or D4-D2-D0 charges in type IIA. The action
{\cal A}
is then identified with
the central charge
Z(\gamma)
, up to an overall factor
4\pi^{2}{\rm i}
.

When not all
c^{a}
vanish at once, one defines a new prepotential by

{\cal A}=-{\rm i}c^{a}{\partial\widetilde{\cal F}_{0}\over\partial t^{a}}.

(3.18)

It differs from the conventional prepotential at most in a quadratic polynomial in the flat coordinates
t^{a}
.
Then, in the holomorphic limit, one has

\mathsf{D}\rightarrow-{\rm i}g_{s}c^{a}{\partial\over\partial t^{a}},

(3.19)

and
\Sigma_{\lambda}
becomes

\Sigma_{\lambda}\rightarrow\tilde{\cal F}(\boldsymbol{t}+{\rm i}\lambda g_{s}\boldsymbol{c};g_{s},{\mathsf{b}})-\tilde{\cal F}(\boldsymbol{t};g_{s},{\mathsf{b}}),

(3.20)

where

\tilde{\cal F}({\boldsymbol{t}};g_{s},{\mathsf{b}})=g_{s}^{-2}\tilde{\cal F}_{0}({\boldsymbol{t}})+\sum_{g\geq 1}g_{s}^{2g-2}{\cal F}_{g}({\boldsymbol{t}};{\mathsf{b}}),

(3.21)

i.e., it differs from the conventional free energy only in the genus zero part.

3.2
Boundary conditions from the conifold

Let us now discuss the boundary conditions for the trans-series. These follow from the
behavior of the free energies, in appropriate
frames, at special points in the moduli space. This behavior determines the large order
asymptotics of the free energies, and this leads
in turn to multi-instanton trans-series.

Let us first review how the conifold boundary condition for the trans-series is determined in the case of
the standard topological string with
{\mathsf{b}}=1

[
33
,
34
]
.
The free energies in the conifold frame have the following behavior near the conifold locus,

{\cal F}_{g}(\boldsymbol{t};{\mathsf{b}}=1)={B_{2g}\over 2g(2g-2)}t_{c}^{2-2g}+{\cal O}(1),\qquad g\geq 2,

(3.22)

which follows from (
2.17
), (
2.20
).
This singular behavior also determines
the growth of the free energies at large genus
close to the conifold locus where
t_{c}=0
. To compute the large order behavior,
one can use the following representation of the Bernoulli numbers

B_{2g}=(-1)^{g-1}{2(2g)!\over(2\pi)^{2g}}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\ell^{-2g}

(3.23)

to obtain the formula

{B_{2g}\over 2g(2g-2)}t_{c}^{2-2g}={1\over 2\pi^{2}}\Gamma(2g-1)\sum_{\ell\geq 1}(\ell{\cal A})^{1-2g}{{\cal A}\over\ell}\left(1+{1\over 2g-2}\right),\qquad g\geq 2,

(3.24)

where

{\cal A}=2\pi{\rm i}t_{c}.

(3.25)

Let us note that, in local CY manifolds, conifold flat
coordinates are linear combinations of large radius periods
\partial F_{0}/\partial t^{i}

and of constant periods (see, e.g.,
[
30
]
), therefore (
3.25
) is the central charge of a D4-D0 BPS state.

The formula (
3.24
) makes manifest the asymptotic factorial growth of the left-hand side for
{g\gg 1}
, and it includes all corrections to the large
g
behavior. Standard arguments in the
theory of resurgence relate the large order behavior of
asymptotic series to exponentially small corrections
(see, e.g.,
[
78
, Section 3.3]
). The basic idea is that factorial growth leads
to singularities in the Borel transform of the asymptotic series, and these in turn lead to
exponentially small discontinuities in lateral Borel resummations. By using these arguments, one finds that
the Borel transform of the perturbative series given by the left-hand side of (
3.24
) has singularities at the point
\ell{\cal A}
,
where
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\{0\}
, and
{\cal A}
is given by (
3.25
). In addition, one finds that
each of these singularities leads to an
\ell
-th instanton amplitude of the Pasquetti–Schiappa form
[
91
]
,

{\cal F}_{\cal A}^{(\ell)}={1\over 2\pi}\left[{1\over\ell}\left({{\cal A}\over g_{s}}\right)+{1\over\ell^{2}}\right]{\rm e}^{-\ell{\cal A}/g_{s}},\qquad\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\{0\}.

(3.26)

This gives the
\ell
-th order trans-series which will provide boundary conditions in the conifold frame.

Another way of finding the trans-series corresponding to the large genus asymptotics of (
3.24
)
is to write an integral formula for the coefficient, of the form

\displaystyle(-1)^{g-1}{B_{2g}\over 2g(2g-2)}

\displaystyle={1\over 2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{{\rm d}z\over z^{2g-1}}\left\{{\rm Li}_{2}\big({\rm e}^{-2\pi/z}\big)-{2\pi\over z}\log\big(1-{\rm e}^{-2\pi/z}\big)\right\}

\displaystyle=-{{\rm i}\over\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{{\rm d}z\over z^{2g-1}}\log\operatorname{\Phi}_{1}\left(-{1\over z}\right),\qquad g\geq 2,

(3.27)

where
\operatorname{\Phi}_{\mathsf{b}}(z)
is Faddeev’s quantum dilogarithm (see Appendix
A
).
Up to the overall factor of
1/\pi
,
the integrand in the last line of (
3.27
) gives the sum over all the multi-instantons trans-series with positive
\ell
:

\sum_{\ell\geq 1}{\cal F}_{\cal A}^{(\ell)}=-{\rm i}\log\operatorname{\Phi}_{1}\left(-{{\cal A}\over 2\pi g_{s}}\right).

(3.28)

This function determines the structure of the Stokes automorphism, as explained
in
[
69
]
, and in line with what was obtained in
[
12
]
.

Let us now consider the generalization of the above result to the refined
topological string.
8
8

8

See
[
16
,
53
]
for earlier studies of the resurgent structure
of the refined topological string on the resolved conifold.

We need a formula for
the coefficient
c_{g}({\mathsf{b}})
appearing in (
2.17
) which generalizes (
3.24
). This formula is

c_{g}({\mathsf{b}})={1\over 2\pi}\Gamma(2g-2)\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\left[{(-1)^{\ell-1}\over\ell}{1\over\sin\left({\pi\ell\over{\mathsf{b}}^{2}}\right)}\left({2\pi{\rm i}\ell\over{\mathsf{b}}}\right)^{2-2g}+\big({\mathsf{b}}\leftrightarrow{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}\big)\right],\qquad g\geq 2,

(3.29)

and it can be derived by using Borel transform techniques, as follows. Let us define the more convenient set of coefficients:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{0}({\mathsf{b}})=1,\qquad\tilde{c}_{1}({\mathsf{b}})={1\over 12}\big({\mathsf{b}}^{2}+{\mathsf{b}}^{-2}\big),\qquad\tilde{c}_{g}({\mathsf{b}})=(-1)^{g-1}{(2g)!\over(2g-3)!}c_{g}({\mathsf{b}}),\qquad g\geq 2,\!\!\!

(3.30)

and its generating function

\varphi(x)=\sum_{g\geq 0}\tilde{c}_{g}({\mathsf{b}})x^{2g}.

(3.31)

The Borel transform of
\varphi(x)
can be obtained in closed form, as

\widehat{\varphi}(\zeta)={\zeta^{2}\over 4\sin\big({\zeta{\mathsf{b}}\over 2}\big)\sin\big({\zeta{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}\over 2}\big)}.

(3.32)

When
{\mathsf{b}}^{2}
is not a rational number, this function has simple pole singularities at

\zeta={2\pi\ell\over{\mathsf{b}}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad 2\pi\ell{\mathsf{b}},\qquad\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\{0\},

(3.33)

with residues

{(2\pi\ell)^{2}(-1)^{\ell}\over{\mathsf{b}}^{3}}{2\over\sin\big({\pi\ell\over{\mathsf{b}}^{2}}\big)}

(3.34)

at the first set of poles. The residues at the second set are obtained by exchanging

{\mathsf{b}}\leftrightarrow{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}
. The standard connection between singularities
of the Borel transform and large order asymptotics gives then the analogue of the formula (
3.24
) for the coefficients
\tilde{c}_{g}({\mathsf{b}})
, and by going back to the original coefficients
c_{g}({\mathsf{b}})
we obtain (
3.29
). In our derivation, we have
assumed that
{\mathsf{b}}^{2}
is not rational. When
{\mathsf{b}}^{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
, we have singularities in the denominators in
(

). One can verify though that the singularities cancel between the two summands related
by
{\mathsf{b}}\leftrightarrow{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}
, as noted in
[
62
]
. The resulting expression for Faddeev’s
quantum dilogarithm when
{\mathsf{b}}^{2}
is rational can be found in
[
50
]
.

We can now use the expression (
A.10

(-1)^{g-1}c_{g}({\mathsf{b}})=-{{\rm i}\over\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{{\rm d}z\over z^{2g-1}}\log\operatorname{\Phi}_{\mathsf{b}}\left(-{1\over z}\right).

(3.35)

Using (
A.10
), we conclude that the relevant trans-series are given by

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{{\cal A},{\mathsf{b}}}^{(\ell)}={(-1)^{\ell-1}\over\ell}{{\rm e}^{-{\ell{\cal A}\over{\mathsf{b}}g_{s}}}\over 2\sin\big({\pi\ell\over{\mathsf{b}}^{2}}\big)},\qquad{\cal F}_{{\cal A},{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}}^{(\ell)}={(-1)^{\ell-1}\over\ell}{{\rm e}^{-{\ell{\cal A}{\mathsf{b}}\over g_{s}}}\over 2\sin\big({\pi\ell{\mathsf{b}}^{2}}\big)},

(3.36)

and

\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\big({\cal F}_{{\cal A},{\mathsf{b}}}^{(\ell)}+{\cal F}_{{\cal A},{\mathsf{b}}^{-1}}^{(\ell)}\big)=-{\rm i}\log\operatorname{\Phi}_{\mathsf{b}}\left(-{{\cal A}\over 2\pi g_{s}}\right).

(3.37)

In particular, the trans-series involves two different actions
{\cal A}/{\mathsf{b}}
and

[
53
]
.

Let us now use these boundary conditions to obtain appropriate trans-series in an arbitrary frame.
After exponentiating, we find

3.4
Stokes automorphism in the refined case

From the above results, and using the methods of
[
69

, one can easily obtain the
Stokes automorphism corresponding to the trans-series (

3.58

as we cross a ray of singularities. This discontinuity is expressed as a formal linear combination of
resummed trans-series associated to the singularities, whose coefficients are the Stokes constants. The Stokes automorphism
is given by this formal linear combination of trans-series. We refer, e.g., to
[
69
]

for additional background and references on the Stokes automorphism and related aspects of the theory of resurgence.

We found that, for the topological free energies in the large radius frame,
the leading Borel singularities near the large radius point

{\mathsf{b}}
and
\ell>0

the Stokes automorphism associated to the discontinuity as we cross both rays.
10
10

10

could in principle consider separately the Stokes automorphism for the ray containing the singularities

4
Refined dual partition functions

In this section, we propose an extension of the notion of dual partition function,
realized as certain generalized theta series encoding five-brane instantons, that incorporates the refinement.
The construction relies on the use of a non-commutative Moyal star product and its extension to the realm of contact geometry.
Our main result is that the same Stokes automorphism (
3.64
) that governs the refined topological string
also arises as the transformation of the kernel of the refined dual partition function
induced by a non-commutative wall-crossing transformation.
Furthermore, by considering higher five-brane charge
k>1

3.64
),
which realizes the double quantization proposed in
[
12
, Section 4]
.

4.2
Refined contact structure

Our goal is to incorporate the refinement parameter into the above construction.
In fact, a refined version of the function
\mathbf{A}^{(k)}(x,\ell)
was already put forward in

[

To fill this gap, we have to find a proper generalization of the dual partition function (

(or more generally (

Therefore, the first step is to find how to lift refined symplectomorphisms to refined contact transformations.
Since the defining property of the latter in the absence of refinement was that they preserve the contact one-form,
this can be seen as constructing a refined version of the contact structure.
This is the problem that we address in this subsection.

Physically, the standard way to introduce a refinement is to switch on an

As was observed in

It is easy to check that with respect to the Moyal product the relation (

The function

The standard (sometimes called compact)
quantum dilogarithm

is defined for
{x,y\in{\mathbb{C}}}
,
|y|<1

as
18
18

18

(A.1)

\displaystyle E_{y}\big(xy^{2}\big)=(1-xy)E_{y}(x),

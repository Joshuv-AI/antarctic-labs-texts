The Biot–Allard poro-elasticity system: equivalent forms and well-posedness
Jakob S. Stokke, Markus Bause, Nils Margenberg, Florin A. Radu

Abstract
We consider the fully dynamic Biot–Allard model, which includes memory effects. Convolution integrals in time model the history of the porous medium. We use a series representation of the dynamic permeability in the frequency domain to rewrite the equations in a coupled system without convolution integrals, suitable for the design of efficient numerical approximation schemes. The main result is the well-posedness of the system, proved by the abstract theory of R. Picard for evolutionary problems.



1
Introduction

In this letter, we consider poro-elasticty with memory effects, referred to as the Biot–Allard model, which models the coupling of flow and deformation in a fully saturated porous medium. Many relevant problems in natural sciences and engineering are described by poro-elasticity systems, including applications in geophysics, bio-mechanics, and bio-medicine. For example, seismic monitoring of the fluid flow during
CO_{2}
injection in the subsurface is important for safe storage. In this case, the memory effects in the Biot–Allard model could be interpreted as a delay effect due to drag forces. Experimental results in
[
1
]
indicate that memory effects due to drag forces are relevant for rocks in seismic wave propagation.
In bone remodeling, the memory effects due to fluid flow along cell bodies play a crucial role in activating bone cells
[
17
]
.

In this work, we consider the fully dynamic Biot–Allard model from
[
15
]
: Find the solid phase displacement
(\bm{u})
and the pressure
(p)
such that there holds

\displaystyle\rho\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u}-\nabla\cdot\bm{C}\varepsilon(\bm{u})+\alpha\nabla p+\partial_{t}\left(\mathcal{A}\ast(\rho_{f}\bm{f}-\nabla p-\rho_{f}\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u})\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\bm{f}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T],

(1a)

\displaystyle c_{0}\partial_{t}p+\nabla\cdot(\alpha\partial_{t}\bm{u})+\nabla\cdot\left(\mathcal{A}\ast(\bm{f}-\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\nabla p-\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u})\right)

\displaystyle=0\quad\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,T],

(1b)

together with initial and boundary conditions

\displaystyle\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0},\quad\partial_{t}\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{1},\quad p(0)

\displaystyle=p_{0},\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad\bm{u}=\bm{0},\quad p=0,\quad\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T].

(1c)

In (
1
), the operator
\ast
denotes the time convolution, i.e.
f\ast g(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(t-s)g(s)ds
. Further,
\Omega
is a bounded domain in

with

,

\alpha
is the Biot coefficient,
\rho=\rho_{f}\varphi+\rho_{s}(1-\varphi)
is the mass density with
\rho_{s}
and
\rho_{f}
being the solid and fluid density respectively and
\varphi
the porosity. Furthermore,

is Gassman’s fourth order tensor,

is the specific storage coefficient, and
\varepsilon(\bm{u}):=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{T})
is the linearized strain tensor.
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
is the dynamic permeability tensor, accounting for the delay memory effects of the interaction between the pore walls and fluid. For a general definition of the dynamic permeability we refer to
[
10
]
, but we note that the low-frequency limit of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)
should equal the static case or a similar condition in the time domain
[
15

. The tensor

By an approximation of the dynamic permeability, we derive a set of equations without convolution terms, in which memory effects are represented by auxiliary differential equations (ADEs). In the future, this new system will allow for efficient and less memory-demanding numerical approximation schemes to the Biot–Allard model.
Further, we prove the well-posedness of the thus obtained Biot–Allard model by rewriting it as a first-order evolutionary problem, i.e., a system of the form

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}M(\partial_{t})+A)\bm{U}=\bm{G},

(2)

where

.
This allows us to apply the abstract theory developed by R. Picard

2

2

[
18

, which is not explicitly done here for brevity.
For an example of the evolutionary theory applied to a hyperbolic or parabolic problem with memory effects, we refer to
[
22
]
.
The regularity, existence, and uniqueness of Biot models were first studied by Showalter
[
19
]
.

The paper is structured in the following way. In Sec.
2

1

3

4

2
. The letter ends with a concluding section.

2
Biot–Allard system with an auxiliary differential equation

Computing a numerical solution of (
1
) is expensive and challenging. Therefore, we derive an ADE to avoid the expensive evaluation of the convolution integral in (
1
). To do this, we use a known result
[
24
]
that for any pore geometry the dynamic permeability in the frequency domain is approximated by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)=\frac{\eta_{k}}{F}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{d_{j}}{1+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mbox{i}}\omega c_{j}},

(3)

where
i
is the imaginary unit,
\eta_{k}=\eta/\rho_{f}
is the kinetic viscosity,
F=\alpha_{\infty}/\varphi
is the formation factor,
\alpha_{\infty}
is the inﬁnite-frequency tortuosity and
c_{j},d_{j}>0
are constants, where
c_{j}<\Phi
with
\Phi
being the principal viscous relaxation time. For a specific frequency range, chosen for a particular application, the constants
c_{j},d_{j}
can be estimated from
N
sample points in a procedure detailed in
[
24
]
. For simplicity in the derivation, we consider the case when
N=1
; for the general case see Sec.
4
. We note that (
3
) implies an exponential decay in time of
\mathcal{A}(t)
, typically found in visco-elastic problems with memory effects. We refer to
[
9
]
for a detailed introduction to Fourier analysis. For our purpose, we introduce an auxiliary variable
\hat{\Psi}_{1}
in the frequency domain, defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{1}:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{F}\frac{d_{1}}{1+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mbox{i}}\omega c_{1}}\left(\hat{\bm{f}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\nabla\hat{p}+\omega^{2}\hat{\bm{u}}\right).

(4)

Then, the convolution in (
1b
) can be expressed in the frequency domain by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\ast\left(\bm{f}-\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\nabla p-\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u}\right)=\hat{\mathcal{A}}\cdot\left(\hat{\bm{f}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\nabla\hat{p}+\omega^{2}\hat{\bm{u}}\right)=\hat{\Psi}_{1}.

(5)

We also have an ADE for the auxiliary variable
\hat{\Psi}_{1}
in the frequency domain

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{1}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mbox{i}}\omega c_{1}\hat{\Psi}_{1}-\frac{d_{1}\eta_{k}}{F}\left(\hat{\bm{f}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\nabla\hat{p}+\omega^{2}\hat{\bm{u}}\right)=0.

(6)

By computing the inverse Fourier transform and reordering the equation, we obtain the ADE

\displaystyle c_{1}\partial_{t}\Psi_{1}+\Psi_{1}+\frac{d_{1}\eta_{k}}{F}\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\nabla p+\frac{d_{1}\eta_{k}}{F}\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u}=\frac{d_{1}\eta_{k}}{F}\bm{f}.

(7)

This results in the coupled system of equations in the time domain

\displaystyle\rho\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u}-\nabla\cdot\bm{C}\varepsilon(\bm{u})+\alpha\nabla p+\rho_{f}\partial_{t}\Psi_{1}

\displaystyle=\rho\bm{f},

(8a)

\displaystyle c_{0}\partial_{t}p+\nabla\cdot(\alpha\partial_{t}\bm{u})+\nabla\cdot\Psi_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

(8b)

\displaystyle c_{1}\rho_{f}\partial_{t}\Psi_{1}+\rho_{f}\Psi_{1}+\frac{d_{1}\eta_{k}}{F}\nabla p+\frac{d_{1}\eta_{k}}{F}\rho_{f}\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{1}\eta_{k}}{F}\rho_{f}\bm{f}.

(8c)

Remark 1
.

The analysis of numerical approximations of poroelastic models has received much attention in the last decade. A big focus has been developing solvers based on sequentially solving the mechanics and flow subproblems. The most popular iterative methods are the fixed-stress split and the undrained split,
based on adding a stabilization term to either the flow or the mechanics equation
[
11
]
. The convergence of these schemes was analyzed in
[
4
,
8
,
14
,
21
]
. A parallel fixed-stress scheme, which allows for solving the mechanics problems in parallel for all time steps, was proposed in
[
5
]
. The iterative schemes for the quasi-static Biot have been extended and analyzed for a dynamic Biot model for soft poroelastic materials in
[
6
]
. A splitting scheme for the dynamic Biot model is proposed in
[
3
]
, with the proof being based on gradient flow techniques
[
7
]
. Furthermore, the fixed-stress split has been extended to a history-dependent Biot model
[
20
]
.

Solution theory for evolutionary problems

In this section, we give a brief presentation of the unified solution theory for evolutionary equations and analyze the problem (
8
) within this setting. We use standard notation for Sobolev spaces. First, we introduce some preliminary definitions. Let
H
be a real Hilbert space equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
, then for a given parameter
\nu\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle H_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};H):=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}\to H:\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f(t)\|_{H}^{2}e^{-2\nu t}dt\right\},\quad\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{H_{\nu}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{H}e^{-2\nu t}dt,

(9)

for
f,g\in H_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};H)
.
The inner product (
9
) induces a norm
\|\cdot\|_{\nu}
.
Let
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};H)
be the space of infinitely differentiable
H
-valued functions with compact support, then we denote by
\partial_{t}
the closure of the operator

\displaystyle\partial_{t}:C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};H)\subset H_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};H)\to H_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};H),\,\,\phi\to\phi^{\prime}.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the Fourier transform. We define the Fourier-Laplace transform as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\nu}:H_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};H)\to H_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};H),\,f\mapsto\mathcal{F}e^{-\nu t}f.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
22

)

For

there holds that
\partial_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{*}(\nu+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mbox{i}}t)\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
M:D(M)\subset\mathbb{C}\to L(H)
be a bounded, analytic function. Then, we define the linear operator

\displaystyle M(\nu+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mbox{i}}m):L^{2}(\mathbb{R};H)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H)

by
(M(\nu+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mbox{i}}m)f)(t)=M(\nu+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mbox{i}}t)f(t)
for

M(\partial_{t})

\displaystyle M(\partial_{t}):=\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{*}M(\nu+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mbox{i}}m)\mathcal{L}_{\nu}\in L(H_{\nu}(\mathbb{R};H)).

Theorem 3.4

(
[
18

)
.

Let
\nu_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and

be a Hilbert space. Let

Conclusions

We considered the Biot–Allard model for flow in deformable porous media with memory effects. We derived
a new equivalent formulation where the memory effects are incorporated by auxiliary differential equations. We proved its well-posedness by an abstract solution theory for evolutionary problems.
The advantage of writing the Biot–Allard model in the convolution-free form (
27
) becomes evident when we turn towards its numerical discretization. The expensive computation of time convolution integrals is avoided. This allows for fast and efficient numerical schemes. For the feasibility and efficiency of the ADE approach (

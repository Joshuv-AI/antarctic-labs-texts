Inhomogeneous condensation in the Gross-Neveu model in noninteger spatial dimensions 1 ≤ d < 3 1\leq d<3 . II. Nonzero temperature and chemical potential
Adrian Koenigstein, Laurin Pannullo

Abstract
We continue previous investigations of the (inhomogeneous) phase structure of the Gross-Neveu model in a noninteger number of spatial dimensions ( 1 ≤ d < 3 1\leq d<3 ) in the limit of an infinite number of fermion species ( N → ∞ N\to\infty ) at (non)zero chemical potential μ \mu Pannullo:2023cat . In this work, we extend the analysis from zero to nonzero temperature T T . The phase diagram of the Gross-Neveu model in 1 ≤ d < 3 1\leq d<3 spatial dimensions is well known under the assumption of spatially homogeneous condensation with both a symmetry broken and a symmetric phase present for all spatial dimensions. In d = 1 d=1 one additionally finds an inhomogeneous phase, where the order parameter, the condensate, is varying in space. Similarly, phases of spatially varying condensates are also found in the Gross-Neveu model in d = 2 d=2 and d = 3 d=3 , as long as the theory is not fully renormalized, i.e., in the presence of a regulator. For d = 2 d=2 , one observes that the inhomogeneous phase vanishes, when the regulator is properly removed (which is not possible for d = 3 d=3 without introducing additional parameters). In the present work, we use the stability analysis of the symmetric phase to study the presence (for 1 ≤ d < 2 1\leq d<2 ) and absence (for 2 ≤ d < 3 2\leq d<3 ) of these inhomogeneous phases and the related moat regimes in the fully renormalized Gross-Neveu model in the μ , T \mu,T -plane. We also discuss the relation between “the number of spatial dimensions” and “studying the model with a finite regulator” as well as the possible consequences for the limit d → 3 d\to 3 .

I.2

In

Especially relevant for our work is the

I.3
Research objective

In the present work, we extend this investigation of spatially inhomogeneous condensation in (non)integer number of spatial dimensions from zero to nonzero temperature in order to map out the
d

IP

This study therefore complements the previously mentioned study in Ref.
Pannullo:2023cat

The latter study already investigated the phase diagram of the

Therefore, we aim at closing a gap in the literature about the

Furthermore, we hope that this work contributes to the general discussion about necessary conditions for the presence/absence of

Here, especially our findings about the role of the spatial dimensionality may be essential to understand the general criteria for the formation of inhomogeneous condensates.

II.1
The action and the potential

The microscopic action of the
GN
model is given by
Gross:1974jv

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[\bar{\psi},\psi]=\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

(1)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{T}}\mathrm{d}\tau\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\big[\bar{\psi}\,(\not{\partial}+\gamma^{0}\mu)\,\psi-\tfrac{\lambda}{2N}\,(\bar{\psi}\,\psi)^{2}\big]\,.\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

Here,
\bar{\psi}=\bar{\psi}(\tau,\vec{x}\,)
and
\psi=\psi(\tau,\vec{x}\,)
are the fermion fields, where
x^{i}\in(-\infty,\infty)
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, are the spatial coordinates of a
d
-dimensional Euclidean space and
\tau\in[0,\frac{1}{T})
denotes the coordinate of the compactified temporal direction that mimics the (inverse) temperature
T
.
The fermions have antiperiodic boundary conditions in the compact direction, come in
N
different species
2
2

2

Occasionally, these species are referred to as different colors or flavors.
and transform as spinors.
We use a
{d_{\gamma}}
-dimensional representation of the gamma matrices of the corresponding Clifford algebra,

\displaystyle\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}_{+}=2\delta^{\mu\nu}\openone_{{d_{\gamma}}}\,,

\displaystyle\mu,\nu\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}\,.

(2)

This generalizes to noninteger
d
dimensions
tHooft:1972tcz
and we use the Kronecker delta as the components of the Euclidean metric.
Furthermore, we use
\lambda
for the four-fermion coupling and introduce the fermion chemical potential
\mu
in the standard way.

For a detailed discussion of the symmetries of this model in different integer dimensions, we refer for example to Refs.
Koenigstein:2023wso
;
Urlichs:2007zz

In order to study four-fermion models (especially in the
N\to\infty
limit) one convenient approach is to bosonize the theory in the
ultra violet (UV)
via a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation
Stratonovich:1957
;
Hubbard:1959ub

Here, the four-fermion interaction is replaced by an auxiliary real scalar bosonic field
\phi

On the level of the partition function the equivalent action is
Gross:1974jv
;
Dashen:1974xz
;
Wolff:1985av

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[\bar{\psi},\psi,\phi]=\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

(3)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{T}}\mathrm{d}\tau\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\big[\tfrac{1}{2\lambda}\,(h\phi)^{2}+\bar{\psi}\,\big(\not{\partial}+\gamma^{0}\mu+\tfrac{h}{\sqrt{N}}\,\phi\big)\,\psi\big]\,,\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

where we also introduced the Yukawa coupling

to obtain canonical energy dimensions for
\phi

Now, in addition to the functional integration over fermion fields, one also has to integrate over the bosonic field
\phi

Since loop corrections for the Yukawa coupling are suppressed for

Braun:2011pp
;
Koenigstein:2023wso

In addition, to correctly take the limit

\sqrt{N}

Hence, we introduce

\displaystyle\sigma=\tfrac{h}{\sqrt{N}}\,\phi

(4)

It is simple to show, see, e.g., Ref.
Pannullo:2019

\displaystyle\langle\sigma\rangle\propto\langle\bar{\psi}\,\psi\rangle\,.

(5)

In an even number of spacetime dimensions

the formation of a nonzero expectation value of

chiral symmetry

\displaystyle\psi\mapsto{\gamma_{\mathrm{ch}}}\psi\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\mapsto-\bar{\psi}\,{\gamma_{\mathrm{ch}}}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma\mapsto-\sigma

(6)

In an odd number of spacetime dimensions as well as for noninteger dimensions the matrix
{\gamma_{\mathrm{ch}}}

\displaystyle\{\gamma^{\mu},{\gamma_{\mathrm{ch}}}\}_{+}=0\,,

\displaystyle\mu\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}\,,

(7)

For detailed discussions of these situations, we refer to Refs.

Gies:2009da

Still, it is possible to study the action

The standard way to proceed from

The resulting action

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{N}\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\sigma]=\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

(8)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{T}}\mathrm{d}\tau\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{2\lambda}-\ln\mathrm{Det}\big[\beta(\not{\partial}+\gamma^{0}\mu+\sigma)\big]\,,\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

where we already divided by the number of species

Considering the limit

This is equivalent to studying the full quantum effective action (the generating functional for one-particle-irreducible vertex functions) and only taking into account the contribution of the fermionic quantum fluctuations

8

It might not even be a well-posed problem for noninteger dimensions.

\sigma(\tau,\vec{x}\,)=\bar{\sigma}=\mathrm{const.}

We define the (homogeneous) effective potential

\displaystyle\bar{U}(\bar{\sigma},\mu,T,d)=\tfrac{1}{N}\,\tfrac{1}{\beta V}\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\bar{\sigma}],

(9)

which is the effective action for homogeneous fields per species and spacetime volume.
The eigenvalues of the Dirac operator for homogeneous fields
\bar{\sigma}
are those of free fermions with mass

Thus, the evaluation of

\displaystyle\bar{U}(\bar{\sigma},\mu,T,d)=\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

(10)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\tfrac{\bar{\sigma}^{2}}{2\lambda}-\tfrac{1}{\beta V}\ln\mathrm{Det}\big[\beta(\not{\partial}+\gamma^{0}\mu+\bar{\sigma})\big]=\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\tfrac{\bar{\sigma}^{2}}{2\lambda}-\tfrac{{d_{\gamma}}}{2}\,l_{0}(\bar{\sigma},\mu,T,d)\,,\vphantom{\Bigg(\Bigg)}

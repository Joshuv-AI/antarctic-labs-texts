Generalized existential completions and their regular and exact completions
Maria Emilia Maietti, Davide Trotta

Abstract
This paper aims to apply the tool of generalized existential completions of conjunctive doctrines, concerning a class Λ \Lambda of morphisms of their base category, to deepen the study of regular and exact completions of existential elementary Lawvere’s doctrines. After providing a characterization of generalized existential completions, we observe that both the subobjects doctrine Sub 𝒞 \operatorname{Sub}_{\mathcal{C}} and the weak subobjects doctrine Ψ 𝒞 \Psi_{\mathcal{C}} of a category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} with finite limits are generalized existential completions of the constant true doctrine, the first along the class of all the monomorphisms of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} while the latter along all the morphisms of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . We then name full existential completion a generalized completion of a conjunctive doctrine along the class of all the morphisms of its base. From this we immediately deduce that both the regular and the exact completion of a finite limit category are regular and exact completions of full existential doctrines since it is known that both the regular completion ( 𝒟 ) reg / lex (\mathcal{D})_{\operatorname{reg}/\operatorname{lex}} and the exact completion ( 𝒟 ) ex / lex (\mathcal{D})_{\operatorname{ex}/\operatorname{lex}} of a finite limit category 𝒟 \mathcal{D} are respectively the regular completion 𝖱𝖾𝗀 ⁡ ( Ψ 𝒟 ) \mathsf{Reg}(\Psi_{\mathcal{D}}) and the exact completion 𝒯 Ψ 𝒟 {\cal T}_{\Psi_{\mathcal{D}}} (as an instance of the tripos-to-topos construction) of the weak subobjects doctrine Ψ 𝒟 \Psi_{\mathcal{D}} of 𝒟 \mathcal{D} . Here we prove that the condition of being a generic full existential completion is also sufficient to produce a regular/exact completion equivalent to a regular/exact completion of a finite limit category. Then, we show more specialized characterizations from which we derive known results as well as remarkable examples of exact completions of full existential completions, including all realizability toposes and supercoherent localic toposes.

3.4
The realizability hyperdoctrine

In this section we are going to prove that all the realizability triposes
[

,

,

are full generalized existential completions as shown independently in
[

and presented in the talk by the second author

in 2020. A different presentation of realizability triposes via free constructions (essentially acting on the representing object of a given representable indexed preorder) can be found in

We start by recalling some related fundamental notions.

A
partial combinatory algebra
(pca) is specified by a set
\mathbb{A}
together with a partial binary operation

for which there exist elements

satisfying for all

that

k\cdot a\downarrow\mbox{ and }(k\cdot a)\cdot a^{\prime}\equiv a

and

s\cdot a\downarrow\mbox{, }(s\cdot a)\cdot a^{\prime}\downarrow\mbox{, and }((s\cdot a)\cdot a^{\prime})\cdot a^{\prime\prime}\equiv(a\cdot a^{\prime\prime})\cdot(a^{\prime}\cdot a^{\prime\prime})

where
e\downarrow
means "
e
is defined" and
e\equiv e^{\prime}
means "
e
is defined if and only
e^{\prime}
is, and in that case they are equal". For more details we refer to
[
37
]
.

Given a pca

, we can consider the
realizability hyperdoctrine

\textstyle{\mathcal{P}\colon\mathrm{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathbf{InfSL}}}

over
\mathrm{Set}
introduced in
[
8
,
9
]
. For each set

, the partial ordered set
(\mathcal{P}(X),\leq)
is defined as follow:

•

\mathcal{P}(X)
is the set of functions
\mathrm{P}(\mathbb{A})^{X}
from

to the powerset
\mathrm{P}(\mathbb{A})
of
\mathbb{A}
;

•

given two elements
\alpha
and

\mathcal{P}(X)

if there exists an element
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{A}

is defined and it is an element of

Then
\mathcal{P}(X)
is defined as the quotient of
\mathrm{P}(\mathbb{A})^{X}
by the equivalence relation generated by
\leq
. The partial order on the equivalence classes

is that induced by
\leq

Given a function
\textstyle{f\colon X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathcal{P}_{f}\colon\mathcal{P}(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

to the element

given by the composition of the two functions. With the these assignments
\textstyle{\mathcal{P}\colon\mathrm{Set}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathbf{InfSL}}}
is an hyperdoctrine, see
[

or
[

.

In particular we recall from some Heyting operations which give the structure of Heyting algebra to the fibres

[

that from the elements

and

which are called

and

•

\top_{X}:=[\lambda x\in X.\mathbb{A}]

•

•

\delta_{X}:=[\lambda(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X.\mathbb{A}\mbox{ if }x_{1}=x_{2},\emptyset\mbox{ otherwise}]

•

for every projection
\textstyle{\operatorname{pr}_{X}\colon X\times Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X}

In particular we have that

is elementary and existential and then, in particular, it has left adjoints along arbitrary functions, i.e. it is a full existential doctrine.

Notice also these left adjoints along any function satisfy BCC and FR (see
[
38

or
[
37

).

Now we show that the realizability hyperdoctrine is an instance of generalized existential completion. Thus, we fix the class
\Lambda_{\mathrm{Set}}
to be the class of all functions of
\mathrm{Set}
. The realizability hyperdoctrine is, in particular, existential and elementary, and then it has left adjoints along all the morphisms of
\mathrm{Set}
, so it is a full existential doctrine. The rest of this section is devoted to prove that
\mathcal{P}
is a full existential completion.

Hence, we need to understand what are the full-existential-free objects of the realizability hyperdoctrine.

Definition 3.66

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a pca, and let

\textstyle{\alpha\colon X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We claim that the singleton predicates are exactly the full-existential-free objects provided that we assume

Proof.

Assume that
\textstyle{[\gamma]\colon X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{P}(\mathbb{A})}

for some

\mathbb{A}

In order to show that this is a full-existential-free object is enough to show that it is a full-existential splitting since the action of

preserves singletons, i.e. for every function
\textstyle{m\colon Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X}
of
\mathrm{Set}

is again a singleton predicates.
To this purpose,
observe that if
[\gamma]\leq\exists_{g}([\beta])
for some

5
Exact completions via generalized existential completion

The tripos-to-topos construction orginally introduced in
[
9
,
8
]
is a fundamental construction of categorical logic that relates the notion of tripos with that of topos.
This construction is an instance of the exact completion
\mathcal{T}_{P}
of an elementary existential doctrine
\textstyle{P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathbf{InfSL}}}

[

]

Given an elementary existential doctrine
\textstyle{P\colon\mathcal{C}^{\operatorname{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{\mathbf{InfSL}}}
, the category
\mathcal{T}_{P}
consists of:

objects:

(A,\rho)
such that
\rho\in P(A\times A)
satisfies

•

symmetry:

\rho\leq P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{2},\operatorname{pr}_{1}\rangle}(\rho)
;

•

transitivity:

P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\rangle}(\rho)\wedge P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{2},\operatorname{pr}_{3}\rangle}(\rho)\leq P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{3}\rangle}(\rho)
, where
\operatorname{pr}_{i}
are the projections from
A\times A\times A
;

arrows:

\textstyle{\phi\colon(A,\rho)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(B,\sigma)}
are objects
\phi\in P(A\times B)
such that

(i)

\phi\leq P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{1}\rangle}(\rho)\wedge P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{2},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\rangle}(\sigma)

(ii)

P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\rangle}(\rho)\wedge P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{3}\rangle}(\phi)\leq P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{2},\operatorname{pr}_{3}\rangle}(\phi)
where
\operatorname{pr}_{i}
are projections from
A\times A\times B

(iii)

P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{2},\operatorname{pr}_{3}\rangle}(\sigma)\wedge P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\rangle}(\phi)\leq P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{3}\rangle}(\phi)
where
\operatorname{pr}_{i}
are projections from
A\times B\times B

(iv)

P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\rangle}(\phi)\wedge P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{3}\rangle}(\phi)\leq P_{\langle\operatorname{pr}_{2},\operatorname{pr}_{3}\rangle}(\sigma)

\operatorname{pr}_{i}

of the elementary existential doctrine

Theorem 5.1

The assignment

Two elementary existential doctrines

Central factorial numbers associated with sequences of polynomials
Dae San Kim, Taekyun Kim

Abstract
. Many important special numbers appear in the expansions of some polynomials in terms of central factorials and vice versa, for example central factorial numbers, degenerate central factorial numbers, and central Lah numbers which are recently introduced. Here we generalize this to any sequence of polynomials. Let ℙ = { p n ​ ( x ) } n = 0 ∞ \mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty} be a sequence of polynomials such that deg p n ​ ( x ) = n , p 0 ​ ( x ) = 1 p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1 . The aim of this paper is to study the central factorial numbers of the second associated with ℙ \mathbb{P} and of the first kind associated with ℙ \mathbb{P} , in a unified and systematic way with the help of umbral calculus technique. The central factorial numbers associated with ℙ \mathbb{P} enjoy orthogonality and inverse relations. We illustrate our results with many examples and obtain interesting orthogonality and inverse relations by applying such relations for the central factorial numbers associated with ℙ \mathbb{P} to each of our examples.

1.
Introduction

The central factorial numbers of the second
T_{2}(n,k)
appear as the coefficients in the expansion of the powers of
x
in terms of the central factorials (see (
2.23
), (
3.2
)), whereas the central factorial numbers of the first kind
T_{1}(n,k)
appear as the coefficients in the expansion of the central factorials in terms of powers of
x
(see (
3.1
)). Combinatorial interpretations of central factorial numbers of the second kind are as follows.
T_{2}(n,k)
is equal to the number of partitions of the set
\left\{1,1^{\prime},2,2^{\prime},...,n,n^{\prime}\right\}
into
k
disjoint nonempty subsets
A_{1},...,A_{k}
such that, for each
1\leq j\leq k
, if
i
is the least integer such that either
i
or
i^{\prime}
belongs to
V_{j}
then
\left\{i,i^{\prime}\right\}
is a subset of
A_{j}
. For example,
T_{2}(3,2)=5
. Indeed, they are given by
\left\{1,1^{\prime},2,2^{\prime}\right\}\left\{3,3^{\prime}\right\},\left\{1,1^{\prime},3,3^{\prime}\right\}\left\{2,2^{\prime}\right\},\left\{1,1^{\prime}\right\}\left\{2,2^{\prime},3,3^{\prime}\right\}
,
\left\{1,1^{\prime},3\right\}\left\{2,2^{\prime},3^{\prime}\right\}
, and
\left\{1,1^{\prime},3^{\prime}\right\}\left\{2,2^{\prime},3\right\}
. It is also known that
T_{2}(2n,2n–-2k)
enumerates the number of ways to pace
k
rooks on a 3
D
-triangle board of size
(n-1)
(see [28]). Further, the number of ways to place
k
rooks on a size
n
triangle board is
T_{2}(n+1,n+1-k)
(see [28]). Even though the central factorial numbers are less known than Stirling numbers, they are as important as Stirling numbers.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
.
The aim of this paper is to study the central factorial numbers of the second
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
associated with
\mathbb{P}
, which are defined as the coefficients in the expansion of
p_{n}(x)
in terms of the central factorials
x^{[k]}
. This is done with the help of umbral calculus technique. We illustrate our results with twenty examples. Moreover, we also introduce the central factorial numbers of the first kind
T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
associated with
\mathbb{P}
, which are defined as the coefficients in the expansion of
x^{[n]}
in terms of the polynomials
p_{k}(x)
. Again, we study them by means of umbral calculus and illustrate our results with the same twenty examples for the central factorial numbers of the second kind.
We note here that the orthogonality and inverse relations for the ordinary central factorial numbers also hold for the central factorial numbers associated with sequences of polynomials
\mathbb{P}
. This observation applied to each individual case yields interesting orthogonality and inverse relations for the twenty examples, which are explicitly stated. For example, in the case of the sequence
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\langle{x\rangle}_{n}\right\}
of rising factorials, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}T_{1}(n,m)(-1)^{m-k}S_{2}(m,k)(-1)^{k-j}S_{1}(k,j)T_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}(-1)^{n-j}S_{1}(n,j)T_{2}(j,k)T_{1}(k,m)(-1)^{m-l}S_{2}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l-k}T_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}S_{1}(k,l)T_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}(-1)^{l-n}T_{1}(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)a_{k},

where
S_{1}(n,k),S_{2}(n,k)
are the Stirling numbers of the first kind and the Stirling numbers of the second kind, respectively.
As another example, in the case of the sequence
\mathbb{P}=\left\{L_{n}(x)\right\}
of Laguerre polynomials of order -1, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}T_{1}(n,m)L(m,k)L(k,j)T_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}(-1)^{j-l}L(n,j)T_{2}(j,k)T_{1}(k,m)L(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l}L(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{1}(n,l)L(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}(-1)^{l}L(k,l)T_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}(-1)^{n}\sum_{l=n}^{k}T_{1}(k,l)L(l,n)a_{k},

where
L(n,k)
are the (unsigned) Lah numbers.
We would like to remark that in this investigation of central factorial numbers we are led to discover natural definitions for central logarithm and central exponential associated to any delta series.
The novelty of this paper is that it is the first paper which studies the central factorial numbers of both kinds associated with any sequence of polynomials in a unified and systematic way with the help of umbral calculus.

In [26], Koutras generalized the classical Eulerian numbers and polynomials to
p_{n}
-associated Eulerian numbers and polynomials with motivation of providing a unified approach to the study of Eulerian-related numbers and with combinatorial, probabilistic and statistical applications in mind (see [2,9,10,29,33]). In addition, he mentioned in the same paper that a lot of special numbers, like the Stirling numbers of the second, the Lah numbers and Gould-Hopper numbers, appear in the expansions of some polynomials in terms of the falling factorials. This observation is the motivation and impetus for the present research on the central factorial numbers of both kinds associated with sequences of polynomials (see [26,27]).

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we will briefly go over umbral calculus. In Section 3, we introduce the central factorial numbers of the second associated with sequences of polynomials. We illustrate our results with twenty examples in Section 4. In Section 5, we introduce the central factorial numbers of the first kind associated with sequences of polynomials. We illustrate our results in Section 6 with the same examples from Section 4 and thereby get interesting orthogonality and inverse relations for each case. Finally, we conclude our paper in Section 7.

2.
Review of umbral calculus

Here we will briefly go over very basic facts about umbral calculus. For more details on this, we recommend the reader to refer to [1,8,31-32]. We remark that recently umbral calculus has been extended to the case of degenerate umbral calculus in order to treat degenerate special polynomials and numbers, which involves degenerate exponentials (see [12]).
Let
\mathbb{C}
be the field of complex numbers. Then
\mathcal{F}
denotes the algebra of formal power series in
t
over
\mathbb{C}
, given by

\mathcal{F}=\bigg\{f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}~\bigg|~a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\bigg\},

and
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{C}[x]
indicates the algebra of polynomials in
x
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\mathbb{P}^{*}
be the vector space of all linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
. If
\langle L|p(x)\rangle
denotes the action of the linear functional
L
on the polynomial
p(x)
, then the vector space operations on
\mathbb{P}^{*}
are defined by

\langle L+M|p(x)\rangle=\langle L|p(x)\rangle+\langle M|p(x)\rangle,\quad\langle cL|p(x)\rangle=c\langle L|p(x)\rangle,

where
c
is a complex number.

For
f(t)\in\mathcal{F}
with
\displaystyle f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle
, we define the linear functional on
\mathbb{P}
by

(2.1)

\langle f(t)|x^{k}\rangle=a_{k}.

From (
2.1
), we note that

\langle t^{k}|x^{n}\rangle=n!\delta_{n,k},\quad(n,k\geq 0),

where
\delta_{n,k}
is the Kronecker’s symbol.

Some remarkable linear functionals are as follows:

\displaystyle\langle e^{yt}|p(x)\rangle=p(y),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle e^{yt}-1|p(x)\rangle=p(y)-p(0),

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\frac{e^{yt}-1}{t}\bigg|p(x)\bigg\rangle=\int_{0}^{y}p(u)du.

Let

(2.3)

f_{L}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\langle L|x^{k}\rangle\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

Then, by (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), we get

\langle f_{L}(t)|x^{n}\rangle=\langle L|x^{n}\rangle.

That is,
f_{L}(t)=L
, as linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
. In fact, the map
L\longmapsto f_{L}(t)
is a vector space isomorphism from
\mathbb{P}^{*}
onto
\mathcal{F}
.

Henceforth,
\mathcal{F}
denotes both the algebra of formal power series in
t
and the vector space of all linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
.
\mathcal{F}
is called the umbral algebra and the umbral calculus is the study of umbral algebra.
For each nonnegative integer
k
, the differential operator
t^{k}
on
\mathbb{P}
is defined by

(2.4)

t^{k}x^{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(n)_{k}x^{n-k},&\textrm{if $k\leq n$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $k>n$.}\end{array}\right.

Extending (
2.4
) linearly, any power series

f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}t^{k}\in\mathcal{F}

gives the differential operator on
\mathbb{P}
defined by

(2.5)

f(t)x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}x^{n-k},\quad(n\geq 0).

It should be observed that, for any formal power series
f(t)
and any polynomial
p(x)
, we have

(2.6)

\langle f(t)|p(x)\rangle=\langle 1|f(t)p(x)\rangle=f(t)p(x)|_{x=0}.

Here we note that an element
f(t)
of
\mathcal{F}
is a formal power series, a linear functional and a differential operator. Some notable differential operators are as follows:

\displaystyle e^{yt}p(x)=p(x+y),

(2.7)

\displaystyle(e^{yt}-1)p(x)=p(x+y)-p(x),

\displaystyle\frac{e^{yt}-1}{t}p(x)=\int_{x}^{x+y}p(u)du.

The order
o(f(t))
of the power series
f(t)(\neq 0)
is the smallest integer for which
a_{k}
does not vanish. If
o(f(t))=0
, then
f(t)
is called an invertible series. If
o(f(t))=1
, then
f(t)
is called a delta series.

For
f(t),g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
with
o(f(t))=1
and
o(g(t))=0
, there exists a unique sequence
s_{n}(x)
(deg
s_{n}(x)=n
) of polynomials such that

(2.8)

\big\langle g(t)f(t)^{k}|s_{n}(x)\big\rangle=n!\delta_{n,k},\quad(n,k\geq 0).

The sequence
s_{n}(x)
is said to be the Sheffer sequence for
(g(t),f(t))
, which is denoted by
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
. We observe from (
2.8
) that

(2.9)

s_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{g(t)}q_{n}(x),

where
q_{n}(x)=g(t)s_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t))
.

In particular, if
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),t)
, then
q_{n}(x)=x^{n}
, and hence

(2.10)

s_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{g(t)}x^{n}.

It is well known that
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
if and only if

(2.11)

\frac{1}{g\big(\overline{f}(t)\big)}e^{x\overline{f}(t)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{s_{k}(x)}{k!}t^{k},

for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
, where
\overline{f}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
f(t)
such that
\overline{f}(f(t))=f(\overline{f}(t))=t
.

The following equations (
2.12
), (
2.13
), and (
2.14
) are equivalent to the fact that
s_{n}\left(x\right)
is Sheffer for
\left(g\left(t\right),f\left(t\right)\right)
, for some invertible
g(t)
:

(2.12)

\displaystyle f\left(t\right)s_{n}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=ns_{n-1}\left(x\right),\quad\left(n\geq 0\right),

(2.13)

\displaystyle s_{n}\left(x+y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}s_{j}\left(x\right)q_{n-j}\left(y\right),

with
q_{n}\left(x\right)=g\left(t\right)s_{n}\left(x\right),

(2.14)

s_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\big\langle{g\left(\overline{f}\left(t\right)\right)^{-1}\overline{f}\left(t\right)^{j}}\big|{x^{n}\big\rangle}x^{j}.

If
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
, then the following recurrence relation holds:

(2.15)

s_{n+1}(x)=\bigg(x-\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\bigg)\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(t)}s_{n}(x).

Let
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
. Then, for any polynomial
p(x)
, we have the polynomial exapnsion given by

(2.16)

\displaystyle p(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{g(t)f(t)^{k}|p(x)\big\rangle}s_{k}(x).

and, for any formal power series
h(t)
, we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle h(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\langle{h(t)|s_{k}\rangle}g(t)f(t)^{k}.

For any formal power series
h(t)
and any polynomial
p(x)
, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\langle{h(at)|p(x)\rangle}=\langle{h(t)|p(ax)\rangle}.

The falling factorials
(x)_{n}
are defined by

(2.19)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad(x)_{0}=1.

More generally, for any real number
\lambda
, the generalized falling factorials are defined by

(2.20)

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad(x)_{0,\lambda}=1

For any nonzero real number
\lambda
, the degenerate exponentials
e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)
and the degenerate logarithms
\log_{\lambda}t
are defined by

(2.21)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

(2.22)

\displaystyle\log_{\lambda}t=\frac{1}{\lambda}(t^{\lambda}-1).

We recall that the Stirling numbers of the first kind
S_{1}(n,k)
and of the second kind
S_{2}(n,k)
are respectively given by

(2.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},

(2.24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k}.

As degenerate versions of the Stirling numbers, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
and of the second kind
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are respectively given by

(2.25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},

(2.26)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}.

The central factorials
x^{[n]}
are defined by

(2.27)

\displaystyle x^{[n]}=x(x+\frac{1}{2}n-1)_{n-1},\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad x^{[0]}=1.

Then, we note that (see [30,31])

(2.28)

\displaystyle x^{[n]}\sim(1,f(t)=e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}).

The following facts are well known (see [30,31]).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f_{1}(t)=e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}
, and let
f_{2}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t)
. Then the following hold true.

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\bar{f_{1}}(t)=2\log\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)=\log\big(1+\frac{t}{2}(t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}\big)\big).

\displaystyle(b)\,\,\bar{f_{2}}(t)=\log_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2\lambda}-1\bigg).

\displaystyle(c)\,\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{[n]}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2x}=\big(1+\frac{t}{2}(t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}\big)\big)^{x}.

\displaystyle(d)\,\,e^{yt}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}y^{[k]}.

Proof.

(a), (b) These can be easily checked.

(c) By (
2.28
),
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{[n]}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x\bar{f}(t)}
, with
\bar{f}(t)
as in (a).

(d) This follows from (
2.17
) and (
2.28
).

3.
Central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with sequences of polynomials

The central factorial numbers of the first kind
T_{1}(n,k)
and of second kind
T_{2}(n,k)
are respectively defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(2\log\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\bigg)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log\big(1+\frac{t}{2}(t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}\big)\big)\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad x^{[n]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k)x^{k},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k)x^{[k]}.

We recommend [3-6,30,31] for general references on central factorial numbers. For recent works on central factorial numbers, one refers to [11-13,19-23].
As degenerate versions of the central factorial numbers, the degenerate central factorial numbers of the first kind
T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
and of the second kind
T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are respectively defined by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\left\{\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2\lambda}-1\bigg)\right\}^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad x^{[n]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{[k]}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
.
In (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we observe that
T_{2}(n,k)
and
T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
arise as the coefficients respectively when we expand
x^{n}
and
(x)_{n,\lambda}
in terms of
x^{[k]}
. In view of this observation, it seems natural to define the central factorial numbers of the second associated with
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
as the coefficients when we expand
p_{n}(x)
in terms of
x^{[k]}
:

(3.5)

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{[k]}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
, with
p_{n}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}p_{n,l}x^{l}
. We let

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{[k]}.

(a)   Then the central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with
\mathbb{P}
are given by

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}|p_{n}(x)\big\rangle}.

More explicitly, it is given by

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)p_{n,l}.

(b)
p_{n,l}=\sum_{k=l}^{n}T_{1}(k,l)T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
.

(c)
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
are alternatively given by

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\Big(\frac{d}{dx}\Big)^{l}p_{n}(x)\big|_{x=-\frac{k}{2}}.

(d)  Let
p_{n}(x)
be Sheffer for the pair
(g(t),f(t))
. Then the generating function of
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{1}{2}\bar{f}(t)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\bar{f}(t)}\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k)\frac{\bar{f}(t)^{n}}{n!}.

(e),  Let
\bar{\mathbb{P}}=\left\{\bar{p}_{n}(x)\right\}
, where
\bar{p}_{n}(x)
is the sequence of polynomials defined by
\bar{p}_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\bar{p}_{n}(x)=xp_{n-1}(x),\,\,(n\geq 1)
. Then we have

\displaystyle T_{2}(n+1,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})=T_{2}(n,k-1;\mathbb{P})+\frac{k}{2}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P}),\,\,(0\leq k\leq n+1).

Proof.

(a)   By noting
x^{[n]}\sim(1,e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})
, this follows from (
2.16
).

From (
2.6
), (
2.16
), and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}|p_{n}(x)\big\rangle}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(\frac{d}{dx}\big)^{l}p_{n}(x)|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)p_{n,l}.

(b) The identity follows from the next observation:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n}p_{n,l}x^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{[k]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\sum_{l=0}^{k}T_{1}(k,l)x^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=l}^{n}T_{1}(k,l)T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{l}.

Note here that the explicit expression of
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
also follows from this by inversion.

(c)  From (a) and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}|p_{n}(x)\big\rangle}=\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}e^{-\frac{k}{2}t}\big|p_{n}(x)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\langle{e^{-\frac{k}{2}t}|t^{l}p_{n}(x)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\Big(\frac{d}{dx}\Big)^{l}p_{n}(x)\big|_{x=-\frac{k}{2}}.

(d)   From (
2.11
) and (
3.5
), and recalling that
e^{yt}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{1}{2}t}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}t})^{k}y^{[k]}
from Proposition 2.1 (d), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}u^{[k]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})u^{[k]}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}e^{u\bar{f}(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{1}{2}\bar{f}(t)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\bar{f}(t)})^{k}u^{[k]}.

(e)   This follows from the next observation:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}T_{2}(n+1,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})x^{[k]}

\displaystyle=xp_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(x-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{k}{2})T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{[k]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{[k+1]}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{k}{2}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{[k]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}T_{2}(n,k-1;\mathbb{P})x^{[k]}+\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{k}{2}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{[k]}.

∎

4.
Examples on central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with sequences of polynomials

(a) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{n}\right\}
. Then
x^{n}\sim(1,t)
.
By the definition in (
3.5
) and Theorem 3.1, we have (see [7,30,31])

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=T_{2}(n,k),\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k},

(see (
3.2
)).

(b) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{(x)_{n,\lambda}\right\}
be the sequence of generalized falling factorials. Then
(x)_{n,\lambda}\sim\big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1)\big)
, with
\bar{f}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)
(see [13,14,16,18,23-25]).
By Theorem 3.1, the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t)\big)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

(see (
3.4
)).
Thus
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
. Alternatively, by Theorem 3.1 it is also given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\lambda^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\lambda^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)
.

(c) The rising factorials
\langle x\rangle_{n}
are defined by (see [7,15,30,31])

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad\langle x\rangle_{0}=1.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\langle{x\rangle}_{n}\right\}
. Then
\langle{x\rangle}_{n}\sim(1,1-e^{-t}),
with
\bar{f}(t)=-\log(1-t)
.
From Theorem 3.1, the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big((1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-(1-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{k}.

More explicitly, from Theorem 3.1, it is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k).

(d) The generalized rising factorials are defined by (see [18])

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=x(x+\lambda)\cdots(x+(n-1)\lambda),\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad\langle x\rangle_{0,\lambda}=1.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\langle{x\rangle}_{n,\lambda}\right\}
. Then
\langle{x\rangle}_{n,\lambda}\sim\big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda t})\big)
, with
\bar{f}(t)=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda t)
.
From Theorem 3.1, the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big((1-\lambda t)^{-\frac{1}{2\lambda}}-(1-\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}}\Big)^{k}.

In terms of central factorial numbers of the second, from Theorem 3.1, it is also given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-\lambda)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-\lambda)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k).

(e) Let
\alpha(t)
be the delta series given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\alpha(t)=2\Big(\frac{1}{2-t}-\frac{1}{2+t}\Big)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{2-2n}t^{2n-1}.

Then its compositional inverse is given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}(t)=\frac{2}{t}\big(\sqrt{t^{2}+1}-1\big)=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)_{n}\frac{1}{n!}t^{2n-1}.

H. K. Kim (see [19]) defined the central Lah numbers, which we call the central Lah numbers of the second kind, by

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(\alpha(t))^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L_{2}^{(c)}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

We also define the central Lah numbers of the first kind by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(\bar{\alpha}(t))^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L_{1}^{(c)}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

The central Lah-Bell polynomials of the second, which is called the central Lah-Bell polynomials (see [19]), and the central Lah-Bell polynomials of the first kind are respectively defined by

\displaystyle LB_{n,2}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{2}^{(c)}(n,k)x^{k},\quad LB_{n,1}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{1}^{(c)}(n,k)x^{k}.

Then it is easy to see that their generating functions are given by

\displaystyle e^{x\alpha(t)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}LB_{n,2}^{(c)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad e^{x\bar{\alpha}(t)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}LB_{n,1}^{(c)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Let
f(t)=e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}
. Then its compositional inverse is given by
\bar{f}(t)=2\log\Big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\Big)
.
Define the central factorial Lah numbers of the first kind by

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{f}(\alpha(t))\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(2\log\Big(\frac{1}{2-t}-\frac{1}{2+t}+\sqrt{\big(\frac{1}{2-t}-\frac{1}{2+t}\big)^{2}+1}\Big)\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}TL_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Now, we observe from (
3.1
) and (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}TL_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}(\alpha(t))^{l}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(l,k)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}L_{2}^{(c)}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{1}(l,k)L_{2}^{(c)}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have shown that

\displaystyle TL_{1}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}L_{2}^{(c)}(n,l)T_{1}(l,k).

Then we define the central factorial Lah Bell polynomials of the first kind
TLB_{n,1}(x)
as

\displaystyle TLB_{n,1}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}TL_{1}(n,k)x^{k}.

Then it is immediate to see from (
4.5
) that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}TLB_{n,1}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x\bar{f}(\alpha(t))}.

Now, we observe that

\displaystyle TLB_{n,1}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}L_{2}^{(c)}(n,l)T_{1}(l,k)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}L_{2}^{(c)}(n,l)\sum_{k=0}^{l}T_{1}(l,k)x^{k}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}L_{2}^{(c)}(n,l)x^{[l]}.

Hence we have shown that
TLB_{n,1}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}L_{2}^{(c)}(n,l)x^{[l]}
, which says that
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P_{1}})=L_{2}^{(c)}(n,k)
, with
\mathbb{P_{1}}=\left\{TLB_{n,1}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. We note that an explicit expression of this polynomial is obtained in Theorem 8 of [19].

To proceed further, we define the central factorial Lah numbers of the second kind as

(4.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(\bar{\alpha}(f(t)))^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{2}{e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}}\Big(\sqrt{\big(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\big)^{2}+1}-1\Big)\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{2}{e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}}\Big(\sqrt{e^{t}+e^{-t}-1}-1\Big)\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}TL_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Now, we observe from (
3.2
) and (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}TL_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}L_{1}^{(c)}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}(f(t))^{l}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}L_{1}^{(c)}(l,k)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(n,l)L_{1}^{(c)}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have shown that

\displaystyle TL_{2}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(n,l)L_{1}^{(c)}(l,k).

We define the central factorial Lah Bell polynomials of the second kind
TLB_{n,2}(x)
as

\displaystyle TLB_{n,2}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}TL_{2}(n,k)x^{k}.

Then it is easy to see from (
4.7
) that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}TLB_{n,2}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x\bar{\alpha}(f(t))}.

Now, we let
\mathbb{P_{2}}=\left\{TLB_{n,2}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. Then, from Theorem 3.1 we have
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P_{2}})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)TL_{2}(n,l)
.

(f) The central Bell polynomials
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)
and the central Bell numbers
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}
are respectively defined by (see [22])

\displaystyle e^{x(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}=\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(1).

Then we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\sim\Big(1,f(t)=2\log\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\Big),\quad\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k)x^{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\right\}
. Then, by Theorem 3.1, we see that

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k).

Note here that the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e^{\frac{1}{2}(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})}\Big)^{k}.

(g) The numbers
T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
and
T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are respectively called the degenerate central factorial numbers of the first kind and of the second kind, and defined by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) (see [23]).
The degenerate central Bell polynomials
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)
and the degenerate central Bell numbers
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}
are respectively defined by (see [23])

\displaystyle e^{x(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t))}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}=\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(1).

Then we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)\sim\Big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2\lambda}-1\bigg)\Big),\quad\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\right\}
. Then, by Theorem 3.1, we see that

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)T_{2,\lambda}(n,l).

We note here that the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e^{\frac{1}{2}\big(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t)\big)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\big(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t)\big)}\Big)^{k}.

(h) Let
x^{[n,\lambda]}
be defined by

\displaystyle x^{[n,\lambda]}=x\big(x+(\frac{1}{2}n-1)\lambda\big)_{n-1,\lambda},\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad x^{[0,\lambda]}=1.

where
x^{[n,\lambda]}\sim\big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\frac{\lambda t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}})\big)
. Here we note that

\displaystyle\bar{f}(t)=\frac{2}{\lambda}\log\bigg(\frac{\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\big(1+\frac{\lambda t}{2}(\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4})\big).

As analogies to the numbers
T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
and
T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
, we may introduce the numbers
R_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
and
R_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
which are defined by

\displaystyle x^{[n,\lambda]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{1,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}R_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{2}{\lambda}\log\bigg(\frac{\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\bigg)^{k},

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{[k,\lambda]},\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}R_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(e^{\frac{\lambda t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}}\big)\Big)^{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{[n,\lambda]}\right\}
. From Theorem 3.1, we get

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)R_{1,\lambda}(n,l).

From Theorem 3.1, the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}-\bigg(\frac{\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}\bigg)^{k}.

(i) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n}^{L}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Lah-Bell polynomials. Then it is given by
e^{x(\frac{t}{1-t})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{L}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
, so that
B_{n}^{L}(x)\sim(1,\frac{t}{1+t})
, and
B_{n}^{L}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)x^{k}
. Here
L(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{n!}{k!}
are the (unsigned) Lah numbers (see [7,15,30,31]) whose generating function is given by
\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(\frac{t}{1-t}\big)^{k}
. By Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)L(n,l).

In addition, by Theorem 3.1, the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{t}{2(1-t)}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2(1-t)}})^{k}.

(j) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of degenerate Lah-Bell polynomials. Then it is given by
e_{\lambda}^{x}(\frac{t}{1-t})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
. Thus we see that
B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)\sim(1,f(t)=\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda+e^{\lambda t}-1})
, with
\bar{f}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\frac{\lambda t}{1-t})
(see [18]). By Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\big(1+\frac{\lambda t}{1-t}\big)\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{\lambda^{l}}\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}S_{1}(m,l)\frac{1}{m!}\big(\frac{\lambda t}{1-t}\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}\lambda^{m-l}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}L(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\lambda^{m-l}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)L(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we obtain
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\lambda^{m-l}L(n,m)S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k).

Alternatively, the generating function is also given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\Big(1+\frac{\lambda t}{1-t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}}-\Big(1+\frac{\lambda t}{1-t}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2\lambda}}\Big)^{k}.

(k) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bell polynomials given by
e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
. Then
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\sim(1,\log(1+t))
, and
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)x^{k}
(see [7,30,31]). By Theorem 3.1, the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{1}{2}(e^{t}-1)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}(e^{t}-1)}\big)^{k}.

More explicitly, from Theorem 3.1, we get

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k).

(l) Let
\mathbb{P_{1}}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of partially degenerate Bell polynomials given by
e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
. Then
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\sim(1,\log_{\lambda}(1+t))
, and
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}
(see [24]). From Theorem 3.1, it is immediate to see that

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P_{1}})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k).

The generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P_{1}})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{1}{2}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}\big)^{k}.

There are several other degenerate versions of Bell polynomials, one of which is the so called fully degenerate Bell polynomials
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)
given by
e_{\lambda}^{x}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.
Then
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\sim\big(1,f(t)=\log_{\lambda}(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1))\big)
, with
\bar{f}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big)

(see [25]). Let
\mathbb{P_{2}}=\left\{\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\right\}
. From Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P_{2}})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\big(1+\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big)\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{\lambda^{l}}\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}S_{1}(m,l)\frac{1}{m!}\big(\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)S_{1}(m,l)\lambda^{m-l}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\lambda^{m-l}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

which verifies that we have
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P_{2}})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\lambda^{m-l}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k).

Alternatively, the generating function is also given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P_{2}})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\big(1+\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big)^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}}-\big(1+\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)\big)^{-\frac{1}{2\lambda}}\Big)^{k}.

(m) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{M_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Mittag-Leffler polynomials. That is,
M_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t)=\frac{e^{t}-1}{e^{t}+1})
, with
\bar{f}(t)=\log\big(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\big)
(see [31]).
By Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\Big(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\Big(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{k}.

Alternatively, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{2t}{1-t}\Big)\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}S_{1}(m,l)2^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\Big(\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}S_{1}(m,l)2^{m}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}L(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}2^{m}L(n,m)S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

This shows that
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}2^{m}L(n,m)S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)
.

(n) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{L_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Laguerre polynomials of order -1 (see [31]).
That is,
L_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t)=\frac{t}{t-1})
, with
\bar{f}(t)=\frac{t}{t-1}
. By Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)(-1)^{l}\frac{1}{l!}\Big(\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)(-1)^{l}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}L(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l}L(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l}L(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)
. Moreover, the generating function is also given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{t}{2(t-1)}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2(t-1)}}\big)^{k}.

(o) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bernoulli polynomials. Then
B_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{e^{t}-1}{t},t\big)
, and
B_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{n-k}x^{k}
(see [31]). By Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)\binom{n}{l}B_{n-l}=\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}T_{2}(n-l,k)B_{l},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}.

(p) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{E_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Euler polynomials. Then
E_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{e^{t}+1}{2},t\big)
(see [31]).
Analogously to (c), by Theorem 3.1 we see that

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(l,k)\binom{n}{l}E_{n-l}=\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}T_{2}(n-l,k)E_{l},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{2}{e^{t}+1}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}.

(q) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{(rx+s)_{n}\right\}
, with
r\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle(rx+s)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}G(n,k;r,s)(x)_{k},

where
G(n,k;r,s)
are called the Gould-Hopper numbers (see [26]).
We note that
(rx+s)_{n}\sim\big(e^{-\frac{s}{r}t},f(t)=e^{\frac{t}{r}}-1\big)
, where
\bar{f}(t)=r\log(1+t)
. By Theorem 3.1, the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=(1+t)^{s}\frac{1}{k!}\big((1+t)^{\frac{r}{2}}-(1+t)^{-\frac{r}{2}}\big)^{k}.

Alternatively, from Theorem 3.1 the generating function is also given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{s}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)r^{l}\frac{1}{l!}(\log(1+t))^{l}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{s}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)r^{l}\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}S_{1}(m,l)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(s)_{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{m}r^{l}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\binom{n}{m}r^{l}(s)_{n-m}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\binom{n}{m}r^{l}(s)_{n-m}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k).

(r) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{b_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bernoulli polynomials of the second kind. Then
b_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1},f(t)=e^{t}-1\big)
, with
\bar{f}(t)=\log(1+t)
, so that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t}{\log(1+t)}(1+t)^{x}
(see [31]). From Theorem 3.1, the generating function is

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t}{\log(1+t)}\frac{1}{k!}\big((1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}-(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{k}.

Alternatively, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\log(1+t)}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}(\log(1+t))^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\log(1+t)}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}S_{1}(m,l)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{m}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\binom{n}{m}b_{n-m}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\binom{n}{m}b_{n-m}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k),

where
b_{n}=b_{n}(0)
are the Bernoulli numbers of the second.

(s) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{C_{n}(x;a)\right\}
be the sequence of Poisson-Charlier polynomials. Here
C_{n}(x;a)\sim\big(e^{a(e^{t}-1)},f(t)=a(e^{t}-1)\big)
, with
a\neq 0
. Then
\bar{f}(t)=\log(1+\frac{t}{a})
, and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}(x;a)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{-t}\big(1+\frac{t}{a}\big)^{x}
(see [31]). From Theorem 3.1, the generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{-t}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\Big(1+\frac{t}{a}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\Big(1+\frac{t}{a}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{k}.

Another expression for generating function follows from Theorem 3.1, which is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log(1+\frac{t}{a})\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}S_{1}(m,l)a^{-m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{m}a^{-m}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\binom{n}{m}(-1)^{n-m}a^{-m}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we see that

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{n}\binom{n}{m}(-1)^{n-m}a^{-m}S_{1}(m,l)T_{2}(l,k).

(t) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
, with
p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{k}(x)B_{n-k}(x)
. This is not a Sheffer sequence. For this, we recall from [17] that

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}B_{m}(x)+(n+1)B_{n}(x).

By (
2.12
) and (
3.2
), we note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}|B_{n}(x)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}T_{2}(n-l,k)B_{l}.

By Theorem 3.1, we see that

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}|p_{n}(x)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}|B_{m}(x)\rangle}+(n+1)\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{k}|B_{n}(x)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}\sum_{l=0}^{m-k}\binom{m}{l}T_{2}(m-l,k)B_{l}+(n+1)\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}T_{2}(n-l,k)B_{l}.

This says that the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{k}(x)B_{n-k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\{\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}\sum_{l=0}^{m-k}\binom{m}{l}T_{2}(m-l,k)B_{l}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad+(n+1)\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}T_{2}(n-l,k)B_{l}\right\}x^{[k]}.

5.
Central factorial numbers of the first kind associated with sequences of polynomials

As in the ‘classical’ case, we would like to introduce the central factorial numbers of the first kind associated with sequences of polynomials. Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
.
In view of our definition of the central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, it is natural to define the central factorial numbers of the first kind associated with
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
as the coefficients when we expand
x^{[n]}
in terms of
p_{k}(x)
:

(5.1)

\displaystyle x^{[n]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
. We let

\displaystyle x^{[n]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x).

(a)   Let
\bar{\mathbb{P}}=\left\{\bar{p}_{n}(x)\right\}
, where
\bar{p}_{n}(x)
is the sequence of polynomials defined by
\bar{p}_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\bar{p}_{n}(x)=xp_{n-1}(x),\,\,(n\geq 1)
. Then we have

\displaystyle T_{1}(n+1,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})=T_{1}(n,k-1;\mathbb{P})-\frac{n}{2}T_{1}(n,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}}).

(b)   Assume that
p_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
. Then the central factorial numbers of the first kind associated with
\mathbb{P}
are given by

\displaystyle T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\frac{1}{k!}\langle{g(t)(f(t))^{k}|x^{[n]}\rangle}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{1}(n,l)\langle{g(t)f(t)^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}.

(c)
\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(1)=(\frac{n}{2})_{n-1},\,\,(n\geq 1).

Proof.

(a)  This follows from the next observation:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}T_{1}(n+1,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})\bar{p}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{[n]}\big(x-\frac{n}{2}\big)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x)-\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})\bar{p}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\bar{p}_{k+1}(x)-\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})\bar{p}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}T_{1}(n,k-1;\mathbb{P})\bar{p}_{k}(x)-\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}T_{1}(n,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})\bar{p}_{k}(x).

(b)   This follows from (
2.16
) and (
3.1
).

(c)   This is clear by definition in (
5.1
) and (
2.27
).
∎

For any delta series
f(t)
, we define the
central logarithm associated to
f(t)
by

\displaystyle LC_{f(t)}t=f\big(2\log\Big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\Big)\big),

In case
p_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t))
,
LC_{f(t)}t
may be also denoted by
LC_{\mathbb{P}}t
and called the
central logarithm associated to
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
. Recalling that
x^{[n]}\sim(1,l(t)=e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})
, we remark that
LC_{f(t)}t=f(\bar{l}(t))
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
p_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t)),
and let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
. Then the generating function of the central factorial numbers of the first kind associated with
\mathbb{P}
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(LC_{f(t)}t)^{k},

where
LC_{f(t)}t=LC_{\mathbb{P}}t=f\big(2\log\Big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\Big)\big)
is the central logarithm associated to
f(t)
.

Proof.

Assume now that
p_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t))
. Then we observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(x)\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{[n]}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\Big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\Big)^{2x}.

On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(x)\frac{1}{k!}\big(LC_{f(t)}t\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=e^{x\bar{f}\big(f\big(2\log\big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\big)\big)\big)}=\Big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\Big)^{2x},

where we used the fact that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}=e^{x\bar{f}(t)}
. This shows the assertion.

We would like to give another proof for this by using Theorem 5.1 (b).

Here we have to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\langle{f(t)^{k}|x^{[n]}\rangle}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(f\big(2\log\Big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\Big)\big)\big)^{k}.

Equivalently, we need to verify that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\langle{f(t)^{k}|x^{[n]}\rangle}\frac{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{n}}{n!}=(f(t))^{k}.

The left hand side of (
5.2
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{1}(n,l)\langle{f(t)^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}\frac{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\langle{f(t)^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}T_{1}(n,l)\frac{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\langle{f(t)^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}=(f(t))^{k}.

∎

The following orthogonality and inverse relations can be easily shown from the definitions of central factorial numbers associated with
\mathbb{P}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
. Then we have

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})T_{2}(k,l;\mathbb{P})=\delta_{n,l},\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})T_{1}(k,l;\mathbb{P})=\delta_{n,l},\,\,(0\leq l\leq n).

\displaystyle(b)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})a_{k}.

\displaystyle(c)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}T_{2}(k,n;\mathbb{P})c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}T_{1}(k,n;\mathbb{P})a_{k}.

Remark 5.4
.

Let
p_{n}(x)
be Sheffer for the pair
(g(t),f(t))
. Then we saw in Theorem 3.2 that the generating function of
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{1}{2}\bar{f}(t)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\bar{f}(t)})^{k}.

Let
f(t)
be any delta series. After introducing the central logarithm associated to
f(t)
, it seems natural to introduce the central exponential associated to
f(t)
as

\displaystyle EC_{f(t)}t=e^{\frac{1}{2}\bar{f}(t)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\bar{f}(t)}.

When
p_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
,
EC_{f(t)}t
may be also denoted by
EC_{\mathbb{P}}t
and called the central exponential associated to
\mathbb{P}
. Recalling that
x^{[n]}\sim(1,l(t)=e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})
, we observe that
EC_{f(t)}t=l(\bar{f}(t))
.

As an example, consider the delta series
f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1)
. Here
\bar{f}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)
. Thus we see that

(5.3)

\displaystyle LC_{f(t)}t=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2\lambda}-1\bigg)=\log_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2},

(5.4)

\displaystyle EC_{f(t)}t=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}}-(1+\lambda t)^{-\frac{1}{2\lambda}}=e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t).

Here we note that, in many of the papers on degenerate special polynomials and numbers,
\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(t^{\lambda}-1\big)
is called the degenerate logarithm and denoted by
\log_{\lambda}t
, and
(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}
is called the degenerate exponential and denoted by
e_{\lambda}(t)
.

6.
Examples on central factorial numbers of the first kind associated with sequences of polynomials

(a) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{n}\right\}
. Then
x^{n}\sim(1,t)
, and
T_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=T_{2}(n,k)
. As
x^{[n]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{k}
, we have (see [7,30,31])

\displaystyle T_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=T_{1}(n,k),\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(2\log\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\bigg)^{k},

(see (
3.1
)). Now, by Theorem 5.3, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}T_{1}(n,k)T_{2}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}T_{2}(n,k)T_{1}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\,\,(0\leq l\leq n),

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}T_{2}(k,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}T_{1}(k,n)a_{k}.

(b) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{(x)_{n,\lambda}\right\}
be the sequence of generalized falling factorials. Then
(x)_{n,\lambda}\sim\big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1)\big)
, and

7.
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied central factorial numbers of both kinds associated with sequences of polynomilas by using umbral calculus techniques, which was motivated by the observation that many important special numbers appear in the expansions of some polynomials in terms of central factorials and vice versa. Our results were illustrated by twenty examples both for the second kind and for the first kind. In all cases of these examples, some interesting orthogonality and inverse relations were obtained as immediate consequences of our results. The novelty of this paper is that it is the first paper which studies the central factorial numbers of both kinds associated with any sequence of polynomials in a unified and systematic way with the help of umbral calculus.

It is one of our future projects to study special numbers and polynomials by making use of various tools, including combinatorial methods, generating functions, umbral calculus,
p
-adic analysis, probability theory, differential equations, analytic number theory, operator theory, special functions and so on.

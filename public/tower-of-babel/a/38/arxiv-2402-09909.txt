A Characterization of Zero Divisors and Topological Divisors of Zero in 𝐂 ⁡ [ 𝐚 , 𝐛 ] \mathbf{C[{a,b}]} and ℓ ∞ \ell^{\infty}
Harish Chandra, Anurag Kumar Patel

Abstract
. We give a characterization of zero divisors of the ring C ⁡ [ a , b ] . C[a,b]. Using the Weierstrass approximation theorem, we completely characterize topological divisors of zero of the Banach algebra C ⁡ [ a , b ] . C[a,b]. We also characterize the zero divisors and topological divisors of zero in ℓ ∞ . \ell^{\infty}. Further, we show that zero is the only zero divisor in the disk algebra 𝒜 ⁡ ( 𝔻 ) \mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D}) and that the class of singular elements in 𝒜 ⁡ ( 𝔻 ) \mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D}) properly contains the class of topological divisors of zero. Lastly, we construct a class of topological divisors of zero of 𝒜 ⁡ ( 𝔻 ) \mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D}) which are not zero divisors.

Lemma 5
.

The set of all topological divisors of zero in a Banach algebra is a closed set.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}

\varphi(a)=\inf_{\|b\|=1}\|ab\|~~\forall a\in\mathcal{A}.

Then we observe that

is a topological divisor of zero if and only if
\varphi(a)=0.
To get the desired conclusion, it is sufficient to prove that
\varphi
is continuous. To this end, let
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{A}
such that
a_{n}\to a
as
n\to\infty.
Let
\epsilon>0.
Then there exists

with
\|b\|=1
such that

(1)

\varphi(a)\leq\|ab\|<\varphi(a)+\epsilon.

Further, we also have
\varphi(a_{n})\leq\|a_{n}b\|
for all
b
with
\|b\|=1
and
\mbox{ for all }n\geq 1.
This together with (
1
) implies that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varphi(a_{n})\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|a_{n}b\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a_{n}b\|=\|ab\|<\varphi(a)+\epsilon,

as
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we get that
\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}~\varphi(a_{n})\leq\varphi(a).

Next, let
\epsilon>0.
Pick a sequence
(b_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{A}
with
\|b_{n}\|=1
such that

(2)

\|a_{n}b_{n}\|<\varphi(a_{n})+\epsilon~~~\forall n\geq 1.

Also, we have

|\|a_{n}b_{n}\|-\|ab_{n}\||\leq\|(a_{n}-a)b_{n}\|\leq\|a_{n}-a\|\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

This gives that for sufficiently large

This together with (

Thus, we must have

This completes the proof.

x-c,&\mbox{ if }c<x\leq\frac{c+d}{2};\\
d-x,&\mbox{ if }\frac{c+d}{2}\leq x<d.\end{cases}

The following theorem gives a complete characterization of the topological divisors of zero in
C[a,b]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

An element
f\in C[a,b]
is a topological divisor of zero if and only if
f
has at least one zero in
[a,b].

Proof.

Let
f\in C[a,b]
which has a zero, say
f(c)=0
for some
c\in[a,b]
. Since
f
is continuous, by the Weierstrass approximation theorem,
for given
\epsilon>0,
there exists a polynomial
p(x)
such that

|f(x)-p(x)|<\epsilon/2\quad\forall~x\in[a,b]

This implies

|f(c)-p(c)|<\epsilon/2,

Thus

|p(c)|<\epsilon/2.

Consider the polynomial
q(x)=p(x)-p(c)
. Then
q(c)=0

and

|f(x)-q(x)|=|f(x)-p(x)+p(c)|\leq|f(x)-p(x)|+|p(c)|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}=\epsilon.

Hence we can find a sequence of polynomials
(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
C[a,b]
such that
q_{n}(c)=0\ \forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}
and
q_{n}\to f
uniformly on
[a,b].

Since
q_{n}(c)=0
,
q_{n}(x)=(x-c)r_{n}(x),

where
r_{n}(x)
is a polynomial in
C[a,b]
.

Now
z(x)=x-c
is a topological divisor of zero, therefore by the Lemma
6
,
q_{n}
is a topological divisor of zero for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Since
q_{n}\to f
uniformly and by Lemma
5
, the class of topological divisors of zero is a closed set, it follows that
f
is a topological divisor of zero.

Conversely,
suppose
f\in C[a,b]
is a topological divisor of zero. Suppose that
f
has no zero in
[a,b]
. Then,
\frac{1}{f}\in C[a,b]
. Let
g(x)=\frac{1}{f(x)}
, then
g(x)f(x)=1\ \forall\ x\in[a,b]

is a topological divisor of zero, there exists a sequence
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in

with
\|f_{n}\|=1\ \forall\ n\in\mathbb{N},
such that

.
Since

Theorem 3.1
.

An element
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{\infty}
is a regular element if and only if
x
is bounded away from zero.

Proof.

Let
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{\infty}
be a regular element, then there exists an element
y=(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\ell^{\infty}
such that
xy=(1,1,...,1,...)=1
. That is
x_{n}y_{n}=1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
This implies that,
y_{n}=\frac{1}{x_{n}}~\forall~n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
Since
y\in\ell^{\infty},

\exists~M>0
such that
|y_{n}|\leq M~\forall~n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
\frac{1}{M}\leq|x_{n}|~\forall~n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
Hence
x
is bounded away from zero.

Conversely, let
x\in\ell^{\infty}
be bounded away from zero. Then there exists a positive constant
M
such that
M\leq|x_{n}|
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
This implies That
\frac{1}{|x_{n}|}\leq\frac{1}{M}~\forall~n\geq 1.
Now choosing
y=(\frac{1}{x_{n}})_{n=1}^{\infty},
we get
y=(y_{n})\in\ell^{\infty}
and
xy=1
. Hence
x
is a regular element of
\ell^{\infty}
.

4.
Zero divisors and Topological divisors of zero in the disk algebra
\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D})

In this section, we show that zero is the only zero divisor in the disk algebra
\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D}).
We also give a class of singular elements in
\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D}),
which are not topological divisors of zero. In the end, we give a class of topological divisors of zero in
\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D}),
which are not zero divisors.

Proposition 1
.

In the disk algebra
\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D})
zero is the only zero divisor.

Proof.

Suppose
0\not\equiv f\in\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D})
is a zero divisor. Then there exists
0\neq g\in\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D})
such that
(fg)(z)=0

\forall\ z\in\mathbb{D}.
Since
f
is continuous and
f\not\equiv 0,
there exists a
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
f(z)\neq 0
in an open disk centered at
z_{0},
say
\mathbb{D}_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{D}.
Since
(fg)(z)=0

\forall\ z\in\mathbb{D}_{1}.
It follows that
g(z)=0

\forall\ z\in\mathbb{D}_{1}
. By Identity principle,
g(z)=0

\forall z\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}.
Thus a non-zero element in
\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{D})
can not be a zero divisor.
∎

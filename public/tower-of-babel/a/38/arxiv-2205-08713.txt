Prime ideals in categories of representations of quivers of type A A
Shunsuke Tada

Abstract
. We study prime tensor ideals in tensor abelian categories of quiver representations. Specifically, we classify the prime tensor ideals in the category of representations of zigzag quivers (with bounded path length) whose vertex set is the set of integers. We show that prime tensor ideals in these categories are in canonical bijection with prime ideals of a Boolean algebra, the power set of integers.

For example, see

]

]

]

[

for an application to mathematics.

The category of representations of a quiver is a tensor (monoidal) category, that is, an abelian category with a monoidal structure. In this category, we can consider prime
\otimes
-ideals (see Definition

) as defined by Tobias J. Peter in
[

. This concept is related to the tensor triangulated geometry developed by Paul Balmer

.

In this paper, we classify the prime
\otimes
-ideals of the category
\operatorname{Rep}^{\operatorname{pfd}}(A_{\mathbb{Z}})
of point-wise finite-dimensional representations (Definition

These prime
\otimes

\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/{2\mathbb{Z}})
. We will obtain the relationship between the prime
\otimes
-ideals of

and the prime ideals of
\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/{2\mathbb{Z}})
using the power set

of the set of integers

. We will obtain following theorem.

.

Let

be a quiver of type

with bounded path length. Then, there is a one-to-one correspondence between each of the three sets

(1)

prime tensor ideals of the category
\operatorname{Rep}^{\operatorname{pfd}}(A_{\mathbb{Z}})

(2)

prime ideals of the ring
\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/{2\mathbb{Z}})
, and

(3)

prime ideals of the boolean algebra

In this theorem, we need the condition

. For example, if the quiver has an infinite length path like

then we will have an inclusion map from the prime ideals of

to the prime

-ideals of

Definition 2.7

(Prime tensor ideal

Let

of morphisms between objects of

Suppose now that
\mathcal{A}
is a tensor abelian category. A

or

.

\operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal{A})
.

Next, we show that

Definition 2.11

(Boolean algebra)
.

Let
B

(B;\lor,\ \land,\ ^{\prime},\ 0,\ 1)
such that

(i)

(i)

,

(ii)

\in J
.

An ideal is

.
A proper ideal

. If we show
\mathcal{I}
is a
\otimes
-ideal, then the tensor unit
I
is in
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I}
by the maximality of
\mathcal{M}
, and therefore, we can show
W^{\prime}=I\otimes W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}.
So it is enough to show that
\mathcal{I}
is a
\otimes
-ideal to prove
\mathcal{M}
is a prime
\otimes
-ideal.

First, we will show that
\mathcal{I}
is closed under extensions. Let
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
be in
\mathcal{I}
. For any short exact sequence

0\to V^{\prime}\to V\to V^{\prime\prime}\to 0,

there exists an exact sequence

0\to V^{\prime}\otimes W^{\prime}\to V\otimes W^{\prime}\to V^{\prime\prime}\otimes W^{\prime}\to 0

such that
V^{\prime}\otimes W^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}\otimes W^{\prime}
are in the
\otimes
-ideal
\mathcal{M}.
Thus, the representation
V\otimes W^{\prime}
is in
\mathcal{M}
and therefore
V
is in
\mathcal{I}
.

Next, we will show that
\mathcal{I}
is closed under multiplication. Let
V
be in
\mathcal{A}
and
V^{\prime}
be in
\mathcal{I}
. We have
(V\otimes V^{\prime})\otimes W^{\prime}=V\otimes(V^{\prime}\otimes W^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}
and therefore
(V\otimes V^{\prime})\in\mathcal{I}.

Then, we will show that
\mathcal{I}
is closed under kernels and cokernels. Let
f:V\to V^{\prime}
be in
\mathcal{I}
. Then, we have the monomorphism
ker(f)\otimes id_{W^{\prime}}:\operatorname{Ker}(f)\otimes W^{\prime}\hookrightarrow V\otimes W^{\prime}
such that
V\otimes W^{\prime}
is in
\mathcal{M}
. Thus
(\operatorname{Ker}(f)\otimes W^{\prime})\otimes(V\otimes W^{\prime})
is in
\mathcal{M}
. By Lemma
3.15
, we have
\operatorname{Ker}(f)\otimes W^{\prime}
as a direct summand of
(\operatorname{Ker}(f)\otimes W^{\prime})\otimes(V\otimes W^{\prime})
and since
\mathcal{M}
is a
\otimes
-ideal, the representation
\operatorname{Ker}(f)\otimes W^{\prime}
is in
\mathcal{M}
. Hence
\operatorname{Ker}(f)
is in
\mathcal{I}
and therefore
\mathcal{I}
is closed under kernels. Similarly we can show that
\mathcal{I}
is closed under cokernels.

We have showed
\mathcal{I}
is a
\otimes
-ideal.
∎

Next corollary is what we wanted.

Corollary 3.22
.

There exists a prime
\otimes
-ideal of
\operatorname{Rep}^{\operatorname{pfd}}(\vec{A_{\mathbb{Z}}})
which contains a representation whose support is
\mathbb{Z}
.

Thus the map

\displaystyle\psi:\operatorname{Spc}(\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{Z}}))

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Spc}(\operatorname{Rep}^{\operatorname{pfd}}(A_{\mathbb{Z}})),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle\mapsto\{V\in\operatorname{Rep}^{\operatorname{pfd}}(A_{\mathbb{Z}}):\operatorname{supp}(V)\in\mathcal{Q}\}

constructed in Proposition
3.2
is not surjective.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.20
, there exists a maximal
\otimes
-ideal of
\operatorname{Rep}^{\operatorname{pfd}}(\vec{A_{\mathbb{Z}}})
that contains a representation whose support is
\mathbb{Z}
and the maximal
\otimes
-ideal is prime
\otimes
-ideal by Corollary
3.21
.
∎

Abstract
. Presilting and silting subcategories in extriangulated categories were introduced by Adachi and Tsukamoto recently. In this paper, we prove that the Gabriel-Zisman localization ℬ / ( 𝗍𝗁𝗂𝖼𝗄 ​ 𝒲 ) \mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W}) of an extriangulated category ℬ \mathcal{B} with respect to a presilting subcategory 𝒲 \mathcal{W} satisfying certain condition can be realized as a subfactor category of ℬ \mathcal{B} . This generalizes the result by Iyama-Yang for silting reduction on triangulated categories. Then we discuss the relation between silting subcategories and tilting subcategories in extriangulated categories, this gives us a kind of important examples of our results. In particular, for a finite dimensional Gorenstein algebra, we get the relative version of the description of the singularity category due to Happel and Chen-Zhang by this reduction.

1.
Introduction

The concept of
silting
appeared for the first time in a paper of Keller-Vossieck
[
KV
]
. Nowadays, silting theory and silting reduction play important roles in the research of triangulated categories, especially derived categories. In
[
KY
]
, it is shown that silting objects have correspondence with many important structures such as
t
-structures, co-
t
-structures, simple-minded collections. Silting reduction is shown to have a close relation with Calabi-Yau reduction
[
IY
,
IY1
]
and is widely used in the representation theory. An overview of recent developments in silting theory can be found in
[
A
]
. Motivated by these fruitful results, we are wondering if we can study such important theory under a more general setting.

Extriangulated category was introduced by Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
by extracting the relevant properties of
{\rm\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits}^{1}
on exact categories and triangulated categories. In particular, triangulated categories and exact categories are extriangulated categories. An important source of examples of extriangulated categories which are neither triangulated nor exact is extension closed subcategories of triangulated categories. To find more examples of extriangulated categories, see
[
NP
,
ZZ
,
HZZ
,
ZhZ
,
NP1
]
.

Adachi and Tsukamoto
[
AT
]
introduced the notion of presilting and silting subcategories (see Definition
2.7
and Definition
2.12
) in extriangulated categories as a generalization of presilting and silting subcategories in triangulated categories. Note that in module categories, this notion is different from the concept of silting module in the sense of Angeleri Hügel, Marks and Vitória
[
AMV
]
.

Throughout this paper,
k
denotes a field and
\mathcal{B}
denotes a Krull-Schmidt, Hom-finite,
k
-linear extriangulated category with enough projectives and enough injectives and satisfies
[
NP
, Condition 5.8]
(see condition
(WIC)
in page 4). Cotorsion pairs in extriangulated categories were introduced by Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
as a generalization of those in triangulated categories and exact categories. We recall its definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
be a pair of full subcategories of
\mathcal{B}
which are closed under direct sums and direct summands.
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
are called a cotorsion pairs if it satisfies the following conditions.

(a)

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=0
.

(b)

For any object
B\in\mathcal{B}
, there exist two
\mathbb{E}
-triangles

V_{B}\to U_{B}\to B\dashrightarrow,\quad B\to V^{B}\to U^{B}\dashrightarrow

where
U_{B},U^{B}\in\mathcal{U}
and
V_{B},V^{B}\in\mathcal{V}
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a presilting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
which satisfies the following condition (the readers can find the definitions of the notions in Definition
2.3
and Page 5):

(CP):

(\mathcal{W}^{\vee},\mathcal{W}^{\bot})
and
({{}^{\bot}}\mathcal{W},\mathcal{W}^{\wedge})
are cotorsion pairs in
\mathcal{B}
.

We show that
\mathcal{Z}:={{}^{\bot}}\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{W}^{\bot}
is a Frobenius extriangulated category where
\mathcal{W}
is the subcategory of projective-injective objects (see Lemma
3.5
). Hence by
[
ZZ
, Theorem 3.13]
, there is an induced triangulated structure on
\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{W}]
. One the other hand, let
\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W}
be the thick subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
generated by
\mathcal{W}
, and
\mathcal{R}
be the class of morphisms in
\mathcal{B}
defined as following:

\mathcal{R}=\{f:A\to B\mid\text{ there exists an }\mathbb{E}\mbox{-triangle}\text{ }A\xrightarrow{f}B\to M\dashrightarrow\text{ with }M\in\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W}\}.

Assume that
\mathcal{B}
is skeletally small. We show that there is a natural triangulated structure on the Gabriel-Zisman localization
\mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W})
of
\mathcal{B}
with respect to
\mathcal{R}
. The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorems
3.14
and
4.4
, and Corollary
4.5
)
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a presilting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
which satisfies condition
(CP)
. There is a triangle equivalence

\mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W})\simeq\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{W}].

Moreover, this equivalence induces a one-to-one correspondence between the silting subcategories in
\mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W})
and the silting subcategories in
\mathcal{B}
which contain
\mathcal{W}
.

This silting reduction generalizes
[
IY
, Theorem 1.1]
.

In extriangulated categories, tilting subcategories were introduced by Zhu and Zhuang
[
ZhZ
]
as a generalization of
n
-tilting modules
[
M
]
(and dually there is the notion of cotilting subcategories). Note that in triangulated categories, this notion is different from the concept of tilting subcategory since the only projective objects in a triangulated category are zeros. In this article, we give a slightly different definition of tilting subcategory (compared with
[
ZhZ
]
, see Definition
5.2
) and study the relation between silting subcategories and tilting subcategories.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
5.11
)
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

If any object
B\in\mathcal{B}
has finite projective dimension, then any tilting subcategory is silting.

(2)

If the projective dimensions of objects in
\mathcal{B}
have a common upper bound, then any silting subcategory is also tilting.

(3)

If there is an object
B\in B
having infinite projective dimension, then any silting (resp. tilting) subcategory is not tilting (resp. silting).

A class of presilting subcategories satisfying condition (CP) are contained in the following result, which generalizes a result of Happel-Unger
[
HU
, Lemma 1.3]
and a result of Xie-Zan-Zhang
[
XZZ
, Theorem 2.6]
.

Proposition 1.4

(Theorem
5.11
and Lemma
5.9
)
.

If
\mathcal{B}
is an abelian category, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{B}
is Gorenstein;

(2)

any tilting subcategory is cotilting;

(3)

any cotilting subcategory is tilting;

(4)

\mathcal{B}
has a tilting-cotilting subcategory.

Under one of these equivalent conditions, any tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
satisfies condition
\operatorname{(CP)}
.

Applying Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.4
to a finite dimensional Gorenstein algebra
A
, we can get that for any finitely generated
n
-tilting
A
-module
T
, there are triangle equivalences

\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229ptA/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229ptT)\simeq(T^{\bot}\cap{{}^{\bot}}T)/[T]\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(A)/\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptT),

where the second equivalence appeared in
[
CZ
, Theorem 2.5]
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the definition of silting subcategory in extriangulated category and some basic properties of it. In Section 3, we show that the localization
\mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W})
with respect to a presilting subcategory
\mathcal{W}
satisfying condition (CP) can be realized as a subfactor category of
\mathcal{B}
, which is a generalization of the well-known silting reduction. In Section 4, we show that the localization
\mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W})
has a natural triangulated structure. In Section 5, we study the relation between silting subcategories and tilting subcategories in extriangulated categories, the results provide a kind of important examples of our silting reduction.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Extriangulated categories

We briefly recall the definition and basic properties of extriangulated categories, see
[
NP
]
for more details.

Denote by
{\rm Ab}
the category of abelian groups. Let
(\mathcal{B},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category in the sense of
[
NP
]
, where
\mathcal{B}
is an additive category equipped with an additive bifunctor

\mathbb{E}:\mathcal{B}^{\rm op}\times\mathcal{B}\rightarrow{\rm Ab},

and
\mathfrak{s}
is an
additive realization
of
\mathbb{E}
in the sense of
[
NP
, Definitions 2.9 and 2.10]
, satisfying certain axioms listed in
[
NP
, Definition 2.12]
. For convenience, we usually say
\mathcal{B}
is an extriangulated category.

For any objects
A,C
of
\mathcal{B}
, an element
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
is called an
\mathbb{E}
-extension
. Since
\mathbb{E}
is an additive bifunctor, for any
\mathbb{E}
-extensions
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
and any morphisms
a:A\to A^{\prime},c:C\to C^{\prime}
, we have
\mathbb{E}
-extensions

a_{\ast}\delta:=\mathbb{E}(C,a)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\text{ and }c^{\ast}\delta:=\mathbb{E}(c,A)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A).

For any
\mathbb{E}
-extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
, there is an associated class
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)
of sequences
A\xrightarrow{~x~}B\xrightarrow{~y~}C
of morphisms in
\mathcal{B}
, where
x
is called an
inflation
and
y
is called a
deflation
. In this case, the pair
(A\xrightarrow{~x~}B\xrightarrow{~y~}C,\delta)
is called an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle
, and is written as

A\overset{x}{\longrightarrow}B\overset{y}{\longrightarrow}C\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}.

Let
A\overset{x}{\longrightarrow}B\overset{y}{\longrightarrow}C\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}
and
A^{\prime}\overset{x^{\prime}}{\longrightarrow}B^{\prime}\overset{y^{\prime}}{\longrightarrow}C^{\prime}\overset{\delta^{\prime}}{\dashrightarrow}
be any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-triangles. Let
a:A\to A^{\prime}
and
c:C\to C^{\prime}
be any pair of morphisms such that
a_{\ast}\delta=c^{\ast}\delta^{\prime}
. There exists
b:B\to B^{\prime}
such that the following diagram commutes.

\scriptstyle{x}
\scriptstyle{a}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

2.2.
Syzygy and cosyzygy

An object
P\in\mathcal{B}
is called
projective
if
for any
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

A\xrightarrow{x}B\xrightarrow{y}C\dashrightarrow

and any morphism
c\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(P,C)
, there exists
b\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(P,B)
satisfying
y\circ b=c
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}
the full subcategory of projective objects in
\mathcal{B}
. Dually, we can define
injective
objects and denote by
\mathcal{I}
the full subcategory of injective objects in
\mathcal{B}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
\mathcal{B}

has enough projectives
, if
for any object
C\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

A\xrightarrow{x}P\xrightarrow{y}C\dashrightarrow

with
P\in\mathcal{P}
. We can define the notion of
having enough injectives
dually.

Throughout the rest of the paper, let
k
be a field and
\mathcal{B}
be a Krull-Schmidt,
k
-linear, Hom-finite extriangulated category with enough projectives and enough injectives. Moreover, we assume that
\mathcal{B}
satisfies
[
NP
, Condition 5.8]
, i.e.,

(WIC):

for any morphisms
f:A\to B
and
g:B\to C
in
\mathcal{B}
, if
g\circ f
is an inflation, then so is
f
; if
g\circ f
is a deflation, then so is
g
.

All triangulated categories and Krull-Schmidt, Hom-finite,
k
-linear exact categories satisfy this condition.

When we say that
\mathcal{C}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
, we always assume that
\mathcal{C}
is full and closed under isomorphisms, direct sums and direct summands. Note that we do not assume any subcategory we construct has such property.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be subcategories of
\mathcal{B}
. We denote the following full subcategories

\operatorname{Cone}\nolimits(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})=\{X\in\mathcal{B}\text{ }|\ \text{there exists an $\mathbb{E}$-triangle }C\to D\to X\dashrightarrow~\mbox{with}~C\in\mathcal{C},\ D\in\mathcal{D}\},

\operatorname{CoCone}\nolimits(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})=\{X\in\mathcal{B}\text{ }|\ \text{there exists an $\mathbb{E}$-triangle }X\to C\to D\dashrightarrow~\mbox{with}~C\in\mathcal{C},\ D\in\mathcal{D}\}.

Definition 2.3
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{B}
is called
closed under cones
(resp.
closed under cocones
) if
\operatorname{Cone}\nolimits(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{C}
(resp.
\operatorname{CoCone}\nolimits(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{C}
).

For any subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{B}
, let
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}_{0}=\mathcal{C}^{\wedge}_{0}=\mathcal{C}
. We denote the full subcategories
\mathcal{C}^{\wedge}_{i}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}_{i}
for
i>0
inductively by

\mathcal{C}^{\wedge}_{i}=\operatorname{Cone}\nolimits(\mathcal{C}^{\wedge}_{i-1},\mathcal{C}),\ \mathcal{C}^{\vee}_{i}=\operatorname{CoCone}\nolimits(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\vee}_{i-1}),

and denote the full subcategories

\mathcal{C}^{\wedge}=\bigcup\limits_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{C}^{\wedge}_{i},\ \mathcal{C}^{\vee}=\bigcup\limits_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{C}^{\vee}_{i}.

For any subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{B}
, let
\Omega^{0}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}
. Then we can define
\Omega^{i}\mathcal{C}
for any
i\geq 1
inductively by
\Omega^{i}\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{CoCone}\nolimits(\mathcal{P},\Omega^{i-1}\mathcal{C})
. Dually, let
\Sigma^{0}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}
. Then we can define
\Sigma^{i}\mathcal{C}
for any
i\geq 1
inductively by
\Sigma^{i}\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Cone}\nolimits(\Sigma^{i-1}\mathcal{C},\mathcal{I})
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{B}
, we write
\Omega X
(resp.
\Sigma X
) as an object in
\mathcal{B}
(which is not necessarily unique) sitting in an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

\Omega X\to P\to X\dashrightarrow
(resp.
X\to I\to\Sigma X\dashrightarrow
)

with
P\in\mathcal{P}
(resp.
I\in\mathcal{I}
). Similarly, one can define
\Omega^{i}X
and
\Sigma^{i}X
for any
i\geq 1
inductively by

\Omega^{i}X=\Omega(\Omega^{i-1}X)\text{ and }\Sigma^{i}X=\Sigma(\Sigma^{i-1}X).

Obviously we have
\Omega^{i}X\in\Omega^{i}\mathcal{C}
(resp.
\Sigma^{i}X\in\Sigma^{i}\mathcal{C}
) if
X\in\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 2.4
.

For any objects
X,Y
of
\mathcal{B}
, we define

as

\mathbb{E}^{i+1}(X,Y):=\mathbb{E}(\Omega^{i}X,Y),i\geq 1.

The following lemma tells us that the definition of higher extensions does not depend on the choice of

Lemma 2.5

(
[

)
.

For any objects
X,Y
of
\mathcal{B}
and any non-negative integers

, we have the following isomorphisms of groups

\mathbb{E}(\Omega^{i+j}X,Y)\simeq\mathbb{E}(\Omega^{i}X,\Sigma^{j}Y)\simeq\mathbb{E}(X,\Sigma^{i+j}Y).

2.3.
Presilting subcategories and cotorsion pairs

We recall the definition of a presilting subcategory in an extriangulated category.

Definition 2.7

(
[
AT
, Section 3]
)
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{B}
is called a
presilting subcategory
if
\mathbb{E}^{i}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})=0,\forall i\geq 1
. An object
S
of
\mathcal{B}
is called a
presilting object
if its additive closure
\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptS
is a presilting subcategory.

We have the following properties of
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}
for a presilting subcategory
\mathcal{S}
.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
AT
, Section 3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a presilting subcategory. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\mathbb{E}^{i}(\mathcal{S}^{\vee},\mathcal{S}^{\wedge})=0,\forall i>0
and
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\wedge}=\mathcal{S}
.

(2)

\mathcal{S}^{\vee}
is closed under direct sums, direct summands, extensions and cocones.

(3)

\mathcal{S}^{\wedge}
is closed under direct sums, direct summands, extensions and cones.

(4)

\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=(\mathcal{S}^{\vee})^{\perp}
and
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}={}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}^{\wedge})
.

(5)

\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\vee}=\mathcal{S}={}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{S}^{\wedge}
.

We recall the definition of a cotorsion pair in an extriangulated category. Note that it is also called a
complete
cotorsion pair in many articles (e.g.,
[
S
]
).

Definition 2.9

(
[
NP
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
be two subcategories of
\mathcal{B}
. We call
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
a
cotorsion pair
if

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=0\text{ and }\mathcal{B}=\operatorname{Cone}\nolimits(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{U})=\operatorname{CoCone}\nolimits(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{U}).

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
. We introduce the following notions:

(1)

\mathcal{C}^{\bot}=\{X\in\mathcal{B}\text{ }|\text{ }\mathbb{E}^{i}(\mathcal{C},X)=0,\forall i>0\}
;

(2)

\mathcal{C}^{\bot_{1}}=\{X\in\mathcal{B}\text{ }|\text{ }\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{C},X)=0\}
;

(3)

{{}^{\bot}}\mathcal{C}=\{X\in\mathcal{B}\text{ }|\text{ }\mathbb{E}^{i}(X,\mathcal{C})=0,\forall i>0\}
;

(4)

{{}^{\bot_{1}}}\mathcal{C}=\{X\in\mathcal{B}\text{ }|\text{ }\mathbb{E}(X,\mathcal{C})=0\}
.

We have the following properties for cotorsion pairs.

Lemma 2.10

(
[

, Remark 3.2]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
be a cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{B}
. Then

(1)

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{U}^{\bot_{1}}
;

(2)

\mathcal{U}={{}^{\bot_{1}}}\mathcal{V}
;

(3)

\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are closed under extensions;

(4)

\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
;

(5)

\mathcal{U}
is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{V}
is covariantly finite in
\mathcal{B}
.

2.4.
Silting subcategories

Definition 2.11
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{B}
is called
thick
provides that for any
\mathbb{E}
-triangle
A\to B\to C\dashrightarrow
, if any two objects of

A,B
and
C
belong to
\mathcal{C}
, then so is the third one.

For any subcategory
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{B}
, we denote by
\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{D}
the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
containing
\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 2.12

(
[
AT
, Definition 5.1]
)
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{B}
is called
silting
if
\mathcal{S}
is presilting and
\mathcal{B}=\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{S}
.

Note that for any presilting subcategory
\mathcal{S}
, since
\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{S}
is closed under extensions, it is an extriangulated category. Moreover, inheriting from
\mathcal{B}
, it is Krull-Schmidt, Hom-finite and
k
-linear. By definition, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a presilting subcategory. If
\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{S}
, then
\mathcal{S}
is a silting subcategory in
\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{S}
.

The following criterion on a presilting subcategory to be silting is a direct consequence of
[
AT
, Theorem 5.5]
.

Theorem 2.14
.

A presilting subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{B}
is silting if and only if
(\mathcal{S}^{\vee},\mathcal{S}^{\wedge})
is a cotorsion pair.

Remark 2.15
.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a Frobenius (i.e.
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{I}
) extriangulated category, by
[
NP
, Corollary 7.4 and Remark 7.5]
,
\mathcal{B}/[\mathcal{P}]
is a triangulated category. In this case, by Lemma
2.8
, any silting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
contains
\mathcal{P}
. By
[
MDZH
, Theorems 1.1 and 4.6]
,
\mathcal{S}
is a silting subcategory in
\mathcal{B}
if and only if
\mathcal{S}/[\mathcal{P}]
is a silting subcategory in
\mathcal{B}/[\mathcal{P}]
.

We give some examples of silting subcategories.

Example 2.16
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{B}
is an abelian category, then any silting subcategory in
\mathcal{B}
is a silting subcategory in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B})
. This is because on one hand,
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{i}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})=0
implies
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B})}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}[i])=0
for any
i>0
; on the other hand,
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathcal{B})=\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{B}=\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{S}
.

(2)

Let
\Lambda
be a finite dimensional
k
-algebra and
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\Lambda
be the category of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules. If
\operatorname{gl.dim}\nolimits\Lambda<\infty
, then the
n
-tilting modules in
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\Lambda
are the silting objects (see Theorem
5.6
for more general results). If
\operatorname{gl.dim}\nolimits\Lambda=\infty
, then by
[
AI
, Example 2.5]
, there is no silting subcategory in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\Lambda)
, hence by (1),
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\Lambda
has no silting subcategory.

(3)

Let
A=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{i}
be a finite dimensional positively graded
1
-Gorenstein algebra with
\operatorname{gl.dim}\nolimits A_{0}<\infty
. By
[
LZ
, Theorem 3.0.3]
, there is a silting object
T:=\bigoplus\limits_{i\geq 0}A(i)_{\leq 0}
in
\mathrm{D}_{sg}(\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt^{\mathbb{Z}}A)
, where
(i)
is the degree shift functor. Since
\mathrm{D}_{sg}(\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt^{\mathbb{Z}}A)\simeq\underline{\mathrm{CM}}^{\mathbb{Z}}(A)
, we have a silting object in
\underline{\mathrm{CM}}^{\mathbb{Z}}(A)
. By the discussion in Remark
2.15
, we get a silting object in
{\mathrm{CM}}^{\mathbb{Z}}(A)
.

3.

In this section, let
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
be a presilting subcategory. We assume that

satisfies the following condition:

(CP)

(\mathcal{W}^{\vee},\mathcal{W}^{\bot})

({{}^{\bot}}\mathcal{W},\mathcal{W}^{\wedge})

Remark 3.1
.

By Lemma

(4), condition (CP) implies

and
\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W}^{\wedge}=\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W}

Definition 3.2
.

Let

be an object of
\mathcal{B}
. The

of

\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{B}
, the

Dually, we can define the

and

Remark 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{W}
is silting, condition (
\operatorname{CP}
) holds. If
\mathcal{W}
is not silting, condition (

) implies that

and
\operatorname{id}\nolimits\mathcal{B}=\infty
. This is because if

or

, we have either
\mathcal{B}=\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W}
or
\mathcal{B}=\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{W}

\mathcal{W}
is a silting subcategory, a contradiction.

Remark 3.4
.

When
\mathcal{B}
is a triangulated category, condition (CP) is equivalent to the conditions in
[

, Theorem 1.1]
by
[

, Proposition 3.2]

5.
Silting subcategories vs tilting subcategories

In this section, we discuss the relation between silting categories and tilting subcategories. This gives us a kind of important examples of the results in Section 3 and Section 4.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A\to B\to C\dashrightarrow
be an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle. Then we have

\operatorname{id}\nolimits A\leq\max\{\operatorname{id}\nolimits B,\operatorname{id}\nolimits C+1\},\ \operatorname{id}\nolimits B\leq\max\{\operatorname{id}\nolimits A,\operatorname{id}\nolimits C\},\operatorname{id}\nolimits C\leq\max\{\operatorname{id}\nolimits B,\operatorname{id}\nolimits A-1\},\text{ and}

This follows directly from the exact sequence in Lemma

.
∎

We give the following definition of tilting subcategories in extriangulated categories, which is sightly different from
[
ZhZ
, Defintion 7]
. Note that by
[
ZhZ
, Remark 4]
, the tilting subcategory defined in
[
ZhZ
, Defintion 7]
also satisfies the following definition.

Definition 5.2
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{B}
is called partial
n
-tilting (
n\geq 1
) if the following hold.

(P1)

\operatorname{pd}\nolimits\mathcal{T}\leq n
.

(P2)

\mathbb{E}^{i}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T})=0,i=1,2,...,n
.

A partial
n
-tilting subcategory
\mathcal{T}
is called
n
-tilting
if the following holds.

(P3)

\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\vee}
.

Any partial
n
-tilting (resp.
n
-tilting) subcategory is simply called a
partial tilting
(resp.
tilting
) subcategory. An object
T
is called an
n
-tilting object if
\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptT
is an
n
-tilting subcategory. The notion of
n
-cotilting
subcategories can be defined dually.

Remark 5.3
.

If
\mathcal{B}
is the category of finitely generated modules of a finite-dimensional algebra, then the
n
-tilting objects in
\mathcal{B}
are exactly the
n
-tilting modules. But if
\mathcal{B}
is a (non-zero) triangulated category, then there is no non-zero tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
because in this case
\mathcal{P}=\{0\}
and for any non-zero subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{B}
, we have
\operatorname{pd}\nolimits\mathcal{T}=\infty
.

We have the following observation.

Lemma 5.4
.

Any partial tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
is presilting. If
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{P}
, then any tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
is silting in
\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Conditions (P1) and (P2) imply that
\mathbb{E}^{i}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T})=0
for any
i>0
. Then the first assertion holds. Conditions (P1) and (P3) imply that
\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{P}=\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{T}
. So by Lemma
2.13
, the second assertion holds.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a partial

-tilting subcategory in

. Consider the following conditions:

(a)

\mathcal{T}
is contravariantly finite and

(b)

(a)
\Rightarrow

, we only need to show that:

(1)

\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{T}^{\bot})^{\vee}
.

(2)

, there are exact sequences

. Since

is an

0=\mathbb{E}^{j}(T_{i},\mathcal{T}^{\bot})\to\mathbb{E}^{j}(R_{i},\mathcal{T}^{\bot})\to\mathbb{E}^{j+1}(R_{i+1},\mathcal{T}^{\bot})\to\mathbb{E}^{j+1}(T_{i},\mathcal{T}^{\bot})=0,\ 0\leq i<n,j\geq 1.

Hence we have isomorphisms

\mathbb{E}^{j}(R_{i},\mathcal{T}^{\bot})\cong\mathbb{E}^{j+1}(R_{i+1},\mathcal{T}^{\bot}),\ 0\leq i<n,j\geq 1.

Since
\mathbb{E}^{n}(R_{n},\mathcal{T}^{\perp})=0
, by induction, we have
\mathbb{E}(R_{1},\mathcal{T}^{\perp})=0

is a left

. Hence there exists a morphism

,

. Hence

.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c): If

.

The following statements hold.

(1)

If for any object

\operatorname{silt}\nolimits\mathcal{B}\supseteq\operatorname{tilt}\nolimits\mathcal{B}

(2)

, then
\operatorname{silt}\nolimits\mathcal{B}=\operatorname{tilt}\nolimits\mathcal{B}
.

(3)

If there exists an object

such that
\operatorname{pd}\nolimits X=\infty
, then
\operatorname{silt}\nolimits\mathcal{B}\cap\operatorname{tilt}\nolimits\mathcal{B}=\emptyset

(1) If any object

has finite projective dimension, then
\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{B}
. By Lemma

, we get the assertion.

(2) If

, by (1), we only need to show

. Let

. Then

is a partial tilting subcategory. By Theorem

. Hence by definition,

is a tilting subcategory.

. This means that for any subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{B}
,

and

can not hold at the same time. It follows that there is no subcategory which is both silting and tiling.

We are interested in the case when

contains a tilting-cotilting subcategory, which gives an important example of the results in Section 4. We generalize

Definition 5.7

\mathcal{B}

\operatorname{pd}\nolimits\mathcal{I}<\infty
and
\operatorname{id}\nolimits\mathcal{P}<\infty

By definition, we have the following observation.

If
\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{I}
are tilting-cotilting subcategories of
\mathcal{B}
.

Consider the following condition.

(AF):

Either
\mathcal{B}
is an abelian category, or
\mathcal{P}
is covariantly finite and
\mathcal{I}
is contravariantly finite.

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.9

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a partial tilting subcategory in
\mathcal{B}
. Under the condition
\operatorname{(AF)}
, if
\mathcal{B}
is Gorenstein, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(2)

(3)

({{}^{\bot}}\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\wedge})

(1)
\Rightarrow

, we have

. Since

(4)
\Rightarrow

Theorem 5.11
.

Under assumption
\operatorname{(AF)}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{B}
is Gorenstein;

(2)

any tilting subcategory is cotilting;

(3)

any cotilting subcategory is tilting;

(4)

\mathcal{B}
has a tilting-cotilting subcategory.

Proof.

“(2)
\Rightarrow
(4)”: Since
\mathcal{P}
is a tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
, by (2) we have that
\mathcal{P}
is cotilting. So we have (4).

“(4)
\Rightarrow
(1)”: By (4), there is an
n
-tilting,
m
-cotilting subcategory
\mathcal{T}
in
\mathcal{B}
. By definition every projective object
P\in\mathcal{P}
admits
\mathbb{E}
-triangles

R_{i}\to T_{i}\to R_{i+1}\dashrightarrow,\ i=0,1,\cdots,n-1

with
R_{0}=P,T_{i}\in\mathcal{T},R_{n}\in\mathcal{T}
. Since
\operatorname{id}\nolimits\mathcal{T}\leq m
, by Lemma
5.1
, we have
\operatorname{id}\nolimits P\leq m+n
. Hence
\operatorname{id}\nolimits\mathcal{P}\leq m+n
. Dually, we have
\operatorname{pd}\nolimits\mathcal{I}\leq m+n
, too. Thus, we get (1).

“(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)”: this follows directly from Lemma
5.9
.

Dually, one can show the implications (1)
\Rightarrow
(3)
\Rightarrow
(4)
\Rightarrow
(1).
∎

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
. If
\mathcal{B}
is Gorenstein and satisfies condition
(AF)
, then we have the following triangle equivalences:

{{}^{\bot}}\mathcal{P}/[\mathcal{P}]\simeq\mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{P})=\mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{B}/(\mathsf{thick}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{I})\simeq\mathcal{I}^{\bot}/[\mathcal{I}]\simeq(\mathcal{T}^{\bot}\cap{{}^{\bot}}\mathcal{T})/[\mathcal{T}].

Let
A
be a Gorenstein algebra and
T
be an
n
-tilting
A
-module. According to
[
H
, Lemma 1.5]
, we have
\mathrm{D}^{b}(A)/\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptT)=\mathrm{D}^{b}(A)/\mathrm{K}^{b}(\operatorname{proj}\nolimits A)
. By
[

, there is a triangle equivalence

\mathrm{D}^{b}(A)/\mathrm{K}^{b}(\operatorname{proj}\nolimits A)\simeq{{}^{\bot}}A/[A].

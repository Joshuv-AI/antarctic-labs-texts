Independence of Singularity Type for Numerically Effective Kähler-Ricci Flows
Hosea Wondo and Zhou Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we show that the singularity type of solutions to the Käher-Ricci flow on a numerically effective manifold does not depend on the initial metric. More precisely, if there exists a type III solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow, then any other solution starting from a different initial metric will also be Type III. This generalises a previous result by Y. Zhang for the semi-ample case and confirms a conjecture by V. Tosatti.

The Kähler-Ricci flow is a geometric evolution equation used to study Kähler geometry. It has proven to be a powerful tool in constructing canonical metrics such as Kähler-Einstein metrics
[

A primary research direction is the study of the analytic minimal model program in birational geometry
[
8
]
, where the flow takes a central role in producing minimal models. Initiated by Song and Tian in
[
12
]
, the program proposes to emulate the procedure carried out by Hamilton and Perelmann in resolving the Poincare conjecture. The idea is to run the flow on algebraic manifolds and perform ‘algebraic surgeries’ such as flips and contractions each time the flow encounters a singularity
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. After finite iterations of this procedure, the resulting manifold would be numerically effective (nef) and hence a minimal model. At this stage, the normalised version of the flow will evolve the metric to a Kähler-Einstein metric or a generalised Kähler-Einstein metric. Significant progress has been made in this direction; see
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
23
,
4
,
3
,
30
,
24
,
20
]
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with numerically effective line bundle, which corresponds to the final step of the analytic minimal model program. A family on Kähler metrics
\omega(t)
is said to evolve by the normalised Kähler-Ricci flow if

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partialderivative{t}\omega(t)&=-\text{Ric}(\omega(t))-\omega(t),\\
\omega(0)&=\omega_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Since the flow is weakly parabolic, we have access to interior estimates, which smooths the metric for short times. In the long run, non-linearities make the metric non-positive, forming a singularity. The precise time of singularity formation is determined by the class of the evolving metric in the cohomology given by

(1.2)

H_{\bar{\partial}}^{1,1}(M,\mathbb{R})=\frac{\{\bar{\partial}\text{-closed real }(1,1)\text{-forms }\}}{\{\bar{\partial}\text{-exact real }(1,1)\text{-forms }\}}.

The singular time,
T
, is characterised by Tian and the second author in
[
21
]
:

(1.3)

T:=\sup\left\{t\mid[\omega(t)]\text{ is K\"{a}hler}\right\}.

By formally taking the cohomology class of (
1.1
) and solving the resulting ODE, we obtain solution

(1.4)

[\omega(t)]=e^{-t}[\omega_{0}]-(1-e^{-t})c_{1}(X).

Since
X
is numerically effective,
-c_{1}(X)
lies in the closure of the Kähler cone, the set of classes with a Kähler representative, and thus, the solution exists for all time.
Furthermore, for any initial metric, the cohomology class converges to the first Chern class,
-c_{1}(X)
in the limit as
t\rightarrow\infty
. This is independent of any starting metric
\omega_{0}
.

The singularity formation reveals the manifold’s underlying topology; therefore, the curvature blow-up should be independent of the starting metric. To describe the curvature blow-up, we utilise the following terminology traditionally used in parabolic geometric flows, such as the Ricci flow and Mean Curvature flow.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a long-time solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow develops a type III singularity if

(1.5)

\sup_{X\times[0,\infty)}|\text{Rm}(\omega(t))|_{\omega(t)}<+\infty,

and a type IIb singularity if

(1.6)

\sup_{X\times[0,\infty)}|\text{Rm}(\omega(t))|_{\omega(t)}=+\infty.

The precise conjecture for metric independence is stated by Tossati in
[
26
]
.

Conjecture 1.2

(Tosatti
[
26
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with
K_{X}
nef, so every solution of the Kähler-Ricci flow exists for all positive time. Then the singularity type at infinity does not depend on the choice of the initial metric
\omega_{0}
.

In the semi-ample case, Tossati-YG. Zhang in
[
24
]
gives an almost complete classification of singularity type based on the topology of

Let X be a compact Kähler manifold with semi-ample

•

•

If

If

An alternative proof to items (1), (2) and a special case of (3) was proven by Y. Zhang in

showed that the set of singular fibers of the semi-ample fibration on which the Riemann curvature blows up at time-infinity is independent of the choice of the initial Kähler metric. For more results pertaining to the curvature behaviour of semiample Kähler-Ricci flows, see

In this paper, we prove Conjecture
1.2
without assuming

is semiample. More precisely, we show that if a solution develops a type III singularity on a numerically effective manifold, then every other solution develops a type III singularity. Consequently, if a solution develops a type IIb singularity, then any other solution must be type IIb.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\widetilde{\omega}(t)
be a solution to the normalised Kähler-Ricci flow (
1.1
) on a manifold

with numerically effective
K_{X}
.

(1.7)

|\text{{Rm}}(\widetilde{\omega}(t))|^{2}_{\widetilde{\omega}(t)}\leqslant C_{0}.

Then for any other solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow,
\omega(t)
, there exists a constant

such that

(1.8)

C^{-1}\widetilde{\omega}(t)\leqslant\omega(t)\leqslant C\widetilde{\omega}(t),

for all

(1.9)

.

Any solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow on a numerically effective manifold exists for all time. Indeed, the estimate (

The proof builds on the results in
[
31

We end the introduction by outlining the structure of this paper. In Section 2, we show a special case of Theorem

With a similar argument as carried out in Section 2, curvature bounds are obtained for the general theorem. Finally, in Section 4, we utilise Theorem

\omega(t)
and
\omega^{-}(t)
in (
3.1
), and using the flow equation, we arrive at a Monge-Ampère equation for
u:=\varphi-\varphi^{-}
given by

(3.2)

\partialderivative{u}{t}=\log\left(\frac{\omega^{n}}{(\omega^{-})^{n}}\right)-u

where

(3.3)

\omega(t)=\omega^{-}(t)+e^{-t}(\omega_{0}-\omega^{-}_{0})+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u.

Equation (
3.2
) will be the primary Monge-Ampère utilised in the proof of the main Theorem. Note that the construction of
\omega^{-}_{0}
ensures
\omega_{*}:=\omega_{0}-\omega^{-}_{0}>0.

Similarly for
\psi:=\varphi^{+}-\varphi^{-}
we have

(3.4)

\partialderivative{\psi}{t}=\log\left(\frac{(\omega^{+})^{n}}{(\omega^{-})^{n}}\right)-\psi.

The function
\psi
relates the two evolving metrics by

(3.5)

\omega^{+}(t)=\omega^{-}(t)+e^{-t}(\omega_{0}^{+}-\omega^{-}_{0})+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi.

Finally, for
v:=\varphi-\varphi^{+}
, we have the following Monge-Ampere equation

(3.6)

\partialderivative{v}{t}=\log\left(\frac{\omega^{n}}{(\omega^{+})^{n}}\right)-v

where

(3.7)

\omega(t)=\omega^{+}(t)+e^{-t}(\omega_{0}-\omega^{+}_{0})+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}v.

3.2.
Potential Estimates

Our aim is to derive potential estimates for
u
and its time derivative. We begin by calculating the evolution of
u
and
\partialderivative{u}{t}
. As before, we drop the
t
dependence on the metrics and potential functions for ease of notation. To denote geometric quantities associated with
\omega^{+}(t)
and
\omega^{-}(t)
with their respective symbol in superscripts.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\omega_{*}:=\omega_{0}-\omega^{-}_{0}>0
, any solution
u
to (
3.2
) satisfies

(3.8)

\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega(t)}\right)u=\partialderivative{u}{t}-n+\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}+e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\omega_{*},

and

(3.9)

\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega(t)}\right)\partialderivative{u}{t}=R^{-}-\tr_{\omega}\text{Ric}(\omega^{-})-\partialderivative{u}{t}-\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}-e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\omega_{*}+n.

Proof.

The first equation, (
3.8
) follows from (
3.1
);

\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega(t)}\right)u=\partialderivative{u}{t}-\tr_{\omega}(\omega-\omega^{-}-e^{-t}\omega_{*})=\partialderivative{u}{t}-n+\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}+e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\omega_{*}.

Equation (
3.9
) is derived using (
3.2
);

\displaystyle\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega(t)}\right)\partialderivative{u}{t}

\displaystyle=\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega(t)}\right)\log\left(\frac{\omega^{n}}{{\omega^{-}}^{n}}\right)-\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega(t)}\right)u

\displaystyle=R^{-}-\tr_{\omega}\text{Ric}(\omega^{-})-\partialderivative{u}{t}+n-\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}-e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\omega_{*}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
3.2
). Then there exists a uniform
C>0
such that

(3.10)

-Ce^{-t}\leqslant u(t)\leqslant C

for all
t\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

The lower bound follows from the maximum principle. Indeed, at a minimum point of
u
,

\partialderivative{t}\left(e^{t}u\right)=e^{t}\log\left(\frac{(\omega^{-}(t)+e^{-t}(\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}^{-})+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u)^{n}}{(\omega^{-})^{n}}\right)\geqslant e^{t}\log(1)=0.

Integrating yields

u(t)\geqslant-Ce^{-t}.

To derive an upper bound for
u
, we first observe that at a maximum for
v
in (
3.6
), we have

\partialderivative{v_{\max}}{t}\leqslant\log\left(\frac{(\omega_{+}+e^{-t}(\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}^{+}))^{n}}{(\omega^{+})^{n}}\right)-v_{\max}\leqslant-v_{\max}.

Then by the maximum principle, we deduce that
v\leqslant 0
. On the other hand, we have
C>0
such that

(3.11)

|\psi|\leqslant C

for all
t>0
. Indeed, the initial metrics
\omega^{+}_{0},\omega^{-}_{0}
and
\widetilde{\omega}_{0}
are scalar multiples of each other, Lemma
2.1
and (
3.4
) imply

\partialderivative{t}\left(e^{t}\psi\right)=e^{t}\log\left(\frac{(\omega^{+})^{n}}{(\omega^{-})^{n}}\right)\leqslant Ce^{t}

for some
C>0
. Integrating the above gives us (
3.11
). It now follows that

u(t)=\varphi(t)-\varphi^{-}(t)=v(t)+\psi(t)\leqslant C

where
C>0
is independent of time.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
3.2
). Then there exists
C>0
such that

(3.12)

\left|\partialderivative{u}{t}\right|\leqslant C,

for all
t\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

We first note that Lemma
2.1
implies that for some
C>0
, we have

(3.13)

C^{-1}\widetilde{\omega}(t)\leqslant\omega^{-}(t)\leqslant C\widetilde{\omega}(t),

and

(3.14)

|\text{Rm}(\omega^{-}(t))|_{\omega^{-}(t)}^{2}\leqslant C,

for all
t\geqslant 0
.
Let

(3.15)

Q:=\partialderivative{u}{u}-Au,

where
A>0
a constant that will be set later. By combining (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), and using (
3.14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)Q

\displaystyle=R^{-}-\tr_{\omega}\text{Ric}(\omega^{-})-(A+1)\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}-(A+1)e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\omega_{*}-(A+1)\partialderivative{u}{t}+(A+1)n

\displaystyle\leqslant-(A+1-C_{0})\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}-(A+1)e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\omega_{*}-(A+1)\partialderivative{u}{t}+(A+1+C_{0})n.

We choose
A=2+C_{0}
to obtain the estimate

\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)Q\leqslant-C\partialderivative{u}{t}+C.

Applying the parabolic maximum principle yields the upper bound

\partialderivative{u}{t}\leqslant C,

for some
C>0
.

For the lower bound, we instead consider the quantity

Q:=\partialderivative{u}{t}+Au,

for some
A>0
to be determined later. Once again, we use (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.14
) to set up a maximum principle argument:

\displaystyle\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)Q

\displaystyle=R^{-}(t)-\tr_{\omega}\text{Ric}(\omega^{-})+(A-1)\tr_{\omega}\widetilde{\omega}+(A-1)e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\omega_{*}+(A-1)\partialderivative{u}{t}+n\left(1-A\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant(C_{0}+1-A)n+(A-1)\partialderivative{u}{t}+(A-C_{0}-1)\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}+(A-1)e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\omega_{*}.

Applying a similar argument to before, we choose
A=2+C_{0}
to obtain an estimate

\displaystyle\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)Q

\displaystyle\geqslant-C+C\partialderivative{u}{t}+\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}

\displaystyle\geqslant-C+C\partialderivative{u}{t}+n\left(\frac{(\omega^{-})^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=-C+C\partialderivative{u}{t}+ne^{-\frac{1}{n}\left(\partialderivative{u}{t}+u\right)}

Then applying the maximum principle, using that
u
is uniformly bounded, shows that there exists a
C>0
independent of time such that

(3.16)

\partialderivative{u}{t}\geqslant-C.

∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We now derive a metric equivalence between the Type III solution
\widetilde{\omega}
and the arbitrary solution
\omega
.

Proof.

From a standard calculation, using (
3.14
), we have for some
C>0
and
C_{0}>0

(3.17)

\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)\log\operatorname{tr}_{\omega}\omega^{-}\leqslant C_{0}\operatorname{tr}_{\omega}\omega^{-}+C,

for all times
t\geqslant 0
.
The uniform bound on
\partialderivative{u}{t}
from Lemma
3.3
implies that

(3.18)

\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)u=\partialderivative{u}{t}-n+\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}+e^{-t}\tr_{\omega}\left(\omega_{0}-\omega_{0}^{-}\right)\geqslant-C+\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}.

Following a similar argument as before, we define
Q:=\log\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}-Au
, then using the two inequalities, we choose
A
large enough such that

(3.19)

\left(\partialderivative{t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)Q\leqslant C-\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}.

Applying the maximum principle, and again using Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, we obtain some
C>0
such that

(3.20)

\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}\leqslant C,

for all
t\geqslant 0
. Combining the potential estimates from Lemma
3.2
and
3.3
, we derive from the Monge–Ampère equation (
3.2
) the volume bounds

(3.21)

C^{-1}\leqslant\frac{\omega^{n}}{(\omega^{-})^{n}}\leqslant C,

for some
C>0
for all
t\geqslant 0
. Similar to before, we employ standard eigenvalue estimates to obtain

\tr_{\omega^{-}}\omega\leqslant n\left(\frac{\omega^{n}}{(\omega^{-})^{n}}\right)\tr_{\omega}\omega^{-}\leqslant C.

Then the following metric equivalence follows from the two trace bounds;

(3.22)

C^{-1}\omega^{-}(t)\leqslant\omega(t)\leqslant C\omega^{-}(t).

Then the above inequality and (
3.13
) yields (
1.8
). To obtain bounds on the curvature tensor, we apply Proposition
2.3
with
\omega^{-}(t)
in place of
\widetilde{\omega}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

We can now apply Theorem
1.4
to answer Conjecture
1.2
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a Kähler manifold with numerically effective canonical line bundle. Then the singularity type of solutions to the Kähler-Ricci flow is independent of the initial metric.

Proof.

Suppose a solution
\widetilde{\omega}
is Type III. Then by Theorem
1.4
, any other solution starting from a different initial metric must also be Type III. On the other hand, if a type IIb solution exists, then any other solution must be Type IIb as otherwise, Theorem
1.4
implies that all solutions must be Type III, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Finally, we note that Theorem
1.4
can be interpreted as evidence for the Kähler extension of the abundance conjecture. Indeed, if a counter example to Theorem
1.4
could be constructed, then Y. Zhang’s result
[
31
]
implies that the manifold cannot be semiample.

4.
Further Remarks

Our results and techniques have applications and generalisations in many settings.

We start with some examples where an explicit solution to the normalised Kähler-Ricci Flow is known. This allows us to deduce the singularity type for an arbitrary initial metric for these manifolds. These examples are obtained from
[
30
]
.

(1)

Calabi-Yau Metrics
: Suppose that
X
admits a Calabi-Yau metric. Then under the Kähler-Ricci flow, we check that
\omega(t)=e^{-t}\omega_{CY}
is a solution with
\omega(0)=\omega_{CY}
. For such solutions, the curvature evolves as

(4.1)

|\text{Rm}(\omega(t))|_{\omega(t)}=e^{t}\left|\text{Rm}\left(\omega_{CY}\right)\right|_{\omega_{CY}},

and therefore develops a type III singularity if and only if
\omega_{CY}
is a flat metric, which is only possible if and only if
X
is a finite quotient of a torus. Therefore, we conclude from Theorem
1.4
that the Kähler-Ricci Flow develops a type III solution on a numerically effective manifold
X
if and only if it is a finite quotient of a torus. Furthermore, this demonstrates Item (1) of Theorem
1.3
without assuming
K_{X}
is semiample.

Remark 4.1
.

Item (2) in Theorem
1.3
assumes
K_{X}
is big. Since nef and big imply semiample, the generalisation to nef is immediate.

(2)

Product Manifold
:
Consider the product manifold
X:=B\times Y
where
Y
is a Calabi-Yau manifold. This is a special case of item (3). Suppose that
\omega
is a Kähler-Einstein metric on
B
, then we check that

(4.2)

\widetilde{\omega}(t)=e^{-t}\omega_{CY}+(1-e^{-t})\omega_{B}

is a solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow. The curvature is given by

(4.3)

|\text{Rm}(\omega(t)|_{\omega(t)}^{2}=e^{2t}|\text{Rm}(\omega_{CY})|_{\omega_{CY}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{2}}|\text{Rm}(\omega_{B})|_{\omega_{B}}^{2}.

Thus, we have a type III singularity if and only if
\omega_{CY}
is flat which occurs if
Y
is a finite quotient of a torus. Thus, a type III singularity develops if and only if
Y
is a finite quotient of a torus.
Thus, using Theorem
1.4
, any initial metric
\omega_{0}
, there exists
C>0
such that

(4.4)

C^{-1}\widetilde{\omega}(t)\leqslant\omega(t)\leqslant C\widetilde{\omega}(t)

and

(4.5)

|\text{Rm}(\omega(t))|_{\omega(t)}\leqslant C,

for all
t\geqslant 0
.

Furthermore, our method can be applied to other flows, for example, the modified Kähler-Ricci flow as studied in
[
32
,
29
,
27
]
. More precisely, the independence of infinite time singularity type should follow from metric equivalence and higher order estimates.

On Sub-Propositional Fragments of Modal Logic
Davide Bresolin, Emilio Muñoz-Velasco, Guido Sciavicco

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the well-known modal logics 𝐊 \operatorname{\mathbf{K}} , 𝐓 \operatorname{\mathbf{T}} , 𝐊𝟒 \operatorname{\mathbf{K4}} , and 𝐒𝟒 \operatorname{\mathbf{S4}} , and we study some of their sub-propositional fragments, namely the classical Horn fragment, the Krom fragment, the so-called core fragment, defined as the intersection of the Horn and the Krom fragments, plus their sub-fragments obtained by limiting the use of boxes and diamonds in clauses. We focus, first, on the relative expressive power of such languages: we introduce a suitable measure of expressive power, and we obtain a complex hierarchy that encompasses all fragments of the considered logics. Then, after observing the low expressive power, in particular, of the Horn fragments without diamonds, we study the computational complexity of their satisfiability problem, proving that, in general, it becomes polynomial.

1.
Introduction

Modal logic has been applied to computer science in several areas, such as artificial intelligence, distributed systems, and computational linguistics, and modal operators have been interpreted in different ways for different applications. Moreover, modal logic is paradigmatic of the whole variety of description logics
[
BCM+03
]
, temporal logics
[
GHR94
]
, spatial logics
[
APvB07
]
, among others. It is well-known that the basic (normal) modal logic
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
, can be
semantically
defined as the logic of all directed relations, or
syntactically
defined via the single axiom
K
:
\Box(p\rightarrow q)\rightarrow(\Box p\rightarrow\Box q)
. Modal logics of particular classes of relations are often referred to as
axiomatic extensions
of
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
. In this paper we consider, besides
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
, some relevant extensions such as the logic
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}
of all
transitive
relations (defined by the axiom
4
:
\Box p\rightarrow\Box\Box p
), the logic
\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
of all
reflexive
relations (defined by the axiom
T
:
\Box p\rightarrow p
), and the logic
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
of all reflexive and transitive relations (preorders), defined by the combination of all axioms above. From a purely semantic perspective, these logics (together with
\operatorname{\mathbf{S5}}
, that is, the logic of all transitive, reflexive and symmetric relations) can be represented in a lattice as in Fig.
1
. The satisfiability problem for these modal logics is
PSpace
-complete for all these logics, except for
\operatorname{\mathbf{S5}}
, for which it becomes
NP
-complete
[
Lad77
]
.

The quest of computationally well-behaved restrictions of logics, and the observation that meaningful statements can be still expressed under sub-propositional restrictions has recently increased the interest in sub-propositional fragments of temporal description logics
[
AKRZ14
]
, temporal logics
[
AKRZ13
]
, and interval temporal logics
[
AKRZ15
,
BMS14
]
. However, sub-propositional modal logics have received little or no attention, with the exception of
[
CL94
,
FP87
,
Ngu04
]
, which are limited to the Horn fragment. There are two standard ways to weaken the classical propositional language based on the clausal form of formulas: the
Horn fragment
, that only allows clauses with at most one positive literal
[
Hor51
]
, and the
Krom fragment
, that only allows clauses with at most two (positive or negative) literals
[
Kro70
]
. The
core fragment
combines both restrictions. Orthogonally, one can restrict a modal language in clausal form by allowing only diamonds or only boxes in positive literals, obtaining weaker fragments that we call, respectively, the
diamond fragment
and
box fragment
. By combining these two levels of restriction, one may obtain several sub-propositional fragments of modal logics, and, by extension, of description, temporal, and spatial logics.

The satisfiability problem for classical propositional Horn logic is
P
-complete
[
CN10
]
, while the classical propositional Krom logic satisfiability problem (also known as the 2-SAT problem) is
NLogSpace
-complete
[
Pap03
]
, and the same holds for the core fragment (this results is an immediate strengthening of the classical
NLogSpace
-hardness of 2-SAT). The satisfiability problem for quantified propositional logic (
\mathbf{QBF}
), which is
PSpace
-complete in its general form, becomes
P
when formulas are restricted to binary (Krom) clauses
[
APT79
]
. In
[
AKRZ13
,
CL93
]
, the authors study different sub-propositional fragments of Linear Temporal Logic (
\mathbf{LTL}
). By excluding the operators Since and Until from the language, and keeping only the Next/Previous-time operators and the box version of Future and Past, it is possible to prove that the Krom and core fragments are
NP
-hard, while the Horn fragment is still
PSpace
-complete (the same complexity of the full language). Moreover, the complexity of the Horn, Krom, and core fragments without Next/Previous-time operators range from
NLogSpace
(core), to
P
(Horn), to
NP
-hard (Krom). Where only a universal (anywhere in time) modality is allowed their complexity is even lower (from
NLogSpace
to
P
). Temporal extensions of the description logic DL-Lite have been studied under similar sub-propositional restrictions, and similar improvements in the complexity of various problems have been found
[
AKRZ14
]
. Sub-propositional fragments of the undecidable interval temporal logic
\mathbf{HS}

[
HS91
]
, have also been studied. The Horn, Krom, and core restrictions of
\mathbf{HS}
are still undecidable
[
BMS14
]
, but weaker restrictions have shown positive results. In particular, the Horn fragment of
\mathbf{HS}
without diamonds becomes
P
-complete in two interesting cases
[
AKRZ15
,
BKMV+17
]
: first, when it is interpreted over dense linear orders, and, second, when the semantics of its modalities are made reflexive. On the bases of these results, sub-propositional interval temporal extensions of description logics have been introduced in
[
AKRZ15
]
. Among other clausal forms of (temporal) logics, it is worth mentioning the fragment
\mathbf{GR(1)}
of
\mathbf{LTL}

[
BJP+12
]
. Lastly, the Horn fragments of modal logic
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
and of several of its axiomatic extensions have been considered in
[

,
FP87
,
Ngu04
]
; in particular, it is known that
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}

\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}

\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}

\operatorname{\mathbf{S5}}

In this paper, we consider sub-propositional fragments of the modal logics
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}
, and
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
, and study their relative expressive power in a systematic way. We consider two different notions of relative expressive power for fragments of modal logic, and we provide several results that give rise to two different hierarchies, leaving only a few open problems. Then, motivated by the observation that the fragment Horn box (and the fragment core box) of the logics we consider are expressively very weak, we launch an investigation about the complexity of their satisfiability problem, and we prove a suitable small-model theorem for such fragments. When paired with the results in
[
Ngu00
,
Ngu16
]
, such a small-model result allows us to prove

\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}
, and
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
.
Finally, we give a deterministic polynomial time satisfiability-checking algorithm that covers all considered fragments and that it is easy to implement.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work where sub-Krom and sub-Horn fragments of
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
have been considered.

Weak logics such as those mentioned in this paper can be applied in practice, but the main motivation to study them is that they are basic modal logics, and many other (practically useful) formalisms are built directly over them. Among these, we may mention
epistemic
logic,
deontic

provability
logic
[

]
(the latter, in particular, is a known variant of
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
), but, also, the entire range of description logics (starting from
\mathcal{\bf ALC}
, which is syntactic variant of
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}

[
vHLP07
]
). Studying sub-propositional fragments, such as Horn-like fragments of logics is naturally motivated by the fact that Horn-like formulas can be seen as rules, and this is the basis of logic programming.
Modal
logic programming is a relatively important topic (see, e.g.,
[
BFG+90
,
DEL92
,
Ngu03
]
). While SLD-resolution can be adapted to several modal logics under the Horn restriction
[
Ngu03
]
, it is natural to imagine that a similar rule can be devised under the Horn box restriction that allow even more efficient deductions — we briefly consider this problem in the last section of this paper. Modal logic programs can be used in modal deductive databases (which consist of facts and positive modal logic rules)
[
Ngu11
]

2.
Syntax and Semantics

Let us fix a unary modal
similarity type
with a single modality and a denumerable set

\mathcal{P}
of propositional letters. The associated
modal language

\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
contains all and only the formulas generated by the following grammar:

(1)

\varphi::=\top\mid p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\vee\varphi\mid{\Diamond}\varphi\mid{\Box}\varphi,

where
p\in\mathcal{P}
,
{\Diamond}
is called
diamond
, and
{\Box}
is called
box
. The other classical operators, such as
\rightarrow
and
\wedge
, can be considered as abbreviations. The
length
of a formula
\varphi
is denoted by
|\varphi|
and it is defined as the number of symbols in
\varphi
. By
md(\varphi)
, we denote the
modal depth
of
\varphi
, that is, the maximum number of nested boxes and diamonds in
\varphi
. Finally,
Cl(\varphi)
denotes the
closure
of the formula
\varphi
, that is, the set of all sub-formulas of
\varphi
. Notice that both the modal depth and the cardinality of the closure are bounded by
|\varphi|
.

A
Kripke

frame
is a relational structure
\mathcal{F}=(W,R)
, where the elements of
W\neq\emptyset
are called
possible worlds
, and
R\in W\times W
is the
accessibility
relation.
In the following, for any given accessibility relation
R
, we denote by
R^{\circlearrowright}
its
reflexive
closure, by
\overrightarrow{R}
its
transitive closure
, and by
R^{*}
its
reflexive and transitive closure
.
A
Kripke structure
over the frame
\mathcal{F}
is a pair
M=(\mathcal{F},V)
, where
V:W\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{P}}
is an
valuation function
, and we say that
M

models

\varphi
at the world
w
, denoted by
M,w\Vdash\varphi
, if and only if either one of the following holds:

•

\varphi=\top
;

•

\varphi=p
, and
p\in V(w)
;

•

\varphi=\neg\psi
, and
M,w\not\Vdash\varphi
;

•

\varphi=\psi\vee\xi
, and
M,w\Vdash\psi
or
M,w\Vdash\xi
;

•

\varphi={\Diamond}\psi
, and there exists
v
such that
wRv
and
M,v\Vdash\psi
;

•

\varphi={\Box}\psi
, and for every
v
such that
wRv
, it is the case that
M,v\Vdash\psi
.

In this case, we say that
M
is a
model
of
\varphi
, and that
\varphi
is
satisfiable
; in the following, we (improperly) use the terms model and structure as synonyms. We define the
size

|M|
of the model
M
as the cardinality of the set of worlds
W
.

The modal language can be interpreted in classes of Kripke frames with specific properties. Notable examples are: the class of all
reflexive
Kripke frames, the class of all
transitive
Kripke frames, the class of all
reflexive and transitive
Kripke frames, and the class of all Kripke frames whose relation is an
equivalence
(that is, it is reflexive, transitive, and symmetric). The corresponding logics are usually called
\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
, and
\operatorname{\mathbf{S5}}
, respectively, and the notion of satisfiability in
\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
, and
\operatorname{\mathbf{S5}}
is modified accordingly.

In order to define sub-propositional fragments of a modal logic
\mathbf{L}
we start from the
clausal form
of
\mathbf{L}
-formulas, whose building blocks are the
positive literals
:

(2)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle::=

\displaystyle\top\mid p\mid{\Diamond}\lambda\mid{\Box}\lambda,

and we say that
\varphi
is in
clausal form
if it can be generated by the following grammar:

(3)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle::=

\displaystyle\lambda\mid\neg\lambda\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\nabla(\neg\lambda_{1}\vee\ldots\vee\neg\lambda_{n}\vee\lambda_{n+1}\vee\ldots\vee\lambda_{n+m})

where
\nabla=\underbrace{\Box\Box\ldots}_{s}
,
s\geq 0
and
\nabla(\neg\lambda_{1}\vee\ldots\vee\neg\lambda_{n}\vee\lambda_{n+1}\vee\ldots\vee\lambda_{n+m})
is a
clause
.

In the following, we use
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots
to denote clauses,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots
to denote positive literals, and
\varphi,\psi,\xi,\ldots
to denote formulas. We write clauses in their implicative form
\nabla(\lambda_{1}\land\ldots\land\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda_{n+1}\vee\ldots\vee\lambda_{n+m})
, and use
\bot
as a shortcut for
\neg\top
.
Moreover, since positive and negative literals
\lambda
and
\neg\lambda
are equivalent to the clauses
\top\rightarrow\lambda
and
\lambda\rightarrow\bot
, respectively, from now on we assume that any formula produced by (
3
) is a conjunction of clauses
\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge\varphi_{l}
, unless specified otherwise.
It is known that every modal logic formula can be rewritten in clausal form
[
Ngu04
]
.

Theorem
\thethm

[
Ngu04
]
.

For every modal logic formula
\varphi
there exists an equisatisfiable conjunction of clauses
\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge\varphi_{l}
.

Sub-propositional
fragments of a modal logic
\mathbf{L}
can be now defined by constraining the cardinality and the structure of clauses: the fragment of
\mathbf{L}
in clausal form where each clause in (
3
) is such that
m\leq 1
is called
Horn
fragment, and denoted by
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
, and when each clause is such that
n+m\leq 2
it is called
Krom
fragment, and it is denoted by
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
; when both restrictions apply we denote the resulting fragment, the
core
fragment, by
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
. It is also interesting to study the fragments that can be obtained from by allowing only the use of
\Box
or
\Diamond
in positive literals, that are generically called the
box
and
diamond
fragments of modal logic
\mathbf{L}
.
In this way, we define
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Diamond}}}
as, respectively, the box and the diamond fragments of
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
. By applying the same restrictions to
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
, we obtain the pair
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom,\Box}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom,\Diamond}}}
from the former, and the pair
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core,\Box}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core,\Diamond}}}
, from the latter. Obviously, it makes no sense to consider the fragments
\mathbf{L}^{\Box}
and
\mathbf{L}^{\Diamond}
, as boxes are diamonds are mutually definable when the full propositional power is allowed.

It should be noted that in the literature there is no unified definition of the different modal or temporal sub-propositional logics. Our definition follows Nguyen
[
Ngu04
]
, with a notable difference: while the definition of clauses is the same, we choose a more restrictive definition of what is a formula. Hence, a formula of
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
by our definition is also a Horn formula by
[
Ngu04
]
, but not vice versa. However, since every Horn formula by the definition of Nguyen can be transformed into a conjunction of Horn clauses, the two definitions are equivalent. The definitions of
[
CL94
,
FP87
]
are equivalent to that of Nguyen, and hence to our own.
Other approaches force clauses to be quantified using a
universal
modality that asserts the truth of a formula in every world of the model. The universal modality is either assumed in the language
[
AKRZ13
]
or it is definable using the other modalities
[
BKMV+17
,
BMS14
]
, but the common choice in the literature of modal (non-temporal) logic is simply excluding the universal modality. However, most of our results in expressive power (see the next section) still hold when clauses are universally quantified; the question of whether our complexity results hold as well, on the contrary, is still open.

Among all sub-propositional fragments that emerge from the above discussion, we shall see that one is particularly interesting, that is,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
, which presents the sharpest improvement in complexity of the satisfiability problem when compared to the original language
\mathbf{L}
. It is worth to notice that such fragment is extremely natural: in fact, it naturally corresponds to first order Horn logic. To see this, consider the clause

p\rightarrow{\Diamond}q,

which is Horn, but not Horn box. It translates to the first order clause

(\forall x)(P(x)\rightarrow\exists y(xRy\wedge Q(y))),

which is not Horn, nor it has a first order Horn equivalent. On the other hand, the clause

p\rightarrow{\Box}q,

which is Horn box, translates to the first order clause

(\forall x)(P(x)\rightarrow\forall y(xRy\rightarrow Q(y))),

that is,

(\forall x)\forall y((P(x)\wedge xRy)\rightarrow Q(y)),

which is a first order Horn clause.

3.
Relative Expressive Power

In this section, we study the relative expressive power of
\operatorname{\mathbf{L}}\in\{\operatorname{\mathbf{K}},\operatorname{\mathbf{T}},\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}},\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}\}
and their fragments. The simplest way to compare the relative expressive power of such fragments is by syntactical containment (see Fig.
2
, where
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Bool}}}
stands for
\mathbf{L}
to stress that we mean the unconstrained language), but, obviously, we want to describe the relationships among fragments in a more precise way.
Given two fragments
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
and
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
(where
\circ,\circ^{\prime}
represent generic restrictions among those we have discussed above), interpreted in the same class of relational frames
\mathcal{C}
, we say that
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
and
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
are
model-preserving equivalent
, denoted
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}\equiv^{p}\mathbf{\mathbf{}}L^{\circ^{\prime}}
, if there exists an effective translation
(\cdot)^{\prime}
from
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
to
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
within the same propositional alphabet such that for every model
M
in
\mathcal{C}
, world
w
in
M
, and formula
\varphi
of
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
, we have
M,w\Vdash\varphi
if and only if
M,w\Vdash\varphi^{\prime}
, and the other way around.
If there exists a translation from
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
to
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
, but not the other way around, then we say that
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
is
weakly less expressive than

\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
, and we denote this situation by
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}\prec^{w}\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
. Moreover, we say that
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
and
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
, interpreted in the same class of relational frames
\mathcal{C}
, are
model-extending equivalent
, denoted
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}\equiv^{e}\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
, if there exists an effective translation
(\cdot)^{\prime}
that transforms any
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
-formula
\varphi
written in the alphabet
\mathcal{P}
into a
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
-formula written in a suitable alphabet
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathcal{P}
, such that for every model
M
in
\mathcal{C}
and world
w
in
M
, we have that
M,w\Vdash\varphi
if and only if we can extend the valuation
V
of
M
from
\mathcal{P}
to
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
to obtain an extended model
M^{\prime}
such that
M^{\prime},w\Vdash\varphi^{\prime}
, and the other way around. In this case, we refer to
M^{\prime}
as an
extension
of
M
. Once again, if there exists such a translation from
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
to
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
, but not from
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
to
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
, then
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
is
strongly less expressive than

\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
, and we denote this situation by
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}\prec^{s}\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
.

So, for example, Theorem
2
proves that modal logic is model-extending equivalent to its clausal form, but not necessarily model-preserving equivalent. In the same way, it is well-known that, in propositional logic, the conjunctive normal form (CNF) is model-extending equivalent, but not necessarily model-preserving equivalent, to the conjunctive normal form where every clause has at most three literals (3CNF).
Thus, the notion of model-extending equivalence is somehow more natural than the notion of model-preserving equivalence; nevertheless, both contribute to the understanding of the relative expressive power of logics.
Notice that if
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
and
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
are not model-extending equivalent, then they are not model-preserving equivalent, either, and that, consequently,
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
may be weakly less expressive than
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
but not strongly less expressive. Thus,
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}\prec^{s}\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
is a stronger notion than
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}\prec^{w}\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
: in the former case, there is no set of propositional letters that we may add to the language to translate formulas of
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
into formulas of
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
, while in the latter case, we know that
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
-formulas cannot be translated into
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
-formulas within the same propositional alphabet. Given
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
and
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
such that
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
is a syntactical fragment of
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
, in order to prove that
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}\prec^{w}\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
(or that
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}\prec^{s}\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
) we show a
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
-formula
\psi
that cannot be written in the language
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
without adding new propositional letters (or by adding finitely many new propositional letters). To this end we proceed by contradiction, assuming that a translation
\varphi\in\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
does exist, and by building a model for
\psi
that is not (cannot be extended to) a model of
\varphi
, following three different strategies: we modify the labeling (Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
), we modify the structure (Theorem
9
and Theorem
10
), or we exploit a property of
\mathbf{L}^{\circ}
that
\mathbf{L}^{\circ^{\prime}}
does not possess (Theorem
1
, Theorem
5
and Theorem
7
). All our results, both about the relative expressive power of fragments as well as about the complexity of their satisfiability problem can be immediately extended to the multi-modal version of the languages.

Following
[
Ngu00
]
, consider any two models
M_{1},M_{2}
based, respectively, on the sets of worlds
W_{1},W_{2}
, and two worlds
w_{1}\in W_{1}
and
w_{2}\in W_{2}
. We say that
M_{1}
is
less than or equal to

M_{2}

relatively to

w_{1}

and

w_{2}
if and only if there exists a relation
\mathcal{S}\subseteq W_{1}\times W_{2}
that respects the following properties:

(i)

(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathcal{S}
;

(ii)

for every
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{S}
,
V_{1}(u_{1})\subseteq V_{2}(u_{2})
;

(iii)

for every
u_{1},v_{1}\in W_{1}
and
u_{2}\in W_{2}
, if

Proof 3.1 .

Since
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}

\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}

(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}

\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}

Theorem 2 .

For
\operatorname{\mathbf{L}}\in\{\operatorname{\mathbf{K}},\operatorname{\mathbf{T}},\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}},\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}\}
:

(1)

\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}\prec^{w}\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Bool}}}
;

(2)

\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}\prec^{w}\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
.

Proof 3.2 .

Since
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
) is a syntactical fragment of
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Bool}}}
(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
), it only has to be proved that there exists a formula that belongs to
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Bool}}}
(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
) and that cannot be translated into
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
) within the same propositional alphabet. Here, we prove that this is the case for a
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
-formula (which is a
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Bool}}}

-formula as well) that cannot be translated into
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
(and, therefore, to
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
, either). Now, consider the
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
-formula

\psi=p\land q\rightarrow r,

and suppose, by contradiction, that there exists a
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
-formula
\varphi
, written in the propositional alphabet
\{p,q,r\}
, such that for every model
M
and every world
w
we have that
M,w\Vdash\psi
if and only if
M,w\Vdash\varphi.
We can assume that
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\land\ldots\land\varphi_{l}
is a conjunction of clauses. Let us denote by
P(\varphi_{i})
the set of propositional letters that occur in
\varphi_{i}
. Now, consider a model
M=\langle\mathcal{F},V\rangle
, where
\mathcal{F}
is based on a set of worlds
W
and it respects the semantical properties of
\operatorname{\mathbf{L}}
,
and let
w\in W
be a world such that
M,w\not\Vdash\psi
. Such a model must exist since
\psi
is not a tautology. By hypothesis,
M,w\not\Vdash\varphi
, and since
\varphi
is a conjunction of Krom clauses, there must exist at least one clause
\varphi_{i}=\nabla(\lambda_{1}\vee\lambda_{2})
such that
M,w\not\Vdash\varphi_{i}
. Hence, there must exist a world
w^{\prime}
such that
M,w^{\prime}\not\Vdash(\lambda_{1}\vee\lambda_{2})
. At this point, three cases may arise (since we are in a fixed propositional alphabet, and we deal with clauses at most binary):

•

P(\varphi_{i})\subseteq\{p,q\}
. In this case, we can build a new model
M^{\prime}=\langle\mathcal{F},V^{\prime}\rangle
such that:

V^{\prime}(p)=V(p),\ V^{\prime}(q)=V(q),\penalty\ \mbox{and}\penalty\ V^{\prime}(r)=W.

Since
r
holds on every world of the model, we have that
M^{\prime}
satisfies
\psi
everywhere, and in particular on
w
. However, since the valuation of
p
and
q
are the same of
M
, and since the relational structure has not changed, we have that
M^{\prime},w^{\prime}\not\models\lambda_{1}\lor\lambda_{2}
, from which we can conclude that
M^{\prime},w\not\Vdash\nabla(\lambda_{1}\lor\lambda_{2})
and thus that
w
does not satisfy
\varphi
.

•

P(\varphi_{i})\subseteq\{p,r\}
. In this case, we can build a new model
M^{\prime}=\langle\mathcal{F},V^{\prime}\rangle
such that:

V^{\prime}(p)=V(p),\ V^{\prime}(r)=V(r),\penalty\ \mbox{and}\penalty\ V^{\prime}(q)=\emptyset.

Since
q
is false on every world of the model, we have that
M^{\prime}
satisfies
\psi
everywhere, and in particular on
w
. However, since the valuation of
p
and
r
are the same of
M
, and since the relational structure has not changed, we have that
M^{\prime},w^{\prime}\not\models\lambda_{1}\lor\lambda_{2}
, from which we can conclude that
M^{\prime},w\not\Vdash\nabla(\lambda_{1}\lor\lambda_{2})
and thus that
w
does not satisfy
\varphi
.

•

P(\varphi_{i})\subseteq\{q,r\}
. In this case, the same argument as above works by using
q
instead of

.

Therefore,
\varphi
cannot exist, and this means that
\psi
cannot be expressed in
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
within the same propositional alphabet. Since the proof does not depend on the properties of the frame, the claim is proved for any logic
\operatorname{\mathbf{L}}\in\{\operatorname{\mathbf{K}},\operatorname{\mathbf{T}},\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}},\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}\}
.

Corollary 3 .

For
\operatorname{\mathbf{L}}\in\{\operatorname{\mathbf{K}},\operatorname{\mathbf{T}},\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}},\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}\}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
are expressively incomparable within the same propositional alphabet.

Proof 3.3 .

As we have seen in Theorem
1
, the
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
-formula
p\lor q
cannot be translated into
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
over any extension of the propositional alphabet, and, as we have seen in Theorem
2
, the
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
-formula
p\land q\rightarrow r
cannot be translated into
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
within the same propositional alphabet.
These two observations, together, prove that we cannot compare
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Krom}}}
within the same propositional alphabet.

Now, we turn our attention to the relative expressive power for box and diamond fragments, starting with sub-Horn fragments without diamonds. To this end, consider two models
M_{1},M_{2}
such that all
M_{i}=(\mathcal{F},V_{i})
are based on the same relational frame: we define the
intersection
model as the unique model
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}}=(\mathcal{F},V_{V_{1}\cap V_{2}})
, where, for each
w\in W
,
V_{V_{1}\cap V_{2}}(w)=V_{1}(w)\cap V_{2}(w)
. We prove now that the Horn fragments without diamonds are closed under intersection, that is, if two models satisfy a formula then their intersection also satisfies the formula.

Lemma 4 .

For
\operatorname{\mathbf{L}}\in\{\operatorname{\mathbf{K}},\operatorname{\mathbf{T}},\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}},\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}\}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
is closed under intersection of models.

Proof 3.4 .

Let
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\varphi_{l}
a
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
-formula such that
M_{1},w\Vdash\varphi
and
M_{2},w\Vdash\varphi
, where
M_{1}=(\mathcal{F},V_{1})
and
M_{2}=(\mathcal{F},V_{2})
; we want to prove that
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w\Vdash\varphi
. Suppose, by way of contradiction, that
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w\not\Vdash\varphi
. Then, there must be some
\varphi_{i}
such that
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w\not\Vdash\varphi_{i}
. As in Theorem
1
, we can assume that
\varphi_{i}
is a clause of the type
\nabla(\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda)
. This means that
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w^{\prime}\Vdash\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}
and
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w^{\prime}\not\Vdash\lambda
for some
w^{\prime}
. We want to prove that, for each
1\leq j\leq n
, both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
satisfy
\lambda_{j}
at
w^{\prime}
. To see this, let
N=md(\lambda_{j})
, and:

•

If
N=0
, then
\lambda_{j}=p
for some propositional letter
p
; but if
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w^{\prime}\Vdash p
, then
p\in V_{1}(w^{\prime})\cap V_{2}(w^{\prime})
, which means that
M_{1},w^{\prime}\Vdash p
and
M_{2},w^{\prime}\Vdash p
.

•

If
N>0
, then
\lambda_{j}={\Box}\lambda^{\prime}
. Since
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w^{\prime}\Vdash{\Box}\lambda^{\prime}
, for every
v
such that
w^{\prime}\mathrel{R}v
it is the case that
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},v\Vdash\lambda^{\prime}
. Thus, for every
v
such that
w^{\prime}\mathrel{R}v
, we know by inductive hypothesis that
M_{1},v\Vdash\lambda^{\prime}
and
M_{2},v\Vdash\lambda^{\prime}
. But this immediately implies that
M_{1},w^{\prime}\Vdash{\Box}\lambda^{\prime}
and
M_{2},w^{\prime}\Vdash{\Box}\lambda^{\prime}
, which completes the induction. Now, we have that
M_{1},w^{\prime}\Vdash\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}
and
M_{2},w^{\prime}\Vdash\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}
; therefore,
M_{1},w^{\prime}\Vdash\lambda
and
M_{2},w^{\prime}\Vdash\lambda
.

A similar inductive argument shows that
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w^{\prime}\Vdash\lambda
, implying that
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w\Vdash\varphi_{i}
; but this contradicts our hypothesis that
M_{M_{1}\cap M_{2}},w\not\Vdash\varphi
.

Theorem 5 .

For
\operatorname{\mathbf{L}}\in\{\operatorname{\mathbf{K}},\operatorname{\mathbf{T}},\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}},\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}\}
:

(1)

\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}\prec^{s}\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
;

(2)

\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core,\Box}}}\prec^{s}\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
.

Proof 3.5 .

Since
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core,\Box}}}
) is a syntactical fragment of
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
), it only has to be proved that there exists a formula that belongs to
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
) and that cannot be translated into
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
(resp.,
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core,\Box}}}
) over any finite extension of the propositional alphabet. Here, we prove that this is the case for a
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core}}}
-formula (which is a
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
-formula as well) that cannot be translated into
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
(and, therefore, to
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core,\Box}}}
, either). Let
\mathcal{P}=\{p\}
, consider the
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn}}}
-formula

\psi={\Diamond}p,

and suppose by contradiction that there exists a propositional alphabet
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathcal{P}
and a
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}

\varphi
written over
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
such that for every model
M
over the propositional alphabet
\mathcal{P}
and every world

we have that
M,w\Vdash\psi
if and only if there exists
M^{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
such that
M^{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}},w\Vdash\varphi.
Let
M_{1}=(\mathcal{F},V_{1})
and
M_{2}=(\mathcal{F},V_{2})
, where
\mathcal{F}
is based on the set

. Let
\mathrel{R}=\{(w_{0},w_{1}),(w_{0},w_{2})\}

V_{i}(w_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{p\}&\mbox{if }i=j,\\
\emptyset&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Clearly,

and
M_{2},w_{0}\Vdash\psi
; since
\varphi
is a
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
-translation of
\psi
, it must be the case that, for some extensions
M_{1}^{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
and
M_{2}^{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
, we have that
M_{1}^{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}},w_{0}\Vdash\varphi

M_{2}^{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}},w_{0}\Vdash\varphi

\varphi
is a translation of
\psi
.

To establish the expressive power of
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Diamond}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core,\Diamond}}}
with respect to other fragments, we now prove a closure property similar to Lemma
4
. Consider two models
M_{1}=(\mathcal{F}_{1},V_{1})
,
M_{2}=(\mathcal{F}_{2},V_{2})
based on two (possibly different) relational frames. We define the

model as the unique model
M_{M_{1}\times M_{2}}=(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\times\mathcal{F}_{2}},V_{V_{1}\times V_{2}})
, where:

(i)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\times\mathcal{F}_{2}}=(W_{1}\times W_{2},\mathrel{R_{R_{1}\times R_{2}}})
, that is, worlds are all and only the pairs of worlds from

and
W_{2}
;
(w_{1},w_{2})\mathrel{R_{R_{1}\times R_{2}}}(w_{1}^{\prime},w_{2}^{\prime})
if and only if
w_{1}R_{1}w_{1}^{\prime}
and
w_{2}R_{2}w_{2}^{\prime}
, that is, worlds in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}\times\mathcal{F}_{2}}
are connected to each other as the component worlds were connected in
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
; and

(ii)

.

We prove now that the Horn fragments without boxes are closed under product.

Lemma 6 .

For
\operatorname{\mathbf{L}}\in\{\operatorname{\mathbf{K}},\operatorname{\mathbf{T}},\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}},\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}\}

Let

is a clause of the type
\nabla(\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda)

and
M_{M_{1}\times M_{2}},(w_{1}^{\prime},w_{2}^{\prime})\not\Vdash\lambda
for some

4.
Complexity

In this section we study the complexity of the satisfiability problem for
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{core,\Box}}}
and we give a modular algorithm for the satisfiability-checking of
\operatorname{\mathbf{L^{Horn,\Box}}}
-formulas.
We begin by proving that if a formula of the Horn box fragment of
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}
, or
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
is satisfiable, then it is satisfiable in a very simple (pre-linear) model of bounded size (Theorem
15
). Then we exploit this result to show that the satisfiability problem for such fragments is
P
-complete (Theorem
16
).
The complexity result does not give us a direct satisfiability-checking procedure. Thus, we define a modular algorithm that builds a model for the formula in deterministic polynomial time. Such a procedure is made of a common part (Algorithms
1
and
3
) and a specific “saturation procedure” that depends on the considered language (Algorithms
2
,
4
,
5
and
6

Correctness, completeness and complexity of the algorithms are proved in Theorem
23
.
Finally, we devote the last part of the section to the study of the core box fragments. We extend the well-known 2SAT algorithm to
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{core,\Box}}}
, thus proving
NLogSpace
-completess of this fragment (Theorem
26
), leaving the exact complexity of the other core box fragments as an open problem.

To prove the small-model theorem for
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}
, or
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
, we need to introduce some preliminary definitions. We say that a relational structure
(W,R)
is
pre-linear
if it is either a simple path or a lasso-shaped path, namely, if:

(i)

there exists a unique node
w_{0}
(called
root
) such that, for every
w\in W
,
w_{0}R^{*}w
;

(ii)

every
w\in W
has at most one
R
-successor.

(Recall that, for a relation
R
, we denote by
R^{*}
its reflexive and transitive closure.)
In a pre-linear structure we can enumerate the worlds in
W
as
w_{0},w_{1},\ldots
, where
w_{0}
is the root and for each
k\geq 0
we have that
w_{k+1}
is the unique
R
-successor of
w_{k}
. In the following, we use the term
pre-linear model
to denote a model built on a pre-linear relational structure. The following lemma, given for
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{Horn,\Box}}}
, will be later generalized to the other cases.

Lemma 13 .

Let
\varphi
be a
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{Horn,\Box}}}
-formula. Then,
\varphi
is satisfiable if and only if it is satisfiable in a pre-linear model.

Proof 4.1 .

Let
\varphi
be a satisfiable
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{Horn,\Box}}}
-formula, and let
M=(\mathcal{F},V)
, where
\mathcal{F}=(W,R)
, be a model that satisfies it at

. We can assume that
\mathcal{F}
is connected
[
BvBW06
]
. We now systematically build a pre-linear model
M^{\prime}
, with set of worlds
W^{\prime}
, as follows. The worlds in
W^{\prime}
are sets of worlds in
W
. Let us first define
W_{0}=\{w_{0}^{\prime}\}
,
w_{0}^{\prime}=\{w_{0}\}
,
R_{0}=\emptyset
, and
V_{0}(w_{0}^{\prime})=V(w_{0})
.
Now, given the generic pre-linear model
M_{k}=(\mathcal{F}_{k},V_{k})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{k}=(W_{k},R_{k})

M_{k+1}
as follows. Consider the ‘last’ world

, and define
w_{k+1}^{\prime}=\{w\mid w\in W\mbox{ and }\exists w^{\prime}\in w_{k}^{\prime}\mbox{ s.t. }(w^{\prime},w)\in R\}

. Clearly,
M^{\prime}=\bigcup_{k}M_{k}
is a pre-linear model.

To conclude the proof we have to show that
M^{\prime},w_{0}^{\prime}\Vdash\varphi
. Assume, by way of contradiction, that this is not the case, that is,
M^{\prime},w_{0}^{\prime}\not\Vdash\varphi
. Since
\varphi
can be thought as a conjunction of clauses, this means that
M^{\prime},w_{0}^{\prime}\not\Vdash\varphi_{i}
for some clause
\varphi_{i}={\Box}^{s}(\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda)

M^{\prime},w_{s}^{\prime}\not\Vdash\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda
, where
w_{s}^{\prime}
is the world reachable from

in exactly

steps. This implies that
M^{\prime},w_{s}^{\prime}\Vdash\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}
and
M^{\prime},w_{s}^{\prime}\not\Vdash\lambda
. Consider a positive literal
\lambda_{j}
in the body of the clause. We want to prove that
M,w\Vdash\lambda_{j}
for each
w\in w_{s}^{\prime}
. If
\lambda_{j}=p
or
\lambda_{j}=\top

w_{s}^{\prime}
is the intersection of the valuation (in
M
) of every world in
w_{s}^{\prime}
. If
\lambda_{j}={\Box}^{m}p
, then consider the world
w_{s+m}^{\prime}
reachable from
w_{s}^{\prime}
in exactly
m
steps: two cases arise. If such a
w_{s+m}^{\prime}
exists, then
M^{\prime},w_{s+m}^{\prime}\Vdash p
, and this means that (in
M
) every world
\overline{w}
in
w_{s+m}^{\prime}
is such that

, that is,

for every

. Otherwise, if
w_{s+m}^{\prime}

for every

by hypothesis, this means that for every
w\in w_{s}^{\prime}
it is the case that
M,w\Vdash\lambda_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda

M,w\Vdash\lambda_{j}
for every antecedent
\lambda_{j}
and every world

Proof 4.4 .

It is known that any Horn formula can be rewritten into a positive logic program (positive clauses) and a set of queries (negative clauses), and thus satisfiability can be reduced to checking the queries w.r.t. to the positive logic program.

[

\operatorname{\mathbf{T^{Horn}}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4^{Horn}}}

and
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4^{Horn,\Box}}}

P

[

We conclude this section by studying the particular case of the fragment
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{core,\Box}}}
. The results obtained so far allow us only to fix the complexity of the satisfiability problem in these cases between

). We want to prove that the satisfiability problem for
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{core,\Box}}}
, the satisfiability problem is, in fact,
NLogSpace
-complete; to this end, we extend the algorithm for 2SAT. The standard algorithm to solve a 2SAT instance exploits the properties of the
implication graph
of the formula. An implication graph is a directed graph in which there is one vertex per propositional variable or negated variable, and an edge connecting one vertex to another whenever the corresponding variables are related by an implication in the formula. A propositional binary formula is unsatisfiable if and only if there exists a path in the implication graph that starts from a variable
p
, leads to its negation
\neg p
and then goes back to
p
. The
NLogSpace
algorithm simply guesses the variable
p
and search nondeterministically for the chain of implications that corresponds to such a contradictory path. An alternative, indirect way to obtain this result would be to explore the characteristics of SLD-resolution
[
Ngu03
]
under the Horn box restriction of
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
.

In the implication graph of a
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{core,\Box}}}
-formula the vertexes are pairs where the first component is a positive literal
\lambda
or its negation
\neg\lambda
, and the second component is a natural number representing the depth of the world where
\lambda
(resp.,
\neg\lambda
) holds, measured as the distance from the root of the model; it is worth to recall that
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{core,\Box}}}
, as
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{Horn,\Box}}}
, enjoys the pre-linear model property, so that the notion of distance from the root makes perfect sense. The edges in the graph correspond either to implications in the formula (as in the propositional case), or to
jumps
from a world at some depth
d
to the world at depth
d+1
or
d-1
. Thus, given
\varphi\in\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{Horn,\Box}}}
, where
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{l}
, and given a natural number
D\leq md(\varphi)
, we define the
D
-implication graph of
\varphi
as a directed graph
G_{\varphi}^{D}=(V,E)
where
V=\{(\lambda,d)\mid\lambda\in Cl(\varphi)
or
\neg\lambda\in Cl(\varphi)
and
0\leq d<D\}
, and:

(i)

((\lambda,d),(\lambda^{\prime},d)),((\neg\lambda^{\prime},d),(\neg\lambda,d))\in E
if and only if
\varphi_{i}={\Box}^{d}(\neg\lambda\lor\lambda^{\prime})
for some
i
;

(ii)

(({\Box}\lambda,d),(\lambda,d+1))\in E
if and only if
d<D-1
;

(iii)

((\lambda,d),({\Box}\lambda,d-1))\in E
if and only if
d>0
;

(iv)

((\neg{\Box}\lambda,d),(\neg\lambda,d+1))\in E
if and only if
d<D-1
; and

(v)

((\neg\lambda,d),(\neg{\Box}\lambda,d-1))\in E
if and only if
d>0
.

Notice that, in this case, we treat
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{core,\Box}}}
-clauses as disjunctions, instead of implications; in this way, clauses that contain a single positive (resp., negative) literal
\lambda
(resp.,
\neg\lambda

(\lambda\vee\bot)
(resp.,
(\neg\lambda\vee\bot)

{\Box}^{s}(\bot\rightarrow\top)
for each

. The main property of the

-implication graph is that the formula on which built is unsatisfiable on (pre-linear) models of length

if and only if there exists a cycle

Lemma 24 .

Let
\varphi
be a formula of
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{Horn,\Box}}}
. Then, if there exists a contradictory cycle

in

is unsatisfiable on a linear model with precisely

Proof 4.12 .

Let
\varphi
be a formula of
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{Horn,\Box}}}
, and suppose that there exists a vertex
(\lambda,d)
and a contradictory cycle
\pi=(\lambda_{1},d_{1}),(\lambda_{2},d_{2}),\ldots,(\lambda_{|\pi|},d_{|\pi|})
in the implication graph
G_{\varphi}^{D}
such that
(\lambda,d)=(\lambda_{1},d_{1})
. Suppose by contradiction that
\varphi
is satisfiable on a pre-linear model
M
with precisely
D
distinct worlds such that
M,w_{0}\Vdash\varphi
. Two cases may arise: either
M,w_{d}\Vdash\lambda
or
M,w_{d}\Vdash\neg\lambda

(\lambda_{i},d_{i})\in\pi
it is the case that
M,w_{d_{i}}\Vdash\lambda_{i}
. We proceed by induction on
i

i=1

(\lambda_{1},d_{1})=(\lambda,d)
, and since

the property trivially holds. Now, suppose that the property is true for the vertex
(\lambda_{i},d_{i})

(\lambda_{i+1},d_{i+1})

•

: in this case we have that the clause
{\Box}^{d_{i}}(\neg\lambda_{i}\lor\lambda_{i+1})
is a conjunct of
\varphi

•

d_{i+1}=d_{i}+1

\lambda_{i}={\Box}\lambda
or
\lambda_{i}=\neg{\Box}\lambda
. In the first case, the inductive hypothesis is that
M,w_{d_{i}}\Vdash{\Box}\lambda
, which is to say that

Lemma 25 .

Let

Proof 4.13 .

d=d^{\prime}

The above lemmas can be then immediately used to devise a non-deterministic algorithm for

Theorem 26 .

The satisfiability problem for

Conclusions

In this paper, we considered the expressive power and complexity of sub-propositional fragments of the basic modal logics of all directed graphs (
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
) and its axiomatic reflexive (
\operatorname{\mathbf{T}}
), transitive (
\operatorname{\mathbf{K4}}
), and reflexive and transitive (
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
) extensions. The relative expressive power and the complexity results of the sub-propositional fragments of modal logic studied in this paper is depicted in Figures
3
and
4
, respectively. In most cases relative expressivity coincides with syntactical containment, with the notable exception of the Krom fragments, that are expressively equivalent, but not weakly expressively equivalent. Because of our very general approach for comparing the expressive power of languages, most of our result can be transferred to other sub-propositional modal logics such as the fragments of
\mathbf{LTL}
without Since and Until studied in
[
AKRZ13
]
and the sub-propositional fragments of
\mathbf{HS}

[
AKRZ15
,
BKMV+17
,
BMS14
]
. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work where sub-Krom and sub-Horn fragments of these modal logics have been considered. Concerning complexity, starting from the known result that every modal logic between
\operatorname{\mathbf{K}}
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
is still
PSpace
-complete even under the Horn restriction
[
Ngu04
]
(in opposition to the case of
\operatorname{\mathbf{S5}}
, which goes from
NP
-complete to
P
-complete), we proved that eliminating the use of diamonds in Horn formulas reduces the complexity of the satisfiability problem in all considered cases; some of our results can be also proved by using the machinery in
[
Ngu00
,
Ngu16
]
, which, on the other hand, does not immediately provide an implementable satisfiability-checking procedure. We proved that the satisfiability problem becomes
P
-complete in the Horn fragment without diamonds, between
NLogSpace
and
P
in the core fragment without diamonds (and
NLogSpace
-complete in the special case of
\operatorname{\mathbf{K^{core,\Box}}}
), and we devised a fast satisfiability-checking algorithms for all considered cases.

While this study has been inspired from similar results in the temporal case, such as for fragments of
\mathbf{LTL}
without Since and Until and fragment of
\mathbf{HS}
, several natural questions concerning sub-Horn temporal logics remain open, and our results can certainly offer a solid starting point to their tackling. To mention one interesting case, consider
\mathbf{HS}
under the Horn restriction without diamonds: it is known that it is still undecidable in the discrete case
[
BKMV+17
]
, but such a proof makes extensive use of many different temporal operators; what would happen by considering simple fragments such as
\mathrm{A}
or
\mathrm{D}
(i.e., the fragments with a single modal operator corresponding to, respectively, Allen’s relation
meets
and Allen’s relation
during
) is unclear. Our technique, based on the intrinsic inability of
\operatorname{\mathbf{S4}}
to force the non-linearity of a model under the considered restriction, seems, at least in principle, applicable to such a case. Other interesting cases include the behaviour of
\mathbf{LTL}
with Since/Until under the restrictions considered in this paper.

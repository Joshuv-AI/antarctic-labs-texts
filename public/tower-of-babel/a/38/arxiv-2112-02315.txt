The Non-cutoff Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann and Vlasov-Poisson-Landau Systems in Union of Cubes Thanks:
Dingqun Deng

Abstract
. This work concerns the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system without angular cutoff and Vlasov-Poisson-Landau system including Coulomb interaction in bounded domain, namely union of cubes. We establish the global stability, exponential large-time decay with specular-reflection boundary condition when an initial datum is near Maxwellian equilibrium. We provide the compatible specular boundary condition for high-order derivatives and a velocity weighted energy estimate.

Primary 76P05; Secondary 35Q20, 82C40.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Equation

We consider the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann (VPB) and Vlasov-Poisson-Landau (VPL) systems describing the motion of plasma particles of two species in domain
\Omega
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}F_{+}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{+}-\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{+}=Q(F_{+},F_{+})+Q(F_{-},F_{+}),\\
&\partial_{t}F_{-}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{-}+\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}F_{-}=Q(F_{+},F_{-})+Q(F_{-},F_{-}),\\
&-\Delta_{x}\phi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(F_{+}-F_{-})\,dv,\\
&F_{\pm}(0,x,v)=F_{0,\pm}(x,v),\quad E(0,x)=E_{0}(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here, the unknown
F=[F_{+},F_{-}]
is the velocity distribution functions for the particles of ions
(+)
and electrons
(-)
, respectively, at position
x\in\Omega
and velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and time
t\geq 0
.
The self-consistent electrostatic field takes the form
E(t,x)=-\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)
.
The boundary condition for
(f,E)
will be given in (
1.7
) and (
1.8
). Next we introduce the collision operator
Q
first.

For Vlasov-Poisson-Landau system, the collision operator
Q
is given by

\displaystyle Q(G,F)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{v}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi(v-v^{\prime})\big[G(v^{\prime})\nabla_{v}F(v)-F(v)\nabla_{v}G(v^{\prime})\big]\,dv^{\prime}.

The non-negative definite matrix-valued function
\phi=[\phi^{ij}(v)]_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}
takes the form of

\displaystyle\phi^{ij}(v)=\Big\{\delta_{ij}-\frac{v_{i}v_{j}}{|v|^{2}}\Big\}|v|^{\gamma+2},

(1.2)

with
\gamma\geq-3
. It is convenient to call it
hard potential
when
\gamma\geq-2
and
soft potential
when
-3\leq\gamma<-2
. The case
\gamma=-3
corresponds to the physically realistic Coulomb interactions; cf.
[
19
]
.

For Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system, the collision operator
Q
is defined by

\displaystyle Q(G,F)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(v-v_{*},\sigma)\big[G(v^{\prime}_{*})F(v^{\prime})-G(v_{*})F(v)\big]\,d\sigma dv_{*}.

In this expression
v,v_{*}
and
v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*}
are velocity pairs given in terms of the
\sigma
-representation by

\displaystyle v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\quad v^{\prime}_{*}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

that satisfy
conservation laws of momentum and energy:

v+v_{*}=v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}_{*},\quad|v|^{2}+|v_{*}|^{2}=|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2}.

The Boltzmann collision kernel
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
depends only on
|v-v_{*}|
and the deviation angle
\theta
through
\cos\theta=\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma
. Without loss of generality we can assume
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
is supported on
0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2
, since one can reduce the situation with
symmetrization
:
\overline{B}(v-v_{*},\sigma)={B}(v-v_{*},\sigma)+{B}(v-v_{*},-\sigma)
. Moreover, we assume

B(v-v_{*},\sigma)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}b(\cos\theta),

and there exist
C_{b}>0
and
0<s<1
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{b}\theta^{1+2s}}\leq\sin\theta b(\cos\theta)\leq\frac{C_{b}}{\theta^{1+2s}},\quad\forall\,\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}].

It is convenient call it
hard potential
when
\gamma+2s\geq 0
and
soft potential
when
-3<\gamma+2s<0
.
Throughout the paper, we will assume

\displaystyle-3\leq\gamma\leq 1\text{ for Landau case,}

\displaystyle\max\{-3,-2s-\frac{3}{2}\}<\gamma<1-2s,\ 0<s<1\ \text{ for Boltzmann case}.

Note that we consider the full range of
0<s<1
for Boltzmann case.

We reformulate problem (
1.1
) near a global Maxwellian as the following.
Let
\mu
be the global Maxwellian equilibrium state:

\mu=\mu(v)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}e^{-|v|^{2}/2}.

We construct a solution to (
1.1
) of the form

F(t,x,v)=\mu+\mu^{1/2}f(t,x,v).

Then
f=[f_{+},f_{-}]
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f_{\pm}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\pm}\pm\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot vf_{\pm}\mp\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\pm}\pm\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot v\mu^{1/2}-L_{\pm}f=\Gamma_{\pm}(f,f),\\
&-\Delta_{x}\phi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{+}-f_{-})\mu^{1/2}\,dv,\\
&f(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\quad E(0,x)=E_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

where the linearized collision operator
L=[L_{+},L_{-}]
and nonlinear collision operator
\Gamma=[\Gamma_{+},\Gamma_{-}]
are given respectively by

L_{\pm}f=\mu^{-1/2}\Big\{2Q(\mu,\mu^{1/2}f_{\pm})+Q(\mu^{1/2}(f_{\pm}+f_{\mp}),\mu)\Big\},

and

\Gamma_{\pm}(f,g)=\mu^{-1/2}\Big\{Q(\mu^{1/2}f_{\pm},\mu^{1/2}g_{\pm})+Q(\mu^{1/2}f_{\mp},\mu^{1/2}g_{\pm})\Big\}.

The kernel of
L
on
L^{2}_{v}\times L^{2}_{v}
is the span of
\{[1,0]\mu^{1/2},[0,1]\mu^{1/2},[1,1]v\mu^{1/2},[1,1]|v|^{2}\mu^{1/2}\}
and we define the projection
\mathbf{P}=[\mathbf{P}_{+},\mathbf{P}_{-}]
from
L^{2}_{v}\times L^{2}_{v}
onto
\ker L
to be

\mathbf{P}f=\Big(a_{+}(t,x)[1,0]+a_{-}(t,x)[0,1]+v\cdot b(t,x)[1,1]+(|v|^{2}-3)c(t,x)[1,1]\Big)\mu^{1/2},

where functions
a_{\pm},b,c
are given by

\displaystyle a_{\pm}

\displaystyle=(\mu^{1/2},f_{\pm})_{L^{2}_{v}},

(1.4)

\displaystyle b_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(v_{j}\mu^{1/2},f_{+}+f_{-})_{L^{2}_{v}},

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}((|v|^{2}-3)\mu^{1/2},f_{+}+f_{-})_{L^{2}_{v}}.

Then for given
f
, one can decompose
f
uniquely as the macroscopic part microscopic part:

f=\mathbf{P}f+(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P})f.

It’s well-known that the solution to (
1.3
) satisfies the conservation laws on mass and energy. That is, the solution
f
to (
1.3
) satisfies the following identities whenever it’s satisfied initially at
t=0
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{+}(t)\mu^{1/2}\,dvdx=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{-}(t)\mu^{1/2}\,dvdx=0,\\
&\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{+}(t)+f_{-}(t))|v|^{2}\mu^{1/2}\,dvdx+\int_{\Omega}|E(t)|^{2}\,dx=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

1.2.
Spatial Domain

In this paper, we consider a domain
\Omega
that is the union of finitely many cubes:

\displaystyle\Omega=\cup_{i=1}^{N}\Omega_{i},

(1.6)

where
\Omega_{i}=(a_{i,1},b_{i,1})\times(a_{i,2},b_{i,2})\times(a_{i,3},b_{i,3})
with
a_{i,j},b_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a_{i,j}<b_{i,j}
. Then

\partial\Omega=\cup_{i=1}^{3}\Gamma_{i}
is the union of three kinds of boundary
\Gamma_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
, where
\Gamma_{i}
is orthogonal to axis
x_{i}
and is the union of finitely many connected sets. We further assume that
\Gamma_{i}
is of
non-zero
spherical measure.
Since the boundary of
\Gamma_{i}
’s are of
zero
spherical measure, we don’t distinguish
\Gamma_{i}
and the interior of
\Gamma_{i}
. Note that
\Omega
could be
non-convex
and be closed to general bounded domains arbitrarily.

The unit normal outer vector
n(x)
exists on
\partial\Omega
almost everywhere with respect to spherical measure. On the interior of
\Gamma_{i}
(i=1,2,3)
, we have
n(x)=e_{i}
or
-e_{i}
, where
e_{i}
is the unit vector with
i
th-component being
1
. We will denote vectors
\tau_{1}(x),\tau_{2}(x)
on boundary
\partial\Omega
such that
(n(x),\tau_{1}(x),\tau_{2}(x))
forms an unit orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that for
j=1,2
,
\tau_{j}=e_{k}
or
-e_{k}
for some
k
. This implies that
\partial_{\tau_{j}}
is the tangent derivative on
\partial\Omega
for
j=1,2
.

The boundary of the phase space is

\displaystyle\gamma:=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\}.

Denoting
n=n(x)
to be the outward normal direction at
x\in\partial\Omega
, we decompose
\gamma
as

\displaystyle\gamma_{-}

\displaystyle=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v<0\},\quad\text{(the incoming set),}

\displaystyle\gamma_{+}

\displaystyle=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v>0\},\quad\text{(the outgoing set),}

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v=0\},\quad\text{(the grazing set).}

Correspondingly, we assume that
F(t,x,v)
satisfies the
specular-reflection
boundary condition:

F(t,x,R_{x}v)=F(t,x,v),\ \text{ on }\gamma_{-},

where for
(x,v)\in\gamma
,

R_{x}v=v-2n(x)(n(x)\cdot v).

This is equivalent to the
specular reflection
boundary condition for perturbation
f
:

\displaystyle f(t,x,R_{x}v)=f(t,x,v),\ \text{ on }\gamma_{-}.

(1.7)

For the boundary condition of electric potential
\phi
, we further assume that

\displaystyle\partial_{n}\phi=0,\ \text{ on }x\in\partial\Omega.

(1.8)

In particular, the Poisson equation for potential
\phi
is a pure Neumann boundary problem and we require zero-mean condition

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{+}-f_{-})\mu^{1/2}\,dvdx=0,\quad\text{ for }t\geq 0,

to ensure its existence, which follows from (
1.5
). Also, the zero-mean condition

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi(t,x)\,dx=0,\ \text{ for }t\geq 0

ensures the uniqueness of solutions.

1.4.
Main Results

In this section, we state our main results on global well-posedness of Vlasov-Poisson-Landau systems and Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann systems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be defined by (
1.6
) and
w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)
be given by (
1.9
).
Let
\gamma\geq-3
for Landau case and
(\gamma,s)\in\{-\frac{3}{2}<\gamma+2s\leq 1,\ 0<s<1\}
for Boltzmann case.
Let
l\geq 3q
with

is given in (
1.10
).
There exists
\varepsilon_{0},\nu>0
such that if
F_{0}(x,v)=\mu+\mu^{1/2}f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0
satisfying (
1.5
) and

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\big(\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}E_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\big)\leq\varepsilon_{0},

(1.14)

then there exists a unique solution

to the specular reflection boundary problem (
1.3
), (
1.7
) and (
1.8
), satisfying that
F(t,x,v)=\mu+\mu^{1/2}f(t,x,v)\geq 0
and for any

,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}e^{\delta t^{p}}\mathcal{E}(t)+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(t)\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},

where
\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(t)
and
\mathcal{E}(t)=\mathcal{E}_{0}(t)
are defined by (
1.11
).

As in
[
7
]
, we consider the bounded domain
\Omega
as union of cubes. In this case, normal derivatives
\partial_{n}
on
\partial\Omega
are also derivatives along axis. By using

v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=v\cdot n(x)\partial_{n}f+v\cdot\tau_{1}(x)\partial_{\tau_{1}}f+v\cdot\tau_{2}(x)\partial_{\tau_{2}}f,

and equation (
1.3
),
one can obtain the compatible high-order specular boundary condition in Lemma
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
. On the other hand,
\partial^{\alpha}
can be rewritten into normal derivative
\partial_{n}
and tangent derivative
\partial_{\tau_{1}}
and
\partial_{\tau_{2}}
on the boundary. Hence, the boundary term generated from
(\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}(v\cdot\nabla_{x}f),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}
vanishes by using high-order specular-reflection boundary condition.

In this work, we use space
H^{3}_{x,v}
up to third derivatives. In order to obtain the specular boundary condition, we need to assume the Neumann boundary condition for potential
\phi
. With the Poisson equation, we can also derive the third order boundary values for
\phi
; see (
3.19
):

\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}x_{i}}\phi=0,\ \ \text{ on }\Gamma_{i}.

Correspondingly, we can obtain the boundary values for macroscopic parts
\partial^{\alpha}[a_{\pm},b,c]
up to third derivatives:

\partial_{x_{i}}c(x)=\partial_{x_{i}}a_{\pm}(x)=\partial_{x_{i}}b_{j}(x)=b_{i}(x)\\
=\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}x_{i}}c(x)=\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}x_{i}}a_{\pm}(x)=\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}x_{i}}b_{j}(x)=\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}}b_{i}(x)=0.

These boundary values enable us to estimate the boundary terms and take integration by parts suitably with respect to
x
.

For the dissipation rate of
(a_{\pm},b,c)
in Section
3
, we use the solutions to Poisson equation
-\Delta_{x}\phi_{h}=h
with mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary condition or pure Neumann boundary condition. One should be careful when dealing with pure Neumann boundary condition. In this case, we need to assume the function
h
on the right hand side has
zero
mean:

\int_{\Omega}h\,dx=0.

Correspondingly, we need to assume the
zero
mean condition for
\phi_{h}
to ensure the uniqueness for pure Neumann boundary problem. By using Poincaré’s inequality, one can obtain the elliptic estimate for Poisson equation with Neumann boundary. The case of mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary problem is much easier since there’s
zero
condition on the boundary and one can apply Sobolev embedding. We will illustrate these calculations in Theorem
3.4
in details.

With the nice property of macroscopic parts, we use
e^{\pm\phi}
as in
[
22
]
to derive the energy estimates.
The exponential weight in (
1.9
) is designed to generate velocity decay after taking derivative. The
2|\beta|
in (
1.9
) for soft potential is designed for obtaining velocity decay when estimating
w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial_{\beta_{1}}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{\beta-\beta_{1}}f
. That is, when
|\beta_{1}|=1
, one can generate velocity decay by using
w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\lesssim\langle v\rangle^{\gamma+2s}w_{l,\nu}(\alpha+e_{i},\beta-e_{i})
. Then we can control it by using dissipation rate.

Using the boundary values carefully, we will use the integration by parts with respect to spatial variable
x
again and again in our analysis. Moreover, we will derive that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}=\|\Delta_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.

Similarly,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}E\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}=\|\Delta_{x}E\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.

Then we only need to estimate
\Delta_{x}f
in our proof, which is one of the key points.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

3
, we give the dissipation macroscopic estimates for VPL and VPB systems. In Section
4
, we prove the global existence with large-time behavior via estimates on instant energy (
1.11

1.12

5

a priori
estimate.

p^{\prime}=\frac{p-1}{\gamma+2s}
for Boltzmann case and
p^{\prime}=\frac{p-1}{\gamma+2}
for Landau case. Then on
\mathbf{E}
, we have

t^{p-1}\leq\langle v\rangle^{\frac{p-1}{p^{\prime}}}
,
and hence
t^{p-1}\mathcal{E}^{low}(t)\lesssim\mathcal{D}(t)
. It follows from (
4.29
) with
\nu=0
that

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}(t)+\lambda t^{p-1}\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim\big(\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{H^{2}_{x}}\big)\mathcal{E}(t)+\lambda t^{p-1}\mathcal{E}^{high}(t).

By solving this ODE, we have

\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim e^{\lambda t^{p}}\mathcal{E}(0)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\lambda(t^{p}-\tau^{p})}\big(\big(\|\partial_{\tau}\phi(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi(\tau)\|_{H^{2}_{x}}\big)\mathcal{E}(\tau)+\lambda t^{p-1}\mathcal{E}^{high}(\tau)\big)\,d\tau.

(4.35)

In what follows we estimate the terms in the time integral on the right-hand side of (
4.35
).
Firstly, by (
4.30
) and (
4.1
), we have

\big(\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{H^{2}_{x}}\big)\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)\lesssim e^{-\frac{3}{2}\delta t^{p}}X^{\frac{3}{2}}(t).

On the other hand, choose
p=p^{\prime}\vartheta
, i.e. choose
p
satisfying (
1.13
). Then on
\mathbf{E}^{c}
, we have

e^{-{\nu\langle v\rangle}}\leq e^{-{\nu t^{p^{\prime}\vartheta}}}=e^{-{\nu t^{p}}},

Recalling the exponential weight in (
1.9
), by (
4.33
), we have

\mathcal{E}^{high}(t)\lesssim e^{-{\nu t^{p}}}\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(t)\lesssim e^{-{\nu t^{p}}}\varepsilon_{0}.

Plugging the above estimates into (
4.35
), we have

\mathcal{E}(t)\lesssim e^{\lambda t^{p}}\mathcal{E}(0)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-\lambda(t^{p}-\tau^{p})}\big(e^{-\frac{3}{2}\delta\tau^{p}}X^{\frac{3}{2}}(\tau)+\varepsilon_{0}e^{-{\nu\tau^{p}}}\big)\,d\tau\lesssim e^{\delta t^{p}}(\varepsilon_{0}+X^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)),

by choosing
\lambda<\nu
.
This completes the claim (
4.34
). Recalling definition (
4.1
) for
X(t)
and using (
4.33
) and (
4.34
), we have

X(t)\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}+X^{\frac{3}{2}}(t).

Then choosing
\delta_{0}
in (
4.2
) sufficiently small, we have the
a priori
estimate:

X(t)\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}.

(4.36)

With (
4.31
), (
4.32
) and (
4.36
) in hand, under the smallness of (
1.14
), it’s now standard to apply the continuity argument with local existence from Section
5
to obtain the global existence, uniqueness and large time decay for initial boundary problem (
1.3
), (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) in bounded domain
\Omega
. The positivity of the solutions can be obtained from
[
22
, Lemma 12, page 800]
for VPL systems and
[
20
, page 1121]
for VPB systems.
This complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

∎

5.
Local Existence

In this section, we are concerned with the local-in-time existence of solutions to problem (
1.3
) in union of cubes. For brevity, we only consider the proof of Vlasov-Poisson-Landau systems when
\gamma\geq-3
, since the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann case is similar.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\gamma\geq-3
,
\Omega
be given by (
1.6
) and
w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)
be given by (
1.9
). Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
T_{0}>0
such that if
F_{0}(x,v)=\mu+\mu^{1/2}f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\big(\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}E_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\big)\leq\varepsilon_{0},

then the specular reflection boundary problem for VPL systems (
1.3
), (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) admits a unique solution
f(t,x,v)
on
t\in[0,T_{0}]
,
x\in\Omega
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, satisfying the uniform estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(t)+\int^{T_{0}}_{0}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(t)\,dt\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\big(\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}f_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}E_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\big),

(5.1)

where
\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(t)
,
\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(t)
are defined by (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) respectively.

We begin with the following linear inhomogeneous problem on the union of cubes:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f_{\pm}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\pm}\pm\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot vf_{\pm}\mp\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\pm}\pm\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot v\mu^{1/2}-A_{\pm}f\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\Gamma_{\pm}(g,h)+Kh,\\
&-\Delta_{x}\phi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{+}-f_{-})\mu^{1/2}\,dv,\\
&f(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\quad E(0,x)=E_{0}(x),\\
&{f}(t,x,R_{x}v)={f}(t,x,v),\text{ on }\gamma_{-},\\
&\partial_{n}\phi=0,\ \text{ on }x\in\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

for a given
h=h(t,x,v)
and
\psi=\psi(t,x)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let the same assumption in (
5.1
) be satisfied.
There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
T_{0}>0
such that if

\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\Big\{\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}h}\|_{L^{2}_{T_{0}}L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{D,w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)}}\\
+\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}h}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}\nabla_{x}\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}L^{2}_{x}}\Big\}+\|\partial_{t}{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

(5.3)

then the initial boundary value problem (
5.2
) admits a unique solution
f=f(t,x,v)
on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(t)+\int^{T_{0}}_{0}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(t)\,dt+\|\partial_{t}{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\big(\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}E_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\big)\\
+T_{0}^{1/2}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}h}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}L^{2}_{x,v}},

(5.4)

where
\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(t)
and
\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(t)
are defined by (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) respectively.

Proof.

We consider equation (
5.2
)
1
with initial data
(f_{0},E_{0})
.
Similar to (
4.11
), applying
\partial^{\alpha}
to (
5.2
)
1
and taking inner product of the resultant equation with
w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,0)e^{\pm\psi}\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\mp\frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{t}\psi\,w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm},e^{\pm\psi}w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\psi\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
\pm\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha_{1}<\alpha}\Big(\partial^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot v\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f_{\pm},e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\Big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
\mp\big(\partial^{\alpha}(\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\pm}),e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\pm\big(\partial^{\alpha}\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot v\mu^{1/2},e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
-\big(L_{\pm}\partial^{\alpha}f,w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}-\big(L_{\pm}\partial^{\alpha}f,(e^{\pm\psi}-1)w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
=\big(\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma_{\pm}(f,f),e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\big(\partial^{\alpha}h,e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,0)\partial^{\alpha}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Similar to (
4.22
), applying
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}
to (
5.2
)
1
and taking inner product of the resultant equation with
w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,2\beta)e^{\pm\psi}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\mp\big(\partial_{t}\psi\,\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm},e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,2\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
+\sum_{|\beta_{1}|=1}\big(\partial_{\beta_{1}}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta-\beta_{1}}f_{\pm},e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,2\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
\pm\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}<\alpha+\beta}\Big(\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}(\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot vf_{\pm}),e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,2\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}\Big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
\mp\big(\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}(\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\pm}),e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,2\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
\pm\big(\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}(\nabla_{x}\psi\cdot v\mu^{1/2}),e^{\pm\psi}w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,2\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
-\big(\partial_{\beta}L_{\pm}\partial^{\alpha}f,w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,2\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\
-\big(\partial_{\beta}L_{\pm}\partial^{\alpha}f,(e^{\pm\psi}-1)w_{2l,2\nu}(2\alpha,2\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{\pm}\big)_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\\

Then solving (

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(e^{Ct}\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(t)\big)+\lambda\int^{T}_{0}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(t)\,dt\lesssim T\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}h\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}},

for some large constant

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Write
(f_{0}^{\varepsilon},E_{0}^{\varepsilon})
to be the mollification of
(f_{0},E_{0})
as the following.
Let
\eta_{v}
and
\eta_{x}
be the standard mollifier in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\Omega
:
\eta_{v},\eta_{x}\in C^{\infty}_{c}
,
0\leq\eta_{v},\eta_{x}\leq 1
,
\int\zeta_{v}dv=\int\zeta_{x}dx=1
. For
\varepsilon>0
, let
\eta^{\varepsilon}_{v}(v)=\varepsilon^{-3}\zeta_{v}{(\varepsilon^{-1}v)}
and
\eta_{x}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-3}\zeta_{x}{(\varepsilon^{-1}x)}
. Then we mollify the initial data as
f_{0}^{\varepsilon}=f_{0}*\eta^{\varepsilon}_{v}*\eta^{\varepsilon}_{x}
,
E^{\varepsilon}_{0}=E_{0}*\eta_{x}^{\varepsilon}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}{f_{0}^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{0}*\eta^{\varepsilon}_{v}*\eta^{\varepsilon}_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{x_{1},v}}\lesssim\|\eta_{v}\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|{\eta_{x}}\|_{L^{1}_{x}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}{f_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}{f_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}},

and similarly,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}{E_{0}^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}_{x_{1}}}\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}{E_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Also,
f^{\varepsilon}_{0}\to f^{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
E^{\varepsilon}_{0}\to E_{0}
in
L^{2}_{x,v}
and
L^{2}_{x}
respectively as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
We now construct the approximation solution sequence as

\displaystyle\{(f^{n}(t,x,v),\phi^{n}(t,x))\}^{\infty}_{n=0}

by using the following iterative scheme:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}f^{n+1}_{\pm}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n+1}_{\pm}\pm\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\phi^{n}\cdot vf^{n+1}_{\pm}\mp\nabla_{x}\phi^{n}\cdot\nabla_{v}f^{n+1}_{\pm}\\
&\qquad\qquad\pm\nabla_{x}\phi^{n+1}\cdot v\mu^{1/2}-A_{\pm}f^{n+1}=\Gamma_{\pm}(f^{n},f^{n+1})+Kf^{n},\\
&-\Delta_{x}\phi^{n+1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f^{n+1}_{+}-f^{n+1}_{-})\mu^{1/2}\,dv,\\
&f^{n+1}(0,x,v)=f^{\frac{1}{n+1}}_{0}(x,v),\quad E^{n+1}(0,x)=E^{\frac{1}{n+1}}_{0}(x),\\
&{f}^{n+1}(t,x,R_{x}v)={f}^{n+1}(t,x,v),\text{ on }\gamma_{-},\\
&\partial_{n}\phi^{n+1}=0,\ \text{ on }x\in\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

for
n=0,1,2,\cdots
, where we set
f^{0}(t,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v)
and
\phi^{0}
given by
-\Delta_{x}\phi^{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f^{0}_{+}-f^{0}_{-})\mu^{1/2}\,dv
and
\partial_{n}\phi^{0}=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
With Lemma
5.2
, it is a standard procedure to apply the induction argument to show that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
T_{0}>0
such that if

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 3}\big(\|{w\partial^{\alpha}f_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}E_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\big)\leq\varepsilon_{0},

then the approximate solution sequence
\{f^{n}\}
is well-defined with estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\mathcal{E}_{\nu}(f^{n},t)+\int^{T_{0}}_{0}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(f^{n},t)\,dt+\|\partial_{t}{\phi^{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\big(\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}E_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\big)+T_{0}^{1/2}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n-1}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{0}^{k/2}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 3}\big(\|{w_{l,\nu}(\alpha,\beta)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|{\partial^{\alpha}E_{0}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\big)\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{2},

by choosing

\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(f^{n},t)

.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(f^{n+1}_{\pm}-f^{n}_{\pm})+v\cdot\nabla_{x}(f^{n+1}_{\pm}-f^{n}_{\pm})\pm\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\phi^{n}\cdot v(f^{n+1}_{\pm}-f^{n}_{\pm})\pm\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{x}\phi^{n}-\nabla_{x}\phi^{n-1})\cdot vf^{n}_{\pm}

\displaystyle\quad\mp\nabla_{x}\phi^{n}\cdot\nabla_{v}(f^{n+1}_{\pm}-f^{n}_{\pm})\mp(\nabla_{x}\phi^{n}-\nabla_{x}\phi^{n-1})\cdot\nabla_{v}f^{n}_{\pm}\pm(\nabla_{x}\phi^{n+1}-\nabla_{x}\phi^{n})\cdot v\mu^{1/2}

. Using the method for deriving (
5.4
) and (
5

[

) and (

Abstract
. We prove a boundary version of the strong form of the Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma with optimal error term. This result provides nonlinear extensions of the boundary Schwarz lemma of Burns and Krantz to the class of negatively curved conformal pseudometrics defined on arbitary hyperbolic domains in the complex plane. Based on a new boundary Harnack inequality for solutions of the Gauss curvature equation, we also establish a sharp rigidity result for conformal metrics with isolated singularities. In the particular case of constant negative curvature this strengthens classical results of Nitsche and Heins about Liouville’s equation Δ ​ u = e u \Delta u=e^{u} .



1.
Introduction

In a recent paper
[
14
]
we established boundary versions of the strong form of the Ahlfors and Schwarz–Pick lemmas with optimal error bounds for the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|z|<1\}
. In the present paper we study the boundary version of the strong form of the Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma for arbitrary hyperbolic subdomains of the Riemann sphere. The emphasis is on the sharpness of the error bound.

The motivation for extending the boundary Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma for the unit
\mathbb{D}
proved in
[
14
, Theorem 2.6]
to other domains and investigating the sharpness of the error term is twofold.
One source of motivation comes from the fact that in the case of the unit disk the boundary Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma of
[
14
]
provides an extension of the well–known boundary Schwarz lemma of Burns and Krantz
[
15
]
, and that much recent work (see e.g.
[
7
,
36
,
50
,
57
]
) on boundary rigidity of holomorphic maps in one and several variables has been devoted to relate the error term in the theorem of Burns–Krantz to the geometry of the domain.

A second motivation comes from the special case of isolated boundary points, a situation which has been of longlasting and continuing interest in complex analysis, nonlinear PDE and two–dimensional conformal geometry, see
[
5
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
25
,
27
,
28
,
33
,
35
,
37
,
39
,
40
,
42
,
43
,
44
,
53
]
.
In particular, we establish with Theorem
2.3
a boundary version of the strong form of the Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma for a punctured disk with best possible remainder term. As a consequence, we obtain a rigidity result for negatively curved conformal metrics with isolated singularities in the spirit of the theorem of Burns–Krantz with an optimal error bound.
This rigidity result seems to be new even in the case of constant negative curvature. As an application we derive an extension of classical results of Nitsche
[
40
]
and Heins
[
27
]
about the solutions of Liouville’s equation
\Delta u=e^{u}
with isolated singularities. The main technical tools are a new boundary Harnack–type inequality (Theorem
4.1
) and an accompanying Hopf lemma (Corollary
4.3
) for solutions of the Gauss curvature equation on punctured disks.

[

,

. However, we collect together the most relevant properties of conformal metrics in Section

.

2.
Results

Let

\Omega
be a hyperbolic subdomain of the Riemann sphere
\hat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
, that is, a non–empty, open and connected subset of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega
contains at least three pairwise distinct points. We denote the hyperbolic (or Poincaré) metric of
\Omega
by
\lambda_{\Omega}(z)\,|dz|

and the associated hyperbolic distance by
\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}
. We emphasize that throughout this paper we use the convention that every hyperbolic metric has constant Gauss curvature
-4
(see Section
3
for the precise definition of the curvature concept which we employ in this paper).

The Poincaré metric
\lambda_{\Omega}(z)\,|dz|
has the following fundamental extremal property. If
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
is any conformal pseudometric on
\Omega
whose curvature
\kappa_{\lambda}
is bounded above by
-4
, then

(2.1)

\frac{\lambda(z)}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z)}\leq 1\quad\text{ for all }z\in\Omega\,.

This is the content of Ahlfors’ celebrated extension
[
2
]
of the Schwarz–Pick lemma.
Heins
[
27
]
(see also Jørgensen
[
29
]
) has made the important observation
that if equality holds in Ahlfors’ inequality (
2.1
) at just one point in the domain, then equality holds for all points. See e.g. Royden
[
46
]
, Minda
[
38
]
, Chen
[
17
]
and also
[
34
, Lemma 3.2]
for different proofs.
This strong form of Ahlfors’ lemma encapsulates a very useful
interior
rigidity property of conformal metrics. It has the following counterpart on the boundary.

Theorem 2.1

(The strong form of the Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma at the boundary)
.

Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic subdomain of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
and let
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
be a conformal pseudometric on
\Omega
with curvature
\leq-4
. Suppose that
\{z_{n}\}
is a sequence of points in
\Omega
tending to a boundary point
p\in\partial\Omega
. If

(2.2)

\frac{\lambda(z_{n})}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z_{n})}=1+o\left(e^{-4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)}\right)\qquad(n\to\infty)

for one – and hence for any –
q\in\Omega
, then

\lambda=\lambda_{\Omega}\,.

By completeness of the hyperbolic distance,
\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)\to+\infty
as
z_{n}
tends to the boundary point
p
. Hence Theorem
2.1
says that if
\lambda(z_{n})/\lambda_{\Omega}(z_{n})
tends to
1
sufficiently fast, that is, faster than
e^{-4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)}
tends to
0
, then this forces
\lambda=\lambda_{\Omega}
.

Remark 1
.

The error term in (
2.2
) is given in terms of the intrinsic hyperbolic geometry of the
domain
\Omega
. It is well–known that for many domains (such as smoothly bounded domains or domains with corners) the error bound can also be stated in Euclidean terms if the need
arises.
For instance, in the case of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|z|<1\}
, the asymptotic condition (
2.2
) takes the explicit form

(2.3)

(1-|z_{n}|^{2})\lambda(z_{n})=1+o\left((1-|z_{n}|)^{2}\right)\,.

This special case of Theorem
2.1
is Theorem 2.6 in
[
14
]
, see also
[
1
, Theorem 2.7.2]
. We leave the statements and proofs of such results for other domains to the reader.

Remark 2

(The Schwarz–Pick lemma for hyperbolic derivatives of Beardon and Minda
[
11
]
)
.

From the viewpoint of geometric function theory, the special
case that the conformal pseudometric
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
in Theorem
2.1

has the form

\lambda(z)=\lambda_{\Omega^{\prime}}(f(z))\,|f^{\prime}(z)|

for a holomorphic map
f
from
\Omega
to some other hyperbolic domain
\Omega^{\prime}

is of particular interest. It is not difficult to see – cf. Section
4.2
for details – that Theorem
2.1
in this case
can also be derived from the Schwarz–Pick lemma of Beardon and Minda for hyperbolic derivatives, see e.g. Corollary 11.3 in
[
11
]
and also
[
9
,
10
]
. For
\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}
this has already been pointed out in
[
14
, Remark 5.6]
, and the general case is analogous. We refer to
[
1
,
6
,
8
,
22
]
for some other recent results and variations on the Schwarz–Pick lemma.

Remark 3

(Theorem
2.1
and the boundary Schwarz Lemma of Burns–Krantz)
.

Theorem
2.1
for
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
and constant curvature
-4
contains in particular the following result:
If
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic map such that

(2.4)

(1-|z_{n}|^{2})\frac{|f^{\prime}(z_{n})|}{1-|f(z_{n})|^{2}}=1+o\left((1-|z_{n}|)^{2}\right)

for some sequence
\{z_{n}\}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|z_{n}|\to 1
, then
f
is a conformal automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
, see also
[
14
]
.
As it has been pointed out in
[
14
, Remark 2.2]
, this special case of Theorem
2.1
generalizes the well–known boundary Schwarz lemma of Burns and Krantz
[
15
]
which asserts that the condition

(2.5)

f(z)=z+o\left(|1-z|^{3}\right)\qquad(z\to 1)

for some holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
implies
f(z)=z
. Accordingly, already the constant curvature case of Theorem
2.1
provides an extension of the boundary Schwarz lemma of Burns–Krantz e.g. to holomorphic maps between arbitary hyperbolic domains.
We refer to
[
7
,
12
,
16
,
20
,
21
,
36
,
41
,
47
,
50
,
51
,
57
]
for some of the many other recent extensions and generalizations of the theorem of Burns and Krantz.
A substantial difference between Theorem
2.1

and the boundary Schwarz lemma of
[
15
]
is the conformally
invariant character of Theorem
2.1
.

We now state the main results of the present paper. First, we show that under some mild topological–geometric assumptions on the domain
\Omega
at the distinguished boundary point
p
, the error term in Theorem
2.1
is sharp.
Let
K
be a connected component of the complement
\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega
of a hyperbolic domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}
. We call
K

nondegenerate
, if
K
does not reduce to a single point. We call the component
K

isolated
, if none of its points belongs to the closure of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(\Omega\cup K)
.
Finally, a point
p\in\partial\Omega\cap\mathbb{C}
is called
accessible
if there is a continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1)\to\Omega
such that
\lim_{t\to 1}\gamma(t)=p
.

Theorem 2.2

(Sharpness of the strong form of Ahlfors’ lemma at the boundary)
.

Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic domain in
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
and let
K
be an isolated nondegenerate connected component
of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega
. Suppose that
p\in K\cap\partial\Omega

is accessible.
Then there exists a sequence
\{z_{n}\}
in
\Omega
tending to
p

and an injective holomorphic function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
such that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(z_{n}))\,|f^{\prime}(z_{n})|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z_{n})}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)}<0\,

for one – and hence any –
q\in\Omega
.

Theorem
2.2

reveals that the error term in Theorem
2.1
is optimal in the sense that
for any accessible boundary point
p
from an isolated nondegenerate connected component
K
of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega
one cannot replace “little
o
” in (
2.2
) by “big
O
”. In contrast to that,
the next result shows that if the distinguished boundary point is

isolated
, then the error term in Theorem
2.1
is not the best
possible. In fact, the exponent
4
in the error term (
2.2
) can be reduced to
2
in this case.

Theorem 2.3

(The strong boundary Ahlfors lemma for hyperbolic domains with punctures)
.

Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic domain in
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
with an isolated boundary point
p
and let

\lambda(z)\,|dz|
be a conformal pseudometric on
\Omega
with curvature
\leq-4
.
Suppose that
\{z_{n}\}
is a sequence in
\Omega
tending to
p\in\partial\Omega
. If

(2.6)

\frac{\lambda(z_{n})}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z_{n})}=1+o\left(e^{-2\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)}\right)\qquad(n\to\infty)

for one – and hence for any –
q\in\Omega
, then

\lambda=\lambda_{\Omega}\,.

Remark 4
.

The improved error term in Theorem
2.3
is optimal in the sense that one cannot
replace “little
o
” in (
2.6
) by “big
O
”, see Example
4.5
below.

Remark 5
.

If
p\in\mathbb{C}
, then
condition (
2.6
) takes – in Euclidean terms – the form

\frac{\lambda(z_{n})}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z_{n})}=1+o\left(\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{|z_{n}-p|}}\right)\,,

see Lemma
4.4
.

Remark 6
.

There is an extensive literature on the asymptotic behaviour of a conformal metric at an isolated singularity, see
[
5
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
25
,
27
,
28
,
33
,
35
,
37
,
39
,
40
,
42
,
43
,
44
,
53
,
54
]
. It is customary
and no loss of generality to consider the case that the isolated singularity is the point
p=0
and that
\Omega
contains the punctured unit disk
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}:=\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. If we assume that the curvature
\kappa_{\lambda}
of the metric
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
has a continuous extension to
p=0
with
\kappa(0)<0
, then
the well–known fundamental classification result
[
40
,
27
,
37
,
33
]
asserts that
there is a continuous remainder function
v:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
such that either

(2.7)

\log\lambda(z)=-\log|z|-\log\log(1/|z|)+v(z)

or there is a real number
\alpha<1
such that

(2.8)

\log\lambda(z)=-\alpha\log|z|+v(z)\,.

The metric
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
is said to have a
logarithmic singularity
or a
singularity of order
1
if (
2.7
) holds and a
conical singularity of order
\alpha<1
if (
2.8
) holds. The prototype for a conformal metric with singularity of order
1
is the Poincaré metric of a domain with an isolated boundary point, see
[
3
,
26
]
.
Note that Theorem
2.3
is relevant only for metrics with singularities of order
1
. Conical singularities
have intensively been studied e.g. in
[
5
,
27
,
49
,
52
]
.

Returning to Theorem
2.3
, we note that under the slightly more restrictive, but customary assumption that the curvature
\kappa_{\lambda}
is locally Hölder continuous up to the isolated singularity the following strong dichotomy arises.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\kappa:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Hölder continuous function and
suppose that
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
is a conformal metric on
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
with a singularity of order
1
at
z=0
and such that
\kappa_{\lambda}(z)=\kappa(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
.
Then the following hold.

(a)

If

\kappa(0)=-4
, then

(2.9)

\log\lambda(z)=\log\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(z)+O\left(\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{|z|}}\right)\qquad(z\to 0)\,.

(b)

If
\kappa_{\lambda}(z)\leq-4
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
and

(2.10)

\log\lambda(z_{n})=\log\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(z_{n})+o\left(\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{|z_{n}|}}\right)\,.

for a sequence
\{z_{n}\}
in
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
tending to
0
, then
\lambda=\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}
.

Comparing (
2.10
) with (
2.5
), Theorem
2.4
(b) is clearly a boundary rigidity result of Burns–Krantz type.
We note that in fact Theorem
2.4
(a) can be deduced from the results
[
33
]
, which generalize earlier results of Nitsche
[
40
]
and Heins
[
27
]
dealing with the constant curvature case. On the other hand, Theorem
2.4
(b) follows at once from Theorem
2.3
and Remark
5
, but seems to be new even in the constant curvature case.

Remark 7

(Constant curvature case of Theorem
2.4
)
.

Clearly, the classical case
\kappa_{\lambda}(z)=-4
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
in Theorem
2.4
has been considered by many authors, including Poincaré
[
44
]
, Picard
[
42
,
43
]
, Heins
[
27
]
, Yamada
[
53
]
, Nitsche
[
40
]
and Minda
[
39
]
. In particular, Nitsche
[
40
]
has found the expansion

(2.11)

\log\lambda(z)=\log\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(z)+\sum\limits_{l,m,n\geq 0}a_{l,m,n}\frac{z^{l}\overline{z}^{m}}{\left(\log\frac{1}{|z|}\right)^{n}}\,,\qquad a_{0,0,0}=0\,,

which obviously implies Theorem
2.4
(a) in the constant curvature case. In fact, (
2.11
) also provides a very weak form of Theorem
2.4
(b) in the constant curvature case. In order to see this, one can just insert the expansion (
2.11
) into the Gauss curvature equation
\Delta\log\lambda=4\lambda^{2}
. Then, as Nitsche has pointed out, a comparison of coefficients yields

a_{0,0,n}=\frac{\gamma^{n}}{n}\qquad\text{ for all $n=1,2,\ldots$ and some constant }\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\,.

In particular, this shows that

a_{0,0,1}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_{0,0,n}=0\quad\text{ for }n=0,1,2,\ldots\,.

However, Theorem
2.4
(b) implies that much more is true, namely

a_{0,0,1}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_{l,m,n}=0\quad\text{ for all }l,m,n=0,1,2,\ldots\,.

This is completely analogous to the ’analytic’ form of the boundary Schwarz lemma of Burns and Krantz: if a holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}

has a holomorphic extension to
z=1
of the form

f(z)=z+\sum\limits_{n=3}^{\infty}a_{n}(z-1)^{n}\,,

then

a_{3}=0\Longrightarrow\quad a_{n}=0\quad\text{ for }n=3,4,5,\ldots\,.

We finally discuss an analogue of Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
for conformal metrics on
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
with a conical singularity of order
\alpha<1
at the origin. A well–known version of Ahlfors’ lemma (see e.g.
[
27
, p. 22]
or
[
34
, Exercise §3.4]
) says that for any such pseudometric
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
with curvature
\leq-4
the inequality

(2.12)

\lambda(z)\leq\lambda_{\alpha}(z):=(1-\alpha)\frac{|z|^{-\alpha}}{1-|z|^{2(1-\alpha)}}\,

holds. Moreover, equality occurs at some interior point of
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
if and only if
\lambda\equiv\lambda_{\alpha}
. This, of course, is analogous to the interior case of equality in the Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma. Since we have not been able to locate this result elsewhere, we include a proof in Section
4.7
below, see Lemma
4.6
.
In any case, our main point here that there is even the following boundary version of this strong form of the Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma for conformal pseudometrics with conical singularities.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
be a conformal pseudometric on
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}

with curvature
\leq-4
and a conical singularity of order
\alpha<1
at
z=0
. Suppose that

(2.13)

\frac{\lambda(z_{n})}{\lambda_{\alpha}(z_{n})}=1+o\left(|z_{n}|^{2(1-\alpha)}\right)

for some sequence
(z_{n})
in
\Omega
such that
z_{n}\to 0
. Then

\lambda=\lambda_{\alpha}\,.

Remark 8
.

Theorem
2.5
sharpens another of Nitsche’s results from
[
40
]
, which is the following. Suppose that is
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
is a conformal metric on
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
with a conical singularity of order
\alpha<1
at
z=0
and constant curvature
-4
. Then there is real number
\beta\leq 1
such that

\frac{\lambda(z)}{\lambda_{\alpha}(z)}=\beta+O\left(|z|^{2(1-\alpha)}\right)\qquad(z\to 0)\,.

In particular, we see that for
\beta=1
the same strong dichotomy arises as in the case of logarithmic singularities (see Theorem
2.4
).

3.
Preliminaries

In this section we briefly recall the notions and results of hyperbolic geometry we need in the paper. We refer the reader to the authorative standard texts such as
[
1
,
3
,
11
,
30
]
for further details.

By a conformal pseudometric
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
on a domain
G
in
\mathbb{C}
we simply mean a continuous nonnegative function
\lambda:G\to\mathbb{R}
. If, in addition,
\lambda
is strictly positive on
G
, then
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
is called conformal metric on
G
. For simplicity, we assume throughout this paper that any conformal pseudometric
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
is of class
C^{2}
on the open set
G_{\lambda}:=\{z\in G\,:\,\lambda(z)>0\}
. This allows us to define the Gauss curvature of
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
by

(3.1)

\kappa_{\lambda}(z):=-\frac{\Delta(\log\lambda)(z)}{\lambda(z)^{2}}\,,\qquad z\in G_{\lambda}\,

just using the standard Laplacian
\Delta
.
Expressions such as “
\kappa\leq-4
” will always mean that

\kappa(z)\leq-4
for all
z\in G_{\lambda}
. We note that there are various ways to generalize Gauss curvature (see
[
2
,
27
,
46
]
), but we do not pursue these extensions in this paper.

Let
D
and
G
be domains in
\mathbb{C}
,

\lambda(w)\,|dw|
a conformal pseudometric on
G
, and
f:D\to G
a holomorphic function. Then the conformal pseudometric

(f^{*}\lambda)(z)\,|dz|:=\lambda(f(z))\,|f^{\prime}(z)|\,|dz

is called the pullback of
\lambda(w)\,|dw|
via
f
. The fundamental property of Gauss curvature is its conformal invariance as expressed by

(3.2)

\kappa_{f^{*}\lambda}(z)=\kappa_{\lambda}(f(z))\,,\qquad z\in G_{f^{*}\lambda}\,.

Using local coordinates and the basic invariance property (
3.2
) one can generalize all these concepts in a straightforward way from subdomains of
\mathbb{C}
to arbitrary Riemann surfaces, that is, in particular to subdomains of the Riemann sphere, see e.g.
[
27
]
.

The hyperbolic metric of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is defined by

\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)\,|dz|:=\frac{|dz|}{1-|z|^{2}}\,;

its Gauss curvature is
\kappa_{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}}=-4
.
If
G
is a hyperbolic subdomain of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
, then uniformization theory shows that there is a holomorphic universal covering map
\pi:\mathbb{D}\to G
, and one can define the hyperbolic metric
\lambda_{G}(z)\,|dz|
by

\lambda_{G}(\pi(z))\,|\pi^{\prime}(z)|\,|dz|:=\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)\,|dz|\,.

The metric
\lambda_{G}(z)\,|dz|
does not depend on the choice of the covering
\pi:\mathbb{D}\to G
and has constant Gauss curvature
-4
. The Schwarz–Pick lemma or the Ahlfors–Schwarz inequality (
2.1
) yields the basic
domain monotonicity principle
, which says that
\lambda_{D}\leq\lambda_{G}
whenever
G\subseteq D
. The hyperbolic metric
\lambda_{G}(z)\,|dz|
induces the hyperbolic distance
\text{d}_{G}
on
G
defined by

\text{d}_{G}(z,w):=\inf\{\int_{\gamma}\lambda_{G}(w)\,|dw|\}
,
where the inf is taken over all piecewise smooth curves
\gamma
in
G
joining
z\in G
and
w\in G
.
Then
(G,\text{d}_{G})
is a complete metric space.

4.
Proofs

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

It is relatively straightforward to deduce Theorem
2.1
from its special case
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
, which has previously been established in
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
. For completeness, we provide the details.

Fix a point
q\in\Omega
and let

\pi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
be a universal holomorphic covering map with
\pi(0)=q
. For every
positive integer
n
there is a curve
\gamma_{n}
in
\Omega
from
q
to
z_{n}

such that

\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)\geq\int\limits_{\gamma_{n}}\lambda_{\Omega}(z)\,|dz|-\frac{1}{n}\,.

Since
\pi
has the pathlifting property there is a curve
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}

in
\mathbb{D}
from
0
to some point
w_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
in the fibre
\pi^{-1}(\{z_{n}\})
of
z_{n}

such that
\pi\circ\tilde{\gamma}_{n}=\gamma_{n}
. Hence, using

(4.1)

\lambda_{\Omega}(\pi(w))\,|\pi^{\prime}(w)|=\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(w)\,\qquad(w\in\mathbb{D})\,,

we see

(4.2)

\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)\geq\int\limits_{\pi\circ\tilde{\gamma}_{n}}\lambda_{\Omega}(z)\,|dz|-\frac{1}{n}=\int\limits_{\tilde{\gamma}_{n}}\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(w)\,|dw|-\frac{1}{n}\geq\mathop{{\rm d}_{\mathbb{D}}}(w_{n},0)-\frac{1}{n}\,.

On the other hand, by the distance nonincreasing property of
\pi:(\mathbb{D},\mathop{{\rm d}_{\mathbb{D}}})\to(\Omega,\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}})
, we have

(4.3)

\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},p)=\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(\pi(w_{n}),\pi(0))\leq\mathop{{\rm d}_{\mathbb{D}}}(w_{n},0)\,.

Since

\mathop{{\rm d}_{\mathbb{D}}}(w,0)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+|w|}{1-|w|}\,,\qquad w\in\mathbb{D}\,,

the two estimates (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) immediately give

(4.4)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{e^{-2\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)}}{1-|w_{n}|}=\frac{1}{2}\,.

The pullback
\mu(w)\,|dw|:=\pi^{*}\lambda(w)\,|dw|
of
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
by the map
\pi:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
is a conformal pseudometric on
\mathbb{D}
with curvature
\leq-4
. Using (
4.1
), we get

\frac{\mu(w_{n})}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(w_{n})}=\frac{\lambda(z_{n})}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z_{n})}=1+o\left(e^{-4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)}\right)

by assumption. In view of (
4.4
) this yields

\frac{\mu(w_{n})}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(w_{n})}=1+o\left((1-|w_{n}|)^{2}\right)\,.

Since clearly
|w_{n}|\to 1
, we are thus in a position to apply Theorem 2.1 in
[
14
]
, and this shows that

\mu=\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}
which is the same as
\lambda=\lambda_{\Omega}
.

4.2.
Theorem
2.1
and the Schwarz–Pick lemma of A. Beardon and D. Minda

Let
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
be hyperbolic domains in
\mathbb{C}
and
f:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
a holomorphic map. Beardon & Minda
[

call the quantity

the hyperbolic distortion factor of

, see
[

for all

Hence, assuming

\frac{\lambda_{\Omega^{\prime}}(f(z_{n}))\,|f^{\prime}(z_{n})|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z_{n})}=f^{\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}}(z_{n})=1+e\left(e^{-4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(z_{n},q)}\right)\qquad(n\to\infty)

for a sequence
\{z_{n}\}
in
\Omega
escaping to the boundary of
\Omega
as in (

), shows that

. By the case of equality of the classical Schwarz–Pick lemma at the interior point

(see
[
11
, Theorem 10.5]
), we deduce that

is a covering, so

\lambda_{\Omega^{\prime}}(f(z))\,|f^{\prime}(z)|=\lambda_{\Omega}(z)\quad\text{ for all }z\in\Omega\,.

We see that the special case
\lambda=\lambda_{\Omega^{\prime}}(f)\,|f^{\prime}|
for some holomorphic map

in Theorem

).

Proof of Theorem
2.2

Since the closed set
K
is connected and contains more than one point,
G:=\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K
is a simply connected domain which contains
\Omega
. Hence, by domain monotonicity of the hyperbolic metric and hyperbolic distance,
\lambda_{G}\leq\lambda_{\Omega}
and
\text{d}_{G}\leq\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}
. Now fix

and let
\psi
be a conformal map of
G
onto
\mathbb{D}
with

\psi(q)=0
. Since
p
is an accessible boundary point of
\Omega
there is a continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1)\to\Omega
such that
\lim_{t\to 1}\gamma(t)=p
.
It is well–known (
[
13
, Proposition 3.3.3]
) that
there exists a point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\lim_{t\to 1}\psi(\gamma(t))=\sigma

and such that the radial limit

\lim_{r\to 1}\psi^{-1}(r\sigma)

exists
and
=p
. By rotating
\psi
if
necessary, we may assume

p=\lim\limits_{r\to 1}\psi^{-1}(r)\,.

We now consider

\phi(z):=z-\frac{1}{12}(z-1)^{3}\,,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\,.

Note that
\phi
provides the standard example for demonstrating the sharpness of the error term in the theorem of Burns–Krantz for the unit disk, see
[
15
,
22
]
.
It is not difficult to show that
\phi
is an injective holomorphic selfmap of
\mathbb{D}
, and a direct computation shows that for
x\in(0,1)
,

(4.6)

(1-x^{2})\,\left(\phi^{*}\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}\right)(x)=\left(1-x^{2}\right)\frac{|\phi^{\prime}(x)|}{1-|\phi(x)|^{2}}=1-\frac{(1-x)^{2}}{12}+o\left((1-x)^{2}\right)\quad\text{ as }x\to 1\,.

We define

f:=\phi\circ\psi\,,

a (univalent) holomorphic map from
G
to
\mathbb{D}
. In view of
\lambda_{\Omega}\geq\lambda_{G}
and
\lambda_{G}=\psi^{*}\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}
, we have

(4.7)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{G}(w)}=\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(\phi(\psi(w))\,|\phi^{\prime}(\psi(w))|}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(\psi(w))}\\
&=&\displaystyle\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\frac{|\phi^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\end{array}

for all
w=\psi^{-1}(z)\in\Omega
.

Since
K
is an isolated component of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega
, the curve

x\mapsto\psi^{-1}(x)
,
x\in[0,1)
, eventually lies in
\Omega
.
In view of (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) we get

\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}\leq(1-x^{2})\frac{|\phi^{\prime}(x)|}{1-\phi(x)^{2}}=1-\frac{(1-x)^{2}}{12}+o\left((1-x)^{2}\right)\qquad(x\to 1)\,.

In combination with
\text{d}_{G}\leq\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}
, this leads to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(w,q)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}-1\right)e^{4\text{d}_{G}(w,q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\mathbb{D}}}(x,0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}-1\right)\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{(1+x)^{2}}{12}+o(1)\qquad\text{ as }x\to 1\,.

Hence

(4.8)

\limsup\limits_{w\to p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(w,q)}\leq-\frac{1}{3}\,,

where this limit is taken along the curve
\psi^{-1}([0,1))
.

We now provide an estimate in the opposite direction. Let
\Omega^{\prime}:=\psi(\Omega)
. Notice that
since
K
is an isolated component of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega
we can choose an annulus
A_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,r<|z|<1\}
such that
A_{r}\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
.
Since
\psi^{-1}
is a holomorphic map from
A_{r}
to
\Omega

\lambda_{\Omega}(\psi^{-1}(z))|(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|\leq\lambda_{\Omega^{\prime}}(z)\leq\lambda_{A_{r}}(z)\,,\qquad z\in A_{r}\,.

This implies for any

and

(4.9)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(w,q)}&\geq&\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega^{\prime}}(z)\,|\psi^{\prime}(w)|}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(w,q)}\\
&\geq&\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{A_{r}}(z)\,|\psi^{\prime}(w)|}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(w,q)}\\[14.22636pt]
&=&\displaystyle\left(\frac{\phi^{*}\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)}{\lambda_{A_{r}}(z)}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(\psi^{-1}(z),\psi^{-1}(0))}\\[14.22636pt]

where we have used again the Schwarz–Pick lemma in the last step.

We now make use of

[

48

) explicit formula

\lambda_{A_{r}}(z)=\frac{\pi}{2|z|\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\,\sin\left(\pi\frac{\log\frac{1}{|z|}}{\log\frac{1}{r}}\right)}\,,

which in particular implies

A tedious computation involving these expressions and the explicit formula (

In view of (

\liminf\limits_{w\to p}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(f(w))\,|f^{\prime}(w)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(w)}-1\right)e^{4\mathop{{\rm d}_{\Omega}}(w,q)}\geq-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right)^{2}}\,,

where this limit is taken along the curve

. In combination with the previous inequality (

of points on

tending to

The proof of Theorem

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

It is well–known that the boundary Schwarz lemma of Burns and Krantz is closely tied to a Harnack inequality for positive subharmonic functions (see
[
15
,
45
]
). It is therefore to be expected
that the key ingredient for the proof of Theorem
2.3
ought to be a Harnack–type inequality for negatively
curved conformal pseudometrics
on domains with isolated boundary points.

Theorem 4.1

(Harnack inequality)
.

Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic domain in
\mathbb{C}
with
z=0
as an isolated
boundary point and let
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
be a conformal pseudometric with curvature

\kappa_{\lambda}\leq-4
on
\Omega
.
Then for any
0<r<R:={\rm dist}(0,\partial\Omega\setminus\{0\})
,

(4.11)

\lambda(z)\leq\left(\max\limits_{|\xi|=r}\frac{\lambda(\xi)}{\lambda_{\Omega}(\xi)}\right)^{C_{r,R}(z)}\cdot\lambda_{\Omega}(z)\quad\text{ on }\quad 0<|z|<r\,,

where

C_{r,R}(z)=\frac{\log(r/R)}{\log(|z|/R)}<1\,.

In the proof of Theorem
4.1
we shall make use of the following auxiliary result

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
be a conformal pseudometric with curvature
\kappa_{\lambda}\leq-4
on a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
.
Then either
\lambda\equiv 0
or
\{z\in\Omega\,:\,\lambda(z)=0\}
is a set of capacity zero.

Proof.

Since
\lambda
is continuous and nonnegative on
\Omega
and of class
C^{2}
in
G_{\lambda}=\{z\in\Omega\,:\,\lambda(z)>0\}
with
\Delta\log\geq 4\lambda^{2}\geq 0
there, it follows that

\log\lambda
is subharmonic on
\Omega
(possibly
\log\lambda\equiv-\infty
). Hence either
\log\lambda\equiv-\infty
or
\{z\in\Omega\,:\,\log\lambda(z)=-\infty\}
is a set of capacity zero.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

It suffices to consider the case
R=1
, so
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
.
The function

u(z):=\log\frac{\lambda(z)}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z)}\,

is
C^{2}
–smooth and nonpositive on
G_{\lambda}=\{z\in\Omega\,:\,\lambda(z)>0\}
by Ahlfors’ lemma. Since
\Delta\log\lambda\geq 4\lambda^{2}
and

\Delta\log\lambda_{\Omega}=4\lambda_{\Omega}^{2}
, the elementary
inequality
e^{2y}-1\geq 2y
which is valid for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
yields

\Delta u\geq 4\lambda_{\Omega}(z)^{2}\left(e^{2u}-1\right)\geq 8\lambda_{\Omega}(z)^{2}u\quad\text{ in }G_{\lambda}\,.

We now define the auxiliary function

v(z):=\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{|z|}}\,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\,,

and observe by direct inspection

\Delta v=8\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(z)^{2}v\,.

By domain monotonicity of the Poincaré metric, we hence get from
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
that

\Delta v\geq 8\lambda_{\Omega}(z)^{2}v\quad\text{
in }\,\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\,.

We next fix
r\in(0,1)
and define

\varepsilon:=-\frac{1}{v(r)}\max\limits_{|\xi|=r}u(\xi).\,

Note that we can assume
\max_{|\xi|=r}u(\xi)>-\infty
, since otherwise
\log\lambda(\xi)=-\infty
for all
|\xi|=r
which, by Lemma
4.2
, would force
\log\lambda\equiv-\infty
in which case (
4.11
)
holds anyway. Hence
\varepsilon\not=+\infty
.
Since
v
is positive and
u\leq 0
, we have
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
We now consider

w:=u+\varepsilon v\,.

Then, by construction,

(4.12)

\Delta w\geq 8\lambda_{\Omega}(z)^{2}w\,\quad\text{ in }\quad G_{\lambda}\cap\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\,,

and

(4.13)

w(z)\leq 0\quad\text{ for all }|z|=r\,.

Since

\lim\limits_{z\to 0}v(z)=0\,,

we also have

(4.14)

\limsup\limits_{z\to 0}w(z)\leq 0\,.

We now show that this implies

(4.15)

w(z)\leq 0\quad\text{ for all }0<|z|\leq r\,.

To prove this, we assume that
w
is positive at some point inside

. In view of the boundary conditions (
4.13
) and (
4.14
),
the function
w
then attains its positive maximum on

at some point
z_{0}

with

. In particular,
z_{0}\in G_{\lambda}
and hence
w
is of class

in
a neighborhood of

, so

by elementary calculus. In

4.12

a contradiction. Hence, we have proved (
4.15

4.11
).
∎

(Hopf)

Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic domain in
\mathbb{C}
with
z=0
as an isolated

4.5.
Sharpness of Theorem
2.3

The following example illustrates that the error term in Theorem
2.3
is optimal.

Example 4.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
be defined by

f(z):=z\exp\left(-\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)\,.

Then

(4.18)

\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(f(z))\,|f^{\prime}(z)|}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(z)}-1\right)\log\frac{1}{|z|}=-1\,.

Hence we see that we cannot replace “little o” by “big O” in Theorem
2.3
.
In order to prove (
4.18
), we just note that

f^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1-4z+z^{2}}{(1-z)^{2}}\frac{f(z)}{z}\,,

and hence

\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(f(z))\,|f^{\prime}(z)|}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(z)}=\frac{|1-4z+z^{2}|}{|1-z|^{2}}\frac{\log\frac{1}{|z|}}{\log\frac{1}{|f(z)|}}=\frac{|1-4z+z^{2}|}{|1-z|^{2}}\frac{\log\frac{1}{|z|}}{\log\frac{1}{|z|}+\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-z|^{2}}}\,.

This implies

\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(f(z))\,|f^{\prime}(z)|}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(z)}-1\right)\log\frac{1}{|z|}=\left(\frac{|1-4z+z^{2}|}{|1-z|^{2}}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{|z|}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-z|^{2}}}}-1\right)\log\frac{1}{|z|}\to-1

as
z\to 0
.

4.6.
Proof of Theorem
2.4

We first prove part (a). By Theorem 3.5 in
[
33
]
, we have

\log\lambda(z)=-\log|z|-\log\log(1/|z|)+w(z)\,,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime},

with a continuous function
v:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
v(0)=-\log\sqrt{-\kappa(0)}=-\log 2
, and the same conclusion holds for
\log\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}
. Hence

\log\lambda(z)=\log\lambda_{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}(z)+w(z)\,,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime},

with a continuous functions
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
w(0)=0
. Now, under the assumption that
\kappa
is locally Hölder continuous in a neighborhood of
z=0
, Theorem 1.1 in
[
33
]
controls the growth of the gradient
\nabla w
of

as follows

\nabla w(z)=O\left(\frac{1}{|z|\left(\log\frac{1}{|z|}\right)^{2}}\right)\qquad(z\to 0)\,.

In combination with
w(0)=0
and

\int\frac{dx}{x\left(\log\frac{1}{x}\right)^{2}}=\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{x}}\,,

an elementary integration therefore gives

w(z)=O\left(\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{|z|}}\right)\qquad(z\to 0)\,,

which proves part (a).

To prove (b) we just note that
\log(1+x)=x+o(x)
as
x\to 0
. In view of Lemma
4.4
,
condition (
2.10
) therefore implies condition (
2.6
) of Theorem
2.3
, and part (b) follows from Theorem
2.3
.

4.7.
Proof of Theorem
2.5

The proof of Theorem
2.5
is more or less identical to the proof of Theorem
2.3
, so we only indicate the main steps. The only real difference is that the ’working horse’ is now the auxiliary function

v_{\alpha}(z):=\frac{|z|^{2(1-\alpha)}}{1-|z|^{2(1-\alpha)}}\,.

However, unlike in the case of a logarithmic singularity (see Remark
10
), we do not have any convincing explanation for this particular choice. Frankly speaking, we have found
v_{\alpha}
by educated guessing.

We begin, however, with an analogue of the interior case of the strong Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma for the situation of conformal pseudometrics with conical singularities.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
be a regular conformal pseudometric with curvature

\kappa_{\lambda}\leq-4
on
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
and a conical singularity at
z=0
of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Suppose that

\lambda(z_{0})=\lambda_{\alpha}(z_{0})\quad\text{ for some }z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\,.

Then

\lambda(z)=\lambda_{\alpha}(z)\quad\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\,.

The proof is a standard application of the standard Hopf maximum principle, see
[
24
, Thm. 3.5]
; we include it for completeness.

Proof.

We show that any point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
has an open neighborhood such that either
\lambda=\lambda_{\alpha}
in this neighborhood or
\lambda(z)<\lambda_{\alpha}(z)
in this neighborhood. Since
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
is connected, the conclusion of the lemma follows. Fix
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
. If
\lambda(z_{0})<\lambda_{\alpha}(z_{0})
, then
\lambda(z)<\lambda_{\alpha}(z)
for all
z
in some open neighborhood of
z_{0}
just by continuity of
\lambda
and
\lambda_{\alpha}
. Now let
\lambda(z_{0})=\lambda_{\alpha}(z_{0})>0
, so
z_{0}\in G_{\lambda}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\,:\,\lambda(z)>0\}
.
The function

u(z):=\log\frac{\lambda(z)}{\lambda_{\alpha}(z)}\,

is
C^{2}
–smooth on
G_{\lambda}
.
Since
\Delta\log\lambda\geq 4\lambda^{2}
and

\Delta\log\lambda_{\alpha}=4\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}
, the elementary
inequality
e^{2y}-1\geq 2y
which is valid for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
yields

\Delta u\geq 4\lambda_{\alpha}(z)^{2}\left(e^{2u}-1\right)\geq 8\lambda_{\alpha}(z)^{2}u\quad\text{ in }G_{\lambda}\,.

Let
D
denote the connected component of the open set
G_{\lambda}
which contains the point
z_{0}
. Since
u(z_{0})=0
and
u(z)\leq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
by (
2.12
), the function
u
attains its maximum on
D
at
z_{0}
. In view of the fact that
\lambda_{\alpha}
is locally bounded on
D
, we are in a position to
apply the Hopf maximum principle. We conclude that
u
has to be constant
in
D
, so
\lambda(z)=\lambda_{\alpha}(z)
for all
z\in D
.
∎

Theorem 4.7

(Harnack inequality for conical singularities)
.

Let
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
be a regular conformal pseudometric with curvature

\kappa_{\lambda}\leq-4
on
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
and a conical singularity at
z=0
of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Suppose that

\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\frac{\lambda(z)}{\lambda_{\alpha}(z)}=1\,.

Then for any
0<r<1
,

(4.19)

\lambda(z)\leq\left(\max\limits_{|\xi|=r}\frac{\lambda(\xi)}{\lambda_{\alpha}(\xi)}\right)^{\gamma_{\alpha,r}(z)}\cdot\lambda_{\alpha}(z)\quad\text{ on }\quad 0<|z|<r\,,

where

\gamma_{\alpha,r}(z)=\frac{v_{\alpha}(z)}{v_{\alpha}(r)}<1\,.

Proof.

In the proof of Lemma
4.6
, we have already observed that
the function

u(z):=\log\frac{\lambda(z)}{\lambda_{\alpha}(z)}\,

is continuous on
\mathbb{D}
and
C^{2}
–smooth and nonpositive on
G_{\lambda}
with

\Delta u\geq 8\lambda_{\alpha}(z)^{2}u\quad\text{ in }G_{\lambda}\,.

Observe that

\Delta v_{\alpha}\geq 8\lambda_{\alpha}(z)^{2}v_{\alpha}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\supseteq G_{\lambda}\,.

We next fix
r\in(0,1)
and define

\varepsilon:=-\frac{1}{v_{\alpha}(r)}\max\limits_{|\xi|=r}u(\xi).\,

As before we we can assume
\max_{|\xi|=r}u(\xi)>-\infty
, so
\varepsilon\in[0,+\infty)
.
Then, from the construction and using

\lim\limits_{z\to 0}v_{\alpha}(z)=0\,,

we see that the function
w:=u+\varepsilon v_{\alpha}
fulflills

(4.20)

\Delta w\geq 8\lambda_{\alpha}(z)^{2}w\,\quad\text{ in }\quad G_{\lambda}\cap\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\,,

and

(4.21)

w(z)\leq 0\quad\text{ for all }|z|=r\,.

as well as

(4.22)

\limsup\limits_{z\to 0}w(z)\leq 0\,.

Making use of (
4.20
)–(
4.22
), we argue as before to conclude that

(4.23)

w(z)\leq 0\quad\text{ for all }0<|z|\leq r\,.

This is equivalent to
(
4.19
).
∎

Corollary 4.8

(Hopf lemma for conical singularities)
.

Let
\lambda(z)\,|dz|
be a conformal pseudometric with curvature

\kappa_{\lambda}\leq-4
on
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
and a conical singularity at
z=0
of order
\alpha<1
. Then either
\lambda\equiv\lambda_{\alpha}
or

(4.24)

\limsup\limits_{z\to 0}\left(\log\frac{\lambda(z)}{\lambda_{\alpha}(z)}\right)\cdot|z|^{2(\alpha-1)}<0\,.

Proof.

We note that
\lambda/\lambda_{\alpha}
has a continuous extension to
z=0
, so we may assume that

\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\frac{\lambda(z)}{\lambda_{\alpha}(z)}=1\,

since otherwise this limit is
<1
and (
4.24
) is trivially true.
As we have already noted, we have
\lambda(z)<\lambda_{\alpha}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
provided that
\lambda(z_{0})<\lambda_{\alpha}(z_{0})
for one
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
. Hence
(
4.16
) follows immediately from (
4.19
).
∎

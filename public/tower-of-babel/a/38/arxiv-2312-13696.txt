Transfer of A ∞ A_{\infty} -structures to projective resolutions
Janina C. Letz

Abstract
. In this article we prove various results about transferring or lifting A ∞ \mathrm{A}_{\infty} -algebra structures along quasi-isomorphisms over a commutative ring.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
16E45 (primary), 18G70, 13D02

1.
Introduction

Algebras that are associative up to strong homotopy can be found in various areas. Such algebras were first introduced by Stasheff
[
Sta63a
,
Sta63b
]
and are called
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras; for an introduction see
[
Kel01
]
. An
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra is a graded module
A
equipped with higher multiplication maps
\mu_{n}\colon A^{\otimes n}\to A
satisfying some relations. One can think of
\mu_{1}
as a differential,
\mu_{2}
as a multiplication, and
\mu_{3}
the homotopy up to which
\mu_{2}
is associative.

In homological algebra
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras turn up in several places. Working over a field, one can endow the homology of a differential graded (dg) algebra with an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure. This
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra is quasi-isomorphic to the original dg algebra. On the other hand, working over a commutative ring any projective resolution of a dg algebra admits an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure so that the dg algebra and its resolution are quasi-isomorphic as algebras. These examples are instances of the same phenomena, the
transfer
of an algebra structure along a quasi-isomorphism:

Theorem A
.

Let
Q
be a commutative ring and
B
an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra over
Q
. Given a quasi-isomorphism
\varepsilon_{1}\colon A\to B
of complexes with
A
a bounded below complex of projective modules, there exists an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
A
and a quasi-isomorphism
\varepsilon\colon A\to B
of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras extending
\varepsilon_{1}
.

Furthermore, if
B
is strictly unital with unit
1_{B}
and
A
splits as
A=Q\oplus\bar{A}
with
\varepsilon_{1}(1_{A})=1_{B}
and
\partial(1_{A})=0
, where
1_{A}
is a free generator of the summand
Q
of
A
, then there exists a strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
A
with the unit
1_{A}
.

Moreover, the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure is unique up to
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-homotopy.

This result can be found in
Corollaries

5.6
and
6.6
. It generalizes various previously known results; many that focused on the case when
Q
a field and thus
\varepsilon_{1}
a homotopy equivalence, or even a strong deformation retract. There are two techniques typically used, the obstruction method and the tensor trick; for an overview see
[
JL01
, Section 2]
. The first result was shown by Kadeishvili
[
Kad80
]
; further results appear in
[
Gug82
,
GS86
,
GL89
,
GLS91
,
HK91
,
Pro11
]
. Explicit formulas are given in
[
Mer99
, Theorem 3.4]
, and for strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structures in
[
CG08
, Theorem 10]
.

Burke
[
Bur18
]
was the first to obtain a transfer result along a quasi-isomorphism of complexes over a commutative ring. He focused on the case when the quasi-isomorphism
\varepsilon_{1}
is surjective. This simplifies many computations, as the lift of a complex map along a surjective quasi-isomorphism yields a commutative diagram. In contrast, the lift along a quasi-isomorphism yields a diagram that is only commutative up to homotopy. Further results over commutative rings appear in
[
Bur
]
and
[
BCLP23
, Section 5]
.

In this work we use a similar method as
[
Kad80
,
Bur18
]
. The strategy is to inductively construct the higher multiplications. We analyze the obstruction of
(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n},0,0,\ldots)
being an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure; see
Section

4
. While it is doable to carry out the computations for the obstruction of an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure by hand, the computations for morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras and homotopies between them are significantly more cumbersome. To simplify the computations we use the bar construction, which allows us to work with coderivations on the tensor coalgebra. Our proof of the transfer theorem is constructive; it yields a step-by-step instruction on how to construct the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure.

When working with
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structures one has to find a balance between writing out explicit formulas, and avoiding the more technical expressions and computations. While we skip some technical computations in this paper, we write out many formulas explicitly for the convenience of the reader.

We finish the article by sketching transfer results for
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-module structures in
Section

7
. The arguments are similar as for
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras, and we skip the proofs.

2.
The tensor coalgebra

Throughout
Q
is a fixed commutative ring. Any module, complex or algebra is a
Q
-module,
Q
-complex or
Q
-algebra, respectively, and any map is
Q
-linear. Unadorned tensor products and Hom sets are taken over
Q
.

For a graded object
M
, we use lower grading in the sense that
M=\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
; unless otherwise specified, graded will mean
\mathbb{Z}
-graded. The degree of an element
m
in
M
, denoted
|m|
, is the integer
i
for which
m\in M_{i}
. The differential of a complex
M
is denoted
\partial^{M}
.

2.1
.

We let
\Sigma
denote the suspension functor: For a graded object
M
, we let
\Sigma M
be the graded object with
(\Sigma M)_{i}=M_{i-1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and there is a naturally defined degree 1 map
{\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}\colon M\to\Sigma M
given by
m\mapsto{\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}m
where the latter is regarded as an element of
(\Sigma M)_{|m|+1}=M_{|m|}
. If
M
is a complex, then we equip
\Sigma M
with the differential
\partial^{\Sigma M}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\partial^{M}
which makes
\Sigma M
a complex.

2.2
.

Let
M,N
be graded modules. We let
\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)
denote the graded module with

\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}(M_{j},N_{i+j})\quad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

When
M,N
are complexes, we equip
\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)
with a differential

\partial^{\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\partial^{N}f-(-1)^{|f|}f\partial^{M}

making the graded module a complex. A map
f\colon M\to N
is
null-homotopic
, if it is a boundary; that is there exists
g\colon M\to N
such that

f=\partial^{\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)}(g)=\partial^{N}g-(-1)^{|g|}g\partial^{M}\,.

In this case, we say
f
is null-homotopic with
homotopy

g
.

2.3
.

Let
M,N
be graded modules. We let
M\otimes N
denote the graded module with

(M\otimes N)_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\coprod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{j}\otimes N_{i-j}\quad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

For maps of graded modules
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
and
g\colon N\to N^{\prime}
, we adopt the Koszul sign-rule:

(f\otimes g)(m\otimes n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(-1)^{|g||m|}f(m)\otimes g(n)\quad\text{for all }m\in M,n\in N\,.

When
M,N
are complexes, we equip
M\otimes N
with a differential

\partial^{M\otimes N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\partial^{M}\otimes\id_{N}+\id_{M}\otimes\partial^{N}

making the graded module a complex.

2.4
.

For a tuple of positive integers
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
we write
|\bm{\alpha}|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{p}
. Given a family of morphisms
f_{k}\colon M^{\otimes k}\to M
we set

\bm{f}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{\alpha_{p}}\,.

We use the convention, that
\mathbb{N}^{0}=\{()\}
is a set with one element and
\bm{f}^{\otimes()}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\id_{Q}
. In particular
\bm{f}^{\otimes()}\otimes g\cong g
for any map
g
.

2.5
.

Let
M
be a (graded) module. The
tensor coalgebra
{\rm T}^{c}(M)
of
M
has the underlying (bi)graded module

{\rm T}^{c}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\coprod_{k\geqslant 0}{\rm T}_{(k)}^{c}(M)\quad\text{with}\quad{\rm T}_{(k)}^{c}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}M^{\otimes k}

and the comultiplication

\Delta(m_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes m_{k})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(m_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes m_{i})\otimes(m_{i+1}\otimes\ldots\otimes m_{k})\,.

The counit is the natural projection onto tensor degree 0. When
M
is a graded module, then we denote the grading that
{\rm T}^{c}(M)
inherits from
M
by
{\rm T}^{c}(M)_{\bullet}
.

A coalgebra
C
is
coaugmented
, if there is a homogeneous morphism of coalgebras
\eta\colon Q\to C
, such that
\eta
is a splitting of the counit as
Q
-modules. The tensor coalgebra is coaugmented with the natural inclusion
\eta\colon Q\to{\rm T}^{c}(M)
.

A morphism of coaugmented coalgebras
F\colon C\to C^{\prime}
is a morphism of coalgebras such that
F\eta=\eta^{\prime}
. We denote the category of coaugmented graded coalgebras by
{\mathsf{C}}\mathrm{oa}^{aug}
.

2.6
.

The morphisms of coaugmented tensor coalgebras are completely determined by the restriction
{\rm T}_{(>0)}^{c}(M)\to N
. Explicitly, the natural map

\begin{gathered}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{C}}\mathrm{oa}^{aug}}({\rm T}^{c}(M),{\rm T}^{c}(N))\to\operatorname{Hom}({\rm T}_{(>0)}^{c}(M),N)\,,\\
F\mapsto({\rm T}_{(>0)}^{c}(M)\to{\rm T}^{c}(M)\xrightarrow{F}{\rm T}^{c}(N)\to{\rm T}_{(1)}^{c}(N)=N)\end{gathered}

is a bijection; see for example
[
LH03
, Lemme 1.1.2.2a]
. Its inverse is

\begin{gathered}\operatorname{cmor}\colon\prod_{k\geqslant 1}\operatorname{Hom}(M^{\otimes k},N)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{C}}\mathrm{oa}^{aug}}({\rm T}^{c}(M),{\rm T}^{c}(N))\,,\\
(f_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}\mapsto\id_{Q}+\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\sum_{p=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=k\end{subarray}}\bm{f}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\end{gathered}

pre-composed with the isomorphism coming from the universal property of the coproduct. The map
\operatorname{cmor}
is well-defined, since the sum over
(p,\bm{\alpha})
is finite for each
k
. We abuse notation and also denote by
\operatorname{cmor}
the pre-composition of
\operatorname{cmor}
with the inclusion of
\coprod_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{Hom}(M^{\otimes k},N)
.

2.7
.

Let
F,G\colon C\to C^{\prime}
be morphisms of graded coalgebras. A
(F,G)
-coderivation of degree
d
is a
Q
-linear map
D\colon C\to C^{\prime}
of degree
d
such that the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\prime}\otimes C^{\prime}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\,.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D\otimes G+F\otimes D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}

We denote the set of
(F,G)
-coderivations of degree
d
by
\operatorname{Cder}^{d}(F,G)
. When
F=G=\id_{C}
, we write
\operatorname{Cder}^{d}(C)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Cder}^{d}(\id_{C},\id_{C})
.

If
D
is a coderivation of odd degree
d
, then
D^{2}
is a coderivation of degree
2d
. The same need not hold for even degree.

2.8
.

On the tensor coalgebra a coderivation is completely determined by the restriction
{\rm T}^{c}(M)\to N
. Explicitly, for morphisms of bigraded coalgebras
F,G\colon{\rm T}^{c}(M)\to{\rm T}^{c}(N)
the natural map

(2.8.1)

\begin{gathered}\operatorname{Cder}^{d}(F,G)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{d}({\rm T}^{c}(M)_{\bullet},N)\\
D\mapsto({\rm T}^{c}(M)\xrightarrow{D}{\rm T}^{c}(N)\to{\rm T}_{(1)}^{c}(N)=N)\end{gathered}

is a bijection; see for example
[
LH03
, Lemme 1.1.2.2b]
. With
F=\operatorname{cmor}((f_{k})_{k\geqslant 1})
and
G=\operatorname{cmor}((g_{k})_{k\geqslant 1})
, its inverse is given by

\begin{gathered}\operatorname{cder}_{(F,G)}\colon\prod_{k\geqslant 0}\operatorname{Hom}^{d}(M^{\otimes k},M)\to\operatorname{Cder}^{d}({\rm T}^{c}(M))\\
(b_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}\mapsto\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,v,w\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{p=0}^{u}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=u\end{subarray}}\sum_{q=0}^{w}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}\\
|\bm{\beta}|=w\end{subarray}}(\bm{f}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\otimes b_{v}\otimes\bm{g}^{\otimes\bm{\beta}})\end{gathered}

pre-composed with the isomorphism coming from the universal property of the coproduct. The map is well-defined since the sum over
(u,v,w,p,\bm{\alpha},q,\bm{\beta})
is finite for each
k
. For convenience we write
\operatorname{cder}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cder}_{(\id,\id)}
. We abuse notation and also denote by
\operatorname{cder}_{(F,G)}
the pre-composition of
\operatorname{cder}_{(F,G)}
with the inclusion of
\coprod_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{Hom}(M^{\otimes k},M)
for any
n
.

3.
A_{\infty}
-algebras

In this section we give the definition of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras, and their morphisms and homotopy equivalences. These structures are closely connected to structures on a tensor coalgebra.
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras were introduced by Stasheff
[
Sta63a
,
Sta63b
]
; also see
[
Kel01
]
.

3.1
.

An
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra
is a graded module
A=\{A_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}
equipped with maps

\mu^{A}_{k}\colon A^{\otimes k}\to A\quad\text{for }k\geq 1

of degree
(k-2)
satisfying the
Stasheff identities

(3.1.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}\mu^{A}_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})=0\quad\text{for }k\geq 1\,.

The Stasheff identity is a relation on maps
A^{\otimes k}\to A
.

At first glance the sign in
Eq.

3.1.1
seems strange. It is in fact chosen so that the identities on
\Sigma A
are much simpler.

3.2
.

Let
A
be an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra. We define
m^{A}_{k}\colon(\Sigma A)^{\otimes k}\to\Sigma A
via
m^{A}_{k}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{\otimes k}=-{\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}\mu^{A}_{k}
. It is straightforward to check, that the
k
th Stasheff identity
Eq.

3.1.1
is equivalent to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}m^{A}_{u+1+w}(\id_{\Sigma A}^{\otimes u}\otimes m^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{\Sigma A}^{\otimes w})=0\,.

The shifted multiplication maps
m^{A}_{k}
are all of degree
-1
. Hence an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure
(\mu^{A}_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
on a graded module
A
is equivalent to a coderivation
D
of degree
-1
on
{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)
with
D\eta=0
and
D^{2}=0
. In fact, the coderivation is given by
D=\operatorname{cder}((m^{A}_{k})_{k\geqslant 1})
.

What is more, for any
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra
({\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A),\Delta,D)
is a dg coalgebra. This is called the
(non-unital) bar construction
.

There are different sign conventions for
Eq.

3.1.1
and the definition of the shifted multiplication maps; see
Remark

7.16
for an in-depth discussion.

3.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras. A
morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras

\varphi\colon A\to B
consists of maps

\varphi_{k}\colon A^{\otimes k}\to B\quad\text{for }k\geq 1

of degree
(k-1)
satisfying

(3.3.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}\varphi_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})=\sum_{p=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=k\end{subarray}}(-1)^{t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})}\mu^{B}_{p}\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}

where
t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}(p-\ell)(\alpha_{\ell}-1)
. A morphism
\varphi\colon A\to B
is
strict
, if
\varphi_{k}=0
for
k\geq 2
.

3.4
.

We can interpret these morphisms in terms of the tensor coalgebra: We define
f_{k}\colon(\Sigma A)^{\otimes k}\to\Sigma B
via
f_{k}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{\otimes k}={\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}\varphi_{k}
. It is straightforward to check, that
Eq.

3.3.1
is equivalent to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}f_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes m^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})=\sum_{p=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=k\end{subarray}}m^{B}_{p}\bm{f}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\,.

The shifted maps
f_{k}
are homogeneous. A morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is equivalent to a morphism of coaugmented coalgebras
F\colon{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)\to{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma B)
such that
FD^{A}=D^{B}F
where
D^{A}
and
D^{B}
the coderivations induced by the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structures on
A
and
B
, respectively. In fact, the morphism of coaugmented coalgebras is given by
F=\operatorname{cmor}((f_{k})_{k\geqslant 1})
.

3.5
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\to B
and
\psi\colon B\to C
be morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras. Their composition is given by

(\psi\circ\varphi)_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{p=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=k\end{subarray}}(-1)^{t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})}\psi_{p}\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\quad\text{for }k\geq 1\,.

It is straightforward to check that this is a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras. On the tensor coalgebras this corresponds the usual composition
\operatorname{cmor}((g_{k})_{k\geqslant 1})\circ\operatorname{cmor}((f_{k})_{k\geqslant 1})=\operatorname{cmor}((h_{k})_{k\geqslant 1})
where
f_{k}
,
g_{k}
and
h_{k}
are the shifted morphisms corresponding to
\varphi_{k}
,
\psi_{k}
and
(\psi\varphi)_{k}
, respectively.

The identity morphism
\id_{A}
of an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra
A
is the strict morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra with
(\id_{A})_{1}
the identity on the module
A
.

3.6
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\colon A\to B
be morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras. We say
\varphi
and
\psi
are
homotopy equivalent
, and write
\varphi\simeq\psi
, if there are maps

\sigma_{k}\colon A^{\otimes k}\to B\quad\text{for }k\geqslant 1

of degree
k
satisfying

(3.6.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}-

\displaystyle\psi_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}\sigma_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{p=0}^{v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=v\end{subarray}}\sum_{q=0}^{w}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}\\
|\bm{\beta}|=w\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vq+t_{2}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta})}\mu^{B}_{p+1+q}(\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\otimes\sigma_{v}\otimes\bm{\psi}^{\otimes\bm{\beta}})\,,

where
t_{2}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta})=t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})+t_{1}(\bm{\beta})+(u-p)(q+1)
. We call
\sigma
an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-homotopy
between
\varphi
and
\psi
.

A morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras
\varphi\colon A\to B
is a
homotopy equivalence
of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras, if there exists a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras
\psi\colon B\to A
such that
\psi\varphi\simeq\id_{A}
and
\varphi\psi\simeq\id_{B}
.

3.7
.

On the tensor coalgebras
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-homotopies correspond to derivations: We define
s_{k}\colon(\Sigma A)^{\otimes k}\to\Sigma B
via
s_{k}{\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{\otimes k}=-{\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}\sigma_{k}
. It is straightforward to check that
Eq.

3.6.1
is equivalent to

\displaystyle f_{k}-

\displaystyle g_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}s_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes m^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{p=0}^{u}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=u\end{subarray}}\sum_{q=0}^{w}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}\\
|\bm{\beta}|=w\end{subarray}}m^{B}_{p+1+q}(\bm{f}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\otimes s_{v}\otimes\bm{g}^{\otimes\bm{\beta}})\,,

where
f_{k}
and
g_{k}
are the shifted maps corresponding to
\varphi_{k}
and
\psi_{k}
, respectively. The shifted maps
s_{k}
are all of degree
1
. Hence an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-homotopy
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is equivalent to a
(F,G)
-coderivation
S
of degree
1
with
F-G=D^{B}S+SD^{B}
, where
F
and
G
are the coalgebra morphisms corresponding to
\varphi
and
\psi
, respectively, and
D^{A}
and
D^{B}
the coderivations induced by the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structures on
A
and
B
, respectively. In fact, the homotopy is given by
S=\operatorname{cder}_{(F,G)}((s_{k})_{k\geqslant 1})
.

4.
Obstructions

In this section we show the technical results used in the proof of the transfer theorem. For this we analyze the terms of the
k
th Stasheff identity, that do not contain
\mu^{A}_{k}
. Some of the results are mentioned in
[
LH03
, Section B.1]
.

4.1
.

For a positive integer, a graded module
A
equipped with multiplication maps
\mu^{A}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{A}_{n}
satisfying the Stasheff identities
Eq.

3.1.1
for
1\leq k\leq n
, is called an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra
. Similar as above, an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra structure
(\mu^{A}_{k})_{k\leqslant n}
on
A
induces a coderivation
D=\operatorname{cder}(m^{A}_{1},\ldots,m^{A}_{n})
, where
m^{A}_{1},\ldots,m^{A}_{n}
are the shifted multiplication maps.

For an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra, we set

\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=n+1\\
u,w\geqslant 0,n\geqslant v\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}\mu^{A}_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})\,.

Then a map
\mu^{A}_{n+1}\colon A^{\otimes(n+1)}\to A
makes
A
an
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-algebra if and only if

(4.1.1)

\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)=-\partial(\mu^{A}_{n+1})\,;

that is
-\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)
is null-homotopic with homotopy
\mu^{A}_{n+1}
.

The following identity is clear for an
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-algebra, but it even holds for an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra
A
one has

\partial(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A))=0\,.

Proof.

We set
D\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cder}(m^{A}_{1},\ldots,m^{A}_{n})
and
D_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cder}(m^{A}_{1})
. Since
A
is an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra, the restriction of
D^{2}
to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(k+i)}\to(\Sigma A)^{\otimes i}
is zero for
k<n
and
i\geq 1
. By definition, the restriction of
\bar{D}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}D-D_{1}
to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes i}\to(\Sigma A)^{\otimes i+j}
is zero for
i\geq 1
and
j\geq 0
. Hence

D^{2}\bar{D}=0=\bar{D}D^{2}

when restricted to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(n+1)}\to\Sigma A
. By straightforward computations we have

D_{1}\bar{D}^{2}=\bar{D}^{2}D_{1}

when restricted to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(n+1)}\to\Sigma A
. After replacing the shifted multiplications
m^{A}_{k}
by the multiplications
\mu^{A}_{k}
, we obtain

-\partial^{A}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)=(-1)^{n+1}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)\partial^{A^{\otimes n+1}}\,.

This is the desired identity.
∎

4.3
.

A morphism
\varphi\colon A\to B
of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras consists of morphisms
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}
satisfying
Eq.

3.3.1
for
1\leq k\leq n
. Similarly as above, a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras
(\varphi_{k})_{k\leqslant n}
induces a morphism of coaugmented coalgebras
\operatorname{cmor}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
, where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
are the shifted morphisms.

For a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras
\varphi\colon A\to B
we set

\displaystyle\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=n+1\\
u,w\geqslant 0,n\geqslant v\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}

\displaystyle\varphi_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})

\displaystyle-\sum_{p=2}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})}\mu^{B}_{p}\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\,.

If
A
and
B
are
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-algebras, then a map
\varphi_{n+1}\colon A^{\otimes(n+1)}\to B
makes
\varphi
a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-algebras, if and only if

(4.3.1)

\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)=\partial(\varphi_{n+1})+\mu^{B}_{n+1}\varphi_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}-\varphi_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1}\,.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras
\varphi\colon A\to B
one has

\partial(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi))=\varphi_{1}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)-\operatorname{obs}^{n}(B)\varphi_{1}^{\otimes n+1}\,.

Proof.

Let
D^{A}
,
\bar{D}^{A}
,
D^{B}
and
\bar{D}^{B}
be the coderivations induced from the multiplications on
A
and
B
, respectively, as in
Lemma

4.2
. We set

\begin{gathered}F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cmor}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\,,\quad F_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cmor}(f_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{F}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F-F_{1}\,.\end{gathered}

By the same argument as in
Lemma

4.2
we have

\bar{D}^{B}FD^{A}=\bar{D}^{B}D^{B}F\,,\quad FD^{A}\bar{D}^{A}=D^{B}F\bar{D}^{A}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{F}D^{A}D^{A}=0=D^{B}D^{B}\bar{F}

when restricted to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(n+1)}\to\Sigma B
. By straightforward computations we have

D^{B}_{1}(\bar{F}\bar{D}^{A}-\bar{D}^{B}\bar{F})+(\bar{F}\bar{D}^{A}-\bar{D}^{B}\bar{F})D^{A}_{1}=F_{1}\bar{D}^{A}\bar{D}^{A}-\bar{D}^{B}\bar{D}^{B}F_{1}

when restricted to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(n+1)}\to\Sigma B
. Replacing the shifted maps by the maps of the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structures, we obtain

\partial^{B}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)+(-1)^{n}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)\partial^{A^{\otimes(n+1)}}=\varphi_{1}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)-\operatorname{obs}^{n}(B)\varphi_{1}\,.

This is the desired identity.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For morphisms
\varphi\colon A\to B
and
\psi\colon B\to C
of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras one has

\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\psi\varphi)=\psi_{1}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)+\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\psi)\varphi_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}+\partial(\sum_{p=2}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})}\psi_{p}\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}})\,.

Morally, the last summand consists of the terms of
(\psi\varphi)_{n+1}
, which do not contain
\varphi_{n+1}
or
\psi_{n+1}
.

Proof.

We use the same notation and arguments as in
Lemmas

4.2
and
4.4
, and further define
G
,
G_{1}
and
\bar{G}
using
\psi
analogously as
F
,
F_{1}
and
\bar{F}
are defined using
\varphi
. By straightforward computations we have

\displaystyle(GF-G_{1}F_{1})

\displaystyle\bar{D}^{A}-\bar{D}^{C}(GF-G_{1}F_{1})

\displaystyle=G_{1}(\bar{F}\bar{D}^{A}-\bar{D}^{B}\bar{F})+(\bar{G}\bar{D}^{B}-\bar{D}^{C}\bar{G})F_{1}+D^{C}_{1}\bar{G}\bar{F}-\bar{G}\bar{F}D^{A}_{1}

when restricted to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(n+1)}\to\Sigma C
. Replacing the shifted maps by the maps of the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structures, we obtain the desired identity.
∎

4.6
.

An
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-homotopy
\sigma
of morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra
\varphi,\psi\colon A\to B
consists of maps
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
satisfying
Eq.

3.6.1
for
1\leq k\leq n
. Similarly as above, an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-homotopy

induces a coderivation

of degree 1, where

and

, and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
are the shifted maps.

For an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-homotopy
\sigma
of
\varphi,\psi\colon A\to B
we set

\displaystyle\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\sigma)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=n+1\\
u,w\geqslant 0,n\geqslant v\geqslant 2\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}\sigma_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=n+1\\
u,w\geqslant 0,n\geqslant v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{p=0}^{u}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=u\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=0\\
p+q<n\end{subarray}}^{w}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}\\
|\bm{\beta}|=w\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vq+t_{2}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta})}\mu^{B}_{p+1+q}(\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\otimes\sigma_{v}\otimes\psi^{\otimes\bm{\beta}})\,.

If
\varphi,\psi
are morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-algebras, then a map
\sigma_{n+1}
makes
\sigma
an
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-homotopy if and only if

(4.6.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{n+1}-\psi_{n+1}=\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\sigma)

\displaystyle+\partial(\sigma_{n+1})+\sigma_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n}\mu^{B}_{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+w=n\\
u,w\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}(\varphi_{1}^{\otimes u}\otimes\sigma_{1}\otimes\psi_{1}^{\otimes w})\,.

Lemma 4.7
.

For any
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-homotopy
\sigma
of morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras
\varphi,\psi\colon A\to B
one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)-\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\psi)=\partial(

\displaystyle\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\sigma))+\sigma_{1}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{n}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(B)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+w=n\\
u,w\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}(\varphi_{1}^{\otimes u}\otimes\sigma_{1}\otimes\psi_{1}^{\otimes w})\,.

Proof.

We use the same notation and arguments as in
Lemmas

4.2
,
4.4
and
4.5
. We set

\begin{gathered}S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cder}_{(F,G)}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\,,\quad S_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cder}_{(F_{1},G_{1})}(s_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}S-S_{1}\,.\end{gathered}

For these the following identities hold

\begin{gathered}(F-G)\bar{D}^{A}=D^{B}S\bar{D}^{A}+SD^{A}\bar{D}^{A}\,,\quad\bar{D}^{B}(F-G)=\bar{D}^{B}D^{B}S+\bar{D}^{B}SD^{A}\\
\text{and}\quad D^{B}D^{B}\bar{S}=0=\bar{S}D^{A}D^{A}\end{gathered}

when restricted to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(n+1)}\to\Sigma B
. By straightforward computations we have

\displaystyle(\bar{F}\bar{D}^{A}-\bar{D}^{B}

\displaystyle\bar{F})-(\bar{G}\bar{D}^{A}-\bar{D}^{B}\bar{G})

\displaystyle=D^{B}_{1}(\bar{D}^{B}\bar{S}+\bar{S}\bar{D}^{A})-(\bar{D}^{B}\bar{S}+\bar{S}\bar{D}^{A})D^{A}_{1}+S_{1}\bar{D}^{A}\bar{D}^{A}-\bar{D}^{B}\bar{D}^{B}S_{1}

when restricted to
(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(n+1)}\to\Sigma B
. Replacing the shifted maps by the maps of the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structures, we obtain the desired identity.
∎

5.
Transfer of
A_{\infty}
-structures

We now give the transfer and lifting results for non-unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras. The arguments are inspired by
[
Kad80
,
Bur18
]
, who proved similar results over a field or for surjective quasi-isomorphisms, respectively. To work with non-surjective quasi-isomorphisms we first need the suitable lifting results for chain complexes.

5.1
.

We consider a commutative diagram of chain maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}

where
g
is degreewise injective with
\coker(g)
a bounded below complex of projectives, and
g^{\prime}
a quasi-isomorphism that is degreewise surjective. Then there exists a lift
h\colon V\to X
such that each triangle commutes; see for example
[
DS95
, Section 7]
.

If one wants to remove the condition that
g^{\prime}
is degreewise surjective, this comes at the price that the lower right triangle need not commute honestly, but only up to homotopy. This result is well-known, through it seems to not appear in the literature in the setting of chain complexes. We give a proof for completeness.

Lemma 5.2
.

We consider a commutative diagram of chain maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}

where
g
is degreewise injective with
\coker(g)
a bounded below complex of projectives, and
g^{\prime}
a quasi-isomorphism. Then there exists a chain map
h\colon V\to X
such that
hg=f
and
f^{\prime}\simeq g^{\prime}h
. Moreover, if
s
is the homotopy witnessing
f^{\prime}\simeq g^{\prime}h
, then
sg=0
.

Proof.

We denote by
\operatorname{cocyl}(g^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Sigma^{-1}\cone(Y\to\cone(g^{\prime}))
the mapping cocylinder of
g^{\prime}
. This is the mapping cylinder in the opposite category; for the mapping cylinder see for example
[
Wei94
, 1.5.5]
. The underlying graded module of the mapping cocylinder is
\Sigma^{-1}Y\oplus X\oplus Y
. This yields a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{cocyl}(g^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\,.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
\id_{X}\\
g^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0&\id_{Y}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}

It is straightforward to check that the vertical maps on the right are homotopy equivalences and thus quasi-isomorphisms. The bottom square satisfies the assumptions of
5.1
, and hence there exists a chain map
(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})^{T}\colon V\to\operatorname{cocyl}(g^{\prime})
such that each triangle commutes. It is straightforward to check that
h_{2}
is a chain map and
f=h_{2}g
and
f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}h_{2}=\partial(h_{1})
and
h_{1}g=0
. Hence
h=h_{2}
and
s=h_{1}
satisfy the desired properties.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
g\colon X\to Y
be a quasi-isomorphism and
f\colon F\to X
a chain map where
F
a bounded below complex of projectives. If
gf
is null-homotopic with homotopy

, then there exists a map

such that

and
s\simeq gh
.

Proof.

We have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cone(\id_{F})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\,.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(gf,s)}

By
Lemma

5.2
, there exists a chain map
(h_{1},h_{2})\colon\cone(\id_{F})\to X
such that the upper triangle commutes and the lower triangle commutes up to homotopy. It is straightforward to check that

satisfies the desired conditions.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
B
be an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra and let
\varepsilon_{1}\colon A\to B
be a quasi-isomorphism of complexes with
(A,\mu^{A}_{1})
a bounded complex of projectives. Then there exists an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
A
and a quasi-isomorphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras
\varepsilon\colon A\to B
extending
\varepsilon_{1}
. If
\varepsilon_{1}
is degreewise surjective, then the morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras
\varepsilon
can be chosen to be strict.

Proof.

We inductively construct maps
\mu^{A}_{n}
and
\varepsilon_{n}
satisfying the
n

3.1.1
and
3.3.1
.

For

we set
\mu^{A}_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\partial^{A}
. It is clear, that the first Stasheff identities are satisfied for
\mu^{A}_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{1}
.

Let
n\geq 2
and assume

is an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra and
\varepsilon
a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras. By
Lemma

4.2
, the obstruction map

\varepsilon_{1}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)=\partial(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varepsilon))+\operatorname{obs}^{n}(B)\varepsilon_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}=\partial(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varepsilon)-\mu^{B}_{n+1}\varepsilon_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)})\,;

the latter equality uses the
(n+1)

4.1.1
for

and that
\varepsilon_{1}
is a chain map. Using

5.3
we obtain a map

of degree

and a map

of degree

such that

\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)=-\partial(\mu^{A}_{n+1})\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varepsilon)-\mu^{B}_{n+1}\varepsilon_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}+\varepsilon_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1}=\partial(\varepsilon_{n+1})\,.

These are precisely

and

, and hence

is an
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-algebra and
\varepsilon
is a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-algebras.
∎

Theorem 5.5

.

. These are the first Stasheff identities

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)-\mu^{B}_{n+1}\varphi_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}+\varphi_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\partial(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\sigma))+\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\psi)+\sigma_{1}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{n}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(C)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+w=n\\
u,w\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}(\varphi_{1}^{\otimes u}\otimes\sigma_{1}\otimes\psi^{\otimes w})-\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varepsilon)\varphi_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}

\displaystyle-\partial(\sum_{p=2}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})}\varepsilon_{p}\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}})-\varepsilon_{1}\mu^{B}_{n+1}\varphi_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}+\varepsilon_{1}\varphi_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1}\,.

Since
A
and
C
are
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras and
\varepsilon
and
\psi
are morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras, the Stasheff identities

and
4.3.1
yield

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)-\mu^{B}_{n+1}\varphi_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}+\varphi_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\partial(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\sigma)+\psi_{n+1}-\sum_{p=2}^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})}\varepsilon_{p}\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}})

\displaystyle+(\varepsilon_{1}\varphi_{1}-\psi_{1})\mu^{A}_{n+1}-\sigma_{1}\partial(\mu^{A}_{n+1})

\displaystyle-\mu^{C}_{n+1}((\varepsilon_{1}\varphi_{1})^{\otimes(n+1)}-\psi^{\otimes(n+1)})+(-1)^{n}\partial(\mu^{C}_{n+1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+w=n\\
u,w\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}(\varphi_{1}^{\otimes u}\otimes\sigma_{1}\otimes\psi^{\otimes w})\,.

By the base case we have

, and thus

Similarly, we obtain for the terms involving

\displaystyle-\mu^{C}_{n+1}((\varepsilon_{1}\varphi_{1})^{\otimes(n+1)}-\psi^{\otimes(n+1)})+(-1)^{n}\partial(\mu^{C}_{n+1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+w=n\\
u,w\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}(\varphi_{1}^{\otimes u}\otimes\sigma_{1}\otimes\psi^{\otimes w})

is null-homotopic with homotopy

\displaystyle\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\sigma)

\displaystyle+\psi_{n+1}+\sigma_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1}

\displaystyle-\sum_{p=2}^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{t_{1}(\bm{\alpha})}\varepsilon_{p}\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}+(-1)^{n}\mu^{C}_{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+w=n\\
u,w\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}((\varepsilon_{1}\varphi_{1})^{\otimes u}\otimes\sigma_{1}\otimes\psi_{1}^{\otimes w})\,.

By

5.3
, there exists a map

\begin{gathered}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)-\mu^{B}_{n+1}\varphi_{1}^{\otimes(n+1)}+\varphi_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1}=\partial(\varphi_{n+1})\quad\text{and}\\
\begin{aligned} \operatorname{obs}^{n}(\sigma)+\psi_{n+1}+&\sigma_{1}\mu^{A}_{n+1}-\sum_{p=1}^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\

&+(-1)^{n}\mu^{C}_{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+w=n\\

These identities are precisely

and

, and hence

is a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
-algebras and
\sigma
an

-homotopy witnessing

Corollary 5.6
.

The transfer of the

Let

6.
Transfer of strictly unital
A_{\infty}
-structures

In many situations the algebra
B
is unital. This naturally leads to the questions whether this property is preserved by the transfer theorem. This is true under some mild conditions on the free resolution. Such results have been shown by
[
CG08
, Theorem 10]
over a field, and over a commutative ring for surjective quasi-isomorphisms by
[
Bur18
,
BCLP23
]
.

6.1
.

An
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
A
is
strictly unital
, if there exists a homogeneous map
\eta^{A}\colon Q\to A
, such that

(6.1.1)

\begin{gathered}\mu^{A}_{2}(\id_{A}\otimes\eta^{A})=\id_{A}=\mu^{A}_{2}(\eta^{A}\otimes\id_{A})\quad\text{and}\\
\mu^{A}_{k}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\eta^{A}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes w})=0\quad\text{for }k\neq 2\text{ and }u+w=k-1\,.\end{gathered}

The element
1_{A}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\eta^{A}(1_{Q})
is called the
strict unit
of
A
. An
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra is
split unital
, if it is strictly unital and
\eta^{A}
splits as a map of graded modules. Then we can write
A\cong Q\oplus\bar{A}
as graded modules. Any split unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
Q\oplus\bar{A}
is fully determined by the restrictions of
\mu_{k}
to

\bar{A}^{\otimes k}\to\bar{A}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{A}^{\otimes k}\to Q\,.

6.2
.

A morphism of strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras is a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras
\varphi\colon A\to B
such that

(6.2.1)

\begin{gathered}\varphi_{1}\eta^{A}=\eta^{B}\quad\text{and}\\
\varphi_{k}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\eta^{A}\otimes\id_{A}^{w})=0\quad\text{for }k\neq 2\text{ and }u+w=k-1\,.\end{gathered}

A homotopy of morphisms of strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras is a homotopy
\sigma
of morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras such that

(6.2.2)

\sigma_{k}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\eta^{A}\otimes\id_{A}^{w})=0\quad\text{for }u+w=k-1\,.

6.3
.

We say an
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra is strictly unital if
Eq.

6.1.1
holds for
1\leq k\leq n
, and we require the corresponding morphisms and homotopies to satisfy
Eqs.

6.2.1
and
6.2.2
for
1\leq k\leq n
.

The strictly unital property also has an implication for the obstructions. For a strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra
A
the unit map
\eta^{A}
induces a chain map

\eta^{A}_{k}\colon\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+w=k-1\\
u,w\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}A^{\otimes u}\otimes Q\otimes A^{\otimes w}\to A^{\otimes k}\,.

With the map
\eta_{k}
the strictly unital condition
Eq.

6.1.1
for
k
can be expressed as
\mu^{A}_{2}\eta^{A}_{2}
being the natural isomorphism for
k=2
, and
\mu^{A}_{k}\eta^{A}_{k}=0
for
k\neq 2
. Similarly,
Eq.

6.2.1
can be expressed as
\varphi_{1}\eta^{A}_{1}=\eta^{B}
for
k=1
and
\varphi_{k}\eta^{A}_{k}=0
for
k\neq 1
, and
Eq.

6.2.2
can be expressed as
\sigma_{k}\eta^{A}_{k}=0
.

It is straightforward to check that

\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)\eta^{A}_{n+1}=0\,,\quad\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)\eta^{A}_{n+1}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\sigma)\eta^{A}_{n+1}=0

for any
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra
A
, morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras
\varphi
and
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-homotopy
\sigma
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
B
be a strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra and let
\varepsilon_{1}\colon A\to B
be a quasi-isomorphism of complexes with
A
a bounded below complex of projectives. We further assume that
A=Q\oplus\bar{A}
as graded modules such that
\partial(Q)=0
and
(Q\to A\to B)=\eta^{B}
. Then there exists a split unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
A
and a quasi-isomorphism of strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras
\varepsilon\colon A\to B
extending
\varepsilon_{1}
. If
\varepsilon
is degreewise surjective, then the morphism of strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras
\varepsilon
can be chosen to be strict.

Proof.

This proof is similar to that of
Theorem

5.4
. The key difference is that we first restrict the maps to
\bar{A}^{\otimes(n+1)}
, before we use the argument of
Theorem

5.4
.

We let
\eta^{A}\colon Q\to A
be the natural inclusion. It will serve as the unit map. For every positive integer
n
the map
\eta^{A}_{n}
induced by
\eta^{A}
is degreewise injective, and
\coker(\eta_{n})=\bar{A}^{\otimes n}
is a bounded complex of projectives.

For
n=1
we set
\mu^{A}_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\partial^{A}
. Then
\mu^{A}_{1}
,
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\eta^{A}
satisfy the Stasheff identities
Eqs.

3.1.1
and
3.3.1
as well as the strictly unital conditions
Eqs.

6.1.1
and
6.2.1
.

For
n=2
we consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes Q+Q\otimes A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\otimes R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{A}_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{1}\otimes\varepsilon_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu^{R}_{2}}

By
Lemma

5.2
, there exists a chain map
\mu^{A}_{2}\colon A\otimes A\to A
such that the upper left triangle commutes and the lower right triangle commutes up to a homotopy
\varepsilon_{2}
. The former ensures the strictly unital conditions
Eqs.

6.1.1
and
6.2.1
, the latter the Stasheff identity
Eq.

3.3.1
. The Stasheff identity for algebras
Eq.

3.1.1
holds since
\mu^{A}_{2}
is a chain map.

Let
n\geq 3
and assume
A
is a strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebra and
\varepsilon
a morphism of strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-algebras. Since
\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)\eta^{A}_{n+1}=0
and
\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)\eta^{A}_{n+1}=0
, the obstructions factor through
\bar{A}^{\otimes(n+1)}
. We denote by
\overline{\operatorname{obs}}^{n}(A)
and
\overline{\operatorname{obs}}^{n}(\varphi)
these maps. As graded modules
\bar{A}^{\otimes(n+1)}
is a direct summand of
A^{\otimes(n+1)}
and the maps
\overline{\operatorname{obs}}^{n}(A)
and
\overline{\operatorname{obs}}^{n}(\varphi)
are in fact the restrictions of
\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)
and
\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)
to
\bar{A}^{\otimes(n+1)}
. Hence they satisfy the analogous identities as
\operatorname{obs}^{n}(A)
and
\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\varphi)
in the proof of
Theorem

5.4
, and by the same argument as in the proof of

5.4
we obtain morphisms
\bar{\mu}^{A}_{n+1}\colon\bar{A}^{\otimes n+1}\to A
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{n+1}\colon\bar{A}^{\otimes(n+1)}\to B
. Their pre-composition with
A^{\otimes n+1}\to\bar{A}^{\otimes n+1}
yields the desired morphisms.
∎

7.
A_{\infty}
-modules

Finally, we sketch the arguments for the transfer of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-module structures. The is approach is analogous to that for
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras, so we mimic
Sections

3
,
4
,
5
and
6
.

7.1
.

Let
A
be an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra over
Q
. An
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules over
A
is a graded module
M=\{M_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}
equipped with maps

\mu^{M}_{k}\colon A^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes M\to M\quad\text{for }k\geq 1

of degree
(k-2)
satisfying

(7.1.1)

\begin{gathered}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u\geqslant 0,v,w\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}\mu^{M}_{u+1+w}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu^{A}_{v}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes(w-1)}\otimes\id_{M})\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=k\\
u\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u}\mu^{M}_{u+1}(\id_{A}^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu^{M}_{v})=0\,.\end{gathered}

These identities are similar to
Eq.

3.1.1
, and provide relations of maps
A^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes M\to M
. For convenience we drop the superscript on the higher multiplication maps and the subscript on the identity maps, and combine the sum as in
Eq.

3.1.1
.

Over a strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-module is
strictly unital
, if

\begin{gathered}\mu^{M}_{2}(\eta\otimes\id_{M})=\id_{M}\quad\text{and}\\
\mu^{M}_{k}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\eta^{A}\otimes\id^{\otimes(w+1)})=0\quad\text{for }k\geq 2\text{ and }u+w=k-2\,.\end{gathered}

For an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-module we define
m^{M}_{k}\colon(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes M\to M
via
m^{M}_{k}({\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes\id_{M})=(-1)^{k-1}\mu^{M}_{k}
. It is straightforward to check, that the Stasheff identity
Eq.

7.1.1
is equivalent to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}m_{u+1+w}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes m_{v}\otimes\id^{\otimes w})=0\,.

The shifted maps
m_{k}
are of degree
-1
. Similar to
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras, an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-module structure on
M
can be encoded as a coderivation
D_{M}
on the comodule
{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)\otimes M
over
{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)
with
D_{M}^{2}=0
; see for example
[
LH03
, Lemme 2.1.2.1b]
.

7.2
.

Let
M
and
N
be
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules over an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra
A
. A
morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules

\rho\colon M\to N
consists of maps

\rho_{k}\colon A^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes M\to N\quad\text{for }k\geq 1

of degree
(k-1)
satisfying

(7.2.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}\rho_{u+1+w}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu_{v}\otimes\id^{\otimes w})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=k\\
u\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}\mu_{u+1}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\rho_{v})\,.

A morphism of strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules needs to additionally satisfy

\rho_{k}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\eta\otimes\id^{\otimes(w+1)})=0\quad\text{for }k\geq 2\text{ and }u+w=k-2\,.

We define
r_{k}\colon(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes M\to N
via
r_{k}({\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes\id)=(-1)^{k-1}\rho_{k}
. It is straightforward to check, that
Eq.

7.2.1
is equivalent to

\begin{gathered}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}r_{u+1+w}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes m_{v}\otimes\id^{\otimes w})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=k\\
u\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}\mu_{u+1}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes r_{v})\,.\end{gathered}

The shifted maps are homogeneous, and a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules corresponds to a morphism of comodules
R\colon{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)\otimes M\to{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)\otimes N
satisfying
RD^{M}=D^{N}R
; see
[
LH03
, Lemme 2.1.2.1a]
.

7.3
.

Let
\rho\colon M\to N
and
\pi\colon L\to M
be morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules over an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebra
A
. The composition
\rho\circ\pi\colon L\to N
is given by

(\rho\circ\pi)_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=k\\
u\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}\rho_{u+1}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\pi_{v})\,.

The identity morphism
\id_{M}\colon M\to M
of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules is given by

(\id_{M})_{k}=\begin{cases}\id_{M}&k=1\\
0&\text{else}\,.\end{cases}

7.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\to B
be a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras and
M
an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-module over
B
. Then there exists a natural
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules structure over
A
on
M
given by

\mu^{\varphi_{*}M}_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mu^{M}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu^{\varphi_{*}M}_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{p=2}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{p-1}\\
|\bm{\alpha}|=k-1\end{subarray}}\mu^{M}_{k}(\bm{\varphi}^{\otimes\bm{\alpha}}\otimes\id_{M})\,;

cf.
[
LPWZ04
, Section 7]
. We write
\varphi_{*}M
for the restricted
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-module over
A
.

7.5
.

A morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules
\rho\colon M\to N
is
null-homotopic
, if there exist maps

\tau_{k}\colon A^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes M\to N\quad\text{for }k\geq 1

of degree
k
satisfying

(7.5.1)

\displaystyle\rho_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+vw}

\displaystyle\tau_{u+1+w}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu_{v}\otimes\id^{\otimes w})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=k\\
u\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u}\mu_{u+1}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\tau_{v})\,.

We call
\tau
an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-homotopy
. We say morphisms
\rho,\pi\colon M\to N
are
homotopic
and write
\rho\simeq\pi
, if
\rho-\pi
is null-homotopic.

When
\rho\colon M\to N
is a morphism of strictly unital
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules, we say that it is null-homotopic if the above condition holds and

\tau_{k}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\eta\otimes\id^{\otimes(w+1)})=0\quad\text{for }k\geq 2\text{ and }u+w=k-2\,.

We define
t_{k}\colon(\Sigma A)^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes M\to N
via
t_{k}({\scriptstyle\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes\id)=(-1)^{k-1}\tau_{k}
. It is straightforward to check that

is equivalent to

r_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=k\\
u,w\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}t_{u+1+w}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes m_{v}\otimes\id^{\otimes w})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=k\\
u\geqslant 0,v\geqslant 1\end{subarray}}m_{u+1}(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes t_{v})\,.

Analogously to
Section

4
we define the
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-structures for modules, morphisms of modules, and homotopies of morphisms of modules. So we can study the obstructions, and we obtain several relations that these obstructions satisfy.

\operatorname{obs}^{n}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=n+1\\

\displaystyle\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\rho)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v+w=n+1\\

\displaystyle(\id^{\otimes u}\otimes\mu_{v}\otimes\id^{\otimes w})

Lemma 7.7
.

For any
\mathrm{A}_{n}
-module
M
one has

\partial(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(M))=0\,.\qed

For any morphism of

\rho_{1}\operatorname{obs}^{n}(M)-\operatorname{obs}^{n}(N)(\id^{\otimes n}\otimes\rho_{1})=\partial(\operatorname{obs}^{n}(\rho))\,.\qed

Remark 7.16
.

There are various sign conventions for the Stasheff identities and for the relations to the shifted maps. We try to give an overview on the most common conventions and how they are related.

For the Stasheff identities of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras there are mainly two different conventions: First there is the convention used in this article, which also, among others, is used in
[
Sta63b
,
Mer99
,
Kel01
,
LV12
]
. For the other convention one replaces the sign
(-1)^{u+vw}
in
Eq.

3.1.1
by
(-1)^{uv+w}
; this is used in
[
LH03
,
Sag10
]
. One sees these conventions are equivalent by replacing
\mu^{A}_{k}
by
(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\mu^{A}_{k}
. The Stasheff identities for morphisms and homotopies need to be adjusted appropriately.

For modules the situation is a bit more complex. First the sign is chosen parallel to that of algebras described above. A second difference is in the connection to the bar construction. In this article we use the equivalence between
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-module structures and square-zero coderivations on
{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)\otimes M
. Additionally, these structures are equivalent to square-zero coderivations on
{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)\otimes\Sigma M
. One obtains different Stasheff identities for morphisms of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-modules, whether one translates a morphism from
{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)\otimes M
or
{\rm T}^{c}(\Sigma A)\otimes\Sigma M
to a morphism of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras; see for example
[
Kel01
, 4.2]
for the latter.

Some sources avoid the problem of a sign convention, by defining the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structures as the shifted structures. This has the advantage of simplifying many formulas. The drawback is that it does not behave as an algebra anymore.

There seem to be two ‘good’ conventions for the relationship between
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structures and the corresponding shifted maps. There is the one used in this article, and then the one obtained by adding a factor
(-1)^{k-1}
to the definition of the shifted map for every structure. In practice this choice does not have a big impact, but differences can for example be observed in the strictly unital condition when expressed using the shifted maps.

Tail asymptotics for extinction times of self-similar fragmentations
Bénédicte Haas

Abstract
We provide the exact large-time behavior of the tail distribution of the extinction time of a self-similar fragmentation process with a negative index of self-similarity, improving thus a previous result on the logarithmic asymptotic behavior of this tail. Two factors influence this behavior: the distribution of the largest fragment at the time of a dislocation and the index of self-similarity. As an application we obtain the asymptotic behavior of all moments of the largest fragment and compare it to the behavior of the moments of a tagged fragment, whose decrease is in general significantly slower. We illustrate our results on several examples, including fragmentations related to random real trees – for which we thus obtain the large-time behavior of the tail distribution of the height – such as the stable Lévy trees of Duquesne, Le Gall and Le Jan (including the Brownian tree of Aldous), the alpha-model of Ford and the beta-splitting model of Aldous.

Introduction

The aim of this article is to give exact bounds for the (upper) tail distribution of the extinction time of a self-similar fragmentation with a negative index of self-similarity. In Probability theory, self-similar fragmentation processes, which are meant to model the evolution of a system of particles that fragment repeatedly, were developed in a general framework including infinite splitting rates by Bertoin at the beginning of the 2000’s
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
, inspired by previous works on fragmentation and coalescence processes (to name a few:
[
28
,
16
,
9
,
10
,
27
,
35
]
). In these models of fragmentations with a
negative
index of self-similarity, particles are subject to successive fragmentations which intensify as the masses of particles decrease. This leads to a shattering phenomenon
[
31
,
16
,
5
]
, where a particle is entirely reduced to dust – a set of zero-mass particles – in finite time which is what we call the extinction time of the particle. Bounds for the
logarithm
of the tail of the distribution of this extinction time where established in
[
19
]
, under a regular variation assumption on the parameters of the model. Our objective here is to refine considerably this result by setting up precise bounds for the
non-logarithmic
tail of the extinction time, within a broad framework of parameters, relaxing the regular variation assumption. As a byproduct we will obtain the large time behavior of all moments of the largest fragment. Interestingly their decrease is significantly slower (this is explicitly quantified) than that of moments of a typical fragment, which was already known. These results also allow us to conjecture the existence of a Yaglom limit for a rescaled version of the fragmentation process conditioned on non extinction.

In practice, a difficulty is to concretize on specific examples the overall rather abstract results that we obtain. We will illustrate a fairly general approach on a series of ‘natural’ examples, for most of which the results we get are new. This unified framework also allows us to retrieve estimates already known (
[
15
]
) for the tail distributions of the heights of stable Lévy trees.

Background on self-similar fragmentations

(
\mathbf{\alpha,\nu}
)-fragmentations.
Different state spaces have been considered to model self-similar fragmentations, depending on whether one wishes to keep track of the genealogy of the fragmentation or is interested only in the evolution of the masses of the particles involved. We refer to the book of Bertoin
[
6
]
for a detailed introduction to these processes. Here we will mainly be interested in the evolution of masses, and so we will work on the set of non-increasing non-negative sequences with sum less or equal to 1:

\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}:=\left\{\mathbf{s}=(s_{i})_{i\geq 1}\in[0,1]^{\infty}:\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{i}\leq 1\right\}.

The formal definition of a self-similar fragmentation with index
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
then holds as follows:

Definition 1
.

A
self-similar fragmentation process
F
with index
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}
-valued Markov process continuous in probability such that if
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{s}}
denotes the distribution of
F
starting from
\mathbf{s}=(s_{i})_{i\geq 1}\in\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}
, then

F\text{ under }\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{s}}\penalty\ \overset{(\mathrm{d})}{=}\penalty\ \left(\left\{s_{i}F^{(i)}(s_{i}^{\alpha}t)\right\}^{\downarrow},t\geq 0\right)

where the
F^{(i)},i\geq 1
are i.i.d. with common distribution
\mathbb{P}_{(1,0,\dots)}
and the descending arrow means that the elements involved in the brackets are ranked in decreasing order.

The dynamics of such a process is therefore characterized by a branching property (given their masses, different particles evolve independently) and a scaling property (a particle fragments at a rate proportional to its mass to the power
\alpha
). In the following we will group these two properties under the name of
fragmentation property
.

Throughout we will focus on self-similar fragmentation processes that are
pure-jump
(i.e. with no erosion, with Bertoin’s vocabulary) and with a
negative index of self-similarity

\alpha
. Also, we will always work under
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{(1,0,\ldots)}
. The distribution of such a process is then characterized by two parameters
[
4
,
2
]
: the index
\alpha<0
and a
\sigma
-finite measure
\nu
on
\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}
such that
\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}}(1-s_{1})\nu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s})<\infty
, generally called the
dislocation measure
.
We can summarize roughly the roles of
\alpha
and
\nu
as follows:

a particle of mass
m
fragments at rate
m^{\alpha}\nu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s})
.

When the measure
\nu
is finite this means that a particle of mass
m
waits an exponential time with parameter
m^{\alpha}
before splitting into sub-particles with masses
m\mathbf{s}
, with
\mathbf{s}
distributed according to
\nu(\cdot)/\nu(\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow})
. For more general measures
\nu
, such a process can be constructed via a Poisson point process and appropriate time changes depending on the ancestral lines of the fragments, see the above mentioned references.

From now on
F
denotes such an
(\alpha,\nu)
-fragmentation process, with
\alpha<0
and
F(0)=(1,0,\ldots)
. We also assume that
\nu(\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow})>0
so that the particles indeed fragment. All these assumptions are implicit is the rest of the paper.

The extinction time
\zeta

As mentioned in the Introduction, since

the first time at which the process is entirely reduced to dust. Our goal is to obtain a precise estimate of
\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)
as

in a fairly general framework.

The functions
\phi
and
\psi
, and connections with a tagged fragment.
The asymptotic behavior of the tail

will depend on a function

and that we now introduce. First we introduce the function
\phi

\phi(x):=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}}\bigg(1-\sum_{i\geq 1}s_{i}^{x+1}\bigg)\nu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}),\quad x\geq 0

(2.1)

which is the Laplace exponent of a subordinator involved in the distribution of the evolution of a typical fragment of the process. To settle this formally, we recall that it is possible to associate to the process

an interval version of this fragmentation, i.e. a process
(O_{F}(t),t\geq 0)

of nested open subsets of

for all times
t_{2}\geq t_{1}\geq 0
) such that

is the decreasing rearrangement of the lengths of the interval components of

, for all
t\geq 0
, see the papers
[
4
,
2
]
. In such an interval version, consider a tagged point

uniformly distributed on
(0,1)
and independent of

and let

denote the size of the fragment (i.e. the interval component) containing

at time

. Then the process

writes

F_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)=\exp(-\xi_{\rho(t)}),\quad t\geq 0

(2.2)

where

is a subordinator with Laplace transform
\phi
and

the Lamperti time-change defined for all

by
\rho(t)=\inf{\{u\geq 0:\int_{0}^{u}\exp(-|\alpha|\xi_{r})\mathrm{d}r>t\}}
, see
[
6
, Chapter 3.2]
or
[
4
, Section 4]
. Note that the process

reaches 0 at time

which belongs to the well-studied family of

F_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)=F_{i}(t)
with probability

for all

, and

.

The function

, which is a bijection from

to
(x_{\psi},\infty)
, where

is the inverse of the right derivative at 0 of

x>x_{\psi}

\frac{\psi(x)}{\phi(\psi(x))}=x.

(2.4)

Note that the functions
\psi
and
x\mapsto\psi(x)/x
are both increasing since, as a Laplace exponent of a subordinator,

is increasing and

is decreasing.

A point that will be crucial in our study is the asymptotic behavior of the tail
\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)
. As said above, exponential integrals of subordinators have been well-studied, see

8
]
for a survey and

\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)\ \propto\ \frac{t(\psi^{\prime}(|\alpha|t))^{1/2}}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\exp\left(-\int_{x_{\psi}+1}^{t}\frac{\psi(|\alpha|r)}{|\alpha|r}\mathrm{d}r\right)

and we have that

\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t+x\frac{|\alpha|t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)}{\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)}\penalty\ \underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\penalty\ \exp(-x),\quad\forall x\geq 0.

This last point is certainly a consequence of (

Remark.
Note that if we set

Bounds for the logarithm of
\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)
.
With the notation introduced above and under the assumption that
\phi

For comparison, we mention that the tail of the extinction time of a non-monotonic

First examples of non-logarithmic estimates.
For the self-similar fragmentations without growth that we study here, there are only a few special cases in the litterature where a non-logarithmic asymptotic expression of

On the tail of

Asymptotics of moments of the largest and tagged fragments

4.2
Connection between the tail of
\zeta
and moments of tagged fragments

Using the first and second moments methods, we get:

Proposition 8
.

Assume
(

H

)
. Then for

large enough

\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)\big]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag},(1)}(t)F_{\mathrm{tag},(2)}(t)\big]}\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)\penalty\ \lesssim\penalty\ \left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{2}{|\alpha|}}\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)\big]

and the right-hand side could be rewritten as

\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)\penalty\ \lesssim\penalty\ \left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t).

Proof.

For
t\geq 0
, let

S(t):=\sum_{i\geq 1}F^{2}_{i}(t).

By definition of the tagged fragment processes, conditionally on
F
and independently for
k\in\{1,2\}

F_{\mathrm{tag},(k)}(t)=F_{i}(t)\quad\text{with probability}\quad F_{i}(t),\quad i\geq 1,

and
F_{\mathrm{tag},(k)}(t)=0
otherwise. Hence
\mathbb{E}[F_{\mathrm{tag},(k)}(t)|F]=S(t)
,

and
\mathbb{E}[F_{\mathrm{tag},(1)}(t)F_{\mathrm{tag},(2)}(t)|F]=(S(t))^{2}
, which gives

\mathbb{E}\left[S(t)\right]=\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)\big]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\big[(S(t))^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag},(1)}(t)F_{\mathrm{tag},(2)}(t)\big].

\bullet
This immediately leads to the lower bound of the proposition since
\{\zeta>t\}=\{S(t)>0\}
, which together with the second moment method gives

\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)=\mathbb{P}(S(t)>0)\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}[S(t)]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}[(S(t))^{2}]}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)\big]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag},(1)}(t)F_{\mathrm{tag},(2)}(t)\big]}.

\bullet
To get the upper bound we use the identity (valid for all
t\geq 0
)

\left(\zeta-t\right)^{+}\penalty\ \overset{(\mathrm{d})}{=}\penalty\ \sup_{i\geq 1}F^{|\alpha|}_{i}(t)\zeta^{(i)},

(4.3)

where

is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables distributed as
\zeta
, independent of
F(t)
. This is a simple consequence of the fragmentation property of
F
at time
t
. Then, by Markov’s inequality, we get for

large enough

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\zeta>t+\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{i\geq 1}F^{|\alpha|}_{i}(t)\zeta^{(i)}>\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{2}{|\alpha|}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\geq 1}F^{2}_{i}(t)\left(\zeta^{(i)}\right)^{\frac{2}{|\alpha|}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{2}{|\alpha|}}\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)\big]\mathbb{E}\Big[\zeta^{\frac{2}{|\alpha|}}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)

where for the last line we use Proposition
7
and the fact that all positive moments of
\zeta
are finite (since the tail of
\zeta
decreases exponentially fast, at least).
Now set for
t>0

f(t):=t+\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\penalty\ \geq\penalty\ t

and note that

\psi(|\alpha|t)/t\leq\psi(|\alpha|f(t))/f(t)

since the function

is increasing. Together with (
2.6
), all this implies that for

large enough

\mathbb{P}\left(\zeta>f(t)\right)\penalty\ \lesssim\penalty\ \left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|f(t))}{f(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}\penalty\ \mathbb{P}\left(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>f(t)\right).

To conclude and get the expected upper bound, it remains to note that the function

is bijective from
(a,\infty)
to
(f(a),\infty)
for

as

which tends to 1 as

and the function

is bounded for

large enough (this is a consequence of (

\mathbb{P}\left(\zeta>t\right)\penalty\ \lesssim\penalty\ \left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}\penalty\ \mathbb{P}\left(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t\right)

or equivalently (by Proposition

\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)\penalty\ \lesssim\penalty\ \left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{2}{|\alpha|}}\mathbb{E}\big[F_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)\big].

\hfill\square

4.3

Proof of Corollary

.
We assume here that
\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}}(1-s_{1})^{-1}\nu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s})<\infty
, in particular
\nu
is finite. So we may and will also assume that
\nu(\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow})=1
, to ease notation. In this case, the fragmentation process

remains constant equal to

during a time

which has an exponential distribution with parameter 1 and then jumps to

which is distributed according to the probability
\nu
and independent of
T_{1}
. This, together with the fragmentation property at time

, leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(T_{1}>t)+\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{i\geq 1}F_{i}^{|\alpha|}(T_{1})\zeta^{(i)}+T_{1}>t,T_{1}\leq t\bigg)

\displaystyle e^{-t}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{u-t}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{i\geq 1}F_{i}^{|\alpha|}(T_{1})\zeta^{(i)}\geq u\bigg)\mathrm{d}u,

where the
\zeta^{(i)},i\geq 1
are i.i.d. distributed as
\zeta
and independent of
(F(T_{1}),T_{1})

is increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
It remains to prove that it is also bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
(it will then be clear with the above equality that the limit is larger than 1).
Thanks to Proposition
3
this is equivalent to show that
t\mapsto e^{t}\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, since
\psi(|\alpha|t)/t
converges to
|\alpha|\nu(\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow})
here. And then, thanks to
[
30
, Section 5]
(or
[
18
, Case 1 in Section 2.2]
), this is indeed the case as soon as the Lévy measure
\pi
of the subordinator
|\alpha|\xi
involved in the construction of
\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}
via (
2.3
) satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-1}\pi(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
. By (
2.1
), this Lévy measure is defined in terms of
\nu
by

\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\pi(\mathrm{d}x)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}}\sum_{i\geq 1}s_{i}f(|\alpha||\ln(s_{i})|)\nu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s})

for any positive measurable function

, and it is now easy to see that the finiteness of

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-1}\pi(\mathrm{d}x)
is equivalent to that of
\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}}(1-s_{1})^{-1}\nu(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s})
(use that

\hfill\square

Proof of Proposition

For the proof of Proposition
6
we will use Proposition
3
and Proposition
7
several times. We proceed in two steps, each setting one of the inequalities. In the following we fix

.

\bullet
Let
p>1
be such that

and let
q:=p/(p-1)
. Using first Hölder’s inequality and then the definition of the tagged fragment we obviously have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F^{a|\alpha|}_{1}(t)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[F^{a|\alpha|}_{1}(t)\mathbf{1}_{\{\zeta>t\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F^{pa|\alpha|}_{1}(t)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}F^{pa|\alpha|}_{i}(t)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F^{pa|\alpha|-1}_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)^{\frac{pa|\alpha|-1}{|\alpha|}\times\frac{1}{p}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)^{\frac{1}{q}}

where the last inequality is obtained by Proposition
7
. Using Proposition
3
and that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, the upper bound can be rewritten as the expected

\left(\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)^{a}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t).

\bullet
To get the inequality in the other direction, we will first show that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big((\zeta-t)^{+}\big)^{a}\Big]\penalty\ \gtrsim\penalty\ \left(\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)^{a}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)

(6.1)

where
x^{+}=\max(x,0)
. To prove this, we use a similar (but more precise) result for the random variable
\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}
, which is that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{a}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left((\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}-t)^{+}\right)^{a}\right]}{\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)}=|\alpha|^{a}\Gamma(a+1).

(6.2)

Roughly, this is a consequence of (
2.6
). See
[
18
, Section 6]
for a proof in the more general setting of exponential functionals of subordinators. Then use that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big((\zeta-t)^{+}\big)^{a}\Big]=a\int_{t}^{\infty}\big((x-t)^{+}\big)^{a-1}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>x)\mathrm{d}x

together with Proposition
3
to get that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big((\zeta-t)^{+}\big)^{a}\Big]\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ a\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|x)}{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}\big((x-t)^{+}\big)^{a-1}\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>x)\mathrm{d}x.

Recalling that the function

is increasing, by definition of

(see (

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}a\int_{t}^{\infty}\big((x-t)^{+}\big)^{a-1}\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(|\alpha|t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big((\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}-t)^{+}\big)^{a}\Big]

\displaystyle\underset{\text{by }(\ref{boundI})}{\asymp}

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)^{a}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t).

Hence (
6.1
).

) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big((\zeta-t)^{+}\big)^{ca}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F^{\frac{ca|\alpha|}{2}}_{1}(t)\sup_{i\geq 1}F^{\frac{ca|\alpha|}{2}}_{i}(t)(\zeta^{i})^{ca}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F^{a|\alpha|}_{1}(t)\right]^{\frac{c}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{i\geq 1}F^{\frac{ca|\alpha|}{2-c}}_{i}(t)(\zeta^{i})^{\frac{2ca}{2-c}}\right]^{\frac{2-c}{2}},

\frac{ca|\alpha|}{2-c}>1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{i\geq 1}F_{i}(t)^{\frac{ca|\alpha|}{2-c}}(\zeta^{i})^{\frac{2ca}{2-c}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}F_{i}(t)^{\frac{ca|\alpha|}{2-c}}(\zeta^{i})^{\frac{2ca}{2-c}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F^{\frac{ca|\alpha|}{2-c}-1}_{\mathrm{tag}}(t)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta^{\frac{2ca}{2-c}}\right]

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)^{\frac{ca}{2-c}-\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{\psi(|\alpha|t)}\right)^{\frac{ca}{2-c}}\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)

7
Some examples and applications

Our objectif is to illustrate more concretely our main results with simple or natural models of fragmentation processes, and related random trees. The general strategy to get explicit bounds is of course to find a sufficiently precise asymptotic expansion of the function
\phi
(
2.1
) related to the model, then deduce one for
\psi
(
2.4
) and conclude with Theorem
2
and/or its corollaries. To illustrate this we start with the following general result. A (simple) proof of the first part of it can be found in
[
18
, Lemma 19]
. The second part is a direct consequence of Corollary
5
.

Lemma 12
.

Assume that
\nu
is a dislocation measure such that the corresponding
\phi
writes

\phi(x)=x^{\gamma}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{c_{i}}{x^{\gamma_{i}}}+O(x^{-1-\varepsilon})\right)

for some
\gamma\in[0,1)
,
1/2<\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}\ldots<\gamma_{k-1}<\gamma_{k}=1
,
\varepsilon>0
and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i\leq k
. Then,

\frac{\psi(x)}{x}=x^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{c_{i}}{(1-\gamma)x^{\frac{\gamma_{i}}{1-\gamma}}}\right)+O(x^{-1-\eta})

for some
\eta>0
. Consequently for any
(\alpha,\nu)
-fragmentation, by Corollary
5
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{|\alpha|}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{c_{k}}{|\alpha|(1-\gamma)}}\exp\left(-|\alpha|^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}(1-\gamma)t^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{c_{i}|\alpha|^{\frac{\gamma-\gamma_{i}}{1-\gamma}}}{1-\gamma_{i}}t^{\frac{1-\gamma_{i}}{1-\gamma}}\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\frac{1}{|\alpha|}}\penalty\ \mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t).

From this, together with Proposition
6
and Proposition
7
, we deduce a precise expression of the decrease of positive moments of the largest and tagged fragments.
We emphasize that for the models where the function
\phi
has an asymptotic expansion as above but with some
\gamma_{i}
s smaller or equal to
1/2
, there will be additional contributions to the non-
O(x^{-1-\eta})
terms of
\psi(x)/x
, and consequently to the asympotic expression of
\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)
: the closer a
\gamma_{i}
is to 0, the more it contributes to a large number of terms (see for example the first calculations for the ‘Beta fragmentations’ in the forthcoming Example 3).

Most of the examples that we detail below are based on the above lemma. We will also need the following asymptotic expansion for a quotient of Gamma functions: for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

\frac{\Gamma(x+c)}{\Gamma(x)}=x^{c}\left(1-\frac{c(1-c)}{2x}+O(x^{-2})\right).

(7.1)

This is a direct consequence of the asymptotic expansion of order 2 of the Gamma function
\Gamma(x)=e^{-x}x^{x-1/2}\sqrt{2\pi}\big(1+(12x)^{-1}+O(x^{-2})\big).
We will need as well the following lemma, which is a direct consequence of (
7.1
).

Lemma 13
.

Let
a>0,b>-1
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(b)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u^{x})u^{a-1}(1-u)^{b-1}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(a+b)}-\frac{\Gamma(x+a)}{\Gamma(x+a+b)}

\displaystyle\underset{\text{as $x\rightarrow\infty$}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(a+b)}-x^{-b}+b\left(a+\frac{b}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)x^{-b-1}+O(x^{-b-2})

where
1/\Gamma
denotes the extension to
\mathbb{C}
by analytic continuation of the function
1/\Gamma
initially defined on
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}(z)>0\}
.

7.1
Examples with finite splitting rate

When the dislocation measure
\nu
is finite, the quotient
\psi(|\alpha|t)/t
converges to a non-zero limit as
t\rightarrow\infty
and the tails
\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)
and
\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}>t)
are therefore asymptotically proportional (by Proposition
3
) and also proportional to the moments of the largest and tagged fragments (by Proposition
6
and Proposition
7
respectively). We can thus restrict ourselves here to statements on the tails of the extinction times.

Since there is no loss of generality in considering a multiple of the measure
\nu
, we will assume throughout this section that
\nu
is a probability. Our goal is to show with simple examples how the distribution of the largest piece under
\nu
influences the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)
. We start with the following immediate consequence of Corollary
4
.

Ex.1. Fragmentations into
k
identical pieces.
In these cases a fragment of mass
m
splits into
k
fragments of same mass
m/k
. Then
\nu=\delta_{(\frac{1}{k},\ldots,\frac{1}{k})}
, and for any
\alpha<0
the extinction time
\zeta
of an
(\alpha,\nu)
-fragmentation satisfies

\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)\penalty\ \propto\penalty\ \mathrm{\exp}(-t).

Ex.2. Uniform fragmentation into
k
pieces.
Here a fragment splits uniformly into
k
sub-fragments, i.e. according to a Dirichlet
\mathrm{Dir}(1,\ldots,1)
distribution on the
k-1
-dimensional simplex. The measure
\nu
is then the push-forward of this distribution by the decreasing rearrangement function and

\phi(x)=1-k\mathbb{E}\big[B_{k}^{x+1}\big]
where
B_{k}
follows a
\mathrm{Beta}(1,k-1)
-distribution. Hence

\phi(x)=1-\frac{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(x+2)}{\Gamma(k+x+1)},\quad\forall x\geq 0.

The behavior of the tail of the extinction time of such an
(\alpha,\nu)
-fragmentation differs therefore significantly according to whether
k=2
or
k\geq 3
. In the latter case,
\phi(x)=1+O(x^{-2})
whereas when
k=2
,
\phi(x)=x/(x+2)
(and therefore
\psi(x)=x-2
for
x\geq 2
). This observation, together with Lemma
12
(or directly Corollary
5
) leads to

\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{\frac{2}{|\alpha|}}\exp(-t)\quad\text{when }k=2,\qquad\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ \exp(-t)\text{ when }k\geq 3.

Ex.3. Beta fragmentations.
To understand better the role of the distribution of the largest piece under the probability
\nu
, consider more generally the (binary) cases where a fragment of mass
m
splits into two fragments of masses
mB
and
m(1-B)
where
B
has a
\mathrm{Beta}(a,b)
distribution on
[0,1]
,
a,b>0
. By symmetry we can assume that
b\geq a
. (Note that when
a=b=1
we recover the uniform binary splitting.) The measure
\nu
is then the distribution of
(\max(B,1-B),\min(B,1-B))
and

\phi(x)=1-\mathbb{E}[B^{x+1}]-\mathbb{E}[(1-B)^{x+1}]=1-\frac{\Gamma(a+b)\Gamma(x+a+1)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(x+a+b+1)}-\frac{\Gamma(a+b)\Gamma(b+x+1)}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(x+a+b+1)}

so by (
7.1
),

\phi(x)=1-\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(b)x^{a}}-\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)x^{b}}+O(x^{-a-1}).

When
a>1/2
, Lemma
12
gives the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)
for such an
(\alpha,\nu)
-fragmentation, leading to six different situations:

\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ \exp(-t)\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }b\geq a>1\\
t^{\frac{b}{|\alpha|}}&\text{ if }b>a=1\\
t^{\frac{2}{|\alpha|}}&\text{ if }b=a=1\\
\exp\left(\frac{\Gamma(a+b)t^{1-a}}{\Gamma(b)(1-a)|\alpha|^{a}}\right)&\text{ if }b>1>a>1/2\\
\exp\left(\frac{\Gamma(a+b)t^{1-a}}{\Gamma(b)(1-a)|\alpha|^{a}}\right)t^{\frac{a}{|\alpha|}}&\text{ if }1=b>a>1/2\\
\exp\left(\frac{\Gamma(a+b)t^{1-a}}{\Gamma(b)(1-a)|\alpha|^{a}}+\frac{\Gamma(a+b)t^{1-b}}{\Gamma(a)(1-b)|\alpha|^{b}}\right)&\text{ if }1>b\geq a>1/2.\end{array}\right.

Otherwise, when
a\leq 1/2
, the smaller
a
and
b
are, the more terms there are in the estimates of
\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)
. To illustrate, we consider the cases when
b\in(1/2,1)
and
a\in(1/3,1/2]
. It is easy to see that then:

\frac{\psi(x)}{x}=1-\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(b)x^{a}}-\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)x^{b}}-\frac{a(\Gamma(a+b))^{2}}{(\Gamma(b))^{2}x^{2a}}-\frac{(a+b)(\Gamma(a+b))^{2}}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)x^{a+b}}+O(x^{-2b})+O(x^{-3a})+O(x^{-a-1}).

And so for
b\in(1/2,1)
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\zeta>t)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\

\displaystyle\exp\left(-t+\frac{\Gamma(a+b)t^{1-a}}{\Gamma(b)(1-a)|\alpha|^{a}}+\frac{\Gamma(a+b)t^{1-b}}{\Gamma(a)(1-b)|\alpha|^{b}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t^{\frac{(\Gamma(b+1/2))^{2}}{2(\Gamma(b))^{2}|\alpha|}}&\text{ if }a=1/2\\
\exp\left(\frac{a(\Gamma(a+b))^{2}t^{1-2a}}{(\Gamma(b))^{2}(1-2a)|\alpha|^{2a}}\right)&\text{ if }a\in(1/3,1/2),\penalty\ a+b>1\\
\exp\left(\frac{a(\Gamma(a+b))^{2}t^{1-2a}}{(\Gamma(b))^{2}(1-2a)|\alpha|^{2a}}\right)t^{\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)|\alpha|}}&\text{ if }a\in(1/3,1/2),\penalty\ a+b=1\\
\exp\left(\frac{a(\Gamma(a+b))^{2}t^{1-2a}}{(\Gamma(b))^{2}(1-2a)|\alpha|^{2a}}+\frac{(a+b)(\Gamma(a+b))^{2}t^{1-a-b}}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)(1-a-b)|\alpha|^{a+b}}\right)&\text{ if }a\in(1/3,1/2),\penalty\ a+b<1.\end{array}\right.

7.2
Examples with infinite splitting rate

When
\nu
is infinite we illustrate our results with self-similar fragmentations related to three families of random rooted real trees: (i) the stable Lévy trees of Duquesne, Le Gall and Le Jan, (ii) the scaling limits of the so-called Ford’s alpha model and (iii) of the Aldous beta-splitting model. (These families all contain as a fundamental example the Brownian tree of Aldous.) It turns out that these cases are mostly in the framework of Lemma
12
. Other examples of random trees related to self-similar fragmentations, as e.g. the
k
-ary trees studied in
[
25
]
can be treated with similar calculations. We leave the details to the interested reader.

In fact, any fragmentation process with a negative index of self-similarity is associated to a random compact rooted real tree, equipped with a probability measure, that describes its genealogies
[
21
]
. The extinction time
\zeta
of the process can then be interpreted as the height (the maximal distance to the root) of the associated real tree and the extinction time of a tagged fragment,
\zeta_{\mathrm{tag}}
, as the height of a typical leaf (chosen according to the probability measure on the tree). As studied in
[
23
,
22
]
, these self-similar fragmentation trees are the scaling limits (in distribution, with respect to the Gromov-Hausdorff-Prokhorov topology) of sequences of discrete trees, satisfying a so-called
Markov-Branching property
. In particular the rescaled heights of these discrete trees converge to the extinction time of the corresponding self-similar fragmentation, for which we have now precise information on the tail.

Ex.4. Stable fragmentations and height of stable Lévy trees.
For
\gamma\in(1,2]
, we let
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}
denote a stable Lévy tree with branching mechanism
x\mapsto x^{\gamma}
. This tree is a compact rooted real tree that describes the genealogy of a continuous-state branching process with branching mechanism
x\mapsto x^{\gamma}

[
29
,
14
]
and arises as the scaling limit of Galton-Watson trees conditioned on having
n
vertices and with an offspring distribution in the domain of attraction of a
\gamma
-stable distribution
[
13
]
. It is naturally equipped with a probability measure
\mu_{\gamma}
which is the limit of the uniform measure on the vertices of the conditioned Galton-Watson trees. In particular, the tree
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is a multiple of the Brownian tree of Aldous.

Bertoin
[
4
]
for the Brownian tree and then Miermont
[
32
]
for the whole family of stable Lévy trees shew that one gets a self-similar fragmentation process by considering the
\mu_{\gamma}
-masses of the connected components of the set of points of
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}
having a height greater than
t
,
t\geq 0
. Its index of self-similarity is
\gamma^{-1}-1
and the function
\phi
associated to its dislocation measure is given by

\phi(x)=\gamma\frac{\Gamma(x+1-\frac{1}{\gamma})}{\Gamma(x)}=\gamma x^{1-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(1-\frac{(1-\frac{1}{\gamma})\frac{1}{\gamma}}{2x}+O(x^{-2})\right).

Below we denote by
F_{\gamma,1}(t)
the mass of the largest fragment of such a fragmentation at time
t
, by
F_{\gamma,\mathrm{tag}}(t)
the mass of a tagged fragment, by

\zeta_{\gamma}
the height of
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}
and by
\zeta_{\gamma,\mathrm{tag}}
the height of a typical leaf of
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}
,
\gamma\in(1,2]
. With our notation of Section
2
,
\zeta_{2}
is then distributed as
\sqrt{2}\penalty\ \zeta_{\mathrm{Br}}
.

Then, by Lemma
12
, we obtain:

Corollary 14
.

For all
\gamma\in(1,2]
,

\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\gamma}>t)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\exp\big(-(\gamma-1)^{\gamma-1}t^{\gamma}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\gamma,\mathrm{frag}}>t)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\exp\big(-(\gamma-1)^{\gamma-1}t^{\gamma}\big).

Moreover for all
a>0

\hskip-14.22636pt\mathbb{E}\left[F^{a}_{\gamma,1}(t)\right]\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{1+\gamma(\frac{1}{2}-a)}\exp\big(-(\gamma-1)^{\gamma-1}t^{\gamma}\big)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\left[F^{a}_{\gamma,\mathrm{tag}}(t)\right].

This estimate of the tail of the height
\zeta_{\gamma}
of the three
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}
is not new: in
[
15
, Theorem 1.5]
, Duquesne and Wang obtain a much more precise result by giving the asymptotic expansion of this tail at all orders. We just wanted to emphasize that we easily get the first order as a consequence of our approach, and moreover some precise information on the decrease of the moments of the largest fragment.

Ex.5. Height of scaling limits of Ford’s trees.
Ford
[
17
]
introduced a family of random cladograms (i.e. rooted binary finite trees) as possible models for phylogenetic trees. It is a family, indexed by a parameter
\emph{\texttt{a}}\in[0,1]
, of sequences of growing trees constructed algorithmically according to a simple rule that only depends on the parameter
a
, that interpolates between the Yule tree (
\emph{\texttt{a}}=0
), the uniform binary tree (
\emph{\texttt{a}}=1/2
) and the comb tree (
\emph{\texttt{a}}=1
). See e.g.
[
11
,
12
,
34
,
37
]
for a few probabilistic references related to these models. For
\emph{\texttt{a}}\in(0,1)

tends to infinity, one observes in the scaling limit a self-similar fragmentation tree (
[
23
, Section 5.2]
) with index of self-similarity
-\emph{\texttt{a}}
and a
binary
dislocation measure (i.e.
\nu(s_{1}+s_{2}<1)=0

\nu(s_{1}\in\mathrm{d}u)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\emph{\texttt{a}})}\left(\emph{\texttt{a}}(u(1-u))^{-\emph{\texttt{a}}-1}+(2-4\emph{\texttt{a}})(u(1-u))^{-\emph{\texttt{a}}}\right),\quad u\in(1/2,1).

Let
\zeta_{\emph{\texttt{a}}}
denote the height of this tree,
\zeta_{\emph{\texttt{a}},\mathrm{tag}}
the height of a typical leaf and
F_{\emph{\texttt{a}}}
the corresponding fragmentation process.
(We emphasize that when
\emph{\texttt{a}}=1/2
this tree corresponds to a multiple of the Brownian tree.) The above definition of
\nu
leads to

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\frac{\emph{\texttt{a}}}{\Gamma(1-\emph{\texttt{a}})}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u^{x})u^{-\emph{\texttt{a}}}(1-u)^{-\emph{\texttt{a}}-1}\mathrm{d}u+\frac{2-4\emph{\texttt{a}}}{\Gamma(1-\emph{\texttt{a}})}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u^{x})u^{1-\emph{\texttt{a}}}(1-u)^{-\emph{\texttt{a}}}\mathrm{d}u

and then, by Lemma
13

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{\emph{\texttt{a}}}\left(1-\left(\frac{3}{2}\emph{\texttt{a}}^{2}-\frac{9}{2}\emph{\texttt{a}}+2\right)x^{-1}+O(x^{-2})\right).

By Lemma
12
, we can therefore conclude that:

Corollary 15
.

For
\texttt{a}\in]0,1[
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\texttt{a}}>t)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{\frac{2\texttt{a}^{2}-7\texttt{a}+4}{2\texttt{a}(1-\texttt{a})}}\exp\big(-\texttt{a}^{\frac{\texttt{a}}{1-\texttt{a}}}(1-\texttt{a})t^{\frac{1}{1-\texttt{a}}}\big)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{\frac{1}{1-\texttt{a}}}\cdot\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\texttt{a},\mathrm{tag}}>t).

Moreover for all
r>0
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\big(F_{\texttt{a},1}(t)\big)^{r}\big]\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{-\frac{r}{1-\texttt{a}}}\cdot t^{\frac{2\texttt{a}^{2}-7\texttt{a}+4}{2\texttt{a}(1-\texttt{a})}}\exp\big(-\texttt{a}^{\frac{\texttt{a}}{1-\texttt{a}}}(1-\texttt{a})t^{\frac{1}{1-\texttt{a}}}\big)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{\frac{1}{1-\texttt{a}}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\big[\big(F_{\texttt{a},\mathrm{tag}}(t)\big)^{r}\big].

Ex.6. Height of
\beta
-splitting trees.
By
\beta
-splitting trees we refer to a family of random rooted real trees

indexed by a parameter

that arise as the scaling limits (see
[
23

[

]

\sigma

(\beta+1,\beta+1)

\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
has for index of self-similarity

\nu(s_{1}\in\mathrm{d}u)=\frac{-1}{\Gamma(1+\beta)}(u(1-u))^{\beta},\quad u\in(1/2,1).

The tree
\mathcal{T}_{-3/2}
is a multiple of the Brownian tree.
By Lemma
13
we notice that

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{\Gamma(1+\beta)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u^{x})u^{\beta+1}(1-u)^{\beta}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{-\beta-1}\left(1-\frac{\Gamma(\beta+2)}{\Gamma(2\beta+3)}x^{\beta+1}-\frac{(\beta+1)(3\beta+4)}{2}x^{-1}+O(x^{-2})\right).

Then by Lemma

denotes the height of the tree,
\zeta_{\beta,\mathrm{tag}}
the height of a typical leaf, and

Corollary 16

For

\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\beta}>t)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{\frac{-2\beta-1}{2(\beta+2)}}\exp\Big(-a_{\beta}t^{\frac{1}{\beta+2}}+b_{\beta}t\Big)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{\frac{1}{\beta+2}}\cdot\mathbb{P}(\zeta_{\beta,\mathrm{tag}}>t)

\mathbb{E}\left[F^{a}_{\beta,1}(t)\right]\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{-\frac{a}{2+\beta}}t^{\frac{-2\beta-1}{2(\beta+2)}}\exp\Big(-a_{\beta}t^{\frac{1}{\beta+2}}+b_{\beta}t\Big)\penalty\ \asymp\penalty\ t^{\frac{1}{2+\beta}}\mathbb{E}\left[F^{a}_{\beta,\mathrm{tag}}(t)\right].

Note that the term

Variational problem on a metric-affine almost product manifold
Vladimir Rovenski, Tomasz Zawadzki

Abstract
We study a variational problem on a smooth manifold with a decomposition of the tangent bundle into k > 2 k>2 subbundles (distributions), namely, we consider the integrated sum of their mixed scalar curvatures as a functional of adapted pseudo-Riemannian metric (keeping the pairwise orthogonality of the distributions) and contorsion tensor, defining a linear connection. This functional allows us to generalize the class of Einstein metrics in the following sense: if all of the distributions are one-dimensional, then it coincides with the geometrical part of the Einstein-Hilbert action restricted to adapted metrics. We prove that metrics in pairs metric-contorsion critical for our functional make all of the distributions totally umbilical. We obtain examples and obstructions to existence of those critical pairs in some special cases: twisted products with statistical connections; semi-symmetric connections and 3-Sasaki manifolds with metric-compatible connections. Keywords Distribution; mixed scalar curvature; variation; statistical connection; semi-symmetric connection; 3-Sasaki manifold Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C15; 57R25

Introduction

State of the art
.
This paper links together two topics of differential geometry:
variational problems for metric and linear connection and almost product manifolds with
k\geq 2
factors.

Many canonical geometrical objects
are critical points of nonlinear variational problems, see
[
9
]
. A particularly famous of them is
the integrated scalar curvature (the geometrical part of Einstein-Hilbert action) with variable Riemannian metric and linear connection,
its Euler-Lagrange equations are the Einstein equation and the spin-connection equation,
e.g.,
[
3
]
.
Both equations form the basis of the Einstein-Cartan theory of gravity within the framework of metric-affine geometry,
that considers a pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
and a linear connection
\bar{\nabla}

(instead of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
) on a manifold as independent variables, e.g.,
[
6
,
17
]
.
The following classes of metric-affine manifolds are popular:

\bullet
Statistical manifolds, where the (0,3)-tensor
\bar{\nabla}g
is symmetric and
\bar{\nabla}
is torsion-free,
are important for probability and statistics as well as information geometry, e.g.,
[
4
]
.

\bullet
Riemann-Cartan manifolds, where
\bar{\nabla}
is metric-compatible, i.e.,
\bar{\nabla}g=0

are important for theoretical physics, e.g.,
[
3
]
.
Semi-symmetric connection, introduced by K. Yano
[
30
]
, is a special case of a metric-compatible connection parameterized by a vector field.

In
[
22
]
, the first author studied the variation problem with mixed scalar curvature

(1)

on a manifold with

,
25
,

This analog of scalar curvature is the averaged sectional curvature of all planes spanned by two unit vectors from different distributions,
and is one of the simplest curvature invariants of an almost product manifold,

[

,
23
,
27
,
29
]
.
Recall that a connected

with a decomposition of the tangent bundle into

subbundles (distributions),

is called an

(see

It appears in such topics as

An almost product manifold

A pseudo-Riemannian metric

Objectives and results
.
We
study critical points of the integrated mixed scalar curvature on
(M,{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
, depending on adapted metric
g

and contorsion tensor
\mathfrak{T}=\bar{\nabla}-\nabla
,

\bar{J}_{\cal D}:(g,\mathfrak{T})\mapsto\int_{M}\overline{\rm S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}}\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\,.

(3)

The mixed scalar curvature
\overline{\rm S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}}
is up to factor 2, see (
13
), the sum of the mixed scalar curvatures

\overline{\rm S}_{\,{\cal D}_{\mu},{\cal D}^{\bot}_{\mu}}
, examined in
[
26
]
, and
if all distributions are 1-dimensional, the mixed scalar curvature reduces to the scalar curvature divided by two. If
M
is a non-compact manifold, we integrate in (
3
) over an arbitrarily large, relatively compact domain
\Omega\subset M
, which contains supports of variations of
g
and
\mathfrak{T}
.
We find the Euler-Lagrange equation for (
3
) with fixed
\mathfrak{T}
for adapted variations of metric preserving the volume of the manifold. It can be presented in the form of the Einstein equation:

\overline{\mathcal{R}ic}_{\,{\cal D}}-(1/2)\,\overline{\cal S}_{{\cal D}}\cdot g+\lambda\,g=0,

(4)

where the Ricci tensor of
\bar{\nabla}
and the scalar curvature are replaced by the Ricci type tensor
\overline{\mathcal{R}ic}_{\,{\cal D}}=\bigoplus\nolimits_{\mu=1}^{k}\overline{\mathcal{R}ic}_{\,{\cal D}\,|\,{\cal D}_{\mu}\times{\cal D}_{\mu}}

(introduced in
[
5
]
for
k=2

\dim M=4
,
\dim{\cal D}_{1}=1

and
\bar{\nabla}=\nabla
)
and its trace
\overline{\cal S}_{{\cal D}}
.
Although
\overline{\mathcal{R}ic}_{\,{\cal D}}
has complicated form even for
k=2
, see
[
26
]
,
we write it explicitly in special cases: for statistical and semi-symmetric connections, and twisted products; if all distributions are one-dimensional, it reduces to the Ricci tensor of
{\bar{\nabla}}
.

We find the Euler-Lagrange equation for (
3
) with a fixed adapted metric for variations of
\mathfrak{T}
,
which can be decomposed into independent equations – some of which do not contain contorsion tensor, but significantly restrict metrics admitting critical contorsions.
Due to such restrictions, a natural setting to consider are twisted and warped products of manifolds, on which we characterize all critical pairs with statistical connections, in terms of contorsion tensor, mean curvatures of distributions and Ricci tensor of the metric.
We also prove the absence of non-trivial critical points of the action on e.g., harmonic distributions with semi-symmetric connections,
or
complete 3-Sasaki manifolds with particular metric connections.
On the other hand,
considering only connections from certain families allows to reduce the variational problem to purely pseudo-Riemannian one.
In particular, we show that some critical pairs
(g,\mathfrak{T})
of the action (
3
) restricted to adapted metrics and statistical connections can be obtained from critical adapted metrics of this action
with the fixed Levi-Civita connection.
Similarly, considering critical points among semi-symmetric connections only slightly modifies the tensor
\overline{\mathcal{R}ic}_{\,{\cal D}}
.

The action (
3
)
may find applications in the theory of nets and theoretical physics, because it
is strongly related to the Einstein-Hilbert action.
Indeed,
(
3
)
and the relation

\overline{\rm S}=2\,\overline{\rm S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}}+\sum\nolimits_{\,\mu=1}^{\,k}\overline{\rm S}({{\cal D}_{\mu}})

of
\overline{\rm S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}}
with the scalar curvature
\overline{\rm S}

(the trace of the Ricci tensor for
\bar{\nabla}
)
and the scalar curvature
\overline{\rm S}({{\cal D}_{\mu}})
of
{\cal D}_{\mu}
,
allow us to generalize
the class of Einstein metrics, i.e.,
in the case of one-dimensional distributions any critical pair
(g,\mathfrak{T})
for the Einstein-Hilbert action is also critical for (
3
).
However, since we consider variations of metric adapted to an almost-product structure, we obtain also critical pairs
(g,\mathfrak{T})
with non-Einstein metrics.
In particular, on manifolds
(M,g)
of constant scalar curvature and certain dimension,
we find decompositions (
2
) that make
g
critical for the action (
3
).
Finally, (
3
) can be combined with the Einstein-Hilbert action in vacuum as

\bar{J}_{{\cal D},\epsilon}:(g,\mathfrak{T})\mapsto\int_{M}(\overline{\rm S}+\epsilon\,\overline{\rm S}_{\,{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k}})\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\ (\epsilon\in\mathbb{R})
,
and considered as a perturbation of the Einstein-Cartan theory.

Preliminaries

Here, we recall the properties of the mixed scalar curvature of a metric-affine almost product manifold
(M,g,\bar{\nabla};{\cal D}_{1},\ldots,{\cal D}_{k})
,
see
[
23
]
.
We will use bar in the notation of objects related to
\bar{\nabla}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{T}=\bar{\nabla}-\nabla
, where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
, is the
contorsion tensor
.

A pseudo-Riemannian metric
g=\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
of index
q
on
M
is an element
g\in{\rm Sym}^{2}(M)

of the space of symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors
such that each
g_{x}\ (x\in M)
is a non-degenerate bilinear form of index
q
on the tangent space
T_{x}M
.
For
q=0
(i.e.,
g_{x}
is positive definite)
g
is a Riemannian metric and for
q=1
it is a Lorentz metric.
A distribution
{\cal D}_{\mu}
on
(M,g)
is
non-degenerate
, if
g_{x}
is non-degenerate on
{\cal D}_{\mu}(x)\subset T_{x}M
for all
x\in M
;
in this case, the orthogonal complement
{\cal D}_{\mu}^{\bot}
is also non-degenerate, e.g.,
[
6
]
.
Given an adapted metric
g
on
(M;{\cal D}_{1},\ldots{\cal D}_{k})
, there is a local orthonormal frame

\{E_{\mu,a}\}
on
M
, where
1\leq a\leq n_{\mu}=\dim{\cal D}_{\mu}
such that

\{E_{\mu,1},\ldots,E_{\mu,n_{\mu}}\}\subset{\cal D}_{\mu}
for
1\leq\mu\leq k
.
All quantities defined below using such frame do not depend on the choice of this frame.
Similarly,
\{{\cal E}_{\mu,i}\}
, where
i=1,\ldots,n^{\bot}_{\mu}
, is an orthonormal frame of
{\cal D}_{\mu}^{\perp}
with
n^{\bot}_{\mu}=\dim{\cal D}_{\mu}^{\perp}
.
Thus, the ranges of indices, e.g.,
a
or
i
, are determined by the index of the distribution,
{\cal D}_{\mu}
or
{\cal D}^{\bot}_{\mu}
, respectively.
Unless explicitly stated otherwise, sums in all formulas will be taken over repeated indices, and always over full ranges of indices.

For the curvature tensor
\bar{R}_{X,Y}=[\bar{\nabla}_{Y},\bar{\nabla}_{X}]+\bar{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}
of
\bar{\nabla}
, we get

\bar{R}_{X,Y}-R_{X,Y}=(\nabla_{Y}\,\mathfrak{T})_{X}-(\nabla_{X}\,\mathfrak{T})_{Y}+[\mathfrak{T}_{Y},\,\mathfrak{T}_{X}]
,
see
[
21
]
,
where
R_{X,Y}=[\nabla_{Y},\nabla_{X}]+\nabla_{[X,Y]}
is the curvature tensor of
\nabla
.
The mixed scalar curvature of a pair of distributions
({\cal D},{\cal D}^{\bot})
on a
manifold
(M,g;\bar{\nabla})
is given by

\bar{\rm S}_{\,{\cal D},{\cal D}^{\bot}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\nolimits_{\,a,b}\varepsilon_{a}\,\varepsilon_{b}(\langle\bar{R}_{\,{E}_{a},{\cal E}_{b}}{E}_{a},{\cal E}_{b}\rangle+\langle\bar{R}_{\,{\cal E}_{b},{E}_{a}}{\cal E}_{b},{E}_{a}\rangle)\,,

where we use a local orthonormal frame on
M
such that
{E}_{a}\in{\cal D}
for
a\leq\dim{\cal D}
,
{\cal E}_{b}\in{\cal D}^{\perp}
for
b\leq\dim{\cal D}^{\perp}

and
\varepsilon_{a}=\langle{E}_{a},{E}_{a}\rangle\in\{-1,1\}
,
\varepsilon_{b}=\langle{\cal E}_{b},{\cal E}_{b}\rangle\in\{-1,1\}
.
If
{\cal D}
is spanned by a unit vector field

then
\bar{\rm S}_{\,{\cal D},{\cal D}^{\bot}}=\varepsilon_{N}\overline{\rm Ric}_{N,N}

\overline{\rm Ric}_{N,N}

\bar{\nabla}

This concept can be generalized to

Given

The symmetric second fundamental form

The mean curvature vector field of
{\cal D}_{\mu}
is given by the trace of second fundamental form:
H_{\mu}=\operatorname{Tr}_{g}\,h_{\mu}=\sum_{\,a}h_{\mu}(E_{\mu,a},E_{\mu,a})
.

\tilde{h}_{\,\mu},\,\tilde{H}_{\,\mu}=\operatorname{Tr}_{g}\tilde{h}_{\,\mu}

{\tilde{H}}_{\mu}=\sum\nolimits_{\,\nu\neq\mu}P_{\mu}H_{\nu}

{\cal H}=\sum\nolimits_{\mu}H_{\mu}=\sum\nolimits_{\mu}{\tilde{H}}_{\mu}.

(6)

To see that the above definition is valid, we use

to obtain

\sum\nolimits_{\,\mu}{\tilde{H}}_{\mu}=\sum\nolimits_{\,\mu}P_{\mu}\sum\nolimits_{\,\nu}H_{\nu}=\sum\nolimits_{\,\nu}\sum\nolimits_{\,\mu}P_{\mu}H_{\nu}=\sum\nolimits_{\,\nu}H_{\nu}\,.

(7)

A distribution
{\cal D}_{\mu}
is called integrable if

, and

is called totally umbilical, harmonic, or totally geodesic,

Totally umbilical and totally geodesic integrable distributions naturally appear on twisted products.

\displaystyle\langle h_{\mu},h_{\mu}\rangle=\sum\nolimits_{\,a,b}\varepsilon_{a}\varepsilon_{b}\,\langle h_{\mu}({E}_{\mu,a},{E}_{\mu,b}),h_{\mu}({E}_{\mu,a},{E}_{\mu,b})\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu},T_{\mu}\rangle=\sum\nolimits_{\,a,b}\varepsilon_{a}\varepsilon_{b}\,\langle T_{\mu}({E}_{\mu,a},{E}_{\mu,b}),T_{\mu}({E}_{\mu,a},{E}_{\mu,b})\rangle,\quad{\rm etc}.

5
Extension of the class of Einstein metrics

In this section we assume that all distributions in (
2
) are one-dimensional, then (
3
) is (up to constant factor

\bar{J}:(g,\mathfrak{T})\mapsto\int_{M}\overline{\rm S}\ {\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\,,

(40)

is the scalar curvature of
{\bar{\nabla}}=\nabla+\mathfrak{T}
on
(M,g)

In particular, we obtain critical pairs

for (

and for

using Hadamard matrices.
There is a rich literature on geometric constructions of metrics of constant scalar curvature, which we will not discuss.

Proposition 4

Let a pair
(g,\mathfrak{T})

)

For one-dimensional distributions

we have

.
Hence, if

is critical for the action (

),
then it is also critical for the action (
3
) with respect to compactly supported adapted variations.

(
16
) and (

).
Notice that while the equations (

∎

Example 2
.

The product of a surface of constant curvature
K\neq 0
and a real line or a circle is a homogeneous space
(M^{3},g)
of scalar curvature
2K
.
Let
\partial_{x},\partial_{y},\partial_{t}
be an adapted orthonormal frame on
(M,g)
. Then
{\rm Ric}_{xx}={\rm Ric}_{yy}=K
and
{\rm Ric}_{tt}=0
.
For
\partial_{1}=\cos\alpha\,\partial_{t}+\sin\alpha\,\partial_{x}
we find
{\rm Ric}_{11}(\alpha)=K\sin^{2}\alpha
.
Thus,
{\rm Ric}_{11}(\alpha)=2K/3
for
\alpha=\arccos(1/\sqrt{3})
.
Set
\partial_{2}=-a\,\partial_{t}+b\,\partial_{x}+c\,\partial_{y}
and
\partial_{3}=-a\,\partial_{t}+b\,\partial_{x}-c\,\partial_{y}
for positive numbers
a,b,c
.
From
1=\langle\partial_{2},\partial_{2}\rangle=\langle\partial_{3},\partial_{3}\rangle
, we find
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=1
.
Equating
{\rm Ric}_{22}={\rm Ric}_{33}=K(b^{2}+c^{2})
to
2K/3
and using
b^{2}+c^{2}=1-a^{2}
, we obtain
a=1/\sqrt{3}
.
Then, from
0=\langle\partial_{1},\partial_{2}\rangle=\langle\partial_{1},\partial_{3}\rangle
we find
b=a\cos\alpha/\sin\alpha=1/\sqrt{6}
.
Thus,
c=1/\sqrt{2}
.
Condition (
42
) is then valid on
(M^{3},g)
for three distributions spanned by
\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3}
.

Example 3

Let
(M,g)
be a 4-dimensional Riemannian manifold of constant scalar curvature
c

Lemma 3

Let

We can find the above decomposition satisfying (

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{\,j,m}a_{ij}r_{jm}a_{im}=\lambda,\quad i=1,\ldots,k\,,

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{\,j}a_{ij}a_{mj}=\delta_{im},\quad i,m=1,\ldots k\,.

Here

r_{2}\leq r_{3}<r_{1}\ \ {\rm or}\ \ r_{2}<r_{3}\leq r_{1}\,,

Let

\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha&0\\

\end{array}\right),\qquad A_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\

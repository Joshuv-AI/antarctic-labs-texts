Legendre transforms for type A n A_{n} and B n B_{n} ∨ \vee -systems
Misha Feigin, Leo Kaminski, Ian A.B. Strachan

Abstract
The Witten–Dijkgraaf–Verlinde–Verlinde (WDVV) equations have a rich structure related to the theory of Frobenius manifolds, with many known families of solutions. A Legendre transformation is a symmetry of the WDVV equations, introduced by Dubrovin. We explicitly compute the results of a Legendre transformation applied to A n A_{n} - and B n B_{n} -type multi-parameter rational solutions, relating them to known and new trigonometric solutions.



1
Introduction

The generalised WDVV equations are the following system of nonlinear partial differential equations:

F_{i}\eta^{-1}F_{j}=F_{j}\eta^{-1}F_{i},

(1.1)

where
F=F(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})
and
F_{i}
is the
n\times n
matrix of third-order derivatives of the function
F
, with
\left(r,s\right)^{th}
entry

F_{irs}:=\frac{\partial^{3}F}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{r}\partial x^{s}}.

The
n\times n
matrix
\eta=\left(\eta_{\alpha\beta}\right)
is a linear combination

\eta=\sum^{n}_{k=1}q_{k}F_{k},

which is assumed to be constant and non-degenerate. In the Frobenius manifold case, we set
q_{k}=\delta_{1k}
where 1 denotes the identity direction
[
4
]
; in general,
q_{k}=q_{k}\left(x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\right)
.
While
\eta
is the matrix of a non-degenerate complex bilinear form, we refer to it as a metric. It can be used to lower indices for the coordinates: that is, we define

x_{\alpha}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}x^{\beta},

where we assume summation over repeated indices.

A symmetry of the WDVV equations is a map

\displaystyle x^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\mapsto\hat{x}^{\alpha},

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\mapsto\hat{\eta}_{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\mapsto\widehat{F}=\widehat{F}\left(\hat{x}^{1},\dots,\hat{x}^{n}\right),

such that the WDVV equations (
1.1
) are preserved. That is,

\widehat{F}_{i}\hat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{F}_{j}=\widehat{F}_{j}\hat{\eta}^{-1}\widehat{F}_{i}

(1.2)

provided that
F
satisfies (
1.1
).
The
(r,s)^{th}
entry of the
n\times n
matrix
\widehat{F}_{i}
is

\widehat{F}_{irs}:=\frac{\partial^{3}\widehat{F}}{\partial\hat{x}^{i}\partial\hat{x}^{r}\partial\hat{x}^{s}}.

We will use the following notation for partial derivatives:

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha}},\;\hat{\partial}_{\alpha}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle F_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}F=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial x^{\alpha}\partial x^{\beta}},

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle:=\hat{\partial}_{\alpha}\hat{\partial}_{\beta}\widehat{F}=\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{F}}{\partial\hat{x}^{\alpha}\partial\hat{x}^{\beta}}.

Note that adding quadratic terms to functions
F
and
\widehat{F}
does not affect the WDVV equations.

An important example of a symmetry was defined by Dubrovin.

Definition 1.1
.

[
4
]

The Legendre transformation
S_{\gamma}
is defined as follows for a choice of
\gamma\in\{1,\dots,n\}
:

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\gamma}F(x),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{\alpha\beta}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\eta_{\alpha\beta}(x),

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=F_{\alpha\beta}(x),

where
\hat{x}_{\alpha}=\hat{\eta}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{x}^{\beta}
.

A consequence of this definition is that the components of the metric
\hat{\eta}
can be written as

\hat{\eta}_{\alpha\beta}\left(\hat{x}\right)=\eta_{\alpha\beta}(x)=\sum_{k}q_{k}\partial_{k}F_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{k,l}q_{k}\frac{\partial\hat{x}^{l}}{\partial x^{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}^{l}}\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{l}\hat{q}_{l}\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta l},

for
\hat{q}_{l}=\sum_{k}q_{k}\frac{\partial\hat{x}^{l}}{\partial x^{k}}
.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
4
]
,
[
17
]
.
The Legendre transformed prepotential
\widehat{F}=S_{\gamma}\left(F\right)
and metric
\hat{\eta}
, given by formulas in Definition
1.1
, satisfy the WDVV equations (
1.2
) provided that
F
satisfies the WDVV equations (
1.1
).

We denote
\widehat{F}=S_{\gamma}\left(F\right)
, although
\widehat{F}
depends on both
F
and
\eta
.
From the computational point of view, difficulties in finding
\widehat{F}
arise when inverting expressions of the form
\hat{x}^{\alpha}(x)
, obtained from (
1.3
). In general, it is not always possible to carry out this inversion.

We are interested in the Legendre transformations of rational solutions of the WDVV equations. Such solutions are defined by a collection of vectors known as
\vee
-systems introduced by Veselov
[
18
]
, which extend the class of Coxeter root systems and can be characterised by geometric properties.
In the Coxeter case, these solutions appear as almost dual prepotentials for the orbit space Frobenius manifolds
\mathbb{C}^{n}/W
, for a finite Coxeter group
W

[
5
]
.
Chalykh and Veselov found rational
\vee
-systems which are multi-parameter deformations of root systems
A_{n}
and
B_{n}

[
3
]
; while such deformations produce solutions to the WDVV equations, they cannot in general be related to a Frobenius manifold by almost duality. We find that Legendre transformations of these two families of rational solutions produce trigonometric solutions of the WDVV equations.

Trigonometric solutions have been constructed as almost dual Frobenius manifold prepotentials on the orbit spaces of extended affine Weyl groups - see
[
14
]
for type
A_{n}
, and were obtained via the (equivariant) Gromov-Witten generating functions for resolutions of
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G

[
2
]
.
Rational and trigonometric solutions also appear in
N=2
supersymmetric gauge theories; Marshakov, Mironov and Morosov showed that perturbative parts of some prepotentials arising in Seiberg-Witten field theory are of this type
[
11
]
, and Hoevenaars and Martini studied trigonometric solutions related to classical root systems
[
10
]
.
There exist multi-parameter families of trigonometric solutions related to deformations of classical root systems
A_{n}
and
BC_{n}
, which were found by Pavlov
[
12
]
, generalised by Alkadhem and Feigin
[
1
]
, and linked to flat connections by Shen
[
15
]
in type
A_{n}
; see also Riley
[
13
]
for other multi-parameter
A_{n}
-type solutions. A large class of trigonometric solutions was considered by Feigin in
[
6
]
.

Consider the following simple example, a rational solution which corresponds to the Weyl group
A_{2}
:

F=\left(x^{1}\right)^{2}\log{x^{1}}+\left(x^{2}\right)^{2}\log{x^{2}}+\left(x^{1}-x^{2}\right)^{2}\log\left(x^{1}-x^{2}\right).

(1.4)

Here, the metric is given by

\eta=x^{1}F_{1}+x^{2}F_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}4&-2\\
-2&4\end{pmatrix}.

Applying the Legendre transformation
S_{1}
to
F
produces the solution

\widehat{F}=S_{1}(F)=\frac{2}{3}\left(\hat{x}^{1}\right)^{3}+\left(\hat{x}^{1}\right)^{2}\hat{x}^{2}+2\,\hat{x}^{1}\left(\hat{x}^{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(\hat{x}^{2}\right)^{3}-\frac{2}{9}\Li_{3}\left(e^{-3\hat{x}^{2}}\right),

(1.5)

where
\Li_{3}
is the trilogarithm function.
Solution (
1.5
) is an example of a trigonometric solution of the WDVV equations which lies outside of the class considered in
[
6
]
.

An early example of rational solutions being mapped to trigonometric solutions by a Legendre transformation is given by Riley and Strachan
[
14
]
. Here, the rational solutions are almost dual to Hurwitz space Frobenius manifolds of rational functions on the sphere with a single finite pole; this pole provides a natural choice of Legendre direction.
A type
B
version of this construction is discussed by Stedman and Strachan in
[
16
]
at the level of superpotentials. Further examples of Legendre transformations are given in
[
19
]
, where it was shown that monodromy data is the same for a pair of Frobenius manifolds related by a Legendre transformation.

Note that the solution in (
1.4
) does not have a distinguished direction associated with it, unlike the settings considered in
[
14
]
,
[
16
]
. Thus in this example we have chosen the coordinate direction
x^{1}
as the Legendre direction. In the general case, discussed in Sections
2
and
3
, we find the results of Legendre transformations in an arbitrary coordinate direction,
x^{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, applied to multi-parameter
A_{n}
- and
B_{n}
-type rational
\vee
-systems in
n
dimensions. For both of these families, we find that the solution produced by such a Legendre transformation is trigonometric; in the
B_{n}
-type case, we show that the Legendre-transformed solution coincides with the family of
BC_{n}
-type trigonometric solutions found in
[
1
]
,
[
12
]
.

In the case of
A_{n}
-type trigonometric solutions, we generalise known solutions from Hoevenaars and Martini
[
10
]
to a multi-parameter family of solutions. The initial solution (which appears only in the arXiv preprint version of
[
10
]
) was also considered by Shen
[
15
]
, where it was presented in a different but equivalent form. The multi-parameter family which we obtain also generalises the multi-parameter solutions from
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
1
]
. We find that this new larger class of solutions contains Legendre transformations of the general
A_{n}
-type rational solutions.

The layout of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we compute the general formula for the Legendre transformation of the
A_{n}
-type multi-parameter rational solutions. In Section
3
, we do the same for the
B_{n}
-type rational solutions. Section
4

-type solutions. Section
5
explains the relations between the Legendre transformations of
A_{n}
- and
B_{n}
-type rational solutions, and the respective families of trigonometric solutions.

2
Legendre transform of
A_{n}
-type solutions

The family of rational
A_{n}
-type WDVV solutions corresponding to multi-parameter
A_{n}
-type
\vee
-systems
[
3
]
is given by

F=F^{\textrm{rat}}_{A_{n}(k)}=\sum^{n}_{i=1}k_{i}\left(x^{i}\right)^{2}\log\left(x^{i}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}k_{i}k_{j}\left(x^{i}-x^{j}\right)^{2}\log\left(x^{i}-x^{j}\right),

(2.1)

with parameters
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
K:=\sum^{n}_{i=1}k_{i}\neq-1.

For the generalised WDVV equations (
1.1
), it is natural to choose
\eta
as follows
[
7
]
:

\eta_{\alpha\beta}=\sum^{n}_{i=1}x^{i}F_{i\alpha\beta}.

(2.2)

This choice of
\eta
is a flat metric with the flat coordinate system
x^{\alpha}
. It has constant entries

\eta_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{cases}2k_{\alpha}(K-k_{\alpha}+1)&\textrm{if }\alpha=\beta,\\
-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then the inverse metric,
\eta^{-1}
, has entries given by

\eta^{\alpha\beta}=\begin{cases}\frac{k_{\alpha}+1}{2k_{\alpha}(K+1)}&\textrm{ if }\alpha=\beta,\\
\frac{1}{2(K+1)}&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.3)

We now compute the result of applying a Legendre transformation
S_{\gamma}
to
F
for any
\gamma\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Using Definition
1.1
, we set
\widehat{F}=S_{\gamma}\left(F\right)
.

Note that since the WDVV equations are unaffected by the addition of quadratic or lower-order parts to a function
F
, we may disregard such terms during calculations.
By definition, the new coordinates
\hat{x}_{\alpha}
are found using second-order derivatives of
F
. For simplicity, constant terms that appear from these calculations can be absorbed with the addition of appropriate quadratic terms to
F
, and the flat coordinates
\hat{x}_{\alpha}
redefined. Since the addition of such terms to
F
is equivalent to a coordinate translation of the resulting
\widehat{F}
, we will find and discuss
\widehat{F}
up to such transformations.
We will use the notation
A\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}B
to denote the equality of
A
and
B
up to constant terms.

Under the Legendre transformation
S_{\gamma}
, the new flat coordinates
\hat{x}_{\alpha}
can be chosen (after constant coordinate shifts) as follows:

\hat{x}_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}2k_{\gamma}\log(x^{\gamma})+2k_{\gamma}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}k_{i}\log\left(x^{\gamma}-x^{i}\right)&\textrm{if }\alpha=\gamma,\\
-2k_{\gamma}k_{\alpha}\log\left(x^{\gamma}-x^{\alpha}\right)&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.4)

The original flat coordinates
x^{\alpha}
now must be rewritten in terms of the new contravariant coordinates
\hat{x}^{\alpha}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The flat coordinates
x^{\alpha}
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle x^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left(1-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}}\right)x^{\gamma}\;\textrm{\emph{for} }\alpha\neq\gamma,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}.

(2.6)

Proof.

The new contravariant coordinates
\hat{x}^{\alpha}
can be found by using the inverse metric
\eta^{-1}
(
2.3
) to raise the indices of the covariant coordinates in (
2.4
). Since the Legendre transformation does not affect the metric, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha\beta}=\hat{\eta}_{\alpha\beta},

which implies that

\displaystyle\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\hat{\eta}^{\alpha\beta}\hat{x}_{\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\hat{x}_{\beta}.

The contravariant coordinates are therefore

\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\frac{k_{\gamma}+1}{K+1}\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)+\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}k_{i}\log\left(x^{\gamma}-x^{i}\right)&\textrm{if }\alpha=\gamma,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{k_{\gamma}}{K+1}\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)-\frac{k_{\gamma}}{K+1}\log\left(x^{\gamma}-x^{\alpha}\right)&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.7)

Considering the case when
\alpha\neq\gamma
, we have

\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\frac{k_{\gamma}}{K+1}\log\left(\frac{x^{\gamma}}{x^{\gamma}-x^{\alpha}}\right),

which can be rearranged to expression (
2.5
).
In the case where
\alpha=\gamma
in (
2.7
), we see

\hat{x}^{\gamma}=\frac{k_{\gamma}+1}{K+1}\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)+\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}k_{i}\log\left(x^{\gamma}-x^{i}\right).

Using (
2.5
) to substitute for
x^{i}
in the summation term gives

\displaystyle\hat{x}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{\gamma}+1}{K+1}\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)+\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}k_{i}\left(\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)+\log\left(e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)-\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\frac{k_{i}}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}.

This is easily rearranged to find (
2.6
).
∎

We may now find the second-order derivatives of
\widehat{F}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The second-order derivatives
\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}
can be expressed by the following formulae for all
\alpha,\beta\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Case 1:
\alpha=\gamma
.

\widehat{F}_{\gamma\beta}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}2k_{\beta}(K-k_{\beta}+1)\hat{x}^{\beta}-2k_{\beta}\sum_{i\neq\beta}k_{i}\hat{x}^{i}.

Case 2:
\alpha=\beta\neq\gamma
.

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}2k_{\alpha}(K+1)\hat{x}^{\gamma}+\frac{2k_{\alpha}}{k_{\gamma}}(k_{\alpha}-k_{\gamma})(K+1)\hat{x}^{\alpha}-\frac{2k_{\alpha}^{2}}{k_{\gamma}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\hat{x}^{i}\\
+2k_{\alpha}\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}}\right)+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{i\neq\alpha,\gamma}k_{i}\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\left(\hat{x}^{\alpha}-\hat{x}^{i}\right)}\right).

Case 3:
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
distinct.

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}-\frac{2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}}{k_{\gamma}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\hat{x}^{i}-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}(\hat{x}^{\alpha}-\hat{x}^{\beta})}\right)+\frac{2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}(K+1)}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\beta}.

Proof.

Case 1:
\alpha=\gamma
.

Using previously defined properties of the Legendre transformation
S_{\gamma}
and the coordinate system
\hat{x}
, we can see that

\widehat{F}_{\gamma\beta}=F_{\gamma\beta}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}\hat{x}_{\beta}=\hat{\eta}_{\alpha\beta}\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\hat{x}^{\alpha},

which leads to the statement.

Case 2:
\alpha=\beta\neq\gamma
.

First we calculate the relevant second-order derivatives of
F
:

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}=F_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}2k_{\alpha}\log\left(x^{\alpha}\right)+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{i\neq\alpha}k_{i}\log\left(x^{\alpha}-x^{i}\right).

Using expression (
2.5
) to make substitutions, we find that

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}2k_{\alpha}(K-k_{\alpha}+1)\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)+2k_{\alpha}\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}}\right)\\
-2k_{\alpha}(K+1)\hat{x}^{\alpha}+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{i\neq\alpha,\gamma}k_{i}\log\left(e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}}-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}}\right).

We continue as follows, using expression (
2.6
) to substitute for
\log(x^{\gamma})
:

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}2k_{\alpha}(K-k_{\alpha}+1)\hat{x}^{\gamma}+\frac{2k_{\alpha}}{k_{\gamma}}\big((k_{\alpha}-k_{\gamma})(K+1)-k_{\alpha}^{2}\big)\hat{x}^{\alpha}\\
+2k_{\alpha}\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}}\right)+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{i\neq\alpha,\gamma}k_{i}\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\left(\hat{x}^{\alpha}-\hat{x}^{i}\right)}\right)\\
+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{i\neq\alpha,\gamma}k_{i}\log\left(e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}}\right)+2k_{\alpha}(K-k_{\alpha}+1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}.

With some rearranging, this leads to the required statement.

Case 3:
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
distinct.

Here we again use the formulae in Lemma
2.1
to show that

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=F_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log\left(x^{\alpha}-x^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log(x^{\gamma})-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log\left(e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\beta}}-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{K+1}{k_{\gamma}}(\hat{x}^{\alpha}-\hat{x}^{\beta})}\right)+\frac{2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}(K+1)}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\beta},

which has the required form.
∎

Recall that the polylogarithm
\Li_{n}
is defined as

\Li_{n}(z)=\begin{cases}-\log(1-z)&\textrm{ if $n=1$,}\\
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{n}}&\textrm{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

with derivatives behaving in the following way:

\frac{\D\Li_{n}\left(e^{u}\right)}{\D u}=\Li_{n-1}(e^{u}).

In this and the following sections, we consider WDVV solutions which can be written in terms of the function

f(z)=\frac{1}{6}z^{3}-\frac{1}{4}\Li_{3}\left(e^{-2z}\right),

(2.8)

where
f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)=\coth{z}
.
It is useful to note the second-order derivative

\frac{\text{d}^{2}}{\text{d}z^{2}}\big(8f(\kappa z/2)\big)=\kappa^{3}z+2\kappa^{2}\log\left(1-e^{-\kappa z}\right),

(2.9)

where
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

The Legendre transform
S_{\gamma}
of the function
F=F^{\textrm{rat}}_{A_{n}(k)}
, given by (
2.1
), has the form

\widehat{F}=\frac{\eta_{\gamma\gamma}}{6}(\hat{x}^{\gamma})^{3}+(\hat{x}^{\gamma})^{2}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\frac{\eta_{i\gamma}}{2}\hat{x}^{i}+\hat{x}^{\gamma}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\frac{\eta_{ii}}{2}(\hat{x}^{i})^{2}-\sum_{i<j<l}\frac{2k_{i}k_{j}k_{l}}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\hat{x}^{l}\\
+\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\left(\frac{k_{i}(\eta_{ii}-\eta_{\gamma\gamma})}{12k_{\gamma}}-\frac{\eta_{ii}^{2}}{24k_{i}k_{\gamma}}+\frac{\eta_{i\gamma}}{12}\right)(\hat{x}^{i})^{3}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
i,j\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{k_{j}\eta_{ii}+k_{i}\eta_{ij}}{4k_{\gamma}}(\hat{x}^{i})^{2}\hat{x}^{j}\\
+\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\frac{8k_{\gamma}^{2}k_{i}}{\left(K+1\right)^{2}}f\left(\frac{K+1}{2k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i,j\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{8k_{\gamma}^{2}k_{i}k_{j}}{\left(K+1\right)^{2}}f\left(\frac{K+1}{2k_{\gamma}}(\hat{x}^{i}-\hat{x}^{j})\right)

(2.10)

up to quadratic terms and coordinate shifts.

Proof.

This can be checked by taking (
2.10
) as an ansatz and comparing its second-order derivatives with the expressions given in Lemma
2.2
. In particular, we use (
2.9
) to rewrite the logarithmic terms.
∎

3
Legendre transform of
B_{n}
-type solutions

The rational prepotentials of
B_{n}
-type
[
3
]
are given by

F^{\textrm{rat}}_{B_{n}(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}2k_{i}(k_{0}+k_{i})(x^{i})^{2}\log(x^{i})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}k_{i}k_{j}(x^{i}\pm x^{j})^{2}\log(x^{i}\pm x^{j}),

(3.1)

with parameters
k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and where the sum of the parameters

K:=\sum^{n}_{i=0}k_{i}\neq 0.

(3.2)

The metric for this system,
\eta
as defined in (
2.2
), has entries

\eta_{\alpha\beta}=4k_{\alpha}K\delta_{\alpha\beta}

while the inverse metric,
\eta^{-1}
, has entries given by

\eta^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{4k_{\alpha}K}.

(3.3)

We can now apply the Legendre transformation
S_{\gamma}
, as in Definition
1.1
, to
F=F^{\text{rat}}_{B_{n}(k)}
for an arbitrary
\gamma\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Throughout this subsection, we set
\widehat{F}=S_{\gamma}(F)
.
As discussed previously, solutions to the WDVV equations are only defined up to quadratic terms and so we may omit, for example, constant terms arising from second-order derivatives of such solutions.
Using the definition of a Legendre transformation, we may choose the set of new (covariant) coordinates
\hat{x}_{\alpha}
as follows

\hskip-5.0pt\hat{x}_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}4k_{\gamma}(k_{\gamma}+k_{0})\log(x^{\gamma})+2k_{\gamma}\displaystyle{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}}k_{i}\log\left((x^{\gamma})^{2}-(x^{i})^{2}\right)&\textrm{if }\alpha=\gamma,\\
2k_{\gamma}k_{i}\log\left(\frac{x^{\gamma}+x^{i}}{x^{\gamma}-x^{i}}\right)&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.4)

To continue applying the transformation, we now must find expressions for coordinates
x^{\alpha}
in terms of
\hat{x}^{\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The flat coordinates
x^{\alpha}
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle x^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=x^{\gamma}\coth\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}\right)\textrm{ \emph{for} }\alpha\neq\gamma,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\log(x^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\hat{x}^{\gamma}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{k_{i}}{2K}\log\left(1-\coth^{2}\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\right)\right).

(3.6)

Proof.

First, we use the inverse metric as in (
3.3
) to raise the indices of the covariant coordinates
\hat{x}_{\alpha}
in (
3.4
) via the formula

\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\hat{\eta}^{\alpha\beta}\hat{x}_{\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\hat{x}_{\beta}.

We obtain

\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\frac{k_{0}+k_{\gamma}}{K}\log(x^{\gamma})+\sum_{i\neq 0,\gamma}\frac{k_{i}}{2K}\log\left(\left(x^{\gamma}\right)^{2}-(x^{i})^{2}\right)&\textrm{if }\alpha=\gamma,\\[5.0pt]
\frac{k_{\gamma}}{2K}\log\left(\frac{x^{\gamma}+x^{\alpha}}{x^{\gamma}-x^{\alpha}}\right)&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.7)

In the case where
\alpha\neq\gamma
, we can invert the expression for
\hat{x}^{\alpha}
to obtain

e^{\frac{2K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}}=\frac{x^{\gamma}+x^{\alpha}}{x^{\gamma}-x^{\alpha}},

which can be rearranged to find

x^{\alpha}=x^{\gamma}\left(\frac{e^{2K\hat{x}^{\alpha}/k_{\gamma}}-1}{e^{2K\hat{x}^{\alpha}/k_{\gamma}}+1}\right)=x^{\gamma}\coth\left(K\hat{x}^{\alpha}/k_{\gamma}\right).

In the case where
\alpha=\gamma
in (
3.7
), we can use expression (
3.5
) to substitute for
x^{i}
with
i\neq\gamma
as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{x}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{0}+k_{\gamma}}{K}\log(x^{\gamma})+\sum_{i\neq 0,\gamma}\frac{k_{i}}{2K}\log\big((x^{\gamma})^{2}(1-\coth^{2}\left(K\hat{x}^{i}/k_{\gamma}\right)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\left(k_{0}+k_{\gamma}+\sum_{i\neq 0,\gamma}k_{i}\right)\log(x^{\gamma})+\sum_{i\neq 0,\gamma}\frac{k_{i}}{2K}\log\left((1-\coth^{2}\left(K\hat{x}^{i}/k_{\gamma}\right)\right).

From this, we obtain (
3.6
).
∎

From the definition of a Legendre transform, we have that

F_{\alpha\beta}=\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}.

We will use this, along with Lemma
3.1
, to compute all the second-order derivatives of
\hat{F}
in terms of the
\hat{x}^{\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The second-order derivatives
\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}
can be expressed, up to constant terms, by the following formulae for all
\alpha,\beta\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Case 1:
\alpha=\gamma
.

\widehat{F}_{\gamma\beta}=4k_{\beta}K\hat{x}^{\beta}.

Case 2:
\alpha=\beta\neq\gamma
.

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}4k_{\alpha}K\hat{x}^{\gamma}+\frac{4k_{\alpha}K}{k_{\gamma}}(K-k_{0}-2k_{\alpha})\hat{x}^{\alpha}\\
+4k_{\alpha}(k_{0}+K)\log\left(1-e^{2K\hat{x}^{\alpha}/k_{\gamma}}\right)+4k_{\alpha}(k_{\alpha}-K)\log\left(1-e^{4K\hat{x}^{\alpha}/k_{\gamma}}\right)\\
+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\alpha,\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}k_{i}\left[\log\left(1-e^{2K(\hat{x}^{i}-\hat{x}^{\alpha})/k_{\gamma}}\right)+\log\left(1-e^{2K(\hat{x}^{i}+\hat{x}^{\alpha})/k_{\gamma}}\right)-\frac{2K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\right].

Case 3:
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
distinct.

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\left[\log\left(1-e^{2K(\hat{x}^{\alpha}+\hat{x}^{\beta})/k_{\gamma}}\right)-\log\left(1-e^{2K(\hat{x}^{\alpha}-\hat{x}^{\beta})/k_{\gamma}}\right)-\frac{2K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\beta}\right].

Proof.

Case 1:
\alpha=\gamma
.

By definition of the new coordinate system, we have

\hat{F}_{\gamma\beta}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}\hat{x}_{\beta}=\eta_{\beta\alpha}\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\eta_{\beta\beta}\hat{x}^{\beta},

since the metric
\eta
is diagonal.

Case 2:
\alpha=\beta
and
\alpha\neq\gamma
.

Calculating
\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}
directly, we have

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}=F_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}4k_{\alpha}(k_{\alpha}+k_{0})\log(x^{\alpha})+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\alpha\end{subarray}}^{n}k_{i}\log\left((x^{i})^{2}-(x^{\alpha})^{2}\right).

We can use expression (
3.5
) to make substitutions, and collect terms in
\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)
as follows:

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}4k_{\alpha}K\log(x^{\gamma})+4k_{\alpha}(k_{\alpha}+k_{0})\log\left(\coth\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}\right)\right)\\
+2k_{\gamma}k_{\alpha}\log\left(1-\coth^{2}\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}\right)\right)\\
+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\alpha,\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}k_{i}\log\left(\coth^{2}\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\right)-\coth^{2}\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}\right)\right).

Next, we use (
3.6
) to substitute for the terms in
\log\left(x^{\gamma}\right)
:

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}4k_{\alpha}K\hat{x}^{\gamma}+4k_{\alpha}(k_{\alpha}+k_{0})\log\left(\coth\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}\right)\right)\\
+2k_{\alpha}(k_{\gamma}-k_{\alpha})\log\left(1-\coth^{2}\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}\right)\right)\\
+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\alpha,\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}k_{i}\log\left(\frac{\coth^{2}\left(K\hat{x}^{i}/k_{\gamma}\right)-\coth^{2}\left(K\hat{x}^{\alpha}/k_{\gamma}\right)}{1-\coth^{2}\left(K\hat{x}^{i}/k_{\gamma}\right)}\right).

Since
\coth(u)=\frac{e^{2u}-1}{e^{2u}+1}
, we note that

\displaystyle 1-\coth^{2}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{4e^{2u}}{(e^{2u}+1)^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\coth^{2}(u)-\coth^{2}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{4e^{2v}(e^{2(u-v)}-1)(e^{2(u+v)}-1)}{(e^{2u}+1)^{2}(e^{2v}+1)^{2}}.

This allows us to expand and simplify
\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}
as follows:

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\alpha}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}4k_{\alpha}K\hat{x}^{\gamma}+\frac{4k_{\alpha}K}{k_{\gamma}}(K-k_{0}-2k_{\alpha})\hat{x}^{\alpha}-\frac{4k_{\alpha}K}{k_{\gamma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\alpha,\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}k_{i}\hat{x}^{i}\\
+4k_{\alpha}(k_{\alpha}+k_{0})\log\left(e^{2K\hat{x}^{\alpha}/k_{\gamma}}-1\right)+4k_{\alpha}(k_{\alpha}-K)\log\left(e^{2K\hat{x}^{\alpha}/k_{\gamma}}+1\right)\\
+2k_{\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\alpha,\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}k_{i}\left[\log\left(e^{2K(\hat{x}^{i}-\hat{x}^{\alpha})/k_{\gamma}}-1\right)+\log\left(e^{2K(\hat{x}^{i}+\hat{x}^{\alpha})/k_{\gamma}}-1\right)\right].

We may rewrite this expression using the following identity:

\log(1+e^{u})=\log(1-e^{2u})-\log(1-e^{u}).

This leads to the required statement.

Case 3:
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
distinct.

By direct calculation, we have

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}=F_{\alpha\beta}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log\left(x^{\alpha}+x^{\beta}\right)-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log\left(x^{\alpha}-x^{\beta}\right),

and we can make substitutions using Lemma
3.1
to find

\widehat{F}_{\alpha\beta}\overset{\text{\tiny$\bullet$}}{=}2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log\left(\coth\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}\right)+\coth\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\beta}\right)\right)\\
-2k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\log\left(\coth\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}\right)-\coth\left(\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\beta}\right)\right).

(3.8)

We note the following identities:

\displaystyle\coth(u)+\coth(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(e^{2(u+v)}-1)}{(e^{2u}+1)(e^{2v}+1)}

and

\displaystyle\coth(u)-\coth(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{2e^{2v}(e^{2(u-v)}-1)}{(e^{2u}+1)(e^{2v}+1)}.

Using these, we can reformulate (
3.8
) to obtain the required expression.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

The Legendre transform
S_{\gamma}
, for arbitrary
\gamma\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, applied to the function

as defined in (

\displaystyle\widehat{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta_{\gamma\gamma}}{6}\left(\hat{x}^{\gamma}\right)^{3}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\eta_{ii}}{2}\hat{x}^{\gamma}\left(\hat{x}^{i}\right)^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\

(3.9)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}^{n}\left[\frac{4k_{i}k_{\gamma}^{2}(k_{0}+K)}{K^{2}}f\left(-\frac{K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\right)+\frac{k_{i}k_{\gamma}^{2}\left(k_{i}-K\right)}{K^{2}}f\left(-\frac{2K}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\right)\right],

up to quadratic terms and coordinate shifts. The function

is as defined in (
2.8
).

Proof.

Taking (
3.9
) as an ansatz, we can compare its second-order derivatives with the expressions given in Lemma

. Note that we use identity (
2.9
) to express logarithmic terms as the function

.
∎

4
Multi-parameter
A_{n}
-type trigonometric solutions

In this section, we generalise solutions found by Hoevenaars and Martini; see Theorem 2.1 in the preprint version of
[
10
]
for full details. These solutions were also considered by Shen
[
15
]
.

We introduce a multi-parameter deformation of these solutions. The corresponding function
F=F^{\textrm{trig}}_{m}
depends on the
n
-tuple
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
,
m_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\,\forall\,i
, as well as on three parameters
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
. The non-polynomial part of
F^{\textrm{trig}}_{m}
is expressed via the function
f(z)
given by formula (
2.8
).
Relations between
a,b,c
and
M=\sum m_{i}
are needed to ensure that
F^{\textrm{trig}}_{m}
solves the WDVV equations. We will first consider a generic case before looking at other situations.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle bM+c

\displaystyle\neq 0,

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle aM^{2}+3bM+c

\displaystyle\neq 0.

(4.2)

Then
\eta=\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{k}
is a constant non-degenerate matrix.
Furthermore, the function

F^{\emph{trig}}_{m}(y)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}m_{i}m_{j}f\left(y_{i}-y_{j}\right)+\frac{a}{6}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}y_{i}\right)^{3}\\
+\frac{b}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}y_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}y_{j}^{2}\right)+\frac{c}{6}\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}y_{i}^{3}

(4.3)

solves the WDVV equations (
1.1
) if the following relation holds:

b^{3}M+3b^{2}c-ac^{2}+aM^{2}+3bM+c=0.

(4.4)

Conversely, the WDVV equations (
1.1
) imply relation (
4.4
) when
n\geq 3
.

In the case when
m_{i}=1
for all
i
, Theorem
4.1
is equivalent to Theorem 2.1 in Hoevenaars and Martini’s work.

From the definition of
f(z)
in (
2.8
) we have that
f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)=\coth{z}
, and so

f^{\prime\prime\prime}(-z)=-f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z).

(4.5)

For convenience, we will use the following shorthand throughout the calculations:

\beta_{ij}=\begin{cases}\coth{(y_{i}-y_{j})}&\textrm{if }i\neq j,\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4.6)

Let
\delta_{ij}
be the Kronecker delta function.
We also define

\delta_{ijr}=\delta_{ij}\delta_{jr}=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if }i=j=r,\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

First, we compute the matrices of third order derivatives of
F
. As previously, let
F_{k}
be the
n\times n
matrix with
\left(r,s\right)^{\text{th}}
entry

F_{krs}=\frac{\partial^{3}F}{\partial y_{k}\partial y_{r}\partial y_{s}}.

Lemma 4.2
.

The matrix
F_{k}
of third order derivatives
F_{krs}
of the function
F=F^{\emph{trig}}_{m}
given by (
4.3
) can be written as

F_{k}=W_{k}+V_{k},

where the
n\times n
matrices
W_{k}
and
V_{k}
have
\left(r,s\right)^{th}
entry
W_{krs}
and
V_{krs}
respectively, given by

\displaystyle W_{krs}

\displaystyle=am_{k}m_{r}m_{s},

\displaystyle V_{krs}

\displaystyle=\delta_{krs}m_{k}\left(\sum_{q=1}^{n}m_{q}\beta_{kq}+c\right)+\delta_{kr}m_{k}m_{s}\beta_{sk}+\delta_{ks}m_{k}m_{r}\beta_{rk}+\delta_{rs}m_{k}m_{r}\beta_{kr}

\displaystyle\hskip 140.00021pt+\delta_{kr}bm_{k}m_{s}+\delta_{ks}bm_{k}m_{r}+\delta_{rs}bm_{k}m_{r}.

Proof.

Considering each term in (
4.3
) individually, we can set out the following collection of statements:

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\partial_{r}\partial_{s}\left(\sum_{i<j}m_{i}m_{j}f(y_{i}-y_{j})\right)=\delta_{krs}\sum_{q=1}^{n}m_{k}m_{q}\beta_{kq}+\delta_{kr}(1-\delta_{rs})m_{k}m_{s}\beta_{sk}

\displaystyle\hskip 90.00014pt+\delta_{ks}(1-\delta_{rs})m_{k}m_{r}\beta_{rk}+\delta_{rs}(1-\delta_{kr})m_{k}m_{r}\beta_{kr},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\partial_{r}\partial_{s}\left(\frac{a}{6}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}y_{i}\right)^{3}\right)=am_{k}m_{r}m_{s},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\partial_{r}\partial_{s}\left(\frac{b}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}y_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}y_{j}^{2}\right)\right)=3b\delta_{krs}m_{k}^{2}+\delta_{kr}(1-\delta_{rs})bm_{k}m_{s}

\displaystyle\hskip 110.00017pt+\delta_{ks}(1-\delta_{rs})bm_{k}m_{r}+\delta_{rs}(1-\delta_{kr})bm_{r}m_{k},

(4.9)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\partial_{r}\partial_{s}\left(\frac{c}{6}\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}y_{i}^{3}\right)=\delta_{krs}c.

(4.10)

The expression (
4.8
) is equal to
W_{krs}
, and the sum of (
4.7
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) equals
V_{krs}
. Note that we can use the fact that
\delta_{ab}\beta_{ab}=0
to simplify (
4.7
) as required.
∎

For the WDVV equations to hold, we now need to check that the linear combination
\eta=\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{k}
is a constant, non-degenerate matrix.

Lemma 4.3
.

The
n\times n
matrix
\eta=\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{k}
has
\left(r,s\right)^{\text{th}}
entry

\eta_{rs}=(aM+2b)m_{r}m_{s}+\delta_{rs}(bM+c)m_{r}.

In particular, the
\left(r,s\right)^{\text{th}}
entry of the matrix
\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_{k}
is given by

\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_{krs}=2bm_{r}m_{s}+\delta_{rs}(bM+c)m_{r}.

(4.11)

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have
\eta=\sum_{k}W_{k}+\sum_{k}V_{k}
.
It is clear that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}W_{krs}=aMm_{r}m_{s},

Additionally, we have that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_{krs}=\delta_{rs}m_{r}\left(\sum_{q=1}^{n}m_{q}\beta_{rq}+c\right)+m_{r}m_{s}\beta_{sr}+m_{s}m_{r}\beta_{rs}\\
+\delta_{rs}m_{r}\sum_{k=1}^{n}m_{k}\beta_{kr}+2bm_{r}m_{s}+\delta_{rs}bMm_{r},

which can be simplified to the form (
4.11
).
∎

Note that the matrix
\eta
can be represented as

\eta=A+uv^{\text{T}},

where the
n\times n
matrix
A
has the entries

A_{rs}=\delta_{rs}(bM+c)m_{r}

(4.12)

and
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{T}
,
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})^{T}
are
n
-dimensional column vectors such that

u_{i}=m_{i}\textrm{ and }v_{i}=(aM+2b)m_{i}.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose the matrix
\eta
from Lemma
4.3
is non-singular. Then

\det\eta=\left(aM^{2}+3bM+c\right)\left(bM+c\right)^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}m_{i},

and the inverse matrix
\eta^{-1}
has entries

\eta^{rs}=\frac{\delta_{rs}}{(bM+c)m_{r}}-\frac{aM+2b}{(aM^{2}+3bM+c)(bM+c)}.

(4.13)

Proof.

Since we can write
\eta=A+uv^{\text{T}}
, this implies that

\det\eta=\det(A+uv^{T}).

(4.14)

By applying the Matrix Determinant Lemma (see Thm. 18.1.1 in
[
9
]
), we have

\det\eta=(1+v^{T}A^{-1}u)\det A.

The matrix
A
, given in (
4.12
), has determinant

\det A=\left(bM+c\right)^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}.

(4.15)

As
A
is a diagonal matrix, it is straightforward to see that
A^{-1}
exists and has the entries

(A^{-1})_{rs}=\frac{\delta_{rs}}{(bM+c)m_{r}}.

(4.16)

We can now compute

1+v^{T}A^{-1}u=1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(aM+2b)m_{i}\delta_{ij}}{(bM+c)}=\frac{aM^{2}+3bM+c}{bM+c}.

(4.17)

It follows from (
4.14
), (
4.15
) and (
4.17
) that

\det\eta=\left(aM^{2}+3bM+c\right)\left(bM+c\right)^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}.

We have
1+v^{T}A^{-1}u\neq 0
, since this is equivalent to the condition that
aM^{2}+3bM+c\neq 0
, which is satisfied as
\eta
is invertible. It is then easy to check (cf. the Sherman-Morrison formula, see e.g.
[
8
]
) that

\eta^{-1}=A^{-1}-\frac{A^{-1}uv^{T}A^{-1}}{1+v^{T}A^{-1}u}.

(4.18)

We have

\displaystyle\left(A^{-1}uv^{T}A^{-1}\right)_{rs}

\displaystyle=\frac{aM+2b}{(bM+c)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\delta_{ri}\delta_{js}}{m_{r}m_{s}}m_{i}m_{j}

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\frac{aM+2b}{(bM+c)^{2}},

and then formulas (
4.16
)–(
4.19

4.13
).
∎

For the WDVV equations (
1.1
) to hold we need

F_{i}\eta^{-1}F_{j}-F_{j}\eta^{-1}F_{i}=0.

(4.20)

From Lemma
4.4
, we have for non-singular
\eta

\displaystyle\left(F_{i}\eta^{-1}F_{j}\right)_{rs}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left[F_{irk}\left(\kappa+\frac{\delta_{kl}}{(bM+c)m_{k}}\right)F_{jls}\right]

\displaystyle=\kappa\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{irk}\sum_{l=1}^{n}F_{jls}+\frac{1}{bM+c}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}}F_{irk}F_{jks}

\displaystyle=\kappa\,\eta_{ri}\eta_{js}+\frac{1}{bM+c}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}}\Big[W_{irk}W_{jks}+W_{irk}V_{jks}

(4.21)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.00026pt+V_{irk}W_{jks}+V_{irk}V_{jks}\Big],

where

\kappa=-\frac{aM+2b}{(aM^{2}+3bM+c)(bM+c)}.

We will consider contributions to (
4.20
) from each of the five terms in (

) individually.

For a four-index expression

, we denote by
M_{[ij]kl}
the anti-symmetrisation

M_{[ij]kl}=M_{ijkl}-M_{jikl}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

\left(F_{i}\eta^{-1}F_{j}-F_{j}\eta^{-1}F_{i}\right)_{rs}=\lambda_{[ij]rs}+\kappa(bM+c)^{2}m_{r}m_{s}(\delta_{ir}\delta_{js}-\delta_{jr}\delta_{is})\\
+\big(\kappa(aM+2b)(bM+c)+a\big)m_{r}m_{s}\big(m_{j}(\delta_{ir}-\delta_{is})+m_{i}(\delta_{js}-\delta_{jr})\big),

where

\lambda_{ijrs}=\frac{1}{bM+c}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}}\left(V_{i}\right)_{rk}\left(V_{j}\right)_{ks}.

(4.22)

Proof.

We start with the first term in (

). From Lemma
4.3

\eta_{ri}\eta_{js}=(aM+2b)^{2}m_{r}m_{s}m_{i}m_{j}+\delta_{ir}(bM+c)(aM+2b)m_{r}m_{j}m_{s}\\
+\delta_{js}(bM+c)(aM+2b)m_{s}m_{r}m_{i}+\delta_{ir}\delta_{js}(bM+c)^{2}m_{r}m_{s},

so the antisymmetrisation becomes

\eta_{ri}\eta_{js}-\eta_{rj}\eta_{is}=(bM+c)^{2}m_{r}m_{s}(\delta_{ir}\delta_{js}-\delta_{jr}\delta_{is})\\
+(aM+2b)(bM+c)m_{r}m_{s}\big(m_{j}(\delta_{ir}-\delta_{is})+m_{i}(\delta_{js}-\delta_{jr})\big).

(4.23)

Next, we use Lemma
4.2
to consider the other terms in (
4.21
). We have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}}W_{irk}W_{jks}=a^{2}m_{i}m_{r}m_{j}m_{s}M,

which leads to

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}}\big[W_{irk}W_{jks}-W_{jrk}W_{iks}\big]=0.

(4.24)

We continue with

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}}W_{irk}V_{jks}=am_{i}m_{r}\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_{kjs},

since it follows from Lemma
4.2

is symmetric in

and

. By Lemma
4.3
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}}W_{irk}V_{jks}

\displaystyle=am_{i}m_{r}\big(2bm_{j}m_{s}+\delta_{js}(bM+c)m_{j}\big)

\displaystyle=2abm_{i}m_{j}m_{r}m_{s}+\delta_{js}a(bM+c)m_{i}m_{j}m_{r},

and so we find

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{k}}\big[W_{irk}V_{jks}-W_{jrk}V_{iks}\big]=a(bM+c)m_{r}m_{s}\left(\delta_{js}m_{i}-\delta_{is}m_{j}\right).

(4.25)

By the symmetry of

and

(4.26)

The antisymmetrisation of (

) is then found by combining (

), (
4.24
), (
4.25
), and (

) with

to represent the remaining terms.

To find the antisymmetrisation of

Lemma 4.6
.

We have

\lambda_{[ij]rs}=\frac{m_{r}m_{s}}{bM+c}\bigg(\left(M\left(b^{2}+1\right)+2bc\right)(\delta_{ir}\delta_{js}-\delta_{jr}\delta_{is})\\

Proof.

be the terms in

where we have used the expression for

given in (

where

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{ijrs}^{(2)}

+b^{2}m_{j}m_{r}m_{s}(\delta_{ir}-\delta_{is})+b^{2}m_{i}m_{r}m_{s}(\delta_{js}-\delta_{jr}).

5
Comparing different trigonometric solutions

5.1
A_{n}
-type systems

We show that an arbitrary choice of Legendre transform
S_{\gamma}
applied to a rational solution
F^{\textrm{rat}}_{A_{n}(k)}
of the form (
2.1
) produces a trigonometric solution
F^{\textrm{trig}}_{m}
of the form (
4.3
), subject to some linear change of variables and a choice of parameters
m_{i},a,b,c
. We will deal with the most general case, where the deformation parameters
k_{i}
have arbitrary (non-zero) values.
In the case when
k_{i}=1
for all
i
, the solution
F^{\textrm{rat}}_{A_{n}(k)}
corresponds to the root system
A_{n}
. In this case, its Legendre transformation is equivalent to the solution found by Hoevenaars and Martini which is discussed in Section
4
.

Let
\widehat{F}=S_{\gamma}\left(F^{\textrm{rat}}_{A_{n}(k)}\right)
for some
\gamma\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, given explicitly in Theorem
2.3
. The coordinate system
\hat{x}
is given by the Legendre transformation
S_{\gamma}
, see formulas (
2.4
). We also set
\widetilde{F}=F^{\textrm{trig}}_{m}
given by formula (
4.3
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Define
a,b,c,
and
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
by

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if }\alpha=\gamma,\\
k_{\alpha}&\textrm{otherwise;}\end{cases}

(5.1)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{K+1};

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=1;

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=-(K+k_{\gamma}+1),

where
K=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}
.
Then functions
\widehat{F}
and
\widetilde{F}
satisfy

\frac{(K+1)^{2}}{8k_{\gamma}^{2}}\widehat{F}\left(\hat{x}\right)=\widetilde{F}\left(y\right)\textrm{ up to quadratic terms,}

(5.2)

where the coordinate system
y
is given by

y_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}-\frac{K+1}{2k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\gamma}&\textrm{if }\alpha=\gamma,\\
\frac{K+1}{2k_{\gamma}}\left(\hat{x}^{\alpha}-\hat{x}^{\gamma}\right)&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(5.3)

Proof.

Inverting the expressions in (
5.3
) gives us

\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\begin{cases}-\frac{2k_{\gamma}}{K+1}y_{\gamma}&\textrm{ if }\alpha=\gamma,\\
\frac{2k_{\gamma}}{K+1}(y_{\alpha}-y_{\gamma})&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We rearrange the sum of the terms in
\widehat{F}
which are proportional to
\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\hat{x}^{l}
as

\displaystyle-\sum_{i<j<l}\frac{2k_{i}k_{j}k_{l}}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\hat{x}^{l}

\displaystyle=-2\hat{x}^{\gamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i,j\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}k_{i}k_{j}\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j<l\\
i,j,l\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{2k_{i}k_{j}k_{l}}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\hat{x}^{l}.

We then make the coordinate transformation
\hat{x}\to y
in the expression for
\widehat{F}
to obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\frac{8k_{\gamma}^{2}k_{i}}{(K+1)^{2}}f(y_{i}-y_{\gamma})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i,j\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{8k_{\gamma}^{2}k_{i}k_{j}}{(K+1)^{2}}f(y_{i}-y_{j})+\textrm{cubic terms}

\displaystyle=\frac{8k_{\gamma}^{2}}{(K+1)^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}m_{i}m_{j}f(y_{i}-y_{j})+\textrm{cubic terms},

since the
m_{\alpha}
are defined by (
5.1
).
The function
\widetilde{F}
can be rewritten as

\widetilde{F}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}m_{i}m_{j}f\left(y_{i}-y_{j}\right)+a\sum_{1\leq i<j<k\leq n}m_{i}m_{j}m_{k}y_{i}y_{j}y_{k}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\sum^{n}_{i\neq j}(am_{i}+b)m_{i}m_{j}y_{i}^{2}y_{j}+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(am_{i}^{2}+3bm_{i}+c)m_{i}y_{i}^{3}.

(5.4)

We now compare coefficients of the various cubic terms in
\widetilde{F}
and
\frac{(K+1)^{2}}{8k_{\gamma}^{2}}\widehat{F}
to show that relation (
5.2
) is satisfied.

After the change of variables
\hat{x}\rightarrow y
in
\widehat{F}
and some algebraic manipulation, we find that the sum of the cubic terms of the form
y_{i}y_{j}y_{k}
in
\frac{(K+1)^{2}}{8k_{\gamma}^{2}}\widehat{F}
, with
i,j,k
distinct, is equal to

-\frac{2}{K+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i,j\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}k_{i}k_{j}y_{\gamma}y_{i}y_{j}-\frac{2}{K+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j<l\\
i,j,l\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}k_{i}k_{j}k_{l}y_{i}y_{j}y_{l}.

This is the same as the second term in (
5.4
), since
m_{\gamma}=1
and
a=-\frac{2}{K+1}.

Next, we consider terms of the form
y_{i}^{2}y_{j}
, with
i\neq j
, in
\frac{(K+1)^{2}}{8k_{\gamma}^{2}}\widehat{F}
.
The coefficient of the sum of the terms proportional to
y_{\gamma}^{2}y_{i}
, for
i\neq\gamma
, in
\frac{(K+1)^{2}}{8k_{\gamma}^{2}}\widehat{F}
is equal to

\frac{k_{\gamma}}{K+1}\left[-k_{i}k_{\gamma}+2k_{i}(K+1-k_{i})-2k_{i}(K-k_{i}-k_{\gamma})-\frac{2k_{i}}{k_{\gamma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<l\\
j,l\neq i,\gamma\end{subarray}}k_{j}k_{l}\right]\\
+\frac{k_{i}}{2(K+1)}\big[2k_{i}(K+1-k_{i})-(K+1)(K+1+k_{\gamma}-k_{i})\big]\\
+\frac{k_{i}}{2(K+1)}\sum_{j\neq i,\gamma}k_{j}\left(3+3K-4k_{i}-2k_{j}\right).

(5.5)

By replacing the first sum in this expression with

-\frac{k_{i}}{k_{\gamma}}\left((K-k_{i}-k_{\gamma})^{2}-\sum_{j\neq i,\gamma}k_{j}^{2}\right)

and some straightforward but substantial manipulations, the expression (
5.5
) can be simplified to
\frac{k_{i}(K-1)}{2(K+1)}
.
All terms of the form
y_{i}^{2}y_{j}
, with
i\neq j
, in
\frac{(K+1)^{2}}{8k_{\gamma}^{2}}\widehat{F}
sum to

y_{\gamma}^{2}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\frac{k_{i}(K-1)}{2(K+1)}y_{i}+y_{\gamma}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\frac{k_{i}(K-2k_{i}+1)}{2(K+1)}y_{i}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
i,j\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{k_{i}k_{j}(K-2k_{i}+1)}{2(K+1)}y_{i}^{2}y_{j},

which is equal to the third term in (
5.4
) since we have set
b=1
.

In computing the coefficient of terms proportional to
y_{\gamma}^{3}
, it is useful to note that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j<l\\
i,j,l\neq\gamma\end{subarray}}k_{i}k_{j}k_{l}=\frac{1}{6}(K-k_{\gamma})^{3}-\frac{1}{2}(K-k_{\gamma})\sum_{i\neq\gamma}k_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i\neq\gamma}k_{i}^{3}.

The sum of all terms of the form
y_{i}^{3}
in
\frac{(K+1)^{2}}{8k_{\gamma}^{2}}\widehat{F}
can be expressed as

\left(\frac{K(1-K)}{6(K+1)}-\frac{k_{\gamma}}{6}\right)y_{\gamma}^{3}+\sum_{i\neq\gamma}\left(-\frac{k_{i}^{3}}{3(K+1)}+\frac{k_{i}^{2}}{2}-\frac{k_{i}(K+k_{\gamma}+1)}{6}\right)y_{i}^{3}.

This agrees with the last term in (
5.4
), as
c=-(K+k_{\gamma}+1)
.
∎

Note that for the choice of parameters in (
5.1
) we have

bM+c=k_{\gamma}\neq 0

and

aM^{2}+3bM+c=-\frac{2k_{\gamma}^{2}}{K+1}\neq 0.

By Theorem
4.1
, it follows that condition (
4.4
) holds, which can also be checked directly.

We also note that in general the family of solutions represented by
\widetilde{F}
is larger than the family given by
\widehat{F}
.

5.2
B_{n}
-type systems

The function
\widehat{F}=S_{\gamma}\left(F^{\textrm{rat}}_{B_{n}(k)}\right)
, given in equality (
3.9
), has a similar form to a known trigonometric solution generated by a
\vee
-system of type
BC_{n-1}
. Following the results of Alkadhem and Feigin in
[
1
]
, specifically Theorem 5.5, the trigonometric
BC_{n-1}
-type WDVV solutions have the form

\widetilde{F}=\frac{1}{3}\xi_{0}^{3}+h\xi_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}m_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}+\lambda r\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}m_{i}\tilde{f}(\xi_{i})\\
+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(sm_{i}+\frac{1}{2}qm_{i}(m_{i}-1)\right)\tilde{f}(2\xi_{i})+\lambda q\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n-1}m_{i}m_{j}\tilde{f}(\xi_{i}\pm\xi_{j}),

(5.6)

with coordinates
\xi=(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and independent parameters
q,r,s\in\mathbb{C}
, such that
q\neq 0
, and
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n-1})\in\left(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right)^{n-1}
.
The constants
h
,
\lambda
,
M\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=r+4s+2q(M-1);

(5.7)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2h^{3}}{q(r+8s+2q(M-2))}\right)^{1/2};

(5.8)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}m_{i};

(5.9)

with the requirement that

q\left(r+8s+2q(M-2)\right)\neq 0.

(5.10)

Finally, the function
\tilde{f}(z)
is given by

\tilde{f}(z)=-f(-iz),

(5.11)

where
f(z)
is defined in (
2.8
).

First, we show that an arbitrary solution of the form
\widehat{F}
is equivalent to a solution of the form
\widetilde{F}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose
K=\sum_{i=0}^{n}k_{i}\neq k_{\gamma}
.
Define
q,r,s,m
as

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=-\frac{2k_{\gamma}^{2}}{RK^{2}};

(5.12)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=-\frac{4k_{\gamma}^{2}(k_{0}+K)}{RK^{2}};

(5.13)

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{\gamma}^{2}(K-1)}{RK^{2}};

(5.14)

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}k_{\alpha}&\textrm{ if $1\leq\alpha<\gamma$},\\
k_{\alpha+1}&\textrm{ if $\gamma\leq\alpha\leq n-1$},\end{cases}

(5.15)

where
R\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is an independent scalar.
Then functions
\widetilde{F}
and
\widehat{F}
satisfy

\frac{R}{\lambda}\widetilde{F}\left(\xi\right)=\widehat{F}\left(\hat{x}\right)

(5.16)

with coordinate transformation

\displaystyle\xi_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4k_{\gamma}(K-k_{\gamma})}{R}\right)^{1/2}\hat{x}^{\gamma};

(5.17)

\displaystyle\xi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{iK}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha}&\textrm{ if $1\leq\alpha<\gamma$},\\
\frac{iK}{k_{\gamma}}\hat{x}^{\alpha+1}&\textrm{ if $\gamma\leq\alpha\leq n-1$}.\end{cases}

(5.18)

Condition (
5.10
) is satisfied by the choice of parameters (
5.12
)–(
5.15
), and we also have
\lambda
,
h\neq 0
.

Proof.

We start by inverting expressions (
5.17
) and (
5.18
). The resulting coordinate transformation
\hat{x}\to\xi
in
\widehat{F}
gives us

\widehat{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left[-\frac{4m_{i}k_{\gamma}^{2}(k_{0}+K)}{K^{2}}\tilde{f}\left(\xi_{i}\right)+\frac{m_{i}k_{\gamma}^{2}\left(K-m_{i}\right)}{K^{2}}\tilde{f}\left(2\xi_{i}\right)\right]\\
-\sum^{n-1}_{i<j}\frac{2m_{i}m_{j}k_{\gamma}^{2}}{K^{2}}\tilde{f}\left(\xi_{i}\pm\xi_{j}\right)+\text{cubic terms},

where we have also used (
5.11
) and (
5.15
).
This expression matches the corresponding terms in
R\widetilde{F}/\lambda
.

Note that
M=K-k_{0}-k_{\gamma}
by (
5.9
).
Since we have
q,r,s,M
in terms of the
k_{\alpha}
, we may use (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) to derive

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\frac{4k_{\gamma}^{2}(k_{\gamma}-K)}{RK^{2}}

(5.19)

and

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{16k_{\gamma}(K-k_{\gamma})^{3}}{RK^{2}}.

(5.20)

We can now consider the cubic terms in
\widehat{F}(\xi)
, which are

\frac{KR^{3/2}}{12k_{\gamma}^{1/2}(K-k_{\gamma})^{3/2}}\xi_{0}^{3}-\frac{k_{\gamma}^{3/2}R^{1/2}}{K(K-k_{\gamma})^{1/2}}\xi_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}m_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}=\frac{R}{3\lambda}\xi_{0}^{3}+\frac{hR}{\lambda}\xi_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}m_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}.

This matches the cubic terms in
R\widetilde{F}/\lambda
: hence, relation (
5.16
) holds.

Substitution of the expressions for
q,r,s,M
in terms of the
k_{\alpha}
into condition (
5.10
) gives us

q\left(r+8s+2q(M-2)\right)=-\frac{8k_{\gamma}^{5}}{R^{2}K^{4}}\neq 0.

Also, expressions (
5.19
), (
5.20
) are non-zero.
∎

The family of solutions
\widehat{F}
depends on
n+1
parameters
k_{0},\dots,k_{n}
while the family
\widetilde{F}
depends on
n+2
parameters
q
,
r
,
s
,
m_{1},\dots,m_{n-1}
. As we introduced an extra scale factor
R
in Theorem
5.2
, we can now relate a general solution of the family
\widetilde{F}
to a solution of the family
\widehat{F}
. More precisely, the following statement holds.

Theorem 5.3
.

Given a solution
\widetilde{F}
, such that
q\neq 2s
, we define:

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{r-2q+4s}{2q}&\textrm{ if $\alpha=0$},\\[5.0pt]
m_{\alpha}&\textrm{ if $0<\alpha<\gamma$},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{2q(2-M)-r-8s}{2q}&\textrm{ if $\alpha=\gamma$},\\[5.0pt]
m_{\alpha-1}&\textrm{ if $\gamma<\alpha\leq n$};\end{cases}

(5.21)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=-\frac{(r+8s+2q(M-2))^{2}}{2q(q-2s)^{2}}.

(5.22)

Then
\widehat{F}
and
\widetilde{F}
are related by
\frac{\lambda}{R}\widehat{F}(\hat{x})=\widetilde{F}(\xi)

with coordinate transformation

\hat{x}^{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\frac{i(r+8s+2q(M-2))}{2(q-2s)}\xi_{\alpha}&\textrm{ if $1\leq\alpha<\gamma$},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{h}{\lambda(q-2s)}\xi_{0}&\textrm{ if $\alpha=\gamma$},\\[5.0pt]
\frac{i(r+8s+2q(M-2))}{2(q-2s)}\xi_{\alpha-1}&\textrm{ if $\gamma<\alpha\leq n$}.\end{cases}

The proof follows by inverting parameter relations and the change of coordinates from Theorem
5.2
. In particular, it is useful to note that

K=k_{0}+k_{\gamma}+M=\frac{q-2s}{q}.

Asymptotic expansion of regular and connected regular graphs
Élie de Panafieu

Abstract
We derive the asymptotic expansion (asymptotics with an arbitrary number of error terms) of k k -regular graphs by applying the Laplace method on a recent exact formula from Caizergues and de Panafieu (2023). We also deduce the asymptotic expansion of connected k k -regular graphs using standard techniques for divergent series developed by Wright (1970) and Bender (1975), and quantify its closeness to the asymptotic expansion of k k -regular graphs.



1
Introduction

A graph
G
is a pair
(V,E)
,
where
V=\{1,\ldots,n(G)\}

denotes the set of labeled vertices,
and
E
is the set of unlabeled unoriented edges.
Loops and multiple edges are forbidden.
The
degree
of a vertex is
the number of edges containing it.
Graphs where all vertices have the same degree
k

are called
k
-regular
.
For example
0
-regular graphs are
graphs without any edge,

1
-regular graphs are sets of
pairs of vertices linked by an edge,

2
-regular graphs are sets of cycles of length at least
3
,
because cycles of length
1
are loops,
and cycles of length
2
are double edges.

Given a formal power series
\tilde{F}(z)
,
let us write

f_{n}\approx a_{n}\tilde{F}(n^{-1})

if for any
r\geq 0
, we have

f_{n}=a_{n}\bigg(\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}[z^{\ell}]\tilde{F}(z)n^{-\ell}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-r})\bigg).

We then say that
a_{n}\tilde{F}(n^{-1})

is the
asymptotic expansion
of
f_{n}
.
Our main result,
Theorem

8
,
is the asymptotic expansion
for the number
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
of
k
-regular on
n
vertices
for
k\geq 2

\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}\approx\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1})

for a formal power series
\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(z)

with
\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(0)=2
,
whose coefficients are functions of
k
,
explicitly given in the theorem.
This extends several previous results.

Read (1959b)
obtained this asymptotic expansion for
k=3
.
For a general fixed
k
,
Bender and Canfield (1978)
and
Bollobás (1980)

derived the main asymptotics of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
,
and
McKay (1983)
and
McKay and Wormald (1991)

conjectured the first three terms of the asymptotic expansion.
A linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients for
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}

has been obtained by
Read (1960)
and
Chen and Louck (1999)
for
k=3
,
by
Read and Wormald (1980)
and
Goulden et al. (1983)
for
k=4

and up to
k=7
by
Chyzak and Mishna (2024)
.
From those recurrences, an asymptotic expansion
is likely computable (
Kauers (2011)
,
Zeilberger (2008)
)
but requires some additional work.

We also investigate the number
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}

of connected
k
-regular graphs on
n
vertices,
proving in
Theorem

14

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}\approx\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1})

for any
k\geq 3
.
This extends the result
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}\sim\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}

from
Bollobás (1981)
and
Wormald (1981)

(main asymptotics).
Going further, we prove
for any
k\geq 3

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}=\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}\left(1+\Theta(n^{-(k+1)(k-2)/2})\right)

in
Theorem

15
.

Our work relies on a recent exact formula
for
k
-regular graphs
from
Caizergues and de Panafieu (2023)
,
to which the Laplace method is applied
(a classic technique for extracting the asymptotics of parametric integrals, see
e.g.

de Bruijn (1958)
,
Flajolet and Sedgewick (2009)

Erdélyi (1956)
,
Olver (1974)
,
Wong (2001)
;
Pemantle and Wilson (2013)
).
The asymptotic expansion of
connected
k
-regular graphs,
stated in
Theorem

14
,
then follows by application of techniques
for extracting the asymptotics of the coefficients
of divergent series,
due to
Wright (1970)
and
Bender (1975)
.

After introducing the problem,
presenting related work
and setting some definitions
in
Section

1
,

Section

2
presents our main result
on the asymptotic expansion of regular graphs,
then
Section

3

extends this result to connected regular graphs.
To simplify the reading of the paper,

in
Section

.

1.1
Related work

The enumeration of regular graphs
started in 1959 when Read
obtained linear recurrences with polynomial coefficients
for the numbers of
3
-regular graphs
and connected
3
-regular graphs (
Read (1959b)
,
Read (1959a)
,
Read (1960)
,
Read (1970)
),
and showed how to extract asymptotic expansions from them.
Since then, those results have been extended
in various directions,
including graphs with a given degree sequence,
graphs with degrees restricted to a given set,
and
k
-regular graphs where
k
varies
with the number of vertices
(see
e.g.

McKay and Wormald (1991)
).
This area of research is too rich
to be fairly covered in this paper,
so we refer the interested reader to the surveys
by
Wormald (2018)
and
Wormald (1999)
,
and restrict our review of the literature
to the exact and asymptotic enumeration
of
k
-regular and connected
k
-regular graphs
for fixed
k
.

Let us start with results on the number
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}

of
k
-regular graphs on
n
vertices.

Read (1959a)
and
Read (1959b)

provided a general exact expression
for
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
, that was not directly amenable
to asymptotic analysis.
Using a probabilistic approach and introducing what is
known today as the
configuration model
,

Bender and Canfield (1978)
and
Bollobás (1980)

derived the main asymptotics of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
for any fixed
k
.
A recurrence for
\operatorname{SG}^{(4)}_{n}

was obtained by
Read and Wormald (1980)
,
and a different recurrence was derived by

Goulden et al. (1983)
.

McKay (1983)
and
McKay and Wormald (1991)

correctly conjectured the first three terms
of the asymptotic expansion of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
.

Chen and Louck (1999)
found the recurrence for
\operatorname{SG}^{(3)}_{n}
using a different approach.
The next important step was the proof by
Gessel (1990)

that the generating function of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
is D-finite.
This is equivalent with the fact that for any
k
,
the sequence
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
satisfies a linear recurrence
with polynomial coefficients.
The corresponding differential equations were calculated by

Chyzak et al. (2005)
and
Mishna (2005)
,
and
Chyzak and Mishna (2024)

recently obtained the differential equation
up to
k=7
.
As mentioned earlier, an asymptotic expansion
is likely computable from this recurrence
(
Kauers (2011)
,
Zeilberger (2008)
).
However, the completeness of the theory for the computation
of an asymptotic expansion from a linear recurrence
(
Birkhoff and Trjitzinsky (1932)
)
is questioned (
(
Flajolet and Sedgewick, 2009
, Section VIII.7 p. 581)
,
Wong and Li (1992)
).
Finally,
Caizergues and de Panafieu (2023)
provided
a new exact expression for
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
.
The present article extracts
an asymptotic expansion from it.

We now turn to connected
k
-regular graphs.
The exact and asymptotic enumeration of connected
3
-regular graphs
was first achieved by
Read (1960)
and
Read (1970)

and was extended by
Wormald (1979)

to more general notions of connectivity.
Then
Bollobás (1981)
and
Wormald (1981)
proved independently
that
k
-regular graphs are asymptotically almost surely connected.
Finally,
Wright (1970)
linked the asymptotic expansions
of a combinatorial family and sets of objects from this family.
Thus, it is no surprise that our asymptotic expansion of regular graphs
translates to an asymptotic expansion for connected regular graphs.
We chose to explicit this link in
Section

2
.

What does it mean to consider a formal value
k
?

The results of
Gessel (1990)
,
Mishna (2005)

show that, for any fixed
k

and given enough computational power,
one can compute the differential equation
characterizing the generating function
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z)

of
k
-regular graphs,
from which an asymptotic expansion for the number
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}

of
k
-regular graphs on
n
vertices
can often be extracted.
For example, stopping at order
1
for the sake of simplicity
and keeping “
k
” non-evaluated in part of the formulas
to highlight their similarities, one finds
for
k=3

\operatorname{SG}^{(3)}_{n}=\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(2-\frac{71}{18}n^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})\right)

and for
k=4

\operatorname{SG}^{(4)}_{n}=\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(2-\frac{235}{24}n^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})\right).

In contrast, our result provides
explicit expressions for the terms
of the asymptotic expansion,
where the value
k
appears as formal.
We will prove in particular for any
k\geq 3

\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}=\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(2-\frac{1}{6}(k^{4}-2k^{2}+3k-1)(kn)^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-2})\right).

More terms of the asymptotic expansion
are provided in
Section

2.3
,
and terms corresponding to connected regular graphs
in
Section

3.3
.

Could the asymptotic expansion be derived using previous techniques?

To our knowledge, this is the first time the asymptotic expansion
of
k
-regular graphs is obtained for a general value
k
.
Could previous methods have obtained the same result?
Many different techniques have been developed
to enumerate regular graphs.
In fact, we suspect regular graphs have become
a playground to test mathematical techniques
and illustrate their scope.
This makes it difficult to answer categorically
the question of this paragraph.
We can however highlight some key differences
with previous approaches.

While counting
k
-regular graphs is challenging,
enumerating some models of
k
-regular multigraphs
(where loops and multiple edges are allowed)
is simple.
This classical observation
(see
e.g.
the configuration model
Bender and Canfield (1978)
,

Bollobás (1980)
or
Caizergues and de Panafieu (2023)
)
leads to counting
k
-regular graphs
by deformation from
k
-regular multigraphs,
removing loops and multiple edges.
Previous works have used inclusion-exclusion
to remove loops and multiple edges from multigraphs,
but they did not have access
to the full description of all possible ways
loops and multiple edges can overlap.
The exact formula from
Caizergues and de Panafieu (2023)

enumerating
k
-regular graphs contains this information
(using inversion instead of inclusion-exclusion),
and the present work builds upon it to extract
the asymptotic expansion.

Some previous works did not rely on a combinatorial decomposition
to enumerate graphs with constrained degrees.
For example,
McKay and Wormald (1991)

consider the generating function of all graphs
with
x_{j}
marking the degree of vertex
j

\prod_{1\leq i<j}(1+x_{i}x_{j})

and write the number of graphs
with degree sequence
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots)
on
n
vertices
as a coefficient extraction, expressed by a Cauchy integral.
For
k
-regular graphs, this leads to

\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}=[x_{1}^{d}\cdots x_{n}^{d}]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1+x_{i}x_{j})=\frac{1}{(2i\pi)^{n}}\oint^{n}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1+z_{i}z_{j})\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}^{d+1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}^{d+1}}.

The main difference with the present work
is that the dimension
n
of the domain of integration
tends to infinity,
while our integrals are on a fixed dimension
k
,
simplifying the extraction of an asymptotic expansion.

1.2
Definitions and elementary properties

Double factorial.

The double factorial notation for odd numbers stands for

(2n-1)!!=\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}=(2n-1)(2n-3)(2n-5)\cdots 1.

It has the following integral representation

(2n-1)!!=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathds{R}}t^{2n}e^{-t^{2}/2}dt.

Hadamard product.

The
exponential Hadamard product
is denoted by and defined as

\sum_{n}a_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\odot_{z}\sum_{n}b_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n}a_{n}b_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

For example, we have

F(z)\odot_{z}e^{z}=F(z).

The exponential Hadamard product was introduced in

(
Bergeron et al., 1997
, Section 2.1, p. 64)

as
Cartesian product
or just
Hadamard product
.
This notion (“
cet ami oublié
”) can be
traced back to
(
Joyal, 1981
, Theorem 3, Equation (8))
.
To avoid confusion with the more common ordinary Hadamard product,
we keep the word “
exponential
”.
We define the evaluated exponential Hadamard product as

F(z)\odot_{z=x}G(z)=\left(F(z)\odot_{z}G(z)\right)_{|z=x}.

It satisfies the elementary property

F(z)\odot_{z=x}G(z)=F(zx)\odot_{z=1}G(z)=F(z)\odot_{z=1}G(zx).

Lemma 1
.

For any polynomial
P(x)
, we have

e^{x^{2}/2}\odot_{x}P(x)=\sum_{n}(2n-1)!![z^{2n}]P(z)x^{2n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathds{R}}P(xt)e^{-t^{2}/2}dt.

When
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
is a vector of variables,
and
F_{j}(z)
are series with
F_{j}(0)=0

(to ensure that
e^{F_{j}(z)}
is a well defined formal power series,
see
e.g.

Kauers et al. (2011)
),
we define the compact notation
\odot_{\bm{z}}
as

e^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}F_{j}(z_{j})}\odot_{\bm{z}}G(\bm{z}):=e^{F_{1}(z_{1})}\odot_{z_{1}}\left(e^{F_{2}(z_{2})}\odot_{z_{2}}\left(\cdots\odot_{z_{k-1}}\left(e^{F_{k}(z_{k})}\odot_{z_{k}}G(\bm{z})\right)\cdots\right)\right).

The notation
\odot_{\bm{z}=\bm{x}}

is defined in the same way,
replacing each
\odot_{z_{j}}

with
\odot_{z_{j}=x_{j}}
.

Corollary 2
.

For any multinomial
P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})

and positive values
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
,
we have

e^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}x_{j}^{2}/2}\odot_{\bm{x}=\bm{1}}P(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k/2}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{k}}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{k}}P(\bm{t})e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{2}/(2\alpha_{j})}d\bm{t}.

Asymptotic expansion.

The theory of asymptotic series was created independently
by Poincaré and Stieltjes (
Jahnke (2003)
).
Consider functions
\varphi_{j}(n)

satisfying

\varphi_{j+1}(n)\underset{n\to+\infty}{=}o(\varphi_{j}(n))

for all
j
.
The notation

a_{n}\approx\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}\varphi_{j}(n)

then means that for any
r\geq 0
, we have

a_{n}=c_{0}\varphi_{0}(n)+\cdots+c_{r-1}\varphi_{r-1}(n)+\mathcal{O}(\varphi_{r}(n)).

We then say that
\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}\varphi_{j}(n)

is the
asymptotic expansion
of
a_{n}

with respect to the
asymptotic scale

(\varphi_{j}(n))_{j\geq 0}
.
The mention of the asymptotic scale
is often clear from the context and omitted.
In particular, given a formal power series

\tilde{F}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}z^{n}
,
we write

a_{n}\approx b_{n}\tilde{F}(n^{-1})

if for any
r\geq 0
, we have

a_{n}=b_{n}(f_{0}+f_{1}n^{-1}+\cdots+f_{r-1}n^{-r+1}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-r})).

Note that this asymptotics holds for any fixed
r
,
as
n
tends to infinity.
The series
\tilde{F}(z)
might have a zero radius of convergence.
Thus, there is no guarantee that for a given
n
,

a_{n}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}b_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}f_{k}n^{-k}.

Stirling approximation is a famous example of divergent asymptotic expansion

n!\approx n^{n}e^{-n}\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(1+\frac{1}{12}n^{-1}+\frac{1}{288}n^{-2}-\frac{139}{51840}n^{-3}-\cdots\right)

(see
e.g.
many different proofs in
Flajolet and Sedgewick (2009)
,
a proof due to Wench available in
(
Comtet, 2012
, p. 267)
,

Marsaglia and Marsaglia (1990)

or the end of
Section

2.1
).
An important property of asymptotic expansions
is that if
c_{n}
converges exponentially fast to
0
,
then it can be added without impact to the asymptotic expansion

b_{n}(\tilde{F}(n^{-1})+c_{n})\approx b_{n}\tilde{F}(n^{-1}).

Indeed, for each
r\geq 0
, we have
c_{n}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-r})
, so

\displaystyle b_{n}(\tilde{F}(n^{-1})+c_{n})

\displaystyle=b_{n}(f_{0}+f_{1}n^{-1}+\cdots+f_{r-1}n^{-r+1}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-r})+c_{n})

\displaystyle=b_{n}(f_{0}+f_{1}n^{-1}+\cdots+f_{r-1}n^{-r+1}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-r})).

2
Regular graphs

Since the sum of the degrees of a graph
is twice its number of edges,
there is no
k
-regular graph on
n
vertices
when
nk
is odd.
Thus, in the following,
we assume without loss of generality
that
nk
is even.
The
k
-regular graphs for
k\in\{0,1\}
being trivials,
we also assume
k\geq 2
.

We proved in a previous work (
Caizergues and de Panafieu (2023)
)
an exact expression enumerating regular graphs.
We will use the following equivalent formulation.

Proposition 3

(
Caizergues and de Panafieu (2023)
)
.

The number
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}

of
k
-regular graphs with
n
vertices
is equal to

\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}=(-1)^{nk/2}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}^{2}/(2j)}\odot_{\bm{x}=\bm{1}}\left([y^{k}]\frac{e^{-i\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}y^{j}}}{\sqrt{1-y^{2}}}\right)^{n}.

The asymptotic expansion of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}

will be extracted using the Laplace method,
presented in the next subsection.

2.1
Laplace method

The Laplace method is a central tool of asymptotic analysis
that provides asymptotics for integrals of the form

\int_{I}A(t)e^{-n\phi(t)}dt,

as
n
tends to infinity.
This type of integrals appears commonly in probability theory and physics,
hence the notation
A(t)
for the amplitude
and
\phi(t)
for the phase.
Many sources present the main asymptotics
(
e.g.

de Bruijn (1958)
,
Flajolet and Sedgewick (2009)
)
or a way to compute the asymptotic expansion
(
Erdélyi (1956)
,
Olver (1974)
,
Wong (2001)
,
Pemantle and Wilson (2013)
).
Some authors
(
Campbell et al. (1987)
,
Wojdylo (2006)
,
Nemes (2013)
)
focus on deriving explicit expressions for the coefficients.
In contrast, we prefer working with generating functions (series)
rather than sequences.
Recent works on asymptotic expansions
(
Borinsky (2017)
,
Dovgal and Nurligareev (2023)
)
show that gathering the coefficients
of an asymptotic expansion into a generating function
is convenient for their manipulation.
We present a version of the Laplace method
following closely the proof by
Pemantle and Wilson (2013)
,
adding only a more explicit expression
for the generating function of the coefficients
of the asymptotic expansion.

Proposition 4
.

Consider a compact interval
I
neighborhood of
0

and functions
A(x)
and
\phi(x)
analytic on
I

such that
A(x)
is not the zero function,

\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
,

\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\neq 0

and
\operatorname{Re}(\phi(x))
reaches its minimum on
I

only at
0
.
Then there exists a formal power series
\tilde{F}(z)

such that the following asymptotic expansion holds

\int_{I}A(t)e^{-n\phi(t)}dt\approx e^{-n\phi(0)}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)n}}\tilde{F}(n^{-1}).

Define the series
\psi(t)
and
T(x)
as

\psi(t)=\left(\frac{\phi(t)-\phi(0)}{\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\right)^{-1/2},\qquad T(x)=x\psi(T(x)).

then

\tilde{F}(z)=e^{zx^{2}/(2\phi^{\prime\prime}(0))}\odot_{x=1}A(T(x))T^{\prime}(x)

or, if one wants more explicit expressions for the coefficients,

[z^{k}]\tilde{F}(z)=\frac{(2k-1)!!}{\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)^{k}}[t^{2k}]A(t)\psi(t)^{2k+1}

for all
k\geq 0
.

We chose the letter
T
for our function
T(x)

because equations of the form
T(x)=x\psi(T(x))

are typical of rooted tree enumeration
(
Flajolet and Sedgewick, 2009
, Section I.5)
.
Our choice of defining
\psi(t)

as
\left(\frac{\phi(t)-\phi(0)}{\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\right)^{-1/2}

instead of
\left(\frac{\phi(t)-\phi(0)}{t^{2}/2}\right)^{-1/2}

ensures that when
\phi(t)
has rational coefficients
(which is often the case in combinatorics),
so does
T(x)
.
This avoids the unnecessary introduction of algebraic numbers
and facilitates the use of computer algebra systems.

A common variant of the previous result is
the case where
I
is an unbounded interval
instead of a compact one.

Proposition 5
.

In
Proposition

4
,
let us remove the assumption that the interval
I
is compact,
and assume it is instead of the form

[-a,+\infty)
,

(-\infty,a]

or
(-\infty,+\infty)

for some positive
a
.
Let us also assume
that
|A(t)e^{-K\phi(t)}|

and
|e^{-K\phi(t)}|

are integrable on
I
for some
K\geq 0
.
Then the asymptotic expansion
from the conclusion of
Proposition

4
holds.

For example
(
Flajolet and Sedgewick, 2009
, Proposition B1, p. 760)
, we recover the classic Stirling approximation
by applying this result to

n!=\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{n}e^{-t}dt.

After the change of variable
x=nt
, we obtain

n!=n^{n+1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{n}e^{-nx}dx=n^{n+1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-n(x-\log(x))}dx,

where the function
x-\log(x)

reaches its unique minimum on
\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}

at
x=1
.
We introduce the function

\phi(t)=1+t-\log(1+t)

and the change of variable
x=1+t

n!=\int_{-1}^{+\infty}e^{-n\phi(t)}dt.

Applying
Proposition

5
yields

n!\approx n^{n}e^{-n}\sqrt{2\pi n}(s_{0}+s_{1}n^{-1}+s_{2}n^{-2}+\cdots)

where for any
k\geq 0
,

s_{k}=(2k-1)!![t^{2k}]\left(\frac{t-\log(1+t)}{t^{2}/2}\right)^{-k-1/2}.

A combinatorial interpretation for those coefficients
has been obtained by Wrench
(
Comtet, 2012
, p. 267)
.
Alternatively, defining

\psi(t)=\left(\frac{t-\log(1+t)}{t^{2}/2}\right)^{-1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad T(x)=x\psi(T(x)),

we have the formal power series representation

\tilde{S}(z):=\sum_{k\geq 0}s_{k}z^{k}=e^{zx^{2}/2}\odot_{x=1}T^{\prime}(x).

2.2
Asymptotic expansion of regular graphs

Using
Corollary

2
,
we replace the exponential Hadamard products
in the expression of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
from
Proposition

3

with integrals,
then apply elementary changes of variables
to derive an expression amenable to the Laplace method.

Lemma 6
.

Assume
k\geq 2
and
nk
even,
and define successively the power series
with rational coefficients

\displaystyle B_{0}(u,y,\bm{t})

3
Connected regular graphs

It is known since
Wright (1970)

that the asymptotic expansion of regular graphs
gives access to the asymptotic expansion of connected regular graphs.
In this section, we detail this process.
Since a connected
2
-regular graph
is just a cycle, we will assume in this section
k\geq 3
.
Let
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}
denote the number
of connected
k
-regular graphs on
n
vertices.
The associated generating function is

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

A
k
-regular graphs is a set of connected
k
-regular graphs,
so the generating functions of
k
-regular graphs
and of connected
k
-regular graphs
are linked by the classic relation

\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z)=e^{\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}(z)},

and

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}(z)=\log(\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z)).

(1)

Notice that in the definition of

\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

we admit the empty graph with
n=0
vertices.
This ensures that
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(0)=1
,
so
Equation

1
properly characterizes
the formal power series
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}(z)
.

Given the asymptotics of the coefficients of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
,
the associated generating function
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z)

has a zero radius of convergence.
Our next section presents tools for extracting
asymptotic expansion of the coefficients
of such divergent series,
based on the work of
Wright (1970)
and
Bender (1975)
.

3.1
Divergent series

Let us first recall a key tool for the computation
of the asymptotic expansion of fast growing coefficients
of formal power series, due to
Wright (1970)
and
Bender (1975)
.

Theorem 9

(
Wright (1970)
,
Bender (1975)
)
.

Consider a function
H(z)
analytic at
z=0
,
a positive integer
R

and a formal power series

A(z)=\sum_{n>0}a_{n}z^{n}

whose coefficients satisfy

a_{n}\neq 0
for all sufficiently large
n
,

a_{n-1}=o(a_{n})
,
and

\sum_{j=R}^{n-R}a_{j}a_{n-j}=\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R}),

then

[z^{n}]H(A(z))=\sum_{j=0}^{R-1}c_{j}a_{n-j}+\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R}),

where

c_{j}=[z^{j}]H^{\prime}(A(z)).

In order to use the previous theorem,
we have to check that
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}

satisfies the hypothesis

\sum_{j=R}^{n-R}\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{j}\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n-j}=\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n-R}).

The next lemma simplifies this task,
requiring only information
on the main asymptotics of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
.

Lemma 10
.

Consider positive
\alpha
,
\beta
, a real value
\gamma

and a positive sequence
(a_{n})_{n>0}
satisfying

a_{n}=\Theta(n^{\alpha}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma})
,
then for any fixed
R\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
,
as
n
tends to infinity, we have

\sum_{j=R}^{n-R}a_{j}a_{n-j}=\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R}).

The following lemma provides the asymptotic expansion
of
a_{n-j}
given the asymptotic expansion of
a_{n}
,
provided it is of a special form
(satisfied by
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
).

Lemma 11
.

Consider
\alpha\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
,

\beta\in\mathds{R}_{>0}

and
\gamma\in\mathds{R}
,
and a sequence
(a_{n})_{n}

with asymptotic expansion

a_{n}\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}(n^{-1})

for some nonzero formal power series
\tilde{A}(z)
.
Define the formal power series

\tilde{A}_{j}(z)=e^{-\alpha j}\beta^{-j}z^{\alpha j}(1-jz)^{\gamma-\alpha j}e^{\alpha z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{A}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right).

Then for any fixed
j\in\mathds{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
we have as
n
tends to infinity

a_{n-j}\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}_{j}(n^{-1}).

Applying
Lemma

10

and
Lemma

11

to
Theorem

9
,
we obtain the tool we will apply
to extract the asymptotic expansion of
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}

for
k
even.

Proposition 12
.

Consider a function
H(z)
analytic at
0

and a formal power series

A(z)=\sum_{n>0}a_{n}z^{n}

whose coefficients satisfy

a_{n}\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}(n^{-1})

for some
\alpha\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
,

\beta\in\mathds{R}_{>0}
,

\gamma\in\mathds{R}

and nonzero formal power series
\tilde{A}(z)
,
then

[z^{n}]H(A(z))\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}_{H}(n^{-1})

where the formal power series
\tilde{A}_{H}(z)

is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{H}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{A}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]H^{\prime}(A(x)),

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha j}\beta^{-j}z^{\alpha j}(1-jz)^{\gamma-\alpha j}e^{\alpha z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{A}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right).

Observe that
\tilde{A}_{j}(z)
has valuation
at least
z^{\alpha j}
,
so each coefficient of
\tilde{A}_{H}(z)

is computable with a finite sum

[z^{\ell}]\tilde{A}_{H}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\ell/\alpha\rfloor}[z^{\ell}]\tilde{A}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]H^{\prime}(A(x)).

When
k
is odd,
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}=0
for all odd
n
.
Thus, the assumption from
Theorem

9

that the coefficients are eventually positive
is not satisfied in that case.
However, as shown in the next corollary,
we can work around this difficulty.
Indeed, for
k
odd, we can work with
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(\sqrt{z})

instead of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z)
.
It is a formal power in
z

with coefficients eventually positive,
so our last proposition is applicable.

Corollary 13
.

Consider a function
H(z)
analytic at
0

and a formal power series

B(z)=\sum_{n>0}b_{n}z^{n}

whose coefficients satisfy
b_{n}=0

for all odd
n
, and for
n
even

b_{n}\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{B}(n^{-1})

for some
\alpha\in\frac{1}{2}\mathds{Z}_{>0}
,

\beta\in\mathds{R}_{>0}
,

\gamma\in\mathds{R}

and nonzero formal power series
\tilde{B}(z)
.
Then for
n
even

[z^{n}]H(B(z))\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{B}_{H}(n^{-1})

where the formal power series
\tilde{B}_{H}(z)

is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{H}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{B}_{2j}(z)[x^{2j}]H^{\prime}(B(x)),

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha j}\beta^{-j}z^{\alpha j}(1-jz)^{\gamma-\alpha j}e^{\alpha z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{B}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right).

3.2
Asymptotic expansion of connected regular graphs

Applying
Proposition

12
(if
k
is even)
and
Corollary

13
(if
k
is odd)
to the expression of
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}

from

we finally express the asymptotic expansion
of connected regular graphs.

Theorem 14
.

For any
k\geq
,
the number
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}
of connected
k
-regular graphs
on
n
vertices has asymptotic expansion

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}\approx\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1})

where the formal power series
\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(z)

is computed using the following equations

\displaystyle\psi(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t-\log(1+t)}{t^{2}/2}\right)^{-1/2},

\displaystyle T(x)

\displaystyle=x\psi(T(x)),

\displaystyle\tilde{S}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{zx^{2}/2}\odot_{x=1}T^{\prime}(x),

\displaystyle f_{k,j}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j=0,\\

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(z)}{\tilde{S}(z)},

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{k!}{k^{k/2}}\right)^{j}f_{k,j}(z)(1-jz)^{-1/2-(k/2-1)j}e^{(k/2-1)z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{A}^{(k)}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right),

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}(z)\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]\frac{1}{\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(x)}.

Since
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}=0
for all

Theorem 15

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}=\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}\left(1+\Theta(n^{-(k+1)(k-2)/2})\right).

We start by recalling a classical lemma to estimate Gaussian-like integrals.
It will be useful for our proof of the Laplace method.

Lemma 16
.

Consider a nonnegative integer
j
,
nonzero complex numbers
\alpha
and
\beta

whose arguments are in
(-\pi/4,\pi/4)
, then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-\alpha n}^{\beta n}z^{j}e^{-z^{2}/2}dz=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $j$ is odd},\\
\sqrt{2\pi}(2\ell-1)!!&\text{if $j=2\ell$.}\end{cases}

and the convergence is exponentially fast in
n
.

Proof.

Define the positive real numbers

a=\operatorname{Re}(\alpha)
and
b=\operatorname{Re}(\beta)
.
We first prove that the integral from
bn
to
\beta n
is negligible.
Indeed, bounding the absolute value of the integral
by the length of the integration path
multiplied by the maximal value of the absolute of the integrand,
we obtain

\left|\int_{bn}^{\beta n}z^{j}e^{-z^{2}/2}dz\right|\leq\operatorname{Im}(\beta n)|n\beta|^{j}e^{-\operatorname{Re}((\beta n)^{2}/2)}\leq n^{j+1}|\beta|^{j+1}e^{-n^{2}\cos(2\arg(\beta))|\beta|^{2}/2}.

Since
2\arg(\beta)
is in
(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
,
the cosine is positive
so this upper bound converges exponentially fast to
0
.
By symmetry, we also have

\left|\int_{-\alpha n}^{-an}z^{j}e^{-z^{2}/2}dz\right|

converging exponentially fast to
0
.

Now consider the integral on
[bn,+\infty)
.
For any
x\geq 1
, we have

x^{j}\leq j!4^{j}e^{x/4}\quad\text{and}\quad e^{-x^{2}/2}\leq e^{-x/2}

so for any
n\geq 1/b

\int_{bn}^{+\infty}x^{j}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx\leq j!4^{j}\int_{bn}^{+\infty}e^{-x/4}dx\leq j!4^{j+1}e^{-bn/4}

which converges exponentially fast to
0
.
Similarly, so does the integral

\int_{-\infty}^{-an}x^{j}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx.

By analyticity, the integration path from
-\alpha n
to
\beta n

can be any continuous piece-wise derivable path
linking
-\alpha n
to
\beta n
.
Adding and subtracting parts of the integration paths,
we deduce that

\left|\int_{-\alpha n}^{\beta n}z^{j}e^{-z^{2}/2}dz-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x^{j}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx\right|

is exponentially small.

By symmetry, for any odd
j
, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x^{j}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx=0.

For any even positive
j
,
integration by parts provides

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x^{j}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx=(j-1)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x^{j-2}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx.

Following an induction
initialized with the Gaussian integral,
we deduce

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x^{j}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx=\sqrt{2\pi}(j-1)!!,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 17
.

For any nonnegative integer
d
and
x>\sqrt{d}
,
we have

\int_{x}^{+\infty}t^{nd}e^{-nt^{2}/2}dt\leq\frac{x^{nd}e^{-nx^{2}/2}}{n\left(x-\frac{d}{x}\right)}.

Furthermore, for any neighborhood
U
of
\sqrt{d}
,
this integral is exponentially small compared to

\int_{U}|t|^{nd}e^{-nt^{2}/2}dt.

Proof.

Introduce
\phi(t)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}-d\log(t)
, so

\int_{x}^{+\infty}t^{nd}e^{-nt^{2}/2}dt=\int_{x}^{+\infty}e^{-n\phi(t)}dt.

The derivatives of the function
\phi(t)
are

\phi^{\prime}(t)=t-\frac{d}{t},\qquad\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)=1+\frac{d}{t^{2}}.

Thus,
\phi(t)
is convex
and it stays above its tangeant at point
x
,
so for any
t

\phi(t)\geq\phi^{\prime}(x)(t-x)+\phi(x).

We inject this bound in the integral

\int_{x}^{+\infty}t^{nd}e^{-nt^{2}/2}dt\leq\int_{x}^{+\infty}e^{-n(\phi^{\prime}(x)(t-x)+\phi(x))}dt.

Since
x>\sqrt{d}
, we have
\phi^{\prime}(x)>0
.
We apply the change of variable
y=n\phi^{\prime}(x)(t-x)

\int_{x}^{+\infty}t^{nd}e^{-nt^{2}/2}dt\leq\frac{e^{-n\phi(x)}}{n\phi^{\prime}(x)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-y}dy\leq\frac{x^{nd}e^{-nx^{2}/2}}{n\left(x-\frac{d}{x}\right)}.

Let us now turn to the second point of the lemma.
For
d=0
, we have

\int_{U}e^{-nt^{2}/2}dt=\Theta(n^{-1/2}),

which is exponentially greater than our bound for
d=0
and
x>0

\frac{x^{nd}e^{-nx^{2}/2}}{n\left(x-\frac{d}{x}\right)}=\frac{e^{-nx^{2}/2}}{nx}.

Assume now
d>0
.
By application of the Laplace method, we have

\int_{U}|t|^{nd}e^{-nt^{2}/2}dt=\Theta\left(\frac{(d/e)^{nd/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

Looking at its derivative,
the function
t\mapsto t^{d}e^{-t^{2}/2}

is strictly decreasing on
\mathds{R}_{\geq\sqrt{d}}
,
so

(d/e)^{d/2}>x^{d}e^{-x^{2}/2},

which implies that
\frac{(d/e)^{nd/2}}{\sqrt{n}}

is exponentially greater
than
\frac{x^{nd}e^{-nx^{2}/2}}{n\left(x-\frac{d}{x}\right)}
,
concluding the proof.
∎

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{a+b+\ell\leq k}\frac{k!u^{\ell-a-b}t_{1}^{r-\ell-a-b}}{a!b!(k-\ell-a-b)!}\left(\frac{k-1}{2}\right)^{a}[z^{\ell-a}s^{r-\ell-a-b}]\frac{\left(\sum_{j=2}^{k}t_{j}z^{j-1}\right)^{b}}{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}(1+T(s))^{\ell+3a+b}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell,\ 0\leq a\leq\ell,\ 0\leq b\\
a+b+\ell\leq\min(k,r)\end{subarray}}\frac{k!u^{\ell+a+b}t_{1}^{r-\ell-a-b}}{a!b!(k-\ell-a-b)!}\left(\frac{k-1}{2}\right)^{a}u_{r-\ell-a-b,\ell+3a+b}v_{\ell-a,b}(\bm{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell,\ 0\leq a\leq\ell,\ 0\leq b\\
a+b+\ell\leq r\end{subarray}}\frac{k!u^{a+b+\ell}t_{1}^{r-a-b-\ell}}{a!b!(k-a-b-\ell)!}\left(\frac{k-1}{2}\right)^{a}u_{r-a-b-\ell,3a+b+\ell}v_{\ell-a,b}(\bm{t})\mathds{1}_{a+b+\ell\leq k}.

The quotient of factorials, rewritten as a product,
simplifies with the indicator function,
because when
a+b+\ell<k
, the product vanishes

\frac{k!}{(k-a-b-\ell)!}\mathds{1}_{a+b+\ell\leq k}=\bigg(\prod_{m=0}^{a+b+\ell-1}(k-m)\bigg)\mathds{1}_{a+b+\ell\leq k}=\prod_{m=0}^{a+b+\ell-1}(k-m).

Observe that
B_{0,r}(u,\bm{t})

is a polynomial with rational coefficients
in the variables
k
,

\mathds{1}_{j\leq k}
for
j
in
[1,r]
,

u
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}
.
By construction, we have

B_{0}(u,s,T(st_{1}),st_{2},\ldots,st_{k})=\sum_{j\geq 0}B_{0,j}(u,\bm{t})s^{j}.

Let us define

C_{1}(u,s,\bm{t})=B_{1}(u,s,T(st_{1}),st_{2},\ldots,st_{k}).

Then
[s^{r}]C_{1}(u,s,\bm{t})

is equal to

[s^{r}]\exp\left(-\frac{\log\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{r+2}B_{0,j}(u,\bm{t})s^{j}\right)-k(k-1)\frac{u^{2}s^{2}t_{2}}{(1+T(st_{1}))^{2}}}{s^{2}}+\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{4(1+T(st_{1}))^{4}}+(2k^{2}u^{2}-k+1)\frac{k-1}{4}\right),

which is a polynomial with rational coefficients
in the variables

k
,

\mathds{1}_{j\leq k}
for
j
in
[1,r+2]
,

u
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r+2}
.
Finally, set

C_{2}(s,\bm{t})=B_{2}(s,T(st_{1}),st_{2},\ldots,st_{k})T^{\prime}(st_{1}),

then

C_{2}(s,\bm{t})=\left(C_{1}\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}},s,\bm{t}\right)+C_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}},s,\bm{t}\right)\right)T^{\prime}(st_{1})

and

\mathds{1}_{j\leq k}
for

and

for some

.
Thus, we rewrite

Lemma

(
10
)
.

Consider positive
\alpha
,
\beta
, a real value
\gamma

and a positive sequence
(a_{n})_{n>0}
satisfying

a_{n}=\Theta(n^{\alpha}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma})
,
then for any fixed
R\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
,
as
n
tends to infinity, we have

\sum_{j=R}^{n-R}a_{j}a_{n-j}=\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R}).

Proof.

For any fixed
j
, as
n
tends to infinity, we have

a_{n-j}=\Theta\left((n-j)^{\alpha(n-j)}\beta^{n-j}(n-j)^{\gamma}\right)=\Theta\left(n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}n^{-\alpha j}\right)=\Theta(a_{n}n^{-\alpha j}).

(8)

Define the function

f(x)=x^{\alpha x}\beta^{x}x^{\gamma},

then

\displaystyle\log(f(x))

\displaystyle=\alpha x\log(x)+x\log(\beta)+\gamma\log(x),

\displaystyle\partial\log(f(x))

\displaystyle=\alpha\log(x)+\alpha+\log(\beta)+\gamma x^{-1},

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\log(f(x))

\displaystyle=\alpha x^{-1}-\gamma x^{-2}.

The second derivative is positive for
x>\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}
.
Thus
f(j)
is log-convex on
j\geq\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}
.
By symmetry, so is
j\mapsto f(n-j)
on
j\leq n-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}

and by product,

j\mapsto f(j)f(n-j)
is log-convex
on
j\in\left[\frac{\gamma}{\alpha},n-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right]

and its minimum is reached at
j=n/2
.
Set
J=\lceil\max\left(\frac{\gamma}{\alpha},R+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\rceil
.

Equation

8
then ensures

f(n-J)=\mathcal{O}(f(n)n^{-\alpha(R+1/\alpha)})=\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R}n^{-1})

and, by log-convexity, for any
j\in[J,n-J]
,

f(j)f(n-j)\leq f(J)f(n-J)=\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R}n^{-1}).

(9)

Since
a_{n}=\Theta(f(n))
,
there exists
C>0
such that
a_{j}\leq Cf(j)
for all
j
.
Let us decompose the sum as follows

\sum_{j=R}^{n-R}a_{j}a_{n-j}\leq 2\sum_{j=R}^{J-1}a_{j}a_{n-j}+C^{2}\sum_{j=J}^{n-J}f(j)f(n-j).

By
Equation

8
,
each of the terms of the first sum is a
\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R})

and
Equation

9
implies that the second sum
is bounded by
n\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R}n^{-1})=\mathcal{O}(a_{n-R})
,
which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma

(
11
)
.

Consider
\alpha\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
,

\beta\in\mathds{R}_{>0}

and
\gamma\in\mathds{R}
,
and a sequence
(a_{n})_{n}

with asymptotic expansion

a_{n}\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}(n^{-1})

for some nonzero formal power series
\tilde{A}(z)
.
Define the formal power series

\tilde{A}_{j}(z)=e^{-\alpha j}\beta^{-j}z^{\alpha j}(1-jz)^{\gamma-\alpha j}e^{\alpha z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{A}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right).

Then for any fixed
j\in\mathds{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
we have as
n
tends to infinity

a_{n-j}\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}_{j}(n^{-1}).

Proof.

\displaystyle a_{n-j}

\displaystyle\approx(n-j)^{\alpha(n-j)}\beta^{n-j}(n-j)^{\gamma}\tilde{A}((n-j)^{-1})

\displaystyle\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}n^{-\alpha j}\left(1-\frac{j}{n}\right)^{\alpha(n-j)+\gamma}\beta^{-j}\tilde{A}\left(\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{1-j/n}\right)

\displaystyle\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}_{j}(n^{-1}).

∎

)
.

and a formal power series

\alpha\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
,

\beta\in\mathds{R}_{>0}
,

\gamma\in\mathds{R}

and nonzero formal power series

,

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{A}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]H^{\prime}(A(x)),

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{j}(z)

Corollary

(
13
)
.

Consider a function
H(z)
analytic at
0

and a formal power series

B(z)=\sum_{n>0}b_{n}z^{n}

whose coefficients satisfy
b_{n}=0

for all odd
n
, and for
n
even

b_{n}\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{B}(n^{-1})

for some
\alpha\in\frac{1}{2}\mathds{Z}_{>0}
,

\beta\in\mathds{R}_{>0}
,

\gamma\in\mathds{R}

and nonzero formal power series
\tilde{B}(z)
.
Then for
n
even

[z^{n}]H(B(z))\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{B}_{H}(n^{-1})

where the formal power series
\tilde{B}_{H}(z)

is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{H}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{B}_{2j}(z)[x^{2j}]H^{\prime}(B(x)),

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha j}\beta^{-j}z^{\alpha j}(1-jz)^{\gamma-\alpha j}e^{\alpha z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{B}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right).

Proof.

Set
a_{n}=b_{2n}
, then

a_{n}\approx n^{2\alpha n}(2^{2\alpha}\beta^{2})^{n}n^{\gamma}2^{\gamma}\tilde{B}\left(\frac{n^{-1}}{2}\right).

The associated generating function is

A(z)=\sum_{n>0}a_{n}z^{n}=B(\sqrt{z}).

Observe that
2\alpha\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}

and
H(B(z))
is a formal power series in
z^{2}
:
its only nonzero coefficients have even indices.
Applying
Proposition

12
yields

[z^{n}]H(A(z))\approx n^{2\alpha n}(2^{2\alpha}\beta^{2})^{n}n^{\gamma}2^{\gamma}\tilde{A}_{H}(n^{-1})

where

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{H}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{A}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]H^{\prime}(A(x)),

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-2\alpha j}(2^{2\alpha}\beta^{2})^{-j}z^{2\alpha j}(1-jz)^{\gamma-2\alpha j}e^{2\alpha z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{B}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{z}{1-jz}\right).

We define

\tilde{B}_{j}(z)=e^{-\alpha j}\beta^{-j}z^{\alpha j}(1-jz)^{\gamma-\alpha j}e^{2\alpha z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{B}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right)

and observe

\tilde{B}_{2j}(z/2)=\tilde{A}_{j}(z).

For
n
even

[z^{n}]H(B(z))=[z^{n/2}]H(A(z))\approx n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}_{H}(2n^{-1}).

Finally, we replace
\tilde{A}_{H}(2z)
with

\tilde{A}_{H}(2z)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{A}_{j}(2z)[x^{j}]H^{\prime}(A(x))=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{B}_{2j}(z)[x^{2j}]H^{\prime}(B(x)).

∎

Theorem

(
14
)
.

For any
k\geq 3
,
the number
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}
of connected
k
-regular graphs
on
n
vertices has asymptotic expansion

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}\approx\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1})

where the formal power series
\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(z)

is computed using the following equations

\displaystyle\psi(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t-\log(1+t)}{t^{2}/2}\right)^{-1/2},

\displaystyle T(x)

\displaystyle=x\psi(T(x)),

\displaystyle\tilde{S}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{zx^{2}/2}\odot_{x=1}T^{\prime}(x),

\displaystyle f_{k,j}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j=0,\\
\sum_{\ell\geq 3}\mathds{1}_{k=\ell}\mathds{1}_{j\,\ell\text{ even}}z^{(\ell/2-1)j}&\text{if }j>0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{(k)}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(z)}{\tilde{S}(z)},

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{k!}{k^{k/2}}\right)^{j}f_{k,j}(z)(1-jz)^{-1/2-(k/2-1)j}e^{(k/2-1)z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{A}^{(k)}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(z)

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}(z)\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]\frac{1}{\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(x)}.

Proof.

Define

H(z)=\log(1+z),\qquad A^{(k)}(z)=\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z)-1.

Then, as we saw at the beginning of this section,

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}(z)=H(A^{(k)}(z)).

When
k
is even, for all sufficiently large
n
,
we have
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}>0
,
so the
n
th coefficients of
A^{(k)}(z)
is nonzero,
as required by
Theorem

9
.
If
k
is odd,
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}=0
for all odd
n

and
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}>0
for all large enough even
n
,
as required by
Corollary

13
.
We have for all
n>0

a^{(k)}_{n}:=[z^{n}]A^{(k)}(z)=[z^{n}](\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z)-1)=\frac{\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}}{n!}.

We recalled Stirling asymptotic expansion
at the end of
Section

2.1
.
Defining the formal power series

\psi(t)=\left(\frac{t-\log(1+t)}{t^{2}/2}\right)^{-1/2},\quad T(x)=x\psi(T(x)),\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{S}(z)=e^{zx^{2}/2}\odot_{x=1}T^{\prime}(x),

we have

n!\approx n^{n}e^{-n}\sqrt{2\pi n}\tilde{S}(n^{-1}).

Combined with the asymptotic expansion of
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
from
Theorem

8
,
we deduce, for
k
fixed and
kn
even,

\displaystyle a^{(k)}_{n}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{n^{n}e^{-n}\sqrt{2\pi n}\tilde{S}(n^{-1})}\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1})

\displaystyle\approx n^{n(k/2-1)}\left(\frac{e(k/e)^{k/2}}{k!}\right)^{n}n^{-1/2}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1})}{\tilde{S}(n^{-1})}

We define

\alpha=\frac{k}{2}-1,\quad\beta=\frac{e(k/e)^{k/2}}{k!},\quad\gamma=-\frac{1}{2},\quad\tilde{A}^{(k)}(z)=\frac{\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(z)}{\tilde{S}(z)},

so for
kn
even

a^{(k)}_{n}\approx\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}^{(k)}(n^{-1}).

The assumption
k\geq 3
ensures
\alpha>0
.
For
k
even,
\alpha
is a positive integer,
while for
k
odd,
\alpha\in\frac{1}{2}\mathds{Z}_{>0}
.
Following
Propositions

12
and
13
, we define

\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)=e^{-\alpha j}\beta^{-j}\mathds{1}_{kj\text{ is even}}z^{\alpha j}(1-jz)^{\gamma-\alpha j}e^{\alpha z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{A}^{(k)}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right).

(10)

The factor
\mathds{1}_{kj\text{ is even}}

is always
1
when
k
is even
(case of application of
Proposition

12
),
but is
0
if
k
and
j
are odd
(case of application of
Corollary

13
).
We obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}

\displaystyle=n![z^{n}]\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}(z)

\displaystyle=n![z^{n}]\log(\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(z))

\displaystyle=n![z^{n}]H(A^{(k)}(z))

\displaystyle\approx n!\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}n^{\alpha n}\beta^{n}n^{\gamma}\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{H}(n^{-1})

where the formal power series
\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{H}(z)

Defining

we conclude, after replacing

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}\approx\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1}).

To ensure the coefficients of the asymptotic expansion

,
we inject, in the expression of

(assuming

,

Theorem

(
15
)
.

For any fixed
k\geq 3
,
the numbers
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}
and
\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}

of
k
-regular graphs and connected
k
-regular graphs
on
n
vertices are linked by the relation

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}=\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}\left(1+\Theta(n^{-(k+1)(k-2)/2})\right).

Proof.

A
k
-regular graph contains either
0
vertex,
or at least
k+1
vertices
(the equality case corresponds to the complete graph)
so
\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}=0
for all
n\in[1,k]
.
This implies for all
j\in[1,k]

[x^{j}]\frac{1}{\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(x)}=0

and thus, using the notation
\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(z)

from
Theorem

14
,

\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(z)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]\frac{1}{\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(x)}=\tilde{S}(z)\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{0}(z)+\sum_{j\geq k+1}\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]\frac{1}{\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(x)}.

We inject

\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{0}(z)=\tilde{A}^{(k)}(z)=\frac{\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(z)}{\tilde{S}(z)}

so

\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(z)=\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(z)+\sum_{j\geq k+1}\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)[x^{j}]\frac{1}{\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}(x)}.

The
valuation
of a formal power series
is defined as the smallest index corresponding
to a non-zero coefficient.
For example, the valuation of the polynomial
7z^{2}+z^{5}

is
2
.
We seek the valuation of

\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)=\left(\frac{k!}{k^{k/2}}\right)^{j}f_{k,j}(z)(1-jz)^{-1/2-(k/2-1)j}e^{(k/2-1)z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}\tilde{A}^{(k)}\left(\frac{z}{1-jz}\right)

for
j\geq k+1
.
We have

\tilde{A}^{(k)}(0)=\frac{\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(0)}{\tilde{S}(0)}=2

of valuation
0
,
and

(1-jz)^{-1/2-(k/2-1)j}e^{(k/2-1)z^{-1}(\log(1-jz)+jz)}

of valuation
0
as well,
so the valuation of
\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)

is equal to the valuation of

f_{k,j}(z)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j=0,\\
\sum_{\ell\geq 3}\mathds{1}_{k=\ell}\mathds{1}_{j\,\ell\text{ even}}z^{(\ell/2-1)j}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For a fixed
k\geq 3
,
the valuation of
f_{k,j}(z)
increases with
j
.
At
j=k+1
, we find

f_{k,j}(z)=z^{(k+1)(k-2)/2}.

Thus,
\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{k+1}(z)
has valuation
(k+1)(k-2)/2

and for any
j>k+1
, the valuation of
\tilde{A}^{(k)}_{j}(z)

is greater.
This implies

\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(z)=\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(z)+\Theta(z^{(k+1)(k-2)/2})=\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(z)\left(1+\Theta(z^{(k+1)(k-2)/2})\right).

By
Theorems

8
and
14
, we deduce
for

\displaystyle\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{\operatorname{SG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1})

\displaystyle\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{(nk/e)^{nk/2}}{k!^{n}}\frac{e^{-(k^{2}-1)/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{\operatorname{CSG}}^{(k)}(n^{-1})

the link

\operatorname{CSG}^{(k)}_{n}=\operatorname{SG}^{(k)}_{n}\left(1+\Theta(n^{-(k+1)(k-2)/2})\right).

∎

5
Conclusion

We derived the asymptotic expansion of
k
-regular graphs
and connected
k
-regular graphs.
The expression we obtained for the error terms,
although explicit, are heavy.
We leave as an open problem the search
for simpler expressions
and a combinatorial interpretation,
possibly requiring a change of asymptotic scale.

Spin-charge separation and unconventional superconductivity in t - J model on honeycomb lattice
Jian-Jian Miao, Zheng-Yuan Yue, Hao Zhang, Wei-Qiang Chen, Zheng-Cheng Gu

Abstract
The physical nature of doped Mott-insulator has been intensively studied for more than three decades. It is well known that the single band Hubbard model or t t - J J model on the bipartite lattice is the simplest model to describe a doped Mott insulator. Unfortunately, the key mechanism of superconductivity in these toy models is still under debate. In this paper, we propose a new mechanism for the d + i ​ d d+id -wave superconductivity (SC) that occurs in the small-doping region of the honeycomb lattice t t - J J model based on the Grassmann tensor product state numerical simulation and spin-charge separation formulation. Moreover, in the presence of anti-ferromagnetic order, a continuum effective field theory for holons is developed near half-filling. It reveals the competition between repulsive and attractive holon interactions induced by spinon fluctuations and gauge fluctuations, respectively. At a large value of t / J t/J , the repulsive interaction dominates, leading to the non-Fermi liquid like behavior; while in a moderate range of t / J t/J , the attractive interaction dominates, leading to the SC order. Possible experimental detection of spin-charge separation phenomena is also discussed.



I
Introduction

Since the discovery of cuprates
Bednorz and Müller 1986
, the mechanism of high-
T_{c}
superconductivity (SC) remains one of the long-standing hardcore problems in condensed matter physics. The
t
-
J
model is a well-accepted microscopic model which potentially captures the essential physics of
\mathrm{CuO_{2}}
layers in high-
T_{c}
cuprates, and can be derived from the large-
U
limit of three-band Hubbard model for the
\mathrm{CuO_{2}}
layers
Zhang and Rice 1988
. The
t
-
J
model Hamiltonian reads:

\displaystyle H=-t{\sum_{\langle ij\rangle,\sigma}}(\hat{c}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{j\sigma}+h.c.)+J{\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}}\left(\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j}-\frac{1}{4}n_{i}n_{j}\right),

(1)

where

n_{i}=\sum_{\sigma}c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{i\sigma}\equiv\sum_{\sigma}n_{i\sigma}
is the electron density operator,

\hat{c}_{i\sigma}=(1-n_{i,-\sigma})c_{i\sigma}
is the electron annihilation operator defined in no-double-occupancy subspace, and

\mathbf{S}_{i}=(1/2)\sum_{\alpha\beta}c^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}c_{i\beta}

is the spin-1/2 operator.
The
t
-term permits the motion of holes while the
J
-term is the superexchange interaction.

Even though having been studied for decades, the global phase diagram of the
t
-
J
model on a square lattice is still controversial. Recently, the study of the
t
-
J
model on a honeycomb lattice attracts a lot of interest since these two types of lattices share similar features – both of them are bipartite lattices that stabilize the anti-ferromagnetic (AFM) order at half-filling.
Remarkably, Grassmann tensor product state methods discovered the emergence of uniform
d+id
-wave SC order at very small doping, while the stripe and the
d
-wave SC orders coexist at relatively large doping in the honeycomb lattice
t
-
J
model
Gu et al. 2013
;
Xu et al. 2022
. In the weak coupling limit, the uniform
d+id
superconductivity may also occur in the doped graphene systems revealed by various numerical methods, such as renormalized mean-field theory
Black-Schaffer et al. 2014
, quantum Monte Carlo
Pathak et al. 2010
;
Ma et al. 2011
;
Ying and Wessel 2018
;
Jiang et al. 2014
;
Wang et al. 2012
, renormalization group
Wang et al. 2012
;
Kiesel et al. 2012
;
Nandkishore et al. 2012
, and dynamical cluster approximation
Xu et al. 2016
. Other pairing symmetries, such as
s
and
p+ip
waves, are also discovered
Faye et al. 2015
;
Uchoa and Castro Neto 2007
;
Gu et al. 2020
. Apparently, the phase diagram of the
t
-
J
model on a honeycomb lattice can provide deep insights into the key mechanism of high-
T_{c}
superconductivity on a square lattice, which is closely related to realistic experimental materials.

Three decades ago, P. W. Anderson proposed the resonating valence bond (RVB) picture to account for the high-
T_{c}
superconductivity
Anderson 1987
, and the corresponding spin-charge separation scenario is systematically studied in terms of slave-boson formalism for the
t
-
J
model
Lee et al. 2006
. Considering the AFM order as a characteristic feature in the small doping region, the slave-fermion formalism is a more appealing approach for small doping; the condensation of Schwinger bosons leads to a long-range magnetic order
Auerbach 1998
.
Nevertheless, previous slave-fermion studies of the square lattice
t
-
J
model suggested the spiral order at finite doping instead of the AFM order
Jayaprakash et al. 1989
, which contradicts numerical and experimental results. However, as there are two sites per unit cell on honeycomb lattice, mean-field solutions with AFM order is still possible at finite doping. We believe that such kind of translation invariant mean-field theory might be crucial for the emergence of uniform
d+id
-wave SC order.

In this paper, we first use the state-of-the-art Grassmann tensor product state numerical method to obtain the global phase diagram of
t
-
J
model on honeycomb lattice at finite doping.
We adopt the slave-fermion mean-field approach and it gives rise to an AFM order on a honeycomb lattice that is consistent with our numerical results. At small doping, we find coexisting short-range AFM and FM correlations, and holon mobility. The mutual dependence of ferromagnetic (FM) correlation and holon mobility is also reproduced, which is consistent with the physics of the Nagaoka state in the infinite-
U
Hubbard model near half-filling
Nagaoka 1966
. We further employ the functional field integral formalism to derive the effective interacting holon theory. The effective holon Hamiltonian contains repulsive interaction generated by exchanging the spinons and attractive interaction via gauge fluctuations. For a moderate
t/J
, the attractive interaction overcomes the repulsive one and leads to the SC orders.
We stress that the attractive interaction
is only possible in the systems with spin-charge separation. We hope that such a mechanism can shed light on the emergence of SC orders in the
t
-
J
model on the square lattice as well and help us to understand the origin of high temperature superconductivity in cuprates.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Sec.
II
presents the Grassmann tensor product state numerical results, including the phase diagram at finite doping of the
t
-
J
model on a honeycomb lattice. The slave-fermion mean-field theory of the
t
-
J
model on honeycomb lattice is presented in Sec.
III
. In Sec.
IV
, we derive the low-energy effective interacting holon theory by including the spinon-holon coupling and gauge fluctuations.
Finally, we summarize the results and discuss the potential experimental detection of spin-charge separation phenomena in Sec.
V
.

To gain more physical understanding for the result from numerical simulations, we first adopt the slave-fermion mean-field theory to analytically study the
t
-
J
model on the honeycomb lattice.
In the slave-fermion representation, the electron operator can be decomposed into fermionic holons and bosonic spinons in the no-double-occupancy subspace as:

c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}=b^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}h_{i},

(2)

where
b_{i\sigma}
are Schwinger-boson (spinon) operators and
h_{i}
are slave-fermion (holon) operators, with no-double-occupancy constraint at each site
i
:

{\textstyle\sum_{\sigma}}b_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{i\sigma}+h^{\dagger}_{i}h_{i}=1.

(3)

In the no-double-occupancy subspace, the spin and electron density operators on each site can be expressed in spinons only:

\mathbf{S}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}b^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}b_{i\beta},\quad n_{i}=\hat{n}^{b}_{i}\equiv\sum_{\sigma}b^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}b_{i\sigma}.

(4)

Then we can re-express the
t
-
J
model Eq. (
1
) as:

\displaystyle H=t\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle,\sigma}(h^{\dagger}_{j}h_{i}b^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}b_{i\sigma}+h.c.)-\frac{J}{2}\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}b^{\dagger}_{j,-\sigma}b^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}b_{i\sigma^{\prime}}b_{j,-\sigma^{\prime}}.

(5)

Through out the whole paper, we label spin-up as
+1
, and spin-down as
-1
. In the summation over bonds, we choose
i
to be on sub-lattice
A
. To obtain the mean-field theory, the no-double-occupancy constraint is only imposed
on average
:

\langle{h^{\dagger}_{i}h_{i}}\rangle=\delta,\quad{\textstyle\sum_{\sigma}}\langle{b^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}b_{i\sigma}}\rangle=1-\delta\quad(0\leq\delta\leq 1),

(6)

which is done by introducing chemical potentials
\lambda_{i}
for spinons and
\mu_{i}
for holons at each site. We further define the spinon pairing (RVB) operators and spinon hopping operators on each bond
\langle{ij}\rangle
as:

\hat{A}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\sigma b_{i\sigma}b_{j,-\sigma},\quad\hat{B}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}b_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{j\sigma}.

(7)

Both are invariant under global
SU(2)
spin rotations. The mean-field ansatz reads:

A_{ij}=\langle{\hat{A}_{ij}}\rangle,\quad B_{ij}=\langle{\hat{B}_{ij}}\rangle,\quad D_{ij}=\langle{h^{\dagger}_{i}h_{j}}\rangle.

(8)

Here
\langle{\,\cdot\,}\rangle
refers to the mean-field average, and
j
is the nearest neighbor of
i
. All these parameters are in general complex numbers. We choose
i\in A
to avoid redundancy, since
A_{ji}=-A_{ij}
,
B_{ji}=B^{*}_{ij}
, and
D_{ji}=D^{*}_{ij}
. Physically,
A
and
B
represent the short-range AFM and FM correlations respectively, and
D
represents the mobility of holons
Jayaprakash et al. 1989

\displaystyle\langle{b_{i\sigma}b_{j\sigma^{\prime}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sigma A_{ij}\delta_{\sigma,-\sigma^{\prime}},

(9)

\displaystyle\langle{b^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}b_{j\sigma^{\prime}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=B_{ij}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\langle{b^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}b_{i\sigma^{\prime}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=(1/2)(1-\delta)\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}.

The mean-field Hamiltonian becomes (see details in Appendix
B.1

\displaystyle H_{\text{MF}}

\displaystyle=H_{h}+H_{b}+H_{0},

(10)

\displaystyle H_{h}

\displaystyle=2t\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle}(h^{\dagger}_{i}h_{j}B^{*}_{ij}+h.c.)-\sum_{i}\mu_{i}h^{\dagger}_{i}h_{i},

(11)

\displaystyle H_{b}

\displaystyle=2t\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle}(D_{ij}\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{ij}+h.c.)+\sum_{i,\sigma}\lambda_{i}b^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}b_{i\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad+J\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle}(B^{*}_{ij}\hat{B}_{ij}-2A^{*}_{ij}\hat{A}_{ij}+h.c.),

(12)

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=-2t\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle}(D_{ij}B^{*}_{ij}+h.c.)-J\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle}(|B_{ij}|^{2}-2|A_{ij}|^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i}[\mu_{i}\delta-\lambda_{i}(1-\delta)]-\frac{3}{4}NJ(1-\delta)^{2},

(13)

where

\displaystyle A_{ij}=A,\ \ B_{ij}=B,\ \ D_{ij}=D,\ \ \mu_{i}=\mu,\ \ \lambda_{i}=\lambda.

(14)

Then we perform Fourier transform

\displaystyle h_{i\in s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}h^{s}_{k}e^{ik\cdot R_{i}},

\displaystyle b_{i\in s,\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}b^{s}_{k\sigma}e^{ik\cdot R_{i}}.

where

is the position of the unit cell containing site

\displaystyle h_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}h^{A}_{k}\\[3.00003pt]

b^{B}_{k\sigma}\end{bmatrix},

(15)

\displaystyle r=tB,\quad p=tD+JB/2,\quad q=JA,

(16)

the mean-field Hamiltonian in momentum space is:

\displaystyle H_{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}h^{\dagger}_{k}H^{h}_{k}h_{k},

(17)

\displaystyle H_{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\begin{bmatrix}b^{\dagger}_{k\uparrow}&b_{-k\downarrow}\end{bmatrix}H^{b}_{k}\begin{bmatrix}b_{k\uparrow}\\

(18)

where the Hermitian matrices

\displaystyle H^{h}_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-\mu&\xi^{f}_{k}\\

\displaystyle H^{b}_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda&\xi^{b}_{k}&&-\Delta_{k}\\

\displaystyle\xi^{f}_{k}

\displaystyle=2r\gamma_{k},\quad\xi^{b}_{k}=p\gamma_{k},\quad\Delta_{k}=q\gamma_{k}.

(21)

Here

To diagonalize Eq. (

\begin{gathered}\begin{bmatrix}b_{k\uparrow}\\

W_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\small\begin{bmatrix}u_{k}s_{q}e^{i\theta_{k}}&u_{k}s_{q}e^{i\theta_{k}}&v_{k}s_{q}e^{i\theta_{k}}&v_{k}s_{q}e^{i\theta_{k}}\\

(25)

\displaystyle H_{\text{MF}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,s}E^{f}_{ks}f^{s\dagger}_{k}f^{s}_{k}+\sum_{k,s,\sigma}E^{b}_{ks}\beta^{s\dagger}_{k\sigma}\beta^{s}_{k\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad+H_{0}-2N\lambda+\sum_{k,s}E^{b}_{ks},

\displaystyle E_{k\pm}^{f}

\displaystyle=\pm|\xi^{f}_{k}|-\mu,

\displaystyle E_{k\pm}^{b}

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k}[n_{f}(E^{f}_{k+})+n_{f}(E^{f}_{k-})],

\displaystyle 1-\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}\Big\{\frac{\lambda}{\chi_{k}}[1+n_{b}(E^{b}_{k+})+n_{b}(E^{b}_{k-})]-1\Big\},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{2\alpha N}\sum_{k}\frac{|\gamma_{k}|^{2}}{\chi_{k}}[1+n_{b}(E^{b}_{k+})+n_{b}(E^{b}_{k-})],

III.2
Gauge structure of the mean-field Hamiltonian

Since the mean field theory does not impose the no-double-occupancy constraint on each site exactly, we must consider the gauge fluctuations to restore such a nontrivial constraint for the low energy subspace.
In the slave-fermion representation Eq. (
2
), the physical electron operator
c_{i\sigma}
is invariant under the local
U(1)
gauge transformation, i.e., for any site
i\in A,B
:

h_{i}\to h_{i}e^{-i\theta_{i}},\quad b_{i\sigma}\to b_{i\sigma}e^{-i\theta_{i}}.

(35)

After projection into the no-double-occupancy physical space, each of the three phases from the mean field theory is characterized by its invariant gauge group (IGG)
Wen 2002
;
Wang 2010
, which consists of local gauge transformations of the form Eq. (
35
) that leave the ansatz unchanged.
The FM phase (
A=0
and
B,D\neq 0
) has the usual
U(1)
gauge structure Eq. (
35
), with IGG
=U(1)
. However, the AFM phase (
A\neq 0
and
B,D=0
) has a
staggered

U(1)
gauge structure

h_{i\in s}\rightarrow h_{i}e^{-is\theta_{i}},\quad b_{i\in s,\sigma}\rightarrow b_{i\sigma}e^{-is\theta_{i}}.

(36)

where we interpret
s=+1
on sub-lattice
A
, and
s=-1
on
B
. This opposite sign on two sub-lattices indicate opposite gauge charges on
A
and
B
sub-lattices.
In the next section, we will show that such a staggered
U(1)
gauge structure eventually leads to an effective attraction between holons on different sub-lattices, and is crucial for the emergence of superconductivity.

Finally, the
A,B,D\neq 0
phase has a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge structure

h_{i}\to s_{i}h_{i},\quad b_{i\sigma}\to s_{i}b_{i\sigma}.

(37)

where
s_{i}=\pm 1
is a sign factor depending on the site
i
, and the corresponding IGG is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In this phase, besides the holon and spinon quasi-particle excitations, there is another excitation corresponding to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
flux of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fields, dubbed as vison
Senthil and Fisher 2000
.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fluctuation corresponds to phase fluctuations of order parameters. It is coupled to the holon and spinon in a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge invariant way and has a finite gap(if we assume the spinon does not condense), which does not confine the holon and spinon; hence it does not change the qualitative results obtained in this section.
The existence of the vison excitation is an indication of fractionalization in topological order
Senthil and Fisher 2001
, and the corresponding topological ground state degeneracy can be detected by DMRG calculations on cylinder or torus.

IV
Mechanism of superconductivity

To understand the key mechanism of SC order in the
t
-
J
model, we need to investigate the effective interactions among holons. We begin with the AFM phase with
A\neq 0
and
B,D=0
, and restrict the discussion to the small doping region (
\delta\ll 1
). After we establish a low energy effective field theory to understand the interactions among holons in this phase, we shall try to understand the whole phase diagram later.

IV.1
Repulsive holon interaction at large
t
/
J

The first step beyond the mean-field theory is to add the spinon-holon interaction from Eq. (
5
), and the total Hamiltonian reads:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=H_{h}+H_{b}+H_{\text{int}},

(38)

\displaystyle H_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=t\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle,\sigma}(h^{\dagger}_{j}h_{i}b_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{j\sigma}+h.c.).

(39)

To study the effective interaction between holons induced by the spinons, we shall use the coherent state path integral formalism. The spinon (or holon) operators are replaced by the corresponding complex (or Grassmann) numbers, with the following notations:

(b_{i\sigma},b_{i\sigma}^{\dagger})\to(b_{i\sigma},\bar{b}_{i\sigma}),\quad(h_{i},h^{\dagger}_{i})\to(h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}).

The partition function of fermion-boson coupled theory in the functional field integral representation is (number terms are dropped)

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int D\bar{h}\,Dh\,D\bar{b}\,Db\,e^{-S[\bar{h},h;\bar{b},b]},

(40)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=S_{h}+S_{b}+S_{\text{int}},

(41)

\displaystyle S_{h}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\Big[\sum_{i}\bar{h}_{i}\partial_{\tau}h_{i}+H_{h}(\tau)\Big],

(42)

\displaystyle S_{b}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\Big[\sum_{i,\sigma}\bar{b}_{i\sigma}\partial_{\tau}b_{i\sigma}+H_{b}(\tau)\Big],

(43)

\displaystyle S_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\,H_{\text{int}}(\tau).

(44)

Now
h
and
b
are Grassmann and complex variables, denoting the holon and spinon degrees of freedom in the coherent state representation. We then integrate out spinons to derive the effective holon Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{h}

\displaystyle=H_{h}+H^{\text{eff}}_{\text{int}},

(45)

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}{\sum_{\{k\}}}^{\prime}V_{k_{1}k_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}h^{B\dagger}_{k_{1}}h^{A\dagger}_{k_{2}}h^{A}_{k^{\prime}_{1}}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}_{2}},

(46)

where
{\sum_{\{k\}}}^{\prime}
is summation over all momentum with conservation (
k_{1}+k_{2}=k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}
). In the small doping limit,
B,D\approx 0
, and the interaction strength is (see Appendix
C
)

\displaystyle V_{k_{1}k_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}=-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\sum_{p}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\chi_{p}\chi_{p^{\prime}}(\chi_{p}+\chi_{p^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\times\Big[J_{b}^{2}(\gamma^{*}_{p}\gamma^{*}_{p^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}-p}\gamma_{k_{2}+p^{\prime}}+\gamma_{p}\gamma_{p^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}+p}\gamma_{k_{2}-p})

\displaystyle\quad+(\chi_{p}\chi_{p^{\prime}}-\lambda^{2})(\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}+p}\gamma_{k_{2}+p^{\prime}}+\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}-p}\gamma_{k_{2}-p})\Big]\bigg\},

(47)

where
p^{\prime}=p+k_{1}-k^{\prime}_{1}
, and
J_{b}=JA
. We have already take the static limit approximation to extract the frequency-independent effective interaction, which is mediated by exchanging two spinons with momentum
p
and
p^{\prime}
. As the minimum of the spinon dispersion is at
k=0
, the dominant contributions to the interaction coefficient in the low energy come from
p\sim p^{\prime}\sim 0
, and we replace
\gamma_{k^{\prime}_{1}-p}\to\gamma_{k^{\prime}_{1}}
and
\gamma_{k_{2}+p^{\prime}}\to\gamma_{k_{2}}
in Eq. (
47

\displaystyle V_{k_{1}k_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\approx-t^{2}\gamma_{-k^{\prime}_{1}}\gamma_{k_{2}}f(k_{1}-k^{\prime}_{1}),

(48)

\displaystyle f(k)\equiv\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p}\left.\frac{J_{b}^{2}\operatorname{Re}(\gamma_{p}\gamma_{p^{\prime}})+(\chi_{p}\chi_{p^{\prime}}-\lambda^{2})}{\chi_{p}\chi_{p^{\prime}}(\chi_{p}+\chi_{p^{\prime}})}\right|_{p^{\prime}=p+k}.

(49)

In the small doping limit, the holon momenta
k_{1},k^{\prime}_{1}
are both expected to be small. Let us then calculate:

\displaystyle f(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p}\frac{1}{2\chi_{p}^{3}}[J_{b}^{2}\operatorname{Re}(\gamma_{p}^{2})+(\chi_{p}^{2}-\lambda^{2})]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p}\frac{J_{b}^{2}}{\chi_{p}^{3}}(\operatorname{Im}\gamma_{p})^{2}<0,

(50)

which is
negative
. Assuming a weak momentum dependence of
f(k)
, we can approximate
f(k)
by a
negative
constant
C/J_{b}
(with
C<0
), an inverse Fourier transform of Eq. (
46

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{t^{2}C}{J_{b}N}{\sum_{\{k\}}}^{\prime}\gamma_{-k^{\prime}_{1}}\gamma_{k_{2}}h^{B\dagger}_{k_{1}}h^{A\dagger}_{k_{2}}h^{A}_{k^{\prime}_{1}}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}_{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{t^{2}C}{J_{b}}\sum_{j\in B}\sum_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}h^{\dagger}_{j}h^{\dagger}_{j-\delta^{\prime}}h_{j-\delta}h_{j},

(51)

where
\delta,\delta^{\prime}
sum over the nearest neighbor vectors
\{\delta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
(see Fig.
1
).
In particular,
H^{\text{eff}}_{\text{int}}
contains the following nearest-neighbor interaction terms (when
\delta=\delta^{\prime}
)

-\frac{t^{2}C}{J_{b}}\sum_{j\in B}\sum_{\delta}h^{\dagger}_{j}h^{\dagger}_{j-\delta}h_{j-\delta}h_{j}=-\frac{t^{2}C}{J_{b}}\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle}h^{\dagger}_{i}h_{i}h^{\dagger}_{j}h_{j}.

(52)

C<0
implies that the nearest neighbor interaction between holons induced by exchanging spinons is
repulsive
.

IV.2

Next we impose the no-double-occupancy constraint on each site exactly.
This is done by a delta-function in the partition function:

\displaystyle\prod_{j}\delta\Big(\bar{h}_{j}h_{j}+\sum_{\sigma}\bar{b}_{j\sigma}b_{j\sigma}-1\Big)

\displaystyle Z=\int D\bar{b}\,Db\,D\bar{h}\,Dh\,D\lambda\,e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\,L},

\displaystyle L=\sum_{j}\Big[\bar{h}_{j}(\partial_{\tau}+i\lambda_{j})h_{j}+\sum_{\sigma}\bar{b}_{j\sigma}(\partial_{\tau}+i\lambda_{j})b_{j\sigma}-i\lambda_{j}\Big]

\displaystyle+t\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle,\sigma}(\bar{h}_{j}h_{i}\bar{b}_{i\sigma}b_{j\sigma}+h.c.)-\frac{J}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\langle{ij}\rangle\\
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\bar{b}_{j,-\sigma}\bar{b}_{i\sigma}b_{i\sigma^{\prime}}b_{j,-\sigma^{\prime}}.

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int D\bar{b}\,Db\,D\bar{h}\,Dh\,D\bar{\Delta}\,D\Delta\,D\lambda\,e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\,L},

(54)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\Big[\bar{h}_{j}(\partial_{\tau}+i\lambda_{j})h_{j}+\sum_{\sigma}\bar{b}_{j\sigma}(\partial_{\tau}+i\lambda_{j})b_{j\sigma}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-i\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}+t\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle,\sigma}(\bar{h}_{j}h_{i}\bar{b}_{i\sigma}b_{j\sigma}+h.c.)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{J}{2}\sum_{\langle{ij}\rangle}\Big[|\Delta_{ij}|^{2}-\sum_{\sigma}\sigma(\bar{\Delta}_{ij}b_{i\sigma}b_{j,-\sigma}+h.c.)\Big],

(55)

where

\begin{aligned} b_{i\in s,\sigma}&\to b_{i\sigma}e^{-is\theta_{i}},\\
h_{i\in s}&\to h_{i}e^{-is\theta_{i}},\end{aligned}\quad\begin{aligned} \Delta_{ij}&\to\Delta_{ij}e^{-i(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j})},\\
\lambda_{i\in s}&\to\lambda_{i}+s\,\partial_{\tau}\theta_{i}.\end{aligned}

(56)

The transformation can be reformulated using

\mathbf{A}

(on sites)

\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}=x_{ij}\cdot\mathbf{A}(i,j),\quad A_{\tau}(i)=\partial_{\tau}\theta_{i}.

(57)

Here

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}

\displaystyle\to\Delta_{ij}e^{-ix_{ij}\cdot\mathbf{A}(i,j)},

(58)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i\in s}

\displaystyle\to\lambda_{i}+sA_{\tau}(i).

In the partition function, we keep configurations of

\bar{\Delta}_{ij}

\lambda_{i}
at the saddle point with fluctuations of the gauge field:

\displaystyle\Delta_{i,i+\delta}

\displaystyle\to\Delta e^{i\delta\cdot\mathbf{A}(i,i+\delta)}

\displaystyle(i\in A),

(59)

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}_{i,i+\delta}

\displaystyle\to\Delta e^{-i\delta\cdot\mathbf{A}(i,i+\delta)}

\displaystyle(i\in A),

\displaystyle\lambda_{i\in s}

\displaystyle\to-i\lambda+sA_{\tau}(i),

where
\Delta=2A
and
\lambda

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int D\bar{b}\,Db\,D\bar{h}\,Dh\,DA\,e^{-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\,L},

(60)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=L_{h}+L_{\text{heis}}+L_{t}+L_{0},

(61)

with each part in the Lagrangian given by

\displaystyle\ \ \ L_{h}=\sum_{s}\sum_{i\in s}\Big[\bar{h}_{i}(\partial_{\tau}+isA_{\tau}(i))h_{i}+\lambda\bar{h}_{i}h_{i}\Big],

(62)

\displaystyle L_{\text{heis}}=\sum_{s}\sum_{i\in s}\Big[\bar{b}_{i\sigma}(\partial_{\tau}+isA_{\tau}(i))b_{i\sigma}+\lambda\bar{b}_{i\sigma}b_{i\sigma}\Big]

\displaystyle-\frac{J\Delta}{2}\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{\delta,\sigma}\sigma\Big[e^{i\delta\cdot\mathbf{A}(i,i+\delta)}\bar{b}_{i\sigma}\bar{b}_{i+\delta,-\sigma}+h.c.\Big],

(63)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ L_{t}=t\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{\delta,\sigma}\bar{h}_{i+\delta}h_{i}\bar{b}_{i\sigma}b_{i+\delta,\sigma}+h.c.,

(64)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ L_{0}=-\sum_{s}\sum_{i\in s}(\lambda+isA_{\tau}(i))+\frac{\alpha JN}{2}\Delta^{2}.

(65)

Here

\{i+\delta_{l}\}_{l=1}^{3}

We then take the continuum limit by setting the nearest neighbor distance

\sum_{i\in A}\text{ or }\sum_{j\in B}\to\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega},\quad\Omega=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}a^{2}.

(66)

Here

is the area of a unit cell. The continuum fields are

\displaystyle h_{i\in s}

\displaystyle\to h^{s}(x),

\displaystyle b_{i\in s,\sigma}

\displaystyle\to b^{s}_{\sigma}(x),

(67)

\displaystyle A_{\tau}(i)

\displaystyle\to A_{\tau}(x),

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(i,i+\delta)

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{A}(x+\tfrac{\delta}{2}).

where
x
is the position of the site
i
. For convenience, we redefine spinon-holon coupling
\alpha t\to t
, and introduce

\displaystyle L_{h}=\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\sum_{s}\Big[\bar{h}^{s}(\partial_{\tau}+isA_{\tau})h^{s}+\lambda\bar{h}^{s}h^{s}\Big],

(68)

\displaystyle L_{\text{heis}}=\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\sum_{s,\sigma}\Big[\bar{b}^{s}_{\sigma}(\partial_{\tau}+isA_{\tau})b^{s}_{\sigma}+\lambda\bar{b}^{s}_{\sigma}b^{s}_{\sigma}\Big]

\displaystyle\ +Qa^{2}\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\Big[(\nabla-i\mathbf{A})\bar{b}^{A}_{\sigma}\cdot(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})\bar{b}^{B}_{-\sigma}+h.c.\Big]

\displaystyle\ -4Q\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\sum_{\sigma}\sigma\Big[\bar{b}^{A}_{\sigma}\bar{b}^{B}_{-\sigma}+h.c.\Big],

(69)

\displaystyle L_{t}=t\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\sum_{\sigma}\Big[\bar{h}^{B}h^{A}\bar{b}^{A}_{\sigma}b^{B}_{\sigma}+h.c.\Big],

(70)

\displaystyle L_{0}=\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\Big[-2\lambda+\frac{\alpha J}{2}\Delta^{2}\Big].

(71)

Define

and
\partial_{\mu}=(\partial_{\tau},\nabla)
; the gauge transformation in continuum limit is

\displaystyle b^{s}_{\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle\to b^{s}_{\sigma}(x)e^{-is\theta(x)},

(72)

\displaystyle h^{s}(x)

\displaystyle\to h^{s}(x)e^{-is\theta(x)},

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(x)

IV.3
Effective theory of holons

Following Ref.
27
, we combine the Schwinger bosons into a low energy field
z
and a high energy field
\pi

z_{\sigma}=(b^{A}_{\sigma}+\sigma\bar{b}^{B}_{-\sigma})/2,\quad\pi_{\sigma}=(b^{A}_{\sigma}-\sigma\bar{b}^{B}_{-\sigma})/2.

(73)

From Eq. (
72

z,\pi
fields are

z_{\sigma}\to z_{\sigma}e^{-i\theta},\quad\pi_{\sigma}\to\pi_{\sigma}e^{-i\theta}.

(74)

We then express the Lagrangian
L
in terms of
z,\pi
fields. The transformed
L_{\text{heis}}
(Eq. (
69

\displaystyle L_{\text{heis}}=\int\frac{d^{2}x}{\Omega}\sum_{\sigma}\bar{z}_{\sigma}[(\lambda-4Q)-Qa^{2}(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})^{2}]z_{\sigma}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{d^{2}x}{\Omega}\sum_{\sigma}[\bar{\pi}_{\sigma}(\partial_{\tau}+iA_{\tau})z_{\sigma}-\pi_{\sigma}(\partial_{\tau}-iA_{\tau})\bar{z}_{\sigma}]

\displaystyle+\int\frac{d^{2}x}{\Omega}\sum_{\sigma}\bar{\pi}_{\sigma}[(\lambda+4Q)+Qa^{2}(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})^{2}]\pi_{\sigma},

(75)

and the spinon-holon interaction
L_{t}
(Eq. (
70

L_{t}=(-t)\int\frac{d^{2}x}{\Omega}\sum_{\sigma}\sigma(\bar{\pi}_{\sigma}\bar{h}^{B}h^{A}\bar{z}_{-\sigma}+h.c.).

(76)

To derive the effective theory of holons, we first integrate over the high-energy field
\pi

The low-energy effective Lagrangian of

\displaystyle L_{hzA}=L_{0}+L_{h}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{d^{2}x}{\Omega}\sum_{\sigma}\Big\{(\lambda-4Q)|z_{\sigma}|^{2}+Qa^{2}|(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})z_{\sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\lambda+4Q}\big[|(\partial_{\tau}+iA_{\tau})z_{\sigma}|^{2}-t^{2}\bar{h}^{A}h^{B}\bar{h}^{B}h^{A}z_{\sigma}\bar{z}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\qquad+t\sigma(\bar{h}^{A}h^{B}z_{-\sigma}\partial_{\tau}z_{\sigma}-\bar{h}^{B}h^{A}\bar{z}_{-\sigma}\partial_{\tau}\bar{z}_{\sigma})\big]\Big\}.

(77)

After scaling
\tau\to\tau/c
(therefore
A_{\tau}\to cA_{\tau}
) and defining

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-16Q^{2}},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\Gamma/c,

(78)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\sqrt{Q(\lambda+4Q)}a,

\displaystyle g^{-1}

\displaystyle=Qa^{2}/(c\Omega),

\displaystyle S_{hzA}

\displaystyle=S_{h}+S_{zA}+S_{t},\quad S_{t}=S_{t}^{1}+S_{t}^{2},

(79)

\displaystyle S_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega}\int d^{3}x\sum_{s}\Big[\bar{h}^{s}(\partial_{\tau}+isA_{\tau})h^{s}+\frac{\lambda}{c}\bar{h}^{s}h^{s}\Big],

(80)

\displaystyle S_{zA}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g}\int d^{3}x\sum_{\sigma}\Big[m^{2}|z_{\sigma}|^{2}+|(\partial_{\mu}+iA_{\mu})z_{\sigma}|^{2}\Big],

(81)

\displaystyle S_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{cg}\int d^{3}x\sum_{\sigma}\sigma(\bar{h}^{A}h^{B}z_{-\sigma}\partial_{\tau}z_{\sigma}-\bar{h}^{B}h^{A}\bar{z}_{-\sigma}\partial_{\tau}\bar{z}_{\sigma})

(82)

Here

, and constant terms from

Note that at half-filling, the holons are eliminated, and the effective action reduces to Eq. (

\mathrm{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}
model
Read and Sachdev 1989

\displaystyle z_{\sigma}(\tau,x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho_{\sigma}(\tau,x)}\exp[i\phi_{\sigma}(\tau,x)],

(83)

\displaystyle\bar{z}_{\sigma}(\tau,x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho_{\sigma}(\tau,x)}\exp[-i\phi_{\sigma}(\tau,x)].

At zero temperature, the spinon (and thus the

and
\rho_{\downarrow}

\rho_{0}/2
, where

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}|(\partial_{\mu}+iA_{\mu})z_{\sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\frac{1}{4\rho_{\sigma}}\Big[(\partial_{\mu}\rho_{\sigma})^{2}+4\rho_{\sigma}^{2}(A_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\phi_{\sigma})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{\sigma}\rho_{\sigma}A_{\mu}^{2}\approx\rho_{0}A_{\mu}^{2}.

(84)

In the last line, we dropped the derivative
\partial_{\mu}\rho_{\sigma}

\sqrt{\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle S_{zA}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\rho_{0}}{g}\int d^{3}x\,(m^{2}+A_{\mu}^{2}),

(85)

\displaystyle S_{t}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{t^{2}\rho_{0}}{c^{2}g}\int d^{3}x\,\bar{h}^{A}h^{A}\bar{h}^{B}h^{B},

\displaystyle S_{hzA}

\displaystyle=S_{0}+S_{1}+S_{2},

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=S_{t}+\int d^{3}x\,m^{2}g^{-1}\rho_{0}

\displaystyle\quad+\Omega^{-1}\int d^{3}x\sum_{s}\Big(\bar{h}^{s}\partial_{\tau}h^{s}+\frac{\lambda}{c}n^{h}_{s}\Big),

(88)

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=i\Omega^{-1}\int d^{3}x\,A_{\tau}(n^{h}_{A}-n^{h}_{B}),

(89)

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\int d^{3}x\,\rho_{0}A_{\mu}^{2}.

\displaystyle Z_{hzA}\cong\int D\bar{h}\,Dh\,DA\,e^{-S_{hzA}}

\displaystyle=\int D\bar{h}\,Dh\,e^{-S_{0}}\underbrace{\int DA\,e^{-(S_{1}+S_{2})}}_{\equiv Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}\equiv\underbrace{\int D\mathbf{A}\exp\bigg\{\int d^{3}x\,(-g^{-1}\rho_{0}\mathbf{A}^{2})\bigg\}}_{\text{a constant factor}}

\displaystyle\times\int DA_{\tau}\exp\bigg\{\int d^{3}x\,\Big[{-\frac{1}{g}\rho_{0}A_{\tau}^{2}-\frac{i}{\Omega}(n^{h}_{A}-n^{h}_{B})A_{\tau}}\Big]\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg\{-\int d^{3}x\,\frac{g}{4\rho_{0}\Omega^{2}}(n^{h}_{A}-n^{h}_{B})^{2}\bigg\},

S_{\text{eff}}=S_{0}+\int d^{3}x\,\frac{g}{4\rho_{0}\Omega^{2}}(n^{h}_{A}-n^{h}_{B})^{2}.

(91)

We identify terms representing the interaction between
A

H_{\text{int}}^{AB}=\bigg[\frac{\alpha^{2}t^{2}\rho_{0}}{c^{2}g}-\frac{g}{2\rho_{0}\Omega^{2}}\bigg]\int d^{2}x\,n^{h}_{A}n^{h}_{B}.

(92)

The first term (inherited from Eq. (

) on a discrete lattice; the second term is attraction, produced by the gauge fluctuation.

The most important implication of Eq. (

below which the attraction due to gauge fluctuation should overcome the repulsion due to spinon exchange, leading to a superconducting order. For a large value of

\frac{\alpha^{2}t^{2}\rho_{0}}{c^{2}g}\lesssim\frac{g}{2\rho_{0}\Omega^{2}}.

(93)

Note that

at low temperature and small doping. Thus

\frac{t}{J}\lesssim\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{2}\rho_{0}}.

(94)

From the mean-field calculation at half-filling and zero temperature (see Appendix
B.2
), we obtain
\Delta\approx 1.2
, and
\rho_{0}\sim n_{0}\approx 0.5
, leading to
t/J\lesssim 1.7
. However, from the renormalization group perspective (by integrating over high-energy holons)
Nagaosa 1999
, the effective attractive between holons will renormalize to a bigger value at low energy. Thus the actual range of
t/J
that allows superconductivity is much larger than our naive estimation. The net attractive interaction leads to (
p+ip
)-wave pairing of the spinless holons
Cheng et al. 2010
. Together with the singlet-pairing of the spinons, we obtain the (
d+id
)-wave pairing of the electrons.

For the
A,B,D\neq 0
region, the order parameter
B
can be regarded as an additional Higgs field which further breaks down the staggered
U(1)
gauge field into a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field, and we believe that such an additional gauge field will not affect the attractive interaction in the presence of AFM long-range order. In fact, a nonzero B is also crucial for the holon mobility, e.g., for the introduction of holon kinetic term, and superconductivity is only possible in this phase. Nevertheless, at very small doping, our estimation for the effective holon interactions in previous sections is still valid since the correction induced by B is always proportional to holon concentration
\delta
.
Physically, as long as we start from the short-range RVB phase
A\neq 0
, the staggered
U(1)
gauge fluctuations will always induce attractive interactions between holons belonging to different sub-lattices, and this naturally explains why
d+id
SC order still survive even in the absence of AFM long-range order in this phase. We stress that the spin-charge separation scenario plays an essential role for the emergence of SC order here, especially for the region without AFM order, and there is no other conventional picture can explain such a novel
d+id
Sc oder emerged in a doped-Mott insulator!

Finally, for the region
A=0
and
B,D\neq 0
, the usual uniform
U(1)
gauge fluctuations will kill the FM long-range order at finite
t/J
and induce repulsive interactions among holons. Such a deconfined phase of
U(1)
gauge field does not support superconductivity and would be a natural candidate for the non-Fermi liquid phase emerged in the large
t/J
limit.

V
Conclusion and discussion

In conclusion, we investigate the global phase diagram of the
t
-
J
model on a honeycomb lattice at small doping region using the Grassmann tensor network numerical method. Furthermore, the slave-fermion mean-field theory is employed to account for the global phase diagram. The effective interacting holon theory is rigorously derived in the presence of the AFM background. The novel phenomena of spin-charge separation and attractive interaction among holons naturally explain the emergence of
d+id
SC orders. Remarkably, the predicted phase boundary for
d+id
SC orders via self-consistent mean-field theory approach and effective field theory analysis is intrinsically close to the results from Grassmann tensor product numerical simulations. We stress that the spin-charge separation mechanism occurs only in strongly correlated systems and is essentially different from the weak coupling mechanism in BCS theory.

We also would like to point out that the attractive interaction between holons belonging to different sub-lattices is induced by gauge fluctuations, and the
staggered

U(1)
gauge structure is crucial to the attractive interaction, since in this case the two sub-lattices
A
and
B
carry opposite gauge charges. Although our derivation is in the region for
A\neq 0
and
B,D=0
, we believe that this mechanism is still valid in the region
A,B,D\neq 0
, and it explains the emergence of SC orders even in the absence of long-range AFM order, as long as the system is still dominated by short-range AFM spin fluctuations.

It is well known that in the conventional BCS theory, the existence of zero-energy bound fermion state in the vortex of superconductor depends on the nontrivial topological class of the bulk.
According to the topological classification,
the
d+id
superconductivity discovered on the honeycomb lattice breaks the time reversal symmetry, but has the
SU(2)
spin-rotation symmetries. Thus it belongs to the class
C
of ten Altland-Zirnbauer classes
Chiu et al. 2016
;
Sato and Ando 2017
.
Althugh the class
C
has no zero-energy states in the vortex from the perspective of noninteracting topological classification, we argue that the unconventional superconductivity in the
t
-
J
model on a honeycomb lattice emerges from the spin-charge separation is a different case. The bosonic spinons carrying the spin degrees of freedom condense in the AFM region. The
p+ip
superconductivity of the spinless holons belongs to the class
D
and
there will be a zero-energy bound state in the vortex, similar to the Majorana zero mode in conventional
p+ip
superconductor
Kopnin and Salomaa 1991
;
Volovik 1999
;
Read and Green 2000
, which serves as a solid evidence for the spin-charge separation scenario. Experimentally, the doped spin
1/2
honeycomb lattice
antiferromagnet
\mathrm{InV_{1/3}Cu_{2/3}O_{3}}

Möller et al. 2008
would be an
appealing candidate to examine the emergence of topological Majorana zero mode in its vortex core.

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{t}{\beta N}\bigg)^{2}\sum_{k,\nu}\sum_{k^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}}\sum_{\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{1}}\sum_{\omega_{2},\omega^{\prime}_{2}}\frac{\delta_{(\nu+\omega_{1})-(\nu^{\prime}+\omega^{\prime}_{1})}\delta_{(\nu^{\prime}+\omega_{2})-(\nu+\omega^{\prime}_{2})}}{(\nu^{2}+\chi_{k}^{2})(\nu^{\prime 2}+\chi_{k^{\prime}}^{2})}\bar{h}^{B}_{k^{\prime}+q_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{1}}h^{A}_{k+q_{1},\omega_{1}}\bar{h}^{A}_{k+q_{2},\omega_{2}}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}+q_{2},\omega^{\prime}_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big[\Delta_{k}\Delta_{k^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}+k+k^{\prime}}\gamma_{q_{2}}+\Delta^{*}_{k}\Delta^{*}_{k^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}}\gamma_{q_{2}+k+k^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\qquad-(i\nu-\lambda)[i(\nu+\omega_{1}-\omega^{\prime}_{1})-\lambda]\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}}\gamma_{q_{2}}-(i\nu+\lambda)[i(\nu+\omega_{1}-\omega^{\prime}_{1})+\lambda]\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}+k+k^{\prime}}\gamma_{q_{2}+k+k^{\prime}}\Big],

(132)

where

{\sum_{\{\omega\}}}^{\prime}\equiv\sum_{\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{1}}\sum_{\omega_{2},\omega^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})-(\omega^{\prime}_{1}+\omega^{\prime}_{2})}

We keep only the
instantaneous
holon interaction by taking the
static limit
, i.e. setting
\omega_{1}=\omega^{\prime}_{1}
in the coefficient of
\bar{h}^{B}h^{A}\bar{h}^{A}h^{B}

\displaystyle S_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{t}{\beta N}\bigg)^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime},\nu}\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}}{\sum_{\{\omega\}}}^{\prime}\frac{\bar{h}^{B}_{k^{\prime}+q_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{1}}h^{A}_{k+q_{1},\omega_{1}}\bar{h}^{A}_{k+q_{2},\omega_{2}}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}+q_{2},\omega^{\prime}_{2}}}{(\nu^{2}+\chi_{k}^{2})(\nu^{2}+\chi_{k^{\prime}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\Delta_{k}\Delta_{k^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}+k+k^{\prime}}\gamma_{q_{2}}+\Delta^{*}_{k}\Delta^{*}_{k^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}}\gamma_{q_{2}+k+k^{\prime}}-(i\nu-\lambda)^{2}\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}}\gamma_{q_{2}}-(i\nu+\lambda)^{2}\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}+k+k^{\prime}}\gamma_{q_{2}+k+k^{\prime}}\Big].

(133)

In the zero-temperature limit, the summation over
\nu

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\nu}

\displaystyle\to\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi},

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(\nu^{2}+\chi_{k}^{2})(\nu^{2}+\chi_{k^{\prime}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\chi_{k}\chi_{k^{\prime}}(\chi_{k}+\chi_{k^{\prime}})},

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\frac{(i\nu\pm\lambda)^{2}}{(\nu^{2}+\chi_{k}^{2})(\nu^{2}+\chi_{k^{\prime}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}-\chi_{k}\chi_{k^{\prime}}}{2\chi_{k}\chi_{k^{\prime}}(\chi_{k}+\chi_{k^{\prime}})}.

We can then inverse Fourier transform
\omega
to
\tau

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}{\sum_{\{\omega\}}}^{\prime}\bar{h}^{B}_{k^{\prime}+q_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{1}}\bar{h}^{A}_{k+q_{2},\omega_{2}}h^{A}_{k+q_{1},\omega_{1}}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}+q_{2},\omega^{\prime}_{2}}

(134)

Therefore (the order of

\displaystyle S_{h}^{2}=-\frac{t^{2}}{2N^{2}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}}\frac{\bar{h}^{B}_{k^{\prime}+q_{1}}\bar{h}^{A}_{k+q_{2}}h^{A}_{k+q_{1}}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}+q_{2}}}{\chi_{k}\chi_{k^{\prime}}(\chi_{k}+\chi_{k^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\quad+(\chi_{k}\chi_{k^{\prime}}-\lambda^{2})(\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}}\gamma_{q_{2}}+\gamma^{*}_{q_{1}+k+k^{\prime}}\gamma_{q_{2}+k+k^{\prime}})\Big].

Redefining summation variables:

\left.\begin{aligned} k_{1}&=k+q_{1}\\
k_{2}&=k^{\prime}+q_{2}\\
q&=k^{\prime}-k\\
p&=-k^{\prime}\end{aligned}\right\}\ \Rightarrow\ \left\{\begin{aligned} k&=-p-q\\
k^{\prime}&=-p\\
q_{1}&=k_{1}+p+q\\
q_{2}&=k_{2}+p\end{aligned}\right.,

we obtain

\displaystyle S_{h}^{2}=-\frac{t^{2}}{2N^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}\sum_{p,q}\frac{\bar{h}^{B}_{k_{1}+q}\bar{h}^{A}_{k_{2}-q}h^{A}_{k_{1}}h^{B}_{k_{2}}}{\chi_{p+q}\chi_{p}(\chi_{p+q}+\chi_{p})}

\displaystyle\quad+(\chi_{p+q}\chi_{p}-\lambda^{2})(\gamma^{*}_{k_{1}+p+q}\gamma_{k_{2}+p}+\gamma^{*}_{k_{1}-p}\gamma_{k_{2}-p-q})\Big].

(136)

Here we used

\chi_{-k}=\chi_{k},\quad\gamma_{-k}=\gamma^{*}_{k},\quad\Delta_{-k}=\Delta^{*}_{k}.

From this effective action, we read off terms in the Hamiltonian that represent interaction between holons (rename

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{\text{int}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k,k^{\prime},q}V_{kk^{\prime}q}h^{B\dagger}_{k+q}h^{A\dagger}_{k^{\prime}-q}h^{A}_{k}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}},

(137)

\displaystyle V_{kk^{\prime}q}\equiv-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\sum_{p}\frac{1}{\chi_{p+q}\chi_{p}(\chi_{p+q}+\chi_{p})}

\displaystyle\ \ \times\Big[\Delta^{*}_{p+q}\Delta^{*}_{p}\gamma^{*}_{k-p}\gamma_{k^{\prime}+p}+\Delta_{p+q}\Delta_{p}\gamma^{*}_{k+p+q}\gamma_{k^{\prime}-p-q}

\displaystyle\quad+(\chi_{p+q}\chi_{p}-\lambda^{2})(\gamma^{*}_{k+p+q}\gamma_{k^{\prime}+p}+\gamma^{*}_{k-p}\gamma_{k^{\prime}-p-q})\Big].

(138)

This effective holon interaction is due to the exchange of two spinons of momenta
p

p+q

Then we get an alternative expression of

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{\text{int}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\{k,k^{\prime}\}}V_{k_{1}k_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}h^{B\dagger}_{k_{1}}h^{A\dagger}_{k_{2}}h^{A}_{k^{\prime}_{1}}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}_{2}},

(139)

\displaystyle V_{k_{1}k_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\equiv-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\sum_{p}\frac{\delta_{(k_{1}+k_{2})-(k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2})}}{\chi_{p}\chi_{p+k_{1}-k^{\prime}_{1}}(\chi_{p}+\chi_{p+k_{1}-k^{\prime}_{1}})}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\Delta^{*}_{p}\Delta^{*}_{p+k_{1}-k^{\prime}_{1}}\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}-p}\gamma_{k^{\prime}_{2}+p}+\Delta_{p}\Delta_{p+k_{1}-k^{\prime}_{1}}\gamma^{*}_{k_{1}+p}\gamma_{k_{2}-p}

\displaystyle\ +(\chi_{p}\chi_{p+k_{1}-k^{\prime}_{1}}-\lambda^{2})(\gamma^{*}_{k_{1}+p}\gamma_{k^{\prime}_{2}+p}+\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}-p}\gamma_{k_{2}-p})\Big].

(140)

Define
p^{\prime}\equiv p+k_{1}-k^{\prime}_{1}

(the momenta of
h^{A}

\displaystyle V_{k_{1}k_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\equiv-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\sum_{p}\bigg\{\frac{\delta_{(k_{1}+k_{2})-(k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2})}}{\chi_{p}\chi_{p^{\prime}}(\chi_{p}+\chi_{p^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\Delta^{*}_{p}\Delta^{*}_{p^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}-p}\gamma_{k_{2}+p^{\prime}}+\Delta_{p}\Delta_{p^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}+p^{\prime}}\gamma_{k_{2}-p}

\displaystyle\ +(\chi_{p}\chi_{p^{\prime}}-\lambda^{2})(\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}+p^{\prime}}\gamma_{k_{2}+p^{\prime}}+\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}-p}\gamma_{k_{2}-p})\Big]\bigg\}.

{\sum_{\{k\}}}^{\prime}\equiv\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{(k_{1}+k_{2})-(k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2})}

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{\text{int}}=\frac{1}{N}{\sum_{\{k\}}}^{\prime}V_{k_{1}k_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}h^{B\dagger}_{k_{1}}h^{A\dagger}_{k_{2}}h^{A}_{k^{\prime}_{1}}h^{B}_{k^{\prime}_{2}}.

(141)

\displaystyle V_{k_{1}k_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}=-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\sum_{p}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\chi_{p}\chi_{p^{\prime}}(\chi_{p}+\chi_{p^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\ \ \times\Big[J_{b}^{2}(\gamma^{*}_{p}\gamma^{*}_{p^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}-p}\gamma_{k_{2}+p^{\prime}}+\gamma_{p}\gamma_{p^{\prime}}\gamma^{*}_{k^{\prime}_{1}+p}\gamma_{k_{2}-p})

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{\delta}(\delta\cdot\mathbf{A})^{2}

Here

\delta
sums over nearest neighbors of a site on sub-lattice

(see Fig.
1
),

are any scalar fields and

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\sum_{s}\Big[\bar{h}^{s}(\partial_{\tau}+isA_{\tau})h^{s}+\lambda\bar{h}^{s}h^{s}\Big],

\displaystyle L_{t}

(149)

Next,

\displaystyle L_{J}\to-\frac{J\Delta}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\sum_{\delta,\sigma}\sigma

\displaystyle=-2\int d^{2}x\,\nabla\bar{b}^{A}_{\sigma}\cdot\nabla\bar{b}^{B}_{-\sigma},

(152)

we calculate the second order terms in

\displaystyle L_{J}^{2}=-\frac{J\Delta}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}x}{2\Omega}\sum_{\delta,\sigma}\sigma\bigg\{\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}[(-\tfrac{\delta}{2}\cdot\nabla)^{2}\bar{b}^{A}_{\sigma}]\bar{b}^{B}_{-\sigma}+\bar{b}^{A}_{\sigma}\tfrac{1}{2}[(\tfrac{\delta}{2}\cdot\nabla)^{2}\bar{b}^{B}_{-\sigma}]+(-\tfrac{\delta}{2}\cdot\nabla)\bar{b}^{A}_{\sigma}(\tfrac{\delta}{2}\cdot\nabla)\bar{b}^{B}_{-\sigma}\Big]

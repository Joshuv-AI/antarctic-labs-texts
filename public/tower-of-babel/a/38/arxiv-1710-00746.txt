Angular distribution of polarised
Λ

Abstract
Rare b → s ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − b\rightarrow s\ell^{+}\ell^{-} flavour-changing-neutral-current processes provide important tests of the Standard Model of particle physics. Angular observables in exclusive b → s ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − b\rightarrow s\ell^{+}\ell^{-} processes can be particularly powerful as they allow hadronic uncertainties to be controlled. Amongst the exclusive processes that have been studied by experiments, the decay Λ b → Λ ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − \mathchar 28931\relax_{b}\!\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\ell^{+}\ell^{-} is unique in that the Λ b \mathchar 28931\relax_{b} baryon can be produced polarised. In this paper, we derive an expression for the angular distribution of the Λ b → Λ ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − \mathchar 28931\relax_{b}\!\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\ell^{+}\ell^{-} decay for the case where the Λ b \mathchar 28931\relax_{b} baryon is produced polarised. This extends the number of angular observables in this decay from 10 to 34. Standard Model expectations for the new observables are provided and the sensitivity of the observables is explored under a variety of new physics models. At low-hadronic recoil, four of the new observables have a new short distance dependence that is absent in the unpolarised case. The remaining observables depend on the same short distance contributions as the unpolarised observables, but with different dependence on hadronic form-factors. These relations provide possibilities for novel tests of the SM that could be carried out with the data that will become available at the LHC or a future e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider.

\begin{split}h^{V,J}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}&=\bar{\ell}(\lambda_{2})\gamma^{\mu}\ell(\lambda_{1})\varepsilon_{\mu}^{*}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\\

(3)

These amplitudes evaluate to

1

\displaystyle h^{V,0}_{+1/2,+1/2}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle h^{V,0}_{+1/2,-1/2}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle h^{A,0}_{+1/2,-1/2}=0\,,

(4)

\displaystyle h^{V,1}_{+1/2,+1/2}

\displaystyle M_{i}=\frac{3}{32\pi^{2}}\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int$}}\nolimits\limits\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{34}M_{j}f_{j}(\vec{\Omega})\right)g_{i}(\vec{\Omega})\mathrm{d}\vec{\Omega}

7
Standard Model predictions

In order to describe the SM contribution to the decay amplitudes, an effective field theory approach is used.
The Hamiltonian for the decay is factorised into local four-fermion operators and Wilson coefficients (see for example Ref.
[
31
]
).
The Wilson coefficients describe the short-distance contributions from the heavy SM particles.

Numerical values for the SM predictions, in the case that
P_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}=1
, are provided in Appendix
B
in two
q^{2}
ranges:
at large hadronic recoil, in the range
1<q^{2}<6\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}^{2}/c^{4}
, and at low hadronic recoil, in the range
15<q^{2}<20\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}^{2}/c^{4}
.
To evaluate SM predictions for the different angular observables we use the
EOS
flavour tool
[
32
]
.
At low hadronic recoil, the SM calculations employ an operator product expansion of the four-quark contributions to the matrix element in powers of
\mathchar 28931\relax_{\rm QCD}/\sqrt{q^{2}}

[
33
]
.
At large recoil,
EOS
uses some of the known
\alpha_{s}
corrections to charm loop processes.
However, potentially large contributions from hard spectator scattering
[
34
]
and soft gluon emission
[
35
]
are neglected.
The form-factors for the
\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax
transition are taken from a recent Lattice QCD calculation in Ref.
[
27
]
.
These form-factors enable the observables to be computed with high-precision.
The form-factors at large hadronic recoil have also been calculated in the framework of light-cone-sum-rules, see for example Refs.
[
36
]
and
[
37
]
.
The SM Wilson coefficients are computed in
EOS
to NNLO in QCD.
The
\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}
lifetime and CKM matrix elements are taken from the latest experimental values
[
26
]
.
The quark masses are taken in the
\overline{\rm MS}

7.1
Low-hadronic recoil

At low hadronic recoil the observables are precisely predicted in the SM.
The uncertainties on the predictions are worse at large recoil, where a large extrapolation in
q^{2}
of the form-factors is needed.
Figures
2
–
9
in Appendix
C
demonstrate how the observables depend on NP contributions to the Wilson coefficients.
In the large-recoil region there is sensitivity to
C_{9}^{\rm NP}
from both the polarised and unpolarised observables.
Interestingly, the observables
M_{23}
and
M_{27}
can also distinguish between two of the possibilities that are favoured by global fits to
b\rightarrow s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
processes: where
C_{9}^{\rm NP}\simeq-1
with
C_{10}^{\rm NP}=0
and where
C_{9}^{\rm NP}=-C_{10}^{\rm NP}\simeq-1

[
41
,
42
,
43
]
.
In the low-recoil range the sensitivity to
C_{9}^{\rm NP}
is reduced.

In Ref.
[
2
]
, the authors point out that the observables at low hadronic recoil place constraints on six combinations of Wilson coefficients

\begin{split}\rho^{\pm}_{1}&=|C_{\rm V}\pm C^{\prime}_{\rm V}|^{2}+|C_{10}\pm C^{\prime}_{10}|^{2}\\
\rho_{2}&={\rm Re}\left(C_{\rm V}C^{*}_{10}-C^{\prime}_{\rm V}C^{\prime*}_{10}\right)-i{\rm Im}\left(C_{\rm V}C^{\prime*}_{\rm V}+C_{10}C^{\prime*}_{10}\right)\\
\rho^{\pm}_{3}&=2{\rm Re}\left((C_{V}\pm C^{\prime}_{\rm V})(C_{10}\pm C^{\prime}_{10})^{*}\right)\\
\rho_{4}&=|C_{\rm V}|^{2}-|C^{\prime}_{\rm V}|^{2}+|C_{10}|^{2}-|C^{\prime}_{10}|^{2}-i{\rm Im}\left(C_{\rm V}C^{*}_{10}-C^{\prime}_{\rm V}C^{\prime*}_{10}\right)~,\end{split}

(24)

where
C_{\rm V}
contains contributions from
C_{7}
and
C_{9}
.
The primed coefficients correspond to right-handed currents whose contribution is vanishingly small in the SM.
The short-distance dependence of
K_{1}
–
K_{34}
on
\rho_{1}^{\pm}
,
\rho_{3}^{\pm}
,
\rho_{2}
and
\rho_{4}
is provided for completeness in Appendix
D
.

If the
\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}
is unpolarised, the decay rate is insensitive to the short-distance contribution
{\rm Im}(\rho_{2})
but provides sensitivity to
\rho^{\pm}_{1}
,
{\rm Re}(\rho_{2})
,
\rho^{\pm}_{3}
,
{\rm Re}(\rho_{4})
and
{\rm Im}(\rho_{4})
.
The polarised observables also depend on these short-distance contributions but have different form-factor dependencies.
This permits a new set of checks of the OPE and the form-factors.
The short-distance combination
{\rm Im}(\rho_{2})
can also be determined from
M_{19}
,
M_{25}
,
M_{30}
and
M_{34}
.
Furthermore, in
K_{1}
–
K_{10}
the short-distance contributions
\rho_{1}^{+}
and
\rho_{1}^{-}
always appear together as a sum.
Using the polarised observables ,
\rho_{1}^{+}
and
\rho_{1}^{-}
can be separated,
e.g.
by using

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{2}+\frac{2}{\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax}P_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}}K_{33}&=16s_{-}|f_{\perp}^{V}|^{2}\rho_{1}^{+}~,\\
K_{2}-\frac{2}{\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax}P_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}}K_{33}&=16s_{+}|f_{\perp}^{A}|^{2}\rho_{1}^{-}~,\end{split}

(25)

where
f_{\perp}^{V}
and
f_{\perp}^{A}
are helicity form-factors (see for example Ref.
[
44
]
).
A similar trick can be used to separate
\rho_{3}^{+}
and
\rho_{3}^{-}
using
K_{24}
and
K_{8}
.
It is also possible to form new short-distance relationships, in which the form-factors cancel by taking ratios of the
K_{i}
,

\displaystyle\frac{K_{16}}{K_{34}}=2\frac{{\rm Re}(\rho_{2})}{{\rm Im}(\rho_{2})}~,\quad\frac{K_{25}}{K_{22}}=-\frac{{\rm Im}(\rho_{2})}{{\rm Im}(\rho_{4})}~,\quad\frac{K_{23}}{K_{10}}=-\frac{{\rm Re}(\rho_{4})}{{\rm Im}(\rho_{4})}P_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}~.

(26)

The short-distance combinations
\rho_{2}
and
\rho_{4}
can then be determined up-to their overall normalisation, independent of the hadronic form-factors, using Eq.
26
and the relationship

\displaystyle\frac{K_{3}}{K_{5}}=-\frac{1}{\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax}}\frac{{\rm Re}(\rho_{2})}{{\rm Re}(\rho_{4})}

(27)

from Ref.
[
2
]
.
Similarly, one can form short-distance relationships that depend only on
\rho_{1}^{\pm}
and
\rho_{3}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}K_{8}+\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax}K_{24}}{K_{27}-K_{17}}=-\frac{\rho_{3}^{-}}{\rho_{1}^{-}}\quad,\quad\frac{P_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}K_{8}-\alpha_{\mathchar 28931\relax}K_{24}}{K_{27}+K_{17}}=\frac{\rho_{3}^{+}}{\rho_{1}^{+}}~.

(28)

Alternatively, it is possible to form ratios that depend only on the form-factors and not on the short-distance physics.
For example,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{K_{7}}{K_{5}}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(m_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}+m_{\mathchar 28931\relax})}{\sqrt{q^{2}}}\frac{f_{0}^{V}}{f_{\perp}^{V}}-\frac{(m_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}-m_{\mathchar 28931\relax})}{\sqrt{q^{2}}}\frac{f_{0}^{A}}{f_{\perp}^{A}}\right)~,\\
\frac{K_{23}}{K_{5}}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(m_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}+m_{\mathchar 28931\relax})}{\sqrt{q^{2}}}\frac{f_{0}^{V}}{f_{\perp}^{V}}+\frac{(m_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}-m_{\mathchar 28931\relax})}{\sqrt{q^{2}}}\frac{f_{0}^{A}}{f_{\perp}^{A}}\right)P_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}~\end{split}

(29)

allow the ratios
f_{0}^{V}/f_{\perp}^{V}
and
f_{0}^{A}/f_{\perp}^{A}
to be determined independent of the
\rho_{i}
.

7.2
Photon-polarisation at large hadronic-recoil

At very large hadronic recoil (
q^{2}\ll 1\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}^{2}/c^{4}
), the angular distribution of the
\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}\!\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay is sensitive primarily to the Wilson coefficients
C_{7}
and
C^{\prime}_{7}
due to a pole-like enhancement of the amplitudes.
The observable
K_{33}
is proportional to
{\rm Re}(C_{7}C^{\prime}_{7})
and can therefore provide a null test of the size of
C^{\prime}_{7}
(in the same way as the
S_{3}
observable in the
B^{0}\!\rightarrow K^{*0}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay).
In this case, however, the observable is suppressed by the size of
P_{\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}}
.

9
Conclusion

In this paper we have derived an expression for the angular distribution of the
\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}\!\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
in the case of non-zero production polarisation.
This extends the number of observables in the decay from 10 to 34.
These observables can be determined from moments of the
\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}\!\rightarrow\mathchar 28931\relax\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
angular distribution.
Explicit expressions have been provided for the observables in terms of the angular moments to enable an experiment to determine the new observables from their dataset.
A phenomenological analysis has also been performed to illustrate how these observables might vary in extensions of the Standard Model.
The analysis shows that there is interesting new sensitivity that can be gained if the
\mathchar 28931\relax_{b}
baryon is produced polarised.

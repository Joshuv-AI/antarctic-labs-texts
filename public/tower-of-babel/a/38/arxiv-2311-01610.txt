Stabilization of codimension of persistence barcodes
Justin Allman, Anran Huang

Abstract
. Given a pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module over a totally ordered set S S , a theorem of Crawley-Boevey guarantees the existence of a barcode. When the set S S is finite, the persistence module is an equioriented type-A quiver representation and the barcode identifies a distinguished point in an algebraic variety. We prove a formula for the codimension of this variety inside an ambient space of comparable representations which depends only on the combinatorics of the barcode. We therefore extend the notion of codimension to persistence modules over any set S S and prove that this extension is well-defined and can be effectively computed via a stabilization property of approximating quiver representations. Further, we prove realization theorems by constructing explicit examples via persistent homology of data for which the codimension can realize any natural number as its value.

1.
Introduction

Fix a field
\mathbb{K}
and a partially ordered set
S
. A
persistence module over
S
, or an
S
-persistence module
, consists of

(i)

an indexed collection of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces
\left(V_{s}~|~s\in S\right)

(ii)

together with a collection of
\mathbb{K}
-linear mappings
\left(f_{st}:V_{s}\to V_{t}~|~s\leq t\right)

where
f_{ss}
is the identity map on
V_{s}
and where we require the composition law
f_{tu}\circ f_{st}=f_{su}
whenever
s,t,u\in S
with
s\leq t\leq u
. When
V
is an
S
-persistence module and
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
, we obtain an
S^{\prime}
-persistence module called the
restriction
of
V
to
S^{\prime}
, denoted
V|S^{\prime}
by considering only those spaces and maps with indices in
S^{\prime}
.

Persistence modules are a central mathematical object of study in the burgeoning field of
topological data analysis
(TDA) due to their appearance in computations for the
persistent homology
(PH) of data; see Section
2
for examples. In this paper we introduce a new flavor of stability for persistence modules by considering a special class of restrictions which closely approximate a given persistence module.

The traditional (and extremely useful!) notion of stability in persistence theory (PT) asserts that important “signatures” (e.g., barcodes and persistence diagrams) exhibit a statistical robustness. However, when
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
is a finite set, algebro-geometric invariants from the representation theory (RT) of quivers can be computed. Morally, we imagine

•

PT stability
. If two
S
-persistence modules only differ “slightly”, then their persistence signatures only differ “slightly”.

•

RT stability
. For finite subsets
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
, the value of a representation-theoretic invariant stabilizes on all “nice enough” restrictions
V|S^{\prime}
.

We consider a specific representation theory / algebro-geometric invariant called
codimension
for which we are able to give a precise formulation of an RT stability property. To our knowledge, the present work is the first example of any algebro-geometric invariant which admits RT stability.

1.1.
Persistence theory

Throughout the paper we assume that our persistence modules are
pointwise finite-dimensional
; i.e., all of the vector spaces
V_{s}
are finite-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-spaces. Furthermore, we will restrict our attention to the case when
S
is a
totally
ordered set. In fact, in most of our examples
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. The quantities

r_{st}=\rank(f_{st}:V_s \to V_t)\text{~for $s\leq t$}

are called the
persistent Betti numbers
, and
the assumption that
\dim(V_{s})<\infty
for all
s\in S
implies

r_{st}\leq\min(\dim(V_{s}),\dim(V_{t}))<\infty\text{~for all $s\leq t$}.

A subset
I\subseteq S
is an
interval
if
I\neq\emptyset
and
s\leq t\leq u
with
s,u\in I
implies
t\in I
. Corresponding to an interval
I\subseteq S
, we define a distinguished
S
-persistence module, the so-called

with

whenever

and
V_{u}=0

. We set

for

(and

otherwise). A theorem of Crawley-Boevey states

Theorem 1.1

(
[
12
]
, Theorem 1.1)
.

Every pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module over a totally ordered set
S
is a direct sum of interval modules. ∎

The collection of these intervals is called the

of the persistence module, and because of this, in the sequel we will also refer to a single interval in a persistence module decomposition as a

. Throughout, for any bar
I\subseteq S
we denote its

or

and its

or
death time
by

. Whenever an interval

satisfies

, we call

a
pointbar
. These occur whenever there exists a nonzero

which is simultaneously in
\coker(f_{sa})
for all
s<a
and
\ker(f_{at})
for all
a<t
.

The barcode provides a useful “signature” of the corresponding persistence module due to its computability (in most applications) and its aforementioned robust PT-stability property. The most general result on PT-stability known to the authors is Theorem 5.23 and 5.24 of
[
11
]
.

,

In this paper, we focus on one such algebro-geometric invariant of the isoclass — namely, its

\Rep=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}\Hom\left(V_{s_{i}},V_{s_{i+1}}\right).

1.3.
Summary of results

A formula for the codimension of an isoclass
\Omega\subset\Rep
in terms of the persistent Betti numbers has been known since the 1980s. However, no description of the codimension has so far been given explicitly in terms of the combinatorics of the barcode. Our first main theorem presents exactly such a description.

We define two bars to be an
interacting pair
if they intersect inside
S
in a prescribed way; see our Definition
4.1
. We then prove

Main Theorem 1

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

Given an equioriented type-A quiver representation, the codimension of its isoclass
\Omega\subset\Rep
is equal to the number of interacting pairs of bars in its barcode.

Since Theorem
1.1
guarantees that a barcode exists for
any
pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module
V
, by extension, we naturally apply the conclusion of Theorem
4.2
to define the
quiver codimension

\Qcodim(V)
to be the number of interacting bars in its barcode; see Definition
5.2
.

Our second main theorem is a precise statement of RT-stability for the codimension. In the statement below, we wait to give the full definitions of
barcode-finite
and
h
-approximation
until the main body of the paper; see Definitions
5.1
and
6.2
, respectively.

Main Theorem 2

(Theorem
6.3
)
.

Consider the barcode-finite
S
-persistence module
V
. Suppose that the finite subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
admits an
h
-approximation
V^{\prime}=V|S^{\prime}
. Provided that
h\geq 2

\codim(\Omega^{\prime})=\Qcodim(V)

where
\Omega^{\prime}
is the isoclass of the quiver representation associated to
V^{\prime}
.

Theorem
6.3
guarantees that the quiver codimension of any persistence module can be effectively computed directly from
quiver representations
by taking appropriate finite sets
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
. The RT-stability property is: the size of
S^{\prime}
may grow to better approximate
S
, and hence the number of nodes of the underlying quiver must grow which, in turn, implies that the ambient representation space
\Rep
from Equation (
1
) must grow (in its number of direct summands). Yet, remarkably the
codimension stabilizes
for the corresponding isoclasses
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Rep
.

Important classes of examples: persistent homology

A
filtration

Z
of topological spaces indexed by
S
is a collection of topological spaces
(Z_{s}~|~s\in S)
along with inclusions
Z_{s}\subseteq Z_{t}
whenever
s\leq t
. For any non-negative integer
d
, we can set
V_{s}=H_{d}(Z_{s},\mathbb{K})
to be the
degree
d
simplicial homology of the space
Z_{s}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
. Further, whenever
s\leq t
, we obtain induced mappings in degree
d
homology
f_{st}:V_{s}\to V_{t}
from the inclusions
Z_{s}\subseteq Z_{t}
; see e.g.,
[
19
, Section 2.1]
as a general reference on simplicial homology. The
S
-persistence module so obtained from this
(V_{s})
and this
(f_{st})
is called the
(degree
d
) persistent homology
(PH) of the filtration and we will denote it by
V=\PH_{d}(Z)
; see e.g.,
[
18
]
as a general reference on persistent homology.

Example 2.1
.

Let
Y
denote any topological space and let
g:Y\to S
. Form the
closed sublevel sets

Y_{s}=\{y\in Y~|~g(y)\leq s\}
. Taken together, the natural nestings
Y_{s}\subseteq Y_{t}
whenever
s\leq t
are called the
sublevel set filtration of the pair
(Y,g)
. Denoting this filtration by
Y^{g}
, we obtain an
S
-persistence module
\PH_{d}(Y^{g})
.

For the next two examples let
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
denote the set of non-negative real numbers and assume
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Consider a metric space
(M,\rho)
and for
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
x\in M
, write
B_{s}(x)=\left\{y\in M~|~\rho(y,x)\leq s\right\}
to be the ball centered on
x
of radius
s
. Let
X=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
denote a finite set of data in
M
; i.e.,
X
is a
point cloud in
M
.

Example 2.2
.

For each
s\in S
, the
Čech complex
is the simplicial complex having a
k
-simplex with vertices
x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}
whenever
\bigcap_{j=0}^{k}B_{s}(x_{i_{j}})\neq\emptyset
. The nerve theorem
[
6
]
implies that the Čech complex is homotopy equivalent to the union
C_{s}=\bigcup_{x\in X}B_{s}(x)
; see e.g.,
[
18
, Section 1.3]
. The filtration given by the inclusions
C_{s}\subseteq C_{t}
is called the
Čech filtration
. Let us denote this filtration by
\mathrm{\check{C}}(X,M)
, and again, we obtain an
S
-persistence module
\PH_{d}(\mathrm{\check{C}}(X,M))
.

Example 2.3
.

Alternatively, for each
s\in S
, we can define the
Vietoris-Rips (VR) complex

X_{s}
to be the simplicial complex having a
k
-simplex with vertices
x_{i_{0}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}
whenever
B_{s}(x_{i_{j}})\cap B_{s}(x_{i_{\ell}})\neq\emptyset
for each pair
1\leq j<\ell\leq k
. The nested sequence
X_{s}\subseteq X_{t}
whenever
s\leq t
defines the
Vietoris-Rips (VR) filtration
which we denote by
\mathrm{VR}(X,M)
. Analogous to the previous examples,
\PH_{d}(\mathrm{VR}(X,M))
is an
S
-persistence module.

The VR filtration is a computationally efficient alternative to the Čech filtration since only the pairwise distances between the points need be computed. Fortunately, the VR filtration also provides a controlled approximation to the Čech filtration; see
[
13
, Theorem 2.5]
for a precise formulation.

Quivers, representations, isoclasses, and codimension

The authors suggest both of the books
[
14
]
and
[
25
]
as general references for the theory of quivers and their representations. Much of the background material of this section is an amalgam of those sources.

3.1.
Quiver generalities

A
quiver

Q
is a directed graph with a finite set of vertices
Q_{0}
and a finite set of directed edges
Q_{1}
. The directed edges are called
arrows
; hence the moniker “quiver”. For any arrow
a\in Q_{1}
, we denote the vertex at its
head
by
ha\in Q_{0}
and at its
tail
by
ta\in Q_{0}
. A family of distinguished quivers are the so-called
equioriented type-A quivers
, depicted below

1\longrightarrow 2\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow n

where
Q_{0}=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and each arrow in
Q_{1}
has the form
(i)\to(i+1)
. The name is justified since all arrows point in the same direction; i.e., are
equioriented
, and readers familiar with representation theory of Lie algebras will recognize that these quivers are orientations of the
type-A Dynkin diagram

[
20
, Section 11]
.

3.2.
Representations

A
representation
of a quiver
Q
, or a
quiver representation
, is a choice of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces
V_{i}
at each vertex
i\in Q_{0}
along with choices of linear mappings
f_{a}:V_{ta}\to V_{ha}
for each arrow
a\in Q_{1}
. The list
\alpha=(\dim(V_{i}))_{i\in Q_{0}}
is called the
dimension vector
of the quiver representation. We observe that a representation of an equioriented type-A quiver is exactly a pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module over the set
S=\{1<2<\ldots<n\}
.

3.3.
Isoclasses and orbits

Fixing the dimension vector
\alpha
, but allowing the mappings
f_{a}
to vary, we form the
ambient space of
\alpha
-dimensional quiver representations

(2)

\Rep_{\alpha}(Q)=\bigoplus_{a\in Q_{1}}\Hom\left(V_{ta},V_{ha}\right)

The algebraic group
G_{\alpha}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}GL(V_{i})
acts naturally on
\Rep_{\alpha}(Q)
by simultaneous change of basis in the head and tail of each arrow; in particular, for
g=(g_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\in G_{\alpha}
and
f=(f_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}}\in\Rep_{\alpha}(Q)
we have the left action

g\cdot f=(g_{ha}f_{a}g_{ta}^{-1})_{a\in Q_{1}}.

We will drop the decorations for
\alpha
and
Q
from
\Rep_{\alpha}(Q)
in the sequel; they will be understood from context. Instead we write, cf. Equation (
1
),

\Rep=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}\Hom\left(V_{i},V_{i+1}\right).

It is a classical result of quiver representation theory that two representations are isomorphic (in the category of quiver representations) if and only if they live in the same
G_{\alpha}
-orbit in
\Rep

[
14
, Corollary 1.3.1]
. Accordingly, we take a
G_{\alpha}
-orbit in
\Rep
as the definition of an
isomorphism class
, aka
isoclass
, for quiver representations.

3.4.
Codimension of isoclasses

Specifically for equioriented type-A representations, the list of persistent Betti numbers
r_{i,j}=\rank(V_i \to V_j)
for
i<j
must be constant on any isoclass since basis changes do not alter ranks. Indeed, the isoclasses are in one-to-one correspondence with a choice of persistent Betti numbers
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Taken together, the seminal papers of Abeasis–Del Fra
[
1
,
2
]
, Abeasis–Del Fra–Kraft
[
3
]
, and Lakshmibai–Magyar
[
23
]
guarantee that, over any base field
\mathbb{K}
, the isoclasses in
\Rep
form algebraic varieties which are Cohen-Macauley, and hence, have pure codimension as subvarieties. We now recall a formula for this codimension, known since the 1980s, in terms of the persistent Betti numbers; see
[
1
]
,
[
23
, Proposition 1]
, and
[
8
, Equation (1.3)]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Given an isoclass
\Omega\subset\Rep
, we have

\codim(\Omega)=\sum_{i<j}(r_{i,j-1}-r_{i,j})(r_{i+1,j}-r_{i,j}).\qed

Organize the persistent Betti numbers in a triangular
rank array

[
8
, Section 2]
.

\matrixquantity{\lx@physics@matrix
r_{11} && r_{22} && r_{33} && \cdots&& r_{nn} \\
& r_{12} && r_{23} && \cdots&& r_{n-1,n} & \\
&& r_{13} && \cdots&& r_{n-2,n} && \\
&&& \ddots&& \stodd&&& \\
&&&& r_{1n} &&&& \\
\endlx@physics@matrix}

We note that
\dim(V_{i})=r_{ii}
and observe that the rank conditions
r_{i,j}=\rank(V_i \to V_j)
only make sense and define a meaningful quiver representation if

r_{i,j}\leq\min(r_{i,j-1},r_{i+1,j})

for all
i<j
. That is, the triangular rank array must be weakly decreasing when moving downward along all diagonals. If we define the triangle of integers
T_{i,j}
to be the local portion of the rank array depicting

\matrixquantity{\lx@physics@matrix
r_{i,j-1} && r_{i+1,j} \\
& r_{i,j} &
\endlx@physics@matrix}

and define the
weight

w(T)=(m-r)(n-r)
for any triangle of integers

T=\left(\matrixquantity{\lx@physics@matrix
m && n \\
& r &
\endlx@physics@matrix}\right)

we see that the rank array allows us to compute the codimension by the formula

(3)

\codim(\Omega)=\sum_{i<j}w(T_{i,j}).

4.
A new formula for the codimension of an isoclass

For any index set
S
, given an interval
I\subseteq S
with birth
b
and death
d
, we will write
I=\langle b,d\rangle
. We adopt the convention, here and throughout the paper, that angle brackets in our interval notation can be interpreted either as inclusion or exclusion of the endpoints. The results that follow are independent of this choice. However, in Section
7
, we will require closed brackets for pointbars
[a,a]
. Further, recall that we will use the words “bar” and “interval” interchangeably. Suppose that
I=\langle b,d\rangle
and
J=\langle\beta,\delta\rangle
. Without loss of generality, assume that
b\leq\beta
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
I
and
J
form an
interacting pair
of bars if either of the following conditions apply.

(i)

b<\beta\leq d<\delta
.

(ii)

d<\beta
, but there is no element of
S
between
d
and
\beta
.

In Case (i) we say that
I
and
J
are
interlaced
; in Case (ii) we say that
I
and
J
are
non-separated
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Given an equioriented type-A quiver representation, the codimension of its isoclass
\Omega\subset\Rep
is equal to the number of interacting pairs of bars in its barcode.

Proof.

We observe that for each
s<t
, the persistent Betti numbers have the property that

(4)

r_{st}=\#\{\text{bars~}I\text{~with~}b(I)\leq s\text{~and~}d(I)\geq t\}.

and recall that in the case of quiver representations, we consider a pointwise finite-dimensional persistence module over the finite set
S=\{1<\cdots<n\}
where
n
is the number of nodes of the quiver. It follows that

(a)

(r_{i,j-1}-r_{i,j})
counts bars with birth
\leq i
but death
exactly equal to

j-1
;

(b)

(r_{i+1,j}-r_{i,j})
counts bars with birth
exactly equal to

i+1
but death
\geq j
.

First, consider an
interlaced
pair
\langle b,d\rangle
and
\langle\beta,\delta\rangle
with
\beta=i+1
and
d=j-1
. The interlaced criterion guarantees that
b<i+1\leq j-1<\delta
. Hence, we must have
j-i\geq 2
as well as
b\leq i
and
\delta\geq j
. For a fixed choice of
i
and
j
, using observations (a) and (b) following Equation (
4
), we conclude that the number of pairs of interlaced bars of the form
\langle b,j-1\rangle
and
\langle i+1,d\rangle
is the product of the quantity

{\left(r_{i,j-1}-r_{i,j}\right)}={\#\{\text{bars with $b\leq i$, $d=j-1$}\}}

with the quantity

{\left(r_{i+1,j}-r_{i,j}\right)}={\#\{\text{bars with $\beta=i+1$, $\delta\geq j$}\}}.

Observe that this product is exactly
w(T_{i,j})
. In other words, the sum
\sum w(T_{i,j})
taken over pairs with
j-i\geq 2
computes the total number of interlaced pairs of bars.

Second, consider a
non-separated
pair
\langle b,d\rangle
and
\langle\beta,\delta\rangle
. The non-separated criterion implies that
d=k
and
\beta=k+1
for some integer
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. For this
k
, and again using observations (a) and (b), the number of non-separated pairs of the form
\langle b,k\rangle
and
\langle k+1,d\rangle
is the product of the quantity

{\left(r_{k,k}-r_{k,k+1}\right)}=\#\{\text{bars with $b\leq k$, $d=k$}\}

with the quantity

{\left(r_{k+1,k+1}-r_{k,k+1}\right)}=\#\{\text{bars with $\beta=k+1$, $\delta\geq k+1$}\}.

This product is
w(T_{k,k+1})
. In other words, the sum
\sum w(T_{i,j})
taken over pairs with
j-i=1
computes the total number of non-separated pairs of bars.

Combining these two computations, we obtain that

\displaystyle\#\{\text{interacting pairs}\}

\displaystyle=\#\{\text{interlaced pairs}\}+\#\{\text{non-separated pairs}\}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{j-i\geq 2}w(T_{i,j})\right]+\left[\sum_{j-i=1}w(T_{i,j})\right]=\sum_{i<j}w(T_{i,j}).

According to Equation (
3
), this last expression is equal to
\codim(\Omega)
.
∎

lacing diagram and showed that the codimension of an isoclass could be computed by counting crossing strands in a
minimal
extension; we will not define the minimality condition in the present paper, but see
[
22
, Theorem 3.8]
and
[
10
, Section 2]
for details. While the Knutson–Miller–Shimozono formulation is combinatorial, our Theorem
4.2
requires
only
the knowledge of the birth and death times to compute the codimension, and not any knowledge of a special extension of the barcode. Nevertheless, it would be interesting to compare our Theorem
4.2
with the construction of a minimal extended lacing diagram; however, this setting is too general for the TDA framework and investigating it is beyond the scope of the current paper.

5.
Persistence modules and Qcodim

Let
V
denote a pointwise finite-dimensional
S
-persistence module. The work of Crawley-Boevey
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
, which we have recalled in the present paper as Theorem
1.1
of the introduction, implies that

(5)

V\cong\bigoplus_{I\subseteq S}\mathbb{K}_{I}^{m_{I}}

where the sum is taken over intervals
I\subseteq S
,
\mathbb{K}_{I}
denotes the corresponding interval module, and
m_{I}
denotes the multiplicity of
\mathbb{K}_{I}
in the decomposition.

Definition 5.1
.

A pointwise finite-dimensional
S
-persistence module
V
is
barcode-finite
if
\sum_{I\subseteq S}m_{I}<\infty
.

In other words,
V
is
barcode-finite
when the number of interval modules in its decomposition (counted with multiplicity) is finite; i.e., if the total number of
bars
in its
barcode
is finite. Even if
V
is a persistence module over an infinite indexing set
S
, as long as
V
is barcode-finite, we use the language of Theorem
4.2
to extend the idea of codimension to this more general setting.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
V
be a barcode-finite
S
-persistence module. The
quiver codimension
of
V
, denoted
\Qcodim(V)
, is the number of interacting pairs of bars in the barcode of
V
.

Definitions
5.1
and
5.2
ensure that the value of
\Qcodim(V)
is always a finite non-negative integer. We have adopted these definitions because, in the sequel of the present paper, we show how to effectively determine
\Qcodim
via codimensions for
bona fide
quiver representations, which are always barcode-finite. However, we comment that we have left open the following possible generalizations, in two directions, for future work.

(1)

The barcode has infinitely-many bars but only finitely many interacting pairs, and hence
\Qcodim(V)
is still finite;

(2)

the barcode happens to admit infinitely-many interacting pairs and we simply allow
\Qcodim(V)=\infty
.

6.
Representation theoretic stability of Qcodim

We view the statement of Theorem
6.3
(see below) as the precise formulation of
RT-stability
for quiver codimension of persistence modules.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
V
be a barcode-finite
S
-persistence module. Define the set of
critical points
for
V
to be subset
Z(V)\subseteq S
given by the union of all births and all deaths of all bars in the barcode. By definition,
Z(V)
is a finite set.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
h
be a non-negative integer. Let
V
be a barcode-finite
S
-persistence module with critical points
Z(V)=\{z_{1}<\cdots<z_{m}\}
. A finite subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
admits an
h
-approximation
V^{\prime}=V|S^{\prime}
to
V
if

(i)

there exists
s,t\in S^{\prime}
with
s\leq z_{1}
and
z_{m}\leq t
,

(ii)

whenever
\langle z_{k},z_{i}\rangle
and
\langle z_{i+1},z_{\ell}\rangle
is a non-interacting pair of bars in the barcode for
V
, there exists
s\in S^{\prime}
with
z_{i}<s<z_{i+1}
, and

(iii)

\#\left(S^{\prime}\cap\langle z_{i},z_{i+1}\rangle\right)\geq h
for all
i=1,\ldots,m-1
,

where the intervals in (ii) and (iii) are taken as subsets of
S
.

Item (iii) in Definition
6.2
is the key condition in the sequel; it ensures that
S^{\prime}
is “dense enough” to detect important features of the barcode. On the other hand, conditions (i) and (ii) address special cases in our analysis. In particular, (i) ensures that our proof of Theorem
6.3
will go through when
z_{1}
is a
death
time or
z_{m}
is a
birth
time. This can occur (say if
S=\mathbb{R}
) when
V
has bars of the form
\langle-\infty,z_{1}\rangle
or
\langle z_{m},\infty\rangle
. Condition (ii) ensures that non-interacting pairs of bars in
V
do not restrict to interacting pairs in
V^{\prime}
; we will need this in the proofs of Lemmas
7.3
and
7.8
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Consider the barcode finite
S
-persistence module
V
. Suppose that the finite subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
admits an
h
-approximation
V^{\prime}=V|S^{\prime}
. Provided that
h\geq 2

\codim(\Omega^{\prime})=\Qcodim(V)

where
\Omega^{\prime}
is the isoclass of the quiver representation associated to
V^{\prime}
.

In other words, the barcodes of
V
and
V^{\prime}
have the same number of interacting pairs. The proof of this theorem is the subject of the next section.

The content of Theorem
6.3
is twofold. First, even if the critical points
Z(V)
can not be computed exactly, it is enough to take a
2
-approximation of
V
and compute the codimension from the resulting quiver representation. Second, the key to the proof is that while the
flavors
of interacting pairs in
V
and
V^{\prime}
may differ—i.e., the number of non-separated vs. interlaced pairs
need not
be constant—the
total number
of interacting pairs
is
constant.

Remark 6.4
.

When
S=\mathbb{R}
(or some connected subset of
\mathbb{R}
), an
S^{\prime}
which admits an
h
-approximation can always be constructed by knowing

•

bounds on the set of critical points; say
m\leq|z|\leq M
for all
z\in Z(V)
, and

•

a lower bound on the distance between critical points;
\min_{i<j}\{|z_{i}-z_{j}|\}\geq\epsilon
.

Then, the finite set
S^{\prime}
formed by the equally spaced sequence

m,m+\delta,m+2\delta,\ldots,m+N\delta

until
m+N\delta\geq M
admits an
h
-approximation provided
\delta\leq\epsilon/h
.

Proof of Theorem
6.3

Throughout the section, we take as blanket hypotheses those of Theorem
6.3
on
V
,
V^{\prime}
,
S
,
S^{\prime}
, and
h
; i.e.,
h\geq 2
and
V^{\prime}=V|S^{\prime}
is an
h
-approximation of the barcode-finite
S
-persistence module
V
.

7.1.
Interactions and restrictions of pointbars

Recall that a pointbar in
S
(respectively
S^{\prime}
) is a bar
I
such that
b(I)=a=d(I)
for some
a\in S
(resp.
a\in S^{\prime})
. We will write such a pointbar as
[a]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

If a pointbar is part of an interacting pair, the pair must be non-separated.

Proof.

Denote the pointbar by
[a]
and the other bar (possibly a pointbar, or not) by
\langle b,d\rangle
. If the pair is interlaced, we must either have
a<b\leq a
or
a\leq d<a
which are both impossible.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose that
I=[a]
is a pointbar for
V
which interacts with a bar
J
in the barcode for
V
. Then we must have that
a\in S^{\prime}
,
I^{\prime}=[a]
is a pointbar for
V^{\prime}
, and
I^{\prime}
interacts with a corresponding bar
J^{\prime}
in the barcode for
V^{\prime}
. Moreover, the bars
I
and
I^{\prime}
have the same multiplicity in their respective barcodes, and the bars
J
and
J^{\prime}
have the same multiplicity in their respective barcodes.

Proof.

Lemma
7.1
ensures that
I
and
J
must interact as a non-separated pair. Write
J=\langle b,d\rangle
where it is possible that
b=d
and so
J
is also a pointbar, and first consider the case that
a<b
. Since
I
and
J
are non-separated, it must be the case that there are no elements of
S
between
a
and
b
. Since
h\geq 2
, we must have that
\langle a,b\rangle\cap S^{\prime}=\{a,b\}
. Taken together, the previous two statements imply that
a,b\in S^{\prime}
and there are no elements of
S^{\prime}
between
a
and
b
. Hence,
I^{\prime}=[a]
is a bar for
V^{\prime}
and there must exist a bar
J^{\prime}=\langle b,d^{\prime}\rangle
with
b\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
with which it interacts. The case
d<a
is similar. The statement on multiplicities follows from the fact that
V^{\prime}=V|S^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose that
I=[a]
is a pointbar for
V
which does not interact with any other bars in the barcode for
V
. One of the following must hold:

•

a\notin S^{\prime}
and thus
I
does not restrict to a pointbar for the restriction
V^{\prime}
;

•

a\in S^{\prime}
, and thus
I=[a]
is a pointbar for
V^{\prime}
but still does not interact with any other bars for
V^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We need only prove the claim in the second mutually exclusive bullet point. Suppose that
J=\langle b,d\rangle
is a bar in
V
. We consider the case that
a<b
; the others are analogous. Condition (ii) of Definition
6.2
ensures there is an
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
with
a<s^{\prime}<b
. If
J
is a pointbar which does not restrict to
V^{\prime}
, there is nothing to prove. However, if
b\in S^{\prime}
and
J
is a pointbar, or
J
is not a pointbar but restricts to
J^{\prime}=\langle b^{\prime},d^{\prime}\rangle
, in either case we have
a<s^{\prime}<b\leq b^{\prime}
and hence
I
interacts with neither the pointbar
J
nor
J^{\prime}
in the barcode for
V^{\prime}
.
∎

7.2.
Interactions and restrictions for bars which are not pointbars

Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the set of bars for
V
which are not pointbars. Analogously, let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
denote the set of bars for
V^{\prime}
which are not pointbars. We define a map
\Phi:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
given as follows. For
I=\langle b,d\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
write
I^{\prime}=\Phi(I)=\langle b^{\prime},d^{\prime}\rangle\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
for some
b^{\prime},d^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
and satisfying the crieteria that

(C1)

b\leq b^{\prime}
and no other elements of
S^{\prime}
are between
b
and
b^{\prime}
;

(C2)

d^{\prime}\leq d
and no other elements of
S^{\prime}
are between
d^{\prime}
and
d
.

Lemma 7.4
.

The map
\Phi
is well-defined and is a one-to-one correspondence. Further, the multiplicity for any
I\in\mathcal{B}
in the barcode for
V
equals the multiplicity of
I^{\prime}=\Phi(I)
in the barcode for
V^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Set
b^{\prime}=\inf\{s\in S^{\prime}~|~s\geq b\}
and
d^{\prime}=\sup\{s\in S^{\prime}~|~s\leq d\}
. Since
S^{\prime}
is at least a
2
-approximation of
S
; i.e.,
h\geq 2
, we are assured that
b^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime}
both exist, and moreover,
b^{\prime}<d^{\prime}
. Since
V^{\prime}
is a restriction of
V
,
\langle b^{\prime},d^{\prime}\rangle
will be in the barcode for
V^{\prime}
and satisfy (C1) and (C2) by construction. The statement on equality of multiplicities is guaranteed by the fact that
V^{\prime}=V|S^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Suppose that
I
and
J
are an interlaced pair of bars for
V
. By Lemma
7.1
, both
I,J\in\mathcal{B}
. The corresponding bars
I^{\prime}=\Phi(I)
and
J^{\prime}=\Phi(J)
interact in the barcode for
V^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Write
I=\langle b,d\rangle
and
J=\langle\beta,\delta\rangle
. Without loss of generality, the interlaced criterion says that
b<\beta\leq d<\delta
.

Case I:
\beta<d
. Let
I^{\prime}=\langle s_{1},s_{3}\rangle
and
J^{\prime}=\langle s_{2},s_{4}\rangle
. Since each of
b<\beta<d<\delta
are critical points in
Z(V)
and
h\geq 2
, we can conclude

•

b\leq s_{1}<\beta
,

•

\beta\leq s_{2}<s_{3}\leq d
, and

•

d<s_{4}\leq\delta
.

Putting these together, we must have that
s_{1}<s_{2}<s_{3}<s_{4}
. Hence
I^{\prime}=\langle s_{1},s_{3}\rangle
and
J^{\prime}=\langle s_{2},s_{4}\rangle
are an interlaced pair of bars for
V^{\prime}
.

Case II:
\beta=d
. Write
I^{\prime}=\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle
and
J^{\prime}=\langle t_{3},t_{4}\rangle
for
t_{1}<t_{2}\leq t_{3}<t_{4}
some elements of
S^{\prime}
. Since
h\geq 2
we can conclude that
t_{1},t_{2}\in\langle b,d\rangle
and
t_{3},t_{4}\in\langle\beta,\delta\rangle
. If
\beta=d
happens to be in
S^{\prime}
, then
t_{2}=t_{3}
and
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
are again interlaced. On the other hand, if
\beta=d
is not an element of
S^{\prime}
, since
t_{2}
is maximal among elements of
S^{\prime}
bounded above by
d
and
t_{3}
is minimal among elements of
S^{\prime}
bounded below by
\beta
, we conclude that there are no elements of
S^{\prime}
between
t_{2}
and
t_{3}
. Hence,
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
form a non-separated pair of bars for
V^{\prime}
.

In either Case I or II, we conclude that the bars
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
interact.
∎

Lemma 7.7
.

Suppose that
I,J\in\mathcal{B}
are a non-separated pair of bars for
V
. Then the corresponding bars
I^{\prime}=\Phi(I)\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}=\Phi(J)\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
are a non-separated pair of bars for
V^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Write
I=\langle b,d\rangle
and

and without loss of generality assume that
b<d<\beta<\delta
. The non-separated criterion implies that there are no elements of

between

between

, the criteria (C1) and (C2) imply that there are no elements of

between

.

Lemma 7.8
.

Suppose that
I,J\in\mathcal{B}
are not an interacting pair of bars for
V
. Then the corresponding bars
I^{\prime}=\Phi(I)
and
J^{\prime}=\Phi(J)
are not an interacting pair for
V^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Write
I=\langle b,d\rangle
,
J=\langle\beta,\delta\rangle
,
I^{\prime}=\langle b^{\prime},d^{\prime}\rangle
, and
J^{\prime}=\langle\beta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}\rangle
. If
I
and
J
do not interact then, without loss of generality, one of the following cases must hold:

(i)

b=\beta
;

(ii)

d=\delta
;

(iii)

b<\beta<\delta<d
;

(iv)

b<d<s<\beta<\delta
for some
s\in S
.

In case (i), the criteria (C1) and (C2) guarantee that
b^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}
. Similarly, in case (ii),
d^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}
. In either case,
I^{\prime}
and

do not form an interacting pair.

In case (iii), the criteria (C1) and (C2) ensure that

and

. The condition that

ensures that
\beta^{\prime}<\delta^{\prime}
and we have

which is sufficient to conclude that
I^{\prime}
and

do not form an interacting pair.

In case (iv), the criteria (C1) and (C2) imply that

and

. Condition (ii) of Definition

ensures that we must have

so that

. Hence, we have an analogous string of inequalities in

to that in

, and we conclude that
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
do not form an interacting pair.
∎

7.3.
Finishing the proof of Theorem
6.3

On the one hand, Lemmas
7.2
,
7.5
, and
7.7
together ensure that every interacting pair of bars for
V
restricts to an interacting pair of bars for
V^{\prime}
. On the other hand, Lemmas
7.3
and
7.8
ensure that every non-interacting pair of bars for
V
either restricts to a non-interacting pair of bars in
V^{\prime}
, or at least one of the bars does not restrict at all to the barcode for
V^{\prime}
, and hence does not contribute any new interactions. This proves that the number of interacting pairs of bars for
V
is equal to the number of interacting pairs of bars for
V^{\prime}
, and we conclude that
\Qcodim(V)=\Qcodim(V^{\prime})=\codim(\Omega^{\prime})
as desired. ∎

Theorem 8.2
.

\Qcodim(\PH_{1}(\mathrm{VR}(X_{c},\mathbb{R}^{2})))=c

Proof.

8.2.
Qcodim from sublevel set filtrations

In this subsection, we will recall the sublevel set filtrations of Example
2.1
. In particular, we will construct a family of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for which the degree
d=0
persistent homology of the sublevel set filtration has quiver codimension which can take
any
non-negative integer value. In other words, the quiver codimension of degree
d=0
persistent homology may still admit interesting information on a filtration outside of the special cases of VR and Čech filtrations; cf. Theorem
8.1
.

Let
c
be a non-negative integer. Form
c+2
points
A_{i}=(i,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
for each integer
i
in the range
-1\leq i\leq c
. Similarly, form
c+2
points
B_{i}=(i,i+2)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
-1\leq i\leq c
. Let
\Sigma_{c}
denote the piecewise linear
sawtooth space
obtained by connecting the
2c+4
points
\{A_{i}\}\cup\{B_{i}\}
via straight line segments in the sequence

A_{-1}\to B_{-1}\to A_{0}\to B_{0}\to\cdots\to A_{c}\to B_{c}

see Figure
4
.

\PH_{0}(\Sigma_{2}^{g})
.

Now, let
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the projection
(x,y)\mapsto y
(and its restriction to
\Sigma_{c}

(\Sigma_{c},g)

\PH_{0}(\Sigma_{c}^{g})

Theorem 8.3
.

\PH_{0}(\Sigma_{c}^{g})
is barcode-finite and
\Qcodim(\PH_{0}(\Sigma_{c}^{g}))=c
.

Proof.

For
s<-1
, the sublevel set
(\Sigma_{c})_{s}
is empty. At
s=-1
, a connected component is born which persists as
s\to\infty
. However, at each integer
m\in S
with
0\leq m\leq c
, a new connected component is born (at the southern tip of a sawtooth), but which subsequently dies at
m+1\in S
(by coinciding with the persistent connected component born at
s=-1
). Hence, the resulting barcode consists of a sequence of bars
\langle-1,\infty\rangle,\langle 0,1\rangle,\langle 1,2\rangle,\ldots,\langle c,c+1\rangle
. The interacting pairs are exactly those of the form
(\langle k-1,k\rangle,\langle k,k+1\rangle)
for integers
k
in the range
1\leq k\leq c
. The result follows from Definition
5.2
.
∎

9.
Discussion and future work

9.1.
Interpretation of quiver codimension as a statistic in TDA

The calculation of
\Qcodim
by counting pairs of interacting bars admits the following intuitive meaning in the setting of PH as a tool for
topological data analysis
(TDA).

Consider the topological features of the underlying data (measured by bars in the barcode) at many different possible “scales” (measured by the birth and death times in the indexing set
S
). A positive codimension; i.e.,
interaction
between “features” in the sense of Definition
4.1
, means that the features persist at scales in
S
which are
different
(they necessarily must have distinct birth and death times) but they
overlap
. Said another way, the larger the quiver codimension associated to the (PH of some filtration on the) data, the less likely we expect to be able to isolate the topological features of the data into separable scales.

9.2.
Distribution of quiver codimension

Consider the case of a fixed metric space
(M,\rho)
and a fixed positive integer
N
. Suppose we take finite
random samples
of point clouds
X=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
from
M
. Now, fix an integer
d\geq 0
and suppose we consider
\Qcodim(\PH_{d}(\mathrm{VR}(X,M)))
as a sample statistic. We can ask

Question 1
.

How is the
\Qcodim
statistic distributed?

Posing this question certainly depends on the structure of
M
and a compatible formulation of “random sample from
M
”; nonetheless, an answer to the question (or an analogous question for other filtrations) sheds light on the relationship between PT-stability and RT-stability. In particular, given the qualitative interpretations of the previous subsection, we might reasonably expect a unimodal distribution clustered just above the number of scales at which the major topological features interact in the population space
M
(since most samples should capture the major features, and “noise” can only add to the codimension but not subtract). PT-stability ensures that the barcode should be stable relative to small perturbations in the data which we may expect from random sampling. RT-stability ensures that
\Qcodim
is well-defined and computable on each sample, but to what extent does “noise” in the barcode affect the variation of
\Qcodim
?

9.3.
“Higher” algebro-geometric invariants

Let
\Omega\subset\Rep
denote the isoclass of a given quiver representation. By definition, the closure
\bar{\Omega}
is a
G_{\alpha}
-stable subvariety of the vector space
\Rep
and furthermore defines an equivariant cohomology class
[\bar{\Omega}]\in H^{*}_{G_{\alpha}}(\Rep)

[
8
]
. Since
\Rep
is equivariantly contractible,
H^{*}_{G_{\alpha}}(\Rep)
is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in the Chern classes of
G_{\alpha}
and
[\bar{\Omega}]
is called the
quiver polynomial
. This polynomial invariant has been well-studied and exhibits remarkable combinatorial, geometric, stability, and positivity properties. One important property in the present context is that the quiver polynomial is homogeneous of degree equal to
\codim(\Omega)
; see e.g.,
[
8
,
9
,
22
,
24
]
.

Question 2
.

Does the quiver polynomial exhibit RT-stability or only its degree?

That is, it would be interesting to consider an extension of the quiver polynomial to general persistence modules which can be approximated by quiver representations in the sense of Definition
6.2
. Furthermore, there is a plethora of characteristic classes associated to an isoclass
\Omega
; e.g., the Chern–Schwartz–Macpherson (CSM) and Segre–Schwartz–Macpherson (SSM) classes in cohomology as well as their K-theoretic, and elliptic-cohomology versions; see e.g.,
[
16
]
for a survey on the current state of the art. These “higher”, non-homogeneous invariants typically recover the quiver polynomial as a specialization to their lowest degree term. The SSM classes may be of particular interest in data analysis, as they can satisfy a normalization property akin to a probability distribution; see, e.g.,
[
15
, Remark 8.8]
.

Abstract
. We study Medvedev reducibility in the context of set theory — specifically, forcing and large cardinal hypotheses. Answering a question of Hamkins and Li [ HL ∞ ] , we show that the Medvedev degrees of countable ordinals are far from linearly ordered in multiple ways, our main result here being that there is a club of ordinals which is an antichain with respect to Medvedev reducibility. We then generalize these results to arbitrary “reasonably-definable” reducibilities, under appropriate set-theoretic hypotheses. We then turn from ordinals to general structures. We show that some of the results above yield characterizations of counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture; another applies to all situations, assigning an ordinal to any reasonable class of structures and “measure” on that class. We end by discussing some directions for future research.

So it is plausible that

3.
Absolute strong reducibilities

Several of the results of the previous section invoked set theoretic principles beyond
ZFC
, either to bring a result from the “generic” context down to apply to genuinely countable ordinals or to bring out combinatorial features which otherwise wouldn’t exist (namely, the appropriate club dichotomy), and that section ended with an example of a natural strong reducibility, of the same complexity as Medvedev reducibility, which can consistently fail to have some of the global structural properties we hope that Medvedev reducibility might satisfy in
ZFC
alone. This tells us that
ZFC
alone will generally not suffice to give satisfying global descriptions of the degrees of countable ordinals under a natural strong reducibility notion.

On the other hand, our use of additional set-theoretic hypotheses was “coarse”: in general, arbitrary strong reducibilities can be tamed by appropriate large cardinal axioms. In this section we treat specifically the projectively-definable case, but it is clear how to extend to broader contexts under stronger hypotheses. The main value of these arguments is in the following section, where they will be used to provide characterizations of counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture.

By a
strong reducibility notion
we mean a subset
\mathfrak{R}
of
\omega\times Struc\times Struc
; we interpret “
\mathfrak{R}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
” as “
e
is an index for a reduction from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
,” and say “
\mathcal{A}\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathcal{B}
” if
\exists e(\mathfrak{R}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}))
; sometimes we refer to
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}
itself as the strong reducibility, when no confusion will arise (note however that strictly speaking
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}
does not determine
\mathfrak{R}
).

Suppose
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\omega\times Struc\times Struc
is definable — by which we shall mean, definable in the language of set theory with real parameters. Then there are two “generic” versions of
\mathfrak{R}
, applicable to all structures (not just the countable ones):

•

\mathfrak{R}^{*,+}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
if for some generic extension
V[G]
, we have
V[G]\models\mathfrak{R}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
(and in particular
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are countable in
V[G]
). We denote the induced preorder by “
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}^{*,+}
.”

•

\mathfrak{R}^{*,-}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
if for every generic extension
V[G]
in which
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are countable, we have
V[G]\models\mathfrak{R}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
. We denote the induced preorder by “
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}^{*,-}
.”

(Note that these are really generic versions of
some fixed definition
of
\mathfrak{R}
; when we speak of a definable reducibility, we really mean a fixed definition of that reducibility.)

When
\mathfrak{R}
is Medvedev reducibility, these coincide by Shoenfield absoluteness; but in general they need not (e.g. take
\mathfrak{R}
to depend on the continuum hypothesis). However, an ameliorating theme in in descriptive set theory is that under large cardinal assumptions, “reasonably definable” objects behave absolutely. At the most basic level, if
\mathfrak{R}
is reasonably definable and appropriate large cardinal assumptions hold, we expect
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}^{*,+}
and
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}^{*,-}
to coincide; but more generally we expect reasonable sentences about such an
\mathfrak{R}
, such as basic properties of the corresponding degree structures, to be forcing-invariant.

So we will restrict our attention to “absolute strong reducibilities.” Here there is a slight issue — it quickly becomes very tedious to state all theorems optimally. To facilitate things, we will adopt an excessively-broad definition of “absolute strong reducibility,” which will in turn require us to assume an excessively-powerful large cardinal axiom; we leave the straightforward improvements to the reader interested in minimizing set-theoretic assumptions. Following Montalbán
[
Mon13
]
, we will look at
projective
definability.

Definition 5
.

An absolute strong reducibility (ASR)
\mathfrak{R}
is a subset of
\omega\times Struc\times Struc
such that the following hold

•

\mathfrak{R}
is projectively definable (or rather, the predicate “
a,b
are codes for structures
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
and
\mathfrak{R}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
holds” is projective in
e,a,b
).

•

(Injectivity)
For each
e
and
\mathcal{B}
, there is at most one
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathfrak{R}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

•

(Transitivity)
If
\mathcal{A}\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathcal{B}\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathcal{A}\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathcal{C}
.

(We could include more axioms, like reflexivity, but will have no need for it.).

ASRs are generally well-behaved (and in particular deserve their name) assuming that large cardinals exist.

Convention 3.1
.

For the rest of this article we assume the large cardinal hypothesis “There is a proper class of Woodin cardinals.” The relevant consequences of this hypothesis are the following:

•

The truth values of projective sentences with real parameters are not changed by forcing. In particular,
\mathfrak{R}^{*,+}=\mathfrak{R}^{*,-}
for every ASR
\mathfrak{R}
.

•

The projective club dichotomy: every projectively definable set of countable ordinals contains or is disjoint from a club. (This is used exclusively in Theorem
3.4
.)

We write “ASR” instead of “PSR” to emphasize that it is the absoluteness of
\mathfrak{R}
, not its projectiveness per se, that really matters.

Remark 3.2
.

A proper class of Woodin cardinals is significant overkill for our purposes. This is visible already at the level of consistency strength, since the consistency strength of projective absoluteness for forcing extensions is equiconsistent with infinitely many strong cardinals (see Hauser
[
Hau95
]
), and the projective club dichotomy is even weaker — it is consistent relative to a single ineffable cardinal, which is a large cardinal property compatible with
V
=
L
(see section
5
of Harrington
[
Ha78
]
). Additionally, the assumption of a proper class of Woodin cardinals in fact would allow us to consider not just projective strong reducibilities but strong reducibilities defined inside the inner model
L(\mathbb{R})
. This is a significantly broader class of reducibility notions, but is also more technical to define.

For an arbitrary ASR
\mathfrak{R}
, we build a picture similar to that of Medvedev reducibility. We define
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{R}}^{ord}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{R}*}^{ord}
,
{{\textsf{M}}}_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathcal{A})
, and

analagously to their Medvedev versions in Section 2, and show that the results of the previous sections hold for arbitrary ASRs.

A couple results generalize easily:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be an ASR. Then:

(1)

There is a (least)
\theta_{sup}^{\mathfrak{R}}<\omega_{1}
such that
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\alpha)<\theta_{sup}^{\mathfrak{R}}
for all ordinals
\alpha
.

(2)

There is no embedding of
\omega_{1}
into
\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{R}}^{ord}
.

Proof.

(1)
is proved via absoluteness as in Corollary
2.8
. For each
\alpha\in Ord
, we have
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\alpha)<\omega_{1}
; since
\mathfrak{R}
is absolute, after collapsing

we have
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\alpha)<\omega_{1}^{V}
, hence is countable, so by absoluteness again we have
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\alpha)<\omega_{1}
in the ground model.

(2)
follows from
(1)
as before: given an embedding of
\omega_{1}
into

, we can find an

∎

By contrast, when we try to extend Theorem

we run into a slight problem — the argument used absoluteness of the relevant properties between arbitrary transitive models (Lemma
2.2
), while our large cardinal assumption only gives us much more restricted forms of absoluteness (e.g. between forcing extensions). Instead, we use the club dichotomy provided by our large cardinal assumption to find a sufficiently homogeneous set of countable ordinals, and then argue that it constitutes an antichain:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be an ASR. Then there is a club
A\subset\omega_{1}
which is an
\mathfrak{R}
-antichain.

Proof.

Fix an ASR
\mathfrak{R}
. For
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, we define the binary relation
O_{\alpha}\subseteq\omega\times\omega
as follows.
O_{\alpha}(c,d)
holds iff one of the two possibilities holds:

(1)

c\not\in{{\textsf{M}}}_{\mathfrak{R}}(\alpha)
;

(2)

c,d\in{{\textsf{M}}}_{\mathfrak{R}}(\alpha)
and
\gamma_{0}<\gamma_{1}
where
\mathfrak{R}(c,\gamma_{0},\alpha)
and
\mathfrak{R}(d,\gamma_{1},\alpha)
.

O_{\alpha}
is a preorder, and it is easy to check that its ordertype is exactly that of
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\alpha)
. Note that if
\beta\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\alpha
then
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\beta)\leq CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\alpha)
. Both
(1)
and
(2)
are projective, hence by our large cardinal assumption for each
m,n\in\omega
the set
\{\alpha\in\omega_{1}:O_{\alpha}(m,n)\}
either contains or is disjoint from a club
C_{m,n}
. Let
D=\bigcap_{m,n\in\omega}C_{m,n}
.
D
is a club, and for each
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in D
we have
CS(\gamma_{0})=CS(\gamma_{1})
.

Let
S(\beta)=\sup\{\gamma:\gamma\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\beta\}
, and note that if
\beta\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\alpha
and
\alpha>S(\beta)
then
CS(\beta)<CS(\alpha)
since
\alpha
is bigger than every ordinal
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\beta
. We now “thin”

: let

E=\{\delta\in D:\forall\epsilon<\delta,S(\epsilon)<\delta\}.

E
remains club — in particular,
S(\eta)
is always countable when
\eta
is — and so it is enough to show that
E
is an
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}
-antichain.

Fix elements
\alpha<\beta
of

. Then
\beta>S(\alpha)
, so
\beta\not\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\alpha
. Conversely, if
\alpha\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}\beta
then we would have
CS(\alpha)<CS(\beta)
since
\beta>S(\alpha)
, but this contradicts the observation above about

. So

is in fact an
\leq_{\mathfrak{R}}
-antichain.
∎

4.
Counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture

We now show that in fact the global picture provided above for ordinals can be significantly generalized, and in fact yields a characterization of counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture.

A key property we used implicitly throughout Section 2 was the fact that forcing adds no new ordinals. This property holds of counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture as well, in a sense studied by Knight, Montalbán, and the author
[
KMS16
]
(and independently by Kaplan and Shelah
[
KS16
]
):

Definition 6
.

A generically presentable structure is a pair
(\nu,\mathbb{P})
such that
\mathbb{P}
is a forcing notion,
\nu
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name, and the evaluation of
\nu
in a generic extension
V[G]
by
\mathbb{P}
is independent of
G
, up to isomorphism, in the sense that

\vdash_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\nu[G]\cong\nu[H].

All isomorphism-invariant data about possible evaluations of a generically presentable structure is already known by
V
by definition. If
T
is a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture, these are all the models there are in generic extensions:

Proposition 4.1

(Knight, Montalbán, S.
[
KMS16
]
)
.

Suppose
T
is a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture. Then if
G
is
\mathbb{P}
-generic and
M\in V[G]
is a model of
T
, we have
M=\nu[G\cap\mathbb{P}_{\leq p}]
for some generically presentable structure of the form
(\nu,\mathbb{P}_{\leq p})
for
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

Briefly, this is because otherwise we can force to add a perfect set of non-isomorphic evaluations of
\nu
, and being a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture is absolute (see Morley
[
Mor70
]
). When
T
is a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture, we will write “
GM(T)
” to denote the class of generically presentable models of
T
.

On the other side of things, we have theories with a perfect set of nonisomorphic models. For such theories the following construction will prove useful:

Construction 4.2
.

Suppose
P
is a perfect set of reals coding pairwise nonisomorphic structures, and conflate
P
with the real coding the underlying tree of
P
. We define an ASR
\mathfrak{W}_{P}
by setting
\mathfrak{W}(e,\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
iff one of the following holds:

•

\mathcal{N}
is coded by some real
f\in P
, and
\Phi_{e+1}^{f\oplus P}\cong\mathcal{M}
.

•

e=0
and
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{N}
.

Note that the relation “
b
and
a
are codes for structures
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}
with
\mathfrak{W}_{P}(e,a,b)
” is
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
in
e,a,b
(with parameter
P
).

We are now ready to give our first characterization of counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture:

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
T
is a theory with uncountably many countable models. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

T
is a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture.

(2)

For each ASR
\mathfrak{R}
, there is some countable ordinal
\alpha
such that for each model
\mathcal{M}
of
T
, the transitive collapse of
\{\beta:\beta
is an ordinal
\mathfrak{R}
-reducible to

(this is

) is
<\alpha
.

(3)

For each ASR
\mathfrak{R}
, there is no embedding of
\omega_{1}
into the
\mathfrak{R}
-degrees of countable models of
T
.

Proof.

Construction
4.2
provides
(3)\rightarrow(1)
and
(2)\rightarrow(1)
. Supposing
T
is not a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture, let
P
be a perfect set of nonisomorphic models of

. Then the ASR
\mathfrak{W}_{P}
given by Construction
4.2
witnesses the failure of both
(3)
and
(2)
:

•

To show that
(3)
fails, let
\{f_{\eta}:\eta<\omega_{1}\}\subseteq P
such that
\{deg(f_{\eta}\oplus P):\eta<\omega_{1}\}
is a strictly increasing sequence of Turing degrees; letting
\mathcal{M}_{\eta}
be the structure coded by

gives us an embedding of
\omega_{1}
into the
\mathfrak{W}_{P}
-degrees of models of
T
.

•

To show that
(2)
fails, note that if
\mathcal{A}\models T

\mathcal{A}
is
\mathfrak{W}_{P}
-above every ordinal below the next admissible above

\omega_{1}
.

For
(1)\rightarrow(2)
, we may compute in

the ordinal
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathcal{M})
for each
\mathcal{M}\in GM(T)
, and (since there are only
\omega

\mathfrak{R}
-reductions) we have
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathcal{M})<\omega_{1}
for all such

. Now consider a forcing extension in which

is countable. This universe also satisfies
CS_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathcal{M})<\omega_{1}^{V}
for each model

of

. But then this model satisfies “

.

Finally, suppose
(1)

(3)
fails as witnessed by
\mathfrak{R}

(1)
, fix some appropriately-definable countable-to-one surjection

onto
\omega_{1}
— for example, height in the Morley tree or (for interesting contrast) place in the Muchnik order modulo an appropriate real as per Theorem 1.V3 of
[

]
. Now define an ASR
\mathfrak{W}
by
\mathfrak{W}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
iff

•

\mathcal{B}\models T
,
\mathcal{A}
is a countable ordinal, and

•

\mathfrak{R}(e,\mathcal{C},\mathcal{B})
for some

with

.

(Without loss of generality, the language of

is disjoint from the fixed language for ordinals, so we dodge transitivity trivially.) Since
(1)
holds, we know

must hold, so let

be the countable ordinal so guaranteed in the case of the ASR

. Since
(3)
fails as witnessed by

, let

. Since

is countable-to-one, we may assume that

. But this means that

for all

, and in particular for some

we must have

. But this contradicts the assumption on

.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

We have treated strong reducibilities in a very abstract way. However, naturally-occurring strong reducibilities have additional structure: they tend to come from relations on individual reals, in the same way that Medvedev reducibility comes from Turing reducibility. This leads naturally to the notion of a fine ASR, which is an ASR
\mathfrak{R}
satisfying
\mathfrak{R}(e,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
iff
\mathfrak{S}(e,a,b)
for all reals
a,b
coding
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
respectively, for some projective relation
\mathfrak{S}\subseteq\omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
.

This means that there are two additional clauses we can add to Theorem
4.3
, by restricting clauses (2) and (3) to specifically fine ASRs. The theorem still holds: while restricting attention to fine ASRs would seem to make
(2)
and
(3)
weaker, the same proofs go through since the ASRs produced by Construction
4.2
are fine.

5.
Further questions

While better calibrating the set-theoretic hypotheses for the results above is a natural direction for further research, its interest seems likely to be limited to set theory. We end by mentioning two directions for future research which we hope will prove more broadly fruitful.

5.1.
Behavior on clubs

Conspicuously missing from Theorem
4.3
is anything resembling Theorem
3.4
. In a sense, it appears both too strong and too weak to provide a direct characterization of counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture.

On the “too strong” side, the natural approach to producing a characterization of counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture analogous to Theorem
3.4
would be to pull back along a definable bijection between
Mod(T)
and
\omega_{1}
when
T
is a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture. However, no such bijection is known to exist:

Question 1
.

Suppose
T
is a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture, and assume appropriate large cardinals. Is there a “reasonably definable” bijection between
Mod(T)
and
\omega_{1}
?

In lieu of a positive answer to this question, merely being a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture doesn’t seem to provide enough power to use a countably closed (pseudo-)ultrafilter on
Mod(T)
for our purposes.

Conversely, on the “too weak” side there is a version of Corollary
2.5
which holds in a broader context — namely, the existence of
\theta_{club}
— but here it holds
too
broadly. Suppose reasonable large cardinals exist,
\mathbb{K}
is a reasonably-definable set of structures, and
\mathcal{U}
is a reasonably-definable countably closed filter on
\mathbb{K}
satisfying the dichotomy principle for reasonably-definable subsets of
\mathbb{K}
. Then by the argument in the proof of Theorem
3.4
up to the “thinning” step,
ZFC
+ appropriate large cardinals proves that
CS(-)
is constant on a
\mathcal{U}
-large subset of
\mathbb{K}
with value some countable ordinal
\theta_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathbb{K}}
.

While this means that Corollary
2.5
seems irrelevant to counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture, it does raise an interesting problem on its own:

Problem 1
.

Assuming large cardinals, calculate
\theta_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathbb{K}}
for natural
\mathcal{U},\mathbb{K}
.

5.2.
Near misses

Characterizations of counterexamples to Vaught’s conjecture can sometimes be modified to provide notions of “near-miss” theories: theories which can be proved to exist, but which satisfy weak “anti-Vaught” properties which are still interesting. For example, Montalbán
[
Mon13
]
showed that any theory satisfying “hyperarithmetic-is-recursive” is a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture; a natural response is to look for theories satisfying bounded versions of “hyperarithmetic-is-recursive,” like “arithmetic-is-recursive,” and indeed a theory satisfying “arithmetic-is-recursive” was recently constructed by Andrews, Harrison-Trainor, Miller, and the author
[
AHMS
]
.

Our characterization, Theorem
4.3
, also admits a notion of “near-miss.” Examining its proof, we see that the strong reducibility witnessing a theory’s failure to be a counterexample to Vaught’s conjecture is
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
— or rather, the predicate “
e
is an
\mathfrak{W}_{P}
-index for a reduction of the structure coded by
b
to the structure coded by

” is
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
in

(with a parameter for

). The obvious way to improve this to

would be to find a perfect set
P
of codes for pairwise nonisomorphic models of
T
such that every model of
T
has a code in
P
— that is, a perfect transversal of
(Mod(T),\cong)
— but the existence of such a

is a very strong condition on
T
.
7
7

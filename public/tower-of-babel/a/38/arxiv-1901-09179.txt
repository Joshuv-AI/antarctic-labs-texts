Finite energy weak solutions of 2d Boussinesq Equations with diffusive temperature
Tianwen Luo, Tao Tao, Liqun Zhang

Abstract
. We show the existence of finite kinetic energy solution with prescribed kinetic energy to the 2d Boussinesq equations with diffusive temperature on torus.



Keywords: Finite energy weak solution, Boussinesq equation, prescribed kinetic energy

AMS Subject Classification (2000):
35Q30, 76D03

1.
Introduction

The Boussinesq equation was introduced for understanding the effect of potentially large conversions between internal energy and mechanical energy in fluids, and simulates many geophysical flows, such as atmospheric fronts and ocean circulations (see, for example,
[
36
]
,
[
41
]
). Moreover, it was used in recent theoretical discussion of the energetics of horizontal convection and the energetics of turbulent mixing in stratified fluids.

In this paper, we consider the following 2-dimensional Boussinesq system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla p+(-\Delta)^{\alpha}v=\theta e_{2},\quad{\rm in}\quad[0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}\\
\hbox{div}v=0,\quad{\rm in}\quad[0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}\\
\partial_{t}\theta+v\cdot\nabla\theta-\triangle\theta=0,\quad{\rm in}\quad[0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\alpha<1
is a positive number,
e_{2}=(0,1)^{T}
and
{\rm T}^{2}
is 2d torus. Here,
v
is the velocity vector,
p
is the pressure,
\theta
denotes the temperature which is a scalar function.

The global well-posedness have been established by many authors for the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) in 2d for regularity data(see, for example,
[
9
]
,
[
25
]
). For the 3-dimensional case, the global existence of smooth solution of (
1.1
) remains open. To understand the turbulence phenomena in hydrodynamics, one needs to go beyond classical solutions, and in this paper we are interested in constructing weak solutions of (
1.1
) with bounded kinetic energy.
The triple
(v,p,\theta)
on
[0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}
is called a weak solution of (
1.1
) if they belong to
L^{\infty}(0,1;L^{2}({\rm T}^{2}))
and solve (
1.1
) in the following sense:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}(v\cdot\partial_{t}\varphi+v\otimes v:\nabla\varphi-(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\varphi\cdot u+\theta e_{2}\cdot\varphi)dxdt=0

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1)\times{\rm T}^{2};R^{2})
with
{\rm div}\varphi(t,x)=0.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}(\partial_{t}\phi\theta+v\cdot\nabla\phi\theta+\triangle\phi\theta)dxdt=0

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1)\times{\rm T}^{2};R)
and

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}v(t,x)\cdot\nabla\psi(x)dx=0

for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2};R)
and any
t\in[0,1]
.

The study of constructing non-unique or dissipative weak solution to fluid system is very fashionable in recent years, and the construction is based on convex integration method which pioneered by De Lellis-Székelyhidi Jr in
[
18
,
21
]
, where the author tackle the Onsager conjecture for the incompressible Euler equation. So far, there are many important work about weak solution of the incompressible Euler equation, see
[
10
,
15
,
19
,
24
,
26
,
28
,
40
,
42
,
43
,
44
,
46
,
47
]
. The Onsager conjecture was proved by P.Isett in
[
29
]
, based on a series of process on this problem in
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
11
,
16
,
17
,
22
,
27
]
, see also

[
6
]
for the construction of admissible weak solution. Moreover, the idea and method can be used to construct dissipative weak solution for other model, see
[
7
,
31
,
34
,
45
,
48
,
49
,
50
]
.

Recently, Buckmaster and Vicol establish the non-uniqueness of weak solution to the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes in
[
8
]
by introducing some new ideas. The main idea is to use “intermittent” building blocks in the convex integration scheme to control the dissipative term
\triangle v
, called “intermittent Beltrami flow”, which are space inhomogeneous version of the classical Beltrami flow, also see
[
2
]
. Compared with the homogenous case, the “intermittent Beltrami flow” has different scaling in different
L^{p}
norms. In particular, one can ensure small
L^{p}
norm for small
p>1
which is key to control the dissipative term. By choosing the parameter suitably, T.Luo and E.S.Titi in
[
32
]
construct weak solution with compact support in time for hyperviscous Navier-Stokes equation. For high dimension
(d\geq 4)
stationary Navier-Stokes equation, X. Luo in
[
35
]
show the non-uniqueness by constructing the concentrated Mikado flows introduced in
[
37
,
38
]
. Moreover, S.Modena and Székelyhidi established the non-uniqueness for the linear transport equation (and transport-diffusion equation) with divergence-free vector in some Sobolev space, see
[
37
,
38
]
, where they use Mikado density and Mikado fields which is highly concentrated such that the
L^{p}
norm of Mikado field is small for
p>1
small.

Motivated by the above earlier works, we consider the 2d Boussinesq equations (
1.1
) and want to know if the similar phenomena can also happen when adding the temperature effects. Following the general scheme in the construction of non-uniqueness to Navier-Stokes equation in
[
8
]
, we obtain the following existence result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any smooth function
e(t):[0,1]\rightarrow[1,+\infty)
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1
, there exist
v\in C([0,1],L^{2}({\rm T}^{2})),\theta\in\bigcap_{2\leq p<\infty}C([0,1],L^{p}({\rm T}^{2}))\cap L^{2}([0,1],H^{1}({\rm T}^{2}))
, which is weak solution of Boussinesq equation (
1.1
) with

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}|v(x,t)|^{2}dx=e(t),\quad\forall t\in[0,1],

and for any
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\theta(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}ds=\frac{1}{2}\|\theta(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Remark 1.2
.

For
0\leq\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, we also can construct weak solution with prescribed energy curve in the class
C([0,1],L^{2}(T^{2}))
by the same method. However, in a separate paper
[
33
]
, we will construct Hölder continuous solution for this case.

Remark 1.3
.

The
\theta
we construct in this paper is rather regular and satisfies energy equality.
When
\theta=0
, the equation (
1.1
) is 2d Navier-Stokes equation with fractional diffusion, and our construction also work for this case.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we state the main proposition and give a proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section 3, we collect some technical tool which will be used frequently. In Section 4, we introduce the intermittent plane wave which is the building block in our perturbation. In Section 5 and 6, we construct velocity perturbations and temperature perturbation, respectively. After the construction, we establish the related estimates. In Section 7, we construct the Reynold-Stress and establish the related estimates. Finally, in Section 8, we give a proof of Proposition
2.2
.

2.
Main proposition and proof of main theorem

In this section, we state our main iterative proposition and give a proof of theorem
1.1
by the help of main proposition.

Definition 2.1
.

Assume that
(v_{0},p_{0},\theta_{0},\mathring{R}_{0})\in C^{\infty}([0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2},R^{2}\times R\times R\times S_{0}^{2\times 2})
. We say that they solve the Boussinesq-Reynold equation if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v_{0}+{\rm div}(v_{0}\otimes v_{0})+\nabla p_{0}+(-\triangle)^{\alpha}v_{0}=\theta e_{2}+{\rm div}\mathring{R}_{0},\quad\quad\mbox{in}\quad[0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}\\[5.0pt]
{\rm div}v_{0}=0,\quad\quad\mbox{in}\quad[0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}\\[5.0pt]
\partial_{t}\theta_{0}+{\rm div}(v_{0}\theta_{0})-\triangle\theta_{0}=0,\quad\quad\mbox{in}\quad[0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

Here and throughout the paper,
S_{0}^{2\times 2}
is the set of trace-free symmetric
2\times 2
matrices.

We now state our main proposition, and Theorem
1.1
is a corollary.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
e(t),\alpha
be as in Theorem
1.1
and
\varepsilon_{0}
be a universal constant from the Geometric Lemma
4.2
. Then there exist universal constant
M
and
\tilde{M}
such that the following hold.

Let
\delta\leq 1
be any positive number,
\theta^{0}\in C^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2})
be any function satisfies
\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}\theta^{0}(x)dx=0
, and
(v_{0},p_{0},\theta_{0},\mathring{R}_{0})
is a solution of Boussinesq-Reynold equation (
2.2
) with

\displaystyle\|\mathring{R}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}{10000},\quad\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}\theta_{0}(t,x)dx=0,\quad\forall t\in[0,1]

(2.3)

and

\displaystyle\frac{3\delta}{4}e(t)\leq e(t)-\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}|v_{0}(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{5\delta}{4}e(t),\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

(2.4)

Then there exist another smooth functions
(v_{1},p_{1},\theta_{1},\mathring{R}_{1})
which is also a solution of Boussinesq-Reynold equation (
2.2
), and for every
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|\mathring{R}_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}{20000},\quad\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}\theta_{1}(t,x)dx=0,

\displaystyle\|v_{1}(t,\cdot)-v_{0}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\sqrt{\delta},

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(0,x)=\theta^{0}(x),

\displaystyle\quad\|\theta_{1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\tilde{M},

\displaystyle\|\theta_{1}(t,\cdot)-\theta_{0}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\theta_{1}(s,\cdot)-\nabla\theta_{0}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{M}\delta,

\displaystyle\|\theta_{1}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\theta_{1}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\|\theta_{1}(0,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle\frac{3\delta}{8}e(t)\leq e(t)-\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}|v_{1}(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{5\delta}{8}e(t).

(2.6)

We will prove the above proposition in the next several sections. Here we first give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

We first fix
\delta=1

\displaystyle v_{0}:=0,\quad\theta_{0}:=0,\quad p_{0}:=0,\quad\mathring{R}_{0}:=0.

Obviously, they solve Boussinesq-Reynolds system (

\displaystyle\frac{3\delta}{4}e(t)\leq e(t)-\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}|v_{0}|^{2}(x,t)dx\leq\frac{5\delta}{4}e(t),\qquad\forall t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\mathring{R}_{0}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}=0\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}{10000}.

Then choosing

\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}\frac{e(t)}{2^{n}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|\mathring{R_{n}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2^{n}\times 10000},

\displaystyle\|v_{n+1}(t,\cdot)-v_{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{M},\quad\theta_{n}(0,x)=\theta^{0}(x),

(2.12)

3.1.
Properties of fast oscillatory

In this subsection, we discuss some properties of fast oscillatory and the proof can be found in

]
,
[

]
, which was inspired by
[

. More precisely, we give an improved Hölder inequality which concern the

For a given function
f:{\rm T}^{2}\rightarrow R
,
\lambda\in{\rm N}
, we set

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(x):=f(\lambda x).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f,g:{\rm T}^{2}\rightarrow R
be smooth functions,
\lambda\in{\rm N}
. Then for every
p\in[1,+\infty]
, we have

\displaystyle\|fg_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{p}}+\frac{C_{p}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{p}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f,g:{\rm T}^{2}\rightarrow R
be smooth function with
\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}g(x)dx=0
, and
\lambda\in{\rm N}.
Then there hold

\displaystyle\Big|\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}fg_{\lambda}dx\Big|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{1}}}{\lambda}.

3.2.
Commutator for fast oscillation

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
\kappa\geq 1
. Let
a\in C^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.
For
1<p<\infty
, and any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, we have

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(a\mathbb{P}_{\geq k}f)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{-1}(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla^{2}a\|_{L^{\infty}})\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

The proof of this Lemma can be found in

4.
Intermittent plane waves

In this section, we describe in detail the construction of the
intermittent plane waves
which will form the building blocks of the convex integration scheme.

We first recall the following stationary solution for the 2d Euler equation. Our building block in this paper is the inhomogeneous version of it.

4.1.
Stationary flows in 2D and Geometric Lemma

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a given finite symmetric subset of
S^{1}\cap Q^{2}
with
\lambda\Lambda\in Z^{2}.
Then for any choice of coefficients
a_{\bar{\xi}}\in{\rm C}
with
\overline{a_{\bar{\xi}}}=a_{-\bar{\xi}}
, the vector field

\displaystyle W(x)=\sum_{\bar{\xi}\in\Lambda}a_{\bar{\xi}}i\bar{\xi}^{\bot}e^{i\bar{\xi}\cdot x},\quad\Psi(x)=\sum_{\bar{\xi}\in\Lambda}a_{\bar{\xi}}e^{i\bar{\xi}\cdot x}

is real-valued and satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm div}(W\otimes W)=\nabla\Big(\frac{|W|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\Psi^{2}}{2}\Big),\quad W(x)=\nabla^{\bot}\Psi(x).

Here and throughout the paper, we denote
\bar{\xi}^{\perp}=(-\bar{\xi}_{2},\bar{\xi}_{1})
if
\bar{\xi}=(\bar{\xi}_{1},\bar{\xi}_{2})
, and denote
\nabla^{\bot}=(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1})
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\big<W\otimes W\big>:=\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}W\otimes W(x)dx=\sum_{\bar{\xi}\in\Lambda}|a_{\bar{\xi}}|^{2}({\rm Id}-\bar{\xi}\otimes\bar{\xi}).

The proof of this Proposition can be found in
[
11
]
, and we omit it here.

Let

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}^{+}=\Big\{e_{1},\frac{3}{5}e_{1}+\frac{4}{5}e_{2},\frac{3}{5}e_{1}-\frac{4}{5}e_{2}\Big\},\quad\Lambda_{0}^{-}=-\Lambda_{0}^{+},\quad\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{0}^{+}\cup\Lambda_{0}^{-},

and
\Lambda_{1}
be given by the rotation of
\Lambda_{0}
counter clock-wise by
\pi/2
:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}^{+}=\Big\{e_{2},\frac{3}{5}e_{2}+\frac{4}{5}e_{1},\frac{3}{5}e_{2}-\frac{4}{5}e_{1}\Big\},\quad\Lambda_{1}^{-}=-\Lambda_{1}^{+},\quad\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{1}^{+}\cup\Lambda_{1}^{-}.

Clearly
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}\subseteq Q^{2}\cap S^{1}
and we have the representation

\displaystyle\frac{25}{32}\Big(\Big(\frac{3}{5}e_{1}+\frac{4}{5}e_{2}\Big)\otimes\Big(\frac{3}{5}e_{1}+\frac{4}{5}e_{2}\Big)+\Big(\frac{3}{5}e_{1}-\frac{4}{5}e_{2}\Big)\otimes\Big(\frac{3}{5}e_{1}-\frac{4}{5}e_{2}\Big)\Big)+\frac{7}{16}e_{1}\otimes e_{1}=\mathrm{Id}.

In fact, such representation holds for
2\times 2
symmetric matrices near
\mathrm{Id}
.

Lemma 4.2

(Geometric Lemma)
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, and smooth positive functions
\gamma_{\xi}
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\xi}\in C^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon_{0}}({\rm Id}))

such that for every
2\times 2
symmetric matrix
R\in B_{\varepsilon_{0}}({\rm Id})
, we have

R=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{0}^{+}}\gamma^{2}_{\xi}(R)\xi\otimes\xi.

Remark 4.3
.

By rotational symmetry, Geometric Lemma
4.2
also holds for
\xi\in\Lambda_{1}^{+}
.
It is convenient to introduce a small geometric constant
c_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle|\xi+\xi^{\prime}|\geq 2c_{0}

for all
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{0}\cap\Lambda_{1},\xi\neq-\xi^{\prime}
. Moreover, for
\xi\in\Lambda_{k}^{-}
with
k=0,1
, we set
\gamma_{\xi}:=\gamma_{-\xi}
.

Remark 4.4
.

When
{\rm Id}-\mathring{R}\in B_{\varepsilon_{0}}({\rm Id})
, by Geometric Lemma, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Id}-\mathring{R}=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{0}^{+}}\gamma_{\xi}^{2}\big({\rm Id}-\mathring{R}\big)\xi\otimes\xi.

Thus, taking trace in both side, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{0}^{+}}\gamma_{\xi}^{2}\big({\rm Id}-\mathring{R}\big)=2.

(4.14)

4.2.
Intermittent plane flow

The Dirichlet kernel
\tilde{D}_{r}
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{r}(x):=\sum_{\xi=-r}^{r}e^{ix\cdot\xi}=\frac{{\rm sin}((r+\frac{1}{2})x)}{{\rm sin}(\frac{x}{2})},

and it obeys the estimates: for any
p>1
,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{D}_{r}\|_{L^{p}}\sim r^{1-\frac{1}{p}},

where the implicit constant only depend only on
p
.
Define a 2d square

\displaystyle\Omega_{r}:=\{(j,k):j,k\in\{-r,...,r\}\}

and normalizing to unit size in
L^{2}
, we obtain a kernel

\displaystyle D_{r}(x):=\frac{1}{2r+1}\sum_{\xi\in\Omega_{r}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}=\frac{1}{2r+1}\sum_{(j,k)\in\Omega_{r}}e^{i(jx_{1}+kx_{2})}

which has the property: for
1<p\leq\infty

\displaystyle\|D_{r}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim r^{1-\frac{2}{p}},\quad\|D_{r}\|_{L^{2}}\thickapprox 1,

where the implicit constant only depend on
p
.
This computation is very easy due to the fact:

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\Omega_{r}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}=\Big(\sum_{i=-r}^{r}e^{ijx_{1}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{i=-r}^{r}e^{ikx_{2}}\Big).

We first fixed a large parameter
\lambda\in{\rm N}
, then introduce a parameter
\sigma
such that
\lambda\sigma\in{\rm N}
which parameterizes the spacing between frequencies. We assume that

\displaystyle\sigma r\leq\frac{c_{0}}{50N},

where
c_{0}
is the constant in Remark
4.3
, and
N
is a fixed integer(for example, we can take
N=5
).
Furthermore, we introduce a parameter
\mu\in(0,\lambda)
, which describes temporal oscillation in the building blocks.

As in
[
8
]

\xi\in\Lambda_{j}^{+}
, we define a directed and rescaled
(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\sigma})^{2}
-periodic Dirichlet kernel by

\displaystyle\eta_{(\xi)}(x,t):=\eta_{\xi,\lambda,\sigma,r,\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{r}\big(\lambda\sigma N(\xi\cdot x+\mu t),\lambda\sigma N\xi^{\bot}\cdot x\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=-r}^{r}e^{ij\lambda\sigma N(\xi\cdot x+\mu t)}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=-r}^{r}e^{ik\lambda\sigma N\xi^{\bot}\cdot x}\Big)

and set
\eta_{(\xi)}(x,t)=\eta_{(-\xi)}(x,t)
for
\xi\in\Lambda_{j}^{-}
, where
N
is a integer(we can set
N=5
duo to our construction of
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}

Observe that
\eta_{(\xi)}(x,t)
satisfies the following important identity:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\partial_{t}\eta_{(\xi)}(x,t)=\xi\cdot\nabla\eta_{(\xi)}(x,t)

(4.15)

for every
\xi\in\Lambda_{j}^{+}
,
j=0,1
.

A change of variable gives that

\displaystyle\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}\eta^{2}_{(\xi)}(x,t)dx=1,\quad\|\eta_{(\xi)}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}r^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

(4.16)

for all
1<p\leq\infty
, for any
t
.

Let
W_{(\xi)}(x)
be the stationary wave at frequency
\lambda
, namely

\displaystyle W_{(\xi)}(x):=W_{\xi,\lambda}=i\xi e^{i\lambda\xi^{\bot}\cdot x}.

We define the
intermittent plane waves

\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}
as

\displaystyle\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}(x,t)=\textbf{W}_{\xi,\lambda,\sigma,r,\mu}(x,t)=\eta_{\xi,\lambda,\sigma,r,\mu}(x,t)W_{\xi,\lambda}(x)=\eta_{(\xi)}(x,t)W_{(\xi)}(x).

Remark 4.5
.

The explicit representation of
\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}(x,t)
is as following:

\displaystyle\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=-r}^{r}e^{ij\lambda\sigma N(\xi\cdot x+\mu t)}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=-r}^{r}e^{ik\lambda\sigma N\xi^{\bot}\cdot x}\Big)i\xi e^{i\lambda\xi^{\bot}\cdot x}.

Some facts about the frequency support of
\eta_{(\xi)}
and
\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}
:

Lemma 4.6
.

We have the following frequency support property:

\displaystyle{\rm P}_{\leq 2\lambda\sigma rN}\eta_{(\xi)}=\eta_{(\xi)},\quad{\rm P}_{\leq 2\lambda}{\rm P}_{\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}}\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}=\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}.

For
\xi+\xi^{{}^{\prime}}\neq 0
, by the definition of
c_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm P}_{\leq 4\lambda}{\rm P}_{\geq c_{0}\lambda}\big(\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}\otimes\textbf{W}_{(\xi^{{}^{\prime}})}\big)=\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}\otimes\textbf{W}_{(\xi^{{}^{\prime}})}.

These facts can be obtained directly from the definition. In fact, the frequency support of
\eta_{(\xi)}
is obvious. Then, using the fact that

2\lambda\sigma rN\leq\frac{c_{0}\lambda}{5}
, it’s easy to obtain the frequency support of
\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}
. Finally, a direct computation gives that

\displaystyle\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}\otimes\textbf{W}_{(\xi^{{}^{\prime}})}=-\eta_{(\xi)}\eta_{(\xi^{{}^{\prime}})}\xi\otimes\xi^{{}^{\prime}}e^{i\lambda(\xi+\xi^{{}^{\prime}})^{\bot}\cdot x}.

Due to

\displaystyle{\rm P}_{\leq 4\lambda\sigma rN}(\eta_{(\xi)}\eta_{(\xi^{{}^{\prime}})})=\eta_{(\xi)}\eta_{(\xi^{{}^{\prime}})},\quad|\xi+\xi^{{}^{\prime}}|\geq 2c_{0}

and the fact

\displaystyle 4\lambda\sigma rN\leq\frac{2\lambda c_{0}}{5},

we obtain the frequency support of
\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}\otimes\textbf{W}_{(\xi^{{}^{\prime}})}
for
\xi+\xi^{{}^{\prime}}\neq 0
.

From these frequency support properties (from which we can use the Berstein inequality) and the estimates for Dirichlet kernel, we have the following estimates.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}
be defined as above. Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{N}\partial_{t}^{K}\textbf{W}_{(\xi)}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(N,K,p)\lambda^{N}(\lambda\sigma\mu r)^{K}r^{1-\frac{2}{p}},

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{N}\partial_{t}^{K}\eta_{(\xi)}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(N,K,p)(\lambda\sigma r)^{N}(\lambda\sigma\mu r)^{K}r^{1-\frac{2}{p}},

for any
1<p\leq\infty
, and
N,K\geq 0
are integer.

These estimates are direct, and we omit the proof here.

5.
The velocity perturbation: Construction and Estimates

In this section, we construct the perturbation of velocity and give some estimates for it.

5.1.
Construction of the velocity perturbation

In this subsection, we give the detailed construction of velocity perturbation.

5.1.1.
Definition of amplitude

Choose two smooth cutoff functions
\tilde{\chi}_{0},\tilde{\chi}_{1}
such that

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\tilde{\chi}_{0}\subseteq[0,4],\quad{\rm supp}\tilde{\chi}_{1}\subseteq\Big[\frac{1}{4},4\Big]

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}_{0}^{2}(y)+\sum_{j\geq 1}\tilde{\chi}_{j}^{2}(y)=1

for any
y>0
, where
\tilde{\chi}_{j}(y)=\tilde{\chi}(4^{-j}y)
. We then define

\displaystyle\chi_{j}(t,x):=\tilde{\chi}_{j}\Big(\Big<\frac{50\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}\Big>\Big)

(5.17)

for all
j\geq 0.
Here and throughout the paper we use the notation
\big<A\big>=(1+|A|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
where
|A|
denotes the standard norm of the matrix
A
. By the definition of the cutoff functions, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\chi_{j}^{2}(t,x)=1,\quad\chi_{j_{1}}\chi_{j_{2}}(t,x)=0\quad{\rm if}\quad|j_{1}-j_{2}|\geq 2.

(5.18)

Moreover, it’s obvious that there exists an index
j_{max}=j_{max}(\mathring{R}_{0},\delta)
such that
\chi_{j}(t,x)=0
for all
j\geq j_{max}.
Another important fact is

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}\chi^{2}_{0}(t,x)dx>0,\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

(5.19)

In fact,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(t,x)=0\Leftrightarrow\Big<\frac{50\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}\Big>\geq 4.

Thus, for fixed
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(t,x)=0\Rightarrow 50|\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)|\geq 3\varepsilon_{0}\delta.

Hence, for fixed
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}|\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)|dx\geq\frac{3}{50}\varepsilon_{0}\delta.

Thus, the assumption (
2.3
) tells us that for every
t\in[0,1]
,
\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}\chi^{2}_{0}(t,x)dx>0.

For
j\in{\rm N}
, denote

\displaystyle\Lambda_{(j)}=\Lambda_{j~\text{mod}~2},\quad\Lambda_{(j)}^{\pm}=\Lambda_{j~\text{mod}~2}^{\pm}.

For
\xi\in\Lambda_{(j)}^{+}
, define the coefficient function
a_{(\xi,j)}
by

\displaystyle a_{(\xi,j)}(t,x):=\sqrt{\rho_{j}}\chi_{j}(t,x)\gamma_{\xi}\Big({\rm Id}-\frac{\mathring{R}_{0}}{\rho_{j}}\Big)

(5.20)

where
\rho_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, are defined by

\displaystyle\rho_{j}:=4^{j}\delta,

(5.21)

and
\rho_{0}
is defined later.

We first claim that
a_{(\xi,j)}(t,x)
is well-defined for
j\geq 1
. We only need to show that
{\rm Id}-\frac{\mathring{R}_{0}}{\rho_{j}}\in B_{\varepsilon_{0}}({\rm Id})
. In fact, when
\chi_{j}\neq 0
, there holds

\displaystyle\Big<\frac{50\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}\Big>\leq 4^{j+1},

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{50|\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)|}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}\leq 4^{j+1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)|}{\rho_{j}}=\frac{|\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)|}{4^{j}\delta}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

thus
a_{(\xi,j)}(t,x)
is well-defined for
j\geq 1
.
We define
\rho_{0}(t)
as following:

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}\chi_{0}^{2}(t,x)dx\Big)^{-1}\Big[e(t)\Big(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)-\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}|v_{0}(t,x)|^{2}dx\Big].

(5.22)

Due to (
5.19
), we deduce that
\rho_{0}(t)
is well-defined. Next, we show that

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{\mathring{R}_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}({\rm supp\chi_{0})}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

In fact, using the assumption (
2.4
), we know that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{4}e(t)\leq e(t)\Big(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)-\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}|v_{0}(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{3\delta}{4}e(t),\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

(5.23)

On the support of
\chi_{0}
, there holds

\displaystyle 50|\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)|\leq 4\varepsilon_{0}\delta.

Thus, on the support of
\chi_{0}
, for any
t\in[0,1]
, there holds

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)}{\rho_{0}(t)}\Big|\leq\frac{4\varepsilon_{0}\delta}{50}\frac{8\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}\chi_{0}^{2}(t,x)dx}{\delta e(t)}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Thus,
a_{(\xi,0)}
is well-defined.

5.1.2.
Construction of velocity perturbation

Let us fix
\lambda_{1},\sigma_{1},r_{1},\mu_{1}
such that
\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1}\in{\rm N}
and the integer
r_{1}
, the parameter
\sigma_{1}
and
\mu_{1}
are defined by

\displaystyle r_{1}=[\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}],\quad\sigma_{1}=\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}},\quad\mu_{1}=[\lambda_{1}^{\frac{3\alpha+1}{4}}].

(5.24)

The principle part of perturbation
w^{p}_{1}
will be defined as

\displaystyle w_{1}^{p}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j}\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{(j)}^{+}}a_{(\xi,j)}\eta_{(\xi)}(W_{(\xi)}+W_{(-\xi)})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j}\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{(j)}}a_{(\xi,j)}\eta_{(\xi)}W_{(\xi)}

(5.25)

where
0\leq j\leq j_{max}
. Here and throughout the paper,
\eta_{(\xi)},W_{(\xi)}
denote, respectively,

\displaystyle\eta_{(\xi)}=\eta_{\xi,\lambda_{1},\sigma_{1},r_{1},\mu_{1}},\quad W_{(\xi)}=W_{\xi,\lambda_{1}},

where
\xi\in\Lambda_{(j)}
.

Then we define an incompressibility corrector

\displaystyle w^{c}_{1}:=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j}\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{(j)}^{+}}\nabla^{\bot}\big(a_{(\xi,j)}\eta_{(\xi)}\big)\frac{e^{i\lambda_{1}\xi^{\bot}\cdot x}+e^{-i\lambda_{1}\xi^{\bot}\cdot x}}{\lambda_{1}}.

(5.26)

Here and throughout the paper, we denote
\nabla^{\bot}
as
\nabla^{\bot}=(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1}).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle w^{p}_{1}+w^{c}_{1}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j}\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{(j)}^{+}}\nabla^{\bot}\Big(a_{(\xi,j)}\eta_{(\xi)}\frac{e^{i\lambda_{1}\xi^{\bot}\cdot x}+e^{-i\lambda_{1}\xi^{\bot}\cdot x}}{\lambda}\Big)

and

\displaystyle{\rm div}(w^{p}_{1}+w^{c}_{1})=0.

As in paper
[
8
]
, in addition to the incompressibility corrector
w_{1}^{c}
, we introduce a temporal corrector
w_{1}^{t}
, which is defined by

\displaystyle w_{1}^{t}:=-\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{j}\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda_{(j)}^{+}}P_{H}P_{\neq 0}\big(a^{2}_{(\xi,j)}\eta^{2}_{(\xi)}\xi\big).

(5.27)

Here
P_{\neq 0}f=f-\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}fdx
and
P_{H}f=f-\nabla\triangle^{-1}{\rm div}f.

Finally, we define the velocity increment
w_{1}
by

\displaystyle w_{1}=w^{p}_{1}+w^{c}_{1}+w^{t}_{1}.

It’s obvious that

\displaystyle{\rm div}w_{1}=0,\quad\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}w_{1}(t,x)dx=0.

After the construction of
w_{1}
, we define the new velocity field
v_{1}
as

\displaystyle v_{1}:=v_{0}+w_{1}.

5.2.
Estimate of the perturbation

In this subsection, we establish some estimates for the velocity perturbation.

Firstly, we collect some estimates concerning the cutoffs function
\chi_{j}(t,x)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a
j_{max}=j_{max}(\mathring{R}_{0},\delta)
such that

\displaystyle\chi_{j}(t,x)=0,\quad{\rm for\quad all}\quad j>j_{max}.

Moreover, for all
0\leq j\leq j_{max}

\displaystyle\rho_{j}\leq 4^{j_{max}}.

Proof.

For
j\geq 1

\displaystyle\chi_{j}(t,x)\neq 0\Leftrightarrow 4^{j-1}\leq\Big<\frac{50\mathring{R}_{0}(t,x)}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}\Big>\leq 4^{j+1}.

Thus,
\chi_{j}(t,x)\neq 0
implies

\displaystyle 4^{j-2}\leq\frac{50|\mathring{R}_{0}|}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}.

Thus, there exists
j_{max}=j_{max}(\mathring{R}_{0},\delta)
such that

\displaystyle\chi_{j}(t,x)=0,\quad{\rm for\quad all}\quad j>j_{max}.

More precisely, we have

\displaystyle 4^{j_{max}}\leq\frac{800|\mathring{R}_{0}|}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}.

(5.28)

\square

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
0\leq j\leq j_{max}
. There holds

\displaystyle\|\chi_{j}\|_{C^{L}_{t,x}}\leq C(\mathring{R}_{0},\delta,L),

where
L
is integer, and the constant
C
also depend on
\varepsilon_{0}
, but
\varepsilon_{0}
is a universal constant and we omit it.

Proof.

Direct computation gives that

\displaystyle\partial_{l}(\big<A\big>)=\frac{A:\partial_{l}A}{\big<A\big>},\quad\partial_{ll}(\big<A\big>)=\frac{\partial_{l}A:\partial_{l}A+A:\partial_{ll}A}{\big<A\big>}-\frac{(A:\partial_{l}A)^{2}}{\big<A\big>^{2}}

hence

\displaystyle|\partial_{l}(\big<A\big>)|\leq|\partial_{l}A|.

Since

\displaystyle\partial_{l}(\chi_{j}(t,x))=\tilde{\chi}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{4^{j}}\Big<\frac{\mathring{R}_{0}}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}\Big>\Big)\frac{1}{4^{j}}\partial_{l}\Big<\frac{\mathring{R}_{0}}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}\Big>,

thus

\displaystyle|\partial_{l}(\chi_{j}(t,x))|\leq\frac{C}{4^{j}}\frac{|\partial_{l}\mathring{R}_{0}|}{\varepsilon_{0}\delta}\leq C(\varepsilon_{0},\mathring{R}_{0},\delta),\quad|\partial_{ll}(\chi_{j}(t,x))|\leq C(\varepsilon_{0},\mathring{R}_{0},\delta).

By the inequality

\displaystyle\|f\circ g\|_{C^{L}}\leq C_{L}\big(\|f\|_{C^{L}}\|g\|_{C^{1}}^{L}+\|f\|_{C^{1}}\|g\|_{C^{L}}\big),

we know that

\displaystyle\|\chi_{j}\|_{C^{L}_{t,x}}\leq C(\varepsilon_{0},\mathring{R}_{0},\delta,L).

MM

\square

(Estimate on the amplitude)

, the estimate on

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho_{j}}\|\chi_{j}\|_{C^{L}_{t,x}}\Big\|\gamma_{\xi}\Big({\rm Id}-\frac{\mathring{R}_{0}}{\rho_{j}}\Big)\Big\|_{C^{L}_{t,x}}

7.1.
Anti-divergence operator

We first recall the anti-divergence operator:

Lemma 7.1
.

There exists an operator
\mathcal{R}
satisfying the following property:

•

For any

,
\mathcal{R}v(x)
is a symmetric trace-free matrix for each

and

{\rm div}\mathcal{R}v(x)=v(x)-\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}v(x)dx.

•

The following estimates hold:
\||\nabla|\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C_{p}
,

\|\mathcal{R}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C_{p},\quad\|\mathcal{R}\|_{C^{0}\rightarrow C^{0}}\leq C.

Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2})
be a solution to

\displaystyle\triangle u=v-\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}v(x)dx,

where
C_{0}^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2})=\{f\in C^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2}):\fint f(x)dx=0\}.

Then set

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}v(x):=\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{T}-({\rm div}u){\rm Id}.

Then
\mathcal{R}
satisfies the above property.

For

From the equation (

), we try

then by Corollary

Proof of main Proposition

To complete the proof of Proposition

Direct computation gives that

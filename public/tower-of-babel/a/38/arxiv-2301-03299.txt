Asymptotic error analysis for the discrete iterated Galerkin solution of Urysohn integral equations with Green’s kernels
Gobinda Rakshit

Abstract
. Consider a Urysohn integral equation x − 𝒦 ⁡ ( x ) = f x-\mathcal{K}(x)=f , where f f and the integral operator 𝒦 \mathcal{K} with kernel of the type of Green’s function are given. In the computation of approximate solutions of the given integral equation by Galerkin method, all the integrals are needed to be evaluated by some numerical integration formula. This gives rise to the discrete version of the Galerkin method. For r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 , a space of piecewise polynomials of degree ≤ r − 1 \leq r-1 with respect to a uniform partition is chosen to be the approximating space. For the appropriate choice of a numerical integration formula, an asymptotic series expansion of the discrete iterated Galerkin solution is obtained at the above partition points. Richardson extrapolation is used to improve the order of convergence. Using this method we can restore the rate of convergence when the error is measured in the continuous case. A numerical example is given to illustrate this theory.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{X}=L^{\infty}[0,1]
. Consider the problem of solving Urysohn integral equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle x(s)-\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t,x(t))\>dt=f(s),\hskip 19.91684pt~s\in[0,1],

where
f\in\mathcal{X}
and
\kappa\in C\left([0,1]\times[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}\right)
are given. Let the Urysohn integral operator
\mathcal{K}:L^{\infty}[0,1]\rightarrow C[0,1]
be defined by

(1.2)

\mathcal{K}({x})(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t,x(t))~dt,\hskip 19.91684ptx\in\mathcal{X},~s\in[0,1].

Since the kernel
\kappa
is continuous,
\mathcal{K}
is compact operator on
\mathcal{X}
. Denoting the equation (
1.1
) by

(1.3)

x-\mathcal{K}x=f.

We assume that the above equation has a solution, say
\varphi
. We also assume that
\mathcal{K}
is twice Frechét differentiable and
1
is not an eigenvalue of the compact linear operator
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}{(\varphi)}.
This gives us that
\varphi
is an isolated solution of (
1.3
). See
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
. We are looking for Galerkin approximations of
\varphi
.

For
r\geq 1
, consider the approximating space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
as a
space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq r-1
with respect to a
uniform partition, say
\Delta^{(n)}
, of
[0,1]
with
n
subintervals each of length
{h=\frac{1}{n}}.
Let
\pi_{n}
be the restriction to
L^{\infty}[0,1]
of the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}[0,1]
to
\mathcal{X}_{n}.
Then the Galerkin solution
\varphi_{n}^{G}
satisfies the following integral equation

\varphi_{n}^{G}-\pi_{n}\mathcal{K}(\varphi_{n}^{G})=\pi_{n}f.

Galerkin method for Urysohn integral equation has been studied extensively in research literature. See
[
5
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
. The iterated Galerkin solution is defined by

\varphi_{n}^{S}=\mathcal{K}(\varphi_{n}^{G})+f.

In
[
5
]
, the following orders of convergence are also obtained.

\|\varphi_{n}^{G}-\varphi\|_{\infty}=O\left(h\right),\;\;\;\|\varphi_{n}^{S}-\varphi\|_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2}\right),\quad\text{ if }r=1,

and

\|\varphi_{n}^{G}-\varphi\|_{\infty}=O\left(h^{r}\right),\;\;\;\|\varphi_{n}^{S}-\varphi\|_{\infty}=O\left(h^{r+2}\right),\quad\text{if }r\geq 2.

It is also shown that the order of convergence of
\varphi_{n}^{S}
at the points of partition
\Delta^{(n)}
, is
h^{2r}
.

If an asymptotic expansion for the error exists, one can apply a well-known techniques to obtain more accurate approximations. Richardson extrapolation one such method for application. In
[
24
]
, an asymptotic expansion for the iterated Galerkin solution of Urysohn integral equation with Green’s function type of kernel, is obtained at the above mentioned partition points. Then, by
[
11
]
and using Richardson extrapolation, an approximate solution with order of convergence
h^{2r+2}
can be obtained.

In the computation of of above approximations, various integrals are involved. There is an integral in the definition of the Urysohn integral operator
\mathcal{K}
. In the definition of the orthogonal projection
\pi_{n}
, the standard inner product on
L^{2}[0,1]
comes into picture. In practice, it is necessary to replace all these integrals by a numerical quadrature formula. This gives rise to the discrete versions of the projection methods. The discrete versions of the Galerkin methods for Urysohn integral with Green’s kernel, are investigated in
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
. Whereas, in
[
17
]
, a different version of discrete projection method is discussed.

In this article, we consider the Urysohn integral equation with Green’s kernel, and discrete Galerkin method is applied for approximations. Then, an asymptotic expansion for the discrete iterated Galerkin solution is obtained.

We choose a fine partition of
[0,1]
with
m
subintervals each of length
\tilde{h}=\frac{1}{m}
and define a composite numerical quadrature formula. Replacing the integrals in the definition of
\mathcal{K}
and
\pi_{n}
, we define the Nyström operator
\mathcal{K}_{m}
and the discrete orthogonal projection
P_{n}
. Then the discrete Galerkin and the discrete iterated Galerkin equations are given by

z_{n}^{G}-P_{n}\mathcal{K}_{m}(z_{n}^{G})=P_{n}f~~~\text{ and }~~~z_{n}^{S}-\mathcal{K}_{m}(P_{n}z_{n}^{S})=f

respectively.
If
\varphi\in C^{r+2}[0,1]
, then from
[
6
]
and
[
17
]
, we have

(1.4)

\left\lVert z_{n}^{G}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(\max\left\{h^{r},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right)

(1.5)

\left\lVert z_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}O\left(\max\left\{h^{2},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right),~\;\;\;r=1,\\
O\left(\max\left\{h^{r+2},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right),~r\geq 2.\end{array}}\right.

In this article, first we find an asymptotic error expansion due to the discrete orthogonal projection. Then using this, the following asymptotic expansion is obtained:

(1.6)

z_{n}^{S}(t_{i})=\varphi(t_{i})+\gamma(t_{i})h^{2r}+O\left(\max\left\{h^{2r+2},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right),

where the function
\gamma
is independent of
h
. If we choose
m
such that
\tilde{h}\leq h^{2r+2}
, then using the Richardson extrapolation, an approximation of
\varphi
of the order of
h^{2r+2}
could be obtained. See
[
11
]
.

This article is organized as follows. Definitions, notations and some preliminary results are given in section 2. In Section 3, a quadrature rule is defined, and using it the discrete orthogonal projection and the Nyström approximations of the integral operators are defined. Section 4 contains the asymptotic error analysis for the approximations. Numerical example is given in Section 5.

Preliminaries

For an integer
\alpha\geq 0
, let
C^{\alpha}[0,1]
denotes the space of all real valued
\alpha
-times continuously differentiable functions on
[0,1]
with the norm

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\alpha,\infty}=\max_{0\leq j\leq\alpha}\left\lVert x^{(j)}\right\rVert_{\infty},

where
x^{(j)}
is the
j^{\text{th}}
derivative of the function
x
, and
\displaystyle{\left\lVert x^{(j)}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\lvert x^{(j)}(t)\rvert}
.
Define

\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\alpha,\infty}=\max_{0\leq i+j+k\leq\alpha}\left\lVert D^{(i,j,k)}\kappa(s,t,u)\right\rVert_{\infty},

where

D^{(i,j,k)}\kappa(s,t,u)=\frac{\partial^{i+j+k}\kappa}{\partial s^{i}\partial t^{j}\partial u^{k}}(s,t,u).

Green’s function type kernel

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and assume that the kernel
\kappa
has the following properties.

(1)

For
i=1,2,3,4
, the functions
\kappa,\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\in C(\Omega),

where
C(\Omega)
denotes the space of all real valued continuous function on
\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

Let

\Omega_{1}=\{(s,t,u):0\leq t\leq s\leq 1,\;u\in\mathbb{R}\}
and

\Omega_{2}=\{(s,t,u):0\leq s\leq t\leq 1,\;u\in\mathbb{R}\}.

There are two functions
\kappa_{j}\in C^{r}(\Omega_{j}),j=1,2,
such that

\kappa(s,t,u)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\kappa_{1}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\kappa_{2}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2}.\end{array}}\right.

(3)

Denote

\ell(s,t,u)=\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}(s,t,u)
and
\lambda(s,t,u)=\frac{\partial^{2}\kappa}{\partial u^{2}}(s,t,u),\>(s,t,u)\in\Omega.
The partial derivatives of
\ell(s,t,u)
and
\lambda(s,t,u)
with respect to
s
and
t
have jump discontinuities on
s=t
.

(4)

There are functions
\ell_{j},\lambda_{j}\in C^{r}(\Omega_{j}),j=1,2,
with

\ell(s,t,u)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ell_{1}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\ell_{2}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2},\end{array}}\right.\hskip 1.70717pt\lambda(s,t,u)=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{1}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\lambda_{2}(s,t,u),\;\;\;(s,t,u)\in\Omega_{2}.\end{array}}\right.

Under the above assumptions, the operator
\mathcal{K}
is four times Fréchet differentiable, and its Fréchet derivatives at
x\in\mathcal{X}
are given by

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(x)v_{1}(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial u}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)\>v_{1}(t)\>dt,

\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)\>v_{1}(t)\cdots v_{i}(t)\>dt,\quad i=2,3,4,

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)=\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\left(s,t,u\right)\rvert_{u=x(t)},\quad i=1,2,3,4

and
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\in\mathcal{X}
. Note that
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(x):\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is linear and
\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x):\mathcal{X}^{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
are multi-linear operators, where
\mathcal{X}^{i}
is the cartesian product of
i
copies of
\mathcal{X}
. See
[
25
]
. The norms of these operators are defined by
\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x)\right\rVert=\sup\left\{\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})\right\rVert_{\infty}:\left\lVert v_{j}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1,j=1,\ldots,i\right\}
for
i=1,2,3,4
. It follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(x)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s,t\leq 1}\left\lvert\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial u^{i}}\left(s,t,x(t)\right)\right\rvert,\qquad i=1,2,3,4.

Note that, if
f\in C^{\alpha}[0,1]
for any positive integer
\alpha
, then
\varphi\in C^{\alpha}[0,1]
. See
[
5
, Corollary 3.2]
,
[
6
, Corollary 4.2]
.

Discretization of Integrals by numerical quadrature Rule

In this section, first we consider a numerical integration formula. We replace the integral in the standard inner product of
L^{2}[0,1]
(i.e.
\left\langle{x}~,\,{y}\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}x(t)y(t)\>dt
) by the quadrature rule and define a discrete inner product. Subsequently, the corresponding discrete orthogonal projection is defined. After that, an asymptotic error expansion for the discrete orthogonal projection is obtained. Next we define the Nyström approximations of the integral operator
\mathcal{K}
and its Fréchet derivatives.

Consider a basic numerical integration formula by

(3.1)

\int_{0}^{1}x(t)dt\approx\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>x(\mu_{q}),

which is exact at least for polynomials of degree
\leq 3r.

If
r=0,
then it is assumed that the quadrature rule is exact atleast for linear polynomials. It follows that
\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}=1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the following uniform partition of
[0,1]:

(3.2)

\Delta^{(n)}~:\qquad 0<\frac{1}{n}<\cdots<\frac{n-1}{n}<1.

Define

t_{j}=\frac{j}{n},\;\Delta_{j}=[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
and
h=t_{j}-t_{j-1}=\frac{1}{n},\;j=1,\ldots,n.
Define the subspace
C^{\alpha}_{\Delta^{(n)}}[0,1]=\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}:x\in C^{\alpha}[t_{j-1},t_{j}],~j=1,2,3,\dots,n\right\}.

For
r\geq 1,
the approximating space

\mathcal{X}_{n}=\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}:x|_{\Delta_{j}}\text{ is a polynomial of degree }\leq r-1\right\}.

Let
p
be a positive integer and
m=pn
. Consider the following uniform partition of
[0,1]:

(3.3)

\Delta^{(m)}~:\qquad 0<\frac{1}{m}<\cdots<\frac{m-1}{m}<1.

Let

\displaystyle{\tilde{h}=\frac{1}{m}\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;s_{i}=\frac{i}{m},\;\;\;i=0,\ldots,m.}

Note : As our goal to find the equation (
1.6
), where the higher order term is
\max\left\{{h^{2r+2},\tilde{h}^{2}}\right\}
, we choose the partition
\Delta^{(m)}
such that
\tilde{h}^{2}\leq h^{r}
.

A composite integration rule with respect to the partition (
3.3
)
is then defined as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x(t)\>dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{s_{i-1}}^{s_{i}}x(t)\>dt\approx\tilde{h}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>x(s_{i-1}+\mu_{q}\tilde{h}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}x(t)\>dt=\int_{(j-1)h}^{jh}x(t)\>dt=\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\int_{(j-1)h+(\nu-1)\tilde{h}}^{(j-1)h+\nu\tilde{h}}x(t)\>dt.

Since
h=p\tilde{h}
,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}x(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\int_{\frac{(j-1)p+\nu-1}{p}h}^{\frac{(j-1)p+\nu}{p}h}x(t)\>dt.

Substituting
t\>=\>\frac{(j-1)p+\nu-1}{p}h+\tilde{h}\sigma\>=\>\frac{(j-1)p+\nu-1+\sigma}{p}h
in the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}x(t)\>dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h}{p}\>\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\int_{0}^{1}x{\left(\frac{(j-1)p+\nu-1+\sigma}{p}h\right)}\>d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h}{p}\>\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\int_{0}^{1}x{\left(t_{j-1}+\frac{\nu-1+\sigma}{p}h\right)}\>d\sigma.

Note that
\frac{\nu-1+\sigma}{p}\in[0,1].

Now using the numerical quadrature formula (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}x(t)\>dt

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{h}{p}\>\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>x{\left(t_{j-1}+\frac{\nu-1+\mu_{q}}{p}h\right)}.

Let

\mu_{q\nu}=\frac{\nu-1+\mu_{q}}{p},\quad q=1,2,\ldots,\rho;~\nu=1,2,\ldots,p.

Then,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}x(t)\>dt

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{h}{p}\>\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>x{\left(t_{j-1}+\mu_{q\nu}h\right)}.

We prove the following lemma which will be used to find an asymptotic error expansion for the discrete orthogonal projection.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L_{\eta}
be the Legendre polynomial of degree
\eta\in\left\{0,1,\dots,r-1\right\}
defined on
[0,1]
. Then for any
k=1,2,\dots,2r-1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\>\sum_{\eta=0}^{r-1}\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>L_{\eta}(\mu_{q\nu})L_{\eta}(\tau)\frac{\left(\mu_{q\nu}-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}\>=\>\int_{0}^{1}\Lambda_{r}(\tau,s)\frac{\left(s-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}\>ds,

where
\displaystyle{\sum_{\eta=0}^{r-1}L_{\eta}(\tau)L_{\eta}(s)\>=\>\Lambda_{r}(\tau,s)}
, for
\tau,s\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Since
L_{\eta}
is a polynomial of degree
0\leq\eta\leq r-1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\>\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>L_{\eta}{\left(\frac{\nu-1+\mu_{q}}{p}\right)}

\displaystyle~=~\frac{1}{p}\>\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\int_{0}^{1}L_{\eta}{\left(\frac{\nu-1+t}{p}\right)}\>dt

\displaystyle=~\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\int_{\frac{\nu-1}{p}}^{\frac{\nu}{p}}L_{\eta}(s)\>ds

\displaystyle=~\int_{0}^{1}L_{\eta}(s)\>ds.

Since the basic quadrature formula (
3.1
) is exact for polynomials of degree
\leq 3r
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\>\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>L_{\eta}{\left(\frac{\nu-1+\mu_{q}}{p}\right)}\left(\frac{\nu-1+\mu_{q}}{p}-\tau\right)^{k}~=~\int_{0}^{1}L_{\eta}(s)\left(s-\tau\right)^{k}\>ds.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\>\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>L_{\eta}{(\mu_{q\nu})}\>\frac{\left(\mu_{q\nu}-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}~=~\int_{0}^{1}L_{\eta}(s)\>\frac{\left(s-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}\>ds,

where
\displaystyle{\mu_{q\nu}=\frac{\nu-1+\mu_{q}}{p}}.
This gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\>\sum_{\eta=0}^{r-1}\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>L_{\eta}(\tau)L_{\eta}(\mu_{q\nu})\>\frac{\left(\mu_{q\nu}-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}~=~\sum_{\eta=0}^{r-1}L_{\eta}(\tau)\int_{0}^{1}L_{\eta}(s)\>\frac{\left(s-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}\>ds.

Let

\sum_{\eta=0}^{r-1}L_{\eta}(\tau)L_{\eta}(s)\>=\>\Lambda_{r}(\tau,s),\quad\tau,s\in[0,1].

Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\>\sum_{\eta=0}^{r-1}\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>L_{\eta}(\tau)L_{\eta}(\mu_{q\nu})\>\frac{\left(\mu_{q\nu}-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}~=~\int_{0}^{1}\Lambda_{r}(\tau,s)\>\frac{\left(s-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}\>ds.

Hence the required result follows.
∎

Discrete Orthogonal Projection

Let
j\in\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
and
x,y\in C(\Delta_{j})
. Define a discrete inner product on
\Delta_{j}
by

(3.5)

\left\langle{x}~,\,{y}\right\rangle_{\Delta_{j},m}=\tilde{h}\sum_{\nu=1}^{p}\sum_{q=1}^{\rho}w_{q}\>x{\left(t_{j-1}+\mu_{q\nu}h\right)}\>y{\left(t_{j-1}+\mu_{q\nu}h\right)}.

Note that, this is an indefinite inner product. For more details on indefinite inner product spaces, see
[
8
]
. However, the properties which we need to define a discrete orthogonal projection, hold true for (
3.5
).
For
\eta=0,1,\ldots,r-1,
let
L_{\eta}
denote the Legendre polynomial of degree
\eta
on
[0,1].

For
j=2,\ldots,n,
and for
\eta=0,1,\ldots,r-1,
define

\displaystyle\varphi_{j,\eta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\frac{1}{h}}L_{\eta}\left(\frac{t-t_{j-1}}{h}\right),\;\;\;t\in(t_{j-1},t_{j}],\\
0,\;\;\;\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}}\right.

and,
\varphi_{1,\eta}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{h}}L_{\eta}\left(\frac{t-t_{0}}{h}\right)
if
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
and

0
otherwise.
Note that

(3.9)

\varphi_{j,\eta}\left(t_{j-1}+\mu_{q\nu}h\right)=h^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{\eta}(\mu_{q\nu})\quad\text{ for all }j=1,2,\dots,n.

Note that
\displaystyle{\{\varphi_{j,\eta}:~j=1,\ldots,n,\;\eta=0,1,\ldots,r-1\}}
be a set of orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{X}_{n},
where
\varphi_{j,\eta}
is the Legendre polynomial of degree
\eta
defined on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
. Since the basic numerical integration (
3.1
) has degree of precision
3r
, the set
\left\{\varphi_{j,\eta}\right\}
is also orthonormal with respect to the discrete inner product (
3.5
). Let
\mathcal{P}_{r,\Delta_{j}}
be the space of polynomials of degree
\leq r-1
on
\Delta_{j}
. Define the discrete orthogonal projection
P_{n,j}:C[t_{j-1},t_{j}]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{r,\Delta_{j}}
as follows:

(3.10)

P_{n,j}x=\sum_{\eta=0}^{r-1}\left\langle{x}~,\,{\varphi_{j,\eta}}\right\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}\varphi_{j,\eta}.

See
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
for more details.
A discrete orthogonal projection
P_{n}:C[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{n}
is defined by

(3.11)

\displaystyle P_{n}x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{n,j}x.

It follows that
P_{n}x(t)=P_{n,j}x(t),
for all
t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
. We also have the following error bound:

\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert<\infty
and also, if

, then

(3.12)

\left\lVert x-P_{n,j}x\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\leq C_{1}\left\lVert x^{(r)}\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}h^{r},

, then

(3.13)

\left\lVert x-P_{n}x\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\leq C_{1}\left\lVert x^{(r)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{r},

where
\displaystyle{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}=\sup_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}|x(t)|}
and,
C_{1}
is a constant independent of

. For details see
[
17
]
.

In (
3.13
) we have a error bound for the discrete orthogonal projection. But, by the following lemma we obtain an asymptotic error expansion for the discrete orthogonal projection, which is more stronger result than (
3.13
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P_{n}
be the discrete orthogonal projection defined by (
3.10
) - (
3.11
). Let
\displaystyle{x\in C^{2r+2}_{\Delta^{(n)}}[0,1]}
and
t=t_{j-1}+\tau h
with
\tau\in[0,1]
. Then

P_{n}x(t)-x(t)=\>\sum_{k=1}^{2r+1}\>J_{k}(\tau)\>x^{(k)}(t_{j-1}+\tau h)\>h^{k}~+O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),

where

\displaystyle{J_{k}(\tau)=\int_{0}^{1}\Lambda_{r}(\tau,s)\frac{\left(s-\tau\right)^{k}}{k!}\>ds},\quad k=1,2,\dots,2r+1.

Proof.

Define a function
v_{j}:[t_{j-1},t_{j}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

v_{j}(t)=1,\quad t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}].

For
\tau\in[0,1]
, let
t=t_{j-1}+h\tau

\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}].
From (
3.10
) it is easy to see that

P_{n,j}v_{j}=v_{j}.

It follows that

\sum_{\eta=0}^{r-1}\left\langle{v_{j}}~,\,{\varphi_{j,\eta}}\right\rangle_{\Delta_{j}}\varphi_{j,\eta}(t)=1.

Since

\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{m})-\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)\right](z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}=O\left(h^{4}\right).

On the other hand

\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}(t_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle{\ell_{m,t_{i}}}~,\,{(I-P_{n,j})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}}\right\rangle_{\Delta_{j},m}.

Since
I-P_{n,j}
is self-adjoint,

\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}(t_{i})\\
=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle{(I-P_{n,j})\ell_{m,t_{i}}}~,\,{(I-P_{n,j})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}}\right\rangle_{\Delta_{j},m}.

It follows that

\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\left\lvert\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}(t_{i})\right\rvert\\
\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert(I-P_{n,j})\ell_{m,t_{i}}\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}\left\lVert(I-P_{n,j})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}\right\rVert_{\Delta_{j},\infty}h.

By Corollary
4.2
and Lemma
4.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\lvert\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}(t_{i})\rvert=O\left(h^{4}\right),\quad r=1.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{m})(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})^{2}(t_{i})=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 2.84526ptO\left(h^{4}\right),\hskip 74.54608ptr=1,\\
O\left(\max\left\{h^{3r},h^{r}\tilde{h}^{4}\right\}\right),\quad r\geq 2.\end{array}}\right.

Then combing (
4.20
), (
4.21
) and (
4
), we obtain

\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}_{m}(z_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}_{m}(\varphi_{m})-{\mathcal{K}}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})\right](t_{i})\\
=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 2.84526ptO\left(h^{4}\right),\hskip 74.54608ptr=1,\\
O\left(\max\left\{h^{3r},h^{r}\tilde{h}^{4}\right\}\right),\quad r\geq 2.\end{array}}\right.

This follows the result.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

If
\varphi_{m}
and
z_{n}^{G}
are respectively the Nyström and the discrete Galerkin approximation of
\varphi
, then

\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n}){\mathcal{K}}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})(t_{i})=O\left(\max\left\{h^{2r+2},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right).

Proof.

Adding and subtracting
\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n}){\mathcal{K}}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})\\
=\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})-\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right](z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})+\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m}).

Then, using (
1.4
), (
3.20
) and the Corollary
4.2
, we obtain for
r\geq 1
,

(4.25)

\left\lvert\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})-\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)\right](z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})(t_{i})\right\rvert\\
\leq C_{4}C_{8}\left(1+\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert\right)h^{r}\tilde{h}^{2}\left(\max\left\{h^{r},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right)

Note that

\displaystyle z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m}=P_{n}z_{n}^{S}-\varphi_{m}

\displaystyle=P_{n}z_{n}^{S}-P_{n}\varphi+P_{n}\varphi-\varphi+\varphi-\varphi_{m}

\displaystyle=P_{n}\left(z_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right)-\left(I-P_{n}\right)\varphi+\left(\varphi-\varphi_{m}\right).

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)P_{n}\left(z_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)\left(I-P_{n}\right)\varphi

\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)\left(\varphi-\varphi_{m}\right).

By the Corollary
4.2

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)P_{n}\left(z_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right)(t_{i})\right\rvert\leq C_{8}\left\lVert\left(I-P_{n}\right)\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)P_{n}\left(z_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right)\right\rVert h^{r},

then by (
1.5
) and Corollary
4.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)P_{n}\left(z_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right)(t_{i})\right\rvert

\displaystyle=O\left(\max\left\{h^{2r+2},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right),\quad\text{ for }r\geq 1.

Also, the Corollary
4.2
and (
4.24
) implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)\left(I-P_{n}\right)\varphi(t_{i})=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right),\quad\text{ for }r\geq 1.

It is easy to see (from (
3.17
) and Corollary
4.2
) that

\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)\left(\varphi-\varphi_{m}\right)=O\left(h^{r}\tilde{h}^{2}\right),\quad\text{ for }r\geq 1.

Therefore, for
r\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\mathcal{K}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi)(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})(t_{i})=O\left(\max\left\{h^{2r+2},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right).

Hence, the result follows from (
4.25
) and the above equation.
∎

We prove the main theorem as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the Urysohn integral operator with Green’s function type kernel
\kappa
, defined by (
1.2
).
Let
\varphi
be the unique solution of the equation (
1.1
). Assume that
1
is not an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\varphi).
Let
\mathcal{X}_{n}
be the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq r-1
with respect to the partition
\Delta^{(n)}:=0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1
defined by (
3.2
). Let
P_{n}:L^{\infty}[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{n}
be the discrete orthogonal projection defined by (
3.11
) and
\tilde{z}_{n}^{S}
be the discrete iterated Galerkin approximation of
\varphi
. Then

\displaystyle\left(z_{n}^{S}-\varphi\right)(t_{i})=\left[\mathcal{E}_{2r}(\varphi)(t_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}(\varphi)(t_{i})\right]h^{2r}+O\left(\max\left\{h^{2r+2},\tilde{h}^{2}\right\}\right),

where the operators
\mathcal{E}_{2r}
and
\mathcal{T}
are respectively defined by (
3.16
) and (
4.5
).

Proof.

We have from (
4
)

\displaystyle z_{n}^{S}-\varphi=

\displaystyle\left[I-{\mathcal{K}}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})\right]^{-1}\left[\mathcal{K}_{m}(z_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}_{m}(\varphi_{m})-{\mathcal{K}}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})\right]

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\left[\mathcal{K}_{m}(z_{n}^{G})-\mathcal{K}_{m}(\varphi_{m})-{\mathcal{K}}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})\right]

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n}){\mathcal{K}}_{m}^{\prime}(\varphi_{m})(z_{n}^{G}-\varphi_{m})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{m}(I-P_{n})\varphi_{m}

\displaystyle+\varphi_{m}-\varphi.

The result now follows from (
3.17
), Proposition
4.1
, Proposition
4.2
, Proposition
4.3
and Proposition
4.4
.

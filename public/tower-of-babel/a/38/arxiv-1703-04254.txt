Cwikel estimates revisited
Galina Levitina, Fedor Sukochev, Dmitriy Zanin

Abstract




1.
Introduction

One of the most beautiful results in operator theory are the so-called Cwikel estimates concerning the singular values of the operator
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)
on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, where
M_{f}
denotes the multiplication operator on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by the function
f
and
\nabla
denotes the gradient
[
39
]
. It was conjectured by Simon
[
38
]
and proved by Cwikel
[
12
]
(see also
[
6
]
) that conditions
f\in L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
g\in L_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
p>2
, ensure that the operator
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)
belongs to the weak Schatten ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and the norm of
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)
in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
is dominated by the product of
\|f\|_{p}
and
\|g\|_{p,\infty}
. Estimates given in Simon’s book
[
39
]
and deeper results in this direction can be found in
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
45
]
.

The operators
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)
and
f(i\nabla)M_{g}
are unitarily equivalent, and therefore, their singular numbers coincide. However, the results given in
[
12
,
39
]
do not reflect the fact that the functions
f
and
g
are interchangeable. Noting this deficiency, T.Weidl in
[
45
]
and
[
46
]
suggested a new, symmetric, approach to Cwikel estimates, proving that

\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}(M_{f}g(-i\nabla))\leq\const C\mu^{2}_{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}(f\otimes g),\quad f\otimes g\in(L_{2}+L_{\infty})({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})

with some absolute constant. Here,
\mu(x)
denotes the singular value function of an operator
x
(see Section
2
for definition) and
C
is the Cesaro operator.
In this paper we prove a much stronger version of this result, namely that

(1)

C\mu^{2}(M_{f}g(-i\nabla))\leq\const C\mu^{2}(f\otimes g).

The trivial estimate
\mu^{2}(M_{f}g(-i\nabla))\leq C\mu^{2}(M_{f}g(-i\nabla))
, immediately implies Weidl’s result above.

In fact, we prove a radically more general version of this estimate for noncommutative variables
f
and
g
. Namely, let
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
be semifinite von Neumann algebras represented on the same (separable) Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and let
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
be faithful normal semifinite traces on
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2},
respectively. We denote by
\pi_{1}
(respectively,
\pi_{2}
) the representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
(respectively,
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
) on
{\mathcal{H}}
. We assume that the representation of the algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
are intertwined in such a way that the estimate holds in the Hilbert-Schmidt ideal, that is, if
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1}),y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
, then
\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{H}})
and

\|\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\|_{2}\leq\const\|x\|_{2}\|y\|_{2}.

Under this assumption we prove our first main result, which implies (
1
) in the special case when
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
coincide with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
x\otimes y\in({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
. Then
\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
and

\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))\prec\prec 532\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{2}(x\otimes y),

in the sense of Hardy-Littlewood majorisation.

As an immediate corollary of this estimate and the Lorentz-Shimogaki interpolation theorem
[
33
]
we obtain abstract Cwikel estimates in an arbitrary symmetric space, which is an interpolation space for the Banach couple
(L_{2},L_{\infty})
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(E(0,\infty),\|\cdot\|_{E})
be an interpolation space for
(L_{2},L_{\infty})
and let
x\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1}),y\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
be such that
x\otimes y\in E({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})
. Then
\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\in E({\mathcal{H}})
and

\|\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\|_{E({\mathcal{H}})}\leq C_{E}\|x\otimes y\|_{E(\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{2})}.

The result of Corollary
1.2
strengthens results of
[
12
]
,
[
5
, Section 4]
,
[
45
]
.
Furthermore, in Theorem
5.1
we show that our assumption on the symmetric function space
E(0,\infty)
in Corollary
1.2
is optimal. That is, if we omit the assumption that
E(0,\infty)
is an
(L_{2},L_{\infty})
-interpolation space, then the corresponding Cwikel estimates fail. The counterexample (see Theorem
5.1
) is yielded by the space
L_{2,\infty}(0,\infty)
, which is not an
(L_{2},L_{\infty})
-interpolation space (and is not covered by
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
39
]
,
[
45
]
) and uses the classical operator
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)
in the one-dimensional setting. Nevertheless, we are in a position to present a new positive result for the ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{2,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
in the classical setting of operators
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)
. Our approach here is based on proving first that the space
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
is logarithmically convex (thus extending the classical result of
[
42
]
and
[
25
]
to the noncommutative setting). Using this result we are able to prove a modified version of Cwikel estimates for the weak Schatten ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{2,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
g\in\ell_{2,\infty}(L_{4})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

f\in\ell_{2,\log}(L_{\infty})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{2,\infty}\leq\const\|f\|_{2,\log}\|g\|_{\ell_{2,\infty}(L_{4})}.

(For the definition of the space
\ell_{2,\log}(L_{\infty})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the reader is referred to Definition
5.5
).

Now, we briefly describe our results for the case
0<p<2
, which are dramatically different from the case
p>2
(see Corollary
1.2
). Recall that the assumption
f,g\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
does not guarantee that the operator
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)
belongs to the trace-class. The best result to date for the weak Schatten ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
,
0<p<2
(see
[
5
]
) is that
f\in\ell_{p}(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
g\in\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
guarantee that
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. Observe that this result has the same asymmetry as Cwikel’s result for
p>2
. We rectify this deficiency in the following

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0<p<2
.

(i)

If
f\otimes g\in\ell_{p}(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
, then
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\|f\otimes g\|_{\ell_{p}(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

(ii)

If
f\otimes g\in\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
, then
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{p,\infty}\leq C_{p}\|f\otimes g\|_{\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

It should be pointed out that our approach of the proof of Theorem
1.4
is totally different and substantially simpler than those used in
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
39
]
. It is based on the classical (strong) majorisation introduced yet by Hardy and Littlewood,
and allows us to substantially strengthen all earlier results.

Now, we discuss applications of our results to classical problems in mathematical physics. Recall that Cwikel estimates were firstly applied in
[
12
]
to prove the now famous Cwikel-Lieb-Rozenblum bounds on the number of negative eigenvalues of the Schroedinger operator
-\Delta+V
. Prompted by development of noncommutative geometry, in
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
the notions of noncommutative plane (or rather noncommutative Euclidean space) and of the corresponding Dirac operator were introduced. In Section
6
we suggest a new approach to this notion, which is based on twisted convolution product approach. Our noncommutative Euclidean space
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
, where
\theta
is
d\times d
antisymmetric and real matrix,
d
is even, is unitary equivalent to the Moyal plane
[
37
]
,
[
19
]
, however, our approach appears to be substantially simpler than the treatment based on the Moyal product.
The abstract Cwikel estimate established in Theorem
1.1
can be immediately applied to obtain the noncommutative analogue of Cwikel-Lieb-Rozenblum bound (see Corollary
7.3
) for the noncommutative Schroedinger operator
-\Delta_{\theta}+x
, where
\Delta_{\theta}
is the Laplacian associated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
,
d\geq 4
, and
x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
has certain decay properties.

We also note, in passing, that although our approach to the definition of Dirac operator associated to
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
differes from
[
17
]
, it allows us to introduce Sobolev spaces
W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
in
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
in a natural way, mimicking the definition of the classical Sobolev spaces
W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. These Sobolev spaces
W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
are drastically different from the spaces introduced in
[
19
]
(see also
[
10
]
). Using this new definition of Sobolev spaces we can significantly improve Cwikel estimates for weak Schatten ideals when
1\leq p\leq 2
compared to those given in
[
19
]
. For
p=1
these Cwikel estimate are crucial for summability condition for nonunital spectral triples (see
[
10
]
).

Finally, in Section
8
we discuss connection between the noncommutative Euclidean space and the magnetic Laplacian and demonstrate the applicability of noncommutative Cwikel estimates to the latter object.

Throughout the paper we use
the convention that constants
C_{d}
,
c_{d,\theta}
,
\const
etc
are strictly positive constants whose value depends only on their subscripts and can change from line to line.

In what follows,
{\mathcal{H}}
is a Hilbert space and
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is the

*
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
,
\mathcal{M}
is
a von Neumann algebra on
{\mathcal{H}}
, equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
. For details on von Neumann algebra
theory, the reader is referred to e.g.
[
14
]
,
[
23
,
24
]

or
[
43
]
.

A linear operator
x:\mathfrak{D}\left(x\right)\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
,
where the domain
\mathfrak{D}\left(x\right)
of
x
is a linear
subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}
, is said to be
affiliated
with
\mathcal{M}

if
yx\subseteq xy
for all
y\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is the commutant of
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
x
be a self-adjoint operator affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
.
We denote its spectral measure by
\{E_{x}\}
. It is well known that if

x
is a closed operator affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
with the
polar decomposition
x=u|x|
, then
u\in\mathcal{M}
and
E\in\mathcal{M}
for all projections
E\in\{E_{|x|}\}
.
An operator
x\in S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
is called
\tau
-measurable if there exists
\lambda>0
such that
\tau(E_{|x|}(\lambda,\infty))<\infty
.

Consider the algebra
\mathcal{M}=L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
of all
Lebesgue measurable essentially bounded functions on
(0,\infty)
.
Algebra
\mathcal{M}
can be seen as an abelian von Neumann
algebra acting via multiplication on the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(0,\infty)
, with the trace given by integration
with respect to Lebesgue measure
m.

It is easy to see that the
algebra of all
\tau
-measurable operators
affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
can be identified with
the subalgebra
S(0,\infty)
of the algebra of Lebesgue measurable functions
L_{0}(0,\infty)
which consists of all functions
x
on
(0,\infty)
such that

m(\{|x|>s\})
is finite for some
s>0
.

If
\mathcal{M}={\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
(respectively,
l_{\infty}
) and
\tau
is the
standard trace
\operatorname{tr}
(respectively, the counting measure on

\mathbb{Z}_{+}
), then it is not difficult to see that

S(\mathcal{M})=S(\mathcal{M},\tau)=\mathcal{M}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
be equipped
with a faithful normal semi-finite trace
\tau
and let
x\in S(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. The generalized singular value function
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(x):t\rightarrow\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(t;x)
of
the operator
x
is defined by setting

\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(t;x)=\inf\{\|xp\|_{\infty}:\ p=p^{*}\in\mathcal{M}\mbox{ is a projection,}\ \tau(\mathbf{1}-p)\leq t\}.

When the algebra
({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is clear from the context, we will use the notation
\mu(\cdot)
instead of
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
.

An equivalent definition in terms of the
distribution function of the operator
x
is the following. For every self-adjoint
operator
x\in S(\mathcal{M},\tau),
setting

d_{x}(t)=\tau(E_{x}(t,\infty)),\quad t>0,

we have (see e.g.
[
15
]
)

(2)

\displaystyle\mu(t;x)=\inf\{s\geq 0:\ d_{|x|}(s)\leq t\}.

It is clear that
\mu(x)=\mu(|x|)
for
x\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
. Moreover,

(3)

\mu^{2}(x)=\mu(x^{*}x)=\mu(xx^{*})

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
32
, Corollary 2.3.16]
) that

(4)

\mu(t+s,x+y)\leq\mu(t,x)+\mu(s,y),\quad x,y\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau),\quad t,s>0.

For positive operators
x,y\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
we have that

(5)

\mu(x^{1/2}yx^{1/2})=\mu^{2}(x^{1/2}y^{1/2})=\mu^{2}(y^{1/2}x^{1/2})=\mu(y^{1/2}xy^{1/2}).

We also have that

(6)

\mu(|x|E_{|x|}(\alpha,\infty))=\mu(x)\chi_{[0,d_{x}(\alpha))},\quad\alpha>0.

If
\mathcal{M}=L^{\infty}(0,\infty)
, then the
generalized singular value function
\mu(x)
is precisely the
decreasing rearrangement
\mu(x)
of the function
x
(see e.g.
[
30
]
) defined by

\mu(t;x)=\inf\{s\geq 0:\ m(\{|x|\geq s\})\leq t\}.

If
\mathcal{M}={\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
and
\tau
is the
standard trace
\operatorname{tr}
, then

\mu(n;x)=\mu(t;x),\quad t\in[n,n+1),\quad n\geq 0.

The sequence
\{\mu(n;x)\}_{n\geq 0}
is just the sequence of singular values of the operator
x

[
20
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A linear subspace
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})
of
S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
equipped with a complete (quasi-)norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
, is called symmetric space (of
\tau
-measurable operators) if
x\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
,
y\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})
and
\mu(x)\leq\mu(y)
imply that
x\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})
and
\|x\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\|y\|_{\mathcal{E}}
. In the case when
({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is
({\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}),\operatorname{tr})
we denote
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})
by
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

It is well-known that any symmetric space
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})
is a normed
{\mathcal{M}}
-bimodule, that is
axb\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})
for any
x\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})
,
a,b\in{\mathcal{M}}
and

\|axb\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\|a\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{\infty}\|x\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

The so-called Calkin correspondence provides a construction of symmetric operator spaces associated with the von Neumman algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
from concrete symmetric function spaces studied extensively in e.g.
[
30
]
. Namely, let
(E(0,\infty),\|\cdot\|_{E(0,\infty)})
be a symmetric function space on the semi-axis
(0,\infty)
. Then the pair

{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})=\{x\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau):\mu(x)\in E(0,\infty)\},\quad\|x\|_{{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})}:=\|\mu(x)\|_{E(0,\infty)}

is a symmetric space on
{\mathcal{M}}

[
27
]
(see also
[
32
]
). For convenience, we denote
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{M}})}
by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
.

In particular, the noncommutative Schatten space
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
,
p>0
, is the symmetric space corresponding to the classical
L_{p}
-space of functions
L_{p}(0,\infty)
, that is

{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{M}})=\{x\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau):\mu(x)\in L_{p}(0,\infty)\}.

This space can be also described as the space of all
\tau
-measurable operator
x
, such that
\tau(|x|^{p})<\infty
with
\|x\|_{p}=(\tau(|x|^{p}))^{1/p}.

For the general
L_{p}
spaces, the Hölder inequality holds as well, that is if
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{1}}({\mathcal{M}})
,
y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{1}}({\mathcal{M}})
,
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{q}
, then
xy\in{\mathcal{L}}_{q}({\mathcal{M}})
and

(7)

\|xy\|_{q}\leq\|x\|_{p_{1}}\|y\|_{p_{2}}.

Moreover, if
x,y\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
are such that
xy,yx\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
, then we also have that (
[
9
, Theorem 17]
)

(8)

\tau(xy)=\tau(yx).

The symmetric space
S_{0}({\mathcal{M}})
of
\tau
-compact operators on
{\mathcal{M}}
is defined as

S_{0}({\mathcal{M}})=\{x\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau):\lim_{s\to\infty}\mu(s,x)=0\}.

Another important example of symmetric spaces are weak Schatten spaces,
p>0
, defined by

{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})=\{x\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau):\mu(x)\in L_{p,\infty}(0,\infty)\},

equipped with the quasi-norm

\|x\|_{p,\infty}=\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}\mu(t,x),\quad x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{M}}).

Next, we recall the definition of tensor product of unbounded operators (see e.g.
[
29
]
,
[
24
]
). Let
x_{1},x_{2}
be closed densely defined operators on Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
with domains
\operatorname{dom}(x_{1})
and
\operatorname{dom}(x_{2})
respectively. The algebraic tensor product
x_{1}\otimes_{\rm alg}x_{2}
of the operators
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
is a linear operator defined on the algebraic tensor product
\operatorname{dom}(x_{1})\otimes\operatorname{dom}(x_{2})
by setting

(x_{1}\otimes_{\rm alg}x_{2})\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}\otimes\eta_{k}\Big)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{1}\xi_{k}\otimes x_{2}\eta_{k},\quad\xi_{k}\in\operatorname{dom}(x_{1}),\eta_{k}\in\operatorname{dom}(x_{2}).

The operator
(x_{1}\otimes_{\rm alg}x_{2})
is densely defined and closable, but not necessarily closed. The closure of the operator
(x_{1}\otimes_{\rm alg}x_{2})
is denoted by
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}
and is called the tensor product of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. If
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are self-adjoint (respectively, positive), then the operator
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}
is also self-adjoint (respectively, positive).

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
,
i=1,2
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra on
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{i})
and let
\tau_{i}
be a faithful normal semifinite trace on
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
. We denote by
({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
the spatial tensor product of
({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1})
and
({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
(see e.g.
[
43
, Section 4.5]
). If
x_{i}
is affiliated with
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, then
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}
is affiliated with
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
. However, if
x_{i}\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{i},\tau_{i})
,
i=1,2
, then the inclusion
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
does not hold in general.

Let
E(0,\infty)
be a symmetric function space. In what follows, the notation
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{A}}_{2})
, means that
x_{i}\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{i},\tau_{i})
,
i=1,2
, are such that
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
and
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{A}}_{2}).

Note, that under the assumption
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
the following equality holds (see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem 3.8]
)

(9)

\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})=\mu\big(\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}(x_{1})\otimes\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x_{2})\big).

For the special case of weak Schatten spaces we have the following lemma (see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

If
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
and
y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{N}})
, then
x\otimes y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{M}}\otimes{\mathcal{N}})
and

\|x\otimes y\|_{p,\infty}\leq\|x\|_{p}\|y\|_{p,\infty}.

Proof.

Let
z(t)=\frac{1}{t^{1/p}}
. By the definition of weak
L_{p}
we have that

\mu(y)\leq\|y\|_{p,\infty}z.

Therefore,

\|x\otimes y\|_{p,\infty}=\|x\otimes\mu(y)\|_{p,\infty}\leq\|y\|_{p,\infty}\|x\otimes z\|_{p,\infty}.

We have
\mu(x\otimes z)=\mu(\mu(x)\otimes\mu(z))=\|x\|_{p}z
, where the second equality follows from (the proof of)
[
31
, Theorem 2.f.2]
. Therefore, since
\|z\|_{p,\infty}=1
, the assertion follows.

2.1.
Majorisation

Let
x,y\in S(\mathcal{M},\tau).
We say that
y
is submajorized
by
x
in the sense of Hardy-Littlewood-Polya if

\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,y)ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,x)ds,\quad\forall\,t>0.

In this case, we write
y\prec\prec x.

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
32
, Theorem 3.3.3]
) that

(10)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\prec\prec\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(x_{k}),\quad x_{k}\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau),\,k=1,\dots n,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

The Hardy-Littlewood-Polya submajorization plays crucial role in our approach to abstract Cwikel estimates as it gives a necessary and sufficient condition for a symmetric function space to be interpolation space for
(L_{p}(0,\infty),L_{\infty}(0,\infty))
,
p\geq 1
.
Namely, it is well-known that a symmetric function space
E(0,\infty)
is an (exact) interpolation space for
(L_{1}(0,\infty),L_{\infty}(0,\infty))
if and only if condition
y\in E(0,\infty)
,
x\in(L_{1}+L_{\infty})(0,\infty)
and
x\prec\prec y
implies that
x\in E(0,\infty)
and
\|x\|_{E}\leq\|y\|_{E}
. The Lorentz-Shimogaki theorem
[
33
]
(see also
[
40
]
) gives a similar description for interpolation spaces for
(L_{2}(0,\infty),L_{\infty}(0,\infty))
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
E(0,\infty)
be a symmetric function space. The space
E(0,\infty)
is an interpolation space for the pair
(L_{2}(0,\infty),L_{\infty}(0,\infty))
if and only if condition
\mu^{2}(f)\prec\prec\mu^{2}(g),f\in S(0,\infty),g\in E(0,\infty)
implies that
f\in E(0,\infty)
and
\|f\|_{E}\leq C_{E}\|g\|_{E}
for some constant
C
independent of
f
and
g
.

For convenience we recall the following elementary lemma (see e.g.
[
33
, (2.8)]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f,g\in(L_{p}+L_{\infty})(0,\infty),p\geq 1
and
f\prec\prec g
. Then
f^{p}\prec\prec g^{p}
.

The Lorentz-Shimogaki Theorem
2.4
allows us to prove (in particular) Cwikel estimates in ideals
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{H}})
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
for
p>2
only. To obtain Cwikel estimate for
p<2
we recall the notion of majorization of operators (see e.g.
[
34
]
).

Definition 2.6
.

We say that
y
is majorised by
x
(notation
y\prec x
), if
y\prec\prec x
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(s,y)ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(s,x)ds,

assuming that both integrals are finite.

The following proposition shows that in contrast to the case, when
p>2
, in the case when
0<p<2
, the spaces
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
respect the majorization in the reverse order. This fact appears to have escaped the attention of experts in this area. We refer to
[
3
]
where the germ of the idea used below can be discerned.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
0<p<2
and let
x,y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
be such that
\mu^{2}(x)\prec\mu^{2}(y)
.

(i)

If
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
, then
y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
and
\|y\|_{p}\leq\|x\|_{p}
.

(ii)

If
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
, then
y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
and
\|y\|_{p,\infty}\leq c_{p}\|x\|_{p,\infty}.

Proof.

Due to Calkin correspondence it is sufficient to prove the assertion for the special case, when
{\mathcal{M}}=L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
.

(i). Let
f,g\in L_{2}(0,\infty)
,
\mu^{2}(f)\prec\mu^{2}(g)
and
f\in L_{p}(0,\infty)
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
f=\mu(f),\,g=\mu(g)
. Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Since

\int_{0}^{n}g^{2}(s)ds\geq\int_{0}^{n}f^{2}(s)ds
, there exists
a_{n}\in(0,n]
such that

\int_{0}^{a_{n}}g^{2}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{n}f^{2}(s)ds.

We set

\displaystyle f_{n}=f\chi_{(0,n)},\quad g_{n}=g\chi_{(0,a_{n})}.

We claim that
f_{n}^{2}\prec g_{n}^{2}
.

Let
0<t\leq a_{n}
. Since
a_{n}\leq n
we have

\int_{0}^{t}f^{2}_{n}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{t}f^{2}(s)ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}g^{2}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{t}g^{2}_{n}(s)ds.

If
t>a_{n}
, then

\int_{0}^{t}f_{n}^{2}(s)ds\leq\int_{0}^{n}f^{2}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{a_{n}}g^{2}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{t}g^{2}_{n}(s)ds.

In addition, the choice of
a_{n}
ensures that

\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{n}^{2}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}g_{n}^{2}(s)ds.

Thus,
f_{n}^{2}\prec g_{n}^{2}
as required.

By
[
3
, Lemma 25]
(scaling the Lebesgue measure on
(0,n)
) we have that

\|g_{n}\|_{p}=\|g_{n}^{2}\|_{p/2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|f_{n}^{2}\|_{p/2}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|f_{n}\|_{p}.

Passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
we infer the inequality

\|g\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p},

as required.

(ii). Let
f,g\in L_{2}(0,\infty)
,
\mu^{2}(f)\prec\mu^{2}(g)
and
f\in L_{p,\infty}(0,\infty)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f=\mu(f),g=\mu(g)
.

Fix
t>0
. Since

\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{2}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{2}(s)ds

and

\int_{0}^{t}f^{2}(s)ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}g^{2}(s)ds

we have

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}g^{2}(s)ds\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}f^{2}(s)ds

Since
f=\mu(f)\in L_{p,\infty}(0,\infty)
we have that
f^{2}\in L_{p/2,\infty}(0,\infty)
and

f^{2}(s)\leq\|f^{2}\|_{\frac{p}{2},\infty}s^{-2/p},\quad s>0.

Therefore, since
p<2
, we have

(12)

\int_{t}^{\infty}f^{2}(s)ds\leq\|f^{2}\|_{p/2,\infty}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-2/p}ds=\const\|f\|^{2}_{p,\infty}t^{1-2/p}.

Since
g^{2}
is decreasing we have

\displaystyle tg^{2}(2t)\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}g^{2}(s)ds\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq1}}}{{\leq}}\int_{t}^{\infty}f^{2}(s)ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq2}}}{{\leq}}\const\|f\|^{2}_{p,\infty}t^{1-2/p}.

Hence, dividing both sides by
t
and taking the square roots we obtain that

g(2t)\leq\const\|f\|_{p,\infty}t^{1/p},

or equivalently

g(t)\leq 2^{-1/p}\const\|f\|_{p,\infty}t^{1/p}.

Thus,
g\in L_{p,\infty}(0,\infty)
and

\|g\|_{p,\infty}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{p,\infty}.

∎

Next, we recall the definition of direct sum of operators (see
[
32
]
).

Definition 2.8
.

Let
x_{k}\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
,
k\geq 0
. If
\{p_{k}\}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
is a sequence of pairwise orthogonal projections and if
y_{k}\in p_{k}S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)p_{k}
are such that
\mu(y_{k})=\mu(x_{k})
, then we write

\bigoplus_{k}x_{k}:=\sum_{k}y_{k}.

The particular choice of operators
y_{k},k\geq 0
is inessential, we are only interested in the fact that

\mu\Big(\bigoplus_{k}x_{k}\Big)=\mu\Big(\bigoplus_{k}\mu(x_{k})\Big).

Hence, if
x_{k}\prec y_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then

(13)

\bigoplus x_{k}\prec\bigoplus y_{k}.

The following lemma allows us to compare the direct sum of operators with series of these operators in term of the majorisation defined in Definition
2.6
.

Lemma 2.9
.

(i)

Let
T_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
be such that
\sum_{k}\|T_{k}\|_{2}^{2}<\infty.

If
\{T_{k}\}
are disjoint from the right or from the left, then

\mu^{2}(\bigoplus_{k}T_{k})\prec\mu^{2}(\sum_{k}T_{k}).

(ii)

Let
x_{k},y_{k}\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
be such that
\sum_{k,l}\|x_{k}y_{l}\|_{2}^{2}<\infty
. If
\{y_{k}\}
are disjoint from the right and
\{x_{k}\}
is disjoint from the left, then

\mu^{2}(\bigoplus_{k,l}x_{k}y_{l})\prec\mu^{2}(\sum_{k,l}x_{k}y_{l}).

Proof.

(i). It is sufficient to prove the assertion under the assumption that
T_{k}
are disjoint from the right, since the assertion for operators disjoint from the left can be proved similarly by taking adjoins.

We firstly note, that since
T_{k}
are disjoint from the right, it follows that

\Big\|\sum_{k=n}^{m}T_{k}\Big\|_{2}^{2}=\tau\Big(\sum_{k,l=n}^{m}T_{k}^{*}T_{l}\Big)=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\tau\Big(T_{k}^{*}T_{k}\Big)=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\|T_{k}\|_{2}^{2},

whenever
m>n,\,n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since the series
\sum_{k}\|T_{k}\|_{2}^{2}
converges, we obtain that the series
\sum_{k}T_{k}
converges in
{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
, and moreover,

\Big|\sum_{k}T_{k}\Big|^{2}=\sum_{k,l}T_{k}^{*}T_{l}=\sum_{k}T_{k}^{*}T_{k}.

By
[
32
, Lemma 3.3.7]
(see also
[
11
]
) we have that

\mu^{2}\Big(\bigoplus_{k}T_{k}\Big)=\mu\Big(\bigoplus_{k}|T_{k}|^{2}\Big)\prec\prec\mu\Big(\sum_{k}T_{k}^{*}T_{k}\Big)=\mu^{2}\Big(\sum_{k}T_{k}\Big),

which proves submajorisation.

Since the series
\sum_{k}T_{k}
converges in
{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu^{2}\Big(s,\bigoplus_{k}T_{k}\Big)ds=\Big\|\bigoplus_{k}T_{k}\Big\|_{2}^{2}=\Big\|\sum_{k}T_{k}\Big\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu^{2}\Big(s,\sum_{k}T_{k}\Big)ds,

which concludes the proof.

(ii). By the assumption we have that the operators
\{x_{k}y_{l}\}_{k,l}
are disjoint from the left and from the right. Hence, repeating the same argument as in part (i), we have that the series
\sum_{k,l}x_{k}y_{l}
converges in
{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
. Since for any fixed
k
the operators
x_{k}y_{l}
are disjoint from the right, part (i) implies that

\mu^{2}\Big(\bigoplus_{l}x_{k}y_{l}\Big)\prec\mu^{2}\Big(\sum_{l}x_{k}y_{l}\Big),\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Therefore, by Lemma
13
we have that

\mu^{2}\Big(\bigoplus_{k,l}x_{k}y_{l}\Big)=\mu^{2}\Big(\bigoplus_{k}\bigoplus_{l}x_{k}y_{l}\Big)\prec\mu^{2}\Big(\bigoplus_{k}(\sum_{l}x_{k}y_{l})\Big).

Using part (i) again for operator
\sum_{l}x_{k}y_{l}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which are disjoint from the left, we obtain that

\mu^{2}\Big(\bigoplus_{k,l}x_{k}y_{l}\Big)\prec\mu^{2}\Big(\sum_{k}(\sum_{l}x_{k}y_{l})\Big)=\mu^{2}\Big(\sum_{k,l}x_{k}y_{l}\Big).

∎

3.
Abstract Cwikel estimates

In this section we prove abstract Cwikel estimates for interpolation spaces in the Banach couple
(L_{2},L_{\infty})
. In particular, we obtain the estimate for the ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
for any
p>2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
be semifinite von Neumann algebras represented on the same (separable) Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and let
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
be faithful normal semifinite traces on
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2},
respectively. We denote by
\pi_{1}
(respectively,
\pi_{2}
) the representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
(respectively,
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
) on
{\mathcal{H}}
. By the same argument as in
[
13
, Lemma 2.4]
(and the paragraph after the lemma) we have that the representation
\pi_{i}
,
i=1,2
can be extended uniquely to a representation of
S({\mathcal{A}}_{i},\tau_{i})
on
{\mathcal{H}}
. In what follows we use the same notation
\pi_{i}
for these extensions.

Throughout this section we assume that Cwikel type estimates hold for the Hilbert-Schmidt ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{H}})
, that is we have the following

Hypothesis 3.1
.

Assume that for every
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1})
and
y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
we have
\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{H}})
and

\|\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{H}})}\leq\const\|x\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{1})}\|y\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{2})}.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\const=1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
0\leq x\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1})
and
0\leq y\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
. Then for every
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty)\geq\sum_{k+l\geq n}E_{x}[2^{k},2^{k+1})\otimes E_{y}[2^{l},2^{l+1}),\quad k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and set

x_{k}:=xE_{x}[2^{k},2^{k+1}),\quad y_{k}:=yE_{y}[2^{k},2^{k+1}).

Taking into account that
(x_{k}\otimes y_{l})(x_{k^{\prime}}\otimes y_{l^{\prime}})=0
unless
k=k^{\prime}
,
l=l^{\prime}
, we have

E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty)=\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{x_{k}\otimes y_{l}}[2^{n},\infty)\geq\sum_{k+l\geq n}E_{x_{k}\otimes y_{l}}[2^{n},\infty).

By the definition of
x_{k}
and
y_{l}
, for
k+l\geq n
we have

x_{k}\otimes y_{l}\geq 2^{k}E_{x}[2^{k},2^{k+1})\otimes 2^{l}E_{y}[2^{l},2^{l+1})\geq 2^{n}E_{x}[2^{k},2^{k+1})\otimes E_{y}[2^{l},2^{l+1}).

Therefore,

E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty)\geq\sum_{k+l\geq n}E_{x}[2^{k},2^{k+1})\otimes E_{y}[2^{l},2^{l+1})

as required.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\cap S_{0}({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1})
,
y\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}\cap S_{0}({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
are positive. If
x\otimes y\in({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})
, then

\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))\prec\prec 130\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x\otimes y).

In particular,

(14)

\|\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\|_{\infty}\leq 130\|x\otimes y\|_{({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})}.

Proof.

Fix
t>0
and choose
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

(15)

\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(t,x_{1}\otimes x_{2})<2^{n}\leq 2\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(t,x_{1}\otimes x_{2}).

By the definition of singular value function (see (
2
)) the first inequality implies that

(16)

\tau_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty))\leq t.

In order to estimate
\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}(s,\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))ds
we decompose
x
and
y
in dyadic parts in the following way

(17)

x_{k}:=xE_{x}[2^{k},2^{k+1}),\quad y_{k}:=yE_{y}[2^{k},2^{k+1}),\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

By the assumptions there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
x_{k},y_{k}=0
for
k\geq N
.

We have (with respect to the weak-operator topology)

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})=\sum_{k+l<n}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})+\sum_{k+l\geq n}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})

(18)

\displaystyle=:A_{n}+B_{n},\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

By the assumptions, the operator
B_{n}
has finitely many summands. We claim that the series
A_{n}
converges in the uniform norm. We also estimate the uniform norm of
A_{n}
and Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
B_{n}
.

Note, that since
x,y
are
\tau_{i}
compact, respectively, it follows that
x_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1})
and
y_{l}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
for all
k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, Hypothesis
3.1
implies that
\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{H}})
for every
k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

For the operator
A_{n}
we write

A_{n}=\sum_{k+l<n}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})=\sum_{m<n}\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l}).

We have

\Big|\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\Big|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+l_{1}=m\\
k_{2}+l_{2}=m\end{subarray}}\pi_{2}(y_{l_{2}})\pi_{1}(x_{k_{2}}x_{k_{1}})\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}}).

Since
x_{k_{2}}x_{k_{1}}=0
when
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
, we have

\Big|\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\Big|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+l_{1}=m\\
k+l_{2}=m\end{subarray}}\pi_{2}(y_{l_{2}})\pi_{1}(x_{k}^{2})\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}})=\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{2}(y_{l})\pi_{1}(x_{k}^{2})\pi_{2}(y_{l}).

By definition of
\{x_{k}\},\{y_{l}\}
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{k+l=m}\|\pi_{2}(y_{l})\pi_{1}(x_{k}^{2})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{k+l=m}\|x_{k}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|y_{l}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\sup_{k+l=m}2^{2(k+1)}\cdot 2^{2(l+1)}

\displaystyle=2^{2m+4}.

Hence, the fact that the operators
\pi_{2}(y_{l})\pi_{1}(x_{k}^{2})\pi_{2}(y_{l}),k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, are pairwise disjoint implies the operator
\Big|\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\Big|^{2}
is bounded and

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\Big\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big|\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\Big|^{2}\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{k+l=m}\|\pi_{2}(y_{l})\pi_{1}(x_{k}^{2})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{2m+4}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{m<n}\Big\|\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\Big\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{m<n}2^{m+2}=2^{n+2}.

Thus, the series
A_{n}=\sum_{m<n}\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})
converges in the uniform norm and

(19)

\displaystyle\|A_{n}\|_{\infty}=\Big\|\sum_{m<n}\sum_{k+l=m}\pi_{1}(x_{k})\pi_{2}(y_{l})\Big\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{n+2}.

Next, since the sum in
B_{n}
has finitely many non-zero summands, we have

\|B_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\operatorname{tr}(B_{n}^{*}B_{n})=\sum_{k_{1}+l_{1}\geq n}\sum_{k_{2}+l_{2}\geq n}\operatorname{tr}(\pi_{2}(y_{l_{2}})\pi_{1}(x_{k_{2}}x_{k_{1}})\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}})).

The fact that

\pi_{2}(y_{l_{2}})\pi_{1}(x_{k_{2}}),\,\pi_{1}(x_{k_{1}})\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{H}}),\quad k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}

together with equality (
8
) implies that

\operatorname{tr}\big([\pi_{2}(y_{l_{2}})\pi_{1}(x_{k_{2}})][\pi_{1}(x_{k_{1}})\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}})]\big)=\operatorname{tr}\big([\pi_{1}(x_{k_{1}})\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}})][\pi_{2}(y_{l_{2}})\pi_{1}(x_{k_{2}})]\big)=0,

whenever
(k_{1},l_{1})\neq(k_{2},l_{2}).

Since
x_{k},y_{l}
are positive, Hypothesis
3.1
implies that for
(k_{1},l_{1})=(k_{2},l_{2}),
we have

\operatorname{tr}(\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}})\pi_{1}(x_{k_{1}}^{2})\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}}))=\|\pi_{1}(x_{k_{1}})\pi_{2}(y_{l_{1}})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|x_{k_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}\|y_{l_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\|B_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k+l\geq n}\|x_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\|y_{l}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k+l\geq n}\|x_{k}\otimes y_{l}\|_{2}^{2}.

Setting

e_{n}=\sum_{k+l\geq n}E_{x}[2^{k},2^{k+1})\otimes E_{y}[2^{l},2^{l+1})\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2},

we have

\sum_{k+l\geq n}\|x_{k}\otimes y_{l}\|_{2}^{2}=\|(x\otimes y)\cdot e_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Lemma
3.2
combined with (
16
) implies that
(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})(e_{n})\leq t
, and therefore,

(x\otimes y)\ e_{n}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})

and

\|(x\otimes y)\ e_{n}\|_{2}\leq\|(x\otimes y)E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty)\|_{2}<\infty.

Thus,

(20)

\|B_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|(x\otimes y)\cdot E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty)\|_{2}^{2}.

Having obtained the estimates on the norms of
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
we can now estimate the required singular value function.
By (
3
) and (
10
) we have

\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))\prec\prec\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(A_{n})+\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(B_{n}).

Hence, by Lemma
2.5
we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(s,\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(s,A_{n})+\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(s,B_{n})\Big)^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}(s,A_{n})+\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}(s,B_{n})\Big)ds\leq 2t\|A_{n}\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}(s,B_{n})ds

\displaystyle=2t\|A_{n}\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|B_{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Therefore, by (
19
) and (
20
) we have

\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}(s,\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))ds\leq 2^{2n+5}t+2\|(x\otimes y)E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty)\|_{2}^{2}.

By (
6
) and (
16
) we have that

\displaystyle\|(x\otimes y)E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{prop_mu_projection}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(E_{x\otimes y}[2^{n},\infty))}\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{2}(s,x\otimes y)ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{abs_Cwikel_trace_E_n}}}{{\leq}}\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{2}(s,x\otimes y)ds.

Hence, combining the above inequalities with the second inequality in
(
15
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(s,\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))ds

\displaystyle\leq 2^{7}t\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{2}(t,x\otimes y)+2\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{2}(s,x\otimes y)ds

\displaystyle\leq 130\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{2}(s,x\otimes y)ds,

that is

\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))\prec\prec 130\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x\otimes y),

as required.

To prove estimate (
14
), we note that since
\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))
is a step function, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(s,\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))ds\leq 130\int_{0}^{1}\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(s,x\otimes y)ds

\displaystyle\leq 130\|x\otimes y\|^{2}_{({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})}.

∎

In the following theorem we extend Lemma
3.3
to general
x,y
, such that
x\otimes y\in({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
x\otimes y\in({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
. Then
\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
and

\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}^{2}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))\prec\prec 532\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{2}(x\otimes y).

Proof.

By equality (
3
) we have that

\displaystyle\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}

\displaystyle(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))=\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{2}(y^{*})\pi_{1}(x^{*}x)\pi_{2}(y))

\displaystyle=\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{2}(y^{*})\pi_{1}(|x|)\pi_{1}(|x|)\pi_{2}(y))=\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(|x|)\pi_{2}(y)\pi_{2}(y^{*})\pi_{1}(|x|))

\displaystyle=\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(|x|)\pi_{2}(|y^{*}|)).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(|x|^{2}\otimes|y|^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{mu_tensor}}}{{=}}\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(\mu(|x|^{2})\otimes\mu(|y|^{2}))

\displaystyle=\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(\mu^{2}(x)\otimes\mu^{2}(y^{*}))

\displaystyle=\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(|x|^{2}\otimes|y^{*}|^{2})=\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(|x|\otimes|y^{*}|).

Thus, without loss of generality we can assume that
x,y\geq 0.

At the first step we suppose that
x\in S_{0}({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1})
,
y\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}\cap S_{0}({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
are such that
x\otimes y\in({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
.
Let us denote
p_{n}=E_{x}[0,n]
and
C_{n}=\pi_{1}(p_{n}x)\pi_{2}(y).
By Lemma
3.3
we have that

(21)

\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(C_{n})\prec\prec 130\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(p_{n}x\otimes y)\leq\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x\otimes y).

Furthermore, by (
14
) we have

\displaystyle\|C_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\const\|(p_{n}x)\otimes y\|_{({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})}\leq\const\|x\otimes y\|_{({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})}:=c.

For arbitrary
\xi,\eta\in H
and
n\geq m\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\langle(C_{n}-C_{m})\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\pi_{1}(1-p_{m})C_{n}\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle C_{n}\xi,\pi_{1}(1-p_{m})\eta\rangle.

Since
1-p_{m}\downarrow 0
it follows that (see e.g.
[
7
, III.2.2.1]
)
\pi_{1}(1-p_{m})\downarrow 0
. Therefore

|\langle(C_{n}-C_{m})\xi,\eta\rangle|\leq\|C_{n}\xi\|\|\pi_{1}(1-p_{m})\eta\|\leq c\|\xi\|\|\pi_{1}(1-p_{m})\eta\|\to 0\quad n,m\to\infty.

Thus, the bilinear form
(\xi,\eta)\mapsto\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle C_{n}\xi,\eta\rangle
is bounded on
{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}
. Therefore, there exists
C
such that
C_{n}\to C
in the weak operator topology. It is clear that
C_{n}=\pi_{1}(p_{n})C
. Therefore
C=\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)
. Thus
\pi_{1}(p_{n}x)\pi_{2}(y)\to\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)
in the weak operator topology, which together with (
21
) implies that

\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))\prec\prec 130\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x\otimes y).

Repeating now the same argument, we can remove the assumption that
y\in{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
. Hence, we assume now
that
x,y\geq 0
are arbitrary with
x\otimes y\in({\mathcal{L}}_{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty})({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
and let

\displaystyle a=\mu(\infty,x),\quad b=\mu(\infty,y),

\displaystyle u=(x-a)_{+},\quad v=(y-b)_{+}.

We have

\displaystyle\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(u+a)\pi_{2}(v+b))

(22)

\displaystyle\prec\prec\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(u)\pi_{2}(v))+a\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{2}(v))+b\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(u))+ab.

Let
b\neq 0
and assume that
x
is not a bounded operator. Let
q=E_{y}(\frac{1}{2}b,\infty)
. Since
x\otimes y
is
\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
-measurable, we have that
x\otimes q
is also
\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
-measurable. Since
b=\mu(\infty,y)\neq 0
we have that
\tau_{2}(q)=\infty
. Hence,

(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})(E_{x\otimes q}(n,\infty))=(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})(E_{x}(n,\infty)\otimes q)=\infty,

which contradicts to
\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
-measurability of

x\otimes q
, and therefore,
E_{x}(n,\infty)=0
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Thus, if
b\neq 0
, then
x
is a bounded operator. In this case, since
\tau_{2}(1)=\infty
, it follows that

\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(u))\leq\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}=\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(u\otimes 1).

Similarly, if
a\neq 0
, then
y
is bounded and
\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{2}(v))\leq\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(1\otimes v).

We can have three different cases:
a=0,b\neq 0
;
a\neq 0
,
b=0
and both
a,b\neq 0
.
We consider only the case, when
a,b\neq 0
, as the other two cases can be proved similarly.
By (
3
) we have

\displaystyle\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))

\displaystyle\prec\prec 4(\mu^{2}(\pi_{1}(u)\pi_{2}(v))+a^{2}\mu^{2}(\pi_{2}(v))+b^{2}\mu^{2}(\pi_{1}(u))+a^{2}b^{2})

\displaystyle\prec\prec 4(130\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(u\otimes v)+3\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x\otimes y))

\displaystyle\leq 532\mu^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x\otimes y).

∎

Combining this result with Theorem
2.4
(with
f=\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})}(\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y))
and
g=\mu_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}(x\otimes y)
) we arrive at the following

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(E(0,\infty),\|\cdot\|_{E})
be an interpolation space for
(L_{2},L_{\infty})
and let
x\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1}),y\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
be such that
x\otimes y\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})
. Then
\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{H}})
and

\|\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\|_{{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{H}})}\leq C_{E}\|x\otimes y\|_{E(\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{2})}.

It is well-known that the spaces
L_{p}(0,\infty)
and
L_{p,\infty}(0,\infty)
for
p>2
are interpolation spaces in the pair
(L_{2}(0,\infty),L_{\infty}(0,\infty))
. Therefore, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
p>2
and let
x\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{1},\tau_{1})
,
y\in S({\mathcal{A}}_{2},\tau_{2})
.

(i)

If
x\otimes y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})
, then,
\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{H}})
and

\|\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{H}})}\leq 532\|x\otimes y\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}(\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{2})}=532\|x\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}}_{1})}\|y\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}}_{2})}.

(ii)

If
x\otimes y\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{2})
, then
\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
and

\|\pi_{1}(x)\pi_{2}(y)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}({\mathcal{H}})}\leq C_{p}\|x\otimes y\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{2})}.

Consider the particular case, when
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and the representation
\pi_{i}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, on the Hilbert space

is given by

\pi_{1}(f)=M_{f},\quad\pi_{2}(g)=g(-i\nabla),

where
M_{f}
is multiplication operator on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by

and
\nabla=(\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{d})
.
It is well known that Hypothesis
3.1
is satisfied. Therefore, the result of
[
12
]
is a particular case of our abstract Cwikel estimate.

The Cwikel estimates for the classical setting

The main result of this section is Theorem
4.5
. Its immediate consequence, Corollary
4.6
, complements the classical result by Cwikel
[
12
]
for the case
p<2
. The similar result is stated in
[
5
, Sections 5.7, 5.8]
which relies on
[
6
, Theorem 11.1]
. However, the authors of the present paper could not confirm the validity of the proof of the latter result from

We start with an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
h\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be supported on
[0,1]^{d}
and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be supported on
[-3,3]^{d}
such that
\phi|_{[0,1]^{d}}=1.

For the function
\psi=\phi\cdot{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}h
and every
0<p\leq 2
we have

\|\{\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})\}_{{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\|_{p}^{p}\leq C_{p}\|h\|_{1},

where
\{\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})\}_{{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
denotes the sequence on Fourier coefficients of
\psi
on the cube
[-\pi,\pi]^{d}
.

Proof.

Since
h\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is compactly supported, it follows (see e.g.
[
41
]
) that
\mathcal{F}^{-1}h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
denotes the Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
By the assumptions, the function
\psi=\phi\cdot\mathcal{F}^{-1}h\in C^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi]^{d})
and vanishes near the boundary together with all its derivatives.

Let us fix an even integer
m>\frac{d}{p}.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf k}\neq 0}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|^{p}=\sum_{{{\mathbf{k}}}\neq 0}|{{\mathbf{k}}}|^{-mp}(|{{\mathbf{k}}}|^{m}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|)^{p}\leq(\sum_{{{\mathbf{k}}}\neq 0}|{{\mathbf{k}}}|^{-mp})\cdot(\sup_{{{\mathbf{k}}}\neq 0}|{{\mathbf{k}}}|^{m}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|)^{p}.

Since
\psi\in C^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi]^{d})
and
m
is even, it follows that
|{{\mathbf{k}}}|^{m}\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})
is the
{\bf k}-
th Fourier coefficient of
(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}(\psi).
Hence,

\sup_{{{\mathbf{k}}}\neq 0}|{{\mathbf{k}}}|^{m}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|\leq\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{T}})^{\frac{m}{2}}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq d^{\frac{m}{2}}\|\psi\|_{C^{m}([-\pi,\pi]^{d})}.

By definition of
\psi
and Leibniz rule, we have

\sup_{{\bf k}\neq 0}|{\bf k}|^{m}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|\leq d^{\frac{m}{2}}2^{m}\|\phi\|_{C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}h\|_{C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

To estimate the norm
\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}h\|_{C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
,
for every
{\bf l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d},
we set
h_{\bf l}(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}^{l_{j}},

t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We have,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}h\|_{C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\max_{\|{\bf l}\|_{1}\leq m}\|h_{\bf l}(\nabla)\mathcal{F}^{-1}h\|_{\infty}=\max_{\|{\bf l}\|_{1}\leq m}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(h_{\bf l}h)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\|{\bf l}\|_{1}\leq m}\|h_{\bf l}h\|_{1}\leq\|h\|_{1},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
h
is supported on
[0,1]
.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf k}\neq 0}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|^{p}\leq\const\Big(\sum_{{{\mathbf{k}}}\neq 0}|{{\mathbf{k}}}|^{-mp}\Big)\cdot\|h\|_{1}^{p}=\const\|h\|_{1}^{p},

which concludes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is an auxiliary version of Cwikel estimates for compactly supported functions.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be supported on
[0,1]^{d}
. For every
0<p\leq 2,
we have that
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{p}\leq\const\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2},

where the constant depends on
d
and
p
only.

Proof.

It is well-known that (see e.g.
[
36
, Theorem IX.29]
)

(23)

(M_{f}g^{2}(-i\nabla)M_{f})\xi)(s)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f(s)f(t)({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g^{2})(s-t)\xi(t)dt,\quad\xi\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Let
\phi
be a Schwartz function which is
1
on
[-1,1]^{d}
and which is
0
outside of
[-3,3]^{d}.
We set
\psi:=\phi\cdot{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}g^{2}
. Since
g\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, it follows that
\psi\in C^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi]^{d})
and vanishes near the boundary together with all its derivatives.
Hence (see e.g.
[
41
, Chapter VII]
) we have that for almost every
{\mathbf{u}}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{d}

(24)

\psi({\mathbf{u}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})e^{i\langle{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{u}}\rangle},\quad\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|<\infty.

Since
\phi
equals
1
for
u\in[-1,1]^{d}
, it follows that

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}g^{2})(u)=\sum_{{\bf k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})e^{i\langle{\bf k},u\rangle},\quad u\in[-1,1]^{d}.

We set
f_{\bf k}(t)=f(t)e^{i\langle{\bf k},t\rangle},
and define the rank-one operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by setting

A_{\bf k}\xi:=\langle\xi,f_{\bf k}\rangle f_{\bf k},\quad\xi\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

By (
23
) we obtain that

\displaystyle(M_{f}g^{2}(-i\nabla)M_{f}\xi)(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{f(s)}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f(t)\xi(t)\sum_{{\bf k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})e^{i\langle{\bf k},s-t\rangle}dt.

By (
24
) and the dominated convergence theorem we infer that

\displaystyle(M_{f}g^{2}(-i\nabla)M_{f}\xi)(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})f(s)e^{i\langle{\bf k},s\rangle}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f(t)\xi(t)e^{-i\langle{\bf k},t\rangle}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\sum_{{\bf k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})(A_{\bf k}\xi)(s).

The quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{p/2}
is
\frac{p}{2}
-subadditive (see e.g.
[
26
]
), and therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|M_{f}g^{2}(-i\nabla)M_{f}\|_{p/2}^{p/2}\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\sum_{{\bf k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|^{p/2}\|A_{\bf k}\|_{p/2}^{p/2}

(25)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\|f\|_{2}^{p}\sum_{{\bf k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{\psi}({\mathbf{k}})|^{p/2}.

By Lemma
4.1
(with
h=g^{2}
) we obtain that

\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{p}^{p}\leq\const\|f\|_{2}^{p}\|g^{2}\|_{1}^{p/2}=\const\|f\|_{2}^{p}\|g\|_{2}^{p},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
E
be a symmetric quasi-Banach sequence space with modulus of concavity
M
. Then for every
p>0
such that
2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}>M
, the operator
T:E({\mathbb{Z}}_{+})\to E({\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{2})
defined by

T:x\mapsto x\otimes\{(k+1)^{-1/p}\},

is bounded.

Proof.

By the Aoki-Rolewicz theorem, for
q>0
satisfying
2^{\frac{1}{q}-1}=K
(passing to an equivalent quasi-norm, if necessary) we have

\|x+y\|_{E}^{q}\leq\|x\|_{E}^{q}+\|y\|_{E}^{q}.

Hence, for
p<q
we have

\|x\otimes\{(k+1)^{-1/p}\}\|_{E}^{q}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-q/p}\|x\|_{E}^{q}=\const\|x\|_{E}^{q}.

∎

The following theorem is the main result of this section. It is stated is somewhat abstract manner. For the case of Schatten and weak Schatten ideals the statement is given explicitly in Corollary
4.6
below.
To formulate the result we introduce firstly the definition of space
E(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. For the special case, when
E=\ell_{p}
or
E=\ell_{p,\infty}
this definition coincides with that given in
[
6
]
(see also
[
39
, Chapter 4]
).

Definition 4.4
.

Let
K
be the unit cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
E\subset\ell_{2}
be a symmetric quasi-Banach sequence space. Let
E(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the space of all (measurable) functions such that the sequence
\{\|f\chi_{K+{\mathbf{m}}}\|_{2}\}_{{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
is in
E
. For
f\in E(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
set

\|f\|_{E(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\Big\|\{\|f\chi_{K+{\mathbf{m}}}\|_{2}\}_{{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\Big\|_{E}.

The spaces
E(L_{q})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
0<q\leq\infty
are defined similarly.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
E\subset\ell_{2}
be a symmetric quasi-Banach sequence space such that
\mu^{2}(x)\prec\mu^{2}(y),\,x\in E
implies that
y\in E
and
\|y\|_{E}\leq c_{E}\|x\|_{E}
. If
f\otimes g\in E(L_{2})({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
, then
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{E}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{{\mathcal{E}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq c_{E,p}\|f\otimes g\|_{E(L_{2})(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}.

Proof.

Let
K
be the unit cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We denote

f_{\mathbf{m}}=f\chi_{{\mathbf{m}}+K},\quad g_{\mathbf{m}}=g\chi_{{\mathbf{m}}+K},\quad{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Since
E\subset\ell_{2}
we have that

\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\|M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\|f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}\otimes g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}=\|f\otimes g\|_{2}^{2}<\infty.

Hence, the assumptions of Lemma
2.9
are satisfied, and therefore

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\Big(\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}

\displaystyle M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\Big)

(26)

\displaystyle\prec\mu^{2}\Big(\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\Big)=\mu^{2}(M_{f}g(-i\nabla)).

We claim that

\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{E}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

The functions
f(\cdot-{\mathbf{m}}_{1})\chi_{K}
and
g(\cdot-{\mathbf{m}}_{2})\chi_{K}
are supported on
[0,1]^{d}
and by the assumptions
f(\cdot-{\mathbf{m}}_{1})\chi_{K},g(\cdot-{\mathbf{m}}_{2})\chi_{K}\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Therefore Lemma
4.2
implies that for
p>0
with

we have

\displaystyle\|M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\|f(\cdot-{\mathbf{m}}_{1})\chi_{K}\|_{2}\|g(\cdot-{\mathbf{m}}_{2})\chi_{K}\|_{2}=c_{p}\|f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}\otimes g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\|_{2}.

Recalling that

\mu(M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla))\leq\|M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\|_{p,\infty}\cdot z_{p}\leq c_{p}\|f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}\otimes g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\|_{2}\cdot z_{p},

where

Hence, (everywhere below the summation is taken over

)

\displaystyle\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}

\displaystyle M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\Big\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}=\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\mu(M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla))\Big\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}c_{p}\|f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}\otimes g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\|_{2}\cdot z_{p}\Big\|_{E}=c_{p}\Big\|\{\|f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}\|_{2}\|g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\|_{2}\}_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\otimes z_{p}\Big\|_{E}

\displaystyle=c_{p}\Big\|T(\{\|f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}\|_{2}\|g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\|_{2}\}_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}})\Big\|_{E}.

By Lemma
4.3
we infer that

\displaystyle\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\Big\|_{{\mathcal{E}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{E,p}\|\{\|f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}\otimes g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\|_{2}\}_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}\|_{E}

(27)

\displaystyle=c_{E,p}\|f\otimes g\|_{E(L_{2})(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}<\infty.

Thus,
\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{E}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
, and therefore (
4

(with

and

and

\displaystyle\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{{\mathcal{E}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq c_{E}\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}_{1}}}g_{{\mathbf{m}}_{2}}(-i\nabla)\Big\|_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{clas_Cwikel_direct_sum_norm}}}{{\leq}}c_{E,p}\|f\otimes g\|_{E(L_{2})(\mathbb{R}^{2d})},

∎

5.
Cwikel estimates for the classical setting for weak Schatten ideal

In this section we show that Cwikel estimate does not hold for
\mathcal{L}_{2,\infty}.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
M_{f}(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\notin\mathcal{L}_{4}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}})).
In particular,
M_{f}(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\notin\mathcal{L}_{2,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}})).

Proof.

Let
f(t):=t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\log(t)|^{-1}\chi_{(0,\frac{1}{2})}(t),

t\in\mathbb{R}.
It is clear that
f\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. We claim that

M_{f}(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{f}\notin\mathcal{L}_{2}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}})).

Since the integral kernel of the operator
(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
can be written in terms of the Macdonald function
(t,s)\to K_{0}(|t-s|),\quad t,s\in\mathbb{R}
(see e.g.
[
1
, (9.25)]
), it follows that the integral kernel of the operator
M_{f}(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{f}
is given by the formula

(t,s)\to f(t)f(s)K_{0}(|t-s|),\quad t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

If
t,s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),
then
|t-s|<1,
and therefore
K_{0}(|t-s|)\approx\log(|t-s|)
(see
[
1
, 9.6.21]
).

We have

\displaystyle\int_{(0,\frac{1}{2})^{2}}\frac{\log^{2}(|t-s|)}{ts\cdot\log^{2}(t)\cdot\log^{2}(s)}dtds

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\geq 2s\\
t,s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})\end{subarray}}\frac{\log^{2}(|t-s|)}{ts\cdot\log^{2}(t)\cdot\log^{2}(s)}dtds

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|t|\geq 2|s|\\
t,s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{ts\cdot\log^{2}(t)}dtds.

Since the latter integral diverges, we infer that the integral kernel of the operator
M_{f}(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{f}
does not belong to
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}).
Therefore, the operator
M_{f}(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{f}
is not in
{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
, which implies that
M_{f}(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\notin{\mathcal{L}}_{4}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
.
∎

In the rest of this section we establish a positive result for Cwikel estimates in
{\mathcal{L}}_{2,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.
The crucially important property here is the logarithmic convexity of
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}}).
It was established by Stein and Weiss
[
42
]
and by Kalton
[
25
]
in the commutative situation. We prove this property for the noncommutative counterpart
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
, where
{\mathcal{M}}
is a semifinite von Neuman algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
. We present the proof of Lemma
5.2
below for convenience of the reader. Its proof is taken from
[
25
]
. Lemma
5.3
below is inspired by
[
25
, Theorem 3.4]
, but the latter is essentially commutative and so, our proof is quite different.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
a_{k}\in[0,1],

1\leq k\leq n,
be such that
\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}=1.
It follows that

(28)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\log(\frac{e}{a_{k}})\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}(1+\log(k)).

Proof.

Consider the function

f:(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\to\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\log(\frac{e}{a_{k}})-2\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\log(k)

on the simplex
\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}):a_{k}\in[0,1],\,k=1,\dots n,\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}=1\}.
. Since
f
is continuous, it attains its supremum on the simplex.

Suppose first, that supremum belongs to the interior of the simplex. We can find it by Lagrange method. Set
F=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}.
We have
\nabla(f-\lambda F)=0
at the maximum point, where
\lambda
is the Lagrange multiplier. Computing the partial derivatives, we obtain that

\log(\frac{1}{a_{k}})-2\log(k)=\lambda,

and therefore, the stationary point of
f
is given by

\tilde{a}_{k}=\frac{1}{e^{\lambda}k^{2}},\quad 1\leq k\leq n.

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}=1,
it follows that
e^{\lambda}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq e
and, therefore,
\lambda\leq 1.

Hence, computing the value of the function
f
at the stationary point
(\tilde{a}_{1},\dots,\tilde{a}_{n})
we have

\displaystyle f(\tilde{a}_{1},\dots,\tilde{a}_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{k}\log(\frac{e}{\tilde{a}_{k}})-2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{k}\log(k)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{e^{\lambda}k^{2}}\log(e^{\lambda}k^{2})-2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{e^{\lambda}k^{2}}\log(k)

\displaystyle=1+\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{e^{\lambda}k^{2}}=(1+\lambda)\leq 2.

This shows that inequality (
28
) holds in this case.

Suppose now that supremum is attained on the boundary of the simplex, that is,
a_{m}=0,
for some
1\leq m\leq n.
Set
b_{k}=a_{k},

1\leq k<m,
and
b_{k}=a_{k+1},

m\leq k<n.
By induction on
n,
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\log(\frac{e}{a_{k}})=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{k}\log(\frac{e}{b_{k}})\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{k}(1+\log(k))\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}(1+\log(k)),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If
x_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}}),

1\leq k\leq n,
then

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\Big\|_{1,\infty}\leq 4\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}(1+\log(k)).

Proof.

Let
x_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}}),

1\leq k\leq n,
and let
x=\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}.
Suppose that

(29)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}=1.

Set
p_{k}=E_{|x_{k}|}(\frac{1}{t},\infty)
and
p=\bigvee_{1\leq k\leq n}p_{k}.
Since
x_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
, we have
\tau(p_{k})\leq t\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}
and, therefore,

\tau(p)=\tau\Big(\bigvee_{1\leq k\leq n}p_{k}\Big)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tau(p_{k})\leq t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}=t.

Since
\mu(t,xp)=0,
equality (
4
), combined with the fact that singular value function is decreasing, implies that

\mu(2t,x)\leq\mu(t,xp)+\mu(t,x(1-p))\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,x(1-p))ds.

It follows from (
10
) that

\displaystyle t\mu(2t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,x(1-p))ds\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{submaj_sum}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,x_{k}(1-p))ds

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,x_{k}(1-p_{k}))ds.

Due to the choice of
p_{k},
for every fixed
s>0
we have that

\mu(s,x_{k}(1-p_{k}))\leq\min\{\frac{1}{t},\mu(s,x_{k})\}\leq\min\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}}{s}\},\quad s>0.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,x_{k}(1-p_{k}))ds=\int_{0}^{t\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}}\frac{1}{t}ds+\int_{t\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}}^{t}\frac{\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}}{s}ds

\displaystyle=\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}-\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}\log(\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty})=\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}\log\Big(\frac{e}{\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}}\Big).

Substituting to (
5
), we infer

\displaystyle t\mu(2t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}\log\Big(\frac{e}{\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}}\Big).

Thus,

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\Big\|_{1,\infty}=\sup_{t>0}t\mu(t,x)\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}\log\Big(\frac{e}{\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}}\Big).

Using Lemma
5.2
, we infer that

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\Big\|_{1,\infty}\leq 4\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}(1+\log(k)).

To conclude the proof one can remove assumption (
29
) by homogeneity.
∎

The following proposition gives logarithmic convexity of
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
, which is crucial for Theorem
5.6
below.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
x_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}}),

k\geq 1,
then

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}\Big\|_{1,\infty}\leq 4\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}(1+\log(k)).

Here, the series in the left hand side converges in
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
provided that the series in the right hand side converges.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}x_{k}\Big\|_{1,\infty}=\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}x_{k+n-1}\Big\|_{1,\infty}\leq 4\sum_{k=1}^{m-n}\|x_{k+n-1}\|_{1,\infty}(1+\log(k))\leq

\leq 4\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}(1+\log(k)).

By the assumption, the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|x_{k}\|_{1,\infty}(1+\log(k))
converges, and therefore the sequence of partial sums of
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}}).
Since the space
({\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}}),\|\cdot\|_{1,\infty})
is complete, we conclude that the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}
converges in
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
and obtain the required estimate.
∎

We now introduce an auxiliary class of functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Definition 5.5
.

Let

\|f\|_{2,\log}=\Big(\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}(1+\log|{\mathbf{m}}|)\|f\chi_{K+{\mathbf{m}}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

The following theorem is the second main result of the present section. It proves a version of Cwikel estimates for the ideal

, the only case in the scale of weak ideals which is covered by neither

Theorem 5.6
.

If
g\in\ell_{2,\infty}(L_{4})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

f\in\ell_{2,\log}(L_{\infty})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{2,\infty}\leq\const\|f\|_{2,\log}\|g\|_{\ell_{2,\infty}(L_{4})}.

Proof.

We denote by
K
the unit cube, and set
f_{\mathbf{m}}=f\chi_{K+{\mathbf{m}}},{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
For every fixed
{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

f_{\mathbf{m}}\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

Hence,
M_{f_{\mathbf{m}}}g^{2}(-i\nabla)M_{f_{\mathbf{m}}}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|M_{f_{\mathbf{m}}}g^{2}(-i\nabla)M_{f_{\mathbf{m}}}\|_{1,\infty}\leq\|f_{\mathbf{m}}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|g\|^{2}_{\ell_{2,\infty}(L_{4})}.

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}}(1+\log|{\mathbf{m}}|)

\displaystyle\|g(-i\nabla)M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{2}}g(-i\nabla)\Big\|_{1,\infty}

\displaystyle=\const\|f\|^{2}_{2,\log}\|g\|^{2}_{l_{2,\infty}(L_{4})}<\infty.

\displaystyle\|M_{f}g(-i\nabla)\|_{2,\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|g(-i\nabla)M_{f^{2}}g(-i\nabla)\|_{1,\infty}=\Big\|\sum_{{\mathbf{m}}}g(-i\nabla)M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{2}}g(-i\nabla)\Big\|_{1,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{\mathbf{m}}(1+\log|{\mathbf{m}}|)\|g(-i\nabla)M_{f_{{\mathbf{m}}}^{2}}g(-i\nabla)\Big\|_{1,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\const\|f\|^{2}_{2,\log}\|g\|^{2}_{l_{2,\infty}(L_{4})},

which concludes the proof.

6.
Noncommutative Euclidean space

The noncommutative Euclidean space, also known as Moyal plane admits several equivalent definitions. For example,

(i)

Define a symmetric bilinear product
\star_{\theta}
on
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. The algebra of smooth functions is then defined to be the algebra
({\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\star_{\theta})
(see e.g.
[
19
]
).

(ii)

Define
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta}
as an isospectral deformation of the manifold
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with respect to a certain
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-action (see e.g.
[
37
]
).

(iii)

Define firstly
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
as twisted (reduced) group von Neumann algebra and then introduce ‘function spaces’ on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta}
as noncommutative spaces associated to that algebra.

In this paper, we stick with the third option. Our approach is unitarily equivalent, via the Fourier transform, to the standard Moyal product approach in (1), and therefore, the content of this section is well known to the experts. For convenience, we give a self contained
exposition with references to the equivalent results based on Moyal product, when possible. For simplicity of exposition we totally avoid terminology from twisted group algebras.

6.1.
Definition and elementary properties of noncommutative Euclidean space

We shall define noncommutative Euclidean space as the von Neumann algebra generated by a strongly continuous family of unitary operators
\{U({\mathbf{t}})\}_{{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
,
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, satisfying the commutation relation

(31)

U({\mathbf{t}}+{\mathbf{s}})=\exp(-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{s}}\rangle)U({\mathbf{t}})U({\mathbf{s}}),\quad{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
\theta
is a fixed antisymmetric real
d\times d
matrix. Namely, we set

(32)

(U({\mathbf{t}})\xi)({\mathbf{u}})=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{u}}\rangle}\xi({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{t}}),\quad\xi\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

It is clear that the family
\{U({\mathbf{s}})\}_{{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
of unitaries is strongly continuous. Moreover, for every
{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle(U({\mathbf{s}})U({\mathbf{t}})\xi)({\mathbf{u}})

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{u}}\rangle}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{s}})\rangle}\xi({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{t}}-{\mathbf{s}})

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{s}}\rangle}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}}+{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{u}}\rangle}\xi({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{t}}-{\mathbf{s}})

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{s}}\rangle}(U({\mathbf{s}}+{\mathbf{t}})\xi)({\mathbf{u}}).

In other words, the operators defined in (
32
) satisfy the commutation relation (
31
).

Definition 6.1
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
\theta
be a fixed antisymmetric real
d\times d
matrix.
The von Neumann algebra on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
generated by
\{U({\mathbf{t}})\}_{{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
, introduced in (
32
), is said to be noncommutative Euclidean space and denoted by
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
.

Remark 6.2
.

(i)

The classical case of Euclidean space is recovered by taking
\theta=0
. Definition
6.1
states that
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0})

is the von Neumann algebra algebra generated by
d
commuting unitary groups, and this is
*
isomorphic to
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

(ii)

Note that relation (
31
) makes no sense without the assumption that
\theta
is antisymmetric and real.
Indeed, we have

\exp(-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{s}}\rangle)=U({\mathbf{t}}+{\mathbf{s}})U({\mathbf{s}})^{*}U({\mathbf{t}})^{*}.

Since the right hand side of the above is unitary, we require that
\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{s}}\rangle
is always real, and so
\theta
must be real. Next, combining the fact that,

\displaystyle U({\mathbf{t}}+{\mathbf{s}})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{s}}\rangle)U({\mathbf{t}})U({\mathbf{s}})

\displaystyle U(-{\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{t}})

\displaystyle=\exp(-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{t}}\rangle)U(-{\mathbf{s}})U(-{\mathbf{t}}),

with the equality
U({\mathbf{t}})^{-1}=U(-{\mathbf{t}})
, it follows from multiplying the above two equations that

\langle{\mathbf{t}},\theta{\mathbf{s}}\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{t}}\rangle=0.

Therefore,
\theta
has to be antisymmetric.∎

Our first step in studying the noncommutative Euclidean space
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is to show a simple and well-known fact (see e.g.
[
37
, Proposition 5.2]
) that we can replace our original family
\{U({\mathbf{t}})\}_{{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
and the matrix
\theta
, so that the commutation relation (
31
) becomes simpler.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
k=\dim\ker\theta
and let
\tilde{\theta}
be the block-diagonal matrix of the form

(33)

\tilde{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&\\
1&0&\\
&&0&-1\\
&&1&0\\
&&&&\ddots\\
&&&&&0&-1\\
&&&&&1&0\\
&&&&&&&0_{d-2k}\end{pmatrix}.

Then, the algebras
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
and
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\tilde{\theta}})
are spatially isomorphic.

Proof.

Since the matrix
\theta
is antisymmetric and real, it follows from the spectral theory that there exists an orthogonal matrix
Q
such that

\theta=Q\theta_{0}Q^{-1}
, where

\theta_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\theta_{1}&\\
\theta_{1}&0&\\
&&0&-\theta_{2}\\
&&\theta_{2}&0\\
&&&&\ddots\\
&&&&&0&-\theta_{k}\\
&&&&&\theta_{k}&0\\
&&&&&&&0_{d-2k}\end{pmatrix},

with
\theta_{k}>0
. Define also the matrix

N=\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{1}^{-1/2}\\
&\theta_{1}^{-1/2}\\
&&\theta_{2}^{-1/2}\\
&&&\theta_{2}^{-1/2}\\
&&&&\ddots\\
&&&&&1_{d-2k}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\{U^{\theta}({\mathbf{t}})\}_{{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
be the family corresponding to
\theta
and let
\{U^{\tilde{\theta}}({\mathbf{t}})\}_{{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
be the family corresponding to
\tilde{\theta}
. Introducing the unitary operator
W
on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by setting

(34)

(W\xi)({\mathbf{t}})=\det(N)^{-1/2}\cdot\xi(N^{-1}Q^{-1}{\mathbf{t}}),\quad{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

one can obtain that

W^{*}U^{\theta}({\mathbf{t}})W=U^{\tilde{\theta}}(N^{-1}Q^{-1}{\mathbf{t}}),\quad{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

which implies the claim.

∎

As an immediate corollary of the preceding proposition we obtain the following classical result (see e.g.
[
37
, Proposition 5.2]
and also
[
19
, Proposition 2.13]
).

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

We have that

L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})\cong\underbrace{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{S})\bar{\otimes}\cdots\bar{\otimes}L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{S})}_{\ell\,{\text{t}imes}}\bar{\otimes}\underbrace{L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\bar{\otimes}\dots\bar{\otimes}L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}_{k\,\text{times}},

where
k=\dim\ker\theta
,
d=k+2\ell
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\theta}
and
\{U^{\tilde{\theta}}({\mathbf{t}})\}_{{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
be the same as in Proposition
6.3
.

For
i=1,\dots,k
,
j=1,\dots,\ell
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we set

u_{j}(t):=U^{\tilde{\theta}}(t{\mathbf{e}}_{2j}),\quad v_{j}(t)=U^{\tilde{\theta}}(t{\mathbf{e}}_{2j-1}),\quad w_{i}(t)=U^{\tilde{\theta}}(t{\mathbf{e}}_{2l+i}).

Then every pair
\{u_{j}(t)v_{j}(t)\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
,
j=1,\dots,\ell,
generates a copy of
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{S})
and
\{w_{i}(t)\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
generates
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
.

∎

Corollary
6.4
shows that we can always split
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})

into the tensor product
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{S})\bar{\otimes}\cdots\bar{\otimes}L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{S})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{k})
, where
k
is the dimension of the kernel of
\theta
. Thus truly noncommutative case appears only when
\theta
is non-degenerate, that is
\det\theta\neq 0
. Therefore, everywhere below in this section we assume in addition that
\theta
is non-degenerate and block-diagonal
d\times d
matrix, with every block being equal to the matrix
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

In particular, this implies that
d
is even.

Next, we show that in fact the algebra
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\theta}^{d})
is
*
-isomorphic to the algebra
{\mathcal{B}}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\frac{d}{2}}))
(see
[
21
, Theorem 2]
and
[
19
, Proposition 2.13]
).

Let
M_{x_{k}}
and
\partial_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,n
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be the operators on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
given by
(M_{x_{k}}\xi)({\mathbf{u}})=u_{k}\xi({\mathbf{u}})
and
(\partial_{k}\xi)({\mathbf{u}})=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}\xi({\mathbf{u}})
,
{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
It is well-known (see e.g.
[
36
]
) that
(M_{x_{1}},\dots,M_{x_{d}})
and
(\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{d})
satisfy Weyl canonical commutation relations:

\displaystyle e^{isM_{x_{k}}}e^{itM_{x_{l}}}

\displaystyle=e^{itM_{x_{l}}}e^{isM_{x_{k}}},

(35)

\displaystyle e^{is\partial_{k}}e^{it\partial_{l}}

\displaystyle=e^{it\partial_{l}}e^{is\partial_{k}},

\displaystyle e^{isM_{x_{k}}}e^{it\partial_{l}}

\displaystyle=e^{-its\delta_{kl}}e^{it\partial_{l}}e^{isM_{x_{k}}}.

Introduce the quadratic form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by setting

(36)

h({\mathbf{s}})=\sum_{j}s_{2j}s_{2j-1},\quad{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Denoting
{\mathbf{s}}^{\prime}=(s_{2},s_{4},\dots,s_{d}),
and
{\mathbf{s}}^{\prime\prime}=(s_{1},s_{3},\dots,s_{d-1})
for
{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, We have that

(37)

e^{i({\mathbf{s}}^{\prime}\cdot M_{x}+{\mathbf{s}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\partial)}=e^{\frac{ih({\mathbf{s}})}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{d/2}e^{is_{2j}M_{x_{2j-1}}}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d/2}e^{is_{2j-1}\partial_{2j-1}}.

Theorem 6.5
.

There exists a spatial
*
-isomorphisms
r
of the algebras
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
and
{\mathcal{B}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d/2}))\otimes 1_{L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d/2})}
such that

\displaystyle r:U({\mathbf{s}})\mapsto e^{i({\mathbf{s}}^{\prime}\cdot M_{x}+{\mathbf{s}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\partial)},\quad{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Proof.

Recall that we assume that the matrix
\theta
is block-diagonal (see Proposition
6.3
) of the form (
33
).

Introduce the unitary operator
V\in{\mathcal{B}}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by setting

(V\xi)({\mathbf{u}})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{i(-\frac{h({\mathbf{u}})}{2}-\langle{\mathbf{u}},\theta{\mathbf{x}}\rangle+h({\mathbf{x}}))}\xi({\mathbf{x}})d{\mathbf{x}}.

Standard computations show that

U({\mathbf{s}})=Ve^{i({\mathbf{s}}^{\prime}\cdot M_{{\mathbf{x}}}+{\mathbf{s}}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\partial)}V^{*},\quad{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

∎

Remark 6.6
.

Let
i_{0}:\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\frac{d}{2}}))\to\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be defined by the formula

i_{0}(T)=T\otimes 1_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\frac{d}{2}})}.

By Theorem
6.5
we have that any
x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
is in the image of
i_{0}
. Define the isomorphism
r_{0}:L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\theta}^{d})\to{\mathcal{B}}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\frac{d}{2}}))
by setting

r_{0}(x)=i_{0}^{-1}(r(x)),\quad x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\theta}^{d}).

Clearly,
r_{0}
is a
*
-isomorphism of the algebras
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\theta}^{d})
and
\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d/2}))
.

Having established the
*
-isomorphism
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})\cong{\mathcal{B}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d/2}))
we can now equip
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau_{\theta}
. Let
\operatorname{tr}
be the standard trace on
{\mathcal{B}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d/2}))
. We set

(38)

\tau_{\theta}(x)=(\operatorname{tr}\otimes 1)(r(x))=(\operatorname{tr}\circ r_{0})(x).

In particular, we can define symmetric function spaces on
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
, which are nothing but symmetric ideals of compact operators on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d/2})
(see
[
20
]
).

Definition 6.7
.

Let
E
be a symmetric sequence space. The symmetric ideal in
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
with respect to the trace
\tau_{\theta}
is denoted by
E({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d},\tau_{\theta})
. In particular, the Schatten ideals associated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
are denoted by
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d},\tau_{\theta})

Remark 6.8
.

Since the algebra
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\theta}^{d})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d/2}))
we have that
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})\subset L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
for
p<q
.

Lemma 6.9
.

An operator
x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
is in
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d},\tau_{\theta})
if and only if

x=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f({\mathbf{s}})U({\mathbf{s}})d{\mathbf{s}}

for some unique
f\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\|x\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}.
Here the integral is the Bochner integral.

We can also define the algebra of continuous functions in
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
.

Definition 6.10
.

The
C^{*}
-algebra

C({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})=\{x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta}):\,x\text{ is }\tau_{\theta}-\text{compact}\},

is said to be the algebra of continuous function (vanishing at infinity). This algebra is
*
-isomorphic to the algebra of compact operators on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d/2})
.

6.2.
Calculus on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta}

In this section we define algebras of smooth functions on noncommutative Euclidean space in
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq d
be multiplication operators

({\mathcal{D}}_{k}\xi)({\mathbf{t}})=t_{k}\xi({\mathbf{t}}),\quad\xi\in\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{D}}_{k})

defined on the domain
\operatorname{dom}({\mathcal{D}}_{k})=\{\xi\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\xi\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},|t|^{2}d{\mathbf{t}})\}.
Note that the space
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is the core of every operator
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,d
.

Fix
{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
\xi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. We have

\displaystyle([{\mathcal{D}}_{k},U({\mathbf{s}})]\xi)({\mathbf{t}})

\displaystyle=({\mathcal{D}}_{k}U({\mathbf{s}})\xi)(t)-(U({\mathbf{s}}){\mathcal{D}}_{k}\xi)(t)

\displaystyle=t_{k}e^{-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{t}}\rangle}\xi({\mathbf{t}}-{\mathbf{s}})-e^{-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{t}}\rangle}(t_{k}-s_{k})\xi({\mathbf{t}}-{\mathbf{s}})

\displaystyle=s_{k}(U({\mathbf{s}})\xi)({\mathbf{t}}).

Thus, for every
k=1,\dots,d
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the operator
[{\mathcal{D}}_{k},U({\mathbf{s}})]
extend to a bounded operator on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

(39)

[{\mathcal{D}}_{k},U({\mathbf{s}})]=s_{k}U({\mathbf{s}}).

In the next lemma we extend equation (
39
) to commutators of
U({\mathbf{s}})
with the unitary group of
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
.

Lemma 6.11
.

For any
{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
k=1,\dots,d
we have that

e^{it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}U({\mathbf{s}})e^{-it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}=e^{its_{k}}U({\mathbf{s}})\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta}),\quad t>0.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. We have

\displaystyle(e^{it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}U({\mathbf{s}})\xi)({\mathbf{u}})

\displaystyle=e^{-{i/2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{u}}\rangle}e^{itu_{k}}\xi({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{s}})

\displaystyle=e^{its_{k}}\cdot e^{-{i/2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{u}}\rangle}e^{it(u_{k}-s_{k})}\xi({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{s}})=e^{its_{k}}(U({\mathbf{s}})e^{it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}\xi)({\mathbf{u}}).

∎

As a corollary of equation (
39
) we obtain the following

Proposition 6.12
.

Let
k=1,\dots,d
and
x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
. If
[{\mathcal{D}}_{k},x]
extends to a bounded operator on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
[{\mathcal{D}}_{k},x]\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
.

Proof.

Since
[{\mathcal{D}}_{k},x]\in{\mathcal{B}}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
,
[
8
, Proposition 3.2.55]
(see also
[
44
, Proposition 2.2]
) implies that

i[{\mathcal{D}}_{k},x]=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{e^{it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}xe^{-it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}-x}{t},

with respect to the strong operator topology.
Therefore, it is sufficient to show that
e^{it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}xe^{-it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
for any
t>0
.

Let
\{x_{i}\}\subset\mathrm{span}\{U({\mathbf{s}}),{\mathbf{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
be such that
x_{i}\to x
in the weak operator topology. By Lemma (
6.11
) we have that
e^{it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}x_{i}e^{-it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
, and therefore

e^{it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}xe^{-it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}=\lim_{i}e^{it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}x_{i}e^{-it{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d}),

with respect to the weak operator topology.
∎

The preceding proposition allows us to introduce mixed partial derivative
\partial^{\alpha}x
of
x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
.

Definition 6.13
.

Let
\alpha
be a multiindex and let
x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
. If every repeated commutator
[{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha_{j}},[{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha_{j}+1},\dots,[{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha_{n}},x]]]
,
j=1,\dots n
extends to a bounded operator on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then the mixed partial derivative
\partial^{\alpha}x
of
x
is defined as

(40)

\partial^{\alpha}x=[{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha_{1}},[{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha_{2}},\dots,[{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha_{n}},x]]].

As usual,
\partial^{0}x:=x
.
By Proposition
6.12
,
\partial^{\alpha}x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
.

Therefore, we can introduce the Sobolev space
W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
associated with noncommutative Euclidean space. Note that our definition of Sobolev spaces is similar to noncommutative Sobolev spaces studied by Kissin and Shulman
[
28
]
, which are called differential Schatten algebras. In our case, we take commutators with a family of self-adjoint operators
({\mathcal{D}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{D}}_{d})
, while for differential Schatten algebras
[
28
]
considers commutators with a single symmetric (not necessarily self-adjoint) operator.

Definition 6.14
.

For a positive integer
m
and
p\geq 1
, the space
W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})

is the space of
x\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
such that every partial derivative
of
x
up to order
m
is also in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
. This space
is equipped with the norm,

\|x\|_{W^{m,p}}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}x\|_{p}

where the sum is taken over the set of multi-indices
\alpha
.

The following proposition summarises some properties of Sobolev spaces.

Proposition 6.15
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We have

(i)

For any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p\geq 1
the Sobolev space
W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
is an algebra.

(ii)

Let
p,q\geq 1
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
y\in W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d}),z\in W^{m,q}({\mathbb{R}}_{d}^{\theta})
. Then
yz\in W^{m,1}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d}).

(iii)

For
p<q
we have
W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})\subset W^{m,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
.

(iv)

An operator
x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
is in the Sobolev class
W^{m,2}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
if and only if

x=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/4}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\mathbf{s}})U({\mathbf{s}})d{\mathbf{s}}

for some (unique)
f\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
{\mathbf{s}}\mapsto\prod_{n_{k}}s_{k}^{n_{k}}f({\mathbf{s}})
,
\sum_{k}n_{k}\leq m,
is square integrable.

(v)

For every positive integer
m
and
p\geq 1
the Sobolev space
W^{m,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
is norm dense in
C({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
(and hence, weakly dense in
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
).

Proof.

The first and the second assertions immediately follow from the Leibniz rule and Hölder inequality, while the third follows from the fact that
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})\subset L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
for
p<q
.
Part (iv) follows from Lemma
6.9
and (
39
). Part (v) is an easy consequence of part (ii)-(iv).

∎

Next we introduce the Laplace and gradient operators associated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
.

Definition 6.16
.

The Laplace operator
\Delta_{\theta}
associated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
is defined on the domain
\operatorname{dom}(-\Delta)=L_{2}({\mathbb{R}},|{\mathbf{t}}|^{4}d{\mathbf{t}})
by

(-\Delta_{\theta}\xi)({\mathbf{t}})=(\sum_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}^{2})\xi(t).

The gradient
\nabla_{\theta}
associated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
is the operator acting in
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{d}

by

\nabla_{\theta}=(-i{\mathcal{D}}_{1},\dots,-i{\mathcal{D}}_{d})=(-iM_{t_{1}},\dots,-iM_{t_{d}})

with the domain

\operatorname{dom}(\nabla_{\theta})=L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},t_{1}^{2}d{\mathbf{t}})\times\dots\times L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},t_{d}^{2}d{\mathbf{t}}).

Definition 6.17
.

Let
N=2^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}
and let
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
be
d
-dimensional
\gamma
-matrices satisfying
\gamma_{i}\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k}\gamma_{j}=2\delta_{j,k}
. The Dirac operator
{\mathcal{D}}
associated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
is the operator on
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\otimes L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
defined by

{\mathcal{D}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}\otimes M_{t_{j}}.

The associated spectral triple is
(1\otimes W^{m,1}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d}),{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\otimes L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),{\mathcal{D}})
.

Remark 6.18
.

It is clear the operators
\Delta_{\theta}
,
\nabla_{\theta}
do not depend on the matrix
\theta
. However, we prefer to use notation with
\theta
to distinguish
\Delta_{\theta}
,
\nabla_{\theta}
from the classical Laplacian
\Delta
and gradient
\nabla
.

7.
Cwikel estimates for the noncommutative Euclidean space

In this section we focus our attention on operators of the form
xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})
.
Everywhere below we assume that
\theta
is non-degenerate, real antisymmetric matrix.
We start with Cwikel estimates in Hilbert-Schmidt class (see
[
19
, Lemma 4.3]
).

Lemma 7.1
.

If
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta},\tau_{\theta})
and if
g\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
then
xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|x\,g(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/4}}\|x\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.9
there exists
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

x=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f({\mathbf{s}})U({\mathbf{s}})d{\mathbf{s}}.

It follows from the definition of
\nabla_{\theta}
, that the operator
g(-i\nabla_{\theta})
is multiplication operator
M_{g}
on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Therefore,

xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})
is an integral operator on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with the kernel

({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})\mapsto f({\mathbf{t}}-{\mathbf{s}})g({\mathbf{s}})e^{\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{t}}\rangle}.

Hence,

\|xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/4}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/4}}\|x\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}<\infty,

where the last equality follows from Lemma
6.9
.
∎

As an immediate corollary of Lemma
7.1
and Theorem
3.5
we have the following result, which yields, in particular, Cwikel estimates for noncommutative Euclidean space in Schatten and weak Schatten ideals for
p>2
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
(E(0,\infty),\|\cdot\|_{E})
be an interpolation space for
(L_{2},L_{\infty})
. If
x\otimes g\in E(L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})\otimes L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
, then
xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\in E(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{E(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq C_{E}\|x\otimes g\|_{E(L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})\otimes L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}.

Proof.

To employ Theorem
3.5
we consider the algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}=L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Let us represent the algebra
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by the identity representation
\pi_{1}(x)=x,x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
and the algebra
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by the representation
\pi_{2}(g)=g(-i\nabla_{\theta})
. By Lemma
7.1
the Hypothesis
3.1
is satisfied in this case. Therefore, the result follows from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
7.2
we can obtain an analogue of Cwikel-Lieb-Rosenblum inequality for noncommutative Euclidean space for
d\geq 4
. We denote by
N(-\Delta_{\theta}+x)
the number of negative eigenvalues of the operator
-\Delta_{\theta}+x
, where
x=x^{*}\in L_{d/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
. Repeating the arguments from Cwikel’s proof of Cwikel-Lieb-Rosenblum inequality
[
12
]
, we obtain the following

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
d\geq 4
,
x=x^{*}\in L_{d/2}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
and let
x_{-}
be the negative part of
x
. Then

N(-\Delta_{\theta}+x)\leq C_{d}\cdot\tau_{\theta}(|x_{-}|^{d/2}),

with the constant
C_{d}
being equal to the constant in
[
12
]
.

In the rest of the section we establish Cwikel estimates in Schatten and weak Schatten ideals for for
1\leq p\leq 2
.
We begin with an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
,
k\geq 0
and let
x\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta}).
Then

(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{\frac{k-1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{k+1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

and

\|(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{\frac{k-1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{k+1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}\|_{p}\leq 2^{k}\|x\|_{W^{k,p}}.

Proof.

Note that by the definitions of
{\mathcal{D}}
and
-\Delta_{\theta}
, we have that
{\mathcal{D}}^{2}=-1\otimes\Delta_{\theta}
, and therefore the assertion is equivalent to the fact that

({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-1-\frac{d}{2}}(1\otimes x)({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-1-\frac{d}{2}}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathbb{C}}^{N}\otimes L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

(41)

\|({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-1-\frac{d}{2}}(1\otimes x)({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-1-\frac{d}{2}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathbb{C}}^{N}\otimes L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq 2^{k}\|x\|_{W^{k,p}}.

We prove the assertion by induction on
k.
For
k=0,

we have

\displaystyle(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}=(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\operatorname{sgn}(x)\cdot|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
|x|^{1/2}\in L_{2p}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
and
(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}\in L_{2p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, Lemma
7.1
implies that

(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}|x|^{\frac{1}{2}},|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. Therefore, the Hölder inequality (
7
) implies that
(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\displaystyle\|(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}

\displaystyle x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\||x|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

\displaystyle=c_{d}\||x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2p}^{2}=c_{d}\|x\|_{p}.

Thus, the assertion holds for
k=0
.

Now suppose that the assertion holds for
k\geq 0
. We prove the equivalent assertion (
41
).
We have

\displaystyle({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}}

\displaystyle(1\otimes x)({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-2}

\displaystyle=({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}(1\otimes x)({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}}[\frac{1}{{\mathcal{D}}-i},1\otimes x]({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-1}

\displaystyle=({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}(1\otimes x)({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}[{\mathcal{D}},1\otimes x]({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-2}.

We set

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}(1\otimes x)({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-1},

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}[{\mathcal{D}},1\otimes x]({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-2}.

By inductive hypothesis,
T_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|T_{1}\|_{p}\leq 2^{k}\|x\|_{W^{k,p}}\leq 2^{k}\|x\|_{W^{k+1,p}}.

For
T_{2}
we have

T_{2}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}(\gamma_{l}\otimes[{\mathcal{D}}_{l},x])({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-2}.

By triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{2}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{d}\|({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}(\gamma_{l}\otimes[{\mathcal{D}}_{l},x])({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-2}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{d}\|({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}(1\otimes[{\mathcal{D}}_{l},x])({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-2}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{d}\|({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}-1}(1\otimes[{\mathcal{D}}_{l},x])({\mathcal{D}}-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-1}\|_{p}.

Using induction hypothesis (as applied to the element
[{\mathcal{D}}_{l},x]
), we obtain

\|T_{2}\|_{p}\leq 2^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\|[{\mathcal{D}}_{l},x]\|_{W^{k,p}}\leq 2^{k}\|x\|_{W^{k+1,p}}.

It follows that

\|(D-i)^{k-\frac{d}{2}}(1\otimes x)(D-i)^{-k-\frac{d}{2}-2}\|_{p}\leq 2^{k+1}\|x\|_{W^{k+1,p}}.

∎

The following lemma shows that in the noncommutative Euclidean space we have a version of translation invariance.

Lemma 7.5
.

For every
{\mathbf{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
there exists a unitary element
V({\mathbf{t}})
on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

[V({\mathbf{t}}),x]=0,\quad x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta})

and

V({\mathbf{t}})e^{is{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}V({\mathbf{t}})^{*}=e^{is({\mathcal{D}}_{k}+t_{k})},

in particular
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
is unitary equivalent to
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}+t_{k}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume
\theta
is block-diagonal
d\times d
matrix, with every block equal to the matrix
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
(otherwise apply
W
defined in (
34
)).

Since for
k\neq n
,
k,n=1,\dots d
the operators
\partial_{k}
and
M_{x_{n}}
commute, we can consider strongly continuous unitary groups defined by

\displaystyle e^{itA_{2j-1}}

\displaystyle=e^{it\partial_{2j-1}}e^{\frac{it}{2}M_{x_{2j}}},

\displaystyle e^{itA_{2j}}

\displaystyle=e^{it\partial_{2j}}e^{-\frac{it}{2}M_{x_{2j-1}}},\quad j=1,\dots\frac{d}{2},

where
A_{k},k=1,\dots,d
denotes the generator of respective group.
We note that by Weyl canonical commutation relations (
6.1
) we have

e^{itA_{2k}}e^{isA_{2k-1}}=e^{its}e^{isA_{2k-1}}e^{itA_{2k}}

and
[e^{itA_{k}},e^{isA_{n}}]=0
otherwise.

Since
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}=M_{t_{k}}
it follows that

(42)

e^{itA_{k}}e^{i{\mathcal{D}}_{l}}=e^{is{\mathcal{D}}_{l}}e^{itA_{k}},\quad l\neq k\\

and

\displaystyle e^{itA_{k}}e^{is{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}

\displaystyle=e^{it\partial_{k}}e^{(-1)^{k-1}\frac{it}{2}M_{x_{k+(-1)^{k-1}}}}e^{isM_{x_{k}}}

(43)

\displaystyle=e^{{}_{i}ts}e^{isM_{x_{k}}}e^{it\partial_{k}}e^{(-1)^{k-1}\frac{it}{2}M_{x_{k+(-1)^{k-1}}}}

\displaystyle=e^{its}e^{is{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}e^{itA_{k}},\quad 1\leq k\leq n.

Moreover, for any
\xi\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
we have

\displaystyle([e^{itA_{2j}},

\displaystyle U({\mathbf{s}})]\xi)({\mathbf{u}})=(e^{itA_{2j}}U({\mathbf{s}})\xi)({\mathbf{u}})-(U({\mathbf{s}})e^{itA_{2j}}\xi)({\mathbf{u}})

\displaystyle=\Big(e^{-i\frac{t}{2}u_{2j-1}}e^{-\frac{it}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{e}}_{2j}\rangle}-e^{-i\frac{t}{2}(u_{2j-1}-s_{2j-1})}\Big)e^{-\frac{i}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{u}}\rangle}\xi({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{s}}+t{\mathbf{e}}_{2j}).

Since
e^{-\frac{it}{2}\langle{\mathbf{s}},\theta{\mathbf{e}}_{2j}\rangle}=e^{\frac{it}{2}s_{2j-1}},
we conclude that

[e^{itA_{2j}},U({\mathbf{s}})]=0.

Repeating similar argument for
[e^{itA_{2j}-1},U({\mathbf{s}})]
one can obtain that

(44)

[e^{itA_{j}},U({\mathbf{s}})]=0,\quad j=1,\dots d,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
{\mathbf{t}}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
we set

V({\mathbf{t}})=\prod_{k=1}^{d}\exp(it_{k}A_{k}).

Clearly
V({\mathbf{t}})
is a unitary operator for any
{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Equality (
44
) implies that

[V({\mathbf{t}}),U({\mathbf{s}})]=0,
for any
{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and therefore
[V({\mathbf{t}}),x]=0,
for any
x\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
.

On the other hand, equations (
42
) and (
7
) imply that

\displaystyle V({\mathbf{t}})e^{is{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}V({\mathbf{t}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\exp(it_{j}A_{j})e^{is{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}\prod_{l=0}^{d-1}\exp(-it_{l}A_{l})

\displaystyle=e^{it_{k}s}e^{is{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}=e^{is({\mathcal{D}}_{k}+t_{k})}.

∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of the present section. The estimates we obtain significantly extend the estimates obtained in
[
19
]
and
[
10
]
.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
.
For every
x\in W^{d,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{\theta})
and
g\in\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
we have that
xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\|xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq\const\|x\|_{W^{d,p}}\|g\|_{\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty})}.

Proof.

For every
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},
we set

B_{\mathbf{n}}=xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\chi_{{\mathbf{n}}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta}).

By Lemma
7.1
we have

\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}\|B_{\mathbf{n}}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\|x\|_{2}^{2}\|g\chi_{K+{\mathbf{n}}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|x\|_{2}^{2}\|g\|_{\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty})}<\infty.

Hence, since,
\{B_{\mathbf{n}}\}
are disjoint from the right, Lemma
2.9
implies that

(45)

\mu^{2}(\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{\mathbf{n}})\prec\mu^{2}(\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{\mathbf{n}})=\mu^{2}(xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})).

We claim that
\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.

For every
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},
we set

A_{\mathbf{n}}:=x\chi_{{\mathbf{n}}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta}).

We have

\displaystyle\mu(B_{\mathbf{n}})

\displaystyle=\mu\big(A_{\mathbf{n}}\cdot g(-i\nabla_{\theta})\chi_{{\mathbf{n}}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\big)\leq\big\|g(-i\nabla_{\theta})\chi_{{\mathbf{n}}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\big\|_{\infty}\mu(A_{\mathbf{n}})

\displaystyle\leq\|g\chi_{K+n}\|_{\infty}\mu(A_{\mathbf{n}}).

Therefore,

\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{\mathbf{n}}\Big\|_{p,\infty}\leq\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|g\chi_{K+{\mathbf{n}}}\|_{\infty}A_{\mathbf{n}}\Big\|_{p,\infty}.

Let
V({\mathbf{n}})
be the unitary operator constructed in Lemma
7.5
. We have

V({\mathbf{n}})A_{\mathbf{n}}V({\mathbf{n}})^{*}=xV({\mathbf{n}})\chi_{{\mathbf{n}}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})V({\mathbf{n}})^{*}=x\chi_{K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})=A_{0}.

Hence, by Lemma
2.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{\mathbf{n}}\Big\|_{p,\infty}\leq\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|g\chi_{K+{\mathbf{n}}}\|_{\infty}V({\mathbf{n}})A_{\mathbf{n}}V({\mathbf{n}})^{*}\Big\|_{p,\infty}

\displaystyle=c_{d}\Big\|A_{0}\otimes\Big\{\|g\chi_{K+{\mathbf{n}}}\|_{\infty}\Big\}_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big\|_{p,\infty}\leq\|A_{0}\|_{p}\cdot\Big\|\Big\{|g\chi_{K+{\mathbf{n}}}\|_{\infty}\Big\}_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big\|_{p,\infty}

\displaystyle=\|A_{0}\|_{p}\|g\|_{\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty})}.

We have

\displaystyle\mu(A_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\mu(x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{2}-1})\Big\|(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{\frac{d}{2}+1}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\chi_{(0,1)}({\mathcal{D}}_{k})\Big\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(1+d)^{\frac{d}{2}+1}\mu(x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}),

in particular,

\|A_{0}\|_{p}\leq\const\|x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\|_{p}.

It follows from Lemma
7.4
(for
k=1+\frac{d}{2}
) that

\|A_{0}\|_{p,\infty}\leq c_{d}\|x\|_{W^{\frac{d}{2}+1,1}}\leq c_{d}\|x\|_{W^{d,p}}.

Thus,
\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and

\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{\mathbf{n}}\Big\|_{p,\infty}\leq c_{d}\|x\|_{W^{d,p}}\|g\|_{\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty})}.

Hence, combining Proposition
2.7
with (
45
) we infer that

xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

and

\|xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq C_{p}\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{\mathbf{n}}\Big\|_{p,\infty}\leq\const\|x\|_{W^{d,p}}\|g\|_{\ell_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty})}.

∎

To conclude this section, we prove Cwikel estimates for the Schatten ideals
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
, for
1\leq p<2
.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
1\leq p<2
. For every
x\in W^{d,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta})
and for every
g\in l_{p}(L_{\infty})({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),
we have that
xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and

\|xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq{\rm const}\|x\|_{W^{d,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta})}\|g\|_{l_{p}(L_{\infty})}.

Proof.

Since
p\geq 2
, we have that
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta})
and
g\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Hence, by Lemma
7.1
, we have that
xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.
Let, as before,
K
be the unit cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. As in the proof of Theorem
7.6
, we have

(46)

\mu^{2}\Big(\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\chi_{{\bf n}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\Big)\prec\mu^{2}\Big(\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\chi_{{\bf n}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\Big)=\mu^{2}(xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})).

By Lemma
7.5
the operators
x\chi_{{\bf n}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})
and
x\chi_{K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})
are unitarily equivalent, and therefore,

\displaystyle\|xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\chi_{{\bf n}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\|g\chi_{{\bf n}+K}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|x\chi_{{\bf n}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle=\|g\chi_{{\bf n}+K}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|x\chi_{K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

By Lemma
7.4
(for
k=1+\frac{d}{2}
) we have

\displaystyle\|x\chi_{K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\chi_{K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{\infty}\|x(1-\Delta_{\theta})^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm const}\|x\|_{W^{d,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta})}.

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\Big\|\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}

\displaystyle xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\chi_{{\bf n}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\Big\|_{p}=\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\chi_{{\bf n}+K}(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq\const\|x\|^{p}_{W^{d,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta})}\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|g\chi_{{\bf n}+K}\|^{p}_{\infty}=\const\|x\|^{p}_{W^{d,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta})}\|g\|^{p}_{\ell_{p}(L_{\infty})({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Hence, Proposition
2.7
(i) together with (
46
) implies that
xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\in\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and

\|xg(-i\nabla_{\theta})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq{\rm const}\|x\|_{W^{d,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\theta})}\|g\|_{l_{p}(L_{\infty})}.

∎

8.
Cwikel estimates for magnetic Laplacian

In this section we apply the abstract Cwikel (see Corollary
3.5
) estimates for magnetic Laplacian ans briefly explain the connection between the noncommutative Euclidean space and magnetic Laplacian.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
be even, let
b>0
and let
{\mathbf{a}}
denotes the magnetic potential

{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})=\frac{b}{2}(-M_{t_{2}},M_{t_{1}},-M_{t_{4}},M_{t_{3}},\dots,-M_{t_{d}},M_{t_{d-1}}),

which corresponds to the isotropic magnetic field of constant strength
b
.
The magnetic Laplacian is the operator on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
defined as

-\Delta_{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\partial_{i}-a_{i})^{2},

as an essentially self-adjoint operator on the set
C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
It is well-known, that the operator
-\Delta_{b}
has pure point spectrum consisting of eigenvalues
\{b(2n+\frac{d}{2})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
of infinite multiplicity
[
4
]
. Note that the magnetic potential
{\mathbf{a}}
can be written as
\frac{b}{2}\theta(M_{t_{1}},\dots,M_{t_{d}})^{T}=\frac{b}{2}\theta(-i\nabla_{\theta}^{T})
, where
\theta
is the matrix, which defines the noncommutative Euclidean space (see Proposition
6.3
with nondegenerate matrix).

The infinitesimal generator of the group
U(t{\mathbf{e}}_{j})
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}},j=1,\dots,d
(where
\{U({\mathbf{t}})\}_{{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
is defined in (
32
)) is the operator
(\partial_{j}-a_{j})
. Since
\{U({\mathbf{t}})\}_{{\mathbf{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
generates the algebra
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
, it follows that
(\partial_{j}-a_{j})
is affiliated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
. Therefore, the operator
-\Delta_{b}
is also affiliated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
. Hence, any bounded function
g
of
-\Delta_{b}
is an operator from
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
.
At the same time the multiplication operator
M_{f}
,
f\in L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, is the function of the gradient
-i\nabla_{\theta}
associated with
L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{\theta}^{d})
(see Definition
6.16
).

Thus, the operator of the form
M_{f}g(-\Delta_{b})
is of the type which we studied in Section
7
, and therefore, one can apply Theorem
7.2
to find estimates on the eigenvalues of the operators of the form
M_{f}g(-\Delta_{b})
. In Theorem
8.3
below we show a straightforward proof of this result for the special case, when
d=2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and let
P_{n}
be the eigenprojection which corresponds to the eigenvalue
(2n+1)b
. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
35
]
) that the integral kernel of
P_{n}
is given by the formula

K_{n}({\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{t}})=\frac{b}{2\pi}L_{n}(\frac{b}{2}|{\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{t}}|^{2})\exp(-\frac{b}{4}|{\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{t}}|^{2}+2i(t_{1}s_{2}-t_{2}s_{1})),\quad{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Here,
L_{n}
is the
n-
th Laguerre polynomial defined by the formula

L_{n}(u)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\frac{(-u)^{m}}{m!},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

Firstly, we need an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 8.1
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and for every
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),
we have
M_{f}P_{n}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
and

\|M_{f}P_{n}\|_{2}=(\frac{b}{2\pi})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}.

Proof.

By the definition of a Hilbert-Schmidt norm, we have

\displaystyle\|M_{f}P_{n}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f|^{2}({\mathbf{s}})L_{n}^{2}(\frac{b}{2}|{\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{t}}|^{2})\exp(-\frac{b}{2}|{\mathbf{s}}-{\mathbf{t}}|^{2})d{\mathbf{s}}d{\mathbf{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f|^{2}({\mathbf{s}})d{\mathbf{s}}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}L_{n}^{2}(\frac{b}{2}|{\mathbf{t}}|^{2})\exp(-\frac{b}{2}|{\mathbf{t}}|^{2})d{\mathbf{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f|^{2}({\mathbf{s}})d{\mathbf{s}}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}L_{n}^{2}(|{\mathbf{t}}|^{2})\exp(-|{\mathbf{t}}|^{2})d{\mathbf{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f|^{2}({\mathbf{s}})d{\mathbf{s}}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{n}^{2}(u)\exp(-u)du=\frac{b}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f|^{2}({\mathbf{s}})d{\mathbf{s}}.

Here, the last equality is guaranteed by
[
1
, 22.2.13]
.
∎

We define the measure
\nu
on the set
b+2b\mathbb{Z}_{+}
by assigning the weight
2b
to every atom. We immediately derive the following result, which ensures that the Hypothesis
3.1
is satisfied. One should also compare this lemma with Lemma
7.1
.

Lemma 8.2
.

For every
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for every
g\in L_{2}(b+2b\mathbb{Z}_{+},\nu),
we have
M_{f}g(-\Delta_{b})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
and

\|M_{f}g(-\Delta_{b})\|_{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

Proof.

Since the spectrum of the operator
-\Delta_{b}
is
\{b+2bn\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
we have that

M_{f}g(-\Delta_{b})=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}M_{f}g(b+2bn)P_{n}.

Therefore, by Lemma
8.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\|M_{f}g(-\Delta_{b})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}|g(b+2bn)|^{2}\|M_{f}P_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\|f\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\frac{b}{2\pi}|g(b+2bn)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

Hence, referring to Theorem
3.5
we obtain

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
E(0,\infty)
be an interpolation space for
(L_{2}(0,\infty),L_{\infty}(0,\infty))
. If
f\otimes g\in E(L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\otimes\ell_{\infty}(b+2b{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\nu)),
then

M_{f}g(-\Delta_{b})\in E(L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
and

\|M_{f}g(-\Delta_{b})\|_{E}\leq\const\|f\otimes g\|_{E(L_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\otimes\ell_{\infty}(b+2b{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\nu))}.

Note, that in the special case when
b=0,
and
E=L_{p}(0,\infty),\,p\geq 2,
or
E=L_{p,\infty}(0,\infty),p>2
, the result exactly coincides with original Cwikel estimates
[
12
]
(see also
[
39
]
).

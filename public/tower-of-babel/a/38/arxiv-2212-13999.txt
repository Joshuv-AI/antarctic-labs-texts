Abstract
For open sets U U in some space X X , we are interested in positive solutions to semi-linear equations L ​ u = φ ⁡ ( ⋅ , u ) ​ μ Lu=\varphi(\cdot,u)\mu on U U . Here L L may be an elliptic or parabolic operator of second order (generator of a diffusion process) or an integro-differential operator (generator of a jump process), μ \mu is a positive measure on U U and φ \varphi is an arbitrary measurable real function on U × ℝ + U\times\mathbbm{R}^{+} such that the functions t ↦ φ ⁡ ( x , t ) t\mapsto\varphi(x,t) , x ∈ U x\in U , are continuous, increasing and vanish at t = 0 t=0 . More precisely, given a measurable function h ≥ 0 h\geq 0 on X X which is L L -harmonic on U U , that is, continuous real on U U with L ​ h = 0 Lh=0 on U U , we give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of positive solutions u u such that u = h u=h on X ∖ U X\setminus U and u u has the same “boundary behavior” as h h on U U (Problem 1) or, alternatively, u ≤ h u\leq h on U U , but u ≢ 0 u\not\equiv 0 on U U (Problem 2). We show that these problems are equivalent to problems of the existence of positive solutions to certain integral equations u + K ​ φ ​ ( ⋅ , u ) = g u+K\varphi(\cdot,u)=g on U U , K K being a potential kernel. We solve them in the general setting of balayage spaces ( X , 𝒲 ) (X,\mathcal{W}) which, in probabilistic terms, corresponds to the setting of transient Hunt processes with strong Feller resolvent.

Balayage spaces

In this section we shall recall the definition of a balayage space and
various facts which we shall use later on (for further details see
[
4
]

or
[
21
]
). The reader who is familiar
with this concept or is mainly interested in our standard examples (or
similar ones) may pass directly to Section
4
and perhaps
go back whenever needed.

3.1
Definition

Let
X
be a locally compact space with countable base of open sets.
For every numerical function
g
on
X
,
let
\hat{g}
denote the largest lower semicontinuous minorant of
g
, that is,

\hat{g}(x):=\liminf_{y\to x}g(y)
,
x\in X
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a convex cone of positive numerical functions on
X
. We consider the following properties that
may hold for
\mathcal{W}
:

(C)

Continuity:
Every
w\in\mathcal{W}
is the supremum of its minorants in

\mathcal{S}^{+}(X):=\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{C}(X).

(S)

Separation:

\mathcal{W}
is linearly separating, that is, for all
x\neq y
and
\gamma>0
, there exists a function
v\in\mathcal{W}

such that
v(x)\neq\gamma v(y)
.

(T)

Transience:
There exist strictly positive
u,v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}(X)
such that
u/v\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(X)
.

Of course, (C) implies that the functions in
\mathcal{W}
are lower semicontinuous, in particular, Borel measurable.

We call
(X,\mathcal{W})
a
balayage space

if the following hold:

(B
0
)

\mathcal{W}
has the properties (C), (S) and (T).

(B
1
)

If
v_{n}\in\mathcal{W}
,
v_{n}\uparrow v
, then
v\in\mathcal{W}
.

(B
2
)

If
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{W}
, then
\widehat{\inf\mathcal{V}}^{f}\in\mathcal{W}
.

(B
3
)

If
u,v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
and
u\leq v^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime}
, then there exist
u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
such that
u=u^{\prime}+u^{\prime\prime}
,
u^{\prime}\leq v^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime\prime}\leq v^{\prime\prime}
.

Here
(B_{1})
means that
\mathcal{W}
has to be closed under increasing limits.
(B_{2})
implies that
\mathcal{W}
is closed under finite infima, whereas arbitrary infima have to be regularized
taking the greatest lower semicontinuous minorant with respect to the
(
\mathcal{W}
-)
fine topology
, hence the superscript
f
.
By definition, the (
\mathcal{W}
-)
fine topology
is the smallest topology on
X

that contains the initial topology and such that every function in
\mathcal{W}
is continuous. Since the functions in
\mathcal{W}

are lower semicontinuous,
(B_{2})
eventually implies that

(3.1)

\widehat{\inf\mathcal{V}}^{f}=\widehat{\inf\mathcal{V}}\qquad\mbox{for all }\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{W}.

The subtlety of requiring the finely lower semicontinuous regularization in
(B_{2})

amounts to a convergence property for
increasing sequences of harmonic functions.
Finally,
(B_{3})
is some lattice property (Riesz property).

For this and a detailed exposition of the theory of balayage spaces the reader may consult
[
4
,
21
]
.
The theory covers the potential theory for a large class of elliptic or parabolic differential equations
of second order. Namely, if
(X,{\mathcal{H}})
is a
\mathcal{P}
-harmonic space and
{}^{\ast}\!{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(X)
denotes
the convex cone of positive hyperharmonic functions on
X
, then
(X,{}^{\ast}\!{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(X))
is a balayage space.
For details the reader may consult
[
19
, Section 7.1]
, see also below.

Moreover, the
isotropic
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes with
0<\alpha<2\wedge d

(and the corresponding Riesz potentials) and many other transient Lévy processes lead to balayage spaces
(see the next subsection and
[
4
, Sections V.3 and V.4]
or
[
21
, Section 3.1]
).

For the sake of generality (which is needed in

.

[

, Lemma 3.3]

7
7

7

For the definition of

).

Proof.

PROPOSITION 4.2
.

Suppose that
\varphi

(i)

(ii)

(i) Trivially,

Suppose that

5

Again let

PROPOSITION 5.1
.

Let

is a compact operator on
\mathcal{B}_{b}(X)

For every

(i)

,
K^{\varphi}u\in\mathcal{P}_{b}(X)-\mathcal{P}_{b}(X)

and
|K^{\varphi}u|\leq cq
.

(ii)

Let
(u_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{B}_{r}(X)

.

(iii)

(

v
is continuous
)
such that
u+K^{\varphi}u=v
.

Proof.

(i) Obvious.

(ii)
Since

for all

, the first statement follows from the compactness of

.
If

(1_{U^{c}}\nu_{n})
is increasing. Hence
also the measures
\nu_{n}^{\prime}:=1_{U}\nu_{n}
(which are supported by
the compact
\overline{U}\setminus V_{n}
) converge weakly.
Their limit
\nu^{\prime}
is a measure on
\partial U
which, of course,
satisfies
\nu^{\prime}\leq\nu
. Therefore
\nu^{\prime}=0
, and hence
\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}^{\prime}(1)=0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}(1_{U}h)=0
.

Finally, let
f\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(X)
.
If
f
is bounded, then obviously
1_{U}H_{U}f
, being bounded by a
multiple of
1_{U}H_{U}1
, is a potential on
U
. In fact, it is now easily seen
that this holds as long as
H_{U}f\in{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(U)
on
U
(approximation of

f
by
f\wedge n
,
n\in\mathbbm{N}
).
∎

8
Application to balayage spaces admitting

a Green function

It should be more or less clear from Section
2
how to apply
our results to situations which are more general than our standard examples.
To be precise, let us consider again a general balayage space
(X,\mathcal{W})
.

DEFINITION 8.1
.

In the following we say that a function
G\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(X\times X)

is a
Green function
for
(X,\mathcal{W})
, if the functions

G(\cdot,y)
,
y\in X
, are non-vanishing potentials on
X
which
are harmonic on
X\setminus\{y\}
.

LEMMA 8.2
.

Let
y\in X
and
g\in{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(X\setminus\{y\})
.

(1)

Then

g\in\mathcal{W}

if and only if
g
is l.s.c. at
y
and
H_{V}g(y)\leq g(y)
for
a fundamental system of neighborhoods
V
of
y
.

(2)

Moreover,
g\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(X)
, if
g\in\mathcal{W}
and
H_{W}g\downarrow 0

locally uniformly as
W\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
increases to
X
.

Proof.

(1) Immediate consequence of
[
4
, III.4.4]
.

(2) It remains to show that
(H_{U}g)|_{U}\in\mathcal{C}(U)
for every
U\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
.
This holds trivially if
y\notin\overline{U}
, since then
H_{U}g=g
.
Hence, by Lemma
3.10
,
it suffices to consider the case, where
y\in U\in\mathcal{V}(U)
.

Fixing
\varepsilon>0
we may choose a neighborhood
W\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
of

\overline{U}
such that
H_{W}g<\varepsilon
on
\overline{U}
, and hence

w:=H_{U}(1_{W^{c}}g)<\varepsilon
on
\overline{U}
(see, for example,
[
4
, VI.9.4]
).
Further, we choose

\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{+}(X)
with compact support such that
\varphi\leq g
on

X
and
\varphi=g
on
W\setminus U
. Since
H_{U}\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(U)
,
we obtain that
h:=H_{U}(1_{W\setminus U}g)\in{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(U)
. Having

H_{U}g=h+w
and
0\leq w<\varepsilon
on
U
, we finally conclude that

(H_{U}g)_{U}\in\mathcal{C}(U)
.
∎

The equation in the following result is often called
Hunt’s
formula
.

PROPOSITION 8.3
.

If
G
is a Green function for
(X,\mathcal{W})
, then, for every open
U
in
X
,
the function
G_{U}\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(U\times U)
, defined by

(8.1)

G_{U}(x,y):=G(x,y)-(H_{U}G(\cdot,y))(x),\qquad x,y\in U,

is a Green function for
(U,\mathcal{W}(U))
.

Proof.

Let
y\in U
and
g:=G(\cdot,y)
.
By Lemma
3.10
and (
3.7
),
H_{U}g\in{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(U)
.
Hence
G_{U}(\cdot,y)
is superharmonic on
U
and harmonic on
U\setminus\{y\}
. It does not vanish, since otherwise
g
would be harmonic on
X
.

Let
z\in U
and
\varepsilon>0
.
We first choose
V\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
with
R_{g}^{V^{c}}(z)=H_{V}g(z)<\varepsilon/2
.
By Remark
3.4
, there exists
W\in{\mathcal{U}}(U\cap V)
such that

R_{g}^{W^{c}}(z)<R_{g}^{(U\cap V)^{c}}(z)+\varepsilon/2
. Trivially,

R_{g}^{(U\cap V)^{c}}\leq R_{g}^{U^{c}}+R_{g}^{V^{c}}
.
So
R_{g}^{W^{c}}(z)<R_{g}^{U^{c}}(z)+\varepsilon
, that is,
H_{W}g(z)<H_{U}g(x)+\varepsilon
.
Since
H_{W}H_{U}=H_{U}
, we see that

H_{W}(G_{U}(\cdot,y))(z)=H_{W}g(z)-H_{U}g(z)<\varepsilon.

Thus
G_{U}(\cdot,y)
is a potential on
U
.
∎

Given a Green function
G
for
X
, let
\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(X)
be the set of
all positive Radon measures
\nu
on
X
such that

(8.2)

G\nu:=\int G(\cdot,y)\,d\nu(y)\in\mathcal{C}(X).

By Fubini’s theorem, we immediately see that, for every measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(U)
,

G_{U}\nu\in{\mathcal{H}}(U\setminus\operatorname*{supp}(\nu))\cap\mathcal{P}(U),

and hence
f\mapsto G_{U}(f\nu)
is the potential kernel

K_{p}
for
p:=G_{U}\nu
.
Note that
\nu
in (
8.2
) is uniquely
determined by the potential
G\nu
(see
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
).

In many cases,
\mathcal{P}(X)=\{G\nu\colon\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(X)\}
, for example,
if the functions
G(x,\cdot)
,
x\in X
, are continuous on
X\setminus\{x\}
, continuous on
X

if
x
is finely isolated, and
there exists
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(X)
charging every non-empty finely open set (see

[
24
]
). Let us note the following.

PROPOSITION 8.4
.

If
G
is a Green function for
(X,\mathcal{W})
such that

\mathcal{P}(X)=\{G\nu\colon\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(X)\}
, then

\mathcal{P}(U)=\{G_{U}\nu\colon\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(U)\}
for every open
U
in
X
.

Proof.

Let
p\in\mathcal{P}(U)
.
There exists a sequence
(p_{n})
in
\mathcal{P}(U)
such that

p=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}
and every
p_{n}
,
n\in\mathbbm{N}
, has a compact support in
U
.
By Theorem
3.17
, there exist
q_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
such that
q_{n}-H_{U}q_{n}=p_{n}
.
Assuming that there are
\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(X)
such that
G\nu_{n}=q_{n}
,
n\in\mathbbm{N}
, we have

\nu_{n}(X\setminus U)=0
and get

\nu:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{C}(U)
with
G_{U}\nu=p
.
∎

REMARK 8.5
.

Let
G
be a Green function on
X
,

\mathcal{P}(X)=\{G\nu\colon\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(X)\}
.

(1) If
p=G\nu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
, then clearly
(
8.1
) and
H_{U}G(1_{U^{c}}\nu)=G(1_{U^{c}}\nu)
imply that

p-H_{U}p=G_{U}(1_{U}\nu)
.

(2) Consider now
q\in\mathcal{P}(U)
which is harmonic outside a
compact
A
in
U
. By Proposition
8.4
,
q=G_{U}\nu
for
some
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(U)
which is supported by
A
. By
Proposition
3.17
, we have a lifting
p\in\mathcal{P}(X)
of
q
. By our assumption on
G
there exists
\widetilde{\nu}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(X)
such that
p=G\widetilde{\nu}
. Of course,
\widetilde{\nu}
is supported by
A
, and we conclude from (1) that

q=p-H_{U}p=G_{U}\widetilde{\nu}
. By uniqueness,
\widetilde{\nu}=\nu
.

In the remainder of this section we assume that
U
is an open set in

X
and that we have a Green function
G_{U}
for
(U,\mathcal{W}(U))
such that

(8.3)

\mathcal{P}(U)=\{G_{U}\nu\colon\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}^{+}(U)\}.

Further, suppose that we have an additive mapping
L\colon\mathcal{P}(U)+{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(U)\to\mathcal{M}_{C}(U)
satisfying

(1)

LG_{U}\nu=-\nu
on
U
for every
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{C}(U)
,

(2)

Lg=0
on
U
for every
g\in{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(U)
.

We observe that we could
define

L
by (1) and (2),
since
\mathcal{P}(U)+{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(U)
is a direct sum by (
3.14
) and,
by
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
, the measure
\nu
is uniquely determined
by the potential
G\nu
.

Let us fix
a Borel measurable function
\varphi\colon U\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

and
h\in{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(U)
such that the functions
(x,t)\to\varphi(x,t)
,
x\in U
, are
continuous, increasing and
\varphi
is locally
h
-Kato (with respect to
\mu
), that is,

G_{U}(1_{A}\varphi(\cdot,h)\mu)\in\mathcal{C}(U)
. We now get the following (cf. Proposition
2.1
).

PROPOSITION 8.6
.

For every
u\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(X)
the following properties are equivalent:

(1)

Lu=\varphi(\cdot,u)\mu
on
U
and

h-u\in\mathcal{P}(U)
.

(2)

u+G_{U}(\varphi(\cdot,u)\mu)=h
.

The function
u\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(X)
having these properties is uniquely
determined by
h
.

Thus the results of Section
6
yield the following.

THEOREM 8.7
.

The equivalences of Theorems
2.3
and
2.5

hold in the present setting
(
where we may replace
h-u\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(U)
,
g-u\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(U)
by
h-u\in\mathcal{P}(U)
,
g-u\in\mathcal{P}(U)
, respectively
)
.

9.1
Products of sub-Markov semigroups

Let
\mathbbm{P}:=(P_{t})_{t>0}
be a sub-Markov semigroup on
X
,
and let
\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}:=(\widetilde{P}_{t})_{t>0}

be a sub-Markov semigroup on
\widetilde{X}
(
\widetilde{X}
being a locally compact space with countable base).
We define a sub-Markov semigroup
\mathbbm{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}=(P_{t}\otimes\widetilde{P}_{t})_{t>0}
on
X\times\widetilde{X}
by

(P_{t}\otimes\widetilde{P}_{t})f(x,\widetilde{x}):=\int\int f(y,\widetilde{y})\,P_{t}(x,dy)\,\widetilde{P}_{t}(\widetilde{x},d\widetilde{y})

for
f\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(X\times\widetilde{X})
,
(x,\widetilde{x})\in X\times\widetilde{X}
(see
[
4
, Section V.5]
and

[
21
, Section 3.3.2]
).

If
(X,{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}})
and
(\widetilde{X},{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}})
are balayage spaces, then

(X\times\widetilde{X},\mathbbm{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}})
is a balayage space if and only if

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
satisfies (C) (which, in particular, holds
if one of the semigroups is strong Feller and the other has a strong Feller resolvent; see
[
4
, V.5.9]

and
[
21
, Proposition 3.2.1]
).

An important case for
\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}
is the semigroup
\mathbbm{T}:=(T_{t})_{t>0}

of uniform translation to the left on
\mathbbm{R}
(see Remark
3.3
,2). We note that

(P_{t}\otimes T_{t})f(x,s):=P_{t}f(\cdot,s-t)(x),\qquad f\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(X\times\mathbbm{R}),\,t>0,\,x\in X,\,s\in\mathbbm{R},

and that
\mathbbm{T}
has a strong Feller resolvent.
The semigroup
\mathbb{T}
is often a testing case, also for product semigroups;
by
[
4
, V.5.10]
, the following holds.

THEOREM 9.1
.

If
\mathbbm{P}
is a sub-Markov semigroup on
X
such that
(X,{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}})
is a balayage space,
the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\mathbbm{P}
is strong Feller.

(2)

(X\times\mathbbm{R},{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}})
is a balayage space.

(3)

(X\times\widetilde{X},{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}})
is a balayage space
for
every
balayage space
(\widetilde{X},{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}})
such that
\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}

is a sub-Markov semigroup on
\widetilde{X}
with strong Feller resolvent.

The next result is borrowed from
[
4
, V.5.6]
. For the proof of
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
, which is not optimal
in
[
4
]
, we note that, for all
r>0
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(X)
,

\lim_{t\downarrow r}P_{t}f=P_{r}f
locally uniformly, by
[
4
, V.2.8 and V.2.11]
,
and hence the resolvent of
\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}
is strong Feller, by
[
4
, V.5.9]
.

THEOREM 9.2
.

For every sub-Markov semigroup
\mathbbm{P}
on
X
, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

(X\times\mathbbm{R},{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}})
is a balayage space.

(2)

\mathbbm{P}
is a strong Feller semigroup,
\lim_{t\to 0}P_{t}f=f
locally uniformly for every

f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with compact support,
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}}
satisfies
(T)
.

As usual, given a sub-Markov semigroup
\mathbbm{P}
and
\lambda\geq 0
, a function
u\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(X)

is called
\lambda
-supermedian
(with respect to
\mathbbm{P}
) if
e^{-\lambda t}P_{t}u\leq u
for all
t>0
.
If
\lambda=0
we speak of supermedian functions.

REMARK 9.3
.

If
\mathbbm{P}
is right continuous, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}}
satisfies (T)
provided there are
\lambda\geq 0
and strictly positive
\lambda
-supermedian functions

u,v\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
u/v\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(X)
.

Indeed, defining
w_{0}(s):=e^{\lambda s}
,
s\in\mathbbm{R}
, we have
T_{t}w_{0}(s)=w_{0}(s-t)=e^{-\lambda t}w_{0}(s)
.
If
w
is any
\lambda
-supermedian function on
X
, then
w^{\prime}\colon(x,s)\mapsto w(x)w_{0}(s)

clearly satisfies

(9.1)

(P_{t}\otimes T_{t})w^{\prime}(x,s)=P_{t}w(x)T_{t}w_{0}(s)\leq e^{\lambda t}w(x)e^{-\lambda t}w_{0}(x)=w^{\prime}(x,s),

hence it is supermedian with respect to
\mathbbm{P}^{\prime}:=\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}
.

Now let
u,v\in\mathcal{C}(X)
be strictly positive
\lambda
-supermedian functions on
X
with

u/v\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(X)
. We may assume that
u\leq 1\leq v
(replace
u
by
u\wedge 1
and
v
by
v+1
).
Then
\widetilde{u}:=u^{\prime}\wedge 1
and
\widetilde{v}:=1+v^{\prime}
are strictly positive continuous
supermedian functions on
X^{\prime}
.
By right continuity of
\mathbbm{P}^{\prime}
, both are excessive. Moreover,
f:=\widetilde{u}/\widetilde{v}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(X^{\prime})
. Indeed,
for every
s\in\mathbbm{R}
,

f(\cdot,s)\leq\frac{u^{\prime}(\cdot,s)}{v^{\prime}(\cdot,s)}=\frac{u}{v}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(X)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad f(\cdot,s)\leq\frac{e^{\lambda s}\wedge 1}{1+e^{\lambda s}}\leq e^{-\lambda|s|},

which clearly implies that
f
tends to
0
at infinity.

9.2
Existence of corresponding Hunt processes

By
[
4
, IV.7.4 and IV.7.5]
), the following holds (for a converse,
see Theorem
3.2
).

PROPOSITION 9.4
.

Let
\mathbbm{P}=(P_{t})_{t>0}
be a sub-Markov semigroup on
X
and
\mathfrak{X}
be a Markov process
on
X
with transition semigroup
\mathbbm{P}

(
such a process always exists
)
. If
(X,{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}})
is
a balayage space, then there is a Hunt process on
X
equivalent to
\mathfrak{X}
.

COROLLARY 9.5
.

Let
\mathbbm{P}=(P_{t})_{t>0}
be a right continuous sub-Markov semigroup on
X
such that
its resolvent is strong Feller and,
for some
\lambda\geq 0
, there exist strictly positive
\lambda
-supermedian
functions
u,v\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
u/v\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(X)
.

Then there is a Hunt process
\mathfrak{X}
on
X
having transition semigroup
\mathbbm{P}
.

Due to the previous results the statement is easily obtained if
even
\mathbbm{P}
is strong Feller. Indeed,
then, by Theorem
9.2
and Remark

9.3
,
(X\times\mathbbm{R},{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}})
is a balayage space.
So, by Theorem
3.9
,
there exists a Hunt process
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
on
X\times\mathbbm{R}
having transition
semigroup
\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}
.
Using the projection
(x,s)\mapsto x

from
X\times\mathbbm{R}
to
X
we get a Hunt process
\mathfrak{X}
on
X
with transition semigroup
\mathbbm{P}
.

In the general case we use the following for the

random walk to the left on
\mathbbm{Z}
.

LEMMA 9.6
.

Let
X=\mathbbm{Z}
and
P(m,\cdot):=\delta_{m-1}
,
m\in\mathbbm{Z}
. Further, let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

a>(1-\alpha)^{-1}
and
u(m):=a^{m}
,
m\in\mathbbm{Z}
.
Then
P_{t}u\leq e^{-\alpha t}u
for every
t>0
.

Proof.

Let
g(t):=e^{(\alpha-1)t}+(e^{\alpha t}-e^{(\alpha-1)t})a^{-1}
,
t>0
.
Since
u(m^{\prime})\leq u(m-1)
for every
m^{\prime}\leq m-1
, (
3.16
) implies that,
for all
t>0
and
m\in\mathbbm{Z}
,

P_{t}u(m)\leq e^{-t}u(m)+(1-e^{-t})u(m-1)=e^{-\alpha t}g(t)u(m).

Clearly,
g(0)=1
and
g^{\prime}(0)=\alpha-1+a^{-1}<0
. So there exists
t_{0}>0
such that, for all
0<t\leq t_{0}
,

g(t)\leq 1
, and hence
P_{t}u\leq e^{-\alpha t}u
. By the semigroup property of
\mathbbm{P}
, we finally
obtain that
P_{t}u\leq e^{-\alpha t}u
for all
t>0
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
9.5
.

Let
Q(m,\cdot):=\delta_{m-1}
,
m\in\mathbbm{Z}
, and
Q_{t}:=e^{-t}\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}/k!Q^{k}
,
t>0
.
Let
a>2
and
w_{0}(m):=a^{m}
,
m\in\mathbbm{Z}
. Of course,
w_{0}
separates
\mathbbm{Z}
.
By Lemma
9.6
,
Q_{t}w_{0}\leq e^{-t/2}w_{0}
for every
t>0
.
Defining
\widetilde{P}_{t}:=Q_{2\lambda t}
we then have

\widetilde{P}_{t}w_{0}\leq e^{-\lambda t}w_{0}\qquad\mbox{for every }t>0.

Proceeding as in Remark
9.3
, we get strictly positive functions

\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v}\in\mathcal{C}(X^{\prime})\cap{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}^{\prime}})
such that
\widetilde{u}/\widetilde{v}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(X^{\prime})
.

Of course,
\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}:=(\widetilde{P}_{t})_{t>0}

is a strong Feller Markov semigroup on
\mathbbm{Z}
with
{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}=S_{Q}
,
and hence
(\mathbbm{Z},{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}})
is a balayage
space, by Proposition
3.15
.
The sub-Markov semigroup

\mathbbm{P}^{\prime}:=\mathbbm{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}
on
X\times\mathbbm{Z}

is right continuous, its resolvent
is strong Feller and its potential kernel is proper
(see Section
9.1
and
[
4
, V.5.2 and V.5.9]
or
[
21
, Propositions 3.2.2 and 3.2.11]
).
Thus
(X\times\mathbbm{Z},{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}})
is a balayage space
and we may finish
now using the projection
(x,m)\mapsto x
from
X\times\mathbbm{Z}
on
X
.
∎

9.3
Harmonic measures for space-time semigroups

Let
\widetilde{X}:=X\times\mathbbm{R}
and let
\mathbbm{P}=(P_{t})_{t>0}
be a
strong Feller sub-Markov semigroup on
X
such that
\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}:=\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}
yields a balayage space
(\widetilde{X},{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}})
(see Section
9.1
for necessary and sufficient
conditions). We recall that
\widetilde{P}_{t}((x,r),\cdot)=P_{t}(x,\cdot)\otimes\delta_{r-t}
, that is,

\widetilde{P}_{t}f(x,r)=P_{t}f(\cdot,r-t)(x),\qquad f\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(\widetilde{X}),\,x\in X,\,r\in\mathbbm{R}.

PROPOSITION 9.7
.

Let
a,r\in\mathbbm{R}
,
a<r
, and
x\in X
. Then

(9.2)

H_{X\times(a,\infty)}((x,r),\cdot)=\widetilde{P}_{r-a}((x,r),\cdot).

Let
\widetilde{U}
be an open neighborhood of
(x,r)
in
X\times(a,\infty)
. Then the harmonic measure

H_{\widetilde{U}}((x,r),\cdot)
is supported by
X\times[a,r]
.

Proof.

Given
w\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
, we define a function
w^{\prime}\leq w

on
\widetilde{X}
by

w^{\prime}(y,s):=\begin{cases}\widetilde{P}_{s-a}w(y,s),&\quad\mbox{ if }s>a,\\
w(y,s),&\quad\mbox{if }s\leq a.\end{cases}

Let
q\in\mathcal{P}(\widetilde{X})
.
If
w\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
with
w\geq q
on
X\times(-\infty,a]
, then clearly
w^{\prime}\geq q^{\prime}
, since the measures
\widetilde{P}_{s-a}((y,s),\cdot)
,
s>a
, are supported by
X\times\{a\}
.
Further, it is easily verified that
q^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
. Therefore

H_{X\times(a,\infty)}q=R_{q}^{X\times(-\infty,a]}=q^{\prime}
. Thus (
9.2
) holds.

Moreover,
\widetilde{A}:=X\times(-\infty,r]
is an absorbing set, since
obviously
1_{X\times(r,\infty)}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
,
(see
[
4
, V.1.2]
). Hence
H_{\widetilde{U}}((x,r),\cdot)
is supported
by
\widetilde{A}
. By Proposition
3.7
,

H_{\widetilde{U}}((x,r),X\times(-\infty,a))\geq H_{X\times(a,\infty)}((x,r),X\times(-\infty,a))=0.

∎

The following results will be used in Sections
9.4
and
9.5
.

LEMMA 9.8
.

Suppose that
x\in X
and
t>0
such that
P_{t}(x,\cdot)\neq\delta_{x}
.
Then there exist
t>0
,
\eta\in(0,1)
and a neighborhood
V\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
such

P_{t}1_{V}\leq\eta
on
V
.

Proof.

Since
P_{t}1\leq 1
, we get that
a:=P_{t}(x,\{x\})<1
. Let

\eta\in(a,1)
. There exists
V_{0}\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
containing
x
such
that
P_{t}(x,V_{0})<\eta
. Since
P_{t}(\cdot,V_{0})\in\mathcal{C}(X)
, there
exists
V\in{\mathcal{U}}(x)
such that
x\in V\subset V_{0}
such that

P_{t}1_{V_{0}}<\eta
on
V
. Since
P_{t}1_{V}\leq P_{t}1_{V_{0}}
, the proof
is finished.
∎

PROPOSITION 9.9
.

Let
V
be open in
X
,
t>0
and
\eta\in(0,1)
with

P_{t}1_{V}\leq\eta
on
V
. Then

\lim_{a\to\infty}H_{V\times(-a,a)}1_{V\times\{-a\}}=0

and

(9.3)

\lim\nolimits_{a\to\infty}H_{V\times(-a,a)}\widetilde{w}=H_{V\times\mathbbm{R}}\widetilde{w}

for every
\widetilde{w}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
such
that
\widetilde{w}
is bounded on
V\times\mathbbm{R}
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{V}:=V\times\mathbbm{R}
,
a>0
,
\widetilde{V}_{a}:=V\times(-a,a)
,
h_{a}:=H_{\widetilde{V}_{a}}1_{V\times\{-a\}}
,

\widetilde{B}_{r}:=V\times\{r\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\gamma_{a}(r):=\sup h_{a}(\widetilde{B}_{r}),\qquad-a<r<a.

Of course,
\gamma_{a}\leq 1
. Suppose that
\widetilde{x}=(x,r)
,
x\in V
,

r<a
, and
s:=r-t>-a
. Defining
\widetilde{W}:=V\times(s,a)

and using Proposition
3.7
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle h_{a}(\widetilde{x})=H_{\widetilde{W}}h_{a}(\widetilde{x})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{a}(s)H_{\widetilde{W}}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{B}_{s})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{a}(s)H_{X\times(s,a)}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{B}_{s})=\gamma_{a}(s)P_{t}(x,V)\leq\eta\gamma_{a}(s).

Thus
\gamma_{a}(s)\leq\eta\gamma_{a}(r)
, and it is straightforward to prove that

\lim\nolimits_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a}=0.

Obviously,
H_{\widetilde{V}}\widetilde{w}=H_{\widetilde{V}_{a}}H_{\widetilde{V}}\widetilde{w}\leq H_{\widetilde{V}_{a}}\widetilde{w}
.
On the other hand, defining
c:=\sup\widetilde{w}(\widetilde{V})
,

H_{\widetilde{V}_{a}}\widetilde{w}(\widetilde{x})\leq H_{\widetilde{V}_{a}}(1_{{V^{c}}\times(-a,a)})\widetilde{w}(\widetilde{x})+c\gamma_{a}(r)\leq H_{\widetilde{V}}\widetilde{w}(\widetilde{x})+c\gamma_{a}(r),

where the last inequality holds by Proposition
3.7
.
Since
\lim_{a\to\infty}\gamma_{a}(r)=0
, we finally conclude that
(
9.3
) holds.
∎

Let
\pi
be the canonical projection
(x,r)\mapsto x
from
\widetilde{X}
to

X
.

COROLLARY 9.10
.

Suppose that
(X,{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}})
is a balayage space,
U
is an open set in

X
and
w\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}}
such that
w\circ\pi
is harmonic on
U\times\mathbbm{R}
.
Assume also that, for every
x\in U
, there exists
t>0
such that

P_{t}(x,\cdot)\neq\delta_{x}
. Then
w
is harmonic on
U
.

Proof.

Clearly,
w\circ\pi\in\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
. Let
x\in U
and let
V_{0}\in{\mathcal{U}}(U)
,
x\in V_{0}
.
By Lemma
9.8
, there exist
t>0
and a neighborhood
V
of

x
in
V_{0}
such that
\sup P_{t}1_{V}(V)<1
.
By assumption,
H_{V\times(-a,a)}(w\circ\pi)=w\circ\pi
for every
a>0
. So
H_{V\times\mathbbm{R}}(w\circ\pi)=w\circ\pi
, by Proposition
9.9
.
By
[
4
, V.8.2]
,
H_{V\times\mathbbm{R}}(w\circ\pi)\widetilde{w}=(H_{V}w)\circ\pi
. Thus
H_{V}w(x)=w(x)
. By
[
4
, III.4.4]
, we finally
conclude that
w
is harmonic on
U
.
∎

9.4
Space-time Green function

In this section we intend to show that, in many cases, a sub-Markov
semigroup
\mathbbm{P}
on a space
X
with “heat kernel”

(x,y,t)\mapsto p_{t}(x,y)
leads to a balayage space

(X\times\mathbbm{R},{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}})
with Green function

((x,r),(y,s))\mapsto 1_{(s,\infty)}(r)p_{r-s}(x,y)
.

Let us suppose that we are in the setting of Section
9.3
and that

P_{t}(x,\cdot)=p_{t}(x,\cdot)m

where
m
is a Radon measure on
X
and

(x,y,t)\mapsto p_{t}(x,y)
is a positive real function on
X\times X\times\mathbbm{R}

having the following properties:

(i)

It is continuous outside
\{(x,x)\colon x\in X\}\times\{0\}
and
p_{t}=0
for
t\leq 0
.

(ii)

For all
s,t\in(0,\infty)
and
x,y\in X
,

p_{s+t}(x,y)=\int p_{s}(x,z)p_{t}(z,y)\,m(dz)\qquad\mbox{(\emph{Chapman-Kolmogorov equations})}.

(iii)

For all
y\in X
and
a>0
,
\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup\{p_{t}(x,y)\colon 0<t\leq a\}=0
.

REMARK 9.11
.

The strong Feller property of
\mathbbm{P}
, which we
assumed, can as well be easily derived from (i) and (iii).

EXAMPLES 9.12
.

Let
X:={\mathbbm{R}^{d}}
,
d\geq 1
. Let
\mathbbm{P}
be one of the
following sub-Markov semigroups on
X
:

1.
Brownian semigroup, given by the transition density

p_{t}(x,y)=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\bigl(-\dfrac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\bigr),\qquad x,y\in{\mathbbm{R}^{d}},\ t>0.

This yields the harmonic space given by the heat equation,
see, e.g.,
[
4
, Section V.6]
or
[
18
, Section 2.3]
.

2. Isotropic
\alpha
-stable semigroup with
0<\alpha<2
, having a
transition density

p_{t}(x,y)\approx t^{-d/\alpha}\land\frac{t}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}},\qquad x,y\in{\mathbbm{R}^{d}},\ t>0,

see, e.g.,
[
27
, (2.11)]
.

For
\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}:=\mathbbm{P}\otimes\mathbbm{T}=(\widetilde{P}_{t})_{t>0}

on
\widetilde{X}:=X\times\mathbbm{R}
we now have

(9.4)

\widetilde{P}_{t}f(x,r)=\int p_{t}(x,z)f(z,r-t)\,dm(z),\qquad f\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(\widetilde{X}).

We define
\widetilde{G}\colon\widetilde{X}\times\widetilde{X}\to[0,\infty)
by

(9.5)

\widetilde{G}((x,r),(y,s)):=p_{r-s}(x,y).

THEOREM 9.13
.

\widetilde{G}
is a Green function for the balayage space
(\widetilde{X},{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}})
.

Proof.

Let us fix
\widetilde{y}=(y,s)\in\widetilde{X}
and define

\widetilde{g}:=\widetilde{G}(\cdot,\widetilde{y}).

By (i),
\widetilde{g}
is continuous on
X\setminus\{\widetilde{y}\}
, l.s.c. at
\widetilde{y}
. For
t>0
,
z\in X
and
r\in\mathbbm{R}
,

\widetilde{g}(z,r-t)=p_{r-t-s}(z,y)
, and hence, by
(
9.4
) and (ii),

(9.6)

\widetilde{P}_{t}\widetilde{g}(x,r)=1_{(s+t,\infty)}(r)\,\widetilde{g}(x,r),\qquad t>0,\,(x,r)\in X.

Since
\widetilde{g}(x,r)=0
for
r\leq s
, we conclude that
\widetilde{g}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
.

We next show that
\widetilde{g}
is harmonic on
X\setminus\{\widetilde{y}\}
. If
\widetilde{x}\in X\times(-\infty,s]
and
V
is an
open neighborhood of
\widetilde{x}
, then
0\leq H_{V}\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{x})\leq\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{x})=0
whence

H_{V}\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{x})=\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{x})=0
.
So let us fix
\widetilde{x}=(x,r)\in X
,
r>s
.
There exists
t>0
such that
r>s+t
. Let
\widetilde{V}
be an open neighborhood
of
\widetilde{x}
such that its closure is a compact in
\widetilde{U}:=X\times(r-t,\infty)
.
We have

\widetilde{g}\geq H_{\widetilde{V}}\widetilde{g}\geq H_{\widetilde{V}}H_{\widetilde{U}}\widetilde{g}=H_{\widetilde{U}}\widetilde{g}
, where
H_{\widetilde{U}}\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{x})=\widetilde{P}_{t}\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{x})=\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{x})
, by Proposition

9.7
and (
9.6
).
So
H_{V}\widetilde{g}(x)=\widetilde{g}(x)
. By
[
4
, III.4.4]
, we conclude
that
\widetilde{g}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{+}(\widetilde{X}\setminus\{\widetilde{y}\})
.

Finally, let
a,b\in\mathbbm{R}
,
a<s\leq r<b
, and let
V\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
,
\varepsilon>0
. By (iii), there exists
W\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
containing
V

such that
\widetilde{g}\leq\varepsilon
on
\widetilde{A}:=(X\setminus W)\times[a,b]
.
Let
\widetilde{W}=W\times(a,b)
. By Proposition
9.7
,
the measures
H_{\widetilde{W}}(\widetilde{x},\cdot)
,
\widetilde{x}\in V\times(a,b)
, are supported by the union
of
\widetilde{A}
and
W\times\{a\}
. Since
\widetilde{g}=0
on
X\times\{a\}
, we
obtain that
H_{\widetilde{W}}\widetilde{g}=H_{\widetilde{W}}(1_{\widetilde{A}}\widetilde{g})\leq\varepsilon

on
V\times(a,b)
. Thus
\widetilde{g}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
, by Lemma
8.2
.
∎

Let
\widetilde{m}:=m\otimes\lambda_{\mathbbm{R}}
and let
\widetilde{V}
be the potential
kernel of
\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}
, that is,
\widetilde{V}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{P}_{t}\,dt
. For all
f\in\mathcal{B}^{+}(\widetilde{X})

and
\widetilde{x}=(x,r)\in\widetilde{X}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{P}_{t}f(\cdot,r-t)(x)\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)f(y,r-t)\,dm(y)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\widetilde{G}((x,r),(y,s))f(y,s)\,d\widetilde{m}(y,s)=:\widetilde{G}(f\widetilde{m})(x).

In particular,
\widetilde{V}
is proper, since for every
bounded interval
(a,b)
in
\mathbbm{R}
,

\widetilde{V}1_{X\times(a,b)}(x,r)=\int_{(r-b)^{+}}^{(r-a)^{+}}\widetilde{P}_{t}1(x)\,dt\leq b-a.

Thus, for every
w\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\mathbbm{P}}}
, there exists a sequence

(f_{n})
in
\mathcal{B}_{b}^{+}(\widetilde{X})

such that

\widetilde{G}(f_{n}\widetilde{m})\uparrow w

(see
[
4
, II.3.11]
). So, by
Theorem
9.13

and
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
, we obtain the following.

COROLLARY 9.14
.

For every
p\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{r}(\widetilde{X})
,
there exists a unique
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\widetilde{X})
such that
p=\widetilde{G}\mu
.

Further, using Propositions
8.3
and
8.4
we get the following.

THEOREM 9.15
.

For every open
U
in
\widetilde{X}
, the definition

\widetilde{G}_{U}(\cdot,\widetilde{y}):=\widetilde{G}(\cdot,\widetilde{y})-H_{U}\widetilde{G}(\cdot,\widetilde{y}),\qquad\widetilde{y}\in U,

yields a Green function
\widetilde{G}_{U}
on
U
and, for every continuous real potential
p
on
U
,
there exists a unique
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(U)
such that
p=\widetilde{G}_{U}\mu
.

9.5
Green function for semigroups with densities

Let us suppose that we are in the setting of the previous subsection,
that
(X,{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}})
is a balayage space and, for every
x\in X
, there
exists
t>0
, such that
P_{t}(x,\cdot)\neq\delta_{x}
, that is,

X
does not contain absorbing points.
We define

G_{y}(x):=G(x,y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt,\qquad x,y\in X.

THEOREM 9.16
.

Suppose that for every neighborhood
V
of a point
y\in X
, the function
G_{y}
is
bounded and continuous on
X\setminus V
and that
G_{y}(y)<\infty
if

y
is isolated. Then
G
is a Green function for
(X,{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}})
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{G}
be the Green function from the previous
section. Clearly, for all
x,y\in X
and
r,s\in\mathbbm{R}
,

G(x,y)=\int_{\mathbbm{R}}\widetilde{G}((x,r),(y,s))\,ds.

Let us fix
y\in X
and let
\widetilde{L}:=\{y\}\times\mathbbm{R}
,

g:=G(\cdot,y)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widetilde{g}:=g\circ\pi.

By Fubini,
g\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{P}}
,
\widetilde{g}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbbm{\widetilde{P}}}

and, using that the functions
\widetilde{G}(\cdot,(y,s))
,
s\in\mathbbm{R}
, are harmonic on
\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{L}
,

H_{\widetilde{V}}\widetilde{g}=\widetilde{g}\qquad\mbox{ for every }\widetilde{V}\in{\mathcal{U}}(\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{L}).

So
\widetilde{g}
is harmonic on
\widetilde{X}\setminus L
and, by
Corollary
9.10
,
g
is harmonic on
X\setminus\{y\}
.

If
V\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
with
y\in V
, then
(H_{V}g)|_{V}\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(V)
, since

g
is bounded on
{V^{c}}
and
1\in\mathcal{W}
. Hence
g
is
superharmonic on
X
, by Lemma
3.10
.

Let
U_{n}\in{\mathcal{U}}(X)
exhaust
X
. Then the functions

h_{n}:=H_{U_{n}}g
,
n\in\mathbbm{N}
, are decreasing to a harmonic
function
h
on
X
. It remains to show that
h=0
.
Defining
\widetilde{V}_{n}:=U_{n}\times(-n,n)
we get

H_{\widetilde{V}_{n}}\widetilde{g}\downarrow 0\quad\text{ on }\{\widetilde{g}<\infty\}.

as
n\to\infty
.
We have
h_{n}\circ\pi=H_{U_{n}\times\mathbbm{R}}\widetilde{g}\leq H_{\widetilde{V}_{n}}\widetilde{g}
.
Hence
h=0
on
X\setminus\{y\}
. By continuity,
h=0
on
X
, unless
y
is
isolated.
If
y
is isolated, then we get directly that
h(y)=0
.
∎

By the same arguments as at the end of the previous section, we now
get the following.

COROLLARY 9.17
.

Assume, in addition, that the functions
G(x,\cdot)
,
x\in X
, are
continuous on
X\setminus\{x\}
and that the potential kernel of
\mathbbm{P}

is proper. Then the following holds:

(1)

For every
p\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{r}(X)
,
there exists a unique
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(X)
such that
p=G\mu
.

(2)

For every open
U
in
X
, the definition

G_{U}(\cdot,y):=G(\cdot,y)-H_{U}G(\cdot,y),\qquad y\in U,

yields a Green function
G_{U}
on
U
and, for every continuous real potential
p
on
U
,
there exists a unique
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(U)
such that
p=G_{U}\mu
.

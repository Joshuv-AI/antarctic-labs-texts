Lie Algebra Cohomology of the positive part of Twisted Affine Lie Algebras
Jiuzu Hong, Shrawan Kumar

Abstract
. The explicit Verlinde formula for the dimension of conformal blocks, attached to a marked projective curve Σ \Sigma , a simple Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} over ℂ \mathbb{C} and integrable highest weight modules of a fixed central charge of the corresponding affine Lie algebra L ^ ​ ( 𝔤 ) \hat{L}(\mathfrak{g}) attached to the marked points, requires (among several other important ingredients) a Lie algebra cohomology vanishing result due to C. Teleman for the positive part L ^ + ​ ( 𝔤 ) \hat{L}^{+}(\mathfrak{g}) with coefficients in the tensor product of an integrable highest weight module with copies of finite dimensional evaluation modules. The aim of this paper is to extend this result of Teleman to a twisted setting where 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is endowed with a special automorphism σ \sigma and the curve Σ \Sigma is endowed with the action of σ \sigma . In this general setting, the affine Lie algebra gets replaced by twisted affine Lie algebras. The crucial ingredient (as in Teleman) is to prove a certain Nakano Identity.



1.
Introduction

We take the base field in this paper to be the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.
The dimension of the space of conformal blocks can be computed by the celebrated Verlinde formula (cf.
[
Ku2
, Theorem 4.2.19]
).
This formula was originally conjectured by Verlinde in Conformal Field Theory. It was mathematically derived by combining the efforts of several mathematicians (cf.
[
Ku2
, 4.C]
). Some of the main ingredients going into its proof consisted of reducing the problem from a genus
g
curve
\Sigma
with any number of marked points to a genus
0
curve (i.e.
\mathbb{P}^{1}
) with three marked points (by using the Factorization Theorem and the existence of a flat projective connection on the bundle of conformal blocks over the moduli space of curves
[
TUY
]
). The problem for three marked points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is encoded in so called the
Fusion Algebra
. Faltings obtained a certain result on the classification of characters of the fusion algebra for the classical simple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
as well as
\mathfrak{g}
of type
G_{2}
. This allows one to derive the explicit dimension formula for these Lie algebras. Then, Teleman proved the cohomology vanishing result mentioned in the Abstract (see below for a precise statement) for any simple
\mathfrak{g}
, thereby one obtains the explicit dimension formula for the space of conformal blocks for any simple
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group acting on a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a projective algebraic curve
\Sigma
. One may define the space of twisted conformal blocks in this equivariant setting. A generalization of the work of Tsuchiya-Ueno-Yamada
[
TUY
]
in this setting was developed in
[
Da
,
HK1
]
. Subsequently, in
[
HK2
]
the authors proved that the calculation of the dimension of twisted conformal blocks can be reduced to the situation that
\Gamma
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
by diagram automorphisms. Moreover, an explicit formula for the dimension of the twisted conformal blocks (akin to the classical Verlinde dimension formula)
can be derived assuming a twisted analogue of Teleman’s vanishing theorem on the Lie algebra (co)homology
(cf.
[
HK2
, Theorem 6.9]
). It should be mentioned that, using the machinery of modular tensor categories, under some restrictions, the dimension formula for the twisted conformal blocks was obtained in
[
DM
]
earlier. However, our approach to the same problem is quite different. In fact, combining the works of
[
HK1
,
HK2
,
DM
]
, one can derive the dimension formula for the twisted conformal blocks for a general
\Gamma
-curve
\Sigma
and any
\Gamma
action on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\Gamma=\langle\sigma\rangle
be the cyclic group acting on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with ramified points only at
\{0,\infty\}
, and
\sigma
acting on
\mathfrak{g}
as a special automorphism (cf. Definition
3.3
). Let
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
be the associated twisted affine Lie algebra, the central extension of the twisted loop algebra
\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}((t))\right)^{\sigma}
and let
L^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma):=(t\mathfrak{g}[t])^{\sigma}
be its Lie subalgebra (called the positive part of
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
).
Let
\mathscr{H}_{c}(\lambda)
be the integrable highest weight module of
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
of level
c
and highest weight
\lambda
. We choose
s+1
points
\vec{z}
in
\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that their
\Gamma
-orbits are distinct, where
\vec{z}=(z_{0}=0,z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
. At each point
z_{i}
, we attach the finite dimensional irreducible representation
V(\mu_{i})
of the fixed point Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma_{i}}
with highest weight
\mu_{i}
,
where
\Gamma_{i}
is the stabilizer group of
\Gamma
at
z_{i}
. Thus, with our choice,
V(\mu_{0})
is an irreducible representation of

\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
, whereas
V(\mu_{i})
(for
1\leq i\leq s
) is an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The choice of
z_{i}
gives rise to an evaluation representation
V(\mu_{i})_{z_{i}}
of
L^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
at
z_{i}
. Thus,
L^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
naturally acts on the tensor product

\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{c}(\lambda)\otimes V(\vec{\mu})_{\vec{z}}:=\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{c}(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu_{0})_{z_{0}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V(\mu_{s})_{z_{s}},

where
\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{c}(\lambda)
is the direct
product
of the energy eigenspaces of
\mathscr{H}_{c}(\lambda)
.

Let
H^{q}(L^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma),\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{c}(\lambda)\otimes V(\vec{\mu}))
be the
q
-th Lie algebra cohomology of
L^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
with coefficients in
\mathscr{H}_{c}(\lambda)\otimes V(\vec{\mu})_{\vec{z}}
. Our main result of this paper is Theorem
3.15
, which asserts the following.

Theorem.

Let
\sigma
be a special automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
of order
m
and let
(\mathfrak{g},m)
be different from
(D_{4},3)
.
For any
q\geq 1
, the Lie algebra cohomology

H^{q}(L^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma),\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{c}(\lambda)\otimes V(\vec{\mu})_{\vec{z}})^{\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}}=0,\quad\text{ if }\,d_{\vec{\mu}}<\frac{c+2\check{h}}{m},

and the points
\vec{z}=\{z_{0}=0,z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}
in the open unit disc are far apart in the hyperbolic metric.
Here
\check{h}
is the dual Coxeter number of
\mathfrak{g}
and
d_{\vec{\mu}}
is defined by the identity (
29
).

The above theorem, in the special case
\sigma
is the identity automorphism, recovers Teleman’s higher cohomology vanishing result (cf.
[
Te
, Theorem 0 (c)]
).

To prove this vanishing theorem, we generally follow the strategy of Teleman’s proof in the untwisted case. However, there is one major difference in that the Möbius transformations do not preserve the twisted affine Lie algebras (unlike the untwisted affine case). In the untwisted case, this allowed Teleman to reduce the problem of any marked point
z
in
\mathbb{C}
to the point
0
, which made some of the computations much easier to deal with. We establish a twisted analogue of Nakano identity in Theorems
3.10
and
3.11
. The proof of this identity requires a cumbersome computation of Laplacian operators, which is carried out in Section
4
. Some results on Casimir operators on graded Lie algebras in Section
3.4
, especially Proposition
3.8
is used crucially in Section
4
. We would like to stress that, in our above vanishing theorem, the restriction on
\vec{\mu}
is not optimal.

As a corollary of the above theorem, we get the following (cf. Corollary
3.18
).

Corollary.

Under the assumptions of the above theorem, for any
q\geq 1
we have

H_{q}(L^{-}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma^{-1}),\mathscr{H}_{c}(\lambda^{*})\otimes V(\vec{\mu}^{*})_{\vec{z}^{-1}})^{\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}}=0,\quad\text{ if }\,d_{\vec{\mu}}<\frac{c+2\check{h}}{m},

where
\vec{z}^{-1}=(\infty,z_{1}^{-1},\cdots,z_{s}^{-1})
,

\mathscr{H}_{c}(\lambda^{*})
is the highest weight module of the twisted affine Lie algebra
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma^{-1})
,
and
V(\vec{\mu}^{*})_{\vec{z}^{-1}}:=V(\mu_{0}^{*})_{\infty}\otimes\cdots\otimes V(\mu_{s}^{*})_{z_{s}^{-1}}
represents the evaluation representation of
L^{-}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma^{-1})
in the usual sense.

The above corollary implies the analogue of the Verlinde dimension formula for twisted conformal blocks on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with three marked points under the restriction on
\vec{\mu}
and
\sigma
as in the above corollary (cf.
[
HK2
, Theorem 6.9]
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Kac-Moody theory

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of order
m
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be the field of Laurent series in the parameter
t
, such that
\sigma(t)=\epsilon^{-1}t
where
\epsilon=e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{m}}
and
\sigma
acts on
\mathbb{C}
trivially. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the field of formal power series in
t
. We now define a central extension
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma):=\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{K})^{\sigma}\oplus\mathbb{C}C
of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{K})^{\sigma}
under the bracket

(1)

[x[P]+zC,x^{\prime}[P^{\prime}]+z^{\prime}C]=[x,x^{\prime}][PP^{\prime}]+m^{-1}\Res_{t=0}\,\bigl(({dP})P^{\prime}\bigr)\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle C,

for
x[P],x^{\prime}[P^{\prime}]\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{K})^{\sigma}
,
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
; where

\Res_{t=0}
denotes the coefficient of
t^{-1}dt
and
\langle\,,\,\rangle
denotes the normalized invariant form on
\mathfrak{g}

so that the induced form on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
satisfies
\langle\theta,\theta\rangle=2
for the highest root
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Throughout the paper, we fix a positive integer (called the level)
c>0
. We also fix an integer
s>0
denoting the number of marked points.

We use
D_{c,\sigma}
to denote the set of highest weights of
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
which parametrizes the integrable highest weight modules of
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
of level
c
, where the level denotes the action of
C
, see
[
HK1
, Section 2]
. When
\sigma
is trivial, we also use
D_{c}
to denote this set for brevity. For each
\lambda\in D_{c,\sigma}
, we will denote by
(\mathscr{H}_{c}(\lambda),\rho_{\lambda})
(or for simplicity
\mathscr{H}_{c}(\lambda)
) the associated integrable highest weight module of
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
of level
c
.

There exists a ‘compatible’ Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
and a ‘compatible’ Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}\supset\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
both stable under the action of
\sigma
such that

(2)

\sigma=\tau\epsilon^{{\rm ad}h},

where
\tau
is a (possibly trivial) diagram automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
of order
r
preserving
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{b}
,
\alpha(h)\in\mathbb{Z}
for any root
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\epsilon^{{\rm ad}h}
is the inner automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
such that for any root
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\epsilon^{{\rm ad}h}
acts on the root space
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
by the multiplication
\epsilon^{\alpha(h)}
, and
\epsilon^{{\rm ad}h}
acts on
\mathfrak{h}
by the identity. Here
h
is an element in
\mathfrak{h}^{\tau}
. In particular,
\tau
and
\epsilon^{{\rm ad}h}
commute. Moreover,
r
divides
m
,
\alpha(h)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
for any positive root
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
and
\theta_{0}(h)\leq\frac{m}{r}
where
\theta_{0}\in(\mathfrak{h}^{\tau})^{*}
denotes the following weight of
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}
:

\theta_{0}=\begin{cases}\text{ highest root of }\mathfrak{g},\text{ if }r=1\\
\text{ highest short root of }\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\text{ if }r>1\text{ and }(\mathfrak{g},r)\neq(A_{2n},2)\\
2\cdot\text{highest short root}\text{ of }\mathfrak{g}^{\tau},\,\text{ if }(\mathfrak{g},r)=(A_{2n},2).\end{cases}

Let
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\tau)
denote the Lie algebra with the construction similar to
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
where
\sigma
is replaced by
\tau
,
m
is replaced by
r
and
\epsilon
is replaced by
\epsilon^{\frac{m}{r}}
. There exists an isomorphism of Lie algebras (cf.
[
Ka
, Theorem 8.5]
):

(3)

\Psi_{\sigma}:\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\tau)\simeq\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)

given by
C\mapsto C
and

x[t^{j}]\mapsto x[t^{\frac{m}{r}j+k}]
, for any
x
an
\epsilon^{\frac{m}{r}j}
-eigenvector of
\tau
, and
x
also a
k
-eigenvector of
{\rm ad}\,h
. Then, the isomorphism
\Psi_{\sigma}
induces a bijection

(4)

D_{c,\sigma}\simeq D_{c,\tau},\quad\lambda\mapsto\bar{\lambda}.

.

The explicit description of
D_{c,\sigma}
is given in
[

, Lemma 2.1]
in terms of

defined there. Also,

can be expressed in terms of numbers

3.
Vanishing theorem for Lie algebra cohomology

3.1.
Contravariant Hermitian form on twisted affine algebras

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
of order
m
. As in (
2
), we write
\sigma=\tau\epsilon^{adh}
with respect to a compatible Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
and a Cartan sublagebra
\mathfrak{h}
contained in
\mathfrak{b}
, where
\epsilon=e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{m}}
. With respect to
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{h}
, we choose a set of Chevalley generators
\{e_{i},f_{i},h_{i}\,|\,i\in I\}
such that they are permuted by
\tau
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
be the real split form of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by
\{e_{i},f_{i},h_{i}\,|\,i\in I\}
. Let
\omega
be the Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
defined by

\omega(e_{i})=-f_{i},\omega(f_{i})=-e_{i},\omega(h_{i})=-h_{i},\quad\text{ for any }i\in I.

Define a
compact (conjugate-linear) involution

\kappa
of
\mathfrak{g}
:

(5)

\kappa=\omega\circ J,

where
J
is the complex conjugation of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The equality
\sigma\circ\kappa=\kappa\circ\sigma
holds on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

For each
i\in I
, we have

\sigma(e_{i})=\epsilon^{\alpha_{i}(h)}e_{\tau(i)},\sigma(f_{i})=\epsilon^{-\alpha_{i}(h)}f_{\tau(i)},\sigma(h_{i})=h_{\tau(i)},

where
e_{i}
belongs to the root space
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{i}}
.
It is now easy to see that the equality
\sigma\circ\kappa=\kappa\circ\sigma
holds on
\{e_{i},f_{i},h_{i}\}
. Thus, the equality holds on
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

Consider the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{\underline{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\,\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{n}}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{n}}
is the
\epsilon^{n}
-eigenspace of
\sigma
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then,
\kappa(\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{n}})=\mathfrak{g}_{-\underline{n}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{0}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
.

As before, let
\langle,\rangle
be the normalized invariant form on
\mathfrak{g}
so that
\langle\theta,\theta\rangle=2
for the highest root
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then,
\langle,\rangle:\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{n}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{-\underline{n}}\to\mathbb{C}
is non-degenerate for each
\underline{n}
, and the eigenspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{n}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-\underline{n}^{\prime}}
for
\underline{n}\neq\underline{n}^{\prime}
are orthogonal with respect to
\langle,\rangle
.

Define a Hermitian form
\{,\}
on
\mathfrak{g}
as follows:

(6)

\{x,y\}=-\langle x,\kappa(y)\rangle,\quad\text{ for any }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then,
\{,\}
is a positive-definite contravariant Hermitian form on
\mathfrak{g}
, where contravariance means

\{[z,x],y\}=-\{x,[\kappa(z),y]\},\,\,\,\text{for all $x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}$}.

Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{\underline{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\,\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{n}}
is an orthogonal decomposition with respect to
\{,\}
. Thus, we can choose an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}
with respect to
\{\,,\,\}
such that
\mathcal{B}_{{\underline{n}}}:=\mathcal{B}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{n}}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\underline{n}}
. We define a function
[\cdot]:\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
given by
[b]=\underline{n}
, if
b\in\mathcal{B}_{{\underline{n}}}
. For each
b\in\mathcal{B}
, set
\check{b}=-\kappa(b)
. Then,
\{\check{b}\}_{b\in\mathcal{B}}
is the dual basis of
\mathcal{B}
with respect to
\langle,\rangle
. Moreover,
[\check{b}]=-\underline{n}
, if
[b]=\underline{n}
.

For
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
x(n):=x[t^{n}]
. The action of
\sigma
on
\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{K})
is given in Section
2.1
and
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
defined there. Moreover,
\sigma
acts trivially on
C
.

Let
\hat{L}^{f}(\mathfrak{g})
denote the Lie subalgebra of
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g}):=\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},I)
obtained by replacing the function field
\mathcal{K}
by the ring of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
. Similarly,

\hat{L}^{f}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma):=\left(\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\right)^{\sigma}\oplus\mathbb{C}C.

Extend the compact involution
\kappa
of
\mathfrak{g}
to
\hat{L}^{f}(\mathfrak{g})
as the conjugate-linear involution defined by

(7)

\kappa(x(n))=\kappa(x)(-n),\quad\text{ for any }x(n)\in\hat{L}^{f}(\mathfrak{g}),\,\,\,\text{and $\kappa(C)=-C$}.

We take

(\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}[t^{\pm}])\oplus{\mathbb{R}}C

as the split real form of
\hat{L}^{f}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Similarly, we extend the contravariant Hermitian form
\{,\}
on
\mathfrak{g}
to
\hat{L}^{f}(\mathfrak{g})

\{x(n),y(n^{\prime})\}=\{x,y\}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}},\,\,\,\text{and $\{x(n),C\}=0$}.

Similar to Lemma

and

. Moreover, the restriction of
\{,\}
to

and

on

\{x(-n),y(-n^{\prime})\}_{0}=\frac{1}{n}\{x(-n),y(-n^{\prime})\},\quad\text{ for any }x(-n),y(-n^{\prime})\in{L}^{-}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma),

Then,

Lemma 3.6

Let

\mathfrak{g}
of order

\theta_{\sigma}
be the element defined below:

(16)

\theta_{\sigma}=\begin{cases}\text{ highest short root of }\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma},\quad(\mathfrak{g},m)\not=(A_{2n},4)\\
\frac{1}{2}\text{ highest root of $\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}$},\quad(\mathfrak{g},m)=(A_{2n},4).\par\end{cases}

Then,
\langle\theta_{\sigma},\theta_{\sigma}+2\rho_{\sigma}\rangle=\frac{2\check{h}}{m}
, where
\check{h}
as earlier is the dual Coxeter number of
\hat{L}(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
(or
\mathfrak{g}

\rho_{\sigma}

\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}

Proof.

Let
(,)
be the standard pairing between
\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma}

(\mathfrak{h}^{\sigma})^{*}

\langle\theta_{\sigma},\theta_{\sigma}+2\rho_{\sigma}\rangle=\frac{(2\check{\theta}_{\sigma},\theta_{\sigma}+2\rho_{\sigma})}{\langle\check{\theta}_{\sigma},\check{\theta}_{\sigma}\rangle},

is the element defined below:

(17)

\check{\theta}_{\sigma}=\begin{cases}\text{ highest coroot of }\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma},\quad(\mathfrak{g},m)\not=(A_{2n},4)\\

When

is different from
(A_{2n},4)
, as
\check{\theta}_{\sigma}
is the highest coroot,
\langle\check{\theta}_{\sigma},\check{\theta}_{\sigma}\rangle=2m
. When
(\mathfrak{g},m)
is
(A_{2n},4)
, from an explicit realization of
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
, one can see that for any short coroot
\check{\beta}
,
\langle\check{\beta},\check{\beta}\rangle=2
. Since
\check{\theta}_{\sigma}
is twice the highest short coroot,
\langle\check{\theta}_{\sigma},\check{\theta}_{\sigma}\rangle=8=2m
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\sigma
be a special automorphism of order
m
of
\mathfrak{g}

(18)

\sum_{k\geq 0,b\in\mathcal{B}\,\text{with}\,[b]=-\underline{k}}ad_{b(-k)}\overline{ad}_{\check{b}(k)}\cdot x(-p)=\frac{2p\check{h}}{m}x(-p).

Observe that

S:=\sum_{k\geq 0,b\in\mathcal{B}\,\text{with}\,[b]=-\underline{k}}ad_{b(-k)}\overline{ad}_{\check{b}(k)}\cdot x(-p)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}C_{-\underline{k}}\cdot x(-p),

C_{-\underline{k}}:=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}\,\text{with}\,[b]=-\underline{k}}ad_{b}ad_{\check{b}}.

Then,

is the Casimir operator of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the normalized invariant form. Its action on any irreducible

By (

Equivalently,

We compare this identity on each degree of

. When the degree of
t
is 0, we get

.
For any

. When

t
in (

This proves (
35

Thus, we can assume

. We induct on

. When

, comparing the coefficients of

, we have

\displaystyle ka_{k}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}(-\bar{z}(k-2)a^{n}_{k-2}+(1+|z|^{2})(k-1)a^{n}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}(-\bar{z}(1-|z|^{2k-4})z^{n-k+2}+(1+|z|^{2})(1-|z|^{2k-2})z^{n-k+1})

\displaystyle=(1-|z|^{2k})z^{n-k}.

Now, we consider the case

in (

), we get

\displaystyle(n+1)a^{n}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}(-(1-|z|^{2})+(n-1)a^{n}_{n-1}(-\bar{z})+na^{n}_{n}(1+|z|^{2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}(-(1-|z|^{2})+(1-|z|^{2n-2})(-|z|^{2})+(1-|z|^{2n})(1+|z|^{2}))

\displaystyle=\bar{z}(1-|z|^{2n}).

When

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}((k-2)a^{n}_{k-2}(-\bar{z})+(k-1)a^{n}_{k-1}(1+|z|^{2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}(-\bar{z}^{k-1-n}(1-|z|^{2n})+\bar{z}^{k-1-n}(1-|z|^{2n})(1+|z|^{2}))

Write

\displaystyle H_{z}^{-1}(x(-p))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>0;b\in\mathcal{B}}\,\langle\check{b},x\rangle\Res_{t=0}\left(\phi^{-1}\circ\hat{H}_{0}^{-1}\circ\phi(t^{n})t^{-p-1}\right)b(-n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>0}\,a^{n}_{p}(x(-n)),\,\,\,\text{where $a^{n}_{p}$ is given by the equation (\ref{eqnnew2.12.3})}

(38)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{0<n<p}\,\bar{z}^{p-n}(1-|z|^{2n})x(-n)+\frac{1}{p}(1-|z|^{2p})\sum_{n\geq p}\,z^{n-p}x(-n).

The extremality of 2 2 -partite Turán graphs with respect to the number of colorings
Melissa M Fuentes

Abstract




Abstract

We consider a problem proposed by Linial and Wilf to determine the structure of graphs that allows the maximum number of
q
-colorings among graphs with
n
vertices and
m
edges. Let
T_{r}(n)
denote the Turán graph - the complete
r
-partite graph on
n
vertices with partition sizes as equal as possible. We prove that for all odd integers
q\geq 5
and sufficiently large
n
, the Turán graph
T_{2}(n)
has at least as many
q
-colorings as any other graph
G
with the same number of vertices and edges as
T_{2}(n)
, with equality holding if and only if
G=T_{2}(n)
. Our proof builds on methods by Norine and by Loh, Pikhurko, and Sudakov, which reduces the problem to a quadratic program.

1
Introduction

For a positive integer
q
, let
[q]=\{1,2,\ldots,q\}
. A function
f:V(G)\to[q]
such that
f(x)\neq f(y)
for every edge
xy
of a graph
G
is called a
proper vertex coloring of
G
in at most
q
colors
, or simply a
q
-coloring
of
G
. The set
[q]
is often referred to as the
set of colors
.

Let
P_{G}(q)
denote the number of all
q
-colorings of a given graph
G
. This number was introduced and studied by Birkhoff
[
2
]
, who proved that it is always a polynomial in
q
, and is known as the
chromatic polynomial
of
G
. The parameter
P_{G}(q)
has been extensively studied over the past century. In particular, Linial
[
9
]
and Wilf
[
1
,
17
,
15
]
had independently posed the problem of determining the graphs that maximize the number of
q
-colorings among all graphs with
n
vertices and
m
edges. The problem was completely solved for
q=2
by Lazebnik in
[
4
]
, but remains largely open in general and has been a topic of extensive research. For a survey of numerous results on this problem, see Lazebnik
[
6
]
.

For a positive integer
r
, let
T_{r}(n)
denote the
r
-partite Turán graph
, that is, the complete
r
-partite graph of order
n
with all parts nearly equal in size (each part is of size
\lfloor n/r\rfloor
or
\lceil n/r\rceil
). Let
t_{r}(n)
denote the number of edges of
T_{r}(n)
. Our main motivation is the following conjecture made by Lazebnik in 1987, although it first appeared in print in
[
7
]
.

Conjecture 1

(
[
7
]
)
.

For all
n\geq r\geq 2
and
q\geq r
, the Turán graph
T_{r}(n)
is the only graph on
n
vertices and
t_{r}(n)
edges that attains the maximum number of
q
-colorings.

When
q=r
, the statement follows from the celebrated Turán Theorem
[
14
]
, since any graph with
n
vertices and
t_{r}(n)
edges that is not
T_{r}(n)
does not have a
r
-coloring. Lazebnik proved Conjecture
1
when
r=2
and
q\geq(n/2)^{5}
in
[
4
]
and when
n
is a positive integer divisible by
r
and
q\geq 2\binom{t_{r}(n)}{3}
in
[
5
]
. For
r=2
and
q=3
, Lazebnik, Pikhurko, and Woldar
[
7
]
proved the conjecture when
n
is even, as well as an asymptotic version when
q=4
for even
n
, as long as
n
is sufficiently large. Their result for
q=4
was extended by Tofts
[
13
]
to all
n\geq 4
. Loh, Pikhurko, and Sudakov proved the conjecture for
q=r+1
for large
n
in
[
10
]
, and their result was later extended to all
n\geq r
by Lazebnik and Tofts in
[
8
]
. This was greatly improved by Norine
[
12
]
, who developed further powerful techniques from
[
10
]
, and showed that for any positive integers
q
and
r
such that
2\leq r<q
and
r
divides
q
, Conjecture
1
is true, provided that
n
is sufficiently large. The most recent result was by Ma and Naves
[
11
]
, who showed that Conjecture
1
is true for all
q\geq 100r^{2}/(\log(r))
, for
n
is sufficiently large.

Conjecture
1
was widely believed to be true, especially since there are many results confirming it in several cases. However, Ma and Naves
[
11
]
constructed counterexamples in some ranges of
r
and
q
. For example, if
r+3\leq q\leq 2r-7
and
r\geq 10
then Conjecture
1
is false. Also, for all integers
r\geq 50000
and
q_{0}
such that
20r\leq q_{0}\leq\frac{r^{2}}{200\log(r)}
, there exists an integer
q
within distance at most
r
from
q_{0}
, such that Conjecture
1
is false for
r
and
q
. Nevertheless, Conjecture
1
is still believed to be true for integers
r
and
q
, where
2\leq r\leq 9
and
q\geq r
. The first case for which there are no explicit or asymptotic results is for
r=2
and odd
q\geq 5
. This motivated the research done in this paper. The main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
q\geq 5
be an odd integer. Then for all sufficiently large
n
, the Turán graph
T_{2}(n)
has more
q
-colorings than any other graph with the same number of vertices and edges.

2
The linear optimization problem and associated graph constructions

In their breakthrough paper, Loh, Pikhurko, and Sudakov
[
10
]
developed the optimization problem
OPT
for future researchers to use to determine the graphs that maximize the number of
q
-colorings among all graphs with the same numbers of vertices and edges. They remark that “the remaining challenge is to find analytic arguments which solve the optimization problem for general
q
.” We will solve particular cases of the optimization problem in Section
4
.

2.1
The linear optimization problem by Loh, Pikhurko, and Sudakov

It is shown in
[
10
]
that solving the problem proposed by Linial
[
9
]
and Wilf
[
1
,
17
,
15
]
for large
n
reduces to a quadratically constrained linear program, which we now define.

Let
\mathbb{R}
denote the set of real numbers. Let
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{A})_{A\subseteq[q],A\neq\emptyset}
be a vector with
2^{q}-1
components
\alpha_{A}\in\mathbb{R}
that are indexed by the nonempty subsets
A\subseteq[q]
. When all components of
\bm{\alpha}
are nonnegative we will write
\bm{\alpha}\geq 0
. The logarithms below and in the rest of the paper are natural.

Fix an integer
q\geq 2
and a real number
\gamma
that satisfies
0<\gamma\leq(q-1)/(2q)
. Consider the following functions of
\bm{\alpha}
:

\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\alpha}):=\sum_{A\neq\emptyset}\alpha_{A}\log|A|;\quad\V_{q}(\bm{\alpha}):=\sum_{A\neq\emptyset}\alpha_{A};\quad\E_{q}(\bm{\alpha}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{A,B\}:A\cap B=\emptyset\\
A\neq\emptyset,B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\alpha_{A}\alpha_{B}.

The sums in
\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
and
V_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
run over all nonempty subsets of
[q]
, and the sum in
\E_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
runs over all
unordered
pairs of disjoint nonempty subsets of
[q]
. In the remainder of this paper, we will suppress mentioning that the sets over which the sums above are taken are nonempty.

Let
\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
be defined by

\FEAS_{q}(\gamma):=\{\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{q}-1}:\bm{\alpha}\geq 0,\,\V_{q}(\bm{\alpha})=1,\,\text{and}\,\E_{q}(\bm{\alpha})\geq\gamma\}.

The elements of
\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
will be referred to as
feasible vectors
. Our goal is to maximize
\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
over
\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
.

Optimization Problem (OPT)
.

Find

\OPT_{q}(\gamma):=\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\alpha}\in\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)}\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\alpha}).

As noted in
[
10
]
, a solution of
OPT
exists by continuity of
\OPT_{q}(\gamma)
and by compactness of the set
\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
. We say that
\bm{\alpha}

solves

\OPT_{q}(\gamma)
(or just
OPT
) if
\bm{\alpha}\in\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
and
\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\alpha})=\OPT_{q}(\gamma)
.

Our objective in solving
OPT
is to obtain the approximate structure of a graph on
n
vertices and at least
\gamma n^{2}
edges that has the most number of
q
-colorings, provided that
n
is sufficiently large. We define such a graph in the next section.

Loh et al.
[
10
]
had solved
OPT
in for all
q\geq 3
when
\gamma
satisfies
0\leq\gamma\leq\kappa_{q}
, where

\kappa_{q}:=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(q/(q-1))}{\log(q)}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(q)}{\log(q/(q-1))}}\right)^{-2}\approx\frac{1}{q\log(q)}.

Norine
[
12
]
presented an argument that solves
OPT
when
\gamma=(r-1)/(2r)
, where
r
is the number of parts in the Turán graph,
T_{r}(n)
, and
q
is divisible by
r
. In particular, Norine completely solved
OPT
for
\gamma=1/4
(that is,
r=2
) and all even integers
q\geq 2
. In Section
4
we extend his solution to all
\gamma
within a closed interval of real numbers
[1/4-\epsilon,1/4]
when
\epsilon>0
is sufficiently small and
q\geq 5
.

2.2
Graph constructions based on feasible vectors

The following is a graph construction from
[
10
]
that is based on an arbitrary feasible vector
\bm{\alpha}
. Note that this construction may not result in a unique graph.

Construction (
\bm{G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)}
)
.

Let
\gamma
be a real number,
n\geq 1
, and
\bm{\alpha}\in\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
. The
n
-vertex graph
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
is constructed by partitioning the
n
vertices into disjoint sets
V_{A}
, indexed by the non-empty subsets
A\subseteq[q]
, such that each
|V_{A}|
differs from
n\alpha_{A}
by less than
1
. For every pair of non-empty
A,B\subseteq[q]
with
A\cap B=\emptyset
join every vertex in
V_{A}
to every vertex of
V_{B}
by an edge.

If all
n\alpha_{A}
happened to be integers, the graph
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
would be unique and
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
would have precisely
\E_{q}(\bm{\alpha})n^{2}
edges and at least

\prod_{A\neq\emptyset}|A|^{n\alpha_{A}}=e^{\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\alpha})n}

q
-colorings, since any coloring in which the vertices in
V_{A}
are only colored with colors in
A
results in a
q
-coloring of
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
. However, if this is not the case, the choice in size of each
V_{A}
may result in a graph with fewer than
\gamma n^{2}
edges (recall that
\E_{q}(\bm{\alpha})n^{2}\geq\gamma n^{2}
). Fortunately, we may use the Proposition
2
below in order to obtain another graph on
n
vertices which is “close” (to be defined next) to
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
, and which has at least
\gamma n^{2}
edges.

Given graphs
G
and
H
with the same set of vertices, their
edit distance
is the minimum number of edges that need to be added or deleted from one graph to obtain a graph isomorphic to the other.

Proposition 2

(
[
10
]
)
.

For any feasible vector
\bm{\alpha}
, the number of edges in any graph
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
differs from
\E_{q}(\bm{\alpha})n^{2}
by less than
2^{q}n
. Also, for any other feasible vector
\bm{\nu}
, the edit distance between
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
and
G_{\bm{\nu}}(n)
is at most
\left\lVert\bm{\alpha}-\bm{\nu}\right\rVert_{1}n^{2}+2^{q+1}n
, where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1}
is the
L^{1}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2^{q}-1}
.

Proposition
2
and Theorem
3
(shown below) will be important in the proof of our main result, Theorem
1
, in Section
6
.

Theorem 3

(
[
10
]
)
.

For any
\delta,\kappa>0
, the following holds for all sufficiently large
n
. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
m
edges, where
m\leq\kappa n^{2}
, which has at least as many
q
-colorings as any other graph with the same number of vertices and edges. Then
G
is
\epsilon n^{2}
-close to a graph
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
for some feasible vector
\bm{\alpha}
which solves
\OPT_{q}(\gamma)
for some
\gamma
, where
|\gamma-m/n^{2}|<\epsilon
and
\gamma\leq\kappa
.

The
support
of a feasible vector
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{A})_{A\subseteq[q],A\neq\emptyset}
, denoted by
\supp_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
, is the collection of sets
A\subseteq[q]
such that
\alpha_{A}>0
. The graph construction below is from
[
11
]
.

Construction (
\bm{\text{SUPP}_{q}(\bm{\alpha})}
)
.

Let
\bm{\alpha}\in\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
. The set of vertices of the graph
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
is
\supp_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
and the edge set is formed by connecting pairs of disjoint sets.

The graph
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
is called the
support graph
of a feasible vector
\bm{\alpha}
. We define two classes of graphs
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
as follows. Let
\mathcal{P}_{k}
be the class of all graphs
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
for which
\supp_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
forms a
k
-partition
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}
of
[q]
, with only nonempty sets. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
be the class of all graphs
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
for which
\supp_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
consists of a
k
-partition
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}
of
[q]
, with only nonempty sets, together with the set
A_{1}\cup A_{2}
.

Fix a positive integer
k
and let
\bm{\alpha}
be a vector such that the graph
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
is in
\mathcal{P}_{k}
or
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
. Consider a restricted version of
OPT
for such vectors
\bm{\alpha}
:

Optimization Problem (OPT 2)
.

Maximize

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{A_{i}}\log(|A_{i}|)+\alpha_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}\log(|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|),

subject to

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{A_{i}}+\alpha_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}=1,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{A_{i}}^{2}+\alpha_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}^{2}+2\alpha_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}(\alpha_{A_{1}}+\alpha_{A_{2}})\leq 1-2\gamma,

and

\alpha_{A_{i}}\geq 0,\alpha_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}\geq 0,\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,k.

The conditions of
OPT 2
are consistent with the conditions of
OPT
, when restricted to vectors with support in
\mathcal{Q}_{k}\cup\mathcal{P}_{k}
. The following observation was made in (
[
11
]
) using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Observation 1
.

We have
k\geq 1/\lceil 1-2\gamma\rceil
in
OPT 2
, with a strict inequality holding if
\alpha_{A_{1}}+\alpha_{A_{2}}>0
and
\alpha_{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}>0
.

Define

\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{\lceil\frac{1}{1-2\gamma}\rceil\leq k\leq q}\mathcal{P}_{k}.

The next theorem determines the structure of the graph
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
when
\bm{\alpha}
solves
OPT
.

Theorem 4

(
[
11
]
)
.

For an integer
q
and real
\gamma
that satisfy
0<\gamma\leq(q-1)/(2q)
, all solutions to
OPT
are such that
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})
is in either
\mathcal{P}
or
\mathcal{Q}_{\lceil 1/(1-2\gamma)\rceil}
. When
\gamma<(q-1)/(2q)
, we have
\SUPP_{q}(\bm{\alpha})\notin\mathcal{P}_{q}
.

3
Relevant solutions of
OPT

In this section we use new methods to provide an analytic solution to
OPT
for odd
q\geq 5
for all
\gamma
sufficiently close to
1/4
. Although a computer solution can be found for fixed
\gamma
, an analytic solution is necessary to solve
OPT
for all
\gamma
within a closed interval of real numbers
[1/4-\epsilon,1/4]
for small
\epsilon>0
.

The main result of this section is the following theorem. For any set
S
, we denote its
complement
by
S^{c}
.

Theorem 5
.

The following holds for all
\gamma
sufficiently close to
1/4
. Any solution
\bm{\alpha}
of
OPT
for odd
q\geq 5
has
\supp_{q}(\bm{\alpha})=\{A,A^{c}\},
where
|A|=\lceil q/2\rceil
, and

\alpha_{A}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\gamma}}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{A^{c}}=1-\alpha_{A},

which gives

\OPT_{q}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\lceil q/2\rceil\cdot\lfloor q/2\rfloor\right)+\frac{\sqrt{1-4\gamma}}{2}\log\left(\frac{\lceil q/2\rceil}{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}\right).

Note that the vector
\bm{\alpha}
defined in Theorem
5
is a feasible vector. We will use a constraint that is equivalent to
\E_{q}(\bm{\beta})\geq\gamma
for all
\bm{\beta}\in\FEAS(\gamma)
, specifically,
1-2\E_{q}(\bm{\beta})\leq 1-2\gamma
. In addition, note that

1-2\E_{q}(\bm{\beta})=\sum_{(B,S):B\cap S\neq\emptyset}\beta_{B}\beta_{S}=\sum_{S}P_{S}(q)\beta_{S},

(1)

where

P_{S}(q):=\sum_{B:B\cap S\neq\emptyset}\beta_{B}.

Therefore,

\sum_{S}P_{S}(q)\beta_{S}=1-2\E_{q}(\bm{\beta})\leq 1-2\gamma.

(2)

Recall that
q
is the number of colors. We continue with two lemmas which hold for all integers
q\geq 2
that will be used in the proof of Theorem
5
. For
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{S})_{S\subseteq[q],S\neq\emptyset}\in\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
and every
S\in\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})
, let

Q_{S}(q):=\frac{|S|}{q}.

Lemma
1
below is a technical result to be used in the proof of Lemma
2
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\gamma
satisfy
0<\gamma\leq 1/4
and let
\bm{\beta}\in\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
. Then

\sum_{S}\left(2\sqrt{2Q_{S}(q)}-(3-4\gamma)\right)\beta_{S}\leq 0.

(3)

Proof.

By a comparison of the geometric and arithmetic mean of two numbers (AMGM) we obtain

2\sqrt{2Q_{S}(q)}\leq(2-4\gamma)\frac{Q_{S}(q)}{P_{S}(q)}+\frac{1}{1-2\gamma}P_{S}(q).

(4)

Thus,

\sum_{S}\left(2\sqrt{2Q_{S}(q)}-(3-4\gamma)\right)\beta_{S}\leq\sum_{S}\left((2-4\gamma)\frac{Q_{S}(q)}{P_{S}(q)}+\frac{1}{1-2\gamma}P_{S}(q)-(3-4\gamma)\right)\beta_{S}.

(5)

Now we show that

\sum_{S}\left((2-4\gamma)\frac{Q_{S}(q)}{P_{S}(q)}+\frac{1}{1-2\gamma}P_{S}(q)-(3-4\gamma)\right)\beta_{S}\leq 0.

(6)

Let us prove that

\sum_{S}\frac{Q_{S}(q)}{P_{S}(q)}\beta_{S}\leq 1.

(7)

Let
\emptyset\neq S\subseteq[q]
, and
\chi_{S}:[q]\to\left\{0,1\right\}
be defined by

\chi_{S}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\in S\\
0&\text{ if }x\notin S.\end{cases}

Take any
x\in[q]
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{S}\frac{\chi_{S}(x)}{P_{S}(q)}\beta_{S}=\sum_{S:x\in S}\frac{1}{P_{S}(q)}\beta_{S}=\sum_{S:x\in S}\left(\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sum_{B:B\cap S\neq\emptyset}\beta_{B}}\right)\beta_{S}\leq\sum_{S:x\in S}\left(\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sum_{B:x\in B}\beta_{B}}\right)\beta_{S}=1,

(8)

where for
x\in S
, the last inequality in (
8
) is due to the fact that
x\in B
implies
B\cap S\neq\emptyset
.

Note that
\sum_{x\in[q]}\chi_{S}(x)=\left|S\right|
. Then, using (
8
), we have

q=\sum_{x\in[q]}1\geq\sum_{x\in[q]}\sum_{S}\frac{\chi_{S}(x)}{P_{S}(q)}\beta_{S}=\sum_{S}\frac{\sum_{x\in[q]}\chi_{S}(x)}{P_{S}(q)}\beta_{S}=\sum_{S}\frac{|S|}{P_{S}(q)}\beta_{S}=q\sum_{S}\frac{Q_{S}(q)}{P_{S}(q)}\beta_{S},

which implies (
7
).

Using (
7
), (
2
), and
\V_{q}(\bm{\beta})=1
, we obtain (
6
). Therefore, (
5
) and (
6
) imply that (
3
) holds. The proof is complete.
∎

Next we show that the sum of the components corresponding to sets of size roughly
q/2
in the support of a feasible vector
\bm{\beta}
with
\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\beta})
being at least as large as the optimal value claimed in Theorem
5
carry more “weight” than the sum of components corresponding to sets of other sizes (recall that
\V_{q}(\bm{\beta})=1
and all components of feasible vectors are nonnegative).

Lemma 2
.

Let
\bm{\beta}\in\FEAS_{q}(\gamma)
, where

also implies that
A=B\cup C
and that

\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})=\{A,B,C,(B\cup C)^{c}\}.

However, then we would have
(q+1)/2=|A|=|B|+|C|=q-1,
which is impossible. Therefore, this entire case is impossible.

Case 4:

The support of
\bm{\beta}
contains exactly one set
A
of size
\lceil q/2\rceil
and
A^{c}
, and
|\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})|>2
.

Since
A,A^{c}\in\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})
and
|\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})|>2
, then any other set in
\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})
must intersect
A
or
A^{c}
. Recall, by Observation
1
, we have

|\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})|>\displaystyle\lceil 1/(1-2\gamma)\rceil+1=3,

since
\lceil 1/(1-2\gamma)\rceil=2
for
\gamma
sufficiently close to
1/4
. One set in
\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})
must be the union of precisely two other set in
\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})
by Theorem
4
. Thus, the only possibility is that

\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})=\{A,A^{c},A_{1},A_{2}\},

where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are disjoint sets which form a
2
-partition of
A
or
A^{c}
.

Without loss of generality, assume that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
form a
2
-partition of
A
. Then the entries of
\bm{\beta}
are subject to the following constraints

\beta_{A}+\beta_{A^{c}}+\beta_{A_{1}}+\beta_{A_{2}}=1,

(24)

\beta_{A}^{2}+\beta_{A^{c}}^{2}+\beta_{A_{1}}^{2}+\beta_{A_{2}}^{2}+2\beta_{A}(\beta_{A_{1}}+\beta_{A_{2}})\leq 1-2\gamma,

(25)

\beta_{A}+\beta_{A^{c}}\geq\psi(q),

(26)

where
\beta_{A}>0
,
\beta_{A^{c}}>0
,
\beta_{A_{1}}>0
, and
\beta_{A_{2}}>0
. We can combine (
25
) and (
25
) by substituting
\beta_{A^{c}}=1-\beta_{A}-\beta_{A_{1}}-\beta_{A_{2}}
into (
25
) to obtain

\displaystyle 1-2\gamma

\displaystyle\geq\beta_{A}^{2}+\beta_{A^{c}}^{2}+\beta_{A_{1}}^{2}+\beta_{A_{2}}^{2}+2\beta_{A}(\beta_{A_{1}}+\beta_{A_{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(2\beta_{A}+2\beta_{A_{1}}+2\beta_{A_{2}}-1)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}-\beta_{A_{1}}^{2}-\beta_{A_{2}}^{2}-2\beta_{A_{1}}\beta_{A_{2}}.

(27)

By solving for
\beta_{A}
in (
3
) we obtain

\beta_{A}\leq\frac{\sqrt{1-4\gamma+2(\beta_{A_{1}}^{2}+\beta_{A_{2}}^{2}+2\beta_{A_{1}}\beta_{A_{2}})}+1}{2}-(\beta_{A_{1}}+\beta_{A_{2}}).

Let
t=\beta_{A_{1}}+\beta_{A_{2}}
. Then
0<t\leq 1-\psi(q)
by (
24
) and (
26
). Note that

\beta_{A_{1}}^{2}+\beta_{A_{2}}^{2}+2\beta_{A_{1}}\beta_{A_{2}}\leq t^{2},

and hence, we have

\beta_{A}\leq\frac{\sqrt{1-4\gamma+2t^{2}}+1}{2}-t.

Thus,

\displaystyle\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\beta})

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(\lfloor q/2\rfloor\right)(\beta_{A^{c}}+\beta_{A_{1}}+\beta_{A_{2}})+\log\left(\lceil q/2\rceil\right)\beta_{A}

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\lceil q/2\rceil}{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}\right)\beta_{A},\quad\text{ by (\ref{case4cons1})}

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\lceil q/2\rceil}{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{1-4\gamma+2t^{2}}+1}{2}-t\right)=:\rho(t,q,\gamma).

We will maximize
\rho(t,q,\gamma)
with respect to
t
. Note that
\sqrt{1-4\gamma+2t^{2}}\geq 0
for
\gamma\leq 1/4
, and hence,

\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\frac{t}{\sqrt{1-4\gamma+2t^{2}}}+1<0

for
t>\sqrt{4\gamma-1}
.
Since
4\gamma-1\leq 0<t
, then
\rho(t,q,\gamma)
is always decreasing with respect to
t
on
[0,1-\psi(q)]
. Thus,

\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\beta})<\rho(0,q,\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\lceil q/2\rceil\cdot\lfloor q/2\rfloor\right)+\frac{\sqrt{1-4\gamma}}{2}\log\left(\frac{\lceil q/2\rceil}{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}\right)=\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\alpha}),

which contradicts (
17
). Therefore, this case is impossible.

Case 5:

The support of
\bm{\beta}
contains exactly one set
A
of size
\lceil q/2\rceil
and
A^{c}
only.

For any
\gamma\leq 1/4
, define the roots of the equation
x(1-x)=\gamma
by

M(\gamma)^{+}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\gamma}}{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad M(\gamma)^{-}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\gamma}}{2}.

In this case, we have
\supp_{q}(\bm{\beta})=\{A,A^{c}\}
. Then by (
19
), we have

\beta_{A}+\beta_{A^{c}}=1,

and we also have

\E_{q}(\bm{\beta})=\beta_{A}\beta_{A^{c}}=\beta_{A}(1-\beta_{A})\geq\gamma,

which implies that
M(\gamma)^{-}\leq\beta_{A}\leq M(\gamma)^{+}
. Similarly, we can also show that
M(\gamma)^{-}\leq\beta_{A^{c}}\leq M(\gamma)^{+}
.
Therefore, it is clear that in this case, we have

\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\beta})\leq\log\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)M(\gamma)^{-}+\log\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)M(\gamma)^{+}=\OBJ_{q}(\bm{\alpha}),

where equality holds everywhere if and only if
\beta_{A^{c}}=M(\gamma)^{-}
and
\beta_{A}=M(\gamma)^{+}
. That is, equality holds if and only if
\bm{\beta}
is of the same form as
\bm{\alpha}
in the statement of Theorem
5
.

We have considered all possible cases for the solution vector
\bm{\beta}
and have shown that the only possibility is that
\bm{\beta}
must be of the same form as the vector
\bm{\alpha}
defined in the statement of Theorem
5
. Our proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. If
\bm{\alpha}
solves
\OPT_{q}(1/4)
for
q\geq 5
, then the graph
G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
is isomorphic to
T_{2}(n)
.

Proof.

If
\bm{\alpha}
is a solution of
\OPT_{q}(1/4)
then according to Theorem
5
,
\supp_{q}(\bm{\alpha})=\left\{A,A^{c}\right\}
, where
A\subseteq[q]
such that
\left|A\right|=\lceil q/2\rceil
, and
\alpha_{A}=\alpha_{A^{c}}=1/2
. Then by Construction
\bm{G_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)}
, the graph

is a complete

-partite graph with parts

and

, where
\left|V_{A}\right|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
and
\left|V_{A^{c}}\right|=\lceil n/2\rceil
, or vice versa. Therefore,

, which is isomorphic to

.
∎

4
Approximate Version of Theorem 1

This section is dedicated to proving an “approximate” version of Theorem
1
. This version is nearly the same as Theorem
1
, but has an additional requirement: namely, that a
(n,t_{2}(n))
-graph
G
must be
\delta n^{2}
-close to
T_{2}(n)
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. That is,
T_{2}(n)
“locally maximizes” the number of
q
-colorings among the class of
(n,t_{2}(n))
-graphs for odd
q\geq 5
.

The main result of this section is as follows.

Theorem 6
.

There exists a
\delta>0
such that the following holds for sufficiently large
n
. Let
q\geq 2
be an odd integer and let
G
be a
(n,t_{2}(n))
-graph such that
G
is
\delta n^{2}
-close to
T_{2}(n)
. Then
G
has at most as many
q
-colorings as
T_{2}(n)
, with equality holding if and only if
G
is isomorphic to
T_{2}(n)
.

Intuitively, Theorem
6
states that if a graph is “close”, with respect to edit distance, in structure to
T_{2}(n)
then the number of its
q
-colorings is at most
P_{T_{2}(n)}(q)
for odd
q\geq 5
.

The following four results will be referenced throughout the proof of Theorem
6
. The first result, Lemma
3
, states the existence of a particular partition of the vertex set of a
(n,t_{2}(n))
-graph that is
\delta n^{2}
-close

.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\delta>0
. If
G
is a
(n,t_{2}(n))
-graph that is
\delta n^{2}
-close to
T_{2}(n)
then there exists a partition
A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\cup$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\cup$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\cup$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cup$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}}A_{2}=V(G)
such that
e(G[A_{1},A_{2}])\geq t_{2}(n)-\frac{\delta}{2}n^{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
T_{2}(n)
has vertex set partition
A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\cup$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\cup$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\cup$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cup$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}}A_{2}=V(T_{2}(n))
. Since
G
is
\delta n^{2}
-close to
T_{2}(n)
, then by definition,
V(G)=V(T_{2}(n))
. We claim that

e(G[A_{1},A_{2}])\geq t_{2}(n)-\frac{\delta}{2}n^{2}.

First observe that

E(G[A_{1},A_{2}])=E(G)\cap E(T_{2}(n))=(E(G)\cup E(T_{2}(n)))\setminus(E(G)\triangle E(T_{2}(n))),

(28)

where the first equality holds since
T_{2}(n)
has all possible edges between the parts
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. By taking the cardinalities of each set in (
28
) we have

e(G[A_{1},A_{2}])\geq 2t_{2}(n)-e(G[A_{1},A_{2}])-\delta n^{2}.

Thus, we have our desired result.
∎

We will need the following upper bound on
P_{G}(q)
from
[

]
later.

Lemma 4

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let

be an

in his proof of Lemma 4.2, with some adjustments, since

is not an even integer.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Throughout the proof we will be making a series of claims which hold for positive
\delta
, sufficiently small as a function of
q
, and for
n
, sufficiently large as a function of
q
and
\delta
. The eventual choice of
\delta
and

will be implicitly made so that all of our claims are valid.

Let
G
be a
(n,t_{2}(n))
-graph, where

. Suppose, to the contrary, that

has more

-colorings than

, there exists a partition
A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\cup$\cr$\displaystyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\cup$\cr$\textstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\cup$\cr$\scriptstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cup$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\crcr}}}}A_{2}=V(G)

Then for sufficiently small
\delta

\delta

Let

\epsilon
(and hence,

).

Let

be a

\mathcal{R}_{f}(i):=\left\{c\in[q]:\left|f^{-1}(c)\cap A_{i}\right|>\epsilon\left|A_{i}\right|\right\},

\mathcal{R}_{f}(i)

We make two observations about

Let us define the vector

\mathcal{R}_{2}

Case 1:
The components of
\bm{\mathcal{R}}=(\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2})

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{G}(\bm{\mathcal{R}})

\displaystyle\leq q^{\left|B\right|}\left(\lfloor q/2\rfloor\lceil q/2\rceil\right)^{(1+\delta)\frac{n}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left(\frac{\lceil q/2\rceil-1}{\lceil q/2\rceil}\right)^{\sqrt{e_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\lceil q/2\rceil\cdot\lfloor q/2\rfloor\right)^{(1+\delta)\frac{n}{2}}\exp\left(\log(q)\left|B\right|-\log\left(\frac{\lceil q/2\rceil}{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}\right)\sqrt{\delta^{2/5}\left|B\right|n}+\log\left(\lceil q/2\rceil\cdot\lfloor q/2\rfloor\right)\frac{\delta}{2}n\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lceil q/2\rceil\cdot\lfloor q/2\rfloor\right)^{n/2}\exp\left(\left(\log(q)-10^{-1/2}\delta^{-1/20}\log\left(\frac{\lceil q/2\rceil}{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}\right)\right)\left|B\right|+\log\left(\lceil q/2\rceil\cdot\lfloor q/2\rfloor\right)\frac{\delta}{2}n\right).

If
\left|B\right|\neq 0
then
\mathcal{P}_{G}(\bm{\mathcal{R}})
once again becomes negligible compared to
\left(\lceil q/2\rceil\cdot\lfloor q/2\rfloor\right)^{n/2}
, as
\delta
approaches
0
. It follows that
\left|B\right|=0
. It suffices to assume that
G[A_{1},A_{2}]
is a complete bipartite graph. Indeed, if this were not the case, then there exist nonadjacent vertices
v_{1}\in A_{1}
and
v_{2}\in A_{2}
and adjacent vertices
x
and
y
, either both in
A_{1}
or both in
A_{2}
. Let the graph
G^{\prime}:=G-xy+v_{1}v_{2}
. If
f
is a
q
-coloring in Case 2, then
f(x)\neq f(y)
and
f(v_{1})\neq f(v_{2})
. Thus, we can apply the coloring
f
to the graph

to obtain a proper
q
-coloring of

that satisfies the conditions of Case 2. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{G}(\bm{\mathcal{R}})\leq\mathcal{P}_{G^{\prime}}(\bm{\mathcal{R}})
. We may repeat this process until all possible edges between
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are present. Then

must be isomorphic to

. Therefore, we have shown that any

-graph

close to

has at most as many
q
-colorings as

, with equality holding if and only if

∎

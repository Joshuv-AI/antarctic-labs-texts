The following infinite products provide useful limiting bounds:

\omega(\infty,q)=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{-i})\quad\textup{and}\quad\omega(\infty,-q)=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-(-q)^{-j})=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{-(2i-1)})(1-q^{-2i}).

.

\displaystyle 1-q^{-1}-q^{-2}+q^{-5}<\omega(\infty,q)<\cdots<\omega(2,q)<\omega(1,q)<\omega(0,q)=1\quad\textup{and,}

\displaystyle 1=\omega(0,-q)<\omega(2,-q)<\cdots<\omega(\infty,-q)<\cdots<\omega(3,-q)<\omega(1,-q)=1+q^{-1}.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\omega(n,q)=\omega(\infty,q)\quad\textup{and}\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}\omega(2m,-q)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\omega(2m+1,-q)=\omega(\infty,-q).

Lemma 5.4

Lemma 5.5
.

Let

W_{\tau}\in{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{e+e'}}^{\varepsilon}_{\tau}
for
\tau\in\{-,+\}
. If
q\geqslant 3
, then
it follows from Theorem
6.6
and Proposition
2.2
(b) that

\rho({\mathbf{O}}^{\varepsilon},V,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})\geqslant\left(1-\frac{43}{16q}\right)\sum_{\tau\in\{+,-\}}\frac{|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{e+e'}}}^{\varepsilon}_{\tau}|\cdot|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{W_\tau}{e}}}^{\tau}_{\sigma}|\cdot|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{W_\tau}{e'}}}^{\tau}_{\sigma^{\prime}}|}{|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{e}}}^{\varepsilon}_{\sigma}|\cdot|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{e'}}}^{\varepsilon}_{\sigma^{\prime}}|}.

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
6.3
we
temporarily set
\omega(n)=\omega(\lfloor n/2\rfloor,q^{2})
.
By Proposition
4.1
(c), for
e
even,
the number of
e
-subspaces of
V
of type
\sigma
equals

(11)

\displaystyle\left|{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{e}}^{\varepsilon}_{\sigma}\right|

\displaystyle=q^{e(d-e)}\kappa(e,d-e,\varepsilon,\sigma)\frac{\omega(d)}{\omega(e)\omega(d-e)}\quad\textup{where}

(12)

\displaystyle\kappa(e,d-e,\varepsilon,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\sigma q^{-e/2})(1+\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\lfloor d/2\rfloor+e/2})}{2(1+\varepsilon q^{-\lfloor d/2\rfloor})}.

Using the formula (
11
) for the other factors in the product
above, we obtain a complicated expression for

\frac{|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{e+e'}}}^{\varepsilon}_{\tau}|\cdot|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{W_\tau}{e}}}^{\tau}_{\sigma}|\cdot|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{W_\tau}{e'}}}^{\tau}_{\sigma^{\prime}}|}{|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{e}}}^{\varepsilon}_{\sigma}|\cdot|{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{e'}}}^{\varepsilon}_{\sigma^{\prime}}|}
which admits two simplifications.
First, the powers of
q
all cancel, and second the following factor which
is independent of
\tau\in\{+,-\}
can be estimated:

\frac{\omega(e+e^{\prime})}{\omega(e)\omega(e^{\prime})}\geqslant 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\omega(d-e)\omega(d-e^{\prime})}{\omega(d-e-e^{\prime})\omega(d)}>1-\frac{1}{2q^{2}\log(q)}

where the first inequality follows from Lemma
5.2
,
and the second follows from applying Lemma
5.5
with
q
replaced by
q^{2}
(as in the last line of the proof of Lemma
6.3
).
Using the above lower bound gives
the following (strict) lower bound for
\rho({\mathbf{O}}^{\varepsilon},V,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
:

\left(1-\frac{43}{16q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{2q^{2}\log(q)}\right)\sum_{\tau\in\{+,-\}}\frac{\kappa(e+e^{\prime},d-e-e^{\prime},\varepsilon,\tau)\kappa(e,e^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\kappa(e^{\prime},e,\tau,\sigma^{\prime})}{\kappa(e,d-e,\varepsilon,\sigma)\kappa(e^{\prime},d-e^{\prime},\varepsilon,\sigma^{\prime})}.

The following technical lemma helps us bound the above sum.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\delta\neq\pm 1.
Then

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\in\{-,+\}}\frac{(1-\tau\alpha)(1-\tau\beta)(1-\tau\gamma)}{1+\tau\delta}=\frac{1+\alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma+\alpha\delta+\beta\gamma+\beta\delta+\gamma\delta+\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}{1-\delta^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be the stated sum. Adding fractions and cancelling “odd”
terms gives

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\alpha)(1+\beta)(1+\gamma)(1+\delta)+(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)(1-\gamma)(1-\delta)}{2(1-\delta)(1+\delta)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma+\alpha\delta+\beta\gamma+\beta\delta+\gamma\delta+\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}{1-\delta^{2}}.\qed

Lemma 6.10
.

Suppose that
d\geqslant e+e^{\prime}+1
where
e,e^{\prime}
are both even and
d
may be odd.
Suppose further that the types

\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\varepsilon
satisfy
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\{-,+\}
and

\varepsilon\in\{-,\circ,+\}
. Then

K\coloneq\sum_{\tau\in\{-,+\}}\frac{\kappa(e+e^{\prime},d-e-e^{\prime},\varepsilon,\tau)\kappa(e,e^{\prime},\tau,\sigma)\kappa(e^{\prime},e,\tau,\sigma^{\prime})}{\kappa(e,d-e,\varepsilon,\sigma)\kappa(e^{\prime},d-e^{\prime},\varepsilon,\sigma^{\prime})}

satisfies

K\geqslant\frac{(1-q^{-3})(1-2q^{-2}-3q^{-3}-q^{-5})}{(1+q^{-2})^{2}}
and hence

K\geqslant 1-\frac{9}{2q^{2}}
for
q\geqslant 3
.

Proof.

Let
K
be as in the statement.
Using the definition (
12
) of
\kappa
and
cancelling (8 of the 15 factors) gives

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\in\{-,+\}}\frac{(1+\varepsilon\tau q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\tau\sigma q^{-\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\tau\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\frac{e}{2}})(1+\varepsilon q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor})}{(1+\tau q^{-\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\varepsilon q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}}{(1+\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})}\cdot\Lambda,

where

(13)

\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\in\{-,+\}}\frac{(1+\varepsilon\tau q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\tau\sigma q^{-\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\tau\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\frac{e}{2}})}{1+\tau q^{-\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}}}.

Suppose that
d\geqslant e+e^{\prime}+2
.
This implies
\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor>\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}\geqslant 2
,
whence
-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e}{2}<-\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}\leqslant-1
,
and so
-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e}{2}\leqslant-2
;
and similarly
-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}\leqslant-2
.
Using Lemma
6.9
gives

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1{\kern-0.05pt+\kern-0.05pt}\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e}{2}}{\kern-0.05pt+\kern-0.05pt}\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}}{\kern-0.05pt-\kern-0.05pt}\varepsilon q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}{\kern-0.05pt+\kern-0.05pt}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}}{\kern-0.05pt-\kern-0.05pt}\sigma q^{-\frac{e}{2}-e^{\prime}}{\kern-0.05pt-\kern-0.05pt}\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}-e}{\kern-0.05pt-\kern-0.05pt}\varepsilon\sigma\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor-\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}}}{1-q^{-e-e^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-q^{-2}-q^{-2}-q^{-3}-q^{-2}-q^{-3}-q^{-3}-q^{-5}

\displaystyle=1-3q^{-2}-3q^{-3}-q^{-5}.

Upon closer inspection,
\varepsilon\sigma
,
\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}
and

\sigma\sigma^{\prime}
cannot all be
-1
, so that the sharper bound

\Lambda\geqslant 1-2q^{-2}-3q^{-3}-q^{-5}
holds.

Inserting this lower bound for
\Lambda
into the expression for
K

we obtain

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1+\varepsilon q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}}{(1+\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})}\cdot\frac{1-2q^{-2}-3q^{-3}-q^{-5}}{1}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{(1-q^{-3})(1-2q^{-2}-3q^{-3}-q^{-5})}{(1+q^{-2})^{2}}.

This proves the first bound when
d\geqslant e+e^{\prime}+2
.
To prove that this lower bound is at least
1-\frac{9}{2q^{2}}

for all
q\geqslant 3
, we rearrange this inequality and show that it is equivalent to
proving that the following is true for all
q\geqslant 3
:

\displaystyle\frac{9}{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{q^{2}((1+q^{-2})^{2}-(1-q^{-3})(1-2q^{-2}-3q^{-3}-q^{-5}))}{(1+q^{-2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4+4q^{-1}+q^{-2}-q^{-3}-3q^{-4}-q^{-6}}{(1+q^{-2})^{2}}.

As
q\to\infty
, the the right side
\to 4
. Indeed the values of
the right side lie between
4
and
\frac{9}{2}
for all
q\geqslant 3
. This proves
the second bound when
d\geqslant e+e^{\prime}+2
.

Now we consider the remaining case where
d=e+e^{\prime}+1
. Here
d
is odd so
\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor=\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}
.
If
\tau=-\varepsilon
, then one summand in the expression for
\Lambda

in (
13
) is zero because the factor

1+\varepsilon\tau q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}}
is zero.
Hence

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\varepsilon q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}}{2(1+\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})}\cdot\frac{(1+q^{-\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\frac{e}{2}})}{(1+\varepsilon q^{-\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\varepsilon q^{-\lfloor\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}\rfloor}}{2(1+\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\frac{e}{2}})}\cdot\frac{(1+q^{0})(1+\varepsilon\sigma q^{-\frac{e^{\prime}}{2}})(1+\varepsilon\sigma^{\prime}q^{-\frac{e}{2}})}{1+\varepsilon q^{-\lfloor\frac{e+e^{\prime}}{2}\rfloor}}.

Hence
K=1
and both of the stated bounds also hold in this final case.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.7
.

As discussed after Remark
6.8
, Theorem
6.7
follows from Theorem
6.6
if
d=e+e^{\prime}
, so assume that
d\geqslant e+e^{\prime}+1
.
Let

be as in Lemma

. It follows from the display
before Lemma

that

\displaystyle\rho({\mathbf{O}}^{\varepsilon},V,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})

\displaystyle>\left(1-\frac{43}{16q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{2q^{2}\log(q)}\right)K.

Setting

in the first lower bound in Lemma
6.10
shows that

\rho({\mathbf{O}}^{\varepsilon},V,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})>0.05
. Thus setting

.
Similarly, if

then again using the first lower bound

, we find that
\rho({\mathbf{O}}^{\varepsilon},V,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})>1-\frac{3.125}{q}
holds.
For

in Lemma

.
This shows

\rho({\mathbf{O}}^{\varepsilon},V,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})>\left(1-\frac{43}{16q}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{2q^{2}\log(q)}\right)\left(1-\frac{9}{2q^{2}}\right)=\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{q}\right)\left(1-\frac{\beta}{q}\right)\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{q}\right)

Hence
\rho({\mathbf{O}}^{\varepsilon},V,\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})\geqslant 1-\frac{3.125}{q}

.
∎

The order barrier for the L 1 L^{1} -approximation of the log-Heston SDE at a single point
Annalena Mickel, Andreas Neuenkirch

Abstract
We study the L 1 L^{1} -approximation of the log-Heston SDE at the terminal time point by arbitrary methods that use an equidistant discretization of the driving Brownian motion. We show that such methods can achieve at most order min ⁡ { ν , 1 2 } \min\{\nu,\tfrac{1}{2}\} , where ν \nu is the Feller index of the underlying CIR process. As a consequence Euler-type schemes are optimal for ν ≥ 1 \nu\geq 1 , since they have convergence order 1 2 − ε \tfrac{1}{2}-\varepsilon for ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 arbitrarily small in this regime. Key words: Heston model, CIR process, L 1 L^{1} -error, lower error bounds, optimal approximation, Euler-type methods 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 65C30; 60H35; 91G60

Introduction and main results

The log-Heston model is a popular stochastic volatility model in mathematical finance.
It is given by the two-dimensional stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=\big(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}V_{t}\big)dt+\sqrt{V_{t}}\Big(\rho dW_{t}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{t}\Big),\\
dV_{t}&=\kappa(\theta-V_{t})dt+\sigma\sqrt{V_{t}}dW_{t},\end{aligned}\qquad t\in[0,T].

(1)

Here
W=(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
,
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
are two independent Brownian motions,
\rho\in[-1,1]
,

\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\kappa,\theta,\sigma>0
and the initial values are assumed to be deterministic, i.e.,
X_{0}=x_{0}
and
V_{0}=v_{0}
with
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
v_{0}>0
.

The stochastic volatility process
(V_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
of the log-asset price process
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is the so-called CIR process, which takes positive values only. This process goes back to the works of Feller in the 1950s, see
[
6
]
. The quantity

\nu=\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma^{2}}

is called Feller index. Since the square-root function, which appears in the coefficients of SDE (
1
), is not globally Lipschitz continuous, SDE (
1
) does not satisfy the so-called standard assumptions. Much effort has been devoted to the numerical analysis of SDEs under non-standard assumptions in the last 25 years, in particular to the CIR process and the Heston SDE, see, e.g.,
[
10
,
7
,
20
]
for a comprehensive survey on the strong approximation of the latter SDEs.

In this note, we focus on the optimal
L^{1}
-approximation of the full log-Heston SDE (
1
) at the terminal time point
T
given an equidistant discretization of the driving Brownian motion, that is on

e(N)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|u(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}},B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{T}\\
V_{T}\\
\end{array}\right)\right\|_{1}\right],

where
\mathcal{U}(N)
is the set of measurable functions
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{2N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t_{k}=k\frac{T}{N}
for all
k\in\{0,...,N\}
.

The optimal approximation of SDEs under standard assumptions, that is for SDEs with globally Lipschitz and sufficiently smooth coefficients, has been studied intensively since the pioneering work of Clark and Cameron
[
4
]
from 1980; see Remark
1.4
. Recently the analysis of the optimal approximation of SDEs has been extended to the case of non-standard coefficients, see
[
17
,
29
,
24
]
and
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
While the first group of articles constructs SDEs with arbitrary slow best possible convergence rates, the second group establishes results, which apply to the optimal approximation of the CIR process. In particular, the works
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
yield that

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,N^{\min\{\nu,1\}}\,\inf_{g\in\mathcal{G}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|g(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}})-V_{T}\big|\right]>0,

where
\mathcal{G}(N)
is the set of measurable functions
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
t_{k}=k\frac{T}{N}
for all
k\in\{0,...,N\}
.

So, which rates are best possible for the approximation of the log-Heston SDE? If
|\rho|\neq 1
SDE (
1
) has non-commutative noise, i.e., the diffusion coefficients

\sigma^{(1)}(x,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\rho\sqrt{v}\\
\sigma\sqrt{v}\end{array}\right),\qquad\sigma^{(2)}(x,v)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\sqrt{v}\\
0\end{array}\right)

do not satisfy the commutativity condition

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{(1)}\partial_{i}\sigma^{(2)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{(2)}\partial_{i}\sigma^{(1)}.

Therefore, it is reasonable to expect an additional cut-off of the best possible convergence order at
\tfrac{1}{2}
, as in the case of standard coefficients. This article is dedicated to
confirm this expectation and to this end we
establish the following new result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
|\rho|\neq 1
,
t_{k}=k\Delta t
for all
k\in\{0,...,N\}
with
\Delta t=\frac{T}{N}
, let
\mathcal{U}(N)
be the set of measurable functions
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{2N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

and

e(N)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|u(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}},B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{T}\\
V_{T}\\
\end{array}\right)\right\|_{1}\right].

Then we have that

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\,N^{\min\{\nu,\frac{1}{2}\}}\,e(N)>0.

The so far best upper bound for
e(N)
has been established in
[
20
]
, where suitable Euler-type discretizations as, e.g.,
\hat{x}_{0}=x_{0},\hat{v}_{0}=v_{0}
and

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{t_{k+1}}=\hat{x}_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle+\big(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{v}^{+}_{t_{k}}\big)(t_{k+1}-t_{k})

(2)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\hat{v}^{+}_{t_{k}}}\Big(\rho\big(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}}\big)+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\big(B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}\big)\Big),

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{t_{k+1}}=\hat{v}_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle+\kappa\big(\theta-\hat{v}^{+}_{t_{k}}\big)(t_{k+1}-t_{k})+\sigma\sqrt{\hat{v}^{+}_{t_{k}}}\big(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}}\big),

for
k=0,\ldots,N-1
, have been analyzed.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
t_{k}=k\Delta t
for all
k\in\{0,...,N\}
with
\Delta t=\frac{T}{N}
, let
(\hat{x}_{N},\hat{v}_{N})
be given by (
2
) and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then we have

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\,N^{\min\{\frac{\nu}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}-\varepsilon}\,e(N)\,\leq\,\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\,N^{\min\{\frac{\nu}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}-\varepsilon}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{x}_{N}\\
\hat{v}_{N}\end{array}\right)-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X_{T}\\
V_{T}\\
\end{array}\right)\right\|_{1}\right]\,=\,0.

Thus for
\nu\geq 1
, Euler-type schemes as (
2
) obtain the optimal convergence order
\tfrac{1}{2}
, up to an arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
. We strongly suppose that the lower error bound in Theorem 1.1 is sharp, i.e., that we have

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\,N^{\min\{\nu,\frac{1}{2}\}}\,e(N)<\infty.

For the optimal approximation of the CIR process, i.e., for

Remark 1.4
.

The pioneering work on optimal approximation of stochastic differential equations is
[
4
]
.
Clark and Cameron studied in particular the optimal
L^{2}
-approximation of

\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=V_{t}dB_{t},\\

Here the optimal method is given by

\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{\frac{1}{N}},...,W_{1},B_{\frac{1}{N}},...,B_{1}\big]

and one has

\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{1}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{\frac{1}{N}},...,W_{1},B_{\frac{1}{N}},...,B_{1}\big]\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}=\frac{1}{2}N^{-1/2}.

Since then, for various error criteria a detailed and exhaustive study of the optimal approximation of general SDEs under standard assumptions has been carried out, even for adaptive discretizations, see, e.g.,
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
3
,
2
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
22
,
21
,
23
]

and
[
18
]
for a survey.
In particular, for the strong approximation under standard assumptions at the terminal time point, the results can be categorized along the so-called commutativity condition, which we mentioned above for the log-Heston SDE. If the commutativity condition is satisfied, then the best possible convergence order is at least one, and if this condition fails, then the best possible convergence order is one half.

A comparison with Theorem
1.1
illustrates now nicely the effect of non-standard coefficients, namely a deterioration of the best possible convergence order for small Feller indices.

As already mentioned, the question of the best possible

-approximation of the CIR process at the final time point has been answered by the works of Hefter, Herzwurm, Jentzen and Müller-Gronbach:

Theorem 2.4

(Corollary 14 in
[

[
10
]

It holds that

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,N^{\min\{\nu,1\}}\,\inf_{g\in\mathcal{G}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|g(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}})-V_{T}\big|\right]>0,

where
\mathcal{G}(N)
is the set of measurable functions
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Since
V
does not depend on
B
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{g\in\mathcal{G}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|g(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}})-V_{T}\big|\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|h(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}},B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-V_{T}\big|\right],

where

\mathcal{H}(N)
is the set of measurable functions
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{2N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Therefore our main result follows, if we can show that

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\,N^{1/2}\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|h(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}},B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-X_{T}\big|\right]>0

(4)

for
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
and
|\rho|\neq 1
.

Our proof of (
4
) will rely on a symmetrization argument. For this we will need an auxiliary result for the quantity

\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)=\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}-\int_{0}^{T}\overline{B}_{t}dW_{t}.

Here,
\overline{B}
denotes the piecewise linear interpolation of
B
on the grid
t_{0},...,t_{N}
, i.e.,
\overline{B}
is defined as

\overline{B}_{t}=B_{t_{k}}+\frac{t-t_{k}}{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}(B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}),\qquad t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],\,\,k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\},

and we also define

B_{t}^{\circ}=B_{t}-\overline{B}_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T].

Lemma 2.5
.

(i) Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\tau_{\ell,n}=\frac{\ell}{2^{n}}\frac{T}{N},\qquad\ell\in\{0,\ldots,2^{n}\},

and

\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2^{n}-1}B^{\circ}_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell,n}}\left(W_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell+1,n}}-W_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell,n}}\right).

We have that

\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)

almost surely and in
L^{2}
.

(ii) It holds

\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}W_{1}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|B_{t}^{\circ}|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

(i) By straightforward calculations using
B^{\circ}=B-\overline{B}
, the independence of
B
and
W
and the Itō isometry we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)-\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)\right|^{2}\right]\leq C\cdot N^{-1}\cdot 2^{-n},

which yields the claimed
L^{2}
-convergence for
n\rightarrow\infty
, and
also implies

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)-\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right]<\infty,

from which the almost sure convergence follows by an application of the Borel-Cantelli lemma.

(ii) Recall that
W
is independent of
B
and therefore also of
B^{\circ}
. The conditional law of
\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)
given

B^{\circ}_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell,n}}=x_{k,\ell},\quad\ell\in\{0,\ldots,2^{n}-1\},k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\},

is therefore Gaussian with zero mean and variance
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2^{n}-1}|x_{k,\ell}|^{2}(\tau_{\ell+1,n}-\tau_{\ell,n})
. We thus have

\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}W_{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2^{n}-1}|B^{\circ}_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell,n}}|^{2}\left(\tau_{\ell+1,n}-\tau_{\ell,n}\right)\right)^{1/2}.

Since also

\int_{0}^{T}|B^{\circ}_{t}|^{2}dt=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2^{n}-1}|B^{\circ}_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell,n}}|^{2}\left(\tau_{\ell+1,n}-\tau_{\ell,n}\right)

almost surely (by continuity of almost all sample paths of
B^{\circ}
), the assertion follows now from part (i).
∎

3.1
Allowing complete information on

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{N})

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z_{H}}

\displaystyle=\{Z\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\,Z\text{ is }\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{N})\text{-measurable}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z_{V}}

\displaystyle=\{Z\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\,Z\text{ is }\sigma(\mathcal{V}_{N})\text{-measurable}\}.

Since

\mathcal{Z_{H}}\subset\mathcal{Z_{V}}

it follows that

\displaystyle\inf_{Z\in\mathcal{Z_{H}}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|Z-X_{T}\big|\right]=\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|h(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}},B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-X_{T}\big|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{Z\in\mathcal{Z_{V}}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|Z-X_{T}\big|\right]=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-X_{T}\big|\right],

where
\mathcal{H}(N)
and
\mathcal{V}(N)
are as above.
Thus, it is sufficient to analyze the quantity

\inf_{Z\in\mathcal{Z_{V}}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|Z-X_{T}\big|\right]=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-X_{T}\big|\right]

(5)

to obtain a lower bound for
e_{X}(N)
.

3.2
Rewriting
X_{T}
and removing the measurable part

Now, we rewrite
X_{T}
. Note that the CIR process
V=(V_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is
\sigma(W)
-measurable (and therefore
\sigma(\mathcal{V}_{N})
-measurable) as the unique strong solution of the SDE

\displaystyle dV_{t}=\kappa(\theta-V_{t})dt+\sigma\sqrt{V_{t}}dW_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],\qquad V_{0}=v_{0}>0.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
we have that

X_{T}=Y_{T}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t},

where

\displaystyle Y_{T}=x_{0}

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\left(V_{T}-v_{0}-\kappa\theta T\right)+\mu T+\left(\frac{\rho\kappa}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}V_{u}du

\displaystyle+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}(\sqrt{V_{T}}B_{T}-A_{T}B_{T})

and

A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left(\frac{\kappa\theta}{2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{V_{s}}}-\frac{\kappa}{2}\sqrt{V_{s}}\right)\chi_{\{V_{s}\in(0,\infty)\}}ds,\qquad t\in[0,T].

(6)

In particular,
A=(A_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
Y_{T}
are
\sigma(\mathcal{V}_{N})
-measurable.

Proof.

Since

V_{T}=v_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\kappa(\theta-V_{u})du+\sigma\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{V_{u}}dW_{u}

we have that

X_{T}=Y_{T}^{(1)}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{V_{u}}dB_{u}

(7)

with

Y_{T}^{(1)}=x_{0}+\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\left(V_{T}-v_{0}-\kappa\theta T\right)+\mu T+\left(\frac{\rho\kappa}{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}V_{u}du.

We can use now Lemma
2.2
to write

\sqrt{V_{t}}=\sqrt{v_{0}}+A_{t}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\,W_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T],

(8)

where
A_{t}
is given by (
6
). So
\sqrt{V}
is a continuous semi-martingale with representation (
8
). Integration by parts now gives

\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{V_{u}}dB_{u}=\sqrt{V_{T}}B_{T}-\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dA_{t}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}

and

\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dA_{t}=A_{T}B_{T}-\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t},

respectively. Together with (
7
) this yields

X_{T}=Y_{T}^{(1)}+Y_{T}^{(2)}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right)

with

Y_{T}^{(2)}=\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}(\sqrt{V_{T}}B_{T}-B_{T}A_{T}),

which finishes the proof.
∎

As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{Z\in\mathcal{Z_{V}}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|Z-X_{T}\big|\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{Z\in\mathcal{Z_{V}}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z-Y_{T}-\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{Z}\in\mathcal{Z_{V}}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\tilde{Z}-\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right)\right|\right]

and it remains to analyze

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right|\right].

(9)

3.3
Removing the smooth part

Since
A=(A_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is smooth enough, asymptotically
\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}
does not matter for our approximation problem.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}-\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}A_{t_{i}}(B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_{i}})\right|\right]\leq C\cdot N^{-5/8}.

Proof.

We have

A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}a_{u}du,\qquad t\in[0,T],

with

a_{u}=\left(\frac{4\kappa\theta-\sigma^{2}}{8}\frac{1}{\sqrt{V_{u}}}-\frac{\kappa}{2}\sqrt{V_{u}}\right)\chi_{\{V_{u}\in(0,\infty)\}},\qquad u\in[0,T].

Since
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
we have by Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|a_{t}|^{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}\right]<\infty,\qquad\sup_{s,t\in[0,T]}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[|A_{t}-A_{s}|^{q}\right]}{|t-s|^{q/4}}<\infty

(10)

for all
q\geq 1
.
The Itō isometry now gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}-\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}A_{t_{i}}(B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_{i}})\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}(A_{t}-A_{\eta(t)})dB_{t}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|A_{t}-A_{\eta(t)}\right|^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|A_{t}-A_{\eta(t)}\right|\left|\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}a_{u}du\right|\right]dt.

Hölder’s inequality with
p=\nu+\frac{1}{2}
,
q=\frac{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|A_{t}-A_{\eta(t)}\right|\left|\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}a_{u}du\right|\right]\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|A_{t}-A_{\eta(t)}\right|^{q}\right]\right)^{1/q}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}a_{u}du\right|^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}.

Using (
10
) we have that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|A_{t}-A_{\eta(t)}\right|^{q}\right]\right)^{1/q}\leq C\cdot(\Delta t)^{1/4}.

Moreover, (
10
) also gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}a_{u}du\right|^{p}\right]\leq\left(\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|a_{u}|^{p}\right]\right)(t-\eta(t))^{p}\leq C\cdot(\Delta t)^{p},

and so we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}-\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}A_{t_{i}}(B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_{i}})\right|^{2}\right]\leq C\cdot N^{-5/4}.

The assertion follows now from the Lyapunov inequality.
∎

Since
\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}A_{t_{i}}(B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_{i}})
is
\sigma(\mathcal{V}_{N})
-measurable, we obtain that

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V(N)}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\int_{0}^{T}A_{t}dB_{t}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\tilde{v}(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})+\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}-\int_{0}^{T}(A_{t}-A_{\eta(t)})dB_{t}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\tilde{v}(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})+\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right|\right]-C\cdot N^{-5/8}

using that
|x|-|y|\leq|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consequently, we have reduced our initial problem to the study of

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right|\right].

(11)

3.4
Inserting Brownian bridges and symmetrization

Our final step relies on a symmetrization argument.
So, let us denote the piecewise linear interpolation of
B
on the grid
t_{0},...,t_{N}
by
\overline{B}
, i.e.,
\overline{B}
is defined as

\overline{B}_{t}=B_{t_{k}}+\frac{t-t_{k}}{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}(B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}),\qquad t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],\,\,k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}.

Then the process
B^{\circ}
given by

B^{\circ}_{t}=B_{t}-\overline{B}_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T],

is a Brownian bridge on
[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
, and moreover the processes

(B^{\circ}_{t})_{t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]},\,(B^{\circ}_{t})_{t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}\,,\,\ldots\,,\,(B^{\circ}_{t})_{t\in[t_{N-1},t_{N}]},\,\,\overline{B},\,\,W

are independent. Since

\int_{0}^{T}\overline{B}_{t}dW_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}B_{t_{k}}\left(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}}{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(t-t_{k})dW_{t}

is
\sigma(\mathcal{V}_{N})
-measurable, we have that

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\tilde{v}(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right]

with

\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)=\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}-\int_{0}^{T}\overline{B}_{t}dW_{t}.

Furthermore
B^{\circ}
and
-B^{\circ}
have the same law, so the independence of
B^{\circ}
from
(W,\overline{B})
implies that

\left(W,\overline{B},B^{\circ}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\left(W,\overline{B},-B^{\circ}\right).

Then, we also have

\left(W,\overline{B},\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\left(W,\overline{B},-\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)\right),

where

\mathcal{I}^{n}(B^{\circ},W)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2^{n}-1}B^{\circ}_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell,n}}\left(W_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell+1,n}}-W_{t_{k}+\tau_{\ell,n}}\right),\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

with

\tau_{\ell,n}=\frac{\ell}{2^{n}}\frac{T}{N}.

Now, Lemma
2.5
implies

\left(W,\overline{B},\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\left(W,\overline{B},-\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right).

Consequently, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})+\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right]

and so

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left(\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)-v(W,B_{t_{1}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})\right)+\left(v(W,B_{t_{1}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})+\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right].

It follows that

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right]

and therefore we have

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V}(N)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\mathcal{I}(B^{\circ},W)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[|W_{1}|\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}|B_{t}^{\circ}|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[|W_{1}|\right]\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[|B_{t}^{\circ}|\right]dt

by Lemma
2.5
(ii) and by Jensen’s inequality.
Using

\mathbb{E}\left[|X|\right]=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\sigma
for
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[|B_{t}^{\circ}|\right]dt=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|B_{t}^{\circ}|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}dt.

Straightforward calculations give

\mathbb{E}\left[|B_{t}^{\circ}|^{2}\right]=\frac{(t-t_{k})(t_{k+1}-t)}{t_{k+1}-t_{k}},\qquad t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],

which in turn yields

\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[|B_{t}^{\circ}|^{2}\right]^{1/2}dt=N\int_{0}^{T/N}\sqrt{\frac{t(T/N-t)}{T/N}}dt=\sqrt{\frac{T^{3}}{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{x(1-x)}dx.

Since
\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{x(1-x)}dx=\pi/8
, we have shown that

\displaystyle N^{1/2}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{V}(N)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v(W,B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-\int_{0}^{T}B_{t}dW_{t}\right|\right]\geq\frac{T}{4}.

(12)

Combining Subsections
3.1
–
3.4
with Equations (
5
), (
9
), (
11
) and (
12
) concludes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

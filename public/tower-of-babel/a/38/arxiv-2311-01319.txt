MMP for locally stable families and wall crossing for moduli of stable pairs Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 14E30, 14J10, 14J17. Keywords : minimal model program, moduli spaces, stable pairs.
Fanjun Meng, Ziquan Zhuang

Abstract
. We construct reduction and wall-crossing morphisms between the moduli spaces of stable pairs as the coefficients vary, generalizing the earlier work of Ascher, Bejleri, Inchiostro and Patakfalvi which deals with the klt case. Along the proof, we show that one can run the MMP with scaling on normal locally stable families over a normal base, and that the existence of good minimal models is preserved when reducing coefficients away from zero.



1.
Introduction

In
[
19
]
, Hassett shows that there are natural reduction and wall-crossing morphisms between the moduli spaces of weighted pointed stable curves as the weights vary. The higher-dimensional generalization of weighted pointed stable curves, whose study goes back to
[
23
]
, is the notion of stable pairs. These are semi-log-canonical (slc) pairs
(X,D=\sum a_{i}D_{i})
(the basic examples are simple normal crossing pairs
(X,D)
with coefficients
a_{i}\in(0,1]
) such that
K_{X}+D
is ample. The moduli theory of stable pairs has been established in the past several decades; we refer to
[
30
]
for a comprehensive discussion. In particular, for any dimension
d
, any coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}
for the boundary divisor
D
, and any volume
v=(K_{X}+D)^{d}>0
, there exists a proper moduli space that parameterizes stable pairs with the given numerical invariants.

It is natural to compare these moduli spaces as the coefficients vary, and one may ask whether the reduction and wall-crossing morphisms generalize to the moduli of stable pairs. Such morphisms play an important role in the study of explicit moduli compactifications, and have been worked out in several explicit examples, see e.g.
[
2
,
20
,
6
]
. The general question is taken up recently in
[
5
]
under a klt assumption. The goal of this article is to remove this klt condition and to construct, in the general setting, the reduction and wall-crossing morphisms between the moduli spaces of stable pairs.

These morphisms are typically obtained through an ample model construction: if we denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}}
the moduli space of stable pairs with coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}
, and let
\mathbf{b}
be another coefficient vector, then the expected natural morphism
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}}\to\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{b}}
should send a stable pair
(X,\sum a_{i}D_{i})
to the semi-log-canonical model of
(X,\sum b_{i}D_{i})
, which is roughly the ample model of
K_{X}+\sum b_{i}D_{i}
. Since semi-log-canonical models of non-normal semi-log-canonical pairs do not always exist (see e.g.
[
26
,
3
]
or Section
7.1
for some examples), this imposes restrictions on the locus where the morphism
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}}\to\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{b}}
can be defined. Another related restriction, observed in
[
5
]
, is that the reduction morphisms can only be defined on the normalization of the moduli spaces. With these in mind, our main results give some optimal statements that one may hope for. Roughly speaking, we show that wall-crossing morphisms exist on the locus that parameterizes degenerations of normal (hence log canonical) stable pairs, while the reduction morphisms exist on the normalization of the same locus.

1.1.
Main theorems

To state our main theorems more precisely, we need to introduce some notation. Fix a locally stable family (see Definition
2.16
)
f\colon(X,\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}D_{i})\to B
over a normal (but not necessarily proper) variety. A special case is when
(X,\sum D_{i})
is log smooth over
B
and
0<a_{i}\leq 1
for all
i
. For ease of notation, we write
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
. For any coefficient vector
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
, we denote by
\mathbf{b}D
the divisor
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}D_{i}
. Following
[
19
,
5
]
, we say that a coefficient vector
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
is admissible if
0<b_{i}\leq a_{i}
for all
i
and
K_{X/B}+\mathbf{b}D
is big over
B
.

Theorem 1.1

(reduction)
.

Assume that the generic fiber of
(X,\mathbf{a}D)\to B
is normal and has a log canonical model. Then for any admissible coefficient vector
\mathbf{b}
, there exists a
(
unique
)
stable family
(Z_{\mathbf{b}},\mathbf{b}\Delta_{\mathbf{b}})\to B
that is the log canonical model of
(X,\mathbf{b}D)\to B

(
i.e. the ample model of
K_{X/B}+\mathbf{b}D
over
B)
. In particular, it induces a unique morphism
\Phi_{\mathbf{b}}\colon B\to\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{b}}
.

In particular, if we denote by
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\lc}
the normalized closure of the locus in the moduli space
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}}
that parameterizes log canonical stable pairs with coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}
, then the above theorem gives the reduction morphism
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\lc}\to\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathbf{b}}^{\lc}
for every admissible coefficient vector
\mathbf{b}
. When the generic fiber of
(X,\mathbf{a}D)\to B
is klt and the base
B
is smooth (this is one of the cases considered in
[
5
]
), Theorem
1.1
follows from
[
7
]
.

We say that a polytope
\mathcal{P}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is admissible if every coefficient vector in
\mathcal{P}
is admissible. Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, as the coefficient
\mathbf{b}
varies in an admissible polytope
\mathcal{P}
, we also have a wall-and-chamber decomposition of
\mathcal{P}
corresponding to the reduced closure
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{b}}:=\overline{\Phi_{\mathbf{b}}(B)}
of the images of the reduction morphisms
\Phi_{\mathbf{b}}\colon B\to\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{b}}
.

Theorem 1.2

(wall crossing)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, let
\mathcal{P}
be an admissible rational polytope. Then there exists a finite rational polyhedral decomposition
\mathcal{P}=\cup_{i=1}^{p}\mathcal{P}_{i}
such that:

(1)

In any open chamber
\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\circ}
, the family
(Z_{\mathbf{b}},\Delta_{\mathbf{b}})\to B
of marked pairs is independent of
\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\circ}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{b}}\cong\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}
for any
\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\circ}
.

(2)

For any
i
, we denote
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{b}}
by
\mathcal{M}_{i}
for any
\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\circ}

(
this is well-defined by part
(1))
. Then for any
i,j
such that
\mathcal{P}_{j}
is a face of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
, there exists a
(
unique
)
morphism
\alpha_{ij}\colon\mathcal{M}_{i}\to\mathcal{M}_{j}
making the following diagram commute.

\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{ij}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}_{j}}

The morphisms
\alpha_{ij}
are what we refer to as the wall-crossing morphisms. Moreover, the morphism
\alpha_{ij}
lifts to a morphism between the universal families over
\mathcal{M}_{i}
and
\mathcal{M}_{j}
. Putting together Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we recover Hassett’s result in the curve case. When the generic fiber of
(X,\mathbf{a}D)\to B
is klt, the above theorem also improves upon
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.8, where the wall-crossing morphisms are constructed on the seminormalization of the corresponding moduli locus.

There are also reduction and wall-crossing morphisms in the semi-log-canonical case if we impose additional assumptions. The following is an example. It is a generalization of
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.1 to the semi-log-canonical setting. For any
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
, we write
\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}
if
b_{i}\leq a_{i}
for all
i
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,\sum D_{i})\to B
be a family of marked pairs over a normal variety and let
\mathcal{P}
be a rational polytope such that
(X,\mathbf{a}D)\to B
is a stable family for every
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{P}
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{a}}
be the normalized closure of the image of the morphism
\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}\colon B\to\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}}
. Then there is a canonical birational reduction morphism
r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}\colon\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{a}}\to\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{b}}
for any
\mathbf{a}\geq\mathbf{b}
in
\mathcal{P}
. Moreover, we have
r_{\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}}\circ r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}=r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}}
for any
\mathbf{a}\geq\mathbf{b}\geq\mathbf{c}
in
\mathcal{P}
, and there is a wall-and-chamber and wall-crossing structure on
\mathcal{P}
corresponding to
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{a}}
as in Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Other results

The proof of the main theorems relies on several other results that might be of independent interest. Some of these results are already known in the klt case, but the extension to the log canonical case is more subtle.

A famous conjecture in birational geometry is that every log canonical pair of nonnegative Kodaira dimension has a good minimal model (Definition
2.6
). For klt pairs of log general type this follows from
[
7
]
, but if the pair is only log canonical then the conjecture is wide open even in the log general type case. This presents one of the difficulties in the construction of the reduction morphisms in the log canonical setting. The first ingredient that goes into our proof of the main theorems is the existence of good minimal models for those special log canonical pairs that come up in Theorem
1.1
. In fact we prove a more general statement that abundance is preserved when reducing coefficients away from zero.

Theorem 1.4

(=Theorem
3.1
)
.

Let
(X,D)
be a log canonical pair over
B
and let
0\leq\Delta\leq D
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor. Assume that
(X,D)
has a good minimal model over
B
,
K_{X}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier, and
\Supp(\Delta)=\Supp(D)
. Then
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model or a Mori fiber space over
B
.

This implies Theorem
1.1
when the base
B
is a single point. In general, we need to show that the log canonical model construction works in locally stable families. Thus the next ingredient is a minimal model program (MMP) for such families.

Theorem 1.5

(=Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
f\colon(X,D)\to B
be a locally stable family over a normal variety with normal generic fiber. Then for any
f
-ample
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
H
on
X
, we may run the
(K_{X/B}+D)
-MMP over
B
with scaling of
H
. Moreover, the MMP terminates with a good minimal model over
B
if the generic fiber has a good minimal model.

Although MMP with scaling for log canonical pairs is known by
[
4
,
11
,
10
,
15
,
17
]
, the singularities of the pair
(X,D)
in the above theorem can be much worse, because the base
B
may have very bad singularities. On the other hand,
(X,D)
can be made log canonical after a base change. The above theorem is therefore a descent statement: in particular, we show that extremal contractions (resp. flips) descend along arbitrary reduced (resp. normal) base change. Ultimately, these rely on, among other things, the invariance of plurigenera for good minimal models of locally stable families. We remark that when the generic fiber of the family is klt, some special cases of Theorem
1.5
are implicit in
[
5
]
when they analyze the invertibility of certain special wall-crossing morphisms. Using Theorem
1.5
we are thus able to give a more direct and intrinsic construction of the reduction morphisms (that is, without analyzing the aforementioned wall-crossing morphisms) even in the klt case.

Finally, to obtain the wall-and-chamber structure in Theorem
1.2
, the third ingredient is the finiteness of ample models as the coefficients vary, see Theorem
5.1
. For klt pairs of log general type, this again follows from
[
7
]
. We refer to Section
5
for the relevant setup and the precise statement.

1.3.
Outline

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
covers some preliminary definitions and statements. Section
3
studies the existence of good minimal models for (some) log canonical pairs, Section

5

such that
X\to Z
is birational. Assume that

K_{X}+D\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},Z}M^{+}-M^{-}

where
M^{+},M^{-}\geq 0
have no common components and
M^{+}
is exceptional over
Z
. Then any
(K_{X}+D)
-MMP over
Z
with scaling of a relatively ample divisor contracts
M^{+}
after finitely many steps.

We should remark that the theorem only implies that after finitely many steps of the
(K_{X}+D)
-MMP over
Z
we get a model
X\dashrightarrow Y
such that
M^{+}_{Y}=0
. However,
(Y,D_{Y})
is not necessarily a minimal model over
Z
(this would require the termination of the
(K_{X}+D)
-MMP over
Z
, which is still open).

2.5.
Families of stable pairs

In this subsection, we recall some definitions and results on families of stable pairs in
[
30
]
. In general, it is quite subtle to give the correct definition of families of stable pairs. Here we mainly focus on the case when the bases of the families are reduced.

We start with the definition of relative Mumford divisors. We only consider the case when the morphism is flat.

Definition 2.13

(
[
30
]
*Definition 4.68)
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a flat morphism with reduced, connected and
S_{2}
fibers of pure dimension
d
over a reduced scheme
B
. A
relative Mumford divisor
on
X
is a divisor
D
on
X
such that

(1)

(Equidimensionality) Every irreducible component of
\Supp D
dominates an irreducible component of
B
, and all nonempty fibers of
\Supp D\to B
are of pure dimension
d-1
.

(2)

(Mumford condition) The morphism
f
is smooth at the generic points of
X_{b}\cap\Supp D
for every
b\in B
.

(3)

(Generic Cartier condition) The divisor
D
is Cartier at the generic points of
X_{b}\cap\Supp D
for every
b\in B
.

Note that when
B
is normal, conditions (1) and (2) imply condition (3) by
[
30
]
*Theorem 4.4.

We will usually fix a marking when we deal with stable pairs. So next we recall the definition of families of marked pairs. Fix a coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
such that
0\leq a_{i}\leq 1
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

Definition 2.14
.

A family of varieties marked with
n
divisors, or an
n
-marked family
over a reduced scheme
B
is
f\colon(X;D_{1},\ldots,D_{n})\to B
such that
f
is flat with demi-normal and connected fibers, and
D_{i}
is an effective, relative Mumford divisor for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

Definition 2.15

(
[
30
]
*Definition 8.4)
.

A
family of marked pairs with coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
over a reduced scheme
B
is a family of pairs
f\colon(X,\sum^{n}_{i=1}a_{i}D_{i})\to B
such that
f\colon(X;D_{1},\ldots,D_{n})\to B
is an
n
-marked family.

Next, we define locally stable families of pairs.

Definition 2.16

(
[
30
]
*Theorem-Definition 4.7)
.

A projective family of marked pairs
f\colon(X,\Delta=\sum^{n}_{i=1}a_{i}D_{i})\to B
with coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
over a reduced scheme
B
is
locally stable
if
K_{X/B}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier, and
(X_{b},\Delta_{b})
is slc for every
b\in B
. We also say that
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to B
is a
locally stable family of pairs with coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, or simply that
f
is
locally stable
.

Note that our definition is slightly different from
[
30
]
*Theorem-Definition 4.7, since here we also have a marking (the definition in
loc. cit.
should be thought of as the unmarked version). Since almost all the families of pairs we consider in this paper are families of marked pairs, we insist on the marking in all the definitions.

When the base is smooth, we have the following simple characterization of locally stable families.

Theorem 2.17

(
[
30
]
*Theorem 4.54 and Corollary 4.55)
.

A projective family of pairs
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to B
over a smooth scheme
B
is locally stable if and only if for every simple normal crossing divisor
D
on
B
, the pair
(X,\Delta+f^{*}D)
is slc.

Similarly, we can define stable families.

Definition 2.18
.

A family of marked pairs
f\colon(X,\Delta=\sum^{n}_{i=1}a_{i}D_{i})\to B
with coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
over a reduced scheme
B
is
stable
if
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to B
is locally stable, and
K_{X/B}+\Delta
is ample over
B
. We also say that
f\colon(X,\Delta)\to B
is a
stable family of pairs with coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, or simply that
f
is
stable
.

The foundational theorem in the moduli theory of higher-dimensional varieties is that stable pairs form a proper moduli.

Theorem 2.19

(
[
30
]
*Theorems 4.1 and 4.8)
.

Fix a vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
such that
0<a_{i}\leq 1
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, a positive integer
d
, and a positive number
v
. Then there exists a proper Deligne–Mumford stack
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a},d,v}
which represents the moduli problem of stable families
f\colon(X,\sum^{n}_{i=1}a_{i}D_{i})\to B
with fibers of dimension
d
and volume
v
for reduced bases
B
.

For ease of notation we will omit
d
in
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a},d,v}
as the dimension is often irrelevant in our discussion. We define
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}}:=\cup_{v}\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a},v}
. By convention, we extend the definition of
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}}
to coefficient vectors
\mathbf{a}
where some of the
a_{i}
are allowed to be zero: in this case, we simply set
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}}
to be
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}
where
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
is obtained by removing the zero entries in
\mathbf{a}
.

A direct consequence of the representability of the moduli problem is the following descent property.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
\varphi\colon B^{\prime}\to B
be a proper morphism between normal varieties with connected fibers, and let
(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})\to B^{\prime}
be a stable family. Assume that for every
b\in B
, the induced family
(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})\times_{B^{\prime}}\varphi^{-1}(b)
is a trivial family. Then there exists a stable family
(X,D)\to B
such that
(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})\cong(X,D)\times_{B}B^{\prime}
.

Here we equip
\varphi^{-1}(b)
with the reduced scheme structure. We say that a stable family
(X,D)\to B
is trivial if
(X,D)\cong(X_{b},D_{b})\times B
over
B
for some (or any)
b\in B
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
2.19
but can also be checked directly. By assumption and standard Hilbert scheme argument, the flat morphism
X^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
descends to
X\to B
. Since
D^{\prime}
has no variations along the fibers of
\varphi
, we see that each component of
D^{\prime}
maps onto a divisor in
X
. Let
D
be the natural pushforward of
D^{\prime}
. Since the base
B
is normal, the relative Mumford divisor condition can be checked on the fibers; since it is true for
D^{\prime}
, the same holds for
D
. Similarly, we know that
(X_{b},D_{b})
is slc for any
b\in B
. By construction, we have
(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})\cong(X,D)\times_{B}B^{\prime}
, and the relatively ample divisor
K_{X^{\prime}/B^{\prime}}+D^{\prime}
descends to
X
. Its descent is necessarily
K_{X/B}+D
, hence
K_{X/B}+D
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier and ample over
B
. These imply that the family
(X,D)\to B
is stable.
∎

3.
Existence of log canonical models

In this section, we prove several results on the existence of good minimal models and log canonical models for log canonical pairs. They form the first main ingredient in the construction of the reduction morphisms in Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Existence of good minimal models

First we show that abundance is preserved when reducing coefficients away from zero.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,D)
be a log canonical pair over
B
and let
0\leq\Delta\leq D
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor. Assume that
(X,D)
has a good minimal model over
B
,
K_{X}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier, and
\Supp(\Delta)=\Supp(D)
. Then
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model or a Mori fiber space over
B
.

Remark 3.2
.

Here the condition that
\Supp(\Delta)=\Supp(D)
is crucial. Without this assumption, the statement becomes equivalent to the full MMP conjecture (including abundance) that any log canonical pair has either a good minimal model or a Mori fiber space. This is because for any log canonical pair
(X,\Delta)
we can always choose some sufficiently ample divisor
H\geq 0
such that
(X,D:=\Delta+H)
is a stable pair. In particular, it satisfies all the other conditions of the above theorem.

Before we prove this theorem, we first consider a special case.

Lemma 3.3
.

In the setting of Theorem
3.1
, assume that
K_{X}+D
is semiample over
B
. Then
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model or a Mori fiber space over
B
.

Proof.

If
K_{X}+\Delta
is not pseudo-effective over
B
, then it has a Mori fiber space over
B
by
[
17
]
*Theorem 1.7. Thus we may assume that
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective over
B
. We need to show that it has a good minimal model over
B
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Z
be the ample model of
K_{X}+D
over
B
. Then
K_{X}+D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}\pi^{*}A
for some
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
A
that is ample over
B
. Since
0\leq\Delta\leq D
and
\Supp(\Delta)=\Supp(D)
by assumption, there exists some
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
0\leq\Gamma\leq\Delta
on
X
such that
\Delta
is a nontrivial convex combination of
\Gamma
and
D
, i.e.

\Delta=t\Gamma+(1-t)D

for some
t\in(0,1)
. We then have

K_{X}+\Delta=t(K_{X}+\Gamma)+(1-t)(K_{X}+D)\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},B}t(K_{X}+\Gamma+\pi^{*}H)

where
0\leq H\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},B}\frac{1-t}{t}A
such that
(X,\Gamma+\pi^{*}H)
is log canonical. Therefore, it suffices to show that
(X,\Gamma+\pi^{*}H)
has a good minimal model over
B
. As
K_{X}+\Gamma+(D-\Gamma)\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},Z}0
, by Theorem
2.10
we see that
(X,\Gamma)
has a good minimal model over
Z
. Since
H
is ample over
B
, the statement we want follows from Theorem
2.11
.
∎

We now consider the general case of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we may assume that
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective over
B
(otherwise it has a Mori fiber space over
B
). We first reduce to the case when the birational contraction to some weak log canonical model of
(X,D)
over
B
is a morphism. By assumption,
(X,D)
has a good minimal model
(Z,D_{Z})
over
B
. Denote by
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow Z
the induced birational contraction over
B
. Choose a common log resolution

\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{Z}

and let
F
be the sum of the
\varphi
-exceptional divisors. Since
(X,D)
is log canonical, we have
K_{W}+D_{W}+F-\varphi^{*}(K_{X}+D)
is effective and
\varphi
-exceptional. In particular, we see that
(Z,D_{Z})
is a weak log canonical model of
(W,D_{W}+F)
over
B
. By Lemma
2.9
, we also know that
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model over
B
if and only if
(W,\Delta_{W}+F)
has a good minimal model over
B
. Thus in order to prove the theorem we may replace
(X,D)
by
(W,D_{W}+F)
and
\Delta
by
\Delta_{W}+F
. In particular, we may assume that the induced birational map
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow Z
to the weak log canonical model
(Z,D_{Z})
over
B
is a morphism. Moreover, we may assume that
(X,D)
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial dlt.

We write

K_{X}+D=\pi^{*}(K_{Z}+D_{Z})+E\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},Z}E

where
E
is
\pi
-exceptional. As
(Z,D_{Z})
is a weak log canonical model of
(X,D)
over
B
, we also know that
E\geq 0
. Recall that
0\leq\Delta\leq D
, thus from

K_{X}+\Delta=K_{X}+D-(D-\Delta)\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},Z}E-(D-\Delta)

we get

K_{X}+\Delta\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},Z}M^{+}-M^{-}

where
M^{+},M^{-}\geq 0
have no common components,
M^{-}\leq D-\Delta
and
M^{+}\leq E
. In particular,
M^{+}
is exceptional over
Z
. Since
(X,D)
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial dlt,
(X,\Delta)
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial dlt as well. By Theorem
2.12
, the
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP over
Z
with scaling of some ample divisor contracts
M^{+}
after finitely many steps. Let
X\dashrightarrow Y
be the corresponding birational contraction over
Z
, let
\varphi\colon Y\to Z
be the induced morphism and let
\Delta^{\prime}=M^{-}_{Y}\geq 0
. Then
M^{+}_{Y}=0
and by pushing forward the previous identities to
Y
we obtain

K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+\Delta^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}(K_{Z}+D_{Z}).

This implies that
(Z,D_{Z})
is also a weak log canonical model of
(Y,D^{\prime}:=\Delta_{Y}+\Delta^{\prime})
over
B
, and moreover,
K_{Y}+D^{\prime}
is semiample over
B
. From
\Delta_{Y}\leq D^{\prime}\leq D_{Y}
and
\Supp(\Delta)=\Supp(D)
we also see that
\Supp(\Delta_{Y})=\Supp(D^{\prime})
, hence Lemma
3.3
applies to
(Y,D^{\prime})
and
\Delta_{Y}
, and we deduce that
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
has a good minimal model over
B
. Since
X\dashrightarrow Y
is part of a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP over
Z
, it follows from Lemma
2.9
(and the remark thereafter) that
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model over
B
as well. The proof is now complete.
∎

3.2.
Log canonical models

When we decrease coefficients from a locally stable family of pairs, we often end up with pairs
(X,\Delta)
such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is no longer
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier. Nonetheless, they are of log canonical type (sometimes also called potentially log canonical), i.e. there exists some
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
D\geq\Delta
on
X
such that
(X,D)
is log canonical. In this subsection, we define and study the log canonical models of such pairs. A key input is the following construction.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
28
]
*Proposition 19)
.

Let
(X,D)
be a log canonical pair and let
\Gamma
be an effective
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor supported on
D
. Then there exists a small birational projective morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
-\Gamma_{Y}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier and
\pi
-ample.

Proof.

We reproduce the proof for the reader’s convenience.
Let
W\to X
be a dlt modification of
(X,D)
and let
E
be the sum of its exceptional divisors. Then
(W,D_{W}+E)
is dlt and

K_{W}+D_{W}+E\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},X}0.

Choose some
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
such that
D\geq\varepsilon\Gamma
. By Theorem
2.10
, the pair
(W,D_{W}-\varepsilon\Gamma_{W}+E)
has a good minimal model over
X
. Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be the corresponding log canonical model over
X
. Note that
K_{Y}+D_{Y}+E_{Y}\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},X}0
, hence

K_{Y}+D_{Y}-\varepsilon\Gamma_{Y}+E_{Y}\sim_{{\mathbb{R}},X}-\varepsilon\Gamma_{Y}

is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier and
\pi
-ample by construction. Let us show that
\pi
is small; in other words,
E_{Y}=0
. To see this, we observe that as
-\Gamma_{Y}
is
\pi
-ample, the
\pi
-exceptional locus is necessarily contained in the support of
\Gamma_{Y}
. Since
E_{Y}
and
\Gamma_{Y}
do not have any common component, we deduce that
E_{Y}=0
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Repeating this construction for each component of
D
, we get

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(X,D)
be a log canonical pair. Then there exists a small birational projective morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
K_{Y}
and every irreducible component of
D_{Y}
are all
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier.

By
[
7
]
, every variety that underlies a klt pair has a small
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial modification, but this is no longer true in the log canonical case. The construction above thus provides a small “partial
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial” modification for log canonical pairs. We should point out that, if the pair
(X,D)
is only slc, then it may not be possible to find a small modification as in Corollary
3.5
. In fact, if
X
is a surface, then the only small birational modification is the identity, but not every irreducible component of
D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier (e.g. if the component intersects some double locus where two components of
X
meet).

We next define log canonical models and good minimal models for pairs of log canonical type.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair of log canonical type over
B
. Suppose that
X\dashrightarrow Z
is a birational contraction over
B
. Then
(Z,\Delta_{Z})
is called a log canonical model (resp. good minimal model) of
(X,\Delta)
over
B
if there exists a small birational projective morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier and
(Z,\Delta_{Z})
is a log canonical model (resp. good minimal model) of
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
over
B
.

Similarly, we can define weak log canonical models for pairs of log canonical type. We leave the details to the reader.

Remark 3.7
.

The existence of such a small birational projective morphism is guaranteed by Lemma
3.4
. Since
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier,
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
is a log canonical pair. Thus we can talk about the log canonical model (resp. good minimal models) of
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
over
B
. By Lemma
2.9
, the existence of the log canonical model (resp. good minimal models) is independent of the choice of the small birational projective morphisms, and the log canonical model of
(X,\Delta)
over
B
, if exists, is unique. Moreover, it coincides with the standard definition of the log canonical model (resp. good minimal models) of
(X,\Delta)
over
B
if
K_{X}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier.

Remark 3.8
.

Let
g\colon Y\to X
be any birational projective morphism such that
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+E)
is a log canonical pair where
E
is the sum of all the
g
-exceptional divisors. Then by Lemma
2.9
, the log canonical model of
(X,\Delta)
over
B
defined above is the same as the log canonical model of
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+E)
over
B
. Since
(X,\Delta)
is a pair of log canonical type over
B
, there exists an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
D\geq\Delta
on
X
such that
(X,D)
is log canonical. In practice, we usually choose
g
to be a dlt modification of
(X,D)
or a log resolution of
(X,D)
. It is convenient to work with these special birational projective morphisms sometimes.

The results from the previous subsection then have straightforward generalizations to the non-
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier setting. In particular, we have

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(X,D)
be a pair of log canonical type over
B
and let
0\leq\Delta\leq D
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor. Assume that
(X,D)
has a good minimal model over
B
,
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective (resp. big) over
B
, and
\Supp(\Delta)=\Supp(D)
. Then
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model (resp. log canonical model) over
B
.

Here we say that
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective over
B
if there exists a small birational projective morphism
Y\to X
such that
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier and pseudo-effective over
B
. This notion is independent of the choice of the small birational projective morphisms.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.5
, there exists a small birational projective morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
K_{Y}+D_{Y}
and
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}
are both
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier. By Remark
3.7
, we see that
(Y,D_{Y})
has a good minimal model over
B
. Thus our assumptions imply that
(Y,D_{Y})
and
\Delta_{Y}
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, which then implies that
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
has a good minimal model over
B
. If
K_{X}+\Delta
is big over
B
, so is
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}
(since
\pi
is small), hence we also get a log canonical model of
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
over
B
. By definition, it follows that
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model (resp. log canonical model if
K_{X}+\Delta
is big over
B
) over
B
.
∎

4.
MMP for locally stable families

4.1.
MMP with scaling

The goal of this section is to develop an MMP theory for locally stable families. The main result is the following statement. This is another main ingredient in the construction of the reduction morphisms.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\colon(X,D)\to B
be a locally stable family over a normal variety with normal generic fiber. Then for any
f
-ample
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
H
on
X
, we may run the
(K_{X/B}+D)
-MMP over
B
with scaling of
H
. Moreover, the MMP terminates with a good minimal model over
B
if the generic fiber has a good minimal model.

When
K_{X/B}+D
is pseudo-effective over
B
and the
(K_{X/B}+D)
-MMP over
B
terminates, we get a minimal model
(X_{m},D_{m})\to B
which is locally stable such that
K_{X_{m}/B}+D_{m}
is nef over
B
. We say that it is a good minimal model of
(X,D)\to B
if
K_{X_{m}/B}+D_{m}
is semiample over
B
. If in addition
K_{X/B}+D
is big over
B
, we may also define the log canonical model of
(X,D)\to B
as the ample model of
K_{X_{m}/B}+D_{m}
over
B
. It is necessarily also the ample model of
K_{X/B}+D
over
B
. All these are analogous to Definition
2.6
.

To prove Theorem
4.1
, we shall first take a resolution
\widetilde{B}\to B
of singularities, run the MMP after the base change, and descend every step of the MMP to
B
. As such, we need to analyze how maps such as extremal contractions and flips descend. The next several lemmas are designed to take care of these.

The first ingredient is the following invariance of plurigenera for good minimal models of locally stable families. By cohomology and base change, it eventually translates into the descent property of the extremal contractions.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\colon(X,D)\to B
be a locally stable family over a connected reduced scheme with normal generic fibers. Assume that
D
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor,
K_{X/B}+D
is nef over
B
and every generic fiber of
f
has a good minimal model. Then for any sufficiently divisible positive integer
m
, the dimension of
H^{0}(X_{b},\mathcal{O}_{X_{b}}(m(K_{X_{b}}+D_{b})))
is independent of
b\in B
.

Proof.

We may freely shrink
B
around any chosen point as needed. In particular, we may assume that
B
is quasi-projective. Since the assumptions and the statement are preserved by any birational proper base change, we may also replace
B
by a resolution of singularities and assume that

4.2.
Semi-log-canonical models

If the locally stable family in Theorem
4.1
does not have a normal generic fiber, then the MMP may not exist, since MMP does not apply to slc pairs in general (see e.g.
[
26
,
3
]
for some related examples). However, if we assume that the generic fiber has a semi-log-canonical model, then the family still has a relative semi-log-canonical model. This is enough for most applications in our work.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
f\colon(X,D)\to B
be a locally stable family over a normal variety and let
0\leq\Delta\leq D
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor. Assume that the generic fiber of
(X,\Delta)\to B
has a semi-log-canonical model. Then the family
(X,\Delta)\to B
has a semi-log-canonical model.

To make this precise, we need to define semi-log-canonical models for the families that appear in the above statement. If the generic fiber is normal and
K_{X/B}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier, then
(X,\Delta)\to B
is locally stable and its (semi-)log canonical model is just the log canonical model defined right after Theorem
4.1
. The
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier assumption can be dropped from the definition in a way similar to Definition
3.6
.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
(X,D)\to B
be a locally stable family over a normal variety with normal generic fiber and let
0\leq\Delta\leq D
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor such that
K_{X/B}+\Delta
is big on the generic fiber. Suppose that
X\dashrightarrow Z
is a birational contraction over
B
and
(Z,\Delta_{Z})\to B
is a stable family. Then
(Z,\Delta_{Z})\to B
is called a log canonical model of
(X,\Delta)\to B
if there exists a small birational projective morphism
Y\to X
such that
(Y,\Delta_{Y})\to B
is locally stable with normal generic fiber and
(Z,\Delta_{Z})\to B
is the log canonical model of
(Y,\Delta_{Y})\to B
.

Remark 4.12
.

Such a small birational projective morphism
Y\to X
always exists by Lemma
4.6
. Since a stable family is determined by its generic fiber (thanks to the separatedness of the moduli of stable pairs), by Remark
3.7
we see that the log canonical model
(Z,\Delta_{Z})\to B
is unique if it exists. Moreover, by applying Theorem
4.1
to the family
(Y,\Delta_{Y})\to B
we deduce that
(X,\Delta)\to B
has a log canonical model if and only if its generic fiber has one. Thus we can also define the log canonical model as a stable family over
B
whose generic fiber is the log canonical model of the generic fiber of
(X,\Delta)\to B
. This interpretation will be convenient in some of our subsequent discussions.

To define semi-log-canonical models in the general setting, the following observation is necessary.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
f\colon(X,D)\to B
be a locally stable family over a normal variety. Then its normalization
(X^{\nu},\Gamma+D^{\nu})\to B
(where
\Gamma
is the conductor) is a disjoint union of locally stable families over
B
.

The main subtlety here is the flatness of
X^{\nu}\to B
.

Proof.

By adding a general relative hyperplane section to
D
we may assume that the family is stable. When
B
is smooth, the lemma is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.17
. In general, let
\varphi\colon\widetilde{B}\to B
be a resolution of singularities and let
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{D})\to\widetilde{B}
be the stable family obtained by base change. Then its normalization
(\widetilde{X}^{\nu},\widetilde{\Gamma}+\widetilde{D}^{\nu})\to\widetilde{B}
is stable (more precisely, it is a disjoint union of stable families over
\widetilde{B}
). We claim that it descends to a stable family over
B
. By Lemma
2.20
, it suffices to check that the family is trivial along the exceptional fibers of
\varphi
.

Let
b\in B
,
W:=\varphi^{-1}(b)
and
Z:=\widetilde{X}^{\nu}\times_{\widetilde{B}}W
. Note that
W
is connected as
B
is normal. As in the proof of Lemma
4.6
, in order to prove the above claim we only need to show that
Z\to W
is a trivial family, since the boundary part is supported on the preimage of
\Supp(D_{b})\cup\Sing(X_{b})
, thus cannot have any variation. Since
(\widetilde{X}^{\nu},\widetilde{\Gamma}+\widetilde{D}^{\nu})\to\widetilde{B}
is stable, the conductor
\widetilde{\Gamma}
does not contain any irreducible component of any fiber of
\widetilde{X}^{\nu}\to\widetilde{B}
. As the normalization morphism
\widetilde{X}^{\nu}\to\widetilde{X}
is an isomorphism away from
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, we deduce that it restricts to a partial normalization
Z_{w}\to X_{b}
for any
w\in W
. The latter is uniquely determined if we know which codimension one nodes in
X_{b}
are normalized, because
Z_{w}
is
S_{2}
. Since there are only finitely many codimension one nodes and
W
is connected, we see that
Z_{w}\to X_{b}
is independent of
w\in W
. Thus the conditions of Lemma
2.20
are verified and we conclude that
(\widetilde{X}^{\nu},\widetilde{\Gamma}+\widetilde{D}^{\nu})\to\widetilde{B}
descends to a stable family
(X^{\nu},\Gamma+D^{\nu})\to B
, which is at least a partial normalization of
(X,D)\to B
.

It remains to check that
X^{\nu}
is normal. Since the fibers of
(X^{\nu},\Gamma+D^{\nu})\to B
are slc, we see that
X^{\nu}\to B
has
S_{2}
fibers. Combined with the normality of
B
, we deduce from
[
12
]
*Corollaire 6.4.2 that
X^{\nu}
is
S_{2}
. Moreover, since
X^{\nu}\to B
has reduced fibers of constant dimension as well as a normal generic fiber, we see that the singular locus of
X^{\nu}
has codimension at least two. These together imply that
X^{\nu}
is normal. Thus
(X^{\nu},\Gamma+D^{\nu})\to B
is the normalization of
(X,D)\to B
and it is stable by construction.
∎

By Kollár’s gluing theory
[

]
*Chapter 5, a stable family is determined by its normalization together with the induced involution on
(\Gamma^{\nu},\Diff_{\Gamma^{\nu}}(D^{\nu}))
, where
\Gamma^{\nu}
is the normalization of
\Gamma

\Diff_{\Gamma^{\nu}}(D^{\nu})
denotes the different (see e.g.
[

]
*Definition 4.2).

Definition 4.14
.

Let
(X,D)\to B
be a locally stable family over a normal variety and let

be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor such that

is big on every irreducible component of the generic fiber. A stable family

over

.

Since

have multiple irreducible components here, birational maps between them are defined as in
[

]
*Definition 1.11. In particular, every irreducible component of

dominates an irreducible component of

is an isomorphism over the generic points of

is the strict transform of the conductor on

.

Proof of Theorem
4.10
.

Let
(X^{\nu},\Gamma+D^{\nu})\to B
be the normalization of
(X,D)\to B
as before and let
\tau
be the induced involution on
(\Gamma^{\nu},\Diff_{\Gamma^{\nu}}(D^{\nu}))
where
\Gamma^{\nu}
is the normalization of
\Gamma
. Let
\Delta^{\nu}
be the strict transform of
\Delta
. By assumption, the generic fiber of the family

has a log canonical model, hence the family

also has a log canonical model

by Remark

. To construct the slc model, we may now assume that

is smooth. Indeed, if the slc model exists after base change via a resolution of singularities

, then as a stable family, the slc model we obtain over

is automatically a trivial family along the exceptional fibers of

(this is because these fibers are connected, and there are only finitely many stable pairs with a given partial normalization; c.f. the proof of Lemma

it descends to give the slc model over

.

Thus we shall assume that
B
is smooth. To proceed, we need to show that the induced birational involution

on the normalization
\Gamma_{Y}^{\nu}
of
\Gamma_{Y}
is a regular involution. This holds over the generic point of
B
, since the generic fiber of
(X,\Delta)\to B
has an slc model by assumption. Note that

is a stable family by construction and hence

and adjunction. By the same argument as in the proof of
[

*Lemma 7.2, we then deduce that

is regular.

We conclude by using Kollár’s gluing theory. Note that

is log canonical by Theorem

, hence

is also log canonical. By
[

]
*Corollary 5.33 and Theorem 5.38, there exists a stable family

whose normalization is

∎

From the above proof, we see that the slc model is unique if it exists. By the same argument, we also get the following corollary.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
B
be an open subset of a normal variety
\overline{B}
, and let
(X,D)\to B
be a stable family. Assume that its normalization
(X^{\nu},\Gamma+D^{\nu})\to B
extends to a stable family
(\overline{X}^{\nu},\overline{\Gamma}+\overline{D}^{\nu})\to\overline{B}
. Then
(X,D)\to B
extends uniquely to a stable family
(\overline{X},\overline{D})\to\overline{B}
whose normalization is
(\overline{X}^{\nu},\overline{\Gamma}+\overline{D}^{\nu})\to\overline{B}
.

5.
Finiteness of ample models

In
[
7
]
*Corollary 1.1.5, results on the finiteness of ample models are presented. To study the wall crossing for moduli of stable pairs in the log canonical setting, we need to establish the finiteness of ample models in a more general setting. This is the main task of this section.

Let us fix some notation. Let
f\colon X\to B
be a projective morphism of normal quasi-projective varieties. Let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
be prime divisors on
X
. Consider the vector space

V:=\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}{\mathbb{R}}D_{i}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

For any
D=\sum^{n}_{i=1}a_{i}D_{i}
, we define

\left\lVert D\right\rVert:=\max_{i}\{|a_{i}|\}

which gives a norm on
V
. For a log canonical pair
(X,D)
over
B
, we define its ample model over
B
as the ample model of
K_{X}+D
over
B
.

We follow
[
7
]
*Section 3.7 for definitions on convex geometry. A polytope (resp. rational polytope) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the convex hull of a finite set of points (resp. rational points). A finite (resp. rational) polyhedral decomposition of a (resp. rational) polytope
\mathcal{P}
is a finite decomposition
\mathcal{P}=\cup_{i=1}^{p}\mathcal{P}_{i}
such that each
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is a (resp. rational) polytope, every face of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
coincides with
\mathcal{P}_{j}
for some
j
, and for all
1\leq i,j\leq p
, the intersection of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
and
\mathcal{P}_{j}
is either empty or a common face of both
\mathcal{P}_{i}
and
\mathcal{P}_{j}
. Usually the interior
\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\circ}
of the polytopes
\mathcal{P}_{i}
are called the chambers, while their faces are called the walls. The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

Notation as above. Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq V
be a rational polytope such that
(X,D)
is a log canonical pair and has a good minimal model over
B
for every
D\in\mathcal{P}
.

Then there exist a finite rational polyhedral decomposition
\mathcal{P}=\cup_{i=1}^{p}\mathcal{P}_{i}
, together with finitely many rational maps
\psi_{j}\colon X\dashrightarrow Z_{j}
over
B
,
1\leq j\leq q
, such that: for any
1\leq i\leq p
, there exists some
1\leq j\leq q
such that
\psi_{j}\colon X\dashrightarrow Z_{j}
is the ample model of
(X,D)
over
B
for any
D\in\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\circ}
.

The proof largely follows the general inductive strategy of
[
7
]
. The key step is Proposition
5.5
, which essentially implies that the finite rational polyhedral decomposition locally exists. Before the proof, we need to first prepare a few auxiliary lemmas.

Let

Remark 6.4
.

In addition to weight reduction morphisms,
[
19
]
also studies forgetting morphisms (which is equivalent to reducing certain weights to zero) between moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{g,\mathbf{a}}
of curves with marked points. For moduli of stable pairs, if we restrict to the components whose generic points parameterize log canonical pairs, then the existence of forgetting morphisms is essentially equivalent to the abundance conjecture. In fact, if abundance holds, then the assumption of Theorem
6.2
is automatically satisfied and hence we get the forgetting morphisms. On the other hand, if
(X,D)
is a log canonical pair such that
K_{X}+D
is nef and big and
H\geq 0
is some general ample divisor on
X
, then
(X,D+\varepsilon H)
is a stable pair for any
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. To construct the forgetting morphism when
\varepsilon\to 0
requires taking the ample model of
K_{X}+D
, and it is well known that abundance for log canonical pairs of log general type is equivalent to abundance in general.

7.

In this section, we give some examples to show that some of the assumptions in our main results are optimal.

7.1.

7.2.

By modifying an example in

is necessary (more specifically, the only place in

that relies on this assumption is their Theorem 7.6).

Example 7.2
.

Consider a line

in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and a smooth plane curve

of degree

that has transversal intersection with

. Let

be the blow up at

, let

(resp.

) be the strict transform of

(resp.

), and let

be the
\pi

(X,a(C+L)+bE)

), the ample model of
(X,a(C+L)+bE)

and volume at most

may have infinitely many connected components. A priori, we could still investigate whether there is some finite wall-and-chamber structure in this setting. We declare that a coefficient vector
\mathbf{a}
sits on a wall if there exist a pair
(X,D)
and some
\mathbf{a}_{i}

(i=1,2,\dots)
converging to
\mathbf{a}
such that
(X,\mathbf{a}_{i}D)
are stable pairs of volume at most
v
for all
i
but
(X,\mathbf{a}D)
is not. The next series of examples show that such walls are often badly behaved.

Example 7.3
.

Let
C_{d}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a smooth plane curve of degree
d\geq 4
, then
(\mathbb{P}^{2},aC_{d})
is stable if and only if
a>\frac{3}{d}
. Thus we hit a wall at
a=\frac{3}{d}
. As
d
varies, we get infinitely many walls near
0
.

Example 7.4
.

Fix an integer
d\geq 2
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, let
C_{m}
be a general member of the base point free linear system
|\mathcal{O}(dm)|
on
\mathbb{P}(1,1,m)
. Then
(\mathbb{P}(1,1,m),aC_{m})
is stable if and only if
a>\frac{1}{d}+\frac{2}{dm}
. Thus there are walls at
\frac{1}{d}+\frac{2}{dm}
for each
m
, and we get infinitely many walls near
\frac{1}{d}
.

In these two examples, the walls accumulate from above. One may ask whether the walls also accumulate from below (equivalently, whether the walls satisfy the ascending chain condition, or ACC). The next example shows that this can also happen.

Example 7.5
.

If
X
is a klt surface with ample canonical divisor, and there exists a divisor
E
over
X
such that
a(E,X,0)<0
, then there is a wall at
-a(E,X,0)
. This is because there exists a birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
that extracts
E
as the unique exceptional divisor, and
K_{Y}+(-a(E,X,0)-\varepsilon)E=\pi^{*}K_{X}-\varepsilon E
is ample when
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. By
[
25
]
*43, there is an unbounded set of klt surfaces with ample canonical divisor and volume
\leq 2
. Moreover, the minimal log discrepancies of these surfaces can be arbitrarily close to zero (this follows from the general boundedness result in
[
13
]
, but can also be deduced through a direct computation). By our previous discussion, this implies that there are walls accumulating near
1
(the accumulation is necessarily from below).

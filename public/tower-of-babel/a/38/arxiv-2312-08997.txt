1.
Introduction

The problem of studying perfect powers in certain sequences has long been
of interest to number theorists. We mention two
well-known results.

•

Pethő
[
20
]
and, independently,
Shorey and Stewart
[
24
]
showed that
there are only finitely many perfect powers in any non-trivial
binary recurrence sequence.

•

Bugeaud, Mignotte, and Siksek
[
5
]

proved that the only perfect powers in the Fibonacci sequence
are
0
,
1
,
8
and
144
.

This paper is concerned with perfect powers in elliptic divisibility
sequences, which we now introduce.
Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve given by an
integral Weierstrass model

(1)

y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6},\ \ a_{1},\ldots,a_{6}\in\mathbb{Z}

and let
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
be a point of infinite order. For
n\geq 1
,
we may write the multiple
nP
as

(2)

nP\;=\;\left(\frac{A_{n}}{B_{n}^{2}},\frac{C_{n}}{B_{n}^{3}}\right)

where
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
B_{n}\geq 1

and
\gcd(B_{n},A_{n}C_{n})=1
.
The sequence
\mathcal{B}_{E,P}=(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is called the
elliptic divisibility sequence
associated to
E
and
P
.
It is a divisibility sequence in the sense that if
m\mid n
, then
B_{m}\mid B_{n}
.
We say that a prime
p
is a
primitive divisor
of the term
B_{n}
if
p\mid B_{n}

but
p\nmid B_{m}
for all
m<n
.
The following theorem of Silverman
[
26
, Lemma 9]
is the most famous result regarding
the arithmetic of elliptic divisibility sequences.

Theorem 1

(Silverman)
.

Let
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an elliptic divisibility sequence.
Then, at most finitely many terms in the sequence do not have a primitive divisor.

In 2007, Everest, Reynolds and Stevens
[
10
]
posed the
question of whether an elliptic divisibility sequence
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}

contains only finitely many perfect powers.
They proved the following somewhat weaker result.

Theorem 2

(Everest, Reynolds and Stevens)
.

Let
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an elliptic divisibility sequence.
For any fixed
f\geq 2
, there are only finitely many
n
for which
B_{n}
is an
f^{\text{th}}
power.

The proof of Theorem
2
crucially relies
on Faltings’ theorem (the Mordell conjecture), and is therefore ineffective.
Theorem
2
has been extended to the
function field setting in
[
6
]

and to products of terms in elliptic divisibility sequences

[
15
]
.
However, the question of Everest, Reynolds and Stevens has remained open. The
only partial results we are aware of are due to Reynolds
[
21
]

and to Alfaraj
[
1
]
.

Reynolds proved that there are finitely many perfect powers in an elliptic
divisibility sequence
arising from a non-integral point on an elliptic curve of the form

y^{2}=x^{3}+d
, where
d\neq 0
is an integer.
Alfaraj proves that there are only finitely many perfect powers
in an elliptic divisibility sequence arising from a non-integral
point on elliptic curves of the form
y^{2}=x(x^{2}+b)
,
where
b
is a positive integer.
Both proofs attach a suitable Frey curve to the
equation
B_{n}=u^{\ell}
and make use of the modularity
of the mod
\ell
Galois representation of the Frey curve.
In the work of Reynolds, the Frey curve is defined over
the rationals, and in Alfaraj’s paper the Frey curve is defined
over a quadratic field.

The purpose of this paper is to generalize the results of Reynolds and Alfaraj
to general Weierstrass models. More precisely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve given by an integral
Weierstrass equation (
1
). Let
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})

and let
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the corresponding
elliptic divisibility sequence. Suppose the following three
conditions hold:

(1)

P
is non-integral (equivalently
B_{1}>1
);

(2)

\Delta(E)>0
where
\Delta(E)
is the discriminant
of
E
;

(3)

P\in E_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, where
E_{0}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the connected
component of the identity.

Then there are only finitely many perfect powers in the sequence

(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.

Remark.

Suppose (i) and (ii) hold, but not (iii). The assumption (ii)
implies
[E(\mathbb{R}):E_{0}(\mathbb{R})]=2
. Thus
2P\in E_{0}(\mathbb{R})
.
It follows from the theorem that the
subsequence
(B_{2n})_{n\geq 1}
contains only finitely many
perfect powers.

A key innovation of our paper is
the construction of a Frey curve
attached to the equation
B_{n}=u^{\ell}
where

B_{n}
arises from a point of infinite order
on a general Weierstrass model.
This Frey curve is defined over a number
field
L
of degree at most
24
, and assumptions (ii) and (iii)
are needed to ensure that this field is totally real,
whence we may use known results on the modularity
of elliptic curves over totally real fields.
We can make do without assumptions (ii) and (iii)
if we assume the three standard conjectures,
which we state in Section
6
,
but for now briefly indicate what they are:

(1)

Serre’s modularity conjecture
over number fields (Conjecture
15
);

(2)

Serre’s uniformity conjecture for elliptic curves over number fields
(Conjecture
16
);

(3)

the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture for eigenforms over number fields
(Conjecture
17
).

Theorem 4
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve given by an integral
Weierstrass equation (
1
). Let
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})

and let
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the corresponding
elliptic divisibility sequence. Suppose
P

is non-integral (equivalently
B_{1}>1
).
Assume Conjectures
15
,
16

and
17
.
Then there are only finitely many perfect powers in the sequence

(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2

we recall standard properties of elliptic divisibility
sequences, and we generalise
a result of Alfaraj that is in turn a consequence
of Silverman’s primitive divisor theorem. In Section
3

we state three theorems concerning Galois representations
attached to elliptic curves over totally real fields.
These theorems play the role, in our setting, that is played
by the theorems of Mazur, Ribet and Wiles in the proof of Fermat’s
Last Theorem. In Section
4
we state a proposition
(Proposition
11
)
that asserts the existence of a suitable Frey curve,
defined over a number field
L
of degree at most
24
,
attached to a solution of
B_{n}=u^{\ell}
;
hypotheses (ii) and (iii) of Theorem
3

ensure that
L
is totally real. We also show
that Theorem
3
follows from Proposition
11
,
using the three theorems. Proposition
11

is proved in Section
5
. Finally, in Section
6

we give precise statements of the three conjectures
above, and we explain how they allow us, together with
relatively minor modifications of earlier arguments,
to deduce Theorem
4
.

2.
Background

The proposition below recalls a standard property of elliptic divisibility sequences.

Proposition 5
.

Let
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an elliptic divisibility sequence generated by a non-torsion point on
an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
given by an integral Weierstrass model.

(1)

Let
p
be a prime, and
n
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose
v_{p}(B_{n})>0
. Then

(3)

v_{p}(B_{nm})=v_{p}(B_{n})+v_{p}(m)+O(1)

where the
O(1)
constant does not depend on
m
and
n
.

(2)

(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a strong divisibility sequence, i.e., for all
n
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4)

\gcd(B_{m},B_{n})=B_{\gcd(m,n)}.

Proof.

These are consequences
of the theory of formal groups of elliptic curves.
For proofs, see Lemma IV.4.3 and Lemma IV.3.1 of
[
29
]
.
∎

We point out
[
29
, Corollary 4.5]
that (
3
) can be simplified
to
v_{p}(B_{nm})=v_{p}(B_{n})+v_{p}(m)
if
p\neq 2
, or
if
p=2
but
a_{1}
is even, where
a_{1}
is the coefficient of
xy
in the Weierstrass model
(
1
).

The following lemma is a crucial step in proving Theorem
3
.
It is a generalisation of Alfaraj’s
[
1
, Lemma 3.4]

which copes with the case where
a_{1}
is odd.

Lemma 6
.

Let
(B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an elliptic divisibility sequence generated by a
non-torsion point on some elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
given by an
integral
Weierstrass model. Suppose that
B_{1}>1
.
Let
T
be a finite set of primes. There exists a positive integer
\kappa

and a prime
p\notin T
such that if
B_{n}
is an
\ell
-th power for some

\ell>\kappa
,
then
p\mid B_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
q\mid B_{1}
be a prime.
Suppose
B_{n}
is an
\ell
-th power.
By (
4
), we have
q\mid B_{n}
, and hence
\ell\leq v_{q}(B_{n})
.
From (
3
), we have

\ell\;\leq\;v_{q}(B_{1})+v_{q}(n)+r,

for some integer
r
, depending only on the elliptic
divisibility sequence. Hence,

(5)

v_{q}(n)\;\geq\;\ell-v_{q}(B_{1})-r.

By Silvermans’s primitive divisor theorem (Theorem
1
),
there exists
\kappa^{\prime}
such that if
m\geq\kappa^{\prime}
,
then
B_{m}
is divisible
by some prime
p\notin T
. Let
\kappa
be an integer such that
that
\kappa>v_{q}(B_{1})+r
and

q^{\kappa-v_{q}(B_{1})-r}>\kappa^{\prime}
.
Then, there is some prime
p\notin T
such that

p\mid B_{q^{\kappa-v_{q}(B_{1})-r}}.

If
\ell>\kappa
, then

q^{\kappa-v_{q}(B_{1})-r}\mid q^{\ell-v_{q}(B_{1})-r}\mid n

by (
5
).
So,

p\mid B_{q^{\kappa-v_{q}(B_{1})-r}}\mid B_{n}.

∎

3.
Preliminaries from Modularity and Galois Representations of Elliptic Curves

We prove Theorem
3
by attaching a Frey elliptic curve, defined over a totally
real field, to a term of the elliptic divisibility sequence that is a perfect power,
and applying the so-called “modular method” that is inspired by the
Wiles’ proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem;
introductions to the method can be found in
[
19
]
and
[
25
]
.
The proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem
rests on three major pillars that we now recall.

•

Modularity of semistable elliptic curves over the rationals due to Wiles
[
31
]
.

•

The irreducibility of Galois representations attached
to the
\ell
-torsion of elliptic curves over the rationals,
for sufficiently large primes
\ell
,
due to Mazur
[
17
]
.

•

Ribet’s Level Lowering Theorem
[
22
]
.

We shall need (somewhat weak) generalisations of such results to the context
of elliptic curves over totally real fields.
In particular, we need the following theorem proved in
[
11
, Theorem 5]
.

Theorem 7

(Freitas, Le Hung and Siksek)
.

Let
L
be a totally real field. There are at most
finitely many
\overline{L}
-isomorphism
classes of elliptic curves over
L
that are non-modular.

The following theorem, stated in
[
12
, Theorem 7]
, is a summary of level lowering theorems
due to Fujiwara, Jarvis and Rajaei, specialised to the context of
modular elliptic curves.

Theorem 8
.

Let
L
be a totally real number field,
and
F/L
an elliptic curve of conductor
\mathcal{N}
. Let
\ell

be a rational prime, and write
\overline{\rho}_{F,\ell}

for the mod
\ell
representation attached to
F
.
For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
,
let
\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}}
be
the discriminant of a local minimal model for
F
at
\mathfrak{q}
. Let

(6)

\mathcal{M}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\mid\mid\mathcal{N}\\
\ell\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}})\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{q},\qquad\mathfrak{N}:=\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{M}}.

Suppose the following

(i)

\ell\geq 5
, the ramification index
e(\mathfrak{l}/\ell)<\ell-1
for all
\mathfrak{l}\mid\ell
, and
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})^{+}\not\subseteq L
;

(ii)

is modular;

(iii)

\overline{\rho}_{F,\ell}
is irreducible;

(iv)

is semistable at all
\mathfrak{l}\mid\ell
;

(v)

\ell\mid v_{\mathfrak{l}}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{l}})

\mathfrak{l}\mid\ell

Then, there is a Hilbert eigenform
\mathfrak{f}
of parallel weight

and some prime ideal

\overline{\rho}_{F,\ell}\sim\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{f},\lambda}

Remark 7
.

Following the notation of Theorem

, suppose that

\overline{\rho}_{F,\ell}\sim\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{f},\lambda}
. Let

\mathfrak{q}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
By comparing the
traces of the images of Frobenius at
\mathfrak{q}
in

(1)

If

(2)

If
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\ell\mathfrak{N}
, and

, then
\pm(\Norm_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{q})+1)\equiv a_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathfrak{f})\pmod{\lambda}

The following result by Freitas and Siksek in
[

This theorem makes essential use of Merel’s celebrated
uniform boundedness theorem
[

.

Theorem 9
.

Let
L
be a Galois totally real field. There is an effective constant

depending only on

\ell>C_{L}

4.
A Key Proposition and the Proof of Theorem
3

In this section, we state a key result towards
the proof of Theorem
3
. This is Proposition

below, which in essence asserts the existence
of a suitable Frey curve for the perfect powers problem
for elliptic divisibility sequences.
We also show that this proposition implies Theorem

,
making use of the theorems in the previous section.
We complete the proof of Theorem

in

.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve given by an integral

.
We suppose that

satisfy hypotheses

Let

Thus we may write

depends only on
E
and
P

Finally, by (b),

is a prime of multiplicative

.
Moreover, as

, we have that
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\ell
.
It follows from Remark

(15)

\pm(\Norm_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})+1)\;\equiv\;a_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{f})\pmod{\lambda}.

Lemma 12

,
z_{2}\mathcal{O}_{L}
,
z_{3}\mathcal{O}_{L}

Write

We let

denote the class group of

We denote by
[\mathfrak{a}]
the class in

of an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}

Minkowski’s Theorem asserts that the classes

with

Thus there is some (integral) ideal

of

6.
Proof of Theorem
4

In this section we give precise statements of the three
conjectures mentioned in the introduction, and then we use
these to prove Theorem
4
.
The main difference between Theorems
3

and
4
is that in the latter the Frey curve
F

is defined over a number field
L
that is not totally real.
Our strategy follows closely arguments of
Şengün and Siksek
[
7
]
,
and of Ţurcaş
[
8
]
, who proved versions
of Fermat’s Last Theorem over certain general number fields,
conditional on Serre’s modularity conjecture
and Serre’s uniformity conjecture.

The first conjecture we need
is a weak version of Serre’s modularity conjecture
over number fields. We recommend Sections 2 and 3 of
[
7
]

for an explanation of mod
\ell
modular forms over number fields
and their Hecke operators, and further details of the
terminology appearing in the statement of the conjecture.

Conjecture 15
.

Let
L
be a number field and write
G_{L}=\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/L)
.
Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime, and
let
\overline{\rho}:G_{L}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
be an odd,
irreducible, continuous representation with Serre conductor
\mathfrak{N}

(prime-to-
\ell

part of its Artin conductor) and trivial character (prime-to-
\ell
part of

{\det}(\overline{\rho})
).
Assume that
\ell
is unramified in
L
and that
\overline{\rho}|_{G_{K_{\mathfrak{l}}}}
arises from a finite-flat group scheme over the completion

L_{\mathfrak{l}}
for every prime
\mathfrak{l}\mid\ell
. Then there is a (weight
2
) mod
\ell
eigenform
\theta
over
L
of level

\mathfrak{N}
such that for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\ell\mathfrak{N}
, we have

\theta(T_{\mathfrak{q}})=\Tr(\overline{\rho}({\Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}})).

The second conjecture we need is widely known as Serre’s uniformity
conjecture (see, for example
[
3
, Conjecture 1.3]
).

Conjecture 16
.

Let
L
be a number field. Then there is a constant
C_{L}
, such that
for any non-CM elliptic curve
E/L
and any prime
\ell>C_{L}
,
the mod
\ell
representation
\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}

is surjective.

The third conjecture we need is a special case
of what is widely known as the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture.
A proof of this for CM fields has recently been
announced by Boxer, Calegari, Gee, Newton and Thorne

[
4
, Theorem A]
.

Conjecture 17
.

Let
L
be a number field.
Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a weight
2
complex eigenform over
L

of level
\mathfrak{N}
, and let
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{N}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
Then
\lvert a_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{f})\rvert\leq 2\sqrt{\Norm_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})}
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Most steps in the proof of Theorem
3
carry on
unchanged to the setting of Theorem
4
.
In particular, Proposition
11

continues to hold, with the only difference being that the number field

given in (
13
) may no longer be totally real.
We explain the derivation of Theorem
4

from Proposition
11
assuming
the three aforementioned conjectures.

Again, we need to show that the exponent

is bounded.
Let

,
\kappa
,
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathfrak{p}
be as in Proposition
11
. There is currently no analogue of the level lowering
Theorem
8
in the setting of general number fields.
Instead we need to rely on Serre’s modularity conjecture
(Conjecture

) which combines the roles
of Theorems
7
and
8
,
and which we apply to the mod
\ell
representation

\overline{\rho}_{F,\ell}

Conjecture
15
requires that

coming from Theorem

with the bound

coming from Conjecture

for this we need that

.
As before,

is semistable at all

and satisfies

7.
Final Remarks

Effectivity

Theorem
3
is not effective, as it relies on
the theorem of Everest, Reynolds and Stevens (Theorem
2
)
which in turn relies on Faltings’ theorem.
It is however natural to ask if the bound for the
exponent
\ell
yielded by the
proof of Theorem
3
is effective.
Alas, it is not, because the finiteness of the
set of non-modular
j
-invariants (Theorem
7
)
is a consequence of Faltings’ theorem.
If we however assume modularity of elliptic curves over

L
then the bound on
\ell
is effectively computable.
The constant
\kappa
in Proposition
11

ultimately comes from Silverman’s primitive divisor
theorem (Theorem
1
) which has
been made effective
[
30
]
. The paper

[
14
]
gives an effective
strategy for computing the constant
C_{L}

in Theorem
9
. Finally, the Hilbert modular
forms appearing in the proof of Theorem
3
,
and their Hecke eigenvalues,
are algorithmically computable
[
9
]
.

The non-integrality hypothesis

The non-integrality hypothesis (assumption (i)
in Theorem
3
) is essential to our argument,
as it had been in previous work of Reynolds
[
21
]

and Alfaraj
[
1
]
. Indeed, this assumption
allows us to conclude the existence of a fixed
multiplicative prime
\mathfrak{p}
that is independent of
\ell
,
and this allowed us to bound
\ell
in the final
step of the proof of Theorem
3
.
If
P
is integral, then
B_{1}=1=1^{\ell}
and hence

\ell
is unbounded. It seems that the non-integrality
hypothesis cannot be eliminated with some major new idea.

Keywords:
Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations, stochastic homogenization, continuum percolation, convex variational analysis, entropy, min-max theorems

Abstract: We prove homogenization properties of random Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations on continuum percolation clusters, almost surely w.r.t.
the law of the environment when the origin belongs to the unbounded component in the continuum.
Here, the viscosity term carries a degenerate matrix, the Hamiltonian is convex and coercive w.r.t. the degenerate matrix and the underlying environment is non-elliptic and its law
is non-stationary w.r.t. the translation group. We do not assume uniform ellipticity inside the percolation cluster, nor any finite-range dependence (i.i.d.) assumption on the percolation models
and the effective Hamiltonian admits a variational formula which reflects some key properties of percolation.
The proof is inspired by a method of Kosygina-Rezakhanlou-Varadhan
[
KRV06
]
developed for the case of HJB equations with constant viscosity and uniformly coercive Hamiltonian
in a stationary, ergodic and elliptic random environment. In the non-stationary and non-elliptic set up, we leverage the coercivity property of the underlying Hamiltonian as well as a relative entropy structure (both being intrinsic properties of HJB, in any framework)
and make use of the random geometry of continuum percolation.

1.
Introduction

Consider a continuum percolation model resulting from the realizations
\omega\in\Omega
of a point process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
. The translation group
\{\tau_{x}\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
acts on
\Omega

and it is natural to assume that the underlying law
\mathbb{P}
of the point process is invariant and ergodic
under this action. If there is a unique
infinite
unbounded component

{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)
containing the origin
0
, for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,
then the event
\Omega_{0}=\{0\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\}
has strictly positive probability, allowing us
to define the
conditional probability

(1.1)

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(\cdot\,|\,\Omega_{0}).

Note that, because of conditioning on
0
being in the infinite cluster,
the probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{0}
, in contrast to its unconditional counterpart
\mathbb{P}
, is
not
invariant under the action of
\{\tau_{x}\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

For
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, we now consider the
Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman
(HJB) equation on the infinite cluster
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)

(1.2)

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathrm{div}\Big(a\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\omega\big)\,\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big)+H\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla u_{\varepsilon},\omega\Big),\qquad\text{in }(0,T)\times\varepsilon\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\\

with an initial condition
f(\cdot)
– we refer to (
2.10
) for a precise formulation. Here the diffusion coefficient

a(x,\omega)=a(\tau_{x}\omega)
is
degenerate elliptic
in the sense that the support
of
x\mapsto a(\tau_{x}\omega)
is contained in the closure of the infinite cluster
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}(\omega)
,
the Hamiltonian
H(x,p,\omega)=H(p,\tau_{x}\omega)
is convex
and coercive in
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
w.r.t. the semi-norm induced by the degenerate matrix
a
and the initial condition
f
is uniformly continuous.
These conditions are natural and they guarantee that, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0

and
T>0
, there is actually a unique viscosity solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of (
1.2
).

Given this background, the goal of the present article is to develop a method for studying
homogenization of
u_{\varepsilon}
almost surely w.r.t. the conditional probability
\mathbb{P}_{0}
–
we will show that,
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-almost surely and as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

u_{\varepsilon}\to u_{\mathrm{hom}}
with
{u_{\mathrm{hom}}}
solving the homogenized equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{\mathrm{hom}}=\overline{H}(\nabla u_{\mathrm{hom}}),&\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u_{\mathrm{hom}}(0,x)=f(x),&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

The
effective Hamiltonian

\overline{H}
admits a variational representation

(1.3)

\overline{H}(\theta)=\inf_{G}\bigg(\esssup\limits_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\big(a(G{+}\theta)\big)+H(G{+}\theta)\bigg]\bigg)\qquad\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and determines the effective equation
u_{\mathrm{hom}}
as a viscosity solution

u_{\mathrm{hom}}(t,x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[f(y)-t\mathcal{I}(\frac{y-x}{t})\Big],\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\mathcal{I}(y):=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}[\langle\theta,y\rangle-\overline{H}(\theta)],

we refer to Theorem
2.1
for a precise statement, and to Corollary
2.2
for an application to an
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s. large deviation principle for a degenerate diffusion with a random drift and on a percolation cluster.

The study of homogenization of HJB equations was initiated in a fundamental work of Lions, Papanicolaou and Varadhan
[
LPV87
]
which treated first order Hamilton-Jacobi equations in the periodic setting – that is, when
a\equiv 0
and
H(\cdot+z,\cdot)\equiv H(\cdot,\cdot)
for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Since then, there have been very important works in the field by Souganidis
[
So99
]
, Ishii
[
I99
]
, Evans
[
E92
]
, Rezakhanlou-Tarver
[
RT00
]
, Lions-Souganidis
[
LS05
,
LS10
]
, Kosygina-Rezakhanlou-Varadhan
[
KRV06
]
(see also Kosygina-Varadhan
[
KV08
]
for time-dependent case), Armstrong-Souganidis
[
AS12
,
AS13
]
and Armstrong-Tran
[
AT14
,
AT15
]
. While particular conditions vary from paper to paper, the main assumptions in these works on the Hamiltonian
H
involve convexity and super-linearity in
p
, some regularity in
p
and
x
as well as uniform continuity on the initial condition
f
. In all these works, homogenization holds in an almost sure (i.e.,
quenched
) sense w.r.t. the law of the random environment, which is assumed to be stationary and ergodic under the translation group. For aforementioned reasons, the latter framework does not cover the conditional measure
\mathbb{P}_{0}
, which is relevant
for studying almost sure behavior of (
1.2
) on percolation clusters, where homogenization of elliptic equations of the form
-\mathrm{div}(a(\nabla u+e))=0
in a reversible and discrete framework have been studied quite extensively in the recent years
[
SS04
,
MP07
,
BB07
,
PRS15
,
LNO15
,
AD18
]
.

In this context and to the best of our knowledge, the present paper is the first instance where homogenization of HJB equations have been studied on percolation clusters – a fundamental class of models for studying statistical mechanics of random media. As we will see, their inherent properties like
non-translation-invariance
and
non-ellipticity
pervade through the sequel (including in the variational formula (
1.3
) of the homogenized limit) and
manifest into fundamental difficulties – we refer to Section
2.3
for the main ideas of the proof. Also, the current method does not require any

finite-range dependence
(i.e. i.i.d.) assumption on the percolation models which are allowed to have long-range correlations, we refer to Appendix
A
for concrete examples of such models. Before turning to formal statements of the main results (cf. Section
2
), it is instructive to first provide the precise mathematical layout of the point processes.

1.1.
Point processes and Palm measures.

1.1.1.
Point processes.

Fix an integer
d\geq 2
, and let
\Omega
be the space of all locally finite
subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We denote by
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to its Borel

\sigma
-algebra. The Lebesgue measure will be denoted by
\lambda
(or by

\lambda_{d}
when we need to emphasize on the dimension). We endow
\Omega
with
the smallest
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}
that makes the maps

\omega\mapsto\#(\omega\cap A)
measurable for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where

\#(\omega\cap A)
denotes the cardinality of
\omega\cap A
. A

Point process
is a probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on

(\Omega,\mathcal{G})
.

On
\Omega
, the group of translations

(\tau_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
acts naturally as

\tau_{x}\omega:=\omega-x=\big\{y{-}x:y\in\omega\big\}.

We say a
point process is stationary if

(1.4)

\mathbb{P}\circ\tau_{x}=\mathbb{P}\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

A stationary point process is ergodic with respect to
(\tau_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}

if

(1.5)

\forall\,A\in\mathcal{G}\ \forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\qquad\tau_{x}A=A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}.

We also define the
intensity measure
of
\mathbb{P}
as the measure on

(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
given by

(1.6)

\Theta(A):=\int\#(\omega\cap A)\mathbb{P}({\rm d}\omega)=\mathbb{E}[\#(\omega\cap A)].

Here and throughout the sequel,
\mathbb{E}
will denote expectation w.r.t.
\mathbb{P}
. Notice that when
\mathbb{P}
is stationary and
\Theta
is locally finite, then there exists
some
\zeta\in(0,\infty)
such that
\Theta=\zeta\lambda
. We call
\zeta

the
intensity
of the point process.

1.1.2.
Palm measures.

We now turn to the definition of
Palm measures
, which, on an intuitive level, formalizes the idea of the
distribution of a Point process conditioned on containing some fixed point

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. First, we define the measure
\mathfrak{C}
on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\Omega
as

(1.7)

\mathfrak{C}(A):=\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{x\in\omega}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A}(x,\tau_{x}\omega)\big]\quad\text{ for }A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{G}.

The measure
\mathfrak{C}
can be decomposed when
\mathbb{P}
is stationary: Indeed,
by
[
SW08
, Theorem 3.3.1]
, if
\mathbb{P}
is a stationary point process (i.e., if (
1.4
) holds) with
intensity
\zeta\in(0,\infty)
, then there exists a unique measure
\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({0}})}}
on

(\Omega,\mathcal{G})
such that

(1.8)

\mathfrak{C}=\zeta\lambda\otimes\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({0}})}}.

We
call
\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({0}})}}
the
Palm measure
corresponding to
\mathbb{P}
. It can be seen as
the distribution of a point process conditioned on containing the origin (see

[
LP18
, Proposition 9.5]
). In particular,
\mathbb{P}^{0}(0\notin\omega)=0
(see

[
LP18
, Eq. (9.7)]
. We can define more generally

\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x}})}}:=\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({0}})}}\circ\tau_{x}\qquad\mbox{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The aforementioned decomposition allows us to disintegrate

\mathbb{P}
in terms of
(\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x}})}})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
: Indeed, by
[
SW08
, Theorem 3.3.3]
, if
\mathbb{P}
is
a stationary point process with intensity
\zeta\in(0,\infty)
, then for all

f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega)
,
\omega\mapsto\sum_{x\in\omega}f(x,\omega)
is
measurable, and

(1.9)

\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{x\in\omega}f(x,\omega)\big]=\zeta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({0}})}}[f(x,\tau_{-x}\omega)]{\rm d}x=\zeta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x}})}}[f(x,\omega)]{\rm d}x.

Similarly, one can define the
n
-fold Palm distribution
\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}}})}}

for
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In this case, we have the equality

(1.10)

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x_{1}\cdots,x_{n}\in\omega}^{\neq}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\omega)\bigg]=\zeta^{n}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\mathbb{E}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}}})}}[f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\omega)]{\rm d}x_{1}\cdots{\rm d}x_{n}

for
f\in L^{1}((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\times\Omega)
, where the sign
\neq
above the sum
indicates that the sum is taken over pairwise distinct elements. Also,
\mathbb{E}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}}})}}
stands for expectation
w.r.t. the
n
-fold Palm distribution
\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}}})}}
.

1.1.3.
Assumptions on the point process
\mathbb{P}
.

For any
\omega\in\Omega
, which denotes a locally finite point set in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define a random domain
\mathcal{C}(\omega)
, which is an open set

(1.11)

\mathcal{C}(\omega):=\bigcup_{x\in\omega}B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
B_{r}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y{-}x|<r\}
denotes an open ball centered at
x
of radius
r>0
.
The set
\mathcal{C}(\omega)
can be decomposed into a
disjoint union of connected components. If there is a unique unbounded
connected component, then this component is denoted by

\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\subset\mathcal{C}(\omega)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Its boundary will
be denoted by
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
, with
\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{C}_{\infty})
denoting the interior.
Moreover, we define

(1.12)

\Omega_{0}:=\big\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\text{ exists, }0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\big\}\subset\Omega.

If
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})>0
(which we will assume in condition
(P3)
stated below),
then we can define the conditional probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{0}
on
\Omega_{0}
via

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\cdot):=\mathbb{P}(\,\cdot\,|\Omega_{0}),\quad\text{viz.\ }\mathbb{P}_{0}(A)=\frac{\mathbb{P}(A)}{\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})}\text{ for all }A\in\mathcal{G}\cap{\Omega_{0}}.

By the openness and the connectedness of
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
, every two points

x,y\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
can be connected by a curve in
C^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{d})

such that the interior distance
{\rm d}_{\omega}
is defined on
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
via

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{\omega}(x,y)=\inf\bigg\{\>\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{r}(s)|{\rm d}s\,\,\colon

\displaystyle\;r\in C^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ r(0)=x,\ r(1)=y,

\displaystyle\;\text{and }r(s)\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\text{ for all }s\in[0,1]\bigg\}.

To state the condition

1.15
below we define
n(\omega,e)\in\mathbb{N}
for all
e\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with

|e|=1
and
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
to be the “successive arrivals” of
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
along a certain direction
e
, i.e., we set

(1.13)

\displaystyle n(\omega,e)=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:ke\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}.

We are now ready to state the following assumptions on the point process
\mathbb{P}
:

(P1)

\mathbb{P}
is stationary ergodic with respect to
(\tau_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. Moreover,
\mathbb{P}
is also stationary ergodic with respect to
\tau_{e}

for all
e\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
|e|_{1}=1
(namely, any
A\in\mathcal{G}
such that

\tau_{e}A=A
satisfies
\mathbb{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}
).

(P2)

Recall the definition of
\Theta
from (
1.6
). Then for any compact set
A\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\Theta(A)<\infty
. In
particular,
\Theta=\zeta\lambda
for some

\zeta\in(0,\infty)
.

(P3)

With the above definition of
\mathcal{C}(\omega)
and

\Omega_{0}
we assume
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})>0
, i.e. with
with positive
\mathbb{P}
-probability, the set
\mathcal{C}(\omega)
has
a unique open, unbounded and
connected component
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
containing the
origin
0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(P4)

There are constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that for each
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.14)

\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x,y}})}}({\rm d}_{\omega}(x,y)\geq c_{0}|x-y|_{\infty};0,x,y\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})\leq c_{1}{\rm e}^{-c_{2}|x-y|_{\infty}}.

where
\mathbb{P}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({x,y}})}}
refers to the two-fold Palm distribution defined above (
1.10
).

(P5)

The FKG-inequality is satisfied, i.e., if
A_{1},A_{2}\subset\Omega
are
increasing events (meaning that if
\omega_{1}\in A_{i}
and

\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{2}
, then
\omega_{2}\in A_{i}
for
i=1,2
), then

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{1}\cap A_{2})\geq{\mathbb{P}}(A_{1}){\mathbb{P}}(A_{2})
.

(P6)

We let
\mathfrak{v}_{e}(\omega):=n(\omega,e)e
and assume that
there exist constants
c_{3},c_{4}>0

such that

(1.15)

\forall\,\varrho>0\ \forall\,e\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{ with }|e|_{1}=1:\quad\mathbb{P}_{0}\big(\,|\mathfrak{v}_{e}(\omega)|>\varrho\,\big)\leq c_{3}{\rm e}^{-c_{4}\varrho}.

Condition
(P1)
-
(P3)
are self-explanatory,

1.14
guarantees that
{\rm d}_{\omega}
is
comparable to the Euclidean distance with high probability, while
(P5)
is a standard monotonicity inequality satisfied by many models.
Since
\mathbb{P}
is ergodic with respect to
\tau_{e}
(by
(P1)
) and since
{\mathbb{P}}(0\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})>0
,
by the Poincaré recurrence theorem (cf.
[
P89
, Sec, 2.3]
) we have
n(\omega,e)<\infty
.
Then
1.15
implies that moving along the coordinate axes has
good recurrence properties and that
\mathfrak{v}_{e}
possesses all moments under
\mathbb{P}_{0}
.
These assumptions are natural and are satisfied by many well-studied models – we refer to Appendix
A
.

2.
Main results

In Section
2.1
, we will introduce the equation (
1.2
) in a precise form and record the necessary assumptions. In Section
2.2
, we will announce our main results, while in Section
2.3
we will outline the principal ingredients of the proof.

2.1.
The HJB equation.

We now state the assumptions on the diffusion coefficient
a
, the Hamiltonian
H
and the initial value
f
appearing in (
1.2
), which guarantee existence and uniqueness of a viscosity solution (cf. Proposition
3.3
).
Stating these assumptions require some further notation.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{d}
the space of
d\times d
symmetric matrices. There is a natural partial order in
\mathcal{S}_{d}
: we say that for
A,B\in\mathcal{S}_{d},A\leq B
if
B-A
is positive semidefinite, i.e., all its eigenvalues are nonnegative. For any symmetric, positive semidefinite matrix
a
(which will be defined below in
(F1)
) denote by

\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}

the unique (symmetric and
positive semidefinite) matrix such that

a=\frac{1}{2}\sigma\sigma\qquad\mbox{on }\Omega_{0}.

We also define the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{a}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{a(\omega)}
as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle_{a}:=\langle a(\omega)x,y\rangle=\langle x,a(\omega)y\rangle\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad\mbox{which defines a semi-norm}

\displaystyle\|x\|_{a}:=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_{a}}.

We impose the following assumptions on
a
,
H
and
f
:

(F1)

The matrix
a:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}_{d}
is positive semidefinite, and

a(x,\omega):=a(\tau_{x}\omega)

defines a stationary process with respect to
\{\tau_{x}\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. The restriction of
a

to
\Omega_{0}
(defined in (
1.12
)) satisfies the following:
there exists
c_{5}\in(0,\infty)
and a measurable function
\xi:\Omega_{0}\to(0,\infty)
such that
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s.

(2.2)

\xi(\omega)|x|^{2}\leq\langle a(\omega)x,x\rangle\leq c_{5}|x|^{2}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Furthermore, there exists
\delta>0
,
\alpha>1+\delta
,
\gamma>d
such that

(2.3)

\mathbb{E}_{0}\big[\xi(\omega)^{-\chi}\big]<\infty,

where

(2.4)

\chi=\chi(\alpha,\gamma,\delta):=\frac{\alpha}{2}\max\left\{\frac{1+\delta}{\alpha-(1+\delta)},\frac{\gamma}{\alpha-1}\right\}.

For
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s.
\omega
, the maps

x\ni\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\sigma(x,\omega):=\sigma(\tau_{x}\,\cdot\,)\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
and

x\ni\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathrm{div}\!\;a(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

are Lipschitz continuous, with Lipschitz constant independent on
\omega
. Moreover,

{\rm supp}(a)\subset\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}

and
|\mathrm{div}\!\;a|
is uniformly bounded. Since
a
is Lipschitz, we can assume that
x\to\xi(\tau_{x}\omega)
is also Lipschitz by taking the minimum eigenvalue of
a
.

(F2)

The Hamiltonian
H:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies for each
\omega\in\Omega
that
p\to H(\pm p,\omega)
is convex. Moreover, there are constants
c_{6},\dots,c_{9}>0
such that for all

(p,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{0}
,

(2.5)

c_{6}\|p\|_{a}^{\alpha}-c_{7}\leq H(p,\omega)\leq c_{8}\|p\|_{a}^{\alpha}+c_{9},

and
H(\cdot,\omega)\equiv 0
outside
\Omega_{0}
. Here,
\alpha>1+\delta
and
\delta>0
are arbitrary (but specified in (
2.3
)).
Equivalently, for
\alpha^{\prime}:=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}
and constants
c_{10},\dots,c_{13}
,

(2.6)

c_{10}\|q\|_{a}^{\alpha^{\prime}}-c_{11}\leq L(q,\omega)\leq c_{12}\|q\|_{a}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+c_{13},

where
L(q,\omega):=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}[\langle p,q\rangle_{a}-H(p,\omega)]
. In particular,
L(\cdot,\omega)\equiv 0
outside
\Omega_{0}
.

(F3)

The map
x\mapsto H(x,p,\omega):=H(p,\tau_{x}\omega)
defines a stationary process with
respect to translations. Moreover, there are constants
c_{14},c_{15},c_{16}>0
such that for any
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
,
x,y\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle|H(x,p,\omega)-H(y,p,\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq(c_{14}|p|^{\alpha}+c_{15})|x-y|,

(2.8)

\displaystyle|H(x,p,\omega)-H(x,q,\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{16}(|p|+|q|+1)^{\alpha-1}|p-q|.

(F4)

The initial condition
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is uniformly
continuous. In particular, for any
\delta>0
, there exists some
K_{\delta}>0

such that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

To define the notion of viscosity solutions, we need some further notation. First, we recall the definition of the upper and lower semicontinuous envelopes. For a set

Definition 3.1
.

Let

and

be a locally bounded function. We define the upper semicontinuous envelope

as

The lower semicontinuous envelope is defined as

.

It follows directly from the definition that

. Moreover,
u_{*}\in\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{V})
while

.

Definition 3.2

(Viscosity sub/super-solutions)
.

Let

.

•

We say that a locally bounded function

•

Similarly, we say a locally bounded function

is a viscosity supersolution of (

if for all
(s,y)\in V

Lemma 3.12
.

Given any uniformly continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, there exists a sequence
(f_{k})_{k}\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
f_{k}\to f
uniformly.

Proof.

First, we check that
f
can be approximated by Lipschitz functions. An explicit construction is given by

f_{k}(x):=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{f(y)+k|x-y|\}.

Since
f_{k}
is the infimum of
k
-Lipschitz functions over a convex set, then
f_{k}
is also
k
-Lipschitz. Clearly
f_{k}\leq f
. To verify that
f_{k}\to f
uniformly, we note that

f(x)-f_{k}(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f(x)-f(y)-k|x-y|].

For each
\delta>0
, let
K_{\delta}>0
such that
|f(x)-f(x)|\leq K_{\delta}|x-y|+\delta
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle f(x)-f_{k}(x)\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}[(K_{\delta}-k)|x-y|]+\delta.

Therefore, for every
\delta>0
, if
k>K_{\delta}
, we have
f(x)-f_{k}(x)\leq\delta
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
\delta
is arbitrary, this shows that
f
can be approximated by Lipschitz functions. On the other hand, any Lipschitz function can be approximated by functions in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by mollification. ∎

Lemma 3.13
.

If the right-hand side is infinite, the claim is trivial. Otherwise, note that it is enough to show the inequality

4.
Correctors

Given any
\delta>0
, we start this section by defining the class of gradients

and the corresponding “correctors”

\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
be the
class of functions
G:\Omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the following properties:

\bullet

L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
-boundedness
: The following inequalities hold:

(4.1)

\|G\|_{L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})}<\infty,

and

(4.2)

\esssup_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(G+\theta))+H(G+\theta)\right]<\infty.

\bullet

Curl-free property on the cluster
: Given any
G:\Omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with a slight abuse of notation we will continue to write

G:\Omega_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\mbox{with}\quad G(\omega,x)=G(\tau_{x}\omega).

Now, for
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, we require that
G
is curl-free, meaning
\nabla\times G(\omega,\cdot)=0
on
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
, or simply, for
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, we have

(4.3)

\int_{\mathrm{C}}G(\omega,\cdot)\,\cdot{\rm d}\mathbf{r}=0

for every rectifiable simple closed path
\mathrm{C}
on
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
. For any
G
satisfying (
4.3
) we define
V_{G}:\Omega_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

(4.4)

V_{G}(\omega,x):=\int_{0\leadsto x}G(\omega,\cdot)\,\,\cdot{\rm d}\mathbf{r},

where
0\leadsto x
is any piecewise smooth curve contained in
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
(and 0 when
x\notin\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
). Note that the choice of the smooth curve is irrelevant, thanks to (
4.3
).

\bullet

Zero induced mean:

\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{0}

4.1.
Controlling directional growth.

The main result of this section, Theorem
4.3
stated below provides a control on the growth of
V_{G}
along coordinate directions.
For this purpose, we fix a unit coordinate vector
e
and for
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, define the
successive arrivals

(n_{k}(\omega))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

of the cluster recursively as follows:
Recall (
1.13
) and define

n_{1}(\omega)=n(\omega,e),\quad\mbox{and for $k\geq 1$ we set}\quad n_{k+1}(\omega):=\min\{l\in\mathbb{N}:l>n_{k}(\omega),le\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
e
be any unit coordinate vector. If
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
, then
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s.,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{|V_{G}(n_{k}(\omega)e,\omega)|}{k}=0.

The proof of Theorem
4.3
will need the following result.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any unit coordinate vector
e
, recall that we denote the first successive
arrival in direction
e
by
\mathfrak{v}_{e}=\mathfrak{v}_{e}(\omega)=n(\omega,e)e
. Then for any
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{0}|V_{G}(\mathfrak{v}_{e},\cdot)|<\infty
. More precisely, there is a constant

C=C(d,\delta,\mathbb{P}_{0})
such that for any
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
,

\mathbb{E}_{0}|V_{G}(\mathfrak{v}_{e},\cdot)|\leq C\|G\|_{L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})}
.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
(assuming Proposition
4.4
).

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, set
x_{0}=0
and
x_{j}=n_{j}e
for
1\leq j\leq k
. We
choose a path
0\leadsto x_{k}
from 0 to
x_{k}
contained in

{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)
such that, for some
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{k}=1
and

r:[0,1]\rightarrow(x_{0}\leadsto x_{k})
, it holds
r(t_{j})=x_{j}
. Then by the
definition of
V_{G}
in (
4.4
),

\displaystyle V_{G}(n_{k}e,\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{0}\leadsto x_{k}}G(r,\omega){\rm d}r=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\int_{x_{j}\leadsto x_{j+1}}G(r,\omega){\rm d}r=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\int_{0\leadsto(x_{j+1}-x_{j})}G(r,\tau_{x_{j}}\omega){\rm d}r

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}V_{G}(x_{j+1}-x_{j},\tau_{x_{j}}\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}V_{G}((n_{j}(\omega)-n_{j-1}(\omega))e,\tau_{n_{j-1}e}\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}V_{G}(n_{1}(\sigma_{e}^{j}(\omega))e,\sigma_{e}^{j}(\omega)).

Recall (
3.18
) for the definition of the induced shift and Definition (
4.4
) for that of the corrector
V_{G}
. We define the function
F(\omega)=V_{G}(n(\omega,e)e,\omega)
, so that

(4.7)

\displaystyle V_{G}(n_{k}(\omega)e,\omega)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}F\circ\sigma_{e}^{j}(\omega).

From Proposition
3.5
, the induced shift
\sigma_{e}
is
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-preserving and ergodic. Furthermore, from Proposition
4.4
, the function
F\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
. Then by Birkhoff’s Ergodic Theorem,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}F\circ\sigma_{e}^{j}(\omega)}{k}=\mathbb{E}_{0}[V_{G}(n(\omega,e)e,\omega)]=0,

where the last equality comes from the induced mean-zero property (
4.5
) of
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
. ∎

To show Proposition
4.4
we require first a lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\ell=\ell(\omega)={\rm d}_{\omega}(0,\mathfrak{v}_{e}(\omega))
be the graph distance between
0
and
\mathfrak{v}_{e}=n(\omega,e)e
. Then there exist constants
a
,
C>0
such that for any
t>0
,

(4.9)

\mathbb{P}_{0}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq n_{1}(\omega)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}{\rm d}_{\omega}(0,se)>t\bigg)\leq C{\rm e}^{-at}.

In particular,

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\ell>t)<C{\rm e}^{-at}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. For
t>0
we write
t_{\varepsilon}:=\lfloor\varepsilon t\rfloor
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq n_{1}}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}{\rm d}_{\omega}(0,se)>t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(n_{1}(\omega)\geq t\varepsilon\right)+\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\varepsilon}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}{\rm d}_{\omega}(0,se)>t\right).

By
1.15
, the claim follows once we prove that the second term goes to zero at an exponential rate. This probability is bounded above by
\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor t\varepsilon\rfloor}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{i-1\leq s\leq i}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}{\rm d}_{\omega}(0,se)>t\right)
. Since the number of summands is growing only polynomially in
t
, it suffices to show that each summand there decays exponentially in
t
. We will proceed as follows:

We define

m:=\min\{l\in\mathbb{N}:l>t_{\varepsilon},\,-le\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\},\quad A_{x,y}=\{{\rm d}_{\omega}(x,y)\geq t/2,\,x,y\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\}.

Now we observe that on the event
\{\sup_{i-1\leq s\leq i}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}{\rm d}_{\omega}(0,se)>t\}
, one of the following cases must hold:

•

m>2t_{\varepsilon}
, or

•

at least one of the points
le
with
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
|l|\leq 2t_{\varepsilon}
is in
{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}
and for some
i-1\leq s\leq i
,
\max\{{\rm d}_{\omega}(0,-le),{\rm d}_{\omega}(-le,se)\}\geq t/2
.

In the first of the two cases above we have
|\mathfrak{v}_{-e}\circ\sigma_{-e}^{m}|>t_{\varepsilon}
for at least one
m=1,...,t_{\varepsilon}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\big(\sup_{i-1\leq s\leq i}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\big\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\big\}{\rm d}_{\omega}(0,se)>t\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{t_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\big(\sigma_{-e}^{m}(\{|\mathfrak{v}_{-e}|\geq t_{\varepsilon}\})\big)+\sum_{\ell=t_{\varepsilon}}^{2t_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\big(\exists i-1\leq s\leq i:A_{0,-le}\cup A_{-le,se}\big).

By
1.15
, the probabilities of the events in the first sum are equal and exponentially small.
The second sum is bounded by

t_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{P}_{0}(A_{0,-le})+\sum_{\ell=t_{\varepsilon}}^{2t_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\exists i-1\leq s\leq i:A_{-le,se}\right).

To bound the first term, we use (
1.10
) and
1.14
to obtain the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}(A_{0,-le})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(0\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\exists x\neq y\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega):|x|\leq\frac{1}{2},|y-le|\leq\frac{1}{2},{\rm d}_{\omega}(x,y)\geq\frac{t}{2}+1;0,x,y\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(0\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x,y\in\omega}^{\neq}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\bigg\{|x|\leq 1/2,|y-le|\leq 1/2,{\rm d}_{\omega}(x,y)\geq t/2+1;0,x,y\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\bigg\}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta^{2}}{\mathbb{P}(0\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{d}}\int_{[le-1/2,le+1/2]^{d}}\mathbb{P}^{x,y}\bigg({\rm d}_{\omega}(x,y)\geq\frac{t}{2}+1;0,x,y\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\bigg){\rm d}x{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-C^{\prime}t_{\varepsilon}}

for some constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
which are independent of
l
, and for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, with
\zeta
defined in (
(P2)
).
Following the same calculations as in the last display, we can also show that

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\exists i-1\leq s\leq i:A_{-le,se}\right)\leq C{\rm e}^{-C^{\prime}t_{\varepsilon}}

for constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
independent of
i
and
l
, for
\varepsilon>0
small enough.
After estimating the probabilities of all events by an exponential upper bound, from the unions we get another factor that is linear in
t
, which can be absorbed by the exponential bound for
t
large enough. Thus the proof of Lemma
4.5
is complete.

∎

Now we are ready to show Proposition
4.4
.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Let
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}(\omega)
be an enumeration of the balls that appear in the construction of
\mathscr{C}(\omega)
(recall
(P3)
). Then define the random variable

(4.10)

\displaystyle\tilde{{\rm d}}_{\omega}(x,y):=

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\text{ $\exists$ $(B_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathscr{B}$ such that $x\in B_{1},y\in B_{n}$, and}

\displaystyle\text{$B_{i-1}\cap B_{i}\neq\emptyset~\forall~1\leq i\leq n$ }\bigg\}

and set

(4.11)

\tilde{\ell}:=\tilde{{\rm d}}_{\omega}(0,\mathfrak{v}_{e}).

Note that there is some constant
c>0
such that for all
n>0
,

(4.12)

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tilde{\ell}>n\right)\leq{\rm e}^{-cn}.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
N_{j}:=\mathbb{Z}/2\cap[-j,j]^{d}
. We consider this set as a graph, where for
x,y\in N_{j}
,
x\sim y
iff
|x-y|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
. Note that if
\tilde{\ell}=j
, then there is a
nearest-neighbor path on
N_{j}
of length
k\leq 3^{d}j
such that for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
, the line segment between
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
is contained in the cluster. Thus, we write

\{\tilde{\ell}=j\}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{3^{d}j}\bigcup_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}}\bigg\{\tilde{\ell}=j\cap A(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})\bigg\},

where

(4.13)

\displaystyle A(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}):=

\displaystyle\bigg\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\text{ is a nearest-neighbor path on }N_{j},

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq i\leq k-1\text{ the line segment between }x_{i-1},x_{i}\text{ is inside }{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\bigg\}.

If
\tilde{\ell}=j
, then one can write for some nearest neighbor path
0=x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}
on
N_{j}
(
1\leq k\leq 3^{d}j
) such that the line segment between
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
is inside
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
for all
0\leq i\leq k-1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0\leadsto\mathfrak{v}_{e}}|G(\omega,\cdot)|\cdot{\rm d}\mathbf{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{0}^{1}|G(\tau_{x_{i}}\omega,t(x_{i+1}-x_{i}))|{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{|e|=1}\int_{0}^{1/2}|G(\tau_{x_{i}}\omega,te)|{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2(3^{d}j)\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\cap N_{j}:|x|\leq 3^{d}j}\sum_{|e|=1}\int_{0}^{1/2}|G(\tau_{x}\omega,te)|{\rm d}t.

Therefore,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}|V_{G}(\mathfrak{v}_{e},\cdot)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[\bigg|\int_{0\leadsto\mathfrak{v}_{e}}G(\omega,\cdot)\cdot{\rm d}\mathbf{r}\bigg|,\tilde{\ell}=j\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|e|=1}\sum_{x\in N_{j}:|x|\leq 3^{d}j}(3^{d}j)\int_{0}^{1/2}\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[|G(\tau_{x}\omega,te)|,x\leadsto x+e\subset\mathcal{C}_{\infty},\tilde{\ell}=j\bigg]{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|e|=1}\sum_{x\in N_{j}:|x|\leq 3^{d}j}(3^{d}j)\int_{0}^{1/2}\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[|G(\tau_{x}\omega,te)|^{1+\delta},x\leadsto x+e\subset\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigg]^{1/(1+\delta)}\mathbb{P}_{0}(\tilde{\ell}=j)^{\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}}{\rm d}t.

Since
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
, then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[|G(\omega,x)|^{1+\delta},x\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\right]\leq\|G\|_{L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})}.

As a consequence, (
4.14
) can be bounded by

C(d)\|G\|_{L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2}\mathbb{P}_{0}(\tilde{\ell}=j)^{\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}}\leq C(d,\delta,\mathbb{P}_{0})\|G\|_{L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})}

due to (
4.12
). This finishes the proof of the proposition.

∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
. Then for any unit coordinate vector
e
and
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s.,

\lim_{s\to\infty}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}\frac{|V_{G}(se,\omega)|}{s}=0.

Proof.

If
se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)
, then there exists
k\geq 0
such that
n_{k}(\omega)\leq s<n_{k+1}(\omega)
. Note that
k\nearrow\infty
as
s\nearrow\infty
. Then we have

\frac{|V_{G}(se,\omega)|}{s}\leq\frac{|V_{G}(n_{k}(\omega)e,\omega)|}{n_{k}(\omega)}+\frac{|V_{G}((s-n_{k}(\omega))e,\tau_{n_{k}(\omega)e}\omega)|}{n_{k}(\omega)}.

By the ergodic theorem (as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
) and
1.15
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{n_{k}(\omega)}{k}=\mathbb{E}_{0}[n_{1}]<\infty

\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s. This fact, together with Theorem
4.3
, allow us to deduce that the first term in the sum above goes to zero as
s\to\infty
. It remains to bound the second term. Note that it suffices to show that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{n_{k}(\omega)\leq s\leq n_{k+1}(\omega)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}\frac{|V_{G}((s-n_{k}(\omega))e,\tau_{n_{k}(\omega)e},\omega)|}{k}=0~\mathbb{P}_{0}\text{-}a.s.

Since
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
, it is enough to prove that

(4.15)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{n_{k}\leq s\leq n_{k+1}}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}\frac{{\rm d}_{\omega}(n_{k}(\omega)e,se)}{k}=0~\mathbb{P}_{0}\text{-}a.s.

By the Borel-Cantelli lemma, it suffices to verify that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.16)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{n_{k}(\omega)\leq s\leq n_{k+1}(\omega)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}{\rm d}_{\omega}(n_{k}(\omega)e,se)>k\varepsilon\right)<\infty.

Since
\mathbb{P}_{0}
is invariant under
\tau_{n_{k}e}
, the sum above is equal to

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq n_{1}(\omega)}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{se\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\}{\rm d}_{\omega}(0,se)>k\varepsilon\right).

By Lemma
4.5
, this sum is finite for each
\varepsilon>0
, concluding the proof.
∎

4.2.
Controlling density of growth.

The main result of this section is the following result:

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-almost all
\omega
,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(2r)^{d}}\int_{x\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega),|x|\leq r}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{|V_{G}(x,\omega)|\geq\varepsilon r\}{\rm d}x=0.

The proof of Proposition
4.7
consists of three main steps.

Step 1:
We start this section with a definition:
Given
K>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, we say that a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
belongs to
\mathscr{G}_{K,\varepsilon}(\omega)
for
\omega\in\Omega
if
x\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)
and

(4.18)

\displaystyle|V_{G}(x+te,\omega)-V_{G}(x,\omega)|\leq K+\varepsilon|t|

for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
e
is a unit coordinate vector such that
x+te\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)
.
We will use the following consequence of Corollary
4.6

in the sequel: for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\mathbb{P}(0\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})=\lim_{K\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(0\in\mathscr{G}_{K,\varepsilon})
.
For
k\in\{1,...,d\}
, let us also define

(4.19)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{r}^{k}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:|x|_{\infty}\leq r\},

which is the
k
-dimensional section of the
d
-dimensional box
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|_{\infty}\leq r\}
, and set

(4.20)

\displaystyle\varrho_{k,\varepsilon}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{y\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\cap\Lambda_{r}^{1}}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{r}^{k}|}\int_{x\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\cap\Lambda_{r}^{k}}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}\{|V_{G}(x,\omega)-V_{G}(y,\omega)|\geq\varepsilon r\}{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\varrho_{k}(\omega)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varrho_{k,\varepsilon}(\omega).

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
1\leq k<d
. If
\varrho_{k}=0

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, then also
\varrho_{k+1}=0

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.

Step 2: Proof of Lemma
4.8
.
For
k\leq d
, we consider the
k
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and we call it
\lambda_{k}
. We assume that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\varrho_{1}=0
. In particular, for each
\varepsilon>0
and large enough
r
, there is some set
\Delta\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\cap\Lambda_{r}^{1}
satisfying

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{r}^{1}\cap{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\setminus\Delta)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{r}^{1}),

\displaystyle|V_{G}(x,\omega)-V_{G}(y,\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon r\quad x,y\in\Delta.

Moreover, for
K>0
large enough (but deterministic), replacing
\Delta
by
\Delta\cap\mathscr{G}_{K,\varepsilon}
grant us the following properties for large
r
:

(i)

\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{r}^{1}\cap{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\setminus\Delta)\leq\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{r}^{1})
,

(ii)

|V_{G}(x,\omega)-V_{G}(y,\omega)|\leq\varepsilon r\quad x,y\in\Delta
,

(iii)

\Delta\subset\mathscr{G}_{K,\varepsilon}
, and

(iv)

\Delta\cap\Lambda_{r}^{1}\neq\emptyset
.

This is a consequence of the fact that
\lim_{K\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(0\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\setminus\mathscr{G}_{K,\varepsilon})=0
, and the ergodic theorem. We stress that even though these conditions are easily satisfied in dimension one, the construction will allow us to obtain the same properties in larger dimensions. In particular, we want that the “base”
\Delta
is contained in each successive step, so that
(iv)
will be always valid.

Next, for
L\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>0
, define

(4.21)

\Xi_{L,r}(\omega):=\{x\in\Lambda_{r}^{1}:\#\{0\leq i\leq L-1:x+ie_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega\}>0\}.

we claim that for each
\delta>0
, there exists some
L=L(\delta)
(deterministic) that satisfies
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\lambda(\Xi_{L,r})\geq(1-\delta)\lambda(\Lambda_{r}^{1})
for large
r
(which may depend on
\omega
). Indeed, by the ergodic theorem, the following equality holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for all
L\in\mathbb{N}

.

We will first prove

Theorem 5.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\overline{H}(\theta)\geq\overline{\Lambda}(\theta).

The rest of Section
5.1
and Section
5.2
are devoted to the proof of the above theorem.
We set

(5.3)

\mathcal{D}:=\big\{g:C^{2}_{c}(\Omega_{0}):g:\Omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R}\big\}

to be the linear space of functions on
\Omega_{0}
with compact support, such that their first and second weak derivatives (defined in Section
3.2.1
) exists and are continuous. For any
g\in\mathcal{D}
, define

(5.4)

R_{\theta}g(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{div}\big(a(\omega)\,(\nabla g(\omega)+\theta)\big),\quad\mbox{and write}\quad R=R_{0}.

As already observed in
[
KRV06
]
, we have that for any
g\in\mathcal{D}
,

(5.5)

\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(Rg+\langle b,\nabla g\rangle_{a}\big)\begin{cases}=0\quad\forall g\in\mathcal{D}\qquad\qquad\,\mbox{if}\qquad(b,\phi)\in\mathcal{E},\\
\neq 0\quad\mbox{for some}\,\,g\in\mathcal{D}\quad\mbox{if}\qquad(b,\phi)\notin\mathcal{E},\end{cases}

and hence, by taking constant multiples if
(b,\phi)\notin\mathcal{E}
, we conclude that the infimum over
g\in\mathcal{D}
in (
5.5
) is
0
if

(b,\phi)\in\mathcal{E}
, and
-\infty
otherwise. Therefore,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\overline{H}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\,\,\sup_{b\in B_{\phi}}\,\,\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\bigg[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a\theta)+\langle\theta,b\rangle_{a}-L(b,\omega))+\big(Rg+\langle b,\nabla g\rangle_{a}\big)\bigg)\bigg],

where

(5.7)

\Phi:=\bigg\{\phi\in L^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{P}_{0}):\int\phi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}=1\bigg\}.

Furthermore, for
\phi\in\Phi
,

(5.8)

B_{\phi}:=\bigg\{b\in L^{1}_{a}(\phi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}):\forall\omega\in\Omega_{0}:x\mapsto b(\tau_{x}\omega)\in\mathrm{Lip}\bigg\},

with
L^{1}_{a}(\phi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0})
being defined in (
3.15
).
We remark that, for any
\phi\in\Phi
, the set
B_{\phi}
contains constant functions
b
. First, we will prove

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\overline{H}(\theta)
be the variational formula defined in (
5.1
) (or equivalently, in (
5.6
)). Then

(5.9)

\overline{H}(\theta)=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\left[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(R_{\theta}g+H(\theta+\nabla g(\omega),\omega)\big)\right].

Proof.

By (
5.6
) and (
5.4
),

(5.10)

\overline{H}(\theta)=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\,\,\sup_{b\in B_{\phi}}\,\,\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\bigg[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}+R_{\theta}g-L(b,\omega)\big)\bigg].

First, we need to exchange the supremum over
b
with the infimum over
g
, for which we would like to apply the min-max theorem from
[
AE84
, Theorem 8, p. 319 ]
, the requirements for which are verified as follows. Note that the map

b\mapsto\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}+R_{\theta}g-L(b,\omega)\big)\qquad\mbox{is concave and upper semicontinuous},

while the map

g\mapsto\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}+R_{\theta}g-L(b,\omega)\big)\qquad\mbox{ is convex and lower semicontinuous.}

We need to verify the remaining compactness (resp. coercivity): we will show that for a fixed
\phi\in\Phi
and
g\in\mathcal{D}
, the level sets

\displaystyle E_{c}:

\displaystyle=\bigg\{b\in B_{\phi}:\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}+R_{\theta}g-L(b,\omega)\big)\geq c\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{b\in L_{a}^{1}(\phi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}):{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\ni x\mapsto b(x,\omega)\in\text{Lip and}~\forall~\omega\in\Omega_{0},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}+R_{\theta}g-L(b,\omega)\big)\geq c\bigg\}

are weakly compact in
L^{1}_{a}(\phi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0})
. Indeed, by the Eberlein-Šmulian theorem (see
[
DS58
, p.430]
), checking the latter condition is equivalent to verifying that the set
E_{c}
above is weakly closed and sequentially weakly compact in
L^{1}_{a}(\phi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0})
. For the second condition, it is enough to show that
E_{c}
is bounded and uniformly integrable, but both these conditions follow from the coercivity of
L
. Indeed, recall (
2.6
) from
(F2)
:

c_{10}\|q\|_{a}^{\alpha^{\prime}}-c_{11}\leq L(q,\omega)\leq c_{12}\|q\|_{a}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+c_{13},\qquad\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1},\,\,1<\alpha<\infty.

On the other hand, using that
g\in\mathcal{D}
has compact support and
\nabla g
is continuous,
|\theta+\nabla g|\leq(|\theta|+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P}_{0})}=:C_{1}(\theta,g)<\infty
. Moreover,
by
(F1)
,
|\mathrm{div}(a)|\leq C
, so we can find a constant
C_{2}(\theta,g)
such that by (
5.4
),

|R_{\theta}g|\leq C_{2}(\theta,g)
. Hence,

(5.11)

\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}+R_{\theta}g\big)\leq C_{2}(\theta,g)+C_{1}(\theta,g)\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\|b\|_{a}<\infty

since
b\in L_{a}^{1}(\phi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0})
, recall (
3.15
).

Thus, it remains to show that
E_{c}
is weakly closed. Since
E_{c}
is convex, it suffices to show that
E_{c}
is strongly closed. Indeed, suppose that
(b_{n})_{n}\subset E_{c}
such that
b_{n}\to b
in
L_{a}^{1}(\phi d\mathbb{P}_{0})
. Passing to a subsequence, since
L
is lower semicontinuous and by Fatou’s lemma, one can easily verify that
\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}+R_{\theta}g-L(b,\omega)\big)\geq c
. We will construct a function
\tilde{b}
such that
\tilde{b}=b

\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s. and for all
\omega\in\Omega_{0},~\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni x\mapsto\tilde{b}(x,\omega)\in
Lip. Let
\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
with
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{0}^{\prime})=1
such that
b_{n}(\omega)\to b(\omega)
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
. For a fixed
\omega\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
, we know that the family
(b_{n}(\cdot,\omega))_{n}
is uniformly equicontinuous and on any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x\in K
,

|b_{n}(x,\omega)|\leq|x|+|b_{n}(0,\omega)|\leq{\rm diam}(K)+\sup_{n}|b_{n}(0,\omega)|.

As
b_{n}(0,\omega)\to b(0,\omega)
, the supremum above is finite. Hence, for fixed
\omega
, the family of continuous functions
(b_{n}(\cdot,\omega))_{n}
is globally uniformly equicontinuous and uniformly bounded on compact sets. By the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, the sequence
(b_{n}(\cdot,\omega))
converges uniformly on compact sets and therefore converges pointwise to some function
\tilde{f}(\cdot,\omega)\in
Lip. By definition,
\tilde{f}(0,\omega)=b(\omega)

\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s. Now let us consider the set

\Omega_{0}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}:=\{\omega\in\Omega_{0}:\exists x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}:\omega=\tau_{x}\omega^{\prime}\}.

Then we define

(5.12)

\tilde{b}(\omega):=\begin{cases}\tilde{f}(x,\omega^{\prime})~\text{ if }\omega=\tau_{x}\omega^{\prime}\text{ for some }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime},\\
0~\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let us first check that
\tilde{b}
is well-defined. Indeed, suppose that
\omega=\tau_{x}\omega^{\prime}=\tau_{y}\omega^{\prime\prime}
for some
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime}\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x,\omega^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}(x,\omega^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}(0,\tau_{x}\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}(0,\tau_{y}\omega^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}(y,\omega^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}(y,\omega^{\prime\prime}).

Hence, the function
\tilde{b}
is well defined. Notice that on
\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
,
\tilde{b}(\omega)=f(0,\omega)=b(\omega)
, so
\tilde{b}=b

\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s. Finally, let us check that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
,
{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)\ni x\mapsto\tilde{b}(x,\omega)\in
Lip. Indeed,

(i)

If
\omega\in\Omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}
, then
\omega=\tau_{z}\omega^{\prime}
for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega_{0}
and for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|\tilde{b}(x,\omega)-\tilde{b}(y,\omega)|=|\tilde{b}(x,\tau_{z}\omega^{\prime})-\tilde{b}(y,\tau_{z}\omega^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=|\tilde{f}(x+z,\omega^{\prime})-\tilde{f}(y+z,\omega^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|.

(ii)

On the other hand, if
\omega\in\Omega_{0}\setminus\Omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}
, then the same holds for
\tau_{x}\omega
for all
x\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}(\omega)
, and the Lipschitz condition is trivially satisfied.

This finishes the proof that
E_{c}
is weakly compact in
L_{a}^{1}(\phi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0})
, and therefore, by the aforementioned min-max theorem, we can exchange the
\sup_{b\in B_{\phi}}
and
\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}

in (
5.10
) to obtain

\overline{H}(\theta)=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\sup_{b\in B_{\phi}}\left[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}+R_{\theta}g-L(b,\omega)\big)\right].

Since the integrand depends locally in
b
, we can bring the supremum over
b
inside the integral, and use the duality between
H
and
L
to conclude that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\overline{H}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\left[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\bigg(R_{\theta}g+\sup_{b\in B_{\phi}}\big[\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}-L(b,\omega)\big]\bigg)\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\bigg[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\big(R_{\theta}g+H(\theta+\nabla g(\omega),\omega)\big)\bigg].

In the last equality we used that, for any
\phi\in\Phi
, the set
B_{\phi}
defined in (
5.8
) contains constants, so that

\displaystyle\sup_{b\in B_{\phi}}\big[\langle\theta+\nabla g,b\rangle_{a}-L(b,\omega)\big]

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\langle\theta+\nabla g,y\rangle_{a}-L(y,\omega)\big]

\displaystyle=H(\theta+\nabla g(\omega),\omega).

∎

We would like to now swap the order of
\sup_{\phi}
and
\inf_{g}
in (
5.13
).

Lemma 5.4
.

With
R_{\theta}
defined in (
5.4
), let

(5.14)

\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)(\omega):=R_{\theta}g(\omega)+H(\theta+\nabla g(\omega),\omega)),\quad g\in\mathcal{D}.

Then for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\overline{H}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\,\,\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\,\,\left[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\,\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)\right]\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\,\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\bigg[\varepsilon\log\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\,\exp\big[\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)\big]\bigg].

Proof.

For any probability density
\varphi\geq 0
on
\Omega_{0}
(i.e.,
\int_{\Omega_{0}}\varphi{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}=1
), let

\mathrm{Ent}_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}(\varphi)=\int\varphi\log\varphi\,{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\geq 0

be the entropy of
\varphi
. Its non-negativity is a consequence of the Jensen’s inequality. Moreover, the map
\varphi\mapsto\mathrm{Ent}_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}(\varphi)
is convex, weakly lower semicontinuous and has weakly compact sub-level sets, meaning, for any
\ell>0
,
\{\varphi:\mathrm{Ent}_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}(\varphi)\leq\ell\}
is compact in the weak topology.
Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
we have the lower bound

\displaystyle\overline{H}(\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\,\,\bigg[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\,\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)-\varepsilon\mathrm{Ent}_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}(\phi)\bigg]=\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\,\,\bigg[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\,(\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)-\varepsilon\log\phi)\bigg].

Similarly as in (
5.11
), we use the fact that
g\in\mathcal{D}
together with the assumptions
(F1)
to conclude that there is a constant
C_{2}(\theta,g)
such that
|R_{\theta}(g)|\leq C_{2}(\theta,g)
, so that

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)\leq C_{2}(\theta,g)+\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi H(\theta+\nabla g)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\theta,g)+c_{8}\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\|\theta+\nabla g\|_{a}^{\alpha}+c_{9}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\theta,g)+c_{8}C(\alpha,\theta,g)+c_{9}<\infty.

where for the second inequality we used the upper bound from (
2.5
) in
(F2)
and for the third inequality we used
\langle a(\omega)x,x\rangle\leq c_{5}|x|^{2}
from
(F1)
and again
that
\sup_{\omega}|\theta+\nabla g(\omega)|\leq|\theta|+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P}_{0})}
for
g\in\mathcal{D}
.
Now, for any fixed
\phi\in\Phi
, the map

g\mapsto\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\,(\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)-\varepsilon\log\phi)

is convex and continuous, while for any fixed
g\in\mathcal{D}
, the map

\varphi\mapsto\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\varphi\,(\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)-\varepsilon\log\varphi)

is concave, upper-semicontinuous and has compact superlevel sets in the weak
L^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
topology, we can again use Von-Neumann’s minimax theorem to justify changing the order of
\sup_{\phi}
and
\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}
.
This means,

\overline{H}(\theta)\geq\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\sup_{\phi}\,\,\bigg[\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\phi\,(\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)-\varepsilon\log\phi)\bigg].

The above variational problem over
\phi
subject to the condition
\int\phi d\mathbb{P}_{0}=1
can be solved explicitly and the maximizing density is

\phi=\frac{\exp[\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)]}{\mathbb{P}_{0}\big[\exp[\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)]\big]}.

We replace this value of
\phi
in the last lower bound for
\overline{H}(\theta)
to obtain

\overline{H}(\theta)\geq\,\,\inf_{g\in\mathcal{D}}\bigg[\varepsilon\log\int{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{0}\,\exp\big[\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g)\big]\bigg].

We let
\varepsilon\to 0
, to deduce the lower bound claimed in (
5.15
).
∎

Given the above results, the lower bound in Theorem
5.2
will now be a
consequence of the following technical result that will be established in
Section
5.2
.

Proposition 5.5
.

For any given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and a sequence of
functions
(g_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}
so that

(5.16)

\overline{H}(\theta)\geq\varepsilon_{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[{\rm e}^{\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g_{n},\cdot)}\bigg]-\varepsilon,

and
G_{n}(\omega):=\nabla g_{n}
converges weakly in
L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
(with

\delta>0
as in (
2.3
)) and in distribution (along a subsequence) to some

G
. Furthermore,
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
, which is defined in Section
4
.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
(assuming Proposition

5.5
).

:
By Proposition
5.5
, for

r>0
, we pick some sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and
g_{n}\in\mathcal{D}

satisfying

\overline{H}(\theta)\geq\varepsilon_{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\big[{\rm e}^{\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g_{n},\cdot)}\big]-r.

For fixed
n
, the map

\lambda\in[0,\infty)\to\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}[{\rm e}^{\lambda\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g_{n},\cdot)}]
is increasing, so for each

\eta,\lambda>0
, if
n
is large enough,

\displaystyle\overline{H}(\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\big[{\rm e}^{\lambda\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g_{n},\cdot)}\big]-r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[{\rm e}^{\lambda\big(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(\omega)(G_{n}(\omega)+\theta))+H(\theta+\nabla G_{n}(\omega),\omega)\big)}\bigg]-r.

For any
M,\lambda>0
, the map

x\mapsto{\rm e}^{\lambda\left(M\wedge\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(\omega)(x+\theta))+H(\theta+x,\omega)\right)\right)}

is continuous and bounded. Thus, letting
n\to\infty
and using the fact
(from Proposition
5.5
) that
G_{n}
converges to
G
in distribution, we conclude
from the above bound that

\overline{H}(\theta)\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[{\rm e}^{\lambda\big(M\wedge\big(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(\omega)(G(\omega)+\theta))+H(\theta+G(\omega),\omega)\big)\big)}\bigg]-r.

Now by letting
M\nearrow\infty
and using monotone convergence theorem, we obtain

(5.17)

\overline{H}(\theta)\geq\log\bigg\|{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(\omega)(G(\omega)+\theta))+H(\theta+G(\omega),\omega)}\bigg\|_{L^{\lambda}(\mathbb{P}_{0})}-r.

Finally, letting
\lambda\to\infty
, we obtain

(5.18)

\displaystyle\overline{H}(\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(G+\theta))+H(G+\theta)\right]-r

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}}\overline{\Lambda}(\theta,G)-r.

Since
r>0
is arbitrary, we are done with the proof of Theorem

5.2
.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Proposition
5.5

We divide the proof of Proposition
5.5
into three subsequent lemmas.

Lemma 5.6
.

For any given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and a sequence of functions
(g_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}
so that (
5.16
) holds, and

G_{n}(\omega):=\nabla g_{n}(\omega)
converges weakly in
L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
(with
\delta>0
as in (
2.3
)) and in distribution along a subsequence
to some random variable
G\in L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
.

Proof.

We start with the bound (
5.15
) in Lemma
5.4
which implies that there exist sequences
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and
(g_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}
satisfying

(5.19)

\varepsilon_{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\big[{\rm e}^{\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g_{n},\cdot)}\big]\leq\overline{H}(\theta).

Using this we will first show that

(5.20)

\sup_{n}\|G_{n}\|_{L^{1+\delta}(\Omega_{0})}<\infty.

In particular, the above bound will imply that
G_{n}
converges weakly in
L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
along a subsequence to some
G
.

We now prove (
5.20
). Note that the map
\lambda\in[0,\infty)\mapsto\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}_{0}[{\rm e}^{\lambda\mathcal{S}_{\theta}(g_{n},\cdot)}]
is increasing. Thus, recalling the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}
from (
5.14
) and using (
5.19
), we obtain
that for
n
large enough,

\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[{\rm e}^{R_{\theta}g_{n}(\omega)+H(\theta+\nabla g_{n}(\omega),\omega))}\right]\leq\overline{H}(\theta).

The lower bound on
H(\cdot,\omega)
from
(F2)
implies

\log\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[{\rm e}^{R_{\theta}g_{n}(\omega)+c_{6}\|\theta+\nabla g_{n}\|_{a}^{\alpha}-c_{7}}\right]\leq\overline{H}(\theta).

Set
G_{n}:=\nabla g_{n}
. Then Jensen’s inequality applied to the bound bound and the definition of
R_{\theta}g_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(\nabla g_{n}+\theta))
from (
5.4
) leads to

\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(G_{n}+\theta))+c_{6}\|\theta+G_{n}\|_{a}^{\alpha}\right]\leq\overline{H}(\theta)+c_{7}.

Since
G_{n}=\nabla g_{n}
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathrm{div}(aG_{n})\right]=0
. Thus by
(F1)
we conclude that for some constant
C=C(\theta,\eta)
,

\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{0}[\|G_{n}\|_{a}^{\alpha}]\leq C,\qquad\qquad\alpha>1.

But by (
2.2
),

\|G_{n}\|_{a}^{\alpha}=\langle a(\omega),G_{n},G_{n}\rangle^{\alpha/2}\geq\xi(\omega)^{\alpha/2}|G_{n}|^{\alpha}.

Combining the last two displays, we have

(5.21)

\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{0}\big[\xi(\omega)^{\alpha/2}|G_{n}|^{\alpha}\big]\leq C.

Hence,

(5.22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}[|G_{n}|^{1+\delta}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\Big[|G_{n}|^{1+\delta}\xi(\omega)^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\xi(\omega)^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}\Big[|G_{n}|^{\alpha}\xi(\omega)^{\alpha/2}\Big]^{\frac{1+\delta}{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[\xi(\omega)^{-\frac{\alpha(1+\delta)}{2(\alpha-1-\delta)}}\bigg]^{\frac{\alpha-1-\delta}{\alpha}}<\infty.

In the first upper bound we used Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{\alpha}{1+\delta}>1
(recall that
\alpha>1+\delta
) and
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1-\delta}
,
and for the second bound we invoked (
5.21
) and (
2.3
) with
\chi=\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{1+\delta}{\alpha-(1+\delta)}
. Hence,

\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{0}[|G_{n}\|^{1+\delta}]<\infty,

with
\delta>0
. Consequently,
G_{n}
converges weakly in
L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
and in distribution along a subsequence to some random variable
G\in L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

The limit
G
of
G_{n}
from Lemma
5.6
satisfies the closed loop condition defined in (
4.3
), i.e., for any simple closed path
\mathrm{C}
contained in the infinite cluster
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
, we have
\int_{\mathrm{C}}G(\omega,\cdot)\cdot{\rm d}r=0
, almost surely w.r.t.
\mathbb{P}_{0}
.

Proof.

We first assert that it suffices to prove that for any measurable set
A\subset\Omega_{0}
,

(5.23)

\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}\int_{\mathrm{C}}G(\omega,\cdot)\cdot{\rm d}r\bigg]=0~\text{ for each simple closed path }\mathrm{C}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\infty}.

Indeed, (
5.23
) says that for any fixed simple closed path,
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s.,
{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{C\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}}\int_{C}G(\omega,\cdot)\cdot{\rm d}r=0
. We want to show that this holds
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s. uniformly on each closed loop. Since line integrals are independent of the parametrization of the path, for each
\mathrm{C}
, we choose any (but fixed from now) smooth function

f_{\mathrm{C}}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\qquad\mbox{ satisfying }f(0)=f(1).

The space

X:=\big\{f\in C^{\infty}[0,1]:f(0)=f(1)\big\}

is separable under the
|\cdot|_{\infty}
norm, so there exists some countable dense subset
Y\subset X
. If (
5.23
) holds, we can show that
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a-s., the closed loop condition holds for each curve
\mathrm{C}
such that
f_{\mathrm{C}}\in Y
. To extend this to all simple closed curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can approximate each curve by a sequence
\mathrm{C}_{n}
such that
f_{\mathrm{C}_{n}}\in Y
. Since the convergence is uniform, it is easy to deduce that
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s.,
{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}}\int_{\mathrm{C}}G(\omega,\cdot)\cdot r=0
for any simple closed curve
\mathrm{C}
. Thus, we only need to show that (
5.23
) holds for fixed
A\subset\Omega_{0}
and simple closed curve
\mathrm{C}
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any smooth function that parametrizes
\mathrm{C}
. For each fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we know that
G_{n}=\nabla g_{n}
satisfies the closed loop condition (because it is a gradient). By Fubini’s theorem we have

\displaystyle 0=\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}\int_{\mathrm{C}}G_{n}(\omega,\cdot)\cdot{\rm d}r\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}\int_{0}^{1}G_{n}(\omega,f(x))\cdot f^{\prime}(x){\rm d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(x)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}G_{n}(\omega,f(x))\bigg]{\rm d}x.

Since
G_{n}
converges weakly to
G
in
L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
(as shown in Lemma
5.6
), for fixed
x\in[0,1]
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}G_{n}(\omega,f(x))\right]=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}G(\omega,f(x))\right].

Using that
\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{0}[G_{n}^{1+\delta}]<\infty
, and that
f^{\prime}
is bounded on

[0,1]
, we can apply dominated convergence theorem to conclude that

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}G_{n}(\omega,f(x))\right]=\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}(x)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}G(\omega,f(x))\right]{\rm d}x.

As
G\in L^{1+\delta}(\mathbb{P}_{0})
, we can again exchange the order of integration
using Fubini’s theorem so the right-hand side in the last display is

\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A\cap(\mathrm{C}\subset{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}})}\int_{\mathrm{C}}G(\omega,\cdot)\cdot{\rm d}r\right]
. This shows (
5.23
)
and concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

.

Proof.

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{R}\sum_{k=1}^{3^{d}j}\sum_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\in N_{j}}\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[\int_{0\leadsto\mathfrak{v}_{e}}G(\omega,\cdot)\cdot{\rm d}\mathbf{r},\tilde{\ell}=j,\tilde{A}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})\bigg]

\displaystyle|\eta_{R}|

Proof of Proposition
5.9
:

Let us first sketch the main idea of the proof. To simplify notation, for a fixed
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we will simply write

u_{\varepsilon}(t,x,\omega)=u_{\varepsilon,\theta}(t,x,\omega).

Recall that for a fixed
t>0

3.9
,

(5.28)

u_{\varepsilon}(t,0,\omega)=\varepsilon\sup_{c\in\mathbf{C}_{T}^{*}}E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}\bigg[\langle\theta,X_{t/\varepsilon}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t/\varepsilon}L(X_{s},c(s)){\rm d}s\bigg].

Next, let us fix any
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
as defined in (
4.1
), with
V_{G}(\omega,x):=\int_{0\leadsto x}G(\omega,\cdot)\,\,\cdot{\rm d}\mathbf{r}
as defined in (
4.4
), and set

h_{G}(x):=\langle\theta,x\rangle+V_{G}(x,\omega)

for a fixed
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
. If
V_{G}
were smooth enough,
\nabla h_{G}=\theta+G
and by Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle\langle\theta,X_{t/\varepsilon}\rangle+V_{G}(X_{t/\varepsilon},\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t/\varepsilon}(\theta+G(X_{s}))\sigma(X_{s}){\rm d}\mathcal{B}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t/\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}(a(\theta+G))(X_{s}){\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t/\varepsilon}\langle c(s),\theta+G(X_{s})\rangle_{a}{\rm d}s.

Therefore, we would obtain

(5.29)

\displaystyle E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}\bigg[\langle\theta,X_{t/\varepsilon}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t/\varepsilon}L(X_{s},c(s)){\rm d}s\bigg]=-E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}[V_{G}(X_{t/\varepsilon},\omega)]

\displaystyle+E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t/\varepsilon}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\left(a(X_{s}))(\theta+G(X_{s})\right)+\langle c(s),\theta+G(X_{s})\rangle_{a}-L(X_{s},c(s))\bigg){\rm d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq-E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}\big[V_{G}(X_{t/\varepsilon},\omega)\big]+E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t/\varepsilon}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(X_{s}))(\theta+G(X_{s}))+H(X_{s},\theta+G(X_{s})){\rm d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq-E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}\big[V_{G}(X_{t/\varepsilon},\omega)\big]+\frac{t}{\varepsilon}~\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(G+\theta))+H(G+\theta)\bigg].

Together with (
5.28
), we would have the bound

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(t,0,\omega)

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\inf_{c\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{T}}E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}[V_{G}(X_{t/\varepsilon},\omega)]+t~\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}(a(G+\theta))+H(G+\theta)\right].

If
V_{G}
were bounded, we could apply Theorem
4.9
and deduce that
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s., for all
r>0
there exists some
c_{r}=c_{r}(\omega)
such that for all
x\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}
,
|V_{G}(x,\omega)|\leq r|x|+c_{r}
, leading to

u_{\varepsilon}(t,0,\omega)\leq t\overline{\Lambda}(\theta)+\varepsilon c_{r}+r\sup_{c\in\mathbf{C}^{*}_{T}}E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}[|\varepsilon X_{t/\varepsilon}|].

By Lemma
3.9
and the inequalities (
3.34
)-(
3.35
), one can deduce (see (
5.34
) below for details) that

is uniformly bounded over

and

. Thus, one simply let first
\varepsilon\to 0
and then

to conclude the proof.

However, a priori
G\in\mathcal{G}_{1+\delta}
is neither smooth enough nor bounded. Nevertheless, we can mollify
G
to get a smooth and bounded version, so that we can apply the same reasoning as above.

If
\rho
is any spherically symmetric mollifier with support the unit ball and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(y){\rm d}y=1
and
r>0
, we set

for some constant depending on

. As a consequence, for any

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}[V_{G}^{r}(X_{t/\varepsilon},\omega)-V_{G}^{r}(0,\omega)-\langle c_{r},X_{t/\varepsilon}\rangle]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon E^{P_{0}^{c,\omega}}\left[\int_{0}^{t/\varepsilon}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\left(a(X_{s}))(\theta+G_{r}(X_{s})-c_{r}\right)+H(X_{s},\theta+G_{r}(X_{s})-c_{r}){\rm d}s\right].

Percolation models satisfying assumptions

-

Let us illustrate some important percolation models that satisfy the assumptions imposed earlier.

The Boolean model:
The simplest continuum percolation model, known as the Boolean model, is defined as
\mathcal{C}(\omega):=\bigcup_{x\in\omega}B_{1/2}(x)
, where
\omega
is sampled with respect to a probability measure
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{\zeta}

•

For any bounded

and any

,

(A.1)

•

For any collection of disjoint, bounded Borel sets

,

(A.2)

{\mathbb{P}^{\zeta}}\big(\#(\omega\cap A_{1})=n_{1},...,\#(\omega\cap A_{k})=n_{k}\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{P}}(\#(\omega\cap A_{i})=n_{i}).

The Boolean model satisfies the above assumptions
(P1)
-
1.15
for
\zeta
large enough.

Proposition A.1
.

Fix

. Then there exists
\zeta_{c}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all

,

satisfies

-
(P5)
and also

.

Proof.

The proof of these results are well-known:

is a consequence of
[

, Propositions 2.6-2.7 ]
.

is consequence of (
A.1
),
(P3)
follows from
[
MR96
, Theorems 3.5-3.6 ]
. Property

can be found in
[
CGY11
, Lemma 3.4]
, and

appears in
[

, Theorem 2.2]

To verify

in

, given
\zeta>\zeta_{c}
, let

be large enough so that in

there almost surely exists an infinite cluster

. That existence is guaranteed by the fact that the critical intensity for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
coincides with the limit of the critical intensities for

\{|\mathfrak{v}_{e}|\geq Lt\}\cap\{0\in{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\}\subset\bigcap_{k\leq\lfloor t\rfloor}\tau_{kLe}(A_{L}^{C}).

Continuum random cluster model:
Besides the Boolean model, we mention briefly a model which presents long-range correlations, namely the continuum random cluster model (CRCM)

-
1.15
. For

Appendix B
Ergodic properties.

Here we will provide the proof of Proposition
3.5
, which is a consequence of the following known result from ergodic theory (see
[
P89
,
BB07
]
). We include it here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{F},\mu)
be a probability space and let
T:X\to X
be invertible, measure preserving and ergodic with respect to
\mu
. Let
A\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mu(A)>0
. If
n:A\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
is defined by

n(x)=\min\{k>0:T^{k}(x)\in A\}

and
S:A\rightarrow A
by
S(x)=T^{n(x)}(x)
for
x\in A
, then
S
is measure preserving and ergodic with respect to
\mu(\cdot|A)
and almost surely invertible with respect to the same measure.

Proof.

We first prove that
S
is measure preserving. By the Poincaré Theorem,
n(x)<\infty
almost surely. For any
j\geq 1
we define
A_{j}=\{x\in A:n(x)=j\}
. By definition, the
A_{j}
are disjoint and as
n(x)<\infty
almost surely,
\mu(A\setminus\cup_{j\geq 1}A_{j})=0
. As the restriction of
S
to
A_{j}
is
T^{j}
and since
T^{j}
is measure preserving,
S
is measure preserving on
A_{j}
. We claim that
S(A_{i})\cap S(A_{j})=\emptyset
. This, together with the fact that
S
is measure preserving on
A_{j}
, proves that
S
is measure preserving on the disjoint union
\cup_{j\geq 1}A_{j}
and therefore on
A
.

Thus, we only owe the claim
S(A_{i})\cap S(A_{j})=\emptyset
. We assume that there exists
x\in S(A_{i})\cap S(A_{j})
for
1\leq i<j
. This requires the existence of
y,z\in A
with
n(y)=i
,
n(z)=j
and
x=T^{i}(y)=T^{j}(z)
. As
T
is invertible,
y=T^{j-i}(z)
. Thus,
n(z)\leq j-i<j
, which is a contradiction to
n(z)=j
and the desired claim follows.

Next, we note that
T
is invertible. Thus,
S
is almost surely invertible, as the intersection
S^{-1}(\{x\})\cap\{S\text{ is well defined}\}
is a one-point set.

We finally want to show that
S
is ergodic. Let
B\in\mathscr{F}
such that
B\subseteq A
is
S
-invariant. Then if
x\in B
and
n\geq 1
, it follows that
S^{n}(x)\notin A\setminus B
. This implies that for any
x\in B
and
k\geq 1
, if
T^{k}(x)\in B
, then
T^{k}(x)\notin A\setminus B
. We conclude that
C=\cup_{k\geq 1}T^{k}(B)
is
T
-invariant and
B\subseteq C\subseteq(X\setminus A)\cup B
. In particular,
\mu(B)\leq\mu(C)\leq 1+\mu(B)-\mu(A)
. Therefore, ergodicity of
T
implies
\mu(C)\in\{0,1\}
, which forces
\mu(B)\in\{0,\mu(A)\}
and thus, the ergodicity of
S
with respect to
\mu(\cdot|A)
.
∎

is invertible, measure preserving and ergodic with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
. It follows from Lemma
B.1
that the induced shift

is
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
-preserving, almost surely invertible and ergodic with respect to

.

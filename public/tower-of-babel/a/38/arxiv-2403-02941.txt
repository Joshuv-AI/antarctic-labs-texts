Ruin Probability Approximation for Bidimensional Brownian Risk Model with Tax
Timofei Shashkov

Abstract




Abstract:

Let
\boldsymbol{B}(t)=(B_{1}(t),B_{2}(t))
,
t\geq 0
be a two-dimensional Brownian motion with independent components and define the
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-reflected process

\boldsymbol{X}(t)=(X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t))=\left(B_{1}(t)-c_{1}t-\gamma_{1}\inf_{s_{1}\in[0,t]}(B_{1}(s_{1})-c_{1}s_{1}),B_{2}(t)-c_{2}t-\gamma_{2}\inf_{s_{2}\in[0,t]}(B_{2}(s_{2})-c_{2}s_{2})\right),

with given finite constants
c_{1},c_{2}
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in[0,2)
. The goal of this paper is to derive the asymptotics of the ruin probability

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,T]}:X_{1}(t)>u,X_{2}(t)>au\right\}

as
u\to\infty
for
T>0
.

Key Words:
bidimensional Brownian risk model; simultaneous ruin probability;
\gamma
-reflected risk model; exact asymptotics; extremes of Gaussian random fields.

AMS Classification:
Primary 60G15; secondary 60G70.

1.
Introduction and main result

The classical Brownian risk model (BRM) of a given insurance portfolio plays a significant role in risk theory (see
[
1
]
). The corresponding risk process
R
is defined by

R(t)=u+ct-B(t),\ \ \ \ \ t\geq 0,

with
B
being a Brownian motion,
u>0
the initial capital and
c>0
the premium rate.

For a finite time horizon
[0,T],T>0
the classical ruin probability is defined by (see e.g.,
[
2
]
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{T}(u)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left\{\inf_{t\in[0,T]}R(t)<0\right\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}(B(t)-ct)>u\right\},\ u>0.

According to
[
3
]
for
T<\infty

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{T}(u)=\Phi\left(-\frac{u}{\sqrt{T}}-c\sqrt{T}\right)+e^{-2cu}\Phi\left(-\frac{u}{\sqrt{T}}+c\sqrt{T}\right),

where
\Phi
is the distribution function of a standard normal random variable. In addition, if
T=\infty
, then

\widetilde{\psi}_{\infty}(u)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left\{\inf_{t\geq 0}R(t)<0\right\}=e^{-2cu}.

An extension of the Brownian risk model, which was motivated in
[
4
]
, accounts for the non-zero tax rate
\gamma\in[0,1]
. The corresponding risk process
X
is given by

X(t)=B(t)-ct-\gamma\inf_{s\in[0,t]}(B(s)-cs),\ \ \ \ \ t\geq 0.

This process is also known in the literature under the name of
\gamma
-reflected Brownian motion, see
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

If
\gamma=1
, then the process
X
is also known in queuing theory as a workload process or a queue length process and it has been studied in several contributions (see, e.g.
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
).

Unlike the classical Brownian model for this type of process it is not possible to calculate this ruin probability explicitly. Therefore it can either be simulated or approximated for large values of the initial capital
u
. According to
[
5
]
, if
\psi_{\gamma,T}(u)=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,T]}:X(t)>u\right\}
for
0<T<\infty
, then

\psi_{\gamma,T}(u)\sim\frac{4}{2-\gamma}\psi\left(\frac{u+cT}{\sqrt{T}}\right)\sim\frac{4}{2-\gamma}\mathbb{P}\left\{B(T)>u+cT\right\},\ u\to\infty,

where
\psi(x)=1/(\sqrt{2\pi}x)\exp(-x^{2}/2)
. In this paper
\sim
means asymptotic equivalence as
u\to\infty
.

In many applications, vector-valued processes are frequently used, making their analysis particularly important.
For the previously discussed
\gamma
-reflected risk model, which is a benchmark model in risk theory, it is hence natural to consider its vector-valued generalization. In this manuscript under certain assumptions we define and study the bivariate Brownian risk model with tax. To this end, consider two independent one-dimensional Brownian motions,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. The model is then defined by
\boldsymbol{X}(t)=(X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t))
with

X_{i}(t)=B_{i}(t)-c_{i}t-\gamma_{i}\inf_{s_{i}\in[0,t]}(B_{i}(s_{i})-c_{i}s_{i}),\ \ \ \ t\geq 0

for
i=1,2
, where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
are tax rates,
c_{1},c_{2}
are premium rates. Even though in practice it is natural to assume
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in[0,1]
, methods which are used in the manuscript to obtain main results are applicable for all
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in[0,2)
.

The main quantities of interest related to the vector-valued process
\boldsymbol{X}
in the context of risk theory are the finite and infinite horizon ruin events. In this paper we study the simultaneous ruin probability in the case of a finite time frame, defined for
T>0
and
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
by the formula

\psi_{\gamma,T}(\boldsymbol{u})=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,T]}:X_{1}(t)>u_{1},X_{2}(t)>u_{2}\right\}.

To be more specific, for a given constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
we are interested in the approximation of
\psi_{\gamma,T}(u,au)
as
u\to\infty
.
Observe that
\psi_{\gamma,T}(u,au)
can be rewritten in another form, namely

\psi_{\gamma,T}(u,au)=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,T],s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,t]}:\begin{array}[]{ccc}B_{1}(t)-c_{1}t-\gamma_{1}(B_{1}(s_{1})-c_{1}s_{1})>u\\
B_{2}(t)-c_{2}t-\gamma_{2}(B_{2}(s_{2})-c_{2}s_{2})>au\end{array}\right\}.

Using the self-similarity property of the Brownian motion, we can assume without loss of generality that
T=1
. In this case denote
\psi_{\gamma}(u,au):=\psi_{\gamma,1}(u,au)
. Moreover due to the symmetric view of
\boldsymbol{X}
we can assume here and below
a\leq 1
.

Here and below bold notation are vector-valued and non-bold designations are scalars. Throughout the whole paper
t\geq 0
,
\boldsymbol{s}=(s_{1},s_{2})\in[0,\infty)^{2}
,
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in[0,2)^{2}
. Additionally, hereafter
\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})=(B_{1}(s_{1}),B_{2}(s_{2}))
,
\boldsymbol{B}(t):=\boldsymbol{B}(t,t)=(B_{1}(t),B_{2}(t))
, and
\boldsymbol{a}:=(1,a)
.

All operations for vectors, such as addition, division, comparison and multiplication by scalar constants, are assumed to be componentwise. For example,
\boldsymbol{x}/\boldsymbol{y}:=(x_{1}/y_{1},x_{2}/y_{2})
for
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Thus in terms of the previously discussed vector notation one can rewrite the formula for
\psi_{\gamma}(u,au)
as

\psi_{\gamma}(u,au)=\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,1],s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,t]}:\boldsymbol{B}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{c}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}.

We formulate next the main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}
be an independent copy of
\boldsymbol{B}
and let
\phi
be the density function of the vector
(B_{1}(1),B_{2}(1))
. Then for all
1\geq a>0

\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)\sim C(a)\frac{1}{u^{2}}\phi(\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}),

where

C(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\geq\boldsymbol{0}}:\boldsymbol{B}^{*}(t)-\boldsymbol{a}t+\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{x}\right\}e^{x_{1}+ax_{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}\in(0,\infty).

If
a\leq 0
and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}=(1,0)
, then

\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)\sim C(a)\frac{1}{u}\phi(\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}u+\boldsymbol{c}),\ \quad C(a)=\frac{4}{2-\gamma_{1}}\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{2}\right)\Phi^{*}(-c_{2}),

with
\Phi^{*}(x):=1
for all
x
if
a<0
and
\Phi^{*}(x):=\Phi(x)
if
a=0
.

This paper is organized as follows: in the next section the result of Theorem
1.1
is discussed followed by two illustrating examples. In Section
3
we present scheme of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Technical details are postponed to the last section.

2.
Discussions and Examples

Remark 2.1
.

Observe that according to
[
13
, Lemma 2]
as
u\to\infty

\frac{1}{u^{2}}\phi(\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c})\sim a\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\}

if
a>0
, and

\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{2}\right)\Phi^{*}(-c_{2})\frac{1}{u}\phi(\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}u+\boldsymbol{c})\sim\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\}

if
a\leq 0
and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}=(1,0).
Hence by Theorem
1.1
for
a>0
we have that

\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)\sim aC(a)\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\}

as
u\to\infty
, where
C(a)
is as in Theorem
1.1
. If
a\leq 0
, then

\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)\sim\frac{4}{2-\gamma_{1}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\},\ \ u\to\infty.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\gamma_{1}=0
. By the Theorem
1.1
we obtain that for
a>0

\psi_{\gamma}(u,au)\sim C(a)\frac{1}{u^{2}}\phi(\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c})\sim aC(a)\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\},

where

C(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,s)\geq\boldsymbol{0}}:\begin{array}[]{ccc}B^{*}_{1}(t)-t>x_{1}\\
B^{*}_{2}(t)-at+\gamma_{2}(B_{2}(s)-as)>x_{2}\end{array}\right\}e^{x_{1}+ax_{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}.

If
a\leq 0
and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}=(1,0)
, then

\psi_{\gamma}(u,au)\sim C(a)\frac{1}{u}\phi(\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}u+\boldsymbol{c})\sim 2\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\},

where

C(a)=2\sqrt{2\pi}e^{\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{2}}\Phi^{*}(-c_{2}),

with
\Phi^{*}(x)
as in Theorem
1.1
.

In particular, if
\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{1}=0
, then for the case
a>0
the constant
C(a)
can be simplified:

C(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\geq 0}:\begin{array}[]{ccc}B_{1}(t)-t>x_{1}\\
B_{2}(t)-at>x_{2}\end{array}\right\}e^{x_{1}+ax_{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}.

Notice that the last observation is consistent with the result
[
14
, Thm 2.1]
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=1
. According to Theorem
1.1
for
a>0

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,1],s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,t]}:\boldsymbol{B}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})+\boldsymbol{c}\boldsymbol{s}>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}\sim C(a)\frac{1}{u^{2}}\phi(\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c})\sim aC(a)\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\},

where

C(a)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\geq\boldsymbol{0}}:\boldsymbol{B}^{*}(t)-\boldsymbol{a}t+\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{s}>\boldsymbol{x}\right\}e^{x_{1}+ax_{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}.

If
a\leq 0
and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}=(1,0)
, then

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{t\in[0,1],s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,t]}:\boldsymbol{B}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})+\boldsymbol{c}\boldsymbol{s}>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}\sim C(a)\frac{1}{u}\phi(\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}u+\boldsymbol{c})\sim 2\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\},

where

C(a)=2\sqrt{2\pi}e^{\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{2}}\Phi^{*}(-c_{2}).

Remark 2.4
.

If
a>0
, the constant cannot be calculated explicitly, but one can obtain the following upper bound on
C(a)
:

C(a)\leq\frac{16}{a(2-\gamma_{1})(2-\gamma_{2})}<\infty.

Proof.

Since for the infinite ruin of Brownian motion

\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{t\geq 0}(B(t)-ct)>u\right\}=e^{-2cu},\quad u>0

one can conclude that
Y_{c}=\sup_{t\geq 0}(B(t)-ct)
has exponential distribution with mean
1/(2c)
.
Notice that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\geq\boldsymbol{0}}:\boldsymbol{B}^{*}(t)-\boldsymbol{a}t+\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{x}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sup_{t_{1}\geq 0}(B^{*}(t_{1})-t_{1})+\gamma_{1}\sup_{s_{1}\geq 0}(B(s_{1})-s_{1})>x_{1}\\
\sup_{t_{2}\geq 0}(B^{*}(t_{2})-at_{2})+\gamma_{2}\sup_{s_{2}\geq 0}(B(s_{2})-as_{2})>x_{2}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}Y^{*}_{1}+\gamma_{1}Y_{1}>x_{1}\\
Y^{*}_{a}+\gamma_{2}Y_{a}>x_{2}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{*}_{1}+\gamma_{1}Y_{1}>x_{1}\right\}\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{*}_{a}+\gamma_{2}Y_{a}>x_{2}\right\},

where
Y_{1},Y_{a},Y_{1}^{*},Y_{a}^{*}
are independent exponentially distributed random variables with

\mathbb{E}\{Y^{*}_{1}\}=\mathbb{E}\{Y_{1}\}=\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }\mathbb{E}\{Y^{*}_{a}\}=\mathbb{E}\{Y_{a}\}=\frac{1}{2a}.

Hence by the independence

\displaystyle C(a):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\geq\boldsymbol{0}}:\boldsymbol{B}^{*}(t)-\boldsymbol{a}t+\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{x}\right\}e^{x_{1}+ax_{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a}\mathbb{E}\{e^{Y^{*}_{1}+\gamma_{1}Y_{1}}\}\mathbb{E}\{e^{aY^{*}_{a}+\gamma_{2}aY_{a}}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\mathbb{E}\{e^{Y^{*}_{1}}\}\mathbb{E}\{e^{\gamma_{1}Y_{1}}\}\mathbb{E}\{e^{aY^{*}_{a}}\}\mathbb{E}\{e^{\gamma_{2}aY_{a}}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\mathbb{E}\{e^{Y^{*}_{1}}\}\mathbb{E}\{e^{\gamma_{1}Y_{1}}\}\mathbb{E}\{e^{aY^{*}_{a}}\}\mathbb{E}\{e^{\gamma_{2}aY_{a}}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\frac{2}{2-1}\frac{2}{2-\gamma_{1}}\frac{2a}{2a-a}\frac{2a}{2a-\gamma_{2}a}

\displaystyle=\frac{16}{a(2-\gamma_{1})(2-\gamma_{2})},

where we used the fact that a moment generating function of an exponential random variable
X
with
\mathbb{E}\{X\}=\frac{1}{\lambda}
is equal to
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}
for
t<\lambda
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Before we prove Theorem
1.1
, we introduce some auxiliary notation. Denote

E=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in[0,1]^{3}:\max(x_{2},x_{3})\leq x_{1}\},

hence we can rewrite

\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,s_{1},s_{2})\in E}:X_{1}(t,s_{1},s_{2})>u,X_{2}(t,s_{1},s_{2})>au\right\},

where for
i=1,2

(1)

X_{i}(t,s_{1},s_{2})=B_{i}(t)-c_{i}t-\gamma_{i}(B_{i}(s_{i})-c_{i}s_{i}),\ \ \ (t,s_{1},s_{2})\in E.

Let
\Lambda,u>0
and define

\Delta(u,\Lambda):=\left[1-\frac{\Lambda}{u^{2}},1\right]\times\left[0,\frac{\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]\times\left[0,\frac{\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right].

To simplify notation, we will omit the parameters
u
and
\Lambda
and simply write
\Delta
, implying that it depends on
u
and
\Lambda
. For any fixed
\Lambda>0
and large
u
we have that
\Delta\in E
.

To verify Theorem
1.1
, divide the set
E
into several parts and address each part separately. For
\Lambda,u>0
introduce two auxiliary functions

\displaystyle M(u,\Lambda):=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,s_{1},s_{2})\in\Delta}:X_{1}(t,s_{1},s_{2})>u,X_{2}(t,s_{1},s_{2})>au\right\},

\displaystyle m(u,\Lambda):=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,s_{1},s_{2})\in\Delta^{c}}:X_{1}(t,s_{1},s_{2})>u,X_{2}(t,s_{1},s_{2})>au\right\},

where for a set
A\subset E
we denote by
A^{c}
the set
E\setminus A,
with ”
E\setminus A
” being the set difference between
E
and
A
.

It turns out that the main contribution to the asymptotics of
\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)
comes from
M(u,\Lambda)
, while
m(u,\Lambda)
is irrelevant for the asymptotics.

Lemma 3.1
.

1. Let
\phi
be the density funtion of
(B_{1}(1),B_{2}(1))
. If
a>0
, then

(2)

\displaystyle M(u,\Lambda)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle C(a,\Lambda)\frac{1}{u^{2}}\phi(\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}),\quad u\to\infty,

where

(3)

\displaystyle C(a,\Lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in[0,\Lambda]^{3}}:\boldsymbol{B}^{*}(t)-\boldsymbol{a}t+\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{x}\right\}e^{x_{1}+ax_{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}\in(0,\infty),

and
\boldsymbol{B}^{*}
is an independent copy of
\boldsymbol{B}.

2. If
a=0
, then

(4)

\displaystyle M(u,\Lambda)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle C(a,\Lambda)\frac{1}{u}\phi(\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}),\quad u\to\infty,

where the constant
C(a,\Lambda)
is equal to

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{I}(x_{2}<0)\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,s)\in[0,\Lambda]^{2}}:B^{*}_{1}(t)-t+\gamma_{1}(B_{1}(s)-s)>x_{1}\right\}e^{x_{1}-\frac{x_{2}^{2}-2c_{2}x_{2}}{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}\in(0,\infty).

If
a<0
, then

(6)

\displaystyle M(u,\Lambda)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle C(a,\Lambda)\frac{1}{u}\phi(\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}u+\boldsymbol{c}),\ \quad u\to\infty,

where

(7)

\displaystyle C(a,\Lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,s)\in[0,\Lambda]^{2}}:B^{*}_{1}(t)-t+\gamma_{1}(B_{1}(s)-s)>x_{1}\right\}e^{x_{1}-\frac{x_{2}^{2}-2c_{2}x_{2}}{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}\in(0,\infty).

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
denote by
A_{\varepsilon}
the following set

A_{\varepsilon}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in[0,1]^{3}:\max(x_{2},x_{3})\leq x_{1}\leq\varepsilon\}\subset E.

Introduce, in addition, the set
A_{0}
which is defined by

A_{0}(\Lambda,u):=\left(\left[1-\frac{\Lambda\ln^{2}u}{u^{2}},1\right]\times\left[0,\frac{\Lambda\ln^{2}u}{u^{2}}\right]\times\left[0,\frac{\Lambda\ln^{2}u}{u^{2}}\right]\right)\setminus\Delta.

Observe that
A_{0}(\Lambda,u)\subset E
for large
u
. For simplicity we will write
A_{0}
. It follows that

m(u,\Lambda)\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{\varepsilon}}:\boldsymbol{X}(t,\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{0}}:\boldsymbol{X}(t,\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{1}}:\boldsymbol{X}(t,\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\},

where
A_{1}:=A_{1}(\Lambda,u):=(\Delta\cup A_{0}\cup A_{\varepsilon})^{c}
and
\boldsymbol{X}
is given by (
1
). Thus it is enough to estimate separately

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{\varepsilon}}:\boldsymbol{X}(t,\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\},\ \ \ \ \mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{0}}:\boldsymbol{X}(t,\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{1}}:\boldsymbol{X}(t,\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

i)
For any
\delta>0
there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta)>0
such that

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{\varepsilon}}:\boldsymbol{B}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{c}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\left(\exp\left(-\delta u^{2}\right)\right),\quad u\to\infty.

ii)
Moreover,

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{1}}:\boldsymbol{B}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{c}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}=o(\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)-\boldsymbol{c}>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}),\quad u\to\infty.

iii)
There exist positive constants
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
such that

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{0}}:\boldsymbol{B}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{c}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}\leq G_{1}e^{-G_{2}\Lambda}\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)-\boldsymbol{c}>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\},\quad u\to\infty.

Observe that for
\delta>(1+a^{2})/2
we have that
\exp\left(-\delta u^{2}\right)=o(\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\})
.

Assume here and below
\delta=1+a^{2}
. As it was shown above, there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta)
such that

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{\varepsilon}}:\boldsymbol{B}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{c}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}=o(\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\}),\ \ u\to\infty.

By the results of Lemma
3.2

\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{m(u,\Lambda)}{\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\}}=0.

Using Lemma
3.1
and observations discussed in Remark
2.1
one can therefore conclude that

\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{m(u,\Lambda)}{\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)}=0.

Since
M(u,\Lambda)\leq\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)\leq M(u,\Lambda)+m(u,\Lambda)
,

\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{M(u,\Lambda)}{\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)}=1.

To complete the proof, we apply the result of Lemma
3.1
together with the monotone convergence theorem to obtain the desired asymptotics of

\psi_{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{a}u)
. For the reader’s convenience, we consider the cases
a>0
and
a\leq 0
separately. If
a>0
, then by Lemma

F,F^{*}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,s_{1})\in\left(\left[1-\frac{\ln^{2}u\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]\times\left[0,\frac{\ln^{2}u\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]\right)\setminus\left(\left[1-\frac{\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]\times\left[0,\frac{\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]\right)}:B_{1}(t)-c_{1}t-\gamma_{1}(B_{1}(s_{1})-c_{1}s_{1})>u\right\}

\displaystyle\leq F^{*}e^{-F\Lambda}\mathbb{P}\left\{B_{1}(1)>u+c_{1}\right\}.

We divide now the set

\left(\left[1-\frac{\ln^{2}u\Lambda}{u^{2}},1\right]\times\left[0,\frac{\ln^{2}u\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]\right)\setminus\left(\left[1-\frac{\Lambda}{u^{2}},1\right]\times\left[0,\frac{\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]\right)

into rectangles

\Delta_{k,l}:=\left[1-\frac{(k+1)\Lambda}{u^{2}},1-\frac{k\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]\times\left[\frac{l\Lambda}{u^{2}},\frac{(l+1)\Lambda}{u^{2}}\right]

for
k^{2}+l^{2}\neq 0
.
Using similar ideas as in the case
a>0
we find positive constants
C^{*},C_{0}^{*},C_{1}^{*}
such that

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,s_{1})\in\Delta_{k,l}}:B_{1}(t)-c_{1}t-\gamma_{1}(B_{1}(s_{1})-c_{1}s_{1})>u\right\}\leq C^{*}e^{-(C_{0}^{*}k+C_{1}^{*}l_{1})\Lambda}\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\}

and then by summing estimates for all sets
\Delta_{k,l}
with
k^{2}+l^{2}\neq 0

we can find constants
G_{0},G_{1},G_{2}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists_{(t,\boldsymbol{s})\in A_{0}}:\boldsymbol{B}(t)-\boldsymbol{c}t-\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{s})-\boldsymbol{c}\boldsymbol{s})>\boldsymbol{a}u\right\}\leq G_{0}e^{-G_{2}\Lambda}\mathbb{P}\left\{B_{1}(1)>u+c_{1}\right\}\leq G_{1}e^{-G_{2}\Lambda}\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{B}(1)>\boldsymbol{a}u+\boldsymbol{c}\right\}.

That finishes the proof for the case
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
.

The cases
\gamma_{1}=0
or
\gamma_{2}=0
can be derived from the case
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
using the same argument as in the proof of inequality (
9
).

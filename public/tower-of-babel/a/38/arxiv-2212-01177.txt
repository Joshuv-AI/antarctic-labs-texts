Douglas’ factorization theorem and atomic system in Hilbert pro − C ∗ − -C^{\ast}- module
Mohamed Rossafi 1∗ , Roumaissae Eljazzar 2 and Ram Mohapatra 3

Abstract
. In the present paper we introduce the generalized inverse operators which have an interesting role in operator theory. We establish Douglas’ factorization theorem type for Hilbert pro- C ∗ C^{\ast} -module. We introduce the notion of atomic system and of K K -frame in Hilbert pro- C ∗ C^{\ast} -module and we study the relationship between them. We also demonstrat some properties of K K -frame by using Douglas’ factorization theorem.Finally we demonstrate that the sum of two K K -frames in a Hilbert pro- C ∗ C^{\ast} -module with certain conditions is once again a K K -frame.

We start by defining the bounded generalized inverse module map. We give an equivalente characterization of a bounded generalized inverse
\mathcal{A}
-module map, which is the main tool to obtain Douglas’ type factor decomposition theorem of some uniformly bounded module maps.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be two Hilbert pro-
C^{*}
-modules over a pro-
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}

T^{\dagger}\in Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

(1)

TT^{\dagger}T=T

(2)

T^{\dagger}TT^{\dagger}=T^{\dagger}

(3)

\left(TT^{\dagger}\right)^{*}=TT^{\dagger};

(4)

\left(T^{\dagger}T\right)^{*}=T^{\dagger}T
,

is called a bounded generalized inverse module map of

.

Remark 2.2
.

According to the conditions of the previous definition, we can easily deduce that
T^{\dagger}
is unique. In fact suppose that
T
has another generalized inverse which we denote by
T^{\prime}
. The definition
2.1
implies
Ran\left(TT^{\dagger}\right)=Ran(T)=Ran\left(TT^{\prime}\right)
and
Ran\left(T^{\dagger}T\right)=Ran\left(T^{*}\right)=Ran\left(T^{\prime}T\right)

TT^{\dagger},T^{\dagger}T,TT^{\prime},T^{\prime}T
are all projections, hence
TT^{\dagger}=TT^{\prime}
and
T^{\dagger}T=T^{\prime}T
. As a consequence,,
T^{\dagger}=T^{\dagger}TT^{\dagger}=T^{\dagger}TT^{\prime}=T^{\prime}TT^{\prime}=T^{\prime}
.

, it follows again by use of the functional calculus that

(2.2)

Note that

\mathcal{A}
such that

. Then by

\displaystyle p(\beta\alpha^{2}\beta)\geq\phi\left(\lambda\alpha^{2}\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\geq\phi\left(\lambda\beta^{2}\lambda\right)+\frac{m}{2}\phi\left(\lambda^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m+2}{2}\phi\left(\lambda\beta^{2}\lambda\right)=\frac{m+2}{2}p(\lambda\beta^{2}\lambda)

This shows that if

On the other hand, if

.

∎

\displaystyle\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\;\;\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

Therefore
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a
L
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
K\in Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
and
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
be a Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{X}
. Suppose that

Then

is
K
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
K\in Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
be an uniformly bounded operator such that
Ran(K)
is closed. The frame operator of a
K
-frame is invertible on the subspace
Ran(K)
of
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
is a
K
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
. Then there exists
A>0
such that

A\langle K^{\ast}\xi,K^{\ast}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\left\langle\xi,\xi_{i}\right\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\left\langle\xi_{i},\xi\right\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}

(4.3)

Since
Ran(K)
is closed, then
KK^{\dagger}\xi=\xi
, for all
\xi\in Ran(K)
. That is,

\left.KK^{\dagger}\right|_{Ran(K)}=I_{Ran(K)}

we have
I_{Ran(K)}^{*}=\left(\left.K^{\dagger}\right|_{Ran(K)}\right)^{*}K^{*}
.
For any
\xi\in Ran(K)
, we obtain

\langle\xi,\xi\rangle=\langle\left(\left.K^{\dagger}\right|_{R(K)}\right)^{*}K^{*}\xi,\left(\left.K^{\dagger}\right|_{R(K)}\right)^{*}K^{*}\xi\rangle\leq\left\|K^{\dagger}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\langle K^{*}\xi,K^{*}\xi\rangle

Therefore

\langle K^{*}\xi,K^{*}\xi\rangle\geq\left\|K^{\dagger}\right\|_{\infty}^{-2}\cdot\langle\xi,\xi\rangle

In combination with
4.3
, we obtain

\sum_{i\in I}\left\langle\xi,\xi_{i}\right\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\left\langle\xi_{i},\xi\right\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\geq A\langle K^{\ast}\xi,K^{\ast}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\geq A\left\|K^{\dagger}\right\|_{\infty}^{-2}\cdot\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\;\;\text{for all}\;\xi\in Ran(K).

Hence, by the definition of
K
-frame, we get

A\left\|K^{\dagger}\right\|_{\infty}^{-2}\cdot\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\left\langle\xi,\xi_{i}\right\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\left\langle\xi_{i},\xi\right\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\;\;\forall\xi\in Ran(K)

Therefore

A\left\|K^{\dagger}\right\|_{\infty}^{-2}\cdot\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\langle S\xi,S\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}

And so
S:Ran(K)\rightarrow Ran(S)
is a bounded linear operator and invertible on
Ran(K)
.

∎

Theorem 4.11

-frame for

, then there exists
A>0
such that
L\geq AKK^{*}
and
L_{0}\geq L_{1}+L_{2}=L\geq AKK^{*}
. From which
\left\{\xi_{i}+L_{1}^{m}\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I_{1}}\cup\left\{\xi_{i}+L_{2}^{n}\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I_{2}}
is a
K
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
.

∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
and
\left\{\eta_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
be Parseval
K
-frames for
\mathcal{X}
, with synthesis operators
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
respectively. If
L_{1}L_{2}^{*}=0
then
\left\{\xi_{i}+\eta_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
is a
2
-tight
K
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
and
\left\{\eta_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
are two Parseval
K
-frames for
\mathcal{X}
. Then there are transform operators
L_{1},L_{2}\in Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
such that
L_{1}e_{i}=\xi_{i}
and
L_{2}e_{i}=\eta_{i}
with
Ran(K)=Ran\left(L_{1}\right),Ran(K)=Ran\left(L_{2}\right)
respectively. For each
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\left\langle\xi,\xi_{i}+\eta_{i}\right\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\left\langle\xi_{i}+\eta_{i},\xi\right\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\langle\left(L_{1}+L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi,\left(L_{1}+L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=\langle L_{1}^{*}\xi,L_{1}^{*}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}+\left\langle L_{2}L_{1}^{*}\xi,\xi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle+\left\langle L_{1}L_{2}^{*}\xi,L_{1}L_{2}^{*}\xi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}+\langle L_{2}^{*}\xi,L_{2}^{*}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=\langle L_{1}^{*}\xi,L_{1}^{*}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}+\langle L_{2}^{*}\xi,L_{2}^{*}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=2\langle K^{*}\xi,K^{*}\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}

∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
and
\left\{\eta_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
be
K
-frames for
\mathcal{X}
, and let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be synthesis operators for sequences
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
and
\left\{\eta_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
respectively, such that
L_{1}L_{2}^{*}=0
and let
T_{j}\in Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
an operator uniformly bounded with
R\left(L_{j}\right)\subseteq R\left(T_{j}L_{j}\right)
, for
j=1,2
. Then
\left\{T_{1}\xi_{i}+T_{2}\eta_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
is a
K
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\left\{\xi_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
and
\left\{\eta_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
are two
K
-frames for
\mathcal{X}
. Then by Theorem
4.5
, there exists an orthonormal basis
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
such that
L_{1}e_{i}=\xi_{i},L_{2}e_{i}=\eta_{i}

and
R(K)\subseteq R\left(L_{1}\right),R(K)\subseteq R\left(L_{2}\right)
. For each
\xi\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\langle\xi,T_{1}\xi_{i}+T_{2}\eta_{i}\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\langle\xi,T_{1}\xi_{i}+T_{2}\eta_{i}\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\langle\xi,T_{1}T_{1}L_{1}e_{i}+T_{2}L_{2}e_{i}\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\langle\xi,T_{1}L_{1}e_{i}+T_{2}L_{2}e_{i}\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\langle\left(T_{1}L_{1}+T_{2}L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi,\left(T_{1}L_{1}+T_{2}L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\left(T_{1}L_{1}\right)^{*}\xi,\left(T_{1}L_{1}\right)^{*}\xi\rangle+\left\langle T_{2}L_{2}L_{1}^{*}T_{1}^{*}\xi,\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle T_{1}L_{1}L_{2}^{*}T_{2}^{*}\xi,\xi\right\rangle+\langle\left(T_{2}L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi,\left(T_{2}L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\left(T_{1}L_{1}\right)^{*}\xi,\left(T_{1}L_{1}\right)^{*}\xi\rangle+\langle\left(T_{2}L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi,\left(T_{2}L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi\rangle

We have that
Ran(K)\subseteq Ran\left(L_{j}\right)\subseteq Ran\left(T_{j}L_{j}\right)
for
j=1,2
. So by Douglas’ factorization theorem, for each
j=1,2
, there exists
\alpha_{j}>0
such that

KK^{*}\leq\alpha_{j}\left(T_{j}L_{j}\right)\left(T_{j}L_{j}\right)^{*}

Then from the above inequality, for each
\xi\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\langle\xi,T_{1}\xi_{i}+T_{2}\eta_{i}\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}\langle\xi,T_{1}\xi_{i}+T_{2}\eta_{i}\rangle_{M(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\langle\left(T_{1}L_{1}\right)^{*}\xi,\left(T_{1}L_{1}\right)^{*}\xi\rangle+\langle\left(T_{2}L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi,\left(T_{2}L_{2}\right)^{*}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}\right)\langle K^{*}\xi,K^{*}\xi\rangle

Hence
\left\{T_{1}\xi_{i}+T_{2}\eta_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
is a
K
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
.

∎

Some new Bollobás-type inequalities
Erfei Yue

Abstract




Abstract

A family of disjoint pairs of finite sets
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
is called a Bollobás system if
A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
for every
i\neq j
,
and a skew Bollobás system if
A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
for every
i<j
.
Bollobás proved that for a Bollobás system, the inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}^{-1}\leqslant 1

holds. Hegedüs and Frankl generalized this theorem to skew Bollobás systems with the inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}^{-1}\leqslant 1+n,

provided
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n]
. In this paper, we improve this inequality to

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left((1+|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|)\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}\right)^{-1}\leqslant 1

with probabilistic method. We also generalize this result to partitions of sets on both symmetric and skew cases.

1
Introduction

Let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
be an
n
-element set. A family
\mathcal{F}
consisting of some subsets of
[n]
is the main object of the research on extremal set theory.
The following concepts of Bollobás system and skew Bollobás system were introduced by Bollobás
[
3
]
and Frankl
[
2
,
4
]
respectively.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
is a family of pairs of sets, where
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n]
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is called a
Bollobás system
if

A_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset\Leftrightarrow i=j;

and a
skew Bollobás system
if

(1)

A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
,

(2)

A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
when
i<j
.

To solve a problem on hypergraphs, Bollobás
[
3
]
proved the following theorem in 1965.
In 1975, Tarján
[
20
]
gave a more elegant proof for it independently.

Theorem 1.2

(Bollobás
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
be a Bollobás system, where
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n]
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}}\leqslant 1.

The following uniform version is a corollary of Theorem
1.2
for
|A_{i}|=a
and
|B_{i}|=b
,
and it was proved by Jaeger and Payan
[
8
]
in 1971 and Katona
[
10
]
in 1974 independently.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
be a Bollobás system, where
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n]
, and
|A_{i}|=a,|B_{i}|=b
for any
i
.
Then
m\leqslant\binom{a+b}{a}
.

Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
play a pivotal role in extremal set theory. They has spurred significant research interest,
leading to generalizations for vector spaces by Lovász
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
in 1977 and affine spaces by Hegedüs
[
6
]
in 2015.

In 1982, using a creative polynomial method, Frankl
[
4
]
proved that the inequality of Corollary
1.3

remains true if
\mathcal{P}
is a skew Bollobás system (instead of Bollobás system).
However, the condition of Theorem
1.2
cannot be weakened in the same way.
So it is natural to ask what can we say for a (nonuniform) skew Bollobás system.
In 2023, Hegedüs and Frankl
[
7
]
answered the question with the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4

(Hegedüs and Frankl
[
7
]
)
.

Suppose
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n],i\in[m]
, and
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
is a skew Bollobás system. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}}\leqslant 1+n.

Although the inequality in Theorem
1.4
is tight, we can still strengthen it in the following way.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq[n],i\in[m]
, and
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
is a skew Bollobás system. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(1+|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|)\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}}\leqslant 1.

In 1984, Furedi
[
5
]
investigated the threshold (or
t
-intersecting) case of Bollobás-type theorem.
Subsequent work by Zhu
[
23
]
, Talbot
[
19
]
, and Kang, Kim, and Kim
[
9
]
further extended the results.
Recently, more variations of Bollobás-type theorem are studied,
such as Király, Nagy, Pálvölgyi, and Visontai
[
11
]
, O’Neill and Verstraëte
[
17
]
,
Scott and Wilmer
[
18
]
, and Yu, Kong, Xi, Zhang, and Ge
[
22
]
.

The uniform version of Bollobás Theorem (Theorem
1.3
) was generalized to
r
-partitions by Alon
[
1
]
.
More specifically, the following theorem decides the maximum cardinality of a (skew) Bollobás system in which every set has a fixed size in each part of the ground set.

Theorem 1.6

(Alon
[
1
]
)
.

Suppose
X=[n]
is the disjoint union of some sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
.
For some fixed
a_{k},b_{k}
, a skew Bollobás system
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
satisfies that

A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq X,|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|=a_{k},|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|=b_{k},\forall i\in[m],k\in[r].

Then we have

m\leqslant\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{a_{k}+b_{k}}{a_{k}}.

In the following theorem, we generalize Theorem
1.6
to the nonuniform version.

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose that
X=[n]
is the disjoint union of some sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
, and
|X_{k}|=n_{k}
.
For some
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq X
,
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
is a skew Bollobás system. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|}\right)^{-1}\leqslant\prod_{k=1}^{r}(1+n_{k})\leqslant\left(1+\frac{n}{r}\right)^{r}.

(1)

This theorem shows two things. Firstly, if we fixed a partition
X=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{r}
, then the maximum value of the left hand side of (
1
) is
\prod_{k=1}^{r}(1+n_{k})
.
Secondly, if we only fixed the ground set
X
, while the partition is variable, then the maximum value of it is
\left(1+\frac{n}{r}\right)^{r}
.
The first inequality in (
1
) is tight for all
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
, and the second inequality is also tight if
r|n
, and
X_{k}
’s are divided equally.
Note that the case
r=1
is Theorem
1.4
.

We can also consider the symmetric version of Theorem
1.7
. Surprisingly, this time the symmetric version is harder than the skew version.
For the case
r=2
, we can prove the following tight inequality for a Bollobás system.

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose that
X=[n]
is the disjoint union of some sets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
,
and for some
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq X
,
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
is a Bollobás system. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{1}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{1}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{1}|}\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{2}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{2}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{2}|}\right)^{-1}\leqslant 1+\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor.

And for an arbitrary
r\geqslant 1
, we can prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.9
.

Suppose that
X=[n]
is the disjoint union of some sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
,
and for some
A_{i},B_{i}\subseteq X
,
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
is a Bollobás system. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|}\right)^{-1}\leqslant\left(1+\frac{n}{r}\right)^{r-1}.

The case
r=1
is the original Bollobás Theorem (Theorem
1.2
). And the case
r=2
leads to an inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{1}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{1}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{1}|}\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{2}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{2}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{2}|}\right)^{-1}\leqslant 1+\frac{n}{2},

which is slightly weaker than the one in Theorem
1.8
, but still tight for even
n
’s.
For the case
r\geqslant 3
, unfortunately, the upper bound given in Theorem
1.9
does not seem to be tight.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 focuses on proving Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.7
. We also discuss the tightness of Theorem
1.7
.
Section 3 presents proofs for Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.9
.
Additionally, we leverage these theorems to derive a generalized form of the LYM-inequality.

2
Proof of the skew version and its tightness

For a fixed (finite) set
X
, let
S(X)
be the group consisting of all permutations on
X
, and
S_{n}=S([n])
.
For a permutation
\sigma\in S(X)
and a subset
A\subseteq X
, denote
\sigma(A)
the image of
A
.
In other words,
\sigma(A)=\{\sigma(a)\mid a\in A\}
.
In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.7
with probabilistic method.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Pick a random permutation
\sigma\in S_{n+1}
uniformly. Note that

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{(\sigma(A_{i}),\sigma(B_{i}))\mid(A_{i},B_{i})\in\mathcal{P}\}

is still a skew Bollobás system. The order of elements in
[n+1]
is rearranged by
\sigma
.
For any
i
, let
E_{i}
be the event that all elements of
A_{i}
go before
B_{i}
, and they are separated by the extra element
n+1
.
In other words,

E_{i}=\{\sigma\in S_{n+1}\mid\sigma(a)<\sigma(n+1)<\sigma(b),\forall a\in A_{i},b\in B_{i}\}.

Now we claim that
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
cannot happen at the same time if
i\neq j
. Suppose for the contradiction that
\sigma\in E_{i}\cap E_{j}
.
For any
a\in A_{i}
and
b\in B_{j}
, we have
\sigma(a)<\sigma(n+1)<\sigma(b)
. Hence
\sigma(A_{i})\cap\sigma(B_{j})=\emptyset
.
Similarly, we can prove that
\sigma(A_{j})\cap\sigma(B_{i})=\emptyset
. That is a contradiction to the fact that
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a skew Bollobás system.
Then we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}(E_{i}).

To finish the proof, we need to calculate the probability of each

.
Note that we only need to consider the order of elements in
\sigma(A_{i}\cup B_{i}\cup\{n+1\})
.
Within the
1+|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|
places, there is exactly
1
slot for
n+1
.
And for the elements in
A_{i}\cup B_{i}
, the chance of any
|A_{i}|

There are
\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}
different possibilities, and exactly

of them is what we need.
Hence
\mathbb{P}(E_{i})=\left((1+|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|)\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}\right)^{-1}
, and so

1\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(1+|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|)\binom{|A_{i}|+|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}}.

Using a similar idea, we can also prove Theorem
1.7
, but the progress would be more tricky.
For a finite set
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
, where
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{k}
, let
X^{+}=X\cup\{x_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\}

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Without loss of generality, suppose
X_{1}=\{1,\ldots,n_{1}\},X_{2}=\{n_{1}+1,\ldots,n_{1}+n_{2}\},\ldots,X_{r}=\{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r-1}+1,\ldots,n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}\}
,
where
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
. Let
X^{\prime}=X_{1}^{+}\cup\cdots\cup X_{r}^{+}
, and

\Omega=\{\sigma\in S(X^{\prime})\mid\sigma(X_{k}^{+})=X_{k}^{+},\forall k\in[r]\}.

Pick a random permutation
\sigma\in\Omega
uniformly. Then

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{(\sigma(A_{i}),\sigma(B_{i}))\mid(A_{i},B_{i})\in\mathcal{P}\}

is still a skew Bollobás system. For any
i\in[m]
and
k\in[r]
, let

E_{ik}=\{\sigma\in\Omega\mid\sigma(a)<\sigma(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}+1/2)<\sigma(b),\forall a\in A_{i}\cap X_{k},b\in B_{i}\cap X_{k}\},

and

E_{i}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}E_{ik}.

Note that for a fixed
i
, the events
E_{ik}
’s are independent. Using this fact and the similar argument adopted in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(E_{i})&=\prod_{k=1}^{r}\mathbb{P}(E_{ik})=\prod_{k=1}^{r}\left((1+|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|)\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|}\right)^{-1}\\
&\geqslant\prod_{k=1}^{r}\left((1+n_{k})\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|}\right)^{-1}.\end{split}

Now we claim
E_{i}\cap E_{j}=\emptyset
for distinct
i,j
. Suppose for the contradiction that
\sigma\in E_{i}\cap E_{j}
.
Then for any
k
, we have
\sigma\in E_{ik}\cap E_{jk}
,so
\sigma(a)<\sigma(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}+\frac{1}{2})<\sigma(b)
for any
a\in A_{i}\cap X_{k},b\in B_{j}\cap X_{k}
.
Then
\sigma(A_{i}\cap X_{k})
and
\sigma(B_{j}\cap X_{k})
are disjoint. Similarly, we can prove that
\sigma(A_{j}\cap X_{k})
and
\sigma(B_{i}\cap X_{k})
are disjoint.
Hence
\sigma(A_{i})\cap\sigma(B_{j})=\sigma(A_{j})\cap\sigma(B_{i})=\emptyset
, which contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a skew Bollobás system.

1\geqslant\mathbb{P}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}E_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{P}(E_{i})\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{r}\left((1+n_{k})\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|}\right)^{-1},

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|}\right)^{-1}\leqslant\prod_{k=1}^{r}(1+n_{k})\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k=1}^{r}(1+n_{k})\right)^{r}=\left(1+\frac{n}{r}\right)^{r}.

∎

The next example shows the tightness of Theorem
1.7
.

Example 1
.

Suppose
X=[n]
is the disjoint union of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
, where
|X_{k}|=n_{k}
.
Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{2^{n}}
be all subsets of
X
, and
|A_{1}|\geqslant\cdots\geqslant|A_{2^{n}}|
.
Then
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},[n]\setminus A_{i})\mid i\in[2^{n}]\}
is a skew Bollobás system.
For a fixed
i
, let
|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|=a_{k}
, and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
is called the type of
A_{i}
.
Then the contribution of
A_{i}
to the sum is
\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{n_{k}}{a_{k}}^{-1}
.
On the other hand, there are altogether
\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{n_{k}}{a_{k}}
many of
A_{j}
’s that have the same type with
A_{i}
.
So the total contribution of a type of
A_{i}
’s to the sum is exactly
1
.
Note that there are
\prod_{k=1}^{r}(1+n_{k})
many of different types, because
a_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,n_{k}\}
, and each
a_{k}
’s are valued independently. Hence

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|+|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|}{|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{k=1}^{r}(1+n_{k}),

and so the first inequality of Theorem
1.7
is tight for any
n
and
r
.
In particular, if
r|n
, and
n_{1}=\cdots=n_{r}=\frac{n}{r}
, the second inequality is also tight.

A family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets is called an anti-chain, if
A\not\subseteq B
for any
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
.
The following LYM-inequality on anti-chains is introduced by Yamamoto
[
21
]
, Meshalkin
[
16
]
, and Lubell
[
15
]
,
and plays an important role in extremal set theory.

Theorem 3.2

(LYM-inequality
[
15
,
16
,
21
]
)
.

Suppose that
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\subseteq[n]
, and the family
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\}
is an anti-chain. Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n}{|F_{i}|}^{-1}\leqslant 1.

Let
A_{i}=F_{i},B_{i}=[n]\setminus F_{i}
, and
\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\mid i\in[m]\}
.
Then
\mathcal{F}
is an anti-chain implies
\mathcal{P}
is a Bollobás system.
So Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.9
lead to the following two variations of LYM-inequality.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose that
X=[n]
is the disjoint union of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
,
and for some
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\subseteq[n]
, the family
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\}
is an anti-chain. Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\binom{|X_{1}|}{|F_{i}\cap X_{1}|}\binom{|X_{2}|}{|F_{i}\cap X_{2}|}\right)^{-1}\leqslant 1+\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor.

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose that
X=[n]
is the disjoint union of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}
,
and for some
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\subseteq[n]
, the family
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\}
is an anti-chain. Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{|X_{k}|}{|F_{i}\cap X_{k}|}\right)^{-1}\leqslant\left(1+\frac{n}{r}\right)^{r-1}.

M T ​ N M_{TN} is all you need: production of multiple semi-invisible resonances at hadron colliders Journal: Physics Letters B
Zhongtian Dong, Kyoungchul Kong, Konstantin T. Matchev, Katia Matcheva

Abstract
The stransverse mass variable M T ​ 2 M_{T2} was originally proposed for the study of hadron collider events in which N = 2 N=2 parent particles are produced and then decay semi-invisibly. Here we consider the generalization to the case of N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 semi-invisibly decaying parent particles. We introduce the corresponding class of kinematic variables M T ​ N M_{TN} and illustrate their mathematical properties. Many of the celebrated features of the M T ​ 2 M_{T2} kinematic endpoint are retained in this more general case, including the ability to measure the mass of the invisible daughter particle from the stransverse mass kink. We describe and validate a numerical procedure for computing M T ​ N M_{TN} in practice. We also identify the configurations of visible momenta which result in nontrivial ( M T ​ N ≠ 0 M_{TN}\neq 0 ) values, and derive a pure phase-space estimate for the fraction of such events for any N N .

{\cal P}_{i}

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\vec{q}_{iT}=\not{\vec{p}}_{T}\equiv-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\vec{p}_{iT}-\vec{u}_{T}\,,

(1)

where we allow for the presence of additional visible radiation in the event with total transverse momentum

M_{T{\cal P}_{i}}(\vec{q}_{iT},\tilde{m}_{\chi})=\sqrt{m_{i}^{2}+\tilde{m}_{\chi}^{2}+2\big(E_{iT}\varepsilon_{iT}-\vec{p}_{iT}\cdot\vec{q}_{iT}\big)},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

(2)

where the transverse energies are defined as

E_{iT}=\sqrt{\vec{p}_{iT}^{\,2}+m_{i}^{2}},\quad\varepsilon_{iT}=\sqrt{\vec{q}_{iT}^{\,2}+\tilde{m}_{\chi}^{2}}\,.

(3)

In analogy to

M_{TN}(\tilde{m}_{\chi})\equiv\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{q}_{iT}\\
\sum_{i}\vec{q}_{iT}=\not{\vec{p}}_{T}\end{subarray}}\left\{\max_{i}\left[M_{T{\cal P}_{i}}(\vec{q}_{iT},\tilde{m}_{\chi})\right]\right\},

(4)

where the minimization over the individual invisible transverse momenta
\vec{q}_{iT}
is done subject to the constraint (
1

3
Mathematical properties of
M_{TN}

The construction outlined in the previous section guarantees that on an event-by-event basis the computed value of
M_{TN}
does not exceed the common mass
M_{\cal P}
of the parents
{\cal P}_{i}
(of course, provided that the correct daughter mass
m_{\chi}
has been used for the ansatz
\tilde{m}_{\chi}
). In other words,

M_{TN}(\tilde{m}_{\chi}=m_{\chi})\leq M_{\cal P}.

(5)

It is easy to see why this bound is always true — in the process of minimizing over
\vec{q}_{iT}
in eq. (
4
), we will eventually hit on the
actual
values of
\vec{q}_{iT}
in that event, for which
M_{TN}(m_{\chi})=M_{\cal P}
by construction. From there, exploring invisible momenta away from the actual ones can only further reduce the value of
M_{TN}
.

In light of the inequality (
5
),
M_{TN}
is a
mass-bound
kinematic variable and provides an event-by-event lower bound on the true invariant mass of the parent particle
Cheng:2008hk
(see also the general discussion in section IX.B of
Barr:2011xt
). However, the usefulness of such variables in practice crucially depends on whether the upper bound in (
5
) is saturated or not. If the bound is saturated, then the measurement of the upper kinematic endpoint
M_{TN}^{max}
of the
M_{TN}
distribution gives the parent mass
M_{\cal P}
as a function of the ansatz
\tilde{m}_{\chi}
:

M_{\cal P}(\tilde{m}_{\chi})=M_{TN}^{max}(\tilde{m}_{\chi}).

(6)

Fortunately, in our setup here, the bound in (
5
) is indeed saturated, and eq. (
6
) holds. This is most easily proven by example, i.e., by finding events with visible momentum configurations for which the minimization over
\vec{q}_{iT}
picks up the
actual
invisible momenta in the event. The one-dimensional colinear momentum configurations discussed below in Section
6
are precisely such events — one can easily check that either one of the two branches in eq. (
13
) satisfies eq. (
5
) (for a related discussion in the case of
M_{T2}
, see
Matchev:2009fh
).

In what follows, we shall focus on the limit of massless visible particles in the final state, i.e.
m_{i}=0,\forall i=1,2,\ldots,N
. This approximation is well motivated for the standard reconstructed objects like electrons, muons, photons and jets, especially at high
p_{iT}
, as required by the triggers. Furthermore, as we have already learned from numerous
M_{T2}
studies, in the massless limit the math simplifies a lot, while keeping the essential physics unchanged.

For
N\geq 3
, the
M_{TN}
kinematic distributions develop a new feature which is not present in the case of
M_{T2}
: a sizable fraction of events have trivial
M_{TN}
values (
M_{TN}=0

points sampled on a circle lie on one semi-circle. If the points on the circle are chosen at random, the answer is given by
2N/N^{2}
and therefore the probability
P_{trivial}
that we get a

P_{trivial}(N)=1-\frac{2N}{2^{N}}.

(7)

This prediction is plotted in Fig.

and hence there are no trivial solutions for

In order to check the validity of the result (
7

N

p_{(n)T}\equiv\text{max}_{n}\left\{p_{iT}\right\},\quad n=1,2,\ldots,N\,,

(8)

where we introduce the notation
\text{max}_{n}\{\cal S\}
to denote the
n

and fix its orientation (e.g., along the

p_{(n)T}\sim U(0,p_{(n-1)T}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \varphi_{(n)}\sim U(0,2\pi),\penalty\ \penalty\ n\geq 2.

(9)

Since the azimuthal angles

7

7

The exhaustive scan of the visible phase space also allowed us to check numerically whether the solutions for
M_{TN}
are balanced or not. (A balanced solution is obtained whenever the minimization in (

M_{T{\cal P}_{1}}=M_{T{\cal P}_{2}}=\ldots=M_{T{\cal P}_{N}}

In this section we describe and validate our numerical code for computing

\vec{p}_{1T}=(a,0)

\vec{q}_{1T}=(\frac{bc}{\varepsilon}-a,0)=(\frac{bc}{\varepsilon a}-1)\,\vec{p}_{1T}

6
Kink structure

A celebrated feature of
M_{T2}
is the existence of a kink (i.e., a discontinuity in the gradient) in the relationship (
6
) derived from the
M_{T2}
kinematic endpoint
Cho:2007qv
,
Gripaios:2007is
,
Barr:2007hy
,
Cho:2007dh
. The location of the kink is precisely at the true value of the daughter mass,
\tilde{m}_{\chi}=m_{\chi}
, which in principle allows for the determination of the masses of
all
the particles involved in the process.

The mathematics behind the
M_{T2}
kink can be easily extended to the case of
M_{TN}
, as we shall now show. In the massless case
m_{i}=0
considered here, the kink arises due to non-vanishing upstream transverse momentum
\vec{u}_{T}\neq 0

Burns:2008va
. For any given choice of the test mass
\tilde{m}_{\chi}
, there are two extreme kinematic configurations of the visible momenta which are potentially responsible for the
M_{TN}
kinematic endpoint
Burns:2008va
,
Matchev:2009fh
. Both configurations are purely one-dimensional, i.e., all relevant transverse vectors
\vec{p}_{1T},\vec{p}_{2T},\cdots,\vec{p}_{NT}
and
\vec{u}_{T}
are aligned along a common axis in the transverse plane. In the first configuration, responsible for the “left" side of the relation (
6
), the visible momenta
\vec{p}_{iT}
are all equal to each other, are parallel to
\vec{u}_{T}
, and have magnitude

p_{TL}\equiv\frac{M_{\cal P}^{2}-m_{\chi}^{2}}{2M_{\cal P}}\left(\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{u_{T}}{NM_{\cal P}}\right)^{2}}-\frac{u_{T}}{NM_{\cal P}}\right)\,.

(11)

In the second configuration, giving the “right" side of (
6
), the visible momenta
\vec{p}_{iT}
are all equal to each other, anti-parallel to
\vec{u}_{T}
, and have magnitude

7
Conclusions

With its continuing operations planned for the next 20 years, the LHC is entering an era when it will be sensitive to the simultaneous production of
N>2
heavy resonances. In many BSM scenarios motivated by the dark matter problem, these resonances are expected to decay semi-invisibly, giving rise to signatures with missing transverse momentum. Additional impetus for studying such event topologies is provided by the leptonic decays of the top quark and
W
boson in the Standard Model. In this letter we generalized the
M_{T2}
concept to the case of
M_{TN}
kinematic variables which are ideally suited for the analysis of these scenarios. We showed that the measurement of the upper kinematic endpoint in the
M_{TN}
distribution provides one constraint on the masses of the parent and daughter particles, while the kink structure (see Fig.
6
) is able to pin down those masses completely. We also identified the visible momentum configurations which correspond to
M_{TN}=0
, thus avoiding the need to numerically compute
M_{TN}
in those cases.

The
M_{TN}
idea is ripe for further investigations, following the same roadmap as in the
M_{T2}
case. For example, one can easily generalize the definition (
4
) to 3+1 dimensions along the lines of
Cho:2014naa
,
Cho:2014yma
,
Cho:2015laa
. It is also worth exploring the properties of
M_{TN}
beyond the massless approximation and for asymmetric event topologies, e.g., with different mother
Barr:2009jv
or daughter
Konar:2009qr
particles. The
M_{TN}
variables can also be used as input features in deep learning multi-variate approaches to LHC data analysis. One could even use deep learning to test whether
M_{TN}
are indeed optimal variables for their respective event topologies
Kim:2021pcz
. In any case, we are hoping that some
M_{TN}
will soon play a role in a new physics discovery at the LHC.

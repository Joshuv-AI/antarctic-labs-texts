Topological skyrmion semimetals
Shu-Wei Liu, Joe H. Winter, Ashley M. Cook

Abstract
We introduce topological skyrmion semimetal phases of matter, characterized by bulk electronic structures with topological defects in ground state observable textures over the Brillouin zone (BZ), rather than topological degeneracies in band structures. We present and characterize toy models for these novel topological phases, focusing on realizing such topological defects in the ground state spin expectation value texture over the BZ. We find generalized Fermi arc bulk-boundary correspondences and chiral anomaly response signatures, including Fermi arc-like states which do not terminate with topological band structure degeneracies in the bulk, but rather with topological defects in the spin texture of bulk insulators . We also consider novel boundary conditions for topological semimetals, in which the 3D bulk is mapped to a 2D bulk plus 0D defect. Given the experimental significance of topological semimetals, our work paves the way to broad experimental study of topological skyrmion phases and the quantum skyrmion Hall effect.

We introduce the topological skyrmion semimetals (TSSs) in this work, both to broadly generalize known topological semimetals and to facilitate the search for TSPs and the QSkHE in experiments. We first present recipes for constructing toy models inspired by Weyl semimetals, and then characterize bulk electronic structure, finding a bulk-boundary correspondence yielding generalizations of Fermi arc surface states, as well as a generalization of the chiral anomaly. Notably, we construct a three-band Bloch Hamiltonian toy model for a TSS, which exhibits generalized Fermi arc states for a bulk
insulator

\mathcal{Q}

Our work is therefore a foundation for broad generalization of concepts of topological semimetals
and insulators

Discussion and conclusion —
We introduce topological skyrmion semimetal (TSS) phases of matter by constructing toy models in which specifically the spin degree of freedom (dof) in systems with multiple dofs (in this case, a generalized particle-hole dof or orbital dof) can realize generalized Fermi arc surface states and chiral anomaly signatures of the spin subsystem. Remarkably, we utilize three-band models for 2D topological skyrmion phases to construct topological skyrmion semimetal Hamiltonians possessing Fermi arc-like surface states in
bulk insulators
, due to topological defects of the momentum-space spin texture. Our work therefore introduces a fundamental generalization of topological semimetals
and insulators
by considering topological phases associated with mappings to myriad observables, rather than the projectors onto occupied states. The three-band TSS is a very low-symmetry topological state, similar to the Weyl semimetal, which makes it a promising platform for experiments.

S2
Analytic calculation of Laudau levels of topological skyrmion semimetal

We analytically calculate the Landau levels in the case of no spin triplet pairing term, so that the diagonal blocks
h(\boldsymbol{k})
and
-h^{*}(\boldsymbol{k})
can be solved separately. Take the expansion of
h(\boldsymbol{k})
around
\boldsymbol{k}=0
:

h(\boldsymbol{k})=k_{x}\sigma_{x}+k_{y}\sigma_{y}+\left[\gamma-\cos(k_{z})+\frac{1}{2}\left(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}\right)\right]\sigma_{z}.

(S5)

Now we take the following gauge transformation:

\displaystyle k_{x}\rightarrow k_{x},\hskip 7.11317ptk_{y}\rightarrow k_{y}+eBx,\hskip 7.11317ptk_{z}\rightarrow k_{z},

so that we construct lowering and raising operators
a=\frac{k_{y}-ik_{x}}{\sqrt{2eB}}
and
a^{\dagger}=\frac{k_{y}+ik_{x}}{\sqrt{2eB}}
such that the following commutation relation is satisfied

[a,a^{\dagger}]=1,

(S6)

and
h(\boldsymbol{k})
can be recast to

\displaystyle h(\boldsymbol{k})=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2eB}\left(a\sigma_{+}+a^{\dagger}\sigma_{-}\right)

\displaystyle+\left[\gamma-\cos(k_{z})+eB\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]\sigma_{z},

(S7)

where

\sigma_{\pm}=\frac{\sigma_{x}\pm i\sigma_{y}}{2}

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{z}|\pm\rangle=\pm|\pm\rangle,\hskip 8.5359pt\sigma_{\pm}|\mp\rangle=|\pm\rangle.

We can therefore express the lowest Landau level as
|0,-\rangle
where the first index denotes the energy level and the second index denotes the spin. The form of Eq. (
S7
) is useful because the
a
operator in the
a\sigma_{+}
term acts on and annihilates the energy part of the ground state the
\sigma_{-}
operator in the
a^{\dagger}\sigma_{-}
term annihilates the spin part of the ground state. The ground state energy is therefore conveniently

E(k_{z})=-\left(\gamma-\cos{k_{z}}+\frac{eB}{2}\right).

(S8)

As for
-h^{*}(\boldsymbol{k})
, the only difference is that the
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{z}
terms pick up a minus sign, so we have

\displaystyle-h^{*}(\boldsymbol{k})=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2eB}\left(a^{\dagger}\sigma_{+}+a\sigma_{-}\right)

\displaystyle-\left[\gamma-\cos(k_{z})+eB\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]\sigma_{z},

(S9)

with the lowest Landau level
|0,+\rangle
and the energy

E(k_{z})=+\left(\gamma-\cos{k_{z}}+\frac{eB}{2}\right)

(S10)

S3
Low-energy theory of Weyl semimetal for 2D system plus defect

Here we derive a low-energy theory for the two-band Weyl semimetal phase with Hamiltonian defined in Eq.(1), for the specified boundary condition in this paper and a semi-infinite geometry. In order to approximate the wave functions around
x=0
, translational symmetry in the
\hat{y}
-direction is therefore broken while momentum component
k_{x}
remains as a good quantum number. Thus, a real-space coordinate is used in the
\hat{y}
-direction to label lattice sites. In addition, the specified geometry requires that the topological phase transition takes place at
\pm\boldsymbol{k}^{*}=\pm\cos^{-1}{\gamma}
, so to keep zero-energy states close to
x=0
, we denote
\gamma=1-\Delta\gamma
where
\Delta\gamma
is a small quantity. The wave functions and energy eigenvalues can be obtained by solving the following equation

\begin{pmatrix}H_{0}&H_{1}&0&0&\cdots\\
H_{1}^{\dagger}&H_{0}&H_{1}&0&\cdots\\
0&H_{1}^{\dagger}&H_{0}&H_{1}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\\
\psi_{3}\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}=E\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\\
\psi_{3}\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}

(S11)

where
\psi_{j}
is the
j^{th}
entry of an eigenstate for this equation corresponding to the
j^{th}
lattice site in the
\hat{x}
-direction. Each
\psi_{j}
has two components corresponding to the spin degrees of freedom at each site in real-space, and the non-zero terms of the Hamiltonian matrix representation on the left-hand side of Eq.
S11
take the following forms: the term on the diagonal is

H_{0}(k_{y},\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}2-\cos{k_{y}}+1-\Delta\gamma-\cos{\lambda}&\sin{k_{x}}\\
\sin{k_{x}}&-(2-\cos{k_{y}}+1-\Delta\gamma-\cos{\lambda})\\
\end{pmatrix},

(S12)

and term off the diagonal is

H_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
-1&-1\\
\end{pmatrix},

(S13)

where
\lambda
is a parameter substituted for the
\hat{z}
-component of the momentum,
k_{z}
, characterizing a defect. Here,
\lambda=n\theta(i,j)
with
\theta
being the relative angle between the defect and site
(i,j)
,
n
is an integer taken to be
1
in this study.

To find zero-energy solutions in analogy to Yan
et al.

Yan et al. 2017
, we first set
k_{x}=0
and
\lambda=0
. The non-zero entries of the Hamiltonian in Eq.
S11
then take the following forms:

H_{0}(0,0)=(1-\Delta\gamma)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\\

(S14)

We then consider eigenstates of
\sigma_{x}
, which are

\displaystyle|\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}

(S15)

\displaystyle|\nu_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}

with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}=+1
and
\lambda_{2}=-1
. Operating the matrices
H_{0},H_{1}
and
H_{1}^{\dagger}
on
|\nu_{1}\rangle
and
|\nu_{2}\rangle
, they produce:

\displaystyle H_{0}\begin{pmatrix}|\nu_{1}\rangle\\
|\nu_{2}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=(1-\Delta\gamma)\begin{pmatrix}-|\nu_{2}\rangle\\
|\nu_{1}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}

(S16)

\displaystyle H_{1}\begin{pmatrix}|\nu_{1}\rangle\\
|\nu_{2}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-|\nu_{2}\rangle\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle H_{1}^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}|\nu_{1}\rangle\\
|\nu_{2}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-|\nu_{1}\rangle\\
\end{pmatrix}

The zero-energy wave functions take the form

|\Psi_{i}\rangle=\sum_{j}a_{i,j}|j\rangle\otimes|\nu_{i}\rangle

(S17)

where
a_{i,j}
is a function of
j
that normalizes
|\Psi_{i}\rangle
and
|j\rangle
indicates localization on the
j^{th}
site:

|j\rangle=(0,0,\cdots,0,1,0,\cdots)^{\top}

(S18)

i.e. having
1
in the
j^{th}
element and
0
everywhere else. We can explore different possibilities of
|\Psi_{i}\rangle
by substituting this expression in Eq.(
S11
) and consider the
j^{th}
element of the equation.

In the case of
i=1
:

H_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1,j-1}|\nu_{1}\rangle+H_{0}a_{1,j}|\nu_{1}\rangle+H_{1}a_{1,j+1}|\nu_{1}\rangle=E=0

(S19)

which simplifies to

\left[0+(1-\Delta\gamma)a_{1,j}-a_{1,j+1}\right]|\nu_{2}\rangle=0

(S20)

and therefore obtaining the following recurrence relation in
a_{1,j}
:

a_{1,j+1}=(1-\Delta\gamma)a_{1,j}

(S21)

which solves to give the general formula:

a_{1,j}=(1-\Delta\gamma)^{j-1}a_{1,1}

(S22)

where

In the case of
i=2

H_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2,j-1}|\nu_{2}\rangle+H_{0}a_{2,j}|\nu_{2}\rangle+H_{1}a_{2,j+1}|\nu_{2}\rangle=E=0

(S23)

which simplifies to

\left[-a_{2,j-1}+(1-\Delta\gamma)a_{2,j}+0\right]|\nu_{1}\rangle=0

(S24)

and therefore obtaining the following recurrence relation in
a_{2,j}
:

a_{2,j+1}=\frac{1}{1-\Delta\gamma}a_{2,j}.

(S25)

which solves to give the general formula:

a_{2,j}=\frac{1}{(1-\Delta\gamma)^{j-1}}a_{2,1}

(S26)

where
a_{2,1}

To satisfy the orthonormality condition of the zero-energy wave functions

\langle\Psi_{i}|\Psi_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij},

(S27)

it suffices to note that for

,
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(a_{i,j})^{2}=1

and
|\nu_{2}\rangle

Next, we consider the Hamiltonian in the neighbourhood of

\displaystyle\Delta H_{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}&k_{x}\\
k_{x}&-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\end{pmatrix}

(S28)

\displaystyle\Delta H_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

(S29)

so that

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(S30)

\displaystyle H_{\textnormal{eff}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\Psi_{1}|\Delta H|\Psi_{1}\rangle&\langle\Psi_{1}|\Delta H|\Psi_{2}\rangle&\\

(S31)

The matrix elements are thus calculated as:

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{1}|\Delta H|\Psi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{1,j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\

\displaystyle=k_{x}

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{1}|\Delta H|\Psi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{1,j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}&k_{x}\\

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}

(S34)

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{2}|\Delta H|\Psi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=(\langle\Psi_{1}|\Delta H|\Psi_{2}\rangle)^{*}

(S35)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}

(S36)

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{2}|\Delta H|\Psi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{2,j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\

\displaystyle=k_{x}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{2,j}^{2}

\displaystyle=k_{x}

\displaystyle H_{\textnormal{eff}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}k_{x}&\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\\
\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}&-k_{x}\end{pmatrix}

(S39)

so that

\displaystyle H_{\textnormal{eff}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}k_{x}^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{4}&0\\

(S40)

Squaring both sides of the time-independent Schrödinger equation and taking
H_{\textnormal{eff}}(k_{x}\rightarrow-i\partial_{x},\lambda\rightarrow y/x)
yields

\displaystyle\left(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{y^{4}}{4x^{4}}\right)\mathbf{I}_{2}|\psi\rangle=E^{2}|\psi\rangle,

(S41)

\chi_{2}=(0,1)

\displaystyle\left(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{y^{4}}{4x^{4}}\right)|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=0

(S42)

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=C_{1}xe^{y^{2}/2x}+\frac{C_{2}xe^{-y^{2}/2x}}{y^{2}}

(S43)

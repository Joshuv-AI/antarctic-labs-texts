Unravelling the Holomorphic Twist: Central Charges
Pieter Bomans, Jingxiang Wu[10mm] Mathematical Institute, University of Oxford Andrew Wiles Building, Radcliffe Observatory QuarterWoodstock Road, Oxford, OX2 6GG, U.K.[5mm] pieter.bomans@maths.ox.ac.uk , jingxiang.wu@maths.ox.ac.uk

Abstract
The holomorphic twist provides a powerful framework to study minimally protected sectors in supersymmetric quantum field theories. We investigate the algebraic structure underlying the holomorphic twist of 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 superconformal field theories in four dimensions. In particular, in holomorphically twisted theories the flavour and conformal symmetry algebras are enhanced to infinite-dimensional higher Kac Moody and higher Virasoro symmetry algebras respectively. We explicitly compute the binary and ternary λ \lambda -brackets and clarify their relation with the underlying infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra. Doing so we show that the central extensions of said symmetry algebras precisely encode the conformal anomalies a a and c c as well as the flavour central charges of the physical four-dimensional theory. This parallels the familiar story in two dimensions where the conformal anomaly c c is encoded in the central extension of the Virasoro algebra.



1  Introduction and summary

The operator product expansion (OPE) lies at the heart of any non-perturbative treatment of quantum field theory. Despite extensive research endeavours, unravelling the detailed structure of the OPE in generic quantum field theories remains an elusive intellectual pursuit. This challenge is highlighted by the scarcity of tools accessible beyond the framework of perturbation theory.

In order to resolve this impasse it proves fruitful to explore theories with additional symmetries. Indeed, the OPE is best understood in conformal field theories (CFTs) due to the extra constraints imposed by conformal invariance. This becomes most evident in two dimensions, where for a large class of theories, known as rational CFTs, the associativity of the OPE combined with the Virasoro symmetry algebra allows one to obtain exact solutions for the correlation functions of local operators
[
1
]
. The key factor in achieving this success lies in the enhancement of the two-dimensional (global) conformal algebra to the infinite-dimensional Virasoro algebra. The presence of such infinite-dimensional algebras is tied to the two-dimensional setup, and at first sight, it is not clear how to lift these tools to higher dimensions.

A second situation in which the OPE is under better control arises in the context of supersymmetric quantum field theories (SQFTs). In four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
theories, one can employ the power of holomorphy
[
2
]
and show that certain observables such as the partition function
[
3
]
or correlation functions in a protected sector of the theory, known as the chiral ring
[
4
]
, only depend holomorphically on the complex structure moduli. In both examples, the holomorphicity in a protected sector greatly simplifies computations and allows for a precise and detailed understanding of the OPE.

These two developments were neatly combined in 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal field theories (SCFTs), where it was shown that the correlation functions in a subsector of the full theory behave precisely like those of a two-dimensional CFT
[
5

\mathbf{Q}\simeq\mathcal{Q}+\mathcal{S}
one obtains a vertex operator algebra (VOA) encoding the OPE of so-called Schur operators when restricted to a complex plane.

The power of holomorphy can be seen in its full glory by passing to the cohomology of a single supercharge

The semi-chiral sector receives contributions from all chiral (semi-)short multiplets and therefore captures the same Hilbert space as the full superconformal index. Warm up: two-dimensional VOAs

The higher VOAs we encounter after performing the holomorphic twist are very similar to the familiar two-dimensional VOAs encountered in the context of two-dimensional CFTs. Before describing their more exotic four-dimensional cousins, let us briefly review some facts about VOAs in two dimensions. The material below is well-known but phased in a slightly unfamiliar way which generalises easily to higher dimensions. For more details on the two-dimensional setting see for example
[
29
]
and references therein.

The algebraic structure underlying a two-dimensional CFT is given by a vertex operator algebra. A VOA consists of the following data:
3

3

•

A vector space
V
; the state space.

•

A linear map
Y
; the state-operator map,

Y\,\,:\,\,V\rightarrow(\text{End}\,V)[\![z]\!]\,\,:\,\,\mathbf{O}\mapsto\mathbf{O}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\frac{\left\{\mathbf{O}\,,\bullet\right\}_{n}}{z^{n+1}}\,.

(1.1)

•

The translation operator
T
; a derivation satisfying the translation axiom
\left(T\mathbf{O}\right)(z)=\partial_{z}\mathbf{O}(z)
.

•

The vacuum vector
\left|{0}\right\rangle\in V
; satisfying
\left|{0}\right\rangle(z)=\text{Id}_{V}
and
\mathbf{O}(z)\left|{0}\right\rangle=\mathbf{O}+(T\mathbf{O})z+\mathcal{O}(z^{2})
.

A VOA is naturally equipped with an infinite number of products represented by the brackets
\left\{\bullet\,,\bullet\right\}_{n}
in terms of which the operator product expansion can be expressed as

\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(w)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,,\mathbf{O}_{2}\right\}_{n}(w)}{(z-w)^{n+1}}\,.

(1.2)

Conversely, the brackets
\left\{\bullet\,,\,\bullet\right\}_{n}
provide us with a convenient way to unpack the OPE data of the VOA, which can be extracted as

\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,,\mathbf{O}_{2}\right\}_{n}(0)=\oint_{S^{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}}z^{n}\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(1.3)

This equation has a very transparent physical meaning. Any holomorphic operator
\mathbf{O}(z)
can be thought of as a current for a global symmetry with conservation law
\bar{\partial}\mathbf{O}(z)=0
. This conservation law continues to hold when we insert any
\bar{\partial}
-closed function
\rho
to construct another current
\rho\,\mathbf{O}(z)
. Such functions are elements of the Dolbeault cohomology,

\rho\,\in\,H^{0,0}_{\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}\right)=\mathbb{C}[z,z^{-1}]\,,

(1.4)

and the collection of all holomorphic currents naturally forms an infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra.

Phrased differently, (
1.3
) is nothing but the action of the topological line operator
\left\{\mathbf{O},\bullet\right\}_{n}
constructed by integrating the current
z^{n}\mathbf{O}(z)
over the codim-1 manifold
S^{1}
. For any holomorphic operator, the topological line operators therefore arise in infinite towers labelled by
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The non-negative modes (
n\geq 0
) capture the singular part of the OPE. These modes can be conveniently collected in a generating function called the
\lambda
-bracket,

\left\{{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,}_{\lambda}\,{\mathbf{O}_{2}}\right\}\equiv\oint_{S^{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda z}\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,,\,\mathbf{O}_{2}\right\}_{n}\,,

(1.5)

This
\lambda
-bracket naturally generalises to higher dimensional situations and will be a central object in this work. A vector space
V
, equipped with a
\lambda
-bracket and a translation operator
T
defines a Lie conformal algebra. Any vertex operator algebra automatically defines a Lie conformal algebra simply by forgetting about the regular part of the OPE. To get more familiar with this structure we refer the interested reader to Appendix
D
where we give some more explicit details on the Kac Moody algebra and Virasoro algebra as Lie conformal algebras.

Higher VOA from higher dimensional holomorphic field theories

After this two-dimensional intermezzo, we are ready to discuss the case of four-dimensional holomorphically twisted theories. After the twist operators only depend holomorphically on the coordinates of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and we can proceed entirely analogous to the two-dimensional case.

For any holomorphic operator
\mathbf{O}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{d})
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}

\rho\,\mathbf{O}
continues to be holomorphic as long as
\rho

\bar{\partial}
-closed. Analogous to the above we naturally run into an infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra. Such
\bar{\partial}
-closed
\rho

\rho\,\in\,H^{0,\bullet}_{\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\backslash\{0\}\right)\,.

(1.6)

In higher dimensions, the Dolbeault cohomology
H^{0,\bullet}_{\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\backslash\{0\}\right)
is known to concentrate on degree
(0,0)
and
(0,d-1)

H^{0,i}_{\bar{\partial}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\backslash\{0\}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{C}\left[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}\right]&i=0\,,\\
\mathbb{C}\left[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{d}\right]\,\omega_{\rm BM}&i=d-1\,,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,,\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

where
\omega_{\rm BM}
is the Bochner-Martinelli kernel introduced in more detail in Appendix
B
. The degree
(0,0)

1.3

\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,,\mathbf{O}_{2}\right\}_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{d}}(0)=\oint_{S^{2d-1}}\frac{{\rm d}^{d}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{n}}\,z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}\,\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\,,\qquad n_{i}\geq 0\,.

(1.8)

Conversely, the negative modes can be generalised by picking
\rho

(0,d-1)
cohomology,

\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,,\mathbf{O}_{2}\right\}_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{d}}(0)=\oint_{S^{2d-1}}\frac{{\rm d}^{d}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{d}}\,\partial_{z_{1}}^{-n_{1}-1}\partial_{z_{2}}^{-n_{2}-1}\cdots\partial_{z_{d}}^{-n_{d}-1}\omega_{\rm BM}\,\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\,,\qquad n_{i}\leq-1\,.

(1.9)

Note that in two dimensions this definition precisely reproduces the negative modes in equation (
1.3
). Following the analogy with two dimensions, one can construct infinitely many codimension-one topological surface operators by integrating
\rho\,\mathbf{O}_{1}
over the
(2d-1)

S^{2d-1}

\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)
by wrapping the topological surface operator on the
S^{2d-1}
linking the origin,

\oint_{S^{2d-1}}\frac{{\rm d}^{d}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{d}}\ \rho\ \mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\,.

(1.10)

Collecting the non-negative modes (
1.8
) we define the
\lambda
-bracket as the following generating function,

\left\{\mathbf{O}_{1}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{O}_{2}\right\}=\oint_{S^{2d-1}}\frac{{\rm d}^{d}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\cdot z}\mathbf{O}_{1}(z)\mathbf{O}_{2}(0)\,.

(1.11)

Unlike in one complex dimension where the brackets generate a Lie conformal algebra and therefore satisfy the Jacobi identity, in higher dimensions, the brackets only satisfy the Jacobi identity homotopically. Higher homotopies are captured by a collection of higher (
\lambda

algebra using the integration cycles in the configuration space of points
[
30
]

[
12

13
]
, which we carefully introduce in Section
2.1

n

\lambda

\mathbb{C}^{d}

Summary of the results

This higher VOA structure naturally arises in the context of holomorphically twisted four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
theories
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. In this case, the holomorphic operators of interest are given by semi-chiral superfields
\mathbf{O}
, which are in one-to-one correspondence with
\mathbf{Q}
-cohomology classes. In particular, whenever the theory preserves some flavour symmetry, the twisted theory contains an associated semi-chiral superfield
\mathbf{J}
. Similarly, any (local) unitary
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFT contains a stress tensor which gives rise to a semi-chiral superfield
\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}
in the twisted theory.

In two-dimensional VOAs, it is well-known that the modes of a conserved current
\mathbf{J}_{n}=\left\{\mathbf{J}\,,\bullet\right\}_{n}
and the modes of the holomorphic stress tensor
\mathbf{L}_{n}=\left\{\mathbf{S}\,,\bullet\right\}_{n+1}
generate respectively a Kac-Moody algebra and Virasoro algebra. The central charges of these algebras encode important physical information about the theory. The Virasoro central charge
c
encodes the conformal anomaly of the 2d CFT, while the central charge of the Kac-Moody algebra equals the flavour central charge
k_{G}
. In the two-dimensional context, these central charges arise in the most singular terms of respectively the
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}
or
\mathbf{J}\mathbf{J}
OPE, where
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{J}
denote the holomorphic stress tensor and the holomorphic current. Similarly, these central charges can be read off from the
\lambda
-brackets

\left\{\mathbf{S}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{S}\right\}=\lambda^{3}\frac{c}{12}+\left(2\lambda+\partial\right)\mathbf{S}\,,\qquad\qquad\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\right\}=f^{ab}{}_{c}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}+\delta^{ab}k_{G}\lambda\,.

(1.12)

In four-dimensional holomorphically twisted theories the modes of conserved current and stress tensor semi-chiral superfields are given by
\mathbf{J}^{a}_{m,n}=\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,,\bullet\right\}_{m,n}
and
\mathbf{L}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{m,n}=\left\{\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\,,\bullet\right\}_{m+1,n+1}
. Analogous to the two-dimensional setup their modes now generate a higher Kac-Moody and higher Virasoro symmetry algebra. It is then natural to expect that the central charges of these higher VOAs encode physical information about the original untwisted theory. We make this expectation precise by explicitly computing binary and ternary
\lambda
-brackets of conserved current and stress tensor semi-chiral superfields. The binary
\lambda
-brackets are given by

\left\{\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,{}_{\lambda}\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\right\}=\lambda_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}+\lambda_{\dot{\beta}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}+\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\,,\qquad\qquad\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\right\}=f^{ab}{}_{c}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\,,

(1.13)

where
f^{ab}{}_{c}
are the anti-symmetric structure constants of the flavour symmetry algebra. Unlike in two dimensions, these expressions do not contain any information about the central charges. However, the main result of this paper lies in the computation of the ternary
\lambda
-brackets. The
\lambda
-bracket for three stress tensor semi-chiral superfields is

\left\{\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}\right\}=\frac{4}{3}\,(3c-2a)\,\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}+16\,(c-a)\,\Lambda_{\rm vector}\,,

(1.14)

where
\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}
and
\Lambda_{\rm vector}
are theory-independent kinematic factors which are defined in the main text in (
3.15
). The coefficients in this expression are precisely given by the
a
and
c
conformal anomalies of the untwisted theory! We compute this
\lambda
-bracket in all generality as an integrated three-point function of (descendants of) stress tensors of the original untwisted theory and check our results in a wide variety of examples. Similarly, we compute the
\lambda
-bracket of the conserved current semi-chiral superfields resulting in the following expression,

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\right\}=k_{G}\,d^{abc}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\,.

(1.15)

In this equation
d^{abc}
are the symmetric structure constants of the flavour symmetry group while
k_{G}
encodes the flavour central charge of the untwisted theory. These results therefore present a clear connection between the central charges in the holomorphic theory obtained after performing the holomorphic twist and observables in the original untwisted theory.

Outlook

The study of the holomorphic twist is still in its early infancy and many interesting questions remain unanswered. Although mathematical results are steadily emerging (see
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
for a necessarily incomplete selection) it remains a challenge to connect observables in the twisted theory to physical observables in the untwisted theory such as correlation functions of local operators. Our work presents a first step towards better understanding the physics behind the holomorphic twist and opens a window into making progress on a variety of fronts.

The first direction to make progress lies in better understanding the
L_{\infty}
structure of the modes and its representation theory. In this work we compute
\lambda
-brackets but the map to the ternary
L_{\infty}
3-bracket is not immediate
[
38
]
.
We plan to come back to this question in the near future. A more challenging question is to understand the
L_{\infty}
modules and null vectors. A similar understanding in two dimensions gives rise to the discovery of minimal models
[
1
]
and allows one to exactly solve the OPE. The discovery of a similar structure in the holomorphic twist would present a major breakthrough in our understanding of supersymmetric quantum field theories.

Although in this work we focus on conformal theories, many aspects of our story continue to hold for non-conformal
\mathcal{N}=1
SQFTs. In this case, the semi-chiral superfield corresponding to the stress tensor is no longer given by
\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}
but rather by
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}=\partial^{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}
. The modes of this semi-chiral superfield generate an infinite dimensional symmetry algebra distinct from the higher Virasoro algebra. It would be interesting to better understand the structure of this algebra and find out in which shape a similar relation between conformal anomalies and central charges persists in this setup. Understanding this mechanism would provide us with an elegant framework to prove for example the
a
-theorem
[
39
]
in a similar fashion as the original
c
-theorem in two-dimensions
[
40
]
and provide us with a deeper understanding of RG flows in
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFTs.

From the structure of the
\lambda
-brackets in (
1.13
)-(
1.15
) we observe a striking similarity with the supersymmetric Casimir energy obtained by integrating the anomaly polynomial
[
41
]
. It would be interesting to better understand the origin of this similarity and relate the supersymmetric Casimir energy to the symmetry algebras discussed in this work. In addition to the flavour central charges discussed in this work the supersymmetric Casimir energy also contains the
k_{FFR}
and
k_{FRR}
anomalies, which can be obtained by computing the three-point functions of resp. two/one flavour current and one/two stress tensors analogous to the computations performed in this work. It would be interesting to explicitly compute these from the holomorphically twisted theories study the combined structure formed by the higher Virasoro and Kac-Moody symmetry algebras. Another observation is that the term in front of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}
is precisely the combination that is manifestly positive in unitary theories through the Hofman-Maldacena bound
[
42
]
. This coincidence hints that it might be possible to re-prove this bound directly in the holomorphically twisted theory and could shed more light on the structure of unitary representations of said symmetry algebras.

Finally, holomorphic theories arise in a variety of other examples. For example, minimal twists of six-dimensional theories with at least
\mathcal{N}=(1,0)
supersymmetry give rise to six-dimensional higher VOAs. Increasing the supersymmetry to six-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,0)

\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
SQFTs there is holomorphic-topological twist
[
31

giving rise to an associated four-dimensional higher VOA. It would be very interesting to better understand this unexplored protected sector and the physical observables it encodes.

Doing so will allow us to extract a plethora of exact detailed OPE data.

2.1  Infinite-dimensional symmetry and the
\lambda
-bracket

The main upshot of the previous discussion is that the holomorphic twist, i.e. the semi-chiral sector (i.e. the

2.2  Higher Kac-Moody algebra

In a two-dimensional VOA with continuous global symmetry
\mathfrak{g}
, the symmetry is enhanced to an infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra known as the Kac-Moody algebra (or the Heisenberg algebra, when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{u}(1)
). For any such symmetry, the theory contains conserved holomorphic currents
\mathbf{J}^{a}(z)
, where
a
is an adjoint
\mathfrak{g}
index. The Kac-Moody algebra is then generated by the following action on local operators
12
12

12

For more details on the two-dimensional Kac-Moody algebra see Appendix

\mathbf{J}^{a}_{n}\cdot O(0)=\oint\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}}z^{n}\mathbf{J}^{a}(z)\mathbf{O}(0),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(2.17)

Similarly, in four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1

\mathfrak{g}
, we find an infinite-dimensional symmetry enhancement in the twisted theory. Every such continuous global symmetry necessarily comes with an associated short current multiplet
A_{2}\overline{A}_{2}[0,0]_{2}^{(0)}

\mathbf{J}=D_{+}\mathcal{J}
, where
\mathcal{J}
denotes the current superfield. Analogous to the two-dimensional setting, the modes of
\mathbf{J}
give rise to a higher Kac-Moody symmetry algebra in the holomorphically twisted theory. In this case, the modes can be defined as

\mathbf{J}^{a}_{m,n}\cdot\mathbf{O}(0)\equiv\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,\,,\mathbf{O}\right\}_{m,n}=\mathbf{Q}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}{\rm d}^{2}z\,\rho_{m,n}\,\mathbf{J}^{a}(z)\mathbf{O}(0)\,,\qquad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(2.18)

We can obtain the commutation relations of the higher Kac-Moody algebra through the associativity relations of the
\lambda

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\left\{\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{O}\right\}\right\}-\left\{\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{O}\right\}\right\}-\left\{\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\right\}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}\mathbf{O}\right\}=0\,.

(2.19)

The binary
\lambda
-bracket,
\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\right\}

\mathrm{SU}(2)

\mathfrak{g}

\lambda

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\right\}=f^{ab}{}_{c}\mathbf{J}^{c}\,,

(2.20)

where

are the structure constants of the flavour symmetry group
\mathfrak{g}
and the normalisation coefficient on the right-hand side has been absorbed in the normalisation of the current operator. Expanding both sides of the associativity relation (

\lambda_{1,\dot{\pm}}

\lambda_{2,\dot{\pm}}

\left[\mathbf{J}_{m,n}^{a}\,,\mathbf{J}_{kl}^{b}\right]=f^{ab}{}_{c}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}_{m+k,n+l}\,.

(2.21)

The ternary
\lambda
-bracket on can similarly be constrained through its symmetries. First of all, the only

\lambda_{1,\dot{\pm}}

\lambda_{2,\dot{\pm}}

\lambda

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\right\}=-\left\{\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\right\}=-\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\,{}_{-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\partial}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\right\}\,

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\right\}=k\,d^{abc}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\,,

(2.23)

where

In contrast to the two-dimensional Kac-Moody symmetry algebra, the higher Kac-Moody algebra has a non-trivial ternary

2.4  Non-conformal theories

In the above, we considered the situation when the physical four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
theory is conformal. Conformal symmetry however is not necessary in order to define the holomorphic twist. Therefore a natural question is to what extent the above discussion generalises in the non-conformal setting. On the level of conserved current multiplets, nothing changes, and we expect to observe a similar infinite-dimensional Kac-Moody algebra in non-conformal theories.

On the other hand, as could be expected, the Virasoro symmetry is specific to the conformal case. In non-conformal theories, the superconformal supercurrent multiplet is not well-defined and instead, we need to use a more general multiplet, the
\mathcal{S}
multiplet
[
50
,
51
]
. This multiplet exists for any supersymmetric theory and satisfies the constraint

\overline{D}^{\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\chi_{\alpha}+\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}\,,

(2.36)

where
\chi_{\alpha}
is a chiral multiplet and
D_{(\alpha}\mathcal{Y}_{\beta)}=\overline{D}^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}=0
. Whenever
\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}
vanishes, the constraint implies that the bottom component of the
\mathcal{S}
multiplet is a conserved current and for the remainder of this work we restrict ourselves to this situation.
14
14

14

In this case the
\mathcal{S}
multiplet is not irreducible but decomposes in a smaller multiplet known as the
\mathcal{R}
multiplet
[
52
,
53
,
54
,
55

The
\mathcal{S}

\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}=\partial^{\dot{\alpha}}D_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+{\dot{\alpha}}}
. This semi-chiral operator still generates an infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra in the twisted theory, but in contrast with the conformal case, the positive modes only generate the sub-algebra of complex symplectomorphisms. We leave a detailed study of this infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra for future work.

Whenever the theory has additional

flavour symmetries along the RG flow they might mix with the UV R-symmetry to form the IR R-symmetry. From the point of view of the
\mathcal{N}=1
(non-conformal) supersymmetry algebra, all such R-symmetries are allowed but only one of those is consistent with superconformal symmetry, i.e. there is a unique IR (superconformal) R-symmetry which can be selected using
a
-maximisation
[
56
]
. This ambiguity is reflected in the fact that we can always improve the
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet by adding improvement terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}+\sum_{i}\zeta_{i}\left[{D_{\alpha}},{\bar{D}_{\dot{\alpha}}}\right]\mathcal{J}_{i}\,,

(2.37)

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha}+\sum_{i}\zeta_{i}\,\frac{3}{2}\,\bar{D}^{2}D_{\alpha}\mathcal{J}_{i}\,,

where
\mathcal{J}_{i}
are real linear multiplets containing the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{i}
flavour currents. In some of the examples in Section
4
we find ourselves exactly in this situation and will explicitly see how this phenomenon manifests itself in the framework of the holomorphic twist.

3  Conformal anomalies from integrated correlators

As introduced above, the symmetry algebra of any four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}\geq 1
superconformal theory gets enhanced to an infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra after passing to the subsector obtained by performing the holomorphic twist. Continuing the analogy with two-dimensional VOAs we expect the central charges of the four-dimensional untwisted theory to be encoded in the central extensions of the associated higher-VOA. In this section, we make this statement precise by explicitly computing the binary and ternary
\lambda
-brackets involving conserved (super)current semi-chiral superfields. Doing so we recover the expressions obtained in the previous section allowing us to fix the constant coefficients in terms of the conformal anomalies
a
and
c
and flavour central charges.

To compute the two- and three-point functions in this section we made use of the Mathematica packages
CFTs4d

[
57
]
and
SpinorAlgebra

[
58
]
. Although conceptually straightforward, extracting the relevant descendant three-point function from superspace expressions, see
[
59
]
and
[
60
,
61
,
58
]
, is a most tedious affair. In order not to obscure the main message, we defer the details of the computations to Appendix
B
.

3.1  Flavour current multiplets

Whenever an
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal theory contains a continuous global symmetry
\mathfrak{g}
, this symmetry is enhanced in the holomorphically twisted theory to an infinite-dimensional four-dimensional higher Kac-Moody symmetry
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{2,k}
. To see how the central charge
k
of this algebra can be related to the flavour central charge
k_{G}
of the original theory we proceed to explicitly compute the binary and ternary
\lambda
-brackets containing resp. two and three conserved current semi-chiral operators.

The conserved current
j^{a}_{\mu}
is embedded in a real linear multiplet
\mathcal{J}^{a}
satisfying

D^{2}\mathcal{J}^{a}=\overline{D}^{2}\mathcal{J}^{a}=0\,,

(3.1)

where
a
is an adjoint index of the flavour symmetry
\mathfrak{g}
. It is straightforward to see that the associated semi-chiral superfield is given by
\mathbf{J}^{a}=D_{+}\mathcal{J}^{a}
. More explicitly we have

\mathbf{J}^{a}={\rm i}j^{a}_{+}+\left(j^{a}_{+{\dot{\alpha}}}+\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}J^{a}\right){\rm d}\bar{z}^{{\dot{\alpha}}}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{j}^{a\dot{\alpha}}{\rm d}^{2}\bar{z}\,,

(3.2)

where
j^{\alpha}
and
\bar{j}^{\dot{\alpha}}
are chiral and anti-chiral spinors respectively and
J
is a scalar and the constraints (
3.1
) guarantee that the current is conserved,
\partial^{\mu}j_{\mu}=0
. With the explicit semi-chiral superfield at hand, we can compute the binary and ternary
\lambda
-bracket following the prescription laid out in equation (
2.13
) and (
2.16
) above. The binary
\lambda
-bracket is computed as follows,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\right\}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\cdot z}\,\bar{\partial}\left[\mathbf{J}^{(1)a}(z)\mathbf{J}^{(0)b}\right]

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\cdot z}\,\bar{\partial}\left[\frac{\left(\bar{z},{\rm d}\bar{z}\right)}{|z|^{4}}\,f^{ab}{}_{c}\mathbf{J}^{(0)c}+\cdots\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{ab}{}_{c}\,\mathbf{J}^{(0)c}(0)\,.

To advance from the first to the second line we used the OPE between
\mathbf{J}^{(1)}
and
\mathbf{J}^{(0)}
. Unlike for a general multiplet, where the OPE of the descendants is not guaranteed to be fixed in terms of the OPE of the superconformal primary, the semi-chiral shortening conditions precisely allow us to do so. For this reason we can obtain the
\mathbf{J}^{(1)}\mathbf{J}^{(0)}
from the
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{J}
OPE, see for example
[
59
,
60
]
. To compute the integral over
\mathbb{C}^{2}
we recognise the Bochner-Martinelli kernel allowing us to use a higher-dimensional residue formula.
15
15

15

See Appendix
B
for more details on the Bochner-Martinelli kernel and higher dimensional residues.
The
\mathbf{J}^{(1)}\mathbf{J}^{(0)}
OPE contains many more terms indicated by the
\cdots
. However, these are all less singular and therefore will not contribute to the integral. Note that the binary
\lambda
-bracket is proportional to the anti-symmetric structure constant
f^{ab}{}_{c}
of the flavour symmetry algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. From this, we immediately see that for abelian symmetries the binary
\lambda
-bracket is trivial, in line with the previous discussion.

Next, we compute the ternary
\lambda
-bracket of three flavour current semi-chiral operators. Following (
2.16
) the ternary
\lambda
-bracket is defined as

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\right\}=\int_{(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{1}{\rm d}^{2}z_{2}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{4}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{1}\cdot z_{1}+\lambda_{2}\cdot z_{2}}\bar{\partial}\left\langle{\mathbf{J}^{a(2)}(z_{1})\mathbf{J}^{b(1)}(z_{2})\mathbf{J}^{c(0)}(0)}\right\rangle\,.

(3.4)

Purely on kinematic grounds, the three-point function in this expression takes the following form
[
57
]
,

\left\langle{\mathbf{J}^{a(2)}(z_{1})\mathbf{J}^{b(1)}(z_{2})\mathbf{J}^{c(0)}(0)}\right\rangle=\frac{c_{1}\,T^{1}_{23}U^{13}T^{1}_{13}+c_{2}\,T^{1}_{23}U^{12}U^{23}+c_{3}\,U^{12}U^{13}U^{21}}{|z_{12}|^{6}|z_{1}|^{4}|z_{2}|^{2}}\,,

(3.5)

where the tensor structures
T^{i}_{jk}
and
U^{ij}
are defined as

U^{ij}=s_{j\alpha}x_{ij}^{\alpha{\dot{\alpha}}}\bar{s}_{i{\dot{\alpha}}}\,,\qquad T^{i}_{jk}=\frac{|x_{ij}|^{2}|x_{ik}|^{2}}{|x_{jk}|^{2}}\left(\frac{s_{j\alpha}x_{ij}^{\alpha{\dot{\alpha}}}\bar{s}_{i{\dot{\alpha}}}}{|x_{ij}|^{2}}-\frac{s_{k\alpha}x_{ik}^{\alpha{\dot{\alpha}}}\bar{s}_{i{\dot{\alpha}}}}{|x_{ik}|^{2}}\right)\,,

(3.6)

where we defined
x_{ij}=x_{i}-x_{j}
and contracted all indices with auxiliary spinors
s_{i}
and
\bar{s}_{j}
. Written as such this expression is not entirely correct and one needs to contract the indices in line with the index structure of the components of the semi-chiral superfield. The constants
c_{1,2,3}
can be fixed by imposing conservation of the current and relating it to the supersymmetric three-point function of conserved current multiplets. Doing so uniquely fixed the three coefficients to be
c_{1,2,3}=k_{G}d^{abc}
. Acting on the resulting three-point function with
\bar{\partial}
results in

\bar{\partial}\left\langle{\mathbf{J}^{(2)a}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\mathbf{J}^{(1)b}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\mathbf{J}^{(0)c}(0)}\right\rangle=-k_{G}\,d^{abc}\,\frac{(\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})}{|z_{12}|^{4}|z_{1}|^{4}|z_{2}|^{4}}\left(1+2\frac{(\bar{z}_{12},z_{1})}{|z_{12}|^{2}}\right)\,,

(3.7)

where
d^{abc}=\mathrm{Tr}\,(T^{a}\left\{{T^{b}},{T^{c}}\right\})
are the symmetric structure constants of the flavour symmetry group, and we introduced the notation
z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}
and analogously
\bar{z}_{ij}=\bar{z}_{i}-\bar{z}_{j}
. Now all that is left is to integrate this function over
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{2}
. This integral is rather non-trivial to perform but can be greatly simplified by employing heat kernel regularisation. In particular, we find that the integral over the heat kernel time is convergent making the result manifestly scheme independent. The details of this procedure are explained and applied to the integral in question in Appendix
B.2
and
B.3
. Following that appendix, we obtain the following result for the ternary
\lambda
-bracket,

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\right\}=k_{G}\,d^{abc}\,(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\,.

(3.8)

This matches with the expression in the previous section starting from the mode algebra and rigorously demonstrates that indeed the central charge of the higher Kac-Moody symmetry algebra encodes the flavour central charge of the physical
\mathcal{N}=1
theory. In the next section, we will confirm this more explicitly in various examples.

3.2  Supercurrent multiplet

Next, we proceed analogously to compute the binary and ternary
\lambda
-bracket for supercurrent semi-chiral superfields. Although the computation is identical in spirit, the details are quite a bit more involved and most of the explicit details are referred to Appendix
B.3
. Whenever a theory has
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal symmetry, we observed that after the holomorphic twist, this symmetry is enhanced to an infinite-dimensional higher Virasoro symmetry algebra. The cohomology classifying the central extensions is two-dimensional
[
49
]
which fits with the expectation that the central charges should be related to the
a
and
c
conformal anomaly of the physical theory.

The higher Virasoro symmetry algebra is generated by the modes of the semi-chiral operator contained in the supercurrent multiplet
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha{\dot{\alpha}}}
. There are various supercurrent multiplets, appropriate in different situations. The most general supercurrent multiplet is the
\mathcal{S}
multiplet, which can be defined for any
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric theory
[
50
,
51
]
. In the context of superconformal
\mathcal{N}=1
theories there necessarily exists a non-anomalous
\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}
symmetry and the Kähler form on the target space is always globally well-defined so we can use a simpler conformal supercurrent multiplet.
16
16

16

Whenever the theory has a non-anomalous
\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}
symmetry or the Kähler form on the target space is globally well-defined there are exist also consistent intermediate multiplets called respectively the
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet
[
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
or the Ferrara-Zumino multiplet
[
62
]
.
This is a real multiplet satisfying

D^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha{\dot{\alpha}}}=\overline{D}^{{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha{\dot{\alpha}}}=0\,.

(3.9)

In components, this multiplet is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu}=

\displaystyle j_{\mu}+{\rm i}\,\theta^{\alpha}S_{\mu\alpha}-{\rm i}\,\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{S}_{\mu\dot{\alpha}}+(\theta\sigma^{\nu}\bar{\theta})\left(2T_{\mu\nu}+\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial^{\rho}j^{\sigma}\right)

(3.10)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}\bar{\theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}(\bar{\sigma}^{\nu})_{\dot{\alpha}\alpha}\partial_{\nu}S_{\mu}^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\theta}^{2}\theta^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\nu})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\nu}\bar{S}_{\mu}^{\dot{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{4}\theta^{2}\bar{\theta}^{2}\partial^{2}j_{\mu}\,,

where the constraints (
3.9
) impose that the
R
-symmetry current, stress tensor and the supersymmetry currents are conserved,
\partial^{\mu}j_{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}S_{\mu\alpha}=\partial^{\mu}\bar{S}_{\mu\dot{\alpha}}=0
, as well as the trace relations
T^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=(\sigma^{\mu})_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\bar{S}_{\mu}^{\dot{\beta}}=(\bar{\sigma}^{\mu})_{\dot{\beta}\beta}S_{\mu}^{\beta}=0
. As already noted in Section
2
above, the supercurrent multiplet is a
\overline{A}_{2}A_{2}[1,1]_{3}^{(0)}
superconformal multiplet which contains a semi-chiral operator
\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}=D_{+}\mathcal{S}_{+{\dot{\alpha}}}
given as

\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}=\,{\rm i}\,S_{+\dot{\alpha}+}+{\rm d}\bar{z}^{\dot{\beta}}\,\left(T_{{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\beta}}}+\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\left(\star{\rm d}j\right)_{{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\beta}}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{2}\,\partial_{{\dot{\beta}}}\bar{S}_{+\dot{\alpha}}^{\dot{\beta}}\,.

(3.11)

where
T_{{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\beta}}}
and
\left(\star{\rm d}j\right)_{{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\beta}}}
are the holomorphic part of the stress tensor and dual R-symmetry field strength. Having introduced the semi-chiral superfield
\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}
we proceed to compute the binary and ternary
\lambda
-brackets. To compute the binary bracket we proceed identically as above. We first use the OPE
[
59
]
after which we compute the binary bracket by using the higher-dimensional residue theorem

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\beta}}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\cdot z}\,\bar{\partial}\left[\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{(1)}(z)\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}^{(0)}(0)\right]

(3.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\cdot z}\,\bar{\partial}\left[\frac{\left(\bar{z},{\rm d}\bar{z}\right)}{|z|^{6}}z_{({\dot{\alpha}}}\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\beta}})}^{(0)}(0)+\frac{(\bar{z},{\rm d}\bar{z})}{|z|^{4}}\partial_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\beta}}}^{(0)}(0)+\cdots\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\beta}}}^{(0)}(0)+\lambda_{{\dot{\beta}}}\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}^{(0)}(0)+\partial_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\beta}}}^{(0)}(0)\,,

in line with the discussion in the previous section. The dots above denote additional terms in the OPE that do not contribute when computing the residue in the third line. Next, we proceed to the ternary bracket,

\left\{\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\beta}}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}\right\}=\int_{(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{1}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{2}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{1}\cdot z_{1}+\lambda_{2}\cdot z_{2}}\bar{\partial}\left\langle{\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}^{(2)}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}^{(1)}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}^{(0)}(0)}\right\rangle\,.

(3.13)

As shown in
[
59
]
the three-point function is completely determined as a sum over tensor structures, where the only theory-dependent data is contained in the prefactors which are linear functions of the conformal anomalies
a
and
c
. In particular, we can use these results to compute the three-point function with the specific components as indicated above. In comparison with the expression above for conserved currents, in this case, there are eleven possible tensor structures appearing as explicitly listed in Appendix
B
. In Appendix
B
we explicitly list the tensor structures and determine the correct coefficients. Since the explicit resulting expression for the integrand is rather lengthy we refrain from listing it here. The evaluation of the remaining integral in (
3.13
) is very tedious and the details are relegated to Appendix
B.3
. Note however that just like for the conserved currents, the integration over the heat kernel time is completely convergent. Note however that the condition for convergence crucially depends on some non-trivial cancellations between different tensor structures. Carefully performing all the steps outlined above we arrive at the final result for the stress tensor ternary
\lambda
-bracket,

\left\{\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}\right\}=\frac{4}{3}\,\left(3c-2a\right)\,\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}+16\,\left(a-c\right)\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{vector}}\,.

(3.14)

This expression contains the (theory independent) kinematic tensors
\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}
and
\Lambda_{\rm vector}
given by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{vector}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})^{2}\Big[\lambda_{1\dot{\alpha}}\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}+\lambda_{2\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{\dot{\rho}\dot{\alpha}}-(\lambda_{1\dot{\gamma}}+\lambda_{2\dot{\gamma}})\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\Big]\,,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3\pi^{2}}\lambda_{1\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{2\dot{\beta}}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})_{\dot{\gamma}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\,.

(3.16)

All theory-dependent data is contained in the two prefactors as linear combinations of the conformal anomalies
a
and
c
. This computation therefore presents us with a precise understanding of how the conformal anomalies
a
and
c
are encoded in central extensions of the infinite-dimensional higher Virasoro symmetry algebra.

4.1  Free vector multiplet

As a first example we consider the free vector multiplet. Its holomorphic twist is given by a
\mathbf{b}\mathbf{c}
system with BV action

S_{\rm BV}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}{\rm d}^{2}z\ \mathbf{b}\bar{\partial}\mathbf{c}\,.

(4.11)

The stress tensor satisfying the closure condition, (
4.7
), is given by

\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}=-\mathbf{b}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbf{c}\,,

(4.12)

and we used the binary
\lambda
-bracket to fix the normalisation. With this stress tensor at hand, we are ready to compute the ternary
\lambda
-bracket. Following the definition (
2.15
) above we compute the
\lambda
-bracket by performing all the Wick rotations and integrating the remaining product of propagators, where as mentioned above each propagator is given by a Bochner-Martinelli kernel. The computation is somewhat involved but entirely algorithmic and was explained in all generality in
[
12
]
. For the details of this particular computation, we refer the reader to section 4.3 of that paper. In Appendix
B
we provide an explicit map from the expression in that paper to the ternary
\lambda
-bracket. The resulting expression is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})^{2}\Big[\lambda_{1\dot{\alpha}}\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}+\lambda_{2\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{\dot{\rho}\dot{\alpha}}-(\lambda_{1\dot{\gamma}}+\lambda_{2\dot{\gamma}})\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\Big]

(4.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{vector}}\,,

(4.14)

explaining the origin of the naming in (
3.14
). Comparing with this equation we find the correct values for the central charges of a single free vector,

a=\frac{3}{16}\,,\qquad\qquad c=\frac{1}{8}\,.

(4.15)

4.2
N_{f}
free chirals

As a second example, consider a theory of
N_{f}
free chiral multiplets. The BV action of the holomorphically twisted theory is given by

S_{\rm BV}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}{\rm d}^{2}z\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\bar{\partial}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}\,.

(4.16)

The theory has a global
\mathrm{U}(N_{f})
symmetry rotating the chirals, with an associated conserved current

\mathbf{J}_{i}^{j}={\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{j},\quad i,j=1,\dots,N_{f}\,,

(4.17)

where the trace
\mathrm{Tr}\,\,\mathbf{J}
generates the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}
symmetry and the remaining generators generate an
\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})
flavour symmetry. In this section, it is more convenient to work with fundamental indices but one can straightforwardly convert the results to adjoint indices using the relevant intertwining matrices. To compute the binary
\lambda
-brackets of two currents we first compute the bracket of a current with (derivatives of) free fields,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{J}_{i}^{j}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\partial_{\dot{+}}^{n_{1}}\partial_{\dot{-}}^{n_{2}}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{k}\right\}

\displaystyle=+\delta_{i}^{k}\sum_{m_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}=0}^{n_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{1}\\
m_{1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{2}\\
m_{2}\end{array}\right)\lambda_{\dot{+}}^{m_{1}}\lambda_{\dot{-}}^{m_{2}}\partial_{\dot{+}}^{n_{1}-m_{1}}\partial_{\dot{-}}^{n_{2}-m_{2}}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{j}

(4.18)

\displaystyle=+\delta_{i}^{k}(\partial_{\dot{+}}+\lambda_{\dot{+}})^{n_{1}}(\partial_{\dot{-}}+\lambda_{\dot{-}})^{n_{2}}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{j}

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{J}_{i}^{j}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\partial_{\dot{+}}^{n_{1}}\partial_{\dot{-}}^{n_{2}}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{k}\}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{k}^{j}\sum_{m_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}=0}^{n_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{1}\\
m_{1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{2}\\
m_{2}\end{array}\right)\lambda_{\dot{+}}^{m_{1}}\lambda_{\dot{-}}^{m_{2}}\partial_{\dot{+}}^{n_{1}-m_{1}}\partial_{\dot{-}}^{n_{2}-m_{2}}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta_{k}^{j}(\partial_{\dot{+}}+\lambda_{\dot{+}})^{n_{1}}(\partial_{\dot{-}}+\lambda_{\dot{-}})^{n_{2}}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}

Combining these equations we can straightforwardly compute the binary
\lambda
-bracket,

\left\{\mathbf{J}_{i}^{j}\,{}_{\lambda}\,\mathbf{J}_{k}^{l}\right\}=\delta_{i}^{l}\mathbf{J}_{k}^{j}-\delta_{k}^{j}\mathbf{J}_{i}^{l}\,,

(4.19)

to confirm that this current indeed has the correct normalisation to reproduce the universal form (
4.6
). Following similar steps as above, we compute the ternary
\lambda
-bracket of three currents resulting in

\left\{\mathbf{J}_{i}^{j}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}_{k}^{l}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}_{m}^{n}\right\}=d_{ij,kl,mn}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)

(4.20)

where the coefficients are precisely the totally symmetric tensor for
U(N_{f})
, which matches precisely with equation (
3.8
).

As explained above, in the presence of additional abelian flavour symmetries the stress tensor can mixed with the derivatives of the current. In this case, the only available symmetry is the overall
\mathrm{U}(1)\subset\mathrm{U}(N_{f})
which in fact can be identified with the superconformal R-symmetry. The stress tensor semi-chiral superfield then takes the form of a one-parameter family,

\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\partial_{{\dot{\alpha}}}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}-\zeta\partial_{{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathbf{J}_{i}^{i}

(4.21)

with an a priori arbitrary coefficient
\zeta
. Computing the ternary
\lambda
-bracket results in,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}\right\}=\frac{3}{2}N_{f}\zeta(1-\zeta)(1-2\zeta)\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}-N_{f}(1-2\zeta)\Lambda_{\mathrm{vector}}

(4.22)

Comparing this equation to (
3.14
), we can identify the central charges

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{16}N_{f}\left(1-8\zeta+18\zeta^{2}-12\zeta^{3}\right)\,,

(4.23)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{8}N_{f}\left(1-11\zeta+27\zeta^{2}-18\zeta^{3}\right)\,,

Note that
a
is maximised at
\zeta=\frac{1}{3}
, which is precisely
\frac{1}{2}r
, where
r=\frac{2}{3}
is the
r
-charge of a free chiral. Plugging this back into the equation above we reproduce the correct values of the central charges for a collection of
N_{f}
free chirals,

a=\frac{N_{f}}{48},\qquad\qquad c=\frac{N_{f}}{24}\,.

(4.24)

4.3
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM

Viewed as a
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFT,
\mathcal{N}=4
super Yang-Mills (SYM) is a gauge theory with
N_{f}=3
chiral multiplets in the adjoint representation of the gauge Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The BV action for the twisted theory is given by

S_{\rm BV}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}d^{2}z\ \mathrm{Tr}\,\mathbf{b}\left(\bar{\partial}\mathbf{c}-\frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}]\right)+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\bar{\partial}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}+\mathbf{c}[{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i},{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}]+{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{1}[{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{2},{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{3}]\,.

(4.25)

The choice of
\mathcal{N}=1
subalgebra of
\mathcal{N}=4
breaks the
\mathrm{SU}(4)_{R}
symmetry into
\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}\times\mathrm{SU}(3)_{f}
where the latter is a global symmetry from an
\mathcal{N}=1
point of view. The current operator of the
\mathrm{SU}(3)_{f}
flavour symmetry is given by

\mathbf{J}^{a}=\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i}(t^{a})^{i}_{j}\gamma^{j},\quad a=1,\cdots,9

(4.26)

where
t^{a}
are orthonormal basis of
SU(3)
, given explicitly in the appendix
A.2
.
The computation proceeds very similarly as above and we find the binary
\lambda
-brackets of the current as

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a},\mathbf{J}^{b}\right\}=f^{ab}{}_{c}J^{c}\,,

(4.27)

matching with the general form (
4.6
). Next, we compute the ternary
\lambda
-bracket of currents, resulting in (for
\mathrm{SU}(2)
gauge group)

\left\{\mathbf{J}^{a}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{J}^{b}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{J}^{c}\right\}=\dim_{\mathfrak{g}}\,d^{abc}\,\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)\,.

(4.28)

The stress tensor, satisfying (
4.7
), takes a form very similar to the examples above,

\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}=-\mathbf{b}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbf{c}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}=3}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}=3}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}({\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i})

(4.29)

where gauge indices are contracted. Note that the coefficient in front of the
R
-symmetry current matches with half the
r
-charge
r=\frac{2}{3}
of the chiral multiplets in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. After checking that this stress tensor indeed is correctly normalised we compute the ternary
\lambda
-bracket with the following result,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\dim_{\mathfrak{g}}}{9\pi^{2}}\lambda_{1\dot{\alpha}}\lambda_{2\dot{\beta}}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})_{\dot{\gamma}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

(4.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{3}\dim_{\mathfrak{g}}\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}

(4.31)

where
\dim_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the dimension of the lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Comparing this to (
3.14
), we find the following values of the central charges,

a=c=\frac{1}{4}\dim_{\mathfrak{g}}\,,

(4.32)

which precisely matches with the expected values for the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM
g
theory. This example thus clarifies our choice of naming for the second kinematic tensor in (
3.14
).

4.4  Fundamental
\mathrm{SU}(2)
SQCD

As our next example, we consider
\mathrm{SU}(2)
SQCD with
N_{f}
pairs of fundamental chiral multiplet and anti-fundamental chiral multiplets and vanishing superpotential. The BV action for the twisted theory is

S_{\rm BV}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}{\rm d}^{2}z\ \mathrm{Tr}\,\mathbf{b}\left(\bar{\partial}\mathbf{c}-\frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}]\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\bar{\partial}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\mathbf{c}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}+\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}^{i}\bar{\partial}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{i}+\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{i}\mathbf{c}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}^{i}\,.

(4.33)

Since the fundamental representation of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
is pseudoreal, the global symmetry is enhanced from
\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})\times\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{b}
to
\mathrm{SO}(4N_{f})\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{b}
, where
U(1)_{b}
is the baryonic symmetry under which
{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}
has charge 1 and
\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{i}
has charge -1. The current operators for the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})\times\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})
symmetry are given by

\mathbf{J}_{i}^{j}={\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{j},\qquad\qquad\tilde{\mathbf{J}}^{i}_{j}=\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}^{i}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{j}\,.

(4.34)

By combining these currents with additional currents with one tilded field we find the currents for the full
\mathrm{SO}(4N_{f})
flavour symmetry. As in the previous examples we can compute the
\lambda
-brackets containing three currents and read off the flavour central charge
k_{G}=2
, independent of
N_{f}
. The semi-chiral superfield corresponding to the baryonic
U(1)_{b}
current is

\mathbf{J}_{b}={\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}-\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}^{i}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{i}\,,

(4.35)

which has vanishing ternary
\lambda
-bracket.

The correctly normalised stress tensor satisfying (
4.7
) is given by

\mathbf{S}_{\alpha}=-\mathbf{b}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbf{c}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}_{i}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}^{i}-\frac{N_{f}-2}{2N_{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\left({\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}^{i}+\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{i}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}^{i}\right)\,.

(4.36)

where again the coefficient matches with half the
r
charge of the chirals. With this expression at hand, we can compute the ternary
\lambda
-bracket,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}\right\}=3\left(1-\frac{4}{N_{f}^{2}}\right)\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}-5\Lambda_{\mathrm{vector}}

(4.37)

Comparing with (
3.14
), we find the value of the central charges to be

a=\frac{21}{16}-\frac{9}{N_{f}^{2}},\quad c=\frac{13}{8}-\frac{9}{N_{f}^{2}}\,,

(4.38)

again reproducing the expected result. In this case, there is no free parameter and no need to perform
a
-maximisation since baryonic symmetries cannot mix with the
R
-symmetry. In the next example, however, we consider adjoint SQCD where
a
-maximisation will be necessary.

4.5  Adjoint
\mathrm{SU}(2)
SQCD

As our final example, we continue with
\mathrm{SU}(2)
SQCD but introduce an additional chiral multiplet
\chi
in the adjoint representation of the gauge algebra, which couples to the fundamental chirals with vanishing superpotential. In the range
3\leq N_{f}\leq 6
this theory is believed to flow to an
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFT in the IR. To distinguish the
\beta
\gamma
system originating from the adjoint chiral multiplet from the others we call the fields

{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{\rm adjoint}={\text{\boldmath$\psi$}}\,,\qquad\qquad{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{\rm adjoint}={\text{\boldmath$\phi$}}\,.

(4.39)

The BV action of the twisted theory is given by

S_{\rm BV}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}d^{2}z\ \mathrm{Tr}\,\mathbf{b}\left(\bar{\partial}\mathbf{c}-\frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}]\right)+{\text{\boldmath$\psi$}}(\bar{\partial}{\text{\boldmath$\phi$}}+[\mathbf{c},{\text{\boldmath$\phi$}}])+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\bar{\partial}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\mathbf{c}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}+\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}^{i}\bar{\partial}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{i}+\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{i}\mathbf{c}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}^{i}\,.

(4.40)

By adding the additional adjoint chiral, the global symmetry is broken to
\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{b}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{c}
where the last
\mathrm{U}(1)_{c}
acts on the adjoint chiral with charge
1
and on the (anti-)fundamental chirals with charge
\frac{1}{2N_{f}}
. The surviving
\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})
symmetry is the diagonal subgroup of the two
\mathrm{SU}(N_{f})
s,

\mathbf{J}_{\rm diag,i}^{j}=\mathbf{J}^{j}_{i}+\tilde{\mathbf{J}}^{j}_{i}\,,

(4.41)

Computing the
\mathbf{J}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{J}

\lambda
-bracket we can read off the flavour central charge which in this case is given by
k_{G}=4
.

In this case, the R-symmetry can mix with the additional non-baryonic
\mathrm{U}(1)_{c}
symmetry. For this reason, we find a one-parameter family of (correctly normalised) stress tensors that satisfy the
\mathbf{Q}
-closure condition (
4.7
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{{\dot{\alpha}}}=\,-\mathbf{b}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbf{c}

\displaystyle+{\text{\boldmath$\psi$}}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}{\text{\boldmath$\phi$}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}\left({\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{i}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}^{i}+\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}_{i}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}^{i}\right)

(4.42)

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta}{2}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\left({\text{\boldmath$\phi$}}{\text{\boldmath$\psi$}}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\zeta}{N_{f}}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}({\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}_{i}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}^{i}+\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}_{i}\tilde{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}}^{i})

With this stress tensor at hand, we once more compute the ternary
\lambda
-bracket,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\gamma}}\right\}=\frac{3}{8}\zeta\left(14-9\zeta+\left(3-\frac{32}{N_{f}^{2}}\right)\zeta^{2}\right)\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}=4}-5\zeta\Lambda_{\mathrm{vector}}\,.

(4.43)

Comparing this with (
3.14
), we find the values of the central charges to be

\displaystyle a=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{32}\zeta\left(32-27\zeta+\left(9-\frac{96}{N_{f}^{2}}\right)\zeta^{2}\right)\,,

(4.44)

\displaystyle c=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{32}\zeta\left(106-81\zeta+\left(27-\frac{288}{N_{f}^{2}}\right)\zeta^{2}\right)\,.

We find the superconformal
R
-symmetry maximising the central charge
a
at

\zeta_{*}=\frac{N_{f}\left(9N_{f}-\sqrt{1024-15N_{f}^{2}}\right)}{-96+9N_{f}^{2}}\,.

(4.45)

At this value of the parameter we find the central charges to be

\displaystyle a_{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{f}\left(1024\sqrt{1024-15N_{f}^{2}}+3N_{f}\left(-4608+189N_{f}^{2}-5N_{f}\sqrt{1024-15N_{f}^{2}}\right)\right)}{48\left(32-3N_{f}^{2}\right)^{2}}\,,

(4.46)

\displaystyle c_{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{f}\left(592\sqrt{1024-15N_{f}^{2}}+3N_{f}\left(-2544+117N_{f}^{2}-5N_{f}\sqrt{1024-15N_{f}^{2}}\right)\right)}{24\left(32-3N_{f}^{2}\right)^{2}}\,,

In line with results from the literature
[
56
,
64
]
. With these examples, we have covered a wide variety of different theories and phenomena firmly establishing the validity of our results. Turning on the superpotential
W={\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}{\text{\boldmath$\phi$}}\tilde{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}
triggers a relevant deformation towards
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD. For
N_{f}=4
, we find that with this superpotential turned on, our result reproduces the correct conformal anomalies as well as the flavour central charges. This is the only
\mathcal{N}=2
case in which the resulting IR theory is conformal and indeed we find that the stress tensor
\mathbf{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}
is not semi-chiral for any other values of
N_{f}

In this appendix, we collect various technical details that were omitted from the main text. This will be a crucial tool for many of the calculations in this work. We proceed by introducing some general aspects of heat kernel regularisation, after which we provide a detailed computation of the ternary
\lambda

3

B.1  Higher-dimensional residues

Cauchy’s integral formula for a disc
D_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
, states that for any smooth function
f:D_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and a point
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

f(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\oint_{\partial D_{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z-w}f(z)+\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{D_{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z-w}\wedge\bar{\partial}f\,.

(B.1)

If
f
is a holomorphic function the last term drops out and we find the well-known residue formula. The goal of this subsection is to introduce a generalisation of this result to higher dimensions.

A special role in (
B.1
) is played by the integration kernel
\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z-w}
. We can generalise Cauchy’s formula to
d
complex dimensions by introducing the Bochner-Martinelli kernel,

\omega_{\rm BM}(z,w)=\frac{(-1)^{d}(d-1)!}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{d}|z-w|^{2d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\bar{z}_{i}-\bar{w}_{i})\wedge{\rm d}\bar{z}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{{\rm d}\bar{z}}_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge{\rm d}\bar{z}_{d}\,.

(B.2)

where the
i
th
anti-holomorphic differential is omitted in the sum and
|z|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
. This is a differential form of type
(0,d-1)
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}\backslash\{w\}
which upon setting
d=1
precisely reduces to the ’Cauchy kernel’ introduced above. The Bochner-Martinelli kernel is a Green’s function for the Dolbeault differential
\bar{\partial}
, i.e.

\bar{\partial}\left({\rm d}^{d}z\,\omega_{\rm BM}(z,0)\right)=\delta^{(2d)}(z,\bar{z}){\rm d}^{d}z\wedge{\rm d}^{d}\bar{z}\,,

(B.3)

and therefore is closely related to the harmonic
(n,n)
-form on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Having introduced this kernel, we can generalise the Cauchy integration formula as follows for any smooth function
f:D_{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
on a
d
-dimensional disc,

f(w)=\oint_{\partial D_{d}}{\rm d}^{d}z\wedge\omega_{\rm BM}(z,w)f(z)+\int_{D_{d}}{\rm d}^{d}z\wedge\omega_{\rm BM}(z,w)\wedge\bar{\partial}f(z)\,.

(B.4)

For holomorphic functions, the second term vanishes, while for cohomologically holomorphic functions it is
\mathbf{Q}
-exact.

In this work, we are mainly interested in the case
d=2
for which the Bochner-Martinelli kernel takes the form,

\omega_{\rm BM}(z,w)=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\frac{\left(\bar{z}-\bar{w},{\rm d}\bar{z}\right)}{|z-w|^{4}}\,.

(B.5)

B.2  Heat kernel regularisation

The integrated correlators encountered in this work are often rather complicated to compute directly. However, we can make progress by introducing heat kernel regularisation. Recall the definition of the heat kernel in
d
complex dimensions,

K_{t}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(\pi t)^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2d}}{t}}\,,\qquad\qquad\text{where }\,x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\,,

(B.6)

which satisfies the heat equation,

\nabla_{x}^{2}K_{t}(x,y)=\partial_{t}K_{t}(x,y)\,,

(B.7)

where
\nabla_{x}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{\bar{x}_{i}}
for all
t>0
, with the initial condition

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}K_{t}(x,y)=\delta(x-y)\,.

(B.8)

From now on we specify to
d=2
where we can use the heat kernel to regularise the Bochner-Martinelli kernel as follows,

\omega_{{\rm BM},\epsilon}(z,0)=\frac{(\bar{z},{\rm d}\bar{z})}{(2{\rm i})^{2}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\ K_{t}(z)\,,

(B.9)

where we have introduced a UV regulator
\epsilon
. Sending
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we recover the standard Bochner-Martinelli kernel as introduced in (
B.5
). Acting on the regularised Bochner-Martinelli kernel with
\bar{\partial}
we find,

\bar{\partial}\omega_{{\rm BM},\epsilon}(z)=\frac{({\rm d}\bar{z},{\rm d}\bar{z})}{(2{\rm i})^{2}}K_{\epsilon}(z),

(B.10)

where we recognise the regularised
\delta
-function
\delta^{(4)}_{\epsilon}(z)=K_{\epsilon}(z)
. More generally, for arbitrary powers of
z_{e}
, we can rewrite inverse powers as follows

\frac{1}{|z_{e}|^{2\Delta}}=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\pi^{2}}{\Gamma(\Delta)}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t^{\Delta-1}}K_{t}(z)\,.

(B.11)

The integrals resulting from the
\lambda
-brackets in Section
3

I\left(\Gamma_{\Delta_{e}}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2n}}\left(\prod_{v=1}^{n}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}z_{v}{\rm d}^{2}\bar{z}_{v}}{(2\pi{\rm i})^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{v}\cdot z_{v}}\right)\left(\prod_{e}\frac{1}{|z_{e}|^{2\Delta_{e}}}\right)f(z_{v},\bar{z}_{v})\,,

(B.12)

where

is a homogeneous function,

Appendix C
Basic aspects of homological algebra

L_{\infty}
algebras are a generalisation of Lie algebras where the Jacobi identities are only satisfied up to homotopy. See e.g.
[
67
,
68
]
for an introduction. Such algebras have wide-ranging applications in physics, ranging from (closed) string field theory to QFTs and holography
[
69
,
70
,
71
,
72
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
. An
L_{\infty}
structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is a collection of multi-linear maps
[\bullet,\dots,\bullet]_{k}:\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes k}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
of degree
2-k
,
k\geq 1
, that are graded skew-symmetric

\left[x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}\right]_{n}=(-1)^{\mathrm{sgn}\,\sigma}\epsilon(\sigma,x)\left[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right]_{n}\,.

(C.1)

where
\epsilon(\sigma,x)
is the Koszul sign of the permutation
\sigma
. In addition, these maps have to satisfy the following set of generalised Jacobi identities,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathop{\rm Unsh}\nolimits(k,n-k)}(-1)^{\sigma}\epsilon(\sigma,x)\left[\left[x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)}\right]_{k},x_{\sigma(k+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}\right]_{n-k+1}=0\,,

(C.2)

where
\mathrm{Unsh}(k,n-k)
is a the subset of the permutation group
S_{n}
called
(k,n-k)
-unshuffle defined such that

\sigma(1)<\sigma(2)<\cdots<\sigma(k),\quad\sigma(k+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n)\,.

(C.3)

For small values of
n
such identities take the form

•

n=1
: It is customary to denote the 1-bracket
\left[\bullet\right]_{1}
by
{\rm d}
. The
n=1
Jacobi identity is simply
{\rm d}^{2}=0
so that
{\rm d}
is a differential on
\mathfrak{g}
.

•

n=2
: In this case (
C.2
) reduces to

{\rm d}\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]_{2}=\left[{\rm d}x_{1},x_{2}\right]+(-1)^{\left|x_{1}\right|}\left[x_{1},{\rm d}x_{2}\right]

(C.4)

which states that the differential
{\rm d}
satisfies a graded Leibniz rule with respect to the 2-bracket
[\bullet,\bullet]_{2}
. In the main text, we omit the subscript
2
and simply denote the 2-bracket as
[\bullet,\bullet]
.

•

n=3
: In this case the generalised Jacobi identity (
C.2
) yields

\displaystyle\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]_{2},x_{3}\right]_{2}+(-1)^{\left(\left|x_{1}\right|+\left|x_{2}\right|\right)\left|x_{3}\right|}\left[\left[x_{3},x_{1}\right]_{2},x_{2}\right]_{2}+(-1)^{\left(\left|x_{2}\right|+\left|x_{3}\right|\right)\left|x_{1}\right|}\left[\left[x_{2},x_{3}\right]_{2},x_{1}\right]_{2}

(C.5)

\displaystyle={\rm d}\left[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\right]_{3}+\left[{\rm d}x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\right]_{3}+(-1)^{\left|x_{1}\right|}\left[x_{1},{\rm d}x_{2},x_{3}\right]_{3}+(-1)^{\left|x_{1}\right|+\left|x_{2}\right|}\left[x_{1},x_{2},{\rm d}x_{3}\right]_{3}

which states that the binary
L_{\infty}
-bracket satisfies the usual Jacobi identity up to homotopy, where the deviation from the Jacobi identity for Lie algebras is given by the ternary
L_{\infty}
-bracket
[\bullet,\bullet,\bullet]_{3}
. In the main text, we omit the subscript
3
and simply denote the ternary
L_{\infty}
-bracket as
[\bullet,\bullet,\bullet]
.

Heuristically, an
L_{\infty}
algebra is a natural structure combining the notion of a Lie algebra and that of a chain complex, such that Jacobi identities are satisfied up to an infinite tower of relations. To give some more intuition into this structure let us consider some familiar special cases.

Ordinary Lie algebra

A Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},[-,-]_{2})
is an
L_{\infty}
algebra for which
\mathfrak{g}
is concentrated in degree zero, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, and the only non-trivial
L_{\infty}
-bracket is the binary bracket. In this case the Jacobi identities (
C.2
) reduce to the familiar expression

\left[\left[x_{1},x_{2}\right]_{2},x_{3}\right]_{2}+\left[\left[x_{3},x_{1}\right]_{2},x_{2}\right]_{2}+\left[\left[x_{2},x_{3}\right]_{2},x_{1}\right]_{2}=0\,,

(C.6)

while all other generalised identities vanish identically.

dg Lie algebra

A differential graded Lie algebra (dgla)
(\mathfrak{g},{\rm d},[\bullet,\bullet]_{2})
is an
L_{\infty}
algebra for which only the 1- and 2-bracket are non-trivial.

Lie 2-algebra

A Lie 2-algebra is to a Lie 2=group as a Lie algebra is to a 2-group. A Lie 2-algebra is an
L_{\infty}
algebra with generators concentrated in the lowest two degrees,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with the only non-trivial
L_{\infty}
brackets being the 1-, 2- and 3-bracket. The 1-bracket encodes the differential
{\rm d}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, the binary bracket when restricted to degree zero is defines a Lie bracket. The ternary bracket
[\bullet,\bullet,\bullet]_{3}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{0}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is called the Jacobiator.

\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathop{\rm Witt}\nolimits)
is defined by a 2-cocycle and can be written as the residue,

\varphi(\mathbf{L}_{n},\mathbf{L}_{m})=\frac{1}{12}\oint\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}}\partial_{z}f(z)\partial^{2}_{z}g(z)\,.

(D.27)

so that

\varphi(\mathbf{L}_{n},\mathbf{L}_{m})=\frac{1}{12}n(n^{2}-1)\delta_{n+m,0}\,.

(D.28)

Altogether, we recover the familiar form for the commutation relation of the Virasoro algebra

\left[\mathbf{L}_{n},\mathbf{L}_{m}\right]=(n-m)\mathbf{L}_{n+m}+\frac{c}{12}n\left(n^{2}-1\right)\delta_{n+m,0}\,.

(D.29)

In some scenarios it can be useful to regard the Virasoro algebra as a Lie conformal algebra. In physical application the Virasoro algebra is obtained as the mode algebra of the holomorphic stress tensor
T(z)
. Note that in physics literature the numbering of modes is usually shifted from the maths notation so that it is customary to define
\mathbf{L}_{n}=\left\{T\,,\bullet\right\}_{n+1}
. We can then define the
\lambda
-bracket of the stress tensor with a primary operator as follows,

\displaystyle\left\{{T(z)\,}_{\lambda}\mathcal{O}(0)\right\}=

\displaystyle\oint_{S^{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda z}\,T(z)\mathcal{O}(0)

(D.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{-1}\,\mathcal{O}(0)+\lambda\,L_{0}\,\mathcal{O}(0)+L_{1}\,\mathcal{O}(0)+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{O}(0)+h\,\lambda\,\mathcal{O}(0)\,,

where
h
is the conformal weight of the primary operator
\mathcal{O}
. From this expression, we can straightforwardly read off the singular part of the OPE

T(z)\mathcal{O}(0)\sim\frac{h\,\mathcal{O}(0)}{z}+\partial\mathcal{O}(0)\,.

(D.31)

Similarly, the we compute the
\lambda
-bracket of two stress tensors as

\left\{T\,{}_{\lambda}\,T\right\}=\lambda^{3}\frac{c}{12}+\Big(2\lambda+\partial\Big)T\,,

(D.32)

which again encodes the singular part of the OPE

T(z)T(0)\sim\frac{c/2}{z^{4}}+\frac{2T}{z^{2}}+\frac{\partial T}{z}\,.

(D.33)

D.4  Higher Virasoro algebra

Similar to the above, we now generalise this to the higher Virasoro algebra in two complex dimensions. The higher Virasoro algebra can be obtained as a central extension of the higher

In this subsection we provide a simple proof of (

D.6  Associativity relations of
\lambda
-brackets

Finally, we finish this appendix by listing the various associativity relations the Higher
\lambda
-brackets satisfy. These relations were instrumental in constraining the allowed expressions for the
\lambda
-brackets in Section
2
. The whole set of associativity relations for general
n
-ary
\lambda
-brackets remains an open question. However, for the purposes of this work we only need a subset of relations derived in
[
13
,
48
]
. The
n
-ary
\lambda
-brackets are graded symmetric under permutations of their arguments and with the
\lambda
parameters. The last entry of the
\lambda
-brackets behaves in a slightly different manner from the others but if we define
\lambda_{n}\equiv-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}-\partial
, with the latter symbol acting outside the bracket they are graded symmetric under permutation of all arguments.

The binary
\lambda
-bracket
\{\bullet\,_{\lambda}\bullet\}
is best under control. In this case the bracket has to satisfy the following set of axioms:

1.

\{\partial A\,{}_{\lambda}\,B\}=-\lambda\{A\,{}_{\lambda}\,B\}
,

2.

\partial\{A\,{}_{\lambda}\,B\}=-\lambda\{A\,{}_{\lambda}\,B\}+\{A\,{}_{\lambda}\,\partial B\}
, which implies

\{A\,{}_{\lambda}\,\partial B\}=(\partial+\lambda)\{A\,{}_{\lambda}\,B\}
,

3.

\{A\,{}_{\lambda}\,B\}=(-1)^{|A||B|}\{B\,{}_{-\lambda-\partial}\,A\}
,

4.

\{A\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\{B\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\,C\}\}-(-1)^{(|A|+1)(|B|+1)}\{B\,{}_{\lambda_{2}}\{A\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,C\}\}+(-1)^{|A|}\{\{A\,{}_{\lambda_{1}}\,B\}\,{}_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}C\}=0
,

where in the equations above
|A|
and
|B|
denote the fermion parity of the operators.

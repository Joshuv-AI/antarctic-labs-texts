Introduction

This paper is devoted to complementing known results on

-estimates for second-order linear elliptic equations with singular drifts terms. Let

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Div(A\nabla u)+\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u+\lambda u&=f+\Div\mathbf{F}&&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Div(A^{T}\nabla v)-\Div(v\mathbf{b})+\lambda v&=g+\Div\mathbf{G}&&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\

and

.

For each

for all

for all

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev’s embedding theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|(v\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u|\,d{x}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|{v}\|_{L^{(p^{\prime})^{*}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p,\Omega)\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\|{u}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|{v}\|_{W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. This proves (

).

Note that
\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u\in W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
by the estimate (
3.1
). To show
\varepsilon
-inequality (
3.2
), note that integration by part shows that the identity

\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u)vdx=-\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v)u\,d{x}-\int_{\Omega}(\Div\mathbf{b})uv\,d{x}

holds for any

,

, and

is dense in

(see

) and the estimate (

) holds, a standard density argument shows that identity (

) holds for any
\mathbf{b}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, and
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Since

is dense in

, there exists

such that

\|{\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}<{\varepsilon}/{C},

where

is the same constant in (
3.1

. Then by (

), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u)v\,d{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla u]v\,d{x}+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u)vdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla u]v\,d{x}-\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v)udx-\int_{\Omega}(\Div\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon})uv\,d{x}.

By (
3.1
) and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u)v|\,d{x}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\|{u}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|{v}\|_{W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\|{\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|{v}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+\|{\Div\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|{v}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\varepsilon\|{u}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+C_{\varepsilon}\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)\|{v}\|_{W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)},

where
C_{\varepsilon}=\|{\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|{\Div\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. Since

was arbitrary chosen, this implies that

\|{\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u}\|_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\|{u}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+C_{\varepsilon}\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

which proves (
3.2
).

To show (
3.3
), suppose that
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and
v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Since

\int_{\Omega}(v\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u\,d{x}=\int_{\Omega}[v(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon})]\cdot\nabla u\,d{x}+\int_{\Omega}(v\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla u\,d{x},

it follows from (
3.1
) and Hölder’s inequality that

\int_{\Omega}|(v\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u|\,d{x}\leq(\varepsilon\|{v}\|_{W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|{v}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)})\|{u}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

(3.5)

Since

\left<\Div(v\mathbf{b}),u\right>=-\int_{\Omega}(v\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u\,d{x}

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it follows from (
3.5
) that

and the estimate (
3.3
) holds. This completes the proof of Proposition

.

.

Since
\Div A\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}a^{ij}D_{i}\left[(D_{j}u)\phi\right]\,d{x}=-\int_{\Omega}\phi\Div A\cdot\nabla u\,d{x}

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Hence
u
satisfies

-\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}a^{ij}D_{ij}u+(\mathbf{b}-\Div A)\cdot\nabla u+\lambda u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Therefore by Theorem
4.2
, we conclude that
u
is identically zero in
\Omega
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
2.5

This section is devoted to a proof of Theorem
2.5
. For the case of sufficiently large
\lambda
, this was done by Theorem
3.3
. To treat the case of small
\lambda
, we use a functional analytic argument as in Kang-Kim
[
19
,
17
]
and Kim-Kwon
[
20
]
to deduce that it suffices to prove the uniqueness part of (i). To explain this, let
2<p<\infty
be fixed. Then

(u,v)\mapsto\left<\mathcal{L}_{p}u,v\right>=\int_{\Omega}(A\nabla u)\cdot\nabla v\,d{x}+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u)v\,d{x}

and

(u,v)\mapsto\left<\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}v,u\right>=\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla u\,d{x}+\int_{\Omega}(v\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u\,d{x}=\left<\mathcal{L}_{p}u,v\right>

define bounded linear operators

\mathcal{L}_{p}:{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-1,p}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}:{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Let
\mathcal{I}_{p}:L^{p}(\Omega)\rightarrow(L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega))^{\prime}
be the isomorphism defined by

\left<\mathcal{I}_{p}u,v\right>=\int_{\Omega}uv\,d{x}\quad\text{for all }(u,v)\in L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega).

We also define
\Div_{p}:L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
by

\left<\Div_{p}\mathbf{F},\varphi\right>=-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{F}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,d{x}\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega).

Then it is easy to show that
u\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of the problem (

D

) if and only if
(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p})u=\mathcal{I}_{p}f+\Div_{p}\mathbf{F}
, while
v\in{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (

D
′

) if and only if
(\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}})v=\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}}g+\Div_{p^{\prime}}\mathbf{G}
.

By Theorem
3.3
, there exist
\gamma=\gamma(p,\delta)>0
,
\theta=\theta(p,\delta)>0
, and
\lambda_{1}\geq 1
such that under Assumptions
2.2

(\gamma)
and
2.3

(\theta)
, the operators
\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{I}_{p}
and
\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}}
are invertible. Then for
0\leq\lambda\neq\lambda_{1}
, the linear operators

\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda)(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{I}_{p})^{-1}\circ\mathcal{I}_{p}:L^{p}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)

and

\mathcal{K}_{p^{\prime},\lambda}^{*}=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda)(\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}})^{-1}\circ\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)

are bounded and even compact by Rellich-Kondrachov’s embedding theorem. Since

\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda}u)v\,d{x}=\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{K}_{p^{\prime},\lambda}^{*}v)u\,d{x}

for all
(u,v)\in L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, it follows that

\mathcal{K}_{p^{\prime},\lambda}^{*}=\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}},

(5.1)

where
\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda}^{\prime}:(L^{p}(\Omega))^{\prime}\rightarrow(L^{p}(\Omega))^{\prime}
is the adjoint operator of
\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda}
. Note also that

\ker(Id-\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda})=\ker(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p})\quad\text{and}\quad\ker(Id-\mathcal{K}_{p^{\prime},\lambda}^{*})=\ker(\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}}).

By (
5.1
) and the Fredholm alternative theorem (see e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 6.6]
), we have

\mathrm{Im}(Id-\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda})=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}uv\,d{x}=0\quad\text{for all }v\in\ker(\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}})\right\},

(5.2)

\mathrm{Im}(Id-\mathcal{K}_{p^{\prime},\lambda}^{*})=\left\{v\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}vu\,d{x}=0\quad\text{for all }u\in\ker(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p})\right\},

(5.3)

and

\dim\ker(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p})=\dim\ker(\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}})<\infty

(5.4)

for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
2.5
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Choose
(\gamma_{0},\theta_{0},\lambda_{1})
from Theorem
3.3
and choose
\gamma_{1}
from Theorem
4.1
, and let
\gamma=\min\{\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\}
. Under Assumptions
2.2

(\gamma)
and
2.3

(\theta)
, Theorem
2.5
holds for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
by Theorem
3.3
. Suppose that
0\leq\lambda<\lambda_{1}
. By (
5.4
) and Theorem
4.1
, we have

\ker(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p})=\ker(\mathcal{L}_{p^{\prime}}^{*}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p^{\prime}})=\{0\}

(5.5)

for any
\lambda\geq 0
. Hence by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we have

\mathrm{Im}(Id-\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda})=\mathrm{Im}(Id-\mathcal{K}_{p^{\prime},\lambda}^{*})=L^{p}(\Omega).

(5.6)

Given
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\mathbf{F}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, let

w=(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{I}_{p})^{-1}(\mathcal{I}_{p}f+\Div_{p}\mathbf{F}).

By (
5.6
), there exists
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that

u-\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda}u=w.

Since
(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{I}_{p})^{-1}
maps
W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
to
{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, it follows from the definition of
\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda}
that
w,\mathcal{K}_{p,\lambda}u\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence

u\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p})u=\mathcal{I}_{p}f+\Div_{p}\mathbf{F}.

This proves that
u
is a weak solution of the problem (

D

). Similarly, we can also prove the existence of weak solution of the problem (

D
′

). By (
5.5
), we also have uniqueness of weak solution of the problem (

D
′

). It remains to show the desired estimate.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\mathbf{F}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Define

\left<\ell,\phi\right>=\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,d{x}-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{F}\cdot\nabla\phi\,d{x}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Then

\ell\in W^{-1,p}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\ell}\|_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega)}\leq\|{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Since the operator
\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p}
is bijective, it follows that there exists
u_{\lambda}\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p})u_{\lambda}=\ell.

Moreover, it follows from the bounded inverse theorem (see e.g.
[
3
, Corollary 2.7]
) that there exists a constant
C_{\lambda}>0
independent of
u_{\lambda}
and
\ell
such that

\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\lambda}\|{\ell}\|_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\lambda}(\|{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}).

In fact, we can choose a constant independent of
\lambda
. Indeed, for
0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}
, let

E_{\lambda}=\left\{\mu\in[0,\lambda_{1}]:C_{\lambda}|\lambda-\mu|<\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

For
\mu\in E_{\lambda}
, since the operator
\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p}
is bijective, there exists
u_{\mu}\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(\mathcal{L}_{p}+\lambda\mathcal{I}_{p})u_{\mu}=\mathcal{I}_{p}f+\Div_{p}\mathbf{F}+(\lambda-\mu)\mathcal{I}_{p}u_{\mu}.

Moreover, we have

\|{u_{\mu}}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq 2C_{\lambda}(\|{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)})

for all
\mu\in E_{\lambda}
. Hence by the compactness of
[0,\lambda_{1}]
, there is a constant
C>0
independent of
u_{\lambda}
,
f
,
\mathbf{F}
, and
\lambda
such that

\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq 2C(\|{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}).

By the Poincaré inequality, we conclude that

\|{\nabla u_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lambda^{1/2}\|{u_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)})

for all
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{1}]
.
This proves the desired estimate for a solution of the problem (

D

). Following exactly the same argument, one can derive a similar estimate for a weak solution of the problem (

D
′

). This completes the proof of Theorem
2.5
.
∎

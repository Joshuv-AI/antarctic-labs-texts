Dark Matter Freeze-out via Catalyzed Annihilation
Chuan-Yang Xing, Shou-hua Zhu

Abstract
We present a new paradigm of dark matter freeze-out, where the annihilation of dark matter particles is catalyzed. We discuss in detail the regime that the depletion of dark matter proceeds via 2 ​ χ → 2 ​ A ′ 2\chi\to 2A^{\prime} and 3 ​ A ′ → 2 ​ χ 3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi processes, in which χ \chi and A ′ A^{\prime} denote dark matter and the catalyst respectively. In this regime, the dark matter number density is depleted polynomially rather than exponentially (Boltzmann suppression) as in classical WIMPs and SIMPs. The paradigm applies for a secluded weakly interacting dark sector with a dark matter in the MeV-TeV mass range. The catalyzed annihilation paradigm is compatible with CMB and BBN constraints, with enhanced indirect detection signals.



I
Introduction

The existence of dark matter (DM) is well established with ample evidence from cosmological and astrophysical observations
Bauer and Plehn 2019
. Though, the nature of dark matter is still unknown.
To solve this puzzle, tremendous efforts have been devoted to searching for dark matter candidates and studying production mechanisms of dark matter in the early universe.
Among all the mechanisms that reproduce the observed abundance of dark matter, the possibility of thermal dark matter where dark matter keeps in thermal equilibrium with Standard Model (SM) particles in the early universe is especially popular and compelling.

For massive thermal dark matter, DM particles remain in thermal and chemical equilibrium while relativistic. As the universe cools down, DM particles are depleted via some certain processes and the abundance of dark matter goes down.
These processes freeze-out when their interaction rate falls below the expansion rate of the universe, and consequently DM abundance is settled.
There are essentially two kinds of process leading to depletion of DM particles in the literature. The first one is that DM particles annihilate into other particles, mostly SM particles. The other one is via number-changing process of dark matter.
For the former case, the most studied scenario is self-annihilation process
Lee and Weinberg 1977
, e.g.
2\text{DM}\to 2\text{SM}
. Especially, weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs) that naturally reproduce correct relic abundance attracted extensive attentions
Bertone et al. 2005
;
Arcadi et al. 2018
;
Roszkowski et al. 2018
.
Other variations on the self-annihilation case include co-annihilation
Griest and Seckel 1991
;
Ellis et al. 1998
;
D’Agnolo et al. 2018
, semi-annihilation
D’Eramo and Thaler 2010
and so on
Griest and Seckel 1991
;
D’Agnolo and Ruderman 2015
;
Pospelov et al. 2008
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2009
;
Feng and Kumar 2008
;

;

;

;

;

;

;

Whereas, for the number-changing process, the most studied process is
3\text{DM}\to 2\text{DM}
annihilation, dubbed as strongly interaction massive particles (SIMPs)
Hochberg et al. 2014
;
Hochberg et al. 2015

In this Letter, we propose a new pattern of dark matter burning in the early universe beside the two aforementioned kinds of process, where the abundance of dark matter is determined by
catalyzed

In catalyzed processes, there are some other particles beside DM that act as the catalyst. The catalyst can
enhance the rate of dark matter burning
, yet the catalyst itself is
not consumed in the reaction

;
Petrucci 2002

Specifically, in this work we study a simple regime of catalyzed annihilation with two processes leading to depletion of DM particles:
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
and
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi

\chi

A^{\prime}

Note that the assisted annihilation

;

;

We acknowledge that catalyzed processes are also considered in the Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN)

The observed DM abundance can be reproduced in the catalyzed annihilation paradigm for a wide mass range of dark matter.
We emphasis that the thermal evolution in the catalyzed annihilation paradigm is unique. Different with WIMPs and SIMPs, where DM number density

n_{\chi}\propto s^{3/2}\propto T^{9/2},

where
s

T

Thus, the catalyzed annihilation lasts longer and freezes-out at late times.
To reproduce correct relic abundance, the cross section of DM annihilation
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
should be enhanced since there is less time to redshift to today
Dror et al. 2016
, which corresponds to enhanced indirect detection signals.

II
Catalyzed Freeze-out

In order for the catalyzed annihilation paradigm to work, there are several requirements listed as follows:

•

The dark sector is nearly secluded.

•

The catalyst is long-lived (
\gtrsim 10^{-9}\text{s}
).

•

The catalyst is slightly lighter than DM (
1<m_{\chi}/m_{A^{\prime}}\lesssim 2
).

•

Annihilation channels as in Figure
1
.

The dark sector should be secluded so that the annihilation channels to SM particles freeze-out before the catalyzed annihilation.
The catalyst is lighter than DM and long-lived so that its number density is large and the
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
process is not suppressed, which ensures that the catalyzed annihilation happens.
If
A^{\prime}
decays fast, the paradigm recovers to the secluded DM regime
Pospelov et al. 2008
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2009
. Besides, the catalyzed annihilation will heat up the dark sector. For simplicity, we assume the dark sector could scatter with SM particles intensely enough to maintain thermal equilibrium with SM particles.

We show in Figure

a typical thermal history of dark matter that freezes-out via catalyzed annihilation. For now, we are focused on the regime that the mass ratio of dark matter and the catalyst
r\equiv m_{\chi}/m_{A^{\prime}}
is no larger than
1.5
. As is shown in the figure, there are four stages in the thermal evolution:

1.

Equilibrium stage.
Both
\chi
and
A^{\prime}
stay in chemical equilibrium due to the number-changing processes in the dark sector. The dominate number-changing process is
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
for
r\gtrsim 1.1
. Other processes with DM in the initial state, e.g.
\chi A^{\prime}A^{\prime}\to\chi A^{\prime}
, are suppressed and negligible, since
n_{\chi}\ll n_{A^{\prime}}
.

n_{\chi}\simeq\bar{n}_{\chi},n_{A^{\prime}}\simeq\bar{n}_{A^{\prime}}.

(2)

n_{\chi,A^{\prime}}
denote the number densities and
\bar{n}_{\chi,A^{\prime}}
are the equilibrium densities.
In the non-relativistic limit, we have
\bar{n}_{\chi,A^{\prime}}=g_{\chi,A^{\prime}}\left(\frac{m_{\chi,A^{\prime}}T}{2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m_{\chi,A^{\prime}}/T}
, where
g
denotes number of internal degrees of freedom.

2.

Chemical stage.

\chi
and
A^{\prime}
are chemically decoupled from equilibrium, but they can still maintain chemical equilibrium with each other via the
2\chi\leftrightarrow 2{A^{\prime}}
process.

n_{\chi}/\bar{n}_{\chi}\simeq n_{A^{\prime}}/\bar{n}_{A^{\prime}}.

(3)

3.

Catalyzed annihilation.

As the rate of
2A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
(inverse process of
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
) descends exponentially at low temperature, the
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
process dominates over it. The evolution of DM number density is now controlled by the catalyzed annihilation, i.e.
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
and
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
. Before freeze-out, the rates of the
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
and
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
reactions are much larger than the rate of change of
n_{\chi}
, as well as the Hubble rate and rates of other reactions (see in Eq.
10
). Thus, neglecting the subdominant terms, we get an approximate relation,

\left<\sigma_{2}v\right>n_{\chi}^{2}\simeq\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>n_{A^{\prime}}^{3}.

(4)

We used
\left<\sigma_{2}v\right>
and
\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>
to denote the thermally averaged cross sections of
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
and
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
respectively. In this stage, since
y_{A^{\prime}}\equiv n_{A^{\prime}}/s
is practically constant and
\left<\sigma_{2}v\right>
and
\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>
are polynomial functions of
T
, Eq.
4
indicates that
n_{\chi}
is polynomially suppressed. It is similar to the scaling of the number density of the assisting particle after DM freeze-out in Ref.
Cline et al. 2017
.

4.

Freeze-out.
As the universe expands, the rate of the catalyzed annihilation descends and dark matter freezes-out.

The equilibrium stage ends when the rate of
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
falls below Hubble constant
H
. The temperature of departure from equilibrium
T_{c}
can be determined approximately with,

\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>\bar{n}_{A^{\prime}}^{3}\simeq H(\bar{n}_{A^{\prime}}+\bar{n}_{\chi}).

(5)

We note that the annihilation channels to SM particles or the
3A^{\prime}\to 2A^{\prime}
process can also deplete dark sector particles and
T_{c}
could be altered if these channels freeze-out later.
The ending of the chemical stage is insignificant since the freeze-out temperature
T_{f}
and relic abundance can be estimated without it.
Lastly, the catalyzed annihilation freezes-out when the rate drops below
H
.
T_{f}
is determined by,

\left<\sigma_{2}v\right>n_{\chi}^{2}\simeq\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>n_{A^{\prime}}^{3}\simeq Hn_{\chi}.

(6)

The relic abundance of DM can be estimated approximately in the same spirit of WIMPs
Kolb and Turner 1990
;
Dror et al. 2016
,

\Omega_{\chi}=\frac{m_{\chi}s_{0}H_{m}}{\rho_{c}s_{m}}\frac{\sqrt{g_{\star,m}}}{\sqrt{g_{\star,f}}}\frac{x_{f}}{\left<\sigma_{2}v\right>}.

(7)

where
x_{f}\equiv m_{\chi}/T_{f}
.
Since
x_{f}
is dependent on
x_{c}\equiv m_{\chi}/T_{c}
, we solve for
x_{c}
with Eq.
5
first. Simplifying Eq.
5
, we find,

x_{c}=\frac{r}{2}\log\left[0.0024\frac{g_{A^{\prime}}^{2}m_{\chi}^{4}M_{\text{Pl}}\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>_{c}}{g_{\star,c}^{1/2}r^{3}x_{c}}\right],

(8)

where
M_{\text{Pl}}
is Planck mass. With
x_{c}
determined in Eq.
8
, we can solve for
n_{A^{\prime}}
. Substituting the result into Eq.
6

x_{f}=\left(1.2\frac{g_{\star,f}^{2}}{g_{\star,c}^{9/4}}\frac{M_{\text{Pl}}^{1/2}\left<\sigma_{2}v\right>_{f}\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>_{f}}{m_{\chi}\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>_{c}^{3/2}}x_{c}^{6}\right)^{1/5}.

(9)

m,c,f

T=m_{\chi},T_{c},T_{f}
, respectively, for the quantities, including entropy density

In previous section, we concentrated on the mass ratio
r\leq 1.5
. In fact, the catalyzed annihilation paradigm can go beyond this limit. Firstly, when the mass ratio is slightly larger than
1.5
, i.e.
3m_{A^{\prime}}<2m_{\chi}
,
\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>
is exponentially suppressed as the temperature goes down,

\left<\sigma_{3}v^{2}\right>\propto e^{-(2r-3)x/r},

(11)

where
x\equiv m_{\chi}/T
. During the catalyzed annihilation period, with less DM particles produced via
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
process since the cross section is smaller, the DM number density shrinks more sharply. Consequently, the catalyzed annihilation freezes-out much earlier.

As the mass ratio grows, when
r\lesssim 2
, it is intriguing to notice that the
4A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
process may play a part in the catalyzed annihilation.
To be specific, after a period of catalyzed annihilation governed by
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
and
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
as usual, there would be an extra stage of catalyzed annihilation predominated by
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
and
4A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
, in which the non-suppressed
4A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
process takes over the role of converting
A^{\prime}
to DM particles since the cross section of
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
is exponentially suppressed (Eq.
11
),
Similar to Eq.
4
, we can deduce an approximate relation that holds in this stage,

\left<\sigma_{2}v\right>n_{\chi}^{2}\simeq\left<\sigma_{4}v^{3}\right>n_{A^{\prime}}^{4},

(12)

where
\left<\sigma_{4}v^{3}\right>
denotes the thermally averaged cross section for
4A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
.
The presence of
4A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
is essential. If it is neglected, as is discussed previously,
n_{\chi}
shrinks sharply and dark matter freezes-out early. Once
4A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
takes charge, the sharply falling of
n_{\chi}
is bent and the polynomial suppression recovers (compared to Eq.
1
).

n_{\chi}\propto s^{2}\propto T^{6}.

(13)

Thus, the catalyzed annihilation freezes-out at later times, leading to enhanced DM relic abundance.

For even larger mass ratio, we expect the processes with more catalysts annihilating to two DM particles, e.g.
5A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
, to possibly play a role in the catalyzed annihilation, especially when the dark sector is strongly coupled.

We show in Figure
3

\Omega_{\chi}h^{2}

1.5
,
\Omega_{\chi}h^{2}
decreases rapidly. On the other hand, for
r\lesssim 2
, relic abundance is uplifted if
4A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
process is included.

IV
A Model

The requirements for realization of the catalyzed annihilation presented in Section
II
can be easily met in many models.
In this section, we simply present a dark photon model
Holdom 1986
;
Raggi and Kozhuharov 2015
;
Bauer et al. 2020
;
Lao et al. 2020
;
Fabbrichesi et al. 2020
with a Dirac fermion
\chi
charged under a novel
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge group and
A^{\prime}
being the gauge field.
The Lagrangian for the dark sector is,

\mathcal{L}_{\text{DS}}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}A^{\prime}_{\mu}A^{\prime\mu}+\bar{\chi}(i\not{D}-m_{\chi})\chi,

(14)

where
\not{D}=\not{\partial}-ig_{D}\not{A^{\prime}}
and
g_{D}
is the gauge coupling constant. The mass of the dark photon can be generated via the Higgs mechanism (or Stueckelberg mechanism
Stueckelberg 1938
;
Ruegg and Ruiz-Altaba 2004
). We assume the dark Higgs boson is heavy and can be neglected.
SM particles are neutral under the
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge group. The dark photon can be kinetically mixed with SM hypercharge field.

\mathcal{L}_{\text{mix}}=-\frac{\epsilon}{2\cos{\theta_{W}}}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}.

(15)

\epsilon
is the mixing constant and
\theta_{W}
denotes the Weinberg angle.
B^{\mu}
is SM hypercharge field. Therefore, the dark sector can communicate with SM particles via the mixing and the dark photon
A^{\prime}
can decay to SM particles.
\epsilon
should be small so that the dark photon is long-lived and acts as the catalyst.
Additionally, the kinetic mixing could not keep the dark sector in thermal equilibrium with SM since
\epsilon
is small. In order to thermalize the dark sector, we need another portal for the dark sector to interact with SM particles, which might be the dark Higgs. Anyhow, we won’t model this part and simply assume that the dark sector stays in thermal equilibrium before freeze-out.

V
Conclusion and discussion

We proposed a novel paradigm for thermal relic dark matter, yielding the observed relic abundance. The distinctive wisdom of the paradigm is that the dark matter freeze-out proceeds via catalyzed annihilation. We discussed in detail the scenario that the catalyzed annihilation includes
2\chi\to 2A^{\prime}
and
3A^{\prime}\to 2\chi
, where
\chi
and
A^{\prime}
are dark matter and the catalyst respectively. The paradigm applies for a wide mass range of dark matter, from
1MeV
to
100TeV
, with a unique thermal history compared with WIMPs and SIMPs. Besides, the paradigm offers rich phenomenology including indirect DM search and long-lived particles.

We note that thermal decoupling effects can significantly modify dark matter relic abundance
Kuflik et al. 2016
;
Pappadopulo et al. 2016
;
Farina et al. 2016
;
D’Agnolo et al. 2017
;
Kuflik et al. 2017
;
Fitzpatrick et al. 2020
. We leave this to future works
Xing and Zhu
.
Additionally, catalyzed annihilation dominating DM abundance can go far beyond the reactions considered here and should be investigated further.

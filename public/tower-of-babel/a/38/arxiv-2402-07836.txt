Mad families of Gowers’ infinite block sequences
Clement Yung

Abstract
. Call a subset of 𝐅𝐈𝐍 k \mathbf{FIN}_{k} small if it does not contain a copy of ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle for some infinite block sequence A ∈ 𝐅𝐈𝐍 k [ ∞ ] A\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]} . Gowers’ 𝐅𝐈𝐍 k \mathbf{FIN}_{k} theorem asserts that the set of small subsets of 𝐅𝐈𝐍 k \mathbf{FIN}_{k} forms an ideal, so it is sensible to consider almost disjoint families of 𝐅𝐈𝐍 k \mathbf{FIN}_{k} with respect to the ideal of small subsets of 𝐅𝐈𝐍 k \mathbf{FIN}_{k} . We shall show that 𝔞 𝐅𝐈𝐍 k \mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{FIN}_{k}} , the smallest possible cardinality of an infinite mad family of 𝐅𝐈𝐍 k \mathbf{FIN}_{k} , is uncountable.



1.
Introduction

For each
k\in\omega\setminus\{0\}
, let
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
denote the set of all mappings
x:\omega\to\{0,\dots,k\}
such that
\operatorname{supp}(x)
is finite and
k\in\operatorname{ran}(p)
. The
support
of some
x\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
is the set of all
n\in\operatorname{dom}(x)
in which
x(n)\neq 0
. For two such maps
x,y
of disjoint support, we may define a partial binary operation
+
on
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
by stipulating that:

\displaystyle(x+y)(i)=\begin{cases}x(i),&\text{if $i\in\operatorname{supp}(p)$},\\
y(i),&\text{if $i\in\operatorname{supp}(q)$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that
(\mathbf{FIN}_{k},+)
forms a partial semigroup. Similar to the previous section, we may define a partial order
<
on
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
, where for any
x,y\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
:

\displaystyle x<y\iff\max(\operatorname{supp}(x))<\min(\operatorname{supp}(y)).

A
block sequence
of elements of
\mathbf{FIN}
is thus a
<
-increasing sequence. We also define the
tetris operation

T:\mathbf{FIN}_{k}\to\mathbf{FIN}_{k-1}
by stipulating that:

\displaystyle T(x)(i):=\max\{x(i)-1,0\}.

For each such
\alpha
, the space of block sequences of length
\alpha
is denoted by
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\alpha]}
(so
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
is the space of all infinite block sequences, and
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[<\infty]}:=\bigcup_{n<\omega}\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[n]}
). Given a finite block sequence
A=(x_{n})_{n<N}
, the
partial semigroup
generated by
A
, denoted as
\langle A\rangle
or
\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{N-1}\rangle
, can be written in one of the following equivalent ways:

\displaystyle\langle A\rangle

\displaystyle:=\{T^{\lambda_{0}}(x_{0})+\cdots+T^{\lambda_{N-1}}(x_{N-1}):\lambda_{i}=0\text{ for some }i<N\},

\displaystyle=\{x=T^{\lambda_{0}}(x_{0})+\cdots+T^{\lambda_{N-1}}(x_{N-1}):k\in\operatorname{ran}(x)\}.

If
A=(x_{n})_{n<\omega}
is an infinite block sequence, then:

\displaystyle\langle A\rangle:=\bigcup_{N<\omega}\langle x_{0},\dots,x_{N-1}\rangle.

This allows us to define a partial order
\leq
on
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
, where
A\leq B
iff
\langle A\rangle subseteq\langle B\rangle
.

The Ramsey theory of
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
was first explored by Gowers
[
2
]
in proving the oscillation stability of uniformly continuous functions
f:S_{c_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
S_{c_{0}}
is the unit sphere in
c_{0}
. The current formulation of infinite block sequences is also due to Todorčević in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Gowers
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
Theorem)
.

For any
Y\subseteq\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
and
A\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
, there exists some
B\leq A
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y
or
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}
.

Call a subset of
Y\subseteq\mathbf{FIN}_{k}

small
if there does not exist some
A\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
such that
\langle A\rangle\subseteq X
. This theorem implies that the set of small subsets of
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
forms an ideal - indeed, if
Y\cup Z\subseteq\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
is not small, say
\langle A\rangle\subseteq Y\cup Z
, then by the above theorem there exists some
B\leq A
such that
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y
or
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Y^{c}
. In the latter case,
\langle B\rangle\subseteq Z
. Consequently, it is reasonable to discuss almost disjoint families with respect to the ideal of small subsets of
\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
.

Definition 1.2
.

(1)

Two subsets
Y,Z\subseteq\mathbf{FIN}_{k}
are
almost disjoint
(or simply
ad
) if
Y\cap Z
is small.

(2)

Two infinite block sequences
A,B\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
are
almost disjoint
if
\langle A\rangle,\langle B\rangle
are almost disjoint.

(3)

A family
\cal{A}\subseteq\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
is an
almost disjoint family
if for all
A\neq B
in
\cal{A}
,
A
and
B
are almost disjoint. If
\cal{A}
is maximal under inclusion, then
\cal{A}
is called a
maximal almost disjoint family
, or simply a
mad family
.

For
k=1
,
A,B\in\mathbf{FIN}^{[\infty]}
are almost disjoint iff
\langle A\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle
are finite, so the uncountability of the cardinal
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{FIN}}
is immediate. This particular cardinal was studied in
[
1
]
, where it was shown that the inequality
\operatorname{non}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{FIN}}
. Consequently,
\mathfrak{a}<\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{FIN}}
in the random model.

The uncountability of the cardinal
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{FIN}_{k}}
is less apparent for
k\geq 2
. Unlike the case of
\mathbf{FIN}
, it is not true that if
A,B\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
are almost disjoint, then
\langle A\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle
is finite.

Example 1.3
.

Let
A=(x_{n})_{n<\omega}
and
B=(y_{n})_{n<\omega}
be defined as follows:

(1)

\operatorname{supp}(x_{0})=\operatorname{supp}(y_{0})=\{0\}
, and
x_{0}(0)=y_{0}(0)=2
.

(2)

For all
n>0
,
\operatorname{supp}(x_{n})=\{2n-1\}
and
x_{n}(2n-1)=2
.

(3)

For all
n>0
,
\operatorname{supp}(y_{n})=\{2n-1,2n\}
,
y_{n}(2n-1)=2
and
y_{n}(2n)=1
.

Observe that for all
n>0
,
T(x_{n})=T(y_{n})
. Therefore, for any
n
, we have that:

\displaystyle z_{n}:=x_{0}+T(x_{n})=y_{0}+T(y_{n})\in\langle A\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle,

and
\max(\operatorname{supp}(z))=2n-1
. Therefore,
\sup\{g(x):x\in\langle A\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle\}=\infty
. We shall show that
\langle A\rangle\cap\langle B/1\rangle=\emptyset
, so
A
and
B
are almost disjoint by Lemma
2.3
.

Suppose otherwise, and let
z\in\langle A\rangle\cap\langle B/1\rangle
. Then
z=\sum_{m\geq 1}T^{\mu_{m}}(y_{m})
, and
\mu_{m}=0
for some

. This implies that
2m\in\operatorname{supp}(y_{m})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(z)
. However, observe that
2m\notin\operatorname{supp}(A)
. Since

,
\operatorname{supp}(z)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(A)
so

, a contradiction.

The main theorem of this paper is thus the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

For all
k\geq 1
,
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbf{FIN}_{k}}\geq\aleph_{1}
.

Proof of Theorem

Fix some

(1)

For all

,

and
B
are almost disjoint iff
\sup\{f(x):x\in\langle A\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle\}<\infty
.

(2)

For all

such that

and

are almost disjoint, and

, there exists some

such that

, and:

\displaystyle\sup\{f(y):y\in\langle a^{\frown}x\rangle\cap\langle A\rangle\}=\sup\{f(y):y\in\langle a\rangle\cap\langle A\rangle\}.

Here, if

we denote

.

Lemma 2.1
.

If there exists a function
f:\mathbf{FIN}_{k}\to\mathbb{N}
satisfying the above two properties, then every infinite mad family
\cal{A}\subseteq\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
is uncountable.

Proof.

Let
\cal{A}:=\{A_{n}:n<\omega\}
be a countable almost disjoint family of infinite block sequences. We shall show that
\cal{A}
is not maximal by constructing some
B=(y_{n})_{n<\omega}
that is almost disjoint from
A_{n}
for all
n<\omega
inductively as follows: If
a:=(y_{i})_{i<n}
has been defined, then by property (2) of
f
. Let
M
be large enough so that for all

and

:

\displaystyle\sup\{f(y):y\in\langle a^{\frown}x\rangle\cap\langle A_{i}\rangle\}=\sup\{f(y):y\in\langle a\rangle\cap\langle A_{i}\rangle\}.

Let
y_{n}\in\langle A_{n}/M\rangle
be any block. Then, for all
n

\displaystyle\sup\{f(x):x\in\langle A_{n}\rangle\cap\langle B\rangle\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{i<\omega}\sup\{f(x):x\in\langle A_{n}\rangle\cap\langle y_{0},\dots,y_{i-1}\rangle\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{f(x):x\in\langle A_{n}\rangle\cap\langle y_{0},\dots,y_{n}\rangle\}

\displaystyle<\infty,

so by property (1) of

,

is almost disjoint from

for all

.
∎

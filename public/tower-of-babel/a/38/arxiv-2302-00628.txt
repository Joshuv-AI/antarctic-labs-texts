Training Normalizing Flows with the Precision-Recall Divergence
Alexandre Verine, Benjamin Negrevergne, Muni Sreenivas Pydi, Yann Chevaleyre

Abstract
Generative models can have distinct mode of failures like mode dropping and low quality samples, which cannot be captured by a single scalar metric. To address this, recent works propose evaluating generative models using precision and recall, where precision measures quality of samples and recall measures the coverage of the target distribution. Although a variety of discrepancy measures between the target and estimated distribution are used to train generative models, it is unclear what precision-recall trade-offs are achieved by various choices of the discrepancy measures. In this paper, we show that achieving a specified precision-recall trade-off corresponds to minimising f f -divergences from a family we call the PR-divergences . Conversely, any f f -divergence can be written as a linear combination of PR-divergences and therefore correspond to minimising a weighted precision-recall trade-off. Further, we propose a novel generative model that is able to train a normalizing flow to minimise any f f -divergence, and in particular, achieve a given precision-recall trade-off.

Introduction

Generative models have emerged as a powerful tool in machine learning. In recent years, a plethora of generative models have been proposed, such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Normalizing Flows, which have shown exceptional performance in learning complex, high-dimensional distributions on image and text data.
Despite the successes, evaluation of generative models remains a tricky problem. While approaches based on GANs often produce high fidelity images, they suffer from mode dropping (inability to produce data from all the modes of the true distribution) and poor likelihoods. Models based on normalizing flows are explicitly trained for achieving high likelihoods, but often produce low quality samples. To address these complexities, recent works have proposed a two-fold evaluation which considers both precision and recall
(
Sajjadi et al. 2018
;
Simon et al. 2019
)
.

Given a target distribution
P

\{P_{\theta}|\theta\in\Theta\}

\widehat{P}=P_{\theta^{*}}
that minimises a divergence or distance metric between
P
and
\widehat{P}

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}

\widehat{P}

f

(
Nowozin et al. 2016

or the Wasserstein distance
(

(
Minka 2005

Question 1
.

What precision-recall trade-off does minimising
D_{f}(P\|\widehat{P})
correspond to?

Intuitively, precision of a generative model measures the quality of the samples produced and recall measures how well the target distribution is covered. Depending on the application, it may be desirable to train a generative model for high precision (for example, image synthesis and data augmentation) or high recall (for example, density estimation and denoising). However existing generative modelling approaches do not afford such flexibility. Although several works exist on efficient evaluation of the precision-recall curves of existing models, there seems to be little work on approaches for training generative models that can target a specific precision-recall trade-off. This leads to the following question.

Related works

The task of improving Normalizing Flows by tackling the trade-off between the natural effect of mass covering of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
and a desired mode seeking result has been done in some previous work. Reject can be used to artificially improve the precision of a model
(
Stimper et al. 2022
;
Issenhuth et al. 2022
;
Tanielian et al. 2020
)
. But by only focusing on the loss, an approach of
Midgley et al. 2022b
trains a Normalizing Flow to minimise an
\alpha
-Divergence for
\alpha=2
, estimated with annealed importance sampling. While the work show the benefits from training with another
f
-divergence, it fixes the trade-off by fixing the
\alpha
. Moreover, their work requires to know the target distribution density
P
as they aim to train the Flow to be sampler.

To generalise the approach of training a flow on any
f
-divergence divergence in an unsupervised setting, our method relies on the work of
Nowozin et al. 2016
for GANs. This framework has been evaluated on Normalizing Flows by
Grover et al. 2018
. It has been shown that Flows can be trained with a discriminator and that adversarially trained model gain from a log-likelihood terms, however the authors restrain their evaluation to Wasserstein distance only and do not explore how the
f
-divergence affects the Flow.

Moreover, we make the link between
f
-divergences and Precision and Recall. Several works have been trying to evaluate the these values with clustering methods reduced dimension space
(
Sajjadi et al. 2018
;
Tanielian et al. 2020
;
Kynkäänniemi et al. 2019
)
. We use likelihood ratio estimation to compute the metrics like
(
Simon et al. 2019
)
, but we offer guaranties on the the quality of the estimation. Finally, while we formulate the precision as an
f
-divergence,
Djolonga et al. 2020
introduce a different evaluation framework based on Rényi divergences. All theses previous works only focus on the evaluation of the model but we build method to train models based on this metric.

Background

3.1
Preliminaries on Normalising Flows

Let
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a latent space. Let
Q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})
denote the standard normal distribution,
\mathcal{N}(0,\mathcal{I}_{d})
.
Given a target distribution
P\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, one seeks a
Normalizing Flow

(
Rezende & Mohamed 2016
)
i.e., a bijection
F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
such that
P(A)=Q(F^{-1}(A))
for any measurable
A\subseteq\mathcal{X}
. One can then use
F
to generate samples from
P
by applying
F^{-1}
to samples from
Q
, or compute the density
p(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{X}
using the change of variable formula,
p(\boldsymbol{x})=q(F(\boldsymbol{x}))|\det\mathrm{Jac}_{F}(\boldsymbol{x})|
, where
\det\mathrm{Jac}_{F}(\boldsymbol{x})
is determinant of Jacobian matrix of
F
at
\boldsymbol{x}
.

In practice,
F
is not known, and the goal is to find a mapping
F^{\prime}
that yields the best approximation

\widehat{P}_{F^{\prime}}
of
P
. To do so, we consider a class of invertible functions
\{F_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}
typically represented using invertible neural network (INN) architectures such as GLOW
(
Kingma & Dhariwal 2018
)
, RealNVP
(
Rezende & Mohamed 2016
)
or ResFlow
(
Behrmann et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2020
)
. We can then train the INN to minimise the KL-divergence
between
P
and
\widehat{P}_{F_{\theta}}
, or equivalently, by maximising the log-likelihood
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim P}\left[\log\hat{p}_{F_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{x})\right]
estimating using a dataset
D
of samples from
P
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\min_{\theta}\ &\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|\widehat{P}_{F_{\theta}})=\min_{\theta}\int_{\mathcal{X}}p(\boldsymbol{x})\log\frac{p(\boldsymbol{x})}{\hat{p}_{F_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{x})}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\\
&=H(P)-\max_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim P}\left[\log\hat{p}_{F_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{x})\right],\end{split}

(1)

where
H(P)
is the entropy of
P
. In the rest of this paper, we omit the dependence on
F_{\theta}
in
\widehat{P}_{F_{\theta}}
when it is clear from context.

In order to perform well in practice, the INN must satisfy several properties
(
Kobyzev et al. 2020
)
: the forward pass and the inverse pass must be efficient to compute ; computing the determinant of the Jacobian matrix must be tractable; finally,
\{F_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}
should be expressive enough to model
P
.
While a number of architectures have been introduced to solve the first two properties, expressivity remains a challenge because of the invertibility constraint. As a consequence
\widehat{P}\neq P
in most practical scenarios, and thus the divergence that is used to measure the distance between
P
and
\widehat{P}
has a critical impact on the resulting model.

3.2
f
-divergences

Following the work by
Nowozin et al. 2016
, we consider the family of
f
-divergences to measure the difference between the true distribution
P
and the estimated distribution
\widehat{P}
.
Given a convex lower semi-continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
f(1)=0
, the
f
-divergence between two probability distributions
P
and
\widehat{P}
is defined as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{f}(P\|\widehat{P})=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})f\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{x})}{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

(2)

Many well known statistical divergences such as the Kullback Leibler divergence (
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
), the reverse Kullback Leibler (
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}
) or the Total Variation (
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TV}}
) are
f
-divergences (see Table
1
).
D_{f}
also admits a dual variational form
(
Nguyen et al. 2009
)
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{f}(P\|\widehat{P})=\sup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[T(\boldsymbol{x})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{P}}\left[f^{*}(T(\boldsymbol{x}))\right]\right),

(3)

where
\mathcal{T}
is the set of measurable functions
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
and
f^{*}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the convex conjugate (or Fenchel transform) of
f
given by
f^{*}(t)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{tu-f(u)\right\}
.
We use
T^{\mathrm{opt}}\in\mathcal{T}
to denote the function that achieves the supremum in (
3
).

Defining,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{f,T}(P\|\widehat{P})=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim P}\left[T(\boldsymbol{x})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\widehat{P}}\left[f^{*}(T(\boldsymbol{x}))\right],

(4)

it is possible to train a generative model for
P
by solving
\min_{F}\max_{T}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{f,T}(P\|\widehat{P})
, where
F
and
T
are represented by a neural network
(
Nowozin et al. 2016
)
.

3.3
Precision-Recall curve for generative models

Typically, generative models are evaluated by a one-dimensional metric like the Inception Score
(
Salimans et al. 2016
)
or the Fréchet Inception Distance
(
Heusel et al. 2017
)
, which are unable to distinguish between the distinct failure modes of low precision (i.e. failure to produce quality samples) and low recall (i.e. failure to cover all modes of
P
). The following definition introduced by
Sajjadi et al. 2018
and later extended by
Simon et al. 2019
remedies this problem.

Definition 3.1

(PRD set, adapted from
Simon et al. 2019
)
.

For
P,\widehat{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, the Precision-Recall set
\mathrm{PRD}(P,\widehat{P})
is defined as the set of Precision-Recall pairs
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}~\times~\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
for which
P\geq\beta\mu
and
\widehat{P}\geq\alpha\mu
.
The precision-recall curve (or PR curve) is defined as
\partial\mathrm{PRD}(P,\widehat{P})=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathrm{PRD}(P,\widehat{P})\mid\nexists(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\mbox{ with }\alpha^{\prime}\geq\alpha\mbox{ and }\beta^{\prime}\geq\beta\}
.

Sajjadi et al. 2018
show that the PR curve is parametrised by
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup \{+\infty\}
as follows:

\begin{split}&\partial\mathrm{PRD}(P,\widehat{P})=\left\{\alpha_{\lambda}(P\|\widehat{P}),\beta_{\lambda}(P\|\widehat{P})~|~\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup \{+\infty\}\right\}\\
&\quad\mbox{with }\begin{cases}\alpha_{\lambda}(P\|\widehat{P})=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\min\left(\lambda p(\boldsymbol{x}),\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\\
\beta_{\lambda}(P\|\widehat{P})=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\min\left(p(\boldsymbol{x}),\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})/\lambda\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(5)

Note that
\beta_{\lambda}(P\| \widehat{P})=\alpha_{\lambda}(P\| \widehat{P})/\lambda
.
We call
\lambda
the
trade-off parameter
since it can be used to adjust the sensitivity to precision (or recall).
An illustration of the PR curve is given in Figure

for a target distribution
P
that is a mixture of two Gaussians, and two candidate models
\widehat{P}_{1}
and
\widehat{P}_{2}
.
We can see on Figure
2
that
\widehat{P}_{1}
offers better results for large values of
\lambda
(with high sensitivity to precision) whereas
\widehat{P}_{2}
offers better results for low values of
\lambda
(with high sensitivity to recall).

4
Precision and Recall trade-off as a
f
-divergence

In this section, we formalise the link between precision-recall trade-off and
f
-divergences, and address Question
1
. We will exploit this link in Section
5
to train models that optimise a particular trade-off between precision and recall.

4.1
Precision-Recall as an
f
-divergence

We start by introducing the
PR-Divergence
as follows.

Definition 4.1

(PR-divergence)
.

Given a trade-off parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup+\infty
, the PR-divergence (denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
) is defined as the
f_{\lambda}
-divergence for
f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

f_{\lambda}:u\mapsto\max(\lambda u,1)-\max(\lambda,1),

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
f_{\lambda}:u\mapsto 0
for
\lambda=+\infty
.

Note that
f_{\lambda}
is continuous, convex, and satisfies
f_{\lambda}(1)=0
for all
\lambda
.
A graphical representation of
f_{\lambda}
can be found in Appendix
A.2
.
The following proposition gives some properties of
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
.

Proposition 4.2

(Properties of the PR-Divergence)
.

•

The Fenchel conjugate

of

is defined on
\mathrm{dom}\left(f_{\lambda}^{*}\right)=\left[0,\lambda\right]
and given by,

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}^{*}\left(t\right)=\begin{cases}t/\lambda&\mbox{ if }\,\lambda\leq 1,\\
t/\lambda+\lambda-1&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(6)

•

The discriminator
T^{\mathrm{opt}}
that achieves the supremum in the variational representation of
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P})
is

\displaystyle T^{\mathrm{opt}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\lambda\mathrm{sign}\left\{\frac{p(\boldsymbol{x})}{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}-1\right\}.

(7)

•

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(\widehat{P}\|P)=\lambda\mathcal{D}_{\frac{1}{\lambda}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P})
.

•

\mathcal{D}_{1{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P})=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P\|\widehat{P})/2
.

Having defined the PR-divergence, we can now show that precision and recall w.r.t
\lambda
can be expressed as a function of the divergence between
P
and
\widehat{P}
.

Theorem 4.3

(Precision and recall as a function of
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
)
.

Given
P,\widehat{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
, the PR curve
\partial\mathrm{PRD}(P,\widehat{P})
is related to the PR-divergence
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P})
as follows.

\displaystyle\alpha_{\lambda}(P\| \widehat{P})

\displaystyle=\min(1,\lambda)-\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P}).

(8)

A direct consequence of Theorem
4.3
is that we can now train the parameters of a model
F_{\theta}
to maximise precision and recall by minimising the corresponding PR-divergence.

\displaystyle\argmax_{\theta}~\alpha_{\lambda}(P\|\widehat{P}_{F_{\theta}})=\argmin_{\theta}~\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P}_{F_{\theta}}).

(9)

In other terms, training a model to minimise
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
means that the model will specifically focus the set trade-off. One might wonder what is the trade-off, ie the function
a_{\lambda}
minimised when the model is trained on the
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
.

4.2
Relation between PR-divergences and other
f
-divergences

In the previous subsection, we showed that for each trade-off parameter
\lambda
, there exists an
f
-divergence that corresponds to optimising for it. This raises the converse question of what trade-off is achieved by optimizing for an arbitrary
f
-divergence. We answer this by showing in the following theorem that any
f
-divergence can be expressed as a weighted sum of PR-divergences.

Theorem 4.4

(
f
-divergence as weighted sums of PR-divergences)
.

For any
P,\widehat{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
supported on all of
\mathcal{X}
and any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

\mathcal{D}_{f}(P\|\widehat{P})=\int^{M}_{m}\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P})\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(10)

where
m=\min_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\frac{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}{p(\boldsymbol{x})}\right)
and
M=\max_{\mathcal{X}}\left(\frac{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}{p(\boldsymbol{x})}\right)
.

Combining Theorem
4.4
with Theorem
4.3
we have the following relation that captures the implicit precision-recall trade-off made by minimising an arbitrary
f
-divergence.

\displaystyle\argmin_{\theta}\mathcal{D}_{f}(P\|\widehat{P})=\argmax_{\theta}\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\alpha_{\lambda}(P\|\widehat{P})\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(11)

In the following corollary, we apply Theorem
4.4
to compare
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}
.

Corollary 4.5

(
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}
as an average of
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
)
.

The
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
Divergence and the
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}
can be written as weighted average of PR-Divergence
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|\widehat{P})=\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P})\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}(P\|\widehat{P})=\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{409564}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{359028}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P})\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(13)

As we can see in this Corollary, both
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}
can be decomposed into a sum of PR-divergences terms
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
, each weighted with
1/\lambda^{2}
and
1\lambda
respectively.
Hence
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
gives more weight to small
\lambda
(more sensitive to precision) than
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}
.
This explains the
mode covering
behaviour observed in Normalizing Flows trained with
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
. Comparatively, the
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}
assigns more weight to terms with a large lambda and less weight to the terms with a small lambda, leading to the
mode covering
behaviour empirically observed with flows trained with the
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rKL}}

(
Midgley et al. 2022a
)
.

5
Optimising for specific Precision-Recall trade-offs

In this section, we address Question
2
by proposing a model that achieves a specified precision-recall trade-off. In light of Theorem
4.3
, a natural approach would be to use the framework of
Nowozin et al. 2016
and train an
f_{\lambda}
-GAN to minimize the dual variational form of
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
for any given
\lambda
, as follows.

\displaystyle\min_{F}\max_{T}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{f_{\lambda},T}(P\|\widehat{P})

\displaystyle=\min_{F}\max_{T}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim P}\left[T(\boldsymbol{x})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim\widehat{P}}\left[f^{*}_{\lambda}(T(\boldsymbol{x}))\right],

where both
F
and
T
are parametrized by neural networks.
As noted in
Nowozin et al. 2016
, we need to use an output activation function
\sigma_{\lambda}
on
T(x)
so that
\sigma(T(x))\in dom(f_{\lambda}^{*})
. From Proposition
4.2
, we have that the domain of
f^{*}_{\lambda}
is bounded (equal to
[0,\lambda]
) unlike most other popular
f
-divergences including
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}
and
rKL
,
\chi^{2}
, Jensen-Shannon and Hellinger, except for the case of Total Variation. Due to this, we are forced to choose an output activation function like the sigmoid that suffers from vanishing gradients. Because of this, we find that
f_{\lambda}
-GAN is notoriously hard to train and performs poorly, not unlike the case of training
f
-GAN with the Total Variation metric.

We overcome this problem by using the primal form of
f
-divergence shown in (
2
) to minimise
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
rather than the dual variational form in (
3
), as explained in the following subsection.

5.1
Minimising the primal estimation of
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}

Figure 3:
Model architecture for training a Normalising Flow
F
that minimises
\mathcal{D}_{g}
using a discriminator
T
that minimises
\mathcal{D}_{f}
.

As explained before, using the the
f
-GAN approach to minimise the dual variational form of
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
is not a feasible approach.
In this subsection, we introduce a novel approach to instead train a Normalizing Flow to minimise
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}
.
We do this by using the primal form of
f
-divergence, given by
\mathcal{D}_{f}(P\|\widehat{P})=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})f\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{x})}{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
. Computing
D_{f}
with the primal form requires the likelihood ratio
p(x)/\widehat{p}(x)
. For this, we use the
T^{\mathrm{opt}}
from the dual form of
D_{f}
. From the dual form, we have the following.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{f}(P\|\widehat{P})=\sup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left(p(x)T(x)-\widehat{P}(x)f^{*}(T(x))\right)dx.

Differentiating the expression inside the integral with respect to
T(x)
, we get the following.

\displaystyle\frac{p(x)}{\widehat{p}(x)}=\nabla f^{*}(T^{\mathrm{opt}}(x)).

(14)

We use the preceding equation to propose the following definition for a
primal estimate
of
f
-divergence.

Definition 5.1

(Primal estimate
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{primal}}_{g,T}
)
.

Let
P,\widehat{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
.
For functions
T:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
and
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the primal estimate
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{primal}}_{g,T}
of the divergence
\mathcal{D}_{g}
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{primal}}_{g,T}(P\|\widehat{P})=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})g\left(r(\boldsymbol{x})\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

(15)

where
r:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is given by,
r(\boldsymbol{x})=\nabla f^{*}(T(\boldsymbol{x}))
.

Crucially, when
T^{\mathrm{opt}}
is as given in (
14
), we have
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{primal}}_{g,T}=\mathcal{D}_{g}
.
Also note that
r(x)
acts as the estimate for the likelihood
p(x)/\widehat{p}(x)
.
With this, we propose the following scheme for training a Normalizing Flow
F
that minimises
\mathcal{D}_{g}
by using a discriminator
T
that minimises
\mathcal{D}_{f}
.

•

The discriminator
T
is trained to minimise
\mathcal{D}_{f}
with the dual variational form.

\displaystyle\argmin_{T}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{f,T}(P\|\widehat{P})

\displaystyle=\argmin_{T}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[T(\boldsymbol{x})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{P}}\left[f^{*}(T(\boldsymbol{x}))\right].

•

The generator, a Normalizing Flow
F
, is trained to maximise
\mathcal{D}_{g}
with the primal form.

\displaystyle\argmin_{T}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{primal}}_{g,T}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{T}\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{P}}\left[g(\nabla f^{*}(T(\boldsymbol{x})))\right].

Note that for
T=T^{\mathrm{opt}}
, we have
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{dual}}_{f,T}(P\|\widehat{P})=\mathcal{D}_{f}
, and consequently,
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{primal}}_{g,T}=\mathcal{D}_{g}
.
Figure

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}

6
Practical considerations

6.1
A new approach to compute
\partial\mathrm{PRD}(P,\widehat{P})

To evaluate our model, the frontier
\partial\mathrm{PRD}(P,\widehat{P})
must be computed. The original work of
Sajjadi et al. 2018
proposes a clustering method to estimate every point
\left(\alpha(\lambda),\beta(\lambda)\right)
but was proven to fail estimate packed data points
(
Kynkäänniemi et al. 2019
)
. A more recent approach
(
Simon et al. 2019
)
proposed to train a binary classifier to make the difference between points from
P
and
\widehat{P}
and then use false positive and false negative ratios to estimate the frontier. Since we have proven that the frontier can be estimated by the primal of

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we present a method for training Normalizing Flows using a new Precision/Recall (PR) divergence within the framework of f-divergences. Our approach offers a unique advantage over other divergences as it allows for explicit control of the precision-recall trade-off in generative models. The PR-divergence results in models that range from extreme mode seeking (high precision) to extreme weight covering (high recall), as well as more balanced models that may be more suitable for various applications. Our framework also provides new insights into other well-known divergences, such as the KL and reverse KL. Our experiments indicate that models trained for high precision (using the reverse KL) often have high precision but poor recall. Given that detecting low recall in a model is more difficult than detecting low precision through visual inspection of model samples, we suspect that existing evaluation methodologies have overemphasised high precision models. As such, we hope that this research will ultimately lead to the development of better tools for evaluating the overall quality of generative models.

\displaystyle\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)f^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\lambda}(u)\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\max(\lambda u,1)\mathrm{d}\lambda-\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\max\left(\lambda,1\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda

(40)

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\max(\lambda u,1)\mathrm{d}\lambda+\int_{1/u}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\max(\lambda u,1)\mathrm{d}\lambda\\
-\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\max\left(\lambda,1\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda-\int_{1}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\max\left(\lambda,1\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda+\int_{1/u}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\lambda u\mathrm{d}\lambda-\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda-\int_{1}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(43)

By integrating by parts, we have:
\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda=\left[c^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda\right]_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}-\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda
so it satisfies:

\displaystyle\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)f^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\lambda}(u)\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda+u\left[c^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda\right]_{1/u}^{1/u_{\min}}-u\int_{1/u}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda\\
-\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda-\left[c^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda\right]_{1}^{1/u_{\min}}+\int_{1}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\left[c^{\prime}(\lambda)\right]_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u}+u\left[c^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda\right]_{1/u}^{1/u_{\min}}-u\left[c(\lambda)\right]_{1/u}^{1/u_{\min}}\\
-\left[c^{\prime}(\lambda)\right]_{1/u_{\max}}^{1}-\left[c^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda\right]_{1}^{1/u_{\min}}+\left[c(\lambda)\right]_{1}^{1/u_{\min}}\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}c^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)-c^{\prime}(0)+uc^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u_{\min}}\right)-uc^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)\frac{1}{u}-uc\left(\frac{1}{u_{\min}}\right)+uc\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)-c^{\prime}(1)\\
+c^{\prime}(0)-c^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u_{\min}}\right)\frac{1}{u_{\min}}+c^{\prime}(1)\times 1+c\left(\frac{1}{u_{\min}}\right)-c(1)\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\left[c^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u_{\min}}\right)\frac{1}{u_{\min}}-c\left(\frac{1}{u_{\min}}\right)\right]\left(u-1\right)+uc\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)-c(1).

(50)

We would like
\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)f^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\lambda}(u)\mathrm{d}\lambda
to be equal to
f
between
u_{\min}
and
u_{\max}
. But since two
f
-divergences generated by
f
and
g
are equals if there is a
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f(u)=g(u)+c(u-1)
, the Divergence generated by
\int_{1/u_{\max}}^{1/u_{\min}}c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)f^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\lambda}(u)\mathrm{d}\lambda
is equal to the divergence generated by
uc\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)-c(1)
. Therefore, we require
c
to satisfy:

\displaystyle\forall u\in[u_{\min},u_{\max}],\quad f(u)=uc\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)-c(1).

By differentiating with respect to
u
, we have:

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(u)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\left[c^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda-c(\lambda)\right]+c\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)-\frac{1}{u}c^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u}\right).

(51)

And finally:

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{u^{2}}c\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)+\frac{1}{u^{2}}c^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)+\frac{1}{u^{3}}c^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)

(52)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u^{3}}c^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{u}\right).

(53)

Consequently, with
\lambda=1/u
, we have that:

\displaystyle c^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right).

(54)

With such a results, with
m=\min_{\mathcal{X}}(\frac{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}{p(\boldsymbol{x})})
and
M=\max_{\mathcal{X}}(\frac{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}{p(\boldsymbol{x})})
, we can write any
f
-divergence as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{f}(P\|\widehat{P})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})f\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{x})}{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)f^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{x})}{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{m}^{M}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})f^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{x})}{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\left[\int_{\mathcal{X}}\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})f^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{x})}{\widehat{p}(\boldsymbol{x})}\right)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\right]\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{m}^{M}\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\mathcal{D}_{\lambda{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{401053}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354083}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{354082}{138261}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mbox{-}\mathrm{PR}$}}}}}}}(P\|\widehat{P})\mathrm{d}\lambda

6.1

The AUC can be computed by integrating with respect to an angle
\theta
on the quadrant:

\displaystyle\mathrm{AUC}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}r^{2}(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta.

(57)

Therefore with
\lambda=\tan\theta
, we have
r(\theta)=\alpha_{\tan(\theta)}(P\|\widehat{P})/\cos\theta
. Thus:

\displaystyle\mathrm{AUC}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\alpha_{\tan(\theta)}(P\|\widehat{P})^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta

(58)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{\lambda}(P\|\widehat{P})^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\lambda\quad\mbox{with }\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\cos^{2}\theta}=\partial\lambda.

(59)

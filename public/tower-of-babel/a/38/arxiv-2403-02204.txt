On ν \nu faces of partial alternating sign matrix polytopes
Dylan Heuer, Sara Solhjem and Jessica Striker

Abstract
. We define and study the ( ν / λ ) (\nu/\lambda) -partial alternating sign matrix polytope, motivated by connections to the Chan-Robbins-Yuen polytope and the ν \nu -Tamari lattice. We determine the inequality description and show this polytope is a face of the partial alternating sign matrix polytope of [Heuer, Striker 2022]. We show that the ( ν / λ ) (\nu/\lambda) -partial ASM polytope is an order polytope and a flow polytope.

05A05, 52B05

1.
Introduction and background

The ASM-CRY polytope is an alternating sign matrix analogue of the CRY polytope: a face of the Birkhoff polytope studied by Chan, Robbins, and Yuen
[
3
]
whose volume is given as a product of Catalan numbers
[
19
]
.
It was shown in
[
9
]
that the ASM-CRY polytope is integrally equivalent to an order polytope, which provided a proof of its lovely combinatorial properties, such as counting formulas for its volume and vertices. More generally,
[
9
]
showed that order polytopes of strongly planar posets are flow polytopes. Subsequent work
[
2
,
8
,
7
,
5
,
17
]
built upon this connection between order and flow polytopes, but there was no interpretation in terms of alternating sign matrices.
In this paper, we reunite these topics.
In particular, we were inspired by the polytope perspective on the
\nu
-Tamari lattice in
[
17
]
to define and study an analogue of the ASM-CRY polytope of
[
9
]
using the partial alternating sign matrix polytope of
[
4
]
. Furthermore, we show this polytope is both an order polytope and a flow polytope.

We first give the definition of alternating sign matrices and discuss relevant prior work on the ASM-CRY polytope; we then describe our main results.

Definition 1.1
.

An
alternating sign matrix
is an
n\times n
matrix
M=\left(M_{ij}\right)
with entries in
\left\{-1,0,1\right\}
such that:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}M_{i^{\prime}j}

\displaystyle\in\left\{0,1\right\},

\displaystyle\mbox{ for all }1\leq i,j\leq n

(1.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}M_{ij^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\in\left\{0,1\right\},

\displaystyle\mbox{ for all }1\leq i,j\leq n

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n}M_{i^{\prime}j}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n}M_{ij^{\prime}}=1.

We denote the set of all
n\times n
alternating sign matrices as
\mathrm{ASM}_{n}
. Define the
alternating sign matrix polytope

\mathrm{ASM}(n)
as the convex hull of these matrices.

Alternating sign matrices arose in the study of the lambda-determinant
[
13
]
. They have a nice enumeration formula
[
10
,
18
,
6
]
and connections to statistical physics
[
6
]
.
The alternating sign matrix polytope was defined as above by Striker in
[
16
]
, where she also found an inequality description, enumerated the facets, and studied its projection to the permutohedron. Independently, Behrend and Knight defined this polytope in terms of inequalities and proved the vertex description
[
1
]
. In addition, they studied lattice points in the
r
th-dilate of the alternating sign matrix
polytope, which they called
higher spin
alternating sign matrices.

In
[
9
]
, a face of the alternating sign matrix polytope with some prescribed zeros in certain parts of the matrix was studied and shown to be integrally equivalent to an order polytope. We give the definition of this polytope and state the relevant theorems below. (Note our convention differs from that of
[
9
]
by vertical reflection of the matrix.)

Let
\delta_{n}=(n-1,n-2,\ldots,2,1)
be the staircase partition and consider the partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})\subseteq\delta_{n}
as the positions
(i,j)
of an
n\times n
matrix given by
\{(i,j)\ |\ 1\leq i\leq\ell,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Define the
\lambda
-ASMCRY polytope

\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n):=\{(a_{ij})^{n}_{i,j=1}\in\mathrm{ASM}(n)\ |\ a_{ij}=0\text{ for }i+j\geq n+3\text{ or }(i,j)\in\lambda
}.

The main results about this polytope from
[
9
]
are the following; see Section
5
for definitions of the notation.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

The polytope
\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n)
is integrally equivalent to the order polytope
\mathcal{O}(P(\delta_{n}/\lambda))

and the flow polytope
\mathcal{F}_{G_{P(\delta_{n}/\lambda)}}
.

Corollary 1.4

(
[
9
, Corollaries 5.7 and 1.2]
)
.

The normalized volume of
\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n)
is given by the number of linear extensions

e(P(\delta_{n}/\lambda))
. Its Ehrhart polynomial
L_{\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n)}(t)
equals the order polynomial

\Omega(P(\delta_{n}/\lambda),t+1)
.

Moreover,
\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\emptyset,n)
has
\mathrm{Cat}(n)=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
vertices, its normalized volume is given by the number of standard Young tableaux of shape
\delta_{n}
, and its Ehrhart polynomial is
L_{\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\emptyset,n)}(t)=\Omega(P(\delta_{n}),t+1)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{2t+i+j-1}{i+j-1}
.

The partial alternating sign matrix polytope
\mathrm{PASM}(m,n)
was introduced and studied in
[
4
]
. This polytope is defined as the convex hull of
partial alternating sign matrices
, a natural extension of alternating sign matrices, whose definition we give in Definition
2.1
. The authors of
[
4
]
proved an inequality description of the partial alternating sign matrix polytope. They also enumerated the facets and characterized the face lattice, in an analogous way to that of the alternating sign matrix polytope.

In this paper, we study polytopes
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
(see Definition
2.6
) contained in partial alternating sign matrix polytopes, with certain entries set equal to
0
, analogous to the ASM-CRY polytope of Definition
1.2
. We find similar connections to order and flow polytopes and the results of
[
9
]
discussed above. Our theorems are not straightforward generalizations of results of either
[
4
]
or
[
9
]
. For instance, our proof of Theorem
3.2
involves a novel induction. Also, we show in Proposition
5.11
that when restricted to square matrices and the appropriate partition shapes,
\mathrm{PASM}(\delta_{n}/\lambda,n,n)
is not equal to
\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n)
, but the polytopes are integrally equivalent.

Our first main result appears in Section
3
, where we prove the following inequality description.

Theorem
3.2
.

Let
\lambda,\nu
be partitions inside the partition
n^{m}
such that
\lambda\subseteq\nu
. Then
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is given by the inequalities:

\displaystyle 0\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}X_{i^{\prime}j}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n

\displaystyle 0\leq\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}X_{ij^{\prime}}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i1}=1

\displaystyle\text{ and }\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{1j}=1

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{ij}=0

\displaystyle\text{ for }2\leq i\leq m\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{m}{1\leq i\leq m}X_{ij}=0\text{ for }2\leq j\leq n

\displaystyle X_{ij}=0

\displaystyle\text{ if }j\leq\lambda_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle X_{ij}=0

\displaystyle\text{ if }\nu_{i-1}<j\leq n\text{ for }i>1,\text{ and }X_{ij}=0\text{ if }\nu_{1}+1<j\leq n.

Then, in Section
4
, we show that
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is a face of
\mathrm{PASM}(m,n)
.

Theorem
4.5
.

Let
\lambda\subseteq\nu\subseteq(n-1)^{m-1}
. Then
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is a face of
\mathrm{PASM}(m,n)
of dimension
|\nu|-|\lambda|
.

Our final main result, in Section
5
, shows that
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is integrally equivalent to an order polytope of a strongly planar graph (and thus a flow polytope, see Corollary
5.10
).

Theorem
5.4
.

The
(\nu/\lambda)
-partial ASM polytope
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is integrally equivalent to the order polytope
\mathcal{O}(P(\nu/\lambda))
.

This leads to the following result on the volume and Ehrhart polynomial of
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
. Note for a poset
P
,
\Omega(P,t)
denotes the number of order-preserving maps
\eta:P\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,t\}
.

Corollary
5.7
.

The normalized volume of
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is
e(P(\nu/\lambda))
and its Ehrhart polynomial is
L_{\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)}(t)=\Omega(P(\nu/\lambda),t+1)
.
In particular,
e(P(\nu/\lambda))
is the number of skew standard Young tableaux, enumerated by the Naruse hook-length formula

e(P(\nu/\lambda))=|\nu/\lambda|!\sum_{D\in\mathcal{E}(\nu/\lambda)}\prod_{u\in[\nu]\setminus D}\frac{1}{h(u)},

where
\mathcal{E}(\nu/\lambda)
is the set of excited diagrams of
\nu/\lambda
and
h(u)
is the hook number of a box
u
.

2.
Definition of the
(\nu/\lambda)
-partial ASM polytope

In this section, we define matrices associated to partitions and use them to define the polytope
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
studied in this paper. We also prove Lemma
2.4
, which is used in the next section in proving the inequality description of this polytope.

A
partition

\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots)
is an infinite sequence of integers satisfying
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{\ell}>\mu_{\ell+1}=0
for some
\ell>0
. Call
\ell
the
length
of the partition.

Definition 2.1

(
[
4
, Def. 2.5]
)
.

An
m\times n

partial alternating sign matrix
is an
m\times n
matrix
M=\left(M_{ij}\right)
with entries in
\left\{-1,0,1\right\}
such that:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}M_{i^{\prime}j}

\displaystyle\in\left\{0,1\right\},

\displaystyle\mbox{ for all }1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n.

(2.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}M_{ij^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\in\left\{0,1\right\},

\displaystyle\mbox{ for all }1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n.

We denote the set of all
m\times n
partial alternating sign matrices as
\mathrm{PASM}_{m,n}
.

The polytope determined as the convex hull of these matrices is defined below. See
[
4
]
for many more facts about this polytope, including its inequality description, vertices, facet count, and face lattice.

Definition 2.2

(
[
4
, Def. 4.1]
)
.

Define the
(m,n)
-partial alternating sign matrix polytope
\mathrm{PASM}(m,n)
to be the convex hull (as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{mn}
) of all matrices in
\mathrm{PASM}_{m,n}
.

Given a partition contained in the rectangular partition
n^{m}
, we specify a partial sum matrix. We then determine the row and column sums of all such matrices.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
\mu
is a partition with
\ell<m
positive parts and
\mu_{1}<n
.
Let
M^{\mu}(m,n)
be the
m\times n
matrix defined entrywise as below, where
k
satisfies
1\leq k\leq m-1
:

(2.3a)

\displaystyle M^{\mu}_{ij}(m,n)=

\displaystyle 1

if

(i,j)=(1,\mu_{1}+1)

(2.3b)

\displaystyle M^{\mu}_{ij}(m,n)=

\displaystyle 1

if

(i,j)=(k+1,\mu_{k+1}+1)

and

\mu_{k}>\mu_{k+1}

(2.3c)

\displaystyle M^{\mu}_{ij}(m,n)=

\displaystyle-1

if

(i,j)=(k+1,\mu_{k}+1)

and

\mu_{k}>\mu_{k+1}

(2.3d)

\displaystyle M^{\mu}_{ij}(m,n)=

\displaystyle 0

otherwise.

When
m,n
are understood, we use the notation
M^{\mu}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
m,n>0
. For any partition
\mu\subseteq n^{m}
,
M^{\mu}(m,n)
is a partial alternating sign matrix with first row sum
1
, first column sum
1
and all other row and column sums
0
.

Proof.

By construction,
M^{\mu}(m,n)
is an
m\times n
matrix with entries in
\{-1,0,1\}
. We need to show (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).

Also by construction, there is at most one
1
in any row or column, so the partial sums cannot exceed
1
. Furthermore, any
-1
, of which there is at most one in any row or column, appears to the right of a
1
in the same row and below another
1
in the same column. We justify this as follows.

Consider row
1
of
M^{\mu}
. Since (
2.3a
) is the only case that applies, row
1
has at most one
1
and no
-1
. This satisfies (
2.2
) for
i=1
. Suppose
\mu_{k}=\mu_{k+1}
, then all entries of row
k+1
in
M^{\mu}
are
0
, satisfying (
2.2
).
Suppose
\mu_{k}>\mu_{k+1}
. Then by (
2.3b
),
M^{\mu}_{k+1,\mu_{k+1}+1}=1
, by (
2.3c
)
M^{\mu}_{k+1,\mu_{k}+1}=-1
if
\mu_{k}<n
, and all other entries of row
k+1
equal
0
. Thus, row
k+1
consists of a
1
that has a
-1
somewhere later in its row and all other entries equal to
0
; this satisfies (
2.2
) for
i=k+1
.

We now show (
2.1
) holds. Consider column
1
of
M^{\mu}
. The only case that applies to column
1
is (
2.3b
) when
k=\ell
, thus column
1
has exactly one
1
and no
-1
. This satisfies (
2.1
) for
j=1
. For column
j>1
, suppose
M^{\mu}_{ij}=-1
. Then by (
2.3c
),
i=k+1
and
j=\mu_{k}+1
for some
k
such that
\mu_{k}>\mu_{k+1}
. Suppose
p
is the largest integer less than
k
such that
\mu_{p}>\mu_{k}
. So
\mu_{p+1}=\mu_{k}
. So by (
2.3b
),
M^{\mu}_{p+1,\mu_{p+1}+1}=M^{\mu}_{p+1,\mu_{k}+1}=1
, so the
-1
is below a
1
and all other entries of column
\mu_{k}+1
equal
0
. This satisfies (
2.1
) for
j>1
.

This means that a
1
is always “before” a
-1
so the partial sums are never negative.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\lambda,\nu
be partitions inside the partition
(n-1)^{m-1}
such that
\lambda\subseteq\nu
. Then
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is given by the inequalities:

•

0
and

:

(3.1)

0\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}X_{i^{\prime}j}\leq 1,\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n.

(3.2)

0\leq\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}X_{ij^{\prime}}\leq 1,\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n.

•

First column and first row sums are
1
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i1}=1\text{ and }\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{1j}=1

•

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{ij}=0\text{ for }2\leq i\leq m\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{ij}=0\text{ for }2\leq j\leq n

•

The entries of

:

(3.5)

X_{ij}=0\text{ if }j\leq\lambda_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n

•

The entries southeast of the border strip associated to
\nu
are fixed to be
0
:

(3.6)

X_{ij}=0\text{ if }\nu_{i-1}<j\leq n\text{ for }i>1,\text{ and }X_{ij}=0\text{ if }\nu_{1}+1<j\leq n

Proof.

Fix
m,n>0
and partitions
\lambda\subseteq\nu\subset(n-1)^{m-1}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
be the polytope with inequality description given by
(\ref{pasmeq2})-(\ref{pasmeq7})
. We wish to show
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is contained in
\mathcal{P}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
. Remark
2.7
notes that
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is contained in
\mathrm{PASM}(m,n)
. By
[
4
, Theorem 4.6]
, the polytope
\mathrm{PASM}(m,n)
satisfies (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Let
X\in\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
. Then
X
is a convex combination
X=\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}M^{\mu}
of matrices
M^{\mu}
with
c_{\mu}>0
and
\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}=1
.

Each
M^{\mu}
has first row sum
1
, first column sum
1
, and all other row or column sums
0
by Lemma
2.4
.
Then

\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{1j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}M^{\mu}_{1j}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}\sum_{j=1}^{m}M^{\mu}_{1j}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}\cdot 1=1

and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}M^{\mu}_{i1}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}M^{\mu}_{i1}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}\cdot 1=1.

So (
3.3
) is satisfied.

Similarly, if
1<k\leq m
,

\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{kj}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}M^{\mu}_{kj}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}\sum_{j=1}^{m}M^{\mu}_{kj}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}\cdot 0=0

and if
1<k\leq n
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ik}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}M^{\mu}_{ik}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}M^{\mu}_{ik}=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\nu}c_{\mu}\cdot 0=0.

So (
3.4
) is satisfied.

Since
\lambda\subseteq\mu
, and the
1
’s and
-1
’s in
M^{\mu}
have been placed outside of
\mu
, all entries in
\lambda
are equal to
0
. So (
3.5
) is satisfied. Also, since

and all

’s and
-1
’s in

have been placed at most one box south and/or east of
\mu
, all entries southeast of the border strip associated to
\nu
are equal to
0
. So (
3.6
) is satisfied.

Now we wish to show that

is contained in

. Suppose that

. By (3.1) and (3.2),

. So we can write it as the convex combination of some partial ASMs:

with

and
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{w}c_{k}=1
.

Suppose one of these partial alternating sign matrices does not satisfy the first equality in (
3.3
). Without loss of generality, say it is
M^{1}
. Since it is a partial alternating sign matrix that does not have a first column total sum of 1, then it must have a first column sum of 0.
So

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i1}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{w}c_{k}M^{k}_{i1}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{w}c_{k}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}M^{k}_{i1}=c_{1}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}M^{1}_{i1}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{w}c_{k}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}M^{k}_{i1}=c_{1}\cdot 0+\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{w}c_{k}\cdot 1<1.

This is a contradiction. We can do a similar proof for the second equality in (
3.3
). So each
M^{k}
satisfies (
3.3
).

Now suppose one of these partial alternating sign matrices does not satisfy the first equality in (
3.4
) for a fixed value of

with
2\leq i\leq m
. Without loss of generality, say this matrix is
M^{1}
. Since this is a partial alternating sign matrix that does not have an
i
th row total sum of 0, then it must have an
i
th row total sum of 1.
So

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{ij}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{w}c_{k}M^{k}_{ij}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{w}c_{k}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}M^{k}_{ij}=c_{1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}M^{1}_{ij}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{w}c_{k}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}M^{k}_{ij}=c_{1}\cdot 1+\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{w}c_{k}\cdot 0=c_{1}>0.

This is a contradiction. We can do a similar proof for the second equality in (
3.4
). So each
M^{k}
satisfies (
3.4
).

Now suppose one of these partial alternating sign matrices, call it
M^{1}
, does not satisfy the (
3.5
) for some
j<\lambda_{i}
. Suppose this is the smallest such value of
j
with this
i
. So this is a partial ASM with
i
th row partial sum
\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}M^{1}_{ij}=1
but
\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j-1}M^{k}_{ij}=0
for all

. But since

satisfies (
3.5
),
\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}X_{ij}=0
.
So

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}X_{ij}=\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{w}c_{k}M^{k}_{ij}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{w}c_{k}\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}M^{k}_{ij}=c_{1}\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}M^{1}_{ij}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{w}c_{k}\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}M^{k}_{ij}=c_{1}\cdot 1+\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{w}c_{k}\cdot 0=c_{1}>0.

This is a contradiction. So each
M^{k}
satisfies (
3.5
). We can do a similar proof for (
3.6

Thus we have shown both

and

∎

By Stanley’s theory of order polytopes

Definition 5.6
.

of

.

A

of a poset

is an order-preserving bijection
\eta:P\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Let

denote the number of linear extensions of

.

.

The normalized volume of

.
∎

be a connected graph on the vertex set
\left[k\right]:=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
with edges directed from smaller to larger vertex. Denote by
\text{in}(e)
the initial vertex of edge
e
and
\text{fin}(e)
the final vertex of edge
e
.

A
flow

fl
of size one on
G

is a function
fl:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E,\text{in}(e)=1}fl(e)=\sum_{e\in E,\text{fin}(e)=k}fl(e)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E,\text{fin}(e)=i}fl(e)=\sum_{e\in E,\text{in}(e)=i}fl(e)

\displaystyle\text{ for }2\leq i\leq k-1.

The
flow polytope

\mathcal{F}_{G}
associated to the graph
G
is the set of all flows
fl:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
of size one on
G
.

Corollary 5.10
.

The
(\lambda,n)
-partial ASM polytope
\mathrm{PASM}(\nu/\lambda,m,n)
is integrally equivalent to the flow polytope
\mathcal{F}_{G_{P(\nu/\lambda)}}

Proof.

Theorem 5.11
.

Given
\lambda\subseteq(n-1,n-2,\ldots,2,1)\subset(n-1)^{n-1}
, an explicit integral equivalence
\varphi
from
\mathrm{PASM}(\delta_{n}/\lambda,n,n)
to
\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n)
is given as follows. For each
M\in\mathrm{PASM}(\delta_{n}/\lambda,n,n)
, define

\varphi(M)=\begin{cases}M_{ij}+1&\text{if }j=n-i+2\\
M_{ij}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By Theorem
5.4
and
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
, respectively, we have that
\mathrm{PASM}(\delta_{n}/\lambda,n,n)
and
\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n)
are each integrally equivalent to the order polytope
\mathcal{O}(P(\delta_{n}/\lambda))
. The map
\varphi
given above is a translation that takes vertices to vertices, as shown below, thus it is an integral equivalence between these two polytopes.
The reason it takes vertices to vertices is as follows. A vertex in
\mathrm{PASM}(\delta_{n}/\lambda,n,n)
is a matrix
M
with first row and column sums of 1 and the others
0
. A vertex in
\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n)
is a matrix with all row and column sums of 1. Adding 1 to the last nonfixed entry in rows 2 through
n
of
M
changes exactly those row and column sums which were 0 into 1, so (
1.3
) is satisfied. Also, since the entries below the main antidiagonal in
M
are either
0
or
-1
, adding
1
to these gives
1
or
0
. If such an entry in
M
was
-1
, there was a
1
somewhere to its left in that row and somewhere above it in the column. If the entry in
M
was
0
, the entire row consisted of
0
entries or there was a
-1
somewhere to its left in that row. Similarly, either the entire column consisted of
0
entries or there was a
-1
somewhere above it in the column. So adding
1
to the entries in all of these cases satisfies (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Thus this results in a vertex of
\mathrm{ASMCRY}(\lambda,n)
.
∎

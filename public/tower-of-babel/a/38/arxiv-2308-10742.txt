Topological dyonic black holes of massive gravity with generalized quasitopological electromagnetism
Askar Ali, Ali Övgün

Abstract
In this paper we investigate new dyonic black holes of massive gravity sourced by generalized quasitopological electromagnetism in arbitrary dimensions. We begin by deriving the exact solution to the field equations defining these black holes and look at how graviton’s mass, dimensionality parameter, and quasitopological electromagnetic field affect the horizon structure of anti-de Sitter dyonic black holes. We also explore the asymptotic behaviour of the curvature invariants at both the origin and infinity to analyze the geometric structure of the resultant black holes. We also compute the conserved and thermodynamic quantities of these dyonic black holes with the help of established techniques and known formulas. After investigating the relevancy of first law, we look at how various parameters influence the local thermodynamic stability of resultant black hole solution. We also examine how thermal fluctuations affect the local stability of dyonic black holes in massive gravity. Finally, we study the shadow cast of the black hole.

II
Topological dyonic black holes

The action of the massive gravity with cosmological constant
\Lambda
and generalized quasitopological electromagnetism can be expressed as

\mathcal{I}=\int d^{d}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg[R+m^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}a_{i}\Xi_{i}(\textbf{g},\textbf{f})-2\Lambda+\mathfrak{L}_{QT}\bigg],

(3)

in which
\mathfrak{L}_{QT}
stands for the Lagrangian density of extended quasitopological electromagnetic field and
R
signifies the Ricci scalar. In addition,
m
denotes the mass of graviton,
a_{i}
’s are the coupling constants, and
\Xi
’s are signifying the non-derivative potentials. Notably, these potentials are symmetric polynomials that specify the eigenvalues of the
d\times d
matrix
\zeta^{\mu}_{\nu}=\sqrt{g^{\mu\alpha}f_{\alpha\nu}}
. These potentials can be addressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Xi_{1}=[\zeta],\Xi_{2}=[\zeta]^{2}-[\zeta^{2}],\Xi_{3}=[\zeta]^{3}-3[\zeta][\zeta^{2}]+2[\zeta^{3}],\\
\Xi_{4}=[\zeta]^{4}-6[\zeta^{2}][\zeta]^{2}+8[\zeta^{3}][\zeta]+3[\zeta^{2}]^{2}-6[\zeta^{4}].\end{split}

(4)

The Lagrangian density of the generalized quasitopological electromagnetism
32b
is stated as

\mathfrak{L}_{QT}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}-\frac{1}{2s!}H_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\cdots\nu_{s}}H^{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\cdots\nu_{s}}-\chi\mathfrak{L}_{int},

(5)

in which
\chi
is the coupling constant and
\mathfrak{L}_{int}
designates the interaction term, which is expressed as

\mathfrak{L}_{int}=\delta^{\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{d}}_{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{d}}F_{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}H_{\rho_{3}\cdots\rho_{d}}F^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}H^{\sigma_{3}\cdots\sigma_{d}}.

(6)

Remember that the following forms are taken into consideration for the formation of above Eq. (
6
), i.e.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&F_{\rho\sigma}\sim h^{\prime}(r)\delta^{x^{0}x^{1}}_{\rho\sigma},\\
&H_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\cdots\nu_{s}}\sim\delta^{x^{2}\cdots x^{d}}_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\cdots\nu_{s}},\end{split}

(7)

with
s=d-2
, while
F_{\mu\nu}
and
H_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\cdots\nu_{s}}
are purely electric and purely magnetic, respectively. It is possible to obtain the field equations characterizing quasitopological electromagnetism through the use of Eqs. (
5
)-(
7
) in Eq. (
3
) as follows:

\nabla_{\gamma}F^{\gamma\rho}-4\chi\delta^{\rho\gamma\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{s}}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{d}}H_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{s}}\nabla_{\gamma}\big(F^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}H^{\sigma_{3}\cdots\sigma_{d}}\big)=0,

(8)

and

\nabla_{\rho}H^{\rho\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{s-1}}+2\chi s!\delta^{\rho\gamma\beta\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{s-1}}_{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{d}}F_{\rho\gamma}\nabla_{\beta}\big(F^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}H^{\sigma_{3}\cdots\sigma_{d}}\big)=0.

(9)

By varying Eq. (
3
) with regards to
g_{\mu\nu}
, the equations of motion of massive gravity sourced by quasitopological electromagnetic field can be derived as

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}+m^{2}X_{\mu\nu}=\mathfrak{T}^{(QT)}_{\mu\nu},

(10)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{\mu\nu}&=-\frac{a_{1}}{2}\bigg(\Xi_{1}g_{\mu\nu}-\zeta_{\mu\nu}\bigg)-\frac{a_{2}}{2}\bigg(\Xi_{2}g_{\mu\nu}-2\Xi_{1}\zeta_{\mu\nu}+2\zeta^{2}_{\mu\nu}\bigg)-\frac{a_{3}}{2}\\
&\times\bigg(\Xi_{3}g_{\mu\nu}-3\Xi_{2}\zeta_{\mu\nu}+6\Xi_{1}\zeta^{2}_{\mu\nu}-6\zeta^{3}_{\mu\nu}\bigg)-\frac{a_{4}}{2}\bigg(\Xi_{4}g_{\mu\nu}-4\Xi_{3}\zeta_{\mu\nu}\\
&+2\Xi_{2}\zeta^{2}_{\mu\nu}-24\Xi_{1}\zeta^{3}_{\mu\nu}+24\zeta^{4}_{\mu\nu}\bigg).\end{split}

(11)

Furthermore, the energy-momentum tensor of the quasitopological electromagnetic field is labeled by
\mathfrak{T}^{(QT)}_{\mu\nu}
and is specified as

\begin{split}\mathfrak{T}^{(QT)}_{\alpha\beta}&=F_{\alpha\rho}F^{\rho}_{\beta}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\alpha\beta}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{2(s-1)!}H_{\alpha\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{s-1}}H^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{s-1}}_{\beta}\\
&-\frac{1}{2(s!)^{2}}\delta^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{s}\rho}_{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{s}(\alpha}g_{\beta)\rho}H_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{s}}H^{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{s}}+\chi g_{\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{L}_{int}.\end{split}

(12)

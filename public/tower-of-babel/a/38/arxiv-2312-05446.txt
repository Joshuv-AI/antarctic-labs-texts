Metric results for the eventually always hitting points and level sets in subshift with specification
Bo Wang, Bing Li

Abstract




Abstract.

We study the set of eventually always hitting points for symbolic dynamics with specification. The measure and Hausdorff dimension of such sets are obtained.
Moreover, we establish the stronger metric results by introducing a new quantity
L_{N}(\omega)
which describes the maximal length of string of zeros of the
prefix among the first
N
iterations of
\omega
in symbolic
space. The Hausdorff dimensions of the
level sets for this quantity are also completely determined.

Key words and phrases
. Eventually always hitting points, Subshift, Specification, Hausdorff dimension, Ruelle operator

Introduction

1.1
The set of eventually always hitting points

Let
(X,d,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a metric measure preserving dynamical system. Namely
\mu
is a probability measure on
(X,\mathcal{B})
and
T
-invariant,

(X,d)
is a metric space. Here the measure and metric structure are compatible meaning that all Borel sets are
\mu
-measurable.
Let
\{E_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of subsets in
\mathcal{B}
and let

H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\}):=\{x\in X:T^{n}x\in E_{n}\ {\rm for\ i.o.}\}.

Here and throughout, “i.o.” stands for “infinitely often”. The sets
E_{n}
can be thought of as targets that the orbit under
T
of points in
X
have to hit.
The interesting situation is usually, when the diameters of
E_{n}
tend to zero as
n
increases.
It is thus natural to refer to
H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\})
as the corresponding shrinking target set associated with the given dynamical system and target sets.
The classical “shrinking target problem” is to consider the
\mu
-measure and the Hausdorff dimension of
H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\})
, which was formulated in
[
15
]
.
There are many results about shrinking target problem, we refer the reader to
[
7
,
11
,
18
,
26
]
for the
\mu
-measure of
H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\})
and
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
32
,
33
]
for the Hausdorff dimension of
H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\})
.
In this paper we will investigate a set which is closely related to
H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\})
, namely the set of eventually always hitting points. This is the set of points
x
satisfies that for all large enough
N
, there exists a
n\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
such that
T^{n}x\in E_{N}
. That is,

\displaystyle H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}):

\displaystyle=\{x\in X:\forall\ N\gg 1,1\leq\exists n\leq N,\ {\rm such\ that}\ T^{n}x\in E_{N}\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{N=m}^{\infty}\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}T^{-n}E_{N}.

Here and throughout, “
N\gg 1
” stands for “
N
large enough”. The name eventually always hitting points was introduced by Kelmer
[
19
]
.
We remark that Kelmer
[
19
]
studied a slightly different definition of eventually always hitting points, and required that for all
N\gg 1
,
we have
T^{n}x\in E_{N}
for some
0\leq n\leq N-1
, whereas we require
1\leq n\leq N
. For the result discussed in this paper,
it is insignificant which definition we use.

Note that
H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\})
is a limsup set and
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
is a liminf set. If
\mu(E_{n})\to 0
and
\{E_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a nested sequence, that is,
E_{n+1}\subset E_{n}
holds for all
n
, then
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})\subset H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\})
except for a null measure set
[
22
]
. In this sense,
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
is smaller than
H_{io}(T,\{E_{n}\})
. Similar with the shrinking target problem, we have the following two problems.

(
P1
) What is the
\mu
-measure of
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
?

(
P2
) What is the Hausdorff dimension of
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
if
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=0
?

There have recently been several results about the
\mu
-measure of the set
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
. If
\mu
is ergodic and
\{E_{n}\}
is a nested sequence,
the
\mu
-measure of
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
is either zero or one (see
[
22
]
).

•

Kelmer gave necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=1
in the setting of flows on hyperbolic manifolds
[
19
]
, and for flows on homogeneous spaces together with Yu
[
21
]
.

•

In the general setting, Kelmer showed
[
19
]
that if there exists
c<1
such that
\mu(E_{n})\leq\frac{c}{n}
for infinitely many
n
, then
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=0
.

•

Kleinbock, Konstantoulas and Richter gave sufficient conditions for
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))

=0
(
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=1
) respecitively in some mixing systems
[
23
]
, such as Gauss map, Product systems and Bernoulli schemes.
For the Gauss map and balls
E_{n}
with centers in 0, they proved that if there exists
c>\frac{1}{\log 2}
, such that
\mu(E_{n})\geq\frac{c\log\log n}{n}
for all but finitely many
n
, then
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=1
, and if there exists
c<\frac{1}{\log 2}
, such that
\mu(E_{n})\leq\frac{c\log\log n}{n}
for all but finitely many
n
, then
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=0
.

•

Kirseborn, Kunde and Persson
[
22
]
obtained results similar to those of Kleinbock, Konstantoulas and Richter.
Their results hold for a wider class of dynamical systems, but they need to add stronger conditions on
\mu(E_{n})
in order to be able to conclude that the measure of
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
is one or zero. For piecewise expanding interval map including the doubling map, Gauss map, and for some quadratic maps, they proved that
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=1
when
\mu(E_{n})\geq\frac{c(\log n)^{2}}{n}
for some
c>0
and all sufficiently large
n
.
Furthermore, they gave a sufficient condition for
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=0
under additional assumptions, they prove that if there exists
c>0
, such that
\mu(E_{n})\leq\frac{c}{n}
for infinitely many
n
, then
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=0
.

•

Ganotaki and Persson
[
13
]
gave sufficient conditions for
\mu(H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\}))=1
in the systems which have exponential decay of correlations for either Hölder continuous functions or functions of bounded variation.
They also gave an asymptotic estimate as
N\to\infty
on the number of
n\leq N
with
T^{n}x\in E_{N}
.

•

Other recent results can be found in
[
20
,
24
]
.

Unfortunately, the sufficient condition and necessary condition above does not coincide.

There are also some results about the Hausdorff dimension of the set
H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
for
\beta
-transformation
T_{\beta}(x)=\beta x({\rm mod}\ 1)
on
X=[0,1]
.

•

Bugeaud and Liao
[
6
]
proved that
\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})=\left(\frac{1-v}{1+v}\right)^{2}
when
E_{n}=[0,\beta^{-vn})
, where
v\in[0,1]
. Here and below,
\dim_{\rm H}
stands for Hausdorff dimension.

•

Wu
[
35
]
extended the result of Bugeaud and Liao to every points
x_{0}
in the unit interval.

•

Wu and Zheng
[
36
]
gave the upper bound and lower bound of
\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(T,\{E_{n}\})
when
E_{n}=[0,\psi(n))
, where
\psi
is a general positive function.

1.2
Main results

In this paper, we will consider the set of eventually always hitting points for symbolic dynamics. Given a positive integer
m\geq 2
, let
\mathcal{A}:=\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
. We denote

\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}=\{\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}\cdots:\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{A},\forall\ i\geq 1\}.

The set
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
is compact in the metric
d(u,\omega)=m^{-\min\{i:u_{i}\neq\omega_{i}\}}
if
u\neq\omega
and
d(u,\omega)=0
if
u=\omega
. Furthermore, the shift map
\sigma:\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
defined by
(\sigma u)_{i}=u_{i+1}
is continuous.
We call
(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}},d,\sigma)
the symbolic space. A subshift on
\mathcal{A}
is a closed set
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
with
\sigma(\Sigma)\subset\Sigma
.
If
\Sigma
has specification, by the theory of Ruelle operator, there exists a unique
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
satisfies the Gibbs property. We refer the reader to Section
3
for more details.

In this paper, we are dedicated to answer (
P1
) and (
P2
) when the targets are balls with center
0^{\infty}
in a subshift with specification.
Assume that
0^{\infty}\in\Sigma
, for function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\lim\limits_{x\to\infty}\psi(x)=0
,
let
E_{n}=B(0^{\infty},\psi(n))
, where
B(0^{\infty},\psi(n)):=\{\omega\in\Sigma:d(\omega,0^{\infty})<\psi(n)\}
.
For simplicity, we denote
H_{ea}(\sigma,\{E_{n}\})
by
H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)
. Denote by
h(\Sigma)
the topological entropy of
\Sigma
, the definition of topological entropy can be found in Section
3
.
Throughout this paper, let
A=e^{h(\Sigma)}
.

We first answer the problems (
P1
) and (
P2
) by the two following theorems. Theorem
1.1
describes the
\mu
-measure of
H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)
.
Theorem
1.3
gives the Hausdorff dimension of
H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)
when
\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{N}
exists.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
has specification,
h(\Sigma)>0
and
0^{\infty}\in\Sigma
. We have

\mu(H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi))=\begin{cases}0,&{\rm if}\ \limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{\log_{A}N}>1,\\
1,&{\rm if}\ \limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{\log_{A}N}<1.\end{cases}

Remark 1.2
.

By Lemma
4.2
, we know that the system
(\Sigma,\sigma,\mu)
is exponentially mixing, which implies
\sigma
is ergodic.
Therefore, the first part of Theorem
1.1
can be deduced from the result of Kirseborn, Kunde and Persson
[
22
, Corollary 1]
.
However, their sufficient condition on
\mu(H_{ea})=1
requires that
X\subset\mathbb{R}
.

In view of Theorem
1.1
, (
P2
) can be regarded as
what is the Hausdorff dimension of
H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)
if
\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{\log_{A}N}>1
? From Corollary

1.5
below, we show that

\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)=\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma

if
\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{\log_{A}N}<+\infty
. The remaining case
is
\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{\log_{A}N}=+\infty
, which means that the growth rate of
-\log_{m}\psi(N)
is larger than
\log N
.
Then the natural candidate for comparing rate is
N
instead of
\log N
. In the following we assume that
\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{N}
exists.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
\Sigma
has specification,
h(\Sigma)>0
and
0^{\infty}\in\Sigma
. Suppose
\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{N}
exists, denoted by
\tau

(
\tau
can be
+\infty
).

(i) If
\tau>1
, then

H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\}).

Here and below,
\sigma^{-1}(\{\omega\}):=\{u\in\Sigma:\sigma(u)=\omega\}
for all
\omega\in\Sigma
.

(ii) If
0\leq\tau\leq 1
, then

\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)=\left(\frac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}\right)^{2}\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

Remark 1.4
.

It is known that

\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma=\frac{h(\Sigma)}{\log m}

for any subshift
\Sigma
(for example, see
[
12
, Proposition III.1]
).
Thus the dimension formula for Theorem
1.3
can be reformulated as

\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)=\left(\frac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}\right)^{2}\frac{h(\Sigma)}{\log m}.

By Theorem
1.3
, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume that
\Sigma
has specification,
h(\Sigma)>0
and
0^{\infty}\in\Sigma
.
For any
\psi
with
\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{\log_{A}N}<+\infty
, we have
\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)=\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma
.

Moreover, we can establish stronger results, which deduce Theorem
1.1
and
1.3
directly due to the quantity
L_{N}(\omega)
describing
the longest string of zeros. Given
\omega\in\Sigma
,
n\geq 1
and
N\geq 1
, define

l_{n}(\omega)=\sup\{k\geq 0:\omega_{n+i}=0,\forall\ 1\leq i\leq k\}

and

L_{N}(\omega)=\max\limits_{1\leq n\leq N}l_{n}(\omega).

The quantity
l_{n}(\omega)
is the maximal length of string of zeros of the prefix of
\sigma^{n}\omega
,
which was introduced by Li and Wu
[
29
]
. Li, Persson, Wang and Wu
[
28
]
gave a kind of classification of
\beta>1
according to
\{l_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}

and calculated the Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff dimension of each class. Fang, Wu and Li
[
10
]
studied the approximation orders of real numbers by

\beta
-expansion in terms of investigating the behaviour of
l_{n}(\omega)
. We introduce a new quantity
L_{N}(\omega)
describing the maximal length of
string of zeros of the prefix among
\{\sigma\omega,\sigma^{2}\omega,\cdots,\sigma^{N}\omega\}
. Recall that

\displaystyle H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Sigma:\forall N\gg 1,1\leq\exists n\leq N,\ {\rm such\ that}\ \sigma^{n}\omega\in E_{N}\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\omega\in\Sigma:\forall N\gg 1,\min_{1\leq n\leq N}d(\sigma^{n}\omega,0^{\infty})<\psi(N)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\omega\in\Sigma:\forall N\gg 1,L_{N}(\omega)>-\log_{m}\psi(N)-1\right\}.

We have

\left\{\omega\in\Sigma:\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}>1\right\}\subset H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)\subset\left\{\omega\in\Sigma:\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}\geq 1\right\}.

(1.1)

So it suffices to consider
\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}
for the study of the set
H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)
. The typical result for the limit of
L_{N}(\omega)
is the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
has specification,
h(\Sigma)>0
and
0^{\infty}\in\Sigma
. For
\mu
-almost all
\omega\in\Sigma
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{\log_{A}N}=1.

Theorem
1.6
tells us that for
\mu
-almost all
\omega\in\Sigma
,
L_{N}(\omega)
increases to infinity with the speed
\log_{A}N
as
N
goes to infinity.
Generally, for any
a\geq 0
, let

\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a):=\left\{\omega\in\Sigma:\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}\geq a\right\}.

By
(
1.1
)
, the study of the Hausdorff dimension of
H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)
can be transformed into the the study of the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a)
.
We choose
\psi_{0}
with
\psi_{0}(N)=m^{-N}
, that is,
-\log_{m}\psi_{0}(N)=N
. So

\mathcal{U}_{\psi_{0}}(a)=\left\{\omega\in\Sigma:\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{N}\geq a\right\}.

The Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{U}_{\psi_{0}}(a)
is given in Corollary
1.9
below. Indeed, we establish the stronger results. That is, let
0\leq a\leq b
, we can further study the level sets

\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b):=\left\{\omega\in\Sigma:\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}\geq a,\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}=b\right\}

and

\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b):=\left\{\omega\in\Sigma:\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}=a,\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}=b\right\}.

Obviously,
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)\subset\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)
. Theorem
1.7
describes the Hausdorff dimensions of
\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)
and
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)
completely when
\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{N}
exists.

Theorem 1.7
.

Assume that
\Sigma
has specification,
h(\Sigma)>0
and
0^{\infty}\in\Sigma
. Suppose
\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{N}
exists,
denoted by
\tau
(
\tau
can be
+\infty
).

Case i
0<\tau<+\infty

(1)
a\geq\tau^{-1}

•

If
b<+\infty
, then

\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=\emptyset.

•

If
b=+\infty
, then

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=0.

(2)
0\leq a<\tau^{-1}

•

If
b<\frac{a}{1-\tau a}
, then

\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=\emptyset.

•

If
b\geq\frac{a}{1-\tau a}
, then

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=\frac{b(1-\tau a)-a}{(1+\tau b)(b-a)}\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

Case ii
\tau=+\infty

•

If
b>0
, then

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=0.

•

If
b=0
, then

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

Case iii
\tau=0

Additionally, suppose
\psi(x)
is strictly decreasing and
\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log_{m}\psi(N-\log_{m}\psi(N))}{\log_{m}\psi(N)}=1
, then

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

Remark 1.8
.

(i) In (2) of Theorem
1.7
Case i, we have

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=\frac{b(1-\tau a)-a}{(1+\tau b)(b-a)}\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma

for all
b\geq\frac{a}{1-\tau a}
. Note that
b(1-\tau a)-a=(1+\tau b)(b-a)=0
when
a=b=0
. However, we know that the denominator can not be zero. In fact, in the proof of Theorem
1.7
, we show that

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(0,0)=\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(0,0)=\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

Thus, we stipulate that
\frac{b(1-\tau a)-a}{(1+\tau b)(b-a)}=1
when
a=b=0
.

(ii) More precisely, in (1) of Theorem
1.7
Case i, we have

•

a>\tau^{-1}
and
b=+\infty

\Rightarrow

\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\})
.

•

\tau^{-1}<a<+\infty

\Rightarrow

\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\psi}(a,b)=\emptyset
.

In Theorem
1.7
Case ii, we have

•

a>0

\Rightarrow

\mathcal{U}_{\psi}(a,b)\subset\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\})
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
has specification,
h(\Sigma)>0
and
0^{\infty}\in\Sigma
. We have

(i) If
a>1
, then

\mathcal{U}_{\psi_{0}}(a)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\}).

(ii) If
0\leq a\leq 1
, then

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi_{0}}(a)=\left(\frac{1-a}{1+a}\right)^{2}\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
modulo Corollary
1.9
.

We use two important facts.

Fact 1: When
\tau>0
, for any
\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
with
\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}>0
, we have

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{N}=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}\cdot\frac{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}{N}=\tau\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}

(2.4)

Furthermore,
(
2.4
)
holds for
0<\tau<+\infty
and any
\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Fact 2: When
\tau=0
, for any
\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
with
\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{N}>0
, we have

\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{N}\cdot\frac{N}{-\log_{m}\psi(N)}=+\infty.

(2.5)

(i) If
\tau>1
, by Corollary
1.9
(i), we have

\mathcal{U}_{\psi_{0}}(\tau)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\}).

(2.6)

The upshot of
(
1.1
)
,
(
2.4
)
and
(
2.6
)
is that
H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)\subset\mathcal{U}_{\psi_{0}}(\tau)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\})
.
On the other hand, for any
\omega\in\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\})
, there exists
n_{0}\geq 0
, such that
\sigma^{n_{0}}\omega=0^{\infty}
.
Thus,
L_{N}(\omega)=+\infty
for every
N\geq n_{0}
, which implies that

. Thus,

\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\})\subset H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)
. Therefore,

H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-n}(\{0^{\infty}\}).

(ii) If
0<\tau\leq 1
, the upshot of
(
1.1
)
and
(
2.4
)
is that

\mathcal{U}_{\psi_{0}}(\tau(1+\epsilon))\subset H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)\subset\mathcal{U}_{\psi_{0}}(\tau)

(2.7)

for any
\epsilon>0
. When
\tau=1
, in view of
(
2.7
)
and Corollary
1.9
(ii), we obtain that
\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)=0
.
When

\epsilon\in(0,\tau^{-1}-1)

(
2.7
)
and Corollary
1.9
(ii) gives

\left(\frac{1-\tau(1+\epsilon)}{1+\tau(1+\epsilon)}\right)^{2}\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma\leq\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)\leq\left(\frac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}\right)^{2}\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

Letting

\dim_{\rm H}H_{ea}(\sigma,\psi)=\left(\frac{1-\tau}{1+\tau}\right)^{2}\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

The remaining case is

and
(

3
Preliminary

3.1
Symbolic dynamics

Let
(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}},d,\sigma)
be the symbolic space. For any
n\geq 1
, define

\mathcal{A}^{n}=\{\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}:\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{A},\forall\ 1\leq i\leq n\}.

The element in
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{n}
is called the infinite word and the word of length
n
, respectively.
We also denote
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
by the collection of all finite words, i.e.
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}=\cup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{A}^{n}
.
For any
\omega,u\in\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
, we write
\omega u
as the concatenation of words
\omega
and
u
.
In particular,
\omega^{n}
denotes the
n
times self-concatenation of
\omega
and
\omega^{\infty}
stands for the infinite times self-concatenation of
\omega
.
The lexicographical order
\prec
on
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
is defined as follows:

\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\cdots\varepsilon_{n}\cdots\prec\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\cdots\omega_{n}\cdots

if there exists an integer
n\geq 1
such that
\varepsilon_{k}=\omega_{k}
for all
1\leq k<n
but
\varepsilon_{n}<\omega_{n}
.
Similarly, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the lexicographical order
\prec
on
\mathcal{A}^{n}
is defined as follows:

\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\cdots\varepsilon_{n}\prec\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\cdots\omega_{n}

if there exists an integer
i\leq n
such that
\varepsilon_{k}=\omega_{k}
for all
1\leq k<i
but
\varepsilon_{i}<\omega_{i}
.
Given
u\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
and positive integers
i\leq j
, let
u_{[i,j]}=u_{i}u_{i+1}\cdots u_{j}
. Let
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{N}}
be a subshift. For each
n\geq 1
, define

\Sigma^{n}=\{\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}:\exists\ u\in\Sigma,{\rm such\ that}\ u_{[1,n]}=\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{n}\}

which is the set of all finite word of length
n
appears in some elements of
\Sigma
.
Similar with
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
, we denote
\Sigma^{\ast}
the collection of all finite words in
\Sigma
, that is,
\Sigma^{\ast}=\cup_{n\geq 1}\Sigma^{n}
.
For
u\in\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\ast}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote
I_{n}(u)
the set of all
\omega\in\Sigma
such that
\omega_{[1,n]}=u_{1}\cdots u_{n}
. We call
I_{n}(u)
the
n
-th cylinder containing
u
. Furthermore, for any
\omega\in\Sigma
and
r>0
, we denote
B(\omega,r):=\{u\in\Sigma:d(u,\omega)<r\}
, which is the ball with center
\omega
and radius
r
. Let
\sharp
denote the cardinality of a finite set. Note that

\sharp\Sigma^{i+j}\leq\sharp\Sigma^{i}\cdot\sharp\Sigma^{j}

(3.1)

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, which is a simple but important property.

We introduce two important definitions, which appears many times in our paper. The first definition is the topological entropy, which can be found in
[
34
, Chapter 7]
.

Definition 3.1
.

The topological entropy of a subshift
\Sigma
is

h(\Sigma):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sharp\Sigma^{n}}{n}=\inf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sharp\Sigma^{n}}{n},

(3.2)

where existence of the limit and the second equality follow from
(
3.1
)
and a standard lemma regarding subadditive sequences.

In fact, the above definition is not the original one
[
34
, Definition 7.6]
, but in view of
[
34
, Theorem 7.13]
, for subshift, the above definition is equivalent to the original one. The below definition of specification is specialised for the symbolic setting and is slightly weaker than Bowen’s original one
[
4
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

A subshift
\Sigma
has the specification property or simply has specification if there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
u,v\in\Sigma^{\ast}
, there exists
\omega\in\Sigma^{M}
such that
u\omega v\in\Sigma^{\ast}
.

There are many examples of symbolic dynamics with specification, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
3
]
.

3.2
Ruelle operator

An important tool to find
\sigma
-invariant measure on
\Sigma
is Ruelle operator.
Denote by
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R})
the space of all continuous functions
\phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
with the supremum norm
\|\phi\|:=\max\limits_{\omega\in\Sigma}|\phi(\omega)|
.
We define a linear operator
\mathcal{L}
as

\mathcal{L}\phi(\omega):=\sum_{u:\sigma u=\omega}\phi(u),\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}.

(3.3)

The above operator is called Ruelle operator. Here we choose a special potential, that is,
\varphi(\omega)=1
,
\forall\ \omega\in\Sigma
.
Note that
d(\sigma u,\sigma v)=m\cdot d(u,v)
for any
u,v\in\Sigma
with
d(u,v)\leq m^{-2}
.
In view of
[
9
, Proposition 1]
, there exists
0<\kappa\leq m^{-2}
, such that
\sharp\sigma^{-1}(u)=\sharp\sigma^{-1}(v)
for all
u,v\in\Sigma
with
d(u,v)\leq\kappa
.
Furthermore, we can arrange
\sigma^{-1}(u)=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
and
\sigma^{-1}(v)=\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\}
such that
d(x_{i},y_{i})\leq m^{-1}\cdot d(u,v)
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Hence, we have
\mathcal{L}\phi\in\mathcal{C}
for every
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the dual space of
\mathcal{C}
, by the Riesz representation theorem,
\mathcal{M}
is actually the space of all Borel measures on
\Sigma
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
be the dual operator of
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
. That is, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
and any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}
,

\int\phi\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\nu=\int\mathcal{L}\phi\mathrm{d}\nu.

For simplicity, we denote
\int\phi\mathrm{d}\nu
by
\langle\nu,\phi\rangle
for each
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}
. Due to the result of Fan and Jiang
[
9
, Theorem 1]
, there are a strictly positive number
\rho
and a strictly positive function
h
such that
\mathcal{L}h=\rho h
. Furthermore, there exists a probability measure
\nu
satsifying
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\nu=\rho\nu
. Choose
h
such that
\langle\nu,h\rangle=1
. Let
\mu:=h\nu
, i.e.
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\nu}=h
. The measure
\mu
has the Gibbs property: there exists a constant
\gamma>1
such that

\gamma^{-1}\rho^{-n}\leq\mu(I_{n}(\omega))\leq\gamma\rho^{-n}

(3.4)

holds for all
\omega\in\Sigma
and
n\geq 1
. What is more, by the result of Fan and Jiang
[
9
, Theorem 1]
,
\mu
is the unique
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measure which satisfies the Gibbs property. Take
\widetilde{\varphi}
as

\widetilde{\varphi}(\omega)=\frac{h(\omega)}{\rho\cdot h(\sigma\omega)}>0,\ \forall\ \omega\in\Sigma.

Consider the Ruelle operator associated to
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{\varphi}}
. The important feature of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is that

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}1=1.

(3.5)

We call it the normalized Ruelle operator. Denote
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}=\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{\varphi}}^{\ast}
, which is the dual operator of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
. We have the following relation between
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}
:

\mathcal{L}^{\ast n}\nu=\rho^{n}h^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast n}(h\nu),\ \forall n\geq 1.

Thus, we have that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}\mu=\mu
.

and
\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{L_{N}(\omega)}{\Phi(N)}=b
.

. Similar with the proof of Lemma
5.1
,
we assign a mass distribution on

. We first assign a mass distribution on all cylinders.

, let

Assume that the measure on

(k\geq 1)

For any

, let

Note that

for all

, thus,

can be extended to a Borel probability measure on

.
Lastly we estimate the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{U}_{P}^{\ast}(a,b)
. We show that

\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{P}^{\ast}(a,b)\geq\left(1-\frac{2}{P}\right)\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma.

(6.3)

Then we have
\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}^{\ast}(a,b)\geq(1-\frac{2}{P})\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma
for every

.
Letting

, we obtain that
\dim_{\rm H}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}^{\ast}(a,b)=\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma
.
To prove

6.3
)
, in view of Proposition
5.4
, we only need to prove that for any

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}\geq\left(1-\frac{2}{P}\right)\dim_{\rm H}\Sigma,

where

is the
N
-th cylinder containing
\omega
. By the definition of
\lambda
, we consider two cases.

Case I:
n_{k}\leq N\leq n_{k}+d_{k}
. Then

\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))=\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega))=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sharp\mathcal{W}_{j}}.

By Proposition
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{-\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{N\log m}\geq\frac{1}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log\sharp\mathcal{W}_{j}}{n_{k}+d_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log\sharp\mathcal{W}_{j}}{n_{k}}\geq\frac{1}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\sharp\mathcal{W}_{k+1}}{n_{k+1}-n_{k}}.

Since
\sharp\mathcal{W}_{k+1}=\left(\sharp\Sigma^{d_{k}-2M-1}\right)^{l_{k+1}}
and

, we obtain that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\sharp\mathcal{W}_{k+1}}{l_{k+1}d_{k}}=B.

(6.4)

Thus,

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}\geq\frac{h(\Sigma)}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{l_{k+1}d_{k}}{n_{k+1}-n_{k}}.

In view of

n_{k+1}-n_{k}=d_{k}(l_{k+1}+1)+r_{k+1}<d_{k}(l_{k+1}+2)

and

l_{k+1}:=\left\lfloor\frac{n_{k+1}-n_{k}}{d_{k}}\right\rfloor-1\geq P-2,

we obtain that

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}\geq\frac{h(\Sigma)}{\log m}\cdot\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{l_{k+1}}{l_{k+1}+2}\geq\left(1-\frac{2}{P}\right)\frac{h(\Sigma)}{\log m}.

Case II:

. Since

, there exists

By Proposition
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega))+(l-1)\log\sharp\Sigma^{d_{k}-1-2M}}{N\log m}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega))+(l-1)\log\sharp\Sigma^{d_{k}-1-2M}}{n_{k}+(l+1)d_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega))+(l+1)\log\sharp\Sigma^{d_{k}-1-2M}}{n_{k}+(l+1)d_{k}}.

The above equality follows from
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\sharp\Sigma^{d_{k}-1-2M}}{d_{k}}\leq\log m
and
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{d_{k}}{n_{k}}=0
.
Since
B=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\sharp\Sigma^{n}}{n}
and
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}d_{k}=+\infty
, for any
\delta\in(0,B)
, we have
\log\sharp\Sigma^{d_{k}-1-2M}>(B-\delta)d_{k}
for all
k
large enough. Thus,

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega))+(B-\delta)(l+1)d_{k}}{n_{k}+(l+1)d_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{B-\delta}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{(B-\delta)^{-1}(-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega)))+(l+1)d_{k}}{n_{k}+(l+1)d_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{B-\delta}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{(B+\delta)^{-1}(-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega)))+(l+1)d_{k}}{n_{k}+(l+1)d_{k}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega))}{n_{k}}=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log\sharp\mathcal{W}_{j}}{n_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\sharp\mathcal{W}_{k+1}}{n_{k+1}-n_{k}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{Bl_{k+1}d_{k}}{n_{k+1}-n_{k}}\leq B,

the second equality is due to
(
6.4
)
and the last inequality is due to
n_{k+1}-n_{k}\geq(l_{k+1}+1)d_{k}
. Therefore, for all

large enough, we have

(B+\delta)^{-1}(-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega)))<n_{k}.

(6.6)

Combining
(
6.5
)
,
(
6.6
)
and the inequality
\frac{y+s}{z+s}\geq\frac{y}{z},0\leq y<z,s\geq 0
, we obtain that

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}\geq\frac{B-\delta}{\log m}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{(B+\delta)^{-1}(-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega)))}{n_{k}}.

Furthermore, by the proof of Case I, we know that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{-\log\lambda(I_{n_{k}}(\omega))}{n_{k}}\geq\left(1-\frac{2}{P}\right)h(\Sigma).

Therefore,

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}\geq\frac{B-\delta}{B+\delta}\left(1-\frac{2}{P}\right)\frac{h(\Sigma)}{\log m}.

Letting
\delta\to 0
, we obtain that

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda(I_{N}(\omega))}{\log|I_{N}(\omega)|}\geq\left(1-\frac{2}{P}\right)\frac{h(\Sigma)}{\log m}.

The combination of Case I and Case II gives

The equality follows from

(see
[
12
, Proposition III.1]
). Similar arguments apply to the remaining cases.
We only give the constructions for the sequences

.

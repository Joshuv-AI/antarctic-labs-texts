On Reversing Operator Choi-Davis-Jensen inequality


Abstract
In this paper, we first provide a better estimate of the second inequality in Hermite-Hadamard inequality. Next, we study the reverse of the celebrated Davis-Choi-Jensen’s inequality. Our results are employed to establish a new bound for the operator Kantorovich inequality. Keywords: Hermite-Hadamard inequality; Davis-Choi-Jensen inequality; convex function; self-adjoint operator; positive operator. 2010 AMS Subject Classification : Primary: 47A12; 47A30 Secondary: 26D15.

Introduction and Preliminaries

A very interesting inequality for convex functions that has been widely studied in the literature is due to Hermit and Hadamard.
It provides a two-sided estimate of the mean value of a convex function. The Hermite-Hadamard inequality (for briefly, H-H inequality)
has several applications in the nonlinear analysis and geometry of Banach spaces, see
[
2
]
. Over the past decades, several
interesting generalizations, specific cases, and formulations of this remarkable inequality have been obtained for some different frameworks. In fact, it provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a function
f
to be convex. Many well-known inequalities
can be obtained using the concept of convex functions. For details, interested readers can refer to
[
1
,
12
,
14
,
15
]
.
This remarkable result of Hermit and Hadamard is as follows:

Theorem A.
Let
f:J\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function, where
a,b\in J
with
a<b
. Then

\displaystyle f\big(\frac{a+b}{2}\big)\leq\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}f(z)dz\leq\frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2}.

(1.1)

A history of this inequality can be found in
[
13
]
. An overview of the generalities and various developments can be found in

[
18
]
.

In this paper, we first provide a better estimate of the second inequality in H-H inequality (
1.1
).

Throughout this paper
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
are complex Hilbert spaces, and

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the algebra of all (bounded linear) operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Recall that an operator

A
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be positive (in symbol:
A\geq 0
) if
\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
.
We write
A>0
if
A
is positive and invertible. For self-adjoint operators
A
and
B
, we write
A\geq B
if
A-B
is positive,
i.e.,
\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq\langle Bx,x\rangle
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
. We call it the usual order. In particular, for
some scalars
m
and
M
, we write
m\leq A\leq M
if

\displaystyle m\langle x,x\rangle\leq\langle Ax,x\rangle\leq M\langle x,x\rangle,\hskip 28.45274pt\forall\;x\in\mathcal{H}.

We extensively use the continuous functional calculus for self-adjoint operators, e.g., see
[
6
, p. 3]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A continuous function
f
defined on the interval
J
is called an operator convex function if

\displaystyle f\big((1-v)A+vB\big)\leq(1-v)f(A)+vf(B)

for every
0<v<1
and for every pair of bounded self-adjoint operators
A
and
B
whose spectra are both in
J
.

Definition 1.2
.

A linear map
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
is positive if

\Phi\left(A\right)
is positive for all positive
A
in
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. It is said to be
unital (or, normalized) if
\Phi\left(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\right)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

We recall the Davis-Choi-Jensen inequality
[
3
,
4
]
for operator convex functions, which is regarded as a
noncommutative version of Jensen’s inequality:

Theorem B.
Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be a self-adjoint operator with the spectra
contained in the interval
J
and
\Phi
be a positive unital linear map from
\mathbb{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
to

\mathbb{B}\left(\mathcal{K}\right)
. If
f
is operator convex function on an interval
J
, then

f\left(\Phi\left(A\right)\right)\leq\Phi\left(f\left(A\right)\right).

(1.2)

Though in the case of convex function the inequality (
1.2
) does not hold in general, we have the following estimate
from
[
11
, Remark 4.14]
.

Theorem C.
Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be a self-adjoint operator with

Sp\left(A\right)\subseteq\left[m,M\right]
for some scalars
m,M
with
m<M
and
\Phi
be a positive unital
linear map from
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{K})
. If
f
is non-negative
convex function, then

\frac{1}{\alpha}\Phi\big(f(A)\big)\leq f\big(\Phi(A)\big)\leq\alpha\Phi\big(f(A)\big),

(1.3)

where
\alpha
is defined by

\displaystyle\alpha=\max_{m\leq t\leq M}\Big\{\frac{1}{f(t)}\Big(\frac{M-t}{M+m}f(m)+\frac{t-m}{M+m}f(M)\Big)\Big\}.

For other generalizations of inequality, we refer the interested readers to
[
9
,
10
,
17
,
19
]
. One of our main aim
in this article is to improve the first inequality in the above. For this purpose, we use some ideas from
[
16
, Theorem 3.4]
.

In this paper, we first provide a better estimate of the second inequality in Hermite-Hadamard inequality.
Our results are employed to establish a new bound for the operator Kantorovich inequality. In particular,
we show that

\displaystyle\Phi(A^{-1})\leq\Phi\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{M\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}-A}{M-m}m^{-t}+\frac{A-m\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}}{M-m}M^{-t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{t}}dt\Big)\leq\frac{(M+m)^{2}}{4Mm}\Phi^{-1}(A),

(1.4)

where
m{{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathcal{H}}}\leq A\leq M{{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathcal{H}}}

and
\Phi
is a positive linear map.

First we start our work by providing a better estimate for H-H inequality (

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

be a continuous function on the interval
J

is convex for all

\displaystyle f\big(\frac{a+b}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}f(z)dz\leq\int_{a}^{b}\Big(\frac{z-a}{b-a}f^{t}(a)+\frac{b-z}{b-a}f^{t}(b)\Big)^{\frac{1}{t}}dz\leq\frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2}.

Proof.

Since
{{f}^{t}}
is a convex function, we have for any
x,y\in J
and
v\in\left[0,1\right]

{{f}^{t}}\left(\left(1-v\right)x+vy\right)\leq\left(1-v\right){{f}^{t}}\left(x\right)+v{{f}^{t}}\left(y\right).

(2.1)

Raising both sides of (
2.1
) to the power of
{1}/{t}\;
, we get

\displaystyle f\left(\left(1-v\right)x+vy\right)

\displaystyle={{\left({{f}^{t}}\left(\left(1-v\right)x+vy\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}

\displaystyle\leq{{\left(\left(1-v\right){{f}^{t}}\left(x\right)+v{{f}^{t}}\left(y\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-v\right){{\left({{f}^{t}}\left(x\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}+v{{\left({{f}^{t}}\left(y\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-v\right)f\left(x\right)+vf\left(y\right).

Consequently,

f\left(\left(1-v\right)x+vy\right)\leq{{\left(\left(1-v\right){{f}^{t}}\left(x\right)+v{{f}^{t}}\left(y\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}\leq\left(1-v\right)f\left(x\right)+vf\left(y\right),

(2.2)

which shows the convexity of the function
f
.

Suppose
z\in\left[a,b\right]
. If we substitute
x=a
,
y=b
, and
1-v={\left(b-z\right)}/{\left(b-a\right)}\;
in (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle f\left(z\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{\left(\frac{b-z}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(a\right)+\frac{z-a}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(b\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b-z}{b-a}f\left(a\right)+\frac{z-a}{b-a}f\left(b\right).

Since
z\in\left[a,b\right]
, it follows that
b+a-z\in\left[a,b\right]
. Now, applying the inequality (
2.3
) to the variable
b+a-z
, we get

\displaystyle f\left(b+a-z\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{\left(\frac{z-a}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(a\right)+\frac{b-z}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(b\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{z-a}{b-a}f\left(a\right)+\frac{b-z}{b-a}f\left(b\right).

By adding inequalities (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we infer that

\displaystyle f\left(b+a-z\right)+f\left(z\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{\left(\frac{z-a}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(a\right)+\frac{b-z}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(b\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}+{{\left(\frac{b-z}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(a\right)+\frac{z-a}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(b\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{z-a}{b-a}f\left(a\right)+\frac{b-z}{b-a}f\left(b\right)+\frac{b-z}{b-a}f\left(a\right)+\frac{z-a}{b-a}f\left(b\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(b\right)+f\left(a\right)

which, in turn, leads to

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{f\left(a+b-z\right)+f\left(z\right)}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({{\left(\frac{z-a}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(a\right)+\frac{b-z}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(b\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}+{{\left(\frac{b-z}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(a\right)+\frac{z-a}{b-a}{{f}^{t}}\left(b\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{f\left(a\right)+f\left(b\right)}{2}.

Now, the result follows by integrating the inequality (

z\in\left[a,b\right]
, and using the fact that

.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be a self-adjoint operator with the spectra contained in the interval
J
and let
x\in\mathcal{H}
. If
f:J\to\mathbb{R}
is convex function, then

f\left(\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle\right)\leq\left\langle f\left(A\right)x,x\right\rangle.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be two self-adjoint operators with the spectra contained in the interval
\left[m,M\right]
with
m<M
, and let
\Phi:\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\to\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{K}\right)
be a positive unital linear map. If
f:\left[m,M\right]\to\left(0,\infty\right)
is a continuous increasing function such that
{{f}^{t}}

t\in\left(0,1\right)
is convex, then for a given positive real number
\alpha

\displaystyle f\left(A\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{\left(\frac{M\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}-A}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(m\right)+\frac{A-m\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(M\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta{{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathcal{H}}}+\alpha f\left(B\right),

holds for
\beta=\underset{m\leq t\leq M}{\mathop{\max}}\,\left\{{{a}_{f}}t+{{b}_{f}}-\alpha f\left(t\right)\right\}
.

Proof.

Our assumption implies

\displaystyle f\left(A\right)\leq{{\left(\frac{M\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}-A}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(m\right)+\frac{A-m\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(M\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}

\displaystyle\leq{{a}_{f}}A+{{b}_{f}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}.

thanks to (
2.6
). Then, for any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(A\right)x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle{{\left(\frac{M\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}-A}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(m\right)+\frac{A-m\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(M\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq{{a}_{f}}\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle+{{b}_{f}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(A\right)x,x\right\rangle-\alpha f\left(\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle{{\left(\frac{M\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}-A}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(m\right)+\frac{A-m\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(M\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}x,x\right\rangle-\alpha f\left(\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{a}_{f}}\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle+{{b}_{f}}-\alpha f\left(\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{a}_{f}}\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle+{{b}_{f}}-\alpha f\left(\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{m\leq t\leq M}{\mathop{\max}}\,\left\{{{a}_{f}}t+{{b}_{f}}-\alpha f\left(t\right)\right\}.

On the other hand, since
A\leq B
and
f
is increasing and convex, then

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(A\right)x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle{{\left(\frac{M\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}-A}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(m\right)+\frac{A-m\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}}}{M-m}{{f}^{t}}\left(M\right)\right)}^{\frac{1}{t}}}x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\beta+\alpha f\left(\left\langle Bx,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq\beta+\alpha\left\langle f\left(B\right)x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\beta{{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathcal{H}}}+\alpha f\left(B\right)\right)x,x\right\rangle.

This completes the proof.
∎

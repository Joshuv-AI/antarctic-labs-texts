Hilbert expansion of Boltzmann equation with soft potentials and specular boundary condition in half-space
Jing Ouyang, Yong Wang

Abstract
. Boundary effects play an important role in the study of hydrodynamic limits in the Boltzmann theory. We justify rigorously the validity of the hydrodynamic limit from the Boltzmann equation of soft potentials to the compressible Euler equations by the Hilbert expansion with multi-scales. Specifically, the Boltzmann solutions are expanded into three parts: interior part, viscous boundary layer and Knudsen boundary layer. Due to the weak effect of collision frequency of soft potentials, new difficulty arises when tackling the existence of Knudsen layer solutions with space decay rate, which has been overcome under some constraint conditions and losing velocity weight arguments.

Introduction and Main Results

1.1.
Introduction

It is well-known that the Boltzmann equation is closely related to the fluid dynamical systems for both compressible and incompressible flows
since the founding work of Maxwell
[
36

[

Based on Hilbert or Chapman-Enskog expansions, the standard fluid theory can be derived formally, for instance: the compressible Euler and Navier-Stokes equations, the incompressible Euler and Navier-Stokes (Fourier) equations,
et. al

,

,

All of the above-mentioned works on the compressible Euler limit were carried out in either spatially periodic domain or the whole space. However, in many important physical models, the physical boundaries occur naturally, and the boundary effects play an important role in the study of hydrodynamic limits in the Boltzmann theory. For initial boundary value problem, by a formal analysis, Sone
[

In the present paper, we aim to justify the hydrodynamic limit to the compressible Euler equations for the Boltzmann equation of soft potentials. The new difficulty for the soft potentials is that it is hard to establish the existence of solution for Knudsen boundary layer with enough space decay rate, which is crucial to close the Hilbert expansion.

We consider the scaled Boltzmann equation

F_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F=\frac{1}{\mathscr{K}_{n}}Q(F,F),

(1.1)

where
F(t,x,v)\geq 0
is the density distribution function for the gas particles with position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x_{3}>0\}
and velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
at time
t>0
, and
\mathscr{K}_{n}>0
is Knudsen number which is proportional to the mean free path. The Boltzmann collision term
Q(F_{1},F_{2})
on the right is defined in terms of the following bilinear form

\displaystyle Q(F_{1},F_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(v-u,\omega)F_{1}(u^{\prime})F_{2}(v^{\prime})\,{d\omega du}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(v-u,\omega)F_{1}(u)F_{2}(v)\,{d\omega du}

\displaystyle:=Q_{+}(F_{1},F_{2})-Q_{-}(F_{1},F_{2}),

(1.2)

where the relationship between the post-collision velocity
(v^{\prime},u^{\prime})
of two particles with the pre-collision velocity
(v,u)
is given by

u^{\prime}=u+[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad v^{\prime}=v-[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega,

for
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, which can be determined by conservation laws of momentum and energy

u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}=u+v,\quad|u^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}=|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}.

The Boltzmann collision kernel
B=B(v-u,\omega)
in (
1.1
) depends only on
|v-u|
and
\theta
with
\cos\theta=(v-u)\cdot\omega/|v-u|
. Throughout this paper, we consider cutoff soft potential model, i.e.,

B(v-u,\omega)=|v-u|^{\kappa}\cdot\beta(\theta),\quad\kappa\in(-3,0),

where we assume the Grad cutoff condition holds, i.e.,

\displaystyle 0\leq\beta(\theta)\leq\beta_{0}|\cos\theta|,

for some constant
\beta_{0}>0
.

Denote
\vec{n}=(0,0,-1)
to be the outward normal of
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
and the phase boundary in the space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
as
\gamma:=\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We split
\gamma
into outgoing boundary
\gamma_{+}
, incoming boundary
\gamma_{-}
, and grazing boundary
\gamma_{0}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma_{+}=\{(x,v):x\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+},v\cdot\vec{n}=-v_{3}>0\},\\
\gamma_{-}=\{(x,v):x\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+},v\cdot\vec{n}=-v_{3}<0\},\\
\gamma_{0}=\{(x,v):x\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+},v\cdot\vec{n}=-v_{3}=0\}.\end{split}

In the present paper, we consider the Boltzmann equation with specular reflection boundary conditions, i.e.,

F(t,x,v)|_{\gamma_{-}}=F(t,x,R_{x}v),

(1.3)

where

R_{x}v=v-2\{v\cdot\vec{n}\}\vec{n}=(v_{1},v_{2},-v_{3})^{t}.

(1.4)

1.2.
Asymptotic expansion

From the formal analysis in
[
40
]
, we know that the thickness of viscous boundary layer is
\sqrt{\mathscr{K}_{n}}
. For simplicity, we use the new parameter
\varepsilon=\sqrt{\mathscr{K}_{n}}
and denote the Boltzmann solution to be
F^{\varepsilon}
, then the Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) is rewritten as

\partial_{t}F^{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}Q(F^{\varepsilon},F^{\varepsilon}).

(1.5)

1.2.1.
Interior expansion

We define the interior expansion

F^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}F_{k}(t,x,v).

(1.6)

Substituting (
1.6
) into (
1.5
) and comparing the order of
\varepsilon
, one obtains

\displaystyle\begin{split}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}:\,\,\quad\quad\quad\quad\qquad 0&=Q(F_{0},F_{0}),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}:\,\,\quad\quad\quad\quad\qquad 0&=Q(F_{0},F_{1})+Q(F_{1},F_{0}),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\varepsilon^{0}:\,\,\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}F_{0}&=Q(F_{0},F_{2})+Q(F_{2},F_{0})+Q(F_{1},F_{1}),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\varepsilon:\,\,\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}F_{1}&=Q(F_{0},F_{3})+Q(F_{3},F_{0})+Q(F_{1},F_{2})+Q(F_{2},F_{1}),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \vdots\\
\displaystyle\varepsilon^{k}:\,\,\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}F_{k}&=Q(F_{0},F_{k+2})+Q(F_{k+2},F_{0})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=k+2\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}Q(F_{i},F_{j}).\end{split}

(1.7)

It follows from
\eqref{c1.7}_{1}
and the celebrated H-theorem that
F_{0}
should be a local Maxwellian, i.e.,

\mu(t,x,v):=F_{0}(t,x,v)\equiv\frac{\rho(t,x)}{[2\pi T(t,x)]^{3/2}}\exp{\left\{-\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}(t,x)|^{2}}{2T(t,x)}\right\}},

(1.8)

where
\rho(t,x)
,
\mathfrak{u}(t,x)=(\mathfrak{u}_{1},\mathfrak{u}_{2},\mathfrak{u}_{3})(t,x),
and
T(t,x)
are defined by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}dv=\rho,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vF_{0}dv=\rho\mathfrak{u},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}F_{0}dv=\rho|\mathfrak{u}|^{2}+3\rho T,

which represent the macroscopic density, velocity and temperature, respectively. Multiplying
\eqref{c1.7}_{3}
by
1,v_{i},|v|^{2}
and integrating on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, one obtains that
(\rho,\mathfrak{u},T)
satisfies the compressible Euler system

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\mbox{\rm div}(\rho\mathfrak{u})=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho\mathfrak{u})+\mbox{\rm div}(\rho\mathfrak{u}\otimes\mathfrak{u})+\nabla p=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\partial_{t}[\rho(\frac{3T}{2}+\frac{|\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2})]+\mbox{\rm div}[\rho\mathfrak{u}(\frac{3T}{2}+\frac{|\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2})]+\mbox{\rm div}(p\mathfrak{u})=0,\end{cases}

(1.9)

where
p=\rho T
is the pressure function. For the compressible Euler equations (
1.9
), we impose the slip boundary condition

\mathfrak{u}\cdot\vec{n}|_{x_{3}=0}=\mathfrak{u}_{3}|_{x_{3}=0}=0.

(1.10)

and the initial data

(\rho,\mathfrak{u},T)(0,x)=(1+\delta\varphi_{0},\delta\Phi_{0},1+\delta\vartheta_{0})(x),

(1.11)

with
\|(\varphi_{0},\Phi_{0},\vartheta_{0})\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\leq 1
where
\delta>0
is a parameter and
s_{0}\geq 3
is some given positive number.
Choose
\delta_{1}>0
so that for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{1}]
, the positivity of
1+\delta\varphi_{0}
and
1+\delta\vartheta_{0}
is guaranteed. Then for each
\delta\in(0,\delta_{1}]
, there is a family of classical solutions
(\rho^{\delta},\mathfrak{u}^{\delta},T^{\delta})\in C([0,\tau^{\delta}];H^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}))\cap C^{1}([0,\tau^{\delta}];H^{s_{0}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}))
of the compressible Euler equations (
1.9
)-(
1.11
) such that
\rho^{\delta}>0
and
T^{\delta}>0
.

For later use, we define the linearized collision operator
\mathbf{L}
by

\mathbf{L}\mathfrak{h}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\Big\{Q(\mu,\sqrt{\mu}\mathfrak{h})+Q(\sqrt{\mu}\mathfrak{h},\mu)\Big\}.

(1.12)

Denote the null space of
\mathbf{L}
as
\mathcal{N}
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\text{span}\{\chi_{0},\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3},\chi_{4}\},

where

\displaystyle\chi_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\sqrt{\mu},\quad\chi_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho T}}(v_{i}-\mathfrak{u}_{i})\sqrt{\mu},\quad\chi_{4}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6\rho}}(\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{T}-3)\sqrt{\mu}.

For each
k\geq 1
, decompose
\displaystyle f_{k}:=\frac{F_{k}}{\sqrt{\mu}}
as

\displaystyle f_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}f_{k}+\{\mathbf{I-P}\}f_{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\left\{\frac{\rho_{k}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\chi_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{T}}u_{k,j}\cdot\chi_{j}+\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{6}}\frac{\theta_{k}}{T}\chi_{4}\right\}+\{\mathbf{I-P}\}f_{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\left\{\frac{\rho_{k}}{\rho}+u_{k}\cdot\frac{v-\mathfrak{u}}{T}+\frac{\theta_{k}}{6T}(\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{T}-3)\right\}\sqrt{\mu}+\{\mathbf{I-P}\}f_{k},

(1.13)

where
\mathbf{P}
is the macroscopic projection onto
\mathcal{N}
.

1.2.2.
Viscous boundary layer expansion

Generally, the solution of interior expansion
F_{i},i=1,2,\cdots
do not satisfy the specular reflection boundary conditions.
To overcome the difficulty coming from the boundary condition, the boundary layer expansion is needed, see
[
20
]
and
[
39
,
40
]
.

We define the scaled normal coordinate:

y:=\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon}.

(1.14)

For simplicity of presentation, we denote

x_{\shortparallel}=(x_{1},x_{2}),\quad\nabla_{\shortparallel}=(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad v_{\shortparallel}=(v_{1},v_{2}).

(1.15)

Motivated by
[
40

, we define the viscous boundary layer expansion as

\displaystyle\bar{F}^{\varepsilon}(t,x_{\shortparallel},y)\sim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\bar{F}_{k}(t,x_{\shortparallel},y,v).

Plugging
F^{\varepsilon}+\bar{F}^{\varepsilon}
into the Boltzmann equation (
1.5
) and comparing the order of
\varepsilon
, then using (
1.7
), in the neighborhood of physical boundary, we have

\begin{split}\displaystyle&\frac{1}{\varepsilon}:\qquad\quad 0=Q(\mu_{0},\bar{F}_{1})+Q(\bar{F}_{1},\mu_{0}),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle&\varepsilon^{0}:\quad v_{3}\frac{\partial\bar{F}_{1}}{\partial y}=[Q(\mu_{0},\bar{F}_{2})+Q(\bar{F}_{2},\mu_{0})]+y[Q(\partial_{3}\mu_{0},\bar{F}_{1})+Q(\bar{F}_{1},\partial_{3}\mu_{0})]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+Q(F_{1}^{0},\bar{F}_{1})+Q(\bar{F}_{1},F_{1}^{0})+Q(\bar{F}_{1},\bar{F}_{1}),\\
&\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \vdots\\
\displaystyle&\varepsilon^{k}:\quad\{\partial_{t}+v_{\shortparallel}\cdot\nabla_{\shortparallel}\}\bar{F}_{k}+v_{3}\frac{\partial\bar{F}_{k+1}}{\partial y}=Q(\mu_{0},\bar{F}_{k+2})+Q(\bar{F}_{k+2},\mu_{0})\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+j=k+2\\
1\leq l\leq\mathfrak{b},\,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{y^{l}}{l!}\big[Q(\partial_{3}^{l}\mu_{0},\bar{F}_{j})+Q(\bar{F}_{j},\partial_{3}^{l}\mu_{0})\big]\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=k+2\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\big[Q(F_{i}^{0},\bar{F}_{j})+Q(\bar{F}_{j},F_{i}^{0})+Q(\bar{F}_{i},\bar{F}_{j})\big]\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+l=k+2\\
1\leq l\leq\mathfrak{b},\,i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{y^{l}}{l!}\big[Q(\partial_{3}^{l}F_{i}^{0},\bar{F}_{j})+Q(\bar{F}_{j},\partial_{3}^{l}F_{i}^{0})\big],\quad\mbox{for}\ k\geq 1,\end{split}

(1.16)

where we have used the Taylor expansions of
\mu
and
F_{i}
at
x_{3}=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mu(t,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},v)

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\mathfrak{b}}\frac{1}{l!}\partial_{3}^{l}\mu_{0}\cdot x_{3}^{l}+\frac{x_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}}{(\mathfrak{b}+1)!}\partial_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}\tilde{\mu},

(1.17)

\displaystyle F_{i}(t,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},v)

\displaystyle=F_{i}^{0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\mathfrak{b}}\frac{1}{l!}\partial_{3}^{l}F_{i}^{0}\cdot x_{3}^{l}+\frac{x_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}}{(\mathfrak{b}+1)!}\partial_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}\mathfrak{F}_{i},\quad i\geq 1.

(1.18)

Here we have used the simplified notations

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{3}^{l}\mu_{0}:&=(\partial_{3}^{l}\mu)(t,x_{1},x_{2},0,v),\quad\partial_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}\tilde{\mu}:=(\partial_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}\mu)(t,x_{1},x_{2},\xi_{0},v),\\
\partial_{3}^{l}F_{i}^{0}:&=(\partial_{3}^{l}F_{i})(t,x_{1},x_{2},0,v),\quad\partial_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}\mathfrak{F}_{i}:=(\partial_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}F_{i})(t,x_{1},x_{2},\xi_{i},v),\end{split}

(1.19)

for some
\xi_{i}\in(0,x_{3})
with
i\geq 0
. The number
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
will be chosen later.

For the macro-micro decomposition of viscous and Knudsen boundary layers, we denote the corresponding linearized operator, macroscopic projection, and null space as

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{0}=\mathbf{L}(t,x_{\shortparallel},0,v),\qquad\mathbf{P}_{0}=\mathbf{P}(t,x_{\shortparallel},0,v),\qquad\mathcal{N}_{0}=\mathcal{N}(t,x_{\shortparallel},0,v).

It is noted that
\mathbf{L}_{0},\mathbf{P}_{0}
and
\mathcal{N}_{0}
are independent of normal variables. We define

\bar{f}_{k}:=\frac{\bar{F}_{k}}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}}},

(1.20)

then it holds that

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{0}\bar{f}_{k}+\{\mathbf{I-P_{0}}\}\bar{f}_{k}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{\bar{\rho}_{k}}{\rho^{0}}+\bar{u}_{k}\cdot\frac{v-\mathfrak{u}^{0}}{T^{0}}+\frac{\bar{\theta}_{k}}{6T^{0}}(\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}^{0}|^{2}}{T^{0}}-3)\right\}\sqrt{\mu_{0}}+\{\mathbf{I-P_{0}}\}\bar{f}_{k},

where and whereafter we always use the notation
(\rho^{0},\mathfrak{u}^{0},T^{0}):=(\rho,\mathfrak{u},T)(t,x_{\shortparallel},0)
.

Throughout the present paper, we always assume the far-field condition

\bar{f}_{k}(t,x_{\shortparallel},y,v)\rightarrow 0,\quad\mbox{as}\ y\rightarrow+\infty.

(1.21)

1.2.3.
Knudsen boundary layer expansion

To construct the solution satisfying the boundary condition at higher orders, we still need the Knudsen boundary layer. We define the new scaled normal coordinate:

\eta:=\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

The Knudsen boundary layer expansion is defined as

\hat{F}^{\varepsilon}(t,x_{\shortparallel},\eta)\sim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\hat{F}_{k}(t,x_{\shortparallel},\eta,v).

Plugging
F^{\varepsilon}+\bar{F}^{\varepsilon}+\hat{F}^{\varepsilon}
into (
1.5
) and comparing the order of
\varepsilon
, then using (
1.7
) and (

), one obtains

\begin{split}\displaystyle&\frac{1}{\varepsilon}:\quad v_{3}\frac{\partial\hat{F}_{1}}{\partial\eta}-\big[Q(\mu_{0},\hat{F}_{1})+Q(\hat{F}_{1},\mu_{0})\big]=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle&\varepsilon^{0}:\quad\ v_{3}\frac{\partial\hat{F}_{2}}{\partial\eta}-\big[Q(\mu_{0},\hat{F}_{2})+Q(\hat{F}_{2},\mu_{0})\big]\\
&\qquad\quad=Q(F_{1}^{0}+\bar{F}^{0}_{1},\hat{F}_{1})+Q(\hat{F}_{1},F_{1}^{0}+\bar{F}^{0}_{1})+Q(\hat{F}_{1},\hat{F}_{1}),\\[5.69054pt]
&\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \vdots\\
\displaystyle&\varepsilon^{k}:\quad v_{3}\frac{\partial\hat{F}_{k+2}}{\partial\eta}-\big[Q(\mu_{0},\hat{F}_{k+2})+Q(\hat{F}_{k+2},\mu_{0})\big]\\
&\qquad=-\{\partial_{t}+v_{\shortparallel}\cdot\nabla_{\shortparallel}\}\hat{F}_{k}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+2l=k+2\\
1\leq l\leq\mathfrak{b},j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\eta^{l}}{l!}\big[Q(\partial_{3}^{l}\mu_{0},\hat{F}_{j})+Q(\hat{F}_{j},\partial_{3}^{l}\mu_{0})\big]\\
&\qquad\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=k+2\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\big[Q(F_{i}^{0}+\bar{F}_{i}^{0},\hat{F}_{j})+Q(\hat{F}_{j},F_{i}^{0}+\bar{F}_{i}^{0})+Q(\hat{F}_{i},\hat{F}_{j})\big]\\
&\qquad\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+2l=k+2\\
i,j\geq 1,1\leq l\leq\mathfrak{b}\end{subarray}}\frac{\eta^{l}}{l!}\big[Q(\partial_{3}^{l}F_{i}^{0},\hat{F}_{j})+Q(\hat{F}_{j},\partial_{3}^{l}F_{i}^{0})\big]\\

i,j\geq 1,1\leq l\leq\mathfrak{b}\end{subarray}}\frac{\eta^{l}}{l!}\big[Q(\partial_{y}^{l}\bar{F}_{i}^{0},\hat{F}_{j})+Q(\hat{F}_{j},\partial_{y}^{l}\bar{F}_{i}^{0})\big],\quad\mbox{for}\ k\geq 1,\end{split}

(1.22)

where we have used (

)-(

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{y}^{l}\bar{F}_{i}^{0}:&=(\partial_{y}^{l}\bar{F}_{i})(t,x_{1},x_{2},0,v),\\
\partial_{y}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}\bar{\mathfrak{F}}_{i}:&=(\partial_{y}^{\mathfrak{b}+1}\bar{F}_{i})(t,x_{1},x_{2},\bar{\xi}_{i},v),\end{split}\qquad\mbox{for}\,\,0\leq l\leq\mathfrak{b},

(1.23)

for some

. For later use, we denote
\displaystyle\hat{f}_{k}:=\frac{\hat{F}_{k}}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}}}
.

1.3.
Hilbert expansion

Now we consider the Boltzmann solution with the following Hilbert expansion of multi-scales

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}=\mu(t,x,v)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon^{i}F_{i}(t,x,v)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon^{i}\bar{F}_{i}(t,x_{\shortparallel},\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon},v)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon^{i}\hat{F}_{i}(t,x_{\shortparallel},\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}},v)+\varepsilon^{5}F^{\varepsilon}_{R}.

(1.24)

Using (
1.7
), (
1.16
) and (
1.22
), one can obtain the equation of
F_{R}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F^{\varepsilon}_{R}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F^{\varepsilon}_{R}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\{Q(\mu,F^{\varepsilon}_{R})+Q(F^{\varepsilon}_{R},\mu)\}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{3}Q(F^{\varepsilon}_{R},F^{\varepsilon}_{R})+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon^{i-2}\{Q(F_{i}+\bar{F}_{i}+\hat{F}_{i},F^{\varepsilon}_{R})+Q(F^{\varepsilon}_{R},F_{i}+\bar{F}_{i}+\hat{F}_{i})\}

\displaystyle\quad+R^{\varepsilon}+\bar{R}^{\varepsilon}+\hat{R}^{\varepsilon},

(1.25)

where
R^{\varepsilon},\bar{R}^{\varepsilon}
and
\hat{R}^{\varepsilon}
are defined in (
4.8
)-(
4.2
).

The main purpose of the present paper is to establish the validity of the Hilbert expansion for the Boltzmann equation around the local Maxwellian
\mu
determined by compressible Euler equations (
1.9
). For later use, we define

F^{\varepsilon}_{R}=\sqrt{\mu}f^{\varepsilon}_{R}.

(1.26)

To use the
L^{2}
-
L^{\infty}
framework
[
17
,
16
]
, we also introduce a global Maxwellian

\mu_{M}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi T_{M})^{3/2}}\exp{\left\{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T_{M}}\right\}},

where

T_{M}<\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}}T(t,x)\leq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}}T(t,x)<2T_{M}.

(1.27)

Using (

Remark 1.3
.

For simplicity of presentation, we only give details of proof for the Boltzmann equation of soft potentials in the present paper. And we point out that it is also valid for the cases of hard potentials by similar arguments.

Now we briefly comment the key points of present paper. To estimate the microscopic part of interior expansions and viscous boundary layers, we need some decay property on pseudo-inverse linear operator
\mathbf{L}^{-1}
and
\mathbf{L}_{0}^{-1}
. For
-\frac{3}{2}<\kappa\leq 1
, the authors
[
30
]
obtained

\displaystyle|\mu^{-\frac{q}{2}}\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathfrak{g}(v)|\lesssim\|\mu^{-\frac{q^{\prime}}{2}}\mathfrak{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}},\quad 0<q^{\prime}<1.

Due to the strong singularity, it is hard to establish above estimate for

-3<\kappa\leq\frac{3}{2}
. In this paper, by observing the feature of Hilbert expansion on interior parts and viscous boundary layers, we can get the following control

\displaystyle|\mu^{-\frac{q}{2}}\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathfrak{g}(v)|^{2}\lesssim\sum\limits_{0\leq\alpha\leq N}\|\partial_{v}^{\alpha}\{\mu^{-\frac{q}{2}}\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathfrak{g}\}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum\limits_{0\leq\alpha\leq N}\|\nu^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{q^{\prime}}{2}}\partial_{v}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{g}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\,\,N\geq 2,\,0<q<q^{\prime}<1,

(1.32)

by losing velocity derivatives, see section 2.2 for details. We point out that the losing velocity derivatives is natural since the interior parts and viscous boundary layers always possess enought regularity with respect to
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

The construction of Knudsen layers is more delicate for Boltzmann equation of soft potentials. Noting (
1.22
), to solve the Knudsen layer, it is equivalent to study the following linear boundary value problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{3}\partial_{\eta}f+{\nu^{0}}(v)f-K^{0}f=\mathfrak{g},\\
f(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=f(0,R_{\eta}v),\\
\lim\limits_{\eta\rightarrow\infty}f(\eta,v)=0.\end{cases}

(1.33)

Especially, by noting the right hand side (RHS) of (
1.22
), we have to get at least space polynomial decay for the solution of (
1.33
) to continue the construction of Hilbert expansion. For hard sphere case, one can obtain even exponential decay with the help of strong effect of collision frequency
\nu(v)\cong 1+|v|
. However, it is hard for the cases of soft potentials since the effect of collision frequency
\nu(v)\cong(1+|v|)^{\kappa}\to 0
as
|v|\to\infty
is very weak.

To solve (
1.33
), we first establish the
a prior
uniform
L^{\infty}
estimate for an approximate problem (see (
3.17
)), i.e.,

\displaystyle\|w_{l}f^{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}}+|w_{l}f^{\lambda}|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{+})}\leq C\Big(\|\sqrt{\nu^{0}}f^{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}_{\eta,v}}+\|(\nu^{0})^{-1}w_{l}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}}+|w_{l+4}r|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{+})}\Big),

(1.34)

see Lemma
3.3
for details. Here we point out that the constant in (
1.34
) is independent of the length of domain
\Omega=[0,d]
. For soft potentials, since the collision effect is weak, the key point is to take the number of collisions with the boundaries to depend on
v
, that is
k=\tilde{k}_{0}|v_{3}|(1+|v|)^{|\kappa|}
with
\tilde{k}_{0}\gg 1
.

Under some constraint conditions, we can have the following
L^{2}_{\eta,v}
decay estimate by lossing velocity weight arguments

\int_{0}^{d}(1+\eta)^{n}\|w_{l}\bar{f}\|_{\nu}^{2}d\eta\leq C_{n}\int_{0}^{d}(1+\eta)^{2p_{n}}\|w_{l+2n+2}g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}d\eta,\quad p_{n}>\frac{n}{2}+1,

(1.35)

see Lemmas
3.9
-
3.10
for details. For the space decay rate in
L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}
, we multiply (
1.33
) by
(1+\eta)^{n}
to obtain

\displaystyle v_{3}\partial_{\eta}\{(1+\eta)^{n}f\}+{\nu^{0}}(v)\{(1+\eta)^{n}f\}-K^{0}\{(1+\eta)^{n}f\}=(1+\eta)^{n}\mathfrak{g}+nv_{3}(1+\eta)^{n-1}f,

which yields that

\displaystyle\|w_{l}\,(1+\eta)^{n}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{l+4}\,(1+\eta)^{n-1}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}}+\|(\nu^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\eta)^{n}f\|_{L^{2}_{\eta,v}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|(\nu^{0})^{-1}w_{l}\,(1+\eta)^{n}\mathfrak{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}}.

Then, using (
1.35
) and an induction arguments on
n
, we finally obtain that

\displaystyle\|w_{l}\,(1+\eta)^{n}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}}\lesssim\|w_{l+4n+4}\,(1+\eta)^{q_{n}}\mathfrak{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}},\quad\text{for}\ q_{n}>n+\frac{3}{2}.

With above estimates, we obtain the existence of Knudsen boundary layer problem with enough space decay estimate in
L^{\infty}_{\eta,v}
.

With the help of above estimates on
\mathbf{L}^{-1}
and Knudsen boundary layer,
by the same arguments as in
[
20
]
, we can establish Hilbert expansion of Boltzmann equation of soft potentials with multi-scales in half-space.

The paper is organized as follows. In section

Comparing zero-parameter theories for the WCA and harmonic-repulsive melting lines
Jeppe C. Dyre, Ulf R. Pedersen

Abstract
The melting line of the Weeks-Chandler-Andersen (WCA) system was recently determined accurately and compared to the predictions of four analytical hard-sphere approximations [Attia et al. , J. Chem. Phys. 157 , 034502 (2022)]. Here, we study an alternative zero-parameter prediction based on the isomorph theory, the input of which relate to properties at a single reference state point on the melting line. The two central assumptions made are that the harmonic-repulsive potential approximates the WCA potential and that pair collisions are uncorrelated. The new approach gives excellent predictions at high temperatures, while the hard-sphere-theory based predictions are better at lower temperatures. Supplementing the WCA investigation, the face-centered-crystal to fluid coexistence line is determined for a system of harmonic-repulsive particles and compared to the zero-parameter theories. The results indicate that the excellent isomorph-theory predictions for the WCA potential at higher temperatures may be partly due to a cancellation of errors between the two above-mentioned assumptions.

Introduction

A central result of classical theories of liquids is that fluid states’ structure, dynamics, and statistical properties are determined mainly by short-ranged repulsive forces, while long-range attractions play second fiddle
Bernal 1964
;
Widom 1967
;
Andersen et al. 1971
;
Chandler et al. 1983
;
Berthier and Tarjus 2009
;
Pedersen et al. 2010
;

;

;
Dyre 2016
;
Chattoraj and Ciamarra 2020
;
Nandi and Bhattacharyya 2021
;
Singh and Singh 2021
;
Toxvaerd 2021
. This simplification is behind the success of classical hard-sphere (HS) theories
Barker and Henderson 1967
;
Barker and Henderson 1976
;
Andersen et al. 1971
;
Heyes and Okumura 2006
;
Attia et al. 2021
and, alternatively, the mapping to harsh effective inverse power-law potential
Hoover et al. 1971
;
Stishov 1975
;
Young 1977
;
Hummel et al. 2015
. Both approaches predict structure, dynamics, and statistical properties that are effective functions of a single parameter in the two-dimensional thermodynamic phase diagram. For HS theories the single parameter is the effective HS packing fraction. Several criteria have been suggested for determining this quantity for a given state point (and model); this is in general a subtle problem, the solution of which
de facto
determines the configurational adiabats of the system in question
Rosenfeld 1977
;
Rosenfeld 1999
. The oldest approximation for determining the effective HS radius dates back to Boltzmann’s thesis
Boltzmann 1864
, which proposed that the HS diameter is the shortest distance obtained by two particles colliding head-on with average thermal velocity. In the 1960’s, Barker & Henderson, Andersen & Weeks & Chandler, and others developed successful theories based on more rigorous thermodynamic arguments.

Since the onset of the present millennium, high-pressure experiments by several groups have established that liquid dynamics is often a function of a single thermodynamic parameter. This has been motivated by mapping to a harsh inverse power-law pair potential
Roland et al. 2005
;
Gundermann et al. 2011
. The isomorph theory generalizes the idea of an effective single-parameter phase diagram if the potential energy function
U(\mathbf{R})
obeys hidden scale invariance
Gnan et al. 2009
;
Schrøder and Dyre 2014
. This is the property that the ordering of configurations according to their potential energy at one density is maintained upon a uniform scaling of all coordinates, expressed in the logical implication

U({\mathbf{R}}_{\rm a})<U({\mathbf{R}}_{\rm b})\Rightarrow U(\lambda{\mathbf{R}}_{\rm a})<U(\lambda{\mathbf{R}}_{\rm b})

Schrøder and Dyre 2014
. While the isomorph theory is mathematically more abstract than the classic HS ideas, it has the advantage of not referring to a specific mapping of pair interactions by viewing the energy landscape in a more holistic way.

III
The harmonic-repulsive phase-transition lines at low temperatures

WCA

Attia et al. 2022

;
Pedersen et al. 2013
;
Pedersen et al. 2015

;
Kofke 1993b
;
Frenkel and Smit 2002
(the data for the coexistence line are available at

IV
Isomorph-theory predictions

Predictions for the thermodynamics of freezing and melting can be made within the framework of isomorph theory
Pedersen et al. 2016
. Isomorphs are lines in the phase diagram along which the excess entropy
S_{\text{ex}}
– the entropy in excess of the ideal gas at the same density and temperature – is constant. Such lines are only termed isomorphs, however, if the system obeys hidden scale invariance (Sec. I) to a good approximation because only in this case are the structure and dynamics predicted to be invariant along isomorphs. Hidden scale invariance can be checked by evaluating the virial potential-energy correlation coefficient
R

Pedersen et al. 2008
;
Gnan et al. 2009
at the relevant state points: if
R
is close to unity, the isomorph theory applies. For more information, we refer the interested reader to Refs.
Bailey et al. 2008
;
Gnan et al. 2009
;
Schrøder and Dyre 2014
;
Dyre 2014
;
Dyre 2018
; it suffices here to mention that for instance the Lennard-Jones, the Yukawa (screened Coulomb), and the EXP system all have isomorphs in their liquid and solid phases. Interestingly, it was recently shown that the WCA system has isomorphs throughout its entire phase diagram because
R>\sqrt{8/3\pi}=0.92\ldots

Attia et al. 2021
.

In the following we apply the isomorph theory of freezing
Pedersen et al. 2016
to the
WCA
system.

\displaystyle p_{\star}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{B}T/[\rho_{l}^{-1}(T)-\rho_{s}^{-1}(T)].

(24)

We have here redefined the
C

\alpha

p_{\star}
that has the unit of pressure. The dashed lines in Fig.

IV.2
Analytical prediction from the isomorph theory

To provide a closed form of Eqs. (
20
)-(
24
) we need analytical expressions for the temperature dependence of the potential energy and the density along the fluid and solid isomorphs. We will do this by the mean-field approach discussed in Ref.
Attia et al. 2021
(exact in infinite dimensions
Maimbourg and Kurchan 2016
).

First, we investigate when a mean-field is appropriate by defining a kissing neighbor as one where the pair distance is shorter than the truncation distance of the pair potential.
The solid red line in Fig.
8
shows the relative frequency of particles with none or a single kissing neighbor along the liquid side of the coexistence line. The analysis shows that it is reasonable to assume that particle collisions are uncorrelated at the lowest temperatures of this study since particles predominantly have none or a single kissing neighbor, i.e., collisions are uncorrelated at low temperatures.

In general, the partition function for configurational degrees of freedom is given by

Z=\int_{V^{N}}d{\bf r}_{1}\ldots d{\bf r}_{N}\exp(-\sum_{i>j}v(r_{ij})/k_{B}T).

However, when collisions are uncorrelated, we can treat interactions in a mean-field way and write the partition function as

Z=Z_{s}^{N}

(25)

where
Z_{s}
is the partition function of a single particle moving in the potential
v_{s}({\bf r})
of all other particles frozen in space.
Z_{s}
has two contributions, one where the moving particle is not kissing and one where it is kissing one other particle. The former is the free volume that we will approximate by the entire volume (a low-density approximation), and the latter is
N
times the integral

Z_{1}=\int_{0}^{r_{c}}4\pi r^{2}\exp(-v(r)/k_{B}T)dr.

(26)

Thus, the single-particle partition function can be written as

Z_{s}/N=Z_{1}+\rho^{-1}.

(27)

To reach a closed form of
Z_{1}
(Eq. (
26
)), we recall that at low temperatures (near the phase transition), WCA particles interact only at distances close to the truncation length
r_{c}
, see Eq. (
5
). With this, we can get a closed form of
Z_{1}
since it can be written as a Gaussian integral (see Appendix
A
).

We now have a mean-field theory for the partition function (
Z
) that can be used to make predictions of thermodynamic quantities. As an example, in Appendix
A
we derive the mean-field prediction of the energy, and in Ref.
Attia et al. 2021
we show that

\gamma\equiv\left.\frac{d\log T}{d\log\rho}\right|_{S_{\text{ex}}}

(28)

in the low-temperature limit is given by

\gamma(T)=\frac{4r_{c}\sqrt{2k_{2}}}{9\sqrt{\pi k_{B}T}}\,\,\,(T\to 0)\,.

(29)

By integration of
\gamma(T)
using
\exp(s)\cong 1+s
for
s\to 0
one finds

\rho_{l}(T)=\rho_{l}(T_{0})\left(1+\frac{9\sqrt{\pi}[\sqrt{k_{B}T}-\sqrt{k_{B}T_{0}}]}{2r_{c}\sqrt{2k_{2}}}\right)

(30)

and a similar expression for
\rho_{s}(T)
, where
\rho_{s}(T_{0})=0.778963\sigma^{-3}
and
\rho_{l}(T_{0})=0.706395\sigma^{-3}
. In Appendix
A
we show that the low-temperature limit of the potential energy is proportional to
T^{3/2}
. Thus, using information from the reference state point only, one can write the temperature dependence of the potential energy as

u_{l}(T)=u_{l}(T_{0})[T/T_{0}]^{3/2}\,\,\,(T\to 0)

(31)

and a similar expression exists for
u_{s}(T)
where
u_{s}(T_{0})=5.51203\times 10^{-3}\varepsilon
and
u_{l}(T_{0})=6.33750\times 10^{-3}\varepsilon
.

V
Discussion

The
WCA

). Two essential assumptions enter into the deviation:

(A)

Particle collisions are uncorrelated (Fig.
8
);

(B)

The
WCA
potential can be approximated by the harmonic-repulsive potential (Eq. (
5
); Fig.

).

Figure

show that the former is valid at
T<0.03k_{B}/\varepsilon
(pragmatically defined as where the red and blue lines cross in Fig.

).

To analyze the validity of the latter approximation we conduct a comparative study of the two modes. To this aim, Fig.

(a) shows the isomorph theory prediction (green dashed) of the harmonic-repulsive melting line (solid black). In the same figure, the red dashed line is the zero-parameter prediction obtained by inserting into Eq. (

;

The blue dashed line is the low-temperature HS prediction derived from Barker-Henderson/Andersen-Weeks-Chandler assumptions (Eq. (

Moreover, compared to the harmonic-repulsive melting line, the theories predict a lower pressure. As mentioned, this low pressure likely results from the ignored many-body effects, i.e., from the above mention assumption (A). This suggests that the good overall isomorph predictions result in part from the cancellation of assumptions (A) and (B).

VI
Summary

We have developed an isomorph-theory-based zero-parameter prediction for the melting lines of the harmonic-repulsive and
WCA
potentials. The new theory generalizes the idea of an effective one-dimensional phase diagram from HS theories without referring to a specific reference potential. In effect, the shape of the coexistence line can be evaluated using any of its points as reference state point. We have shown how to generate an analytical prediction using the theory for the shape of the isomorph presented in Ref.
Attia et al. 2021
. Alternatively, we note that Bøhling
et al.

Bøhling et al. 2014
provided an approach to get an analytic expression for the shape of the isomorph for any pair-potential, which provides an alternative way to arrive at a closed-form expression of the melting line. We leave this line of reasoning for the future.

Appendix A
Temperature dependence of energy

In the main part of the paper, we use the approximation
u(T)\propto T^{\frac{3}{2}}
at low temperatures. Here, that approximation is justified.

In Ref.
Attia et al. 2021
we show that in the low-density, the low-temperature limit of a pairwise quantity
A(r)
, which is zero for
r>r_{c}
, has an expectation value that is computed as is

\langle A\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{s}(\rho,T)}\int_{0}^{r_{c}}A(r)p(r)dr

(38)

where

p(r)=4\pi r^{2}\exp[-v(r)/k_{B}T]

(39)

is the unnormalized probability,

Z_{s}(\rho,T)/N=Z_{1}(T)+\frac{1}{\rho}

(40)

(see Eq. (
27
)) and

Z_{1}=\int_{0}^{r_{c}}p(r)dr.

(41)

To give an approximation of

u(T)=\langle v\rangle

(42)

for the pair potential
v(r)=\frac{k_{2}}{2}(r_{c}-r)^{2}
we need to evaluate integrals of the form

\displaystyle I_{m}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r_{c}}[v(r)]^{m}p(r)dr

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi k_{2}^{m}}{2^{m}}\int_{0}^{r_{c}}(r_{c}-r)^{2m}r^{2}\exp\left[-\frac{k_{2}(r_{c}-r)^{2}}{2k_{B}T}\right]dr

From Eq. (
38
) the expectation value of the energy per particle (
\langle v\rangle
=
\langle U\rangle/N
) can be written as

\langle v\rangle=\frac{I_{1}(T)}{I_{0}(T)+\frac{1}{\rho}}\,.

(44)

By substitution of

t=(r_{c}-r)^{2}/\tilde{T}

(45)

where

\tilde{T}\equiv 2k_{B}T/k_{2}

(46)

so
r=r_{c}-\tilde{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
dr=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}dt
, we can write the integral as

I_{m}(T)=\frac{4\pi k_{2}^{m}}{2^{m+1}}\int_{0}^{r_{c}^{2}/\tilde{T}}\left[r_{c}^{2}\tilde{T}^{m+\frac{1}{2}}t^{m-\frac{1}{2}}+\tilde{T}^{m+\frac{3}{2}}t^{m+\frac{1}{2}}+2r_{c}\tilde{T}^{m+1}t^{m}\right]\exp(-t)dt

(47)

In the low-temperature limit,
\tilde{T}\to 0
, the integral approaches

I_{m}(T)=\frac{4\pi r_{c}^{2}k_{2}^{m}}{2^{m+1}}\tilde{T}^{m+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\exp(-t)dt\,.

(48)

Thus, the integral of the above approaches the gamma function,

\Gamma(z)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}\exp(-t)dt\,,

(49)

leading to

I_{m}(T)=\frac{4\pi r_{c}^{2}k_{2}^{m}}{2^{m+1}}\tilde{T}^{m+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,.

(50)

From this we get

I_{0}(T)=2\pi r_{c}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi k_{B}T}{k_{2}}}

(51)

and

I_{1}(T)=\frac{r_{c}^{2}k_{2}}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi k_{B}T}{k_{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

(52)

At low temperatures, the denominator of Eq. (
44
) approaches
1/\rho
since
I_{0}(T)\propto\sqrt{T}
. The change of
\rho
in this study is in the order of 10% while the enumerator of Eq. (
44
),
I_{1}(T)\propto T^{\frac{3}{2}}
changes orders of magnitude. This justifies the approximation

u(T)\propto T^{\frac{3}{2}}

(53)

used in the main part of the paper.

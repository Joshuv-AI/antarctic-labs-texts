Arthur’s Conjectures and the Orbit Method for Real Reductive Groups
Lucas Mason-Brown

Abstract
. The first half of this article is expository—I will review, with examples, the main statements of the Langlands classification and Arthur’s conjectures for real reductive groups as formulated in [ ABV92 ] . In the second half, I will turn my attention to the Orbit Method, a conjectural scheme for classifying irreducible unitary representations of a real reductive group. I will give a definition of the Orbit Method in the case when the group is complex. The main input is the theory of unipotent ideals and Harish-Chandra bimodules, developed in [ LMM21 ] . I will show that the Orbit Method I define is related to Arthur’s conjectures via a natural duality map. Finally, I will sketch a possible generalization of this Orbit Method for arbitrary real groups.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a complex connected reductive algebraic group and let
\sigma
be a real form of
G
(i.e. an anti-holomorphic involution
\sigma:G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G
). Consider the set

\Pi(G,\sigma):=\{\text{equivalence classes of irreducible admissible representations of }G^{\sigma}\}.

The local aim over
\mathbb{R}
of the Langlands correspondence is to parameterize this set. This problem was solved completely by Langlands, Knapp, and Zuckerman. More precisely, Langlands in
[
Lan89
]
introduced some finite sets called
L
-packets
which partition
\Pi(G,\sigma)
. The representations in these packets were later parameterized by the work of Knapp and Zuckerman (
[
KZ82
]
). Taken together, these results provide a complete paramaterization of
\Pi(G,\sigma)
, called the
Langlands classification for real reductive groups
. In Section
2.1
, I will recall the main statements of this classification, as formulated in
[
ABV92
]
.

Each irreducible representation
\pi\in\Pi(G,\sigma)
can be realized as the unique irreducible quotient of a
standard representation
(this is a representation which is induced from a certain (limit of) discrete series representation of a Levi subgroup of
G^{\sigma}
).
The standard and irreducible representations of
G^{\sigma}
form two distinct bases, in natural bijection, for the Grothendieck group of finite-length representations of
G^{\sigma}
. A problem which is distinct from, but related to, the classification of
\Pi(G,\sigma)
is to compute the matrix which relates these two bases (it turns out this problem is essentially equivalent to the problem of computing irreducible characters, see Section
2.2
below). The classical formulation of the Langlands classification does not address this problem (and it is not well-suited for doing so). In
[
ABV92
]
, Adams, Barbasch, and Vogan introduced a reformulation of the Langlands classification which relates this change of basis matrix to the geometry of a certain algebraic variety of modified Langlands parameters.

An irreducible admissible representation
\pi\in\Pi(G,\sigma)
is
unitary
if it admits a positive-definite invariant Hermitian form. Write

\Pi_{u}(G,\sigma)\subset\Pi(G,\sigma)

for the subset of irreducible unitary representations of
G^{\sigma}
. A basic problem in representation theory is to identify this subset. This problem is open—it is perhaps the most fundamental unsolved problem in the representation theory of Lie groups. There are two main paradigms for approaching this problem:
Arthur’s conjectures
and
the Orbit Method
. Both seek to parameterize the unitary dual (or at least, a large chunk of it) in terms of Lie-theoretic data.

In a bit more detail, both approaches predict the existence of certain finite sets (or packets) of irreducible unitary representations, which should satisfy various desiderata and should exhaust (a large portion of)
\Pi_{u}(G,\sigma)
. In the case of Arthur’s conjectures, these packets are indexed by so-called
Arthur parameters
—certain Lie-theoretic objects related to the Langlands dual group. In the case of the Orbit Method, these packets are indexed by (roughly speaking) finite covers of co-adjoint
G^{\sigma}
-orbits.

In
[
ABV92
]
, Adams, Barbasch, and Vogan proposed a definition of Arthur packets for arbitrary groups involving microlocal geometry on their space of Langlands parameters. Their packets have been shown to possess all of the expected properties,
except for unitarity
, which remains an open question. I will review their construction in Section
3
.

The Orbit Method picture is much less developed. The corresponding packets have not yet been defined in general. In Section
4
, I will define them in the case when
G^{\sigma}
is complex. I will also sketch a generalization for arbitrary groups. These definitions are based on recent joint work with Ivan Losev and Dmitryo Matvieievksyi (
[
LMM21
]
) on filtered quantizations of nilpotent covers.

There are some structural similarities between the predictions of Arthur’s conjectures and those of the Orbit Method. So it is natural to look for a relationship between these two points of view. In that direction, I will define for complex groups a natural duality map

\mathsf{D}:\{\text{Arthur parameters}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\{\text{covers of co-adjoint orbits}\}

which ‘intertwines’ Arthur’s conjectures and the Orbit Method, see Section
4.5
.

2.
Langlands classification

2.1.
Langlands classification: classical formulation

Let
G^{\vee}
be the (complex reductive) dual group of
G
, and let
G^{L}
be the
L
-group associated to the inner class of
\sigma
, see
[
Bor79
, Section 2.1]
. If we write
\Gamma
for the Galois group of
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}
, there is a short exact sequence of algebraic groups

1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Gamma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}1

Let
W_{\mathbb{R}}
be the Weil group of
\mathbb{R}
. Recall that
W_{\mathbb{R}}
is a (disconnected) Lie group generated by
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and a single element
j
, subject to the relations

j^{2}=1,\qquad jzj=\bar{z}

So there is a short exact sequence of Lie groups

1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}W_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\Gamma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}1.

Definition 2.1.1
.

A Langlands parameter for
G^{L}
is a continuous homomorphism

\varphi:W_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{L}

such that

(i)

the image of
\varphi
consists of semisimple elements, and

(ii)

the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{\mathbb{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G^{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

Write
P(G^{L})
for the set of Langlands parameters for
G^{L}
and write
\Phi(G^{L})
for the set of
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy classes in
P(G^{L})
. A Langlands parameter is
tempered
if the image of
\varphi
is bounded. Write
P_{\mathrm{temp}}(G^{L})
for the set of tempered Langlands parameters and
\Phi_{\mathrm{temp}}(G^{L})
for the set of
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy classes in
P_{\mathrm{temp}}(G^{L})
.

Since
W_{\mathbb{R}}
is generated by
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
j
, a Langlands parameter is determined by its differential at the identity and its value at
j
. Its differential at the identity is given by a pair of commuting semisimple elements in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
(encoding the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic components of the differential) and its value at
j
is a semisimple element in the non-identity component of
G^{L}
. The requirement that these elements determine a group homomorphism imposes a small number of additional conditions. The result of this analysis is a very concrete Lie-theoretic description of Langlands parameters.

Proposition 2.1.2

(Proposition 5.6,
[
ABV92
]
)
.

There is a natural
G^{\vee}
-equivariant bijection between
P(G^{L})
and pairs
(y,\lambda)
such that

(i)

y
is a semisimple element in
G^{L}\setminus G^{\vee}
.

(ii)

\lambda
is a semisimple element in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
.

(iii)

y^{2}=\exp(2\pi i\lambda)
.

(iv)

[\lambda,\Ad(y)\lambda]=0
.

The map
\varphi\mapsto(\lambda(\varphi),y(\varphi))
is defined as follows:
\lambda(\varphi)
is the holomorphic part of
d\varphi:\mathbb{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
and
y(\varphi)=\exp(\pi i\lambda(\varphi))\phi(j)
.

Theorem 2.1.3

(
[
Lan89
]
)
.

For each Langlands parameter
\varphi\in P(G^{L})
, there is an associated finite (possibly empty) set
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)\subset\Pi(G,\sigma)
called the
L-packet
for
\varphi
such that

(i)

Two packets are equal (if parameters are conjugate) or disjoint (otherwise).

(ii)

The packets partition
\Pi(G,\sigma)
.

The construction of the packets
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)
is complicated and technical, we refer the reader to
[
Lan89
, Section 3]
or
[
Bor79
, Section III.10]
. The construction partially reduces (via parabolic induction and formation of discrete series) to the construction for tori, where it can be verified by hand.

The next proposition describes the constistuents of
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)
in terms of the pair
(y,\lambda)
. It is an easy consequence of the construction of
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)
.

Proposition 2.1.4
.

Suppose
\varphi\in P(G^{L})
and let
(y,\lambda)=(y(\varphi),\lambda(\varphi))
be the corresponding pair (cf. Proposition
2.1.2
). Choose maximal torus
H^{\vee}\subset G^{\vee}
such that
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}
and
\Ad(y)
normalizes
H^{\vee}
. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

some representation in
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)
is a (limit of) discrete series.

(ii)

all representations in
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)
are (limits of) discrete series.

(iii)

\Ad(y)
acts by inversion on
H^{\vee}
.

and the following are equivalent

(i)

some representation in
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)
is tempered.

(ii)

all representations in
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)
are tempered.

(iii)

\lambda+\Ad(y)\lambda\in X_{*}(H^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}i\mathbb{R}
.

(iv)

\varphi
is tempered (cf. Definition
2.1.1
).

Note that
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}\simeq\mathfrak{h}^{*}
determines an infinitesimal character for
\mathfrak{g}
by means of the Harish-Chandra isomorphism. All of the representations in
\Pi_{\varphi}(G,\sigma)
have infinitesimal character
\lambda
.

2.2.
Langlands classification: ABV formulation

A fundamental invariant of an admissible representation is its
distribution character
. A natural question is the following:

Question 2.2.1
.

Let
\pi\in\Pi(G,\sigma)
. Can we compute the character of
\pi
from its (complete) Langlands parameter?

In
[
Lan89
]
, Langlands introduced the notion of a
standard representation
. This is a finite-length admissible representations which is parabolically induced from a certain (limit of) relative discrete series representation (see
[
Lan89
, Section 3]
or
[
ABV92
, Chapter 11]
). Assuming still that
G
is adjoint, write

\displaystyle M(G,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\{\text{equivalence classes of standard representations of }G^{\sigma}\}

\displaystyle M(G,[\sigma])

\displaystyle:=\bigsqcup_{\sigma^{\prime}\in[\sigma]}M(G,\sigma^{\prime})

Each standard representation has a unique irreducible quotient. This defines a bijection

M(G,\sigma)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi(G,\sigma).

Composing with the bijection of Theorem
2.1.7
, we get a further bijection

M:\Phi^{c}(G^{L})\xrightarrow{\sim}M(G,[\sigma])

Write

\displaystyle K(G,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\text{Grothendieck group of finite-length representations of }G^{\sigma}

\displaystyle K(G,[\sigma])

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma^{\prime}\in[\sigma]}K(G,\sigma^{\prime})

Of course,
\Pi(G,[\sigma])
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
K(G,[\sigma])
. Thus, we can write

M(\varphi,\tau)=\sum_{(\varphi^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\in\Phi^{c}(G^{L})}m_{r}((\varphi,\tau),(\varphi^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}))\pi(\varphi^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}),\qquad(\varphi,\tau)\in\Phi^{c}(G^{L})

Here,
m_{r}=\{m_{r}((\varphi,\tau),(\varphi^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}))\mid(\varphi,\tau),(\varphi^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\in\Phi^{c}(G^{L})\}
is an (infinite) square matrix with nonnegative integer entries. In fact, if we choose an appropriate ordering on
\Phi^{c}(G^{L})
, this matrix is upper triangular with ones along the diagonal (see
[
Vog81
, Proposition 6.6.7]
). Consequently,
m_{r}
is invertible and
M(G,[\sigma])
forms a second
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
K(G,[\sigma])
. Crucially, the distribution characters of the standard representations
M(G,[\sigma])
are relatively easy to compute. Thus, Question
2.2.1
becomes.

Question 2.2.2
.

Can we compute the (infinite, square) change of basis matrix
m_{r}
?

A powerful idea, implicit in the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjectures, is that the change-of-basis matrix
m_{r}
should be related to the singularities of the
G^{\vee}
-orbit closures in the space
P(G^{L})
of Langlands parameters. Unfortunately, we find that all
G^{\vee}
-orbits in
P(G^{L})
are closed (this is because the image in
G^{L}
of a Langlands parameter
\varphi:W_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{L}
consists of
semisimple
elements). So the
G^{\vee}
-orbit closures in
P(G^{L})
are non-singular. The solution, proposed in
[
ABV92
]
, is to modify the definition of a Langlands parameter to get a more interesting geometry.

Given a semisimple element
\lambda\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
, define the reductive subalgebra

\mathfrak{m}(\lambda):=Z_{\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}}(\exp(2\pi i\lambda))\qquad\text{(subalgebra corresponding to integral roots)}.

There is a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\mathfrak{m}(\lambda)

\mathfrak{m}(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{m}(\lambda)_{n},\qquad\mathfrak{m}(\lambda)_{n}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{m}(\lambda)\mid[\lambda,X]=nX\}.

We get a parabolic subalgebra

\mathfrak{p}(\lambda)=\mathfrak{l}(\lambda)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(\lambda),\qquad\mathfrak{l}(\lambda)=\mathfrak{m}(\lambda)_{0},\qquad\mathfrak{u}(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{n>0}\mathfrak{m}(\lambda)_{n}.

The
canonical flat
through
\lambda
is the affine subspace of
\mathfrak{m}(\lambda)

F(\lambda):=\lambda+\mathfrak{u}(\lambda)=P(\lambda)\lambda\subset\mathfrak{m}(\lambda).

Since
\exp(2\pi i\lambda)
is central in
M^{\vee}
, the function
\exp(2\pi i\bullet)
is constant on
F(\lambda)
.

Definition 2.2.3

(Definitions 6.9, 7.6,
[
ABV92
]
)
.

A
geometric parameter
is a pair
(y,F)
such that

(i)

y
is a semisimple element in
G^{L}\setminus G^{\vee}
.

(ii)

F
is a canonical flat in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
.

(iii)

y^{2}=\exp(2\pi iF)
.

Write
X(G^{L})
for the set of geometric parameters and
\Xi(G^{L})
for the set of
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy classes in
X(G^{L})
. A
complete geometric parameter
is a pair
(\xi,\tau)
consisting of a geometric parameter
\xi\in X(G^{L})
and an irreducible representation of the component group of
Z_{G^{\vee}}(\xi)
. Write
X^{c}(G^{L})
for the set of complete geometric parameters and
\Xi^{c}(G^{L})
for the set of
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy classes in
X^{c}(G^{L})
.

Proposition 2.2.4

(Proposition 6.17,
[
ABV92
]
)
.

The natural map

P(G^{L})\twoheadrightarrow X(G^{L}),\qquad(y,\lambda)\mapsto(y,F(\lambda))

induces a bijection

(2.2.1)

\Phi(G^{L})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Xi(G^{L})

If
(y,\lambda)\in P(G^{L})
, then
Z_{G^{\vee}}(y,\lambda)
is a maximal reductive subgroup of
Z_{G^{\vee}}(y,F(\lambda))
. In particular, there is a canonical isomorphism

Z_{G^{\vee}}(y,\lambda)/Z_{G^{\vee}}(y,\lambda)^{\circ}\xrightarrow{\sim}Z_{G^{\vee}}(y,F(\lambda))/Z_{G^{\vee}}(y,F(\lambda))^{\circ}

So (
2.2.1
) lifts to a bijection

(2.2.2)

\Phi^{c}(G^{L})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Xi^{c}(G^{L})

Composing the bijection of Theorem
2.1.7
with (
2.2.2
), we get a bijection

\pi:\Xi^{c}(G^{L})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi(G,[\sigma])

In contrast to
P(G^{L})
, the set
X(G^{L})
of geometric parameters has an interesting geometry, which we will now describe. There is a decomposition

X(G^{L})=\bigsqcup_{\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}}X(\mathbb{O},G^{L}),\qquad X(\mathbb{O},G^{L})=\{(y,F)\in X(G^{L})\mid F\subset\mathbb{O}\}

where the disjoint union runs over all semisimple
G^{\vee}
-orbits
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
. The pieces
X(\mathbb{O},G^{L})
can be built out of the following spaces

\displaystyle F(\mathbb{O})

\displaystyle=\{F(\lambda)\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{O}\}

\displaystyle e(\mathbb{O})

\displaystyle=\{\exp(2\pi i\lambda)\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{O}\}

\displaystyle\sqrt{e(\mathbb{O})}

\displaystyle=\{y\in G^{L}\setminus G^{\vee}\mid y^{2}\in e(\mathbb{O})\}

Note that
G^{\vee}
acts by conjugation on all three spaces above.

The following proposition tells us all that we need to know about the geometry of
X(\mathbb{O},G^{L})
. The main conclusion is that the
G^{\vee}
-orbit structure on
X(\mathbb{O},G^{L})
is completely determined by the orbit structure of a symmetric subgroup acting on a (partial) flag variety.

Proposition 2.2.5

(Proposition 6.16,
[
ABV92
]
)
.

The spaces
F(\mathbb{O})
,
e(\mathbb{O})
, and
\sqrt{e(\mathbb{O})}
are algebraic varieties with
G^{\vee}
-action. The spaces
F(\mathbb{O})
and
e(\mathbb{O})
are homogeneous for
G^{\vee}
. If we fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{O}
, there are
G^{\vee}
-equivariant isomorphisms

(i)

G^{\vee}/P(\lambda)\xrightarrow{\sim}F(\mathbb{O})
.

(ii)

G^{\vee}/M(\lambda)\xrightarrow{\sim}e(\mathbb{O})
.

The space
\sqrt{e(\mathbb{O})}
has finitely many
G^{\vee}
-orbits, denoted
\sqrt{e(\mathbb{O})}_{i}
. For each
i
, choose
y_{i}\in\sqrt{e(\mathbb{O})}_{i}
with

(Conjecture 1.3.2,

]
)
.

4.
The Orbit Method

Let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be the real points of a connected reductive algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{R}
(since we will be working in this section with a
single
real form of
G
, we will drop the symbol ‘
\sigma
’ from all of our notation). Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
be the Lie algebra of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
. Note that
G_{\mathbb{R}}
acts on the real vector space
\Hom_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}},i\mathbb{R})
. By a
real co-adjoint orbit
we will mean a
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-orbit
\mathbb{O}
on
\Hom_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}},i\mathbb{R})
and by a
real co-adjoint cover
we will mean a homogeneous
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-space
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
equipped with a finite
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-equivariant map
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{O}
to a real co-adjoint orbit. Write
\mathsf{Orb}^{i\mathbb{R}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
(resp.
\mathsf{Cov}^{i\mathbb{R}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
) for the set of real co-adjoint orbits (resp. isomorphism classes of real co-adjoint covers). The
orbit method
is a guiding philosophy in unitary representation theory, originating in the work of Kirillov (
[
Kir62
]
) and Kostant (
[
Kos70
]
). Here is a (somewhat precise) formulation due to Vogan (
[
Vog00
]
).

Conjecture 4.0.1
.

To each real co-adjoint cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\in\mathsf{Cov}^{i\mathbb{R}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
(satisfying a suitable integrality condition) there is an associated finite set of irreducible unitary representations

\Pi^{\mathsf{Kir}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})\subset\Pi_{u}(G_{\mathbb{R}}).

The union

\bigcup_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}\Pi^{\mathsf{Kir}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})

should exhaust most of
\Pi_{u}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
.

There are several problems with Conjecture
4.0.1
(they are problems with our simplified formulation, not with the discussion in
[
Vog00
]
):

(1)

The ‘integrality condition’ and the phrase ‘most of
\Pi_{u}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
’ have not been precisely defined.

(2)

In some cases, we also expect packets attached to
unions
of co-adjoint covers.

We will return to Problem 1 in Section
4.2
. Problem 2 will not concern us (it does not arise in the case of complex groups, which is the main case will consider).

Below, we will define the orbit method in the case of
complex groups
. So let
G
be a complex connected reductive algebraic group, regarded as a real Lie group by restriction of scalars. Of course,
G
is the real points of a complex connected reductive algebraic group (isomorphic to the product
G\times G
), but to simplify the notation we will write
\Pi(G)
(resp.
\Pi_{u}(G)
, resp.
\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi}(G)
) instead of
\Pi(G\times G,\sigma)
(resp.
\Pi_{u}(G\times G,\sigma)
, resp.
\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi}(G\times G,\sigma)
).

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
denote the
complex
dual space, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}^{*}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{C})
. By a
complex co-adjoint orbit
we will mean a
G
-orbit
\mathbb{O}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and by a
complex co-adjoint cover
we will mean a homogeneous
G
-space
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
equipped with a finite
G
-equovariant map
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{O}
to a complex co-adjoint orbit. Write
\mathsf{Orb}(G)
(resp.
\mathsf{Cov}(G)
) for the set of isomorphism classes of complex co-adjoint orbits (resp. co-adjoint covers). A co-adjoint cover is
nilpotent
if the underlying orbit is nilpotent. To define our orbit method, it will be convenient to relate real and complex co-adjoint covers. This is accomplished using the following easy lemma. The trivial proof is omitted.

Lemma 4.0.2
.

If
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, define
\iota(\mu)\in\Hom_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{g},i\mathbb{R})
by

(\iota(\mu))(X)=\mathrm{Im}(\mu(X)).

where
\mathrm{Im}(\bullet)
denotes the imaginary part of a complex number. Then
\mu\mapsto\iota(\mu)
defines a
G
-equivariant isomorphism of real vector spaces

(4.0.1)

\iota:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{g},i\mathbb{R}).

The inverse isomorphism is defined by the formula

\iota^{-1}(\lambda)(X)=\lambda(X)-i\lambda(iX).

The isomorphism
\iota
induces a bijection (also denoted by
\iota
)

(4.0.2)

\iota:\mathsf{Orb}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Orb}^{i\mathbb{R}}(G)

If
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, then the centralizers
G^{\mu}
and
G^{\iota(\mu)}
coincide. So (

) lifts to a bijection (still denoted by
\iota
)

(4.0.3)

\iota:\mathsf{Cov}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Cov}^{i\mathbb{R}}(G)

4.1.
Birational induction

Definition 4.1.1

(Section 7.8,
[
LMM21
]
, see also Definition 1.2,
[
Los16
]
)
.

A
birational induction datum
is a triple

(L,\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L},\xi)

consisting of

•

A Levi subgroup
L\subset G
.

•

A complex nilpotent cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
.

•

An element
\xi\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{*}
.

Note that
G
acts by conjugation on the set of birational induction data. Denote the set of
G
-conjugacy classes by
\Omega(G)
.

We will define a correspondence called
birational induction
from birational induction data to complex co-adjoint covers. This map is essentially defined in
[
Los16
, Section 4]
.

First, choose a parabolic subgroup
P=LN\subset G
and write
\mathfrak{p}^{\perp}
for the annihilator of
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Consider the twisted generalized Springer map

\mu:G\times_{P}(\xi+\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}+\mathfrak{p}^{\perp})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}

Here, we view
\xi+\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}+\mathfrak{p}^{\perp}
as a (closed,
P
-invariant) subset of
\mathfrak{n}^{\perp}
. The image of
\mu
is the closure of a single co-adjoint
G
-orbit, denoted
\Ind(L,\mathbb{O}_{L},\xi)\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
(if
\xi=0
, then
\Ind(L,\mathbb{O}_{L},\xi)
is the induced nilpotent orbit in the sense of
[
LS79
]
).

Next, let
\widetilde{X}_{L}:=\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}])
. This is a normal, irreducible, affine variety with an action of
L
, an open embedding
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\subset\widetilde{X}_{L}
, and a finite
L
-equivariant surjection
\mu_{L}:\widetilde{X}_{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
. Varfieties of this form have been studied extensively in
[
LMM21
]
. Let
P
act on the variety
\{\xi\}\times\widetilde{X}_{L}\times\mathfrak{p}^{\perp}
in the following manner: let
L
act diagonally and
N
by the formula

n\cdot(\xi,x,y)=(\xi,x,n\mu_{L}(x)-\mu_{L}(x)+ny),\quad n\in N,\quad x\in\widetilde{X}_{L},\quad y\in\mathfrak{p}^{\perp},

By our construction of the
P
-action on
\{\xi\}\times\widetilde{X}_{L}\times\mathfrak{p}^{\perp}
, there is a
P
-equivariant map
\{\xi\}\times\widetilde{X}_{L}\times\mathfrak{p}^{\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\xi+\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}+\mathfrak{p}^{\perp}
and hence a
G
-equivariant map
G\times^{P}(\{\xi\}\times\widetilde{X}_{L}\times\mathfrak{p}^{\perp})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G\times^{P}(\xi+\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}+\mathfrak{p}^{\perp})
of fiber bundles over
G/P
. Consider the composition

\widetilde{\mu}:G\times^{P}(\{\xi\}\times\widetilde{X}_{L}\times\mathfrak{p}^{\perp})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G\times^{P}(\xi+\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}+\mathfrak{p}^{\perp})\overset{\mu}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

The image of
\widetilde{\mu}
is the closure of
\Ind(L,\mathbb{O}_{L},\xi)
and the restriction of
\widetilde{\mu}
to the preimage of
\Ind(L,\mathbb{O}_{L},\xi)
is a (finite, connected,
G
-equivariant) cover, denoted
\mathrm{Bind}(L,\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L},\xi)
. We note that the co-adjoint orbit
\Ind(L,\mathbb{O}_{L},\xi)
and its cover

are both independent of the choice of parabolic

, see
[
Los16
, Lemma 4.1]
and
[
Mat20
, Corollary 4.3]
. Moreover, conjugate birational induction data give rise to isomorphic covers. Thus, we obtain a well-defined map

(4.1.1)

\mathrm{Bind}:\Omega(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathsf{Cov}(G)

It is easy to see that this map is surjective.

A co-adjoint cover is
birationally rigid
if it cannot be obtained via birational induction from a proper Levi subgroup (if a cover is birationally rigid, it must be nilpotent). Accordingly, a birational induction datum
(L,\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L},\xi)
is
minimal
if
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
is birationally rigid. Note that the set of minimal birational induction data is stable under conjugation by
G
. Write
\Omega_{m}(G)\subset\Omega(G)
for the subset of
G
-conjugacy classes of minimal birational induction data. The following is contained in
[
LMM21
, Theorem 7.8.1]
(where the proof is adapted from
[
Los16
, Corollary 4.6]
).

Proposition 4.1.2
.

The map (
4.1.1
) restricts to a bijection

\mathrm{Bind}:\Omega_{m}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Cov}(G)

4.3.
Unipotent representations

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
be a complex nilpotent cover. In
[
LMM21
]
, we construct a finite set of irreducible admissible
G
-representations

\mathrm{Unip}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G)\subset\Pi(G)

The constituents of
\mathrm{Unip}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G)
are called
unipotent representations
(this presents a small problem of terminology. These unipotent representations are a priori distinct from the representations attached to unipotent Arthur parameters (Definition
3.0.3
). However, we will see below that the latter set is contained in the former set, so we will not bother to make our terms more precise). We will now briefly review the construction of these sets and some of their essential properties.

The group
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
acts by dilation on
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Suitably normalized, this action can be lifted to the co-adjoint cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
. There is also a natural symplectic structure on
\mathbb{O}
(defined using the Lie bracket). This too can be lifted to
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
. Thus the ring of regular functions
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}]
is a graded Poisson algebra. Accordingly, it is reasonable to talk about
filtered quantizations
of
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}]
. Formally, these are pairs
(\mathcal{A},\theta)
consisting of a an associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
equipped with an increasing filtration
\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{A}_{\leq i}
such that
[\mathcal{A}_{\leq i},\mathcal{A}_{\leq j}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\leq i+j-1}
and an isomorphism of graded Poisson algebras
\theta:\gr(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}]
(the Poisson bracket on
\gr(\mathcal{A})
is defined by
\{x+\mathcal{A}_{\leq i-1},y+\mathcal{A}_{\leq j-1}\}=[x,y]+\mathcal{A}_{i+j-2}
). A general discussion of filtered quantizations (relevant to the study of co-adjoint covers) can be found in
[
LMM21
, Section 4]
.

Write
\mathrm{Quant}(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
for the set of isomorphism classes of filtered quantizations of
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}]
. For an arbitrary graded Poisson algebra, filtered quantizations may be impossible to classify. But the algebra
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}]
has a very special property which can be used to our advantage: its spectrum
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
has
symplectic singularities
in the sense of Beauville
[
Bea00
, Definition 1.1]
. Because of this fact, we can apply the deep results of Losev
[
Los16
]
to parameterize the set
\mathrm{Quant}(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
. The conclusion is that there is a complex vector space
\mathfrak{P}
(recovered from the geometry of
\Spec(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])
), a finite reflection group
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{P})
, and a canonical bijection

\mathfrak{P}/\mathcal{W}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Quant}(\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}])

This is
[
Los16
, Proposition 3.3, Theorem 3.4]
.

The
canonical quantization
of
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}]
is the filtered quantization
(\mathcal{A}_{0},\theta_{0})
corresponding to the point
0\in\mathfrak{P}
. The
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}]
lifts to a
G
-action on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and the classical co-moment map
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}]
(induced by the map
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
) lifts to a
G
-equivariant filtered algebra homomorphism
\Phi_{0}:U(\mathfrak{g})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{A}_{0}
from the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
(the first fact is automatic since
G
is reductive, the second fact is
[
LMM21
, Lemma 4.11.2]
).

Definition 4.3.1

(Definition 6.0.1,
[
LMM21
]
)
.

The
unipotent ideal
attached to
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
is the two-sided ideal

I_{0}(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}):=\ker{(\Phi_{0}:U(\mathfrak{g})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{A}_{0})}

The unipotent ideal

is a completely-prime primitive ideal with associated variety
\overline{\mathbb{O}}

[
LMM21
, Proposition 6.1.2]
. In fact, it is a

[

. Recall that a

-bimodule

if the adjoint action of

\ad(X)m=Xm-mX,\qquad X\in\mathfrak{g},\ m\in M

integrates to an algebraic action of

. There is a well-known bijection between irreducible admissible

[

]
).

(Definition 6.0.2,
[

]
)
.

•

In

]
and
[

4.4.
Kirillov packets

We are now prepared to define the Kirillov packets
\Pi^{\mathsf{Kir}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G)
for a complex group
G
. The basic idea is already contained in the writing of Vogan on the orbit method (see,
[
Vog92
]
,
[
Vog00
]
): use parabolic induction to reduce to the case when
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
is nilpotent. When
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
is nilpotent, we take
\Pi^{\mathsf{Kir}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G)=\mathrm{Unip}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G)
(the significant new idea, then, is a definition of
\mathrm{Unip}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G)
).

Definition 4.4.1
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\in\mathsf{Cov}^{i\mathbb{R}}_{int}(G)
. Then
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
corresponds to an integral complex co-adjoint cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Cov}_{int}(G)
under the bijection (
4.0.3
). Choose
(L,\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L},\xi)\in\Omega_{m,int}(G)
such that
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Bind}(L,\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L},\xi)
. By Proposition
4.1.2
,
(L,\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L},\xi)
is unique up to conjugation by
G
. The element
\xi\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})^{*}_{int}
corresponds to an element
\iota(\xi)\in\Hom_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l}),i\mathbb{R})_{int}
under the isomorphism (
4.0.1
) (applied to the vector space
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{l})
) which in turn corresponds to a unitary character of
L
under the bijection (
4.2.1
) (applied to the group
L
). Denote this unitary character also by
\iota(\xi)
. The
Kirillov packet
attached to
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
is the finite set of irreducible representations

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathsf{Kir}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G):=\{X\in\Pi(G)\mid

\displaystyle\exists\ X_{0}\in\mathrm{Unip}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}}(L)\text{ such that }

\displaystyle X\text{ is a composition factor in }\Ind^{G}_{P}(X_{0}\otimes\iota(\xi))\}

Remark 4.4.2
.

As stated in the remarks following Definition
4.3.2
, we believe that the constituents of
\mathrm{Unip}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}}(L)
should be unitary (and we have proved this in many cases). Under this assumption, the induced representations
\Ind^{G}_{P}(X_{0}\otimes\iota(\xi))
appearing in Definition

are unitary and completely reducible. So the constituents of
\Pi^{\mathsf{Kir}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G)
are unitary as well.

4.5.
Duality

In this section, we will relate Arthur packets (Definition
3.0.3
) to Kirillov packets (Definition
4.4.1
) for complex groups. More precisely, we will construct a natural injective map from Arthur parameters
\Psi(G^{\vee})
to integral real co-adjoint covers
\mathsf{Cov}^{i\mathbb{R}}_{int}(G)
such that the Arthur packet
\Pi_{\psi}^{\mathsf{Art}}(G)
corresponding to an Arthur parameter
\psi
coincides with the Kirillov packet
\Pi^{\mathsf{Kir}}_{\mathsf{D}(\psi)}(G)
corresponding to the co-adjoint cover
\mathsf{D}(\psi)
. The main difficulty is defining such a map on
unipotent
Arthur parameters. This was essentially done in
[
LMM21
, Section 9]
.

Suppose
\psi\in\Psi(G^{\vee})
. Since
G
is complex, we can think of
\psi
as a continuous homomorphism
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G^{\vee}
. Let
L^{\vee}
denote the centralizer in
G^{\vee}
of
\psi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Note that
L^{\vee}
is a Levi subgroup of

Theorem 4.5.1
.

The map (

(i)

\mathsf{D}
is injective.

(ii)

(iii)

Proof sketch.

(i) follows immediately from the injectivity of

(

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathsf{Art}}_{\psi}(G)=\{X\in\Pi(G)\mid\

This result shows that all of Arthur’s representations for complex groups arise via the orbit method. We note that the map
\mathsf{D}
is not in general surjective, i.e. there are many (unitary) representations of

not considered by Arthur which arise via the orbit method (for example, the metaplectic representations of

4.6.
The general case

In this section, we will sketch a generalization of our complex Orbit Method (Definition
4.4.1
) for arbitrary real groups.

Let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be the real points of a connected reductive algebraic group
G
. Choose a Cartan involution
\theta:G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}G
and let
K\subset G
denote the fixed points of
\theta
(so that
K\cap G_{\mathbb{R}}\subset G_{\mathbb{R}}
is a maximal compact subgroup). By a result of Harish-Chandra, the category of finite-length admissible representations of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
is naturally equivalent to the category
M(\mathfrak{g},K)
of finite-length
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules. In particular, the set
\Pi(G_{\mathbb{R}})
of equivalence classes of irreducible admissible
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-representations is in bijection with the set of equivalence classes of irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules (in practice, we will often identify these two sets).

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
denote the nilpotent cone, and let
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{g}
denote the
-1
-eigenspace of the involution
d\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
K
acts on
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}:=\mathcal{N}\cap\mathfrak{p}^{*}
with finitely many orbits (
[
Vog91
, Corollary 5.22]
), and each
K
-orbit is a Lagrangian subvariety of its
G
-saturation (
[
Vog91
, Proposition 5.19]
). Write
\mathsf{Orb}^{\theta}_{nilp}(G)
for the set of
K
-orbits on
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}
and
\mathsf{Cov}^{\theta}_{nilp}(G)
for the set of isomorphism classes of finite connected
K
-equivariant covers of
K
-orbits on
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}
. The following result is essentially due to Kostant (
[
KR71
]
) and Sekiguchi (
[
Sek87
]
). The formulation below comes from
[
Vog91
, Section 6]
.

Proposition 4.6.1
.

There is a natural bijection

(4.6.1)

\iota:\mathsf{Orb}^{\theta}_{nilp}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Orb}^{i\mathbb{R}}_{nilp}(G_{\mathbb{R}})

such that

G\mathbb{O}=G\iota(\mathbb{O}),\qquad\mathbb{O}\in\mathsf{Orb}^{\theta}_{nilp}(G).

For each
\mathbb{O}\in\mathsf{Orb}^{\theta}_{nilp}(G)
, the
K
-equivariant fundamental group of
\mathbb{O}
is naturally identified with the
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-equivariant fundamental group of
\iota(\mathbb{O})
. So (
4.6.1
) lifts to a bijection

(4.6.2)

\iota:\mathsf{Cov}^{\theta}_{nilp}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Cov}^{i\mathbb{R}}_{nilp}(G_{\mathbb{R}})

To each irreducible irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module

-invariant subset

called the

of

. In general,

The first step towards defining an Orbit Method for

is to define a suitable set of ‘building blocks’.

.

Remark 4.6.6
.

If we assume Conjecture

is true, then the induced modules

Remark 4.6.7
.

In the form given above, it is not clear how (if at all) Definition

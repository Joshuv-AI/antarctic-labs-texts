Abstract
. A Loday-Pirashvili module over a Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is a Lie algebra object ( G → 𝑋 𝔤 ) \bigl(G\xrightarrow{X}\mathfrak{g}\bigr) in the category of linear maps, or equivalently, a 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -module G G which admits a 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -equivariant linear map X : G → 𝔤 X:G\to\mathfrak{g} . We study dg Loday-Pirashvili modules over Lie algebras, which is a generalization of Loday-Pirashvili modules in a natural way, and establish several equivalent characterizations of dg Loday-Pirashvili modules. To provide a concise characterization, a dg Loday-Pirashvili module is a non-negative and bounded dg 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -module V V paired with a weak morphism of dg 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -modules α : V ↝ 𝔤 \alpha\colon V\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g} . Such a dg Loday-Pirashvili module resolves an arbitrarily specified classical Loday-Pirashvili module in the sense that it exists and is unique (up to homotopy). Dg Loday-Pirashvili modules can also be characterized through dg derivations. This perspective allows the calculation of the corresponding twisted Atiyah classes. By leveraging the Kapranov functor on the dg derivation arising from a dg Loday-Pirashvili module ( V , α ) (V,\alpha) , a Leibniz [ 1 ] ∞ {}_{\infty}[1] algebra structure can be derived on ∧ ∙ 𝔤 ∨ ⊗ V [ 1 ] \wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes V[1] . The binary bracket of this structure corresponds to the twisted Atiyah cocycle. To exemplify these intricate algebraic structures through specific cases, we utilize this machinery to a particular type of dg Loday-Pirashvili modules stemming from Lie algebra pairs.

Introduction

The motivation

The inception of this study is rooted in the realm of differential graded (abbreviated as dg) geometry. Recall that a dg manifold is deemed as a manifold endowed with a graded structure, accompanied by a homological vector field. These entities first appeared over two decades ago in the study of topological quantum field theory
[
3
]
, which is now commonly referred to as the AKSZ (Alexandrov-Kontsevich-Schwarz-Zaboronsky) construction. Building upon this foundational knowledge, Ciocan-Fontanine and Kapranov meticulously laid out a comprehensive framework for defining dg manifolds
[
14
]
. Dg manifolds have a rich structure and play an important role in various fields, including but not limited to gauge theory, deformation quantization, Lie theory, higher structures, and most recently, generalized geometry.

In this note we focus on a specific category of objects originating from Lie algebras. A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is interpreted as a formal pointed dg manifold from the perspective of formal geometry, as noted by
[
25
]
— The Lie structure inherent in
\mathfrak{g}
leads to the creation of a dg manifold, conventionally labeled as
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. The function algebra associated with
\mathfrak{g}[1]
is
\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
, and the corresponding homological vector field is the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential. Within this framework, dg vector bundles over
\mathfrak{g}[1]
exhibit a direct correspondence with dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. For instance, the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
is linked with the shifted tangent bundle
T[-1]\mathfrak{g}[1]\cong\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}[1]
.

In their study
[
34
]
of the tensor category of linear maps, Loday and Pirashvili demonstrated that any Lie algebra object within this category can be represented by a linear map
X\colon G\to\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra,
G
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, and
X
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-equivariant map. This representation is encapsulated in the form of
\bigl(G\xrightarrow{X}\mathfrak{g}\bigr)
, termed as a
Loday-Pirashvili module
, which is central to our discussion; see Section
1.2
for detailed discussions. These objects are also referred to as
averaging operators
of Lie algebras
[
1
]
or
augmented Leibniz algebras
in
[
2
]
; further studies on this topic can be found in
[
11
,
40
]
with respect to associative algebras.

From the aforementioned dg geometry point of view, a Loday-Pirashvili module
\bigl(G\xrightarrow{X}\mathfrak{g}\bigr)
can be rephrased as a dg vector bundle
G\times\mathfrak{g}[1]
over
\mathfrak{g}[1]
whose fiber
G
concentrates in degree
0
. This construction also includes a morphism
G\times\mathfrak{g}[1]\xrightarrow{X^{!}}T[-1]\mathfrak{g}[1]
, which is a map of dg vector bundles obtained from the map
X\colon G\to\mathfrak{g}
. The map
X^{!}
is commonly referred to as an anchor map in differential geometry. Therefore, one may treat Loday-Pirashvili modules as “anchored” dg vector bundles with the stipulation that their fibers are concentrated in degree
0
. In order to explore further features of Loday-Pirashvili modules from this dg perspective, it is pertinent to consider how we can relax the original constraints on the degrees of vector spaces and broaden the definition of these modules to align more closely with the dg settings and the context of differential geometry.

Based on these ideas and as a homotopy replacement,
we introduce the concept of a dg Loday-Pirashvili module as a modification of the traditional Loday-Pirashvili module framework. Specifically, a dg Loday-Pirashvili module is defined as a dg vector bundle
V\times\mathfrak{g}[1]
over
\mathfrak{g}[1]
, together with an anchor map represented by a morphism
V\times\mathfrak{g}[1]\to T[-1]\mathfrak{g}[1]
of dg vector bundles. This formulation can be expressed in terms of Lie algebras and their modules, as highlighted in Definition
1.11
. Additionally, we provide alternative characterizations of dg Loday-Pirashvili modules. In essence, a dg Loday-Pirashvili module (over
\mathfrak{g}
) comprises a dg
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
along with a set of linear maps
V^{k}\otimes\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, which demonstrate compatibility with the relevant
\mathfrak{g}
-module structures, as stated in Proposition
1.14
. Having established a precise definition, there is no necessity to revisit the original concept. Consequently, we avoid further discussion on their dg geometry counterpart in subsequent sections.

Related works

In the context of maximal gauged supergravities, the concept of Loday-Pirashvili modules is closely linked to embedding tensors, as investigated by Nicolai and Samtleben
[
38
,
37
]
. Nicolai and Samtleben define an embedding tensor as a linear mapping denoted as
X
, which operates from the space of gauge fields, denoted as
G
, to the Lie algebra of the rigid symmetry group
P
. The group
P
is representative of the prevalent duality property observed in the realm of string theory and M-theory. It is noteworthy that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
possesses a representation on the space of gauge fields
G
. Moreover, a significant physical constraint, stemming from the physics background, imposes that the linear map
X\colon G\to\mathfrak{g}
must adhere to a closure constraint, resulting in the mathematical necessity that the image given by
\mathfrak{g}_{0}:=X(G)\subset\mathfrak{g}
forms a Lie subalgebra, with
\theta
exhibiting
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-equivariance. Thus, in the terminology of this paper, the map
X\colon G\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}
constitutes an Loday-Pirashvili module. Additionally, Kotov and Strobl
[
26
]
demonstrated that the essential data required for an embedding tensor align precisely with those of a Leibniz algebra, from which the associated
{L_{\infty}}
algebra can be deduced. Remarkably, Strobl and Wagemann
[
41
]
have recently explored enhanced Leibniz algebras originating from higher gauge theories, thereby capturing embedding tensors as a distinctive case.

In studies of gauged supergravities
[
16
,
21
]
, the invariance of the Lagrangian’s kinetic terms necessitates a covariant expression for the field strength, thus leading to the concept of tensor hierarchies. The theory of embedding tensor gives rise to tensor hierarchies, which involve the successive introduction of higher gauge transformations. These hierarchies play a crucial role in modeling the dynamics of the system (see
[
16
,
20
,
21
,
17
,
19
,
18
]
). Furthermore, these tensor hierarchies can be organized into an
{L_{\infty}}
algebra, as demonstrated in works by various researchers
[
22
,
33
,
39
,
31
,
28
,
32
]
. It is anticipated that future research will benefit from the insights provided by these mathematical physicists, fostering further exploration and enlightenment in this field.

The letter

Given a

(d^{E}_{\mathrm{CE}}e)(x_{1})=x_{1}\triangleright e,

for

, and

\displaystyle\quad(d^{E}_{\mathrm{CE}}\omega)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}x_{i}\triangleright\omega(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\omega([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{n+1}),

\omega\in\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes E
(

In particular,
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}:=\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathbb{K})=\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}

\displaystyle(d_{\mathrm{CE}}\omega)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})=\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\omega([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{n+1}).

The cohomology of the dg algebra

\displaystyle d^{W}_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ f_{-1}^{0}+d^{W}_{-2}\circ f^{1}_{-1},\quad\mbox{as a map}\quad V^{-1}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes W^{-1},~

\displaystyle f_{1}^{0}\circ d^{V}_{0}

\displaystyle d^{W}_{0}\circ f_{0}^{0},\quad\mbox{as a map}\quad V^{0}\to W^{1},~

\displaystyle f_{0}^{0}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}+f_{1}^{1}\circ d^{V}_{0}

Consider the

-modules

By Equations (

-modules.

These facts boil down to a functor

from the category of dg

-modules to the category of

-modules: a dg

Definition 1.6
.

Let

and

be dg lifts of the
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
\textbf{H}^{0}(V)
and
\textbf{H}^{0}(W)
, respectively. Let

be a map of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. A

of

is a weak morphism of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
f
and

be weak morphisms of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. We say that

and

are

between

and

, i.e., a degree

and

-linear map

We will write

for the

of the weak morphism

. It is evident that two homotopic weak morphisms are sent by
\textbf{H}^{0}
to the same morphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. So it is natural to consider the reverse problem, i.e., the existence and uniqueness of dg lifts of ordinary Lie algebra module morphisms. The following theorem gives a sufficient condition, whose proof is given in the appendix.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let

and

be two non-negative dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Assume that

and

are dg lifts of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

and

, respectively. If

is a resolution of

(i.e.,

is acyclic), then to every
\mathfrak{g}
-module morphism

, there exists a weak morphism

of dg

-modules which is a dg lift of

. Moreover, any two dg lifts of

Our motivation comes from what we call Loday-Pirashvili modules introduced by Loday and Pirashvili in

.

1.3.
Dg Loday-Pirashvili modules

From now on, we only consider
non-negative
dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
V=(V^{\bullet},d^{V})
.
In particular, one has the 1-term dg
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}[0]
, i.e., the trivial cochain complex which concentrates in degree
0
, and endowed with the standard adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. The corresponding (total) complex is denoted by
(\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g}),d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathfrak{g}})
(which has trivial
d^{\mathfrak{g}}
differential).

We are in a position to give the key notion of dg Loday-Pirashvili modules, which is a generalization of Loday-Pirashvili modules.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra.
A
dg Loday-Pirashvili module
over
\mathfrak{g}
is a non-negative and bounded dg
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V=(V^{\bullet},d^{V})
together with a weak morphism of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
\alpha\colon V\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
.
In other words,
\alpha
is a degree
0
morphism of
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules

\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V)\to\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g})
which intertwines the relevant dg structures, i.e.,

(1.9)

~\alpha\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{tot}}=d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\alpha.

Such a dg Loday-Pirashvili module is denoted by a pair
(V,\alpha)
.

As a degree
0
and
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-linear map, each dg Loday-Pirashvili module
\alpha\colon\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V)\to\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by a family of linear maps
\alpha_{k}:~V^{k}\to\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. The index
k
ranges from
0
to the top degree
\mathrm{top}(V)
of
V
. Clearly,
\alpha_{k}
is trivial unless
0\leqslant k\leqslant\dim\mathfrak{g}
.
In the sequel, let us denote

(1.10)

u=\mathrm{min}\{\mathrm{top}(V),\dim\mathfrak{g}\},

and hence for
\alpha_{k}
we only need to consider those with the index
0\leqslant k\leqslant u
. Out of this range
\alpha_{k}
is set to be zero by default.
Each
\alpha_{k}
can be also considered as an
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-linear map
\alpha_{k}:\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V)\to\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g})
of degree
0
by setting

\alpha_{k}(\omega\otimes v^{k})=\omega\wedge\alpha_{k}(v^{k})~\mbox{and }\alpha_{k}(\omega\otimes v^{j})=0~\mbox{for }j\neq k.

In doing so, condition (
1.9
) can be reformulated in terms of
\alpha_{k}
as follows:

(1.11)

~\alpha_{k+1}\circ d^{V}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\circ d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{V}=d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\alpha_{k},\quad\mbox{as a map }V^{k}\to\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes\mathfrak{g},

for all
0\leqslant k\leqslant u
.
In particular, if
V
is concentrated in degree
0
, i.e.,
V=V^{0}
and
d^{V}=0
, then a dg Loday-Pirashvili module
(V,\alpha)
reduces to an ordinary Loday-Pirashvili module
(V^{0},\alpha)
.

Definition 1.12
.

A
dg Loday-Pirashvili class
is a pair
(V,[\alpha])
, where
(V,\alpha)
is a dg Loday-Pirashvili module over
\mathfrak{g}
and
[\alpha]
is the homotopy class of
\alpha\colon V\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
.

Two dg Loday-Pirashvili classes
(V,[\alpha])
and
(V,[\alpha^{\prime}])
coincide if
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are homotopic, i.e., there exists a family of linear maps

h=\{h_{k}\colon V^{k}\to\wedge^{k-1}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\}_{1\leqslant k\leqslant u+1}

such that for all
0\leqslant k\leqslant u
, one has

(1.12)

~\alpha_{k}^{\prime}-\alpha_{k}=d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\circ h_{k}+h_{k}\circ d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{V}+h_{k+1}\circ d^{V}_{k},~\mbox{as a map }~V^{k}\to\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes\mathfrak{g}.

We now define morphisms between dg Loday-Pirashvili modules.

Definition 1.13
.

Let
(V,\alpha)
and
(W,\beta)
be two dg Loday-Pirashvili modules. A morphism
f
from
(V,\alpha)
to
(W,\beta)
is a weak morphism
f\colon V\rightsquigarrow W
of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules such that
\beta\circ f=\alpha\colon V\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
.

Furthermore, a morphism
[f]\colon(V,[\alpha])\to(W,[\beta])
is the homotopy class of a weak morphism
f\colon V\rightsquigarrow W
of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules such that the triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

is commutative

in the category of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

It follows from direct verification that the collection of dg Loday-Pirashvili modules over
\mathfrak{g}
together with their morphisms forms a category which we denote by
\mathcal{WLP}(\mathfrak{g})
.
By
[\mathcal{WLP}](\mathfrak{g})
we denote the category of dg Loday-Pirashvili classes over

together with their morphisms.
Many statements about dg Loday-Pirashvili modules in the sequel are indeed homotopy invariant, and thus holds at the level of dg Loday-Pirashvili classes.

1.5.
Dg Lifts of Loday-Pirashvili modules

In Section
1.1
, we have introduced the functor
\textbf{H}^{0}
from the category of dg modules to the category of ordinary
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Clearly, it also induces a functor from the category
[\mathcal{WLP}](\mathfrak{g})
of dg Loday-Pirashvili classes to the category
\mathcal{LP}(\mathfrak{g})
of Loday-Pirashvili modules.
In specific, a dg Loday-Pirashvili class
(V,[\alpha])
is sent by the functor
\textbf{H}^{0}
to the Loday-Pirashvili module:

\textbf{H}^{0}(V)\xrightarrow{\textbf{H}^{0}(\alpha)}{\mathfrak{g}},

where
\textbf{H}^{0}(V)=\ker(V^{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{V}}V^{1})

and
\textbf{H}^{0}(\alpha)
is the restriction of
\alpha_{0}\colon V^{0}\to\mathfrak{g}
on
\textbf{H}^{0}(V)
.
This definition does not depend on the choice of
\alpha
that represents the homotopy class
[\alpha]
.

Similarly, a morphism of dg Loday-Pirashvili classes

[f]\colon(V,[\alpha])\rightsquigarrow(W,[\beta])
is mapped to the morphism of Loday-Pirashvili modules

\textbf{H}^{0}(f)\colon(\textbf{H}^{0}(V),\textbf{H}^{0}(\alpha))\to(\textbf{H}^{0}(W),\textbf{H}^{0}(\beta)),

where
\textbf{H}^{0}(f)=f_{0}^{0}|_{\textbf{H}^{0}(V)}
. Note that
\textbf{H}^{0}(f)
is well-defined since it is independent of the choice of
f
representing
[f]
.

Definition 1.18
.

Given a Loday-Pirashvili module
(G,X)
, a
dg lift
of
(G,X)
is a dg Loday-Pirashvili module
(V,\alpha)
such that
(\textbf{H}^{0}(V),\textbf{H}^{0}(\alpha))
is isomorphic to
(G,X)
as Loday-Pirashvili modules.

We state and prove the first fundamental theorem of the paper.

Theorem 1.19
.

Let
G
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module and
V=(V^{\bullet},d^{V})
a resolution of
G
. To every Loday-Pirashvili module
(G,X)
over
\mathfrak{g}
, there exists a dg lift
(V,\alpha)
of
(G,X)
. Moreover, the dg lift is unique up to homotopy.

Proof.

A Loday-Pirashvili module
(G,X)
is just a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
X\colon G\to\mathfrak{g}
. By Theorem
1.8
, we can find a dg lift of
X
, i.e., a weak morphism
\alpha\colon V\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, where we regard
\mathfrak{g}
as a trivial complex concentrated in degree
0
and a dg module of
\mathfrak{g}
in the obvious sense. The uniqueness (up to homotopy) of
\alpha
is a consequence of Theorem
1.8
.
∎

It is natural to consider dg lifts of morphisms of Loday-Pirashvili modules. We give the definition in a standard fashion.

Definition 1.20
.

Let
(V,\alpha)
and
(W,\beta)
be, respectively, dg lifts of Loday-Pirashvili modules
(G,X)
and
(H,Y)
. Let
\varphi\colon(G,X)\to(H,Y)
be a morphism of Loday-Pirashvili modules.
A
dg lift
of
\varphi
is a morphism of dg Loday-Pirashvili classes
f\colon(V,\alpha)\rightsquigarrow(W,\beta)
such that
\textbf{H}^{0}(f)
is compatible with
\varphi
, i.e., the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\textbf{H}^{0}(V),\textbf{H}^{0}(\alpha))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\textbf{H}^{0}(W),\textbf{H}^{0}(\beta))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(G,X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(H,Y).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textbf{H}^{0}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

The second fundamental theorem concerns the existence and uniqueness of dg lifts of morphisms of Loday-Pirashvili modules.

Theorem 1.21
.

Let
(V,\alpha)
and
(W,\beta)
be the dg lifts of Loday-Pirashvili modules
(G,X)
and
(H,Y)
respectively. Suppose that
V
is also a resolution of
G
. Then, to every morphism of Loday-Pirashvili modules
\varphi\colon(G,X)\to(H,Y)
, there exists a dg lift of
\varphi

f\colon(V,\alpha)\rightsquigarrow(W,\beta),

which is unique up to homotopy.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.8
,
\varphi
lifts to a weak morphism of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
f\colon V\rightsquigarrow W
, which is unique up to homotopy.
Since
\beta\circ f\colon V\rightsquigarrow\mathfrak{g}
lifts
Y\circ\varphi\colon G\to\mathfrak{g}
which is identically
X
, we know that
\beta\circ f
and
\alpha
are necessary to be homotopic, i.e.,
[\beta]\circ[f]=[\alpha]
, as desired. ∎

2.
Twisted Atiyah classes of dg Loday-Pirashvili modules

Atiyah introduced a cohomology class in
[
7
]
, which has become known as the Atiyah class, to characterize the obstruction to the existence of holomorphic connections on a holomorphic vector bundle. The notion of Atiyah classes has been extensively developed in the past decades for diverse purposes (see
[
8
,
10
,
12
,
15
,
13
,
29
,
30
,
36
]
). In this section, we first interpret each dg module
(V,\alpha)
over
\mathfrak{g}
as a dg derivation of the commutative dg algebra
(\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}=\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{\vee},d_{\mathrm{CE}})
. Then we study the twisted Atiyah class of this dg derivation in the sense of
[
12
]
. It turns out that the twisted Atiyah class induces a Leibniz algebra structure on the (
(-1)
-shifted) total Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the dg module
V
.

2.1.
Dg derivations

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative dg algebra over
\mathbb{K}
(simplified as dg algebra) is a pair
(\mathscr{A},d_{\mathscr{A}})
, where
\mathscr{A}
is a commutative and
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathbb{K}
-algebra,
and
d_{\mathscr{A}}\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}
, usually called the differential, is a homogeneous degree
(+1)
derivation which squares to zero.
By a dg
\mathscr{A}
-module, we mean an
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{E}
, together with a degree
(+1)
and square zero endomorphism
\partial_{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathscr{E}}
of the graded
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
\mathscr{E}
, also called the differential, such that

\partial^{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}(ae)=(d_{\mathscr{A}}a)e+(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert}a\partial_{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathscr{E}}(e)

holds for all
a\in\mathscr{A},e\in\mathscr{E}
.
To work with various different dg
\mathscr{A}
-modules, the differential
\partial_{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathscr{E}}
of any dg
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{E}
will be denoted by the same notation
\partial_{\mathscr{A}}
.

In this note, we assume that
all
\mathscr{A}
-modules are projective
.
For example, if
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra, then the space
\mathscr{A}=\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}=\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
together with
d_{\mathscr{A}}=d_{\mathrm{CE}}
is a dg algebra.
More generally, for a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
E
, we have the standard Chevalley-Eilenberg differential
\partial_{\mathscr{A}}=d^{E}_{\mathrm{CE}}
on
\mathscr{E}=\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(E)=\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes E
, and
\mathscr{E}
becomes a dg
\mathscr{A}=\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-module.

We recall the notion of dg module valued derivations (dg derivations for short) introduced in
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathscr{A},d_{\mathscr{A}})
be a dg algebra and
(\Omega,\partial_{\mathscr{A}})
a dg
\mathscr{A}
-module.

•

A
dg derivation
of
\mathscr{A}
valued in
(\Omega,\partial_{\mathscr{A}})
is a degree
0
derivation
\delta\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\Omega
of the graded algebra
\mathscr{A}
valued in the
\mathscr{A}
-module
\Omega
,

\delta(aa^{\prime})=\delta(a)a^{\prime}+a\delta(a^{\prime})=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert a^{\prime}\rvert}a^{\prime}\delta(a)+a\delta(a^{\prime}),\;\;\;\forall a,a^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A},

which commutes with the differentials as well:

(2.1)

~\delta\circ d_{\mathscr{A}}=\partial_{\mathscr{A}}\circ\delta\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\Omega.

Such a dg derivation is simply denoted by
\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{\delta}\Omega
.

•

Let
\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}
be two
(\Omega,\partial_{\mathscr{A}})
-valued dg derivations of
\mathscr{A}
. They are said to be
homotopic
, written as
\delta\sim\delta^{\prime}
,
if there exists a degree
(-1)
derivation
h
of
\mathscr{A}
valued in the
\mathscr{A}
-module
\Omega
such that

\delta^{\prime}-\delta=[\partial_{\mathscr{A}},h]=\partial_{\mathscr{A}}\circ h+h\circ d_{\mathscr{A}}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\Omega.

A typical example of dg derivations arises from complex geometry — Consider a complex manifold
M
. The Dolbeault dg algebra
\mathscr{A}=(\Omega_{M}^{0,\bullet},\bar{\partial})
is a dg algebra. Let
\Omega=(\Omega_{M}^{0,\bullet}((T^{1,0}M)^{\vee}),\bar{\partial})
be the dg
\mathscr{A}
-module generated by the section space of holomorphic cotangent bundle
(T^{1,0}M)^{\vee}
. Then
\partial:\mathscr{A}\to\Omega
is an
\Omega
-valued derivation of
\mathscr{A}
.
Examples of dg module valued derivations arise from dg Lie algebroids and Lie pairs can be found in
[
12
]
.

Denote the
\mathscr{A}
-dual
\Omega^{\vee}
of
\Omega
by
\mathcal{B}
, which is also a dg
\mathscr{A}
-module. A dg derivation

can be alternatively described by covariant derivations along elements in

\nabla_{b}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A},\qquad\nabla_{b}(a):=\langle b|\delta(a)\rangle,\quad~\forall b\in\mathcal{B},a\in\mathscr{A}.

The above condition (
2.1
) is also expressed by the relation

d_{\mathscr{A}}(\nabla_{b}a)=\nabla_{\partial_{\mathscr{A}}b}~a+(-1)^{|b|}\nabla_{b}(d_{\mathscr{A}}a).

We need the category of dg derivations, denoted by
\operatorname{dgDer}_{\mathscr{A}}
, whose objects are dg derivations
\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{\delta}\Omega
as in Definition

Definition 2.2
.

A morphism
\phi
of dg derivations from

to
\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{\eta}\Theta
is a morphism
\phi\colon\Omega\rightarrow\Theta
of dg
\mathscr{A}
-modules such that

\eta=\phi\circ\delta\colon\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\Theta.

Sh (short for strongly homotopy) Leibniz algebras, also known as Leibniz

In our earlier work

3.1.
Sh Leibniz algebras and related notions

In academic literature, the terms Sh Leibniz algebra and Leibniz
∞
-algebra are interchangeable. In this note, we opt to use the equivalent terminology of Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
-algebras
[
13
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
-algebra over
\mathbb{K}
is a graded vector space
N=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}N^{n}
, together with a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\colon\otimes^{k}N\rightarrow N\}_{k\geqslant 1}
of degree
(+1)
and multilinear maps satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{k=j}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{sh}(k-j,j-1)}\epsilon(\sigma)(-1)^{\lvert v_{\sigma(1)}\rvert+\cdots+\lvert v_{\sigma(k-j)}\rvert}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lambda_{i}\big(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-j)},\lambda_{j}(v_{\sigma(k-j+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-1)},v_{k}),v_{k+1},\cdots,v_{n}\big)=0,

for all
n\geqslant 1
and all homogeneous elements
v_{i}\in N
, where
\operatorname{sh}(p,q)
denotes the set of
(p,q)
-shuffles (
p,q\geqslant 0
), and
\epsilon(\sigma)
is the Koszul sign of
\sigma
.

Definition 3.2
.

A morphism of Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
-algebras from
(N,\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1})
to
(N^{\prime},\{\lambda^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1})
is a sequence
\{f_{k}\colon N^{\otimes k}\rightarrow N^{\prime}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
of degree
0
and multilinear maps satisfying the following compatibility condition:

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k+p\leqslant n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{sh}(k,p)}\epsilon(\sigma)(-1)^{\dagger^{\sigma}_{k}}f_{n-p}\big(b_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,b_{\sigma(k)},\lambda_{p+1}(b_{\sigma(k+1)},\cdots,b_{\sigma(k+p)},b_{k+p+1}),\cdots,b_{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\geqslant 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I^{1}\cup\cdots\cup I^{q}=\mathbb{N}^{(n)}\\
I_{1},\cdots,I_{q}\neq\emptyset\\
i_{\lvert I^{1}\rvert}^{1}<\cdots<i^{q}_{\lvert I^{q}\rvert}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(I^{1},\cdots,I^{q})\lambda_{q}^{\prime}\big(f_{\lvert I^{1}\rvert}(b_{I^{1}}),\cdots,f_{\lvert I^{q}\rvert}(b_{I^{q}})\big),

for all
n\geqslant 1
, where
\dagger^{\sigma}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lvert b_{\sigma(i)}\rvert
,
I^{j}=\{i^{j}_{1}<\cdots<i^{j}_{\lvert I^{j}\rvert}\}\subset\mathbb{N}^{(n)}=\{1,\cdots,n\}
, and
(b_{I^{j}})=(b_{i^{j}_{1}},\cdots,b_{i^{j}_{\lvert I^{j}\rvert}})
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant q
.

In the above definition, the first component
f_{1}\colon(N,\lambda_{1})\rightarrow(N^{\prime},\lambda_{1}^{\prime})
, called the tangent morphism, is a morphism of cochain complexes. We call the Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
morphism
f_{\bullet}\colon(N,\lambda_{\bullet})\rightarrow(N^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}_{\bullet})
an
isomorphism
if
f_{1}
is an isomorphism.
In fact, there is a standard way to find its inverse

f^{-1}_{\bullet}\colon(N^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}_{\bullet})\rightarrow(N,\lambda_{\bullet})
(see
[
4
]
).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(N,\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1})
be a Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
-algebra.
An
(N,\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1})
-module is a graded vector space
U
together with a sequence
\{\mu_{k}\colon N^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes U\rightarrow U\}_{k\geqslant 1}
of degree
(+1)
and multilinear maps, called actions, satisfying the identities

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{k=j}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{sh}(k-j,j-1)}\epsilon(\sigma)(-1)^{\dagger^{\sigma}_{k-j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\mu_{i}\big(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-j)},\lambda_{j}(v_{\sigma(k-j+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-1)},v_{k}),v_{k+1},\cdots,v_{n-1},w\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant n}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{sh}(k,j)}\epsilon(\sigma)(-1)^{\dagger^{\sigma}_{n-j}}\mu_{i}\big(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(n-j)},\mu_{j}(v_{\sigma(n-j+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(n-1)},w)\big)=0,

for all
n\geqslant 1
and all homogeneous vectors
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n-1}\in N,w\in U
, where
\dagger^{\sigma}_{j}=\lvert v_{\sigma(1)}\rvert+\cdots+\lvert v_{\sigma(j)}\rvert
for all
j\geqslant 0
.

In this note, we are interested in Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
-algebras over a dg algebra
\mathscr{A}

\mathscr{A}
-algebras.

A morphism of Leibniz

3.2.

We inherit the previous notations in Section

3.2.1.
Kapranov sh Leibniz algebras

Choose a
\delta
-connection

. We introduce a sequence of degree

\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k}\colon\mathcal{B}^{\otimes k}\rightarrow\mathcal{B},k\geqslant 1

which are defined as follows:

•

The first one, also known as the unary map, is
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{1}=\partial_{\mathscr{A}}\colon\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}

•

The second one, also known as the binary bracket, is
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}:=\operatorname{At}_{\mathcal{B}}^{\nabla}

•

The higher operations

\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k+1}=\nabla(\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k})

Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k+1}(b_{0},b_{1},\cdots,b_{k})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lvert b_{0}\rvert}[\nabla_{b_{0}},\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k}](b_{1},\cdots,b_{k})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lvert b_{0}\rvert}\nabla_{b_{0}}\circ\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{k})

(3.1)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{\lvert b_{0}\rvert(\lvert b_{1}\rvert+\cdots+\lvert b_{i-1}\rvert)}\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k}(b_{1},\cdots,b_{i-1},\nabla_{b_{0}}b_{i},b_{i+1},\cdots,b_{k}),

for all
b_{0},\cdots,b_{k}\in\mathcal{B}

Proposition 3.6
.

The
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathcal{B}
, together with the sequence of operators

{}_{\infty}[1]

\mathscr{A}
-algebra.

This method originates from Kapranov’s construction of

24

{}_{\infty}[1]

\mathscr{A}
-algebra.
The Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]

\mathscr{A}
-algebra

Here the superscript

{}_{\infty}[1]

\mathscr{A}

\delta
-connection on
\mathcal{B}
.

Moreover, the assignment of a Kapranov Leibniz

\delta
-connection

Let

In other words, we have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{\delta^{\prime}}\Omega^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\{\mathcal{R}^{\nabla^{\prime}}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{\delta}\Omega)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{B},\{\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Kap}^{c}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Kap}^{c}}

vanish for

. So the Kapranov Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]

\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-algebra structure on
\mathcal{B}=\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V[1])=\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(G[1])
consists of two maps
d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{G[1]}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}
.
In other words,
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(G[1])
is a dg Leibniz
[1]
algebra.
The pure degree
(-1)
part of which, i.e.,
G[1]
, is a Leibniz
[1]
algebra with the bracket (by (2) of Theorem
3.8
):

(3.4)

\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(g[1],g^{\prime}[1])=-X(g)\triangleright g^{\prime}[1],\quad\forall g,g^{\prime}\in V^{0}=G,

and hence
G
is a Leibniz algebra. This recovers the original construction of Leibniz algebra structure on
V
by Loday and Pirashvili
[
34
]
.

As an application, we consider an ordinary Loday-Pirashvili module
\bigl(G\xrightarrow{X}\mathfrak{g}\bigr)
. Let
V=(V^{\bullet},d^{V})
be a non-negative dg
\mathfrak{g}
-module which is also a resolution of
G
. Hence we can identify
G
directly with the sub
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\ker(d_{0}^{V})
of
V^{0}
.
By Theorem
1.19
for the existence of dg lift of a Loday-Pirashvili module, to the Loday-Pirashvili module
(G,X)
, there exists a dg lift
(V,\alpha)
of
(G,X)
, which is unique up to homotopy.
In particular we have a map
\alpha_{0}\colon V^{0}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
which extends
X:G\to\mathfrak{g}
.

Now, applying Theorem
3.8
to
(V,\alpha)
, namely, the Kapranov functor to the associated dg derivation
\delta_{\alpha}\colon\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V^{\vee}[-1])
,
we obtain a Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]

\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-algebra

\operatorname{Kap}(\delta_{\alpha})=(\mathcal{B},\{\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1})
on the
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
\mathcal{B}=\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V[1])
.
The resulting Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]

\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-algebra structure is unique up to isomorphism (independent of the choice of
\alpha
), hence it depends only on the given Loday-Pirashvili module structure
X:~G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
. We observe that the binary bracket
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}
, when restricted to

, is again given by Equation (
3.4
), which matches exactly with the Leibniz algebra structure on

by Loday and Pirashvili
[
34
]
. Thereby, the Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]

\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-algebra

(\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V[1]),\{\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1})
can be regarded as a resolution of the dg Leibniz
[1]
algebra
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(G[1])
.

We summarize the above discussions into the following proposition which is a supplement to the previous Theorem
1.19
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let

4.
Application to Lie algebra pairs

In this section, we extend our discussion to the construction of a specific type of dg Loday-Pirashvili modules derived from Lie algebra pairs (i.e. inclusions of Lie algebras). We aim to apply the comprehensive theory and methodology detailed in previous sections to accomplish two objectives. Firstly, we focus on calculating in detail the twisted Atiyah class associated with the dg Loday-Pirashvili modules. Additionally, we investigate the Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
algebra structures that emerge from this construction.

4.1.
Dg Loday-Pirashvili modules arising from Lie algebra pairs

Given a Lie algebra pair
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{g})
, i.e., two Lie algebras
\mathfrak{L}
and
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{L}
is a Lie subalgebra, there is a short exact sequence of vector spaces

(4.1)

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{i}\mathfrak{L}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}}\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow 0.

Both vector spaces
\mathfrak{L}
and
\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
admit
\mathfrak{g}
-module structures defined respectively by

\displaystyle x\triangleright m:=[x,m]_{\mathfrak{L}},~

and

\displaystyle x\triangleright\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}(m):=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}([x,m]_{\mathfrak{L}}),

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},m\in\mathfrak{L}
. The said
\mathfrak{g}
-action on
\mathfrak{L}
is the adjoint action, and the action on
\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
is canonical.

We form a 2-term cochain complex
V=(V^{0}\xrightarrow{d^{V}}V^{1})
, where

V^{0}=\mathfrak{L},\quad V^{1}=\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g},

and the differential
d^{V}
is the projection map
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}
. It is easy to see that
V
is a dg representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Fix a splitting of Sequence (
4.1
), namely a pair of linear maps
j\colon\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}
and
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathfrak{L}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
that satisfy

\displaystyle\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ i=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle\qquad\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}\circ j=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}},~\mbox{ and }~i\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}+j\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{L}}.

So we can decompose
\mathfrak{L}\cong\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
as a vector space, and an element in
\mathfrak{L}
is written as
m=(m_{x},m_{b})
, where
m_{x}\in\mathfrak{g}
,
m_{b}\in\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
.
Via this decomposition,
\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
is considered as a sub vector space in
\mathfrak{L}
(but not as a sub
\mathfrak{g}
-module).

We define two linear maps

\alpha_{0}:=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathfrak{L}=V^{0}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g},\quad\mbox{i.e.,}\quad\alpha_{0}(m_{x},m_{b})=m_{x},

and

\alpha_{1}\colon V^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g},\qquad\mbox{such that }\alpha_{1}(b\mid x)=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([j(b),i(x)]_{\mathfrak{L}}),~\forall~b\in\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g},x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let us verify that these two maps give rise to a dg Loday-Pirashvili module structure on
V
over
\mathfrak{g}
.
It suffices to examine Equation (
1.13
) for
k=0
and
k=1
.
For the
k=0
case, we take elements
m=(m_{x},m_{b})\in\mathfrak{L}=V^{0},x\in\mathfrak{g}
, and compute

\displaystyle\quad\alpha_{1}(d_{V}(m)\mid x)-\alpha_{0}(x\triangleright m)+[x,\alpha_{0}(m)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}(m_{b}\mid x)-\alpha_{0}([x,m]_{\mathfrak{L}})+[x,m_{x}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([m_{b},x]_{\mathfrak{L}})-([x,m_{x}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,m_{b}]_{\mathfrak{L}}))+[x,m_{x}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0.

The
k=1
case is similar because it amounts to check the following identity: for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
b\in\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}=V^{1}
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\bigl(x_{2}\triangleright b\mid x_{1}\bigr)-\alpha_{1}\bigl(x_{1}\triangleright b|x_{2}\bigr)-\alpha_{1}(b\mid[x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=\left[x_{2},\alpha_{1}\bigl(b|x_{1}\bigr)\right]-[x_{1},\alpha_{1}\bigl(b\mid x_{2}\bigr)],

for which we omit the details of verification.

In summary, we obtain a dg Loday-Pirashvili module
(V,\alpha)=(V^{0}\xrightarrow{d^{V}}V^{1},\alpha)
with
\alpha=\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\}
as above when a splitting
(j,\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}})
of Sequence (
4.1
) is given.

Suppose that
(j^{\prime},\operatorname{pr}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is another splitting of Sequence (
4.1
).
Note that the difference between the two splittings defines a linear map
h_{1}=j-j^{\prime}:~V^{1}=\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
. And accordingly, we have for all
m\in\mathfrak{L}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{pr}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{g}}(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m-j^{\prime}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}(m)=m-j\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}(m)+h_{1}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(m)+h_{1}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}(m).

Thus the dg Loday-Pirashvili module structure maps corresponding to
(j^{\prime},\operatorname{pr}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{g}})
are

\alpha_{0}^{\prime}=\operatorname{pr}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}+h_{1}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}=\alpha_{0}+h_{1}\circ d^{V},

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{\prime}(b\mid x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}([j^{\prime}(b),i(x)]_{\mathfrak{L}})=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([j(b)-h_{1}(b),i(x)]_{\mathfrak{L}})+h_{1}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}([j(b),i(x)]_{\mathfrak{L}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(b\mid x)+[x,h_{1}(b)]-h_{1}(x\triangleright b).

These relations evidently show that
h=\{h_{1}\}
defines a homotopy between
\alpha=\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\}
and
\alpha^{\prime}=\{\alpha^{\prime}_{0},\alpha^{\prime}_{1}\}
by Proposition
1.16
.

We summarize the above results in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

The above construction defines a canonical dg Loday-Pirashvili module class
(V,[\alpha]=[\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}])
arising from a given Lie algebra pair
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{g})
.
Moreover, it is sent by the functor
\textbf{H}^{0}
to the obvious Loday-Pirashvili module

\textbf{H}^{0}(V)\xrightarrow{\textbf{H}^{0}(\alpha)}\mathfrak{g},

where
\textbf{H}^{0}(V)=\ker(V^{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{V}}V^{1})=\mathfrak{g}
and
\textbf{H}^{0}(\alpha)
is the identity map on
\mathfrak{g}
.

We now provide a concrete example of Lie algebra pairs.

Example 4.2
.

Consider
\mathfrak{L}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with the standard basis

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\Bigr),

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\Bigr),

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\Bigr)

which is subject to the relations

\displaystyle[e,f]

\displaystyle=h,

\displaystyle[h,e]

\displaystyle=2e,

\displaystyle[h,f]

\displaystyle=-2f.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{h,e\}\subset\mathfrak{L}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then
V^{1}=\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
is a one-dimensional vector space generated by
b
, the image of
f
by projection. The
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
V^{1}
is given by

\displaystyle e\triangleright b

\displaystyle=0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle h\triangleright b

\displaystyle=-2b.

The associated dg Loday-Pirashvili module structure map
\alpha
on the dg representation
V=\mathfrak{L}\oplus\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is given by two linear maps
{\alpha_{0}}\colon\mathfrak{L}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and
{\alpha_{1}}\colon(\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that

\displaystyle{\alpha_{0}}(e)=e,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{\alpha_{0}}(h)=h,\qquad\qquad{\alpha_{0}}(f)=0,

\displaystyle{\alpha_{1}}(b|e):=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([f,e])=-h,

\displaystyle\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad{\alpha_{1}}(b|h):=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}([f,h])=0.

Of course, one may come up with similar examples from
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and general semi-simple Lie algebras.

4.2.
Twisted Atiyah cocycles and Kapranov sh Leibniz algebras of Lie algebra pairs

Let
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{g})
be a Lie algebra pair.
We fix a splitting so that we treat
\mathfrak{L}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
directly.
Consider the dg Loday-Pirashvili module
(V,\alpha)
discussed in the previous Section
4.1
.
For convenience, we introduce the following three maps:

\displaystyle\nabla\colon

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}b

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}[x,b]_{\mathfrak{L}},

\displaystyle\Delta\colon

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle\Delta_{b}x

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}[b,x]_{\mathfrak{L}},

\displaystyle\triangleright\colon

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L},

\displaystyle x\triangleright m

\displaystyle:=[x,m]_{\mathfrak{L}},

for all
m\in\mathfrak{L},b\in\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
, and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The twisted Atiyah cocycle
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}\colon V[1]{\otimes}V[1]\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V[1])
has the following terms:

(1)

V^{0}[1]\otimes V^{0}[1]\rightarrow V^{0}[1]
-part given by

(m_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(m_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])=-[\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}m_{1},m_{2}]_{\mathfrak{L}}[1]=-(\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}m_{1})\triangleright m_{2}[1];

(2)

the
V^{0}[1]\otimes V^{1}[1]\rightarrow V^{1}[1]
-part given by

(m_{1}[1],b_{2}[1])\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(m_{1}[1],b_{2}[1])=-\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}[\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}m_{1},b_{2}]_{\mathfrak{L}}[1]=-\nabla_{\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}m_{1}}(b_{2})[1];

(3)

the
V^{1}[1]\otimes V^{0}[1]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes V^{0}[1]
-part given by

(b_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(b_{1}[1],m_{2}[1]),

such that

\iota_{x}\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(b_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])=[\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}[b_{1},x]_{\mathfrak{L}},m_{2}]_{\mathfrak{L}}[1]=(\Delta_{b_{1}}x)\triangleright m_{2}[1];

(4)

the
V^{1}[1]\otimes V^{1}[1]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes V^{1}[1]
-part given by

(b_{1}[1],b_{2}[1])\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(b_{1}[1],b_{2}[1]),

such that

\iota_{x}\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(b_{1}[1],b_{2}[1])=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}}[\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}[b_{1},x]_{\mathfrak{L}},b_{2}]_{\mathfrak{L}}[1]=\nabla_{(\Delta_{b_{1}}x)}b_{2}[1],

for all
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{L}=V^{0},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}=V^{1}
, and all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 4.3
.

Compared with the original Atiyah cocycle of a Lie pair introduced by Calaque, Căldăraru, and Tu
[
9
]
(see also Chen-Stiénon-Xu’s
[
13
]
),
we see that the twisted Atiyah cocycle we obtain, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}
, has three more terms (1)
\sim
(3).
Only its fourth component coincides with the original one.
In particular, if our Lie pair arises from a Lie bialgebra as in the setting of
[
23
]
, the fourth component that we obtain recovers the computation done in
op.cit.
.

We then consider the Kapranov Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
-algebra given by Theorem
3.8
arising from the dg Loday-Pirashvili module
(V,\alpha)
.
The unary bracket is standard. The binary bracket is already given by the above
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}
.
By Equation (
), we can also work out higher brackets
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{n}
for
n\geqslant 3
.
For example, since
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}
is
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-multilinear, it can be determined by extending the following maps:

(1)

the
V^{0}[1]\otimes V^{1}[1]\otimes V^{0}[1]\rightarrow V^{0}[1]
-part given by

(m_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(m_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])=(\Delta_{b_{1}}(\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}m_{0}))\triangleright m_{2}[1];

(2)

the
V^{0}[1]\otimes V^{1}[1]\otimes V^{1}[1]\rightarrow V^{1}[1]
-part given by

(m_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],b_{2}[1])\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(m_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],b_{2}[1])=\nabla_{\Delta_{b_{1}}(\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}}m_{0})}b_{2}[1];

(3)

the
V^{1}[1]\otimes V^{1}[1]\otimes V^{0}[1]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes V^{0}[1]

(b_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(b_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])

such that

\iota_{x}\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(b_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],m_{2}[1])=-(\Delta_{b_{1}}\Delta_{b_{0}}x)\triangleright m_{2}[1];

(4)

the

(b_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],b_{2}[1])\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(b_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],b_{2}[1]),

such that

\iota_{x}\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(b_{0}[1],b_{1}[1],b_{2}[1])=-\nabla_{(\Delta_{b_{1}}\Delta_{b_{0}}x)}b_{2}[1].

The interested reader will find that expressions of higher-order brackets

(for

We finally give the Kapranov Leibniz

of Example

Example 4.4
.

We use the same notations as introduced in Example
4.2
. The dg representation
V=\mathfrak{L}\oplus\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
has basis
\{h,e,f\}
for
\mathfrak{L}
(concentrated in degree
0
) and
\{b\}
for
\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{g}
(concentrated in degree
1
). Denote by
\{h^{\vee},e^{\vee}\}
the basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
in duality to
\{h,e\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
With these vectors, the graded space
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is given by the exterior algebra generated by
\{h^{\vee},e^{\vee}\}
tensoring with
\{h,e,f,b\}
. Let us write the Leibniz
{}_{\infty}[1]
-algebra structure maps on
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},V)
explicitly by giving their formulas on generators.

(1)

The unary map is the differential
d_{\mathrm{tot}}^{V}
sending

to
0
,
e^{\vee}
to
(-2h^{\vee}\wedge e^{\vee})
,
h
to
(-2e^{\vee}\otimes e)
,
e
to
2h^{\vee}\otimes e
,

(-2h^{\vee}\otimes f+e^{\vee}\otimes h+b)
, and
b
to
(-2h^{\vee}\otimes b)

(2)

For the binary bracket, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(h,e)=-2e,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(h,f)=2f,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(e,f)=-h,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(f,e)=0,\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(h,b)=2b,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(e,b)=0,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(b,h)=0,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(b,e)=-2e^{\vee}\otimes e,\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(b,f)=2e^{\vee}\otimes f,\quad\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{2}(b,b)=2e^{\vee}\otimes b.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

(3)

The only nontrivial trinary bracket is given by:

\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(e,b,e)=-2e,\mbox{ ~and~ }~\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(e,b,f)=2f.

All other generating relations of
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{3}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
are zero.

(4)

All higher
\mathcal{R}^{\nabla}_{n}
(for
n\geqslant 4
) operations vanish.

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
1.8

Let us assume simply that
E=\textbf{H}^{0}(V)
and
F=\textbf{H}^{0}(W)
.
We first prove the existence of the lift
f
, or
f_{k}\colon V^{k}\to\oplus_{l+s=k}\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes W^{s}
,
k=0,1,\cdots,\mathrm{top}
.
Our objective is to construct these
f_{k}
so that they satisfy Equation (
1.4
), or

(A.1)

~f_{k+1}\circ d^{V}_{k}=-f_{k}\circ d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{V}+(d^{W}+d^{W}_{\mathrm{CE}})\circ f_{k},\quad k\geqslant 0.

We will show the existence of
f_{k}
by induction on
k
. The approach is as follows. If
f_{k}
is given, then we can use Equation (
A.1
) to recursively define
f_{k+1}
on

\mathrm{img}(d^{V}_{k})\subset V^{k+1}
, provided that the right hand side of Equation (
A.1
) vanishes on
\ker(d^{V}_{k})
. We will prove this fact and then define
f_{k+1}
on
V^{k+1}
by arbitrary extension (based on the assumption that every
V^{\bullet}
is finite-dimensional).

Let
f_{0}\colon V^{0}\to W^{0}
be an arbitrary extension of
\varphi
. To define
f_{1}\colon V^{1}\to W^{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes W^{0}
, we must show that the right hand side of Equation (
A.1
) vanishes on
\ker(d^{V}_{0})
, i.e.,

d^{W}_{0}\circ f_{0}^{0}(v)=0,

and

d^{W}_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ f_{0}^{0}(v)-f_{0}^{0}\circ d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{V}(v)=0,

for
v\in\ker(d_{0}^{V})
. These identities are easy to check since
f_{0}^{0}
restricts to the
\mathfrak{g}
-module morphism
\varphi\colon\textbf{H}^{0}(V)\to\textbf{H}^{0}(W)
.

Now suppose that
f_{0}
,
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n-1},f_{n}
are defined for some
n\geqslant 1
, so that Equation (
1.4
) holds for all
k=1,\cdots,n-1
. Let us show that the right hand side of Equation (
A.1
) vanishes on
\ker(d^{V}_{n})
, and then one defines
f_{n+1}
accordingly.
We need the assumption that
(V,d^{V})
is acyclic, and hence we have
\ker(d^{V}_{n})=\mathrm{img}(d^{V}_{n-1})
in particular.
So for
v\in\ker(d^{V}_{n})
, one can write
v=d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})
, for some
v_{0}\in V^{n-1}
. Applying to
v
the right hand side of Equation (
A.1
) where
k=n
, we have

\displaystyle-f_{n}\circ d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{V}(v)+d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ f_{n}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{n}\circ d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{V}\circ d^{V}(v_{0})+d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ f_{n}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{n}\circ d^{V}\circ d_{\mathrm{CE}}^{V}(v_{0})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ(d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ f_{n-1}-f_{n-1}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}})(v_{0})\qquad\mbox{by Eq.\eqref{atc} and the induction assumption};

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f_{n}\circ d^{V}+f_{n-1}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ f_{n-1})\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\qquad\mbox{by the induction assumption}.

The statement of existence is thus justified.

We then prove that, if
f
and
f^{\prime}\colon V\rightsquigarrow W
are both dg lifts of
\varphi
, then they are homotopic. It suffices to prove that, if
f
is a dg lift of the trivial morphism
\varphi=0\colon\textbf{H}^{0}(V)\to\textbf{H}^{0}(W)
(i.e.,
f|_{\textbf{H}^{0}(V)=\ker(d^{V}_{0})}=0
), then
f
is homotopic to zero, i.e., there exists a degree
(-1)
and
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
-linear map
h:\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V)\to\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(W)
such that

(A.2)

~f=d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ h+h\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{tot}}\colon\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V)\to\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(W).

Let us assume that
h
is generated by a family of linear maps

h_{k}\colon V^{k}\to\oplus_{l+s=k-1}\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes W^{s},\quad k=1,2,\cdots,

with
h_{0}=0
, and each
h_{k}
extended to a map
\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(V^{k})\to\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(W^{s})
, by setting

h_{k}(\omega\otimes v)=(-1)^{p}\omega\otimes h_{k}(v),\quad\omega\in\wedge^{p}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee},v\in V^{k}.

Then Equation (
A.2
) becomes the relation

(A.3)

~h_{k+1}\circ d^{V}_{k}=f_{k}-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ h_{k}-h_{k}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}\colon V^{k}\to\oplus_{l+s=k}\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes W^{s},

for all
k\geq 0
.
Again we use induction to construct these
h_{k}
s. The initial
h_{0}=0
is given. The condition for
h_{1}
is

h_{1}\circ d^{V}_{0}=f_{0}.

The construction of
h_{1}
that meets this condition is clear because
f_{0}
vanishes on
\ker d^{V}_{0}
.

Suppose that
h_{0},h_{1},\cdots,h_{n}
(
n\geqslant 1
) are found and they all satisfy Equation (
A.3
) for
k=0,1,\cdots,n-1
.
We then use Equation (
A.3
) to define
h_{n+1}
on
\mathrm{img}d^{V}_{n}
. Of course, one needs to examine that the right hand side of Equation (
A.3
), for
k=n
, yields zero when it is applied to
\ker d^{V}_{n}
. This is verified below.

For all
v\in\ker(d^{V}_{n})
, again by
(V,d^{V})
being acyclic, one can write
v=d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})
, for some
v_{0}\in V^{n-1}
. Thus, we can compute

\displaystyle f_{n}(v)-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ h_{n}(v)-h_{n}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{n}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ h_{n}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})-h_{n}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{n}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ h_{n}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})+h_{n}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{n}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ\circ(f_{n-1}-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ h_{n-1}-h_{n-1}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}})(v_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad+(f_{n-1}-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ h_{n-1}-h_{n-1}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}})\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{n}\circ d^{V}_{n-1}(v_{0})-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ f_{n-1}(v_{0})+f_{n-1}\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{CE}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circ d^{V}_{\mathrm{tot}}(v_{0})-d^{W}_{\mathrm{tot}}\circ f_{n-1}(v_{0})=0.

Here we have used the induction assumption, and the fact that
f\colon V\rightsquigarrow W
is a morphism of dg
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

This proves that
h_{n+1}|_{\mathrm{img}d^{V}_{n}}
is well defined, and then we define
h_{n+1}
by an arbitrary extension to
V^{n+1}
. By induction, we complete the proof.

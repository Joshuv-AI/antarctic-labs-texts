Honda-Tate theory for log abelian varieties over finite fields
Xiaoyu Zhang, Heer Zhao

Abstract
. In this article we study the Honda-Tate theory for log abelian varieties over an fs log point S = ( Spec 𝐤 , M S ) S=(\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathbf{k},M_{S}) for 𝐤 = 𝔽 q \mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q} a finite field, generalizing the classical Honda-Tate theory for abelian varieties over 𝐤 \mathbf{k} . For the standard log point S S , we give a complete description of the isogeny classes of such log abelian varieties using Weil q q -numbers of weight 0,1, and 2. In the general case where M S M_{S} admits a global chart P → 𝐤 P\to\mathbf{k} with P = ℕ k P=\mathbb{N}^{k} , we also give a complete description of simple isogeny classes of log abelian varieties over S S in terms of rational points in generalized simplices.

1.
Introduction

Honda-Tate theory is an important result in the theory of abelian varieties over finite fields (
[
Hon68
,
Tat66
]
). It gives a complete description of the isogeny classes of simple abelian varieties over a finite field
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q
elements in terms of Galois conjugacy classes of Weil
q
-numbers (of weight 1).
It has many deep applications, for example, in the proof of the Manin conjecture on the Newton polygons of abelian varieties by Honda and Serre(
[
Tat68
]
); in the point counting problems on Shimura varieties (
[
Kot92
]
). There are also many generalizations and refinements of this theory, see for example
[
KMS22
]
.

Recall that for an integer
w
, a
Weil
q
-number of weight
w
is an algebraic integer whose images in
\mathbb{C}
all have absolute value equal to
q^{w/2}
. In particular, a Weil
q
-number of weight
0
is a root of unity by a classical result of Kronecker on algebraic integers (
[
Kro57
]
, see also
[
Pra04
, Theorem 4.5.4]
for a convenient reference). We write

q\text{-Weil}(w)

for the set of Galois conjugacy classes
[\alpha]
of Weil
q
-numbers
\alpha
of weight
w
.
For an abelian variety
A
over
\mathbf{k}
, we write
[A]
for its isogeny class and

P_{A,\pi_{A}}

for the characteristic polynomial of the
q
-Frobenius endomorphism
\pi_{A}
on
A
(or equivalently on any member of
[A]
), see
[
Mil08
, §10, p.48]
.
Then the theory of Honda-Tate states as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. There is a bijection between the set of isogeny classes of simple abelian varieties over
\mathbf{k}
and the set of Galois conjugacy classes of Weil
q
-numbers of weight
1

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\text{simple abelian varieties over }\mathbf{k}\right\}/\text{isogeny}\rightarrow&q\text{-Weil}(1)\\
[A]\mapsto&[\alpha],\end{split}

where
\alpha
is a root of
P_{A,\pi_{A}}
.

Throughout this introduction we write

\Gamma:=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{k}}/\mathbf{k})=\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle}

for the absolute Galois group of
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\gamma:\mathbf{k}\to\mathbf{k},\,x\mapsto x^{q}

the canonical topological generator.
The characteristic polynomial
P_{A,\pi_{A}}
of
\pi_{A}
can also be obtained as the characteristic polynomial of
\gamma
on the
\ell
-adic Tate module
T_{\ell}(A)
of
A
, where
\ell\neq\mathrm{char}(\mathbf{k})
is a prime number (see
[
Mil08
, Chap. II, §2, the paragraph after Theorem 2.9]
).
This fact will be important for dealing with log abelian varieties (see below), for which we are only able to use
\ell
-adic Tate module to define a polynomial which plays the role of
P_{A,\pi_{A}}
for an abelian variety
A
, see Definition
2.23
and (
8
).

Log abelian varieties, developed by Kajiwara, Kato and Nakayama in their series of papers starting with
[
KKN08
]
, are proper (log) smooth group objects which degenerate abelian varieties. They are expected to behave like abelian varieties in many aspects. In particular, it would be desirable to classify log abelian varieties up to isogeny over an fs log point
S=(\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathbf{k},M_{S})
with
\mathbf{k}
a finite field, just as in the case of abelian varieties over
\mathbf{k}
.

Our first main result reduces the problem to the classification of log abelian varieties without abelian part, and the latter is essentially a problem of Galois modules over
\mathbf{k}
.

Theorem 1.2

(See Theorem
4.4
)
.

Let
S=(\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathbf{k},M_{S})
be an fs log point with
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
finite. Then for any log abelian variety
A
over
S
, we have an isogeny

A\sim A_{1}\times B,

where
B
is an abelian variety over
\mathbf{k}
and
A_{1}
is a log abelian variety over
S
whose corresponding log 1-motive
\mathbf{M}_{1}
(see
[

, Theorem 3.4]
) has no abelian part, i.e.
\mathbf{M}_{1}=[Y_{1}\to T_{1\log}]
with

a torus over
\mathbf{k}
.

By Theorem
1.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\text{simple LAVs over }S\right\}/\text{isogeny}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\text{simple AVs over }\mathbf{k}\right\}/\text{isogeny}\bigsqcup\left\{\text{simple LAVs without abelian part over $S$}\right\}/\text{isogeny},

where AV (resp. LAV) stands for abelian variety (resp. log abelian variety).
Therefore in order to classify log abelian varieties over
S
up to isogeny, it suffices to classify log abelian varieties without abelian part over
S
up to isogeny.

Outline

In §

of

2.1.

We review in this subsection the theory of 1-motives.
Let

Definition 2.1
.

Let

by a torus

\mathbf{M}=[Y\xrightarrow{u}G]

in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{fl}}
, where
Y

A morphism of 1-motives

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S
be a scheme, and let

\displaystyle H^{-1}(Y\otimes^{L}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\to H^{-1}(G\otimes^{L}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\to H^{-1}(\mathbf{M}\otimes^{L}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\to

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S)
-action, where
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S)
denotes the étale fundamental group of
S
.

In the rest of this subsection, the base
S
will be
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathbf{k}
with
\mathbf{k}
a field. We will use the notation
\Gamma:=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{k}}/\mathbf{k})
for

.

Definition 2.3
.

A non-zero 1-motive
[Y\to G]
over
\mathbf{k}
is called

, if any non-zero morphism

(f_{-1},f_{0})\colon[Y^{\prime}\to G^{\prime}]\to[Y\to G],

which is injective as a morphism of complexes, has to be an isogeny.

Remark 2.4
.

Clearly a simple 1-motive over
\mathbf{k}
must be of the form
Y[1]
, or
T
, or
B
, where

(1)

Y
is a
simple lattice
, i.e. it contains no proper sub-lattice,

(2)

T
is a
simple torus
, i.e. it contains no proper sub-torus,

(3)

and

is a simple abelian variety.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f=(f_{-1},f_{0})
be an endomorphism of a 1-motive
\mathbf{M}=[Y\to G]
over
\mathbf{k}
. Let
\ell
be a prime number which is invertible in
\mathbf{k}
, and let
P_{\mathbf{M},f,\ell}(\theta)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\theta]
be the characteristic polynomial of the endomorphism
T_{\ell}(f)
on
T_{\ell}(\mathbf{M})
.
In case that
\mathbf{M}=Y[1]
, we have
f=(f_{-1},0)
and also write
P_{\mathbf{M},f}
as
P_{Y,f_{-1}}
. Similarly, in case that
\mathbf{M}=G
, we also write
P_{\mathbf{M},f}
as
P_{T,f_{0}}
.
Then we have the following.

(1)

P_{\mathbf{M},f,\ell}[\theta]=P_{Y,f_{-1},\ell}[\theta]\cdot P_{G,f_{0},\ell}[\theta]=P_{Y,f_{-1},\ell}[\theta]\cdot P_{B,f_{\mathrm{ab}},\ell}[\theta]\cdot P_{T,f_{\mathrm{t}},\ell}[\theta]
, where
f_{\mathrm{ab}}:B\to B
(resp.
f_{\mathrm{t}}:T\to T
) denotes the abelian (resp. torus) part of
f_{0}
(see Lemma
A.3
(1)).

(2)

P_{\mathbf{M},f,\ell}[\theta]\in\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
.

(3)

P_{\mathbf{M},f,\ell}[\theta]
is independent of the choice of
\ell
.

Proof.

(1)

After choosing
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-basis for
T_{\ell}(Y)
,
T_{\ell}(T)
and
T_{\ell}(B)
, it is easy to see that the endomorphism
T_{\ell}(f)
is represented by a matrix of the following form

\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}&a_{13}\\
0&a_{22}&a_{23}\\
0&0&a_{33}\end{pmatrix}

with
a_{11}\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{dim}(T)}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
,
a_{22}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2\mathrm{dim}(B)}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
and

a_{33}\in\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{rk}(Y)}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
. Thus the characteristic polynomial of
T_{\ell}(f)
is the product of the characteristic polynomials of
a_{11}
,
a_{22}
and
a_{33}
, which are respectively the characteristic polynomials of
T_{\ell}(f_{\mathrm{t}})
,
T_{\ell}(f_{\mathrm{ab}})
and
T_{\ell}(f_{-1})
.

(2)

It is well known that
P_{B,f_{\mathrm{ab}},\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
(see
[
Mum70
, Chap. IV, §19, Theorem 4]
). Write
X=\mathcal{H}om(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})
for the character group of
T
, which is étale locally a free abelian group of finite rank. The endomorphism
f_{\mathrm{t}}
on
T
induces an endomorphism
f_{\mathrm{t}}^{\ast}
on
X
. Moreover the canonical pairing
\langle-,-\rangle\colon X\times T\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
satisfies

\langle f_{\mathrm{t}}^{\ast}(x),t\rangle=\langle x,f_{\mathrm{t}}(t)\rangle,\quad\forall x\in X(\overline{\mathbf{k}}),\,t\in T(\overline{\mathbf{k}}).

We have an induced pairing
\langle-,-\rangle\colon T_{\ell}(X[1])\times T_{\ell}(T)=(X\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\times T_{\ell}(T)\to T_{\ell}(\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
such that

\langle(f_{\mathrm{t}}^{\ast}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}})(x),t\rangle=\langle x,T_{\ell}(f_{\mathrm{t}})(t)\rangle,\quad\forall x\in X\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell},\,t\in T_{\ell}(T).

After fixing
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-basis for
X\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and
T_{\ell}(T)
, we see immediately that the matrices representing
f_{\mathrm{t}}^{\ast}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}
and
T_{\ell}(f_{\mathrm{t}})
are transpose of each other. In particular,

P_{T,f_{\mathrm{t}},\ell}=P_{X,f_{\mathrm{t}}^{\ast},\ell}.

So it is enough to show that
P_{Y,f_{-1},\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
for any
Y
and any endomorphism
f_{-1}
on
Y
. However, we have

T_{\ell}(Y[1])=Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell},\quad T_{\ell}(f_{-1})=f_{-1}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}.

So
P_{Y,f_{-1},\ell}
is exactly the characteristic polynomial of
f_{-1}
on
Y
, which clearly lies in
\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
.

(3)

Again it is well known that
P_{B,f_{\mathrm{ab}},\ell}
is independent of
\ell
(see
loc.cit.
). From the above argument, it is enough to show that
P_{Y,f_{-1},\ell}
is independent of
\ell
for any
Y
and any
f_{-1}
, which again follows from the fact that
T_{\ell}(f_{-1})=f_{-1}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}
.

∎

Definition 2.6
.

Let
f=(f_{-1},f_{0})
be an endomorphism of a 1-motive
\mathbf{M}=[Y\xrightarrow{u}G]
over
\mathbf{k}
. By Proposition
2.5
,

P_{\mathbf{M},f}(\theta):=P_{\mathbf{M},f,\ell}(\theta)\in\mathbb{Z}[\theta]

is independent of the choice of
\ell
, and we call it the
characteristic polynomial of
f
.

Now assume that
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
is finite, then
\Gamma
is topologically generated by the
q
-Frobenius map:

(5)

\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{k}}/\mathbf{k})=\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle}\text{ with }\gamma:\overline{\mathbf{k}}\to\overline{\mathbf{k}},x\mapsto x^{q}.

The lattice
Y
over
\mathbf{k}
amounts to a
\mathbb{Z}
-representation
\rho_{Y}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{Z}^{r})
for some
r>0
, where we identify
Y\times_{\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathbf{k}}\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\overline{\mathbf{k}}
with
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. Since
\Gamma
is commutative, the linear map
\rho_{Y}(\gamma):\mathbb{Z}^{r}\to\mathbb{Z}^{r}
gives rise to an endomorphism of
Y
, and we denote it by
\pi_{Y}:Y\to Y
. Let
\pi_{G}:G\to G
(resp.
\pi_{B}:B\to B
, resp.
\pi_{T}:T\to T
) be the geometric Frobenius morphism of
G
(resp.
B
, resp.
T
) over
\mathbf{k}
, see
[
Mil08
, Chap. II, the beginning of §1]
. Actually
\pi_{Y}
can be regarded as the geometric Frobenius of the scheme
Y
over
\mathbf{k}
. One can check easily that
\pi_{G}\circ u=u\circ\pi_{Y}
, and thus
\pi_{\mathbf{M}}=(\pi_{Y},\pi_{G})
defines an endomorphism of
\mathbf{M}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=[Y\xrightarrow{u}G]
be a 1-motive over the finite field
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
B
(resp.
T
) be the abelian (resp. torus) part of
G
, and let
X
be the character group of
T
.

(1)

The roots of the characteristic polynomial
P_{Y,\pi_{Y}}(\theta)
(resp.
P_{X,\pi_{X}}(\theta)
) of the endomorphism
\pi_{Y}
(resp.
\pi_{X}
) of
Y
(resp.
X
) are roots of unity. In particular they are Weil
q
-numbers of weight 0.

(2)

For the characteristic polynomial
P_{T,\pi_{T}}(\theta)
of the endomorphism
\pi_{T}
of
T
, we have

P_{T,\pi_{T}}(\theta)=\frac{(-\theta)^{n}}{\det(\pi_{X})}P_{X,\pi_{X}}(\frac{q}{\theta}),

where
n
denotes the rank of
X
and
\det(\pi_{X})
denotes the determinant of the linear map
\pi_{X}
on
X
regarded as a finite rank free abelian group.
In particular, the roots of
P_{T,\pi_{T}}(\theta)
are of the form
q\alpha^{-1}
with
\alpha
a root of
P_{X,\pi_{X}}(\theta)
, and thus they are Weil
q
-numbers of weight 2.

(3)

The roots of the characteristic polynomial
P_{B,\pi_{B}}(\theta)
of the endomorphism
\pi_{B}
of
B
are Weil
q
-numbers of weight 1.

(4)

P_{\mathbf{M},\pi_{\mathbf{M}}}(\theta)=P_{Y,\pi_{Y}}(\theta)\cdot P_{B,\pi_{B}}(\theta)\cdot P_{T,\pi_{T}}(\theta)
.

(5)

Let
\rho_{\mathbf{M},\ell}
(resp.
\rho_{G,\ell}
, resp.
\rho_{Y,\ell}
) denote the canonical action of
\Gamma
on
T_{\ell}(\mathbf{M})
(resp.
T_{\ell}(G)
, resp.
T_{\ell}(Y[1])=Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
). Then

T_{\ell}(\pi_{G})=\rho_{G,\ell}(\gamma),\quad T_{\ell}(\pi_{Y})=\pi_{Y}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}=\rho_{Y,\ell}(\gamma),\quad T_{\ell}(\pi_{\mathbf{M}})=\rho_{\mathbf{M},\ell}(\gamma).

In particular,
P_{\mathbf{M},\pi_{\mathbf{M}}}(\theta)
is the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{\mathbf{M},\ell}(\gamma)
.

Proof.

(1)

Since the action of
\Gamma\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
on
Y[1]
factors through the finite quotient
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
for some
m>0
,
\pi_{Y}^{m}=\mathrm{Id}
, thus the roots of
P_{Y,\pi_{Y}}
are roots of unity and are Weil
q
-numbers of weight
0
. The same holds for
X
.

(2)

Write
\pi_{T}^{\ast}
for the endomorphism on
X
induced by
\pi_{T}
. We claim that

\pi_{T}^{\ast}=q\cdot\pi_{X}^{-1}
.
The canonical pairing
\langle-,-\rangle\colon X\times T\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
is Galois equivariant. In particular, for any
x\in X(\overline{\mathbf{k}})
and
t\in T(\overline{\mathbf{k}})
, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{T}^{*}(x)(t)=

\displaystyle\langle x,\pi_{T}(t)\rangle=\langle\pi_{X}(\pi_{X}^{-1}(x)),\pi_{T}(t)\rangle=\langle\pi_{X}^{-1}(x),t\rangle^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{X}^{-1}(x)^{q},t\rangle=(q\cdot\pi_{X}^{-1}(x))(t),

and thus
\pi_{T}^{*}(x)=q\cdot\pi_{X}^{-1}(x)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle P_{T,\pi_{T}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\det(\theta-\pi_{T})=\det(\theta-\pi_{T}^{\ast})=\det(\theta-q\pi_{X}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\det(-\theta\pi_{X}^{-1})\det(\frac{q}{\theta}-\pi_{X})=\frac{(-\theta)^{n}}{\det(\pi_{X})}P_{X,\pi_{X}}(\frac{q}{\theta}).

(3)

This follows from the classical Honda-Tate theory.

(4)

This follows from Proposition
2.5
(1).

(5)

The two equalities
T_{\ell}(\pi_{G})=\rho_{G,\ell}(\gamma)
and
T_{\ell}(\pi_{Y})=\rho_{Y,\ell}(\gamma)
are clear. We show the last one.
Since
T_{\ell}(\pi_{\mathbf{M}})|_{T_{\ell}(G)}=T_{\ell}(\pi_{G})
and similarly
\rho_{\mathbf{M},\ell}(\gamma)|_{T_{\ell}(G)}=\rho_{G,\ell}(\gamma)
, the map
T_{\ell}(\pi_{\mathbf{M}})-\rho_{\mathbf{M},\ell}(\gamma)
factors as

T_{\ell}(\mathbf{M})\twoheadrightarrow Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to T_{\ell}(G)\hookrightarrow T_{\ell}(\mathbf{M}).

Due to weight reason, there is no non-trivial Galois equivariant map between
Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and
T_{\ell}(G)
. It follows that
T_{\ell}(\pi_{\mathbf{M}})-\rho_{\mathbf{M},\ell}(\gamma)=0
. So we are done.

∎

\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{r}
\scriptstyle{G_{i}}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F_{i}}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathrm{coker}{F_{i}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

5.1.
Representation theory of
\Gamma

In this subsection, we collect some facts on the theory of rational representations of
\Gamma
. Then we deduce some simple properties of the two categories
\mathrm{Mod}_{\Gamma}
and
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma}^{\mathbb{N},\mathrm{pPol}}
.

We fix a compatible family of roots of unity
(\zeta_{r})_{r>0}
(that is,
\zeta_{rr^{\prime}}^{r}=\zeta_{r^{\prime}}
for any
r,r^{\prime}>0
). For a positive integer
n
, we write the (unique) finite cyclic quotient of order
n
of
\Gamma
as follows

\Gamma_{n}:=\Gamma/\Gamma^{n}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}.

We still denote the image of
\gamma
in
\Gamma_{n}
by
\gamma
. Then we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras induced by the projections
\Gamma_{n}\rightarrow\Gamma_{r}
for all
r\mid n
:

(16)

\mathbb{Q}[\Gamma_{n}]\simeq\prod_{r\mid n}\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r}).

In particular, each
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
for
r\mid n
is a
rational
representation of
\Gamma_{n}
and we can make the representation explicit as follows

(17)

\Gamma_{n}\times\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})\to\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r}),\quad(\gamma,x)\mapsto\zeta_{r}x\,(\text{the multiplication of $\zeta_{r}$ and $x$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})$}).

We write
\phi\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
for Euler’s totient function, in particular, we have

\phi(r)=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})).

We have the following easy observation.

Lemma 5.1
.

Fix a positive integer
n>0
, then any irreducible rational representation of
\Gamma_{n}
is of the form
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
for some
r\mid n
and these
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
are mutually non-isomorphic.

Proof.

The proof is well known to experts, which we include here for the convenience of the reader.
First we show that each
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
is an irreducible
\mathbb{Q}
-linear representation of
\Gamma_{n}
for
r|n
. Indeed,
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
is a
\mathbb{Q}[\Gamma_{n}]
-module and the image of
\mathbb{Q}[\Gamma_{n}]
in
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r}))
is exactly
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
, which is a field. We deduce that
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
is irreducible as
\mathbb{Q}[\Gamma_{n}]
-module.

Next we show that for distinct positive integers
r>r^{\prime}
,
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r^{\prime}})
are not isomorphic to each other as representations of
\Gamma_{n}
. Indeed, the action of
\Gamma_{n}
on
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
factors through
\Gamma_{r}
and does not factor through
\Gamma_{r/d}
for any positive integer
1<d\mid r
. If
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r^{\prime}})
are isomorphic, then the action of
\Gamma_{n}

Lemma 5.7

Proof.

Clearly

\Gamma

For

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})/\mathbb{Q}}(xt\overline{y})=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})/\mathbb{Q}}(yt\overline{x}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})\\

Moreover this bilinear map is

-equivariant. Indeed, by bilinearity, it suffices to show

As above, this gives rise to an isogeny of free

. By Corollary
6.3
, the matrices
X_{1}^{\mathrm{t}}\Lambda,\cdots,X_{k}^{\mathrm{t}}\Lambda
are all symmetric, positive semi-definite and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{i}^{\mathrm{t}}\Lambda
is symmetric and positive definite. We deduce that
(X_{1}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}A,\cdots,(X_{k}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}A
and
(X_{1}^{\prime\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}D,\cdots,(X_{k}^{\prime\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}D
are all symmetric and positive semi-definite and moreover
\sum_{i=1}^{k}(X_{i}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}A
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}(X_{i}^{\prime\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}D
are both symmetric and positive definite. We define a linear map
\lambda^{\prime}\colon M^{\prime}\to(M^{\prime})^{\vee}
represented by the matrix
A
.
It follows from Corollary
6.3
that

(M^{\prime},(M^{\prime})^{\vee},\Phi^{\prime}:=\Phi\mid_{M^{\prime}})

is polarizable in
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{P}
with polarization
\lambda^{\prime}
. In particular, we have constructed an object
(M^{\prime},(M^{\prime})^{\vee},\Phi^{\prime})
in
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma}^{P,\mathrm{pPol}}
together with a morphism

\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\colon(M^{\prime},(M^{\prime})^{\vee},\Phi^{\prime})\to(M^{\prime}\oplus M^{\prime\prime},(M^{\prime})^{\vee}\oplus(M^{\prime\prime})^{\vee},\Phi)

where
\psi_{1}
(resp.
\psi_{2}
) is the canonical inclusion (resp. projection) map. Clearly
\mathrm{coker}(\psi_{1})
and
\mathrm{coker}(\psi_{2}^{\vee})
are both of rank equal to
\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M^{\prime\prime})>0
, so
\psi
is not an isogeny and thus
(M^{\prime}\oplus M^{\prime\prime},(M^{\prime})^{\vee}\oplus(M^{\prime\prime})^{\vee},\Phi)
is
not
simple.

∎

6.2.
Polarizations on
(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}],\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{\vee},\langle-,-\rangle)

In this subsection we will concentrate on objects in
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma}^{P}
of the form

(M,N,\Phi)=(M,N,\langle-,-\rangle)=(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}],\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{\vee},\langle-,-\rangle).

Then the morphisms
\Phi_{i}\colon M\rightarrow N^{\vee}
in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\Gamma}
are of the form

(28)

\Phi_{i}\colon\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}],\quad m\mapsto t_{i}m

for some
t_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]
.

Let
\lambda\colon M\rightarrow N
be a morphism in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\Gamma}
, so it is of the form
\lambda=\lambda_{t}
for some non-zero
t\in\mathfrak{d}_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]}^{-1}
(see the proof of Proposition
5.8
).
Then for
i=1,\cdots,k
and
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]
, we have

\langle x,\lambda(y)\rangle_{i}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})/\mathbb{Q}}(xt_{i}\overline{ty}).

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
(M,N,\langle-,-\rangle)=(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}],\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{\vee},\langle-,-\rangle)
and
\lambda=\lambda_{t}
be as above.

(1)

Suppose
\lambda
is a polarization on
(M,N,\langle-,-\rangle)
. Then for any
i=1,\cdots,k
,
t_{i}\overline{t}
is either totally positive or equal to
0
. Fix one such
i
, if moreover
\langle m_{0},\lambda(m_{0})\rangle_{i}>0
for some
m_{0}\in M
, then
t_{i}\overline{t}
is totally positive. In particular,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\overline{t}
is totally positive.

(2)

Conversely, if for any
i=1,\cdots,k
,
t_{i}\overline{t}
is either totally positive or equal to
0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\overline{t}
is totally positive, then
\lambda
is a polarization on
(M,N,\langle-,-\rangle)
.

Proof.

(1)

By definition, for any
x,y\in M
,

\langle x,\lambda(y)\rangle_{i}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})/\mathbb{Q}}(xt_{i}\overline{ty}),

which is symmetric in
x
and
y
, thus as in the proof of Proposition
5.8
, we know
t_{i}\overline{t}
is totally real. Moreover, for any
x\in M
,

\langle x,\lambda(x)\rangle_{i}\geq 0.

Again as in the proof of Proposition
5.8
,
t_{i}\overline{t}
is totally positive or zero. This gives the first part of the proposition.

For the second part, by assumption,

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})/\mathbb{Q}}(m_{0}\overline{m_{0}}t_{i}\overline{t})\neq 0.

This clearly implies that
t_{i}\overline{t}\neq 0
and thus it is totally positive.

(2)

We apply Proposition
6.1
.

∎

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
(M,N,\langle-,-\rangle)=(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}],\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{\vee},\langle-,-\rangle)
be as above. It has a polarization if and only if
t_{1},\cdots,t_{k}
are not all zero and for any
i,j=1,\cdots,k
,
t_{i}\overline{t_{j}}
is either totally positive or zero.

Proof.

The direction
\Rightarrow
is clear by Proposition
6.5
(1).

Conversely, the morphism
\lambda_{t}\colon M\to N
with
t=t_{1}+\cdots+t_{k}
is a polarization on
(M,N,\langle-,-\rangle)
. Indeed, suppose that
t_{i_{0}}\neq 0
for some
i_{0}=1,\cdots,k
. Then
t_{i}/t_{i_{0}}=t_{i}\overline{t_{i_{0}}}/(t_{i_{0}}\overline{t_{i_{0}}})
is totally positive or zero, and not all of them are zero. Thus
t/t_{i_{0}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}/t_{i_{0}}
is totally positive, in particular,
t\neq 0
. Now for any
i=1,\cdots,k
, we have

t_{i}\overline{t}=t_{i}\overline{t_{i_{0}}}\times\overline{t/t_{i_{0}}},

which is totally positive or zero. Moreover,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\overline{t}=t\overline{t}
is totally positive. By Proposition
6.5
(2), we conclude.
∎

6.3.
Isogeny classes of
(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a},(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a})^{\vee},\langle-,-\rangle)

We are now ready for the classification of a certain class of objects in
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma}^{P,\mathrm{pPol}}
up to isogeny as follows. Let us first give a definition

Definition 6.7
.

Recall that
P=\mathbb{N}^{k}
.

(1)

For a positive integer

T_{r,k}=\left\{(t_{1},\cdots,t_{k})\in(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})^{+})^{k}\mid t_{1},\cdots,t_{k}\text{ totally positive or zero and }\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}=1\right\}

(2)

\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]
, which induces an embedding
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{\phi(r)}(\mathbb{Q})

). We define an action of
\mathrm{GL}_{a}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r}))
on

by

Y(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}):=(YX_{1}Y^{-1},\cdots,YX_{k}Y^{-1}),\quad\forall\,Y\in\mathrm{GL}_{a\phi(r)}(\mathbb{Q}),\,(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k})\in T_{r,k}^{(a)}.

Then we write

for the quotient of

by the action of

in

.

Remark 6.8
.

(1)

For
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})^{+}=\mathbb{Q}
(that is,

), regarding
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})^{+}
as a subfield of

canonically, we have a canonical embedding into the

is just

.

(2)

It is easy to see that both

and

are independent of the choice of the
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]
.

We have the following two classification results, which are the main results of this section

Theorem 6.9

Let

.
The following map defines a bijection

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{(M,N,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma}^{P,\mathrm{pPol}}\mid M\text{ simple free $\Gamma$-module}\right\}/\text{isogeny}&\rightarrow\bigsqcup_{r>0}T_{r,k}\\

(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a},(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a})^{\vee},\Phi)
is polarizable in
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{P}
with polarization
\lambda
(again we can assume
\Gamma^{\prime}
acts trivially on
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a}
). So under the fixed basis of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a}
and the dual basis of
(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a})^{\vee}
, up to replacing

\lambda
by a positive integer multiple of
\lambda
, we can assume that

\lambda
is represented by a matrix of the form

\Lambda=Z^{\mathrm{t}}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}Z

with
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M^{\prime})}(\mathbb{Z})
. Note that

X_{i}^{\mathrm{t}}\Lambda=Z^{\mathrm{t}}\begin{pmatrix}X_{i}^{\prime}&\ast\\
0&X_{i}^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}^{\mathrm{t}}Z^{-\mathrm{t}}Z^{\mathrm{t}}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}Z=Z^{\mathrm{t}}\begin{pmatrix}(X_{i}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}A&\ast\\
\ast&\ast\end{pmatrix}Z.

Since
X_{1}^{\mathrm{t}}\Lambda,\cdots,X_{k}^{\mathrm{t}}\Lambda
are all symmetric and positive semi-definite and
X^{\mathrm{t}}X
is symmetric and positive definite, it follows that
(X_{1}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}A,\cdots,(X_{k}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}A
are all symmetric and positive semi-definite and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}(X_{i}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{t}}A
is symmetric and positive definite. Then it follows that the map
\lambda^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{b}\to(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{b})^{\vee}
represented by the matrix
A
is a polarization for the object
(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{b},(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{b})^{\vee},\Phi^{\prime})
in
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{P}
and thus
(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{b},(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{b})^{\vee},\Phi^{\prime})
is pointwise polarizable in
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma}^{P}
. We conclude that
(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a},(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a})^{\vee},\Phi)
is
not
simple.

∎

Remark 6.12
.

It is not hard to see that
(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a},(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{r}]^{a})^{\vee},\Phi)
is polarizable in
\mathrm{Mor}_{\Gamma}^{P,\mathrm{pPol}}
if and only if for the
k
-tuple

(X_{1}X^{-1},\cdots,X_{k}X^{-1})\in T_{r,k}^{(a)}
, we can choose
\Lambda\in\mathrm{GL}_{a}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r}))
such that
X_{1}^{\mathrm{t}}\Lambda,\cdots,X_{k}^{\mathrm{t}}\Lambda
are all symmetric (as elements in
\mathrm{Mat}_{a\phi(r)}(\mathbb{Q})
), positive semi-definite.

Combining Theorems
6.9
and
6.11
, we get

Corollary 6.13
.

The following map is a bijection

T_{r,k}\simeq T_{r,k}^{(1)}\simeq T_{r,k}^{(1)}/\sim,\quad(t_{1},\cdots,t_{k})\mapsto[t_{1},\cdots,t_{k}].

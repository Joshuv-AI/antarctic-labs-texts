Strongly coupled magnon-plasmon polaritons in graphene- 2D ferromagnet heterostructures
A. T. Costa, M. I. Vasilevskiy, J. Fernández-Rossier, N. M. R. Peres

Abstract
Magnons and plasmons are two very different types of collective modes, acting on the spin and charge degrees of freedom, respectively. At first sight, the formation of hybrid plasmon-magnon polaritons in heterostructures of plasmonic and magnetic systems would face two challenges, the small mutual interaction, via Zeeman coupling of the electromagnetic field of the plasmon with the spins, and the energy mismatch, as in most systems plasmons have energies in the eV range, orders of magnitude larger than magnons. Here we show that graphene plasmons form polaritons with the magnons of two-dimensional ferrromagnetic insulators, placed up to to half a micron apart, with Rabi couplings in the range of 100 GHz (dramatically larger than cavity QED magnonics). This strong coupling is facilitated both by the small energy of graphene plasmons and the cooperative super-radiant nature of the plasmon-magnon coupling afforded by phase matching. We show that the Rabi coupling can be modulated both electrically and mechanically and we propose a attenuated total internal reflection experiment to implement ferromagnetic resonance experiments on 2D ferromagnets driven by plasmon excitation.

There are three main bottlenecks for the existing techniques. One is having

Again, this is limited by the smallness of light’s coupling to magnetic dipoles, and puts a constraint

\begin{split}H_{\mathrm{Z}}=\mu_{B}\sum_{l}\sum_{\vec{q}}iF(q,z)\left[(q_{y}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{l}-q_{x}\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{l})e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{R}_{l}}a_{\vec{q}}-\right.\\

F(q,z)=-\epsilon\frac{\omega^{2}_{\mathrm{pl}}(q)}{c^{2}q\kappa_{\vec{q}}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2A\epsilon_{0}\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(q)\Lambda(\vec{q})}}e^{-\kappa_{\vec{q}}|z|},

(4)

2

\begin{split}H_{\mathrm{Z}}=\mu_{B}\sqrt{N}\sum_{l}\sum_{\vec{q}}iF(q,z)\left[(q_{y}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{\vec{q}}-q_{x}\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{\vec{q}})a_{\vec{q}}-\right.\\
\left.(q_{y}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{-\vec{q}}-q_{x}\hat{\sigma}^{y}_{-\vec{q}})a^{\dagger}_{\vec{q}}\right].\end{split}

(5)

(6)

We now propose to take advantage of the magnon-plasmon coupling to carry out ferromagnetic resonance of monolayers using an attenuated total reflection set-up (see Fig.

Exciting plasmons directly with optical beams is impossible due to the kinematic mismatch between plasmons and
propagating light.
Gonçalves and Peres 2016
By placing a prism of a high dielectric constant material on top of the hBN layer,
it is possible to generate evanescent waves within the hBN that will excite the surface polaritons of the heterostructure.

vectors and frequencies lie between the light cones inside hBN and the dielectric of which the prism is
made. Germanium, for instance, would be a convenient material to use for the prism. It is transparent to electromagnetic
radiations of frequencies below 1 THz and its relative dielectric constant within the same frequency range is
\epsilon_{\mathrm{Ge}}\approx 16
.

Appendix B
Plasmon Green function

The two-time, retarded Green function of the plasmon can be defined as

\mathcal{G}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime};t)\equiv\left\langle\!\!\!\left\langle a_{\vec{k}}(t);a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle\!\!\!\right\rangle\equiv-i\theta(t)\left\langle\left[a_{\vec{k}}(t),a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right]\right\rangle,

(19)

where time evolution is determined in the Heisenberg representation. Wherever the time argument of an operator
is omitted it should be taken as
t=0
. It is straightforward to show that, since the system is translationally invariant,

\mathcal{G}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime};t)=\mathcal{G}(\vec{k};t)\delta_{\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime}}
.
The plasmon Green function obeys the equation of motion,

i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{G}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime};t)=\delta(t)\left\langle\left[a_{\vec{k}},a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right]\right\rangle+\left\langle\!\!\!\left\langle\left[a_{\vec{k}},H\right](t);a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle\!\!\!\right\rangle.

(20)

But,

\left[a_{\vec{k}},H_{\mathrm{pl}}\right]=\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(k)a_{\vec{k}},

(21)

\left[a_{\vec{k}},H_{\mathrm{Z}}\right]=\Omega^{*}_{-\vec{k}}(z)b_{-\vec{k}}+\Omega_{\vec{k}}(z)b^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}.

(22)

Thus,

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{G}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime};t)=\delta(t)\delta_{\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime}}+\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(k)\mathcal{G}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime};t)+\Omega^{*}_{-\vec{k}}(z)\left\langle\!\!\!\left\langle b_{-\vec{k}}(t);a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle\!\!\!\right\rangle+\Omega_{\vec{k}}(z)\left\langle\!\!\!\left\langle b^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}(t);a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle\!\!\!\right\rangle.

(23)

Thus, the equation of motion for the plasmon Green function is one of a (closed) system of coupled equations which also involves the magnon Green function,

G(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime};t)\equiv\left\langle\!\!\!\left\langle b_{\vec{k}}(t);b^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle\!\!\!\right\rangle\equiv-i\theta(t)\left\langle\left[b_{\vec{k}}(t),b^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right]\right\rangle,

(24)

and mixed plasmon-magnon Green functions, such as

\displaystyle\left\langle\!\!\!\left\langle b_{-\vec{k}}(t);a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle\!\!\!\right\rangle\equiv-i\theta(t)\left\langle\left[b_{-\vec{k}}(t),a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right]\right\rangle,

(25)

\displaystyle\left\langle\!\!\!\left\langle b^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}(t);a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle\!\!\!\right\rangle\equiv-i\theta(t)\left\langle\left[b^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}(t),a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}\right]\right\rangle.

(26)

After Fourier transforming to the frequency domain and some subsequent algebra, we obtain

\mathcal{G}(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime},\mathcal{E})=\frac{\delta_{\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{E}-\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(q)-\Sigma_{\mathrm{pl}}(\vec{k},\mathcal{E})},

(27)

where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mathrm{Pl}}(\vec{k},\mathcal{E})\equiv|\Omega_{\vec{k}}(z)|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}-\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k})}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}+\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(-\vec{k})}\right)\times

\displaystyle\left[1+|\Omega_{\vec{k}}(z)|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}-\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k})}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}+\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(-\vec{k})}\right)\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}+\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(k)}\right]^{-1}.

(28)

If the magnon dispersion relation is reciprocal, i.e.,
\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k})=\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(-\vec{k})
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mathrm{pl}}(\vec{k},\mathcal{E})\equiv 2\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(k)|\Omega_{\vec{k}}(z)|^{2}\left[\mathcal{E}^{2}-(\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k}))^{2}\right]^{-1}\times

\displaystyle\left\{1+2\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k})|\Omega_{\vec{k}}(z)|^{2}\left[{\mathcal{E}^{2}-(\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k}))^{2}}\right]^{-1}\left[\mathcal{E}+\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(k)\right]^{-1}\right\}^{-1}.

(29)

We also obtain the magnon Green function,

\displaystyle G(\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime};\mathcal{E})=\frac{\delta_{\vec{k},\vec{k}^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{E}-\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k})-\Sigma_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k};\mathcal{E})},

(30)

where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mathrm{mag}}(\vec{k};\mathcal{E})\equiv 2\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(k)|\Omega_{\vec{k}}(z)|^{2}\left[\mathcal{E}^{2}-(\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(k))^{2}\right]^{-1}\times

\displaystyle\left\{1+2\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(k)|\Omega_{\vec{k}}(z)|^{2}\left[\mathcal{E}^{2}-(\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{pl}}(k))^{2}\right]^{-1}\left[\mathcal{E}+\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{mag}}(-\vec{k})\right]^{-1}\right\}^{-1}.

(31)

Convex optimization via inertial algorithms with vanishing Tikhonov regularization: fast convergence to the minimum norm solution
Hedy Attouch, Szilárd Csaba László

Abstract
In a Hilbertian framework, for the minimization of a general convex differentiable function f f , we introduce new inertial dynamics and algorithms that generate trajectories and iterates that converge fastly towards the minimizer of f f with minimum norm. Our study is based on the non-autonomous version of the Polyak heavy ball method, which, at time t t , is associated with the strongly convex function obtained by adding to f f a Tikhonov regularization term with vanishing coefficient ϵ ⁡ ( t ) \epsilon(t) . In this dynamic, the damping coefficient is proportional to the square root of the Tikhonov regularization parameter ϵ ⁡ ( t ) \epsilon(t) . By adjusting the speed of convergence of ϵ ⁡ ( t ) \epsilon(t) towards zero, we will obtain both rapid convergence towards the infimal value of f f , and the strong convergence of the trajectories towards the element of minimum norm of the set of minimizers of f f . In particular, we obtain an improved version of the dynamic of Su-Boyd-Candès for the accelerated gradient method of Nesterov. This study naturally leads to corresponding first-order algorithms obtained by temporal discretization. In the case of a proper lower semicontinuous and convex function f f , we study the proximal algorithms in detail, and show that they benefit from similar properties.

Introduction

Throughout the paper,
\mathcal{H}
is a real Hilbert space which is endowed with the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, with
\|x\|^{2}=\langle x,x\rangle
for
x\in\mathcal{H}
.
We consider
the convex minimization problem

\min\left\{f(x):\ x\in\mathcal{H}\right\},

(1)

where
f:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex continuously differentiable function whose solution set
S=\argmin f
is nonempty.
We aim at finding by rapid methods the element of minimum norm of
S
.
As an original aspect of our approach, we start from the Polyak heavy ball with friction dynamic for strongly convex functions, and then adapt it to treat the case of general convex functions. Recall that
a function
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\mu
-strongly convex for some
\mu>0
if
f-\frac{\mu}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex.
In this setting, we have the exponential convergence result:

Theorem 1.1

Suppose that
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
which is
\mu
-strongly convex for some
\mu>0
.
Let
x(\cdot):[t_{0},+\infty[\to{\mathcal{H}}
be a solution trajectory of

\ddot{x}(t)+2\sqrt{\mu}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f(x(t))=0.

(2)

Then, the following property holds:

f(x(t))-\min_{\mathcal{H}}f=\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\sqrt{\mu}t}\right)
as
t\to+\infty
.

Let us see how to take advantage of this fast convergence result, and how to adapt it to the case of a general convex differentiable function
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}
. The main idea is linked to Tikhonov’s method of regularization. It consists in considering the corresponding non-autonomous dynamic which at time
t
is governed by the gradient of the strongly convex function
f_{t}:{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}

f_{t}(x):=f(x)+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x\|^{2}.

Then replacing
f
by
f_{t}
in
(
2
), and noticing that
f_{t}
is
\epsilon(t)
-strongly convex, we obtain the dynamic

{\rm(TRIGS)}\qquad\ddot{x}(t)+\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f(x(t))+\epsilon(t)x(t)=0,

with
\delta=2
. (TRIGS) stands shortly for Tikhonov regularization of inertial gradient systems.
In order not to asymptotically modify the equilibria, we suppose that
\epsilon(t)\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
. This condition implies that (TRIGS) falls within the framework of the inertial gradient systems with asymptotically vanishing damping.
The importance of this class of inertial dynamics has been highlighted by several recent studies

AAD1
,
ABotCest
,
AC10
,
ACPR
,
AP
,
CD
,
SBC
, which make the link with the accelerated gradient method of Nesterov
Nest1
;
Nest2
.

Second order gradient dynamics

where the damping coefficient

In the quest for a faster convergence, the following system

1.2
Model results

To illustrate our results, let us consider the case
\epsilon(t)=\frac{c}{t^{r}}
where
r
is positive parameter satisfying

. The case

is of particular interest, it is related to the continuous version of the accelerated gradient method of Nesterov, with optimal convergence rate for general convex differentiable function

.

1.2.1
Case
r=2

Let us consider the (TRIGS) dynamic

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f\left(x(t)\right)+\frac{c}{t^{2}}x(t)=0,

(7)

where the parameter
\alpha\geq 3
plays a crucial role.
As a consequence of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
we have

Theorem 1.2

Let
x:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathcal{H}
be a solution of (
7
).
We then have the following results:

i)
If
\alpha=3
, then

\displaystyle f\left(x(t)\right)-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f=O\left(\frac{\ln t}{t^{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

ii)
If
\alpha>3
, then

\displaystyle f\left(x(t)\right)-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.
Further, the trajectory
x
is bounded,

\displaystyle\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
, and
there is strong convergence to the minimum norm solution:

\liminf_{t\to+\infty}{\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|}=0.

1.2.2
Case
r<2

As a consequence of Theorems
2.4
and
4.2
, we have:

Theorem 1.3

Take
\epsilon(t)=1/t^{r}
,
\frac{2}{3}<r<2
.
Let
x:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathcal{H}
be a global solution trajectory of

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\delta}{t^{\frac{r}{2}}}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f\left(x(t)\right)+\frac{1}{t^{r}}x(t)=0.

Then, we have fast convergence the values, and strong convergence to the minimum norm solution:

f(x(t))-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f=\mathcal{O}\left(\displaystyle{\frac{1}{t^{\frac{3r}{2}-1}}}\right)\mbox{ and }\liminf_{t\to+\infty}{\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|}=0.

These results are completed by showing that, if there exists
T\geq t_{0}
, such that the trajectory
\{x(t):t\geq T\}
stays either in the open ball
B(0,\|x^{\ast}\|)
or in its complement, then
x(t)
converges strongly to
x^{*}
as

Corresponding results for the associated proximal algorithms, obtained by temporal discretization, are obtained in Section
5
.

A remarkable property of the above results is that the rate of convergence of values is comparable to the Nesterov accelerated gradient method. In addition, we have a strong convergence property to the minimum norm solution, with comparable numerical complexity.
These results represent an important advance compared to previous works by producing new dynamics for which we have both rapid convergence of values and strong convergence towards the solution of minimum norm.
Let us stress the fact that in our approach the fast convergence of the values and the strong convergence towards the solution of minimum norm are obtained for the same dynamic, whereas in the previous works
ACR
,
AttCza1
, they are obtained for different dynamics obtained for different settings of the parameters.
It is clear that the results extend naturally to obtaining strong convergence towards the solution closest to a desired state
x_{d}
.
It suffices to replace in Tikhonov’s approximation
\|x\|^{2}
by

\|x-x_{d}\|^{2}
. This is important for inverse problems.

2
Convergence analysis for general
\epsilon(t)

We are going to analyze via Lyapunov analysis the convergence properties as
t\to+\infty
of the solution trajectories of the inertial dynamic (TRIGS) that we recall below

\ddot{x}(t)+\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f(x(t))+\epsilon(t)x(t)=0.

(8)

Throughout the paper, we assume that
t_{0}
is the origin of time,
\delta
is a positive parameter, and

(H_{1})

f:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is convex and differentiable,
\nabla f
is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets.

(H_{2})

S:=\mbox{argmin}f\neq\emptyset
. We denote by
x^{*}
the element of minimum norm of
S
.

(H_{3})

\epsilon:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a nonincreasing function, of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
, such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\epsilon(t)=0
.

2.1
Existence and uniqueness for the Cauchy problem

Let us first show that the Cauchy problem for (TRIGS) is well posed.

Theorem 2.1

Given
(x_{0},v_{0})\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}
, there exists a unique global classical solution
x:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathcal{H}
of the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\ddot{x}(t)+\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla f\left(x(t)\right)+\epsilon(t)x(t)=0\vskip 5.69054pt\\
x(t_{0})=x_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0}.\end{cases}

(9)

Proof

The proof relies on the combination of the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem with energy estimates.
First consider the Hamiltonian formulation of (
9
) as the first order system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)-y(t)=0\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\dot{y}(t)+\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}y(t)+\nabla f\left(x(t)\right)+\epsilon(t)x(t)=0\vskip 2.84526pt\\
x(t_{0})=x_{0},\,y(t_{0})=v_{0}.\end{cases}

(10)

According to the hypothesis
(H_{1}),(H_{2}),(H_{3})
, and by applying the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem in the locally Lipschitz case, we obtain the existence and uniqueness of a local solution.
Then, in order to pass from a local solution to a global solution, we rely on the energy estimate obtained by taking the scalar product of (TRIGS) with
\dot{x}(t)
. It gives

\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+f(x(t))+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(t)\|x(t)\|^{2})\Big)+\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\epsilon}(t)\|x(t)\|^{2}=0.

From
(H_{3})
,
\epsilon(\cdot)
is non-increasing. Therefore, the energy function
t\mapsto W(t)
is decreasing where

W(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+f(x(t))+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(t)\|x(t)\|^{2}.

The end of the proof follows a standard argument. Take a maximal solution defined on an interval
[t_{0},T[
. If
T
is infinite, the
proof is over. Otherwise, if
T
is finite, according to the above energy estimate, we have that
\|\dot{x}(t)\|
remains bounded, just like
\|x(t)\|
and
\|\ddot{x}(t)\|
(use (TRIGS)). Therefore, the limit of
x(t)
and
\dot{x}(t)
exists when
t\to T
. Applying the local existence result at
T
with the initial conditions thus obtained gives a contradiction to the maximality of the solution.

2.2
General case

The control of the decay of
\epsilon(t)
to zero as
t\to+\infty
will play a key role in the Lyapunov analysis of (TRIGS).
Precisely, we will use the following condition.

Definition 1

Given
\delta>0
, we say that
t\mapsto\epsilon(t)
satisfies the controlled decay property
{\rm(CD)}_{K}
, if it is a nonincreasing function which satisfies: there exists
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
such that for all
t\geq t_{1},

\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}}\right)^{\prime}\leq\min(2K-\delta,\delta-K),

where
K
is a parameter such that
\frac{\delta}{2}<K<\delta
for
0<\delta\leq 2
, and
\frac{\delta+\sqrt{\delta^{2}-4}}{2}<K<\delta
for
\delta>2
.

Theorem 2.2

Let
x:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathcal{H}
be a solution trajectory of
(TRIGS)
. Let
\delta
be a positive parameter.
Suppose that
\epsilon(\cdot)
satisfies the condition
{\rm(CD)}_{K}
for some
K>0
.
Then, we have the following rate of convergence of values: for all
t\geq t_{1}

f(x(t))-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f\leq\frac{K\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{M}(t)}\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}(s)\mathfrak{M}(s)ds+\frac{C}{\mathfrak{M}(t)},

(11)

where

\mathfrak{M}(t)=\exp\left({\displaystyle{\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\mu(s)ds}}\right),\quad\mu(t)=-\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2\epsilon(t)}+(\delta-K)\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}

and

C=\left(f(x(t_{1}))-f(x^{\ast})\right)+\frac{\epsilon(t_{1})}{2}\|x(t_{1})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|K\sqrt{\epsilon(t_{1})}(x(t_{1})-x^{\ast})+\dot{x}(t_{1})\|^{2}.

Proof

Lyapunov analysis.
Set
f^{\ast}:=f(x^{\ast})=\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f
. The energy function
\mathcal{E}:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathbb{R}_{+},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t):=\left(f(x(t))-f^{\ast}\right)+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|c(t)(x(t)-x^{\ast})+\dot{x}(t)\|^{2},

(12)

will be the basis for our Lyapunov analysis.
The function
c:[t_{0},+\infty[\to{\mathbb{R}}
will be defined later, appropriately.
Let us differentiate
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
. By using the derivation chain rule, we get

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle{\nabla}f(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle+\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\epsilon(t)\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)\rangle

(13)

\displaystyle+\langle c^{\prime}(t)(x(t)-x^{*})+c(t)\dot{x}(t)+\ddot{x}(t),c(t)(x(t)-x^{\ast})+\dot{x}(t)\rangle.

According to the constitutive equation (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\ddot{x}(t)=-\epsilon(t)x(t)-\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}\dot{x}(t)-\nabla f(x(t)).

(14)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle c^{\prime}(t)(x(t)-x^{*})+c(t)\dot{x}(t)+\ddot{x}(t),c(t)(x(t)-x^{\ast})+\dot{x}(t)\rangle

(15)

\displaystyle=\langle c^{\prime}(t)(x(t)-x^{*})+(c(t)-\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)})\dot{x}(t)-(\epsilon(t)x(t)+\nabla f(x(t))),c(t)(x(t)-x^{\ast})+\dot{x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}(t)c(t)\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}+(c^{\prime}(t)+c^{2}(t)-\delta c(t)\sqrt{\epsilon(t)})\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle+(c(t)-\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)})\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\epsilon(t)\langle x(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle-\langle\nabla f(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle-c(t)\langle\epsilon(t)x(t)+\nabla f(x(t)),x(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle.

By combining (
13
) with (
15
), we get

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+c^{\prime}(t)c(t)\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}+(c^{\prime}(t)+c^{2}(t)-\delta c(t)\sqrt{\epsilon(t)})\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle

(16)

\displaystyle+(c(t)-\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)})\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}-c(t)\langle\epsilon(t)x(t)+\nabla f(x(t)),x(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle.

Consider the function

f_{t}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\,f_{t}(x)=f(x)+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x\|^{2}.

According to the strong convexity property of
f_{t}
, we have

f_{t}(y)-f_{t}(x)\geq\langle{\nabla}f_{t}(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x-y\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

Take
y=x^{*}
and
x=x(t)
in the above inequality. We get

\displaystyle f(x^{*})+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}-f(x(t))-\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle-\langle{\nabla}f(x(t))+\epsilon(t)(x(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle-\langle{\nabla}f(x(t))+\epsilon(t)x(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(f(x(t))-f(x^{*}))

(17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.

By multiplying (
17
) with
c(t)
and injecting in (
16
) we get

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-c(t)(f(x(t))-f^{*})+\left(\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2}-c(t)\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\right)\|x(t)\|^{2}

(18)

\displaystyle+\left(c^{\prime}(t)c(t)-c(t)\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\right)\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}+(c(t)-\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)})\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+(c^{\prime}(t)+c^{2}(t)-\delta c(t)\sqrt{\epsilon(t)})\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle+c(t)\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}.

On the other hand, for a positive function
\mu(t)
we have

\displaystyle\mu(t)\mathcal{E}(t)=

\displaystyle\mu(t)\left(f(x(t))-f^{\ast}\right)+\mu(t)\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu(t)c^{2}(t)\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\mu(t)c(t)\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle.

By adding (
18
) and (
19
) we get

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)+\mu(t)\mathcal{E}(t)\leq

\displaystyle(\mu(t)-c(t))(f(x(t))-f^{\ast})+\left(\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2}-c(t)\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}+\mu(t)\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\right)\|x(t)\|^{2}

(20)

\displaystyle+\left(c^{\prime}(t)c(t)-c(t)\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu(t)c^{2}(t)\right)\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(c(t)-\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}+\frac{1}{2}\mu(t)\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(c^{\prime}(t)+c^{2}(t)-\delta c(t)\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}+\mu(t)c(t)\right)\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle+c(t)\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}.

\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle

c^{\prime}(t)+c^{2}(t)-\delta c(t)\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}+\mu(t)c(t)=0.

(21)

Take

\mu(t)=-\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2\epsilon(t)}+(\delta-K)\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}.

According to this choice for
\mu(t)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)+\mu(t)\mathcal{E}(t)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\epsilon(t)}\left(-\dot{\epsilon}(t)+2(\delta-2K){\epsilon(t)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)(f(x(t))-f^{\ast})

(22)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(\dot{\epsilon}(t)+2\left(\delta-2K\right)\epsilon(t)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\|x(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{K}{4}\left(K\dot{\epsilon}(t)+2\epsilon(t)^{\frac{3}{2}}(-K^{2}+\delta K-1)\right)\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\epsilon(t)}\left(-\dot{\epsilon}(t)+2(K-\delta){\epsilon(t)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{K\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2}\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}(t).

Let us show that the condition
{\rm(CD)}_{K}
provide the nonpositive sign for the coefficients in front of the terms of the right side of (

Recall that, according to the hypotheses
{\rm(CD)}_{K}
, for all

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq c\|x^{*}\|^{2}\frac{\ln t}{t^{2}}-c\|x^{*}\|^{2}\frac{\ln t_{0}}{t^{2}}+\frac{t_{0}^{2}\mathcal{E}(t_{0})}{t^{2}}.

Consequently, we have

(55)

By definition of

f(x(t))-\min f=O\left(\frac{\ln t}{t^{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

(56)

which gives the claim.

3.2
Strong convergence

Theorem 3.2

Let
t_{0}>0
and, for some starting points

The proof combines energetic and geometric arguments, as it was initiated in

I.

\forall t>0\;\;\|x_{t}\|\leq\|x^{*}\|,\;\mbox{ and }\;\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|=0.

(57)

Using the gradient inequality for the strongly convex function
f_{t}

f_{t}(x(t))-f_{t}(x_{t})\geq\frac{c}{2t^{2}}\|x(t)-x_{t}\|^{2}.

On the other hand

By adding the last two inequalities we get

f_{t}(x(t))-f_{t}(x^{*})\geq\frac{c}{2t^{2}}(\|x(t)-x_{t}\|^{2}+\|x_{t}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2}),

(58)

\displaystyle=(f(x(t))-f(x^{*}))+\frac{c}{2t^{2}}(\|x(t)\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{K}{t}(x(t)-x^{*})+\dot{x}(t)\right\|^{2}.

\displaystyle+

5
Fast inertial algorithms with Tikhonov regularization

On the basis of the convergence properties of continuous dynamic (TRIGS), one would expect to obtain similar results for the algorithms resulting from its temporal discretization.
To illustrate this, we will do a detailed study of the
associated proximal algorithms, obtained by implicit discretization.
A full study of the associated first-order algorithms would be beyond the scope of this article, and will be the subject of further study.
So, for
k\geq 1
, consider the discrete dynamic

(x_{k+1}-2x_{k}+x_{k-1})+\frac{\alpha}{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})+\nabla f(x_{k+1})+\frac{c}{k^{2}}\xi_{k}=0,

(76)

with time step size equal to one. We take
\xi_{k}=x_{k}
, which gives

{\rm(IPATRE)}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}y_{k}=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\\
\rule{0.0pt}{15.0pt}x_{k+1}={\rm prox}_{f}\left(y_{k}-\frac{c}{k^{2}}x_{k}\right),\end{array}\right.

where (IPATRE) stands for Inertial Proximal Algorithm with Tikhonov REgularization.
According to (
76
) we have

x_{k+1}=\alpha_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\nabla f(x_{k+1})+\left(1-\frac{c}{k^{2}}\right)x_{k}.

(77)

5.1
Convergence of values

We have the following result.

Theorem 5.1

Let
(x_{k})
be a sequence generated by
(IPATRE)
. Assume that
\alpha>3
. Then for all
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right[
the following hold:

(i)

f(x_{k})-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f=o(k^{-2s})
,
\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|=o(k^{-s})
and
\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|=o(k^{-s})
as
k\to+\infty.

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2s-1}(f(x_{k})-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f)<+\infty,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2s-1}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}<+\infty
,
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2s}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}<+\infty
.

Proof

Given
x^{*}\in\argmin f
, set
f^{*}=f(x^{*})=\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f
.
For
k\geq 2
, consider the discrete energy

E_{k}:=\|a_{k-1}(x_{k-1}-x^{*})+b_{k-1}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}+{\nabla}f(x_{k}))\|^{2}+d_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}\|^{2},

(78)

where
a_{k}=ak^{r-1},\;2<a<\alpha-1
and
b_{k}=k^{r}
,
r\in]0,1]
. The sequence
(d_{k})
will be defined later. Set shortly
c_{k}:=\frac{c}{k^{2}}.

Let us develop
E_{k}
.

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{k-1}^{2}\|x_{k-1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+b_{k-1}^{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k-1}^{2}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}+2a_{k-1}b_{k-1}\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k-1}-x^{*}\rangle

(79)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2a_{k-1}b_{k-1}\langle{\nabla}f(x_{k}),x_{k-1}-x^{*}\rangle+2b_{k-1}^{2}\langle{\nabla}f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle+d_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}.

Further

\displaystyle 2a_{k-1}b_{k-1}\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k-1}-x^{*}\rangle=a_{k-1}b_{k-1}(\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x_{k-1}-x^{*}\|^{2})

\displaystyle 2a_{k-1}b_{k-1}\langle{\nabla}f(x_{k}),x_{k-1}-x^{*}\rangle=2a_{k-1}b_{k-1}\langle{\nabla}f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle-2a_{k-1}b_{k-1}\langle{\nabla}f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle.

Consequently, (
79

\displaystyle E_{k}=a_{k-1}b_{k-1}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+(a_{k-1}^{2}-a_{k-1}b_{k-1})\|x_{k-1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+(b_{k-1}^{2}-a_{k-1}b_{k-1})\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+b_{k-1}^{2}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}+2a_{k-1}b_{k-1}\langle{\nabla}f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+(2b_{k-1}^{2}-2a_{k-1}b_{k-1})\langle{\nabla}f(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle+d_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}.\hskip 227.62204pt

(80)

(81)

\displaystyle-2\alpha_{k}b_{k}^{2}c_{k}\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k}\rangle-2a_{k}b_{k}c_{k}\langle x_{k},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle+d_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}.

Further,

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{k}a_{k}b_{k}\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle=-\alpha_{k}a_{k}b_{k}(\|x_{k-1}-x^{*}\|-\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2})

(82)

\displaystyle+\eta_{k}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k-1}^{2}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq a_{k}b_{k}c_{k}\|x^{*}\|^{2}.

Finally, take
d_{k-1}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k}b_{k}^{2}c_{k}.
Then,
\sigma_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{k+1}b_{k+1}^{2}c_{k+1}\sim Ck^{2r-2}
,
\sigma_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\geq k_{5}=\max(\alpha-1,k_{4}).
Further,
\mu_{k},m_{k},\nu_{k},n_{k},s_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
are nonnegative for all
k\geq k_{5}
and
r\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
.

Assume now that
\frac{1}{2}\leq r<1.

According to
\sum_{k\geq k_{5}}a_{k}b_{k}c_{k}\|x^{*}\|^{2}=ac\|x^{*}\|^{2}\sum_{k\geq k_{5}}k^{2r-3}=C<+\infty
, by summing up (
91
) from
k=k_{5}
to
k=n>k_{5}
, we obtain that there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle E_{n+1}\leq C_{1},

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(f(x_{n})-f^{*})\leq C_{1},\mbox{ hence }f(x_{n})-f^{*}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-2r}),

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq k_{5}}m_{k}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})\leq C_{1},\mbox{ hence }\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-1}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})<+\infty,

\displaystyle\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq C_{1},\mbox{ hence }\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(n^{1-r}),

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq k_{5}}n_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq C_{1},\mbox{ hence }\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-3}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}<+\infty,

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}\leq C_{1},\mbox{ hence }\|x_{n}\|=\mathcal{O}(n^{1-r}),

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq k_{5}}s_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}\leq C_{1},\mbox{ hence }\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-3}\|x_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq k_{5}}\eta_{k}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}\leq C_{1},\mbox{ hence }\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-1}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}<+\infty

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq k_{5}}b_{k-1}^{2}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq C_{1},\mbox{ hence }\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}<+\infty.

Since
\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}<+\infty
, we have
\|{\nabla}f(x_{n})\|=o(n^{-r}).
Combining this property with
E_{n+1}\leq C_{1}
yields
\sup_{n\geq 1}\|an^{r-1}(x_{n}-x^{*})+n^{r}(x_{n+1}-x_{n})\|+\frac{c}{2}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)n^{2r-2}\|x_{n-1}\|^{2}<+\infty
.

Let us show now, that
f(x_{n})-f^{*}=o(n^{-2r})
and
\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|=o(n^{-r}).

From (
91
) we get

\sum_{k\geq 1}[(E_{k+1}+\mu_{k}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})+\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2})-(E_{k}+\mu_{k-1}(f(x_{k-1})-f^{*})+\nu_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}-x^{*}\|^{2})]_{+}<+\infty.

Therefore, the following limit exists

\lim_{k\to+\infty}(\|ak^{r-1}(x_{k}-x^{*})+k^{r}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\|^{2}+d_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}+\mu_{k}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})+\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}).

Note that
d_{k}\sim Ck^{2r-2},\,\mu_{k}\sim Ck^{2r}
and
\nu_{k}\sim Ck^{2r-2}.
Further, we have

\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-3}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}<+\infty
,
\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-1}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}<+\infty
,
\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-1}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})<+\infty
and
\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-3}\|x_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty
, hence

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}(\|ak^{r-1}(x_{k}-x^{*})+k^{r}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\|^{2}+d_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}+\mu_{k}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})+\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2})<+\infty.

Since
\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}=+\infty
we get

\lim_{k\to+\infty}(\|ak^{r-1}(x_{k}-x^{*})+k^{r}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\|^{2}+d_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}+\mu_{k}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})+\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2})=0

and the claim follows.

Remark 4

The convergence rate of the values is
f(x_{k})-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f=o(k^{-2s})
for any
0<s<1
.
Practically it is as good as the rate
f\left(x(t)\right)-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)
obtained for the continuous dynamic.

5.2
Strong convergence to the minimum norm solution

Theorem 5.2

Take
\alpha>3
. Let
(x_{k})
be a sequence generated by
(IPATRE)
. Let
x^{*}
be the minimum norm element of
\argmin f
. Then,
\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|=0
. Further,
(x_{k})
converges strongly to
x^{*}
whenever
(x_{k})
is in the interior of the ball
B(0,\|x^{*}\|)
for
k
large enough, or
(x_{k})
is in the complement of the ball
B(0,\|x^{*}\|)
for
k
large enough.

Proof

Case I.

Assume that there exists
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\|x_{k}\|\geq\|x^{*}\|
for all
k\geq k_{0}.

Set
c_{k}=\frac{c}{k^{2}},
and define
f_{c_{k}}(x):=f(x)+\frac{c}{2k^{2}}\|x\|^{2}
.
Consider the energy function defined in (
78
) with
r=1
, that is
a_{k}=a
and
b_{k}=k^{2}
, where we assume that
\max(2,\alpha-2)<a<\alpha-1.
Then,

E_{k}=\|a(x_{k-1}-x^{*})+(k-1)^{2}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}+{\nabla}f(x_{k}))\|^{2}+d_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}\|^{2},

where the sequence
(d_{k})
will be defined later. Next, we introduce another energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}c_{k-1}(\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})+\|a(x_{k-1}-x^{*})+(k-1)^{2}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}+\nabla f(x_{k}))\|^{2}+d_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}.

(92)

Note that
\mathcal{E}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}c_{k-1}(\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})+E_{k}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}c_{k}(\|x_{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}c_{k-1}(\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})+E_{k+1}-E_{k}.

(93)

According to (
91
), there exists
k_{1}\geq k_{0}
such that for all
k\geq k_{1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}+\mu_{k}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})-\mu_{k-1}(f(x_{k-1})-f^{*})+m_{k}(f(x_{k})-f^{*})

(94)

\displaystyle+\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\nu_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+n_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\eta_{k}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k-1}^{2}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq-\sigma_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}+\sigma_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-s_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}c_{k}(\|x_{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}c_{k-1}(\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})+a_{k}b_{k}c_{k}\|x^{*}\|^{2}.

Adding
\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{k}+m_{k})c_{k}(\|x_{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{k-1}c_{k-1}(\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})
to both side of (
94
)
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}+\mu_{k}(f_{c_{k}}(x_{k})-f_{c_{k}}(x^{*}))-\mu_{k-1}(f_{c_{k-1}}(x_{k-1})-f_{c_{k-1}}(x^{*}))+m_{k}(f_{c_{k}}(x_{k})-f_{c_{k}}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle+\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\nu_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+n_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\eta_{k}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k-1}^{2}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}

(95)

\displaystyle\leq-\sigma_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}+\sigma_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-s_{k}\|x_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{k}+m_{k}+1)c_{k}(\|x_{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{k-1}+1)c_{k-1}(\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})+a_{k}b_{k}c_{k}\|x^{*}\|^{2}.

The right hand side of (
95
) can be written as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{k}+m_{k}+1)c_{k}-\sigma_{k}-s_{k}\right)(\|x_{k}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})

\displaystyle+\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{k-1}+1)c_{k-1}+\sigma_{k-1}\right)(\|x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2})+(a_{k}b_{k}c_{k}-\sigma_{k}-s_{k}+\sigma_{k-1})\|x^{*}\|^{2}.

In this case we have
\mu_{k}=2b_{k}^{2}-2a_{k}b_{k}=2k^{2}-2ak
and
m_{k}=2b_{k-1}^{2}-2b_{k}^{2}+2a_{k}b_{k}=2(a-2)k+2
. Further,

\sigma_{k}=\alpha_{k+1}b_{k+1}^{2}c_{k+1}-d_{k}=c-\frac{\alpha c}{k+1}-d_{k}
and
s_{k}=b_{k}^{2}c_{k}^{2}-\alpha_{k+1}b_{k+1}^{2}c_{k+1}+\alpha_{k}b_{k}^{2}c_{k}+a_{k}b_{k}c_{k}=\frac{\alpha c}{k+1}+\frac{c(a-\alpha)}{k}+\frac{c^{2}}{k^{2}}.

Now, take
d_{k}=\frac{(a+2-\alpha)c}{2k}\geq 0
and an easy computation gives that there exists
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
such that for all
k\geq k_{2}
one has

\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{k}+m_{k}+1)c_{k}-\sigma_{k}-s_{k}=-\frac{(a+2-\alpha)c}{2k}+\frac{2c^{2}-3c}{2k^{2}}\leq 0,

-\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{k-1}+1)c_{k-1}+\sigma_{k-1}=\frac{c(a-2-\alpha)}{2(k-1)}+\frac{\alpha c}{k(k-1)}-\frac{c}{2(k-1)^{2}}\leq 0

a_{k}b_{k}c_{k}-\sigma_{k}-s_{k}+\sigma_{k-1}=\frac{(a+2-\alpha)c}{2k}-\frac{(a+2-\alpha)c}{2(k-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{k^{2}}\leq 0.

Now, since by assumption
\|x_{k}\|\geq\|x^{*}\|
for
k\geq k_{0}
, we get that the right hand side of (
95
) is nonpositive for all
k\geq k_{2}.
Hence, for all
k\geq k_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k+1}-\mathcal{E}_{k}+\mu_{k}(f_{c_{k}}(x_{k})-f_{c_{k}}(x^{*}))-\mu_{k-1}(f_{c_{k-1}}(x_{k-1})-f_{c_{k-1}}(x^{*}))+m_{k}(f_{c_{k}}(x_{k})-f_{c_{k}}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle+\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\nu_{k-1}\|x_{k-1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+n_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\eta_{k}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k-1}^{2}\|{\nabla}f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq 0.

(96)

Note that
\nu_{k}\sim C
. Therefore, from (
96
), similarly as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we deduce that

\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|
is bounded, and therefore
(x_{k})
is bounded.
Further,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}(\|a(x_{k}-x^{*})+k(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\|^{2}+\mu_{k}(f_{c_{k}}(x_{k})-f_{c_{k}}(x^{*}))+\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2})=0,

that is,
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\nu_{k}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}=0
and hence

\lim_{k\to+\infty}x_{k}=x^{*}.

Case II.
Assume that there exists
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\|x_{k}\|<\|x^{*}\|
for all
k\geq k_{0}.
From there we get that
(x_{k})
is bounded. Now, take
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{H}
a weak sequential cluster point of
(x_{k}),
which exists since
(x_{k})
is bounded. This means that there exists a sequence
\left(k_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[k_{0},+\infty)\cap{\mathbb{N}}
such that
k_{n}\to+\infty
and
x_{k_{n}}
converges weakly to
\bar{x}
as
n\to+\infty
. Since
f
is weakly lower semicontinuous, according to Theorem
5.1
we have

f(\bar{x})\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}f\left(x_{k_{n}}\right)=\min f\,,
hence
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{argmin}f.

Since the norm is weakly lower semicontinuous, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{c}\|\bar{x}\|\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left\|x_{k_{n}}\right\|\leq\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|.\end{array}

According to the definition of
x^{\ast}
, we get
\bar{x}=x^{*}.
Therefore
(x_{k})
converges weakly to
x^{\ast}
. So

\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|x_{k}\|\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|x_{k}\|\leq\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|.

Therefore, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|x_{k}\|=\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|.

From the previous relation and the fact that
x_{k}\rightharpoonup x^{\ast}
as
k\to+\infty,
we obtain the strong convergence, that is

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}x_{k}=x^{\ast}.

Case III.
Suppose that for every
k\geq k_{0}
there exists
l\geq k
such that
\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|>\|x_{l}\|
, and suppose also there exists
m\geq k
such that
\left\|x^{*}\right\|\leq\|x_{m}\|
.
So, let
k_{1}\geq k_{0}
and
l_{1}\geq k_{1}
such that
\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|>\|x_{l_{1}}\|.

Let
k_{2}>l_{1}
and
l_{2}\geq k_{2}
such that
\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|>\|x_{l_{2}}\|.
Continuing the process, we obtain
(x_{l_{n}})
, a subsequence of
(x_{k})
with the property that
\|x_{l_{n}}\|<\|x^{*}\|
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
By reasoning as in
Case II
, we obtain that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}x_{l_{n}}=x^{\ast}.

Consequently,

\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|x_{k}-x^{\ast}\|=0.

5.3
Non-smooth case

Let us extend the results of the previous sections to the case of a proper lower semicontinuous and convex function
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}
.
We rely on the basic properties of the Moreau envelope
f_{\lambda}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
(
\lambda
is a positive real parameter), which is defined by

f_{\lambda}(x)=\min_{z\in{\mathcal{H}}}\left\{f(z)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|z-x\|^{2}\right\},\quad\text{for any $x\in{\mathcal{H}}$.}

Recall that
f_{\lambda}
is a convex differentiable function, whose gradient is
\lambda^{-1}
-Lipschitz continuous, and such that
\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f=\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f_{\lambda}
,
\argmin_{{\mathcal{H}}}f_{\lambda}=\argmin_{{\mathcal{H}}}f
.
The interested reader may refer to
BC
;
Bre1
for a comprehensive treatment of the Moreau envelope in a Hilbert setting. Since the set of minimizers is preserved by taking the Moreau envelope, the idea is to replace
f
by
f_{\lambda}
in the previous algorithm, and take advantage of the fact that
f_{\lambda}
is continuously differentiable. Then, algorithm
{\rm(IPATRE)}
applied to
f_{\lambda}
now reads (recall that
\alpha_{k}=1-\frac{\alpha}{k}
)

\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm(IPATRE)}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}y_{k}=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\\
\rule{0.0pt}{15.0pt}x_{k+1}={\rm prox}_{f_{\lambda}}\left(y_{k}-\frac{c}{k^{2}}x_{k}\right).\end{array}\right.\end{array}

By applying Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
, we obtain fast convergence of the sequence
(x_{k})
to the element of minimum norm of
f
.
Thus, we just need to formulate these results in terms of
f
and its proximal mapping. This is straightforward thanks to the following formulae from proximal calculus
BC
:

1.

f_{\lambda}(x)=f(\prox_{\lambda f}(x))+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-\prox_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}
.

2.

\nabla f_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(x-\prox_{\lambda f}(x)\right)
.

3.

\prox_{\theta f_{\lambda}}(x)=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\theta}x+\frac{\theta}{\lambda+\theta}\prox_{(\lambda+\theta)f}(x).

We obtain the following relaxed inertial proximal algorithm (NS stands for non-smooth):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\rm\mbox{(IPATRE-NS)}}\quad\begin{cases}y_{k}=x_{k}+(1-\frac{\alpha}{k})(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\\
x_{k+1}=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\left(y_{k}-\frac{c}{k^{2}}x_{k}\right)+\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\prox_{(\lambda+1)f}\left(y_{k}-\frac{c}{k^{2}}x_{k}\right).\end{cases}\end{array}\vskip 5.69054pt

Theorem 5.3

Let
f:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}
be a convex, lower semicontinuous, proper function. Assume that
\alpha>3
. Let
(x_{k})
be a sequence generated by

(IPATRE-NS)
. Then for all
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right[
, we have:

(i)
f({\prox}_{\lambda f}(x_{k}))-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f=o(k^{-2s})
,
\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|=o(k^{-s})
,

\|x_{k}-{\prox}_{\lambda f}(x_{k}))\|=o(k^{-s})
as
k\to+\infty.

(ii)
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2s-1}(f({\prox}_{\lambda f}(x_{k}))-\min_{{\mathcal{H}}}f)<+\infty,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2s-1}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}<+\infty
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2s}\|x_{k}-{\prox}_{\lambda f}(x_{k}))\|^{2}<+\infty
.

(iii)
\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|=0
. Further,
(x_{k})
converges strongly to
x^{*}
the element of minimum norm of

\argmin f
, if
(x_{k})
is in the interior of the ball
B(0,\|x^{*}\|)
for
k
large enough, or if
(x_{k})
is in the complement of the ball
B(0,\|x^{*}\|)
for
k
large enough.

6
Conclusion, perspective

In the framework of convex optimization in general Hilbert spaces, we have introduced an inertial dynamic in which the damping coefficient and the Tikhonov regularization coefficient vanish as time tends to infinity.
The judicious adjustment of these parameters makes it possible to obtain trajectories converging quickly (and strongly) towards the minimum norm solution.
This seems to be the first time that these two properties have been obtained for the same dynamic. Indeed, the Nesterov accelerated gradient method and the hierarchical minimization attached to the Tikhonov regularization are fully effective within this dynamic.
On the basis of Lyapunov’s analysis, we have developed an in-depth mathematical study of the dynamic which is a valuable tool for the development of corresponding results for algorithms obtained by temporal discretization. We thus obtained similar results for the corresponding proximal algorithms.
This study opens up a large field of promising research concerning first-order optimization algorithms.
Many interesting questions such as the introduction of Hessian-driven damping to attenuate oscillations

ACFR
,
APR
,
BCL
, and the study of the impact of errors, perutrbations, deserve further study.
These results also adapt well to the numerical analysis of inverse problems for which strong convergence and obtaining a solution close to a desired state are key properties.

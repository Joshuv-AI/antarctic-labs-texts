On the Post-Lie Structure in SISO Affine Feedback Control Systems
Kurusch Ebrahimi-Fard, W. Steven Gray, Venkatesh G. S

Abstract
. The main objective of this work is to show that the single-input, single-output (SISO) affine feedback group, a transformation group in the context of the affine feedback interconnection of Chen–Fliess series, is a post-group in the sense of Bai, Guo, Sheng and Tang.



1.
Introduction

Consider two normed linear spaces of real-valued functions
U
and
Y
defined on a common interval
I
and two maps
F_{\text{f}}:U\rightarrow Y
and
F_{\text{fb}}:Y\rightarrow U
. An
additive feedback connection
with
F_{\text{f}}
in the forward path and
F_{\text{fb}}
in the feedback path is described by a
third map
F:U\rightarrow Y
satisfying the feedback equations:

y=F[u],\quad v=F_{\text{fb}}[y],\;\text{ and }\;y=F_{\text{f}}[u+v]

for all
u\in U
. Such recursive interconnections are a central object of study in control theory
[
21
]
, where they are represented diagrammatically in Figure
1
.

{F_{\mathrm{f}}}
+
u
v
{F_{\mathrm{fb}}}
y

Figure 1
.
Map
F_{\text{f}}
in additive feedback with map
F_{\text{fb}}
.

The general objective is to determine
F_{\text{fb}}
for a given
F_{\text{f}}
so that
F
has certain desirable properties such as having a finite operator norm. An
affine feedback connection
is an important generalization of this set up where now the feedback equations take the form:

(1)

y=F[u],\;v_{i}=F_{\text{fb},i}[y],\ i=1,2,\ \text{and}\ y=F_{\text{f}}[uv_{1}+v_{2}]

for all
u\in U
. Here the product
uv_{1}
of real-valued functions is defined pointwise on
I
.

It is well known that many types of feedback connections can be re-cast in terms of a feedback transformation group acting on a given map
F_{\text{f}}

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
15
,
17
,
22
,
23
,
30
]
.
The affine feedback transformation group presented in
[
17
]
describes the affine feedback connection when all the maps involved have Chen–Fliess series representations
[
10
,
11
,
21
]
. An important invariant under this group action is the
relative degree
of
F_{\text{f}}
. Loosely speaking, it describes the minimum number of integrations that
F_{\text{f}}
applies to
u
. It can be defined and computed in terms of a smooth finite dimensional state space realization for
F_{\text{f}}
when one is available
[
21
,
29
]
or in a
coordinate free manner when
F_{\text{f}}
is a single-input, single-output (SISO) system and has only a Chen–Fliess series representation
[
16
]
.
Maps with relative degree are known to be left-invertible so that there exists an
F_{\text{fb}}
rendering
F
exactly linear
[
21
,
29
]
. So-called
feedback linearization
is a standard design method in control engineering for systems with nonlinear dynamics.

Given
F_{\text{f}}
and
F_{\text{fb},i}
, solving the feedback equations (
1
) for
F
ultimately involves computing an inverse in the affine feedback transformation group. This calculation can be done inductively using the graded connected commutative polynomial Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}
of coordinate functions associated with the group
[
17
]
. Specifically, the generating series for the Chen–Fliess representation of
F
is computed using the antipode of
\mathcal{H}
.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is a free commutative, right-handed Hopf algebra, there is an underlying right pre-Lie product defined on the space of infinitesimal characters associated with the group
[
27
]
. Its exact form was determined in
[
17
]
by treating the group as a formal Lie group and computing the set of all left-invariant vector fields at the identity element. Subsequently, it was discovered by Foissy in
[
13
]
that the corresponding Lie algebra for this group coincides exactly with a suitably defined post-Lie algebra
\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{SISO}}},\curvearrowleft,\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\right)
with
\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{SISO}}},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket\right)
being the derived Lie algebra. Since
\mathcal{H}
is commutative, by the Cartier–Milnor–Moore theorem, the graded dual of
\mathcal{H}
, say
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
, is isomorphic to the enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{SISO}},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket\right)
as connected graded Hopf algebras.

It is shown in
[
2
]
that the integration of a post-Lie algebra results in a post-group (see
[
1
]
for a detailed review of post-groups). The main contribution of the current work is to present a concise description of the post-group
\left(G,\cdot,\triangleleft\right)
whose Grossman–Larson group
\left(G,\star\right)
is the affine feedback transformation group.
The group
\left(G,\cdot\right)
, which is isomorphic to the semi-direct product of the additive group and the shuffle group of formal power series, went unnoticed in the previous works on affine feedback interconnections of Chen–Fliess series
[
17
,
13
]
. The post-group structure is derived purely from the
ab initio
view of the affine feedback interconnection of Chen–Fliess series stemming from the earlier work of the first and second authors
[
17
]
. Thus, the polynomial Hopf algebra of coordinate functions,
\mathcal{H}
, has a cointeracting bialgebra structure, where the two cointeracting coproducts
\delta
and
\Delta
come from dualizing the two group products
\left(G,\cdot\right)
and the Grossman–Larson group product
\left(G,\star\right)
associated with the post-group. The cointeraction picture in
\mathcal{H}
is another contribution of the present work.

2.
Post-Groups: An Overview

The notion of a post-group recently appeared in the work of Bai, Guo, Sheng and Tang
[
2
]
. See
[
26
,
25
]
for an equivalent formulation using the notion of a crossed morphism. Post-Lie algebras are the infinitesimal objects of post-groups as explained in Section
5
. The main definition is given
below.

Definition 2.1
.

[
2
]

a)

A (right) post-group is a group
(G,\cdot)
equipped with a product
\triangleleft:G\times G\longrightarrow G
such that:

(i)

For all
a\in G
, the right multiplication map
R^{\triangleleft}_{a}:G\longrightarrow G

R^{\triangleleft}_{a}(b):=b\triangleleft a

is an automorphism of the group
\left(G,\cdot\right)
, that is,

\displaystyle R^{\triangleleft}_{a}(b\cdot c)=R^{\triangleleft}_{a}(b)\cdot R^{\triangleleft}_{a}(c).

(ii)

For any
a,b\in G
, the following relation holds

(2)

\displaystyle R^{\triangleleft}_{a}\circ R^{\triangleleft}_{b}=R^{\triangleleft}_{R^{\triangleleft}_{a}(b)\cdot a}.

b)

If
\left(G,\cdot\right)
is Abelian, then
\left(G,\cdot,\triangleleft\right)
is called a pre-group.

From (
2
), one can deduce that the collection
\{R^{\triangleleft}_{a}:a\in G\}
of automorphisms of the group
\left(G,\cdot\right)
itself forms a group with the composition (group) product defined by

(3)

\displaystyle b\star a:=R^{\triangleleft}_{a}(b)\cdot a=\left(b\triangleleft a\right)\cdot a

for all
a,b
in the post-group
\left(G,\cdot,\triangleleft\right)
such that
R^{\triangleleft}_{a}\circ R^{\triangleleft}_{b}=R^{\triangleleft}_{b\star a}
. Indeed, the following result confirms this statement (see
[
1
]
for a brief survey of the main results in
[
2
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

[
2
]

If
\left(G,\cdot,\triangleleft\right)
is a post-group, then
\left(G,\star\right)
forms a group called the Grossman–Larson group and shares the unit with
(G,\cdot)
. The inverse with respect to (
3
) is

\displaystyle a^{\star-1}=(R^{\triangleleft}_{a})^{\circ-1}(a^{\cdot-1}),

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} R^{\triangleleft}:\left(G,\star\right)&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\left(\left(G,\cdot\right),\circ\right)\\
a&\longmapsto R^{\triangleleft}_{a}\end{aligned}

is a group homomorphism.
1
1

1

Note that in
[
2
]
,
\left(G,\star\right)
was called the sub–adjacent group. The authors believe, however, that the name Grossman–Larson group
[
1
]
is more adequate in light of the origins of this notion in the work of Guin–Oudom
[
20
]
on the Lie enveloping algebra of a pre-Lie algebra. See also
[
7
]
for the post-Lie case.

(1)

Observe that for any
a,b\in G

(a\triangleleft b^{\star-1})\star b=\big((a\triangleleft b^{\star-1})\triangleleft b\big)\cdot b=a\cdot b.

(2)

Given a post-group
\left(G,\cdot,\triangleleft\right)
, the opposite post-group
\left(G,\centerdot,\blacktriangleleft\right)
is defined in terms of the opposite group product,
a\centerdot b:=b\cdot a
, and for any
a,b\in G
the product
a\blacktriangleleft b:=a\cdot(a\triangleleft b)\cdot a^{\cdot-1}

[
1
]
.

(3)

Natural examples of pre- and post-groups are found in the universal enveloping algebras of free pre-, respectively, post-Lie algebras (see Section
5
). In the former case, the pre-group is the Butcher group, i.e., the characters of the Butcher–Connes–Kreimer Hopf algebra of rooted trees
[

Theorem 4.3
.

5.
From post-Lie Group to post-Lie Algebra

Let
\mathbb{K}
denote the base field of characteristic zero. Given a binary product
\diamond
on a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
V
, the associator is defined for elements
x,y,z\in V
as

{\mathrm{a}}_{\diamond}(x,y,z):=(x\diamond y)\diamond z-x\diamond(y\diamond z).

When different from zero, the associator expresses the non-associativity of the product
\diamond
.

Definition 5.1
.

[
28
,
32
]

A (right)
post-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\curvearrowleft)
consists of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with Lie bracket
[-,-]
and a product
\curvearrowleft:{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, called the post-Lie product, such that the following relations hold for all elements
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle[x,y]\curvearrowleft z

\displaystyle=[x\curvearrowleft z,y]+[x,y\curvearrowleft z],

\displaystyle z\curvearrowleft[x,y]

\displaystyle={\mathrm{a}}_{\curvearrowleft}(z,x,y)-{\mathrm{a}}_{\curvearrowleft}(z,y,x).

Given a post-Lie algebra, a second Lie bracket can be defined on
{\mathfrak{g}}

(12)

\llbracket x,y\rrbracket:=x\curvearrowleft y-y\curvearrowleft x+[x,y],\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

If the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
in Definition
5.1
has a trivial Lie bracket, then the post-Lie algebra reduces to a (right)
pre-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\curvearrowleft)
, defined in terms of the (right) pre-Lie identity

(13)

(x\curvearrowleft y)\curvearrowleft z-x\curvearrowleft(y\curvearrowleft z)=(x\curvearrowleft z)\curvearrowleft y-x\curvearrowleft(z\curvearrowleft y).

In this case, the Lie bracket in (
12
) reduces to

(14)

\llbracket x,y\rrbracket:=x\curvearrowleft y-y\curvearrowleft x.

The pre-Lie relation (
13
) implies that (
14
) is a Lie algebra. Hence, anti-symmetrization is a covariant functor from the category of pre-Lie algebras over
\mathbb{K}
to Lie algebras over
\mathbb{K}
. For more details on pre–Lie algebras, refer
[
24
]
.

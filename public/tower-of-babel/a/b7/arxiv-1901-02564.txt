All-heavy tetraquarks
Ming-Sheng Liu 1,4 , Qi-Fang Lü 1,4 , Xian-Hui Zhong 1,4 , Qiang Zhao 2,3,4

Abstract
In this work, we study the mass spectra of the all-heavy tetraquark systems, i.e, c ​ c ​ c ¯ ​ c ¯ cc\bar{c}\bar{c} , b ​ b ​ b ¯ ​ b ¯ bb\bar{b}\bar{b} , b ​ b ​ c ¯ ​ c ¯ / c ​ c ​ b ¯ ​ b ¯ bb\bar{c}\bar{c}/cc\bar{b}\bar{b} , b ​ c ​ c ¯ ​ c ¯ / c ​ c ​ b ¯ ​ c ¯ bc\bar{c}\bar{c}/cc\bar{b}\bar{c} , b ​ c ​ b ¯ ​ b ¯ / b ​ b ​ b ¯ ​ c ¯ bc\bar{b}\bar{b}/bb\bar{b}\bar{c} , and b ​ c ​ b ¯ ​ c ¯ bc\bar{b}\bar{c} , within a potential model by including the linear confining potential, Coulomb potential, and spin-spin interactions. It shows that the linear confining potential has important contributions to the masses and is crucial for our understanding of the mass spectra of the all-heavy tetraquark systems. For the all-heavy tetraquarks Q 1 ​ Q 2 ​ Q ¯ 3 ​ Q ¯ 4 Q_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}\bar{Q}_{4} , our explicit calculations suggest that no bound states can be formed below the thresholds of any meson pairs ( Q 1 ​ Q ¯ 3 ) (Q_{1}\bar{Q}_{3}) - ( Q 2 ​ Q ¯ 4 ) (Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{4}) or ( Q 1 ​ Q ¯ 4 ) (Q_{1}\bar{Q}_{4}) - ( Q 2 ​ Q ¯ 3 ) (Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}) . Thus, we do not expect narrow all-heavy tetraquark states to be existing in experiments.

II.2
Hamiltonian for the multiquark system

The following nonrelativistic Hamiltonian is adopted for the calculation of the masses of the all-heavy
Q_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}\bar{Q}_{4}
system:

H=(\sum_{i=1}^{4}m_{i}+T_{i})-T_{G}+\sum_{i<j}V_{ij}(r_{ij}),

(5)

where
m_{i}
and
T_{i}
stand for the constituent quark mass and kinetic energy of the
i
th quark, respectively;
T_{G}
stands for the center-of-mass (c.m.) kinetic energy of the
Q_{1}Q_{2}\bar{Q}_{3}\bar{Q}_{4}
system;
r_{ij}\equiv|\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j}|
is the distance between the
i
th quark and
j
th quark; and
V_{ij}(r_{ij})
stands for the effective potential between the
i
th and
j
th quark.
In this work, we adopt a widely used potential form for
V_{ij}(r_{ij})

Eichten:1978tg
;
Godfrey:1985xj
;
Swanson:2005
;
Godfrey:2015dia
;
Godfrey:2004ya
;
Lakhina:2006fy
;
Lu:2016bbk
;
Li:2010vx
;
Deng:2016stx
;
Deng:2016ktl
, i.e,

V_{ij}(r_{ij})=V_{ij}^{OGE}(r_{ij})+V_{ij}^{Conf}(r_{ij})\ ,

(6)

where
V^{OGE}_{ij}
stands for the one-gluon-exchange (OGE) potential which describes the short-range quark-quark interactions, while
V^{Conf}_{ij}(r_{ij})
stands for the confinement potential which describes the long-range interaction behaviors. The form of
V^{OGE}_{ij}
is given by

V^{OGE}_{ij}=\frac{\alpha_{ij}}{4}({\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}}_{i}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}}_{j})\left\{\frac{1}{r_{ij}}-\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{\sigma^{3}_{ij}e^{-\sigma^{2}_{ij}r_{ij}^{2}}}{\pi^{3/2}}\cdot\frac{4}{3m_{i}m_{j}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}}_{i}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}}_{j})\right\},

(7)

where
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{i}
are the Pauli matrices, and
\alpha_{ij}
stands for the strong coupling strength between two quarks.
If the interaction occurs between two quarks or antiquarks, the
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{j}
operator appearing in Eq. (
7
) is defined as

\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{j}\equiv\sum_{a=1}^{8}\lambda_{i}^{a}\lambda_{j}^{a}
, while if the interaction occurs between a quark and antiquark, the
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{j}
operator is defined as
\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{j}\equiv\sum_{a=1}^{8}-\lambda_{i}^{a}\lambda_{j}^{a*}
, where
\lambda^{a*}
is the complex conjugate of the Gell-Mann matrix
\lambda^{a}
. The OGE potential
V^{OGE}_{ij}
is composed of the Coulomb type potential
V^{OGE}_{coul}\propto(\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{j})(1/r_{ij})
which provides the short-range interaction, and the color-magnetic interaction
V^{OGE}_{CM}\propto(\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{j})({\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}}_{i}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}}_{j})
which provides mass splittings. The form of
V^{Conf}_{ij}(r_{ij})
is given by

V^{Conf}_{ij}(r_{ij})=-\frac{3}{16}({\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}}_{i}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}}_{j})\cdot br_{ij},

(8)

where the parameter
b
denotes the strength of the confinement potential.

,
m_{b}
,

,
\alpha_{bb}
,
\alpha_{bc}
,

II.3
Matrix elements in color and spin spaces

In order to obtain the mass of a tetraquark state from the nonrelativistic Hamiltonian defined in Eq. (
5
),
first one needs to calculate the matrix elements of
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}_{j}\rangle

II.4
Matrix elements in the coordinate space

The trail wave function of the four-quark states without any spatial excitations in the coordinate space is
expanded by a series of Gaussian functions,

\psi({\mathbf{r_{1}},\mathbf{r_{2}},\mathbf{r_{3}},\mathbf{r_{4}}})=\prod_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i\ell}\left(\frac{1}{\pi b^{2}_{i\ell}}\right)^{3/4}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2b^{2}_{i\ell}}r^{2}_{i}\right],

(25)

where the parameters
b_{i\ell}
are related to the harmonic oscillator frequencies
\omega_{\ell}
with
1/b^{2}_{i\ell}=m_{i}\omega_{\ell}
.
With the ansatz of the mass independent frequency
\omega_{\ell}
for a quark of mass
m_{i}

Straub:1988gj
, i.e.,

1/b^{2}_{i\ell}=1/b^{2}_{\ell}=m_{u}\omega_{\ell}
(
m_{u}
=313 MeV), the trail wave function of the four quark states can be simplified to be

\displaystyle\psi({\mathbf{r_{1}},\mathbf{r_{2}},\mathbf{r_{3}},\mathbf{r_{4}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\left(\frac{m_{i}\omega_{\ell}}{\pi}\right)^{3/4}\exp\left[-\frac{m_{i}\omega_{l}}{2}r^{2}_{i}\right]

(26)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\phi(\omega_{\ell},\mathbf{r}_{i}),

which is often adopted in the calculations of the multiquark systems
Zhang:2007mu
;
Zhang:2005jz
.

In the coordinate space, we need work out the matrix elements of
\langle 1/r_{ij}\rangle
,
\langle e^{-\sigma^{2}_{ij}r_{ij}^{2}}\rangle
, and
\langle r_{ij}\rangle
. Combing the trail wave functions defined in Eq. (
26
), we obtain

\left\langle\psi(\omega_{\ell},\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j})\left|\frac{1}{r_{ij}}\right|\psi(\omega_{\ell^{\prime}},\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j})\right\rangle=2\sqrt{\frac{m_{ij}}{\pi}}\frac{(\omega_{\ell}\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})^{3/2}}{(\frac{\omega_{\ell}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}}{2})^{5/2}},

(27)

\left\langle\psi(\omega_{\ell},\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j})\left|e^{-\sigma^{2}_{ij}r_{ij}^{2}}\right|\psi(\omega_{\ell^{\prime}},\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j})\right\rangle=\left(\frac{m_{ij}(\frac{2\omega_{\ell}\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}}{\omega_{\ell}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}})}{m_{ij}\frac{\omega_{\ell}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}}{2}+\sigma_{ij}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}},

(28)

\left\langle\psi(\omega_{\ell},\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j})\left|r_{ij}\right|\psi(\omega_{\ell^{\prime}},\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j})\right\rangle=2\sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi m_{ij}}}\frac{(\omega_{\ell}\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})^{3/2}}{(\frac{\omega_{\ell}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}}{2})^{7/2}},

(29)

where
\psi(\omega_{i},\mathbf{r}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{j})\equiv\phi(\omega_{\ell},\mathbf{r}_{i})\phi(\omega_{\ell},\mathbf{r}_{j})
,
m_{ij}=m_{i}m_{j}/(m_{i}+m_{j})
.

To separate out the center-of-mass kinetic energy
T_{G}
and finally work out the kinetic energy matrix element
\langle\sum_{i=1}^{4}T_{i}-T_{G}\rangle
, we need to redefine the coordinates by the following Jacobi coordinates,

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{1}}-\mathbf{r_{2}},

(30)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{3}}-\mathbf{r_{4}},

(31)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}\mathbf{r_{1}}+m_{2}\mathbf{r_{2}}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}-\frac{m_{3}\mathbf{r_{3}}+m_{4}\mathbf{r_{4}}}{m_{3}+m_{4}},

(32)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{4}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}\mathbf{r_{1}}+m_{2}\mathbf{r_{2}}+m_{3}\mathbf{r_{3}}+m_{4}\mathbf{r_{4}}}{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}+m_{4}},

(33)

with these one can rewritten the Eq. (
26
) as

\psi(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{3},\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{4})=\sum^{n}_{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\left(\frac{\mu_{i}\omega_{\ell}}{\pi}\right)^{3/4}\exp\left[-\frac{\mu_{i}\omega_{\ell}}{2}\xi^{2}_{i}\right],

(34)

where
\mu_{1}\equiv m_{1}m_{2}/(m_{1}+m_{2})
,
\mu_{2}\equiv m_{3}m_{4}/(m_{3}+m_{4})
,
\mu_{3}\equiv(m_{1}+m_{2})(m_{3}+m_{4})/M
,
\mu_{4}=M\equiv m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}+m_{4}
.
With the trail wave function defined in Eq. (
34
), the kinetic energy matrix element is worked out to be

\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{4}T_{i}-T_{G}\right\rangle=\frac{9}{4}\sum_{\ell}^{n}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}\mathcal{C}_{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{(\omega_{\ell}\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})^{4}}{(\frac{\omega_{\ell}+\omega_{\ell^{\prime}}}{2})^{7}}.

(35)

III
results and discussions

In this work, we adopt the variation principle to solve the Schrödinger equation.
Following the method used in Ref.
Hiyama:2003cu
, the oscillator length
b_{\ell}
are set to be

b_{\ell}=b_{1}a^{\ell-1}\ \ \ (\ell=1,...,n),

(36)

where
n
is the number of Gaussian functions, and
a
is the ratio coefficient. There are three parameters
\{b_{1},b_{n},n\}
to be determined through variation method. It is found that when we take
b_{1}=0.1
fm,
b_{n}=4
fm,
n=15
, we will obtain stable solutions for the four-quark systems.

When all the matrix elements have been worked out, we can solve the generalized matrix eigenvalue problem,

\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{n}(H_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}-E_{\ell}N_{\ell\ell^{\prime}})\mathcal{C}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell}=0,

(37)

where

H_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\left\langle\psi(\omega_{\ell})\varphi\zeta\chi\Big|H\Big|\psi(\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})\varphi\zeta\chi\right\rangle,

(38)

N_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\left\langle\psi(\omega_{\ell})\varphi\zeta\chi\Big|\psi(\omega_{\ell^{\prime}})\varphi\zeta\chi\right\rangle,

(39)

with
\psi(\omega_{\ell})=\prod_{i=1}^{4}\left(\frac{\mu_{i}\omega_{\ell}}{\pi}\right)^{3/4}\exp\left[-\frac{\mu_{i}\omega_{\ell}}{2}\xi^{2}_{i}\right]
.

\varphi
,
\zeta
, and
\chi
stand for the flavor, color, and spin wave functions, respectively. The physical state corresponds to the solution with a minimum energy
E_{m}
. By solving this generalized matrix eigenvalue problem, the mass of the tetraquark configuration and its spacial wave function can be determined.

III.1
The
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
and
bb\bar{b}\bar{b}
systems

The predicted mass spectrum for the
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
system has been given in Table
6
and also shown in Fig.
1
(a).
From Table
6
, it is found that in the two
I^{G}(J^{PC})=0^{+}(0^{++})
states there is a sizable configuration mixing between
|\{cc\}^{6}_{0}\{\bar{c}\bar{c}\}^{\bar{6}}_{0}\rangle^{0}_{0}
and
|\{cc\}^{\bar{3}}_{1}\{\bar{c}\bar{c}\}^{3}_{1}\rangle^{0}_{0}
.
The
J^{PC}=0^{++}
state with a higher mass, 6550 MeV, is dominant by the
|\{cc\}^{6}_{0}\{\bar{c}\bar{c}\}^{\bar{6}}_{0}\rangle^{0}_{0}
configuration, while another
J^{PC}=0^{++}
state with a lower mass, 6455 MeV, is dominant by the
|\{cc\}^{\bar{3}}_{1}\{\bar{c}\bar{c}\}^{3}_{1}\rangle^{0}_{0}
configuration. The mass splitting between these two
J^{PC}=0^{++}
states is about
95
MeV. The other two states
J^{PC}=1^{+-}
and
J^{PC}=2^{++}
are also located in a similar mass region, i.e.
\sim 6.5
GeV, and the mass splitting between them is about 20 MeV. As shown in Fig.
1
(a), the two
J^{PC}=0^{++}
states are above the thresholds of the charmonium pairs for about
260\sim 580
MeV. It suggests that the
J^{PC}=0^{++}
states are unstable, and they can easily decay into the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi J/\psi
final states through quark rearrangements. The
J^{PC}=1^{+-}
state lies about 420 MeV above the mass threshold of
\eta_{c}J/\psi
, while
J^{PC}=2^{++}
is about 330 MeV above the mass threshold of
J/\psi J/\psi
, they can also easily decay into
\eta_{c}J/\psi
and
J/\psi J/\psi
; respectively, through the quark rearrangements.

As a comparison, our predicted masses and some other typical results from other works are collected in Table
7
.
It shows that our predicted masses for the
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
system are roughly compatible with the nonrelativistic quark model predictions of Refs.
Lloyd:2003yc
;
Ader:1981db
, where both confining and Coulomb potentials are considered. It is also interesting to find that similar results are given by the QCD sum rules
Chen:2016jxd
. In contrast, the masses predicted by us are much larger than those predicted in Refs.
Berezhnoy:2011xn
;
Debastiani:2017msn
;
Wang:2017jtz
;
Barnea:2006sd
;
Karliner:2016zzc
;
Anwar:2017toa
.
These methods which obtained small masses have some common features: either no confining potentials were explicitly included
Berezhnoy:2011xn
;
Barnea:2006sd
;
Karliner:2016zzc
;
Anwar:2017toa
or a diquark picture was adopted in the calculations
Wang:2017jtz
;
Debastiani:2017msn
. Recently, Wu
et al.
also obtained a large mass
\sim 6.8-7.0
GeV for the
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
system with the heavier constituent
c
-quark mass
1.72
GeV adopted
Wu:2016vtq
.

We further analyze the contributions from each part of the Hamiltonian for the
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
system. The results are listed in Table
9
. It shows that the averaged kinetic energy
\langle T\rangle
, the confining potential
\langle V^{Conf}\rangle
, and the Coulomb potential
\langle V^{OGE}_{coul}\rangle
have the same order of magnitude. In particular, the contributions from the confining potential are sizeable and apparently cannot be neglected. Note that the confining potential contributes a positive energy to the system. Thus, neglecting this contribution will lead to much lower masses for the all-heavy system. In Refs.
Berezhnoy:2011xn
;
Barnea:2006sd
;
Karliner:2016zzc
;
Anwar:2017toa
, the confining potential was explicitly neglected. Although part of the confining potential effects can be taken into account by the effective constituent quark masses in the ground states, our calculation shows that the impact from the inclusion of the confining potential seems not to be on the constituent quark masses in the heavy quark sector, but rather on the relative strengths of the averaged matrix elements among the terms of the nonrelativistic Hamiltonian.

In order to examine the role played by the confining potential in the spectrum of heavy quark system, we compare the contributions from the OGE and confining potential for the
\eta_{c}
meson, i.e,
\langle V^{OGE}_{coul}\rangle\simeq-637
MeV and
\langle V^{Conf}\rangle\simeq 233
MeV, which are consistent with our previous study in Ref.
Deng:2016stx
.
The ratio between the confining potential
\langle V^{Conf}\rangle
and color Coulomb potential
\langle V^{OGE}_{coul}\rangle
can reach up to

\left|\frac{\langle V^{conf}\rangle}{\langle V^{OGE}_{coul}\rangle}\right|\simeq 36\%.

(40)

This explicit result suggests that the neglect of confining potential cannot be justified for the
c\bar{c}
system.

As a general conclusion, we find that the confining potential has significant
contributions to the masses of the
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
system, and are the same order of magnitude as the color Coulomb potential. This will enhance the masses of the
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
system and does not support the existence of a bound tetraquark of
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
with narrow widths.

IV
Summary

In this work, we study the mass spectra of the all-heavy
cc\bar{c}\bar{c}
,
bb\bar{b}\bar{b}
,
bb\bar{c}\bar{c}/cc\bar{b}\bar{b}
,
bc\bar{c}\bar{c}/cc\bar{b}\bar{c}
,
bc\bar{b}\bar{b}/bb\bar{b}\bar{c}
, and
bc\bar{b}\bar{c}
systems in the potential quark model with the linear confining potential, Coulomb potential, and spin-spin interactions included. We find that the linear confining potential contributes large positive energies to the eigenvalues of the ground states of these tetraquark systems. This is different from some existing calculations in the literature in which the neglect of the confining potential contributions leads to relatively low masses for the all-heavy systems and some of those can be lower than the two-body decay thresholds. In our case, all these states are found to have masses above the corresponding two meson decay thresholds via the quark rearrangement. This implies that narrow all-heavy tetraquark states may not exist in reality. Nevertheless, our explicit calculations suggest that the confining potential still plays an important role in the heavy flavor multiquark system, and it is crucial to include it in dynamical calculations in order to gain a better understanding of the multiquark dynamics. More experimental information from the Belle-II and LHCb analyses would be able to clarify these issues in the near future.

Strong well-posedness and separation properties for a bulk-surface convective Cahn–Hilliard system with singular potentials
Patrik Knopf, Jonas Stange

Abstract




This is a preprint version of the paper. Please cite as:

P. Knopf, J. Stange,

Journal of Differential Equations

439
, no. 113408, 2025,

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jde.2025.113408

Abstract

This paper addresses the well-posedness of a general class of bulk-surface convective Cahn–Hilliard systems with singular potentials. For this model, we first prove the existence of a global-in-time weak solution by approximating the singular potentials via a Yosida regularization, applying the corresponding results for regular potentials, and eventually passing to the limit in this approximation scheme. Then, we prove the uniqueness of weak solutions and their continuous dependence on the velocity fields and the initial data. Afterwards, assuming additional regularity of the domain as well as the velocity fields, we establish higher regularity properties of weak solutions and eventually the existence of strong solutions. In the end, we discuss strict separation properties for logarithmic type potentials in both two and three dimensions.

Keywords:
convective Cahn–Hilliard equation, bulk-surface interaction, dynamic boundary conditions, strong solutions, separation property, Yosida approximation.

1  Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the following
bulk-surface convective Cahn–Hilliard system
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi+\textnormal{div}(\phi\boldsymbol{v})=\textnormal{div}(m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla\mu)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ Q,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\mu=-\epsilon\Delta\phi+\epsilon^{-1}F^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ Q,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi+\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(\psi\boldsymbol{w})=\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta)-\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\theta=-\epsilon_{\Gamma}\kappa\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+\epsilon_{\Gamma}^{-1}G^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\epsilon\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\epsilon K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=\alpha\psi-\phi&\text{if}\ K\in[0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=0&\text{if}\ K=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(1.1e)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Lm_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=\beta\theta-\mu&\text{if}\ L\in[0,\infty),\\
m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=0&\text{if}\ L=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(1.1f)

\displaystyle\phi|_{t=0}=\phi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega,

(1.1g)

\displaystyle\psi|_{t=0}=\psi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma.

(1.1h)

This system of equations has already been investigated in
[
43
]
, and in the special case
K=0
, it appears as a subsystem in the Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard model with dynamic boundary conditions that was derived in
[
37
]
.

In (
1.1
),
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(with
d=2,3
) denotes a bounded domain with boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
,
T>0
is a given final time, and for brevity we write
Q\coloneqq\Omega\times(0,T)
and
\Sigma\coloneqq\Gamma\times(0,T)
. We denote by
\mathbf{n}
the outward pointing unit normal vector on
\Gamma
and by
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}
the outward normal derivative on the boundary. Furthermore, the symbols
\nabla_{\Gamma}
,
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}
, and
\Delta_{\Gamma}
stand for the surface gradient, the surface divergence, and the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\Gamma
, respectively.

The functions
\phi:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\mu:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
represent the phase-field and the chemical potential in the bulk, whereas
\psi:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\theta:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the phase-field and the chemical potential on the surface, respectively. Moreover, the functions
\boldsymbol{v}:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{w}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
are prescribed velocity fields corresponding to the flow of the two materials in the bulk and on the boundary, respectively. Furthermore, the parameter
\epsilon>0
is related to the thickness of the diffuse interface in the bulk, while
\epsilon_{\Gamma}>0
corresponds to the width of the diffuse interface on the boundary. The constant
\kappa>0
acts as a weight for surface diffusion effects.

In (
1.1
), the time evolution of
\left(\phi,\mu\right)
is given by the
bulk convective Cahn–Hilliard subsystem
(
1.1a
)-(
1.1b
), while the evolution of
\left(\psi,\theta\right)
is determined by the
surface convective Cahn–Hilliard subsystem
(
1.1c
)-(
1.1d
), which is coupled to the bulk variables by means of the involved normal derivatives
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi
and
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu
. Moreover, the bulk and surface quantities are coupled by the boundary conditions (
1.1e
) and (
1.1f
), which depend on parameters
K,L\in[0,\infty]
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

In (
1.1e
) and (
1.1f
), the parameters
K,L\in[0,\infty]
are used to distinguish different cases, each corresponding to a certain solution behavior related to a physical situation. The case that has been studied most extensively in the literature is
K=0
, which corresponds to the Dirichlet condition
\phi=\alpha\psi
on
\Sigma
. This case makes particular sense along with
\alpha=1
, if the materials on the surface are the same as in the bulk. Nevertheless, we also want to cover the case where the materials on the boundary are not the same as in the bulk. For instance, this might be the case if the materials on the boundary are transformed by chemical reactions. This is taken into account in our model by the choice
K\in(0,\infty]
. If
K=\infty
, the boundary condition (
1.1e
) degenerates to a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition for the phase-field
\phi
. Although, for most applications, this might not be the preferred choice, as such a Neumann boundary condition enforces the diffuse interface to always intersect the boundary at a perfect angle of ninety degrees, we include this case in our analysis for the sake of completeness. In the case
K\in(0,\infty)
, (
1.1e
) can be regarded as a Robin-type boundary condition. It can be used to describe a physical scenario where
\phi
and
\psi
represent different materials, and therefore,
\psi
and the trace of
\phi
are (in general) not proportional. However, this setup still allows for a dynamic change of the contact angle between the diffuse interface and the boundary.
In some sense, including the cases
K\in(0,\infty)
is also helpful for the mathematical analysis. For instance, even for regular potentials
F
and
G
, a Faedo–Galerkin scheme to construct weak solutions cannot be used in the case
K=0
and
L\in(0,\infty]
. For more details, we refer to
[
20
,
43
]
.

The bulk and surface chemical potentials
\mu
and
\theta
are coupled through the boundary condition (
1.1e
) involving a parameter
L\in[0,\infty]
, which accounts for a possible transfer of material between bulk and surface, see
[
40
]
. For the non-convective case, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{v}\equiv\mathbf{0}
and
\boldsymbol{w}\equiv\mathbf{0}
, the choice
L=0
was first proposed in
[
23
]
and
[
38
]
. Then,
\mu
and
\theta
are coupled through the Dirichlet condition
\mu=\beta\theta
on
\Sigma
, which means that
\mu
and
\theta
are assumed to always remain in chemical equilibrium. In this case, a rapid transfer of material between bulk and boundary can be expected (see, e.g.,
[
40
]
). The choice
L=\infty
was introduced in
[
44
]
. In this case, (
1.1f
) is a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition on
\mu
, which means that the mass flux between the bulk and the surface is zero, and consequently, no transfer of material between bulk and surface will occur. In such a situation, the chemical potentials
\mu
and
\theta
are not directly coupled. The choice
L\in(0,\infty)
was proposed and analyzed in
[
40
]
. Here, the chemical potentials
\mu
and
\theta
are coupled by a Robin-type boundary condition (
1.1f
). In this case, a transfer of material between bulk and surface will occur, and the coefficient
L^{-1}
is related to the kinetic rate associated with adsorption and desorption processes or chemical reactions at the boundary. Furthermore, it was shown in
[
40
,
47
]
(in the non-convective case) and in
[
43
]
(in the convective case), that the limits
L\rightarrow 0
and
L\rightarrow\infty
can be used to recover the boundary condition (
1.1f
) with
L=0
and
L=\infty
, respectively.

The functions
F^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}
are the derivatives of double-well potentials
F
and
G
, respectively.
A physically motivated example of the potentials
F
and
G
, especially in applications related to material science, is the
logarithmic potential
, which is also referred to as the
Flory–Huggins potential
. It is given by

\displaystyle W_{\textup{log}}(s)\coloneqq\frac{\Theta}{2}\big[(1+s)\ln(1+s)+(1-s)\ln(1-s)\big]-\frac{\Theta_{c}}{2}s^{2},\hskip 10.00002pts\in[-1,1].

(1.2)

Here,
\Theta
represents the temperature of the system and
\Theta_{c}
stands for the critical temperature under which phase separation occurs. These constants are supposed to satisfy
0<\Theta<\Theta_{c}
. The logarithmic potential is often approximated by a
regular double-well potential
which reads as

\displaystyle W_{\textup{reg}}(s)\coloneqq c(s^{2}-1)^{2},\hskip 10.00002pts\in\mathbb{R}

(1.3)

for a suitable constant
c>0
. A further common choice is the
double-obstacle potential
, which is given by

\displaystyle W_{\textup{obst}}(s)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1-s^{2},&\text{if\penalty\ }s\in[-1,1],\\
+\infty,&\text{if\penalty\ }s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-1,1].\end{cases}

(1.4)

In contrast to the regular potential
W_{\text{reg}}
, the potentials
W_{\text{log}}
and
W_{\text{obst}}
are classified as
singular potentials
as these functions or their derivatives exhibit singularities at
\pm 1
.
Physically reasonable singular potentials can be decomposed as the sum of a smooth part and a convex part, which might not be differentiable but it at least possesses a subdifferential that is a maximal monotone graph.

An important quantity associated with the system (
3.1
) is the energy functional

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{K}(\phi,\psi)&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+\epsilon^{-1}F(\phi)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\epsilon_{\Gamma}\kappa}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right|^{2}+\epsilon_{\Gamma}^{-1}G(\psi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\alpha\psi-\phi\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\end{split}

(1.5)

where the function

\displaystyle\sigma(K)\coloneqq\begin{cases}K^{-1},&\text{if }K\in(0,\infty),\\
0,&\text{if }K\in\{0,\infty\}\end{cases}

is used to distinguish the different cases corresponding to the choice of
K
. Sufficiently regular solutions of the system (
3.1
) satisfy the the
mass conservation law

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L=\infty\end{cases}

(1.6)

for all
t\in[0,T]
. This means that the mass is conserved separately in
\Omega
and
\Gamma
in the case
L=\infty
, whereas in the case
L\in[0,\infty)
, a transfer of material between bulk and surface is allowed. Furthermore, sufficiently regular solutions satisfy the
energy identity

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{K}(\phi,\psi)&=\int_{\Omega}\phi\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla\mu\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\sigma(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\end{split}

(1.7)

on
[0,T]
. Note that in the non-convective case, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{v}\equiv\mathbf{0}
and
\boldsymbol{w}\equiv\mathbf{0}
, the right-hand side of (
1.7
) is clearly non-positive. This means that the energy dissipates over the course of time, and the terms on the right-hand side of (
1.7
) can be interpreted as the dissipation rate. In the convective case, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{v}
and/or
\boldsymbol{w}
are non-trivial, we can (in general) not infer dissipation of the energy from the energy identity (
1.7
). However, if it holds
\boldsymbol{w}=\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau}
and the velocity field
\boldsymbol{v}
is determined by a Navier–Stokes equations (cf.
[
37
,
26
]
), or a Brinkman/Stokes equation (cf.
[
20
]
), an energy dissipation for the corresponding total energy can be obtained.

Goals and novelties of this paper.

(I)

Well-posedness of the convective Cahn–Hilliard system (
1.1
) with singular potentials.

In the present paper, we first extend the results for regular potentials obtained in
[
43
]
to the case of singular potentials.
This means we establish the existence of a global weak solution (see Theorem
3.4
) as well as continuous dependence on the initial data and the velocity fields in the case of constant mobilities (see Theorem
3.6
), which entails the uniqueness of the respective weak solution.
Thanks to
[
43
]
, we already have the existence of a global weak solution of (
1.1
) at hand, provided that
F
and
G
are regular potentials. Therefore, our strategy to approach singular potentials is to regularize the subdifferentials of their convex parts by means of a Yosida approximation. Then, the approximate problem can be solved by means of the results established in
[
43
]
.
Next, we derive suitable bounds on the approximate solutions which are uniform with respect to the approximation parameter
\varepsilon
from the Yosida approximation. Eventually, using compactness arguments, we can pass to the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, which yields a weak solution to (
1.1
) with the original singular potentials. The continuous dependence estimate can be proved by the energy method.
In this way, our new results extend the existing literature concerning the well-posedness of convective Cahn–Hilliard system with dynamic boundary conditions and singular potentials (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
19
,
34
]
).

(II)

Existence of strong solutions.
The next step is to prove higher regularity results for weak solutions in the case of constant mobilities and under additional regularity assumptions of the velocity fields (see Theorem
3.7
). These higher regularity results are established by approximating the time derivatives in (
1.1
) by suitable difference quotients, deriving uniform estimates, and applying regularity theory for bulk-surface elliptic systems (see Proposition
A.1
and
[
40
, Theorem 3.3]
).

(III)

Separation properties.

Eventually, we show strict separation properties for the unique strong solution (see Theorem
3.11
).
This means that the corresponding phase-fields will stay strictly away from the pure phases
\pm 1
. For a general class of singular potentials (see
(S1)
-
(S4)
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\text{For almost all $t\in[0,T]$, there exists $\delta(t)\in(0,1]$ such that:}\\
&\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1-\delta(t)\quad\text{and}\quad\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq 1-\delta(t).\end{aligned}

(1.8)

If
G
is a logarithmic type potential (see
(S6)
),
\psi
even satisfies the uniform strict separation property:

\displaystyle\text{There exists $\delta_{\ast}\in(0,1]$ such that for all $(z,t)\in\Sigma$:}\hskip 10.00002pt\left|\psi(z,t)\right|\leq 1-\delta_{\ast}.

(1.9)

If
d=2
and
F
is a logarithmic type potential (see
(S5)
), we analogously obtain the uniform strict separation property for
\phi
:

\displaystyle\text{There exists $\delta_{\star}\in(0,1]$ such that for all $(x,t)\in Q$:}\hskip 10.00002pt\left|\phi(x,t)\right|\leq 1-\delta_{\star}.

(1.10)

It is not surprising that this result only holds in two dimensions, since even for the standard Cahn–Hilliard equation with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions on both
\phi
and
\mu
, the uniform strict separation property (
1.10
) has not yet been established in three dimensions. However, as the boundary
\Gamma
is a two-dimensional submanifold, we are still able to obtain (
1.9
) by adapting the techniques for the two-dimensional Cahn–Hilliard equation in the bulk.

For the non-convective bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard system (i.e., (
1.1
) with
\boldsymbol{v}\equiv\mathbf{0}
and
\boldsymbol{w}\equiv\mathbf{0}
), such separation properties have already been established in the cases
(K,L)\in\big(\{0\}\times[0,\infty]\big)\cup\big([0,\infty)\times\{\infty\}\big)
(see
[
22
,
45
,
46
]
).

In the present paper, we extend these results by showing the above separation properties for the
convective
bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard sysem (i.e., with non-trivial velocity fields
\boldsymbol{v}
and
\boldsymbol{w}
) for all parameter choices
(K,L)\in[0,\infty]^{2}
.

We further refer to
[
29
]
and
[
24
,
35
,
54
]
for strict separation properties of the anisotropic Cahn–Hilliard equation and the nonlocal Cahn–Hilliard equation, respectively.

Structure of this paper.

After introducing the notion of a weak solution of (
3.1
), we state our main results in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we prove the existence of weak solutions to the Cahn–Hilliard system (
3.1
). Afterwards, in Section
5
, we establish the uniqueness of the weak solutions and its continuous dependence on the velocity fields and the initial data. Section
6
is devoted to proving higher regularity results and in particular, the existence of strong solutions under suitable additional assumptions. Lastly, for logarithmic type potentials, we establish the separation properties in Section
7
.

2.2  Assumptions

(A1)

We consider a bounded domain
\emptyset\neq\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\in\{2,3\}
with Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
and a final time

\displaystyle Q\coloneqq\Omega\times(0,T),\hskip 10.00002pt\Sigma\coloneqq\Gamma\times(0,T).

(A2)

The constants occurring in the system (

) satisfy
\epsilon,\epsilon_{\Gamma},\kappa>0
, and

2.3  Preliminaries

(P1)

For any real numbers
s\geq 0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{p}\coloneqq L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Gamma),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{W}^{s,p}\coloneqq W^{s,p}(\Omega)\times W^{s,p}(\Gamma),

provided that
\Gamma
is sufficiently regular. We abbreviate
\mathcal{H}^{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{W}^{s,2}
and identify
\mathcal{L}^{2}
with
\mathcal{H}^{0}
. Note that
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product

\displaystyle\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\big)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\coloneqq\left(\phi,\zeta\right)_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+\left(\psi,\xi\right)_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{s}

and its induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\coloneqq\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{1/2}
. We recall that the duality pairing can be expressed as

\displaystyle\big<\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}=\left(\phi,\zeta\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(\psi,\xi\right)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

for all
\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{s}
if
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
.

(P2)

Let
L\in[0,\infty]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be real numbers. We introduce the subspace

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}^{1},&\text{if }L\in(0,\infty],\\
\{(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}:\phi=\beta\psi\text{ a.e.\penalty\ on }\Gamma\},&\text{if }L=0,\end{cases}

endowed with the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}}\coloneqq\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}
and its induced norm. The space
\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}
is a Hilbert space. Moreover, we define the product

\displaystyle\langle\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}}\coloneqq\left(\phi,\zeta\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(\psi,\xi\right)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
. By means of the Riesz representation theorem, this product can be extended to a duality pairing on
(\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1})^{\prime}\times\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}
, which will also be denoted as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}}
.

(P3)

Let
L\in[0,\infty]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be real numbers. We define for
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}
the generalized bulk-surface mean

\displaystyle\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)\coloneqq\frac{\langle\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\beta,1\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}}}{\beta^{2}\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|},

which reduces to

\displaystyle\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)=\frac{\beta\left|\Omega\right|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}+\left|\Gamma\right|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}}{\beta^{2}\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|}

if
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\psi\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
. We then define the closed linear subspaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}:\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)=0\},&\text{if\penalty\ }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}:{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}={\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\},&\text{if\penalty\ }L=\infty.\end{cases}

Note that these subspaces are Hilbert spaces with respect to the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}
.

(P4)

Let
L\in[0,\infty]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be real numbers. We set

\displaystyle\sigma(L)\coloneqq\begin{cases}L^{-1},&\text{if }L\in(0,\infty),\\
0,&\text{if }L\in\{0,\infty\},\end{cases}

and we define a bilinear form on
\mathcal{H}^{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{1}
by

\displaystyle\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\big)_{L,\beta}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\sigma(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\psi-\phi)(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Moreover, we set

\displaystyle\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta}\coloneqq\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\phi,\psi\right)\big)_{L,\beta}^{1/2}

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}

(P5)

\displaystyle=-\phi\hskip 20.00003pt

4  Existence of weak solutions

4.1  Maximal monotone graphs and the Yosida approximation

In this subsection, we briefly review the basic concepts regarding the Yosida approximation of maximal monotone operators. For simplicity, we will restrict ourselves to the case which is relevant to us, i.e., we only consider
F_{1},G_{1}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
as in
(S1)
. For a more comprehensive overview related to maximal monotone operators and the Yosida approximation, we refer the reader to
[
5
, Chapter 2]
,
[
4
, Chapter 2]
,
[
56
, Chapter IV]
and
[
31
, Section 5]
.

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we define the
Yosida approximations

f_{1,\varepsilon},g_{1,\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of the subdifferentials
f_{1}=\partial F_{1}
and
g_{1}=\partial G_{1}
, respectively, as

\displaystyle f_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(r-(I+\varepsilon f_{1})^{-1}(r)\Big),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(r-(I+\varepsilon g_{1})^{-1}(r)\Big)

(4.1)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
, respectively, where
I
denotes the identity on
\mathbb{R}
. Note that while
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
might be multi-valued, their Yosida approximations are single-valued. It is well known that for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds

\displaystyle\left|f_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\right|\leq\left|f_{1}^{\circ}(r)\right|\ \text{for all\penalty\ }\varepsilon\in(0,1),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and\penalty\ }f_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\rightarrow f_{1}^{\circ}(r)\ \text{as\penalty\ }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(4.2)

The analogous properties hold for
g_{1,\varepsilon}
. Furthermore, both
f_{1,\varepsilon}
and
g_{1,\varepsilon}
are increasing and Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
. In particular, since
0\in f_{1}(0)\cap g_{1}(0)
, this implies in combination with (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle\left|f_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left|r\right|,\hskip 10.00002pt\left|g_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left|r\right|

(4.3)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Now, we define for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
the
Moreau–Yosida approximations

F_{1,\varepsilon}
and
G_{1,\varepsilon}
of
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
, respectively, as

\displaystyle F_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\coloneqq\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left|r-s\right|^{2}+F_{1}(s)\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptG_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\coloneqq\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left|r-s\right|^{2}+G_{1}(s)\right\}

(4.4)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
, respectively. It is well known that
F_{1,\varepsilon}
is convex and differentiable with
F_{1,\varepsilon}^{\prime}=f_{1,\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
(see, e.g.,
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
).
Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle 0\leq F_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\leq F_{1}(r)\ \text{for all\penalty\ }\varepsilon\in(0,1),\ \text{and\penalty\ }F_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\nearrow F_{1}(r)\ \text{as\penalty\ }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

(4.5)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
. The analogous properties hold for
G_{1,\varepsilon}
. In particular, we may write

\displaystyle F_{1,\varepsilon}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}f_{1,\varepsilon}(s)\;\mathrm{d}s,\hskip 10.00002ptG_{1,\varepsilon}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}g_{1,\varepsilon}(s)\;\mathrm{d}s

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
. In addition, we readily infer from the definition (
4.4
) and the fact that
F_{1}(0)=G_{1}(0)=0
that

\displaystyle\left|F_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left|r\right|^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\left|G_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left|r\right|^{2}

(4.6)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
.
Now, the domination condition (
2.2
) implies that

\displaystyle\left|f_{1,\varepsilon}(\alpha r)\right|\leq\kappa_{1}\left|g_{1,\varepsilon}(r)\right|+\kappa_{2}

(4.7)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
and all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, where the constants
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
are the same as in (
2.2
) (see
[
7
, Lemma 4.4]
). In summary, we conclude that the approximate potentials
F_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq F_{1,\varepsilon}+F_{2}
and
G_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq G_{1,\varepsilon}+G_{2}
fulfill the following growth conditions:

(R1)

There exist constants
c_{F_{\varepsilon}},c_{G_{\varepsilon}},c_{F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}},c_{G_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\geq 0
, which may depend on
\varepsilon
, such that the first-order derivatives of
F_{\varepsilon}
and
G_{\varepsilon}
satisfy the growth conditions

\displaystyle\left|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}(1+\left|s\right|),

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left|G_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{G_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}(1+\left|s\right|),

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left|F_{\varepsilon}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{F_{\varepsilon}}(1+\left|s\right|^{2}),

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left|G_{\varepsilon}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{G_{\varepsilon}}(1+\left|s\right|^{2})

(4.11)

for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Furthermore, the growth conditions in
(S1)
entail (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Section 3]
):

(R2)

There exists
\varepsilon_{\star}\in(0,1)
as well as constants
C_{\Omega},C_{\Gamma}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\left|s\right|^{2}-C_{\Omega},

(4.12)

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\left|s\right|^{2}-C_{\Gamma}

(4.13)

for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\star})
.

In particular, this implies that for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\star})
,
F_{\varepsilon}
and
G_{\varepsilon}
are bounded from below by
-C_{\Omega}
and
-C_{\Gamma}
, respectively. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume that
F_{\varepsilon}
and
G_{\varepsilon}
are non-negative provided that
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\star})
. (Otherwise, we simply replace
F_{2}
by
F_{2}+C_{\Omega}
and
G_{2}
by
G_{2}+C_{\Gamma}
.)

4.2  Construction of a weak solution

Step 1.
We first construct a sequence of approximate solutions.
Therefore, let
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\star})
with
\varepsilon_{\star}
as in
(R2)
.
Then, due to
[
43
, Theorem 3.2]
, there exists a weak solution
(\phi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon})
satisfying the weak formulation (
3.10a
)-(
3.10b
) with
F=F_{\varepsilon}
and
G=G_{\varepsilon}
. It is worth mentioning that in
[
43
, Theorem 3.2]
, the stronger regularity
\boldsymbol{w}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{3}_{\tau}(\Gamma))
was imposed for convenience. However, it is easy to see that by slightly modifying the proof, this result actually holds true for the regularity
\boldsymbol{w}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}_{\tau}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma))
that is prescribed in the present paper. As a further consequence of
[
43
, Theorem 3.2]
, the energy inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E^{\varepsilon}_{K}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\left|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s+\sigma(L)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq E_{K}^{\varepsilon}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\end{split}

(4.14)

holds for all
t\in[0,T]
. Here,
E_{K}^{\varepsilon}
denotes the energy given by (
3.2
) with
F_{\varepsilon}
and
G_{\varepsilon}
in place of
F
and
G
, respectively. Additionally, as also stated in
[
43
, Theorem 3.2]
, if
\Omega
is of class
C^{2}
, then
\left(\phi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})
, and the equations

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}=-\Delta\phi_{\varepsilon}+F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ in }Q,

(4.15a)

\displaystyle\theta_{\varepsilon}=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}+G_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\psi_{\varepsilon})+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ on }\Sigma,

(4.15b)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon}=\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon}&\text{if}\ K\in[0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon}=0&\text{if}\ K=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ on }\Sigma

(4.15c)

are fulfilled in the strong sense.

Step 2.
We next establish uniform bounds on the approximate solution following from (
4.14
).
By the definition of
E_{K}^{\varepsilon}
, we infer from (
4.14
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}F_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\left|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s+\sigma(L)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\leq E_{K}^{\varepsilon}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

(4.16)

holds for all
t\in[0,T]
. Now, observe that due to (
3.3a
) and (
4.5
), we find

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{0})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{0})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\leq C,

and thus,
E_{K}^{\varepsilon}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\leq C
. On the other hand, for the convective terms on the right-hand side of (
4.16
), we use Hölder’s and Young’s inequality to obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{m_{\Omega}^{\ast}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\frac{m_{\Gamma}^{\ast}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma)}\Big)\|\left(\phi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

(4.17)

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. In view of
(R2)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}F_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma&\geq\int_{\Omega}\left|\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-C\\
&=\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}-C\end{split}

(4.18)

for all
t\in[0,T]
since
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\star})
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}&\leq C+\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}F_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\end{split}

(4.19)

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. Hence, combining (
4.16
), (
4.17
) and (
4.19
), and recalling that
F_{\varepsilon}
and
G_{\varepsilon}
are also non-negative, an application of Gronwall’s lemma readily yields that

\displaystyle\|\left(\phi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}+\|\left(F_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon}),G_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\right)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{1})}+\sigma(K)\|\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\|\left(\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2})}+\sigma(L)\|\beta\theta_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq C.

(4.20)

Step 3.
We derive a uniform estimate on the time derivatives of the phase-fields
\phi_{\varepsilon}
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}
.

To this end, we test (
3.6a
) with an arbitrary test functions
\left(\zeta,\zeta_{\Gamma}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}
. Then, recalling that the mobility functions
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
are bounded (see
(A3)
), we use Hölder’s inequality in combination with the continuous embeddings
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Gamma)
for all
r\in[1,\infty)
to infer

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\langle\left(\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}\right),\left(\zeta,\zeta_{\Gamma}\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}}\right|\\
&\leq\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)}\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+C\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad+\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma)}\|\zeta_{\Gamma}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}+C\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}\|\zeta_{\Gamma}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\\
&\quad+\sigma(L)\|\beta\theta_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|\beta\zeta_{\Gamma}-\zeta\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\end{split}

a.e. on
[0,T]
. Thus, after taking the supremum over all
\left(\zeta,\zeta_{\Gamma}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}
with
\|\left(\zeta,\zeta_{\Gamma}\right)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\leq 1
, we take the square of the resulting inequality and integrate in time from
0
to
T
. This yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\left(\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime})}^{2}\\
&\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega))}+C\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\quad+C\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma))}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma))}+C\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma))}\\
&\quad+C\sigma(L)^{2}\|\beta\theta_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}.\end{split}

In view of the uniform estimate (
4.20
) and the regularity of the velocity fields
\boldsymbol{v}
and
\boldsymbol{w}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\left(\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon},\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime})}\leq C.

(4.21)

Step 4.
We next establish
L^{1}
-bounds on
f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})
and
g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})
.
Therefore, we define

\displaystyle m_{P}\coloneqq\text{mean}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptm_{C}\coloneqq\text{mean}(\mu_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon}).

In the following, we will only consider the case
L\in[0,\infty)
. The proof in the case
L=\infty
works similarly, but one uses the means
{\langle\phi_{0}\rangle}_{\Omega}
and
{\langle\psi_{0}\rangle}_{\Gamma}
as well as
{\langle\mu_{\varepsilon}\rangle}_{\Omega}
and
{\langle\theta_{\varepsilon}\rangle}_{\Gamma}
instead of the ones defined above. This distinction is related to the mass conservation law (
3.8
) and the conditions on the initial data (
3.3b
) and (
3.3c
). We refer to
[
20
]
for more details if
K\in[0,\infty)
and
L=\infty
. The case
K=L=\infty
is similar, but easier.

To handle the cases
K=0
and
K\in(0,\infty]
simultaneously, we introduce the the notation

\displaystyle\gamma(K)\coloneqq\begin{cases}0,&\text{if\penalty\ }K\in(0,\infty],\\
1,&\text{if\penalty\ }K=0.\end{cases}

We now test (
3.6b
) with

\displaystyle\left(\eta,\eta_{\Gamma}\right)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P},\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P}\right),\quad&\text{if\penalty\ }K\in(0,\infty],\\
\left(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\alpha\beta m_{P},\psi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P}\right),&\text{if\penalty\ }K=0,\end{cases}

which clearly belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
. After some rearrangements, as well as adding and subtracting
\beta m_{P}
and
m_{P}
multiple times in the case
K=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\int_{\Gamma}g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}(\mu_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{C})(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}(\theta_{\varepsilon}-m_{C})(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon})\big(\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon}-(\alpha\beta m_{P}-m_{P})\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(\phi_{\varepsilon})(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}g_{2}(\psi_{\varepsilon})(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\gamma(K)\Bigg(\int_{\Omega}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\beta m_{P}-\alpha\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\theta_{\varepsilon}(m_{P}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad\qquad-\int_{\Omega}F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})(\beta m_{P}-\alpha\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}G^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})(m_{P}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\Bigg).\end{split}

(4.22)

Note that the subtracted mean values
\beta m_{C}
and
m_{C}
in the first two summands on the right-hand side of (
4.22
) do not change the values of the respective integrals, since

\displaystyle\beta\left|\Omega\right|{\langle\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P}\rangle}_{\Omega}+\left|\Gamma\right|{\langle\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P}\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0

due to the mass conservation law (
3.8
).

For the left-hand side of (
4.22
), we can use the Miranville–Zelik inequality (see
[
52
, Appendix A.1]
or
[
32
, p. 908]
) due to the assumption that
\beta m_{P}
and
m_{P}
lie in the interior of the effective domains
D(f_{1})
and
D(g_{1})
, respectively, see (
3.3b
). Thus, there exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
and a non-negative constant
c_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&c_{1}\|f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+c_{2}\|g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}-c_{3}\\
&\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(4.23)

On the other hand, we use the bulk-surface Poincaré inequality stated in
(P6)
for the first two terms on the right-hand side of (
4.22
) to find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}(\mu_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{C})(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}(\theta_{\varepsilon}-m_{C})(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq C\big(1+\|\left(\phi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big)\|\left(\mu_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{L,\beta}.\end{split}

(4.24)

Furthermore, in view of the Lipschitz continuity of
f_{2}
and
g_{2}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(\phi_{\varepsilon})(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}g_{2}(\psi_{\varepsilon})(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=-\int_{\Omega}\big(f_{2}(\phi_{\varepsilon})-f_{2}(\beta m_{P})\big)(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Gamma}\big(g_{2}(\psi_{\varepsilon})-g_{2}(m_{P})\big)(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(\beta m_{P})(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}g_{2}(m_{P})(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq C+C\int_{\Omega}\left|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{\Gamma}\left|\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq C\Big(1+\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\Big),\end{split}

and thus

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon})\big(\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon}-(\alpha\beta m_{P}-m_{P})\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(\phi_{\varepsilon})(\phi_{\varepsilon}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\int_{\Gamma}g_{2}(\psi_{\varepsilon})(\psi_{\varepsilon}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\leq C\Big(1+\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\Big).\end{split}

(4.25)

For the remaining terms on the right-hand side of (
4.22
), which are only present if
K=0
, we make use of the additional boundary regularity. In this case, we know that
\left(\phi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})
and that the equations (
4.15
) are satisfied. Hence, after integration by parts, we deduce that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}(F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})-\mu_{\varepsilon})(\alpha\beta m_{P}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}(G_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\psi_{\varepsilon})-\theta_{\varepsilon})(\beta m_{P}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}\Delta\phi_{\varepsilon}(\alpha\beta m_{P}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}(\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}-\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon})(\beta m_{P}-m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(\alpha m_{P}-\beta m_{P})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq C\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

(4.26)

Collecting (

)-(
4.26
), we find that

\displaystyle\|f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\leq\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t)+C\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

(4.27)

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
, where
t\mapsto\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t)
denotes a function in
L^{2}(0,T)
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C,

(4.28)

(cf. the right-hand side of (
4.24
) and (
4.25
)).

To eventually pass to the limit

in the weak formulation (
3.6
), estimate (
4.27
) is not sufficient. To this end, we prove
L^{2}
-bounds for the terms

and
g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})
. For this, we take advantage of the domination property (

) in the case

as well as the additional boundary regularity for

. This leads to the fact that the related analysis has to be performed differently for the cases
K\in(0,\infty]
and

.

Step 5.
We first derive
L^{2}
-bounds of
f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})
and
g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})
in the case

.

In this case, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}+\|g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\Gamma))}\leq C.

(4.29)

Furthermore, since both
f_{2}
and
g_{2}
are Lipschitz continuous, exploiting the estimate (

) yields

\displaystyle\|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}+\|G_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\Gamma))}\leq C.

(4.30)

Now, since

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}=\mathcal{H}^{1}
and may test (

) with

Whence, using (

\displaystyle\|\left(\mu_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon}\right)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2})}\leq C.

Since
f_{1,\varepsilon}
and

are Lipschitz continuous, a chain rule for the combination of Lipschitz and Sobolev functions (see, e.g.,
[

with

\displaystyle\nabla\big(f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\big)

\displaystyle=f_{1,\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.e.\penalty\ in $\Omega$},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}\big(g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\big)

\displaystyle=g_{1,\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.e.\penalty\ on $\Gamma$}.

Testing (

) with

&\quad=\int_{\Omega}\big(\mu_{\varepsilon}-f_{2}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\big)f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\big(\theta_{\varepsilon}-g_{2}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\big)g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\

First, note that due to the monotonicity of

\displaystyle\|f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq C

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon})(\alpha g_{1,\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon})-f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon}))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}-\phi_{\varepsilon})(f_{1,\varepsilon}(\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon})-f_{1,\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon}))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\

from which we deduce (

5  Continuous dependence and uniqueness results

In this section, we present the proof of the result on continuous dependence and uniqueness of weak solutions to the system (

) as stated in Theorem
3.6
.

As the mobilities
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
are supposed to be constant, we assume, without loss of generality, that
m_{\Omega}\equiv m_{\Gamma}\equiv 1
. In the following,
C
denotes generic positive constants that may change their value from line to line and only depend on
\Omega
, the parameters of the system (
3.1
), and the norms of the initial data.

We consider two weak solutions
(\phi_{1},\psi_{1},\mu_{1},\theta_{1},\xi_{1},\xi_{\Gamma,1})
and
(\phi_{2},\psi_{2},\mu_{2},\theta_{2},\xi_{2},\xi_{\Gamma,2})

, respectively, and set

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta,\xi,\xi_{\Gamma})\coloneqq(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1},\psi_{2}-\psi_{1},\mu_{2}-\mu_{1},\theta_{2}-\theta_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\xi_{\Gamma,2}-\xi_{\Gamma,1}),

as well as

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})=(\boldsymbol{v}_{2}-\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{w}_{2}-\boldsymbol{w}_{1}).

Then, repeating the line of argument presented in
[
43
, Theorem 3.5]
, we can show that for any
\varepsilon>0
it holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}-C\varepsilon\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\right)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\omega}}^{2}+C\|\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{w}_{1}\right)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\omega}}^{2}\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}-\big(\left(\mu,\theta\right),\left(\phi,\psi\right)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\end{split}

(5.1)

almost everywhere on
[0,T]
, where we use the notation

. For the last term on the right-hand side of (

and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\big(\left(\mu,\theta\right),\left(\phi,\psi\right)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}&=-\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\xi\,\phi+[F^{\prime}_{2}(\phi_{2})-F^{\prime}_{2}(\phi_{1})]\;\phi\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{\Gamma}\,\psi+[G^{\prime}_{2}(\psi_{2})-G^{\prime}_{2}(\psi_{1})]\;\psi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

almost everywhere on
[0,T]
. Then, using the fact that

and

are maximal monotone graphs, we infer together with the Lipschitz continuity of
F_{2}^{\prime}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\int_{\Omega}\xi\,\phi+[F^{\prime}_{2}(\phi_{2})-F^{\prime}_{2}(\phi_{1})]\phi\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{\Gamma}\,\psi+[G^{\prime}_{2}(\psi_{2})-G^{\prime}_{2}(\psi_{1})]\psi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq C\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(5.2)

almost everywhere on
[0,T]
. Combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) along with an application of Ehrling’s lemma yields that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\right)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\omega}}^{2}+C\|\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{w}_{1}\right)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\omega}}^{2}\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}

almost everywhere on
[0,T]

Then, the uniqueness for the phase-fields immediately follows by taking

6.2  Existence of strong solutions

In view of the regularity properties established in Proposition

Step 1.
We start by showing

For any function

As the mobilities are assumed to be constant, we simply set

and

We now extend our velocity fields onto the time interval
[0,T+1]
such that we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}

\displaystyle\in H^{1}(0,T+1;\mathbf{L}^{6/5}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T+1;\mathbf{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{3}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}

\displaystyle\in H^{1}(0,T+1;\mathbf{L}^{1+\omega}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}(0,T+1;\mathbf{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Gamma)).

Note that the final result is independent of the extension as it does not depend on the values of
\boldsymbol{v}
and
\boldsymbol{w}
on
(T,T+1]

We now also extend the solution
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
onto the interval
[0,T+1]
. This is possible since the final time

in Theorem

is arbitrary, and due to the uniqueness established in Theorem
3.6
, the solutions corresponding to

[0,T]
.

Owing to (
3.6a
), for any

, the weak solution satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big<\big(\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\big),\big(\zeta,\zeta_{\Gamma}\big)\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}}&=\big(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\,\boldsymbol{v},\nabla\zeta\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\big(\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{v}\,\phi(\cdot+h),\nabla\zeta\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad+\big(\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\,\boldsymbol{w},\nabla_{\Gamma}\zeta_{\Gamma}\big)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\big(\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{w}\,\psi(\cdot+h),\nabla_{\Gamma}\zeta_{\Gamma}\big)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
&\quad-\big(\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu,\partial_{t}^{h}\theta\right),\left(\zeta,\zeta_{\Gamma}\right)\big)_{L,\beta}\end{split}

(6.21)

almost everywhere on
[0,T]
for all
\left(\zeta,\zeta_{\Gamma}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}
, where

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{h}\mu=-\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi+\partial_{t}^{h}F^{\prime}(\phi)\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ in\penalty\ }Q,

(6.22a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{h}\theta=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}^{h}\psi+\partial_{t}^{h}G^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ on\penalty\ }\Sigma,

(6.22b)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi=\alpha\partial_{t}^{h}\psi-\partial_{t}^{h}\phi&\text{if}\ K\in[0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi=0&\text{if}\ K=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ on }\Sigma.

(6.22c)

By definition of the operator

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi),\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu,\partial_{t}^{h}\theta\right)\big)_{L,\beta}=-\big(\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right),\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu,\partial_{t}^{h}\theta\right)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}.

Recalling that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}+\big(\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu,\partial_{t}^{h}\theta\right),\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\\
&\quad=-\big(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\,\boldsymbol{v},\nabla\mathcal{S}^{\Omega}_{L,\beta}(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi)\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\big(\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{v}\,\phi(\cdot+h),\nabla\mathcal{S}^{\Omega}_{L,\beta}(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi)\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\

&\quad\eqqcolon\sum_{k=1}^{4}J_{k}.\end{split}

(6.23)

Next, exploiting (
6.22
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\big(\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu,\partial_{t}^{h}\theta\right),\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}&=-\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{h}[F^{\prime}(\phi)]\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x\\

(6.24)

almost everywhere on

Thus, combining (

\displaystyle-\big(\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu,\partial_{t}^{h}\theta\right),\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq-\frac{3}{4}\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+C\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}.

\boldsymbol{w}

\displaystyle J_{1}+J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)}\|\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi)\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma)}\|\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi)\|_{L^{{2(2+\omega)/\omega}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+C\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\right)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\omega}}^{2}\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2},

\displaystyle J_{2}+J_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\phi(\cdot+h)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{6/5}(\Omega)}\|\mathcal{S}^{\Omega}_{L,\beta}(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi)\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}

(6.27)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi(\cdot+h)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\|\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1+\omega}(\Gamma)}\|\mathcal{S}^{\Gamma}_{L,\beta}(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi)\|_{L^{{(1+\omega)/\omega}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{v},\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{w}\right)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\omega}}^{2}+C\|\left(\phi(\cdot+h),\psi(\cdot+h)\right)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{1,\infty}}^{2}\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2},

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi\right)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}\\

where

We readily note that in view of the regularity of the phase-fields and the velocity fields we have

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in(0,1)}\|\Lambda_{h}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}<\infty.

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle\big\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{v}(t),\partial_{t}^{h}\boldsymbol{w}(t)\right)\big\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\omega}}\leq\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\|\left(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}(s),\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{w}(s)\right)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\omega}}\;\mathrm{d}s,

(6.30)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\|\left(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}(s),\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{w}(s)\right)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\omega}}\;\mathrm{d}s=\|\left(\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}(t),\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{w}(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\omega}}

for almost every

Thus, in view of the regularity of

Next, we intend to control the initial data. Arguing as for (

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\big(\left(\mu-\mu_{0},\theta-\theta_{0}\right),\left(\phi-\phi_{0},\psi-\psi_{0}\right)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}&\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\phi-\phi_{0},\psi-\psi_{0}\right)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}\\
&\qquad+C\|\left(\phi-\phi_{0},\psi-\psi_{0}\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}.\end{split}

We now recall

a.e. on
\Sigma
.
Invoking the weak formulations (

)-(

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|\left(\phi-\phi_{0},\psi-\psi_{0}\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}\\
&\quad=-\big(\left(\mu,\theta\right),\left(\phi-\phi_{0},\psi-\psi_{0}\right)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\\

for all

6.28

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(t),\partial_{t}^{h}\psi(t)\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}&\leq\|\left(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi(0),\partial_{t}^{h}\psi(0)\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}\exp\Big(C\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{h}\;\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)\\

Step 2:
By means of elliptic regularity theory we now show
\left(\mu,\theta\right)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})
.

In view of the regularities established in Step 1, and using that
\boldsymbol{v}
and
\boldsymbol{w}
are divergence-free in
\Omega

, respectively, we find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mu(t)\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta(t)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\zeta_{\Gamma}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\sigma(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta(t)-\mu(t))(\beta\zeta_{\Gamma}-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi(t)\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\psi(t)\zeta_{\Gamma}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{v}(t)\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{w}(t)\zeta_{\Gamma}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\end{split}

\displaystyle-\Delta\mu(t)=-\partial_{t}\phi(t)-\nabla\phi(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{v}(t)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}\theta(t)+\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu(t)=-\partial_{t}\psi(t)-\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{w}(t)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Gamma,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu(t)=\beta\theta(t)-\mu(t)&\text{if}\ L\in[0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu(t)=0&\text{if}\ L=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Gamma

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. Applying regularity theory for elliptic problems with bulk-surface coupling (see Proposition
A.1
with

), we find that

\displaystyle\|\left(\mu(t),\theta(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\left(-\partial_{t}\phi(t)-\nabla\phi(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{v}(t),-\partial_{t}\psi(t)-\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{w}(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\left(\partial_{t}\phi(t),\partial_{t}\psi(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+C\|\left(\nabla\phi(t),\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\|\left(\boldsymbol{v}(t),\boldsymbol{w}(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\left(\partial_{t}\phi(t),\partial_{t}\psi(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+C\|\left(\phi(t),\psi(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{1,\infty}}\|\left(\boldsymbol{v}(t),\boldsymbol{w}(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. Squaring and integrating this inequality in time from

Step 3:
We next show
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{2,6})\cap\big(C(\overline{Q})\times C(\overline{\Sigma})\big)
,
\left(\mu,\theta\right)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})

To prove these regularities, we restrict ourselves to the case
L\in[0,\infty)
. If
L=\infty
, we can argue analogously (with obvious modifications).

First, note that, in view of Theorem
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\phi+F^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ in }Q,

(6.43a)

\displaystyle\theta=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+G^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ on }\Sigma,

(6.43b)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=\alpha\psi-\phi&\text{if}\ K\in[0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=0&\text{if}\ K=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{a.e.\penalty\ on }\Sigma.

(6.43c)

Due to regularity theory for bulk-surface elliptic systems (see Proposition
A.1
with
p=6
), we further have the estimate

\displaystyle\|\left(\phi(t),\psi(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,6}}\leq C\big(\|\left(\mu(t),\theta(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{6}}+\|\left(F^{\prime}(\phi(t)),G^{\prime}(\psi(t)\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{6}}\big)

(6.44)

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. Our goal is to estimate the right-hand side of (
6.44
). To do so, we first notice that by the Miranville–Zelik inequality (see
[
52
, Appendix A.1]
or
[
32
, p.908]
), there exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
and a non-negative constant
c_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&c_{1}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+c_{2}\|G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi)\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}-c_{3}\\
&\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi)(\phi-\beta\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right))\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi)(\psi-\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(6.45)

Next, we multiply (
6.43a
) and (
6.43b
) by
\big(\beta^{2}\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|\big)^{-1}\beta
and
\big(\beta^{2}\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|\big)^{-1}
, respectively, and employ integration by parts. This yields

\displaystyle\left|\textnormal{mean}\left(\mu,\theta\right)\right|\leq C\big(1+\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi)\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\big).

it holds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}\sqrt{r}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

(7.17)

for some constant
C_{\Omega}
depending only on
\Omega
(see, e.g.,
[
57
, p. 479]
). Consequently, we infer from (
7.9
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}&\leq C\sqrt{r}\Big(\|\left(\mu,\theta\right)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}+\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}\Big)\\
&\leq C\sqrt{r}\end{split}

(7.18)

almost everywhere on
[0,T]
for all
r\in[2,\infty)
. This inequality allows us to argue similarly to the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
, and we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|e^{C_{2}\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi)\right|^{\lambda}}\right|^{m}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq C(m)

(7.19)

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and some constant
C(m)
depending on
m
and the constant
C
from (
7.18
). In view of (
7.16
), inequality (
7.19
) with
m=6
immediately entails that

\displaystyle\|F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))}\leq C.

(7.20)

Thus, utilizing the chain rule as well as the continuous embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,6}(\Omega)
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega))}&\leq\|F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{6}(\Omega))}\\
&\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C.\end{split}

(7.21)

Finally, using (
7.18
) written for
r=3
, and exploiting the continuous embedding
W^{1,3}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
, we infer from (
7.21
) that

\displaystyle\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,3}(\Omega))}\leq C\eqqcolon C_{\star}

(7.22)

for almost every
t\in[0,T]
. Since Theorem
3.7
implies that
\phi\in C(\overline{Q})
, the inequality (
7.22
) holds true even for every
t\in[0,T]
. Lastly, taking
\delta_{\star}\coloneqq 1-(F_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(C_{\star})
, leads to the desired conclusion.
∎

Appendix:
L^{p}
regularity theory for bulk-surface elliptic systems

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\in\{2,3\}
is a bounded domain of class
C^{2}
with boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
, and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
K\in[0,\infty]
be arbitrary. Moreover, suppose that the pair
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
with
p\geq 2
fulfills the compatibility condition

\begin{cases}\alpha\left|\Omega\right|{\langle f\rangle}_{\Omega}+\left|\Gamma\right|{\langle g\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0&\text{if $K\in[0,\infty)$},\\
{\langle f\rangle}_{\Omega}=0\quad\text{and}\quad{\langle g\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0&\text{if $K=\infty$},\end{cases}

and assume that the pair
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
is a weak solution of the bulk-surface elliptic problem

\displaystyle-\Delta u=f

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega$},

(A.1a)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=g

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma$},

(A.1b)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u=\alpha v&\text{if $K=0$},\\
K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\alpha v-u&\text{if $K\in(0,\infty)$},\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=0&\text{if $K=\infty$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma$},

(A.1c)

which means that

\big((u,v),(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{K,\alpha}=\big((f,g),(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all $(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}$}.

Then, it holds

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in W^{2,p}(\Omega),

(A.2a)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\in W^{2,p}(\Gamma),

(A.2b)

and there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
u
and
v
such that

\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,p}}\leq C\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

(A.3)

We will consider the cases
K=0
,
K\in(0,\infty)
and
K=\infty
separately. In this proof, the letter
C
denotes generic positive constants that are independent of
u
and
v
.

The case
K=0
.
The regularity theory for bulk-surface elliptic system developed in
[
40
, Theorem 3.3]
already yields
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
and the equations in (
A.1
) are fulfilled in the strong sense.
Therefore, in the following, we assume
p>2
.
Moreover,
[
40
, Theorem 3.3]
provides the estimate

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}.

(A.4)

Recalling that the boundary is a
(d-1)
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Sobolev’s embedding theorem implies

u|_{\Gamma}=\alpha v\in W^{t,p}(\Gamma)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002ptt=\frac{5}{2}+\frac{d-1}{p}-\frac{d}{2}.

Since
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, we use elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with an inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
10
, Theorem A.2]
) to deduce

u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002pts=\min\left\{2,\frac{5}{2}+\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}\right\}\geq 1+\frac{2}{p}.

This directly entails

\nabla u\in\mathbf{W}^{s-1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{W}^{2/p,p}(\Omega).

Since
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p}
is positive and not an integer, the trace theorem
(see, e.g.,
[
6
, Chapter 2, Theorem 2.24]
) implies

\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\in W^{1/p,p}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Gamma).

Since
g\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
, applying regularity theory for the Laplace–Beltrami equation (see, e.g.,
[
58
, Lemma B.1]
), we thus conclude (
A.2b
) along with the estimate

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}.

(A.5)

This, in turn, yields

u|_{\Gamma}=\alpha v\in W^{2,p}(\Gamma).

Hence, by means of elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with an inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
10
, Theorem A.2]
), we conclude that (
A.2a
) holds with

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Gamma)}\big)\leq C\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

(A.6)

Combining (
A.5
) and (
A.6
), we obtain (
A.3
).

The case
K\in(0,\infty)
.

Also in this case, the regularity theory for bulk-surface elliptic system developed in
[
40
, Theorem 3.3]
already provides
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
and the equations in (
A.1
) are fulfilled in the strong sense.
Therefore, in the following, we assume
p>2
.
Using the trace theorem as well as Sobolev’s embedding theorem, we have

u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow W^{t,p}(\Gamma)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002ptt=2+\frac{d-1}{p}-\frac{d}{2}.

In particular, we also have
v\in H^{2}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow W^{t,p}(\Gamma)
.
This directly implies

\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\frac{1}{K}\big(\alpha v-u\big)\in W^{t,p}(\Gamma).

Since
g\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
, applying regularity theory for the Laplace–Beltrami equation (see, e.g.,
[
58
, Lemma B.1]
), we thus conclude (
A.2b
) with

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Gamma)}\leq C\big(\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{W^{t,p}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\quad\leq C\big(\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big)\leq C\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.\end{split}

(A.7)

Moreover, as
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, we use elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with an inhomogeneous Neumann boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
10
, Theorem A.2]
) to deduce

u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002pts=\min\left\{2,3+\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}\right\}\geq 1+\frac{2}{p}

along with the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{W^{t,p}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\quad\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big)\leq C\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.\end{split}

(A.8)

Since
1+\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{p}=1+\frac{1}{p}
is positive and not an integer, the trace theorem implies

u|_{\Gamma}\in W^{1+1/p,p}(\Gamma).

This entails

\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\frac{1}{K}\big(\alpha v-u\big)\in W^{1+1/p,p}(\Gamma).

Hence, using again elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with an inhomogeneous Neumann boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
10
, Theorem A.2]
), we conclude that (
A.2a
) holds with

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{W^{1+1/p,p}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\quad\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\big)\leq C\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.\end{split}

(A.9)

Combining (
A.7
) and (
A.9
), we obtain (
A.3
).

The case
K=\infty
.

In this case, the Poisson–Neumann problem
\big(\eqref{BSE:1},\eqref{BSE:3}\big)
and the Laplace–Beltrami equation (
A.1b
) are completely decoupled. Applying elliptic regularity for Poisson’s equation with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
10
, Theorem A.2]
) and regularity theory for the Laplace–Beltrami equation (see, e.g.,
[
58
, Lemma B.1]
), respectively, we directly conclude (
A.2a
) and (
A.2b
) along with estimate (
A.3
).

As the regularities (
A.2a
) and (
A.2b
) as well as the estimate (
A.3
) have been established for every choice
K\in[0,\infty]
, the proof is complete.
∎

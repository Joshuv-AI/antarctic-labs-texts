On the logarithmic slice filtration
Federico Binda, Doosung Park, Paul Arne Østvær

Abstract
. We consider slice filtrations in logarithmic motivic homotopy theory. Our main results establish conjectured compatibilities with the Beilinson, BMS, and HKR filtrations on (topological, log) Hochschild homology and related invariants. In the case of perfect fields admitting resolution of singularities, we show that the slice filtration realizes the BMS filtration on the p p -completed topological cyclic homology. Furthermore, the motivic trace map is compatible with the slice and BMS filtrations, yielding a natural morphism from the motivic slice spectral sequence to the BMS spectral sequence. Finally, we consider the Kummer étale hypersheafification of logarithmic K K -theory and show that its very effective slices compute Lichtenbaum étale motivic cohomology.

Outline of the proofs

Let us discuss the main idea of the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. In
[
17
]
, we constructed motivic spectra in
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
representing logarithmic Hochschild, periodic, and cyclic homology. Such spectra are Bott periodic spectra, in the sense that the strict Nisnevich sheaf
\mathrm{HH}(-)
of spectra on
\mathrm{lSch}/S
satisfies
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathrm{HH}\simeq\mathrm{HH}
(and similarly for
\mathrm{HP}(-)
and
\mathrm{HC}^{-}(-)
). The compatibility of the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle formula with the HKR and the Beilinson filtration allows us to show (see Proposition
4.11
) that we can promote the construction and obtain filtered motivic
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-spectra, satisfying the equivalence

\mathrm{Fil}_{i}^{\mathrm{HKR}}\mathrm{HH}(-/R)(1)[2]\simeq\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{HKR}}_{i+1}\mathrm{HH}(-/R)

and similarly for
\mathrm{HP}
and
\mathrm{HC}^{-}
with the Beilinson filtration, for any ring
R
. At this point, the co-effectivity of
\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{HKR}}^{n}\mathrm{HH}
with respect to the
t
-structure given by the very effective slice tower is a direct consequence of the vanishing of the sheaf cohomology
H^{2j}(X,\Omega^{j}_{X/R})
for negative
j
.

The topological counterpart, that is, for
\mathrm{THH}
and variant, is proven by a conceptually similar method. More precisely, one shows using the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle formula that the BMS filtration on
\mathrm{THH}
gives an equivalence

\mathrm{Fil}_{i}^{\mathrm{BMS}}\mathrm{THH}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{p})(1)[2]\simeq\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{BMS}}_{i+1}\mathrm{THH}(-;\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

and similarly for
\mathrm{TP}
,
\mathrm{TC}^{-}
and
\mathrm{TC}
, see Proposition
5.11
. The co-effectivity statement can then be deduced by passing to the associated graded, reducing to vanishing in (log) prismatic cohomology. This is shown by log quasi-syntomic descent. Overall, the co-effectivity results immediately imply the existence of the natural morphisms between the very effective slice tower and the BMS, HKR, and Beilinson filtrations.

Let us now consider the natural functor
\omega^{*}\colon\mathrm{SH}(S)\to\mathrm{logSH}(S)
, right adjoint to the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-localization functor from
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
to
\mathrm{SH}(S)
for
S
a scheme.
When
S=\Spec(k)
is a perfect field that admits resolution of singularities, it is easy to show that
\omega^{*}
commutes with the (very effective) slice filtration on both sides. Using the results in
[
17
, §7.7]
and the main result of Levine in
[

, Theorems 6.4.2, 9.0.2]
,
we can then identify the very effective slices of
\omega^{*}\mathbf{KGL}
with motivic cohomology in
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
. Combining this with Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
we obtain Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.

Finally, in §

, we consider the very effective slices of Kummer étale
K
-theory. This allows us to consider a spectrum representing Lichtenbaum étale motivic cohomology in
\mathrm{logSH}_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}(k)
: if

. See Theorem

. Using Geisser-Levine as input, together with the identification

We record some elementary properties of the slice filtration.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\tau^{\prime}
be a topology on
\mathrm{SmlSm}/S
finer than
\tau
.
Then for every integer
i
,
the
\tau^{\prime}
-localization functor

L_{\tau^{\prime}}\colon\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)\to\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau^{\prime}}(S,\Lambda)

sends
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
into
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau^{\prime}}(S,\Lambda)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
,
and its right adjoint

\iota\colon\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau^{\prime}}(S,\Lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)

sends
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau^{\prime}}(S,\Lambda)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
into
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
.
A similar result holds for the very effective version, too.

Proof.

We only give proof for the effective version.
The statement about the left adjoint is a consequence of the fact that
L_{\tau^{\prime}}
preserves colimits and sends
\Sigma^{2n,n}\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+}
to
\Sigma^{2n,n}\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+}
for every integer
n
.
Use the natural isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)}(\mathcal{F},\iota\mathcal{G})\cong\Hom_{\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau^{\prime}}(S,\Lambda)}(L_{\tau^{\prime}}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})

for
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\geq i+1}^{\mathrm{eff}}
and
\mathcal{G}\in\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau^{\prime}}(S,\Lambda)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
to deduce the statement about the right adjoint.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
be a map of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings.
Then the base-change functor

-\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda^{\prime}\colon\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)\to\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda^{\prime})

sends
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
into
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda^{\prime})_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
,
and the restriction functor

\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda^{\prime})\to\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)

sends
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda^{\prime})_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
to
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
.
A similar result holds for the very effective version, too.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the fact that
-\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda^{\prime}
preserves colimits and sends
\Sigma^{2n,n}\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+}
to
\Sigma^{2n,n}\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+}
for every
X\in\mathrm{SmlSm}/S
and integer
n
.
∎

In particular, the restriction functor for the morphism
\mathbb{S}\to H\mathbb{Z}

\mathrm{logDA}(S)=\mathrm{logSH}(S,H\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{logSH}(S)

sends
\mathrm{logDA}(S)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
to
\mathrm{logSH}(S)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
.

The following easy Lemma will be useful for the main theorem later in the text.

Lemma 2.12
.

If
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)
admits a filtration such that
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
for every integer
i
,
then, there exists a unique natural morphism of filtered objects

\mathrm{f}_{\bullet}^{\tau}\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{Fil}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}

whose morphism of underlying objects is
\mathrm{id}\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
.
Similarly, if
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)
admits a filtration such that
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)^{\mathrm{veff}}_{\leq i}
for every
i
, then there exists a unique natural morphism of filtered objects

\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\bullet}^{\tau}\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{Fil}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}

whose morphism of underlying objects is
\mathrm{id}\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

We only give proof for the effective version.
Consider the naturally induced maps

\mathrm{f}_{\tau}^{i}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{f}_{\tau}^{i}(\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F},

where the first map is obtained by functoriality and the second map is the inverse of the counit of the adjunction, which is an equivalence since
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
for every integer
i
.
By taking
\fib(\mathcal{F}\to(-))
,
we get the desired morphism

\mathrm{f}_{\bullet}^{\tau}\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{Fil}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}
.
For its uniqueness,
consider the induced exact sequence

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)}(\Sigma^{1,0}\mathrm{f}_{i+1}^{\tau}\mathcal{F},\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F})\to\Hom_{\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)}(\mathrm{f}_{\tau}^{i}\mathcal{F},\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)}(\mathcal{F},\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F})\to\Hom_{\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)}(\mathrm{f}_{i+1}^{\tau}\mathcal{F},\mathrm{Fil}^{i}\mathcal{F}).

The first and fourth terms are
0
since
\Sigma^{1,0}
sends
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\geq i+1}^{\mathrm{eff}}
into itself (note that this works verbatim for
\mathrm{logSH}_{\tau}(S,\Lambda)_{\geq i+1}^{\rm veff}
as well, since it is closed under colimits, hence in particular under positive suspensions).
This establishes the uniqueness of
f^{\bullet}\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{Fil}^{\bullet}\mathcal{F}
and hence the uniqueness of
\mathrm{f}_{\bullet}^{\tau}\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{Fil}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}
.
∎

3.
Slice filtration on K-theory

One of the fundamental results in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory is the equivalence

\mathrm{s}_{i}\mathbf{KGL}\simeq\Sigma^{2i,i}\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathrm{SH}(k)
,
where
k
is a perfect field, due to Voevodsky
[
52
]
in characteristic zero and Levine
[
33
]
in general (this is now known more generally,
e.g., over Dedekind schemes by
[
7
]
,
[
46
, Theorem 2.19]
.
The effective and the very effective slices of
\mathbf{KGL}
coincide,
see
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
,
so that, in particular, the motivic Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum
\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}
is very effective.

In this section,
we promote this to
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
, assuming resolution of singularities.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S
be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme.
Then, for every integer
i
,
we have the inclusion

\omega^{*}\mathrm{SH}(S)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}\subset\mathrm{logSH}(S)_{\leq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}

A similar result holds for the very effective version, too.

Proof.

By adjunction,
it suffices to show
\omega_{\sharp}\mathrm{logSH}(S)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}\subset\mathrm{SH}(S)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
and

\omega_{\sharp}\mathrm{logSH}(S)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{veff}}\subset\mathrm{SH}(S)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{veff}}
.
This holds since
\omega_{\sharp}
preserves colimits and
\omega_{\sharp}\Sigma^{n,0}\Sigma^{\infty}Y_{+}\simeq\Sigma^{n,0}\Sigma^{\infty}(Y-\partial Y)_{+}
for
Y\in\mathrm{SmlSm}/S
and integer
n
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
k
be a perfect field admitting resolution of singularities.
Then, for every integer
i
,
we have the inclusion

\omega^{*}\mathrm{SH}(k)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}\subset\mathrm{logSH}(k)_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{eff}}.

A similar result holds for the very effective version, too.

Proof.

For
X\in\mathrm{Sm}/k
,
there exists proper
Y\in\mathrm{SmlSm}/k
such that
Y-\partial Y\cong X
by resolution of singularities.
Due to
[
43
, Theorem 1.1(2)]
,
we have an equivalence

\omega^{*}\Sigma^{n,0}\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+}\simeq\Sigma^{n,0}\Sigma^{\infty}Y_{+}
for every integer
n
.
To conclude,
observe that
\omega^{*}
preserves colimits by
[
43
, Theorem 1.1(4)]
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
k
be a perfect field admitting resolution of singularities.
For
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{SH}(k)
and integer
i
,
there is a natural equivalence

\omega^{*}\mathrm{f}_{i}\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathrm{f}_{i}\omega^{*}\mathcal{F}

in
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
.
A similar result holds for the very effective version, too.

Proof.

We have the natural fiber sequence

\mathrm{f}_{i}\omega^{*}\mathrm{f}_{i}\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{f}_{i}\omega^{*}\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{f}_{i}\omega^{*}\mathrm{f}^{i-1}\mathcal{F}

in
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
.
Proposition
3.2
implies
\mathrm{f}_{i}\omega^{*}\mathrm{f}_{i}\mathcal{F}\simeq\omega^{*}\mathrm{f}_{i}\mathcal{F}
,
and Proposition
3.1
implies

\mathrm{f}_{i}\omega^{*}\mathrm{f}^{i-1}\mathcal{F}\simeq 0
.
From these,
we obtain the desired equivalence.
∎

Let now
\mathbf{KGL}
denote the motivic
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-spectrum representing algebraic
K
-theory in
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
constructed in
[
17
, Definition 6.5.6]
(denoted
\mathbf{logKGL}
in
loc. cit.
), and let
\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}\in\mathrm{logSH}(k)
denote the motivic
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-spectrum representing integral motivic cohomology constructed in
[
17
, Definition 7.7.5]
, denoted
\mathbf{logM}\mathbb{Z}
in
loc. cit.
). Since
\mathbf{KGL}
and
\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}
satisfy the property that
\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}\simeq\omega^{*}\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}
when
k
admits resolution of singularities, and
\mathbf{KGL}\simeq\omega^{*}\mathbf{KGL}
in general, we may unambiguously drop the prefix
\mathbf{log}
from the notation.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
k
be a perfect field admitting resolution of singularities.
Then the slice filtration and the very effective slice filtration on
\mathbf{KGL}\in\mathrm{logSH}(k)
agree
and are complete and exhaustive.
Furthermore,
there are natural equivalences

\mathrm{s}_{i}\mathbf{KGL}\simeq\widetilde{\mathrm{s}}_{i}\mathbf{KGL}\simeq\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}(i)[2i]

in
\mathrm{logSH}(k)
for every integer
i
, where
\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}(i)[2i]
denotes the suspension
\Sigma^{2i,i}\mathbf{M}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We have equivalences

(2) is due to Proposition

,
(3) is due to
[

,
and (4) is due to
[

, Proposition 7.7.6]
.

We also have

.
To show that the slice filtration on

is complete and exhaustive,

preserves colimits and limits.
The fact that

For the very effective slice of

∎

4.
HKR filtration on logarithmic Hochschild homology

Throughout this section, we fix

(R,P)
a pre-log ring,
and
S
a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme over
\Spec(R)
.
In practice,
the reader can ignore
P
in most statements and assume that
R
is always considered with a trivial log structure.

Our goal is to construct the HKR filtration on
\mathbf{HH}(-/R)
and Beilinson filtrations on
\mathbf{HC}^{-}(-/R)
and
\mathbf{HP}(-/R)
in
\mathrm{logDA}(S)
,
which are the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-stabilized versions of the corresponding filtrations on
\mathrm{HH}(-/R)
,
\mathrm{HC}^{-}(-/R)
, and
\mathrm{HP}(-/R)
, built, in the log setting, in our previous works
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
.
We also explore their fundamental properties.

Definition 4.1
.

For a map of pre-log rings
(R,P)\to(A,M)
and integer
i
,
we set

\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}^{i}:=\wedge_{A}^{i}\mathbb{L}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}.

where
\mathbb{L}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
is Gabber’s logarithmic cotangent complex
[
42
, §8]
, and
\wedge^{i}_{A}(-)
is the
i
th derived exterior power. The
logarithmic derived de Rham cohomology
of
(R,P)\to(A,M)
is defined as the total complex

\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}:=\mathrm{Tot}(\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}^{0}\to\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}^{1}\to\cdots).

This admits the Hodge filtration given by

\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}^{\geq i}:=\mathrm{Tot}(0\to\cdots\to 0\to\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}^{i}\to\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}^{i+1}\to\cdots)

where
\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}^{i}
is in cohomological degree
i
for
i\geq 0
and
\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}^{\geq i}:=\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
for
i<0
.
Let
\widehat{\mathbb{L}\Omega}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
be the completion of
\mathbb{L}\Omega_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
with respect to the Hodge filtration,
and let
\widehat{\mathbb{L}\Omega}_{X/(R,P)}^{\geq n}
be the
n
th term of the induced Hodge filtration of
\widehat{\mathbb{L}\Omega}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
.

Remark 4.2
.

By Zariski descent,
we can also define the sheaf of complexes
\mathbb{L}\Omega_{X/(R,P)}^{i}
,
\mathbb{L}\Omega_{X/(R,P)}
,
\mathbb{L}\Omega_{X/(R,P)}^{\geq i}
,
\widehat{\mathbb{L}\Omega}_{X/(R,P)}
, and
\widehat{\mathbb{L}\Omega}_{X/(R,P)}^{\geq i}
on
X_{\mathrm{Zar}}
for every log scheme
X
over
(R,P)
.

We now list some facts that we will use in the paper.

(1)

For any map of pre-log rings
(R,P)\to(A,M)
, the logarithmic Hochschild homology

\mathrm{HH}((A,M)/(R,P))
of
[
12
, Definition 5.3]
admits a separated, descending filtration with
graded pieces
\wedge_{A}^{i}\mathbb{L}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}[i]
.
This is obtained by Kan extension of the Postnikov filtration in the polynomial case.
We shall refer to this as the log HKR filtration, see
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
.

(2)

The negative cyclic homology and the periodic homology
\mathrm{HC}^{-}((A,M)/(R,P))
and
\mathrm{HP}((A,M)/(R,P))
admit the complete exhaustive

\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{B}}_{\geq\bullet}\mathrm{HC}^{-}((A,M)/(R,P)),\quad\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{B}}_{\geq\bullet}\mathrm{HP}((A,M)/(R,P))

with graded pieces
\widehat{\mathbb{L}\Omega}^{\geq n}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}[2n]
and
\widehat{\mathbb{L}\Omega}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}[2n]
.
The non-log case is due to Antieau
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
, while the log case is
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
.
If
(A,M)\in\mathrm{Poly}_{(R,P)}
,
then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{B}}_{i}\mathrm{HC}^{-}((A,M)/(R,P)):=\tau_{\geq 2i}^{\mathrm{B}}\tau_{\geq\bullet}\mathrm{HC}^{-}((A,M)/(R,P)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fil}^{\mathrm{B}}_{i}\mathrm{HP}((A,M)/(R,P)):=\tau_{\geq 2i}^{\mathrm{B}}\tau_{\geq\bullet}\mathrm{HP}((A,M)/(R,P)),

where
\tau_{\geq i}^{\mathrm{B}}
denotes the truncation functor for the Beilinson
t
-structure on the filtered derived category
\mathrm{DF}(A)

[
4
, Definition 2.2]
.
We obtain the filtrations for general

by left Kan extension and regarding
\mathrm{HC}^{-}
and
\mathrm{HP}
as bicomplete bifiltered complexes,
see
[
4
, Remark 4.4]
, or
[
13
, 2.2]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

There is an

-equivariant equivalence of filtered complexes

\mathrm{Fil}_{i}((\mathbb{Z}[1])\{-1\})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $i\leq 1$},\\

∎

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
k
be a perfect field admitting resolution of singularities.
For

,

Assume first that

(L_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\mathrm{K}(X))^{\wedge}_{p}\simeq\mathrm{TC}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{p})

[

, Theorem 4.2.6]
.
In general, the desired equivalence for

follows from the Gysin sequence
[
17
, Theorem 3.2.21]
by induction on the number of smooth irreducible components
r
of
\partial X
. More precisely, if
Z
is a smooth divisor on proper
X\in\mathrm{Sm}/k
, then we have the fiber sequence in
\mathrm{logSH}(k)

(7.3)

\Sigma^{\infty}(X,Z)_{+}\to\Sigma^{\infty}X_{+}\to\mathrm{Th}(\mathrm{N}_{Z}X),

where

is the motivic Thom space defined in
[
17
, Definition 3.2.7]
. Note that by applying the natural functor

K
-theory spectrum
\mathbf{KGL}

K
-theory in light of
[
17
, Theorem 6.5.7]
.
On the other hand, if we apply the log TC spectrum
\mathbf{TC}
to (
7.3
) we obtain by definition the Gysin or residue sequence in logarithmic topological cyclic homology.
By Construction
7.9
,
we obtain a commutative diagram of spectra

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(L_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}\mathrm{K}(Z))^{\wedge}_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(L_{\mathrm{k\acute{e}t}}\mathrm{K}(X))^{\wedge}_{p}}

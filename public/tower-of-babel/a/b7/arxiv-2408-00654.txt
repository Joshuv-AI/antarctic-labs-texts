1.
Introduction

This paper focuses on the existence of nontrivial solutions for the following class of weighted Choquard-type equations

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=\lambda u+\Bigg(\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u)&&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u&=0&&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary,
0<\mu<2
,
\lambda
is a positive parameter but not equal to any of the eigenvalues of the operator
(-\Delta,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and
Q
is a continuous weight but can have a singularity at the origin. The nonlinearity
f
is a continuous function satisfying critical exponential growth along with some suitable conditions specified later, and
F(s)=\int\limits_{0}^{s}f(t)dt
.

The investigation of equation (
1.1
) is inspired by the following equation:

(1.2)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\Bigg(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)|u|^{p-2}u\ \ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
N\geq 3
,
0<\mu<N
,
p\geq 2
and
V
is a real valued continuous potential. The equation (
1.2
), commonly referred to as the Choquard or Hartree-type equation, arises in various physical scenarios. In 1954, Pekar
[
42
]
was the first to introduce the equation (
1.2
) with
N=3,\mu=1,p=2
. He used this equation to study the polaron at rest in the quantum field theory. As discussed in
[
28
]
by Lieb, P. Choquard also studied (
1.2
) in an approximation to Hartree-Fock’s theory of one component plasma to describe an electron trapped in its hole (see
[
29
]
for physical context).

For the last few decades, many authors have studied the existence and qualitative behavior of solutions for the class of problem (
1.2
). In 1977, Lieb
[
28
]
established the existence and uniqueness of the solution by using symmetric decreasing rearrangement inequalities with
N=3,\mu=1,p=2
, and
V
as a positive constant. In 1980, Lions
[
31
]
showed the existence of infinitely many radially symmetric solutions for the equation (
1.2
) with
N=3,\mu=1,p=2
and
V(x)\equiv\lambda>0
. In
[
35
]
, authors studied the regularity and positivity of the ground state solutions for the case
\frac{N-2}{2N-\mu}<\frac{1}{p}<\frac{N}{2N-\mu}
with
V(x)\equiv 1
in (
1.2
). They established that all positive ground states are radially symmetric and have decaying properties. For a thorough and insightful review of the Choquard equations of type (
1.2
), we refer
[
1
,
8
,
7
,
16
,
26
,
36
,

,
38
,

]
along with the references therein. For other related nonlocal problems dealing variational methods, we refer to
[

,

,

,
33
,

]
and their references.

To deal with the problem variationally, the nonlocal term present in the equation (
1.1
) is treated with the help of the following result introduced in
[
46
]
.

Proposition 1.1

(Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
s,r>1
and
0<\mu<N
with
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{\mu}{N}+\frac{1}{r}=2
. Let
f\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
h\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. There exists a sharp constant
C(s,N,\mu,r)
, independent of
f,h
, such that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\leq C(s,N,\mu,r)\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|h\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

We give the following definition of the solution of the problem in (
1.1
).

Definition 1
.

We say that a function
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to be a weak solution of (
1.1
) if

\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\phi\ dx=\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}u\phi\ dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)|F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u(x))\phi(x)\ dx,

for all
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

The problem has a variational structure in the sense that the weak solutions of (
1.1
) are the critical points of the energy functional
J:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated to (
1.1
) and defined as

(1.3)

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\lambda u^{2}\ dx-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)|F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u(x))\ dx.

The case when
N=2
is special as the critical Sobolev exponent
2^{*}
becomes
\infty
. It is well known that for bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the corresponding Sobolev embeddings
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
with
1\leq p<+\infty
holds but
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\not\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. To overcome the problem of finding an optimal space in Sobolev embedding, the Trudinger-Moser inequality
[
39
,
49
]
can be seen as a suitable alternative. It provides an embedding of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
space into orlicz spaces and is stated as below:

If
\alpha>0
and
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
then
\int\limits_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx<+\infty.

Moreover,

\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq 1}\int\limits_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx\leq C|\Omega|\ \ \text{if}\ \ \alpha\leq 4\pi,

where
C=C(\alpha)>0
and
|\Omega|
denotes Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
.

In
[
32
]
, Lions established a generalized version of the above Trudinger-Moser inequality.
Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be a sequence satisfying
\|u_{n}\|=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
0<\|u_{0}\|<1
, then for all
0<p<\frac{4\pi}{1-\|u_{0}\|^{2}}
, we have

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int\limits_{\Omega}e^{p|u_{n}|^{2}}\,dx<+\infty.\quad

Inspired by the Trudinger-Moser inequality, we can define a notion of criticality, which was introduced by Adimurthi and Yadava
[
3
]
. It is also discussed in de Figueiredo, Miyagaki, and Ruf
[
21
]
. We say that a function
f
has critical exponential growth if there exists a constant
\alpha_{0}>0
such that

(1.4)

\lim_{|s|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{|f(s)|}{e^{\alpha s^{2}}}=\begin{cases}0,&\forall\alpha>\alpha_{0},\\
+\infty,&\forall\alpha<\alpha_{0}.\end{cases}

Recently, the nonlocal Choquard-type equation with exponential critical growth in

for

, specifically addressing the resonant case (see

Precisely, the following assumption is satisfied by the weight function

(Q)

\displaystyle Q\in C(0,\infty),Q(r)>0\text{ for }r>0\text{ and there exists }b_{0}>-\frac{4-\mu}{2},b\in\mathbb{R},C_{0}>0\text{ and }R>0

Example 1

The standard example of a weight function is given by

) with

Example 2

In

0<Q(|x|)\leq\frac{A}{1+|x|^{b_{2}}},

this weight verifies the condition (

.

Note that, compared to the equation (

), the major challenge is due to the presence of the weight function

in the nonlocal term in equation (

Consequently, we need to prove a variant of the Sobolev embedding theorem and the Trudinger-Moser inequality for our scenario (see Lemma

On the other hand, the

1.1.
Principle of symmetric criticality

Let
\mathcal{O}(N)
denote the group of all orthogonal matrices on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
H
be any closed subgroup of
\mathcal{O}(N)
. Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a Banach space, an action of a group
H
on
X
is defined by a smooth continuous map

*:H\times X\to X\text{ which maps }(h,u)\mapsto h*u,

such that the following conditions will hold

1*u=u,\ \ (h*g)*u=h*(g*u),\ \ u\mapsto h*u\text{ is linear. }

If
\|h*u\|_{X}=\|u\|_{X}
, for all
h\in H
and
u\in X
, then we say that the action
*
is isometric. A function
u\in X
is called
H
-invariant if, and only if,
h*u=u
. Moreover, the set of all
H
-invariant functions in
X
is denoted by
Fix_{H}(X)
, i.e.

Fix_{H}(X)=\{u\in X:h*u=u,\ \forall\ h\in H\}.

We use the following version of Palais principle of symmetric criticality
[
41
]
due to Kobayashi and Ôtani
[
25
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Principle of symmetric criticality)
.

Let
X
be a reflexive and strictly convex Banach space and
H\subset\mathcal{O}(N)
be a group that acts on
X
linearly and isometrically. If
J:X\to\mathbb{R}
is an
H
-invariant,
C^{1}
functional, then

(J|_{Fix_{H}(X)})^{\prime}(u)=0\text{ implies that }J^{\prime}(u)=0\text{ and }u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Note that, if we consider
X=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
H=\mathcal{O}(N)
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and the action is defined as the standard linear isometric map given by

hu(x)=u(h^{-1}x),\ \forall\ x\in\Omega,\ \forall\ h\in\mathcal{O}(N),

then the set of all invariant functions of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, with respect to
\mathcal{O}(N)
correspond to the space of all radial functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.
Fix_{\mathcal{O}(N)}(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))=H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If
H(\Omega)=\Omega
, then we say
\Omega
is
H
-invariant. If
\Omega
is
H
-invariant and a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
f(h(x))=f(x),\ \forall\ h\in H
, then
f
is called
H
-invariant.

Since the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is reflexive and strictly convex Banach space, to apply the principle of symmetric criticality, it is sufficient to show the functional
J
is
\mathcal{O}(2)
-invariant, where
\mathcal{O}(2)
denotes the group of all orthogonal matrices on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For this, we need to assume that
\Omega
is invariant with respect to
\mathcal{O}(2)
.

1.2.
Spectral properties of the Laplacian operator

Consider the following eigenvalue problem

(1.8)

\displaystyle-\Delta u=\lambda u\ \text{ in }\ \Omega,\ u=0\ \text{ on }\ \partial\Omega.

It is well known that we get a sequence of eigenvalues of the problem (
1.8
) and we denote it as
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\cdots
with
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\infty
as

.
The eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
corresponding to each
\lambda_{k}
forms an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
and an orthogonal basis for
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. To prove the existence results, we need to decompose the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as:

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)=H_{k}(\Omega)\oplus H_{k}^{\perp}(\Omega),\text{where }H_{k}(\Omega)=span\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{k}\},

and the orthogonal complement has to be taken with respect to the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
defined on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. The following characterization of eigenvalues is shown in
[
34
]

(1.9)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\min_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}

(1.10)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\min_{u\in H_{k}^{\perp}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}\text{ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ for }k\geq 1,

where
\|u\|_{2}=\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the norm in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Similar to the characterization of
\lambda_{k+1}
in (
1.10
), we can show that
\lambda_{k}
can also be characterized as

(1.11)

\lambda_{k}=\max_{u\in H_{k}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{\|u\|^{2}_{2}}

1.3.
Assumptions and main results

In order to study equation (
1.1
) by variational method, we need to take some assumptions on
f
. Assume that the nonlinearity
f
satisfies the following hypotheses:

(H1)

\displaystyle f\in C(\mathbb{R}),f(s)=0\text{ for all }s\leq 0,f\text{ has critical exponential growth defined in \eqref{ceg}. }

\displaystyle\text{ Moreover, }f(s)=o(s^{\frac{2-\mu}{2}}).

(H2)

\displaystyle\text{ there exists }K>1\text{ such that }0<KF(s)\leq f(s)s,\text{ for all }s>0.

(H3)

\displaystyle\text{ there exist }s_{0}>0,M_{0}>0,\text{ and }v\in(0,1]\text{ such that }0<s^{v}F(s)\leq M_{0}f(s),\text{ for all }

\displaystyle s\geq s_{0}.

(H4)

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{F(s)}{e^{\alpha_{0}s^{2}}}:=\beta_{0}>0.

Under the assumption (
H1
) on
f
, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
q>1
and for fixed
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
, there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,q,\varepsilon)>0
such that

(1.12)

|f(s)|\leq\varepsilon|s|^{\frac{2-\mu}{2}}+C|s|^{q-1}e^{\alpha s^{2}}\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R},

and there exist
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
C_{1}>0
satisfying

(1.13)

|F(s)|\leq\varepsilon_{1}|s|^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}+C_{1}|s|^{q}e^{\alpha s^{2}}\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Assumption (
H2
) is required in order to prove the Palais-Smale sequence is bounded. Whereas the assumptions (
H3
) and (
H4
) are used to estimate the minimax level of the functional associated with our problem. Moreover, using these two conditions, we do not need the control on
\beta_{0}
.

The objective of this paper is to establish the existence of a nontrivial solution for (
1.1
). Based on the position of the parameter
\lambda
relative to the eigenvalues of
(-\Delta,H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
with Dirichlet boundary conditions, we present our findings through the following two main theorems. The first theorem addresses the situation when the parameter
\lambda
lies in the interval
(0,\lambda_{1})
. In this case, the classical mountain-pass theorem guarantees the existence of a critical point of the energy functional associated to the problem (
1.1
). Due to the presence of weight function

, we need to work in the function space

. As we discussed earlier, to prove the solutions of (

\Omega

is
\mathcal{O}(2)
-invariant, where
\mathcal{O}(2)
is the group of all orthogonal matrices on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Our first result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1})
, (
H1
)-(
H4
) and (
Q
) holds. Then, the problem (

) has a nontrivial solution.

Next, we deal with the case when

(ii)

For each open ball
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the space
H^{1}_{0,rad}(B_{R})
is continuously embedded in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})
. In particular,
H^{1}_{0,rad}(B_{R})
is continuously embedded in
L^{p}(B_{R})
, for
1\leq p<\infty
.

Next,
1\leq p<\infty
we define weighted Lebesgue spaces as

L_{Q}^{p}(\Omega):=\{u:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{ is measurable and }\int\limits_{\Omega}Q(|x|)|u|^{p}dx<\infty\}

endowed with the norm
\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q(|x|)|u|^{p}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
Finally, we provide a version of the weighted Sobolev embedding result that is suitable for our problem. The following proof is inspired to
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that (
Q
) holds true, for all
\frac{4-\mu}{4}\leq p<\infty
, the embedding
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p_{\mu}}_{Q_{\mu}}:=\left\{u:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{ is Lebesgue measurable }\Big|\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx<\infty\right\}
is compact.

Proof.

We prove the lemma in two steps. First, we establish that the embedding from
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
to
L^{p_{\mu}}_{Q_{\mu}}
is continuous. In the second step, we show that this embedding is compact.

Step 1.
Suppose that
0<r<R
. Choosing
r
small enough and
R\geq R_{1}
, where
R_{1}
is defined in Lemma
2.1
. Then, we claim that for all
u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
and
\frac{4-\mu}{4}\leq p<\infty
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

(2.1)

\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx\leq C\Bigg(R^{-\frac{2p}{4-\mu}}\|u\|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}+r^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}+\frac{2}{v}}\|u\|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}+\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}\}}|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg),

where
v>1
and
b_{0}v>-\frac{4-\mu}{2}
.

If (
2.1
) holds, then by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}\}}|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx\leq C_{p}\|u\|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}.

Therefore, we fix
R
and
r
in (
2.1
), such that there is a constant
\overline{C}>0
satisfying

\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx\leq\overline{C}\|u\|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}},

which further implies the continuity of the embedding. Hence, to prove continuous embedding, it is sufficient to verify (
2.1
).

From the assumption (
Q
), we have,
Q(|x|)\leq C_{0}|x|^{b}
, for all
|x|\geq R
. Using the fact that
|x|^{b}
is bounded in
\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}
, for any
b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}|x|^{\frac{4b}{4-\mu}}|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx\leq C_{1}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx,

this together with Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx\leq C_{2}R^{-\frac{2p}{4-\mu}}\|u\|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}.

Again by hypothesis (
Q
), there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
satisfying

Q(|x|)\leq C_{3}|x|^{b_{0}},\text{ for all }0<|x|<r_{0}.

Now, we estimate the integral on
\Omega\cap B_{r}
. Applying Hölder’s inequality with
b_{0}v>-\frac{4-\mu}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}|x|^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}}|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx\leq C_{3}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}|x|^{\frac{4b_{0}v}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}|u|^{\frac{4pv^{\prime}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}}.

Using Lemma
2.2
, we get

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx\leq C_{4}r^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}+\frac{2}{v}}\|u\|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}.

Consider the integral on an annular region
\Omega\cap\{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}\}
. Because
Q(|x|)
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
, we can define
M_{r,R}=\displaystyle\max_{r\leq t\leq R}Q(t)
. Using this, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}\}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq M_{r,R}^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}\}}|u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx.

Adding (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we can find a constant
C>0
such that the estimate in (
2.1
) holds. This proves the continuity of the embedding.

Step 2.
We assume that
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
such that
\|u_{n}\|\leq C
, for some
C>0
. This implies that there exists
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
. To prove compactness, we need to show that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
L^{p_{\mu}}_{Q_{\mu}}
with
\frac{4-\mu}{4}\leq p<\infty
,
i.e.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx=0.

For a given
\varepsilon>0
, choosing
R>0
sufficiently large and
r>0
sufficiently small such that the following holds

r^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}+\frac{2}{v}}<\varepsilon\text{ and }R^{-\frac{2p}{4-\mu}}<\varepsilon.

Since
H^{1}_{0,rad}(B_{R}\setminus B_{r})
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(B_{R}\setminus B_{r})
, for
1\leq p<\infty
, it follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}\}}|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx=0.

Now, as
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
, there is a constant
C_{5}>0
satisfying
\|u_{n}-u_{0}\|\leq C_{5}
, for all
n
. Therefore, by the above relations, we get

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}-u|^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C(2\varepsilon C_{5}^{\frac{4p}{4-\mu}}+o_{n}(1)).

This completes the proof by taking
n\to\infty
.

∎

2.2.
A version of weighted Trudinger-Moser inequality

As discussed earlier, due to the presence of a weight function
Q
, we prove the following version of Trudinger-Moser inequality, inspired from
[
5
]
, that will be an important tool for our results. The statement follows as

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose (
Q
) holds. If
u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
and
\alpha>0
, then
Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover,

\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq 1}\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx<+\infty,\ \text{ for }\ \alpha\leq 4\pi\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big).

Proof.

Fix
R>R_{1}>0
, where
R_{1}
is given in Lemma
2.1
. From the assumption (
Q
), we can find
C_{0}>0
such that

(2.5)

Q(|x|)\leq C_{0}|x|^{b},\ \forall\ |x|\geq R,\text{ and }\ Q(|x|)\leq C_{0}|x|^{b_{0}},\ \forall\ 0<|x|\leq R.

Using (
2.5
), then we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx=\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}u^{2j}}{j!}dx\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}|x|^{\frac{4b}{4-\mu}}u^{2j}dx.

Since
|x|^{b}
is bounded on
\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}
, for any
b\in\mathbb{R}
and applying Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx\leq C_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}u^{2j}dx\leq C_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}(C\|u\|)^{2j}}{j!}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}|x|^{-j}dx\leq C_{2}e^{\alpha C^{2}\|u\|^{2}}.

It follows that, for any
u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
, we get
Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}\in

L^{1}(\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c})
. Additionally, we can say that

(2.6)

\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq 1}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx<+\infty,\ \text{ for all }\ \alpha>0.

Now, we estimate the Trudinger-Moser inequality on a ball
B_{R}
.
To do this, we need to consider the following two cases:

Case 1.
If
-\frac{4-\mu}{2}<b_{0}\leq 0
, assumption (
2.5
) and the singular Trudinger-Moser inequality by
[
2
]
implies that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}|x|^{\frac{-4b_{0}}{4-\mu}}e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx<\infty,\ \forall\ u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega).

Moreover, from (
1.6
)

\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega\cap B_{R}),\|u\|\leq 1}\int\limits_{B_{R}}|x|^{\beta}e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx<\infty,\text{ if and only if }\alpha\leq 4\pi\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big).

Case 2.
If
b_{0}>0
, by (
2.5
) and the classical Trudinger-Moser inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}|x|^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}}e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}R^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}+2}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{1}}|x|^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}}e^{\alpha v^{2}}dx<\infty,\ \forall\ v\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega\cap B_{1}),

where we have used change of variables as
v(x)=u(Rx)
,
x\in B_{1}
and also
\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}
. Hence, we can apply a version of the Trudinger-Moser inequality given in (
1.7
). Then, the above integral is finite for all
u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega\cap B_{R})
. Moreover, using (
1.7
), we get

\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega\cap B_{R}),\|u\|\leq 1}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}|x|^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}}e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx<\infty,\ \forall\ \alpha\leq 4\pi\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big).

Combining both the cases and from (
2.6
), we can conclude that the result holds.
∎

Remark 1
.

It is easy to show that if
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, for any
\alpha>0
. To see this, we consider

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\alpha u^{2}}\ dx\leq C_{0}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}|x|^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}}e^{\alpha u^{2}}\ dx+C_{0}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}}|x|^{\frac{4b}{4-\mu}}e^{\alpha u^{2}}\ dx.

The second integral is finite by the classical Trudinger-Moser inequality together with the fact that
|x|^{\frac{4b}{4-\mu}}
is bounded on
\Omega\cap B_{R}^{c}
. Since
b_{0}>-\frac{4-\mu}{2}
, choose
t>1
such that
b_{0}t>-\frac{4-\mu}{2}
. Thus, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}|x|^{\frac{4b_{0}}{4-\mu}}e^{\alpha u^{2}}\ dx\leq\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}|x|^{\frac{4b_{0}t}{4-\mu}}\ dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{t}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{R}}e^{\frac{\alpha t}{t-1}u^{2}}\ dx\Bigg)^{\frac{t-1}{t}}<\infty.

The next lemma is an improvement of the classical Trudinger-Moser inequality. This result is introduced by Lions
[
32
]
, and we see the following version:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
be a sequence satisfying
\|u_{n}\|=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
and
0<\|u\|<1
, then for
0<p<\frac{4\pi}{1-\|u\|^{2}}\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big)
, we have

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int\limits_{\Omega}Q(|x|)e^{p|u_{n}|^{2}}\,dx<+\infty.\quad

The proof can be done by using a similar idea as in
[
40
]
.

3.
Variational framework

First, we have to show that the functional
J
introduced in (
1.3
) is well-defined in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. For this, we need to prove the nonlocal term is well-defined. This can be done by using the Proposition
1.1
.

Given
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, taking
s=r=\frac{4}{4-\mu}
in Proposition
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u(x))dx\right|\leq C_{\mu}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|F(u(x))|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}.

We use (
1.13
) with
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
and
q\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|F(u(x))|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{2}dx+C_{1}\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}e^{\frac{4\alpha}{4-\mu}|u|^{2}}dx.

Using Lemma
2.3
with
p=\frac{4-\mu}{2}
to obtain
\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{2}dx\leq C_{q}\|u\|^{2}
.
By Hölder’s inequality and again using Lemma
2.3
with
p=qv
,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}e^{\frac{4\alpha|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4qv}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha v^{\prime}|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{qv}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha v^{\prime}|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}},

where we have used Hölder’s inequality with
v>1
,
\frac{1}{v}+\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}=1
. Combining the above relations with Lemma
2.4
and Remark
1
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u(x))dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mu}\Bigg(CC_{q}\|u\|^{2}+C_{1}C_{qv}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha v^{\prime}|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}}\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|^{4-\mu}+C\|u\|^{2q}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha v^{\prime}|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{2v^{\prime}}}<+\infty.

Hence, the functional
J
is well-defined on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Using the standard arguments, we can say that
J
is
C^{1}
and for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, its derivative is given by

J^{\prime}(u)v=\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\ dx-\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}u\cdot v\ dx-\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u(x))v\ dx.

Clearly, by the definition of weak solution, if
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a critical point of
J
, then it is a weak solution of (
1.1
). As discussed earlier, because of the weight function
Q
, which can be singular at zero, we have used a version of Trudinger-Moser inequality in the radial space
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
for an improved range of
\alpha
. From now on, we restrict ourselves to
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
as function space rather than
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Now, we recall the definition of the Palais-Smale sequence.

Definition 3
.

We say that a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
J
at a level
c
if
J(u_{n})\rightarrow c
and
J^{\prime}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, we say that
J
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at level
c
if every Palais-Smale sequence has a convergent subsequence in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
.

The following lemma is very crucial for our main results. In the proof, we have used assumption (
H2
), also known as the Ambrosetti-Rabonowitz condition.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume (
H1
), (
H2
) holds and if
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
be a Palais-Smale sequence of
J
, then
\{u_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a Palais-Smale sequence of
J
at a level
c
in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
, then by definition

(3.1)

\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u_{n}(x))dx\rightarrow c

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and for all
v\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
,

(3.2)

\left|\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla vdx-\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}\cdot vdx-\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))v(x)\ dx\right|\leq\varepsilon_{n}\|v\|,

where
\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty
. Putting
v=u_{n}
in (
3.2
) and using (
3.1
), there exists
C>0
such that,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)\ dx\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx-\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{2}dx+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|

\displaystyle\quad\ \ \leq 2C+\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u_{n}(x))dx+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|.

\displaystyle\quad\ \ \leq 2C+\frac{1}{K}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)dx+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|,

where at the last step, we had applied (
H2
). We can find constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(3.3)

\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)dx\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|.

Now, we consider the following two cases:

Case 1.
When
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
.
Since
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, using characterization of
\lambda_{1}
given in (
1.8
), we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\min_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{\|u\|^{2}_{2}}\leq\min_{u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{\|u\|^{2}_{2}}

Therefore,
\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\|u\|^{2}
for all
u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
.
Taking
v=u_{n}
as test function in (
3.2
) together with (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{2}dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)\ dx+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}}\|u_{n}\|^{2}+C_{1}+C_{2}\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|+\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|,

since
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, it follows that
\{u_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
.

Case 2.
When
\lambda_{k}<\lambda<\lambda_{k+1}
, for
k\geq 1
.

We can decompose the radial space
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
as similar to
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
by defining two subspaces,
H_{k,r}(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)\cap H_{k}(\Omega)
and
H^{\perp}_{k,r}(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)\cap H^{\perp}_{k}(\Omega)
of
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
. This implies that for any
u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
, we can write
u=u^{k}+u^{\perp}
, where
u^{k}\in H_{k,r}(\Omega)
and
u^{\perp}\in H^{\perp}_{k,r}(\Omega)
. It is easy to verify that

(3.4)

Remark 2
.

The above result holds even if we consider the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Since every Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
and the space
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
is reflexive, therefore, by the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem,
\{u_{n}\}
is relatively compact in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
, i.e. up to a subsequence, there exists
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
. We know that
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p<\infty
, when
\Omega
is bounded. This implies that
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, this implies up to a subsequence,
u_{n}(x)\rightarrow u_{0}(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
.

We need to prove the Palais-Smale condition in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
to prove the existence result. For this, we establish the following lemmas, which are very important tools in proving the Palais-Smale condition.

Lemma 3.2
.

) and (
H3
) holds. If

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{0}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u_{0}(x))dx.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume (

Proof.

Since

is a Palais-Smale sequence in

and from Lemma

, it is bounded. Then, there exists a

, we define a non-negative sequence

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\zeta_{n}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))dx\ +\ \lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}(x)\ dx.

Using Hölder’s inequality and (
3.7
), we get a constant

satisfying

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\zeta_{n}dx\leq C+C\varepsilon_{n}\|u_{n}\|\ +\lambda|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int\limits_{\Omega}|u_{n}(x)|^{2}\ dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By the embedding
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
with
1\leq p<\infty
, for bounded domain
\Omega
, we have
\|u\|_{2}\leq C_{1}\|u\|
for all
u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
. This together with the fact that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
, we obtain a constant

, independent of

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\zeta_{n}(x)\ dx\leq C+\lambda|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{n}\|<C^{\prime}.

This implies that the sequence
\{\zeta_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, then up to a subsequence, there exists a Radon measure
\mu
such that
\zeta_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu
in the weak* topology, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left[\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))\phi+\lambda u_{n}\phi\right]\ dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}\phi\ d\mu,\ \ \forall\ \phi\in C_{0,rad}^{\infty}(\Omega).

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi\ dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}\phi\ d\mu.

As

in

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla\phi\ dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}\phi\ d\mu.

Therefore, the Radon measure
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Hence, the Radon-Nikodym theorem implies that there exists a function
\zeta\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
such that, for any

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\phi\ d\mu=\int\limits_{\Omega}\phi\zeta\ dx.

Then, we conclude using the above relations that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left[\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))\phi+\lambda u_{n}\phi\right]\ dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\phi\zeta\ dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}\left[\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{0}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{0}(x))\phi+\lambda u_{0}\phi\right]\ dx.

Since

a.e. in
\Omega
, so

can be identified as
\displaystyle\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{0}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{0}(x))+\lambda u_{0}(x)
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))\phi\ dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{0}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{0}(x))\phi\ dx.

This completes the proof.

4.
Mountain-pass case when
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}

To prove the existence of a nontrivial solution of equation (
1.1
) when
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, we use the mountain-pass theorem due to A. Ambrosetti and P. Rabinowitz
[
12
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
J:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
functional on a Banach space
(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|)
satisfying
J(0)=0.
Assume that there exist positive numbers
\rho
and
\delta
such that

(i)

J(u)\geq\delta
for all
u\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\|u\|=\rho
.

(ii)

There exists
v\in\mathcal{H}
such that
J(v)<\delta
for some
v\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|v\|\geq\rho
.

(iii)

There exists some
\beta>0
such that
J
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition, for all
c\in(0,\beta)
.

Consider
\displaystyle\Gamma=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],\mathcal{H}):\gamma(0)=0\text{ and }\gamma(1)=v\}
and set
\displaystyle c=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J(\gamma(t))\geq\delta
. Then
c\in(0,\beta)
is a critical value of the functional
J
.

In the following propositions, we show that
J
satisfies the geometry (i) and (ii).

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume (
H1
). Then there exists
\delta,\rho>0
such that
J(u)\geq\delta
, for
u\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
satisfying
\|u\|=\rho
.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
q>1
and
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
, there exists
C>0
in (
1.13
), we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|F(u)|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)\Bigg(\varepsilon_{1}|u|^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}+C|u|^{q}e^{\alpha|u|^{2}}\Bigg)^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{2}dx+C_{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}e^{\frac{4\alpha|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx.

Now, from Lemma
2.3
with
p=\frac{4-\mu}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{2}dx\leq C\|u\|^{2}.

Using Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{1}{v}+\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}=1
,
qv\geq\frac{4-\mu}{2}
and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}e^{\frac{4\alpha|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u|^{\frac{4qv}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha v^{\prime}|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha v^{\prime}|u|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}}.

Choosing
v
close to 1,
\alpha
close to
\alpha_{0}
and
\|u\|=\rho
sufficiently small such that

\frac{4\alpha v^{\prime}}{4-\mu}\|u\|^{2}\leq 4\pi\Bigg(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Bigg)
. Combining the above three inequalities with Lemma
2.4
, we can find constants
C_{3},C_{4}>0
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|F(u)|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\|u\|^{2}+C_{4}\|u\|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}.

Using Proposition
1.1
, (
4.1
) and by characterization of
\lambda_{1}
given in (
1.9
), we can obtain

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u(x))dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2\lambda_{1}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{C_{\mu}}{2}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|F(u(x))|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}}\Bigg)\|u\|^{2}-C_{5}\|u\|^{4-\mu}-C_{6}\|u\|^{2q}.

Next, we denote
g(\rho)=C\rho^{2}-C_{5}\rho^{4-\mu}-C_{6}\rho^{2q}
. Observe that
g(\rho)=0
when
\rho=0
and
4-\mu>2
,
2q>2
. Hence, for sufficiently small
\rho>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
g(\rho)\geq\delta>0
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume (
H1
)-(
H2
). Then there exists
v\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
with
\|v\|>\rho
such that
J(v)<0
.

Proof.

Fix

. For each

\displaystyle\psi(t)=I\Big(\frac{tu_{0}}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big),\text{ where }I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u(x))dx.

Then using assumption (
H2

\displaystyle\frac{\psi^{\prime}(t)}{\psi(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi^{\prime}(t)t}{\psi(t)t}\geq\frac{I^{\prime}\Big(\frac{tu_{0}}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)\frac{tu_{0}}{\|u_{0}\|}}{I\Big(\frac{tu_{0}}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)t}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\Big(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F\Big(u\Big(\frac{tu_{0}(y)}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)\Big)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Big)Q(|x|)f\Big(u\Big(\frac{tu_{0}(y)}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)\Big)u\Big(\frac{tu_{0}(x)}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)dx}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\Big(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F\Big(u\Big(\frac{tu_{0}(y)}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)\Big)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Big)Q(|x|)F\Big(u\Big(\frac{tu_{0}(x)}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)\Big)dx}\geq\frac{2K}{t},\ \ \forall\ \ t>0.

Integrating the above integral over
[1,s\|u_{0}\|]
with

, then

\displaystyle\log(\psi(s\|u_{0}\|))-\log(\psi(1))

\displaystyle\geq 2K(\log(s\|u_{0}\|)).

By simple calculation, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle I(su_{0})\geq I\Big(\frac{u_{0}}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)\|u_{0}\|^{2K}s^{2K}.

By the definition of the functional
J
and from (

), we can get

\displaystyle J(su_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s^{2}}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}-\frac{\lambda s^{2}}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{2}-I\Big(\frac{u_{0}}{\|u_{0}\|}\Big)\|u_{0}\|^{2K}s^{2K}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}s^{2}-C_{2}s^{2}-C_{3}s^{2K},

which implies that

as

. Therefore, for

with

∎

4.1.
The minimax level

The eventual loss of compactness of the Palais-Smale sequence occurs when dealing with critical problems. We need to bring the level
c
below a threshold value to restore compactness. This requires us to prove that
J
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at the level
c
. To reestablish the compactness of
\{u_{n}\}
, we first calculate the upper bound of the mountain-pass level
c
. Inspired from
[
4
,
6
,
21
]
, let us consider the Moser’s functions defined as follows:

M_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left\{\begin{aligned} \sqrt{\log{n}},&&|x|<\frac{1}{n}\\
\frac{\log{(1/|x|)}}{\sqrt{\log{n}}},&&\frac{1}{n}\leq|x|<1\\
0,&&|x|\geq 1\end{aligned}\right.

By simple computations, we see that
M_{n}
satisfies the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|M_{n}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{B_{1}}|M_{n}|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\log{n}rdr+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1}\frac{\log^{2}{1/r}}{\log{n}}rdr=\frac{1}{4\log{n}}-\frac{1}{4n^{2}\log{n}}-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}.

Now,

\displaystyle\|M_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{B_{1}}|\nabla M_{n}|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1}\Bigg(\frac{r}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}\cdot\frac{-1}{r^{2}}\Bigg)^{2}rdr=\int\limits_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1}\frac{1}{r\log{n}}dr=1.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume (
H1
), (
H3
), (
H4
). Then, mountain-pass level
c<\frac{(4-\mu)\pi}{2\alpha_{0}}\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big)
.

Proof.

Because for
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
,
J(su_{0})\to-\infty
as
s\to\infty
by Proposition
4.3
, and since
c\leq\displaystyle\max_{[0,1]}J(tu_{0})
for
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
satisfying
J(u_{0})<0
, it is sufficient to find a
w\in H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
such that
\|w\|=1
and

\max_{s\geq 0}J(sw_{n})<\frac{(4-\mu)\pi}{2\alpha_{0}}\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big).

For this, consider the sequence of Moser functions
\{M_{n}\}
defined as above. We claim that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\displaystyle\max_{s\geq 0}J(sw_{n})<\frac{(4-\mu)\pi}{2\alpha_{0}}\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big)
. Suppose by contradiction, the above inequality does not hold. Then, this maximum is larger than or equal to
\frac{(4-\mu)\pi}{2\alpha_{0}}\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big)

(4.4)

Then

This implies that

(4.5)

From (
4.4
),
\frac{d}{ds}J(sw_{n})\left|{}_{s=s_{n}}=0\right.
. We multiply by
s_{n}
and using the above proposition, we have

\displaystyle s_{n}^{2}-\lambda s_{n}^{2}-\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(s_{n}w_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(s_{n}w_{n}(x))s_{n}w_{n}(x)dx=0.

This further implies that

(4.6)

\displaystyle s_{n}^{2}\geq\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q(|y|)\frac{F(s_{n}w_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(s_{n}w_{n}(x))s_{n}w_{n}(x)dx.

Combining (
H3
) and (
H4
), for all

From (

\displaystyle s_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits\limits_{B_{1/n}(0)}\Bigg(\int\limits\limits_{B_{1/n(0)}}\frac{Q(|y|)F(s_{n}w_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(s_{n}w_{n}(x))s_{n}w_{n}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq F\Bigg(\frac{s_{n}\sqrt{\log{n}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Bigg)f\Bigg(\frac{s_{n}\sqrt{\log{n}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Bigg)\frac{s_{n}\sqrt{\log{n}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits\limits_{B_{1/n}(0)}\int\limits\limits_{B_{1/n}(0)}\frac{Q(|x|)Q(|y|)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq M_{0}^{-1}(\beta_{0}-\varepsilon)^{2}\Bigg(\frac{\log{n}}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{\frac{v+1}{2}}s_{n}^{v+1}e^{\alpha_{0}s_{n}^{2}\pi^{-1}\log{n}}\int\limits\limits_{B_{1/n}(0)}\int\limits\limits_{B_{1/n}(0)}\frac{|x|^{b_{0}}|y|^{b_{0}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\geq M_{0}^{-1}(\beta_{0}-\varepsilon)^{2}\Bigg(\frac{\log{n}}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{\frac{v+1}{2}}\frac{s_{n}^{v+1}e^{\alpha_{0}s_{n}^{2}\pi^{-1}\log{n}}}{n^{2b_{0}}}\int\limits\limits_{B_{1/n}(0)}\int\limits\limits_{B_{\frac{1}{n}-|x|}(0)}\frac{dzdx}{|z|^{\mu}}

By some simple computations, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle s_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq M_{0}^{-1}(\beta_{0}-\varepsilon)^{2}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{\frac{v+1}{2}}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(2-\mu)(3-\mu)(4-\mu)}s_{n}^{v+1}e^{[\alpha_{0}s_{n}^{2}\pi^{-1}-(4+2b_{0}-\mu)]\log{n}+\frac{v+1}{2}\log\log{n}}.

For large

\displaystyle(1-v)\log{s_{n}}

\displaystyle\geq\log\left[M_{0}^{-1}(\beta_{0}-\varepsilon)^{2}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigg)^{\frac{v+1}{2}}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(2-\mu)(3-\mu)(4-\mu)}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+[\alpha_{0}s_{n}^{2}\pi^{-1}-(4+2b_{0}-\mu)]\log{n}.

If

in (

), this implies a contradiction. Therefore,

holds.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume (
H1
) and (

). Then, there exists a function

Proof.

Let

\{\|u_{n}\|\}

in

\Omega

. On the contrary, suppose

) holds true for some

, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Big(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Big)Q(|x|)F(u_{n}(x))dx

(4.9)

\displaystyle\quad\ \ \ =\int\limits_{\Omega}\Big(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{0}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\Big)dyQ(|x|)F(u_{0}(x))dx=0

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|^{2}=2c<\frac{(4-\mu)\pi}{\alpha_{0}}\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big).

From Proposition

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)f(u_{n}(y))u_{n}(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)dx\Bigg|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{\mu}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{1}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}|^{2}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}+C_{2}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}e^{\frac{4\alpha|u_{n}|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}.

Putting

in Lemma

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)f(u_{n}(y))u_{n}(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)dx\Bigg|

sufficiently close to
\alpha_{0}
and

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}e^{\frac{4\alpha|u_{n}|^{2}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ \ \leq C\|u_{n}\|^{2q}.

Using the above estimate in (

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)f(u_{n}(y))u_{n}(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)dx\Bigg|\leq C_{3}\|u_{n}\|^{4-\mu}+C_{4}\|u_{n}\|^{2q}\leq C.

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)dx\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg[\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)f(u_{n}(y))u_{n}(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)dx\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\times

\displaystyle\quad\quad\Bigg[\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u_{n}(x))dx\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

This together with

, which further implies from (

) that

, hence we get a contradiction. Therefore

. Furthermore, from Lemma
3.3
and

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume (
H1
) and (
H2
). Then the functional

(PS)_{c}
condition for all

Suppose

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)

a.e. in
\Omega
. For each

, we define
v_{n}
and

as

\displaystyle v_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|},\text{ and }v=\frac{u_{0}}{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|}.

By Fatou’s lemma,
0<\|u_{0}\|\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|

\|u_{n}\|\to\|u_{0}\|
which together with
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}

. Since

, from Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle 1-\|v\|^{2}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\|u_{0}\|^{2}}{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c+\lambda\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{2}+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{0}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u_{0}(x))dx-\|u_{0}\|^{2}}{{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}}\|u_{n}\|^{2}}.

This implies that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|^{2}=\frac{2c-2J(u_{0})}{1-\|v\|^{2}}.

From Lemma
4.5
,
J^{\prime}(u_{0})=0
, then we have

\displaystyle J(u_{0})=J(u_{0})-\frac{1}{2}J^{\prime}(u_{0})u_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{0}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)[f(u_{0}(x))u_{0}(x)-F(u_{0}(x))]dx,

which together with (

), we can say that

.
Using this inequality with (
4.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2c}{1-\|v\|^{2}}<\frac{(4-\mu)\pi}{\alpha_{0}(1-\|v\|^{2})}\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big).

Choosing
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
sufficiently close to
\alpha_{0}
and
p>1
close to 1 such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
and

\displaystyle\frac{4\alpha p\|u_{n}\|^{2}}{4-\mu}<\frac{4\pi}{1-\|v\|^{2}}\Bigg(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Bigg).

Then from above inequality and Lemma

, we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha p}{4-\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha p}{4-\mu}\|u_{n}\|^{2}|v_{n}|^{2}}dx<+\infty.

Next, we claim that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))(u_{n}(x)-u_{0}(x))dx\to 0.

To prove this claim, we use Proposition
1.1
,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)f(u_{n}(x))(u_{n}(x)-u_{0}(x))dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mu}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|F(u_{n})|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{4}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|f(u_{n})(u_{n}-u_{0})|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{4-\mu}{4}}.

We estimate first integral using (
1.13
), Lemma
2.3
and (
4.13
), we can find a constant
\overline{C}
satisfying

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|F(u_{n})|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}|^{2}dx+C\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}e^{\frac{4\alpha}{4-\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{2}+C\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}|^{\frac{4qp^{\prime}}{4-\mu}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha p}{4-\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{2}+C\|u_{n}\|^{\frac{4q}{4-\mu}}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)e^{\frac{4\alpha p}{4-\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\overline{C}<+\infty.

On the other hand, from (

) with

, Lemma

, (

) and taking

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|f(u_{n})(u_{n}-u_{0})|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}|^{\frac{2(2-\mu)}{4-\mu}}|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}dx+C\int\limits_{\Omega}Q^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}(|x|)|u_{n}-u_{0}|^{\frac{4}{4-\mu}}|u_{n}|^{\frac{4(q-1)}{4-\mu}}e^{\frac{4\alpha}{4-\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}}dx

. Since

5.
Linking case where
\lambda_{k}<\lambda<\lambda_{k+1}

When
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
, Theorem
4.1
-(i) no longer holds, thus our previous existence approach fails. In such cases, we use the following critical point theorem known as the Linking theorem, due to A. Ambrosetti and
P. Rabinowitz
[
12
]
, which provides a milder version of Theorem
4.1
-(i).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
J:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
functional on a Banach space
(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|)
such that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
with
dim\mathcal{H}_{1}<\infty
. If
J
satisfies the following:

(i)

There exist constants
\rho,\delta>0
such that
J(u)\geq\delta
for all
u\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
satisfying
\|u\|=\rho
.

(ii)

There exists
z\notin\mathcal{H}_{1}
with
\|z\|=1
and
R>\rho
such that
J(u)\leq 0
for all
u\in\partial\mathcal{Q}
, where

\mathcal{Q}=\{v+sz:v\in\mathcal{H}_{1},\|v\|\leq R\text{ and }0\leq s\leq R\}.

(iii)

There exists some
\beta>0
such that
J
satisfies the
(PS)_{c}
for
c\in(0,\beta)
.
Then
c
is defined as

c=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{u\in\mathcal{Q}}J(\gamma(u)),

where
\Gamma=\{\gamma\in C(\overline{\mathcal{Q}},\mathcal{H}):\gamma(u)=u,\text{ if }u\in\partial\mathcal{Q}
}
, is a critical value of
J
.

In the following propositions, we show the above geometry.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{k}<\lambda<\lambda_{k+1}
and
f
satisfies (
H1
). Then there exists
\delta,\rho>0
such that
J(u)\geq\delta
, for
\|u\|=\rho
and
u\in H_{k,r}^{\perp}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in H_{k,r}^{\perp}(\Omega)
, proceeding as similar to (
4.2
) and using the characterization of
\lambda_{k+1}
given in (
1.10
), we have

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{2}-C_{5}\|u\|^{4-\mu}-C_{6}\|u\|^{2q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\Bigg)\|u\|^{2}-C_{5}\|u\|^{4-\mu}-C_{6}\|u\|^{2q}

\displaystyle\geq C\|u\|^{2}-C_{5}\|u\|^{4-\mu}-C_{6}\|u\|^{2q},

this implies that for sufficiently small
\|u\|=\rho>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
J(u)\geq\delta
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\lambda_{k}<\lambda<\lambda_{k+1}
and (
H1
), (
H2
) holds. Define
\mathcal{Q}=\{v+sz:v\in H_{k,r}(\Omega),\|v\|\leq R\text{ and }0\leq s\leq R,\text{ for some }R>\rho\}
, where
\rho
is given in Proposition
5.2
and
z\in H_{k,r}^{\perp}(\Omega)
with
\|z\|=1
. Then
J(u)\leq 0
for all
u\in\partial\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

For some
R>0
, we split
\partial\mathcal{Q}
into following three parts:

\mathcal{Q}_{1}=\{u\in H_{k,r}(\Omega):\|u\|\leq R\}

\mathcal{Q}_{2}=\{u+sz:u\in H_{k,r}(\Omega),\|u\|=R\text{ and }0\leq s\leq R\}

\mathcal{Q}_{3}=\{u+Rz:u\in H_{k,r}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq R\}.

Case 1. If
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
, this implies
u\in H_{k,r}(\Omega)
and by characterization of
\lambda_{k}
given in (
1.11
) together with the fact that
F(s)\geq 0
, we have

J(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Bigg)\|u\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits\limits_{\Omega}\Bigg(\int\limits\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)F(u(y))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\Bigg)Q(|x|)F(u(x))dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Bigg)R^{2}<0,

for any choice of
R>0
.

Before verifying the claim on
\mathcal{Q}_{2},\mathcal{Q}_{3}
, let us fix some
u_{0}\in H_{k,r}(\Omega)
and define a map
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as
\phi(t)=J(tu_{0})
. From (
4.3
), we have

\phi(t)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}-\frac{\lambda t^{2}}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{2}-Ct^{2K}\|u_{0}\|^{2K},\ \text{where }K>1,

which implies that
\phi(t)\rightarrow-\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Case 2. If
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{2}
, there exists
v\in H_{k,r}(\Omega)
and
0\leq s\leq R
such that
u=v+sz
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}=\|v+sz\|^{2}=\|v\|^{2}+s^{2}\|z\|^{2}\geq\|v\|^{2}=R^{2}.

Therefore, if we choose
R>0
sufficiently large, we have
J(u)<0
.

Case 3. Now, if
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{3}
, then there exists
v\in H_{k,r}(\Omega)
such that
u=v+Rz
. Moreover,

\|u\|^{2}=\|v+Rz\|^{2}=\|v\|^{2}+R^{2}\|z\|^{2}\geq R^{2}.

For
R>0
large enough we have
J(u)<0
.
∎

5.1.
The minimax level

We have to choose a
z\in H_{k,r}^{\perp}(\Omega)
such that
\|z\|=1
and
J(u)<\frac{(4-\mu)\pi}{2\alpha_{0}}\Big(1+\frac{2b_{0}}{4-\mu}\Big),\ \forall\ u\in Q.
Let
P_{k}:H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)\rightarrow H_{k,r}^{\perp}(\Omega)
be the orthogonal projection. Let us define

(5.1)

W_{n}(x)=P_{k}(M_{n}(x))

Since
H_{k,r}
is a finite dimensional subspace, then there exists
A_{0}>0
and
B_{0}>0
such that

(5.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\|u\|^{2}\leq A_{0}\|u\|^{2}_{2}\ \text{ and }\\
&\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{B_{0}}{B}\|u\|_{2}\end{aligned}\ \ \ \forall\ \ \ u\in H_{k},\right.

where
B>0
such that
\|M_{n}\|_{2}\leq\frac{B}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
W_{n}
be defined in (
5.1
). Then the following estimates hold:

(i)

1-\frac{A_{0}}{\log{n}}\leq\|W_{n}\|^{2}\leq 1
.

(ii)

W_{n}(x)\geq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{-B_{0}}{\sqrt{\log{n}}},&&\ \Omega\setminus B_{\frac{1}{n}}(0)\\
&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{\log{n}}-\frac{B_{0}}{\sqrt{\log{n}}},&&\ B_{\frac{1}{n}}(0)\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

By simple computations, we have

\displaystyle\|W_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|M_{n}\|^{2}-\|(I-P_{k})M_{n}\|^{2}\ \text{ and }(I-P_{k})M_{n}\in H_{k,r}(\Omega)

5.1
implies that
u_{0}
is a critical point of the functional
J
in
H^{1}_{0,rad}(\Omega)
. By using Theorem
1.2
(we refer to Appendix for the proof), we conclude that
u_{0}
is a critical point of
J
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. So, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Appendix

In this section, we show that the functional
J
is
\mathcal{O}(2)
-invariant, where
\mathcal{O}(2)
is defined in Section
1
. For this, we need to assume that
\Omega
is invariant with respect to
\mathcal{O}(2)
, then using change of variable and
|det(h^{-1})|=1
, we have

\displaystyle J(hu)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)Q(|x|)F(hu(y))F(hu(x))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dydx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y|)Q(|x|)F(u(h^{-1}y))F(u(h^{-1}x))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dydx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|det(h^{-1})|^{2}}\int\limits_{H(\Omega)}\int\limits_{H(\Omega)}\frac{Q(|h(y)|)Q(|h(x)|)F(u(y))F(u(x))}{|h(x)-h(y)|^{\mu}}dydx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|h(y)|)Q(|h(x)|)F(u(y))F(u(x))}{|h(x)-h(y)|^{\mu}}dydx.

Since
h
is linear and it is isometry, then

|h(x)-h(y)|=|x-y|\text{ and }|h(x)|=|x|

From the above two inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle J(hu)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{Q(|y)|)Q(|x)|)F(u(y))F(u(x))}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dydx=J(u).

Hence
J
is
\mathcal{O}(2)
-invariant, so by the principle of symmetric criticality,
u
is a critical point of
J
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
).

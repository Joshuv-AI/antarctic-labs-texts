Characterizing the intersection of QMA and coQMA.
Serge Massar, Miklos Santha

Abstract
We show that the functional analogue of QMA ∩ coQMA \mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}} , denoted F ⁡ ( QMA ∩ coQMA ) {\rm F}(\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}) , equals the complexity class Total Functional QMA ( TFQMA ). To prove this we need to introduce an alternative definition of QMA ∩ coQMA \mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}} in terms of a single quantum verification procedure. We show that if TFQMA equals the functional analogue of BQP ( FBQP ), then QMA ∩ coQMA = BQP \mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}=\mbox{{BQP}} . We show that if there is a QMA complete problem that (robustly) reduces to a problem in TFQMA , then QMA ∩ coQMA = QMA \mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}=\mbox{{QMA}} . Our results thus imply that if some of the inclusions between functional classes FBQP ⊆ TFQMA ⊆ FQMA \mbox{{FBQP}}\subseteq\mbox{{TFQMA}}\subseteq\mbox{{FQMA}} are in fact equalities, then the corresponding inclusions in BQP ⊆ QMA ∩ coQMA ⊆ QMA \mbox{{BQP}}\subseteq\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}\subseteq\mbox{{QMA}} are also equalities.

It lies between Functional

, finding Nash equilibria
[

. Although there probably do not exist complete problems for

For recent work in this direction, see
[

One of the results of the founding paper
[

]
concerns the relation between

and other complexity classes. The inclusions

(1)

are obvious. But what would be the consequences if some of these inclusions were not strict, but replaced by equality?

In
[
1
]
this question is connected with the inclusions

\mbox{{P}}\subseteq\mbox{{NP}}\cap\mbox{{coNP}}\subseteq\mbox{{NP}}\ .

(2)

It was first shown in
[

that an inclusion in Eq. (

The quantum analogue of

However in

The analogs of Eqs. (

\mbox{{FBQP}}\subseteq\mbox{{TFQMA}}\subseteq\mbox{{FQMA}}

(4)

and

\mbox{{BQP}}\subseteq\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}\subseteq\mbox{{QMA}}\ .

We first show that

equals the

\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}
:

\mbox{{TFQMA}}={\rm F}(\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}})\ .

We then show that if
\mbox{{FBQP}}=\mbox{{TFQMA}}
, then
\mbox{{BQP}}=\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}
.
We finally show that if there is a

, then
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}=\mbox{{QMA}}

But while the proofs in the classical case are elementary, the

Showing that
\mbox{{TFQMA}}={\rm F}(\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}})
requires a detailed enquiry into what is the correct definition of
{\rm F}(\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}})

The difficulty is that

in
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}
is naturally defined by two quantum verification procedures

. But these two quantum verification procedures do not necessarily commute. Therefore it is not clear how to define a witness for
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}

We will see that the solution to this conundrum is to append to the state a bit

one only tests

However, while this solution is natural, it is not clear whether it is the unique way to solve this problem. In order to address this, we need a deeper understanding of
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}

To this end we introduce two alternative definitions of
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}
. The more
important is a definition in terms of a single 2-outcome quantum verification procedure which takes as input a single witness.
This definition is particularly useful because it allows us to apply to
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}
the notions of of eigenbasis, spectrum, and eigenspace of a quantum verification procedure which we introduced in
[
12
]
(based on the earlier work of Marriott and Watrous
[
13
]
).
These notions are then used to provide a natural definition for

which parallels the definition for
FQMA
given in
[
12
]
.

Furthermore, by extending the
notion of QMA amplification as developed in
[
13
]
, we show that our definition of
{\rm F}(\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}})
is independent of the completeness and soundness bounds.

Together these results allow us to define precisely the right hand side of Eq. (
6
), in such a way that it has the same structure as the left hand side of Eq. (
6

Quantum Verification Procedures

Definition 1
.

d
-Outcome Quantum Verification Procedure.

For any integer
d
larger or equal to
2

d
-outcome quantum verification procedure is a
polynomial time
uniform family of quantum circuits
Q=\{Q_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

with

Q_{n}
taking as input
(x,|\psi\rangle\otimes|0^{k}\rangle)

x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
is a binary string of length
n

|\psi\rangle
is a state of
m

poly

The last
k

|0^{k}\rangle

\mathcal{H}_{k}
,
and the
m

|\psi\rangle
form the witness Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{m}
.
The outcome of the run of

is a word
w\in\{0,\ldots d-1\}

It is obtained by measuring the first

.
We denote this outcome by

.

In most of this article we will consider 2-outcome quantum verification procedures. For brevity throughout this work we use the following terminology which is standard in the literature:

Definition 2
.

Quantum Verification Procedure.

A 2-outcome quantum verification procedure is called a quantum verification procedure.

Note that a
d
-outcome quantum verification procedure can of course also take as input a mixed state
\rho
, rather than a pure state
|\psi\rangle
. Mixed states can be written as convex combinations of pure states. The acceptance (rejection) probability for the mixed state is the convex combination of the acceptance (rejection) probabilities for the constituent pure states.

In order to simplify notation, in what follows we will mainly consider the case where
Q_{n}
takes as input a pure state. In view of the above remark, the extension to mixed state inputs is immediate. In some cases the argument requires that
Q_{n}
takes as input a mixed state, in which case,
abusing slightly the notation, we write
Q_{n}(x,\rho)
for the outcome of the quantum verification procedure on the mixed state
\rho
.

QMA

Definition 3
.

(a,b)–Quantum Verification Procedure.

Let

7.2
Functional classes based on eigenstates

The above definitions are natural. However they are not completely satisfactory for several reasons.

First,
we do not know whether
\mbox{{FQMA}}_{W}(a)
is independent of the threshold
a

Second, while the sets

Third, the very useful concept of eigenstate and eigenspace is not captured by the functional classes based on witnesses.
This is illustrated in the following example:

Example 1

that decreases faster than

–quantum verification procedure

whose spectrum

Definition 29

Iterative Procedures and Eigenspace Preserving Maps

We now show how iterative procedures can be used to construct e-maps.

The results in the present section are purely classical, and can be phrased as the following problem. Given

Indeed it is known that when the gap between the largest and second largest eigenvalue of the quantum verificaton procedure and/or

Conclusion

In the present work we have shown that
that
\mbox{{TFQMA}}={\rm F}(\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}})
; that
if
\mbox{{FBQP}}=\mbox{{TFQMA}}
, then
\mbox{{BQP}}=\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}
; and
that if there is a
QMA
complete problem that strongly reduces to a problem in

TFQMA
, then
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}=\mbox{{QMA}}
.

These results are not very surprising, as they are immediate generalisations of the analog classical results. However they are significantly more complicated to prove than the classical results.

In the appendix we sketch how similar results can be obtained for the classical probabilistic classes
\rm MA
and
\rm coMA
. However since one can always take
\rm MA
to have one sided error
[
14
]
, slightly stronger results should hold in this case. We leave the detailed results to further work.

It would be interesting to better understand the inclusions

\mbox{{BQP}}\subseteq\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}\subseteq\mbox{{QMA}}\ .

(151)

In one direction, exhibiting problems in
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}
that do not seem to be in
BQP
, and problems in
QMA
that do not seem to be in
\mbox{{QMA}}\cap\mbox{{coQMA}}
, would provide evidence that the inclusions are strict.

In the statement of Theorem
18
we use the notion of robust reduction, see Definition
27
. It may be that the theorem can be proven using the weaker notion of reduction given in
definition
25
, but this probably requires new proof techniques. Alternatively, it is possible that the notion of robust reduction is the natural notion. Some evidence for this is given at the end of subsection
8.2
.

The questions we ask and answer in the present work concerning

\rm TFMA

However in

A probabilistic verification procedure is a polynomial time uniform family of classical circuits

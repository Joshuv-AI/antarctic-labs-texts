A Braided Lambda Calculus
Masahito Hasegawa

Abstract
We present an untyped linear lambda calculus with braids, the corresponding combinatory logic, and the semantic models given by crossed G G -sets.

Why braids?

Braids do not play a serious role in most of the conventional computational models,
and for the time being this work is largely a mathematical exercise with no immediate
application.
Nevertheless, let us say a little bit more on the motivation of this work and its
potential applications.

Extensionally, permutations (symmetry/exchange) are used for
swapping two data.
On the other hand, braids provide non-extensional information on
how to implement permutations in three dimensions. If braids have
some computational meaning, it should be something about
low-level (intermediate) codes to be compiled in some 3D computational architectures.
One such computational model allowing “braids for implementation”
reading is
Topological Quantum Computation

[
11
]
, where the topological
information of anyons in 3D space-time does matter;
we hope that this work will find some usage in this context.
In a larger perspective, this work forms part of our research project on
relating low-level codes and low-dimensional topology via categorical machineries.

From a more abstract point of view, our braided lambda calculus and the corresponding
braided combinatory algebras are algebraic structures which can be
described in terms of
PROBs
(
pro
ducts and
b
raids categories), the braided
version of PROPs (products and permutations categories).
It seems that the theory of PROBs is a sort of folklore and
there are very few published works on it (cf.
[
13
,
17
]
); we expect that the
braided lambda calculus serves as a good test case of PROBs, e.g. the
treatment of substitutions in braided algebras.

Why linear?

Our calculus is linear, as there is no non-trivial braid in a non-linear setting.
When the tensor product is cartesian, any braid
\sigma_{A,B}:A\times B\rightarrow B\times A
is equal to
the symmetry

\langle\pi^{\prime}_{A,B},\pi_{A,B}\rangle:A\times B\rightarrow B\times A

because

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\sigma_{A,B}&=&\langle\pi_{B,A},\pi^{\prime}_{B,A}\rangle\circ\sigma_{A,B}&\langle\pi,\pi^{\prime}\rangle=\mathit{id}\\
&=&\langle\pi_{B,A}\circ\sigma_{A,B},\pi^{\prime}_{B,A}\circ\sigma_{A,B}\rangle\\
&=&\langle\pi^{\prime}_{A,B},\pi_{A,B}\rangle\end{array}

where
\pi_{B,A}\circ\sigma_{A,B}=\pi^{\prime}_{A,B}
because

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\pi_{B,A}\circ\sigma_{A,B}&=&(\mathit{id}_{B}\times!_{A})\circ\sigma_{A,B}\\
&=&\sigma_{1,B}\circ(!_{A}\times\mathit{id}_{B})&\mbox{naturality of $\sigma$}\\
&=&\mathit{id}_{B}\circ\pi^{\prime}_{A,B}&\sigma_{1,B}=\mathit{id}_{B}\\
&=&\pi^{\prime}_{A,B}\end{array}

Similarly
\pi^{\prime}_{B,A}\circ\sigma_{A,B}=\pi_{A,B}
holds.
Hence linearity is essential for studying a braided calculus in a
meaningful way.

Why untyped?

Our calculus is untyped. Compared to the typed case (including braided MLL
[
6
]

and tensorial logic
[
12
]
), we have a simpler syntax
and subtler, more challenging semantics - while the simply typed braided lambda calculus can be
modelled by any braided monoidal closed category, the untyped calculus requires
a reflexive object, which is hard to find in the well-known braided categories
in TQFT
[
16
]
. We overcome this difficulty by using a braided relational
model constructed in our previous work
[
8
]
. As far as we know, this is the
first non-trivial example of a reflexive object in a non-symmetric ribbon category.

Contributions

Our contributions are summarized as follows.

•

We formulate a braided lambda calculus whose syntax is a mild modification of the untyped linear lambda calculus with explicit braids (Section 2).

•

We introduce the corresponding combinatory logic and show
the combinatory completeness which ensures that our combinatory logic is as
expressive as the braided lambda calculus (Section 3).

•

We give categorical semantics given by reflexive objects in braided
monoidal closed categories, and present some concrete models using crossed
G
-sets
(Section 4).

Remark 1
(Contexts are redundant)

In the braided lambda calculus, the context
is always uniquely determined by the term, thus redundant.
Given a braided lambda term
M
, we define the list
\mathrm{cxt}(M)

of free variables in
M
as follows:

\mathrm{cxt}(x)=x
,

\mathrm{cxt}(M\,N)=\mathrm{cxt}(M),\mathrm{cxt}(N)
,

\mathrm{cxt}(\lambda x.M)=\Gamma
where
\mathrm{cxt}(M)=\Gamma,x
,
and

\mathrm{cxt}([s]M)=s(\mathrm{cxt}(M))

where
s(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{s(1)},\dots,x_{s(n)}
.

It follows that
\Gamma\vdash M
iff
\mathrm{cxt}(M)=\Gamma
.
Hence the context of a braided term is unique:
if both
\Gamma\vdash M
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\vdash M
are derivable,
then
\Gamma
is identical to
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

Defining substitutions

Define the substitution map
(-)[i:=m]:B_{n}\rightarrow B_{n+m-1}

for
1\leq i\leq n
and
m\geq 0
as follows.

•

e[i:=m]\equiv e
.

•

({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j}s)[i:=m]\equiv{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j+m-1}(s[i:=m])
when

i\leq j\!-\!1
.

•

({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j}s)[i:=m]\equiv{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j}(s[i:=m])
when

i\geq j\!+\!2
.

•

({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j}s)[j:=m]\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s[j+1:=0]&m=0\\
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j+m-1}\cdots{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j+1}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j}(s[j+1:=m])&m\geq 1\end{array}\right.

•

({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j}s)[j+1:=m]\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s[j:=0]&m=0\\
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j+1}\cdots{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j+m-1}(s[j:=m])&m\geq 1\end{array}\right.

•

Similarly for
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j}^{-1}s
.

The substitution map is well-defined:
s[i:=m]
does not depend on the choice of

g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in\{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}^{\pm},\dots,{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n}^{\pm}\}
such that
s=g_{1}\cdots g_{k}
.
Note that
s[i:=1]\equiv s
holds for any
s\in B_{n}
and
i
.
We give some examples of the substitution map in Figure

4.1
Categorical Models

A model of the braided lambda calculus (without
\eta
) can be given by an object
X

in a braided monoidal closed category
[
9
]

such that the internal hom
[X,X]
is a retract of
X
.
The situation is largely the same as that of the models of the untyped lambda calculus
given by reflexive objects in cartesian closed categories. Let us sketch how it works.
Let
\mathit{ev}:[X,X]\otimes X\rightarrow X
be the evaluation map given by
the monoidal closure, and write
\Lambda(f):\Gamma\rightarrow[X,X]
for
the currying of an arrow
f:\Gamma\otimes X\rightarrow X
.
Assume an arrow
\varphi:[X,X]\rightarrow X
with a right inverse

\psi:X\rightarrow[X,X]
. Then we can interpret
a braided lambda term
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\vdash M
as an arrow

[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\vdash M]\!]
from

X^{\otimes n}=\overbrace{X\otimes\dots\otimes X}^{n}
to
X

as follows.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}[\![x\vdash x]\!]&=&\mathit{id}_{X}\\
[\![\Gamma\vdash\lambda x.M]\!]&=&\Lambda([\![\Gamma,x\vdash M]\!]);\varphi\\
[\![\Gamma,\Delta\vdash M\,N]\!]&=&([\![\Gamma\vdash M]\!];\psi\otimes[\![\Delta\vdash N]\!]);\mathit{ev}\\
[\![x_{s(1)},\dots,x_{s(n)}\vdash[s]M]\!]&=&[\![s]\!];[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\vdash M]\!]\end{array}

where
;
denotes the relational composition, and the interpretation
[\![s]\!]

is the interpretation of the braid
s
on
X^{\otimes n}
.
The
\beta
-equality is validated because
\varphi;\psi=\mathit{id}_{[X,X]}

hold.
An extensional model (i.e., validating
\eta
) is given by an
X
such that

[X,X]
is isomorphic to
X
, i.e.,
\psi=\varphi^{-1}
.

There are plenty of braided monoidal closed categories in the literature —
many of them are found in the context of representation theory of
quantum groups
[
16
]
. However, finding a braided monoidal closed category with
a non-trivial reflexive object is not easy — impossible if we stick to
finite dimensional linear representations, as the dimension of
[X,X]
is strictly higher than that of
X
unless
X
is one-dimensional.
Below we present models using braided relational semantics
[
8
]

where the problem of dimensions disappears.

G

Fix a group

Recall that a

is a set

G

\bullet:G\times X\rightarrow X
and a valuation map
|\_|:X\rightarrow G

|g\bullet x|=g|x|g^{-1}

There is a

16
]

\mathbf{XRel}(G)

whose objects are crossed
G
-sets and
a morphism from

to

is a binary relation

between
X
and
Y

such that
(x,y)\in r

The dual of a crossed
G
-set

X^{*}=(X,\bullet,|\_|^{-1})
.
The tensor of
X=(X,\bullet,|\_|)
and
Y=(Y,\bullet,|\_|)
is

X\otimes Y=(X\times Y,(g,(x,y))\mapsto(g\bullet x,g\bullet y),(x,y)\mapsto|x||y|)
.
For this monoidal structure we have a braiding

\sigma_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\otimes X

as

\sigma_{X,Y}=\{((x,y),(|x|\bullet y,x))~|~x\in X,y\in Y\}.

See
[
8
]
for further details of

.

Below we will give a crossed
G
-set

\mathcal{T}
such that the internal hom

is a retract of
\mathcal{T}
, which forms a model of the braided lambda calculus.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the set of binary trees
whose leaves are labelled by elements of
G

(or the implicational formulas generated from

):

t~::=~g~|~t\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\,$\multimap$\,}t~~~~~(g\in G)

\mathcal{T}
is a crossed
G
-set with
the valuation
|\_|:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow G
given by

|g|=g
and
|x\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\,$\multimap$\,}y|=|x||y|^{-1}

and the
G
-action
\bullet:G\times\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}

given by

g\bullet h=ghg^{-1}~~(h\in G),~~~~g\bullet(x\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\,$\multimap$\,}y)=(g\bullet x)\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\,$\multimap$\,}(g\bullet y)

Moreover the map
\varphi:\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}

sending

to

gives a morphism

\varphi=\{((x,y),x\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{\,$\multimap$\,}y)~|~x,y\in\mathcal{T}\}:\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}

(without
\eta
)
using

as follows. A term

5
Conclusion

We introduced the syntax and semantics of an untyped braided lambda calculus.
Future work will include
the typed variants, complete axiomatization of the braided combinatory logic,
extension to the tangled lambda calculus, and applications
to novel computational models making use of braids, most notably topological
quantum computation.

Let
\lambda_{\mathit{lin}}
be the set of linear lambda terms
and
\mathbf{BCI}
be the set of terms generated by variables (each occurring just once),
\mathbf{B}
,
\mathbf{C}
,
\mathbf{I}
and application.
Let
=_{\mathbf{BCI}}
be the smallest congruence on
\mathbf{BCI}
satisfying the axioms

Define translations

The following lemma is crucial and the most difficult:

Lemma 3

P=_{\mathbf{BCI}}Q
implies
\lambda^{*}x.P=_{\mathbf{BCI}}\lambda^{*}x.Q
.

Proof

For each axiom
P=Q
with free
x
we show
\lambda^{*}x.P=\lambda^{*}x.Q
.
The relevant cases are (B), (C) and (I).
For the case of (I), we are to show
\lambda^{*}x.\mathbf{I}\,M=\lambda^{*}x.M

with free

in

,
which follows from

\lambda^{*}x.\mathbf{I}\,M\equiv\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{I}\,(\lambda^{*}x.M)=\mathbf{I}\,(\lambda^{*}x.M)=\lambda^{*}x.M

The case of (B) contains three sub-cases depending on where the free
x
occurs.
For instance, showing
\lambda^{*}x.\mathbf{B}\,L\,M\,N=\lambda^{*}x.L\,(M\,N)

with free
x
in
N
amounts to showing

\mathbf{B}\,L\,(\mathbf{B}\,M\,(\lambda^{*}x.N))=\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,L\,M)\,(\lambda^{*}x.N)

for which it suffices to show the associativity
\mathbf{B}\,L\,(\mathbf{B}\,M\,N)=\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,L\,M)\,N
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathbf{B}\,L\,(\mathbf{B}\,M\,N)&=&\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,L)\,(\mathbf{B}\,M)\,N&(B)\\
&=&\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,L\,(\mathbf{B}\,M)\,N&(B)\\
&=&\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,L)\,\mathbf{B}\,M\,N&(B)\\
&=&\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,L)\,M\,N&(C)\\
&=&\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B})\,(\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B})\,L\,M\,N&(B)\\
&=&\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B})\,\mathbf{B}\,L\,M\,N&(\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B})\,\mathbf{B}~=~\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B})\,(\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}))\\
&=&\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,L)\,M\,N&(B)\\
&=&\mathbf{B}\,(\mathbf{B}\,L\,M)\,N&(B)\end{array}

Other two sub-cases of (B) and three sub-cases of (C) are similar (and more lengthy).

Price’s law and precise late-time asymptotics for subextremal Reissner–Nordström black holes
Yannis Angelopoulos, Stefanos Aretakis, Dejan Gajic

Abstract
In this paper, we prove precise late-time asymptotics for solutions to the wave equation supported on angular frequencies greater or equal to ℓ \ell on the domain of outer communications of subextremal Reissner–Nordström spacetimes up to and including the event horizon. Our asymptotics yield, in particular, sharp upper and lower decay rates which are consistent with Price’s law on such backgrounds. We present a theory for inverting the time operator and derive an explicit representation of the leading-order asymptotic coefficient in terms of the Newman–Penrose charges at null infinity associated with the time integrals. Our method is based on purely physical space techniques. For each angular frequency ℓ \ell we establish a sharp hierarchy of r r -weighted radially commuted estimates with length 2 ​ ℓ + 5 2\ell+5 . We complement this hierarchy with a novel hierarchy of weighted elliptic estimates of length ℓ + 1 \ell+1 .

1.3
Overview of the proof

In this section we provide a summary of the main ideas of the proof of the asymptotics presented in the previous section. To make our methods clearer, we will present a schematic version of the main estimates in this section, omitting terms that do not play an important role for the structure of the arguments. We also omit difficulties such as capturing the redshift and trapping effects which have been extensively addressed in the literature. We note that all the integrals are taken with respect to the volume form corresponding to the induced metric of the integrating region.

1.3.1
Almost-sharp time decay of the energy flux

Recall that the energy-momentum tensor corresponding to a linear wave is given by:

\mathbb{T}_{\mu\nu}[\psi]\doteq\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial_{\nu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\alpha}\psi.

The energy current

1.3.2
Almost sharp decay for the radiation field

We can derive almost sharp decay for the radiation field
r\psi_{\geq\ell}|_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}
using the fundamental theorem of calculus and a Hardy inequality:

\begin{split}r\psi_{\geq\ell}\lesssim\sqrt[4]{\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}J^{N}[\psi_{\geq\ell}]\cdot n_{\tau}\,d\mu_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\cdot\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}r^{2}\cdot J^{N}[\psi_{\geq\ell}]\cdot n_{\tau}\,d\mu_{\Sigma_{\tau}}}\lesssim&\sqrt[4]{\frac{1}{\tau^{2\ell+5}}\cdot\frac{1}{\tau^{2\ell+3}}}=\frac{1}{\tau^{\ell+2-\epsilon}}.\end{split}

This is the optimal rate for
r\psi_{\geq\ell}|_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}
, however it is far from optimal for
\psi_{\geq\ell}|_{r=r_{0}}
. For
\psi_{\geq\ell}
itself we make use of

\displaystyle-\partial_{\rho}(r^{-2\ell}\psi_{\geq\ell}^{2})

\displaystyle=2\ell r^{-2\ell-1}\psi_{\geq\ell}^{2}+2r^{-2\ell}\psi_{\geq\ell}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}

\displaystyle\leq(2\ell+1)r^{-2\ell-1}\psi_{\geq\ell}^{2}+r^{-2\ell+1}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\sim r^{-2\ell-1}r^{2}J^{N}[\psi_{\geq\ell}]\cdot n_{\tau}

to conclude that

r^{-2\ell+\epsilon}\psi_{\geq\ell}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}r^{-2\ell-1+\epsilon}J^{N}[\psi_{\geq\ell}]\cdot n_{\tau}\,d\mu_{\Sigma_{\tau}}.

(1.12)

Hence, in order to get the (almost) sharp decay for
r\psi_{\geq\ell}|_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}
it suffices to obtain the sharp decay for
r^{-2\ell+\epsilon}\psi_{\geq\ell}^{2}
and hence of the weighted energy flux with decreasing weights
r^{p}
with
p<0
. For this we present a new hierarchy of elliptic estimates.

1.3.3
A novel hierarchy of elliptic estimates

Let us define the following weighted derivative

\tilde{\partial}_{\rho}=r\partial_{\rho},

where
\partial_{\rho}
is the radial tangential vector field on the asymptotically hyperboloidal hypersurfaces
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
.

The hierarchy of elliptic estimates that we derive schematically take the following form:

\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}\frac{1}{r^{p}}\left[D^{2}\cdot J^{N}[\tilde{\partial}^{m+1}_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}]+J^{N}[\tilde{\partial}^{m}_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}]\right]\cdot n_{\tau}\,d\mu_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}\frac{1}{r^{p-2}}\sum_{s=0}^{m}J^{N}[T\tilde{\partial}^{s}_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}]\cdot n_{\tau}\,d\mu_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}

(1.13)

with

-1<p<2\ell+1,\ \ \ \ \ 0\leq m\leq\ell,

with the degenerate factor
D
given by (
3.1
), and where
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
are asymptotically hyperboloidal hypersurfaces (see Figure
1
).
Dropping the degenerate term, the uncommuted version (
m=0
) reads:

\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}\frac{1}{r^{p}}J^{N}[\psi_{\geq\ell}]\cdot n_{\tau}\,d\mu_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}\frac{1}{r^{p-2}}J^{N}[T\psi_{\geq\ell}]\cdot n_{\tau}\,d\mu_{\mathcal{S}_{\tau}}

(1.14)

with

-1<p<2\ell+1.

The elliptic estimates show that we can add a
T
derivative at the expense of increasing the power of the weight
r
by 2. If
\psi
is supported on angular frequences
\geq\ell
then we can commute
\ell

\tilde{\partial}_{\rho}
and in this way we can then replace
\psi

T\psi

The proof relies on the fact that we can integrate by the equation

\partial_{\rho}((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})+(Dr^{2})^{\prime}\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell}+2\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}=\partial_{\rho}\bar{F}_{T},

where
F_{T}
involves
T
derivatives of
\psi_{\geq\ell}
. We note that this is possible for the proof of our estimates due to the fact that the right hand side of the last equation defines an elliptic operator. This should be compared and contrasted to the Kerr case where something like that is not possible, see section 7 of
[
AAG21
]

1.3.6
Newman–Penrose charges

In the remaining sections of the introduction, we will summarize our method that allows us to go beyond almost-sharp time-decay estimates and obtain the precise late-time asymptotics for angular modes
\psi_{\ell}
. For this reason we will assume that
\psi=\psi_{\ell}
in the remainder of this introduction.

We first need to introduce the Newman–Penrose charges and constants. The Newman–Penrose constants have been presented previously in the context of the Maxwell and the Einstein equations (see
[
NP65

NP68
]
for the case of Maxwell and Einstein equations on Minkowski, and also the recent
[
Ma20
]
for the Maxwell equations on Schwarzschild), but in this paper we derive them rigorously in the context of the wave equation for all angular frequencies on subextremal Reissner–Nordström.

Recall that the modified derivative
\Phi_{(\ell+1)}
was defined in (
1.10
). Then the limiting function on null infinity

I_{\ell}[\psi](\theta,\varphi)\doteq\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\Phi_{(\ell+1)}(u,r,\theta,\varphi),

(1.17)

is independent of
u
. By further decomposing the function

relative to spherical harmonics we obtain

I_{\ell}[\psi](\theta,\varphi)\doteq\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}I_{m,\ell}[\psi]Y_{m,\ell}(\theta,\varphi),

(1.18)

and define the constants
I_{m,\ell}
to be the Newman–Penrose constants of
\psi
.

1.3.8
Improved decay of
\partial_{\rho}^{\ell+1}\psi
and global asymptotics

One way to propagate the asymptotics for a quantity, say
Q
, from
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma_{\alpha}}
to the rest of the black hole exterior region, all the way up to the event horizon, is to derive faster decay for the radial derivative
\partial_{\rho}Q
and the use the fundamental theorem of calculus for

. However, as is evident from (

), the (sharp) decay rate for all the radial derivatives up to the
\ell^{\text{th}}
-order is the same. For this reason we turn to the radial derivative
\partial_{\rho}^{\ell+1}\psi
.

Using the wave equation we have for all
k\geq 0
that:

\partial^{k+1}_{\rho}\psi+\frac{1}{r}2k\cdot\partial^{k}_{\rho}\psi=\frac{1}{r^{2}}[-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}-k(k+1)]\partial^{k-1}_{\rho}\psi+...

(1.20)

The omitted terms are
\partial_{\rho}
derivatives of
T\psi
, they decay sufficiently fast and can be thought of as lower-order terms.

If we consider
\psi=\psi_{\ell}
we have that coefficient of the term
\partial^{k-1}_{\rho}\psi
does not vanish for
k\leq l
, and so no result can be obtained for
\partial^{k+1}_{\rho}\psi
since it is non-trivially coupled with the lower order derivatives. On the other hand when
k=\ell+1
, the coefficient of
\partial^{k-1}_{\rho}\psi=\partial^{\ell}_{\rho}\psi

vanishes
, and this yields

\partial^{\ell+2}_{\rho}\psi+\frac{1}{r}2(\ell+1)\cdot\partial^{\ell+1}_{\rho}\psi=...

(1.21)

Multiplying the above equation with
r^{2(\ell+1)}
yields

r^{2(\ell+1)}\partial^{\ell+2}_{\rho}\psi+2(\ell+1)\cdot r^{2\ell+1}\partial^{\ell+1}_{\rho}\psi=\partial_{\rho}\left(r^{2(\ell+1)}\cdot\partial^{\ell+1}_{\rho}\psi\right)=...

(1.22)

Integrating in
r
the above yields decay for
\partial^{\ell+1}_{\rho}\psi

faster
than
\tau^{-2\ell-3}
which can then be used to propagate the precise asymptotics of
\partial^{\ell}_{\rho}\psi
everywhere in the exterior region. We can then inductively propagate the asymptotics of
\partial^{k}_{\rho}\psi
everywhere from
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
in the black hole exterior region for all
k=\ell-1,\ell-2,...,2,1,0
.

Note that in the previous computation this is the only part of our proof where we use the precise form of the Reissner–Nordström metric, and more specifically we use that
(Dr^{2})^{\prime\prime}=2
.

1.3.9
A general construction of the time integral

If we assume that for an angular mode
\psi=\psi_{\ell}
the Newman–Penrose charge
I_{\ell}[\psi]
vanishes then we can construct the time integral of
\psi
, namely a suitably regular solution
\tilde{\psi}
to the wave equation such that
T\tilde{\psi}=\psi
. We define the time-inverted Newman–Penrose charge of
\psi
to be

I_{\ell}^{(1)}[\psi]=I_{\ell}[\tilde{\psi}].

We note that the construction of
\tilde{\psi}
requires repeated integration in
r
(the number of integrations depending on
\ell
), however one can still obtain explicit, yet complicated, formulas for
I_{\ell}^{(1)}[\psi]
in terms of the initial data of
\psi
.

We also note that in our case we can still construct the time-inverse by solving an ODE, similarly to the spherically symmetric case, and in contrast to the Kerr case where such an approach is not possible and where this step involves the inversion of an operator that is elliptic outside the ergoregion, see section 9 of
[
AAG21
]
for details.

1.3.10
Asymptotics for
I_{\ell}=0

We now have all the tools needed to prove the precise asymptotics (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) in the case where
I_{\ell}[\psi]=0
(again for an angular mode
\psi=\psi_{\ell}
). First we construct the time integral
\tilde{\psi}
of
\psi
and show that generically its Newman–Penrose charge is non-vanishing. We can then apply the methods of Sections
1.3.8
and

1.3.7
to obtain the asymptotics for
\tilde{\psi}
. Finally, commuting the derived estimates with
T
yields the asymptotics for
\psi
.

Moreover, we note that if the time-inverted
\ell
-th Newman–Penrose charge happens to vanish as well, we can repeat the process, and prove asymptotics that decay one power faster. Using our results, one can also try to recover expressions similar to the ones given for
I_{0}^{(1)}
in
[
AAG18a
]
, for compactly supproted data. In this case, for time-symmetric data (i.e.
T\psi|_{\Sigma_{0}}=0
) that also vanish at the event horizon, the time-inverted
\ell
-th Newman–Penrose charge will vanish as well, hence in that situation we can show the polynomial late-time law of
\tau^{-2\ell-4}
(this should be contrasted with the suboptimal rates obtained for such data in
[
DSS12
]
and
[
DSS11
]
).

As a final remark, let us also note that one can use the results of the present paper and the methods developed in
[
AAG19
]
to obtain second order asymptotics.

Preliminaries

3.1
The subextremal Reissner–Nordström spacetimes

The Reissner–Nordström spacetimes are the unique spherically symmetric and asymptotically flat 2-parameter family of solutions of the Einstein–Maxwell equations, the two parameters being the mass
M>0
and the electromagnetic charge
e
. We consider a subextremal Reissner–Nordström black hole spacetime
(\mathcal{M},g)
where
g
in ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates
(v,r,\theta,\varphi)
has the form:

g=-Ddv^{2}+2dvdr+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}),

with

D=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{e^{2}}{r^{2}}

(3.1)

where
0\leq|e|<M
.

Note that
D
has two roots
0<r_{-}<r_{+}
. We will work work in the domain of outer communications
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{M}
up to and including the future event horizon, that is we will always have
r\geq r_{+}
, the future event horizon being the hypersurface
\mathcal{H}^{+}:=\{(v,r,\theta,\varphi)|r=r_{+}\}
.

In Bondi coordinates
(u,r,\theta,\varphi)
(that are valid outside the event horizon)
g
has the form:

g=-Ddu^{2}-2dudr+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}).

In
\mathcal{D}
we have that
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\in[r_{+},\infty)
,
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and
\varphi\in(0,2\pi)
. By using the tortoise coordinate
r^{*}(r)=r+\frac{1}{2\kappa_{+}}\ln\left|\frac{r-r_{+}}{r_{+}}\right|+\frac{1}{2\kappa_{-}}\ln\left|\frac{r-r_{-}}{r_{-}}\right|+C
, for a constant
C
(that we will fix shortly), for
\kappa_{\pm}=\left.\frac{1}{2}\frac{dD}{dr}\right|_{r=r_{\pm}}
, we define
v=u+2r^{*}
and in double coordinates
(u,v,\theta,\varphi)
the metric
g
takes the form

g=-Ddudv+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}),

and in these coordinates we can define
future null infinity

\mathcal{I}^{+}
which is the limiting null hypersurface foliated by 2-spheres where the null hypersurfaces
\{u=u^{\prime}\}
terminate as
v\rightarrow\infty
(i.e. it is the limiting hypersurface that is formed by the limit points as
r\rightarrow\infty
of future null geodesics). We additionally note that on
r_{ph}=\frac{3M}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{8e^{2}}{9M^{2}}}\right)
(where we choose
C
such that
r^{*}(r_{ph})=0
) we have the
photon sphere
which imposes a derivative loss in the Morawetz estimates that we present in the next section.

The vector field
T=\partial_{u}
(in
(u,r,\theta,\varphi)
) is Killing. By
\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
we denote the covariant derivative on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with respect to its standard metric, and by
\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
the Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Moreover by
\Omega_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
we denote the three Killing vector fields associated to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
which can be expressed as:

\Omega_{1}:=\sin\varphi\partial_{\theta}+\cot\theta\cos\varphi\partial_{\varphi},

\Omega_{2}:=-\cos\varphi\partial_{\theta}+\cot\theta\sin\varphi\partial_{\varphi},

\Omega_{3}:=\partial_{\varphi},

and using the above we can also define the
\Omega^{\alpha}
vector fields by
\Omega^{\alpha}:=\Omega_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\Omega_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\Omega_{3}^{\alpha_{3}}
where
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
and
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\alpha_{i}
. We also consider the vector field

\partial_{\rho}:=\partial_{r}+h(r)T,

for a smooth function
h:[r_{+},\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{1}{\max_{r\in[r_{+},R]}D(r)}\leq h(r)<\frac{2}{D(r)}\mbox{ for $r\leq R$, }

0<\frac{2}{D(r)}-h(r)=O(r^{-1-\eta})\mbox{ for $r>R$, for some $\eta>0$, for some $R>r_{+}$}.

We will consider two foliations of
\mathcal{D}
. The first foliation consists of spacelike-null hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{\tau}
that are constructed as follows: consider a spacelike and asymptotically flat hypersurface
\mathbf{\Sigma}
that intersects
\mathcal{H}^{+}
on a 2-sphere, a null hypersurface
\mathcal{N}_{\tau}:=\{(u=\tau,r,\theta,\varphi)|r\geq R
for some
R>r_{+}
, take some
u_{0}
such that
(\mathbf{\Sigma}\cap\{r=R\})=(\{u=u_{0}\}\cap\{r=R\})
, and consider

\Sigma_{u_{0}}:=(\mathbf{\Sigma}\cap\{r\leq R\})\cup\mathcal{N}_{u_{0}}.

Then define
\Sigma_{\tau}:=f_{\tau}(\Sigma_{u_{0}})
for
f
the flow of
T
. The second foliation consists of hyperboloidal hypersurfaces
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}
, where

\mathcal{S}:=\{(v,r,\theta,\varphi)|v=v_{0}-\int_{r}^{R}h(r^{\prime})\,dr^{\prime},r\geq r_{+}\},

and
v_{0}
is large enough and depends on
h
and
R
, and now we can define
\mathcal{S}_{\tau}:=f_{\tau}(\mathcal{S})
. Finally in the region
\{r\geq R\}
we define also
\mathcal{I}_{\tau}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}):=\{v=\tau|u_{0}\leq\tau_{1}\leq u\leq\tau_{2}\}\cap\{r\geq R\}
in double null coordinates.

Let us introduce the projection operators
P_{\geq\ell},P_{\ell}:L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
by noticing that any smooth function

f=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}P_{\ell}f,

We also define

P_{\geq\ell}f=\sum_{l\geq\ell}P_{l}f.

Finally by

We note that the linear wave equation

\Box_{g}\psi=0,

is globally well-posed in
\mathcal{D}

Note that in the rest of the paper we will always assume that we work with a smooth solution

In particular we will consider smooth data with respect to the conformal compactification of

\sup_{r}\left.\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{j}(r^{2}\partial_{r})^{k}T^{m}(r\psi)|^{2}\,d\omega\right)\right|_{\Sigma_{u_{0}}}(r)<\infty,

7
Novel hierarchies of elliptic estimates

In this section we will present bound
\partial_{r}
derivatives of a linear wave supported on angular frequencies
\geq\ell
for some
\ell\geq 1
, by
T
derivatives, via
r^{-k}
-weighted energy estimates, where the range of
k
depends on
\ell
. These estimates will allow us to obtain
almost sharp upper bounds on fixed
r
hypersurfaces
(something that will be done in the following sections).

Our estimates have the form of a weighted hierarchy, where the weights depend on the lowest angular frequencies on which our linear wave is supported. Note that here we make the choice
\frac{2}{D}-h=\frac{c}{r^{1+\eta}}+O(r^{-2})
for some
c\neq 0
and
\eta>0
(which implies that we work on the
\mathcal{S}
hypersurfaces as noted before), for
\partial_{\rho}=\partial_{r}+hT
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq 1}\lim_{\rho\to\infty}rT^{k}\psi<

\displaystyle\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\infty}r^{2}\partial_{r}\psi=

\displaystyle 0.

Let
\psi
be a solution to (
1.1
) and let
\ell\geq 1
. Then we can estimate

\begin{split}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}))^{2}+Dr^{-k}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}+r^{-2-k}(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho\\
\leq&\>C(D,\ell,k)\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[r^{2-k}(\partial_{\rho}T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k-2\eta}(T^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k}(T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho,\end{split}

(7.1)

for all

-3<k<2\ell-1,

and any
\eta>0
.

Proof.

Recall that in
(v,r,\theta,\varphi)
ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates, and with
\partial_{\rho}=\partial_{r}+hT
, we have that the wave equation (
1.1
) becomes:

\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)+\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi=r^{2}F_{T},

(7.2)

where

F_{T}:=(2+O(r^{-1-\eta}))\partial_{\rho}T\psi+O(r^{-1-\eta})T^{2}\psi+(2r^{-1}+O(r^{-2-\eta}))T\psi.

Therefore, by squaring and integrating both sides of (
7.2
) and multiplying by
r^{-2-k}
, we arrive at

\begin{split}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}&r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi))^{2}r^{2}+r^{-2-k}(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi)^{2}+2r^{-2-k}\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\,d\omega d\rho\\
\leq&\>C\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{2-k}F_{T}^{2}\,d\omega d\rho.\end{split}

(7.3)

We first consider the mixed derivative term on the left-hand side of (
7.3
). We integrate over
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and integrate by parts in
\rho
and the angular variables.

\begin{split}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}2r^{-2-k}\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\,d\omega d\rho=&\>\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}2(2+k)r^{-1-k}D\partial_{\rho}\psi\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\\
&-2Dr^{-k}\partial_{\rho}\psi\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\psi\,d\omega d\rho\\
=&\>\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}2(2+k)r^{-1-k}D\partial_{\rho}\psi\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\\
&+2Dr^{-k}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\psi|^{2}\,d\omega d\rho,\end{split}

(7.4)

where we used that all resulting boundary terms vanish by
D(r_{+})=0
and the asymptotics on
\psi
as
\rho\to\infty
in the assumptions.

We apply (
7.2
) again to estimate

\begin{split}2(2+k)r^{-1-k}D\partial_{\rho}\psi\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi=&-2(2+k)r^{-1-k}D\partial_{\rho}\psi\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)\\
&+2(2+k)r^{-1-k}D\partial_{\rho}\psi\cdot r^{2}F_{T}\\
=&-(2+k)r^{-3-k}\partial_{\rho}((Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2})+2(2+k)r^{-1-k}D\partial_{\rho}\psi\cdot r^{2}F_{T}\\
\geq&-(2+k)r^{-3-k}\partial_{\rho}((Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2})-\epsilon_{1}(2+k)r^{-k}D^{2}(\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2}\\
&-(2+k)\epsilon_{1}^{-1}r^{-2-k}r^{4}F_{T}^{2},\end{split}

where we will take
\epsilon_{1}>0
sufficiently small later.

We can further integrate by parts to estimate

-\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}(2+k)r^{-3-k}\partial_{\rho}((Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2})\,d\rho=-\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}(2+k)(3+k)r^{-4-k}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2}\,d\rho,

where we used that the boundary term vanishes at
r=r_{+}
and moreover
\lim_{\rho\to\infty}r^{1-k}D(\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2}=0
if
k>-3
.
We furthermore apply (
7.2
) once more estimate to express the
(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi)^{2}
term on the left-hand side of (
7.3
) in terms of
(\partial_{\rho}((Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi))^{2}
:

r^{-2-k}(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi)^{2}\geq(1-\epsilon_{2})r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}((Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi))^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{2}}\right)r^{-k}r^{2}F_{T}^{2},

where we will take
\epsilon_{2}>0
suitably small later.

Putting all the above estimates together and filling them into the inequality (
7.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(2-\epsilon_{2})r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{r}\psi))^{2}+2Dr^{-k}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}\\
&-(2+k)(3+k+\epsilon_{1})r^{-4-k}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2}\,d{\omega}d\rho\\
\leq&\>C\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{2-k}F_{T}^{2}\,d{\omega}d\rho\end{split}

(7.5)

We apply a Hardy inequality to absorb part of the third term on the left-hand side into the first term

\begin{split}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}&\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon_{3})(3+k+\epsilon_{1})^{2}r^{-4-k}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2}\,d\rho\\
\leq&\>2(1-\epsilon_{3})\left(\frac{3+k+\epsilon_{1}}{3+k}\right)^{2}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi))^{2}\,d\rho,\end{split}

where we used that
\lim_{\rho\to\infty}r^{-3-k}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2}=0
if
k>-3
by assumption, and we take
\epsilon_{i}>0
suitably small.

We are left with absorbing the integral of the term:

\left[\left(2+k-\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon_{3})(3+k+\epsilon_{1})\right)(3+k+\epsilon_{1})\right]r^{-4-k}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi)^{2}

into the left-hand side of (
7.5
). At this point we localize in angular frequencies
\geq\ell
and we therefore need

\begin{split}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[\left(2+k-\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon_{3})(3+k+\epsilon_{1})\right)(3+k+\epsilon_{1})\right]r^{-k}D^{2}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\,d\omega d\rho\\
\leq&\>2\ell(\ell+1)\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-k}D^{2}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\,d\omega d\rho\\
\leq&\>2\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-k}D^{2}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}\,d\omega d\rho,\end{split}

where we applied a Poincaré inequality to
\psi_{\geq\ell}
in the final inequality.

We can absorb the very right-hand side of the above equation into the
|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}
term on the left-hand side of (
7.5
), provided used that
|D|\leq 1
and
k
satisfies the inequality:

(4+2k-(1-\epsilon_{3})(3+k+\epsilon_{1}))(3+k+\epsilon_{1})\leq 4\ell(\ell+1),

which we can rewrite as

(1+k+\epsilon_{3}k+3\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{3})(3+k+\epsilon_{1})\leq 4\ell(\ell+1).

(7.6)

Equivalently, whenever

(1+k)(3+k)<4\ell(\ell+1).

(7.7)

we can find
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{3}
suitably small such that (
7.6
) is satisfied. The inequality (
7.7
) holds when
-3-2\ell<k<2\ell-1
. Then we use the above estimates to obtain:

\begin{split}\int_{r_{\rm min}}^{\infty}&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi))^{2}+Dr^{-k}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\psi|^{2}\,d\rho\\
\leq&\>C(\ell,k)\int_{r_{\rm min}}^{\infty}r^{2-k}F_{T}^{2}\,d{\omega}d\rho.\end{split}

(7.8)

Hence, by applying Young’s inequality to the terms in
F_{T}^{2}
, we are left with:

\begin{split}\int_{r_{\rm min}}^{\infty}&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}))^{2}+Dr^{-k}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}+r^{-2-k}(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\,d{\omega}d\rho\\
\leq&\>C_{k,\ell}\int_{r_{\rm min}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{2-k}(\partial_{\rho}T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k}(T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k-2\eta}(T^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\,d{\omega}d\rho.\end{split}

∎

We demonstrate now a localized version of the previous result, which can be seen as a “spacelike” redshift estimate.

Proposition 7.2
.

Assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq 1}\lim_{\rho\to\infty}rT^{k}\psi<

\displaystyle\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\infty}r^{2}\partial_{r}\psi=

\displaystyle 0.

Let
\psi
be a solution to (
1.1
) and let
\ell\geq 1
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\,d\omega d\rho+

\displaystyle\left.\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}\,d\omega\right|_{\rho=r_{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[r^{2-k}(\partial_{\rho}T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k-2\eta}(T^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k}(T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho,

for any
-3<k<2\ell-1
, any
\eta>0
, and where
C=C(D,\ell,k)
.

Proof.

We multiply equation (
7.2
) with
\partial_{\rho}\psi
and after integrating it in
\{r_{+}\leq r\leq R\}
we get that:

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{R}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})\left(\partial_{\rho}(Dr^{2}\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})\right)+(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{\geq\ell})\right]d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(Dr^{2})^{\prime}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\,d\omega d\rho+\frac{1}{2}\left.\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(D(R)R^{2})(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\,d\omega\right|_{\rho=R}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left.\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}\,d\omega\right|_{\rho=r_{+}}-\frac{1}{2}\left.\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}\,d\omega\right|_{\rho=R},

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{R}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(Dr^{2})^{\prime}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\,d\omega d\rho+\frac{1}{2}\left.\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(D(R)R^{2})(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right|_{\rho=R}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left.\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}\right|_{\rho=r_{+}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{r_{+}}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|F_{T}|^{2}+\epsilon\int_{r_{+}}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left.\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{\geq\ell}|^{2}\right|_{\rho=R},

and by absorbing the second term of the right hand side in the left hand side, and by using estimate (
7.1
) of Theorem
7.1
for the last term (which we can use for any
-3<k<2\ell-1
as we work in a compact region) after applying an averaging argument and Poincaré’s inequality (
A.5
), we get the desired result.
∎

A higher order version of the previous proposition is the following.

Corollary 7.3
.

Assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq 1}\lim_{\rho\to\infty}rT^{k}\psi<

\displaystyle\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\infty}r^{2}\partial_{r}\psi=

\displaystyle 0.

Let
\psi
be a solution to (
1.1
) and let
\ell\geq 1
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{R}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Big[(\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})|^{2}\Big]\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{r_{+}}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\partial_{\rho}F_{T}|^{2}\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle+C\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[r^{2-k}(\partial_{\rho}T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k-2\eta}(T^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k}(T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho,

for any
-3<k<2\ell-1
, any
\eta>0
, and where
C=C(D,\ell,k)
.

Proof.

We consider the equation

\partial_{\rho}((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})+(Dr^{2})^{\prime}\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell}+2\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell}=\partial_{\rho}\bar{F}_{T},

(7.9)

where

\bar{F}_{T}=(2hDr^{2}-2r^{2})\partial_{\rho}T\psi_{\geq\ell}+(2hr^{2}-h^{2}Dr^{2}+2h^{2}Dr^{2}-2hr^{2})T^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell}+[(hDr^{2})^{\prime}-2hDr+2hDr-2r]T\psi_{\geq\ell}.

We multiply equation (
7.9
) by
r\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell}
and we integrate it over
\{r_{+}\leq r\leq R\}
and we have that

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{R}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Big[2r(Dr^{2})^{\prime}(\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}-(Dr^{2})(\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\Big]\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle+\int_{r_{+}}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})|^{2}-(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\Big]\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|r(\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})(\partial_{\rho}\bar{F}_{T})|\,d\omega d\rho,

and we get the required estimate by applying Cauchy-Schwarz on the term in the right-hand side, absorbing the term involving
\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell}
from the left-hand side (which has a term with
\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell}
and no degeneracy in
D
by the choice of
R
as the second term of the left-hand side above can be absorbed by the first), and by applying Theorem
7.1
to the term with the minus sign of the left-hand side.
∎

Note that by the Corollary above and Theorem
7.1
we also get that

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{R}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Big[r^{2-k}(\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}\psi_{\geq\ell})|^{2}\Big]\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{r_{+}}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\partial_{\rho}F_{T}|^{2}\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle+C\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[r^{2-k}(\partial_{\rho}T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k-2\eta}(T^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k}(T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho,

for
-3<k<2\ell-1
, any
\eta>0
, and where

is a constant.

Now we generalize Theorem
7.1
for higher

Theorem 7.4
.

Fix some
\ell\geq 1
. Assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{l\leq m+1}\lim_{\rho\to\infty}rT^{l}\psi<

\displaystyle\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to\infty}r^{2+l}\partial_{r}^{l+1}\psi=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[r^{-2-k}\left(\partial_{\rho}\left((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell}\right)\right)^{2}+r^{-k}(\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle C\sum_{s=1}^{m+1}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[r^{2-k-2(m-s+1)}(\partial_{\rho}^{s}T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k-2\eta-2(m-s+1)}(\partial_{\rho}^{s-1}T^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho

for some

and for any
\eta>0
.

Proof.

We will argue by induction. The base case is
m=0
which was proven through a combination of Theorem
7.1
and Corollary
7.2
. We assume that the desired estimate is true for

, and we also make the following induction hypothesis which due to Corollary
7.3
is true in the base case: we assume that for some small enough

that

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Big[r^{2-k^{\prime}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k^{\prime}}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell})|^{2}\,d\omega d\rho

(7.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{r_{c}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}F_{T}|^{2}d\omega d\rho

(7.11)

\displaystyle+\sum_{s=1}^{m}\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left[r^{2-k^{\prime}-2(s-1)}(\partial_{\rho}^{s}T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k^{\prime}-2\eta-2(s-1)}(\partial_{\rho}^{s-1}T^{2}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+r^{-k^{\prime}-2(s-1)}(\partial_{\rho}^{s-1}T\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho,

(7.12)

for
-3-2(m-1)<k^{\prime}<2\ell-2(m-1)-1
. Now we examine the case

. We have the equation

\partial_{\rho}\left((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell}\right)+m(Dr^{2})^{\prime}\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell}+m(m+1)\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell}+\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell})=\partial_{\rho}^{m}\bar{F}_{T},

(7.13)

with

as given after equation (
7.9
). We rearrange it as

\partial_{\rho}\left((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell}\right)+\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell})=\partial_{\rho}^{m}\bar{F}_{T}-m(Dr^{2})^{\prime}\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell}-m(m+1)\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell},

and after multiplying it by

and integrating in

we have that:

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}\left[\left(\partial_{\rho}\left((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell}\right)\right)^{2}+(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell}))^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle+2\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}\left((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell}\right))\cdot(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell}))\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\bar{F}_{T})^{2}\,d\omega d\rho+2\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}\Big[m^{2}[(Dr^{2})^{\prime}]^{2}(\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+m^{2}(m+1)^{2}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\Big]\,d\omega d\rho.

For the cross term we have that

\displaystyle 2\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

\displaystyle r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}\left((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi\right))\cdot(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi))\Big]\,d\omega d\rho=\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}2(Dr^{2})|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi)|^{2}\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle+\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}\Big[2(2+k)D^{\prime}r-2(2+k)(1+k)D\Big]|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi)|^{2}\,d\omega d\rho.

Using the last two computations we now have that

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}\left[\left(\partial_{\rho}\left((Dr^{2})\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell}\right)\right)^{2}+Dr^{-k}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell})|^{2}+(\not{\Delta}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell}))^{2}\right]\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\bar{F}_{T})^{2}\,d\omega d\rho+2\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}\Big[m^{2}[(Dr^{2})^{\prime}]^{2}(\partial_{\rho}^{m+1}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}+m^{2}(m+1)^{2}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell})^{2}\Big]\,d\omega d\rho

\displaystyle+\int_{r_{+}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{-2-k}|\not{\nabla}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\partial_{\rho}^{m}\psi_{\geq\ell})|^{2}\,d\omega d\rho.

For the last three terms of the right-hand side we now use the induction hypothesis, and specifically estimate (
7.10
). Note that we can do this because now as

we have that
-3-2(m-1)<k+2<2\ell-2(m-1)-1

∎

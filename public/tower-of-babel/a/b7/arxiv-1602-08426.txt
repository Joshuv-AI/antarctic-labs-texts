A Union of Euclidean Metric Spaces is Euclidean
Yury Makarychev

Abstract
Suppose that a metric space X X is the union of two metric subspaces A A and B B that embed into Euclidean space with distortions D A D_{A} and D B D_{B} , respectively. We prove that then X X embeds into Euclidean space with a bounded distortion (namely, with distortion at most 7 ​ D A ​ D B + 2 ​ ( D A + D B ) 7D_{A}D_{B}+2(D_{A}+D_{B}) ). Our result settles an open problem posed by Naor. Additionally, we present some corollaries and extensions of this result. In particular, we introduce and study a new concept of an “external bi-Lipschitz extension”. In the end of the paper, we list a few related open problems.

1.1
Preliminaries

We denote the
k
dimensional Euclidean space by
\ell_{2}^{k}
and the (separable) Hilbert space by
\ell_{2}^{\infty}
.
The Lipschitz constant of a map
f
from a metric space
(X,d_{X})
to a metric space
(Y,d_{Y})
is

\|f\|_{Lip}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in X\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{X}(x,y)}
. The distortion
of an embedding
f:X\hookrightarrow Y
is
\|f\|_{Lip}\|f^{-1}\|_{Lip}
(where
f^{-1}
is the inverse map from

f(X)\subset Y
to
X
).

For a metric space
X
and a Banach space
V
, let the Lipschitz extension constant
e_{k}(X,V)
be the minimal constant
C
such that the following holds:
for every subset
Y
of
X
of size at most
k
and every map
f:Y\to V
there exists an extension
\hat{f}:X\to V
such that

\|\hat{f}\|_{Lip}\leq C\|f\|_{Lip}
.
In this paper, we use the Kirszbraun theorem that states that every map
f
from a subset
Y
of
\ell_{2}^{a}
to
\ell_{2}^{b}
can be extended
to a map
\tilde{f}:\ell_{2}^{a}\to\ell_{2}^{b}
so that
\|\tilde{f}\|_{Lip}=\|f\|_{Lip}
; in particular,
e_{k}(\ell_{2}^{a},\ell_{2}^{b})=1
for every
k

[
5
]
(see also
[
10
]
).

1.2
Our Results

We prove the following theorem that answers Question

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider a metric spaces
(X,d)

is the union of two metric subspaces

\ell_{2}^{a}

7D_{A}D_{B}+2(D_{A}+D_{B}).

If

, then

embeds into
\ell_{2}^{a+b+1}
with distortion at most
8.93
.

In this theorem,

and

may be finite or infinite.

As we show in Lemma
2.9
in Section
2.1
, it is sufficient to prove Theorem
1.1
only for finite metric spaces —
the result for arbitrary metric spaces follows from the result for finite metric spaces.
So we assume below that

Let

such that every metric space

embeds into

with distortion at most

It is interesting to understand the dependence of

and

The result of Theorem

{\cal D}(D_{A},D_{B})\leq 7D_{A}D_{B}+2(D_{A}+D_{B})\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal D}(1,1)<8.93\quad.

There is a trivial lower bound on

{\cal D}(D_{A},D_{B})\geq\max(D_{A},D_{B})
. We also prove that

.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every

, there exists a finite metric space
(X,d)
, which is
the union of two metric subspaces

and

•

We present some corollaries and extensions to Theorem

and

We construct a map

as in Lemma

\psi_{B}=\psi
. Similarly (switching

with

in the statement of Lemma

Let
\beta=(1+\alpha)(2D_{A}D_{B}+1)
and

. Define a map

by
\psi_{\Delta}(a)=\gamma R_{a}
for

\psi_{\Delta}(b)=-\gamma R_{b}
for
b\in B
. Finally, define an embedding

First, we show that
\Psi
is a non-contracting map. For

\|\Psi(a_{1})-\Psi(a_{2})\|\geq\|\psi_{A}(a_{1})-\psi_{A}(a_{2})\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny by (\ref{eq:psi-b-b})}}}{{\geq}}d(a_{1},a_{2}).

Similarly, for

.
For

By Lemma

\displaystyle\|\psi_{A}(a)-\psi_{A}(b)\|

\displaystyle\geq d(a,b)-\beta R_{b},

\displaystyle\|\psi_{B}(a)-\psi_{B}(b)\|

\displaystyle\geq d(a,b)-\beta R_{a},

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\Delta}(a)-\psi_{\Delta}(b)\|

\displaystyle=\gamma(R_{a}+R_{b}).

By a simple case analysis, we show that these bounds imply that

Claim 2.8
.

\|\psi(a)-\psi(b)\|\geq d(a,b).

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
R_{a}\leq R_{b}
. Consider three cases. Assume first that
\beta R_{b}\leq d(a,b)
. Then

\displaystyle\|\psi(a)-\psi(b)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(d(a,b)-\beta R_{a})^{2}+(d(a,b)-\beta R_{b})^{2}+\beta^{2}(R_{a}+R_{b})^{2}/2

\displaystyle=d(a,b)^{2}+(d(a,b)-\beta R_{a}-\beta R_{b})^{2}+\beta^{2}(R_{a}-R_{b})^{2}/2\geq d(a,b)^{2}.

Assume now that
\beta R_{a}\leq d(a,b)\leq\beta R_{b}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\psi(a)-\psi(b)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(d(a,b)-\beta R_{a})^{2}+\beta^{2}(R_{a}+R_{b})^{2}/2

\displaystyle=d(a,b)^{2}-2\beta d(a,b)R_{a}+\beta^{2}(3R_{a}^{2}+2R_{a}R_{b}+R_{b}^{2})/2

\displaystyle\geq d(a,b)^{2}-2\beta^{2}R_{a}R_{b}+\beta^{2}(3R_{a}^{2}+2R_{a}R_{b}+R_{b}^{2})/2

\displaystyle=d(a,b)^{2}+\beta^{2}((\sqrt{3}R_{a}-R_{b})^{2}+2(\sqrt{3}-1)R_{a}R_{b})/2\geq d(a,b)^{2}.

Finally, assume that

. Then

\|\psi(a)-\psi(b)\|^{2}\geq\beta^{2}(R_{a}+R_{b})^{2}/2\geq 2\beta^{2}R_{a}^{2}\geq 2d(a,b)^{2}.

∎

We conclude that
\Psi
is a non-contracting map.
We now upper bound the Lipschitz constant of

For

\displaystyle\|\Psi(a_{1})-\Psi(a_{2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\psi_{A}(a_{1})-\psi_{A}(a_{2})\|^{2}+\|\psi_{B}(a_{1})-\psi_{B}(a_{2})\|^{2}+\|\psi_{\Delta}(a_{1})-\psi_{\Delta}(a_{2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(D_{A}^{2}+4(1+1/\alpha)^{2}D_{A}^{2}D_{B}^{2})d(a_{1},a_{2})^{2}+\gamma^{2}(R_{a_{1}}-R_{a_{2}})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(D_{A}^{2}+4(1+1/\alpha)^{2}D_{A}^{2}D_{B}^{2}+\gamma^{2})d(a_{1},a_{2})^{2},

here, we used that

Similarly, for

a\in A
and

\displaystyle\leq(\xi_{A}^{2}+\xi_{B}^{2})d(a,b)^{2}+\gamma^{2}(R_{a}+R_{b})^{2}\leq(\xi_{A}^{2}+\xi_{B}^{2}+4\gamma^{2}\bigr)d(a,b)^{2},

here, we used that

We first derive an upper bound for the Lipschitz constant of

Compactness argument

In this section, we prove that it is sufficient to prove Theorem

only for finite metric spaces.

Lemma 2.9
.

I. Let
(X,d)
be a separable metric space. Assume that every finite subset of

embeds into
V=\ell_{2}^{m}
with distortion at most
D
(where

Then
(X,d)
embeds into
\ell_{2}^{m}
with distortion at most
D
.

II. Furthermore, assume that
X=A\cup B
. Let
\alpha:A\times A\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
\beta:B\times B\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous functions.
Assume that for every finite subset

of
X
there is an non-contracting embedding
f:Y\hookrightarrow V
with
\|f\|_{Lip}\leq D
such that

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\alpha(x,y)
for every
x,y\in A\cap Y
and
\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\beta(x,y)
for every
x,y\in B\cap Y
.
Then there exists an non-contracting embedding
f:X\hookrightarrow V
with
\|f\|_{Lip}\leq D
such that

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\alpha(x,y)
for every
x,y\in A
and
\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\beta(x,y)
for every
x,y\in B
.

Proof.

I. Let
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots
be a dense sequence in
X
. Consider an orthonormal basis

e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
in
\ell_{2}^{m}
, if
m<\infty
, or
e_{1},\dots,e_{k},\dots
in
\ell_{2}^{\infty}
,
if

. Let
V_{k}
be the linear span of vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
if

and

if

. In particular, let

.

By the condition of the lemma, for every
k
, there exists an embedding
f_{k}
of

to

with distortion at most

.
We assume without loss of generality that

d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq\|f_{k}(x_{i})-f_{k}(x_{j})\|\leq Dd(x_{i},x_{j}).

Further, we assume that
f_{k}(x_{0})=0
. Applying the Gram–Schmidt process to

f_{k}(x_{0}),\dots,f_{k}(x_{k})
,
we get an isometry

such that

. Note that

•

f_{k}^{\prime}(x_{i})=0\in B_{i}

\|f_{k}^{\prime}(x_{i})\|=\|f_{k}^{\prime}(x_{i})-f_{k}^{\prime}(x_{0})\|\leq Dd(x_{i},x_{0})

Therefore, the sequence

Since for every

II. We proceed as in item I, except that we choose sequence

3
Lower bound on distortion

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
Before we proceed to the proof, recall the definition and some properties of graph Laplacians.
Consider a graph
G=(V,E)
. The Laplacian
{\cal{L}}
of
G
is a matrix with entries

{\cal{L}}_{uv}=\begin{cases}\deg u,&\text{ if }u=v,\\
-1,&\text{ if }(u,v)\in E,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We write
X\succeq Y
for two symmetric
n\times n
matrices
X
and
Y
, if
X-Y
is positive semidefinite.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G=(A\cup B,E)
be a complete bipartite graph with parts
A
and
B
of size
n
each.
There exists a partition of the set of edges
E
into two disjoint sets
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
such that

\frac{1}{(1+\delta)}{\cal{L}}_{1}\preceq\frac{1}{2}{\cal{L}}\preceq(1+\delta){\cal{L}}_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{(1+\delta)}{\cal{L}}_{2}\preceq\frac{1}{2}{\cal{L}}\preceq(1+\delta){\cal{L}}_{2},

where
\delta=c/\sqrt{n}
(for some absolute constant
c
), and
{\cal{L}}
,
{\cal{L}}_{1}
and
{\cal{L}}_{2}
are the Laplacians of
G
,
G_{1}=(A\cup B,E_{1})
and
G_{2}=(A\cup B,E_{2})
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
E_{1}
be a random subset of
E
chosen uniformly among all subsets of
E
. Let
E_{2}=E\setminus E_{1}
. Then
G_{1}
is a random bipartite
G(n,n,1/2)
graph.
Thus
(1+\delta)^{-1}{\cal{L}}_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}{\cal{L}}\leq(1+\delta){\cal{L}}_{1}
with probability at least
2/3
(see
[
4
]
).
Similarly,
G_{2}
is a random bipartite
G(n,n,1/2)
graph, and

(1+\delta)^{-1}{\cal{L}}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}{\cal{L}}\leq(1+\delta){\cal{L}}_{2}
with probability at least
2/3

(
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are, of course, not independent).
Therefore, with probability at least
1/3
,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
satisfy the conditions of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G=(A\cup B,E)
,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be as in Lemma
3.1
.
Consider a map
f:A\cup B\to\ell_{2}
. Then for
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}

\frac{1}{(1+\delta)^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}_{(u,v)\in E}\left[\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{(u,v)\in E_{i}}\left[\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}\right]\leq(1+\delta)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{(u,v)\in E}\left[\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}\right]

Proof.

Let
h:A\cup B\to\{0,1\}
be a function equal to
0
on
A
and
1
on
B
.
Then,

|E_{i}|=\langle h,{\cal{L}}_{i}h\rangle\leq(1+\delta)\langle h,{\cal{L}}h\rangle/2=(1+\delta)|E|/2.

Let
f_{j}(u)
be the
j
-th coordinate of
f(u)
in some fixed orthonormal basis. We have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(u,v)\in E_{i}}

\displaystyle\bigl[\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}\bigr]=\frac{1}{|E_{i}|}\sum_{(u,v)\in E_{i}}{\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}}=\frac{1}{|E_{i}|}\sum_{j}\langle f_{j},{\cal{L}}_{i}f_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{(1+\delta)|E|}\cdot\sum_{j}\langle f_{j},\Bigl(\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}{\cal{L}}\Bigr)f_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{|E|}\sum_{j}\langle f_{j},{\cal{L}}f_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta)^{2}}\cdot\frac{\sum_{(u,v)\in E}\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}}{|E|}=\frac{1}{(1+\delta)^{2}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{(u,v)\in E}\left[\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}\right].

The proof of the other part of the inequality is analogous.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume that
\varepsilon<1
. Let
\delta=\varepsilon/6
. Choose
n
so that
c/\sqrt{n}<\delta
.
Let
A
and
B
be two disjoint sets consisting of
n

G=(A\cup B,E)
with parts
A
and
B
.

and
E_{2}
as in Lemma

.
Denote the Laplacians of

{\cal{L}}
,
{\cal{L}}_{1}

Define a metric space on

4.1
Bi-Lipschitz extension

In this section, we prove an “external” bi-Lipschitz extension theorem. Let
A\subset\ell_{2}^{a}
,
B\subset\ell_{2}^{b}
,
and
f
be a Lipschitz map from
A
to
B
. The Kirszbraun theorem states that the map
f
can be extended to a Lipschitz
map
\tilde{f}
from
\ell_{2}^{a}
to
\ell_{2}^{b}
. Is there a counterpart of this theorem for bi-Lipschitz maps?
Note that there may be no bi-Lipschitz extension even for a map from a subset of
\mathbb{R}
to a subset of
\mathbb{R}
.
Consider, for instance, a bi-Lipschitz map
f
from
\left\{0,1,2\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
to
\left\{0,1,2\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
that maps
0
to
0
,
1
to
2
and
2
to
1
.
There is no continuous injective extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}
.

We prove, however, that there is an “external” extension of a bi-Lipschitz map.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
A\subset\ell_{2}^{a}
and
B\subset\ell_{2}^{b}
. Let
f:A\to B
be a bi-Lipschitz map. We say that a pair of maps
f_{1}:\ell_{2}^{a}\to\ell_{2}
and

is an external extension
of

with distortion
D
if

•

f_{1}
and
f_{2}
have distortion at most
D
.

•

for every
a\in A
,
f_{1}(a)=f_{2}(f(a))
.

Theorem 4.2

(External Bi-Lipschitz Extension Theorem)
.

Let
A\subset U=\ell_{2}^{a}
and
B\subset V=\ell_{2}^{b}
. Let
f:A\to B
be a bi-Lipschitz map with distortion
D
.
Then there exists an external extension
(f_{1},f_{2})
of
f
with distortion at most
O(D)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f
is non-contracting and
\|f\|_{Lip}\leq D
. We assume that
U
and
V
are disjoint
(by replacing
V
with an isometric copy of
V
if necessary).
We are now going to take the union of
U
and
V
, identify each point
a\in A
with
f(a)\in B
, and
consider the shortest path metric on the obtained space.
Formally, let
X=(U\cup V)/\left\{(a,b):b=f(a)\right\}
(the union of
U
and
V
with each point
a\in A
identified
with
f(a)\in B
). Let
d
be the shortest path metric on
X
; specifically, define metric

as follows.

•

For
u,v\in U
, let
d(u,v)=\|u-v\|
.

•

For

and

, let
d(u,v)=\inf_{x\in A}(\|u-x\|+\|f(x)-v\|)
.

•

For

\displaystyle d(u,v)

\displaystyle=\min(\|u-v\|,\inf_{w\in A}(d(u,w)+d(w,v)))

\displaystyle=\min(\|u-v\|,\inf_{x,y\in B}(\|u-x\|+\|f^{-1}(x)-f^{-1}(y)\|+\|y-v\|)).

Note that the distance between identified points

and

An analog of Theorem
1.1
for arbitrary normed spaces

Theorem
1.1
applies only to embeddings into Euclidean spaces:
if

and

are

,

and
B
embed into
U
and
V
with distortions
D_{A}
and
D_{B}
, then

X=A\cup B
embeds into
U\oplus V\oplus{\mathbb{R}}
with distortion

.
However, observe that the proof of Theorem
1.1
uses only once that
U
and
V
are Euclidean –
when it extends map
g
to a map
\tilde{g}
using the Kirszbraun theorem.
In this section, we note that it is possible to generalize Theorem
1.1
to arbitrary normed spaces
U
and
V

Since spaces
U
and
V
do not necessarily

X

and
V
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Consider a metric spaces
(X,d)
. Assume that
X
is the union of two finite metric subspaces

and
B
that embed into normed spaces
U
and
V
with distortions

D_{A}
and
D_{B}
, respectively. Denote

).

X

U\oplus V\oplus{\mathbb{R}}
with distortion at most

The proof of the theorem is almost identical to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The only difference is that
in the proof of Lemma
2.6
, we define
\psi(x)
for
x\in A
not as

\tilde{g}(\varphi_{A}(x))

\|\varphi_{B}f\|\leq D_{B}2(1+1/\alpha)
and
\|\psi|_{A}\|_{Lip}\leq 2E_{A}D_{B}(1+1/\alpha)
.

Now we present a corollary of this theorem communicated to us by Naor.

Corollary 4.4

(Naor)
.

Consider a metric space
X=A\cup B
on
n

and
B
into normed space

.

Question 3

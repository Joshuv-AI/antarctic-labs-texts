Nonreciprocal electron hydrodynamics under magnetic fields: applications to nonreciprocal surface magnetoplasmons
Ryotaro Sano, Riki Toshio, Norio Kawakami

Abstract
Recent experiments have elucidated that novel nonequilibrium states inherent in the so-called hydrodynamic regime are realized in ultrapure metals with sufficiently strong momentum-conserving scattering. In this letter, we formulate a theory of electron hydrodynamics with broken inversion symmetry under magnetic fields and find that novel terms emerge in hydrodynamic equations which play a crucial role for the realization of the nonreciprocal responses. Specifically, we clarify that there exist a novel type of nonreciprocal collective modes dubbed nonreciprocal surface magnetoplasmons arising from an interplay between magnetic fields and the orbital magnetic moment. We reveal that these nonreciprocal collective modes indeed give rise to the nonreciprocity in magneto-optical responses such as the reflectivity. The physics discussed here will bridge the two important notions of magnetoplasmonics and nonreciprocity in electron hydrodynamic materials with inversion symmetry breaking and magnetic fields.

In this Letter, we derive the hydrodynamic equations for noncentrosymmetric metals under magnetic fields, which are correct up to the second order in electric fields and the first order in magnetic fields. We elucidate the appearance of a novel anomalous term in the momentum flux, which induces unprecedented nonreciprocal hydrodynamic flows, as a consequence of an interplay between magnetic fields and the orbital magnetic moment of electron wavepackets. From a symmetry viewpoint, we find that this term can appear only in the systems without TRS and inversion symmetry. Furthermore, as a demonstration of our nonreciprocal electron hydrodynamic theory, we provide an analysis of
nonreciprocal surface magnetoplasmons

An estimation of the order of the nonreciprocity is also given with an effective model for strained graphene with a staggered sublattice potential.

Formulation—

We outline how to derive the magnetohydrodynamic equations for noncentrosymmetric metals where TRS is broken by an external magnetic field (For detail, see
Supplemental Materials). We start from the Boltzmann equation which governs the evolution of the electron distribution function
f^{\alpha}
for band
\alpha
,

\partial_{t}f^{\alpha}+\dot{{\bf\it r}}^{\alpha}\dotproduct\gradient_{{\bf\it r}}f^{\alpha}+\dot{{\bf\it p}}^{\alpha}\dotproduct\gradient_{{\bf\it p}}f^{\alpha}=\mathcal{C}[f^{\alpha}],

(1)

where
\mathcal{C}
is the collision integral due to the scattering processes. The semi-classical equations of motion read
Xiao et al. 2010

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}\dot{{\bf\it r}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\tilde{{\bf\it v}}^{\alpha}+\frac{e}{\hbar}{\bf\it E}\times{\bf\it\Omega}^{\alpha}+\frac{e}{\hbar}(\tilde{{\bf\it v}}^{\alpha}\dotproduct{\bf\it\Omega}^{\alpha}){\bf\it B},

(2)

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}\dot{{\bf\it p}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-e{\bf\it E}-e\tilde{{\bf\it v}}^{\alpha}\times{\bf\it B}-\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}({\bf\it E}\dotproduct{\bf\it B}){\bf\it\Omega}^{\alpha},

where the electric field
{\bf\it E}
depends on position and time, while the magnetic field
{\bf\it B}
is assumed to depend only on time for simplicity.
\alpha
and
\tilde{{\bf\it v}}^{\alpha}\equiv\gradient_{{\bf\it p}}\tilde{\epsilon}^{\alpha}
are a band index and the group velocity of Bloch wavepacket,
\tilde{\epsilon}^{\alpha}=\epsilon_{0}^{\alpha}-{\bf\it B}\dotproduct{\bf\it m}^{\alpha}
is defined by the sum of the band energy and the Zeeman energy for the orbital magnetic moment
{\bf\it m}^{\alpha}
of quasiparticles. Note that the orbital magnetic moment can be intuitively interpreted as the self-rotation of the Bloch wavepacket
Xiao et al. 2010
.
{\bf\it\Omega}^{\alpha}
is the Berry curvature of the Bloch electrons in band
\alpha
, defined as
{\bf\it\Omega}^{\alpha}\equiv\gradient_{{\bf\it k}}\times{\bf\it A}^{\alpha}
with
{\bf\it A}^{\alpha}\equiv i\innerproduct{u_{\alpha\vb*{k}}}{\grad_{\vb*{k}}u_{\alpha\vb*{k}}}
. Here, we have defined the lattice-momentum
{\bf\it p}\equiv\hbar{\bf\it k}
.
Due to the lack of inversion symmetry, both
{\bf\it m}^{\alpha}
and
{\bf\it\Omega}^{\alpha}
are allowed to have nonzero values for any
{\bf\it p}
.
In addition,
D^{\alpha}\equiv 1+(e/\hbar){\bf\it B}\dotproduct{\bf\it\Omega}^{\alpha}
is the modified phase-space density of states.

Following the standard approach
Lifshitz and Pitaevskii 1981
;
Lucas and Fong 2018
, the continuity equations for the particle density and the momentum are obtained in the relaxation-time approximation for momentum-relaxing scattering processes as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n_{{\bf\it B}}+\divergence{\bf\it J}^{n}_{{\bf\it B}}=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P_{{\bf\it B},i}+\partial_{j}\Pi_{{\bf\it B},ij}=F_{i}+\Gamma_{i},

(4)

where
n_{{\bf\it B}}
and
{\bf\it P}_{{\bf\it B}}
are the particle density and the momentum of electrons under magnetic fields respectively,
{\bf\it F}
is the driving force due to external fields and
{\bf\it\Gamma}
is the momentum-relaxing force. In Eqs.(
3
) and (
4
),
{\bf\it J}^{n}_{{\bf\it B}}
and
\Pi_{{\bf\it B},ij}
are the fluxes of particle density and the momentum.

In the hydrodynamic regime, the system reaches the local equilibrium via normal electron-electron scatterings, which conserve the total momentum of the electron system. For this reason, we can assume that the distribution functions are described with the Lagrange multiplier
{\bf\it u}
as follows:
f_{\mathrm{local}}^{\alpha}({\bf\it r},{\bf\it p},t)=[e^{\beta(\tilde{\epsilon}^{\alpha}({\bf\it p})-{\bf\it u}\dotproduct{\bf\it p}-\mu)}+1]^{-1}
, which is referred to as the local equilibrium distribution function. Note that the inverse temperature
\beta
, as well as the chemical potential
\mu
and the drift velocity
{\bf\it u}
generally depend on position and time.

Under an assumption that the band energy has a parabolic dispersion
\epsilon^{\alpha}_{0}=p^{2}/2m
for simplicity, we obtain the momentum flux as

\displaystyle\Pi_{{\bf\it B},ij}

\displaystyle=mn_{0}u_{i}u_{j}+p\delta_{ij}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\epsilon_{jkl}E_{k}C_{il}

\displaystyle+\frac{e}{\hbar}B_{k}\left(u_{l}M_{lk}\delta_{ij}+u_{j}M_{ik}\right)+\frac{e}{\hbar}B_{j}u_{l}O_{il},

(5)

where
n_{0}
is the particle density in the absence of magnetic field. The momentum flux contains three novel geometrical tensors:
\hat{C}
,
\hat{M}
and
\hat{O}
. While
\hat{C}
was first derived in Ref.
Toshio et al. 2020
and expresses an electric field induced torque especially for chiral systems
Funaki and Tatara 2021
,
\hat{M}
and
\hat{O}
are new geometrical tensors peculiar to the noncentrosymmetric fluids under magnetic fields, defined as

\displaystyle M_{il}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{m\hbar}{e}\sum_{\alpha}\int[dp]\frac{\partial m_{l}^{\alpha}}{\partial p_{i}}f_{0}(\epsilon_{0}^{\alpha}),

(6)

\displaystyle O_{il}

\displaystyle\equiv-\sum_{\alpha}\int[\differential{\vb*{p}}]p_{i}p_{k}\Omega^{\alpha}_{k}\partial_{p_{l}}f_{0}(\epsilon_{0}^{\alpha}),

(7)

where we have introduced the notation
\int[\differential{\vb*{p}}]\equiv\int\differential{\vb*{p}}/(2\pi\hbar)^{d}
. These geometrical terms play a crucial role for the realization of nonreciprocal hydrodynamic flow, which especially includes the nonreciprocal surface magnetoplasmons, as seen below.
From a symmetry viewpoint, we readily find that these terms, which have the form
\hat{\Pi}^{B}\propto{\bf\it B}{\bf\it u}
, can appear only in the systems without TRS and inversion symmetry.

Combining the particle conservation law Eq.(
3
) and the momentum conservation law Eq.(
4
), we end up with the generalized Euler equation

\displaystyle mn_{0}\partialderivative{u_i}{t}+mn_{0}({\bf\it u}\dotproduct\gradient)u_{i}+\partial_{i}p+en_{0}(E_{i}+\epsilon_{ikl}u_{k}B_{l})

\displaystyle+\frac{e}{\hbar}M_{ij}\partialderivative{B_j}{t}+\frac{e}{\hbar}B_{k}\left[M_{lk}\partial_{i}u_{l}+M_{ik}\partial_{j}u_{j}+O_{il}\partial_{k}u_{l}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{mn_{0}u_{i}}{\tau},

(8)

where the terms with spatial derivatives of
T
and
\mu
are omitted for simplicity. This result indicates that the symmetry lowering of the crystal leads to the emergence of anomalous driving forces proportional to
\hat{M}
or
\hat{O}
in the conventional Euler equation
Landau and Lifshitz 1987
. For example, one of these forces is proportional to
\partial{\bf\it B}/\partial t
, and thus represents the inverse Edelstein effect
Shen et al. 2014
, which is an anomalous electric flow induced by the oscillating magnetic field. On the other hand, the other forces that are proportional to
\partial_{i}u_{j}
are also understood as nonreciprocal forces, which never change their direction under the spatial inversion.
Here, we note that the geometrical tensor
\hat{O}
becomes zero in 2D-layered systems [see Eq.(
7
)] because it contains the inner product of momentum and Berry curvature which are generally perpendicular in 2D-systems. In the following analysis, we focus on 2D electron hydrodynamic materials such as ML-graphene for simplicity.

To relate the hydrodynamic theory with an observable current in optical experiments, we further need to introduce the so-called local current
Xiao et al. 2006
when we consider the magneto-optical responses,

{\bf\it j}=-e\int[\differential{\vb*{p}}]D^{\alpha}\dot{{\bf\it r}}^{\alpha}f^{\alpha}+\curl\int[\differential{\vb*{p}}]{\bf\it m}^{\alpha}f^{\alpha}.

(9)

After straightforward calculations, we obtain the local current in terms of hydrodynamic variables in the same conditions as in Eq.(
8
),

{\bf\it j}=-en_{0}{\bf\it u}-\frac{me^{2}}{\hbar}{\bf\it E}\times({}^{t}\hat{D}{\bf\it u}+{}^{t}\hat{Y}{\bf\it B})+\frac{e}{\hbar}\gradient\times({}^{t}\hat{M}{\bf\it u}),

(10)

where the first term is a familiar part appearing in the conventional hydrodynamics, and the others are anomalous parts reflecting the symmetry lowering of the fluids. The terms proportional to
\hat{D}
and
\hat{Y}
can be understood as hydrodynamic counterparts of two types of the Hall current known in the ohmic or the ballistic regime
Sodemann and Fu 2015
;
Morimoto et al. 2016
.

Nonreciprocal surface plasmons in 2D electron hydrodynamic materials—

Collective behavior is one of the most intriguing aspects of the hydrodynamic approach. In fact, a number of previous works have applied hydrodynamics to analyze collective modes
Gorbar et al. 2018b
;
Pellegrino et al. 2015
;
Narozhny et al. 2021
;
Gorbar et al. 2017
;
Petrov and Svintsov 2019
;
Song and Rudner 2016
;
Kumar et al. 2016
, particularly plasma modes
Narozhny et al. 2021
;
Gorbar et al. 2017
;
Kumar et al. 2016
;
Song and Rudner 2016
;
Petrov and Svintsov 2019
. We emphasize that our theory highlights both the effects of crystal symmetry and the geometrical effect of Bloch wave function, which have been rarely addressed in these previous works. In the study of collective modes, we assume that deviations of the local thermodynamic parameters from their equilibrium values are small, justifying the use of linearized hydrodynamic equations. The components of linear conductivity tensor are obtained by looking for a solution in the form of plane waves, i.e.
{\bf\it u}=\tilde{{\bf\it u}}e^{i{\bf\it q}\dotproduct{\bf\it r}-i\omega t}
together with similar expressions for other oscillating variables.

In the following analysis, we consider surface plasmons in 2D electron hydrodynamic materials such as monolayer graphene
Bandurin et al. 2016
;
Bandurin et al. 2018
;
Crossno et al. 2016
;
Sulpizio et al. 2019
;
Berdyugin et al. 2019
;

The equation which determines the dispersion relation of surface plasmons is given by

;
Kumar et al. 2016
;
Yudin et al. 2015

\left[\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{\kappa_{1}}+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\kappa_{2}}+\frac{i\sigma_{yy}}{\epsilon_{0}\omega}\right]\left[\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}-\frac{i\omega\sigma_{xx}}{\epsilon_{0}c^{2}}\right]=\frac{\sigma_{xy}\sigma_{yx}}{(\epsilon_{0}c)^{2}}.

(11)

Here
\kappa_{i}=\sqrt{q^{2}-\epsilon_{i}(\omega/c)^{2}}\ (i=1,2)
,
\epsilon_{i}
are the relative dielectric constants in the medium above (
i=1
) and below (
i=2
) the graphene,
\epsilon_{0}
and
c
are the dielectric constant and the speed of light in free space respectively, and
\sigma_{ij}
are the components of the conductivity tensor. From this equation, we readily notice that the conductivity tensor
\hat{\sigma}(\omega,{\bf\it q})
must have the odd part with respect to
{\bf\it q}
in order to realize nonreciprocal magnetoplasmon.

According to symmetry consideration

\hat{M}
vanishes in a pseudo-vector constrained in the 2D plane:
M_{ij}=M_{i}\delta_{jz}

{\bf\it M}
along
y

In this condition, substituting the solution of Eq.(
8

10

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}(1-i\omega\tau)(1+\zeta^{2}q_{y}^{2})/Z,

(12)

\displaystyle\sigma_{xy}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}[(1+\zeta^{2}q_{x}^{2})\omega_{c}\tau+i\zeta q_{y}(1-i\zeta q_{x})(1-i\omega\tau)]/Z

\displaystyle\qquad-me^{2}Y_{zz}B_{0}/\hbar,

(13)

\displaystyle\sigma_{yx}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}(1+\zeta^{2}q_{x}^{2})[\omega_{c}\tau+i\zeta q_{y}(1+i\zeta q_{x})(1-i\omega\tau)]/Z

\displaystyle\qquad+me^{2}Y_{zz}B_{0}/\hbar,

(14)

\displaystyle\sigma_{yy}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}(1-i\omega\tau)(1+\zeta^{2}q_{x}^{2})/Z.

Here,
\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}
is the Drude weight and

Z\equiv(1-i\omega\tau)(1-i\omega\tau+2i\omega_{\mathrm{c}}\tau\zeta q_{y})+(\omega_{c}\tau)^{2}(1+\zeta^{2}q_{x}^{2})

with

and the cyclotron frequency

of electrons. We find that the nonreciprocity appears most strongly when

{\bf\it q}

is perpendicular to
{\bf\it q}

Nonreciprocity in reflectance—

Next, in order to reveal the role of nonreciprocal surface magnetoplasmons, we consider the magneto-optical responses in Kretschmann configuration, whose schematic picture is dipicted in Fig.
1
. Importantly, we clarify that these modes cause the nonreciprocity in the magneto-optical reflectance. Notice that the polarization plane of the reflective light rotates through the magneto-optical Kerr effect.

After straightforward calculations, we can obtain the reflectance
R

The reflectance has a minimum where the phase matching condition between the incident light and a surface magnetoplasmon is satisfied: namely the horizontal component of momentum of light matches the real part of momentum of surface magnetoplasmon polaritons. This condition can be written
as

k_{\parallel}=k_{0}n_{1}\sin\theta_{\mathrm{r}}=\Re{k_{\mathrm{smp}}(\omega)}

Model for quantitative estimation—

Finally, let us consider a simple model for strained graphene with a staggered sublattice potential to estimate the observables quantitatively. As is well-known, the Hamiltonian around the
K
and
K^{\prime}
valley points is given by
Sodemann and Fu 2015

H=vp_{x}\sigma_{y}-\alpha vp_{y}\sigma_{x}+\alpha sp_{y}\hat{\bm{1}}_{2}+\Delta\sigma_{z}

(19)

where
\alpha=\pm 1
denotes the valley index at
{\bf\it P}_{\pm}=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{a}(\pm 2/3,0)
and
\Delta
is the mass gap.
s
denotes the strain parameter which is required for a nonzero
M_{y}
(see below). This Hamiltonian has a mirror symmetry
\mathcal{M}_{y}\ (y\rightarrow-y)
and thus the geometrical tensor
\hat{M}
must be in the
y
-direction (detailed symmetry consideration in
Toshio et al. 2020
). By diagonalizing the Hamiltonian, we can obtain the expression of the energy dispersion
\epsilon_{0}^{\alpha}=\alpha sp_{y}+\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+v^{2}{\bf\it p}^{2}}
, the Berry curvature and the orbital magnetic moment respectively,

\displaystyle\Omega^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)}{2}\frac{\alpha v^{2}\hbar^{2}\Delta}{(\Delta^{2}+v^{2}{\bf\it p}^{2})^{3/2}},

(20)

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\alpha e\hbar v^{2}\Delta}{\Delta^{2}+v^{2}{\bf\it p}^{2}}.

(21)

Here
\mu>0

(\mu<0)
for the conduction (valence) band. Especially in the low carrier and week strain limit, we can approximate the dispersion with an isotropic parabolic form :
\epsilon\simeq\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)\left[\Delta+({\bf\it p}+{\bf\it p}^{\alpha}_{0})^{2}/2m\right]+O(s/v)^{2}
where
m=\Delta/v^{2}
and
{\bf\it p}^{\alpha}_{0}=(0,\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)\alpha s\Delta/v^{2})
. The hydrodynamic coefficients
\hat{M}
and
\hat{Y}
can also be easily calculated in the above limit, and finally we arrive at the following analytic formula :
M_{xz}=0
and

\displaystyle M_{yz}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)}{2\pi}\frac{smv^{2}p_{F}^{2}}{\Delta^{2}+v^{2}p_{F}^{2}}+O(s/v)^{2},

(22)

\displaystyle Y_{zz}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{sgn}(\mu)}{4\pi}\frac{e\hbar v^{4}\Delta^{2}}{(\Delta^{2}+v^{2}p_{F}^{2})^{5/2}}+O(s/v)^{2},

(23)

where
p_{F}=\sqrt{2m(\mu-\Delta)}
is the radius of the Fermi surface. We can estimate a typical scale of
M_{yz}
and
Y_{zz}
for ML-graphene as
-1.4\times 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}
and
2.5\times 10^{24}\,\mathrm{C\cdot s/kg^{2}}
, using the parameters
v\sim 4.5\times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}
,
\Delta\sim 1.5\,\mathrm{eV}
,
s/v=0.1
, and
(\mu-\Delta)/\Delta=0.1
which corresponds to
n_{0}\simeq 8\times 10^{13}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}
. Unfortunately, a quantitative estimation shows the nonreciprocity in the reflectance by use of these parameters is of the order of
10^{-6}
when substituting
B_{0}=1\,\mathrm{T}
and is too small to observe in experiments (Fig.
3
). However, such a nonreciprocity is expected to be much enhanced in Weyl semimetals or anomalous Hall systems. In the former, the orbital magnetic moment behaves divergently at a Weyl point and thus it is expected that
M_{y}
has much larger values. On the other hand, in the latter, the spontaneous magnetic order provides a much stronger effective magnetic field which also leads to an enhancement of the nonreciprocity. Ideal candidates for the latter could be realized in doped topological insulators such as Cr-doped bismuth telluride
Chang et al. 2013
or atomic layered materials (graphene or TMDs) on a magnetic substrate
Wang et al. 2015
;
Tang et al. 2018
;
Averyanov et al. 2018
, both of which satisfy the requirements in our theory. These issues will be left for future investigations.

Conclusion—

In summary, we have developed a basic framework of nonreciprocal electron hydrodynamics in noncentrosymmetric metals under magnetic fields, which is composed of a generalized Euler equation Eq.(
8
) and the hydrodynamic expression of local current Eq.(
10
). In addition, we have identified the interplay between the orbital magnetic moment of electron wavepackets and magnetic fields as a geometric origin of nonreciprocity in dispersion relation of surface magnetoplasmons. Furthermore, we have shown that the peak splitting in the magneto-optical reflectance stems from the nonreciprocity in surface magnetoplasmons. Our results offer valuable insights into the nonreciprocal response and will pave the way to terahertz plasmonics applications.

Abstract
. This was research presented at the Worldwide Federation of National Math Competitions in Bulgaria in 2022. For any real-valued k > 1 k>1 , we consider the tree rooted at 0, where each positive integer n n has parent ⌊ n k ⌋ \lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor . The average number of children per node is k k , thus this definition gives a natural way to extend k k -ary trees to irrational k k . We focus on the sequence r d r_{d} : the count of nodes at depth d d . We first prove there exists some constant ρ ⁡ ( k ) \rho(k) such that r d ∼ ρ ⁡ ( k ) ⋅ k d r_{d}\sim\rho(k)\cdot k^{d} . We then study a family of values k = a + a + 4 ​ b 2 k=\frac{a+\sqrt{a+4b}}{2} , where we prove the sequence satisfies the exact recurrence r d = a ⋅ r d − 1 + b ⋅ r d − 2 r_{d}=a\cdot r_{d-1}+b\cdot r_{d-2} . This generalizes a special case when k k is the golden ratio and r d r_{d} is the Fibonacci sequence.

0&1&\dfrac{-a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}{2\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}=\dfrac{-k_{2}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}\end{bmatrix}

So, if

,

r_{d}=\frac{k_{1}^{d+1}-k_{2}^{d+1}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}.

By solving the same original matrix with

swapped with
a
, we can get that when

,

Now, we can plug these closed formulas into
\rho(k)
. When
b\geq 0,

\displaystyle\rho(k)

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\dfrac{r_{d}}{k_{1}^{d}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\dfrac{k_{1}^{d+1}-k_{2}^{d+1}}{k_{1}^{d}\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\dfrac{k_{1}^{d+1}}{k_{1}^{d}\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{k_{1}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}.

Similarly, when
b<0
,

\rho(k)=\dfrac{k_{1}-1}{\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}.

Note that when

, then

, so we can also write

due to

So, we have found a closed formula for the irrational case, even when the rational case doesn’t have the same type of behavior. This is a rare case where irrationality seems to behave more nicely than their rational counterpart. Also, we have a closed formula for these golden-like trees. This is interesting and motivates the extension from a

-ary tree.

Also, note that the
\phi
-tree has a regular language that describes its paths. In
[

]
, they say that all rational numbers

that are not integers do not have this property in the

-tree, so it would be interesting to see which other irrational numbers have this property. This isn’t true for other “golden-like” numbers, or numbers of the form

Furthermore, one of the original reasons that

Within this paper there was the surprising connection to the Josephus problem

]
, and the article

looks specifically at the constant

in regard to the Collatz Conjecture and mentions the Josephus problem. The connection to the Collatz Conjecture is light, as one can simplify the process into this tree. The Collatz Conjecture states that if you take some

, if it is even divide it by 2, otherwise take

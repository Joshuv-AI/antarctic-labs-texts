Static black holes in higher dimensional Einstein-Skyrme models
Bobby E. Gunara, Fiki T. Akbar, Rizqi Fadli, Deden M. Akbar, and Hadi Susanto

Abstract
In this paper we construct a class of hairy static black holes of higher dimensional Einstein-Skyrme theories with the cosmological constant Λ ≤ 0 \Lambda\leq 0 whose scalar is an S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) valued field. The spacetime is set to be conformal to ℳ 4 × 𝒩 N − 4 \mathcal{M}^{4}\times\mathcal{N}^{N-4} where ℳ 4 \mathcal{M}^{4} and 𝒩 N − 4 \mathcal{N}^{N-4} are a four dimensional spacetime and a compact Einstein ( N − 4 ) (N-4) -dimensional submanifold for N ≥ 5 N\geq 5 , respectively, whereas N = 4 N=4 is the trivial case. We discuss the behavior of solutions near the boundaries, namely, near the (event) horizon and in the asymptotic region. Then, we establish local-global existence of black hole solutions and show that black holes with finite energy exist if their geometries are asymptotically Ricci flat. At the end, we perform a linear stability analysis using perturbative method and give a remark about their stability.

5
Linear stability analysis: a remark

Finally, it is of interest to check the linear stability of the models using similar method as
ShiikiPRD2005
. The main idea is to take the metric functions
\delta(r),m(r),f(r)
to be also time dependent in advance, namely,
\delta_{t}(r,t),\linebreak m_{t}(r,t),f_{t}(r,t)
and then expand them around
\delta(r),m(r),f(r)
. The detailed computation of this method can be found in Appendix
A
, where we obtain that the linear stability is determined by the perturbation dynamics of
f(r)
.

Writing
f_{t}(r,t)=f(r)+\varepsilon\frac{\psi(r)e^{i\omega t}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
(see (
A.9
) and (
A.19
)), where
\varepsilon
is a small parameter, will yield an eigenvalue problem, i.e. Sturm-Liouville equation, in terms of
\psi(r)
and
\omega
(see (
A.20
)). We say the solution of (
2.13
), (
2.14
), (
2.15
) and (
2.17
) to be linearly stable if all spectra of the eigenvalue problem satisfy
\omega^{2}>0
. Using the Sturm-Liouville argument, this is similar to requiring the existence of a positive definite solution (except at the boundaries), i.e.
\psi(r)>0
, with
\omega^{2}>0
. On the other hand, to show instability, it is sufficient to obtain that there is an eigenvalue with
\omega^{2}<0
.

However, it is difficult to solve the eigenvalue problem exactly. Our approach in this paper, i.e., to consider solution behavior near the boundaries, namely, the horizon and the asymptotic region, cannot be used to establish their stability. Therefore, in the following we will only make a remark on the linear eigenvalue problem and some of their spectrum.

At the horizon
\psi(r_{H})=0
, while in the asymptotic region
\psi(r\to+\infty)\to 0
. Then, using (
3.14
), (
3.15
), (
3.17
), and (
4.23
) in the limit of
r\to+\infty
, the eigenvalue problem can be simplified to

\displaystyle\omega^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\Lambda^{2}r^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}\psi}\left(r^{2}\psi^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{N\Lambda^{2}r^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}(N-2)}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{4\Lambda}{(N-1)(N-2)}+\frac{2(N-4)n\tilde{C}_{1}\Lambda^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}C_{0}}r^{-n+3}\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{\psi}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2(N-4)n\tilde{C}_{1}\Lambda^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}(N-2)C_{0}}r^{-n+2}.

Suppose there exists a constant
\lambda_{0}>0
such that we could have, for example,

-\frac{4\Lambda^{2}r^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}\psi}\left(r^{2}\psi^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{N\Lambda^{2}r^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}(N-2)}=\lambda_{0},

(5.2)

whose solution is

\psi(r)=r^{-1/2}\psi_{0}J\left(-1/2\sqrt{N(N-2)+1};\lambda^{1/2}_{0}/2|\Lambda|(N-1)(N-2)r^{-1/2}\right),

\displaystyle\omega^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}-\frac{4\Lambda}{(N-1)(N-2)}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(N-4)n\tilde{C}_{1}\Lambda^{2}}{2(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}C_{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left((N-2)C_{0}+1+\sqrt{N(N-2)+1}\right)r^{-n+2}.

for

It is worth pointing out that we could have a more general condition, that is, there exists a constant

-\frac{4\Lambda^{2}r^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}\psi}\left(r^{2}\psi^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{N\Lambda^{2}r^{2}}{(N-1)^{2}(N-2)}\\

We could simply solve (

Further analysis for the stability of the black holes is addressed for future work.

Comments on the model with

This section is devoted to discuss a model with positive cosmological constant

and Theorem
.

First, we discuss the solution at the horizon

. In the case of

\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{2}
or

In

7
Conclusion and outlook

We have constructed a family of static black holes in the higher dimensional Einstein-Skyrme model with the cosmological constant
\Lambda
where the most part of the discussion in this paper is for
\Lambda\leq 0
case. The geometry of the black holes is conformal to
\mathcal{M}^{4}\times\mathcal{N}^{N-4}
endowed with the metric (
2.1
) where
\mathcal{M}^{4}
and
\mathcal{N}^{N-4}
are the four dimensional spacetime and the compact
(N-4)
-dimensional Einstein submanifold with the cosmological constant
\Lambda_{N-4}\geq 0
, respectively. The Skyrme field is an
SU(2)
valued field defined locally on the hypersurface
\mathcal{S}^{3}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{4}
whose form is particularly given by (
2.10
). This implies that the black holes in general have local topological numbers.

The behavior of the metric functions
\delta(r),\penalty\ m(r)
,
C(r)
, and the profile function
f(r)
near the horizon converge to the positive constants
\delta_{H},\penalty\ m(r_{H})
,
C_{H}
, and
f_{H}
, respectively. The analysis on the Ricci scalar shows that these constants should satisfy the inequality (
3.8
) in order to have physical black holes. Furthermore, we have so called primary and secondary branches which are simply determined by the value of the cosmological constant
\Lambda
. In
N=4,5
dimensions, we could have both the primary and secondary branches, while in
N\geq 6
dimensions, there exists only primary branches.

In the asymptotic region, the functions
\delta(r),\penalty\ m(r)
,
C(r)
, and
f(r)
should satisfy (
3.14
), (
3.15
), and (
3.17
) which follows that the geometry becomes Einstein with the cosmological constant
2\Lambda/(N-2)
. We also show using the Komar integral that the black hole mass is constant.

Then, we established local existence and uniqueness of black hole solutions of (
2.13
), (
2.14
), (
2.15
), and (
2.17
) using Picard’s iteration and contraction mapping properties. We further obtain global existence using the maximal solution technique and showed using the expansions (
3.14
), (
3.15
), and (
3.17
) that this solution is finite and globally regular. Finally, using the 00-component of the Einstein field equation we showed that the black holes have finite energy if it satisfies (
4.23
) and the black hole geometries are asymptotically Ricci flat as stated in Theorem
1
.

While in this work we could make a remark on the corresponding linear eigenvalue problem, i.e., Sturm-Liouville equation, and some of its spectrum, stability of the black holes is still an open problem. Our asymptotic analysis cannot establish stability as one needs information of the eigenvalue problem on the whole domain. One possibility is to address it by solving the governing equation and the Sturm-Liouville problem numerically, which is planned for future work.

A Proof Procedure For Separation Logic With Inductive Definitions and Theory Reasoning
Mnacho Echenim, Nicolas Peltier

Abstract
A proof procedure, in the spirit of the sequent calculus, is proposed to check the validity of entailments between Separation Logic formulas combining inductively defined predicates denoting structures of bounded tree width and theory reasoning. The calculus is sound and complete, in the sense that a sequent is valid iff it admits a (possibly infinite) proof tree. We also show that the procedure terminates in the two following cases: (i) When the inductive rules that define the predicates occurring on the left-hand side of the entailment terminate, in which case the proof tree is always finite. (ii) When the theory is empty, in which case every valid sequent admits a rational proof tree, where the total number of pairwise distinct sequents occurring in the proof tree is doubly exponential w.r.t. the size of the end-sequent.



1
Introduction

Separation Logic (SL)
[
19
,
29
]
, is a well-established framework for
reasoning on programs manipulating pointer-based data structures.
It forms the basis of several industrial-scale static program
analyzers

[
4
,
2
,
10
]
.
The logic uses specific connectives to assert that formulas are satisfied on disjoint parts of the memory,
which allows for more concise and more natural specifications.
Recursive data structures are specified using
inductively defined predicates
, which provide a specification
mechanism similar to the definition of a recursive data type in an
imperative programming language.
Many verification tasks boil down to
checking entailments between formulas built on such atoms.
More precisely, the logic may be used to express pre- or post-conditions describing
the shape of the data structures (linked lists, trees, doubly linked lists, etc.) manipulated by the program,
and to express structural integrity properties, such as acyclicity of linked lists, absence of dangling pointers, etc.
Investigating the entailment problem for SL formulas is thus of theoretical and practical interest.
In practice, it is essential to offer as much flexibility as possible, and to
handle a wide class of user-defined data structures (e.g., doubly linked lists, trees with child-parent links, trees with chained leaves etc.), possibly involving external theories, such as arithmetic.
In general, the entailment problem is undecidable
for formulas
containing inductively defined predicates

[
18
]
,
and a lot of effort has been devoted to identifying decidable fragments and devising proof procedures, see e.g.,

[
1
,
6
,
7
,
15
,
14
,
8
]
.
In particular, a general class of decidable entailment problems is described in

[
17
]
. It is based on the decidability of the satisfiability
problem for monadic second order logic over graphs of a bounded treewidth, for formulas involving no theory other than equality.
This class is defined by restricting the form of the inductive rules, which must fulfill
3
conditions, formally defined below: the
progress
condition (every rule allocates a single memory location),
the
connectivity
condition (the set of allocated locations has a tree-shaped structure)
and the
establishment
condition (every existentially quantified variable is eventually allocated).
More recently, a

2
-
\mathsf{EXPTIME}
algorithm was proposed for such entailments

[
24
]
.
In
[
11
]

we showed that this bound is
tight and in
[
12
]
we devised a new algorithm, handling more general classes of inductive definitions.
The algorithms in
[
24
,
11
]
work by computing some abstract representation of the set of models of SL formulas.
The
abstraction is precise enough to allow checking that all the models of the left-hand side are also models of the right-hand side, also general enough to ensure termination of the entailment checking
algorithm.
Other approaches have been proposed to check entailments in various fragments, see e.g.,
[
7
,
14
,
18
]
.
In particular, a sound and complete proof procedure is given in
[
31
]
for inductive rules satisfying conditions that are strictly more restrictive than those in
[
17
]
.
In
[
16
]
a labeled proof systems is presented for separation logic formulas
handling arbitrary inductive definitions and all connectives (including negation and separated implication).
Due to the expressive power of the considered language, this proof system is of course not terminating or complete in general.

In the present paper,
we extend these results by defining a proof procedure in the style of sequent calculi to
check the validity of entailments, using top-down decomposition rules.
Induction is performed by considering infinite (rational) proof trees modeling proofs by infinite descent, as in
[
3
]
.
We also tackle the combination of SL reasoning with theory reasoning, relying on external decision procedures
for checking the validity of formulas in the considered theory. This issue is of uttermost importance
for applications, as reasoning on data structures without taking into account the properties of the data stored in these structures has a limited scope,
and the combination of SL with data constraints has been considered by several authors (see, e.g.,
[
27
,
28
,
26
,
32
,
21
]
).
Beside the fact that it is capable of handling theory reasoning, our procedure has several advantages over the model-based bottom-up
algorithms that were previously devised
[
24
,
11
]
.
It is goal-oriented: the rules apply backward and reduce the considered entailments to simpler ones, until
axioms are reached.
The advantage is that the proof procedure is driven by the form of the current goal.
The procedure is also better-suited for interactive theorem proving (e.g., the user can guide the application of the inference rules, while the procedures in
[
24
,
11
]
work as “black boxes”).
If the entailment is valid, then the produced proof tree serves as a certification of the result, which can be checked if needed by the user or another system,
while the previous procedures
[
24
,
11
]
produce no certification.
Finally, the correctness proof of the algorithm is also simpler and more modular.
More specifically, we establish several new results in this paper.

1.

First, we show that the proof procedure is sound (Theorem
61
) and complete (Theorem
65
), in the sense that an entailment is valid
iff it admits a proof tree. The proof tree may be infinite, hence the result does not entail
that checking entailments is semi-decidable. However, this shows that the procedure can be used
as a semi-decision procedure for checking non-validity (i.e., an entailment is not valid iff the procedure is stuck eventually in some branch), provided the base theory is decidable.

2.

If the theory only contains the equality predicate, then we show that
the entailments can be reduced to entailments in the empty theory (Theorem
42
). The intuition is that all the equality and disequality constraints
can be encoded in the formulas describing the shape of the data structures.
This result is also described in a paper
[
13
]
accepted for presentation at ASL 2022 (workshop with no formal proceedings).

3.

By focusing on the case where the theory is empty, we show (Theorem
83
) that every valid
entailment admits a proof tree that is rational (i.e., has a finite number of pairwise distinct subtrees, up to a renaming of variables). Furthermore, the number of sequents occurring in the tree is at most
\mathcal{O}(2^{2^{n}})
, where
n
is the size of the initial sequent. In combination with the previous result, this theorem allows us to reestablish the
2
-
\mathsf{EXPTIME}
membership of the entailment problem for inductive systems satisfying the conditions above in the theory of equality
[
25
]
.

4.

We also show that the proof tree is finite if the inductive rules that define the predicates
occurring on the left-hand side of the entailment terminate (Corollary
68
).

2.5
Establishment

The notion of establishment
[
17
]
is defined as follows:

Definition 14
.

A pc-SID is established
if
for every atom
\alpha
, every predicate-free formula

\exists\mathbf{x}.\phi
such that

\alpha\Leftarrow_{{\cal R}}^{*}\exists\mathbf{x}.\phi
, and every
x\in\mathbf{x}
,
\phi
is of the form
x^{\prime}\mapsto(y_{1},\dots,y_{\kappa})*\chi*\psi

\chi
is a separating conjunction of equations (possibly
\mathtt{emp}
) such that

All the rules in Example

are trivially established. For instance,

fulfills the condition, since every predicate-free unfolding of

contains a formula of the form
x^{\prime}\mapsto\dots
and
\mathtt{emp}\models_{{\cal T}}x^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}
.

The following lemma states a key property of an established pc-SID:
every
location referred to in the heap is allocated, except possibly those associated with a free variable.

Lemma 15
.

Let
{\cal R}
be an established pc-SID and
let
\phi
be a quantifier-free symbolic heap.
If
(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{h})\models_{{\cal R}}\phi
then

\mathit{loc}(\mathfrak{h})\setminus\mathit{dom}(\mathfrak{h})\subseteq\mathfrak{s}(\mathit{fv}\left(\phi\right))
.

Proof.

By definition,
\phi\Leftarrow_{{\cal R}}^{*}\exists\mathbf{y}.~\psi
, where

\psi
is a quantifier-free and predicate-free formula, and

(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})\models_{{\cal R}}\psi
for some store
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
coinciding with
\mathfrak{s}

on all the variables not occurring in
\mathbf{y}
.
Let
\ell\in\mathit{loc}(\mathfrak{h})\setminus\mathit{dom}(\mathfrak{h})
.
Since
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})\models_{{\cal R}}\psi
, necessarily
\psi
contains an atom of the form

y_{0}\mapsto(y_{1},\dots,y_{\kappa})
with
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(y_{i})=\ell
, for some

.
If

, then
\mathfrak{s}(y_{i})=\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(y_{i})
and the proof is completed.
Otherwise,
y_{i}\in\mathbf{y}
, and by the establishment condition

\psi

y_{0}^{\prime}\mapsto(y_{1}^{\prime},\dots,y_{\kappa}^{\prime})

and a
{\cal T}

In the remainder of the paper, we assume that every considered pc-SID is established.

Extending the Syntax

We extend the syntax of formulas by considering
constructs of the form
\Phi_{\beta\mathrel{\mbox{$\hskip-0.24582pt\mathord{-}\hskip-3.27773pt\mathord{-}\hskip-2.95001pt\scalebox{0.9}{$\mathord{\bullet}$}$\hskip-0.03989pt}}p(\mathbf{x})}^{\mathbf{u}}[\mathbf{v}]

-atoms

The intuition is that a

-atom is valid in a structure if

unfolding of

that is true in the considered structure, and the formula
\beta
denotes the part that is not unfolded.

Definition 16
.

A
pu
-predicate is an expression of the form

\Phi_{\beta\mathrel{\mbox{$\hskip-0.24582pt\mathord{-}\hskip-3.27773pt\mathord{-}\hskip-2.95001pt\scalebox{0.9}{$\mathord{\bullet}$}$\hskip-0.04225pt}}p(\mathbf{x})}^{\mathbf{u}}

\beta
is a possibly empty
separating conjunction of predicate atoms,
p\in{\cal P}_{S}
and
\mathbf{u}
is a vector of pairwise distinct variables containing all the variables in
\mathit{fv}\left(\beta\right)\cup\mathbf{x}
.
A
pu
-atom is an expression of the form

\alpha[\mathbf{v}]
, where
\alpha
is a
pu
-predicate
\Phi_{\beta\mathrel{\mbox{$\hskip-0.24582pt\mathord{-}\hskip-3.27773pt\mathord{-}\hskip-2.95001pt\scalebox{0.9}{$\mathord{\bullet}$}$\hskip-0.04225pt}}p(\mathbf{x})}^{\mathbf{u}}
and
\|\mathbf{u}\|=\|\mathbf{v}\|
.

For every
pu
-atom

, we define

\alpha[\mathbf{v}]\sigma=\alpha[\mathbf{v}\sigma]
, and we let
\mathit{size}(\Phi_{\beta\mathrel{\mbox{$\hskip-0.24582pt\mathord{-}\hskip-3.27773pt\mathord{-}\hskip-2.95001pt\scalebox{0.9}{$\mathord{\bullet}$}$\hskip-0.04225pt}}p(\mathbf{x})}^{\mathbf{u}}[\mathbf{v}])=\mathit{size}(\beta)+\mathit{size}(p(\mathbf{x}))+\mathit{size}(\mathbf{u})+\mathit{size}(\mathbf{v})+1

is necessarily of the form
\mathbf{u}\theta

are pairwise distinct.
Note also that when applying a substitution to
\alpha[\mathbf{v}]
the variables occurring in the
pu

Formally, the semantics of these constructs is defined as follows.

For every pc-SID

Our proof procedure handles sequents which are defined as follows.

Definition 26
.

A sequent is an expression of the form

\displaystyle\mathit{size}(\phi_{0}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i=0}^{n}\mathit{size}(\phi_{i})+\mathit{size}({\cal R}),\hskip 18.49988pt\mathit{fv}\left(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}\right)=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\mathit{fv}\left(\phi_{i}\right),

is injective,

Example 28
.

For instance,

\mathtt{ils}(x_{1},x_{2})*x_{2}\mapsto(x_{3})*x_{2}<x_{3}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\mathtt{als}(x_{1},x_{3})

is the set of rules from Example

and

On the other hand, the sequent

Remark 29
.

The restriction to injective countermodels is for technical convenience only and does not entail any loss of generality, since it is possible to enumerate all the
equivalence relations on the free variables occurring in the sequent and test entailments separately
for each of these relations, by replacing all the variables in the
same class by the same representative. The number of such relations is simply exponential w.r.t. the number of free variables, thus the reduction does not affect the overall
2
-
\mathsf{EXPTIME}
membership result derived from Theorem
83
.
Note that the condition “
\mathfrak{s}
is injective” in Definition
27
could be safely replaced by the slightly weaker condition “
\mathfrak{s}
is injective on
\mathit{fv}\left(\phi\right)\cup\mathit{fv}\left(\Gamma\right)
”. Indeed, since
{\cal L}
is infinite, we can always find an injective store
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
coinciding with
\mathfrak{s}
on all variables in
\mathit{fv}\left(\phi\right)\cup\mathit{fv}\left(\Gamma\right)
.

Every formula
\phi
can be reduced to an equivalent disjunction of symbolic heaps, using the well-known equivalences (if

):

\phi*(\psi_{1}\vee\psi_{2})

\equiv

(\phi*\psi_{1})\vee(\phi*\psi_{2})

(\exists x.\phi)*\psi

\equiv

\exists x.(\phi*\psi)

\exists x.(\phi_{1}\vee\phi_{2})

\equiv

(\exists x.\phi_{1})\vee(\exists x.\phi_{2})

Consequently any sequent
\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma
can be reduced to an equivalent sequent of the form
\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}\right)\vdash_{{\cal R}}\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}
, where
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n},\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}
are disjunction-free.
Moreover, it is clear that the latter sequent is valid iff all the sequents in the set
\{\phi_{i}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
are valid.
Thus we will assume in Section
8
that the considered sequents
are disjunction-free.
Note that for all
i
, we have
\mathit{width}(\phi_{i}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m})\leq\mathit{width}(\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma)
, hence the reduction (although exponential) preserves the complexity result in Theorem
83
.

Proposition 30
.

For every set of expressions (formulas, sequents or rules)
E
, if
\mathit{size}(e)\leq n
for all
e\in E
, then

\mathit{card}(E)=\mathcal{O}(2^{c\cdot n})
, for some constant
c
.

Proof.

By definition every element
e\in E
is a word with
\mathit{size}(e)\leq n
, on a vocabulary of some fixed cardinality
m
, thus

\mathit{card}(E)\leq m^{n}=2^{n\cdot\log(m)}
.
∎

Proposition 31
.

Let
E
be a set of expressions (formulas, sequents or rules) such that
\mathit{size}(e)\leq n
, for all
e\in E
.
Then
\mathit{size}(E)\leq\mathcal{O}(2^{d\cdot n})
, for some constant

.

Proof.

By Proposition
30
,
\mathit{card}(E)=\mathcal{O}(2^{c\cdot n})
. Thus

\mathit{size}(E)\leq n\cdot\mathit{card}(E)=\mathcal{O}(2^{d\cdot n})
with

.
∎

-compatibility

-compatible set.
This restriction ensures that the set of free variables

Let

For any disjunction-free and

-free formula

\mathit{alloc}(\chi)

=

if

is a
{\cal T}
-formula (or
\mathtt{emp}
)

\mathit{alloc}(x\mapsto(y_{1},\dots,y_{\kappa}))

=

\{x\}

\mathit{alloc}(p(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))

if
p\in{\cal P}_{S}

\mathit{alloc}(\phi_{1}*\phi_{2})

\mathit{alloc}(\exists x.~\phi)

\mathit{alloc}(\phi)\setminus\{x\}

Definition 32
.

An established pc-SID

Example 33
.

The set

6
Eliminating Equations and Disequations

We show that the equations and disequations can always be eliminated from established sequents, while preserving equivalence.
The intuition is that equations can be discarded by instantiating the inductive rules,
while disequations can be replaced by assertions that the considered variables
are allocated in disjoint parts of the heap.
We wish to emphasize that the result does not follow from existing translations of SL formulas to graph grammars (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
20
]
), as the rules allow for disequations as well as equations. In particular the result crucially relies on the establishment property: it does not hold for non-established rules.

Definition 41
.

Let
P\subseteq{\cal P}_{\cal T}
.
A formula
\phi
is
P
-constrained if
for every formula
\psi
such that
\phi\Leftarrow_{{\cal R}}\psi
, and for every symbol
p\in{\cal P}_{\cal T}
occurring in
\psi
, we have
p\in P
.
A sequent
\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma
is
P
-constrained if
all the formulas in
\phi,\Gamma
are
P
-constrained.

In particular, if
\phi
is
\emptyset
-constrained, then the unfoldings of
\phi
contain no symbol in
{\cal P}_{\cal T}
.

Theorem 42
.

Let
P\subseteq{\cal P}_{\cal T}
.
There exists an algorithm that transforms every
P
-constrained established sequent
\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma

into an equivalent
(P\setminus\{\approx,\not\approx\})
-constrained established sequent
\phi^{\prime}\vdash_{{\cal R}^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\prime}
.
This algorithm runs in exponential time
and
\mathit{width}(\phi^{\prime}\vdash_{{\cal R}^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\prime})
is polynomial w.r.t.
\mathit{width}(\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma)

Proof.

We consider a
P
-constrained established sequent

.
This sequent is transformed in several steps, each of which is illustrated in Example

Step 1.

The first step consists in transforming all the formulas in
\phi,\Gamma
into disjunctions of symbolic heaps.
Then for every symbolic heap
\gamma
occurring in the obtained sequent, we add all the variables freely occurring
in
\phi
or
\Gamma
as parameters of every predicate symbol occurring in unfoldings of
\gamma

We obtain an equivalent sequent
\phi_{1}\vdash_{{\cal R}_{1}}\Gamma_{1}

denotes the total number of free variables occurring in
\phi,\Gamma

\mathit{width}(\phi_{1}\vdash_{{\cal R}_{1}}\Gamma_{1})\leq v\cdot\mathit{width}(\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma)^{2}

By Definition

Step 2.

All the equations involving an existential variable can be eliminated in a straightforward way

\phi\{x\leftarrow y\}

We then replace every formula

with free variables

by the disjunction of all the formulas
of the form

\exists\mathbf{z}.\phi\sigma*\mathop{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.5}{$\displaystyle*$}}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\textstyle*$}}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\scriptstyle*$}}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle*$}}}_{z\in\mathbf{z},z^{\prime}\in\mathbf{z}\cup\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\},z\not=z^{\prime}}z\not\approx z^{\prime},

Similarly we replace every rule

We denote by
\phi_{2}\vdash_{{\cal R}_{2}}\Gamma_{2}

Let

Since at most one disequation is added for every pair of variables, and the size of every variable is bounded by

\mathit{width}(\phi_{2}\vdash_{{\cal R}_{2}}\Gamma_{2})=\mathit{width}(\phi_{1}\vdash_{{\cal R}_{1}}\Gamma_{1})+v^{\prime}\cdot(v+v^{\prime})\cdot(1+2*\mathit{width}(\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma))=\mathcal{O}(\mathit{width}(\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma)^{3})

It is clear that

By induction on the derivation, we can show that all atoms occurring in an unfolding of the formulas in the sequent

Figure 1:
Inference rules

The inference rules, except for the axioms, are depicted in Figure
1
.
The rules are intended to be applied
bottom-up: a rule is
applicable
on a sequent
\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma
if there exists an instance of the rule
the conclusion of which is
\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma
. We
assume from now on that all the considered sequents are disjunction-free
(as explained above, this is without loss of generality since every formula is equivalent to a disjunction of symbolic heaps) and that the formula on the left-hand side is in prenex form.
We do not assume that the formulas occurring on the right-hand side of the sequents are in prenex form, because one of the
inference rules (namely the Existential Decomposition rule) will actually
shift existential quantifiers inside separating conjunctions.
Note that none of the rules rename the existential variables occurring in the conclusion (renaming is only used on the existential variables occurring in the rules). This feature will be used in the termination analysis.
We now provide some explanations on these rules. We refer to Figure
1
for the notations.

The Skolemisation rule (
Sk
) gets rid of existential quantifiers on the left-hand side of the sequent. The rule replaces an existential variable
x
by a new free variable
x^{\prime}
.
Note that the case where
x=x_{i}
for some
i=1,\dots,n
must be considered apart because countermodels must be injective.

The Heap Functionality rule (
HF
)
exploits the fact that every location refers to at most one tuple to instantiate some existential variables occurring on the right-hand side of a sequent. Note that the vector
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
may be empty, in which case there is no existential quantification.

The Left Unfolding rule (
UL
) unfolds a predicate atom on the left-hand side.
Note that the considered set of formulas is finite, up to
\alpha
-renaming, since
{\cal R}
is finite.
All the formulas
\phi_{i}*\phi
are implicitly transformed into prenex form.

The Right Unfolding rule (
UR
) unfolds a predicate atom on the right-hand side, but only when the unfolding yields a single points-to spatial atom.
Note that
this rule always applies on formulas
\exists\mathbf{x}.(\alpha*\psi)
: in the worst case, the set
\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}\}
is empty (
n=0
), in which case the rule simply removes the considered formula from the right-hand side.

Proof Trees

Definition 52
.

A proof tree is a possibly infinite tree, in which each node is labeled by a sequent,
and if a node is labeled by some sequent
\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma
, then its successors
are labeled by
\phi_{i}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma_{i}
with
i=1,\dots,n
,
for some rule application

\phi_{1}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma_{1}\cdots\phi_{n}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma_{n}

\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma

.
A proof tree is rational if it contains a finite number of subtrees, up to a renaming of variables.
The end-sequent of a proof tree is the sequent labeling the root of the tree.

In practice one is of course interested in constructing rational proof trees. Such rational proof trees can be infinite, but they can be represented finitely.
The cycles in a rational proof tree may be seen as applications of the induction principle.
We provide a simple example showing applications of the rules.

Theorem 61
.

(Soundness)

Let
{\cal R}
be an
\mathit{alloc}
-compatible pc-SID.

\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma
is valid.

\phi_{1}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma_{1}\ \dots\ \phi_{n}\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma_{n}

Remark 62
.

In general, with infinite proof trees, soundness requires some
well-foundedness condition, to rule out infinite paths of invalid statements (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
).
In our case, no further condition is needed because every rule application either reduces the considered sequent into simpler ones or unfolds a predicate atom.

Completeness And Termination Results

10.1
The General Case

We first show that every valid sequent admits a (possibly infinite) proof tree.
Together with Theorem
61
, this result shows that the calculus can be used as a semi-decision procedure for detecting non-validity: the procedure
will be “stuck” eventually in some branch, in the sense that one obtains a sequent on which no rule is applicable, iff the initial sequent is non-valid.

We shall assume that all the formulas in the considered root sequent are in prenex form.
However, the sequents occurring within the proof tree will fulfill a slightly less restrictive condition, stated below:

Definition 63

A sequent

\phi
is in prenex form and
all the formulas
\phi_{i}
that are not in prenex form are separating conjunctions of two prenex formulas.

It is easy to check that the premises of a rule with a quasi-prenex conclusion are always quasi-prenex.

10.2
Entailments Without Theories

In this section, we show that every valid,
\emptyset
-constrained sequent admits a rational proof tree. This shows that the calculus is a decision procedure for
\emptyset

-constrained sequents.
We first introduce a notion of strong validity:

Definition 69

A sequent
\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma
is strongly valid relatively to a decomposition
\phi=\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}
(modulo AC)
if, for every structure
(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{h}_{1}\uplus\mathfrak{h}_{2})

•

\mathfrak{s}
is injective,

•

(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{h}_{i})\models_{{\cal R}}\phi_{i}

•

\exists\mathbf{x}.(\psi_{1}*\psi_{2})
in
\Gamma

\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
coinciding with
\mathfrak{s}
on every variable not occurring in
\mathbf{x}

such that

(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},\mathfrak{h}_{i})\models_{{\cal R}}\psi_{i}
for

Using Lemma
72

\emptyset

The intuition is that in a strongly valid sequent, the decomposition of the heap associated with the separating conjunction on the

corresponds to a syntactic decomposition of a formula on the right-hand side. Note that the
notion of strong validity is relative to a decomposition

which will always be clear from the context.
For instance, the sequent

\mathtt{ls}(x,y)*\mathtt{ls}(y,z)\vdash_{{\cal R}}\mathtt{ls}(x,z)
is valid (where
\mathtt{ls}
is defined as in Example
47

does not correspond to any decomposition of the right-hand side.
On the other hand,
\mathtt{ls}(x,y)*\mathtt{ls}(y,z)\vdash_{{\cal R}}\exists u.~(\mathtt{ls}(x,u)*\mathtt{ls}(u,z))
is strongly valid.

Definition 70

Lemma 72
.

Let
\phi\vdash_{{\cal R}}\Gamma
be a
\emptyset
-constrained sequent, where

admits a countermodel
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})

•

•

\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\eta(\mathfrak{h})

Proof.

Since
\phi
is
\emptyset

\phi\Leftarrow_{{\cal R}}^{*}\exists\mathbf{x}.x_{1}\mapsto\mathbf{y}_{1}*\dots*x_{n}\mapsto\mathbf{y}_{n}

\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}

\mathbf{x}

We assume, w.l.o.g., that

Modulo

Since

&=&\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\setminus\mathbf{x}.\end{array}

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be
a store mapping all variables to pairwise distinct locations not occurring in

\mathfrak{s}
is injective by construction. Let
\hat{\mathfrak{s}}

\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}(x)

\mathfrak{s}

({\mathfrak{s}},\mathfrak{h})\models_{{\cal R}}\phi

\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(x)

Note that
\eta

\mathfrak{s}
and
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
are injective, and

\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}=\eta\circ\mathfrak{s}
.
Let
x
be a variable. If

then
\hat{\mathfrak{s}}(x)=\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}(x)

\mathfrak{s}
we have
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}(x)\not\in\mathit{img}(\mathfrak{s})
, hence

\eta(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}(x))=\mathfrak{s}^{\prime\prime}(x)

Definition 74
.

A path from
\ell
to
\ell^{\prime}
in a heap
\mathfrak{h}

is a nonempty sequence of locations
(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n})
such that

\ell_{1}=\ell
,
\ell_{n}=\ell^{\prime}
and for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,
\ell_{i+1}\in\mathfrak{h}(\ell_{i})
.

The following proposition states an important property of pc-SIDs.

Proposition 75
.

Let
p(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be a predicate atom and let

(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{h})
be a model of
p(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.
If
\ell=\mathfrak{s}(x_{1})
and
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathit{loc}(\mathfrak{h})
then there exists a path from
\ell

to
\ell^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
\mathit{card}(\mathit{dom}(\mathfrak{h}))
.
We have
p(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\Leftarrow_{{\cal R}}\exists\mathbf{x}.\phi
for some quantifier-free
formula
\phi
, and there exists a store
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
coinciding with
\mathfrak{s}
on all variables not occurring in
\mathbf{x}
, such that

(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},\mathfrak{h})\models\phi
.
If
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
then the result is immediate (a path of length
1
always exists from any location
\ell
to
\ell
). Assume that
\ell\not=\ell^{\prime}
.
By the progress condition,
\phi
is of the form
x_{1}\mapsto(y_{1},\dots,y_{\kappa})*p_{1}(z_{1}^{1},\dots,z_{\#(p_{1})}^{1})*\cdots*p_{m}(z_{1}^{m},\dots,z_{\#(p_{m})}^{m})*\chi
, for some
{\cal T}
-formula
\chi
.
Thus there exist disjoint heaps
\mathfrak{h}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{h}_{m}
such that

\mathfrak{h}=\{(\mathfrak{s}(x_{1}),\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(y_{1}),\dots,\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(y_{\kappa}))\}\uplus\mathfrak{h}_{1}\uplus\dots\uplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}
and
(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{h}_{i})\models p_{i}(z_{1}^{i},\dots,z_{\#(p_{i})}^{i})
for
i=1,\dots,m
.
If
\mathfrak{s}(y_{i})=\ell^{\prime}
, for some
i=1,\dots,\kappa
we have

\ell^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}(\mathfrak{s}(x_{1}))=\mathfrak{h}(\ell)
and the proof is completed.
Otherwise,
\ell^{\prime}
cannot occur in
\{\mathfrak{s}(x_{1}),\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(y_{1}),\dots,\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(y_{\kappa})\}
, hence
\ell^{\prime}
necessary occurs in
\mathit{loc}(\mathfrak{h}_{i})
for some
i=1,\dots,m
, since
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathit{loc}(\mathfrak{h})
by hypothesis.
By the induction hypothesis, there exists a path in
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
from
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(z_{1}^{i})
to
\ell^{\prime}
,
and by the connectivity condition
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(z_{1}^{i})\in(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(y_{1}),\dots,\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}(y_{\kappa}))=\mathfrak{h}(\mathfrak{s}(x_{1}))=\mathfrak{h}(\ell)
.
Thus there exists a path in
\mathfrak{h}
from
\ell
to
\ell^{\prime}
.
∎

\mathit{alloc}

The complexity of the procedure for

since entailment checking is

]
.
The high complexity of entailment testing is unsatisfactory in practice. We thus plan to investigate fragments of inductive rules for which the devised calculus yields an efficient decision procedure.
As emphasized by the lower-bound result in
[
11
]
, which relies on very simple data structures and by the fact that language inclusion is already
\mathsf{EXPTIME}
-complete for tree automata
[
30
]
, there is no hope that this can be achieved by restricting only the shape of
the structures: it is also necessary to strongly restrict the class of inductive rules (forbidding for instance overlapping rules).
We will also try to identify classes of entailments for which the procedure terminates for nonempty theories, under reasonable
conditions on the theory.
Another problem that can be of practical interest is to extract countermodels of
non-valid (or irreducible) entailments.
Finally, we plan to extend the proof procedure in order to solve bi-abduction problems,
a generalized form of abduction which
plays a central rôle for the efficiency and scalability of program analysis algorithms
[
5
]
.

New classes of null hypersurfaces in indefinite Sasakian space-forms
Samuel Ssekajja*

Abstract
. We introduce two classes of null hypersurfaces of an indefinite Sasakian manifold, ( M ¯ , ϕ ¯ , ζ , η ) (\overline{M},\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta) , tangent to the characteristic vector field ζ \zeta , called; contact screen conformal and contact screen umbilic null hypersurfaces. These hypersurfaces come in to fill the existing gap in screen conformal and screen totally umbilic null hypersurfaces. We prove that such hypersurfaces are contained in indefinite Sasakian space forms of constant ϕ ¯ \overline{\phi} -sectional curvature of − 3 -3 .

Primary 53C25; Secondary 53C40, 53C50

1.
Introduction

The theory of non-degenerate submanifolds
[
2
,
3
,
12
]
of Riemannian or semi-Riemannian manifolds is one of the most important topics of differential geometry. But the theory of null submanifolds of semi-Riemannian manifolds is relatively new and in a developing stage, with a lot of applications to mathematical physics (general relativity and electromagnetism). The geometry of null submanifolds becomes more difficult and is completely different from that of non-degenerate submanifolds. Such difficulty stems from a non-trivial intersection of the normal bundle of a null submanifold and its tangent bundle. In 1996, Duggal and Bejancu published their work
[
5
]
on null submanifolds of semi-Riemannian manifolds and indefinite Kaehler manifolds. This was later, in 2009, updated to
[
6
]
by Duggal and Sahin, to include the geometry of indefinite almost contact manifolds. In particular, the geometry of null submanifolds of indefinite Sasakian manifolds is in Chapter 7.

Recently several authors have studied the geometry of null submanifolds of indefinite Sasakian manifolds
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. In particular,
[
8
]
proves that there exist no null hypersurfaces of an indefinite Sasakian space form of constant
\overline{\phi}
-sectional curvature different from
1
(see Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 1). On the other hand,
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
considers totally umbilic, screen totally umbilic,
\eta
-totally umbilic, screen conformal and invariant null hypersurfaces in indefinite Sasakian manifolds. In this paper, we prove that all the above null hypersurfaces are non-existent in indefinite Sasakian manifolds, (see Theorem
3.4
), if they are assumed to be tangent to the structure vector field, and that there exist other null hypersurfaces in indefinite Sasakian space forms of constant
\overline{\phi}
-sectional curvature different from
1
(see Theorems
4.3
and
4.8
).

The rest of the paper is arranged as follows; in Section
2
we quote the basic notions required in the rest of the paper. In Section
3
, we prove a non-existence result of null hypersurfaces, tangent to the structure vector field. Finally, in Section
4
, we introduce two classes of null hypersurfaces in indefinite Sasakian manifolds.

2.
Preliminaries

A
(2n+1)
-dimensional differentiable manifold
\overline{M}
is said to have an almost contact structure
(\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta)
if it admits a vector field
\zeta
, a 1-form
\eta
and a field
\overline{\phi}
of endomorphism of the tangent vector space satisfying

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}^{2}=-I+\eta\otimes\zeta,\;\;\;\eta(\zeta)=1.

(2.1)

It follows that
\overline{\phi}\zeta=0
,
\eta\circ\overline{\phi}=0
and
\mathrm{rank}(\overline{\phi})=2n
. Then, the manifold
\overline{M}
, with a
(\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta)
-structure is called an
almost contact manifold

[
2
]
. From the point of view of differential geometry it is desirable to define a metric on a paracompact manifold
\overline{M}
. We say that a semi-Riemannian metric
\overline{g}
is an associated metric of an almost contact structure
(\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta)
of
\overline{M}
if

\displaystyle\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}X,\overline{\phi}Y)=\overline{g}(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),\;\;\;\forall\,X,Y\in\Gamma(T\overline{M}).

(2.2)

Here, and in the rest of this paper,
\Gamma(\Xi)
denotes the smooth sections of a vector bundle
\Xi
. Then,
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
, with
\overline{g}
satisfying (
2.2
), is called an
almost contact metric manifold
with a
(\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta)
-structure and
\overline{g}
is called its compatible (or associated) metric, whose fundamental 2-form
\Omega
is defined by
\Omega(X,Y)=\overline{g}(X,\overline{\phi}Y)
, for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(T\overline{M})
. Note that Since
\Omega
, also satisfies
\eta\wedge\Omega^{n}
. A
(2n+1)
-dimensional manifold
\overline{M}
is called a contact manifold if there exists a 1-form
\eta
on
\overline{M}
such that
\eta\wedge(d\eta^{n})\neq 0
everywhere. When
\Omega=d\eta
(i.e.,
\Omega
is closed), then,
(\overline{M},\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta)
is called a
contact metric manifold

[
3
]
. These metrics can be constructed by the polarization of
\Omega=d\eta
evaluated on a local orthonormal basis of the tangent space with respect to an arbitrary metric, on the contact subbundle
\mathcal{D}
, such that
T\overline{M}=\mathcal{D}\perp\mathbb{R}\zeta
. Here, and in the rest of this paper,
\mathbb{R}\zeta
denotes the line bundle spanned by
\zeta
. Thus, a contact metric manifold is an analogue of an almost Kaehler manifold, in odd dimensions. The almost contact structure
(\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta)
on
\overline{M}
is said to be
normal
if
\overline{\phi}
is integrable. It is known (see Blair
[
3
]
for details) that
\overline{M}
has a
normal contact structure
if
N_{\overline{\phi}}+2d\eta\otimes\zeta=0
, where
N_{\overline{\phi}}=[\overline{\phi},\overline{\phi}]
is the Nijenhuis tensor field of
\overline{\phi}
. A normal contact metric manifold is called a
Sasakian manifold
. It is well-known
[
3
]
that an almost contact metric manifold
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
is Sasakian if and only if

\displaystyle(\overline{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\phi})Y=\overline{g}(X,Y)\zeta-\eta(Y)X,\;\;\;\;\forall\,X,Y\in\Gamma(T\overline{M}),

(2.3)

where
\overline{\nabla}
is the metric connection on
\overline{M}
. Replacing
Y
by
\zeta
in (
2.3
), and using (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{X}\zeta=-\overline{\phi}X,\;\;\;\,\forall\,X\in\Gamma(T\overline{M}).

(2.4)

A plane section
\pi
in
T_{x}\overline{M}
of a Sasakian manifold
\overline{M}
is called a
\overline{\phi}
-section if it is spanned by a unit vector
X
orthogonal to
\zeta
and
\overline{\phi}X
, where
X
is a non-null vector field on
\overline{M}
. The sectional curvature
K(X,\overline{\phi}X)
of a
\overline{\phi}
-section is called a
\overline{\phi}
-sectional curvature. If
\overline{M}
has a
\overline{\phi}
-sectional curvature
c
which does not depend on the
\overline{\phi}
-section at each point, then,
c
is constant in
\overline{M}
and
\overline{M}
is called a
Sasakian space form
, denoted by
\overline{M}(c)
. Moreover, the curvature tensor
\overline{R}
of
\overline{M}
satisfies (see
[
3
]
)

\displaystyle\overline{R}(X,Y)Z=

\displaystyle\frac{(c+3)}{4}\{\overline{g}(Y,Z)X-\overline{g}(X,Z)Y\}+\frac{(c-1)}{4}\{\eta(X)\eta(Z)Y

\displaystyle-\eta(Y)\eta(Z)X+\overline{g}(X,Z)\eta(Y)\zeta-\overline{g}(Y,Z)\eta(X)\zeta

\displaystyle+\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}Y,Z)\overline{\phi}X-\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}X,Z)\overline{\phi}Y-2\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}X,Y)\overline{\phi}Z\},

(2.5)

for all
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(T\overline{M}).

Let
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
be a
(2n+1)
-dimensional semi-Riemannian manifold with index
q
, where
0<q<2n+1
, and let
(M,g)
be a hypersurface of
\overline{M}
. Let
g
be the induced tensor field by
\overline{g}
on
M
. Then,
M
is called a
null hypersurface
of
\overline{M}
if
g
is of constant rank
2n-1
and the normal bundle
TM^{\perp}
is a distribution of rank 1 on
M

[
5
]
. Here, the fibers the vector bundle
TM^{\perp}
are defined as
T_{x}M^{\perp}=\{Y_{x}\in T_{x}\overline{M}:\overline{g}_{x}(X_{x},Y_{x})=0,\;\;\forall\,X_{x}\in T_{x}M\}
, for any
x\in M
. Let
M
be a null hypersurface, we consider the complementary distribution
S(TM)
to
TM^{\perp}
in
TM
, which is called a
screen distribution
. It is well-known that
S(TM)
is non-degenerate (see
[
5
]
). Thus,
TM=S(TM)\perp TM^{\perp}
. As
S(TM)
is non-degenerate with respect to
\overline{g}
, we have
T\overline{M}=S(TM)\perp S(TM)^{\perp}
,
where
S(TM)^{\perp}
is the complementary vector bundle to
S(TM)
in
T\overline{M}|_{M}
. Let
(M,g)
be a null hypersurface of
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
. Then, there exists a unique vector bundle
tr(TM)
, called the
null transversal bundle

[
5
]
of
M
with respect to
S(TM)
, of rank 1 over
M
such that for any non-zero section
E
of
TM^{\perp}
on a coordinate neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subset M
, there exists a unique section
N
of
tr(TM)
on
\mathcal{U}
satisfying

\displaystyle\overline{g}(\xi,N)=1,\;\overline{g}(N,N)=\overline{g}(N,Z)=0,\;\;\;\forall\,Z\in\Gamma(S(TM)).

(2.6)

Consequently, we have the following decomposition of
T\overline{M}
.

\displaystyle T\overline{M}|_{M}=S(TM)\perp\{TM^{\perp}\oplus tr(TM)\}=TM\oplus tr(TM).

Let
\nabla
and
\nabla^{*}
denote the induced connections on
M
and
S(TM)
, respectively, and
P
be the projection of
TM
onto
S(TM)
, then the local Gauss-Weingarten equations of
M
and
S(TM)
are the following
[
5
]
.

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y+B(X,Y)N,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{X}N=-A_{N}X+\nabla_{X}^{t}N=-A_{N}X+\tau(X)N,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}PY=\nabla^{*}_{X}PY+h^{*}(X,PY)=\nabla^{*}_{X}PY+C(X,PY)\xi,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\xi=-A^{*}_{\xi}X+\nabla_{X}^{*t}\xi=-A^{*}_{\xi}X-\tau(X)\xi,\;\;\;A_{\xi}^{*}\xi=0,

(2.10)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
,
\xi\in\Gamma(TM^{\perp})
and
N\in\Gamma(tr(TM))
, where
\overline{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
\overline{M}
. In the above setting,
B
is the local second fundamental form of
M
and
C
is the local second fundamental form on
S(TM)
.
A_{N}
and
A^{*}_{\xi}
are the shape operators on
TM
and
S(TM)
respectively, while
\tau
is a 1-form on
TM
. The above shape operators are related to their local fundamental forms by

\displaystyle g(A^{*}_{\xi}X,Y)=B(X,Y)\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;g(A_{N}X,PY)=C(X,PY),

(2.11)

for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. It follows from (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) that

\displaystyle B(X,\xi)=0,\;\;\;\;\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM).

(2.12)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\overline{g}(A^{*}_{\xi}X,N)=0\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\overline{g}(A_{N}X,N)=0,

(2.13)

for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
. From relations (
2.13
), we notice that
A_{\xi}^{*}
and
A_{N}
are both screen-valued operators.

Let
\vartheta=\overline{g}(N,\boldsymbol{\cdot})
be a 1-form metrically equivalent to
N
defined on
\overline{M}
. Take
\theta=i^{*}\vartheta

to be its restriction on
M
, where
i:M\rightarrow\overline{M}
is the inclusion map. Then it is easy to show that

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}g)(Y,Z)=B(X,Y)\theta(Z)+B(X,Z)\theta(Y),

(2.14)

for all
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM)
. Consequently,
\nabla
is generally
not
a metric connection with respect to
g
. However, the induced connection
\nabla^{*}
on
S(TM)
is a metric connection. Denote by
\overline{R}
and
R
the curvature tensors of the connection
\overline{\nabla}
on
\overline{M}
and the induced linear connections
\nabla
, respectively. Using the Gauss-Weingarten formulae, we obtain the following Gauss-Codazzi equations for
M
and
S(TM)
(see details in
[
5
,
6
]
).

\displaystyle\overline{g}(\overline{R}(X,Y)Z,\xi)=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}B)(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}B)(X,Z)+\tau(X)B(Y,Z)

\displaystyle-\tau(Y)B(X,Z),

(2.15)

\displaystyle\overline{g}(\overline{R}(X,Y)PZ,N)=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}C)(Y,PZ)-(\nabla_{Y}C)(X,PZ)-\tau(X)C(Y,PZ)

\displaystyle+\tau(Y)C(X,PZ),

(2.16)

for all
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM)
,
\xi\in\Gamma(TM^{\perp})
and
N\in\Gamma(tr(TM))
, where
\nabla B
and
\nabla C
are defined as follows;

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}B)(Y,Z)=XB(Y,Z)-B(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-B(Y,\nabla_{X}Z),

(2.17)

and

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}C)(Y,PZ)=XC(Y,PZ)-C(\nabla_{X}Y,PZ)-C(Y,\nabla^{*}_{X}PZ).

(2.18)

A null hypersurface
(M,g)
of a semi-Riemannian manifold
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
is called
screen conformal

[
5
, p. 51]
if there exist a non-vanishing smooth function
\psi
on a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
in
M
such that
A_{N}=\psi A^{*}_{\xi}
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle C(X,PY)=\psi B(X,PY),

(2.19)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. We say that
M
is
screen homothetic
if
\psi
is a constant function on
M
. The null hypersurface
M
is said to be
totally umbilic

[
5
]
if

\displaystyle B(X,Y)=\rho g(X,Y),

(2.20)

where
\rho
is a smooth function on a coordinate neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subset TM
. In case
\rho=0
, we say that
M
is
totally geodesic
. In the same line,
M
is called

if

\displaystyle C(X,PY)=\varrho g(X,PY),

(2.21)

where
\varrho
is a smooth function on a coordinate neighborhood
\mathcal{U}\subset TM
. When
\varrho=0
, we say that
M
is
screen totally geodesic
.

3.
A nonexistance result

Let
(M,g)
be a null hypersurface of an indefinite Sasakian manifold
(\overline{M},\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta)
, which is
tangent
to the structure vector field
\zeta
. That is;
\zeta\in\Gamma(TM)
. In such a case,
\zeta
belongs to
S(TM)

[
4
]
. As seen in the previous section, let
\xi
and
N
the metric normal and the transversal sections, respectively. Since
(\overline{\phi},\zeta,\eta))
is an almost contact structure and
\overline{\phi}\xi
is a null vector field, it follows that
\overline{\phi}N
is null too. Moreover,
\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}\xi,\xi)=0
and, thus,
\overline{\phi}\xi
is tangent to
TM
. Let us consider
S(TM)
containing
\overline{\phi}TM^{\perp}
as a vector subbundle. Consequently,
N
is orthogonal to
\overline{\phi}\xi
and we have
\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}N,\xi)=-\overline{g}(N,\overline{\phi}\xi)=0
and
\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}N,N)=0
. This means that
\overline{\phi}N
is tangent to
TM
and in particular, it belongs to
S(TM)
. Thus,
\overline{\phi}tr(TM)
is also a vector subbundle of
S(TM)
. In view of (
2.2
), we have

\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}\xi,\overline{\phi}N)=1
.

It is then easy to see that
\overline{\phi}TM^{\perp}\oplus\overline{\phi}tr(TM)
is a non-degenerate vector subbbundle of
S(TM)
, with 2-dimensional fibers. Since
\zeta
is tangent to
M
, and that
\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}E,\zeta)=\overline{g}(\overline{\phi}N,\zeta)=0
, then there exists a non-degenerate distribution
D_{0}
on
TM
such that

\displaystyle S(TM)=\{\overline{\phi}TM^{\perp}\oplus\overline{\phi}tr(TM)\}\perp D_{0}\perp\mathbb{R}\zeta.

(3.1)

Here,
\mathbb{R}\zeta
denotes the line bundle spanned by
\zeta
. It is easy to check that
D_{0}
is an almost complex distribution with respect to
\overline{\phi}
, i.e.,
\overline{\phi}D_{0}=D_{0}
. The decomposition of
TM
becomes

TM=\{\overline{\phi}TM^{\perp}\oplus\overline{\phi}tr(TM)\}\perp D_{0}\perp\mathbb{R}\zeta\perp TM^{\perp}
. If we set
D:=TM^{\perp}\perp\overline{\phi}TM^{\perp}\perp D_{0}
and
D^{\prime}=\overline{\phi}tr(TM)
, then

\displaystyle TM=D\oplus D^{\prime}\perp\mathbb{R}\zeta.

(3.2)

Here,
D
is an almost complex distribution and
D^{\prime}
is carried by
\overline{\phi}
just into the transversal bundle. Let us set

\displaystyle U:=-\overline{\phi}N\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;V:=-\overline{\phi}\xi.

(3.3)

Then, from (
3.2
), any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
is written as
X=RX+QX+\eta(X)\zeta
and
QX=u(X)U
, where
R
and
Q
are the projection morphisms of
TM
onto
D
and
D^{\prime}
, respectively, and
u
is a differential 1-form defined on
M
by

\displaystyle u(X)=g(V,X),\;\;\;\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM).

(3.4)

Applying
\overline{\phi}
to
X
and using (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}X=\phi X+u(X)N,

(3.5)

where
\phi
is a (1,1) tensor field defined on
M
by
\phi X=\overline{\phi}RX
. Furthemore, we have

\displaystyle\phi^{2}X=-I+\eta(X)\zeta+u(X)U,\;\;\;\;u(U)=1,\;\;\;\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM).

(3.6)

It is easy to show that

\displaystyle g(\phi X,\phi Y)=g(X,Y)\eta(X)\eta(Y)-u(Y)v(X)-u(X)v(Y),

(3.7)

where
v
is a 1-form locally defined on
M
by

\displaystyle v(X)=g(U,X),\;\;\;\forall\,X\in\Gamma(TM).

(3.8)

Note that

\displaystyle g(\phi X,Y)=-g(X,\phi Y)-u(X)\theta(Y)-u(Y)\theta(X),

(3.9)

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The vector fields

and

in (

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}U

\displaystyle=\phi A_{N}X-\theta(X)\zeta+\tau(X)U,

\displaystyle-\phi\nabla_{X}U=v(X)\zeta-B(X,U)U+A_{N}X,

\zeta
. To that end,
(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}_{q},\overline{\phi}_{0},\zeta,\eta,\overline{g})

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{1}{2}\left(dz-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y^{i}dx^{i}\right),\;\;\;\;\zeta=2\partial z,

\displaystyle\overline{g}=\eta\otimes

\displaystyle\eta+\frac{1}{4}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{q/2}dx^{i}\otimes dx^{i}+dy^{i}\otimes dy^{i}+\sum_{i=q+1}^{n}dx^{i}\otimes dx^{i}+dy^{i}\otimes dy^{i}\right),

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{0}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}\partial x^{i}+Y_{i}\partial y^{i})+Z\partial z\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i}\partial x^{i}-X_{i}\partial y^{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}y^{i}\partial z,

Example 3.3
.

Let
\overline{M}=(\mathbb{R}_{2}^{7},\overline{g})
be a semi-Euclidean space, where
\overline{g}
is of signature
(-,+,+,-,+,+,+)
with respect to canonical basis

\{\partial x^{1},\partial x^{2},\partial x^{3},\partial y^{1},\partial y^{2},\partial y^{3},\partial z\}.

Suppose
M
is a submanifold of
\mathbb{R}_{2}^{7}
defined by
x^{1}=y^{3}
. It is easy to see that a local frame of
TM
is given by

\displaystyle\xi=2(\partial x^{1}+\partial y^{3}+y^{1}

\displaystyle\partial z),\;\;\;Z_{1}=2(\partial x^{3}-\partial y^{1}+y^{3}\partial z),

\displaystyle Z_{2}=2(\partial x^{2}+y^{2}\partial z),\;\;\;Z_{3}

\displaystyle=2\partial y^{2},\;\;\;\;Z_{4}=\partial x^{3}+\partial y^{1}+y^{3}\partial z,

\displaystyle Z_{6}

Large Time Behavior of Solutions to Hamilton–Jacobi Equations on Networks
Marco Pozza

Abstract
Starting from [ 18 ] \parentext \citefield NamahRoquejoffre99[journaltitle]shortjournal, \bibhyperref [NamahRoquejoffre99] [ 18 ] and [ 10 ] \parentext \citefield Fathi98[journaltitle]shortjournal, \bibhyperref [Fathi98] [ 10 ] , the large time asymptotic behavior of solutions to Hamilton–Jacobi equations has been extensively investigated by many authors, mostly on smooth compact manifolds and the flat torus. They all prove that such solutions converge to solutions to a corresponding static problem. We extend this study to the case where the ambient space is a network. The presence of a “flux limiter”, that is the choice of appropriate constants on each vertex of the network necessary for the well-posedness of time-dependent problems on networks, enables a richer statement for the convergence compared to the classical setting. We indeed observe that solutions converge to subsolutions to a corresponding static problem depending on the value of the flux limiter. A finite time convergence is also established.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:

35B40, 35R02, 49L25, 37J51.

Keywords:

Hamilton–Jacobi equations, large time behavior, Aubry set, embedded networks.

1
Introduction

This paper is about the large time behavior of solutions to time-dependent Hamilton–Jacobi equations posed on networks.

The subject has been extensively investigated on compact manifolds, in particular on the flat torus, first in
[
18
,
10
]
and subsequently in many other papers, among which we cite
[
2
,
20
,
9
,
3
]
. They all show under suitable assumptions, that, given the solution
v
to the time-dependent problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}v+H(x,Dv)=0,\\
&v(x,0)=\phi(x),\end{aligned}\right.

and letting
c
be the critical value of the Hamiltonian
H
, the function
v(x,t)+ct
uniformly converges, as
t
positively diverges, to a solution
u
of the critical equation

H(x,Du)=c.

We consider a connected finite network
\Gamma
embedded in
\mathds{R}^{N}
with vertices linked by regular simple curves
\gamma
parametrized in
[0,1]
, called arcs of
\Gamma
. A Hamiltonian on
\Gamma
is a collection of Hamiltonians
H_{\gamma}:[0,1]\times\mathds{R}\to\mathds{R}
indexed by the arcs, depending on state and momentum variable, with the crucial feature that Hamiltonians associated to arcs possessing different support are totally unrelated.

The equations we deal with are accordingly of the form

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:loctprob}\partial_{t}U(s,t)+H_{\gamma}(s,\partial_{s}U(s,t))=0,\qquad\text{on }(0,1)\times(0,\infty),

(1)

on each arc
\gamma
, and a solution on
\Gamma
is a continuous function
v:\Gamma\times[0,\infty)\to\mathds{R}
such that, for each arc
\gamma
,
v(\gamma(s),t)
solves
Equation

1
in the viscosity sense and satisfies suitable additional conditions on the discontinuity interfaces

\{(x,t),t\in[0,\infty)\}\qquad\text{with }x\in\mathbf{V},

where
\mathbf{V}
denotes the set of vertices. It has been established in
[
13
]
, in the case of junctions, and in
[
22
]
, for general networks, that to get existence and uniqueness of solutions, equations
Equation

1
must be coupled not only with a continuous initial datum at
t=0
, but also with a flux limiter, that is a choice of appropriate constants
c_{x}
on each vertex
x
. In
[
15
,
17
]
, the time-dependent problem is studied on junctions, possibly multidimensional, with Kirchoff type Neumann conditions at vertices, without using flux limiters.

In
[
22
]
flux limiters crucially appear in the conditions that a solution must satisfy on the interfaces and, among other things, bond from above the time derivatives of any subsolution on it. Even if an initial datum is fixed, solutions can change according to the choice of flux limiter, and they actually play a significant role in our analysis. Recently in
[
19
]
it has been given a Lax–Oleinik representation formula for the flux limited solutions to the evolutive problem, extending the result of
[
14
]
where such a formula is given for the case of junctions.

We prove here that, if
v
is a solution to the time-dependent problem on
\Gamma
, then there is a unique constant
a
depending on the flux limiter such that
v(x,t)+at
possesses a uniform limit
u
, as
t
positively diverges,
u
such that
u\circ\gamma
is a viscosity solution to the local problem

H_{\gamma}(s,\partial_{s}U(s,t))=a,\qquad\text{on }(0,1),

for every arc
\gamma
. Under suitable assumptions the value
a
coincides with the critical value of the Hamiltonian. This notion, as well as an extension of Weak KAM theory, has been first studied in
[
23
]
in the framework of networks/graphs.

A relevant peculiarity of the large time behavior problem on network is that the geometry of the network allows, under specific conditions, a finite time convergence. This will be useful for future applications and numerical analysis.

We employ a dynamical approach to the problem exploiting the Lax–Oleinik formula given in
[
19
]
, the dynamic characterization of the solutions of the Eikonal equations and the properties of the Aubry set. To our knowledge there is no previous literature about the large time behavior of solutions to Hamilton–Jacobi equations on networks.

The paper is organized as follows: in
Section

2
we fix some notation and conventions. In
Section

3
we provide some basic facts about networks and Hamiltonians defined on them, and give our main assumptions. In
Section

4
we introduce Eikonal and time-dependent equations, together with some results relevant to our analysis. In
Section

5
we present the results of the asymptotic analysis. We distinguish three main cases according to the values of the flux limiter and the initial datum. In
Section

6
we briefly discuss a characterization of the critical value involving the large time behavior.

In the appendices we provide some auxiliary results. The reparametrization of curves on
\Gamma
and their relationship with the representation formulas is the subject of
Appendix

A
.
Appendix

B
is about the Lipschitz continuity of the minimal action functional. In
Appendix

C
we provide the proof of a technical
Lemma
.

Given an open set

and a continuous function

) to

. See also
[

.

If

its Clarke’s generalized gradient, see
[
7
,
6
]
. We point out that convex functions are locally Lipschitz continuous.

3
Networks

3.1
Basic Definitions

An
embedded network
, or
continuous graph
, is a subset
\Gamma\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
of the form

\Gamma=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{E}}\gamma([0,1])\subset\mathds{R}^{N},

where
\mathcal{E}
is a finite collection of regular (i.e.,
C^{1}
with non-vanishing derivative) simple oriented curves, called
arcs
of the network, that we assume, without any loss of generality, parameterized on
[0,1]
. Note that we are also assuming existence of one-sided derivatives at the endpoints 0 and 1. We stress out that a regular change of parameters does not affect our results.

Observe that on the support of any arc
\gamma
, we also consider the inverse parametrization defined as

\widetilde{\gamma}(s)\coloneqq\gamma(1-s),\qquad\text{for }s\in[0,1].

We call
\widetilde{\gamma}
the
inverse arc
of
\gamma
. We assume

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:nosovrap}\gamma((0,1))\cap\gamma^{\prime}([0,1])=\emptyset,\qquad\text{whenever }\gamma^{\prime}\neq\gamma,\widetilde{\gamma}.

(2)

We call
vertices
the initial and terminal points of the arcs, and denote by
\mathbf{V}
the sets of all such vertices. It follows from
Equation

2
that vertices are the only points where arcs with different support intersect and, in particular,

\gamma((0,1))\cap\mathbf{V}=\emptyset,\qquad\text{for any }\gamma\in\mathcal{E}.

We assume that the network is connected, namely given two vertices there is a finite concatenation of arcs linking them. A
loop
is an arc with initial and final point coinciding. The unique restriction we require on the geometry of the network is

(A1)

\mathcal{E}
does not contain loops.

This condition is due to the fact that in the known literature about time-dependent Hamilton–Jacobi equations on networks no loops are admitted, see, e.g.,
[
13
,
22
,
15
]
.

For each
x\in\mathbf{V}
, we define
\Gamma_{x}\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\mathcal{E}:\gamma(1)=x\}
.

The network
\Gamma
inherits a geodesic distance, denoted with
d_{\Gamma}
, from the Euclidean metric of
\mathds{R}^{N}
. It is clear that given
x
,
y
in
\Gamma
there is at least a geodesic linking them. The geodesic distance is in addition equivalent to the Euclidean one.

We also consider a differential structure on the network by defining the
tangent bundle
of
\Gamma
,
T\Gamma
in symbols, as the set made up by the
(x,q)\in\Gamma\times\mathds{R}^{N}
with
q
of the form

q=\lambda\dot{\gamma}(s),\qquad\text{if $x=\gamma(s)$, $s\in[0,1]$, with $\lambda\in\mathds{R}$}.

Note that
\dot{\gamma}(s)
is univocally determined, up to a sign, if
x\in\Gamma\setminus\mathbf{V}
or in other words if
s\neq 0,1
.

We proceed recalling a result taken from
[
19
]
on this topic.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any given arc
\gamma
and curve
\xi:[0,T]\to\gamma([0,1])
, the function

\gamma^{-1}\circ\xi:[0,T]\to[0,1]

is absolutely continuous, and

\frac{d}{dt}\gamma^{-1}\circ\xi(t)=\frac{\dot{\gamma}\left(\gamma^{-1}\circ\xi(t)\right)\cdot\dot{\xi}(t)}{|\dot{\gamma}(\gamma^{-1}\circ\xi(t))|_{2}^{2}},\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T].

Remark 3.2
.

We notice that, for any given curve
\xi:[0,T]\to\Gamma
, there is an at most countable collection of open disjoint intervals
\{I_{i}\}
with
\bigcup_{i}\overline{I}_{i}=[0,T]
such that

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:curveascomp1}\xi\left(\overline{I}_{i}\right)\subseteq\gamma_{i}([0,1]),\qquad\text{for each index }i,

(3)

where
\gamma_{i}
is an arc of the network. Indeed if
\xi
is constant this is trivial, otherwise we let

E\coloneqq\{t\in[0,T]:\xi(t)\in\mathbf{V}\}.

E
is closed therefore
[
4
, Theorem 1.9]
yields

[0,T]\setminus E=\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}I_{i},

where
\mathcal{I}
is an at most countable index set and
\{I_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
is a collection of open disjoint intervals. Similarly

[0,T]\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\overline{I}_{i}\right)=\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{J}}I_{j},

where
\mathcal{J}
is an at most countable index set and
\{I_{j}\}_{\in\mathcal{J}}
is a collection of open disjoint intervals. It is apparent that
\bigcup\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{J}}\overline{I}_{i}=[0,T]
and
Equation

3
holds.

We point out that each
\eta_{i}\coloneqq\gamma_{i}^{-1}\circ\xi|_{\overline{I}_{i}}
is an absolutely continuous curve by
Lemma

3.1
.

3.2
Hamiltonians on
\Gamma

A Hamiltonian on
\Gamma
is a collection of Hamiltonians
\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{H_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\mathcal{E}}
, where

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}:[0,1]\times\mathds{R}

\displaystyle\>\longrightarrow\>

\displaystyle\mathds{R}

\displaystyle(s,\mu)

\displaystyle\>\longmapsto\>

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}(s,\mu),

satisfying

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:condcompH}H_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(s,\mu)=H_{\gamma}(1-s,-\mu),\qquad\text{for any arc }\gamma.

(4)

We emphasize that, apart the above compatibility condition, the Hamiltonians
H_{\gamma}
are
unrelated
.

We require every
H_{\gamma}
to be:

(H1)

continuous in both arguments;

(H2)

\lim\limits_{|\mu|\to\infty}\inf\limits_{s\in[0,1]}\dfrac{H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)}{|\mu|}=\infty
for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{E}
;

(H3)

convex in
\mu
;

(H4)

strictly quasiconvex in
\mu
, which means that, for any
s\in[0,1]
,
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}
and
\rho\in(0,1)
,

H_{\gamma}\left(s,\rho\mu+(1-\rho)\mu^{\prime}\right)<\max\left\{H_{\gamma}(s,\mu),H_{\gamma}\left(s,\mu^{\prime}\right)\right\}.

If
(H3)
is satisfied, the above assumption is equivalent to

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:sublevelH}\interior\{\mu\in\mathds{R}:H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)\leq a\}=\{\mu\in\mathds{R}:H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)<a\},\qquad\text{for any }a\in\mathds{R},\,s\in[0,1],

(5)

where
\interior
denotes the interior of a set.

Note that if
H_{\gamma}
is strictly convex in the momentum variable then it satisfies both
(H3)
and
(H4)
.

We define the support functions

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:sigmadef}\sigma_{\gamma,a}^{+}(s)\coloneqq\max\{\mu\in\mathds{R}:H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)=a\},\qquad\sigma_{\gamma,a}^{-}(s)\coloneqq\min\{\mu\in\mathds{R}:H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)=a\},

(6)

with the assumption that when
\{\mu\in\mathds{R}:H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)=a\}
is empty
\sigma_{\gamma,a}^{+}(s)=-\infty
and
\sigma_{\gamma,a}^{-}(s)=\infty
. It follows from
Equation

4
that

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:sigcomp}\sigma_{\widetilde{\gamma},a}^{+}(s)=-\sigma_{\gamma,a}^{-}(1-s).

(7)

Notice that
\{\mu\in\mathds{R}:H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)=a\}
is not empty if and only if
a\geq\min\limits_{\mu\in\mathds{R}}H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)
, thus
\sigma_{\gamma,a}^{+}(s)\neq-\infty
for any
s\in[0,1]
if and only if
a\geq a_{\gamma}
, where

a_{\gamma}\coloneqq\max_{s\in[0,1]}\min_{\mu\in\mathds{R}}H_{\gamma}(s,\mu).

Proposition 3.3
.

i)

For each
\gamma\in\mathcal{E}
and
s\in[0,1]
the function
a\mapsto\sigma_{\gamma,a}^{+}(s)
is continuous and increasing in
\left[\min\limits_{\mu\in\mathds{R}}H_{\gamma}(s,\mu),\infty\right)
;

ii)

for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{E}
and
a\geq a_{\gamma}
the function
s\mapsto\sigma_{\gamma,a}^{+}(s)
is continuous in
[0,1]
.

Proof.

(i)
follows from
Equation

5
. For the proof of

see
[
23
, Proposition 5.1]
.
∎

Under assumptions

,
(H2)
and
(H3)
it is natural to define, for any

, the

where the supremum is actually achieved thanks to

. We have for each

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:lagcomp}L_{\gamma}(s,\lambda)=L_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(1-s,-\lambda).

We also derive that the Lagrangians

are superlinear. We will define later on a Lagrangian defined on the whole network, assuming suitable gluing conditions on the

It follows from the definition that, for any

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q:loclagmin}\min_{s\in[0,1]}L_{\gamma}(s,0)=\min_{s\in[0,1]}\max_{\mu\in\mathds{R}}(-H_{\gamma}(s,\mu))=-\max_{s\in[0,1]}\min_{\mu\in\mathds{R}}H_{\gamma}(s,\mu)=-a_{\gamma}.

(9)

Appendix A
Reparametrizations of Curves

Solutions to the time-dependent problem
Equation

H

JE
are given through a Lax–Oleinik type operator, while the solutions to the Eikonal problem (
\mathcal{H}
J
c
) are identified via a Hopf–Lax type formula exploiting the weak KAM theory. It is then clear that in order to perform our asymptotic analysis we need to establish a relationship between these two representation formulas. Following
[
9
,
12
]
, this is done through reparametrizations of curves on
\Gamma
.

In addition to their relevance for the asymptotic analysis, these results are also crucial for the proof of
Proposition

B.3
and consequently of the local Lipschitz continuity of the solutions to the evolutive problem, see
Theorem

4.14
.

Definition A.1
.

Given an absolutely continuous curve
\xi:[0,T]\to\mathds{R}^{N}
, a curve
\zeta:[0,T^{\prime}]\to\mathds{R}^{N}
is called a
reparametrization
of
\xi
if there exists a nondecreasing surjective absolutely continuous function
\psi
from
[0,T^{\prime}]
onto
[0,T]
with

\zeta(t)=\xi\circ\psi(t),\qquad\text{for any }t\in\left[0,T^{\prime}\right].

Note that if
\zeta
is a reparametrization of
\xi
, the converse property in general is not true for
\psi
could have not strictly positive derivative for a.e.
t
, see Zarecki criterion for an absolutely continuous inverse in
[
5
]
. We have that reparametrizations are absolutely continuous:

Lemma A.2
.

[
21
, Corollary 4]
Let
\xi:[0,T]\to\mathds{R}^{N}
be a curve and
\psi:[0,T^{\prime}]\to[0,T]
be absolutely continuous and nondecreasing. Then the reparametrization
\zeta\equiv\xi\circ\psi
of
\xi
is absolutely continuous and

\frac{d}{dt}\zeta(t)=\dot{\xi}(\psi(t))\cdot\dot{\psi}(t),\qquad\text{a.e.\ in }\left[0,T^{\prime}\right].

Lemma A.3
.

If the curve
\zeta:[0,T^{\prime}]\to\Gamma
is a reparametrization of a curve
\xi:[0,T]\to\Gamma
, then

\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\sigma_{a}\left(\zeta,\dot{\zeta}\right)d\tau=\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{a}\left(\xi,\dot{\xi}\right)d\tau,\qquad\text{for every }a\in\mathds{R}.

Proof.

It follows from the definition that
(x,q)\mapsto\sigma_{a}(x,q)
is positively homogeneous on
q
, thus, if we let
\psi
be the nondecreasing absolutely continuous function such that
\zeta\equiv\xi\circ\psi
and consider the change of variable
r=\psi(\tau)
, we get from
Lemma

A.2
that, for every
a\in\mathds{R}
,

\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\sigma_{a}\left(\zeta,\dot{\zeta}\right)d\tau=\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\sigma_{a}\left(\xi\circ\psi,\dot{\xi}\circ\psi\right)\cdot\dot{\psi}(\tau)d\tau=\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{a}\left(\xi,\dot{\xi}\right)dr.

∎

Proposition
comes from classical results of analysis in metric space, see
[
5
]
and
[

, Lemma 3.11]

Proposition A.4
.

Any curve in
[0,T]
is the reparametrization of a curve
\xi
with constant speed, namely with
|\dot{\xi}|_{2}\equiv\mathrm{constant}
a.e., defined on a bounded interval.

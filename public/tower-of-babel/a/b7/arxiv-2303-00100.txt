Rather than working with

Let

We think of

In the characteristic zero setting, the dual group of the integers is isomorphic to the torus

Then define

Note that d-

More generally, for

Any polynomial can be written in the form

where

We say that

if

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{f(a(n))}}=\int_{\mathbb{F}_{p}((t^{-1}))/\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]}{f~dm}

for every continuous function
f:\mathbb{F}_{p}((t^{-1}))/\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]\to\mathbb{C}
and every Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
.
A more refined notion of equidistribution is as follows.
A function

is
well-distributed mod
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
in a subgroup

H\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}((t^{-1}))/\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
if

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{f(a(n))}}=\int_{H}{f~dm_{H}}

for every continuous function
f:\mathbb{F}_{p}((t^{-1}))/\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]\to\mathbb{C}
and every Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
.
For a subgroup
H
and a finite set
F\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}((t^{-1}))/\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
, we say that
a:\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}((t^{-1}))
is

well-distributed in the components of

if there exists

such that,
for every
k\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
, the sequence
\left(a(mn+k)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]}
is well-distributed in

.

We can now state our main result, which is an asymptotic version of Theorem

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
P(y)\in(\mathbb{F}[t])[y]
be a nonconstant polynomial of degree
d
and derivational degree

Write
P(y)=a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\eta_{i}(y^{r_{i}})}

Let

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{f(x+P(y))}-\E_{z\in H_{Q}}{f(x+a_{0}+z)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q})}\leq\left(p^{2\left\lfloor\log_{p}{d}\right\rfloor}\frac{k-1}{\text{lpf}(Q)}\right)^{1/2^{k-1}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q})},

where

.

Remark 1.11
.

(1) In the case

, we get that the average
\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{f(x+P(y))}

is approximated in

which is the projection of

onto the space of

-invariant functions,
so Theorem

can indeed be seen as a finitary version of Theorem

(2) The phenomenon encompassed by Theorem

is simpler in the low degree situation

.
In the context of finite fields (

with

a closely related result was previously established in
[

, Lemma 3]

In particular, it is shown that for any

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)\mathbbm{1}_{B}(x+P(y))}-\frac{|A||B|}{q^{2}}\right|\leq\left(\frac{q^{d-1}-(q-1)^{d-1}}{q^{d-1}}\right)^{1/2^{d-1}}\ll q^{-1/2^{d-1}};

see the statement of Lemma 3 in
[

]
and the formula for

at the top of page 713.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.10
,
we obtain the following power savings for the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem:

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
P(y)\in(\mathbb{F}[t])[y]
be an intersective polynomial of degree
d
and derivational degree
k
.
Let
Q(t)\in\mathbb{F}[t]^{+}
.
If
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
does not contain distinct

with

for some

, then

\displaystyle|A|\ll_{P}|Q|\cdot\text{lpf}(Q)^{-1/2^{k-1}}.

In particular, if
Q
is irreducible (so that
\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
is a field with
|Q|
elements), then

\displaystyle|A|\ll_{P}|Q|^{1-1/2^{k-1}}.

Remark 1.13
.

(1) If we restrict to
\text{lpf}(Q)
being sufficiently large (so that
P(y)
does not reduce to the zero polynomial mod
Q
),
then the implicit constant in the conclusion of Corollary
1.12
depends only on
the degree
d
and the derivational degree
k
.

(2) The bound given in Theorem
1.4
is difficult to compute in general
and to compare with the bound in Corollary
1.12
.
We can, however, highlight some general features of the different bounds.
The power savings obtained in Corollary
1.12
depends only on the derivational degree
k

of the polynomial
P
and applies to all intersective polynomials with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}[t]
.
By contrast, the Li–Sauermann bound depends on the degree
d
of the polynomial
P

and on the characteristic
p
of the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and applies only to polynomials
with zero constant term and with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

The disadvantage of our bound is that the power saving
\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}

decays exponentially with the derivational degree.
How the quantity
t_{p,d}
appearing in Theorem
1.4
depends on
p
and
d

is not immediately clear from the definition.
However, a related bound due to Green
[
G17
, Theorem 1.2]
(which Li and Sauermann optimize)
gives power savings of

\displaystyle c^{\prime}(p,d)=\frac{1}{2d^{2}(p-1)^{2}(1+\log_{p}{d})^{2}\log{p}}

for a degree
d
polynomial
P(y)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[y]
with
P(0)=0
.
That is, for any
q=p^{k}
, the largest subset of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with no nontrivial pattern
\{x,x+P(y)\}
has cardinality

\displaystyle|A|\ll_{p,d}q^{1-c^{\prime}(p,d)}.

Therefore, for fixed
p
, there is a regime of sufficiently high degree polynomials
for which the Li–Sauermann bound beats ours.
On the other hand, the quantity
c^{\prime}(p,d)
decays as
p\to\infty
, so the bound in Corollary
1.12

will outperform this bound in sufficiently high characteristic (for fixed degree
d
).

Thus, neither of the power saving bounds is universally better than the other,
and both methods have their advantages and disadvantages.
The main goal of our work is not to produce the best possible power saving bounds
but to provide a heuristic backing for why any power saving bound should hold at all
and to place the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem over finite fields within the appropriate general framework.

Combining Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
,
we may deduce several finitary combinatorial statements from an infinitary statement about equidistribution.
Say that a polynomial
P(y)\in(\mathbb{F}[t])[y]
is
good for irrational equidistribution
if
\left(P(n)\alpha\right)_{n\in\mathbb{F}[t]}

is well distributed for every irrational
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}((t^{-1}))\setminus\mathbb{F}(t)
.

Proposition 1.14
.

A polynomial
P(y)
is good for irrational equidistribution if and only if
for any totally ergodic system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{F}[t]}\right)
,
any Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{F}[t]
, and any
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{T_{P(n)}f}}=\int_{X}{f~d\mu}.

Proposition
1.14
can be proved along the lines of
Theorem
1.6
outlined earlier using the spectral theorem for unitary actions of
\mathbb{F}[t]
.
For the “only if” direction, upon replacing
T_{n}
by the multiplication operators
(U_{n}h)(x)=e(nx)h(x)

on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{F}((t^{-1}))/\mathbb{F}[t],\sigma\right)
, we use the fact that
P(y)
is good for irrational equidistribution to conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{U_{P(n)}h(x)}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{e(P(n)x)h(x)}}=\begin{cases}h(0),&\text{if}~x=0;\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

in
L^{2}(\sigma)
.
This corresponds to the desired convergence result

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{T_{P(n)}f}}=\int_{X}{f~d\mu}.

For the “if” direction: suppose
P(y)
is not good for irrational equidistribution, and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}((t^{-1}))\setminus\mathbb{F}(t)

such that
\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{e\left(P(y)\alpha\right)}}\neq 0
.
We then take as our totally ergodic system
X=\mathbb{F}((t^{-1}))/\mathbb{F}[t]
,
\mathcal{X}=Borel(X)
,
\mu=m_{X}
, and
T_{n}x=x+n\alpha
.
For the function

, we have
\int_{X}{f(x)~dx}=0
, since
x\mapsto e(x)
is a nontrivial character on
X
, while

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{T_{P(n)}f}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\E_{n\in\Phi_{N}}{e(P(n)\alpha)}}f\neq 0.

Remark 1.15
.

The naive analogue of Proposition

That is, a polynomial

is good for irrational equidistribution

K=K(P,Q,r)\in\mathbb{N}
and
c=c(P,Q,r)>0
such that
for any
k\geq K
and any
r
-coloring
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{C_{i}}
,
there are at least
c(p^{k})^{2}
monochromatic solutions to the equation
P(x)-P(y)=Q(z)
.
That is,

\displaystyle\left|\left\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}^{3}:P(x)-P(y)=Q(z)~\text{and}~\{x,y,z\}\subseteq C_{i}~\text{for some}~i\in\{1,\dots,r\}\right\}\right|\geq c(p^{k})^{2}.

We are in fact able to prove partition regularity under a weaker (but more technically cumbersome) condition on
Q
;
see Corollary
6.7
.
Under this weaker assumption, one application of note is a polynomial Schur theorem over finite fields:

Corollary 1.20
.

Let
P(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
with
P(0)=0
.
Then for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
N=N(P,r)\in\mathbb{N}
and
c=c(P,r)>0

such that if
q>N
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{C_{i}}
,
then there are at least
cq^{2}
monochromatic solutions to the equation
P(x)+P(y)=P(z)
.
In particular, if the coefficients of
P
are not all divisible by the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then there are
\gg_{P,r}q^{2}

monochromatic solutions with
P(x),P(y),P(z)\neq 0
.

Remark 1.21
.

In the case
P(x)=x^{d}
, Corollary
1.20
corresponds to the Fermat equation
x^{d}+y^{d}=z^{d}
.
Schur proved the existence of solutions to the Fermat equation in all
prime fields of sufficiently high characteristic using his eponymous partition regularity theorem in
[
S16
]
.
(This was in fact the original motivation for Schur’s theorem.)
The much stronger property of partition regularity of the Fermat equation
was established previously in the context of prime fields in
[
CGS12
, Theorem 4]

and generalized to a family of related polynomial equations in
[
L18
]
.
We complete the picture here by extending the partition regularity property to arbitrary finite fields
of sufficiently large order (with no assumption on the characteristic).

Related density results for Pythagorean pairs and triples in finite fields were obtained in
[
DLMS23
, Section 6]
,
where they also show that a density version (“density regularity”) of Corollary
1.20

fails already for the Pythagorean equation
x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section

, we prove Theorem

appropriately captures the phenomenon of asymptotic total ergodicity.
Section

The final three sections concern applications of Theorem

Section

deals with power saving bounds in the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem
for intersective polynomials in the presence of asymptotic total ergodicity (Corollary

Section

with further enhancements of the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem
for polynomials with good equidistributional behavior
(Theorems

and

and Section

with partition regularity of polynomial equations over finite fields.
The relevant tools are introduced in the corresponding sections as needed.

3.
Asymptotic projection theorem

We now turn to proving Theorem
1.10
with Fourier analysis.
Characters on
\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
take the form
\chi(x)=e(sx/Q)
for some
s\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
.
For a function
f:\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}\to\mathbb{C}
, we therefore define its Fourier transform
\widehat{f}:\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(s)=\E_{x\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{f(x)e(-sx/Q)}.

We state some basic properties of the Fourier transform

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
f:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{C}
, one has

•

Fourier inversion:

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{\widehat{f}(s)e(sx/Q)}.

•

Parseval’s identity:

\displaystyle\E_{x\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{\left|f(x)\right|^{2}}=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{\left|\widehat{f}(s)\right|^{2}}.

Define
F(x):=\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{f(x+P(y))}-\E_{z\in H_{Q}}{f(x+a_{0}+z)}
.
Then

\displaystyle\widehat{F}(s)=\left(\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e(sP(y)/Q)}-e(sa_{0}/Q)\mathbbm{1}_{H_{Q}^{\perp}}(s)\right)\widehat{f}(s),

where
H_{Q}^{\perp}=\left\{s\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}:e(sz)=1~\text{for all}~z\in H_{Q}\right\}
is the annihilator of the subgroup
H_{Q}
.
Hence, by Parseval’s identity

\displaystyle\E_{x\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{\left|F(x)\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e(sP(y)/Q)}-e(sa_{0}/Q)\mathbbm{1}_{H_{Q}^{\perp}}(s)\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{f}(s)\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{s\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e(sP(y)/Q)}-e(sa_{0}/Q)\mathbbm{1}_{H_{Q}^{\perp}}(s)\right|}\right)^{2}\E_{x\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{|f(x)|^{2}}.

All that remains to show is the inequality:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
P(y)\in(\mathbb{F}[t])[y]
be a nonconstant polynomial of degree
d
and derivational degree
k
.
Write
P(y)=a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\eta_{i}(y^{r_{i}})}
.
Then for any
Q(t)\in\mathbb{F}[t]^{+}
and any
s\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
,

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e(sP(y)/Q)}-e(sa_{0}/Q)\mathbbm{1}_{H_{Q}^{\perp}}(s)\right|^{2^{k-1}}\leq p^{2\left\lfloor\log_{p}{d}\right\rfloor}\frac{k-1}{\text{lpf}(Q)}.

A key ingredient in Proposition
3.2
is the following van der Corput-type inequality.
We do not use any ring structure for this result, so we state and prove it in the setting of an arbitrary finite abelian group
G
.
For a function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
, define a multiplicative differencing operator by
\Delta_{v}f(u)=f(u+v)\overline{f(u)}
,
and let
\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}}f=\Delta_{v_{k}}\left(\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}f\right)

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in G
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, and let
H\leq G
be a subgroup.
For any function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G}{f(x)}\right|^{2^{k}}\leq\E_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in H}{\E_{u\in G}{\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}}f(u)}}

Remark 3.4
.

It is worth commenting on two special cases of Lemma
3.3
.
When
H=G
, the quantity on the right hand side is equal to
\left\|f\right\|_{U^{k}(G)}^{2^{k}}
,
so the conclusion of Lemma
3.3
reduces to the inequality
\left\|f\right\|_{U^{1}(G)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{U^{k}(G)}
,
which is a special case of monotonicity for the Gowers (semi)norms.
On the other hand, when
H=\{0\}
, the right hand side is equal to
\E_{u\in G}{|f(u)|^{2^{k}}}
,
so the conclusion of Lemma
3.3
follows by Jensen’s inequality.
The general case can be seen as interpolating between these two extremes.

Proof.

Suppose
k=1
.
Note that

\displaystyle\E_{x\in G}{f(x)}=\E_{x\in G}{\E_{h\in H}{f(x+h)}}.

Therefore, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G}{f(x)}\right|^{2}=\left|\E_{x\in G}{\E_{h\in H}{f(x+h)}}\right|^{2}\leq\E_{x\in G}{\left|\E_{h\in H}{f(x+h)}\right|^{2}}=\E_{x\in G}{\E_{h_{1},h_{2}\in H}{f(x+h_{1})\overline{f(x+h_{2})}}}.

Interchanging the order of averaging and making the substitutions
v=h_{1}-h_{2}
,
u=x-h_{2}
,
we obtain the desired inequality

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G}{f(x)}\right|^{2}\leq\E_{v\in H}{\E_{u\in G}{f(u+v)\overline{f(u)}}}.

Suppose the inequality holds for
k-1
.
Then

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G}{f(x)}\right|^{2^{k}}=\left(\left|\E_{x\in G}{f(x)}\right|^{2^{k-1}}\right)^{2}\leq\left|\E_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}\in H}{\E_{u\in G}{\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}f(u)}}\right|^{2},

which is in turn bounded above by

\displaystyle\E_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}\in H}{\left|\E_{u\in G}{\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}f(u)}\right|^{2}}.

For fixed
v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}
, applying the
k=1
case with the function
\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}f
gives

\displaystyle\left|\E_{u\in G}{\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}f(u)}\right|^{2}\leq\E_{v_{k}\in H}{\E_{u\in G}{\Delta_{v_{k}}\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}f(x)}}=\E_{v_{k}\in H}{\E_{u\in G}{\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1},v_{k}}f(x)}}.

Putting everything together,

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G}{f(x)}\right|^{2^{k}}\leq\E_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in H}{\E_{u\in G}{\Delta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}}f(u)}}.

∎

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

We first make a reduction to separable polynomials.
If
s\in H_{Q}^{\perp}
, then
e(sP(y)/Q)=e(sa_{0}/Q)e(s(P(y)-a_{0})/Q)=e(sa_{0}/Q)
for every
y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
,
since
P(y)-a_{0}\in H_{Q}
.
Suppose now that
s\notin H_{Q}^{\perp}
.
We want to show

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e\left(sP(y)/Q\right)}\right|^{2^{k-1}}\leq p^{2\left\lfloor\log_{p}{d}\right\rfloor}\frac{k-1}{\text{lpf}(Q)}.

Noting that
H_{Q}^{\perp}=\cap_{i=1}^{n}{H_{i,Q}^{\perp}}
, we have

\displaystyle I=\left\{1\leq i\leq n:s\notin H_{i,Q}^{\perp}\right\}\neq\emptyset.

Moreover, for any
y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
,

\displaystyle e\left(sP(y)/Q\right)=e(sa_{0}/Q)e\left(s\sum_{i\in I}{\eta_{i}(y^{r_{i}})}/Q\right).

Now, for each
i\in I
, the function
\chi_{i}(x)=e(s\eta_{i}(x)/Q)
is a nontrivial character on
\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
,
so there exists
s_{i}\neq 0
such that
\chi_{i}(x)=e(s_{i}x/Q)
.
It therefore suffices to prove the following:
for any nonconstant separable polynomial
P(y)\in(\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q})[y]
,

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e\left(P(y)/Q\right)}\right|^{2^{k-1}}\leq p^{2\left\lfloor\log_{p}{d}\right\rfloor}\frac{k-1}{\text{lpf}(Q)}.

Suppose
k=1
.
Then
P(y)=s_{1}y+s_{0}\in(\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q})[y]
with
s_{1}\neq 0
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e(P(y)/Q)}=e(s_{0}/Q)\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e(s_{1}y/Q)}=0.

Now suppose
k\geq 2
.
Let
P(y)=\sum_{i=i}^{n}{s_{i}y^{r_{i}}}+P^{\prime}(y)
, where d-
\deg{y^{r_{i}}}=k
and d-

.
By Lemma

,

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e(P(y)/Q)}\right|^{2^{k-1}}\leq\E_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}\in K}{\E_{u\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e\left(\partial_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}P(u)/Q\right)}}

for any subgroup
K\leq(\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q},+)
.
(We will take a convenient choice for
K

We now wish to obtain an expression for
\partial_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}}P(u)
that will allow us to bound the avaerage

\displaystyle\E_{u}{e\left(s\partial_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}P(u)/Q\right)}.

For
v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
, one has that
\partial_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}P^{\prime}(u)
is constant (as a function of
u

since d-

e\left(s\partial_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}P^{\prime}(u)\right)

outside of the average.

Let
m=\left\lfloor\log_{p}{d}\right\rfloor
so that
p^{m}\leq d
and
p^{m+1}>d
.
For each
i=1,\dots,n
, we may write
r_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{c_{i,j}p^{j}}

with
c_{i,j}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
and
\sum_{j=0}^{m}{c_{i,j}}=k
.
Since
P
is separable by assumption, we have
c_{i,0}\neq 0
for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\partial_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}(y^{r_{i}})=b_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{S_{i,j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})u^{p^{j}}}+R_{i}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}),

where

,
S_{i,j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{k-1}{v_{l}^{p^{j_{l}}}}

with

\displaystyle\left|\left\{1\leq l\leq k-1:j_{l}=j^{\prime}\right\}\right|=\begin{cases}c_{i,j^{\prime}},&\text{if}~j^{\prime}\neq j\\
c_{i,j}-1,&\text{if}~j^{\prime}=j\end{cases}

and
R_{i}
is a symmetric polynomial in
k-1
variables.
(If
c_{i,j}=0
, then
S_{i,j}=0
.)
We can therefore write

\displaystyle\partial_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}P(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{s_{i}b_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{S_{i,j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})u^{p^{j}}}}+R(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{s_{i}b_{i}S_{i,j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})}\right)u^{p^{j}}}+R(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}),

Let

\displaystyle\eta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{s_{i}b_{i}S_{i,j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})}\right)u^{p^{j}}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\E_{u}{e\left(\partial_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}P(u)/Q\right)}\right|=\left|\E_{u}{e\left(\eta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}(u)/Q\right)}\right|=0

Noting that
e\left(\eta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}(\cdot)/Q\right)
is a character on
\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
,
it may be written in the form
e\left(\varphi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})u/Q\right)

for some
\varphi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}
.
We have thus obtained the bound

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}}{e(P(y)/Q)}\right|^{2^{k-1}}\leq\frac{\left|\left\{(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})\in K^{k-1}:\varphi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})=0\right\}\right|}{|K|^{k-1}}.

First, we show that, for a convenient choice of

,

k-1
variables.
Next, we establish a bound on the number of roots of multivariable polynomials mod
Q
.

Recall
m=\left\lfloor\log_{p}{d}\right\rfloor
.
Let
K=\{x^{p^{m}}:x\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}\}
.
This is a subgroup, since the function
x\mapsto x^{p^{m}}
is a homomorphism.
For
0\leq j\leq m

C_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{s_{i}b_{i}S_{i,j}}
so that

\displaystyle\eta_{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{C_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})u^{p^{j}}},

and each of the polynomials

is an additive polynomial

in each coordinate.
In particular,
v_{i}\mid C_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})
for each

.
Making the substitution

\displaystyle\eta_{w_{1}^{p^{m}},\dots,w_{k-1}^{p^{m}}}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{C_{j}\left(w_{1}^{p^{m}},\dots,w_{k-1}^{p^{m}}\right)u^{p^{j}}}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{\widetilde{C}_{j}\left(w_{1},\dots,w_{k-1}\right)w_{1}^{p^{j}}\dots w_{k-1}^{p^{j}}u^{p^{j}}}

For each

\displaystyle\psi(w_{1},\dots,w_{k-1})=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{z_{j}\widetilde{C}_{j}(w_{1},\dots,w_{k-1})w_{1}\dots w_{k-1}}.

\displaystyle C_{0}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{s_{i}b_{i}S_{i,0}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})}.

Let

, and let

\displaystyle\left|\left\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{l})\in\mathbb{F}[t]_{Q}^{l}:T(y_{1},\dots,y_{l})=0\right\}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}{d_{i}}\right)\frac{|Q|^{l}}{\text{lpf}(Q)}.

Proof of Lemma.

Let us first consider the case

We view
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{d}
as elements of

.
Let

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[y]
be additive polynomials, and let

\eta=\eta_{1}\circ\zeta_{1}+\eta_{2}\circ\zeta_{2}

If

Suppose now that
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are both nonzero.
Let
H=H_{1}+H_{2}
.
Write
\eta_{1}(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}{a_{i}y^{p^{i}}}

\eta_{2}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}{b_{j}y^{p^{j}}}

Without loss of generality,

Define

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}_{1}(y)=b_{l}\eta_{1}(y)-a_{k}\eta_{2}\left(y^{p^{k-l}}\right)=\eta_{1}(b_{l}y)+\eta_{2}\left(-a_{k}y^{p^{k-l}}\right),

.
Then
\deg{\eta^{\prime}_{1}}<\deg{\eta_{1}}
.

Claim

.

For any

, (

) expressed
\eta^{\prime}_{1}(y)

and an element of

.

Rearranging (

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(y)=b_{l}^{-1}\eta^{\prime}_{1}(y)+b_{l}^{-1}a_{k}\eta_{2}\left(y^{p^{k-l}}\right).

6.
Partition regularity of polynomial equations over finite fields

In this section, we deduce additional applications of Theorem

to partition regularity of polynomial equations over finite fields.
As a first step, we observe the following criterion for the existence of solutions
to polynomial equations of the form

over finite fields:

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\in(\mathbb{F}[t])[x]
be nonconstant polynomials with
P_{i}(0)=0
.
Let
d_{i}=\deg{P_{i}}
and
k_{i}=\text{d-}\deg{P_{i}}
.
Let
H_{q,i}=\left\langle P_{i}(x):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\rangle\leq(\mathbb{F}_{q},+)
, and let
H_{q}=H_{q,1}+H_{q,2}+H_{q,3}
.
Then for any

,

\displaystyle\left|\left\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}:P_{1}(x)+P_{2}(y)+P_{3}(z)=c\right\}\right|=\frac{q^{3}}{|H_{q}|}+O_{q\to\infty;d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\left(q^{2-\gamma}\right),

.
In particular, if

is sufficiently large, then

\displaystyle P_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})+P_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})+P_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=H_{q}.

Remark 6.2
.

The main term,
\frac{q^{3}}{|H_{q}|}

There are
q^{3}

(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
and
|H_{q}|
possible values

that are free from obvious algebraic obstructions to solvability of
P_{1}(x)+P_{2}(y)+P_{3}(z)=c
.
Proposition
6.1
states that, asymptotically,
the polynomial
P_{1}(x)+P_{2}(y)+P_{3}(z)
takes on each such value of
c
with roughly equal frequency.

Proof.

For any prime power
q
, any
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and any functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
let

denote the number of solutions

\sum_{i=1}^{r}{f_{i}(x_{i})}=c
.
Our goal is to show

\displaystyle N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=\frac{q^{3}}{|H_{q}|}+O_{q\to\infty}\left(q^{2-\gamma}\right)

for

.

For each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, let
\eta_{i}
be an additive polynomial with

.

Claim

N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},\eta_{3})+O_{q\to\infty;d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},k_{3}}\left(q^{2-1/2^{k_{3}-1}}\right)

.
Then

\displaystyle N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f_{1}(x)f_{2}(c-x-P_{3}(y))}=q^{2}\E_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f_{1}(x)f_{2}(c-x-P_{3}(y))}.

\displaystyle N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},\eta_{3})=q^{2}\E_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f_{1}(x)f_{2}(c-x-\eta_{3}(y))}=q^{2}\E_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\E_{z\in H_{q,3}}{f_{1}(x)f_{2}(c-x-z)}}.

\displaystyle\left|N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})-N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},\eta_{3})\right|\leq q^{2}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f_{2}(c-x-P_{3}(y))}-\E_{z\in H_{q,3}}{f_{2}(c-x-z)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}.

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f_{2}(c-x-P_{3}(y))}-\E_{z\in H_{q,3}}{f_{2}(c-x-z)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\ll_{d_{3},k_{3}}q^{-1/2^{k_{3}-1}}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}.

Finally, for each

, the polynomial equation

has at most

,

\displaystyle N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},\eta_{3})+O_{q\to\infty;d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},k_{3}}\left(q^{2-1/2^{k_{3}-1}}\right)

as claimed.

Applying the claim also to

and

\displaystyle N(q,c;P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=N(q,c;\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3})+O_{q\to\infty;d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\left(q^{2-\gamma}\right).

Let

\eta(x,y,z)=\eta_{1}(x)+\eta_{2}(y)+\eta_{3}(z)

Then

is a group homomorphism with image

.
Therefore,

is constant in

, so

\displaystyle N(q,c;\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3})=\frac{\left|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}\right|}{|H_{q}|}=\frac{q^{3}}{|H_{q}|}.

∎

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(3)}}{{=}}\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}{\E_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{n}}}^{s+1}}{\mathbbm{1}_{A^{(n)}}(\mathbf{x})\mathbbm{1}_{A^{(n)}}\left(T^{(n)}_{y}\mathbf{x}\right)}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(4)}}{{\geq}}\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}{\left(\frac{|A^{(n)}|}{p^{sk_{n}}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(5)}}{{=}}\mu^{s}(A)^{2}>0.

The steps are justified as follows.
Step (1) is a direct application of Proposition
6.5
.
The equality (2) comes from the definition of the Loeb measure
\mu^{s+1}
.
In step (3), we have taken the limit of both sides of (
6.1
) along
\mathcal{U}
.
The inequality (4) holds for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle\E_{(\mathbf{x},y)\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{n}}}^{s+1}}{\mathbbm{1}_{A^{(n)}}(\mathbf{x})\mathbbm{1}_{A^{(n)}}\left(T^{(n)}_{y}\mathbf{x}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A^{(n)}},\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{n}}}}{T^{(n)}_{y}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{(n)}}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{n}}}}{T^{(n)}_{y}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{(n)}}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{n}}}^{s}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\left|A^{(n)}\right|}{p^{sk_{n}}}\right)^{2}.

Finally, (5) follows from the definition of the Loeb measure
\mu^{s}
.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mu\left(\left\{z\in\mathbb{F}_{\infty}:\mu^{s}\left(A\cap T_{Q(z)}A\right)>0\right\}\right)>0.

Let
G=\left\{z\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}{C_{i}}:\mu^{s}\left(A\cap T_{Q(z)}A\right)>0\right\}
.
Since the set
\bigcup_{i=s+1}^{r}{C_{i}}
has Loeb measure zero,
\mu(G)>0
.

For
z\in G
, let
i(z)\in\{1,\dots,s\}
such that
z\in C_{i(z)}
.
Noting that

\displaystyle\mu^{s}\left(A\cap T_{Q(z)}A\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}{\mu\left(A_{i}\cap(A_{i}+Q(z))\right)},

it follows that

\displaystyle\mu\left(A_{i(z)}\cap\left(A_{i(z)}+Q(z)\right)\right)>0.

The set
A_{i(z)}
lies in the image of
P
by definition, so taking the inverse image under
P
,

\displaystyle\mu\left(C_{i(z)}\cap P^{-1}\left(A_{i(z)}+Q(z)\right)\right)>0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mu_{V}(M)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}{\frac{|M_{n}|}{|V_{n}|}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}{\frac{|M_{n}|}{(p^{k_{n}})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}{\frac{1}{(p^{k_{n}})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{n}}}}{\mathbbm{1}_{C_{n,i}}(z)\left|\left\{(x,y)\in C_{n,i}^{2}:P(x)-P(y)=Q(z)\right\}\right|}}}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\E_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{n}}}}{\mathbbm{1}_{C_{n,i}}(z)\frac{\left|C_{n,i}\cap P^{-1}\left(A_{n,i}+Q(z)\right)\right|}{p^{k_{n}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\int_{\mathbb{F}_{\infty}}{\mathbbm{1}_{C_{i}}(z)\mu\left(C_{i}\cap P^{-1}\left(A_{i}+Q(z)\right)\right)}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{G}{\mu\left(C_{i(z)}\cap P^{-1}\left(A_{i(z)}+Q(z)\right)\right)~d\mu(z)}

\displaystyle>0.

This final inequality contradicts Claim 2, so the theorem follows by
reductio ad absurdum
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.19
is sufficiently flexible as to allow for many variations.
We note that in the proof of Theorem
1.19
, we do not use the full strength of the equidistribution assumption on
Q
.
Carefully following each step in the proof reveals the following characterization of polynomials
Q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
for which
the conclusion of Theorem
1.19
holds:

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
Q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.
Write
Q(x)=Q(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\eta_{i}\left(x^{r_{i}}\right)}
with
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
distinct with
p\nmid r_{i}

and
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
additive polynomials.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
H^{(k)}_{i}=\eta_{i}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}})
and
H^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{H^{(k)}_{i}}
.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
Q(0)\in H^{(k)}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
;

(ii)

for any nonconstant polynomial
P(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
and any
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exist
K=K(P,r)\in\mathbb{N}
and
c(P,r)>0
such that for any
k\geq K

and any
r
-coloring
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{C_{i}}
,
there are at least
c\left(p^{k}\right)^{2}
monochromatic solutions to the equation
P(x)-P(y)=Q(z)
.

Remark 6.8
.

Condition (i) in Corollary
6.7
holds for intersective

;
see the first two lines in the proof of Corollary

.

Another feature of the proof of Theorem
1.19
is the following.
Taking the ultraproduct of a sequence of finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with characteristic growing to infinity,
the same method shows that for any nonconstant polynomials
P(x),Q(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,
the equation
P(x)-P(y)=Q(z)
is partition regular over all fields of sufficiently high characteristic.
This follows by noting that
P
and
Q
will be nonconstant and separable
(hence good for irrational equidistribution; see Example
1.16
(1) above)
once the characteristic exceeds the degrees of
P
and
Q
and the size of some nonconstant coefficient.

In the special case
P=Q
, Theorem
1.19
can be seen as a
polynomial version of Schur’s theorem over finite fields.
Indeed, the classical theorem of Schur asserts that the equation
x+y=z
is partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
.
We have just established partition regularity of the equation
P(x)+P(y)=P(z)
over finite fields
whenever
P
satisfies the equidistribution assumption in item (i) of Corollary
6.7
.
While the condition (i) in Corollary
6.7
depends on the characteristic
p
,
it is automatically satisfied for polynomials with zero constant term.
Hence, Corollary
1.20
holds.

The equation
P(x)-P(y)=Q(z)
is often not partition regular (and may not even be solvable) over
\mathbb{N}
.
A key fact leveraged in the proof of Theorem
1.19
is that polynomials
take on a positive proportion of values in finite fields, something that is far from the case in
\mathbb{N}
.
It remains an interesting and difficult open problem, asked by Erdős and Graham in
[
EG80
]
,
whether the Pythagorean equation
x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
is partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
.
(This was settled with a computer-assisted proof in the case of 2-colorings in
[
HKM16
]
but is wide open for 3 or more colors.)

Some comments are in order on the use of ultraproducts in the proofs of the aforementioned partition regularity results.
The basic strategy we have taken is to discard those colors that have zero Loeb measure in the ultraproduct
and then to use recurrence along the polynomial
Q
to find the desired points
x,y,z
with
P(x)-P(y)=Q(z)
.
One may be tempted to carry out this strategy in purely finitary terms, avoiding the use of ultraproducts and Loeb measure.
Unfortunately, this does not work (at least in its most straightforward implementation).
The following discussion illuminates the issues that arise.
Fix a polynomial
Q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
that is good for irrational equidistribution.
For simplicity, we will consider
P(x)=x
.
Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose
k\in\mathbb{N}
is large and an
r
-coloring
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{C_{i}}
is given.
We wish to use a function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\to\left[0,\frac{1}{r}\right]
as a cutoff for distinguishing “large” from “small” color classes.
That is, we will consider a color class
C_{i}
large if
|C_{i}|\geq\Phi(k)p^{k}
and small if
|C_{i}|<\Phi(k)p^{k}
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
C_{1},\dots,C_{s}
are large and
C_{s+1},\dots,C_{r}
are small
for some
s\in\{1,\dots,r\}
.
(The requirement that
\Phi(k)\leq\frac{1}{r}
guarantees that at least one color class is large.)
We now proceed as in the proof of Theorem
1.19
, using “large” as a replacement for having positive Loeb measure.
Let
A=C_{1}\times\dots\times C_{s}
.
Proposition
6.3
gives the bound

\displaystyle\E_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}}{\frac{\left|A\cap\left(A+\left(Q(z),\dots,Q(z)\right)\right)\right|}{p^{sk}}}\geq\left(\frac{|A|}{p^{sk}}\right)^{2}+O_{k\to\infty}\left(p^{-\gamma k}\right)

where
\gamma=2^{-(\text{d-}\deg{Q-1)}}
.
Since
\left|A\cap\left(A+\left(Q(z),\dots,Q(z)\right)\right)\right|\leq|A|
for each
z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}:A\cap\left(A+\left(Q(z),\dots,Q(z)\right)\right)\neq\emptyset\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p^{(s+1)k}}{|A|}\E_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}}{\frac{\left|A\cap\left(A+\left(Q(z),\dots,Q(z)\right)\right)\right|}{p^{sk}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|A|}{p^{(s-1)k}}+O_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{p^{(s+1)k}}{|A|}p^{-\gamma k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\Phi(k)^{s}p^{k}+O_{k\to\infty}\left(\Phi(k)^{-s}p^{(1-\gamma)k}\right),

where in the last step we have used the bound
|A|\geq\Phi(k)^{s}p^{sk}
.
In order to complete the argument, we want to find
z\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}{C_{i}}

satisfying
A\cap\left(A+\left(Q(z),\dots,Q(z)\right)\right)\neq\emptyset
.
To that end, one would like to show

\displaystyle\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}:A\cap\left(A+\left(Q(z),\dots,Q(z)\right)\right)\neq\emptyset\right\}\right|>\left|\bigcup_{i=s+1}^{r}{C_{i}}\right|.

The total size of the small color classes is bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{i=s+1}^{r}{C_{i}}\right|\leq(r-1)\Phi(k)p^{k}.

The goal, then, is to choose the function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\to\left[0,\frac{1}{r}\right]
so that

\displaystyle\Phi(k)^{s}p^{k}>(r-1)\Phi(k)p^{k}+O_{k\to\infty}\left(\Phi(k)^{-s}p^{(1-\gamma)k}\right).

Dividing by
p^{k}
, this reduces to the inequality

\displaystyle\Phi(k)^{s}>(r-1)\Phi(k)+O_{k\to\infty}\left(\Phi(k)^{-s}p^{-\gamma k}\right).

But for
r\geq 2
, this requires
\Phi(k)>1
, which violates the condition that
0\leq\Phi(k)\leq\frac{1}{r}
.

Working with the ultraproduct allows us to replace “small” with measure zero.
This is crucial, as we have just seen that “small” contributions in the finitary setting may accumulate
and overtake individual “large” terms.
In contrast, finite unions of measure zero sets remain of measure zero.
However, our infinitary methods come at a cost:
we are unable to provide any quantitative control on the values

K
and
c
appearing in the statement of Theorem
1.19
and related corollaries.
It is therefore an interesting problem to obtain a purely finitary proof of Theorem
1.19
with effective bounds.

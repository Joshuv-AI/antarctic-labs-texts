Maximal stable lattices in representations over discretely valued fields
Amit Ophir and Ariel Weiss

Abstract
. Let ρ : G → GL n ⁡ ( K ) \rho\colon G\to\GL_{n}(K) be an continuous irreducible representation of a compact group over a complete discretely valued field K K . Let W i , W j W_{i},W_{j} be two irreducible subrepresentations of ρ ¯ s ​ s \overline{\rho}^{ss} , the semisimplification of the residual representation. We study the structure of the G G -stable lattices Λ ⊆ K n \Lambda\subseteq K^{n} with a view to understanding the question of when ρ \rho realises a non-split extension of W i W_{i} by W j W_{j} . In particular, we introduce the notion of a maximal G G -stable lattice and prove that any non-split extension of W i W_{i} by W j W_{j} that can be realised by ρ \rho can also be realised by a maximal lattice. As applications, we give a new proof and a strengthening of Bellaïche’s generalisation of Ribet’s Lemma [ 4 ] , which assures the abundancy of non-split extensions that can be realised by ρ \rho . On the other hand, we also show that, if the representations W i , W j W_{i},W_{j} occur with multiplicity one in ρ ¯ s ​ s \overline{\rho}^{ss} , then ρ \rho can realise at most one non-split extension of W i W_{i} by W j W_{j} .

Introduction

In his celebrated 1976 article
[
11

A key component of his method is the so-called

, uniformiser
\pi

such that the semisimplification of the residual representation
\overline{\rho}^{ss}

\overline{\rho}^{ss}=\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2}

[

*Prop. 2.1 asserts that there is a

More generally, suppose that

1.1.

Throughout this section, let

2.1.
The Bruhat–Tits building of
\PGL(V)

Recall that a lattice
\Lambda

\mathcal{O}_{K}

Two lattices

Given a homothety class

Definition 2.1

(Bruhat–Tits building of

)
.

The set

is equipped with a structure of an abstract simplicial complex as follows:

•

The vertices are the points of
\mathcal{X}(V)
.

•

A collection of

As a simplicial complex,

In particular,

is connected and pure of dimension

Definition 2.2
.

Let

.
The

is the collection of all vertices represented by lattices of the form

Apartments are connected subcomplexes of

Let
\Lambda\subseteq V

We say that

For each nonzero vector

We equip

Definition 3.2

Fix a nonzero vector

(i)

We say that a

is maximal in

(ii)

We say that a vertex

3.1.
Existence of maximal vertices

We first show that, for every nonzero
v\in V
, there exists at least one lattice that is maximal with respect to
v
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
(V,\rho)
is irreducible.
Let
v\in V
be a nonzero vector.

(i)

Any strictly increasing sequence of lattices in
\mathcal{L}_{v}
is finite.

(ii)

There exists a maximal lattice with respect to
v
.
Moreover, any invariant lattice
\Lambda
that is normalised at
v
is contained in a maximal lattice with respect to
v
.

Proof.

Part
(ii)
is a simple consequence of part
(i)
, which we now prove. Let

\Lambda_{0}\subsetneq\Lambda_{1}\subsetneq\Lambda_{2}\subsetneq\dots

be a strictly increasing sequence of lattices in
\mathcal{L}_{v}
.
Since
V
is irreducible, if
W
is a nonzero
\mathcal{O}_{K}[G]
-submodule of
V
, then
W\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}[G]}K[G]=V
. Hence,
W
is either a lattice or the whole of
V
.
Therefore, the
\mathcal{O}_{K}[G]
-module
L=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\Lambda_{k}
, which is nonzero and does not contain
\pi^{-1}v
, is a lattice.
Since any two lattices in
V
are commensurable, there exists an integer
m
such that
\Lambda_{0}\subseteq L\subseteq\pi^{-m}\Lambda_{0}
.
The ring
R=\mathcal{O}_{K}/\pi^{m}\mathcal{O}_{K}
is Noetherian, and
\pi^{-m}\Lambda_{0}/\Lambda_{0}
is a finitely generated module over
R
, so it is a Noetherian module.
It follows that the sequence

\Lambda_{0}/\Lambda_{0}\subsetneq\Lambda_{1}/\Lambda_{0}\subsetneq\Lambda_{2}/\Lambda_{0}\subsetneq\dots

is finite, and therefore that the sequence
\Lambda_{0}\subsetneq\Lambda_{1}\subsetneq\Lambda_{2}\subsetneq\dots
is finite as well.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
v\in V
be a nonzero vector and let
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{v}
be a
\rho(G)
-invariant lattice that is normalised at
v
.
The following are equivalent.

(i)

\Lambda
is maximal with respect to
v
.

(ii)

The socle of
\Lambda/\pi\Lambda
is irreducible and generated by the image of
v
.

Proof.

Assume
(i)
. Let
\overline{v}
be the image of
v
in
\Lambda/\pi\Lambda
. Then
\overline{v}
is nonzero by definition.
It is sufficient to show that any nonzero subrepresentation of
\Lambda/\pi\Lambda
contains
\overline{v}
.

Let
U\subseteq\Lambda/\pi\Lambda
be a subrepresentation not containing
\overline{v}
.
We will show that
U=0
.
Let
\Lambda^{\prime}
be the inverse image of
U
under the quotient map
\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda/\pi\Lambda
. Then
\pi\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda
and by definition,
v\notin\Lambda^{\prime}
. Since
\pi v\in\pi\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
, we see that
v\in\pi^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
, so that the lattice
\pi^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}
is normalised at
v
and contains
\Lambda
.
By the maximality of
\Lambda
with respect to
v
,
\pi^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda
.
Therefore,
U=0
.

Assume
(ii)
.
Let
\Lambda^{\prime}
be an invariant lattice containing
\Lambda
and normalised at
v
.
Consider the map

\varphi\colon\Lambda/\pi\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda^{\prime}/\pi\Lambda^{\prime}

induced by the inclusion
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
. We will show that
\varphi
is an isomorphism, whence
\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda
.

Since
\varphi
is a map of
\mathbf{F}
-vector spaces of the same dimension, it is sufficient to show that it is injective. Let
\overline{v}
denote the image of

. Then
\varphi(\overline{v})
is the image of

\Lambda^{\prime}/\pi\Lambda^{\prime}
which is, by assumption, nonzero. Hence
\overline{v}\notin\ker(\varphi)

\Lambda/\pi\Lambda
contains
\overline{v}

. So

is maximal with respect to

.
∎

We say that a sharp subquotient

lies above an irreducible subquotient

and

.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be an irreducible subquotient of
\overline{V}
.

(i)

There exists a sharp subquotient lying above
(V_{1},V_{2})
.

(ii)

Let
(V_{1}^{\prime},V_{2}^{\prime})
be a sharp subquotient lying above
(V_{1},V_{2})
.
Then

Proof.

(i)

Let

be a maximal subrepresentation of
\overline{V}
containing
V_{1}
but not containing

.
Denote
V^{\prime}_{2}=V_{1}^{\prime}+V_{2}
.
Then
(V_{1}^{\prime},V_{2}^{\prime})

(V_{1},V_{2})
.

(ii)

The map

strictly contains

∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

.

Let

.
Consider the irreducible subquotients

, for

Now suppose that

Lemma 3.10

∎

It follows that the map

Proof.

Let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be sharp subquotient of

Let

4.
Applications

One of the key applications of invariant lattices, first pioneered by Ribet
[
11
]
, is to construct interesting representations of the group
G
over the residue field
\mathbf{F}
.

Let
\rho\colon G\to\GL(V)
be a representation. Given a stable lattice
\Lambda\subseteq V
, we can view
\rho
as a representation
\rho\colon G\to\GL(\Lambda)
, and obtain the residual representation
\overline{\rho}_{\Lambda}\colon G\to\GL(\Lambda/\pi\Lambda)
. In general, the isomorphism class of
\overline{\rho}_{\Lambda}
depends on
\Lambda
, but its semisimplification,

is independent of all choices. We assume that
\overline{\rho}^{ss}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}W_{i}^{m_{i}}
, where the
W_{i}
’s are distinct, irreducible representations of
G
over
\mathbf{F}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\Ext_{\mathbf{F}[G]}(W_{i},W_{j})
be a class of non-split extensions of
W_{i}
by
W_{j}
, for some
1\leq i,j\leq r
. We say that an invariant lattice
\Lambda\subseteq V

realises

\alpha
if there exist subrepresentations
U_{0}\subseteq U_{1}\subseteq\Lambda/\pi\Lambda
such that
U_{1}/U_{0}
fits into a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow W_{j}\rightarrow U_{1}/U_{0}\rightarrow W_{i}\rightarrow 0

that defines the extension class
\alpha
.
We say that
\rho
realises
\alpha
if some invariant lattice
\Lambda\subset V
realises
\alpha
.

Let
U
be a representation of
G
over
\mathbf{F}
, and suppose that it fits into a short exact sequence

(4.1)

0\rightarrow W_{j}\xrightarrow{f}U\xrightarrow{g}W_{i}\rightarrow 0.

Then we denote by
[U,f,g]
the extension class in
\Ext_{\mathbf{F}[G]}(W_{i},W_{j})
defined by
Equation

4.1
.
In general, the maps
f
and
g
are not unique: the map
f
is only unique up to the action of
\Aut_{G}(W_{j})
, and the map
g
is only unique up to the action of
\Aut_{G}(W_{i})
.

Definition 4.2
.

We say that two extension classes
[U,f,g]
and
[U^{\prime},f^{\prime},g^{\prime}]
are
equivalent
if
U\cong U^{\prime}
and there are automorphisms
\varphi\in\Aut_{G}(W_{i})
and
\psi\in\Aut_{G}(W_{j})
such that

and
g=\psi\circ g^{\prime}
.

4.1.
Uniqueness of extensions realised by modular representations

In this subsection, we show that if

is a non-split extension of

by

, for some

, that can be realised by

can also be realised by a maximal lattice. As a consequence, if

and

occur in

with multiplicity one, we prove that

can realise at most one non-split extension of

by

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
1\leq i\leq r
.

(i)

There exists

such that
\soc(\Lambda_{x}/\pi\Lambda_{x})\simeq W_{i}

(ii)

If

, there is a unique

such that
\soc(\Lambda_{x}/\pi\Lambda_{x})\simeq W_{i}
.

Proof.

(i)
. Choose any invariant lattice
\Lambda
.
Since
W_{i}
appears in the semi-simplification of
\Lambda/\pi\Lambda

(V_{1},V_{2})
of

.

From the definition of

(ii)
. Let

be two invariant lattices such that

By scaling, we may assume that

The map

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
\rho

.

Proof.

Let

.
Then there exist subrepresentations

.
The subquotient

, there exists a sharp subquotient
(V_{1}^{\prime},V_{2}^{\prime})
lying over
(V_{1},V_{2})

.
Moreover, we have

In this section, we prove

We say that a subrepresentation

of

occurs in the

is a subrepresentation of
\frac{\soc^{i}(\overline{V})}{\soc^{i-1}(\overline{V})}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\overline{V}
be a representation of

over

. Let

and suppose that

-th level of the socle filtration of

-th level of the socle filtration of

, and representations

.

Quotienting by
\soc^{i-1}(\overline{V})
, we may assume that

4.3.
Geometry of maximal vertices

In
[
15
]
, Suh studies the extremal vertices of
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
, and shows two
abundancy results: a lower bound on the number of extremal vertices, and a geometric result that says that
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
is the convex hull of its extremal vertices.

The goal of this section is to prove stronger versions of these two results by re-framing them in terms of maximal lattices. We first note that every maximal lattice is an extremal lattice. We then give a lower bound on the number of maximal lattices, which is already stronger than the lower bound given in
[
15
]
*Thm. 1.1.2. Finally, we show that
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
is the

Recall from

that an extremal vertex of
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
is a vertex

such that for all

, if
x
lies on the geodesic
[yz]
, then

or

. We write
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\rho)
for the set of extremal vertices of
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Any maximal vertex

is an extremal vertex of
\mathcal{X}(\rho)

Proof.

Let

and let

be a representative lattice.
By
3.4
,
\Lambda_{x}/\pi\Lambda_{x}
is indecomposable, and it follows from
[

]
*Proposition 1.1.1 that

is an extremal vertex.
∎

In
[
15
]
*Thm. 1.1.2, Suh shows that

. We prove the following strengthening of this result.

Theorem 4.9
.

We have

\lvert\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)\rvert\geq 1+\dim(\mathcal{X}(\rho)).

with equality if

is residually multiplicity free. In particular, we have
\lvert\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\rho)\rvert\geq 1+\dim(\mathcal{X}(\rho))
.

Proof.

By
4.8
, we have
\lvert\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\rho)\rvert\geq\lvert X_{\max}(\rho)\rvert
, so it suffices to prove the first statement.

By
[
4
]
*Prop. 3.3.2, we have

1+\dim(\mathcal{X}(\rho))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i},

where
\overline{\rho}^{ss}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}W_{i}^{m_{i}}

Let
\Lambda
be an invariant lattice.
Then the length of the representation

is

By

and

Then

∎

Next, we show that the invariant building

is, in a sense, spanned by the maximal vertices, and that the maximal vertices are a minimal spanning set.

Definition 4.10

(
[

]
)

We say that a simplicial subcomplex

is

that represent vertices in

Both
\mathcal{X}(V)

Proof.

The tropical convexity of

are invariant lattices, then so is

.
∎

Definition 4.12
.

Let

\Lambda
and maximal in
\mathcal{L}_{v}
.
Then
\Lambda^{\prime}
represents
x
, for some
x\in\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)
.
In addition,
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\Lambda\not\subseteq\pi\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Thus,
\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda_{x}
.
It follows that
L
is also normalised at
v
.
The map
\Lambda/\pi\Lambda\rightarrow L/\pi L
, induced by the inclusion
\Lambda\subseteq L
, is injective.
Therefore,
\Lambda=L
.

If the residue field
\mathbf{F}
is infinite, then the set
\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)
can also be infinite.
Thus, it remains to show that
\Lambda=\bigcap_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)}\Lambda_{x}
is actually a finite intersection. But for any
x\in\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)
, there exists an integer
n
such that
\pi^{n}\Lambda_{x}\subseteq\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{x}
, so
\Lambda_{x}/\Lambda
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module. In particular,
\Lambda_{x}/\Lambda
is Artinian, so there is a finite subset
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)
such that
\Lambda=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda_{x_{i}}
.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

We expect that the tropical convex hull of a set
S\subset\mathcal{X}(V)
of vertices is always contained in the simplicial convex hull of
S
.
If that is true, then
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
is also the simplicial convex hull of
\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)
.
We note that
[
15
]
*Thm. 1.1.2(1), shows that
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
is the simplicial convex hull of the set of
all
extremal vertices of
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
. Our result suggests that we only need the subset of maximal vertices.

Finally, we show that
\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)
is the smallest subset whose tropical convex hull is
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
.

Proposition 4.15
.

If
S\subseteq\mathcal{X}(\rho)
is a subset whose tropical convex hull is
\mathcal{X}(\rho)
, then
\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)\subseteq S
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{X}_{\max}(\rho)
and suppose that
x
is maximal with respect to
v\in V
.
Let
\Lambda_{x}
be a lattice representing
x
and normalised at
v
.
Then there exist
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in S
and lattices
\Lambda_{i}
representing
s_{i}
such that

\Lambda_{x}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda_{i}.

Since
v\in\Lambda_{x}\setminus\pi^{-1}\Lambda_{x}
, there must exist some
1\leq i\leq n
such that
\Lambda_{i}
is normalised at
v
.
Then
\Lambda_{x}\subseteq\Lambda_{i}
, and since
\Lambda_{x}
is maximal with respect to
v
, we have
\Lambda_{x}=\Lambda_{j}
.
Therefore,
x\in S
.
∎

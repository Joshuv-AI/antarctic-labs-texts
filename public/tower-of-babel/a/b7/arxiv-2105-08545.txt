Hodge Numbers of O’Grady 6 Via Ngô Strings
Ben Wu

Abstract
We give an alternative computation of the Betti and Hodge numbers for manifolds of O ​ G ​ 6 OG6 type using the method of Ngô Strings introduced by de Cataldo, Rapagnetta, and Saccà.



1
Introduction

The Betti and Hodge numbers of manifolds of
OG6
type were first computed by Mongardi, Rapagnetta, and Saccà in
[
MRS18
]
. The purpose of this paper is to provide a new computation of the Betti and Hodge numbers of these manifolds using the method of Ngô strings introduced by de Cataldo, Rapagnetta, and Saccà in
[
dCRS19
]
. We then prove Theorem
1
, which gives a description of the Hodge structure of some special manifolds of
OG6
type in terms of the Hodge structure of an Abelian surface. Although we do not describe the Hodge structure of all manifolds of
OG6
type, as is done in
[
GKLR20
]
, we note that our computation of the Hodge structure using Ngô strings does not require prior knowledge of the Hodge numbers as an input.

The computation of the Betti and Hodge numbers follows the strategy in
[
dCRS19
]
for the
OG10
case and is summarized below. We consider two special manifolds, both admitting Lagrangian fibrations onto
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. The first, denoted by
\widetilde{M}
, is of
OG6
type and the second, denoted by
N
, is deformation equivalent to a generalized Kummer variety of dimension six. We note that the Lagrangian fibration
\widetilde{M}\to\mathbb{P}^{3}
is the same as the ones considered in
[
Rap07
,
MRS18
]
. The two fibrations
\widetilde{M}\to\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
N\to\mathbb{P}^{3}
are linked via a group scheme
G\to\mathbb{P}^{3}
which acts fiberwise on both
\widetilde{M}
and
N
. In particular,
\widetilde{M}
contains an open dense subvariety which is a
G
-torsor over the entire base and
N
contains an open dense subvariety which is a
G
-torsor over an open dense subvariety strictly contained in the base. Given this relationship between
\widetilde{M}
and
N
, we are able to use the known cohomology of generalized Kummer varieties, as well as some smaller dimensional fibrations, to deduce the Betti and Hodge numbers of
\widetilde{M}
by working in an appropriate Grothendieck group. Noting that the pure Hodge structure of
N
and these smaller dimensional fibrations can all be described in terms of the Hodge structure of an Abelian surface
J
, we then describe the Hodge structure of
\widetilde{M}
in terms of the Hodge structure of
J
(see Theorem
1
). As in the
OG10
case, the main technical tool that allows us to compare the cohomology of
\widetilde{M}
and
N
is the Ngô Support Theorem (see Section
2.2
). A key difference with the
OG10
case is that the fibers of the group scheme
G
become disconnected over various loci in the base.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

We work over the field of complex numbers. A variety is a separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. By point, we always mean closed point. Given a morphism of varieties
f\colon X\to Y
and a subvariety
Z\subseteq Y
, we set
X_{Z}\coloneqq f^{-1}(Z)
. In particular, if
Z=\{y\}
is a point, then
X_{y}=f^{-1}(y)
denotes the fiber.

Given a variety
X
, we work with the bounded constructible derived category,
D^{b}(X,\mathbb{Q})
(see
[
dCM09
]
for the basics and references) and Saito’s bounded derived category of algebraic mixed Hodge modules which rational structure,
D^{b}MHM_{alg}(X)
(see
[
Sai90
]
for the foundations and
[
Sch14
]
for basics and references). There is an exact functor
\textup{rat}\colon D^{b}MHM_{alg}(X)\to D^{b}(X,\mathbb{Q})
which takes the standard
t
-structure on
D^{b}MHM_{alg}(X)
to the middle perversity
t
-structure on
D^{b}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. An important fact is that a splitting of an object
K\in D^{b}MHM_{alg}(X)
induces a splitting of the object
\textup{rat}(K)\in D^{b}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.

If
Z\subseteq X
is a closed irreducible subvariety and
\mathscr{L}
is a polarizable variation of rational pure Hodge structures of weight
w(\mathscr{L})
on some Zariski dense open subset
V\subseteq Z^{reg}
, then the intersection cohomology object
IC_{Z}(\mathscr{L})\in MHM_{alg}(X)
yields, via
rat
, the usual perverse intersection cohomology complex of
Z
with coefficients in the underlying local system
\mathscr{L}
. We denote by
\mathscr{IC}_{Z}(\mathscr{L})\coloneqq IC_{Z}(\mathscr{L})[-\dim(Z)]
the non-perverse intersection complex; this is useful when making geometric statements.

2.2
The Ngô Support Theorem

We begin by defining the notion of a
\delta
-regular weak Abelian fibration, first introduced by B.C. Ngô in
[
Ngô10
]
. Let
g\colon G\to B
be a smooth commutative group scheme over
B
and
g^{0}\colon G^{0}\to B
be the identity component. Given a point
b\in B
, there is the canonical Chevalley devissage short exact sequence

0\to R_{b}\to G^{0}_{b}\to A_{b}\to 0

of the fiber
G^{0}_{b}
of
G^{0}
over the perfect field
k(b)
, where
A_{b}
is an Abelian variety and
R_{b}
is affine, connected, and maximal with respect to these properties.

The function
\delta\colon B\to\mathbb{Z}
sending
b\in B
to
\dim_{k(b)}R_{b}

is upper-semicontinuous and we define the
\delta
-loci
B_{i}
by
B_{i}\coloneqq\{b\in B\mid\delta(b)=i\}
. Now suppose that
d=\dim_{k(b)}G_{b}
is constant on
B
(it is so on the connected components of
B
by smoothness). For any fixed prime
\ell
, the Tate module of
G/B
, denoted by
T_{\textup{et},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G/B)
, is defined to be the
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-adic counterpart of the object
R^{2d-1}g_{!}^{0}\mathbb{Q}_{G^{0}}(d)
, i.e. it is defined by the same formula using the étale topology/cohomology formalism. The Tate module
T_{\textup{et},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G/B)
is defined to be polarizable if étale locally, there is a pairing

T_{\textup{et},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G/B)\otimes T_{\textup{et},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G/B)\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}(1)

such that for every
b\in B
, the kernel of the pairing at
b
is
T_{\textup{et},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(R_{b})
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

A weak Abelian fibration, denoted as a triple
(M,B,G)
is a pair of of morphisms
M\xrightarrow{f}B\xleftarrow{g}G
such that
(a)

f
is proper;
(b)

G
is a smooth commutative group scheme over
B
;
(c)

f
and
g
have the same pure relative dimension
d
;
(d)
there is an action
a\colon G\times_{B}M\to M
of
G
on
M
over
B
;
(e)
the action has affine stabilizers at every point
m\in M
;
(f)
the Tate module
T_{\textup{et},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(G/B)
is polarizable. If in addition the
\delta
-loci,
B_{i}
, satisfy the inequality
\codim B_{i}\geq i
for every non-negative integer
i
, then
(M,B,G)
is said to be a
\delta
-regular weak Abelian fibration. In particular, this inequality implies that the general fiber
G^{0}_{b}
is an Abelian variety.

We now state the refinement of the Ngô Support Theorem given by de Cataldo, Rapagnetta, and Saccà in
[
dCRS19
]
. We work with the following set up. Let
(M,B,G)
be a
\delta
-regular weak Abelian fibration. Suppose that
d=\dim(M/B)=\dim(G/B)
,
M/B
is projective,
B
is irreducible, and
M
is a rational homology manifold, i.e.
\mathscr{IC}_{M}\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{M}
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a finite set enumerating the supports of
Rf_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{M}
, so that the supports are denoted
Z_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}
. There is an open dense subvariety
V_{\alpha}\subset Z_{\alpha}
and a short exact sequence of smooth commutative algebraic group schemes over
V_{\alpha}
with connected fibers

0\to R_{V_{\alpha}}\to G^{0}|_{V_{\alpha}}\to A_{V_{\alpha}}\to 0

realizing the Chevalley devissage point-by-point on
V_{\alpha}
(cf.
[
Ngô10
, §7.4.8]
). Denote the natural map
A_{V_{\alpha}}\to V_{\alpha}
by
g_{\alpha}
and let
\delta^{ab}_{\alpha}
denote the relative dimension of
A_{V_{\alpha}}/V_{\alpha}
. Let
\Lambda_{\alpha}^{\bullet}\coloneqq R^{\bullet}g_{\alpha*}\mathbb{Q}_{A_{V_{\alpha}}}
where
0\leq\bullet\leq\delta^{ab}_{\alpha}.

Ngô Support Theorem
.

Let
\langle\bullet\rangle\coloneqq[-2\bullet](-\bullet)
. With the set-up above, there is an isomorphism

Rf_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{M}\simeq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}\langle d-\delta^{ab}_{\alpha}\rangle

in
D^{b}MHM_{alg}(B)
of pure objects of weight
0
, where

\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\bullet=0}^{2\delta^{ab}_{\alpha}}\mathscr{IC}_{Z_{\alpha}}(\Lambda_{\alpha}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\alpha})[-\bullet]

and
\mathscr{L}_{\alpha}
is a polarizable variation of pure Hodge structures of weight
0
. The summands
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}
are called Ngô strings.

,
M

will always be the Jacobian of a fixed genus 2 curve

is a symmetric theta divisor, i.e.

is invariant under pullback by the natural

-involution on

. Using the theta divisor, we can identify

with the dual Abelian variety,

We now recall some basic definitions about pure dimension one sheaves on the principally polarized Abelian surface
(J,\theta)
. A coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
J
is pure of dimension one if for every non-trivial subsheaf
\mathscr{G}\subset\mathscr{F}
, the dimension of the schematic support of
\mathscr{G}
is one. A pure dimension one sheaf
\mathscr{F}
is Gieseker (semi-)stable with respect to
\theta
if for all proper pure dimension one quotients
\mathscr{F}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{G}
, the following inequality holds,

\frac{\chi(\mathscr{F})}{c_{1}(\mathscr{F})\cdot\theta}\mathrel{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle($}}\leq\mathrel{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}\frac{\chi(\mathscr{G})}{c_{1}(\mathscr{G})\cdot\theta}.

If
\mathscr{F}
is a pure dimension one sheaf, a key property is that there exists a length one resolution by locally free sheaves of the same rank (cf.
[
HL97
, §1.1]
). Using this resolution, Le Potier defines in
[
LP93
, §2.2]
the Fitting support of
\mathscr{F}
as the vanishing subscheme of the induced morphism between determinant bundles. We note that this definition is independent of the resolution.

Remark 2.3.1
.

The Fitting support of a pure dimension one sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
J
contains the schematic support of
\mathscr{F}
and represents
c_{1}(\mathscr{F})
in the Chow ring of
J
. In particular, two pure dimension one sheaves
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{G}
have isomorphic determinant bundles if and only if their Fitting supports are linearly equivalent.

Consider a Mukai vector of the form
v=(0,2\theta,\chi)\in H^{ev}_{alg}(J,\mathbb{Z})
with
\chi\neq 0
. Sheaves on
J
with such a Mukai vector are pure of dimension one. Let
\mathbf{M}_{v}
be the moduli space of semi-stable sheaves on

with Mukai vector

. By
[

]
,
\mathbf{M}_{v}
is a normal irreducible projective variety of dimension
10
and the smooth locus, which is precisely the locus parameterizing strictly stable sheaves, admits a symplectic form. There is a natural morphism

\mathbf{a}_{v}\colon\mathbf{M}_{v}\to J^{\vee}\times J;\hskip 10.00002pt[\mathscr{F}]\mapsto\big(\det(\mathscr{F})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{J}(2\theta)^{\vee},\textstyle\sum c_{2}(\mathscr{F})\big),

(1)

where
[\mathscr{F}]
is the
S
-equivalence class of
\mathscr{F}
(see
[
HL97
, Definition 1.5.3]
),
\det(\mathscr{F})
is the determinant bundle of
\mathscr{F}
, and

is the sum in

of any representative of

in

[

The fiber of this morphism

(cf.

Proposition 3.0.1

•

Nijenhuis operators on Leibniz algebras
Bibhash Mondal, Ripan Saha 1. Department of Mathematics, Behala CollegeBehala, 700060, Kolkata, IndiaEmail: mondaliiser@gmail.com2. Department of Mathematics, Raiganj UniversityRaiganj 733134, West Bengal, India

Abstract




ABSTRACT

In this paper, we study Nijenhuis operators on Leibniz algebras. We discuss the relationship of Nijenhuis operators with Rota-Baxter operators and modified Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras. We define a representation theory of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebras and construct a cohomology theory. Next, we define a one-parameter formal deformation theory of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebras and study infinitesimals, rigidity, and equivalences along the line of Gerstenhaber deformation theory. As an application of our cohomology theory, we show that our cohomology is deformation cohomology and study abelian extensions of such algebras.

Key words
: Leibniz algebra, Nijenhuis operator, Cohomology, Formal deformation, Abelian Extension.

Introduction

J. L. Loday
[
12
]
introduced Leibniz algebra in 1993. Leibniz algebra is a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a bilinear operation (called the bracket)
[~,~]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the following identity:

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]],~~~~\mbox{for}~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Leibniz algebra is often considered as noncommutative Lie algebra; as a result several research have been done to generalize the result of Lie algebra to Leibniz algebra. In recent years, various linear operators like Rota-Baxter operator
[
15
]
, modified Rota Baxter operator
[
14
]
etc., and their induced cohomology, deformation is studied.
In this paper, we study Nijenhuis operators on Leibniz algebras.

A Nijenhuis operator on
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map
N:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the following condition

[N(x),N(y)]=N\bigg([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-N[x,y]\bigg),~~~~\mbox{for all}~~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Dorfman
[
5
]
studied Nijenhuis operator via deformation of Lie algebra. Moreover, Nijenhuis operators on Lie algebras play an interesting role in the study of integrability of nonlinear evolution equations
[

]
. Introduction of Dirac structures by Dorfan gave new interpretations to the already existing Nijenhuis set ups. In 2004, Gallardo and Nunes
[

introduced Dirac Nijenhuis structures. Longguang and Baokang
[

]
separately developed Dirac Nijenhuis manifolds in 2004. In 2011, Kosmann-Schwarzbach
[
10
]
studied Dirac Nijenhuis structures on Courant algebroid. A notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a 3-Lie algebra was introduced in
[
19
]
to study the 1-order deformations of a 3-Lie algebra. In
[

]
, the authors defined the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a pre-Lie algebra which generates a trivial deformation of the pre-Lie algebra. Das and Sen
[

In this paper, we study Nijenhuis operators on Leibniz algebras. We call a Leibniz algebra with a Nijenhuis operator defined on it by Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra. We give an interesting example of such algebras in dimension 2. We discuss relationship between Nijenhuis operator with Rota-Baxter operator and modified Rota-Baxter operator defined on a Leibniz algebra. It is believed that one can learn more about a mathematical object by studying its deformations
[
7
,
6
]
. To study deformation theory of a type of algebra one needs a suitable cohomology, called deformation cohomology which controls deformations in question. In the case of associative algebras, deformation cohomology is Hochschild cohomology and for Lie algebras, the associated deformation cohomology is Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology. In this paper, we study one-parameter formal deofrmation theory of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebras and associated deformation cohomology. We study the infinitesimal, rigidity and equivalences for such deformation. We also show that our cohomology is related with the abelian extensions of such algebras.

Definition 2.1

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
be a Nijehnuis Leibniz algebra. We define
[x,y]_{*}=[x,N(y)]+[N(x),y]-N([x,y])
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then,

1.

(g,[~,~]_{*})
is a Leibniz algebra.

2.

N
is also a Nijehnuis operator on
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]_{*})

3.

The map
N:(g,[~,~])\rightarrow(g,[~,~]_{*})
is a morphism of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra.

Proof.

1.

It is obvious to observe that
[~,~]_{*}
is a bilinear map. Now we will show that the Leibniz identity hold for this bilinear map that is the identity

[a,[b,c]_{*}]_{*}=[[a,b]_{*},c]_{*}+[b,[a,c]_{*}]_{*},~~~~\mbox{for}~a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}

holds.
Now,

\displaystyle[a,[b,c]_{*}]_{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Na,[b,c]_{*}]+[a,N([b,c]_{*})]-N([a,[b,c]_{*}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Na,[Nb,c]]+[Na,[b,Nc]]-[Na,N([b,c])]+[a,[Nb,Nc]]

\displaystyle-N([a,[Nb,c]])-N([a,[b,Nc]])+N([a,N([b,c])])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Na,[Nb,c]]+[Na,[b,Nc]]+N([a,[b,c]])

\displaystyle-N([Na,[b,c]])-N([a,N[b,c]])+[a,[Nb,Nc]]

\displaystyle-N([a,[Nb,c]])-N([a,[b,Nc]])+N([a,N([b,c])])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Na,[Nb,c]]+[Na,[b,Nc]]+[a,[Nb,Nc]]+N([a,[b,c]])

\displaystyle-N([Na,[b,c]])-N([a,[Nb,c]])-N([a,[b,Nc]])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Na,[Nb,c]]+[Na,[b,Nc]]+[a,[Nb,Nc]]+N([a,[b,c]])

\displaystyle-N([Na,[b,c]])+[a,[Nb,c]]+[a,[b,Nc]]).

In the same manner as above, we have

\displaystyle[[a,b]_{*},c]_{*}=[[Na,Nb],c]+[[Na,b],Nc]+[[a,Nb],Nc]+N([[a,b],c])

\displaystyle-N([[Na,b],c]+[[a,Nb],c]+[[a,b],Nc]).

and

\displaystyle[b,[a,c]_{*}]_{*}

\displaystyle=[Nb,[Na,c]]+[Nb,[a,Nc]]+[b,[Na,Nc]]+N([b,[a,c]])

\displaystyle-N([Nb,[a,c]])+[b,[Na,c]]+[b,[a,Nc]]).

Now using the Leibniz identity of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, we conclude

[a,[b,c]_{*}]_{*}=[[a,b]_{*},c]_{*}+[b,[a,c]_{*}]_{*},~~~~\mbox{for}~a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}

holds. Hence,
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]_{*})
is a Leibniz algebra.

2.

Again, for any
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle[Na,Nb]_{*}

\displaystyle=[N(Na),Nb]+[Na,N(Nb)]-N([Na,Nb])

\displaystyle=N([N(Na),b]+[Na,Nb]-N([Na,b]))

\displaystyle~~+N([Na,Nb]+[a,N(Nb)]-N([a,Nb]))

\displaystyle~~-N^{2}([Na,b]+[a,Nb]-N([a,b]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\bigg(\ [N(Na),b]+[Na,Nb]-N([Na,b])

\displaystyle~~+[Na,Nb]+[a,N(Nb)]-N([a,Nb])

\displaystyle~~-N([Na,b]+[a,Nb]-N([a,b]))\bigg)

\displaystyle=N\bigg([Na,b]_{*}+[a,Nb]_{*}-N([a,b]_{*})\bigg).

Hence,
N
is also a Nijenhuis operator on the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]_{*}).

3.

It is obvious to observe that the map
N:(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~])\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~]_{*})
,
g\mapsto N(g)
is a morphism of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra.

Example 2.10
.

Let us consider the Leibniz algebra
(\mathbb{R}^{2},[~,~])
defined in
2.2

N:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

is Nijenhuis operator if and only if

To prove the above , Let

be a Nijenhuis operator. Thus,

[N(x),N(y)]=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-N[x,y]),~~~~\mbox{for all}~~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Observe that, we have

(i)

If
x=e_{1}
and
y=e_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle[ae_{1}+ce_{2},ae_{1}+ce_{2}]=N([ae_{1}+ce_{2},e_{2}]),

\displaystyle(ac+c^{2})e_{1}=cNe_{1}=c(ae_{1}+ce_{2}),

\displaystyle\text{Therefore,}~c=0

(ii)

If
x=e_{1}
and
y=e_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle[ae_{1}+ce_{2},be_{1}+de_{2}]=N([ae_{1}+ce_{2},e_{2}]),

\displaystyle(bc+cd)e_{1}=cNe_{1}=c(ae_{1}+ce_{2}).

Since,
c=0
, we have no condition from here.

(iii)

If
x=e_{2}
and
y=e_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle[be_{1}+de_{2},ae_{1}]=N([be_{1}+de_{2},e_{1}]+[e_{2},ae_{1}]-N[e_{2},e_{1}]),

\displaystyle ade_{1}=N(de_{1}+ae_{1}-ae_{1})=ade_{1}.

Hence, this also gives no additional condition.

(iv)

If
x=e_{2}
and
y=e_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle[be_{1}+de_{2},be_{1}+de_{2}]=N([be_{1}+de_{2},e_{2}]+[e_{2},be_{1}+de_{2}]-N[e_{2},e_{2}]),

\displaystyle(bd+d^{2})e_{1}=N(de_{1}+be_{1}+de_{1}-ae_{1}),

\displaystyle bd+d^{2}=(2d+b-a)a~~\mbox{ i.e.}~(a-d)^{2}=b(a-d).

This implies either
a=d
or

The converse is a routine verification.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
N:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a linear operator where
\mathfrak{g}
is a Leibniz algebra. Then

(a)

If
N^{2}=0
then
N
is a nijenhuis operator if and only if
N
is a Rota-Baxter operator.

(b)

If
N^{2}=N
then
N
is a nijenhuis operator if and only if
N
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1.

(c)

If
N^{2}=\pm Id
then
N
is a nijenhuis operator if and only if
N
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\mp 1.

Proof.

1.

Let
N^{2}=0.
Suppose
N
is a Nijenhuis operator. Then for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle N(x),N(y)]

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-N[x,y])

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)])-N^{2}[x,y]

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)])

Hence,
N
is a Rota-Baxter operator. Proof of the converse part is similar.

2.

Let
N^{2}=N.
Suppose
N
is a Nijenhuis operator. Then for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
we have

\displaystyle N(x),N(y)]

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-N[x,y])

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)])-N^{2}[x,y]

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-[x,y]).

Hence,
N
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1
. Similarly, the converse can be shown.

3.

Let
N^{2}=I.
Suppose
N
is a Nijenhuis operator. Then, for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle N(x),N(y)]

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-N[x,y])

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)])-N^{2}[x,y]

\displaystyle=N([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)])-[x,y].

Hence,
N
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1
. In a similar way the other cases can be shown.

∎

Representations of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebras

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
12
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Leibniz algebra. A
\mathbf{representation}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a triple
(V,l_{V},r_{V})
where
V
is vector space together with linear maps (called the left and right
\mathfrak{g}
-actions respectively )
l_{V}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes V\rightarrow V~~\mbox{and}~~r_{V}:V\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
satisfying the following conditions

l_{V}(x,l_{V}(y,u))=l_{V}([x,y],u)+l_{V}(y,l_{V}(x,u))

l_{V}(x,r_{V}(u,y))=r_{V}(l_{V}(x,u),y)+r_{V}(u,[x,y])

r_{V}(u,[x,y])=r_{V}(r_{V}(u,x),y)+l_{V}(x,r_{V}(u,y))

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u\in V.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
be a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra. A representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
is a quadruple
(V,l_{V},r_{V},N_{V})

l_{V}(Nx,N_{V}(u))=N_{V}\bigg(l_{V}(Nx,u)+l_{V}(x,N_{V}(u))-(N_{V}\circ l_{V})(x,u)\bigg)

r_{V}(N_{V}(u),Nx)=N_{V}\bigg(r_{V}(N_{V}(u),x)+r_{V}(u,Nx)-(N_{V}\circ r_{V})(u,x)\bigg)

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u\in V.

.
Observe that, we also have

\displaystyle l_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}(Nx,N_{V}(u))

\displaystyle+N_{V}\bigg(l_{V}(Nx,N_{V}(u))-(N_{V}\circ l_{V})(x,N_{V}(u))+l_{V}(x,N_{V}(N_{V}(u)))\bigg)

\displaystyle-(N_{V}\circ N_{V})\bigg(l_{V}(Nx,u)-(N_{V}\circ l_{V})(x,u)+l_{V}(x,N_{V}(u))\bigg)

\displaystyle=(N_{V}\circ l_{V}^{{}^{\prime}})(Nx,u)+(N_{V}\circ l_{V}^{{}^{\prime}})(x,N_{V}(u))-(N_{V}\circ(N_{V}\circ l_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}))(x,u).

Hence,

holds for all

and

. Similarly, for all

r_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}(N_{V}(u),N(x))=N_{V}(r_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}(N_{V}(u),x)+r_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}(u,N(x))).

Hence,
(V,l_{V}^{{}^{\prime}},r_{V}^{{}^{\prime}},N_{V})
is a representation of the Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~]_{*}).

4
Cohomology of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebras

Consider a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a representation
(V,l_{V},r_{V}).
Now, for any
n\geq 0
, we define an abelian group
C^{n}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},V):=Hom(\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes n},V)
and a map
\delta^{n}:C^{n}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},V)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},V)
defined by

\displaystyle(\delta^{n}(f))(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}l_{V}(x_{i},f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1}))+(-1)^{n+1}r_{V}(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}],x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n+1}),

where
f\in C^{n}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},V)
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

It is well known that
\{C_{LA}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V),\delta^{n}\}
is a cochain complex (For details see Loday-Pirashvili cohomology for Leibniz algebra in
[
12
]
). The corresponding cohomology groups are called the cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the representation
V
and the
n
th cohomology group is denoted by
H^{n}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},V).

We will follow the notation
l_{V}(x,u)=[x,u]
and
r_{V}(u,x)=[u,x]
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},~u\in V
. Then the above coboundary map
\delta^{n}:C^{n}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},V)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},V)
becomes

\displaystyle(\delta^{n}(f))(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}[x_{i},f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})]+(-1)^{n+1}[f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),x_{n+1}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}],x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n+1}),

where
f\in C^{n}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},V)
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{N}
be a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra with representation
(V,l_{V},r_{V},N_{V})
. Now by Proposition
2.9
and
3.3
, we get a new Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~]_{*})
with representation
(V,l^{{}^{\prime}}_{V},r_{V}^{{}^{\prime}},N_{V})
induced by the Nijenhuis operator
N
. We consider the Loday-Pirashvili cochain complex of this induced Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]_{*})
with representation
(V,l^{{}^{\prime}}_{V},r_{V}^{{}^{\prime}})

as follows:

For any
n\geq 0
, define cochain groups
C^{n}_{NO}(\mathfrak{g},V):=Hom(\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes n},V)
and boundary map
\partial^{n}:C^{n}_{NO}(\mathfrak{g},V)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{NO}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

\displaystyle(\partial^{n}(f))(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}l^{{}^{\prime}}_{V}(x_{i},f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1}))+(-1)^{n+1}r^{{}^{\prime}}_{V}(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{*},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n+1}),

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}[Nx_{i},f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})]-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}N_{V}([x_{i},f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})])

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}[x_{i},N_{V}(f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1}))]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}[f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),N(x_{n+1})]-(-1)^{n+1}N_{V}([f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),x_{n+1}])

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}[N_{V}(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})),x_{n+1}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{j-1},[Nx_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{i},Nx_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-N([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}),x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

where
f\in C^{n}_{NO}(\mathfrak{g},V)
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

The above map satisfies the condition
\partial^{n+1}\circ\partial^{n}=0
as it a coboundary map for the induced Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]_{*}).

Therefore,
\{C^{n}_{NO}(\mathfrak{g},V),\partial^{n}\}
is a cochain complex. This cochain complex is called the cochain complex of the Nijenhuis operator
N
and the corresponding cohomology groups are called the cohomology of Nijenhuis operator
N
with coefficients in the representation
V
and is denoted by
H^{n}_{NO}(\mathfrak{g},V).

5
Deformation of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra

In this section, we study formal one-parameter deformation of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra. We denote the bracket
[~,~]
by
\mu.

Definition 5.1
.

A one-parameter formal deformation of a Nijenhuis algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
is a pair of two power series
(\mu_{t},N_{t})

\mu_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mu_{i}t^{i},~\mu_{i}\in C^{2}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~~~~N_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}N_{i}t^{i},~N_{i}\in C^{1}_{NO}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

such that
(\mathfrak{g}[[t]]_{N_{t}},\mu_{t})
is a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra with
(\mu_{0},N_{0})=(\mu,N)
, where
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
, the space of formal power series in
t
with coefficients from
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
module,
\mathbb{K}
being the ground field of
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
.

The above definition holds if and only if for any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
the following conditions are satisfied

\mu_{t}(x,\mu_{t}(y,z))=\mu_{t}(\mu_{t}(x,y),z)+\mu_{t}(y,\mu_{t}(x,z)),

and

\mu_{t}(N_{t}(x),N_{t}(y))=N_{t}\bigg(\mu_{t}(x,N_{t}(y))+\mu_{t}(N_{t}(x),y)-N_{t}(\mu_{t}(x,y))\bigg).

Expanding the above equations and equating the coefficients of
t^{n}
from both sides we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\mu_{i}(x,\mu_{j}(y,z))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\mu_{i}(\mu_{j}(x,y),z)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\mu_{i}(y,\mu_{j}(x,z)),

(5.1)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}\mu_{i}(N_{j}(u),N_{k}(v))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}N_{i}(\mu_{j}(N_{k}(u),v))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}N_{i}(\mu_{j}(u,N_{k}(v)))

(5.2)

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}N_{i}N_{j}(\mu_{k}(u,v))

Observe that, for
n=0
, the above conditions are exactly the conditions in the definitions of Leibniz algebra and the Nijenhuis operator.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(\mu_{t},N_{t})
be a one-parameter deformation of a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
. Then
(\mu_{1},N_{1})
is a
2
- cocyle in the cochain complex
\{C^{n}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g}),d^{n}\}.

Proof.

First we put
n=1
in equation
5.1
, we get

\mu(x,\mu_{1}(y,z))+\mu_{1}(x,\mu(y,z))=\mu(\mu_{1}(x,y),z)+\mu_{1}(\mu(x,y),z)\\
+\mu_{1}(y,\mu(x,z))+\mu(y,\mu_{1}(x,z))

Hence, we have
\delta^{2}(\mu_{1})(x,y,z)=0\in C^{2}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Now, putting
n=1
in equation
5.2
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(Nu,Nv)+\mu(N_{1}u,Nv)+\mu(Nu,N_{1}v)

\displaystyle=N_{1}(\mu(Nu,v))+N(\mu_{1}(Nu,v))+N(\mu(N_{1}u,v))

\displaystyle N_{1}(\mu(u,Nv))+N(\mu_{1}(u,Nv))+N(\mu(u,N_{1}v))

\displaystyle-N_{1}N(\mu(u,v))-NN_{1}(\mu(u,v))-N^{2}(\mu(u,v)).

Hence,

\displaystyle-N_{1}N(\mu(u,v))-\mu(N_{1}u,Nv)-\mu(Nu,N_{1}v)+N_{1}(\mu(u,Nv))

\displaystyle N_{1}(\mu(Nu,v))+N(\mu(N_{1}u,v))+N(\mu(u,N_{1}v))-\mu(u,NN_{1}v)-\mu(NN_{1}u,v)

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}(Nu,Nv)-N(\mu_{1}(Nu,v))-N(\mu_{1}(u,Nv))+N^{2}(\mu_{1}(u,v))

\displaystyle+NN_{1}(\mu(u,v))-\mu(u,NN_{1}(v))-\mu(NN_{1}u,v).

Therefore,

-\partial^{1}(N_{1})(u,v)=\phi^{2}(\mu_{1})(u,v).

or,

-\partial^{1}(N_{1})(u,v)-\phi^{2}(\mu_{1})(u,v)=0.

Hence,
d^{2}(\mu_{1},N_{1})=0
. Therefore,
(\mu_{1},N_{1})
is a
2
-cocycle in the cochain complex
C^{n}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

∎

Definition 5.3
.

Let
(\mu_{t},N_{t})
and

be two deformation of a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra

. A formal isomorphism from

is a power series

, where

are linear maps with

satisfying the following conditions

\displaystyle\psi_{t}\circ\mu_{t}^{{}^{\prime}}=\mu_{t}\circ(\psi_{t}\circ\psi_{t}),

\displaystyle\psi_{t}\circ N_{t}^{{}^{\prime}}=N_{t}\circ\psi_{t}.

Here we call
(\mu_{t},N_{t})
and

are equivalent. Now from the above conditions, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\psi_{i}(\mu_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}(x,y))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}\mu_{i}(\psi_{j}(x),\psi_{k}(y)),~~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\psi_{i}\circ N^{{}^{\prime}}_{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}N_{i}\circ\psi_{j}.

(5.4)

Theorem 5.4
.

The infinitesimal of two equivalent formal one-parameter deformation of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
are in the same cohomology class.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{t},N_{t})
and
(\mu_{t}^{{}^{\prime}},N_{t}^{{}^{\prime}})
are two equivalent formal deformation of a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
with a formal isomorphism
\psi_{t}:(\mu_{t},N_{t})\rightarrow(\mu_{t}^{{}^{\prime}},N_{t}^{{}^{\prime}})
. Now puting
n=1
in equation
5.3
and
5.4
, we get

\displaystyle\mu^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}(x,y)=\mu_{1}(x,y)+\mu(x,\psi_{1}(y))+\mu(\psi_{1}(x),y)-\psi_{1}(\mu(x,y)),~~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle N_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=N_{1}+N\circ\psi_{1}-\psi_{1}\circ N.

Hence,

(\mu_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},N_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})-(\mu_{1},N_{1})=(\delta^{1}(\psi_{1}),-\phi^{1}(\psi_{1}))=d^{1}(\psi_{1},0)\in C^{1}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
be a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra. If
H^{2}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=0
, then
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{t},N_{t})
be a deformation of
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
. Since,
(\mu_{1},N_{1})
is a
2
-cocyle and
H^{2}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=0
, thus, there exists a map
\psi_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
x\in\mathbb{K}
, where
\mathbb{K}
is the ground field of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{T},\mu)
, such that

(\psi_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},x)\in C^{1}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=C^{1}_{LA}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus Hom(\mathbb{K},\mathfrak{g}),

and
(\mu_{1},N_{1})=d^{1}(\psi_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},x)
. Hence,
\mu_{1}=\delta^{1}(\psi_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})~\mbox{and}~N_{1}=-\partial^{0}(x)-\phi^{1}(\psi_{1})
. Let
\psi_{1}=\psi_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}+\delta^{0}(x)
, then
\mu_{1}=\delta^{1}(\psi_{1}),~N_{1}=-\phi^{1}(\psi_{1})
. Let
\psi_{t}=Id_{\mathfrak{g}}-t\psi_{t}
. Then we have two equivalent deformation

(\mu_{t},N_{t})
and
(\bar{\mu_{t}},\bar{N_{t}})
, where

\bar{\mu_{t}}=\psi_{t}^{-1}\circ\mu_{t}\circ(\psi_{t}\times\psi_{t}),~~\bar{N_{t}}=\psi_{t}^{-1}\circ N_{t}\circ\psi_{t}.

By Theorem
5.4
, we have
\bar{\mu_{1}}=0,\bar{N_{1}}=0
. Hence,

\displaystyle\bar{\mu_{t}}=\mu+\bar{\mu_{2}}t^{2}+\ldots,

\displaystyle\bar{N_{t}}=N+\bar{N_{2}}t^{2}+\ldots~.

Thus, the linear terms of
(\bar{\mu_{2}},\bar{N_{2}})
vanishes, hence, repeatedly applying the same argument we conclude that
(\mu_{t},N_{t})
is equivalent to the trivial deformation. Hence,
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},\mu)
is rigid.

∎

N_{V}
on

\mu

\mu:V\times V\rightarrow V
by
\mu(x,y)=0
for all
x,y\in V
makes
(V_{N_{V}},\mu)
a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra.

.

by

.

From the above definition it follows that for an abelian extension of
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~])
there exists a commutative diagram

.

Definition 6.3
.

Let

be an abelian extension of a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~])
by
(V_{N_{V}},\mu)
. A linear map
s:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a section of this abelian extension if
p\circ s=Id_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{N}},[~,~]_{\wedge})
be an abelian extension of
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~])
by
(V_{N_{V}},\mu)
with a section
s:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Now suppose
\bar{l}_{V}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes V\rightarrow V
and
\bar{r}_{V}:V\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
be two maps defined by
\bar{l}_{V}(x,u)=[s(x),u]_{\wedge}
and
\bar{r}_{V}(u,x)=[u,s(x)]_{\wedge}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.
Then,
(V,\bar{l}_{V},\bar{r}_{V},N_{V})
is a representation of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~]).

Proof.

As
s([x,y])-[s(x),s(y)]_{\wedge}\in V
, hence
[s([x,y]),u]_{\wedge}=[[s(x),s(y)]_{\wedge},u]_{\wedge}
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\bar{l}_{V}(x,\bar{l}_{V}(y,u))-\bar{l}_{V}([x,y],u)-\bar{l}_{V}(y,\bar{l}_{V}(x,u))

\displaystyle=\bar{l}_{V}(x,[s(y),u]_{\wedge})-[s([x,y]),u]_{\wedge}-\bar{l}_{V}(y,[s(x),u]_{\wedge}).

\displaystyle=[s(x),[s(y),u]_{\wedge}]_{\wedge}-[s([x,y]),u]_{\wedge}-[s(y),[s(x),u]_{\wedge}]_{\wedge}.

\displaystyle=[s(x),[s(y),u]_{\wedge}]_{\wedge}-[[s(x),s(y)]_{\wedge},u]_{\wedge}-[s(y),[s(x),u]_{\wedge}]_{\wedge}.

\displaystyle=0.

Similarly, we can show that

\bar{l}_{V}(x,\bar{r}_{V}(u,y))=\bar{r}_{V}(\bar{l}_{V}(x,u),y)+\bar{r}_{V}(u,[x,y])

\bar{r}_{V}(u,[x,y]=\bar{r}_{V}(\bar{r}_{V}(u,x),y)+\bar{l}_{V}(x,\bar{r}_{V}(u,y))

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u\in V.
Hence,
(V,\bar{l}_{V},\bar{r}_{V})
is a representation of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]).

Now,
s(N(x))-\hat{N}(s(x))\in V
, hence,
[s(N(x)),u]=[\hat{N}(s(x)),u]
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\bar{l}_{V}(N(x),N_{V}(u))=[s(N(x)),N_{V}(u)]_{\wedge}=[\hat{N}(s(x)),\hat{N}(u)]_{\wedge}

\displaystyle=\hat{N}\bigg([\hat{N}(s(x)),u]_{\wedge}+[s(x),\hat{N}(u)]_{\wedge}-\hat{N}([s(x),u])\bigg)

\displaystyle=N_{V}\bigg([s(N(x)),u]_{\wedge}+[s(x),N_{V}(u)]_{\wedge}-N_{V}([s(x),u]_{\wedge})\bigg)

\displaystyle=N_{V}\bigg(\bar{l}_{V}(Nx,u)+\bar{l}_{V}(x,N_{V}(u))-(N_{V}\circ\bar{l}_{V})(x,u)\bigg).

Hence,

\bar{l}_{V}(Nx,N_{V}(u))=N_{V}\bigg(\bar{l}_{V}(Nx,u)+\bar{l}_{V}(x,N_{V}(u))-(N_{V}\circ\bar{l}_{V})(x,u)\bigg)

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u\in V.

Similarly, it can be shown that

\bar{r}_{V}(N_{V}(u),Nx)=N_{V}\bigg(\bar{r}_{V}(N_{V}(u),x)+\bar{r}_{V}(u,Nx)-(N_{V}\circ\bar{r}_{V})(u,x)\bigg)

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u\in V.
Hence,
(V,\bar{l}_{V},\bar{r}_{V},N_{V})
is a representation of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~]).

∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{N}},[~,~]_{\wedge})
be an abelian extension of
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~])
by
(V_{N_{V}},\mu)
. Then any two distinct sections
s_{1},s_{2}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
give the same Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra representation
(V,\bar{l}_{V},\bar{r}_{V},N_{V}).

Proof.

Let
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
be any two distinct sections. Now define
\gamma:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
by

\gamma(x)=s_{1}(x)-s_{2}(x),~\mbox{for all }~x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Since,
\mu(u,v)=0
for all
u,v\in V.

Thus,

\displaystyle[s_{1}(x),u]_{\wedge}=[\gamma(x)+s_{2}(x),u]_{\wedge}=[\gamma(x),u]_{\wedge}+[s_{2}(x),u]_{\wedge}=[s_{2}(x),u]_{\wedge}.

Similarly,
[u,s_{1}(x)]_{\wedge}=[u,s_{2}(x)]_{\wedge}
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.

Thus, two distinct sections gives the same representation.
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{N}},[~,~]_{\wedge})
be an abelian extension of
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~])
by
(V_{N_{V}},\mu)
. Let
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
and
\chi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
be two maps defined by

\displaystyle\psi(x\otimes y)=[s(x),s(y)]_{\wedge}-s([x,y]),

\displaystyle\chi(x)=\hat{T}(s(x))-s(T(x)),~~\mbox{for all}~x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\mbox{respcetively.}

Then, the cohomological class of
(\psi,\chi)
is independent of the choice of section.

Proof.

Let
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
be two distinct sections. Define
\gamma:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
by

\gamma(x)=s_{1}(x)-s_{2}(x),~\mbox{for all }~x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Since,
\mu(u,v)=0
for all
u,v\in V.

Therefore, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V,

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=[s_{1}(x),s_{1}(y)]_{\wedge}-s_{1}([x,y])

\displaystyle=[s_{2}(x)+\gamma(x),s_{2}(y)+\gamma(y)]_{\wedge}-(s_{2}([x,y])+\gamma([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}))

\displaystyle=[s_{2}(x),s_{2}(y)]_{\wedge}-s_{2}([x,y])+[\gamma(x),s_{2}(y)]_{\wedge}+[s_{2}(x),\gamma(y)]_{\wedge}-\gamma([x,y])

\displaystyle=\psi_{2}(x,y)+\delta^{1}(\gamma)(x,y).

Again,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(x)=\hat{N}(s_{1}(x))-s_{1}(N(x))

\displaystyle=\hat{N}(s_{2}(x)+\gamma(x))-s_{2}(N(x))-\gamma(N(x))

\displaystyle=\chi_{2}(x)+N_{V}(\gamma(x))-\gamma(N(x))=\chi_{2}(x)-\phi^{1}(\gamma)(x).

Therefore,
(\psi_{1},\chi_{1})-(\psi_{2},\chi_{2})=(\delta^{1}(\gamma),~-\phi^{1}(\gamma))=d^{1}(\gamma)
. Hence,
(\psi_{1},\chi_{1})
and
(\psi_{2},\chi_{2})
are in the same cohomology class
H^{2}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

Two isomorphic abelian extension of a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~])
by
(V_{N_{V}},\mu)
give rise to the same element in
H^{2}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.

Proof.

Let
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{N_{1}}},[~,~]_{\wedge_{1}})
and
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{N_{2}}},[~,~]_{\wedge_{2}})
be two isomorphic abelian extension of
(\mathfrak{g}_{N},[~,~])
by
(V_{N_{V}},\mu)
. Let
s_{1}
be a section of
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{N_{1}}},[~,~]_{\wedge_{1}})
. Thus, we have

p_{2}\circ(\xi\circ s_{1})=p_{1}\circ s_{1}=Id_{\mathfrak{g}}
as
p_{2}\circ\xi=p_{1}
, where
\xi
is the map between the two abelian extensions. Hence,
\xi\circ s_{1}
is a section of
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\hat{N_{2}}},[~,~]_{\wedge_{2}})
.
Now define
s_{2}:=\xi\circ s_{1}
. Since,
\xi
is a homomorphism of Nijenhuis Leibniz algebras such that

\xi|_{V}=Id_{V},~\xi([s_{1}(x),u]_{\wedge_{1}})=[s_{2}(x),u]_{\wedge_{2}}
. Thus,
\xi|_{V}:V\rightarrow V
is compatible with the induced representations.

Now, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(x\otimes y)=[s_{2}(x),s_{2}(y)]_{\wedge_{2}}-s_{2}([x,y])=[\xi(s_{1}(x)),\xi(s_{1}(y)])_{\wedge_{2}}-\xi(s_{1}([x,y]))

\displaystyle=\xi([s_{1}(x),s_{1}(y)]_{\wedge_{1}}-s_{1}([x,y]))=\xi(\psi_{1}(x\otimes y))=\psi_{1}(x\otimes y)

and

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(x)=\hat{N_{2}}(s_{2}(x))-s_{2}(N(x))=\hat{N_{2}}(\xi(s_{1}(x)))-\xi(s_{1}(N(x)))

\displaystyle=\xi(\hat{N_{1}}(s_{1}(x))-s_{1}(N(x)))=\xi(\chi_{1}(x))=\chi_{1}(x).

Therefore, two isomorphic abelian extension give rise to the same element in
H^{2}_{NLA}(\mathfrak{g},V).

∎

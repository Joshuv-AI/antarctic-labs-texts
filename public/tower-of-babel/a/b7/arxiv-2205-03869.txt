Shellability of Polyhedral Joins of Simplicial Complexes and Its Application to Graph Theory
Kengo Okura

Abstract
. We investigate the shellability of the polyhedral join 𝒵 M ∗ ​ ( K , L ) \mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L) of simplicial complexes K , M K,M and a subcomplex L ⊂ K L\subset K . We give sufficient conditions and necessary conditions on ( K , L ) (K,L) for 𝒵 M ∗ ​ ( K , L ) \mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L) being shellable. In particular, we show that for some pairs ( K , L ) (K,L) , 𝒵 M ∗ ​ ( K , L ) \mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L) becomes shellable regardless of whether M M is shellable or not. Polyhedral joins can be applied to graph theory as the independence complex of a certain generalized version of lexicographic products of graphs which we define in this paper. The graph obtained from two graphs G , H G,H by attaching one copy of H H to each vertex of G G is a special case of this generalized lexicographic product and we give a result on the shellability of the independence complex of this graph by applying the above results.

1.
Introduction

A
finite simple graph

G
is a pair
G=(V(G),E(G))
of a finite set
V(G)
and a set
E(G)\subset\{e\subset V(G)\ |\ |e|=2\}
. We drop adjectives “finite simple” and call
G
a
graph
.
v\in V(G)
is called a
vertex
of
G
and
e\in E(G)
is called an
edge
of
G
. The
independence complex
of
G
is an abstract simplicial complex
I(G)
defined by

\displaystyle I(G)=\{\sigma\subset V(G)\ |\ \{u,v\}\notin E(G)\text{ for any $u,v\in\sigma$ }\}.

A simplex of
I(G)
is called an
independent set
of
G
.

A simplicial complex
K
is
shellable
if its facets can be arranged in a linear order
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{t}
(which we call a
shelling
) in such a way that the subcomplex
\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}\langle F_{i}\rangle\right)\cap\langle F_{k}\rangle
is pure and
(\dim F_{k}-1)
-dimensional for all
k=2,\ldots,t
.
(Note that this is “non-pure” shellability defined by Björner and Wachs
[
3
,
4
]
.)
A graph is called
shellable
if its independence complex is shellable.
The shellability (including
vertex decomposability
, which is one of the sufficient conditions for a graph being shellable) of graphs has been studied by many researchers, such as

[
6
,
8
,
9
,
11
]
.
In this paper, we focus on the following result by Hibi, Higashitani, Kimura, and O’Keefe
[
6
]
.
Here, a graph
G
is called
well-covered
if every maximal independent set of
G
has the same cardinality.

Theorem 1.1

(Based on Hibi, Higashitani, Kimura, and O’Keefe
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on a vertex set
V(G)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
. Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\geq 2
be integers. Then the graph
G^{\prime}
obtained from
G
by attaching the complete graph
K_{k_{i}}
to
u_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
is well-covered and shellable.

Motivated by Theorem
1.1
, we consider the graph
G[H;\{v_{0}\}]
defined as follows.
Let
G
,
H
be graphs and
v_{0}
be a vertex of
H
. Define
G[H;\{v_{0}\}]
as the graph obtained from
G\sqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{u\in V(G)}H_{u}\right)
, where
H_{u}
is a copy of
H
, by identifying
u\in G
with
v_{0}\in H_{u}
.
Theorem
1.1
implies that if
H
is a complete graph, then
G[H;\{v_{0}\}]
is well-covered and shellable for any graph
G
.

In this paper, we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition on
H
and
v_{0}
for
G[H;\{v_{0}\}]
being well-covered and shellable for any graph
G
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
H
be a graph and
v_{0}
be a vertex of
H
. Then the following two conditions are equivalent.

(1)

For any graph
G
,
G[H;\{v_{0}\}]
is well-covered and shellable.

(2)

H
is well-covered, both
H
and
H\setminus\{v_{0}\}
are shellable, and for any maximal independent set
\tau
of
H\setminus\{v_{0}\}
, there exists
v\in\tau
such that
\{v_{0},v\}\in E(H)
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we need to investigate the independence complex of
G[H;\{v_{0}\}]
. With
I(G)
and
I(H)
, the independence complex
I(G[H;\{v_{0}\}])
is described as a
polyhedral join
. Polyhedral join is a construction of simplicial complexes introduced by Ayzenberg
[
1
, Definition 4.2, Observation 4.3]
. It is similar to
polyhedral product

\mathcal{Z}_{K}(\underline{X},\underline{A})
, a well-known construction of spaces, where
K
is a simplicial complex and
(\underline{X},\underline{A})=\{(X_{u},A_{u})\}_{u\in V(K)}
is a family of pairs of spaces. The definition of polyhedral joins is obtained from the definition of polyhedral products by replacing “pairs of spaces” and “product of spaces” with “pairs of simplicial complex and its subcomplex” and “join of simplicial complexes”, respectively.
Polyhedral joins appear in previous studies, including when they are called by other names.
Bahri, Bendersky, Cohen, and Gitler
[
2
, Definition 2.1]
defined a simplicial complex
K(J)
for a simplicial complex
K
on
V(K)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and a tuple
J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})
of positive integers.
Using our notation of polyhedral joins,
K(J)
is denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{K}(\underline{\Delta^{J-1}},\underline{\partial\Delta^{J-1}})\text{, where }(\underline{\Delta^{J-1}},\underline{\partial\Delta^{J-1}})=\{(\Delta^{j_{i}-1},\partial\Delta^{j_{i}-1})\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}.

Here,
\Delta^{d}
is the
d
-simplex and
\partial\Delta^{d}
is its boundary. They obtained the decomposition of polyhedral products, more precisely,
moment-angle complexes
, denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{K}(D^{2},S^{1})=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{K}(\Delta^{1},\partial\Delta^{1})}(D^{1},S^{0}).

We note that the above observation is mentioned by Vidaurre
[
10
]
, who investigated the polyhedral products over polyhedral joins.
Another example is
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})
-expansion of
K
, which is introduced by Moradi and Khosh-Ahang
[
7
, Definition 2.1]
. It is denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{K}(\underline{\mathrm{pt}^{J}},\{\emptyset\})\text{, where }(\underline{\mathrm{pt}^{J}},\{\emptyset\})

\displaystyle=\left\{\left({\bigsqcup}_{j_{i}}\mathrm{pt},\{\emptyset\}\right)\right\}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}.

They studied the shellability and vertex decomposability of expansions. We generalize one of their results
[
7
, Theorem 2.12]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we define terminologies and notations on simplicial complexes and state some basic properties of shellable simplicial complexes. Section
3
provides the definition of polyhedral joins and the explicit description of simplices and facets of polyhedral joins. Section
4
is the main part of this paper. Here we obtain two necessary conditions and two sufficient conditions for polyhedral joins being shellable, giving counterexamples of converse propositions. Note that some of the results are not relevant to Theorem
1.2
. Finally, in Section
5
, we apply the results obtained in Section
4
to the independence complexes of graphs and prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

In the following, for a positive integer
m
, we set
[m]=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.

An
abstract simplicial complex

K
is a collection of finite subsets of a given set
V(K)
such that
if
\sigma\in K
and
\tau\subset\sigma
, then
\tau\in K
. In this paper, we drop the adjective “abstract”.
An element of
K
is called a
simplex
of
K
. An element of
V(K)
is called a
vertex
of
K
.
We suppose that
\{v\}\in K
for any
v\in V(K)
.
We set
\dim K=\max_{\sigma\in K}|\sigma|-1
, where
|\sigma|
is the cardinality of
\sigma\subset V(K)
. If
\dim K=d
, then
K
is called
d
-dimensional
.
A maximal simplex with respect to the inclusion is called a
facet
of
K
.
K
is called
pure
if every facet of
K
has the same cardinality.

L\subset K
is called a
subcomplex
of
K
if
L
is a simplicial complex. In this paper, we call
(K,L)
a
pair of simplicial complexes
if
K
is a simplicial complex and
L\subset K
is a subcomplex of
K
.
For a vertex
v\in V(K)
of
K
, we define a subcomplex
\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)
of
K
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v)=\{\sigma\in K\ |\ v\notin\sigma\}.

Let
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in[m]}
be a family of simplicial complexes. We define a simplicial complex
K_{1}*\cdots*K_{m}
, which we call the
join
of
K_{1},K_{2},\ldots
, and
K_{m}
, by

\displaystyle K_{1}*\cdots*K_{m}=\left\{\sigma\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K_{i})\ \middle|\ \sigma\cap V(K_{i})\in K_{i}\text{ for any $i\in[m]$}\right\}.

Let
V
be a finite set and
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\subset V
be a collection of subsets of
V
such that
F_{i}\nsubseteq F_{j}
for any
i\neq j
. Define a simplicial complex
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\rangle
on
V
by

\displaystyle\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\rangle=\{\sigma\subset V\ |\ \sigma\subset F_{i}\text{ for some }i\in[t]\}.

As defined in Section
1
, a simplicial complex
K
is
shellable
if its facets can be arranged in a linear order
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{t}
(which we call a
shelling
) in such a way that the subcomplex
\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}\langle F_{i}\rangle\right)\cap\langle F_{k}\rangle
is pure and
(\dim F_{k}-1)
-dimensional for all
k=2,\ldots,t
. Here we state some of the properties of shellable simplicial complexes without proofs.

Lemma 2.1

(Based on
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex. An order
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{t}
of the facets of
K
is a shelling if and only if for every
i,k
with
1\leq i<k\leq t
, there exists an index
j
with
1\leq j<k
and a vertex
x\in F_{k}\setminus F_{i}
such that
F_{j}\cap F_{k}=F_{k}\setminus\{x\}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Based on
[
3
, Lemma 2.6]
)
.

Let
K
be a shellable simplicial complex. Then there exists a shelling
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{t}
such that
|F_{i}|\geq|F_{j}|
for any
1\leq i<j\leq t
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
4
, Remark 10.22]
)
.

The join of two simplicial complexes is shellable if and only if each of the simplicial complex is shellable.

For a simplicial complex
X
, we denote the set of all facets of
X
by
\mathbf{F}_{X}
.
Note that for a pair
(K,L)
of simplicial complexes, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\ |\ \sigma\notin L\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{L}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{K}

\displaystyle=\left\{\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}\ |\ \text{there exists $\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}$ such that $\tau\subsetneq\sigma$}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{K}\cap\mathbf{F}_{L}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\ |\ \sigma\in L\right\}.

3.
Facets of Polyhedral Joins

The main subject of this paper is
polyhedral join
. It is a construction of simplicial complexes, which is based on the definition by Ayzenberg
[
1
, Definition 4.2, Observation 4.3]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a simplicial complex on
[m]
and
(\underline{K},\underline{L})=\{(K_{i},L_{i})\}_{i\in[m]}
be a family of pairs of simplicial complexes. For a simplex
S\in M
, we define a simplicial complex
(\underline{K},\underline{L})^{*S}
by

\displaystyle(\underline{K},\underline{L})^{*S}=X_{1}*X_{2}*\cdots*X_{m},\ X_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &K_{i}&&(i\in S),\\
&L_{i}&&(i\notin S).\end{aligned}\right.

(\underline{K},\underline{L})^{*S}
is a subcomplex of
K_{1}*\cdots*K_{m}
.

\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
is a subcomplex of
K_{1}*\cdots*K_{m}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})=\bigcup_{S\in M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})^{*S}

(union is taken in
K_{1}*\cdots*K_{m}
).

Let
(K,L)
be a pair of simplicial complexes. If
K_{i}=K,L_{i}=L
for any
i\in[m]
, we write
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
instead of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
.

We give an explicit description of simplices and facets of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
M
be a simplicial complex on
[m]
and
(\underline{K},\underline{L})=\{(K_{i},L_{i})\}_{i\in[m]}
be a family of pairs of simplicial complexes.
For
\phi\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K_{i})
, we set

\displaystyle\phi_{i}

\displaystyle=\phi\cap V(K_{i})\ (i\in[m]),

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}

\displaystyle=\{i\in[m]\ |\ \phi_{i}\notin L_{i}\}.

Then
\phi\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
if and only if
\phi_{i}\in K_{i}
for any
i\in[m]
and
\overline{\phi}\in M
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
. Then
\phi
is a simplex of
\phi\in(\underline{K},\underline{L})^{*S}
for some
S\in M
.
So, by the definition of the join, we have

•

\phi\cap V(K_{i})=\phi_{i}\in K_{i}
for any
i\in S
,

•

\phi\cap V(K_{i})=\phi_{i}\in L_{i}\subset K_{i}
for any
i\notin S
.

It follows that
\phi_{i}\in K_{i}
for any
i\in[m]
and that
\overline{\phi}\subset S
. Since
M
is a simplicial complex and
S\in M
, we have
\overline{\phi}\in M
.

Conversely, let
\phi\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K_{i})
be a set such that
\phi_{i}\in K_{i}
for any
i\in[m]
and
\overline{\phi}\in M
. By the definition of
\overline{\phi}
,
i\notin\overline{\phi}
implies
\phi_{i}\in L_{i}
. So, we have
\phi\in(\underline{K},\underline{L})^{*\overline{\phi}}\subset\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
M
be a simplicial complex on
[m]
and
(\underline{K},\underline{L})=\{(K_{i},L_{i})\}_{i\in[m]}
be a family of pairs of simplicial complexes.
Then
\phi\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K_{i})
is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
if and only if

•

\phi_{i}\in\mathbf{F}_{K_{i}}\cup\mathbf{F}_{L_{i}}
for any
i\in[m]
,

•

\overline{\phi}\in M
, and

•

\overline{\phi}\cup\{i\}\notin M
for any
i\in[m]
such that
\phi_{i}\in\mathbf{F}_{L_{i}}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{K_{i}}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
. For
k\in[m]
and
\sigma\in K_{k}
, define
\phi^{(k,\sigma)}\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K_{i})
by

\displaystyle\phi^{(k,\sigma)}_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phi_{i}&&(i\neq k)\\
&\sigma&&(i=k).\end{aligned}\right.

First, assume that there exists
\sigma\in K_{k}
such that
\phi_{k}\subsetneq\sigma
for some
k\in\overline{\phi}
.
We have
\sigma\notin L_{k}
since
\sigma\supset\phi_{k}\notin L_{k}
. Therefore, we get
\overline{\phi^{(k,\sigma)}}=\overline{\phi}\in M
. So,
\phi^{(k,\sigma)}
is a simplex of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
which satisfies
\phi^{(k,\sigma)}\supsetneq\phi
, a contradiction. Thus,
\phi_{k}
is a facet of
K_{k}
.

Second, assume that there exists
\tau\in L_{k}
such that
\phi_{k}\subsetneq\tau
for some
k\notin\overline{\phi}
.
Since
\phi_{k},\tau\in L_{k}
, we get
\overline{\phi^{(k,\tau)}}=\overline{\phi}\in M
. So,
\phi^{(k,\tau)}
is a simplex of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
which satisfies
\phi^{(k,\tau)}\supsetneq\phi
, a contradiction. Thus,
\phi_{k}
is a facet of
L_{k}
.

Finally, assume that there exists
j\in[m]
such that
\phi_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{L_{j}}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{K_{j}}
and
\overline{\phi}\cup\{j\}\in M
. Then there must be
\rho\in K_{j}\setminus L_{j}
such that
\phi_{j}\subsetneq\rho
since
\phi_{j}
is a facet of
L_{j}
and is not a facet of
K_{j}
. We get that
\phi^{(j,\rho)}
is a simplex of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
since
\overline{\phi^{(j,\rho)}}=\overline{\phi}\cup\{j\}\in M
. This is a contradiction to the maximality of
\phi
. By the above three arguments, we conclude that a facet
\phi
of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
satisfies three conditions in the proposition.

Conversely, suppose that
\phi\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K_{i})
satisfies three conditions in the proposition.
Since
\phi_{i}\in\mathbf{F}_{K_{i}}\cup\mathbf{F}_{L_{i}}\subset K_{i}
for any
i\in[m]
and
\overline{\phi}\in M
,
\phi
is a simplex of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
.
We assume that there exists
\psi\in\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
such that
\phi\subsetneq\psi
and deduce a contradiction. There must exists
k\in[m]
such that
\phi_{k}\subsetneq\psi_{k}
. This means that
\phi_{k}
is not a facet of
K_{k}
, which implies
\phi_{k}\in\mathbf{F}_{L_{k}}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{K_{k}}
.
It follows that
k\in\overline{\psi}
since
\phi_{k}\subsetneq\psi_{k}
and
\phi_{k}
is a facet of
L_{k}
. Thus, we get
\overline{\phi}\cup\{k\}\subset\overline{\psi}\in M
, which contradicts to the third condition in the proposition. Therefore,
\phi
is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
\mathbf{F}_{L_{i}}\subset\mathbf{F}_{K_{i}}
for any
i\in[m]
, then by Proposition
3.3
,
\phi\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K_{i})
is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})
if and only if
\phi_{i}\in\mathbf{F}_{K_{i}}
for any
i\in[m]
and
\overline{\phi}\in M
.

Before investigating the shellability of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
, we show a necessary and sufficient condition for
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
being pure.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
M\neq\{\emptyset\}
be a simplicial complex which is not a simplex and
K\supsetneq L
be a pair of simplicial complexes. Then
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is pure if and only if

•

K
is pure and
\mathbf{F}_{L}\subset\mathbf{F}_{K}
, or

•

K,L,M
are pure.

Proof.

We set
V(M)=[m]
.

Assume that
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is pure.
For
S\in\mathbf{F}_{M}
,
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}
and
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
, define
\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau)\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K)
by

\displaystyle\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau)_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sigma&&(i\in S)\\
&\tau&&(i\notin S).\end{aligned}\right.

Here, remark that
\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau)
is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
if
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
or
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\cap\mathbf{F}_{L}
by Proposition
3.3
. If
\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau)
is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
, then we have

\displaystyle|\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[m]}|\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau)_{i}|=|S||\sigma|+(m-|S|)|\tau|.

Note that we have
0<|S|<m
since
M
is neither
\{\emptyset\}
nor a simplex.

Suppose that
\mathbf{F}_{K}\cap\mathbf{F}_{L}\neq\emptyset
. Take
S\in\mathbf{F}_{M}
,
\sigma^{0}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\tau^{0}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\cap\mathbf{F}_{L}
. For any facets
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}
of
K
,
\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau^{0})
and
\Phi_{S}(\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{0})
are facets of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
. Since
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is pure, we have
|\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau^{0})|=|\Phi_{S}(\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{0})|
, namely

\displaystyle|S||\sigma|+(m-|S|)|\tau^{0}|=|S||\sigma^{\prime}|+(m-|S|)|\tau^{0}|.

It follows from
|S|>0
that

\displaystyle|\sigma|=|\sigma^{\prime}|.

Therefore,
K
is pure. For any facets
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
of
L
,

\Phi_{S}(\sigma^{0},\tau)
and
\Phi_{S}(\sigma^{0},\tau^{\prime})
are facets of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
. Since
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is pure, we have
|\Phi_{S}(\sigma^{0},\tau)|=|\Phi_{S}(\sigma^{0},\tau^{\prime})|
, namely

\displaystyle|S||\sigma^{0}|+(m-|S|)|\tau|=|S||\sigma^{0}|+(m-|S|)|\tau^{\prime}|.

It follows from
|S|<m
that

\displaystyle|\tau|=|\tau^{\prime}|.

Therefore,
L
is pure. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim K=|\tau^{0}|-1=\dim L.

If there exists
\tau^{1}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{K}
, then there must exist
\sigma^{1}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
such that
\tau^{1}\subsetneq\sigma^{1}
. So, we get

\displaystyle\dim K=|\sigma^{1}|-1>|\tau^{1}|-1=\dim L,

which is a contradiction. Thus, we conclude that
\mathbf{F}_{L}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{K}=\emptyset
, namely
\mathbf{F}_{L}\subset\mathbf{F}_{K}
.

Next, suppose that
\mathbf{F}_{K}\cap\mathbf{F}_{L}=\emptyset
. Take
S\in\mathbf{F}_{M}
and
\tau^{0}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
. Since
\tau^{0}
is not a facet of
K
, there exist
\sigma^{0}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}
such that
\tau^{0}\subsetneq\sigma^{0}
. For any facet
\sigma
of
K
,
\Phi_{S}(\sigma,\tau^{0})
is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
since
\mathbf{F}_{K}=\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
. Therefore, by the same argument as above, we obtain that both
K
and
L
are pure. For any facet
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{M}
of
M
,
\Phi_{T}(\sigma^{0},\tau^{0})
and
\Phi_{T^{\prime}}(\sigma^{0},\tau^{0})
are facets of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
. Since
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is pure, we have
|\Phi_{T}(\sigma^{0},\tau^{0})|=|\Phi_{T^{\prime}}(\sigma^{0},\tau^{0})|
, namely

\displaystyle|T||\sigma^{0}|+(m-|T|)|\tau^{0}|=|T^{\prime}||\sigma^{0}|+(m-|T^{\prime}|)|\tau^{0}|.

It follows that

\displaystyle|T|=|T^{\prime}|

since
\sigma^{0}\supsetneq\tau^{0}
. Therefore, we conclude that
M
is pure.

Conversely, assume that
K
is pure and
\mathbf{F}_{L}\subset\mathbf{F}_{K}
. Then for any facet
\phi\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)}
, we get

\displaystyle|\phi|=\sum_{i\in[m]}|\phi_{i}|=m(\dim K+1).

Therefore,
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is pure.

Finally, assume that
K,L,M
are pure and that there exists
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{K}
. Since there exists
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
such that
\tau\subsetneq\sigma
, we get

\displaystyle\dim K=|\sigma|-1>|\tau|-1=\dim L.

So, we obtain
\mathbf{F}_{K}\cap\mathbf{F}_{L}=\emptyset
since both
K
and
L
are pure. Thus, for any
\phi\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)}
,
\overline{\phi}
is a facet of
M
. This is because we have
\overline{\phi}\cup\{i\}\notin M
for any
i\notin\overline{\phi}
since
\mathbf{F}_{L}=\mathbf{F}_{L}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{K}
. Hence, we get

\displaystyle|\phi|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\overline{\phi}}|\phi_{i}|+\sum_{i\notin\overline{\phi}}|\phi_{i}|

\displaystyle=|\overline{\phi}|(\dim K+1)+(m-|\overline{\phi}|)(\dim L+1)

\displaystyle=(\dim M+1)(\dim K+1)+(m-\dim M-1)(\dim L+1)

for any facet
\phi\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)}
since
M
is pure.
Therefore, we conclude that
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is pure.
∎

4.
Shellability of Polyhedral Joins

We first present two sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
being shellable.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be an arbitrary simplicial complex and
K\supset L
be a pair of simplicial complexes. Suppose that
K,L
satisfy the following three conditions.

(1)

K
is shellable,

(2)

\mathbf{F}_{L}\subset\mathbf{F}_{K}
, and

(3)

there exists a shelling
<_{K}
on
K
such that
\tau<_{K}\sigma
for any
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
and any
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
.

Then
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is shellable.

Proof.

We set
V(M)=[m]
.
Consider a linear order
<
on
\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)}
such that
\phi<\psi
if and only if

\displaystyle\phi_{1}=\psi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{j-1}=\psi_{j-1},\phi_{j}<_{K}\psi_{j}

for some
j\in[m]
. We show that this order is a shelling on
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
.

Let
\phi,\psi
be facets of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
such that
\phi_{1}=\psi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{j-1}=\psi_{j-1},\phi_{j}<_{K}\psi_{j}
. Since
<_{K}
is a shelling on
K
, there exists
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}
and
x\in\psi_{j}\setminus\phi_{j}
such that
\sigma<_{K}\psi_{j}
and
\sigma\cap\psi_{j}=\psi_{j}\setminus\{x\}
by Lemma
2.1
. Define
\chi\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K)
by

\displaystyle\chi_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\psi_{i}&&(i\neq j)\\
&\sigma&&(i=j).\end{aligned}\right.

If
\psi_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
, then we have
\overline{\chi}\subset\overline{\psi}\in M
.
If
\psi_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
, then by condition (3),
\sigma
must be a facet of
L
since
\sigma<_{K}\psi_{j}
. So, we have
\overline{\chi}=\overline{\psi}\in M
.
In both cases, we get that
\overline{\chi}\in M
, which implies that
\chi
is a simplex of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
. Furthermore,
\chi
is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
by Remark
3.4
.

We have
\chi<\psi
because

\displaystyle\chi_{1}=\psi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{j-1}=\psi_{j-1},\chi_{j}=\sigma<_{K}\psi_{j}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{i}\cap\psi_{i}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\psi_{i}&&(i\neq j)\\
&\sigma\cap\psi_{j}&&(i=j)\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\psi_{i}&&(i\neq j)\\
&\psi_{j}\setminus\{x\}&&(i=j).\end{aligned}\right.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi\cap\psi=\psi\setminus\{x\}.

Finally, we have
x\in\psi\setminus\phi
since
x\in\psi_{j}\setminus\phi_{j}
.
Therefore, by the above argument, we get
\chi\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)}
and
x\in\psi\setminus\phi
such that

\chi<\psi
and
\chi\cap\psi=\psi\setminus\{x\}
. By Lemma
2.1
, we conclude that
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is shellable.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

M
be a simplicial complex on
[m]
and
(\underline{K},\underline{L})=\{(K_{i},L_{i})\}_{i\in[m]}

Suppose that

•

on
\mathbf{F}_{L_{i}}
.
Here we remark that we have

for any

<
on
\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})}

•

.

, there exists

.

.

\chi_{k}\cap\sigma=\chi_{k}\cap\Phi^{\sigma}_{k}=\Phi^{\sigma}_{k}\setminus\{x\}=\sigma\setminus\{x\}
.
Furthermore, we get
\Phi^{\chi_{k}}<\chi<\Phi^{\sigma}
.
Therefore,
\chi_{k}\in\mathbf{F}_{K_{k}}
and

\chi_{k}<_{K}\sigma
and
\chi_{k}\cap\sigma=\sigma\setminus\{x\}
. By Lemma

,
<_{K}
is a shelling on
K_{k}
.

Next, we prove that

is shellable. There exists
\sigma^{0}\in\mathbf{F}_{K_{k}}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L_{k}}
since

. For a facet
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L_{k}}
, define
\Psi^{\tau}\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})}

\displaystyle\Psi^{\tau}=\min\left\{\psi\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})}\ \middle|\ \psi_{i}=\sigma^{0}\text{ for any }i\in S,\text{ and }\psi_{k}=\tau\right\}.

Note that
\Psi^{\tau}
is well-defined. This is because there exists a facet
\psi\in\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(\underline{K},\underline{L})}

\displaystyle\psi_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sigma^{0}&&(i\in S)\\
&\tau&&(i\notin S).\end{aligned}\right.

For
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{L_{k}}

\tau<_{L}\tau^{\prime}
by
\Psi^{\tau}<\Psi^{\tau^{\prime}}

\chi_{k}\neq\tau

On the other hand, we get

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
M
be a simplicial complex which is not a simplex and
K\supsetneq L
be a pair of simplicial complexes. Suppose that
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is shellable. Then there exists a pair
(\sigma,\tau)
of
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
such that

\displaystyle|\sigma\cap\tau|=\max_{\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}}|\rho|-1.

Proof.

Fix a shelling
\phi^{1}<\cdots<\phi^{t}
on
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
such that
|\phi^{p}|\geq|\phi^{q}|
for any
1\leq p<q\leq t
, which exists by Lemma
2.2
.

We first show that there exists an index
s
such that
\overline{\phi^{p}}\nsupseteq\overline{\phi^{s}}
for any
p\in[s-1]
. Since
M
is not a simplex, there exists
j\in[m]
such that
j\notin\overline{\phi^{1}}
. Take a facet
S\ni j
of
M
. Here, it follows from
K\neq L
that there exists a facet
\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
of
K
. Now, consider a subset
\psi\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K)
which satisfies

•

if
i\in S
, then
\psi_{i}=\rho
,

•

if
i\notin S
, then
\psi_{i}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
.

We have
\psi_{i}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\cup\mathbf{F}_{L}
for any
i\in[m]
,
\overline{\psi}=S\in M
and
\overline{\psi}\cup\{i\}\notin M
for any
i\notin S
since
S
is a facet of
M
. By Proposition
3.3
,
\psi
is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
. Therefore, we have
\psi=\phi^{q}
for some
q\in[t]
. Since
j\in S\setminus\overline{\phi^{1}}=\overline{\phi^{q}}\setminus\overline{\phi^{1}}
, we get
\overline{\phi^{q}}\nsubseteq\overline{\phi^{1}}
.
Now we set
s=\min\{r\in[t]\ |\ \overline{\phi^{r}}\nsubseteq\overline{\phi^{1}}\}
. It follows from the minimality of
s
that
\overline{\phi^{p}}\subset\overline{\phi^{1}}
for any
p\in[s-1]
. Thus, we get
\overline{\phi^{s}}\nsubseteq\overline{\phi^{p}}
for any
p\in[s-1]
as desired.

Now, take an index
s
as above.
For an index
p^{\prime}
such that
\overline{\phi^{p^{\prime}}}=\overline{\phi^{s}}
,
p^{\prime}
must be larger than
s
, which implies
|\phi^{p^{\prime}}|\leq|\phi^{s}|
. So,
\phi^{s}
is the largest facet among facets
\phi
such that
\overline{\phi}=\overline{\phi^{s}}
.
By the definition of a shelling, there must exist
p
with
1\leq p<s
such that
|\phi^{p}\cap\phi^{s}|=|\phi^{s}|-1
. This equality implies that we have

\displaystyle|\phi^{p}_{i}\cap\phi^{s}_{i}|\geq|\phi^{s}_{i}|-1

for any
i\in[m]
.
On the other hand, it follows from
\overline{\phi^{p}}\nsupseteq\overline{\phi^{s}}
that there exists
j\in\overline{\phi^{s}}\setminus\overline{\phi^{p}}
, namely
j\in[m]
such that
\phi^{p}_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\phi^{s}_{j}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
.
Since
\phi^{s}_{j}
is a facet of
K
, we have
\phi^{p}_{j}\cap\phi^{s}_{j}\subsetneq\phi^{s}_{j}
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle|\phi^{p}_{j}\cap\phi^{s}_{j}|=|\phi^{s}_{j}|-1.

Assume that there exists
\mu\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}

|\mu|>|\phi^{s}_{j}|
. Then, a subset
\chi\subset\bigsqcup_{i\in[m]}V(K)

\displaystyle\chi_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phi^{s}_{i}&&(i\neq j)\\
&\mu&&(i=j)\end{aligned}\right.

is a facet of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
. Since
|\chi|>|\phi^{s}|
and
\overline{\chi}=\overline{\phi^{s}}

|\phi^{s}|
. Hence, we obtain

Then,

\displaystyle|\sigma\cap\tau|=|\sigma|-1=\max_{\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}}|\rho|-1,

which is the desired conclusion.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
M
be a simplicial complex and
K\supset L
be a pair of simplicial complexes. If
\dim K-\dim L\geq 2
, then
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is not shellable.

In particular, for any simplicial complex
M
and
K
such that
\dim K\geq 1
,
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,\{\emptyset\})
is not shellable.

Proof.

It follows from
\dim K>\dim L
that there must be
\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}
such that
|\rho|=\dim K+1
and
\rho\notin\mathbf{F}_{L}
.
So, we get

\displaystyle\dim K=\max_{\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}}|\rho|-1.

For any
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}

\displaystyle|\sigma\cap\tau|\leq|\tau|\leq\dim L+1\leq\dim K-1=\max_{\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}}|\rho|-2.

Therefore, there is no pair
(\sigma,\tau)
of
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
such that

\displaystyle|\sigma\cap\tau|=\max_{\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}}|\rho|-1.

4.9
, we conclude that
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is not shellable.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
M
be a simplicial complex which is not a simplex and
K
be a simplicial complex. For a vertex
v_{0}
of
K
, suppose that
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v_{0})}\subset\mathbf{F}_{K}
. Then
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v_{0}))
is shellable if and only if both
K
and
\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v_{0})
are shellable.

Proof.

We set
L=\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v_{0})
.
If
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{M}(K,L)
is shellable and
\mathbf{F}_{L}\subset\mathbf{F}_{K}
, then by Theorem
4.6
,
K
and
L
are shellable. In order to prove the converse, it is sufficient to show that there is a shelling
<_{K}
on
K
such that
\tau<_{K}\sigma
for any
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
and any
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
. Then the proof is completed by Theorem
4.1
.

Let
<
be a shelling on
K
and
<^{\prime}
be a shelling on
L
. Define a relation
<_{K}
on
\mathbf{F}_{K}
by

•

\tau<_{K}\sigma
for any
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
,

•

for any
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
,
\tau<_{K}\tau^{\prime}
if and only if
\tau<^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}
, and

•

for any
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
,
\sigma<_{K}\sigma^{\prime}
if and only if
\sigma<\sigma^{\prime}
.

It is obvious that
<_{K}
is a linear order. We prove that
<_{K}
is a shelling on
K
. The goal of the proof is to show that for any
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}
such that
\rho<_{K}\rho^{\prime}
, there exists
\rho^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}
and
u\in\rho^{\prime}\setminus\rho
which satisfy
\rho^{\prime\prime}<_{K}\rho^{\prime}
and
\rho^{\prime\prime}\cap\rho^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime}\setminus\{u\}
.

For any
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
such that
\tau<^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}
, there exists
\tau^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
x\in\tau^{\prime}\setminus\tau
such that
\tau^{\prime\prime}<^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}\cap\tau^{\prime}=\tau^{\prime}\setminus\{x\}
. Since
\tau^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}<^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}
, we get
\tau^{\prime\prime}<_{K}\tau^{\prime}
.

For any
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
such that
\sigma<\sigma^{\prime}
, there exists
\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}
and
x\in\sigma^{\prime}\setminus\sigma
such that
\rho<\sigma^{\prime}
and
\rho\cap\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime}\setminus\{x\}
. If
\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
, then we obtain
\rho<_{K}\sigma^{\prime}
since
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
. If
\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
, then we obtain
\rho<_{K}\sigma^{\prime}
again since
\rho<\sigma^{\prime}
. In both cases, we have
\rho<_{K}\sigma^{\prime}
.

For
\tau\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
, we have
v_{0}\in\sigma\setminus\tau
since
\sigma\notin\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v_{0})
and
\tau\in\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v_{0})
. A simplex
\sigma\setminus\{v_{0}\}\in L
is not a facet of
L
since
\sigma\setminus\{v_{0}\}
is not a facet of
K
and we have
\mathbf{F}_{L}\subset\mathbf{F}_{K}
by the assumption. Therefore, there exists a facet
\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{L}

\rho\in\mathrm{dl}_{K}(v_{0})

We also get
\rho<_{K}\sigma
because
\rho\in\mathbf{F}_{L}
and
\sigma\in\mathbf{F}_{K}\setminus\mathbf{F}_{L}
.

E(H\setminus U)=\{uv\in E(H)\ |\ u,v\notin U\}
.

Proof.

Remark that for
\phi\subset V(G)\times V(H)=V(\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{I(G)}(I(H),I(H\setminus U)))
,
\phi_{u}
(
u\in V(G)
) and
\overline{\phi}
in Proposition
3.2
are reformulated as follows:

\displaystyle\phi_{u}

\displaystyle=\{v\in V(H)\ |\ (u,v)\in\phi\},

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}

\displaystyle=\{u\in V(G)\ |\ \phi_{u}\cap U\neq\emptyset\}.

Let
\phi\in I(G[H;U])
be an independent set of
G[H;U]
. Then, we have the followings.

•

For any
u\in V(G)
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(H)
, suppose that
v_{1},v_{2}\in\phi_{u}
. Then we have
(u,v_{1})(u,v_{2})\notin E(G[H;U])
since
(u,v_{1}),(u,v_{2})\in\phi
and
\phi
is an independent set of
G[H;U]
. By the definition of
E(G[H;U])
, we obtain
v_{1}v_{2}\notin E(H)
. Therefore,
\phi_{u}
is an independent set of
H
, namely
\phi_{u}\in I(H)
.

•

For any
u_{1},u_{2}\in\overline{\phi}
, there exist
w_{1},w_{2}\in U
such that
(u_{1},w_{1}),(u_{2},w_{2})\in\phi
. Then
(u_{1},w_{1})(u_{2},w_{2})\notin E(G[H;U])
since
\phi
is an independent set of
G[H;U]
. By the definition of
E(G[H;U])
, we obtain
u_{1}u_{2}\notin E(G)
. Therefore,
\overline{\phi}
is an independent set of
G
, namely
\overline{\phi}\in I(G)
.

So, by Proposition
3.2
,
\phi
is a simplex of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{I(G)}(I(H),I(H\setminus U))
.

Conversely, let
\psi
be a simplex of
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{I(G)}(I(H),I(H\setminus U))
. It follows from Proposition
3.2
that we have

\psi_{u}\in I(H)
for any
u\in V(G)
and
\overline{\psi}\in I(G)
. Then, for any
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\psi
, we have the followings.

•

If
u_{1}=u_{2}
, then
v_{1}v_{2}\notin E(H)
since
v_{1},v_{2}\in\psi_{u_{1}}
and
\psi_{u_{1}}
is an independent set of
H
. Furthermore,
u_{1}=u_{2}
implies that
u_{1}u_{2}\notin E(G)
since
G
has no loops. Therefore, we get
(u_{1},v_{1})(u_{2},v_{2})\notin E(G[H;U])
.

•

If
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in U
, then
u_{1}u_{2}\notin E(G)
since
u_{1},u_{2}\in\overline{\psi}
and
\overline{\psi}
is an independent set of
G
. Therefore, we get
(u_{1},v_{1})(u_{2},v_{2})\notin E(G[H;U])
.

•

If
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
and
v_{1}\notin U
or
v_{2}\notin U
, then
(u_{1},v_{1})(u_{2},v_{2})\notin E(G[H;U])
.

So, we conclude that
(u_{1},v_{1})(u_{2},v_{2})\notin E(G[H;U])
. Thus,
\psi
is an independent set of
G[H;U]
, namely
\psi\in I(G[H;U])
.
∎

Example 5.5
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least one edge and
H
be a graph which is not a complete graph.
Vander Meulen and Van Tuyl
[
9
, Theorem 2.3]
proved that
I(G[H])
is not shellable.
We can deduce this result from Example
5.2
, Proposition
5.4
and Corollary
4.12
since
I(G)
is not a simplex and
I(H)
is not
0
-dimensional.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

First, we show that (1) implies (2). By Proposition
5.4
, (1) is equivalent to the condition that
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{I(G)}(I(H),I(H\setminus\{v_{0}\}))
is pure and shellable for any graph
G
. It follows from Theorem
3.5
that
I(H)
is pure, namely
H
is well-covered. Now consider the cycle on
4
vertices
C_{4}
, namely the graph defined by

\displaystyle V(C_{4})=\{1,2,3,4\},

\displaystyle E(C_{4})=\{12,23,34,41\}.

Since
I(C_{4})=\langle\{1,3\},\{2,4\}\rangle
and
I(C_{4}[H,H\setminus\{v_{0}\}])
is shellable, Claim
4.2
indicates that
\mathbf{F}_{I(H\setminus\{v_{0}\})}\subset\mathbf{F}_{I(H)}
. Hence, for any maximal independent set
\tau
of
H\setminus\{v_{0}\}
, we get that
\tau\cup\{v_{0}\}
is not an independent set of
H
. Namely there exists
v\in\tau
such that
v_{0}v\in E(H)
. Therefore, by Theorem
4.6
, both
I(H)
and
I(H\setminus\{v_{0}\})
are shellable.

Next, we deduce (1) from (2). By the conditions in (2),
I(H)
is pure, both
I(H)
and
I(H\setminus\{v_{0}\})
are shellable, and
\mathbf{F}_{I(H\setminus\{v_{0}\})}\subset\mathbf{F}_{I(H)}
. Therefore, it follows from Theorem
3.5
that
I(G[H;H\setminus\{v_{0}\}])
is pure for any graph
G
and from Theorem
4.13
that
I(G[H;H\setminus\{v_{0}\}])
is shellable for any graph
G
which has at least one edge. For graph
G
which has no edges, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{I(G)}(I(H),I(H\setminus\{v_{0}\}))=\underbrace{I(H)*\cdots*I(H)}_{|V(G)|}

is shellable since
I(H)
is shellable. Therefore, we conclude that
\mathcal{Z}^{*}_{I(G)}(I(H),I(H\setminus\{v_{0}\}))
is pure and shellable, namely
G[H;\{v_{0}\}]
is well-covered and shellable, for any graph
G
.
∎

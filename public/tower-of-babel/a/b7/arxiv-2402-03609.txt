A regularity theory for evolution equations with time-measurable pseudo-differential operators in weighted mixed-norm Sobolev-Lipschitz spaces
Jae-Hwan Choi

Abstract
. This paper investigates the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions to evolution equations with time-measurable pseudo-differential operators in weighted mixed-norm Sobolev-Lipschitz spaces. We also explore trace embedding and continuity of solutions.

1.
Introduction and Main results

1.1.
Goal and Setting

Consider the following Cauchy problem:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)+f(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here,
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
represents a pseudo-differential operator defined by

\displaystyle\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(t,\xi)\mathcal{F}[u(t,\cdot)](\xi)\mathrm{e}^{ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(t,\xi)u(t,y)\mathrm{e}^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform with respect to
x
-variables.
The symbol
\psi
belongs to a class
\mathbb{S}(\gamma,\kappa,M)
, which includes symbols of second-order elliptic operators with time-measurable coefficients:

\psi(t,\xi)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a^{ij}(t)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\Longleftrightarrow\psi(t,-i\nabla)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a^{ij}(t)D_{x^{i}x^{j}},

where
\xi=(\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
A(t):=[a^{ij}(t)]_{d\times d}
is a real
d\times d
matrix satisfying ellipticity condition.

Definition 1.1
.

For
M\geq\kappa>0
and
\gamma>0
, the set
\mathbb{S}(\gamma,\kappa,M)
is a collection of complex-valued measurable function
\psi(t,\xi)
satisfying

\Re[-\psi(t,\xi)]\geq\kappa|\xi|^{\gamma},\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

and

|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)|\leq M|\xi|^{\gamma-|\alpha|},\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}),

for any (
d
-dimensional) multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1
.

In this paper, we investigate the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) in weighted mixed norm Sobolev-Lipschitz space
L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}:=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}w(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/p},\quad&p\in[1,\infty)\\
\esssup\limits_{t\in(0,T)}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad&p=\infty.\end{cases}

In this context,
\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes a Lipschitz space of variable order
\phi
.
Additionally, the weight function
w
belongs to the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
class.

Definition 1.2

(Lipschitz space of variable order
\phi
)
.

(i)
For a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we define

s_{\phi}(\lambda):=\sup_{t>0}\frac{\phi(\lambda t)}{\phi(t)}\,,

such that
s_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}+\rightarrow(0,\infty]
.
For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(a,b):=\{\phi:\text{$s_{\phi}(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{a})$ as $\lambda\downarrow 0$, and $s_{\phi}(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{b})$ as $\lambda\uparrow\infty$}\}.

(1.2)

We also define
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
, where
o
indicates the replacement of the big
O
notation with little
o
notation in (
1.2
).

(ii)
Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
L>0
, and
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,L)
.
The space
\Lambda_{p}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

\Lambda_{p}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{f\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{\Lambda_{p}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\},

where the norm
\|f\|_{\Lambda_{p}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is given by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\Lambda_{p}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\begin{cases}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(|h|^{-1})^{p}\|\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L_{0}}f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{|h|^{d}}\right)^{1/p},\quad&p\in[1,\infty),\\
\sup\limits_{h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(|h|^{-1})\|\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L_{0}}f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad&p=\infty.\end{cases}

Here
\mathcal{D}_{h}f(x):=f(x+h)-f(x)
,
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{n}f(x)=\mathcal{D}_{h}^{n-1}(\mathcal{D}_{h}f)(x)
and
L_{0}
is the smallest integer which is greater than or equal
to
L
.
We denote
\Lambda_{\infty}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 1.3
.

It can be easily checked that
\Lambda_{p}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
encompasses non-separable Banach spaces, such as the classical Hölder space.
For example, if we choose
\phi(\lambda):=\lambda^{\alpha}
(for
\alpha\in(0,1)
), belonging to
\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,1)
, then
L_{0}
is set to 1.
Under these conditions,
\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equivalent to the Hölder space of order
\alpha
, denoted as
C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Additional properties and characteristics of the function classes
\mathcal{I}(a,b)
and
\mathcal{I}_{o}(a,b)
are detailed in
[
4
,
11
]
.

Definition 1.4

(Muckenhoupt’s class)
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, let
A_{p}=A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the class of all nonnegative and locally integrable functions
w
satisfying

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{\mathcal{Q}\,\text{cubes in }\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(x)^{-1/(p-1)}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

(1.3)

The class of
A_{1}=A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as a collection of nonnegative and locally integrable functions
w
satisfying

\mathcal{M}w(x)\leq Cw(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and
C
is independent of
x
.
The class
A_{\infty}=A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
could be defined as the union of
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
i.e.

A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{p\in[1,\infty)}A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

1.2.
Background and Main results

Firstly, we revisit some historical results on the regularity estimates of second-order parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs) within the mixed norm space
L_{p}(\mathbb{R};C^{2+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
The equation of interest is as follows:

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a^{ij}(t)D_{x^{i}x^{j}}u(t,x)+f(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.4)

The case for
p=\infty
, known as the
partial Schauder estimates
, gained prominence following Brandt’s work
[
1
]
.
In
[
1
]
, Brandt demonstrated that, unlike the
classical Schauder estimates
(refer to
e.g.
,
[
14
,
22
]
), the partial Schauder estimates are valid even if
a^{ij}
in (
1.4
) are merely measurable.
Additional references and works on partial Schauder estimates can be found in
[
6
,
9
,
12
,
18
,
21
,
23
]
.

To the best of author’s knowledge, the case for
p\neq\infty
was initially introduced by Krylov in
[
16
,
17
]
. The primary concepts in Krylov’s studies are summarized as follows:

(1)

The main idea of
[
16
]
was the development of a systematic approach, as laid out in
[
15
]
, using the vector-valued Calderón-Zygmund theorem. For further generalizations of
[
15
]
, refer to
[
7
,
8
,
15
,
19
,
24
]
.

(2)

The focus of
[
17
]
was refining Brandt’s estimates (from
[
1
]
) using simple heat potential estimates and well-established properties of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function.

These approaches led to significant generalizations, as seen in
[
13
,
25
]
.
Stinga and Torrea (
[
25
]
) expanded upon Krylov’s methodology in
[
16
]
, proving regularity estimates of solutions to (
1.4
) in
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}t;C^{2+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
w
is a weight function in the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
class.
Kim (
[
13
]
) proposed regularity estimates of solutions to (
1.1
) with zero initial data in
L_{p}((0,T);\Lambda^{\gamma+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
\Lambda^{\gamma+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Lipschitz space of order
\gamma+\alpha
(see Definition
1.2
and Remark
1.3
).
Kim’s main idea in
[
13
]
involved an interpolation between two estimates: a partial Schauder estimate and a weak
L_{1}
estimate.
For these estimates, refined calculations of the fundamental solution (see Definition
2.3
) for (
1.1
) were necessary.

Despite the advancements in the study of evolution equations within mixed norm spaces such as
L_{p}(\mathbb{R};C^{2+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
L_{p}((0,T);\Lambda^{\gamma+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, as evidenced by the aforementioned results, certain fundamental and intriguing questions remain unaddressed.
These questions include:

(1)

Weighted Regularity and Variable Order:
Apart from Stinga and Torrea’s results, all previous studies have focused on non-weighted regularity results in spaces of constant order, such as
C^{2+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This raises the question:
Is it feasible to achieve regularity estimates for (
1.1
) within the weighted mixed norm space
L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
?

(2)

Initial Data and Trace in
L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
:
None of the studies mentioned earlier, such as
[
13
,
16
,
17
,
25
]
, consider the initial data.
This leads to the inquiry:
Are the findings of
[
13
,
16
,
17
,
25
]
still applicable in
L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
when initial data is factored in? If so, what is the optimal initial data space?

(3)

Continuity of the Solution:
When a solution is uniquely determined in our target space
L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,
Can we ascertain Hölder or Zygmund type regularity for these solutions, such as in
C^{\alpha,\beta}_{t,x}
?

(4)

General operators and Strong Solution:
Excluding Kim’s research
[
13
]
, the predominant focus has been on second-order parabolic PDEs, rendering the discussion about the type of solution (mild, weak, or strong) less pertinent. However, Kim’s study
[
13
]
, due to the non-local nature of the main operator
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
, addressed only the regularity estimates of the
weak
solution. This raises a crucial question:
Is it possible to discuss a strong solution for (
1.1
)?

This paper is dedicated to addressing the questions previously outlined.
To achieve this, we have developed a unified approach that synergizes the methodologies of Krylov, as seen in
[
17
]
, with those of Kim from
[
13
]
.
Krylov’s contribution, detailed in
[
17
]
, involves proving the following point-wise estimates using straightforward heat potential calculations,

[D^{2}_{x}u(t,\cdot)]_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{2r}\int_{t-r}^{t+r}\left[\partial_{s}u(s,\cdot)-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a^{ij}(s)D_{x^{i}x^{j}}u(s,\cdot)\right]_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{d}s,

(1.5)

where
[f(t,\cdot)]_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(t,x)-f(t,y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}
.
In (
1.5
), the right-hand side represents the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator:

\mathcal{M}f(t):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{2r}\int_{t-r}^{t+r}|f(s)|\mathrm{d}s.

Given that
\mathcal{M}:L_{p}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R})
is bounded for
p\in(1,\infty)
(see, for example,
[
10
, Theorem 2.1.6]
), Krylov’s a priori estimates are thus obtained.
In
[
13
]
, Kim proved the partial Schauder estimates and weak
L_{1}
estimates by leveraging the fundamental solution and the Littlewood-Paley characterization of the Lipschitz space. The following estimates were established:

\begin{gathered}\|u\|_{L_{\infty}((0,T);\Lambda^{\gamma+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq N\|\partial_{t}u-\psi(\cdot,-i\nabla)u\|_{L_{\infty}((0,T);\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},\\
\|u\|_{L_{1,\infty}((0,T);\Lambda^{\gamma+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq N\|\partial_{t}u-\psi(\cdot,-i\nabla)u\|_{L_{1}((0,T);\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.\end{gathered}

The Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem gives Kim’s a priori estimates.
Inspired by these results, our strategy is twofold:

•

Deriving estimates on the fundamental solution (Theorem
2.2
).

•

Developing point-wise estimates in the vein of (
1.5
), utilizing the Littlewood-Paley characterization (Lemma
2.6
).

For addressing the initial data and trace, we primarily adopt the approach delineated in
[
3
,
4
]
, which elaborates on results concerning the initial data and trace in the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{2}\,\mathrm{d}x))
.

Here are the main results of this paper.
The proofs will be given in Section
2
and
3
.

Definition 1.5

(Solution space)
.

For
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,
L>0
,
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,L)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
, denote

\phi_{\gamma}(\lambda):=\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\gamma},\quad\phi_{\gamma,p,w}(\lambda):=\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\lambda^{-\gamma}}w(s)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/p}.

The space
\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a collection of
u\in L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
which satisfies that there exist
u_{0}\in\Lambda_{p}^{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that

u(t,x)=u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\mathrm{d}s

for all
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We also define

\|u\|_{\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|u\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|u_{0}\|_{\Lambda_{p}^{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

Theorem 1.6

(Existence, Uniqueness, and Regularity of solutions)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty]
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
\psi\in\mathbb{S}(\gamma,\kappa,M)
and
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,L)
.
Then for
u_{0}\in\Lambda_{p}^{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
to

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)+f(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.6)

with the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N\left(\|u_{0}\|_{{\Lambda_{p}^{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right),

(1.7)

where
N=N(d,\delta,\gamma,\kappa,M,p,T,[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R})})
.

Remark 1.7
.

Theorem
1.6
partially holds for the case
p=1
:

(i)

If
f\equiv 0
, then Theorem
1.6
is valid for the case
p=1
.

(ii)

Theorem
1.6
holds when the solution
u
is in the weak space
L_{1,\infty}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, rather than in
L_{1}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Here, the norm of
L_{1,\infty}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is defined as

\|u\|_{L_{1,\infty}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda w(\{t\in(0,T):\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geq\lambda\}),

where
w(A):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{A}(t)w(t)\mathrm{d}t
.

Corresponding estimates for cases (i) and (ii) will be detailed in Theorem
2.5
.

Theorem 1.8

(Embedding theorems)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty]
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,L)
and
u\in\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(i)

(Trace embedding)
Then there exists a constant
N=N(p,[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R})})
such that,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\Lambda_{p}^{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N\|u\|_{\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1.8)

(ii)

(Continuity of solutions)
Then there exists a constant
N=N(L_{0},p,[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R})})
such that,

\|u\|_{C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{(s,h_{1},h_{2})\in D_{T}}\frac{\|\mathcal{D}_{(h_{1}/L_{0},h_{2})}^{L_{0}}u(s,\cdot)\|_{C(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\widetilde{W}(s+h_{1},s)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}+\phi_{\gamma,p,w}(|h_{2}|^{-1})^{-1}}\leq N\|u\|_{\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
\widetilde{W}(t,s)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}:=\left(\int_{s}^{t}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(r)\mathrm{d}r\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,
\mathcal{D}_{(h_{1},h_{2})}u(t,x)(=\mathcal{D}_{(h_{1},h_{2})}^{1}u(t,x)):=u(t+h_{1},x+h_{2})-u(t,x)
,
\mathcal{D}_{(h_{1},h_{2})}^{n+1}u(t,x):=\mathcal{D}_{(h_{1},h_{2})}^{n}(\mathcal{D}_{(h_{1},h_{2})}u)(t,x)
and
D_{T}:=\{(s,h_{1},h_{2}):0\leq s\leq s+h_{1}\leq T,h_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
.

Remark 1.9
.

If
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
w(t):=1
and
\phi(\lambda):=\lambda^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,\gamma)
, then

\widetilde{W}(s+h_{1},s)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\simeq h_{1}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},\quad\phi_{\gamma,p,w}(\lambda)\simeq\lambda^{\alpha+\gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}.

This implies that

C_{t,x}^{\widetilde{W}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=C_{t,x}^{1-\frac{1}{p},\alpha+\gamma-\frac{\gamma}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.9)

where
C_{t,x}^{\widetilde{W}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes a normed space with the norm defined as

\|u\|_{C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{(s,h_{1},h_{2})\in D_{T}}\frac{\|\mathcal{D}_{(h_{1}/L_{0},h_{2})}^{L_{0}}u(s,\cdot)\|_{C(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\tilde{W}(s+h_{1},s)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}+\phi_{\gamma,p,w}(|h_{2}|^{-1})^{-1}},

and

C_{t,x}^{1-\frac{1}{p},\alpha+\gamma-\frac{\gamma}{p}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the anisotropic Hölder space with the norm defined as

\|u\|_{C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{(t,x),(s,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(t,x)-u(s,y)|}{|t-s|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}+|x-y|^{\alpha+\gamma-\frac{\gamma}{p}}}.

For general
w
and
\phi
, the equivalence such as (
1.9
) cannot be expected.
However, there is a relation between
C_{t,x}^{\widetilde{W}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the classical anisotropic Hölder spaces.
Drawing from
[
10
, Proposition 7.1.5-(8), Corollary 7.2.6, and Proposition 7.2.8]
and
[
4
, Lemma 2.3]
, there exists an
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
such that the following inequalities hold:

|h_{1}|^{1-\varepsilon}\lesssim\widetilde{W}(s+h_{1},s)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim|h_{1}|^{\varepsilon},\quad|h_{2}|^{L+\gamma-\varepsilon}\lesssim\phi_{\gamma,p,w}(|h_{2}|^{-1})^{-1}\lesssim|h_{2}|^{\varepsilon},

where
(s,h_{1},h_{2})\in D_{T}
and
|h_{2}|\leq 1
.
Consequently, we can deduce that

C_{t,x}^{\varepsilon,\varepsilon}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq C_{t,x}^{\widetilde{W}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq C_{t,x}^{1-\varepsilon,L+\gamma-\varepsilon}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

1.3.
Further research directions

This study focused on evolution equations with time-measurable pseudo-differential operators independent of the spatial variable
x
.
However, several intriguing extensions remain unexplored:

(1)

x
-dependent operators.

The results presented in this paper, as well as prior studies, focus on leading operators
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
that are independent of the spatial variable
x
.
Extending the theory to spatially dependent operators, such as second-order elliptic operators in either divergence or non-divergence form, is a natural and significant next step.
For instance:

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a^{ij}(t,x)D_{x^{i}x^{j}},\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}D_{x^{i}}\left(a^{ij}(t,x)D_{x^{j}}\right).

(2)

Variable-order non-local operators.

The current study assumes operators of constant order
\gamma
.
Exploring variable-order non-local operators, such as the infinitesimal generators of subordinate Brownian motions, would be an interesting extension.

I am preparing research to address these extensions, focusing on the challenges posed by spatially dependent operators and variable-order non-local operators.

\mathbb{N}
are the set of all real numbers, rational numbers, integers, and natural numbers, respectively.

\bullet
For a set
\{a,b,\ldots\}
and
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}
, we say
X\lesssim_{a,b,\ldots}Y
, if
X\leq NY
holds for some
N=N(a,b,\ldots)>0
. Sometimes we omit
a,b,\ldots
and just say
X\lesssim Y
if the dependency on
a,b,\ldots
is clear from the context.

\bullet

For given
p\in[1,\infty)
, a normed space
F
, and a measure space
(X,\mathcal{M},\mu)
,
L_{p}(X,\mathcal{M},\mu;F)
denotes the space of all
\mathcal{M}^{\mu}
-measurable functions
u:X\to F
with the norm

\left\|u\right\|_{L_{p}(X,\mathcal{M},\mu;F)}:=\left(\int_{X}\left\|u(x)\right\|_{F}^{p}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/p}<\infty

where
\mathcal{M}^{\mu}
denotes the completion of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to the measure
\mu
. We also denote by
L_{\infty}(X,\mathcal{M},\mu;F)
the space of all
\mathcal{M}^{\mu}
-measurable functions
u:X\to F
with the norm

\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(X,\mathcal{M},\mu;F)}:=\inf\left\{r\geq 0:\mu(\{x\in X:\|u(x)\|_{F}\geq r\})=0\right\}<\infty.

If there is no confusion for the given measure and
\sigma
-algebra, we usually omit them.

\bullet

For
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a normed space
F
, we denote by
C(\mathcal{O};F)
the space of all
F
-valued continuous functions
u:\mathcal{O}\to F
with the norm

|u|_{C}:=\sup_{x\in O}|u(x)|_{F}<\infty.

\bullet
For
p\in[1,\infty]
,
p^{\prime}:=\frac{p}{p-1}
is the Hölder conjugate (
\frac{1}{0}:=\infty
and
\frac{\infty}{\infty}:=1
).

\bullet

1_{A}
is a characteristic function on a set
A
.

2.
Existence, Uniqueness, and Regularity of solutions: Proof of Theorem
1.6

In this section, we focus on proving Theorem
1.6
.
We begin by introducing the Littlewood-Paley characterization of the space
\Lambda_{p}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We choose a function
\Psi
from the Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, whose Fourier transform,
\mathcal{F}[\Psi]
, is nonnegative, supported within the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
.
We ensure that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)=1
for all
\xi\not=0
.
Then, we define the Littlewood-Paley projection operators
\Delta_{j}
and
S_{0}
as

\Delta_{j}f(x):=(\Psi_{j}\ast f)(x),\quad S_{0}f(x):=(\Phi\ast f)(x),

where
\Psi_{j}(x):=2^{jd}\Psi(2^{j}x)
and
\Phi(x):=\sum_{j\leq 0}\Psi_{j}(x)
, respectively.
For
p\in[1,\infty]
,
L>0
, and
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,L)
, the norm equivalence for
\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{\phi}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is established as follows, as presented in
[
5
]
:

\|f\|_{\Lambda_{p}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\simeq\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{p}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad&p\in[1,\infty),\\
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\phi(2^{j})\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad&p=\infty,\end{cases}

(2.1)

Henceforth, we will utilize the norm equivalence presented in (
2.1
) in place of Definition
1.2
.

2.1.
Continuity of operators

In general, functions within
\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are not integrable, as evidenced by non-zero constant functions.
Consequently, the expression
\psi(t,-i\nabla)f
for any
f\in\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined in the sense of tempered distributions.
To discuss a strong solution, it is necessary to propose an alternative definition for the operator
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
.

Let us consider
\psi\in\mathbb{S}(\gamma,\kappa,M)
and
f\in\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We define the following:

\begin{gathered}\psi_{j}(t,-i\nabla)f(x):=\sum_{k=-1}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(t,-i\nabla)\Psi_{j+k}(y)\Delta_{j}f(x-y)\mathrm{d}y,\\
\psi_{0}(t,-i\nabla)f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(t,-i\nabla)(\Phi+\Psi_{1})(x)S_{0}f(x-y)\mathrm{d}y.\end{gathered}

(2.2)

and

\tilde{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)f(x):=\psi_{0}(t,-i\nabla)f(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\psi_{j}(t,-i\nabla)f(x).

(2.3)

If
f\in\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the almost orthogonality of
\Delta_{j}
and
S_{0}
implies that:

\tilde{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\psi(t,\cdot)\mathcal{F}[f]](x)=:\psi(t,-i\nabla)f(x).

Henceforth, the notation
\psi(t,-i\nabla)f
for any
f\in\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes
\tilde{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)f
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbb{S}(\gamma,\kappa,M)
and
f\in\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(i)

Then for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(t,-i\nabla)f(x)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{0}(t,-i\nabla)f(x)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{j}(t,-i\nabla)f(x)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\bar{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x,

where
\bar{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)\varphi(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\overline{\psi(t,\cdot)}\mathcal{F}[\varphi]\right](x)
and
\overline{\psi(t,\xi)}
is the complex conjegate of
\psi(t,\xi)
.

(ii)

Then
\psi(t,-i\nabla):\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a bounded linear operator:

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)f\|_{\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N(d,\gamma,M,\Phi,\Psi)\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

(i)
Due to the dominated convergence theorem, the first equality holds.
If we use Fubini’s theorem, Plancherel’s theorem, and the dominated convergence theorem, then the second equality is also obtained

(ii)
By
(i)
and almost orthogonal property of Littlewood-Paley operators,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(\psi(t,-i\nabla)f)(x)

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{j-1}+\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j+1})\Delta_{j}(\psi(t,-i\nabla)f)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Delta_{j-1}+\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j+1})f(x-y)\bar{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)\Psi_{j}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

To complete the proof, it suffices to prove that
for
c>0
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)h_{c}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N(d,\gamma,h,M)c^{\gamma},

(2.4)

where
h_{c}(x):=c^{d}h(cx)
and
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Indeed, if we have (
2.4
), then

\displaystyle\phi(2^{j})\|\Delta_{j}(\psi(t,-i\nabla)f)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq N\phi(2^{j})\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)\Psi_{j}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{k=-1}^{1}\|\Delta_{j+k}f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq N\phi(2^{j})2^{j\gamma}\sum_{k=-1}^{1}\|\Delta_{j+k}f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq N(d,\gamma,M,\Psi)\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Similarly, we also have

\|S_{0}(\psi(t,-i\nabla)f)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N(d,\gamma,M,\Phi,\Psi)\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We end the proof by proving (
2.4
).
First, we decompose
\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)h_{c}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
into two parts;

\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)h_{c}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\int_{|x|\leq c^{-1}}\cdots\mathrm{d}x+\int_{|x|>c^{-1}}\cdots\mathrm{d}x=:I_{c,1}(t)+I_{c,2}(t).

By the assumptions on
\psi
and the change of variable formula, we have

\displaystyle|I_{c,1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq Nc^{-d}\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)h_{c}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leq Nc^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\gamma}|\mathcal{F}[h](c^{-1}\xi)|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=Nc^{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}[h](\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi=N(d,\gamma,h,M)c^{\gamma}.

Since
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the integration
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}[h](\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi
in (
2.5
) is finite.
For
I_{c,2}
, we use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Plancherel’s theorem;

\displaystyle|I_{c,2}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{|x|>c^{-1}}|x|^{-2d_{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{|x|>c^{-1}}|x|^{2d_{2}}|\psi(t,-i\nabla)h_{c}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq N(d,\gamma,h,M)c^{d_{2}-d/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|D_{\xi}^{d_{2}}\left(\psi(t,\cdot)\mathcal{F}[h](c^{-1}\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2}\leq Nc^{\gamma},

where
d_{2}=\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1
.
The theorem is proved.
∎

2.2.
Notion of solutions and their properties

In this subsection, we discuss the notion of solutions to

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)+f(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(2.6)

It turns out that

\displaystyle p(t,s,x):=1_{0\leq s<t}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\mathrm{e}^{ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi+1_{t=s}\delta_{0}(x).

is the
fundamental solution
of (
2.6
),
i.e.
,
p(\cdot,s,\cdot)
satisfies

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}p(t,s,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)p(t,s,x),\quad(t,x)\in(s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
p(s,s,x)=\delta_{0}(x).\end{cases}

Here
\delta_{0}
is the centered Dirac delta distribution.
The sharp
L_{p}
-estimates for the fundamental solution and their proof were presented in
[
2
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
3
, Theorem 5.1, Corollary 5.3]
, thus, we present the following theorem without proofs.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\psi\in\mathbb{S}(\gamma,\kappa,M)
.
Then for
m\in\{0,1\}
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive constant
N=N(d,\delta,\gamma,\kappa,M,m)
such that for all
t>s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}\Delta_{j}p(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa|t-s|2^{j\gamma}\times\frac{(1-\delta)}{2^{\gamma}}}2^{jm\gamma},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}S_{0}p(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N,

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}p(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N(t-s)^{-m},

\displaystyle\left\||\cdot|^{d_{2}}\partial_{t}^{m}p(t,s,\cdot)\right\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N(t-s)^{-m+\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(d_{2}-\frac{d}{2}\right)},

where
d_{2}:=\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1
.

In general, the symbol
\psi(t,\xi)
is complex-valued, thus

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,y)\mathrm{d}y=\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,0)\mathrm{d}r\right)\neq 1.

However,

\lim_{t\downarrow s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,y)\mathrm{d}y=1.

(2.7)

Now, we provide the concept of solutions.

Definition 2.3

(Solutions)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty]
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,L)
,
\psi\in\mathbb{S}(\gamma,\kappa,M)
,
u_{0}\in\Lambda_{p}^{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

(i)

We say that

u(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,0,x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

is the
mild solution
of (
2.6
).

(ii)

We say that
u\in L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is a
weak solution
of (
2.6
) if for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(t,x)-u_{0}(x))\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(u(s,x)\bar{\psi}(s,-i\nabla)\varphi(x)+f(s,x)\varphi(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

for all
t\in[0,T)
.
Here
\bar{\psi}(s,-i\nabla)
is a pseudo-differential operator with a symbol
\overline{\psi(s,\xi)}
.

(iii)

We say that
u\in L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is a
strong solution
of (
2.6
) if

u(t,x)=u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}(\psi(s,-i\nabla)u(s,x)+f(s,x))\mathrm{d}s

for all
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Here
\psi(s,-i\nabla)u(s,x)
is a function defined in (
2.3
).

Remark 2.4
.

Due to Theorem
2.1
, if
u
is a strong solution to (
2.6
), then
u
is a weak solution also.

For the mild solution of (
2.6
), the following regularity estimate holds, and this plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,L)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\psi\in\mathbb{S}(\gamma,\kappa,M)
.
Let also

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{G}f(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s,\\
\mathcal{G}_{0}u_{0}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,0,x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y.\end{gathered}

(i)
If
p\in(1,\infty]
and
f\in{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
, then
\mathcal{G}f\in{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
;

\|\mathcal{G}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq N\|f\|_{{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}},

where
N=N(d,\delta,\gamma,\kappa,M,p,T,[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R})})
.

(ii)
If
f\in{L_{1}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
, then
\mathcal{G}f\in L_{1,\infty}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
;

\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda w(\{t\in(0,T):\|\mathcal{G}f(t,\cdot)\|_{\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geq\lambda\})\leq N\|f\|_{{L_{1}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}},

where
N=N(d,\delta,\gamma,\kappa,M,T,[w]_{A_{1}(\mathbb{R})})
.

(iii)
If
p\in(0,\infty]
,
w\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
u_{0}\in\Lambda^{{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\|\mathcal{G}_{0}u_{0}\|_{L_{p}((0,T),w\,\mathrm{d}t;\Lambda^{\phi_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\Lambda^{{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
N=N(d,\delta,\gamma,\kappa,M,p,T,[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R})})
.

The proof of Theorem
2.5
will be provided in Section
2.4
.

2.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

In this subsection, we claim that the mild solution of (
2.6
) is a unique strong solution of (
2.6
).

Uniqueness.

Let
u
be a strong solution to

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=0.\end{cases}

By Remark
2.4
and
the almost orthogonality of
\Delta_{j}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(s,y)\bar{\psi}(s,-i\nabla)\Psi_{j}(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{j}u(s,y)\bar{\psi}(s,-i\nabla)(\Psi_{j-1}+\Psi_{j}+\Psi_{j+1})(x-y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, by Minkowski’s inequality and (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{\psi}(s,-i\nabla)\Psi_{j+k}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\Delta_{j}u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq N2^{j\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta_{j}u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{d}s.

Due to Grönwall’s inequality, for all
t\in[0,T)
,
\|\Delta_{j}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0
.
Similarly, we also have for all
t\in[0,T)
,
\|S_{0}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0
.
This implies that for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\left(S_{0}\varphi(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{j}\varphi(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)S_{0}\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\Delta_{j}\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S_{0}u(t,x)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{j}u(t,x)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\|S_{0}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{j}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)=0

for all
t\in[0,T)
.
By the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations,
u=0
.
The uniqueness is proved.

Existence and estimates.

Let
u
be the mild solution of (
2.6
).
By the definition of
\psi(s,-i\nabla)
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\psi(s,-i\nabla)u(s,x)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(s,-i\nabla)p(s,0,x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(s,-i\nabla)p(s,r,x-y)f(r,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s.

Since
p
is the fundamental solution, by the Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(s,-i\nabla)p(s,0,x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(s,-i\nabla)p(s,r,x-y)f(r,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{s}p(s,0,x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{s}p(s,r,x-y)f(r,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(s,0,x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r}^{t}\partial_{s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(s,r,x-y)f(r,y)\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=u(t,x)-\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(s,0,x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y-\lim_{s\downarrow r}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(s,r,x-y)f(r,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}r.

If we have

\lim_{t\downarrow r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,r,x-y)h(y)\mathrm{d}y=h(x)

(2.8)

for
h\in\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then by the fundamental theorem of calculus, the existence is proved.

Since
h\in\Lambda^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

|x-y|<\delta\implies|h(x)-h(y)|<\varepsilon.

This implies that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,r,x-y)h(y)\mathrm{d}y-h(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{|x-y|<\delta}p(t,r,x-y)h(y)\mathrm{d}y-h(x)\right|+\left|\int_{|x-y|\geq\delta}p(t,r,x-y)h(y)\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x-y|<\delta}|p(t,r,x-y)||h(y)-h(x)|\mathrm{d}y+\left|\left(\int_{|x-y|<\delta}p(t,r,x-y)\mathrm{d}y-1\right)h(x)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\|h\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{|x-y|\geq\delta}|p(t,r,x-y)|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{|x-y|<\delta}|p(t,r,x-y)|\mathrm{d}y+\|h\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left|\int_{|x-y|<\delta}p(t,r,x-y)\mathrm{d}y-1\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\|h\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{|x-y|\geq\delta}|p(t,r,x-y)|\mathrm{d}y.

By Theorem

\displaystyle\leq N\|u\|_{\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{L_{0}}_{(h_{1}/L_{0},h_{2})}u(s,x)|}{\widetilde{W}(s+h_{1},s)^{1-1/p}+\phi_{\gamma,p,w}(|h_{2}|^{-1})^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{L_{0}}\frac{|u(s+ih_{1}/L_{0},\cdot)-u(s+(i-1)h_{1}/L_{0},\cdot)|_{C(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\widetilde{W}(s+h_{1},s)^{1-1/p}}+\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{L_{0}}_{h_{2}}u(\cdot,x)|_{C((0,T))}}{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}(|h_{2}|^{-1})^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{L_{0}}\frac{|u(s+ih_{1}/L_{0},\cdot)-u(s+(i-1)h_{1}/L_{0},\cdot)|_{C(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\widetilde{W}(s+ih_{1}/L_{0},s+(i-1)h_{1}/L_{0})^{1-1/p}}+\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{L_{0}}_{h_{2}}u(\cdot,x)|_{C((0,T))}}{\phi_{\gamma,p,w}(|h_{2}|^{-1})^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq N\|u\|_{\mathbf{H}_{p,w}^{\phi,{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The theorem is proved.
∎

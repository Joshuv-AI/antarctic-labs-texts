Introduction

In this paper, we consider the 3D defocusing, cubic wave equation with zero Dirichlet boundary value outside of the ball

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{t}u-\Delta u+u^{3}=0,&(t,x)\in I\times\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,x)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
0\in I
, the exterior domain
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\bar{B}(0,1)

u:I\times\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})

\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{s-1}_{D}(\Omega)

\displaystyle E(u)(t)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{t}u(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|u(t,x)|^{4}\right)\;dx=E(u)(0),\quad\forall\,t\in I.

(1.2)

Definition 1.1

The equation (

(2)

2.
Distorted Fourier Transform and Littlewood-Paley theory

In this section, we consider the 3D Dirichlet-Laplacian operator outside of the ball
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\bar{B}(0,1)
, which we denote by
-\Delta_{\Omega}
. we recall the distorted Fourier transform adapted to the operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
in the radial case from
[
35
,
36
]
and the Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D}(\Omega)
in
[
30
]
. We can also refer to
[
1
,
39
,
50
]
for spectral properties of Schrödinger operator and
[
2
,
21
,
38
,
47
]
for classical Fourier analysis and the Littlewood-Paley theory on the whole space.

The general spectral theory of the Dirichlet-Laplacian operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
is standard. The essential spectrum of the operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
is
[0,\infty)
; The operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
has no positive eigenvalues embedded into
(0,\infty)
and has no negative eigenvalues; Moreover
0
is not an eigenvalue or a resonance of the operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
.

The spectral resolution for radial functions on
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\bar{B}(0,1)
is expressed by the radial, generalized eigenfunctions

-\Delta\;e_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}\;e_{\lambda}

for all
\lambda>0
, which satisfy the Sommerfeld radiation condition, namely

e_{\lambda}(r)=\frac{\sin\lambda(r-1)}{r},\quad r=|x|\geq 1,

(2.1)

which palys the similar rule as the functions
(r\lambda)^{-1/2}\cdot J_{1/2}(r\lambda)
for the radial funtion on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
J_{1/2}(r\lambda)
is Bessel function of order
1/2
(see
[
21
]
).

For the radial, tempered distributions
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, supported on
\Omega
, we denote the distorted Fourier transformation
\mathcal{F}_{D}
for
\lambda>0
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{D}f(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{1}e_{\lambda}(s)f(s)s^{2}\;ds=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{\sin\lambda(s-1)}{s}f(s)s^{2}\;ds.

(2.2)

Note that the following resolution of identity

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}e_{\lambda}(r)\,e_{\lambda}(s)\,d\lambda=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}e_{\lambda}(r)\,e_{\lambda}(s)\,d\lambda=\frac{\delta(r-s)}{s^{2}},

from which it follows that

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{D}\mathcal{F}_{D}f=f

for the radial function
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
supported in
\Omega
, where
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{D}
is the formal adjoint, defined on the tempered distributions
g
as the restriction to
r=|x|\geq 1
of

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{D}g(r)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{0}e_{\lambda}(r)g(\lambda)\;d\lambda=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\sin\lambda(r-1)}{r}g(\lambda)\;d\lambda.

(2.3)

Consequently,
f\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{D}f
induces an isometric map

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{D}:\;L^{2}\left([1,\infty),s^{2}\,ds\right)\longrightarrow L^{2}\left([0,\infty),\,d\lambda\right)

It is worth noting that unlike classical Fourier transform on the whole space, the spectral supports are not additive under function multiplication for the above distorted Fourier transform.

Given a bounded function
m(\lambda)
, which for convenience we assume to be defined on all of
\mathbb{R}
and even in
\lambda
, and radial function
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, we define

\displaystyle m\left(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}\right)f(r)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{D}\big(m(\cdot)\,\mathcal{F}_{D}f\big)(r).

This defines a functional calculus on
L^{2}_{rad}(\Omega)
and takes the expression as

\displaystyle m(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})f(r)=\int^{\infty}_{1}K_{m}(r,s)f(s)\,s^{2}\,ds

with

\displaystyle K_{m}(r,s)=\frac{2}{\pi}\,\int^{\infty}_{0}e_{\lambda}(r)\,e_{\lambda}(s)\,m(\lambda)\;d\lambda.

In general, we have the following Mikhlin Multiplier theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
30
,
35
]
)
.

Suppose
m:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
obeys

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{k}_{\lambda}m(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-k}

for all integer
k\in[0,2]
. Then
m(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})
, which we define via the
L^{2}
functional calculus, extends uniquely from
L^{2}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\Omega)
, for all
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

In the radial case, we can use the Schur’s Lemma and interpolation to obtain the result, please refer to
[
35
]
for more details, and in general case, we need to use the heat kernel estimate on the exterior domain
\Omega
in
[
51
]
and classical Calderón-Zygmund theory to show the boundedness result, please see more details in
[
30
]
.
∎

More Mikhlin multiplier results adapted to potential scattering, we can refer to
[
19
,
40
]
. Based on the above Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem, we can now describe basic ingredients of Littlewood-Paley theory adapted to the Dirichlet-Laplacian operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
.
Fix
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
a smooth non-negative function obeying

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda)=1\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq\lambda\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\phi(\lambda)=0\quad\text{for}\quad\lambda\geq 2.

For each dyadic number
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(\lambda):=\phi(\lambda/N)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{N}(\lambda):=\phi_{N}(\lambda)-\phi_{N/2}(\lambda).

Notice that
\big\{\psi_{N}(\lambda)\big\}_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}
forms a partition of unity for
(0,\infty)
. With these functions, we now define the Littlewood-Paley projections:

\displaystyle P^{\Omega}_{\leq N}f:=\phi_{N}(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})f,\quad P^{\Omega}_{N}f:=\psi_{N}(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})f,\quad P^{\Omega}_{>N}f:=I-P^{\Omega}_{\leq N}f,

and

\displaystyle\tilde{P}^{\Omega}_{N}f:=\tilde{\psi}_{N}\left(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}\right)f=\psi_{N-1}\left(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}\right)f+\psi_{N}\left(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}\right)f+\psi_{N+1}\left(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}\right)f.

We introduce the homogeneous Besov space as the following.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
. The homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{D,q,r}(\Omega)
consists of the distributions
f
supported on
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{D,q,r}(\Omega)}:=\left(\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}N^{sr}\|P^{\Omega}_{N}f\|^{r}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/r}<\infty.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
30
,
35
]
)
.

For any radial function
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|P^{\Omega}_{\leq N}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+

\displaystyle\left\|P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

(2.4)

\displaystyle N^{s}\left\|P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\approx\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

(2.5)

for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left\|P^{\Omega}_{\leq N}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\left\|P^{\Omega}_{N}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\lesssim N^{3\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\big)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

for all
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. The implicit constants depend only on
p,q
and
s
.

In general case, the estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) only hold for
1<p<\infty
by Theorem
2.1
. In the radial case, the correspoding integral kernels have good prpperties by the distorted Fourier transform, see more details in
[
35
]
.
Therefore, for any
1<p<\infty
and any
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, we have the following homogeneous decomposition

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}P^{\Omega}_{N}f(x).

In particular, the sums converge in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.4
.

For
s\geq 0
and
1<p<\infty
, Let
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D}(\Omega)
and
H^{s,p}_{D}(\Omega)
denote the completions of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
under the norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D}}:=\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{H^{s,p}_{D}}:=\left\|\big(I-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

When
p=2
, we write
\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)
and
H^{s}_{D}(\Omega)
for
\dot{H}^{s,2}_{D}(\Omega)
and
H^{s,2}_{D}(\Omega)
, respectively.

We will use the radial Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D,rad}(\Omega)
and
H^{s,p}_{D,rad}(\Omega)
in the context. If the radial function
f
belongs to
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{D}(-\Delta_{\Omega}f)(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}\mathcal{F}_{D}(f)(\lambda),

and
\mathcal{F}_{D}
induces an isometric map

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{D}:\;\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\longrightarrow L^{2}\left([0,\infty),\lambda^{2}\,d\lambda\right).

The Littlewood-Paley square function estimates and the dense result then follow from the multiplier theorem by the usual argument.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
30
]
)
.

Fix
1<p<\infty
and
s\geq 0
. Then for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\sim\left\|\left(\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}N^{2s}\big|P^{\Omega}_{N}f(x)\big|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
30
]
)
.

For
1<p<\infty
, and
s<1+\frac{1}{p}
,
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
is dense in
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D}(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.7
.

The space
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{00}(\Omega)
and
H^{s,p}_{00}(\Omega)
are defined as the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in
\dot{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, respectively.

At last, the equivalence between
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D}(\Omega)
and
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{00}(\Omega)
with proper exponents then follows from the Hardy’s inequalities for the exterior domain
\Omega
and the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in
[
30
]
.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
30
]
)
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
and
0\leq s<\min\{1+\frac{1}{p},\frac{3}{p}\}
, then for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\approx_{p,s}\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big)^{s/2}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

Thus
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D}(\Omega)=\dot{H}^{s,p}_{00}(\Omega)
for these values of the parameters.

Remark 2.9
.

As the direct corollary of the above equivalence, the fractional product rule directly follows from the classical Euclidean setting. More precisely, for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}\big(fg\big)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\Omega)}\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\Omega)}

with the exponents satisfying

,
1<q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}},\quad 0<s<\min\left(1+\frac{1}{p_{1}},1+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\frac{3}{p_{1}},\frac{3}{p_{2}}\right).

And the fractional chain rule holds in a similar way.

3.
Linear estimates

In this part, we will make use of the distorted Fourier transform adapted to the Dirichlet-Laplacian operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
to show the dispersive estimate and the global-in-time (endpoint) Strichartz estimate of the linear wave equation with zero Dirichlet boundary value outside of the ball with radial data, we can also refer to
[
35
,
36
]
for the dispersive and Strichartz estimates of the Schrödinger equation outside of the ball with radial data.

Now we consider the 3D Dirichlet boundary value problem of the linear wave equation with radial data

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{t}u-\Delta u=F,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,x)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\bar{B}(0,1)
and
u_{0},u_{1}
and the inhomogeneous term
F
are radial in
x
. By the funcional calculus, we have

u(t,x)=\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})u_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}u_{1}+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}F(s,x)\,ds.

Let us denote the half-wave operator as

U(t)=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}},

then

\displaystyle\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})u_{0}=\frac{U(t)+U(-t)}{2}u_{0},\quad\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}u_{1}=\frac{U(t)-U(-t)}{2i\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}u_{1}.

By the distorted Fourier transform and the stationary phase estimate, we have the following uniform dispersive estimate in the radial case.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
2\leq r\leq\infty
, and the radial function
f
is supported on
\Omega
, then

\displaystyle\big\|U(t)f\big\|_{\dot{B}^{-\beta(r)}_{D,r,2}(\Omega)}\lesssim|t|^{-\gamma(r)}\big\|f\big\|_{\dot{B}^{\beta(r)}_{D,r^{\prime},2}(\Omega)}

where
\beta(r)=\gamma(r)=1-\frac{2}{r}
.

Proof.

Taking the distorted Fourier transform, we have

\displaystyle\big\|U(t)f\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\big\|f\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, by the interpolation theorem, it suffices to show the following uniform dispersive estimate

\displaystyle\big\|U(t)f\big\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{D,\infty,2}(\Omega)}\lesssim|t|^{-1}\big\|f\big\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{D,1,2}(\Omega)}.

(3.2)

Notice the fact that
P^{\Omega}_{N}f(r)=\tilde{P}^{\Omega}_{N}P^{\Omega}_{N}f(r)
for
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
r=|x|\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle U(t)P^{\Omega}_{N}f(r)=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{1}e_{\lambda}(r)e_{\lambda}(s)e^{it|\lambda|}\tilde{\psi}_{N}(\lambda)\big(P^{\Omega}_{N}f\big)(s)s^{2}\;ds\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{1}K_{N}(t,r;s)\big(P^{\Omega}_{N}f\big)(s)s^{2}\;ds

(3.3)

where the integral kernel
K_{N}(t,r;s)
is

\displaystyle K_{N}(t,r;s)=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int^{+\infty}_{0}e_{\lambda}(r)e_{\lambda}(s)e^{it|\lambda|}\tilde{\psi}_{N}(\lambda)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{\sin\lambda(r-1)}{r}\frac{\sin\lambda(s-1)}{s}e^{it|\lambda|}\tilde{\psi}_{N}(|\lambda|)\;d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{\sin N\lambda(r-1)}{r}\frac{\sin N\lambda(s-1)}{s}e^{itN|\lambda|}\tilde{\psi}_{0}(|\lambda|)\;d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N^{3}}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{s-1}{s}\frac{r-1}{r}\frac{\sin N\lambda(s-1)}{N(s-1)}\frac{\sin N\lambda(r-1)}{N(r-1)}e^{iNt|\lambda|}\psi_{0}(|\lambda|)d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N^{3}}{\pi}\frac{r-1}{r}\frac{s-1}{s}e^{it{\sqrt{-\Delta_{rad}}}}(N(s-1),N(r-1);Nt)\psi_{0}

for the usual radial wave propagator

e^{it{\sqrt{-\Delta_{rad}}}}(t,r;s)f:=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin\lambda s}{s}\frac{\sin\lambda r}{r}e^{it|\lambda|}f(\lambda)\,d\lambda,\quad r,s>0.

In fact, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{rad}}}f=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{it|\xi|}\mathcal{F}f(\xi)).

where
\mathcal{F}^{\pm 1}
is the usual Fourier transform for the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
. By the properties of Bessel function
J_{n}(r)
and van der Corput’s lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{rad}}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim|t|^{-1},

which together with Young’s inequality implies that

\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{rad}}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim|t|^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

By the supported property of
\psi_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{r,s\geq 1}\big|K_{N}(t,r;s)\big|\lesssim N^{3}\,|Nt|^{-1}\,\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim|t|^{-1}N^{2}.

(3.4)

By (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and the Minkowski inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|U(t)P^{\Omega}_{N}f\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim|t|^{-1}N^{2}\big\|P^{\Omega}_{N}f\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

which impies the result and completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(Endpoint Strichartz estimates with radial data)
.

Let
u_{0},u_{1}
and
F
be radial in
x
variable, and
u:I\times\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a solution to linear wave equation (
3.1
) with
0\in I
. If
q>4
and

s=1-3/q,

then we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(I\times\Omega)}\lesssim\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{s-1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{1}_{t}\left(I;\,\dot{H}^{s-1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)}.

Proof.

By the energy estimate, it suffices to show the following homogeneous estimate for the half wave operator
U(t)

\displaystyle\big\|U(t)f\big\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(I\times\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}.

By the distorted Fourier transform once again, we have

\displaystyle U(t)f(r)=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\sin(\lambda(r-1))}{r}\,e^{it|\lambda|}\left(\mathcal{F}_{D}f\right)(\lambda)\,d\lambda=\frac{1}{i\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{e^{i\lambda(r-1)}-e^{-i\lambda(r-1)}}{r}\,e^{it|\lambda|}\left(\mathcal{F}_{D}f\right)(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

By the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, we have

\displaystyle U(t)f(r)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{N}e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}\tilde{P}^{\Omega}_{N}P^{\Omega}_{N}f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{N}\frac{1}{i\pi r}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(e^{i\lambda(r-1)}-e^{-i\lambda(r-1)}\right)\,e^{it|\lambda|}\tilde{\psi}_{N}(\lambda)\left(\mathcal{F}_{D}P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right)(\lambda)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{N}\frac{N}{i\pi r}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(e^{iN\lambda(r-1)}-e^{-iN\lambda(r-1)}\right)\,e^{itN|\lambda|}\tilde{\psi}_{0}(\lambda)\left(\mathcal{F}_{D}P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right)(N\lambda)\,d\lambda.

Notice that
\mathcal{F}_{D}(P^{\Omega}_{N}f)
is supported on
(N/2,2N)
, we can make a decomposition by Fourier series as the following,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}_{D}P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right)(N\lambda)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}^{N}e^{ik\frac{2\pi}{2N}N\lambda},

then we have

\displaystyle e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}\tilde{P}^{\Omega}_{N}P^{\Omega}_{N}f(r)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\,Nc_{k}^{N}}{i\pi r}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(e^{iN\lambda(r-1)}-e^{-iN\lambda(r-1)}\right)\,e^{itN|\lambda|}\tilde{\psi}_{0}(\lambda)e^{i\pi k\lambda}\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\,Nc_{k}^{N}}{i\pi r}\Big(\psi^{+}_{k}(t,r)-\psi^{-}_{k}(t,r)\Big),

where

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}_{k}(t,r)=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{\pm iN\lambda(r-1)}\,e^{itN|\lambda|}\tilde{\psi}_{0}(\lambda)e^{i\pi k\lambda}\,d\lambda.

By the integrating by parts, we have the local dispersive esitmate for any
M\geqslant 1
that

\left|\psi^{\pm}_{k}(t,r)\right|\lesssim\frac{C_{M}}{\big(1+N|t+\frac{\pi k}{N}\pm(r-1)|\big)^{M}}.

Then we can make the simple calculation and obtain

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{D}}}P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{x}(\Omega)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{1}\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}^{N}\,\psi^{\pm}_{k}(t,r)\right|^{q}\frac{1}{r^{q}}r^{2}\;dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{1}\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|c_{k}^{N}|}{\big(1+N|t+\frac{\pi k}{N}\pm(r-1)|\big)^{M}}\right|^{q}r^{2-q}\;dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{|c_{k}^{N}|^{q}}{\big(1+N||t+\frac{\pi k}{N}|-r|\big)^{2}}(1+r)^{2-q}\;dr,

where we choose
M
with
(M-2/q)\cdot q^{\prime}>1
and use the Young inequality in the last inequality. We have for
s=1-\frac{3}{q}
that

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{D}}}P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right\|_{L^{q}_{x}(\Omega)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{|c_{k}^{N}|^{q}}{\big(1+N||t+\frac{\pi k}{N}|-r|\big)^{2}}(1+r)^{2-q}\;dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}N^{q-3}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{|c_{k}^{N}|^{q}}{\big(1+||Nt+\pi k|-r|\big)^{2}}(1+r)^{2-q}\;dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{s}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|c_{k}^{N}|^{q}}{\big(1+|Nt+\pi k|\big)^{q-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim N^{s}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|c_{k}^{N}|^{2}}{\big(1+|Nt+\pi k|\big)^{2-\frac{4}{q}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

where we use the embedding fact that
l^{2}\subset l^{q}
in the last inequality. By Bernstein’s inequality, we have for
q>4
that

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{D}}}P^{\Omega}_{N}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(I\times\Omega)}\lesssim N^{2s}\int_{I}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|c_{k}^{N}|^{2}}{\big(1+|Nt+\pi k|\big)^{2-\frac{4}{q}}}\,dt\lesssim N^{2s}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{k}^{N}|^{2}\lesssim\|P_{N}^{\Omega}f\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)},

which together with Proposition
2.5
implies the result and completes the proof.
∎

Let
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 1

4.1.
Global analysis for High frequency part

Let us consider the following Dirichlet problem of nonlinear wave equation outside of the ball with high-frequency-localized radial data.

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{t}w-\Delta w+w^{3}=0,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega,\\
w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)=P_{>2^{J}}u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}w(0,x)=w_{1}(x)=P_{>2^{J}}u_{1}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
w(t,x)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(4.1)

Notice that the distorted Fourier transform preserve the zero Dirichlet boundary value structure.

Let
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, and choose the dyadic number
J=J(\epsilon)\gg 1
such that

\displaystyle\left\|(w_{0},w_{1})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{s-1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}=\left\|\big(P_{>2^{J}}u_{0},P_{>2^{J}}u_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{s-1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}\lesssim\epsilon.

(4.2)

By the Strichartz estimates in Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.4
, the standard well-posedness theory in
[
37
,
45
]
together with the regularity theory implies that

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<s\leq 1
,
There exists a large constant
J=J(\epsilon)>0
such that if

2^{J(\frac{1}{2}-s)}\lesssim\epsilon

then (
4.1
) is global well-posedness in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D}(\Omega)\cap\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have the following estimates.

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{3}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{6}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{J(\frac{1}{2}-s)},

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left(\mathbb{R};\,\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon.

(4.4)

Proof.

The result follows from the standard contraction mapping argument. More precisely, by the Strichartz estimates in Theorem
3.2
, we will show the following map
w\mapsto\mathcal{T}(w)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(w):=\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})w_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}w_{1}-\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}w^{3}(s)\;ds

is a contraction on the set
X\subset C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)

\displaystyle X:=\Big\{w\in C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)

\displaystyle\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega):

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\big(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\leq 2\,C\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}\Big\}

under the metric given by

d(w_{1},w_{2}):=\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}.

Here
C
denotes the constant from the Strichartz inequality.

Using the Strichartz estimate and Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(w)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)\cap L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\big\|w^{3}\big\|_{L^{4/3}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\big\|w\big\|^{3}_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;2\,C\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}.

Arguing as above, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(w_{1})-\mathcal{T}(w_{2})\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\|w_{1}^{3}-w_{2}^{3}\big\|_{L^{4/3}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\|w_{1}-w_{2}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\left(\big\|w_{1}\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}+\big\|w_{2}\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\right).

Thus, choosing
J=J(\epsilon)
even larger (if necessary), we can guarantee that
\mathcal{T}
maps the set
X
back to itself and is a contraction on the set
X
. By the contraction mapping theorem, it follows that
\mathcal{T}
has a fixed point
w
in
X
. In addition, by the endpoint Strichartz estimate in Theorem
3.4
, the Sobolev embedding that
L^{3/2}(\Omega)\subset\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D}(\Omega)
and the product rule in Remark
2.9
, we have

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{6}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\big\|w^{3}\big\|_{L^{1}_{t}\left(\mathbb{R};\,\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\big\|w^{3}\big\|_{L^{1}_{t}\left(\mathbb{R};\,L^{3/2}_{x}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\big\|w\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\big\|w\big\|_{L^{2}_{t}\left(\mathbb{R};\,L^{6}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{-1/2}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}\lesssim 2^{J(\frac{1}{2}-s)},

and

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)\cap L^{4}_{t}\dot{H}^{s-1/2,4}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{s-1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\big\|w^{3}\big\|_{L^{4/3}_{t}\left(\mathbb{R};\,\dot{H}^{s-1/2,4/3}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{s-1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\big\|w\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\big\|w\big\|_{L^{4}_{t}\left(\mathbb{R};\,\dot{H}^{s-1/2,4}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\left\|\big(w_{0},w_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times\dot{H}^{s-1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}\lesssim\epsilon,

then we completes the proof.

4.2.
Local-in-time energy analysis for low frequency part

Let
w
be the small solution of (
4.1
) in Proposition
4.1

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
w
be the solution in Proposition
4.1

Proof.

The result also follows from the standard contraction mapping argument. More precisely, using the Strichartz estimates from Theorem

, we will show the following map
v\mapsto\mathcal{T}(v)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(v):=\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})v_{0}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}v_{1}

denotes the constant from the Strichartz inequality.

\displaystyle\;\|\mathcal{T}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left([0,T);\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\left\|\big(v_{0},v_{1}\big)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)}+\big\|v^{3}+3v^{2}w+3vw^{2}\big\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}([0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\;T\big\|v_{1}-v_{2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left([0,T);\,L^{6}_{x}(\Omega)\right)}\left(\big\|v_{1}\big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left([0,T);\,L^{6}_{x}(\Omega)\right)}+\big\|v_{2}\big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left([0,T);\,L^{6}_{x}(\Omega)\right)}\right)+\big\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{6}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\big\|v_{1}-v_{2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left([0,T);\,L^{6}_{x}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\;T\big\|v_{1}-v_{2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}\left(\big\|v_{1}\big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}+\big\|v_{2}\big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}\right)+\big\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{6}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\Omega)}\big\|v_{1}-v_{2}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}.

Thus, by Proposition
4.1
and choosing

we can guarantee that

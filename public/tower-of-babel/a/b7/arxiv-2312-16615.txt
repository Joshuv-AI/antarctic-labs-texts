Constrained quantization for a uniform distribution
1 Pigar Biteng, 2 Mathieu Caguiat, 3 Dipok Deb, 4 Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury, 5 Beatriz Vela Villanueva

Abstract
. Constrained quantization for a Borel probability measure refers to the idea of estimating a given probability by a discrete probability with a finite number of supporting points lying on a specific set. The specific set is known as the constraint of the constrained quantization. A quantization without a constraint is known as an unconstrained quantization, which traditionally in the literature is known as quantization. Constrained quantization has recently been introduced by Pandey and Roychowdhury. In this paper, for a uniform distribution with support lying on a side of an equilateral triangle, and the constraint as the union of the other two sides, we obtain the optimal sets of n n -points and the n n th constrained quantization errors for all positive integers n n . We also calculate the constrained quantization dimension and the constrained quantization coefficient.

1.
Introduction

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
equipped with a Euclidean metric
d
induced by the Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
. For a given closed subset
S
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
the distortion error for
P
with respect to a set
\alpha\subseteq S
, denoted by
V(P;\alpha)
, is defined as

V(P;\alpha)=\int\mathop{\min}\limits_{a\in\alpha}d(x,a)^{2}dP(x).

Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
th constrained quantization
error
for
P
is defined as

(1)

V_{n}:=V_{n}(P)=\inf\Big\{V(P;\alpha):\alpha\subseteq S,1\leq\text{card}(\alpha)\leq n\Big\},

where
\text{card}(A)
represents the cardinality of a set
A
. The set
S
is known as the
constraint
of the constrained quantization error. If
S=R^{2}
, i.e., if there is no constraint, then the definition of constrained quantization error reduces to the definition of unconstrained quantization error, which traditionally in the literature is known as quantization.
For more details about unconstrained quantization error, one can see
[
DFG
,
DR
,
GG
,
GL
,
GL1
,
GL2
,
GL3
,
GN
,
KNZ
,
P
,
P1
,
R1
,
R2
,
R3
,
Z1
,
Z2
]
. On the other hand, constrained quantization has recently been introduced in
[
PR1
,
PR2
]
and it has much more broad applications in the areas such as information theory, machine learning and data compression, signal processing and national security.
We assume that
\int d(x,0)^{r}dP(x)<\infty
to make sure that the infimum in (
1
) exists (see
[
PR1
]
). A set
\alpha\subseteq S
for which the infimum in (
1
) exists and does not contain more than
n
elements is called an
optimal set of
n
-points
for
P
. Elements of an optimal set are called
optimal elements
.
Let
V_{n}(P)
be a strictly decreasing sequence, and write
V_{\infty}(P):=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}V_{n}(P)
.
Then, the number
D(P)
defined by

\displaystyle D(P):=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))}

if it exists,
is called the
constrained quantization dimension
of
P
and is denoted by
D(P)
. The constrained quantization dimension measures the speed at which the specified measure of the constrained quantization error converges as
n
tends to infinity.
For any
\kappa>0
, the number

\lim_{n}n^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P)),

if it exists, is called the
\kappa
-dimensional constrained quantization coefficient
for
P
.

Let
\triangle OAB
be an equilateral triangle with vertices
O(0,0)
,
A(2,0)
,
B(1,\sqrt{3})
. Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
which has support the closed interval
[0,2]
. Moreover,
P
is uniform on its support. Let
S_{1}
be the side
OB
and
S_{2}
be the side
AB
. In this paper, we take the constraint as
S:=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
. Then, in Section
3
for the uniform distribution
P
we obtain the optimal sets of
n
-points and the
n
th constrained quantization errors for all positive integers
n
. In Section
4
, we calculate the constrained quantization dimension and the constrained quantization coefficient. As mentioned in Remark
4.3
, though the constrained quantization dimension of the uniform distribution defined in this paper is one, which is equal to the Euclidean dimension of the space where the support of the probability measure is defined, it is not always true (see
[
PR1
,
PR3
]
).

Remark 1.1
.

In this paper, to keep our calculation simple to investigate the constrained quantization, we have considered the equilateral triangle with height
\sqrt{3}
, and the probability measure as the uniform distribution on the base of the triangle. People can consider any triangle with any height and any probability measure with support on any side of the triangle.

2.
Preliminaries

With respect to a finite set
\alpha\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, by the
Voronoi region
of an element
a\in\alpha
, it is meant the set of all elements in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which are nearest to
a
among all the elements in
\alpha
, and is denoted by
M(a|\alpha)
.
For any two elements
(a,b)
and
(c,d)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we write

\rho((a,b),(c,d)):=(a-c)^{2}+(b-d)^{2},

which gives the squared Euclidean distance between the two elements
(a,b)
and
(c,d)
.
Two elements
p
and
q
in an optimal set of
n
-points are called
adjacent elements
if they have a common boundary in their own Voronoi regions. Let
e
be an element on the common boundary of the Voronoi regions of two adjacent elements
p
and
q
in an optimal set of
n
-points. Since the common boundary of the Voronoi regions of any two elements is the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining the elements, we have

\rho(p,e)-\rho(q,e)=0.

We call such an equation a
canonical equation
.
Notice that any element
x\in\mathbb{R}
can be identified as an element
(x,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus, the nonnegative real-valued function
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by

\rho:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)\text{ such that }\rho(x,(a,b))=(x-a)^{2}+b^{2},

represents the squared Euclidean distance between an element
x\in\mathbb{R}
and an element
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Recall that
P
is a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
which is uniform on its support the closed interval
[0,2]
. Hence, the probability density function
f
for
P
is given by

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }0\leq x\leq 2,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Hence, we have
dP(x)=P(dx)=f(x)dx
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let the constraint
S
be the union of the sides of the equilateral triangle except the base, i.e.,
S=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
, where

\displaystyle S_{1}:

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0\leq x\leq 1\text{ and }y=\sqrt{3}x\}\text{ and }

\displaystyle S_{2}:

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:1\leq x\leq 2\text{ and }y=-\sqrt{3}(x-2)\}.

Note 2.1
.

The perpendicular through a point
(a,a\sqrt{3})
on the line
y=\sqrt{3}x
intersects the support of
P
at the point
(4a,0)
so that
0\leq 4a\leq 2
, i.e.,
0\leq a\leq\frac{1}{2}
. The perpendicular through a point
(b,-\sqrt{3}(b-2))
on the line
y=-\sqrt{3}(x-2)
intersects the support of
P
at the point
(4b-6,0)
so that
0\leq 4b-6\leq 2
, i.e.,
\frac{3}{2}\leq b\leq 2
.
Let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-points for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
S:=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
is the constraint, we must have

\alpha_{n}=(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{1})\cup(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{2}).

For some
\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\alpha_{n}\cap S_{1}=\{(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}),(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3}),\cdots,(a_{\ell},a_{\ell}\sqrt{3})\}
, and
\alpha_{n}\cap S_{2}=\{(b_{m},-(b_{m}-2)\sqrt{3}),(b_{m-1},-(b_{m-1}-2)\sqrt{3}),\cdots,(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3})\}
. Then, without any loss of generality we can assume that

(2)

0\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{\ell}\leq\frac{1}{2}<\frac{3}{2}\leq b_{m}<b_{m-1}<\cdots<b_{2}<b_{1}\leq 2.

Under the assumption (
2
), the elements
(a_{i},a_{i}\sqrt{3})
and
(a_{i+1},a_{i+1}\sqrt{3})
for
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
;
(a_{\ell},a_{\ell}\sqrt{3})
and
(b_{m},-(b_{m}-2)\sqrt{3})
; and
(b_{j+1},-(b_{j+1}-2)\sqrt{3})
and
(b_{j},-(b_{j}-2)\sqrt{3})
for
1\leq j\leq m-1
, are the adjacent elements.

The following proposition that is well-known in unconstrained quantization is also true in constrained quantization.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
P
be a continuous Borel probability measure. Let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-points for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

(i)

P(M(a|\alpha_{n}))>0
, and
(ii)

P(\partial M(a|\alpha_{n}))=0
for
a\in\alpha_{n}
, where
\partial M(a|\alpha_{n})
represents the boundary of the Voronoi region
M(a|\alpha_{n})
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-points for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\alpha_{n}\cap S_{1}
and
\alpha_{n}\cap S_{2}
be the sets as defined in Note
2.1
. Then, there is no common boundary between the Voronoi regions of any two elements in
\alpha_{n}
unless the two elements are adjacent elements.

3.
Optimal sets of
n
-points for all
n\geq 1

In this section, we determine the optimal sets of
n
-points and the
n
th constrained quantization errors for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
under the condition that the optimal elements lie on the set
S
.

Proposition 3.1
.

An optimal set of one-point is given by
\{(\frac{1}{4},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4})\}
or
\{(\frac{7}{4},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4})\}
with constrained quantization error
\frac{13}{12}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=\{(a,b)\}
be an optimal set of one-point. The following two cases can happen:

Case 1.
(a,b)\in S_{1}
.

In this case, as
b=a\sqrt{3}
, the distortion error is given by

V(P;\alpha)=\int\rho(x,(a,a\sqrt{3}))\,dP(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2}\rho(x,(a,a\sqrt{3}))\,dx=4a^{2}-2a+\frac{4}{3},

the minimum value of which is
\frac{13}{12}
, and it occurs when
a=\frac{1}{4}
.

Case 2.
(a,b)\in S_{2}
.

In this case,
b=-(a-2)\sqrt{3}
. Then, the distortion error is given by

V(P;\alpha)=\int\rho(x,(a,-(a-2)\sqrt{3}))\,dP(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2}\rho(x,(a,(a,-(a-2)\sqrt{3})))\,dx=4a^{2}-14a+\frac{40}{3},

the minimum value of which is
\frac{13}{12}
, and it occurs when
a=\frac{7}{4}
.

Hence, an optimal set of one-point is given by either
\{(\frac{1}{4},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4})\}
or
\{(\frac{7}{4},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4})\}
with constrained quantization error
\frac{13}{12}
, which is the proposition (see Figure
1
).
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

The optimal set of two-points is
\{(\frac{1}{8},\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{3}),(\frac{15}{8},\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{3})\}
with constrained quantization error
V_{2}=\frac{13}{48}

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be an optimal set of two-points. The following cases can arise:

Case 1: Both the elements are on
S_{1}
.

In this case, we can assume that the two elements are
(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3})
and
(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3})
. Since the probability measures of the Voronoi regions are positive, we have
0\leq a_{1}<a_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. The perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining the two elements intersects the support of
P
at the point

(2(a_{1}+a_{2}),0)
. Hence, the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle V(P;\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int\min_{a\in\alpha}\rho(x,a)\,dP(x)=\int_{0}^{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}\min_{a\in\alpha}\rho(x,a)\,dP(x)+\int_{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}^{2}\min_{a\in\alpha}\rho(x,a)\,dP(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}\rho(x,(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}))f(x)\,dx+\int_{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}^{2}\rho((x,0),(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3}))f(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=2a_{1}^{3}+2a_{2}a_{1}^{2}-2a_{2}^{2}a_{1}-2\left(a_{2}-1\right){}^{2}a_{2}+\frac{4}{3},

the minimum value of which is
\frac{49}{48}
, and it occurs when
a_{1}=\frac{1}{8}
and
a_{2}=\frac{3}{8}
.

Case 2: Both the elements are on
S_{2}
.

This case is the reflection of Case 1 with respect to the line
x=1
.
Hence, if the two elements are
(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3})
and
(b_{2},-(b_{2}-2)\sqrt{3})
, then
the distortion error is
V(P;\alpha)=\frac{49}{48}
, and it occurs when
b_{1}=\frac{15}{8}
and
b_{2}=\frac{13}{8}
.

Case 3: One element is on
S_{1}
and one element is on
S_{2}
.

In this case, let the two elements be
(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3})
and
(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3})
. Let the boundary of the Voronoi regions of the two elements
(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3})
and
(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3})
intersect the support of
P
at the point
(d,0)
. Then, the canonical equation is

\rho((a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}),(d,0))-\rho((b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3}),(d,0))=0

implying
d=\frac{2\left(a_{1}^{2}-b_{1}^{2}+3b_{1}-3\right)}{a_{1}-b_{1}}
.
Hence, the distortion error
V(P;\alpha)
is given by

\displaystyle V(P;\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int\min_{a\in\alpha}\rho(x,a)\,dP(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{d}\rho(x,(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}))\,dP(x)+\int_{d}^{2}\rho(x,(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3}))\,dP(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\left(-6a_{1}^{2}\left(b_{1}^{2}-3b_{1}+3\right)+a_{1}\left(6b_{1}^{2}-21b_{1}+20\right)+3a_{1}^{4}+3b_{1}^{4}-24b_{1}^{3}+66b_{1}^{2}-74b_{1}+27\right)}{3\left(a_{1}-b_{1}\right)},

the minimum value of which is
\frac{13}{48}
, and it occurs when
a_{1}=\frac{1}{8}
and
b_{1}=\frac{15}{8}
.

Among all the above possible cases, we see that the distortion error is smallest in Case 3. Hence, the optimal set of two-points is
\{(\frac{1}{8},\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{3}),(\frac{15}{8},\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{3})\}
with constrained quantization error
V_{2}=\frac{13}{48}
(see Figure
1
). Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

The set
\alpha:=\{(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}),(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3}),(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3})\}
, where

a_{1}=\frac{1}{12}\left(26-7\sqrt{13}\right),a_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(26-7\sqrt{13}\right),\text{ and }b_{1}=\frac{73}{12}-\frac{7\sqrt{13}}{6},

forms an optimal set of three-points with quantization error
V_{3}=\frac{1}{108}(-637)\left(28\sqrt{13}-101\right)
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be an optimal set of three-points. The following cases can happen:

Case 1: All the three elements lie on one of the sides
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
.

Without any loss of generality, we can assume that all the elements lie on the side
S_{1}
. Then,

\alpha=\{(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}),(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3}),(a_{3},a_{3}\sqrt{3})\}

such that by Note
2.1
, we have
0\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Notice that the boundary line of the Voronoi regions of the elements
(a_{1},\sqrt{3}a_{1})
and
(a_{2},\sqrt{3}a_{2})
intersects the support of
P
at the point
(2(a_{1}+a_{2}),0)
; the boundary line of the Voronoi regions of the elements
(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3})
and
(a_{3},a_{3}\sqrt{3})
intersects the support of
P
at the point
(2(a_{2}+a_{3}),0)
. Hence, if
V(P;\alpha)
is the distortion error, then

\displaystyle V(P;\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int\min_{a\in\alpha}\rho(x,a)\,dP=\int_{0}^{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}\rho(x,(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}))\,dP+\int_{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}^{2(a_{2}+a_{3})}\rho(x,(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3}))\,dP

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{2(a_{2}+a_{3})}^{2}\rho(x,(a_{3},a_{3}\sqrt{3}))\,dP

\displaystyle=2a_{1}^{3}+2a_{2}a_{1}^{2}-2a_{2}^{2}a_{1}-2a_{3}^{3}-2\left(a_{2}-2\right)a_{3}^{2}+2\left(a_{2}^{2}-1\right)a_{3}+\frac{4}{3},

the minimum value of which is
\frac{109}{108}
and it occurs when
a_{1}=\frac{1}{12},\,a_{2}=\frac{1}{4},\,a_{3}=\frac{5}{12}
.

Case 2: Two elements are on one side and one element is on another side.

Without any loss of generality, we can assume that two elements lie on
S_{1}
and one element lies on
S_{2}
. Then,

\alpha=\{(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}),(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3}),(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3})\},

where
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<1<b_{1}<2
.
Let the boundaries of the Voronoi regions of
(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3})
and
(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3})

intersect the support of
P
at the point
(2(a_{1}+a_{2}),0)
. Let the boundary of the Voronoi regions of
(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3})
and
(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3})
intersect the support of
P
at the points
(d,0)
. Then, solving the canonical equation

\rho((a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3}),(d,0))-\rho((b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3}),(d,0))=0,

we have

d=\frac{2\left(a_{2}^{2}-b_{1}^{2}+3b_{1}-3\right)}{a_{2}-b_{1}}.

Then, the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle V(P;\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int\min_{a\in\alpha}\rho(x,a)\,dP

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}\rho(x,(a_{1},\sqrt{3}a_{1}))\,dP+\int_{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}^{d}\rho(x,(a_{2},\sqrt{3}a_{2}))\,dP

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{d}^{2}\rho(x,(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3}))\,dP.

The distortion error is minimum if

\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}(V(P;\alpha))=0\text{ for }i=1,2,\text{ and }\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{1}}(V(P;\alpha))=0.

Upon simplification, we see that
V(P;\alpha)
is minimum if
a_{1}=\frac{1}{12}\left(26-7\sqrt{13}\right)
,
a_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(26-7\sqrt{13}\right)
, and
b_{1}=\frac{73}{12}-\frac{7\sqrt{13}}{6}
, and then
V(P;\alpha)=\frac{1}{108}(-637)\left(28\sqrt{13}-101\right)
.

Considering Case 1 and Case 2, we see that the distortion error is minimum in Case 2, i.e., the set
\alpha:=\{(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}),(a_{2},a_{2}\sqrt{3}),(b_{1},-(b_{1}-2)\sqrt{3})\}
in Case 2 forms an optimal set of three-points with quantization error
V_{3}=\frac{1}{108}(-637)\left(28\sqrt{13}-101\right)
, which is the proposition (see Figure
1
).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-points for
P
. Then,
\alpha_{n}
contains points from both
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, the lemma is true for
n=2
. Let us now prove the lemma for
n\geq 3
. Let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-points for
n\geq 3
with the corresponding
n
th constrained quantization error
V_{n}
. For the sake of contradiction, without any loss of generality, we can assume that all the elements in
\alpha_{n}
lie on the side
S_{1}
. Then, we can write

\alpha_{n}:=\{(a_{i},a_{i}\sqrt{3}):1\leq i\leq n\},

where
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}<\cdots<a_{n}<1
. In fact, by Note
2.1
, we have
0\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}<\cdots<a_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
The quantization error
V_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{0}^{2(a_{1}+a_{2})}\rho(x,(a_{1},a_{1}\sqrt{3}))\,dx+\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}\int_{2(a_{i-1}+a_{i})}^{2(a_{i}+a_{i+1})}\rho(x,(a_{i},a_{i}\sqrt{3}))\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{2(a_{n-1}+a_{n})}^{2}\rho(x,(a_{n},a_{n}\sqrt{3}))\,dx\Big).

Since
V_{n}
gives the optimal error and is differentiable with respect to
a_{i}
, we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}V_{n}=0
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
Solving the equations
\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}V_{n}=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we have

2a_{1}=a_{2}-a_{1}=a_{3}-a_{2}=\cdots=a_{n}-a_{n-1}=u\text{ implying }a_{i}=\frac{(2i-1)u}{2}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n,

where
u
is a constant depending on
n

\displaystyle+6(u-2\ell u)^{2}((1-2m)^{2}v^{2}+(2-4m)v+4)+(1-2m)^{2}(4m^{2}-4m-3)v^{4}

\displaystyle-64m(2m^{2}-3m+1)v^{3}+24(1-2m)^{2}v^{2}-16(2m-1)v+16\Big).

Notice that
V_{n}
is a function of
\ell,\,m,\,u
and
v
.
V_{n}
being optimal we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial u}V_{n}=0\text{ and }\frac{\partial}{\partial v}V_{n}=0.

Solving the above two equations, we deduce that

u=\frac{1}{2\left(\frac{\left(12\ell^{2}+1\right)m}{\sqrt{\left(12\ell^{2}+1\right)\left(12m^{2}+1\right)}}+\ell\right)}\text{ and }v=\frac{1}{2\left(\frac{\ell\left(12m^{2}+1\right)}{\sqrt{\left(12\ell^{2}+1\right)\left(12m^{2}+1\right)}}+m\right)}.

After substituting the values of
u
and
v
in
V_{n}
, and then upon simplification, we have

V_{n}=\frac{\left(12\ell^{2}+1\right)\left(12m^{2}+1\right)}{12\left(24\ell^{2}m^{2}+2\ell m\sqrt{\left(12\ell^{2}+1\right)\left(12m^{2}+1\right)}+\ell^{2}+m^{2}\right)}.

Thus, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

4.
Constrained quantization dimension and constrained quantization coefficient

In this section, we show that the constrained quantization dimension
D(P)
of the uniform distribution
P
exists and equals one. We further show that the
D(P)
-dimensional constrained quantization coefficient exists as a finite positive number.

Theorem 4.1
.

The constrained quantization dimension
D(P)
of the probability measure
P
exists, and
D(P)=1
.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 6
, let
\ell(n)
be the unique natural number such that
2{\ell(n)}\leq n<2({\ell(n)+1})
. Then,

V_{2(\ell(n)+1)}\leq V_{n}\leq V_{2{\ell(n)}}
. By Corollary
3.10
, we see that
V_{2({\ell(n)+1})}\to\frac{1}{4}
and
V_{2{\ell(n)}}\to\frac{1}{4}
as
n\to\infty
, and so
V_{n}\to\frac{1}{4}
as
n\to\infty
,
i.e.,
V_{\infty}=\frac{1}{4}
.
We can take
n
large enough so that
V_{2{\ell(n)}}-V_{\infty}<1
. Then,

0<-\log(V_{2{\ell(n)}}-V_{\infty})\leq-\log(V_{n}-V_{\infty})\leq-\log(V_{2({\ell(n)+1})}-V_{\infty})

yielding

\frac{2\log 2\ell(n)}{-\log(V_{2({\ell(n)+1})}-V_{\infty})}\leq\frac{2\log n}{-\log(V_{n}-V_{\infty})}\leq\frac{2\log 2(\ell(n)+1)}{-\log(V_{2{\ell(n)}}-V_{\infty})}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log 2\ell(n)}{-\log(V_{2({\ell(n)+1})}-V_{\infty})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log 2\ell(n)}{-\log\frac{1}{48(\ell(n)+1)^{2}}}=1,\text{ and }

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log 2(\ell(n)+1)}{-\log(V_{2{\ell(n)}}-V_{\infty})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log 2(\ell(n)+1)}{-\log\frac{1}{48(\ell(n))^{2}}}=1.

Hence,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log(V_{n}-V_{\infty})}=1
, i.e., the constrained quantization dimension
D(P)
of the probability measure
P
exists and
D(P)=1
.
Thus, the proof of
the theorem is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

The
D(P)
-dimensional constrained quantization coefficient for
P
exists as a finite positive number and equals
\frac{1}{12}
.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 6
, let
\ell(n)
be the unique natural number such that
2{\ell(n)}\leq n<2({\ell(n)+1})
. Then,

V_{2(\ell(n)+1)}\leq V_{n}\leq V_{2{\ell(n)}}
, and
V_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{n}=\frac{1}{4}
. Since

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(V_{n}-V_{\infty})\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}(2\ell(n))^{2}(V_{2(\ell(n)+1)}-V_{\infty})=\lim_{n\to\infty}(2\ell(n))^{2}\frac{1}{48(\ell(n)+1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{12},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(V_{n}-V_{\infty})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}(2(\ell(n)+1))^{2}(V_{2\ell(n)}-V_{\infty})=\lim_{n\to\infty}(2(\ell(n)+1))^{2}\frac{1}{48(\ell(n))^{2}}=\frac{1}{12},

by the squeeze theorem, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(V_{n}-V_{\infty})=\frac{1}{12}
, which is the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

For the absolutely continuous probability measure, considered in this paper, we have obtained that the constrained quantization dimension is one which equals the dimension of the underlying space where the support of the probability measure is defined. This fact is not true, in general, in constrained quantization, for example, one can see
[
PR1
,
PR3
]
. In this regard, we would like to mention that the unconstrained quantization dimension of an absolutely continuous probability measures always equals the dimension of the underlying space where the support of the probability measure is defined (see
[
BW
]
).

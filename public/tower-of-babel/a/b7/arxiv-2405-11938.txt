Convergence of the area functional on spaces with lower Ricci bounds and applications
Alessandro Cucinotta

Abstract
We show that the heat flow provides good approximation properties for the area functional on proper 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( K , ∞ ) \mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty) spaces, implying that in this setting the area formula for functions of bounded variation holds and that the area functional coincides with its relaxation. We then obtain partial regularity and uniqueness results for functions whose hypographs are perimeter minimizing. Finally, we consider sequences of 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( K , N ) \mathsf{RCD}(K,N) spaces and we show that, thanks to the previously obtained properties, Sobolev minimizers of the area functional in a limit space can be approximated with minimizers along the converging sequence of spaces. Using this last result, we obtain applications on Ricci-limit spaces.



Introduction

In recent years there has been a growing interest in the study of metric (measure) spaces with a notion of lower bound on the Ricci curvature.
Initially, this was motivated by the interest in Ricci limits, i.e. spaces arising as Gromov-Hausdorff limits of manifolds with a uniform lower Ricci curvature bound.
Later on, intrinsic formulations of such curvature bound were found, leading to the theory of
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces.

An
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space is a metric measure space where
K\in\mathbb{R}
plays the role of a lower bound on the Ricci curvature and
N\in[1,+\infty]
plays the role of an upper bound on the dimension.
This class includes measured Gromov-Hausdorff limits of smooth manifolds of fixed dimension with uniform Ricci curvature lower bounds and finite dimensional Alexandrov spaces with sectional curvature bounded from below. Moreover, the
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
class is closed with respect to measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. For this reason,
in the
\mathsf{RCD}
setting, the study of objects that retain their properties along converging sequences of spaces is particularly relevant.
This is one of the motivations that led to an extensive study of sets of finite perimeter and perimeter minimizing sets in the non-smooth context of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.

In the paper, we study some approximation properties of the area functional on
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces and Ricci limits, extending a series of results that were previously obtained for sets of finite perimeter in this context. We then derive some new applications.
The first central result concerns the possibility of approximating functions via the heat flow on proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces in a way that involves the area. This is the content of Theorem
1
.
Given a metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, an open set
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
and
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
, we denote by
|Du|
its total variation measure, by
|\nabla u|
the density of the absolutely continuous part of
|Du|
w.r.t.
\mathfrak{m}
and by
|D^{s}u|
the singular part of
|Du|
. For every measurable set
E\subset\Omega
, we then define the area of
u
in
E
as

\mathsf{A}(u,E):=\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E).

We also define

\mathsf{Hyp}(u):=\{(x,t)\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}:t\leq u(x)\}
and we denote by
\mathsf{L}^{0}(\Omega)
the set of measurable functions on
\Omega
. Given two sets
A,B\subset\mathsf{X}
, we denote by
\mathsf{P}(A,B)
the perimeter of
A
in
B
. Finally, we denote the heat flow on an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space by
P_{t}(\cdot)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space and let
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\mathsf{X})\cap\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
. Then, for every bounded open set
E\subset\mathsf{X}
such that
|D^{s}u|(\partial E)=0
,
it holds

\lim_{t\to 0}\mathsf{A}(P_{t}(u),E)=\mathsf{A}(u,E).

As a first application of Theorem
1
we obtain that the area formula holds on proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces, i.e. the area of a function of bounded variation coincides with the perimeter of its hypograph.
This is the content of Theorem
2
.
Before stating the theorem, we recall that related results were proved for
PI
spaces in
[
11
]
, where it was conjectured that the area formula for functions of bounded variation should hold in that setting.
In particular,
[
11
, Theorem
5.1
]
implies that the area formula holds for Sobolev functions on
PI
spaces where weak and relaxed gradients coincide (and finite dimensional
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces fit in this framework).
Such formula was then proved for
\mathsf{BV}
functions on finite dimensional
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces in
[
12
]
, while in the next theorem we extend the result to the infinite dimensional framework of proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and bounded and let
u\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then
\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})<+\infty
if and only if
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. In this case, for every Borel set
E\subset\Omega
, it holds

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})=\mathsf{A}(u,E).

Moreover, if
u\in\mathsf{L}^{0}(B_{2r}(x))
and
\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),B_{2r}(x)\times\mathbb{R})<+\infty
, then
u\in\mathsf{BV}(B_{r}(x))
and the previous equation holds for every Borel set
E\subset B_{r}(x)
.

As anticipated, the previous result complements the one proved for doubling spaces supporting a Poincaré inequality in
[
11
]
, where it was shown that if
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is such a space and
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
, then

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})\geq\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega).

Note that, although
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces are not necessarily doubling, the proof of the inequality given in
[
11
]
works with very slight changes also in our setting. On the other hand, the proof that we give of the reverse inequality is a consequence of Theorem
1
and truly relies on the curvature assumption.

A second consequence of Theorem
1
is that on proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces the relaxed area functional coincides with the area functional itself. This is the content of Theorem
3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open bounded set and let
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. For every open set
E\subset\Omega
we denote by
\mathsf{Lip}(E)
the set of Lipschitz functions on
E
and given
f\in\mathsf{Lip}(E)
we denote by
\mathsf{lip}(f)
its local Lipschitz constant. The relaxed area functional is defined by

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E):=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{1}(E)\cap\mathsf{Lip}(E),\|f_{n}-u\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(E)}\to 0\Big\}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and bounded and let
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. Then, for every open set
E\subset\Omega
, it holds

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E)=\mathsf{A}(u,E).

The previous theorem extends the analogous result proved in
[
11
]
for Sobolev functions. Note that in general metric measure spaces (even if they are doubling and support a Poincaré inequality) the relaxed area functional does not coincide with the area functional itself, as shown in
[
31
]
.
Theorem
3
, in particular, allows to apply the mild regularity results for minimizers of the relaxed area functional in metric measure spaces established in
[
30
]
to the area functional itself in the
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
setting.
Next, we obtain a uniqueness result for perimeter minimizers (which follows through the identification between area and perimeter). In what follows, when we refer to an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, we implicitly mean that
N<+\infty
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space, let
\Omega\subsetneq\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a function whose hypograph is perimeter minimizing.
If
v\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
has hypograph which is perimeter minimizing and
\{u\neq v\}\subset\subset\Omega
, then
u=v
.

Corollary
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subsetneq\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a function whose hypograph is perimeter minimizing.
If
v\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
has hypograph which is perimeter minimizing and
\{u\neq v\}\subset\subset\Omega
, then
u=v
.

We then consider sequences of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces and, relying on Theorems
1
and
4
, we show that Sobolev minimizers of the area functional in a limit space can be approximated with minimizers along the converging sequence of spaces.
This is the content of Theorem
5
below.
Theorem
5
is useful when working with Ricci limit spaces, because it allows to pass information from minimizers of the area on smooth manifolds (for which there is a rich theory) to minimizers on the limit space.
We work with functions that minimize the area (see Definition
2.6
), but we could have worked with functions whose hypographs are perimeter minimizing since the two notions are equivalent (see Proposition
2.9
).

When considering pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence (pmGH) of spaces, we adopt the extrinsic viewpoint, meaning that we implicitly assume all spaces to be embedded in a common metric space
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})
where the convergence is realized. In particular, balls in this setting are to be intended w.r.t. the distance in
Z
(where all spaces are embedded), while we will specify from time to time which measure is to be considered.
We refer to the beginning of Section
3
for more details on the notation used.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},x_{i})
be a sequence of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces converging in pmGH sense to
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x)
. Let
r>0
be such that
B_{r}(x)\subsetneq\mathsf{X}
and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(B_{r}(x),\mathfrak{m})
be an area minimizer.
For every
s\in(0,r)
, modulo passing to a subsequence, there exist area minimizers
u_{i}\in\mathsf{BV}(B_{s}(x),\mathfrak{m}_{i})
such that
u_{i}1_{B_{s}(x)}
converge in
\mathsf{L}_{1}
to
u1_{B_{s}(x)}
and
\mathsf{A}(u_{i},B_{s}(x),\mathfrak{m}_{i})\to\mathsf{A}(u,B_{s}(x),\mathfrak{m})
.

Finally, in the Appendix, we collect some applications of the previously obtained theorems in the setting of Ricci limit spaces.

1
Preliminaries

We will work on metric measure spaces
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, where
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d})
is a separable complete metric space and
\mathfrak{m}
is a non-negative Borel measure on
\mathsf{X}
which is finite on bounded sets.
Given an open set
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
we denote by
\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
,
\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
and
\mathsf{Lip}_{b}(\Omega)
respectively the set of Lipschitz functions, Lipschitz functions with compact support and bounded Lipschitz functions on
\Omega
. Similarly, we denote by
C(\Omega)
the set of continuous functions on
\Omega
and by
\mathsf{L}^{0}(\Omega)
the one of measurable functions.
If
u\in\mathsf{L}^{0}(\Omega)
we also define

\mathsf{Hyp}(u):=\{(x,t)\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}:t\leq u(x)\}
.
If
f\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
and
x\in\Omega
we define

\mathsf{lip}(f)(x):=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{L}(f):=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}.

We say that
\Omega^{\prime}
is well contained in
\Omega
, and we denote it by
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
, if
\Omega^{\prime}
is bounded and
\mathsf{d}(\Omega^{\prime},{{}^{c}}\Omega)>0
. When we consider the spaces
\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
and
\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\Omega)
we implicitly mean that we are localizing w.r.t. well contained subsets of
\Omega
; note that these are not necessarily precompact in
\Omega
(although they will be precompact if
\mathsf{X}
is a finite dimensional
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space or a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space). We now introduce Sobolev spaces in the setting of metric measure spaces, the main references being
[
14
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
26
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set. A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
is said to be in the Sobolev space
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
if there exists a sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
converging to
f
in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{lip}(f_{i})^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}<+\infty.

For every
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
one can define a function
|\nabla f|\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that for every open set
A\subset\Omega
we have

\int_{A}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{2}(A)\cap\mathsf{Lip}(A),\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(A)}\to 0\Big\}.

The quantity in the previous expression will be called Cheeger energy and will be denoted by
\mathsf{Ch}(f)
while
|\nabla f|
will be called relaxed gradient.
We define
\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathsf{Ch}(f)
. One can check that with this norm the space
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
is Banach.

Remark 1.2
.

Given a metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, an open set
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
and a function
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, there exists a sequence
f_{n}\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
such that
\mathsf{lip}_{a}(f_{n})\to|\nabla f|
in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, where

\mathsf{lip}_{a}(f)(x):=\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{y,z\in B_{r}(x)}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|}{\mathsf{d}(y,z)}.

This fact follows since in the definition of Sobolev spaces one could replace local Lipschitz constants
\mathsf{lip}
with asymptotic Lipschitz constants
\mathsf{lip}_{a}
and still obtain the same space (see
[
3
]
and
[
7
, Remark
5.12
]
).

We now introduce functions of bounded variation following
[
36
]
(see also
[
4
]
).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set. A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
is said to be of bounded variation if there exists a sequence of
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
converging to
f
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{lip}(f_{i})\,d\mathfrak{m}<+\infty.

The space of such functions is denoted by
\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
.

For every
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and any open set
A\subset\Omega
we define

|Df|(A)=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{1}(A)\cap\mathsf{Lip}(A),\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(A)}\to 0\Big\}.

One can check that the quantity in the previous expression is the restriction to the open subsets of
\Omega
of a finite measure. We denote by
|\nabla f|
the density of
|Df|
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
and by
|D^{s}f|
the singular part of
|Du|
. We define
\|f\|_{\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+|Df|(\Omega)
. One can check that with this norm the space
\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
is Banach. The function
f
belongs to
\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
if
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and
|Df|<<\mathfrak{m}
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open. We define the perimeter of
E\subset\Omega
in
\Omega
by

\mathsf{P}(E,\Omega):=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega),\|f_{n}-1_{E}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)}\to 0\Big\}.

We recall that
\mathsf{P}(E,\cdot)
is the restriction to open sets of a Borel measure and that if
E\subset\Omega
has finite measure, then it has finite perimeter if and only if
1_{E}\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and in this case
|D1_{E}|=\mathsf{P}(E,\cdot)
.

Definition 1.5
.

We say that a metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian if the space
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\mathsf{X})
is a Hilbert space.

We will consider pointed Gromov-Hausdorff (pGH) and pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff (pmGH) convergence of metric measure spaces, and we refer to
[
28
]
for the relevant background.
We recall that in the case of a sequence of uniformly locally doubling metric measure spaces
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathsf{x}_{i})

(as in the case of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces), pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence to
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
can be equivalently characterized asking for the existence of a proper
metric space
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{z})
such that all the metric spaces
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i})
are isometrically embedded
into
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{z})
,
\mathsf{x}_{i}\to\mathsf{x}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{i}\to\mathfrak{m}
weakly in duality with continuous boundedly supported functions in

. In this case we say that the convergence is realized in the space

(see
[
28
]

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})

We now recall some properties of the heat flow in the
\mathsf{RCD}
setting, referring to
[
6
,
8
]
for the proofs of these results.
Given an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, the heat flow
P_{t}:\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathsf{X})
is the
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathsf{X})
-gradient flow of the Cheeger energy
\mathsf{Ch}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
denote the space of probability measures with finite second moment.
A key result in the
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
theory is that the dual heat semigroup
\bar{P}_{t}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X})
defined by

\int_{\mathsf{X}}\,d\bar{P}_{t}\mu:=\int_{\mathsf{X}}P_{t}f\,d\mu\quad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathsf{X}),\quad\forall f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{b}(\mathsf{X}),

is
K
-contractive with respect to the Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
and for
t>0
maps probability measures into absolutely continuous probability measures.
This allows to define a stochastically complete heat kernel
p_{t}:\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}\to[0,+\infty)
, so that
the definition of
P_{t}(f)
can be then extended to
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}
functions by setting

P_{t}(f)(x):=\int_{\mathsf{X}}f(y)p_{t}(x,y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y).

The heat flow has good approximation properties, in particular if
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\mathsf{X})
, then
P_{t}(f)\to f
in
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\mathsf{X})
; while if
f\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(X)
, then
P_{t}f\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{X})
for every
t>0
.

In
[
27
]
it was proved that, for a
proper

\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, if
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
then

|DP_{t}(f)|\leq e^{-Kt}\bar{P}_{t}(|Df|).

(1)

Combining the previous contraction estimate with a lower semicontinuity argument we obtain that, if
\mathsf{X}
is proper and

\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
is open with
|Df|(\partial\Omega)=0
, then

\lim_{t\to 0}|DP_{t}(f)|(\Omega)=|Df|(\Omega).

Lemma 1.8

Let

We now recall some properties of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.
The finite dimensional
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
condition is obtained coupling the finite dimensional Curvature Dimension condition
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
with the infinitesimal Hilbertianity assumption and was formalised in
[
26
]
. For a thorough introduction to the topic we refer to the survey
[
1
]
and the references therein.
Let us also mention that in the literature one can find also the (a-priori weaker)
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}(K,N)
. It was proved in
[
25
,
10
]
, that
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}(K,N)
is equivalent to the dimensional Bochner inequality. Moreover,
[
13
]
(see also
[
34
]
) proved that
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}(K,N)
and
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
coincide. We now recall some results on
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces that we will need later in the paper.

The
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
condition implies that the measure is locally doubling (see
[
39
]
) and, as mentioned before, the validity of a Poincaré inequality. In particular, if
f
is a locally Lipschitz function on an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, its relaxed gradient coincides with its local Lipschitz constant
\mathsf{lip}(f)
(see
[
32
, Theorem
12.5.1
]
after
[
14
]
).

The next theorem is taken from
[
19
, Theorem
1.2
]
. Given a metric space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d})
, we denote by
\mathsf{H}^{n}
the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure relative to
\mathsf{d}
.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathsf{H}^{n}_{i},x_{i})
be a sequence of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,n)
spaces such that
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},x_{i})
converges in pGH sense to a metric space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},x)
. Then precisely one of the following holds:

1.

\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\mathsf{H}^{n}_{i}(B_{1}(x_{i}))>0.

In this case the
\limsup
is a limit and the pGH convergence can be improved to pmGH convergence to
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathsf{H}^{n},x)
.

2.

\lim_{i\to+\infty}\mathsf{H}^{n}_{i}(B_{1}(x_{i}))=0.

In this case the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathsf{X}
is bounded above by
n-1
.

We now recall the definition of
\mathsf{L}^{1}
-convergence of functions along a sequence of spaces converging in pmGH sense.
As anticipated, we use the so-called extrinsic viewpoint and, when considering spaces
\mathsf{X}_{i}\to\mathsf{X}
in pmGH sense, we work in the space
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})
which realizes the convergence. In particular, whenever we consider a ball
B_{r}(x)
in this context, unless otherwise specified, we mean the ball of radius
r
centered in
x
in the space
Z
.
Similarly, every function
u_{i}:\mathsf{X}_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
will be considered, unless otherwise specified, as a function on
Z
(extending it to zero outside of the embedding of
\mathsf{X}_{i}
in
Z
). To avoid confusion we will specify from time to time which measure is to be considered when working with objects defined on
Z
.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathsf{x}_{i})
be a sequence of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces converging in pmGH sense to
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{y})
. We say that the functions
f_{i}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i})
converge to
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\mathfrak{m})
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}
-sense if

\sigma\circ f_{i}\mathfrak{m}_{i}\rightharpoonup\sigma\circ f\mathfrak{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathsf{X}_{i}}|f_{i}|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{i}\to\int_{\mathsf{X}}|f|\,d\mathfrak{m},

where
\sigma(z):=\text{sign}\sqrt{|z|}
and weak convergence is intended w.r.t. boundedly supported functions in
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{z})
, which is the space realizing the convergence. We say that
f_{i}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i})
converge in
\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}
-sense to
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X},\mathfrak{m})
if
f_{i}1_{B_{r}(x_{i})}\to f1_{B_{r}(x)}
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}
-sense for every
r>0
.

In the previous definition,
\sigma
is needed to recover the usual
\mathsf{L}^{1}
convergence when we restrict to a fixed measured space
(X,\mathfrak{m})
. Indeed, since
\mathsf{L}^{1}(X)
in not uniformly convex in general, weak convergence together with convergence of the norms does not imply strong convergence. On the other hand, if
f_{i}\to f
in
L^{1}
sense on a fixed space
(X,\mathfrak{m})
according to Definition
1.11
, then
\sigma\circ f_{i}\to\sigma\circ f
in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(X)
, and one can check that this implies
\int_{X}|f_{i}-f|\,d\mathfrak{m}\to 0
.

[
2
, Proposition
3.3
]
.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathsf{x}_{i})

Then there exists a (non relabeled) subsequence and

with
\mathsf{supp}{f}\subset\bar{B}_{r}(x)
such that

\mathsf{L}^{1}
.

Definition 1.14
.

. If we say that
E

\Omega=\mathsf{X}
.

.

2
Heat flow approximation of the area and applications in
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces

In this section, we prove Theorems
1
to
4
. We denote by
\mathsf{d}_{\times}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
respectively the product distance and the product measure on
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.
The next two propositions contain the inequality in the area formula that was proved in
[
11
]
for
PI
spaces. Similar proofs work in our setting and we repeat them (with the few due modifications) for the sake of completeness as they will be needed to prove Theorems
1
and
2
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. For every measurable
E\subset\Omega
we define the area of
u
on
E
as

\mathsf{A}(u,E):=\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set. If
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
, then for every Borel set
E\subset\Omega
, we have

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})\geq\mathsf{A}(u,E).

Proof.

We will consider the measure
\mu:=P(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),\cdot\times\mathbb{R})
on the Borel subsets of
\Omega
and we will show separately that

\mu\geq\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\mathfrak{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu\geq|D^{s}u|.

(2)

Since
\mathfrak{m}\perp|D^{s}u|
this will then imply that
\mu\geq\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|
.

Thanks to Lemma
1.9
, to obtain (
2
), it is sufficient to show that for every open set
E\subset\Omega
we have

\mu(E)\geq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(E)\geq|D^{s}u|(E).

We now repeat an argument from
[
11
]
to obtain that, for every open set
E\subset\mathsf{X}
and
a,b\in C(E)
such that
a^{2}+b^{2}\leq 1
, it holds

\mu(E)\geq\int_{E}a\,d\mathfrak{m}+\int_{E}b\,d|Du|.

(3)

We report the argument for the sake of completeness. Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
g_{n}\in\mathsf{Lip}(E\times(-N,N))
be convergent to
1_{\mathsf{Hyp}(u)}
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(E\times(-N,N))
. Modulo passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
g_{n}^{t}\to 1_{\mathsf{Hyp}(u)}^{t}
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(E)
for
\lambda^{1}
-a.e.
t\in(-N,N)
and that
g_{n}^{x}\to 1_{\mathsf{Hyp}(u)}^{x}
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}((-N,N))
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in E
. For every
a,b\in C(E)
with
a^{2}+b^{2}\leq 1
, applying Proposition
1.7
, we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{E\times(-N,N)}|\nabla g_{n}|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\times}\geq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{E\times(-N,N)}a(x)|\nabla g_{n}^{x}|(t)+b(x)|\nabla g_{n}^{t}|(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(x,t)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{E}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{(-N,N)}a(x)|\nabla g_{n}^{x}|(t)\,dtd\mathfrak{m}(x)+\int_{(-N,N)}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathsf{X}}b(x)|\nabla g_{n}^{t}|(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}(x)dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{E}a1_{\{u\in(-N,N)\}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+\int_{(-N,N)}\int_{E}b\,d|D1_{\{u>t\}}|dt.

Since
g_{n}
is arbitrary, we get

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times(-N,N))\geq\int_{E}a1_{\{u\in(-N,N)\}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+\int_{(-N,N)}\int_{E}b\,d|D1_{\{u>t\}}|dt,

so that letting
N\uparrow+\infty
and using the coarea formula we deduce (
3
).

Equation (
3
) then implies that

\mu(E)\geq\int_{E}a+b|\nabla u|\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Taking the supremum over all couples
a,b
of the aforementioned type and using Lemma
1.8
, we get

\mu(E)\geq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Similarly, taking
a\equiv 0
and
b\equiv 1
in (
3
), we obtain

\mu(E)\geq|Du|(E)\geq|D^{s}u|(E),

concluding the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set. If
u\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
, then, for every Borel set
E\subset\Omega
, it holds

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})=\mathsf{A}(u,E).

Proof.

Thanks to the previous proposition, we only need to show that

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})\leq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Thanks to Lemma
1.9
, we only need to verify the previous inequality under the assumption that
E
is open. Let
\chi_{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be monotonically convergent to
1_{(0,+\infty)}
, with values in
[0,1]
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\,dt\leq 1
. Then the functions

g_{\epsilon}(x,t):=\chi_{\epsilon}(u(x)-t)

are Lipschitz in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
for every
\epsilon>0
and converge in
\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
to the characteristic function of
\mathsf{Hyp}(u)
. Moreover Proposition
1.7
gives

|\nabla g_{\epsilon}|(x,t)=\chi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u(x)-t)\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}(x)}\quad\text{for }\mathfrak{m}_{\times}\text{-a.e. }(x,t)\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}.

Integrating both sides and using Fubini’s Theorem, we get

\int_{E\times\mathbb{R}}|\nabla g_{\epsilon}|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\times}\leq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m},

which implies

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})\leq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

∎

We now prove Theorem
1
from the Introduction. Here we add the properness assumption on the space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
as we will need to use (
1
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space and let
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\mathsf{X})\cap\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
. Then, for every bounded open set
E\subset\mathsf{X}
such that
|D^{s}u|(\partial E)=0
,
it holds

\lim_{t\to 0}\mathsf{A}(P_{t}(u),E)=\mathsf{A}(u,E).

Proof.

For every
t>0
, we set
u_{t}:=P_{t}(u)
and we note that this function is Lipschitz.
We first show that

\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E)\leq\liminf_{t\to 0}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Using Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
and the lower semicontinuity of perimeters we get

\displaystyle\int_{E}

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E)\leq\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{t\to 0}\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u_{t}),E\times\mathbb{R})=\liminf_{t\to 0}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m},

as desired.

We now show that

\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E)\geq\limsup_{t\to 0}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Note that

\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b)\in C(E)\times C(E)\\
a^{2}+b^{2}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\int_{E}a+|\nabla u_{t}|b\,d\mathfrak{m}.

For every such pair
(a,b)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E}

\displaystyle a+|\nabla u_{t}|b\,d\mathfrak{m}\leq\int_{E}a\,d\mathfrak{m}+\int_{E}be^{-Kt}\,d\bar{P}_{t}(|Du|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E}a\,d\mathfrak{m}+\int_{E}be^{-Kt}\,P_{t}(|\nabla u|)\,d\mathfrak{m}+e^{-Kt}\bar{P}_{t}(|D^{s}u|)(E).

Hence, passing to the supremum with respect to the pairs
(a,b)
, we get

\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}\leq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+e^{-2Kt}P_{t}(|\nabla u|)^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+e^{-Kt}\bar{P}_{t}(|D^{s}u|)(E).

Moreover, by definition of
\bar{P}_{t}
, we have that
\bar{P}_{t}(|D^{s}u|)
converges weakly to
|D^{s}u|
preserving the mass for every
t
, and since
|D^{s}u|(\partial E)=0
this implies that
\bar{P}_{t}(|D^{s}u|)(E)\to|D^{s}u|(E)
. In particular, passing to the limit as
t\to 0
in the previous inequality, we get

\limsup_{t\to 0}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}\leq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E).

∎

The next two results are needed to prove Theorem
2
from the Introduction.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open bounded set and let
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. For every Borel set
E\subset\Omega
it holds

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})=\mathsf{A}(u,E).

Proof.

The inequality

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})\geq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E)

is true by Proposition
2.2
so we only show the reverse one.

Suppose first that
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
.
It is clear that it is sufficient to consider the case when
E
is well contained in
\Omega
and such that
|Du|(\partial E)=0
and then argue by approximation.
So we assume that
E
has these properties and, modulo multiplying
u
by a cut off function which is equal to
1
in a neighborhood of
\bar{E}
, we can also suppose that
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
with
\mathsf{supp}(u)\subset\subset\Omega
.
In particular, when we extend
u
to be
0
outside of
\Omega
, we obtain a function in
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\mathsf{X})\cap\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
. When we refer to
u
in the next lines we implicitly mean its extension to
\mathsf{X}
.
By lower semicontinuity of perimeters and the previous proposition we then get

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})\leq\liminf_{t\to 0}\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(P_{t}(u)),E\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{t\to 0}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla P_{t}(u)|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E),

concluding the proof when
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
.

If
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
is not bounded, we consider for every
k>0
the function
u^{k}:=-k\vee u\wedge k
and we exploit the lower semicontinuity of perimeters to obtain that for every open set
E\subset\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u^{k}),E\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u^{k}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u^{k}|(E).

Moreover, since
|Du^{k}|\leq|Du|
as measures, the last term is controlled by

\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E),

concluding the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space. If
u:B_{2r}(x)\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function whose hypograph has finite perimeter, then
u\in\mathsf{BV}(B_{r}(x))
. Similarly if
\Omega
is bounded and
u\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
has hypograph of finite perimeter, then
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\Omega
is bounded and that
u\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
is a function whose hypograph has finite perimeter.
Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
g_{n}\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega\times(-N,N))
be convergent to
1_{\mathsf{Hyp}(u)}
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega\times(-N,N))
. Modulo passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
g_{n}^{t}\to 1_{\mathsf{Hyp}(u)}^{t}
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
for
\lambda^{1}
-a.e.
t\in(-N,N)
. Then

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega\times(-N,N)}|\nabla g_{n}|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\times}\geq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega\times(-N,N)}|\nabla g_{n}^{t}|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{\times}\geq\int_{-N}^{N}\mathsf{P}(\{u>t\},\Omega)dt.

Since
g_{n}
is arbitrary, letting
N\uparrow+\infty
, we deduce

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathsf{P}(\{u>t\},\Omega)dt<+\infty.

(4)

Since
u\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
, this is sufficient to conclude that
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
.

If
u:B_{2r}(x)\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable
we need to prove that
u\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(B_{r}(x))
, and then we get that
u\in\mathsf{BV}(B_{r}(x))
by the previous part of the proof. To this aim we write

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x)}|u|

\displaystyle\,d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{m}(\{|u|\geq t\}\cap B_{r}(x))\,dt

\displaystyle\leq T\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))+\int_{T}^{\infty}\mathfrak{m}(\{|u|\geq t\cap B_{r}(x)\})\,dt.

If
T\in\mathbb{N}
is large enough, for every
t\geq T
the isoperimetric inequality gives

\mathfrak{m}(\{|u|\geq t\cap B_{r}(x)\})\leq c(K,r)\mathsf{P}(\{|u|\geq t\},B_{2r}(x)).

In particular, combining this with (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x)}|u|\,d\mathfrak{m}\leq T\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))+c(K,r)\int_{T}^{\infty}\mathsf{P}(\{|u|\geq t\},B_{2r}(x))\,dt<+\infty,

concluding the proof.
∎

Combining Theorem
2.4
and the previous proposition we immediately get Theorem
2
, which we recall below.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and bounded and let
u\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then
\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})<+\infty
if and only if
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. In this case, for every Borel set
E\subset\Omega
, it holds

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),E\times\mathbb{R})=\mathsf{A}(u,E).

Moreover, if
u\in\mathsf{L}^{0}(B_{2r}(x))
and
\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),B_{2r}(x)\times\mathbb{R})<+\infty
, then
u\in\mathsf{BV}(B_{r}(x))
and the previous equation holds for every Borel set
E\subset B_{r}(x)
.

We now turn our attention to Theorem
3
, which will be an easy consequence of the approximation property of Theorem
1
.
As anticipated in the Introduction, given
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and an open set
E\subset\Omega
, we define the relaxed area functional as

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E):=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{1}(E)\cap\mathsf{Lip}(E),\|f_{n}-u\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(E)}\to 0\Big\}.

In
[
11
]
it was proved that for every
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
, we have

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(f,E)=\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla f|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m},

and in Theorem
3
we extend this to functions in
\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and bounded and let
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. Then, for every open set
E\subset\Omega
, it holds

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E)=\mathsf{A}(u,E).

Proof.

We first show that
for every open set
E\subset\Omega
we have

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E)\geq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E).

To this aim fix an open set
E
and let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{Lip}(E)
be such that

\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}\to\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E)

and
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(E)
. By the lower semicontinuity of perimeters, we get

\mathsf{A}(u,E)=\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E).

We now show that

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E)\leq\mathsf{A}(u,E)

for every open set
E\subset\Omega
.
To this aim it is sufficient to consider open sets
E\subset\subset\Omega
such that
|D^{s}u|(\partial E)=0
and then argue by approximation. Let
u^{k}:=-k\vee u\wedge k
and note that for every
\epsilon>0
we have that if
k
is sufficiently large

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u)(E)\leq\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u^{k})(E)+\epsilon.

Using the lower semicontinuity of
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}
, the fact that
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}
is the area functional on Lipschitz functions and Theorem
1
, we then obtain that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u^{k},E)\leq\liminf_{t\to 0}\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(P_{t}(u^{k}),E)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{t\to 0}\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla P_{t}(u^{k})|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u^{k}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u^{k}|(E).

In particular we get that, if
k
is large enough, we have

\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E)\leq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u^{k}|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u^{k}|(E)+\epsilon,

so that
\tilde{\mathsf{A}}(u,E)\leq\int_{E}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+|D^{s}u|(E)+\epsilon
. Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrarily small, we conclude.
∎

We conclude the section with some basic properties of minimizers of the area functional in
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces that are needed to prove Theorem
4
from the Introduction.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let
u\in\mathsf{BV}_{loc}(\Omega)
. We say that that
u
is an area minimizer in
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
if for every
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega^{\prime})
such that
\{f\neq u\}\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime}
we have

\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega^{\prime})\leq\mathsf{A}(f,\Omega^{\prime}).

If we say that
u\in\mathsf{BV}_{loc}(\Omega)
is an area minimizer, we mean that the previous condition is satisfied for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
.

The following proposition follows from
[
30
, Theorem
4.2
]
keeping in mind that in our setting the area functional coincides with the relaxed area functional thanks to Theorem
3
. Since the setting of
[
30
]
is the one of
PI
spaces (and the proof of
[
30
, Theorem
4.2
]
relies crucially on the doubling assumption), the next result holds in the finite dimensional setting of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let
u\in\mathsf{BV}_{loc}(\Omega)
be an area minimizer, then
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
.

A similar result holds for functions whose hypograph is perimeter minimizing
adapting an argument that can be found in
[
29
]
. We report such argument with the due adaptations.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and let
u\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
be a function whose hypograph minimizes the perimeter. Then
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\Omega
and suppose for simplicity that
B_{3}(x)\subset\subset\Omega
.
We will show that
u
is uniformly bounded in its Lebesgue points in
B_{1}(x)
, which will then imply the claim. To this aim let
y\in B_{1}(x)
be a Lebesgue point for
u
and assume without loss of generality that
u(y)>0
.
Denoting by
\mathsf{Hyp}(u)
the closed representative of the hypograph, we have that
(y,u(y))\in\partial\mathsf{Hyp}(u)
, so that the density estimates from Theorem
1.15
imply that

\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(B_{1}^{\times}(y,u(y))\cap\mathsf{Hyp}(u))\geq c(K,N).

In particular if
T\in\mathbb{N}
and
u(y)>2T
we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u)\cap B_{1}(y)\times[0,2T])

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u)\cap B^{\times}_{1}(y,2i))

\displaystyle\geq T\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(B_{1}^{\times}(y,u(y))\cap\mathsf{Hyp}(u))\geq Tc(K,N).

This implies that

\int_{B_{2}(x)}u\vee 0\,d\mathfrak{m}\geq\int_{B_{1}(y)}u\vee 0\,d\mathfrak{m}\geq Tc(K,N).

Putting everything together, if
y\in B_{1}(x)
is a Lebesgue point of
u
and
u(y)>2T
, then
T\leq C(x,u,K,N)<+\infty
. This shows that for every such
y
we have
u(y)\leq 2C(x,u,K,N)+1
, proving the claim and the statement.
∎

The proof of the next proposition follows by adapting arguments from
[
17
, Section
3.2
]
and we report it for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2.9
.

Given a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, an open set
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
and a function
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
, then
u
is a minimizer of the area functional in
\Omega
if and only if its hypograph is a perimeter minimizer in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

If the hypograph of
u
is perimeter minimizing, then
for every
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\{f\neq u\}\subset\subset\Omega
we have
\mathsf{Hyp}(u)\Delta\mathsf{Hyp}(f)\subset\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, so that applying Theorem
2
we obtain

\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega)\leq\mathsf{A}(f,\Omega).

Similarly, if
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
is such that
\{f\neq u\}\subset\subset\Omega
, then considering
f^{c}:=-c\vee f\wedge c
with
c:=\|u\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}}+1
we get by the previous case that

\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega)\leq\mathsf{A}(f^{c},\Omega)\leq\mathsf{A}(f,\Omega).

We now prove the reverse implication, i.e. that if

u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
is a minimizer of the area functional in
\Omega
, then its hypograph is a perimeter minimizer in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.

To this aim, consider a set
E\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathsf{Hyp}(u)\Delta E\subset\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. Modulo translating vertically, we may suppose that there exists
c>1
such that
u
takes values in
(1,c-1)
and
\mathsf{Hyp}(u)\Delta E\subset\subset\Omega\times(1,c-1)
.
We then define
w(E):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

w(E)(x):=\int_{0}^{c}1_{E}(x,s)\,ds

and we claim that

w(E)\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{A}(w(E),\Omega)\leq\mathsf{P}(E,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}).

(5)

If the claim holds, noting that by construction
w(E)
is a competitor for
u
, we get

\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})=\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega)\leq\mathsf{A}(w(E),\Omega)\leq\mathsf{P}(E,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}).

To prove the claim, we consider a sequence
f_{n}\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega\times(0,c))
converging in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega\times(0,c))
to
1_{E}
and such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}|Df_{n}|(\Omega\times(0,c))=\mathsf{P}(E,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}).

Modulo truncating, we can assume that
f_{n}\equiv 1
on
\Omega\times\{0\}
and
f_{n}\equiv 0
on
\Omega\times\{c\}
.
We then define
w(f_{n}):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as

w(f_{n})(x):=\int_{0}^{c}f_{n}(x,s)\,ds.

These functions are Lipschitz since

\frac{|w(f_{n})(x)-w(f_{n})(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}\leq\int_{0}^{c}\frac{|f_{n}(x,s)-f_{n}(y,s)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}\,ds\leq c\mathsf{L}(f_{n}).

We now use the notation defined before Proposition
1.7
.
By reverse Fatou Lemma and the fact that each
f_{n}
is Lipschitz, for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X
it holds

|\nabla w(f_{n})|(x)=\mathsf{lip}(w(f_{n}))(x)\leq\int_{0}^{c}|\nabla f_{n}^{s}|(x)\,ds.

(6)

Moreover, using the tensorization property of Proposition
1.7
, it holds

\displaystyle|Df_{n}|(\Omega\times(0,c))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times(0,c)}|\nabla f_{n}|(x,s)\,d\mathfrak{m}\,ds=\int_{\Omega\times(0,c)}\sqrt{|\nabla f_{n}^{s}|^{2}(x)+|\nabla f_{n}^{x}|^{2}(s)}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b)\in C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega)\\
a^{2}+b^{2}\leq 1\\
a,b\geq 0\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega\times(0,c)}a(x)|\nabla f_{n}^{s}|(x)+b(x)|\nabla f_{n}^{x}|(s)\,d\mathfrak{m}\,ds.

Combining with (
6
) and the fact that
f_{n}=1
on
\Omega\times\{0\}
and
f_{n}=0
on
\Omega\times\{c\}
, we deduce

\displaystyle|Df_{n}|(\Omega\times(0,c))\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b)\in C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega)\\
a^{2}+b^{2}\leq 1\\
a,b\geq 0\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla w(f_{n})|(x)+b(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}=\mathsf{A}(w(f_{n}),\Omega).

Moreover,
w(f_{n})\to w(E)
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
as
n\to+\infty
since

\int_{\Omega}|w(f_{n})-w(E)|\,d\mathfrak{m}\leq\int_{\Omega\times(0,c)}|f_{n}(x,s)-1_{E}(x,s)|\,d\mathfrak{m}\,ds.

These facts imply that
w(E)\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
as claimed. Concerning the area of
w(E)
, we get

\mathsf{A}(w(E),\Omega)\leq\liminf\mathsf{A}(w(f_{n}),\Omega)\leq\liminf|Df_{n}|(\Omega\times(0,c))=\mathsf{P}(E,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}),

proving the claim (
5
).
∎

If the space in question is finite dimensional, the previous result holds without the assumption that
u
is bounded. This is the content of Proposition
2.10
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and let
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
, then
u
is a minimizer of the area functional in
\Omega
if and only if its hypograph is a perimeter minimizer in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

If
u
minimizes the area, then, by Proposition
2.7
,
u
is locally bounded in
\Omega
and we can conclude by Proposition
2.9
.

Conversely, if the hypograph of
u
minimizes the perimeter, then
u
is locally bounded by Proposition
2.8
.
Consider a competitor
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
for
u
and a subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
such that
\{f\neq u\}\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime}
. We define
f^{c}:=-c\vee f\wedge c
for
c:=\|u\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}+1
, so that

\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega^{\prime})=\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(u),\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R})\leq\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{Hyp}(f^{c}),\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R})=\mathsf{A}(f^{c},\Omega^{\prime})\leq\mathsf{A}(f,\Omega^{\prime}),

which implies

\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega)\leq\mathsf{A}(f,\Omega).

∎

We now prove Theorem
4
and its corollary.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a proper
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
space, let
\Omega\subsetneq\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a function whose hypograph is perimeter minimizing.
If
v\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
has hypograph which is perimeter minimizing and
\{u\neq v\}\subset\subset\Omega
, then
u=v
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2
,
v\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and by Proposition
2.9
, both
u
and
v
are area minimizers in
\Omega
.

For every
t\in(0,1)
we consider the convex combination
tu+(1-t)v
. We have

\mathsf{A}(tu+(1-t)v,\Omega)\leq t\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega)+(1-t)\mathsf{A}(v,\Omega)=\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega).

Since
tu+(1-t)v
is a competitor for
u
, the previous inequality implies

\mathsf{A}(tu+(1-t)v,\Omega)=\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega).

(7)

The function
a\mapsto\sqrt{1+a^{2}}
is strictly convex on the positive real line. Hence, (
7
) implies that, for every
t\in(0,1)
, it holds

|\nabla(tu+(1-t)v)|=|\nabla u|\quad\mathfrak{m}\text{-almost everywhere in }\Omega.

Taking the squares in the previous identity and evaluating at
t=0
and
t=1/2
, we deduce

\begin{cases}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=|\nabla u|^{2}\\
|\nabla v|^{2}=|\nabla u|^{2},\end{cases}

which in turn implies

|\nabla(u-v)|=0\quad\mathfrak{m}\text{-almost everywhere in }\Omega.

Hence, the function
\psi:=v-u
satisfies
|D\psi|=|D^{s}\psi|
and has support well contained in
\Omega
. Moreover, the function
t\mapsto\mathsf{A}(u+t\psi,\Omega)
is constant in
(0,1)
. Hence

0=\frac{d}{dt}\mathsf{A}(u+t\psi,\Omega)=\frac{d}{dt}|D^{s}(u+t\psi)|(\Omega)=\frac{d}{dt}|D^{s}(t\psi)|(\Omega)=|D^{s}\psi|(\Omega)=|D\psi|(\Omega).

In particular,
\psi
is constant. Since the support of
\psi
is well contained in
\Omega
and
\Omega\subsetneq\mathsf{X}
, it holds
\psi\equiv 0
.
∎

Corollary
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subsetneq\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a function whose hypograph is perimeter minimizing.
If
v\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
has hypograph which is perimeter minimizing and
\{u\neq v\}\subset\subset\Omega
, then
u=v
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
both
u
and
v
are locally bounded. The statement follows by Theorem
4
.
∎

3
Approximation of minimal graphs in
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
3.3
, which implies Theorem
5
.

We consider spaces
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},x_{i})
converging to a space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x)
in pmGH sense and functions
u_{i}\in\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X}_{i})
converging in
\mathsf{L}^{1}
to
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
. As anticipated, we use the so-called extrinsic viewpoint and we work in the space
(Z,\mathsf{d}_{Z})
which realizes the convergence. In particular, whenever we consider a ball
B_{r}(x)
in this context, unless otherwise specified, we mean the ball of radius
r
centered in
x
in the space
Z
(observe that the intersection of this ball with the embedding of
\mathsf{X}
in
Z
would give the corresponding ball in
\mathsf{X}
).
Similarly, unless otherwise stated, each function
u_{i}
will be considered as a function on
Z
(extending it to zero outside of the embedding of
\mathsf{X}_{i}
in
Z
), so that it will make sense
to consider products of the form
u_{i}1_{B_{r}(x)}:Z\to\mathbb{R}
. To avoid ambiguity, we will denote by
P_{t}^{\mathsf{X}_{i}}
the heat flow on the space
\mathsf{X}_{i}
.

Similarly, given
f:Z\to\mathbb{R}
, we will write
\mathsf{A}(f,B_{r}(x),\mathfrak{m})
meaning the area of
f
on
B_{r}(x)\subset Z
w.r.t. to the pushforward of the measure
\mathfrak{m}
in
Z
and we will write
f\in\mathsf{BV}(B_{r}(x),\mathfrak{m})
(and the same notation will be used for Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces) to specify that the measure considered is again the pushforward of the measure
\mathfrak{m}
in
Z
. We remark that
f\in\mathsf{BV}(Z,\mathfrak{m})
if and only if (restricting
f
to
\mathsf{X}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is concentrated)
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
, and in this case their total variation measures coincide (see
[
20
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},x_{i})
be a sequence of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces converging in pmGH sense to
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x)
.

1.

If
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X},\mathfrak{m})
, and
u_{i}\in\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i})
is a sequence such that
u_{i}\to u
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}
,
then for every open set
A\subset Z
we have

\mathsf{A}(u,A,\mathfrak{m})\leq\liminf_{i}\mathsf{A}(u_{i},A,\mathfrak{m}_{i}).

2.

If
u\in\mathsf{BV}(B_{r}(x),\mathfrak{m})
,
then for every
s\in(0,r)
such that
|D^{s}u|(\partial B_{s}(x))=0

there exists a sequence of Lipschitz functions
u_{i}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(B_{s}(x),\mathfrak{m}_{i})
such that

u_{i}1_{B_{s}(x)}
converges in
\mathsf{L}^{1}
-sense to
u1_{B_{s}(x)}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\mathfrak{m})
and
\mathsf{A}(u_{i},B_{s}(x),\mathfrak{m}_{i})\to\mathsf{A}(u,B_{s}(x),\mathfrak{m})
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows by combining the localized lower semicontinuity of total variations given in
Proposition
1.13
and the identification between area and perimeter given in Theorem
2
.

We now prove the second assertion following the proof in
[
9
, Theorem
8.1
]
.

Fix
s\in(0,r)
and observe that, modulo multiplying with a cut off function, we can assume that
u
has support well contained in
B_{r}(x)
. Also, modulo truncating (and using a diagonal argument), we can also assume that
u\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(B_{r}(x),\mathfrak{m})
(as the areas of the truncated functions converge to the area of the initial function).
In what follows, when we refer to
u
, we implicitly mean its extension to zero on the whole
Z
.

Since
|D^{s}u|(\partial B_{s}(x))=0
, Theorem
1
guarantees that

\mathsf{A}(P^{\mathsf{X}}_{t}(u),B_{s}(x),\mathfrak{m})\to\mathsf{A}(u,B_{s}(x),\mathfrak{m})\quad\text{as }t\to 0.

Hence, if we manage to approximate in area the functions
P^{\mathsf{X}}_{t}(u)

Fix
r/2<t<s<r

be the area minimizers given by Theorem

Each function

,we have

\sup_{B^{m}_{t/c}(\bar{x}_{k})}|\nabla u_{k}|\leq ce^{ct^{-1}(u_{k}(x_{k})-\inf_{y\in B_{t}(x_{k})}u_{k}(y))}.

Observe now that the Kuratowski convergence of the graphs of

\sup_{B^{m}_{t/c}(\bar{x}_{k})}|\nabla u_{k}|\leq ce^{ct^{-1}(\sup_{y\in B_{\delta}(x)}u(y)-\inf_{y\in B_{s}(x)}u(y)+2\delta)}.

Theorem A.1
.

Assume that (
MVP
) holds. Let

Example A.9
.

On generic metric measure spaces, Sobolev minimizers of the area functional are not locally Lipschitz. To see this, consider the metric measure space
(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},\mu)
, where
\mathsf{d}_{e}
is the Euclidean distance and
\mu:=|x|\lambda^{1}
. We claim that the function
1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
belongs to
\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},\mu)
and minimizes the area.
Indeed, consider the Lipschitz functions
f_{n}(x):=(1-n\mathsf{d}_{e}(x,\mathbb{R}_{+})\vee 0
. For every
r>0
, it holds

|D1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|(-r,r)\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-r}^{r}\mathsf{lip}f_{n}(x)\,d\mu=\lim_{n\to+\infty}n\int_{-1/n}^{0}|x|\,dx=0.

This proves that
|D1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|=0
, so that, in particular,
1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},\mu)
and
\mathsf{A}(1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}},\Omega)=\mu(\Omega)
for every
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Let now
u\in\mathsf{BV}(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},\mu)
be a competitor for
1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
with
\{u\neq 1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\}\subset\subset\Omega
for some open set
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{R}
. Then, by definition of area, it holds

\mathsf{A}(u,\Omega)\geq\mu(\Omega)=\mathsf{A}(1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}},\Omega),

showing that
1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is indeed a minimizer.

Lemma A.10
.

Assume that (
MVP
) holds. Let
r_{0}>0
,
v>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d},x)
be a manifold with
\mathsf{Ric}_{\mathsf{M}^{n}}\geq 0
and
\mathsf{H}^{n}(B_{r_{0}}(x))\geq vr_{0}^{n}
. There exists
c(n,v)>1
such that, if
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{cr_{0}}(x))
is an area minimizer, then given
r>0
such that

r^{2}=r_{0}^{2}-4\Big(\sup_{y\in B_{r/2}(x)}|u(y)-u(x)|\Big)^{2},

we have

\sup_{y\in B_{r/2}(x)}|\nabla u|(y)\leq c\Big(1+r^{-1}\sup_{y\in B_{r/2}(x)}|u(y)-u(x)|\Big)^{n}.

Proof.

Thanks to Propositions
A.3
and (
MVP
),
there exists a constant
c(n,v)>0
such that

\frac{1}{\mathsf{H}^{n}(B^{m}_{r_{0}}(\bar{x}))}\int_{B^{m}_{r_{0}}(\bar{x})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\,d\mathsf{H}^{n}\leq c\inf_{y\in\pi(B^{m}_{r_{0}/2}(\bar{x}))}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}(y),

where
\mathsf{H}^{n}
is the Hausdorff measure in
\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}
and we are using the previously defined convention for functions defined on
\mathsf{M}
evaluated on the graph of
u
.

Hence, possibly changing
c
at every step, we get

\sup_{y\in\pi(B^{m}_{r_{0}/2}(\bar{x}))}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}(y)\leq c\frac{\mathsf{H}^{n}(B^{m}_{r_{0}}(\bar{x}))}{\mathsf{H}^{n}(\pi(B^{m}_{r_{0}}(\bar{x})))},

which, together with the fact that
u
is an area minimizer, implies that

\sup_{y\in\pi(B^{m}_{r_{0}/2}(\bar{x}))}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}(y)\leq\frac{cr_{0}^{n}}{\mathsf{H}^{n}(\pi(B^{m}_{r_{0}}(\bar{x})))}.

By our hypothesis on
r>0
we then get that
B_{r/2}(x)\subset\pi(B^{m}_{r_{0}/2}(\bar{x}))\subset\pi(B^{m}_{r_{0}}(\bar{x}))
, while Bishop Gromov’s inequality and the fact that

\mathsf{H}^{n}(B_{r_{0}}(x))\geq vr_{0}^{n}
imply that
\mathsf{H}^{n}(B_{r/2}(x))\geq 2^{-n}vr^{n}
. Putting these facts together we get, changing again
c>0
,

\sup_{y\in\pi(B^{m}_{r_{0}/2}(\bar{x}))}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}(y)\leq c\frac{r_{0}^{n}}{r^{n}}\leq c\Big(1+r^{-2}\sup_{y\in B_{r/2}(x)}|u(y)-u(x)|^{2}\Big)^{n/2},

which then implies the statement.
∎

Lemma A.12
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},x)
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)

If

is a function whose hypograph minimizes the perimeter and such that

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{\max\{-u(y),0\}}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}=0,

(14)

then

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{|u(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}=0.

\mathsf{L}(u)
is the Lipschitz constant of

(Theorem

Theorem A.2
.

Assume that (
MVP
) holds. Let

be fixed and let
(\mathsf{M}^{n}_{k},\mathsf{d}_{k},x_{k})
be a sequence of pointed Riemannian manifolds with
\mathsf{Ric}_{\mathsf{M}^{n}_{k}}\geq 0
converging in pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},x)
such that

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\mathsf{H}^{n}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{n}}\geq v>0.

If

is an area minimizer such that

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{\max\{-u(y),0\}}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}=0,

Proof.

By Lemma
A.5
and Theorem
A.8
we know that
u\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{X})
. Hence the previous lemma gives that

\lim_{y\to+\infty}\frac{|u(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}=0.

We claim that
|\nabla u|=0
in
B_{1}(x)
. A rescaling argument then gives
|\nabla u|=0
in
B_{r}(x)
for every
r>0
, yielding the statement.

To this aim, we fix
\epsilon>0
and we choose
R>1
large enough so that, for every
y\in\mathsf{X}
with
\mathsf{d}(x,y)>R
, it holds

|u(y)|\leq\epsilon\mathsf{d}(x,y).

Consider
R_{1}>R
to be fixed later and the minimizers
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}(B_{5R_{1}}(x)\cap\mathsf{M}_{k})
converging in
\mathsf{L}^{1}
to
u
on
B_{5R_{1}}(x)
.
For every
k
large enough, since the hypographs of
u_{k}
converge in Kuratowski sense to the hypograph of
u
in
B_{5R_{1}}(x)\times\mathbb{R}
, we have that

|u_{k}(y)|\leq 2\epsilon\mathsf{d}_{k}(x,y)

(15)

for every
y\in B_{R_{1}}(x_{k})
such that
\mathsf{d}_{k}(x_{k},y)>2R
.

So far we estimated the growth of the functions
u_{k}
exploiting the growth condition on
u
; now we estimate their Lipschitz constants.
For every
s>0
let
c_{s}
be the quantity defined in Theorem
A.8
relative to
u
and let
c_{s}^{k}
denote the same quantity relative to each
u_{k}
.
Note that by our assumption on the growth of
u
, we have that there exists
c>1
such that
c_{s}\leq c
for every
s
.

Since the hypographs of the functions
u_{k}
converge in Kuratowski sense to the one of
u
, we have that
c_{4R_{1}}^{k}\to c_{4R_{1}}
, so that by the a-priori gradient estimate Theorem
A.8
, for

\sup_{z\in B_{3R_{1}/(16c)}(x_{k})}|\nabla u_{k}|(z)\leq 2c.

(16)

To shorten the notation we set
R_{2}:=3R_{1}/(16c)
.
A posteriori, since (
16
) holds for every
R_{1}
large enough, we see that we could have chosen
R_{1}>0
(large enough) so that

B_{2R}(x_{k})\subset\pi(B^{m}_{R_{2}}(\bar{x}_{k})).

(17)

We now denote by
\mathsf{H}^{n}_{k}
and

respectively the Hausdorff measure and the gradient in

\int_{B^{m}_{R_{2}}(\bar{x}_{k})}|\nabla_{m}u_{k}|^{2}\,d\mathsf{H}^{n}_{k}\leq 4\epsilon^{2}\mathsf{H}^{n}_{k}(B^{m}_{2R_{2}}(\bar{x}_{k})).

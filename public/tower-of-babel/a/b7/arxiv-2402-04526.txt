A polynomial time algorithm to find star chromatic index on bounded treewidth graphs with given maximum degree
Yichen Wang, Mei Lu

Abstract
A star edge coloring of a graph G G is a proper edge coloring with no 2-colored path or cycle of length four. The star edge coloring problem is to find an edge coloring of a given graph G G with minimum number k k of colors such that G G admits a star edge coloring with k k colors. This problem is known to be NP-complete. In this paper, for a bounded treewidth graph with given maximum degree, we show that it can be solved in polynomial time.



Keywords:
edge coloring; star edge coloring; bounded treewidth.

1
Introduction

A proper edge coloring of a graph
G
with vertex set
V
and edge set
E
is an assignment of colors to the edges of
G
such that no two adjacent edges have the same color.
Under additional constraints on the proper edge coloring, we have a variety of colorings such as strong edge coloring, vertex distinguishing coloring and so on.
A
star edge coloring
of
G
is a proper edge coloring where at least three distinct
colors are used on the edges of every path and cycle of length four, i.e., there is neither
bichromatic path nor cycle of length four.
We say
G
is
k
-star-edge-colorable
if
G
admits a star edge coloring using at most
k
colors
and the
star chromatic index

\chi_{st}^{\prime}(G)
of
G
is the minimum number
k
for which

G
has a star edge coloring with
k
colors.

Star edge coloring is invited by Liu and Deng
[
14
]
motivated by the vertex version
[
1
,
7
,
9
]
. In the same paper, Liu and Deng presented an upper bound on the star chromatic index of
graphs with maximum degree
\Delta\geq 7
. In
[
8
]
, Dvořák, Mohar and Šámal presented some upper bounds and lower
bounds on the star chromatic index of complete graphs and subcubic graphs (i.e. with maximum degree at most three). Some bounds on the star
chromatic index of subcubic outerplanar graphs, trees and outerplanar graphs can be find in
[
11
,
13
,
15
,
17
,
20
]
.
The relevant research results of star chromatic index can be referred to the survey
[
12
]
.

In
[
13
]
, Lei, Shi and Song showed that it is
NP
-complete to determine whether
\chi_{st}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3
for an arbitrary graph
G
.
In
[
16
]
, Omoomi, Roshanbin and Dastjerdi presented a polynomial time algorithm that finds an optimum star edge-coloring
for every tree.

The treewidth of a graph is an important invariant in graph theory. The concept of treewidth was originally introduced by Bertelé and Brioschi
[
3
]
under the name of dimension. It was later rediscovered by Halin
[
10
]
in 1976 and by Robertson and Seymour
[
18

in 1984.
The treewidth of a graph gives an indication of how far away the graph is from being
a tree or forest. The treewidth also
is a parameter that plays a fundamental role in various graph algorithms. It is well-known that many NP-complete problems can be solved in polynomial time on bounded treewidth graphs
[
21

19
,
6
]
.
In this paper, for bounded treewidth graphs with given maximum degree, we propose a polynomial time algorithm which can determine the star chromatic index of
G
. Our main idea comes from
[
19
]
.

2
Preliminaries and definitions

In this section, we give definitions involving in treewidth. The treewidth of a graph is defined through
the concept of tree-decomposition.

Definition 2.1

A tree-decomposition of a graph
G=(V,E)
is a pair
(X,T)
,
where
T(I,F)
is a tree with vertex set
I
and edge set
F
, and
X=\{X_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is a family of subsets of
V
, one for each node of
T
, such that:

•

\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}X_{i}=V
.

•

for each edge
uv\in E
, there exists an
i\in I
such that
u,v\in X_{i}
.

•

for all
i,j,s\in I
, if
j
is on the path from
i
to
s
in
T
, then
X_{i}\cap X_{s}\subseteq X_{j}
.

Suppose
i
is a node of
T
, there exists a vertex set
X_{i}
corresponds to
i
.
In the following, we also call
X_{i}
the node of
T
for convenience. If
X_{i}
is a non-leaf node of
T
, we call it an internal node.
The width of a tree-decomposition
(X,T)
is
\max\limits_{i\in I}|X_{i}|-1
.
The treewidth of a graph
G
is the minimum treewidth over all possible tree-decompositions of
G
.
The problem of deciding whether a graph has tree-decomposition of treewidth at most
k
is NP-complete
[
2
]
and Bodlaender
[
5
]
proved that the problem is fixed-parameter tractable, that is, there is a polynomial
p
and an algorithm that, for a given graph
G=(V,E)
, computes a tree-decomposition of
G
of width
k
in time at most

2^{p(k)}|V|
.

We say a tree-decomposition
(X,T)
of treewidth
k
is
smooth
, if
T
is a binary tree and:

•

for all
i\in I
,
|X_{i}|=k+1
.

•

every internal node
X_{i}
has two children, and if
X_{L},X_{R}
are its children, then either
X_{L}=X_{i}
or
X_{R}=X_{i}
.

•

for all
(i,j)\in F
:
k\leq|X_{i}\cap X_{j}|\leq k+1
.

•

for each edge
uv\in E
, there is at least one leaf
i\in I
, with
u,v\in X_{i}
.

It can be shown that any tree-decomposition of a graph
G
can be transformed into a smooth tree-decomposition of
G
with the same treewidth and size
O(n)
in linear time
[
4
,
5
]
.

For a given graph
G
with treewidth at most
k
, let
(X,T)
be its tree-decomposition.
For each node
X_{i}
of
T
, we define
T_{i}
to be the subtree of
T
rooted at
i
.
Let
X_{i}
be a node of
T
.
If
X_{i}
is a leaf, then we define
V_{i}=X_{i}
,
E_{i}=\{uv\in E~|~u,v\in X_{i}\}
; if
X_{i}
is an internal node with children
X_{L},X_{R}
, then we define
V_{i}=V_{L}\cup V_{R},E_{i}=E_{L}\cup E_{R}
.
The graph
G(V_{i},E_{i})
is denoted by
G_{i}
.

For every edge
e\in E
, there is at least one leaf
i
of
T
such that
u,v\in X_{i}
.
We choose one such
i
as the
representative
of
e
and denote it by
rep(e)
.
If
X_{i}
is a leaf, we define
E_{i}^{\prime}=\{e\in E~|~rep(e)\in T_{i}\}
; if
X_{i}
is an internal node with children
X_{L},X_{R}
, we define
E_{i}^{\prime}=E_{L}^{\prime}\cup E_{R}^{\prime}
.
Note that by this definition, for every node
X_{i}
,
E_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq E_{i}
, and if
X_{i}
is an internal node with children
X_{L},X_{R}
, then
E_{L}^{\prime}\cap E_{R}^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
We denote the graph on vertex set
V_{i}
and edge set
E_{i}^{\prime}
by
G_{i}^{\prime}
.

In the rest of this paper, when
X_{i}
is an internal node in a smooth tree-decomposition, we always use
X_{L}
and
X_{R}
to represent its two children.
Without loss of generality, we assume
X_{L}=X_{i}
,
X_{i}-X_{R}=\{v^{\prime}\}
and
X_{R}-X_{i}=\{v^{\prime\prime}\}
. If
e\in E(G)
, we also use
e
to denote the subset consisting of two vertices of
e
for short.
Let
\Delta
be the maximum degree of
G
. The following facts are obvious from definition.

Fact 2.1

Let
X_{i}
be an internal node in a smooth tree-decomposition and
X_{L}
and
X_{R}
its two children.
If
e\in E_{R}^{\prime}
(resp.
e\in E_{L}^{\prime}
), then
v^{\prime}\notin e
(resp.
v^{\prime\prime}\notin e
).

Fact 2.2

Let
X_{i}
be an internal node in a smooth tree-decomposition and
X_{L}
and
X_{R}
its two children.
If
e_{1}\in E_{L}^{\prime},e_{2}\in E_{R}^{\prime}
and
\{v\}=e_{1}\cap e_{2}
, then
v\in X_{i}-\{v^{\prime}\}
.

3
The star edge coloring algorithm

In this section, we give a polynomial time algorithm to
find the star chromatic index of bounded treewidth graphs with given maximum degree. Here we present the main theorem of our paper.

Theorem 3.1

For everg graph
G
of order
n
with treewidth
k
and maximum degree
\Delta
, and integer
c
,
there is a deterministic algorithm that determines in time
O(nc^{2{(k+1)}^{2}\Delta^{6}})
whether
G
has a star edge coloring using at most
c
colors or not and finds such star edge coloring if it exists.

Let
(X,T)
be a smooth tree-decomposition of
G
and
C=\{1,2,\ldots,c\}
the set of colors.
For a node
X_{i}
of
T
, a mapping
f:E_{i}^{\prime}\rightarrow C
is called a
partial coloring
of
G_{i}^{\prime}
.
We say a partial coloring is a
proper
edge coloring if no two adjacent edges have the same color.
We say a partial coloring
f
of
G_{i}^{\prime}
is
valid
if it is a star edge coloring of
G_{i}^{\prime}
, that is, it is proper and no path or cycle of length
4
in
G_{i}^{\prime}
is bicolored.

Consider a partial coloring
f
of
G_{i}^{\prime}
.
For a fixed color pair
(c_{1},c_{2})\in C^{2}
, we define the neighbour sets as the following equations.

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}\in X_{i}~|~\exists\{v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2}\}\subseteq E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v_{1})=c_{1},f(v_{1}v_{2})=c_{2}\},

(1)

\displaystyle N^{(2)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}\in X_{i}~|~\exists\{v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2}\}\subseteq E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v_{1})=c_{2},f(v_{1}v_{2})=c_{1}\},

\displaystyle N^{(3)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{(v_{0},v_{2})\in X_{i}^{2}~|~\exists\{v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2}\}\subseteq E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v_{1})=c_{1},f(v_{1}v_{2})=c_{2}\},

\displaystyle N^{(4)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}\in X_{i}~|~\exists\{v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2},v_{2}v_{3}\}\subseteq E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v_{1})=c_{1},f(v_{1}v_{2})=c_{2},f(v_{2}v_{3})=c_{1}\},

\displaystyle N^{(5)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}\in X_{i}~|~\exists\{v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{2},v_{2}v_{3}\}\subseteq E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v_{1})=c_{2},f(v_{1}v_{2})=c_{1},f(v_{2}v_{3})=c_{2}\},

\displaystyle N^{(6)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}\in X_{i}~|~\exists v_{0}v\in E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v)=c_{1}\},

\displaystyle N^{(7)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}\in X_{i}~|~\exists v_{0}v\in E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v)=c_{2}\},

\displaystyle N^{(8)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{(v_{0},v_{1})\in X_{i}^{2}~|~\exists v_{0}v_{1}\in E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v_{1})=c_{1}\},

\displaystyle N^{(9)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2})

\displaystyle=\{(v_{0},v_{1})\in X_{i}^{2}~|~\exists v_{0}v_{1}\in E_{i}^{\prime},f(v_{0}v_{1})=c_{2}\}.

Let
S
be a set. We use
2^{S}
to represent the power set of
S
.
Denote

\mathcal{S}(X_{i})=\{\big(A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(9)}\big)~|~A^{(j)}\subseteq X_{i},j=1,2,4,5,6,7\mbox{~and~}A^{(j)}\subseteq X_{i}^{2},j=3,8,9\}.

In the following, we abbreviate
\big(A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(9)}\big)
to
{\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}
.
For a partial coloring
f
of
G_{i}^{\prime}
, define the color class function
CL_{f}:\mathcal{S}(X_{i})\rightarrow 2^{C^{2}}
as Eq. (
2
).
In this case, we say
CL_{f}
is the color class function on
X_{i}
.

\displaystyle CL_{f}\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)=\{(c_{1},c_{2})\in C^{2}~|~A^{(j)}=N^{(j)}_{f}(c_{1},c_{2}),1\leq j\leq 9\}.

(2)

A color class function
CL_{f}
is

if and only if there exists a valid partial coloring

.

Fact 3.1

Let
f
be a partial coloring of

. Then

\{CL_{f}({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9})~|~{\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\in\mathcal{S}(X_{i})\}
forms a partition of

.

Our idea of developing an algorithm is to calculate all active color class functions on a node

.
For any partial coloring
f
of
G_{i}^{\prime}
, the color class function is only concerned with the coloring of edges adjacent to a vertex whose distance is at most
3
to some vertex in
X_{i}

(the distance between two vertices is the minimum length of a path connecting them).
Since at most
(k+1)\Delta^{3}
vertices have a distance at most

to some vertex in
X_{i}
, at most
{(k+1)}^{2}\Delta^{6}
edges have an effect on the color class function.
Therefore, at most

What we are most concerned about is how to determine whether a color class function is active.
To do this, we first need to analyse the relationship of color class functions on
X_{i},X_{L}
and
X_{R}

Let
f
be a partial coloring of

For any

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}~|~(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{L}^{(8)},v_{1}\in A_{R}^{(7)}\}\cup\{v_{0}~|~(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{R}^{(8)},v_{1}\in A_{L}^{(7)}\},

(3)

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}~|~(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{L}^{(9)},v_{1}\in A_{R}^{(6)}\}\cup\{v_{0}~|~(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{R}^{(9)},v_{1}\in A_{L}^{(6)}\},

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\{(v_{1},v_{2})~|~\exists(v_{1},v_{3})\in A_{L}^{(8)},(v_{3},v_{2})\in A_{R}^{(9)}\}\cup\{(v_{1},v_{2})~|~\exists(v_{1},v_{3})\in A_{R}^{(8)},(v_{3},v_{2})\in A_{L}^{(9)}\},

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{L}^{(3)},v_{1}\in A_{R}^{(6)}\}\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{L}^{(8)},(v_{1},v_{2})\in A_{R}^{(9)},v_{2}\in A_{L}^{(6)}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{L}^{(8)},v_{1}\in A_{R}^{(2)}\}\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{R}^{(3)},v_{1}\in A_{L}^{(6)}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{R}^{(8)},(v_{1},v_{2})\in A_{L}^{(9)},v_{2}\in A_{R}^{(6)}\}\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{R}^{(8)},v_{1}\in A_{L}^{(2)}\},

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{(5)}

\displaystyle=\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{1},v_{0})\in A_{L}^{(3)},v_{1}\in A_{R}^{(7)}\}\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{L}^{(9)},(v_{1},v_{2})\in A_{R}^{(8)},v_{2}\in A_{L}^{(7)}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{L}^{(9)},v_{1}\in A_{R}^{(1)}\}\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{1},v_{0})\in A_{R}^{(3)},v_{1}\in A_{L}^{(7)}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{R}^{(9)},(v_{1},v_{2})\in A_{L}^{(8)},v_{2}\in A_{R}^{(7)}\}\cup\{v_{0}~|~\exists(v_{0},v_{1})\in A_{R}^{(9)},v_{1}\in A_{L}^{(1)}\},

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{(j)}

\displaystyle=\emptyset,~~6\leq j\leq 9.

Then, we have the following lemmas.

For

, we have

\displaystyle A^{(j)}

\displaystyle=A_{L}^{(j)}\cup A_{R}^{(j)}\cup\bar{A}^{(j)}-\{v^{\prime\prime}\},

(4)

Lemma 3.3

CL_{f}\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)=\bigcup\left(CL_{g_{L}}({\big(A_{L}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9})\cap CL_{g_{R}}({\big(A_{R}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9})\right),

(6)

and
{\big(A_{R}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}

Moreover, if
CL_{g_{L}}\left({\big(A_{L}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)\cap CL_{g_{R}}\left({\big(A_{R}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)\neq\emptyset

and

(i)

(ii)

Let

g_{R}=f_{R}

, Eq. (
6

(c_{1},c_{2})\in CL_{g_{L}}({\big(A_{L}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9})\cap CL_{g_{R}}({\big(A_{R}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9})

{\big(A_{L}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\in\mathcal{S}(X_{L})
and
{\big(A_{R}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\in\mathcal{S}(X_{R})

(c_{1},c_{2})\in CL_{f_{L}}({\big(A_{L}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9})
and
(c_{1},c_{2})\in CL_{f_{R}}({\big(A_{R}^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9})
.

) hold. Since

, (

(

The proofs of (

Proof of Theorem
3.1

In order to prove Theorem
3.1
, we describe our algorithm.
For a given graph
G
and a number of colors
c
, we first calculate the smooth tree-decomposition of
G
.
Then, we calculate every active color class functions on each node
X_{i}
in the following way.

1.

X_{i}
is a leaf node. Enumerate all active
CL_{f}
of some
f
on
X_{i}
.
There are at most
c^{{(k+1)}^{2}\Delta^{6}}
color class functions.

2.

X_{i}
is an internal node.
For each active color class functions
CL_{f_{L}}
and
CL_{f_{R}}
on
X_{L}
and
X_{R}
respectively,
we calculate all active color class function
CL_{f}
of
f
on
X_{i}
by Lemma
3.3
.
There are at most
c^{2{(k+1)}^{2}\Delta^{6}}
pair of active
CL_{f_{L}},CL_{f_{R}}
and verify Lemma
3.3
for each color class pair takes
O(1)
time.

Suppose
X_{root}
is the root node in the smooth tree-decomposition.
Finally, there is an active color class function
CL_{f_{r}}
of
X_{root}
if and only if
G
have a valid star edge coloring within
c
colors.
Since there are at most
O(n)
nodes in the smooth tree-decomposition, the whole algorithm takes
O(nc^{2{(k+1)}^{2}\Delta^{6}})
time.

From the above Lemmas, we can easily prove that there is a valid partial of
X_{i}
if and only if the corresponding color class function
CL
can be computed by the above algorithm.
We did not describe how to find a star edge coloring using the specified number of colors.
But this can be done easily, by doing slight modifications in the algorithm (using extra bookkeeping to store coloring information). We skip the details.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

It is worth to mention that the algorithm can run parallelly in
O(c^{2{(k+1)}^{2}\Delta^{6}}\log n)
time on
O(n)
terminals.

4
Discussion

In our algorithm,
\Delta
must be considered a constant because we need to count the number of color class functions.
To remove the restriction of
\Delta
, we define color class count function
CF\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)=\left|CL\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)\right|
.
Since
CL
is a partition of
C^{2}
, we have:

\sum_{{\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\in\mathcal{S}(X_{i})}CF\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)=c^{2}.

The number of color class count functions is at most
c^{2^{3k^{2}+6k}}
.
If the following Conjecture
4.1
is proved to be true, we can store
CF
for each
X_{i}
rather than
CL
, and design a polynomial time algorithm.

Conjecture 4.1

If
f,g
are two partial coloring of
G_{i}^{\prime}
with the same color class count function, i.e.
CF_{f}\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)=CF_{g}\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)
for all
{\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\in\mathcal{S}(X_{i})
,
then there exists a permutation
\pi
of colors such that
CL_{\pi\circ f}\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)=CL_{g}\left({\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\right)
, for all
{\big(A^{(j)}\big)}_{1\leq j\leq 9}\in\mathcal{S}(X_{i})
.

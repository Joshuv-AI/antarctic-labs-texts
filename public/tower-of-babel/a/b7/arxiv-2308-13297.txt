Uniform distribution via lattices: from point sets to sequences
Damir Ferizović

Abstract
In this work we construct many sequences S = S b , d □ S=S^{\Box}_{b,d} and S = S b , d ⊞ S=S^{\boxplus}_{b,d} in the d d –dimensional unit hypercube, which for d = 1 d=1 are (generalized) van der Corput sequences or Niederreiter’s ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -sequences in base b b , respectively. Further, we introduce the notion of f f -subadditivity and use it to define discrepancy functions which subsume the notion of L p L^{p} -discrepancy, Wasserstein p p -distance, and many more methods to compare empirical measures to an underlying base measure. We will relate bounds for a given discrepancy function 𝒟 \mathscr{D} of the multiset of projected lattice sets (treated as empirical measures), P ⁡ ( b − m ​ ℤ d CLOSE P(b^{-m}\mathbb{Z}^{d} ), to bounds of 𝒟 ⁡ ( E Z N ) \mathscr{D}(E_{Z_{N}}) , i.e. the initial segments of the sequence Z = P ⁡ ( S ) Z=P(S) for any N ∈ ℕ N\in\mathbb{N} . We show that this relation holds in any dimension d d and for any map P P defined on a hypercube, for which bounds on 𝒟 ⁡ ( E P ⁡ ( b − m ​ ℤ d + v ) ) \mathscr{D}(E_{P(b^{-m}\mathbb{Z}^{d}+v)}) can be obtained. We apply this theorem in d = 1 d=1 to obtain bounds for the L p L^{p} –discrepancy of van der Corput and Niederreiter (0,1) sequences in terms of digit sums for all 0 < p ≤ ∞ 0<p\leq\infty . In d = 2 d=2 an application of our construction yields many sequences on the two-sphere, such that the initial segments Z N Z_{N} have small L ∞ L^{\infty} –discrepancy.

Simplified Main Result.
Let

Remark
.

It is in general very hard to bound the discrepancy of every initial segment of a sequence because for instance such point sets do not satisfy optimality conditions which could be used in a proof.
Our theorem thus provides a powerful and simple tool to give such bounds via discrepancies of point sets with a well understood structure, and in order to apply this theorem all we really need are bounds on these

We will apply this theorem for

The ”full” main result is much heavier on notation and the reason for it is to implicitly address the following three issues.

1.

Real world applications deal with very large ordered sets instead of sequences and our theorem holds with simpler requirements in this finite case, where in the definition of

instead of a supremum only the maximum over a finite set needs to be computed, i.e. for a given

3
Applications in Uniform Distribution Theory

Uniform distribution refers to a special discretization of a compact set by a collection of finite points. Some of the most common ways to quantify this property is in terms of the
L^{p}
–discrepancy, which we introduce below. The start of the investigation in such matters on the unit hypercube is attributed to Weyl
[
45
]
(see
[
26
]
for a historic review).

Definition 4
.

Let
\chi_{A}
be the indicator function of
A\subset\mathbb{M}
, let
(\mathbb{M},d_{\mathbb{M}},\mu)
be a metric measure space with
r_{\mathbb{M}}
the diameter of
\mathbb{M}
,
r_{\mathbb{M}}\leq\infty
, and let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with Borel sigma algebra
\Sigma(\mathbb{M})
. A ball with center
w\in\mathbb{M}
and radius
t\in(0,r_{\mathbb{M}})
is the set

B(w,t)=\big\{x\in\mathbb{M}\ |\ d_{\mathbb{M}}(w,x)\leq t\big\}.

The
L^{p}
-discrepancy

\sqrt[p]{\mathcal{D}_{p}(Z_{N})}
of a multiset
Z_{N}
of
\mathbb{M}
,
p\in(0,\infty)
, is defined via

\mathcal{D}_{p}(Z_{N})=\frac{1}{N^{p}}\int_{0}^{r_{\mathbb{M}}}\int_{\mathbb{M}}|D_{B(w,t)}(Z_{N})|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(w)\mathrm{d}t,

where, with
Z_{N}=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}\}
and empirical measure
E_{Z_{N}}
, we use the abbreviation

D_{\star}(Z_{N})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{\star}(z_{j})-N\mu(\star)=E_{Z_{N}}(\star)-E_{Z_{N}}(\mathbb{M})\mu(\star).

Furthermore, we define for a collection of
\mu
–measurable test-sets
T\subset\Sigma(\mathbb{M})

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(Z_{N})=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(Z_{N},T)=\frac{1}{N}\sup_{A\in T}\big|D_{A}(Z_{N})\big|.

Often,
T=\{B(w,t)\ |\ w\in\mathbb{M},\ t<r_{\mathbb{M}}\}
and one speaks of the
L^{\infty}
–discrepancy in this case; it is also referred to as spherical cap discrepancy if
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Remark
.

Other versions of
L^{p}
-discrepancy exist where test sets might not be balls or certain weights are introduced, see for instance Leobacher and Pillichshammer
[
31
]
, Sloan and Woźniakowski
[
39
]
or Stolarsky
[
41
]
.

On
[0,1)
we let
\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\mathrm{d}x
, while on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
\mu
is the normalized surface measure.
The
L^{p}
-discrepancy of the unit
d
-dimensional hypercube, in contrast, only averages over boxes with a corner in the origin, and all these different notions give rise to an admissible function-pair. For instance, we have
N^{p}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{p}(Z_{N})=\mathscr{D}\big(E_{Z_{N}}\big)
with

\mathscr{D}(E_{Z_{N}})=\int_{0}^{r_{\mathbb{M}}}\int_{\mathbb{M}}\big|\big(E_{Z_{N}}-E_{Z_{N}}(\mathbb{M})\mu\big)\big(B(w,t)\big)\big|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(w)\ \mathrm{d}t,

where
f(n)=n^{p-1}
for
p\geq 1
and
f\equiv 1
for
0<p<1
. To get the mentioned
L^{p}
-discrepancy on
\mathbb{M}=[0,1)^{d}
, one uses integrals over side lengths of axis-parallel boxes (not metric balls). Furthermore, if

\mathscr{D}(E_{Z_{N}})=\sup_{A\in T}\big|\big(E_{Z_{N}}-E_{Z_{N}}(\mathbb{M})\mu\big)(A)\big|\hskip 8.5359pt\mbox{then}\hskip 8.5359ptN\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(Z_{N})=\mathscr{D}\big(E_{Z_{N}}\big).

Corollary 2
.

The
L^{\infty}
–discrepancy of
\big(S^{\Box}_{b,d}\big)_{N}
is
O\big(N^{(d-1)/d}\big)
for
d>1
.

Proof.

We use Theorem
1
with
P=\mathrm{id}
,
\mathbb{M}=[0,1)^{d}
and
N\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(Z_{N})=\mathscr{D}\big(E_{Z_{N}}\big)
with test-sets the axis parallel boxes with one corner at the origin. Let
A
be an axis-parallel box with side lengths 1 in
d-1

\epsilon
in the remaining axis direction, i.e. volume
(A)=\mu(A)=\epsilon
, then it follows by Equation (
6
) that
S^{\Box}_{N}=b^{-m}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap[0,1)^{d}
(with
N=b^{md}
) satisfies
(\mu(A)=\epsilon\rightarrow 0
)

b^{(d-1)m}\leq\mathscr{D}(E_{S^{\Box}_{N}})\leq b^{(d-1)m}

since any
(d-1)
-dimensional face of
[0,1)^{d}
that contains the origin has
b^{(d-1)m}
-many points of the lattice set
b^{-m}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap[0,1)^{d}
which gives the lower bound, and the upper bound follows after some thought. A similar argument applies to shifted lattice sets and implies that

and hence

\mathscr{D}(E_{S^{\Box}_{N}})\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\epsilon_{j}b^{j(d-1)}\leq C_{b,d}b^{n(d-1)}=C_{b,d}N^{\log(b)(d-1)/\log(b^{d})}

Remark
.

This corollary shows that our construction is far from optimal in the unit hypercube for

Probabilistic algorithms can construct such

\displaystyle=12\big(|A|+|B|\big)\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(G(A\cup B))

\displaystyle=\sup_{F\in T}\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{12(|A|+|B|)}\chi_{F}(x_{j})-12\big(|A|+|B|\big)\mu(F)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 12|A|\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(G(A))+12|B|\cdot\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(G(B)).

Let

, then Theorem C states that, for

,

\sup_{(L_{m},v)\in\Omega_{m}}\mathscr{D}\big(E_{T_{v}(L_{m})}\big)=h_{m}^{\infty}\ \leq\ 2^{m}\big(5+\sqrt{2}\big)4+1000.

Let

8
Extensions to Self-Similar Sets

The underlying idea for our construction of the sequences
S^{\Box}
and
S^{\boxplus}
can be extended to more general sets than hyper-cubes. Indeed, after the first version of this text was uploaded to arXiv, we found the work by Basu & Owen
[
4
]
who defined a sequence of points in a triangle. Later still we found the work of Infusino and Volčič
[
29
]
who gave the same method based on Hutchinson’s results as we do here (that’s why we recognized our construction in theirs in the first place), but our algorithm is slightly more general.

The next definition is a combination of notions as introduced in Hutchinson
[
28
]
, where we added the restriction that
\mathscr{H}_{n}(K)<\infty
which follows from the results anyway.

Definition 7
.

Let
\mathscr{I}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
be a set of
contraction maps
on a complete metric space
(X,\delta)
, i.e.
A_{j}:X\rightarrow X
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
\lambda_{j}<1
.

\mathscr{I}
is said to satisfy the
open set condition
if there is a non-empty, open set
U\subset X
with

\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}(U)\subset U,\hskip 8.5359pt\mbox{and}\hskip 8.5359ptA_{j}(U)\cap A_{s}(U)=\emptyset,\hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{if }j\neq s.

A non-empty set
K\subset X
is called
invariant
with respect to
\mathscr{I}
if

K=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}(K).

We call
K

self-similar
wrt.
\mathscr{I}
if
0<\mathscr{H}_{n}(K)<\infty
for some
n>0
, where
\mathscr{H}_{n}
denotes the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and for
j\neq s
we have

\mathscr{H}_{n}\big[A_{j}(K)\cap A_{s}(K)\big]=0.

Theorem D
(Hutchinson
[
28
, p.713]
) Let
\mathscr{I}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
be a set of contraction maps on a complete metric space
(X,\delta)
, then there exists a
unique, closed, bounded and compact set

K_{\mathscr{I}}\subset X
which is invariant with respect to
\mathscr{I}
.

Theorem E
(Hutchinson
[
28
, p.714]
) Let
(X,\delta)=(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|)
and
\mathscr{I}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
be a set of maps satisfying
A_{j}=\sigma_{j}\circ\tau_{j}\circ O_{j}
where
\sigma_{j}(x)=r_{j}\cdot x
for
r_{j}\in[0,1)
,
\tau_{j}(x)=x-b_{j}
for some
b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
O_{j}
is an orthonormal map (see
[
28
, p.717]
). Let
\mathscr{I}
satisfy the open set condition. Then from Theorem D we obtain the set
K_{\mathscr{I}}
which is now guaranteed to be self-similar with Hausdorff dimension given by the unique number
n
, such that

\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{j}^{n}=1.

Definition 8
.

Let
\mathscr{I}_{n}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
be as in Theorem E with
r_{j}=k^{-1/n}
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the unique, compact, self-similar set, invariant with respect to
\mathscr{I}_{n}
and necessarily of Hausdorff dimension
n
.

Remark
.

It follows easily that
\mathscr{H}_{n}\big(A_{j}(\Omega)\big)=\tfrac{1}{k}\mathscr{H}_{n}(\Omega).
Rectangles and triangles embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are such
\Omega
, so are
k
-reptiles (see Matoušek & Safernová
[
34
]
).

Figure
7
illustrates two examples for
\Omega
being the triangle
\triangle
with corners at
v_{1}=(0,0)^{t},v_{2}=(1,0)^{t},v_{3}=(0,1)^{t}
, where
A_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{2}M_{j}(x)+\frac{1}{2}v_{j}
with
M_{t}=\mathds{1}
(= identity matrix) for
t<4
and
M_{4}=-\mathds{1}
,
v_{4}=(1,1)^{t}
. (For every other triangle
T
we have
T=M\triangle+v
for some invertible matrix
M
and vector
v
.)

Algorithm
.

Let
\Omega
and
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
be as in Definition
8
. We define a sequence
S=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots)
in
\Omega
recursively as follows: Choose
s_{0}\in\Omega
arbitrarily and fix a sequence
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\ldots)
of permutations of
k
elements. Define for
1\leq m\leq k

V_{m}=A_{\pi_{1}(m)}(\Omega).

Choose
q_{m}\in V_{m}
arbitrarily. Choose the smallest
p
such that
s_{0}\in V_{p}
for some
p\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Define
s_{j}=q_{j}
for
j<p
and
s_{j-1}=q_{j}
for
j>p
and
0<j\leq k
.

Suppose elements
\{s_{0},\ldots,s_{k^{t}-1}\}
and subsets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k^{t}}
have been defined. Then, for
1\leq m\leq k^{t}
and
1\leq r\leq k
choose arbitrary
q^{r}_{m}
such that

q^{r}_{m}\in V_{(r-1)\cdot k^{t}+m}:=A_{\pi_{t+1}(r)}(V_{m}).

There are
1\leq p_{1}<\ldots<p_{k^{t}}\leq k^{t+1}
with
s_{j}\in V_{p_{j+1}}
. Consider the vector:

\big(q^{1}_{1},q^{2}_{1},\ldots,q^{k}_{1},q^{1}_{2},q^{2}_{2},\ldots,q^{k}_{2},q^{1}_{3},q^{2}_{3},\ldots,q^{k}_{3},\ldots,q^{1}_{k^{t}},q^{2}_{k^{t}},\ldots,q^{k}_{k^{t}}\big)

from which we remove elements
q^{r}_{u}
with
[(r-1)\cdot k^{t}+u]\in\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k^{t}}\}
to obtain

(s_{k^{t}},\ldots,s_{k^{t+1}-1});

i.e. the next elements of
S
. (Thus
s_{k^{t}}=q^{1}_{1}
, unless
1=p_{j}
for some
j
, etc.)

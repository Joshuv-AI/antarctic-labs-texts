Minimal Surface Equation and Bernstein Property on 𝖱𝖢𝖣 \mathsf{RCD} spaces
Alessandro Cucinotta

Abstract
We show that if ( 𝖷 , 𝖽 , 𝔪 ) (\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m}) is an 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( K , N ) \mathsf{RCD}(K,N) space and u ∈ 𝖶 l ​ o ​ c 1 , 1 ​ ( 𝖷 ) u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X}) is a solution of the minimal surface equation, then u u is harmonic on its graph (which has a natural metric measure space structure). If K = 0 K=0 this allows to obtain an Harnack inequality for u u , which in turn implies the Bernstein property, meaning that any positive solution to the minimal surface equation must be constant. As an application, we obtain oscillation estimates and a Bernstein Theorem for minimal graphs in products 𝖬 × ℝ \mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R} , where 𝖬 \mathsf{M} is a smooth manifold (possibly weighted and with boundary) with non-negative Ricci curvature.

Introduction

In
[
12
]
Bombieri, De Giorgi and Miranda showed that the only entire positive solutions of the minimal surface equation in Euclidean space are the constant functions. If we replace the Euclidean space with a Riemannian manifold the validity of the aforementioned result depends on the geometry of the manifold. For this reason the following definition was introduced in
[
19
]
.

Definition
.

We say that a Riemannian manifold
(\mathsf{M},g)
has the Bernstein Property if the only entire positive solutions of the minimal surface equation are the constant functions.

For example, positive non constant solutions of the minimal surface equation in the hyperbolic plane were constructed in
[
44
]
, while the Bernstein Property holds for manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature and a lower sectional curvature bound thanks to
[
46
]
. Recently this was improved in
[
19
]
to manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature and no sectional curvature constraint (see also
[
20
]
for an even stronger result in the same fashion), while Ding proved in
[
23
]
that the Bernstein Property holds in manifolds that are doubling and support a Poincaré inequality.

The fact that manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature have the Bernstein Property and the recent generalization of some properties of minimal surfaces to
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces (i.e. metric measure spaces with a notion of lower bound on the Ricci curvature) suggest that an analogue of the Bernstein Property might hold in this setting as well.
We recall that an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space is a metric measure space where
K\in\mathbb{R}
plays the role of a lower bound on the Ricci curvature, while
N\in[1,+\infty)
plays the role of an upper bound on the dimension. This class includes measured Gromov-Hausdorff limits of smooth manifolds of fixed dimension with uniform Ricci curvature lower bounds and finite dimensional Alexandrov spaces with sectional curvature bounded from below.
With this in mind we can state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. If
u
is positive, then it is constant.

The previous theorem, as anticipated, is part of a wider class of recent results that aim at generalizing to the non smooth setting properties of minimal surfaces (
[
10
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
42
]
, etc.).
Moreover, specializing Theorem
1
to the smooth category, we obtain that the Bernstein Property holds for certain weighted manifolds with boundary. This is the content of Theorem
2
. Given a manifold
(\mathsf{M},g)
we denote by
\mathsf{Vol}_{g}
its volume measure and by
\mathsf{d}_{g}
its distance. If
V:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, we say that the metric measure space
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}\mathsf{Vol}_{g})
is a weighted manifold. Given
\Omega\subset\mathsf{M}
, we say that a function
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a solution of the weighted minimal surface equation on
\Omega\setminus\partial\mathsf{M}
if

\mathsf{div}\Big(\frac{e^{-V}\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\Big)=0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{on }\Omega\setminus\partial\mathsf{M}.

We say that the boundary of a manifold with boundary is convex if its second fundamental form w.r.t. the inward pointing unit normal is positive.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}\mathsf{Vol}_{g})
be a weighted manifold with convex boundary such that there exists
N>n
satisfying

\mathsf{Ric}_{\mathsf{M}}+\mathsf{Hess}_{V}-\frac{\nabla V\otimes\nabla V}{N-n}\geq 0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ on }\mathsf{M}\setminus\partial\mathsf{M}.

(1)

If
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathsf{M})
is a positive solution of the weighted minimal surface equation on
\mathsf{M}\setminus\partial\mathsf{M}
whose gradient vanishes on
\partial\mathsf{M}

u
is constant.

The previous result is new (to the best of our knowledge) both in the boundaryless weighted setting and in the framework of unweighted manifolds with boundary. Theorem
2

1

A second consequence of Theorem
1

A similar result for solutions to elliptic problems in Euclidean spaces was obtained in

\mathsf{Osc}_{x,r}(f):=\sup\{|f(y)-f(x)|:y\in B_{r}(x)\},

We now turn our attention to the proof of Theorem
1
. To this aim let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
be a solution of the minimal surface equation.
The proof that we give follows the one in
[
23
]
to prove the analogous result for manifolds supporting a doubling volume measure and a Poincaré inequality. In that work, Ding shows first that Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities for the height function hold on the graph of
u
. Then, he uses a Moser-type iteration argument, which relies on the fact that
u
is harmonic on its graph, to obtain the Harnack inequality for
u
and the Bernstein Property.

The first obstacle for adapting the previously outlined strategy is that the graph of
u
in our setting has very little structure, and it is not clear what it means for
u
to be harmonic on its graph. A key result in order to give the graph of
u
a metric measure space structure is Theorem
4
. We denote by
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
the epigraph of
u
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
. The following conditions are equivalent.

1.

\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is locally perimeter minimizing in
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.

2.

\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is perimeter minimizing in
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.

3.

u
solves the minimal surface equation on
\mathsf{X}
.

Remark
.

Thanks to Theorem
4
, if we assume that the spaces in question satisfy appropriate parabolicity constraints, then Theorems
1
,
2
and
3
follow from the fact that parabolic
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
spaces have the Half Space Property (see
[
22
]
). We stress that the proofs of these theorems in our setting (i.e. without any parabolicity assumption) use completely different techniques from the ones of
[
22
]
.

Thanks to the previous theorem, if
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
is a solution of the minimal surface equation, we can consider the closed representative of
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
(which exists since this is a perimeter minimizing set), and we can define a complete separable metric measure space which plays the role of the graph of
u
. More precisely, we consider
(\mathsf{G}(u),\mathsf{d}_{g},\mathfrak{m}_{g})
, where
\mathsf{G}(u):=\partial\mathsf{Epi}(u)
,
\mathsf{d}_{g}
is the restriction of the product distance in
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
to
\mathsf{G}(u)
and
\mathfrak{m}_{g}
is the restriction of the perimeter measure of
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
to
\mathsf{G}(u)
. We also denote by
u_{g}:\mathsf{G}(u)\to\mathbb{R}
the height function on
\mathsf{G}(u)
.
With this definition of graph space, the Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities for
u_{g}
on
\mathsf{G}(u)
follow mimicking the proofs in
[
23
]
(with the due modifications), so that the problem reduces to proving that
u_{g}
is harmonic on
\mathsf{G}(u)
in a generalized sense that allows to repeat the iteration argument in
[
23
]
.

The key results in this sense are given by Theorems
5
and
6
. Given
f:\mathsf{G}(u)\to\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathsf{G}(u)
, we denote by
\mathsf{lip}_{g}(f)(x)
the local Lipschitz constant of
f
at
x
(see (
2
)). At every point of
\mathsf{G}(u)
where it makes sense, given a second function
g:\mathsf{G}(u)\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(f)\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(g):=\frac{1}{4}(\mathsf{lip}_{g}(f+g)^{2}-\mathsf{lip}_{g}(f-g)^{2}).

We denote by
\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))
and
\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\mathsf{G}(u))
respectively the set of Lipschitz continuous and compactly supported Lipschitz continuous functios on
\mathsf{G}(u)
.
Theorem
5
shows that the previously defined product, when restricted to Lipschitz functions, behaves according to the usual rules of products of gradients.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. The function
\cdot:\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))\times\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))\to\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\mathsf{G}(u))
given by

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\mapsto\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})

is symmetric, bilinear, it satisfies the chain rule and the Leibniz rule in both entries and
\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})\leq\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})
.

Theorem
6
is then the analogue in our setting of the fact that in the smooth category
u_{g}
would be harmonic on its graph.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation.
Let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\mathsf{G}(u))
, then
{\mathsf{lip}}_{g}(\phi)\cdot{\mathsf{lip}}_{g}(u_{g})\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{G}(u))
, and

\int_{\mathsf{G}(u)}\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi)\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(u_{g})\,d\mathfrak{m}_{g}=0.

Finally, we outline the main ideas in the proofs of Theorems
5
and
6
. We will assume for simplicity that
u
is locally Lipschitz on
\mathsf{X}
and we will denote by
\nabla
the relaxed gradient on
\mathsf{X}
(for Lipschitz functions this coincides with the local Lipschitz constant
\mathsf{lip}
thanks to
[
18
]
). Both theorems can be easily obtained if one can show that given
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{X})
and the projection on the graph
i_{g}:\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{G}(u)
, then the local Lipschitz constant of
\phi\circ i_{g}^{-1}
(i.e.
\phi
seen as a function on the graph
\mathsf{G}(u)
) satisfies

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi\circ i_{g}^{-1})^{2}\circ i_{g}=|\nabla\phi|^{2}-\frac{(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u)^{2}}{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e. on }\mathsf{X}.

To obtain such an identity (which is what we would get on a Riemannian manifold) we use a technical blow-up argument so that we reduce the problem to the Euclidean case.

To outline such argument we first need to recall a blow-up property of Lipschitz functions on metric measure spaces due to Cheeger (see
[
18
]
).
Let
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d}_{y},\mathfrak{m}_{y})
be a space with a locally doubling measure which supports a local Poincaré inequality (i.e. a
PI
space) such that for
\mathfrak{m}_{y}
-a.e.
y\in\mathsf{Y}
the tangent space of
\mathsf{Y}
at
y
is a Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(see Definition
2.10
). Given a point
y\in\mathsf{Y}
of the previous type and a sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
, passing to a subsequence, we denote by
\psi_{n}:\bar{B}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{n}(0)\to(Y,\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{y},y)
the Gromov Hausdorff maps realizing the blow-up (see Definition
4.1
).
Thanks to
[
18
]
we have that for every
\theta\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{Y})
, for
\mathfrak{m}_{y}
-a.e.
y\in\mathsf{Y}
there exists a linear function
\theta^{\infty}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
, called the blow-up of
\theta
at
y
, such that

\|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}(\theta\circ\psi_{n}-\theta(y))-\theta^{\infty}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(B^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{1}(0))}\to 0.

Moreover, we have that the relaxed gradient
\nabla_{y}\theta(y)
of
\theta
in
y
(which coincides with the local Lipschitz constant
\mathsf{lip}(\theta)(y)
) and the local Lipschitz constant
\mathsf{lip}(\theta^{\infty})(0)
of
\theta^{\infty}
in
0
coincide.

In particular, this blow-up property of Lipschitz functions holds for
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{G}(u)
as these are both
PI
spaces (since
u
is now assumed to be Lipschitz).
So we fix
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{X})
and a sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
. We pick a point
x\in\mathsf{X}
where the blow-up of
\mathsf{X}
is realized (modulo passing to a subsequence) by the
Gromov Hausdorff maps
\psi_{n}:\bar{B}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{n}(0)\to(\mathsf{X},\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d},x)
and the functions
u
and
\phi
admit blow-ups
u^{\infty}
and
\phi^{\infty}
respectively.
Let then
j:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathsf{Graph}(u^{\infty})
be the projection on the graph. It turns out that the maps

\psi^{\prime}_{n}:=i_{g}\circ\psi_{n}\circ j^{-1}:j(B^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{1}(0))\to(i_{g}(B^{\mathsf{X}}_{\epsilon_{n}}(x)),\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{g})

are Gromov Hausdorff maps realizing the blow-up of
\mathsf{G}(u)
at
(x,u(x))
. In particular this implies that the blow-up of
\phi\circ i_{g}^{-1}
on
\mathsf{G}(u)
is
\phi^{\infty}\circ j^{-1}
. From this and the fact that blow-ups of functions preserve the Lipschitz constant, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi\circ i_{g}^{-1})^{2}(i_{g}(x))=\mathsf{lip}(\phi^{\infty}\circ j^{-1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{lip}(\phi^{\infty})^{2}-\frac{(\mathsf{lip}(\phi^{\infty})\cdot\mathsf{lip}(u^{\infty}))^{2}}{1+\mathsf{lip}(u^{\infty})^{2}}=\Big(|\nabla\phi|^{2}-\frac{(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u)^{2}}{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}\Big)(x).

If
u
is not Lipschitz, the above strategy needs to be modified to ensure both existence of a blow-up of
u
at points of
\mathsf{X}
and existence of blow-ups of functions in
\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))
at points in
\mathsf{G}(u)
.
Note in addition that in both cases we need the Lipschitz constant of the blow-up to coincide with the local Lipschitz constant of the initial function.
These issues are addressed by using a geometric property of perimeter minimizers, i.e. the fact that these admit tangent balls to their boundary (see
[
42
]
for the proof of this property in the non-collapsed case).

Finally, let us mention that Theorem
1
might hold in the more general framework of doubling spaces supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality (as this is the case in the Riemannian setting, see
[
23
]
). Our strategy though, fails in this more general context. The main issues are the potential lack of regularity of tangent spaces to the ambient space and to the perimeter minimizing set, the non-linearity of the Laplacian and the possible failure of Theorem
4
(due to the absence of a formula for the area of a graph in this more general setting).

Preliminaries

Throughout the note will work on metric measure spaces
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, where
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d})
is a separable complete length space where balls are precompact and
\mathfrak{m}

\mathsf{X}

\mathsf{X}

\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
we denote by
\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
,
\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\Omega)
and
\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
respectively Lipschitz functions, locally Lipschitz and Lipschitz functions with compact support in
\Omega

and

we define

\mathsf{lip}(f)(x):=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathsf{L}(f):=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}.

(2)

Given a closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we say that a rectifiable curve
\gamma:I\to\mathsf{X}
is a geodesic if its length coincides with the distance between its endpoints. Unless otherwise specified we assume that geodesics have constant unit speed. Throughout the note
\mathsf{d}_{e}

In many proofs we will use the notation

2.1
Sobolev spaces and Gromov Hausdorff convergence

We now recall some basic notions about Sobolev spaces in the setting of metric measure spaces, the main references being
[
18
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]

[

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space,
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
an open set and let
p>1
. A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{p}(\Omega)
is said to be in the Sobolev space
\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
if there exists a sequence of locally Lipschitz functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\Omega)
converging to
f
in
\mathsf{L}^{p}(\Omega)
such that

\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{lip}(f_{i})^{p}\,d\mathfrak{m}<+\infty.

A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{p}_{loc}(\Omega)
is said to be in the Sobolev space
\mathsf{W}^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega)
if for every
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
we have
f\eta\in\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

For any
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
one can define an object
|\nabla f|
(a priori depending on
p
, but independent of the exponent in the spaces that we will work on) such that for every open set
A\subset\Omega
we have

\int_{A}|\nabla f|^{p}\,d\mathfrak{m}=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})^{p}\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{p}(A)\cap\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(A),\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{p}(A)}\to 0\Big\}.

The quantity in the previous expression will be called
p
-Cheeger energy and denoted by
\mathsf{Ch}_{p}(f)
while
|\nabla f|
will be called relaxed gradient. Later we will often write
\mathsf{Ch}
in place of
\mathsf{Ch}_{2}
for simplicity of notation.
We define
\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\mathsf{Ch}_{p}(f)
. One can check that with this norm the space
\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is Banach.
We now introduce functions of bounded variation following
[
40
]
(see also
[
2
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
an open set. A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
is said to be of bounded variation if there exists a sequence of of locally Lipschitz functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\Omega)
converging to
f
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{lip}(f_{i})\,d\mathfrak{m}<+\infty.

The space of such functions is denoted
\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
is said to be in
\mathsf{BV}_{loc}(\Omega)
if for every
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
we have
f\eta\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
.

For any
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and any open set
A\subset\Omega
we define

|Df|(A)=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{1}(A)\cap\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(A),\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(A)}\to 0\Big\}.

One can check that the quantity in the previous expression is the restriction to the open subsets of
\Omega
of a finite measure. We define
\|f\|_{\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+|Df|(\Omega)
. One can check that with this norm the space
\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
is Banach. A function
f
belongs to
\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
if
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and
|Df|\ll\mathfrak{m}
. In this case we denote by
|\nabla f|
the density of
|Df|
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
.

Let

Definition 2.4

A metric measure space

2.2
General properties of RCD(K,N) spaces

We now recall some properties of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces, i.e. infinitesimally Hilbertian metric measure spaces with Ricci curvature bounded from below by
K\in\mathbb{R}
and dimension bounded from above by
N\in[1,+\infty)
in synthetic sense.

The Riemannian Curvature Dimension condition
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
was introduced in
[
6
]
(see
also
[
28
,
4
]
) coupling the Curvature Dimension condition
\mathsf{CD}(K,\infty)
, previously pioneered
in
[
47
,
48
]
and independently in
[
39
]
, with the infinitesimal Hilbertianity assumption. The finite dimensional counterpart
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
is then obtained coupling the finite dimensional Curvature Dimension condition
\mathsf{CD}(K,N)
with the infinitesimal Hilbertianity assumption and was proposed in
[
28
]
. For a complete introduction to the topic we refer to the survey
[
1
]
and the references therein.
Let us mention that in the literature one can find also the (a priori weaker)
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}(K,N)

[
24
,
7
]
, that
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}(K,N)
is equivalent to the dimensional Bochner inequality. Moreover,
[
17
]
(see also
[
38
]
) proved that
\mathsf{RCD}^{*}(K,N)
and
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
coincide.
We now recall the properties that we will use later on in the note.

The
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
condition implies that the measure is locally doubling (see
[
47
]
) and the validity of a Poincaré inequality (see
[
45
]
). In particular, if
f
is a locally Lipschitz function on a
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, its relaxed gradient coincides with its local Lipschitz constant
\mathsf{lip}(f)
(see
[
35
, Theorem
12.5.1
]
after
[
18
]
).

The next theorem follows from
[
41
,
15
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. There exists
k\in\mathbb{N}\cap[1,N]
, called essential dimension of
\mathsf{X}
, such that for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in\mathsf{X}
we have
\mathsf{Tan}_{x}(\mathsf{X})=\{(\mathbb{R}^{k},\mathsf{d}_{e})\}
. Any such point will be called a regular point for

.

We now recall some properties of the heat flow in the
\mathsf{RCD}
setting, referring to
[
4
,
6
]
for the proofs of these results.
Given an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, the heat flow
P_{t}:\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathsf{X})
is the
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathsf{X})
-gradient flow of the Cheeger energy
\mathsf{Ch}
.
It turns out that one can obtain a stochastically complete heat kernel
p_{t}:\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}\to[0,+\infty)
, so that
the definition of

can be then extended to
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}

The heat flow has good approximation properties, in particular if

, then
P_{t}(f)\to f
in
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\mathsf{X})
; while if
f\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\mathsf{X})
, then
P_{t}f\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{X})
for every

.

The next proposition follows combining the contractivity estimates for the heat flow of
[
29
]
with a standard lower semicontinuity argument.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let

|Df|(\partial\Omega)=0

\lim_{t\to 0}|DP_{t}(f)|(\Omega)=|Df|(\Omega).

2.3
Sets of finite perimeter and minimal sets

Definition 2.13
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

. For every Borel subset

. We denote

\mathsf{Per}(E,\cdot)

The next result is the relative isoperimetric inequality on
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)

such that if

has finite perimeter,

and

\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))^{\frac{1}{N}}\min\{\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{r}(x)),\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)\setminus E)\}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\leq CrP(E,B_{r}(x)).

We now turn our attention to minimal sets.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set.
Let
E\subset\Omega

\Omega

x\in\Omega
,
r>0
and

P(E,B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\leq P(F,B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)
. If we say that
E
is perimeter minimizing we implicitly mean
that
\Omega=\mathsf{X}
.

Similarly,
E
is locally perimeter minimizing in
\Omega
if for every
x\in\Omega
there exists
r>0
such that for every
F\subset\Omega
such that
F\Delta E\subset\subset B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega
we have
P(E,B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\leq P(F,B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)
.
If we say that
E
is locally perimeter minimizing we implicitly mean that
\Omega=\mathsf{X}
.

The next theorem comes from
[
36
, Theorem
4.2
and Lemma

]

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. There exist
C,\gamma_{0}>0
depending only on
K
and
N
such that the following hold. If
E\subset\mathsf{X}
is a set minimizing the perimeter in an open set

, then, up to modifying
E
on an
\mathfrak{m}
-negligible set, for any

and
0<r<1
such that

\frac{\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}>\gamma_{0},\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)\setminus E)}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}>\gamma_{0}

and

\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{Cr}\leq P(E,B_{r}(x))\leq\frac{C\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{r}.

From the previous result one deduces that locally perimeter minimizing sets admit both a closed and an open representative, and these have the same boundary which in addition is
\mathfrak{m}
-negligible. Whenever we consider the boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set, we will implicitly be referring to the boundary of its closed (or open) representative. The next corollary follows easily from Theorem

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

depending only on

,

and

Let

be the closed representative of a set minimizing the perimeter in

. For every

and

such that

\frac{\mathfrak{m}(E\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}\geq\gamma.

). Given

\mathsf{Epi}(f):=\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}:t>f(x)\}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(f):=\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}:t<f(x)\}.

(3)

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space.
Let
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
. For every Borel set
B\subset\mathsf{X}
we have

P(\mathsf{Epi}(f),B\times\mathbb{R})=\int_{B}\sqrt{1+|\nabla f|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Given
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
we will use the notation
W_{f}:=\sqrt{1+|\nabla f|^{2}}
.

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. Let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be continuous at
x_{0}
and such that
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is locally perimeter minimizing in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, then there exists an open set
A\subset\Omega
containing
x_{0}
such that
for every
\phi\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(A)\cap\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(A)
compactly supported in
A
we have

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=0.

(4)

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is locally perimeter minimizing in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
there exists a cilynder
B_{\delta}(x_{0})\times(u(x_{0})-\epsilon,u(x_{0})+\epsilon)\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
where
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
minimizes the perimeter. Moreover, modulo decreasing
\delta
, we may assume that
|u-u(x_{0})|<\epsilon/2
in
B_{\delta}(x_{0})
by the continuity hypothesis on
u
. We then set
A:=B_{\delta}(x_{0})
. Note that if
\phi
is as in the statement, then the graph of the function
u+t\phi
restricted to
A
, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
small enough, will be contained in the cylinder
B_{\delta}(x_{0})\times(u(x_{0})-\epsilon,u(x_{0})+\epsilon)
.
This implies that the function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

f(t):=P(\mathsf{Epi}(u+t\phi),A\times\mathbb{R})

has a minimum in zero.
By Proposition
2.18
we can write
f
as

f(t)=\int_{A}\sqrt{1+|\nabla(u+t\phi)|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m},

so that by standard arguments involving Dominated Convergence Theorem (see
[
27
, Theorem 2.27]
) we have that
f
is smooth and that

f^{\prime}(0)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Since
f
is smooth and has a minimum in zero we deduce that
f^{\prime}(0)=0
as desired.
∎

Remark 2.20
.

The same argument of the previous proposition combined with a truncation argument shows that
if

is bounded and such that
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is perimeter minimizing in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, then for every compactly supported
\phi\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
we have

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=0.

In this section we sketch the main steps to prove Theorem

The proof of the next result mimics the one of

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space.
Let
E\subset\mathsf{X}
be a perimeter minimizing set and consider the distance function
\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
from
\bar{E}
. Then
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}
is a Radon measure in
\mathsf{X}
and we have
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}=\mathsf{Per}(E,\cdot)+\Delta\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.2pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.50375pt\vrule height=0.50375pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.03749pt}(\mathsf{X}\setminus\bar{E})
in distributional sense.

Proof.

We do the proof assuming that
K=0
, the general case requiring only a slight modification.
Thanks to
[
13
, Theorem
5.2
and Proposition
6.1
]
for
\lambda^{1}
-a.e.
r>0
and for every positive
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\mathsf{X})
, it holds

\int_{\{\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}>r\}}\phi\,d\Delta\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}+\int_{\{\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}>r\}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{\mathsf{X}}\phi\,d\mathsf{Per}(\{\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}>r\}).

(5)

Combining
[
42
, Lemma
2.41
]
and the Coarea Formula,
we obtain that there exists a Radon measure
\mu
which is the weak limit (in duality with compactly supported continuous functions) of the Radon measures
\mathsf{Per}(\{\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}>r_{i}\})
for some sequence
r_{i}\downarrow 0
.
We claim that
\mu\ll\mathsf{Per}(E,\cdot)
.
It is clear that
\mu
is supported on
\partial E
, so it is sufficient to prove that there exists a constant
c
such that for every
x\in\partial E
, considering a sequence
t_{k}
that goes to zero, we have

\limsup_{t_{k}\to 0}\frac{\mu(B_{t_{k}}(x))}{\mathsf{Per}(E,B_{t_{k}}(x))}\leq c.

So fix
t_{k}
and note that

\mu(B_{t_{k}}(x))\leq\liminf_{i\to+\infty}\mathsf{Per}(\{\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}>r_{i}\},B_{t_{k}}(x)).

Combining again
[
42
, Lemma
2.41
]
and the Coarea Formula, the r.h.s. is controlled by
CP(E,B_{2t_{k}}(x))
, which by Theorem
2.16
is in turn controlled by
C^{\prime}P(E,B_{t_{k}}(x))
. Hence, we get

\mu(B_{t_{k}}(x))\leq C^{\prime}P(E,B_{t_{k}}(x)),

as desired.

With this in mind we pass to the limit in (
5
) along the sequence
r_{i}
and obtain

\lim_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\{\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}>r_{i}\}}\phi\,d\Delta\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}+\int_{\mathsf{X}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{\mathsf{X}}\phi\,d\mu.

The first summand in the l.h.s. of the previous equation is a linear functional on the continuous compactly supported functions.
Moreover it has negative sign because of
[
31

5.2
]
(we recall that we are assuming

\nu
.
This implies, by the same equation, that
\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}
has measure valued Laplacian and that
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{\bar{E}}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.2pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.50375pt\vrule height=0.50375pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.03749pt}(\mathsf{X}\setminus\bar{E})=\nu

\lim_{t_{k}\to 0}\frac{\mu(B_{t_{k}}(x))}{\mathsf{Per}(E,B_{t_{k}}(x))}=1.

5.4
Step 2]

\Gamma_{E}:=\{y\in\partial E:\exists x\in\Gamma:\mathsf{d}(y,x)=h\}
. Then, setting
G:=\{x:\mathsf{d}_{E}(x)+\mathsf{d}_{\Gamma}(x)=h\}
, it holds
P(\{\mathsf{d}_{E}<h\},\Gamma)\leq P(E,\Gamma_{E})+\int_{G}t_{K,N}(\mathsf{d}_{E})\,d\mathfrak{m}
, where
t_{K,N}\in C^{\infty}([0,+\infty))
and
t_{K,N}(0)=0
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and set
\phi_{\epsilon}:=\epsilon^{-1}(h+\epsilon-\mathsf{d}_{E}-\mathsf{d}_{\Gamma})\vee 0
. For any
\delta\in(0,h)
set
g_{\delta}
to be the function such that
g_{\delta}(0)=g_{\delta}^{\prime}(0)
and
g_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}=\delta^{-1}(\chi_{[0,\delta]}-\chi_{[h-\delta,h]})
. By
[
31
, Theorem 5.2]
and the chain rule, it holds
\Delta g_{\delta}(\mathsf{d}_{E})\leq g^{\prime\prime}_{\delta}(\mathsf{d}_{E})+g_{\delta}^{\prime}(\mathsf{d}_{E})t_{K,N}(\mathsf{d}_{E})
.

Consider an open neighbourhood
F
of
G
. By
[
13
, Theorem
5.2
]
(taking into account the simplifications explained in
[
42
, lines after (6.15)]
), one deduces that, for
\epsilon>0
small enough,

\int_{F}g_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}(\mathsf{d}_{E})\phi_{\epsilon}\,d\mathfrak{m}\geq-\int_{F}g_{\delta}^{\prime}(\mathsf{d}_{E})t_{K,N}(\mathsf{d}_{E})\phi_{\epsilon}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

By definition of
g_{\delta}
, it holds

\delta^{-1}\int_{F}\chi_{[0,\delta]}(\mathsf{d}_{E})\phi_{\epsilon}\,d\mathfrak{m}\geq\delta^{-1}\int_{F}\chi_{[h-\delta,h]}(\mathsf{d}_{E})\phi_{\epsilon}\,d\mathfrak{m}-\int_{F}g_{\delta}^{\prime}(\mathsf{d}_{E})t_{K,N}(\mathsf{d}_{E})\phi_{\epsilon}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Passing to the limit as
\delta\to 0

2.23
, the lower semicontinuity of total variations and the fact that
g_{\delta}^{\prime}\to 1
as
\delta\to 0
, we deduce

\int_{F}\phi_{\epsilon}\,d\mathsf{Per}(E)\geq\int_{F}\phi_{\epsilon}\,d\mathsf{Per}(\{\mathsf{d}_{E}<h\})-\int_{F}t_{K,N}(\mathsf{d}_{E})\phi_{\epsilon}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Since
\phi_{\epsilon}\to\chi_{G}
as
\epsilon\to 0
, passing to the limit in the previous inequality we conclude.

Theorem 2.25
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let

be a set locally minimizing the perimeter in
\Omega
. For
\mathsf{Per}
-a.e.

there exists balls

and
B_{2}\subset\Omega\setminus E
such that
\partial B_{1}\cap\partial B_{2}=\{x\}
. These balls are called tangent balls to
E
at

.

Proof.

For simplicity we assume that
E\subset\mathsf{X}
is perimeter minimzing.
Let
x\in\partial E
. By
[
42
, Corollary 6.42]
(the proof works exactly in the same way in our setting), there exist
c(K,N),r_{0}(K,N)>0
such that for every
0<r<r_{0}
there exists

such that

P(B_{\rho}(x),\{0<\mathsf{d}_{E}<r\})\leq crP(E,B_{2}(x)),\hskip 9.24994ptP(\{0<\mathsf{d}_{E}<r\},B_{\rho}(x))=0.

(6)

Set now
\Gamma_{r}:=\{\mathsf{d}_{E}=r\}\cap B_{\rho}(x)
and let
\Gamma_{r,E}:=\{y\in\partial E:\exists z\in\Gamma_{r}:\mathsf{d}(y,z)=r\}
.
Since

is perimeter minimizing, it holds

\displaystyle P(E,B_{1/2}(x))

\displaystyle\leq P(E\cup(\{\mathsf{d}_{E}<r\}\cap B_{\rho}(x)),B_{1/2}(x)),

so that combining with (
6
) and Proposition

, it holds

\displaystyle P(E,B_{\rho}(x))

\displaystyle\leq P(\{\mathsf{d}_{E}<r\},B_{\rho}(x))+P(B_{\rho}(x),\{0<\mathsf{d}_{E}<r\})

\displaystyle\leq P(E,\Gamma_{r,E})+crP(E,B_{2}(x))+o(r).

Note that

In particular,
\mathsf{Per}(E)
-a.e.

admits a tangent ball from outside. By a similar argument,

-a.e.

3
Minimal surface equation and perimeter minimizers

We will denote by
\mathsf{d}_{\times}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
respectively the product distance and the product measure in
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.
We recall that given
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
and
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
we denote by
\mathsf{Epi}(f)
the set
\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}:t>f(x)\}
and by
\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(f)
the set
\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}:t<f(x)\}
.
The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem (which coincides with Theorem
4
in the Introduction when
\Omega=\mathsf{X}
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
. The following conditions are equivalent.

1.

\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is locally perimeter minimizing in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.

2.

\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is perimeter minimizing in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.

3.

u
solves the minimal surface equation on
\Omega
.

In Subsection
3.1
we show that
1
implies
3
, while in Subsection
3.2
we will show that
3
implies
2
.

3.1
Locally perimeter minimizing implies MSE

The proof of the next result is inspired from
[
32
, Theorem
14.10
]
. We recall that whenever we refer to a locally perimeter minimizing set we implicitly mean its open representative. Moreover
whenever we refer to the pointwise behavior of
u
we mean its precise representative defined by

u(x):=\limsup_{r\to 0}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}u\,d\mathfrak{m}.

(7)

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open. Let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be such that
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is locally perimeter minimizing in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, then
u
is continuous at its Lebesgue points.

Proof.

Let
x\in\Omega
be a Lebesgue point for
u
and suppose that
u(x)=0
. Modulo a vertical translation we always trace back to this case. Since
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
minimizes the perimeter locally, there exists an open set of the form
B_{\epsilon}(x)\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
where
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is perimeter minimizing. Now consider
r>0
such that
\epsilon>4r
and
y\in B_{r}(x)
. Suppose for now that
u(y)\geq 0
.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2ir\in[0,\min\{u(y),\epsilon/4\}]
we have that
(y,2ir)
is in the closure of
\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u)
and
B_{r}(y,2ir)\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
is contained in
B_{\epsilon/2}\times(-\epsilon/2,\epsilon/2)
.

We denote by
c_{1},c_{2},\cdots
positive constants depending on
K
and
N
.
Applying Corollary
2.17
in the product space
(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\mathfrak{m}_{\times})
, it holds

\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(B_{r}(y,2ir)\cap\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u))\geq c_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(B_{r}(y,2ir))\geq c_{2}r\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y)).

In particular, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2r}(x)}|u|\,d\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cap[0,\min\{u(y)/2r,\epsilon/8r\}]}\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(B_{r}(y,2ir)\cap\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u))

\displaystyle\geq c_{3}r(\min\{u(y)/2r,\epsilon/8r\}-2)\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(y)).

By the doubling property,

c_{3}r+\fint_{B_{2r}(x)}|u|\,d\mathfrak{m}\geq c_{5}\min\{u(y),\epsilon\}.

In particular, since
u(y)
was assumed to be positive and
x
is a Lebesgue point with
u(x)=0
, we deduce that

\sup_{B_{r}(x)}u\vee 0\to 0

as
r\downarrow 0
.
By an analogous argument, it holds

\inf_{B_{r}(x)}u\wedge 0\to 0

as well,
proving the continuity of
u
in
x
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Given an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
we define the codimension
1
spherical Hausdorff measure to be the measure obtained with the Carathéodory construction using coverings made by balls and gauge function

B_{r}(x)\mapsto\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))/r.

We denote such measure by
\mathsf{H}^{h}
.

The next proposition corresponds to
[
37
, Theorem
4.1
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open. Let
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
. Then
\mathsf{H}^{h}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
is a Lebesgue point of
f
.

The next proposition is an intermediate step to prove that
3
implies
1
in Theorem
3.1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open. Let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be such that
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is locally perimeter minimizing in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. There exists an open set
A\subset\Omega
with
\mathsf{H}^{h}(\Omega\setminus A)=0
such that, for every
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(A)
, it holds

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=0.

Proof.

Combining Propositions
3.2
and
3.4
we get that
u
is continuous
\mathsf{H}^{h}
-almost everywhere. For every continuity point
x
of
u
consider the set
A_{x}\subset\Omega
given by Proposition
2.19
and denote with
A
the union of these sets. It is clear that
\mathsf{H}^{h}(\Omega\setminus A)=0
.
Now let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(A)
and let
\{A_{x_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a finite cover of
the support of
\phi
. Let
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be Lipschitz functions such that their sum is equal to
1
on the support of
\phi
, while each
\eta_{i}
is compactly supported in
A_{x_{i}}
.
It is easy to check that such functions exist.
We get

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{A_{i}}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\eta_{i}\phi)}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=0.

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open. Let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be such that
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is locally perimeter minimizing in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. For every
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
, it holds

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}=0.

Proof.

Let
A
be the set given by Proposition
3.5
and call
C:=\Omega\setminus A
. We claim that we can construct a sequence
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of Lipschitz compactly supported functions in
\Omega
that are equal to
1
in a neighbourhood of
C\cap\mathsf{supp}(\phi)
and such that
\|\eta_{i}\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)}\to 0
as
i
goes to
+\infty
.

Assume for the moment that the claim holds. In this case, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\eta_{i}\phi)}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla((1-\eta_{i})\phi)}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta_{i}}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta_{i}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{W_{u}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Since
|\nabla u|/W_{u}\leq 1
, the last expression tends to zero as
i
goes to
+\infty
since
\|\eta_{i}\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)}\to 0
.

So we only need to prove our previous claim. We have that
C\cap\mathsf{supp}(\phi)
is compact and
\mathsf{H}^{h}(C)=0
. So for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a finite collection
\{B_{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x_{i}^{\epsilon})\}_{i=1}^{m_{\epsilon}}
of balls with radii in
(0,\epsilon)
and centers in
C\cap\mathsf{supp}(\phi)
, whose union covers
C\cap\mathsf{supp}(\phi)
, and such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\epsilon}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x_{i}^{\epsilon}))}{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}<\epsilon.

For every such ball define
\eta_{i}^{\epsilon}:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\eta_{i}^{\epsilon}(x)=\Big(1-\frac{\mathsf{d}(B_{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x_{i}^{\epsilon}),x)}{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}\Big)\vee 0.

It is clear that, for some constant
c_{1}(K,N)>0
, it holds
\|\eta_{i}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X})}\leq\mathfrak{m}(B_{2r_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x_{i}^{\epsilon}))\leq c_{1}\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x_{i}^{\epsilon}))
, and

\|\nabla\eta_{i}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X})}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{2r_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x_{i}^{\epsilon}))}{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}\leq c_{1}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x_{i}^{\epsilon}))}{r_{i}^{\epsilon}}.

In particular defining
\eta_{\epsilon}:=(\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\epsilon}}\eta_{i}^{\epsilon})\wedge 1
we obtain a Lipschitz function compactly supported in
\Omega
(if
\epsilon
is small enough) which is equal to
1
on a neighbourhood of
C\cap\mathsf{supp}(\phi)
and with
\|\eta_{\epsilon}\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\mathsf{X})}\leq 2c_{1}\epsilon
. This concludes the proof.
∎

The function

belongs to

by
[
27
, Theorem 2.27]
. Since
u
solves the minimal surface equation, it holds
f^{\prime}(0)=0
. By taking second derivatives and using again
[

f
convex, so that it has a minimum in
0
.
∎

The previous proposition implies that the theory of De Giorgi Classes can be applied to

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

.

The next proposition contains a technical approximation result that will be crucial for the remaining part of the section.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. Let
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
be open sets and let
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
and
\phi\in\mathsf{BV}_{c}(\Omega^{\prime})
be both bounded. There exist functions
f_{t}\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})
converging in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})
to
f+\phi
such that

\limsup_{t\to 0}P(\mathsf{Epi}(f_{t}),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})=P(\mathsf{Epi}(f+\phi),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})

(8)

and

|D(f-f_{t})|(\Omega^{\prime\prime}\setminus\Omega^{\prime})\to 0.

(9)

Proof.

Since the statement concerns precompact sets of
\Omega
, modulo using a cut off, we suppose that
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
with compact support in
\Omega
. Moreover, modulo enlarging
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
, we may also suppose that
\mathfrak{m}(\partial\Omega^{\prime\prime})=0
. Whenever we refer to
f
as a function on
\mathsf{X}
we implicitly mean its extension to zero.

Let
B
be an open set such that
\mathsf{supp}(\phi)\subset\subset B\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime}
.
We claim that, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a function
f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
with compact support, whose restriction to
\Omega\setminus B
is Lipschitz, and such that
\|f^{\prime}-f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})}\leq\epsilon
,

|P(\mathsf{Epi}(f+\phi),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})-P(\mathsf{Epi}(f^{\prime}+\phi),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})|<\epsilon,

and

|D(f-f^{\prime})(\Omega^{\prime\prime}\setminus\Omega^{\prime})|<\epsilon.

To prove the claim

A
such that

\mathsf{supp}(\phi)\subset\subset A\subset\subset B,

let
\delta>0
, and let
v\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
be such that
\|f-v\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)}<\delta
.
Let then
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(B)
be a positive function, taking value less than or equal to
1
and identically equal to
1
on
A
.
We define
f^{\prime}:=\eta f+(1-\eta)v
. This function trivially satisfies
f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)

\|f^{\prime}-f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})}+|D(f-f^{\prime})(\Omega^{\prime\prime}\setminus\Omega^{\prime})|<c(\eta)\delta

|P(\mathsf{Epi}(f+\phi),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})-P(\mathsf{Epi}(f^{\prime}+\phi),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})|

By Proposition
2.18
and triangular inequality, this last quantity is controlled by

|D(f^{\prime}-f)|({{}^{c}}A)\leq|D(1-\eta)(v-f)|(\Omega)\leq c(\eta)\delta.

In particular, choosing
\delta
small enough we have proved the claim.

f
to
\Omega\setminus B
is Lipschitz (and we still have that
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
and has compact support).

To this aim we define
f_{t}:=P_{t}(f+\phi)
and we claim that these functions restricted to
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
have the right properties.
First of all the functions
f_{t}
are Lipschitz by the
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}

in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})

|D(f-f_{t})|(\Omega^{\prime\prime}\setminus\Omega^{\prime})\to 0.

\tau\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega^{\prime})

P_{t}((1-\tau)f)\to(1-\tau)f

\Omega^{\prime\prime}

In particular, when we restrict these functions to

|D(f-P_{t}((1-\tau)f))|(\Omega^{\prime\prime}\setminus\Omega^{\prime})\to 0.

|D(P_{t}(\tau f+\phi))|(\Omega^{\prime\prime}\setminus{\Omega}^{\prime})\to|D(\tau f+\phi)|(\Omega^{\prime\prime}\setminus{\Omega}^{\prime})=0.

Finally, condition (

[

.

Proposition 3.11

\epsilon>0
. Let
\phi\in\mathsf{BV}_{c}(\Omega^{\prime})

By Proposition

, there exists

\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})
be positive, taking value less than or equal to

and equal to

on a neighbourhood of

.
Note that
f\eta+(1-\eta)u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
and differs from
u
on a precompact set of
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
, so that

P(\mathsf{Epi}(u),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})\leq P(\mathsf{Epi}(f\eta+(1-\eta)u),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R}).

At the same time

|P(\mathsf{Epi}(f\eta+(1-\eta)u),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})-P(\mathsf{Epi}(f),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})|\leq|D(1-\eta)(f-u)|(\Omega^{\prime\prime}\setminus\bar{\Omega}^{\prime})\leq C(\eta)\epsilon.

Putting these inequalities together, it holds

\displaystyle P(\mathsf{Epi}(\phi+u),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})\geq P(\mathsf{Epi}(f),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})-\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq P(\mathsf{Epi}(f\eta+(1-\eta)u),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})-(C(\eta)+1)\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq P(\mathsf{Epi}(u),\Omega^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R})-(C(\eta)+1))\epsilon,

concluding the proof.
∎

Given
f:\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
(x,t)\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
we denote by
f^{t}
and
f^{x}
respectively the restriction of

to
\mathsf{X}\times\{t\}
and to
\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open. Let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. Then
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
is a perimeter minimizing set in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Consider a set
E\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u)\Delta E\subset\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. Modulo translating vertically, we may suppose that there exists
c>1
such that
u
takes values in
(1,c-1)
and
\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u)\Delta E\subset\subset\Omega\times(1,c-1)
.
We then define
w(E):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

w(E)(x):=\int_{0}^{c}1_{E}(x,s)\,ds

and we claim that

w(E)\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994ptP(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(w(E)),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})\leq P(E,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}).

(10)

If the claim holds, noting that by construction
\{w(E)\neq u\}\subset\subset\Omega
, by Proposition
3.11
, we get

P(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})\leq P(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(w(E)),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})\leq P(E,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}).

To prove the claim, we consider a sequence
f_{n}\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega\times(0,c))
converging in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega\times(0,c))
to
1_{E}
and such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}|Df_{n}|(\Omega\times(0,c))=P(E,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}).

Modulo truncating, we can assume that
f_{n}\equiv 1
on
\Omega\times\{0\}
and
f_{n}\equiv 0
on
\Omega\times\{c\}
.
We then define
w(f_{n}):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as

w(f_{n})(x):=\int_{0}^{c}f_{n}(x,s)\,ds.

These functions are Lipschitz since

\frac{|w(f_{n})(x)-w(f_{n})(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}\leq\int_{0}^{c}\frac{|f_{n}(x,s)-f_{n}(y,s)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}\,ds\leq c\mathsf{L}(f_{n}).

By reverse Fatou Lemma and the fact that each
f_{n}
is Lipschitz, for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in\mathsf{X}
it holds

|\nabla w(f_{n})|(x)=\mathsf{lip}(w(f_{n}))(x)\leq\int_{0}^{c}|\nabla f_{n}^{s}|(x)\,ds.

(11)

Moreover, by the tensorization property of the Cheeger energy (see
[
8
]
), it holds

\displaystyle|Df_{n}|(\Omega\times(0,c))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times(0,c)}|\nabla f_{n}|(x,s)\,d\mathfrak{m}\,ds=\int_{\Omega\times(0,c)}\sqrt{|\nabla f_{n}^{s}|^{2}(x)+|\nabla f_{n}^{x}|^{2}(s)}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b)\in C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega)\\
a^{2}+b^{2}\leq 1\\
a,b\geq 0\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega\times(0,c)}a(x)|\nabla f_{n}^{s}|(x)+b(x)|\nabla f_{n}^{x}|(s)\,d\mathfrak{m}\,ds.

Combining with (
11
) and the fact that
f_{n}=1
on
\Omega\times\{0\}
and
f_{n}=0
on
\Omega\times\{c\}
, we deduce

\displaystyle|Df_{n}|(\Omega\times(0,c))\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b)\in C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega)\\
a^{2}+b^{2}\leq 1\\
a,b\geq 0\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla w(f_{n})|(x)+b(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{1+|\nabla w(f_{n})|^{2}}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

Moreover,
w(f_{n})\to w(E)
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
as
n\to+\infty
since

\int_{\Omega}|w(f_{n})-w(E)|\,d\mathfrak{m}\leq\int_{\Omega\times(0,c)}|f_{n}(x,s)-1_{E}(x,s)|\,d\mathfrak{m}\,ds.

These facts imply that
w(E)\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
as claimed. Concerning the perimeter of
w(E)
, we get

\displaystyle P(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(w(E)),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf P(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(w(f_{n})),\Omega\times\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf|Df_{n}|(\Omega\times(0,c))=P(E,\Omega\times\mathbb{R}),

proving the claim (
10
).
∎

In this section, we prove Theorems

from the introduction. In Section

4.1
Blow-ups of functions

In
[
18
, Theorem 10.2]
, it is shown that a Lipschitz function

on a PI space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is infinitesimally generalized linear for
\mathfrak{m}
-almost every

at

at

is a function

which is linear in a generalized sense. Moreover, the local Lipschitz constant of

at

In this section, we consider a variation of this property. Given an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
and a solution of the minimal surface equation
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, we would like to use an analogue of
[
18
, Theorem 10.2]
for
u
. Since

is not Lipschitz, we cannot apply Cheeger’s result directly.
Theorem
4.8
shows that

is nevertheless infinitesimally generalized linear at
\mathfrak{m}
-almost every point of its domain.

The next two definitions are needed to exploit the machinery from
[
18
]
in our context.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})

\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

Let

The next lemma and the subsequent proposition are the key tools to prove Theorem

.
Lemma
4.5
shows the following. Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

be a regular point of
\mathsf{X}

(whose centers lie on a line through the origin, and whose radius is approximately the distance of their centers from the origin) which are sent by

, for

\mathsf{X}\setminus E
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
x\in\mathsf{X}
be a regular point of
\mathsf{X}
. Let
E
be a perimeter minimizing set admitting tangent balls at
x\in\partial E
. Let
(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n})
be a blow-up of
\mathsf{X}
in
x
. There exist
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
\{c_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
|a_{n}|\to+\infty
and
c_{n}\to 0
, such that

\psi_{n}(B^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{|a_{n}|-c_{n}}(a_{n}))\subset E\setminus\partial E,\hskip 9.24994pt\psi_{n}(B^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{|a_{n}|-c_{n}}(-a_{n}))\subset\mathsf{X}\setminus(E\cup\partial E).

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic connecting the centers of the tangent balls to
E
at
x
which passes through
x
. We remark that such a geodesic exists, since concatenating geodesics from
centers of tangent balls to the corresponding tangent point still yields a geodesic.

Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in\partial B_{n\epsilon_{n}}(x)
be the points of intersection of
\gamma
with
\partial B_{n\epsilon_{n}}(x)
.

Since the maps
\psi_{n}
are
\delta_{n}
-GH maps (for some
\delta_{n}>0
such that
n\delta_{n}\to 0
), there exist points
b^{n}_{1},b^{n}_{2}\in B_{n}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(0)
such that
\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(\psi_{n}(b^{n}_{1}),y_{1})\leq\delta_{n}
and
\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(\psi_{n}(b^{n}_{2}),y_{2})\leq\delta_{n}
. Hence,

|b^{n}_{1}-b^{n}_{2}|\geq 2n-3\delta_{n},

so that

|b^{n}_{1}+b^{n}_{2}|^{2}=2|b^{n}_{1}|^{2}+2|b^{n}_{2}|^{2}-|b_{1}^{n}-b^{n}_{2}|^{2}\leq 12n\delta_{n}+9\delta_{n}^{2}.

(12)

Setting
a_{n}:=b^{n}_{1}
, it trivially holds

\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(\psi_{n}(a_{n}),y_{1})\leq\delta_{n}
. At the same time, using (
12
) in the last inequality, it holds

\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(\psi_{n}(-a_{n}),y_{2})\leq\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(\psi_{n}(b_{2}^{n}),y_{2})+|b^{n}_{2}+a_{n}|+\delta_{n}\leq 12n\delta_{n}+9\delta_{n}^{2}+2\delta_{n}.

We set

\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(\psi_{n}(-a_{n}),y_{2})\leq c_{n}/100,\hskip 9.24994pt\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(\psi_{n}(a_{n}),y_{1})\leq c_{n}/100.

Using again that
\psi_{n}
almost preserves distances, we deduce

\psi_{n}(B_{n-c_{n}}(a_{1}))\subset B^{\mathsf{X}}_{n\epsilon_{n}}(y_{1})\,\hskip 9.24994pt\psi_{n}(B_{n-c_{n}}(-a_{1}))\subset B^{\mathsf{X}}_{n\epsilon_{n}}(y_{2}).

Since

large enough, the balls

are contained in the tangent balls to

at

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})

\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

If

Modulo translating, we may assume that

(13)

The next theorem, which is needed for Theorem

, can be found in
[

. The proof in the

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a Lipschitz function
f_{\epsilon}
such that
\mathfrak{m}(\{f\neq f_{\epsilon}\})\leq\epsilon
.

We now prove the main theorem of the section, showing that
a solution
u
of the minimal surface equation is infinitesimally generalized linear at
\mathfrak{m}
-almost every point of its domain.
The idea is the following.
By the previous theorem, given a Lipschitz function

, it is enough to show the statement at points where
\{f=u\}
has density
1
. So we restrict ourselves to these points. By Cheeger’s Theorem
4.4
,

will have a blow-up given by a linear function

at

. By the previous proposition, modulo staying out of a negligible set, the graph of
u
at
(x,u(x))
is well approximated at small scales by a Euclidean hyperplane

. Since we were at a density point of
\{f=u\}
, this forces

, giving the desired conclusion.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set, and let

\mathfrak{m}

\mathsf{X}
at

. Modulo passing to a subsequence, there exists a linear function
u^{\infty}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that any
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with
\mathsf{Graph}(v)\subset\partial\mathsf{Epi}(u)

(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n},u^{\infty})
at

.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let

be such that
\mathfrak{m}(\{f\neq u\})\leq\epsilon

is infinitesimally generalized linear and
\{f=u\}
has density
1
. Modulo translating, we may assume
u(x)=f(x)=0
.

Let
(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n})
be a blow-up of
\mathsf{X}
at
x
.
Let
r>0
and let
x_{1},...,x_{k}
be points in
\mathbb{R}^{k}

Since
\{f=u\}
has density
1
in
x
, for
n
large enough,
B_{r/2}(\psi_{n}(x_{i}))\cap\{f=u\}
is non-empty for every
i=1,\cdots k
.
Hence, we can consider a blow-up
(\epsilon_{n},\psi^{\prime}_{n})
of
\mathsf{X}
at
x
, such that
\psi^{\prime}_{n}\neq\psi_{n}
at a finite number of points and, for every
i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
, there exists
p^{n}_{i}\in B_{r}(x_{i})
with
\psi^{\prime}_{n}(p^{n}_{i})\in\{f=u\}
. The
k
-tuples
(p^{n}_{1},\cdots,p^{n}_{k})
converge to a
k
-tuple
(p_{1},\cdots,p_{k})
of
k
linearly independent points.

Since
f
is Lipschitz, by Theorem
4.4
, there exists a linear function
f^{\infty}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}f\circ\psi^{\prime}_{n}-f^{\infty}\|_{\infty,B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(0)}\to 0.

Modulo passing to a subsequence, let
H^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
be the hyperplane relative to
(\epsilon_{n},\psi^{\prime}_{n})
given by Proposition
4.6
. Since by construction the points
\{(p_{i},f^{\infty}(p_{i}))\}_{i=1}^{k}
belong to
H^{\prime}
, it holds
H^{\prime}=\mathsf{Graph}(f^{\infty})
.

Let now
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
be the hyperplane relative to
(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n})
given by Proposition
4.6
. Out of the countable set where
\psi_{n}\neq\psi^{\prime}_{n}
for some

\epsilon_{n}^{-1}u\circ\psi_{n}
coincide with
\epsilon^{-1}_{n}u\circ\psi^{\prime}_{n}
and converge pointwise to

. Hence

, so that the statement follows by item
2
in Proposition
4.6
.

∎

4.2
Minimal graphs as metric measure spaces

In this section, we prove Theorems
5
and
6
from the introduction.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation.
We would like to say that Lipschitz functions on
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
, when restricted to the graph of
u
, are infinitesimally generalized linear at almost every point. Since we do not know whether the graph of
u
is a PI space, we cannot apply Cheeger’s result
[
18
, Theorem 10.2]
directly. This difficulty is overcome with Theorem
4.17
, which in turn relies on the results from the previous section.

We first define the metric measure space structure on the graph of a solution of the minimal surface equation.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. Let
\mathsf{G}(u)\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
be the boundary of
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
. We define the metric measure space
(\mathsf{G}(u),\mathsf{d}_{g},\mathfrak{m}_{g})
, where
\mathsf{d}_{g}
is the product distance of
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
restricted to
\mathsf{G}(u)\times\mathsf{G}(u)
and
\mathfrak{m}_{g}
is the perimeter measure induced by
\mathsf{Epi}(u)
on the Borel subsets of
\mathsf{G}(u)
.

We denote by
\mathsf{lip}_{g}
the local Lipschitz constant of a function
f:\mathsf{G}(u)\to\mathbb{R}
and by
i_{g}:\Omega\to\mathsf{G}(u)
the function given by

i_{g}(x):=(x,u(x)).

(15)

We now prove a few short technical lemmas describing the behavior of Lipschitz functions on
\mathsf{G}(u)
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set, let
u\in\mathsf{W}_{loc}^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap\mathsf{L}^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
, and let
f\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R})
. Let
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by
g(x):=f(x,u(x))
. Then,
g\in\mathsf{W}_{loc}^{1,1}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Modulo multiplying by a cut off function, we may suppose that
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\mathsf{X})\cap\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\mathsf{X})
with compact support. Let
u_{n}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})\cap\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X})
be such that
u_{n}\to u
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X})
, with uniformly bounded norms of the gradients
\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X})}
, and such that
|\nabla u_{n}|\,d\mathfrak{m}\to|\nabla u|\,d\mathfrak{m}
weakly in duality with continuous compactly supported functions.

Consider the function
g_{n}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
defined by
g_{n}(x):=f(x,u_{n}(x))
. It is easy to check that
\mathsf{lip}(g_{n})\leq\mathsf{L}(f)(1+|\nabla u_{n}|)
. Since
g_{n}\to g
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
, it holds
g\in\mathsf{BV}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
and
|Dg|\leq\mathsf{L}(f)(1+|\nabla u|)
. In particular,
g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set, and let

. For
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. regular point of

where
u

is a blow-up of

, then
|\nabla u|(x)=\mathsf{lip}(u^{\infty})

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let

be a function with
\mathfrak{m}(\{f\neq u\})\leq\epsilon
. Let
x
be a point where
u
and

\{f=u\}

1
at

(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n},f^{\infty})

u
and
f

To conclude, by Theorem
4.4

f
and
u
are infinitesimally generalized linear, if we consider a blow-up
(\epsilon_{n},\psi^{\prime}_{n})

\mathsf{X}

and
f

\bar{B}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{1}(0)

Since
\{f=u\}
has density

in

large enough,
B_{r/2}(\psi_{n}(x_{i}))\cap\{f=u\}
is non-empty for every

\mathsf{X}

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set, and let

. For
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. regular point of

where both

and

are blow-ups of

and

at

, then
\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=\nabla u^{\infty}\cdot\nabla v^{\infty}
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set, let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation.
The next lemma shows that, for almost every point of
\mathsf{G}(u)
, the local Lipschitz constant of a function on

coincides with the local Lipschitz constant of its restriction to
\mathsf{Graph}(u)

\mathfrak{m}

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set, let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. Let
\theta\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))
and let
x\in\Omega
be a point where
u
is infinitesimally generalized linear and continous. Then, there exist points
x_{n}\to x
such that

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\theta)(i_{g}(x))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{|\theta(i_{g}(x_{n}))-\theta(i_{g}(x))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x),i_{g}(x_{n}))}.

Proof.

Let
\bar{x}_{n}:=(x_{n},t_{n})\in\mathsf{G}(u)
be points such that

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\theta)(i_{g}(x))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{|\theta(\bar{x}_{n})-\theta(i_{g}(x))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x),\bar{x}_{n})}.

Since
u
is continuous at
x
, it holds
\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}\cap\mathsf{G}(u)=\{(x,u(x))\}
. In particular,
x_{n}\neq x
.
It is sufficient to show that

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\theta)(i_{g}(x))\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{|\theta(i_{g}(x_{n}))-\theta(i_{g}(x))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x),i_{g}(x_{n}))}.

We first show

\frac{|t_{n}-u(x_{n})|}{\mathsf{d}(x,x_{n})}\to 0.

(16)

This then implies

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x),\bar{x}_{n})}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x),i_{g}(x_{n}))}=1.

(17)

Let
\epsilon_{n}:=\mathsf{d}(x,x_{n})
and let
(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n})
be a blow-up of
\mathsf{X}
at
x
such that
x_{n}\in\mathsf{Im}(\psi_{n})
with
\psi_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n})=x_{n}
. By Theorem
4.8
, there exists a linear function
u^{\infty}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n},u^{\infty})
is a blow-up of any function
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with
\mathsf{Graph}(v)\subset\mathsf{G}(u)
. Hence, setting
v=u
outside of
\{x_{n}\}
and
v(x_{n}):=t_{n}
, it holds

\frac{|t_{n}-u(x_{n})|}{\mathsf{d}(x,x_{n})}\leq|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}(v(\psi_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n}))-v(x))-u^{\infty}(x^{\prime}_{n})|+|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}(u(\psi_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n}))-u(x))-u^{\infty}(x^{\prime}_{n})|.

Since the r.h.s. converges to zero by the previous remarks,
\frac{|t_{n}-u(x_{n})|}{\mathsf{d}(x,x_{n})}\to 0
as well, proving (
16
).

By (
17
), to conclude, we need to show that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{|\theta(\bar{x}_{n})-\theta(i_{g}(x))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}),i_{g}(x))}\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{|\theta(i_{g}(x_{n}))-\theta(i_{g}(x))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}),i_{g}(x))}.

(18)

Since

is Lipschitz and
\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}),i_{g}(x))\geq\mathsf{d}(x_{n},x)
, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{|\theta(\bar{x}_{n})-\theta(i_{g}(x))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}),i_{g}(x))}\leq\frac{|\theta(i_{g}(x_{n}))-\theta(i_{g}(x))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}),i_{g}(x))}+\frac{\mathsf{L}(\theta)|t_{n}-u(x_{n})|}{\mathsf{d}(x_{n},x)}.

Combining with (

The next lemma shows a locality property for the local lipschitz constant of a function on a minimal graph.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set, let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. Let
x\in\Omega
be a point where
u
is infinitesimally generalized linear and continuous. Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))
be such that
\theta_{1}(i_{g}(x))=\theta_{2}(i_{g}(x))
and
\{\theta_{1}\circ i_{g}=\theta_{2}\circ i_{g}\}
has density
1
in
x
. Then,
\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\theta_{1})(x)=\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\theta_{2})(x)
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})=0
. Let
f:=\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
. Let
x_{n}\to x
be the sequence given by the previous lemma such that

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(f)(x)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{|f(i_{g}(x))-f(i_{g}(x_{n}))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}),i_{g}(x))}.

Since
\{f\circ i_{g}=0\}
has density
1
in
x
, for every
\epsilon>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
n\geq N
and
y_{n}\in\{f\circ i_{g}=0\}
such that
\mathsf{d}(y_{n},x_{n})\leq\epsilon\mathsf{d}(x,x_{n})
. Since
x
is a point where
u
is infinitesimally generalized linear, we can also require that
|u(y_{n})-u(x_{n})|\leq c\epsilon\mathsf{d}(x,x_{n})
, where
c
is a constant depending on
|\nabla u|(x)
. Hence,

\frac{|f(i_{g}(y_{n}))-f(i_{g}(x_{n}))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(y_{n}),i_{g}(x_{n}))}\geq\frac{|f(i_{g}(x))-f(i_{g}(x_{n}))|}{2c\epsilon\mathsf{d}(x,x_{n})}\geq\frac{|f(i_{g}(x))-f(i_{g}(x_{n}))|}{2c\epsilon\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}),i_{g}(x))}.

Taking limits, we deuce
c\epsilon\mathsf{L}(f)\geq\mathsf{lip}_{g}(f)(x)
. Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude.

The next theorem is the key step to prove Theorem
4.17
.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. Let
x\in\Omega
be a point where
u
is infinitesimally generalized linear and continuous, let
(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n})
be a blow-up of
\mathsf{X}
at
x
, and let
(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n},u^{\infty})
be the corresponding blow-up of
u
given by Theorem
4.8
.
Let
j:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathsf{Graph}(u^{\infty})\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}
be the projection on the graph, i.e.
j(x):=(x,u^{\infty}(x))
.
Then, there exists a sequence
\{\delta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
decreasing to zero such that the maps

\psi^{\prime}_{n}:=i_{g}\circ\psi_{n}\circ j^{-1}:j(\bar{B}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{1}(0))\to(\mathsf{G}(u)\cap\bar{B}^{\mathsf{X}}_{\epsilon_{n}}(x)\times\mathbb{R},\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{g})

are
\delta_{n}
-

We first prove that for every
\delta>0
, if
n
is sufficiently large,
\psi^{\prime}_{n}(j(\bar{B}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{1}(0)))
is a
\delta
-net in
(\mathsf{G}(u)\cap\bar{B}^{\mathsf{X}}_{\epsilon_{n}}(x)\times\mathbb{R},\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{g})
.

Assume by contradiction that this is not the case. Hence, there exist
\delta>0
and a sequence of points
\bar{x}_{n}:=(x_{n},t_{n})\in\mathsf{G}(u)\cap\bar{B}^{\mathsf{X}}_{\epsilon_{n}}(x)\times\mathbb{R}
, such that
\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{g}(\bar{x}_{n},\bar{y})>\delta
for every
\bar{y}
in the image of
i_{g}\circ\psi_{n}
.

Consider the function
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
which coincides with
u
outside of
\{x_{n}\}
and such that
v(x_{n})=t_{n}
. Note that the graph of this functions belongs to
\partial\mathsf{Epi}(u)
, so that we can apply Theorem
4.8
.
Let
(\epsilon,\tilde{\psi}_{n})
be a blow-up of
\mathsf{X}
at
x
such that
\psi_{n}=\tilde{\psi}_{n}
outside of a singleton
\{x^{\prime}_{n}\}
and such that
\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n})=x_{n}
.

By Theorem
4.8
(and Remark
4.3
), there exists a sequence
\delta_{n}\to 0
such that, assuming for simplicity that
u(x)=0

|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}v(\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n}))-u^{\infty}(x^{\prime}_{n})|\leq\delta_{n},\hskip 9.24994pt|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}u(\psi_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n}))-u^{\infty}(x^{\prime}_{n})|\leq\delta_{n}.

Hence,

\epsilon_{n}^{-1}|t_{n}-u(\psi_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n}))|\leq 2\delta_{n}.

(19)

Since
\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(x_{n},\psi_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n}))\to 0
,
it follows from (

) that
\mathsf{d}_{g}((x_{n},t_{n}),i_{g}(\psi_{n}(x^{\prime}_{n}))\to 0

Now we need to show that

almost preserves distances. We pick two points
(x_{1},u^{\infty}(x_{1}))
and
(x_{2},u^{\infty}(x_{2}))
in the domain of
\psi^{\prime}_{n}
and we need to compute

\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{g}(\psi^{\prime}_{n}(x_{1},u^{\infty}(x_{1})),\psi^{\prime}_{n}(x_{2},u^{\infty}(x_{2}))).

The previous expression is equal to

\displaystyle\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{\times}((\psi_{n}(x_{1}),u(\psi_{n}(x_{1}))),(\psi_{n}(x_{2}),u(\psi_{n}(x_{2}))))

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\epsilon_{n}^{-2}\mathsf{d}(\psi_{n}(x_{1}),\psi_{n}(x_{2}))^{2}+\epsilon_{n}^{-2}|u(\psi_{n}(x_{1}))-u(\psi_{n}(x_{2}))|^{2}}.

\displaystyle|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{g}(\psi^{\prime}_{n}(x_{1},u^{\infty}(x_{1})),\psi^{\prime}_{n}(x_{2},u^{\infty}(x_{2})))-\mathsf{d}_{e}((x_{1},u^{\infty}(x_{1})),(x_{2},u^{\infty}(x_{2})))|

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d}_{y})

, and let

A\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}

and
y_{n}\in A_{n}

\epsilon_{n},\delta_{n}

\delta_{n}
-
GH

\psi_{n}:A\to(A_{n},\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{y})
with
\psi_{n}(0)=y
and
\psi_{n}(x_{n})=y_{n}

. If the sequence
x_{n}
does not have
0

\mathsf{lip}(f)(y)=\mathsf{lip}(f^{\infty})(0)

We first show that
\mathsf{lip}(f)(y)\leq\mathsf{lip}(f^{\infty})(0)
.
Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
f(y)=0
, so that

|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}f(y_{n})-f^{\infty}(x_{n})|\to 0.

Note that
\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}(y,y_{n})

\psi_{n}
are
\delta_{n}
-GH approximations. Hence, it holds

\Big|\frac{f(y_{n})}{\mathsf{d}_{y}(y_{n},y)}-\frac{f^{\infty}(x_{n})}{\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{y}(y,y_{n})}\Big|\to 0.

\Big|\frac{f^{\infty}(x_{n})}{\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{y}(y,y_{n})}-\frac{f^{\infty}(x_{n})}{|x_{n}|}\Big|\to 0.

Since the sequence

-GH approximations with
\psi_{n}(x_{n})=y_{n}
.

We now show that
\mathsf{lip}(f^{\infty})(0)\leq\mathsf{lip}(f)(y)
. Since
f^{\infty}
is linear, there exists
x_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
|x_{\infty}|=1
and
\mathsf{lip}(f^{\infty})(0)=f^{\infty}(x_{\infty})
.
By hypothesis, assuming as before that
f(y)=0
, it holds

|{\epsilon_{n}}^{-1}f(\psi_{n}(x_{\infty}))-f^{\infty}(x_{\infty})|\to 0,

and
\mathsf{d}_{y}(\psi_{n}(x_{\infty}),y)\leq\epsilon_{n}(1+\delta_{n})
. Therefore

\mathsf{lip}(f^{\infty})(0)=f^{\infty}(x_{\infty})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}{\epsilon_{n}}^{-1}|f(\psi_{n}(x_{\infty}))|

\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{(1+\delta_{n})|f(\psi_{n}(x_{\infty}))|}{\mathsf{d}_{y}(\psi_{n}(x_{\infty}),y)}\leq\mathsf{lip}(f)(y),

concluding the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})

\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

. Then,
\theta\circ i_{g}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
and, for
\mathfrak{m}
-almost every

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\theta)^{2}\circ i_{g}=|\nabla\theta\circ i_{g}|^{2}-\frac{(\nabla\theta\circ i_{g}\cdot\nabla u)^{2}}{W_{u}^{2}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.10
, it holds
\theta\circ i_{g}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
.
By Theorem
4.7
, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
f\in\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
, such that
\mathfrak{m}\{f\neq\theta\circ i_{g}\}\leq\epsilon
.
Let
x\in\Omega
satisfy the following.

1.

\{f=\theta\circ i_{g}\}
has density
1
in
x
.

2.

f
is infinitesimally generalized linear at
x
.

3.

u
satisfies the property of Theorem
4.8
in
x
(in particular, it is is infinitesimally generalized linear at
x

4.

\mathsf{lip}(f)(x)=|\nabla f|(x)=|\nabla(\theta\circ i_{g})|(x)
.

5.

\nabla\theta\circ i_{g}\cdot\nabla u=\nabla f\cdot\nabla u
.

Consider the function
f\circ i_{g}^{-1}:\mathsf{Graph}(u)\to\mathbb{R}
. It is easy to check that
f\circ i_{g}^{-1}
is Lipschitz, so that we consider its Lipschitz extension
f_{g}:\mathsf{G}(u)\to\mathbb{R}
.
By Lemma
4.14
, it holds

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(f_{g})(i_{g}(x))=\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\theta)(i_{g}(x)).

(20)

By Lemma
4.13
, there exist points
x_{n}\to x
such that

\mathsf{lip}_{g}(f_{g})(i_{g}(x))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{|f_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}))-f_{g}(i_{g}(x))|}{\mathsf{d}_{g}(i_{g}(x_{n}),i_{g}(x))}.

Let
\epsilon_{n}:=\mathsf{d}(x,x_{n})
and, modulo passing to a subsequence, let
(\epsilon_{n},\psi_{n})

\mathsf{X}
in

Let
f^{\infty}

4.15

\psi^{\prime}_{n}:=i_{g}\circ\psi_{n}\circ j^{-1}:j(\bar{B}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{1}(0))\to(\mathsf{G}(u)\cap\bar{B}^{\mathsf{X}}_{\epsilon_{n}}(x)\times\mathbb{R},\epsilon_{n}^{-1}\mathsf{d}_{g})

\|\epsilon_{n}^{-1}(f_{g}\circ i_{g}\circ\psi_{n}\circ j^{-1}-f_{g}(i_{g}(x)))-f^{\infty}\circ j^{-1}\|_{\infty,j(\bar{B}^{\mathbb{R}^{k}}_{1}(0))}

We now recall the definition of a product of gradients in a generic metric space. This will allow to consider products of gradients on a minimal graph.

Definition 4.19
.

Let
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d})
be a metric space and
f,g:\mathsf{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
be functions with finite local Lipschitz constant in
y\in\mathsf{Y}
. We define

\mathsf{lip}(f)\cdot\mathsf{lip}(g):=\frac{1}{4}(\mathsf{lip}(f+g)^{2}-\mathsf{lip}(f-g)^{2}).

Observe that the object defined in the previous definition may fail to be a quadratic form on generic metric spaces. This additional regularity, in our setting, is a consequence of Corollary
4.18
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. The function
\cdot:\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))\times\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))\to\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\mathsf{G}(u))
given by

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\mapsto\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})

is symmetric, bilinear, it satisfies the chain rule and the Leibniz rule in both entries and
\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})\leq\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})
.

Proof.

The fact that
\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})\leq\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})
follows from the fact that
\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})\leq\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})+\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})
and
\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1})\leq\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})+\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi_{2})
. All the other properties follow from the representation given by Corollary
4.18
.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation.
Let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\mathsf{G}(u))
, then
{\mathsf{lip}}_{g}(\phi)\cdot{\mathsf{lip}}_{g}(u_{g})\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{G}(u))
, and

\int_{\mathsf{G}(u)}\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi)\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(u_{g})\,d\mathfrak{m}_{g}=0.

Proof.

Since
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\mathsf{G}(u))
and
u_{g}\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{G}(u))
, then
{\mathsf{lip}}_{g}(\phi)\cdot{\mathsf{lip}}_{g}(u_{g})\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{G}(u))
trivially.
Since
\mathfrak{m}_{g}(B\times\mathbb{R})=\int_{B}W_{u}\,d\mathfrak{m}
for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
, it holds

\int_{\mathsf{G}(u)}\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi)\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(u_{g})\,d\mathfrak{m}_{g}=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi)\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(u_{g})\Big)\circ i_{g}\,W_{u}d\mathfrak{m}.

By Corollary
4.18
, it follows

\int_{\mathsf{G}(u)}\mathsf{lip}_{g}(\phi)\cdot\mathsf{lip}_{g}(u_{g})\,d\mathfrak{m}_{g}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla(\phi\circ i_{g})\cdot\nabla u}{W_{u}}d\mathfrak{m}.

(22)

The function
\phi\circ i_{g}
has compact support in
\Omega
and, by Lemma
4.10
, it holds
\phi\circ i_{g}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
. Since
u
solves the minimal surface equation in
\Omega
, the right hand side of (
22
) vanishes, concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.20
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open, and let

By the chain rule and the Leibniz rule, it holds

The first summand in the previous expression vanishes by Theorem
6
, concluding the proof.

The goal is to mimic the strategy used in

]
to prove the Harnack inequality for

on

.
The challenge in adapting the aforementioned strategy is to prove that

is harmonic on
\mathsf{G}(u)
and this was done in Section

.
Besides harmonicity on the graph, the other key steps are the validity of Poincaré and Sobolev inequalities on
\mathsf{G}(u)
i.e. Theorems
5.6
and

.

These theorems can be obtained in our setting with the same ideas used in

. For this reason we only give a detailed proof of Theorem

, so that one sees what changes need to be made from the corresponding proof in

23
]
; the same exact changes allow then to obtain also the Sobolev inequality from the analogous result in the aforementioned work.
Once the harmonicity of
u
on

and the Poincaré and Sobolev inequalities are established, the Harnack inequality follows formally repeating the same argument of

]

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space, let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be open, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a solution of the minimal surface equation.
Let
(x,t),(y,s)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
and define
\rho_{(x,t)}(y,s):=\mathsf{d}(x,y)+|t-s|
.
Given
\bar{x}\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, we set

D_{\bar{x},r}:=\{\bar{y}\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}:\rho_{\bar{x}}(\bar{y})<r\}

and

A_{\bar{x},r}:=\{x\in\Omega:\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}\cap\mathsf{G}(u)\cap D_{\bar{x},r}\neq\emptyset\}.

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we also set

E_{\bar{x},t}:=\{\bar{y}\in\mathsf{G}(u):u_{g}(\bar{y})>t+u_{g}(\bar{x})\}

and

E^{\prime}_{\bar{x},t}:=\{\bar{y}\in\mathsf{G}(u):u_{g}(\bar{y})<t+u_{g}(\bar{x})\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space, let
B_{R}(p)\subset\mathsf{X}
be a ball, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(B_{R}(p))
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. There exists a constant
C(N)>0
such that, for every
(x,t_{x})=:\bar{x}\in\mathsf{G}(u)
, and

r,s\in(0,R-\mathsf{d}(x,p))
with
r>s
, it holds

\mathfrak{m}_{g}(D_{\bar{x},s})\geq C\mathfrak{m}_{g}(D_{\bar{x},r})\frac{s^{N}}{r^{N}}

and

\frac{1}{C}\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(x))\leq\mathfrak{m}_{g}(D_{\bar{x},s})\leq C\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(x)).

(23)

Proof.

We denote by
c_{1},c_{2}\cdots
positive constants depending on
N
.
From Theorem
2.16
, the fact that
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
is doubling and the fact that the ball
B_{s}(\bar{x})\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
contains the rectangle
B_{s/2}(x)\times(t_{x}-s/2,t_{x}+s/2)
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{g}(D_{\bar{x},s})\geq\mathfrak{m}_{g}(B_{s/2}(\bar{x}))\geq c_{1}\frac{2\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(B_{s/2}(\bar{x}))}{s}\geq c_{2}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(B_{s}(\bar{x}))}{s}

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{s/2}(x))s/2}{s}\geq c_{3}\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(x)).

By a similar argument, one obtains

\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(x))\geq c_{4}\mathfrak{m}(B_{2s}(x))\geq c_{5}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{\times}(B_{s}(\bar{x}))}{s}\geq c_{6}\mathfrak{m}_{g}(D_{\bar{x},s}),

proving (
23
).
Since
\mathfrak{m}
is doubling, by a standard argument (see
[
23
, Equation
(2.1)
]
),
it holds

c_{3}\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(x))\geq c_{7}\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))\frac{s^{N}}{r^{N}}.

Combining with (
23
), the statement follows.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space, let
B_{R}(p)\subset\mathsf{X}
be a ball, and let
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(B_{R}(p))
be a solution of the minimal surface equation. There exists a constant
C(N)>0
such that, for every
(x,t_{x})=:\bar{x}\in\mathsf{G}(u)
and every
r>0
with
B_{3r}(x)\subset B_{R}(p)
, for
\lambda^{1}
-a.e.
t\in(-r,r)
it holds

\mathfrak{m}(\{u>u_{g}(\bar{x})+t\}\cap B_{r-t}(x))\geq C\,\mathfrak{m}_{g}(E_{\bar{x},t}\cap D_{\bar{x},r}).

(24)

Moreover, if

\mathfrak{m}_{g}(E_{\bar{x},t}\cap D_{\bar{x},r})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}_{g}(D_{\bar{x},r}),

(25)

then

\mathfrak{m}(\{u<u_{g}(\bar{x})+t\}\cap B_{r+|t|}(x))\geq C\,\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)).

(26)

Analogous inequalities hold replacing
E_{\bar{x},t}
with
E_{\bar{x},t}^{\prime}
and reversing the inequality signs appearing in the left hand sides of inequalities (
24
) and (
26
).

Proof.

We first prove (
26
). Modulo requiring that
t
is outside of a negligible set, we can assume that
P(\mathsf{Epi}(u),\mathsf{X}\times\{t+t_{x}\}=0)
.
We denote
\bar{x}_{t}:=(x,t_{x}+t)
and we consider the compact set
V\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
given by the closure of

\mathsf{Epi}(u)\cap D_{\bar{x}_{t},r+|t|}\cap\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,t_{x}+t)

and we define the competitor
C:=\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u)\cup V
.

Since
\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u)
minimizes the perimeter, it holds

P(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u),D_{\bar{x},3r})\leq P(C,D_{\bar{x},3r}).

(27)

We now claim that

P(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u),(\mathsf{G}(u)\setminus\partial V)\cap D_{\bar{x},3r})=P(C,(\partial C\setminus\partial V)\cap D_{\bar{x},3r}).

(28)

To prove the claim observe that, by the definition of
V
, we have
\partial C\setminus\partial V=\mathsf{G}(u)\setminus\partial V
and, if
q\in\mathsf{G}(u)\setminus\partial V
, then in a small ball centered at
q
we have that
C=\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u)
, which implies the claim.

Subtracting (
28
) from (
27
), we deduce that

P(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u),\partial V\cap\mathsf{G}(u)\cap D_{\bar{x},3r})\leq P(C,\partial V\cap\partial C\cap D_{\bar{x},3r}).

(29)

We denote by
c_{1}.c_{2},\cdots
constants depending only on
N
. Using first the definition of
V
, then (
25
), and finally Lemma
5.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle P(\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u),\partial V\cap\mathsf{G}(u)\cap D_{\bar{x},3r})\geq\mathfrak{m}_{g}(E^{\prime}_{\bar{x},t}\cap D_{\bar{x},r})

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{g}(D_{\bar{x},r})\geq c_{2}\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x)),

so that, combining with (
29
), to prove (
26
) it is enough to show that

P(C,\partial V\cap\partial C\cap D_{\bar{x},3r})\leq c_{3}\mathfrak{m}(\{u<u_{g}(\bar{x})+t\}\cap B_{r+|t|}(x)).

To this aim observe that

is the disjoint union of

If
q\in A
, then in a sufficiently small ball centered at
q
the set
C
coincides with the subgraph of the constant function
t+t_{x}
. This, together with the area formula given by Proposition
2.18
, implies that

P(C,A)\leq\mathfrak{m}(\{u<t_{x}+t\}\cap B_{r+|t|}(x)).

Similarly, if
q\in B
, then in a sufficiently small ball centered at
q
the set
C
coincides with the epigraph of a function with Lipschitz constant

. Moreover if
q\in B
, then its projection on
\mathsf{X}\times\{t+t_{x}\}
belongs to
A
(this is clear from the picture, and depends on the fact that we defined

using

and not

). This implies that
P(C,B)\leq c_{4}P(C,A)
.

Finally for
\lambda^{1}
-a.e.
t\in(-r,r)
we have that
\mathfrak{m}_{g}(\mathsf{X}\times\{t_{x}+t\})=0
and

\mathfrak{m}_{g}(\partial D_{\bar{x},r+|t|})=0
. Using the area formula given by Proposition

we get that for every such

\mathfrak{m}(\pi_{\mathsf{X}}(\bar{A}\setminus A))=\mathfrak{m}(\pi_{\mathsf{X}}(\bar{B}\setminus B))=0
, which in turn implies that
P(C,D)=0
.

In particular,

P(C,\partial V\cap\partial C)=P(C,A\cup B)\leq c_{5}\mathfrak{m}(\{u<t_{x}+t\}\cap B_{r+|t|}(x)),

as desired.

The proof of the other identity follows an identical argument replacing
V
with the closure of

\mathsf{Epi}^{\prime}(u)\cap D_{\bar{x}_{t},r-t}\cap\mathsf{X}\times(t_{x}+t,\infty).

In this case, since since the r.h.s. is
\mathfrak{m}_{g}(E_{\bar{x},t})
, we do not need to use the condition (
25
), which was needed to say that
\mathfrak{m}_{g}(E^{\prime}_{\bar{x},t})\geq c_{1}\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))
.

The next lemma corresponds to Lemma

in

]
. Our formulation is slightly different and, later on, it will allow us to avoid the use of the Coarea formula on

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)

be a ball, and let

There exists a constant

such that for every

and

the following happens. For
\lambda^{1}
-a.e.

,
if

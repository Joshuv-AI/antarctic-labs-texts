Semi-galois Categories IV: A deformed reciprocity law for Siegel modular functions
Takeo Uramoto

Abstract
This paper is a sequel to our previous work [ 19 ] , where we proved the “modularity theorem” for algebraic Witt vectors over imaginary quadratic fields. This theorem states that, in the case of imaginary quadratic fields K K , the algebraic Witt vectors over K K are precisely those generated by the modular vectors whose components are given by special values of deformation family of Fricke modular functions; arithmetically, this theorem implies certain congruences between special values of modular functions that are not necessarily galois conjugate. In order to take a closer look at this modularity theorem, the current paper extends it to the case of CM fields. The main results include (i) a construction of algebraic Witt vectors from special values of deformation family of Siegel modular functions on Siegel upper-half space ℌ g \mathfrak{H}_{g} given by ratios of theta functions, and (ii) a galois-theoretic characterization of which algebraic Witt vectors arise in this modular way, intending to exemplify a general galois-correspondence result which is also proved in this paper.

Introduction

The goal of this paper is to extend our previous work on the
modularity theorem for algebraic Witt vectors
over imaginary quadratic fields (Theorem 4.3.1,
[
19
]
) to the case of CM fields in particular. This theorem states that, if
K
is an imaginary quadratic field, the
K
-algebra
E_{K}=K\otimes W_{O_{K}}^{a}(O_{\bar{K}})
of
algebraic Witt vectors
over

K
-algebra

generated by the
modular vectors

\widehat{f}_{a}
, whose components are given by special values of Fricke’s modular functions

(

), cf. §
2
. This theorem connects two objects (algebraic/analytic) of different nature; and technically this theorem is proved using
Shimura’s reciprocily law

modular functions (§6.8
[
12

not necessarily galois conjugate
; in this sense, our modularity theorem strengthens/deforms Shimura’s reciprocity law in a way analogous to
Hida theory
, e.g. mod-

[
8

[

10

Our proof of the modularity theorem

with locally constant,

-equivariant functions on the

generate all
galois objects

[
17
]
of the semi-galois category
(\mathscr{C}_{K},\mathrm{F}_{K})
of certain
\Lambda
-rings whose fundamental monoid
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{K},\mathrm{F}_{K})
was known isomorphic to
DR_{K}

[
3
]
; this identification itself holds for arbitrary number fields

of Fricke’s modular functions

indexed by

K^{ab}
-valued,

-equivariant functions on

from these

) actually generate

over
K
; this last part is our key step, in which we utilized the

of finite Deligne-Ribet monoids

[
19
]
in particular).

As mentioned above too, the current work also follows basically this line; but in the case of CM fields, major differences occur in the second and third steps. First, we shall replace Fricke modular functions
f_{a}:\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with certain

; nevertheless, the basic idea behind these two families of modular functions is essentially the same, in that both families of functions (Fricke functions and ratios of theta functions) are those equally appearing in the construction of projective embedding of complex tori (resp. of dimension one and higher dimension)

12

11
]
, which will be a central step in our construction of modular vectors; essentially, their values (= components of modular vectors) both represent the coordinates of torsion points on elliptic curves and abelian varieties respectively. Our major subject in the second step is to give the right setting of necessary constructions and a proof that the ratios of theta functions naturally produce locally constant
K^{ab}
-valued

-equivariant functions on

Second, unlike the case of imaginary quadratic fields, these modular vectors do not generate all algebraic Witt vectors over CM fields; thus it should be of natural concern to characterize which algebraic Witt vectors are generated by our modular vectors. The subject of §

It would be possible to extend the results of the current paper to more general situations, say, along the line of general Shimura varieties

Related works

The current work is motivated by Yalkinoglu’s work
[
20
]
(2012) in particular. In that paper, Yalkinoglu constructed an arithmetic subalgebra of the Bost-Connes system for number fields containing CM fields using Siegel modular functions on Siegel modular varieties (§7
[
20
]
); and if we restrict his arithmetic subalgebra (consisting of certain functions on a
BCM groupoid
; cf. §6
[
20
]
) to the subspace of its objects (which is isomorphic to
DR_{K}
), we can in fact obtain a subalgebra of
E_{K}
under the identification
E_{K}=\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{K}}(DR_{K},K^{ab})
; in other words, up to this identification, his construction of arithmetic subalgebra can be also understood as a construction of algebraic Witt vectors over CM fields from special values of Siegel modular functions; this way, Yalkinoglu’s work
[
20
]
is relevant to our major goal in the current paper.

Comparing our construction to Yalkinoglu’s
[
20
]
, however, highlights the above mentioned fact that our result can be regarded as a deformation of Shimura’s reciprocity law. On the one hand, the algebraic Witt vectors arising from Yalkinoglu’s construction, viewed as functions on
DR_{K}
, have their support only on the unit group
DR_{K}^{\times}=G_{K}^{ab}\subset DR_{K}
(§7
[
20
]
), while those of our construction have their support on the whole
DR_{K}
(cf. §
3
–§
4
). Technically speaking, it is due to this aspect that our algebraic Witt vectors can relate special values of Siegel modular functions living at different levels that are not necessarily galois conjugate, unlike Shimura’s reciprocity law only relates special values of galois-conjugate functions.

To be more specific, an algebraic Witt vector
\xi\in E_{K}
, viewed as a function on
DR_{K}
, is locally constant and thus factors through a finite quotient
DR_{K}\twoheadrightarrow DR_{\mathfrak{f}}
for some conductor
\mathfrak{f}
; this finite monoid
DR_{\mathfrak{f}}
has a natural action of its unit group
DR_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\times}
isomorphic to the ray class group
C_{\mathfrak{f}}
, and then, has a
C_{\mathfrak{f}}
-orbit decomposition of the form
DR_{\mathfrak{f}}=\coprod_{\mathfrak{d}\mid\mathfrak{f}}C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}
; the
G_{K}
-equivariance of
\xi
together with
K^{ab}
-valuedness implies that
\xi
is a function
\coprod_{\mathfrak{d}}C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}\rightarrow K^{ab}
such that, when restricted on each component
\xi|_{C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}}:C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}\rightarrow K^{ab}
, its value
\xi(\sigma)
at
\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}
is equal to
\xi(1)^{\sigma}
and thus determined only by its value at the unit
1\in C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}
. This way, the values of
\xi
within a single component
C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}
are correlated by galois conjugate; but
\xi
being an algebraic Witt vector, the values of
\xi
on the whole
DR_{\mathfrak{f}}=\coprod_{\mathfrak{d}}C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}
(i.e. those on different components too) must be correlated so that the distribution of the values of
\xi
on the whole
DR_{\mathfrak{f}}=\coprod_{\mathfrak{d}}C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}
are
arithmetically smooth
(i.e. infinitely differentiable with respect to arithmetic derivatives) up to fixed denominator (i.e.
\xi
multiplied by some integer gets arithmetically smooth). In this sense, an algebraic Witt vector
\xi
is a data consisting, generally, not only of galois-conjugate values but connecting (or smoothly
interpolating
) non-galois conjugate values too, where ‘‘smooth’’ is in the sense of arithmetic derivative.
Phrased in these specific terms, the algebraic Witt vectors arising from Yalkinoglu’s construction consist only of galois-conjugate values (= special values of galois conjugate Siegel modular functions) by his definition, and thus, in the above general sense, do not give interpolation of special values of non-galois-conjugate modular functions; on the other hand, our construction induces such interpolations in a natural way.

That said, it should be also recognized that Yalkinoglu’s work has his own goal that belongs to a natural research context on Bost-Connes systems, and for this goal, his construction was sufficient; our above argument is just to see that, reviewing Yalkinoglu’s work in the above specific way, our research subject (interpolations of special values of non-galois-conjugate Siegel modular functions) emerges naturally; solidifying this subject itself, as a sequel to our previous work
[
19
]
, is also a major theme of the current work.

Technically speaking, it seems characteristic that, in our construction of algebraic Witt vectors (i.e. modular vectors) by special values of Siegel modular functions (living at different levels), moduli interpretations of Siegel modular varieties (or Siegel upper-half space
\mathfrak{H}_{g}
) play an intrinsic key role, which is also in a sharp contrast to Yalkinoglu’s work. In fact, as mentioned above, our modular vectors over CM fields (including imaginary quadratic fields) are constructed by choosing specific generators of the field
\mathfrak{F}
of modular functions, namely, Fricke functions
f_{a}
(
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) and ratios
\theta^{k/l}_{a}
(
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{2g}/\mathbb{Z}^{2g}
) of theta functions (cf. §
3
); and these specific modular functions have natural moduli interpretation that they represent coordinates of torsion points of (projectively embedded) elliptic curves and abelian varieties. Based on this choice of generator, we can deform modular functions by deforming the indices
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{2g}/\mathbb{Z}^{2g}
(cf. §
3
) of the generators (i.e.
f_{a}
and
\theta^{k/l}_{a}
), which correspond to descending the levels of torsion points of abelian varieties; our modular vectors are constructed by special values of thus deformed modular functions. In a sense, the reason why the values of our modular vectors
\xi:\coprod_{\mathfrak{d}\mid\mathfrak{d}}C_{\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{d}}\rightarrow K^{ab}
can be correlated even among different components is that their values are controlled uniformly by this well-behaved deformation family
\theta^{k/l}_{a}
; in fact we can even characterize what algebraic Witt vectors are generated by our modular vectors, by geometry of abelian varieties, thanks to their geometric origin (cf. §
4
).

In the case of imaginary quadratic fields, the current work also overlaps with our own previous work
[
19
]
; in particular, we now have two classes of modular vectors, i.e. those by Fricke functions
f_{a}
and those by the ratios
\theta^{k/l}_{a}
of theta functions. Although the latter cannot generate all algebraic Witt vectors over CM field of higher degree in general, we can see by our result in §
4
that, at least in the case of imaginary quadratic fields, the latter too can generate all algebraic Witt vectors, as in the former case
[
19
]
; so, in view of the fact that the latter makes sense for any CM fields, modular vectors by the ratios of theta functions have a theoretical advantage/canonicity; in fact, our choice of ratios of theta functions for general CM case is a natural consequence of Mumford’s general theory of projective embeddings of abelian varieties
[
11
]
and Shimura’s theory of complex multiplication
[
13
,
14
]
. Also as Iwao Kimura has told us in the context of our work
[
6
]
with Yasuhiro Ishitsuka on the decidability of integral Witt vectors over imaginary quadratic fields, numerical computations of special values of Fricke functions can be made fast by representing them by theta series
[
7
]
. In view of this, the modular vectors by ratios of theta functions may also have a computational advantage even for the case of imaginary quadratic fields only.
Also, in the case of CM fields, Streng
[
15
]
has developed some computational results on Shimura’s reciprocity law for Siegel modular functions, which is also relevant to the current work (§
3
–§
4
); in particular, Streng’s result may be helpful for concrete numerical computations on our general theory of modular vectors developed below.

Witt vectors as functions on

As outlined in §

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}\sim_{\mathfrak{f}}\mathfrak{b}

\displaystyle\exists t\in K_{+}\cap(1+\mathfrak{f}\mathfrak{b}^{-1}),\hskip 5.69046pt\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{-1}=(t),

\displaystyle DR_{K}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\lim_{\leftarrow\mathfrak{f}\in I_{K}}DR_{\mathfrak{f}}

By definition we have a canonical monoid homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{K}}(DR_{K},K^{ab})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{K}}(DR_{K},K^{ab})

(2.3)

\displaystyle\xi(*)

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle\xi(\mathfrak{p}\cdot*)

With these operators

.

These functions
\widehat{f}_{a}:DR_{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are equal to those in
[
19
]
, hence define locally constant
K^{ab}
-valued
G_{K}
-equivariant functions on
DR_{K}
, or equivalently, modular vectors

.

Proof.

The only slight difference from the original construction is that, in the above construction, we did not re-scale the lattice

into the form
\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z}
with

; therefore, the lattice

2.2
Basic facts on CM fields

Now we prepare some basic results necessary to extend the above construction of modular vectors to the case of CM fields. In this process, as outlined in §
1
too, we replace elliptic curves with abelian varieties of higher dimension; accordingly, we need to recall projective embedding of complex tori of higher dimension by theta functions
[
11
]
, which replaces projective embedding of one-dimensional complex tori
\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}w_{1}+\mathbb{Z}w_{2})
by the Weierstrass
\wp
-function
\wp
(and
\wp^{\prime}
). Starting from a few basic facts on CM fields, this subsection gathers some results related to projective embedding of complex tori with complex multiplication in CM fields; for more details on these topics, the reader is referred to e.g.
[
1
,
9
,
13
,
14
]
.

2.2.1
CM fields and Riemann forms

When
K
is an imaginary quadratic field, the complex tori
\mathbb{C}/sO_{K}\simeq K_{\infty}/sO_{K}
are polarizable for any
s\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{K,f}
, which is not the case for complex tori of higher dimension in general. However, when
K
is a CM field (say, of degree
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2g
), the complex tori
\mathbb{C}^{g}/sO_{K}\simeq K_{\infty}/sO_{K}
are always polarizable, hence, can be embedded into projective space as abelian varieties.

To be precise, let
K
be a CM field of degree
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2g
; also, fix a
CM type

\Phi=\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{g}\}
. That is,
\phi_{i}:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
are embeddings that are neither equal nor conjugate to each other; therefore, denoting by
\bar{\phi}_{i}:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
the complex conjugate of
\phi_{i}
, the set
\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{g},\bar{\phi}_{1},\cdots,\bar{\phi}_{g}\}
is precisely that of all embeddings
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. We abusively use the same symbol
\Phi:K\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{g}
to denote also the map
\Phi:K\ni a\mapsto(\phi_{1}(a),\cdots,\phi_{g}(a))\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
; by this map
\Phi
, the
O_{K}
-modules
sO_{K}:=s\widehat{O}_{K}\cap K\subseteq K
for
s\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{K,f}
are embedded into
\mathbb{C}^{g}
as lattices
\Lambda_{s}:=\Phi(sO_{K})\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{g}
, which then induce the complex tori
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda_{s}
.
The first fact necessary in this paper is that the complex tori
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda_{s}
are
polarizable
in the following sense, for any
s\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{K,f}
of the CM field
K
:

Definition 2.2.1

(polarization on complex tori)
.

For a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{g}
, the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
is said to be
polarizable
if there exists an alternating
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear form
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{g}\times\mathbb{C}^{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that:

1.

\psi(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
u,v\in\Lambda
;

2.

\psi(J(u),J(v))=\psi(u,v)
for any
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
;

3.

\psi(u,J(u))>0
for any non-zero
u\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
;

where
J:\mathbb{C}^{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{g}
is the standard complex structure. Such a form
\psi
is called a (non-degenerate)
Riemann form
on the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
.

The fact that the complex tori
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda_{s}
are polarizable is based on a few structural properties of the CM field
K
(e.g. Theorem 4, §6.2
[
13
]
): Being a CM field,
K
is by definition a totally imaginary quadratic extension of its maximal totally real subfield
K_{0}
; take a totally imaginary element
e\in K
such that
-e^{2}
is a totally positive element of
K_{0}
and
\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{i}(e))>0
for all
\phi_{i}\in\Phi
, where
\mathrm{Im}(z)
denotes the imaginary part of
z\in\mathbb{C}
. With this
e
, define
\psi_{e}:\mathbb{C}^{g}\times\mathbb{C}^{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by:

\displaystyle\psi_{e}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{g}\phi_{i}(e)\cdot(u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{u}_{i}v_{i})

(2.9)

for
u=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{g}),v=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{g})\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
. In particular, for each
a,b\in K
, we have:

\displaystyle\psi_{e}(\Phi(a),\Phi(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(e\cdot a\cdot b^{\iota}),

(2.10)

where
\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the trace over
\mathbb{Q}
; and
\iota\in Gal(K/K_{0})
denotes the non-trivial element of order 2. In particular, this means that, the
O_{K}
-module
sO_{K}
for any
s\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{K,f}
being a fractional ideal of
K
, we have
n_{s}\cdot\psi_{e}(sO_{K},sO_{K})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
for some integer
n_{s}
; and then, this
\psi:=n_{s}\cdot\psi_{e}
gives a Riemann form on the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda_{s}
.

Remark 2.2.2

(symplectic basis and type)
.

Given any Riemann form
\psi
on a complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
in general, the lattice
\Lambda
has a
symplectic basis
, which is a basis of the lattice
\Lambda
over
\mathbb{Z}
by which the Riemann form
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{g}\times\mathbb{C}^{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
can be expressed by the matrix of the form:

\displaystyle\psi(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&\delta\\
-\delta&0\end{pmatrix}\cdot v^{t}

(2.11)

where
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
in the left-hand side are re-represented in the right-hand side as real (row) vectors
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{2g}
with respect to the symplectic basis of
\Lambda
; also
\delta\in M_{g}(\mathbb{Z})
is a matrix of the form:

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&d_{2}&&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&d_{g}\end{pmatrix}

(2.12)

with
0<d_{i}
and
d_{i}\mid d_{i+1}
; in the following we denote
\delta=\mathrm{diag}[d_{1},\cdots,d_{g}]
. This
\delta
is independent of the choice of symplectic basis of
\Lambda
for the Riemann form
\psi
; thus it makes sense to say that the Riemann form
\psi
on the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
is
of type
\delta
.

2.2.2
Projective embedding

By the above fact that the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda_{s}
is polarizable for any
s\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{K,f}
of the CM field
K
with a fixed CM type
\Phi
, it follows that the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda_{s}
can be isomorphically embedded as an abelian variety in a suitable projective space
{\bf P}^{d}(\mathbb{C})
. Such an embedding can be concretely provided by
theta functions

[
11
]
, which depends on a choice of Riemann form on the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda_{s}
and its type.

To see such embeddings and theta functions explicitly, take a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{g}
such that we have a Riemann form
\psi
on the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
of type
\delta=\mathrm{diag}[d_{1},\cdots,d_{g}]
; then the lattice
\Lambda
has a symplectic basis
(\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{g},\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{g})=:(\tau,\delta)\in M_{g\times 2g}(\mathbb{C})
so that
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{g}\cdot\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\cdot\delta
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{g}
consists of row vectors and
\tau_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{g},\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}
are column vectors. Here the
g\times g
matrix
\tau\in M_{g}(\mathbb{C})
is in fact a member of the
Siegel upper-half space
\mathfrak{H}_{g}
defined by:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{g}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\biggl\{\tau\in M_{g}(\mathbb{C})\mid\tau^{t}=\tau,\mathrm{Im}(\tau)>0\biggr\}

(2.13)

where
\mathrm{Im}(\tau)>0
means that the imaginary part
\mathrm{Im}(\tau)\in M_{g}(\mathbb{R})
of
\tau
is positive definite. We note conversely that, given
\tau\in\mathfrak{H}_{g}
, one obtains a lattice
\Lambda_{\tau}:=\mathbb{Z}^{g}\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\delta
such that the complex torus
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda_{\tau}
has a Riemann form
\psi_{\tau}
of type
\delta
, given by
\psi_{\tau}(u,v):=\mathrm{Im}(u\cdot(\mathrm{Im}(\tau))^{-1}\cdot\bar{v}^{t})
; then
(\tau,\delta)
gives a symplectic basis of
\Lambda_{\tau}
for this Riemann form
\psi_{\tau}
. Therefore, it follows that the complex tori
\mathbb{C}^{g}/\Lambda
with Riemann forms
\psi
of type
\delta
are precisely those of the form
\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\delta)
for some
\tau\in\mathfrak{H}_{g}
(cf. e.g. §8.1
[
1
]
).

Now we are ready for projective embeddings of the complex tori
\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\delta)
with Riemann forms
\psi
of type
\delta
by the following
theta functions
:

Definition 2.2.3

(theta function
\theta^{k}
)
.

For each
k\in\delta^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}/\mathbb{Z}^{g}
, define
\theta^{k}:\mathbb{C}^{g}\times\mathfrak{H}_{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as follows:

\displaystyle\theta^{k}(u;\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in k+\mathbb{Z}^{g}}{\bf e}(\frac{w^{t}\cdot\tau\cdot w}{2}+u\cdot w),

(2.14)

where
{\bf e}(t):=e^{2\pi it}
; also
u\in\mathbb{C}^{g}
are row vectors, while
k\in\delta^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}/\mathbb{Z}^{g}
and
w\in k+\mathbb{Z}^{g}
are supposed to be column vectors.

The theta functions
\theta^{k}
define projective embedding of complex tori of type
\delta=\mathrm{diag}[d_{1},\cdots,d_{g}]
when
3\leq d_{1}
in the following manner:

Definition 2.2.4

(projective embedding)
.

Put
d:=|\delta^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}/\mathbb{Z}^{g}|-1=d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{g}
- 1; then for each
\tau\in\mathfrak{H}_{g}
, we can define
\Theta_{\tau}:\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\delta)\rightarrow{\bf P}^{d}(\mathbb{C})
by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\delta)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\Theta_{\tau}}

\displaystyle{\bf P}^{d}(\mathbb{C})

(2.15)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle[\theta^{k}(u;\tau)]_{k\in\delta^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}/\mathbb{Z}^{g}}

(2.16)

where
[\theta^{k}]_{k\in\delta^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{g}/\mathbb{Z}^{g}}
denotes the homogeneous coordinate on the projective space
{\bf P}^{d}(\mathbb{C})
; by our assumption on
\delta
, this map
\Theta_{\tau}:\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\delta)\rightarrow{\bf P}^{d}(\mathbb{C})
defines a projective embedding, whose image
\Theta_{\tau}(\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\delta))\subseteq{\bf P}^{d}(\mathbb{C})
is an abelian variety and will be denoted
A_{\tau}\subseteq{\bf P}^{d}(\mathbb{C})
, thus
\Theta_{\tau}:\mathbb{C}^{g}/(\mathbb{Z}^{g}\tau+\mathbb{Z}^{g}\delta)\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{\tau}
.

2.2.3
Complex multiplication

In the case when
K
is an imaginary quadratic field, the special value
f(\tau)
of a modular function on
\mathfrak{H}
(rational over
\mathbb{Q}^{ab}
) at
\tau\in K\cap\mathfrak{H}
is abelian over
K
; Shimura’s reciprocity law then shows that the galois conjugate
(f(\tau))^{[s]^{-1}}
of this special value
f(\tau)
for
s\in\mathbb{A}_{K,f}^{\times}
is equal to the special value
f^{q_{\tau}(s)}(\tau)
of a suitably defined galois conjugate modular function
f^{q_{\tau}(s)}
(cf. Theorem 6.18
[
12
]
). In terms of elliptic curves, this reciprocity law is proved using the following
main theorem of complex multiplication
:

Theorem 2.2.5

(main theorem of complex multiplication, Theorem 5.4
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a fractional ideal of an imaginary quadratic field
K\subseteq\mathbb{C}
; and
\Theta:\mathbb{C}/\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow E_{\mathfrak{a}}
an isomorphism of the complex torus
\mathbb{C}/\mathfrak{a}
to an elliptic curve
E_{\mathfrak{a}}
. Then, for any automorphism
\sigma:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
over
K
with
\sigma|_{K^{ab}}=[s]^{-1}
for some
s\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{K,f}
, there exists a unique isomorphism
\Theta^{\prime}:\mathbb{C}/s\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow E^{\sigma}
such that the following diagram commutates:

\textstyle{K/\mathfrak{a}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}/\mathfrak{a}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Theta}
\textstyle{E_{\mathfrak{a}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{K/s\mathfrak{a}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}/s\mathfrak{a}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Theta^{\prime}}
\textstyle{E_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\sigma}}

(2.17)

In the case when
K
is a CM field of higher degree in general, a similar theorem still holds, where elliptic curves are replaced with abelian varieties of higher dimension; but in order to state it in the case of degree greater than
2
, we need to consider not only
K
itself but also its
reflex field

K^{*}
for a CM type of
K
:

Definition 2.2.6

(reflex field)
.

Let
K
be a CM field of degree
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2g
and
\Phi=\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{g}\}
a CM type of
K
. Then the reflex field
K^{*}
of the CM type
(K,\Phi)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle K^{*}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{g}\phi_{i}(e)\mid e\in K\biggr).

4
Galois correspondence

Unlike the case of imaginary quadratic fields, the modular vectors constructed above do not generate all algebraic Witt vectors over a CM field
K
of higher degree; therefore, it becomes reasonable to ask what algebraic Witt vectors are generated by our modular vectors. Denoting by
M_{K}
the set of modular vectors, we can see by definition that
M_{K}
is in fact a subalgebra of
E_{K}
over
K
; moreover, it can be easily seen that
M_{K}
is a
\Lambda
-subalgebra of
E_{K}
(i.e. closed under the action of
\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}

[
19
]
. Therefore, the
general galois correspondence
mentioned in Remark 3.1.8
[
19
]
implies that we have a dual characterization of modular vectors in terms of profinite quotient of
DR_{K}
.

This section is devoted to this problem and divided into two subsections. In the first subsection (§
4.1
), we give a proof of the general galois correspondence mentioned in Remark 3.1.8
[
19
]
without proof, which claims a canonical bijective correspondence between (i)
\Lambda
-subalgebras of
E_{K}
, (ii) profinite quotients of
DR_{K}
, and (iii) semi-galois full subcategories of
\mathscr{C}_{K}
. This general correspondence, however, is not quite satisfactory in itself in that it just tells us only an existence of such a quotient but not its explicit structure. To remedy this issue, the second subsection (§
4.2
) is devoted to an explicit characterization of modular vectors, where we give an adelic description of the profinite quotient of
DR_{K}
that corresponds to the
\Lambda
-subalgebra of modular vectors

under our general galois correspondence.

Since the modular vectors constitute a

-subalgebra

As the first step, we define finite quotients

(Lemma

in a purely adelic term, which provides us a

).

Remark 4.2.7
.

When
E
is imaginary quadratic, the above results readily imply yet another form of the modularity theorem (cf. Theorem 4.3.1
[
19
]
); that is, the modular vectors constructed by ratios of theta functions also exhaust all algebraic Witt vectors as in the case of those constructed by Fricke functions.
To see this it suffices to show that
[\rho,s]\sim_{N}[\lambda,t]
if and only if
[\rho,s]=[\lambda,t]\in DR_{NO_{K}}
, in other words,
D_{N}
is isomorphic to
DR_{NO_{K}}
.

Since
E
is imaginary quadratic, note first that its reflex field
K=E^{*}
coincides with
E
itself; also the reflex norm
\eta:\mathbb{A}_{K,f}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{E,f}
is the identity. Then Theorem
4.2.6
says that
[\rho,s]\sim_{N}[\lambda,t]
if and only if there exists some
u\in(O_{K}/NO_{K})^{\times}
such that
\rho u=\lambda
in
O_{K}/NO_{K}
and
s^{-1}t\in T_{\rho,u}
; but as in the proof of Lemma
4.2.4
, one can also see that
s^{-1}t\in T_{\rho,u}
if and only if the actions of
[s]
and
[ut]
on the subset
A_{K}[S_{\rho}]
of the
N
-torsion points on the elliptic curve
A_{K}
coincide, or equivalently,
[s]=[ut]
in the ray class group
C_{\mathfrak{d}_{\rho}}
for the conductor
\mathfrak{d}_{\rho}:=NO_{K}/(\rho O_{K},NO_{K})
. That is, the finite monoid
D_{N}
is isomorphic to
(O_{K}/NO_{K})\times_{(O_{K}/NO_{K})^{\times}}C_{NO_{K}}=DR_{NO_{K}}
, hence the claim.

In this sense, Theorem
4.2.6
can be seen as a natural generalization of our previous modularity theorem for algebraic Witt vectors over imaginary quadratic fields (Theorem 4.3.1
[
19
]
) to the case of (primitive) CM fields; in particular, we showed that the use of ratios of theta functions instead of Fricke functions enables this higher-dimensional extension in a natural way, and also highlights the geometric nature of the modularity theorem in the sense that we discussed in §
3
.

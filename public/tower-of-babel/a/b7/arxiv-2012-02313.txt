Periodic solutions for one-dimensional nonlinear nonlocal problem with drift including singular nonlinearities
Lisbeth Carrero, Alexander Quaas

Abstract
In this paper, we prove existence results of a one-dimensional periodic solution to equations with the fractional Laplacian of order s ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) s\in(1/2,1) , singular nonlinearity, and gradient term under various situations, including nonlocal contra-part of classical Lienard vector equations, as well other nonlocal versions of classical results know only in the context of second-order ODE. Our proofs are based on degree theory and Perron’s method, so before that we need to establish a variety of priori estimates under different assumptions on the non-linearities appearing in the equations. Besides, we obtain also multiplicity results in a regime where a priori bounds are lost and bifurcation from infinity occurs.

The fractional Laplacian operator can be defined for

Before describing our main results, notice that the type of equations studied in

u^{\prime\prime}(t)+f(u(t))u^{\prime}(t)+g(u(t))=e(t)\;\;\;\;t\in\mathbb{R},

Proposition 1.2

The semi-linear periodic problems

In this section, first we will prove some preliminary results that will facilitate the proof of Theorem

1.2

Notice that by Proposition
1.2
of
[
8
]
the fractional Laplacian of a smooth,

-periodic function reduces to

(\triangle)^{s}u(t)=-\mathcal{L}u(t)\;\;\;t\in(0,2\pi),

(2.1)

where

\mathcal{L}u(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(u(t)-u(y))K(t-y)dy,\;\;\;t\in(0,2\pi),

(2.2)

and

K(z)=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{|z-2\pi n|^{1+2s}},\;\;\;0<|z|<2\pi.

We consider the space
X
defined in
[
8
]
as the closure of the set of
2\pi
-periodic functions
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})

with the norm

\|u\|_{X}:=\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(u(t)-u(y))^{2}K(t-y)dydt+\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}u^{2}(t)dt\right)^{\dfrac{1}{2}},

where
X
is a Hilbert space when provided with the inner product

<u,v>:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(u(t)-u(y))(v(t)-v(y))K(t-y)dydt+\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}u(t)v(t)dt.

The space
X
possesses good embedding properties which follow directly from the trivial relation
\|u\|_{H^{s}(0,2\pi)}\leq\|u\|_{X}
for
u\in X
.

Consider now the family of equations

(\triangle)^{s}v(t)+\lambda f(v(t))v^{\prime}(t)=\lambda w(t)\quad\lambda\in(0,1).

(2.3)

We want to establish Schauder type estimates for equation (
2.3
), but for this we need first a

by the fact that

\displaystyle v=\bar{v}+u\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad w=\bar{w}+e,

which implies that
\bar{u}=0
and
\bar{e}=0

(\triangle)^{s}u(t)+\lambda f(C+u(t))u^{\prime}(t)=\lambda e(t),

where

.
With that change we have the following Lemma.

.

Let

) with

\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}[0,2\pi]}\leq K\|e\|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi])},

is a

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(-\triangle)^{s}uu-\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}f(C+u)u^{\prime}u=-\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}eu.

Then

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}f(C+u(x))u^{\prime}(t)u(t)dx

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{d}{dt}(H^{\prime}(C+u(t)))u(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u]_{H^{s}(0,2\pi)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|e(t)u(t)|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\|e\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, we claim that we have the following Poincaré type inequality. There exist

\displaystyle 2\pi|u(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|u(t)-u(y)|^{2}dy

The following result is a basic property of our interest, where we will need the constant
C(1,s)
which precisely given by

C(1,s)=\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1-\cos(\xi_{1})}{|\xi|^{1+2s}}d\xi\right)^{-1}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u
a
2\pi
-periodic function, then

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(-\triangle)^{s}u(x)dx=0.

Proof:

Indeed, as
u
is a periodic function, we can write its Fourier series:

u(x)=a_{0}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}\cos(nx)+b_{n}\sin(nx).

Then,

\displaystyle(-\triangle)^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1,s}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\cos(nx)-\cos(ny)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}dy+b_{n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\sin(nx)-\sin(ny)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}dy\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1,s}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\cos(nx)-\cos(nx-nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz+b_{n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\sin(nx)-\sin(nx-nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\right),

so

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\cos(nx)-\cos(nx-nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\cos(nx)-\cos(nx)\cos(nz)-\sin(nx)\sin(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1-\cos(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\cos(nx)-\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\sin(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\sin(nx),

where the second integral of the above is zero because we are integrating an odd function in a symmetric (with respect to zero) domain.
Similarly to the previous computation, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\sin(nx)-\sin(nx-nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\sin(nx)-\sin(nx)\cos(nz)+\cos(nx)\sin(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1-\cos(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\sin(nx)-\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\sin(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\cos(nx).

Hence substituting this in (
2
) gives

\displaystyle(-\triangle)^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1,s}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1-\cos(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\cos(nx)+b_{n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1-\cos(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\sin(nx)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1-\cos(\xi_{1})}{|\xi|^{n+2s}}d\xi\right)^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1-\cos(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\cos(nx)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+b_{n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1-\cos(nz)}{|z|^{1+2s}}dz\sin(nx)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}n^{2s}\cos(nx)+b_{n}n^{2s}\sin(nx).

Hence,

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(-\triangle)^{s}u(x)dx=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}n^{2s}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(nx)dx+b_{n}n^{2s}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(nx)dx=0.

\blacksquare

Lemma 4.2

Limit shapes for skew Howe duality
Dan Betea \addressmark 1, Anton Nazarov \addressmark 2, Travis Scrimshaw \addressmark 3

Abstract
We study large random partitions boxed into a rectangle and coming from skew Howe duality, or alternatively from dual Schur measures. As the sides of the rectangle go to infinity, we obtain: 1) limit shape results for the profiles generalizing the Vershik–Kerov–Logan–Shepp curve; and 2) universal edge asymptotic results for the first parts in the form of the Tracy–Widom distribution, as well as less-universal critical regime results introduced by Gravner, Tracy and Widom. We do this for a large class of Schur parameters going beyond the Plancherel or principal specializations previously studied in the literature, parametrized by two real valued functions f f and g g . Connections to a Bernoulli model of (last passage) percolation are explored.

Introduction

Motivation.

The study of random partitions has been, for the past 50 years, a fruitful area of research at the interface of algebraic combinatorics, representation theory, probability, and mathematical physics.
Two cornerstone results are the limit shape result of Vershik–Kerov–Logan–Shepp and the Baik–Deift–Johansson Theorem providing random matrix-like asymptotic fluctuations for the longest increasing subsequence of random permutations.
See
[
10
, Ch. 1 and 2]
and references therein.

In this extended abstract we generalize both of the above by looking at a class of measures called dual Schur measures with arbitrary parameters.
Such measures fall into the category of Schur measures first introduced by Okounkov
[
9
]
, and we use his techniques along the way.
The novelty here is twofold.
On one hand, we derive our asymptotic results keeping the two parameter sets generic and settle on a class indexed by two real functions.
On the other hand, because we are using dual Schur measures, the partitions are forced to be contained within a certain rectangle, and we see this introduces special edge behavior in the so-called critical scaling regime case (discovered by Gravner, Tracy, and Widom in
[
2
]
), while allowing for universal fluctuations of the Tracy–Widom GUE type away from this regime.
As our parameters are reasonably generic, this can be seen as a form of universality.

Combinatorial description.

The Cauchy identity is a classical identity in algebraic combinatorics that has a representation-theoretic interpretation as characters computed in two different ways on the

Howe duality

Using this, we can form a probability measure on partitions, a form of Okounkov’s Schur measure
[

The Schur measure and the associated combinatorics have been well-studied from various perspectives and with a broad range of applications (

In particular, it is a quintessential example of a determinantal point process, where the joint correlation kernel is described by a determinant of pairwise correlations.

There is another

Free fermions and finite-size correlations

We briefly recall the free fermion Fock space and half vertex operators as a method to construct the skew Schur functions as matrix elements.
Using this, we derive the correlation kernel though an application Wick’s theorem.

A

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)

\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i+1}\geq 0

Fermionic Fock space
(of charge
0

is a span of certain vectors indexed by partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dotsc)

\lvert\lambda\rangle=v_{a_{1}}\wedge v_{a_{2}}\wedge v_{a_{3}}\cdots

(a_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i+\frac{1}{2})_{i=1}^{\infty}

Maya sequence
of

These vectors are obtained from the vacuum vector
\lvert 0\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\wedge-\frac{3}{2}\wedge-\frac{5}{2}\wedge\cdots
by the sequence of actions of the fermionic creation operators
\psi_{m}
that adjoin

to the Maya sequence with the proper sign, and fermionic annihilation operators
\psi^{*}_{m}
that remove

They satisfy the canonical anti-commutation relations
\{\psi_{m},\psi^{*}_{\ell}\}=\delta_{m,\ell}
.
4
4

4

Here
\{a,b\}=ab+ba
, and all other anti-commutators vanish:
\{\psi_{m},\psi_{\ell}\}=\{\psi^{*}_{m},\psi^{*}_{\ell}\}=0

Let
\langle\mu\rvert

\langle\mu|\lambda\rangle=\delta_{\mu,\lambda}
.
From these operators we construct their generating series
\psi(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2}\psi_{m}z^{m},\psi^{*}(w)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2}\psi^{*}_{m}w^{-m}

\Gamma_{\pm}(X)=\exp\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{p_{j}(x)\alpha_{\pm j}}{j}

p_{j}(X)=\sum_{i}x_{i}^{j}

\alpha_{j}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2}\psi_{m-j}\psi_{m}^{*}

The following properties are useful:

\begin{gathered}[\Gamma_{+}(X),\Gamma_{+}(Y)]=[\Gamma_{-}(X),\Gamma_{-}(Y)]=1,\quad[\Gamma_{+}(X),\Gamma_{-}(Y)]=H(X;Y)\\
\Gamma_{+}(X)\lvert 0\rangle=\lvert 0\rangle,\qquad\langle 0\rvert\Gamma_{-}(Y)=\langle 0\rvert,\\
\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma_{\pm}(X)}\psi(z)=H(X;z^{\pm})\psi(z),\quad\mathrm{Ad}_{\Gamma_{\pm}(X)}\psi^{\dagger}(w)=H(X;w^{\pm 1})^{-1}\psi^{\dagger}(w),\\
\langle 0\rvert\psi(z)\psi^{\dagger}(w)\lvert 0\rangle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}z^{-1/2-\ell}w^{1/2+\ell}=\frac{\sqrt{zw}}{z-w}\quad\text{for }\lvert z\rvert<\lvert w\rvert\end{gathered}

(2.1)

where

H(X;Y):=\prod_{i,j}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}
,
[u,v]:=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}
, and
\mathrm{Ad}_{u}x:=uxu^{-1}
.
We denote

E(X;Y)=\prod_{i,j}(1+x_{i}y_{j})=H(X;-Y)^{-1}.

\langle\lambda\rvert\Gamma_{-}(X)\lvert 0\rangle=\langle 0\rvert\Gamma_{+}(X)\lvert\lambda\rangle\ =s_{\lambda}(X)

\langle\lambda\rvert\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}(X)\lvert 0\rangle=\langle 0\rvert\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}(Y)\lvert\lambda\rangle=s_{\lambda^{\prime}}(Y)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}(X)s_{\lambda^{\prime}}(Y)=\langle 0\rvert\Gamma_{+}(X)\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}(Y)\lvert 0\rangle=E(X;Y).

(2.2)

Asymptotics for the limit shape

We discuss how to obtain the limit shape alluded to in Theorem
1.1

We look at the behavior of

The limit density of particles turns out to be well-defined and gives the desired limit curve

(Bulk asymptotics)

4
Boundary asymptotics

To study boundary asymptotics, we zoom in around
x_{+}
and see what happens to the process locally.
In the asymptotic analysis described in the previous section, the two roots
z,\overline{z}
coincide to a double critical point we called
z_{+}
.

Sketch of proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Before we proceed further, we assume for simplicity
c
is such that
\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{ds}\,f(s)<c\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{ds}\,\frac{1}{g(s)}
.
This means,
a posteriori
, in the notation of Theorem
1.2
, that we are looking at fluctuations for
\lambda_{1}\ll k
.
In other words, the limit shape
\Omega(t)
is convex around
x_{+}
, and in turn
x_{+}<c
, so we do not have full support for
\Omega
.
In Figure
2
, we are dealing with a limit shape
sitting under the diagonal of the rectangle
.

Near the double critical point
z_{+}\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
we have
S(z)=S^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{+})(z-z_{+})^{3}/6+\mathcal{O}[(z-z_{+})^{4}]
.
Scaling around
z=z_{+}e^{\sigma\xi n^{-1/3}}\approx z_{+}(1+\sigma\xi n^{-1/3}+\cdots)
as
n\to\infty
, with
\sigma
a yet-to-be-determined constant, we see that we need to scale the matrix entries of
K(m,m^{\prime})
as
(m,m^{\prime})\approx x_{+}n+(\xi,\eta)n^{1/3}\sigma^{-1}
. Let
\sigma^{-1}=2^{-1/3}S^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{+})^{1/3}z_{+}>0
.
Then one can show

n^{1/3}\mathcal{K}(m,m^{\prime})\to\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Airy}}(\xi,\eta):=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathbf{i})^{2}}\int_{i\mathbb{R}-\epsilon}\mathrm{d}\omega\int_{i\mathbb{R}+\epsilon}\mathrm{d}\zeta\,\frac{\exp(\zeta^{3}/3-\xi\zeta-\omega^{3}/3+\eta\omega)}{\zeta-\omega},

(4.1)

where
0<\epsilon\ll 1
and
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Airy}}(\xi,\eta)
is an integral representation of the Airy kernel
[
12
]
.

For a quick justification notice that

n\bigl(S(z)-S(w)\bigr)\approx\frac{1}{6}\sigma^{3}z_{+}^{3}S^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{+})(\zeta^{3}-\omega^{3})=\frac{1}{6}\sigma^{3}\left(\left.(z\partial_{z})^{3}S(z)\right|_{z=z_{+}}\right)(\zeta^{3}-\omega^{3})

(4.2)

for
z=z_{+}e^{\sigma\xi n^{-1/3}},w=z_{+}e^{\sigma\eta n^{-1/3}}
in the vicinity of
z_{+}
, and we are therefore interested in

(z\partial_{z})^{3}S(z)\bigg|_{z=z_{+}}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{ds}\left(\frac{2f^{2}(s)z_{+}^{2}}{(1-f(s)z_{+})^{3}}+\frac{2cg(s)z_{+}^{2}}{(z_{+}+g(s))^{3}}\right),

(4.3)

to determine
\sigma
.
Assuming this integral converges, we can conclude the convergence from (
4.1
).
Further technical estimates along the same lines show that Fredholm determinants converge to Fredholm determinants, and the Tracy–Widom distribution is a Fredholm determinant of the Airy kernel on
\mathcal{L}^{2}(s,\infty)
.
Therefore we conclude that the distribution of
\frac{\lambda_{1}-x_{+}n}{\sigma^{-1}n^{1/3}}
is asymptotically the Tracy–Widom distribution GUE distribution. See Figure
3
(left) for an illustration.

Remark 4.1
.

In the case of Example
3.2
(
f\equiv\alpha,g\equiv 1
), we have
x_{\pm}=\frac{\alpha(c-1)\pm 2\sqrt{\alpha c}}{\alpha+1}
and
z_{+}=\frac{\alpha(c+1)-(\alpha+1)\sqrt{\alpha c}}{\alpha(\alpha c-1)}
.
This yields (recall
c,\alpha>0
)
\sigma=\frac{(\alpha+1)c^{\frac{1}{6}}}{\alpha^{\frac{1}{6}}(\sqrt{c}-\sqrt{\alpha})^{\frac{2}{3}}(1+\sqrt{\alpha c})^{\frac{2}{3}}}
.
We see that
\sigma<\infty
whenever
\alpha\neq c
.

Remark 4.2
.

Continuing on the previous remark,
\alpha=c
implies
\sigma=\infty

Formally this would yield an infinitely thin Tracy–Widom distribution; in practice, we obtain the critical case of Theorem
1.3
(first discussed in

, as the models are the same).
This happens when
\lambda_{1}
hits the east-most corner of the rectangle bounding it; from the point of view of asymptotic analysis, the critical point considered is

, a singularity of the action.

Sketch of proof of Theorem

.

Now we turn to Theorem
1.3
.
We land in this case when there is an obstruction to our asymptotic analysis of the generic case.
This happens when the double critical point
z_{+}
of the action under consideration is
z_{+}=0
.
In this situation the constant
\sigma=\infty
, and the integral in (
4.3
) diverges.
Combinatorially, it happens when
\lambda_{1}=nc-o(1)
(when
\lambda_{1}
just hits the east-most corner of the rectangle bounding it).
In terms of our functions
f
and
g

\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{ds}\,f(s)=c\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{ds}}{g(s)}.

(4.4)

Notice that
x_{+}=c
is a root of (
3.7
) for
z=0
, and then (
3.8
) gives the above condition.

Now we cannot pass the contours through the double critical point at
0
anymore (being a singularity of the action).
This also implies that our limiting distribution will be discrete: we will not scale the
m,m^{\prime}
coordinates continuously.
Instead we take
m=cn-h,m^{\prime}=cn-h^{\prime}
for some
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}+\tfrac{1}{2}
and consider the asymptotics of
K(m,m^{\prime})
as such.
The contours encircle
0
and satisfy
\lvert w\rvert<\lvert z\rvert
.
The
w

0
.
Since the contour for
z
encircles all
-y_{j}

1/x_{i}

-\mathbf{i}\infty
to
\mathbf{i}\infty
with a small bump to the right of
0
that contains contour for
w
.
We first integrate over
w
.
Since we have a critical point of the action at
0

S^{\prime}(0)=0
, we can approximate the action as
-nS(w)\approx-nS(0)-\frac{n}{2}S^{\prime\prime}(0)w^{2}
; we thus need to compute the integral
\oint_{C_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\frac{n}{2}S^{\prime\prime}(0)w^{2}}w^{-h^{\prime}}\frac{\sqrt{zw}}{z-w}\frac{\mathrm{dw}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}w}
.
As
\lvert w\rvert<\lvert z\rvert
, we can expand
\frac{1}{z-w}
and
e^{-\frac{n}{2}S^{\prime\prime}(0)w^{2}}
into power series in
w

\oint\limits_{C_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\mathrm{dw}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}w}e^{-\frac{n}{2}S^{\prime\prime}(0)w^{2}}w^{-h^{\prime}}\frac{\sqrt{zw}}{z-w}=\oint\limits_{C_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\mathrm{dw}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}w}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}n^{\ell}(S^{\prime\prime}(0))^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}\ell!}w^{2\ell}\right)w^{-h^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{j}}{z^{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

After the residue calculation we get
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\left\lfloor h^{\prime}/2-1/4\right\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}n^{\ell}(S^{\prime\prime}(0))^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}\cdot\ell!}z^{-h^{\prime}+2\ell}
.
To integrate over
z

z

\operatorname{Re}S(z)<0
and therefore as
n\to\infty

0

We again can approximate
nS(z)\approx nS(0)+\frac{n}{2}S^{\prime\prime}(0)z^{2}
and we cancel
nS(0)
from the
w

\int_{-\mathbf{i}\infty}^{\mathbf{i}\infty}\frac{\mathrm{dz}}{2\pi\mathbf{i}z}e^{\frac{n}{2}S^{\prime\prime}(0)z^{2}}z^{h}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\left\lfloor h^{\prime}/2-1/4\right\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}n^{\ell}(S^{\prime\prime}(0))^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}\cdot\ell!}z^{-h^{\prime}+2\ell}\right)
.
We do a substitution

; the new integration contour starts at
+\infty
, loops around
0

+\infty
and we have:

\mathcal{K}_{n}(h,h^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\left\lfloor h^{\prime}/2-1/4\right\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{(h-h^{\prime}+1)/2}2^{(h-h^{\prime})/2-1}}{(nS^{\prime\prime}(0))^{(h-h^{\prime})/2}\ell!}\int_{\infty}^{0^{+}}\mathrm{dt}\,t^{(h-h^{\prime})/2+\ell-1}e^{-t}.

(4.5)

If
(h-h^{\prime})+l\geq 0

\Gamma

\Gamma(z)=-\frac{1}{2\mathbf{i}\sin\pi z}\int_{\infty}^{0^{+}}\mathrm{dt}\,(-t)^{z-1}e^{-t}

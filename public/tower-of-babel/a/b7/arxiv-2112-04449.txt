Optimal Hardy-weights for the ( p , A ) (p,A) -Laplacian with a potential term
Idan Versano

Abstract
. We construct new optimal L p L^{p} Hardy-type inequalities for elliptic Schrödinger-type operators with a potential term. 2000 ​ Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 35A23; Secondary 35B09, 35J08, 35J25



1.
Introduction

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a positive definite matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, let

|\xi|_{A}:=\sqrt{\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Consider a second order half-linear operator of the form

Q_{p,A,V}(u):=-\mathrm{div}\,\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}_{A}A\nabla u\right)+V|u|^{p-2}u

defined in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and assume that
Q_{p,A,V}
admits a positive solution in
\Omega
.
We are interested to find an optimal weight function
W\gneqq 0
(see Definition
2.29
) such that the equation
Q_{p,A,V-W}(u)=0
admits a positive solution in
\Omega
.
Equivalently
[
19
, Theorem 4.3]
, we are interested to find an optimal weight function
W\gneqq 0
such that the following Hardy-type inequality is satisfied:

(1.1)

\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla\phi|_{A}^{p}+V|\phi|^{p})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}W|\phi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

In some definite sense, an optimal weight
W\gneqq 0
is ”as large as possible” nonnegative function such that (
1.1
) is satisfied for all nonnegative

\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

The search for Hardy-type inequalities with optimal weight
function
W
was originally proposed by Agmon, who raised this problem in connection with
his theory of exponential decay of Schrödinger eigenfunctions
[
1
, p. 6]
.
In the past four decades, the problem of improving Hardy-type inequalities has engaged many authors. In particular,
Hardy-type inequalities were established for a vast class of operators (e.g., elliptic operators, Schrödinger operators on graphs, fractional differential equations) with different types of boundary conditions, see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
22
]
.
In
[
9
]
, Devyver and Pinchover studied the problem of
optimal
weights for the operator

Q_{p,A,V}
. However, they managed to find optimal weights only in the case where
A
is the identity matrix and
V=0
. They proved (under certain assumptions )
that the
p
-Laplace operator,
-\mathrm{div}\,\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)
, admits an optimal Hardy-weight.
More specifically, it is proved that if
1<p\leq n
, then

W=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\left|\frac{\nabla G}{G}\right|^{p}
an optimal Hardy-weight,
where
G
is the associated positive minimal Green function with singularity at
0
. For
p>n
, several cases should be considered, depending on the behavior of a positive
p
-harmonic function with singularity at
0
.

In the present paper we make a nontrivial progress towards the study of (
1.1
) in the case where
A
is not necessarily the identity matrix, and
V
is a slowly growing potential function.
Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain and
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Let

Q_{p,A,V}
be a subcritical operator in
\Omega
satisfying Assumptions
2.8
in
\Omega
. Suppose that
Q_{p,A,V}
admits a (nonnegative) Green potential,
G_{\varphi}(x)
, in
\Omega
(see Definition
2.22
) satisfying

\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G_{\varphi}(x)=0;\quad\int_{\Omega}VG_{\varphi}(x)^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<0;\quad\int_{\Omega}|V|G_{\varphi}(x)^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

where
\overline{\infty}
denotes the ideal point in the one-point compactification of
\Omega
.
Then the operator
Q_{p,A,{V}/{c_{p}}}
admits an optimal Hardy-weight in
\Omega
, where
c_{p}=\big(p/(p-1)\big)^{p-1}
.

As a corollary of the proof of Theorem
1.1
we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain and
x_{0}\in K\Subset\Omega
. Let

Q_{p,A,V}
be a subcritical operator in
\Omega
satisfying Assumptions
2.8
with
V\leq 0
in
\Omega
. Suppose that
Q_{p,A,V}
admits a positive minimal Green function
G(x)
in
\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\}
(see Definition
2.22
) satisfying

(1.2)

\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G(x)=0,\quad\mbox{ and }\int_{\Omega\setminus K}|V||G(x)|^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

where
\overline{\infty}
denotes the ideal point in the one-point compactification of
\Omega
.
Then the operator
Q_{p,A,{V}/{c_{p}}}
admits an optimal Hardy-weight in
\Omega
, where
c_{p}=\big(p/(p-1)\big)^{p-1}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notation and recall some previously obtained results needed in the present paper. We proceed in Section
3
, with proving essential results needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and then we prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
.

2.1.
Gauss-Green formula

We continue with several definitions and results concerning the Gauss-Green theorem
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set.

(1)

We denote by
\mathcal{M}(D)
the space of all signed Radon measures
\mu
on
D
such that
\int_{D}\,\text{d}|\mu|<\infty
.

(2)

A vector field
F\in L^{\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called a divergence measure field, written as
F\in\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}(D)
, if
\mathrm{div}\,(F)=\mu\in\mathcal{M}(D)
, i.e., there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(D)
such that

\int_{D}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{D}\nabla\phi\cdot F\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\forall\,\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(D).

(3)

We say that a vector field
F\in L^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(D,\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(D)
if for any open subset
E\Subset D
, we have
F\in\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}(E)
.

Definition 2.2

(cf.
[
7
]
and
[
11
, Section 5]
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set.
A function
f\in L^{1}(D)
has a bounded variation in
D
if

\sup\left\{\int_{D}f\,\mathrm{div}\,(\phi)\,\mathrm{d}x:\phi\in C_{0}^{1}(D,\mathbb{R}^{n}),|\phi|\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

Denote by
\mathrm{BV}(D)
the space of all functions
f\in L^{1}(D)
having bounded variation.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set.
A measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be a set of finite perimeter in
D
if
\chi_{D}\in\mathrm{BV}(D)
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
11
, Theorem 5.9, p. 212]
)
.

Let
E\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
0\leq f\in\mathrm{BV}(E)\cap C^{1}(E)
. Then, for a.e.
t\in[0,\infty)
the set

\{x\in E:f(x)>t\}
has finite perimeter.
In particular, for a.e.
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}

the set

\{x\in E:t_{1}<f(x)<t_{2}\}
has finite perimeter.

We proceed with the following Gauss-Green theorem of divergence measure fields over sets of finite perimeter (see
[
7
, Theorems 5.2 and 7.2]
and
[
9
, Proposition 3.1]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. Suppose that

F\in\mathcal{DM}^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(D)
with
\mathrm{div}\,(F)=\mu\in\mathcal{M}(D)
. Let
E\Subset D
be a set of finite perimeter satisfying

•

\partial E=\left(\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D_{k}\right)\cup N
,

•

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
D_{k}
is
(n-1)
- dimensional
C^{1}
surface, and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(N)=0
.

Then,

\int_{E}\mathrm{div}\,(F)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial E}F\cdot\vec{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
\vec{n}
is a classical outer unit normal to
\partial E
which is defined
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
\partial E
.

2.2.
Local Morrey spaces

In the present subsection we introduce a certain class of Morrey spaces that depend on the index
p
, where
1<p<\infty
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty]
and
\omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For a measurable, real valued function
f
defined in
\omega
, we set

\|f\|_{M^{q}(\omega)}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\omega\\
r<\mathrm{diam}(\omega)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r^{n/q^{\prime}}}\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}(y)}|f|\,\mathrm{d}x.

We write
f\in M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
if for any
\omega\Subset\Omega
we have
\|f\|_{M^{q}(\omega)}<\infty
.

Next, we define a special local Morrey space
M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(p;\Omega)
which depends on the values of the exponent
p
.

Definition 2.7
.

For
p\neq n
, we define

M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(p;\Omega):=\begin{cases}M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)\mbox{~with~}q>n/p&\mbox{~if~}p<n\\
L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)&\mbox{~if~}p>n,\end{cases}

while for
p=n
,
f\in M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(n;\Omega)
means that for some
q>n
and any
\omega\Subset\Omega
we have

\|f\|_{M^{q}_{n;\Omega}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\omega\\
r<\mathrm{diam}(\omega)\end{subarray}}\varphi_{q}(r)\int_{\omega\cap B_{r}(y)}|f|\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

where
\varphi_{q}(r):=\log(\mathrm{diam}(\omega)/r)^{q/n^{\prime}}
and
0<r<\mathrm{diam}(\omega)
.

For the regularity theory of equations with coefficients in Morrey spaces we refer the reader to
[
18
,
19
]
.

We associate to any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
an
exhaustion
, i.e. a sequence of smooth, precompact domains
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

such that
\Omega_{1}\neq\emptyset
,

\Omega_{j}\Subset\Omega_{j+1}
and
\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{j}=\Omega
.

2.3.
Criticality theory for
Q_{p,A,V}

Let
1<p<\infty
, and consider the operator

(2.1)

Q_{p,A,V}(u):=-\Delta_{p,A}(u)+V\mathcal{I}_{p}(u),

defined on a domain

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
,
where
\Delta_{p,A}:=\mathrm{div}\,(|\nabla u|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla u)
and
\mathcal{I}_{p}(u):=|u|^{p-2}u
.
Unless otherwise stated, we always assume that the matrix
A
and the potential function
V
satisfy the following regularity assumptions:

Assumptions 2.8
.

•

A(x)\!=\!(a^{ij}(x))_{i,j=1}^{n}\in C^{\alpha}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}})
is a symmetric positive definite matrix which is locally
uniformly elliptic, that is, for any compact
K\Subset\Omega
there exists
\Theta_{K}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Theta_{K}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Theta_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}^{2}\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ and }\forall x\in K.

•

V\in M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(p;\Omega)
is a real valued function.

The associated energy functional for the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
is defined by

\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}^{\Omega}(\phi):=\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla\phi|_{A}^{p}+V|\phi|^{p})\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Definition 2.9
.

We say that
u\in W^{1,p}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
is a (weak) solution (resp. supersolution) of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
if for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
(resp.
0\leq\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}V|u|^{p-2}u\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=0\;(\mathrm{resp.}\geq 0).

It should be noted that the above definition makes sense due to the
following Morrey-Adams Theorem (see for example,
[
19
, Theorem 2.4]
and references therein).

Theorem 2.10

(Morrey-Adams theorem)
.

Let
\omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
V\in M^{q}(p;\omega)
.

(1)

There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q)>0
such that for any
\delta>0

\int_{\omega}|V||u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\delta\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\ \frac{C}{\delta^{n/(pq-n)}}\|V\|^{pq/(pq-n)}_{M^{q}(p;\omega)}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega)}\;\;\forall u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\omega).

(2)

For any
\omega^{\prime}\Subset\omega
with Lipschitz boundary, there exists a positive constant
C=C(n,p,q,\omega^{\prime},\omega,\delta,\|V\|_{M^{q}(p;\omega)})
and
\delta_{0}
such that for
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}

\int_{\omega^{\prime}}|V||u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\delta\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\omega^{\prime})}\quad\forall u\in W^{1,p}(\omega^{\prime}).

We denote the set of all positive solutions (resp., supersolutions) of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
by
\mathcal{C}^{Q_{p,A,V}}(\Omega)
(resp.,
\mathcal{K}^{Q_{p,A,V}}(\Omega)).

We say that the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
is
nonnegative
(in short
Q_{p,A,V}\geq 0
) in
\Omega
if

\mathcal{C}^{Q_{p,A,V}}\neq\emptyset
.

Remark 2.11
.

A weak (super)solution of the equation
-\Delta_{p,A}(u)=0
in
\Omega
is said to be a
(p,A)
-(super)harmonic function in
\Omega
.

It is well known that under Assumptions
2.8
any positive solution of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
belongs to
C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)
(see for example
[
19
, Remark 1.1]
).
Furthermore, the following
Harnack convergence principle holds true.

Proposition 2.12

(Harnack convergence principle
[
13
, Proposition 2.7]
)
.

Let
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an exhaustion of
\Omega
.
Assume that
\{A_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of symmetric and locally uniformly positive definite matrices
such that the local ellipticity constants does not depend on
k
, and
\{A_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega_{k},\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}})

converges weakly in
L^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}})
to a matrix
A\in L^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}})
. Assume further that
\{V_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(p;\Omega_{k})
converges weakly in
M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(p;\Omega)
to
V\in M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(p;\Omega)
.
For each
k
, let
v_{k}
be a positive solution of the equation
Q_{p,A_{k},V_{k}}(u)=0
in
\Omega_{k}
such that
v_{k}(x_{0})=1
, where
x_{0}
is a fixed reference point in
\Omega_{1}
.
Then there exists
0<\eta<1
such that, up to a subsequence,
\{v_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly in
W^{1,p}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
and in
C_{{\rm loc}}^{\beta}(\Omega)
to a positive weak solution
v
of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
(here
0<\beta<\alpha
).

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
A principal eigenvalue of
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
is an eigenvalue
\lambda
of the problem

\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(u)=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u&\mbox{in~}\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{on~}\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

with a nonzero nonnegative
u
which is called a principal eigenfunction.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
19
, Theorem 3.9]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and assume that
A
is a uniformly elliptic, bounded matrix in
\Omega
, and
V\in M^{q}(p;\Omega)
.
Then, the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
admits a unique principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
\lambda_{1}
is simple and its principal eigenfunction is the minimizer of the Rayleigh-Ritz
variational problem

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)=\min\limits_{u\in W^{1,p}_{0}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{\mathcal{Q}^{\Omega}_{p,A,V}(u)}{\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}\,.

The following well-known Allegretto-Piepenbrink theorem (in short, the
AP
theorem) connects between the nonnegativity of
Q_{p,A,V}
and the nonnegativity of its associated energy functional
\mathcal{Q}^{\Omega}_{p,A,V}

[
19
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Theorem 2.15

(
AP
theorem)
.

The following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{Q}_{p,A,V}^{\Omega}(\phi)\geq 0
for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

(2)

\mathcal{C}^{Q_{p,A,V}}(\Omega)\neq\emptyset.

(3)

\mathcal{K}^{Q_{p,A,V}}(\Omega)\neq\emptyset.

Definition 2.16
.

Assume that
Q_{p,A,V}\geq 0
in
\Omega.
We say that
Q_{p,A,V}
is subcritical in
\Omega
if there exists
0\lneqq W\in M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(p;\Omega)
such that
Q_{p,A,V-W}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. We say that
Q_{p,A,V}
is critical in
\Omega
if for all
0\lneqq W\in M^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(p;\Omega)
the equation

Q_{p,A,V-W}(u)=0
does not admit a positive solution in
\Omega.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
\omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. We say that
Q_{p,A,V}
satisfies the (generalized) weak maximum principle in
\omega
if for any
u\in W^{1,p}(\omega)
satisfying
Q_{p,A,V}(u)\geq 0
in
\omega
and
u\geq 0
on
\partial\omega
, we have
u\geq 0
in
\omega
.

We say that
Q_{p,A,V}
satisfies the strong maximum principle in
\omega
if for any
u\in W^{1,p}(\omega)
satisfying
Q_{p,A,V}(u)\!\geq\!0
in
\omega
and
u\!\geq\!0
on
\partial\omega
, either
u\!=\!0
, or
u\!>\!0
in
\omega
.

Lemma 2.18

(
[
19
, Theorem 3.10]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and assume that
A
is
a uniformly elliptic, bounded matrix in
\Omega
, and

V\in M^{q}(p;\Omega)
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

Q_{p,A,V}
satisfies the (generalized) weak maximum principle in
\Omega
.

(2)

Q_{p,A,V}
satisfies the strong maximum principle in
\Omega
.

(3)

The equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
admits a positive supersolution in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
which is not a solution.

(4)

The equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
admits a positive supersolution in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
which is not a solution.

(5)

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)>0
.

(6)

For any
0\leq g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique nonnegative solution in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=g
.

Corollary 2.19
.

If there exists a weak positive (super)solution of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\lambda_{1}(\Omega^{\prime})>0
for any bounded Lipschitz subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
K_{0}
be a compact subset of
\Omega
. A positive solution
u
of
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega\setminus K_{0}
is said to be a positive
solution of minimal growth in a neighborhood of infinity in
\Omega
, and denoted by
u\in\mathcal{MG}_{A,V,\Omega;K_{0}}
, if for any smooth
compact subset

of
\Omega
with

, and any positive supersolution

of
Q_{p,A,V}(w)=0
in

, we
have

u\leq v\mathrm{~on~}\partial K\Longrightarrow u\leq v\mathrm{~in~}\Omega\setminus K.

If
u\in\mathcal{MG}_{A,V,\Omega;\emptyset},
then
u
is called an Agmon ground state of
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega.

Lemma 2.21

(
[
13
, Proposition 3.17]
)
.

Let

, and suppose that

in

Then for any

admits a unique (up to a multiplicative constant) solution

Definition 2.22
.

A function

having a nonremovable singularity at

is called a minimal positive Green function of
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
with singularity at

We denote such a function by

Lemma 2.23

(
[

, Theorem 5.9]
)
.

Suppose that

in
\Omega
. Then
Q_{p,A,V}
is critical in

if and only if the equation

admits a ground state in
\Omega
.

Definition 2.24
.

A sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is called a null-sequence with respect to a nonnegative operator

in

if

(1)

\phi_{k}\geq 0
for all

(2)

for all

Lemma 2.25

(

, Theorem 4.15]
)
.

A nonnegative operator

The next lemma shows that the energy functional
\mathcal{Q}^{\Omega}_{p,A,V}
is equivalent to a

that does not explicitly depend on

and contains only nonnegative terms.

Lemma 2.26

(
[
20
, Lemma 3.4]
)
.

Let

. Then, for any
0\leq u\in W^{1,p}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
having compact support in
\Omega
, and
such that

, we have

(2.2)

\mathcal{Q}^{\Omega}_{p,A,V}(u)\asymp\mathcal{Q}^{\Omega}_{\mathrm{sim},p,A,V}(w):=\int_{\Omega}v^{2}|\nabla w|_{A}^{2}\left(w|\nabla v|_{A}+v|\nabla w|_{A}\right)^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Remark 2.27
.

Lemma

is proved in

]

. However, the proof is purely algebraic and therefore, holds for

As a corollary of (
2.2
) and Hölder’s inequality we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.28
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{C}^{Q_{p,A,V}}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\alpha}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
and let
X(w):=\int_{\Omega}v^{p}|\nabla w|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x
and

.
Then, for any continuous function

having compact support in

, the following assertions hold true.

1.
\qquad\mathcal{Q}^{\Omega}_{p,A,V}(vw)\asymp\mathcal{Q}^{\Omega}_{\mathrm{sim},p,A,V}(w)
.

\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!2.\qquad\mathcal{Q}^{\Omega}_{\mathrm{sim},p,A,V}(w)\leq\begin{cases}CX(w)&1<p\leq 2,\\
C\left[X(w)+X(w)^{2/p}Y(w)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\right]&p>2.\end{cases}

2.4.
Optimal Hardy-weights

Let
\overline{\infty}
denote the ideal point in the one-point compactification of
\Omega
. Let us define the notion of an optimal Hardy-weight for the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
.

Definition 2.29

(
[
9
]
)
.

Suppose that
Q_{p,A,V}
is subcritical in
\Omega
. We say that
0\lneqq W
is an optimal Hardy-weight of
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
if the following two assertions are satisfied:

(1)

Criticality:

Q_{p,A,V-W}
is critical in
\Omega.

(2)

Null-criticality with respect to
W
:

\int_{\Omega}|\psi|^{p}W\,\mathrm{d}x=\infty
, where
\psi
is the (Agmon) ground state of
Q_{p,A,V-W}
in
\Omega.

Remark 2.30
.

Let us discuss Definition
2.29
.
Suppose that
Q_{p,A,V}
is subcritical in a domain
\Omega
containing
x_{0}
, and let
x_{0}\in K\Subset\Omega
. Then, for any

0\lneqq W\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
there exists
\tau>0
such that

Q_{p,A,V-\tau W}
is critical in
\Omega
(see for example
[
21
, Proposition 4.4]
and
[
19
]
). On the other hand, the ground state of
Q_{p,A,V-\tau W}
,
\phi
, satisfies

\phi\asymp G^{\Omega}_{Q_{p,A,V}}(x,x_{0})\qquad\mathrm{in}~\Omega\setminus K.

Therefore, there are infinity many weight functions
0\lneqq W\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

Q_{p,A,V-W}
is critical in
\Omega
, obviously, for such a weight
W
, the operator
Q_{p,A,V-W}
is not null-critical with respect to
W
.

Definition 2.31
.

We say that a Hardy-weight
W
is optimal at infinity in
\Omega
if for any
K\Subset\Omega
, we have

\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid Q_{p,A,V-\lambda W}\geq 0\mbox{ in }\Omega\setminus K\}=1.

Remark 2.32
.

The definition of an optimal Hardy-weight in
[
8
]
includes the requirement that
W
should be optimal at infinity. But, it is proved in
[
15
]
that if
Q-W
is null-critical with respect to
W
in
\Omega
, then
Q-W
is optimal at infinity. The same proof applies under the assumptions considered in the present paper, hence, in Definition
2.29
we avoid the requirement of optimality at infinity.

The following coarea formula is a direct consequence of
[
9
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.33
.

(Coarea formula)
Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\!\geq\!2
, and
G\!\in\!C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)
is a positive
(p,A)
-harmonic function in
\Omega^{*}\!:=\!\Omega\!\setminus\!\{0\}
. Assume that for any
0\!<\!t_{1}<\!t_{2}\!<\!\infty
, the set

\mathcal{A}\!:=\!\{x\!\in\!\Omega^{*}\mid t_{1}<G(x)<t_{2}\}
is bounded.
Let
h\in{C^{2}(0,\infty)}
be a positive function satisfying
h^{\prime}(s)>0
for all
s>0
, and denote
v:=h(G)
.

Then there exists
C>0
, independent on
t
, such that for any locally bounded real measurable function
f
such that
f(v)
has a compact support in
\Omega^{*}
, we have

(2.3)

\int_{\Omega^{*}}f(v)|\nabla v|_{A}^{p}\mathrm{d}x=C\int_{h(\inf_{\Omega*}G)}^{h(\sup_{\Omega*}G)}\frac{f(\tau)}{((h^{-1})^{\prime}(\tau))^{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Proof.

Since
G\in C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega^{*})
and
1<p
, then
\frac{|\nabla G|_{A}^{p}}{|\nabla G|}\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega^{*})
and we may use the (classical) coarea formula (
[
7
, Theorem 2.32]
) to obtain
for
v=h(G)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{*}}f(v)|\nabla v|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega^{*}}f(h(v))|h^{\prime}(G)|^{p}\frac{|\nabla G|_{A}^{p}}{|\nabla G|}|\nabla G|\,\mathrm{d}x=

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(h(t))h^{\prime}(t)^{p}\int_{\{G=t\}}\frac{|\nabla G|_{A}^{p}}{|\nabla G|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

By (a generalized) Sard’s theorem for
C^{1,\alpha}
functions
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\{G=t\}\cap\mathrm{Crit}(G)\right)=0.

The fact that
G\in C^{1,\alpha}
and Proposition
2.4
imply that (for a.e.
t_{1}<t_{2}
) the set

\mathcal{A}:=\{t_{1}<G<t_{2}\}
has a finite perimeter.
In particular,
\nabla G\neq 0
and
\vec{n}
is well defined on
\partial\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e..
Let
\partial_{+}=\{x\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}:G(x)=t_{2}\}

and
\partial_{-}=\{x\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}:G(x)=t_{1}\}
.
The Gauss-Green theorem (Lemma
2.5
) implies that

\displaystyle 0\!=\!-\!\int_{\mathcal{A}}\!\!\mathrm{div}(|\nabla G|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla G)\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!=\!\!\int_{\partial_{+}}+\int_{\partial_{-}}\!|\nabla G|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla G\!\cdot\!\vec{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\!=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial_{+}}|\nabla G|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla G\cdot\frac{\nabla G}{|\nabla G|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\partial_{-}}|\nabla G|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla G\cdot\frac{\nabla G}{|\nabla G|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\{G=t_{2}\}}\frac{|\nabla G|_{A}^{p}}{|\nabla G|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\{G=t_{1}\}}\frac{|\nabla G|_{A}^{p}}{|\nabla G|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

In particular,
for any
t>0
,

\int_{\{G=t_{1}\}}\frac{|\nabla G|_{A}^{p}}{|\nabla G|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=C
.
By (
2.4
),

\int_{\Omega^{*}}f(v)|\nabla v|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=C\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(h(t))h^{\prime}(t)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t.

The change of the variable
h(t)=\tau
then implies (
2.3
). ∎

The following theorem is proved in
[
9
]
for the case
A=\mathbf{1}
. However, it can be easily checked that the validity of Lemma
2.33
for a general matrix
A
satisfying Assumptions
2.8
, gives rise to the following theorem.

Theorem 2.34

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
\overline{\infty}
denote the ideal point in the one point compactification of
\Omega
. Suppose that
-\Delta_{p,A}
is subcritical in
\Omega
, and admits a positive
(p,A)
-harmonic function
G(x)
in
\Omega^{*}:=\Omega\setminus\{0\}
satisfying one of the following conditions (
2.5
),(
2.6
):

(2.5)

1<p\leq n,\qquad\lim\limits_{x\to 0}G(x)=\infty,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G(x)=0,

(2.6)

p>n,\qquad\lim\limits_{x\to 0}G(x)=\gamma\geq 0,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G(x)=\begin{cases}\infty&\mbox{if~}\gamma=0,\\
0&\mbox{if~}\gamma>0.\end{cases}

Define a positive function
v
and a nonnegative weight
W
on
\Omega^{*}
as follows:

(1)

If either (
2.5
) is satisfied, or (
2.6
) is satisfied with
\gamma=0
, then

v:=G^{(p-1)/p},\;\mbox{ and }\;W:=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\left|\frac{\nabla G}{G}\right|_{A}^{p}.

(2)

If (
2.6
) is satisfied with
\gamma>0
, then

v\!:=\![G(\gamma-G)]^{(p-1)/p}
, and

W\!:=\!\left(\!\frac{p-1}{p}\!\right)^{p}\!\left|\frac{\nabla G}{G(\gamma-G)}\right|_{A}^{p}\!|\gamma-2G|^{p-2}[2(p-2)G(\gamma-G)+\gamma^{2}].

Then the following Hardy-type inequality holds in
\Omega^{*}
:

(2.7)

\int_{\Omega^{*}}|\nabla\phi|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega^{*}}W|\phi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\forall\,\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega^{*}),

and
W
is an optimal Hardy-weight of
-\Delta_{p,A}
in
\Omega^{*}
.
Moreover, up to a multiplicative constant,
v
is the ground state of
-\Delta_{p,A}-W\mathcal{I}_{p}
in
\Omega^{*}
.

The following simple observation concerns the existence of optimal Hardy-weights for a ‘small perturbation’ of an operator with a given optimal Hardy-weight.

Lemma 2.35
.

Assume that
Q_{p,A,V}
is subcritical in
\Omega
and admits an optimal Hardy-weight
W
in
\Omega^{*}\!:=\!\Omega\!\setminus\!\{0\}
.
Let
V_{1}\in M^{q}_{\rm loc}(p;\Omega)
satisfy
V_{1}\geq-\varepsilon W
for some
0\leq\varepsilon<1
and
q>n/p
.
Then
W+V_{1}
is an optimal Hardy-weight for
Q_{p,A,V+V_{1}}
in
\Omega^{*}
.

Proof.

Consider the function
W\!+\!V_{1}
.
Then,
Q_{p,A,V\!+\!V_{1}}\!-\!(W\!+\!V_{1})\mathcal{I}_{p}\!=\!Q_{p,A,V}\!-\!W\mathcal{I}_{p}
is a critical operator in
\Omega^{*}
.

Obviously,
W+V_{1}\gneqq 0
, and the ground state
\psi
of
Q_{p,A,V}-W\mathcal{I}_{p}
in
\Omega^{*}
is the ground state of
Q_{p,A,V+V_{1}}-(W+V_{1})\mathcal{I}_{p}
in
\Omega^{*}
. Moreover,

\int_{\Omega^{*}}(W+V_{1})|\psi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq(1-\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega^{*}}W|\psi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\infty,

implying that
Q_{p,A,V+V_{1}-(W+V_{1})}
is null-critical in
\Omega^{*}
with respect to
W+V_{1}
. In particular,
W+V_{1}
is an optimal Hardy-weight of
Q_{p,A,V+V_{1}}
in
\Omega^{*}
.
∎

3.
Optimal Hardy-weights for nonpositive potentials

Lemma
2.35
obviously applies when
V_{1}\geq 0
. The main goal in the current section is to obtain optimal Hardy-weights for a general subcritical operator
Q_{p,A,V}
in a domain
\Omega
, without assuming
V=0
in
\Omega
. In particular, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

First, we recall the following weak comparison principle
[
19
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Weak comparison principle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
Assume that
A
is a uniformly elliptic and bounded matrix in
\Omega
,
V\in M^{q}(p;\Omega)
and
0\leq g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Assume further that
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)>0
, where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is the principal eigenvalue of the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
. Let
u_{2}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
be a (weak) solution of

\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(u_{2})=g&\mbox{in~}\Omega,\\
u_{2}>0&\mbox{on ~}\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If
u_{1}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies

\begin{cases}Q_{p,A,V}(u_{1})\leq Q_{p,A,V}(u_{2})&\mbox{in~}\Omega,\\
u_{1}\leq u_{2}&\mbox{on ~}\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

then
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
in
\Omega
.

In the following lemma we generalize the notion of Green potential for
Q_{p,A,V}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
Q_{p,A,V}
is subcritical in
\Omega
, and
let
0\lneqq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a positive function
G_{\varphi}\in W^{1,p}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
, such that
G_{\varphi}
is a positive solution of minimal growth at infinity and satisfies

Q_{p,A,V}(G_{\varphi})=\varphi
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Fix
0\lneqq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and let
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a smooth exhaustion of
\Omega
with

\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\Subset\Omega_{1}
.
Lemma
2.18
implies that there exists a unique positive solution
G^{k}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{k})
to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p,A}(w)+(V+\frac{1}{k})|w|^{p-2}w=\varphi&\text{~in~}\Omega_{k},\\
w=0&\text{~on~}\partial\Omega_{k}.\end{cases}

By the weak comparison principle (Lemma
3.1
),
\{G^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a monotone increasing sequence of functions.
Assume first that the sequence
\{G^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is not locally uniformly bounded in
\Omega
, and let
x_{1}\in\Omega_{2}\setminus\Omega_{1}
.
By Harnack’s convergence principle there exists a subsequence of
\{z_{k}(x):={G^{k}(x)}/{G^{k}(x_{1})}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges locally uniformly to a positive solution
G
, of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
.
Therefore,
G
is a positive solution of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=0
in
\Omega
which clearly has minimal growth in a neighborhood of infinity in
\Omega
, i.e.,
G
is a ground state. This is a contradiction to the subcriticality of the operator
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
.

Consequently, Harnack inequality (
[
19
, Theorem 2.7]
) implies that the sequence
\{G^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is locally uniformly bounded in
\Omega
. By Harnack convergence principle and the strong maximum principle, it converges locally uniformly (up to a subsequence) to a positive solution,
G_{\varphi}
, of the equation
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=\varphi
in
\Omega
.
In fact,
[
16
, Theorem 5.3]
implies that there exists

0<\alpha<1
such that
G_{\varphi}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let
0\!\lneqq\!\varphi\!\in\!C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. A positive solution

u\!\in\!G_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{MG}_{A,V,\Omega,\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}
that satisfies
Q_{p,A,V}(u)=\varphi
in
\Omega
, is called a Green potential of
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega
with a density
\varphi
.

We proceed with the following technical proposition (cf.
[
9
, Lemma 2.10]
).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f(t)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfying
f,f^{\prime},-f^{\prime\prime}>0
. Then, for all
0\leq u\in C^{1}(\Omega)

Q_{p,A,V}(f(u))\!=\!-\Delta_{p}^{1D}(f)(u)|\nabla u|^{p}_{A}+(f^{\prime}(u))^{p-1}\!\!\left(\!\!-\Delta_{p,A}(u)\!+\!V\left(\frac{f(u)}{f^{\prime}(u)u}\!\right)^{\!p-1}\!|u|^{p-1}\!\right)

in the the weak sense.
Here
-\Delta_{p}^{1D}f(t):=-(|f^{\prime}(t)|^{p-2}f^{\prime}(t))^{\prime}
is the one-dimensional
p
-Laplacian.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Lemma 2.10]
(which clearly holds for the
(p,A)
-Laplacian), we have:

(3.1)

-\Delta_{p,A}(f(u))=-|f^{\prime}(u)|^{p-2}\left[(p-1)f^{\prime\prime}(u)|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}+f^{\prime}(u)\Delta_{p,A}(u)\right]

in the weak sense.
Since
f\in C^{2},f,f^{\prime},-f^{\prime\prime}>0
we have

-|f^{\prime}(u)|^{p-2}(p-1)f^{\prime\prime}(u)|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}=-\frac{d}{dt}[|f^{\prime}(t)|^{p-1}](u)|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}=-\Delta_{p}^{1D}(f)(u)|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}\,,

and together with (
3.1
) the proposition is proved.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We remark that if
f(t)=t^{\frac{p-1}{p}}
, then

-\Delta_{p}^{1D}(f(t))-\!\!\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\!\frac{f(t)^{p-1}}{t^{p}}\!=\!0,\;\;\mbox{and }c_{p}:=\!\left(\frac{f(u)}{f^{\prime}(u)u}\right)^{p-1}\!\!=\!\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\!\!>1.

Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
3.4
imply:

Corollary 3.6
.

Assume that
Q_{p,A,c_{p}V}
is subcritical in
\Omega
. For
0\lneq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, let
G_{\varphi}
be a Green potential satisfying
Q_{p,A,c_{p}V}(G_{\varphi})=\varphi
in
\Omega
, and let
f(t)=t^{\frac{p-1}{p}}
.
Then,

(3.2)

Q_{p,A,V}(f(G_{\varphi}))=-\Delta_{p}^{1D}(f)(G_{\varphi})|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p}+(f^{\prime}(G_{\varphi}))^{p-1}\varphi\gneqq 0.

In particular,
f(G_{\varphi})
is a positive solution of the equation

Q_{p,A,V-W}(v)=0
, where

W=\frac{Q_{p,A,V}(f(G_{\varphi}))}{f(G_{\varphi})^{p-1}},\quad\mbox{and }\quad W=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\left|\frac{\nabla G_{\varphi}}{G_{\varphi}}\right|_{A}^{p}\qquad\text{in}~\Omega\setminus\mathrm{supp}(\varphi).

The following lemma is a generalization of Lemma
2.33
to the case
V\neq 0
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
Q_{p,A,V}
is subcritical in
\Omega
, and let

G_{\varphi}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
be a Green potential (with respect to
0\lneq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
), and assume that

(3.3)

\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G_{\varphi}=0;\qquad\quad\int_{\Omega}VG_{\varphi}^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<0;\quad\quad\int_{\Omega}|V||G_{\varphi}|^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Then, there exists
0<M_{\varphi}<\sup\limits_{\Omega}G_{\varphi}
such that
for almost every
0<t<M_{\varphi}
, satisfying

\text{supp}(\varphi)\Subset\Omega_{t}:=\{x\in\Omega:G_{\varphi}(x)>t\},

there exists
C>0
, independent of
t
, such that

(3.4)

C^{-1}\leq\int_{G_{\varphi}=t}|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{A}\leq C,

where
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{A}=\frac{|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}}{|\nabla G_{\varphi}|}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.

Proof.

The assumption
\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G_{\varphi}=0
, and Proposition
2.4
imply that for a.e.
t>0
the set

\Omega_{t}
has finite perimeter.
Furthermore, (
3.3
) implies that

|V|G_{\varphi}^{p-1}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega^{\prime})
. Finally, Sard’s theorem for
C^{1,\alpha}
-functions implies that the conditions in Gauss-Green theorem (Lemma
2.5
are satisfied in
\Omega^{\prime}
. Hence,

\!\int_{\Omega_{t}}\!\!(\varphi\!-\!V|G_{\varphi}|^{p-2}G_{\varphi}\!)\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!=\!-\!\!\int_{\Omega_{t}}\!\!\!\!\mathrm{div}\,\!(|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p-2}\!\!A\nabla G_{\varphi})\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!=\!-\!\!\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\!\!\!\!|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p-2}\!A\nabla G_{\varphi}\cdot\vec{n}\!\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

The assumptions
\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G_{\varphi}=0,
and
\int_{\Omega}VG_{\varphi}^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<0
imply that for a sufficiently small
M_{\varphi}>0
and
0<t<M_{\varphi}
,

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega_{t}}\big(\varphi-V|G_{\varphi}|^{p-2}G_{\varphi}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\big(\varphi+|V||G_{\varphi}|^{p-1}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C.

Moreover, the assumption
\text{supp}(\varphi)\Subset\Omega_{t}
implies

C^{-1}\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{t}}\big(\varphi-V|G_{\varphi}|^{p-2}G_{\varphi}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Consequently,

\int_{\Omega_{t}}\big(\varphi-V|G_{\varphi}|^{p-2}G_{\varphi}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\asymp C,

and
C
does not depend on
t
.
Sard’s theorem for
C^{1,\alpha}
functions implies that for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega^{\prime}
,
|\nabla G(x)|\neq 0
. Furthermore, the definition of
\Omega^{\prime}
implies that
G_{\varphi}\geq t
in
\Omega^{\prime}
, and hence,
\vec{n}=-\frac{\nabla G_{\varphi}}{|\nabla G_{\varphi}|}
for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega^{\prime}
. Therefore,

-\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p-2}A\nabla G_{\varphi}\cdot\vec{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p-1}\frac{|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}}{|\nabla G_{\varphi}|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\asymp C.\quad\qed

Remark 3.8
.

The assumption
\int_{\Omega}VG_{\varphi}^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<0
in Lemma
3.7
is needed for arguing (
3.5
). In particular, the lemma still holds once assuming instead that
V\leq 0
in
\Omega
.

We proceed with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9

(cf.
[
9
, Propositions 5.1 and 5.5]
)
.

Let
0\lneqq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and assume that
Q_{p,A,c_{p}V}
is subcritical in
\Omega
. Let

G_{\varphi}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
be a Green potential satisfying
Q_{p,A,c_{p}V}(G_{\varphi})=\varphi\quad\text{in }\Omega
, and assume that

\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G_{\varphi}=0;\qquad\quad\int_{\Omega}VG_{\varphi}^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<0;\quad\quad\int_{\Omega}|V||G_{\varphi}|^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Consider the function
f(t)=t^{\frac{p-1}{p}},
and let

W:=\frac{Q_{p,A,V}(f(G_{\varphi}))}{f(G_{\varphi})^{p-1}}\,.

Then
Q_{p,A,V-W}
is critical in
\Omega,
with a ground state
f(G_{\varphi})
and
\int_{\Omega}Wf(G_{\varphi})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\infty
.
Hence,
W
is an optimal Hardy-weight for
Q_{p,A,V}
in
\Omega.

Proof.

Criticality:

Let
M_{\varphi}
be given by Lemma
3.7
, and let
K\Subset\Omega
be a precompact smooth subdomain satisfying

\text{supp}\,\varphi\Subset K
,
\max\limits_{\Omega\setminus K}G_{\varphi}<M_{\varphi}

and
G_{\varphi}<1
for all
x\in\Omega\setminus K
.
Assume without loss of generality that
\inf\limits_{K}G_{\varphi}\geq 1
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the function
\phi_{k}(f(G_{\varphi}))
, where
f(t)=t^{\frac{p-1}{p}}
and

\phi_{k}(t)=\begin{cases}0&0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}},\\
2+\frac{\log t}{\log k}&\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq t\leq\frac{1}{k},\\
1&\frac{1}{k}\leq t\leq k,\\
2-\frac{\log t}{\log k}&k\leq t\leq k^{2},\\
0&t\geq k^{2}.\end{cases}

We claim that
u_{k}=\phi_{k}(f(G_{\varphi}))f(G_{\varphi})
is a null-sequence of
Q_{p,A,V-W}
in
\Omega
. Indeed, by (
2.2
),
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sim}}(w)\asymp\mathcal{Q}(wf(G_{\varphi}))=\mathcal{Q}(u)
, where

\mathcal{Q}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla u|_{A}^{p}+(V-W)|u|^{p}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x,

and

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sim}}(w)=\int_{\Omega}f(G_{\varphi})^{2}|\nabla w|_{A}^{2}\left(w|\nabla(f(G_{\varphi}))|_{A}+f(G_{\varphi})|\nabla w|_{A}\right)^{p-2}\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, by Corollary
2.28
we have

\mathcal{Q}_{\text{sim}}(w)\leq\begin{cases}CX(w)&1<p\leq 2,\\
C\left[X(w)+X(w)^{2/p}Y(w)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\right]&p>2,\end{cases}

where

X(w)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|_{A}^{p}f(G_{\varphi})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad Y(w)=\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p}|\nabla(f(G_{\varphi}))|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

By the (classical) coarea formula (
[
7
, Theorem 2.32]
),

\displaystyle X(\phi_{k}(f(G_{\varphi})))=\int_{\Omega\setminus K}f(G_{\varphi})^{p}|\phi_{k}^{\prime}(f(G_{\varphi}))|^{p}|f^{\prime}(G_{\varphi})|^{p}|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\max\limits_{\Omega\setminus K}G_{\varphi}}f(t)^{p}|\phi_{k}^{\prime}(f(t))|^{p}f^{\prime}(t)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\int_{G_{\varphi}=t}|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p-1}\text{d}\sigma_{A}.

By Lemma
3.7
, for a.e.
0<t<\max\limits_{\Omega\setminus K}G_{\varphi}
we have
\int_{G_{\varphi}=t}|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p-1}\text{d}\sigma_{A}\asymp 1
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\max\limits_{\Omega\setminus K}G_{\varphi}}f(t)^{p}|\phi_{k}^{\prime}(f(t))|^{p}f^{\prime}(t)^{p}\!\,\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle C(p)\int_{0}^{f(\max\limits_{\Omega\setminus K}G_{\varphi})}\frac{|s\phi_{k}^{\prime}(s)|^{p}}{s}\!\,\mathrm{d}s=

\displaystyle\frac{C(p)}{\log^{p}\!k}\int_{\frac{1}{k^{2}}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\!\frac{1}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\asymp\left(\!\frac{1}{\log k}\!\right)^{\!\!p-1}.

Consequently,
X(\phi_{n}(f(G_{\varphi})))\asymp\left(\dfrac{1}{\log k}\right)^{p-1}
.
By a similar calculation,

\displaystyle Y(\phi_{k}(f(G_{\varphi})))=\int_{\Omega\setminus K}|\phi_{k}(f(G_{\varphi}))|^{p}f^{\prime}(G_{\varphi})^{p}|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\asymp\int_{0}^{1}|\phi_{k}(f(t))|^{p}f^{\prime}(t)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\asymp

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{f(1)}|\phi_{k}(s)|^{p}\frac{ds}{s}=\int_{1/k^{2}}^{1/k}\left(2+\frac{\log s}{\log k}\right)\frac{1}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{1/k}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\asymp\int_{1/k}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\asymp\log k.

It follows that

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sim}}(w_{k})=\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{sim}}(\phi_{k}(f(G_{\varphi})))\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, and therefore,

\mathcal{Q}(u_{k})=\mathcal{Q}(\phi_{k}(f(G_{\varphi})f(G_{\varphi})))\to 0~\text{as}~k\to\infty.

Let us specialize
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that the set
B=\{x\in\Omega:\varepsilon_{0}/2<f(G_{\varphi})<\varepsilon_{0}\}
is nonempty, bounded,
and contained in
\Omega\setminus K.

Therefore,

(3.6)

\int_{B}|u_{k}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B}|\phi_{k}(f(G_{\varphi}))|^{p}f(G_{\varphi})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\asymp 1.

Thus, the sequence
\{u_{k}\}
is a null-sequence, and in light of Lemma
2.25
,
Q_{p,A,V-W}
is critical in

\Omega
.

Null-criticality:

Let
K\Subset\Omega
be a precompact smooth subdomain as in the first part of the proof.

For almost every
0<\tau<1
we consider the set
\Omega_{\tau}:=\{x\in\Omega\mid\tau<G_{\varphi}<\min\limits_{K}G_{\varphi}\}

which has finite perimeter.
Recall that

W=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\frac{|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p}}{G_{\varphi}^{p}}\qquad\mbox{in }\Omega_{\xi}.

By the (classical) coarea formula and (
3.4
),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\tau}}W(f(G_{\varphi}))^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega_{\tau}}\frac{|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p}}{G_{\varphi}^{p}}(f(G_{\varphi}))^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(\frac{f(t)}{t}\right)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\int_{G_{\varphi=t}}|\nabla G_{\varphi}|_{A}^{p-1}\text{d}\sigma_{A}\asymp C\int_{\tau}^{\min\limits_{K}G_{\varphi}}\left(\frac{f(t)}{t}\right)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t.

By letting
\tau\to 0
we obtain that
\int_{\Omega\setminus K}Wf(G_{\varphi})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\infty.

∎

Zimo Hao, Michael Röckner and Xicheng Zhang

Abstract
. Motivated by the probabilistic representation for solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations, we introduce a novel class of stochastic differential equations that depend on the entire flow of its time marginals. We establish the existence and uniqueness of both strong and weak solutions under one-sided Lipschitz conditions and for singular drifts. These newly proposed distribution-flow dependent stochastic differential equations are closely connected to quasilinear backward Kolmogorov equations and Fokker-Planck equations. Furthermore, we investigate a stochastic version of the 2D-Navier-Stokes equation associated with fractional Brownian noise. We demonstrate the global well-posedness and smoothness of solutions when the Hurst parameter H H lies in the range ( 0 , 1 2 ) (0,\frac{1}{2}) and the initial vorticity is a finite signed measure.



1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper we fix
T>0
and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and write

{\mathbb{D}}_{T}:=\{(s,t):0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T\}.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}:={\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the space of all probability measures over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, which is endowed with the weak topology.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{d}_{\mathcal{P}}:=C({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
be
the space of all continuous probability measure-valued functions from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Let
\{W_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
be a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion on some probability space
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
.
We consider the following nonlinear stochastic differential equation (SDE), also called distribution-flow dependent SDE (abbreviated as DFSDE): for
(s,t,x)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle X_{s,t}^{x}=x+\int_{s}^{t}B(r,X_{s,r}^{x},\mu_{r,T}^{\centerdot},\mu_{s,r}^{\centerdot}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\Sigma(r,X_{s,r}^{x},\mu_{r,T}^{\centerdot},\mu_{s,r}^{\centerdot}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r},

(1.1)

where
\mu^{x}_{s,t}={\mathbb{P}}\circ(X_{s,t}^{x})^{-1}
is the probability distribution measure of
X_{s,t}^{x}
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mu^{x}_{s,r}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)\mu^{y}_{r,t}=\mu^{x}_{s,t},\quad\text{for all }0\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant t\leqslant T\text{and $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}$},

(1.2)

and

(B,\Sigma):[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{C}}^{d}_{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{C}}^{d}_{\mathcal{P}}\to({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

are two Borel measurable functions.

The main feature of SDE (
1.1
) is that the coefficients depend on the distribution-flow
x\mapsto\mu^{x}_{s,t}
of the solution itself, even the future distribution.
Of course, one can regard
\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t}
as a probability kernel.
Such type of SDEs naturally arises in the stochastic representation of Navier-Stokes equations as we shall see in the next subsection.
Before we continue the discussion, we first introduce the following notion of a solution to the above SDE:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{F}}:=(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},({\mathscr{F}}_{s})_{s\geqslant 0},{\mathbb{P}})
be a stochastic basis.
We call a pair of stochastic processes
((X^{x}_{s,t})_{(s,t,x)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]})
defined on
{\mathfrak{F}}
a weak solution of DFSDE (
1.1
), if

(i)

W_{t}
is a standard
d
-dimensional
{\mathscr{F}}_{t}
-Brownian motion;

(ii)

For each
(s,t)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}
,

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\ni x\to\mu^{x}_{s,t}:={\mathbb{P}}\circ(X_{s,t}^{x})^{-1}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is weakly continuous and the family
\mu^{x}_{s,t}
,
(s,t,x)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, satisfies (
1.2
);

(iii)

For each
(s,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{T}|B(r,X_{s,r}^{x},\mu_{r,T}^{\centerdot},\mu_{s,r}^{\centerdot})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{s}^{T}|\Sigma(r,X_{s,r}^{x},\mu_{r,T}^{\centerdot},\mu_{s,r}^{\centerdot})|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r<\infty,\quad{\mathbb{P}}-\text{a.s.}

and the pair of processes a.e. satisfies equation (
1.1
) for all
t\in[s,T]
,
s\geqslant 0
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

If, in addition,
X^{x}_{s,t}
is adapted to the filtration generated by the Brownian motion
{\mathscr{F}}^{W}_{t}:=\sigma\{W_{r},r\in[0,t]\}
, then it is called a strong solution of DFSDE (
1.1
).

McKean-Vlasov SDEs, also referred to as distribution-dependent SDEs (DDSDEs), represent a significant class of stochastic differential equations where the coefficients are depending on the distribution of the solution process itself. These equations extend the scope of standard SDEs by incorporating the influence of interactions among particles or agents within a system. Initially introduced by Henry McKean
[
48
]
in 1966
in the context of nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations and later by Anatoli Vlasov
[
59
]
in 1968 in plasma physics, McKean-Vlasov SDEs have now attracted considerable attention across diverse fields such as mathematical finance, statistical physics, population dynamics, and mean field games (see, for example,
[
8
,
9
,
17
]
).

Furthermore, DDSDEs exhibit substantial connections to vortex models like the Navier-Stokes and Euler equation (see, e.g.,
[
52
,
19
]
). DDSDEs with singular vortex kernels have been further developed by researchers such as Jabin-Wang
[
35
]
, Serfaty
[
56
]
, and other who at the same time contributed significantly to the advancement of propagation of chaos results.

For McKean-Vlasov SDEs, the dynamics of each individual particle is influenced by the collective behavior of the entire population, resulting in complex collective phenomena. This modeling framework allows the analysis of systems comprising a large number of interacting components, for which traditional approaches are inadequate. The general form of such McKean-Vlasov SDEs reads:

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}b(r,Y_{r},\mu_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(r,Y_{r},\mu_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r},

(1.3)

where
\mu_{r}
denotes the distribution of
Y_{r}
.
We emphasize that these McKean-Vlasov SDEs differ from (
1.1
) in several key aspects. First, (
1.3
) represents a single equation with a given initial condition
\xi
, whereas (
1.1
) describes a system of SDEs. Even if we consider (
1.3
) with
\xi\sim\delta_{x}
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and arbitrary starting times
s\geqslant 0
, the corresponding
\mu^{x}_{s,t}
does not satisfy (
1.2
). Instead, it only fulfills the following flow property:

\mu^{x}_{s,t}=\mu^{\mu^{x}_{s,r}}_{r,t},

which is the law of
Y_{t}
, where
Y
is the solution of (
1.3
) started at time
r\geqslant s
with
\xi\sim\mu^{x}_{s,r}
.

Considerable attention has been paid to exploring the well-posedness of DDSDEs (
1.3
) with singular drifts. Mishura and Veretenikov
[
49
]
established the strong well-posedness of DDSDEs (
1.3
) if the coefficient
b
is only measurable and of at most linear growth, and additionally is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the distribution
\mu
, while
\sigma
is assumed to be uniformly non-degenerate and Lipschitz continuous both in the spatial and measure vaiable. Later, Röckner and Zhang
[
55
]
extended this to cases involving local
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
-drift. Additionally, Lacker
[
38
]
used the relative entropy method and Girsanov’s theorem to obtain well-posedness results for DDSDEs with linear growth and
\sigma={\mathbb{I}}
, further extended by Han
[
26
]
to situations involving
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
-drifts. Zhao
[
67
]
used heat kernel estimates and the Schauder-Tychonoff fixed-point theorem to establish well-posedness results for a more general class of DDSDEs with singular coefficients.

Through Zvonkin’s transformation and the entropy method, the authors in
[
27
]
proved the strong well-posedness for DDSDEs in cases where
\sigma
is independent of
\mu
and
b
belongs to certain mixed
L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
-spaces. For specific cases, such as where
\sigma={\mathbb{I}}
and
b(t,y,\mu)=b*\mu(t,y)
, both weak and strong well-posedness have been proved by various researchers
[
13
,
14
,
28
]
. The Nemytskii-type DDSDEs, where

(b,\sigma)(t,y,\mu)=(b,\sigma)\Big(t,y,\tfrac{\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y}(y)\Big),

has been studied conducted by Barbu and Röckner
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
, and subsequently also in
[
32
]
. Moreover, for kinetic cases of DDSDEs, further studies can be found in
[
33
,
28
,
29
,
31
]
and the references therein.

Now let us return to the SDEs of type (
1.1
). One of the main motivations for studying such equations arises from the Navier-Stokes equation, which provides an example of (
1.1
) through its stochastic representation.

1.1.
Motivation

Consider the following Navier-Stokes equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d=2,3
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u=\Delta u-u\cdot\nabla u-\nabla p,\\
&\mathord{{\rm div}}u=0,\quad u_{0}=\varphi,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

where
u:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is the velocity field and
p
stands for the pressure, and
\varphi
is the initial velocity.
In
[
16
]
, Constantin and Iyer presented the following probabilistic representation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}u(s,X_{s}^{x}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\sqrt{2}W_{t},\quad t\geqslant 0,\\
&u(t,x)={\bf P}{\mathbb{E}}[\nabla^{\rm t}_{x}Y^{x}_{t}\cdot\varphi(Y^{x}_{t})],\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

where
Y^{x}_{t}
is the inverse of the flow mapping
x\to X_{t}^{x}
,
\nabla^{\rm t}
denotes the transpose of the Jacobi matrix
(\nabla X)_{ij}:=\partial_{x_{j}}X^{i}
,
and
{\mathbf{P}}:={\mathbb{I}}-\nabla\Delta^{-1}\mathord{{\rm div}}
is the Leray projection onto the space of divergence free vector fields. In particular, there is a one-to-one correspondence between (
1.4
) and (
1.5
)
when
u
is smooth. Here an interesting question is how irregular
\varphi
may be such that (
1.5
) admits a unique solution.

Now, for a velocity field
u
, let us consider its vorticity

\displaystyle w=\text{curl}u=\begin{cases}\partial_{2}u_{1}-\partial_{1}u_{2},\quad&d=2;\\
\nabla\times u,\quad&d=3.\end{cases}

It is well-known that
u
can be recovered from
w
by the Biot-Savart law, i.e.,

u=K_{d}*w,\ \ d=2,3,

with

\displaystyle K_{2}(x):=(x_{2},-x_{1})/(2\pi|x|^{2}),\ K_{3}(x)h=(x\times h)/(4\pi|x|^{3}).

(1.6)

Let
u
be a smooth solution of (
1.4
).
By direct calculations, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}w(t,x)=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{E}}\left((\text{curl}\varphi)(Y^{x}_{t})\det(\nabla_{x}Y^{x}_{t})\right),\ &d=2,\\
{\mathbb{E}}\left(\nabla^{\rm t}_{x}Y^{x}_{t}\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(Y^{x}_{t})\right),\ &d=3.\end{cases}\end{split}

(1.7)

By a change of variables and since
\det(\nabla Y^{x}_{t})=1
, we get (cf.
[
64
]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(t,x)=(K_{d}*w(t))(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K_{2}(x-X_{t}^{y})\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right),&d=2,\\
&{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}K_{3}(x-X_{t}^{y})\cdot\nabla_{y}X^{y}_{t}\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right),&d=3.\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(1.8)

In particular, for
d=2
, if we let

\displaystyle B(x,\mu^{\centerdot}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(K_{2}*\mu^{y})(x)\text{curl}\varphi(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y,

and
\mu^{y}_{t}:={\mathbb{P}}\circ(X^{y}_{t})^{-1}
,
t\in[0,T]
, then
X^{x}_{t}
solves the following SDE:

\displaystyle X_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}B(X^{x}_{s},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\sqrt{2}W_{t},

(1.9)

which leads to the system (
1.1
).

Remark 1.2
.

It should be noted that in both (
1.7
) and (
1.8
),
w
and
u
do not depend linearly on the initial velocity
\varphi
, as the solution
X^{y}_{t}
to the SDE (
1.5
) also depends on the initial velocity.

DFSDE (
1.9
) was introduced by Chorin
[
15
]
as the random vortex method to simulate viscous incompressible fluid flows for smooth kernels. Then it was further developed by Beale-Majda
[
7
]
, Marchioror-Pulvirenti
[
46
]
and Goodman
[
24
]
. In particular, Long
[
44
]
showed the optimal convergence rate of the related particle system for mollifying kernels
K_{2}
. Later,
the interaction particle system and propagation of chaos related to (
1.9
) have been attracted the attention of more and more investigators (see
[
19
,
35
]
).
However, the solvability of (
1.9
) has not been tackled in the above references until the recent papers
[
65
,
14
,
28
]
(See below for a further discussion).

If
d=3
,
formally,

\displaystyle X_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}K_{3}(X_{s}^{x}-\bar{X}_{s}^{y})\cdot\nabla\bar{X}^{y}_{s}\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\sqrt{2}W_{t},

(1.10)

where
\bar{X}^{y}_{t}
is an independent copy of
{X}^{y}_{t}
and
\bar{\mathbb{E}}
is the expectation w.r.t.
\bar{X}^{y}_{\cdot}
(see
[
64
]
and
[
53
]

for its numerical simulations under the assumption of smoothness on the interaction kernel).
To write down the above SDE in the form of (
1.1
), we introduce a matrix-valued process
U^{x}_{t}:=\nabla X^{x}_{t}
.
Formally,
U
solves the following linear ODE:

U^{x}_{t}={\mathbb{I}}_{3\times 3}+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\mathbb{E}}\left({U^{x}_{s}\cdot\nabla}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}K_{3}(\cdot-\bar{X}_{s}^{y})\cdot{\bar{U}}^{y}_{s}\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right)(X_{s}^{x}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Let
(\mu^{x})_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}
be a family of probability measures over
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{3}
, where
{\mathcal{M}}^{3}
stands for the space of all
3\times 3
-matrices. Now let us introduce

B(x,\mu):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{3}}K_{3}(x-z)\cdot M\mu^{y}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\times{\mathord{{\rm d}}}M)\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

Then we obtain the following closed SDE

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &X^{x}_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}B(X^{x}_{r},\mu^{\centerdot}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\sqrt{2}W_{t},\\
&U^{x}_{t}={\mathbb{I}}_{3\times 3}+\int_{0}^{t}{(U^{x}_{r}\cdot\nabla}B)(\cdot,\mu^{\centerdot}_{r})(X^{x}_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.11)

where
\mu^{x}_{t}:={\mathbb{P}}\circ(X^{x}_{t},U^{x}_{t})^{-1}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{3})
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

Remark 1.3
.

We note that

\displaystyle\quad U^{x}_{s}\cdot\nabla\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}K_{3}(\cdot-\bar{X}_{s}^{y})\cdot{\bar{U}}^{y}_{s}\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right)(X_{s}^{x})

\displaystyle\neq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(U^{x}_{s}\cdot\nabla K_{3})(X_{s}^{x}-\bar{X}_{s}^{y})\cdot{\bar{U}}^{y}_{s}\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

Specifically, for the gradients of the Biot-Savart kernel
K_{d}
, we note that
|\nabla K_{d}(x)|\lesssim|x|^{-d}\notin L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, which leads that

\displaystyle(\nabla K_{d})*f(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}\nabla K_{d}(x-y)f(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

is a Calderón-Zygmund operator.

However,
\nabla(K_{d}*f)\neq(\nabla K_{d})*f
.
For
d=2
and
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we have the following expression:

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}(K_{2}*f)^{i}=(\nabla_{j}K_{2}^{i})*f+\frac{1}{2}{\rm sign}(i-j)f,\quad i,j=1,2.

For
d=3
,
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and any
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, the expression is:

\displaystyle h\cdot\nabla(K_{3}*f)(x)=

\displaystyle(h\cdot\nabla K_{3})*f(x)+\frac{1}{3}f(x)\times h,

where explicitly,

\displaystyle(h\cdot\nabla K_{3})*f(x)={\rm p.v.}\frac{3}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{[(x-y)\times f(y)]\otimes(x-y)}{|x-y|^{5}}h{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y+{\rm p.v.}\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{f(y)\times h}{|x-y|^{3}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

Further details and derivations of these results can be found in
[
45
, Section 2.4.3, p. 76]
.

In the present paper, we only consider the case
d=2
. A detailed investigation of (
1.11
) for
d=3
will be addressed in future work.

On the other hand, if we set
\tilde{u}(t,x):=-u(T-t,x)
and
\tilde{p}(t,x):=p(T-t,x)
, then
\tilde{u}
solves the following backward Navier-Stokes equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{u}+\Delta\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}+\nabla\tilde{p}=0,\\
\mathord{{\rm div}}\tilde{u}=0,\quad\tilde{u}_{T}=\varphi.\end{cases}

In
[
62
]
, the author provided a probabilistic representation for
\tilde{u}
as well:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{X}_{s,t}^{x}=x+\int_{s}^{t}\tilde{u}(r,\tilde{X}_{s,r}^{x}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\sqrt{2}(W_{t}-W_{s}),\quad(s,t)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T},\\
&\tilde{u}(t,x)={\bf P}{\mathbb{E}}[\nabla^{\rm t}\tilde{X}_{t,T}^{x}\cdot\varphi(\tilde{X}_{t,T}^{x})].\end{aligned}\right.

(1.12)

As above, in the two dimensional case, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{w}(t,x):=\text{curl}\tilde{u}(t,x)={\mathbb{E}}[(\text{curl}\varphi)(\tilde{X}_{t,T}^{x})]=\langle\text{curl}\varphi,\tilde{\mu}_{t,T}^{x}\rangle,

where
\tilde{\mu}_{s,t}^{x}:={\mathbb{P}}\circ(X_{s,t}^{x})^{-1}
. By the Biot-Savart law, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(t,x)=(K_{2}*\tilde{w}(t))(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K_{2}(x-y)\langle\text{curl}\varphi,\tilde{\mu}_{t,T}^{y}\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

Thus (
1.12
) is transformed into the following DFSDE:

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{s,t}^{x}=x+\int_{s}^{t}B(\tilde{X}_{s,r}^{x},\tilde{\mu}_{r,T}^{\centerdot}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\sqrt{2}(W_{t}-W_{s}),

(1.13)

where

\displaystyle B(x,\mu^{\centerdot})=K_{2}*\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\text{curl}\varphi(y)\mu^{\centerdot}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)\right)(x).

In particular, SDE (
1.13
) is exactly an example of DFSDE (
1.1
) with
B(r,x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot})=B(x,\mu^{\centerdot})
.
For the three dimensional case, as in (
1.11
), we have the following representation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{X}^{x}_{s,t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}B(\tilde{X}^{x}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\sqrt{2}(W_{t}-W_{s}),\\
&\tilde{U}^{x}_{s,t}={\mathbb{I}}_{3\times 3}+\int_{s}^{t}(\tilde{U}^{x}_{s,r}\cdot\nabla)B(\cdot,\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T})(\tilde{X}^{x}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.14)

where
\mu^{x}_{s,t}:={\mathbb{P}}\circ(\tilde{X}^{x}_{s,t},\tilde{U}^{x}_{s,t})^{-1}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{3})
, and

B(x,\mu):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}K_{3}(x-y)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{3}}M^{\rm t}\cdot(\text{curl}\varphi)(z)\mu^{y}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\times{\mathord{{\rm d}}}M)\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y.

We must point out that (
1.9
) and (
1.13
) are essential different as we discuss in the next subsection.

1.2.
Main results

Our first result is about the strong well-posedness of DFSDE (
1.1
) with regular coefficients. More precisely,
let
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
be the space of all continuous probability measure-valued functions from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with finite first order moment (see Section 2 for more details about
the space
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
). We assume that
B
and
\Sigma
satisfy the following assumptions:

(H
0
)

For each
t\in[0,T]
, the function

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}\ni(x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot})\mapsto(B,\Sigma)(t,x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot})\in({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\mbox{is continuous,}

and there are constants
\kappa_{0},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\kappa_{4}>0
and
\kappa_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
for any
(t,x,\mu,\nu)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\langle x,B(t,x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot})\rangle+2\|\Sigma(t,x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot})\|^{2}_{\rm HS}\leqslant\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}|x|^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\|\mu^{\centerdot}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}+\|\nu^{\centerdot}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}),

(1.15)

and for any
(t,x_{i},\mu_{i},\nu_{i})\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
,
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\qquad&\langle x_{1}-x_{2},B(t,x_{1},\mu_{1}^{\centerdot},\nu_{1}^{\centerdot})-B(t,x_{2},\mu_{2}^{\centerdot},\nu_{2}^{\centerdot})\rangle+2\|\Sigma(t,x_{1},\mu_{1}^{\centerdot},\nu_{1}^{\centerdot})-\Sigma(t,x_{2},\mu_{2}^{\centerdot},\nu_{2}^{\centerdot})\|^{2}_{\rm HS}\\

Theorem 1.4

Under

Our second main result is about the well-posedness of DFSDE related to the 2D-Navier-Stokes equation driven by the fractional Brownian motion (fBm).
Recall that a Gaussian process

Clearly,

(W^{H}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}(\lambda^{-H}W^{H}_{\lambda t})_{t\geqslant 0}.

Consider the following DFSDE related to the 2D-Navier-Stokes equation driven by fBm:

\displaystyle X_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(K_{2}*\mu_{s}^{y})(X^{x}_{s})\nu_{0}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+W^{H}_{t},

1.3.
Related works

In this section, we review some literature relevant for our main Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
. We begin by considering a classical DDSDE with a singular Biot-Savart interaction kernel, often referred to as the 2D random vortex model:

\displaystyle X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(K_{2}*\mu_{s})(X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\sqrt{2}W_{t},

(1.21)

where
K_{2}
is the Biot-Savart kernel,
\mu_{t}
denotes the law of

, and
{\mathbb{P}}\circ X_{0}^{-1}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=\mu_{0}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)
. Suppose
\mu_{t}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=\rho_{t}(y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y
. Then, by Itô’s formula,
\rho_{t}
satisfies the following vorticity form of the Navier-Stokes equation (
1.4
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta\rho-\mathord{{\rm div}}((K_{2}*\rho)\rho).

(1.22)

In other words,
u(t,x):=K_{2}*\rho_{t}(x)
solves the Navier-Stokes equation (
1.4
).
In
[
65
]
, the second author utilized the De-Giorgi method to establish the existence of a weak solution to (
1.21
) when
X_{0}=x
, while the uniqueness remains an open question. Weak existence for (
1.21
) for
X_{0}=x
was also proved in
[
6
]
using a nonlinear variant of the superposition principle (see
[
57
]
). Furthermore, letting
X^{\zeta}_{r,t}
denote the weak solution to (
1.21
) started at

, with
X_{0}\sim\zeta
, it was proved in
[
6
]
, that the path law
P_{(s,\zeta)}
,
s\geqslant 0
,
\zeta\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
, of
(X^{\zeta}_{r,t})_{t\geqslant r}
form a nonlinear Markov process in the sense of McKean
[
47
]
. Moreover , it is proved in
[
6
]
that if
X_{0}
has a density
\rho_{0}\in L^{4}
, then (
1.21
) has a strong solution and that pathwise uniqueness holds for (
1.21
) in the class of all solutions having time marginal law densities in
L^{4/3}([0,T];L^{4/3})
. Furthermore, if the initial data
X_{0}
admits a density
\rho_{0}\in{\mathbb{L}}^{1+}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure, weak and strong well-posedness for (

[
14
]
and
[

It’s important to note that if the initial vorticity of the Navier-Stokes equation is not a probability measure, then there is no one-to-one correspondence between (
1.21
) and (
1.4
). To address this issue, we consider the forward and backward random vortex models, as introduced in Section 1.1:

\displaystyle X^{x}_{s,t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}K_{2}(X^{x}_{s,r}-y)g(z)\mu_{s,r}^{z}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}z+W^{H}_{t}-W_{s}^{H}

(1.23)

and

\displaystyle X^{x}_{s,t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}K_{2}(X^{x}_{s,r}-y)g(z)\mu_{r,T}^{y}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}z){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y+W_{t}^{H}-W_{s}^{H},

where
g
represents the initial vortex,
\mu^{x}_{s,t}
denotes the time marginal law of the solution
X^{x}_{s,t}
, and

is the fractional Brownian motion with

(see Section

for details).

For the forward DFSDE (
1.23
), we focus on
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Recent advancements in regularization by averaging paths (see
[
12

21
]
) and the stochastic sewing lemma (see
[
39

41

) have led to an increased interest in the well-posedness of SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion of the form:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}^{H},

where

[
51
]
and Lê in
[
39
]

Nualart-Ouknine, using the Girsanov transformation, established weak well-posedness for

and

[
39

extended this result by introducing the stochastic sewing lemma and gave a new proof for the weak well-posedness.
The strong well-posedness was obtained as well in
[
39
]
when
1/q+Hd/p<1/2-H

Furthermore, Galeati and Gubinelli in
[
21

employed the averaging paths technique to achieve path-by-path well-posedness for

and

In fact, upon assuming
X_{t}^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-H}X_{\varepsilon t}
and

with some

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}^{\varepsilon}=b_{\varepsilon}(t,X_{t}^{\varepsilon}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\varepsilon^{-H}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{\varepsilon t}^{H},

where

remains an fBm with the Hurst index

Note that the scaling hypothesis
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{t}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}_{x}}=0

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{q}+\tfrac{Hd}{p}<1-H.

Under condition (

recently established the existence of a solution for

[

-drift. Indeed,

cover various measure and distributional cases respectively. More recently, Butkovsky and Gallay in

Beyond these results, a lot of related works exists, and interested readers can refer to the comprehensive overview in
[

In the context of the following DDSDE driven by fBm

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=(b*\mu_{t})(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}^{H},

Following this review, we examine the condition on

For the backward DFSDE (

1.4.
Organization of the paper

In Section 2, we provide preliminary results concerning the space of probability kernels and fBms. Some of these results are novel and are crucial in proving Theorems

Section 3 is dedicated to proving Theorem

Section 4 focuses on demonstrating weak and strong well-posedness for a broad class of DFSDEs driven by fBms.
For

2.
Prelimiaries

In this section, we first introduce several spaces of flow probability measures associated with the Wasserstein-1 metric, the total variation distance, and localized

L^{p}
-probability kernels. Then, we also recall the definition and basic properties of fractional Brownian motions (fBms). In particular, we demonstrate an important exponential estimate for the functional of fBm, which is crucial for solving singular SDEs driven by fBms using Girsanov’s theorem.

2.1.
Flow probability measure space

Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}:={\mathcal{P}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the space of all probability measures with finite first order moment and
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
the space of
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
-valued continuous functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
w.r.t. the Wasserstein-1
distance
{\mathcal{W}}_{1}
defined by

{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{{\mathbb{P}}\circ X^{-1}=\mu,{\mathbb{P}}\circ Y^{-1}=\nu}{{\mathbb{E}}|X-Y|}.

Note that by the duality of Monge-Kantorovich (cf.
[
58
, (6.3)]
),

{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\|g\|_{\rm Lip}\leqslant 1}|\mu(g)-\nu(g)|.

For two
\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot}\in{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
, we introduce a distance between
\mu^{\centerdot}
and
\nu^{\centerdot}
by

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot}):=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu^{x},\nu^{x})}{1+|x|}.

(2.1)

For simplicity, we write

\|\mu^{\centerdot}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}:={\rm d}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu^{\centerdot},\delta_{0})=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|y|\mu^{x}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)}{1+|x|}.

Moreover, the total variation distance
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathrm{var}}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\|\mu-\nu\|_{{\mathrm{var}}}:=\sup_{A\in{\mathscr{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
be the space of all continuous probability kernels
x\mapsto\mu^{x}
w.r.t
\|\cdot\|_{\rm var}
with the distance

\|\mu^{\centerdot}-\nu^{\centerdot}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{var}}}:=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|\mu^{x}-\nu^{x}\|_{{\mathrm{var}}}.

Under these distances, it is easy to see that
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
are complete metric spaces. We would like to point out that the distance
{\rm d}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}
is only used in the study of DFSDEs with
regular coefficients.

Next we introduce some localized
L^{p}
-spaces for later use. Let
(D_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be the set of all unit cubes with center at the integer lattice so that

\displaystyle 0\in D_{1},\ \ \cup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}D_{i}={\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ \ D_{i}\cap D_{j}=\emptyset,\ \ i\not=j.

(2.2)

For
z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we shall write

D_{z}:=D_{1}+z=\big\{x:-1/2\leqslant x_{i}-z_{i}<1/2\big\}.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
, let
{\mathbb{L}}^{p}=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the usual
L^{p}
-space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
. We also introduce the Banach spaces

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}:=\left\{f\in L^{p}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}:=\sup_{i}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{D_{i}}f\|_{p}<\infty\right\}

(2.3)

and

\bar{\mathbb{L}}^{p}:=\left\{f\in L^{p}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{p}:=\sum_{i}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{D_{i}}f\|_{p}<\infty\right\}.

By a finite covering technique, there is a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(p,d)>1
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant\sup_{z}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{D_{z}}f\|_{p}\leqslant C_{1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}.

(2.4)

The advantage of using localized space
\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
is the following inclusion: for
p_{1}\geqslant p_{2}
,

\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}\subset\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{2}}.

This is quite convenient for treating singular potentials like
|x|^{-\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in(0,d)
, since it does not belong to any
L^{p}
-space, but belongs to
\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
for
p<\frac{d}{\alpha}
.
About the spaces
\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
and
\bar{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
, we have the following properties, that are similar to the classical
L^{p}
-spaces. For the readers’ convenience,
we provide detailed proofs in Appendix A.

Proposition 2.1
.

(i)

For each
p\in[1,\infty]
, it holds that
\bar{\mathbb{L}}^{p}\subset{\mathbb{L}}^{p}\subset\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
, and

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\asymp\sup_{|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|g|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant 1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)g(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x,\ \ |\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|g|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{p}\asymp\sup_{|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p^{\prime}}\leqslant 1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)g(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x,

(2.5)

where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate index of
p
.

(ii)

For any
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
with
1+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
, the following Young’s inequalities hold: for some
C=C(d,p,q,r)>0
,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f*g|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{r}\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|g|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{q},\quad\ |\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f*g|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{r}\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{p}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|g|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{q}.

(2.6)

Finally, we introduce a space of probability kernels that will be used in the study of backward DFSDEs.
Let
{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{P}}
be the set of all probability kernels from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
the set of all sub-probability kernels from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to
{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
,
where
{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
is the space of all sub-probability measures over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For given
p\in[1,\infty]
, we introduce two subclasses

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}_{s}:=\Big\{\mu^{\centerdot}\in{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{P}}_{s}:\|\mu^{\centerdot}\|_{p}:=\sup_{\|\phi\|_{p}\leqslant 1}\|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)\|_{p}<\infty\Big\}

(2.7)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}:=\Big\{\mu^{\centerdot}\in{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{P}}:|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mu^{\centerdot}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}:=\sup_{|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant 1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}<\infty\Big\}.

(2.8)

Similarly, we also introduce the subclasses
{\mathcal{L}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
.
It is easy to see that

{\mathcal{L}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}_{s},\ \ \tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}_{s}.

Remark 2.2
.

Here
\sup_{\|\phi\|_{p}\leqslant 1}\|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)\|_{p}<\infty
means that for any
\phi\in L^{p}
,
\mu^{x}(\phi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(y)\mu^{x}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)
is well-defined for Lebesgue almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and that
\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)
belongs to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
This condition implies that
\mu^{x}(\phi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(y)\mu^{x}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)
is independent of the representative of
\phi
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Indeed, if
\phi(x)=\tilde{\phi}(x)
for Lebesgue almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then
\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)=\mu^{\centerdot}(\tilde{\phi})
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. This follows from the estimate
\|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi-\tilde{\phi})\|_{p}\lesssim\|\phi-\tilde{\phi}\|_{p}=0
.

One would like to point out that such classes of kernels naturally appear in the study of stochastic Lagrangian flows (see
[
63
]
). More precisely, consider the following SDE:

X^{x}_{t}=x+\int^{t}_{0}b(X^{x}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+W_{t},

where
b
is a divergence free Lipschitz vector field. Let
\mu^{x}_{t}
be the law of the unique solution
X^{x}_{t}
. It is well-known that
for any
p\in[1,\infty]
and
t\geqslant 0
(see
[
63
]
),

\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{t}(f)\|_{p}\leqslant\|f\|_{p},\ \ f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{p}.

We have the following important properties that are also proven in Appendix A.

Proposition 2.3
.

(i) Let
\mu^{\centerdot}\in{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
. For any
p\in[1,\infty)
,
we have

\displaystyle\|\mu^{\centerdot}\|_{p}=\sup_{\phi\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\|\phi\|_{p}\leqslant 1}\|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)\|_{p}=\sup_{\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\|\phi\|_{p}\leqslant 1}\|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)\|_{p},

(2.9)

and

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mu^{\centerdot}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}=\sup_{\phi\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant 1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}=\sup_{\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant 1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}.

(2.10)

(ii)
{{\mathcal{L}}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
and
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}_{s}
are complete metric spaces with respect to the distance

\displaystyle\|\mu^{\centerdot}-\nu^{\centerdot}\|_{p}:=\sup_{\|\phi\|_{p}\leqslant 1}\|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)-\nu^{\centerdot}(\phi)\|_{p},\ \ |\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mu^{\centerdot}-\nu^{\centerdot}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}:=\sup_{|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\leqslant 1}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mu^{\centerdot}(\phi)-\nu^{\centerdot}(\phi)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}.

Moreover, with the above distances,
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}
is still complete, but
{{\mathcal{L}}}^{p}{\mathcal{P}}
is not complete.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that
\|\mu^{\centerdot}-\nu^{\centerdot}\|_{\infty}=|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mu^{\centerdot}-\nu^{\centerdot}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\infty}=\|\mu^{\centerdot}-\nu^{\centerdot}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{var}}}
for
\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot}\in{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
.

2.2.
Fractional Brownian motion and Girsanov’s theorem

In this section, we recall the definition and basic properties of fBm and the related Girsanov theorem (see
[
18
,
50
]
).

A
d
-dimensional Gaussian process
(W^{H}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
defined on some probability space
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
is called an fBm with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
if for any
0\leqslant s\leqslant t
,

{\mathbb{E}}(W^{H,i}_{t}W^{H,j}_{s})=\tfrac{1}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}){\mathbf{1}}_{i=j},\ \ i,j=1,\cdots,d.

For fixed
r\geqslant 0
, it is easy to see that for any
t,s\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}((W^{H,i}_{t+r}-W^{H,i}_{r})(W^{H,j}_{s+r}-W^{H,j}_{r}))={\mathbb{E}}(W^{H,i}_{t}W^{H,j}_{s}).

(2.11)

This means that
(W^{H}_{t+r}-W^{H}_{r})_{t\geqslant 0}
is another standard fBm.
The value of
H
tells the behavior of fBm:
when
H=1/2
, the process is exactly a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion;
when
H>1/2
, the increments of the process are positively correlated;
when
H<1/2
, the increments of the process are negatively correlated.

In what follows, we fix
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and introduce two constants used below

q_{H}:=\tfrac{1}{1-H},\ \ c_{H}:=\sqrt{2H/((1-2H){\mathcal{B}}(1-2H,H+\tfrac{1}{2}))}{\mathbf{1}}_{H\in(0,\frac{1}{2})}+{\mathbf{1}}_{H=\frac{1}{2}},

where
{\mathcal{B}}(\alpha,\beta)
is the usual Beta function defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(\alpha,\beta):=\int^{1}_{0}(1-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{\beta-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,\ \ \alpha,\beta>0.

(2.12)

It is well-known that fBm has the following representation (cf.
[
18
, Corollary 3.1]
):

\displaystyle W^{H}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},

(2.13)

where
W
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion and
K_{H}
is given by

K_{H}(t,s)=c_{H}\left((t/s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-H\right)s^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\int_{s}^{t}r^{H-\frac{3}{2}}(r-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right).

Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{T}:=C([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the space of all continuous functions from
[0,T]
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. It is also well-known that
there is a continuous functional
\Phi:{\mathbb{C}}_{T}\to{\mathbb{C}}_{T}
so that (cf.
[
10
, Proposition A.1]
)

\displaystyle W_{t}=\Phi(W^{H}_{\cdot})(t),\ \ t\in[0,T].

(2.14)

Convention:
If there is no special declaration, we always write

{\mathscr{F}}_{t}:=\sigma\{W_{s}:s\leqslant t\}=\sigma\{W^{H}_{s}:s\leqslant t\}.

From the very definition, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle K_{H}(t,s)\geqslant c_{H}(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\left((t/s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-H\right)s^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\int_{s}^{t}r^{H-\frac{3}{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)=c_{H}(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}},

(2.15)

and by
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
,

K_{H}(t,s)\leqslant c^{\prime}_{H,T}(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}s^{H-\frac{1}{2}}.

To state the Girsanov theorem for fBm, we introduce a function

\widetilde{K}_{H}(t,s):=t^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-H}s^{\frac{1}{2}-H},\ \ 0\leqslant s<t.

By the integration by parts and elementary calculus, one sees that

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{s}K_{H}(t,r)\widetilde{K}_{H}(r,s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\equiv 1,\ \ 0\leqslant s<t.

(2.16)

For given
q\in[1,\infty)
, let
{\mathbb{H}}^{q}_{T}
be the space of all absolutely continuous function
f:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
f(0)=0
and
\dot{f}\in L^{q}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})=:{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}
,
which is a Banach space under the norm

\|f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q}_{T}}:=\|\dot{f}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}}.

Now, for any function
f\in C^{1}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}f(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{K}_{H}(t,s)\dot{f}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Lemma 2.5
.

The operator
\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}
can be extended to a bounded linear operator
from
{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}
to
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}
, and there is a constant
C=C(H)>0
such that for all
f\in{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}
,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\leqslant C\|f\|_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}

(2.17)

and

\displaystyle f(t)=\int^{t}_{0}K_{H}(t,s)\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}f(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

(2.18)

Proof.

Estimate (
2.17
) follows by
\widetilde{K}_{H}(t,s)\leqslant(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-H}
and Hard-Littlewood’s inequality
[
1
, Theorem 1.7]
. Equality (
2.18
) follows by Fubini’s theorem and (
2.16
).
∎

We have the following Girsanov theorem (see
[
18
, Theorem 4.9]
).

Theorem 2.6
.

Recall
q_{H}=\frac{1}{1-H}
.
Let
h(\cdot,\omega)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}
be an
{\mathscr{F}}_{s}
-adapted process satisfying

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left(\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}\right)<\infty.

(2.19)

Then
\widetilde{W}^{H}_{t}:=W^{H}_{t}+h(t)
is a new
f
Bm with Hurst parameter
H
under the new probability measure
{\mathbb{Q}}:=Z_{T}{\mathbb{P}}
with

\displaystyle Z_{T}:=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T}(\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}h)(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}h\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right),

where
W
defined by (
2.14
) is a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion.

Proof.

By (
2.18
), we have

\widetilde{W}^{H}_{t}=W^{H}_{t}+h(t)=\int^{t}_{0}K_{H}(t,s)({\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}+\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}h(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s).

By (
2.19
) and Novikov’s criterion,
{\mathbb{E}}Z_{T}=1
.
Thus by the classical Girsanov theorem,
\widetilde{W}_{t}:=W_{t}+\int^{t}_{0}\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}h(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
is still a standard Brownian motion under
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and therefore,
\widetilde{W}^{H}_{t}=\int^{t}_{0}K_{H}(t,s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}\widetilde{W}_{s}
is an fBm under
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
∎

Now we prove the following basic estimate. The new point is that we are using the localized
L^{p}
-space.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. For any
p\in[1,\infty]
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, there is a constant
C=C(j,p,d,H)>0
such that for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant t
and
f\in\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}
,

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}(\nabla^{j}f(W_{t}^{H}))|\lesssim_{C}(t-s)^{-\frac{Hd}{p}-jH}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}.

Proof.

Note that by the representation (
2.13
),

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}(W^{H}_{t})=\int^{s}_{0}K_{H}(t,r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r}

and

W^{H}_{s,t}:=W^{H}_{t}-{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}(W^{H}_{t})=\int^{t}_{s}K_{H}(t,r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r}.

Clearly,
W^{H}_{s,t}
is independent of
{\mathscr{F}}_{s}
and

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}(W^{H}_{t})\sim N(0,\sigma^{H}_{s,t}),\ \ W^{H}_{s,t}\sim N(0,\lambda^{H}_{s,t}),\ \

where

\sigma^{H}_{s,t}:=\int^{s}_{0}|K_{H}(t,r)|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{BC5}}}{{\geqslant}}c_{H}^{2}\int^{s}_{0}|t-r|^{2H-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

and

\displaystyle\lambda^{H}_{s,t}:=\int^{t}_{s}|K_{H}(t,r)|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{BC5}}}{{\geqslant}}c_{H}^{2}\int^{t}_{s}|t-r|^{2H-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r=\frac{c^{2}_{H}}{2H}(t-s)^{2H}.

(2.20)

By the independence of
W^{H}_{s,t}
and
{\mathscr{F}}_{s}
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}[\nabla^{j}f(W^{H}_{t})]={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}[\nabla^{j}f(W^{H}_{s,t}+{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}(W^{H}_{t}))]=F^{(j)}_{s,t}({\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}(W^{H}_{t})),

where

F^{(j)}_{s,t}(y):={\mathbb{E}}[\nabla^{j}f(W^{H}_{s,t}+y)].

By Lemma
B.1
, we have

|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}[\nabla^{j}f(W^{H}_{t})]|\leqslant\|F^{(j)}_{s,t}\|_{\infty}\lesssim(\lambda^{H}_{s,t})^{(d/p-j)/2}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}.

Combining the above calculations, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Below for simplicity of notations, we always write

{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}:=L^{q}([0,T];\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}).

As a result of the previous estimate, we have the following Krylov-type estimate.

Lemma 2.8
.

For any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
with
\alpha:=1-(1/q+Hd/p)>0
, there is a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(d,p,q,H)>0
such that for all
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
0\leqslant s<t
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f(r,W_{r}^{H})(t-r)^{k\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)\leqslant C_{0}k^{-\alpha}(t-s)^{(k+1)\alpha}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}.

(2.21)

Proof.

Since
\alpha=1-(1/q+Hd/p)>0
, we must have
q>1
and
q^{\prime}Hd/p<1
, where
q^{\prime}=q/(q-1)
.
By Lemma
2.7
and Hölder’s inequality, one sees that

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}f(r,W_{r}^{H})(t-r)^{k\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}\int_{s}^{t}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f(r)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}(r-s)^{-\frac{Hd}{p}}(t-r)^{k\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\leqslant|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}(r-s)^{-q^{\prime}\frac{Hd}{p}}(t-r)^{q^{\prime}k\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the Beta function defined in (
2.12
).
By a change of variable and Lemma
B.2
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathscr{I}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}(t-s)^{(k+1)\alpha}{\mathcal{B}}\left(1-q^{\prime}\tfrac{Hd}{p},q^{\prime}k\alpha+1\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(p,q,H,d)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}(t-s)^{(k+1)\alpha}k^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{Hd}{p}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Then we have the following moment estimate.

Lemma 2.9
.

For any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
with
\alpha:=1-(1/q+Hd/p)>0
, there is a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(d,H,p,q)>0
such that for all
f\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}f(s,W_{s}^{H}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\|_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\leqslant C_{1}t^{\alpha}m^{1-\alpha}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}},\ \forall t\in(0,T].

(2.22)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
f\geqslant 0
. For simplicity of notation, we write

h(t):=f(t,W^{H}_{t}).

By the symmetric of integral and (
2.21
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}h(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{m}

\displaystyle=m!{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}h(s_{1})\int_{s_{1}}^{t}h(s_{2})\cdots\int_{s_{m-1}}^{t}h(s_{m}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{m}\cdots{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{1}

\displaystyle=m!{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}h(s_{1})\int_{s_{1}}^{t}h(s_{2})\cdots\left[{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s_{m-1}}}\int_{s_{m-1}}^{t}h(s_{m}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{m}\right]\cdots{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{0}m!|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}h(s_{1})\cdot\cdot\cdot\int_{s_{m-2}}^{t}(t-s_{m-1})^{\alpha}h(s_{m-1}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{m-1}\cdot\cdot\cdot{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{1}.

Then, by (
2.21
) and induction, we have for any
k=1,...,m-1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}h(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{m}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{0}^{k}m!((k-1)!)^{-\alpha}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}h(s_{1})\cdot\cdot\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathscr{F}}_{s_{m-k-1}}}\int_{s_{m-k-1}}^{t}(t-s_{m-k})^{k\alpha}h(s_{m-k}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{m-k}\cdot\cdot\cdot{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{0}^{m}(m!)^{1-\alpha}t^{m\alpha}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}^{m}.

Finally, by Stirling’s formula, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}h(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{m}\leqslant C_{1}^{m}m^{m(1-\alpha)}t^{m\alpha}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}^{m}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Now we can show the following important Khasminskii’s type estimate.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
q_{1},p_{1}\in[\frac{1}{1-H},\infty]
with
\alpha:=\frac{1}{2}-(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{Hd}{p_{1}})>0
.
Then for any
\lambda>0
,
there is a constant
C=C(\lambda,p_{1},q_{1},d,H)>0
such that for all
b\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\lambda\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right\}\leqslant\exp\left\{C\left(1+\kappa_{b}^{2/\alpha}\right)\right\},

(2.23)

where
{\mathscr{I}}_{b}(t):=\int^{t}_{0}b(s,W^{H}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
and
\kappa_{b}:=|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}}
.

Proof.

By (
2.17
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\lambda\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right\}\leqslant{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{C_{0}\lambda\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}\right\}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(C_{0}\lambda)^{m}}{m!}{\mathbb{E}}\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b}\|^{2m}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}.

(2.24)

Observing that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b}\|^{2m}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|b(s,W_{s}^{H})|^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{2m/q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}\leqslant\left[{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|b(s,W_{s}^{H})|^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{2m}\right]^{1/q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}},

and
\alpha:=\frac{1}{2}-(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{Hd}{p_{1}})>0
and
q_{1},p_{1}\in[q_{H},\infty]
, by (
2.22
) with
(p,q)=(\frac{p_{1}}{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}},\frac{q_{1}}{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}})
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b}\|^{2m}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left[(C_{1}T^{\alpha})^{2m}(2m)^{m(1-2\alpha)q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||b|^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}/q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}/q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}}^{2m}\right]^{1/q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}

\displaystyle=C_{2}^{m}m^{m(1-2\alpha)}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}}^{2m}=C_{2}^{m}m^{m(1-2\alpha)}\kappa_{b}^{2m}.

Substituting this into (
2.24
) and by Stirling’s formula, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\lambda\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(C_{0}\lambda C_{2})^{m}m^{m(1-2\alpha)}\kappa_{b}^{2m}}{m!}\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{C_{3}^{m}\kappa_{b}^{2m}}{(m!)^{2\alpha}}.

The proof is complete by
\alpha>0
and Lemma
B.3
.
∎

.

) and (

) have been established for VMO processes, as demonstrated in

, Corollary 3.5]

3.
Well-posedness of DFSDE: Regular coefficients case

In this section, we show the strong well-posedness (existence of strong solution and pathwise uniqueness) of the DFSDE (
1.1
) and prove Theorem
1.4
.

We first prepare the following standard result for later use.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{D}}_{T}\ni(s,t)\mapsto\mu_{s,t}\in{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
be a measurable function with

\gamma^{\mu}_{T}:=\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T}\|\mu_{s,t}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}<\infty.

Consider the following classical SDE:

\displaystyle X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}B(r,X^{x,\mu}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\Sigma(r,X^{x,\mu}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r}.

(3.1)

Under
(H
0
)
, there is a unique strong solution to the above SDE with the estimate:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}|^{2}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}|x|^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{0}(\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}-1)}{\kappa_{1}}+\kappa_{2}\int^{t}_{s}\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-r)}(\|\mu_{r,T}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}+\|\mu_{s,r}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

(3.2)

Moreover, let
\nu_{s,t}
be another
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
-valued function with
\gamma^{\nu}_{T}:=\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T}\|\nu_{s,t}\|_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}<\infty.
Then
there is a constant
C_{T}=C_{T}(\kappa_{i},\gamma^{\mu}_{T},\gamma^{\nu}_{T})>0
such that for all
(s,t,x)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}-X^{x,\nu}_{s,t}|^{2}}{1+|x|^{2}}\leqslant C_{T}\int^{t}_{s}\left({\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu_{r,T},\nu_{r,T})+{\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu_{s,r},\nu_{s,r})\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

(3.3)

Proof.

Under
(H
0
)
, the strong well-posedness to SDE (
3.1
) are well-known (see
[
43
]
).
We only show the estimates (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). Fix
s\in[0,T)
.
By Itô’s formula and (
1.15
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}_{t}(\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{1}(t-s)}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{1}(t-s)}\Big[\big(\langle X^{x,\mu}_{s,t},B(t,X^{x}_{s,t},\mu^{\centerdot}_{t,T},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t})\rangle+2\|\Sigma(t,X^{x,\mu}_{s,t},\mu^{\centerdot}_{t,T},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t})\|^{2}_{\rm HS}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\kappa_{1}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}|^{2}\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\langle X^{x,\mu}_{s,t},\Sigma(t,X^{x,\mu}_{s,t},\mu^{\centerdot}_{t,T},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{1}(t-s)}\big(\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{2}(\|\mu_{t,T}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}+\|\mu_{s,t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}})\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}M_{t},

where

t\mapsto M_{t}:=\int^{t}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{1}(r-s)}\langle X^{x,\mu}_{s,r},\Sigma(r,X^{x,\mu}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r}\rangle

is a continuous local martingale.
By a standard stopping time technique and integrating both sides with respect to the time variable
t
from
s
to
t
, we derive that

\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{1}(t-s)}{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}|^{2}\leqslant|x|^{2}+\int^{t}_{s}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{1}(r-s)}(\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{2}(\|\mu_{r,T}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}+\|\mu_{s,r}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}})){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

From this we get (
3.2
).
For (
3.3
), by Itô’s formula again and (
1.16
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}-X^{x,\nu}_{s,t}|^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{s}\Big(\kappa_{3}{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,r}-X^{x,\nu}_{s,r}|^{2}+\kappa_{4}(1+{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,r}|^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\nu}_{s,r}|^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left({\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu_{r,T},\nu_{r,T})+{\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu_{s,r},\nu_{s,r})\right)\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we get for all
t\in[s,T]
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}-X^{x,\nu}_{s,t}|^{2}\lesssim\int^{t}_{s}(1+{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,r}|^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\nu}_{s,r}|^{2})\left({\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu_{r,T},\nu_{r,T})+{\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu_{s,r},\nu_{s,r})\right)\Big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

(3.4)

Note that by (
3.2
),

\sup_{t\in[s,T]}{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,\mu}_{s,t}|^{2}\leqslant C(T,\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})(1+|x|^{2})(1+\gamma^{\mu}_{T}).

Substituting this into (
3.4
), we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Now we can give

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We use the method of freezing the distribution.
Let
\mu^{x,0}_{s,t}:=\delta_{x}
for all
(s,t,x)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by Lemma
3.1
, we can recursively define the approximation solution
X^{x,n}_{s,t}
by

\displaystyle X^{x,n+1}_{s,t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}B(r,X^{x,n+1}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{r,T},\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\Sigma(r,X^{x,n+1}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{r,T},\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r}.

(3.5)

By (
3.2
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x;n+1}_{s,t}|^{2}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}|x|^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{0}(\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}-1)}{\kappa_{1}}+\kappa_{2}\int^{t}_{s}\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-r)}(\|\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{r,T}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}+\|\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,r}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

(3.6)

Noting that

\displaystyle\|\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\frac{{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,n}_{s,t}|}{1+|x|}\right)^{2}\leqslant\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,n}_{s,t}|^{2}}{(1+|x|)^{2}}=:f_{n}(s,t),

(3.7)

we have

\displaystyle f_{n+1}(s,t)\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}+\frac{\kappa_{0}(\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}-1)}{\kappa_{1}}+\kappa_{2}\int^{t}_{s}\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-r)}(f_{n}(r,T)+f_{n}(s,r)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, if we let

F_{m}(s,t):=\sup_{n\leqslant m+1}f_{n}(s,t),

then for each
0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle F_{m}(s,t)

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\int_{s}^{t}[F_{m}(s,r)+F_{m}(r,T)]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\leqslant 1+\int_{s}^{T}F_{m}(r,T){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{s}^{t}F_{m}(s,r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

By (
3.7
) and Gronwall’s inequality (see Lemma
B.4
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{m}\sup_{(s,t)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}}\|\mu^{\centerdot,m}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}\leqslant\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sup_{(s,t)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}}F_{m}(s,t)<\infty.

(3.8)

Next, we show that the sequence
\{X^{x,n}_{s,\cdot}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in a suitable norm.
By (
3.3
), we have for any
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,n+1}_{s,t}-X^{x,m+1}_{s,t}|^{2}}{1+|x|^{2}}\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\left({\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{r,T},\mu^{\centerdot,m}_{r,T})+{\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot,m}_{s,r})\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

(3.9)

Noting that

H^{n,m}_{s,t}:={\rm d}^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,t},\mu^{\centerdot,m}_{s,t})\leqslant\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,n}_{s,t}-X^{x,m}_{s,t}|^{2}}{(1+|x|)^{2}},

by (
3.9
) we have

H^{n+1,m+1}_{s,t}\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\left[H^{n,m}_{s,r}+H^{n,m}_{r,T}\right]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

Thus, by (
3.8
) and Fatou’s lemma, we derive that for all
t\in[s,T]
,

\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}H^{n+1,m+1}_{s,t}\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\left[\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}H^{n,m}_{s,r}+\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}H^{n,m}_{r,T}\right]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

which implies by Gronwall’s inequality that

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}H^{n,m}_{s,t}=\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu^{x,n}_{s,t},\mu^{x,m}_{s,t})}{1+|x|}\right)^{2}=0.

(3.10)

Substituting this into (
3.9
), we obtain

\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}\sup_{(t,x)\in[s,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,n+1}_{s,t}-X^{x,m+1}_{s,t}|^{2}}{1+|x|^{2}}=0.

In particular, for each fixed
(s,x)\in[0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, there is an adapted process
\{X^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\in[s,T]}
so that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[s,T]}{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x,n}_{s,t}-X^{x}_{s,t}|^{2}=0,

and by (
3.10
), for each
s\leqslant t
, there is a family of probability measures
(\mu^{x}_{s,t})_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\in{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
so that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu^{x,n}_{s,t},\mu^{x}_{s,t})}{1+|x|}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm d}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,t},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t})=0.

Since
\mu^{x,n}_{s,t}={\mathbb{P}}\circ(X^{x,n}_{s,t})^{-1}
, we have

\mu^{x}_{s,t}={\mathbb{P}}\circ(X^{x}_{s,t})^{-1},\ \ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Finally, by the continuity of
(x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot})\mapsto B(t,x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot})
and taking limits for equation (
3.5
), one sees that
for each
(s,t,x)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

X^{x}_{s,t}=x+\int_{s}^{t}B(r,X^{x}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\Sigma(r,X^{x}_{s,r},\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T},\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{r}.

Thus we obtain the existence. The pathwise uniqueness is derived by the same argument.
Moreover, by (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X^{x}_{s,t}|^{2}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}|x|^{2}+\frac{\kappa_{0}(\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}-1)}{\kappa_{1}}+\kappa_{2}\int^{t}_{s}\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-r)}(\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}+\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,r}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

(3.11)

which implies by (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}+\frac{\kappa_{0}(\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-s)}-1)}{\kappa_{1}}+\kappa_{2}\int^{t}_{s}\mathrm{e}^{\kappa_{1}(t-r)}(\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}+\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,r}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

By Gronwall’s inequality (see Lemma
B.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}\leqslant C_{T}.

(3.12)

If
\kappa_{1}<0
and
2\kappa_{2}<|\kappa_{1}|
, then

\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}\leqslant 1+\frac{\kappa_{0}}{|\kappa_{1}|}+\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{|\kappa_{1}|}\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T}\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T}\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}\leqslant(1+\frac{\kappa_{0}}{|\kappa_{1}|})/(1-\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{|\kappa_{1}|}).

(3.13)

Substituting (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) into (
3.11
), we get the desired estimates.
∎

Example 3.2
.

Let

satisfy

\displaystyle(1+|x|)\,b_{1},\nabla b_{1}\in L^{1}\ \mbox{and }\nabla b_{2}\in L^{\infty}.

Let

be two Borel measurable functions with

\displaystyle\varphi_{1},\nabla\varphi_{1}\in L^{\infty}\ \text{and}\ (1+|x|)\,\varphi_{2}\in L^{1}.

For
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
, we introduce

\displaystyle B(x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}b_{1}(x-y)\mu^{y}(\varphi_{1}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(b_{2}*\nu^{z})(x)\varphi_{2}(z){\mathord{{\rm d}}}z=:B_{1}(x,\mu^{\centerdot})+B_{2}(x,\nu^{\centerdot}).

Then one sees that (
1.16
) and (
1.15
) hold. Indeed, for
B_{1}(x,\mu^{\centerdot})
, we have

\displaystyle|B_{1}(x_{1},\mu^{\centerdot}_{1})-B_{1}(x_{2},\mu^{\centerdot}_{2})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|b_{1}(x_{1}-y)-b_{1}(x_{2}-y)|\,|\mu_{1}^{y}(\varphi_{1})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|b_{1}(x_{2}-y)|\,|(\mu^{y}_{1}-\mu^{y}_{2})(\varphi_{1})|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}|x_{1}-x_{2}|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int^{1}_{0}|\nabla b_{1}(x_{1}-y+\theta(x_{2}-x_{1}))|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\theta{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|b_{1}(x_{2}-y)|(1+|y|){\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right)\|\nabla\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}{\rm d}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu^{\centerdot}_{1},\mu^{\centerdot}_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla b_{1}\|_{1}|x_{1}-x_{2}|

\displaystyle\quad+(|x_{2}|+1)\|(1+|\cdot|)b_{1}\|_{1}\|\nabla\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}{\rm d}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}_{1}}(\mu^{\centerdot}_{1},\mu^{\centerdot}_{2}).

For
B_{2}(x,\nu^{\centerdot})
, we have

\displaystyle|B_{2}(x_{1},\nu^{\centerdot}_{1})-B_{2}(x_{2},\nu^{\centerdot}_{2})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|b_{2}(x_{1}-y)-b_{2}(x_{2}-y)|\nu^{z}_{1}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)|\varphi_{2}(z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|b_{2}*\nu^{z}_{1}-b_{2}*\nu^{z}_{2}|(x_{2})|\varphi_{2}(z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle\leqslant|x_{1}-x_{2}|\|\nabla b_{2}\|_{\infty}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{1}+\|\nabla b_{2}\|_{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\nu^{z}_{1},\nu^{z}_{2})|\varphi_{2}(z)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

), for any

) holds with

In particular, for any
\lambda>\varepsilon+\|\nabla b_{2}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|(1+|\cdot|)\varphi_{2}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{1}/(2\varepsilon)
, by (
1.17
), we have uniform moment estimate in time

) with diffusive coefficient
{\mathbb{I}}
and drift
B(x,\mu^{\centerdot},\nu^{\centerdot})-\lambda x
.

Example 3.3
.

4.
Well-posedness of DFSDE: Singular drift case

In this section, we consider the DFSDE driven by fractional Brownian motion with a fixed value of
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
.
In Subsection
4.1
, we focus on the well-posedness of SDEs driven by fBm using Girsanov’s theorem. Specifically, we extend the results of Nualart-Ouknine
[
50
]
to the case where the drift term
belongs to
{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
with
p,q
in the range of
[\frac{1}{1-H},\infty]
and satisfying the condition
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{p}<\frac{1}{2}
. Notably, allowing
q

to be smaller than
2
is crucial for applications to the 2D-Navier-Stokes equation associated with fBm.
In Subsection
4.2
, we establish the weak well-posedness for DFSDEs driven by fBm using the entropy method.
In Subsection
4.3
, we examine a backward DFSDE driven by Brownian motion by utilizing Itô’s formula and PDE’s estimates.
This analysis will be instrumental in demonstrating the well-posedness of the backward Navier-Stokes equation with
{\mathbb{L}}^{1+}
-initial vorticity.

4.1.
SDEs driven by fBm

Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{T}:=C([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the space of all continuous functions from
[0,T]
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, which is endowed with the uniform convergence topology.
The canonical process on
{\mathbb{C}}_{T}
is defined by

w_{t}(\omega):=\omega_{t},\ \ \omega\in{\mathbb{C}}_{T}.

Let
{\mathscr{B}}_{t}:=\sigma\{w_{s}:s\leqslant t\}
be the natural filtration. Let
b:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{C}}_{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a
{\mathscr{B}}_{t}
-progressively measurable vector field.
In this section we consider the following SDE:

\displaystyle X_{t}=X_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}b(s,X_{\cdot}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+W^{H}_{t},

(4.1)

where
W^{H}
is an fBm with
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
.
To emphasize the dependence on
b
, we shall call SDE (
4.1
) as SDE
b
. We introduce the following definition of a weak solution to SDE
b
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. We call a probability measure
{\mathbf{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}}_{T})
a weak solution of SDE
b
starting from the initial distribution
\nu
if
{\mathbf{P}}\circ w_{0}^{-1}=\nu
and

\displaystyle t\mapsto w_{t}-w_{0}-\int^{t}_{0}b(s,w_{\cdot}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=:{\mathscr{W}}^{b}_{t}

(4.2)

is an
f
Bm with Hurst parameter
H
under
{\mathbf{P}}
. The set of all weak solutions of SDE
b
with initial distribution
\nu
is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}(b,\nu)
.
We call the uniqueness in law holds for SDE
b
if any two
{\mathbf{P}}_{1},{\mathbf{P}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}(b,\nu)
are the same.

Recall that for two
{\mathbf{P}}_{1},{\mathbf{P}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}}_{T})
, the relative entropy is defined by

{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbf{P}}_{1}|{\mathbf{P}}_{2}):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{1}}\ln({\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\mathbf{P}}_{1}/{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\mathbf{P}}_{2}),&\ {\mathbf{P}}_{1}\ll{\mathbf{P}}_{2},\\
&\infty,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

By Csiszár-Kullback-Pinsker’s inequality (abbreviated as CKP’s inequality) (see
[
58
, (22.25)]
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{P}}_{1}-{\mathbf{P}}_{2}\|_{\rm var}\leqslant\sqrt{2{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbf{P}}_{1}|{\mathbf{P}}_{2})}.

(4.3)

We now prepare the following result about the relative entropy
(see Lacker
[
38
, Lemma 4.1]
for a version of Brownian case).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
b_{i}:[0,T]\times{\mathbb{C}}_{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
i=1,2
be two progressively measurable vector fields
and
{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}(b_{i},\nu)
.
Suppose that the uniqueness in law holds for SDE
{}_{b_{2}}
and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{1}}\exp\left\{\lambda\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}\right\}<\infty,\ \ \forall\lambda>0,

(4.4)

where
{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}}(t):=\int^{t}_{0}(b_{1}-b_{2})(s,w_{\cdot}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
.
Then for some
C=C(H)>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbf{P}}_{1}|{\mathbf{P}}_{2})\lesssim_{C}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{1}}\left(\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}\right).

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, by definition, one has

{\mathscr{W}}^{b_{i}}_{t}:=w_{t}-w_{0}-\int^{t}_{0}b_{i}(s,w_{\cdot}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\mbox{is an {\rm f}Bm with respect to ${\mathbf{P}}_{i}$.}

Let
W_{t}:=\Phi({\mathscr{W}}^{b_{1}}_{\cdot})(t)
(see (
2.14
)). Then
W
is a standard Brownian motion under
{\mathbf{P}}_{1}
.
Write

Z_{T}:=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T}(\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}})(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right).

By (
2.17
), (
4.4
) and Novikov’s criterion,
{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{1}}Z_{T}=1
.
Thus, by Girsanov’s theorem (Theorem
2.6
),

t\mapsto{\mathscr{W}}^{b^{1}}_{t}+{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}}(t)={\mathscr{W}}^{b^{2}}_{t}

is still an fBm under
{\mathbf{Q}}:=Z_{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{1}
.
Thus
{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\mathcal{S}}(b_{2},\nu)
.
By the uniqueness in law of SDE
{}_{b_{2}}
, we have

Z_{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{1}={\mathbf{Q}}={\mathbf{P}}_{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbf{P}}_{1}|{\mathbf{P}}_{2})=-\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{T}}\ln Z_{T}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\mathbf{P}}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{1}}\left(\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{AS2}}}{{\lesssim}}_{C}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{1}}\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}.

(4.5)

Thus we complete the proof.
∎

By Theorem
2.10
and Girsanov’s theorem, it is by now standard to show the following result (see

50
, Theorem 2]
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that for some
(p_{1},q_{1})\in[\frac{1}{1-H},\infty]^{2}
with
\alpha:=\frac{1}{2}-(\tfrac{1}{q_{1}}+\tfrac{Hd}{p_{1}})>0
,

\kappa_{b}:=|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}}<\infty.

Then for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, there is a unique weak solution
{\mathbf{P}}_{x}\in{\mathcal{S}}(b,\delta_{x})
in the class that

{\mathbf{P}}_{x}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|b(s,w_{s})|^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s<\infty\right)=1.

Moreover, we have the following conclusions:

(i)

For any
1<p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
,
there is a constant
C=C(T,H,d,p_{1},q_{1},p,q)>0
such that for all
f\in\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
and
t\in(0,T]
,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{\cdot}}f(w_{t})|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}\leqslant\exp\left\{C\left(1+\kappa_{b}^{2/\alpha}\right)\right\}t^{\frac{dH}{q}-\frac{dH}{p}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}.

(4.6)

(ii)

For any
p,q\in(1,\infty]
with
\beta:=1-(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{p})>0
, there is a
C=C(T,H,d,p_{1},q_{1},\kappa_{b},p,q)>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
m\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int^{t}_{0}f(s,w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{C}}_{T};{\mathbf{P}}_{x})}\leqslant Ct^{\beta}m^{1-\beta}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}\tilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p}}.

(4.7)

(iii)

For any
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
and
p_{b},q_{b}\in(q_{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}},\infty]
satisfying

\beta_{H}:=1-q_{H}(\tfrac{1}{q}_{b}+\tfrac{Hd}{p_{b}})>0,

there is a constant
C=C(\gamma,d,H,p,p_{b},q_{b},\kappa_{b},T)>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
,
f\in{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{x}}f(\cdot-w_{t})-f(\cdot-x)\|_{p}\leqslant Ct^{H\gamma}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma}_{p}}+t^{\beta_{H}}\|f\|_{p}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q_{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p_{b}}},

(4.8)

where
{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
denotes the Sobolev space consisting of all functions
f
with

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\gamma}_{p}}:=\sup_{h\neq 0}\frac{\|f(\cdot+h)-f(\cdot)\|_{p}}{|h|^{\gamma}}+\|f\|_{p}<\infty.

(4.9)

Proof.

(Existence)

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
W^{H}
be an fBm over a probability space
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
. Define

\tilde{W}^{H}_{t}:=W^{H}_{t}-\int^{t}_{0}b(s,W^{H}_{s}+x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=:W^{H}_{t}-{\mathscr{I}}^{x}_{b}(t).

Since
b\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}-(\tfrac{1}{q_{1}}+\tfrac{Hd}{p_{1}})>0
, by Theorem
2.10
, for any
\lambda>0
, there is a
constant
C=C(\lambda,p_{1},q_{1},d,H)>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left\{\lambda\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}^{x}_{b}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right\}\leqslant\exp\left\{C\left(1+\kappa_{b}^{2/\alpha}\right)\right\}.

(4.10)

By Theorem
2.6
,
\tilde{W}^{H}
is an fBm under
{\mathbb{Q}}_{x}=Z^{x}_{T}{\mathbb{P}}
, where

Z^{x}_{t}=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}(\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}^{x}_{b})(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}^{x}_{b}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right),

is an exponential martingale. Here
W_{t}:=\Phi(W^{H}_{\cdot})(t)
(see (
2.14
)).
Now if we let
X^{x}_{t}:=W^{H}_{t}+x
, then

X^{x}_{t}=x+\int^{t}_{0}b(s,X^{x}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\tilde{W}^{H}_{t}.

In particular,
{\mathbf{P}}_{x}:={\mathbb{Q}}_{x}\circ(X^{x}_{\cdot})^{-1}\in{\mathcal{S}}(b,\delta_{x})
is a solution of SDE
b
.

(Uniqueness)

For
i=1,2
, let
{\mathbf{P}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}(b,\delta_{x})
so that
{\mathscr{W}}^{b}
is an fBm with Hurst parameter
H
under
{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
, and

{\mathbf{P}}_{i}(w_{0}=x)=1,\ \ {\mathbf{P}}_{i}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|b(s,w_{s})|^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s<\infty\right)=1.

Define a
{\mathscr{B}}_{t}
-stopping time by

\tau_{n}:=\inf\left\{t\in[0,T]:\int^{t}_{0}|b(s,w_{s})|^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s>n\right\}.

Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}(\tau_{n}=T)=1
. Let
{\mathscr{I}}_{b}(t):=\int^{t}_{0}b(s,w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
.
Note that

\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\leqslant C\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}\leqslant Cn^{1/q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}.

By Girsanov’s theorem,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{W}}^{b}_{t}+{\mathscr{I}}_{b}(t\wedge\tau_{n})=w_{t}-w_{0}-\int^{t}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}b(s,w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

(4.11)

is still an fBm under the new probability
{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}_{i}:=Z^{n}_{T}{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
, where

Z^{n}_{T}:=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{T}(\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{n}))(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{\bf K}_{H}{\mathscr{I}}_{b}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{n})\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}_{T}}\right).

In particular, for any
t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{m}\leqslant T
and
\Gamma_{i}\in{\mathscr{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, by (
4.11
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}_{1}(w_{t_{1}}\in\Gamma_{1},\cdots,w_{t_{m}}\in\Gamma_{m};\tau_{n}=T)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{T}}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{w_{t_{1}}\in\Gamma_{1},\cdots,w_{t_{m}}\in\Gamma_{m};\tau_{n}=T\}}/Z^{n}_{T}{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}_{1}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}_{T}}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{w_{t_{1}}\in\Gamma_{1},\cdots,w_{t_{m}}\in\Gamma_{m};\tau_{n}=T\}}/Z^{n}_{T}{\mathbf{Q}}^{n}_{2}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{P}}_{2}(w_{t_{1}}\in\Gamma_{1},\cdots,w_{t_{m}}\in\Gamma_{m};\tau_{n}=T).

Letting
n\to\infty
, we conclude the proof of uniqueness.

(Proofs of (i) and (ii))

Let
1<p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
and
p_{0}\in(1,p)
. Set
\gamma:=\frac{p}{p_{0}}
and
\frac{1}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}}=1
.
By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{x}}f(w_{t})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}_{x}}(f(X^{x}_{t}))={\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(Z^{x}_{T}f(W^{H}_{t}+x))\leqslant({\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(Z^{x}_{T})^{\gamma^{\prime}})^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}({\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}|f(W^{H}_{t}+x)|^{\gamma})^{1/\gamma}.

Noting that by (
4.10
) and Hölder’s inequality,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}|Z^{x}_{t}|^{\gamma^{\prime}}\leqslant\exp\left\{C\left(1+\kappa_{b}^{2/\alpha}\right)\right\}=:C_{0},

by Lemma
B.1
, we further have

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{\cdot}}f(w_{t})|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{0}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}|f(W^{H}_{t}+\cdot)|^{\gamma}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{1/\gamma}_{q/\gamma}\lesssim t^{\frac{dH}{q}-\frac{dH}{\gamma p_{0}}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||f|^{\gamma}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{1/\gamma}_{p_{0}}=t^{\frac{dH}{q}-\frac{dH}{p}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}.

Thus we get (
4.6
). For (
4.7
), it is similar by (
2.22
) and Hölder’s inequality.

(Proof of (iii)):
Let
{\mathbf{Q}}_{x}\in{\mathcal{S}}(0,\delta_{x})
and note that

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{x}}f(\cdot-w_{t})-f(\cdot-x)\|_{p}\leqslant\|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{Q}}_{x}}f(\cdot-w_{t})-f(\cdot-x)\|_{p}+\|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{x}}f(\cdot-w_{t})-{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{Q}}_{x}}f(\cdot-w_{t})\|_{p}.

Since
{\mathbf{Q}}_{x}
is the law of fBm starting from
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{Q}}_{x}}f(\cdot-w_{t})-f(\cdot-x)\|_{p}\leqslant\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\cdot-x-z)p_{t}^{H}(z){\mathord{{\rm d}}}z-f(\cdot-x)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|f(\cdot-z)-f(\cdot)\|_{p}p_{t}^{H}(z){\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|z|^{\beta}p_{t}^{H}(z){\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\lesssim t^{\beta H}\|f\|_{{\mathbf{B}}^{\beta}_{p}},

where
p_{t}^{H}(z)=(2\pi\lambda^{H}_{0,t})^{-d/2}e^{|z|^{2}/\lambda^{H}_{0,t}}
and
\lambda^{H}_{0,t}
is defined in (
2.20
).

Moreover, by (
4.3
), (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{x}}f(\cdot-w_{t})-{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{Q}}_{x}}f(\cdot-w_{t})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f\|_{p}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{x}\circ w_{t}^{-1}-{\mathbf{Q}}_{x}\circ w_{t}^{-1}\|_{\rm var}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}\left({\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{Q}}_{x}}\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b}\|_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}_{t}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\beta_{H}}\|f\|_{p}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}||b|^{q_{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}/q_{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}_{T}\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p_{b}/q_{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}=t^{\beta_{H}}\|f\|_{p}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q_{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{b}}_{T}\widetilde{{\mathbb{L}}}^{p_{b}}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In comparison with
[
51
, Theorem 2]
, we relax the condition
p_{1},q_{1}\geqslant 2
in
[
51
, Theorem 2]
to
p_{1},q_{1}\geqslant 1/(1-H)
in Theorem
4.3
.
This allows us to treat the Biot-Savart kernel in subsequent discussions.

It should be noted that recently, Butkovsky and Gallay
[
10
]
showed the existence of the weak solution under the weaker assumption for

with

which coincides with the result in
[
36
]
for
H=1/2
. But the uniqueness in this case is still open.
However,
based on the entropy estimate in Lemma
4.2
, we have the following partial result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
p_{1},q_{1}\in[\frac{1}{1-H},\infty)
satisfy
\frac{Hd}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-H}{q_{1}}<(1-H)^{2}
. Assume

be

in

as

. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let

be the unique strong solution of

Then the law
{\mathbf{P}}_{n}
of

{\mathbb{C}}_{T}
weakly converges to a solution

{\mathbf{P}}
a regular solution of SDE
b
. Moreover, for any two

{\mathbf{P}}_{i}
be the unique regular solution of SDE
{}_{b_{i}}
starting from
x
, where
i=1,2
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbf{P}}_{1}|{\mathbf{P}}_{2})\lesssim_{C}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{1}}\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{1}-b_{2}}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}.

(4.12)

Proof.

Let
\frac{Hd}{p}+\frac{1-H}{q}<1-H
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
By
[
10

, there is a constant

such that for all

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{n}}\left|\int^{T}_{0}f(s,w_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{m}={\mathbb{E}}\left|\int^{T}_{0}f(s,X^{n}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right|^{m}\leqslant C\|f\|^{m}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}}.

(4.13)

By Lemma
4.2
and the above Krylov’s estimate with

and

, where

, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbf{P}}_{n}|{\mathbf{P}}_{m})\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbf{P}}_{n}}\|{\mathscr{I}}_{b_{n}-b_{m}}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{T}}\lesssim\||b^{n}-b^{m}|^{q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}\|^{2/q_{{\lx@scalerel@obj{H}}}}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{p}}=\|b_{n}-b_{m}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}},

which implies by CKP’s inequality (
4.3
),

\lim_{n,m\to\infty}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{n}-{\mathbf{P}}_{m}\|^{2}_{\rm var}\leqslant 2\lim_{n,m\to\infty}{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbf{P}}_{n}|{\mathbf{P}}_{m})\lesssim\lim_{n,m\to\infty}\|b_{n}-b_{m}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}}=0.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}}_{T})
be the limit point so that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathbf{P}}_{n}-{\mathbf{P}}\|^{2}_{\rm var}=0.

(4.14)

It is easy to see
{\mathbf{P}}\in{\mathcal{S}}(b,\delta_{x})

k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
t_{1}\leqslant t_{2}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant t_{k}

where

Proof.

Let

and

We divide the proof into three steps.

(H
{}^{s}_{2}
)
with

In step 3, we show that

(Step 1).

Let

satisfy
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}
.
For any

(5.14)

and by the fact

and Lemma
B.5
,

, for any

).

(Step 2).

Let

, one sees that

\displaystyle\|\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,t}-\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,t}\|_{p_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\frac{1+d/p_{1}}{2}}\|B_{g}(\cdot,\mu^{\centerdot}_{r,T})-B_{g_{n}}(\cdot,\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{r,T})\|_{p_{1}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-u\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}_{T}{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|B_{g_{n}}(\cdot,\mu^{\centerdot,n}_{s,T})-B_{g}(\cdot,\mu^{\centerdot}_{s,T})\|_{p_{1}}

(5.15)

(Step 3).

Technical Lemmas

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\xi\sim N(0,\sigma^{2})
be a
d
-dimensional normal random variable with mean zero and variance
\sigma^{2}
.
For any
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, there is a constant
C=C(j,q,p,d)>0
such that for all
f\in\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}
,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbb{E}}\nabla^{j}f(\xi+\cdot)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}\lesssim_{C}(\sigma^{-j}+\sigma^{d/q-d/p-j})|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}.

(B.1)

Proof.

Note that by the integration by parts,

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\nabla^{j}f(\xi+x)|

\displaystyle=(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-d/2}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(y+x)\nabla^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-|y|^{2}/2\sigma^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-d/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(y+x)|\,|\nabla^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-|y|^{2}/2\sigma^{2}}|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\sigma^{-d-j}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(y+x)|\mathrm{e}^{-c|y|^{2}/\sigma^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y=\sigma^{-d-j}|f|*\phi_{\sigma}(x),

where we have used that for some
c>0
,

|\nabla^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-|y|^{2}/2\sigma^{2}}|\lesssim\sigma^{-j}\mathrm{e}^{-c|y|^{2}/\sigma^{2}}=:\phi_{\sigma}(y).

Let
1+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}
. By Young’s convolution inequality (
2.6
), we get

|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbb{E}}\nabla^{j}f(\xi+\cdot)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}\lesssim\sigma^{-d-j}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi_{\sigma}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{r}.

By the definition of
|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\cdot|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi_{\sigma}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{*}_{r}=\sum_{i}\left(\int_{D_{i}}\mathrm{e}^{-cr|x|^{2}/\sigma^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{1/r}\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\int_{D_{z}}\mathrm{e}^{-cr|x|^{2}/\sigma^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{1/r}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z,

where
D_{z}
is the unit cube with center
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Noting that for
|z|\geqslant\sqrt{d}
and
x\in D_{z}
,

|x|\geqslant|z|-|x-z|\geqslant|z|-\sqrt{d}/2\geqslant|z|/2,

we have

\int_{|z|\geqslant\sqrt{d}}\left(\int_{D_{z}}\mathrm{e}^{-cr|x|^{2}/\sigma^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{1/r}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-c|z|^{2}/4\sigma^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\lesssim\sigma^{d}.

On the other hand, we clearly have

\int_{|z|\leqslant\sqrt{d}}\left(\int_{D_{z}}\mathrm{e}^{-cr|x|^{2}/\sigma^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{1/r}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-cr|x|^{2}/\sigma^{2}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{1/r}\lesssim\sigma^{d/r}.

Hence,

|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbb{E}}\nabla^{j}f(\xi+\cdot)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{q}\lesssim\sigma^{-d-j}(\sigma^{d}+\sigma^{d/r})|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p},

which in turn gives the desired estimate.
∎

Recall

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(\alpha,\beta):=\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha-1}(1-r)^{\beta-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,\quad\text{for $\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0$}.

Lemma B.2

(Estimate for Beta functions)
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\beta>0
, we have for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(\alpha,k\beta+1)\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)k^{-\alpha}.

Proof.

For any
h\in(0,1)
, one sees that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(\alpha,k\beta+1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{h}r^{\alpha-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+h^{\alpha-1}\int_{h}^{1}(1-r)^{k\beta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\leqslant\frac{1}{\alpha}h^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{k\beta+1}h^{\alpha-1}\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{k\beta}h^{-1}\right)h^{\alpha}.

Taking
h=k^{-1}
, we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma B.3
.

For any
\alpha>0
, there is a constant
C=C(\alpha)>0
such that for all
\lambda\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{(m!)^{\alpha}}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{C\lambda^{1/\alpha}\ln\lambda}.

Proof.

By Stirling’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{(m!)^{\alpha}}\leqslant 1+C\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m^{m\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+C\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{\lambda^{x}}{x^{x\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=1+C\int^{\infty}_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha x\ln(\lambda^{1/\alpha}/x)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+C\int^{2\lambda^{1/\alpha}}_{1}\mathrm{e}^{x\ln\lambda}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x+C\int^{\infty}_{2\lambda^{1/\alpha}}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha x\ln 2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.

From this we derive the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma B.4

(Gronwall’s inequality)
.

Let
f(s,t),g(s,t):{\mathbb{D}}_{T}\to[0,\infty)
and
h:[0,T]\to[0,\infty)
be measurable functions. Assume that for all
(s,t)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{T}
,

f(s,t)\leqslant g(s,t)+\int^{t}_{s}h(r)(f(s,r)+f(r,T)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

Then we have

f(s,t)\leqslant G(s,t)+\int^{t}_{s}H(s^{\prime},t)\left(G(s^{\prime},T)+\int^{T}_{s^{\prime}}G(r,T)H(r)\mathrm{e}^{\int^{r}_{s^{\prime}}H(r^{\prime}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s^{\prime},

where

G(s,t):=g(s,t)+\int^{t}_{s}g(s,r)h(r)\mathrm{e}^{\int^{t}_{r}h(r^{\prime}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

and

H(r,t):=h(r)\left(1+\int^{t}_{r}h(r^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{\int^{t}_{r^{\prime}}h(r^{\prime\prime}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime}\right).

Proof.

For fixed
s\in[0,T]
, by the assumption we have

f(s,t)\leqslant F(s,t)+\int^{t}_{s}h(r)f(s,r){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

where

F(s,t):=g(s,t)+\int^{t}_{s}h(r)f(r,T){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

By the usual Gronwall’s inequality we get

\displaystyle f(s,t)

\displaystyle\leqslant F(s,t)+\int^{t}_{s}F(s,r)h(r)\mathrm{e}^{\int^{t}_{r}h(r^{\prime}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle=g(s,t)+\int^{t}_{s}h(r)f(r,T){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r+\int^{t}_{s}g(s,r)h(r)\mathrm{e}^{\int^{t}_{r}h(r^{\prime}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{s}\left(\int^{r}_{s}h(r^{\prime})f(r^{\prime},T){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime}\right)h(r)\mathrm{e}^{\int^{t}_{r}h(r^{\prime}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

\displaystyle=G(s,t)+\int^{t}_{s}H(r,t)f(r,T){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

where
G
and
H
are defined in the lemma.
In particular,

f(s,T)\leqslant G(s,T)+\int^{T}_{s}H(r,T)f(r,T){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

By Gronwall’s inequality again, we have

f(s,T)\leqslant G(s,T)+\int^{T}_{s}G(r,T)H(r)\mathrm{e}^{\int^{r}_{s}H(r^{\prime}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r^{\prime}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r,

Combining the above calculations, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma B.5
.

Let
E_{i},
be two Banach spaces with norms
\|\cdot\|_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Let
G:E_{1}\to E_{2}
be a Lipschitz mapping with
G(0)=0
and define

\displaystyle F(x):=G(x){\mathbf{1}}_{\|x\|_{1}\leqslant 1}+G(x){\mathbf{1}}_{\|x\|_{1}>1}/|x|_{1}.

Then for any
x,y\in E

\displaystyle\|F(x)-F(y)\|_{2}\leqslant 2\|G\|_{\rm Lip}\|x-y\|_{1}.

Proof.

We consider three cases: (i)
\|x\|_{1}\wedge\|y\|_{1}\leqslant 1
; (ii)
\|x\|_{1}\leqslant 1<\|y\|_{1}
; (iii)
\|x\|_{1}\wedge\|y\|_{1}>1
.
In case (i),
F(x)=G(x)
, it is trivial.
In case (ii), one sees that

\displaystyle\|F(y)-F(x)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|G(y)/\|y\|_{1}-G(x)\right\|_{2}\leqslant\left\|(G(y)-G(x))/\|y\|_{1}\right\|_{2}+\|G(x)/\|y\|_{1}-G(x)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\|G\|_{\rm Lip}\|x-y\|_{1}+\|G(x)\|_{2}(\|y\|_{1}-1)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|G\|_{\rm Lip}\left(\|x-y\|_{1}+\|x\|_{1}(\|y\|_{1}-\|x\|_{1})\right)\leqslant 2\|G\|_{\rm Lip}\|x-y\|_{1}.

In case (iii), we have

\displaystyle\|F(y)-F(x)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{G(x)\|y\|_{1}-G(y)\|x\|_{1}}{\|x\|_{1}\|y\|_{1}}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{\|(G(x)-G(y))\|y\|_{1}-G(y)(\|x\|_{1}-\|y\|_{1})\|_{2}}{\|x\|_{1}\|y\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|G\|_{\rm Lip}\|x-y\|_{1}+\|G(y)\|_{2}\|x-y\|_{1}/\|y\|_{1}\leqslant 2\|G\|_{\rm Lip}\|x-y\|_{1}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Consider the following PDE:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u,\quad u_{0}=\phi.

(B.2)

Theorem B.6
.

Let
q_{1},p_{1},p_{0}\in(1,\infty]
satisfy
\tfrac{2}{q_{1}}+\tfrac{d}{p_{1}}<1.

Assume
b\in{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
For any
p\in[p_{0}\vee\frac{p_{1}}{p_{1}-1},\infty]
with
\frac{2}{q_{1}}+\frac{d}{p_{0}}<1+\frac{d}{p}
,
there is a unique solution
u
to PDE (
B.2
) with

|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{j}u(t)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\lesssim t^{-\frac{j+d/p_{0}-d/p}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}.

Proof.

Let
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be the Gaussian heat semigroup. By Duhamel’s formula, we have

\displaystyle u(t)=P_{t}\phi+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}(b\cdot\nabla u)(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Let
p\in[p_{0}\vee p_{3},\infty]
satisfy
\tfrac{1}{p_{3}}=\tfrac{1}{p_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{p}\leqslant 1.

For
j=0,1
, by Lemma
B.1
, we have

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla^{j}u(t)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{j+d/p_{0}-d/p}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{j+d/p_{3}-d/p}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b(s)\cdot\nabla u(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{3}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{j+d/p_{0}-d/p}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{j+d/p_{1}}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{1}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla u(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

(B.3)

Suppose
\tfrac{2}{q_{1}}+\tfrac{d}{p_{0}}<1+\tfrac{d}{p}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla u(t)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q^{\prime}_{1}}_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-q^{\prime}_{1}\frac{1+d/p_{0}-d/p}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}+|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|b|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q^{\prime}_{1}}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q_{1}}_{T}\tilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-q_{1}^{\prime}\frac{1+d/p_{1}}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla u(s)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|^{q_{1}^{\prime}}_{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

which implies that by Gronwall’s inequality of Volterra’s type,

|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\nabla u(t)|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1+d/p_{0}-d/p}{2}}|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|\phi|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p_{0}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

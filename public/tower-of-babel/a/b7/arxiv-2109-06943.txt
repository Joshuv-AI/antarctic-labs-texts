Abstract
. The second named author and David Kalaj introduced a pseudometric on any domain in the real Euclidean space ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , defined in terms of conformal harmonic discs, by analogy with Kobayashi’s pseudometric on complex manifolds, which is defined in terms of holomorphic discs. They showed that on the unit ball of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , this minimal metric coincides with the classical Beltrami–Cayley–Klein metric. In the present paper we investigate properties of the minimal pseudometric and give sufficient conditions for a domain to be (complete) hyperbolic, meaning that the minimal pseudometric is a (complete) metric. We show in particular that a convex domain is complete hyperbolic if and only if it does not contain any affine 2-planes. One of our main results is that a domain with a negative minimal plurisubharmonic exhaustion function is hyperbolic, and a bounded strongly minimally convex domain is complete hyperbolic. We also prove a localization theorem for the minimal pseudometric.

Introduction

A conformal structure on a surface is given by a smooth atlas whose charts are related
by conformal diffeomorphisms of plane domains. A surface endowed with a conformal structure is
a
conformal surface
. An oriented conformal surface is a Riemann surface, and
a connected nonorientable conformal surface
M
admits a two-sheeted conformal
covering
\widetilde{M}\to M
by a Riemann surface.
A conformal surface is
hyperbolic
if its universal conformal covering
space is the disc
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. Every hyperbolic surface carries a (Riemannian)

Poincaré metric
,
\mathcal{P}_{M}
, such that any
conformal universal covering
h:\mathbb{D}\to M
is a local isometry from
(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}})

to
(M,\mathcal{P}_{M})
. The Poincaré metric on the disc is given by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,\xi)=\frac{|\xi|}{1-|z|^{2}}

(z\in\mathbb{D},\ \xi\in\mathbb{C})
. See
[
2
, Chapter 1]
for more details
on conformal surfaces and maps.

Let
G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the Grassman manifold of
2
-planes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
On any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 2)
,
Forstnerič and Kalaj
[
13
]
introduced a Finsler pseudometric

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}:\Omega\times G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty)
, and an associated Finsler pseudometric

g_{\Omega}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and pseudodistance

\rho_{\Omega}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
having the following two properties:

•

every conformal harmonic map
M\to\Omega
from a hyperbolic conformal surface,
M
,
with its Poincaré metric
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is metric- and distance-decreasing, and

•

the pseudometric
g_{\Omega}
and pseudodistance
\rho_{\Omega}
are the largest ones
with this property.

Recall that a nonconstant conformal harmonic map
M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
parameterizes
a possibly branched minimal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and every minimal surface
with isolated singularities arises in this way
(see
[
2
,
39
,
42
]
).
Hence, the pseudometric
g_{\Omega}
and pseudodistance
\rho_{\Omega}
describe the fastest
possible rate of growth of conformal minimal surfaces in
\Omega
.

The pseudometrics
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
and
g_{\Omega}
, and the pseudodistance
\rho_{\Omega}

are defined in terms of conformal harmonic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
,
by analogy with Kobayashi’s definition of his pseudometric on complex manifolds in terms of
holomorphic discs (see
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
).
We recall their construction and basic properties in Section
2
.
In Section
3
we show that the pseudodistance

\rho_{\Omega}
equals the infimum of the Poincaré lengths of chains
of conformal harmonic discs in
\Omega
connecting a given pair of points,
in analogy to Kobayashi’s definition of his pseudodistance by chains of holomorphic discs.
On domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}=\mathbb{C}
,
g_{\Omega}
and
\rho_{\Omega}
agree
with the Kobayashi pseudometric and pseudodistance, respectively.

The main part of the paper is devoted to developing geometric sufficient conditions for a
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
to be hyperbolic or complete hyperbolic, in the sense that

\rho_{\Omega}
is a (complete) metric on
\Omega
; see Definition
2.1
.
Note that a hyperbolic domain does not contain any minimal surfaces parameterized by
\mathbb{C}

(such as a catenoid, a helicoid, the Enneper’s surface, among others).
The main task in establishing (complete) hyperbolicity is to obtain suitable lower bounds
on the minimal pseudometric
g_{\Omega}
.

In Section
4
we show that a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is hyperbolic
if and only if the minimal pseudometric
g_{\Omega}
is bounded away from zero
on a neighbourhood of any point, and this holds if and only if the family
\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
of conformal
harmonic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
is pointwise equicontinuous for some metric inducing the topology of
\Omega
(see Theorem
4.2
).
These characterizations are analogous to Royden’s results

[
40
, Theorem 2]
on Kobayashi hyperbolicity.
We also establish a connection between (complete) hyperbolicity and tautness of a domain

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the latter condition means that
\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)

is a normal family; see Definition
4.6
.
The notion of a taut complex manifold was introduced by Wu
[
43
]
, and
the analogous result for the Kobayashi metric is due to Kiernan
[
28
]
.

In Section
5
we show that a hyperbolic convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is also complete hyperbolic, and this holds if and only if the domain
does not contain any affine
2
-planes, which is an obvious necessary condition
for hyperbolicity. The analogous results for Kobayashi hyperbolicity of convex domains
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
are due to Barth
[
4
]
, Harris
[
17
]
,
and Bracci and Saracco
[
7
]
. Our proof differs
substantially from those in the cited works.

In Section
6
we show that if the tube
\Omega\times\mathfrak{i}\mathbb{R}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

over a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is Kobayashi (complete) hyperbolic, then

is
(complete) hyperbolic; the converse fails in general.

In Section
7
we introduce another pseudometric

\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}:\Omega\times G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
on any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
,
defined by minimal plurisubharmonic functions on
\Omega
and satisfying

\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
(see Definition
7.5
and Proposition
7.6
).
Recall that an upper-semicontinuous function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is said to be

minimal plurisubharmonic

map

from a conformal surface

,

is a subharmonic function on

.
We summarize basic properties of such functions in Section
7
and
refer to
[
2
, Chapter 8]
for a more complete treatment.
(Note that MPSH functions are a special case with

of

-plurisubharmonic
functions that were introduced and studied in different geometries by Harvey and Lawson

[
18
,
19
,
20
]
.)
The pseudometric
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
is an analogue of the Sibony pseudometric on complex manifolds

[
41
]
, which is defined in terms of usual plurisubharmonic functions.
We show that most results for the Sibony pseudometric, obtained in
[
41
]
,
have their analogues for
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
. In particular, a domain with a negative MPSH
exhaustion function is hyperbolic (see Theorem
7.9
) and does not contain any

11.4
).

In Section
8
we obtain a localization result for the minimal pseudometric.
Given a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
at which there exists a local
negative MPSH function
u
on
\overline{\Omega}
peaking at
\mathbf{p}
, with
u(\mathbf{p})=0
,
we prove the estimate

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\cdotp)\geq\bigl(1-c|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}|\bigr)\,g_{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},r_{0})}(\mathbf{x},\cdotp)

with a constant
c=c(r_{0})>0
, where
\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},r_{0})
is the open Euclidean ball of radius
r_{0}

centred at
\mathbf{p}
(see Theorems
8.1
and
8.5
).
In particular, the quotient of the two metrics converges to
1
linearly in
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}|
as
\mathbf{x}\to\mathbf{p}
.
Localization results for biholomorphically invariant metrics on complex manifolds
(such as those of Kobayashi, Sibony, and Azukawa) have a long history; see the papers
[
41
,
14
,
5
,
15
,
38
,
24
,
10
,
12
]
, and the list is not exhaustive.

Localization results are often useful in establishing (complete) hyperbolicity.
In Section
9
we show that every bounded

strongly minimally convex domain

\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is complete hyperbolic;
see Theorem
9.2
.
(In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, such domains are also called
strongly mean-convex
since they are
characterized by their boundaries having positive mean curvature at every point.
Minimally convex domains play an important role in the theory of minimal surfaces, and
we refer to
[
11
]

and
[

for surveys and further references.
They are a particular case of
p
-convex domains, which were studied by Harvey and Lawson

[
18
,
19
,
20
]
.)
On a strongly minimally convex domain,
the minimal metric

is asymptotic to
|\mathbf{v}|/\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},b\Omega)

in the radial direction and to

in the tangential direction.
Theorem

is an analogue of the
result of Graham

that a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain in

[

We could not adapt these proofs to our situation.
Instead, we follow the approach developed by Ivashkovich and Rosay in
[
24

in the context of the Kobayashi metric on almost complex manifolds.

In Section

into hyperbolic domains across punctures. Theorem

is an analogue of a theorem
of Kwack
[

In Section

2.
Definition and basic properties of the minimal metric

To motivate the discussion and set the stage, we begin by recalling the definition of the
Kobayashi pseudometric on a connected complex manifold
X
.

We denote by
\mathscr{O}(\mathbb{D},X)
the space of holomorphic maps
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}\to X
.
Given a point
x\in X
, the Kobayashi pseudonorm of a tangent vector
\xi\in T_{x}X
is

(2.1)

\mathcal{K}_{X}(x,\xi)=\inf\bigl\{1/r>0:\exists f\in\mathscr{O}(\mathbb{D},X),\ f(0)=x,\ f^{\prime}(0)=r\xi\bigr\}\geq 0.

The Kobayashi pseudodistance
\mathrm{dist}_{X}(x,y)\geq 0
between a pair of points
x,y\in X

is the infimum of the numbers

\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\,dt

over all piecewise smooth paths
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
.
A complex manifold
X
is said to be (Kobayashi)
hyperbolic

if
\mathrm{dist}_{X}
is a metric on
X
, and

complete hyperbolic
if
(X,\mathrm{dist}_{X})
is a complete metric space.
Hyperbolicity clearly implies that there are no nonconstant holomorphic maps
\mathbb{C}\to X
.
If
X
is compact then the converse also holds according to Brody’s theorem

[
8
]
.
On the disc, the Kobayashi distance coincides with the Poincaré distance

\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}}}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+d(z,w)}{1-d(z,w)},\quad\text{where}\ d(z,w)=\frac{|z-w|}{|1-z\overline{w}|}\ \text{for}\ z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Any holomorphic map
X\to Y
between complex manifolds is
Kobayashi metric- and distance-decreasing, and this is the biggest
pseudometric having this property which
coincides on the disc
\mathbb{D}
with the Poincaré metric.

Suppose now that
\Omega
is a connected domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\geq 3
.
We recall from
[
13
, Section 6]
the definition of the minimal pseudometric

g_{\Omega}
and the minimal pseudodistance
\rho_{\Omega}
.
The point is to follow Kobayashi’s idea but using conformal harmonic discs instead of holomorphic discs.
The same construction applies in any Riemannian manifold.

A
conformal frame
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a pair of vectors
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

|\mathbf{u}|=|\mathbf{v}|\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{u}\,\cdotp\mathbf{v}=0.

The dot stands for the Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
|\mathbf{u}|=\sqrt{\mathbf{u}\,\cdot\mathbf{u}}

is the Euclidean norm of a vector
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with Euclidean coordinates
(x,y)
.
An immersion
f:D\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 2)
is said to be
conformal
if its
differential
df_{p}
at any point
p\in D
preserves angles. It is elementary
(cf.
[
2
, Lemma 1.8.4]
) that this holds if and only if

(2.2)

|f_{x}|=|f_{y}|\ \ \text{and}\ \ f_{x}\,\cdotp f_{y}=0.

In other words, the partial derivatives
(f_{x},f_{y})
are a conformal frame at every point.
A map
f:D\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}

(not necessarily an immersion) is called conformal if
(
2.2
) holds at each point.
In the same way we define conformal maps
M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
from any
conformal surface, in particular from any Riemann surface.

We denote by
\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
the space of (not necessarily immersed)
conformal harmonic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
.

We now introduce a couple of Finsler pseudometrics on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\ n\geq 3
.
The first one, called the
minimal metric
and denoted
g_{\Omega}
, is defined on the tangent bundle
T\Omega=\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
; its value at a point
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and tangent vector

\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

(2.3)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})=\inf\bigl\{1/r>0:\exists f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega),\ f(0)=\mathbf{x},\ f_{x}(0)=r\mathbf{v}\bigr\}.

Clearly,
g_{\Omega}
is upper-semicontinuous on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and absolutely homogeneous:

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},t\mathbf{v})=|t|g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

The
minimal pseudodistance

\rho_{\Omega}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

is obtained by integrating
g_{\Omega}
:

(2.4)

\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}g_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\,dt,\quad\ \mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Omega.

The infimum is over all piecewise smooth paths
\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
with
\gamma(0)=\mathbf{x}

and
\gamma(1)=\mathbf{y}
. Obviously,
\rho_{\Omega}
satisfies the triangle inequality, but it need not
be a distance function. In particular,
\rho_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
vanishes identically.
For every conformal harmonic disc
f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
we have

(2.5)

g_{\Omega}(f(z),df_{z}(\xi))\leq\frac{|\xi|}{1-|z|^{2}}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,\xi),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

and
g_{\Omega}
is the biggest pseudometric on
\Omega
with this property.
For
z=0
this follows from the definition of
g_{\Omega}
. For an arbitrary point

z\in\mathbb{D}
it is obtained by replacing
f
by the conformal harmonic disc
f\circ\phi
,
where
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
is a holomorphic automorphism of the disc interchanging

0
and
z
. (Explicitly, take
\phi(w)=\frac{z-w}{1-\bar{z}w}
and note that
|\phi^{\prime}(0)|=1-|z|^{2}
.)

It follows that conformal harmonic maps
M\to\Omega
from any conformal surface
are distance-decreasing in the Poincaré metric on
M
and the minimal metric on
\Omega
:

(2.6)

\rho_{\Omega}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}_{M}}(x,x^{\prime}),\quad\ x,x^{\prime}\in M;

furthermore,
\rho_{\Omega}
is the biggest pseudodistance on
\Omega
having
this property. For the disc
\mathbb{D}
, this follows directly from (
2.5
).
For other surfaces
M
, it is seen by passing to the universal conformal covering
\mathbb{D}\to M

(see
[
13
, Proposition 6.1]
). If
M
is not hyperbolic
then both sides of the inequality (
2.6
) vanish.

A map
R:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be a
rigid transformation

if it is a composition of an orthogonal map, a dilation,
and a translation. If
R(\Omega)\subset\Omega^{\prime}
for a pair of domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

(2.7)

g_{\Omega^{\prime}}(R(\mathbf{x}),dR_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{u}))\leq g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}),\quad\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\ \mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The same holds for any rigid embedding
R:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
for
3\leq n<N
.
In particular, if
R(\Omega)=\Omega^{\prime}
then

R:(\Omega,g_{\Omega})\to(\Omega^{\prime},g_{\Omega^{\prime}})

is an isometry. Rigid transformations are the only self-maps of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
taking
any conformal minimal surface to another such surface.
This is a marked difference from the Kobayashi pseudometric —
any holomorphic map between a pair of complex manifolds takes
complex curves to complex curves, and hence is distance-decreasing.
This difference reflects the fact that conformal minimal surfaces
are much more abundant than holomorphic curves, and the space of such surfaces
is preserved by a considerably smaller space of maps. This has major consequences when
estimating the minimal metric since nonrigid coordinate changes are not allowed.

The second Finsler pseudometric is defined on
\Omega\times G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
where
G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the Grassmann manifold of
2
-planes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For a point
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and a
2
-plane
\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we set

(2.8)

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)=\inf\bigl\{1/\|df_{0}\|:f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega),\ f(0)=\mathbf{x},\ df_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\Lambda\bigr\},

where
\|df_{0}\|
denotes the operator norm of the differential
df_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
It is immediate that
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
is upper-semicontinuous.
Comparing with the definition of
g_{\Omega}
(
2.3
)
it clearly follows that for any vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ |\mathbf{v}|=1
we have that

(2.9)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})=\inf\big\{\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda):\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda\}.

The set of
2
-planes
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
\mathbf{v}
is parameterized
by the sphere
S^{n-2}
in the normal space
\mathbf{v}^{\perp}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.
This illuminates an important difference from the Kobayashi metric
on a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
: a given nonzero vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
determines a
unique complex line
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{v}
, and for the Kobayashi metric we only consider
holomorphic discs tangent to that line.

In Section
7
we introduce another pseudometric

\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}:\Omega\times G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by minimal
plurisubharmonic functions on
\Omega
and satisfying
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
.
This lower bound helps us to establish several hyperbolicity results, a notion which we
now introduce.

Definition 2.1
.

A domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
is
hyperbolic
if
\rho_{\Omega}
is a distance function
on
\Omega
(i.e.,
\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})>0
for any pair of distinct points
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Omega
),
and is
complete hyperbolic
if
(\Omega,\rho_{\Omega})
is a complete metric space.

Example 2.2

(Minimal metric on the ball)
.

It was shown by Forstnerič and Kalaj
[
13
, Theorem 6.2]
that the metric
g_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}

on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
equals the
Beltrami–Cayley–Klein metric

whose value at any point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and tangent vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
equals

(2.10)

\mathcal{CK}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})^{2}=\frac{(1-|\mathbf{x}|^{2})|\mathbf{v}|^{2}+|\mathbf{x}\,\cdotp\mathbf{v}|^{2}}{(1-|\mathbf{x}|^{2})^{2}}=\frac{|\mathbf{v}|^{2}}{1-|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}+\frac{|\mathbf{x}\,\cdotp\mathbf{v}|^{2}}{(1-|\mathbf{x}|^{2})^{2}}.

This metric is complete, and it agrees with the restriction of the Kobayashi metric
(and, up to a multiplicative constant, of the Bergman metric) on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

to points of the real ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}=\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}
and real tangent vectors.
The extremal conformal minimal discs in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
are conformal parameterizations
of affine discs, intersections of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with affine 2-planes.
The operator norm
\|df_{z}\|
of the differential of such a disc equals
1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}

in this pair of metrics. However, the extremal discs are not isometries:
by
[
13
, Theorem 2.6]

the equality holds in (
2.5
) for some
(z,\xi)\in\mathbb{D}\times(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})

if and only if
f
is a conformal diffeomorphism onto the affine disc
\Sigma=(f(z)+df_{z}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathbb{B}^{n}

and the vector
df_{z}(\xi)
is tangent to the diameter of
\Sigma
through the point
f(z)
.
This differs from the complex case where the extremal holomorphic discs in

\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
are the affine complex discs, and their conformal parameterizations are isometries
in the Poincaré metric on
\mathbb{D}
and the Kobayashi metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

The ball seems to be the only hyperbolic domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

for which an explicit expression for the minimal metric is known.
Considering the ball
\mathbb{B}^{2n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
as a complex ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
we have that
g_{\mathbb{B}^{2n}}\leq\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}
but the two metrics are not equal.
By
[
13
, Corollary 2.3]
the Kobayashi extremal discs in
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}

are precisely those extremal conformal minimal discs which are complex, namely,
the proper affine complex discs in
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

\rho_{\Omega}
is an
inner pseudodistance
,
i.e., the pseudodistance between any pair of points equals the infimum of
g_{\Omega}
-lengths
of curves connecting them. Hence, the Hopf–Rinow theorem
[
22
]

(see also Jost
[
26
, Theorem 1.4.8]
) implies the following;
cf. Royden
[
40
, Proposition 7]
and Abate
[
1
, Proposition 2.3.17]

for the complex case.

Proposition 2.3
.

A hyperbolic domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is complete
hyperbolic if and only if for every point
\mathbf{p}\in\Omega
and number
r>0
the closed
ball
\{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega:\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x})\leq r\}
is compact.

When estimating the minimal pseudometric, one may use the
comparison principle
,
which says that for any triple of domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have that

(2.11)

\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega_{1}\ \Longrightarrow\ g_{\Omega_{1}}\leq g_{\Omega_{0}}\ \text{holds on $\Omega_{0}$}.

This is obvious from the definition of
g_{\Omega}
. The corresponding inequalities also
hold for the respective pseudometrics
\mathcal{M}
and pseudodistances
\rho

on the two domains. Together with Example
2.2
we obtain:

Corollary 2.4
.

Every bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
is hyperbolic.

The following example shows that there are bounded domains which are not complete hyperbolic.
A domain
\Omega
in this example clearly cannot be convex. In fact, we will show that every
hyperbolic convex domain is also complete hyperbolic; see Theorem
5.1
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{D}^{*}=\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
.
Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a connected domain whose boundary
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
near a point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
.
If there exists a conformal harmonic disc
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such
that
f(0)=\mathbf{p}
and
f(\mathbb{D}^{*})\subset\Omega
, then
\mathbf{p}
is at finite minimal distance
from
\Omega
.

Since the Poincaré metric on the punctured disc
\mathbb{D}^{*}

is complete at the origin (see
[
33
, p. 78]
),
we cannot make this conclusion by using the fact that
f:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\Omega

is distance-decreasing. Instead, we proceed as follows.
Let
\mathbf{N}
denote the inward unit normal vector to
b\Omega
at
\mathbf{p}
. Consider the family of conformal
harmonic discs
f_{t}(z)=f(z)+t\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
t\geq 0
. There are constants

c>0
and
0<\delta<1
such that
f_{t}(\delta\overline{\mathbb{D}})\subset\Omega
for all
0<t\leq c
.
Replacing
f(z)
by
f(\delta z)
we may assume that
\delta=1
.
Choose a point
a\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
, so

f(a)\in\Omega
. Given
\epsilon\in(0,c)
, we consider the path

\gamma_{\epsilon}:[0,1]\to\Omega
defined as follows:

\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}f(a)+(c+2t(\epsilon-c))\mathbf{N},&0\leq t\leq 1/2;\\
f(2(1-t)a)+\epsilon\mathbf{N},&1/2\leq t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Note that
\gamma_{\epsilon}(0)=f(a)+c\mathbf{N}
and
\gamma_{\epsilon}(1)=\mathbf{p}+\epsilon\mathbf{N}
.
The minimal length of the first part of
\gamma_{\epsilon}
for
0\leq t\leq 1/2

is smaller than the minimal length of the compact arc
\{f(a)+s\mathbf{N}:0\leq s\leq c\}\subset\Omega
,
which is a finite number
C>0
independent of
\epsilon
.
Since a conformal harmonic disc is metric-decreasing (
2.5
),
the minimal length of the second part of
\gamma_{\epsilon}
is less than or equal to
the Poincaré distance in
\mathbb{D}
from
0
to
a
, which is
\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+|a|}{1-|a|}
.
This shows that
\mathbf{p}
is contained in the closure of the minimal ball in
\Omega
of radius

C+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+|a|}{1-|a|}
centred at
f(a)+c\mathbf{N}\in\Omega
.
By Proposition
2.3
,
\Omega
fails to be complete hyperbolic at
\mathbf{p}
.
∎

By the comparison principle (
2.11
), an inscribed ball in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

provides an upper bound on
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
and
g_{\Omega}
.
If
\Omega
has
\mathscr{C}^{2}
boundary then by using a family of inscribed osculating balls,
we obtain upper bounds on
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
and
g_{\Omega}
, which are asymptotically optimal up to
a multiplicative constant when the point approached the boundary
b\Omega
.

The main problem when trying to establish (complete) hyperbolicity
is to obtain suitable lower bounds on
g_{\Omega}
. By using circumscribed balls, one can show
that every bounded strongly convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is complete hyperbolic,
and asymptotic estimates for the minimal metric
g_{\Omega}
follow from the formula (
2.10
)
for the minimal metric on the ball (see
[
13
, Theorem 6.9]
).
More generally, we show in Lemma
5.2
that the minimal distance to any affine
hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is infinite, which leads to a characterization of (possibly unbounded)
convex domains which are complete hyperbolic; see Theorem
5.1
.
However, the comparison principle does not provide good lower bounds on nonconvex domains,
and we shall develop more powerful methods to deal with this issue.

We conclude this section by comparing the minimal metric
g_{\Omega}

on a domain
\Omega
in an even dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

(n\geq 2)

with the Kobayashi metric
\mathcal{K}_{\Omega}
when considering
\Omega
as a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Since every holomorphic disc is also conformal harmonic, we see that
\mathcal{K}_{\Omega}\geq g_{\Omega}
.
In particular, if
\Omega
is minimal (complete) hyperbolic then it is Kobayashi (complete) hyperbolic.
The converse fails as the following example shows.

Example 2.6
.

Consider
\mathbb{D}
as the unit disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, identified with the real subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
The domain
\Omega=\mathbb{D}\times\mathfrak{i}\mathbb{R}^{2}
is not minimal hyperbolic since it
contains affine planes, but it is Kobayashi hyperbolic by Barth
[
4
]

since it does not contain any affine complex line.
∎

3.
The minimal distance is given by chains of conformal harmonic discs

In this section we show that the minimal pseudodistance
\rho_{\Omega}
(
2.4
)
can be defined by chains of conformal harmonic discs in
\Omega
. This definition of

\rho_{\Omega}
was introduced in
[
13
, Section 6]
,
and here we show that it coincides with the integral of the minimal pseudometric

g_{\Omega}
(
2.3
).
We follow the spirit of Kobayashi’s original definition in
[
30
]
of his pseudodistance
on complex manifolds, replacing holomorphic discs by conformal harmonic ones.
The proof of the desired equality is similar to Royden’s proof
[
40
, Theorem 1]

for the Kobayashi pseudometric. See also Kruglikov
[
34
]
for the corresponding result on almost complex manifolds.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
.
Fix a pair of points
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Omega
. Given a finite chain of conformal harmonic
discs
f_{i}:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
and points
a_{i}\in\mathbb{D}

(i=1,\ldots,k)
such that

(3.1)

f_{1}(0)=\mathbf{x},\quad f_{i+1}(0)=f_{i}(a_{i})\ \text{for $i=1,\ldots,k-1$},\quad f_{k}(a_{k})=\mathbf{y},

we define the length of the chain to be the number

(3.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}}}(0,a_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+|a_{i}|}{1-|a_{i}|}\geq 0.

We define the pseudodistance

(3.3)

\tau_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\geq 0,\quad\ \mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Omega

as the infimum of the lengths (
3.2
) of all chains
as in (
3.1
) (cf.
[
13
, Sect. 6]
).
Note that the Kobayashi pseudodistance on a complex manifold
X
is defined in
the same way by using chains of holomorphic discs
\mathbb{D}\to X
(see Kobayashi
[
30
,
31
]
).

We denote by
\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},\delta)
the open Euclidean ball of radius
\delta>0
centred at a
point
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and by
\overline{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{p},\delta)
its closure. By

[
13
, Theorem 6.2]
we have

\tau_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\rho_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{CK}},

where
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{CK}}
is the distance function induced by Beltrami–Cayley–Klein metric
(
2.10
). On other Euclidean balls we obtain the minimal distance
by translation and dilation of coordinates.
Hence, for every domain
\Omega
, point
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\Omega
,
and number
\delta>0
such that

\overline{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\delta)\subset\Omega
there is a
C>0
such that

(3.4)

\tau_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq C|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|\ \text{ for every }\mathbf{x},\,\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\delta).

Theorem 3.1
.

For any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
we have that

\rho_{\Omega}=\tau_{\Omega},

where
\rho_{\Omega}
and
\tau_{\Omega}
are defined by (
2.4
) and (
3.3
),
respectively.

Proof.

We follow Royden’s proof of the analogous result for the Kobayashi pseudodistance
(see
[
40
, Theorem 1, p. 130]
). Let
f_{i}:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{D}

(i=1,\ldots,k)

be as in (
3.1
).
Since conformal harmonic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
are distance-decreasing from the Poincaré distance
on
\mathbb{D}
to the pseudodistance
\rho_{\Omega}
(see (
2.6
)), we have that

\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{\Omega}(f_{i}(0),f_{i}(a_{i}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(0,a_{i}).

Taking the infimum over all finite chains as in (
3.1
)
gives
\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq\tau_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.

To prove the converse inequality, fix
\epsilon>0
and choose a smooth path

\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega
with
\gamma(0)=\mathbf{x}
and
\gamma(1)=\mathbf{y}
satisfying

\int_{0}^{1}g_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))dt<\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\epsilon.

Since
g_{\Omega}
is upper-semicontinuous, there is a continuous function

h:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
with
h(t)>g_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))
for all
t\in[0,1]
and such that

\int_{0}^{1}h(t)dt<\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\epsilon
. Then for every sufficiently fine partition

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1
of
[0,1]
we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(t_{i-1})(t_{i}-t_{i-1})<\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\epsilon.

By (
3.4
) there are constants
C>0
and
\delta>0

such that

(3.5)

\tau_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq C|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|\text{ for every }\mathbf{x},\,\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{B}(\gamma(t),\delta)\ \text{and}\ t\in[0,1].

Fix
t\in[0,1]
. Since
h(t)>g_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))
, there are a map
f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)

and a number
r>0
such that
f(0)=\gamma(t)
,
f_{x}(0)=r\dot{\gamma}(t)
, and
h(t)>1/r
.
Then for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
close to
0
we have

f(s/r)=\gamma(t)+\frac{s}{r}f_{x}(0)+O(s^{2})=\gamma(t)+s\dot{\gamma}(t)+O(s^{2}),

and hence

\displaystyle\tau_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\gamma(t)+s\dot{\gamma}(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau_{\Omega}(f(0),f(s/r))+\tau_{\Omega}(f(s/r),\gamma(t)+s\dot{\gamma}(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(0,s/r)+C|f(s/r)-\gamma(t)-s\dot{\gamma}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|s|/r+O(|s|^{2})<|s|\cdot h(t)+O(|s|^{2}).

(We also used that
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}(0,a)=|a|+O(|a|^{2})
for
a\in\mathbb{D}
close to
0
.)
Therefore for
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,1]
close enough we obtain

\displaystyle\tau_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\gamma(t^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\gamma(t)+(t^{\prime}-t)\dot{\gamma}(t))+\tau_{\Omega}(\gamma(t)+(t^{\prime}-t)\dot{\gamma}(t),\gamma(t^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|t^{\prime}-t|\cdot h(t)+O(|t^{\prime}-t|^{2}),

where the second term on the right hand side was estimated by (
3.5
).
This implies that for all sufficiently fine partitions
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1
of
[0,1]
we have that

\displaystyle\tau_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{\Omega}(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(t_{i}-t_{i-1})h(t_{i-1})(1+\epsilon)<(1+\epsilon)(\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\epsilon).

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, this concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Characterizations of hyperbolic domains

Recall that
g_{\Omega}
denotes the pseudometric (
2.3
)
on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The following concept mimics Royden’s notion of hyperbolicity
in a complex manifold (see
[
40
, p. 133]
).

Definition 4.1
.

A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
is
hyperbolic at a point
\mathbf{p}\in\Omega
if
there are a neighbourhood
U\subset\Omega
of
\mathbf{p}
and a constant
c>0
such that

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\geq c|\mathbf{u}|\ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in U$ and $\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

The domain
\Omega
is
R-hyperbolic
if it is hyperbolic at every point.

Recall (see Kelley
[
27
]
) that a family
\mathscr{F}
of mappings of a topological space
X

to a topological space
Y
is said to be an
even family
if, given points
x\in X
and
y\in Y
and
a neighbourhood
U\subset Y
of
y
, there are a neighbourhood
V\subset X
of
x

and a neighbourhood
W\subset U
of
y
such that for every
f\in\mathscr{F}

with
f(x)\in W
we have that
f(V)\subset U
.

The following is an analogue of Royden’s result
[
40
, Theorem 2, p. 133]
,
which pertains to the Kobayashi pseudodistance on a complex manifold.

Theorem 4.2
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(i)

The family
\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
of conformal harmonic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega

is pointwise equicontinuous for some metric
\rho
on
\Omega
inducing
the natural topology.

(ii)

The family
\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
is an even family.

(iii)

The domain
\Omega
is R-hyperbolic (see Definition
4.1
).

(iv)

The domain
\Omega
is hyperbolic in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

(v)

The distance function
\rho_{\Omega}
induces the topology of
\Omega
.

Remark 4.3
.

Following the terminology introduced for complex manifolds and holomorphic maps
\mathbb{D}\to\ \Omega

by Wu
[
43
]
and Royden
[
40
, p. 133]
,
a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with a metric
\rho
inducing the natural
topology of
\Omega
is called
tight
if condition (i) holds. Hence:

(\Omega,\rho)\ \text{is tight}\ \Longrightarrow\ \Omega\ \text{is hyperbolic}\ \Longrightarrow\ (\Omega,\rho_{\Omega})\ \text{is tight}.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

We follow Royden’s proof in
[
40
, Theorem 2]

of the corresponding results for the Kobayashi pseudodistance. Similar arguments were given by
Kiernan
[
28
]
.

Since every pointwise equicontinuous family of maps with respect to some metric on
\Omega
inducing the
topology of
\Omega
is an even family (see Kelley
[
27
, p. 237]
), (i) implies (ii).

Assume that (ii) holds. Choose
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and
\epsilon>0
such that

\overline{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)\subset\Omega
. Since
\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
is an even family, there are
a number
0<\delta<1
and a neighbourhood
W\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)
of
\mathbf{x}
such that for
any
f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
with
f(0)\in W
we have
f(\delta\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)
.
(Here,
\delta\mathbb{D}=\{z=x+\mathfrak{i}y\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<\delta\}
.) Fix a point
\mathbf{y}\in W
and a vector
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then, for any
f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
such that
f(0)=\mathbf{y}
and
f_{x}(0)=r\mathbf{u}
for some
r>0
, the map
h(z)=f(\delta z)

(z\in\mathbb{D})
is conformal harmonic,
h
maps
\mathbb{D}
into
\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)
,

h(0)=\mathbf{y}
, and
h_{x}(0)=\delta r\mathbf{u}
. This implies that

\delta g_{\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})\leq g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})
.
Since the metric
g_{\mathbb{B}}
on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

agrees with the Beltrami–Cayley–Klein metric (see Example
2.2
),
there is a
C>0
such that
g_{\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})\geq C|\mathbf{u}|
for all
\mathbf{y}\in W
.
Hence
\Omega
is R-hyperbolic at
\mathbf{x}
, which implies (iii).

The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) is an immediate consequence of the definition of
R-hyperbolicity (see Definition
4.1
) and the definition (
2.4
)
of the pseudodistance
\rho_{\Omega}
.

Assume that (iv) holds. By (
3.4
) and Theorem
3.1
the topology induced by

\rho_{\Omega}
is weaker than the standard topology on
\Omega
. To see that they are equivalent,
fix a point
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and a number
\epsilon>0
such that
\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},2\epsilon)\subset\Omega
.
By (
3.4
) and Theorem
3.1

the function
\mathbf{y}\mapsto\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is continuous in the standard topology.
Therefore, there is a point
\mathbf{y}_{0}
with
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}_{0}|=\epsilon
such that

\inf\{\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|=\epsilon\}=\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0}).

By (iv) the number
\delta=\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0})
is positive.
For any
\mathbf{z}\in\Omega\setminus\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)
each smooth path

\gamma
connecting
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{z}
crosses the boundary
b\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)
.
Denote the first such point by
\mathbf{y}
. Then the
\rho_{\Omega}
-length of
\gamma

is larger than or equal to the length of the restriction of
\gamma
to the part
connecting
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
, which is at least
\delta
. Taking the infimum over all paths connecting

\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{z}
we get that
\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})\geq\delta
. This gives

\{\mathbf{z}\in\Omega:\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})<\delta\}\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{x},\epsilon)
, so the topology
defined by
\rho_{\Omega}
is stronger than the standard topology. This implies (v).

Finally, the implication (v)
\Rightarrow
(i) is a consequence of the distance-decreasing
property conformal harmonic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
, see (
2.6
).
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

The following are equivalent for a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
.

(a)

The domain
\Omega
is hyperbolic.

(b)

The metric
g_{\Omega}
is comparable to the Euclidean metric
ds^{2}=dx_{1}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{n}^{2}

on any compact subset of
\Omega
.

(c)

The distance
\rho_{\Omega}
is comparable to the Euclidean distance
on any compact subset of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Assume that (a) holds. Pick a point
\mathbf{p}\in\Omega
. Let
B^{\prime}\Subset B\subset\Omega
be
a pair of balls centred at
\mathbf{p}
. Then
g_{\Omega}\leq g_{B}
holds on
B
by the comparison principle.
Since
g_{B}
is a Riemannian metric (see Example
2.2
),
it is comparable to the Euclidean metric
ds^{2}
on any smaller ball
B^{\prime}
,
and hence
g_{\Omega}\leq c^{\prime}ds^{2}
on a neighbourhood of
\mathbf{p}
for some constant
c^{\prime}>0
. (This holds on any domain.)
Since
\Omega
is hyperbolic, it is
R
-hyperbolic by Theorem
4.2
,
which means that
g_{\Omega}\geq cds^{2}
on a neighbourhood of
\mathbf{p}

for some
c>0
(see Definition
4.1
). This proves (a)
\Rightarrow
(b).
The implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(c) is obvious by integration
and using the argument in the proof of the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) in
Theorem
4.2
. The implication (c)
\Rightarrow
(a) is trivial.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
. For every conformal
surface,
M
, the set
\mathrm{CH}(M,\Omega)
is pointwise equicontinuous, and hence an even family.

Proof.

If
M
is not hyperbolic (i.e., its universal conformal covering space is
\mathbb{C}
),
then every conformal harmonic map
M\to\Omega
into a hyperbolic domain
\Omega
is constant.
Assume now that
M
is hyperbolic and let
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}}
denote the Poincaré distance
on
M
. Then, every conformal harmonic map
f:M\to\Omega
satisfies

\rho_{\Omega}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{P}_{M}}(x,x^{\prime})
for any pair of points
x,x^{\prime}\in M

(see (
2.6
)), so

\mathrm{CH}(M,\Omega)
is uniformly Lipschitz in this pair of metrics.
By Corollary
4.4
,
\rho_{\Omega}
is locally comparable
to the Euclidean metric. It follows that
\mathrm{CH}(M,\Omega)
is pointwise
equicontinuous, and hence an even family by Kelley
[
27
, p. 237]
.
∎

Recall that a complex manifold
\Omega
is called
taut
if the family
\mathscr{O}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)

of holomorphic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
is a
normal family
(see Wu
[
43
]
and Royden

[
40
, p. 135]
). We introduce the corresponding notion for domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

by using conformal harmonic discs.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(i)

A sequence
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
is
compactly divergent
if for any pair of
compact sets
K\subset\mathbb{D}
and
K^{\prime}\subset\Omega
,
f_{i}(K)\cap K^{\prime}
is empty
for all but finitely many indices
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

A family of maps
\mathscr{F}\subset\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
is
normal

if each sequence in
\mathscr{F}
has a subsequence that is either uniformly convergent on
compact sets in
\Omega
or compactly divergent.

(iii)

The domain
\Omega
is
taut
if the family
\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)

of conformal harmonic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
is a normal family.

Remark 4.7
.

Tautness of a domain
\Omega
is equivalent to the family

\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
being locally compact in the compact-open topology.
If
\Omega
is taut, then for any connected open Riemann surface,
M
, the space
\mathrm{CH}(M,\Omega)

of conformal harmonic maps
M\to\Omega
is also locally compact in the compact-open topology
(a normal family). In fact, given a point
p\in M
and a compact set
K\subset\Omega
,
the set
\{f\in\mathrm{CH}(M,\Omega):f(p)\in K\}
is compact.
∎

The next theorem is an analogue of the corresponding result for the Kobayashi metric,
due to Kiernan
[
28
]
and Royden
[
40
]
.

Theorem 4.8
.

The following hold for any domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
:

(i)

If
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic, then it is taut.

(ii)

If
\Omega
is taut, then it is hyperbolic.

\text{complete hyperbolic}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{taut}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{hyperbolic}

Proof.

Part (i) is a consequence of the Hopf–Rinow theorem

[
22
]
,
[
26
, Theorem 1.4.8]
;
see Proposition
2.3
and Royden
[
40
, Corollary, p. 136]
.
It also follows from the result of Kobayashi
[
33
, Theorem 3.1]
,
which pertains to more general situations.
Part (ii) is proved along the same lines as for the Kobayashi pseudodistance, see
Kiernan
[
28
, Proposition 2]
. ∎

5.
Hyperbolicity of convex domains

In this section we provide a geometric characterization of hyperbolic convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The corresponding result for Kobayashi hyperbolicity
of convex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is due to Barth
[
4
]
,
Harris
[
17
, Theorem 24]
, and Bracci and Saracco

[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
.

(i)

The domain
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic.

(ii)

For any open Riemann surface,
M
, the family
\mathrm{CH}(M,\Omega)
of conformal harmonic maps

M\to\Omega
is a normal family. In particular,
\Omega
is taut (see Definition
4.6
).

(iii)

The domain
\Omega
is hyperbolic.

(iv)

The domain
\Omega
does not contain any
2
-dimensional affine subspaces.

(v)

The domain
\Omega
has
n-1
linearly independent separating hyperplanes.

Recall that a hyperplane
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
separating
for a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

if
\Sigma\cap\Omega=\varnothing
, so
\Omega
lies in one of the two half-spaces in

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Sigma
.

It is a classical result of convexity theory that a convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

containing an affine line
L
is the product
\Omega=D\times L
, where
D
is a convex
domain in a hyperplane
perpendicular to
L
. Theorem
5.1
says that such
\Omega

is (complete) hyperbolic if and only if
D
does not contain any affine lines.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

The implications (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) hold for any domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

by Theorem
4.8
.
The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) is trivial. Indeed, an affine plane
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

contains arbitrarily big conformal linear discs, and hence for any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

containing
L
the minimal pseudodistance
\rho_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
between any pair of points

\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in L
vanishes.

The proof of (iv)
\Rightarrow
(v)
follows
[
7
, proof of Proposition 3.5]
, which pertains to the complex analytic case.
Assume that (iv) holds. We may assume by translation that
\mathbf{0}\in\Omega
.
We shall construct
n-1
linearly independent separating hyperplanes by induction.
Choose a point
\mathbf{p}\in\Omega^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. Since
\Omega
is convex,
there exists a separating hyperplane through
\mathbf{p}
, i.e., there are a point
\mathbf{y}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and a number
a_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\Omega\subset\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{1}>a_{1}\}
and
\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{1}=a_{1}
.
Assume inductively that for some integer
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}
there are
linearly independent separating hyperplanes determined by
the vectors
\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
. Their intersection

L=\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\bigl\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{j}=0\bigr\}

is an
(n-k)
-dimensional linear subspace.
Since
\Omega
does not contain any affine
2
-dimensional subspace,

L\cap\Omega^{c}
is nonempty, and we choose a point
\mathbf{q}\in L\cap\Omega^{c}
.
By the argument at the beginning of the proof there exists a separating
hyperplane through
\mathbf{q}
, i.e.,
there are
\mathbf{y}_{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a_{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}

such that
\Omega\subset\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{k+1}>a_{k+1}\}
and
\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{k+1}=a_{k+1}
.
By the construction, the vectors
\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{k+1}
are linearly independent.
This completes the induction and shows that (v) holds.

In the proof of the implication (v)
\Rightarrow
(i) we shall need the following lemma, which shows that
an affine hyperplane
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is at infinite minimal distance
from any point of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Sigma
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
be Euclidean coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, and let

\mathbb{H}
denote the half-space

\mathbb{H}=\bigl\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{1}>0\bigr\}.

If
\mathbf{x}(t)=(x_{1}(t),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}(t))\in\mathbb{H}

(t\in[0,1))
is a smooth path such that
x_{1}(t)
clusters at
0
or
+\infty

(i.e., there is a sequence
t_{j}\in[0,1)
with
\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}=1
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}x_{1}(t_{j})\in\{0,+\infty\}
), then

\int_{0}^{1}g_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbf{x}(t),\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t))\,dt=+\infty.

Proof.

Denote by
z=x+\mathfrak{i}y
the complex coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
.
Fix a point
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}
and a vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and consider
a harmonic map
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}):\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{H}
such that
f(0)=\mathbf{x}
and

f_{x}(0)=r\mathbf{v}
for some
r>0
. (We need not assume that
f
be conformal.)
Hence,
f_{1}:\mathbb{D}\to(0,+\infty)
is a harmonic function
with
f_{1}(0)=x_{1}
and
(f_{1})_{x}(0)=rv_{1}
. It follows that

(5.1)

r|v_{1}|=|(f_{1})_{x}(0)|\leq|\nabla f_{1}(0)|\leq 2|f_{1}(0)|=2x_{1},

where the second inequality holds by the Schwarz lemma for positive harmonic
functions. Therefore,

\frac{1}{r}\geq\frac{|v_{1}|}{2x_{1}}.

The infimum of the left hand side over all
r>0
as above equals
g_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
, so

g_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq\frac{|v_{1}|}{2x_{1}}.

Given a smooth path
\mathbf{x}(t)=(x_{1}(t),\mathbf{x}^{\prime}(t))\in\mathbb{H}

(t\in[0,1))
, the above shows that

g_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbf{x}(t),\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t))\geq\frac{|\dot{x}_{1}(t)|}{2x_{1}(t)},\quad\ t\in[0,1).

If
x_{1}(t)\in(0,\infty)
clusters at
0
or
+\infty
as
t\to 1
, then

\int_{0}^{1}g_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbf{x}(t),\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t))dt\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\dot{x}_{1}(t)|}{x_{1}(t)}dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}|d\log x_{1}(t)|=+\infty.

This proves the lemma.
∎

At any boundary point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
of a convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

there is a supporting affine hyperplane
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
passing through

\mathbf{p}
such that
\Omega
is contained in one of the half-spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Sigma
.
Hence, by Lemma
5.2
and the comparison principle (
2.11
),
any smooth path
\mathbf{x}(t)\in\Omega

(t\in[0,1))
which clusters at
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega

as
t\to 1
has infinite
g_{\Omega}
-length. This gives:

Corollary 5.3
.

A convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)

is locally complete hyperbolic at every boundary point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
.

We are now ready to prove the implication (v)
\Rightarrow
(i).
Assume that
\Omega
satisfies condition (v).
Up to a translation and rotation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there are linearly independent unit vectors

\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{n-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\{0\}
such that, setting

(5.2)

\mathbb{H}_{i}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{i}>0\},\quad\ i=1,\ldots,n-1,

we have that
\Omega\subset\bigcap_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{H}_{i}
.
Let
\mathbf{x}(t)=(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}(t),x_{n}(t))
,
t\in[0,1)
, be a divergent path in
\Omega
. Set

(5.3)

x_{i}(t):=\mathbf{x}(t)\,\cdotp\mathbf{y}_{i}=\mathbf{x}^{\prime}(t)\,\cdotp\mathbf{y}_{i}>0,\quad\ i=1,\ldots,n-1,\quad t\in[0,1).

If
\mathbf{x}(t)
clusters at some point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
as
t\to 1
,
then
\mathbf{x}(t)
has infinite
g_{\Omega}
-length by Corollary
5.3
.
Likewise, if one of the functions
x_{i}(t)
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
clusters at

+\infty
, then by Lemma
5.2
the path
\mathbf{x}(t)
has infinite minimal length
in
\mathbb{H}_{i}
, and hence also in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}_{i}
.

It remains to consider the case when the functions
x_{i}(t)
in (
5.3
) are bounded,

(5.4)

0<\mathbf{x}(t)\,\cdotp\mathbf{y}_{i}\leq c_{1},\quad\ t\in[0,1),\ i=1,\ldots,n-1,

and the path
\mathbf{x}(t)\in\Omega
does not cluster anywhere on
b\Omega
.
In this case, the last component
x_{n}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
of
\mathbf{x}(t)
clusters at
\pm\infty
as
t\to 1
,
and hence
\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{x}_{n}(t)|dt=+\infty
. Hence, to see that the path
\mathbf{x}(t)
has infinite

g_{\Omega}
-length, it suffices to show that

(5.5)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x}(t),\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t))\geq c_{2}|\dot{x}_{n}(t)|

for constant
c_{2}>0
depending on
c_{1}>0
in (
5.4
)
and the vectors
\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{n-1}
.

Fix a point
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
satisfying (
5.4
) and a unit vector
\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{v}^{\prime},v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and consider a conformal harmonic map
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}):\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
such that
f(0)=\mathbf{x}
and

f_{x}(0)=r\mathbf{v}
for some
r>0
. Then,
f_{y}(0)=r\mathbf{w}=r(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},w_{n})

where
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})
is an orthonormal frame:

0=\mathbf{v}\,\cdotp\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\,\cdotp\mathbf{w}^{\prime}+v_{n}w_{n},\quad\ |\mathbf{v}|=|\mathbf{w}|=1.

From this and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality it follows that

v_{n}^{2}(1-|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}|^{2})=v_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{2}=|\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\,\cdotp\mathbf{w}^{\prime}|^{2}\leq|\mathbf{v}^{\prime}|^{2}|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}|^{2}=(1-v_{n}^{2})|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}|^{2},

and hence

|v_{n}|\leq|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}|\leq c_{3}\max_{i=1,\ldots,n-1}|\mathbf{w}\,\cdotp\mathbf{y}_{i}|

where the constant
c_{3}>0
depends only on the (linearly independent) vectors

\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{n-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\{0\}
. Therefore,

r|v_{n}|\leq c_{3}\max_{i=1,\ldots,n-1}r|\mathbf{w}\,\cdotp\mathbf{y}_{i}|\leq 2c_{3}\max_{i=1,\ldots,n-1}\mathbf{x}\,\cdotp\mathbf{y}_{i},

where the second estimate follows from (
5.1
). (It suffices to apply
(
5.1
) to the conformal harmonic disc
z\mapsto\tilde{f}(z)=f(\mathfrak{i}z)
in each of the
half-spaces
\mathbb{H}_{i}
(
5.2
). Note that
\tilde{f}_{x}(0)=f_{y}(0)=r\mathbf{w}
.)
Together with the assumption (
5.4
) and setting
c_{2}=(2c_{1}c_{3})^{-1}
,
this gives

\frac{1}{r}\geq\frac{|v_{n}|}{2c_{3}\max_{i=1,\ldots,n-1}\mathbf{x}\,\cdotp\mathbf{y}_{i}}\geq\frac{|v_{n}|}{2c_{1}c_{3}}=c_{2}|v_{n}|

for any
r>0
as above. Taking the infimum of the left hand side
gives
g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq c_{2}|v_{n}|
. Applying this with
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}(t)
and
\mathbf{v}=\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)

yields (
5.5
). This proves that
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic, so (i) holds.
∎

The famous
half-space theorem
of Hoffman and Meeks
[
21
]

says that a half-space in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
does not contain any non-flat minimal surfaces which are
proper in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Clearly, properness is a key assumption in their result.
However, we make the following observation.

Proposition 5.4

(Half-space theorem for parabolic minimal surfaces)
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a half-space in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and let
M
be the complement of finitely many points
in a compact Riemann surface
R
without boundary. Then, every conformal minimal surface

f:M\to\mathbb{H}
(possibly branched, not necessarily proper) is flat, with image
contained in an affine plane parallel to
b\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{H}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x>0\}
. The function
u(x)=\frac{1}{x+1}

is strongly convex on
(0,\infty)
and takes values in
(0,1)
. Hence,
u
is MPSH on
\mathbb{H}
,
considered as a function independent of the
y
and
z
variables.
Given a conformal harmonic map
f=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}):M\to\mathbb{H}
, the function
u\circ f=f_{1}
is bounded
subharmonic on
M
, and hence it extends across the punctures to a subharmonic
function on
R
. Since
R
is compact, the extended function is constant by
the maximum principle, so
f(M)
lies in a hyperplane
x=const>0
.
∎

6.
The minimal metric on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the Kobayashi metric on
\Omega\times\mathfrak{i}\mathbb{R}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

In this section we show that the minimal metric on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
is in a certain sense bounded from below by the Kobayashi metric on the tube

\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}=\Omega\times\mathfrak{i}\mathbb{R}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In particular, if

\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
is Kobayashi (complete) hyperbolic then
\Omega
is
(complete) hyperbolic; see Theorem
6.1
.

Fix a point
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and a
2
-plane
\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and let

(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a conformal frame spanning
\Lambda
.
Then, the complex vectors
\mathbf{u}\pm\mathfrak{i}\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
are
null vectors
, i.e.,
they belong to the
null quadric

\mathcal{A}=\bigl\{\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+\ldots+z_{n}^{2}=0\bigr\}.

(See
[
2
, Section 2.3]
for the details.)
Conversely, the real and imaginary part of a null vector
0\neq\xi\in\mathcal{A}
is a conformal frame.
A pair of conformal frames
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
and
(\mathbf{u}^{\prime},\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
span the same
2
-plane
if and only if the corresponding complex vectors satisfy

\mathbf{u}+\mathfrak{i}\mathbf{v}=c(\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\pm\mathfrak{i}\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\ \ \text{for some}\ \ c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
be a conformal harmonic disc with
f(0)=\mathbf{x}
and
df_{0}\neq 0
.
Denote by
\zeta=x+\mathfrak{i}y
the complex coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
.
The vectors

\mathbf{u}=f_{x}(0)/|f_{x}(0)|,\quad\ \mathbf{v}=f_{y}(0)/|f_{y}(0)|=f_{y}(0)/|f_{x}(0)|

form an orthonormal frame spanning the 2-plane
\Lambda=df_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and

\|df_{0}\|=|f_{x}(0)|=|f_{y}(0)|
. Let
F=f+\mathfrak{i}g:\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}

be the holomorphic extension of
f
with
g(0)=0
. Then
F
is a holomorphic null disc
with
F(0)=\mathbf{x}
, in the sense that its complex derivative map
F^{\prime}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
has range in the
null quadric
\mathcal{A}
. The Cauchy–Riemann equations imply

F^{\prime}(0)=f_{x}(0)+\mathfrak{i}g_{x}(0)=f_{x}(0)-\mathfrak{i}f_{y}(0)=|f_{x}(0)|(\mathbf{u}-\mathfrak{i}\mathbf{v}).

Recall that
\mathcal{K}
denotes the infinitesimal Kobayashi pseudometric.
Since holomorphic null discs in
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}

are a subset of the space of all holomorphic discs
\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
, it follows that

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)\ \geq\ \mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}}\left(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}-\mathfrak{i}\mathbf{v}\right).

From this and the definition of
g_{\Omega}
(
2.3
)
we infer that for any
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have that

(6.1)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\ \geq\ \inf_{\mathbf{v}}\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}+\mathfrak{i}\mathbf{v}),

where the infimum is over all vectors
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
is a conformal frame.
(If
\mathbf{u}\neq 0
then vectors
\mathbf{v}
with this property form an
(n-2)
-sphere in the hyperplane
orthogonal to
\mathbf{u}
. If
\mathbf{u}=0
then both sides equal zero.)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
, and set

\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}=\Omega\times\mathfrak{i}\mathbb{R}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

(i)

If
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
is Kobayashi hyperbolic, then
\Omega
is hyperbolic.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
is Kobayashi complete hyperbolic, then
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic.

Proof.

Part (i) is a consequence of (
6.1
) and Royden’s characterization
of hyperbolicity (see Definition
4.1

and Theorem
4.2
for minimal hyperbolicity, and

[
40
, Theorem 2, p. 133]
for Kobayashi hyperbolicity).

If
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
is Kobayashi complete hyperbolic, then it is taut and hence pseudoconvex
(see Wu
[
43
]
or Kobayashi
[
32
, Theorem 5.2.1]
).
It follows that
\Omega
is convex
[
23
, Corollary 2.5.12]
.
Since
\Omega
is hyperbolic by part (a), Theorem
5.1
implies that it is complete
hyperbolic.
∎

Example 6.2
.

The implication in Theorem
6.1
(i) cannot be reversed.
Indeed, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a convex domain which contains an affine line, but it does
not contain any affine
2
-plane. Since the tube in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
over an affine line in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
contains an affine copy of
\mathbb{C}
,

\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
is not Kobayashi hyperbolic. On the other hand, since
\Omega
does not contain
any affine plane, it is (complete) hyperbolic by Theorem
5.1
.
∎

7.
A pseudometric defined by minimal log-plurisubharmonic functions

In this section we introduce a Finsler pseudometric
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}

on any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defined by minimal log-plurisubharmonic functions
\Omega\to[0,1]
;
see Definition
7.5
. Its main interest is that
it gives a lower bound for the minimal pseudometric
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
(see Proposition
7.6
),
which can often be used to establish hyperbolicity; see e.g. Theorem
7.9
.
We follow ideas from Sibony’s paper
[
41
]
, which pertain to the complex case.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be Euclidean coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Given a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

and a function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x},\cdotp)

its Hessian form at
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
, given by the symmetric matrix
H_{u}(\mathbf{x})=(u_{x_{i}x_{j}}(\mathbf{x}))_{i,j=1}^{n}

of second order partial derivatives of
u
at
\mathbf{x}
. The trace of
\mathrm{Hess}_{u}
is the Laplace operator:

\mathrm{tr}\,\mathrm{Hess}_{u}=\Delta u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{x_{i}x_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i},

where
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{Hess}_{u}
.
Given
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and a
2
-plane
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})

denote the trace of the restriction of
\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})
to
\Lambda
.
Choosing an orthonormal frame
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})
for
\Lambda
and setting

\tilde{u}(x,y)=u(\mathbf{x}+x\mathbf{v}+y\mathbf{w})
for
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
near
(0,0)
, it is immediate that

(7.1)

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})=\Delta\tilde{u}(0,0)=\Delta_{(x,y)}u(\mathbf{x}+x\mathbf{v}+y\mathbf{w})|_{x=y=0}.

If
\Lambda
is a complex line in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
then

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})
equals the Levi form
\langle dd^{c}u|_{\mathbf{x}},\xi\wedge J\xi\rangle

of
u
at
\mathbf{x}
on any unit vector
\xi\in\Lambda
. (Here,
J
is the standard almost complex
structure operator on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.)

We recall the following notion from
[
2
, Definition 8.1.1]
.

Definition 7.1
.

An upper-semicontinuous function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is

minimal plurisubharmonic
, abbreviated MPSH, if for every affine
2
-plane
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

the restriction
u:L\cap\Omega\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is subharmonic
in any conformal linear coordinates on
L
.

Note that every MPSH function on a domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}=\mathbb{C}^{n}

is also plurisubharmonic in the usual sense of complex analysis.
Indeed, an upper-semicontinuous function is plurisubharmonic if and only if its
restriction to every affine complex line is subharmonic (see Klimek
[
29
]
);
the latter constitute a small subset of the set of all affine
2
-planes.

By
[
2
, Proposition 8.1.2]
, a function

u\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\Omega)
is MPSH if and only if

(7.2)

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0\ \ \text{holds for every $(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)\in\Omega\times G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$},

and this holds if and only if
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{x})+\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
,
where
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{x})
and
\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{x})
denote the smallest two eigenvalues of

\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})
.
We say that
u\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\Omega)
is
strongly minimally plurisubharmonic
if strong
inequality holds in (
7.2
). The key property of MPSH functions pertaining
to minimal surfaces is the following; see
[
2
, Corollary 8.1.7]
.

Proposition 7.2
.

An upper-semicontinuous function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is MPSH if and only if
for each conformal harmonic map
f:M\to\Omega
from an open conformal surface
the composition
u\circ f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a subharmonic function on
M
.
If in addition
u\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\Omega)
is strongly MPSH and
f
is an immersion,
then
u\circ f
is strongly subharmonic.

For functions
u
of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}(\Omega)
, this follows from the following formula,
which holds for every conformal harmonic map
f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega

(see
[
2
, Lemma 8.1.3]
):

(7.3)

\Delta(u\circ f)(z)=\mathrm{tr}_{df_{z}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(f(z))\,\cdotp\|df_{z}\|^{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Note that the second order derivative of
f
does not appear in the formula, and the expression vanishes at
any critical point of
f
. If
f
is not conformal harmonic, the formula contains an additional term
involving the mean curvature vector field of
f
;
see
[
2
, Eq. (8.6)]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be the Euclidean coordinate on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
.

(a)

The function
\log|\mathbf{x}|
is MPSH on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(b)

If
u
is MPSH and
\chi
is a convex increasing function defined
on the range of
u
, then
\chi\circ u
is MPSH.
In particular,
\mathbf{x}\mapsto|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{p}
is MPSH on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for every
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p>0
.

(c)

If
u
is MPSH on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
then
the function
\mathbf{x}\mapsto|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{u(\mathbf{x})}
and its logarithm are MPSH on
\Omega
.

Proof.

The Hessian matrix of
\log|\mathbf{x}|=\frac{1}{2}\log|\mathbf{x}|^{2}
equals

H(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}I-\frac{2}{|\mathbf{x}|^{4}}\bigl(x_{i}x_{j}\bigr)_{i,j=1}^{n},

where
I
stands for the
n\times n
identity matrix.
At the point
(p,0,\ldots,0)
with
p>0
this equals
p^{-2}\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,\ldots,+1)
,
so the sum of the smallest two eigenvalues equals zero. By rotational symmetry
the same holds at every point of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, so
\log|\mathbf{x}|
is MPSH.
(See also
[
20
, Example 2.8]
.)

Part (b) is a consequence of the fact that for any pair of
functions
g:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(7.4)

\Delta(\chi\circ g)=(\chi^{\prime}\circ g)\Delta g+(\chi^{\prime\prime}\circ g)|\nabla g|^{2}.

Applying this with
\chi(t)=e^{pt}
and using that
\log|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|
is MPSH
gives the second statement in (b). Part (c) follows from (a) and (b), noting that if

u
and
v
are MPSH then so are
u+v
and
\mathrm{e}^{u}
.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

In the complex case, the function
\log{\sum_{i=1}^{k}|f_{i}|^{2}}
is plurisubharmonic
for any collection of holomorphic functions
f_{i}
which are not all identically zero
on a given connected complex manifold. Nothing similar holds for MPSH functions.
In fact, it can be seen that for an
n\times n
matrix
A
, the function
\log|A\mathbf{x}|

is MPSH if and only if
A=rO
where
r>0
and
O
is an orthogonal matrix.
One can get functions which are MPSH in some neighbourhood of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by adding
higher order terms to the argument of
\log
, but such functions do not seem
useful for our subsequent analysis. This again reflects the fact that
only rigid motions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
preserve the class of conformal minimal surfaces.
∎

Recall that
G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the Grassman manifold of
2
-planes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 7.5
.

The pseudometric
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}:\Omega\times G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

(7.5)

\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{u}\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})},\quad\ \mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\ \Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where the supremum is over all MPSH functions
u:\Omega\to[0,1]

which are of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
near
\mathbf{x}
such that
u(\mathbf{x})=0
and

\log u
is MPSH on
\Omega
. If there are no such functions other than

u=0
, which may happen if
\Omega
is unbounded, we set
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)=0
.

Proposition 7.6
.

On any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
we have that
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Fix
(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)\in\Omega\times G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
be such that
f(0)=\mathbf{x}

and
df_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\Lambda
. Also, let
u:\Omega\to[0,1]
be as in
the definition of the pseudometric
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
(see Definition
7.5
).
The function
v=u\circ f:\mathbb{D}\to[0,1]

is then subharmonic, of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
near the origin,
v(0)=0
, and

\log v=\log u\circ f:\mathbb{D}\to[-\infty,0)
is also subharmonic.
By Sibony
[
41
, Proposition 1]
we have that
\Delta v(0)\leq 4
.
(The unique extremal function with
\Delta v(0)=4
is
v(x+\mathfrak{i}y)=x^{2}+y^{2}
.)
Hence, (
7.3
) implies

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})\,\cdotp\|df_{0}\|^{2}=\Delta v(0)\leq 4.

Equivalently,

\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})}\leq\frac{1}{\|df_{0}\|}.

The supremum of the left hand side over all admissible functions
u
equals

\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)
(see (
7.5
)), while the infimum of the right hand side
over all conformal harmonic discs
f
as above equals
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)

(see (
2.8
)). This proves Proposition
7.6
.
∎

We now give some applications of the lower bound in Proposition
7.6

in the spirit of those in
[
41
]
.
Pick a smooth increasing function
\theta:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
such that

(7.6)

\theta(t)=t\ \ \text{for}\ 0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad\ \ \theta(t)=1\ \ \text{for}\ t\geq 1.

Let
A>0
be chosen such that

(7.7)

(\log\theta)^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq-A\ \ \text{for all $t\geq\frac{1}{2}$}.

Fix a constant
\beta>0
and set

(7.8)

h(\mathbf{x})=\log\theta(\beta|\mathbf{x}|^{2}),\quad\ \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Lemma 7.7
.

If
h
is given by (
7.8
) and
A
satisfies (
7.7
), then

(7.9)

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{h}(\mathbf{x})\geq-4A\beta\ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}$ and $\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\theta
and in view of Lemma
7.3
(a),
h
is MPSH except perhaps
on the spherical shell
\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\leq 1
. Fix a point
\mathbf{x}
in this shell and a 2-plane

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
be an orthonormal frame for
\Lambda
.
Consider the function
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

g(x,y)=\beta|\mathbf{x}+x\mathbf{u}+y\mathbf{v}|^{2}=\beta\left(|\mathbf{x}|^{2}+2x(\mathbf{x}\cdotp\mathbf{u})+2y(\mathbf{x}\,\cdotp\mathbf{v})+x^{2}+y^{2}\right).

We have that
\Delta g=4\beta
,
g_{x}(0,0)=2\beta\,\mathbf{x}\cdotp\mathbf{u}
,

g_{y}(0,0)=2\beta\,\mathbf{x}\cdotp\mathbf{v}
, and hence (since
\beta|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\leq 1
)

(7.10)

|\nabla g(0,0)|^{2}=4\beta^{2}\left(|\mathbf{x}\cdotp\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\mathbf{x}\cdotp\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)\leq 4\beta^{2}|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\leq 4\beta.

Applying the formula (
7.4
) with the increasing function
\chi=\log\theta
gives

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{h}(\mathbf{x})=\Delta(\log\theta\circ g)(0,0)\geq\theta^{\prime\prime}(\beta|\mathbf{x}|^{2})|\nabla g(0,0)|^{2}\geq-4A\beta.

This is the estimate (
7.9
).
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
,
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
r>0
.
Assume that
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,0)
is an MPSH function of class

\mathscr{C}^{2}
such that for some
c>0
we have

(7.11)

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})\geq c>0\ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},2r)$ and $\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

Let the constant
A>0
satisfy (
7.7
). Then we have that

(7.12)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\geq\frac{1}{r}\,\mathrm{e}^{2Au(\mathbf{x})/cr^{2}}|\mathbf{u}|\ \ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},r)\cap\Omega$ and $\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Proof.

Let the function
\theta
be as in (
7.6
). Fix a point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},r)\cap\Omega
.
Given a constant
\lambda>0
, we consider the smooth function

\Psi(\mathbf{y})=\theta\left(r^{-2}|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u(\mathbf{y})},\quad\ \mathbf{y}\in\Omega.

Note that
0\leq\Psi\leq 1
,
\Psi(\mathbf{x})=0
, and
\log\Psi
is MPSH except perhaps on the
spherical shell

S_{r}=\left\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}\leq|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}|\leq r\right\}\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},2r)

intersected with
\Omega
.
On
S_{r}\cap\Omega
we have by Lemma
7.7
and the assumption (
7.11
) that

(7.13)

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{\log\Psi}\geq-\frac{4A}{r^{2}}+c\lambda,\quad\ \Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Choosing
\lambda=\frac{4A}{cr^{2}}
we ensure that
\log\Psi
is MPSH on
\Omega
.
For this value of
\lambda
, the function
\Psi
is a candidate
for computing
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)
(
7.5
). A calculation gives
for all
\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that

(7.14)

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{\Psi}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{4}{r^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda u(\mathbf{x})}=\frac{4}{r^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{4Au(\mathbf{x})/cr^{2}}.

Taking into account (
7.5
) and Proposition
7.6
it follows that

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)\geq\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{\Psi}(\mathbf{x})}=\frac{1}{r}\,\mathrm{e}^{2Au(\mathbf{x})/cr^{2}}.

From this and the property (
2.9
) of the metric
g_{\Omega}
we clearly get (
7.12
).
∎

The following result is an analogue of
[
41
, Theorem 3, Corollary 5, and Proposition 6]
.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
.

(a)

If there is a negative MPSH function on
\Omega
which is smooth strongly MPSH on a neighbourhood
of a point
\mathbf{p}\in\Omega
, then
\Omega
is hyperbolic at
\mathbf{p}
(see Definition
4.1
).

(b)

If
u
is a negative
\mathscr{C}^{2}
function on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(7.15)

\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})\geq c>0\ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega$ and $\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$}

and
A>0
is as in (
7.7
), then the minimal metric
g_{\Omega}
satisfies the estimate

(7.16)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq\sqrt{\frac{c}{4A\mathrm{e}}}\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{\sqrt{|u(\mathbf{x})|}}\ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega$ and $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

(c)

A domain with a bounded strongly MPSH function is hyperbolic.

(d)

If
\Omega
admits a continuous MPSH exhaustion function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,0)

(such a domain is called hyperconvex, see Definition
11.3
),
then
\Omega
is hyperbolic.

Proof.

Part (a) is an immediate consequence of Lemma
7.8
and Theorem
4.2
.

Part (b) follows from (
7.12
) by choosing
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{p}\in\Omega
and
r^{2}=4A|u(\mathbf{x})|/c
.

If the assumption in (c) holds then the domain is hyperbolic at every point by (a), and hence it is hyperbolic
by the implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv) in Theorem
4.2
.

To prove (d), assume first that
u:\Omega\to(-\infty,0)
is an MPSH exhaustion function of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
.
Pick a number
t_{0}<0
. The set
K=\{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega:u(\mathbf{x})\leq t_{0}\}
is compact since
u
is an exhaustion.
Choose a relatively compact neighbourhood
V\Subset\Omega
of
K
and set

\mu=\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in bV}u(\mathbf{x})>t_{0},\qquad c=\max_{\mathbf{x}\in bV}\left(u(\mathbf{x})+|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\right).

Pick an increasing convex function
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

h(t)=t\ \ \text{for $t\leq t_{0}$},\quad\ h(\mu)>c.

Define the function
\psi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\psi(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\max\{u(\mathbf{x})+|\mathbf{x}|^{2},h(u(\mathbf{x}))\},&\text{if $\mathbf{x}\in V$};\\
h(u(\mathbf{x})),&\text{if $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\setminus V$}.\end{cases}

Then,
\psi
is a bounded MPSH exhaustion function on
\Omega
such that
\psi(\mathbf{x})=u(\mathbf{x})+|\mathbf{x}|^{2}

for
\mathbf{x}
in a neighbourhood of
K
, so it is strongly MPSH there. It follows that
\Omega
is
hyperbolic at every point of
K
. Letting
t_{0}\to 1
, the compact set
K=\{u\leq t_{0}\}
increases to
\Omega
,
so
\Omega
is hyperbolic by Theorem
4.2
(the equivalence
(iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv)). This proves (d) if
u
is smooth.

Suppose now that
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,0)
is a continuous MPSH exhaustion function.
Choose
t_{0}<0
close enough to
0
such that the compact set
K=\{u\leq t_{0}\}

contains the level set
\{u=-\infty\}
in its interior.
Taking the convolution of
u
with a smooth radially symmetric approximate identity
gives a smooth MPSH function
v\geq u
on a neighbourhood of
K
.
(See
[
2
, Proposition 8.1.6]
. Note that
smoothing also applies if
u
assumes the value
-\infty
,
the reason being that an MPSH function is subharmonic in the usual
sense, hence locally integrable.) The same procedure as in the special
case above, using
v(\mathbf{x})+|\mathbf{x}|^{2}
as the first item under
\max
in the definition of
\psi
,
yields another bounded MPSH exhaustion
\tilde{u}
on
\Omega
which is strongly MPSH in a
neighbourhood of
K
, so
\Omega
is hyperbolic there. Letting
t_{0}\to 0
completes the proof.
∎

Here are examples of unbounded domains satisfying conditions in Theorem
7.9
(c), (d).

Example 7.10
.

Consider the function
u(x,y,z)=x^{2}+y^{2}-az^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Its Hessian matrix has eigenvalues
2,2,-2a
, so
u
is strongly MPSH and satisfies
condition (
7.15
) when
a<1
. For such
a
and any
b\in\mathbb{R}
the unbounded
strongly minimally convex domain

\Omega_{a,b}=\{u<0\}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x^{2}+y^{2}<az^{2}+b\}

is hyperbolic by Theorem
7.9
(c).
Part (b) of the same theorem shows that

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq const\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{\sqrt{|u(x,y,z)|}}

for all
(x,y,z)\in\Omega
and
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Using this estimate and the fact that

\Omega_{a,b}
is strongly minimally convex, it can be seen that
\Omega_{a,b}

is in fact complete hyperbolic (see Corollary
10.3
).
∎

Example 7.11
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\to(-\infty,0)
be a smooth function such that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}h(x)=0
and

|h^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq c<+\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. An example is
h(x)=-1/(1+x^{2})
. Consider the function

u(x,y,z)=h(x)+a(y^{2}+z^{2}),\quad\ (x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

for some
a>0
. The Hessian matrix of
u
at
(x,y,z)
is the diagonal matrix with the eigenvalues

h^{\prime\prime}(x),2a,2a
. Since
|h^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq c
, the sum of any two eigenvalues is nonnegative
if
a\geq c/2
. For such
a
,
u
is a negative MPSH exhaustion function on
the unbounded domain

\Omega=\{u<0\}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:y^{2}+z^{2}<-h(x)/a\}

containing the line
\mathbb{R}\times\{(0,0)\}
.
Hence,
\Omega
is hyperbolic by Theorem
7.9
(d).
∎

8.
Localization theorems for the minimal pseudometric

Given domains
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, their minimal pseudometrics
satisfy
g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\leq g_{\Omega_{0}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{0}

and
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
; see (
2.11
).
Assuming that a point
\mathbf{p}
is contained in the relative interior of

b\Omega\cap b\Omega_{0}
, it is often possible to obtain the inverse inequality
up to a positive multiplicative constant.
The motivation is that it is easier to estimate the metric from below
on small subdomains. There are several known estimates of this type for
biholomorphically invariant metrics; see the papers

[
41
,
14
,
5
,
15
,
38
,
24
,
10
,
12
]
, among others.
We shall prove the following result concerning localization of the minimal pseudometric.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. Assume that for some point

\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
there exist a neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\mathbf{p}

and a continuous function
u:\overline{\Omega}\cap U\to(-\infty,0]
such that
u(\mathbf{p})=0
,

u<0
on
\overline{\Omega}\cap U\setminus\{\mathbf{p}\}
,
u
is MPSH on
\Omega\cap U

(see Definition
7.1
), and

(8.1)

|u(\mathbf{x})|\leq c_{0}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}|,\quad\ \mathbf{x}\in\Omega\cap U

holds for some
c_{0}>0
. Given
r_{0}>0
, there is a constant
c>0
such that

(8.2)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq(1-c|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}|)\,g_{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},r_{0})}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}),\quad\ \mathbf{x}\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},r_{0}),\ \mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This result is close in spirit to
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
; see also Theorem
8.5
.

Remark 8.2
.

(A) The estimate (
8.2
)
says in particular that
\Omega
is hyperbolic at every point close enough to
\mathbf{p}
.
An analogous result for the Kobayashi metric is
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Our proof gives a stronger conclusion under weaker hypotheses also in that situation:
the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
need not be bounded, the local peak function
u

may be plurisubharmonic instead of holomorphic, and there is no condition
on the upper bound of
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}|
in terms of
|u(\mathbf{x})|
.

(B) Our proof of (
8.2
) can be adjusted to the situation
studied by Ivashkovich and Rosay in
[
24
, Sect. 2a]
.
The upshot is that, under their hypotheses, the quotient of the infinitesimal Kobayashi metrics
of a domain
\Omega
and the subdomain
\Omega\cap\,\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},r_{0})

in an almost complex manifold

approaches

linearly in terms of

.
(Ivashkovich and Rosay did not provide a quantitative estimate of the rate of convergence.)
∎

In the proof of Theorem

we shall need the following lemma.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
K
be a nonempty compact set in the circle
\mathbb{T}=b\mathbb{D}=\{|z|=1\}
,
and let
v(z)
be the positive harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
whose boundary values
on
\mathbb{T}
agree with the characteristic function of
K
.
Let
|K|
denote the normalized Lebesgue measure of
K
.
Then, for any
\mu\in(0,1)
we have that

(8.3)

v(z)\leq\mu\ \ \text{for}\ \ |z|\leq 1-\frac{2}{\mu}|K|.

Proof.

A positive harmonic function
v
on
\mathbb{D}
satisfies Harnack’s inequality

\frac{1-r}{1+r}v(0)\leq v(r\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})\leq\frac{1+r}{1-r}v(0),\quad 0\leq r<1,\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

In our case we have that
v(0)=|K|
, so

v(r\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})\leq\frac{1+r}{1-r}v(0)\leq\frac{2}{1-r}|K|\leq\mu

where the last inequality holds for
r\leq 1-\frac{2}{\mu}|K|
. This gives (
8.3
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

The estimate (
8.2
) clearly follows from the following.

Lemma 8.4
.

(Assumptions as in Theorem
8.1
.)

Given
r_{0}>0
, there is
c=c(r_{0})>0
such that for every conformal harmonic disc
f:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega

we have that

(8.4)

|f(z)-\mathbf{p}|\leq r_{0}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ |z|\leq 1-c|f(0)-\mathbf{p}|.

Setting
\epsilon=|f(0)-\mathbf{p}|
, condition (
8.4
) says that
f
maps the
disc of radius
r=1-c\epsilon
into the ball
\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},r_{0})
. It follows that if

(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
is such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(0)=\mathbf{p}
,
then the sequence
f_{k}
converges to
\mathbf{p}
uniformly on compacts in
\mathbb{D}
.

It remains to prove the lemma.
Replacing the function
u
in Theorem
8.1

by
\max\{u,-c_{1}\}
for a suitably chosen
c_{1}>0
,
we may assume that
u
is defined on
\overline{\Omega}
and MPSH
on
\Omega
, and (
8.1
) holds for all
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
.

For convenience of exposition, we first consider the case when
\Omega
is bounded.
We may assume that
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}
and

\Omega
is contained in the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
centred at the origin.
It suffices to prove (
8.4
) for small numbers
r_{0}\in(0,1)
and for discs
f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)

with centres
f(0)
close to
\mathbf{0}
. The assumptions on

imply that

(8.5)

-\epsilon_{0}:=\sup\bigl\{u(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega},\ |\mathbf{x}|\geq r_{0}^{2}\bigr\}<0.

Fix

and set
\epsilon=-u(f(0))>0
. Since
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}
, (
8.1
) gives

(8.6)

\epsilon\leq c_{0}|f(0)|.

Since
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded harmonic map, it has a nontangential
limit at almost every point of the circle
\mathbb{T}=b\mathbb{D}
, the boundary map is of class
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
,
and
f
is the Poisson integral of its boundary function. Consider the measurable set

(8.7)

K=\bigl\{t\in[0,2\pi]:|f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})|\geq r_{0}^{2}\big\}.

The choice of
\epsilon_{0}
in (
8.5

Since the function

is negative subharmonic on

(see Proposition

), it follows that

-\epsilon=u(f(0))\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}u\circ f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})\frac{dt}{2\pi}\leq\int_{K}u\circ f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})\frac{dt}{2\pi}\leq-\epsilon_{0}|K|.

From this and (
8.6
) we obtain the estimate

(8.8)

|K|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_{0}}\leq\frac{c_{0}|f(0)|}{\epsilon_{0}}.

By the assumption made at the beginning of the proof, the function
\log|f|<0
is negative
on
\mathbb{D}
, and it is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
by Lemma

(a).
Hence,
\log|f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})|\leq 0
for all

, and

\log|f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})|<\log r_{0}^{2}=2\log r_{0}<0\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in[0,2\pi]\setminus K

in view of the definition of the set
K

).
Let

be the harmonic function whose

on

and

on
\mathbb{T}\setminus K

Then,

on

. Note that

By Lemma

If

in the last step of the proof

∎

9.
Bounded strongly minimally convex domains are complete hyperbolic

A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
with
\mathscr{C}^{2}
boundary is

strongly minimally convex
if it admits a
\mathscr{C}^{2}
defining function
u:U\to\mathbb{R}
on a
neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\overline{\Omega}

(i.e.,
\Omega=\{\mathbf{x}\in U:u(x)<0\}
and
du\neq 0
on
b\Omega=\{u=0\}
)
which is strongly MPSH on a neighbourhood
V\subset U
of
b\Omega
.
If
\overline{\Omega}
is compact, then
u
can be chosen strongly MPSH on
a neighbourhood of
\overline{\Omega}
.
(See
[
2
, Definition 8.1.18 and Lemma 8.1.19]
.)
Since
|u(\mathbf{x})|
is comparable to
\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},b\Omega)
for
\mathbf{x}
sufficiently close
to
b\Omega
, the following is a corollary to Theorem
7.9
(b).

Corollary 9.1
.

For every bounded strongly minimally convex domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n},\ n\geq 3,

there is a constant
C>0
such that

(9.1)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq C\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{\sqrt{\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},b\Omega)}}\ \ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega$ and $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

From the formula (
2.10
) for the metric
g_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
on the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

we see that, except for the size of the constant
C
, the asymptotic
estimate (
9.1
) is the best possible for all tangent vectors
\mathbf{v}
.
However, if
\mathbf{v}
is normal to the boundary
b\Omega

at the closest point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
to
\mathbf{x}
(such
\mathbf{p}
is unique if
\mathbf{x}
is close enough to

b\Omega
), we expect a lower bound

(9.2)

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq C\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},b\Omega)}

for some
C>0
. The estimate (
9.2
)
implies that
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic.
This follows from
[
24
, Lemma 1.1, p. 2396]

applied to conformal harmonic discs.

For the Kobayashi metric on strongly pseudoconvex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
the estimate (
9.2
) for radial vectors
\mathbf{v}
was obtained by
Graham
[
16
]
; see also
[
41
, Proposition 7]
.
Another proof under the weaker assumption
that
b\Omega
is strongly pseudoconvex near a point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega

and
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
is close to
\mathbf{p}
was given by Forstnerič and Rosay

[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
.
We could not adapt those proofs to the present situation.
Instead, we obtain the estimate (
9.2
)
by following
[
24
, proof of Theorem 1]
,
thereby obtaining the following result.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a (not necessarily bounded) domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
.
If the boundary
b\Omega
is strongly minimally convex at a point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
, then

\mathbf{p}
is at infinite minimal distance from
\Omega
. In particular, every bounded strongly
minimally convex domain is complete hyperbolic.

Remark 9.3
.

A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathscr{C}^{2}
boundary
is strongly minimally convex if and only if at every point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega

the interior principal curvatures
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\nu_{n-1}
of
b\Omega

satisfy
\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}>0
(see
[
2
, Theorem 8.1.13]
).
If at some point
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
we have
\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}<0
,
then
\mathbf{p}
is at finite minimal distance from
\Omega
.
Indeed, there is an embedded conformal minimal disc in
\Omega\cup\{\mathbf{p}\}

centred at
\mathbf{p}

[
20
, Lemma 3.13]
,
so Example
2.5
applies.
The following consequence seems worthwhile recording.
We do not know whether the converse holds; see Problem C in Section
12
.

Corollary 9.4
.

If
M
is a smooth embedded surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that the minimal distance
to any point of
M
from both sides is infinite, then
M
has vanishing mean curvature,
and hence is a minimal surface.

To prove Theorem
9.2
we shall need a couple of lemmas.
The first one is an analogue of
[
24
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 9.5
.

If the boundary of the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\ n\geq 3,
is strongly minimally convex
near
\mathbf{p}\in b\Omega
, then for any
r\in(0,1)
there are constants
\delta>0
and
C>0

such that every
f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
with
|f(0)-\mathbf{p}|<\delta
satisfies

(9.3)

|f(z)-f(0)|\leq C\sqrt{\mathrm{dist}(f(0),b\Omega)}\ \ \ \text{for all $|z|\leq r$}.

Proof.

Pick a ball
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
around
\mathbf{p}
such that
\Omega
admits
a strongly MPSH defining function
u
in a neighbourhood of
\overline{\Omega\cap U}
.
For
\epsilon>0
small, the function
\rho_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{x})=u(\mathbf{x})-\epsilon|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}|^{2}

is strongly MPSH on
\overline{\Omega\cap U}
for every
\mathbf{q}\in b\Omega\cap U
.
Up to shrinking
U
around
\mathbf{p}
if necessary, there are constants
a,b>0
such that for
every
\mathbf{q}
as above we have

(9.4)

-b|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}|\leq\rho_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{x})\leq-a|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{q}|^{2},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega}\cap U.

Fix
r\in[0,1)
and let
r^{\prime}=(1+r)/2
. There is a
\delta>0

such that for every
f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
with
\mathrm{dist}(f(0),\mathbf{p})<\delta
we have that

f(r^{\prime}\mathbb{D})\subset U\cap\Omega
by Lemma
8.4
.
Hence, by rescaling the disc we may assume that this holds for
r^{\prime}=1
.
(This will change the final constant
C
by a positive factor.)
Let
\mathbf{q}\in b\Omega\cap U
be the nearest point to
f(0)
in
b\Omega
.
By Harnack’s theorem (on the growth on positive harmonic functions in the disc) and
subharmonicity of the function
z\mapsto|f(z)-\mathbf{q}|^{2}
there is a constant
c=c(r)>0
such that

|f(z)-\mathbf{q}|^{2}\leq c\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})-\mathbf{q}|^{2}\frac{dt}{2\pi}\quad\text{for all $|z|\leq r$}.

Since
\rho_{\mathbf{q}}\circ f
is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
, it follows that for all
|z|\leq r
we have

b|f(0)-\mathbf{q}|\geq-\rho_{\mathbf{q}}(f(0))\geq-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho(f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t}))\frac{dt}{2\pi}\geq a\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{i}t})-\mathbf{q}|^{2}\frac{dt}{2\pi}\geq\frac{a}{c}|f(z)-\mathbf{q}|^{2}.

(The first and the third inequality hold by (
9.4
), the second one is due to subharmonicity
of
\rho_{\mathbf{q}}\circ f
, and the last one uses the previous estimate.) Setting
C=bc/a>0
we obtain

|f(z)-\mathbf{q}|^{2}\leq C|f(0)-\mathbf{q}|=C\,\mathrm{dist}(f(0),b\Omega)\quad\text{for all $|z|\leq r$}.

Since
|f(z)-f(0)|^{2}\leq 2(|f(z)-\mathbf{q}|^{2}+|f(0)-\mathbf{q}|^{2})=2(|f(z)-\mathbf{q}|^{2}+\mathrm{dist}(f(0),b\Omega)^{2})
,
we obtain the desired estimate (
9.3
) with a different constant
C
provided

\mathrm{dist}(f(0),b\Omega)
is small enough, which holds if
|f(0)-\mathbf{p}|
is small enough.
∎

The next lemma provides a Cauchy estimate for harmonic discs in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
; we omit the proof.

Lemma 9.6
.

For every
r\in[0,1)
there is a constant
C>0
such that every bounded
harmonic map
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies

(9.5)

|\nabla f(z)|\leq C\sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}|f(\zeta)-f(0)|,\quad\ |z|\leq r.

Proof of Theorem
9.2
.

Intersecting
\Omega
with a small ball centred at
\mathbf{p}
and smoothing the corners
we obtain a bounded strongly minimally convex domain
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega
such that

\mathbf{p}
is contained in the relative interior of
b\Omega\cap b\Omega_{1}
.
Hence, in view of the localization (see Theorem
8.1
), we may assume that
\Omega

is bounded strongly minimally convex. Let
u
be a strongly MPSH function on a neighbourhood of

\overline{\Omega}
such that
\Omega=\{u<0\}
and
du\neq 0
on
b\Omega=\{u=0\}
.
Note that for
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
,
-u(\mathbf{x})=|u(\mathbf{x})|
is comparable to
\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},b\Omega)
.
Lemmas
9.5
and
9.6

provide a constant
c>0
such that every
f\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)
whose

is close enough to

satisfies

Together with (

for some constant
c_{1}>0
and

. We claim that this implies

with another constant
C_{2}>0
.
By
[

, Lemma 1.1, p. 2396]
, the estimate (

, so this will prove the theorem.

The proof of (

) is essentially the same as the proof
in
[
24
]
that (2.6) implies (2.4) in that paper. We recall the argument.
Rescaling the disc
\mathbb{D}
to
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{D}

(with a different constant).

Consider the function

) with the constant

10.
Complete hyperbolicity of some unbounded strongly minimally convex domains

Combining Theorem
9.2
with the localization theorem
(see Theorem
8.1
), we now give examples of unbounded strongly minimally convex
domains
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which are complete hyperbolic. The mentioned results show that
any divergent path in
\Omega
clustering at a finite boundary point of
\Omega
has infinite length.
It remains to ensure that the minimal length of any path in
\Omega
diverging to infinity is infinite.
Let us record this observation.

Proposition 10.1
.

If
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are (not necessarily bounded)
domains such that
\Omega^{\prime}
is complete hyperbolic and
\Omega
is strongly
minimally convex, then
\Omega
is also complete hyperbolic.

Estimating the length of a path diverging to infinity can sometimes be done with the help
Theorem
7.9
(b). Here is a result in this direction.

Theorem 10.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be an unbounded strongly minimally convex domain.
Assume that there is a
\mathscr{C}^{2}
function
u:\Omega\to(-\infty,0)
having
the following two properties:

(a)

There is
c>0
such that
\mathrm{tr}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Hess}_{u}(\mathbf{x})\geq c>0
for all

\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and
\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(b)

There is
c^{\prime}>0
such that
|u(\mathbf{x})|\leq c^{\prime}|\mathbf{x}|^{2}
for
all
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
with
|\mathbf{x}|\geq 1
.

Then
\Omega
is complete hyperbolic.

Proof.

As said above, any divergent path in
\Omega
which terminates at a boundary point of
\Omega

has infinite length. Suppose now that
\gamma:[0,1)\to\Omega
is a path with

\lim_{t\to 1}|\gamma(t)|=\infty
. By condition (a) in the theorem and
(
7.16
) in Theorem
7.9
there is a
C>0
such that

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq C\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{\sqrt{|u(\mathbf{x})|}}\ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega$ and $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

In view of condition (b) we further get

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\geq C^{\prime}\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{x}|}\ \ \text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega$ with $|\mathbf{x}|\geq 1$ and $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}

with the constant
C^{\prime}=C/\sqrt{c^{\prime}}>0
. Hence,

g_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\geq C^{\prime}\frac{|\dot{\gamma}(t)|}{|\gamma(t)|}

holds for all
t
close to
1
. It clearly follows that

\int_{0}^{1}g_{\Omega}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))dt=+\infty
.
∎

Corollary 10.3
.

For any
a<1
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
the domain

\Omega_{a,b}=\left\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x^{2}+y^{2}<az^{2}+b\right\}

is complete hyperbolic. Hence, every strongly minimally convex domain contained
in
\Omega_{a,b}
is complete hyperbolic.

Proof.

When
a<1
, the function
u(x,y,z)=x^{2}+y^{2}-az^{2}-b
satisfies condition (a)
in Theorem
10.2
(see Example
7.10
), and it clearly satisfies

|u(x,y,z)|\leq a|z|^{2}+b
on
\Omega_{a,b}
. Hence, condition (b) in
Theorem
10.2
holds as well, and the conclusion follows.
∎

11.
Extending conformal minimal surfaces across punctures

Recall that
\mathbb{D}^{*}=\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0<|z|<1\}
. We prove the following result.

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, and let

f:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\Omega
be a conformal harmonic map.
If there is a sequence
z_{k}\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
converging to
0\in\mathbb{D}
such
that
f(z_{k})
converges to a point in
\Omega
, then
f
extends
to a conformal harmonic map
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
.

The analogue of Theorem
11.1
for punctured holomorphic discs in Kobayashi hyperbolic manifolds
(and, more generally, in hyperbolic complex spaces) is due to Kwack
[
35
]
;
see also Kobayashi
[
33
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Kwack’s theorem generalizes the big Picard theorem, which says that
a holomorphic map
\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
extends to a holomorphic
map
\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. More generally, if
Y
is a compact hyperbolic manifold,

X
is a connected complex manifold, and
A
is a proper closed complex subvariety of
X
,
then every holomorphic map
X\setminus A\to Y
extends to a holomorphic map
X\to Y

[
33
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem
11.1
fails in general if
\Omega
is not hyperbolic.
For example, if
h
is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
with an essential singularity at
0
,
then
(h,0):\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a conformal harmonic map for which the theorem fails.
The same is true for the map

f=(\mathrm{e}^{h},0):\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\Omega=(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(Note that
\Omega
is the complement of a line in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.)

Theorem
11.1
is trivial if
\Omega
is a bounded domain (since a bounded harmonic function
extends harmonically across a puncture), but is nontrivial on unbounded domains.
(Every bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is hyperbolic by Corollary
2.4
.)
Combining with Theorem
5.1
gives:

Corollary 11.2
.

The conclusion of Theorem
11.1
holds in every convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
which does not contain any affine
2
-plane.

Proof of Theorem
11.1
.

We begin with preliminaries. On the disc
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
we have the
Poincaré–Bergman metric

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}}=\frac{|dz|}{1-|z|^{2}}

of constant Gaussian curvature
-4
.
(This normalization is used in the definition of the minimal metric
g_{\Omega}
.)
The Poincaré metric on the punctured disc
\mathbb{D}^{*}=\{0<|z|<1\}
of
constant curvature
-4
, which makes the universal holomorphic covering map

\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
a local isometry, equals

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}^{*}}=\frac{|dz|}{2|z|\log(1/|z|)},\quad\ 0<|z|<1.

(See
[
33
, p. 78]
. Kobayashi uses a metric with constant
curvature
-1
, so the multiplicative constants differ, which is irrelevant for the proof.)
The metric
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{D}^{*}}
is complete, and the distance from any fixed point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
to
z\in\mathbb{D}^{*}

grows as
\frac{1}{2}\log\log(1/|z|)+O(1)
when
z\to 0
or
|z|\to 1
.
The circle
|z|=r\in(0,1)
has Poincaré length

(11.1)

L(r)=\frac{\pi}{\log 1/r}.

In particular,
L(r)
decreases to
0
as
r\to 0
.

The scheme of our proof follows
[
33
, proof of Theorem 3.1]
, but is considerably simpler. It also gives a simple proof of Kwack’s theorem for punctured holomorphic discs in Kobayashi hyperbolic domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
denote Euclidean coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Assume that
f
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
11.1
, so we have
a sequence
z_{k}\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
with

(11.2)

\lim_{k\to\infty}z_{k}=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \lim_{k\to\infty}f(z_{k})=\mathbf{p}\in\Omega.

We must must show that for every
\epsilon>0
there is a
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
f

maps the punctured disc
\delta\mathbb{D}^{*}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0<|z|<\delta\}
into the ball

\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},\epsilon)
. We shall assume that
\epsilon>0
is small enough such that

\overline{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbf{p},\epsilon)\subset\Omega
.

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the sequence

r_{k}=|z_{k}|>0

(k\in\mathbb{N})
is strictly monotonically decreasing to
0
.
Let
\gamma_{k}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=r_{k}\}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By (
11.1
) we have that
\lim_{k\to\infty}L(r_{k})=0
.
Since
f:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\Omega
is metric-decreasing from the
Poincaré metric on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
to the minimal metric
g_{\Omega}
on
\Omega
,
the length of the curve
f(\gamma_{k})\subset\Omega
with respect to
g_{\Omega}

converges to zero as
k\to\infty
.
Since
\Omega
is hyperbolic,
g_{\Omega}
induces the standard topology
on
\Omega
by Theorem
4.2
.
Hence, since
z_{k}\in\gamma_{k}
and
f(z_{k})\to\mathbf{p}
as
k\to\infty
,
there is a
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f(\gamma_{k})\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},\epsilon)\ \ \text{for all}\ \ k\geq k_{0}.

Let

A_{k}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:r_{k+1}<|z|<r_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{D}^{*},\quad\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that
bA_{k}=\gamma_{k}\cup\gamma_{k+1}
, and hence
f(bA_{k})\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},\epsilon)

for
k\geq k_{0}
. By the maximum principle for minimal surfaces it follows that

f(A_{k})\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},\epsilon)
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Since

\bigcup_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}A_{k}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:0<|z|\leq r_{k_{0}}\}=r_{k_{0}}\mathbb{D}^{*},

we see that
f(r_{k_{0}}\mathbb{D}^{*})\subset\mathbb{B}(\mathbf{p},\epsilon)
.
∎

We now give an application of Theorem
11.1
.
By analogy with domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we introduce the following notion
(see Kobayashi
[
32
, p. 190]
).

Definition 11.3
.

A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
is
hyperconvex
if it admits
a continuous negative MPSH exhaustion function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,0)
.

Theorem
7.9
(d) says that every hyperconvex domain is hyperbolic.
Such a domain may be unbounded as shown by Example
7.11
.

Corollary 11.4
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
is a hyperconvex domain, then
any conformal harmonic map
\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\Omega
extends
to a conformal harmonic map
\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
.

In particular,
\Omega
does not contain any nonconstant conformal minimal surface

M\to\Omega
such that
M
is the complement of finitely many points in a
compact Riemann surface.

It is easy to find a bounded (hence hyperbolic) domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

and a conformal harmonic punctured disc
f:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\Omega
, which extends to a
conformal harmonic disc
f:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{\Omega}
but
f(0)\in b\Omega
.
(See Example
2.5
.) Hence, Corollary
11.4

fails in general on hyperbolic domains which are not hyperconvex.

Proof of Corollary
11.4
.

Let
u:\Omega\to(-\infty,0)
be a continuous negative MPSH exhaustion function.
Assume that
f:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to\Omega
is a conformal harmonic map.
If
f
does not extend to the origin
0\in\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)\in\Omega
,
then Theorem
11.1
shows that for every sequence
z_{k}\in\mathbb{D}

with
\lim_{k\to\infty}z_{k}=0
the sequence
f(z_{k})
diverges to
b\Omega\cup\{\infty\}
.
Since
u
is a negative exhaustion function on
\Omega
, it follows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}u(f(z_{k}))=0
. This shows that
u\circ f:\mathbb{D}^{*}\to(-\infty,0)
extends to
a continuous function
h:\mathbb{D}\to(-\infty,0]
with
h(0)=0
. Since
f
is conformal harmonic
on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
and
u
is MPSH,
u\circ f
is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}^{*}
, and hence the extension

h
is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
. The fact that the extended function reaches
maximal value
h(0)=0
at
0\in\mathbb{D}
contradicts the maximum principle for
subharmonic functions.
This contradiction shows that
f
extends across the origin to a conformal harmonic map

\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
. The last statement follows from the fact that every harmonic function
on a compact Riemann surface
\widehat{M}
is constant.
∎

12.
Open problems

A. Continuity of the minimal metric.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. Recall from Section
2

that the minimal pseudometric
g_{\Omega}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is upper-semicontinuous.

Problem 12.1
.

Is there a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose minimal metric
g_{\Omega}

is not continuous?

There are domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
with discontinuous Kobayashi metric.
We wish to thank Peter Pflug for the following example (private communication).
There is a pseudoconvex balanced domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
for any
n>1

with a non-continuous Minkowski function
h
. Then the Kobayashi metric of

\Omega
at the origin equals
\mathcal{K}_{\Omega}(0;\cdot)=h
, hence it is not continuous.
See
[
25
, Theorem 3.5.3]
for the details.

B. Relationship between
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}
and
g_{\Omega}
.

Recall (see (
2.9
)) that for any unit vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ |\mathbf{v}|=1
we have

g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})=\inf\big\{\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda):\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda\}.

On the ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we also have that

(12.1)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)=\max\bigl\{g_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}):\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda,|\mathbf{v}|=1\bigr\}.

(See
[
13
, Eq. (2.10)]
.)
This fails in general as the following example shows.

Example 12.2
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Taking
\Lambda=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\}
we have that

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{0},\Lambda)=1
, the extremal disc being the linear embedding

x+\mathfrak{i}y\mapsto(x,y,0)
. Indeed, given
f=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})\in\mathrm{CH}(\mathbb{D},\Omega)

with
f(0)=\mathbf{0}
and
df_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\Lambda
, the projection
(f_{1},f_{2}):\mathbb{D}\to\Lambda\cap\Omega=\mathbb{D}

is a harmonic map which is conformal at the origin, so the claim follows from the Schwarz–Pick
lemma due to Forstnerič and Kalaj
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Given a unit vector
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},0)\in\Lambda
, take
\mathbf{v}=(0,0,1)
and let
\Sigma_{\mathbf{u}}

be the
2
-plane spanned by the orthonormal frame
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
. Then,

\Sigma_{\mathbf{u}}\cap\Omega
is conformally equivalent to the strip

S=(-1,+1)\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}
. The function

f(z)=\frac{2\mathfrak{i}}{\pi}\log\frac{1+z}{1-z},\quad\ z\in\mathbb{D}

provides a conformal diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{D}
onto
S
. We have

f^{\prime}(0)=4\mathfrak{i}/\pi
, so
\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_{\mathbf{u}})\leq 1/\|df_{0}\|=\pi/4
.
Hence,
g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{u})\leq\pi/4<1=\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{0},\Lambda)

for all unit vectors
\mathbf{u}\in\Lambda
.
∎

The argument in the above example shows that property (
12.1
) fails on any
bounded strongly convex domains in
\mathbb{B}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
with the base
\mathbb{B}^{2}\times\{0\}

which is sufficiently big in the third coordinate direction.
Hence, it seems that the ball is rather exceptional in this sense.

Problem 12.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
such that

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\Lambda)=\max\bigl\{g_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}):\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda,|\mathbf{v}|=1\bigr\}

holds for every
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and
\Lambda\in G_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Is
\Omega
is
equivalent to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
by a rigid transformation?

C. Distance to a minimal surface.

By Lemma
5.2
, any point in a half-space
\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

is at infinite minimal distance from the hyperplane
\Sigma=b\mathbb{H}
.
This fails for more general minimal hypersurfaces if
n\geq 4
; however,
the following is an interesting question in dimension
3
.

Problem 12.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be an embedded minimal surface in a bounded domain

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Is the minimal distance in
\Omega\setminus\Sigma

to any point
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma
infinite?

The analogous problem for the Kobayashi metric has affirmative answer:
the distance to a complex hypersurface is infinite, also in the nonintegrable case; see
Ivashkovich and Rosay
[
24
]
. Hence, if a bounded weakly
pseudoconvex domain
D
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
contains a Levi-flat piece in the boundary
bD
,
then the Kobayashi distance to it is also infinite. Concerning more general weakly pseudoconvex domains,
Catlin proved in 1989
[
9
]
that every bounded weakly pseudoconvex domain
of finite type in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is complete hyperbolic. A partial result in the nonintegrable case
was obtained by Bertrand
[
6
]
in 2012.
It would be of interest to see whether minimally convex domains behave better in this respect.

Problem 12.5
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded minimally convex domain with smooth
boundary; is
\Omega
necessarily complete hyperbolic in the minimal metric
g_{\Omega}
?
∎

D. Extremal minimal discs in convex domains.

If
\Omega
is a bounded strongly convex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then by the seminal result
of Lempert
[
36
,
37
]
there exists for every point

\mathbf{p}\in\Omega
and vector
0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
a unique extremal holomorphic disc

F=F_{p,\xi}:\mathbb{D}\to\Omega
such that

F(0)=\mathbf{p}
and the derivative
F^{\prime}(0)=r\xi

(r>0)
is the biggest possible.
This extremal disc is properly embedded in
\Omega
, and it is
an isometry from
\mathbb{D}
with the Poincaré metric
onto its image
F(\mathbb{D})\subset\Omega

with the Kobayashi metric
\mathcal{K}_{\Omega}
. Extremal holomorphic discs also exist in taut
unbounded convex domains of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(see
[
7
, Lemma 3.3]
).

Problem 12.6
.

Does the analogue of Lempert’s theorem hold for conformal harmonic discs in a (smoothly)
bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
?

The answer is affirmative if
\Omega
is the ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in which case the extremal discs
are the proper affine discs in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
(see Example
2.2
and
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
).
It seems that the ball is the only domain for which the answer to Problem
12.6

is known at this time.

E. Gromov hyperbolicity of strongly minimally convex domains.

It was shown by Balogh and Bonk
[
3
]
that the Kobayashi metric on
any smooth bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is Gromov hyperbolic.
Since in many respects strongly pseudoconvex domains play a similar role in complex analysis as the
strongly minimally convex domains play in the theory of minimal surfaces, the following is a natural
question.

Problem 12.7
.

Is every smooth bounded strongly minimally convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
Gromov hyperbolic
in the minimal metric?

Simpler Hamiltonian structure of Gravity in degenerate limit
Sandipan Sengupta

Abstract
We analyze Hamiltonian gravity in the limit of a vanishing (spatial) triad determinant. In the context of Barbero-Immirzi formulation, the Lorentzian constraints in this limit are found to exhibit a formal similarity to the Euclidean constraints of Ashtekar’s self-dual formulation, modulo the difference that the spatial diffeomorphisms become trivial. Further, we show that the Carrollian limit of gravity emerges as a special case, thus providing it a geometric interpretation independent of either the speed of light or coupling constants.



I
Introduction

The obstacles to a canonical quantization of Lorentzian gravity are fairly well-known, irrespective of whether the canonical variables are defined within a metric or a connection formulation. The structure of the Hamiltonian constraint itself, containing both extrinsic and spatial curvature, poses a serious challenge given the inherent operator ordering ambiguity, non-polynomiality and regularization issues. The hunt for a simpler Hamiltonian structure have also led to efforts towards constructing different limits of gravity. It could be worthwhile to mention a few of these here, such as the strong coupling limit
Teitelboim 1973
;
Henneaux 1979
,
G\rightarrow 0
limit
Smolin 1992
;
Barros e Sa and Bengtsson 1999
,
G\rightarrow\infty
limit
Husain 1988
, the topological limit
Liu et al. 2010
and so on.

In the Hamiltonian theory of Hilbert-Palatini gravity based on the connection formalism, the densitized triad fields emerge as a natural canonical variable (momenta). In a quantum theory, a discrete quantum geometry could be anticipated, where trivial densitized triads could potentially define the lowest eigenstates provided the other (regularized) constraints are also satisfied. Hence, it is worthwhile to explore classical gravity first in a limit where the spatial geometry becomes trivial in the sense of densitized triads and the determinant of the four-metric. Such a limit for gravity has not been explicitly constructed so far.

To this end, in this brief note we explore a limit where the determinant of the spatial triad (and also of the four-metric) vanishes. We implement the limit by defining a local scaling transformation on the canonical variables within the connection formalism.

First, we apply this limit to the Barbero-Immirzi Hamiltonian formulation
Barbero G. 1995
;
Immirzi 1997
.
We elucidate two different reductions of the constraints based on two different degenerate limits introduced here. In both cases, the resulting Hamiltonian theories reflect remarkable simplications. In the first case, we find that the Lorentzian constraints exhibit a formal similarity to the Euclidean constraints of Ashtekar’s self-dual gravity
Sen 1982
;
Ashtekar 1986
;
*ashtekar1
, modulo differences regarding the action of spatial diffeomorphism. As an example, we find a solution to this limiting gravity theory in the context of inhomogeneous and anisotropic cosmology. In case of the second limit, we find a Hamiltonian structure whose quantum counterpart would be free of any operator ordering ambiguity. Next, we find that a special case of the degenerate limit corresponds to Carrollian gravity, the latter being originally defined for a vanishingly small speed of light
Lévy-Leblond 1965
;
Sen Gupta 1966
;
Teitelboim 1973
;
Henneaux 1979
;
Henneaux and Salgado-Rebolledo 2021
;
Sengupta 2023
.

Apart from exploring possible new limits of Hamiltonian gravity, our analysis could provide a fresh perspective towards a perturbative expansion of gravity around vanishing densitized triad, different from the standard approach of expanding around a fixed background geometry with
G
being the perturbative parameter.

In the next section, we first set up the relevant notation through a very brief outline of Barbero-Immirzi canonical gravity. The limit to a degenerate tetrad through a set of scaling transformations is then introduced. The implications of this limit for the Hamiltonian theory is discussed, along with an explicit example. In Sec-III we explore a different limit associated with a degenerate tetrad.
This is followed by an analysis of the special case of global scaling transformations, the resulting theories being shown to be equivalent to the Carrolian gravity theories. We conclude our analysis with a few relevant remarks. In the Appendix, as a relevant digression, we present a corollary on Birkhoff’s theorem in ‘electric’ Carroll gravity based on the associated Hamiltonian structure.

II
Degenerate limit of Barbero-Immirzi gravity

A modern perspective on Hamiltonian gravity based on the connection formulation is provided by the Barbero-Immirzi
SU(2)
formulation. This is based on a Lagrangian density containing the Hilbert-Palatini and the topological Nieh-Yan density with a constant coefficient
Date et al. 2009

\displaystyle{\cal L}(e,\omega)=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\Big[ee^{\mu}_{I}e^{\nu}_{J}R_{\mu\nu}^{~~~IJ}(\omega)~+~\eta\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Big(D_{\mu}e_{\nu}^{I}D_{\alpha}e_{\beta I}-e_{\mu}^{I}e_{\nu}^{J}R_{\alpha\beta}^{~~~KL}(\omega)\Big)\Big]

Limit to degenerate tetrad

In order to implement the limit to vanishing triad determinant, we introduce a local scaling transformation on the triad fields as:

\displaystyle e_{a}^{i}=\delta_{(a)}e^{{}^{\prime}i}_{a}.

(6)

The brackets in the spatial label ‘
(a)
’, which can run over the three space coordinates, imply that it is not summed over when repeated. The primed variables are assumed to remain finite in the limit. To emphasize, each of the three vectors for a fixed
(a)
need not vanish in this limit. The triad determinant
\sqrt{q}=\delta\sqrt{q^{\prime}}
goes to zero as
\delta=\prod_{a}\delta_{(a)}\rightarrow 0
, which defines the ‘degenerate’ limit. This in turn implies that:

\delta_{(a)}\delta_{(b)}=\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(c)}}\rightarrow 0
for any
a\neq b
in the limit. An explicit realization of these properties is presented through an example later.

Under the condition that the symplectic form be preserved, eq.(
6
) leads to the following transformation of the canonical pairs:

\displaystyle A_{a}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{(a)}}{\delta}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i},~E^{a}_{i}=\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}.

(7)

Note that
E^{a}_{i}\rightarrow 0
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. The natural transformation of the spatial derivative which preserves the tetrad one-form is given by:
\partial^{\prime}_{a}=\frac{\partial_{a}}{\delta_{(a)}}
. Throughout this article we assume that the coupling constants do not scale.

Under (
7
), the transformed constraints are given by:

\displaystyle G^{rot}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{ijk}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}k}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{j}-\eta\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\partial_{a}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}-\eta\partial_{a}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}};

\displaystyle H_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{(a)}}{\delta}\Big[-\frac{1}{\eta}\epsilon^{ijk}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}j}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}k}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}-\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial_{a}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}i}-\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial_{b}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}i}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\frac{\delta_{(b)}}{\delta}\partial_{a}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}+E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}\frac{\delta_{(a)}}{\delta}\partial_{a}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\Big],

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2\delta\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}E^{{}^{\prime}[a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b]}_{j}\Big[A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}j}~+~\delta^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{(a)}}\partial_{a}\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}ij}+\omega_{a}^{{}^{\prime}ik}\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}kj}\Big)+\omega^{{}^{\prime}ij}_{b}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta_{(a)}\delta_{(b)}}\partial_{a}\frac{\delta_{(b)}}{\delta}\Big]

In writing the last line above we have used the expression (
5
) for the spatial connection components.
With the assumptions:
\partial_{a}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}\rightarrow 0\Rightarrow~\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}\partial_{c}\frac{\delta_{(b)}}{\delta}\rightarrow 0
for any
a,b,c
, the spatial derivative terms in the constraints above drop out. Redefining the shift and lapse as:
N^{a}=\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}N^{{}^{\prime}a},~N=\delta N
, we find, as
\delta\rightarrow 0
:

\displaystyle G^{{}^{\prime}rot}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{ijk}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}k}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{j};

\displaystyle H^{\prime}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}}G^{{}^{\prime}rot}_{i};

\displaystyle H^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}E^{{}^{\prime}[a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b]}_{j}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}j}

(8)

Noting that the
SU(2)
covariant derivative and the field-strength in the limit are given by:
D^{\prime}_{a}(A^{\prime})E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}=-\frac{1}{\eta}\epsilon^{ijk}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{j}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}j}E^{{}^{\prime}ak},~F_{ab}^{{}^{\prime}~i}=-\frac{1}{\eta}\epsilon^{ijk}A^{{}^{\prime}j}_{a}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}k}
, the above set may be rewritten as:

\displaystyle G^{{}^{\prime}rot}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta D^{\prime}_{a}(A^{\prime})E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{j};

\displaystyle H^{\prime}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}F_{ab}^{{}^{\prime}~i}(A^{\prime});

\displaystyle H^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa\eta}{2\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}\epsilon^{ijk}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{j}F_{ab}^{{}^{\prime}~k}(A^{\prime})

(9)

This form precisely resembles the set of Euclidean constraints of Ashtekar’s self-dual gravity (which could be obtained by setting
\eta=i
in Barbero-Immirzi formulation). The important difference, however, is that the Lorentzian set here corresponds to a simpler canonical structure, given that the spatial diffeomorphism constraints are trivially satisfied.

Example: Ultralocal physics

The properties of the scaling fields
\delta_{(a)}
allow us to describe a dynamical spacetime where all the spatial directions need not contract in general as the triad (tetrad) degenerates.
As an example of an inhomogeneous and anisotropic universe, let us consider the following spacetime:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+t^{2\alpha(x,y,z)}dx^{2}+t^{2\beta(x,y,z)}dy^{2}+t^{2\gamma(x,y,z)}dz^{2}

where we assume that:
\alpha+\beta>0,~\beta+\gamma>0,~\alpha+\gamma>0
. A similar metric was first considered by BKL
Belinsky et al. 1970
;
*bkl1
, which could be a solution of Einstein gravity in presence of matter. Assuming that the effect of matter could be neglected in the degenerate limit (which here represents a curvature singularity at
t=0
), we proceed to find the associated solution.

Let us first demonstrate that the assumed generic properties of the scaling transformation are all satisfied. The metric degenerates as
t\rightarrow 0
. This limit could be parametrized as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
where
t=\epsilon t^{\prime}
(
t^{\prime}
being finite). Inserting this in the metric solution just obtained above, we find:

\displaystyle\delta_{(x)}=\epsilon^{\alpha(x,y,z)},~\delta_{(y)}=\epsilon^{\beta(x,y,z)},~\delta_{(z)}=\epsilon^{\gamma(x,y,z)}.

Thus, the degenerate limit is equivalent to
\delta=\epsilon^{\alpha+\beta+\delta}\rightarrow 0
.
Let us note that since
\epsilon^{\sigma}\ln\epsilon\rightarrow 0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
for any
\sigma>0
(as a consequence of L’Hospital’s rule), we have:
\partial_{a}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}\rightarrow 0
for any
a,b
.
This in turn implies that
\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\frac{\delta_{(b)}}{\delta}\partial_{c}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}\rightarrow 0
for any
a,b,c
.
Thus, the scaling transformation satisfies the assumptions made earlier.

The nontrivial components of the momenta are given by (suppressing the primes which are implicit in the variables and constraints):

\displaystyle E^{x}_{1}=t^{\beta+\gamma},~E^{y}_{2}=t^{\gamma+\alpha},~E^{z}_{3}=t^{\alpha+\beta}.

The time evolution of the canonical pair with respect to the Hamiltonian constraint (
II
) leads to (in the gauge
\omega_{t}^{~ij}=0
):

\displaystyle\dot{E}^{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[E^{a}_{i},\int{\cal H}\right]=\frac{N}{\sqrt{E}}E^{[a}_{i}E^{b]}_{j}A_{b}^{j};

\displaystyle\dot{A}_{a}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[A_{a}^{i},\int{\cal H}\right]=-\frac{N}{\sqrt{E}}\Big[E^{b}_{j}A_{[a}^{i}A_{b]}^{j}+\frac{\sqrt{E}}{2}E_{a}^{i}H\Big]

(10)

From the first equation above, the nontrivial components of the coordinates are obtained as:

\displaystyle A_{x}^{1}=\alpha t^{\alpha-1},~A_{y}^{2}=\beta t^{\beta-1},~A_{z}^{3}=\gamma t^{\gamma-1}

The second evolution equation in (
II
) for the coordinates, along with the Hamiltonian constraint, imply:

\displaystyle\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha=0,

\displaystyle\alpha(\alpha+\beta+\gamma-1)=0,

\displaystyle\beta(\alpha+\beta+\gamma-1)=0,

\displaystyle\gamma(\alpha+\beta+\gamma-1)=0.

(11)

Assuming
\alpha\neq 0,~\beta\neq 0,~\gamma\neq 0
, these are solved as:
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1=\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}
.
The rotation constraints are trivially satisfied.

As the above solution reflects, the spatial dependence of these exponents are arbitrary. Thus, the spatial points are uncorrelated and at each point there could be an independent set of Kasner exponents. In other words, the degenerate limit in this context leads to a classical realization of ultralocal physics.

III
A different degenerate limit: Hamiltonian structure without ordering ambiguity

In this section, we explore a different scaling transformation to set up the degenerate limit. This is to elucidate the fact that it is possible to define the limit so that the spatial derivative terms do not necessarily disappear from the constraints.

Let us consider the following scaling transformations:

\displaystyle A_{a}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{(a)}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i},~E^{a}_{i}=\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i},

(12)

The triad transforms as earlier, whereas the gauge field now transform differently. Note that this does not preserve the symplectic form.
Let us also demand that the
E^{a}_{i}\partial_{a}
be preserved (instead of the tetrad one-form), implying that:
\partial_{a}=\frac{\delta_{(a)}}{\delta}\partial^{\prime}_{a}
.

Using the above transformations, the constraints become:

\displaystyle G^{rot}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\eta\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\partial_{a}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}+\delta\epsilon^{ijk}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}k}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{j}-\eta E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}\partial_{a}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}},

\displaystyle H_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{(a)}}{\delta}\Big[-\frac{\delta}{\eta}\epsilon^{ijk}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}j}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}k}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta_{(a)}}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial_{a}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}i}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta_{(b)}}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial_{b}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}i}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}\partial_{a}\delta_{(b)}-E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\partial_{b}\delta_{(a)}\Big],

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}E^{{}^{\prime}[a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b]}_{j}\Big[A_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}A_{b}^{{}^{\prime}j}~+~(1+\eta^{2})\delta^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{(a)}}\partial_{a}\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}ij}+\omega_{a}^{{}^{\prime}ik}\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}kj}\Big)~+~(1+\eta^{2})\omega^{{}^{\prime}ij}_{b}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta_{(a)}\delta_{(b)}}\partial_{a}\frac{\delta_{(b)}}{\delta}\Big]

The properties of the scaling transformation imply that the terms involving derivatives of the scaling fields vanish in the limit, leading to:

\displaystyle G^{{}^{\prime}rot}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\eta D^{\prime}_{a}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i},

\displaystyle H^{\prime}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle H^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa(1+\eta^{2})}{2\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}E^{{}^{\prime}[a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b]}_{j}\bar{R}_{ab}^{{}^{\prime}ij}(E^{\prime}),

where
D^{\prime}_{a}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}=\partial_{a}E^{a}_{i}
and
\bar{R}_{ab}^{{}^{\prime}ij}(E^{\prime})=\partial_{[a}\omega_{b]}^{{}^{\prime}ij}(E^{\prime})+\omega_{[a}^{{}^{\prime}ik}(E^{\prime})\omega_{b]}^{{}^{\prime}kj}(E^{\prime})
is the spatial three-curvature. In obtaining the above, we have redefined the Lagrange multipliers as:
\omega_{t}^{~ij}=\frac{1}{\delta}\omega_{t}^{{}^{\prime}ij},~N^{a}=\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}N^{{}^{\prime}a}
.

This reflects another intriguing limit, where the diffeomorphism becomes trivial as in the earlier section. The rotation and Hamiltonian constraints above depend only on the conjugate momenta
E^{a}_{i}
. The quantization of this theory would be free of any operator ordering ambiguity problem.

IV
A Connection between degenerate and Carroll limit

In this section, we demonstrate that the degenerate limit of Hilbert-Palatini gravity contains Carroll gravity as a special case.
The latter had originally been formulated as a truncated Hamiltonian theory in the limit
c\rightarrow 0
. Recently, both the ‘electric’ and ‘magnetic’ forms of this limit was shown to originate from the Hilbert-Palatini action, and the associated connection-based Hamiltonian formulations were also presented
Sengupta 2023
.

Using the same parametrization as earlier, the Hilbert-Palatini constraints are given by:

\displaystyle G^{rot}_{i}~

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\epsilon^{ijk}K_{a}^{k}E^{a}_{j},

\displaystyle H_{a}~

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~E^{b}_{i}\partial_{[a}K_{b]}^{i}-K_{a}^{i}\partial_{b}E^{b}_{i},

\displaystyle H~

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-~\frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt{E}}E^{[a}_{i}E^{b]}_{j}\Big[K_{a}^{i}K_{b}^{j}~+~\partial_{a}\omega_{b}^{~ij}(E)+\omega_{b}^{~ik}(E)\omega_{b}^{~kj}(E)\Big]

(13)

where
K_{a}^{i}:=\omega_{a}^{~0i}
.
The analogue of the scaling transformation (
7
) on conjugate pairs is given by:

\displaystyle K_{a}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{(a)}}{\delta}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i},~E^{a}_{i}=\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}

(14)

Note that the transformation of the derivatives and of
\omega_{a}^{{}^{\prime}ij}(E)
remain the same as in section-II.
Let us now find the reduced set of constraints in the degenerate limit, where the scaling transformation is global:
\delta_{(a)}\equiv const.

IV.1
Electric Carroll gravity

Under (
14
), the rotation constraint under the scaling remain form-invariant:

\displaystyle G^{rot}_{i}=\epsilon^{ijk}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}k}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{j}:=G^{{}^{\prime}rot}_{i}

(15)

The spatial diffeomorphism constraints transform as:

\displaystyle H_{a}=\delta_{(a)}\Big[\frac{1}{\delta_{(a)}}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial_{a}K_{b}^{{}^{\prime}i}-\frac{1}{\delta_{(b)}}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial_{b}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}-\frac{1}{\delta_{(b)}}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}\partial_{b}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\Big]

Redefining the shift as:
N^{a}:=\frac{1}{\delta_{(a)}}N^{{}^{\prime}a}
, we find:

\displaystyle H^{\prime}_{a}=E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{a}K_{b}^{{}^{\prime}i}-E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{b}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}-K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}\partial^{\prime}_{b}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}

(16)

Finally, the Hamiltonian constraint becomes:

\displaystyle H=-\frac{\kappa}{2\delta\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}E^{{}^{\prime}[a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b]}_{j}\Big[K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}K_{b}^{{}^{\prime}j}+~\delta^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{(a)}}\partial_{a}\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}ij}+\omega_{a}^{{}^{\prime}ik}\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}kj}\Big)\Big]

As
\delta\rightarrow 0
, we obtain:

\displaystyle H^{\prime}=-\frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}E^{{}^{\prime}[a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b]}_{j}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}K_{b}^{{}^{\prime}j}

(17)

with the redefinition:
N:=\delta N^{\prime}
.

Notice that the Hamiltonian structure above corresponds precisely to the ‘electric’ Carroll limit in connection formulation
Sengupta 2023
. The additional factor
\frac{1}{\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}
in the Hamiltonian constraint
H
here compared to ref.
Sengupta 2023
originates due to the use of a scalar lapse.

IV.2
Magnetic Carroll gravity

Next, we consider a global scaling analogous to eq.(
12
), which provides another way to implement the degenerate limit:

\displaystyle K_{a}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{(a)}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i},~E^{a}_{i}=\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{i}.

(18)

Following the procedure already elucidated in section-III, we find:

\displaystyle G^{rot}_{i}=\delta\epsilon^{ijk}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}k}E^{{}^{\prime}a}_{j},

\displaystyle H_{a}=\delta_{(a)}\Big[\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial_{a}K_{b}^{{}^{\prime}i}-\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\partial_{b}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}-\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(b)}}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}\partial_{b}E^{{}^{\prime}b}_{i}\Big]

\displaystyle H=-\frac{\kappa}{2\delta\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}E^{{}^{\prime}[a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b]}_{j}\Big[\frac{\delta}{\delta_{(a)}}\partial_{a}\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}ij}(E^{\prime})+\omega_{a}^{{}^{\prime}ik}(E^{\prime})\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}kj}(E^{\prime})~+~\delta^{2}K_{a}^{{}^{\prime}i}K_{b}^{{}^{\prime}j}\Big]

Using the same redefinition of the Lagrange multipliers as in sec-III, we find that the rotation and spatial diffeomorphism constraints remain form-invariant, whereas the Hamiltonian constraint reduces to:

\displaystyle H^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt{E^{\prime}}}E^{{}^{\prime}[a}_{i}E^{{}^{\prime}b]}_{j}\Big[\partial^{\prime}_{a}\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}ij}(E^{\prime})+\omega_{a}^{{}^{\prime}ik}(E^{\prime})\omega_{b}^{{}^{\prime}kj}(E^{\prime})\Big]

This set of constraints resembles the Hamiltonian form of the ‘magnetic’ Carroll gravity
Bergshoeff et al. 2017
;
Henneaux and Salgado-Rebolledo 2021
in the connection formulation
Sengupta 2023
.

V
Conclusions

Here we have constructed a limit of Hamiltonian gravity theory corresponding to vanishing densitized triad. This is equivalent to the limit to a vanishing metric determinant. This limit is relevant in the context of connection-triad formulation of canonical quantum gravity.

Here we unravel two different limits of the Barbero-Immirzi formulation. In both cases, the spatial diffeomorphism constraints are satisfied trivially. Further, the respective Hamiltonian constraints exhibit significant simplifications. In the case of the first limit, an ultralocal behaviour (absence of space derivatives) emerges. A similar behaviour is also encountered in other contexts, for instance, in studies of the BKL limit to singularity. However, let us also emphasize that the associated Hamiltonian structure here is quite different from the one corresponding to a BKL behaviour
Ashtekar et al. 2009
;
*sloan1
. A canonical quantization of this degenerate limit may be taken up based on either the triad or connection representation. For the second limit, a triad representation would be more appropriate since the constraints in this case depend only on the densitized triad and its derivatives.

Our analysis also provides a different interpretation to Carroll gravity in terms of the spatial metric determinant. This may be contrasted with the original interpretation in terms of the

Appendix A
A Corollary on the violation of Birkhoff’s theorem in electric Carroll gravity

As a relevant extension to our discussion of Carroll gravity in this general context, we shall elucidate an important implication of the limiting Hamiltonian theory in the context of spherical symmetry.

Let us first consider ‘electric’ Carrollian limit of gravity defined by the constraints (
15
), (
16
) and (
17
).

Given the standard ansatz for a spherically symmetric spacetime:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{\mu(t,r)}dt^{2}+e^{\lambda(t,r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

the canonical momenta
E^{a}_{i}
are given by:

\displaystyle E^{r}_{1}=r^{2}\sin\theta,~E^{\theta}_{2}=e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}r\sin\theta,~E^{\phi}_{3}=e^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}r.

(20)

From their time evolution with respect to the total Hamiltonian, the only nontrivial canonical coordinate reads:

\displaystyle K_{r}^{1}:=\omega_{r}^{~01}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\lambda}e^{\frac{\lambda-\mu}{2}}.

(21)

All the constraints are satisfied trivially, except the radial component of the spatial diffeomorphism, which implies:

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{1}=0\Rightarrow\lambda=\lambda(r)

(22)

Hence,
\lambda
could be any arbitrary radial function, whereas the lapse (or,
\mu(t,r)
) is left undetermined.

This demonstrates that the Birkhoff’s theorem is violated by the ‘electric’ limit of Carrollian gravity.

For the other possible Carrollian case, namely the so-called ‘magnetic’ limit, it is straightforward to show that the limiting theory reproduces the Schwarzschild geometry as the unique solution, thus satisfying the Birkhoff’s theorem.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{H}})
represent the
C^{*}
-algebra comprising all bounded linear operators on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
The absolute value of
T,
is defined as
|T|=(T^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}},
where
T^{*}
denotes the Hilbert adjoint of the operator
T.
The numerical range of
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is denoted by
W(T)
, is the image of the unit sphere of
\mathcal{H}
under the mapping
x\rightarrow\langle Tx,x\rangle.
The numerical radius and the operator norm of an operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, are denoted by
w(T)
and
\|T\|
respectively, and are defined as

w(T)=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\left|\langle Tx,x\rangle\right|

and
\|T\|=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|Tx\|.

It is universally acknowledged that
w(\cdot)
constitutes a norm on

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
which is comparable to the operator norm through the following inequality:
\frac{\|T\|}{2}\leq w(T)\leq\|T\|.

Significant progress has been made in addressing this inequality in recent years. Here, we emphasize a few of these noteworthy advancements.
In
[
15
]
, Kittaneh demonstrated that

w(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\||T|+|T^{*}|\right\|.

This inequality was further generalized by El-Haddad et al.
[
12
]
, who asserted that

w^{2r}(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\||T|^{2r}+|T^{*}|^{2r}\right\|,r\geq 1
.
In 2015, Abu-Omar et al.
[
1
]
demonstrated that

w^{2}(T)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\||T|^{2}+|T^{*}|\right\|+\frac{1}{2}w(T^{2}).

In 2021, Bhunia et al.
[
10
]
proved that

w^{2}(T)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\||T|^{2}+|T^{*}|^{2}\right\|+\frac{1}{2}w\left(|T||T^{*}|\right).

Subsequently, Dragomir
[
13
]
derived the following inequality for the product of two operators, demonstrating that for
T,S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
r\geq 1
,

w^{r}(S^{*}T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\||T|^{2r}+|S|^{2r}\right\|.

(1.1)

For further details on recent work regarding the numerical radius inequalities, readers can see
[

,

,

,

.

An Orlicz function is a mathematical function used in the context of Orlicz spaces, which generalize classical Lebesgue spaces.

Definition 1.1
.

An Orlicz function
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a continuous, convex and increasing function that satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle~(i)~

\displaystyle\phi(0)=0

\displaystyle~~(ii)~

\displaystyle\phi(t)>0~~\forall t>0

\displaystyle~~(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\phi(t)=\infty.

Example 1.2
.

Most common examples of Orlicz function include

(i)
\phi(t)=t^{p}
with
p\geq 1,
which corresponds to the standard
{\ell}_{p}
spaces.

(ii)
\phi(t)=e^{t}-1,
which defines the exponential Orlicz space.

(iii)
\phi(t)=t^{p}\log(1+t),
for
p>0
mixed power exponential function.

(iv)
\phi(t)=e^{t^{2}}-1,
quadratic exponential function.

An Orlicz function is said to be sub-multiplicative if
\phi(t_{1}t_{2})\leq\phi(t_{1})\phi(t_{2}),
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0.

The study of numerical radius inequalities through the lens of Orlicz functions offers a fascinating exploration of operator theory within complex Hilbert spaces. By employing Orlicz functions, one can achieve sharper bounds on the numerical radius of operators, which has significant implications for both theoretical and applied mathematics.

The renowned Young inequality asserts that for any two positive real numbers
x
and
y
, along with
t
lying in the interval
[0,1]
,

x^{t}y^{1-t}\leq tx+(1-t)y
holds.
For
t={1}/{2}
, we encounter the famous arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, which stipulates that for any two positive real numbers
x
and
y
, the inequality

\sqrt{xy}\leq\frac{x+y}{2}
holds.

In this paper, we obtain the numerical radius inequalities of bounded linear operators involving the Orlicz functions. Choosing different Orlicz functions we deduce some new bounds for the numerical radius which refines the existing classical ones. We also deduce the existing numerical radius inequalities mentioned here.

2.
Main Results

In this section, we will establish various generalizations and enhancements of the upper bounds for numerical radii via Orlicz functions. To achieve this, we will employ the following well-known lemmas. The first lemma arises as a consequence of the spectral theorem, combined with Jensen’s inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
17
]

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a positive operator and
x
be an unit vector in
\mathcal{H}.
Then, for
r\geq 1,
the inequality
\langle Tx,x\rangle^{r}\leq\langle T^{r}x,x\rangle
holds.

The second lemma presents a norm inequality for a non-negative convex function.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
3
]

Let
\psi
be a non-negative convex function on
[0,\infty)
and
T,S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be positive operators. Then
\left\|\psi\left(\frac{T+S}{2}\right)\right\|\leq\left\|\frac{\psi(T)+\psi(S)}{2}\right\|

holds.
In particular, for
r\geq 1
,

\left\|\left(\frac{T+S}{2}\right)^{r}\right\|\leq\left\|\frac{T^{r}+S^{r}}{2}\right\|.

The third lemma presents a generalized formulation of the mixed Schwarz inequality.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
16
]

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
. Let
\psi,\eta
are two non-negative continuous function on
[0,\infty)
satisfying
\psi(t)\eta(t)=t.
Then

|\langle Tx,y\rangle|\leq\left\|\psi(|T|)x\right\|\left\|\eta(|T^{*}|)y\right\|.

The following outcome pertains to Buzano’s renowned extension of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
11
]

Let
x,y,e\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|e\|=1.
Then

|\langle x,e\rangle\langle e,y\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|x\|\|y\|+|\langle x,y\rangle|\right).

The next lemma represents the operator form of the classical Jensen’s inequality.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
16
]

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a self-adjoint operator whose spectrum contained in the interval
J
, and let
x\in\mathcal{H}
be a unit vector. If
h
is a convex function on
J
, then

h(\langle Tx,x\rangle)\leq\langle h(T)x,x\rangle.

The following lemma is a refinement of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
19
, Lemma 2.7]

Let
f:(0,1)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a well-defined function. Then,

|\langle x,y\rangle|^{2}\leq\frac{f(t)}{1+f(t)}\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{1+f(t)}|\langle x,y\rangle|\|x\|\|y\|\leq\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}\quad\text{for any $x,y\in\mathcal{H}$}.

First we present an enhancement of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality through the Orlicz extension of the Buzano’s inequality. The proof follows from Lemma
2.4
and the convexity property of the Orlicz function.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
x,y,e\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|e\|=1.
Then for any Orlicz function
\phi,

\phi\left(\left|\langle x,e\rangle\langle e,y\rangle\right|\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\phi\left(\|x\|\|y\|\right)+\phi\left(|\langle x,y\rangle|\right)\right).

In particular, for
\phi(t)=e^{t}-1
and
e^{t^{2}}-1
,

\displaystyle\left|\langle x,e\rangle\langle e,y\rangle\right|\leq\log\left(\frac{1}{2}{e^{\|x\|\|y\|}+\frac{1}{2}e^{|\langle x,y\rangle|}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x\|\|y\|

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle\left|\langle x,e\rangle\langle e,y\rangle\right|\leq\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{1}{2}{e^{\|x\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}e^{|\langle x,y\rangle|^{2}}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x\|\|y\|.

(2.2)

It is given in
[
15
]
that for any
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

w(T)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|T\|+\|T^{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

We ontain an extended version of the above bound via the Orlicz function, the proof follows by using the convexity property of the Orlicz function.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).
Then for any Orlicz function
\phi
,

\phi\left(w(T)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\phi(\|T\|)+\phi\left(\|T^{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right).

In particular, for
\phi(t)=e^{t}-1,

\displaystyle w(T)\leq\log\left(\frac{1}{2}{e^{\|T\|}+\frac{1}{2}e^{\|T^{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T\|.

(2.3)

The bound (
2.3
) refines the bound
w(T)\leq\|T\|.

Next we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
T,S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).
Let
f:(0,1)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be any well-defined function. Then for any sub-multiplicative Orlicz function
\phi
,

\displaystyle\phi\left(w^{2}(T^{*}S)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(w(T^{*}S)\right)\left\|\phi(|T|^{2})+\phi(|S|^{2})\right\|

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(w(|S|^{2}|T|^{2})\right)+\frac{f(t)}{4(1+f(t))}\left\|\phi(|T|^{4})+\phi(|S|^{4})\right\|.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
be an unit vector. By using the convex nature of
\phi
, we get that

\displaystyle\phi\left(\left|\langle T^{*}Sx,x\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\left(\left|\langle Tx,Sx\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\phi\left(\frac{1}{(1+f(t))}\|Tx\|\|Sx\||\langle Tx,Sx\rangle|+\frac{f(t)}{(1+f(t))}\|Tx\|^{2}\|Sx\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(\mbox{using Lemma \ref{gen cauchy}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+f(t))}\phi\left(\|Tx\|\|Sx\||\langle T^{*}Sx,x\rangle|\right)+\frac{f(t)}{(1+f(t))}\phi\left(\|Tx\|^{2}\|Sx\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+f(t))}\phi\left(\left\langle\left(\frac{|T|^{2}+|S|^{2}}{2}\right)x,x\right\rangle\right)\phi\left(\left|\langle T^{*}Sx,x\rangle\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{(1+f(t))}\phi\left(\langle|T|^{2}x,x\rangle\langle x,|S|^{2}x\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,~(\mbox{using A.M-G.M inequality and sub-multiplicity of $\phi$})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1+f(t))}\left\langle\left(\phi\left(|T|^{2}\right)+\phi\left(|S|^{2}\right)\right)x,x\right\rangle\phi\left(\left|\langle T^{*}Sx,x\rangle\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{(1+f(t))}\phi\left(\frac{\left\||T|^{2}x\right\|\left\||S|^{2}x\right\|+|\left\langle|T|^{2}x,|S|^{2}x\right\rangle|}{2}\right)

\displaystyle~\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(\mbox{using Lemma \ref{buzano}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1+f(t))}\left\langle\left(\phi\left(|T|^{2}\right)+\phi\left(|S|^{2}\right)\right)x,x\right\rangle\phi\left(\left|\langle T^{*}Sx,x\rangle\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(\left\langle\left(\frac{|T|^{4}+|S|^{4}}{2}\right)x,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(|\left\langle\left(|S|^{2}|T|^{2}\right)x,x\right\rangle|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1+f(t))}\left\langle\left(\phi\left(|T|^{2}\right)+\phi\left(|S|^{2}\right)\right)x,x\right\rangle\phi\left(\left|\langle T^{*}Sx,x\rangle\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{4(1+f(t))}\left\langle\left(\phi\left(|T|^{4}\right)+\phi\left(|S|^{4}\right)\right)x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(|\left\langle\left(|S|^{2}|T|^{2}\right)x,x\right\rangle|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(w(T^{*}S)\right)\left\|\phi(|T|^{2})+\phi(|S|^{2})\right\|

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(w(|S|^{2}|T|^{2})\right)+\frac{f(t)}{4(1+f(t))}\left\|\phi(|T|^{4})+\phi(|S|^{4})\right\|.

By taking the supremum over all
x
with
\|x\|=1,
we derive the desired inequality.
∎

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}w^{r}\left(|S|^{2}|T|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\||T|^{4r}+|S|^{4r}\right\|.

By considering the Orlicz function
\phi(t)=t^{r}

r\geq 1
in Theorem
2.9

Our next theorem is as follows.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
g,h

\phi

\displaystyle\phi\left(w^{2}(T)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{f(t)}{4(1+f(t))}\left\|\phi\left(g^{4}(|T|)\right)+\phi\left(h^{4}(|T^{*}|)\right)\right\|

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(w\left(h^{2}(|T^{*}|)g^{2}(|T|)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(w(T)\right)\left\|\phi\left(g^{2}(|T|)\right)+\phi\left(h^{2}(|T^{*}|)\right)\right\|.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
be an unit vector. Employing the convexity property of
\phi,

\displaystyle\phi\left(\left|\langle Tx,x\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{f(t)}{1+f(t)}\phi\left(\left|\langle Tx,x\rangle\right|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{1+f(t)}\phi\left(\left|\langle Tx,x\rangle\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{f(t)}{1+f(t)}\phi\left(\left\langle g^{2}(|T|)x,x\right\rangle\left\langle h^{2}(|T^{*}|)x,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(|\left\langle h^{2}(|T^{*}|)g^{2}(|T|)x,x\right\rangle|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(|\langle Tx,x\rangle|\right)\left\langle\left\{\phi\left(g^{2}(|T|)\right)+\phi\left(h^{2}(|T^{*}|)\right)\right\}x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(\left\langle\left(\frac{g^{4}(|T|)+h^{4}(|T^{*}|)}{2}\right)x,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(|\left\langle h^{2}(|T^{*}|)g^{2}(|T|)x,x\right\rangle|\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(|\langle Tx,x\rangle|\right)\left\langle\left\{\phi\left(g^{2}(|T|)\right)+\phi\left(h^{2}(|T^{*}|)\right)\right\}x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{f(t)}{4(1+f(t))}\left\langle\left(\phi\left(g^{4}(|T|)\right)+\phi\left(h^{4}(|T^{*}|)\right)\right)x,x\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{f(t)}{2(1+f(t))}\phi\left(\left\langle h^{2}(|T^{*}|)g^{2}(|T|)x,x\right\rangle\right)

Corollary 2.15
.

\displaystyle{w(T)}\leq\sqrt[n]{\log\left[\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}e^{w(T^{n})}+\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\right)e^{\|T\|^{n}}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T\|.

(2.9)

In particular, for n=2,

\displaystyle{w(T)}\leq\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{w(T^{2})}+\frac{1}{2}e^{\|T\|^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T\|.

(2.10)

Following result gives an estimation for the numerical radius of nilpotent operators.

Corollary 2.30
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. If
T^{n}=0
for some
n(\geq 2)\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\displaystyle{w(T)}\leq\sqrt[n]{\log\left[\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\right)e\right]}\,\|T\|.

Proof.

Consider
\|T\|=1.
Then from (
2.9
), we get
{w^{n}(T)}\leq\log\left[\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\right)e\right].

This completes the proof.
∎

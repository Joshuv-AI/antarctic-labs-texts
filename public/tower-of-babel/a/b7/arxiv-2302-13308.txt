Wooyeon Kim and Jens Marklof

Abstract
. We prove the convergence of moments of the number of directions of affine lattice vectors that fall into a small disc, under natural Diophantine conditions on the shift. Furthermore, we show that the pair correlation function is Poissonian for any irrational shift in dimension 3 and higher, including well-approximable vectors. Convergence in distribution was already proved in the work of Strömbergsson and the second author, and the principal step in the extension to convergence of moments is an escape of mass estimate for averages over embedded SL ⁡ ( d , ℝ ) \operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R}) -horospheres in the space of affine lattices.



1.
Introduction

It is often difficult to rigorously determine the pseudorandom properties of a given sequence of real numbers modulo one, including even the simplest second-order correlation functions. In the present paper we consider the problem in higher dimension and construct an explicit sequence of points
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{1},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{2},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{3},\ldots
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
whose two-point statistics converge to that of a Poisson point process. This sequence is given by the unit vectors
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j}=\|\mathbf{y}_{j}\|^{-1}\mathbf{y}_{j}
representing the directions of vectors
\mathbf{y}_{j}
in a fixed affine lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of unit covolume. Here the
\mathbf{y}_{j}
are listed in increasing length
\|\mathbf{y}_{j}\|
, where
\|\,\cdot\,\|
denotes the Euclidean norm. If there are two or more vectors of the same length we take them in arbitrary order (our results will not depend on the choice made). If there are several lattice points with the same direction, they will appear repeatedly in the sequence.
Our approach extends the results of
[
EMV15
]
, which in turn builds on
[
MS10
]
, from
d=2
to higher dimensions.
We are furthermore able to relax the Diophantine hypotheses imposed in
[
EMV15
]
.

A sequence
(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is called
uniformly distributed
, if for any set
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
with
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\partial\mathcal{D})=0
we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\#\left\{j\leq N:\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}\right\}=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\mathcal{D})}{V_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}},

where
V_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}=\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. The
pair correlation function
of the partial sequence
(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j})_{j=1}^{N}
is defined as

R^{2}_{N}(s)=\frac{1}{N}\#\left\{(j_{1},j_{2})\,:\,j_{1},j_{2}\leq N,\,j_{1}\neq j_{2},\,c_{d}N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\mathbf{d}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{1}},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{2}})\leq s\right\},

where
c_{d}=V_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}^{-\frac{1}{d-1}}
and
\mathbf{d}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
is the standard geodesic distance for the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. The scaling by
c_{d}N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}
ensures we are measuring correlations in units where the mean density of points is one (note that the scaled sphere
c_{d}N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
has volume
N
). The function
R^{2}_{N}(s)
is known as Ripley’s
K
-function in the statistical literature.

We say the pair correlation of the sequence
(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is
Poissonian
, if for any
s>0

(1.1)

{\lim_{N\to\infty}R^{2}_{N}(s)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}s^{d-1}}{\Gamma(\tfrac{d+1}{2})},}

which is the volume of a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
of radius
s
. This limit holds for example almost surely for a sequence of independent and uniformly distributed random points on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. It coincides with the pair correlation function of a Poisson point process in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
of intensity one, hence the term “Poissoninan”.

Every affine lattice of unit covolume can be explicitly written as
\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}=(\mathbb{Z}^{d}+\boldsymbol{\xi})M_{0}
,
where
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
M_{0}\in G=\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
. For integer shift
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we obtain the underlying lattice
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}M_{0}
. It follows from classical asymptotics for the number of affine lattice points in expanding sectors with a fixed opening angle that the sequence of directions is uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, for any shift
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

It is an interesting observation that a Poissonian pair correlation implies uniform distribution on general compact manifolds
[
M20
]
. This fact was first proved in the case of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
by Aistleitner, Lachmann, and Pausinger
[
ALP18
]
, and independently by Larcher and Stockinger
[
LS20
]
. For the convergence of the pair correlation function, we will however, unlike the case of uniform distribution, require Diophantine conditions on the lattice shift
\boldsymbol{\xi}
. For
\kappa\geq d
, we say that
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is Diophantine of type
\kappa
if there exists
C_{\kappa}>0
such that

|\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{m}|_{\mathbb{Z}}>C_{\kappa}|\mathbf{m}|^{-\kappa}

for any
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the supremum norm of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the supremum distance from
0\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. It is known that Lebesgue-almost all
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are of type
\kappa
for any
\kappa>d
. We will in fact only require a milder Diophantine condition. Define the function
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{N}
by

(1.2)

{\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},T):=\min\left\{N\in\mathbb{N}:\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\\
0<|\mathbf{m}|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{m}|_{\mathbb{Z}}\leq\frac{1}{T}\right\}.}

In view of Dirichlet’s pigeon hole principle, we have that
\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},T)\leq T^{1/d}
and, if
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is of Diophantine type
\kappa\geq d
, then
\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},T)>(C_{\kappa}T)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}
.

We say
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
(\rho,\mu,\nu)
-vaguely
Diophantine, if

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{\rho}\,2^{\mu}\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},2^{l-1})^{-\nu}<\infty.

Thus, if
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is Diophantine type
\kappa
, then it is also
(\rho,\mu,\nu)
-vaguely Diophantine for
\kappa\mu<\nu
.
If
\boldsymbol{\xi}
satisfies the weaker Brjuno Diophantine condition
[
LDG19
,
BF19
]
, then it is
(\rho,0,\nu)
-vaguely Diophantine for
0\leq\rho<\nu-1

(see Appendix
A
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{d}
; furthermore if
d=2
assume that
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
(0,0,2)
-vaguely Diophantine. Then the pair correlation function of the sequence
(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
of directions is Poissonian.

We note that the hypothesis on
\boldsymbol{\xi}
(in the case
d=2
) is satisfied for all Brjuno vectors, and thus in particular for Diophantine vectors of any type. In Appendix
B
we prove that there is a set of second Baire category of
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2}
for which the pair correlation function diverges. This shows that the Diophantine condition is indeed required.

The pair correlation function also exists for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
if
d\geq 3
and is closely related to the two-point statistics of multi-dimensional Farey sequences
[
BZ05
,
M13
]
and visible lattice points
[
BCZ00
,
MS10
]
. The deeper reason why we see a Poisson pair correlation for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\notin\mathbb{Q}^{d}
is that the limit distribution is expressed through the Haar measure on the semi-direct product group
\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where the averages over double-lattice sums reduces to Siegel’s mean value formula; cf. Proposition
7.1
and
[
EMV15
, Proposition 14]
. In the case of
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
, we need to apply Rogers’ formulas which exhibit non-trivial correlations in the lattice sums, which explains the non-Poissonian correlations in this case. This is also the reason why three-point and higher-order correlation functions for directions in affine lattices with
\boldsymbol{\xi}\notin\mathbb{Q}^{d}
are non-Poissonian. Fine-scale statistics of directions have also been studied in the context of quasicrystals
[
BGHJ14
,
MS15
,
H22
]
.

It is worth highlighting that in the analogous problem of directions in hyperbolic lattices, the pair correlation statistics are not Poissonian; see
[
MV18
]
and references therein.

Theorem
1.1
provides an example of a
deterministic
sequence in higher dimension whose pair correlation density is Poissonian. Other local statistics, however, deviate from the Poisson distribution in other statistical tests, as shown in
[
MS10
]
. By
deterministic
we mean here that convergence is proved not just almost surely or in probability, but for a fixed, explicit sequence. An interesting non-Poisson random point process with Poissonian pair correlation is discussed in
[
BS84
]
.

Theorem
1.1
generalises results of
[
EMV15
]
for two-dimensional affine lattices and under a stronger Diophantine condition on
\boldsymbol{\xi}
, as well as earlier work by Boca and Zaharescu
[
BZ06
]
which was limited to almost every
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Other examples of deterministic sequences with Poissonian pair correlation in one dimension include
\sqrt{n}\bmod 1
(excluding
n
that are perfect squares)
[
EM04
,
EMV15b
]
and the recent paper by Lutsko, Sourmelidis and Technau
[
LST21
]
on
\alpha n^{\theta}\bmod 1
which holds for every
\alpha>0
and
\theta\leq 1/3
. Sequences such as
\alpha n^{2}\bmod 1

[
RS98
]
or
\alpha 2^{n}\bmod 1

[
RZ99
]
have Poissonian pair correlation for almost every
\alpha
, but with no explicit instances of
\alpha
currently known. For more references on recent developments on metric pair correlation problems, we refer the reader to
[
AEM21
,
LS20b
]
and references therein.

Finally we mention work of Bourgain, Rudnick and Sarnak
[
BRS16
,
BRS17
]
, who considered the fine-scale statistics of lattice points (without a shift) on large spheres, rather than radially projected points as in our setting. Remarkably, in dimension two, Kurlberg and Lester have recently been able to prove that all correlation functions converge to Poisson along density-one subsequences of eligible radii
[
KL22
]
.

The next section will recall the convergence in distribution for the directions in affine lattices from
[
MS10
]
, and then state an extension to convergence of mixed moments (Theorem
2.2
), which is the main result of this paper. An application of the Siegel mean value formula gives explicit expressions for all second-order statistics, and in particular shows that the pair correlation function is Poissonian (Corollaries
2.3
and
2.4
). These results thus immediately imply Theorem
1.1
. Section
3
introduces the space of affine lattices. In Section
5
we prove escape-of-mass estimates for spherical averages that allow us to pass from convergence in distribution to convergence of moments. Sections
6
and
7
supply the proofs of our Main Lemma, which immediately implies Theorem
2.2
, and Corollaries
2.3
and
2.4
, respectively.

2.
Limit distribution and higher moments

We consider the set
\mathcal{P}_{T}
of affine lattice points
y\in\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
inside the ball of radius
\mathcal{B}_{T}^{d}
or, more generally,
\mathcal{P}_{c,T}
the lattice points in
the spherical shell

\mathcal{B}_{c,T}^{d}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:cT\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq T\right\},\quad 0\leq c<1.

The well known asymptotics for the number of lattice points in a large ball yields for
T\to\infty
,

\#\mathcal{P}_{c,T}\sim\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{B}_{c,1}^{d})T^{d}=\frac{1-c^{d}}{d}V_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}T^{d}.

For
\sigma>0
and
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we define
\mathfrak{D}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
to be the open disc with center
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}
and volume

\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\mathfrak{D}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon}))=\frac{\sigma d}{1-c^{d}}T^{-d}.

Then the radius of
\mathfrak{D}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon})
is
\asymp T^{-\frac{d}{d-1}}
, and for
T\to\infty
,

\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\mathfrak{D}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon}))}{V_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}\sim\frac{\sigma}{\#\mathcal{P}_{c,T}}.

Thus
\sigma
measures the disc’s volume in terms of the average density of points on the sphere; this scale is compatible with the one introduced above for the pair correlation function.

We define the counting function

\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon}):=\#\left\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{P}_{c,T}:\|\mathbf{y}\|^{-1}\mathbf{y}\in\mathfrak{D}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\right\}

for the number of affine lattice points whose direction is contained in
\mathfrak{D}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon})
.
Note that on average over
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}
, uniform distribution implies (cf.
[
MS10
, Section 2.3]
) that for any Borel probability measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
with continuous density, we have

(2.1)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\lambda(d\boldsymbol{\upsilon})=\sigma.}

This says that the expected number of affine lattice points, with direction contained in
\mathfrak{D}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon})
for random
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}
, is
\sigma
.

We recall the following result from
[
MS10
]
, which provides the full limit distribution of
\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\upsilon})
with random
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}
distributed according to a general Borel probability measure
\lambda
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
MS10
]

For
\underline{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
, there is a probability distribution
E_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\cdot,\underline{\sigma})
on
\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{\geq 0}
such that, for any
\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{\geq 0}
and any Borel probability measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\lambda({\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{1},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})=r_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{m},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})=r_{m}})=E_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma}).

The limit distribution satisfies the following properties, cf. Section
4
:

(a)

E_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma})
is independent of
\lambda
and
\mathcal{L}
.

(b)

\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}}r_{j}E_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma})=\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}rE_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(r,\sigma_{j})=\sigma_{j}
for any
j\leq m
.

(c)

For
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
,
\sum_{\underline{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}}\|\underline{r}\|^{s}E_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma})<\infty
for
0\leq s<d
, and
=\infty
for
s\geq d
.

(d)

For
\boldsymbol{\xi}\notin\mathbb{Q}^{d}
,
E_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma})=:E_{c}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma})
is independent of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
.

(e)

For
\boldsymbol{\xi}\notin\mathbb{Q}^{d}
,
\sum_{\underline{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}}\|\underline{r}\|^{s}E_{c}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma})<\infty
for
0\leq s<d+1
, and
=\infty
for
s\geq d+1
.

A key ingredient of the proof of Theorem
2.1
is Ratner’s measure classification theorem, which allows one to prove equidistribution of horospheres embedded in the space of affine lattices. An effective version of this statement was established only recently
[
K21
]
.

Let us now turn to the main outcome of the present investigation, which extends the results of
[
EMV15
]
to arbitrary dimension. For
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}>0
,
\lambda
a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
let

(2.2)

{\mathbb{M}_{\lambda}(T,\underline{z}):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{1},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})+1)^{z_{1}}\cdots(\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{m},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})+1)^{z_{m}}\lambda(d\boldsymbol{\upsilon}).}

We denote the positive real part of
z\in\mathbb{C}
by
\operatorname{Re}_{+}(z):=\max\left\{\operatorname{Re}(z),0\right\}
.

The following is the principal theorem of this paper.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}>0
, and
\lambda
a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
with continuous density. Choose
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\underline{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, such that one of the following hypotheses holds:

(A1)

\operatorname{Re}_{+}(z_{1})+\cdots+\operatorname{Re}_{+}(z_{m})<d.

(A2)

\eta:=\operatorname{Re}_{+}(z_{1})+\cdots+\operatorname{Re}_{+}(z_{m})<d+1
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
(0,\eta-2,2)
-vaguely Diophantine if
d=2
and
(d-1,\eta-d,1)
-vaguely Diophantine if
d\geq 3
.

Then

(2.3)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{M}_{\lambda}(T,\underline{z})=\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}}(r_{1}+1)^{z_{1}}\cdots(r_{m}+1)^{z_{m}}E_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma}).}

We note that if
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is Diophantine of type
\kappa
then under (A2) we have
\eta<2+\frac{2}{\kappa}
if
d=2
and
\eta<d+\frac{1}{\kappa}
if
d\geq 3
. Thus in particular for badly approximable
\boldsymbol{\xi}
(where
\kappa=d
) we have
\eta<3
if
d=2
and
\eta<d+\frac{1}{d}
if
d\geq 3
.

We define the restricted moments to explain the key step of the proof of Theorem
2.2
:

(2.4)

{\mathbb{M}_{\lambda}^{(K)}(T,\underline{z}):=\int_{\max_{j}\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{j},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\leq K}(\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{1},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})+1)^{z_{1}}\cdots(\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{m},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})+1)^{z_{m}}\lambda(d\boldsymbol{\upsilon}).}

Then Theorem
2.1
implies that, for any
K>0
,

(2.5)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{M}_{\lambda}^{(K)}(T,\underline{z})=\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m},|\underline{r}|\leq K}(r_{1}+1)^{z_{1}}\cdots(r_{m}+1)^{z_{m}}E_{c,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma}),}

where
|\underline{r}|:=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq m}r_{m}
. Thus, for the proof of Theorem
2.2
it remains to show that under (A1)–(A2),

(2.6)

{\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{M}_{\lambda}(T,\underline{z})-\mathbb{M}_{\lambda}^{(K)}(T,\underline{z})\right|=0.}

We will prove this in Section
6
.

The following corollaries of Theorem
2.2
state that in particular the second moment and pair correlation converge and are Poisonnian.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\lambda
be as in Theorem
2.2
. Let
d\geq 2
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{d}
; furthermore if
d=2
assume that
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
(0,0,2)
-vaguely Diophantine. Then, for any
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>0
,

(2.7)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{1},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\mathcal{N}_{c,T}(\sigma_{2},\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\lambda(d\boldsymbol{\upsilon})=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}+\min\left\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right\}.}

Let
N=N_{c}(T)
be the number of points in
\mathcal{P}_{c,T}
, and let

\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j}=\|\mathbf{y}_{j}\|^{-1}\mathbf{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be the directions of the vectors
\mathbf{y}_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{c,T}
, with
j=1,\ldots,N_{c}(T)
. For
f\in\operatorname{C}_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R})
(continuous, real-valued, and with compact support), we define the two-point correlation function

(2.8)

{R_{N}^{2}(f)=\frac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},j_{2}=1\\
j_{1}\neq j_{2}\end{subarray}}^{N}f(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{1}},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{2}},c_{d}N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\mathbf{d}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{1}},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{2}})).}

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
0\leq c<1
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{d}
; furthermore if
d=2
assume that
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
(0,0,2)
-vaguely Diophantine. Then for any
f\in\operatorname{C}_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R})

(2.9)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}R_{N_{c}(T)}^{2}(f)=\frac{V_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}}{V_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}f(\boldsymbol{\upsilon},\boldsymbol{\upsilon},s)\,d\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\,s^{d-2}ds.}

Corollary
2.4
implies Theorem
1.1
by approximating the characteristic function from above/below by
\operatorname{C}_{0}
functions. The additional dependence of
f(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{1},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{2},s)
on
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{1},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
can be used to generalise Theorem
1.1
to pair counting where
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{1}}
and
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{2}}
are restricted to different subsets of
\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Set

R^{2}_{N}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},s)=\frac{1}{N}\#\bigg\{(j_{1},j_{2})\,:\,j_{1},j_{2}\leq N,\,j_{1}\neq j_{2},\\
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{1}}\in\mathcal{D}_{1},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{2}}\in\mathcal{D}_{2},\,\,c_{d}N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\mathbf{d}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{1}},\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{2}})\leq s\bigg\}.

We then have the following.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
0\leq c<1
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{d}
; furthermore if
d=2
assume that
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
(0,0,2)
-vaguely Diophantine. Then for any
\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
with
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{1})=\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{2})=0
and
s>0
, we have that

(2.10)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}R_{N_{c}(T)}^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},s)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}s^{d-1}}{\Gamma(\tfrac{d+1}{2})}\;\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}_{2})}{V_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}.}

3.
The space of affine lattices

Let
G=\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
and
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
. Define
G^{\prime}=G\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

(M,\mathbf{b})(M^{\prime},\mathbf{b}^{\prime})=(MM^{\prime},\mathbf{b}M^{\prime}+\mathbf{b}^{\prime}),

and let
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
denote the corresponding arithmetic subgroup. The right action of

Lemma 5.5
.

For
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
let

\omega_{s,\boldsymbol{\xi}}(k,p,l)=\begin{cases}dk-(d+1-s)p-d\log_{2}\frac{\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},2^{l-1})}{4\|M_{0}\|}&\text{if $p\leq k-\log_{2}\frac{\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},2^{l-1})}{4\|M_{0}\|}$,}\\

sk-p&\text{if $p>k$}.\end{cases}

For any
\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq K_{0}}^{s}
,
\underline{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{s}
, and
\underline{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}_{\geq 0}
,

Moreover, if there exists
1\leq j\leq s
such that
p_{j}\geq jk_{j}+K
or

, then
\#\Lambda_{\underline{k}}(\underline{p})=0
. Here,
K
is a sufficiently large constant depending on the choice of lattice
\Lambda

Proof.

For
(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{s})\in\Lambda_{\underline{k}}^{\underline{l}}(p)\subseteq\Lambda_{\underline{k}}(p)

(5.25)

{\left\|\bigwedge_{i=1}^{j}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\|\leq 2^{-p_{j}}\|\mathbf{x}_{j}\|\left\|\bigwedge_{i=1}^{j-1}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\|}

for

. Given
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{j-1}

\mathbf{x}_{j}\in\Lambda_{k_{j}}

\ll 2^{\omega_{s}(k_{j},p_{j})}=2^{sk_{j}-p_{j}}
if

. Hence, it is enough to show that this bound can be improved under the assumption
p_{j}\leq k_{j}
.

We first consider the case

\ll 2^{k_{j}-p_{j}}

\mathbf{x}_{j}

5.25

along the directions perpendicular to
V

2^{k_{j}}
along the directions of
V
. This region can be covered with at most

\ll 2^{dk_{j}-(d+1-j)p_{j}}\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},\delta_{d,r}^{-1}2^{l_{j}-1})^{-d}\ll 2^{dk_{j}-(d+1-j)p_{j}}\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},2^{l_{j}-1})^{-d}

We claim that there is at most one point of
\Lambda_{k_{j}}
satisfying (
5.24
) in each cube with sidelength
\|M_{0}\|^{-1}\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},\delta_{d,r}^{-1}2^{l_{j}-1})
. To see this, suppose that there are two distinct points

with distance

5.24
). Then we have

satisfying (

) and (
5.25

.

\mathbf{x}_{j}
satisfying (

Proposition 5.7
.

Let

{\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left|\int_{\boldsymbol{\upsilon}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{+}}F_{R,\eta,r}\left(\Gamma^{\prime}(1,\boldsymbol{\xi})M_{0}k(\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\Phi_{t}\right)d\lambda(\boldsymbol{\upsilon})\right|=0.}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{\prime}(1,\boldsymbol{\xi})M_{0}\widetilde{n}(\mathbf{y}(\boldsymbol{\upsilon}))\Phi_{t}\left(\begin{matrix}c(\boldsymbol{\upsilon})&0\\

\left(\begin{matrix}c(\boldsymbol{\upsilon})&0\\

Proposition 7.1
.

[

If

{\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}_{1}\neq\mathbf{m}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}F(\mathbf{m}_{1}g,\mathbf{m}_{2}g)\,dm_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}}(g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{x}\,d\mathbf{y}.}

g=\iota(\Gamma^{\prime}g)\in G^{\prime}
as the representative of the coset
\Gamma^{\prime}g
in the fundamental domain
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.

For the proof of Corollary
2.3
, we first prove

(7.1)

{\displaystyle\sum_{\underline{r}=(r_{1},r_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}}r_{1}r_{2}E_{c}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma})=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}+\min\left\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right\},}

for
\underline{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle\sum_{(r_{1},r_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}}r_{1}r_{2}E_{c}(\underline{r},\underline{\sigma})=\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}_{1},\mathbf{m}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\chi_{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\sigma_{1})}(\mathbf{m}_{1}g)\chi_{\mathfrak{C}_{c}(\sigma_{2})}(\mathbf{m}_{2}g)\,dm_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}}(g).

Recall that for
\sigma>0

, the off-diagonal part of the right-hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\chi_{\mathfrak{C}}(\mathbf{m}g)\,dm_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{\prime}}(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{\mathfrak{C}}(\mathbf{x}M)\,d\mathbf{x}\,dm_{\Gamma\backslash G}(M)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{\mathfrak{C}}(\mathbf{x})\,dx=\min\left\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right\}.

This completes the proof of (
7.1
).

For the proof of Corollary

, observe that for

holds under the assumption of Corollary

In this section we show that Corollary
2.3
implies Corollary

. Throughout this section we assume that the statement of Corollary

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
h\in\operatorname{C}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>0
. Then

(7.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},j_{2}=1\\
j_{1}\neq j_{2}\end{subarray}}^{N}\chi_{[0,\sigma_{1}]}(N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\mathbf{d}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j_{1}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\chi_{[0,\sigma_{2}]}(N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\mathbf{d}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j_{2}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}))h(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}h(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.3
, the left-hand side of (
7.2
) without the restriction

converges to

(7.3)

{\min\left\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right\}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}h(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}h(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}.}

On the other hand, the diagonal part
j_{1}=j_{2}
of the left-hand side of (

) is

(7.4)

{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{[0,\min\left\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right\}]}(N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\mathbf{d}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j},\boldsymbol{\alpha}))h(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}.}

Since

is continuous and
\mathbf{d}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j},\boldsymbol{\alpha})\ll_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}N^{-\frac{1}{d-1}}
for any

{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{[0,\min\left\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\right\}]}(N^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\mathbf{d}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j},\boldsymbol{\alpha}))h(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}}

Appendix A
Brjuno type condition

Following
[
LDG19
]
(cf. also
[
BF19
]
) we say that
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
s
-Brjuno vector if

{\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-\frac{n}{s}}\displaystyle\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\\
0<|\mathbf{m}|\leq 2^{n}\end{subarray}}\log\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{m}|_{\mathbb{Z}}}<\infty.}

The classical Brjuno condition corresponds to
s=1
.

In this section, we prove that for
s>\frac{\rho+1}{\nu}
, every
s
-Brjuno vector is
(\rho,0,\nu)
-vaguely Diophantine.
Given
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
let us define
\phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}
by

{\phi(N):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\\
0<|\mathbf{m}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\log\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{m}|_{\mathbb{Z}}}.}

Then the definition of
\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},T)
can be written in terms of
\phi(N)
as follows:

\displaystyle\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},T)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{N\in\mathbb{N}:e^{-\phi(N)}\leq T^{-1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{N\in\mathbb{N}:\phi(N)\geq\log T\right\}.

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
s
-Brjuno type for some
s>\frac{\rho+1}{\nu}
. Then we have
\sum_{n}2^{-\frac{n}{s}}\phi(2^{n})<\infty
, hence
\phi(t)\leq t^{\frac{1}{s}}\log 2
for sufficiently large
t
. It follows that

{\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},2^{l-1})=\min\left\{N\in\mathbb{N}:\phi(N)\geq(l-1)\log 2\right\}\geq(l-1)^{s}}

for sufficiently large
l
. Thus, it implies that
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is
(\rho,0,\nu)
-vaguely Diophantine since

{\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{\rho}\,\zeta(\boldsymbol{\xi},2^{l-1})^{-\nu}\ll\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{\rho-s\nu}<\infty.}

Appendix B
Counterexamples

The following theorem establishes that for
d=2
there exist uncountably many
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{2}
such that the pair correlation function of the directions of the affine lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}+\boldsymbol{\xi}
diverges, whilst converging to the Poisson limit along a subsequence.

Theorem B.1
.

Fix
s>0
. Then there exists a set
{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of second Baire category with the property that for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in{\mathcal{C}}
there are sequences
(M_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(N_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with
M_{j},N_{j}\to\infty
such that

(B.1)

R^{2}_{M_{j}}(s)\geq\frac{\log M_{j}}{\log\log\log M_{j}}

and

(B.2)

\lim_{j\to\infty}R^{2}_{N_{j}}(s)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}s^{d-1}}{\Gamma(\tfrac{d+1}{2})}.

The proof of this statement follows from Theorem
1.1
and the next lemma, by a Baire category argument identical to that used in
[
Sa97
]
; see also
[
M03
, §9]
.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
such that for any
s>0
and
T>0
we have

(B.3)

R^{2}_{N_{0}(T)}(s)\geq C_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\log N_{0}(T).

Proof.

We first observe that for any
s>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}

R^{2}_{N}(s)\geq\frac{1}{N}\#\left\{(j_{1},j_{2})\,:\,j_{1},j_{2}\leq N,\,j_{1}\neq j_{2},\,\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{1}}=\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{j_{2}}\right\}.

Let us first consider the case
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(0,0)
. We denote the set of primitive lattice points by
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\operatorname{prim}}
. Then

\displaystyle R^{2}_{N_{0}(T)}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{0}(T)}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\operatorname{prim}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\neq 0,\;k\neq l,\\
\|k\mathbf{v}\|,\|l\mathbf{v}\|\leq T\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{T^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m<n\leq\frac{T}{2},\\
\operatorname{gcd}(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{T}{n}\right)\left(\frac{T}{n}-1\right)

\displaystyle\gg\sum_{1\leq n\leq\frac{T}{2}}\frac{\varphi(n)}{n^{2}}\gg\log T\gg\log N_{0}(T),

where
\varphi
denotes Euler’s totient function. The above lower bound for the sum over the totient functions follows (via diadic decomposition) from the classic asymptotics

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\varphi(n)\sim\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}x^{2}
.

The case
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
is similar to the above. Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\frac{1}{q}\mathbf{p}
for some
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have

\displaystyle R^{2}_{N_{0}(T)}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{0}(T)}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\operatorname{prim}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\neq 0,\;k\neq l,\\
\|k\mathbf{v}\|\leq qT,\;k\mathbf{v}\equiv\mathbf{p}\;(\operatorname{mod}\;q),\\
\|l\mathbf{v}\|\leq qT,\;l\mathbf{v}\equiv\mathbf{p}\;(\operatorname{mod}\;q)\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{T^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m<n\leq\frac{T}{2q},\\
\operatorname{gcd}(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{T}{n}\right)\left(\frac{T}{n}-1\right)

\displaystyle\gg\sum_{1\leq n\leq\frac{T}{2q}}\frac{\varphi(n)}{n^{2}}\gg\log T\gg\log N_{0}(T).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

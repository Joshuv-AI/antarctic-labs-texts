Abstract
. We investigate Zariski multiples of plane curves Z 1 , … , Z N Z_{1},\dots,Z_{N} such that each Z i Z_{i} is a union of a smooth quartic curve, some of its bitangents, and some of its 4 4 -tangent conics. We show that, for plane curves of this type, the deformation types are equal to the homeomorphism types, and that the number of deformation types grows as O ⁡ ( d 62 ) O(d^{\,62}) when the degree d d of the plane curves tends to infinity.

1.
Introduction

By a
plane curve
,
we mean a reduced, possibly reducible, complex projective plane curve. We say that two plane curves
C
and
C^{\prime}
have
the same
combinatorial type

if there exist tubular neighbourhoods
T(C)
of
C

and
T(C^{\prime})
of
C^{\prime}

such that
(T(C),C)
and
(T(C^{\prime}),C^{\prime})
are homeomorphic,
whereas we say that
C
and
C^{\prime}
have
the same
homeomorphism type

if
(\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2},C)
and
(\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2},C^{\prime})
are homeomorphic.

A
Zariski
N
-ple
is a set of plane curves

\{C_{1},\dots,C_{N}\}

such that
the curves
C_{i}
have the same combinatorial type,
but their homeomorphism types are pairwise different.
The notion of Zariski
N
-ples was introduced by Artal-Bartolo
[
1
]

in reviving a classical example of
6
-cuspidal curves of degree
6
due to Zariski.
Since then,
this notion has been studied by many people from various points of view.
Some of the tools that have been used in this investigation are:
Alexander polynomials, characteristic varieties, fundamental groups of complements,
topological invariants of branched coverings, and so on. See the survey paper
[
3
]
.
Recently,
Bannai et al.
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
have investigated Zariski
N
-ples
such that each member is a union of a smooth quartic curve and some of its bitangents.

In this paper,
we introduce
4
-tangent conics of a smooth quartic curve,
and consider
Zariski
N
-ples
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{N}

such that each
Z_{i}
is a union of a smooth quartic curve, some of its bitangents,
and some of its
4
-tangent conics.

Let
Q
be a smooth quartic curve.
A
bitangent
of
Q
is a line whose intersection multiplicity
with
Q
is even at each intersection point.
It is well known that every smooth quartic curve has
exactly
28
bitangents.
We say that a bitangent
\bar{l}
of
Q
is
ordinary

if
\bar{l}
is tangent to
Q
at distinct
2
points.
A smooth conic
\bar{c}\subset\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is called a
4
-tangent conic of
Q

if
\bar{c}
is tangent to
Q
at
4
distinct points.
Every smooth quartic curve has
63
one-dimensional connected families of
4
-tangent conics
(see Theorem
4.1
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
m
and
n
be non-negative integers such that
m\leq 28
.
We say that a plane curve
Z
is a
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curve

if
Z
is of the form

(1.1)

Z=Q+\bar{l}_{1}+\cdots+\bar{l}_{m}+\bar{c}_{1}+\cdots+\bar{c}_{n},

where
Q
is a smooth quartic curve,

\bar{l}_{1},\dots,\bar{l}_{m}
are distinct bitangents of
Q
, and

\bar{c}_{1},\dots,\bar{c}_{n}
are distinct
4
-tangent conics of
Q
,
and they satisfy that

(i)

the bitangents
\bar{l}_{1},\dots,\bar{l}_{m}
are ordinary,

(ii)

the intersection of any three of
Q,\bar{l}_{1},\dots,\bar{l}_{m},\bar{c}_{1},\dots,\bar{c}_{n}
is empty,
and

(iii)

the intersection of any two of
\bar{l}_{1},\dots,\bar{l}_{m},\bar{c}_{1},\dots,\bar{c}_{n}
is transverse.

It is obvious that any two
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curves have
the same combinatorial type.
We construct a non-singular variety
\mathord{\mathcal{Z}}^{(m,n)}

parameterizing all
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curves in Section
5
.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that two
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curves
have the same
deformation type

if they belong to the same connected component of
the parameter space
\mathord{\mathcal{Z}}^{(m,n)}
.

It is obvious that
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curves of the same deformation type
have the same homeomorphism type.
Our main results are the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

If two
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curves
have the same homeomorphism type,
then they have the same deformation type.

We put

(1.2)

d^{(m,n)}:=\binom{28}{m}\cdot\binom{n+62}{62},

which grows as
O(n^{62})
when
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 1.4
.

The number
N^{(m,n)}
of
deformation types of
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curves
satisfies

(1.3)

d^{(m,n)}/1451520\leq N^{(m,n)}\leq d^{(m,n)}.

,

The
4
-tangent conics of a smooth quartic curve
Q

are related to the
2
-torsion points
of the Jacobian of
Q
(see Remark
4.3
).
A similar idea applied to plane cubic curves
enabled us to construct in
[
15
]

certain equisingular families of plane curves with many connected components.
In
[
15
]
, it was also shown that
these connected components cannot be distinguished by the fundamental groups of the complements,
because they are all ablelian. Then it was shown in
[
8
]
and
[
18
]

that the homeomorphism types of distinct connected components of these families
can be distinguished by the invariant called
linking numbers
.

The embedding topology of
reducible plane curves whose irreducible components are tangent to each other was also investigated by
Artal Bartolo, Cogolludo, and Tokunaga in
[
2
]
from the view point of dihedral covering of the plane
branched along the curve.
In this case, the complement can have a non-abelian fundamental group.
See
[
2
, Corollary 1]
.

It would be an interesting problem to study the fundamental groups of the complements of
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curves,
and their related invariants such as linking numbers and/or (non-)existence of finite coverings of the plane with prescribed Galois groups.

Via the cyclic covering of the plane of degree
4
branched along a smooth quartic curve,
the geometry of
\mathord{\mathcal{Q}}^{(m,n)}
-curves is related to the geometry of
K3
surfaces.
By considering
the double covering branched along a singular sextic curve and employing Torelli theorem for complex
K3
surfaces,
we have investigated in
[
16
]
Zariski
N
-ples of plane curves of degree
6
with only simple singularities.
We expect that a similar idea can be applied to Zariski
N
-ples
associated with singular quartic curves.

\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

For a positive integer

3.
Monodromy

Let
u
be a point of
\mathord{\mathcal{U}}
.
It is well known
that the lattice

SY_{u}=H^{2}(Y_{u},\mathord{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to the lattice of rank
8

whose Gram matrix is the diagonal matrix

\mathord{\mathrm{diag}}(1,-1,\dots,-1)
,
and that
the orthogonal complement

\Sigma_{u}:=(\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}h_{u}\hookrightarrow SY_{u})^{\perp}

of the ample class
h_{u}
in
SY_{u}

is isomorphic to the negative-definite root lattice of type
E_{7}
.
The deck transformation

\iota_{u}\colon Y_{u}\to Y_{u}

of
\pi_{u}\colon Y_{u}\to\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

acts on
\Sigma_{u}
as
-1
.
Note that the group
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(\Sigma_{u})
of isometries of
\Sigma_{u}

is equal to the Weyl group

W(E_{7})
, which is of order
2903040
.
Hence there exists an injective homomorphism

(3.1)

\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(SY_{u},h_{u}):=\{\,{g\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(SY_{u})}\,|\,{h_{u}^{g}=h_{u}}\,\}\;\;\hookrightarrow\;\;W(E_{7}).

It is easy to check that
the action on
\Sigma_{u}
of
each of the standard generators of
W(E_{7})
lifts
to an isometry of
SY_{u}
that fixes
h_{u}
.
Hence the homomorphism (
3.1
) is in fact an isomorphism.
The family of lattices
\{\,{SY_{u}}\,|\,{u\in\mathord{\mathcal{U}}}\,\}

forms a locally constant system

\mathrm{\mathcal{SY}}\to\mathord{\mathcal{U}}.

Let
b
be a general point of
\mathord{\mathcal{U}}
,
which will serve as a base point of
\mathord{\mathcal{U}}
.
The monodromy action
of
\pi_{1}(\mathord{\mathcal{U}},b)
on the lattice
SY_{b}

preserves
h_{b}\in SY_{b}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Harris
[
9
]
)
.

The monodromy homomorphism

(3.2)

\pi_{1}(\mathord{\mathcal{U}},b)\to\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(SY_{b},h_{b})\cong W(E_{7})

is surjective.

The original statement in
[
9
]

is not on the monodromy action on the lattice
SY_{b}
,
but on the Galois group
W(E_{7})/\{\pm 1\}\cong\mathord{\mathrm{GO}}_{6}(\mathord{\mathbb{F}}_{2})
of bitangents of
Q_{b}
.
Moreover the proof in
[
9
]
is via the proof of a similar result
on cubic surfaces with
E_{7}
replaced by

.
Hence we give a direct and simple proof of Theorem

For the proof, we prepare some more notation,

,
we have an identification

\displaystyle L_{u}

\displaystyle\;\cong\;

\displaystyle\{\,{v\in\Sigma_{u}^{\vee}}\,|\,{\langle v,v\rangle_{Y}=-3/2}\,\},

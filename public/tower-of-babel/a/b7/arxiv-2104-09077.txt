Global Well-posedness of Incompressible Elastodynamics in Three Dimensional Thin Domain
Yuan Cai, Fan Wang

Abstract
In this article, we prove global existence of classical solutions to the incompressible isotropic Hookean elastodynamics in three-dimensional thin domain Ω δ = ℝ 2 × [ 0 , δ ] \Omega_{\delta}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\delta] with periodic boundary condition. This system is essentially two dimensional quasilinear wave type equations. Following the classical vector field theory and the generalized energy method of Klainerman, we introduce the anisotropic weighted Sobolev type inequalities, the anisotropic generalized energy and the anisotropic weighted L 2 L^{2} norm adapted to the thin domain. The main issue in the anisotropic generalized energy estimate is that, the pressure gradient brings an extra singular δ − 1 \delta^{-1} factor arising from the ∂ 3 2 \partial^{2}_{3} derivative. Based on the inherent cancellation structure, we introduce an auxiliary anisotropic generalized energy to overcome this difficulty. The ghost weight technique introduced by Alinhac [ 2 ] and the strong null condition introduced by Lei [ 32 ] play an important role for temporal decay estimates.



1
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the global existence of classical solutions to
the Cauchy problem of incompressible nonlinear elastodynamics
in three dimensional thin domain
\Omega_{\delta}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\delta]
with periodic boundary condition.
For homogeneous, isotropic and hyperelastic materials, the motion of
the elastic fluids is determined by the following Lagrangian
functional of flow maps
[
32
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(X;T,\Omega_{\delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\big(\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{t}X(t,y)|^{2}-W(\nabla X(t,y))

(1.1)

\displaystyle+\ p(t,y)\big[\det\big(\nabla X(t,y)\big)-1\big]\big)dydt.

Here
X(t,y)
is the flow map,
W\in C^{\infty}(GL_{3},\mathbb{R}_{+})
is the strain energy
function which depends only on the deformation tensor
\nabla X
,

p(t,y)
is a Lagrangian
multiplier which forces the flow maps to be
incompressible.
Without loss of generality, we will only focus on
the typical Hookean case, in
which the strain energy functional is given by

W(\nabla X)=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla X|^{2}.

In this case, the Euler-Lagrangian equation of (
1.1
) takes

\partial_{t}^{2}X-\Delta X=-(\nabla X)^{-\top}\nabla p.

(1.2)

On the other hand,
according to the conservation law of mass, there holds

\int_{\Omega}dy=\int_{\Omega_{t}}dX,\quad\Omega_{t}=\{X(t,y)|y\in\Omega\}

for any smooth bounded connected domain
\Omega
. This implies that

\det(\nabla X)\equiv 1.

(1.3)

The above (
1.3
) is equivalent to the incompressible
condition.

There are lots of works on the nonlinear wave equations and elastodynamics in the whole space, see for instance
[
3
,
13
,
38
]
.
However, the research in the thin domain is almost blank.
Hence, it’s very interesting to study
the corresponding problem.
Our main goal in this paper is to establish the global existence result of (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) in the thin domain.

1.1
A review of related results

Let us first review a few highlights from the
existence theory of nonlinear wave equations and elastodynamics from various aspects.
For quasilinear wave equations in dimension three, and for small initial
data, John and Klainerman
[
21
]
obtained the first non-trivial almost global existence result.
The global existence of nonlinear wave and elastic systems in dimension no bigger than three hinges on two basic assumptions: the smallness of the initial data and the null
condition of nonlinearities. The omission of either of these assumptions can lead to the break down of solutions in finite time
[
18
,
19
,
50
]
.
Under the null condition, the fundamental work of global existence
of classical solutions for 3D scalar quasilinear wave equation
was achieved by Klainerman
[
25
]
and by Christodoulou
[
7
]
independently.
The existence question is more delicate in 2-D case due to the
much weaker time decay rate. Even
under the assumption of the null condition, quadratic nonlinearities
have at most critical time decay.
A series of articles considered
the case of cubically nonlinear equations satisfying the null
condition, see for example
[
8
,
12
,
22
,
23
]
. Alinhac

[
2
]
was the first to establish global existence for 2D
scalar wave equation with null bilinear forms, his argument combines
vector fields with
the ghost energy method.

For compressible elastodynamic systems, commonly referred as elastic waves in literature,
John
[
20
]
showed the existence of almost global solutions
for small displacement (see also
[
26
]
).
The breakthrough work of global well-posedness is due to Sideris
[
45
,
46
]
and also Agemi
[
1
]
, under a nonresonance condition which is
physically consistent with the system.
With an
additional repulsive Poisson term, the global existence was established in
[
15
]
which
allows a general form for the pressure.
For the incompressible elastodynamics,
the
global-well-posedness for the 3D case was obtained by Sideris and Thomases in
[
47
,
49
]
. We also refer to
[
48
]
for a unified treatment, and
[
35
]
for some
improvement on the uniform time-independent bound on the highest order energy.
The uniform bound of the highest-order energy of the 2D case was proved recently by Cai
[
4
]
.

The first non-trivial long time existence result was established
by Lei, Sideris and Zhou

[
37
]
, in which the authors proved the almost global
existence for the 2D incompressible isotropic elastodynamics
in Eulerian coordinate.
The global well-posedness was finally solved by Lei
[
32
]

where he found that in Lagrangian coordinate, the incompressible elastodynamics
actually inherently satisfies the strong null condition.
Afterwards, Wang
[
51
]

gave a new proof of this latter result using space-time resonance method and
a normal form transformation.

Now we recall some results on the well-posedness of the thin domain.
In the early 1990s, Hale and Raugel studied reaction diffusion equations and damped wave equations on thin domain, see

[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. Later on, Raugel and Sell
[
43
,
44
]
further studied the existence of strong solutions of the Navier-Stokes

See also
[
16
,
17
,
27
,
28
,
52
]
for further results.
In all these works, a dissipative structure (with viscosity) is a key ingredient to study the long time existence. Consequently, these arguments cannot be applied to study long time solutions for wave type equations in thin domain.

Before ending this subsection, let us mention some related works on

The global well-posedness with near equilibrium states was first

There are mainly three ingredients in proving Theorem

Firstly, we establish a series of anisotropic weighted Sobolev type inequalities using generalized derivative in the thin domain.
Those inequalities are dimensionless in terms of the thickness parameter
\delta
. According to the scaling in

direction in the thin domain, when taking

The definition of generalized energy

Secondly, we can not directly adapt the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality in the thin domain due to the absence of Lorentz invariance.
This is compensated by introducing the anisotropic weighted

.
Thirdly, due to the quasilinearity, in the anisotropic generalized energy estimate, the pressure
gradient brings an extra singular

derivative.
To overcome this difficulty, we introduce an auxiliary anisotropic generalized energy
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}
, which always contains an extra

prompts us to control the pressure with twice traditional derivative (see Section

Our observation is that the inherent cancellation structure gives us some freedom to move one derivative.
Thus the generalized energy estimate can be closed.

Now let us discuss some other strategies related in this paper. Inspired by

for the lower order energy estimate, instead of estimating the unknowns,
we turn to estimate its curl-free part since divergence-free part is quadratic nonlinearity and thus negligible.

supercritical decay in time. Hence we need to use the inherent strong null structure and the ghost weight energy to enhance the temporal decay.

Weighted anisotropic Sobolev inequalities

We first study the weighted anisotropic Sobolev type inequalities in thin domain.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
g(x_{3})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\delta})

with
\mathbb{T}^{\delta}=[0,\delta]
, there holds

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\delta})}\lesssim\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\delta})}+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\delta})},

provided the right hand side is finite.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(x_{3})=g(\delta x_{3})\ \textrm{in}\ [0,1].

By Sobolev imbedding, there holds

\displaystyle\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\lesssim\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}+\|\partial_{3}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\delta})}=\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}+\|\partial_{3}\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}

\displaystyle=\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\delta})}+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\delta})}.

This gives the desired result of this lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For all

provided the right hand side is finite.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x_{v},x_{3})\lesssim\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\|\partial_{v}^{a}\tilde{f}(\cdot,x_{3})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Then combined with Lemma

Next we state a lemma which concerns the decay of solutions away from the light cone.
Before then, we first state a decay lemma in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
There holds

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|\partial_{v}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\ln^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(e+t\big)+\frac{1}{1+t}\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\partial_{v}^{2}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\big).

This lemma comes from
[
32
]
(Lemma 3.1).

Proof.

We first introduce the Littlewood-Paley theory on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
\phi
be a
smooth function supported in
\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}:\frac{3}{4}\leq\tau\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
such that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{-j}\tau)=1.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we set

\Delta_{j}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\phi(2^{-j}|\xi|)\mathcal{F}(f)\big)

and

S_{j}f=\sum_{-\infty<k\leq j-1,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{k}f.

Here
\mathcal{F}
denotes the usual Fourier transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

\mathcal{F}^{-1}
the inverse Fourier transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

By the following well-known Bernstein inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad\|S_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{j}\|S_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

one has

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-N}\|S_{-N}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\sum_{-N\leq j\leq N}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\sum_{j\geq N+1}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\sqrt{2N}\|\partial_{v}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+2^{-N}\|\partial_{v}^{2}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Choosing
N=\frac{\ln(e+t)}{\ln 2}
, one has

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|\partial_{v}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\ln^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(e+t\big)+\frac{1}{1+t}\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\partial_{v}^{2}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\big).

This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

.

Let
t\geq 1
,
f\in H^{4}(\Omega_{\delta})

There holds

\displaystyle t\ln^{-\frac{1}{2}}(e+t)\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}(r\leq t/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|(t-r)\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(t-r)\partial_{3}\partial_{v}\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\|\nabla Z^{a}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|a|\leq 2}\|\nabla^{2}Z^{a}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.4
, there holds

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\ln^{\frac{1}{2}}(e+t)\big(\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{v}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\partial_{v}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})})

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{1+t}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|\partial^{2}_{v}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{1+t}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|\partial_{3}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|\partial_{3}\partial^{2}_{v}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}).

Next let us choose a radial cutoff function

For each fixed

4
Elliptic estimate in thin domain

Next, we study the elliptic estimates in the thin domain.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
p(x)
defined on the
\Omega_{\delta}
satisfying

\displaystyle\Delta p=\partial_{v}f,

with periodic boundary condition. Then
there holds that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{v}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{3}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(x_{v},x_{3})=p(x_{v},\delta x_{3}),\quad\tilde{f}(x_{v},x_{3})=f(x_{v},\delta x_{3})\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\Omega_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}.

Then

\displaystyle\partial_{3}\tilde{p}=\delta\partial_{3}p.

Let
\Delta_{\delta}=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}+\delta^{-2}\partial_{3}^{2}
.
Then

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta}\tilde{p}=\partial_{v}\tilde{f}.

Note that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}=\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},\quad\|\partial_{3}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}=\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Solving pressure gives

\displaystyle\partial_{v}\tilde{p}=\partial_{v}\Delta_{\delta}^{-1}\partial_{v}\tilde{f},\quad\partial_{3}\tilde{p}=\partial_{3}\Delta_{\delta}^{-1}\partial_{v}\tilde{f}.

Note the multipliers are
\frac{\xi_{v}^{2}}{\xi_{v}^{2}+\delta^{-2}\xi_{3}^{2}}

and
\frac{\xi_{v}\xi_{3}}{\xi_{v}^{2}+\delta^{-2}\xi_{3}^{2}}=\delta\frac{\xi_{v}\cdot\delta^{-1}\xi_{3}}{\xi_{v}^{2}+\delta^{-2}\xi_{3}^{2}}
, respectively, where
\xi_{v}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\xi_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
. Hence

\displaystyle\|\partial_{v}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})},\quad\|\partial_{3}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\delta\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}.

This gives that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{v}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}=\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{v}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{3}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}=\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For
p(x)
defined on the
\Omega_{\delta}

\displaystyle\ \Delta p=\partial_{3}g,

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(x_{v},x_{3})=p(x_{v},\delta x_{3}),\quad\tilde{g}(x_{v},x_{3})=g(x_{v},\delta x_{3})\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}.

Then

.
Then

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta}\tilde{p}=\partial_{v}\tilde{f},\quad\Delta_{\delta}\tilde{p}=\delta^{-1}\partial_{3}\tilde{g}.

Note that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}=\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},\quad\|\partial_{3}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}=\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

\displaystyle\|\partial_{v}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})},\quad\|\partial_{3}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\delta\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}.

\displaystyle\|\partial_{3}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}=\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\tilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}=\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

∎

Estimate of the special quantity

In this section, we give a preliminary estimate for the weighted

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

Remark 5.2

On the right hand side of (

Proof.

First of all, using the decomposition for gradient operator (

)

(5.2)

Let us further write

\displaystyle(t^{2}-r^{2})\Delta Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{2}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y-r^{2}(\Delta Y-\partial_{r}^{2}Y)+t^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}Y-r^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{2}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y-r^{2}(\Delta Y-\partial_{r}^{2}Y)

\displaystyle+\ (t\partial_{t}-r\partial_{r})(t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}-1)Y.

Hence, using (
5.2
), one has

\displaystyle|(t-r)\Delta Y|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y|+r|\Delta Y-\partial_{r}^{2}Y|+|\partial SY|+|\partial Y|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\partial ZY|+|\partial Y|+t|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y|.

Next, using (
5.3
), integration by parts and the periodic boundary condition, one immediately
has

\displaystyle\|(t-r)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}=\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(t-r)^{2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}Y\partial_{i}\partial_{j}Ydy

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(t-r)\omega_{i}\partial_{j}Y\partial_{i}\partial_{j}Ydy-\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(t-r)^{2}\partial_{j}Y\Delta\partial_{j}Ydy

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(t-r)\omega_{i}\partial_{j}Y\partial_{i}\partial_{j}Ydy-2\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(t-r)\omega_{j}\partial_{j}Y\Delta Ydy

\displaystyle\quad+\ \int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(t-r)^{2}\Delta Y\Delta Ydy

\displaystyle\leq 8\|\nabla Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|(t-r)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}(t-r)^{2}\Delta Y\Delta Ydy.

Using (
5.3
), one obtains that

\displaystyle\|(t-r)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\lesssim\|\partial Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|\partial ZY\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+t\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(5.4)

To estimate
|(t-r)\partial_{t}\nabla Y|
, let us first write

\displaystyle(t-r)\partial_{t}\partial_{r}Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\partial_{t}-\partial_{r})(t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}-1)Y+t\partial_{t}^{2}Y-r\partial_{r}^{2}Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\partial_{t}-\partial_{r})(t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}-1)Y+t(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y

\displaystyle+\ (t-r)\Delta Y+r(\Delta Y-\partial_{r}^{2}Y).

Then using (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), one has

\displaystyle|(t-r)\partial_{t}\partial_{r}Y|\lesssim|\partial Y|+|\partial ZY|+t|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y|.

(5.5)

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle|(t-r)\partial_{t}\partial_{j}Y|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|(t-r)\partial_{t}\omega_{j}\partial_{r}Y|+|(t-r)r^{-1}\partial_{t}\Omega Y\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(t-r)\omega_{j}\partial_{t}\partial_{r}Y|+\big|r^{-1}(t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r})\Omega Y-\Omega(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})Y\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|(t-r)\partial_{t}\partial_{r}Y|+r^{-1}|\Omega SY|+|\Omega\partial_{t}Y|+|\Omega(\omega\cdot\nabla Y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|(t-r)\partial_{t}\partial_{r}Y|+r^{-1}|\Omega SY|+|\Omega\partial_{t}Y|+\ |\nabla Y|+|\Omega\nabla Y|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\partial Y|+|\partial ZY|+t|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y|,

which gives

\displaystyle\|(t-r)\partial_{t}\partial_{j}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\lesssim\|\partial Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|\partial ZY\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+t\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(5.6)

At last, if
r\leq 2t
, we deduce using (
5.3
) that

\displaystyle|(t-r)\partial_{t}^{2}Y|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big|(t-r)(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y\big|+|(t-r)\Delta Y|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|(t-r)(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y|+|\partial ZY|+|\partial Y|+t|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\partial ZY|+|\partial Y|+|t(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y|,

and if
r>2t
, we deduce that

\displaystyle t|\partial_{t}^{2}Y|\leq t|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y\big|+t|\Delta Y|.

Hence, by (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{r\leq 2t}(t-r)^{2}|\partial_{t}^{2}Y|^{2}dy+\int_{r>2t}t^{2}|\partial_{t}^{2}Y|^{2}dy

(5.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2}\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}+\||\partial Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\||\partial ZY\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\int_{r>2t}t^{2}|\Delta Y|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2}\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}+\|\partial Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|\partial ZY\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Then the lemma follows from (
5.4
), (
5.6
) and
(
5.7
).
∎

6

Now we are going to estimate the
L^{2}
weighted generalized energy

\mathcal{X}_{\kappa}
. First of all, we prove the following lemma
which says that the rescaled
L^{2}
norm of
\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}p
and

(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y
, which involve the

(|\alpha|+2)
-th order derivatives of unknowns, can be bounded by
certain quantities which only involve
(|\alpha|+1)
-th order
derivatives of unknowns. This surprising result is
based on the inherent special structures of nonlinearities in the system.
We refer to
[
32
]
of Lemma 4.1 for the two dimensional isotropic version.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
\|\nabla Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}\ll 1
. There holds

\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\lesssim\Pi(\alpha,2)

where

\displaystyle\Pi(\alpha,2)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ \beta\neq\alpha}\Pi_{1}+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\leq|\gamma|}\Pi_{2}+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|>|\gamma|}\Pi_{3}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha,\ \beta\neq\alpha}\Pi_{4}+\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\alpha|/3}\Pi_{5},

where
\Pi_{1}
,
\Pi_{2}
and
\Pi_{3}
are given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}=\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y||\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}=\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\cdot\big((\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},

\displaystyle\Pi_{3}=\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\cdot\big(\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},

and
\Pi_{4}
,
\Pi_{5}
are given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{4}=\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\cdot|\nabla\Gamma^{\iota}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})},

\displaystyle\Pi_{5}=\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\big\||\partial\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\nabla\Gamma^{\iota}Y|+|\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\partial\nabla\Gamma^{\iota}Y|\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Proof.

We begin by writing (
2.3
) as follows

\displaystyle-\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}p=(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y.

Applying the divergence operator to the above equation and then
applying the operator
\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ \gamma\neq\alpha}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot[C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y]

\displaystyle+\ \nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot[(\nabla Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y]

\displaystyle+\ \nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y.

Using the
L^{2}
boundedness of the Riesz transform in Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
,
one has

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

(6.1)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ \gamma\neq\alpha}\|(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle+\ \delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|(\nabla Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

As we will see later, the first two terms on the right hand side of (
6.1
) will be
treated directly. In what follows, we focus our mind on the calculation of

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(6.2)

Using (
2.4
), we compute that

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\end{matrix}\right|

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}.

Thus (
6.2
) are reduced to the computation of the quadratic nonlinear term

\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}J_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

(6.3)

and the cubic term

\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}J_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(6.4)

We first take care of the quadratic nonlinearity
J_{1}
.
Note the structural identity:

\displaystyle-\big(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\big)=(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}.

J_{1}
can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

(6.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad+(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,|\beta|\leq|\gamma|}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big((\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,|\beta|>|\gamma|}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big(\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\big).

Inserting
(
6.5
)
into (
6.3
), one has

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}J_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Pi(\alpha,2)+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y||\nabla Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Next, let us compute
J_{2}
.

\displaystyle J_{2}=-(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\end{matrix}\right|.

Expanding the determinant, one has

\displaystyle\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}f&\partial_{2}f&\partial_{3}f\\
\partial_{1}g&\partial_{2}g&\partial_{3}g\\
\partial_{1}h&\partial_{2}h&\partial_{3}h\end{matrix}\right|

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}f\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{2}g&\partial_{3}g\\
\partial_{2}h&\partial_{3}h\end{matrix}\right|-\partial_{2}f\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}g&\partial_{3}g\\
\partial_{1}h&\partial_{3}h\end{matrix}\right|+\partial_{3}f\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}g&\partial_{2}g\\
\partial_{1}h&\partial_{2}h\end{matrix}\right|

\displaystyle=\nabla f\cdot\left(\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{2}g&\partial_{3}g\\
\partial_{2}h&\partial_{3}h\end{matrix}\right|,-\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}g&\partial_{3}g\\
\partial_{1}h&\partial_{3}h\end{matrix}\right|,\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}g&\partial_{2}g\\
\partial_{1}h&\partial_{2}h\end{matrix}\right|\right).

In order to simplify the computations, we introduce the three dimensional curl operator on
two dimensional vector maps as follows:

\displaystyle\nabla\times(g,h)=\left(\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{2}g&\partial_{3}g\\
\partial_{2}h&\partial_{3}h\end{matrix}\right|,-\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}g&\partial_{3}g\\
\partial_{1}h&\partial_{3}h\end{matrix}\right|,\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}g&\partial_{2}g\\
\partial_{1}h&\partial_{2}h\end{matrix}\right|\right).

It’s easy to check the following property holds.

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\nabla\times(f,h)=0,

\displaystyle\nabla f\cdot\nabla\times(g,h)=\nabla\cdot\big(f\nabla\times(g,h)\big),

\displaystyle\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}f&\partial_{2}f&\partial_{3}f\\
\partial_{1}g&\partial_{2}g&\partial_{3}g\\
\partial_{1}h&\partial_{2}h&\partial_{3}h\end{matrix}\right|=\nabla f\cdot\nabla\times(g,h)=\nabla g\cdot\nabla\times(h,f)=\nabla h\cdot\nabla\times(f,g).

Now we calculate using the curl operator as follows:

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\end{matrix}\right|

(6.6)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\cdot\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\cdot\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3},\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\cdot\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1},\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2})

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\cdot\partial_{t}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\cdot\partial_{k}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}).

The first three terms on the right hand side of (
6.6
) can be organized as follows:

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

(6.7)

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1},\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}).

The remaining two lines of (
6.6
) are divided into three types:

|\beta|\geq|\alpha|/3
,
|\gamma|\geq|\alpha|/3

and
|\iota|\geq|\alpha|/3
.
Due the symmetry, their treatment is the same. Hence
we only present the details for
|\beta|\geq|\alpha|/3
.

\displaystyle-2\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\alpha|/3}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\cdot\partial_{t}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

(6.8)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\alpha|/3}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\cdot\partial_{k}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

\displaystyle=-2\nabla\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\alpha|/3}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\partial_{t}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

\displaystyle\quad+2\nabla\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\alpha|/3}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\partial_{k}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}).

Inserting (
6.7
) and (
6.8
) into (
6.6
), (
6.4
) will be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}J_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}\big\||(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\cdot|\nabla\Gamma^{\iota}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\alpha|/3}\|\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\big\||\partial\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\cdot|\nabla\Gamma^{\iota}Y|+|\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\cdot|\partial\nabla\Gamma^{\iota}Y|\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y|\cdot|\nabla Y|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\Pi(\alpha,2).

Collecting all the above calculation, (

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\Pi(\alpha,2)+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y||\nabla Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

(6.9)

\displaystyle+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y|\cdot|\nabla Y|^{2}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Combined with (
2.3
) yields

7
Second derivative estimate of pressure

This section is devoted to the estimate of

.
We remark that a direct estimate of
\|\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

is insufficient to close the energy estimate due to the appearance of

.
Instead, we are going to estimate

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\kappa\geq 15
. There exists
0<\eta\ll 1
such that
if
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}\leq\eta
.
Then for
|\alpha|\leq\kappa-5
, there holds

\delta^{(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\delta^{(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa-5}\Phi(\alpha,2),

where

\displaystyle\Phi(\alpha,2)=

\displaystyle\delta^{(h-\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle+\delta^{(h-\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{k}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\omega_{k}\partial_{t}+\partial_{k})\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle+\delta^{(h-\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}\big\||\nabla\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\partial\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y,\Gamma^{\iota}Y)|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Proof.

Applying the divergence operator to (
2.3
) and then
applying the operator
\nabla^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\nabla^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot[C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y]

\displaystyle+\ \nabla^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y.

Using the
L^{2}
boundedness of the Riesz transform in thin domain in Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
,
one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

(7.1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\|\nabla\big[(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\big]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\|\nabla\cdot(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\nabla\cdot(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

It remains to compute the last line of (
7.1
).
Using (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y

(7.2)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\end{matrix}\right|.

We first compute the first line on the right hand side of (
7.2
).
Note the structural identity

\displaystyle-\big(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\big)=(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}.

We write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

(7.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}.

The last two lines of (
7.3
) are organized as follows to show the strong null condition.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{t}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{k}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{k}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{k}(\omega_{k}\partial_{t}+\partial_{k}))(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{t}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}

\displaystyle\quad-\partial_{k}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(\omega_{k}\partial_{t}+\partial_{k})(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}.

Consequently,
we have

\displaystyle\|\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\cdot(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

(7.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\omega_{k}\partial_{t}+\partial_{k})\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Next, we compute the last line of (
7.2
).
Using the curl operator introduced previously, we have

\displaystyle-(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\end{matrix}\right|

(7.5)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\cdot\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\cdot\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3},\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\cdot\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1},\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2})

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\cdot\partial_{t}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\nabla\partial_{k}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\cdot\partial_{k}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}).

Taking the
L^{2}
norm (

), we deduce that

\displaystyle\Big\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\end{matrix}\right|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

(7.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}\big\||\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y,\Gamma^{\iota}Y)|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}\big\||\nabla\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\partial\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y,\Gamma^{\iota}Y)|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Inserting (
7.4
) and (
7.6
) into (
7.1
),
we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\nabla\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||\nabla(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y,\Gamma^{\iota}Y)|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

\kappa\geq 15
,

8
Higher-order energy estimate

This section is devoted to the estimate of the anisotropic higher-order generalized energy
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}
.
The ghost weight method introduced by
Alinhac in
[
2
]
plays an important role.

Let
\kappa\geq 15
,
|\alpha|\leq\kappa-1
,
\sigma=t-r
and

. Taking the

of (
2.3

\displaystyle\quad-\ 2\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}e^{-q(\sigma)}\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\cdot\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}pdy

\displaystyle\quad-\ 2\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}e^{-q(\sigma)}\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\cdot\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Ydy,

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\|\nabla\Gamma^{\alpha}p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\|(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\Big).

Thus we are left to deal with the the last line of (
8.1
).
We will perform the estimate in two cases:
t\leq 1
and
t\geq 1
.
In the first case, we should be careful of the double derivative
\partial_{3}^{2}
.
In the second case, we need to earn enough decay in time.

Case of

\kappa\geq 15
,
|\alpha|\leq\kappa-1
. By Lemma

6.5
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}\sim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa-5}\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\partial_{3}\partial(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

Proof.

We begin by the following div-curl identity.

\displaystyle\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\partial_{3}\partial\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}

(9.4)

\displaystyle=\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\partial_{3}\partial(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\cdot\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}+\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\partial_{3}\partial(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}.

Now let us calculate
\partial_{3}\partial\nabla\cdot\Gamma^{\alpha}Y

in (
9.4
).
By (
2.4
), one has

\displaystyle\partial_{3}\partial\nabla\cdot\Gamma^{\alpha}Y=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}\partial_{3}\partial\big(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}C_{\alpha-\beta}^{\gamma}\partial_{3}\partial\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\end{matrix}\right|.

Note that

\displaystyle-\partial_{3}\partial\big(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}-\partial_{i}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\big)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\cdot\big(\partial_{3}\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\big)+(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\cdot\big(\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\cdot\big(\partial_{3}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\big)+(-\partial_{j},\partial_{i})\cdot\big((\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{i}\partial_{3}\partial\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{j}\big)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{3}\partial\left|\begin{matrix}\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\\
\partial_{1}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{2}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}&\partial_{3}\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\end{matrix}\right|

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\big(\partial_{3}\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})\big)+\nabla\cdot\big(\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1}\partial_{3}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla\cdot\big(\partial_{3}\partial\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})\big)-\nabla\cdot\big(\partial\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2}\partial_{3}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1},\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3})\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla\cdot\big(\partial_{3}\partial\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1},\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2})\big)+\nabla\cdot\big(\partial\Gamma^{\iota}Y^{3}\partial_{3}\nabla\times(\Gamma^{\beta}Y^{1},\Gamma^{\gamma}Y^{2})\big).

Consequently, by Lemma
3.2
, one has

\displaystyle\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\partial_{3}\partial(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\cdot\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\|\partial_{3}\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y\partial\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}+\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\sum_{\beta+\gamma+\iota=\alpha}\|\partial_{3}\partial\Gamma^{\beta}Y\partial\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\partial\Gamma^{\iota}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\kappa-3}.

Inserting the above inequality into (
9.4
)
and summing over
|\alpha|\leq\kappa-5
for
\kappa\geq 15
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}\lesssim\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\kappa-3})+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa-5}\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\partial_{3}\partial(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}.

Note the assumption that
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-5}\leq\eta\ll 1

yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa-5}\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\partial_{3}\partial(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, the reverse bound also holds.
Thus the lemma is proved.
∎

Now let us perform the scaled energy estimate for
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}
.
Let
\kappa\geq 15
and
|\alpha|\leq\kappa-5
.
Applying
\partial_{3}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times
onto (
2.3
) and then
taking the
L^{2}
inner product
of the resulting equations with
\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\partial_{3}\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y
. Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\big(|\partial_{3}\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y|^{2}+|\partial_{3}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y|^{2}\big)dy

\displaystyle=-\ 2\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\int_{\Omega_{\delta}}\partial_{3}\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\cdot\partial_{3}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Ydy

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\|\partial_{3}\big[(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\big]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(9.5)

Now we focus our mind on

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\|\partial_{3}\big[(\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y)^{\top}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\big]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\partial_{3}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

We will perform the estimate in two cases:
t\leq 1
and
t\geq 1
.

We first take care of the case
t\leq 1
. By Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
7.2
, there holds

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

(9.6)

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\leq|\gamma|}\|\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\gamma|}\|\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{2}}\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}})(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}).

Similarly, by Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
7.2
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\partial_{3}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}).

Next we consider the case for
t\geq 1
. We first deal with

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

(9.7)

For (
9.7
), if
|\beta|\leq|\gamma|
, by Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
6.4
, one has

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\leq|\gamma|}\big\||\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\leq|\gamma|}\delta^{l}\|\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}\delta^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|\beta|\leq[\alpha/2]}\delta^{l}\|\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}\cdot t^{-1}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}).

By Lemma
3.5
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{|\beta|\leq[\alpha/2]}\delta^{l}\|\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(r\leq t/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{X}_{[\alpha/2]+5}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{E}_{[\alpha/2]+5}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{[\alpha/2]+5}^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}).

On the other hand,
if
r\geq t/2
, since
r\leq r_{v}+1
,
t\geq 1
,
\delta\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle t/2\leq r\leq r_{v}+1.

By Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{|\beta|\leq[\alpha/2]}\delta^{l}\|\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(r\geq t/2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+r_{v})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{[\alpha/2]+5}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{[\alpha/2]+5}^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}).

For (
9.7
),
if
|\beta|\geq|\gamma|
, by Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
6.4
, (
9.6
)
is bounded by

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\gamma|}\big\||\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\gamma|}\delta^{l-\frac{1}{2}}\|(1-\varphi^{t})\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\delta^{m}\|(1-\varphi^{t})(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\gamma|}\delta^{l-\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi^{t}\partial_{3}\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}\delta^{m}\|\varphi^{t}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-2}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3})+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha,\ |\beta|\geq|\gamma|}(\mathcal{E}_{|\beta|+2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{|\beta|+2}^{\frac{1}{2}})\delta^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi^{t}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-2}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3})+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}})\sum_{|\gamma|\leq[\alpha/2]+3}\delta^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi^{t}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t^{-2}+t^{-\frac{3}{2}})(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}).

Next, we take care of

\displaystyle\delta^{h-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\big\||\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y|\cdot|\partial_{3}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}\delta^{l}\|\nabla\Gamma^{\beta}Y\|_{L^{\infty}}\delta^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}(\mathcal{E}_{|\beta|+4}+\mathcal{X}_{|\beta|+4})^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)\Gamma^{\gamma}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa-5}\delta^{2(h-\frac{1}{2})}\|\partial_{3}\partial(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\times\Gamma^{\alpha}Y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta})}^{2}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{\kappa-3}^{\frac{1}{2}}).

(9.9)

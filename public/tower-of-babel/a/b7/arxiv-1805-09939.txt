Introduction

•
Let
x=(x_{n})_{n}
be a bounded complex sequence and let

M_{x}=\displaystyle\sup_{n}\left|x_{n}\right|.

For all integer
n
and scalar
\lambda
, with
\left|\lambda\right|\leq M_{x}
, we have
\left|x_{n}+\lambda\right|\leq\left|x_{n}\right|+\left|\lambda\right|\leq 2M_{x}
. It results that

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C},~\left|\lambda\right|\leq M_{x}}\sup_{n}\left|x_{n}+\lambda\right|\leq 2M_{x}.

(1)

However, the equality

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C},~\left|\lambda\right|\leq M_{x}}\sup_{n}\left|x_{n}+\lambda\right|=2M_{x}

(2)

is less obvious.

In this paper, we prove the equality (
2
) by using a normaloid operator related to the sequence
x=(x_{n})_{n}
. For this purpose, we need some notations and results from the literature.

Let
\mathcal{B(H)}
be the algebra of all bounded linear operators acting on a complex Hilbert space (
\mathcal{H},\langle.,.\rangle
). For
A\in\mathcal{B(H)}
, the numerical range of
A
is denoted and defined by

\mathit{W}(A)=\{\langle Ax,x\rangle:x\in\mathcal{H},\ \left\|x\right\|=1\}.

It is a celebrated result due to Toeplitz and Hausdorff that
\mathit{W}(A)
is a nonempty bounded convex subset of the complex plane (not necessarily closed), and its supremum modulus, denoted and given by

w(A)=\sup\{|\lambda|:~\lambda\in\mathit{W}(A)\},

is called the numerical radius of
A
.
It is well known that
w(.)
defines a norm on
\mathcal{B(H)}
, which is equivalent to the
C^{*}
-norm
\left\|.\right\|
. In fact, the following inequalities are well-known:

\frac{1}{2}\left\|A\right\|\leq w(A)\leq\left\|A\right\|,

for all
A\in\mathcal{B(H)}
, and an operator
A\in\mathcal{B(H)}
is said to be normaloid if the second inequality becomes an equality, i.e.,
w(A)=\left\|A\right\|
.

For more material about the numerical radius and other information on the basic theory of algebraic numerical range and normaloid operators, we refer the reader to the books
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

From now on,
\mathcal{B(H)}
will denote the algebra of all bounded linear operators acting on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. We shall denote the complex numbers by
\mathbb{C}
.

2
Main results

Let us start with a definition and a lemma that we will use for giving two characterizations of a normaloid operator. These characterizations will allow us to prove the equality (
2
).

Definition 2.1

Let
\Delta
be a nonempty bounded subset of
\mathbb{C}
. The supremum modulus of
\Delta
is denoted by
\left|\Delta\right|
and given by

\left|\Delta\right|=\sup\{|\lambda|:~\lambda\in\Delta\}.

In particular, if
A\in\mathcal{B(H)}
, then
\left|\mathit{W}(A)\right|=\left|\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}\right|=w(A)
, where
\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}
is the closure of
\mathit{W}(A)
.

Lemma 2.2

Let
\{0\}\neq K
be a nonempty compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
. Then there exists
s\in K
,
s\neq 0
such that

\left|K+s\right|=\left|K\right|+\left|s\right|.

By a compactness argument, there exists
s\in K
,
s\neq 0
such that
\left|K\right|=\left|s\right|
. So,
\left|K+s\right|\leq\left|K\right|+\left|s\right|=2\left|s\right|
. But
2s\in K+s
, then
2\left|s\right|\leq\left|K+s\right|
and therefore
\left|K+s\right|=\left|K\right|+\left|s\right|
.

Theorem 2.3

Let
A\in\mathcal{B(H)}
. Then the following are equivalent statements

1.

A
is normaloid,

2.

\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=\left\|A\right\|+\left|\lambda\right|
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Assume that
A
is normaloid; that is
\left|\mathit{W}(A)\right|=w(A)=\left\|A\right\|
. Since
\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
, by Lemma
2.2
, there exists
\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}
(
\left|\lambda\right|=w(A)
) satisfying

\left|\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}+\lambda\right|=\left|\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}\right|+\left|\lambda\right|=\left\|A\right\|+\left|\lambda\right|.

But
\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}+\lambda=\overline{W(A+\lambda)}
, then
\left|\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}+\lambda\right|=\left|\overline{W(A+\lambda)}\right|=w(A+\lambda)\leq\left\|A+\lambda\right\|
. It results that
\left\|A\right\|+\left|\lambda\right|\leq\left\|A+\lambda\right\|
and so,
\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=\left\|A\right\|+\left|\lambda\right|
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Let
\lambda
be a nonzero scalar such that
\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=\left\|A\right\|+\left|\lambda\right|
. By Barraa-Boumazgour
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
we have

\left|\lambda\right|\left\|A\right\|\in\overline{W(\overline{\lambda}A)}=\overline{\lambda}~\overline{W(A)}.

It results that
\left\|A\right\|\leq w(A)
and hence
\left\|A\right\|=w(A)
. This is exactly to say that
A
is normaloid.

Corollary 2.4

Let
A\in\mathcal{B(H)}
. Then
A
is normaloid if and only if

\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}}\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=2\left\|A\right\|.

Proof.

Note first that, since
\left|\lambda\right|\leq\left\|A\right\|
for any
\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}
, we always have

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}}\left\|A+\lambda\right\|\leq\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}}(\left\|A\right\|+\left|\lambda\right|)\leq 2\left\|A\right\|.

If
A
is normaloid, then Theorem
2.3
states that there exists
\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}
,
\left|\lambda\right|=w(A)=\left\|A\right\|
and
\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=\left\|A\right\|+\left|\lambda\right|=2\left\|A\right\|
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}}\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=2\left\|A\right\|.

Conversely, since
\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}
is compact, there exists
\mu\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}
such that

\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}}(\left\|A\right\|+\left|\lambda\right|)=\left\|A\right\|+\left|\mu\right|.

If
A
is not normaloid, then
\left|\mu\right|<\left\|A\right\|
and we obtain

\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}}\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=\left\|A\right\|+\left|\mu\right|<2\left\|A\right\|.

Remark 2.5

If the operator
A
is normaloid, the supermum
2\left\|A\right\|
is attained by "some" scalars
\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}
with
\left|\lambda\right|=\left\|A\right\|
. Then, we can write

\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)}}\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A)},~\left|\lambda\right|\leq\left\|A\right\|}\left\|A+\lambda\right\|=2\left\|A\right\|.

(3)

Now, we are ready to prove the following theorem wich establishes the desired equality (
2
).

Theorem 2.6

Let
x=(x_{n})_{n}
be a bounded complex sequence. Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C},~\left|\lambda\right|\leq M_{x}}\sup_{n}\left|x_{n}+\lambda\right|=2M_{x},

where
M_{x}=\displaystyle\sup_{n}\left|x_{n}\right|.

Proof.

Let
A_{x}
be the diagonal operator defined by

A_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&0&\dots&&\\
0&x_{2}&&&&\\
\vdots&&x_{3}&&&\\
&&&&&\\
&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&\end{pmatrix}.

It is clear that
A_{x}
is normaloid, indeed
w(A_{x})=\left\|A_{x}\right\|=M_{x}
. Accoroding to the equality (
3
), we get

\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A_{x})},~\left|\lambda\right|\leq\left\|A_{x}\right\|}\left\|A_{x}+\lambda\right\|=2\left\|A_{x}\right\|.

Since
\left\|A_{x}+\lambda\right\|=\displaystyle\sup_{n}\left|x_{n}+\lambda\right|
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\left\|A_{x}\right\|=M_{x}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in\overline{\mathit{W}(A_{x})},~\left|\lambda\right|\leq M_{x}}\sup_{n}\left|x_{n}+\lambda\right|=2M_{x}.

It suffices to use the inequality (
1
) and the fact that
\overline{\mathit{W}(A_{x})}
is a subset of
\mathbb{C}
to conclude that

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C},~\left|\lambda\right|\leq M_{x}}\sup_{n}\left|x_{n}+\lambda\right|=2M_{x}.

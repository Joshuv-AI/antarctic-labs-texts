Exploring the impact of magnetic fields, Lorentz violation and EDM on e + ​ e − → l + ​ l − \displaystyle e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow l^{+}l^{-} scattering
D. S. Cabral, A. F. Santos

Abstract
This paper explores the annihilation process of an electron-positron pair into a heavier lepton-antilepton pair, taking into account the presence of an external classical magnetic field. Additionally, it investigates corrections arising from the breakdown of Lorentz symmetries and the existence of Electric Dipole Moments (EDM) for leptons. Graphics are constructed to illustrate the influence of EDM and Lorentz violation on the cross section. Strong magnetic field limit is analyzed. Furthermore, the investigation utilizes experimental data on EDM values to explore the upper limits of the Lorentz violation parameters.

II
Electromagnetic form factors and EDM

In this section, we derive the electromagnetic form factors by utilizing the Dirac current. It is known that the Dirac 4-current
\displaystyle J^{\mu}
must satisfy the continuity equation

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=\partial_{0}\rho+\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}=0.

(1)

By introducing the change
\displaystyle\vec{J}\to\vec{J}+\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{\mathcal{J}}
, this new current can be expressed in a form that preserves the continuity equation. In the covariant form, the current becomes
\displaystyle J^{\mu}\to\Theta^{\mu}=\left(\rho,\vec{J}+\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{\mathcal{J}}\right)
. In terms of the Dirac field
\displaystyle\psi
we have
[
29
]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\vec{J}\to\vec{J}+\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{\mathcal{J}}=-\frac{i}{2m}\left[\bar{\psi}\left(\vec{\nabla}\psi\right)-\left(\vec{\nabla}\bar{\psi}\right)\psi\right]+\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{\mathcal{J}}

(2)

which implies that the modified current
\displaystyle\Theta^{\mu}(x)
can be a combination of a certain set of gamma matrices.

To proceed, our task is to generate a new current immediately after encountering
\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{J}}
, achieved by combining Dirac
\displaystyle 4\times 4
matrices. Our aim is to investigate the behavior of the quantity
\displaystyle\bra{p}\Theta^{\mu}(x)\ket{p^\prime}=e^{-i(p^{\prime}-p)x}\bra{p}\Theta^{\mu}(0)\ket{p^\prime}
. Furthermore, these combinations of gamma matrices must adhere to specific conditions
[
13
]
, i.e., (i) Lorentz covariance:
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mu}(x)
transforms as a 4-vector under Lorentz transformations. (ii) Hermiticity:
\displaystyle\Theta_{\mu}^{\dagger}(x)=\Theta_{\mu}(x)
behaves like a Hermitian operator. (iii) Gauge invariance:
\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\Theta^{\mu}(x)=0
, charge needs to be conserved. These requirements can be translated into momentum space, that is,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bra{p}\Theta^{\mu}(x)\ket{p^\prime}=e^{-i(p^{\prime}-p)x}\bar{u}(p)O^{\mu}(q)u(p^{\prime}),

(3)

where
\displaystyle q^{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}-p^{\mu}
is the momentum transfer. The characteristics of Lorentz covariance, hermiticity and gauge invariance can be rewritten to the momentum-space operator
\displaystyle O^{\mu}(q)
. Then, we can propose some candidates for that set of matrices. The first options are
\displaystyle\{\mathbb{I},\gamma^{5},\sigma_{\mu\nu}\}
, along with combinations involving
\displaystyle\{p^{\mu},p^{\prime\mu}\}
, and those formed by the matrices
\displaystyle\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{5}\}
in conjunction with the Levi-Civita tensor
\displaystyle\{\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\}
.

Using some gamma matrix identities and the Gordon decomposition, the following contributions can be written

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{u}(p)O_{\mu}(q)u(p^{\prime})=\bar{u}(p)\left\{F_{1}(q^{2})\gamma_{\mu}+i\frac{F_{2}(q^{2})}{2m}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}+\frac{F_{3}(q^{2})}{2m}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}\gamma^{5}\right\}u(p^{\prime}).

(4)

These form factors are defined, at zero recoil
\displaystyle q^{2}=0
, as

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{1}(0)=\text{Charge};\quad\quad\frac{1}{2m}\left[F_{1}(0)+F_{2}(0)\right]=\text{Magnetic moment};\quad\quad-\frac{1}{2m}F_{3}(0)=\text{EDM}.

(5)

This definition can be derived from an analysis of the non-relativistic limit of an interaction Hamiltonian describing a particle in an electromagnetic field, as demonstrated by
[
13
]
.

The charge form factor is a well-established term in QED theory. It becomes evident when coupling Eq. (

\displaystyle\bar{u}(p)O_{\mu}(q)A^{\mu}u(p^{\prime})=\bar{u}(p)F_{1}(q^{2})\gamma_{\mu}A^{\mu}u(p^{\prime})
, which implies an electric charge of
\displaystyle F_{1}(q^{2})=-e

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{3}(0)=\frac{275\alpha}{18\pi}\frac{p^{\mu}d_{\mu\nu}^{(S)}p^{\nu}}{m^{2}},

III
The Dirac field

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\bar{\psi}\left[i\left(\gamma^{\mu}+c^{\nu\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+d^{\nu\mu}\gamma^{5}\gamma_{\nu}+e^{\mu}+if^{\mu}\gamma^{5}+\frac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\nu\mu}\sigma_{\lambda\nu}\right)\mathcal{D}_{\mu}-m\right]\psi,

\displaystyle d^{\nu\mu}

\displaystyle c_{\mu\nu}
sector, by a field redefinition
[

]

\displaystyle d^{\nu\mu}
for our purposes. Then the Lagrangian becomes

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\left[i\left(\gamma^{\mu}+d^{\nu\mu}\gamma^{5}\gamma_{\nu}\right)\mathcal{D}_{\mu}-m\right]\psi

(8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\,i\left[\left(\partial_{\mu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}\partial^{\nu}\right)-ie\left(A_{\mu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}A^{\nu}\right)\right]\gamma^{\mu}\psi-m\bar{\psi}\psi.

Here,

with
\displaystyle q=-e
being the electron charge and
\displaystyle A_{\mu}
representing the gauge field. This equation implies a ”new” free Dirac equation that can couple to the photonic field in two different vertices, namely
\displaystyle ie\gamma_{\mu}
and
\displaystyle d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\nu}

If we want to study a problem with an electromagnetic external field in addition to those describing the interacting photons, we need to express
\displaystyle A^{\mu}=A_{D}^{\mu}+A_{B}^{\mu}

\displaystyle A_{D}^{\mu}
and
\displaystyle A_{B}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\displaystyle i(g_{\mu\nu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5})\gamma^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\psi+e(g_{\mu\nu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5})\gamma^{\mu}A^{\nu}_{B}\psi-m\psi=0,

IV
The scattering

In this section, the main objective is to calculate the cross section for the
\displaystyle e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow l^{+}l^{-}
scattering and analyze the corrections to this quantity due to Lorentz violation, EDM, and the presence of an external magnetic field. Returning to the initial Lagrangian, Eq. (
8
),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\bar{\psi}i\left[\left(\partial_{\mu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}\partial^{\nu}\right)-ie\left(A_{\mu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}A^{\nu}\right)\right]\gamma^{\mu}\psi-m\bar{\psi}\psi,

(54)

we can write the interaction Lagrangian as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=e\bar{\psi}\left(A_{\mu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}A^{\nu}\right)\gamma^{\mu}\psi.

(55)

From Eq. (
55
) we can extract an interacting current
\displaystyle j^{\mu}
by analogy with the coupling term
\displaystyle j_{\mu}A^{\mu}
. However, to take lepton EDM into account in the problem, it is necessary to return at the form factors given by Eq. (
4
) and pay a special attention to that which represents this contribution, i. e., the term
\displaystyle\frac{F_{3}(q^{2})}{2m}\sigma^{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}\gamma^{5}
. By momentum conservation, it is simple to see that the zero recoil of momentum transfer condition
\displaystyle q^{2}=0
is already satisfied. Therefore, it is necessary to make a change in the coupling current, and the interaction lagrangian (
55
) becomes

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\displaystyle e\bar{\psi}\left[\left(A_{\mu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}A^{\nu}\right)\gamma^{\mu}+\frac{F_{3}(0)}{2m}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}A^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\right]\psi

(56)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle e\bar{\psi}\left(A_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}+d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}A^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}-\varrho_{l}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}A^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\right)\psi,

where
\displaystyle\varrho_{l}
is the lepton EDM, as shown in Eq. (
5
).

Notice that we are dealing with an extension of the usual QED beyond the Standard Model. That is, a theory involving electrons, positrons, and photons subject to a very strong external field and the effects of a nature that violates Lorentz symmetry through the coefficient
\displaystyle d_{33}
. In this new theory, there are three types of vertices, as mentioned before. The diagrams of these vertices are represented in Figure
1
.

Three types of interaction vertices are represented: (a) the usual QED, (b) the LV-contribution, and (c) the lepton EDM consideration.

The classical external field is present in the problem through the leptonic field operator. In a more general way it is given by
[
41
]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\psi=\int\frac{dp_{x}dp_{z}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n,s}N_{n}\left[a_{s}u_{s}e^{-ip\cdot r_{\cancel{y}}}+b_{s}^{\dagger}v_{s}e^{ip\cdot r_{\cancel{y}}}\right].

(57)

In the present case, it reduces to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\psi=\int\frac{dp_{z}}{2\pi}\left[N_{u}(p_{z})a(p_{z})u(y,p_{z})e^{-ip\cdot r_{\cancel{y}}}+N_{v}(p_{z})b^{\dagger}(p_{z})v(y,p_{z})e^{ip\cdot r_{\cancel{y}}}\right].

(58)

Taking a scattering process of the type
\displaystyle e^{+}e^{-}\to l^{+}l^{-}
when only the final leptons contribution of the EDM is considered, the diagrams that describe this process are given in the Figure
2
.

Figure 2:
Representation of scattering processes: (a) Represents the usual QED scattering. (b) and (c) display LV-processes. (d) Represents the contribution from the final lepton EDM.

It is important to note that, typically, for an interaction Lagrangian with three terms, the scattering would require nine diagram contributions: the usual QED, three LV-corrections (two of first order and one of second), three EDM-corrections (one for each vertex separately and one for both vertices simultaneously), and finally, two LV-EDM contributions. However, since the EDM depends directly on the
\displaystyle d_{\mu\nu}
term and we are considering contributions only up to the lowest possible order, the number of diagrams drops to five. If we consider only the EDM of the final lepton, we need to study only the graphs presented in Figure
2
. The reason for discarding the electronic contribution of the dipole moment is that, as mentioned before, this quantity is much lower compared to the heavier leptons, especially at high energies.

The second order element of the scattering operator is given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{S}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(-i)^{2}}{2}\int d^{4}r_{1}d^{4}r_{2}\mathcal{T}\left[\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}(r_{1})\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}(r_{2})\right]

(59)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\int d^{4}r_{1}d^{4}r_{2}\mathcal{T}\left[\bar{\psi}_{1}A_{1\beta}\gamma^{\beta}\psi_{1}\bar{\psi}_{2}A_{2\beta}\gamma^{\beta}\psi_{2}\right]-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\int d^{4}r_{1}d^{4}r_{2}\mathcal{T}\left[\bar{\psi}_{1}A_{1\beta}\gamma^{\beta}\psi_{1}\bar{\psi}_{2}d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}A_{2}^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\int d^{4}r_{1}d^{4}r_{2}\mathcal{T}\left[\bar{\psi}_{1}d_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}A_{1}^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{1}\bar{\psi}_{2}A_{2\beta}\gamma^{\beta}\psi_{2}\right]+\frac{e^{2}}{2}\int d^{4}r_{1}d^{4}r_{2}\mathcal{T}\left[\bar{\psi}_{1}A_{1\beta}\gamma^{\beta}\psi_{1}\bar{\psi}_{2}\varrho_{l}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}A_{2}^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{a}+\mathcal{M}_{b}+\mathcal{M}_{c}+\mathcal{M}_{d}

with
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}
being the time order operator.

Assuming that the initial and final states are given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ket{i}=a^{\dagger}(p_{z})b^{\dagger}(k_{z})\ket{0};\quad\quad\ket{f}=a^{\dagger}(p^{\prime}_{z})b^{\dagger}(k^{\prime}_{z})\ket{0},

(60)

the scattering amplitude

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{a}

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\int d^{4}r_{1}d^{4}r_{2}\bra{f}\mathcal{T}\left[\bar{\psi}_{1}A_{1\beta}\gamma^{\beta}\psi_{1}\bar{\psi}_{2}A_{2\beta}\gamma^{\beta}\psi_{2}\right]\ket{i}

(61)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-ie^{2}\int dy\left[\bar{v}(k_{z})\gamma_{\mu}u(p_{z})\right]\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{s}\left[\bar{u}(p^{\prime}_{z})\gamma_{\nu}v(k^{\prime}_{z})\right].

Here the photon propagator definition, i.e.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bra{0}\mathcal{T}\left[A_{1}^{\nu}A_{2}^{\beta}\right]\ket{0}=i\int\frac{d^{4}\kappa}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-i(r_{2}-r_{1})\kappa}\frac{g^{\nu\beta}}{\kappa^{2}},

(62)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{a(p),a^{\dagger}(k)\right\}=\frac{(2\pi)}{N_{u}}\delta(p_{z}-k_{z}),\quad\quad\left\{b(p),b^{\dagger}(k)\right\}=\frac{(2\pi)}{N_{v}}\delta(p_{z}-k_{z})

have been used. The delta function
\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\cancel{y}}^{4}(p_{2}+k_{2}-p_{1}-k_{1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{b}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

(64)

(65)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{d}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle ie^{2}\int dy\left[\bar{v}(k_{z})\gamma_{\beta}u(p_{z})\right]\frac{g^{\beta\mu}}{s}\left[\bar{u}(p^{\prime}_{z})\varrho_{l}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}\gamma^{5}v(k^{\prime}_{z})\right].

Furthermore, we consider a frame in which the momenta become

\displaystyle\displaystyle p=(E_{i}(\vec{p}_{i}),\vec{p}_{i});\quad\quad k=(E_{i}(-\vec{p}_{i}),-\vec{p}_{i}).\quad\quad p^{\prime}=(E_{f}(\vec{p}_{f}),\vec{p}_{f});\quad\quad k^{\prime}=(E_{f}(-\vec{p}_{f}),-\vec{p}_{f}).

(68)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 4ie^{2}\sqrt{\frac{eB}{2\pi}}\frac{1+2\left[d_{33}^{(e)}+d_{33}^{(l)}\right]}{s}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon(p_{i}+p_{f})+p_{i}p_{f}}{m_{e}m_{l}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{16e^{4}}{s^{2}m_{e}^{2}m_{l}^{2}}\frac{eB}{2\pi}\biggl\{\biggl[1+2\bigg(d_{33}^{(e)}+d_{33}^{(l)}\bigg)\biggr]^{2}(\varepsilon-p_{f})^{2}(\varepsilon-p_{i})^{2}+2\biggl(d_{33}^{(e)^{3}}+d_{33}^{(l)^{3}}\biggr)(\varepsilon-p_{f})(\varepsilon-p_{i})p_{i}p_{f}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

where was used
\displaystyle E_{i/f}=\varepsilon_{i/f}-d^{(i/f)}_{33}p_{i/f}
, but by the momentum conservation
\displaystyle p_{1}+k_{1}=p_{2}+k_{2}\to\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}\equiv\varepsilon

[

]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^{2}}{8p_{f}s\sqrt{s-4m_{e}^{2}}}.

(75)

Therefore, using Eq. (
74
) the cross section becomes

V
Conclusions

The annihilation process of an electron-positron pair decaying into a heavier lepton-antilepton pair was examined. The formalism adopted began with a Lorentz-violating Dirac field in the presence of an external classical magnetic field oriented in the
\displaystyle z
-direction, using the chiral representation. The breakdown of Lorentz symmetries was introduced into the field via the term
\displaystyle d_{33}
and an additional vertex in the scattering diagrams. The EDM of the final leptons, calculated from quantum corrections, is enhanced depending on the LV-coefficient and energy, manifesting as an additional vertex in the scattering process. The new spinor form is a function of energy, momenta, and a discrete index
\displaystyle n
used for energy quantization, with a dependence on the spatial coordinate
\displaystyle y
through a new function
\displaystyle I_{n}
. The limit of stronger fields is approached by setting
\displaystyle n=0
. Furthermore, the energy arises from a new dispersion relation that splits the energy of spin-up and spin-down solutions for particles and antiparticles by
\displaystyle\pm d_{33}p_{z}
.

Experimental data on the upper limits of EDM values, along with the expression for EDM enhancement, are utilized to establish upper limits of the LV-parameter
\displaystyle d_{33}
for both the muonic and tauonic cases. These values are then compared with an extensive list of top limits on LV-parameters. While there are numerous results available for the electronic sector, data for the heavier leptons are limited. Therefore, this study provides an estimation of the
\displaystyle d_{33}
value for all leptons.

Furthermore, the cross-section was calculated and plotted for
\displaystyle e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
\displaystyle e^{+}e^{-}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
scatterings. In our calculations, at high energies around
\displaystyle\sqrt{s}\sim 1\text{TeV}
, the cross-section
\displaystyle\sigma
is predominantly influenced by the EDM contribution for the muon and by the LV contribution for the tau. In both cases, these contributions lead to significant corrections to the total cross-section, deviating from the expectations of conventional QED theory in the absence of these factors. Moreover, the presence of an external magnetic field, a natural component in astrophysical processes of this nature, serves to enhance the cross-section values and accentuate these additional effects compared to QED processes with
\displaystyle\vec{B}=0
.

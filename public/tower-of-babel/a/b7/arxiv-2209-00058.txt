The Fundamental Solution of an Elliptic Equation with Singular Drift
Vladimir Maz’ya, Robert McOwen

Abstract
. For n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we study the existence and asymptotic properties of the fundamental solution for elliptic operators in nondivergence form, ℒ ( x , ∂ x ) = a i ​ j ( x ) ∂ i ∂ j + b k ( x ) ∂ k {\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})=a_{ij}(x)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}+b_{k}(x)\partial_{k} , where the a i ​ j a_{ij} have modulus of continuity ω ⁡ ( r ) \omega(r) satisfying the square-Dini condition and the b k b_{k} are allowed mild singularities of order r − 1 ​ ω ​ ( r ) r^{-1}\omega(r) . A singular integral is introduced that controls the existence of the fundamental solution. We give examples that show the singular drift b k ∂ k b_{k}\partial_{k} may act as a perturbation that does not dramatically change the fundamental solution of ℒ o = a i ​ j ∂ i ∂ j {\mathcal{L}}^{o}=a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j} , or it could change an operator ℒ o {\mathcal{L}}^{o} that does not have a fundamental solution to one that does.

Introduction

Consider a 2nd-order operator in nondivergence form with a 1st-order term

(1)

{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})u=a_{ij}(x)\,\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u+b_{k}(x)\,\partial_{k}u,\quad\hbox{for $x\in U$},

where the coefficients
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
and
b_{k}
are measurable, real-valued functions in an open set
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
; here and throughout the paper we use the summation convention for repeated indices.
We assume
{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})
is pointwise elliptic, i.e. the matrix
a_{ij}(x)
is positive definite for each
x\in U
.
Our objective is to study the existence and asymptotics of its
fundamental solution
, i.e. a function
F(x,y)
satisfying
F(x,\cdot)\in L^{1}_{{\ell\mathrm{o}c}}(U)
for each
x\in U
and

(2)

-{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})F(x,y)=\delta(x-y)\quad\hbox{for}\ x,y\in U,

in a distributional sense that needs to be made clear. In the classical case that the coefficients are sufficiently smooth (e.g. Hölder continuous), the existence of a fundamental solution and its asymptotic properties are well-known: cf. Miranda
[
17
]
.

In
[
11
]
we studied this problem for
{\mathcal{L}}^{o}(x,\partial_{x})=a_{ij}(x)\,\partial_{i}\partial_{j}
when the modulus of continuity
\omega(r)
for the
a_{ij}
satisfies the
square-Dini condition

(3)

\int_{0}^{1}\omega^{2}(r)\frac{dr}{r}<\infty.

Notice that this assumption is weaker than
\lambda
-Hölder continuity since (
3
) is satisfied by
\omega(r)=r^{\lambda}
for
0<\lambda<1
; (
3
) is also weaker than the Dini-condition,
\int_{0}^{1}r^{-1}\omega(r)\,dr<\infty
. The hypotheses and conclusions of
[
11
]
are most easily stated if we fix
y=0
and let
U=B_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|x|<\varepsilon\}
for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. By an affine change of coordinates, we may arrange
a_{ij}(0)=\delta_{ij}
, so we may assume

(4)

\sup_{|x|=r}|a_{ij}(x)-\delta_{ij}|\leq\omega(r)\quad\hbox{for}\ 0<r<\varepsilon.

It was found in
[
11
]
that the existence of a solution of

(5)

-{\mathcal{L}}^{o}(x,\partial_{x})F(x)=\delta(x)\quad\hbox{in}\ B_{\varepsilon},

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small depends on the behavior of the integral

(6)

I^{o}(r)=\frac{1}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{r<|z|<\varepsilon}\left(\hbox{tr}({\bf A}_{z})-n\frac{\left\langle{\bf A}_{z}z,z\right\rangle}{|z|^{2}}\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n}}

as
r\to 0
; here
{\bf A}_{x}
denotes the matrix
(a_{ij})
and
\langle{\bf A}_{z}z,z\rangle=a_{ij}(z)z_{i}z_{j}
. If
I^{o}(0)=\lim_{r\to 0}I^{o}(r)
exists and is finite, then we showed there is a solution of (
5
). However, if
I^{o}(r)\to-\infty
as
r\to 0
, then we found a solution
F(x)
of

(7)

{\mathcal{L}}^{o}(x,\partial_{x})F(x)=0\quad\hbox{in}\ B_{\varepsilon},

which has a singularity at
x=0
, but
F(x)=o(|x|^{2-n})
as
|x|\to 0
and
F(x)
is not a solution of (
5
); this violates the “extended maximum principle” of
[
5
]
.

In the present paper, we generalize the results of
[
11
]
by including the 1st-order term
b_{k}\partial_{k}
, which is called a
drift
term: cf.
[
2
]
,
[
1
]
.
If the
b_{k}
are bounded in
U
, they will not effect the existence of the fundamental solution, so we will allow the drift to be singular at
x=0
, but satisfy the condition:

(8)

\sup_{|x|=r}|x|\,|b_{k}(x)|\leq c\,\omega(r)\quad\hbox{for}\ 0<r<\varepsilon.

(Here and throughout this paper,
c
denotes a generic constant.)
We again construct an unbounded solution
Z(x)
of

(9)

{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})Z(x)=0\quad\hbox{for}\ x\in B_{\varepsilon}\backslash\{0\},

and then check to see whether
Z
has the appropriate singular behavior as
|x|\to 0
so that, in a distributional sense,

(10)

-{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})Z(x)=C_{0}\,\delta(x)\quad\hbox{for some constant $C_{0}$.}

If so, then setting
F(x):=C_{0}^{-1}\,Z(x)
defines a solution of

(11)

-{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})F(x)=\delta(x)\quad\hbox{for}\ x\in B_{\varepsilon}.

To determine whether this can be done, the quantity (
6
) is generalized to

(12)

I(r)=\frac{1}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{r<|z|<\varepsilon}\left(\hbox{tr}({\bf A}_{z})-n\frac{\left\langle{\bf A}_{z}z,z\right\rangle}{|z|^{2}}+\langle{\bf B}_{z},z\rangle\right)\frac{dz}{|z|^{n}},

where
{\bf B}_{z}
denotes the vector with components
b_{k}(z)
and
\langle{\bf B}_{z},z\rangle=b_{k}(z)\,z_{k}
. In general,
I(r)
may have a singularity at
r=0
, but it is weaker than logarithmic: for any
\lambda>0
there exists
c_{\lambda}>0
such that

(13)

|I(r)|\leq\lambda\,|\log r|+c_{\lambda}\quad\hbox{for}\ 0<r<\varepsilon.

Before stating the main results of this paper, we need some notation and an additional assumption.
For an open set
U
,
1<p<\infty
, and integer
m=0,1,2
, let
W^{m,p}_{\ell\mathrm{o}c}(U)
denote the Sobolev space of functions whose derivatives up to order
m
are locally
L^{p}
-integrable in
U
. The
L^{p}
-mean of
w\in L^{p}_{\ell\mathrm{o}c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
on the annulus
A_{r}:=\{x:r<|x|<2r\}
is defined by

(14a)

M_{p}(w,r):=\left({\diagup\hskip-11.9501pt\int}_{A_{r}}|w(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

Similarly, we define

(14b)

M_{1,p}(w,r)=rM_{p}(Dw,r)+M_{p}(w,r)\quad\hbox{for}\ w\in W^{1,p}_{\ell\mathrm{o}c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),

(14c)

M_{2,p}(w,r)=r^{2}M_{p}(D^{2}w,r)+M_{1,p}(w,r)\quad\hbox{for}\ w\in W^{2,p}_{\ell\mathrm{o}c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).

For
p=\infty
we can analogously define
M_{\infty}(w,r)
,
M_{1,\infty}(w,r)
and
M_{2,\infty}(w,r)
.
The
L^{p}
-mean over annuli centered at
y\not=0
will be denoted by
M_{p}(w,r;y)
and similarly for
M_{1,p}(w,r;y)
and
M_{2,p}(w,r;y)
.
We also define

(15)

\sigma(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\omega^{2}(\rho)\frac{d\rho}{\rho},

which satisfies
\sigma(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0
because of (
3
). Finally, as a modulus of continuity we want
\omega(r)
to be
nondecreasing for
0<r<1
, but we also assume for some
\kappa\in(0,1)
that

(16)

\omega(r)\,r^{-1+\kappa}\quad\hbox{is nonincreasing on $0<r<1$.}

This is natural since we are interested in moduli of continuity that vanish slower than
r
as
r\to 0
.

Our first result generalizes Theorem 1 in
[
11
]
, which only applied to
{\mathcal{L}}^{o}(x,\partial_{x})
.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 3
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, assume
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
satisfy (
4
) while the
b_{k}
satisfy (
8
).
Then for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, there is a solution of (
9
) in the form

(17a)

Z(x)=\int_{|x|}^{\varepsilon}s^{1-n}\,e^{I(s)}\,ds\,(1+\zeta(|x|))+v(x)\quad\hbox{for}\ |x|<\varepsilon,

where
I(r)
is given by (
12
),
\zeta(r)
satisfies

(17b)

M_{2,p}(\zeta,r)\leq c\,\max(\omega(r),\sigma(r)),

and
v(x)
satisfies

(17c)

M_{2,p}(v,r)\leq c\,r^{2-n}\,e^{I(r)}\,\omega(r).

Moreover,
for any
u\in W^{2,p}_{\ell\mathrm{o}c}(\overline{B_{\varepsilon}}\backslash\{0\})

that is a strong solution of
{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})u=0
in
\overline{B_{\varepsilon}}\backslash\{0\}
subject to the growth condition

(18)

M_{2,p}(u,r)\leq c\,r^{1-n+\varepsilon_{0}}\ \hbox{where}\ \varepsilon_{0}>0,

there exist constants
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
(depending on
u
) such that

(19)

u(x)=c_{0}\,Z(x)+c_{1}+w(x),

where
M_{2,p}(w,r)\leq c\,r^{1-\varepsilon_{1}}
for any
\varepsilon_{1}>0
.

We will prove this result in Section
1
, but let us here observe that
I(r)
satisfies
|I^{\prime}(r)|\leq c\,r^{-1}\,\omega(r)
, so we may integrate by parts and take
p>n
to conclude

(20)

Z(x)=\frac{|x|^{2-n}\,e^{I(|x|)}}{n-2}\left(1+\xi(x)\right),

where
M_{1,\infty}(\xi,r)\leq c\,\max(\omega(r),\sigma(r))
.
This shows that
I(r)
controls how closely
Z
adheres to the fundamental solution of the Laplacian.

Our second result generalizes Theorem 2 in
[
11
]
and shows that the existence and finiteness of the limit
I(0)=\lim_{r\to 0}I(r)
determines whether
Z
solves (
10
) for some constant
C_{0}
.

Theorem 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small:

(i)

If
I(0)=\lim_{r\to 0}I(r)
exists and is finite, then
we can solve (
10
) in
B_{\varepsilon}
with
C_{0}=|S^{n-1}|\,e^{I(0)}
.

(ii)

If
I(r)\to-\infty
as
r\to 0
, then solving (
10
) in
B_{\varepsilon}
yields
C_{0}=0
, and so
Z
solves
-{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})Z(x)=0
in
B_{\varepsilon}
, despite its singularity at
x=0
.

We will prove this result in Section
2
, but let us observe that in case (i) we have found a solution of
(
11
) of the form

(21)

F(x)=\frac{|x|^{2-n}}{(n-2)|S^{n-1}|}\,(1+o(|x|))\quad\hbox{as}\ |x|\to 0,

so the comparison with the fundamental solution of the Laplacian is even more explicit.
On the other hand, if
I(r)\to+\infty
as
r\to 0
, then the singular solution
Z(x)
grows more rapidly as
|x|\to 0
than the fundamental solution for the Laplacian, and we are not able to solve (
10
).

Let us consider a simple example to illustrate the effect of the drift term on the fundamental solution.

Example 1.
Let
a_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
so that our operator (
1
) becomes

(22)

{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})=\Delta+b_{k}(x)\partial_{x},

and the quantity
I(r)
in (
12
) reduces to

(23)

I(r)=\frac{1}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{r<|x|<\varepsilon}\frac{\langle{\bf B}_{z},z\rangle}{|z|^{n}}\,dz.

If
\omega(r)
satisfies the Dini condition, then from condition (
8
) we easily conclude that
I(0)
exists and is finite; but this finite limit may exist without the Dini condition: e.g. we could take
b_{k}(x)=x_{k}\,\sin(|x|^{-1})\,\omega(|x|)/|x|^{2}
. In any case, provided
I(0)
is finite, we have a solution
F(x)
of (
10
) that is comparable to the fundamental solution of the Laplacian.

In
[
2
]
, Cranston and Zhao consider operators of the form
{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta+b\cdot\nabla
with vector field
b
.
Assuming
U
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and
b(x)
satisfies the conditions

(24a)

\lim_{r\to 0}\,\sup_{x\in U}\int_{|x-y|<r}\frac{|b(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n-2}}dy=0=\lim_{r\to 0}\,\sup_{x\in U}\int_{|x-y|<r}\frac{|b(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-1}}dy,

they conclude that the Green’s function
G(x,y)
for
{\mathcal{L}}
in
U
exists and is comparable to the Green’s function
G_{0}(x,y)
for
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta
, i.e.

(24b)

c^{-1}\,G_{0}(x,y)\leq G(x,y)\leq c\,G_{0}(x,y)\quad\hbox{for}\ x,y\in U,\ x\not=y.

If
U
contains the origin, then setting
F(x):=G(x,0)
defines a solution of (
11
), so
let us compare our hypotheses and conclusions with those of
[
2
]
. If
b
has a singularity at
x=0
of the form

|b(x)|=|x|^{-1}\,\omega(|x|),
the conditions in (
24a
) require

\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\omega^{2}(\rho)}{\rho}\,d\rho<\infty\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\omega(\rho)}{\rho}\,d\rho<\infty.

The first of these is the square-Dini condition (
3
) that we have required, while the second is the Dini condition that we have
not
required: to conclude that the fundamental solution exists at
x=0
, we only require the function
I(r)
given in (
23
) to have a finite limit
I(0)=\lim_{r\to 0}I(r)
. Moreover, (
20
) is a sharper estimate than (
24b
).

In Example 1, the drift term plays the role of a perturbation which, if not too large, does not affect the existence of the fundamental solution.
We now consider an example where singular drift can convert an operator
{\mathcal{L}}^{o}
that does not have a fundamental solution to one for which a fundamental solution exists!

Example 2.
Consider
{\mathcal{L}}^{o}=a_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}
with coefficients

(25)

a_{ij}(x)=\delta_{ij}+g(r)\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2}},

where
|g(r)|\leq\omega(r)
with
\omega
satisfying (
3
).
Coefficients of the form (
25
) were first considered by Gilbarg & Serrin
[
5
]
, and have proven useful in both nondivergence and divergence form equations (cf.
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
).
We can use (
6
) to calculate

(26)

I^{o}(r)=(1-n)\int_{r}^{1}g(\rho)\frac{d\rho}{\rho}.

Note that
I^{o}(0)\!=\!\lim_{r\to 0}I^{o}(r)
exists and is finite when
\omega
satisfies the Dini condition, but this finite limit may exist without Dini continuity: e.g.
g(r)=\sin(r^{-1})\omega(r)
.

Now let us assume
g(r)>0
and
\omega
does not satisfy the Dini condition. Then
I^{o}(r)\to-\infty
as
r\to 0
, so the fundamental solution for
{\mathcal{L}}^{o}
does not exist at
y=0
.
However, if we add the first-order coefficients

(27)

b_{k}(x)=(n-1)\,\frac{x_{k}}{|x|^{2}}\,[g(|x|)+g^{2}(|x|)]

to obtain
{\mathcal{L}}
as in (
1
), then we can use (
12
) to calculate

(28)

I(r)=(n-1)\int_{r}^{1}\frac{g^{2}(\rho)}{\rho}\,d\rho.

Since
g^{2}(r)\leq\omega^{2}(r)
and
\omega
satisfies (
3
), we see that
I(r)
has a finite limit as
r\to 0
, and so we can solve (
10
) to conclude the fundamental solution exists at
y=0
.

As previously stated, we will prove Theorem 1 in Section
1
and Theorem 2 in Section
2
; but in those sections we will also state and prove Corollaries 1 and 2 respectively, which show how the formulas for the singular solution
Z
change when

and

. To formulate these results, we need to generalize (

, choose

so that

and assume

\sup_{|x-y|=r}\|{\bf A}_{x}-{\bf A}_{y}\|\leq\omega(r)\quad\hbox{for}\ 0<r<\varepsilon.

We continue to assume the

We also generalize the function

I_{y}(r)=\frac{1}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{r<|z-y|<\varepsilon}H(z,y)\,\frac{dz}{|z-y|^{n}}\,,

where the integrand

is

(30b)

{\rm tr}({\bf A}_{z}{\bf A}_{y}^{-1})-n\frac{\langle{\bf A}_{z}{\bf A}_{y}^{-1/2}(z-y),{\bf A}_{y}^{-1/2}(z-y)\rangle}{|z-y|^{2}}+\langle{\bf B}_{z}{\bf A}_{y}^{-1/2},(z-y)\rangle,

with
{\bf A}_{y}^{-1}
denoting the inverse matrix.
If

and
{\bf A}_{0}=I
, then (

and we also stipulate

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})
as in (
1
) is an elliptic operator in a bounded open set
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, where the coefficients
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
are continuous functions with modulus of continuity
\omega(r)
satisfying (
3
).
Suppose
U
contains the origin and the
b_{k}
satisfy (
8
) but otherwise are bounded in
U
.
For each
y\in U
assume that the limit
I_{y}(0)=\lim_{r\to 0}I_{y}(r)
exists and is finite. Then

{\mathcal{L}}(x,\partial_{x})
has a fundamental solution
F(x,y)
in
U
and it has the asymptotic behavior

(31)

F(x,y)=\frac{\langle{\bf A}_{y}^{-1}(x-y),(x-y)\rangle^{\frac{2-n}{2}}}{(n-2)|S^{n-1}|\sqrt{\det{\bf A}_{y}}}(1+o(1)))\quad\hbox{as}\ x\to y.

This is proved in Section 3. Note that the leading asymptotic in (
31
) is familiar as the “Levi function” that occurs in the classical case (cf.
[
17
]
).
As a consequence of Theorem 3, we see that the singular drift may affect the existence of the fundamental solution but does not play a role in its asymptotic behavior as
x\to y
.

Many of the arguments in this paper also appear in
[

]

From a more general perspective, the asymptotic analysis used here is related to that developed in

[

]

1.
Construction of the singular solution
Z

In this section, we will not only prove Theorem 1, but we will state and prove Corollary 1, which shows how the formulas change when we no longer assume
y=0
and
a_{ij}(y)=\delta_{ij}
. Instead of constructing
Z(x)
in a small ball, we replace the condition that
\omega(r)
satisfies (
3
) with

(34)

\sigma(1)=\int_{0}^{1}\omega^{2}(\rho)\frac{d\rho}{\rho}<\mu^{2}

where
\mu>0
is sufficiently small, and then show existence in the unit ball
B_{1}
. In fact, with
\kappa\in(0,1)
as in
(
16
), this also implies

(35)

\omega(r)<c_{\kappa}\,\mu\quad\hbox{for}\ 0<r\leq 1,

since

\mu^{2}>\int_{0}^{r}\omega^{2}(\rho)\frac{d\rho}{\rho}\geq\omega^{2}(r)r^{-2+2\kappa}\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{1-2\kappa}d\rho=\frac{\omega^{2}(r)}{2(1-\kappa)}.

Moreover, it will be useful to consider
{\mathcal{L}}
on all of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, so we assume

(36)

a_{ij}(x)=\delta_{ij}\ \hbox{and}\ b_{k}(x)=0\ \hbox{for}\ |x|>1,

and construct a solution of
{\mathcal{L}}Z=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof of Theorem 1.
As in
[
11
]
, we use spherical means: for a function
f(x)
we denote its mean value over the sphere
|x|=r
by
\overline{f}(r)
:

\overline{f}(r)={\diagup\hskip-11.9501pt\int}_{S^{n-1}}f(r\theta)\,d\theta,

where
S^{n-1}
is the unit sphere, the slashed integral denotes mean value,
r=|x|
,
\theta=x/|x|\in S^{n-1}
, and
d\theta
denotes standard surface measure on
S^{n-1}
.
Let us write

(37a)

Z(x)=h(|x|)+v(x),\quad\hbox{where}\ h(r):=\overline{Z}(r),

so that

(37b)

\overline{v}(r)=0.

If we take the spherical mean of the equation
{\mathcal{L}}(h+v)=0
, we obtain

(38a)

In particular, we have

(49b)

), (

), and Hölder’s inequality to conclude

\sigma
sufficiently small that
\sup_{x\in B_{\sigma}}|\tilde{b}_{k}(x)|\leq\varepsilon\,|x|^{-1}
.
For
1<p<n
, by Hardy’s inequality we have
\|{\mathcal{L}}_{2}w\|_{L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon\,\|w\|_{W^{2,p}}
for
w\in W^{2,p}
with support in
B_{\sigma}
. If we take
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, we can arrange that the à priori inequality
\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(U)}\leq c\,\|{\mathcal{L}}v\|_{L^{p}(U)}
holds for
v\in W^{2,p}(U)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(U)
. We conclude that (
75
) admits a unique solution
v(\cdot,y)\in W^{2,p}(U)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(U)
for
1<p<n
. Taking
p\in(n/2,n)
, we see that
v(x,y)
is continuous in
x\in U
.

To confirm that
F(x,y)
satisfies (
31
), we know from (
74
) and (
64
) that

F(x,y)=\frac{\langle{\bf A}_{y}^{-1}(x-y),(x-y)\rangle^{\frac{2-n}{2}}}{(n-2)|S^{n-1}|\sqrt{\det{\bf A}_{y}}}\,e^{I_{y}(\sqrt{\langle{\bf A}_{y}^{-1}(x-y),(x-y)\rangle})-I_{y}(0)}(1+\xi_{y}(x))

where
M_{1,\infty}(\xi_{y},r;y)\leq c\,\max(\omega(r),\sigma(r))
for
0<r<\varepsilon
. Since the exponential term tends to 1 as
|x-y|\to 0
, we obtain
(
31
).

Appendix B
The Laplacian on weighted Sobolev spaces

Many authors have studied the mapping properties of elliptic operators such as the Laplacian on weighted Sobolev spaces on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and other noncompact manifolds with conical or cylindrical ends: cf.
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
. We will recall some of these results for punctured Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{o}:={\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
. Since we are mostly concerned in this paper with singularities at
x=0
, let us first investigate the weighted
L^{p}
-norm on the punctured unit ball
B_{o}:=B_{1}\backslash\{0\}
. For
\delta\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
1<p<\infty
, define the Banach space
L^{p}_{\delta}(B_{o})
by the norm

(79)

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{\delta}(B_{o})}:=\int_{|x|<1}|x|^{\delta p}\,|u(x)|^{p}\,dx.

For example, the constants are in
L^{p}_{\delta}(B_{o})
if and only if
\delta>-n/p
.
To compare (
79
) with
L^{p}
-means, it is easy to see (cf.
[
11
]
) that
M(u,r)\leq c\,r^{\varepsilon}
for
0<r<1
implies that
u\in L_{\delta}^{p}(B_{o})
provided
\varepsilon+\delta>-n/p
, and conversely
u\in L_{\delta}^{p}(B_{o})
implies
M_{p}(u,r)\leq c_{\varepsilon}\,r^{\varepsilon}
for
0<r<1
if
\varepsilon=-\delta-n/p
.

Now we introduce the weighted
L^{p}
-norm for functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{o}
with separate weights at the origin and at infinity. For
\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, define

(80)

\displaystyle\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}}}:=\int_{|x|<1}|x|^{\delta_{0}p}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx+\int_{|x|>1}|x|^{\delta_{1}p}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx.

We then define the weighted Sobolev space
W^{2,p}_{\delta_{o},\delta_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{o})
to be functions
u\in W^{2,p}_{{\ell\mathrm{o}c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{o})
for which

(81)

\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{\delta_{o},\delta_{1}}}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}\||x|^{|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}}}

is finite. It is clear that

(82)

\Delta:W^{2,p}_{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{o})\to L^{p}_{\delta_{0}+2,\delta_{1}+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{o})

is a bounded linear operator, and (using the analysis of
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
, for example) it can be shown that
(
82
) is an isomorphism for
-n/p<\delta_{0},\delta_{1}<-2+n/p^{\prime}
, where
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
. (Since
n\geq 3
, such
\delta_{0},\delta_{1}
exist.)
Moreover, provided
\delta_{0},\delta_{1}
do not take the values
-n/p-k
or
-2+n/p^{\prime}+k
where
k
is a nonnegative integer, then (
82
) is a Fredholm operator whose nullspace and cokernel are easily described in terms of harmonic polynomials.
Since we are principally interested in the behavior of functions at the origin, we will fix

(83)

-n/p<\delta_{1}<-2+n/p^{\prime}

and allow
\delta_{0}
to vary. We only require a small range of values for
\delta_{0}
.

Proposition 2
.

Assume
1<p<\infty
and (
83
). Then the map (
82
) is

(a)

an isomorphism for
-n/p<\delta_{0}<-2+n/p^{\prime}
;

(b)

surjective with nullspace spanned by
|x|^{2-n}
if
-2+n/p^{\prime}<\delta_{0}<-1+n/p^{\prime}
;

(c)

injective with cokernel spanned by

if
-1-n/p<\delta_{0}<-n/p
.

Hydrodynamic helicity polarization in relativistic heavy ion collisions
Cong Yi, Shi Pu, Jian-Hua Gao, Di-Lun Yang

Abstract
We study helicity polarization through the (3+1) dimensional relativistic viscous hydrodynamic models at s N ​ N = 200 \sqrt{s_{NN}}=200 GeV Au+Au collisions. Similar to the local spin polarization, we consider the helicity polarization beyond global equilibrium and investigate the contributions induced by thermal vorticity, shear viscous tensor, and the fluid acceleration. We find that the local helicity polarization induced by thermal vorticity dominates over other contributions. It also implies that in the low-energy collisions, the the fluid vorticity as part of thermal vorticity may play the crucial role to the total helicity polarization. Such a finding could be useful for probing the local strength of vorticity in rotational quark gluon plasmas by measuring helicity polarization. Our simulation confirms the strict space reversal symmetry, whereas we also compare our numerical results with approximated relations derived from ideal Bjorken flow. Our studies also provide a baseline for the future investigation on local parity violation through the correlations of helicity polarization.

II
Helicity polarization

In this section, we briefly review the formalism for helicity polarization
based on Ref.
(
Becattini_prl2018_bk
;
Becattini:2020xbh
;
Gao:2021rom
)
.
For simplicity, we concentrate on the helicity polarization induced
by hydrodynamic variables and neglect the contribution from axial
chemical potential.

We start from the single-particle mean spin vector
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(p)

by the modified Cooper-Frye formula
(
Becattini:2013fla
;
Fang:2016uds
)
.

\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(\mathrm{p})=\frac{\int d\Sigma\cdot p\mathcal{J}_{5}^{\mu}(p,X)}{2m_{\Lambda}\int d\Sigma\cdot\mathcal{N}(p,X)},

(1)

where the
m_{\Lambda}
is the mass of
\Lambda
hyperons,
\Sigma_{\mu}

is the normal vector of the freeze-out surface, the
\mathcal{N}^{\mu}(p,X)

and
\mathcal{J}_{5}^{\mu}(p,X)
are number density and axial-charge
current density in phase space, respectively. The
\mathcal{N}^{\mu}(p,X)

and
\mathcal{J}_{5}^{\mu}(p,X)
can be derived from the quantum
kinetic theory
(
Hidaka:2018mel
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\mu}(p,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{p\cdot n}\left[\mathcal{J}_{+}^{\mu}(p,X)+\mathcal{J}_{-}^{\mu}(p,X)\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{5}^{\mu}(p,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{p\cdot n}\left[\mathcal{J}_{+}^{\mu}(p,X)-\mathcal{J}_{-}^{\mu}(p,X)\right],

(2)

where
\int_{p\cdot n}\equiv\int dp\cdot np\cdot n\theta(p\cdot n)/(2\pi)

with
n^{\mu}
being chosen as the fluid velocity
u^{\mu}
in thermal
equilibrium,
\mathcal{J}_{+}^{\mu}(p,X)
and
\mathcal{J}_{-}^{\mu}(p,X)

are the Wigner functions for the right and left-handed fermions, respectively.

Inserting the expression of
\mathcal{J_{\pm}^{\mu}}(p,X)
into Eq.

\mu_{R}=\mu_{L}\equiv\mu

\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})=\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{thermal}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})+\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{shear}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})+\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{accT}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})+\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{chemical}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})+\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{EB}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p}),

(3)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{thermal}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\frac{u_{\beta}}{T},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{shear}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\frac{\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{\nu}}{(u\cdot p)T}\left\{p^{\rho}(\partial_{\rho}u_{\alpha}+\partial_{\alpha}u_{\rho}-u_{\rho}Du_{\alpha})u_{\beta}\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{accT}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\frac{1}{T}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{\nu}u_{\alpha}(Du_{\beta}-\frac{1}{T}\partial_{\beta}T),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{chemical}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\frac{1}{(u\cdot p)}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{\alpha}u_{\beta}\partial_{\nu}\frac{\mu}{T},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{EB}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\left[\frac{1}{(u\cdot p)T}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{\alpha}u_{\beta}E_{\nu}+\frac{B^{\mu}}{T}\right],

(4)

and,

F^{\mu}=\frac{\hbar}{8m_{\Lambda}N}p^{\mu}f_{V}^{(0)}(1-f_{V}^{(0)}),\;N=\int d\Sigma^{\mu}p_{\mu}f_{V}^{(0)},

(5)

Here,
T
is the temperature
and

is the Fermic-Dirac distribution function. The subscripts,
thermal, shear, accT, chemical

stand for the terms related to thermal vorticity, shear viscous tensor,

\mu/T
, and electromagnetic fields, respectively. The

and

are given by,

E^{\mu}=F^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu}

.
Note that Ref.
(
Yi:2021ryh
)
has roughly extended the case for
massless fermions

4
)
for massive fermions, one may refer to Refs.

;

;

;

)

\displaystyle S^{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{$\widehat{\mathbf{p}}$$\cdot$}\mathcal{\boldsymbol{S}}(\mathbf{p})=\widehat{p}^{x}\mathcal{S}^{x}+\widehat{p}^{y}\mathcal{S}^{y}+\widehat{p}^{z}\mathcal{S}^{z},

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{thermal}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}p_{0}\epsilon^{0ijk}\widehat{p}_{i}\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{u_{k}}{T}\right),

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{shear}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\frac{\epsilon^{0ijk}\widehat{p}^{i}p_{0}}{(u\cdot p)T}\left\{p^{\sigma}(\partial_{\sigma}u_{j}+\partial_{j}u_{\sigma}-u_{\sigma}Du_{j})u_{k}\right\},

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{accT}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\frac{1}{T}\epsilon^{0ijk}\widehat{p}^{i}p_{0}u_{j}(Du_{k}-\frac{1}{T}\partial_{k}T),

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{chemical}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle-2\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\frac{1}{(u\cdot p)}p_{0}\epsilon^{0ijk}\hat{p}_{i}\left[\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{\mu}{T}\right)\right]u_{k},

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{EB}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\left[\frac{1}{(u\cdot p)T}\epsilon^{0ijk}\widehat{p}^{i}p_{0}E_{j}u_{k}+\frac{\widehat{p}^{i}B^{i}}{T}\right],

where we implicitly impose the on-shell condition
p_{0}=\sqrt{|{\bf p}|^{2}+m^{2}}

with
m
being the fermionic mass in the end. We emphasize that
only the spatial components of thermal vorticity contribute to the

Now we review the symmetric properties
for
S_{\textrm{thermal}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})
in hydrodynamical models.
For simplicity, we consider
S_{\textrm{thermal}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})

in an ideal fluid and eventually implement our result in a Bjorken
flow. More detailed analyses based on symmetries are shown in Refs.

(
Becattini_prl2018_bk
;

;

)
.

In an ideal fluid, the temperature vorticity,

\Omega_{T}^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}(Tu^{\nu})-\partial^{\nu}(Tu^{\mu}),

(8)

is conserved along the velocity, i.e.
\Omega_{T}^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu}=0

(
Becattini:2015ska
;
Deng_2016PRC
;
Wu:2019eyi
)
and satisfies the
relativistic Kelvin circulation theorem
(
MahajanPRL2010
;
Gao:2014coa
;
Yang:2017asn
;
Wang:2020ewj
)
.
As a consequence, if
\Omega_{T}^{\mu\nu}
is zero at initial time,
it will always be vanishing during the evolution of this ideal fluid.
Then, by using this condition, we can express the
(\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}u_{\mu})

as,

(\partial_{\mu}u_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}u_{\mu})=-\frac{1}{T}(u_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}-u_{\mu}\partial_{\mu})T.

(9)

Using Eq. (
9
), we simplify
\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{thermal}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

in Eq. (
4
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{thermal}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{T^{2}}\int d\Sigma^{\sigma}F_{\sigma}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{\nu}(u_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}T).

(10)

To compute the
\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{thermal}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

in hydrodynamics at the freeze-out hyper-surface, we can assume that
the distribution function
f_{V}^{(0)}
is approximately at the equilibrium.
Then, we can use the relation
\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\sigma}}f_{V}^{(0)}=-\frac{u_{\sigma}}{T}f_{V}^{(0)}(1-f_{V}^{(0)})

to remove the fluid velocity and obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{thermal}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4m_{\Lambda}N}\int d\Sigma_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\nu}\frac{1}{T}(\partial_{\sigma}T)\frac{\partial f_{V}^{(0)}}{\partial p^{\text{$\rho$}}},

(11)

Integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{thermal}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4m_{\Lambda}N}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\text{$\rho$}}}\left[\int d\Sigma_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}\frac{1}{T}(\partial_{\sigma}T)f_{V}^{(0)}\right]

(12)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4m_{\Lambda}N}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\nu}\int d\Sigma_{\rho}\frac{\partial_{\sigma}T}{T}f_{V}^{(0)}.

As argued in Ref.
(

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\textrm{thermal}}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4m_{\Lambda}}\frac{1}{T}\left.\frac{dT}{d\tau}\right|_{\Sigma}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2nv_{n}(p_{T},Y)\sin n\phi\right],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4m_{\Lambda}N}\frac{1}{T}\left.\frac{dT}{d\tau}\right|_{\Sigma}\partial_{\phi}\int d\Sigma_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}f_{V}^{(0)}\sinh\eta

) after the rapidity integration in Sec.
III
.

Meanwhile, the system should have the space reversal symmetry
(
Becattini_prl2018_bk
;
Becattini:2020xbh
)
,
i.e.
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{S}}}(\mathbf{p})=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{S}}}(-\mathbf{p}),

or

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{S}}}(Y,\phi_{p})=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{\boldsymbol{S}}}(-Y,\phi_{p}+\pi),

(19)

and from Eq. (
6
) we can obtain the following relation,

S^{h}(Y,\phi_{p})=-S^{h}(-Y,\phi_{p}+\pi).

(20)

Note that, Eq. (
20
) should be satisfied
for each part in Eq. (
7
). We will test it
in the later hydrodynamic simulations.

At last, let us discuss the frame dependence of helicity polarization. All the quantities mentioned above are chosen in the laboratory frame. In experiments, the polarization of
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
hyperons are measured in their own rest frames. Since the momentum in the rest frame of hyperons
p^{\prime\mu}=(m_{\Lambda},0)
is related to the momentum
p^{\mu}=(E_{\Lambda},\mathbb{p})
in the laboratory frame by Lorentz transformation, i.e.
p^{\prime\mu}=\Lambda^{\mu}_{\;\nu}p^{\nu}
, we obtain the polarization vector
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\mu}
in the rest frame of hyperons,

\mathcal{S}^{\prime\mu}=\left(0,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}-\frac{(\mathbb{p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}})\mathbb{p}}{E_{\Lambda}(E_{\Lambda}+m_{\Lambda})}\right).

(21)

Similar to the definition in Eq. (
6
), one can also define the helicity polarization as
\hat{\mathbb{p}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, which is connected to our helicity polarization
S^{h}
by,

\hat{\mathbb{p}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}=\frac{m_{\Lambda}}{E_{\Lambda}}\hat{\mathbb{p}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}=\frac{m_{\Lambda}}{E_{\Lambda}}S^{h}.

(22)

Therefore, it is straightforward to transform our simulation results to
\hat{\mathbb{p}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}

(
Becattini_prl2018_bk
;
Becattini:2020xbh
)
.

Before end this section, we emphasize that in general the axial chemical potential
\mu_{A}=\mu_{R}-\mu_{L}

can also contribute to the
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})
and leads
to the extra helicity polarization, which could be a possible signal
of the parity violation
(
Becattini:2020xbh
)
. Since we have
already assumed that
\mu_{A}=0
in Eqs. (
4
), there
are no such corrections in our case. One can also see Refs.

(
Becattini:2020xbh
;
Shi:2020qrx
;
Liu:2021nyg
)
for other corrections
for
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}(\mathbf{p})
.

In Fig.

For our case,

\textrm{sign}(P_{H}^{\pm\text{thermal}})=\pm\textrm{sign}(P_{\text{thermal}}^{\pm z})

P_{H}^{+\text{thermal}}=-P_{H}^{-\text{thermal}}

and

and spin polarization

induced by shear viscous tensor and fluid acceleration in Figs.

3

Other important observation in Figs.

IV
Conclusions and discussions

We have studied the helicity polarization in hydrodynamic approaches.
Following our previous work
(
Yi:2021ryh
)
, we decompose the
helicity polarization
S^{h}
introduced in Refs.
(
Becattini:2020xbh
;
Gao:2021rom
)

into several components in local equilibrium, such as helicity
polarization induced by thermal vorticity
S_{\textrm{thermal}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})
,
shear viscous tensor
S_{\textrm{shear}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})
, fluid
acceleration
S_{\textrm{accT}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})
, other terms related
to electromagnetic fields
S_{\textrm{EB}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})
, and
the gradient of the ratio to a vector chemical potential and
temperature
S_{\textrm{chemical}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})
. For simplicity,
we neglect possible contributions from an axial chemical
potential. We then obtain the space reversal symmetry of
S^{h}

in Eq.(
20
) and discuss the property of

S_{\textrm{thermal}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})
in the ideal Bjorken flow
shown in Eq. (
16
). We then implement the (3+1) dimensional
viscous hydrodynamic package CLVisc with AMPT initial conditions at

20\%-50\%
centrality of
\sqrt{s_{NN}}=200\text{ GeV}
Au-Au collisions
to study helicity polarization. We neglect
S_{\textrm{EB}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})

and
S_{\textrm{chemical}}^{h}(\mathbf{p})
in current studies and
analyze the azimuthal angle
\phi_{p}

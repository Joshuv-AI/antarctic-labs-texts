Effects of center-of-mass correction and nucleon anomalous magnetic moments on nuclear charge radii
Yusuke Tanimura, Myung-Ki Cheoun

Abstract
Effects of the center-of-mass (CM) correction together with the nucleon electromagnetic form factors on the nuclear charge radius are systematically studied with a relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov model. Both one- and two-body parts of the CM correction are taken into account. It is found that the one- and two-body CM corrections, and the spin-orbit effect originating from the nucleon anomalous magnetic moments are all of the same order in magnitude, and that they give sizable impacts on the charge radius from light to heavy nuclei.

IV
Summary

We have studied the effects of the one- and two-body CM corrections,
and the SO term originating from the anomalous magnetic moment
of nucleon on the nuclear charge radius.
The former is required by the inevitable breaking of translational invariance
in the mean-field model,
whereas the latter is the electromagnetic property of nucleon
affecting directly the nuclear charge-density distribution.
The finite-size effects of nucleon from both Dirac and Pauli form factors were also included.
We employed an RHB model with DDME2 for
ph
channel and Gogny D1S for
pp
channel.

We have observed sizable impacts of each correction on the charge radius
from light to heavy nuclei.
The light nuclei are significantly affected by the both CM and SO corrections,
while the heavier nuclei are much less affected by the former, as expected.

The CM correction consists of one- and two-body parts.
The heavier is the system, the more significant is the effect of the two-body part,
thus it should not be neglected. We also find that the harmonic-oscillator model
is not a good approximation in light or weakly-bound systems although
it is nearly satisfactory for heavy systems.

The magnetic SO effect is more sensitive to the shell structure than the CM correction.
In particular, it leads to remarkable improvement of Sn and Pb isotope shifts for DDME2 functional.
The SO effect also produces additional kinks at
24
O and
68
Ni,
latter of which is not observed in experimental data.

The two corrections seemingly improve also the agreement with the measured charge radii
in very light H and C isotopes.
Although the beyond-mean-field correlations are likely to be important in these lighter systems,
it was shown that the present mean-field model roughly follows the trend of the measured charge radii.

It would also be interesting to study the effects of the CM correction
on the other kinds of radius.
More detailed analyses including those of the matter radius and neutron skin
thickness will be reported elsewhere.

The CM correction affects also the deformation parameters.
The correction of the quadrupole moments can be made in a similar way as the radius
since it is quadratic in coordinates.
The corrections of higher moments will be much more complicated because
there arise three-body and higher operators.
However, it is expected that the the CM correction is
small for the deformation parameters because cancellation of
the correction terms would occur among different spatial directions.

Appendix A
Derivation of CM correction on radius

Derivation of Eqs. (

In general, the expectation value of the square of an observable

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}^{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[A^{2}\rho]+\left({\rm Tr}[A\rho]\right)^{2}-{\rm Tr}[A\rho A\rho]-{\rm Tr}[A^{*}\kappa^{*}A\kappa],

The first term in the right hand side is the one-body operator part of

If

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}^{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\alpha\beta}(v_{\alpha}^{2}v_{\beta}^{2}-u_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}u_{\beta}v_{\beta})\left|\langle\alpha|A|\beta\rangle\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}^{2}\langle\alpha|A^{2}|\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle-\sum_{\alpha\beta}(v_{\alpha}^{2}v_{\beta}^{2}+u_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}u_{\beta}v_{\beta})\left|\langle\alpha|A|\beta\rangle\right|^{2}.

(26)

Note the opposite signs of the last terms in Eqs. (

\displaystyle=

In this appendix, we give an analytic estimate, similar to the one in Ref.

Let us consider particles with

filling HO shells up to the one of

\displaystyle N_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\bar{N}}\nu\frac{1}{2}(n+1)(n+2)=\frac{\nu}{6}(\bar{N}+1)(\bar{N}+2)(\bar{N}+3).

(28)

The squared radius within the HO model is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}^{2}\langle\alpha|r^{2}|\alpha\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{\nu}{8}(\bar{N}+1)(\bar{N}+2)^{2}(\bar{N}+3)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}N_{p}(\bar{N}+2),

where

term coming from the pairing tensor

Using the HO matrix element of

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\beta}v_{\alpha}^{2}v_{\beta}^{2}|\langle\alpha|\bm{r}|\beta\rangle|^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{8}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\bar{N}(\bar{N}+1)(\bar{N}+2)(\bar{N}+3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}N_{p}\bar{N}.

(30)

The real solution for the algebraic equation (

\displaystyle\bar{N}+2=f_{\nu}(N_{p})^{1/3}+\frac{1}{3f_{\nu}(N_{p})^{1/3}},

(31)

where

\displaystyle f_{\nu}(N_{p})=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(\frac{3N_{p}}{\nu}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{27}}+\frac{3N_{p}}{\nu}.

(32)

), and (

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\beta}v_{\alpha}^{2}v_{\beta}^{2}|\langle\alpha|\bm{r}|\beta\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}N_{p}\left[f_{\nu}(N_{p})^{1/3}+\frac{1}{3}f_{\nu}(N_{p})^{-1/3}-2\right].

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega_{n}}\left[f_{2}(N)^{1/3}+\frac{1}{3}f_{2}(N)^{-1/3}\right],

\displaystyle\langle r^{2}\rangle_{p}=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega_{p}}\left[f_{2}(Z)^{1/3}+\frac{1}{3}f_{2}(Z)^{-1/3}\right],

\displaystyle\langle r^{2}\rangle_{m}=

respectively.

and the CM2 term is given as

\displaystyle\Delta^{({\rm CM}2)}_{p}=

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega_{p}}\frac{Z}{A^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2A}{Z}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[f_{2}(Z)^{1/3}+\frac{1}{3}f_{2}(Z)^{-1/3}-2\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega_{n}}\frac{N}{A^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left[f_{2}(N)^{1/3}+\frac{1}{3}f_{2}(N)^{-1/3}-2\right].

Note that we treat neutrons and protons separately and
do not set

that remains yet to be determined,

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega_{n}}=\frac{3}{4}\frac{\hbar}{m\omega_{p}}=\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{1/3}\frac{3}{5}r_{0}^{2}A^{1/3},

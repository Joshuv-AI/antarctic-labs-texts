Weierstrass Representations of Lorentzian Minimal Surfaces in ℝ 2 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
Ognian Kassabov, Velichka Milousheva

Abstract
. The minimal Lorentzian surfaces in ℝ 2 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}_{2} whose first normal space is two-dimensional and whose Gauss curvature K K and normal curvature ϰ \varkappa satisfy K 2 − ϰ 2 > 0 K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0 are called minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type. These surfaces admit canonical parameters and with respect to such parameters are determined uniquely up to a motion in ℝ 2 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}_{2} by the curvatures K K and ϰ \varkappa satisfying a system of two natural PDEs. In the present paper we study minimal Lorentzian surfaces in ℝ 2 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}_{2} and find a Weierstrass representation with respect to isothermal parameters of any minimal surface with two-dimensional first normal space. We also obtain a Weierstrass representation with respect to canonical parameters of any minimal Lorentzian surface of general type and solve explicitly the system of natural PDEs expressing any solution to this system by means of four real functions of one variable.

Primary 53B30, Secondary 53A10, 53A35

1.
Introduction

The study of minimal surfaces is one of the main topics in classical differential geometry. In the last years, great attention is paid to Lorentzian surfaces in pseudo-Euclidean spaces, since pseudo-Riemannian geometry has many important applications in Physics.
Minimal Lorentzian surfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{1}
have been classified by B.-Y. Chen
[
5
]
.
Classification results for minimal Lorentzian surfaces in pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
with arbitrary dimension
m
and arbitrary index
s
are obtained in
[
6
]
. Minimal Lorentzian surfaces in the pseudo-Euclidean 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
whose first normal space is two-dimensional and whose Gauss curvature
K
and normal curvature
\varkappa
satisfy the inequality
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0
are studied in
[
1
]
under the name minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type. This class of surfaces is characterized in terms of a pair of smooth functions satisfying a system of two natural partial differential equations. The approach to the study of minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type in
\mathbb{E}^{4}_{2}
is based on the introducing of special geometric parameters which are called canonical parameters.

A representation of a minimal Lorentzian surface was given by M.P. Dussan and M. Magid in
[
8
]
where they solved the Björling problem for timelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
constructing a special normal frame and a split-complex representation formula. The Björling problem for timelike surfaces in the Lorentz-Minkowski spaces
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
and
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is solved in
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
, respectively. Spinor representation of Lorentzian surfaces in the pseudo-Euclidean 4-space with neutral metric is given in
[
3
]
.
In
[
14
]
, V. Patty gave a generalized Weierstrass representation of a minimal Lorentzian surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
using spinors and Lorentz numbers (also known as para-complex, split-complex, double or hyperbolic numbers) thus extending the Weierstrass representation of a minimal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
given by J. Konderak
[
13
]
.

A special Weierstrass representation, called canonical Weierstrass representation, for a maximal spacelike surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
is obtained in
[
12
]
. These Weierstrass formulas give explicitly the solutions to the system of PDEs describing maximal spacelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
in terms of two holomorphic functions. The explicit solving of the system of PDEs and a relation between maximal spacelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
and maximal spacelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}
is given in
[
11
]
- a paper that is a follow-up to a series of articles on explicit solving of the system of natural PDEs of minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and minimal spacelike surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}

[
9
,
10
]
.

In the present paper we study minimal Lorentzian surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
. In Section
3
we find a Weierstrass representation with respect to isothermal parameters (Theorem
3.3
) of any minimal Lorentzian surface with two-dimensional first normal space. The Weierstrass representation
formula given in Theorem
3.3
is equivalent to the representation formula in
[
8
]
.
But the formula in
[
8
]
uses Lorentz numbers (split-complex numbers), while our formula gives a representation in terms of real functions of one real variable which is much more convenient. Moreover, this Weierstrass
formula gives a clear analytic form of the surface.

Further, we obtain a Weierstrass representation
formula with respect to canonical parameters of any minimal Lorentzian surface of general type which describes locally all these surfaces in
terms of four real functions (Theorem
4.3
).
Using the canonical Weierstrass representation we solve explicitly the system of
PDEs that describes minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, expressing any solution to this system by means of four real functions of one variable (Theorem
4.4
).

In the last section, using the canonical Weierstrass representation formula we give examples of minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type parametrized by canonical parameters.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
be the four-dimensional pseudo-Euclidean space with the standard flat
metric
\langle\,,\,\rangle
of signature
(2,2)
, also known as pseudo-Euclidean space with neutral metric, and consider a minimal Lorentzian surface in it. A local parametrization of such
surfaces is given by the following fundamental result:

Theorem A
[
5
]
.

Let
S
be a minimal Lorentzian surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
.
Then it can be locally parametrized in the form

\Psi(u,v)=\beta(u)+\theta(v),

where
\beta
and
\theta
are null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, such that

\langle\beta^{\prime}(u),\theta^{\prime}(v)\rangle\neq 0
for every
u,\,v
.

Concerning generalizations of this theorem for more general situations see
[
2
]
, p. 53 and
[
6
]
.

The Gauss curvature
K
and the curvature of the normal connection (the normal curvature)
\varkappa
of any minimal surface in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and any spacelike maximal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
satisfy the inequality
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}\geq 0
. The equality case characterizes the so-called
super-conformal
surfaces.
Minimal Lorentz surfaces in the pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
can be divided into three basic classes:

•

surfaces characterized by
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0
;

•

surfaces characterized by
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}=0
;

•

surfaces characterized by
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}<0
.

In the case
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0
, Lorenzian minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
have similar properties to minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
or spacelike maximal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}

[
15
]
.
Minimal Lorentzian surfaces from the first class, i.e. satisfying the inequality
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0
are studied in
[
1
]
. In the special case when the first normal space is one-dimensional at each point, the minimal surface is either a degenerate hyperplane, a flat umbilic surface, a quasi-umbilic surface, or lies in a non-degenerate hyperplane. In the case when the first normal space is two-dimensional, the surfaces are called in
[
1
]

minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type
, and we will follow this terminology. Note that minimal surfaces satisfying the inequality
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}<0
do not exist neither in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
nor in the family of spacelike maximal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
and they need a different approach to be studied with.

In what follows we consider minimal Lorentzian surface of general type, i.e. at any point
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0

and the first normal space is two-dimensional.

Let the minimal Lorentzian surface of general type

S:\Psi=\Psi(u,v),\;(u,v)\in\mathcal{D},\,\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

be parametrized by isothermal parameters, i.e.

E=-G
,
F=0
. Then the coefficients of the first fundamental form are expressed as

E=f^{2}(u,v),\;F=0,\;G=-f^{2}(u,v)
for some positive function
f(u,v)
.
Denote by
x
,
y
the unit tangent vector fields in the
directions of
\Psi_{u}
, resp.
\Psi_{v}
, i.e.
x=\displaystyle{\frac{\Psi_{u}}{f}}
,
y=\displaystyle{\frac{\Psi_{v}}{f}}
.
Obviously,
\langle x,x\rangle=1
,
\langle y,y\rangle=-1
,
\langle x,y\rangle=0
.
We choose a local normal frame field
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
such that
\langle e_{1},e_{1}\rangle=1
,
\langle e_{2},e_{2}\rangle=-1
,
\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=0
and define functions
a(u,v),b(u,v),c(u,v),d(u,v)
by

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(x,x)=ae_{1}+be_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(x,y)=ce_{1}+de_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(y,y)=ae_{1}+be_{2},\end{array}

where
\sigma
is the second fundamental tensor. Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be the shape operators corresponding to
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, respectively. Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054ptA_{1}(x)=ax-cy;&A_{2}(x)=-bx+dy;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptA_{1}(y)=cx-ay;&A_{2}(y)=-dx+by.\end{array}

Using the above formulas and the Ricci equation we obtain that the Gauss curvature
K
and the curvature of the normal connection
\varkappa
are expressed by

(2.1)

K=b^{2}-a^{2}+c^{2}-d^{2},

(2.2)

\varkappa=2(bc-ad).

In
[
1
]
it is proved that in a neighbourhood of any point of a minimal Lorentzian surface of
general type we can introduce a special orthonormal frame field

\{x,y,n_{1},n_{2}\}
such that the vector fields
\sigma(x,x)
and
\sigma(x,y)
are collinear to
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, respectively, i.e.

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(x,x)=\nu n_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(x,y)=\qquad\mu n_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(y,y)=\nu n_{1},\end{array}

where
\langle x,x\rangle=1
,
\langle y,y\rangle=-1
,

\langle n_{1},n_{1}\rangle=\varepsilon
,
\langle n_{2},n_{2}\rangle=-\varepsilon

(
\varepsilon=\pm 1
) and
\nu\mu
never vanishes. The tangent directions determined by the tangent vector fields
x
and
y

are called
canonical directions
of the surface.

It follows from
(
2.1
) and (
2.2
) that the following equality holds:

(2.3)

K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}=(\mu^{2}-\nu^{2})^{2}.

Since we are studying surfaces for which
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0
, we assume that
\mu^{2}-\nu^{2}\neq 0
.

Moreover, if

(2.4)

E^{2}=G^{2}=\frac{1}{|\mu^{2}-\nu^{2}|},

the parameters of the surface are called
canonical

[
1
]
. We shall use the following

Theorem B
[
1
]
.

The Gauss curvature
K
and the curvature of the normal connection
\varkappa

of any minimal Lorentzian surface of general type in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}

in canonical parameters satisfy the system

(2.5)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\root 4 \of{K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}}\Delta^{h}\ln(K^{2}-\varkappa^{2})=8K\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\root 4 \of{K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}}\Delta^{h}\ln\frac{K+\varepsilon\varkappa}{K-\varepsilon\varkappa}=4\varepsilon\varkappa\end{array}

where
\Delta^{h}
is the hyperbolic Laplacian,
\varepsilon=\pm 1
. Conversely, for any solution
(K(u,v),\varkappa(u,v))
to this
system with non-vanishing functions
K
and
\varkappa
, satisfying
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0
,
there exists a unique minimal Lorentzian surface of general type with Gauss curvature
K
and
curvature of the normal connection
\varkappa
.
Moreover, the parameters
(u,v)
are canonical.

3.
The Weierstrass-type formula

The following statement is a trivial consequence of Theorem A:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\beta
and
\theta
be null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, such that

\langle\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0
.
Then

S:\Psi(u,v)=\beta(u+v)+\theta(u-v)

is a minimal Lorentzian surface in isothermal parameters. Conversely, every
minimal Lorentzian surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
can be parametrized locally in this way.

Now let

\beta(u)=\big(\beta_{1}(u),\beta_{2}(u),\beta_{3}(u),\beta_{4}(u)\big),\quad u\in J\subset\mathbb{R}

be a null curve in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
, i.e.

(3.1)

(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}+(\beta_{2}^{\prime})^{2}-(\beta_{3}^{\prime})^{2}-(\beta_{4}^{\prime})^{2}=0.

First we suppose that
(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}=(\beta_{3}^{\prime})^{2}=(\beta_{4}^{\prime})^{2}
in an interval
J_{0}\subset J
. Then
(\beta_{2}^{\prime})^{2}=(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}
and hence, there exists a function
f(u),\;u\in J_{0}
such that

\beta^{\prime}(u)=f(u)\big(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\big),\quad\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1,\;i=1,2,3.

Now assume that
(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}-(\beta_{3}^{\prime})^{2}\neq 0
at a point
u_{0}\in J
. Then the function

(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}-(\beta_{3}^{\prime})^{2}
doesn’t vanish in an interval
J_{0}\subset J
,
u_{0}\in J_{0}
.
We introduce in
J_{0}
the following functions:

f=\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{1}+\beta^{\prime}_{3}}{2},\qquad g=\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{2}+\beta^{\prime}_{4}}{\beta^{\prime}_{1}+\beta^{\prime}_{3}},\qquad h=\frac{-\beta^{\prime}_{2}+\beta^{\prime}_{4}}{\beta^{\prime}_{1}+\beta^{\prime}_{3}}.

Hence,

\beta^{\prime}_{2}=f(g-h),\qquad\beta^{\prime}_{4}=f(g+h).

Note that the function
f
doesn’t vanish and
g
,
h
are well defined because
(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}-(\beta_{3}^{\prime})^{2}\neq 0
.
Using (
3.1
) we get also

\beta^{\prime}_{1}=f(1+gh),\qquad\beta^{\prime}_{3}=f(1-gh).

Finally, we obtain that if
(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}-(\beta_{3}^{\prime})^{2}
doesn’t vanish in
J_{0}
, then

(3.2)

\beta^{\prime}(u)=f(u)\big(1+g(u)h(u),g(u)-h(u),1-g(u)h(u),g(u)+h(u)\big),\quad u\in J_{0}.

If
(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}-(\beta_{4}^{\prime})^{2}\neq 0
at a point
u_{0}\in J
, then there exists
J_{0}\subset J
such that the function

(\beta_{1}^{\prime})^{2}-(\beta_{4}^{\prime})^{2}\neq 0
in
J_{0}
. In such case we can introduce in
J_{0}
functions:

f=\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{1}+\beta^{\prime}_{4}}{2},\qquad g=\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{2}+\beta^{\prime}_{3}}{\beta^{\prime}_{1}+\beta^{\prime}_{4}},\qquad h=\frac{-\beta^{\prime}_{2}+\beta^{\prime}_{3}}{\beta^{\prime}_{1}+\beta^{\prime}_{4}}.

Using (
3.1
) we get

\beta^{\prime}_{1}=f(1+gh),\qquad\beta^{\prime}_{2}=f(g-h),\qquad\beta^{\prime}_{3}=f(g+h),\qquad\beta^{\prime}_{4}=f(1-gh).

So, in this case we obtain

(3.3)

\beta^{\prime}(u)=f(u)\big(1+g(u)h(u),g(u)-h(u),g(u)+h(u),1-g(u)h(u)\big),\quad u\in J_{0}.

Now, we substitute

\tilde{g}=\frac{1-h}{1+h},\qquad\tilde{h}=\frac{1-g}{1+g}

and get

g-h=\frac{2(\tilde{g}-\tilde{h})}{(1+\tilde{h})(1+\tilde{g})},\qquad g+h=\frac{2(1-\tilde{g}\tilde{h})}{(1+\tilde{h})(1+\tilde{g})},

1+gh=\frac{2(1+\tilde{g}\tilde{h})}{(1+\tilde{h})(1+\tilde{g})},\qquad 1-gh=\frac{2(\tilde{g}+\tilde{h})}{(1+\tilde{h})(1+\tilde{g})}.

Setting

\tilde{f}=\frac{2f}{(1+\tilde{h})(1+\tilde{g})}

we get

\beta^{\prime}(u)=\tilde{f}(u)\big(1+\tilde{g}(u)\tilde{h}(u),\tilde{g}(u)-\tilde{h}(u),1-\tilde{g}(u)\tilde{h}(u),\tilde{g}(u)+\tilde{h}(u)\big).

Hence, omitting points at which
1+h=0
or
1+g=0
we can transform (
3.3
) in (
3.2
).

Consequently, we have proved the following statement.

Proposition 3.2
.

Each null curve
\beta=\big(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4}\big)
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
can be parametrized locally (in a neighbourhood of a given point) in such a way that

(1)
\beta^{\prime}(u)=f(u)\big(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\big),\quad\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1,\;i=1,2,3;

or

(2)
\beta^{\prime}(u)=f(u)\big(1+g(u)h(u),g(u)-h(u),1-g(u)h(u),g(u)+h(u)\big)

for some smooth functions
f(u)
,
g(u)
,
h(u)
, such that
f(u)
does not vanish.

In what follows we use Propositions
3.1

and
3.2
to find a Weierstrass representation of a minimal Lorentzian surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
with two-dimensional first normal space.

Let
S
be a minimal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
. According to Proposition
3.1
,
S
can be parametrized in isothermal parameters by

S:\Psi(u,v)=\beta(u+v)+\theta(u-v),

where
\beta
and
\theta
are null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
such that
\langle\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0
.

(i) Let us suppose that both
\beta
and
\theta
are of type (1) in Proposition
3.2
, i.e.

\beta^{\prime}=f_{1}\big(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\big),\qquad\theta^{\prime}=f_{2}\big(1,\varepsilon_{4},\varepsilon_{5},\varepsilon_{6}\big),\quad\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1,\;i=1,\dots,6.

We denote by
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
the constant vectors
\big(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\big)
and
\big(1,\varepsilon_{4},\varepsilon_{5},\varepsilon_{6}\big)
, respectively.
Then

\beta^{\prime}(u+v)=f_{1}(u+v)\,c_{1},\qquad\theta^{\prime}(u-v)=f_{2}(u-v)\,c_{2}.

Hence, the tangent space of
S
is spanned by the vector fields

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{u}=f_{1}(u+v)\,c_{1}+f_{2}(u-v)\,c_{2},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{v}=f_{1}(u+v)\,c_{1}-f_{2}(u-v)\,c_{2}\end{array}

and we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{uu}=f^{\prime}_{1}(u+v)\,c_{1}+f^{\prime}_{2}(u-v)\,c_{2},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{uv}=f^{\prime}_{1}(u+v)\,c_{1}-f^{\prime}_{2}(u-v)\,c_{2},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{vv}=f^{\prime}_{1}(u+v)\,c_{1}+f^{\prime}_{2}(u-v)\,c_{2}.\end{array}

The last three equalities show that
\sigma(\Psi_{u},\Psi_{u})=0
,
\sigma(\Psi_{u},\Psi_{v})=0
,
\sigma(\Psi_{v},\Psi_{v})=0
, i.e.
the surface
S
has vanishing second fundamental tensor
\sigma
. Hence,
S
is part of a plane.

(ii) Let
\beta
be of type (1) and
\theta
be of type (2) in Proposition
3.2
, i.e.

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\beta^{\prime}=f_{1}\,c,\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\theta^{\prime}=f_{2}\big(1+gh,g-h,1-gh,g+h\big),\end{array}

where
c=\big(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\big),\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1
,
\beta^{\prime}
depends on
u+v
,
\theta^{\prime}
depends on
u-v
.
Hence, the tangent space of
S
is spanned by

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{u}=f_{1}(u+v)\,c+\theta^{\prime}(u-v),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{v}=f_{1}(u+v)\,c-\theta^{\prime}(u-v),\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{uu}=f^{\prime}_{1}(u+v)\,c+\theta^{\prime\prime}(u-v),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{uv}=f^{\prime}_{1}(u+v)\,c-\theta^{\prime\prime}(u-v),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{vv}=f^{\prime}_{1}(u+v)\,c+\theta^{\prime\prime}(u-v).\end{array}

The above equalities imply that
\sigma(\Psi_{u},\Psi_{u})=-\sigma(\Psi_{u},\Psi_{v})=\sigma(\Psi_{v},\Psi_{v})
, since
f^{\prime}_{1}(u+v)\,c
is a tangent vector field. Hence, the first normal space of the surface is one-dimensional.

Consequently, when the first normal space of the surface is two-dimensional, the null curves
\beta

and
\theta
are both of type (2) in Proposition
3.2
. So, we have:

Theorem 3.3
.

Up to change of the parameters any minimal Lorentzian surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
with two-dimensional first normal space has locally
a Weierstrass type representation in isothermal parameters

\Psi(u,v)=\beta(u+v)+\theta(u-v)

for some null curves
\beta
and
\theta
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\beta^{\prime}(t)=f_{1}(t)\big(1+g_{1}(t)h_{1}(t),g_{1}(t)-h_{1}(t),1-g_{1}(t)h_{1}(t),g_{1}(t)+h_{1}(t)\big);\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\theta^{\prime}(s)=f_{2}(s)\big(1+g_{2}(s)h_{2}(s),g_{2}(s)-h_{2}(s),1-g_{2}(s)h_{2}(s),g_{2}(s)+h_{2}(s)\big),\end{array}

and
\langle\beta^{\prime}(t),\theta^{\prime}(s)\rangle\neq 0
.

4.
Minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type and their canonical Weierstrass representation

Let
S:\Psi=\Psi(u,v),\;(u,v)\in\mathcal{D},\,\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a minimal Lorentzian surface of general type.
According to Theorem
3.3
,
S
is parametrized by

(4.1)

\Psi(u,v)=\beta(u+v)+\theta(u-v),

where
\beta
and
\theta
have the form

(4.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\beta^{\prime}(t)=f_{1}(t)\big(1+g_{1}(t)h_{1}(t),g_{1}(t)-h_{1}(t),1-g_{1}(t)h_{1}(t),g_{1}(t)+h_{1}(t)\big),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\theta^{\prime}(s)=f_{2}(s)\big(1+g_{2}(s)h_{2}(s),g_{2}(s)-h_{2}(s),1-g_{2}(s)h_{2}(s),g_{2}(s)+h_{2}(s)\big).\end{array}

Then the tangent space is spanned by

(4.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{u}=\beta^{\prime}(u+v)+\theta^{\prime}(u-v),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi_{v}=\beta^{\prime}(u+v)-\theta^{\prime}(u-v),\end{array}

and the coefficients
E
,
F
, and
G
of the first fundamental form of
S
are

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptE=4f_{1}(u+v)f_{2}(u-v)\big(g_{1}(u+v)-g_{2}(u-v)\big)\big(h_{1}(u+v)-h_{2}(u-v)\big),\\
\vskip 5.69054ptF=0,\\
\vskip 5.69054ptG=-E.\end{array}

We consider the orthonormal tangent frame field
x=\displaystyle{\frac{\Psi_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}}
,
y=\displaystyle{\frac{\Psi_{v}}{\sqrt{-G}}}
.
Note that (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) imply

K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}=(a^{2}-b^{2})^{2}+(c^{2}-d^{2})^{2}+2(a^{2}-b^{2})(c^{2}-d^{2})-4(ac-bd)^{2}

or equivalently

(4.4)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\vskip 5.69054ptK^{2}-\varkappa^{2}=&\langle\sigma(x,x),\sigma(x,x)\rangle^{2}+\langle\sigma(x,y),\sigma(x,y)\rangle^{2}\\
&+2\langle\sigma(x,x),\sigma(x,x)\rangle\langle\sigma(x,y),\sigma(x,y)\rangle-4\langle\sigma(x,x),\sigma(x,y)\rangle^{2}.\end{array}

Using (
4.3
) we get

(4.5)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(x,x)=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{E}\left(\beta^{\prime\prime}+\theta^{\prime\prime}-\frac{\langle\beta^{\prime\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle}{\langle\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle}\,\beta^{\prime}-\frac{\langle\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}\rangle}{\langle\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle}\,\theta^{\prime}\right)};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\sigma(x,y)=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{E}\left(\beta^{\prime\prime}-\theta^{\prime\prime}-\frac{\langle\beta^{\prime\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle}{\langle\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle}\,\beta^{\prime}+\frac{\langle\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}\rangle}{\langle\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle}\,\theta^{\prime}\right)}.\end{array}

On the other hand, from (
4.2
) it follows that

(4.6)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\langle\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle\beta^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}\rangle=-4(f_{1})^{2}g_{1}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle\theta^{\prime\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}\rangle=-4(f_{2})^{2}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle\theta^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime}\rangle=2f_{1}f_{2}^{\prime}(g_{2}-g_{1})(h_{2}-h_{1})+2f_{1}f_{2}((g_{2}-g_{1})h_{2}^{\prime}+(h_{2}-h_{1})g_{2}^{\prime});\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\langle\beta^{\prime\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle=2f_{2}f_{1}^{\prime}(g_{2}-g_{1})(h_{2}-h_{1})+2f_{1}f_{2}((h_{1}-h_{2})g_{1}^{\prime}+(g_{1}-g_{2})h_{1}^{\prime}).\\
\end{array}

Now, using (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), by a straightforward calculation we obtain

(4.7)

K^{2}\!-\!\varkappa^{2}=\frac{g_{1}^{\prime}(u\!+\!v)g_{2}^{\prime}(u\!-\!v)h_{1}^{\prime}(u\!+\!v)h_{2}^{\prime}(u\!-\!v)}{(f_{1}(u\!+\!v))^{2}(f_{2}(u\!-\!v))^{2}\big(g_{1}(u\!+\!v)-g_{2}(u\!-\!v)\big)^{4}\big(h_{1}(u\!+\!v)-h_{2}(u\!-\!v)\big)^{4}}.

Note that the condition
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}\neq 0
and (
4.7
) imply that none of the
functions
g_{1}^{\prime}
,
g_{2}^{\prime}
,
h_{1}^{\prime}
,
h_{2}^{\prime}
can vanish. Moreover, since
K^{2}-\varkappa^{2}>0
for minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type, then
g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}>0
. Hence, as a consequence of Theorem
3.3
we obtain the Weierstrass representation of minimal Lorentzian surfaces of general type given below.

Theorem 4.1
.

Up to change of the parameters any minimal Lorentzian surface of general type in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
has locally
a Weierstrass type representation in isothermal parameters

\Psi(u,v)=\beta(u+v)+\theta(u-v)

for some null curves
\beta
and
\theta
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\beta^{\prime}(t)=f_{1}(t)\big(1+g_{1}(t)h_{1}(t),g_{1}(t)-h_{1}(t),1-g_{1}(t)h_{1}(t),g_{1}(t)+h_{1}(t)\big);\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\theta^{\prime}(s)=f_{2}(s)\big(1+g_{2}(s)h_{2}(s),g_{2}(s)-h_{2}(s),1-g_{2}(s)h_{2}(s),g_{2}(s)+h_{2}(s)\big),\end{array}

and
f_{1}f_{2}(g_{1}-g_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2})\neq 0
,
g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}>0
everywhere.

Further, we shall obtain a condition for the surface to be parametrized by canonical parameters. A necessary condition for a canonical parametrization of a minimal surface of general type is
\sigma(\Psi_{u},\Psi_{u})
and
\sigma(\Psi_{u},\Psi_{v})
to be orthogonal
[
1
]
. It follows from (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\langle\sigma(\Psi_{u},\Psi_{u}),\sigma(\Psi_{u},\Psi_{v})\rangle\\
\vskip 5.69054pt=\langle\beta^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}\rangle(u+v)-\langle\theta^{\prime\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime}\rangle(u-v)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt=-4(f_{1}(u+v))^{2}g_{1}^{\prime}(u+v)h_{1}^{\prime}(u+v)+4(f_{2}(u-v))^{2}g_{2}^{\prime}(u-v)h_{2}^{\prime}(u-v).\end{array}

Hence, for a canonical parametrization we must have

(f_{1}(u+v))^{2}g_{1}^{\prime}(u+v)h_{1}^{\prime}(u+v)=(f_{2}(u-v))^{2}g_{2}^{\prime}(u-v)h_{2}^{\prime}(u-v)=const\neq 0.

Suppose that

f_{1}=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{|g_{1}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}|}},\qquad f_{2}=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{|g_{2}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}|}}.

Then, using (
2.3
) and (
4.7
) we see that

\left|\mu^{2}-\nu^{2}\right|=16\frac{g_{1}^{\prime}(u+v)h_{1}^{\prime}(u+v)g_{2}^{\prime}(u-v)h_{2}^{\prime}(u-v)}{\big(g_{1}(u+v)-g_{2}(u-v)\big)^{2}\big(h_{1}(u+v)-h_{2}(u-v)\big)^{2}}.

So, (
2.4
) also holds and hence, the surface is parametrized by canonical parameters.
Consequently, we have proved:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\beta
and
\theta
be null curves in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
satisfying

(4.8)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{|g_{1}^{\prime}(t)h_{1}^{\prime}(t)|}}\big(1\!+\!g_{1}(t)h_{1}(t),g_{1}(t)\!-\!h_{1}(t),1\!-\!g_{1}(t)h_{1}(t),g_{1}(t)\!+\!h_{1}(t)\big)\\
\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{|g_{2}^{\prime}(s)h_{2}^{\prime}(s)|}}\big(1\!+\!g_{2}(s)h_{2}(s),g_{2}(s)\!-\!h_{2}(s),1\!-\!g_{2}(s)h_{2}(s),g_{2}(s)\!+\!h_{2}(s)\big)\end{array}

with
(g_{1}-g_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2})\neq 0
,
g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}>0
. Then

(4.9)

\Psi(u,v)=\beta(u+v)+\theta(u-v)

is a minimal Lorentzian surface of general type in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
parametrized by canonical parameters.

Now we want to see whether it is possible to change the parameters of a minimal surface
given in a Weierstrass form so that the obtained representation be canonical. Consider a minimal surface given by (
4.1
), such that the null
curves
\beta
and
\theta
satisfy (
4.2
) and
f_{1}f_{2}(g_{1}-g_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2})\neq 0
,
g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}>0
. We look
for a change of the parameters

u=p(\bar{u}+\bar{v})+q(\bar{u}-\bar{v})\ ,\qquad v=p(\bar{u}+\bar{v})-q(\bar{u}-\bar{v})

for some functions
p
,
q
of one variable, such that the function

\overline{\Psi}(\bar{u},\bar{v})=\Psi\big(p(\bar{u}+\bar{v})+q(\bar{u}-\bar{v}),p(\bar{u}+\bar{v})-q(\bar{u}-\bar{v})\big)

has the form

(4.10)

\overline{\Psi}(\bar{u},\bar{v})=\bar{\beta}(\bar{u}+\bar{v})+\bar{\theta}(\bar{u}-\bar{v})

for some functions
\bar{\beta}
and
\bar{\theta}
satisfying

(4.11)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\bar{\beta}^{\prime}(\bar{u})\!=\!\frac{1}{4\sqrt{|\bar{g}_{1}^{\prime}(\!\bar{u}\!)\bar{h}_{1}^{\prime}(\!\bar{u}\!)|}}\big(\!1\!+\!\bar{g}_{1}(\bar{u})\bar{h}_{1}(\bar{u}),\bar{g}_{1}(\bar{u})\!-\!\bar{h}_{1}(\bar{u}),1\!-\!\bar{g}_{1}(\bar{u})\bar{h}_{1}(\bar{u}),\bar{g}_{1}(\bar{u})\!+\!\bar{h}_{1}(\bar{u})\!\big),\\
\displaystyle\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(\bar{v})\!=\!\frac{1}{4\sqrt{|\bar{g}_{2}^{\prime}(\bar{v})\bar{h}_{2}^{\prime}(\bar{v})|}}\big(\!1\!+\!\bar{g}_{2}(\bar{v})\bar{h}_{2}(\bar{v}),\bar{g}_{2}(\bar{v})\!-\!\bar{h}_{2}(\bar{v}),1\!-\!\bar{g}_{2}(\bar{v})\bar{h}_{2}(\bar{v}),\bar{g}_{2}(\bar{v})\!+\!\bar{h}_{2}(\bar{v})\!\big),\end{array}

and as a consequence of Proposition
4.2
this
will be a representation of the surface in canonical parameters. It
can be seen that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\overline{\Psi}_{\bar{u}}=(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})\Psi_{u}+(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})\Psi_{v}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\overline{\Psi}_{\bar{v}}=(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})\Psi_{u}+(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})\Psi_{v}\end{array}

imply

\bar{\beta}^{\prime}(z)=2\beta^{\prime}(p(z))p^{\prime}(z),\qquad\bar{\theta}^{\prime}(z)=2\theta^{\prime}(q(z))q^{\prime}(z).

Hence, we derive

(4.12)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(p^{\prime}(z))^{2}=\frac{1}{8|f_{1}(p(z))|\sqrt{|g_{1}^{\prime}(p(z))h_{1}^{\prime}(p(z))|}},\quad\bar{g}_{1}(z)=g_{1}(p(z)),\;\;\bar{h}_{1}(z)=h_{1}(p(z));\\
\displaystyle(q^{\prime}(z))^{2}=\frac{1}{8|f_{2}(q(z))|\sqrt{|g_{2}^{\prime}(q(z))h_{2}^{\prime}(q(z))|}},\quad\bar{g}_{2}(z)=g_{2}(q(z)),\;\;\bar{h}_{2}(z)=h_{2}(q(z)).\end{array}

Consequently, we may determine not only the change of the parameters, i.e. the functions
p(z)
,
q(z)
, but also
a canonical representation of the given surface. Finally, we proved:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
S
be a minimal Lorentzian surface of general type in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
parametrized by (
4.1
),
such that the null curves
\beta
and
\theta
satisfy (
4.2
),
where
f_{1}f_{2}(g_{1}-g_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2})\neq 0
,
g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}>0
. Then,
S
has a canonical representation
(
4.10
) where
\bar{\beta}
and
\bar{\theta}
are null curves satisfying (
4.11
).
This canonical representation is determined by equations (
4.12
).

We can also find the Gauss curvature
K
and the curvature of the normal connection
\varkappa

of a minimal Lorentzian surface of general type in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
given in canonical
parameters. Namely, for the surface (
4.9
), where the null curves

\beta
and
\theta
are given by (
4.8
), we obtain:

(4.13)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.53581pt\displaystyle K=-8\frac{\sqrt{g_{1}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}}\big((h_{1}-h_{2})^{2}g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}+(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}\big)}{(g_{1}-g_{2})^{3}(h_{1}-h_{2})^{3}};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\varkappa=-8\sqrt{g_{1}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}}\left|\frac{(h_{1}-h_{2})^{2}g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}-(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}}{(g_{1}-g_{2})^{3}(h_{1}-h_{2})^{3}}\right|,\end{array}

where
g_{1},\,h_{1}
depend on
u+v
and

g_{2},\,h_{2}
depend on
u-v
. Hence, using Theorem B we obtain

Theorem 4.4
.

The system of partial differential equations (
2.5
) has solutions of type
(
4.13
)
for arbitrary smooth functions
g_{1},g_{2},h_{1},h_{2}
, such that
g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}h_{2}^{\prime}>0

and the differences
g_{1}-g_{2}
and

5.
Examples

In this section, using Theorem
3.3
we give some examples of
minimal Lorentzian surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
. Moreover,
according to Theorem
4.1
they are of general type and
according to Proposition
4.2
they are in canonical
parametrization.

Example 5.1
.

Let
g_{1}(t)=t+1
,
h_{1}(t)=t
,
g_{2}(s)=s
,
h_{2}(s)=s+1
. Using (
4.8
) we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{4}\big(1+t(t+1);1;1-t(t+1);2t+1\big);\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{4}\big(1+s(s+1);-1;1-s(s+1);2s+1\big).\end{array}

Then up to constant vectors the null curves
\beta
and
\theta
are given by

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\beta(t)=\frac{1}{24}\big(2t^{3}+3t^{2}+6t;6t;-2t^{3}-3t^{2}+6t;6t^{2}+6t\big);\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\theta(s)=\frac{1}{24}\big(2s^{3}+3s^{2}+6s;-6s;-2s^{3}-3s^{2}+6s;6s^{2}+6s\big).\end{array}

Applying (
4.9
) we obtain the surface

\Psi(u,v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}\big(2u^{3}+3u^{2}+3v^{2}+6u(v^{2}+1)\big)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{v}{2}\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}\big(-2u^{3}-3u^{2}-3v^{2}-6u(v^{2}-1)\big)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(u+u^{2}+v^{2}\big)\ .\end{array}\right.

In this example,
(g_{1}(u+v)-g_{2}(u-v))(h_{1}(u+v)-h_{2}(u-v))=(2v+1)(2v-1)
, so we assume that
v\neq\pm\frac{1}{2}
.

According to (
4.13
) the Gauss curvature
K
and the curvature of the normal connection
\varkappa
are expressed by:

K=\frac{16(1+4v^{2})}{(1-4v^{2})^{3}}\ ;\qquad\varkappa=-\left|\frac{64v}{(1-4v^{2})^{3}}\right|\ .

Example 5.2
.

Let
g_{1}(t)=t
,
h_{1}(t)=t
,
g_{2}(s)=e^{s}
,
h_{2}(s)=-e^{-s}
. Using (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) we obtain the surface

\Psi(u,v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}(u+v)^{3}+\frac{1}{4}(u+v)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sinh(u-v)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle-\frac{1}{12}(u+v)^{3}+\frac{1}{4}(3u-v)\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cosh(u-v)+\frac{1}{4}(u+v)^{2}.\end{array}\right.

In this example,
(g_{1}(u+v)-g_{2}(u-v))(h_{1}(u+v)-h_{2}(u-v))=(u+v-e^{u-v})(u+v+e^{-(u-v)})
, so we assume that the parameters

u
and
v
satisfy
u+v\neq e^{u-v}
,
u+v\neq-e^{-(u-v)}
, or equivalently
(u+v)^{2}-1\neq 2(u+v)\sinh(u-v)
.

The Gauss curvature
K
and the curvature of the normal connection
\varkappa
are:

K=-16\frac{\cosh(u-v)\big((u+v)^{2}+1\big)}{\big((u+v)^{2}-1-2(u+v)\sinh(u-v)\big)^{3}}\ ,

\varkappa=-16\left|\frac{\big((u+v)^{2}-1\big)\sinh(u-v)+2(u+v)}{\big((u+v)^{2}-1-2(u+v)\sinh(u-v)\big)^{3}}\right|\ .

Note, that according to Theorem
4.4
, the functions
K
and
\varkappa
from Example
5.1

and Example
5.2
give solutions to the system of partial differential equations (
2.5
).

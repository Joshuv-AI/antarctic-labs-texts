Dark matter produced from neutrinos Preprint: ULB-TH/21-14
Marco Hufnagel, and Xun-Jie Xu

Abstract
In the presence of interactions between neutrinos and dark matter (DM), DM can potentially be produced via freeze-in from the neutrino sector. We investigate the implications of such a scenario for the evolution of both DM and neutrinos in the early Universe, and show that the future cosmic neutrino detection experiment PTOLEMY might be sensitive to neutrino signals that originate from DM annihilation in this model.

Framework

In this work, we extend the Standard Model (SM) by a dark sector that includes a DM particle
\chi
as well as a mediator
\phi
. Within this setup, we then study the production of DM from SM neutrinos via reactions of the form
\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}
, which can happen via either
t
- or
s
-channel mediator exchange.

Lagrangians

To implement the
\boldsymbol{t}
-channel
scenario, we assume that the mediator is a complex scalar boson
\phi
, which couples to the SM neutrinos and the DM particle via the interaction Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{t}\supset y_{\chi\nu}{\sum}_{i}\phi\bar{\chi}P_{L}\nu_{i}+{\rm h.c.}\;,

(1)

where the left-handed projector
P_{L}=(1-\gamma^{5})/2
ensures that only left-handed neutrinos are participating in the interactions, and the sum
\sum_{i}
goes over all participating neutrino flavors. For now, we do not make any assumption regarding the number of active neutrinos that take part in the interaction, but we will later limit our considerations to only one participating neutrino at a time. Models like this are mainly motivated by previous studies on DM-neutrino interactions from a neutrino portal
Orlofsky:2021mmy
;
Berryman:2017twh
;
Becker:2018rve
;
Batell:2017rol
;
Falkowski:2009yz
, which often feature a scalar that is charged under a hidden symmetry in order to guarantee DM stability.

While the Lagrangian in eq. (
1
) does enable the intended reaction
\nu\bar{\nu}\leftrightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}
, it also induces the additional production process
\nu\bar{\nu}\leftrightarrow\phi\phi^{*}
, which might lead to secondary DM production via the decay of the mediator, as well as the two (inverse) decay channels
\phi\leftrightarrow\chi\bar{\nu}
if
m_{\phi}>m_{\chi}
and
\chi\leftrightarrow\phi\bar{\nu}
if
m_{\phi}<m_{\chi}
. In order for
\chi
to be stable, we thus enforce the additional condition
m_{\phi}>m_{\chi}
, which renders the latter decay channel irrelevant. Consequently, there remain three relevant processes in this scenarios, which correspond to the Feynman diagrams that are depicted in fig.

For the
\boldsymbol{s}
-channel

{\cal L}_{s}\supset y_{\chi}\phi_{\mu}\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\chi+y_{\nu}{\sum}_{i}\phi_{\mu}\bar{\nu}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\nu_{i}\;.

(2)

Such interactions frequently occur in models with kinetic mixing between the SM gauge bosons and the one of a new

Alternatively, it would also be possible to consider a scalar mediator with lepton-number violating interactions, analogous to the Majoron

Matrix elements

Given these two Lagrangians, the squared matrix elements for the relevant production processes can be calculated with

In the case of

-channel

\chi

\phi

\displaystyle|{\cal M}^{(t)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}|^{2}=N_{\nu}y_{\chi\nu}^{4}\left(\frac{t-m_{\chi}^{2}}{t-m_{\phi}^{2}}\right)^{2}\;\quad\text{and}\quad|{\cal M}^{(t)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\phi\phi^{*}}|^{2}=N_{\nu}y_{\chi\nu}^{4}\frac{tu-m_{\phi}^{4}}{(t-m_{\chi}^{2})^{2}}\;,

(3)

respectively. Here,
t=(p_{3}-p_{1})^{2}
,
s=(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}
, and

are the Mandelstam variables, and

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(t)}_{\phi\rightarrow\chi\bar{\nu}}=\frac{N_{\nu}y_{\chi\nu}^{2}\left(m_{\phi}^{2}-m_{\chi}^{2}\right){}^{2}}{16\pi m_{\phi}^{3}}\;.

(4)

Given the Lagrangian from eq. (
2

-

\displaystyle|{\cal M}^{(s)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}|^{2}=\frac{4N_{\nu}y_{\chi}^{2}y_{\nu}^{2}\left(u-m_{\chi}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(s-m_{\phi}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(m_{\phi}\Gamma_{\phi}^{(s)}\right)^{2}}

(5)

with the total decay width
\Gamma_{\phi}^{(s)}
of
\phi

m_{\phi}<2m_{\chi}

and is given by

\Gamma^{(s)}_{\phi}=\Gamma^{(s)}_{\phi\rightarrow\nu\bar{\nu}}=\frac{N_{\nu}y_{\nu}^{2}}{24\pi}m_{\phi}\;.

(6)

Given the above matrix elements, both scenarios can be described by the mass
m_{\chi}
, the ratio
m_{\phi}/m_{\chi}
, the coupling

\displaystyle y\equiv\begin{cases}y_{\chi\nu}&\qquad t\text{-channel}\\
\sqrt{y_{\chi}y_{\nu}}&\qquad s\text{-channel}\end{cases}\;,

(7)

and, additionally in case of an
s

\tau_{\phi}^{(s)}=1/\Gamma_{\phi}^{(s)}

\tau_{\phi}\lesssim 10^{-21}\,\mathrm{s}\times(1\,\mathrm{MeV}/m_{\phi})
, which corresponds to large couplings
y_{\nu}\gtrsim 7.04/\sqrt{N_{\nu}}
above the pertubativity limit. We are thus not interested in couplings of this magnitude and therefore simply set
\tau_{\phi}\rightarrow\infty
for the remainder of this work, corresponding to
y_{\nu}\rightarrow 0
. In a next step, we now strive to solve the appropriate Boltzmann equation for the freeze-in process in order to calculate the resulting abundance of
\chi
for each combination of parameters
(m_{\chi},m_{\phi}/m_{\chi},y)
. This procedure then allows us to fix
y
in such a way to obtain the observed DM relic abundance, which only leaves us with two free parameters in both scenarios.

4.2
Constraints from phase-space occupation

Since
\chi
is considered to be fermionic DM, there exists a lower bound on the mass of
\chi
from Pauli blocking, when assuming that
\chi
accounts for the entire amount of DM in the Milky Way or other galaxies. In fact, given a known DM mass distribution
\rho_{\chi}
in galaxies, a sufficiently small value of
m_{\chi}
would imply a large number density
n_{\chi}=\rho_{\chi}/m_{\chi}
, possibly exceeding the limit imposed by the Fermi-Dirac statistics, as each non-relativistic
\chi
occupies a finite spatial volume. Constraints of this type have already been derived in a series of studies
Tremaine:1979we
;
Boyarsky:2008ju
;
Angus:2009sw
;
DiPaolo:2017geq
;
Savchenko:2019qnn
;

\alpha

In order to estimate this bounds, let us note that the Fermi-Dirac distribution is always smaller than unity, while
f_{\chi}=1
corresponds to a fully degenerate state. Furthermore, DM particles in the galactic halo must have velocities below the escape velocity
v_{\text{esc}}
, corresponding to a maximal momentum
p_{\text{max}}=m_{\chi}v_{\text{esc}}
. The number density
n_{\chi}^{\text{cap}}
of particles not escaping the galaxy is thus given and limited by

\displaystyle n_{\chi}^{\text{cap}}=\int_{0}^{p_{\text{max}}}f_{\chi}\frac{4\pi p^{2}\text{d}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\leq\int_{0}^{p_{\text{max}}}\frac{4\pi p^{2}\text{d}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}=\frac{p_{\text{max}}^{3}}{6\pi^{2}}\;.

(25)

Then by using the local DM density
\rho_{{\rm DM}}\approx 0.4\,{\rm GeV}/{\rm cm}^{3}

Read:2014qva
with
\rho_{{\rm DM}}=2n_{\chi}^{\text{cap}}m_{\chi}
and the escape velocity
v_{{\rm esc}}\approx 550\,{\rm km}/{\rm sec}
, we find that only masses with
m_{\chi}\gtrsim 11\,\mathrm{eV}
are allowed. Using DM densities from other locations in the Galaxy with their respective escape velocities would lead to similar results, but also introduce a dependence on the halo profile.

4.3
Constraints from
\Delta N_{{\rm eff}}

Finally, the production of DM might also change the effective number
N_{\text{eff}}
of neutrinos if this process concludes after neutrino decoupling. However, the most recent

\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
. To this end, let us note that the production process
\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}
conserves the combined number of neutrinos in both scenarios. In case of
t
-channel annihilation, however, there exists the additional process
\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\phi\phi^{*}
as well as subsequent decay
\phi\rightarrow\chi\bar{\nu}
, which does not produce any new neutrinos. Hence, if
n_{\chi}
is the number of DM particles not originating from the

with

being the number density od neutrinos in the standard thermal history. If we then further assume that the production process does not significantly change the neutrino spectrum, meaning that it remains approximately Fermi-Dirac, we also have
\rho_{\nu}\simeq kn_{\nu}T_{\nu}=\rho_{\nu}^{\text{st}}-kn_{\chi}T_{\nu}
with

. Using this expression for the calculation of the effective number of neutrinos, we thus obtain

\displaystyle N_{\text{eff}}^{0}+\Delta N_{\text{eff}}=\frac{\rho_{\nu,\text{rec}}}{\rho_{\text{ref}}}\simeq\frac{\rho^{\text{st}}_{\nu,\text{rec}}-kn_{\chi,\text{rec}}T_{\nu,\text{rec}}}{\rho_{\text{ref}}}=N_{\text{eff}}^{0}-\frac{kn_{\chi,\text{rec}}T_{\nu,\text{rec}}}{\rho_{\text{ref}}}

with

and

\displaystyle\Delta N_{\text{eff}}\simeq-3.15\times\frac{n_{\chi,\text{rec}}T_{\nu,\text{rec}}}{\rho_{\text{ref}}}\;.

(27)

Hence, for a given set of parameters
(m_{\chi},m_{\phi},y)
, this relation can be used to approximate

from the solution

of eq. (

from eq. (

Nevertheless, this statement also implies that

PTOLEMY
we also consider another experimental configuration with
M_{3}=10\,\mathrm{kg}
and a 10 year effective exposure time.

PTOLEMY
. However, these regions do not include the case where

m_{\chi}\sim 10-50\,\mathrm{eV}

6
Conclusions

In the presence of interactions between a DM particle
\chi
and neutrinos, one possible way of producing
\chi
is via freeze-in from the neutrino bath, e.g. via reactions of the form
\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}
. In this work, we considered two distinct scenarios, in which the relevant annihilation reactions are either mediated by a
t
-channel scalar or an
s
-channel vector boson (see figs.
1
and
2
). Within these scenarios, we then calculated the DM relic abundance by solving the full Boltzmann equation at the level of the number density and found that in order to obtain the observed abundance of
\Omega_{\rm DM}h^{2}=0.12
, the required couplings must be
\sim 10^{-6}
, almost independent of the actual mass of
\chi
(cf. fig.
3
).
Additionally, by solving the Boltzmann equation at the level of the spectrum, we found that
\chi
does not follow a thermal distribution in this scenario and generally has a lower mean momentum than a Fermi-Dirac distribution (cf. fig.
4
). More specifically, we found
\langle p\rangle_{\chi}/T_{\nu}\sim 2
, which is smaller than the value obtained from a Fermi-Dirac distribution, i.e.
\langle p\rangle_{\nu}/T_{\nu}\lesssim 3.15
. Hence, DM that is produced via freeze-in from neutrinos is usually colder than the neutrinos themselves. Using the solution of the Boltzmann equation, we then further studied possible constraints that need to be imposed on our scenario, including bounds from Lyman-
\alpha
data, phase-space occupation, and
\Delta N_{\mathrm{eff}}
. We then compared these constraints with the potential signal from DM annihilation to neutrinos in today’s galactic center. In general, we found that the
PTOLEMY
experiments, which aims at the detection of the C
\nu
B, can potentially be sensitive to such a signal. In fact, within the proposed sensitivity, a considerably large part of the
s
-channel parameter space could potentially be explored. In addition, it might also be possible to increase the detection prospects even further by building more specific experiments, in which case it would also be feasible to probe the
t
-channel scenario.

Appendix A
Matrix elements, cross sections and decay widths

In this appendix, we detail our calculation of the relevant matrix elements and further derive the corresponding cross sections and decay widths.

A.1
Scattering

For the
t
- and
s
-channel diagrams, the squared amplitudes for the process
\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}
read

\displaystyle|{\cal M}^{(t)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\rm spins}}y_{\chi\nu}^{4}|\bar{u}_{3}P_{L}u_{1}\frac{1}{t-m_{\phi}^{2}}\bar{v}_{2}P_{R}v_{4}|^{2}\;,

(39)

\displaystyle|{\cal M}^{(s)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\rm spins}}y_{\chi}^{2}y_{\nu}^{2}|\bar{u}_{3}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}v_{4}\frac{1}{s-m_{\phi}^{2}+im_{\phi}\Gamma_{\phi}}\bar{v}_{2}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}u_{1}|^{2}\;.

(40)

Here,
u_{\cdot}
and
v_{\cdot}
denote the fermion and anti-fermion spinors while the sum goes over all particle spins. Note that there is no averaging factor, since all initial-state particles are purely left-handed.
Then, by using standard trace technology as well as
Package-X

Patel:2015tea
to compute the resulting traces, we obtain

\displaystyle|{\cal M}^{(t)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=y_{\chi\nu}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{t-m_{\phi}^{2}}\right)^{2}{\rm tr}\left[u_{3}\bar{u}_{3}P_{L}u_{1}\bar{u}_{1}P_{R}\right]{\rm tr}\left[v_{2}\bar{v}_{2}P_{R}v_{4}\bar{v}_{4}P_{L}\right]

\displaystyle=y_{\chi\nu}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{t-m_{\phi}^{2}}\right)^{2}(2p_{1}\cdot p_{3})(2p_{2}\cdot p_{4})=y_{\chi\nu}^{4}\left(\frac{t-m_{\chi}^{2}}{t-m_{\phi}^{2}}\right)^{2}

(41)

as well as

\displaystyle|{\cal M}^{(s)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\chi}^{2}y_{\nu}^{2}}{(s-m_{\phi}^{2})^{2}+m_{\phi}^{2}\Gamma_{\phi}^{2}}{\rm tr}\left[u_{3}\bar{u}_{3}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}v_{4}\bar{v}_{4}\gamma_{\nu}P_{L}\right]{\rm tr}\left[v_{2}\bar{v}_{2}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}u_{1}\bar{u}_{1}\gamma^{\nu}P_{L}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{16y_{\chi}^{2}y_{\nu}^{2}}{(s-m_{\phi}^{2})^{2}+m_{\phi}^{2}\Gamma_{\phi}^{2}}(p_{1}\cdot p_{4})(p_{2}\cdot p_{3})=4y_{\chi}^{2}y_{\nu}^{2}\frac{\left(u-m_{\chi}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(s-m_{\phi}^{2}\right)^{2}+m_{\phi}^{2}\Gamma_{\phi}^{2}}\;.

(42)

Additionally, the
t
-channel diagram for the process
\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\phi\phi^{*}
in fig.
1
yields

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\phi\phi^{*}}^{(t)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\rm spins}}y_{\chi\nu}^{4}|\bar{v}_{2}P_{R}\frac{q_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}}{q^{2}-m_{\chi}^{2}}P_{L}u_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=y_{\chi\nu}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{t-m_{\chi}^{2}}\right)^{2}{\rm tr}\left[v_{2}\bar{v}_{2}P_{R}q_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}u_{1}\bar{u}_{1}P_{R}q_{\nu}\gamma^{\nu}P_{L}\right]

\displaystyle=y_{\chi\nu}^{4}\frac{tu-m_{\phi}^{4}}{\left(t-m_{\chi}^{2}\right)^{2}}

(43)

with
q=p_{1}-p_{3}
.

Given the above matrix elements, we can now calculate the total cross section via the relation
Zyla:2020zbs

\sigma=\int\frac{|{\cal M}|^{2}}{64\pi s|\mathbf{p}_{1{\rm cm}}|^{2}}\text{d}t\;.

(44)

Here, the integral covers the range (assuming
m_{1}=m_{2}
and
m_{3}=m_{4}
)
-(|\mathbf{p}_{1{\rm cm}}|+|\mathbf{p}_{3{\rm cm}}|)^{2}\leq t\leq-(|\mathbf{p}_{1{\rm cm}}|-|\mathbf{p}_{3{\rm cm}}|)^{2}
with
\mathbf{p}_{i{\rm cm}}
(
i=1
,
3
) being the spatial part of the momentum of the
i
-th particle in the center-of-mass frame, i.e.
|\mathbf{p}_{i{\rm cm}}|=\sqrt{s/4-m_{i}^{2}}
. By applying eq. (
44
) to (
41
) and (
43
), we thus obtain for the
t
-channel scenario

\displaystyle\sigma_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\chi\nu}^{4}}{16\pi s^{2}}\left[\frac{\sqrt{s^{2}-4m_{\chi}^{2}s}\left(2\delta m^{4}+m_{\phi}^{2}s\right)}{\delta m^{4}+m_{\phi}^{2}s}-4\delta m^{2}\acoth\frac{s+2\delta m^{2}}{\sqrt{s^{2}-4m_{\chi}^{2}s}}\right]\;,

(45)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\phi\phi^{*}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\chi\nu}^{4}}{8\pi s^{2}}\left[-\sqrt{s^{2}-4m_{\phi}^{2}s}+\left(s-2\delta m^{2}\right)\acoth\frac{s-2\delta m^{2}}{\sqrt{s^{2}-4m_{\phi}^{2}s}}\right]

(46)

with
\delta m^{2}\equiv m_{\phi}^{2}-m_{\chi}^{2}
.

For the
s
-channel diagram, we instead have to replace
u
with
s
and
t
according to the relation
s+t+u=\sum_{i}m_{i}^{2}
, which yields

\sigma^{(s)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}=\frac{y_{\chi}^{2}y_{\nu}^{2}}{12\pi}\frac{s-m_{\chi}^{2}}{(s-m_{\phi}^{2})^{2}+(m_{\phi}\Gamma_{\phi}^{(s)})^{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\chi}^{2}}{s}}\;.

(47)

The large
s
limits of these cross sections can be computed by noting that
\acoth(1+x)\approx\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{2}{x}\right)+\frac{x}{4}-\frac{x^{2}}{16}+{\cal O}(x^{3})

for
0<x\ll 1
, which implies

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\sigma^{(t)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\chi\nu}^{4}}{16\pi s}\;,

(48)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\phi\phi^{*}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\chi\nu}^{4}}{16\pi s}\left[\log\left(\frac{s}{m_{\chi}^{2}}\right)-2\right]\;,

(49)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\sigma^{(s)}_{\nu\bar{\nu}\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\chi}^{2}y_{\nu}^{2}}{12\pi s}\;.

(50)

A.2
Decay

Next, we compute the decay widths of
\phi
in both scenarios. We find that the squared amplitudes are given by

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{\phi\rightarrow\chi\bar{\nu}}^{(t)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\rm spins}}y_{\chi\nu}^{4}|\bar{u}_{2}P_{L}v_{3}|^{2}\;,

(51)

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{\phi\rightarrow X\bar{X}}^{(s)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\rm spins}}\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\epsilon}y_{X}^{2}|\bar{u}_{2}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}v_{3}\epsilon_{\mu}|^{2}

(52)

with
X\in\{\nu,\chi\}
,
\bar{X}\in\{\bar{\chi},\phi^{*}\}
and an additional factor
1/3
from the vector polarization sum. Then by evaluating the traces, we obtain

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{\phi\rightarrow\chi\bar{\nu}}^{(t)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=y_{\chi\nu}^{2}{\rm tr}[u_{2}\bar{u}_{2}P_{L}v_{3}\bar{v}_{3}P_{R}]=y_{\chi\nu}^{2}(2p_{2}\cdot p_{3})=y_{\chi\nu}^{2}\left(m_{\phi}^{2}-m_{\chi}^{2}\right),

(53)

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{\phi\rightarrow X\bar{X}}^{(s)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}y_{X}^{2}\left(\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{m_{\phi}^{2}}-g_{\mu\nu}\right){\rm tr}[u_{2}\bar{u}_{2}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}v_{3}\bar{v}_{3}\gamma^{\nu}P_{L}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}y_{X}^{2}\frac{4(p_{2}\cdot q)(p_{3}\cdot q)-2q^{2}(p_{2}\cdot p_{3})}{m_{\phi}^{2}}+\frac{4}{3}y_{X}^{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}y_{X}^{2}\left(m_{\phi}^{2}-m_{X}^{2}\right).

(54)

Using these matrix elements, we can then obtain the decay width via the relation

\Gamma=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int|{\cal M}|^{2}\frac{|\mathbf{p}_{2{\rm cm}}|}{m_{\phi}^{2}}\text{d}\Omega_{2}=\frac{|{\cal M}|^{2}|\mathbf{p}_{2{\rm cm}}|}{8\pi m_{\phi}^{2}}

(55)

with
\Omega_{2}
being the solid angle of particle 2, while
|\mathbf{p}_{2{\rm cm}}|
is given by

|\mathbf{p}_{2{\rm cm}}|=\frac{\sqrt{m_{1}^{4}+m_{2}^{4}+m_{3}^{4}-2m_{2}^{2}m_{1}^{2}-2m_{3}^{2}m_{1}^{2}-2m_{2}^{2}m_{3}^{2}}}{2m_{1}}\;.

(56)

For
\phi\rightarrow\chi\bar{\nu}
and
\phi\rightarrow X\bar{X}
,
we have
|\mathbf{p}_{2{\rm cm}}|=(m_{\phi}^{2}-m_{\chi}^{2})/(2m_{\phi})

and
\sqrt{m_{\phi}^{2}/4-m_{X}^{2}}
, respectively. Finally, by ubstituting eqs. (
53
) and (
54
) into (
55
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi\bar{\nu}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\chi\nu}^{2}\left(m_{\phi}^{2}-m_{\chi}^{2}\right){}^{2}}{16\pi m_{\phi}^{3}}\;,

(57)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\nu\bar{\nu}}^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\nu}^{2}m_{\phi}}{24\pi}\;,

(58)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi\bar{\chi}}^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{\chi}^{2}}{24\pi}\left(1-\frac{m_{\chi}^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{2}}\right)\sqrt{m_{\phi}^{2}-4m_{\chi}^{2}}\;.

(59)

Appendix B
Sommerfeld enhancement

In the presence of a light mediator, DM annihilation in the non-relativistic regime can be significantly enhanced by an effect known as Sommerfeld enhancement
Sommerfeld
. In general, this effect can be taken into account by multiplying the cross section with a Sommerfeld enhancement
factor, which in the Hulthen potential approximation has the following analytic form
Cassel:2009wt
;
Slatyer:2009vg
;
Feng:2010zp
:

S\simeq\frac{\pi\sinh\left(\frac{12\epsilon_{v}}{\pi\epsilon_{\phi}}\right)}{\epsilon_{v}\left[\cosh\left(\frac{12\epsilon_{v}}{\pi\epsilon_{\phi}}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{12\epsilon_{v}}{\pi\epsilon_{\phi}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{2}\epsilon_{\phi}}{6\epsilon_{v}^{2}}-1}\right)\right]}\;,

(60)

with

\epsilon_{v}\equiv\frac{4\pi}{y_{\chi}^{2}}v\,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\epsilon_{\phi}\equiv\frac{4\pi}{y_{\chi}^{2}}\frac{m_{\phi}}{m_{\chi}}\;.

(61)

In this expression,
v
is the relative velocity between
\chi
and
\bar{\chi}
. A noteworthy feature of the so-defined
S
factor is that it has local maxima at

\epsilon_{\phi}^{{\rm peak}}=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}n^{2}}\qquad\text{for}\quad n=1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \dots

(62)

with a height that is approximately given by

S^{{\rm peak}}\simeq\frac{\pi^{2}\epsilon_{\phi}}{6\epsilon_{v}^{2}}=\left(\frac{y_{\chi}^{2}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{n^{2}v^{2}}\qquad\text{for}\quad n=1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \dots

(63)

In fig.
8
, we plot this factor as a function of
\epsilon_{\phi}
, which illustrated that eqs. (
62
) and (
63
)
are rather accurate approximation of the peak position for small
n
.

Figure 8:
The Sommerfeld enhancement factor
S
as a function of
\epsilon_{\phi}
. The blue solid curve is obtained from eq. (
60
)
with
\epsilon_{v}=10^{-3}
. Dotted curves
show the peak positions indicated by eqs. (
62
) and (
63
).

The
S
factor in eq. (
60
) is velocity-dependent. In order
to apply this formalism to DM annihilation in the Galaxy, one can define the velocity-averaged Sommerfeld enhancement factor via
Feng:2010zp

\bar{S}\simeq\frac{1}{v_{0}^{3}{\cal N}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{v_{{\rm esc}}}S(v)e^{-v^{2}/(2v_{0}^{2})}v^{2}\text{d}v\;,

(64)

with the escape velocity
v_{{\rm esc}}
, the mean velocity
v_{0}
, and

{\cal N}\equiv\text{erf}\left(\frac{v_{{\rm esc}}}{\sqrt{2}v_{0}}\right)-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{v_{{\rm esc}}}{v_{0}}e^{-v_{{\rm esc}}^{2}/(2v_{0}^{2})}\;.

(65)

Taking
v_{0}=220\,\mathrm{km/s}
and
v_{{\rm esc}}=550\,\mathrm{km/s}
, the peaks from eqs. (
62
) and (
63
) are then located at

\bar{S}^{{\rm peak}}\simeq\left(\frac{y_{\chi}^{2}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{2.04\times 10^{6}}{n^{2}}\;.

(66)

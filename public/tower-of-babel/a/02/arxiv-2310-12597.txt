A uniform estimate for the quaternionic Gauduchon metric with prescribed volume form
Jiaogen Zhang

Abstract
The quaternionic Calabi conjecture, posed by Alesker and Verbitsky [ 5 ] , predicts that the quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation can always be solved on any compact HKT manifold. Motivated by this conjecture, we will introduce a quaternionic version of the Gauduchon conjecture on any compact S ​ L ​ ( n , ℍ ) SL(n,\mathbb{H}) -manifold, specifically addressing the existence of quaternionic Gauduchon metrics with prescribed volume form. We reframe this question as a special case of fully nonlinear elliptic equations of second order and subsequently establish a uniform estimate for the potential function.



Keywords:
Uniform estimates, Quaternionic Gauduchon metric, Auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation

Introduction

A hypercomplex manifold is a smooth manifold
M
of quaternionic dimension
n
equipped with three anti-commuting complex structures
(I,J,K)
that satisfy the quaternionic relations

IJ=K\,.

A Riemannian metric
g
on a hypercomplex manifold
(M,I,J,K)
is said to be hyperhermitian if it is Hermitian with respect to each of
I,J
and
K
. Any hyperhermitian metric induces a sympletic form

\Omega_{0}=\omega_{J}+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{K}

associated to
g
, where
\omega_{J}
and
\omega_{K}
are the fundamental forms of
(g,J)
and
(g,K)
respectively. The form
\Omega_{0}
, which is of type
(2,0)
with respect to
I
, satisfies the
q
-real condition
\overline{J\Omega_{0}}={\Omega}_{0}
(here
J
acts on
\Omega_{0}
as
J\Omega_{0}(\cdot,\cdot)=\Omega_{0}(J\cdot,J\cdot)
) and is positive in the sense that
\Omega_{0}(X,JX)>0
for every nowhere vanishing real vector field
X
on
M
. A hyperhermitian manifold
(M,I,J,K,g)
is hyperkähler if and only if
\Omega_{0}
is closed, whereas it is called HKT (hyperkähler with torsion) if
\Omega_{0}
is
\partial
-closed, where
\partial
is taken with respect to
I
. The geometry of HKT manifolds is extensively studied in the literature
[
4
;
6
;
14
;
22
;
18
;
26
;
27
;
30
;
36
;
41
;
42
;
43
]
and the reference therein.

Motivated by the study of   “canonical”  HKT metrics, in analogy to the famous Calabi conjecture demonstrated by Yau in
[
46
]
, Alesker and Verbitsky proposed in
[
5
]
to study the quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation:

(\Omega_{0}+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi)^{n}={\rm e}^{F+b}\Omega^{n}_{0}

(1.1)

on a compact HKT manifold, where
\partial_{J}=J^{-1}\bar{\partial}J

is the twisted differential operator introduced by Verbitsky
[
41
]
and
F\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
is given, while
(\varphi,b)\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}
is the unknown. Even though the solvability of the quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation in its general form has remained an open problem over the past decade, several partial results have been reported in the literature
[
1
;
2
;
3
;
5
;
10
;
15
;
16
;
34
;
35
]
.

The solvability of Equation (
1.1
) has a nice geometric application, implying the existence of a unique balanced metric
\tilde{g}
on a compact HKT manifold
(M,I,J,K,g)
with holomorphically trivial canonical bundle relative to
I
. Furthermore, the form
\tilde{\Omega}
induced by
\tilde{g}
is a
\partial\partial_{J}\varphi
perturbation of
\Omega
(cf.
[
43
]
). From this perspective, Equation (
1.1
) can be regarded as the “quaternionic counterpart” of the complex Monge-Ampère equation in Kähler geometry, where balanced HKT metrics play a role analogous to that of Calabi-Yau metrics in Kähler geometry.

It is well-known that a Hermitian metric
\omega
over a compact complex manifold
X
is referred to as “Gauduchon metric”  if

\mathfrak{i}\partial\overline{\partial}(\omega^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X-1})=0\,.

A celebrated result by Gauduchon
[
21
]
suggests that every Hermitian metric is conformal equivalent to a Gauduchon metric, which is unique within its conformal class except for a constant multiplier. Thus, the Gauduchon metric holds a significant position among Hermitian metrics in the study of non-Kähler geometry. Given the similarity to the Gauduchon metric in the complex case, exploring the quaternionic Gauduchon metric within the hyperhermitian framework appears to be a natural direction of study. Recently, Grantcharov, Lejmi, and Verbitsky
[
22
]
presented a quaternionic analogue of the Gauduchon metric on compact hyperhermitian manifolds, among many other intriguing ideas and findings.

Definition 1.1
.

A quaternionic positive
(2,0)
-form
\Omega
on a compact hypercomplex manifold
(M,I,J,K)
of quaternionic dimension
n
is quaternionic Gauduchon if
\partial\partial_{J}(\Omega^{n-1})
vanishes identity.

Yau’s proof of Calabi conjecture establishes the existence of a unique Kähler-Einstein metric on any compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{0})
with its first Chern class that is either negative or zero. The latter case was subsequently transformed into finding a new Kähler metric
\omega\in[\omega_{0}]
such that its
n
-power
\omega^{n}
is exactly a prescribed smooth positive volume form with a mass equal to
\int_{M}\omega_{0}^{n}
. When
M
no longer admits a Kähler metric, in the same spirit as the Calabi-Yau theorem, Gauduchon conjectured in 1984 that there always exists a Gauduchon metric with a prescribed volume form.

As demonstrated by Obata

As we have elaborated earlier, it is an established fact that every compact Hermitian manifold admits the existence of at least one Gauduchon metric. Within the realm of hyperhermitian geometry, the analogous result retains its validity and applicability to any compact hyperhermitian manifold. More specifically,

To streamline subsequent discussions, it is imperative to introduce the following definition of quaternionic Aeppli cohomology group, which pertains specifically to the cohomology classes of
(2p,0)
-forms on

Definition 1.5
.

For each integer

H^{2p,0}_{A}(M)=\frac{\{\phi\in\Lambda_{I}^{2p,0}(M)\mid\partial\partial_{J}\phi=0\}}{\partial\Lambda_{I}^{2p-1,0}(M)+\partial_{J}\Lambda_{I}^{2p-1,0}(M)}\,.

In the reference

It is a fundamental and intriguing question to investigate whether an adaptation of Gauduchon’s celebrated volume form conjecture, tailored to the realm of quaternionic geometry, can be formulated and subsequently applied to any given hyperhermitian manifold.

Question 1.6
.

Suppose
(M,I,J,K,g,\Omega_{0})
is a connected compact
SL(n,\mathbb{H})
-manifold. Let
\Omega
be a quaternionic Gauduchon metric on
M
. Then for every smooth real-valued
F
, there exist a unique Gauduchon metric
\tilde{\Omega}
and a unique constant
b
such that

\begin{cases}&[\tilde{\Omega}^{n-1}]=[\Omega^{n-1}]\qquad\mathrm{in}\,H^{2n-2,0}_{A}(M)\\[5.69054pt]
&\tilde{\Omega}^{n}=e^{F+b}\Omega_{0}^{n}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

To rephrase the aforementioned equality (
1.2
) in accordance with Definition
1.5
, we will endeavor to find a new HKT form, denoted as
\tilde{\Omega}
, that satisfies the following

\tilde{\Omega}^{n-1}=\Omega^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial(\partial_{J}\varphi\wedge\Omega_{0}^{n-2})-\partial_{J}(\partial\varphi\wedge\Omega_{0}^{n-2})\right)

(1.3)

for some smooth potential function
\varphi
.
In the work by Verbitsky
[
41
]
, it has been established that the operators
\partial
and
\partial_{J}

exhibit anticommutativity, i.e.

\partial\partial_{J}=-\partial_{J}\partial.

Therefore, the equality (
1.3
) can be reformulated as:

\tilde{\Omega}^{n-1}=\Omega^{n-1}+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi\wedge\Omega_{0}^{n-2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial\varphi\wedge\partial_{J}\Omega_{0}^{n-2}-\partial_{J}\varphi\wedge\partial\Omega_{0}^{n-2})\,.

We proceed by introducing the definition of the Hodge star operator, which is defined with respect to
\Omega_{0}
. To that end, it is sufficient to exploit its actions specifically on the
q
-real
(2n-2)
-forms residing on the manifold
M
. At a given point, we consider the inner product of two forms belonging to
\Lambda^{p,0}_{I}(M)
, denoted as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{g}
, and its definition is given by

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}=\frac{1}{p!}g^{r_{1}\bar{s}_{1}}\cdots g^{r_{p}\bar{s}_{p}}\alpha_{r_{1}\cdots r_{p}}\overline{\beta_{s_{1}\cdots s_{p}}}\,,\qquad\text{for every }\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda^{p,0}_{I}(M)\,,

where any
(p,0)
-form
\alpha
is locally written as

\alpha=\frac{1}{p!}\alpha_{r_{1}\cdots r_{p}}dz^{r_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dz^{r_{p}}

and
(g^{r\bar{s}})
is the inverse of the Hermitian matrix
(g_{r\bar{s}})
induced by the
I
-Hermitian metric
g
. Then, the Hodge star operator
*\colon\Lambda_{I}^{2n-2,0}(M)\to\Lambda_{I}^{2,0}(M)
is explicitly defined as

\alpha\wedge*\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}\frac{\Omega_{0}^{n}}{n!}\,,\qquad\text{for }\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda_{I}^{2n-2,0}(M)\,.

As demonstrated by Gentili and the author in
[
17
]
that

\Big(\frac{\tilde{\Omega}^{n}}{\Omega_{0}^{n}}\Big)^{n-1}=\frac{\Big(*(\tilde{\Omega}^{n-1})\Big)^{n}}{\Big(*(\Omega_{0}^{n-1})\Big)^{n}}=\frac{\Big(*\Big(\frac{\tilde{\Omega}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\Big)\Big)^{n}}{\Omega_{0}^{n}}\,.

Consequently, Equation (
1.2
) is transformed into

\Big(*\Big(\frac{\tilde{\Omega}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\Big)\Big)^{n}=e^{(n-1)(F+b)}\Omega_{0}^{n}\,.

(1.4)

It has been established in
[
17
]
that for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})

\frac{1}{(n-1)!}*\left(\partial\partial_{J}\varphi\wedge\Omega_{0}^{n-2}\right)=\frac{1}{n-1}\left[(\Delta_{g}\varphi)\Omega_{0}-\partial\partial_{J}\varphi\right]\,,

(1.5)

where the canonical Laplacian
\Delta_{g}
is defined by

\Delta_{g}\varphi:=\frac{n\partial\partial_{J}\varphi\wedge\Omega_{0}^{n-1}}{\Omega_{0}^{n}}\,.

Given that the HKT metric
\Omega
is positive on
M
, there correspondingly exists a positive definite form

H:=\frac{*(\Omega^{n-1})}{(n-1)!}\in\Lambda_{I}^{2,0}(M)

(1.6)

that is intimately associated with it. Let us introduce a
q
-real
(1,1)
-form

Z[\varphi]:=\frac{1}{2(n-1)!}*\{\partial\varphi\wedge\partial_{J}\Omega_{0}^{n-2}-\partial_{J}\varphi\wedge\partial\Omega_{0}^{n-2}\}

(1.7)

which is explicitly defined in terms of linear dependencies on the first-order derivatives of
\varphi
. From the relations stated in Equations (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), it is immediately evident that the form

*\Big(\frac{\tilde{\Omega}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\Big)=H+\frac{1}{n-1}\left[(\Delta_{g}\varphi)\Omega_{0}-\partial\partial_{J}\varphi\right]+Z[\varphi]

(1.8)

determines a positive definite element in
\Lambda_{I}^{2,0}(M)
, owing to the positivity of
\tilde{\Omega}^{n-1}

Definition 1.7

A function
\varphi\in C^{2}(M,\mathbb{R})

Consider a closed Hermitian manifold
(M,I,g)
of complex dimension
m=2n
(where
n\in\mathbb{N}
). Assume that
J
is an another complex structure defined on
M
that is compatible with respect to the metric
g
, satisfying the orthogonality condition

JI+JI=0.

Given the Kähler form
\omega
associated with the metric
g
and
\omega_{h}
another specified Hermitian metric on
M

\begin{split}&\mathrm{PSH}_{J}(M,\omega,\omega_{h})\\
&=\Big\{\varphi\in C^{2}(M,\mathbb{R}):\omega_{h}+\frac{1}{m-1}\big[(\Delta_{I,g}\varphi)\omega-\frac{1}{2}(\mathfrak{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi-J\mathfrak{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)\big]+W[\varphi]>0\Big\}\,.\end{split}

Here,
\Delta_{I,g}
represents the canonical Laplacian defined on
(M,I,g)
, which is given by

\Delta_{I,g}\varphi=\frac{m(\mathfrak{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi-J\mathfrak{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)\wedge\omega^{m-1}}{2\omega^{m}}\,,

and
W[\varphi]
is a Hermitian tensor that depends linearly on the first-order derivatives of
\varphi
, namely

W[\varphi]:=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}(\varphi_{i}\bar{\beta}_{j}+\beta_{i}\varphi_{\bar{j}})dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}

for a smooth
(1,0)
form

which is locally expressed as
\beta=\beta_{j}dz_{j}
. For the sake of clarity and convenience, let us denote

\tilde{g}_{i\bar{j}}=h_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{1}{m-1}[(\Delta_{I,g}\varphi)g_{i\bar{j}}-\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{i\bar{j}}+J^{\bar{l}}_{i}J^{k}_{\bar{j}}\varphi_{k\bar{l}})]+\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{i}\bar{\beta}_{j}+\beta_{i}\varphi_{\bar{j}})\,,

where
h_{i\bar{j}}
is locally defined by
\omega_{h}=\sqrt{-1}h_{i\bar{j}}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}
.

In order to solve the Equation (
1.9
), we need to to obtain the a priori estimates for the following complex
(m-1)
-form Monge-Ampère equation on
(M,I,g)

\begin{cases}\ \det(\tilde{g}_{i\bar{j}})=e^{G}\det(g_{i\bar{j}})\,,\\[5.69054pt]
\ \sup_{M}\varphi=0,\quad\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}_{J}(M,\omega,\omega_{h})\,,\end{cases}

(1.10)

where
G
is a given smooth function on
M
. Intuitively, the uniform estimate occupies a pivotal position in the investigation of fully nonlinear elliptic equations, as it serves as a fundamental prerequisite for establishing higher-order regularities.

Inspired by the groundbreaking concept introduced by Guo-Phong-Tong
[

in their recent work on the auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation, we present a uniform estimate for (

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose
(M,I,J,K,g,\Omega_{0})
is a connected compact
SL(n,\mathbb{H})
-manifold. Given any constant
p>2m
. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}_{J}(M,\omega,\omega_{h})
be a smooth solution for the
(m-1)
-form Monge-Ampere equation (
1.10

C>0
depends only on
n,\,\omega,\,\omega_{h},\,p
and
\|e^{2G}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}
such that

\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\,.

It is worth emphasizing that the attainment of higher-order regularity for the Equation (
1.10
) poses a significantly greater challenge in its general form, partly attributed to the intricacies arising from the derivatives of

. In the specific instance where the term

in (
1.9
) vanishes, the question of solvability has been resolved by G. Gentili and the author
[

]
for flat hyperkähler manifolds, and additionally by Fu-Xu-Zhang
[
13
]
for the more general class of hyperkähler manifolds.

Auxiliary real Monge-Ampère equations and exponential integrability

Assume that
\varphi\in C^{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
serves as a solution to the Equation (
1.10
). To establish Theorem
1.8
, it suffices to derive a lower bound for
\varphi
. By compactness, we can assume that the function
\varphi
attains its minimum value at a certain point on

z_{\mathrm{min}}
, i.e.

\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})=\inf_{z\in M}\varphi(z).

Hence, in the subsequent sections, we are expected to exploit the lower bound for
\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})
in the manner as follows

Proposition 2.1
.

Given
p>2m
, there exists a positive constant
C
which depends only on
m
,
\omega
,
\omega_{h}
,
p
and
\|e^{2G}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}
such that

\qquad\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})\geq-C.

The proof of Proposition
2.1
is somewhat extensive and necessitates additional efforts, we will carry out it at the end of present paper.

2.1
Auxiliary real Monge-Ampère equations

Let
V
be an open subset that is isomorphic to an open subset within
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
, with the property that
z_{\mathrm{min}}
lies within
V
. Then, there exists a uniform positive constant
r_{0}
for which the ball
B=B(z_{\mathrm{min}},2r_{0})
is contained entirely within
V
. If necessary, Shrink the value of
r_{0}
to ensure that the following inequality holds on
\overline{B}

\frac{1}{C_{1}}\,\omega_{{}_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}}\leq\omega_{h}\leq C_{1}\,\omega_{{}_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}}\,,\qquad\omega_{\mathbb{C}^{m}}=\mathfrak{i}\partial\overline{\partial}|z|^{2}\,.

(2.1)

Here,
C_{1}
is a positive constant that depends solely on
\omega_{h}
.

We introduce a small, positive constant
c_{0}
, whose specific value will be determined later. For any
s
within the interval
(0,c_{0}r_{0}^{2})
, we consider the following auxiliary function

\varphi_{s}(z)=\varphi(z)-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})+c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}-s,\qquad z\in\overline{B}.

Firstly, observe that for any

, we have the inequality
\varphi_{s}(z)\geq c_{0}r_{0}^{2}-s>0
. Therefore, the sub-level set
B_{s}
, which is defined by

B_{s}=\{z\in B:\varphi_{s}(z)<0\},

is an open subset of
B
. Furthermore,
B_{s}
is non-empty and has a compact closure within
B
.

For each index
i=1,\cdots,m
, we express the complex number
z_{i}
as
x_{2i-1}+\sqrt{-1}x_{2i}
, ensuring that the set
\{x_{a}\}_{a=1}^{2m}
constitutes a local real coordinate chart. In this local real coordinate chart, we denote the standard volume form in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
by
{\rm dx}
. To streamline upcoming discussions and for the sake of notational conciseness, we abbreviate the expression

{\rm d}\sigma=(\det g_{i\bar{j}})^{2}{\rm dx}

and introduce two pivotal quantities that will be recurrently used

\Phi_{s}=\int_{B_{s}}(-\varphi_{s})e^{2G}{\rm d}\sigma,\qquad\Psi_{s}=\int_{B_{s}}e^{2G}{\rm d}\sigma\,,

(2.2)

for any

.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
0<t<s<c_{0}r_{0}^{2}
, we have

t\Psi_{s-t}\leq\Phi_{s}\,.

Proof.

The proof is somewhat straightforward, we are about to provided a sketch here for the sake of completeness. According to the definition of
B_{s}
, it follows that
\varphi_{s-t}
is strictly negative (i.e.
t<-\varphi_{s}
) on

t\int_{B_{s-t}}e^{2G}{\rm d}\sigma\leq\int_{B_{s-t}}(-\varphi_{s})e^{2G}{\rm d}\sigma\leq\int_{B_{s}}(-\varphi_{s})e^{2G}{\rm d}\sigma\,.

This shows our desired inequality.

Select a sequence of smooth and strictly positive functions

3.1
A comparison lemma

Following the approach adopted in
[
38
]
for demonstrating the Gauduchon conjecture, we aim to introduce a highly instrumental tensor
\hat{\Theta}
with

\hat{\Theta}^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{m-1}\Big\{\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\omega\cdot g^{i\bar{j}}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}+\tilde{g}^{k\bar{l}}J_{k}^{\bar{j}}J_{\bar{l}}^{i}\Big)\Big\}\,.

Regarding the tensor
\hat{\Theta}
, we can establish the following crucial inequality.

Lemma 3.1
.

At every point, the tensor
\hat{\Theta}
is positive definite, and it satisfies

\det(\hat{\Theta}^{i\bar{j}})\geq e^{-G}\det(g^{i\bar{j}})\,.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}
denote the eigenvalues of the tensor
\tilde{\omega}
with respect to the background metric
\omega
. In the vicinity of a specified point
z_{\mathrm{min}}
, there exists a local
I
-holomorphic coordinate system
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
such that at
z_{\mathrm{min}}
, the components of the metrics of
g
and
\tilde{g}
satisfy
g_{i\bar{j}}=\delta_{ij}
and
\tilde{g}_{i\bar{j}}=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}
. As a consequence,

\begin{split}\hat{\Theta}^{i\bar{j}}|_{z_{\mathrm{min}}}=&\frac{1}{m-1}\Big\{\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\delta_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\delta_{kl}J_{k}^{\bar{j}}J_{\bar{l}}^{i}\Big)\Big\}\\
=&\frac{1}{m-1}\Big(\sum_{k\neq i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)\delta_{ij}\,.\end{split}

(3.1)

Applying the arithmetic and geometric mean inequality, we can observe that

\det(\hat{\Theta}^{i\bar{j}})=\frac{1}{(m-1)^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\sum_{k\neq i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}=e^{-G}\det(g^{i\bar{j}})\,.

This ends our proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Consider the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
defined as
\mathcal{L}v=\hat{\Theta}^{i\bar{j}}v_{i\bar{j}},

we have the following

\mathcal{L}\varphi=m-\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\omega_{h}-\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}W[\varphi]\,.

(3.2)

Proof.

A direct calculation yields that

\begin{split}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\Big(\tilde{\omega}-\omega_{h}-W[\varphi]\Big)=&\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\Big(\mathfrak{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi-J\mathfrak{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}+\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}J^{\bar{l}}_{i}J^{k}_{\bar{j}}\varphi_{k\bar{l}})\Big)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}+\tilde{g}^{k\bar{l}}J_{k}^{\bar{j}}J_{\bar{l}}^{i}\Big)\varphi_{i\bar{j}}=\hat{\Theta}^{i\bar{j}}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}\,.\end{split}

Then the proof is completely.
∎

In the subsequent discussion, our focus will be directed towards a comparative analysis of the functions
\varphi_{s,k}
and
\varphi_{s}\,
. This will be pretty crucial in our proof of Proposition
2.1
at the end of this paper.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\epsilon=\Phi_{s,k}^{\frac{1}{2m+1}}(\frac{m}{2m+1})^{\frac{2m}{2m+1}}
. The inequality

-\varphi_{s}\leq\epsilon(-\varphi_{s,k}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{2m}{2m+1}}\quad\mathrm{in}\,B

(3.3)

holds true for a specific constant
\varepsilon>0
depending only on
m,

\Phi_{s,k}
and
\beta
.

Proof.

Firstly, we direct our attention towards the following auxiliary function defined as

\eta=-\epsilon\Gamma(\varphi_{s,k})-\varphi_{s},

where
\Gamma:(-\infty,0]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a smooth, non-increasing, concave function, whose precise form will be specified subsequently. Notably, this function
\eta
is designed such that its maximum value on
\overline{B}
satisfies
\max_{\overline{B}}\eta\leq 0
.

Upon unraveling the definition of
\varphi_{s}
, it becomes immediately apparent that
\eta\leq 0
holds true within the set
\overline{B}\setminus B_{s}
. Hence, we can reasonably assume the maximum of

is achieved at an interior point within

, which we denote as

Adopting the same local coordinate system in the vicinity of

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}|_{z_{\max}}&=\sum_{i}\tilde{\Theta}^{i\bar{i}}=\frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{i}\Big\{\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\omega\cdot g^{i\bar{i}}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tilde{g}^{i\bar{i}}+\tilde{g}^{k\bar{l}}J_{k}^{\bar{i}}J_{\bar{l}}^{i}\Big)\Big\}\\

Taking into account the coordinates as specified in (

\mathcal{L}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}|_{z_{\max}}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{m-1}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\omega|_{z_{\max}}\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}\omega_{h}|_{z_{\max}}.

(3.4)

In order to facilitate the subsequent discussions, we introduce the following notations

[f]_{i}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}\,,\qquad[f]_{\bar{j}}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}\,,\qquad[f]_{i\bar{j}}=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{i}\partial\bar{z}_{j}}

, and for any indices

. Consequently, at the point

, we obtain

[\varphi]_{i}=\epsilon\Gamma^{\prime}[\varphi_{s,k}]_{i}-\epsilon c_{0}(z_{i}-z_{\min,i})\,,

as well as

\begin{split}0\geq&\mathcal{L}\eta|_{z_{\max}}=-\epsilon\mathcal{L}\Big(\Gamma(\varphi_{s,k})\Big)-\mathcal{L}\varphi-c_{0}\mathcal{L}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}\\
=&-\epsilon\Gamma^{\prime}\hat{\Theta}^{i\bar{j}}[\varphi_{s,k}]_{i\bar{j}}-\epsilon\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\hat{\Theta}^{i\bar{j}}[\varphi_{s,k}]_{i}[\varphi_{s,k}]_{\bar{j}}-m+\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\omega_{h}+\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}W[\varphi]-c_{0}\mathcal{L}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}\\

where the second inequality arises from the facts that
\Gamma
is a smooth, non-increasing, and concave function, the arithmetic and geometric mean inequality, and the inequality (
3.4

3.1

2^{2m}\det\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{s,k}}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}\Big)^{2}\geq\det\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{s,k}}{\partial x_{a}\partial x_{b}}\Big)\,.

Now, we proceed to estimate

in terms of

\begin{split}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}W[\varphi]&=\mathrm{Re}\Big(\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}[\varphi]_{i}\bar{\beta}_{j}\Big)\\

(3.6)

On the one hand, suppose there exists a positive constant
C_{\beta}
that depends solely on
\beta
and
\omega_{h}
such that

\sqrt{-1}\beta\wedge\bar{\beta}\leq C^{2}_{\beta}\omega_{h}\,.

Furthermore, the regularity of

\begin{split}\epsilon\Gamma^{\prime}\mathrm{Re}\Big(\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\bar{\beta}_{j}[\varphi_{s,k}]_{i}\Big)&\geq\epsilon\Gamma^{\prime}\Big|\mathrm{Re}\Big(\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\bar{\beta}_{j}[\varphi_{s,k}]_{i}\Big)\Big|\\
&\geq\epsilon\Gamma^{\prime}(0)\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\sqrt{-1}\beta\wedge\bar{\beta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\varphi_{s,k}\wedge\bar{\partial}\varphi_{s,k}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\geq\epsilon\Gamma^{\prime}(0)C(m)C_{\beta}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\omega_{h}\,.\end{split}

(3.7)

On the other hand, since

lies within the ball

-\epsilon c_{0}r_{0}\mathrm{Re}\Big(\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\bar{\beta}_{j}(z_{i}-z_{\min,i})\Big)\geq-2\epsilon c_{0}C_{\beta}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\omega_{h}\,.

(3.8)

Substituting the inequalities (

•

4
L^{q}
estimate and proof of Proposition
2.1

In this section, we aim to derive the lower bound estimate
\varphi(z_{\min})
as exhibited in Proposition
2.1
. Our proof will crucially depend on not only the inequality (
3.17
) presented in Lemma
3.8
but also on the
L^{q}
estimate elaborated upon in the subsequent subsection.

4.1
The
L^{q}
estimate

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varphi
be the smooth solution of (
1.10
). Then, there exist positive constants
q,C
depend only on the given data, such that

\int_{M}(-\varphi)^{q}{\rm d}\sigma\leq C\,.

(4.1)

Proof.

For the proof, we will adopt an approach inspired by
[
37
]
and
[
17
]
, which focuses on deriving
L^{q}\,
estimates for a broader class of Monge-Ampère type equations.

Firstly, the quaternionic
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonicity assumption
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}_{J}(M,\omega,\omega_{h})
inherently implies that

\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}\omega_{h}+\Delta_{I,g}\varphi+\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}W[\varphi]\geq 0\,.

This, in turn, yields a lower bound for the drift Laplacian of
\varphi
:

\Delta_{d}\varphi:=\Delta_{I,g}\varphi+\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}W[\varphi]\geq-\sup_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}\omega_{h}:=-C\,.

(4.2)

It is evident that
\Delta_{d}
is an elliptic operator of second order.

Now, we proceed to verify the desired inequality (
4.1
) by using the weak Harnack inequality. Select an appropriate open covering of
M
comprised of coordinate balls
\{B_{2r_{i}}(x_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
such that a subset of these balls, namely
\{B_{i}=B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, still covers
M
. Since
\varphi
is non-positive and satisfies the inequality (
4.2
), application of the weak Harnack inequality (cf.
[
23
, Theorem 9.22]
) leads to the conclusion that

\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(B_{i})}=\Big(\int_{B_{i}}(-\varphi)^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\Big(\inf_{B_{i}}(-\varphi)+1\Big)\,,

where
q,C>0
are constants that depend only on the covering and the background metric.

Observing that we have assumed
\sup_{M}\varphi=0
, it follows that there exists at least one index
j
for which
\inf_{B_{j}}(-\varphi)=-\sup_{B_{j}}\varphi=0
. Hence, we have
\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(B_{j})}\leq C
. This bound can be extended to all balls
B_{i}
such that
B_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
, indeed one can bound
\inf_{B_{i}}(-\varphi)
from above in terms of
\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(B_{j})}\,
:

\begin{split}\inf_{B_{i}}(-\varphi)&\leq\inf_{B_{i}\cap B_{j}}(-\varphi)\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{i}\cap B_{j})^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(B_{i}\cap B_{j})}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(B_{i}\cap B_{j})^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(B_{j})}\,.\end{split}

Reiterating the argument and in a finite number of steps, we will have bound
\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(B_{i})}
for each
i=1,\cdots,N
, and thus also
\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(M)}.
This completes our proof of inequality (
4.1
).
∎

4.2
Proof of Proposition
2.1

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})<-2
. If necessary, shrink the ball
B
to ensure that its radius
r_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}.
Taking into account all these factors, we observe that for every point
z\in B_{c_{0}r_{0}^{2}}\,
, it follows that

-\varphi(z)-c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}>-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2}>2-c_{0}\cdot(\frac{1}{2})^{2}>1.

Therefore, the function

\log\frac{-\varphi(z)-c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}}{(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\Big(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2}\Big)

(4.3)

is strictly positive in
B_{c_{0}r_{0}^{2}}
.

Select a number
p
such that
p>2m
, and then we define

v:=p\Big(\log\frac{-\varphi(z)-c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}}{(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}>0\,.

Given that the inequality
pt^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq qt+C_{p,q}
holds for all
t\geq 0
, with
C_{p,q}
being a positive constant
C_{p,q}
that depends only on both
p
and
q
, where
q
is a constant as specified in Lemma
4.1
, we can then apply subsequently apply this inequality to
v
, and as a consequence, the inequality (
3.16
) leads to the following

\begin{split}&\frac{p^{p}}{2^{p}}\log\frac{-\varphi(z)-c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}}{(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{2G}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{p}}v^{p}e^{2G}\leq e^{2G}(1+|G|)^{p}+C_{p}e^{v}\\
&\leq e^{2G}(1+|G|)^{p}+C_{p}\exp\Big\{q\log\frac{-\varphi(z)-c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}}{(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+C_{p,q}\Big\}\\
&=e^{2G}(1+|G|)^{p}+C_{p}\exp\{C_{p,q}\}\Big(\frac{-\varphi(z)-c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}}{(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)^{q}\,.\end{split}

(4.4)

By integrating over
B_{c_{0}r^{2}_{0}}\,
, we conclude that

\begin{split}&\frac{p^{p}}{2^{p+1}}C_{2}\log(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r^{2}_{0})\\
&\leq\int_{B_{c_{0}r^{2}_{0}}}\frac{p^{p}}{2^{p}}\log\frac{-\varphi(z)-c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}}{(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{2G}{\rm d}\sigma\\
&\leq\|e^{2G}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}+\frac{C_{p}\exp\{C_{p,q}\}}{\Big(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}}\int_{B_{c_{0}r^{2}_{0}}}\Big(-\varphi(z)-c_{0}|z-z_{\mathrm{min}}|^{2}\Big)^{q}{\rm d}\sigma\\
&\leq\|e^{2G}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}+\frac{C_{7}C_{p}\exp\{C_{p,q}\}}{(-\varphi(z_{\mathrm{min}})-c_{0}r_{0}^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}}\,,\end{split}

which yields the desired inequality stated in this proposition. Here, the second line utilizes inequalities (
3.17
) and (
4.3
), while the third line stems from the application of (
4.4
). The fourth line, in turn, is a direct consequence of (
4.1
). This concludes our proof of the proposition.
∎

On the small boundary property, 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -absorption, and Bauer simplexes
George A. Elliott, Zhuang Niu

Abstract
. Let X X be a metrizable compact space, and let Δ \Delta be a closed set of Borel probability measures on X X . We study the small boundary property of the pair ( X , Δ ) (X,\Delta) . In particular, it is shown that ( X , Δ ) (X,\Delta) has the small boundary property if it has a certain divisibility property. As an application, it is shown that, if A A is the crossed product C*-algebra C ⁡ ( X ) ⋊ ℤ d \mathrm{C}(X)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{d} , where ( X , ℤ d ) (X,\mathbb{Z}^{d}) is a free minimal topological dynamical system, or if A A is an AH algebra with diagonal maps, then A A is 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -absorbing if the set of extreme tracial states is compact, regardless of its dimension.

Introduction

The small boundary property was introduced in
[
11
]
as a dynamical systems analogue of the usual definition of zero dimensional space. It was shown to be equivalent to zero mean dimension (
[
10
]
; see
[
6
]
for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions), and implies the
\mathcal{Z}
-stability of the crossed product C*-algebra

(
[

]
). In this paper, we shall formulate the small boundary property for a pair
(X,\Delta)
(see Definition
2.2
), where
X
is a compact metrizable space and
\Delta
is a closed set of Borel probability measures on
X

\Delta
consists of the invariant probability measures, but also includes the examples where
\mathrm{C}(X)
is a Cartan subalgebra of
A

As suggested by the arguments of
[
10
]

[

]
, the small boundary property can be characterized in the context of the uniform trace norm:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
2.9
)
.

(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP) if, and only if, for any continuous real-valued function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a continuous real-valued function
g:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

\left\|f-g\right\|_{2,\Delta}<\varepsilon
, and

(2)

\mu(g^{-1}(0))<\varepsilon
for all
\mu\in\Delta

This property of

(Definition

2.1.
Small boundary property

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a compact metrizable space, and let
\Delta
be a set of Borel probability measures. The following properties are equivalent:

(1)

for any subsets
V\subseteq U
, where
V
is closed and
U
is open, there is a closed set
V^{\prime}
such that
V\subseteq V^{\prime}\subseteq U
, and
\mu(\partial V^{\prime})=0
, for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

(2)

for any subsets
V\subseteq U
, where
V
is closed and
U
is open, there is an open set
V^{\prime}
such that
V\subseteq V^{\prime}\subseteq U
, and
\mu(\partial V^{\prime})=0
, for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

(3)

for any
x\in X
and any open set
U\ni x
, there is a closed neighbourhood
V\subseteq U
of
x
such that
\mu(\partial V)=0
, for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

(4)

for any
x\in X
and any open set
U\ni x
, there is an open neighbourhood
V\subseteq U
of
x
such that
\mu(\partial V)=0
, for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\mathrm{int}(W)}\setminus\mathrm{int}(W)\subseteq\partial W=W\setminus\mathrm{int}(W)
for any closed set
W
, (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) and (3)
\Rightarrow
(4) are immediate.

For (2)
\Rightarrow
(3), choose an open set
U^{\prime}
with
\overline{U^{\prime}}\subseteq U
. Since
\{x\}
is closed, by
(2)
, there is an open set
V\ni x
such that
V\subseteq U^{\prime}
and
\mu(\partial V)=0
for all
\mu\in\Delta
. Then the closed neighbourhood
\overline{V}
of
x
satisfies (3).

For (4)
\Rightarrow
(1), choose an open set
U^{\prime}
with
V\subseteq U^{\prime}
and
\overline{U^{\prime}}\subseteq U
. Since
V
is compact, by (4), there is an open cover
\{V_{1},V_{2},...,V_{n}\}
of
V
such that
V_{i}\subseteq U^{\prime}
,
i=1,...,n
and
\mu(\partial V_{i})=0
,
i=1,...,n
, and
\mu\in\Delta
. Then
V^{\prime}:=\overline{V_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup\overline{V_{n}}
satisfies (1).
∎

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a metrizable compact space, fixed for the rest of the paper, and let
\Delta
be a closed set of Borel probability measures on
X
. Then the pair
(X,\Delta)
(or the pair
(\mathrm{C}(X),\Delta)
, where
\Delta
is regarded as as closed set of tracial state of
\mathrm{C}(X)
) is said to have the Small Boundary Property (SBP) if the equivalent conditions of Lemma
2.1
are satisfied.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Delta
be a closed set of Borel probability measures on
X
. Let
c\geq 0
, and let
V\subseteq X
be a closed set with
\mu(V)<c
for all
\mu\in\Delta
. Then there is an open set
U\supseteq V
such that
\mu(U)<c
for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

Proof.

Assume the statement were not true. Then there would exist a decreasing sequence of open sets
(U_{i})
with
\overline{U_{i+1}}\subseteq U_{i}
and
\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i}=V
and a sequence
(\mu_{i})
in
\Delta
such that

\mu_{i}(U_{i})\geq c
for all
i=1,2,...
(and hence
\mu_{k}(U_{i})\geq c
for all
k\geq i
,
k
,
i=1,2,...
). Since
\Delta
is closed (and so compact), passing to a subsequence, one may assume that
(\mu_{i})
converges to a measure
\mu_{\infty}\in\Delta
. Then, since

\mu_{\infty}(U_{i})\geq\mu_{\infty}(\overline{U_{i+1}})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(\overline{U_{i+1}})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}(U_{i+1})\geq c,

and
\mu_{\infty}\in\Delta
, one has

c>\mu_{\infty}(V)=\mu_{\infty}(\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{\infty}(U_{i})\geq c,

which is absurd.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(Proposition 5.3 of
[
11
]
)
.

If
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP), then, for every open cover
\alpha
of
X
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a subordinate partition of unity
\phi_{i}:X\to[0,1]
,
i=1,2,...,\left|\alpha\right|
, such that

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\alpha\right|}\phi_{i}(x)=1
,
x\in X
,

(2)

\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{i})\subseteq U
for some
U\in\alpha
, and
i=1,2,...,\left|\alpha\right|
, and

(3)

\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\left|\alpha\right|}\phi_{i}^{-1}(0,1))<\varepsilon
for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

Proof.

List
\alpha=\{U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{\left|\alpha\right|}\}
.
Using the (SBP), for each
i=1,...,\left|\alpha\right|
, one finds an open set
U^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq U_{i}
such that
U^{\prime}_{i}
,
i=1,2,...,\left|\alpha\right|
, still form an open cover of
X
, and
\mu(\partial U^{\prime}_{i})=0
for all
\mu\in\Delta
. By Lemma
2.3
, there is
\delta>0
such that

\mu((\partial U^{\prime}_{i})_{\delta})<\varepsilon/\left|\alpha\right|,

where
(\partial U^{\prime}_{i})_{\delta}
is the
\delta
-neighbourhood of
\partial U^{\prime}
. One may assume that
\delta
is small enough that
(\partial U^{\prime}_{i})_{\delta}\subseteq U_{i}
for each
i=1,...,\left|\alpha\right|
.

Define

\psi_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&x\in U^{\prime}_{i},\\
\max(0,1-\delta^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial U^{\prime}_{i})),&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then, define

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\phi_{1}(x)&=&\psi_{1}(x),\\
\phi_{2}(x)&=&\min(\psi_{2},1-\phi_{1}(x)),\\
\phi_{3}(x)&=&\min(\psi_{3},1-\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{2}(x)),\\
&\vdots&\\
\phi_{\left|\alpha\right|}(x)&=&\min(\psi_{\left|\alpha\right|},1-\phi_{1}-\cdots-\phi_{\left|\alpha\right|-1}(x)).\end{array}\right.

Then,
\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{i})\subseteq U_{i}
,
i=1,...,\left|\alpha\right|
, and

\bigcup_{i=1}^{\left|\alpha\right|}\phi_{i}^{-1}(0,1)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{\left|\alpha\right|}\psi_{i}^{-1}(0,1).

∎

The following lemma is contained in the proof of Theorem 6.2 of

Lemma 2.5

(Section 6 of
[
10
]
; Section 5 of
[
6
]
)
.

(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP) if, and only if, for any continuous real-valued function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a continuous real-valued function
g:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

\left\|f-g\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
,

(2)

\mu(g^{-1}(0))<\varepsilon
for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

Proof.

Assume the properties of the lemma. In order to show that
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP), it is enough to show that the set

\{f\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X):\mu(f^{-1}(0))=0,\ \mu\in\Delta\}

is dense in
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
(it is always a
G_{\delta}
set; see Lemma 6.4 of
[

]

x\in X
and let
U
be an open neighbourhood of
x
, and pick a continuous function
f:X\to[-1,1]
such that
f(x)=-1
and
f(X\setminus U)=\{1\}
. Since the set above is dense, there is
g
such that
\left\|g-f\right\|<1/4
and
\mu(g^{-1}(0))=0
for all
\mu\in\Delta
. Consider the set
V=g^{-1}([-1,0])\subseteq U
. It is a closed neighbourhood of
x
with
\partial V=g^{-1}(0)
, and hence
\mu(\partial V)=0
for all
\mu\in\Delta
. So
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP).

For each
c>0
, consider the set

Z_{c}:=\{f\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X):\mu(f^{-1}(0))<c,\ \mu\in\Delta\}.

Note that the properties of the lemma imply that
Z_{c}
is dense in
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
.

Let us show that
Z_{c}
is also open in
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
. Indeed, let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that
\mu(f^{-1}(0))<c
for all
\mu\in\Delta
, and consider the closed set
V:=f^{-1}(0)
. By Lemma
2.3
, there is an open set
U\supseteq V
such that
\mu(U)<c
for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

Therefore, the set

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X):\mu(f^{-1}(0))=0,\ \mu\in\Delta\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\{f\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X):\mu(f^{-1}(0))<1/n,\ \mu\in\Delta\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}Z_{1/n}

is dense in
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
(a dense
G_{\delta}
, in fact), and
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP).

For the converse, assume that
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP), and let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and any
\varepsilon>0
be given. By Lemma
2.4
, there exists a partition of unity
\phi_{i}:X\to[0,1]
,
i=1,2,...,N
, such that

(2.1)

\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{i}^{-1}((0,1))<\varepsilon,\quad\mu\in\Delta,

\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|<\varepsilon/2,\quad x,y\in\phi_{i}^{-1}(0,1]),\ i=1,...,N.

For each
i=1,...,N

x_{i}\in\phi_{i}^{-1}(0,1]
and a real number

such that

y_{i}\neq 0\quad\textrm{and}\quad\left|y_{i}-f(x_{i})\right|<\varepsilon/2.

Define

g=\sum_{i=1}^{N}y_{i}\phi_{i}.

Note that for each
x\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{i}^{-1}(1)
, one has that
g(x)\neq 0
, and hence

g^{-1}(0)\subseteq X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{i}^{-1}(1)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{i}^{-1}((0,1)).

By (
2.1
), direct calculations show that

(1)

\left\|f-g\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
, and

(2)

\mu(g^{-1}(0))<\varepsilon
for all
\mu\in\Delta
,

as desired.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Delta
be a closed subset of probability Borel measures on
X
. Then define

\left\|f\right\|_{2,\Delta}=\sup\{(\int\left|f\right|^{2}d\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}:\mu\in\Delta\},\quad f\in\mathrm{C}(X).

Lemma 2.7

(Markov’s inequality)
.

\mu(E_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int\left|f\right|^{2}d\mu,

where
E_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in X:\left|f(x)\right|>\varepsilon\}
.

Proof.

\varepsilon^{2}\mu(E_{\varepsilon})=\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{2}d\mu\leq\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}\left|f\right|^{2}d\mu+\int_{E_{\varepsilon}^{c}}\left|f\right|^{2}d\mu=\int\left|f\right|^{2}d\mu.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f,g
be real-valued continuous functions on
X
satisfying

\left\|f-g\right\|_{2,\Delta}^{2}<\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{8}

and

\mu(g^{-1}(0))<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad\mu\in\Delta.

Then, there is a real-valued continuous function
g^{\prime}
on
X
such that

(1)

\left\|f-g^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
, and

(2)

\mu((g^{\prime})^{-1}(0))<\varepsilon
for all
\mu\in\Delta
.

Proof.

Define

E_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in X:\left|f(x)-g(x)\right|>\varepsilon/2\}.

Then, by Markov’s inequality,

\mu(E_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|f-g\right\|_{2,\Delta}^{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad\mu\in\Delta.

Pick an open neighbourhood
U
of
E_{\varepsilon}^{c}
such that

\left|f(x)-g(x)\right|<\varepsilon,\quad x\in U,

and pick a continuous function
h:X\to[0,1]
such that

h|_{E_{\varepsilon}^{c}}=1\quad\mathrm{and}\quad h|_{U^{c}}=0.

Define

g^{\prime}(x)=h(x)g(x)+(1-h(x))f(x).

Then

\left|f(x)-g^{\prime}(x)\right|<\varepsilon,\quad x\in X.

Moreover, since

(g^{\prime})^{-1}(0)\subseteq g^{-1}(0)\cup E_{\varepsilon},

one has

\mu((g^{\prime})^{-1}(0))\leq\mu(g^{-1}(0))+\mu(E_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon,

as desired.

2.2.
Real rank zero

It is useful to characterize the (SBP) using the real rank zero property of a certain sequence algebra. Let us start with the following lemma:

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume that
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP). Let
f\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there are sequences
(g_{n}),(h_{n})
in
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
such that for each
n=1,2,...
,

(1)

\left\|f-g_{n}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
,

(2)

\tau_{\mu}(\left|g_{n}h_{n}-1\right|^{2})<1/2^{n}
for all
\mu\in\Delta
,

(3)

\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{\infty}<4/\varepsilon
,

(4)

\left\|g_{n}-g_{n+1}\right\|_{2,\Delta}<{1}/{2^{n}}
and
\left\|h_{n}-h_{n+1}\right\|_{2,\Delta}<{1}/{2^{n}}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, one may assume that
\varepsilon<1
and
\left\|f\right\|=1
.

Choose an open cover
\{U_{1},...,U_{N}\}
of
X
such that

\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|<\varepsilon/2,\quad x,y\in U_{i},\ i=1,...,N.

By Lemma
2.4
, there is a partition of unity
\phi^{(1)}_{i}:X\to[0,1]
, subordinate to
U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{N},
such that

(2.2)

\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}(\phi^{(1)}_{i})^{-1}((0,1)))<\varepsilon/2N{({4}/{\varepsilon}+5)^{2}},\quad\mu\in\Delta.

Consider the collection of open sets

(2.3)

U^{(1)}_{i}=(\phi_{i}^{(1)})^{-1}((0,1]),\quad i=1,2,...,N,

which is an open cover of
X
satisfying

U_{i}^{(1)}\subseteq U_{i},\quad i=1,...,N.

Using Lemma
2.4
again, one obtains
a partition of unity
\phi^{(2)}_{i}:X\to[0,1]
, subordinate to
U^{(1)}_{1},U^{(1)}_{2},...,U^{(1)}_{N}
, such that

\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}(\phi^{(2)}_{i})^{-1}((0,1)))<\varepsilon/2^{2}{N({4}/{\varepsilon}+5)^{2}},\quad\mu\in\Delta.

Noting that, for each
i=1,...,N
, by (
2.3
),
\phi^{(2)}_{i}(x)=1
for all
x
satisfying
\phi^{(1)}_{i}(x)=1
(so
x\notin U_{j}^{(1)}
,
j\neq i
), one then has

(\phi_{i}^{(2)})^{-1}((0,1))\subseteq(\phi^{(1)}_{i})^{-1}((0,1))\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}(\phi^{(1)}_{i})^{-1}((0,1)),

and therefore, by (
2.2
),

\left\|\phi_{i}^{(1)}-\phi_{i}^{(2)}\right\|_{2,\Delta}<\varepsilon/2N{({4}/{\varepsilon}+5)^{2}}<\varepsilon/2N.

Repeating this process, one obtains partitions of unity
\phi^{(n)}_{i}:X\to[0,1]
,
i=1,2,...,N
, subordinate to
U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{N}
, such that

(2.4)

\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}(\phi^{(n)}_{i})^{-1}((0,1)))<\varepsilon/N2^{n}{({4}/{\varepsilon}+5)^{2}},\quad\mu\in\Delta,

and

(2.5)

\left\|\phi_{i}^{(n)}-\phi_{i}^{(n+1)}\right\|_{2,\Delta}<{\varepsilon}/{2^{n}}N.

For each
i=1,...,N
, pick
x_{i}\in U_{i}
and then pick a real number
y_{i}
such that

\left|y_{i}\right|>\varepsilon/4\quad\textrm{and}\quad\left|y_{i}-f(x_{i})\right|<\varepsilon/2.

Define

g_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}y_{i}\phi^{(n)}_{i}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad h_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{y_{i}}\phi^{(n)}_{i}.

Then

\left\|f-g_{n}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon

and

\left|h_{n}(x)\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\phi^{(n)}_{i}(x)}{\left|y_{i}\right|}<\frac{4}{\varepsilon},\quad x\in X.

Note that

(g_{n}h_{n})(x)=1
whenever
x\in\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}(\phi^{(n)}_{i})^{-1}(1)
, and then, together with (
2.4
), for all
\mu\in\Delta
,

\tau_{\mu}(\left|g_{n}h_{n}-1\right|^{2})<(\frac{4\left\|g_{n}\right\|}{\varepsilon}+1)^{2}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{n}N(\frac{4}{\varepsilon}+5)^{2}}<(\frac{4(1+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}+1)^{2}\frac{1}{2^{n}(\frac{4}{\varepsilon}+5)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

By (
2.5
),

\left\|g_{n}-g_{n+1}\right\|_{2,\Delta}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}y_{i}(\phi_{i}^{(n)}-\phi_{i}^{(n+1)})\right\|_{2,\Delta}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|y_{i}\right|\left\|\phi_{i}^{(n)}-\phi_{i}^{(n+1)}\right\|_{2,\Delta}\leq 1/2^{n}

and

\left\|h_{n}-h_{n+1}\right\|_{2,\Delta}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{y_{i}}(\phi_{i}^{(n)}-\phi_{i}^{(n+1)})\right\|_{2,\Delta}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\frac{1}{y_{i}}\right|\left\|\phi_{i}^{(n)}-\phi_{i}^{(n+1)}\right\|_{2,\Delta}\leq 1/2^{n},

as desired.
∎

Consider
l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))
and consider the ideal

J_{2,\omega,\Delta}:=\{(f_{1},f_{2},...)\in l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X)):\lim_{n\to\omega}(\sup\{\tau(\left|f_{n}\right|^{2}):\tau\in\Delta\})=0\}.

In general, if
A
is a C*-algebra and
\Delta
is a closed set of tracial states of
A
, one also considers
l^{\infty}(A)
and the ideal

J_{2,\omega,\Delta}:=\{(a_{1},a_{2},...)\in l^{\infty}(A):\lim_{n\to\omega}(\sup\{\tau(\left|a_{n}\right|^{2}):\tau\in\Delta\})=0\}.

Theorem 2.12
.

The C*-algebra
l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}
has real rank zero if, and only if,
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP).

Proof.

Assume that
l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}
has real rank zero. Let
f\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
, and let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Consider the image of the constant sequence
\overline{(f,f,...)}\in l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}.
By the real rank zero assumption, there is an invertible self-adjoint element
\overline{(g_{1},g_{2},...)}\in l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}
such that

\left\|\overline{(f,f,...)}-\overline{(g_{1},g_{2},...)}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon.

In particular,

(2.6)

\left\|\overline{(f,f,...)}-\overline{(g_{1},g_{2},...)}\right\|_{2,\omega,\Delta}<\varepsilon,

where

\left\|(a_{1},a_{2},...)\right\|_{2,\omega,\Delta}=\lim_{n\to\omega}(\sup\{\tau(\left|a_{n}\right|^{2}):\tau\in\Delta\}),\quad(a_{1},a_{2},...)\in l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X)).

Since
\overline{(g_{1},g_{2},...)}
is invertible, there is
\delta>0
such that (with
\chi_{\delta}
as in Theorem
2.9
)

\chi_{\delta}(\overline{(g_{1},g_{2},...)})=0.

Hence,

\mathrm{dist}_{2,\omega,\Delta}((f-g_{1},f-g_{2},...),J_{2,\omega,\Delta})<\varepsilon\quad\mathrm{and}\quad(\chi_{\delta}(g_{1}),\chi_{\delta}(g_{2}),...)\in J_{2,\omega,\Delta},

and then, with
n
sufficiently close to
\omega
,

\left\|f-g_{n}\right\|_{2,\Delta}<\varepsilon\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\tau(\chi_{\delta}(g_{n}))<\varepsilon,\quad\tau\in\Delta.

By Theorem
2.9
,
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP).

Now, assume that
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP), and let us show that
l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}
has real rank zero. Let
f\in l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}
be a self-adjoint element, and let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Pick a self-adjoint representative
(f_{1},f_{2},...)
of
f
. By Lemma
2.11
(with
f=f_{n}
,
n=1,2,...
), there are self-adjoint elements
g_{1},g_{2},...
and
h_{1},h_{2},...
in
\mathrm{C}(X)
such that for each
n=1,2,...
,

(1)

\left\|f_{n}-g_{n}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
,

(2)

\tau_{\mu}(\left|g_{n}h_{n}-1\right|^{2})<1/n
for all
\mu\in\Delta
, and

(3)

\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{\infty}<4/\varepsilon
.

Then, with
g:=\overline{(g_{1},g_{2},...)}
and

in

\left\|f-g\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\quad\mathrm{and}\quad gh=1.

That is,

is invertible. Since

is arbitrary, this shows that

has real rank zero.
∎

Note that in the proof of
\mathrm{(RR0)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(SBP)}
above, it is the following weak real rank zero property that is used: each self-adjoint element is approximated in the trace norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2,\omega,\Delta}
by invertible self-adjoint elements (see (

Corollary 2.13
.

The pair
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP) if (and only if) the invertible self-adjoint elements of

are dense in the set of self-adjoint elements with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2,\omega,\Delta}

Thus, we also have the following (possibly slightly surprising) corollary:

Corollary 2.14
.

The C*-algebra
l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}
has real rank zero if (and only if) its invertible self-adjoint elements are dense in the set of self-adjoint elements with respect to

The following corollary (directly of Theorem
2.12
) is straightforward:

Corollary 2.15
.

Consider
(X,\Delta_{1})
and
(Y,\Delta_{2})
such that

has the (SBP) if, and only if,
(Y,\Delta_{2})
has the (SBP).

Inside
l^{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X))/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}
, consider the sub-C*-algebra consisting of the classes of bounded sequences of

which are
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2,\Delta}
-Cauchy. This C*-algebra is the closure of
\mathrm{C}(X)
inside

under the norm

.

Theorem 2.16
.

If the pair
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP), then any constant sequence in
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
can be approximated in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
by self-adjoint invertible elements of
\overline{\mathrm{C}(X)}^{\Delta}

On the other hand, if any constant sequence in
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
can be approximated in

Assume that any self-adjoint element of
\mathrm{C}(X)\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{C}(X)}^{\Delta}
is in the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2,\omega,\Delta}
closure of invertible self-adjoint elements. Let

, and let

be arbitrary. Consider the image of the constant sequence

\left\|\overline{(f,f,...)}-\overline{(g_{1},g_{2},...)}\right\|_{2,\omega,\Delta}<\varepsilon.

Then there is

\chi_{\delta}(\overline{(g_{1},g_{2},...)})=0.

Hence

and so, with sufficiently large

2.9
,
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP).

Now, assume that
(X,\Delta)
has the (SBP). It follows from Lemma

that a constant sequence
(f)
with

∎

Since

Corollary 2.17
.

The pair
(X,\Delta)

.

In contrast to Theorem

3.
A stronger version of uniform property
\Gamma
and the small boundary property

In this section, let us use the characterization of the (SBP) of Theorem
2.9
to connect it to a notion of approximate divisibility on traces (Theorem
3.5
).

Recall (
[
2
]
) that a C*-algebra
A
is said to have the uniform McDuff property if for each
n
, there is a unital embedding

\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to(l^{\infty}(A)/J_{2,\omega,\Delta})\cap A^{\prime},

and the C*-algebra
A
is said to have uniform property
\Gamma
if for each
n
, there is a partition of unity

p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n}\in(l^{\infty}(A)/J_{2,\omega,\Delta})\cap A^{\prime}

such that

\tau(p_{i}ap_{i})=\frac{1}{n}\tau(a),\quad a\in A,\ i=1,2,...,n,\ \tau\in\mathrm{T}(A)_{\omega},

where
\mathrm{T}(A)_{\omega}
is the set of traces of
l^{\infty}(A)
of the form

\tau((a_{i}))=\lim_{i\to\omega}\tau_{i}(a_{i}),\quad\tau_{i}\in\mathrm{T}(A),

and
a
is regarded as the constant sequence
(a)\in l^{\infty}(A)
.

Let us introduce the following stronger versions of these properties, restricting the partitions to belong to a subalgebra
D\subseteq A
:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra and let
D\subseteq A
be a sub-C*-algebra. The pair
(D,A)
is said to have the strong uniform McDuff property if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a unital embedding

\phi:\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to(l^{\infty}(A)/J_{2,\omega,\Delta})\cap A^{\prime}

such that

\phi(e_{ii})\in l^{\infty}(D)/J_{2,\omega,\Delta},\quad i=1,...,n.

The pair
(D,A)
is said to have the strong uniform property
\Gamma
if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a partition of unity

p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n}\in(l^{\infty}(D)/J_{2,\omega,\Delta})\cap A^{\prime}

such that

\tau(p_{i}ap_{i})=\frac{1}{n}\tau(a),\quad a\in A,\ \tau\in\mathrm{T}(A)_{\omega},

where
\mathrm{T}(A)_{\omega}
is as above.

Let us now formulate weaker versions of these two properties, relaxing the approximate centrality of
p_{1},...,p_{n}
, or removing the algebra
A
altogether.

Definition 3.2
.

A pair
(D,A)
, where
A
is a unital C*-algebra and
D\subseteq A
is a unital subalgebra, will be said to be (tracially) approximately divisible if there is
K>0
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a partition of unity

p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n}\in l^{\infty}(D)/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}

such that

\tau(p_{i}ap_{i})\leq\frac{1}{n}K\tau(a),\quad a\in D^{+},\ \tau\in\mathrm{T}(A)_{\omega},\ i=1,...,n.

Similarly, a pair
(D,\Delta)
(or the pair
(X,\Delta)
), where
D=\mathrm{C}(X)
and
\Delta
is a closed set of Borel probability measures, is said to be approximately divisible if there is
K>0
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a partition of unity

p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n}\in l^{\infty}(D)/J_{2,\omega,\Delta}

such that

\tau(p_{i}ap_{i})\leq\frac{1}{n}K\tau(a),\quad a\in D^{+},\ \tau\in\Delta_{\omega},\ i=1,...,n,

where
\Delta_{\omega}
is the set of traces of
l^{\infty}(D)
of the form

\tau((a_{i}))=\lim_{i\to\omega}\tau_{i}(a_{i}),\quad\tau_{i}\in\Delta.

Remark 3.3
.

It is clear that the strong uniform McDuff property of
(D,A)
implies the strong uniform property
\Gamma
(and in particular approximate divisibility).

The property of approximate divisibility can be formulated locally as follows:

Lemma 3.4
.

The pair
(D,\Delta)
is approximately divisible if, and only if, there is
K>0
such that for any finite set
\mathcal{F}\subseteq D^{+}
, any
\varepsilon>0
, and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there are positive contractions
p_{1},...,p_{n}\in D
such that

(1)

\left\|p_{i}p_{j}\right\|_{2,\Delta}<\varepsilon
if
i\neq j
,

(2)

\left\|p_{i}-p_{i}^{2}\right\|_{2,\Delta}<\varepsilon
,
i=1,...,n
,

(3)

\left\|1-(p_{1}^{2}+\cdots+p_{n}^{2})\right\|_{2,\Delta}<\varepsilon
,

(4)

\tau(p_{i}fp_{i})<\frac{1}{n}K\tau(f)+\varepsilon
,
i=1,...,n
,
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
\tau\in\Delta
.

It turns out that approximate divisibility implies the (SBP):

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
D
be a commutative C*-algebra and let
\Delta
be a closed set of probability Borel measures. If
(D,\Delta)
is approximately divisible, then
(D,\Delta)
has the (SBP).

The converse holds in the case that
\Delta
is
\mathrm{T}(D\rtimes\Gamma)
restricted to
D\subseteq D\rtimes\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is a discrete amenable group acting freely on
D
.

Let us start with some preparations:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{C}(X)
be a self-adjoint element, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and self-adjoint elements
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}\in\mathrm{C}(X)
, such that

(1)

\left\|f-f_{i}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
,
i=1,2,...,n
, and

(2)

\frac{1}{n}|\{1\leq i\leq n:f_{i}(x)=0\}|<\varepsilon,\quad x\in X.

Proof.

Choose an open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
such that

\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|<\varepsilon,\quad x,y\in U,\ U\in\mathcal{U}.

Choose a partition of unity
\{\phi_{U}:U\in\mathcal{U}\}
, subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
, and choose
x_{U}\in U
for each
U\in\mathcal{U}
. Consider the function

g:=\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}f(x_{U})\phi_{U},

and it is straightforward to verify that

\left\|f-g\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon.

It is straightforward to check that
g
has the factorization

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{N(\mathcal{U})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R}}

where
N(\mathcal{U})
is the nerve complex of
\mathcal{U}
(see, for instance, Definition IX 2.2 of
[
4
]
for the definition of the nerve complex of an open cover), the map
\Phi:X\to N(\mathcal{U})
is given by

x\mapsto\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\phi_{U}(x)[U],

and the map
\Psi:N(\mathcal{U})\to\mathbb{R}
is the linear map

\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{U}[U]\mapsto\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}f(x_{U})\alpha_{U}.

Pick
n>\mathrm{dim}(N(\mathcal{U}))/\varepsilon
, and consider the map

(\Psi,...,\Psi):N(\mathcal{U})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By Lemma 5.7 of
[
6
]
, there is a map

(\Psi_{1},...\Psi_{n}):N(\mathcal{U})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that

\left\|\Psi-\Psi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon,\quad 1\leq i\leq n

and

|\{1\leq i\leq n:\Psi_{i}(y)=0\}|<\mathrm{dim}(N(\mathcal{U})),\quad y\in N(\mathcal{U}).

Then

(f_{1},...,f_{n}):=(\Psi_{1}\circ\Phi,...,\Psi_{n}\circ\Phi)

satisfies the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The open cover
\mathcal{U}
can be chosen to have order
1
, and hence the simplex
N(\mathcal{U})
to have dimension
1
. Therefore, the number
n
in the proof can be chosen to be
\lfloor 1/\varepsilon\rfloor+1
, which is independent of
X
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{C}(X)
be a self-adjoint element, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta>0
, and self-adjoint elements
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}\in\mathrm{C}(X)
such that

(1)

\left\|f-f_{i}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
,
i=1,2,...,n
, and

(2)

\frac{1}{n}|\{1\leq i\leq n:|f_{i}(x)|<\delta\}|<\varepsilon,\quad x\in X.

In particular,

\frac{1}{n}(\tau((f_{1})_{\delta})+\cdots+\tau((f_{n})_{\delta}))<\varepsilon,\quad\tau\in\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{C}(X)),

where
(f)_{\delta}=\chi_{\delta}(f)
with
\chi_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\chi_{\delta}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&|t|<\delta/2,\\
0,&|t|>\delta,\\
\textrm{linear},&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By the lemma above, there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and self-adjoint elements
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}\in\mathrm{C}(X)
such that

(1)

\left\|f-f_{i}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
,
i=1,2,...,n
, and

(2)

\frac{1}{n}|\{1\leq i\leq n:|f_{i}(x)|=0\}|<\varepsilon,\quad x\in X.

Since
f_{1},...f_{n}
are continuous, for each
x\in X
, there exist a neighbourhood
U_{x}\ni x
and
\delta_{x}>0
such that

\frac{1}{n}|\{1\leq i\leq n:|f_{i}(y)|<\delta_{x}\}|<\varepsilon,\quad y\in U_{x}.

Then the corollary follows from the compactness of
X
.
∎

It is worth pointing out that the corollary above holds for a general unital C*-algebra.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Let
f\in A
be a self-adjoint element, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta>0
, and self-adjoint elements
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}\in A
such that

(1)

\left\|f-f_{i}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
,
i=1,2,...,n
, and

(2)

\frac{1}{n}(\tau((f_{1})_{\delta})+\cdots+\tau((f_{n})_{\delta}))<\varepsilon,\quad\tau\in\mathrm{T}(A).

Proof.

The statement follows directly from Corollary
3.8
applied to the sub-C*-algebra
D:=\textrm{C*}\{1,f\}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

To show that the pair
(D,\Delta)
has the (SBP), by Corollary

, it is enough to show that for any self-adjoint element
f\in D
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a self-adjoint element
g\in D
such that

(1)

\|f-g\|_{2,\Delta}<\varepsilon
, and

(2)

\mu(g^{-1}(0))<\varepsilon
,
\mu\in\Delta
.

By Corollary
3.8
, for the given
\varepsilon
, there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
and self-adjoint elements

f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}\in D

such that

(3.1)

\left\|f-f_{i}\right\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon,\quad i=1,2,...,n,

and
there is

Definition 4.1

is the limit trace of

Example 4.2

Let

be a unital C*-algebra, and let

be a unital commutative sub-C*-algebra.
Then the set

is ample with respect to any positive contraction

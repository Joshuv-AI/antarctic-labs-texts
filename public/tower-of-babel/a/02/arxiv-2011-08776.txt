Annihilators of the ideal class group of a cyclic extension of a global function field
Pascal Stucky

Abstract
Let K K be a global function field and fix a place ∞ \infty of K K . Let L / K L/K be a finite real abelian extension, i.e. a finite, abelian extension such that ∞ \infty splits completely in L L . Then we define a group of elliptic units C L C_{L} in 𝒪 L × \mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times} analogously to Sinnott’s cyclotomic units and compute the index [ 𝒪 L × : C L ] [\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}:C_{L}] . In the second part of this article, we additionally assume that L L is a cyclic extension of prime power degree. Then we can use the methods from Greither and Kučera to take certain roots of these elliptic units and prove a result on the annihilation of the p p -part of the class group of L L .

1
Introduction

In
[
18
]
F. Thaine studied the relation of the ideal class group
Cl(L)
of a totally real absolutely abelian number field
L
and a certain group of cyclotomic units introduced by W. Sinnott in
[
16
]
. These can be used to produce annihilators of the
p
-Sylow subgroup
Cl(L)_{p}
of the ideal class group. His methods were generalized by K. Rubin to abelian extensions of an imaginary quadratic base field
K
in
[
15
]
, where the cyclotomic units are replaced by elliptic units. These approaches are closely related to Kolyvagin’s Euler system machinery, in fact, Rubin already works with so called
special numbers
in a quite general setting and then specialises to the case of an imaginary quadratic base field. This method yields nice results when
p
does not divide
[L:\mathbb{Q}]
(resp.
[L:K]
), but when
p
is a divisor of the degree of the extension, the obtained annihilation statement is not satisfying.

When
L/\mathbb{Q}
is a cyclic extension of degree
p^{k}
, the ideal class group (considered as the Galois group of the Hilbert class field of
L
) splits into a genus part (corresponding to the extension
F_{I}/L
, where
F_{I}
denotes the genus field of
L
) and a so-called
non-genus part
. In order to study this non-genus part
(\sigma-1)Cl(L)_{p}
, where
\sigma
denotes a generator of
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
, C. Greither and R. Kučera extended Rubin’s method in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
to so-called
semispecial numbers
. These satisfy weaker conditions but are still sufficient to produce annihilators. The source of semispecial numbers in this case are certain roots with respect to group ring valued exponents of Sinnott’s cyclotomic units.

It was shown by D. Burns and A. Hayward that the annihilation result of Greither and Kučera can also be deduced from the equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture (see
[
1
]
), however, the proof of Greither and Kučera is constructive whereas the method of Burns and Hayward uses abstract arguments. In particular, the explicit construction of the roots of circular units enables Greither and Kučera to refine their method in
[
6
]
and weaken the conditions on
L
to cover even more cases. They use results on Sinnott’s module from
[
5
]
which are formulated in an abstract way without specifying a certain extension of number fields. Hence, these results can also be used in other cases. This is done by H. Chapdelaine and R. Kučera in
[
2
]
, where they prove an annihilation result for a cyclic extension of an imaginary quadratic field of prime power degree. They take roots of elliptic units studied by H. Oukhaba in
[
13
]
to obtain semispecial numbers and can then adapt the methods of Greither and Kučera to this case.

In this article, we want to apply the methods described above to the case of global function fields. For this purpose, we explicitly construct elliptic units based on the torsion points of rank-1 sign-normalized Drinfeld modules as in
[
7
]
. As in the case of cyclotomic units (see e.g.
[
8
]
) there are several methods to construct elliptic units in an arbitrary real abelian extension of global function fields. We use the function field version of Sinnott’s cyclotomic units and are hence able to prove an index formula for this subgroup of the units of
L
analogously to the ones in the rational case (
[
16
]
) and in the imaginary quadratic case (
[
13
]
). There exist some other index formulas for elliptic units in function fields, e.g. by L. Yin (
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
) who studied cyclotomic units in ray class fields in the sense of L. Washington (
[
19
]
) or by H. Oukhaba (
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
) who studied elliptic units in extensions where at most one prime ideal ramifies in
L/K
. However, there is no discussion of an index formula for a general abelian extension of global function fields known to the author. Moreover, we can use the methods of Greither and Kučera to extract roots from the defined elliptic units and obtain an annihilation result similar to the one in the number field case.

The article has the following structure: For the convenience of the reader, we first present a collection of the necessary notation and state the main results afterwards. Then we introduce the elliptic units and prove an index formula for them (Sections
2
-
4
). This part will closely follow
[
13
]
. The rest of the article (Sections
5
-
8
) will deal with the desired annihilation result and will have the same structure as
[
2
]
.

1.2
Main results

Let
L/K
be a finite real abelian extension with Galois group
G
. Then the elliptic units
C_{L}
of
L
are essentially the norms of torsion points in
K_{\mathfrak{m}}
together with certain unramified units (for a precise definition see Section
2.3
). These form a subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
which has finite index given by

Theorem A
.

We get

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}:C_{L}]=\frac{(h_{K}w_{\infty})^{[L:K]-1}w_{K}h_{L}}{w_{L}h_{K}}\frac{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}[L\cap H_{\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}}:L_{(1)}]}{[L:L_{(1)}]}\frac{[\mathbb{Z}[G]:U^{\prime}]}{d(L)}\ .

Remarks
.

•

The Sinnott module
U^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}[G]
is defined in
[
16
]
. The Sinnott index
[\mathbb{Z}[G]:U^{\prime}]
as well as the number
d(L)
can be computed in certain cases (cf. Remark
4.6
and Proposition
4.5
).

•

This index formula is the analogue of
[
16
, Thm. 4.1]
and
[
13
, Thm. 1]
. It is proven as Theorem
4.4
.

Now let
p
be an odd prime not dividing the class number
h_{K}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the characteristic of
K
and the number of roots of unity
w_{K}
of
K
. Suppose that
L/K
is cyclic of degree
p^{k}
for some
k>0
, then we can define
\mathcal{C}_{L}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
satisfying
\mathcal{C}_{L}^{h_{K}}\cdot\mu(L)=C_{L}
. Let
\eta
be a top generator of
\mathcal{C}_{L}
(precisely defined in Section
5
) and
\mathfrak{p}_{1},...,\mathfrak{p}_{s}
be the primes of
K
which ramify in
L
. We assume
s\geq 2
. Let
\sigma
be a generator of
\Gal(L/K)
. Then there is a certain subextension
K\subseteq L^{\prime}\subset L
such that we get

Theorem B
.

Define
y:=\prod_{i=2}^{s}(1-\sigma^{n_{i}})
, where
n_{i}
is the index of the decomposition group of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
in
\Gal(L/K)
. Then there exists a unique
\alpha\in L
with
\eta=\alpha^{y}
and
N_{L/L^{\prime}}(\alpha)=1
.

Remarks
.

•

The element
\alpha
from Theorem B is a semispecial number in the sense of Definition
7.3
(cf. Theorem
7.4
).

•

This result is the analogue of
[
6
, Thm. 1.2]
and
[
2
, 4.2]
. The field

is defined right before Theorem
5.12
.

Now we can extend

by

(taking a root for each subextension

) to obtain

\displaystyle[\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}:\overline{\mathcal{C}_{L}}]=w_{\infty}^{p^{k}-1}\cdot\frac{h_{L}}{h_{K}}\cdot\varphi_{L}^{-1}

There exists a certain number

\displaystyle\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gal(L/K)]}((\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}/\overline{\mathcal{C}_{L}})_{p})\subseteq\Ann_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gal(L/K)]}((1-\sigma^{p^{r}})Cl(\mathcal{O}_{L})_{p})\ .

.

This is the analogue of
[

and
[

2
Elliptic units in global function fields

Let
\Omega
be the completion of the algebraic closure of
K_{\infty}
and let
\Gamma
be a lattice in
\Omega
, i.e. a finitely generated projective
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module. The
exponential function
associated to
\Gamma
is defined by

\displaystyle e_{\Gamma}:\Omega

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\longmapsto z\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma\\
\gamma\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{z}{\gamma}\right).

We say that
\Gamma
is
special
, if the rank-1 Drinfeld module associated to
\Gamma
(see
[
7
, §5]
) is sign-normalized w.r.t. the fixed sign-function
sgn
. For each
\Gamma
, there exists an invariant
\xi(\Gamma)\in\Omega^{\times}
such that
\xi(\Gamma)\Gamma
is special. This invariant is unique up to multiplication by an element of
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
.

2.1
Unramified elliptic units

Following
[
12
, Sec. 2]
, we can fix a fractional ideal
\mathfrak{c}
of
K
and a choice of the invariant
\xi(\mathfrak{c})
such that the sign-normalized rank-1 Drinfeld module associated to
\Gamma:=\xi(\mathfrak{c})\mathfrak{c}
is exactly
\rho
. Let
D
be the differential of the twisted polynomial ring (see e.g.
[
7
, §4]
). Then for any non-zero integral ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
K
, the rank-1 Drinfeld module associated to
D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}})\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\Gamma
is sign-normalized w.r.t
sgn
, hence we can choose

\xi(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\mathfrak{c})=D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}})\xi(\mathfrak{c})
. Any fractional ideal of
K
is of the form
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{-1}\mathfrak{c}
and setting
\tau:=(\mathfrak{d}^{-1}\mathfrak{c},K_{(1)}/K)
we can define

\displaystyle\xi(\mathfrak{d})=\frac{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{b}})}{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}})^{\tau}}\xi(\mathfrak{c}).

Lemma 2.1
.

The element
\xi(\mathfrak{d})
is well-defined, i.e. it is independent of the choice of
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
. It depends on the choice of
\mathfrak{c}
and
\xi(\mathfrak{c})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{-1}\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime-1}\mathfrak{c}
. This implies
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}
and hence

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}=\rho_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}}.

The ideal class group acts on the set of isomorphism classes of rank-1 Drinfeld modules and via this action we obtain (cf.
[
14
, Prop. 13.15]
)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}\rho_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{\rho}_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}=\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{\rho}_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}}\rho_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{\rho}_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}}=\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{\rho}_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}.

Since
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\neq 0
(we only consider nonzero ideals), we have
D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}})\neq 0
. Because of
\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}=\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}}=\tau\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{b}})}{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}})^{\tau}}\right)^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}})}{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}}})}=\frac{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\mathfrak{b}})}{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}})}{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}})}=\frac{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}})}{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}})}=\left(\frac{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}})}{D(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}})^{\tau}}\right)^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}}.

∎

With these definitions, we obtain the following explicit form of the principal ideal theorem

Lemma 2.2

(
[
12
, Lemma 3]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{d}_{1},\mathfrak{d}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{d}
be fractional ideals of
K
. Then the ideal
\mathfrak{d}_{2}\mathfrak{d}_{1}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{K_{(1)}}
is principal generated by
\xi(\mathfrak{d}_{1})/\xi(\mathfrak{d}_{2})
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\xi(\mathfrak{d}_{1})}{\xi(\mathfrak{d}_{2})}\right)^{(\mathfrak{d},K_{(1)}/K)}=\frac{\xi(\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{d}^{-1})}{\xi(\mathfrak{d}_{2}\mathfrak{d}^{-1})}.

Now let
\sigma\in\Gal(H/K)
be arbitrary and let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
be such that
(\mathfrak{a}^{-1},H/K)=\sigma
. Let
x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a generator of the principal ideal
\mathfrak{a}^{h}
, then we can define

\displaystyle\partial(\sigma):=\left(x\xi(\mathfrak{a})^{h}\right)^{w_{\infty}/w_{K}}.

Remark 2.3
.

(i)

The element
\partial(\sigma)^{w_{K}}
is well-defined, i.e. it is independent of the choice of
\mathfrak{a}
and
x
. Indeed, it is even independent of the choice of
\mathfrak{c}
and
\xi(\mathfrak{c})
: If
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
and
\xi^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c}^{\prime})
was used to define invariants
\xi^{\prime}(\mathfrak{d})
for any fractional ideal
\mathfrak{d}
, then
\xi^{\prime}(\mathfrak{d})\mathfrak{d}
would again correspond to a sign-normalized rank-1 Drinfeld module. Since these lattices only differ by an element of
\mu(H)
(see e.g.
[
12
, Sec. 2]
), we obtain
\xi(\mathfrak{d})=\zeta\xi^{\prime}(\mathfrak{d})
for some
\zeta\in\mu(H)
. Taking the
w_{\infty}
-th power kills the root of unity, so the element
\partial(\sigma)^{w_{K}}
will be the same.

(ii)

The above definition differs from the one given in
[
12
]
by the factor
1/w_{K}
in the exponent. This definition of
\partial(\sigma)
still depends on the choice of the generator
x
and of the ideal
\mathfrak{c}
and
\xi(\mathfrak{c})
. However, two different choices only differ by an element of
\mu(K)
. Since we are only interested in subgroups of the units containing
\mu(K)
, it suffices to define
\partial(\sigma)
"up to roots of unity".

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\sigma,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Gal(H/K)
. Then
\frac{\partial(\sigma_{1})}{\partial(\sigma_{2})}\in\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\times}
and

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial(\sigma_{1})}{\partial(\sigma_{2})}\right)^{\sigma}=\frac{\partial(\sigma_{1}\sigma)}{\partial(\sigma_{2}\sigma)}\ .

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma
2.2
and
[\mathcal{O}_{K_{(1)}}^{\times}:\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\times}]=\frac{w_{\infty}}{w_{K}}
(see
[
21
, Lemma 1.5 (1)]
).
∎

2.2
Ramified elliptic units

Using the exponential function, we can define the element

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\xi(\mathfrak{m})e_{\mathfrak{m}}(1)

for each integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}\neq(1)
. It is shown in
[
7
, §5]
that this element is a generator of the
\mathfrak{m}
-torsion points
\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{m}}
of the sign-normalized rank-1 Drinfeld module
\rho^{\prime}
associated to
\xi(\mathfrak{m})\mathfrak{m}
. The construction of
K_{\mathfrak{m}}
does not depend on the chosen Drinfeld module but only on the sign-function, hence
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\in K_{(1)}(\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{m}})=K_{\mathfrak{m}}
(cf.
[
7
, §4]
). Indeed, if
\mathfrak{b}
is an integral ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
\mathfrak{b}
is prime to
\mathfrak{m}
and
(\mathfrak{b},K_{(1)}/K)=(\mathfrak{m}^{-1},K_{(1)}/K)
one can show that
(\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c},K_{\mathfrak{m}}/K)
defines a bijection
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{m}}
(note that
\xi(\mathfrak{m})\mathfrak{m}
is associated to the Drinfeld module
\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}*\rho
, then use
[
7
, Thm. 4.12]
). It is also shown in
[
7
, Thm. 4.17]
that

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}:=-N_{K_{\mathfrak{m}}/H_{\mathfrak{m}}}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})=\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}^{w_{\infty}}\in H_{\mathfrak{m}}

is a unit if
\mathfrak{m}
is not a prime power and that
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}^{k}}
generates the ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{w_{\infty}/w_{k}}
.

Remark 2.5
.

(i)

The element
\lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
depends on the choice of
\mathfrak{c}
which was used to define the invariants
\xi(\mathfrak{m})
. As already noted in Remark
2.3
, changing
\mathfrak{c}
would change
\xi(\mathfrak{m})
by a root of unity in

2.4

Let

. Let

\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of

\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}\in G

\displaystyle\chi(\mathfrak{p})=\chi(\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}e_{T_{\mathfrak{p}}}).

Note that we have
\chi(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
if and only if

For a finite set of primes
S
of

-truncated

\displaystyle\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}(1-\chi(\mathfrak{p})N\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathrm{Re}(s)>1,

where the product runs over all primes of
K
which are not contained in

.

If

\displaystyle L(\chi,s)=L_{\emptyset}(\chi,s).

If

\displaystyle L_{S}(1,s)=\zeta_{K,S}(s)

-truncated Dedekind

-function of

We summarize some results on

-functions in the next

Proposition 2.10
.

(i)

has a meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane which will also be denoted by

. If

(ii)

We have

\displaystyle\zeta_{K}(s)=\frac{P(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-s})(1-q^{1-s})},

where

is a polynomial satisfying

and

.

(iii)

If

is a finite abelian extension of
K
with Galois group

and
\psi
is the inflation of
\chi
to
G^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle L_{S}(\chi,s)=L_{S}(\psi,s),

i.e. the

-function is invariant under inflation.

(iv)

We have

\displaystyle\zeta_{L}(s)=\zeta_{K}(s)\cdot\prod_{\chi\neq 1}L(\chi,s),

where the product runs over all non-trivial characters of
G
.

Proof.

(i)

This is
[
14
, Thm. 9.25]
.

(ii)

This is
[
14
, Thm. 5.9]
.

(iii)

This is
[

, Ch. VII, Thm. (10.4)(iii)]

(iv)

Notation
.

Let
L^{*}_{S}(\chi,0)
be the leading term of the Taylor expansion of

at

.

Now suppose that
L
is a
real
abelian extension of
K
. Define

lying over

(if the extension is clear, we will simply write

). Since

is real, these are exactly

\displaystyle(-d_{\infty}\ord_{w}(u_{i}))_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in S_{\infty}(L)\setminus\{w_{0}\}\\

(2.1)

We have

\displaystyle\zeta_{L,S_{\infty}}^{*}(0)

\displaystyle=-(\log(q))^{[L:K]-1}\frac{h_{L}R_{L}}{w_{L}}.

This is

\displaystyle\zeta_{K,\{\infty\}}^{*}(0)=-\frac{h}{w_{K}}

2.5
Kronecker’s Limit Formulae

We fix a prime
w_{0}\in S_{\infty}(H_{\mathfrak{m}})
. Then for each subfield
L
of
H_{\mathfrak{m}}
, there is a unique prime in
S_{\infty}(L)
below
w_{0}
. Since
\infty
splits completely in
H_{\mathfrak{m}}
, the valuations of these primes are compatible. By abuse of notation, we denote each of these valuations by
\ord_{\infty}
, i.e. for an element
x\in H_{\mathfrak{m}}
we implicitly set

\displaystyle\ord_{\infty}(x):=\ord_{w_{0}}(x)

and analogously for each subfield
L
of
H_{\mathfrak{m}}
. The same convention will be used for absolute values.

For
\mathfrak{n}\neq(1)
let
S_{\mathfrak{n}}:=\{\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}\mid\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime},\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{n}\}
be the support of
\mathfrak{n}
. Now we can state Kronecker’s second limit formula:

Proposition 2.13
.

(i)

Let
(1)\neq\mathfrak{n}\mid\mathfrak{m}
and let
\chi\in\widehat{\Gal(H_{\mathfrak{n}}/K)}
. Then we have

\displaystyle L_{S_{\mathfrak{n}}}(\chi,0)=\frac{1}{w_{\infty}}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gal(H_{\mathfrak{n}}/K)}\ord_{\infty}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\sigma})\chi(\sigma).

(ii)

For any non-trivial character
\chi\in\widehat{\Gal(H/K)}
, we have

\displaystyle L(\chi,0)=\frac{1}{w_{\infty}h}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gal(H/K)}\ord_{\infty}(\partial(\sigma))\chi(\sigma).

Proof.

Part (i) is exactly the last equation in
[
7
]
, whereas part (ii) follows directly from
[
12
, Proof of Prop. 3]
and Remark
2.3
.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

By the proposition above, we can regard the ramified elliptic units as Stark units. Indeed, if
\mathfrak{n}\neq(1)
then the set
S:=S_{\mathfrak{n}}\cup\{\infty\}
contains all places which ramify in
H_{\mathfrak{n}}/K
and
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\geq 2
. Moreover,
S
contains the completely split prime
\infty
. Then Stark’s conjecture (cf.
[
17
, Ch. IV, Conj. 2.2]
) predicts the existence of an element
\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{S}(\chi,0)=-\frac{1}{w_{H_{\mathfrak{n}}}}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gal(H_{\mathfrak{n}}/K)}\log\left\lvert\varepsilon^{\sigma}\right\rvert_{\infty}\chi(\sigma)

for all
\chi\in\widehat{\Gal(H_{\mathfrak{n}}/K)}
. By definition of the
L
-function, we obtain

\displaystyle L_{S}(\chi,s)=(1-\chi(\infty)N\infty^{-s})L_{S_{\mathfrak{n}}}(\chi,s)=(1-N\infty^{-s})L_{S_{\mathfrak{n}}}(\chi,s)

and hence

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{S}(\chi,0)

\displaystyle=\log(N\infty)L_{S_{\mathfrak{n}}}(\chi,0)=-\frac{1}{w_{\infty}}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gal(H_{\mathfrak{n}}/K)}\log\left(N\infty^{-\ord_{\infty}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\sigma})}\right)\chi(\sigma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{w_{\infty}}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gal(H_{\mathfrak{n}}/K)}\log\left\lvert\alpha_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\sigma}\right\rvert_{\infty}\chi(\sigma).

4
An index formula

We briefly recall the definition of Sinnott’s Index (see
[
13
, §4]
). Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space over
L=\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{R}
. A subgroup
X
of
V
is a
lattice
if
\rk_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)=\dim_{L}(V)
and
L\cdot X=V
. If
A
and
B
are lattices of
V
and
\gamma
is an automorphism of
V
such that
\gamma(A)=B
, then we define

\displaystyle[A:B]:=\left\lvert\det(\gamma)\right\rvert.

If
B\subseteq A
, then
[A:B]
is the usual group index. Now we can prove

Proposition 4.1

(cf.
[
13
, Prop. 7]
)
.

We have

\displaystyle[U^{\prime}_{0}:l_{L}^{*}(P_{L})]=\left(\frac{hw_{\infty}}{d_{\infty}}\right)^{[L:K]-1}\cdot\frac{w_{K}h_{L}R_{L}}{w_{L}h}

Proof.

Using Proposition
2.10
(iv) and

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{\{\infty\}}(\chi,0)=\log(N\infty)\cdot L(\chi,0)=d_{\infty}\log(q)\cdot L(\chi,0),

we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta^{*}_{L,S_{\infty}}(0)

\displaystyle=\zeta^{*}_{K,\{\infty\}}(0)\cdot\prod_{\chi\neq 1}L^{\prime}_{\{\infty\}}(\chi,0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{h}{w_{K}}(d_{\infty}\log(q))^{[L:K]-1}\prod_{\chi\neq 1}L(\chi,0).

Together with the analytic class number formula
2.11
, this yields

\displaystyle\prod_{\chi\neq 1}L(\chi,0)=\frac{w_{K}h_{L}R_{L}}{w_{L}hd_{\infty}^{[L:K]-1}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\det(\omega)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\prod_{\chi\neq 1}\chi(\omega)=(hw_{\infty})^{[L:K]-1}\prod_{\chi\neq 1}L(\overline{\chi},0)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{hw_{\infty}}{d_{\infty}}\right)^{[L:K]-1}\cdot\frac{w_{K}h_{L}R_{L}}{w_{L}h},

where the first equality follows from
[
16
, Lemma 1.2 (b)]
. Since this is non-zero, we find that multiplication by
\omega
is an automorphism of
V=\mathbb{Q}\cdot U^{\prime}_{0}
. By Corollary
3.4
we have

and hence the desired Sinnott index exists and is given by

\displaystyle[U^{\prime}_{0}:l_{L}^{*}(P_{L})]=[U^{\prime}_{0}:\omega U^{\prime}_{0}]=\left\lvert\det(\omega)\right\rvert=\left(\frac{hw_{\infty}}{d_{\infty}}\right)^{[L:K]-1}\cdot\frac{w_{K}h_{L}R_{L}}{w_{L}h}.

Let

Proposition 3.1
.

\mu_{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{r},\chi}=\mu_{a_{1},\chi}\ast\cdots\ast\mu_{a_{r},\chi}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\C\left(U\right)
and let
r=2
. Since
\mu_{K}
is normalized, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle U$}}$}}\nolimits f(g)\;d\mu_{a_{1},\chi}\ast\mu_{a_{2},\chi}(g)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle U$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle U$}}$}}\nolimits f(g_{1}g_{2})d\mu_{a_{1},\chi}(g_{1})d\mu_{a_{2},\chi}(g_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits f(k_{1}a_{1}k_{2}k_{3}a_{2}k_{4})\chi^{-1}(k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4})\;d\mu_{K}(k_{1})d\mu_{K}(k_{2})d\mu_{K}(k_{3})d\mu_{K}(k_{4})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\left(\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits f(k_{1}a_{1}k_{2}k_{3}a_{2}k_{4})\chi^{-1}(k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4})\;d\mu_{K}(k_{2})\right)d\mu_{K}(k_{1})d\mu_{K}(k_{3})d\mu_{K}(k_{4})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits f(k_{1}a_{1}k_{5}a_{2}k_{4})\chi^{-1}(k_{1}k_{5}k_{4})\;d\mu_{K}(k_{5})\right)d\mu_{K}(k_{1})d\mu_{K}(k_{3})d\mu_{K}(k_{4})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits f(k_{1}a_{1}k_{2}a_{2}k_{3})\chi^{-1}(k_{1}k_{2}k_{3})\;d\mu_{K}(k_{1})d\mu_{K}(k_{2})d\mu_{K}(k_{3})

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be a Borel subset of

{\mathds{1}}_{E}
be the characteristic function of

(i)

\mu_{a,\chi_{l}}(E)=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle U$}}$}}\nolimits{\mathds{1}}_{E}(x)\,d\mu_{a,\chi_{l}}(x)=\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{K}\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{K}{\mathds{1}}_{E}\left(k_{1}ak_{2}\right)\chi_{l}\left(k_{1}k_{2}\right)^{-1}d\mu_{K}\left(k_{1}\right)d\mu_{K}\left(k_{2}\right),

(ii)

Proof.

Consider the continuous mapping
\tau_{a}:K\times K\longrightarrow U
such that

\tau_{a}(k_{1},k_{2})=k_{1}ak_{2}.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}_{a}
be the push-forward of the product measure
\mu_{K}\times\mu_{K}
on

under the map

, i.e., for a Borel subset

of

Hence

\lambda_{a}^{\prime}\left(E\right)=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{K}\mathop{\text{\Large$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{K}{\mathds{1}}_{E}\left(k_{1}ak_{2}\right)d\mu_{K}\left(k_{1}\right)d\mu_{K}\left(k_{2}\right).

By (Corollary 1
[
13
]

is regular.

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(E)=\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{K}\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{K}{\mathds{1}}_{E}\left(k_{1}ak_{2}\right)\chi_{l}\left(k_{1}k_{2}\right)^{-1}d\mu_{K}\left(k_{1}\right)d\mu_{K}\left(k_{2}\right).

4.

f_{a_{1},\dots,a_{r},\chi_{l}}

Let
\chi:K\longrightarrow GL\left(E_{\chi}\right)
be a representation of
K
. Define an action of
K
on
U\times E_{\chi}
from the right by

\left(u,X\right)k=\left(uk,\chi\left(k^{-1}\right)X\right),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,u\in U,\,\,X\in E_{\chi}.

Define an equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on
U\times E_{\chi}
by

\left(u_{1},X_{1}\right)\mathcal{R}\left(u_{2},X_{2}\right)\Leftrightarrow\exists\,k\in K\text{ such that }u_{2}=u_{1}k\text{ and }X_{2}=\chi\left(k^{-1}\right)X_{1}.

The equivalence class of an element
\left(u,X\right)
of
U\times E_{\chi}
will be denoted by
\left[u,X\right]
and the set of equivalence classes by
{\mathbb{E}_{\chi}}=U\times_{K}E_{\chi}
, i.e.,

{\mathbb{E}_{\chi}}=U\times_{K}E_{\chi}=\left\{\left[u,X\right]\,\,\arrowvert\,\,u\in U\text{ and }X\in E_{\chi}\right\}.

Then
\mathbb{E}_{\chi}
is a complex vector bundle over
U/K
of rank equal to
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}E_{\chi}
.
In case
\chi
is a character, i.e.,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}E_{\chi}=1
, or equivalently
E_{\chi}\cong\mathbb{C}
, then
\mathbb{E}_{\chi}
is a line bundle, which we will denote, following tradition, by
\mathbb{L}_{\chi}
. It is easy to see that the smooth sections
\Gamma\left(U/K,\,\mathbb{L}_{\chi}\right)
of
\mathbb{L}_{\chi}
can be identified with smooth functions
f
on
U
satisfying

f\left(uk\right)=\chi(k)^{-1}f\left(u\right),\mbox{ for all }k\in K\mbox{ and }u\in U.

Let

\displaystyle L^{2}\left(U/K,\,\mathbb{L}_{\chi}\right)=\left\{f\in L^{2}(U)\mid f(uk)=\chi_{l}(k^{-1})f(u),\text{ for all }k\in K\right\},

and let
L^{2}\left(U//K,\,\mathbb{L}_{\chi}\right)
be the subspace in
L^{2}\left(U/K,\,\mathbb{L}_{\chi}\right)
of functions satisfying

f(k_{1}uk_{2})=\chi_{l}(k_{1}k_{2})^{-1}f(u),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ for all }k_{1},k_{2}\in K.

Recall that we denoted by
\pi_{\lambda}:U\longrightarrow GL(E_{\lambda})
the irreducible unitary representation of
U
with highest weight
\lambda
, and for a non-trivial character
\chi_{l}
of
K
, we denoted by
E_{\lambda}^{l}
the subspace of
E_{\lambda}
defined in
\left(\ref{fixed vectors by K}\right)
. Let
P_{l,\lambda}
be the orthogonal projection from
E_{\lambda}
to
E_{\lambda}^{l}
defined by

\displaystyle P_{l,\lambda}(v)=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle K$}}$}}\nolimits\chi_{l}(k^{-1})\pi_{\lambda}(k)v\;d\mu_{K}(k),\;\;\;v\in E_{\lambda}.

The Fourier transform of a function
f
, denoted by
\mathcal{F}(f)
, is defined by

\F\left(f\right)(\lambda)=\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle U$}}$}}\nolimits f\left(u\right)\pi_{\lambda}\left(u\right)d\mu_{U}\left(u\right).

So for
f\in L^{2}\left(U/K,\,\mathbb{L}_{\chi}\right)
we have

(5)

\displaystyle\F\left(f\right)(\lambda)=\F\left(f\right)(\lambda)P_{l,\lambda}.

Hence for
\lambda\not\in\Lambda^{+}_{l}\left(U\right)
we have

\displaystyle\F\left(f\right)(\lambda)v=0,\text{ for all }v\in E_{\lambda}.

Denote by
\left(.,.\right)_{HS}
, the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product on
\End\left(E_{\lambda}\right)
and

H^{s}(SU(p+q))\subseteq\C^{\nu}\left(SU(p+q)\right).

Hence the Main Theorem.

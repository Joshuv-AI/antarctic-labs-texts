The Normality of Products Under Perfect Preimages
Lucas D. O’Brien

Abstract
. A proof of the following theorem is given, answering an open problem attributed to Kunen: suppose that T T is compact and that Y Y is the image of X X under a perfect map, X X is normal, and Y × T Y\times T is normal. Then X × T X\times T is normal.

1. Introduction

In
[
8
]
, Mary Ellen Rudin proved that given a compact space
T
, a normal space
X
, and a space
Y
that is the image of
X
under a closed map, that
X\times T
is normal only if
Y\times T
is normal. Rudin’s proof relied on a characterization of normality of products with a compact factor which appeared as Theorem 3 in
[
8
]
, and is included as Theorem
2.7
in this paper. This characterization loosely says that the normality of the product
X\times T
is equivalent to certain open covers of
X
having locally finite open refinements. Rudin called these open covers
X
-
separations
; we follow the convention of
[
5
]
and call them
\mathscr{B}
-
coverings
(see Definition
2.3
). In view of Theorem
2.7
, there is correlation between paracompactness and the normality of a product with a compact factor. Given a compact space
T
, Morita defines a space
X
to be
T
-
paracompact
provided
X\times T
is normal
[
3
]
.

We may intuitively expect the property of
T
-paracompactness to be preserved in a similar way to paracompactness under mappings. Indeed, Rudin’s theorem shows that
T
-paracompactness is preserved under images of closed maps. The question of whether
T
-paracompactness is preserved under preimages of perfect maps is attributed to Kunen and appeared as an open problem in
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
; this paper shows that this is indeed the case.

By the Tietze extension theorem, any continuous map from a closed subspace of a normal space
X
to
\mathbb{R}
can be extended to a continuous map of all of
X
into
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, the study of the normality of products has applications to the problem of the extendability of continuous functions from subsets of product spaces. For more details, see
[
4
]
.

2.  Characterization of Normality in Products with a Compact Factor

In the following,
T
denotes a compact Hausdorff space and
\mathscr{B}
denotes a base of
T
which is closed with respect to finite unions and intersections. A
normal space
refers to a topological space which is both
\text{T}_{4}
and
\text{T}_{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a collection of sets, and let
\mathcal{B}
be a collection of ordered pairs of sets. We will say a map
G:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
is
intersection preserving
provided
G(A,B)\cap G(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})=G(A\cap A^{\prime},B\cap B^{\prime})
for all
(A,B),(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Suppose
G:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
is intersection preserving. Then, for
(A,B),(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}
such that
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
and
B\subseteq B^{\prime}
, we have
G(A,B)\subseteq G(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
5
]
Fix a base
\mathscr{B}
of
T
, and define
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}=\{(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}|\ \overline{B}\cap\overline{D}=\emptyset\}
. An open cover
\mathscr{G}
of a space
X
is called a
\mathscr{B}
-
covering of

X
if there exists an intersection preserving surjection
G:\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\to\mathscr{G}
. We call
G

the associated map
to the
\mathscr{B}
-covering.

Let
X
be a space, and let
G:\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\to\mathscr{P}(X)
be an intersection preserving map. Then, the set
\mathscr{G}=\{G(B,D)\ |\ (B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\}
is a
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
if and only if
\mathscr{G}
is an open cover of
X
.

Definition 2.4
.

An open cover
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
of a space
X
is called a
pre
-
\mathscr{B}
-
covering of

X
if there exists an intersection preserving surjection
G:\mathscr{B}^{2}\to\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{G}=\{G(B,D)\ |\ (B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\}\subseteq\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
is a
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
. We call
\mathscr{G}
the
\mathscr{B}
-
covering associated with

\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
X
be a normal space, and let
K,L\subseteq X\times T
be closed, disjoint sets. For
B,D\in\mathscr{B}
, define

G(B,D)=\{x\in X\ |\ K_{x}\subseteq B\text{ and }L_{x}\subseteq D\},

where
K_{x}=\{t\in T\ |\ (x,t)\in K\}
and
L_{x}=\{t\in T\ |\ (x,t)\in L\}
. Then, the set
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}=\{G(B,D)\ |\ (B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}\}
is a pre-
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
.

Indeed, by definition,
G:\mathscr{B}^{2}\to\mathscr{G},(B,D)\mapsto G(B,D)
is surjective, and it is easy to check that
G
is intersection preserving. Thus, it remains to show that
\mathscr{G}=\{G(B,D)\ |\ (B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\}
covers
X
and
G(B,D)
is open for each
(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}
. Let
x\in X
. Since
K
and
L
are disjoint, so are
K_{x}
and
L_{x}
. Furthermore, notice that
K_{x}=\pi_{T}(K\cap(\{x\}\times T))
and
L_{x}=\pi_{T}(L\cap(\{x\}\times T))
, where
\pi_{T}:X\times T\to T
is the projection map. Since
K\cap(\{x\}\times T)
and
L\cap(\{x\}\times T)
are compact,
K_{x}
and
L_{x}
are closed. Thus, there exist
B,D\in\mathscr{B}
such that
K_{x}\subseteq B
,
L_{x}\subseteq D
and
\overline{B}\cap\overline{D}=\emptyset
since
\mathscr{B}
is closed with respect to finite unions. Then,
x\in G(B,D)
. Notice that for each
(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}
, we may write

G(B,D)=X\setminus\pi_{X}((K\setminus(X\times B))\cup(L\setminus(X\times D))),

where
\pi_{X}:X\times T\to X
is the projection map. Since
T
is compact,
\pi_{X}
is a closed map, and therefore
G(B,D)
is open.

The next lemma follows from the techniques used in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X
be a normal space. For every
\mathscr{B}
-covering
\mathscr{G}
of
X
, there exists a pre-
\mathscr{B}
-covering
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
of
X
such that the
\mathscr{B}
-covering associated with
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
is an open cover refinement of
\mathscr{G}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
, and let
G:\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\to\mathscr{G}
be the associated map. Define

K=(X\times T)\setminus\bigcup\{G(B,D)\times(T\setminus\overline{B})\ |\ (B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\}

and

L=(X\times T)\setminus\bigcup\{G(B,D)\times(T\setminus\overline{D})\ |\ (B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\}.

Evidently
K
and
L
are closed, we will show they are disjoint. Indeed, let
(x,t)\in X\times T
. Since
\mathscr{G}
covers
X
, there exists
(B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}
such that
x\in G(B,D)
. We must have
t\notin\overline{B}
or
t\notin\overline{D}
, and therefore either
(x,t)\in G(B,D)\times(T\setminus\overline{B})
or
(x,t)\in G(B,D)\times(T\setminus\overline{D})
, so by the definition of
K
and
L
, we must have either
(x,t)\in(X\times T)\setminus K
or
(x,t)\in(X\times T)\setminus L
. Therefore,
K
and
L
are disjoint.

For
B,D\in\mathscr{B}
, define

H(B,D)=\{x\in X\ |\ K_{x}\subseteq B\text{ and }L_{x}\subseteq D\},

where
K_{x}=\{t\in T\ |\ (x,t)\in K\}
and
L_{x}=\{t\in T|(x,t)\in L\}
. As in Example
2.5
,
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}=\{H(B,D)\ |\ (B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}\}
is a pre-
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
. Therefore, to show that the
\mathscr{B}
-covering associated with
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
refines
\mathscr{G}
, it remains to show that
H(B,D)\subseteq G(B,D)
for all
(B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}
.

Let
(B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}
, and let
x\in H(B,D)
. Then,
K_{x}\subseteq B
and
L_{x}\subseteq D
. By definition of
K
and
L
, we have

\{x\}\times(T\setminus B)\subseteq\bigcup\{G(B^{\prime},D^{\prime})\times(T\setminus\overline{B^{\prime}})\ |\ B^{\prime},D^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B},\overline{B^{\prime}}\cap\overline{D^{\prime}}=\emptyset\},

and

\{x\}\times(T\setminus D)\subseteq\bigcup\{G(B^{\prime\prime},D^{\prime\prime})\times(T\setminus\overline{D^{\prime\prime}})\ |\ B^{\prime\prime},D^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{B},\overline{B^{\prime\prime}}\cap\overline{D^{\prime\prime}}=\emptyset\}.

Since
\{x\}\times(T\setminus B)
is compact, it is covered by finitely many elements
\{G(B^{\prime}_{i},D^{\prime}_{i})\times(T\setminus\overline{B_{i}^{\prime}})|1\leq i\leq n\}
. Therefore, we have
T-B\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(T\setminus\overline{B_{i}^{\prime}})
and
x\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}G(B_{i},D_{i})
. Similarly, we may find a finite family
\{(B^{\prime\prime}_{1},D^{\prime\prime}_{1}),\dots,(B^{\prime\prime}_{m},D^{\prime\prime}_{m})\}
such that
T-D\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}(T\setminus\overline{D_{i}^{\prime\prime}})
and
x\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}G(B^{\prime\prime}_{i},D^{\prime\prime}_{i})
. Therefore, we have
B\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}B^{\prime}_{i}
,
D\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}D^{\prime\prime}_{i}
, and

x\in G(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}B^{\prime}_{i}\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}B^{\prime\prime}_{i},\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}D^{\prime}_{i}\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}D^{\prime\prime}_{i})\subseteq G(B,D).

Thus, we have that
H(B,D)\subseteq G(B,D)
for all
(B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}
. Therefore, the
\mathscr{B}
-covering associated with
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
refines
\mathscr{G}
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
3.1
relies on the following characterization of normality of products with a compact factor, which allows us to work with
\mathscr{B}
-coverings instead of disjoint closed sets. The proof of this particular statement of Rudin’s Lemma can be found in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.7

(Rudin’s Lemma)
.

[
5
,
8
,
1
]
The product space
X\times T
is normal if and only if
X
is normal and every
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
has a locally finite open refinement.

3. Proof of Theorem

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
T
is compact and that
Y
is the image of
X
under a perfect map
p
,
X
is normal, and
Y\times T
is normal. Then,
X\times T
is normal.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
T
is not a singleton.

By Rudin’s Lemma, it suffices to show that every
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
has a locally finite open refinement. For any
\mathscr{B}
-covering
\mathscr{G}
of
X
, there exists a pre-
\mathscr{B}
-covering whose associated
\mathscr{B}
-covering refines
\mathscr{G}
by Lemma
2.6
, so it is enough to show that for any pre-
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
, the associated
\mathscr{B}
-covering has a locally finite open refinement.

Let
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
be a pre-
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
X
, and let
G:\mathscr{B}^{2}\to\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
be the map associated with
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}
. We will show that
\mathscr{G}=\{G(B,D)|(B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\}
has a locally finite open refinement. For each
(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}
, define

H(B,D)=\{y\in Y\ |\ p^{-1}\{y\}\subseteq G(B,D)\}.

It is easy to check that
H
is intersection preserving. Define
{\mathscr{H}^{\prime}=\{H(B,D)|(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}\}}
; we claim that
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
is an open cover of
Y
. Indeed, for each
y\in Y
,
p^{-1}\{y\}
can be covered by finitely many elements
\{G(B_{1},D_{1}),\dots,G(B_{n},D_{n})\}
of
\mathscr{G}
. Then, since
\mathscr{B}
is closed under finite union, by Remark
2.2
,

p^{-1}\{y\}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}G(B_{i},D_{i})\subseteq G(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i},\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}D_{i})\in\mathscr{G}^{\prime},

so
y\in H(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i},\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}D_{i})
. Furthermore, since
G(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i},\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}D_{i})
is an open set containing
p^{-1}\{y\}
, from Theorem 1.4.13 in
[
2
]
we know there is some neighbourhood
U
of
y
such that
p^{-1}(U)\subseteq G(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i},\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}D_{i})
; therefore
U\subseteq H(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i},\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}D_{i})
and so
H(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i},\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}D_{i})
is open.

For each
B,D\in\mathscr{B}
, define

K_{B}=((Y\setminus H(B,T))\times T)\cup(Y\times\overline{B})

and

L_{D}=((Y\setminus H(T,D))\times T)\cup(Y\times\overline{D}).

Define

K=\bigcap_{B\in\mathscr{B}}K_{B},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ L=\bigcap_{D\in\mathscr{B}}L_{D}.

Evidently
K
and
L
are closed. We show they are disjoint; indeed, note that for
B,D\in\mathscr{B}
, we have

K_{B}\cap L_{D}=((Y\setminus(H(B,T)\cup H(T,D)))\times T)\cup((Y\setminus H(T,D))\times\overline{B})\cup((Y\setminus H(B,T))\times\overline{D})\cup(Y\times(\overline{B}\cap\overline{D})).

It suffices to show that each of the terms in the above union are individually empty when intersected over all pairs
(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}
. The second and third terms are both empty when this intersection is taken since
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
covers
Y
, and for any
H(B,D)\in\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
, we have
H(B,D)\subseteq H(B,T)
and
H(B,D)\subseteq H(T,D)
. Since
T
is normal and contains at least two distinct points, there are basis elements
B,D\in\mathscr{B}
such that
\overline{B}\cap\overline{D}=\emptyset
, so the fourth term is empty under this intersection. Thus, we have

K\cap L=\bigcap_{(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}}K_{B}\cap L_{D}=((Y\setminus\bigcup_{(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}}(H(B,T)\cup H(T,D)))\times T).

Since
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
covers
Y
, we have

\bigcup_{(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}}H(B,T)\cup H(T,D)\supseteq\bigcup_{(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}}H(B,T)\cap H(T,D)=\bigcup_{(B,D)\in\mathscr{B}^{2}}H(B,D)=Y,

and therefore
K\cap L=\emptyset
.

We claim that
\mathscr{H}=\{H(B,D)\ |\ (B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}\}\subseteq\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
is a
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
Y
. It remains to show that
\mathscr{H}
covers
Y
. Let
y\in Y
. As shown in Example
2.5

K_{y}=\{t\in T\ |\ (y,t)\in K\}
and
L_{y}=\{t\in T\ |\ (y,t)\in L\}
are closed disjoint subsets of
T
, so there exist
B,D\in\mathscr{B}
with
\overline{B}\cap\overline{D}=\emptyset
such that
K_{y}\subseteq B
and
L_{y}\subseteq D
by the fact that
\mathscr{B}
is closed under finite unions. We show that
y\in H(B,T)
, an analogous argument shows that
y\in H(T,D)
and so
y\in H(B,T)\cap H(T,D)=H(B,D)
.

Define
\mathcal{J}=\{B^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}\ |\ y\in H(B^{\prime},T)\}
, we claim that there is some
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}
such that
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
, and thus
y\in H(B^{\prime},T)\subseteq H(B,T)
. By definition of
K
, we must have
\bigcap_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}}\overline{B^{\prime}}\subseteq K_{y}\subseteq B
. Therefore,
\{T\setminus\overline{B^{\prime}}\ |\ B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}\}
is an open cover of
T\setminus B
, so there exists a finite subcover
\{T\setminus\overline{B^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,T\setminus\overline{B^{\prime}_{n}}\}
. In particular, we have
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\overline{B^{\prime}_{i}}\subseteq B
. But
y\in H(B^{\prime}_{i},T)
for each
1\leq i\leq n
, and therefore

y\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}H(B^{\prime}_{i},T)\subseteq H(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}B^{\prime}_{i},T)

so
B^{\prime\prime}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}B^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{J}
. But since
B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B
, we have

y\in H(B^{\prime\prime},T)\subseteq H(B,T),

and so
y\in H(B,T)
. Therefore, for each
y\in Y
, there exist
(B,D)\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}
such that
y\in H(B,D)
.

Since
\mathscr{H}
is a
\mathscr{B}
-covering of
Y
, by Rudin’s Lemma, it has a locally finite open refinement
\mathscr{V}
. For each
V\in\mathscr{V}
, there exists
(B_{V},D_{V})\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{B}}
such that
V\subseteq H(B_{V},D_{V})
, so by definition of
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
, we have that for each
V\in\mathscr{V}
,

p^{-1}(V)\subseteq p^{-1}(H(B_{V},D_{V}))\subseteq G(B_{V},D_{V}).

Therefore, the set
\{p^{-1}(V)\ |\ V\in\mathscr{V}\}
is a locally finite open refinement of
\mathscr{G}
. Thus, by Rudin’s Lemma,
X\times T
is normal.

∎

This allows us to answer another problem in
[
5
]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose
T
is compact, and
X\times T
is normal. Then,
X\times T^{n}
is normal for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

This follows by induction on
n
, noticing that the projection
p:X\times T\to X
is perfect because
T
is compact.
∎

Corollary
3.2
does not hold when
n
is replaced with
\omega
. To see why, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
3
]
Let
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
be any two compact Hausdorff spaces. If
Q^{\prime}
is either a closed subset or a continuous image of
Q
, then if
X\times Q
is normal, so is
X\times Q^{\prime}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists a space
X
and a compact set
T
such that
X\times T
is normal but
X\times T^{\omega}
is not normal.

Proof.

Let
T=2
with the discrete topology, and let
X
be a Dowker space, that is, a normal space for which
X\times[0,1]
is not normal
[
6
]
. Since
T
is discrete and
X
is normal, we know that
X\times T
is normal. Suppose that
X\times 2^{\omega}
is normal. Since
2^{\omega}
is the Cantor set,
[0,1]
is the continuous image of
2^{\omega}
(see
[
2
]
), so by Lemma
3.3
,
X\times[0,1]
is normal, contradicting the fact that
X
is Dowker.
∎

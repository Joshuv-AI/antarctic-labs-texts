Lagrangian Multiform for Cyclotomic Gaudin Models
Vincent CAUDRELIER a, Anup Anand SINGH a and Benoît VICEDO b

Abstract
We construct a Lagrangian multiform for the class of cyclotomic (rational) Gaudin models by formulating its hierarchy within the Lie dialgebra framework of Semenov-Tian-Shansky and by using the framework of Lagrangian multiforms on coadjoint orbits. This provides the first example of a Lagrangian multiform for an integrable hierarchy whose classical r r -matrix is non-skew-symmetric and spectral parameter-dependent. As an important by-product of the construction, we obtain a Lagrangian multiform for the periodic Toda chain by choosing an appropriate realisation of the cyclotomic Gaudin Lax matrix. This fills a gap in the landscape of Toda models as only the open and infinite chains had been previously cast into the Lagrangian multiform framework. A slightly different choice of realisation produces the so-called discrete self-trapping (DST) model. We demonstrate the versatility of the framework by coupling the periodic Toda chain with the DST model and by obtaining a Lagrangian multiform for the corresponding integrable hierarchy.

Introduction

A characteristic property of an integrable system is that its equations of motion can be seen as members of a hierarchy of compatible equations. Traditional Lagrangians fail to capture this notion of commuting (Hamiltonian) flows, or its discrete analog known as multidimensional consistency
[
9
,
25
]
. This obstacle to describing integrable hierarchies variationally was overcome for the first time in
[
23
]
by introducing a new object – a Lagrangian multiform – together with a generalised variational principle applied to an appropriate generalisation of a classical action, and noticing a fundamental property of the multiform: the so-called closure relation. Since its introduction, this idea has been developed in several directions. Its connections with more traditional features of integrability (Lax pairs and Hamiltonian structures, for instance) have been established in the various realms of integrable systems: discrete and continuous finite-dimensional systems
[
11
,
27
,
36
,
43
]
, continuous infinite-dimensional systems – field theories in
1+1
dimensions
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
28
,
32
,
33
,
37
]
and in
2+1
dimensions
[
26
,
34
]
– and semi-discrete systems
[
35
]
. The relations between discrete and continuous multiforms were explored in
[
40
]
. The concept has even been extended to non-commuting flows in
[
12
]
.

In general, hierarchies of models in
d
spacetime dimensions are described by a Lagrangian multiform which is a
d
-form integrated over a hypersurface of dimension
d
in a so-called multi-time space of dimension greater than
d
to yield an action functional depending not only on the field configurations but also on the hypersurface itself. This last point is the crucial new ingredient of the generalised variational principle used in Lagrangian multiform theory. One postulates a principle of least action which must be valid for
any
hypersurface embedded in the multi-time space. This captures the commutativity of the flows variationally and was adopted as a definition of pluri-Lagrangian systems, see
[
27
,
28
]
and references therein. In Lagrangian multiform theory, there is an additional postulate which leads to the
closure relation
. The latter has been shown
[
11
,
36
]
to be equivalent to the Poisson involutivity of Hamiltonians, the Liouville criterion for integrability.

The generalised variational principle produces equations that come in two flavours: 1) Euler–Lagrange equations associated with each of the coefficients of the Lagrangian multiform which form a collection of Lagrangian densities; 2) Corner or structure equations on the Lagrangian coefficients themselves which select possible models and ensure the compatibility of the various equations of motion imposed on a common set of fields. This paper deals with a finite-dimensional system, so let us illustrate the ingredients for this case. The basic objects are a Lagrangian 1-form

\mathscr{L}[q]=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathscr{L}_{k}[q]\,\mathrm{d}t_{k}

and the related generalised action

S[q,\Gamma]=\int_{\Gamma}\mathscr{L}[q],

where
\Gamma
is a curve in the multi-time
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with (time) coordinates
t_{1},\dots,t_{N}
, and
q
denotes generic configuration coordinates. For instance,
q
could be a position vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d
, or as will be the case for us, an element of a (matrix) Lie group. The notations
\mathscr{L}[q]
and
\mathscr{L}_{k}[q]
mean that these quantities depend on
q
and a finite number of derivatives of
q
with respect to the times
t_{1},\dots,t_{N}
. In this paper, we restrict ourselves only to the case of first derivatives and simply write
\mathscr{L}_{k}
for the Lagrangian coefficients.
The application of the generalised variational principle leads to the following multi-time Euler–Lagrange equations
[
36
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathscr{L}_{k}}{\partial q}-\partial_{t_{k}}\frac{\partial\mathscr{L}_{k}}{\partial q_{t_{k}}}=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathscr{L}_{k}}{\partial q_{t_{\ell}}}=0,\qquad\ell\neq k,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathscr{L}_{k}}{\partial q_{t_{k}}}=\frac{\partial\mathscr{L}_{\ell}}{\partial q_{t_{\ell}}},\qquad k,\ell=1,\dots,N.

(1.3)

Note that (
1.1
) is simply the standard Euler–Lagrange equation for each
\mathscr{L}_{k}
. Equations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are the corner equations. It turns out that these corner equations will, in fact, be identically satisfied by our Lagrangian multiforms, but in general they can represent non-trivial equations for
q
.
The closure relation then stipulates that

\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}[q]=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\partial_{t_{k}}\mathscr{L}_{j}-\partial_{t_{j}}\mathscr{L}_{k}=0

on solutions of (
1.1
)–(
1.3
).

Gaudin models are a general class of integrable systems associated with Lie algebras with a nondegenerate invariant bilinear form. They were first introduced for the Lie algebra

At both the classical and quantum levels, the integrable structure of this model, associated with a Lie algebra

, is underpinned by the rational skew-symmetric solution

r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}^{0}(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{C_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}}{\mu-\lambda}

(1.4)

of the classical Yang--Baxter equation

The rational classical

and depends on spectral parameters

The cyclotomic Gaudin model of interest in this paper arises as a specialisation of a general procedure which can be traced back to reduction group ideas

[

to the rational classical

(1.5)

U_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda)\equiv U(\lambda)\otimes\mathbb{1}

The idea is to use a Lie algebra automorphism appropriately extended to a loop algebra automorphism. For our purpose, we use an automorphism
\sigma
of order
T

\mathfrak{g}
and define

\phi\colon\ U(\lambda)\mapsto\omega\sigma^{-1}(U(\omega\lambda)),

where
\omega
is a
T

is an automorphism of the Poisson algebra (

L(\lambda)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\omega^{-k}\sigma^{k}U\bigl(\omega^{-k}\lambda\bigr),\qquad\sigma(L(\lambda))=\omega L(\omega\lambda).

(1.6)

The Poisson algebra for
L(\lambda)
closes into

\bigl\{L_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda),L_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr\}=\bigl[r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu),L_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda)\bigr]-\bigl[r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{21}}}}(\mu,\lambda),L_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr],

(1.7)

where

r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}^{k}C_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}}{\mu-\omega^{-k}\lambda}

(1.8)

is a non-skew-symmetric solution of CYBE. These facts are special cases of

. It is well known

8

that the Poisson algebra (

{\rm Tr}L(\lambda)^{p}
Poisson commute and the equations of motion generated by them with respect to (

H(\mu)=\frac{1}{p+1}\Tr L(\mu)^{p+1},\qquad p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

satisfies

\{L(\lambda),H(\mu)\}=[M(\lambda,\mu),L(\lambda)],

where by definition
\{L(\lambda),H(\mu)\}=\{L^{a}(\lambda),H(\mu)\}I_{a}

M_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda,\mu)=\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)L_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)^{p}.

This yields an infinite number of Hamiltonians by specifying

If one applies this construction to a Lax matrix of rational Gaudin model type, with poles in the finite set
D=\{0,\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{N},\infty\}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{1}

U(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{0}-1}\frac{U_{0}^{(n)}}{\lambda^{n+1}}+\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{r}-1}\frac{U_{r}^{(n)}}{(\lambda-\zeta_{r})^{n+1}}+\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\infty}}U_{\infty}^{(n)}\lambda^{n},\qquad U_{s}^{(n)}\in\mathfrak{g},

(1.9)

then one obtains the so-called cyclotomic Gaudin model
[

,
41
,
42
]
and its associated integrable hierarchy. Note that in (
1.9
), we have anticipated that the pole at
0
and at infinity behave differently in (
1.6
) compared to the poles at
\lambda=\zeta_{r}\neq 0,\infty
. Although we will present the general algebraic setup, for simplicity, in our examples we will only consider the case where
U(\lambda)
has simple poles at all
\zeta_{r}
,

, and double poles at the origin and at
infinity. This will be sufficient for our application to the periodic Toda chain and the discrete self-trapping (DST) model. The corresponding Lax matrix of the cyclotomic Gaudin model then takes the form

L(\lambda)=\frac{X_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{X_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}X_{r}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}}+X_{\infty}

(1.10)

with
\sigma X_{0}^{(j)}=\omega^{-j}X_{0}^{(j)}
and
\sigma X_{\infty}=\omega X_{\infty}
.

Our goal is to give a Lagrangian multiform description of the cyclotomic Gaudin hierarchy. In a nutshell, what is required is to interpret
L(\lambda)
as an element of an appropriate coadjoint orbit so that we can write our Lagrangian multiform following the construction introduced in
[
11
]
. The latter is based on using the Lie dialgebra formalism
[
30
]
which is reviewed in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we show how to construct the required algebraic ingredients for the cyclotomic Gaudin model. This is applied in Section
3.4
, where we obtain a Lagrangian multiform for the cyclotomic Gaudin hierarchy and discuss its properties.

Section
4

4.1
) and the integrable case of the DST model (Section
4.2

3
. The construction of the periodic Toda multiform, in particular, complements the results of
[
11
]
where a Lagrangian multiform for the open Toda chain was derived, while in
[

5
.

2
Lagrangian multiforms on coadjoint orbits

In this section, we briefly review the results of
[
11
]
where a Lagrangian multiform was obtained which produces a hierarchy of equations of motion in Lax form as its multi-time Euler–Lagrange equations. We will, in fact, be interested in applying this framework in the infinite-dimensional setting for which we refer more precisely to
[
11

The framework of Lie dialgebras for constructing hierarchies of compatible Lax equations involves a number of ingredients. For the purpose of this paper, we will actually only require a particular instance of this general framework corresponding to the so-called Adler–Kostant–Symes scheme

38

1.

A Lie algebra
\mathbb{g}
, with Lie group
\mathbb{G}
, which we assume is equipped with a nondegenerate invariant bilinear pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\ V\times\mathbb{g}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

(2.1)

with some representation
V
of
\mathbb{G}
, allowing one to use
V
as a model for the dual space
\mathbb{g}^{\ast}
of
\mathbb{g}
. We will denote the representation of
\mathbb{G}
by
\Ad^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{G}\times V\to V
,
(\varphi,\xi)\mapsto\Ad^{\ast}_{\varphi}\xi
and the corresponding Lie algebra representation by
\ad^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{g}\times V\to V
,
(X,\xi)\mapsto\ad^{\ast}_{X}\xi
, and refer to these as the coadjoint representations.

Note that if
\mathbb{g}
were finite-dimensional then a good choice of representation space

would be the algebraic dual
\mathbb{g}^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{g}
, in which case (
2.1
) is given by the canonical pairing. In the infinite-dimensional setting we are considering, the algebraic dual is too big. When

is equipped with a nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form

2.

A linear map

satisfying the

(2.2)

This is the crucial ingredient providing us with the Lie dialgebra
(\mathbb{g},\mathbb{g}_{R})
where
\mathbb{g}_{R}
is the vector space
\mathbb{g}

3
Cyclotomic Gaudin model

The purpose of this section is to formulate a Lagrangian multiform for the cyclotomic Gaudin model using the machinery developed in
[
11
]
.
As outlined at the end of Section
2
, the first immediate task is to introduce the relevant infinite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathbb{g}
and a suitable model
V
for its dual space
\mathbb{g}^{\ast}
by constructing a nondegenerate invariant bilinear pairing as in (

). This will be achieved in Section
3.1
, which culminates in a description of the relevant coadjoint orbits
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{g}^{\ast}
in Lemma
3.5
. In Section
3.2
, we specialise to the case of simple poles at
\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}
,
k\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
,
r\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, and double poles at the origin and at infinity, and then describe the Lax matrix (
1.10
) of the cyclotomic Gaudin model as an element of this coadjoint orbit. In Section
3.3
, we then describe the Lax equations as flows on this coadjoint orbit, as in (

). Finally, in Section
3.4
, we put everything together to obtain the Lagrangian multiform for the cyclotomic Gaudin model.

3.1
Lie dialgebra framework

Let us fix
T\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and pick a primitive
T
-th root of unity
\omega\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. We can then define

\Gamma\coloneqq\bigl\{1,\omega,\omega^{2},\dots,\omega^{T-1}\bigr\},

a copy of the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{T}
of order
T
that acts on
\mathbb{C}
by multiplication. Further, we introduce the finite set of points
D=\{0,\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{N},\infty\}\subset\mathbb{C}P^{1}
including the point at infinity such that the
\Gamma
-orbits of the points
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{N}
are pairwise disjoint, that is,

\Gamma\zeta_{r}\cap\Gamma\zeta_{s}=\varnothing\qquad\text{for all}\quad 1\leq r\neq s\leq N.

Note that unlike the non-zero finite points
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{N}
, the origin and the point at infinity are fixed under the action of
\Gamma
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with an automorphism
\sigma
of order
T
. For simplicity, we will only consider matrix Lie algebras in this work, with the Lie bracket being the commutator, and a nondegenerate invariant bilinear pairing given by the trace. We also assume that the automorphism
\sigma
preserves the trace, that is,
\Tr(\sigma(x)\sigma(y))=\Tr(xy)
for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. The eigenspaces of
\sigma
,

\mathfrak{g}^{(k)}=\bigl\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\sigma(X)=\omega^{k}X\bigr\},\qquad k\in\{0,\dots,T-1\},

(3.1)

form a
\mathbb{Z}_{T}
-gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
, namely

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}\dotplus\dots\dotplus\mathfrak{g}^{(T-1)}\qquad\text{with}\quad\bigl[\mathfrak{g}^{(k_{1})},\mathfrak{g}^{(k_{2})}\bigr]=\mathfrak{g}^{(k_{1}+k_{2}\,\text{mod}\,T)}.

(3.2)

From now on, we will simply write
\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}
to mean
\mathfrak{g}^{(n\,\text{mod}\,T)}
. Further, let us define the local parameters

\lambda_{0}=\lambda,\qquad\lambda_{r}=\lambda-\zeta_{r}\quad\text{for}\ r\in\{1,\dots,N\},\qquad\lambda_{\infty}=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}.

(3.3)

It will be convenient to also introduce an additional set of local parameters
\lambda_{r,k}=\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}
for all
k\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
and
r\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. We also introduce an index set
S=\{0,1,\dots,N,\infty\}
where
0
and
\infty
denote labels for indices rather than points in
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}
the algebra of rational functions in the formal variable
\lambda
with values in
\mathfrak{g}
that are regular outside
D^{\prime}=\bigl\{\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}\mid k\in\{0,\dots,T-1\},\,r\in\{1,\dots,N\}\bigr\}\cup\{0,\infty\}
. Two subspaces of this algebra will be of relevance here: the subspace
\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
of
equivariant functions
and the subspace
\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
of
equivariant one-forms
that we define as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}\mid\sigma(f(\lambda))=f(\omega\lambda)\},\qquad\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\coloneqq\{g\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}\mid\sigma(g(\lambda))=\omega g(\omega\lambda)\}.

In general, equivariant functions and equivariant one-forms take the forms

\displaystyle f=\sum_{n=0}^{M_{0}-1}\frac{X_{0}^{(n)}}{\lambda^{n+1}}+\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\sum_{n=0}^{M_{r}-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}X_{r}^{(n)}}{\bigl(\omega^{-k}\lambda-\zeta_{r}\bigr)^{n+1}}+\sum_{n=0}^{M_{\infty}}\frac{X_{\infty}^{(n)}}{\lambda_{\infty}^{n}},

\displaystyle g=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{0}-1}\frac{Y_{0}^{(n)}}{\lambda^{n+1}}+\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{r}-1}\frac{\omega^{-k}\sigma^{k}Y_{r}^{(n)}}{\bigl(\omega^{-k}\lambda-\zeta_{r}\bigr)^{n+1}}+\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\infty}}\frac{Y_{\infty}^{(n)}}{\lambda_{\infty}^{n}},

respectively, with

\displaystyle X_{0}^{(n)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(-n-1)},\qquad

\displaystyle X_{r}^{(n)}\in\mathfrak{g}\quad\text{for}\ r\in\{1,\dots,N\},\qquad

\displaystyle X_{\infty}^{(n)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(n)},

\displaystyle Y_{0}^{(n)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(-n)},\qquad

\displaystyle Y_{r}^{(n)}\in\mathfrak{g}\quad\text{for}\ r\in\{1,\dots,N\},\qquad

\displaystyle Y_{\infty}^{(n)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(n+1)}.

(3.4)

One can check that the subspace
\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
is, in fact, a Lie subalgebra of
\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}
. The subspace
\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
, on the other hand, contains the Lax matrix (
1.10
) of the model. To construct the cyclotomic Gaudin multiform à la
[
11
]
, we will realise this Lax matrix as an element of a certain coadjoint orbit. To do so, let us define the loop algebra of formal Laurent series in the local parameter
\lambda_{r}
with coefficients in
\mathfrak{g}
, for each
r\in S
,

\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}((\lambda_{r})),

(3.5)

with Lie bracket

\bigl[X\lambda_{r}^{i},Y\lambda_{r}^{j}\bigr]=[X,Y]\lambda_{r}^{i+j},\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{g},

(3.6)

and then consider the direct sum

\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}=\bigoplus_{r\in S}\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}.

The Lie bracket of two elements
X,Y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}
is defined component-wise

[X,Y]_{r}(\lambda_{r})=[X_{r}(\lambda_{r}),Y_{r}(\lambda_{r})].

Of interest to us here are two particular subspaces of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}
,

\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{\Gamma,0}\oplus\bigoplus_{r=1}^{N}\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\infty}^{\Gamma,0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{\Gamma,1}\oplus\bigoplus_{r=1}^{N}\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\infty}^{\Gamma,1},

where the twisted spaces attached to the origin and infinity are defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}^{\Gamma,k}=\bigl\{X_{0}(\lambda_{0})\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\mid\sigma(X_{0}(\lambda_{0}))=\omega^{k}X_{0}(\omega\lambda_{0})\bigr\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\infty}^{\Gamma,k}=\bigl\{X_{\infty}(\lambda_{\infty})\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\infty}\mid\sigma(X_{\infty}(\lambda_{\infty}))=\omega^{k}X_{\infty}\bigl(\omega^{-1}\lambda_{\infty}\bigr)\bigr\}

(3.7)

for
k=0,1
. Elements of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
are tuples of the form

X=\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{M_{0}-1}\frac{X_{0}^{(n)}}{\lambda^{n+1}},\sum_{n=-\infty}^{M_{1}-1}\frac{X_{1}^{(n)}}{(\lambda-\zeta_{1})^{n+1}},\dots,\sum_{n=-\infty}^{M_{N}-1}\frac{X_{N}^{(n)}}{(\lambda-\zeta_{N})^{n+1}},\sum_{n=-\infty}^{M_{\infty}}\frac{X_{\infty}^{(n)}}{\lambda_{\infty}^{n}}\right),

(3.8)

and

Y=\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N_{0}-1}\frac{Y_{0}^{(n)}}{\lambda^{n+1}},\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N_{1}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{(n)}}{(\lambda-\zeta_{1})^{n+1}},\dots,\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N_{N}-1}\frac{Y_{N}^{(n)}}{(\lambda-\zeta_{N})^{n+1}},\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N_{\infty}}\frac{Y_{\infty}^{(n)}}{\lambda_{\infty}^{n}}\right),

(3.9)

respectively, where the coefficients
X_{r}^{(n)}
and
Y_{r}^{(n)}
,
r\in S
, satisfy the conditions in (
3.4
). Note that each coefficient in (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) is a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued Laurent series, i.e., an element of (
3.5
) for
{r\in S}
. This is clear from changing variables in each sum from
n
to
-n
. The reason for the unusual choice of range for the indices
n
in each of the above sums is to make the expressions for the maps in (
3.12
) defined below slightly more transparent.

The subspace
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}
which defines the desired infinite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathbb{g}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
in the notation of Section
2
. Continuing the identification of the different ingredients from the Lie dialgebra framework listed in Section
2
, notice that
V=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
is clearly a representation of the Lie algebra
\mathbb{g}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
since we have
\bigl[\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}^{\Gamma,0},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}^{\Gamma,1}\bigr]\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}^{\Gamma,1}
for
r\in\{0,\infty\}
. In the notation of Section
2
, the representation
\ad^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{g}\times V\to V
is given explicitly by
(X,Y)\mapsto[X,Y]
. The next proposition identifies
V=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
as a suitable model for the dual space
\mathbb{g}^{\ast}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The bilinear pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}\times\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\displaystyle\langle Y,X\rangle=T\sum_{r=1}^{N}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr(Y_{r}(\lambda_{r})X_{r}(\lambda_{r})){\rm d}\lambda+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr(Y_{r}(\lambda_{r})X_{r}(\lambda_{r})){\rm d}\lambda

(3.10)

for any
Y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
and
X\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
, is nondegenerate and invariant under the adjoint action of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
.

Proof.

Recall that the trace
\Tr\colon\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
,
(x,y)\mapsto\Tr(xy)
is a nondegenerate invariant bilinear pairing on
\mathfrak{g}
and invariant under the action of
\sigma
. Given any
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}
and
y\in\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}
, we have
\Tr(xy)=\Tr(\sigma(x)\sigma(y))=\omega^{m+n}\Tr(xy)
. Therefore,
\Tr(xy)=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}
and
y\in\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}
if
m+n\neq 0\mod T
. Now, since
\Tr\colon\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
is nondegenerate on
\mathfrak{g}
, given
y\in\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}
, there is
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\Tr(xy)\neq 0
. From (
3.2
), we have
x=x^{(0)}+\dots+x^{(T-1)}
, and from the previous result
\Tr(xy)=\Tr\bigl(x^{(m)}y\bigr)
with
m+n=0\mod T
. Hence,
\Tr\bigl(x^{(m)}y\bigr)\neq 0
and we get a nondegenerate pairing between the subspaces
\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}
with
m+n=0\mod T
.

Now given any non-zero element
Y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
, it has a non-zero component
Y_{r}^{(m)}\lambda_{r}^{-m-1}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\in S
. If
r=0
then
Y_{0}^{(m)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(-m)}
by (
3.4
), and we can find an
X_{0}^{(-m-1)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}
which pairs non-trivially with it under the trace. So,
X_{0}^{(-m-1)}\lambda^{m}
pairs non-trivially with
Y
. Likewise, if
r=\infty
then
Y_{\infty}^{(m)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(m+1)}
by (
3.4
) and we can find an
X_{\infty}^{(-m-1)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(-m-1)}
which pairs non-trivially with it under the trace so that
X_{\infty}^{(-m-1)}\lambda_{\infty}^{m+1}
pairs non-trivially with
Y
. And if
r\in\{1,\dots,N\}
then pick any
X_{r}^{(-m-1)}\in\mathfrak{g}
which pairs non-trivially with
Y_{r}^{(m)}\in\mathfrak{g}
under the trace so that
X_{r}^{(-m-1)}\lambda_{r}^{m}
pairs non-trivially with
Y
. Therefore, the bilinear pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is nondegenerate in the first argument. A similar argument establishes the nondegeneracy in the second argument.

Finally, the invariance of the bilinear pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
under the adjoint action of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
follows from the definition (
3.6
) of the Lie bracket in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}
and the invariance of the trace under the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

Next, we turn to the identification of the subalgebras

in the notation of Section

\displaystyle\iota_{\lambda}\colon\ \Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)},\qquad g\longmapsto(\iota_{\lambda_{0}}g,\iota_{\lambda_{1}}g,\dots,\iota_{\lambda_{N}}g,\iota_{\lambda_{\infty}}g),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{n=0}^{M_{0}-1}\frac{X_{0}^{(n)}}{\lambda^{n+1}}+\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\sum_{n=0}^{M_{r}-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}X_{r}^{(n)}}{(\omega^{-k}\lambda-\zeta_{r})^{n+1}}+\sum_{n=0}^{M_{\infty}}\frac{X_{\infty}^{(n)}}{\lambda_{\infty}^{n}},

Proposition 3.2
.

The spaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}

For all

(3.18)

It follows that both
\iota_{\mu}\iota_{\lambda}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}
and
\iota_{\lambda}\iota_{\mu}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}
are elements of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}\,\widetilde{\otimes}\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
where the loop parameter
\lambda
is in the first tensor factor and
\mu
in the second. Now, pick an arbitrary
X\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
and let
X=W+Z
be its decomposition relative to (
3.13
) where the two components can be written explicitly as

W=\Biggl(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}W_{0}^{(n)}\lambda^{n},\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}W_{1}^{(n)}(\lambda-\zeta_{1})^{n},\dots,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}W_{N}^{(n)}(\lambda-\zeta_{N})^{n},\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{\infty}^{(n)}\lambda_{\infty}^{n}\Biggr)\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(0)},

and

Z=\iota_{\lambda}\Biggl(\sum_{n=0}^{N_{0}-1}\frac{Z_{0}^{(n)}}{\lambda^{n+1}}+\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{r}-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}Z_{r}^{(n)}}{\bigl(\omega^{-k}\lambda-\zeta_{r}\bigr)^{n+1}}+\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\infty}}\frac{Z_{\infty}^{(n)}}{\lambda_{\infty}^{n}}\Biggr)\in\iota_{\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}.

Using the expansion (
3.17
), we get

\displaystyle T\sum_{r=1}^{N}\Res_{\mu_{r}=0}\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigl(\iota_{\mu_{r}}\iota_{\lambda_{s}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)W_{r_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu_{r})\bigr){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\mu_{r}=0}\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigl(\iota_{\mu_{r}}\iota_{\lambda_{s}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)W_{r_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu_{r})\bigr){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}W_{s}^{(m)}(\lambda-\zeta_{s})^{m},\qquad\text{when}\quad s\in\{0,1,\dots,N\},

and

\displaystyle T\sum_{r=1}^{N}\Res_{\mu_{r}=0}\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigl(\iota_{\mu_{r}}\iota_{\lambda_{s}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)W_{r_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu_{r})\bigr){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\mu_{r}=0}\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigl(\iota_{\mu_{r}}\iota_{\lambda_{s}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)W_{r_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu_{r})\bigr){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}W_{\infty}^{(m)}\lambda_{\infty}^{m},\qquad\text{when}\quad s=\infty.

So, we find that

{\bigl\langle\iota_{\mu}\iota_{\lambda}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}},W_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigr\rangle}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}=W.

(3.19)

To evaluate
{\bigl\langle\iota_{\mu}\iota_{\lambda}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}},Z_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigr\rangle}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}
, we note that for an arbitrary equivariant rational function
{f\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}}
and equivariant rational one-form
g\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
, we have the following relation:

\displaystyle\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr(g(\lambda)f(\lambda)){\rm d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\sigma^{k}(g(\lambda))\sigma^{k}(f(\lambda))\bigr){\rm d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\omega^{k}g(\omega^{k}\lambda)f(\omega^{k}\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\Res_{\lambda_{r,k}=0}\Tr(g(\lambda)f(\lambda)){\rm d}\lambda

(3.20)

for all
k\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
. This allows us to rewrite each entry of the tuple
{\bigl\langle\iota_{\mu}\iota_{\lambda}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}},Z_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigr\rangle}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}
as

\displaystyle T\sum_{r=1}^{N}\Res_{\mu_{r}=0}\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigl(\iota_{\mu_{r}}\iota_{\lambda_{s}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)Z_{r_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu_{r})\bigr){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\mu_{r}=0}\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigl(\iota_{\mu_{r}}\iota_{\lambda_{s}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)Z_{r_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu_{r})\bigr){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\Res_{\mu_{r,k}=0}\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigl(\iota_{\mu_{r}}\iota_{\lambda_{s}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)Z_{r_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu_{r})\bigr){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\mu_{r}=0}\Tr_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigl(\iota_{\mu_{r}}\iota_{\lambda_{s}}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)Z_{r_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu_{r})\bigr){\rm d}\mu,\qquad s\in S,

which is a sum over all the residues of a meromorphic one-form on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
. Hence, we deduce

{\bigl\langle\iota_{\mu}\iota_{\lambda}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}},Z_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigr\rangle}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}=0.

(3.21)

From (
3.19
) and (
3.21
), we then have

{\bigl\langle\iota_{\mu}\iota_{\lambda}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}},X_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigr\rangle}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}=W=P_{+}(X)=R_{+}(X).

Since
X\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
was arbitrary, this establishes the first relation in (
3.16
). Similarly, using the expansion (
3.18
), we find
{\bigl\langle\iota_{\lambda}\iota_{\mu}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}},W_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigr\rangle}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}=0
and
{\bigl\langle\iota_{\lambda}\iota_{\mu}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}},Z_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigr\rangle}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}=-Z
from which we conclude that

{\bigl\langle\iota_{\lambda}\iota_{\mu}r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}},X_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}\bigr\rangle}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}=-Z=-P_{-}(X)=R_{-}(X).

Again, since
X\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
was arbitrary, this establishes the second relation in (
3.16
).
∎

It follows from Proposition
3.3
that the kernel of the linear map
R=P_{+}-P_{-}
and that of the identity map
\text{Id}=P_{+}+P_{-}
(with respect to the bilinear pairing of Proposition
3.1
) are

(\iota_{\mu}\iota_{\lambda}+\iota_{\lambda}\iota_{\mu})r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\iota_{\mu}\iota_{\lambda}-\iota_{\lambda}\iota_{\mu}),r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu),

respectively.

Recall that in Proposition
3.2
we identified the Lax matrix (
1.9
) of the cyclotomic Gaudin model, or rather its image under the embedding
\iota_{\lambda}
in (
3.11
), as living in the subspace
{V_{-}=\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}}
of our model
V=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
for the dual space
\mathbb{g}^{\ast}
.
The next proposition establishes that this subspace
V_{-}\subset V
is the orthogonal complement of
\iota_{\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{g}_{-}\subset\mathbb{g}
, with respect to the nondegenerate bilinear pairing of Proposition

. This completes the proof of the claim that the decomposition

obtained in Proposition

Proposition 3.4
.

An element
Y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
lies in
\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
if and only if
\langle Y,X\rangle=0
for all
X\in\iota_{\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
.
Moreover,
Y\!\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
lies in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(1)}
if and only if
\langle Y,X\rangle=0
for all
X\!\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(0)}
.

Proof.

This is a particular case of the
\Gamma
-equivariant strong residue theorem
[
41
, Appendix A]
. We recall the proof here in the present setting for completeness.
We start by proving that if
Y\in\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
, then
\langle Y,X\rangle=0
for all
X\in\iota_{\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
. Let us pick elements
f\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
and
g\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
, and let
X=\iota_{\lambda}f
and
Y=\iota_{\lambda}g
. Since
g(\lambda)f(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda
is a meromorphic one-form on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
, by the residue theorem we have

\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\Res_{\lambda_{r,k}=0}\Tr\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{r,k}}g(\lambda)\iota_{\lambda_{r,k}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{r}}g(\lambda)\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda=0.

Using the relation (
3.20
) valid for any
f\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
and
g\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
, we may rewrite the first term on the left-hand side as
T
times a sum over the residues at the points
\zeta_{r}
for
r\in S
, so that

T\sum_{r=1}^{N}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{r}}g(\lambda)\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{r}}g(\lambda)\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda=0.

(3.22)

By definition of the bilinear pairing from Proposition
3.1
, we thus have
\langle Y,X\rangle=0
, as desired.

Let us now prove the converse. Namely, let
Y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
be arbitrary and suppose that
\langle Y,X\rangle=0
for all
X\in\iota_{\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
. We must show that, in fact,
Y(\lambda)\in\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
. It follows from Proposition
3.2
that
Y=(Y_{0},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{N},Y_{\infty})\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
has a unique decomposition as a direct sum of the tuples
\mathcal{P}_{+}(Y)=(\mathcal{P}_{0+}(Y_{0}),\mathcal{P}_{1+}(Y_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{P}_{N+}(Y_{N}),\mathcal{P}_{\infty+}(Y_{\infty}))\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(1)}
and
\mathcal{P}_{-}(Y)=\iota_{\lambda}g
for some
{g\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}}
. Now, let
X=\iota_{\lambda}f
for some
f\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
. Since
\langle Y,X\rangle=0
, writing this out explicitly means

\displaystyle T\sum_{r=1}^{N}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{r+}(Y_{r}(\lambda_{r}))\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{r+}(Y_{r}(\lambda_{r}))\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda

\displaystyle\qquad{}+T\sum_{r=1}^{N}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{r}}g(\lambda)\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda+\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{r}}g(\lambda)\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda=0.

From (
3.22
), we have that the last two terms on the left-hand side vanish on their own. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle T\sum_{r=1}^{N}\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{r+}(Y_{r}(\lambda_{r}))\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda+\!\!\sum_{r\in\{0,\infty\}}\!\!\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\Tr\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{r+}(Y_{r}(\lambda_{r}))\iota_{\lambda_{r}}f(\lambda)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda=0.

One can then show, along the same lines as the argument in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, that if
\mathcal{P}_{+}(Y)\neq 0
then by picking a suitable
f\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
adapted to
\mathcal{P}_{+}(Y)\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(1)}
, one can ensure that the expression on the left-hand side is non-zero, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we conclude that
\mathcal{P}_{+}(Y)=0
, and hence
Y\in\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
, as required.
The proof of the “moreover” part is completely analogous.
∎

The upshot of Proposition
3.4
is that we are now exactly in the setting recalled in points 1 and 2 of Section
2
. In particular, the coadjoint representation
\Ad^{R\ast}\colon\mathbb{G}_{R}\times V\to V
of the group
\mathbb{G}_{R}
on our model
V=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
for the dual space
\mathbb{g}^{\ast}
is given by formula (
2.5
). This will be used below in Lemma
3.5
to give a concise description of the desired coadjoint orbit for the Lax matrix of the cyclotomic Gaudin model.

Before stating the lemma, we first give an explicit description of the infinite-dimensional Lie group
\mathbb{G}_{+}\!=\widetilde{G}_{D+}^{(0)}
associated with the Lie algebra
\mathbb{g}_{+}\!=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(0)}
. Its elements are of the form

\varphi_{+}=(\varphi_{0+},\varphi_{1+},\dots,\varphi_{N+},\varphi_{\infty+}),

(3.23)

where
\varphi_{r+}(\lambda_{r})
is a Taylor series in the local parameter
\lambda_{r}
with values in
G
, the matrix Lie group associated with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{r+}(\lambda_{r})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{r}^{(n)}\lambda_{r}^{n},\qquad r\neq\infty,

\displaystyle\varphi_{\infty+}(\lambda_{\infty})=\bm{1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{\infty}^{(n)}\lambda_{\infty}^{n}.

(3.24)

The Lie group
\mathbb{G}_{+}=\widetilde{G}_{D+}^{(0)}
has a natural action on
V=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
given by conjugation. This defines the coadjoint representation
\Ad^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{G}_{+}\times V\to V
from Section
2
explicitly as

(\varphi_{+},Y)\longmapsto\Ad^{\ast}_{\varphi_{+}}Y=\bigl(\varphi_{0+}Y_{0}\varphi_{0+}^{-1},\varphi_{1+}Y_{1}\varphi_{1+}^{-1},\dots,\varphi_{N+}Y_{N}\varphi_{N+}^{-1},\varphi_{\infty+}Y_{\infty}\varphi_{\infty+}^{-1}\bigr).

We are now in a position to give an explicit description of the
\mathbb{G}_{R}
-coadjoint orbit where the Lax matrix of the cyclotomic Gaudin model lives and which will act as our phase space.

Lemma 3.5
.

The orbit of the coadjoint action of
\mathbb{G}_{R}=\!\bigl(\widetilde{G}_{D}^{(0)}\bigr)_{R}
on an element
{\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\in V_{-}=\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}}
has the explicit form

\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}=\bigl\{\mathcal{P}_{-}\bigl(\Ad_{\varphi_{+}}^{\ast}\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\bigr)\mid\varphi_{+}\in\widetilde{G}_{D+}^{(0)}\bigr\}.

Proof.

By definition, the coadjoint orbit of
\bigl(\widetilde{G}_{D}^{(0)}\bigr)_{R}
on any
\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\in\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
is given by

\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}=\bigl\{\Ad_{\varphi}^{R\ast}\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\mid\varphi\in\bigl(\widetilde{G}_{D}^{(0)}\bigr)_{R}\bigr\}.

Using the explicit form (
2.5
) for the coadjoint action of
\mathbb{G}_{R}
, we have

\Ad^{R\ast}_{\varphi}\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda=\mathcal{P}_{-}\bigl(\Ad^{\ast}_{\varphi_{+}}\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\bigr)+\mathcal{P}_{+}\bigl(\Ad^{\ast}_{\varphi_{-}}\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\bigr)=\mathcal{P}_{-}\bigl(\Ad^{\ast}_{\varphi_{+}}\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\bigr),

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\in V_{-}=\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
, so that
\Ad^{\ast}_{\varphi_{-}}\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\in V_{-}
also and hence
\mathcal{P}_{+}\bigl(\Ad^{\ast}_{\varphi_{-}}\iota_{\lambda}\Lambda\bigr)=0
.
∎

It will be useful in practice to express the action of the projector
\mathcal{P}_{-}
on
Y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
as

\mathcal{P}_{-}(Y)=\iota_{\lambda}\circ\pi_{\lambda}^{(1)}(Y).

(3.25)

In the remaining sections, we will put this setup to use to describe the Lax matrix of the cyclotomic Gaudin model (
1.9
) as a point in a coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
for some suitable
\Lambda\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
and then introduce the ingredients from point 3 in Section

to derive the associated Lax equations.

3.2
Lax matrix

Our algebraic setup covers the case of the cyclotomic Gaudin model with arbitrary multiplicities. However, as mentioned at the start of this section, from now on we will restrict to the case with simple poles at all
\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}
,
k\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
,
r\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, and double poles at the origin and at infinity, since this is the setting required for our examples in Section
4
. The discussion in the remaining sections is easily generalised to the case of arbitrary multiplicities.

To describe a coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}\in\iota_{\lambda}\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
from Lemma
3.5
corresponding to the Lax matrix (
1.9
) of the cyclotomic Gaudin model, we fix a non-dynamical element
\Lambda\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
with the same pole structure as (
1.9
), namely we introduce

\Lambda(\lambda)=\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{r}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}}+\Lambda_{\infty}\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}.

According to Lemma
3.5
and formula (
3.25
), the corresponding coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
then consists of elements of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{-}\Biggl(\varphi_{+}\iota_{\lambda}\Biggl(\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{r}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}}+\Lambda_{\infty}\Biggr)\varphi_{+}^{-1}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\iota_{\lambda}\circ\pi_{\lambda}^{(1)}\Biggl(\frac{A_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{A_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}},\frac{1}{T}\frac{A_{1}}{\lambda-\zeta_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{T}\frac{A_{N}}{\lambda-\zeta_{N}},A_{\infty}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\iota_{\lambda}\Biggl(\frac{A_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{A_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{r=1}^{T-1}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}A_{r}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}}+A_{\infty}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\equiv\iota_{\lambda}L(\lambda),

(3.26)

where, recalling the definitions (
3.23
) and (
3.24
), we have set

\displaystyle A_{0}^{(0)}=\phi_{0}^{(0)}\Lambda_{0}^{(0)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1}+\bigl[\phi_{0}^{(1)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1},\phi_{0}^{(0)}\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1}\bigr],

\displaystyle A_{0}^{(1)}=\phi_{0}^{(0)}\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1},

\displaystyle A_{r}=\phi_{r}\Lambda_{r}\phi_{r}^{-1},\qquad r\in\{1,\dots,N\},

\displaystyle A_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty},

(3.27)

with
\phi_{r}^{(0)}
denoted by
\phi_{r}
, for
r\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, to simplify notations. Notice that we have the relations
\sigma\phi_{0}^{(0)}=\phi_{0}^{(0)}
and
\sigma\phi_{0}^{(1)}=\omega\phi_{0}^{(1)}
for the field elements which ensure that
A_{0}^{(0)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}
and
A_{0}^{(1)}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(-1)}
. This gives us the desired parameterisation of the cyclotomic Gaudin Lax matrix

L(\lambda)=\frac{A_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{A_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{r=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}A_{r}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}}+A_{\infty}

(3.28)

viewed as an element of the coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
.

3.3
Lax equations

To derive Lax equations for the Lax matrix (
3.28
) of the cyclotomic Gaudin model, let us return to the hierarchy of Lax equations (
2.6
) induced by the family of Hamiltonians in involution with respect to
\{\,,\,\}_{R}
. In our current setup,
\{\,,\,\}_{R}
is the Lie–Poisson bracket on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
associated with the linear map
R=P_{+}-P_{-}
. Invariant functions on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
take the form

H_{p,r}\colon\ Y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}\longmapsto\Res_{\lambda_{r}=0}\bigl(\ell_{p,r}(\lambda_{r})\Tr\bigl(Y_{r}(\lambda_{r})^{p+1}\bigr)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda,\qquad p\geq 1,\qquad r\in S,

(3.29)

where
\ell_{p,r}(\lambda_{r})\in\mathbb{C}((\lambda_{r}))
is a collection of Laurent polynomials for
p\geq 1
and
r\in S
. It follows from Proposition
3.1
that for these functions to be non-trivial, the Laurent polynomials
\ell_{p,r}(\lambda_{r})
for
r\in\{0,\infty\}
should be chosen such that
\ell_{p,r}(\lambda_{r})Y_{r}(\lambda_{r})^{p}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{r}^{\Gamma,0}
, while
\ell_{p,r}(\lambda_{r})
for
r\in\{1,\dots,N\}
can be any Laurent polynomials. Let us then choose

\displaystyle\ell_{p,r}(\lambda_{r})=\iota_{\lambda_{r}}\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p+1}\qquad\text{for}\quad r\in\{0,\infty\},

\displaystyle\ell_{p,r}(\lambda_{r})=\iota_{\lambda_{r}}\frac{T\lambda^{p}}{p+1}\qquad\text{for}\quad r\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

(3.30)

The restriction of the functions
H_{p,r}
to
\iota_{\lambda}L
are Hamiltonians (in involution) of the model and generate the elementary (pairwise-commuting) flows
\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}
. However, by virtue of the choice (
3.30
) we made, it follows from (
3.20
) that
\sum_{r\in S}H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L)=0
by the residue theorem, for each
p\geq 1
.

In what follows, it will thus be sufficient to focus on the Hamiltonians
H_{p,r}
for
r\neq\infty
and look at the associated equations of motion they produce through (
2.6
).
Using
R_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(R\pm\text{id})
, these equations can be rewritten as

\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}\iota_{\lambda}L=\bigl[R_{\pm}\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L),\iota_{\lambda}L\bigr].

(3.31)

As it suffices to calculate only one of the two expressions
R_{+}\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L)
and
R_{-}\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L)
, let us compute the latter. The gradient of
H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L)
is an element of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}
and satisfies

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L+\epsilon\eta)-H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L)}{\epsilon}=\langle\eta,\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L)\rangle

(3.32)

for all
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
. Using Proposition
3.2
, we may decompose this gradient as follows:

\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L)=N_{r}^{(p)}+\iota_{\lambda}h_{r}^{(p)},\qquad N_{r}^{(p)}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(0)},\qquad h_{r}^{(p)}\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma},

and rewrite (
3.31
) as

\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}\iota_{\lambda}L=[R_{-}\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L),\iota_{\lambda}L]=-[P_{-}(\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L)),\iota_{\lambda}L]=-\bigl[\iota_{\lambda}h_{r}^{(p)},\iota_{\lambda}L\bigr].

Since
\iota_{\lambda}
is an embedding which also clearly commutes with the Lie brackets
[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\times\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}\to\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
and
[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(0)}\times\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
, the above equation implies

\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}L=-\bigl[h_{r}^{(p)},L\bigr].

(3.33)

In order to calculate
h_{r}^{(p)}\in\mathcal{F}_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma}
, by virtue of Proposition
3.4
, it is sufficient to restrict
\eta
in (
3.32
) to live in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(1)}
. We then have

\bigl\langle\eta,N_{r}^{(p)}\bigr\rangle=0\qquad\text{for all}\quad\eta\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(1)},\quad N_{r}^{(p)}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D+}^{(0)},

and the right-hand side of (
3.32
) becomes

T\sum_{s=1}^{N}\Res_{\lambda_{s}=0}\Tr\bigl(\eta_{s}(\lambda_{s})\iota_{\lambda_{s}}h_{r}^{(p)}\bigr)+\sum_{s\in\{0,\infty\}}\Res_{\lambda_{s}=0}\Tr\bigl(\eta_{s}(\lambda_{s})\iota_{\lambda_{s}}h_{r}^{(p)}\bigr),

while for the left-hand side we find

for any
\eta_{s}(\lambda_{s})\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{s+}
,
s\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, and
\eta_{s}(\lambda_{s})\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{s+}^{\Gamma,1}
,

. By definition of

in (

\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{s}}h_{r}^{(p)}\bigr)_{-}=\begin{cases}0&\text{for}\ s\neq r,\\
\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{r}}\lambda^{p}\iota_{\lambda_{r}}L^{p}\bigr)_{-}&\text{for}\ s=r\end{cases}

, where

Explicitly, for

\partial_{t_{1}^{r}}L=-\bigl[h_{r}^{(1)},L\bigr]\qquad\text{with}\quad h_{r}^{(1)}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{A_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda}&\text{for}\ r=0,\\
\dfrac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\dfrac{\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}\sigma^{k}A_{r}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{r}}&\text{for}\ r\in\{1,\dots,N\}.\end{cases}

(3.34)

Finally, we turn to the primary goal of this paper which is to give a Lagrangian multiform for the cyclotomic Gaudin model that will provide a variational description of the hierarchy of Lax equations in (

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{p,r}

4
Realisations of the cyclotomic Gaudin model

In this section, we study two different realisations of the cyclotomic Gaudin model – the periodic Toda chain and the discrete self-trapping (DST) model – with the objective of describing their corresponding hierarchies variationally. We will then go on to show how our framework allows for a straightforward coupling of integrable hierarchies, by using the approach devised in
[
15
, Section 7]
to couple together hierarchies of integrable field theories.

In what follows, we will work with the cyclotomic Gaudin model associated with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\mathfrak{gl}_{T}(\mathbb{C})
and the automorphism
\sigma\in\text{Aut}\,\mathfrak{g}
defined by
\sigma(E_{ij})=\omega^{j-i}E_{ij}
, for every
i,j=1,\dots,T
. Here
\omega
is a primitive
T
-th root of unity, and by
E_{ij}
,
i,j=1,\dots,T
, we denote the standard basis of
\mathfrak{gl}_{T}(\mathbb{C})
taking the indices
i
and
j
modulo
T
by convention. The eigenspaces of
\sigma
defined by (
3.1
) are then given by
\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}=\text{span}\{E_{i,i+n}\}_{i=1}^{T}
.

Let us also note the following useful identity that we shall frequently make use of

\frac{z_{1}^{T-1-[l]}z_{2}^{[l]}}{z_{1}^{T}-z_{2}^{T}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\omega^{-kl}}{z_{1}-\omega^{k}z_{2}}

(4.1)

for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
[l]\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
is such that
l=[l]\mod T
.

4.1
Periodic Toda chain

The periodic Toda chain
[
39
]
describes a system of particles connected by “exponential springs” together with a periodic boundary condition, and has been extensively studied in the Hamiltonian formalism. See, for instance,
[
18
]
for the widely used Flaschka change of coordinates, and
[
3
]
for a proof of its integrability.

Lax matrix.
We will work with the Lax matrix

L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}\lambda^{-1}&1&0&&\dots&&a_{T}\lambda^{-2}\\
a_{1}\lambda^{-2}&p_{2}\lambda^{-1}&1&&\dots&&0&\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&&\ddots&&&&\vdots\\
0&&a_{i-1}\lambda^{-2}&p_{i}\lambda^{-1}&1&&0\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&&\dots&&a_{T-2}\lambda^{-2}&p_{T-1}\lambda^{-1}&1\\
1&&\dots&&0&a_{T-1}\lambda^{-2}&p_{T}\lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

(4.2)

where
a_{i}={\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}
, and
q_{i}
,
p_{i}
are the canonical coordinates satisfying the canonical Poisson bracket relations
\{p_{i},q_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}
for
i,j=1,\dots,T
. We also have the periodic boundary conditions
(p_{0},q_{0})=(p_{T},q_{T})
and
(p_{T+1},q_{T+1})=(p_{1},q_{1})
.

The standard Lax matrix for the periodic Toda chain (see
[
7
, Section 6]
, for instance)

\widetilde{L}_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}&a_{1}^{1/2}&0&&\dots&&a_{T}^{1/2}\lambda^{-1}\\
a_{1}^{1/2}&p_{2}&a_{2}^{1/2}&&\dots&&0&\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&&\ddots&&&&\vdots\\
0&&a_{i-1}^{1/2}&p_{i}&a_{i}^{1/2}&&0\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&&\dots&&a_{T-2}^{1/2}&p_{T-1}&a_{T-1}^{1/2}\\
a_{T}^{1/2}\lambda&&\dots&&0&a_{T-1}^{1/2}&p_{T}\\
\end{pmatrix}

is related to
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
by conjugation by the diagonal matrix
\mathcal{Q}\!=\!{\rm diag}\bigl({\rm e}^{-q_{1}/2}\lambda^{-1},\dots,{\rm e}^{-q_{T}/2}\lambda^{-T}\bigr)
and multiplication by an overall factor of
\lambda^{-1}
, together with a change of
\lambda
-dependence, as follows:

L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}\mathcal{Q}\widetilde{L}_{\text{Toda}}\bigl(\lambda^{T}\bigr)\mathcal{Q}^{-1}.

(4.3)

The Poisson bracket of the Lax matrix
\widetilde{L}_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
can be written as

\bigl\{\widetilde{L}_{\text{Toda}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda),\widetilde{L}_{\text{Toda}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr\}=\bigl[\widetilde{r}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu),\widetilde{L}_{\text{Toda}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda)+\widetilde{L}_{\text{Toda}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr],

where
\widetilde{r}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)
is the skew-symmetric
r
-matrix

\widetilde{r}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mu+\lambda}{\mu-\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}+\frac{1}{\mu-\lambda}\biggl(\mu\sum_{i<j}+\lambda\sum_{i>j}\biggr)E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}.

Under the gauge transformation (
4.3
), on using the identity (
4.1
), we find that the Lax matrix
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
satisfies the Poisson bracket

\bigl\{L_{\text{Toda}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda),L_{\text{Toda}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr\}=\bigl[r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu),L_{\text{Toda}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda)\bigr]-\bigl[r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{21}}}}(\mu,\lambda),L_{\text{Toda}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr],

where
r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)
is the non-skew-symmetric cyclotomic
r
-matrix

r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\omega^{k(j-i)}}{\mu-\omega^{-k}\lambda}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}\,

(4.4)

that we have been working with. This explains our choice of Lax matrix (
4.2
) as opposed to the more traditional one. As just proved, it satisfies the Poisson algebra of the cyclotomic
\mathfrak{gl}_{T}(\mathbb{C})
-Gaudin model and therefore allows us to obtain the periodic Toda chain as a certain realisation of that model. Specifically, the Lax matrix
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
can be seen as a realisation of the cyclotomic Gaudin Lax matrix with double poles at the origin and at infinity, that is,

L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)=\frac{J_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{J_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+J_{\infty},

(4.5)

where

J_{0}^{(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}p_{i}E_{ii},\qquad J_{0}^{(1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}E_{i+1,i},\qquad J_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{i,i+1}.

Orbit realisation.
Set
D=\{0,\infty\}
, and choose the non-dynamical element

\Lambda_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)=\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+\Lambda_{\infty}\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma},

where

\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{i+1,i}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(-1)},\qquad\Lambda_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{i,i+1}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}.

The group elements
\varphi_{+}=\left(\varphi_{0+},\varphi_{\infty+}\right)
, defined by (
3.24
), contain the dynamical degrees of freedom. From (
3.27
), we know that the components of
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
can now be expressed as

J_{0}^{(0)}=\bigl[\phi_{0}^{(1)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1},J_{0}^{(1)}\bigr],\qquad J_{0}^{(1)}=\phi_{0}^{(0)}\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1},\qquad J_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty}.

(4.6)

This gives us a parametrisation of the Lax matrix
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
in (
4.5
) as an element of the coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}_{\Lambda}^{\text{Toda}}
. Since
\sigma(\varphi_{0+}(\lambda))=\varphi_{0+}(\omega\lambda)
, we have
\sigma\phi_{0}^{(n)}=\omega^{n}\phi_{0}^{(n)}
. In particular,
\phi_{0}^{(0)}
and
\phi_{0}^{(1)}
have the form

\phi_{0}^{(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}u_{i}E_{ii},\qquad\phi_{0}^{(1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}v_{i}E_{i,i+1}.

For convenience, define
(u_{0},v_{0})=(u_{T},v_{T})
and
(u_{T+1},v_{T+1})=(u_{1},v_{1})
to encode the periodic boundary conditions. Then, from (
4.6
), we get

J_{0}^{(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\biggl(\frac{v_{i}}{u_{i}}-\frac{v_{i-1}}{u_{i-1}}\biggr)E_{ii},\qquad J_{0}^{(1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\dfrac{u_{i+1}}{u_{i}}E_{i+1,i},\qquad J_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{i,i+1}.

Defining

p_{i}=\frac{v_{i}}{u_{i}}-\frac{v_{i-1}}{u_{i-1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{i}=-\ln{u_{i}}\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,T,

we now have the desired realisation of the coefficients of the Lax matrix
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
. The coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}_{\Lambda}^{\text{Toda}}
where
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
lives is parameterised by
a_{i}
,
p_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,T
, satisfying
\prod_{i}^{T}a_{i}=1
and
\sum_{i}^{T}p_{i}=0
.

Lax equations.
Let us now look at the Lax equations associated with the invariant functions on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
defined in (
3.36
) for the general case. The only functions we need to consider are

H_{p,0}=\dfrac{1}{p+1}\Res_{\lambda=0}\bigl(\lambda^{p}\Tr\bigl(\iota_{\lambda_{0}}L_{\text{Toda}}^{p+1}\bigr)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda.

The set of functions
H_{p,\infty}
are not independent: we have
H_{p,\infty}=-H_{p,0}
for all
p\geq 1
. The Lax equations for the periodic Toda chain with respect to the elementary times
t_{p}^{0}
are given by

\partial_{t_{p}^{0}}\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda}}=[R_{\pm}\nabla H_{p,0}(\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda}}),\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda}}].

For
p=1
, these take the form of the set of Lax equations in (
3.34
):

\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}L_{\text{Toda}}=[M_{1,0},L_{\text{Toda}}]\qquad\text{with}\quad M_{1,0}=-\dfrac{J_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda}.

(4.7)

Taking residues on both sides in (
4.7
) gives the equations of motion for
p_{i}
,
q_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,T
. To get the equations of motion for
q_{i}
it is most convenient to “undo” the dressing and write the corresponding equation as

\bigl[\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}\phi_{0}^{(0)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1}-J_{0}^{(0)},J_{0}^{(1)}\bigr]=0.

This tells us that the diagonal matrix

\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1}\bigl(\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}\phi_{0}^{(0)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1}-J_{0}^{(0)}\bigr)\phi_{0}^{(0)}

must commute with
\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}
, and is therefore equal to
\alpha\bm{1}
. Using the freedom
\phi_{0}^{(0)}\to\phi_{0}^{(0)}g
, where
g
is diagonal, to set
\det\phi_{0}^{(0)}=1
, we see that
\alpha=0
by taking the trace of

\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}\phi_{0}^{(0)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1}-J_{0}^{(0)}=\alpha\bm{1}.

Thus, we have the desired Toda equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}q_{i}=-p_{i},

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}p_{i}={\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}-{\rm e}^{q_{i-1}-q_{i}}.

(4.8)

Lagrangian description.
Using Theorem
3.6
, we can now write a Lagrangian multiform for the periodic Toda hierarchy as

\mathscr{L}_{\text{Toda}}=\sum_{p=1}^{M}\mathscr{L}_{p,0}\,\mathrm{d}t_{p}^{0}

with

\mathscr{L}_{p,0}=-\sum_{i=1}^{T}p_{i}\partial_{t_{p}^{0}}q_{i}-\dfrac{1}{p+1}\Res_{\lambda=0}\bigl(\lambda^{p}\Tr\bigl(L_{\text{Toda}}^{p+1}\bigr)\bigr){\rm d}\lambda.

For
p=1
, this gives us the Lagrangian coefficients for the periodic Toda chain with respect to the time
t_{1}^{0}
:

\mathscr{L}_{1,0}=-\sum_{i=1}^{T}p_{i}\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}q_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{T}p_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}.

This is the expected
phase space
Lagrangian that our method produces and which gives Hamilton’s equations (
4.8
) for periodic Toda. It corresponds to the (tangent bundle) Lagrangian

\mathscr{L}_{1,0}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\bigl(\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}q_{i}\bigr)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}.

4.2
DST model

The discrete self-trapping (DST) equation was introduced in
[
17
]
to describe the dynamics of small molecules, which then led to detailed studies of the DST dimer using different methods. The DST model we cast into our framework here is a generalisation of the dimer case to arbitrary (finite) degrees of freedom. This general case first appeared in
[
16
]
where its relationship with the periodic Toda chain was also hinted at. Our motivation here being different, we do not delve into this connection between the two theories. The interested reader is referred to
[
22
]
where this aspect was explored further.

Lax matrix.
We work here with the following avatar of the Lax matrix of the DST model

L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}E_{ii}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\omega^{k(j-i)}x_{i}X_{j}E_{ij}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{i,i+1},

where
c_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,T
, are complex parameters, and
x_{i}
,
X_{i}
are the canonical coordinates satisfying the canonical Poisson bracket relations
\{X_{i},x_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}
for
i,j=1,\dots,T
, and the periodic conditions
(X_{0},x_{0})=(X_{T},x_{T})
and
(X_{T+1},x_{T+1})=(X_{1},x_{1})
.

The DST Lax matrix in
[
22
, equation (3.8)]
is given as

\widehat{L}_{{\rm DST}}(\mu)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\frac{b^{T+i-j}x_{i}X_{j}E_{ij}}{\mu-b^{T}}+\mu E_{T1}+\sum_{i\geq j}b^{i-j}x_{i}X_{j}E_{ij}+\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}E_{ii}+\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}E_{i,i+1},

where
b,c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
are parameters of the model, and
\mu
is the spectral parameter of the Lax matrix. In the present setup where we realise the DST model as a cyclotomic Gaudin model, the role of the parameter
b
is played by the location of the pole
\zeta_{1}
on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\setminus\{0,\infty\}
.
One obtains the Lax matrix
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
by conjugating
\widehat{L}_{{\rm DST}}\bigl(\lambda^{T}\bigr)
by the diagonal matrix
\mathcal{D}={\rm diag}\bigl(\lambda^{-1},\dots,\lambda^{-T}\bigr)
followed by an overall multiplication by
\lambda^{-1}
, together with a change of
\lambda
-dependence, that is,

L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}\mathcal{D}\widehat{L}_{{\rm DST}}\bigl(\lambda^{T}\bigr)\mathcal{D}^{-1},

(4.9)

and then using the identity (
4.1
).

The Poisson bracket of the Lax matrix
\widehat{L}_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
is given as

\bigl\{\widehat{L}_{{\rm DST}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda),\widehat{L}_{{\rm DST}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr\}=\bigl[\widehat{r}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu),\widehat{L}_{{\rm DST}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda)\bigr]-\bigl[\widehat{r}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{21}}}}(\mu,\lambda),\widehat{L}_{{\rm DST}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr],

where

\widehat{r}_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{1}{\mu-\lambda}\biggl(\mu\sum_{i\leq j}+\lambda\sum_{i>j}\biggr)E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}.

Using the identity (
4.1
) once again, one finds that the gauge transformation (
4.9
) gives for
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
the
r
-matrix (
4.4
) associated with the cyclotomic
\mathfrak{gl}_{T}(\mathbb{C})
-Gaudin model, as we would have anticipated. We then have the Poisson bracket

\bigl\{L_{{\rm DST}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda),L_{{\rm DST}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr\}=\bigl[r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{12}}}}(\lambda,\mu),L_{{\rm DST}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{1}}}}(\lambda)\bigr]-\bigl[r_{{}_{\mathbf{\underline{21}}}}(\mu,\lambda),L_{{\rm DST}\,_{\mathbf{\underline{2}}}}(\mu)\bigr].

Similar to the case of the periodic Toda chain, the Lax matrix
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
can be seen as a realisation of the cyclotomic Gaudin Lax matrix, this time with simple poles at the origin and all
\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}
,
k\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
, for some
\zeta_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, and a double pole at infinity, that is,

L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)=\frac{K_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}K_{1}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}}+K_{\infty},

(4.10)

where

K_{0}^{(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}E_{ii},\qquad K_{1}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}x_{i}X_{j}E_{ij},\qquad K_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{i,i+1}.

(4.11)

Orbit realisation.
Set
D=\{0,1,\infty\}
, and choose the non-dynamical element

\Lambda_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)=\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{1}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}}+\Lambda_{\infty}\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma},

where

\Lambda_{0}^{(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}E_{ii}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(0)},\qquad\Lambda_{1}=E_{11}\in\mathfrak{g},\qquad\Lambda_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{i,i+1}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}.

The coadjoint action of the group elements
\varphi_{+}=\left(\varphi_{0+},\varphi_{1+},\varphi_{\infty+}\right)
, defined by (
3.24
), on
\Lambda_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
gives the orbit where
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
lives. From (
3.27
), we know that the components of
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
can then be expressed as

\displaystyle K_{0}^{(0)}=\phi_{0}^{(0)}\Lambda_{0}^{(0)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1},\qquad K_{1}=\phi_{1}\Lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1},\qquad K_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty},

(4.12)

where we have denoted
\phi_{1}^{(0)}
by
\phi_{1}
for simplicity.
This gives us a parameterisation of the Lax matrix
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
in (
4.14
) as an element of the coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}_{\Lambda}^{{\rm DST}}
. We parameterise
\phi_{1}
as

\phi_{1}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}s_{ij}E_{ij},

and denote by
\hat{s}_{ij}
,
i,j=1,\dots,T
, the entries of its inverse, that is,

\phi_{1}^{-1}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\hat{s}_{ij}E_{ij}.

From (
4.12
), we then have

K_{0}^{(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}E_{ii},\qquad K_{1}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}s_{i1}\hat{s}_{1j}E_{ij},\qquad K_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}E_{i,i+1}.

Defining

x_{i}=s_{i1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{i}=\hat{s}_{1i}\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,T,

gives us the desired realisation of the Lax matrix
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
as an element of the coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}_{\Lambda}^{{\rm DST}}
. Notice that
\Tr K_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}x_{i}X_{i}=1
is an orbit invariant and can be seen as being generated by the symmetry
x_{i}\rightarrow ax_{i}
,
X_{i}\rightarrow a^{-1}X_{i}
.

Lax equations.
Let us choose invariant functions
H_{p,r}
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
as defined in (
3.36
), and treat
H_{p,0}
and
H_{p,1}
as the independent functions. The Lax equations for the DST model with respect to the elementary times
t_{p}^{r}
are then given by

\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}\iota_{\lambda}L_{{\rm DST}}=[R_{\pm}\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L_{{\rm DST}}),\iota_{\lambda}L_{{\rm DST}}].

For
p=1
, these take the form of the set of Lax equations in (
3.34
):

\partial_{t_{1}^{r}}L_{{\rm DST}}=[M_{1,r},L_{{\rm DST}}]\qquad\text{with}\quad M_{1,r}=\begin{cases}\quad 0&\text{for}\ r=0,\\
-\dfrac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\dfrac{\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}\sigma^{k}K_{1}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}}&\text{for}\ r=1.\end{cases}

The
t_{1}^{0}
equations are trivial. Similar to the case of the periodic Toda chain, the easiest way to get the
t_{1}^{1}
equations of motion for
x_{i}
and
X_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,T
, is to undo the dressing. With
{M=K_{0}^{(0)}+\zeta_{1}K_{\infty}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}\sigma^{k}K_{1}}
, we find that
\phi_{1}^{-1}(\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}\phi_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}-M)\phi_{1}
must commute with
\Lambda_{1}
, and hence be block diagonal with a scalar “block”, say
\rho
, and a
(T-1)\times(T-1)
block which is irrelevant. Then we find, collecting
x_{i}
in the vector
{\bf x}
, and
X_{i}
in the vector
{\bf X}
,

\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}{\bf x}-M{\bf x}=\rho{\bf x},\qquad\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}{\bf X}^{T}-{\bf X}^{T}M=\rho{\bf X}^{T}.

Using the freedom
x_{i}\rightarrow ax_{i}
,
X_{i}\rightarrow a^{-1}X_{i}
mentioned above with
a={\rm e}^{B}
,
\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}B=\rho
, we can set
{\rho=0}
. Explicitly, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}X_{i}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}x_{i}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}X_{i}=-c_{i}X_{i}-\zeta_{1}X_{i-1}-\dfrac{1}{T}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{T-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{T}\omega^{k(j-i)}x_{j}X_{j}X_{i},

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}x_{i}=c_{i}x_{i}+\zeta_{1}x_{i+1}+\dfrac{1}{T}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{T-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{T}\omega^{k(j-i)}X_{j}x_{j}x_{i}.

(4.13)

Lagrangian description.
We can now write a Lagrangian multiform for the DST hierarchy using Theorem
3.6
as

\mathscr{L}_{{\rm DST}}=\sum_{p=1}^{M}\mathscr{L}_{p,0}\,\mathrm{d}t_{p}^{0}+\sum_{p=1}^{M}\mathscr{L}_{p,1}\,\mathrm{d}t_{p}^{1}

with

\mathscr{L}_{p,r}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}X_{i}\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}x_{i}-H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L_{{\rm DST}}),\qquad r\in\{0,1\},

where the potential term
H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L_{{\rm DST}})
is given by (
3.36
) for
r\in\{0,1\}
. Notice that we have dropped the kinetic contribution to
\mathscr{L}_{p,r}
from the pole at origin since being a total derivative it will not enter the Euler–Lagrange equations.
For
p=1
, the Lagrangian coefficients explicitly read

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{1,0}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}X_{i}\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}x_{i}-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{1,1}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}X_{i}\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}x_{i}-\dfrac{1}{2T}\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\omega^{k(j-i)}x_{i}x_{j}X_{i}X_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}x_{i}X_{i}-\zeta_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{T}x_{i+1}X_{i}.

It can be checked that the Euler–Lagrange equations obtained from varying
\mathscr{L}_{1,0}
and
\mathscr{L}_{1,1}
with respect to
X_{i}
,
x_{i}
are exactly the equations in (
4.13
).

4.3
Coupled Toda-DST system

Finally, we turn to the task of coupling together the two hierarchies we have described variationally in this section. The Lax matrix of the coupled hierarchy can be expressed as

L_{\text{Toda-DST}}(\lambda)=L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)+\beta L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda),

(4.14)

where
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
is the Lax matrix of the periodic Toda chain in (
4.5
),
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
is the DST Lax matrix (
4.10
), and the parameter
\beta
is a real-valued scalar parameter dictating the strength of coupling between the two hierarchies. Naturally, the Lax matrix
L_{\text{Toda-DST}}(\lambda)
can be seen as a realisation of the cyclotomic Gaudin Lax matrix:

L_{\text{Toda-DST}}(\lambda)=\frac{J_{0}^{(0)}+\beta K_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{J_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\beta}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}K_{1}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}}+J_{\infty}+\beta K_{\infty}

with the
\mathfrak{g}
-valued coefficients defined in (
4.6
) and (
4.11
).

As the number of finite (non-zero) poles in a cyclotomic Gaudin model is arbitrary, our construction allows us, in principle, to couple together an arbitrary number of copies of the DST model and a copy of the periodic Toda chain. Here we only illustrate it for a single copy each of the periodic Toda chain and the DST model.

Orbit realisation.
Set
D=\{0,1,\infty\}
. Since we already have a parameterisation of the Lax matrices
L_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)
and
L_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)
as orbit elements, we only need to check that the action of a generic group element
\varphi_{+}=(\varphi_{0+},\varphi_{1+},\varphi_{\infty+})\in\widetilde{G}_{D+}^{(0)}
defined by (
3.24
) on the non-dynamical element

\Lambda_{\text{Toda-DST}}(\lambda)=\Lambda_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)+\beta\Lambda_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)\in\Omega_{D^{\prime}}^{\Gamma},

where

\Lambda_{\text{Toda}}(\lambda)=\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda^{2}}+\Lambda_{\infty},\qquad\Lambda_{{\rm DST}}(\lambda)=\frac{\Lambda_{0}^{(0)}}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sigma^{k}\Lambda_{1}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}}+\Lambda_{\infty}

results in the Lax matrix
L_{\text{Toda-DST}}(\lambda)
. Indeed, using the result in (
3.27
), we have that the components of
L_{\text{Toda-DST}}(\lambda)
take the form

\displaystyle J_{0}^{(0)}+\beta K_{0}^{(0)}=\bigl[\phi_{0}^{(1)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1},J_{0}^{(1)}\bigr]+\beta\phi_{0}^{(0)}\Lambda_{0}^{(0)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1},

\displaystyle J_{0}^{(1)}=\phi_{0}^{(0)}\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1},

\displaystyle\beta K_{1}=\beta\phi_{1}\Lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1},\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle J_{\infty}+\beta K_{\infty}=\Lambda_{\infty}+\beta\Lambda_{\infty},

as desired. This gives us a parameterisation of the Lax matrix
L_{\text{Toda-DST}}(\lambda)
in (
4.14
) as an element of the coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}_{\Lambda}^{\text{Toda}}\times{\cal O}_{\Lambda}^{{\rm DST}}
.

Lax equations.
As earlier, we will choose invariant functions
H_{p,r}
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{D}^{(1)}
as defined in (
3.36
), for
r\in\{0,1\}
. The Lax equations for the DST-Toda model with respect to the elementary times
t_{p}^{r}
are given by

\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda-DST}}=[R_{\pm}\nabla H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda-DST}}),\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda-DST}}].

For
p=1
, these take the form of the set of Lax equations in (
3.34
):

\partial_{t_{1}^{r}}L_{\text{Toda-DST}}=[M_{1,r},L_{\text{Toda-DST}}]

(4.15)

with

M_{1,r}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{J_{0}^{(1)}}{\lambda}&\text{for}\ r=0,\\
-\dfrac{\beta}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\dfrac{\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}\sigma^{k}K_{1}}{\lambda-\omega^{k}\zeta_{1}}&\text{for}\ r=1.\end{cases}

Taking residues on both sides in (
4.15
) gives the equations of motion for
p_{i}
,
q_{i}
,
X_{i}
,
x_{i}
, for
{i=1,\dots,T}
. Like in the cases of the periodic Toda chain and the DST model, to get the equations of motion for
q_{i}
,
X_{i}
,
x_{i}
, one can “undo” the dressing in the corresponding equations. Explicitly, one gets the following equations for
p_{i}
,
q_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,T
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}p_{i}=(1+\beta)\bigl({\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}-{\rm e}^{q_{i-1}-q_{i}}\bigr)+\dfrac{\beta}{\zeta_{1}}\bigl({\rm e}^{q_{i-1}-q_{i}}x_{i-1}X_{i}-{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}x_{i}X_{i+1}\bigr),

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}q_{i}=-p_{i}-\beta c_{i},

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}p_{i}=\dfrac{\beta}{\zeta_{1}}\bigl({\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}x_{i}X_{i+1}-{\rm e}^{q_{i-1}-q_{i}}x_{i-1}X_{i}\bigr),

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}q_{i}=-\beta x_{i}X_{i},

(4.16)

and the following equations for
X_{i}
,
x_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,T
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}X_{i}=\dfrac{1}{\zeta_{1}}{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}X_{i+1},

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{0}}x_{i}=-\dfrac{1}{\zeta_{1}}{\rm e}^{q_{i-1}-q_{i}}x_{i-1},

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}X_{i}=-p_{i}X_{i}-\beta c_{i}X_{i}-\dfrac{1}{\zeta_{1}}{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}X_{i+1}-\dfrac{\beta}{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{T}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{T-1}\omega^{k(j-i)}x_{j}X_{j}X_{i}-(1+\beta)\zeta_{1}X_{i-1},

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{1}^{1}}x_{i}=p_{i}x_{i}+\beta c_{i}x_{i}+\dfrac{1}{\zeta_{1}}{\rm e}^{q_{i-1}-q_{i}}x_{i-1}+\dfrac{\beta}{T}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{T}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{T-1}\omega^{k(j-i)}X_{j}x_{j}x_{i}+(1+\beta)\zeta_{1}x_{i+1}.

(4.17)

Setting
\beta=0
in the above equations produces the equations of motion in (
4.8
) for the periodic Toda chain. In the limit
\beta\rightarrow\infty
, these reduce to the equations of motion we obtained for the DST model in (
4.13
). To see this, note that the Lax matrix
L_{{\rm DST}}
comes as
\beta L_{{\rm DST}}
in the coupled system. Therefore, in the limit
\beta\rightarrow\infty
, the time flow
t_{p}^{r}
is rescaled such that
\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}
rescales to
\beta^{p}\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}
.

Lagrangian description.
Using Theorem
3.6
, we can now write a Lagrangian multiform for the Toda-DST hierarchy:

\mathscr{L}_{\text{Toda-DST}}=\sum_{p=1}^{M}\mathscr{L}_{p,0}\mathrm{d}t_{p}^{0}+\sum_{p=1}^{M}\mathscr{L}_{p,1}\mathrm{d}t_{p}^{1}

with

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{p,r}

\displaystyle{}=\Tr\bigl(J_{0}^{(0)}\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}\phi_{0}^{(0)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1}\bigr)+\beta\Tr\bigl(K_{0}^{(0)}\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}\phi_{0}^{(0)}\phi_{0}^{(0)\,-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\beta\Tr\bigl(K_{1}\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}\phi_{1}\phi_{1}^{-1}\bigr)-H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda-DST}}),\qquad r\in\{0,1\},

where the potential term
H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda-DST}})
is given by (
3.36
) for
r\in\{0,1\}
. Notice that
K_{0}^{(0)}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}E_{ii}
. So, the second term on the right-hand side is, in fact, a total derivative and will not enter the Euler–Lagrange equations. So, we will simply drop it. In terms of the canonical coordinates, we then have

\mathscr{L}_{p,r}=-\sum_{i=1}^{T}p_{i}\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}q_{i}+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{T}X_{i}\partial_{t_{p}^{r}}x_{i}-H_{p,r}(\iota_{\lambda}L_{\text{Toda-DST}}),\qquad r\in\{0,1\}.

(4.18)

The decoupled limits of the periodic Toda and the DST hierarchies are easily recovered by setting
\beta=0
and taking the limit
\beta\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.18
), respectively.

For
p=1
, (
4.18
) gives the Lagrangian for the coupling of the periodic Toda chain and the DST model with the potential terms

\displaystyle H_{1,0}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{T}p_{i}^{2}+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}p_{i}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}^{2}+(1+\beta)\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}-\frac{\beta}{\zeta_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}x_{i}X_{i+1},

\displaystyle H_{1,1}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{2T}\sum_{i,j=1}^{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\omega^{k(j-i)}x_{i}x_{j}X_{i}X_{j}+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{T}p_{i}x_{i}X_{i}+\beta^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{T}c_{i}x_{i}X_{i}

\displaystyle\hphantom{H_{1, 1} =}{}+\frac{\beta}{\zeta_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\rm e}^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}x_{i}X_{i+1}+\bigl(\beta+\beta^{2}\bigr)\zeta_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{T}x_{i+1}X_{i}.

The variation
\delta\mathscr{L}_{1,r}
gives the Euler–Lagrange equations for the coupled system with respect to the time flow
t_{1}^{r}
, for each
r\in\{0,1\}
. It can be checked that these are exactly the equations we obtained in (
4.16
) and (
4.17
).

5
Conclusion

From the point of view of Lagrangian multiforms, our main result is the construction of a Lagrangian multiform for the class of cyclotomic Gaudin models which includes, as a particular example, the emblematic periodic Toda chain. The main technical aspect of this result lies in the pinning down of the necessary algebraic setup (in Section
3
) associated with the classical
r
-matrix of the cyclotomic Gaudin model which is both non-skew-symmetric and depends on spectral parameters. This is the first time that a Lagrangian multiform has been constructed for an
r
-matrix with both of these properties, as previous general constructions were either only presented for skew-symmetric ones
[
11
,
15
]
or for non-skew-symmetric
r
-matrices but without spectral parameter
[
11
]
.

The non-skew-symmetric
r
-matrix of the cyclotomic Gaudin model arises from a decomposition of a direct sum of (twisted) loop algebras into a pair of complementary subalgebras, which controls the corresponding Lie dialgebra structure entering its Lagrangian multiform. While finishing this work, the preprint
[
1
]
appeared on the construction of solutions of the general classical Yang–Baxter equation based on more general decompositions of such Lie algebras and to which one can associate generalised notions of Gaudin models. In particular, it appears that the cyclotomic setup considered here is a special case of their construction. It would be interesting to investigate if our Lagrangian multiform construction can be extended to the more general non-skew-symmetric
r
-matrices and associated generalised Gaudin models of
[
1
]
.

Lepton phenomenology of Stueckelberg portal to dark sector Preprint: TTP21-049, RBI-ThPhys-2022-4
Aliaksei Kachanovich, Sergey Kovalenko, Serguei Kuleshov, Valery E. Lyubovitskij, Alexey S. Zhevlakov

Abstract
We propose an extension of the Standard Model (SM) with a U A ′ ​ ( 1 ) U_{A^{\prime}}(1) gauge invariant dark dector connected to the SM via a new portal — the Stueckelberg portal, arising in the framework of dark photon A ′ A^{\prime} mass generation via Stueckelberg mechanism. This portal opens through the effective dim=5 operators constructed from the covariant term of the auxiliary Stueckelberg scalar field σ \sigma providing flavor non-diagonal renormalizable couplings of both σ \sigma and A ′ A^{\prime} to the SM fermions ψ \psi . The Stueckelberg scalar plays a role of Goldstone boson in the generation of mass of the Dark Photon. Contrary to the conventional kinetic mixing portal, in our scenario flavor diagonal A ′ A^{\prime} - ψ \psi couplings are not proportional to the fermion charges and are, in general, flavor nondiagonal. These features drastically change the phenomenology of dark photon A ′ A^{\prime} relaxing or avoiding some previously established experimental constraints. We focus on the phenomenology of the described scenario of the Stueckelberg portal in the lepton sector and analyze the contribution of the dark sector fields A ′ A^{\prime} to the anomalous magnetic moment of muon ( g − 2 ) μ (g-2)_{\mu} , lepton flavor violating decays l i → l k ​ γ l_{i}\to l_{k}\gamma and μ − e \mu-e conversion in nuclei. We obtain limits on the model parameters from the existing data on the corresponding observables.

Introduction

The idea of the dark sector (DS) of the Universe, existing almost independently of
the Standard Model (SM) sector, has attracted growing interest in recent years.
Originally, DS was thought to be populated by only one dark species, necessary to make up
for the lack of matter in the Universe with dark matter (DM).
In particular, extensions of DS were motivated by the popular scenario of light sub-GeV DM.
It was realized that in this case a dark boson, known as the dark photon, would need
to be introduced to prevent the Universe from overclosing. An extended DS can have not
only cosmological, but also interesting phenomenological consequences.
This DS physics beyond the SM can manifest itself in the phenomena observable experimentally
(for a status report see, e.g., Ref.

II
Theoretical setup

We consider an extension the SM with a
U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
gauge invariant dark sector described by the
Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm SM+DS}={\cal L}_{\rm SM}+{\cal L}_{\rm DS}+{\cal L}_{\rm port}\,,

(1)

where
{\cal L}_{\rm SM}
and
{\cal L}_{\rm DS}
are the SM and dark sector Lagrangians.
Communication between these two sectors takes place through a portal interactions
{\cal L}_{\rm port}
.

We suppose that the DS, blind to the SM interactions,
is populated with Dirac fermions
\chi_{i}
charged under
U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
.
The lightest of which is stable and plays the role of dark matter.
By definition, all the SM fields are neutral with respect to this group.
The gauge boson,
A^{\prime}
, of the dark sector
U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
group is called the dark photon.
In the conventional dark photon scenario
A^{\prime}
acquires its mass
m_{A^{\prime}}
from spontaneous breaking
of the
U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
group.
In contrast, in our approach its mass is a gauge-invariant quantity generated by
the Stueckelberg mechanism. This requires the introduction of a scalar Stueckelberg field

\sigma
, which plays a role of Goldstone boson in the generation of mass of the dark photon.
The
U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
gauge invariant Stueckelberg Lagrangian
{\cal L}_{\rm DS}

with one specie of Dark fermion,
\chi
, reads

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm DS}=-\frac{1}{4}\,{A}_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}{A}^{\prime\mu\nu}\,+\,\frac{1}{2}D_{\mu}\sigma D^{\mu}\sigma\,+\,\bar{\chi}\,(i\not\!\!D_{\chi}-m_{\chi})\,\chi\,,

(2)

where, as usual,
A_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{\prime}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{\prime}
and

\Big(iD_{\chi}\Big)_{\mu}=i\partial_{\mu}-g_{A^{\prime}}A^{\prime}_{\mu}

is the conventional covariant derivative.

The Stueckelberg covariant derivative is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}{\bf\sigma}=\partial_{\mu}{\bf\sigma}-m_{A^{\prime}}A^{\prime}_{\mu}

(3)

The
U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
-symmetry is realized on the dark sector fields according to the transformations

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{\mu}+\frac{i}{g_{A^{\prime}}}\,\partial_{\mu}\Omega_{A^{\prime}}\,\Omega^{-1}_{A^{\prime}}\,,\qquad A_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}\,\to\,A_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\sigma-\frac{m_{A^{\prime}}}{g_{A^{\prime}}}\theta_{A^{\prime}}\,,\qquad\partial_{\mu}\sigma\,\to\,\partial_{\mu}\sigma+\frac{im_{A^{\prime}}}{g_{A^{\prime}}}\,\partial_{\mu}\Omega_{A^{\prime}}\,\Omega^{-1}_{A^{\prime}}\,,\qquad D_{\mu}\sigma\to D_{\mu}\sigma\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\chi_{D}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\Omega_{A^{\prime}}\,\chi_{D}\,,\qquad i\not\!\!D_{\chi_{D}}\,\chi_{D}\,\to\,\Omega_{A^{\prime}}\,i\not\!\!D_{\chi_{D}}\,\chi_{D}\,,

(6)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{A^{\prime}}(x)=\exp\Big[i{\theta}_{A^{\prime}}(x)\Big]\,.

(7)

As seen from (
5
), the
\sigma

is an axionlike field shift transformed under the
U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
.
Quantization of the
U(1)_{A^{\prime}}
dark sector requires adding to the Lagrangian
(
2
) a gauge-fixing term
Kors:2005uz
. We choose it in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{gf}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\xi}\left(\partial_{\mu}A^{\prime\mu}+\xi m_{A^{\prime}}\sigma\right)^{2},

(8)

where
\xi
is a gauge parameter.
Then the dark sector Lagrangian can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\prime}_{\rm DS}={\cal L}_{\rm DS}+\mathcal{L}_{gf}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\,{A}_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}{A}^{\prime\mu\nu}\,+\,\frac{m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}A^{\prime}_{\mu}A^{\prime\mu}\,+\,\bar{\chi}\,(i\not\!\!D_{\chi}-m_{\chi})\,\chi-\frac{1}{2\xi}\left(\partial_{\mu}A^{\prime\mu}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\sigma\partial^{\mu}\sigma-\xi\frac{m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}\sigma^{2}\,.

In this gauge the Stueckelberg field is decoupled from other fields, making the theory manifestly unitary and renormalizable. Note that the mass of the
\sigma
field is proportional to the gauge parameter
\xi
, signaling that this field is unphysical.

In the gauge (
8
) the dark photon propagator takes the form

\displaystyle D_{A}^{\mu\nu}(k;\xi)=\frac{1}{k^{2}-m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}\,\biggl[g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{k^{\mu}k^{\nu}\,(1-\xi)}{k^{2}-\xi m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}}\biggr]\,.

(9)

Let us turn to the SM-DS portals
{\cal L}_{\rm port}
possible in the present model.
The well-known example of these is the generic renormalizable portal given by
kinetic mixing of the dark and the SM photons,
A-A^{\prime}
, according to

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm mix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon_{A}}{2}\,F_{\mu\nu}A^{\prime\mu\nu}\,,

(10)

where
\epsilon_{A}
is the mixing parameter. It has a rather particular phenomenology,
which we comment on latter.

In the Stueckelberg framework there exists another specific portal, which relies on the

SU_{c}(3)\times SU_{L}(2)\times U_{Y}(1)\times U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
gauge-invariant effective operator

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm int}=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\,D_{\mu}\sigma\,\sum_{ij}\,\biggl[\bar{Q}^{i}\chi^{ij}\gamma^{\mu}Q^{j}\,+\,\bar{u}_{R}^{i}\chi^{ij}_{u}\gamma^{\mu}u_{R}^{j}\,+\,\bar{d}_{R}^{i}\chi^{ij}_{d}\gamma^{\mu}d_{R}^{j}\,+\,\bar{L}^{im}\kappa^{ij}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}L^{jm}\,+\,\bar{\ell}_{R}^{i}\kappa^{ij}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}\ell_{R}^{j}\biggr]\,.

(11)

The fields in this expression belong to the following representations of the

SU_{c}(3)\times SU_{L}(2)\times U_{Y}(1)\times U_{A^{\prime}}(1)
group

Q(3,2;1/3;0)
,
u_{R}(3,1;4/3;0)
,
d_{R}(3,1;-2/3;0)
,
L(1,2;-1;0)
,
\ell_{R}(1,1;-2;0)
.
The parameter
\Lambda
is the characteristic scale of this effective operator, defining
when it opens up in terms of renormalizable interactions of an UV completion.
The parameters
\chi^{ij}
and
\kappa^{ij}
form

3\otimes 3
Hermitian matrices leading to the neutral current flavor violation both
in quark and lepton sectors. In the present work we focus only on the lepton sector and make an
ad hoc assumption
\chi=\chi_{u}=\chi_{d}=0
.

Let us look closely at the effective operator (
11
).
At first glance, it looks a nonrenormalizable operator of dimension 5. However, after
the substitution of the expression (
3
), we find that the gauge-invariant
operator (
11
) generates dimension-4 interactions of dark photon
with the SM fermions
\psi
in the form

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\rm A^{\prime}-\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{\mu}\sum_{ij}\bar{\psi}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\left(g^{V}_{ij}+g^{A}_{ij}\gamma_{5}\right)\psi_{j}\,,

(12)

where vector
g^{V}
and axial-vector
g^{A}
dimensionless couplings are defined as

\displaystyle g_{ij}^{V}=\frac{m_{A^{\prime}}}{\Lambda}v_{ij}

and

\displaystyle g_{ij}^{A}=\frac{m_{A^{\prime}}}{\Lambda}a_{ij}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle v_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\kappa_{R}+\kappa_{L}\right)_{ij}

and

\displaystyle a_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\kappa_{R}-\kappa_{L}\right)_{ij}

As seen, these couplings are linearly scaled with the dark photon mass
m_{A^{\prime}}
,
which is crucial for our analysis of the
A^{\prime}
contribution to the lepton sector observables
and setting limits on the corresponding couplings in function of the intermediate-state mass.

The operator (
11
) also contains the interaction
of the unphysical Goldstone-like field with the SM fermions of the form
j^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\sigma
.
In principle, these interactions should be taken into account in calculations made in
the
R_{\xi}
gauge (
8
) with the arbitrary parameter
\xi
.
To avoid this complication we select from now on the unitary-type gauge

\xi\to\infty
in which, as seen from (
II
),
the
\sigma
becomes infinitely heavy and decouples completely from the observable sector.
Therefore, the only physical interactions generated by (
11
)
in this gauge are due to the renormalizable couplings of the dark photon to
the SM fermions (
12
).

These interactions also absorb the kinetic portal (
10
).
In fact, the latter can be removed from the Lagrangian by the conventional field redefinition
converting its effect to the flavor diagonal vector interactions of the form (
12
).
As usual, we shift the SM photon field

\displaystyle A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}-\epsilon_{A}\,A_{\mu}^{\prime}\,

(14)

and, then, rescale the dark photon field

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{\mu}\to A^{\prime}_{\mu}\,(1-\epsilon_{A}^{2})^{-1/2}\,.

(15)

These redefinitions generate flavor diagonal couplings of the SM fermions

\psi
to the dark photon:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm mix}^{\rm A^{\prime}-\psi}=e\,\epsilon_{A}\,A^{\prime}_{\mu}\,\bar{\psi}_{i}\,\gamma^{\mu}\,T_{Q}\,\psi_{i}\,,

(16)

originating from the kinetic mixing term (
10
).
Here
T_{Q}
is the charge matrix of SM fermions.These interactions feature the characteristic property
of the kinetic portal requiring the
A^{\prime}
couplings to the SM fermions to be proportional
to their electric charges.
It is clear that terms (
16
) are completely absorbed by
redefinition of the flavor diagonal vector couplings
g^{V}_{ii}
in (
12
).

Thus, our model contains the following free parameters
g_{ij}^{V},\ g_{ij}^{A},\ m_{A^{\prime}}

with
g_{ij}^{V,A}=g_{ji}^{V,A}
.
Let us highlight two principal differences between the conventional kinetic portal and
the Stueckelberg portal scenarios of dark sector. First, in the latter case contrary to
the former one the
A^{\prime}
couplings to the SM fermions are not proportional to
the SM fermion electric charges. Second, these couplings are flavor non-diagonal leading
to reach LFV phenomenology.
Note that the first point can significantly affect the conclusions following from the existing
searches of dark photon.
In particular, the conventional
dark photon from the kinetic portal scenario has been strongly constrained from the data of
NA64 experiment at SPS CERN
Banerjee:2016tad
;
Banerjee:2017hhz
. For the Stueckelberg
dark photon these constraints can be significantly relaxed.

A^{\prime}
to
muon anomalous magnetic moment
(g-2)_{\mu}
and LFV decays
l_{i}\to l_{k}\gamma
as well as

\mu-e
conversion in nuclei.

VII
Conclusions

We constructed a phenomenological Lagrangian approach which combines SM and DM sectors
based on the Stueckelberg mechanism for the generation mass of the dark
U_{D}(1)
gauge boson
(or dark photon). The DM sector contains dark photon, dark scalar, and generic dark
fermion fields. Note that the dark scalar generates the mass of the dark photon and plays a role of Goldstone boson in our gauge-invariant formalism. Stueckelberg portal opens new possibilities for
study of phenomenology of BSM physics and can be important for running and planning
experiments at world-wide facilities (e.g.,
for the NA64 Experiment at SPS CERN
Banerjee:2016tad
;
Banerjee:2017hhz
).

We derived the limits on the effective couplings of our Lagrangian using data
on lepton AMMs, LFV lepton decays
l_{i}\to\gamma l_{k}
,
and
\mu-e
conversion. It is known that the latter are very useful because they give
more stringent limits on the couplings of effective Lagrangian.
We also found that the
(g-2)
anomaly cannot be preferably solved within
the Stueckelberg portal scenario by the light dark photon in the framework of conservative
scenario with taking into account lepton universality. However, the simultaneous explanation of these both anomalies becomes possible once we allow approximately one-order of magnitude hierarchy between the flavor diagonal couplings of
A^{\prime}
to electron
g_{ee}
and to muon
g_{\mu\mu}
, which can be treated as moderately unnatural. We mentioned the possible ways for relaxing this tension with the naturalness. We leave a detailed study of these aspects of our model for the future publications.

In the future we plan to study a possible role of the Stueckelberg portal in different LFV processes
including semileptonic decays. We plan include scalar, pseudoscalar dark bosons
into the Stueckelberg portal of DM. We also intend to extended our ideas on non-Abelian scenario
for the dark sector.

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\mu}}{\epsilon}\,\biggl[1+\frac{3}{2}\,\frac{m_{j}^{2}}{m^{2}}-\frac{m_{i}^{2}+m_{k}^{2}+m_{i}m_{k}}{2m^{2}}\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4b;T}+M^{UV;V/A}_{4b;L}\,,

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4b;T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\mu}}{\epsilon}\,\frac{m_{i}\mp 2m_{j}}{m_{i}-m_{k}}\,,

(62)

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4b;L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\mu}}{\epsilon}\,\frac{m_{k}\mp m_{j}}{m_{i}-m_{k}}\biggl[1+\frac{m_{j}^{2}}{m^{2}}-\frac{m_{k}(m_{k}\pm m_{j})}{2m^{2}}\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4c;T}+M^{UV;V/A}_{4c;L}\,,

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4c;T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\mu}}{\epsilon}\,\frac{m_{k}\mp 2m_{j}}{m_{k}-m_{i}}\,,

(63)

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4c;L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\mu}}{\epsilon}\,\frac{m_{i}\mp m_{j}}{m_{k}-m_{i}}\biggl[1+\frac{m_{j}^{2}}{m^{2}}-\frac{m_{i}(m_{i}\pm m_{j})}{2m^{2}}\biggr]\,.

Here
\pm
corresponds to exchange of a
vector or axial particle.

It is easy to show that in case of vector and particle exchange we also get exact
cancellation of the divergences and it occurs separately for transverse and
longitudinal contribution of the propagator of the exchange particle:

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4a}+M^{UV;V/A}_{4b}+M^{UV;V/A}_{4c}=0\,,

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4a;T}+M^{UV;V/A}_{4b;T}+M^{UV;V/A}_{4c;T}=0\,,

(64)

\displaystyle M^{UV;V/A}_{4a;L}+M^{UV;V/A}_{4b;L}+M^{UV;V/A}_{4c;L}=0\,.

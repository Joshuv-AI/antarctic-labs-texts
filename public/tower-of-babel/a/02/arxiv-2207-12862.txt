Noncommutative Instantons in Diverse Dimensions
Richard J. Szabo, Michelangelo Tirelli

Abstract
This is a mini-review about generalized instantons of noncommutative gauge theories in dimensions 4, 6 and 8, with emphasis on their realizations in type II string theory, their geometric interpretations, and their applications to the enumerative geometry of non-compact toric varieties.

1
Introduction

Instantons in Yang-Mills theory on
4
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbbm{R}^{4}
are solutions to (anti-)self-duality equations for gauge fields with suitable boundary conditions at infinity
Belavin:1975fg
. They are labelled by their topological Pontryagin charge, called the instanton number, and are absolute minima of the Yang-Mills action functional.

In the quantum theory, instantons are of particular interest as they provide non-perturbative contributions to the path integral. However, the instanton partition functions are divergent because the instanton moduli spaces are non-compact, due to singularities where instantons shrink to zero size and also due to the non-compactness of the ambient space
\mathbbm{R}^{4}
in which instantons can run away to infinity.
A UV regularization which resolves the small instanton singularities is provided by deforming the equations to instanton equations in noncommutative field theory
Nekrasov:1998ss
. An IR regularization is provided by putting the gauge theory in an
\Omega
-background and regarding the instanton partition function as the corresponding equivariant integral over the instanton moduli space
SWcounting
.

From a geometric point of view, the compactified instanton moduli space is isomorphic to a moduli space of torsion free sheaves on the complex projective plane
\mathbbm{P}^{2}
with suitable characteristic classes and framing conditions
Nakajimabook
. From a physical perspective, instantons arise in type II string theory as D
p
–D
(p{+}4)
-brane bound states in the low energy limit
Moore:1998et
. The noncommutative deformation corresponds to turning on a non-zero constant background
B
-field in the flat
10
-dimensional target spacetime
Seiberg:1999vs
.

Yang-Mills instantons on
\mathbbm{R}^{4}
can be extended to ‘generalized instantons’, which are Euclidean BPS solutions of Yang-Mills theories in higher even dimensions
2n>4
satisfying generalized (anti-)self-duality equations
Corrigan:1982th
. They are defined on spaces of special holonomy and naturally appear in type II string theory, where one is interested in instantons in dimensions up to
8
(
n=4
). The corresponding instanton moduli spaces are related to various problems of interest in enumerative algebraic geometry, which has contributed to the ongoing fruitful exchange of ideas and techniques between physics and mathematics.

It is the purpose of this mini-review to survey these and other interactions between noncommutative geometry, string theory and algebraic geometry. We present both old and recent developments from a unified perspective with emphasis on their commonalities and differences in the various dimensions
4,6,8
. Throughout we highlight the many open problems that remain, with plenty of pointers to the relevant literature for more details and further reading. In Section
2
we review the constructions and geometry of instantons on noncommutative
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
, explaining their realisations in string theory in Section
3
. In Section
4
we explain the computation of equivariant instanton partition functions and their applications to enumerative geometry. In Section
5
we survey developments beyond the case of flat space
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
including noncommutative instantons on orbifolds, on defects, and on general toric Calabi-Yau manifolds.

2
Noncommutative Instantons on
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}

2.1
Generalized Instanton Equations

Let
M_{n}
be a connected oriented Riemannian manifold of even dimension
2n\geq 4
, and let
\ast
be the associated Hodge duality operator acting on differential forms on
M_{n}
. A (
generalised
)
instanton
is a localized finite-action solution to the classical Euclidean field equations of a gauge theory on
M_{n}
. They are solutions to first order
\Sigma
-self-duality equations
8d
;
8ds
;
Popov:2010rf

\displaystyle\Sigma\wedge F=\ast\,F

(1)

for a
\mathsf{U}(r)
-connection
1
-form
A
with curvature
2
-form
F=\mathrm{d}A+A\,\wedge\,A
. Here
\Sigma
is a differential form of degree
2n-4
which is taken to be a singlet of the holonomy group
\mathsf{H}\subset\mathsf{SO}(2n)
, a maximal subgroup of the structure group of the frame bundle of
M_{n}
which is preserved by the equations (
1
).

If
\Sigma
is closed,
\mathrm{d}\Sigma=0
, then the instanton solutions of (
1
) are labelled by topological numbers corresponding to the Chern classes of the associated gauge bundle
\mathcal{E}\rightarrow M_{n}
; in particular, the
n
-th Chern class
{\rm ch}_{n}(\mathcal{E})
determines the instanton charge
k\in\mathbbm{Z}
. The Bianchi identity
\mathrm{d}_{A}F=\mathrm{d}F+A\wedge F=0
together with (
1
) imply

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{A}\ast F=\mathrm{d}_{A}(\Sigma\wedge F)=\Sigma\wedge\mathrm{d}_{A}F=0\ .

(2)

In this case every solution of the instanton equations is a solution of the second order Yang-Mills equations, i.e. an extremum of the Yang-Mills action functional
S_{\textrm{\tiny YM}}
on
M_{n}
.

\bm{n=2}
In
4
dimensions,
\Sigma
is a locally constant function on
M_{2}
and the maximal holonomy group is
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{SO}(4)
. Since
\ast^{2}=\mathbbm{1}
when acting on
2
-forms, in this case

\displaystyle F=\ast\,(\Sigma\,F)=\Sigma\ast F=\Sigma^{2}\,F\ ,

(3)

and so
\Sigma=\pm\,1
. We will work with anti-self-dual instantons
inst
;
Vandoren:2008xg
, satisfying
\ast\,F=-F
.

\bm{n=3}
In
6
dimensions,
\Sigma
is a closed
2
-form and the only allowed holonomy groups are
\mathsf{SO}(4){\times}\mathsf{SO}(2)
and
\mathsf{U}(3)

Matrix
. The first choice corresponds to the case where
M_{3}
is the direct product of a
4
-manifold and a Riemann surface. We will assume that
\Sigma
is non-degenerate and defines a Kähler structure
\omega
on
M_{3}
. Then
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{U}(3)
.

\bm{n=4}
In
8
dimensions,
\Sigma
is a closed
4
-form and the maximal holonomy group is
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{Spin}(7)\subset\mathsf{SO}(8)

Matrix
;
Ward:1983zm
. We will take
\Sigma
to be a
\mathsf{Spin}(7)
-structure on
M_{4}
, which in a local chart is given by the Cayley
4
-form
spin
. In this case the self-adjoint operator
\ast\circ(\Sigma\wedge-)
acting on
2
-forms has eigenvalues
\lambda=-3,1
. We will take
\lambda=1
.

2.2
Instanton Singularities

Derrick’s Theorem
Derrick:1964ww
forbids localized solutions on
M_{n}\!=\!\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
with finite Yang-Mills action when
n>2
: If
A=A_{\mu}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}
is a localized solution of the Yang-Mills equations, we can deform the field
A
by rescaling the coordinates as
x\rightarrow\lambda\,x
for
\lambda\in\mathbbm{R}_{>0}
. Then the Yang-Mills functional
S_{\textrm{\tiny YM}}
changes to
S_{\textrm{\tiny YM}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{4-2n}\,S_{\textrm{\tiny YM}}
with
S^{\prime}_{\textrm{\tiny YM}}(1)=(4-2n)\,S_{\textrm{\tiny YM}}<0
, and hence
A
is no longer a localized solution.
It follows that the moduli space of solutions to the generalized instanton equations (
1
) is empty.

This problem is cured by deforming the equations (
1
) to noncommutative instanton equations. Since the theory is no longer formulated on flat Euclidean space, Derrick’s Theorem does not apply. The coordinates cannot be rescaled without affecting the Moyal-Weyl commutation relations
[x^{\mu},x^{\nu}]=\mathrm{i}\,\theta^{\mu\nu}
.

However, even in
4
dimensions, we have to contend with the small instanton UV singularities. For example, let
\eta^{a}_{\mu\nu}
for
a=1,2,3
be the ’t Hooft symbols and
\sigma_{a}
the generators of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(2)
. An
\mathsf{SU}(2)
instanton solution
A=A^{a}\otimes\sigma_{a}
on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
is given by the celebrated family of BPST instantons
tHooft:1976snw

\displaystyle A^{a}=2\,\frac{\eta_{\mu\nu}^{a}\,(x-x_{0})^{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}}{|x-x_{0}|^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\ ,

(4)

which is parametrized by the center of mass modulus
x_{0}\in\mathbbm{R}^{4}
and the instanton scale
\lambda\in\mathbbm{R}_{>0}
. This solution becomes singular when its scale is shrunk to zero size (
\lambda\rightarrow 0
).

These singularities are again resolved by considering noncommutative instantons. This introduces a minimum size
\xi
for the instantons, and so it resolves the moduli space singularities as instantons cannot reach the singularity where their size vanishes. Because of the additional length scale
\xi
, there is no commutative equivalent of this resolution of singularities.

Finally, for any
n\geq 2
, we still have to contend with the non-compact IR singularities which arise from instantons that run away to infinity in
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
. These are resolved by coupling the gauge theory to Nekrasov’s supergravity
\Omega
-background
SWcounting
.

2.3
Hermitian Yang-Mills Equations

The noncommutative deformation is equivalent to the choice of a complex structure
J
on
M_{n}=\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
, which induces a Poisson bivector
\theta=\xi\,\omega^{-1}
that we wish to quantize, where
\xi\in\mathbbm{R}_{>0}
and
\omega
is the corresponding Kähler
(1,1)
-form of
M_{n}
. The instanton equations are now generally defined on the Kähler
n
-fold
(M_{n},\omega,J)
, and under suitable conditions they are equivalent to the Hermitian Yang-Mills equations

\displaystyle F^{0,2}=0\quad,\quad\omega^{n-1}\wedge F^{1,1}=\mu\ \omega^{n}\,\mathbbm{1}_{r}\ ,

(5)

where
F=F^{2,0}+F^{1,1}+F^{0,2}
is the decomposition of the field strength in the standard basis of
(1,0)
- and
(0,1)
-forms, and
\mu
is a constant which is non-zero only when the gauge bundle
\mathcal{E}
has non-trivial first Chern class. The first equation turns
\mathcal{E}
into a holomorphic bundle, while the second equation can be regarded as a stability condition.

We will usually consider gauge fields with vanishing first Chern form
\mathrm{ch}_{1}(\mathcal{E})=0
, or alternatively we may consider only the traceless part of the field strength tensor
F
, and hence assume
\mu=0
. In this case the equations (
5
) coincide with the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau (DUY) equations
Donaldson:1985zz
;
UhlenbeckYau
which describe stable holomorphic vector bundles over
M_{n}
with finite characteristic classes.

\bm{n=2}
In
4
dimensions, the introduction of a complex structure
J
breaks the holonomy group to
\mathsf{U}(2)\subset\mathsf{SO}(4)
, and the anti-self-duality equations
\ast\,F=-F
are equivalent to the Hermitian Yang-Mills equations (
5
) for
n=2
and
\mu=0
.

\bm{n=3}
In
6
dimensions, the holonomy group is similarly
\mathsf{U}(3)\subset\mathsf{SO}(6)
, and the generalized instanton equations
\omega\wedge F=\ast\,F
are again equivalent to (
5
) for
n=3
and
\mu=0
.

\bm{n=4}
In
8
dimensions,
J
defines a Calabi-Yau structure and reduces the holonomy to
\mathsf{SU}(4)\subset\mathsf{Spin}(7)
. A compatible
\mathsf{Spin}(7)
-structure is determined by
\Sigma=\frac{1}{2}\,\omega\wedge\omega-{\rm Re}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is the non-degenerate holomorphic
4
-form associated to the
\mathsf{SU}(4)
-structure. The complex conjugate of
\Omega
defines an antilinear involution
\ast_{\Omega}
acting on
(0,2)
-forms on
M_{4}
, which enables one to introduce the anti-self-dual part
F_{-}^{0,2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big(F^{0,2}-\ast_{\Omega}\,F^{0,2}\big)

of
F^{0,2}
in the
-1
eigenspace of
\ast_{\Omega}

Donaldson:1996kp
;
Donaldson:2009yq
. The
\mathsf{Spin}(7)
-instanton equation
\ast\,(\Sigma\wedge F)=F
can be reduced along the inclusion
\mathsf{SU}(4)\subset\mathsf{Spin}(7)
to

\displaystyle F_{-}^{0,2}=0\quad,\quad\omega\wedge\omega\wedge\omega\wedge F^{1,1}=0\ .

(6)

Any solution of the Hermitian Yang-Mills equations (
5
) for
n=4
and
\mu=0
is automatically a solution of the
\mathsf{Spin}(7)
-instanton equations (
6
), but not conversely unless the topological number
\int_{M_{4}}\,\Omega\wedge\mathrm{Tr}\big(F^{0,2}\wedge F^{0,2}\big)
vanishes
inprep
.

2.4
Noncommutative Gauge Theory

The noncommutative deformation of the generalized instanton equations on the Kähler manifold
M_{n}=\mathbbm{R}^{2n}\simeq\mathbbm{C}^{n}
uses Berezin-Toeplitz quantization with the Poisson structure
\theta=\xi\,\omega^{-1}
. Starting from the trivial prequantum line bundle
L\rightarrow M_{n}
with holomorphic polarization, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=H^{0}(M_{n},L)=\ker\big(\bar{\partial}_{\alpha}\big)
is the space of holomorphic sections of
L
, where
\omega=\xi\,\mathrm{d}\alpha
. Holomorphic functions on
M_{n}
are naturally realized as operators on
\mathcal{H}
.

In particular, the Toeplitz quantization map sends the local complex coordinates
\{z_{a},\bar{z}_{\bar{a}}\}_{a=1}^{n}
of
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
to operators with the commutation relations

\displaystyle[z_{a},z_{b}]=0=[\bar{z}_{\bar{a}},\bar{z}_{\bar{b}}]\ ,\ [z_{a},\bar{z}_{\bar{b}}]=\xi\,\delta_{a\bar{b}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\ .

(7)

By the Stone-von Neumann theorem, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is isomorphic to the unique irreducible representation of this algebra, the Fock module

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1\cdots n}&=\mathbbm{C}[\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}]\,|{\vec{0}}\,\rangle\\[4.0pt]
&=\mbox{$\bigoplus\limits_{{\vec{n}}\in\mathbbm{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}$}\,\mathbbm{C}\,|{\vec{n}}\rangle\ ,\end{split}

(8)

where the vacuum vector
|\vec{0}\,\rangle
is a fixed section in
\ker(\bar{\partial}_{\alpha})
and
\vec{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{n})
. The operators
\bar{z}_{\bar{a}}
and
z_{a}
act on
\mathcal{H}
as creation and annihilation operators, respectively,
and so
\mathcal{H}
is spanned by the eigenstates
|\vec{n}\,\rangle
of the number operators
N_{a}=\frac{1}{\xi}\,\bar{z}_{\bar{a}}\,z_{a}
with eigenvalues
n_{a}
for
a=1,\dots,n
.

Using the Segal-Bargmann representation
\bar{z}_{\bar{a}}=-\xi\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{a}}
of the algebra (
7
), the
\mathsf{U}(r)
instanton equations (
5
) (with
\mu=0
) and (
6
) can now be rewritten as equations for infinite-dimensional matrices
Z_{a}=\mathbbm{1}_{r}\otimes\bar{z}_{\bar{a}}+\mathrm{i}\,\xi\,(A_{2a-1}+\mathrm{i}\,A_{2a})
acting on
\mathcal{H}^{r}=\mathbbm{C}^{r}\otimes\mathcal{H}
. They are algebraic operator equations for the noncommutative fields given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}[Z_{a},Z_{b}]&=\left\{\begin{matrix}0\ ,&n=2,3\\[5.0pt]
\tfrac{1}{2}\,\epsilon_{ab\bar{c}\bar{d}}\,\big[Z^{\dagger}_{\bar{c}},Z^{\dagger}_{\bar{d}}\big]\ ,&n=4\end{matrix}\right.\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mbox{$\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n}$}&\,\big[Z_{\bar{a}}^{\dagger},Z_{a}\big]=-n\,\xi\,\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}\ .\end{split}

(9)

These equations are much easier to analyse and solve than the original first order partial differential equations.

2.5
Explicit Solutions

The simplest solution to the equations (
9
) is the vacuum solution

\displaystyle Z_{a}=\mathbbm{1}_{r}\otimes\bar{z}_{\bar{a}}

(10)

with vanishing gauge connection. More general solutions can be found using the solution generating technique which applies partial gauge transformations to the vacuum solution (
10

Hashimoto:2000kq

Gross:2000ss

Kraus:2001xt

Nekrasov:2002kc

NS-Type Instantons.
The Nekrasov-Schwarz (NS) type instantons include the instanton solutions that were originally found in
Nekrasov:1998ss
;
Furuuchi:1999kv
via a noncommutative version of the ADHM construction. Focusing momentarily on the rank
1
case
r=1
, we fix an integer
\ell>0
and a partial isometry
S_{\ell}
which projects out the finite-dimensional subspace of states
|\vec{n}\,\rangle
with
|\vec{n}|:=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\,n_{a}<\ell
from the Fock space
\mathcal{H}
. Explicitly

\displaystyle S_{\ell}^{\dagger}\,S_{\ell}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}-\varPi_{\ell}\quad,\quad S_{\ell}\,S_{\ell}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\ ,

(11)

with
\varPi_{\ell}=\sum_{|\vec{n}|<\ell}\,|\vec{n}\rangle\langle\vec{n}|
.
Then the NS-type instantons are given by

\displaystyle Z_{a}=S_{\ell}\,\bar{z}_{\bar{a}}\,f_{\ell}(N)\,S_{\ell}^{\dagger}\ ,

(12)

where
N=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\,N_{a}
is the total number operator,
and
f_{\ell}
is a real function of
N
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{\ell}(N)&=\sqrt{1-\tfrac{\ell\,(\ell+1)\cdots(\ell+n-1)}{(N+1)\cdots(N+n)}}\ (\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}-\varPi_{\ell})\ .\end{split}

(13)

These solutions have finite Yang-Mills action and topological charge

\displaystyle\begin{split}k=\mathrm{ch}_{n}(\mathcal{E})&=\frac{(-\pi\,\xi)^{n}}{n!}\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\Big(\frac{F}{2\pi}\Big)^{n}\\[4.0pt]
&=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}(\varPi_{\ell})=\binom{\ell+n-1}{\ell-1}\ ,\end{split}

(14)

which is the number of states removed by the shift operator
S_{\ell}
. They correspond to localized instantons sitting outside a characteristic radius
\sqrt{\ell\,\xi}
from the origin in
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
. This demonstrates how the noncommutative deformation resolves the singular configuration space of commutative instantons which would sit on top of each other. Higher rank analogues of these solutions are described in
Popov:2005ik
;
Ivanova:2006ek
;
Cirafici:2008sn
.

ABS Construction.
By relaxing the conditions (
11
) one can generate more general solutions of higher rank
r>1
with instanton charge
k
by appealing to the Toeplitz algebra of the space
\mathcal{H}^{r}=\mathbbm{C}^{r}\otimes\mathcal{H}
. A Toeplitz operator is a partial isometry
T_{k}:\mathcal{H}^{r}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{r}
with trivial kernel and
k
-dimensional cokernel, that is, it obeys

\displaystyle T_{k}^{\dagger}\,T_{k}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}\quad,\quad T_{k}\,T_{k}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}-P_{k}\ ,

(15)

where
P_{k}
is a rank
k
projector on the space
\mathcal{H}^{r}
. Then the basic shift operator soliton is given by
Z_{a}=T_{k}\,(\mathbbm{1}_{r}\otimes\bar{z}_{\bar{a}})\,T_{k}^{\dagger}
.

An explicit realization of these Toeplitz operators for rank
r=2^{n-1}
is given by the noncommutative ABS construction
Harvey:2000te
. We use real polarization and set
T_{k}=(T)^{k}
with

\displaystyle T^{\dagger}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{(\gamma\cdot x)\,(\gamma\cdot x)^{\dagger}}}\,\gamma\cdot x\ ,

(16)

where
\gamma\cdot x=\gamma_{\mu}\,x^{\mu}
and the
r{\times}r
matrices
\gamma_{\mu}
obey anticommutation relations such that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu}={\small\bigg(\begin{matrix}0&-\gamma_{\mu}\\
\gamma_{\mu}^{\dagger}&0\end{matrix}}\normalsize\bigg)

(17)

generate the Clifford algebra of the inner product space
(\mathbbm{R}^{2n},\delta_{\mu\nu})
. The operator
T
has trivial kernel, while
T^{\dagger}
has a
1
-dimensional kernel which is spanned by the vector
|\alpha\rangle\otimes|\vec{0}\,\rangle
, where
|\alpha\rangle
denotes the lowest weight spinor of
\mathsf{SO}(2n)
. The ABS construction (
16
) can be straightforwardly generalized in order to introduce
2\,n\,k
real moduli into the solution which specify the locations of the
k
noncommutative instantons on
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}

Lechtenfeld:2003cq
.

2.6
Moduli Spaces of Instantons

The explicit solutions obtained in Section
2.5
elucidate the geometric description of the instanton moduli spaces
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
for
n=2,3,4
, that is, the (suitably compactified) quotient space of charge
k
solutions to the rank
r
instanton equations (
5
) (with
\mu=0
) and (
6
) on
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
by gauge transformations. Focusing momentarily on the rank
1
case, there is a correspondece between
\mathsf{U}(1)
instantons of charge
k
on noncommutative
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
and ideals
\mathcal{I}
of codimension
k
in the polynomial ring
\mathbbm{C}[w_{1},\dots,w_{n}]
: In all of the solutions discussed in Section
2.5
, the partial isometries bijectively identify the Fock space
\mathcal{H}
with a subspace

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathcal{I}[\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}]|\vec{0}\,\rangle\ ,

(18)

where
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal of codimension
k
formed by the polynomials
f\in\mathbbm{C}[w_{1},\dots,w_{n}]
satisfying

\displaystyle P_{k}f(\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n})|\vec{0}\,\rangle=0\ .

(19)

These ideals parametrize the Hilbert scheme
{\sf Hilb}^{k}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
of
k
points in
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
, and hence there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{k,1}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})\,\simeq\,{\sf Hilb}^{k}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})\ .

(20)

Geometrically,
{\sf Hilb}^{k}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
parametrizes
0
-dimensional subschemes
Z\subset\mathbbm{C}^{n}
with Hilbert-Poincaré polynomial
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})=k
. For
n=2
and generic
k
, the Hilbert scheme
{\sf Hilb}^{k}(\mathbbm{C}^{2})
is a smooth manifold of complicated topology
Nakajimabook
.
For
n=3,4
and
k>3
, however, the Hilbert scheme is not smooth: it is not even a manifold, as in general it has several different branches of varying dimension.

In the general higher rank case
r>1
, we note that the vacuum solution (
10
) in noncommutative gauge theory is a realization of the free coherent sheaf of sections of the trivial rank
r
holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r}
on
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
. Then each choice of partial isometry with the identifications (
18
) and (
19
) realises a
0
-dimensional quotient
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}
of
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r}
with
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})=k
. These parametrize the Quot scheme
{\sf Quot}^{k}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r})
of
k
points in
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
, and hence there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})\,\simeq\,{\sf Quot}^{k}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r})\ .

(21)

For
r=1
this is the same as the Hilbert scheme:
{\sf Quot}^{k}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n}}(\mathcal{O})={\sf Hilb}^{k}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
.

The same moduli space
{\sf Quot}^{k}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r})
also parametrizes framed torsion free sheaves

on projective space
{\mathbbm{P}}^{n}
of rank
r
and
\mathrm{ch}_{n}(\mathcal{E})=k

Cazzaniga:2020xru
: Any such sheaf sits in a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbm{P}}^{n}}^{\oplus r}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{Z}\longrightarrow 0\ ,

(22)

where
\mathcal{S}_{Z}=\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
is a coherent sheaf supported on a set of
k
points
Z\subset\mathbbm{C}^{n}
under the canonical open embedding
\iota:\mathbbm{C}^{n}\hookrightarrow{\mathbbm{P}}^{n}
. For
n=2
, these are the celebrated Gieseker-Nakajima moduli spaces
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{2})
which parametrize charge
k
noncommutative
\mathsf{U}(r)
instantons on
\mathbbm{R}^{4}

Nekrasov:1998ss
, framed rank
r
torsion free sheaves
\mathcal{E}
on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{2}
with
{\rm ch}_{2}(\mathcal{E})=k

Nakajimabook

3
Noncommutative Instantons from String Theory

3.1
Cohomological Gauge Theory

The Hermitian Yang-Mills equations have a long history in string theory dating back to the mid-1980s, where they naturally appear as the conditions for survival of at least one unbroken supersymmetry in the low-energy effective field theory in
d\leq 4
dimensions. They have acquired renewed interest due to their relevance in the description of D-branes in type IIA string theory. The low-energy effective dynamics on a stack of
r
D-branes can be obtained by dimensional reduction from
10
-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory with gauge group
\mathsf{U}(r)
, which after topological twisting become cohomological field theories
Blau:1997pp
. Generalized instanton equations then appear as the BRST fixed point loci on which these theories localize.

We start by compactifying type IIA string theory on

\displaystyle\mathbbm{R}^{1,9-2n}\times M_{n}\ ,

(23)

where
M_{n}
is a Kähler
n
-fold, and consider the (twisted) low-energy effective field theories on
r
D
(2n)
-branes wrapping the space
M_{n}

Bershadsky:1995qy
. The gauge theory is defined on
M_{n}
, while holomorphic coordinates of
\mathbbm{R}^{1,9-2n}
become complex scalar fields of the theory. The original Lorentz symmetry
\mathsf{SO}(1,9)
in ten dimensions is broken generically to
\mathsf{SO}(1,9-2n)\times\mathsf{U}(n)
. In the bosonic sector, the non-compact R-symmetry
\mathsf{SO}(1,9-2n)
acts only on the scalars.

Instantons of charge
k=\mathrm{ch}_{n}(\mathcal{E})
in this theory are realised as supersymmetric bound states of
k
D0-branes inside the D
(2n)
-branes
Szabo:2004uy
;
Polchinski:1998rr
. More generally, the Chern forms
\mathrm{ch}_{p}(\mathcal{E})
couple to Ramond-Ramond
(2n{-}2p)
-form fields on
M_{n}
with
1\leq p\leq n
and represent Pontryagin charges of D
(2n{-}2p)
–D
(2n)
bound states.

\bm{n=2}
We reduce
10
-dimensional supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory on a
4
-dimensional Kähler manifold
(M_{2},\omega)
with
\mathsf{U}(2)
holonomy. After the Vafa-Witten twist
Vafa:1994tf
, the bosonic field content consists of a gauge field
A
, a self-dual
2
-form
B^{+}
which is an auxiliary field, and a complex Higgs field
\Phi
, all valued in the adjoint representation of the gauge group
\mathsf{U}(r)
. The bosonic action is minimized by the equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}F^{+}+\tfrac{1}{8}\,[B^{+}\,{\scriptstyle{\mathbf{\times}}}\,B^{+}]+\tfrac{1}{2}\,[B^{+},\Phi]&=0\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mathrm{d}^{\ast}_{A}B^{+}+\mathrm{d}_{A}\Phi&=0\ ,\end{split}

(24)

where
F^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\,(F+\ast\,F)
is the self-dual part of the curvature
2
-form. We will only consider solutions of the partial differential equations (
24
) which have
B^{+}=0
, yielding
4
-dimensional instantons, together with parallel Higgs fields that will play an important role below.

\bm{n=3}
We similarly reduce the theory in ten dimensions on a
6
-dimensional Kähler manifold
(M_{3},\omega)
with
\mathsf{U}(3)
holonomy. After twisting, the bosonic spectrum consists of a gauge field
A
, a
(3,0)
-form
\varrho
and a complex Higgs field
\Phi
, all valued in the adjoint representation of
\mathsf{U}(r)
. The twisted gauge theory corresponds to the moduli problem associated with the field equations

;
Iqbal:2003ds

\displaystyle\begin{split}F^{2,0}+\bar{\partial}_{A}^{\ast}\,\varrho&=0\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\omega\wedge\omega\wedge F^{1,1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,[\varrho,\bar{\varrho}]&=\mu\ \omega\wedge\omega\wedge\omega\ \mathbbm{1}_{r}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mathrm{d}_{A}\Phi&=0\ .\end{split}

(25)

We will always assume
\mu=0
, i.e.
\mathrm{ch}_{1}(\mathcal{E})=0
, which in the IIA string picture excludes D
4
–D
6
bound states. If we further restrict to
\mathsf{SU}(3)
holonomy, the Calabi-Yau structure enables us to set
\varrho=0
because of uniqueness of the holomorphic
3
-form in that case. Then the first two equations of (
25

\bm{n=4}
Dimensional reduction of
10
-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory on a manifold
M_{4}
of
\mathsf{Spin}(7)
-holonomy is equivalent to a topological twist of the resulting
8
-dimensional supersymmetric gauge theory
Baulieu:1997jx
. The bosonic field content of the reduced theory on the Kähler
4
-fold
(M_{4},\omega)
, with holonomy
\mathsf{SU}(4)\subset\mathsf{Spin}(7)
, consists of a complex gauge field
A
and adjoint Higgs field
\Phi
, and the path integral localizes onto the moduli space of solutions of the equations
inprep

\displaystyle\begin{split}F^{0,2}_{-}&=0\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\omega\wedge\omega\wedge\omega\wedge F^{1,1}&=\mu\ \omega\wedge\omega\wedge\omega\wedge\omega\ \mathbbm{1}_{r}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mathrm{d}_{A}\Phi&=0\ .\end{split}

(26)

Setting
\mu=0
amounts to excluding D
6
–D
8
bound states, in which case the first two equations coincide with the
\mathsf{Spin}(7)
-instanton equations (
6
). We may further exclude D
4
–D
8
bound states by restricting to solutions which have vanishing charge
\int_{M_{4}}\,\Omega\wedge\mathrm{Tr}\big(F^{0,2}\wedge F^{0,2}\big)=0
, in which case (

Noncommutative gauge theories arise from the dynamics of open strings on D-branes in the presence of a large Kalb-Ramond field on
M_{n}=\mathbbm{R}^{2n}

, which allows for a unified treatment of worldvolume theories of D-branes of various dimensions

;

\displaystyle[Z_{a},\Phi]=0=[Z_{\bar{a}}^{\dagger},\Phi]\ ,

(27)

3.2
ADHM Quiver Matrix Model

The equations (
9
) for
\mathsf{U}(r)
noncommutative instantons of charge
k
describe the low-energy interactions of
k
D
0
-branes with
r
D
(2n)
-branes in type IIA string theory in a large Kalb-Ramond field, from the perspective of the worldvolume gauge theory on the D
(2n)
-branes. On the other hand, from the perspective of the D
0
-branes, wherein the D
(2n)
-branes are heavy, the bound states are described by (generalised) ADHM equations, deformed by a Fayet-Iliopoulos coupling
\xi
related to the non-zero
B
-field
Nekrasov:1998ss
;
Ohta:2001dh
;
Hiraoka:2002wm
;
Cirafici:2008sn
;
Nekrasov:2017cih
;
Nekrasov:2018xsb
;
Bonelli:2020gku
;
inprep
: introducing noncommutativity corresponds to adding a Fayet-Iliopoulos term
\xi
in string theory. This gives a physical derivation of the ADHM construction of instantons
inst
: the low-energy effective theory on the D
0
-branes is a cohomological matrix model
Moore:1997dj
;
Moore:1998et
, whose field content can be succinctly encoded in a representation of a quiver, called the (generalized) ADHM quiver (see Figure
1
). This perspective in the various dimensionalities is nicely reviewed in
Kanno:2020ybd
.

The BRST fixed points of the cohomological matrix model yield relations for the ADHM quiver, together with D-term conditions. These are called ADHM equations and they can be identified with the vacuum equations of the supersymmetric gauge theory. This gives an algebraic description of the instanton moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
as a moduli space of stable representations of the bounded ADHM quiver, which is the moduli space of vacua of the Higgs branch from the perspective of the D
0
-brane theory.

The case
n=2
is special. In
4
dimensions the ADHM quiver is the double of the framed Jordan quiver, and the choice
\xi=0
is permitted, which corresponds to turning off the
B
-field. This is why commutative instantons exist on
\mathbbm{R}^{4}
. However, in this case the small instanton singularity persists: it corresponds to the transition between the Coulomb and Higgs branches of the D
0
-brane theory

;
Closset:2020scj
, where the D
0
-branes escape from the D
4
-branes.

In marked contrast, choosing
\xi=0
when
n=3,4
would reduce the open string spectrum to only the D
0
–D
0
strings, which does not describe instantons. In other words, instanton solutions on
\mathbbm{R}^{6}
or
\mathbbm{R}^{8}
only exist for
\xi>0
: there are no stable BPS bound states of D
0
–D
(2n)
-branes in
2n
dimensions for
n=3,4
unless a (supersymmetry preserving) Kalb-Ramond field is turned on
Witten:2000mf
.

3.3
Instanton Partition Functions

The local geometry of the moduli space of
\mathsf{U}(r)
instantons on the Kähler
n
-fold
M_{n}
is captured by the
3
-term instanton deformation complex, which for
n=2,3,4
respectively reads as

\displaystyle\Omega^{0}(M_{2},\mathfrak{g})\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{A}}\Omega^{0,1}(M_{2},\mathfrak{g})\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{A}}\Omega^{0,2}(M_{2},\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle\Omega^{0}(M_{3},\mathfrak{g})\xrightarrow{\!\binom{\bar{\partial}_{A}}{0}\!}\begin{matrix}\Omega^{0,1}(M_{3},\mathfrak{g})\\
\oplus\\
\Omega^{0,3}(M_{3},\mathfrak{g})\end{matrix}\xrightarrow{(\bar{\partial}_{A}\ \bar{\partial}_{A}^{\ast})}\Omega^{0,2}(M_{3},\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle\Omega^{0}(M_{4},\mathfrak{g})\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{A}}\Omega^{0,1}(M_{4},\mathfrak{g})\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{A}^{-}}\Omega_{-}^{0,2}(M_{4},\mathfrak{g})

(28)

where
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbbm{C})
. The first arrows are infinitesimal complex gauge transformations, while the second arrows are the linearizations of the first of the equations in (
24
)–(
26
). For
n=2
we set
B^{+}=0
in (
24
), while for
n=4
we defined
\bar{\partial}_{A}^{-}:=P_{\Omega}^{-}\circ\bar{\partial}_{A}

where
P_{\Omega}^{-}=\frac{1}{2}\,(\mathbbm{1}-\ast_{\Omega})
is the projection to the
-1
eigenspace
\Omega^{0,2}_{-}(M_{4},\mathfrak{g})
of the involution
\ast_{\Omega}
from Section
2.3
.

The supersymmetric field theory provides an integral representation of the enumerative moduli problem captured by these elliptic complexes, whose cohomology groups are spanned by the fermionic zero modes of the supermultiplets of the cohomological gauge theory. The contribution to the path integral of a given point in the moduli space is non-zero if the Yukawa interactions saturate these zero modes. The degree
0
cohomology describes infinitesimal automorphisms, which we assume vanishes by restricting to irreducible connections. The degree
1
cohomology describes infinitesimal deformations and represents the tangent space to the moduli space at a fixed holomorphic connection
A
. The degree
2
cohomology parametrizes obstructions to deformations, which is spanned by the antighost zero modes and defines the obstruction bundle over the moduli space. It represents a virtual fundamental class, and its presence means that only the virtual tangent bundle, and not the stable tangent bundle on the coarse moduli space, is well-defined. One may (very roughly) think of the virtual fundamental class as the Poincaré dual of the Euler class of the obstruction bundle; its degree is the difference between the ranks of the tangent and obstruction bundles, called the virtual dimension of the moduli space. The BRST symmetry localizes the path integral (in a fixed topological sector) to an integral over this cycle.

When
M_{n}=\mathbbm{C}^{n}
we can stratify the moduli space into its connected components
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
labelled by the instanton number
k\in\mathbbm{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The Atiyah-Singer index theorem computes their virtual dimensions as Euler characters of the deformation complexes (
28
) with the results

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textrm{vdim}\,\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})=\begin{cases}2\,r\,k\ ,\quad&n=2\\
0\ ,\quad&n=3\\
r\,k\ ,\quad&n=4\end{cases}\ .\end{split}

(29)

Partition functions of the topological field theory on
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
are then given by integrating the Euler class of a ‘matter bundle’
\mathscr{K}_{k,r}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
whose rank equals the virtual dimension of the moduli space. The instanton paritition function is formally the generating function for the Euler characteristics of
\mathscr{K}_{k,r}
and has the form

\displaystyle Z^{r}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n}}(q)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,q^{k}\ \int_{[\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})]^{\rm vir}}\ e(\mathscr{K}_{k,r})\ ,

(30)

where the counting parameter
q
weighs the instanton number and is determined by the UV gauge coupling.

\bm{n=2}
The first complex in (
28
) has trivial obstruction bundle, and so the stable tangent bundle
T\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{2})
is well-defined. The instanton moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{2})
can be constructed as a smooth variety
Nakajimabook
;
Nakajima:2003pg
, and a natural choice for the matter bundle is the tangent bundle itself:
\mathscr{K}_{k,r}=T\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{2})
. This corresponds to the coupling of topologically twisted
\mathcal{N}=2
gauge theory to an adjoint hypermultiplet, and (
30
) defines the Vafa-Witten partition function of
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory in
4
dimensions.

Another canonical choice is the matter bundle
\mathscr{K}_{k,r}=\mathscr{V}_{k,r}\otimes\mathbbm{C}^{2r}
, where
\mathscr{V}_{k,r}
is the ‘natural bundle’ on the instanton moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{2})
. Its fibre over a gauge orbit
[A]
is the vector space
\mathbbm{C}^{k}
that features in the ADHM construction of Section
3.2
, on which the D
0
–D
0
strings act. In this case (
30
) is the instanton partition function of
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory on
\mathbbm{R}^{4}
coupled to
2r
flavours of fundamental fermions.

\bm{n=3}
The second complex in (
28
) defines a perfect obstruction theory
Behrend:1996fey
;
Li
, which has been used to construct a virtual fundamental class
[\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{3})]^{\rm vir}
in
Donaldson:1996kp
;
Thomas:1998uj
. In this case the partition function (
30
) is only defined for the pure

;
Iqbal:2003ds

to get the generating function for the (virtual) ‘volumes’ of the instanton moduli spaces. For further discussion see
Cirafici:2012qc

\bm{n=4}
The self-dual obstruction bundle restricts to real vector bundles
{\sf Ob}_{k,r}^{-}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{4})
. The obstruction theory defined by the third complex in (
28

; more general constructions use derived geometry

. It
depends on a choice

of orientation of

. The stable tangent bundle is not well-defined, but a natural choice of matter bundle on
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{4})
is again obtained from the ADHM description as
\mathscr{K}_{k,r}=\mathscr{V}_{k,r}\otimes\mathbbm{C}^{r}
; it represents the coupling of the pure
\mathcal{N}=2
cohomological gauge theory in

r

inprep

4
Equivariant Gauge Theory

4.1
\Omega
-Deformation

The integrals (
30
) as they stand are not generally well-defined because the non-compactness of the ambient variety
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
generally precludes the mentioned constructions of virtual fundamental cycles to integrate over. A particularly powerful approach uses equivariant localization to define them via equivariant integrals
\oint_{\,[\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})]^{\rm vir}}
over the instanton moduli spaces.

The global symmetry group of the cohomological field theory is

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}_{n}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{U}(r)_{\rm col}\times\mathsf{U}(n)\ ,\ n=2,3\\
\mathsf{U}(r)_{\rm col}\times\mathsf{SU}(4)\times\mathsf{U}(r)_{\rm fla}\ ,\ n=4\end{cases}

(31)

where the colour symmetry
\mathsf{U}(r)_{\rm col}
acts by rotating the framing of the gauge bundle at infinity in
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}
, and the flavour symmetry
\mathsf{U}(r)_{\rm fla}
acts on its vector representation
\mathbbm{C}^{r}
. (When the
4
-dimensional theory is coupled to fundamental matter the symmetry is extended to
\mathsf{G}_{2}\times\mathsf{U}(2r)_{\rm fla}
.) This group can be rotated into its maximal torus

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\mathtt{a}}}\,\times\,\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\varepsilon}}\ ,\quad&n=2,3\\
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\mathtt{a}}}\,\times\,\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\varepsilon}}\,\times\,\mathsf{T}_{\vec{m}}\ ,\quad&n=4\end{cases}

(32)

where we label the torus factors by the corresponding complexified Cartan subalgebra elements
\vec{t}\
: the Coulomb parameters
\vec{\mathtt{a}}=(\mathtt{a}_{1},\dots,\mathtt{a}_{r})
are vacuum expectation values of the complex Higgs field
\Phi
and
\vec{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n})
are the weights for the natural complex scaling action of the
n
-torus
(\mathbbm{C}^{\times})^{n}
on
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
, while
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
are masses of
r
fundamental fermion fields.

We restrict the path integral to gauge field configurations which are
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}\ }
-invariant.
This can be achieved by coupling the gauge theory to Nekrasov’s
\Omega
-background
SWcounting
through a shift of the BRST operator by inner contraction
with the vector field generating the
\mathsf{SO}(2n)
rotational isometries of
\mathbbm{R}^{2n}\simeq\mathbbm{C}^{n}
, restricted to
\mathsf{U}(2)
,
\mathsf{U}(3)
and
\mathsf{SU}(4)
holonomy which preserve the instanton equations on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n=2,3,4
, respectively. The
\Omega
-deformation does not change the instanton equations, but it provides a natural compactification of the moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
by giving
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
a finite equivariant volume
\oint_{\,\mathbbm{C}^{n}}\,1=\frac{1}{e_{\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\varepsilon}}}(T_{\vec{0}}\mathbbm{C}^{n})}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}\cdots\varepsilon_{n}}
.
However, it deforms the equations (
27
) for the Higgs field
\Phi
to

\displaystyle[\Phi,Z_{a}]=\varepsilon_{a}\,Z_{a}\,\quad,\quad[\Phi,Z_{\bar{a}}^{\dagger}]=-\varepsilon_{a}\,Z_{\bar{a}}^{\dagger}\ .

(33)

For the NS-type instantons (
12
) in rank
1
the solutions are

\displaystyle\Phi=S_{\ell}\,\Big(\,\mbox{$\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n}$}\,\varepsilon_{a}\,N_{a}\Big)\,S_{\ell}^{\dagger}+\mathtt{a}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}\ .

(34)

The
\Omega
-deformation localizes integrals over the virtual fundamental cycle
[\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})]^{\rm vir}
onto the isolated
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}\ }
-fixed points of the moduli space, and the equivariant integral
\oint_{\,[\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})]^{\rm vir}}\,e(\mathscr{K}_{k,r})
is the pushforward of
e(\mathscr{K}_{k,r})
to a point in the
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}\ }
-equivariant cohomology of
\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
, whose coefficient ring is
\mathbbm{C}[\vec{t}\ ]
. The virtual localization theorem
Graber
then computes the integrals in (
30
) as a sum over these fixed points, giving the instanton partition function as a formal power series in
q
and the equivariant parameters
\vec{t}\,
:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!Z_{\mathbbm{C}^{n}}^{r}(q;\!\vec{t}\ ):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,q^{k}\ \oint_{[\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})]^{\rm vir}}\ e(\mathscr{K}_{k,r})

(35)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{k}\!\sum_{\vec{p}\in\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})^{\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{e_{\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}}}\big(({\sf Ob}_{k,r})_{\vec{p}}\big)\,e_{\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}}}\big((\mathscr{K}_{k,r})_{\vec{p}}\big)}{e_{\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}}}\big(T_{\vec{p}\,}\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})\big)}\ .

We shall now explain how to evaluate the
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}\ }
-equivariant Euler classes appearing in this expression as bosonic and fermionic fluctuation determinants in the path integral for the noncommutative cohomological gauge theory on the D
(2n)
-branes
Moore:1997dj
, which represent quantum fluctuations around
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}\ }
-invariant instanton solutions.

4.2
Combinatorics

The fixed points of the instanton moduli spaces have a combinatorial significance.
Recall from Section
2.6
that each
\mathsf{U}(1)
noncommutative instanton of charge
k
corresponds to an ideal of codimension
k
in the polynomial ring
\mathbbm{C}[w_{1},\dots,w_{n}]
. A
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\varepsilon}\,}
-fixed point of the instanton moduli space corresponds to a
monomial

\mathcal{I}
, which in turn can be identified with a set of lattice points

;
Okounkov:2006wj

\displaystyle\!\!\!\mathscr{Y}_{\mathcal{I}}=\Big\{(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbbm{Z}_{>0}^{n}\,\Big|\,\mbox{$\prod\limits_{a=1}^{n}\,w_{a}^{b_{a}-1}$}\notin\mathcal{I}\Big\}\ .

(36)

These can be regarded as labelling the corners of
k
unit hypercubes piled on top of each other in the positive
n
-multiant of
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, called an
n
-dimensional Young diagram, generalizing the more familiar (ordinary) Young diagrams which are obtained for
n=2
. They correspond to
n
-dimensional partitions, i.e. non-increasing sequences of non-negative integers
p=\{p_{i_{1}\cdots i_{n-1}}\}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n-1}\geq 1}
: the
n
-dimensional Young diagram
\mathscr{Y}
is recovered from

as
\mathscr{Y}=\{(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbbm{Z}^{n}_{>0}~|~1\leq b_{n}\leq p_{b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}}\}
, where the number of hypercubes in
\mathscr{Y}
is the size
k=|\mathscr{Y}|:=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n-1}\geq 1}\,p_{i_{1}\cdots i_{n-1}}
. For
n=2
these are the familiar partitions (see e.g.
Nakajima:2003pg
;
Szabo:2015wua
), while for
n=3
they are
plane
partitions (see e.g.
Iqbal:2003ds
;
Cirafici:2008sn
;
Szabo:2015wua
) and for
n=4

solid
partitions (see e.g.
Nekrasov:2017cih
;
inprep
). In Figure
2
we illustrate the cases
n=2
and
n=3
.

Figure 2:

12
boxes. Right: A plane partition represented as a

-dimensional Young diagram with

A
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}\ }
-invariant
\mathsf{U}(r)
noncommutative instanton of charge
k
corresponds to a decomposition
\mathcal{I}=\bigoplus_{l=1}^{r}\,\mathcal{I}_{l}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{l}
are monomial ideals of codimension
k_{l}
in the polynomial ring
\mathbbm{C}[w_{1},\dots,w_{n}]
with
\sum_{l=1}^{r}\,k_{l}=k
. These are in one-to-one correspondence coloured
n
-dimensional Young diagrams
\vec{\mathscr{Y}}=(\mathscr{Y}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{Y}_{r})
, where
\mathscr{Y}_{l}
has
k_{l}
hypercubes, or equivalently with coloured
n
-dimensional partitions
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{r})
, where
|\mathscr{Y}_{l}|=k_{l}
.

From the string theory perspective, the
r
D
(2n)
-branes are separated in the Coulomb phase of the gauge theory, and the Coulomb parameters label the positions of neighbouring D
(2n)
-branes relative to one another. The hypercubes of the
n

4.3
Characters

All ingredients needed for the evaluation of the equivariant instanton partition functions (
35

\mathbbm{C}^{n}
can be encoded into a normalized character

. For the contribution of a noncommutative instanton associated to a collection of ideals
\mathcal{I}

as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ch}_{\mathcal{I}}(t):\!

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\prod\limits_{a=1}^{n}$}\,(1-\mathrm{e}^{\,t\,\varepsilon_{a}})\ \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{I}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\,t\,\Phi}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\sum\limits_{l=1}^{r}$}\,\mathrm{e}^{\,t\,\mathtt{a}_{l}}\,\Big(1-\mbox{$\prod\limits_{a=1}^{n}$}\,(1-\mathrm{e}^{\,t\,\varepsilon_{a}})

(37)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\mbox{$\sum\limits_{(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathscr{Y}_{l}}$}\,\mathrm{e}^{\,t\,\sum_{a=1}^{n}\,\varepsilon_{a}\,(b_{a}-1)}\Big)\ .

This computes the top equivariant Chern class of the gauge bundle at a BPS state
\vec{\mathscr{Y}}
through

\displaystyle{\rm ch}^{\mathsf{T}_{\vec{t}}}_{n}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{I}})=\oint_{\mathbbm{C}^{n}}\,\mathsf{Ch}^{(n)}_{\mathcal{I}}=\frac{\mathsf{Ch}^{(n)}_{\mathcal{I}}}{\varepsilon_{1}\cdots\varepsilon_{n}}\ ,

(38)

where

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\mathsf{Ch}_{\mathcal{I}}^{(n)}=\mbox{$\sum\limits_{l=1}^{r}$}\Big(\frac{\mathtt{a}_{l}^{n}}{n!}{-}(-1)^{n}\,\varepsilon_{1}\cdots\varepsilon_{n}\!\!\mbox{$\sum\limits_{(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathscr{Y}_{l}}$}1\Big)

(39)

\prod_{l=1}^{r}\,q^{-\mathtt{a}_{l}^{n}/n!\,\varepsilon_{1}\cdots\varepsilon_{n}}

in (

4.4

Recall that for

The equivariant gauge theory also allows for the definition of the instanton partition functions for a larger class of

5.1
Instantons on Orbifolds

Type II string compactifications on singular spaces such as orbifolds can be well-defined, and we may use these as a first non-trivial generalization of our constructions beyond the flat spaces
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
. A central role is played by orbifolds that admit crepant resolutions, which preserve Calabi-Yau properties and allow computation of the string spectrum on a smooth Calabi-Yau space obtained by blow-ups of the orbifold singularities
Alexandrov:2011va
. The string dynamics can then be considered in ‘orbifold’ or ‘large radius’ phases, which are related by collapsing the compact cycles of the resolution
Douglas:2000qw
.

This enables the extension of the noncommutative instanton theories to the toric Calabi-Yau orbifolds
M_{n}=\mathbbm{C}^{n}/{\sf\Gamma}
, where
{\sf\Gamma}\subset\mathsf{SL}(n,\mathbbm{C})
is a finite abelian group which commutes with the torus
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\varepsilon}}
of the
\Omega
-background, and
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
is viewed as the fundamental representation of
\mathsf{SL}(n,\mathbbm{C})
. As a
\mathsf{\Gamma}
-module,
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
has branching weights
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
. We denote by
\widehat{\sf\Gamma}
the finite abelian group of irreducible representations
\rho_{s}
of
\mathsf{\Gamma}
.

The orbifold field theory is naturally constructed by keeping only contributions from
\mathsf{\Gamma}
-invariant instantons, via projection to the trivial representation
s=0
of the orbifold group
\mathsf{\Gamma}
, which involves an action of
\mathsf{\Gamma}
on the Chan-Paton space
\mathbbm{C}^{r}
, defined by a homomorphism
\mathsf{\Gamma}\to\mathsf{U}(r)
. Focusing momentarily on the rank
1
case, the Hilbert space (
8
) for the noncommutative gauge theory is a
\mathsf{\Gamma}
-module which admits an isotopical decomposition into irreducible representations

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\textstyle\bigoplus\limits_{s\in\widehat{\Gamma}}\,\mathcal{H}_{s}\ ,

(56)

where
\mathcal{H}_{s}
is the subspace of
\mathcal{H}
spanned by states
|\vec{n}\,\rangle
satisfying
\vec{n}\cdot\vec{s}:=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\,n_{a}\,s_{a}\equiv s
, by which is meant
\bigotimes_{a=1}^{n}\,\rho_{s_{a}}^{\otimes n_{a}}\simeq\rho_{s}
at the level of
\mathsf{\Gamma}
-modules.

The
\mathsf{\Gamma}
-equivariant operators
\vec{Z}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbbm{C}^{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}
decompose into maps
Z_{a}^{(s)}:\mathcal{H}_{s}\to\mathcal{H}_{s+s_{a}}
, and the noncommutative instanton equations (
9
) become

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!Z_{a}^{(s+s_{b})}\,Z_{b}^{(s)}-Z_{b}^{(s+s_{a})}\,Z_{a}^{(s)}

(57)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0\ ,\ n=2,3\\
\tfrac{1}{2}\,\epsilon_{ab\bar{c}\bar{d}}\,\big(Z_{\bar{c}}^{\dagger(s-s_{d}-s_{c})}\,Z_{\bar{d}}^{\dagger(s-s_{d})}\\
\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt-Z_{\bar{d}}^{\dagger(s-s_{d}-s_{c})}\,Z_{\bar{c}}^{\dagger(s-s_{c})}\big)\ ,\ n=4\ ,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n}\,\big(Z_{\bar{a}}^{(s)\dagger}\,Z_{a}^{(s)}-Z_{\bar{a}}^{(s-s_{a})}\,Z_{a}^{(s-s_{a})\dagger}\big)=-n\,\xi\,P^{(s)}\ ,

where
P^{(s)}
is the projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto the isotopical subspace
\mathcal{H}_{s}
, and (
57
) should be understood with the Calabi-Yau condition
|\vec{s}\,|\equiv 0

5.2
Instantons on Divisor Defects

Instantons on orbifolds are also useful for the description of instantons in gauge theories in the presence of divisor defects, on which gauge fields become singular with fixed monodromy. Near a defect the ambient
n
-fold has the local product form
M_{n}=D\times C
, where
D
is a divisor and
C
is a local fiber of the normal bundle. When restricted to
C
, the gauge connection assumes the form

\displaystyle A=-\mathrm{i}\,{\mathsf{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}\log w\,+\,\text{non-singular terms}\ ,

(60)

where
w
is a local coordinate on
C
, and
\mathsf{\alpha}
is an element in the Cartan subalgebra of the gauge group
\mathsf{U}(r)
which parametrizes the singularity of the (full) divisor operator. The curvature
2
-form
F
of (
60
) has a singularity proportional to the de Rham current which is Poincaré dual to the divisor
D
.

The pair
(D,\mathsf{\alpha})
is called a parabolic structure. It breaks the gauge symmetry along
D
to the commutant subgroup
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{\alpha}}\subset\mathsf{U}(r)
of
\mathsf{\alpha}
, called a (minimal) Levi subgroup. The Levi subgroups
\mathsf{L}
correspond to parabolic subgroups of
\mathsf{GL}(r,\mathbbm{C})
, which can be thought of as stabilizers of flags of vector spaces
0=U_{0}\subset U_{1}\subset\cdots\subset U_{r}=\mathbbm{C}^{r}
.

For
n=2,3
, the moduli space of instantons in the presence of a defect in
M_{n}=\mathbbm{C}^{n}
is parametrized by framed flags

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}(D)\subset\mathcal{F}_{-r+1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{-1}\subset\mathcal{F}_{0}

(61)

of rank
r
torsion free sheaves on
({\mathbbm{P}}^{1})^{n}
with
\mathrm{ch}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{l})=l\,D
and
\mathrm{ch}_{n}(\mathcal{F}_{l})=k_{l}
. These are called parabolic sheaves
Negut:2011aa
and the corresponding moduli space is denoted by
\mathfrak{D}_{\vec{k},r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
, with
\vec{k}=(k_{0},k_{1}\dots,k_{r-1})
.

The moduli spaces
\mathfrak{D}_{\vec{k},r}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})
are the connected components of the moduli space

\mathfrak{M}_{k,r}^{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(\mathbbm{C}^{n})

of
\mathsf{U}(r)
noncommutative orbifold instantons on
\mathbbm{C}^{n}
with topological charge
k=|\vec{k}\,|
for the action of a group
\mathsf{\Gamma}
whose fixed point locus is the divisor
D
and which preserves the parabolic structures; for a detailed discussion see
Alday:2010vg
;
Kanno:2011fw
;
Cirafici:2013nja
. For example, for a defect operator located on the divisor
D=\{\vec{z}\in\mathbbm{C}^{n}\,|\,z_{n}=0\}
, the orbifold group is the cyclic group
\mathsf{\Gamma}=\mathbbm{Z}_{r}
acting on the target space as
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n-1},z_{n})\mapsto(z_{1},\dots,z_{n-1},\mathrm{e}^{\,2\pi\,\mathrm{i}/r}\,z_{n})
. The analysis of these noncommutative instantons then follows the orbifold quiver description from Section
5.1

Kanno:2011fw
;
Cirafici:2013nja
.

For
n=4
these types of instantons have not yet been studied in detail. The general setup of the problem is sketched in
Cirafici:2013nja
.

5.3
Spiked/Tetrahedron Instantons

Another generalization of noncommutative instantons on

5.4
Instantons on Toric Varieties

The equivariant gauge theory can be defined on any toric

with isometries. There are two distinct classes, corresponding to compact and non-compact

and extra characteristic classes of the noncommutative instantons.

A smooth toric Calabi-Yau
n

is completely determined by its toric diagram

from a set of rules which glue together

The set of

\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\varepsilon}\,}
-fixed points of

represent
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\varepsilon}\,}
-invariant projective lines

connecting a pair of fixed points with normal bundle

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\,m_{i}=2

centered at the fixed point on which the torus
\mathsf{T}_{\vec{\varepsilon}\,}
acts with weights

The idea is to solve the noncommutative instanton equations in each patch and then glue the local solutions together into a global contribution to the path integral. The instanton partition function that we describe below enumerates bound states of D

5.5
Further Developments

There is a more extensive body of literature on noncommutative instantons in other settings which do not directly fit into the main line of development discussed in this paper, but which are certainly worthy of mention here.

A close avatars of our theories in
4
dimensions are the instantons on toric noncommutative manifolds such as spheres
Landi:2006qw
;
Brain:2012cj
, which uses techniques from the theory of isospectral deformations and noncommutative geometry in braided monoidal categories. By combining these approaches with techniques from toric geometry and noncommutative algebraic geometry, one can quantize toric varieties directly, which offers an alternative approach to noncommutative instantons compared to the patching construction discussed in Section
5.4

Cirio:2010ip
;
Cirio:2011eh
;
Cirio:2012sw
.

These types of noncommutative instantons come from
q
-deformations, of the type originally discussed in
Kapustin:2000ek
. They also appear in gauge theories on various noncommutative spaces based on quantum groups
Dabrowski:2000nz
;
Bonechi:2000ia
;
Frenkel:2002pm
;
DAndrea:2013kye
. Some of these theories have the curious feature of producing noncommutative moduli spaces of noncommutative instantons
Fiore:2006kh
;
Landi:2007ry
;
Brain:2009it
.

Finally, we mention for completeness that instantons have also been studied in gauge theories on fuzzy spaces and in matrix models
Balachandran:1999hx
;
Grosse:2001ss
;
Valtancoli:2006dw
.

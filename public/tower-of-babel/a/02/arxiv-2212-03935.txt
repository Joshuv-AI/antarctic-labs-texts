Group coset monogamy games and an application to device-independent continuous-variable QKD
Eric Culf, Thomas Vidick, Victor V. Albert

Abstract
We develop an extension of a recently introduced subspace coset state monogamy-of-entanglement game [Coladangelo, Liu, Liu, and Zhandry; Crypto’21] to general group coset states, which are uniform superpositions over elements of a subgroup to which has been applied a group-theoretic generalization of the quantum one-time pad. We give a general bound on the winning probability of a monogamy game constructed from subgroup coset states that applies to a wide range of finite and infinite groups. To study the infinite-group case, we use and further develop a measure-theoretic formalism that allows us to express continuous-variable measurements as operator-valued generalizations of probability measures. We apply the monogamy game bound to various physically relevant groups, yielding realizations of the game in continuous-variable modes as well as in rotational states of a polyatomic molecule. We obtain explicit strong bounds in the case of specific group-space and subgroup combinations. As an application, we provide the first proof of one sided-device independent security of a squeezed-state continuous-variable quantum key distribution protocol against general coherent attacks.

Introduction & summary of results

Quantum entanglement between several parties can be considered as a shared resource. The principle of
monogamy
of entanglement informally states that two parties
cannot be maximally entangled with each other if they are also entangled
with a third. One way to understand this restriction is through the notion of a
monogamy
game
, in which two players, Bob and Charlie, are tasked with simultaneously
determining features of a state sent to them by a third
party Alice. Both Bob and Charlie have to extract their feature correctly in order to win the game. Winning is not always possible since the desired
features are stored in the multipartite correlations between Alice
and the duo of Bob and Charlie, and those correlations cannot be made
available to both Bob and Charlie due to entanglement monogamy. This can be seen as a consequence of the no-cloning property of quantum information.

Monogamy games have been developed to prove strong statements about the
viability of various quantum cryptographic protocols. The initial example of a monogamy game was used to prove that the BB’84 quantum key
distribution (QKD) protocol
[
BB84
]
is secure in a one-sided device-independent model,

i.e.
secure even if one does not make assumptions about the receiver’s measurement device
[
TFKW13
,
PGT+22
]
. More recently, a monogamy game based on an extension of BB’84 states called
subspace coset states
was introduced
[
CLLZ21
]
, with
applications to uncloneable decryption and copy-protection
of pseudorandom functions.
This game can be seen as a variant of the original
n
-qubit game, and in
[
CV22
]
an exponentially decaying (in the number of qubits
n
) bound was shown on the maximum winning probability in the game.

We generalize
this monogamy game in several directions by recasting the original
game in group-theoretic terms and studying the resulting formulation for a variety of groups. Our reformulation reveals an unexpected link between the monogamy game and states studied in quantum error
correction. We prove several monogamy bounds for a wide range of groups, notably non-abelian groups and continuous (topological) groups. As a motivating application of our results, we highlight a proof of one-sided device-independent security for a continuous-variable quantum-key distribution that is resistant to coherent attacks; to the best of our knowledge, this is the first such proof.

Coset states, group theory, and quantum error correction

We recall the key ingredient behind the subspace coset monogamy game of
[
CLLZ21
]
— the coset state — in group-theoretic terms; generalizations of such states will serve as the backbone of our new monogamy games.

Coset states were originally defined on an
n
-qubit space, whose computational basis states are labeled by elements of the group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
. The coset states are

\lvert H,s,s^{\prime}\rangle\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H|}}\sum_{u\in H}(-1)^{u\cdot s^{\prime}}\lvert u+s\rangle\,.

(1)

Here,
H
is a set of binary strings that is closed under binary
addition — a subgroup of
G
; this set labels which canonical basis
elements participate in the superposition of the “base” subspace state

\lvert H,0,0\rangle
. The binary strings
s,s^{\prime}
can be set,
respectively, by applying appropriate Pauli
X
and
Z
operators
on this base state (a.k.a. Pauli-twirling or one-time padding).
In the group-theoretic interpretation, the string
s
is a coset representative of the quotient
G/H
that partitions
G
into a union of cosets

s+H
, while
(-1)^{u\cdot s^{\prime}}\equiv\gamma_{s^{\prime}}(u)
is an irreducible representation
of
H
labelled by the string
s^{\prime}
.

From the perspective of quantum error correction, the coset states (
1
) are precisely the code or error words of a
CSS stabilizer quantum error-correcting code. Following related work for continuous-variable (CV) spaces
[
GKP01
]
, such codes have recently
been generalized
[
ACP20
]
to the setting of group-valued Hilbert spaces for a general group
G
spanned by

\{|g\rangle\,|\,g\in G\}
, where each canonical basis state is in
one-to-one correspondence with an element of a group. As in the

n
-qubit case above, we can construct coset states
\lvert aH^{\gamma}_{m,n}\rangle
, where
H
is a subgroup of
G
,
a
is a coset representative of
G/H
, and
\gamma_{m,n}
is a matrix element of an irreducible representation of
H
.

A wide variety of interesting states can be represented as
H\subset G
coset states, notably, eigenstates of position and momentum quadratures of a CV mode (
\mathbb{R}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
), CV GKP states (
\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}
)
[
GKP01
]
, and quantum superpositions of orientations of an asymmetric molecule or, more generally, any 3D rigid body (
H\subset SO(3)
)
[
ACP20
]
. For each group type, there is a connection to a stabilizer-like error-correcting code on the corresponding group space. We list all the group types considered in the paper, along with their corresponding codes, in Table
1
.

For fixed
H
, the set of coset states forms a complete set of states for the Hilbert space. If the group is finite, they are orthonormal as basis states in the Kronecker sense, and if the group is continuous they are orthogonal as distributions in the Dirac sense — what is generally known as a Zak basis (see
[
ACP20
, Appx. F]
for more context). For fixed
H
and
\gamma
, the set of such states houses an induced representation
[
Aro
]
of the parent group
G
.

Since coset states of infinite groups are not normalizable, approximate or “damped” versions
[
GKP01
,
Men14
,
ACP20
,
ISGA22
]
have to be constructed in order to utilize such states in the lab. Thus, to formally discuss preparation of coset states, we need to take into account the damping operation used. However, we can avoid this when we discuss measurement in a basis of coset states. There we may represent the measurement process using an operator-valued generalization of a probability measure, thereby avoiding problems of normalization and convergence.

Generalized monogamy-of-entanglement games

We study general coset monogamy games from two perspectives, corresponding to an entanglement-based and a state-sending version of the game, respectively.

The state-sending version is closer to the original subspace coset game of
[
CLLZ21
]
. In the original multi-qubit case utilizing the abelian coset states (
1
), Alice sends the state
\lvert H,s,s^{\prime}\rangle
with randomly chosen
H
,
s
, and
s^{\prime}
.
Bob and Charlie can split the state in an arbitrary way (including the application of an arbitrary CPTP map to it) and then separate.
Once they are separated, they are each given a description of
H
and tasked with determining
s
and
s^{\prime}
, respectively, up to the choice of representatives.
In our non-Abelian finite-group case, Alice prepares and sends a coset state
\lvert aH^{\gamma}_{m,n}\rangle
, and Bob and Charlie attempt to guess
a
and
\gamma_{m,n}
, respectively.

In the entanglement-based version, Bob and Charlie prepare a tripartite shared entangled state. Once all parties are separated, Alice measures her system in a randomly-chosen coset basis to get outcomes
a,\gamma_{m,n}
. She then informs Bob and Charlie of her measurement basis, and they make guesses of
a
and
\gamma_{m,n}
, respectively.

The entanglement-based game can be studied more directly, so we focus on that one in all the cases we consider. This especially important for infinite groups, as Alice’s measurement can be expressed using an operator measure, hence avoiding discussion of the non-normalisable coset states. As in the case of
n
-qubit games
[
BL20
,
CLLZ21
]
, it is possible to transform a strategy for the state-sending game into a strategy for the entanglement-based game, which leads to a bound on the former as well. We work this relationship out formally in the abelian case, but note that it holds in the same way in the non-abelian case. The result of this transformation naturally provides a state-sending coset monogamy game where Alice sends the damped version of the coset states.

Device-independent continuous-variable QKD

Inspired by the one-sided device-independent quantum key distribution (QKD) security proof introduced in
[
TFKW13
]
, we analyze a QKD protocol using continuous-variable (CV) coset states for
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
H=\mathbb{R}^{n/2}
— conceptually the closest continuous generalisation to the original
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n/2}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
qubit protocol
[
CV22
]
. The CV protocol considered reduces to a Gaussian one: the unnormalizable coset states are infinitely squeezed states, but their damped versions are practically-realizable finitely squeezed states.

We show that these squeezed-state protocols are one-sided device independent (one-sided DI) secure against coherent attacks in the finite-key regime, making them the first CV protocols with such a level of security.
Previous one-sided DI proofs of security for CVQKD protocols were limited to memoryless attackers
[
FFB+12
,
GHD+15
]
; indeed, overcoming this limitation for the case of discrete-variable (qubit-based) QKD protocols was one of the main motivations for the introduction of the
n
-qubit coset monogamy game in
[
TFKW13
]
.

Our analysis leads to an error tolerance which is comparable to the one obtained for DV protocols in
[
TFKW13
]
. While our protocol, employing squeezed states (cf.
[
GP01
]
), remains more challenging than the coherent-state based Gaussian CV protocols
[
GGDL19
]
, the security benefits of one-sided device independence may outweigh the experimental challenges.

While we consider only the
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
protocol in detail in this manuscript, we note that similar protocols should be possible for the other group spaces, and do not see an obstruction to proving analogous device-independent security for such protocols. In particular, our general formulation should allow for QKD protocols utilizing GKP states (
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
H\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
).
Moreover, our formulation paves the way for analyzing more general subgroups of
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
that form degenerate lattices or products of lattices and planes, with the former corresponding to the recently developed GKP-stabilizer codes
[
NGJ20
]
that protect an entire logical mode against small fluctuations in all physical modes.

2
The coset monogamy game on
U(1)

We introduce the group-valued space of the planar rotor
G=U(1)
and its associated coset states and monogamy game.

2.1
Planar rotor states

Systems confined to rotate in a two-dimensional plane may be described as a planar rotor. For such a system, the set of classical states can be represented by the group of rotations in the plane
G=U(1)
. There are various ways to work with this group, but we will consider it as
U(1)=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and make use of the set of representatives
[0,2\pi)
. Since the space is continuous, the Hilbert space of quantum states is the space of square-integrable functions
L^{2}(U(1))
. The inner product on this space is provided by the Haar measure – the unique normalized measure invariant under the action of the group – which in the case of
U(1)
takes the form

\displaystyle\vphantom{\psi\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\overline{\psi(x)}\phi(x)dx\;.

(2)

The Fourier series provides the canonical orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(U(1))
: the basis of states
\lvert\ell\rangle
for
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
given by functions
\psi_{\ell}(x)=e^{i\ell x}
for
x\in[0,2\pi)\cong U(1)
. This corresponds to the basis of angular momentum eigenstates of a
U(1)
system. Dual to this basis are the position eigenstates
\lvert\theta\rangle
given by
\psi_{\theta}(x)=\delta_{U(1)}(\theta-x)=2\pi\delta(\theta-x)
for
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, which satisfy the generalised orthonormality condition
\vphantom{\theta\theta^{\prime}}\left\langle\smash{\theta}\middle|\smash{\theta^{\prime}}\right\rangle=2\pi\delta(\theta-\theta^{\prime})
. These are however not states, since the Dirac delta is not a function, so they cannot be normalized. Accordingly, it takes some care to work with this kind of state. We will approach this in two different but complementary ways. First, we can consider the basis not as a set of physical states but as a measurement, which allows us to treat it in a measure-theoretic way. For any state
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(U(1))
, the probability of measuring a position in some set
E\subseteq U(1)
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{E}\left\lvert\vphantom{\theta\psi}\left\langle\smash{\theta}\middle|\smash{\psi}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}d\theta=\vphantom{\psi\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{E}\vphantom{\theta}\left\lvert\smash{\theta}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\theta}\right\rvert d\theta\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{E}\vphantom{\theta}\left\lvert\smash{\theta}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\theta}\right\rvert d\theta}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle.

(3)

In this way, we take the operator measure of a (Borel measurable) set in
U(1)
as the operator
A^{U(1)}(E):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{E}\vphantom{\theta}\left\lvert\smash{\theta}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\theta}\right\rvert d\theta
, which can be seen a continuous-variable generalization of a projective measurement or an operator-valued generalization of a probability measure. Note also that these are well-defined operators on
L^{2}(U(1))
, acting as

\displaystyle(A^{U(1)}(E)\lvert\psi\rangle)(x)=\chi_{E}(x)\psi(x)=\begin{cases}\psi(x)&x\in E\\
0&x\notin E\end{cases}.

(4)

In the operator measure picture, the completeness of the basis is expressed by showing that the measure is a POVM —
A^{U(1)}(U(1))=\mathbb{I}
. The mathematical formalism of operator measures is worked out in
Appendix

A
.

The other way we approach unnormalizable states is by damping, that is we act on the state by an operator that makes it normalizable. A common way to do this is to replace the deltas by Gaussians:

\displaystyle\lvert\theta\rangle\mapsto\sqrt{\frac{a}{\pi}}\int_{\theta-\pi}^{\theta+\pi}e^{-a(x-\theta)^{2}}\lvert x\rangle dx.

(5)

In the limit
a\rightarrow\infty
, this returns to the original delta function; so, for large
a
, this provides a very good approximation to the behaviour of the position eigenstates despite being normalizable. We formalise this in
Appendix

B
and work out how to pass from operator measures to damped states without going through an unnormalizable basis.

The position and momentum bases, though disparate, are particular cases of the same construction, the
coset state basis
. A coset state basis is a generally unnormalizable basis corresponding to a closed subgroup of
G
; the position basis corresponds to the subgroup of all elements
G
and the momentum basis corresponds to the trivial subgroup
\{0\}
. The remaining closed subgroups are
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\leq U(1)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, groups of rotations by multiples of
2\pi/n
. Fixing
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-
subgroup state
as the uniform superposition over all elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,
1
1

1

We use a different normalization convention than previous work
[
ACP20
, Eq. (124)]
throughout the paper, resulting in rescaled Dirac-delta functions on relevant quotient spaces. Our convention translates to using a normalized Haar measure in the case of compact
G
,
\frac{1}{|\mathbb{Z}_{n}|}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\to\int_{G}dg
, while in the previous convention,
\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbb{Z}_{n}|}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\int_{G}dg
with

the group volume.

\displaystyle\lvert\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{Z}_{n}|}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\lvert x\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\lvert 2\pi k/n\rangle\;.

(6)

Note also that for the position and angular momentum bases,
\lvert\{0\}\rangle=\lvert 0\rangle
and
\lvert U(1)\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\lvert x\rangle dx=\lvert\ell=0\rangle
. To extend the subgroup state to a basis we orthogonalise in two ways: use superpositions with orthogonal supports in the basis, and introduce phases. The canonical way to move to an orthogonal support is to consider analogous superpositions over a
coset
of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
rather than the group itself. The cosets are the equivalence classes
U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, which as
U(1)
is abelian form a group
U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}\cong U(1)
. Thus, for
x+\mathbb{Z}_{n}\in U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, the coset state
\lvert x+\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\lvert x+y\rangle
. Also, we introduce phases given by the dual group
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, the group of continuous group homomorphisms
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rightarrow\left\{{z\in\mathbb{C}}\middle|{|z|=1}\right\}
under multiplication. For the usual representation as a quotient of
\mathbb{Z}
, the dual group of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{n}
with action given by
\gamma_{k}(m)=e^{2\pi i\frac{km}{n}}
. In the same way, for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
seen as a subgroup of
U(1)
, the action
\gamma_{k}(x)=e^{ikx}
. Then, the subgroup coset states are defined by

\displaystyle\lvert n,x,k\rangle:=\lvert x+\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\gamma_{k}}\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\gamma_{k}(y)\lvert x+y\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}e^{2\pi i\frac{km}{n}}\lvert x+2\pi m/n\rangle\;.

(7)

These are unnormalizable, but they are orthogonal in the sense that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{n,x,kn,x^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\left\langle\smash{n,x,k}\middle|\smash{n,x^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\right\rangle&=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}e^{i(k^{\prime}y^{\prime}-ky)}\delta_{U(1)}((x+y)-(x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}))\\
&=\delta_{U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(x-x^{\prime}+\mathbb{Z}_{n})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}e^{i(k^{\prime}(y+(x-x^{\prime}))-ky)}\\
&=\delta_{U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(x-x^{\prime}+\mathbb{Z}_{n})\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\;,\end{split}

(8)

and complete in the sense that the position eigenstates are contained in their span. In general, the definition of the coset state basis depends on a choice of coset representatives, but since this only changes the states up to global phase, we do not need to consider it. This definition directly extends the coset states of a finite group. Again, to work with them more rigorously, we can consider the coset measure they induce. Now, as the basis is indexed by
U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\cong U(1)\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, the coset operator measure is an operator-valued measure on that set. For Borel measurable
E\subseteq U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,

\displaystyle A^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(E)=n\int_{E}\vphantom{n,x,k}\left\lvert\smash{n,x,k}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{n,x,k}\right\rvert d(x+\mathbb{Z}_{n},k)=\frac{n}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{E_{n}}\vphantom{n,x,k}\left\lvert\smash{n,x,k}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{n,x,k}\right\rvert dx\;,

(9)

where we write
E=E_{0}\times\{0\}\cup\ldots\cup E_{n-1}\times\{n-1\}
. The additional coefficient
n
is required because the dual measure on
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is normalized so that
\mu(\{\gamma_{0}\})=1
and not
\mu(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})=1
. Again, this provides a well-defined operator:

\displaystyle\vphantom{\phi A^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{E_{k}}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}}e^{ik(y-y^{\prime})}\bar{\phi}(x+y)\psi(x+y^{\prime})dx\;.

(10)

We note again that this measure satisfies
A^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n})=\mathbb{I}
, which is equivalent to completeness of the basis. For a general (locally compact) abelian group, the coset measure is formally introduced in
Section

8.1
.

2.2
Monogamy game and winning probability

We can use these planar-rotor coset states to play a monogamy game inspired by the strong monogamy game of
[
CLLZ21
]
. Here, we describe the entanglement-based version of the game, which can be understood using the coset measure. In this game, two cooperating players, Bob and Charlie, play against an honest referee, Alice. Let
p_{1}<\ldots<p_{N}
be a set of distinct primes and let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{\pi}{p_{N}^{2}}
. The game proceeds as follows:

1.

Bob and Charlie prepare a shared state
\rho_{ABC}
but then are no longer allowed to communicate.

2.

Alice chooses
j=1,\ldots,N
uniformly at random and measures her register in the basis
\left\{{\lvert p_{j},x,k\rangle}\right\}
to get measurements
x\in U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}
.

3.

Alice sends
p_{j}
to Bob and Charlie. Bob answers with a guess
x_{B}
for
x
and Charlie answers with a guess
k_{C}
for
k
.

4.

Bob and Charlie win if
|x-x_{B}|<\varepsilon
in
U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
k=k_{C}
.

In this section, we do not formally introduce strategies for this game. Nevertheless, we note that Charlie makes a measurement with a finite number of outcomes, so his measurement may in general be expressed by a POVM. On the other hand, Bob has infinitely many (in fact continuously many) measurement outcomes. This means that Bob’s measurement is modelled by an operator-valued measure. Also, Bob’s winning condition is slackened compared to the game with finite information, as he needs to only guess within a neighborhood. This is because we cannot expect Bob to answer with infinite precision in a continuous space. First, this is physically infeasible as he would need to transmit infinitely many bits to Alice, and also the winning probability would always be
0
, as the space of correct answers would have measure
0
.

General games of this form for abelian groups are introduced in
Section

8.2
; they are parametrized by the underlying group
G
, the collection of subgroups
\mathcal{S}
, as well as Bob and Charlie’s neighborhoods of correct answers
E\subseteq G
and
F\subseteq\hat{G}
. For the above game,
\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon}
, we have
G=U(1)
,
\mathcal{S}=\left\{{U_{p_{1}},\ldots,U_{p_{N}}}\right\}
,
E=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, and
F=\{\gamma_{0}\}
. We make use of the general bound of
Theorem

8.7
to find an upper bound on the winning probability of
\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon}
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\,\leq\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sup_{H\in\mathcal{S},g\in G}\sqrt{\frac{\left\lvert H\cap(g+E+\pi_{j}(H))\right\rvert~\left\lvert F\right\rvert}{|H|}}\;.

(11)

In this formula,
\pi_{j}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
is a collection of orthogonal permutations — bijections such that
\pi_{j}(H)=\pi_{k}(H)
only if
j=k
. For any set, there always exists such a family, in our case the cycles
\pi_{j}(U_{p_{k}})=\pi_{j}(U_{p_{k+j}})
, which is the family we will use to get the bound on the winning probability of
\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p_{1}<\ldots<p_{N}
and
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{\pi}{p_{N}^{2}}
. The winning probability of the
U(1)
monogamy game satisfies

\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\,\leq\,\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{1}}}\;.

Proof.

First, for any
j=1,\ldots,N
and
\gamma\in\hat{G}
,
\left\lvert F\right\rvert=\left\lvert\{\gamma_{0}\}\right\rvert=1
. Also, for any
j\neq k
and
2\pi r\in U(1)
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lvert\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}\cap(2\pi r+E+\mathbb{Z}_{p_{k}})\rvert&=\left\lvert\left\{{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}}\middle|{\exists\;m\in\mathbb{N}.\left\lvert\tfrac{n}{p_{j}}-r-\tfrac{m}{p_{k}}\right\rvert<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi}}\right\}\right\rvert\\
&\leq\left\lvert\left\{{[n]\in\mathbb{Z}_{p_{j}}}\middle|{\exists\;m\in\mathbb{N}.\left\lvert np_{k}-rp_{j}p_{k}-mp_{j}\right\rvert<\tfrac{1}{2}}\right\}\right\rvert\leq 1,\end{split}

(12)

as the interval
(rp_{j}p_{k}-\frac{1}{2},rp_{j}p_{k}+\frac{1}{2})
contains at most one integer, and
np_{k}
is equal to this integer modulo
p_{j}
for at most one value of
n
by inversion modulo
p_{j}
. Thus, as
\pi_{0}
is the identity,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\leq\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=2}^{N}\sup_{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{k}}}\leq\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{1}}}.

(13)

∎

4
The coset monogamy game on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

We introduce the continuous-variable space of
n
modes (
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and its associated continuous-subgroup coset states and monogamy game. This section is in essence a generalization of the special case of
n=2
from the previous section.

4.1
Optical quadrature coset states

With
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the position states are the multimode quadratures
\lvert q\rangle
for
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the inner products
{\langle x|q\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\delta(q_{i}-x_{i})}
, for
x=\sum_{i}x_{i}e_{i}
the expansion of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the canonical orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
. Noting that the dual
\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
with action given by the dot product
\gamma_{x}(y)=e^{2\pi ix\cdot y}
, the momentum states are
\lvert p\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ip\cdot q}\lvert q\rangle dq
.

We consider subgroups corresponding to linear subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Intuitively, this is the continuous case closest to the original finite case of subspaces of a finite vector space. Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a subspace. The quotient
\mathbb{R}^{n}/P\cong P^{\perp}
, the usual orthogonal subspace of the standard inner product; and the dual
\hat{P}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}/P^{\perp}\cong P
with the action inherited from the dual of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Due to the normalization of the dual action, the Haar measure on all of these is simply the usual Lebesgue measure. This gives, for
q\in P^{\perp}
and
p\in P
, coset states

\displaystyle\lvert P_{q,p}\rangle=\lvert q+P^{\gamma_{p}}\rangle=\int_{P}e^{2\pi ip\cdot x}\lvert x+q\rangle d_{P}x.

(22)

It is important to note that for register subspaces,
i.e.

P=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\left\{{e_{i}}\middle|{i\in I}\right\}
for some subset
I\subseteq[n]
, the coset states expand as quadrature modes

\displaystyle\lvert P_{q,p}\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\begin{cases}\lvert q=q_{i}\rangle&i\notin I\\
\lvert p=p_{i}\rangle&i\in I\end{cases}.

(23)

This naturally extends the expression of finite subspace coset states of register subspaces as conjugate-coding (BB84) states
[
VZ21
]
.

Making contact with error correction, the subspace
P
represents the code subspace of an analog stabilizer code, encoding
\mathrm{dim}(P)
logical modes into
n
physical modes. Register-subspace coset states
\lvert P_{0,p}\rangle
(
23
) provide a basis of momentum states for this subspace and are eigenvalue-zero eigenstates of the position quadrature operators of the modes outside of
I
, which are called nullifiers in this context (see
[
VAW+18
, Appx. E]
[
ecz22b
]
).

To deal with measurement in this basis rigorously, we also work with the coset operator measure. This is the operator measure on
\mathscr{B}(P^{\perp})\otimes\mathscr{B}(P)\cong\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle A^{P}(E)=\int_{E}\vphantom{P_{q,p}}\left\lvert\smash{P_{q,p}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{P_{q,p}}\right\rvert d_{P^{\perp}\times P}(q,p),

(24)

or more formally,

\displaystyle\vphantom{\phi A^{P}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{P}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\int_{E}\overline{(\mathcal{F}_{P}\lvert\phi\circ q\rangle)(p)}(\mathcal{F}_{P}\lvert\psi\circ q\rangle)(p)d_{P^{\perp}\times P}(q,p),

(25)

where
\lvert\phi\rangle,\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\mathcal{F}_{P}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Fourier transform with respect to
P
, defined on
\psi
continuous with compact support as
(\mathcal{F}_{P}\lvert\psi\rangle)(p)=\int_{P}e^{-2\pi ip\cdot x}\psi(x)d_{P}x
, and extended by continuity.

A simple and natural damping operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is simply the
n
-fold tensor product of the damping operators on
\mathbb{R}
,
\Delta_{a,b}^{\otimes n}
. On each mode, this operator sends quadrature eigenstates to squeezed coherent states:

\displaystyle\lvert q\rangle\mapsto e^{-aq^{2}}\int e^{-b(q-x)^{2}}\lvert q=x\rangle dx=\left\lparen\tfrac{\pi}{2b}\right\rparen^{1/4}e^{-aq^{2}}\lvert b,q,0\rangle

(26)

\displaystyle\lvert p\rangle\mapsto\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{ab}}e^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{a+b}p^{2}}\int e^{-\pi^{2}\frac{a+b}{ab}\left\lparen y-\tfrac{1}{1+\frac{a}{b}}p\right\rparen^{2}}\lvert p=y\rangle dy=\left\lparen\tfrac{\pi^{3}}{2ab(a+b)}\right\rparen^{1/4}e^{-\frac{\pi^{2}}{a+b}p^{2}}\lvert\tfrac{ab}{a+b},0,\tfrac{bp}{a+b}\rangle,

(27)

where the general squeezed state

\lvert a,x_{0},p_{0}\rangle=\left\lparen\tfrac{2a}{\pi}\right\rparen^{1/4}\int e^{-a(x-x_{0})^{2}+2\pi ip_{0}x}\lvert x\rangle dx~.

(28)

In particular, for
a=\frac{\pi^{2}b}{b^{2}-\pi^{2}}
there is equal squeezing on both quadratures. With this operator, the damped coset state of a register subspace
P
is

\displaystyle\lvert P_{q,p}|_{a,b}\rangle=\frac{c\Delta_{a,b}^{\otimes n}\lvert P_{q,p}\rangle}{\|\Delta_{a,b}^{\otimes n}\lvert P_{q,p}\rangle\|}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\begin{cases}\lvert b,q_{i},0\rangle&,\;i\notin I\\
\lvert\tfrac{ab}{a+b},0,-\frac{bp_{i}}{a+b}\rangle&,\;i\in I\end{cases}.

(29)

The distribution of damped states is

\displaystyle\pi^{P}_{a,b}(q,p)=\frac{\|\Delta_{a,b}^{\otimes n}\lvert P_{q,p}\rangle\|^{2}}{\|\Delta_{a,b}\|_{2}^{2n}}=\left\lparen\tfrac{2a}{\pi}\right\rparen^{(n-\dim P)/2}\left\lparen\tfrac{2\pi}{a+b}\right\rparen^{\dim P/2}e^{-2a\|q\|^{2}_{2}-\tfrac{2\pi^{2}}{a+b}\|p\|^{2}_{2}}.

(30)

\rho_{ABC}

for

for

.

Bob and Charlie win if
\|q-q_{B}\|_{\infty}<\delta

\texttt{G}_{n,\delta,\varepsilon}
with collection of subspaces

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{n,\delta,\varepsilon})\leq\frac{1}{\binom{n}{n/2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n/2}\binom{n/2}{k}^{2}(2\sqrt{\delta\varepsilon})^{k}\leq\sqrt{e}\left\lparen\tfrac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\delta\varepsilon}\right\rparen^{n/2}\;.

To show the claimed bound we use the following strengthening of the overlap bound
Lemma

8.9
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
P=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\{{e_{i}}|{i\in I}\}
and
Q=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\{{e_{i}}|{i\in J}\}
be register subspaces,

p\in P
, and
q\in Q^{\perp}
. Then,

\displaystyle\|A^{P}(P^{\perp}\times(F\cap P+p))A^{Q}((E\cap Q^{\perp}+q)\times Q)\|\leq(2\sqrt{\delta\varepsilon})^{n/2-|I\cap J|}.

(32)

Proof.

First, we note that as in
Lemma

8.9
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{\psi A^{Q}((E\cap Q^{\perp}+q)\times Q)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{Q}((E\cap Q^{\perp}+q)\times Q)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle&=\int_{E\cap Q^{\perp}+q}\int_{Q}\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{P}\lvert\psi\circ q^{\prime}\rangle)(p^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}dp^{\prime}dq^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{E\cap Q^{\perp}+q}\int_{Q}\left\lvert\psi(p^{\prime}+q^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}dp^{\prime}dq^{\prime}=\int_{q+E+Q}\left\lvert\psi(q^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}dq^{\prime},\end{split}

(33)

that is
A^{Q}((E\cap Q^{\perp}+q)\times Q)=\Pi_{q+E+Q}
, the projector onto
q+E+Q
. Now, fixing
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuous with compact support,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|A^{P}(P^{\perp}\times(F\cap P+p))\Pi_{q+E+Q}\lvert\psi\rangle\|^{2}&=\int_{P^{\perp}}\int_{F\cap P+p}\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{P}(\Pi_{q+E+Q}\lvert\psi\rangle\circ q^{\prime}))(p^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}d_{P}p^{\prime}d_{P^{\perp}}q^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{P^{\perp}}\int_{F\cap P+p}\left\lvert\int_{P\cap(q-q^{\prime}+E+Q)}e^{-2\pi ip^{\prime}\cdot x}\psi(x+q^{\prime})d_{P}x\right\rvert^{2}d_{P}p^{\prime}d_{P^{\perp}}q^{\prime}.\end{split}

(34)

To simplify this, we first study the set
P\cap(q-q^{\prime}+E+Q)
. By definition,

\displaystyle P\cap(q-q^{\prime}+E+Q)=\{{x\in P}|{\exists y\in Q~\text{s.t.}~\;\|x-y+q^{\prime}-q\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon}\}.

(35)

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu_{P\cap Q^{\perp}}(E\cap(P\cap Q^{\perp}))&=\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{\dim P\cap Q^{\perp}}}((-\delta,\delta)^{\dim P\cap Q^{\perp}})\\

and identically

4.2.2
Quadrature measurement outcome discretization

For applications such as QKD it is advantageous to work with discretized versions of the outputs. Though working with error neighborhoods is more natural in the context of a monogamy game, this is not particularly amenable to discretization. Rather, it is better to partition the space into disjoint bins, as is commonly done in this context. For

and

\mathbb{R}

1.

Alice chooses a register subspace
P=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\left\{{e_{i}}\middle|{i\in\mathcal{I}}\right\}
of dimension
n/2
uniformly at random and samples
(q,p)
according to the distribution

. She prepares the squeezed state
\lvert P_{q,p}|_{a,b}\rangle
(
29
) and sends it to Bob and Charlie.

2.

Bob and Charlie attempt to split the state using an arbitrary channel
\Phi
, and then are no longer allowed to communicate.

3.

Alice sends
P
to Bob and Charlie. Bob answers with a bin index

and Charlie answers with a bin index

Corollary 4.4
.

The winning probability

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}^{\text{binned}}_{n,\delta,\varepsilon,\gamma,a,b})\leq\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{n,\delta,\varepsilon,\gamma,a,b})\leq 2^{\left[(1-\gamma)\lg\left\lparen\tfrac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\delta\varepsilon}\right\rparen+h(\gamma)+\tfrac{1}{(\ln 2)n}\right]\tfrac{n}{2}}.

Fix a strategy
S
for
\texttt{G}^{\text{binned}}_{n,\delta,\varepsilon,\gamma,a,b}

\texttt{S}^{\prime}
for

that wins with at least

4.3
Application: squeezed-state one-sided device-independent QKD

As an application of our monogamy bound for coset states over
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
we give a proof of security for continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CVQKD) with squeezed states in the one-sided device-independent (one-sided DI) model. What this means is that security holds even in the case where the receiver’s (Bob) quantum measurement device is untrusted. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first one-sided DI proof of security for CVQKD that is secure against the most general class of attacks, coherent attacks.

The idea of one-sided DI for QKD was first introduced in
[
TR11
]
, where the authors show one-sided DI security of the BB’84 prepare-and-measure protocol for qubits based on the use of an entropic uncertainty relation. As later pointed out in
[
TFKW13
]
, the proof of security from
[
TR11
]
only holds under the assumption that Bob’s measurement device is memoryless, and indeed removing this assumption is one of the motivations for the monogamy-of-entanglement game studied in
[
TFKW13
]
. The approach to one-sided DI security via uncertainty relations was later extended to squeezed-state CV protocols in
[
FFB+12
]
, and has been experimentally demonstrated
[
GHD+15
]
.

Our extension of the monogamy game from
[
TR11
]
to subgroups of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
enables us to obtain the first one-sided DI proof of security for CVQKD. While for Gaussian CVQKD protocols there has recently been a full security proof against coherent attacks
[
Lev17
]
, there is currently no one-sided DI security proof, even against collective attacks, for such protocols. As we will see, our analysis leads to an error tolerance which is comparable to the one obtained for DV protocols in
[
TFKW13
]
. While our protocol, employing squeezed states, remains more challenging than coherent-state CV protocols, the important benefits of one-sided device independence may outweigh the experimental challenges.

4.3.2
Protocol and completeness

We use the following protocol, based on standard tools: the preparation of squeezed states by Alice and the use of homodyne detection for Bob.

Protocol 4.8

(Squeezed-state device-independent continuous-variable QKD)
.

State preparation

Alice samples a register subspace
P=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\left\{{e_{i}}\middle|{i\in\mathcal{I}}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of dimension
n/2
uniformly at random. She samples
(q,p)
according to
\pi^{P}_{a,b}
(
30

-M\delta\leq q_{i}<M\delta
and
-N\varepsilon\leq p_{i}<N\varepsilon
for all
i

\lvert P_{q,p}|_{a,b}\rangle
(
29

Parameter estimation

Bob acknowledges receipt of the state, then Alice sends
P
and
q^{\delta,M}_{I}
, where
I\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{c}
is a subset of size
\theta n/2
. Bob measures the modes of his received states with homodyne detection to get guesses
(\hat{q},\hat{p})
, measuring in position for
i\notin\mathcal{I}
and momentum for
i\in\mathcal{I}
.
If any of the terms are outside the expected ranges, he replaces them with

Error correction

Alice sends
\mathrm{syn}(p^{\varepsilon,N})

\left\lparen-(1+a/b)\hat{p}\right\rparen^{\varepsilon,N}
using this to get
\bar{p}^{\varepsilon,N}

\eta n_{N}n/2

J,p^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}
to Bob; if
\bar{p}^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}\neq p^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}

Alice chooses
f\in\mathcal{F}
uniformly random and sends it to Bob. Alice computes the key
k=f(p^{\varepsilon,N})
and Bob computes
\hat{k}=f(\bar{p}^{\varepsilon,N})
.

Proposition 4.9
.

4.8
is
\left\lparen 1-\frac{2d}{n_{N}n}\right\rparen^{\eta n_{N}n/2}
-correct.

As long as

Proof.

First, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pr[K\neq\hat{K}\land F=1]&=\Pr\left[F(P^{\varepsilon,N})\neq F(\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N})\land\lvert\{{i\in I}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert\leq\gamma\theta n/2\land\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}=P^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}\right]\\
&\leq\Pr[P^{\varepsilon,N}\neq\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N}\land\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}=P^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}]\end{split}

Note that

(-(1+a/b)\hat{p})^{\varepsilon,N}
is corrected to something different from

\varDelta(\bar{p}^{\varepsilon,N},p^{\varepsilon,N})\geq d
. Hence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pr[K\neq\hat{K}\land F=1]&\leq\Pr[\varDelta(P^{\varepsilon,N},\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N})\geq d\land\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}=P^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}]\\
&\leq\frac{\binom{n_{N}n/2-d}{\eta n_{N}n/2}}{\binom{n_{N}n/2}{\eta n_{N}n/2}}\leq\left\lparen 1-\frac{2d}{n_{N}n}\right\rparen^{\eta n_{N}n/2}.\end{split}

(46)

∎

We first verify completeness in the protocol when there is no noise on the quantum communication channel.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\alpha>0
and let
\pi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a probability density function that is decreasing in the sense that if
|x|\geq|y|
, then
\pi(x)\leq\pi(y)
. Then,

\displaystyle\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor x+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor y+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert e^{-\alpha(x-y)^{2}}\pi(x)dxdy\leq\frac{6}{\alpha}

(47)

Proof.

Naively, we can try to approximate
\left\lvert\left\lfloor x+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor y+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert
by
|x-y|
. However, this is not an upper bound. Even if we were to take a scalar multiple of
|x-y|
as the candidate upper bound, there are values of
x
and
y
where the bound does not hold. To remedy that, we use the property that
\pi(x)
is decreasing. First, note that
\left\lvert\left\lfloor x+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor y+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert\leq 3|x-y|
except for on an infinite sequence of regions contained in
\left\lvert|x-n-1/2|+|y-n-1/2|\right\rvert\leq 1/3
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. To cover those regions as well, we upper bound them by the integrals on
\left\lvert|x-n-1/3|+|y-n-1/3|\right\rvert\leq 1/3
and
\left\lvert|x-n+1/3|+|y-n+1/3|\right\rvert\leq 1/3
, which is a bound as
\pi(x)
decreases with
|x|
. As such,

\displaystyle\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor x+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor y+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert e^{-\alpha(x-y)^{2}}\pi(x)dxdy\leq\iint 6|x-y|e^{-\alpha(x-y)^{2}}\pi(x)dxdy.

(48)

To finish,

\displaystyle\iint 6|x-y|e^{-\alpha(x-y)^{2}}\pi(x)dxdy=12\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}ue^{-\alpha u^{2}}du\pi(x)dx=\frac{6}{\alpha}.

(49)

∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Suppose that the error parameter
\gamma>\frac{6}{\sqrt{2\pi b}\delta}
, and the error-correcting code distance is
{d>\frac{n}{2}\frac{3\sqrt{a(1+a/b)}}{\pi^{3/2}\varepsilon}}
. Then,
4.8
is
(\mathrm{id},p)
-complete,
where

\displaystyle p=\exp\left\lparen-\left\lparen\gamma-\frac{6}{\sqrt{2\pi b}\delta}\right\rparen^{2}\right\rparen^{\theta n}+\exp\left\lparen-\left\lparen\tfrac{2d}{nn_{N}}-\tfrac{3\sqrt{a(1+a/b)}}{n_{N}\pi^{3/2}\varepsilon}\right\rparen\right\rparen^{n}\;.

(50)

If the conditions in the theorem are satisfied, then we get exponentially good completeness in the case of no errors.

Proof.

The probability of aborting is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pr\left[F=0\right]&=\Pr\left[\lvert\{{i\in I}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert>\gamma\theta n/2\lor P_{J}^{\varepsilon,N}\neq\bar{P}_{J}^{\varepsilon,N}\right]\\
&\leq\Pr\left[\lvert\{{i\in I}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert>\gamma\theta n/2\right]+\Pr\left[P^{\varepsilon,N}\neq\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N}\right]\end{split}

(51)

For each
i
, let
\Gamma_{i}
be the random variable that is
0
if
Q_{i}^{\delta}=\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}
and
1
otherwise. Writing
\pi(q)=e^{-aq^{2}}/\int_{-M\delta}^{M\delta}e^{-at^{2}}dt
for the distribution of position quadratures, the expectation value

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbbm{E}\Gamma_{i}&\leq\mathbbm{E}|Q_{i}^{\delta}-\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}|=\mathbbm{E}\left\lvert\left\lfloor\tfrac{Q_{i}}{\delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\tfrac{\hat{Q}_{i}}{\delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert\\
&=\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor\tfrac{q}{\delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\tfrac{\hat{q}}{\delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert\left\lvert\vphantom{\hat{q}b,q,0}\left\langle\smash{\hat{q}}\middle|\smash{b,q,0}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}\pi(q)dqd\hat{q}\\
&=\sqrt{\tfrac{2b}{\pi}}\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor\tfrac{q}{\delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\tfrac{\hat{q}}{\delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert e^{-2b(q-\hat{q})^{2}}\pi(q)dqd\hat{q}\\
&=\sqrt{\tfrac{2b}{\pi}}\delta\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor x+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor y+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert e^{-2b\delta^{2}(x-y)^{2}}\delta\pi(\delta x)dxdy,\end{split}

(52)

using the change of variables
x=q/\delta
,
y=\hat{q}/\delta
. So, we can apply
Lemma

4.10
and get
\mathbbm{E}\Gamma_{i}\leq\frac{6}{\sqrt{2\pi b}\delta}
. As the random variables are independent, we can invoke Hoeffding’s inequality in the case that
\gamma>\frac{6}{\sqrt{2\pi b}\delta}
and get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pr\left[\lvert\{{i\in I}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert>\gamma\theta\tfrac{n}{2}\right]&=\Pr\Big[\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i}-\theta n/2\mathbbm{E}\Gamma_{i}>(\gamma-\mathbbm{E}\Gamma_{i})\theta\tfrac{n}{2}\Big]\leq e^{-\tfrac{2\left\lparen(\gamma-\mathbbm{E}\Gamma_{i})\theta n/2\right\rparen^{2}}{\theta n/2}}\\
&=e^{-(\gamma-\mathbbm{E}\Gamma_{i})^{2}\theta n}\leq\exp\left\lparen-\left\lparen\gamma-\frac{6}{\sqrt{2\pi b}\delta}\right\rparen^{2}\right\rparen^{\theta n}.\end{split}

(53)

Now, we bound the other term. Due to the error-correcting code, the event
P^{\varepsilon,N}\neq\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N}
implies
\varDelta(P^{\varepsilon,N},(-(1+\tfrac{a}{b})\hat{P})^{\varepsilon,N})\geq d
. Let the random variable
\Delta_{i}=\varDelta(P_{i}^{\varepsilon,N},(-(1+\tfrac{a}{b})\hat{P}_{i})^{\varepsilon,N})
. This is a random variable supported on the interval
[0,n_{N}]
. Then, if
\tfrac{2d}{n}>\mathbbm{E}\Delta_{i}
, Hoeffding’s inequality gives that
\Pr\left[\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}\geq d-\tfrac{n}{2}\mathbbm{E}\Delta_{i}\right]\leq e^{-\tfrac{n}{n_{N}^{2}}(2d/n-\mathbbm{E}\Delta_{i})^{2}}
. It remains to compute
\mathbbm{E}\Delta_{i}
. We have, writing
\pi(p)=e^{-\tfrac{2\pi^{2}}{a+b}p^{2}}/\int_{-N\varepsilon}^{N\varepsilon}e^{-\tfrac{2\pi^{2}}{a+b}t^{2}}dt
for the distribution of the momentum quadratures,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbbm{E}\Delta_{i}&=\mathbbm{E}\varDelta((P_{i})^{\varepsilon,N},(-(1+\tfrac{a}{b})\hat{P}_{i})^{\varepsilon,N})\\
&\leq\mathbbm{E}\left\lvert(P_{i})^{\varepsilon}-(-(1+\tfrac{a}{b})\hat{P}_{i})^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert=\mathbbm{E}\left\lvert\left\lfloor\tfrac{P_{i}}{\varepsilon}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\tfrac{-(1+a/b)\hat{P}_{i}}{\varepsilon}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert\\
&=\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor\tfrac{p}{\varepsilon}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\tfrac{-(1+a/b)\hat{p}}{\varepsilon}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert\left\lvert\vphantom{\hat{p}\tfrac{ab}{a+b},0,-\tfrac{bp}{a+b}}\left\langle\smash{\hat{p}}\middle|\smash{\tfrac{ab}{a+b},0,-\tfrac{bp}{a+b}}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}\pi(p)dpd\hat{p}\\
&=\sqrt{2\pi\left\lparen\tfrac{1}{a}+\tfrac{1}{b}\right\rparen}\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor\tfrac{p}{\varepsilon}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\tfrac{-(1+a/b)\hat{p}}{\varepsilon}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert e^{-2\pi^{2}\left\lparen\tfrac{1}{a}+\tfrac{1}{b}\right\rparen\left\lparen\hat{p}+\tfrac{1}{1+a/b}p\right\rparen^{2}}\pi(p)dpd\hat{p}\\
&=\sqrt{\tfrac{2\pi}{a(1+a/n)}}\varepsilon\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor x+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor y+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert\varepsilon\pi(\varepsilon x)e^{-\tfrac{2\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{a(1+a/b)}(x-y)^{2}}dxdy,\end{split}

(54)

using a change of variables
x=p/\varepsilon
,
y=-\left\lparen 1+a/b\right\rparen\hat{p}/\varepsilon
. Then, as
x\mapsto\varepsilon\pi(\varepsilon x)
is a probability density function that satisfies the hypothesis of
Lemma

4.10
, we can apply that and get that
\mathbbm{E}\Delta_{i}\leq\frac{3\sqrt{a(1+a/b)}}{\pi^{3/2}\varepsilon}
.
∎

Proof.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbbm{E}\Gamma_{i}&\leq\frac{1}{\pi xy}\iint\iint\left\lvert\left\lfloor\tfrac{q}{\delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\tfrac{\hat{q}}{\delta}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rvert\left\lvert\vphantom{\hat{q}D(\xi,\phi)b,q,0}\left\langle\smash{\hat{q}}\middle\rvert\smash{D(\xi,\phi)}\middle\rvert\smash{b,q,0}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}\pi(q)dqd\hat{q}e^{-(\xi/x)^{2}-(\phi/y)^{2}}d\xi d\phi\\

(57)

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\lvert\{{i\in[n]}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert\leq(\gamma+\tau)n/2\land P^{\varepsilon}=P_{C}^{\varepsilon}\right]_{\sigma}\leq p_{0}.

(66)

Due to the bounds we have imposed, this gives that

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\lvert\{{i\in[n]}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}=\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert\leq(\gamma+\tau)n/2\land P^{\varepsilon,N}=P_{C}^{\varepsilon,N}\right]_{\rho}\leq Cp_{0}.

(67)

Fix the subset
I\subseteq[n]
that Bob checks and let
\Omega
be the event

\displaystyle\tfrac{2}{n}\lvert\{{i\in[n]}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert\leq\tfrac{2}{\theta n}\lvert\{{i\in I}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert+\tau.

(68)

Using Hoeffding’s inequality, we can see that
\Pr\left[\lnot\Omega\right]_{\rho}\leq e^{-\tau^{2}\theta n}
, giving

\displaystyle\left\|\rho-\rho_{|\Omega}\right\|_{\Tr}=\left\|\left\lparen 1-\tfrac{1}{\Pr[\Omega]_{\rho}}\right\rparen\rho_{\land\Omega}+\rho_{\land\lnot\Omega}\right\|=\left\lparen\tfrac{1}{\Pr[\Omega]_{\rho}}-1\right\rparen\Pr[\Omega]_{\rho}+\Pr[\lnot\Omega]_{\rho}=2\Pr[\lnot\Omega]_{\rho}\leq 2e^{\tau^{2}\theta n}.

(69)

On
\rho_{\land\Omega}
, we get

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\lvert\{{i\in I}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert\leq\gamma\theta n/2\land P^{\varepsilon,N}=P_{C}^{\varepsilon,N}\right]_{\rho_{\land\Omega}}\leq Cp_{0},

(70)

Now, let
\Omega_{0}
be the event
\lvert\{{i\in I}|{Q_{i}^{\delta}\neq\hat{Q}_{i}^{\delta}}\}\rvert\leq\gamma\theta n/2
. Fixing the measurement Bob makes to guess
q
, write
p_{1}=\Pr\left[\Omega_{0}\right]_{\rho_{\land\Omega}}
so
\frac{Cp_{0}}{p_{1}}\geq\Pr\left[P^{\varepsilon,N}=P_{C}^{\varepsilon,N}\Big|\Omega_{0}\right]_{\rho_{\land\Omega}}
. Therefore, we have the min-entropy relation

\displaystyle H_{\min}(P^{\varepsilon,N}|SE)_{\rho_{|\Omega_{0}\land\Omega}}\geq-\lg\tfrac{p_{0}}{p_{1}}=-\lg Cp_{0}+\lg p_{1}.

(71)

Until the privacy amplification, Eve also receives
(q_{I})^{\delta,M}
(
\theta n_{M}n/2
bits),
\mathrm{syn}(p^{\varepsilon,N})
(
s
bits),
p^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}
(
\eta n_{N}n/2
bits). Giving Eve’s register
E^{\prime}=SI(Q_{I})^{\delta,M}\mathrm{syn}(P^{\varepsilon,N})JP^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}E
and hence by chain rule,

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{\min}(P^{\varepsilon,N}|E^{\prime})_{\rho_{|\Omega_{0}\land\Omega}}&\geq-\lg Cp_{0}+\lg p_{1}-\left[\theta n_{M}+\tfrac{2s}{n}+\eta n_{N}\right]\tfrac{n}{2}.\end{split}

(72)

If
P^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}\neq\bar{P}^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}
, Bob aborts, but as
\rho_{|\Omega_{0}\land(\Omega\land P^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}=\bar{P}_{J}^{\varepsilon,N})}\leq\rho_{|\Omega_{0}\land\Omega}
, this does not change the entropy relation. Let
\Omega_{1}
be the event
P^{\varepsilon,N}_{J}=\bar{P}_{J}^{\varepsilon,N}
.
Now, using the universal hash lemma,

\displaystyle\|\rho_{e(Q^{\varepsilon,N},R)RE^{\prime}|\Omega_{0}\land(\Omega_{1}\land\Omega)}-\mu_{K}\otimes\mu_{R}\otimes\rho_{E^{\prime}|\Omega_{0}\land(\Omega_{1}\land\Omega)}\|_{\Tr}\leq 2^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lparen-\lg Cp_{0}+\lg p_{1}-\left[\theta n_{M}+\tfrac{2s}{n}+\eta n_{N}\right]\tfrac{n}{2}-(\ell-2)\right\rparen}.

(73)

Combining this with the other case and noting that Eve’s final register is
E^{\prime\prime}=RE^{\prime}
gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\rho_{KE^{\prime\prime}\land(F=1\land\Omega)}-\mu_{R}\otimes\rho_{E^{\prime\prime}\land(F=1\land\Omega)}\|_{\Tr}&\leq 2^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lparen-\lg Cp_{0}-\left[\theta n_{M}+\tfrac{2s}{n}+\eta n_{N}\right]\tfrac{n}{2}-(\ell-2)\right\rparen}\\
&=\sqrt{C}2^{\tfrac{n}{4}\left[(1-\gamma-\tau)\lg\left\lparen\tfrac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\delta\varepsilon}\right\rparen+h(\gamma+\tau)+\theta n_{M}+\tfrac{2s}{n}+\eta n_{N}+\tfrac{2(\ell-2)}{n}+\tfrac{1}{(\ln 2)n}\right]}\end{split}

(74)

Now, we can remove the event
\Omega
using the trace norm bound:
\|\rho_{KE^{\prime\prime}\land(F=1)}-\mu_{R}\otimes\rho_{E^{\prime\prime}\land(F=1)}\|_{\Tr}\leq\sqrt{C}2^{\tfrac{n}{4}\left[(1-\gamma-\tau)\lg\left\lparen\tfrac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\delta\varepsilon}\right\rparen+h(\gamma+\tau)+\theta M+\tfrac{2s}{n}+\eta N+\tfrac{2(\ell-2)}{n}+\tfrac{1}{(\ln 2)n}\right]}+4e^{-\tau^{2}\theta n}
. Finally, it remains to bound
C
. First, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{C}&=\Pr[\|Q\|_{\infty}\leq M\delta\land\|P\|_{\infty}\leq N\varepsilon]=\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{c}}\Pr[|Q_{i}|\leq M\delta]\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Pr[|P_{i}|\leq N\varepsilon]\\
&=\left\lparen 1-2\sqrt{\tfrac{2a}{\pi}}\int_{M\delta}^{\infty}e^{-2aq^{2}}dq\right\rparen^{n/2}\left\lparen 1-2\sqrt{\tfrac{a+b}{2\pi}}\int_{N\varepsilon}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{a+b}p^{2}}dp\right\rparen^{n/2}\\
&\geq\left\lparen 1-2\sqrt{\tfrac{2a}{\pi}}\int_{M\delta}^{\infty}\frac{q}{M\delta}e^{-2aq^{2}}dq\right\rparen^{n/2}\left\lparen 1-2\sqrt{\tfrac{a+b}{2\pi}}\int_{N\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{p}{N\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{a+b}p^{2}}dp\right\rparen^{n/2}\\
&=\left\lparen 1-\tfrac{e^{-2aM^{2}\delta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi aM^{2}\delta^{2}}}\right\rparen^{n/2}\left\lparen 1-\sqrt{\tfrac{a+b}{\pi^{3}N^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{a+b}N^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rparen^{n/2}.\end{split}

(75)

Thus, we may bound

\displaystyle\sqrt{C}=2^{\tfrac{1}{2}\lg C}\leq 2^{-\tfrac{n}{4}\left[\lg\left\lparen 1-\tfrac{e^{-2aM^{2}\delta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi aM^{2}\delta^{2}}}\right\rparen+\lg\left\lparen 1-\sqrt{\tfrac{a+b}{\pi^{3}N^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{a+b}N^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rparen\right]},

(76)

giving the result.
∎

5
The coset monogamy game on
\mathbb{R}

As in the previous two sections, we study oscillators, concentrating this time on a single mode, that corresponds to the real group
\mathbb{R}
. The only proper non-trivial closed subgroups in this case are discrete and infinite, corresponding to the integers
\mathbb{Z}
. This yields a connection to the GKP code
[
GKP01
]
, whose coset states correspond to lattices in the CV phase space.

5.1
GKP states and coset states

Quantum states of the oscillator are normalized elements of the square-integrable functions of the real line
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Nevertheless, as before, it often useful to consider “bases” of this space composed of non-normalizable states. The two canonical choices are the position states
q(x)=\delta(q-x)
with normalization
\vphantom{qq^{\prime}}\left\langle\smash{q}\middle|\smash{q^{\prime}}\right\rangle=\delta(q-q^{\prime})
and the momentum states
p(x)=e^{2\pi ipx}
with
\vphantom{pp^{\prime}}\left\langle\smash{p}\middle|\smash{p^{\prime}}\right\rangle=\delta(p-p^{\prime})
. The position states correspond to points in
\mathbb{R}
and the momentum states correspond to elements of the dual
\hat{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}
. The isomorphism takes the natural form
x\rightarrow\gamma_{x}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\gamma_{x}(y)=e^{2\pi ixy}
.

An important family of states on the oscillator are the GKP code states
[
GKP01
]
. They take the form

\displaystyle\lvert\alpha,d,k\rangle\propto\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\lvert q=(k+dn)\alpha\rangle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{d\alpha}n}\lvert p=\tfrac{n}{d\alpha}\rangle,

(77)

for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
k=0,\ldots,d-1
. Again, this is non-normalizable: in fact these states are infinite both in position and in momentum, as they are an equal superposition of infinitely many states in either basis. The standard way to handle this is to turn this into a normalizable state in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by damping the Dirac deltas
\lvert q\rangle
, replacing them with Gaussians
q_{a,b}(x)=e^{-aq^{2}}e^{-b(q-x)^{2}}
. As in the case of
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\Delta_{a,b}
be the operator that effects this transformation.

In the same way as subspace states are generalised to subspace coset states
[
CLLZ21
]
, GKP states can be generalised to subgroup coset states. The relevant subgroup is
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\leq\mathbb{R}
, as the GKP state is a countable superposition of position states. Then, the cosets
\mathbb{R}/\alpha\mathbb{Z}\cong U(1)
can be indexed by
[0,\alpha)
. Also, the dual group of
\hat{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}\cong\hat{\mathbb{R}}/(\alpha\mathbb{Z})^{\perp}\cong U(1)
, as
(\alpha\mathbb{Z})^{\perp}\cong\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
under the isomorphism
\hat{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}
, so the characters are indexed by
[0,1/\alpha)
. Thus, for
x\in[0,\alpha)
and
y\in[0,1/\alpha)
, the subspace coset states take the form

\displaystyle\lvert\alpha,x,y\rangle=\lvert x+\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{\gamma_{y}}\rangle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi iy\alpha n}\lvert q=x+\alpha n\rangle.

(78)

These satisfy orthogonality as well, in the form
\vphantom{\alpha,x,y\alpha,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\left\langle\smash{\alpha,x,y}\middle|\smash{\alpha,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\right\rangle=\delta(x-x^{\prime})\delta(y-y^{\prime})
, and can be transformed into normalizable states via the same damping operation. However, it is useful to again consider this basis as an operator measure, which will allow us to rigorously interact with these unnormalizable objects. As the basis is indexed by
[0,\alpha)\times[0,1/\alpha)
, the measurable sets are the Borel sets of this space
\mathscr{B}([0,\alpha)\times[0,1/\alpha))
, and the operator measure of a set
E\in\mathscr{B}([0,\alpha)\times[0,1/\alpha))
is the projector onto the "span" of the states
\lvert\alpha,x,y\rangle
such that
(x,y)\in E
:

\displaystyle A^{\alpha}(E)=\int_{E}\vphantom{\alpha,x,y}\left\lvert\smash{\alpha,x,y}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\alpha,x,y}\right\rvert d(x,y).

(79)

This is in fact a well-defined bounded operator. We go through the rigorous definition of this operator measure and the proof of this for a general abelian group in
Section

8
.

Alternately, we can again work with damped states
\lvert\alpha,x,y|_{a,b}\rangle:=\frac{c\Delta_{a,b}\lvert\alpha,x,y\rangle}{\|\Delta_{a,b}\lvert\alpha,x,y\rangle\|}
, distributed according to
\pi^{\alpha}_{a,b}(x,y)=\frac{\|\Delta_{a,b}\lvert\alpha,x,y\rangle\|^{2}}{\|\Delta_{a,b}\|_{2}^{2}}
.

5.2
Monogamy game analysis

The definition and analysis of the GKP state monogamy game is a bit more involved, as the general overlap bound we work out is not directly useful in analysing these states. First, we demonstrate where the difficulty arises, and then adapt the game and bound to make it work.

Naively, the basic GKP state monogamy game should take the following form. Fix
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{N}>0
real numbers. The GKP state monogamy game, played between a referee Alice and two cooperating players Bob and Charlie, proceeds as follows.

1.

Bob and Charlie prepare a shared state
\rho_{ABC}
but then are no longer allowed to communicate.

2.

Alice chooses
i=1,\ldots,N
uniformly at random and measures her register in basis
\left\{{\lvert\alpha_{i},x,y\rangle}\right\}
to get measurements
x,y
.

3.

Alice sends
i
to Bob and Charlie. Bob answers with a guess
x_{B}
for
x
and Charlie answers with a guess
y_{C}
for
y
.

4.

Bob and Charlie win if
|x-x_{B}|<\delta
and
|y-y_{C}|<\varepsilon
(modulo
\alpha_{i}
and
1/\alpha_{i}
, respectively).

Then, we might make use of the bound of
Theorem

8.7
to upper bound the game. Crucially, this bound relies on the overlap
c(\alpha,\beta):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\mu_{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha\mathbb{Z}\cap(x+(-\delta,\delta)+\beta\mathbb{Z}))\mu_{\hat{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}}((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))}
. However, for any
\delta>0
the set
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\cap((-\delta,\delta)+\beta\mathbb{Z})
is infinite. The cardinality, and hence the measure, of this set is equal to the number of
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\left\lvert\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\frac{m}{n}\right\rvert<\frac{\delta}{\beta n}
: for rational
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}
, there are infinitely many
n
such that for some
m
,
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}=\frac{m}{n}
; and for irrational
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}
, Dirichlet’s approximation theorem gives that there are infinitely many fractions
\frac{m}{n}
such that
\left\lvert\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\frac{m}{n}\right\rvert<\frac{1}{n^{2}}
, and thus since there must be infinitely many
n\geq\frac{\beta}{\delta}
such that this is satisfied,
\left\lvert\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\frac{m}{n}\right\rvert<\frac{1}{n^{2}}\leq\frac{\delta}{\beta n}
for these
n
. Hence, for any reasonable instantiation of the game, the bound is trivial.

Nevertheless, it is possible to get a nontrivial bound by working more directly with the state-sending version of the game. Most importantly, the damped GKP states only have significant support in a finite interval. Then, by translating the strategy for a state-sending version of the game to the original version using
Theorem

8.13
, we need only work with states that have significant support but in a particular finite interval. By projecting onto this interval, the overlap becomes finite and manageable, with a small perturbation of the winning probability. First, we present the state-sending game, then formalise the preceding argument.

1.

Alice chooses
i
uniformly at random and samples
(x,y)
according to the distribution
\pi^{\alpha_{i}}_{a,b}
. She prepares the state
\lvert\alpha_{i},x,y|_{a,b}\rangle
(
77
) and sends it to Bob and Charlie.

2.

Bob and Charlie attempt to split the state using an arbitrary channel
\Phi
, and then are no longer allowed to communicate.

3.

Alice sends
\alpha_{i}
to Bob and Charlie. Bob answers with a guess
x_{B}
for
x
and Charlie answers with a guess
y_{C}
for
y
.

4.

Bob and Charlie win if
|x-x_{B}|<\delta
and
|y-y_{C}|<\varepsilon
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}
be an ascending sequence of prime numbers. For any
M>0
, the winning probability of the GKP state-sending monogamy game

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{GKP})\leq\frac{1}{N}+2\sqrt{\left\lparen\alpha_{N}+\frac{2M}{\alpha_{1}}\right\rparen\varepsilon}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi a}}}e^{-aM^{2}}

(80)

This gives a good bound on the winning probability if
N\gg 1
,
\alpha_{i}\sim\sqrt{M}
,
\varepsilon^{2}\ll 1/M\ll a^{1/2}
. First, we prove a variant of the overlap lemma involving the projection onto the interval
[-M,M]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\varepsilon,\delta,M>0
. Let
E=(-\delta,\delta)
,
F=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
(which we might consider modulo
\alpha
,
\beta
,
1/\alpha
, or
1/\beta
implicitly depending on the context), and
\Pi\in\mathcal{P}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
be the projector
(\Pi\lvert\psi\rangle)(x)=\psi(x)
if
|x|\leq M
and
0
otherwise. Then, for any
r\in[0,1/\alpha)
,
s\in[0,\beta)
,

\displaystyle\|\Pi A^{\alpha}([0,\alpha)\times(r+F))\Pi A^{\beta}((s+E)\times[0,1/\beta))\Pi\|\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{2\alpha\varepsilon|(x+\alpha\mathbb{Z})\cap(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]|}.

(81)

Also, if
\alpha,\beta
are integers and
2\varepsilon\leq 1
, the bound simplifies to

\displaystyle\|\Pi A^{\alpha}([0,\alpha)\times(r+F))\Pi A^{\beta}((s+E)\times[0,1/\beta))\Pi\|\leq\sqrt{4\alpha\left\lparen 1+\frac{2M}{\mathrm{lcm}(\alpha,\beta)}\right\rparen\varepsilon}.

(82)

Proof.

We follow the method of
Lemma

8.9
, and additionally keep track of the projector
\Pi
. First, we have that
A^{\beta}((s+E)\times[0,1/\beta))=\Pi_{s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z}}
, so

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\Pi A^{\alpha}([0,\alpha)\times(r+F))\Pi A^{\beta}((s+E)\times[0,1/\beta))\Pi\|\\
&\qquad=\|\Pi A^{\alpha}([0,\alpha)\times(r+F))\Pi_{(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]}\|\\
&\qquad\leq\|A^{\alpha}([0,\alpha)\times(r+F))\Pi_{(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]}\|\end{split}

(83)

Thus, for any
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|A^{\alpha}([0,\alpha)\times(r+F))\Pi_{(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]}\lvert\psi\rangle\|^{2}\\
&\qquad=\int_{[0,\alpha)\times(r+F)}\left\lvert\int_{\alpha\mathbb{Z}\cap(s-x+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M-x,M-x]}\psi(x+h)\overline{\gamma_{y}(h)}d_{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}h\right\rvert^{2}d_{\mathbb{R}/\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\hat{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}}(x,y)\\
&\qquad=\int_{0}^{\alpha}\alpha\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\left\lvert\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{(s-x+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M-x,M-x]}(\alpha n)\psi(x+\alpha n))e^{-2\pi i\alpha n(r+y)}\right\rvert^{2}dydx\\
&\qquad\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{\alpha}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\psi(x+\alpha n))|^{2}\sup_{x}|\alpha\mathbb{Z}\cap(s-x+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M-x,M-x]|dydx\\
&\qquad=\|\lvert\psi\rangle\|^{2}2\alpha\varepsilon\sup_{x}|(x+\alpha\mathbb{Z})\cap(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]|.\end{split}

(84)

Hence, we get the bound
\sup_{x}\sqrt{2\alpha\varepsilon|(x+\alpha\mathbb{Z})\cap(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]|}
on the overlap.

It remains to bound
|(x+\alpha\mathbb{Z})\cap(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]|
in the case where
\alpha,\beta
are integers. First, note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|(x+\alpha\mathbb{Z})\cap(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]|&=\left\lvert\{{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|{|x+\alpha n|\leq M;\;\exists m\in\mathbb{Z}.\;|(x+\alpha n)-(s+\beta m)|<\delta}\}\right\rvert\\
&\leq 1+\left\lvert\{{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|{|n|\leq\tfrac{2M}{\alpha};\;\exists m\in\mathbb{Z}.\;|\alpha n-\beta m|<2\delta}\}\right\rvert\\
&\leq 2+2\left\lvert\{{n\in\mathbb{N}}|{n\leq\tfrac{2M}{\alpha};\;\exists m\in\mathbb{Z}.\;|\alpha n-\beta m|<2\delta}\}\right\rvert,\end{split}

(85)

where the inequality following from noting that the difference of any two elements of the left hand set is an element of the right hand set. Now, using the hypothesis,
|\alpha n-\beta m|<2\delta
if and only if
\alpha n-\beta m=0
, that is if
\frac{m}{n}=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}
. Thus, the elements of the set take the form
n=\frac{k\beta}{\mathrm{gcd}(\alpha,\beta)}
, so

\displaystyle\begin{split}|(x+\alpha\mathbb{Z})\cap(s+E+\beta\mathbb{Z})\cap[-M,M]|&\leq 2+2\left\lvert\{{k\in\mathbb{N}}|{k\leq\tfrac{2M\mathrm{gcd}(\alpha,\beta)}{\alpha\beta}}\}\right\rvert\leq 2+\frac{4M}{\mathrm{lcm}(\alpha,\beta)}.\end{split}

(86)

∎

Now, we can proceed to the proof of the theorem.

Proof of
Theorem

5.1
.

Fixing a strategy for the state-sending game, the construction of
Theorem

8.13
gives a reexpression as a strategy for the original monogamy game:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}_{GKP}}(\texttt{S})=\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Tr\big[(A^{\alpha_{i}}\otimes B^{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}}\otimes C^{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}})(E_{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}})(\mathbb{I}\otimes\Phi)(\vphantom{\Psi_{a,b}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\right\rvert)\big],

(87)

where
\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle
is the maximally-entangled state corresponding to the damping operator
\Delta_{a,b}
. Now, using the projector
\Pi=\Pi_{[-M,M]}
and writing
P_{i}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi^{\dagger})\left\lparen(A^{\alpha_{i}}\otimes B^{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}}\otimes C^{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}})(E_{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}})\right\rparen
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}_{GKP}}&(\texttt{S})=\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\left\lparen\vphantom{\Psi_{a,b}\Pi P_{i}\Pi\Psi_{a,b}}\left\langle\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\middle\rvert\smash{\Pi P_{i}\Pi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\right\rangle+\vphantom{\Psi_{a,b}(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)P_{i}\Pi\Psi_{a,b}}\left\langle\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\middle\rvert\smash{(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)P_{i}\Pi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\right\rangle+\vphantom{\Psi_{a,b}P_{i}(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\Psi_{a,b}}\left\langle\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\middle\rvert\smash{P_{i}(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)}\middle\rvert\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\right\rangle\right\rparen\\
&\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\left\lparen\vphantom{\Psi_{a,b}\Pi P_{i}\Pi\Psi_{a,b}}\left\langle\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\middle\rvert\smash{\Pi P_{i}\Pi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\right\rangle+\left\|(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle\right\|\left\|P_{i}\Pi\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle\right\|+\left\|P_{i}\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle\right\|\left\|(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle\right\|\right\rparen\\
&\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Tr\big[(\Pi A^{\alpha_{i}}\Pi\otimes B^{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}}\otimes C^{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}})(E_{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}})(\mathbb{I}\otimes\Phi)(\vphantom{\Psi_{a,b}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\right\rvert)\big]+2\left\|(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle\right\|.\end{split}

(88)

First, we bound the second term. Using
Appendix

B
, the singular-value decomposition
\Delta_{a,b}=\sum_{i}s_{i}\vphantom{\phi_{i}\chi_{i}}\left\lvert\smash{\phi_{i}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\chi_{i}}\right\rvert
gives
\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{a,b}\|_{2}}\sum_{i}s_{n,i}\lvert\phi_{i}\rangle\otimes c\lvert\chi_{i}\rangle
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle\|^{2}=\vphantom{\Psi_{a,b}\mathbb{I}-\Pi\Psi_{a,b}}\left\langle\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\middle\rvert\smash{\mathbb{I}-\Pi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{a,b}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{i}s_{i}^{2}\vphantom{\phi_{i}\mathbb{I}-\Pi\phi_{i}}\left\langle\smash{\phi_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{\mathbb{I}-\Pi}\middle\rvert\smash{\phi_{i}}\right\rangle=\frac{\Tr[\Delta_{a,b}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\Delta_{a,b}]}{\Tr[\Delta_{a,b}^{\dagger}\Delta_{a,b}]}.

(89)

So, we have
\Tr[\Delta_{a,b}^{\dagger}\Delta_{a,b}]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{a,b}\lvert x\rangle\|^{2}dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2b}}e^{-2\bar{a}x^{2}}dx=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\bar{a}b}},
and similarly

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Tr[\Delta_{a,b}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\Delta_{a,b}]&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\Delta_{a,b}\lvert x\rangle\|^{2}dx=2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{M}^{\infty}e^{-2\tilde{a}x^{2}-2b(x-y)^{2}}dydx\\
&=2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2(\tilde{a}+b)}}\int_{M}^{\infty}e^{-2\frac{\tilde{a}b}{\tilde{a}+b}y^{2}}dy\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2(\tilde{a}+b)}}\int_{M}^{\infty}\frac{y}{M}e^{-2\frac{\tilde{a}b}{\tilde{a}+b}y^{2}}dy\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{\pi(\tilde{a}+b)/2}}{4\tilde{a}bM}e^{-2\frac{\tilde{a}b}{\tilde{a}+b}M^{2}},\end{split}

(90)

which gives
2\|(\mathbb{I}-\Pi)\lvert\Psi_{a,b}\rangle\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi a}}}e^{-aM^{2}}
.

For the first term, we follow the template of
Theorem

8.7
, replacing the use of
Lemma

8.9
with
Lemma

5.2
. We take the trivial set of orthogonal permutations
\pi_{i}(\alpha_{j})=\alpha_{i+j}
, where the addition is modulo
N
. Then,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Tr\big[(\Pi A^{\alpha_{i}}\Pi\otimes B^{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}}\otimes C^{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}})(E_{\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Z}})(\mathbb{I}\otimes\Phi)(\vphantom{\Psi_{a,b}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{a,b}}\right\rvert)\big]\\
&\qquad\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\sup_{j=1,\ldots,N;r,s\in\mathbb{R}}\|\Pi A^{\alpha_{i}}([0,\alpha_{i})\times(r+F))\Pi\Pi A^{\alpha_{i+j}}((s+E)\times[0,1/\alpha_{i+j}))\Pi\|\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\neq 0}\sup_{j}\sqrt{4\left\lparen\alpha_{i}+\frac{2M}{\alpha_{i+j}}\right\rparen\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{N}+2\sqrt{\left\lparen\alpha_{N}+\frac{2M}{\alpha_{1}}\right\rparen\varepsilon},\end{split}

(91)

giving the wanted result.
∎

6
The coset monogamy game on
SO(3)

We move on to study the case
G=SO(3)
— a compact Lie group whose elements label the distinct orientations of a rigid body. This case is akin to the planar rotor (see
Section

2
) in that the position states are labelled by elements of a compact space, meaning that the dual irreducible representation indices are discrete. However, there is an extra complication of the group being non-abelian, meaning that irreducible representations become matrix-valued.

6.1
Rigid rotor states

We consider a molecule with no symmetries as a rigid body in 3D. Then, the configuration space of rotational states of the molecule corresponds to the rotation group
G=SO(3)
. As before, the associated Hilbert space
L^{2}(SO(3))
can be spanned by the unnormalizable position eigenstates
\lvert R\rangle
,
R\in SO(3)
. The dual basis consists of the angular momentum eigenstates, given by the Wigner
D
-matrices extending the spherical harmonic basis for
S^{2}\cong SO(3)/U(1)
. For
\ell\geq 0
,
-\ell\leq m,n\leq\ell
,

\displaystyle\lvert^{\ell}_{m,n}\rangle=\sqrt{\tfrac{2\ell+1}{8\pi^{2}}}\int D^{\ell}_{m,n}(R)\lvert R\rangle dR.

(92)

These form a bone fide orthonormal basis.

As before, we can consider coset states of an arbitrary closed subgroup
H\leq G
. Since there are multiple types of interesting subgroups, corresponding to the proper point groups, we will not for the moment specialise to a particular group structure. As for finite non-abelian groups, the characters of the continuous representations no longer span
L^{2}(H)
; however the matrix elements of the representations do. Write
\textsf{IB}(H)=\{{\gamma_{m,n}}\}
for the set of matrix elements of a full collection of inequivalent continuous finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
H
. By the Peter-Weyl theorem, these matrix elements are complete and orthogonal in the sense that

\displaystyle\int_{H}\overline{\gamma_{m,n}(h)}\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}(h)dh=d_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}},

(93)

where
d_{\gamma}
is the dimension of the representation
\gamma
. Note that, unlike the abelian case, if
G
were not compact,
H
may not be compact, and hence these properties may not hold. On the other hand,
H
is not necessarily normal, so
G/H
will not be a group. Since we cannot appeal to the dual group structure as in the abelian case, we fix a set of coset representatives
\textsf{CS}(H)
. We can find simple fundamental domains in all the relevant cases
[
ACP20
]
.

The coset states, indexed by
R\in\textsf{CS}(H)
and
\gamma_{m,n}\in\textsf{IB}(H)
, generalise as

\displaystyle\lvert RH^{\gamma}_{m,n}\rangle=\sqrt{d_{\gamma}}\int_{H}\gamma_{m,n}(h)\lvert Rh\rangle dh.

(94)

However, other than the
G=H
case corresponding to the angular momentum basis, these are not normalizable states: we have
\vphantom{RH^{\gamma}_{m,n}R^{\prime}H^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}\left\langle\smash{RH^{\gamma}_{m,n}}\middle|\smash{R^{\prime}H^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}\right\rangle=\delta_{G/H}(R^{-1}R^{\prime}H)\delta_{\gamma_{m,n},\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}
. Hence, as in the case of infinite abelian groups, we make use of the coset measure. Here, we can endow the coset representatives
\textsf{CS}(H)
with a continuous measure induced by the quotient measure on the symmetric space
SO(3)/H
; and
\textsf{IB}(H)
can simply be given a discrete structure via the counting measure. For a measurable set
E=\bigcup_{\gamma_{m,n}}E_{\gamma_{m,n}}\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\subseteq\textsf{CS}(H)\times\textsf{IB}(H)
, the operator measure is

\displaystyle A^{H}(E)=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}\int_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}}\vphantom{RH^{\gamma}_{m,n}}\left\lvert\smash{RH^{\gamma}_{m,n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{RH^{\gamma}_{m,n}}\right\rvert dR.

(95)

6.2
Monogamy game analysis

To construct a coset monogamy game for
SO(3)
, we first need to choose a suitable collection of subgroups. One way to do this is to consider subgroups isomorphic to
U(1)
. The canonical example of this is the subgroup of rotations around the
z
axis,
H_{0}=\left\{{Z(\theta)}\middle|{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\right\}
, where

\displaystyle Z(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta&0\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

(96)

We can choose the collection of subgroups to be rotations of this group around the
x
axis,
H_{\theta}=X(\theta)H_{0}X(-\theta)
, where

\displaystyle X(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
0&\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{bmatrix}.

(97)

Since these groups are all copies of the abelian group
U(1)
, we have that cosets form the symmetric space isomorphic to the sphere,
SO(3)/H_{\theta}\cong S^{2}
, and the irreducible representations are simply the characters, indexed by
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and acting as
\gamma_{n}(X(\theta)Z(\varphi)X(\theta))=e^{in\varphi}
. Using Euler angles
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
to parameterize arbitrary 3D rotations, we can write

R=RX(\theta)X(-\theta)=Z(\alpha)X(\beta)Z(\gamma)X(-\theta)
for
R\in SO(3)
.
This yields the coset
RH_{\theta}=\left\{{Z(\alpha)X(\beta)Z(\varphi)X(-\theta)}\middle|{\varphi\in[0,2\pi)}\right\}
, parametrized by the two angles
\alpha,\beta
. This gives coset states of the form

\displaystyle\lvert\theta,\alpha,\beta,n\rangle=\lvert Z(\alpha)X(\beta-\theta)H_{\theta}^{\gamma_{n}}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{in\varphi}\lvert Z(\alpha)X(\beta)Z(\varphi)X(-\theta)\rangle d\varphi.

(98)

For some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, fix the set of subgroups
\mathcal{S}_{N}=\left\{{H_{\theta_{k}}}\middle|{k=0,\ldots,N-1}\right\}
, where
\theta_{k}=\frac{2\pi k}{N}
. It remains to fix the neighborhood of unity corresponding to the correct answers: we take it to be the ball of radius
\varepsilon>0
in an appropriate metric
E_{\varepsilon}=\left\{{R\in SO(3)}\middle|{d(R,I)<\varepsilon}\right\}
. There are a variety of options for this metric; however, since
SO(3)
is compact, they are all equivalent. We choose a metric that arises naturally from the quaternion representation of rotations. There is a surjective group homomorphism from the unit quaternions to
SO(3)
that acts as
\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\mapsto Z(2\theta)
,
\cos\theta+k\sin\theta\mapsto X(2\theta)
. Using the Euclidean norm on the quaternions, we take the metric to be the distance between the preimages,
i.e.
for
R,S\in SO(3)
, if
p\mapsto R
and
q\mapsto S
,
d(R,S):=\min\{\|p-q\|_{2},\|p+q\|_{2}\}
. A nice property of this metric is that it is translation-invariant by construction.

The game then proceeds as usual.

1.

Bob and Charlie prepare a shared state
\rho_{ABC}
but then are no longer allowed to communicate.

2.

Alice chooses
k=1,\ldots,N
uniformly at random and measures her register in basis
\left\{{\lvert\theta_{k},\alpha,\beta,n\rangle}\right\}
to get measurements
\alpha,\beta,n
.

3.

Alice sends
k
to Bob and Charlie. Bob answers with a guess
(\alpha_{B},\beta_{B})
for
(\alpha,\beta)
and Charlie answers with a guess
n_{C}
for
n
.

4.

Bob and Charlie win if there exists
\varphi
such that
d\left\lparen Z(\alpha_{B})X(\beta_{B}),Z(\alpha)X(\beta)Z(\varphi)\right\rparen<\varepsilon
and
n=n_{C}

We bound the winning probability of this game using
Theorem

9.5
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be even and
0<\varepsilon<2\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2N}
. Let the compact coset monogamy game
\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon}=(SO(3),\mathcal{S}_{N},E_{\varepsilon})
. Then, the winning probability

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\leq\frac{2}{N}+2\sqrt{\pi\varepsilon}.

(99)

First, we compute the overlaps.

Lemma 6.2
.

For
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{R\in SO(3)}\mu_{H_{0}}(H_{0}\cap RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }|\cos\theta|>\cos\eta\\
\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{1-\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\eta}{\sin^{2}\theta}}}&\text{else}\end{cases},

(100)

where
\cos\tfrac{\eta}{2}=1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}

Proof.

First, we can assume that
\sin\theta>0
: if
\sin\theta=0
, we have
H_{\theta}=H_{0}
and
|\cos\theta|=1
, so we have that the measure is
1
; if
\sin\theta<0
, we can conjugate by
Z(\pi)
, which does not change
H_{0}
and sends
H_{\theta}\mapsto H_{-\theta}
. To get a description of
H_{0}\cap RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta}
, we note that

\displaystyle RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta}=\{g\in SO(3)|d(g,RX(\theta)Z(\varphi)X(-\theta))<\varepsilon\text{ for some }\varphi\},

(101)

so
H_{0}\cap RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta}
is the set of elements of
H_{0}
that are less than
\varepsilon
away from some element of
RH_{\theta}
. First, we find the distance between arbitrary elements of
H_{0}
and
RH_{\theta}
. There exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in[0,2\pi)
such that
R=Z(\alpha)X(\beta)Z(\gamma)X(-\theta)
; therefore as every element of
H_{\theta}
is of the form
X(\theta)Z(\chi-\gamma)X(-\theta)
for some
\chi\in[0,2\pi)
,
Z(\alpha)X(\beta)Z(\chi)X(-\theta)
parametrizes all the elements of
RH_{\theta}
. Similarly, every element of
H_{0}
may be expressed
Z(\varphi+\alpha)
for some
\varphi\in[0,2\pi)
, so the distance between those elements is

\displaystyle\begin{split}d\left\lparen Z(\varphi+\alpha),Z(\alpha)X(\beta)Z(\chi)X(-\theta)\right\rparen&=d\left\lparen Z(\varphi)X(\theta),X(\beta)Z(\chi)\right\rparen\\
&\hskip-28.45274pt=\min_{\pm}\sqrt{2(1\pm\cos\tfrac{\chi}{2}\cos\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\cos\tfrac{\theta-\beta}{2}\pm\sin\tfrac{\chi}{2}\sin\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\cos\tfrac{\theta+\beta}{2})}.\end{split}

(102)

To find how close
g=Z(\varphi+\alpha)
is to any element of
RH_{\theta}
, we need to minimize with respect to
\chi
, yielding

\displaystyle d(g,RH_{\theta})=\sqrt{2\left\lparen 1-\sqrt{\cos^{2}\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\cos^{2}\tfrac{\theta-\beta}{2}+\sin^{2}\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\cos^{2}\tfrac{\theta+\beta}{2}}\right\rparen}~.

(103)

Since
g\in RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta}
iff
d(g,RH_{\theta})<\varepsilon
, which is iff

\cos(\theta-\beta)-\cos(\eta)>\sin^{2}\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\sin(\beta)\sin(\theta).
Integrating with normalization
\frac{1}{2\pi}
gives that the set of
\varphi\in[0,2\pi)
that satisfy this has measure

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{\cos(\theta-\beta)-\cos\eta}{\sin\theta\sin\beta}}.

(104)

To maximise the measure with respect to
g
, it remains to maximise this measure with respect to
\beta
. If
|\cos\theta|>\cos\eta
, then taking
\cos\beta=\frac{\cos\eta}{\cos\theta}
gives that the set is measure
1
. Else, value of
\beta
that does this is
\beta=\arccos\left\lparen\frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\eta}\right\rparen
. Using this value, the measure becomes
\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{1-\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\eta}{\sin^{2}\theta}}}
, giving the result.
∎

Now, we can pass to the proof of the theorem.

Proof of
Theorem

6.1
.

We make use of the bound of
Theorem

9.5
. To do so, we need to choose a collection of orthogonal permutations: we make use of the trivial option
\pi_{i}(H_{\theta_{k}})=H_{\theta_{k+i}}
. Then, it is direct to note by conjugation that
\mu_{H_{\theta_{k}}}(H_{\theta_{k}}\cap RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta_{k+i}})=\mu_{H_{0}}(H_{0}\cap RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta_{i}})
, giving the bound

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\sup_{R\in SO(3)}\sqrt{\mu_{H_{0}}(H_{0}\cap RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta_{i}})}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sup_{R\in SO(3)}\sqrt{\mu_{H_{0}}(H_{0}\cap RE_{\varepsilon}H_{\theta_{i}})}.

(105)

Now, using
Lemma

6.2
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\leq\frac{2}{N}+\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N/2-1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt{1-\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\eta}{\sin^{2}\theta_{i}}}}}.

(106)

It remains to simplify this upper bound. First, using
\arcsin x\leq\frac{\pi}{2}x
,
\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\leq\frac{2}{N}+\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N/2-1}\left\lparen 1-\sqrt{1-\frac{\sin^{2}\eta}{\sin^{2}\theta_{i}}}\right\rparen^{1/4}
. Next, using
\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\geq 1-x^{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\leq\frac{2}{N}+\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N/2-1}\left\lparen 1-\left\lparen 1-\frac{\sin^{2}\eta}{\sin^{2}\theta_{i}}\right\rparen\right\rparen^{1/4}=\frac{2}{N}+\frac{2}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N/2-1}\sqrt{\frac{\sin\eta}{\sin\theta_{i}}}.

(107)

First, we see that
\sin\eta=2\varepsilon\sqrt{1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}}\left\lparen 1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right\rparen\leq 2\varepsilon
. Also,
\sin x\geq\frac{2}{\pi}x
, so

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\leq\frac{2}{N}+\frac{2\sqrt{\pi\varepsilon}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{i}}}.

(108)

Approximating the sum by an integral,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{N,\varepsilon})\leq\frac{2}{N}+\sqrt{\pi\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}dx=\frac{2}{N}+2\sqrt{\pi\varepsilon}.

(109)

∎

7
Monogamy games for finite groups

In this section, we introduce the coset monogamy game on non-abelian, but finite, groups. This serves to bridge the gap between the monogamy game on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
of
[
CLLZ21
]
and the infinite-dimensional monogamy games of the following sections.

7.1
Non-abelian coset states

Let
G
be an arbitrary finite group. The corresponding Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{G}=\spn_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{{\lvert g\rangle}\middle|{g\in G}\right\}
, where the
\lvert g\rangle
form an orthonormal basis, is finite-dimensional. Hence, we can, as in the original
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
case, work with a basis of coset states rather than a measure.

Let
H\leq G
be a subgroup and fix
g\in G
. In order to have a basis of coset states, the states on the coset
gH
need to be an orthonormal basis of
\spn_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{{\lvert gh\rangle}\middle|{h\in H}\right\}
. For an abelian group, it is sufficient to use the characters, giving coset states of the form

\displaystyle\lvert gH^{\chi}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|H|}}\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h)\lvert gh\rangle.

(110)

However, if
H
is non-abelian, then the irreducible representations are not one-dimensional and the characters only span the space of class functions, not the space of all functions on
H
. In this case, the natural generalization of the irreducible characters are the matrix elements of the irreducible representations. Let
\gamma:H\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(d_{\gamma})
be an irreducible representation, and write the
(m,n)
matrix element, for
1\leq m,n\leq d_{\gamma}
as
\gamma_{m,n}:H\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. The associated coset state is

\displaystyle\lvert gH^{\gamma}_{m,n}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{d_{\gamma}}{|H|}}\sum_{h\in H}\gamma_{m,n}(h)\lvert gh\rangle.

(111)

The orthonormality of these states is given by the Schur orthogonality relations: if
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
are either equal or inequivalent irreducible representations, and
1\leq m,n\leq d_{\gamma}
,
1\leq m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\leq d_{\gamma^{\prime}}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in H}\overline{\gamma_{m,n}(h)}\gamma_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}(h)=\frac{|H|}{d_{\gamma}}\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}.

(112)

However, unlike the characters, the matrix elements of a representation are not unique in the sense that the equivalent but unequal representations may have different matrix elements. This implies that the coset states for different choices of irreducible representations and coset representatives are not equal up to global phase, as they are in the abelian case. To remedy that we need to fix choices of representatives. Let
\textsf{CS}(H)
be a set of coset representatives of
G/H
, let
\textsf{Irr}(H)
be a full set of inequivalent irreducible representations (irreps) of
H
, and define
\textsf{IB}(H)=\{{\gamma_{m,n}}|{\gamma\in\textsf{Irr}(H);1\leq m,n\leq d_{\gamma}}\}
. With this, we take the basis of coset states to be

\displaystyle\left\{{\lvert gH^{\gamma}_{m,n}\rangle}\middle|{g\in\textsf{CS}(H),\gamma_{m,n}\in\textsf{IB}(H)}\right\}.

(113)

An interesting property of non-abelian coset states is that, whereas abelian coset states are equal superpositions of the elements of
gH
, non-abelian states are no longer, as we do not have always that
|\gamma_{m,n}(h)|=1
. We give a simple example to illustrate that.

Consider
G=D_{N}
, the dihedral group defined with generators and relations
D_{N}=\langle{r,t};{r^{N}=t^{2}=(tr)^{2}=1}\rangle
, for
N
odd and its subgroup
H=\langle{r^{N/p},t}\rangle\cong D_{p}
for
p
a divisor of
N
. There are two one-dimensional irreps of
H
: the trival
\gamma_{0}(r^{N/p})=\gamma_{0}(t)=1
, and the representation
\gamma_{-1}(r^{N/p})=1
,
\gamma_{-1}(t)=-1
. The remaining irreps are two-dimensional, indexed by
k=1,\ldots,\frac{1}{2}(p-1)
:
\gamma_{k}(r^{N/p})=\exp(\frac{2\pi ik}{p}Z)
,
\gamma_{k}(t)=X
. Further, we may take
\textsf{CS}(H)=\left\{{1,r,\ldots,r^{N/p-1}}\right\}
. Then, the coset states are

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lvert r^{q}H^{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2p}}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\left\lparen\lvert r^{Nj/p+q}\rangle+\lvert tr^{Nj/p-q}\rangle\right\rparen\\
&\lvert r^{q}H^{-1}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2p}}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\left\lparen\lvert r^{Nj/p+q}\rangle-\lvert tr^{Nj/p-q}\rangle\right\rparen\\
&\lvert r^{q}H^{k}_{m,n}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}e^{(-1)^{m}2\pi i\frac{jk}{p_{I}}}\lvert t^{m+n}r^{Nj/p+(-1)^{m+n}q}\rangle.\end{split}

(114)

Note in particular that each of the states
\lvert r^{q}H^{k}_{m,n}\rangle
is a superposition of only half the elements of
r^{q}H
.

7.2
Non-abelian coset monogamy game

Definition 7.1
.

A
non-abelian coset monogamy game
is a pair
\texttt{G}=\left\lparen G,\mathcal{S}\right\rparen
, where
G
is a finite group and
\mathcal{S}
is a finite set of subgroups of
G
.

A
(quantum) strategy
for a coset measure game
G
is a tuple
\texttt{S}=\left\lparen\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},B,C,\rho\right\rparen
, where
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are Hilbert spaces,
B=\{{B^{H}:\textsf{CS}(H)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B})}|{H\in\mathcal{S}}\}
and
C=\{{C^{H}:\textsf{IB}(H)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}|{H\in\mathcal{S}}\}
are collections of POVMs, and
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{G}\otimes\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{C})
is a shared density operator.

Let
G
be a coset game and
S
be a strategy for it. The
winning probability
of
S
is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})=\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\textsf{CS}(H)\\
\gamma_{m,n}\in\textsf{IB}(H)\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\Tr[(\vphantom{gH^{\gamma}_{m,n}}\left\lvert\smash{gH^{\gamma}_{m,n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{gH^{\gamma}_{m,n}}\right\rvert\otimes B^{H}_{g}\otimes C^{H}_{\gamma_{m,n}})\rho],

(115)

where the expection with respect to
H\in\mathcal{S}
is uniform.

The winning probability of
G
is
\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G})=\sup_{\texttt{S}}\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})
.

Our main result in this section is an analogous bound to the one of
[
CV22
]
on the winning probability of such a general game.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\texttt{G}=(G,\mathcal{S})
be a coset monogamy game, and take a set of mutually orthogonal permutations

8
Monogamy games for abelian topological groups

Here, we generalise the coset monogamy game to arbitrary locally compact Abelian groups, on which Pontryagin duality provides a generalization of the Fourier transform. By replacing the coset states with the appropriate operator-valued measure, we find a family of continuous-variable versions of this game, and via a similar analysis we can bound the winning probability, but as a function of not the number of outcomes but the measurement precision. We also study the version of the game where Alice sends damped coset states and find that the same winning probability bound applies.

8.1
Infinite-outcome measurements and coset measures

In the context of the original coset monogamy game, we take the space of classical states to be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
, the space of
n
-bit strings. We are interested in extending the space of classical states to an arbitrary locally compact (Hausdorff) abelian group
G
, for example
U(1)
,
\mathbb{Z}
, or
\mathbb{R}
. In this way, we will be able to work with games where the answer sets are non-trivially infinite. The Hilbert space corresponding to such a set of classical states is
L^{2}(G)
, the space of square-integrable functions
G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, modulo the subspace of almost-everywhere zero functions. We will write
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
for a class, and the
\psi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
for a representative of this class. Also, we write the Haar measure
\mu_{G}:\mathscr{B}(G)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, where
\mathscr{B}(G)
is the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets, and write the Haar integral as
\int f(g)d_{G}g
.

The subgroups
H\leq G
we consider will always be closed, since this is necessary and sufficient for the quotient space
G/H
to be Hausdorff as well. Endowing
H
with the subspace topology and
G/H
with the quotient topology, both
H
and
G/H
are locally compact abelian groups, so they have Haar measures. In fact, fixing Haar measures on
G
and
H
, there is a unique Haar measure on
G/H
such that
\int f(g)d_{G}g=\iint f(gh)d_{H}hd_{G/H}(gH)
. This holds for a much larger class of topological groups, such as compact groups
[
Nac76
]
. As much as possible, we will assume the Haar measures to be normalized such that if
G
is compact
\mu_{G}(G)=1
and else if
G
is discrete
\mu_{G}(\{1\})=1
.

In order to work with games with infinitely many answers, we need to be able to handle quantum measurements with infinitely many outcomes. If the outcomes are discrete, then it is possible to handle the measurement as a POVM in the usual way. However, the set of measurement outcomes will not in general be discrete, in which case problems arise. One way to handle this is to use a basis of unnormalizable states to represent the measurement basis. For example, we can take and unnormalizable basis of
L^{2}(G)
as
\left\{{\lvert g\rangle}\middle|{g\in G}\right\}
, where each
\lvert g\rangle
is a Dirac delta function such that
\langle h|g\rangle=0
when
g\neq h
and
\vphantom{gh}\left\langle\smash{g}\middle|\smash{h}\right\rangle=\delta_{G}(g^{-1}h)
. Of course, none of these are elements of
L^{2}(G)
, but
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
can nonetheless be represented as
\lvert\psi\rangle=\int\psi(g)\lvert g\rangle d_{G}g
. The drawback of this approach is the difficulty of handling convergence of such expressions, which can make the handling of the probability of a measurement outcome ambiguous. To remedy this, we generalise the measurement with respect to a basis not by a generalised basis, but by an operator-valued generalization of a probability measure. For instance, in the previous example, for a measurable subset
E\subseteq G
, we see the probability of measuring some
g\in E
on some state
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
is
\int_{E}\left\lvert\psi(g)\right\rvert^{2}d_{G}g=\vphantom{\psi\int_{E}\vphantom{g}\left\lvert\smash{g}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{g}\right\rvert d_{G}g\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{\int_{E}\vphantom{g}\left\lvert\smash{g}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{g}\right\rvert d_{G}g}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
X
be a measurable space,
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, and
\mathscr{S}_{X}
be a
\sigma
-algebra on
X
. An
operator-valued measure
is a map
P:\mathscr{S}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
that is weakly countably additive,
i.e.
, for all countable collections of disjoint measurable sets
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathscr{S}_{X}
and
\lvert\phi\rangle,\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\vphantom{\phi P\Big\lparen\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\Big\rparen\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{P\Big\lparen\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\Big\rparen}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\vphantom{\phi P(E_{i})\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{P(E_{i})}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle.

(126)

Additionally,
P
is

•

a
POVM
if
P(E)
is positive for all
E\in\mathscr{S}_{X}
and
P(X)=\mathbb{I}
;

•

projective
(or spectral) if
P(E\cap F)=P(E)P(F)
for all
E,F\in\mathscr{S}_{X}
;

•

a
PVM
if it is a projective POVM.

Let
P
be a POVM measure. For any
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(L^{2}(G))
,
E\mapsto\Tr(P(E)\rho)
is a probability measure, representing the probability of measuring an outcome in
E
. POVM measures constitute a physically natural generalization of usual finite POVMs because of the role they play in the spectral theorem for general Hermitian operators. For any Hermitian operator,
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, there exists a spectral measure
P:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

A=\int\lambda dP(\lambda)

[
BB03
]
; as such, since any observable in quantum mechanics is represented by a Hermitian operator, the distribution of measurement outcomes is naturally represented by a POVM measure. The role of operator-valued measures in quantum theory has been studied from a variety of perspectives
[
Hol11
,
Mor17
]
.

Before moving on to the particular measure of interest here, we state a general result on POVM measures that allows us to dilate any POVM measure to a PVM measure.

Theorem 8.2

(Naimark
[
Pau02
]
)
.

Let
X
be a measurable space,
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, and
Q:\mathscr{S}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a POVM measure. Then, there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, an isometry
V:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
, and a PVM measure
P:\mathscr{S}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that, for all measurable
E\subseteq X
,

\displaystyle Q(E)=V^{\dagger}P(E)V.

(127)

We go into detail about integration with respect to POVM measures in
Appendix

A
.

The original coset monogamy game, given a subspaces
H\leq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
, utilizes bases indexed by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}/H\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}/H^{\perp}
.
H^{\perp}
is defined using the dot product, which can be seen as the family of homomorphisms
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\rightarrow S^{1}=\{{z\in\mathbb{C}}|{\lvert z\rvert=1}\}
indexed by
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
defined
x\mapsto(-1)^{a\cdot x}
. For a general abelian topoloogical group, this natural family of homomorphisms is given not by
G
itself but by the dual group
\hat{G}
, which the group of continuous homomorphisms
G\rightarrow S^{1}
, with product
(\gamma\eta)(g)=\gamma(g)\eta(g)
.
G
is isomorphic to
\hat{G}
if
G
is finite. If
G
is locally compact abelian, then so is
\hat{G}
. Then, the natural generalization of

\displaystyle H^{\perp}=\left\{{\gamma\in\hat{G}}\middle|{\gamma(h)=1\forall h\in H}\right\},

(128)

and we have that
\hat{G}/H^{\perp}\cong\hat{H}
homeomorphically by Pontryagin duality. Thus, the basis of coset states should generalise to an operator-valued measure on
G/H\times\hat{H}
. An important tool for working with the function spaces on these groups is the Fourier transform.

Definition 8.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group. The
Fourier transform
is the operator
\mathcal{F}_{G}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G),L^{2}(\hat{G}))
defined on
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
continuous with compact support as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{G}\lvert\psi\rangle)(\gamma)=\int\psi(g)\overline{\gamma(g)}d_{G}g,

(129)

and extended uniquely to all
L^{2}(G)
by continuity.

There exists a unique scaling of the Haar measure on
\hat{G}
, called the dual measure, that makes the Fourier transform an isometry. Using that measure, the inverse Fourier transform on
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(\hat{G})
continuous with compact support is
(\mathcal{F}_{G}^{-1}\lvert\psi\rangle)(g)=\int\psi(\gamma)\gamma(g)d_{\hat{G}}g
, which again extends to the whole space by continuity.

Finally, we need the concept of a Fourier transform with respect to a closed subgroup
H\leq G
. For
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
, we can simply take
\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\rangle=\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi|_{H}\rangle:\hat{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. In general,
\lvert\psi|_{H}\rangle
is not always in
L^{2}(H)
, but in the cases we consider, this will be true almost everywhere.

Definition 8.4
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group and
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup. The
coset operator measure
is the map
A^{H}:\mathscr{B}(G/H)\otimes\mathscr{B}(\hat{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))
defined, for
E\subseteq G/H\times\hat{H}
measurable and
\lvert\phi\rangle,\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
as

\displaystyle\vphantom{\phi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\int_{E}\overline{(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\phi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)}(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)d_{{}_{G/H\times\hat{H}}}(gH,\gamma),

(130)

where
\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle(h)=\psi(gh)
.

Note that the integral defining
\vphantom{\phi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle
is well-defined, since we have that
\mathcal{F}_{H}(\lvert\psi\circ gh\rangle)(\gamma)=\gamma(h)\mathcal{F}_{H}(\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)
for all
h\in H
, so
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{H}(\lvert\phi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)}\mathcal{F}_{H}(\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)
does not depend on the choice of coset representative. Also, as
\int f(g)d_{G}g=\iint f(gh)d_{H}hd_{G/H}(gH)
,
\lvert\psi\circ g|_{H}\rangle
is in
L^{2}(H)

\mu_{G/H}
-almost everywhere, so the integrand is in fact integrable. From the definition, it is direct that
\vphantom{\phi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle
is in fact linear in
\lvert\psi\rangle
and antilinear in
\lvert\phi\rangle
. We can show that it is bounded and that it is a PVM simultaneously.

Theorem 8.5
.

A^{H}
is a PVM measure.

Proof.

First, we have that, for any measurable
E\subseteq G/H\times\hat{H}
and
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
,

\displaystyle\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\int_{E}\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)\right\rvert^{2}d_{G/H\times\hat{H}}(gH,\gamma)\geq 0,

(131)

so
A^{H}(E)
is positive. Next, if
E\subseteq E^{\prime}
,
\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle\leq\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E^{\prime})\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E^{\prime})}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle
. In particular,
\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle\leq\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(G/H\times\hat{H})\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(G/H\times\hat{H})}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle
. We have, by Plancherel’s theorem, that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(G/H\times\hat{H})\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(G/H\times\hat{H})}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle&=\int_{G/H}\int_{\hat{H}}\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)\right\rvert^{2}d_{\hat{H}}\gamma d_{G/H}(gH)\\
&=\int_{G/H}\int_{H}\left\lvert\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle(h)\right\rvert^{2}d_{H}hd_{G/H}(gH)\\
&=\int_{G/H}\int_{H}\left\lvert\psi(gh)\right\rvert^{2}d_{H}hd_{G/H}(gH)\\
&=\int_{G}|\psi(g)|^{2}d_{G}g=\vphantom{\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{\psi}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(132)

Thus,
A^{H}(G/H\times\hat{H})
is bounded, and therefore
A^{H}(E)
is bounded.

To get that
A^{H}
is a POVM, we need next that it is weakly countably additive. Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a countable collection of disjoint measurable sets in
G/H\times\hat{H}
. Then, by monotone convergence

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{\psi A^{H}\Big\lparen\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\Big\rparen\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}\Big\lparen\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\Big\rparen}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle&=\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}}\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)\right\rvert^{2}d_{G/H\times\hat{H}}(gH,\gamma)\\
&=\int\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\chi_{E_{i}}(gH,\gamma)\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)\right\rvert^{2}d_{G/H\times\hat{H}}(gH,\gamma)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int\chi_{E_{i}}(gH,\gamma)\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)\right\rvert^{2}d_{G/H\times\hat{H}}(gH,\gamma)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E_{i})\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E_{i})}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(133)

Using the polarization identity gives the full form of weak countable additivity.

Finally, we want to show that
A^{H}
is projective. To see that, note
(A^{H}\lvert\psi\rangle)(g)=\int_{(E)_{gH}}\left\lparen\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle\right\rparen(\gamma)d_{\hat{H}}\gamma
almost everywhere, where
(E)_{gH}=\{{\gamma\in\hat{H}}|{(gH,\gamma)\in E}\}
is the cross-section of
E
at
gH
. In fact, for any
\lvert\phi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
continuous with compact support, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{\phi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle&=\int_{E}\int_{H}\overline{\phi(gh)}\gamma(h)d_{H}h(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)d_{{}_{G/H\times\hat{H}}}(gH,\gamma)\\
&=\int_{\hat{H}}\chi_{(E)_{gH}}(\gamma)\int_{G/H}\int_{H}\overline{\phi(gh)}(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ gh\rangle)(\gamma)d_{H}hd_{G/H}gHd_{\hat{H}}\gamma\\
&=\int_{(E)_{gH}}\int_{G}\overline{\phi(g)}(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)d_{G}gd_{\hat{H}}\gamma\\
&=\int_{G}\overline{\phi(g)}\int_{(E)_{gH}}\left\lparen\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle\right\rparen(\gamma)d_{\hat{H}}\gamma d_{G}g.\end{split}

(134)

Thus, for any measurable
E,F
, we get that
(A^{H}(E)A^{H}(F)\lvert\psi\rangle)(g)=\int_{(E)_{gH}}\left\lparen\mathcal{F}_{H}(A^{H}(F)\lvert\psi\rangle\circ g)\right\rparen(\gamma)d_{\hat{H}}\gamma
and since, for
h\in H
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}(A^{H}(F)\lvert\psi\rangle\circ g)(h)&=(A^{H}(F)\lvert\psi\rangle)(gh)=\int_{(F)_{ghH}}(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ gh\rangle)(\gamma)d_{\hat{H}}\gamma\\
&=\int_{(F)_{gH}}\gamma(h)(\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)d_{\hat{H}}\gamma\\
&=\mathcal{F}_{H}^{-1}\lparen\chi_{(F)_{gH}}\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle\rparen(h),\end{split}

(135)

if
\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle
is continuous with compact support. By Fourier inversion, this set is dense, so this identity holds for all
\lvert\psi\rangle
. As such,

\displaystyle\begin{split}(A^{H}(E)A^{H}(F)\lvert\psi\rangle)(g)&=\int_{(E)_{gH}}\left\lparen\mathcal{F}_{H}\left\lparen\mathcal{F}_{H}^{-1}\lparen\chi_{(F)_{gH}}\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle\rparen\right\rparen\right\rparen(\gamma)d_{\hat{H}}\gamma\\
&=\int_{(E)_{gH}}\chi_{(F)_{gH}}(\gamma)\lparen\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle\rparen(\gamma)d_{\hat{H}}\gamma\\
&=\int_{(E\cap F)_{gH}}\lparen\mathcal{F}_{H}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle\rparen(\gamma)d_{\hat{H}}\gamma=(A^{H}(E\cap F)\lvert\psi\rangle)(g).\end{split}

(136)

∎

8.2
The coset measure game

Definition 8.6
.

An
abelian coset measure monogamy game
is a tuple
\texttt{G}=\left\lparen G,\mathcal{S},E,F\right\rparen
, where
G
is a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group,
\mathcal{S}
is a finite set of closed subgroups of
G
, and
E\subseteq G
and
F\subseteq\hat{G}
are symmetric neighborhoods of identity,
i.e.

E=E^{-1}
and
1\in E
and the same for
F
.

A
(quantum) strategy
for a coset measure game
G
is a tuple
\texttt{S}=\left\lparen\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},B,C,\rho\right\rparen
, where
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are Hilbert spaces,
B=\{{B^{H}:\mathscr{B}(G/H)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B})}|{H\in\mathcal{S}}\}
and
C=\{{C^{H}:\mathscr{B}(\hat{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}|{H\in\mathcal{S}}\}
are collections of POVM measures, and
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(L^{2}(G)\otimes\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{C})
.

Let
G
be a coset measure game and
S
be a strategy for it. The
winning probability
of
S
is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})=\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Tr\left[(A^{H}\otimes B^{H}\otimes C^{H})(E_{H})\rho\right],

(137)

where
E_{H}=\{{(gH,\gamma|_{H},egH,(\varphi\gamma)|_{H})}|{g\in G,\gamma\in\hat{G},e\in E,\varphi\in F}\}\in G/H\times\hat{H}\times G/H\times\hat{H}
and the expection with respect to
H\in\mathcal{S}
is uniform.

The winning probability of
G
is
\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G})=\sup_{\texttt{S}}\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})
.

Theorem 8.7
.

Let
\texttt{G}=(G,\mathcal{S},E,F)
be a coset measure game for second-countable
G
, and let
\pi^{i}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
for
i=1,\ldots,|\mathcal{S}|
be a set of orthogonal permutations,
i.e.

\pi_{i}\circ\pi_{j}^{-1}
has no fixed points unless
i=j
. Then, the winning probability of
G
is bounded above as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G})\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\sup_{H\in\mathcal{S},g\in G}\sqrt{\mu_{H}\big\lparen H\cap gE\pi_{i}(H)\big\rparen\mu_{\hat{H}}\big\lparen F\big\rparen}.

(138)

Lemma 8.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{K}
be a separable Hilbert space,
X
be a measurable space,
P:\mathscr{S}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a POVM measure, and
F:X\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
be a bounded weakly measurable function. Then,

\displaystyle\Big\|\int F\otimes dP\Big\|\leq\sup_{x\in X}\|F(x)\|.

(139)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Using
Lemma

A.5
, there exists a sequence of simple functions
\lparen F_{n}=\sum_{l}M^{n}_{l}\chi_{E^{n}_{l}}\rparen
that converges strongly pointwise to
F
and
\|F_{n}(x)\|\leq\sup_{y}\|F(y)\|+\varepsilon
. Since this implies
\sup_{n}\left\|\int F_{n}\otimes dP\right\|<\infty
,
Theorem

A.9
gives that
\int F_{n}\otimes dP\rightarrow\int F\otimes dP
weakly. We know that there exist
\lvert u\rangle,\lvert v\rangle\in\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{H}
with
\|\lvert u\rangle\|,\|\lvert v\rangle\|\leq 1
such that
\left\lvert\vphantom{u\int F\otimes dPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{\int F\otimes dP}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle\right\rvert>\left\|\int F\otimes dP\right\|-\varepsilon
. Then, using the weak convergence, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left\lvert\vphantom{u\int(F_{n}-F)\otimes dPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{\int(F_{n}-F)\otimes dP}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle\right\rvert<\varepsilon

\forall n\geq N
, so

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big\|\int F\otimes dP\Big\|&<\Big\lvert\vphantom{u\int F\otimes dPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{\int F\otimes dP}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle\Big\rvert+\varepsilon\\
&<\Big\lvert\vphantom{u\int F_{n}\otimes dPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{\int F_{n}\otimes dP}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle\Big\rvert+2\varepsilon\\
&\leq\Big\|\sum_{l}M^{n}_{l}\otimes P(E^{n}_{l})\Big\|+2\varepsilon\\
&\leq\sup_{l}\|M^{n}_{l}\|+2\varepsilon\\
&<\sup_{x\in X}\|F(x)\|+3\varepsilon.\end{split}

(140)

As this holds for all
\varepsilon>0
, we get
\Big\|\int F\otimes dP\Big\|\leq\sup_{x\in X}\|F(x)\|
as wanted.
∎

Lemma 8.9
.

Let
H,K\leq G
be closed subgroups, and let
E\subseteq G
and
F\subseteq\hat{G}
be symmetric neighborhoods of the identity. Then, for any
\eta\in\hat{G}
and
qK\in G/K

\displaystyle\left\|A^{H}\big\lparen G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H}\big\rparen A^{K}\big\lparen EqK/K\times\hat{K}\big\rparen\right\|\leq\sup_{g\in G}\sqrt{\mu_{H}\big\lparen H\cap gEK\big\rparen\mu_{\hat{H}}\big\lparen F\big\rparen}

(141)

Proof.

First, we use Fourier inversion as a continuous-valued version of orthogonality of the characters to simplify
A^{K}\big\lparen EqK/K\times\hat{K}\big\rparen
. For any
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
continuous with compact support and
\|\lvert\psi\rangle\|=1
, and
g\in G
, since
(EqK/K\times\hat{K})_{gK}=\hat{K}
if
g\in EqK
and
\varnothing
otherwise,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lparen A^{K}\big\lparen EqK/K\times\hat{K}\big\rparen\lvert\psi\rangle\right\rparen(g)&=\chi_{EqK}(g)\int_{\hat{K}}(\mathcal{F}_{K}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(\gamma)d_{\hat{K}}\gamma\\
&=\chi_{EqK}(g)(\mathcal{F}_{K}^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{K}\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle)(1)\\
&=\chi_{EqK}(g)\lvert\psi\circ g\rangle(1)=\chi_{EqK}(g)\psi(g).\end{split}

(142)

Write
A^{K}\big\lparen EqK/K\times\hat{K}\big\rparen=\Pi_{EqK}
, the projector onto the subspace of
L^{2}(G)
where
\psi(g)=0
if
g\notin EqK
. Then, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|A^{H}(G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H})\Pi_{EqK}\lvert\psi\rangle\|^{2}&=\int_{G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H}}\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{H}(\Pi_{EqK}\lvert\psi\rangle)\circ g)(\gamma)\right\rvert^{2}d_{{}_{G/H\times\hat{H}}}(gH,\gamma)\\
&=\int_{G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H}}\left\lvert\int_{H}\chi_{EqK}(gh)\psi(gh)\overline{\gamma(h)}d_{H}h\right\rvert^{2}d_{{}_{G/H\times\hat{H}}}(gH,\gamma)\\
&\leq\int_{G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H}}\int_{H}\left\lvert\psi(gh)\right\rvert^{2}d_{H}h\int_{H}\left\lvert\chi_{EqK}(gh)\overline{\gamma(h)}\right\rvert^{2}d_{H}hd_{{}_{G/H\times\hat{H}}}(gH,\gamma)\\
&=\int_{G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H}}\int_{H}\left\lvert\psi(gh)\right\rvert^{2}d_{H}h\mu_{H}(H\cap g^{-1}EqK)d_{{}_{G/H\times\hat{H}}}(gH,\gamma)\\
&\leq\int_{G/H}\int_{H}\left\lvert\psi(gh)\right\rvert^{2}d_{H}hd_{G/H}gH\sup_{g\in G}\mu_{H}(H\cap g^{-1}EqK)\mu_{\hat{H}}{(F\eta)|_{H}}\\
&\leq\|\lvert\psi\rangle\|^{2}\sup_{g\in G}\mu_{H}(H\cap g^{-1}EqK)\mu_{\hat{H}}\left\lparen(F\eta)|_{H}\right\rparen.\end{split}

(143)

Finally, we note by Haar invariance that
\mu_{\hat{H}}\left\lparen(F\eta)|_{H}\right\rparen=\mu_{\hat{H}}\left\lparen F(\eta|_{H})\right\rparen=\mu_{\hat{H}}\left\lparen F\right\rparen
, giving the result.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

8.7
.

Let
\texttt{S}=\left\lparen\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},B,C,\rho\right\rparen
be a strategy for
\texttt{G}=\left\lparen G,\mathcal{S},E,F\right\rparen
. Due to Naimark’s theorem
Theorem

8.2
, we may assume that
B
and
C
are PVMs. Writing, for each
H\in\mathcal{S}
,
\Pi^{H}=(A^{H}\otimes B^{H}\otimes C^{H})(E_{H})
, the winning probability may be expressed as
\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})=\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Tr(\Pi^{H}\rho)
. Since the overlap result
Lemma

7.3
doesn’t depend on the dimension of the underlying space, we apply it to upper bound the winning probability of the game, getting

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})\leq\Big\|\underset{S\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Pi^{H}\Big\|\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\sup_{H\in\mathcal{S}}\left\|\Pi^{H}\Pi^{\pi_{i}(H)}\right\|,

(144)

since
(\Pi^{H})^{2}=\Pi^{H}
. Fixing
H,K\in\mathcal{S}
, it remains to simplify
\left\|\Pi^{H}\Pi^{K}\right\|
. Given that

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{H}&\subseteq\left\{{(gH,\gamma|_{H},g^{\prime}H,(\varphi\gamma)|_{H})}\middle|{g,g^{\prime}\in G;\gamma\in\hat{G},\varphi\in F}\right\}\\
&=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\hat{G}}G/H\times\{\gamma|_{H}\}\times G/H\times(F\gamma)|_{H},\end{split}

(145)

we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pi^{H}&\leq\int_{\hat{H}}(A^{H}\otimes B^{H})(G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H}\times G/H)\otimes dC^{H}(\eta)\\
&=\int_{\hat{H}}A^{H}(G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H})\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}\otimes dC^{H}(\eta).\end{split}

(146)

Similarly,
E_{K}\subseteq\bigcup_{g\in G}\{gK\}\times\hat{K}\times EgK/K\times\hat{K}
, so
\Pi^{K}\leq\int_{G/H}A^{K}(EgK/K\times\hat{K})\otimes dB^{K}(gK)\otimes\mathbb{I}_{C}
. Thus, we may bound the norms

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\Pi^{H}\Pi^{K}\right\|&\leq\Big\|\int\!A^{H}(G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H})\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}\otimes dC^{H}(\eta)\int\!A^{K}(EgK/K\times\hat{K})\otimes dB^{K}(gK)\otimes\mathbb{I}_{C}\Big\|\\
&=\Big\|\int A^{H}(G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H})A^{K}(EgK/K\times\hat{K})\otimes d(B^{K}\otimes C^{H})(gK,\eta)\Big\|.\end{split}

(147)

Using
Lemma

8.8
,
\left\|\Pi^{H}\Pi^{K}\right\|\leq\sup_{gK\in G/K,\eta\in\hat{H}}\left\|A^{H}(G/H\times(F\eta)|_{H})A^{K}(EgK/K\times\hat{K})\right\|
; and then using
Lemma

8.9
.
\left\|\Pi^{H}\Pi^{K}\right\|\leq\sup_{g\in G}\sqrt{\mu_{H}(H\cap gEK)\mu_{\hat{H}}(F)}
. Thus, putting it together,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\sup_{H\in\mathcal{S},g\in G}\sqrt{\mu_{H}(H\cap gE\pi_{i}(H))\mu_{\hat{H}}(F)},

(148)

and since this holds for every strategy, we have the wanted result.
∎

Note that it follows directly from the proof that we also have the bound

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G})\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\sup_{H\in\mathcal{S},g\in G}\min\left\{{1,\sqrt{\mu_{H}\big\lparen H\cap gE\pi_{i}(H)\big\rparen\mu_{\hat{H}}\big\lparen F\big\rparen}}\right\},

(149)

which may be better in the case that the Haar measure of one of the groups is not
1
.

8.3
State-sending version of the game

In this section, we additionally assume that, for the subgroups
H\leq G
we consider, the Haar measures on
G/H
and
\hat{H}
are
\sigma
-finite, that is the sets may be written as the countable union of sets of finite measure. This holds for all our groups of interest here, in particular
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{C}
, and
U(1)
, which appear above.

We use approximate maximally entangled states to be able to interpret the coset monogamy game as a game where Alice samples a random pair
(gH,\gamma)
, prepares a damped version of the associated unnormalizable coset states, and then sends to it to Bob and Charlie through an adversarially-chosen channel. This will allow us to generalise the original interpretation of the coset monogamy game as a quantum encoding of classical messages as in
[
CLLZ21
]
.

Definition 8.10
.

Let
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup, let
c:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(G)
be a complex conjugate, and
(\Delta_{n})
be a damping sequence in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))
that
\mu_{G/H}\times\mu_{\hat{H}}
-damps
A^{H}
. By
Lemma

B.5
, there exists measurable
\rho_{n}:G/H\times\hat{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(L^{2}(G))
and integrable
\pi_{n}:G/H\times\hat{H}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}A^{H}(E)\Delta_{n}=\int\rho_{n}(gH,\gamma)\pi_{n}(gH,\gamma)d(gH,\gamma)
. The
state associated to
H
is the measurable function

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sigma^{H}_{n}:gH\times\hat{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(L^{2}(G))\\
&\sigma^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma)=c\rho_{n}(gH,\gamma)c;\end{split}

(150)

and the
probability measure associated to
H
is

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mu^{H}_{n}:\mathscr{B}(G/H)\otimes\mathscr{B}(\hat{H})\rightarrow[0,1]\\
&\mu^{H}_{n}(E)=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}\int_{E}\pi_{n}(gH,\gamma)d(gH,\gamma).\end{split}

(151)

\mu^{H}_{n}
is in fact a probability measure, as

\displaystyle\mu^{H}_{n}(G/H\times\hat{H})=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}\int\Tr(\rho_{n})\mu_{n}d\mu=\frac{\Tr(\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}A^{H}(G/H\times\hat{H})\Delta_{n})}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}=\frac{\Tr(\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}\Delta_{n})}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}=1,

(152)

and we have
\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}c\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}A^{H}(E)\Delta_{n}c=\int_{E}\sigma^{H}_{n}d\mu^{H}_{n}
.

It is also important to note that, due to the definition of
A^{H}
in terms of the measure
\mu_{G/H}\times\mu_{\hat{H}}
,
any
damping sequence
\mu_{G/H}\times\mu_{\hat{H}}
-damps it.

Definition 8.11
.

Let
\texttt{G}=\left\lparen G,\mathcal{S},E,F\right\rparen
be a coset monogamy game,
c:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(G)
be a complex conjugate, and
(\Delta_{n})
be a damping sequence in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))
that
\mu_{G/H}\times\mu_{\hat{H}}
-damps
A^{H}
for each
H\in\mathcal{S}
. The
state-sending version
of
G
is the sequence of tuples
\texttt{G}_{n}=(G,\mathcal{S},E,F,\Delta_{n},c)
.

A
strategy
for
\texttt{G}_{n}
is a tuple
\texttt{S}=(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},B,C,\Phi)
, where
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are the Hilbert spaces held by Bob and Charlie,
B=\{{B^{H}:\mathscr{B}(G/H)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B})}|{H\in\mathcal{S}}\}
and
C=\{{C^{H}:\mathscr{B}(\hat{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}|{H\in\mathcal{S}}\}
are collections of POVM measures, and
\Phi:\mathcal{T}_{1}(L^{2}(G))\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{C})
is a completely positive trace preserving map.

The
winning probability
of
S
is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}_{n}}(\texttt{S})=\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\int\Tr\left[(B^{H}\otimes C^{H})(EgH\times(F\gamma)|_{H})\Phi(\sigma^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma))\right]d\mu^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma).

(153)

The winning probability of
\texttt{G}_{n}
is
\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{n})=\sup_{\texttt{S}}\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}_{n}}(\texttt{S})
.

Now, we bound the winning probability of the state-sending version by the winning probability monogamy version.

Lemma 8.12
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
(\Delta_{n})
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be damping sequence,
c:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a complex conjugate, and
(\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle)
be the associated approximate maximally entangled state. Writing
\Tr_{1}:\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
the partial trace on the first copy on
\mathcal{H}
, we have that for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\Tr_{1}\left[(A\otimes\mathbb{I})\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert\right]=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}c\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}A^{\dagger}\Delta_{n}c.

(154)

Proof.

Using the singular-value decomposition
\Delta_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}\vphantom{\phi_{n,i}\chi_{n,i}}\left\lvert\smash{\phi_{n,i}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\chi_{n,i}}\right\rvert
, the associated approximate maximally entangled state
\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}\lvert\phi_{n,i}\rangle\otimes c\lvert\chi_{n,i}\rangle
. Then,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Tr_{1}\left[(A\otimes\mathbb{I})\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert\right]&=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{i,j}s_{n,i}s_{n,j}\vphantom{\phi_{n,j}A\phi_{n,i}}\left\langle\smash{\phi_{n,j}}\middle|\smash{A\phi_{n,i}}\right\rangle\vphantom{c\chi_{n,i}c\chi_{n,j}}\left\lvert\smash{c\chi_{n,i}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{c\chi_{n,j}}\right\rvert\\
&=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{i,j}s_{n,i}s_{n,j}\vphantom{cA\phi_{n,i}c\phi_{n,j}}\left\langle\smash{cA\phi_{n,i}}\middle|\smash{c\phi_{n,j}}\right\rangle\vphantom{c\chi_{n,i}c\chi_{n,j}}\left\lvert\smash{c\chi_{n,i}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{c\chi_{n,j}}\right\rvert\\
&=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{i,j}s_{n,i}s_{n,j}\vphantom{c\chi_{n,i}c\phi_{n,i}}\left\lvert\smash{c\chi_{n,i}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{c\phi_{n,i}}\right\rvert(cAc)^{\dagger}\vphantom{c\phi_{n,j}c\chi_{n,j}}\left\lvert\smash{c\phi_{n,j}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{c\chi_{n,j}}\right\rvert.\end{split}

(155)

To simplify this, note that for any
\lvert\phi\rangle,\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}s_{n,i}\vphantom{\psi c\chi_{n,i}}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{c\chi_{n,i}}\right\rangle\!\!\vphantom{c\phi_{n,i}\phi}\left\langle\smash{c\phi_{n,i}}\middle|\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=\sum_{i}s_{n,i}\vphantom{c\phi\phi_{n,i}}\left\langle\smash{c\phi}\middle|\smash{\phi_{n,i}}\right\rangle\!\!\vphantom{\chi_{n,i}c\psi}\left\langle\smash{\chi_{n,i}}\middle|\smash{c\psi}\right\rangle=\vphantom{c\phi\Delta_{n}c\psi}\left\langle\smash{c\phi}\middle|\smash{\Delta_{n}c\psi}\right\rangle=\vphantom{c\Delta_{n}c\psi\phi}\left\langle\smash{c\Delta_{n}c\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=\vphantom{\psi(c\Delta_{n}c)^{\dagger}\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{(c\Delta_{n}c)^{\dagger}\phi}\right\rangle,

(156)

so
\Tr_{1}\left[(A\otimes\mathbb{I})\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert\right]=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}(c\Delta_{n}c)^{\dagger}(cAc)^{\dagger}(c\Delta_{n}c)
. Finally, note that conjugation by a complex conjugate commutes with the adjoint: for any
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\vphantom{\psi(cTc)^{\dagger}\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{(cTc)^{\dagger}}\middle\rvert\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=\vphantom{cTc\psi\phi}\left\langle\smash{cTc\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=\vphantom{c\phi Tc\psi}\left\langle\smash{c\phi}\middle|\smash{Tc\psi}\right\rangle=\vphantom{T^{\dagger}c\phi c\psi}\left\langle\smash{T^{\dagger}c\phi}\middle|\smash{c\psi}\right\rangle=\vphantom{\psi cT^{\dagger}c\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{cT^{\dagger}c\phi}\right\rangle,

(157)

and therefore
\Tr_{1}\left[(A\otimes\mathbb{I})\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert\right]=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}c\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}ccA^{\dagger}cc\Delta_{n}c=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}c\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}A^{\dagger}\Delta_{n}c
.
∎

Theorem 8.13
.

Let
\texttt{G}_{n}=(G,\mathcal{S},E,F,\Delta_{n},c)
be the state-sending version of a coset monogamy game
G
, and
(\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle)
be the approximate maximally entangled state associated to
(\Delta_{n})
. For any strategy
\texttt{S}=(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},B,C,\Phi)
for
\texttt{G}_{n}
, write
\texttt{S}_{n}=\left\lparen\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},B,C,(\mathbb{I}\otimes\Phi)(\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert)\right\rparen
, which is a strategy for
G
. We have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}_{n}}(\texttt{S})=\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S}_{n}).

(158)

In particular, this implies that
\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{n})\leq\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G})
. However, we do not necessarily have that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G}_{n})=\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G})
, as this does not even always hold in the case of finite information.

Proof.

First, we may write

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S}_{n})&=\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Tr\left[(A^{H}\otimes B^{H}\otimes C^{H})(E_{H})(\mathbb{I}\otimes\Phi)(\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert)\right]\\
&=\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Tr\left[\left\lparen\int dA^{H}(gH,\gamma)\otimes(B^{H}\otimes C^{H})(EgH\times(F\gamma)|_{H})\right\rparen(\mathbb{I}\otimes\Phi)(\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert)\right].\end{split}

(159)

Fix some
H\in\mathcal{S}
and let
(F_{k}=\sum_{i}M^{k}_{i}\chi_{E^{k}_{i}})
be a sequence of simple functions
G/H\times\hat{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{H})
that converges strongly pointwise to
(gH,\gamma)\mapsto(B^{H}\otimes C^{H})(EgH\times(F\gamma)|_{H})
. Then.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Tr\left[\left\lparen\int dA^{H}\otimes F_{k}\right\rparen(\mathbb{I}\otimes\Phi)(\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert)\right]&=\sum_{i}\Tr\left[\left\lparen A^{H}(E^{k}_{i})\otimes M^{k}_{i}\right\rparen(\mathbb{I}\otimes\Phi)(\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert)\right]\\
&=\sum_{i}\Tr\left[M^{k}_{i}\Phi\left\lparen\Tr_{1}\left[(A^{H}(E^{k}_{i})\otimes\mathbb{I})\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\lvert\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rvert\right]\right\rparen\right]\\
&=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}\Tr\left[M^{k}_{i}\Phi(c\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}A^{H}(E^{k}_{i})\Delta_{n}c)\right]\\
&=\sum_{i}\int_{E^{k}_{i}}\Tr\left[M^{k}_{i}\Phi(\sigma^{H}_{n})\right]d\mu^{H}_{n}\\
&=\int\Tr\left[F_{k}(gH,\gamma)\Phi(\sigma^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma))\right]d\mu^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma).\end{split}

(160)

By weak convergence of the integral, we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S}_{n})&=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\int\Tr\left[F_{k}(gH,\gamma)\Phi(\sigma^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma))\right]d\mu^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma)\\
&=\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\int\Tr\left[(B^{H}\otimes C^{H})(EgH\times(F\gamma)|_{H})\Phi(\sigma^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma))\right]d\mu^{H}_{n}(gH,\gamma)\\
&=\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}_{n}}(\texttt{S}).\end{split}

(161)

∎

9
Monogamy games for compact groups

In this section, we extend the bound to games on compact groups – groups that may be both non-abelian and infinite. However, the compactness is required here, unlike in the abelian case of
Section

8
, in order to be able to make use of the Peter-Weyl theorem when considering the representations. This provides a class of groups that, although infinite, are conceptually more similar to the finite groups of
Section

7
.

9.1
Compact coset measure

Let
G
be a compact Hausdorff group. The space of quantum states on
G
is again
L^{2}(G)
with inner product given by the Haar integral. We know, by the Peter-Weyl theorem, that the matrix elements of the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
G
are orthogonal, and span
L^{2}(G)
as a Hilbert space. We can again choose a full set of finite-dimensional irreps
\textsf{Irr}(G)
, by making use of the axiom of choice, and their matrix elements
\textsf{IB}(G)
. Then, for
\gamma_{m,n},\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\in\textsf{IB}(G)
, we get the orthogonality relation extending Schur orthogonality

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma_{m,n},\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{G}:=\int\overline{\gamma_{m,n}(g)}\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}(g)d_{G}g=\frac{1}{d_{\gamma}}\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}.

(162)

Also, since we will be summing over elements of
\textsf{IB}(G)
, we can trivially endow it with the
\sigma
-algebra of all sets
\mathscr{P}(\textsf{IB}(G))
and the counting measure.

Let
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup. Unlike the abelian case,
G/H
is not necessarily a group, so we will need to fix a set of coset representatives
\textsf{CS}(H)
, again using the axiom of choice. For
g\in G
, write
[g]_{H}\in\textsf{CS}(H)
for its representative, dropping the subscript when
H
is obvious. As the Haar measures of
G
and
H
induce a measure on
G/H
, they induce a measure on
\textsf{CS}(H)
. The
\sigma
-algebra is the Borel algebra of the quotient topology
\mathscr{B}(\textsf{CS}(H))=\left\{{E\subseteq\textsf{CS}(H)}\middle|{EH\in\mathscr{B}(G)}\right\}
, and the corresponding measure
\mu_{\textsf{CS}(H)}(E)=\mu_{G}(EH)
. We denote
d[g]=d\mu_{\textsf{CS}(H)}([g])
. This measure interacts well with the Haar measures on
G
and
H
.

Lemma 9.1
.

•

The measurable functions
\textsf{CS}(H)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
may be identified with the measurable functions
G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
that are constant on the cosets of
H
. If
f:\textsf{CS}(H)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is integrable

\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}f([g])d[g]=\int_{G}f([g])d_{G}g.

•

If
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is integrable,

\int_{G}f(g)d_{G}g=\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\int_{H}f([g]h)d_{H}hd[g].

•

If
E\in\mathscr{B}(G)
, then

\mu_{G}(E)=\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\mu_{H}([g]^{-1}E\cap H)d[g].

Proof.

Let
f:\textsf{CS}(H)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be measurable. So for each Borel set of
B\subseteq\mathbb{C}
,
f^{-1}(B)\in\mathscr{B}(\textsf{CS}(H))

\iff

f^{-1}(B)H\in\mathscr{B}(G)
. Letting
f^{\prime}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be
f^{\prime}(g)=f([g])
,
(f^{\prime})^{-1}(B)=\{g\in G|f([g])\in B\}=f^{-1}(B)H
, so
f^{\prime}
is measurable. Conversely, if
f^{\prime}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a measurable function constant on the cosets, let
f^{\prime\prime}=f^{\prime}|_{\textsf{CS}(H)}
. Then,
(f^{\prime\prime})^{-1}(B)H=((f^{\prime})^{-1}B\cap\textsf{CS}(H))H=(f^{\prime})^{-1}(B)\in\mathscr{B}(G)
. So,
f\leftrightarrow f^{\prime}
provides the bijective correspondence we were looking for. Let
f:\textsf{CS}(H)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is integrable. There exists a sequence of simple functions that
(f_{n})
that converges pointwise to
f
. Then,
(f_{n}^{\prime})
converges to
f^{\prime}
. For some
n
, writing
f_{n}=\sum_{i}c_{i}\chi_{E_{i}}
,

\int f_{n}([g])d[g]=\sum_{i}c_{i}\mu_{\textsf{CS}(H)}(E_{i})=\sum_{i}c_{i}\mu_{G}(E_{i}H)=\int f_{n}^{\prime}(g)d_{G}g.

Therefore,

\int f([g])d[g]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int f_{n}([g])d[g]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int f_{n}^{\prime}(g)d_{G}g=\int f^{\prime}(g)d_{G}g=\int f([g])d_{G}g.

Let
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be integrable. Consider the function
G\times H\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
(g,h)\mapsto f(gh)
. As the product is continuous, this is a composition of measurable functions, so measurable. Also, by invariance of the Haar measure on
G
,

\int_{H}\int_{G}|f(gh)|d_{G}gd_{H}h=\int_{H}\int_{G}|f(g)|d_{G}gd_{H}h=\int_{G}|f(g)|d_{G}g,

which means by Tonelli’s theorem that the map is integrable on the product measure space. So, the integral
\int f(gh)d_{H}h
exists for almost every
g\in G
and the map
g\mapsto\int f(gh)d_{H}h
is measurable with

\int_{G}\int_{H}f(gh)d_{H}hd_{G}g=\int_{H}\int_{G}f(gh)d_{G}gd_{H}h=\int_{G}f(g)d_{G}g

by Fubini’s theorem. Finally, by invariance of the Haar measure on
H
,
\int_{H}f(gh)d_{H}h
is constant on the cosets of
H
, so

\int_{G}f(g)d_{G}g=\int_{G}\int_{H}f(gh)d_{H}hd_{G}g=\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\int_{H}f([g]h)d_{H}hd[g].

For the last point, take
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
to be
f=\chi_{E}
. Then,
f
is bounded and measurable, so integrable, giving

\mu_{G}(E)=\int_{G}\chi_{E}(g)d_{G}g=\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\int_{H}\chi_{E}([g]h)d_{H}hd[g]=\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\mu_{H}([g]^{-1}E\cap H)d[g].

∎

Now, similarly to
Definition

8.4
, we define the coset operator measure for
H
.

Definition 9.2
.

Let
G
be a compact Hausdorff group and
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup. The
coset operator measure
is the map
A^{H}:\mathscr{B}(\textsf{CS}(H))\otimes\mathscr{P}(\textsf{IB}(H))\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))
defined, for
E=\bigcup_{\gamma_{m,n}}E_{\gamma_{m,n}}\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\subseteq\textsf{CS}(H)\times\textsf{IB}(H)
measurable and
\lvert\phi\rangle,\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
, as

\displaystyle\vphantom{\phi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}}\left\langle\phi\circ[g],\gamma_{m,n}\right\rangle_{H}\left\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\right\rangle_{H}d[g],

(163)

where
[g]
is seen as the left shift
[g](h)=[g]h
.

It is direct to see that
A^{H}(E)
is linear, where it is defined, by linearity of the integral. Next, it is well-defined as a function on
L^{2}(G)
as it does not depend on the choice of representative of
\lvert\psi\rangle
. In fact, if
\lvert\psi\rangle=0
, then
\int_{H}|\psi([g]h)|^{2}d_{H}h=0
for almost all
[g]\in\textsf{CS}(H)
, so
\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}=0
almost everywhere, and therefore
A^{H}(E)\lvert\psi\rangle=0
. Positivity is due to the fact that

\displaystyle\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}}\lvert\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}\rvert^{2}d[g]\geq 0,

(164)

and that
A^{H}(E)
is Hermitian, given that

\displaystyle\vphantom{\varphi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\varphi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}}\overline{\langle\gamma_{m,n},\varphi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}\langle\psi\circ[g],\gamma_{m,n}\rangle_{H}}d[g]=\overline{\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\varphi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\varphi}\right\rangle}.

(165)

To show boundedness, it follows from the polarization identity that we need only show that the collection of
\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle
for all
\|\lvert\psi\rangle\|=1
is bounded. First, note that by monotonicity of the integral, if
E\subseteq E^{\prime}
, we have
E_{\gamma_{m,n}}\subseteq E^{\prime}_{\gamma_{m,n}}
for all
\gamma_{m,n}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle&=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}}\lvert\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}\rvert^{2}d[g]\\
&\leq\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int_{E^{\prime}_{\gamma_{m,n}}}\lvert\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}\rvert^{2}d[g]=\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E^{\prime})\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E^{\prime})}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(166)

So,
\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle\leq\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(\textsf{CS}(H)\times\textsf{IB}(H))\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(\textsf{CS}(H)\times\textsf{IB}(H))}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle
, and using Tonelli’s theorem

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(\textsf{CS}(H)\times\textsf{IB}(H))\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(\textsf{CS}(H)\times\textsf{IB}(H))}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle&=\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\langle\psi\circ[g],\gamma_{m,n}\rangle_{H}\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}d[g]\\
&=\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\langle\psi\circ[g],\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}d[g]\\
&=\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\int_{H}|\psi([g]h)|^{2}d_{H}hd[g]\\
&=\int_{G}|\psi(g)|^{2}d_{G}g=1.\end{split}

(167)

Thus,
\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle\leq 1
and the positivity implies
\|A^{H}(E)\|\leq 1
.

Lemma 9.3
.

A^{H}
is a PVM measure.

Proof.

Most importantly, we need that
A^{H}
is weakly countably additive. Let
E^{1},E^{2},\ldots\subseteq\textsf{CS}(H)\times\textsf{IB}(H)
be a countable disjoint collection of measurable sets. We have that, for each
\gamma_{m,n}\in\textsf{IB}(H)
,
E^{1}_{\gamma_{m,n}},E^{2}_{\gamma_{m,n}},\ldots
are disjoint. So, using monotone convergence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{\psi A^{H}\Big\lparen\bigcup_{i}E^{i}\Big\rparen\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}\Big\lparen\bigcup_{i}E^{i}\Big\rparen}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle&=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int\chi_{\bigcup_{i}E^{i}_{\gamma_{m,n}}}([g])\lvert\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}\rvert^{2}d[g]\\
&=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\chi_{E^{i}_{\gamma_{m,n}}}([g])\lvert\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}\rvert^{2}d[g]\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int\chi_{E^{i}_{\gamma_{m,n}}}([g])\lvert\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}\rvert^{2}d[g]\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E^{i})\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E^{i})}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle,\end{split}

(168)

where
\chi_{E}
is the characteristic function, that is
\chi_{E}(x)=1
if
x\in E
and
\chi_{E}(x)=0
if
x\notin E
. Therefore, by the polarization identity,
A^{H}\left\lparen\bigcup_{i}E^{i}\right\rparen=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}A^{H}(E^{i})
weakly.

Next, we want that
A^{H}
is projective. From the work before the lemma, we have that
A^{H}(\textsf{CS}(H)\otimes\textsf{IB}(H))=\mathbb{I}_{L^{2}(G)}
, so it is a measurement. To show that
A^{H}
is projective, note that the definition directly implies that

\displaystyle(A^{H}(E)\lvert\psi\rangle)(g)=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\chi_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}}([g])\gamma_{m,n}([g]^{-1}g)\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}.

(169)

So, as everything is nice and bounded, we can invoke Fubini’s theorem a couple times to get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E)A^{H}(F)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E)A^{H}(F)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=\int_{G}\overline{(A^{H}(E)\lvert\psi\rangle)(g)}(A^{H}(F)\lvert\psi\rangle)(g)d_{G}g\\
&=\int_{G}\sum_{\gamma_{m,n},\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}d_{\gamma}d_{\gamma^{\prime}}\chi_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}\cap F_{\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}}([g])\overline{\gamma_{m,n}}([g]^{-1}g)\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}([g]^{-1}g)\langle\psi\circ[g],\gamma_{m,n}\rangle_{H}\langle\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}d_{G}g\\
&=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n},\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}d_{\gamma}d_{\gamma^{\prime}}\int_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}\cap F_{\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}}\langle\psi\circ[g],\gamma_{m,n}\rangle_{H}\langle\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}\langle\gamma_{m,n},\gamma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\rangle_{H}d_{G}[g]\\
&=\sum_{\gamma_{m,n}}d_{\gamma}\int_{E_{\gamma_{m,n}}\cap F_{\gamma_{m,n}}}\langle\psi\circ[g],\gamma_{m,n}\rangle_{H}\langle\gamma_{m,n},\psi\circ[g]\rangle_{H}d_{G}[g]\\
&=\vphantom{\psi A^{H}(E\cap F)\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{H}(E\cap F)}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(170)

Therefore,
A^{H}(E)A^{H}(F)=A^{H}(E\cap F)
.
∎

9.2
Compact coset measure game

Definition 9.4
.

An
compact coset measure monogamy game
is a tuple
\texttt{G}=\left\lparen G,\mathcal{S},E\right\rparen
, where
G
is a compact Hausdorff group,
\mathcal{S}
is a finite set of closed subgroups of
G
, and
E\subseteq G
and is a symmetric neighborhood of identity,
i.e.

E=E^{-1}
and
1\in E
.

A
(quantum) strategy
for a coset measure game
G
is a tuple
\texttt{S}=\left\lparen\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C},B,C,\rho\right\rparen
, where
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are Hilbert spaces,
B=\{{B^{H}:\mathscr{B}(\textsf{CS}(H))\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B})}|{H\in\mathcal{S}}\}
and
C=\{{C^{H}:\mathscr{P}(\textsf{IB}(H))\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{C})}|{H\in\mathcal{S}}\}
are collections of POVM measures, and
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(L^{2}(G)\otimes\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{C})
.

Let
G
be a coset measure game and
S
be a strategy for it. The
winning probability
of
S
is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})=\underset{H\in\mathcal{S}}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\Tr\left[(A^{H}\otimes B^{H}\otimes C^{H})\lparen E_{H}\rparen\rho\right],

(171)

where
E_{H}=\left\{{([g]_{H},\gamma_{m,n},[eg]_{H},\gamma_{m,n})}\middle|{g\in G,\gamma_{m,n}\in\textsf{IB}(H),e\in E}\right\}
and the expectation with respect to
H\in\mathcal{S}
is uniform.

The winning probability of
G
is
\mathfrak{w}(\texttt{G})=\sup_{\texttt{S}}\mathfrak{w}_{\texttt{G}}(\texttt{S})
.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
\texttt{G}=(G,\mathcal{S},E)
be a compact coset measure game. Then, for any complete set of orthogonal permutations
\pi_{i}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
, the winning probability is bounded by

\displaystyle w(\texttt{G})\leq\underset{i}{\text{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{$\mathbb{E}$}}}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathcal{S}\\
\gamma\in\textsf{Irr}(H)\\
g\in G\end{subarray}}\sqrt{d_{\gamma}\mu_{H}(H\cap gE\pi_{i}(H))}.

(172)

Lemma 9.6
.

Let
H,K\leq G
be closed subgroups and let
E\subseteq G
be a symmetric open neighborhood of the identity. Then, for any
\gamma_{m,n}\in\textsf{IB}(H)
and
q^{\prime}\in\textsf{CS}(K)
,

\displaystyle\left\|A^{H}\left\lparen\textsf{CS}(H)\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\right\rparen A^{K}\left\lparen[Eq^{\prime}]_{K}\times\textsf{IB}(K)\right\rparen\right\|\leq\sup_{g\in G}\sqrt{d_{\gamma}\mu_{H}(H\cap gEK)}.

(173)

Proof.

Since
\textsf{IB}(K)
gives rise to an orthonormal basis, for any
\lvert\varphi\rangle,\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vphantom{\varphi A^{K}\left\lparen[Eq^{\prime}]_{K}\times\textsf{IB}(K)\right\rparen\psi}\left\langle\smash{\varphi}\middle\rvert\smash{A^{K}\left\lparen[Eq^{\prime}]_{K}\times\textsf{IB}(K)\right\rparen}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle&=\sum_{\varrho_{i,j}\in\textsf{IB}(K)}d_{\varrho}\int_{[Eq^{\prime}]_{K}}\left\langle\varphi\circ[q],\varrho_{i,j}\right\rangle_{K}\left\langle\varrho_{i,j},\psi\circ[q]\right\rangle_{K}d[q]\\
&=\int_{[Eq^{\prime}]_{K}}\left\langle\varphi\circ[q],\psi\circ[q]\right\rangle_{K}d[q]=\int_{[Eq^{\prime}]_{K}}\int_{K}\overline{\varphi}([q]k)\psi([q]k)d_{K}kd[q]\\
&=\int_{Eq^{\prime}K}\overline{\varphi}(q)\psi(q)d_{G}q.\end{split}

(174)

That is,
A^{K}\left\lparen[Eq^{\prime}]_{K}\times\textsf{IB}(K)\right\rparen=\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}K}
, the projector onto the subspace of states
\lvert\psi\rangle
such that
\psi(g)=0
for almost all
g\notin Eq^{\prime}K
. To get the norm, we consider
\|A^{H}\left\lparen\textsf{CS}(H)\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\right\rparen\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\lvert\psi\rangle\|
for some unit vector
\lvert\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(G)
and then take the supremum. First,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|A^{H}\left\lparen\textsf{CS}(H)\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\right\rparen\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\lvert\psi\rangle\|^{2}&=\vphantom{\psi\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}H}A^{H}\left\lparen\textsf{CS}(H)\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\right\rparen\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\psi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}H}A^{H}\left\lparen\textsf{CS}(H)\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\right\rparen\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}K}}\middle\rvert\smash{\psi}\right\rangle\\
&=d_{\gamma}\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}\left\lvert\left\langle\gamma_{m,n},(\chi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\psi)\circ[g]\right\rangle_{H}\right\rvert^{2}d[g].\end{split}

(175)

Now, let
p_{[g]}=\int_{H}|\psi([g]h)|^{2}d_{H}h
– this is finite for almost every
[g]\in\textsf{CS}(H)
and the map
[g]\mapsto p_{[g]}
is in

– and then
\lvert\psi_{[g]}\rangle=\frac{\lvert\psi\rangle}{\sqrt{p_{[g]}}}
. In particular,
\int_{H}|\psi_{[g]}([g]h)|^{2}d_{H}h=1
for almost all
[g]
, and we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|A^{H}\left\lparen\textsf{CS}(H)\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\right\rparen\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\lvert\psi\rangle\|&=\sqrt{d_{\gamma}\int_{\textsf{CS}(H)}p_{[g]}\left\lvert\left\langle\gamma_{m,n},(\chi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\psi_{[g]})\circ[g]\right\rangle_{H}\right\rvert^{2}d[g]}\\
&\leq\sqrt{d_{\gamma}}\sup_{[g]\in\textsf{CS}(H)}\left\lvert\left\langle\gamma_{m,n},(\chi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\psi_{[g]})\circ[g]\right\rangle_{H}\right\rvert\\
&=\sqrt{d_{\gamma}}\sup_{[g]\in\textsf{CS}(H)}\Big\lvert\int_{H}\overline{\gamma_{m,n}}(h)\chi_{Eq^{\prime}K}([g]h)\psi_{[g]}([g]h)d_{H}h\Big\rvert\\
&\leq\sqrt{d_{\gamma}}\sup_{[g]\in\textsf{CS}(H)}\int_{H\cap[g]^{-1}Eq^{\prime}K}|\gamma_{m,n}(h)||\psi_{[g]}([g]h)|d_{H}h\\
&\leq\sqrt{d_{\gamma}}\sup_{[g]\in\textsf{CS}(H)}\int_{H\cap[g]^{-1}Eq^{\prime}K}|\psi_{[g]}([g]h)|d_{H}h.\end{split}

(176)

As
H\cap[g]^{-1}Eq^{\prime}K
is a finite measure space
\|f\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{\mu_{H}(H\cap[g]^{-1}Eq^{\prime}K)}\|f\|_{2}
, giving

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lvert\left\langle\gamma_{m,n},(\chi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\psi)\circ[g]\right\rangle\right\rvert&\leq\sqrt{\mu_{H}(H\cap[g]^{-1}Eq^{\prime}K)}\int_{H\cap[g]^{-1}Eq^{\prime}K}|\psi_{[g]}([g]h)|^{2}d_{H}h\\
&\leq\sqrt{\mu_{H}(H\cap[g]^{-1}Eq^{\prime}K)}.\end{split}

(177)

Taking the supremum over
\lvert\psi\rangle
gives

\displaystyle\left\|A^{H}\left\lparen\textsf{CS}(H)\times\{\gamma_{m,n}\}\right\rparen\Pi_{Eq^{\prime}K}\right\|\leq\sup_{[g]\in\textsf{CS}(H)}\sqrt{d_{\gamma}\mu_{H}(H\cap[g]^{-1}Eq^{\prime}K)}\leq\sup_{g\in G}\sqrt{d_{\gamma}\mu_{H}(H\cap gEK)}.

(178)

∎

Appendix A
Integration by an operator-valued measure

In this section, we fix a measurable space
(X,\mathscr{S})
, a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, and
P:\mathscr{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
a POVM measure. We generally follow the standard notation of
[
Axl20
]
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function. If

\displaystyle\sup\left\{{\int|f|d\vphantom{vPv}\left\langle\smash{v}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle}\middle|{\lvert v\rangle\in\mathcal{H},\|\lvert v\rangle\|\leq 1}\right\}<\infty,

(184)

we say

is operator integrable and define the integral
\int fdP\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
as the operator such that

\displaystyle\vphantom{u\int fdPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{\int fdP}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle=\int fd\vphantom{uPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle

(185)

for all
\lvert u\rangle,\lvert v\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
.

As the complex measure
\vphantom{uPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle
is linear in
\lvert v\rangle
and antilinear in
\lvert u\rangle
,
\int fdP
is in fact a linear operator; and, by the polarization identity,
\|\int fdP\|<\infty
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
f=\sum_{i}c_{i}\chi_{A_{i}}
be a measurable simple function. Then,
\int fdP=\sum_{i}c_{i}P(A_{i})
.

Proof.

Let
\lvert u\rangle,\lvert v\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
. Then,

\displaystyle\vphantom{u\int fdPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{\int fdP}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle=\sum_{i}c_{i}\int\chi_{A_{i}}d\vphantom{uPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle=\vphantom{u\sum_{i}c_{i}P(A_{i})v}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{\sum_{i}c_{i}P(A_{i})}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle

(186)

∎

We can, as for a usual measure, approximate the operator integral of a function by the integrals of simple functions that approximate it. Let
(f_{n})
be a sequence of simple functions that converges pointwise to
f
. Then, as for each
\lvert u\rangle,\lvert v\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
,
\int f_{n}d\vphantom{uPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle\rightarrow\int fd\vphantom{uPv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle
, the sequence
\left\lparen\int f_{n}dP\right\rparen
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
converges
weakly
to
\int fdP
.

We would like to able to integrate not just scalar functions, but operator-valued functions by a POVM measure. The following proceeds similarly to Pettis’ integral
[
Yos95
]
.

Definition A.3
.

We say that
F:X\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
is
weakly measurable

\lvert u\rangle,\lvert v\rangle\in\mathcal{K}
,
\vphantom{uFv}\left\langle\smash{u}\middle\rvert\smash{F}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle
is measurable.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\Delta\geq 0
. The open ball

\displaystyle B_{\Delta}:=\left\{{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})}\middle|{\|A\|<\Delta}\right\}

(187)

is separable in the strong operator topology.

In particular,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})=\bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty}B_{N}
is separable.

Proof.

Let
D
be a countable dense subset of
\mathcal{K}
and
\left\{{\lvert n\rangle}\middle|{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right\}
be an orthonormal basis. Set

\displaystyle D_{\Delta}=\{{\vphantom{v_{1}1}\left\lvert\smash{v_{1}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{1}\right\rvert+\ldots+\vphantom{v_{n}n}\left\lvert\smash{v_{n}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{n}\right\rvert}|{n\in\mathbb{N};\lvert v_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\lvert v_{n}\rangle\in D}\}\cap B_{\Delta}.

(188)

D_{\Delta}
is countable as
D
is countable, and I claim
D_{\Delta}
is dense in
B_{\Delta}
. Let

. By density of

and

such that
\|\lvert v^{n}_{i}\rangle-A\lvert i\rangle\|<\min\left\{{\frac{1}{n^{2}},\frac{\Delta-\|A\|}{n}}\right\}
. Set
A_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\vphantom{v^{n}_{i}i}\left\lvert\smash{v^{n}_{i}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{i}\right\rvert
, so we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|A_{n}\|\leq\Big\|A\sum_{i=1}^{n}\vphantom{i}\left\lvert\smash{i}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{i}\right\rvert\Big\|+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\lvert v^{n}_{i}\rangle-A\lvert i\rangle\|<\|A\|+(\Delta-\|A\|)=\Delta,\end{split}

(189)

giving

. I claim now that the sequence
(A_{n})
converges strongly to
A
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\lvert v\rangle\in\mathcal{K}
. There exists

\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}\left\lvert\vphantom{iv}\left\langle\smash{i}\middle|\smash{v}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}<\left\lparen\frac{\varepsilon}{2\Delta}\right\rparen^{2}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(A-A_{n})\lvert v\rangle\|&\leq\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(A\lvert i\rangle-\lvert v^{n}_{i}\rangle)\vphantom{iv}\left\langle\smash{i}\middle|\smash{v}\right\rangle\Big\|+\Big\|A\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\vphantom{iv}\left\langle\smash{i}\middle|\smash{v}\right\rangle\lvert i\rangle\Big\|\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|A\lvert i\rangle-\lvert v^{n}_{i}\rangle\|\|v\|+\|A\|\sqrt{\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\left\lvert\vphantom{iv}\left\langle\smash{i}\middle|\smash{v}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}}\\
&<\frac{\|v\|}{n}+\Delta\frac{\varepsilon}{2\Delta}<\varepsilon.\end{split}

(190)

∎

Lemma A.5
.

Let

Proof.

Let

is bounded, we can corestrict it to

in
\mathcal{K}

Definition A.6

Let

\vphantom{k_{i}F_{n}k_{j}}\left\langle\smash{k_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{F_{n}}\middle\rvert\smash{k_{j}}\right\rangle\rightarrow\vphantom{k_{i}Fk_{j}}\left\langle\smash{k_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{F}\middle\rvert\smash{k_{j}}\right\rangle

\lvert v\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{p_{i}}\lvert k_{i}\rangle\otimes\lvert h_{i}\rangle
, we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}S&\leq\Big\lvert\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sqrt{p_{i}p_{j}}\int\vphantom{k_{i}F_{n}k_{j}}\left\langle\smash{k_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{F_{n}}\middle\rvert\smash{k_{j}}\right\rangle d\vphantom{h_{i}Ph_{j}}\left\langle\smash{h_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{h_{j}}\right\rangle\Big\rvert+\sum_{i,j}^{m}p_{i}p_{j}=\Big\lvert\vphantom{v\sum_{l}M^{n}_{l}\otimes P(E^{n}_{l})v}\left\langle\smash{v}\middle\rvert\smash{\sum_{l}M^{n}_{l}\otimes P(E^{n}_{l})}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle\Big\rvert+1\\
&\leq\sup_{l}\|M^{n}_{l}\|+1=\sup_{x\in X}\|F_{n}(x)\|+1,\end{split}

(199)

and thus
S\leq\sup_{x}\|F(x)\|+2
, giving the result.
∎

Theorem A.9
.

Let
F:X\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
be norm bounded and weakly measurable. For any sequence of weakly operator integrable simple functions
(F_{n})
that converges to
F
strongly pointwise and
\sup_{n}\left\|\int F_{n}\otimes dP\right\|<\infty
, we have that
\int F_{n}\otimes dP\rightarrow\int F\otimes dP
weakly.

Note that
Lemma

A.5
shows that such a sequence of simple functions exists.

Proof.

Write
F_{n}=\sum_{l}M^{n}_{l}\chi_{E^{n}_{l}}
and
W=\max\left\{{\|\int F\otimes dP\|,\sup_{n}}\right\}<\infty
. Fix
\lvert v\rangle\in\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{H}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Via the Schmidt decomposition, we may write
\lvert v\rangle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{p_{i}}\lvert k_{i}\rangle\otimes\lvert h_{i}\rangle
. As the norm of
\lvert v\rangle
is finite, there exists
I\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{i=I+1}^{\infty}p_{i}<\min\{\frac{\varepsilon}{4(2\|\lvert v\rangle\|+1)W},1\}
; let
\lvert v^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{i=I+1}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{i}}\lvert k_{i}\rangle\otimes\lvert h_{i}\rangle
. Next, as
\vphantom{k_{i}F_{n}k_{j}}\left\langle\smash{k_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{F_{n}}\middle\rvert\smash{k_{j}}\right\rangle\rightarrow\vphantom{k_{i}Fk_{j}}\left\langle\smash{k_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{F}\middle\rvert\smash{k_{j}}\right\rangle
pointwise, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq N
,
\left\lvert\int\vphantom{k_{i}F_{n}-Fk_{j}}\left\langle\smash{k_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{F_{n}-F}\middle\rvert\smash{k_{j}}\right\rangle d\vphantom{h_{i}Ph_{j}}\left\langle\smash{h_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{h_{j}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2I\|\lvert v\rangle\|^{2}}
for all
i,j\leq I
. Then, for any
n\geq N
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big\lvert\vphantom{v\int(F_{n}-F)\otimes dPv}\left\langle\smash{v}\middle\rvert\smash{\int(F_{n}-F)\otimes dP}\middle\rvert\smash{v}\right\rangle\Big\rvert&\leq\Big\lvert\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\sqrt{p_{i}p_{j}}\int\vphantom{k_{i}F_{n}-Fk_{j}}\left\langle\smash{k_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{F_{n}-F}\middle\rvert\smash{k_{j}}\right\rangle d\vphantom{h_{i}Ph_{j}}\left\langle\smash{h_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{h_{j}}\right\rangle\Big\rvert\\
&\qquad+(2\left\|\lvert v\rangle\right\|+1)\|\lvert v^{\prime}\rangle\|\Big\|\int(F_{n}-F)\otimes dP\Big\|\\
&\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\sqrt{p_{i}p_{j}}\Big\lvert\int\vphantom{k_{i}F_{n}-Fk_{j}}\left\langle\smash{k_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{F_{n}-F}\middle\rvert\smash{k_{j}}\right\rangle d\vphantom{h_{i}Ph_{j}}\left\langle\smash{h_{i}}\middle\rvert\smash{P}\middle\rvert\smash{h_{j}}\right\rangle\Big\rvert+(2\left\|\lvert v\rangle\right\|+1)\|\lvert v^{\prime}\rangle\|2W\\
&<\Big\lparen\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sqrt{p_{i}}\Big\rparen^{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{2I\|\lvert v\rangle\|^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\varepsilon.\end{split}

(200)

∎

Definition A.10
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{S},P)
and
(Y,\mathscr{T},Q)
be POVM operator measure spaces. Then, the
tensor product measure
on the measurable space
(X\times Y,\mathscr{S}\otimes\mathscr{T})
is

\displaystyle(P\otimes Q)(E)=\int_{Y}P((E)^{y})\otimes dQ(y),

(201)

where
(E)^{y}=\left\{{x\in X}\middle|{(x,y)\in E}\right\}
is the cross section of
E
at
Y
.

This definition is sensible since the map
y\mapsto P((E)^{y})
is weakly measurable and bounded in norm by
1
. This definition allows us to immediately generalise Fubini’s theorem, as the matrix elements must all satify it: for any weakly operator integrable
F:X\times Y\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
,

\displaystyle\int F\otimes d(P\otimes Q)=\int_{Y}\Big\lparen\int_{X}F(x,y)\otimes dP(x)\Big\rparen\otimes dQ(y)=\int_{X}\int_{Y}F(x,y)\otimes dP(x)\otimes dQ(y).

(202)

Appendix B
Damping operators and maximally-entangled states

In this section, let
\mathcal{H}
be any Hilbert space. Write
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
for the set of trace-class operator and for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
the trace norm
\|T\|_{1}=\Tr(\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A})
; and write
\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
for the Hilbert-Schmidt operators and for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
the Hilbert-Schmidt norm
\|T\|_{2}=\sqrt{\Tr(A^{\dagger}A)}
.

Definition B.1
.

A
complex conjugate
on
\mathcal{H}
is an antilinear involutive isometry
c:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
,
i.e.

c(\alpha\lvert\psi\rangle+\beta\lvert\phi\rangle)=\overline{\alpha}c\lvert\psi\rangle+\overline{\beta}c\lvert\phi\rangle
,
c^{2}=1
, and
\left\|c\lvert\psi\rangle\right\|=\left\|\lvert\psi\rangle\right\|
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
.

We quickly work out some properties of a complex conjugate, and use that to show that any complex conjugate as defined above may be expressed as a complex conjugate with respect to some basis — the map that acts by taking the conjugates of the coefficients in the expansion with respect to a fixed orthonormal basis. For any
\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
,
\lvert\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\lvert\psi\rangle+c\lvert\psi\rangle)+\frac{1}{2}(\lvert\psi\rangle-c\lvert\psi\rangle)
so we may decompose
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-}
where
H_{\pm}=\left\{{\lvert\psi\rangle\in H}\middle|{c\lvert\psi\rangle=\pm\lvert\psi\rangle}\right\}
. As
c
is
\mathbb{R}
-linear,
\mathcal{H}_{+}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-}
are
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces, and
\mathcal{H}_{-}=i\mathcal{H}_{+}
. For
\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\vphantom{c\psi c\phi}\left\langle\smash{c\psi}\middle|\smash{c\phi}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{3}i^{k}\|c\lvert\psi\rangle+i^{k}c\lvert\phi\rangle\|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{3}i^{k}\|\lvert\psi\rangle+(-i)^{k}\lvert\phi\rangle\|^{2}=\overline{\vphantom{\psi\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi}\right\rangle},

(203)

which implies that
\vphantom{c\psi\phi}\left\langle\smash{c\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=\vphantom{c\psi c^{2}\phi}\left\langle\smash{c\psi}\middle|\smash{c^{2}\phi}\right\rangle=\overline{\vphantom{\psi c\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{c\phi}\right\rangle}
. Thus, for
\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{+}
,
\vphantom{\psi\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=\overline{\vphantom{\psi\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi}\right\rangle}
, so
\mathcal{H}_{+}
is a real inner product space. So, there exists an orthonormal basis
\left\{{\lvert n\rangle}\middle|{n\in\Gamma}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{+}
; this basis is countable iff
\mathcal{H}_{+}
is separable, which is iff
\mathcal{H}
is. Using the decomposition,
\mathcal{H}
is the closure of
\spn_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{{\lvert n\rangle}\middle|{n\in\Gamma}\right\}
. Thus, we may expand any
\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
as
\lvert\psi\rangle=\sum_{n\in\Gamma}\psi_{n}\lvert n\rangle
, so

\displaystyle c\lvert\psi\rangle=\sum_{n\in\Gamma}\overline{\psi}_{n}c\lvert n\rangle=\sum_{n\in\Gamma}\overline{\psi}_{n}\lvert n\rangle,

(204)

giving that any complex conjugate may be expressed as the complex conjugate with respect to a basis.

Definition B.2
.

•

A
damping sequence
is a sequence
(\Delta_{n})
in
\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\|\Delta_{n}\|\leq 1
, and
\Delta_{n}\lvert\psi\rangle\rightarrow\lvert\psi\rangle
and
\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}\lvert\psi\rangle\rightarrow\lvert\psi\rangle
for all
\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
.

•

The
pure tensor norm
on
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
is
\|\lvert\Psi\rangle\|_{\otimes}=\sup\big\{{\left\lvert(\langle\psi\rvert\otimes\langle\phi\rvert)\lvert\Psi\rangle\right\rvert}\big|{\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H};\|\lvert\psi\rangle\|,\|\lvert\phi\rangle\|\leq 1}\big\}
.

•

Let
c:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a complex conjugate. An
approximate maximally entangled state
is a sequence
(\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle)
in
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
such that
\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|=1
, and
\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}(\langle\psi\rvert\otimes\mathbb{I})\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\rightarrow c\lvert\psi\rangle
and
\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}(\mathbb{I}\otimes\langle\psi\rvert)\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\rightarrow c\lvert\psi\rangle
for all
\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
.

It is direct to see that every damping sequence generates an approximate maximally entangled state, and vice versa.

Lemma B.3
.

•

Let
(\Delta_{n})
be a damping sequence. Writing the singular-value decompositions
\Delta_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}\vphantom{\phi_{n,i}\chi_{n,i}}\left\lvert\smash{\phi_{n,i}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{\chi_{n,i}}\right\rvert
, the sequence
(\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle)
defined as
\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}\lvert\phi_{n,i}\rangle\otimes c\lvert\chi_{n,i}\rangle
is an approximate maximally entangled state.

•

Let
(\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle)
be an approximate maximally entangled state. Writing the Schmidt decomposition
\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{n,i}}\lvert\phi_{n,i}\rangle\otimes\lvert\chi_{n,i}\rangle
, the sequence
(\Delta_{n})
defined as
\Delta_{n}=\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{n,i}}\vphantom{\phi_{n,i}c\chi_{n,i}}\left\lvert\smash{\phi_{n,i}}\middle\rangle\!\middle\langle\smash{c\chi_{n,i}}\right\rvert
is a damping sequence.

Proof.

For the first point, note first that
\vphantom{\Psi_{n}}\left\langle\smash{\Psi_{n}}\middle|\smash{\Psi_{n}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\Tr(\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}\Delta_{n})}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}^{2}=1
. Next, for any
\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\left\lparen\langle\psi\rvert\otimes\mathbb{I}\right\rparen\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}\vphantom{\psi\phi_{n,i}}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi_{n,i}}\right\rangle c\lvert\chi_{n,i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}\lvert\chi_{n,i}\rangle\!\!\vphantom{\phi_{n,i}\psi}\left\langle\smash{\phi_{n,i}}\middle|\smash{\psi}\right\rangle=c\frac{\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}\lvert\psi\rangle.

(205)

Since
\left\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\right\|_{\otimes}=\frac{\|\Delta_{n}\|}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}
and
\|\Delta_{n}\|\rightarrow 1
, we have that
\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}\left\lparen\langle\psi\rvert\otimes\mathbb{I}\right\rparen\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle=c\frac{\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}}{\|\Delta_{n}\|}\lvert\psi\rangle\rightarrow c\lvert\psi\rangle
. In the same way,

\displaystyle\left\lparen\mathbb{I}\otimes\langle\psi\rvert\right\rparen\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}\vphantom{\psi c\chi_{n,i}}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{c\chi_{n,i}}\right\rangle\lvert\phi_{n,i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{n,i}\lvert\phi_{n,i}\rangle\vphantom{\chi_{n,i}c\psi}\left\langle\smash{\chi_{n,i}}\middle|\smash{c\psi}\right\rangle=\frac{\Delta_{n}}{\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}}c\lvert\psi\rangle,

(206)

and hence
\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}\left\lparen\langle\psi\rvert\otimes\mathbb{I}\right\rparen\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle=\frac{\Delta_{n}}{\|\Delta_{n}\|}c\lvert\psi\rangle\rightarrow\lvert\psi\rangle
.

For the second point, we have that
\|\Delta_{n}\|=\frac{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}=1
and also
\|\Delta_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{n,i}=\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}^{2}}<\infty
, so it is well-defined. Finally, for any
\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\lvert\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{n,i}}\lvert\phi_{n,i}\rangle\!\!\vphantom{c\psi\chi_{n,i}}\left\langle\smash{c\psi}\middle|\smash{\chi_{n,i}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}(\mathbb{I}\otimes\langle c\psi\rvert)\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\rightarrow c^{2}\lvert\psi\rangle=\lvert\psi\rangle

(207)

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}\lvert\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{p_{n,i}}\lvert\chi_{n,i}\rangle\!\!\vphantom{\psi\phi_{n,i}}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle|\smash{\phi_{n,i}}\right\rangle=c\frac{1}{\|\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\|_{\otimes}}(\langle\psi\rvert\otimes\mathbb{I})\lvert\Psi_{n}\rangle\rightarrow c^{2}\lvert\psi\rangle=\lvert\psi\rangle.

(208)

∎

Finally, we use damping sequences to construct states from operator-valued measures.

Definition B.4
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{S},\mu)
be a measure space and
P:\mathscr{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an operator-valued measure. A damping sequence
(\Delta_{n})
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

\mu
-damps
P
if
\|\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P\Delta_{n}\|_{1}\ll\mu
,
i.e.
if
\mu(E)=0
then
\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}=0
.

Note first that this is well-defined. As
\Delta_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
,
P(E)\Delta_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{H})
, and therefore
\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
, so the trace norm is defined on this operator. Also,
(\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n})
converges weakly to
P(E)
, so the sequence can be seen to approximate the operator-valued measure. This is because, for any
\lvert\phi\rangle,\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\lvert\vphantom{\psi\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}-P(E)\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}-P(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\phi}\right\rangle\big\rvert&\leq\big\lvert\vphantom{\psi\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}-\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}-\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\phi}\right\rangle\big\rvert+\big\lvert\vphantom{\psi\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)-P(E)\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)-P(E)}\middle\rvert\smash{\phi}\right\rangle\big\rvert\\
&\leq\big\|P(E)^{\dagger}\Delta_{n}\lvert\psi\rangle\big\|\big\|(\Delta_{n}-\mathbb{I})\lvert\phi\rangle\big\|+\big\|\lvert\psi\rangle\big\|\big\|(\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}-\mathbb{I})P(E)\lvert\phi\rangle\big\|\\
&\rightarrow 0.\end{split}

(209)

The definition of
\mu
-damping is meant to be a way to formalise the idea of damping unnormalizable states. That is, for Casimir operator
C
, we have a candidate damping sequence
(e^{-\frac{C}{2n^{2}}})
, where
\frac{1}{n}
represents the damping strength, which we refer to as
Casimir damping
. In order to extract a state-valued function
X\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
from the damped measure
\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P\Delta_{n}
, we may make use of the Radon-Nikodym theorem.

Lemma B.5
.

Let
P:\mathscr{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a POVM measure, and let
(\Delta_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
that
\mu
-damps
P
, for
\mu:\mathscr{S}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
a
\sigma
-finite measure. Then, for each
n
, there exists a measurable function
\rho_{n}:X\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
and an integrable function
\pi_{n}:X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that, for all
E\in\mathscr{S}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}=\int_{E}\rho_{n}\pi_{n}d\mu

(210)

Proof.

As
\|\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P\Delta_{n}\|=\Tr(\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P\Delta_{n})\ll\mu
, this implies that, in particular,
\vphantom{\psi\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P\Delta_{n}\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P\Delta_{n}}\middle\rvert\smash{\phi}\right\rangle\ll\mu
for all
\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
. Thus, by the Radon-Nikodym theorem, there exist integrable functions
f_{\psi,\phi}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
g:X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
\vphantom{\psi\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P(E)\Delta_{n}}\middle\rvert\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=\int f_{\psi,\phi}d\mu
and
\Tr(\Delta_{n}^{\dagger}P\Delta_{n})=\int gd\mu
. Then, it is direct to see that, for almost every
x\in X
,
(\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle)\mapsto f_{\psi,\phi}(x)
is linear in
\lvert\phi\rangle
and antilinear in
\lvert\psi\rangle
. Then, there exists a function
F:X\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\vphantom{\psi F(x)\phi}\left\langle\smash{\psi}\middle\rvert\smash{F(x)}\middle\rvert\smash{\phi}\right\rangle=f_{\psi,\phi}(x)
almost everywhere. Next, as
\|F(x)\|_{1}=g(x)=\Tr(F(x))
almost everywhere, we may assume that
F
is bounded, trace class, and positive. Finally, we take
\rho_{n}(x)=\frac{F(x)}{\Tr(F(x))}
and
\pi_{n}(x)=\Tr(F(x))
.
∎

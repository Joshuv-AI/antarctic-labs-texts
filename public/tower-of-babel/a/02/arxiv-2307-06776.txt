Approximation algorithms for the square min-sum bin packing problem ‡ \ddagger
Rachel Vanucchi Saraiva, Rafael C. S. Schouery

Abstract
In this work, we study the square min-sum bin packing problem (SMSBPP), where a list of square items has to be packed into indexed square bins of dimensions 1 × 1 1\times 1 with no overlap between the areas of the items. The bins are indexed and the cost of packing each item is equal to the index of the bin in which it is placed in. The objective is to minimize the total cost of packing all items, which is equivalent to minimizing the average cost of items. The problem has applications in minimizing the average time of logistic operations such as cutting stock and delivery of products. We prove that classic algorithms for two-dimensional bin packing that order items in non-increasing order of size, such as Next Fit Decreasing Height or Any Fit Decreasing Height heuristics, can have an arbitrarily bad performance for SMSBPP. We, then, present a 53 22 \frac{53}{22} -approximation and a PTAS for the problem.



keywords
Approximation algorithm, Bin Packing, Square Packing, Completion Time

Introduction

The bin packing problem (BPP) is a classic NP-hard problem where a list of items with varying sizes must be packed into bins of identical capacity. It models relevant logistic problems for industries such as the storage of products and digital files, loading of cargo into vehicles, task scheduling on several machines, and many others
(
Eliiyi \BBA Eliiyi \APACyear2009
)
.

In classic BPP, the goal is to minimize the number of bins needed to pack all items, which, in real applications, leads to minimizing storage costs and waste of resources.
Epstein \BBA Levin \APACyear2007
defined the min-sum bin packing problem (MSBPP), where bins are indexed and the cost of packing an item is equal to the index of the bin in which it is placed. The objective is to minimize the total cost of packing all items, which is equivalent to minimizing the average cost of the items. For example, if each bin represents the items a single vehicle carries for one trip, and the indexes represents the order of the trips the vehicle makes, then the solution of MSBPP minimizes the average number of trips that it takes to transport an item.

The same authors have also defined a weighted version of the problem, the minimum weighted sum bin packing problem (MWSBPP), where each item has an associated weight to it, and the cost of packing an item is the index of the bin in which it is placed multiplied by the weight of the item. This represents the idea that certain items might have priority over others and should be packed first. MWSBPP is also equivalent to a batch-scheduling problem where a machine can process, in parallel, a batch of jobs of size at most
1
, and the objective is to minimize the weighted sum of completion times. This problem is noted as
1|p\text{-\emph{batch}},s_{j},p_{j}=1|\sum w_{j}C_{j}
in the three-field notation for batch scheduling introduced by
Graham \BOthers. \APACyear1979
. For more details on batch scheduling, we refer to the recent survey done by
Fowler \BBA Mönch \APACyear2022
.

Both MSBPP and MWSBPP define items and bins as one-dimensional, that is, each item is associated with a single size, and the sum of the sizes of all items packed in a bin cannot exceed the capacity of the bin. In this paper, we define the square min-sum bin packing problem (SMSBPP) as follows: the input is a list of
n
square items. We define the size of a square as the size of its height (and thus also its width) and, for
i
such that
1\leq i\leq n
, the size of the
i
-th square in the list is
s_{i}\in(0,1]
. All items in the list must be packed in indexed square bins
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}
of side
1
, and the cost of packing an item in
B_{j}
is
j
. The objective is to minimize the total cost
{\sum_{j=1}^{m}j\cdot|B_{j}|}
of packing all items, in which
|B_{j}|
is the amount of items packed in bin
B_{j}
. A feasible packing of items in a bin is one where all items are within the internal area of the bin, with no intersection between the internal area of an item with the borders of the bin or between the internal areas of any two items. Items are packed with their sides parallel to the sides of the bin. These constraints capture extra challenges not represented by the one-dimensional definition when it comes to the placement of physical items in a bin.

In the following section, we discuss previous works related to min-sum and square bin packing. In Section
3
, we present a
\frac{53}{22}
-approximation for SMSBPP, and, in Section
4
, a PTAS for the problem. Finally, in Section
5
we discuss our conclusions from the study and present directions for future work.

2
Preliminary work

To the best of our knowledge, there is no previous work on min-sum bin packing with items and bins with more than one dimension. For MWSBPP,
Epstein \BBA Levin \APACyear2007
present a PTAS and a Dual-PTAS. As for MSBPP,
Epstein \BOthers. \APACyear2018
present a Next Fit Increasing heuristic with an absolute approximation ratio of at most
2
and an asymptotic approximation ratio of at most
1.6188
, along with a heuristic with an asymptotic approximation ratio of at most
1.5604
. The authors also presents a PTAS for MSBPP similar to the one for MWSBPP.

Much work exists in relation to classic BPP with multidimensional items and bins. We refer to the survey done by
Christensen \BOthers. \APACyear2017
for further details on approximation algorithms for the problem. For the packing of two-dimensional items, we will focus on level-oriented heuristics which pack items into levels within the bin. A level is a strip with width equal to the width of the bin, and height equal to the first item packed in the level. Usually, the items are also ordered by non-increasing order of height.

Among these heuristics, there is Next Fit Decreasing Height (NFDH), where items are packed in the current level until an item cannot fit in, which leads to a new level being opened. The previous levels do not receive any additional items. Similarly, a new bin is only opened once it cannot fit a new level, and once it is opened no levels are created in the previous bins as well.

Other level-oriented heuristics are those from the family Any Fit Decreasing Height (AFDH), where a new level is only opened if the current item cannot fit in any of the previous levels, and, similarly, a new bin is only opened when a level will not fit in any of the previous bins.

For NFDH,
Coffman \BOthers. \APACyear1980
show that, if
\delta
is an upper bound on the areas of the items packed by the algorithm, then the unoccupied area left in every bin except possibly the last one is at most
2\delta
.
Van Stee \APACyear2004
has also proved that, if the items packed by NFDH are all squares of size at most
1/3
, then the area occupied in every bin, except possibly the last one, is at least
9/16
. We use NFDH and these results in our proposed algorithms, however we also prove in the following lemma that NFDH and AFDH heuristics alone do not have a constant approximation ratio for SMSBPP, as ordering items by non-increasing order of size is clearly unhelpful for this problem. The solution given by NFDH can, however, have its value greatly reduced by reordering the bins in non-increasing order of the amount of items after packing.

Lemma 1
.

NFDH and AFDH heuristics do not have a constant approximation ratio for SMSBPP.

Proof.

Let
t\geq 3
be an integer. We create an instance
I
of SMSBPP with
t^{2}
items of size
1/t
, which we refer to as small items from here on, and
t
items of size
{1-1/t}
, which we refer to as large items. Any AFDH heuristic will first pack one large item per bin, since two large items cannot fit together in a same bin. Each bin can then still fit
t+1
small items, one in the same level as the large item, and
t
in a new level above it. Therefore,
t
bins are used to pack all the items,
t-1
of them containing
t+2
items, and the last one containing
2
items. Figure
1
shows an example of this packing. The value of the solution is, therefore,

\mathrm{AFDH}(I)=\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}j\cdot(t+2)+2t=\frac{t^{3}+t^{2}+2t}{2}.

Now consider another solution, also depicted in Figure
1
, where all small items are packed in the first bin, and, then,
t
bins are opened to pack one large item each. The value of this solution is

t^{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{t+1}j=t^{2}+\frac{(t+3)t}{2}=\frac{3t^{2}+3t}{2}\geq\mathrm{OPT}(I),

in which
\mathrm{OPT}(I)
is the value of an optimal solution for
I
.

Figure 1:
Packing of
I
with
t=3
done by an AFDH heuristic (above) compared to another possible packing (below).

Therefore, the approximation ratio of any AFDH heuristic is at least

\frac{\mathrm{AFDH}(I)}{\mathrm{OPT}(I)}\geq\frac{t^{3}+t^{2}+2t}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{3t^{2}+3t}\geq\frac{t}{3}.

The NFDH heuristic also first packs one large item per bin. Then, only the last of these bins packs
t+1
small items, and the remaining
t^{2}-t-1
small items are packed in a new bin, as shown in Figure
2
. Thus, the value of the solution is

\displaystyle\mathrm{NFDH}(I)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}j+t(t+2)+(t+1)(t^{2}-t-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{2t^{3}+3t^{2}-t-2}{2}.

Figure 2:
Packing of
I
with
t=3
done by NFDH.

Since
2t^{2}>t+2
for every
t\geq 2
, the approximation ratio of NFDH is at least

\frac{\mathrm{NFDH}(I)}{\mathrm{OPT}(I)}\geq\frac{2t^{3}+3t^{2}-t-2}{3t^{2}+3t}>\frac{2t^{3}+t^{2}}{3t^{2}+3t}>\frac{t}{3}.

For this particular instance, if the bins of the NFDH solution are ordered in non-increasing order of the amount of items, then its value becomes

\displaystyle t^{2}-t-1+2(t+1)+\sum_{j=3}^{t+1}j=\frac{3t^{2}}{2}+\frac{5t}{2}-1,

which is much closer to our bound on
\mathrm{OPT}(I)
.
∎

Bansal \BOthers. \APACyear2006
have proved that there is no APTAS for the general case of BPP with two-dimensional items. They have, however, also proved the existence of an APTAS for the packing of
d
-dimensional cubes into unit
d
-dimensional bins.

On the topic of packing square items,
Moon \BBA Moser \APACyear1967
have shown that NFDH and AFDH heuristics can fit a set of items of total area at most
1/2
into a single bin.
Ferreira \BOthers. \APACyear1999
have presented the First Fit Decreasing Size (FFDS) algorithm for optimal packing of items of size greater than
1/3
. The way the algorithm works is described as follows. It orders the items of size greater than
1/2
in non-decreasing order of size and packs them one per bin, in the bottom left corner of the bin. Then, it orders the remaining items in non-increasing order of size and checks if the first item in the list fits in any of the bins already opened. If it fits, then the three first items of the list are packed into the first bin they fit, one in each unoccupied corner of the bin. If the item doesn’t fit any existing bin, then a new bin is opened and the four first items of the list are packed in it. The process then repeats until all items have been packed. Figure
3
shows an example of a packing by FFDS.

Figure 3:
Example of packing done by FFDS.

Note that
4
is the maximum amount of items of size greater than
1/3
that can be packed in a single bin, and FFDS packs as many bins with this amount of items as it is possible. Therefore, if we reorder the bins of the FFDS packing in non-increasing order of the amount of items, the solution is also optimal for an instance of SMSBPP with these items. Combining NFDH and FFDS,
Van Stee \APACyear2004
presents a
2
-approximation for BPP with square items. We use a similar approach in our
\frac{53}{22}
-approximation for SMSBPP in the following section.

3
A
\frac{53}{22}
-approximation

We now present a
\frac{53}{22}
-approximation for SMSBPP. We assume
{n\geq 266730}
, as otherwise the size of the instance is limited by a constant, and thus the possible partitions of items can be enumerated in constant time. It is also possible to test in constant time if a subset with a constant amount of items can be feasibly packed into a bin, and, therefore, an optimal solution for the instance can be found by exhaustive enumeration in constant time. Despite that, we will also show that, for instances where
{n<266730}
, the algorithm proposed below returns a packing of value less than
\frac{53}{22}\mathrm{OPT}(I)+27769
.

The algorithm is described as follows. First, we classify items according to their size. Items of size at most
1/3
are small items, items of size greater than
1/2
are large items, and the remaining are medium items. We also define
\mathrm{AREA}(C)
as the sum of the areas of items in a set
C
. We order the items in non-decreasing order of their sizes and then divide them into groups
G_{1},\dots,G_{q}
such that each group
G_{i}
receives items until
\mathrm{AREA}(G_{i})>1
, or until there are no more items in the instance (for the final group).

Let
r
be the amount of groups containing only small items, and let
G^{S}_{r+1}
be the set of small items in
G_{r+1}
. We pack the items of each group
G_{1},\dots,G_{r}
and
G^{S}_{r+1}
in order with NFDH. Afterward, we pack the large and medium items with FFDS, and sort the solution found by FFDS in a non-increasing order of the amount of items. Finally, we place the bins of the packing found with FFDS for medium and large items after the packing found by NFDH for small items. As both NFDH and FFDS can be implemented with time complexity
O(n\log n)

(
Ferreira \BOthers. \APACyear1999
)
, the complexity of our algorithm is also
O(n\log n)
.

3.1
Bounds

Let
A(I)
be the value of the solution given by our algorithm for an instance
I
. For analysis of the approximation ratio, we now find lower bounds
\mathrm{LB}_{1}
and
\mathrm{LB}_{2}
on
\mathrm{OPT}(I)
such that
{A(I)-\alpha\mathrm{LB}_{1}-(\frac{53}{22}-\alpha)\mathrm{LB}_{2}\leq 0}
for some
{0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{53}{22}}
, as that implies
A(I)\leq\frac{53}{22}\mathrm{OPT}(I)
and thus the algorithm is a
\frac{53}{22}
-approximation.

The first bound
\mathrm{LB}_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{q}i|G_{i}|
is obtained from the definition of the groups
G_{i}
. As
Epstein \BOthers. \APACyear2018
have shown, if
\mathrm{AREA}(G_{i})>1
, no feasible solution can pack more than
{\sum_{i=1}^{t}}|G_{i}|
items in the first
t
bins, and so the following lemma applies.

Lemma 2

(Epstein et al., 2018)
.

\mathrm{OPT}(I)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{q}i|G_{i}|
.

As mentioned before, every bin with only small items packed by NFDH except possibly the last one has occupied area of at least
9/16

(
Van Stee \APACyear2004
)
. Since the area of any small item is at most
1/9
and
\mathrm{AREA}(G_{i})
is greater than
1
by a single item,
\mathrm{AREA}(G_{i})\leq 10/9<9/8
for
i\leq r
, and
{\mathrm{AREA}(G^{S}_{r+1})\leq 1}
. Therefore, for
i\leq r+1
, the small items of the first
i
groups are packed by NFDH in at most
2i
bins and the cost of a small item of
G_{i}
in this packing is at most
2i
. Thus, the total cost of packing all small items by our algorithm is at most

\sum_{i=1}^{r}2i|G_{i}|+(2r+2)|G^{S}_{r+1}|.

By Lemma
2
, this means the algorithm is a
2
-approximation for instances with only small items.

The lower bound given by Lemma
2
is, however, less helpful for the analysis of instances with large items, as a group
G_{i}
can contain up to four large items, and yet only a single large item can be packed in each bin, thus their cost might be close to
4i
in our packing. Because of that, we define another lower bound on
\mathrm{OPT}(I)
based on the fact that the packing of FFDS is optimal for the subset of large and medium items of the instance.

Let
\mathrm{FFDS}(d)
be the cost of packing large and medium items with FFDS and bin reordering after a bin of index
d
(if
d=0
, then the bins of FFDS are the first bins in the solution). We make the following observation that, for any solution, the cost of packing two disjunct subsets
A
and
B
of items of the instance is at least the cost of an optimal solution for
A
plus the cost of an optimal solution for
B
.

Observation 1
.

\mathrm{OPT}(A\cup B)\geq\mathrm{OPT}(A)+\mathrm{OPT}(B)
.

By Lemma
2
,
\sum_{i=1}^{r}i|G_{i}|+(r+1)|G^{S}_{r+1}|
is a lower bound of the cost of an optimal solution for the small items. The packing done by FFDS is optimal for the large and medium items. Therefore, by Observation
1
, another lower bound,
\mathrm{LB}_{2}
, on the value of an optimal solution for the whole instance is

\mathrm{LB}_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}i|G_{i}|+(r+1)|G^{S}_{r+1}|+\mathrm{FFDS}(0).

We now develop the bound
\mathrm{LB}_{1}
a little further based on the FFDS packing. For ease of notation, let
R=\sum_{i=1}^{r}i|G_{i}|+(r+1)|G^{S}_{r+1}|
. Let
k
be the amount of medium items in the instance plus the amount of large items packed in the same bin as some medium item by FFDS, and let
b
be the amount of large items packed alone in a bin by FFDS. Since
\mathrm{AREA}(G_{i})
is greater than
1
by a single item, any group
G_{i}
contains at most
9
medium items and at most
4
large items. Therefore, the amount of groups containing medium items plus the large items packed with a medium item by FFDS is at least
\lfloor k/9\rfloor
, and the amount of groups containing the large items packed alone by FFDS is at least
\lfloor b/4\rfloor
. Thus, since
\lfloor k/9\rfloor\geq k/9-1
and
\lfloor b/4\rfloor\geq b/4-1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{LB}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{q}i|G_{i}|

\displaystyle\geq R+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor k/9\rfloor}9(r+i)+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor b/4\rfloor}4(r+\lfloor k/9\rfloor+i)

\displaystyle\geq R+9r\left(\frac{k}{9}-1\right)+\frac{9\left(\frac{k}{9}-1\right)\frac{k}{9}}{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4\left(r+\frac{k}{9}-1\right)\left(\frac{b}{4}-1\right)+\frac{4\left(\frac{b}{4}-1\right)\frac{b}{4}}{2}

\displaystyle=R+rk-13r+\frac{k^{2}}{18}-\frac{17k}{18}+rb+\frac{kb}{9}-\frac{3b}{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4+\frac{b^{2}}{8}.

3.2
Analysis of the algorithm

Having defined our lower bounds on
\mathrm{OPT}(I)
, we now bound the value of the solution given by our algorithm. As mentioned before, the cost of packing the small items with the algorithm is
\sum_{i=1}^{r}2i|G_{i}|+(2r+2)|G^{S}_{r+1}|=2R

2r+2

A(I)\leq 2R+\mathrm{FFDS}(2r+2).

The amount of items packed by FFDS is
k+b
, and placing the solution after

Observation 2

\mathrm{FFDS}(d)=(k+b)d+\mathrm{FFDS}(0)

With this observation, we can begin comparing the value of the solution of our algorithm with the bounds on

4
PTAS

We now present a PTAS for SMSBPP. The idea of the algorithm is similar to the PTAS for MSBPP presented by
Epstein \BOthers. \APACyear2018
.

Let
\varepsilon
be such that
1/\varepsilon
is an integer and
{\varepsilon\leq 1/4}
. We assume
n\geq 1/\varepsilon^{3}
. Our algorithm classifies items as small, medium or large such that at least
1/\varepsilon^{6}
small items can be packed in the area occupied by a single large item. We also define medium items such that they are few compared to the total amount of items in the instance, and that their total area is also small compared to the total area of all items.

We round up the sizes of the large items and find an optimal packing for these items in polynomial time, find an unfeasible solution for the small items with value no greater than the value of an optimal solution for these items, then combine the two packings into a feasible packing without increasing the value of the solution by much. Then, we pack the medium items at the end, which will also not impact the value of the solution much, given how they are chosen.

We now define the medium items. Let
M_{i}=\{j:s_{j}\in[\varepsilon^{(3/\varepsilon)(i+1)},\varepsilon^{(3/\varepsilon)i})\}
for
i=1,\dots,1/\varepsilon^{3}
. This defines
1/\varepsilon^{3}
disjunct subsets of the items in an instance and, therefore, there is at least one value of
i
for which
|M_{i}|\leq\varepsilon^{3}n
. For one such value of
i
, let
M_{i\ell}=\{j\in|M_{i}|:s_{j}\in[\varepsilon^{(3/\varepsilon)i+3(\ell+1)},\varepsilon^{(3/\varepsilon)i+3\ell})\}
for
\ell=0,\dots,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-1
. Again, there is at least one value of
\ell
for which
\mathrm{AREA}(M_{i\ell})\leq\varepsilon\mathrm{AREA}(I)
. Let
M=M_{i\ell}
, and, for ease of notation, let
p=(3/\varepsilon)i+3\ell
. We define the items in
M
as medium items, the items larger than the medium items, that is, of size at least
\varepsilon^{p}
, as large items (denoted by
L
), and the items smaller than the medium items, that is, of size less than
\varepsilon^{p+3}
, as small items (denoted by

).

We define a Next Fit Increasing Height (NFIH) heuristic to pack the small items. The PTAS algorithm

With the lemmas in the previous section, we can now present the full algorithm for our PTAS.

\displaystyle\leq(1+30\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}(I).

. By Lemma

, the final packing has value of at most

\displaystyle(1+7\varepsilon)(1+30\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}(I)

\displaystyle=(1+37\varepsilon+210\varepsilon^{2})\mathrm{OPT}(I)

Therefore, for some
\alpha\leq\varepsilon/90

5
Conclusion

In this work we investigated the square min-sum bin packing problem, presenting an approximation algorithm of simple implementation, constant approximation ratio, and
O(n\log n)
time complexity. We have also presented a PTAS for the problem. There is no proof that the approximation ratio of
\frac{53}{22}
for the first algorithm is tight, and a better analysis might be possible for future work. One possible direction for that is finding better lower bounds for the value of an optimal solution.

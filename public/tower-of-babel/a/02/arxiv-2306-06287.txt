Abstract
. We design and compute a class of optimal control problems for reaction-diffusion systems. They form mean field control problems related to multi-density reaction-diffusion systems. To solve proposed optimal control problems numerically, we first apply high-order finite element methods to discretize the space-time domain and then solve the optimal control problem using augmented Lagrangian methods (ALG2). Numerical examples, including generalized optimal transport and mean field control problems between Gaussian distributions and image densities, demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed modeling and computational methods for mean field control problems involving reaction-diffusion equations/systems.

1.
Introduction

Reaction-diffusion systems are essential classes of modeling dynamics
[
37
]
, which have applications in tumor growth modeling
[
9
]
, propagation of pandemic spread
[
24
,
25
]
, evolutionary games
[
19
]
, etc. The reaction term reflects general nonlinear interacting behaviors of agents/particles in complex systems. Patterns of population behaviors often arise in the solution of reaction-diffusion equations, which model the collective behaviors of particles/agents.

In recent years, mean field control problems
[
20
,
22
]
have been studied, which are optimal control problems for mean-field limits of infinitely many identical particles/agents. The problem models the complex behaviors of identical particles/agents interacting with each other. It is worth mentioning that a special example of a mean-field control problem forms the dynamical optimal transport problem
[
3
]
. It studies a particular optimal control problem in density space, which transfers an initial density toward the terminal density function. The optimal transport problem also introduces a functional distance, namely the Wasserstein distance. It helps study and compute a class of initial value evolutionary dynamics, namely, Wasserstein gradient flows
[
21
]
. They are also valuable for modeling interaction behaviors of particle dynamics.

Nowadays, optimal transport and mean field control problems have vast applications in modeling and computations. Classical studies of optimal transport and mean field control problems are often limited to a single-density function. The complex interaction between densities is a vital modeling factor that has not been systemically studied.

In this paper, we generalize a class of mean field control of reaction-diffusion equations/systems proposed in
[
27
]
. It forms an optimal control problem among multiple density functions interacting with each other with a nonlinear reaction vector function. After change of variables, we reformulate the optimal control problem into an optimization problem with a Fisher information type potential functional. We apply the high-order finite element method to discretize the spatial-time domain and use the augmented Lagrangian method, ALG2 from
[
15
]
, to compute the discretized optimization problems. Numerical examples, including mean field control problems between Gaussian densities and a system with
12
images, demonstrate the solution of the proposed generalized mean field control problems.

Generalized optimal transport and mean field control problems have been widely investigated in
[
6
,
7
,
14
,
35
]
.
For example, multi-population mean field games were discussed in
[

]
, and generalized optimal transport distances between vector densities were formulated in
[

]
. Meanwhile, unnormalized and unbalanced optimal transport were proposed in
[

,

,

]
, which allows to control densities with different total masses. In applications, one also applies mean field control problems to model the propagation of pandemics
[
24
,
25
]
.
They are all important examples of optimal control problems for reaction-diffusion systems. Modeling and computational mean-field control problems for general convection-reaction-diffusion systems are new research directions
[
28
,
29
]
. They have potential applications in classical modeling dynamics based on reaction-diffusion equations and systems. This paper proposes a class of mean field control of reaction-diffusion systems. Meanwhile, the optimal control problem of gradient flows in Wasserstein space is widely studied, namely Schrödinger bridge problems
[
10
,
13
,
26
,
31
,
36
]
. In addition, the generalized gradient flows in multiple-density spaces have been studied
[

,

Thus, we study the mean field control using formalisms of controlling gradient flows. Generalized optimal transport and mean field control of reaction-diffusion systems

2.1.
Background: Reaction-diffusion induced metric distances

Before delving into the mean field control problems that will be discussed in this manuscript, we first review the definitions of two metric distances: one for scalars and one for systems, which are obtained from generalized optimal transport type gradient flow problems. The material in this subsection follows closely our previous work on variational time implicit schemes for reaction-diffusion systems in
[
17
]
.
We only give the definition of these metric distances without further elaboration, but refer to the references
[
1
,
26
,
27
,
34
]
for more details on
optimal transport type gradient flows, distances, mean-field control and related problems.

2.1.1.
The metric distance: scalar case

The scalar metric distance is derived from the following reaction-diffusion equation
[
27
,
17
]
:

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho=\nabla\cdot(V_{1}(\rho)\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho))-V_{2}(\rho)\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho),\text{ on }[0,T]\times\Omega,\end{split}

(2.1)

with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

V_{1}(\rho)\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho)\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, where
\bm{\nu}
is the unit outward normal direction on the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Here
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the spatial domain,
\rho\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a scalar non-negative density function satisfying

\rho(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{M}=\{\rho\in H^{1}(\Omega):\rho\geq 0\},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(2.2)

\mathcal{E}\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an energy functional,
V_{1},V_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
are two positive mobility functions, and
\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}
is the first variation operator in
L^{2}
space.

A crucial property of the equation (
2.1
) is that it
satisfies an energy-dissipation law:

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(\rho)=-\int_{\Omega}\Big[\|\nabla\tfrac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho)\|^{2}V_{1}(\rho)+|\tfrac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho)|^{2}V_{2}(\rho)\Big]dx\leq 0,

(2.3)

where we use the fact that functions
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
are non-negative.
In the literature, the above right hand side (dissipation rate)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\rho)=\int_{\Omega}\Big[\|\nabla\tfrac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho)\|^{2}V_{1}(\rho)+|\tfrac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho)|^{2}V_{2}(\rho)\Big]dx,

(2.4)

is called the generalized
Fisher information functional
.
This Fisher information functional induces a metric in the space
\mathcal{M}
, which further defines a distance between two densities
\rho^{0},\rho^{1}\in\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.1

(Scalar distance functional)
.

Define a distance functional

\mathrm{Dist}_{V_{1},V_{2}}\colon\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

as below. Consider the following optimal control problem:

\mathrm{Dist}_{V_{1},V_{2}}(\rho^{0},\rho^{1})^{2}:=\inf_{\rho,\bm{v}_{1},v_{2}}\quad\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\|\bm{v}_{1}\|^{2}V_{1}(\rho)+|v_{2}|^{2}V_{2}(\rho)\Big]dxdt,

(2.5a)

where the infimum is taken among
\rho(t,x)\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\bm{v}_{1}(t,x)\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

v_{2}(t,x)\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
such that
\rho
satisfies a reaction-diffusion type equation with drift vector field
\bm{v}_{1}
, drift mobility
V_{1}
, reaction rate
v_{2}
, reaction mobility
V_{2}
, connecting initial and terminal densities
\rho^{0}
,
\rho^{1}\in\mathcal{M}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(V_{1}(\rho)\bm{v}_{1})=V_{2}(\rho)v_{2},\quad\text{on }[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
&\rho(0,x)=\rho^{0}(x),\quad\rho(T,x)=\rho^{1}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5b)

with no-flux boundary condition
V_{1}(\rho)\bm{v}_{1}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

Introducing the flux function
\bm{m}(t,x)\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and source function
s(t,x)\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\bm{m}(t,x)=V_{1}(\rho(t,x))\bm{v}_{1}(t,x),\quad s(t,x)=V_{2}(\rho(t,x))v_{2}(t,x),

the distance in Definition
2.1
is rewritten as
the following optimization problem with a linear constraint:

\mathrm{Dist}_{V_{1},V_{2}}(\rho^{0},\rho^{1})^{2}:=\inf_{\rho,\bm{m},s}\quad\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{V_{2}(\rho)}\Big]dxdt,

(2.6a)

such that

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}(t,x)=s(t,x),\quad\text{in }[0,T]\times\Omega,\\
&\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\nu}=0,\quad\text{ on }[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
&\rho(0,x)=\rho^{0}(x),\;\rho(T,x)=\rho^{1}(x),\quad\text{in }\Omega.\end{split}

(2.6b)

The optimization functional in (
2.6a
)
is convex under the condition that both

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are positive concave functions.

2.1.2.
The metric distance: system case

The system metric distance is derived from the following reaction-diffusion system
with

M
species and
R
reactions
[
17
,
34
]
:

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho_{i}=\;\nabla\cdot\left(V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})\right)\;-\sum_{p=1}^{R}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\gamma_{i,p}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}_{j}(\rho_{j}),\end{split}

(2.7)

with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

for
1\leq i\leq M
.
Here
\rho_{i}
is the density such that

\rho_{i}(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{M}
,

\mathcal{E}_{i}\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the energy functional
and
V_{1,i}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the positive mobility function
for
i
-th species, and
V_{2,p}\colon\mathbb{R}^{M}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the positive mobility function for
p
-th reaction.
The bolded density
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{M})
is simply the collection of
M
densities.
Moreover, the coefficient matrix
\Gamma=(\gamma_{i,p})\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times R}
satisfies

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\gamma_{i,p}=0
for all
1\leq p\leq R
, which is related to the total mass conservation:

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\rho_{i}\,dx=0.

The equation (
2.7
)
satisfies the energy-dissipation law:

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})=-\bm{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{\rho})\leq 0,

(2.8)

where
\bm{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{\rho})
denotes the generalized Fisher information functional

\bm{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{\rho}):=\int_{\Omega}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}_{i}}{\delta\rho}\|^{2}V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}_{j}}{\delta\rho}\right|^{2}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\Big]dx.

(2.9)

In the above, we use the fact that functions
V_{1,i}
,
V_{2,p}
,
1\leq i\leq M
,
1\leq p\leq R
, are non-negative. The functional
\bm{\mathcal{I}}
, again, induces a metric function in space
\mathcal{M}^{M}
, which defines distances between two system densities
\bm{\rho}^{0},\bm{\rho}^{1}\in\mathcal{M}^{M}
.

Definition 2.2

(System distance functional)
.

Define a distance functional

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{Dist}}_{\bm{V}_{1},\bm{V}_{2}}\colon\mathcal{M}^{M}\times\mathcal{M}^{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

as below.

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{Dist}}_{V_{1},V_{2}}(\bm{\rho}^{0},\bm{\rho}^{1})^{2}:=

\displaystyle\inf_{\bm{\rho},\underline{\bm{m}},\bm{s}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{|\bm{m}_{i}|^{2}}{V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|s_{p}|^{2}}{V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}\right)dx{dt}:\;\;

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{tabular}[]{l}$\partial_{t}\rho_{i}+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}_{i}=\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}s_{p},\forall 1\leq i\leq M$,\\
$\bm{m}_{i}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0$,
$\bm{\rho}(0,\cdot)=\bm{\rho}^{0},\;\;\bm{\rho}(T,\cdot)=\bm{\rho}^{1}.$\end{tabular}\Big\},

where
\underline{\bm{m}}=(\bm{m}_{1},\cdots,\bm{m}_{M})
is the collection of fluxes, and

\bm{s}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{R})
is the collection of sources.

Again, the optimization functional in (
2.10
)
is convex under the condition that

V_{1,i}
and
V_{2,p}
are positive concave functions for all
1\leq i\leq M
and
1\leq p\leq R
.

2.2.
MFC for scalar reaction-diffusion

In this subsection, we introduce the following MFC problem. This can be viewed as a generalization of the scalar distance (
2.6
).

Definition 2.3

(Scalar MFC problem)
.

Suppose that there exist two positive mobility functions
V_{1},V_{2}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
a terminal time
T>0
, a non-negative regularization parameter
\beta\geq 0
,
an energy functional
\mathcal{E}\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
a potential functional
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
and a terminal functional

\mathcal{G}\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Consider

\begin{split}&\inf_{\rho,\tilde{\bm{m}},\tilde{s}}\quad\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\|\tilde{\bm{m}}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|\tilde{s}|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}\right)dx-\mathcal{F}(\rho)\right]dt+\mathcal{G}(\rho(T,\cdot)),\end{split}

(2.11a)

where the infimum is among all densities

\rho
with
\rho(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{M}
for
t\in[0,T]
, flux
\tilde{\bm{m}}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and source
\tilde{s}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot\tilde{\bm{m}}-\tilde{s}=\beta\left(\nabla\cdot(V_{1}(\rho)\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho))-V_{2}(\rho)\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho)\right),

(2.11b)

with boundary condition

\displaystyle\left.\left(\tilde{\bm{m}}-\beta V_{1}(\rho)\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho)\right)\cdot\bm{\nu}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(2.11c)

and fixed initial density
\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho^{0}
in
\Omega
.

From the modeling perspective, the variational problem in Definition
2.3
models the movement of the density from the initial density under the dynamical constraint of reaction-diffusion equations. It aims to find the optimal choices of vector fields and reaction rate functions under general kinetic, potential, and terminal energies.

We will construct numerical scheme for problem (
2.3
) based on the following change of variables.

Proposition 2.1

(Scalar MFC reformulation)
.

Denote
\bm{m}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and
s\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\bm{m}=\tilde{\bm{m}}-\beta V_{1}(\rho)\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho),\quad s=\tilde{s}-\beta V_{2}(\rho)\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho),

and denote

V_{3}(\rho)=\frac{1}{V_{1}(\rho)|\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\rho^{2}}\mathcal{E}(\rho)|^{2}},

(2.12)

where
\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\rho^{2}}\mathcal{E}(\rho)
is the second variational derivative of the energy functional
\mathcal{E}(\rho)
.
Then, the scalar MFC problem (
2.3
) is equivalent to the following optimization problem:
Consider

\begin{split}&\inf_{\rho,\bm{m},s}~~\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\beta^{2}\frac{|\nabla\rho|^{2}}{2V_{3}(\rho)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}{|\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\rho}|^{2}}{V_{2}(\rho)}\Big]dxdt\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}(\rho)\,dt+\mathcal{G}(\rho(T,\cdot))+\beta[\mathcal{E}(\rho(T,\cdot))-\mathcal{E}(\rho^{0})],\end{split}

(2.13a)

where the infimum is among all densities
\rho
with
\rho(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{M}
, for
t\in[0,T]
, flux
\bm{m}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and source
s\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}(t,x)-s(t,x)=0,

(2.13b)

with no-flux boundary condition

\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

and fixed initial density
\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho^{0}
.

The proof of the above proposition follows from a similar argument in
[
8
, Proposition 2]
. We present the detailed derivation in the Appendix. The main idea is to use integration by parts to show that

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\tilde{\bm{m}}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{\|\tilde{s}\|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}\Big]dxdt\\
=&\;\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|{\bm{m}}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{\|s\|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\beta^{2}\frac{|\nabla\rho|^{2}}{2V_{3}(\rho)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}{|\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\rho}|^{2}}{V_{2}(\rho)}\Big]dxdt\\
&\;+\beta[\mathcal{E}(\rho(T,\cdot))-\mathcal{E}(\rho^{0})].\end{split}

For simplicity of discussions, we assume that there are two functions
F:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

and
G:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(t,x,\rho)dx=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{F}(\rho(t,\cdot))-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}{|\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta\rho}(\rho(t,x))|^{2}}{V_{2}(\rho(t,x))}dx,\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(x,\rho)dx=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{G}(\rho)+\beta\mathcal{E}(\rho).

Then the functional in (
2.13a
)
simplifies to the following formula:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\frac{\beta^{2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}}{2V_{3}(\rho)}-F(t,x,\rho)\Big]dxdt+\int_{\Omega}G(x,\rho(T,x))dx.

This is the form we use in our numerical scheme.

For completeness, we formulate the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) condition, i.e., the critical point system, for the optimization problem (
2.13
). The proof is presented in the Appendix; see also
[
27
,
30
]
.

Proposition 2.2

(KKT system for (
2.13
))
.

Let
(\rho,\bm{m},s)
be the critical point of the optimization problem (
2.13
). Then there exists a function
\phi\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\frac{\bm{m}(t,x)}{V_{1}(\rho(t,x))}=\nabla\phi(t,x),\quad\frac{s(t,x)}{V_{2}(\rho(t,x))}=\phi(t,x),

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)+\nabla\cdot(V_{1}(\rho(t,x))\nabla\phi(t,x))-V_{2}(\rho(t,x))\phi(t,x)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\phi(t,x)+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi(t,x)\|^{2}V_{1}^{\prime}(\rho(t,x))+\frac{1}{2}|\phi(t,x)|^{2}V^{\prime}_{2}(\rho(t,x))\\
&\hskip 179.25244pt+\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\Big[\mathcal{F}(\rho)-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\mathcal{I}(\rho)\Big]=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.14)

where

\mathcal{I}(\rho)

is the generalized Fisher information functional given in (
2.4
), with initial and terminal time boundary conditions

\rho(0,x)=\rho^{0}(x),\quad\phi(T,x)=-\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\Big(\mathcal{G}(\rho(T,\cdot))+\beta\mathcal{E}(\rho(T,\cdot))\Big).

(2.15)

Remark 2.4

(Comparison with existing models)
.

When the terminal density
\rho(T,\cdot)=\rho^{1}
is prescribed (or when
\gamma\rightarrow+\infty

for the terminal function in Table
1
), the problem (
2.13
) is called the planning problem for MFC. This problem with
\beta=0
(no
V_{3}
) was consider in the work
[
27
]
.

Taking the regularization parameter
\beta>0
in MFC (
2.13
) forms a Schrödinger bridge type problem
[
10
,
26
]
. A related planning problem with
\beta>0
and no potential
F=0
was considered recently in
[
9
]
, known as unbalanced regularized optimal mass transport (urOMT). It has applications in cancer imaging.
Therein,
V_{1}(\rho)=V_{3}(\rho)=\rho
,
V_{2}(\rho)=\alpha(t,x)\rho
for
\alpha\geq 0
,
and the objective function has an additional term

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\beta s\log(\rho)dxdt
compared with our formulation (
2.13a
) under the same linear constraint (
2.13b
).

2.3.
MFC for reaction-diffusion systems

In this subsection, we introduce a system of MFC problem.
It can be viewed as a generalization of the system distance (
2.10
).

Definition 2.5

(System MFC problem)
.

Suppose there exist
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
species and
R\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
reactions with reaction coefficient matrix
\Gamma=(\gamma_{i,p})\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times R}
. Let
V_{1,i}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

for
1\leq i\leq M
,
and

V_{2,p}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

for
1\leq p\leq R

be positive mobility functions.
Take terminal time
T>0
, and non-negative regularization parameter
\beta\geq 0
. Choose energy functionals
\mathcal{E}_{i}\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for
1\leq i\leq M
,
an potential functional
\bm{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathcal{M}^{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
and a terminal functional

\bm{\mathcal{G}}\colon\mathcal{M}^{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Consider

\begin{split}&\inf_{\bm{\rho},\tilde{\underline{\bm{m}}},\tilde{\bm{s}}}\quad\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{|\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}|^{2}}{2V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|\tilde{s}_{p}|^{2}}{2V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}\right)dx-\bm{\mathcal{F}}(\bm{\rho})\right]dt+\bm{\mathcal{G}}(\bm{\rho}(T,\cdot)),\end{split}

(2.16a)

where the infimum is among all densities

\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{M})
with
\bm{\rho}(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{M}^{M}
, fluxes

\tilde{}\underline{\bm{m}}=(\tilde{\bm{m}}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\bm{m}}_{M})
with
\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and sources
\tilde{\bm{s}}=(\tilde{s}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{s}_{M})\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{M}
, such that

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho_{i}+\nabla\cdot\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}-\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}\tilde{s}_{p}\\
=&\;\beta\left(\nabla\cdot(V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i}))-\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}_{j}(\rho_{j})\right),\end{split}

(2.16b)

with boundary condition

\displaystyle\left.\left(\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}-\beta V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})\right)\cdot\bm{\nu}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(2.16c)

for all
1\leq i\leq M
,
and fixed initial density
\bm{\rho}(0,\cdot)=\bm{\rho}^{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{M}
.

Again, our numerical scheme is based on the following equivalent formulation after the change of variables, whose proof is given in the Appendix.

Proposition 2.3

(System MFC reformulation)
.

For
1\leq i\leq M
and
1\leq p\leq R
, denote
\bm{m}_{i}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
s_{p}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\bm{m}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}-\beta V_{1_{i}}(\rho_{i})\nabla\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i}),\quad s_{p}=\tilde{s}_{p}-\beta V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}_{j}(\rho_{j}),

and denote

V_{3,i}(\rho_{i})=\frac{1}{V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})|\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\rho^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})|^{2}}.

(2.17)

Then, the system MFC problem (
2.5
) is equivalent to the following optimization problem:
Consider

\begin{split}&\inf_{\rho,\bm{m},s}~~\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\frac{|\bm{m}_{i}|^{2}}{2V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\beta^{2}\frac{|\nabla\rho_{i}|^{2}}{2V_{3,i}(\rho_{i})}\right)dxdt\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{p=1}^{R}\left(\frac{|s_{p}|^{2}}{2V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}{|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}_{j}}{\delta\rho}(\rho_{j})|^{2}}{V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}\right)dxdt\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\int_{0}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{F}}(\bm{\rho})\,dt+\bm{\mathcal{G}}(\bm{\rho}(T,\cdot))+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{M}[\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i}(T,\cdot))-\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{0})],\end{split}

(2.18a)

subject to the constraints

\partial_{t}\rho_{i}(t,x)+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}_{i}(t,x)-\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}s_{p}(t,x)=0,

(2.18b)

with no-flux boundary condition

\bm{m}_{i}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

for all
1\leq i\leq M
,

and fixed initial density
\bm{\rho}(0,\cdot)=\bm{\rho}_{0}=(\rho_{1}^{0},\cdots,\rho_{M}^{0})\in\mathcal{M}^{M}
.

Note that when
M=1
and
R=1
, the MFC problem (
2.18
) corresponds to the scalar case considered in Definition (
2.3
).

Similar to the scalar case, we assume that there are two functions
\bm{F}:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

and
\bm{G}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bm{F}(t,x,\bm{\rho})dx=

\displaystyle\;\bm{\mathcal{F}}(\bm{\rho}(t,\cdot))-\int_{\Omega}\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}{|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}_{j}}{\delta\rho}(\rho_{j})|^{2}}{V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bm{G}(x,\bm{\rho})dx=

\displaystyle\;\bm{\mathcal{G}}(\bm{\rho})+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i}).

This simplifies the functional in (
2.18a
) as follows:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\frac{|\bm{m}_{i}|^{2}}{2V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\beta^{2}\frac{|\nabla\rho_{i}|^{2}}{2V_{3,i}(\rho_{i})}\right)+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|s_{p}|^{2}}{2V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}-\bm{F}(t,x,\bm{\rho})\right]dxdt\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\bm{G}(x,\bm{\rho}(T,x))dx,\end{split}

The KKT system for the optimization problem (
2.18
) is given below. Its proof is again presented in the Appendix.

Proposition 2.4

(KKT system for (
2.18
))
.

Let
(\bm{\rho},\underline{\bm{m}},\bm{s})
be the critical point of the optimization problem (
2.18
). Then there exists a function
\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{M})\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{M}
, such that

\frac{\bm{m}_{i}(t,x)}{V_{1,i}(\rho_{i}(t,x))}=\nabla\phi_{i}(t,x),\quad\frac{s_{p}(t,x)}{V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho}(t,x))}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\phi_{j}(t,x),

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho_{i}+\nabla\cdot(V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla\phi_{i})-\sum_{p=1}^{R}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\gamma_{i,p}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\phi_{j}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\phi_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{i}\|^{2}V_{1,i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{R}|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\phi_{j}|^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho_{i}}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\\
&\hskip 179.25244pt+\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho_{i}}\Big[\bm{\mathcal{F}}(\bm{\rho})-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\bm{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{\rho})\Big]=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.19)

where
\bm{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{\rho})

is the generalized Fisher information functional given in (
2.9
),
with initial and terminal time boundary conditions

\rho_{i}(0,x)=\rho_{i}^{0}(x),\quad\phi_{i}(T,x)=-\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho_{i}}\Big(\bm{\mathcal{G}}(\bm{\rho}(T,\cdot))+\beta\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i}(T,\cdot))\Big).

(2.20)

3.
High order discretizations and optimization algorithms

In this section, we present the high-order spatial-time finite element formulations for the proposed MFC problems (
2.13
) and (
2.18
).

3.1.
Scalar case

In this subsection, we discretize the scalar model (
2.13
).
By introducing a new approximation variable
\bm{n}=\beta\nabla\rho
, the optimization problem (
2.13
) is rewritten as follows.
Consider

\inf_{\rho,\bm{m},s,\bm{n}}~\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\frac{|\bm{n}|^{2}}{2V_{3}(\rho)}-F(\rho)\Big]dxdt+\int_{\Omega}G(\rho(T,x))dx,

(3.1a)

where the infimum is taken over density
\rho\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, flux
\bm{m}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, source
s\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
vector field

\bm{n}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}-s

\displaystyle\;=0,

(3.1b)

\displaystyle\bm{n}-\beta\nabla\rho

\displaystyle\;=0,

(3.1c)

on
[0,T]\times\Omega
,
with fixed initial density
\rho(0,x)=\rho^{0}(x)
in
\Omega

and no-flux boundary condition
\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\nu}=0
on
[0,T]\times\partial\Omega
.
Here we suppress the coordinate dependence of functions
F
and
G
for simplicity of presentation.

Following the high-order finite element scheme for MFC problems in
[
16
]
, we discretize the constraint optimization problem (
3.1
) using high-order finite element methods
and apply the ALG2 optimization algorithm to solve the discrete saddle-point problem.

3.1.1.
The continuous saddle-point formulation

We introduce a scalar Lagrange multiplier
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{T})
, where
\Omega_{T}:=[0,T]\times\Omega
is the space-time domain,
for the constraint (
3.1b
),
and a vectorial Lagrange multiplier
\bm{\sigma}\in L^{2}([0,T])\otimes H(\mathrm{div}_{0};\Omega)
for the constraint (
3.1c
), where

H(\mathrm{div}_{0};\Omega):=\{\bm{\tau}\in H(\mathrm{div};\Omega):\bm{\tau}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\},

in which the H(div)-conforming space

H(\mathrm{div};\Omega):=\{\bm{\tau}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}:\nabla\cdot\bm{\tau}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}.

We reformulate the constrained optimization problem (
3.1
)
into the following saddle-point system:
Find the critical point of the system

\begin{split}\inf_{\rho,\bm{m},s,\bm{n},\rho_{T}}\sup_{\phi,\bm{\sigma}}~&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\frac{|\bm{n}|^{2}}{2V_{3}(\rho)}-F(\rho)\Big]dxdt\\
&\hskip-22.76228pt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[(\rho\,\partial_{t}\phi+\bm{m}\cdot\nabla\phi+s\phi)+(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\sigma}+\beta\rho\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma})\Big]dxdt\\
&\hskip-22.76228pt+\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho_{T}\,\phi(T,\cdot)-\rho^{0}\,\phi(0,\cdot)\right)dx+\int_{\Omega}G(\rho_{T})dx,\end{split}

(3.2)

where
the variables
\bm{m},\bm{n}\in[L^{2}(\Omega_{T})]^{d}
,
\rho,s\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
,

\rho_{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega)

with
\rho,\rho_{T}\geq 0
a.e.,
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{T})
,
and
\bm{\sigma}\in L^{2}([0,T])\otimes H(\mathrm{div}_{0};\Omega)
.
Notice that integration by parts is used to incorporate the linear constraints (
3.1b
)–(
3.1c
) to the saddle point problem (
3.2
). Moreover, since no derivative information is needed for the physical variables
\bm{m},\bm{n},\rho
and
s
, it is natural to approximate them using
L^{2}
-conforming spaces.

To simplify the notation, we collect the variables

\displaystyle\bm{u}:=\;(\rho,\bm{m},s,\bm{n}),\text{ and }\Phi:=\;(\phi,\bm{\sigma}),

(3.3a)

and define the differential operator

\mathcal{D}

by

\mathcal{D}(\Phi):=(\partial_{t}\phi+\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma},\nabla\phi,\phi,\bm{\sigma})\in[L^{2}(\Omega_{T})]^{2d+2}.

(3.3b)

With this notation, the above saddle-point problem simplifies to the following:
Find the critical point of

\begin{split}\inf_{\bm{u},\rho_{T}}\sup_{\Phi}~&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[H(\bm{u})-\bm{u}\cdot\mathcal{D}(\Phi)\Big]\,dxdt\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\int_{\Omega}\Big[G(\rho_{T})+\rho_{T}\,\phi(T,\cdot)-\rho^{0}\,\phi(0,\cdot)\Big]dx,\end{split}

(3.4)

where
the variables
\bm{u}\in[L^{2}(\Omega_{T})]^{2d+2}
,
\rho_{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega)

with
\rho,\rho_{T}\geq 0
a.e., and

\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{T})\times\Big(L^{2}([0,T])\otimes H(\mathrm{div}_{0};\Omega)\Big)
,
the nonlinear functional
H(\bm{u})
is given by

H(\bm{u}):=\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\frac{|\bm{n}|^{2}}{2V_{3}(\rho)}-F(\rho),

and the inner-product is defined by

\bm{u}\cdot\mathcal{D}(\Phi):=\rho(\partial_{t}\phi+\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma})+\bm{m}\cdot\nabla\phi+s\,\phi+\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\sigma}.

3.1.2.
The high-order finite element scheme and numerical integration

Following the work
[
16
]
, we approximate the physical variables
\bm{m},\bm{n},s
,
\rho
using (discontinuous) high-order integration rule spaces,
the variable
\phi
using a high-order
H^{1}
-conforming finite element space, and
the variable
\bm{\sigma}
using a vectorial
H^{1}
-conforming finite element space.
Let
\Omega_{h}=\{T_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N_{S}}
be a conforming mesh of the spatial domain
\Omega
with
N_{S}
elements, and

I_{h}=\{I_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{T}}
be a uniform discretization of the temporal domain
[0,T]

with
N_{T}
line segments. We denote the space-time mesh
\Omega_{T,h}:=I_{h}\otimes\Omega_{h}
.
For simplicity, we restrict ourselves to the case where the domain
\Omega=[0,1]^{d}
is a unit hypercube and its spatial mesh
\Omega_{h}
is a Cartesian mesh with uniform hypercubic elements.
Given a polynomial degree
k\geq 1
,
we denote the following
H^{1}

and
L^{2}
finite element spaces:

\displaystyle V_{h}^{k}:=

\displaystyle\;\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{T}):\;\;v|_{I_{j}\times T_{\ell}}\in Q^{k}(I_{j})\otimes Q^{k}(T_{\ell})\;\;\forall j,\ell\},

(3.5)

\displaystyle W_{h}^{k-1}:=

\displaystyle\;\{w\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T}):\;\;w|_{I_{j}\times T_{\ell}}\in Q^{k-1}(I_{j})\otimes Q^{k-1}(T_{\ell})\;\;\forall j,\ell\},

(3.6)

\displaystyle M_{h}^{k-1}:=

\displaystyle\;\{\mu\in L^{2}(\Omega):\;\;\;\;\;\mu|_{T_{\ell}}\in Q^{k-1}(T_{\ell})\;\;\forall\ell\},

(3.7)

where
Q^{k}(S)
is the tensor-product polynomial space of degree no greater than
k
in each coordinate direction.

Using the above finite element spaces, we obtain the following discrete saddle point problem:
Find the critical point of the discrete system

\begin{split}\inf_{\bm{u}_{h},\,\rho_{T,h}}\sup_{\Phi_{h}}~&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[H(\bm{u}_{h})-\bm{u}_{h}\cdot\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h})\Big]\,dxdt\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\int_{\Omega}\Big[G(\rho_{T,h})+\rho_{T,h}\,\phi_{h}(T,\cdot)-\rho^{0}\,\phi_{h}(0,\cdot)\Big]dx,\end{split}

(3.8)

where the variables

\bm{u}_{h}:=(\rho_{h},\bm{m}_{h},s_{h},\bm{n}_{h})

with
\bm{m}_{h},\bm{n}_{h}\in[W_{h}^{k-1}]^{d}
,
\rho,s\in W_{h}^{k-1}
,

\rho\geq 0
a.e.,
\rho_{T,h}\in M_{h}^{k-1}

with
\rho_{T,h}\geq 0
a.e.,
and
\Phi_{h}:=(\phi_{h},\bm{\sigma}_{h})

with
\phi_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}

and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{d}

with
\bm{\sigma}_{h}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

Remark 3.1

(On the choice of the finite element spaces)
.

In the scheme (
3.8
), we use
L^{2}
-conforming spaces to approximate the
physical variables
\bm{u}_{h}
and
\rho_{T,h}
, which is natural as no derivative information is needed. Moreover, we use
H^{1}(\Omega_{T})
-conforming finite element spaces to approximate the Lagrange multipliers
\Phi_{h}
. The use of
H^{1}
-conforming space to approximate the scalar variable
\phi_{h}
is natural as the formulation (
3.8
) requires space-time derivative information for
\phi_{h}
.
On the other hand, the use of
H^{1}(\Omega_{T})
-conforming space for the vectorial Lagrange multiplier
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
is only for the simplicity of implementation.
A more natural finite element space for this variable is the following:

[W_{h}^{k}]^{d}\cap\left(L^{2}([0,T])\otimes H(\mathrm{div}_{0};\Omega)\right),

which is discontinuous in time and
H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming in space.
We will implement this choice in future work.

Here we use polynomial degree of one order lower to approximate the physical variables
\bm{u}_{h}
than that for the Lagrange multipliers
\Phi_{h}
.
Our lowest order scheme is a staggered grid scheme where the physical variables stay in cell centers and the Lagrange multipliers stay on mesh vertices.

In practice, the integrals in (
3.8
) are discretized using
Gauss-Legendre quadrature rules with
k
integration points per direction.
The total number of integration points for the space-time integral on
[0,T]\times\Omega
is
(kN_{T})\times(k^{d}N_{S})
, and that for the spatial integral on
\Omega

is
k^{d}N_{S}
, where
N_{T}
is the number of temporal cells and
N_{T}
is the number of spatial cells.
Denoting these spatial/temporal Gauss-Legendre quadrature points as

\{\bm{\xi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}},\text{ and }\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}

(3.9a)

with their associated quadrature weights as

\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}},\text{ and }\{\zeta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{kN_{T}},

(3.9b)

we write the discrete integrals as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle f(t,x)\rangle\!\rangle_{h}:=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}f(\eta_{j},\bm{\xi}_{i})\omega_{i}\zeta_{j},

(3.9c)

\displaystyle\langle f(x)\rangle_{h}:=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}f(\bm{\xi}_{i})\omega_{i}.

(3.9d)

Furthermore, we use the Gauss-Legendre basis for the
L^{2}
-conforming spaces
W_{h}^{k-1}

and
M_{h}^{k-1}
in the implementation, i.e., a function
w_{h}\in W_{h}^{k-1}

is expressed as

w_{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\mathsf{w}_{ij}\,\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),

with coefficient
\mathsf{w}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Here the tensor-product Gauss-Legendre basis function

\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x)\in W_{h}^{k-1}
satisfies the nodal interpolation property

\psi_{j}(\zeta_{j^{\prime}})=\delta_{jj^{\prime}},\text{ and }\varphi_{i}(\bm{\xi}_{i^{\prime}})=\delta_{ii^{\prime}},

where
\delta_{ii^{\prime}}=1
if
i=i^{\prime}
and

\delta_{ii^{\prime}}=0
if
i\not=i^{\prime}
.
A function
v_{h}\in M_{h}^{k-1}
is expressed as

v_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\mathsf{v}_{i}\varphi_{i}(x)

with coefficient
\mathsf{v}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Here
\mathrm{w}_{ij}=w_{h}(\zeta_{j},\bm{\xi}_{i})
is the value of
w_{h}

on the space-time integration point
(\zeta_{j},\bm{\xi}_{i})
, and

\mathrm{v}_{i}=v_{h}(\bm{\xi}_{i})
is the value of
v_{h}

on the spatial integration point
\bm{\xi}_{i}
.
Hence there holds

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle f(w_{h})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}:=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}f(\mathsf{w}_{ij})\omega_{i}\zeta_{j},

\displaystyle\quad\forall w_{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\mathsf{w}_{ij}\,\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x)\in W_{h}^{k-1},

\displaystyle\langle f(v_{h})\rangle_{h}:=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}f(\mathsf{v}_{i})\omega_{i},

\displaystyle\quad\forall v_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\mathsf{v}_{i}\varphi_{i}(x)\in M_{h}^{k-1}.

With these notation, the fully discrete scheme (
3.8
)
with numerical integration is given as follows:
Find the critical point of the fully discrete system

\begin{split}\inf_{\bm{u}_{h},\,\rho_{T,h}}\sup_{\Phi_{h}}~&\langle\!\langle H(\bm{u}_{h})-\bm{u}_{h}\cdot\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\langle G(\rho_{T,h})+\rho_{T,h}\,\phi_{h}(T,\cdot)-\rho^{0}\,\phi_{h}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{h}.\end{split}

(3.10)

Here the unknowns
\rho_{T,h}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\uprho_{T,i}\varphi_{i}(x)

with non-negative coefficient
\uprho_{T,i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\bm{u}_{h}:=(\rho_{h},\bm{m}_{h},s_{h},\bm{n}_{h})
with

\displaystyle\rho_{h}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}{\uprho}_{ij}\,\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),\quad\bm{m}_{h}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\bm{\mathrm{m}}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),

\displaystyle s_{h}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}{\mathrm{s}}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),\quad\bm{n}_{h}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),

where coefficients
\uprho_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\bm{\mathrm{m}}_{ij},\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mathrm{s}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
\Phi_{h}:=(\phi_{h},\bm{\sigma}_{h})\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{d+1}

with
\bm{\sigma}_{h}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

We further note that the use of Gauss-Legendre integral rule along with the (nodal) Gauss-Legendre bases for the discontinuous spaces
W_{h}^{k-1}
and
M_{h}^{k-1}
significantly simplifies the optimization problem (
3.10
), as no
explicit degrees of freedom coupling is introduced among the physical variables
\bm{u}_{h}
and

\rho_{T,h}
.
In particular, given a fixed Lagrange multiplier
\Phi_{h}
, the nonlinear optimization problem (
3.10
) for
\bm{u}_{h}
and

\rho_{T,h}
can be solved in a pointwise fashion per integration point.
Moreover, positivity of the (space-time) density
\rho_{h}
and the terminal density

\rho_{T,h}
is guaranteed on all quadrature points by design as the admissible set requires
their coefficients to be non-negative:
\uprho_{ij},\uprho_{T,i}\geq 0
.
The scheme (
3.10
) does not prescribe the choice of basis functions for the
H^{1}
-conforming space
V_{h}^{k}
. The results are independent of the specific choice of bases for
V_{h}^{k}
. We use a nodal Gauss-Lobatto base in our implementation.

The scheme (
3.10
) is our fully discrete high-order finite element discretization to the scalar constraint optimization problem (
3.1
).
Next, we present its optimization solver using the ALG2 algorithm
[
15
]
, which is also referred to as the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) method
[
5
]
.

3.1.3.
Duality and augmented Lagrangian

We introduce the dual variables

\bm{u}_{h}^{*}=(\rho_{h}^{*},\bm{m}_{h}^{*},s_{h}^{*},\bm{n}_{h}^{*})\in[W_{h}^{k-1}]^{2d+2},\text{ and }\rho_{T,h}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\uprho_{T,i}^{*}\varphi_{i}(x)\in M_{h}^{k-1}.

To further simplify the notation, we denote

\bm{u}_{h}=\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}_{ij}\,\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),\text{ and }\bm{u}_{h}^{*}=\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}_{ij}^{*}\,\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),

where

\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}:=(\uprho_{ij},\bm{\mathrm{m}}_{ij},\mathrm{s}_{ij},\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d+2},\text{ with }\uprho_{ij}\geq 0,

and

\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}^{*}:=(\uprho_{ij}^{*},\bm{\mathrm{m}}_{ij}^{*},\mathrm{s}_{ij}^{*},\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{ij}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d+2}.

Introducing the following Legendre transforms:

\displaystyle H^{*}(\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}^{*}):=

\displaystyle\;\sup_{\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d+2},\uprho_{ij}\geq 0}\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}\cdot\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}^{*}-H(\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}),

(3.11a)

\displaystyle G^{*}(\uprho_{T,i}^{*}):=

\displaystyle\;\sup_{\uprho_{T,i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\uprho_{T,i}\cdot\uprho_{T,i}^{*}-G(\uprho_{T,i}),

(3.11b)

by duality, there holds

\displaystyle H(\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}):=

\displaystyle\;\sup_{\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d+2}}\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}\cdot\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}^{*}-H^{*}(\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}^{*}),

(3.11c)

\displaystyle G(\uprho_{T,i}):=

\displaystyle\;\sup_{\uprho_{T,i}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}}\uprho_{T,i}\cdot\uprho_{T,i}^{*}-G^{*}(\uprho_{T,i}^{*}).

(3.11d)

Plugging the relations (
3.11c
) and (
3.11d
) back to the scheme (
3.10
), we have the following dual formulation of (
3.10
):
Find the critical point of

\begin{split}\sup_{\bm{u}_{h},\,\rho_{T,h}}\inf_{\Phi_{h},\bm{u}_{h}^{*},\,\rho_{T,h}^{*}}~&\langle\!\langle H^{*}(\bm{u}_{h})+\bm{u}_{h}\cdot(\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h})-\bm{u}_{h}^{*})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\langle G^{*}(\rho_{T,h}^{*})-\rho_{T,h}\,(\phi_{h}(T,\cdot)+\rho_{T,h}^{*})+\rho^{0}\,\phi_{h}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{h}.\end{split}

(3.12)

It is clear that in the above formulation (
3.12
),
the variable
\bm{u}_{h}
is the Lagrange multiplier for the constraint
\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h})-\bm{u}_{h}^{*}=0
, while the variable
\rho_{T,h}
is the Lagrange multiplier for the terminal constraint
\phi_{h}(T,x)+\rho_{T,h}^{*}(x)=0
.

Hence, the critical point of the system (
3.12
) is equivalent to the
following augmented Lagrangian system

\begin{split}\sup_{\bm{u}_{h},\,\rho_{T,h}}\inf_{\Phi_{h},\bm{u}_{h}^{*},\,\rho_{T,h}^{*}}~&\langle\!\langle H^{*}(\bm{u}_{h}^{*})+\bm{u}_{h}\cdot(\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h})-\bm{u}_{h}^{*})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\langle G^{*}(\rho_{T,h}^{*})-\rho_{T,h}\,(\phi_{h}(T,\cdot)+\rho_{T,h}^{*})+\rho^{0}\,\phi_{h}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\frac{r}{2}\langle\!\langle(\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h})-\bm{u}_{h}^{*})\cdot(\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h})-\bm{u}_{h}^{*})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\frac{r}{2}\langle(\phi_{h}(T,\cdot)+\rho_{T,h}^{*})^{2}\rangle_{h},\end{split}

(3.13)

where
r>0
is the augmented Lagrangian parameter.

3.1.4.
The ALG2 algorithm and its efficient (modified) implementation

The ALG2 solves the optimization problem (
3.13
) in a splitting fashion. One update from iteration level
\ell-1
to
\ell
contains three steps:

•

Step A: Given data
\bm{u}_{h}^{\ell-1},\rho_{T,h}^{\ell-1},\bm{u}_{h}^{*,\ell-1},\rho_{T,h}^{*,\ell-1}
,
compute
\Phi_{h}^{\ell}
by optimizing the target functional in (
3.13
)
with respect to
\Phi_{h}
.

•

Step B: Given data

\bm{u}_{h}^{\ell-1},\rho_{T,h}^{\ell-1},\Phi_{h}^{\ell}
,
compute
\bm{u}_{h}^{*,\ell}
and
\rho_{T,h}^{*,\ell}
by optimizing the target functional in (
3.13
) with respect to
\bm{u}_{h}^{*}
and
\rho_{T,h}^{*}
.

•

Step C: Update the Lagrange multipliers

\bm{u}_{h}^{\ell},\rho_{T,h}^{\ell}
by the following explicit expressions:

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}^{\ell}=

\displaystyle\;\bm{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}+r(\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h}^{\ell})-\bm{u}_{h}^{*,\ell}),

(3.14a)

\displaystyle\rho_{T,h}^{\ell}=

\displaystyle\;\rho_{T,h}^{\ell-1}-r(\phi_{h}^{\ell}(T,\cdot)+\rho_{T,h}^{*,\ell}).

(3.14b)

Here Step A contains a global constant-coefficient linear system solver for the variables
\Phi_{h}=(\phi_{h},\bm{\sigma}_{h})
,
Step B is a pointwise nonlinear equation solved per quadrature point that involves the dual functions
H^{*}
and
G^{*}
, and Step C is a simple pointwise update.
In practical implementation, we apply two further simplifications to the above ALG2 algorithm: (1) apply splitting to solve
\phi_{h}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{h}
sequentially; (2) use duality to solve pointwise nonlinear problems for
\bm{u}_{h}
and
\rho_{T,h}
, and then apply a simple pointwise update for the dual variables
\bm{u}_{h}^{*}
and
\rho_{T,h}^{*}
.
We note that the second modification avoids the
explicit computation of the dual functions, which has already been used in our previous work
on ALG2 for variational time implicit schemes for reaction-diffusion systems
[
17
]
.
One iteration of the modified ALG2 algorithm is documented in Algorithm

1
below.
We skip the detailed derivation of each step. Since the whole algorithm is very similar to Algorithm 2 in
[
17
]
with simple modifications.

Algorithm 1

One iteration of (modified) ALG2 for (
3.13
).

1:

Step A1.
Find
\phi_{h}^{\ell}\in V_{h}^{k}

such that, for all
\psi_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\partial_{t}\phi_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\partial_{t}\psi_{h}+\nabla\phi_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\nabla\psi_{h}+\phi_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\psi_{h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}+\langle\phi_{h}^{\ell}(T,\cdot)\psi_{h}(T,\cdot)\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle(\rho_{h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\rho_{h}^{\ell-1}}{r}-\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{h}^{\ell-1})\partial_{t}\psi_{h}+(\bm{m}_{h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\bm{m}_{h}^{\ell-1}}{r})\cdot\nabla{\psi}_{h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle\;+\langle\!\langle(s_{h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{s_{h}^{\ell-1}}{r})\cdot{\psi}_{h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}-\langle(\rho_{T,h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\rho_{T,h}^{\ell-1}}{r})\cdot\psi_{h}(T,\cdot)\rangle_{h}-\langle\frac{\rho^{0}}{r}\cdot\psi_{h}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{h}.

2:

Step A2.
Find
\bm{\sigma}_{h}^{\ell}\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{d}
with
\bm{\sigma}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

such that

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\beta^{2}(\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{h}^{\ell})(\nabla\cdot\bm{\tau}_{h})+\bm{\sigma}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{\tau}_{h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle\beta(\rho_{h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\rho_{h}^{\ell-1}}{r}-\partial_{t}\phi_{h}^{\ell})\nabla\cdot\bm{\tau}_{h}+(\bm{n}_{h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\bm{n}_{h}^{\ell-1}}{r})\cdot\bm{\tau}_{h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}

for all
\bm{\tau}_{h}\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{d}
with
\bm{\tau}_{h}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

3:

Step A3. Evaluate
\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{h}^{\ell})
on each space-time quadrature point
(\zeta_{j},\bm{\xi}_{i})
, and
\phi_{h}^{\ell}(T,\cdot)
on each spatial quadrature point
\bm{\xi}_{i}
.
Denote these values as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\Phi_{ij}^{\ell}:=(\mathrm{d}\uprho_{ij}^{\ell},\bm{\mathrm{dm}}_{ij}^{\ell},\mathrm{ds}_{ij}^{\ell},\bm{\mathrm{dn}}_{ij}^{\ell}),\quad\mathrm{d}\uprho_{T,i}^{\ell}=\phi_{h}^{\ell}(T,\bm{\xi}_{i}).

Set

\overline{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}_{ij}^{\ell}=(\overline{\uprho}_{ij}^{\ell},\overline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{ij}^{\ell},\overline{{\mathrm{s}}}_{ij}^{\ell},\overline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{ij}^{\ell}):=\mathcal{D}\Phi_{ij}^{\ell}+\bm{\mathrm{u}}_{ij}^{\ell-1}/r,

and

\overline{\uprho}_{T,i}^{\ell}:=\uprho_{T,i}^{\ell-1}/r-\mathrm{d}\uprho_{T,i}^{\ell}.

4:

Step B1. For each quadrature point
(\zeta_{j},\bm{\xi}_{i})
, find the nonnegative
density coefficient
\uprho_{ij}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that it minimizes the function

\displaystyle L_{ij}(\uprho):=\frac{r^{2}|\overline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{ij}^{\ell}|^{2}}{r+V_{1}(\uprho)}+\frac{r^{2}|\overline{{\mathrm{s}}}_{ij}^{\ell}|^{2}}{r+V_{2}(\uprho)}+\frac{r^{2}|\overline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{ij}^{\ell}|^{2}}{r+V_{3}(\uprho)}+\frac{(\uprho-r\overline{\uprho}_{ij}^{\ell})^{2}}{r}-2F(\uprho),

and find the nonnegative terminal density coefficient

\uprho_{T,i}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that it minimizes the function

L_{T,i}(\uprho):=2G(\uprho)+{(\uprho-r\overline{\uprho}_{T,i}^{\ell})^{2}}/r.

5:

Step B2. Update the following coefficients:

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{m}}_{ij}^{\ell}=\;\frac{rV_{1}(\uprho_{ij}^{\ell})}{r+V_{1}(\uprho_{ij}^{\ell})}\overline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{ij}^{\ell},\;\;{\mathrm{s}}_{ij}^{\ell}=\;\frac{rV_{2}(\uprho_{ij}^{\ell})}{r+V_{2}(\uprho_{ij}^{\ell})}\overline{{\mathrm{s}}}_{ij}^{\ell},\;\;\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{ij}^{\ell}=\;\frac{rV_{3}(\uprho_{ij}^{\ell})}{r+V_{3}(\uprho_{ij}^{\ell})}\overline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{ij}^{\ell}.

6:

Step C. update the dual variables according to the following:

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{ij}^{*,\ell}=\overline{\bm{u}}_{ij}^{\ell}-\bm{u}_{ij}^{\ell}/r,\quad\uprho_{T,i}^{*,\ell}=\overline{\rho}_{T,i}^{\ell}-\rho_{T,i}^{\ell}/r.

Remark 3.2

(Computational complexity for Algorithm
1
)
.

Step A1 involves solving a symmetric positive definite linear system
for a constant-coefficient reaction-diffusion equation, for which we use preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) method with a geometric multigrid preconditioner. It achieves a linear computational complexity
\mathcal{O}(N)
with
N=\dim V_{h}^{k}
being the total number of degrees of freedom.
Step A2 is an
H(div)
-elliptic linear system problem.
This system is well-conditioned for small parameter
\beta\ll 1
, which is usually
the case in our applications. We use the PCG method with a Jacobi preconditioner, which achieves optimal linear computational complexity
\mathcal{O}(dN)
.
Step B and C involve pointwise updates, which again have a linear complexity of
\mathcal{O}(N)
.
Hence the overall computational complexity of applying one iteration of Algorithm
1

is linear.
It is also clear that Step B1 guarantees the positivity of density on all quadrature points.

Remark 3.3

(On unique solvability for Step B1)
.

Unique solvability of the one-dimensional minimization problem in Step B1 of Algorithm
1
can be guaranteed when the functions
L_{ij}
and
L_{T,i}

are strongly convex.
Strong convexity is ensured if the mobility functions
V_{1},V_{2}
, and
V_{3}
are positive and concave functions, the potential function
F(\uprho)
is concave, and
the function
G(\uprho)
is convex, which are satisfied by the choices in Table
1
.

3.2.
System case

In this subsection, we discretize the model (
2.18
) with
M
species and
R
reactions.
We note that due to our formulation, there are no additional technical difficulties for the system derivation compared with the scalar case. We still document details of the finite element scheme and its splitting optimization solver for the completeness of this paper.

Again, we introduce the vectors
\bm{n}_{i}:=\beta\nabla\rho_{i}
to avoid derivative evaluation of the densities for the scheme.
To simplify the notation, we
denote the collection of densities
\bm{\rho}=(\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{M})
,
fluxes
\underline{\bm{m}}=(\bm{m}_{1},\cdots,\bm{m}_{M})
, vectors
\underline{\bm{n}}=(\bm{n}_{1},\cdots,\bm{n}_{M})
, and sources
\bm{s}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{R})
.
Then optimization problem (
2.18
) is rewritten as follows:

\begin{split}\inf_{\bm{\rho},\underline{\bm{m}},\bm{s},\underline{\bm{n}}}~&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\frac{\|\bm{m}_{i}\|^{2}}{2V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\frac{|\bm{n}_{i}|^{2}}{2V_{3,i}(\rho_{i})}\right)+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|{s}_{p}|^{2}}{2V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}\right]\,dxdt\\
&-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\bm{F}(\bm{\rho})\,dxdt+\int_{\Omega}\bm{G}(\bm{\rho}(T,x))dx,\end{split}

(3.15a)

subject to constraints

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{i}+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}_{i}-\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}s_{p}

\displaystyle\;=0,

(3.15b)

\displaystyle\bm{n}_{i}-\beta\nabla\rho_{i}

\displaystyle\;=0,

(3.15c)

for all
1\leq i\leq M
on
[0,T]\times\Omega
,
with fixed initial densities
\rho_{i}(0,x)=\rho_{i}^{0}(x)
in
\Omega

and no-flux boundary conditions
\bm{m}_{i}\cdot\bm{\nu}=0
on
[0,T]\times\partial\Omega
.

Similar to the scalar case in (
3.4
), we reformulate the constrained optimization problem (
3.1
)
into the following saddle-point system:
Find the critical point of

\begin{split}\inf_{\underline{\bm{u}},\bm{\rho}_{T}}\sup_{\bm{\Phi}}~&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\bm{H}(\underline{\bm{u}})-\underline{\bm{u}}\cdot\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\Phi})\Big]\,dxdt\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\int_{\Omega}\Big[\bm{G}(\bm{\rho}_{T})+\bm{\rho}_{T}\cdot\bm{\phi}(T,\cdot)-\bm{\rho}^{0}\cdot\bm{\phi}(0,\cdot)\Big]dx.\end{split}

(3.16)

Here
the variables

\displaystyle\underline{\bm{u}}:=\;(\bm{\rho},\underline{\bm{m}},\bm{s},\underline{\bm{n}})\in[L^{2}(\Omega_{T})]^{(2d+1)M+R},

(3.17)

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}_{T}:=\;(\rho_{1,T},\cdots,\rho_{M,T})\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{M},

(3.18)

\displaystyle\Phi:=\;(\bm{\phi},\underline{\bm{\sigma}})\in[H^{1}(\Omega_{T})]^{M}\times\left[L^{2}([0,T])\otimes H(\mathrm{div}_{0};\Omega)\right]^{M},

(3.19)

with

\bm{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{M}),\text{ and }\underline{\bm{\sigma}}=(\bm{\sigma}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\sigma}_{M}),

and initial data
\bm{\rho}^{0}:=(\rho_{1}^{0},\cdots,\rho_{M}^{0})
.
Here the nonlinear function

\bm{H}(\underline{\bm{u}}):=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\frac{\|\bm{m}_{i}\|^{2}}{2V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\frac{|\bm{n}_{i}|^{2}}{2V_{3,i}(\rho_{i})}\right)+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|s_{p}|^{2}}{2V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}-\bm{F}(\bm{\rho}),

and the operator

\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\Phi}):=(\partial_{t}\bm{\phi}+\beta\nabla\cdot\underline{\bm{\sigma}},\nabla\bm{\phi},\Gamma^{T}\bm{\phi},\underline{\bm{\sigma}}),

where

\nabla\cdot\underline{\bm{\sigma}}:=(\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{1},\cdots,\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{M})

is the component-wise divergence and

\nabla\bm{\phi}:=(\nabla\phi_{1},\cdots,\nabla\phi_{M})

is the component-wise gradient, and

\Gamma=(\gamma_{i,p})\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times R}

is the reaction coefficient matrix.
Note that the inner-product

\bm{u}\cdot\mathcal{D}(\Phi)

has the following component-wise form

\bm{u}\cdot\mathcal{D}(\Phi)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\rho_{i}(\partial_{t}\phi_{i}+\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{i})+\bm{m}_{i}\cdot\nabla\phi_{i}+\bm{n}_{i}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{i}\right)+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\sum_{i=1}^{M}s_{p}\gamma_{i,p}\phi_{i}.

Replacing the function spaces with appropriate (high-order) finite element spaces and applying numerical integration,
we derive the following fully discrete scheme:
Find the critical point of the fully discrete system

\begin{split}\inf_{\underline{\bm{u}}_{h},\,\bm{\rho}_{T,h}}\sup_{\bm{\Phi}_{h}}~&\langle\!\langle\bm{H}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h})-\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}\cdot\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\Phi}_{h})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\langle\bm{G}(\bm{\rho}_{T,h})+\bm{\rho}_{T,h}\cdot\bm{\phi}_{h}(T,\cdot)-\bm{\rho}^{0}\cdot\bm{\phi}_{h}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{h}.\end{split}

(3.20)

Here the unknowns
\bm{\rho}_{T,h}=\sum_{{i}=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}{\bm{\uprho}}_{T,{i}}\varphi_{i}(x)

with non-negative coefficient
{\bm{\uprho}}_{T,i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}
,

\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}:=(\bm{\rho}_{h},\underline{\bm{m}}_{h},\bm{s}_{h},\underline{\bm{n}}_{h})
with

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}_{h}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{{j}=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{{i}=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}{\bm{\uprho}}_{ij}\,\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),\quad\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\underline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),

\displaystyle\bm{s}_{h}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}{\bm{\mathrm{s}}}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),\quad\underline{\bm{n}}_{h}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\underline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),

where coefficients
\bm{\uprho}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}
,
\underline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{ij},\underline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{dM}
, and
\bm{\mathrm{s}}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{R}
,
and
\bm{\Phi}_{h}:=(\bm{\phi}_{h},\underline{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{(d+1)M}

with
\underline{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

We introduce the dual variables

\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{*}=(\bm{\rho}_{h}^{*},\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{*},\bm{s}_{h}^{*},\underline{\bm{n}}_{h}^{*})\in[W_{h}^{k-1}]^{(2d+1)M+R},

and

\bm{\rho}_{T,h}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\bm{\uprho}_{T,i}^{*}\varphi_{i}(x)\in M_{h}^{k-1}
, and denote

\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}=\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\underline{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}_{ij}\,\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),\text{ and }\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{*}=\;\sum_{j=1}^{kN_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{k^{d}N_{S}}\underline{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}_{ij}^{*}\,\psi_{j}(t)\varphi_{i}(x),

with coefficients

\underline{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}_{ij}:=(\bm{\uprho}_{ij},\underline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{ij},\bm{\mathrm{s}}_{ij},\underline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{(2d+1)M+R},\text{ with }\bm{\uprho}_{ij}\geq 0,

and

\underline{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}_{ij}^{*}:=(\bm{\uprho}_{ij}^{*},\underline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{ij}^{*},\bm{\mathrm{s}}_{ij}^{*},\underline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{ij}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{(2d+1)M+R}.

Using these dual variables,
we obtain the following augmented Lagrangian formulation of (
3.20
):

\begin{split}\sup_{\underline{\bm{u}}_{h},\,\bm{\rho}_{T,h}}\inf_{\bm{\Phi}_{h},\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{*},\,\bm{\rho}_{T,h}^{*}}~&\langle\!\langle\bm{H}^{*}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{*})+\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}\cdot(\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\Phi}_{h})-\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{*})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\langle\bm{G}^{*}(\bm{\rho}_{T,h}^{*})-\bm{\rho}_{T,h}\cdot(\bm{\phi}_{h}(T,\cdot)+\bm{\rho}_{T,h}^{*})\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\frac{r}{2}\langle\!\langle(\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\Phi}_{h})-\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{*})\cdot(\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\Phi}_{h})-\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{*})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\hskip-5.69046pt+\frac{r}{2}\langle|\bm{\phi}_{h}(T,\cdot)+\bm{\rho}_{T,h}^{*}|^{2}\rangle_{h}+\langle\!\langle\bm{\rho}^{0}\cdot\bm{\phi}_{h}(0,\cdot)\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\end{split}

(3.21)

Finally, we introduce a splitting algorithm (modified ALG2) for
the saddle-point system (
3.21
), where we sequentially compute each component of

\bm{\Phi}_{h}
in the linear elliptic update (Step A)
and of the densities
\bm{\rho}_{h}
and
\bm{\rho}_{T,h}
in the nonlinear update (Step B1); see also
[
17
]
.

The coupled global elliptic linear system for
\bm{\Phi}_{h}=(\bm{\phi}_{h},\underline{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})
in ALG2 takes the following form:
Find
(\bm{\phi}_{h},\underline{\bm{\sigma}}_{h})\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{(d+1)M}
with

\underline{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
,
such that, for all

(\bm{\psi}_{h},\underline{\bm{\tau}}_{h})\in[V_{h}^{k}]^{(d+1)M}
with

\underline{\bm{\tau}}_{h}\cdot\bm{\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, there exists

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{M}\langle\!\langle(\partial_{t}\phi_{i,h}+\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{i,h})(\partial_{t}\psi_{i,h}+\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\tau}_{i,h})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\quad+\langle\!\langle\nabla\phi_{i,h}\cdot\nabla\psi_{i,h}+\bm{\sigma}_{i,h}\cdot\bm{\tau}_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}+\langle\phi_{i,h}(T,\cdot)\psi_{i,h}(T,\cdot)\rangle_{h}\\
&+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\langle\!\langle(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\gamma_{i,p}\phi_{i,h})(\sum_{i=1}^{M}\gamma_{i,p}\psi_{i,h})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
=\;\;&\sum_{i=1}^{M}\langle\!\langle(\rho_{i,h}^{*}-\frac{\rho_{i,h}}{r})(\partial_{t}\psi_{i,h}+\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\tau}_{i,h})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\quad+\langle\!\langle(\bm{m}_{i,h}^{*}-\frac{\bm{m}_{i,h}}{r})\cdot\nabla\psi_{i,h}+(\bm{n}_{i,h}^{*}-\frac{\bm{n}_{i,h}}{r})\cdot\bm{\tau}_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\quad-\langle(\rho_{i,T,h}^{*}-\frac{\rho_{i,T,h}}{r})\psi_{i,h}(T,\cdot)\rangle_{h}+\langle(\rho_{i,0}^{*}-\frac{\rho_{i,0}}{r})\psi_{i,h}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{h}\\
&+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\langle\!\langle((s_{p,h}^{*}-\frac{s_{p,h}}{r}))(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\gamma_{i,p}\psi_{i,h})\rangle\!\rangle_{h}.\end{split}

(3.22)

This system is solved sequentially in practice to drive down the overall computational cost.
The
i
-th component scalar reaction-diffusion solver for
\phi_{i,h}^{\ell}
at
\ell
-th iteration reads as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle\!\langle\partial_{t}\phi_{i,h}^{\ell}\partial_{t}\psi_{i,h}+\nabla\phi_{i,h}^{\ell}\cdot\nabla\psi_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&+\langle\phi_{i,h}^{\ell}(T,\cdot)\psi_{i,h}(T,\cdot)\rangle_{h}+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\langle\!\langle\gamma_{i,p}^{2}\phi_{i,h}^{\ell}\psi_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
=\;\;&\langle\!\langle(\rho_{i,h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\rho_{i,h}^{\ell-1}}{r}-\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{i,h}^{\ell-1})\partial_{t}\psi_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&+\langle\!\langle(\bm{m}_{i,h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\bm{m}_{i,h}^{\ell-1}}{r})\cdot\nabla\psi_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\quad-\langle(\rho_{i,T,h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\rho_{i,T,h}^{\ell-1}}{r})\psi_{i,h}(T,\cdot)\rangle_{h}+\langle(\rho_{i,0}^{*}-\frac{\rho_{i,0}}{r})\psi_{i,h}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{h}\\
&+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\langle\!\langle(s_{p,h}^{*}-\frac{s_{p,h}}{r}-\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\gamma_{j,p}\phi_{j,h}^{\ell}-\sum_{j=i+1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\phi_{j,h}^{\ell-1})\gamma_{i,p}\psi_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}.\end{split}

(3.23)

The
i
-th component
H(\mathrm{div})
-elliptic solver for
\bm{\sigma}_{i,h}^{\ell}
reads as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle\!\langle\beta^{2}(\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{i,h}^{\ell})(\nabla\cdot\bm{\tau}_{i,h})+\bm{\sigma}_{i,h}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{\tau}_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}\\
&\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{M}\langle\!\langle(\rho_{i,h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\rho_{i,h}^{\ell-1}}{r}-\partial_{t}\phi_{i,h}^{\ell})\beta\nabla\cdot\bm{\tau}_{i,h}+(\bm{n}_{i,h}^{*,\ell-1}-\frac{\bm{n}_{i,h}^{\ell-1}}{r})\cdot\bm{\tau}_{i,h}\rangle\!\rangle_{h}.\end{split}

(3.24)

Meanwhile, the nonlinear update on each quadrature point for
the densities
\bm{\uprho}_{ij}^{\ell}=({\uprho}_{1,ij}^{\ell},\cdots,{\uprho}_{M,ij}^{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}

takes the following form:
find
\bm{\uprho}_{ij}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}
that minimizes the function

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{ij}(\bm{\uprho}):=&\;\sum_{\mathsf{m}=1}^{M}\frac{r^{2}|\overline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell}|^{2}}{r+V_{1,\mathsf{m}}(\uprho_{\mathsf{m}})}+\frac{r^{2}|\overline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell}|^{2}}{r+V_{3,\mathsf{m}}(\uprho_{\mathsf{m}})}+\frac{(\uprho_{\mathsf{m}}-r\overline{\uprho}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell})^{2}}{r}\\
&\;-2\bm{F}(\bm{\uprho})+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{r^{2}|\overline{{\mathrm{s}}}_{p,ij}^{\ell}|^{2}}{r+V_{2,p}(\bm{\uprho})}.\end{split}

(3.25)

And the nonlinear update for the terminal densities

\bm{\uprho}_{T,i}^{\ell}=({\uprho}_{1,T,i}^{\ell},\cdots,{\uprho}_{M,T,i}^{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}

takes the following form:
find
\bm{\uprho}_{T,i}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}
that minimizes the function

\displaystyle L_{T,i}(\bm{\uprho}):=

\displaystyle\;2\bm{G}(\bm{\uprho})+{|\bm{\uprho}-r\overline{\bm{\uprho}}_{T,i}^{\ell}|^{2}}/r.

(3.26)

Here the bar-values take the same form as in Step A3 of Algorithm
1
,
namely,

\displaystyle\overline{\underline{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}}_{ij}^{\ell}:=(\overline{\bm{\uprho}}_{ij}^{\ell},\underline{\overline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}}_{ij}^{\ell},\overline{\bm{\mathrm{s}}}_{ij}^{\ell},\overline{\underline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}}_{ij}^{\ell}):=\underline{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\Phi}_{ij}^{\ell}+\underline{\bm{\mathrm{u}}}_{ij}^{\ell}/r,\;\overline{\bm{\uprho}}_{T,i}^{\ell}:=\bm{\uprho}_{T,i}^{\ell}/r-\bm{\phi}_{h}^{\ell}(T,\bm{\xi}_{i}).

(3.27)

The pointwise optimization problems in (
3.23
) and (
3.26
)
are
M
-dimensional problems. These problems are usually loosely coupled among the density components. We further reduce the computational cost by separately solving for each density component sequentially, which results in
M
one-dimensional minimization problems per integration point. The positivity of the densities is guaranteed in our algorithm at the quadrature points.
After the densities
\bm{\uprho}_{ij}^{\ell}
and
\bm{\uprho}_{T,i}^{\ell}
have been computed, we then update the other physical variables on the quadrature point as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{rV_{1,\mathsf{m}}(\uprho_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell})}{r+V_{1,\mathsf{m}}(\uprho_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell})}\overline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell},\;\;

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell}=\;\frac{rV_{3,\mathsf{m}}(\uprho_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell})}{r+V_{3,\mathsf{m}}(\uprho_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell})}\overline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell},

(3.28a)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{*,\ell}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{r}{r+V_{1,\mathsf{m}}(\uprho_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell})}\overline{\bm{\mathrm{m}}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell},\;\;

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{*,\ell}=\;\frac{r}{r+V_{3,\mathsf{m}}(\uprho_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell})}\overline{\bm{\mathrm{n}}}_{\mathsf{m},ij}^{\ell},

(3.28b)

\displaystyle{\bm{\uprho}}_{ij}^{*,\ell}=

\displaystyle\;\overline{\bm{\uprho}}_{ij}^{\ell}-{\bm{\uprho}}_{ij}^{\ell}/r,\;\;

\displaystyle{\bm{\uprho}}_{T,i}^{*,\ell}=\;\overline{\bm{\uprho}}_{T,i}^{\ell}-{\bm{\uprho}}_{T,i}^{\ell}/r,

(3.28c)

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathrm{s}}}_{p,ij}^{\ell}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{rV_{2,p}(\bm{\uprho}_{ij}^{\ell})}{r+V_{2,p}(\bm{\uprho}_{ij}^{\ell})}\overline{{\mathrm{s}}}_{p,ij}^{\ell},\;\;

\displaystyle{\mathrm{s}}_{p,ij}^{*,\ell}=\;\frac{r}{r+V_{2,p}(\bm{\uprho}_{ij}^{\ell})}\overline{{\mathrm{s}}}_{p,ij}^{\ell}.

(3.28d)

For completeness, we collect the above procedures into the following algorithm.

Algorithm 2

One iteration of (modified) ALG2 for (
3.21
).

1:

Step A. For
i=1,\cdots,M
, solve the linear system (
3.23
) for the unknown
\phi_{i,h}^{\ell}
, and solve the linear system (
3.24
)
for the unknown
\bm{\sigma}_{i,h}^{\ell}
.

on quadrature points, and compute the bar-values (on quadrature points) in (

2:

3.25

3:

Step C. Update the other variables on quadrature points according to (

5.
Discussions

In this paper, we model and compute a class of generalized optimal transport and mean field control problems for reaction-diffusion equations and systems. The control problems are constructed by general choices of transport and reaction mobility functions, such as
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
, and
V_{3}
, derived from entropy dissipation properties. We apply a high-order spatial-time finite element method to discretize the spatial-time domain and use the ALG2 algorithm to compute mean field control problems. Numerical examples in Section
4
, including transporting two Gaussian distributions and a system of
12
images, demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed mean field control models and computations.

In future work, we shall study general modeling, computation, and inverse mean field control problems for reaction-diffusion systems. The generalized nonlinear reaction functions often represent complex behaviors between different populations, exhibiting patterns in social dynamical systems. The mean field control problem over transportation and reactions provides new patterns in population behaviors observed from our numerical examples. The analytical study of these new patterns could be a future research direction. We also expect that the mean field control problem of reaction-diffusion systems has vast applications in pandemic control, computer vision, and image processing problems. The other important direction is the parallel and high-order computation of generalized optimal transport and mean field control problems on three-dimensional spatial domains.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Denote
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\int_{\Omega}E(\rho(x))dx
. Thus
\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{E}(\rho)=E^{\prime}(\rho)
.
By the change of variable formula, we obtain

\bm{m}=\tilde{\bm{m}}-\beta V_{1}(\rho)\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho),\quad{s}=\tilde{s}-\beta V_{2}(\rho)E^{\prime}(\rho).

The constraint set (
2.16b
) satisfies

\begin{split}0=&\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\tilde{\bm{m}}-\beta V_{1}(\rho)\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho))-(\tilde{s}-\beta V_{2}(\rho)E^{\prime}(\rho))\\
=&\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}-{s}.\end{split}

Moreover, the terms in objective functional (
2.11a
) satisfy

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\tilde{\bm{m}}(t,x)\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho(t,x))}+\frac{|\tilde{s}(t,x)|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho(t,x))}\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}+\beta V_{1}(\rho)\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho)\|}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s+\beta V_{2}(\rho)E^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}+\beta^{2}V_{1}(\rho)^{2}\|\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho)\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}+\beta^{2}V_{2}(\rho)^{2}|E^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}\Big]dxdt\\
&+\beta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\bm{m}\cdot\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho)-s\cdot E^{\prime}(\rho)\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\|\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho)\|^{2}V_{1}(\rho)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}|E^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}V_{2}(\rho)\Big]dxdt\\
&+\beta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}E^{\prime}(\rho)\Big[-\nabla\cdot\bm{m}+s\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\|\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho)\|^{2}V_{1}(\rho)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}|E^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}V_{2}(\rho)\Big]dxdt\\
&+\beta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[E^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{t}\rho\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\|\nabla E^{\prime}(\rho)\|^{2}V_{1}(\rho)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}|E^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}V_{2}(\rho)\Big]dxdt\\
&+\beta\int_{\Omega}\Big[E(\rho(T,\cdot))-E(\rho^{0})\Big]dx,\end{split}

where the last equality follows the fact that
\int_{0}^{T}E^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{t}\rho dt=\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}E(\rho)dt=E(\rho(T,\cdot))-E(\rho^{0})
.
Thus we derive the variational problem (
2.13
) using the definition of
V_{3}
in (
2.12
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Denote the Lagrange multiplier of problem (
2.13
) as
\phi\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Consider the following saddle point problem:

\begin{split}\inf_{\bm{m},s,\rho,\rho_{T}}\sup_{\phi}\quad\mathcal{L}(\bm{m},s,\rho,\rho_{T},\phi),\end{split}

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}(\bm{m},s,\rho,\phi)=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{2V_{1}(\rho)}+\frac{|s|^{2}}{2V_{2}(\rho)}+\phi\Big(\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}-s\Big)\Big]dxdt\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\mathcal{I}(\rho)-\mathcal{F}(\rho)\Big]dt+\mathcal{G}(\rho_{T})+\beta\mathcal{E}(\rho_{T}).\end{split}

Assume
\rho>0
. By solving the saddle point problem of
\mathcal{L}
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{split}&\frac{\delta}{\delta\bm{m}}\mathcal{L}=0,\\
&\frac{\delta}{\delta s}\mathcal{L}=0,\\
&\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\mathcal{L}=0,\\
&\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi}\mathcal{L}=0,\\
&\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho_{T}}\mathcal{L}=0,\\
\end{split}\right.\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\{\begin{split}&\frac{\bm{m}}{V_{1}}=\nabla\phi,\\
&\frac{s}{V_{2}}=\phi,\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|\bm{m}\|^{2}}{V_{1}^{2}}V_{1}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|s|^{2}}{V_{2}^{2}}V_{2}^{\prime}+\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho}\Big[\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\mathcal{I}(\rho)-\mathcal{F}(\rho)\Big]-\partial_{t}\phi=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}-s=0,\\
&\phi_{T}+\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho_{T}}\Big(\mathcal{G}(\rho_{T})+\beta\mathcal{E}(\rho_{T})\Big)=0.\end{split}\right.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

The proof is similar to the scalar case. Denote
\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})=\int_{\Omega}E_{i}(\rho_{i}(x))dx
. Thus
\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho_{i}}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\rho_{i})=E_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})
.
By the change of variable formula, we obtain

\bm{m}_{i}=\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}-\beta V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}),\quad{s}_{p}=\tilde{s}_{p}-\beta V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}E^{\prime}_{j}(\rho_{j}).

The constraint set (
2.18b
) satisfies

\begin{split}0=&\partial_{t}\rho_{i}+\nabla\cdot(\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}-\beta V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}))-(\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}\tilde{s}_{p}-\beta\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}E^{\prime}_{j}(\rho_{j}))\\
=&\partial_{t}\rho_{i}+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}_{i}-\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}{s}_{p}.\end{split}

Moreover, the terms in objective functional (
2.18a
) satisfies

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\|\tilde{\bm{m}}_{i}(t,x)\|^{2}}{2V_{1,i}(\rho_{i}(t,x))}+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|\tilde{s}_{p}(t,x)|^{2}}{2V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho}(t,x))}\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\|\bm{m}_{i}+\beta V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})\|}{2V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|s_{p}+\beta\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})E^{\prime}_{j}(\rho_{j})|^{2}}{2V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\|\bm{m}_{i}\|^{2}+\beta^{2}V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})^{2}\|\nabla E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})\|^{2}}{2V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|s_{p}|^{2}+\beta^{2}|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}V_{2,p}(\rho)E^{\prime}_{j}(\rho_{j})|^{2}}{2V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}\Big]dxdt\\
&+\beta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{m}_{i}\cdot\nabla E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})-\sum_{p=1}^{R}s_{p}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})E^{\prime}_{j}(\rho_{j})\Big]dxdt.\end{split}

We only need to show that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{m}_{i}\cdot\nabla E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})-\sum_{p=1}^{R}s_{p}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}E^{\prime}_{j}(\rho_{j})\Big]dxdt=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Big[E_{i}(\rho_{i}(T,\cdot))-E_{i}(\rho_{i}^{0})\Big]dx.

This is true from the following fact:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{m}_{i}\cdot\nabla E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})-\sum_{p=1}^{R}s_{p}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}E^{\prime}_{j}(\rho_{j})\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{M}E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})\cdot\Big[-\nabla\cdot\bm{m}_{i}+\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}s_{p}\Big]dxdt\\
=&\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{M}E^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})\cdot\partial_{t}\rho_{i}dtdx\\
=&\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Big[E_{i}(\rho_{i}(T,\cdot))-E_{i}(\rho_{i}^{0})\Big]dx,\end{split}

where the last equality uses the integration by parts in the time variable. This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

The proof is similar to the scalar case. We derive the minimizer system for variational problem (
2.18
). Denote
\phi_{i}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as the Lagrange multiplier of constraint (
2.18a
), for
i=1,2,\cdots,M
. Write
\phi=(\phi_{i})_{i=1}^{M}
. Consider the following saddle point problem

\inf_{\bm{m},s,\rho,\rho_{T}}\sup_{\phi}\quad\mathcal{L}_{1}(\bm{m},s,\bm{\rho},\bm{\rho}_{T},\phi),

where

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{1}(\bm{m},s,\bm{\rho},\bm{\rho}_{T},\phi)\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\|\bm{m}_{i}\|^{2}}{V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|s_{p}|^{2}}{V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\bm{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{\rho})\Big]dx-\mathcal{F}(\bm{\rho}(t,\cdot))dt\\
&+\mathcal{G}(\bm{\rho}_{T})+\beta\Big(\mathcal{E}(\bm{\rho}_{T})-\mathcal{E}(\bm{\rho}_{0})\Big)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{i}\cdot\Big\{\partial_{t}\rho_{i}+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}_{i}-\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}s_{p}\Big\}dxdt.\end{split}

Assume
\rho_{i}>0
,
i=1,2,\cdots,M
. By solving the saddle point problem of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{split}&\frac{\delta}{\delta\bm{m}_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0,\\
&\frac{\delta}{\delta s_{p}}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0,\\
&\frac{\delta}{\delta\rho_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0,\\
&\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0,\\
&\frac{\delta}{\delta\bm{\rho}_{T}}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0,\end{split}\right.\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\{\begin{split}&{\bm{m}_{i}}=V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})\nabla\phi_{i},\\
&s_{p}=V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\sum_{j=1}^{M}\gamma_{j,p}\phi_{p},\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\|\bm{m}_{i}\|^{2}}{V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})^{2}}V_{1,i}(\rho_{i})^{\prime}\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{R}\frac{|s_{p}|^{2}}{V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho_{i}}V_{2,p}(\bm{\rho})\\
&+\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho_{i}}[\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\bm{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{\rho})-\mathcal{F}(\bm{\rho})]-\partial_{t}\phi_{i}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\rho_{i}+\nabla\cdot\bm{m}_{i}-\sum_{p=1}^{R}\gamma_{i,p}s_{p}=0,\\
&\phi_{T}+\frac{\delta}{\delta\bm{\rho}_{T}}\mathcal{G}(\bm{\rho}_{T})+\beta\frac{\delta}{\delta\bm{\rho}_{T}}\mathcal{E}(\bm{\rho}_{T})=0.\end{split}\right.

This finishes the proof.
∎

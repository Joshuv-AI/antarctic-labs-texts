Abstract
. We obtain some nonlocal characterizations for a class of variable exponent Sobolev spaces arising in nonlinear elasticity, in the theory of electrorheological fluids as well as in image processing for the regions where the variable exponent p ⁡ ( x ) p(x) reaches the value 1 1 .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J92, 35P30, 34L16

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain,
p\geq 1
and
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a continuous function, except at a finite number of points, such that
\varphi(0)=0
and

\gamma_{n,p}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi(t)\,t^{-(p+1)}\,dt=1,

where

\gamma_{n,p}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}|\omega\cdot\mathbf{e}|^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega),\quad\mathbf{e}\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

If
\varphi
satisfies further growth conditions, Brezis and Nguyen proved recently in
[
7
]
that for
every function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
p>1

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\delta^{p}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi\left(|u(x)-u(y)|/\delta\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx.

Hence the
p
-Dirichlet energy functional

W^{1,p}(\Omega)\ni u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx

can be approximated by a suitable class of nonlocal nonconvex energy functionals.

A similar pointwise convergence result was investigated in
[
6
]
for smooth functions in the
case
p=1
, relevant for image processing. In this case, the above nonlocal nonconvex functionals are related to image processing theories. For nonsmooth
functions the situation is much more involved and the convergence only holds in the sense of
\varGamma
-convergence

[
6
]
. As a model function for the above results one can think of

(1.1)

\varphi(t)=a\begin{cases}t^{p+1},&\ \mathrm{for}\ 0\leq t\leq 1,\\
1,&\ \mathrm{for}\ t>1,\end{cases}\qquad a>0.

Other types of nonlocal nonconvex approximations of
p
-Dirichlet energies where previously investigated by Nguyen in
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
after more classical convex approximations like the one by Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu where studied, see
[
3
,
4
]
.

On the other hand, differential equations and variational problems involving
variable
p(x)
-growth conditions, and hence variable exponent Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p(\cdot)}
, arise
from nonlinear elasticity theory and electrorheological fluids, and have been the target of various
investigations, especially in regularity theory, see
[
10
,
1
,
2
]
.
A model investigated by Chen, Levine e Rao
[
8
]
was elaborated with the idea
of merging parts of the domain where
p(x)=2
(isotropic diffusion) is a suitable
choice and parts where
p(x)=1
(total variation case) is instead more suitable.
They investigated the minimization problem

\min\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{p(x)}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u-f|^{2}dx

with
1\leq p(x)\leq 2
and
\lambda\geq 0
.
It is thus natural to wonder if also the
p(\cdot)
-Dirichlet energy

W^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)\ni u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx

can be approximated by a suitable class of nonlocal nonconvex energy functionals.
We will prove that, in fact, under suitable assumptions on the function
\varphi(x,t)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\delta^{p(x)}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x)-u(y)|/\delta\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx,

for every
u\in W^{1,p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)\cap W^{1,p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)
in the case
\inf p>1
and for
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
in the case
\inf p=1
.
As an example of function
\varphi
consistent with the meaningful constant case (
1.1
),
one can consider

\varphi(x,t)=\begin{cases}a(x)t^{p(x)+1},&\ \mathrm{for}\ 0\leq t\leq 1,\\
b(x),&\ \mathrm{for}\ t>1,\end{cases}

where
a(x)
is a measurable function and

b(x)=p(x)\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{n,p(x)}}-a(x)\right),\qquad a(x)\leq\frac{p(x)}{\gamma_{n,p(x)}\left(p(x)+1\right)}\leq b(x),

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
,
in order to fulfill the normalization

(1.2)

\gamma_{n,p(x)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,t)\,t^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt=1,

where we have set, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\gamma_{n,p(x)}:=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}|\omega\cdot\mathbf{e}|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega),\quad\mathbf{e}\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

Previously, in the recent work
[
12
]
, two of the authors proved a different approximation result
in the variable exponent case. More precisely, for

u\in W^{1,p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap W^{1,p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, then

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{n,p(x)}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx,

where we have set

K_{n,p(x)}:=\frac{1}{p(x)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\omega\cdot\mathbf{e}\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega),\quad\mathbf{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

This extends the results in
[
14
]
.

The limitations to

u\in W^{1,p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)\cap W^{1,p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)

in place of
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
is related to failure of the
continuity inequality for the maximal
function when
p(x)
is not constant. More precisely, taken any
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, we define

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}(u)(x)=\sup_{h>0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}|u(x+s\omega)|\,ds\,

as the maximal function of
u
along the direction
\omega
. By
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]

there exists
C>0
such that, for all
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}(u)(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx,\qquad\forall u\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right).

For variable exponents the inequality fails in general
[
9
,
13
]
. For instance, if

p(x)=2
on
(-\infty,-2)
and
p(x)\geq 4
on
[2,+\infty)
, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{p(x)}dx<+\infty
,
but
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{M}(u)|^{p(x)}dx=+\infty
for the function
u(x)=|x|^{-1/3}\chi_{[2,\infty)}(x)
.

2.
Main results

Let us now formulate the main results.
Consider
p:\Omega\to[1,+\infty)
measurable and set

p^{-}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,inf}_{\Omega}p\qquad\text{and}\qquad p^{+}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{\Omega}p.

We set

W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\Omega\right):=W^{1,p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)\cap W^{1,p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right),

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a continuous function – except at a finite number of points in
(0,+\infty)
– such that
\varphi(x,0)=0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and satisfying the following assumptions

(2.1)

\exists a\geq 0:\quad\varphi(x,t)\leq at^{p(x)+1},\quad\forall t\in[0,1],

(2.2)

\exists b\geq 0:\quad\varphi(x,t)\leq b,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In the spirit of
[
7
]
, we introduce the following nonlocal functionals

\Lambda_{\delta}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,|u(x)-u(y)|\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy,

where

\varphi_{\delta}(x,u):=\delta^{p(x)}\varphi(x,u/\delta),\qquad\delta>0.

As anticipated, we will prove that, in the above framework, if
1\leq p^{-}\leq p^{+}<+\infty
, then

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx

for
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\Omega\right)
when
p^{-}>1

and for
u\in C^{1}\left(\bar{\Omega}\right)
when
p^{-}=1
. As already pointed out this
is not a technical limitation, but a true difference between
p^{-}>1
and
p^{-}=1
.
Further sufficient conditions for a function to belong to
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)

can be found in Proposition
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
.
The integrals of
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
corresponds to
\varphi(x,t)=\chi_{(1,+\infty)}(t)
,
up to small changes in the formulation of the results.

2.1.
The case
p^{-}>1

We have the following main result

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<+\infty
and
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\Omega\right)
. Assume (
1.2
), (
2.1
), (
2.2
). Then

(a)

there exists
C>0
, depending on
p^{\pm}
and the domain
\Omega
, such that for every
\delta>0

\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{p^{+}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{p^{-}}\right);

(b)

we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Proof.

First of all, we prove the results in the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By making the change of variables
z=x-y
and using polar coordinates for
z
, we can write
\Lambda_{\delta}(u)
as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\delta^{p(x)}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x)-u(y)|/\delta\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\delta^{p(x)}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{|h\omega|^{n+p(x)}}h^{n-1}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\delta^{p(x)}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx.

By setting
h=\delta\tilde{h}
and relabeling
\tilde{h}
as
h
, we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\delta^{p(x)}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\delta^{p(x)}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta\tilde{h}\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{\left(\delta\tilde{h}\right)^{p(x)+1}}\,\delta d\tilde{h}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx,

so that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,|u(x)-u(y)|\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx.

Now, from the fundamental theorem of calculus, the inequalities chain

\displaystyle\frac{\left|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)\right|}{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta h}\left|\frac{d}{ds}u(x+s\omega)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\delta h}\left|{\nabla u(x+s\omega)\cdot\omega}\right|\,ds\leq h\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)

follows for a.e.
(x,h,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, where
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)
denotes the maximal function of
\nabla u
along the direction
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
that, for a general function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, is given by

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(f\right)(x):=\sup_{h>0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}\left|f(x+s\omega)\right|\,ds.

Let’s set

\tilde{\varphi}(x,t):=\begin{cases}at^{p(x)+1}\,,&t\in[0,1)\\
b\,,&t\in[1,+\infty)\end{cases}.

\tilde{\varphi}(x,\cdot)
is a non-decreasing function for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and, according to hypothesis (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we have

\varphi(x,t)\leq\tilde{\varphi}(x,t),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\,\,\text{for a.e.}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For these reasons,

\varphi\left(x,\left|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)\right|/\delta\right)\leq\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,\left|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)\right|/\delta\right)\leq\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,h\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right)

for a.e.
(x,h,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, so – being
\tilde{\varphi}
a non-negative function – we can increase
\Lambda_{\delta}(u)
as follows

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,h\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)}{t}\right)^{p(x)+1}\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)}\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)t^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx,

where we have set
t=h\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)
. We are now ready to prove the existence of a constant
C>0
, depending on
p^{\pm}
, such that

\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}\right).

First of all, for how the function
\tilde{\varphi}
was built, let’s observe that, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the term

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)t^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt

is bounded. In fact, recalling that
1<p^{-}\leq p(x)\leq p^{+}<+\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)t^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(at^{p(x)+1}\right)t^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt+\int_{1}^{+\infty}bt^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq a+b\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{p^{-}+1}}\,dt=\alpha<+\infty,

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so that equation (
2.3
) becomes

(2.4)

\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\leq\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx.

At this point, the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx

can be splitted over the sets of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\leq 1\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)>1,

in order to increase the integrand function by using, respectively, the exponents
p^{-}
or
p^{+}
and then by extending both the integrals over the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this way, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p^{-}}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p^{+}}\,dx,\quad\forall\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

By the theory of maximal functions, as treated in
[
18
]
and adapted to the variable exponent case in
[
12
]
, there exists positive constants
C_{p^{\pm}}
, depending on
p^{\pm}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p^{-}}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p^{+}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p^{-}}\,dx+C_{p^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p^{+}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{p^{\pm}}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}}\right),\qquad\forall\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},

where
C_{p^{\pm}}:=\max\left\{C_{p^{-}},C_{p^{+}}\right\}
. For this reason, equation (
2.4
) becomes

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx.

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\,C_{p^{\pm}}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}}\right)\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\right).

The assertion follows taking
C:=\alpha\,C_{p^{\pm}}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\right)
.

By the notion of directional derivative, let’s observe that

(2.5)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|}{\delta}=\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h

for a.e.
(x,h,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. As a consequence, since – for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
–
\varphi(x,\cdot)
is continuous at
0
and almost everywhere on
(0,+\infty)
, we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|}{\delta}\right)=\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)

for a.e.
(x,h,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. Making the integral of this quantity over the sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and respect to
h\in[0,+\infty)
, by replacing
t=\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h
, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we get

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|^{p(x)+1}}{t^{p(x)+1}}\varphi(x,t)\frac{1}{\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|}\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,t)t^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega).

Since, for every
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\left|{V\cdot\omega}\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)=|V|^{p(x)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|{\omega\cdot\mathbf{e}}\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega),\quad\mathbf{e}\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

taken
V=\nabla u(x)
, equation (
2.6
) becomes

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|{\omega\cdot\mathbf{e}}\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{-(p(x)+1)}\varphi(x,t)\,dt

\displaystyle=|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\gamma_{n,p(x)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,t)t^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt=|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)},

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where we used hypothesis (
1.2
) on
\varphi
. Integrating the last equation with respect to
x
over the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Keeping into account what we have done in the first part of the proof, we are able to apply the dominated convergence theorem, so that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx

and the assertion follows.

We are now ready to discuss the case in which
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded and smooth domain.

Let
D\Subset\Omega
and fix
t>0
small enough such that

B(x,t)=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|<t\right\}\Subset\Omega,\quad\forall x\in D.

For every
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
, we have

\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\geq\int_{D}\int_{B(x,t)}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,|u(x)-u(y)|\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy\,dx.

By using polar coordinates and – more precisely – making the change of variables
x-y=\delta h\omega
, for
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, the previously equation becomes

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t/\delta}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\delta^{p(x)}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\penalty\delta\right)}{\left(\delta h\right)^{n+p(x)}}\,\delta^{n}h^{n-1}dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t/\delta}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\penalty\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

By employing Fatou’s lemma, remembering equations (
2.5
) and (
2.7
), it follows

\displaystyle\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t/\delta}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\penalty\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{D}\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{t/\delta}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\penalty\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx=\int_{D}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

so, from the arbitrariness of
D\Subset\Omega
, we have

(2.8)

\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\geq\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Now, we want to prove that

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx,\quad\forall u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\Omega\right).

Let
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\Omega\right)
. Taken a bounded subset
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing the domain
\Omega
, there exists an extension
\tilde{u}\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
of
u
, defined over the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that

i.

\tilde{u}(x)=u(x)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
;

ii.

\tilde{u}
is compactly supported in
V
, that is
\operatorname{supt}(\tilde{u})\subset V
;

iii.

there exist constants
C_{\pm}>0
, depending on
p^{\pm}
,
\Omega
and
V
, such that

\|\tilde{u}\|_{W^{1,p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq C_{+}\|u\|_{W^{1,p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)},\qquad\|\tilde{u}\|_{W^{1,p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq C_{-}\|u\|_{W^{1,p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)}

and

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq C_{+}\|u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)},\qquad\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq C_{-}\|u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)}.

Notice that it is possible to find an extension over the entire space
W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
because, in the classical extension theorem, the approximation sequences involved do not depend on the exponent
p
of the Sobolev space considered, but only the constant
C
– satisfying the norms inequality – depends on it (See
[
11
, Part II, Chapter 5]
).

Once we have extended
u
through
\tilde{u}
, we have

\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\leq\int_{\Omega}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy,

so, by making the superior limit as
\delta\to 0^{+}
– being now in the
\mathbb{R}^{n}
case –, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}dx\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,|\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{u}(y)|\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\tilde{u}(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Remembering equation (
2.8
),

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq\liminf_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\leq\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx,

so that the limit exists and is

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx,

as we wanted to prove.

Finally, let’s show that

\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{p^{+}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{p^{-}}\right).

Up to replacing
u(x)
with
u(x)-\int_{\Omega}u(\xi)\,d\xi
,
we may assume that
\int_{\Omega}u(x)\,dx=0
.
Then, since
\Omega
is smooth – as shown in
[
5
, Chapter 9]
– there is an extension
\tilde{u}\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\tilde{u}(x)|^{p^{+}}\,dx\leq C_{p^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p^{+}}\,dx\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\tilde{u}(x)|^{p^{-}}\,dx\leq C_{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p^{-}}\,dx.

Being

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}\left(u,\Omega\right)\leq\Lambda_{\delta}\left(\tilde{u},\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\left(\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{p^{+}}+\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{p^{-}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\left(C_{p^{-}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{p^{+}}+C_{p^{+}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)}^{p^{-}}\right),

the assertion follows.
∎

We assume now that

(2.9)

\varphi(x,\cdot)\text{ is a non-decreasing function},

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<+\infty
and let
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)\cap C^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
. Suppose that
\varphi
satisfies the following properties

(2.10)

\exists\alpha,a>0:\quad\alpha t^{{p^{+}}+1}\leq\varphi(x,t)\leq at^{p(x)+1},\quad\forall t\in[0,1],\,\,\text{for a.e.}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.11)

\exists\beta,b>0:\quad\beta\leq\varphi(x,t)\leq b,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\,\,\text{for a.e.}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and also hypothesis (
1.2
) and (
2.9
). Then

\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\geq\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

In particular,

whenever
\sup_{\delta>0}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)<+\infty
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to treat the case

F:=\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)<+\infty,

otherwise the inequality is obvious.
First of all, let us suppose further that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and let

(2.12)

A:=2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)}.

Taken
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
and fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1/\penalty 2)
, we have

(2.13)

T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0}):=\int_{0}^{\delta_{0}}\varepsilon\delta^{\varepsilon-1}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\,d\delta=\int_{0}^{\delta_{0}}\varepsilon\delta^{\varepsilon-1}\,d\delta\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}\varphi\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\delta\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy.

By making the change of variables
t=\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\penalty\delta
, we get

\displaystyle T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\int_{+\infty}^{\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|}{\delta_{0}}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{\varepsilon-1}}{t^{\varepsilon-1}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)}}{t^{p(x)}}\left(-\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|}{t^{2}}\right)\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dt\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\int_{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\delta_{0}}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,t)t^{-1-p(x)-\varepsilon}\,dt.

It follows that

(2.14)

\displaystyle T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\int_{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\delta_{0}}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,t)t^{-1-p(x)-\varepsilon}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|<\delta_{0}^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\int_{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\delta_{0}}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,t)t^{-1-p(x)-\varepsilon}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|<\delta_{0}^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

where, using (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we introduced
c_{0}>0
(depending only on
p
and
\beta
)
through the following inequalities (recall that

|u(x)-u(y)|/\delta_{0}<\delta_{0}
on the integration set)

\displaystyle\int_{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\delta_{0}}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,t)t^{-1-p(x)-\varepsilon}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\delta_{0}}^{1}\alpha t^{1+p^{+}}t^{-1-p(x)-\varepsilon}\,dt+\int_{1}^{+\infty}\beta t^{-1-p(x)-\varepsilon}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\int_{\delta_{0}}^{1}t^{p^{+}-p^{-}-\varepsilon}\,dt+\beta\int_{1}^{+\infty}t^{-1-p^{+}-\varepsilon}\,dt\geq\frac{\beta}{2p^{+}}=:c_{0}

Now equation (
2.14
) can be written as

T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})\geq c_{0}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy-c_{0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\geq{\delta_{0}}^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon A^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy,

remembering that we posed
A:=2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)}
.
Let
\tau>0
and
\delta_{0}
small enough such that

(2.15)

c_{0}\geq(1-\tau)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\omega\cdot\mathbf{e}\right|^{p^{+}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\right)^{-1}

and

(2.16)

\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\leq F+\tau,\qquad\forall\delta\in\left(0,\delta_{0}\right).

We have

(2.17)

\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\geq\gamma}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty,\qquad\forall\gamma>0.

In fact, let’s fix
t_{0}>0
such that
\inf_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,t_{0}\right)>0
and observe that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\geq\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\geq\gamma}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\right)}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\delta^{p^{+}}\inf_{x\in\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\gamma/\delta\right)\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\geq\gamma}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy.

Taken

and applying equation (
2.16
), we get

F+\tau\geq\delta^{p^{+}}\inf_{x\in\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\gamma/\delta\right)\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\geq\gamma}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy,

so (
2.17
) follows since

. Thanks to this equation, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}c_{0}

\displaystyle\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\geq\delta_{0}^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon A^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\varepsilon c_{0}\max\left\{A^{p^{+}+\varepsilon},A^{p^{-}+\varepsilon}\right\}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\geq\delta_{0}^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy=0,

so that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}

\displaystyle c_{0}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}c_{0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\geq{\delta_{0}}^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon A^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}c_{0}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy.

Remembering – as shown in
[

and – being

for a.e.

(2.19)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\,\omega\cdot\mathbf{e}\,\right|^{p^{+}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy.

We are now ready to conclude. By combining (
2.18
), (
2.15
) and (

) , we get

(2.20)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}c_{0}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geq(1-\tau)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\omega\cdot\mathbf{e}\right|^{p^{+}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\right)^{-1}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geq(1-\tau)\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

From the definition of
T\left(\varepsilon,\delta_{0}\right)
stated in (

T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0}):=\int_{0}^{\delta_{0}}\varepsilon\delta^{\varepsilon-1}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\,d\delta\leq\int_{0}^{\delta_{0}}\varepsilon\delta^{\varepsilon-1}(F+\tau)\,d\delta=(F+\tau)\delta_{0}^{\varepsilon}

so it follows that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})\leq F+\tau.

Thanks to equations (
2.20
) and (
2.21
), we have

\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)+\tau\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}T(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})\geq(1-\tau)\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

so for the arbitrariness of

, we finally get

\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)\geq\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx

for all

bounded.

If instead

Theorem 2.3

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq M}dx\int_{0}^{+\infty}dh\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,\left|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)\right|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega).

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\left|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)\right|}{\delta}=\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\gamma_{n,p(x)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi(x,t)t^{-(p(x)+1)}\,dt=|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}

where we have used again that, for

and the normalization condition on
\varphi
.
As a consequence, we have

\int_{|x|\leq M}dx\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)=\int_{|x|\leq M}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Setting
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left[0,+\infty\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)=\begin{cases}at^{p(x)+1},&\ \mathrm{for}\ 0\leq t\leq 1\\
b,&\ \mathrm{for}\ t>1\end{cases},

then
\tilde{\varphi}(x,\cdot)
is a non-decreasing function for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\varphi(x,t)\leq\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)\qquad t\geq 0,\,\,\text{for a.e.}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and

(2.26)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(t)t^{-(p(x)+1)}dt<+\infty.

Since
u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\left|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)\right|/\delta\leq Ch\quad\forall(x,h,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left[0,+\infty\right)\times{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}

for some constant
C\geq 0
. Then

\varphi\left(x,|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)|/\delta\right)/h^{p(x)+1}

is dominated by
\tilde{\varphi}(x,Ch)/h^{p(x)+1}
, which is summable as

\int_{|x|\leq M}dx\int_{0}^{+\infty}dh\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,Ch\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)<+\infty.

We are now able to apply the dominated convergence theorem, getting

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{\delta}(u)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{|x|\leq M}dx\int_{0}^{+\infty}dh\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,\left|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)\right|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq M}dx\int_{0}^{+\infty}dh\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,\left|{\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega}\right|h\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq M}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}dx.

The proof of (
2.22
) is very similar, as a consequence of equations (
2.24
), (
2.25
) and Fatou’s lemma.

Let’s now suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth and bounded domain and let
u\in C^{1}\left(\bar{\Omega}\right)
. If we take
D\Subset\Omega
and fix
t>0
small enough such that

B(x,t)=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|y-x\right|<t\right\}\Subset\Omega,\quad\forall x\in D,

than we have

\Lambda_{\delta}(u)=A_{\delta}+B_{\delta}+C_{\delta},

where

\displaystyle A_{\delta}:=\int_{D}\int_{B(x,t)}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy\,dx,

\displaystyle B_{\delta}:=\int_{D}\int_{\Omega\setminus B(x,t)}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy\,dx,

\displaystyle C_{\delta}:=\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy\,dx.

By the change of variables
x-y=\delta h\omega
and dominated convergence theorem, we get

(2.27)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}A_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{D}\int_{B(x,t)}\frac{\varphi_{\delta}\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{t/\delta}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,\left|u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)\right|/\delta\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dh\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\frac{\varphi\left(x,\left|\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega\right|h\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dh\,dx=\int_{D}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Now we claim that

(2.28)

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}B_{\delta}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{\delta}\leq C\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(\Omega\setminus D\right).

In fact, for all
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\delta^{p(x)}\,dx\int_{\Omega\setminus B(x,t)}\frac{\varphi\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\penalty\delta\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{D}\delta^{p(x)}\,dx\int_{\Omega\setminus B(x,t)}\frac{b}{t^{n+p(x)}}\,dy\ \leq\frac{\delta}{\min\left\{t^{n+1},t^{n+p^{+}}\right\}}b\left(\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(\Omega\right)\right)^{2}.

Secondly, since

\displaystyle C_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\delta^{p(x)}\,dx\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\penalty\delta\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\delta^{p(x)}\,dx\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|/\penalty\delta\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\delta^{p(x)}\,dx\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,L\left|x-y\right|/\delta\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dy,\qquad\forall\delta\in(0,1),

where
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
u
over
\Omega
,
by making the change of variables
z=L\left(y-x\right)/\penalty\delta
and using the definition of
\tilde{\varphi}
, we have

\displaystyle C_{\delta}\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\delta^{p(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{L}{\delta}\right)^{p(x)}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,\left|z\right|\right)}{\left|z\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\delta^{p(x)}\,dx\int_{\Omega\cap\left\{|z|\leq 1\right\}}\left(\frac{L}{\delta}\right)^{p(x)}\frac{a}{\left|z\right|^{n-1}}\,dz

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\delta^{p(x)}\,dx\int_{\Omega\cap\left\{|z|>1\right\}}\left(\frac{L}{\delta}\right)^{p(x)}\frac{b}{\left|z\right|^{n+p(x)}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus D}L^{p(x)}\,dx\left(a\int_{\left\{|z|\leq 1\right\}}\frac{1}{\left|z\right|^{n-1}}\,dz+b\int_{\left\{|z|>1\right\}}\frac{1}{\left|z\right|^{n+1}}\,dz\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(\Omega\setminus D\right),

where
C
depends on
L
,
a
,
b
and
n
. Being

\left|\Lambda_{\delta}(u)-\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\right|\leq\left|A_{\delta}(u)-\int_{D}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\right|+B_{\delta}+C_{\delta}+\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

by using (
2.27
) and (
2.28
), we get

\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\left|\Lambda_{\delta}(u)-\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\right|\leq C\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(\Omega\setminus D\right)+\int_{\Omega\setminus D}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

By the arbitrariness of
D\Subset\Omega
the assertion follows.

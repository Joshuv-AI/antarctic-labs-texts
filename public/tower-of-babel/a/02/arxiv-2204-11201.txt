A SLOW BLOW UP SOLUTION FOR THE FOUR DIMENSIONAL energy critical SEMI LINEAR HEAT EQUATION
Tongtong Li, Liming Sun, Shumao Wang

Abstract
. We consider the energy critical four dimensional semi-linear heat equation ∂ t v − Δ ​ v − v 3 = 0 , ( t , x ) ∈ ℝ × ℝ 4 . \partial_{t}v-\Delta v-v^{3}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}. Formal computation of Filippas et al. (R. Soc. Lond. Proc. 2000) conjectures the existence of a sequence of type II blow-up solutions with various blow-up rates ∥ v ( t ) ∥ L ∞ ​ ( ℝ 4 ) ≈ | log ⁡ ( T − t ) | 2 ​ L 2 ​ L − 1 ( T − t ) L , L = 1 , 2 , ⋯ . \|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\approx\frac{|\log(T-t)|^{\frac{2L}{2L-1}}}{(T-t)^{L}},\hskip 10.00002ptL=1,2,\cdots. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setting of the problem

Consider the following semi-linear heat equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v-\Delta v=|v|^{p-1}v,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
v\mid_{t=t_{0}}=v_{0}.\end{cases}

Because of the simplicity of the nonlinearity, problem (
1.1
) has been widely considered as a popular model for testing the methods designed to analysis the behavior of solutions near singularity formation.
It has been extensively studied in the literature, for example
[
9
,
10
,
15
,
24
,
3
]
. It is well-known that for a large class of initial data (for instance, bounded and continuous) there is a unique maximal classical solution
v(t,x)
for
t\in(0,T)
. If
T
is finite, then
u
will blow up at time
t=T
. There are two types of blow-ups depending on the rate

\begin{cases}\limsup_{t\rightarrow T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}||v(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<+\infty&\text{type I},\\
\limsup_{t\rightarrow T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}||v(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=+\infty&\text{type II}.\end{cases}

In this article, we will focus on the radial type II blow-up solution in energy critical case, that is
p=p_{S}:=\frac{d+2}{d-2}
. In this
situation, the total dissipated energy

(1.2)

E(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{p+1}dx

is left invariant by the scaling symmetry of the problem

v(t,x)\mapsto\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}v\left(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x\right).

The blow-up of (
1.1
) is almost completely understood in the sub-critical range
1<p<p_{S}
, for instance, by
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
25
,
29
]
. The solution always blows up in type I in this range. The existence of type II blow-up has been established in various settings, for instance by
[
15
,
14
,
24
]
when
p>p_{JL}
, where

(1.3)

\displaystyle p_{{JL}}=\begin{cases}\infty&\text{ if }d\leq 10,\\
1+\frac{4}{d-4-2\sqrt{d-1}}&\text{ if }d\geq 11.\end{cases}

On the other hand, when
p_{S}<p<p_{JL}
,
Matano and Merle 2004
excludes the occurrence of a type II blow-up for radial solutions. Therefore, in dimension
3\leq d\leq 10
, the choice
p=p_{S}
is the only one for which a type II blow-up occurs for radial data.

Recently, there are active researches in the energy critical case
p=p_{S}
. In the pioneering work by
Filippas et al. 2000
, they find that
u
can exhibit type II blow-up in finite time in lower dimensions in the energy critical case
p=p_{S}
. They formally obtain sign changing type II blow-up solutions using the matched asymptotic expansion technique.
Also, they give a sequence of blow-up speeds (corrected by
Harada 2020b
)

(1.4)

\displaystyle||v(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\begin{cases}(T-t)^{-L}&d=3,\\
\frac{|\log(T-t)|^{\frac{2L}{L-1}}}{(T-t)^{L}}&d=4,\\
(T-t)^{-3L}&d=5,\\
{(T-t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}|\log(T-t)|^{-\frac{15}{4}}}&d=6,\end{cases}

where
L\geq 1
is an integer. Recently, there is a surge of interest in constructing such type II blow-up solutions as predicted by
[
8
]
.

Schweyer 2012
first rigorously constructs a radial blow-up
solution in the case
d=4
and
L=1
. He uses a strategy developed
in the study of geometrical dispersive problems by
Merle and Raphaël 2005
,
Merle and Raphaël 2006
,
Merle et al. 2011
and
Raphaël and Rodnianski 2012
. The nature of his approach is energy estimates and making no use of the maximum principle. Meanwhile, del Pino, Wei and their collaborators develop an inner-outer gluing method and lead to a series of works on construction. They apply their methods to construct type II blow-up solutions in several cases. To be more precise,
del Pino et al. 2020
constructs solutions of
d=3
and all
L\geq 1
.
del Pino et al. 2019
establishes the existence of solutions of
d=5
and
L=1
. Later on,
Harada 2020a
completes the construction for
d=5
and all
L\geq 2
. Using this inter-outer gluing method,
Harada 2020b
also shows the existence of type II blow-up solution with the specific rate for
d=6
in (
1.4
).

One may wonder what will happen for
d\geq 7
.
Collot et al. 2017

proves no existence of type II blow-up solution in
d\geq 7
cases near the ground state
solitary wave
and gives a complete classification of its asymptotic behavior. Recently,
Wang and Wei 2021
precludes the type II blow-up for all
positive
solutions in
d\geq 7
.

After all the works mentioned above, it seems that the cases
d=4
and
L\geq 2
are still unsettled. The goal of this paper is to fill the gap of these remaining cases.

1.2.
Statement of the result

We consider the energy critical semi-linear heat equation in dimension
d=4

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v-\Delta v-v^{3}=0,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4},\\
v\mid_{t=t_{0}}=v_{0}.\end{cases}

In this article, we construct a radial type II blow-up solution based
on the Talenti-Aubin soliton

(1.6)

Q(r)=\frac{1}{1+\frac{r^{2}}{8}},\hskip 10.00002pt\Delta Q+Q^{3}=0.

Our result is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\alpha^{*}>0
, there exists
C^{\infty}

radial initial data
v_{0}
with

(1.7)

E(Q)<E(v_{0})<E(Q)+\alpha^{*}

such that the solution to (
1.5
) blows up in finite time
T=T(v_{0})<\infty

in a type II regime: there exists
v^{*}\in\dot{H}^{1}
such that

(1.8)

v(t,x)-\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}Q\left(\frac{x}{\lambda(t)}\right)\rightarrow v^{*}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in}\ \dot{H}^{1}\ \text{as}\ t\to T,

and for some
c(v_{0})>0
,

(1.9)

\lambda(t)=c(v_{0})(1+o_{t\to T}(1))\frac{(T-t)^{2}}{|\log(T-t)|^{\frac{4}{3}}}.

Remark 1.2
.

(i) Our method relies on the approach to construct slow blow-up dynamics
for the corotational energy-critical harmonic heat flow of
Raphaël and Schweyer 2015
. People have observed a deep connection between dimension four
energy critical semi-linear heat equation and two-dimensional harmonic map flows, and interested
readers can check
[
27
,
4
]
and references therein. Our result verifies the existence of the blow-up speed corresponding to
L=2
as conjectured
in
[
8
]

because
v
will blow up in the speed of the reciprocal of
\lambda
.

(ii) The construction to the case of
L=2
is much more complicate than the one of
L=1
. First, the approximate solution in the case of
L=1
needs to be sharpened here in order to get (
1.9
). This requires a better approximation to the blow-up solution to further reduce the errors which are produced in the case of
L=1
. See Step 1 in subsection
1.4
for more details. Second, we need to deal with two different “unstable directions”
in our setting. One is from the Schrödinger operator
H
in (
2.4
), and the
other one is from modulation parameter
b=(b_{1},b_{2})
in (
1.22
).
In this problem, we can deal with them at the same time. See Step 3 in subsection
1.4
.
For the case of
L>2
, these two types of difficulty persist and are all the essential ones. Actually, one can introduce an appropriate class of functions to continue the approximate process (see a similar argument in
[
27
]
). This will also produce more unstable directions but can be handled similarly as here. Since the proof of
L>2
is just a tautology of the idea here, we will not present it in this paper.

(iii) The method we rely on is a powerful tool. It has been applied to construct finite time blow-up solutions in Schrödinger map
[
21
]
, focusing energy supercritical Schrödinger equation
[
22
]
, defocusing energy supercritical Schrödinger equation in
[
23
]
, energy supercritical wave equation in
[
2
]
, nonradial energy supercritical heat equation in
[
1
]
.

1.3.
Notations

We introduce the differential operator

\Lambda f:=f+y\cdotp\nabla f\hskip 10.00002pt\text{(energy critical scaling)}.

Given a positive number
b_{1}>0
, we let

B_{0}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{1}}},\ B_{1}:=\frac{|\log b_{1}|}{\sqrt{b_{1}}}.

Given a parameter
\lambda>0
, we let

u_{\lambda}(r):=\frac{1}{\lambda}u(y)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\ y=\frac{r}{\lambda}.

We let
\chi
be a smooth non-increasing cutoff function with

\chi(y)=\begin{cases}1&\text{for}\ y\leq 1,\\
0&\text{for}\ y\geq 2,\end{cases}

and use the notation

\int f:=\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(r)r^{3}dr.

1.4.
Strategy of the proof

Now we sketch the main points in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Step1: Approximate solution
Q_{b}
.
We first reparametrize (
1.5
)
by

(1.10)

v(t,r)=\frac{1}{\lambda}u(s,y),\hskip 10.00002pty=\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(t)}

which leads to

(1.11)

\partial_{s}u+b_{1}\Lambda u-\Delta u-u^{3}=0,\ b_{1}=-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}.

We look for blow-up solution
u
close to
Q
in
\dot{H}^{1}

topology. In this case,
b_{1}
remains small and the flow is controlled by the linearized Hamiltonian

(1.12)

H=-\Delta-V=-\Delta-3Q^{2}

which has a resonance by the scaling symmetry

(1.13)

H\Lambda Q=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\Lambda Q\sim-\frac{8}{y^{2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002pty\to+\infty.

Besides, we need
T_{1},T_{2}
given by

(1.14)

HT_{2}=-T_{1},\ HT_{1}=-\Lambda Q

with asymptotics

(1.15)

T_{1}\sim-4\log y+2,\ T_{2}\sim y^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\log y-\frac{5}{8}\right)

as
y
tends to infinity.

The linearization of the flow implies a possible approximate solution

(1.16)

Q+b_{1}T_{1}+b_{2}T_{2}

with
a priori
bound
|b_{2}|\lesssim b_{1}^{2}
.
Indeed, the
O(b_{1})
error vanishes

(1.17)

b_{1}(\Lambda Q+HT_{1})=0

by the definition of
T_{1}
.
At
O(b_{1}^{2})
level, the leading term is

(1.18)

(b_{1})_{s}T_{1}+b_{1}^{2}\Lambda T_{1}-b_{2}T_{1},

which can, since
\Lambda T_{1}\sim T_{1}
, be canceled by setting

(1.19)

(b_{1})_{s}+b_{1}^{2}-b_{2}=0.

Similar calculation at
O(b_{1}^{3})
level suggests that we take

(1.20)

(b_{2})_{s}+3b_{1}b_{2}=0.

Certainly some other terms
S_{j}(b,y)
are needed to further reduce the
error, and we construct the approximate solution as

(1.21)

Q_{b}(y)=Q(y)+b_{1}T_{1}+b_{2}T_{2}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4}=Q(y)+\alpha(b,y).

which generates a small error
\Psi_{b}
. More importantly, from
flux computation, some
\log b_{1}
term should be introduced to the
dynamical system for
b=(b_{1},b_{2})
. That is, we should take

(1.22)

\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}=0,\\
(b_{1})_{s}+b_{1}^{2}(1+c_{b_{1}})-b_{2}=0,&\text{with}\quad c_{b_{1}}\sim\frac{2}{|\log b_{1}|}\\
(b_{2})_{s}+b_{1}b_{2}(3+c_{b_{1}})=0.\end{cases}

Indeed, this ODE system has a solution with
\lambda\to 0^{+}
at
finite time
T=T(v_{0})<+\infty
and

(1.23)

\lambda\sim c_{1}(v_{0})\frac{(\log s)^{\frac{4}{9}}}{s^{\frac{2}{3}}},\ b_{1}\sim b_{1}^{e}=\frac{2}{3s}-\frac{4}{9s\log s},\ b_{2}\sim b_{2}^{e}=-\frac{2}{9s^{2}}+\frac{20}{27s^{2}\log s}.

The problem is that this solution might be unstable. In fact, we define
U
by

(1.24)

b_{k}=b_{k}^{e}+\frac{U_{k}}{s^{k}(\log s)^{\frac{5}{4}}},\hskip 10.00002ptU=\begin{bmatrix}U_{1}\\
U_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Then the dynamical system for
b
above implies

(1.25)

s\frac{dU}{ds}=AU+\text{error terms,}

which is, after diagonalization, equivalent to

(1.26)

s\frac{dV}{ds}=D_{A}V+\text{error terms},\hskip 10.00002ptD_{A}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&\\
&\frac{2}{3}\end{bmatrix},\ V=PU

where the first unstable direction
V_{2}
occurs corresponding to the positive eigenvalue
\frac{2}{3}
of
A
. And it must be controlled
a priori
to avoid disrupting the dynamic for
b
. For technical reasons we modify it to
\tilde{V}_{2}
, see Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
4.3
.

Step 2: Decomposition of the flow.
To get (
1.22
), we decompose
u
as

(1.27)

u=Q_{b}+\varepsilon

for some small
\varepsilon(s,y)
subject to the orthogonal
conditions

(1.28)

(\varepsilon,H^{k}\Phi_{M})=0\ \hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for}\ 0\leq k\leq 2.

where
(\ ,\ )
denotes
L^{2}
inner product and
\Phi_{M}
is a compactly supported substitute for
\Lambda Q
(since
\Lambda Q\notin L^{2}
).
Then Implicit Function Theorem ensures the existence and uniqueness
of the decomposition as long as
\|\varepsilon\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
remains small.
Moreover, it turns out that (
1.28
) is enough to derive
the expected modulation equations

(1.29)

\left|\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right|+|(b_{1})_{s}+b_{1}^{2}(1+c_{b_{1}})-b_{2}|+|(b_{2})_{s}+b_{1}b_{2}(3+c_{b_{1}})|\lesssim\|\varepsilon\|_{loc}+\frac{b_{1}^{3}}{|\log b_{1}|}

for some local-in-space norm
\|\varepsilon\|_{loc}
.

So we need to bound
\|\varepsilon\|_{loc}
. Thanks
to the Hardy type bounds below ensured by (
1.28
)

(1.30)

\varXi_{2k}:=\int|H^{k}\varepsilon|^{2}\gtrsim\int\frac{|\varepsilon|^{2}}{(1+y^{4k})(1+|\log y|^{2})},\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq k\leq 3,

we turn to bound
\varXi_{2k}
. Here comes the second unstable direction, i.e. the projection

(1.31)

\tau(t)=(\varepsilon,\psi).

Here
\psi
corresponds to the only non-positive spectrum of
H

(1.32)

H\psi=-\varsigma\psi,\ \varsigma>0.

If uncontrolled, its growth would destroy any bound of
\varXi_{2k}
.
Assuming suitable
a priori
bound on
\tau
, we can derive some Lyapounov monotonicity

(1.33)

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\varXi_{6}}{\lambda^{10}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{12}}\frac{b_{1}^{6}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}}

which leads to the estimate

(1.34)

\varXi_{6}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{6}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}}.

This together with similar bounds on
\varXi_{4}
and
\varXi_{2}

serves to control
\|\varepsilon\|_{loc}
. The required smallness of
\|\varepsilon\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

is ensured by the dissipation of energy and the sub-coercivity of
H
.

Step 3: Control of the two unstable directions.
With the above analysis, we see the core of the proof is to control
the unstable models
\tilde{V}_{2}
and
\tau

at the same time
. This
is possible since
\tilde{V}_{2}
comes from the development of modulation parameter
b
while
\tau
comes from
the projection of
\varepsilon
to the non-positive eigenvalue direction of
H
.
Hence they are (almost) independent of each other as long as both are reasonably small so as not to destroy the bounds for
b
and
\varepsilon
.

The method is to apply a Brouwer type argument. We set initial data
v_{0}
as

(1.35)

v_{0}=Q_{b(0)}+\varepsilon(0)=Q_{b(0)}+\tau(0)\tilde{\psi}

where
\tilde{\psi}
satisfies

(1.36)

(\tilde{\psi},\psi)=1,\hskip 10.00002pt(\tilde{\psi},H^{k}\Phi_{M})=0,\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq k\leq 2.

so that (
1.28
) and (
1.31
) are satisfied at
t=0
. It can be seen that
v_{0}
is uniquely determined by
U_{1}(0)
,
\tilde{V}_{2}(0)
and
\tau(0)
. We simply take
U_{1}(0)=0
and assume
a priori

(1.37)

\tilde{V}_{2}(t)\in[-1,1],\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{\tau}(t):=\tau(t)\cdotp\frac{\log b_{1}(t)}{b_{1}(t)^{3+\frac{1}{2}}}\in[-1,1].

If (
1.37
) fails at
t=\tilde{T}_{exit}(v_{0})<T(v_{0})

for any reasonable initial data
v_{0}
, we will have a map

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}=[-1,1]\times[-1,1]

\displaystyle\to\partial\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle(\tilde{V}_{2}(0),\tilde{\tau}(0))

\displaystyle\mapsto(\tilde{V}_{2}(\tilde{T}_{exit}),\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{T}_{exit})).

Moreover, the dynamics for
b
and
\varepsilon
yield, respectively,

(1.38)

|s(\tilde{V}_{2})_{s}-\frac{2}{3}\tilde{V}_{2}|\lesssim{(\log s)^{-\frac{1}{4}}},\ |\tau_{s}-\varsigma\tau|\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{4}}{|\log b_{1}|},

which ensure the strictly outgoing behavior

(1.39)

\frac{d}{ds}\tilde{V}_{2}^{2}(\tilde{T}_{exit})>0,\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{d}{ds}\tilde{\tau}^{2}(\tilde{T}_{exit})>0.

Hence classical PDE theory ensures that the map above is continuous
and leaves the boundary points fixed, which contradicts Brouwer fixed-point theorem. So we
conclude that some
v_{0}
exists such that (
1.37
) holds
for all
t<T(v_{0})
, hence deduce (
1.22
) and Theorem
1.1
.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we first construct the approximate self-similar solution
Q_{b}
, give the sharp estimates about the error term
\Psi_{b}
and supply a local version of
Q_{b}
. Then we derive the dynamical system of
b=(b_{1},b_{2})
in Section
2.3
, which corresponds to one of the unstable directions. In Section
3
, we first give a suitable decomposition of the solution
v(t,x)
and design the bootstrap regime. Then we derive the modulation equations in Section
3.2
. Finally, in the end of this section, we derive the fundamental monotonicity of the Sobolev-type norms
\varXi_{2},\varXi_{4}
and
\varXi_{6}
. In Section
4
, we first get improved control of
\varXi_{2},\varXi_{4}
and
\varXi_{6}
. Then by a standard Brouwer argument, we control the two unstable directions at the same time by suitably choosing the initial data, which is the heart of our analysis. The above analysis finishes the bootstrap regime, which easily implies the blow up statement of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
APPROXIMATE PROFILE

Introduce a parameter
\lambda=\lambda(t)>0
. Let
v(t,\cdotp)=u_{\lambda}(s,\cdotp)
,
or

(2.1)

v(t,r)=\frac{1}{\lambda}u(s,y),\hskip 10.00002pty=\frac{r}{\lambda}

where

(2.2)

s=s_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)},\hskip 10.00002pts_{0}>0.

then (
1.5
) becomes

(2.3)

\partial_{s}u-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda u-\Delta u-u^{3}=0.

In this section we construct an explicit approximate solution to (
2.3
)
close to
Q
. The construction relies on the spectral properties
of linearized Hamiltonian

(2.4)

H=-\Delta-V=-\Delta-3Q^{2}

which are well known and are summarized below:

(i)
H
has a unique negative eigenvalue

(2.5)

H\psi=-\varsigma\psi,\hskip 10.00002pt\varsigma>0

and
\psi
decays exponentially.

(ii)
H
has a resonance at the origin induced by the scaling symmetry

(2.6)

H\Lambda Q=0

with
\Lambda Q\notin L^{2}
, or more precisely

(2.7)

\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for}\ 0\leq i\leq 10,\hskip 10.00002pt\Lambda^{i}Q=\begin{cases}1+O(y^{2})&\text{as}\ y\to 0,\\
(-1)^{i}\frac{8}{y^{2}}+O(\frac{1}{y^{4}})&\text{as}\ y\to+\infty.\end{cases}

ODE theory provides another solution to
H\Gamma=0
for
y>0
:

(2.8)

\begin{split}\Gamma(y)&=-\Lambda Q(y)\int_{1}^{y}\frac{dx}{x^{3}[\Lambda Q(x)]^{2}}\\
&=\frac{y^{2}-8}{\left(y^{2}+8\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{y^{2}}{16}+6\log y-\frac{583}{112}-\frac{4}{y^{2}}\right)-\frac{64}{\left(y^{2}+8\right)^{2}}\end{split}

and the asymptotic behavior

(2.9)

\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for}\ 0\leq i\leq 10,\hskip 10.00002pt\Lambda^{i}\Gamma(y)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{i}\frac{2}{y^{2}}+O(|\log y|)&\text{as}\ y\to 0,\\
\frac{1}{16}+O(\frac{\log y}{y^{2}})&\text{as}\ y\to+\infty.\end{cases}

(iii) By (ii), the solution to
Hu=f
is given by

(2.10)

u=H^{-1}f=\Gamma(y)\int_{0}^{y}f(x)\Lambda Q(x)x^{3}dx-\Lambda Q(y)\int_{0}^{y}f(x)\Gamma(x)x^{3}dx

up to the addition of
\alpha Q(y)+\beta\Gamma(y)
. We restrict ourselves to the case where
f
is smooth and take
\alpha=\beta=0
,
which is equivalent to require
u
is smooth and
u(0)=0
.

Now we turn to the construction. In the following subsection, we assume

(2.11)

\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}=0,\\
(b_{1})_{s}+b_{1}^{2}(1+c_{b_{1}})-b_{2}=0,\\
(b_{2})_{s}+b_{1}b_{2}(3+c_{b_{1}})=0.\end{cases}

where
c_{b_{1}}
is defined by (
2.26
).

2.1.
Construction of the approximate blow-up profile

Proposition 2.1
.

(Construction of the approximate profile).
Let
M>0
be large enough. Then there exists a small enough universal
constant
b^{*}(M)>0
, such that the following holds true. Let there
be a
C^{1}
map

b=(b_{k})_{1\leq k\leq 2}:[s_{0},s_{1}]\longmapsto(-b^{*}(M),b^{*}(M))\times(-b^{*}(M),b^{*}(M))

with a priori bounds on
[s_{0},s_{1}]

(2.12)

0<b_{1}<b^{*}(M),\hskip 10.00002pt|b_{2}|\lesssim b_{1}^{2}.

Then there exist profiles
T_{1},T_{2},S_{2},S_{3}
and
S_{4}
,
such that

(2.13)

Q_{b}(y)=Q(y)+b_{1}T_{1}+b_{2}T_{2}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4}=Q(y)+\alpha(b,y)

generates an error

(2.14)

\Psi_{b}:=\partial_{s}Q_{b}-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda Q_{b}-\Delta Q_{b}-Q_{b}^{3}

(2.67)

\displaystyle\int|H^{3}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{8}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}},

(2.68)

\displaystyle\int\frac{1+|\log y|^{2}}{1+y^{12-2i}}|\partial_{y}^{i}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{8}|\log b_{1}|^{C}\ \hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for}\ 0\leq i\leq 4,

(2.69)

\displaystyle\int_{y\leq 2M}|H^{k}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{10}M^{C}\ \hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for}\ 0\leq k\leq 3.

Proof.

From localization we compute

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}_{b}=

\displaystyle\ \chi_{B_{1}}\Psi_{b}+b_{1}(1-\chi_{B_{1}})\Lambda Q+\alpha\partial_{s}\chi_{B_{1}}-\alpha\Delta\chi_{B_{1}}-2\partial_{y}\alpha\partial_{y}\chi_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle\ -[(Q+\chi_{B_{1}}\alpha)^{3}-Q^{3}]+\chi_{B_{1}}[(Q+\alpha)^{3}-Q^{3}].

We estimate the
H^{3}
level bound (
2.67
) term by term,
as follows.

(i) Start with
\chi_{B_{1}}\Psi_{b}
. From (
2.57
) we estimate
for
0\leq i\leq 6,\ B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}

|\partial_{y}^{i}\Psi_{b}|\lesssim b_{1}^{2}y^{-i}+b_{1}^{3}y^{2-i}+\frac{b_{1}^{4}y^{4-i}}{|\log b_{1}|}+b_{1}^{5}y^{4-i}|\log b_{1}|^{C}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{4}y^{4-i}}{|\log b_{1}|}.

Together with (
2.16
) we get

\int|H^{3}(\chi_{B_{1}}\Psi_{b})|^{2}\lesssim\int_{y\leq B_{1}}|H^{3}\Psi_{b}|^{2}+\frac{b_{1}^{8}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}}\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{1}{y^{4}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{8}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}}.

(ii) The second term is easily controlled by

\int\left|H^{3}[(1-\chi_{B_{1}})\Lambda Q]\right|^{2}\lesssim\int_{y\geq B_{1}}\frac{1}{y^{16}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{6}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}}.

(iii) For the third term
\alpha\partial_{s}\chi_{B_{1}}
, with
a priori
bound
|(b_{1})_{s}|\lesssim b_{1}^{2}
, we have

0\leq i\leq 10,\hskip 10.00002pt|\partial_{y}^{i}\partial_{s}\chi_{B_{1}}|=|(b_{1})_{s}\partial_{b_{1}}\partial_{y}^{i}\chi_{B_{1}}|\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{y^{i}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}},

and our calculations in the previous subsection imply

|\partial_{y}^{i}\alpha|\lesssim b_{1}^{2}y^{2-i}\log y\ \text{for}\ B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}.

So we have

\int|H^{3}(\alpha\partial_{s}\chi_{B_{1}})|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{4}\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{|\log y|^{2}}{y^{8}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{8}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}}.

(iv) The next two terms are bounded by

\int|H^{3}(\alpha\Delta\chi_{B_{1}}+2\partial_{y}\alpha\partial_{y}\chi_{B_{1}})|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{4}\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{|\log y|^{2}}{y^{8}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{8}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}}.

(iv) Finally, note that

-[(Q+\chi_{B_{1}}\alpha)^{3}-Q^{3}]+\chi_{B_{1}}[(Q+\alpha)^{3}-Q^{3}]=3(\chi_{B_{1}}-\chi_{B_{1}}^{2})Q\alpha^{2}+(\chi_{B_{1}}-\chi_{B_{1}}^{3})\alpha^{3},

and

\displaystyle\int\left|H^{3}[3(\chi_{B_{1}}-\chi_{B_{1}}^{2})Q\alpha^{2}+(\chi_{B_{1}}-\chi_{B_{1}}^{3})\alpha^{3}]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\left|\frac{1}{y^{6}}[b_{1}^{4}y^{2}(\log y)^{2}+b_{1}^{6}y^{6}(\log y)^{3}]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle b_{1}^{10}|\log b_{1}|^{C}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{8}}{|\log b_{1}|^{2}}.

This concludes the proof of (
2.67
). Proofs for the other three
are similar(and simpler).
∎

Proposition 2.3

Let

3.
THE TRAPPED REGIME

From now on, we assume that the initial data

In this section we describe our choice of initial data and design

Our main analysis is on the Lyapounov monotonicity in subsection 3.3.

3.1.
Modulation theory

We first try to decompose the solution as

Given

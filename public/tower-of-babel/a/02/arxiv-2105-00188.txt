ℓ ​ r \ell r -Multisemigroups and Modal Convolution Algebras
Uli Fahrenberg, Christian Johansen, Georg Struth, Krzysztof Ziemiański

Abstract
We show how modal quantales arise as convolution algebras Q X Q^{X} of functions from ℓ ​ r \ell r -multisemigroups X X , that is, multisemigroups with a source map ℓ \ell and a target map r r , into modal quantales Q Q , which can be seen as weight or value algebras. In the tradition of boolean algebras with operators we study modal correspondences between algebraic laws in X X , Q Q and Q X Q^{X} . The class of ℓ ​ r \ell r -multisemigroups introduced in this article generalises Schweizer and Sklar’s function systems and object-free categories to a setting isomorphic to algebras of ternary relations as used in boolean algebras with operators and in substructural logics. Our results provide a generic construction recipe for weighted modal quantales from such multisemigroups. This is illustrated by many examples, ranging from modal algebras of weighted relations as used in fuzzy mathematics, category quantales in the tradition of category algebras or group rings, incidence algebras over partial orders, discrete and continuous weighted path algebras, weighted languages of pomsets with interfaces, and weighted languages associated with presimplicial and precubical sets. We also discuss how these results can be combined with previous ones for concurrent quantales and generalised to a setting that supports reasoning with stochastic matrices or probabilistic predicate transformers. Keywords: multisemigroups, quantales, convolution algebras, (object-free) categories, modal algebras, quantitative program verification.

Multisemigroups

Categories can be axiomatised in object-free style
[
MacLane98
]
,
essentially as partial monoids with many units that satisfy a certain
locality condition. We have previously studied their correspondence
to relational monoids
[
CranchDS20
]
, which are sets
X
equipped
with ternary relations
R\subseteq X\times X\times X
with many
relational units that satisfy certain relational associativity and
unit laws. Here we use an isomorphic representation of
multioperations of type
X\times X\to\mathcal{P}X
(the isomorphism being
that between the category
\mathbf{Rel}
and the Kleisli category of
the powerset monad). A multioperation on
X
, like the corresponding
ternary relation on
X
, relates pairs in
X\times X
with a set of
elements of
X
, including the empty set. This encompasses partial and
total operations, where each such pair is related to at most one
element and to precisely one element, respectively. Multioperations
have a long history in mathematics, see
[
KudryavtsevaM15
]
for a
discussion, references and additional material.

Dealing with categories that are not small requires classes instead of
sets in some of our examples. In the tradition of object-free
categories, we then tacitly extend the following definitions to
classes, yet do not formally distinguish between sets and
classes—the simple constructions in this article do not lead to
paradoxes.

So let
\odot:X\times X\to\mathcal{P}X
be a multioperation on set
X
. We
write
D_{xy}
in place of
x\odot y\neq\emptyset
to indicate that
the composition
x\odot y
is non-empty, which intuitively means
defined, and extend
\odot
to an operation

\odot:\mathcal{P}X\times\mathcal{P}X\to\mathcal{P}X
defined, for all

A,B\subseteq X
, by

A\odot B=\bigcup\{x\odot y\mid x\in A\text{ and }y\in B\}.

We write
x\odot B
instead of
\{x\}\odot B
,
A\odot x
instead
of
A\odot\{x\}
and
f(A)
for
\{f(a)\mid a\in A\}
. Note that

A\odot\emptyset=\emptyset\odot B=\emptyset
.

A
multimagma

(X,\odot)
is simply a non-empty set
X
with a
multioperation
\odot:X\times X\to\mathcal{P}X
.

•

The multioperation
\odot
is

if

x\odot(y\odot z)=(x\odot y)\odot z
.

•

It is
local
if

u\in x\odot y\land D_{yz}\Rightarrow D_{uz}
for all

u,x,y,z\in X
.

•

It is a
partial operation
if
|x\odot y|\leq 1
and a

(total) operation
if
|x\odot y|=1
, for all
x,y\in X
.

•

An element

is a

\exists x.\ x\in e\odot x
and

\forall x,y.\ x\in e\odot y\Rightarrow x=y

right

in

\exists x.\ x\in x\odot e

\forall x,y.\ x\in y\odot e\Rightarrow x=y

.

Remark 2.1
.

Intuitively, locality states that if

and
y

and

can be composed with

reduces to the composition pattern of categories, binary relations,
paths in digraphs and many other examples, as explained below.
Locality has previously been called

coherence

[
CranchDS20
]
. It will become clear below why
we are now adopting a different name.

A multimagma
X
is

if for every
x\in X
there exist

e,e^{\prime}\in E
such that
D_{ex}
and
D_{xe^{\prime}}
. This definition of
units follows that for object-free categories. Equivalently, we may
require that there exists a set

such that, for all

E\odot x=\{x\}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad x\odot E=\{x\}.

A

is an associative multimagma; a

a unital multisemigroup.

These definitions imply that a multisemigroup
(X,\odot)
is a

partial semigroup
if
\odot

\odot
is a total operation—and likewise for
partial monoids and monoids.

Spelling out associativity yields

x\odot(y\odot z)=\bigcup\{x\odot v\mid v\in y\odot z\}=\bigcup\{u\odot z\mid u\in x\odot y\}=(x\odot y)\odot z.

Multimagmas and relatives form categories in several ways. A

multimagma morphism

f:X\to Y
satisfies

f(x\odot_{X}y)\subseteq f(x)\odot_{Y}f(y).

The morphism is
bounded
if, in addition,

f(x)\in u\odot_{Y}v\Rightarrow\exists y,z.\ x\in y\odot_{X}z\land u=f(y)\land v=f(z).

Obviously,
f
is a multimagma morphism if and only if

x\in y\odot_{X}z\Rightarrow f(x)\in f(y)\odot_{Y}f(z)
. This is a
natural generalisation of

x=y\odot_{X}z\Rightarrow f(x)=f(y)\odot_{Y}f(z)
, and hence of

f(x\odot_{X}y)=f(x)\odot_{Y}f(y)
, for total operations. For
partial operations, it implies that the right-hand side of the
inclusion must be defined whenever the left-hand side is, and, in the
bounded case, that the left-hand side is defined whenever the
right-hand side is.

A morphism

of unital multimagmas needs to preserve units as

f(e)\in E_{Y}

such that

holds if

More generally, bounded morphisms are standard in modal logic as
functional bisimulations. The isomorphisms between categories of
relational semigroups and monoids and those of multisemigroups and
multimonoids are explained in Appendix

In every multimagma, every unit

In every multimonoid, each element has precisely one left and one
right unit: if

3
\ell r
-Multisemigroups

The correspondence between units in relational monoids and
multimonoids and source/target functions motivates alternative
axiomatisations of these structures and thus of object-free categories
that generalise the function systems of Schweizer and
Sklar
[
SchweizerS67
]
. Such categories can be found in Chapter
XII.5 of Mac Lane’s book
[
MacLane98
]
. Domain axioms for ordered
semigroupoids, with a view on allegories, have already been considered
by Kahl
[
Kahl08
]
. We define multisemigroups with such
functions. An isomorphic alternative based on relational semigroups
has been outlined in
[
CranchDS20
]
.

An
\ell r
-multimagma
is a structure
(X,\odot,\ell,r)
such that

(X,\odot)
is a multimagma and the operations
\ell,r:X\to X

satisfy, for all
x,y\in X
,

D_{xy}\Rightarrow r(x)=\ell(y),\qquad\ell(x)\odot x=\{x\},\qquad x\odot r(x)=\{x\}.

Recall that
D_{xy}\Leftrightarrow x\odot y\neq\emptyset
.
Henceforth we often write
xy
instead of
x\odot y
and
AB
instead
of
A\odot B
.

An
\ell r
-multisemigroup
is an associative

\ell r
-multimagma. An
\ell r
-multimagma is
\ell r
-local

whenever
r(x)=\ell(y)\Rightarrow D_{xy}
and therefore

D_{xy}\Leftrightarrow r(x)=\ell(y)
. Locality in the sense of
multimonoids and
\ell r
-locality coincide in

\ell r
-multisemigroups; henceforth we simply speak about locality of

\ell r
-multisemigroups.

Duality
for
\ell r
-multimagmas arises by interchanging
\ell

and
r
as well as the arguments of
\odot
. The classes of

\ell r
-multimagmas and semigroups, hence also

\ell r
-multisemigroups, are closed under this transformation;
locality and partiality are self-dual. Hence the dual of any property
that holds in any of these classes, obtained by making these
replacements, holds as well. This generalises opposition in
categories.

Lemma 3.1
.

In any
\ell r
-multimagma,

1.

the
compatibility laws

\ell\circ r=r
,
r\circ\ell=\ell
and
retraction laws

\ell\circ\ell=\ell
,
r\circ r=r
hold,

2.

the
idempotency law

\ell(x)\ell(x)=\{\ell(x)\}
holds,

3.

the
commutativity law

r(x)\ell(y)=\ell(y)r(x)
holds,

4.

the
export laws

\ell(\ell(x)y)=\ell(x)\ell(y)
and

r(xr(y))=r(x)r(y)
hold,

5.

the
weak twisted laws

\ell(xy)x\subseteq x\ell(y)
and
xr(yx)\subseteq r(y)x
hold.

All proofs have been checked with Isabelle.

\displaystyle s(\textit{id}(X))=X,\qquad t(\textit{id}(X))=X,\qquad s(f;g)=s(f),\qquad t(f;g)=t(g),

\displaystyle f;(g;h)=(f;g);h,\qquad\textit{id}(s(f));f=f,\qquad f;\textit{id}(t(f))=f.

In a category,

and

4
Convolution Quantales

We have already extended the multioperation

\odot:X\times X\to\mathcal{P}X
to type
\mathcal{P}X\times\mathcal{P}X\to\mathcal{P}X

defining

A\odot B=\bigcup\{x\odot y\mid x\in A\text{ and }y\in B\}
and
the maps
\ell,r:X\to X
to type
\mathcal{P}X\to\mathcal{P}X
by taking
images. We wish to explore the algebraic structure of such powerset
liftings over
\ell r
-multimagmas and their relatives. It is known
that powerset liftings of relational monoids yield unital
quantales
[
Rosenthal97
,
DongolHS21
]
. Yet the precise lifting of
source and target operations remains to be explored. This requires
some preparation.

A
quantale

[
Rosenthal90
]

(Q,\leq,\cdot,1)
is a complete
lattice
(Q,\leq)
equipped with a monoidal composition
\cdot
with
unit
1
that preserves all sups in its first and second argument.

We write
\bigvee
for the sup and
\bigwedge
for the inf operator, and

\vee
and
\wedge
for their binary variants. We also write

\bot=\bigwedge Q=\bigvee\emptyset
for the least and

\top=\bigvee Q=\bigwedge\emptyset
for the greatest element of
Q
. In the
literature, quantales are often defined without
1
(those with
1

are then called
unital
), but we have no use for these. We write

Q_{1}=\{\alpha\in Q\mid\alpha\leq 1\}
for the set of

subidentities
of quantale
Q
.

A quantale is
boolean
if its lattice reduct is a complete
boolean algebra—a complete lattice and a boolean algebra.

We write
-
for boolean complementation. In a boolean quantale,
Q_{1}

forms a complete boolean subalgebra with complementation

\lambda x.\ x^{\prime}=\lambda x.\ 1-x
, and in which composition
coincides with meet
[
FahrenbergJSZ20
]
.

Our examples in Section
9
require weaker notions of
quantale. A
prequantale
is a quantale in which the
associativity law is absent
[
Rosenthal90
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\odot,\ell,r)
be an
\ell r
-multisemigroup. Then

(\mathcal{P}X,\subseteq,\odot,E)
forms a boolean quantale in
which the complete boolean algebra is atomic.

Proof.

If
(X,\odot,\ell,r)
is an
\ell r
-multisemigroup, then
(X,\odot,E)
is a
multimonoid by Proposition
3.10
, hence
isomorphic to a relational monoid
and its powerset algebra a quantale
[
Rosenthal97
]
(see
also
[
DongolHS21
]
). The complete lattice on
\mathcal{P}X
is
trivially boolean atomic.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
X
is an
\ell r
-multimagma instead of a

\ell r
-multisemigroup, then
\mathcal{P}X
forms a prequantale instead
of a quantale
[
DongolHS21
,
CranchDS20a
]
. This weaker result is
needed in Section
6
.

Remark 4.3
.

Dualities between
n+1
-ary relational structures and boolean algebras
with
n
-ary (modal) operators have been studied by Jónsson and
Tarski
[
JonssonT51
]

substructural logics such as the Lambek calculus

we study ternary relations—as binary multioperations—and the

Example 4.4

(Powerset Quantales over

\ell r
-Semigroups)

While the lifting works for arbitrary
\ell r
-multisemigroups, we

Let

The following laws help calculating with convolutions. First of all,

as its unit. It is important for defining generalised metric
spaces and t-norms.

3.

The unit interval
([0,1],\leq,\cdot,1)
forms a quantale with

\bigvee
as supremum. It is isomorphic to the Lawvere quantale via the
function
(\lambda x\cdot-\ln x)
as its inverse, and important in
probability applications.

4.

The structures
([0,1],\leq,\min,1)
and
([0,1],\geq,\max,0)
and
their variants with
[0,1]
replaced by
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\infty}
(and
unit
\infty
for the first) form other quantales over the unit
interval, which are at the basis of
max-plus algebra

[
HeidergottOW06
]
and similar structures.∎

All these examples are distributive, but not boolean quantales, and
they justify our approach to domain in non-boolean cases below.

5
Modal Quantales

The results of Section
4
do not lift the source and
target structure of
\ell r
-multisemigroups to
Q^{X}
faithfully: all
relational units are mapped to the unit in
Q
by
\textit{id}_{E}

rather bluntly. A more fine-grained approach, in which different
elements of
E
are mapped to different elements in the powerset
quantale or different values or weights in
Q
, is possible.

Example 5.1

(Relational domain and codomain)
.

In the relation quantale, the standard relational domain and
codomain operations are

\textit{dom}(R)=\{(a,a)\mid\exists b.\ (a,b)\in R\}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\textit{cod}(R)=\{(b,b)\mid\exists a.\ (a,b)\in R\}.

Thus
\textit{dom}(R)=\{\ell(x)\mid x\in R\}=\ell(R)
and

\textit{cod}(R)=\{r(x)\mid x\in R\}=r(R)
.∎

We can capture this more abstractly. Formally, a
domain
quantale

[
FahrenbergJSZ20
]
is a quantale
(Q,\leq,\cdot,1)

equipped with a domain operation
\textit{dom}:Q\to Q
that satisfies, for all

\alpha,\beta\in Q
,

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\leq\textit{dom}(\alpha)\cdot\alpha,

\displaystyle\textit{dom}\,(\alpha\cdot\textit{dom}(\beta))

\displaystyle=\textit{dom}(\alpha\cdot\beta),

\displaystyle\textit{dom}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq 1,

\displaystyle\textit{dom}(\bot)

\displaystyle=\bot,

\displaystyle\textit{dom}(\alpha\vee\beta)

\displaystyle=\textit{dom}(\alpha)\vee\textit{dom}(\beta).

We refer to the domain axioms as
absorption
,
locality
,

subidentity
,
strictness
and
(binary) sup
preservation
, respectively. Absorption can be strengthened to an
identity:
\textit{dom}(\alpha)\alpha=\alpha
.

The domain axioms are precisely those of domain
semirings
[
DesharnaisS11
]
; domain quantales are thus quantales
that are also domain semirings with addition as binary sup.
Properties of domain semirings are therefore inherited, for instance

•

the export law
\textit{dom}(\textit{dom}(\alpha)\beta)=\textit{dom}(\alpha)\textit{dom}(\beta)
;

•

order preservation
\alpha\leq\beta\Rightarrow\textit{dom}(\alpha)\leq\textit{dom}(\beta)
;

•

the weak twisted law:
\alpha\textit{dom}(\beta)\leq\textit{dom}(\alpha\beta)\alpha
;

•

least left absorption (lla):
\textit{dom}(\alpha)\leq\rho\Leftrightarrow\alpha\leq\rho\alpha
; and

•

the adjunction
\textit{dom}(\alpha)\leq\rho\Leftrightarrow\alpha\leq\rho\top
.

In the last two laws,

\rho\in Q_{\textit{dom}}=\{\alpha\mid\textit{dom}(\alpha)=\alpha\}
(they need not
hold for all
\rho\in Q_{1}\supseteq Q_{\textit{dom}}
).

Domain axioms for arbitrary sups are
unnecessary
[
FahrenbergJSZ20
]
. In every domain quantale,
dom

preserves arbitrary sups,
\textit{dom}\left(\bigvee A\right)=\bigvee\textit{dom}(A)
,
hence in particular
\textit{dom}(\top)=1
, and distributes weakly with
infs,
\textit{dom}\left(\bigwedge A\right)\leq\bigwedge\textit{dom}(A)
. Domain elements
also left-distribute over non-empty infs,

\textit{dom}(\alpha)\cdot\bigwedge A=\bigwedge(\textit{dom}(\alpha)A)
for all
A\neq\emptyset
.

Much of the structure of the domain algebra induced by
dom
is
inherited from domain semirings as well. It holds that

Q_{\textit{dom}}=\textit{dom}(Q)
. The
domain algebra

(Q_{\textit{dom}},\leq,\cdot,1)

is therefore a subquantale of
Q
that forms a bounded distributive
lattice with
\cdot
as binary inf. It contains the largest boolean
subalgebra of
Q
bounded by
\bot
and
1

[
DesharnaisS11
]
. The
elements of
Q_{\textit{dom}}
are called
domain elements
of
Q
. Yet, in
the quantalic case, the lattice
Q_{\textit{dom}}
is
complete
[
FahrenbergJSZ20
]
:
\textit{dom}(\bigvee\textit{dom}(A))=\bigvee\textit{dom}(A)

follows from sup-preservation of
dom
. All sups of domain elements
are therefore again domain elements, but sups and infs in
Q_{\textit{dom}}

need not coincide with those in
Q
.

Quantales are closed under opposition: interchanging the order of
composition in quantale
Q
yields a quantale
Q^{\textit{op}}
; properties of
quantales translate under this duality. The opposite of the domain
operation on a domain quantale is of course a codomain operation.

A
codomain quantale
is the opposite of a domain quantale, just
like a codomain semiring is the opposite of a domain semiring.
Codomain quantales can be axiomatised using a codomain operation

\textit{cod}:Q\to Q
that satisfies the dual domain axioms, making

(Q^{\textit{op}},\textit{cod})
a domain quantale.

A
modal quantale
is a domain and codomain quantale

(Q,\leq,\cdot,1,\textit{dom},\textit{cod})
that satisfies the following

compatibility
axioms, which make the domain and codomain
algebras
Q_{\textit{dom}}
and
Q_{\textit{cod}}
coincide:

\textit{dom}\circ\textit{cod}=\textit{cod}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\textit{cod}\circ\textit{dom}=\textit{dom}.

Remark 5.2
.

In modal semirings,
dom
and
cod
are usually modelled
indirectly through their boolean complements in the subalgebras of
subidentities, that is, by antidomain and antirange operations.
This allows expressing boolean complementation in
Q_{\textit{dom}}
, which
then becomes the largest complete boolean subalgebra of
Q
bounded
by
\bot
and
1

[
DesharnaisS11
]
. As we intend to lift from

\ell r
-multisemigroups, where complements of source and target
operations may not exist, we do not follow this approach.

In a boolean quantale
Q
, the subalgebra
Q_{1}
of subidentities forms
a complete boolean algebra with quantalic composition as binary inf,
hence in particular
Q_{\textit{dom}}=Q_{1}
. One can then axiomatise domain by
the adjunction

\textit{dom}(\alpha)\leq\rho\Leftrightarrow\alpha\leq\rho\top
, for all

\rho\in Q_{1}
, and weak locality

\textit{dom}(\alpha\beta)\leq\textit{dom}(\alpha\textit{dom}(\beta))

[
FahrenbergJSZ20
]
.
Dual results hold for codomain. Using the adjunction alone yields only

predomain
and
precodomain

operations
[
DesharnaisMS06
]
. Finally, in a boolean quantale,
antidomain and anticodomain operations can be defined as

\textit{adom}=(\lambda x.\ x^{\prime})\circ\textit{dom}
and its dual. The axioms of
antidomain and anticodomain semirings
[
DesharnaisS11
]
can then be
derived
[
FahrenbergJSZ20
]
.

Some of the
\ell r
-magmas,
\ell r
-semigroups and
\ell r
-monoids
in the examples in Section
9
fail to yield
associativity or locality laws when lifted. This requires a more
fine-grained view on modal quantales.

•

A
modal prequantale
is a prequantale in which the locality
axioms for
dom
and
cod
are replaced by the export axioms

\textit{dom}(\textit{dom}(\alpha)\beta))=\textit{dom}(\alpha)\textit{dom}(\beta)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\textit{cod}(\alpha\textit{cod}(\beta))=\textit{cod}(\alpha)\textit{cod}(\beta).

The algebra
Q_{\textit{dom}}=\textit{dom}(Q)=\textit{cod}(Q)=Q_{\textit{cod}}
still forms a complete
distributive lattice in this case. Yet neither the weak locality laws

\textit{dom}(\alpha\beta)\leq\textit{dom}(\alpha\textit{dom}(\beta)
or

\textit{dom}(\alpha\beta)\geq\textit{dom}(\alpha\textit{dom}(\beta)
nor their opposites
for
cod
are derivable in this setting.

•

A
weakly local modal quantale
is a modal quantale in
which the locality axioms for
dom
and
cod
have once again been
replaced by the export axioms above.

In the presence of associativity, the
weak locality
laws

\textit{dom}(\alpha\beta)\leq\textit{dom}(\alpha\textit{dom}(\beta))\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\textit{cod}(\alpha\beta)\leq\textit{cod}(\textit{cod}(\alpha)\beta)

are now derivable, but not the opposite inequalities; locality
therefore does not hold.

As mentioned before, this foliation of definitions is reflected by
lifting and modal correspondence properties in
Sections
6
,
7
and
8

and justified by mathematically meaningful examples in
Section
9
.

The definitions and properties mentioned have been verified with
Isabelle, they also transfer to dioids, but have not yet been
developed in greater detail for these.

6
Modal Powerset Quantales

We show how
\ell r
-multisemigroups can be lifted to powerset
algebras, using the lifted operations
\ell,r:\mathcal{P}X\to\mathcal{P}X

defined, for all
A\subseteq X
, by

\ell(A)=\{\ell(x)\mid x\in A\}
and

r(A)=\{r(x)\mid x\in A\}
.

Remark 6.1
.

To relate this with the lifting of the ternary relation
R
to a
binary modality
\odot
on the powerset quantale in the sense of
Jónsson and Tarski, consider

\mathcal{R}(\ell)=\{(x,y)\mid y=\ell(x)\}
, the graph of

\ell
, which is a functional binary relation, and similarly the
graph
\mathcal{R}(r)
of
r
. Then

\ell(A)=\{y\mid(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}(\ell)\text{ for some }x\in A\}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad r(A)=\{y\mid(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}(r)\text{ for some }x\in A\},

which shows that
\ell
and
r
are modal diamond operators on

\mathcal{P}X
. The following lemmas show that they indeed preserve binary
sups and are strict, and hence are operators on boolean algebras.

Our main aim is to verify the domain quantale axioms and then use
properties of domain and codomain to infer that local

\ell r
-multisemigroups can be lifted to boolean modal quantales at
powerset level. We develop this theorem step-by-step, starting from

\ell r
-multimagmas, to clarify correspondences.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
X
be an
\ell r
-multimagma. For
A,B\subseteq X
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}X
,

1.

the compatibility laws
\ell(r(A))=r(A)
and
r(\ell(A))=\ell(A)
hold,

2.

the absorption laws
\ell(A)\cdot A=A
and
A\cdot r(A)=A

hold,

3.

the sup-preservation laws
\ell\left(\bigcup\mathcal{A}\right)=\bigcup\{\ell(A)\mid A\in\mathcal{A}\}
and
r\left(\bigcup\mathcal{A}\right)=\bigcup\{r(A)\mid A\in\mathcal{A}\}
hold,

4.

the binary sup-preservation laws
\ell(A\cup B)=\ell(A)\cup\ell(B)
,

r(A\cup B)=r(A)\cup r(B)
and the zero laws

\ell(\emptyset)=\emptyset=r(\emptyset)
hold,

5.

the commutativity laws
f(A)g(B)=g(B)f(A)
hold for
f,g\in\{\ell,r\}
,

6.

the subidentity laws
\ell(A)\subseteq X_{\ell}
and
r(A)\subseteq X_{r}
hold,

7.

the export laws
\ell(\ell(A)\cdot B)=\ell(A)\ell(B)

and
r(A\cdot r(B))=r(A)r(B)
hold.

The proof has been verified with Isabelle and is subsumed by that of
Theorem
7.1
. Yet because powerset liftings are
important and the proof may be instructive, we present details in
Appendix
. Lemma
6.2
shows that the
domain axioms, except locality, can already be lifted from

\ell r
-multimagmas. The identities in (4) are subsumed by those in
(3) in the powerset quantale, but they are needed for lifting to modal
semirings; see Section
7
. That is why we are listing
them.

Lifting weak variants of locality for
\ell
and
r
requires

\ell r
-multisemigroups; lifting locality, in addition, requires
locality.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
X
be an
\ell r
-multisemigroup and
A,B\subseteq X
. Then

\ell(AB)\subseteq\ell(A\ell(B))\qquad\text{
and }\qquad r(AB)\subseteq r(r(A)B).

The converse inclusions hold if
X
is local.

The proofs have again been checked with Isabelle and can be found in
Appendix
. Weak locality holds in any weakly local modal
semiring and quantale (which satisfy export axioms, see
Section
5
).

The results of the previous two lemmas can be summarised as follows.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
X
be an
\ell r
-multimagma.

1.

Then
(\mathcal{P}X,\subseteq,\odot,E,\textit{dom},\textit{cod})
is a boolean modal
prequantale in which
\textit{dom}(A)=\ell(A)
,
\textit{cod}(A)=r(A)
, for
all
A\subseteq X
, and the complete boolean algebra is atomic.

2.

It is a weakly local modal quantale if
X
is an
\ell r
-multisemigroup.

3.

It is a modal quantale if
X
satisfies locality.

Proof.

We have derived the respective variants of modal prequantale and
quantale axioms in Lemmas
6.2
and

6.3
for
\ell r
-magmas,
\ell r
-semigroups
and local
\ell r
-multisemigroups. They hold in addition to the
boolean prequantale and quantale axioms lifted via
Proposition
4.1
and the remark following it.
∎

This shows the particular role of weak locality and locality in the
three stages of lifting. The construction shown is one direction of
the well known Jónsson-Tarski duality between relational structures
and boolean algebras with operators
[
JonssonT51
]
, which
generalises to categories of relational structures and boolean
algebras with operators
[
Goldblatt89
]
.
Theorem
6.4
is an instance of this duality. Like in
modal logic, there are correspondences between relational structures
and boolean algebras with operators. The identities lifted in
Lemma
6.2
and
6.3
are one

.

Example 6.5

(Modal Powerset Quantales over
\ell r
-Semigroups)
.

1.

Any category as a local partial
\ell r

As an instance, in the modal powerset quantale over the pair

the domain and codomain elements are the relational domains and

.

\mathit{Id}_{X}

3.11
(

In this and the next example, locality at powerset-level

\ell r

In these examples, antidomain and anticodomain operations can be

7
Modal Convolution Quantales

In this section we prove one of the main theorems in this article, and
perhaps its most useful one. It refines Proposition
4.1
as
a powerset lifting. Here we aim at a similar refinement of
Theorem
4.5
as a lifting to functions valued in domain
quantales.

Once again we aim to expose the conditions on the algebras used in the
lifting, including modal prequantales and weakly local modal
quantales. Yet first we need to generalise the definition of domain
and codomain at the powerset level to make it suitable for convolution
quantales. For every
\ell r
-multimagma
X
, modal prequantale
Q

and functions
f,g:X\to Q
we define the operations
Dom
and
Cod

by

\textit{Dom}(f)=\bigvee_{x\in X}\textit{dom}(f(x))\cdot\delta_{\ell(x)}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\textit{Cod}(f)=\bigvee_{x\in X}\textit{cod}(f(x))\cdot\delta_{r(x)}.

These definitions imply that
\textit{Dom}(f)(x)=\textit{Cod}(f)(x)=\bot
for

x\notin E
. Restricted to functions
E\to Q
, they are equivalent to

\textit{Dom}(f)\circ\ell=\textit{dom}\circ f
and
\textit{Cod}(f)\circ r=\textit{cod}\circ f
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(X,\odot,\ell,r)
be an
\ell r
-multimagma and
Q
a modal
prequantale.

1.

Then
(Q^{X},\leq,\ast,\textit{id}_{E},\textit{Dom},\textit{Cod})
is a
modal prequantale.

2.

It is a weakly local modal quantale if
X
is an
\ell r
-multisemigroup and
Q
a weakly local modal quantale.

3.

It is a modal quantale if
X
is also local and
Q
a modal
quantale.

Proof.

Relative to Theorem
4.5
and the remark following it we
need to check the domain and codomain axioms as well as the
compatibility axioms. We show proofs up-to duality. We point out
where an
\ell r
-multimagma
X
together with a modal prequantale

Q
or an
\ell r
-multisemigroup together with a weakly local modal
quantale suffices for the lifting: this is the case in (1)-(5)
below. Here it is convenient to view
\alpha\cdot\delta_{x}
as an
element of the convolution algebra or an associated (imaginary)

Q
-module.

1.

First we show sup-preservation in
Q^{X}
, assuming the
corresponding law in
Q
:

\displaystyle\textit{Dom}\left(\bigvee F\right)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{x}\textit{dom}\left(\bigvee\{f(x)\mid f\in F\}\right)\cdot\delta_{\ell(x)}

\displaystyle=\bigvee\left\{\bigvee_{x}\textit{dom}(f(x))\cdot\delta_{\ell(x)}\mid f\in F\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigvee\{\textit{Dom}(f)\mid f\in F\}.

Preservation of binary sups and domain strictness,
\textit{Dom}(\lambda x.\ \bot)=\bot
, then follow.

2.

For the first compatibility axiom in
Q^{X}
, assuming the
corresponding laws in
X
and
Q
,

\displaystyle(\textit{Dom}\circ\textit{Cod})(f)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{x}\textit{dom}\left(\bigvee_{y}\textit{cod}\left(f(y)\right)\cdot\delta_{r(y)}(x)\right)\cdot\delta_{\ell(x)}

\displaystyle=\textit{dom}\left(\bigvee_{y}\textit{cod}(f(y))\right)\cdot\delta_{\ell(r(y))}

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{y}\textit{dom}(\textit{cod}(f(y)))\cdot\delta_{\ell(r(y))}

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{y}\textit{cod}(f(y))\cdot\delta_{r(y)}

\displaystyle=\textit{Cod}(f).

3.

For the domain subidentity axiom
in
Q^{X}
, assuming the corresponding law in
Q
,

\textit{Dom}(f)=\bigvee_{x}\textit{dom}(f(x))\cdot\delta_{\ell(x)}\leq\bigvee_{x\in E}1\cdot\delta_{x}=\textit{id}_{E}.

4.

For domain absorption in
Q^{X}
, assuming the corresponding laws
in
X
and
Q
,

\displaystyle\textit{Dom}(f)\ast f

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{y}f(y)\cdot\delta_{y}

\displaystyle=f,

\displaystyle\textit{Dom}(\textit{Dom}(f)\ast g)

Example 9.1

as objects and sequences

(v_{1},e_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n-1},e_{n-1},v_{n}):v_{1}\to v_{n}

vertices and edges alternate, as arrows. Composition

\pi_{1}\cdot\pi_{2}
of
\pi_{1}:v_{3}\to v_{4}
and
\pi_{2}:v_{1}\to v_{2}
is
defined whenever
v_{2}=v_{3}
, and it concatenates the two paths while
gluing the common end
v_{2}=v_{3}
. Sequences
(v)
of length
1
are
identities. Path categories become local partial
\ell r
-semigroups
if we introduce an edge
i_{v}
for every vertex
v
and functions

\ell(e)=i_{s(e)}
and
r(e)=i_{t(e)}
for every edge

in

is a modal quantale for any modal quantale

by a modal semiring.

2.

A special path category is generated by the one-point quiver
with

\ell r

then trivial. Lifting along Theorem

yields the
quantale or semiring of weighted languages.

3.

Forgetting edges represents paths as sequences of vertices. All

4.

Forgetting the internal structure of paths and keeping only
their ends, brings us back to the pair groupoid and weighted binary
relations.∎

In computing, paths arise as execution sequences of automata or
transition systems, and are sometimes called traces. Sets of traces

constructs modal quantales of traces at powerset level; lifting along
Theorem

the associated modal convolution quantales
of
Q

[

]
.

Without the modal structure, such convolution quantales have already

. When the underlying partial or
total orders are
locally finite
, that is, all segments and
intervals are finite (all arrows in the poset category are finitely
decomposable), values can be taken in (semi)rings and the
convolution algebras become the incidence algebras à la
Rota
[

]
. The general setting supports algebraic
generalisations of duration calculi
[

,
DongolHS21
]
; it
specialises to interval and interval temporal

,

]
for powerset liftings. The additional modal
structure yields richer algebras for reasoning about states as well

Our next example considers
\ell r
-multisemigroups that arise from
composing digraphs, yet we specialise to finite partial orders. We
show how locality can be obtained by introducing interfaces.

Example 9.3

(Weighted Poset Languages)
.

We restrict our attention to finite posets.

1.

Finite posets form partial
\ell r
-multisemigroups (based on
classes) with respect to serial composition, which is the disjoint
union of posets with all elements of the first poset preceding that
of the second one in the order of the composition. This yields
partial monoids with the empty poset as unit, and hence partial

\ell r
-semigroups in which
\ell
and
r
map every poset to the
empty poset. The algebra is therefore not local and does not form a
category. The convolution algebras are therefore weakly local
quantales, but the modal structure is trivial as
Dom
and
Cod

map the empty poset to
\bot
and any other element to

\textit{id}_{\{1\}}
. The powerset lifting yields poset languages.

2.

The points of finite posets can be labelled with letters from
some alphabet and equivalence classes of such labelled posets can be
taken with respect to isomorphism that preserves the order structure
and labels, but forgets the names of nodes. This leads to partial
words
[
Grabowski81
]
, which are also known as
pomsets
in
concurrency theory. The serial composition passes to a total
operation on equivalence classes, which makes the resulting

\ell r
-monoid a monoid and hence a category.

3.

Pomsets can be equipped with interfaces
[
Winkowski77
]
. The
source interface of a pomset consists of all its minimal elements
(with their labels); its target interface of all its maximal
elements (again with their labels). Pomsets with interfaces form a
partial
\ell r
-semigroup with
\ell
mapping every pomset to its
source interface,
r
mapping every poset to its target interface,
and composition defined by gluing pomsets on their interfaces
whenever they match, and extending the order as in the previous
example. The partial
\ell r
-semigroup of such pomsets with
interfaces is local and hence a category. Winkowski also defines a
parallel composition that turns pomsets with interfaces into a weak
monoidal category in which parallel composition is a partial
tensor. Details are beyond the scope of this article, but see
Section
10
for initial steps that extend modal
quantales towards concurrency. ∎

Winkowski’s pomsets with interfaces have recently been extended to
posets in which interfaces may be arbitrary subsets of the sets of minimal or
maximal elements of posets
[
FahrenbergJST20
]
. Our lifting results
extend to these. Compositions of digraphs with interfaces can also be
defined differently, simply by juxtaposing these objects and then
making interface nodes disappear in the composition. This yields again
categories, yet with different units given by identity
relations
[
Hotz65
,
FioreD13
]
. The usual liftings therefore apply.
In all these examples one considers an additional operation of
parallel composition, which is simply disjoint union of posets or
dags. For these, the powerset lifting yields standard models of
concurrency.

\ell r
-multimagma to a modal
prequantale.

(Weighted Path Languages in Topology)

Alternatively, categories of

(s_{p}(x),x,t_{q}(x))\in(s_{i}(y),y,t_{j}(y))\odot(s_{k}(z),z,t_{l}(z))\\

\displaystyle=(s_{i}(s_{i}(x)),s_{i}(x),t_{i}(s_{i}(x)))=(s_{i}(x),s_{i}(x),s_{i}(x)),

\displaystyle r(s_{i}(x),x,t_{j}(x))

An

Quantitative Modal Algebras

The domain and codomain yield modal diamond and box operators on the
convolution algebra, as usual for modal
semirings
[

]
. Many properties developed for such
semirings transfer automatically to quantales, yet additional
properties hold. Forward and backward modal diamond operators can be
defined in a modal quantale

\alpha,\beta\in Q

|\alpha\rangle\beta=\textit{dom}(\alpha\beta)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\langle\alpha|\beta=\textit{cod}(\beta\alpha).

The two operators are related by opposition and the following
conjugation, as for modal semirings
[
DesharnaisS11
]
.

Lemma 11.1
.

In every modal quantale
Q
, for all
\alpha\in Q
and
\rho,\sigma\in Q_{\textit{dom}}
,

\rho\cdot|\alpha\rangle\sigma=\bot\Leftrightarrow\langle\alpha|\rho\cdot\sigma=\bot.

Proof.

\rho|\alpha\rangle\sigma=\bot\Leftrightarrow\rho\textit{dom}(\alpha\sigma)=\bot\Leftrightarrow\rho\alpha\sigma=\bot\Leftrightarrow\textit{cod}(\rho\alpha)\sigma=\bot\Leftrightarrow\langle\alpha|\rho\cdot\sigma=\bot
.
∎

The laws

\textit{cod}(\alpha)\beta=\bot\Leftrightarrow\alpha\beta=\bot\Leftrightarrow\alpha\textit{dom}(\beta)=\bot
used in this proof hold in
any modal quantale or modal semiring because

\textit{dom}(\alpha)=\bot\Leftrightarrow\alpha=\bot\Leftrightarrow\textit{cod}(\alpha)=\bot
and by locality
[
DesharnaisS11
]
. We have
seen similar laws for
\ell r
-multisemigroups in
Lemma
3.9
in Section

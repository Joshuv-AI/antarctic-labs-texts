Time periodic solutions of first order mean field games from the perspective of Mather theory
Panrui Ni

Abstract
In this paper, the existence of non-trivial time periodic solutions of first order mean field games is proved. It is assumed that there is a non-trivial periodic orbit contained in the Mather set. The whole system is autonomous with a monotonic coupling term. Moreover, the large time convergence of solutions of first order mean field games to time periodic solutions is also considered. Keywords. mean field games; minimal measures; time periodic solutions

Introduction

The mean field games (MFGs) was introduced by Lasry and Lions in
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
and by Caines, Huang and Malhamé in
[
20
,
21
]
to analyze large population stochastic differential games. In this paper, we consider the evolutionary first order mean field games with a finite horizon
T>0
and an initial-final condition, which is a system consists of a Hamilton-Jacobi equation coupled with a continuity equation (cf.
[
24
, Eqs. (49)-(51)]
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)+H(x,Du(x,t))=F(m(t))+c,\quad(x,t)\in M\times(0,T).

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}m(x,t)+\textrm{div}(\partial_{p}H(x,Du(x,t))m(x,t))=0,\quad(x,t)\in M\times(0,T).

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=\varphi(x),\quad m(x,T)=m_{T}(x).

(1.3)

Here we assume
M
is a compact connected and smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. Let
D
stand for the spacial gradient with respect to
x\in M
, and let
d(x,y)
be the Riemannian distance between
x
and
y
in
M
. We denote by
TM
and
T^{*}M
the tangent and cotangent bundle over
M
respectively. The function
H:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is called the Hamiltonian. Let
\mathcal{P}(M)
be the space of all Borel probability measures on
M
. The function
m(x,t)
is the density of the mean field term
m(t)\in\mathcal{P}(M)
if it is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, i.e.
dm(t)=m(x,t)dx
. In previous works, the coupling term
F
is usually assumed to depend on the spacial variable
x\in M
, i.e.,
F=F(x,m)
is from
M\times\mathcal{P}(M)
to
\mathbb{R}
. In the present paper, we assume the coupling term
F:\mathcal{P}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
is a functional independent of
x\in M
, and
c\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. Although
F
does not depend on
x
, the results of this article can still handle the case where the coupling term is of the form
F(m)+f(x)
, because the Hamiltonian
H
depends on
x
.

The solution of the above system is defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

(Viscosity solution)
A continuous function
u:M\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a viscosity subsolution (resp. supersolution) of (
1.1
) if for each test function
\phi:M\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
, when
u-\phi
attains its local maximum (resp. minimum) at
(x,t)
, then

\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)+H(x,D\phi(x,t))\leq F(m(t))+c,\quad(\textrm{resp}.\ \geq F(m(t))+c).

A continuous function
u
is called a viscosity solution of (
1.1
) if it is both a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution.

Definition 1.2
.

A weak solution of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is a couple
(u(x,t),m(t))\in C(M\times[0,T])\times L^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{P}(M))
such that (
1.1
) is satisfied in viscosity sense and (
1.2
) is satisfied in distributions sense, i.e.,

\int_{M}\varphi(x,T)dm_{T}(x)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}(\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)+\langle\partial_{p}H(x,Du(x,t)),D\varphi(x,t)\rangle)dm(t)(x),

for each
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M\times(0,T])
. Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
represents the canonical pairing between the tangent space and cotangent space,
C^{\infty}_{c}(M\times(0,T])
stands for the set of all smooth functions on
M\times(0,T]
with compact support.

Now we recall some facts on the space of probability measures
\mathcal{P}(M)
. A sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{P}(M)
is
w^{*}
-convergent to
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
if

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{M}f(x)d\mu_{k}=\int_{M}f(x)d\mu,\quad\forall f\in C(M).

We shall work with the Monge-Wasserstein distance
d_{1}
, which is defined by

d_{1}(m_{1},m_{2})=\sup_{\phi}\int_{M}\phi d(m_{1}-m_{2}),\quad\forall m_{1},\ m_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(M),

where the supremum is taken over all 1-Lipschitz continuous functions on
M
. We recall that
d_{1}
metricizes the
w^{*}
-topology. Assume the coupling term
F:\mathcal{P}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(F)

Lipschitz continuity:
there is
C>0
such that
|F(m_{1})-F(m_{2})|\leq Cd_{1}(m_{1},m_{2})
.

Consider the stationary system corresponding to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
)

\displaystyle H(x,Du(x))=F(m)+c,\quad x\in M.

(1.4)

\displaystyle\textrm{div}(\partial_{p}H(x,Du(x))m(x))=0,\quad x\in M.

(1.5)

A natural goal of the study of MFGs is to prove the convergence of solutions of evolutionary MFGs (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) to equilibrium states (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) as the horizon
T\to+\infty
. There are many works on the long time average of solutions of evolutionary MFGs. Usually, we assume
F:M\times\mathcal{P}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the monotone condition

\int_{M}(F(x,m)-F(x,m^{\prime}))d(m-m^{\prime})\geq 0,\quad\forall m,\ m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(M).

(1.6)

Without the monotone condition, the solution of evolutionary MFGs may not converges to equilibrium states, see
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
19
]
. In this paper, we provide a case in which there are non-trivial time periodic solutions of first order MFGs. Since
F:\mathbb{P}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
is independent of
x\in M
, for all probability measures
m
and
m^{\prime}
, we have

\int_{M}(F(m)-F(m^{\prime}))d(m-m^{\prime})=(F(m)-F(m^{\prime}))\int_{M}d(m-m^{\prime})=0.

Then the coupling term
F:\mathcal{P}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
considered in the present paper satisfies the monotone condition (
1.6
). The results in this paper show that the large time behavior of MFGs is related to the minimal measures introduced by J. Mather in
[
26
]
. For the further study, we may consider the coupling term
F
depending on the spacial variable
x\in M
.

Before presenting the results obtained in this paper, we first recall some recent works on the large time behavior of mean field games. For the second order mean field games, the large time behavior is quite well understood. One can refer to
[

10

[

,

,
4
,

,

]
. In these works, the monotone condition (
1.6
) plays a center role. In
[
2

, the author proved the uniform convergence of
u
and the convergence of
m
in the distance
d_{1}
to stationary solutions when the Hamiltonian of the form
|p|^{2}/2
. For mean field games with non-monotone coupling term, there are also some works on the existence of time periodic solutions. For congestion problem,
[

]
discussed the traveling wave solution, which is the first example of time periodic solutions. For second order mean field games with the Hamiltonian of the form

[

[

, by discussing the minimal points of an energy functional, the authors found time periodic solutions of first order mean field games on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In
[
26
]
, in order to generalize the Aubry-Mather theory for twist maps, J. Mather introduced the concept of minimal measures, which are also called the Mather measures. Assume the Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
is
C^{2}
, and satisfies the Tonelli condition

(H1)

Strictly convexity:
the Hessian matrix
\partial^{2}_{pp}H(x,p)
is positively definite for all
(x,p)\in T^{*}M
.

(H2)

Superlinearity:

H(x,p)/|p|\to+\infty
as
|p|\to+\infty
for all
x\in M
.

Define the Legendre dual of
H
, which is called the Lagrangian as follows

L(x,\dot{x}):=\sup_{p\in T^{*}_{x}M}\{\langle\dot{x},p\rangle-H(x,p)\}.

Then
L:TM\to\mathbb{R}
is also of class
C^{2}
, and satisfies the Tonelli condition with respect to
\dot{x}
(cf.
[
6
, Appendix]
). For each
x,y\in M
, consider the action minimizing problem

h_{t}(x,y):=\min\int_{0}^{t}L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))ds,

(1.7)

where the minimum is taken among absolutely continuous curves
\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow M
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(t)=y
. Here
h_{t}
is called the minimal action. By the Tonelli Theorem, the minimum can be achieved, see
[
18
]
. The curves achieving the minimum satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}(x,\dot{x})\bigg)=\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}(x,\dot{x}).

Denote by
\Phi^{L}_{t}
the flow of the Euler-Lagrange equation. Denote by
\Phi^{H}_{t}
the dual flow of
\Phi^{L}_{t}
through the Legendre transformation, which is the flow of the Hamilton equation

\dot{x}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(x,p),\quad\dot{p}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(x,p).

(1.8)

In the following, we denote by
m
the measures on
M
and
\mu
the measures on
TM
. Consider the action minimizing problem

-c_{0}=\min_{\mu}\int_{TM}L(x,\dot{x})d\mu,

where
\mu
varies among all Borel probability measures on
TM
invariant under the action of the flow
\Phi^{L}_{t}
. The constant
c_{0}
is the unique constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
H(x,Du)=c
has solutions, and is called the critical value or the effective Hamiltonian, cf.
[
25
, Theorem 1]
. We call the measures achieve this minimum the minimal measures, and denote by
\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}
the set of all minimal measures. Define the set of all projected minimal measures as

\mathfrak{M}:=\{m\in\mathcal{P}(M):\ m=\pi_{\#}\mu,\ \mu\in\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}\},

where
\pi:TM\to M
is the canonical projection, and
\pi_{\#}
is the push-forward induced by
\pi
. The Mather set is defined as

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\overline{\bigcup_{\mu}\ \textrm{supp}(\mu)}\subset TM,

where
\textrm{supp}(\mu)
is the support of
\mu
,
\bar{A}
is the closure of
A\subset TM
, and the union is taken over all minimal measures
\mu\in\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}
. Let
\mathcal{M}=\pi(\tilde{\mathcal{M}})
be the projected Mather set. By
[
26
]
,
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is a Lipschitz graph over
\mathcal{M}
.

Now we show that Mather’s minimal measures give solutions of (
1.4
)-(
1.5
). Let
\mu\in\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}
. Since
\mu
is
\Phi^{L}_{t}
-invariant, by
[
26
]
, we have

\int_{TM}\langle\dot{x},d\varphi(x)\rangle d\mu=0,

By
[
18
]
, let
u_{0}
be a solution of

H(x,Du)=c_{0}.

(1.9)

Then
u_{0}
is differentiable on
\mathcal{M}
, cf.
[
18
, Theorem 5.2.2]
. The derivative of
u_{0}
is given by

Du_{0}(x)=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}(x,\dot{x}),\quad(x,\dot{x})\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}},

(1.10)

which is independent of
u_{0}
, and is Lipschitz continuous. Let
m_{0}=\pi_{\#}\mu\in\mathfrak{M}
be a projected minimal measure. Then

\int_{M}\bigg\langle\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(x,Du_{0}(x)),d\varphi(x)\bigg\rangle dm_{0}=\int_{\tilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\dot{x},d\varphi(x)\rangle d\mu=0,

which implies that
(u_{0},m_{0})
is a solution of (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) with
c=c_{0}-F(m_{0})
. Assume

(H3)

there is a non-trivial periodic orbit
\Gamma(t)=(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))
on
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
with period
\tau
.

Here we recall that,
[
28
]
proved that the minimal measure is unique for generic Lagrangian. It was conjectured by R. Mañé that, the unique minimal measure is supported on a fixed point or a periodic orbit.

Remark 1.1
.

For the one dimensional case
M=\mathbb{T}\simeq\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, we can give more description on (H3) from the perspective of the Mather’s theory. Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Mather’s
\alpha
-function on the cohomology (see
[
18
, Section 4.10]
). Then for each
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha(a)
is the unique constant

such that

is solvable. By Lemma

below, the Mather set consists of either fixed points or exactly one periodic orbit. Assume that there is a fixed point in the Mather set

. Then the rotation number of all minimal measures is equal to zero, Equivalently,
\alpha^{\prime}(a)=0
, one can see
[
26
]
for more details. If (H3) holds, then
\alpha^{\prime}(a)\neq 0
. Consider

H_{a}(x,p)=\frac{1}{2}(p+a)^{2}+V(x),

where

that attains its maximum at exactly two points

and
X
and such that
V(0)=V(X)=0
. Let
a_{0}:=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{-2V(x)}dx
. Then
\alpha(a)=0
for

and
\alpha(a)>0
for

(see
[

for details). Since

is convex (see

Remark 1.2
.

The main differences between the present work and
[
14
]
are

•

The method in the present paper is from the weak KAM theory and the Aubry-Mather theory instead of finding minimal points of an energy functional.

•

In
[
14
]
, the potential of the coupling term is a sort of “double-well” potential. Then the convexity of the potential does not hold, which implies that the coupling term is not monotone. In the present paper, as stated before, the monotone condition (

) holds.

•

The time periodic solutions given in the present paper are weak solutions defined in Definition
1.2
, which may have more regularity. In particular, for

, the time periodic solutions can be classical solutions of class

, see Items (1)(3) in Theorem

•

Item (4) in Theorem

considers the uniform convergence of
u
and the convergence of
m
in

as the horizon

.

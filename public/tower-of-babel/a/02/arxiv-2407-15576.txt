Curvature-dimension condition, rigidity theorems and entropy differential inequalities on Riemannian manifolds
Xiang-Dong Li

Abstract




Abstract
In this paper, we use the information-theoretic approach to study curvature-dimension condition, rigidity
theorems and entropy differential inequalities on Riemannian manifolds. We
prove the equivalence of the
{\rm CD}(K,m)
-condition
for
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in[n,\infty]
and some entropy differential inequalities along the geodesics on the Wasserstein space over a Riemannian manifold. The rigidity models of the entropy differential inequalities are the
K
-Einstein manifolds and the
(K,m)
-Einstein manifolds with Hessian solitons. Moreover, we prove the monotonicity and
rigidity theorem of the
W
-entropy along the geodesics on the Wasserstein space over Riemannian manifolds with CD
(0,m)
-condition. Finally, we compare our work with the synthetic geometry developed by Lott-Villani and Sturm.

Keywords
: curvature-dimension condition, entropy differential inequalities, rigidity theorems,
W
-entropy, Lott-Villani-Sturm’s synthetic geometry

On the other hand, the Shannon and Rényi entropies and the corresponding entropy powers have been extensively studied in the information theory. In his 1948 seminal paper
[
41
]

with probability distribution

\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\displaystyle H(X)=H(f)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx,

and the related entropy power by

\displaystyle N(X)=N(f)=e^{{2\over n}H(X)}.

In
[
41
]
, Shannon discovered the Entropy Power Inequality (EPI): Let
X
and
Y
be two independent random vectors with probability density functions

f
and
g
respectively on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\displaystyle N(X+Y)\geq N(X)+N(Y).

(1.4)

Equivalently

\displaystyle N(f*g)\geq N(f)+N(g),

where
f*g
denote the convolution of
f
and
g
, i.e., the probability density of the law of
X+Y
.
See Stam
[
42
]
and Blachman
[
4
]
for the complete proofs of the Shannon Entropy Power Inequality.
Moreover, it is known that the equality holds in
(\ref{NXY})
if and only if
X
and
Y
are normally distributed with proportional covariance matrices. See
[
41
,
42
,
4
,
11
]
.

In
[
10
]
, Costa proved the Entropy Power Concavity Inequality (EPCI) for the heat equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. More precisely,
let
u
be a positive solution to the heat equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\Delta u.

(1.5)

Then the Shannon entropy power

is concave in

, i.e.,

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}N(u(t))\leq 0.

(1.6)

Using an argument based on the Blachman-Stam inequality

The concavity property of the Shannon entropy power for the heat equation
(\ref{heat1})
has been extended to the Rényi entropy power by

40

More precisely, let

(1.7)

where

\displaystyle H_{p}(u)={1\over 1-p}\log\int_{M}u^{p}dx

be the

\displaystyle N_{p}(u)=\exp\left(\sigma H_{p}(u)\right),

[

In
[
28
]
, Songzi Li and the author of this paper extend the concavity of Shannon entropy power to complete Riemannian manifolds.
More precisely, the Shannon entropy power is concave along
the heat equation associated with the Witten Laplacian on complete Riemannian
manifolds with the Bakry-Emery curvature-dimension CD
(0,m)
-condition. In particular, the Shannon entropy power is concave along the heat equation

-condition, it is proved in

that the rigidity models of the Shannon entropy power are Einstein or quasi Einstein manifolds
with Hessian solitons.
Moreover, it is also proved in
[
28

that the Shannon entropy power is convex along the conjugate heat equation introduced by G. Perelman for Ricci flow and the rigidity models of the Shannon entropy power are the shrinking Ricci solitons. This gives a new understanding of Perelman’s mysterious
W
-entropy functional for the Ricci flow from the information theoretic point of view.

In
[
29

with Hessian solitons.

(\ref{heat1})
can be realized as the gradient flow of the Boltzmann entropy on the

Notation and main results

Let
(M,g)

be a complete Riemannian manifold,
V\in C^{2}(M)
and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}^{*}X=-e^{V}\nabla\cdot(e^{-V}X)=-\nabla\cdot X+\nabla V\cdot X.

For any

\nabla_{\mu}^{*}(f\nabla\phi)=-fL\phi-\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi\rangle.

For any

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle d\mu=-\int_{M}Luvd\mu=-\int_{M}uLvd\mu.

Thus,

is the infinitesimal generator of the Dirichlet form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,v)=\int_{M}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle d\mu,\ \ \ \ u,v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M).

By Itô’s theory, the Stratonovich SDE on

\displaystyle dX_{t}=\sqrt{2}U_{t}\circ dW_{t}-\nabla V(X_{t})dt,\ \ \ \ \ \nabla_{\circ dX_{t}}U_{t}=0,

Moreover, the transition probability density of

\displaystyle u(t)\leq u(0)\exp\left(-{H(t)+H_{m,K}(t)-H(0)-H_{m,K}(0)\over m}\right).

In particular, as
u(0)=H^{\prime}(t)-H_{m,K}^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle u(t)\leq 0.

Integrating along geodesic from
\rho(0)
to
\rho(1)
, the Shannon entropy power inequality
(\ref{EPIW})
implies that

\displaystyle N_{m}(\rho(t))\geq N_{m,K}(t).

(3.41)

From which we derive
(\ref{NmKk})
.

\square

4.2
The
W
-entropy and NIW formula for Rényi entropy

In this subsection,
we introduce the
W
-entropy and prove the
W
-entropy formula for the Rényi entropy along the geodesic flow on the Wasserstein space over complete
Riemannian manifolds.
Then we prove the NIW formula which indicates the relationship between the entropy power
N
, the Fisher information
I=H^{\prime}
and the
W
-entropy related to the Rényi entropy for the geodesic
flow on the Wasserstein space over
compact Riemannian manifolds.

When
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\rho_{m}(x,t)={e^{-{|x|^{2}\over 4t^{2}}}\over(2\pi t^{2})^{m/2}}
, and
\phi_{m}(x,t)={|x|^{2}\over 2t}
.
Then
(\rho_{m},\phi_{m})
is a special solution to the geodesic equation on
P_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m},dx)
. See
[
27
]
.
The Rényi entropyof
\rho_{m}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle H_{p}(\rho_{m}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 1-p}\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\rho_{m}(x,t)^{p}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 1-p}\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}{e^{-{p|x|^{2}\over 4t^{2}}}\over(4\pi t^{2})^{mp/2}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 1-p}\left(\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-{p|x|^{2}\over 4t^{2}}}dx-\log(4\pi t^{2})^{mp/2}\right)

Using the fact

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-{p|x|^{2}\over 4t^{2}}}dx=(4\pi t^{2}p^{-1})^{m/2},

we have

\displaystyle H_{p}(\rho_{m}(t))={n\over 2}\log(4\pi et^{2})+{m\over 2}{\log p\over p-1}-{m\over 2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 1}H_{p}(\rho_{m}(t))=H_{1}(\rho_{m}(t))={m\over 2}\log(4\pi et^{2}).

Proof of Theorem
2.3
. To simplify the notation, we write
H_{p}^{\prime}=H_{p}^{\prime}(\rho(t))={d\over dt}H_{p}(\rho(t))
, etc.
Note that

\displaystyle H_{p}^{\prime}={1\over 1-p}{U^{\prime}\over U}=\int_{M}L\phi d\gamma=I_{p},

and

\displaystyle H_{p}^{\prime\prime}={1\over 1-p}{U^{\prime\prime}U-U^{\prime 2}\over U^{2}}=-{U^{\prime\prime}\over\int_{M}p_{1}(\rho)d\mu}+(p-1)\left(\int_{M}L\phi d\gamma\right)^{2}.

Now

\displaystyle U^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left[\left(p-1+{1\over m}\right)|L\phi|^{2}+Ric_{m,n}(L)(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\right]p_{1}(\rho)d\mu

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\left({m-n\over mn}\left(L\phi+{m\over m-n}\nabla V\cdot\nabla\phi\right)^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{\Delta\phi\over n}g\right\|^{2}\right)p_{1}(\rho)d\mu.

Therefore

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime}_{p}+{2\over t}H^{\prime}_{p}-{m\over t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left[\left(p-1+{1\over m}\right)|L\phi|^{2}+Ric_{m,n}(L)(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\right]d\gamma

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left({m-n\over mn}\left(L\phi+{m\over m-n}\nabla V\cdot\nabla\phi\right)^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{\Delta\phi\over n}g\right\|^{2}\right)d\gamma

\displaystyle+(p-1)\left(\int_{M}L\phi d\gamma\right)^{2}+{2\over t}\int_{M}L\phi d\gamma-{m\over t^{2}}.

By

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{g\over t}\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over n}\left|\Delta\phi-{n\over t}\right|^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{\Delta\phi\over n}g\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}

(4.85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over n}\left|L\phi-{m\over t}+\left(\nabla V\cdot\nabla\phi+{m-n\over t}\right)\right|^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{\Delta\phi\over n}g\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over m}\left|L\phi-{m\over t}\right|^{2}-{1\over m-n}\left(\nabla V\cdot\nabla\phi+{m-n\over t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+{m-n\over mn}\left[L\phi+{m\over m-n}\nabla V\cdot\nabla\phi\right]^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{\Delta\phi\over n}g\right\|_{\rm HS}^{2},

we have

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime}_{p}+{2\over t}H^{\prime}_{p}-{m\over t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left[\left(p-1+{1\over m}\right)|L\phi|^{2}+Ric_{m,n}(L)(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\right]d\gamma

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{g\over t}\right\|^{2}d\gamma-{1\over m-n}\int_{M}\left(\nabla V\cdot\nabla\phi+{m-n\over t}\right)^{2}d\gamma

\displaystyle+{1\over m}\int_{M}\left|L\phi-{m\over t}\right|^{2}d\gamma+(p-1)\left(\int_{M}L\phi d\gamma\right)^{2}+{2\over t}\int_{M}L\phi d\gamma-{m\over t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(p-1)\left[\int_{M}|L\phi|^{2}d\gamma-\left(\int_{M}L\phi d\gamma\right)^{2}\right]-\int_{M}Ric_{m,n}(L)(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)d\gamma

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left(\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{g\over t}\right\|^{2}+{1\over m-n}\left(\nabla V\cdot\nabla\phi+{m-n\over t}\right)^{2}\right)d\gamma.

By the definition formula of
W_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle{1\over t}{d\over dt}W_{m,p}(\rho,t)=H^{\prime\prime}_{p}+{2\over t}H^{\prime}_{p}-{m\over t^{2}}.

This completes the proof of
(\ref{derivativeofW})
.
\square

Theorem 4.4

(NIW formula) Under the same notations as in Theorem
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle{m\over N_{m,p}(t)}{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}N_{m,p}(\rho(t))={1\over m}\left|I_{p}-{m\over t}\right|^{2}+{1\over t}{d\over dt}W_{m,p}(\rho,t).

(4.86)

In particular, when
Ric_{m,n}(L)\geq Kg
, we have

\displaystyle{d\over dt}W_{m,p}(\rho,t)\leq-t\int_{M}K|\nabla\phi|^{2}d\gamma-{t\over m}\left|I_{p}-{m\over t}\right|^{2}.

(4.87)

Proof
. Note that

\displaystyle N_{m,p}^{\prime\prime}={1\over m}N_{m,p}\left(H_{p}^{\prime\prime}+{1\over m}H_{p}^{\prime 2}\right),

and

\displaystyle H_{p}^{\prime\prime}+{1\over m}H_{p}^{\prime 2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{p}^{\prime\prime}+{2\over t}H_{p}^{\prime}-{m\over t^{2}}+{1\over m}H_{p}^{\prime 2}-{2\over t}H_{p}^{\prime}+{m\over t^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over t}{d\over dt}W_{m,p}(t)+{1\over m}\left|H_{p}^{\prime}-{m\over t}\right|^{2}.

This completes the proof of
(\ref{NIW-1})
. By Theorem
4.3
and
H_{p}^{\prime}=I_{p}
, we obtain
(\ref{NIW-1})
.
\square

4.3
Rigidity theorem of
W
-entropy for Rényi entropy

Note that, when
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
M=\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we have

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}H_{p}(\rho_{m}(t))+{2\over t}{d\over dt}H_{p}(\rho_{m}(t))-{m\over t^{2}}=0.

(4.88)

and

\displaystyle W_{m,p}(\rho,t)={d\over dt}(tH_{m,p}(\rho,t))=0.

(4.89)

Due to this observation and inspired by Theorem
3.5
, we have the following rigidity theorem of the
W
-entropy for the Rényi entropy along the Benamou-Brenier geodesic on the Wasserstein space over Riemannian manifold with CD
(0,m)
-condition.

Theorem 4.5

Let
(M,g,V)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry condition as in Theorem
4.1
, and let
(\rho(t),\phi(t))
be a smooth Benamou-Brenier geodesic on the Wasserstein space over Riemannian manifold satisfying growth condition as required in Theorem
4.1
. Suppose that

Ric_{m,n}(L)\geq 0
,
p\geq 1
, and
{d\over dt}W_{m,p}(\rho,t)=0
at some
t=\tau>0
. Then
M
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
m=n
,
V
is a constant and
(\rho,\phi)=(\rho_{m},\phi_{m})
.

Proof
. The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.5
given in
[
27
]
. For the completeness of the paper, we reproduce it as follows.

Under the condition
Ric_{m,n}(L)\geq 0
, and using the
W
-entropy formula
(\ref{derivativeofW})
, we see that

{d\over dt}W_{m,p}(\rho,t)=0
at some
t=\tau>0
if and only if

\displaystyle{\rm Var}_{\mu}(L\phi)=0,\ \ Ric_{m,n}(L)(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)=0,

and

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{g\over\tau}\right\|^{2}+{1\over m-n}\left(\nabla V\cdot\nabla\phi+{m-n\over\tau}\right)^{2}=0.

Equivalently,
\nabla^{2}\phi={g\over\tau}
,
Ric_{m,n}(L)(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)=0
and
\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla V+{m-n\over\tau}=0
. By the Taylor expansion along geodesics,
\phi(\cdot,\tau)
has a unique minimum point
x_{0}\in M
, and every
level set of
\phi
is compact subset of
M
. Thus,
\phi(\cdot,\tau)
is a strongly convex exhaustion function on
complete non-compact Riemannian manifold
M
. By Theorem 3 in
[
17
]
,
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Integrating
\nabla^{2}\phi(\cdot,\tau)={g\over\tau}
along the shortest geodesics between
x_{0}
and

x
on
M
shows that

\displaystyle 2\tau(\phi(x,\tau)-\phi(x_{0},\tau))=d^{2}(x_{0},x),\ \ \ \ \forall x\in M.

This yields

\displaystyle\Delta d^{2}(x_{0},x)=2n,\ \ \ \forall\ x\in M,

which implies that
(M,g)
is isometric to the Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},(\delta_{ij}))
. For this, see
[
30
]
and references therein.
By the generalized Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem (see Theorem 1.3, p. 565 in
[
15
]
), we can derive that
V
must be a constant and
m=n
.

Thus
\phi(\cdot,\tau)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
\nabla^{2}\phi(x,\tau)={\delta_{ij}\over\tau}
. This yields
\nabla\phi(x,\tau)={x\over\tau}
under the assumption
\nabla\phi(0,\tau)=0
, and
\phi(x,\tau)={\|x\|^{2}\over 2\tau}
up to an additional constant.
By the Hopf-Lax formula for the solution to the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
2.13
) with
\phi(x,\tau)={\|x\|^{2}\over 2\tau}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(x,t)=\inf\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{{\|y\|^{2}\over 2\tau}+{\|x-y\|^{2}\over 2(t-\tau)}\right\}={\|x\|^{2}\over 2t},\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ t>\tau,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By the uniqueness of the smooth solution to the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
2.13
), we see that
\phi(x,t)={\|x\|^{2}\over 2t}=\phi_{m}(x,t)
for all
t>0
.
Solving the transport equation (
2.12
) with the initial data
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\rho(x,t)=\delta_{0}(x)
, we have

\rho(x,t)={e^{-{\|x\|2\over 4t^{2}}}\over(4\pi t^{2})^{n/2}}=\rho_{m}(x,t)
for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

\square

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}S_{N}(\rho(t))\geq{1\over N}\int_{M}K|\nabla\phi|^{2}\rho^{1-1/N}d\mu.

(4.99)

We will see in Section 5 that
(\ref{SNK1a})
is the differential form of
(\ref{S(K, N)})
. Hence

(vi)
in Theorem
2.2
implies
(viii)
.

4.6
Limiting case
N\rightarrow\infty
and
p\rightarrow 1

Letting
N\rightarrow\infty
in
(\ref{SNK1a})
, and using

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}N[1+S_{N}(\rho)]=\int_{M}\rho\log\rho d\mu,

we can derive

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\int_{M}\rho d\rho d\mu\geq K\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}d\mu.

i.e.,

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}{\rm Ent}(\rho(t))\geq KW_{2}^{2}(\rho(0),\rho(1)),

That is to say, under CD

-condition, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Hess}~{\rm Ent}(\rho)\geq K.

5.1
Lott-Villani and Sturm

For
N<\infty
, Sturm
[
45

proved that in the Riemannian case the curvature-dimension CD
(0,N)
for

N\geq n={\rm dim}M
holds if and only if for all
N^{\prime}\geq N
the quantity
S_{N^{\prime}}(\cdot|m)
is convex
on the Wasserstein space
P_{2}(M,d)
. This is basically due to the fact that the Jacobian determinant
J_{t}={\rm det}dF_{t}
of any optimal transport map

F_{t}=\exp(-t\nabla\phi):M\rightarrow M
satisfies

\displaystyle{\partial^{2}\over\partial t^{2}}J_{t}^{1/N}\leq 0

(5.100)

if and only if
N\geq n={\rm dim}M
and
Ric\geq 0
. As pointed out in
[
45
]
,
(\ref{JN0})
is essentially equivalent to the Brunn-Minkowski inequality

\displaystyle m(A_{t})^{1/N^{\prime}}\geq(1-t)m(A_{0})^{1/N^{\prime}}+tm(A_{1})^{1/N^{\prime}}.

(5.101)

for any
N^{\prime}\geq N
, any
t\in[0,1]
and any pair of Borel sets
A_{0},A_{1}\subset M
, where
A_{t}
denotes the set of
points
\gamma_{t}
on the geodesics with endpoints
\gamma_{0}\in A_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}\in A_{1}
.

The curvature-dimension condition CD
(K,N)
for general
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N<\infty
is more involved. In this case, the inequality
(\ref{JN0})
is replaced by

\displaystyle{\partial^{2}\over\partial t^{2}}J_{t}^{1/N}(x)\leq-{K\over N}J_{t}^{1/N}(x)d^{2}(x,F_{1}(x)).

(5.102)

When
\mu=v
is the standard volume measure, Sturm
[
45
]
proved that
(\ref{JNK})
is equivalent to
(\ref{S(K, N)})
if and only if
N\geq n={\rm dim}
and
Ric\geq K
. In
[
45
]
, Sturm used
(\ref{S(K, N)})
to
introduce the definition of the CD
(K,N)
-condition on metric measure spaces.
Independent of Sturm
[
45
]
, Lott and Villani
[
33
]
introduced the curvature-dimension condition CD
(0,N)
on metric measure spaces in the same form as
(\ref{S(0, N)})
.

By Sturm
[
45
]
, assume for simplicity that for

m\otimes m
-a.e.
(x,y)\in M^{2}
there exists a unique
geodesic
t\rightarrow\gamma_{t}(x,y)
depending in a
measurable way on the endpoints
x
and
y
. Then CD
(K,N)

states that that for any pair of absolutely continuous probability
measures
\rho_{0}m
and
\rho_{1}m

on
M
there exists an optimal coupling
q
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{t}^{-1/N}(\gamma_{t}(x,y))\leq\tau_{K,N}^{(1-t)}(d(x,y))\rho_{0}^{-1/N}(x)+\tau_{K,N}^{(t)}(d(x,y))\rho_{1}^{-1/N}(y)

(5.103)

for all

and
q
-a.e.

, where

is the density of the push-forward of

under

Indeed,

. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(x)=\rho_{t}(F_{t}(x)){\rm det}(dF_{t}(x))=\rho_{t}(F_{t}(x))J_{t}.

Equivalently

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(F_{t}(x))=\rho_{0}(x)J_{t}^{-1}.

Hence

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\rho_{t}^{-1/N}(F_{t}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{-1/N}(x){d^{2}\over dt^{2}}J_{t}^{1/N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{Kd^{2}(x,F_{1}(x))\over N}\rho_{0}^{-1/N}(x)J_{t}^{1/N}.

That is to say

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\rho_{t}^{-1/N}(F_{t}(x))\leq{Kd^{2}(x,F_{1}(x))\over N}\rho_{t}^{-1/N}(F_{t}(x)).

(5.104)

Integrating in
t
,
(\ref{rho4})
implies
(\ref{rho1})
.

Moreover, one can show that
(\ref{rho4})
is equivalent to
(\ref{S(K, N)})
. Indeed, if

hold, then

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}S_{N}(\rho(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\int_{M}\rho_{t}^{-1/N}(F_{t}(x))\rho_{0}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\rho_{t}^{-1/N}(F_{t}(x))\rho_{0}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-{1\over N}\int_{M}K(x)d^{2}(x,F_{1}(x))\rho_{t}^{-1/N}(F_{t}(x))\rho_{0}(x)d\mu(x).

Hence

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}S_{N}(\rho(t))\leq-{1\over N}\int_{M}K(x)d^{2}(x,F_{1}(x))\rho_{t}^{1-1/N}(F_{t}(x))J_{t}(x)d\mu(x).

When
K
is a constant, we have

\displaystyle{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}S_{N}(\rho(t))\leq-{K\over N}\int_{M}d^{2}(x,F_{1}(x))\rho_{t}^{1-1/N}(F_{t}(x))J_{t}(x)d\mu(x).

(5.105)

On the other hand, if
(\ref{SNK1})
holds for all
\rho_{0}(x)

(\ref{rho4})
also holds.

Now we prove that

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)|^{2}\rho_{t}^{1-1/N}(x)d\mu(x)=\int_{M}d^{2}(x,F_{1}(x))\rho_{t}^{1-1/N}(F_{t}(x))J_{t}(x)d\mu(x).

Indeed

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi_{t}(x)|^{2}\rho_{t}^{1-1/N}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\rho_{t}^{1-1/N}(y)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi_{t}(F_{t}(x))|^{2}\rho_{t}^{1-1/N}(F_{t}(x)){\rm det}(dF_{t}(x))d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\phi_{t}(F_{t}(x))|^{2}\rho_{t}^{-1/N}(F_{t}(x))\rho_{0}(x)d\mu(x).

Note that

, and

\displaystyle|\nabla\phi_{t}(F_{t})|^{2}=|\dot{F}_{t}(x)|^{2}=|\dot{F}_{1}(x)|^{2}=d^{2}(x,F_{1}(x)).

This proves
(\ref{IJ})

(\ref{SNm4b})
) for

in Theorem

(5.112)

Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle N_{m}^{\prime\prime}\leq-{KN_{m}\over m}W_{2}^{2}(\rho_{0},\rho_{1}).

(5.113)

In particular, when

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime}+{H^{\prime 2}\over n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over n}\int_{M}\left|\Delta\phi-\int_{M}\Delta\phi\rho d\mu\right|^{2}\rho d\nu-\int_{M}Ric(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\rho d\nu

(5.114)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\int_{M}\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{\Delta\phi\over n}g\right\|^{2}_{\rm HS}\rho d\nu

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left[Ric(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)+\left\|\nabla^{2}\phi-{\int_{M}\Delta\phi\rho d\nu\over n}g\right\|^{2}_{\rm HS}\right]\rho d\nu.

Hence, under the condition

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime}+{H^{\prime 2}\over n}\leq-K\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\rho d\nu=-KW_{2}^{2}(\rho(0),\rho(1)).

(5.115)

Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle N_{n}^{\prime\prime}\leq-{KN_{m}\over n}W^{2}(\rho_{0},\rho_{1}).

(5.116)

Conversely, we now prove that, if
(\ref{hhhh2})
or

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v,

5.3
Summary and further problems

Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
indicate the equivalence between the curvature-dimension CD
(K,m)
-condition, entropy differential inequalities (EDI) and entropy power differential inequalities (EPDI)
along the geodesics on the Wasserstein space over Riemannian manifolds. In particular, each of the equivalent conditions (ii)-(vii) in Theorem
2.2
is equivalent to the definition inequality
(\ref{S(K, N)})
(for
N^{\prime}\geq N=m
)
which was used by Sturm
[
45
]
to define the CD
(K,N)
-condition on metric measure spaces. They are also equivalent to Lott-Villani’s characterization of CD
(K,N)
condition using functionals in
the class
\mathcal{DC}_{N}
. Therefore, we can
use each of (ii)-(vii) or its integratral forms to introduce definition of CD
(K,N)
-condition on

Curvature Properties of 3 3 - ( α , δ ) (\alpha,\delta) -Sasaki Manifolds
Ilka Agricola, Giulia Dileo, Leander Stecker

Abstract
We investigate curvature properties of 3 3 - ( α , δ ) (\alpha,\delta) -Sasaki manifolds, a special class of almost 3 3 -contact metric manifolds generalizing 3 3 -Sasaki manifolds (corresponding to α = δ = 1 \alpha=\delta=1 ) that admit a canonical metric connection with skew torsion and define a Riemannian submersion over a quaternionic Kähler manifold with vanishing, positive or negative scalar curvature, according to δ = 0 \delta=0 , α ​ δ > 0 \alpha\delta>0 or α ​ δ < 0 \alpha\delta<0 . We shall investigate both the Riemannian curvature and the curvature of the canonical connection, with particular focus on their curvature operators, regarded as symmetric endomorphisms of the space of 2 2 -forms. We describe their spectrum, find distinguished eigenforms, and study the conditions of strongly definite curvature in the sense of Thorpe.

Introduction

The present paper is devoted to the curvature properties of

The geometry of

The canonical connection plays a central role in this picture, as it preserves both the vertical and the horizontal distribution, and in fact, when applied to basic vector fields, it projects onto the Levi-Civita connection of the quaternionic Kähler base space. Beyond this introduction, the remaining part of Section 1 will be devoted to a short review of the notions and results needed in this work.

Let

Typical examples of manifolds admitting metric connections with parallel skew torsion include Sasaki,

Furthermore, the curvature tensor

R(X,Y,Z,V)=R(Z,V,X,Y).

(1.2)

(1.3)

An

-form associated to the structure is defined by

The almost contact metric structure is said to be

is the Nijenhuis torsion of

]

An

For

An

(ijk)
of
(123)

[

]

The tangent bundle of

In particular

The Canonical Curvature Operator

2.1
The canonical curvature and the canonical submersion

The canonical curvature is particularly well behaved on the defining tensors of a
3
-
(\alpha,\delta)
-Sasaki manifold. We will make use of this to compute directly related curvature identities in the following two propositions. These, in turn, allowed us to prove the existence of the canonical submersion in
[
ADS21
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(M,\varphi_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i},g)
be a
3
-
(\alpha,\delta)
-Sasaki manifold. Let
\nabla
be the canonical
connection and
R
the curvature tensor of
\nabla
. Then, the following equations hold:

\begin{split}&R(X,Y)\varphi_{i}Z-\varphi_{i}R(X,Y)Z=2\alpha\beta\{\Phi_{k}(X,Y)\varphi_{j}Z-\Phi_{j}(X,Y)\varphi_{k}Z\}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-2\alpha\beta\{(\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j})(X,Y)\varphi_{j}Z-(\eta_{k}\wedge\eta_{i})(X,Y)\varphi_{k}Z\},\end{split}

(2.1)

\begin{split}R(X,Y)\xi_{i}&=2\alpha\beta\{\Phi_{k}(X,Y)\xi_{j}-\Phi_{j}(X,Y)\xi_{k}\}\\
&\quad-2\alpha\beta\{(\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j})(X,Y)\xi_{j}-(\eta_{k}\wedge\eta_{i})(X,Y)\xi_{k}\},\end{split}

(2.2)

where
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
(ijk)
is an even permutation of
(123)
.

Proof.

Applying the Ricci identity, (
1.8
) and (
1.6
), we have

\displaystyle R(X,Y)\varphi_{i}Z-\varphi_{i}R(X,Y)Z

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}(\nabla_{Y}\varphi_{i}))Z-(\nabla_{Y}(\nabla_{X}\varphi_{i}))Z-(\nabla_{[X,Y]}\varphi_{i})Z

\displaystyle=\beta\{X(\eta_{k}(Y))\varphi_{j}Z+\eta_{k}(Y)(\nabla_{X}\varphi_{j})Z-X(\eta_{j}(Y))\varphi_{k}Z-\eta_{j}(Y)(\nabla_{X}\varphi_{k})Z\}

\displaystyle\quad-\beta\{Y(\eta_{k}(X))\varphi_{j}Z+\eta_{k}(X)(\nabla_{Y}\varphi_{j})Z-Y(\eta_{j}(X))\varphi_{k}Z-\eta_{j}(X)(\nabla_{Y}\varphi_{k})Z\}

\displaystyle\quad-\beta\{\eta_{k}([X,Y])\varphi_{j}Z-\eta_{j}([X,Y])\varphi_{k}Z\}

\displaystyle=\beta\{d\eta_{k}(X,Y)\varphi_{j}Z-d\eta_{j}(X,Y)\varphi_{k}Z\}

\displaystyle\quad+\beta^{2}\{\eta_{k}(Y)(\eta_{i}(X)\varphi_{k}Z-\eta_{k}(X)\varphi_{i}Z)-\eta_{j}(Y)(\eta_{j}(X)\varphi_{i}Z-\eta_{i}(X)\varphi_{j}Z)\}

\displaystyle\quad-\beta^{2}\{\eta_{k}(X)(\eta_{i}(Y)\varphi_{k}Z-\eta_{k}(Y)\varphi_{i}Z)-\eta_{j}(X)(\eta_{j}(Y)\varphi_{i}Z-\eta_{i}(Y)\varphi_{j}Z)\}

\displaystyle=2\alpha\beta\{\Phi_{k}(X,Y)\varphi_{j}Z-\Phi_{j}(X,Y)\varphi_{k}Z\}

\displaystyle\quad+\{2\beta(\alpha-\delta)+\beta^{2}\}\{(\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j})(X,Y)\varphi_{j}Z-(\eta_{k}\wedge\eta_{i})(X,Y)\varphi_{k}Z\},

which gives (
2.1
), since
\beta=2(\delta-2\alpha)
. We obtain (
2.2
) by setting
Z=\xi_{i}
in (
2.1
) and applying
\varphi_{i}
from the left.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

The curvature tensor
R
of the canonical connection of a
3
-
(\alpha,\delta)
-Sasaki manifold
satisfies for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{H}
and
i,j,k,l=1,2,3
the identities

\displaystyle\hskip 61.17325ptR(X,\xi_{i},Y,\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=R(X,Y,Z,\xi_{i})=R(\xi_{i},\xi_{j},\xi_{k},X)=0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle R(\xi_{i},\xi_{j},\xi_{k},\xi_{l})

\displaystyle=-4\alpha\beta(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}),

(2.4)

and for an even permutation
(ijk)
of
(123)

\displaystyle R(\xi_{i},\xi_{j},X,Y)

\displaystyle=2\alpha\beta\Phi_{k}(X,Y),\hskip 113.81102pt

(2.5)

\displaystyle R(X,Y,Z,\varphi_{i}Z)+R(X,Y,\varphi_{j}Z,\varphi_{k}Z)

\displaystyle=2\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y)\|Z\|^{2}.

(2.6)

Proof.

Considering the symmetries of
R
we immediately obtain the first three expressions from equation (
2.2
). Using (
2.1
) for
\varphi_{j}
we obtain

\displaystyle R(X,Y,\varphi_{j}Z,\varphi_{k}Z)

\displaystyle=g(\varphi_{j}R(X,Y)Z,\varphi_{k}Z)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\alpha\beta(\Phi_{i}(X,Y)g(\varphi_{k}Z,\varphi_{k}Z)-\Phi_{k}(X,Y)g(\varphi_{i}Z,\varphi_{k}Z))

\displaystyle=-R(X,Y,Z,\varphi_{i}Z)+2\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y)\|Z\|^{2}.\qed

Remark 2.1
.

The identities (
2.3
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
) are used to prove the canonical submersion in
[
ADS21
]
.

Considering now the canonical submersion
\pi\colon M\to N
defined in Theorem
1.1
, in the next Theorem we will relate the missing purely horizontal part of the canonical curvature tensor to the curvature of the quaternionic Kähler base space
N
. We recall a computational lemma from
[
ADS21
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
ADS21
, Lemma 2.2.1]
)
.

For any vertical vector field
X\in\mathcal{V}
and for any basic vector field
Y\in\mathcal{H}
we have

(\nabla_{X}Y)_{\mathcal{H}}=-2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{3}\eta_{i}(X)\varphi_{i}Y.

Theorem 2.1
.

The canonical curvature on
\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}\otimes\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}
is given by

\displaystyle R(\overline{X},\overline{Y},\overline{Z},\overline{V})=R^{g_{N}}(X,Y,Z,V)+4\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Phi_{i}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\Phi_{i}(\overline{Z},\overline{V}),

where
X,Y,Z,V\in TN
with horizontal lifts
\overline{X},\overline{Y},\overline{Z},\overline{V}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

We note that by
\nabla^{g_{N}}_{X}Y=\pi_{*}(\nabla_{\overline{X}}\overline{Y})
the vector field
\nabla_{\overline{X}}\overline{Y}\in\mathcal{H}
is
\pi
-related to
\nabla^{g_{N}}_{X}Y
and thus
\nabla_{\overline{X}}\overline{Y}=\overline{\pi_{*}(\nabla_{\overline{X}}\overline{Y})}
. We obtain

\displaystyle g_{N}(\nabla^{g_{N}}_{X}\nabla^{g_{N}}_{Y}Z,V)

\displaystyle=g_{N}(\nabla^{g_{N}}_{X}\pi_{*}(\nabla_{\overline{Y}}\overline{Z}),V)=g(\nabla_{\overline{X}}\overline{\pi_{*}(\nabla_{\overline{Y}}\overline{Z})},\overline{V})=g(\nabla_{\overline{X}}\nabla_{\overline{Y}}\overline{Z},\overline{V}),

\displaystyle g_{N}(\nabla^{g_{N}}_{[X,Y]}Z,V)

\displaystyle=g_{N}(\pi_{*}\nabla_{\overline{[X,Y]}}\overline{Z},V)=g(\nabla_{[\overline{X},\overline{Y}]}\overline{Z}-\nabla_{[\overline{X},\overline{Y}]_{\mathcal{V}}}\overline{Z},\overline{V})

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{[\overline{X},\overline{Y}]}\overline{Z},\overline{V})+4\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Phi_{i}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\Phi_{i}(\overline{Z},\overline{V})

where we have used (
1.7
) and
Lemma 2.1
. Plugging these identities into the curvature we find

\displaystyle R^{g_{N}}(X,Y,Z,V)

\displaystyle=g_{N}(\nabla^{g_{N}}_{X}\nabla^{g_{N}}_{Y}Z-\nabla^{g_{N}}_{Y}\nabla^{g_{N}}_{X}Z-\nabla^{g_{N}}_{[X,Y]}Z,V)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{\overline{X}}\nabla_{\overline{Y}}\overline{Z}-\nabla_{\overline{Y}}\nabla_{\overline{X}}\overline{Z}-\nabla_{[\overline{X},\overline{Y}]}\overline{Z},\overline{V})-4\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Phi_{i}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\Phi_{i}(\overline{Z},\overline{V})

\displaystyle=R(\overline{X},\overline{Y},\overline{Z},\overline{V})-4\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Phi_{i}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})\Phi_{i}(\overline{Z},\overline{V}).\qed

2.2
Decomposition of the Canonical Curvature Operator

We now want to look at the canonical curvature as a curvature operator and consider its eigenvalues and definiteness. Recall that the canonical curvature operator
\mathcal{R}\colon\Lambda^{2}M\to\Lambda^{2}M
defines a symmetric operator. Rewriting (
2.3
) as operator identities we obtain

\langle\mathcal{R}(X\wedge\xi_{i}),Y\wedge\xi_{j}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{R}(X\wedge Y),Z\wedge\xi_{i}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{R}(\xi_{i}\wedge\xi_{j}),\xi_{k}\wedge X\rangle=0

showing that the canonical curvature operator vanishes on
\mathcal{V}\wedge\mathcal{H}
. Thus, it can be considered as a symmetric operator
\mathcal{R}\colon\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{V}\oplus\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}\to\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{V}\oplus\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}
. It does not restrict to the individual summands but we can accomplish a more nuanced decomposition.

Remark 2.2
.

From here on out we will freely identify
TM
and
T^{*}M
as well as their exterior products. In particular, we write
\xi_{jk}\coloneqq\xi_{j}\wedge\xi_{k}=\eta_{jk}=\eta_{j}\wedge\eta_{k}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
can be decomposed as

\mathcal{R}=\alpha\beta\mathcal{R}_{\perp}+\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{par}}

where
\mathcal{R}_{\perp}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\perp}\coloneqq\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize i,j,k}}{{\mathfrak{S}}}(\Phi_{i}-\xi_{jk})\otimes(\Phi_{i}-\xi_{jk})

(2.7)

and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{par}}
is trivial outside of the horizontal part, i.e.
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{par}}|_{(\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H})^{\perp}}=0
.

Proof.

Equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) in terms of the curvature operator read

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{R}(\xi_{i}\wedge\xi_{j}),\xi_{k}\wedge\xi_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=4\alpha\beta\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}\Phi_{\mu}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})\Phi_{\mu}(\xi_{k},\xi_{l}),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{R}(\xi_{i}\wedge\xi_{j}),X\wedge Y\rangle

\displaystyle=2\alpha\beta\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}\Phi_{\mu}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})\Phi_{\mu}(X,Y).

(2.9)

We observe that identities (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) are of the form
C\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Phi_{i}\otimes\Phi_{i}
, where the coefficient
C
is
4\alpha\beta
or
2\alpha\beta
. The comparison with

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\perp}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize i,j,k}}{{\mathfrak{S}}}(\Phi_{i}-\xi_{jk})\otimes(\Phi_{i}-\xi_{jk})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\big(4(\Phi_{i}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{V}}\otimes\Phi_{i}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{V}})+2(\Phi_{i}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}}\otimes\Phi_{i}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{V}})+2(\Phi_{i}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{V}}\otimes\Phi_{i}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}})+(\Phi_{i}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}}\otimes\Phi_{i}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}})\big).

shows that
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{par}}\coloneqq\mathcal{R}-\alpha\beta\mathcal{R}_{\perp}
is trivial outside
\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}
.
∎

The notation
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{par}}
is justified by the fact that in the parallel case (
\beta=0
) we have
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{par}}
.
Taking into account the canonical submersion
\pi\colon M\to N
, we may consider the Riemannian curvature operator
\mathcal{R}^{g_{N}}
on the base
N
as a curvature operator
\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}\to\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}
via the horizontal lift. From
Theorem 2.1
, we have

\mathcal{R}|_{\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}\otimes\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{R}^{g_{N}}-4\alpha^{2}\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu}\otimes\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu}.

Note the sign change due to our convention of sign in
\mathcal{R}
compared to
R
. Comparing with the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{par}}
in
Proposition 2.3
and expanding
\beta=2(\delta-2\alpha)
yields

\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{par}}=\mathcal{R}^{g_{N}}-2\alpha\delta\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu}\otimes\Phi^{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu}.

(2.10)

.

\mathcal{R}^{g_{N}}=\nu\mathcal{R}_{0}+\mathcal{R}_{1}
, where
\nu=4\alpha\delta

4

4.1

We now investigate strongly non-negative and even strongly positive curvature on

\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}^{g}
are related by

\mathcal{R}^{g}=\mathcal{R}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{G}_{T}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{S}_{T}.

In particular,
(M,g)
is strongly non-negative with 4-form

if and only if

\mathcal{R}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{G}_{T}\geq 0.

Observe that
\mathcal{G}_{T}
is non-negative by definition, so we have directly strong non-negativity if
\mathcal{R}
is non-negative.
Theorem 2.3
thus yields (recall that
\beta:=2(\delta-2\alpha)
)

Let

3

(\alpha,\delta)
-Sasaki manifold with
\alpha\beta\geq 0
and

is strongly non-negative with
4

-\frac{1}{4}\sigma_{T}

As we later see this will be sufficient for homogeneous spaces, but in general the condition
\mathcal{R}^{g_{N}}\geq 0
is too strong. However, we can relax the condition on the base to strong non-negativity, but we need an additional assumption on the
4
-form. To do all this, we need some notation.

For

denote the
2
-dimensional spaces

. Then decompose the space of

where the

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}_{1}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}N_{i},\quad\Lambda^{2}_{2}=\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}\cap\{\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{H}},\Phi_{2}^{\mathcal{H}},\Phi_{3}^{\mathcal{H}}\}^{\perp},\quad\Lambda^{2}_{3}=\mathcal{V}\wedge\mathcal{H}.

For a linear map

Let us motivate this decomposition. The obvious

does not restrict to

and
\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}

\Lambda^{2}_{1}
and
\Lambda^{2}_{2}

if for every point

\displaystyle=g([X,\xi_{i}],[Z,\xi_{j}])=\delta^{2}g(\varphi_{i}X,\varphi_{j}Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{2}}{4\alpha^{2}}g(T(X\wedge\xi_{i}),T(Y\wedge\xi_{j}))=\frac{\delta^{2}}{4\alpha^{2}}g(\mathcal{G}_{T}(X\wedge\xi_{i}),Y\wedge\xi_{j}).

Thus by the previous lemma
\tau=-\varepsilon\sigma_{T}
accomplishes strong fatness for sufficiently small

v)

The final condition is for the base to be one of

\mathbb{H}P^{n}

\mathbb{C}P^{2}

Note that i)-iv) are valid for all positive homogeneous examples not only for the spheres, real projective spaces and

4.3
Some Inhomogeneous Example

Let us recall
3
-Sasaki reduction introduced in
[
BGM94
]
. Let
(M,\varphi_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i},g)
be a
3
-Sasaki manifold and
G
a connected compact Lie group acting on
M
by
3
-Sasaki automorphisms. We consider the
3
-Sasaki moment map

\displaystyle\mu\colon M

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto(X\to\eta_{i}(\overline{X}_{x}))_{i=1,2,3}

where
\overline{X}_{x}
is the fundamental vector field of
X\in\mathfrak{g}
at
x\in M
.

Theorem 4.6

(
[
BGM94
]
)
.

Assume that
0
is a regular value of
\mu
and that
G
acts freely on the preimage
\mu^{-1}(\{0\})
. Denote the embedding
\iota\colon\mu^{-1}(\{0\})\to M
and the submersion
\pi\colon\mu^{-1}(\{0\})\to\mu^{-1}(\{0\})/G
. Then
(\mu^{-1}(\{0\})/G,\check{\varphi}_{i},\check{\xi}_{i},\check{\eta}_{i},\check{g})_{i=1,2,3}
is a smooth
3
-Sasaki manifold, where the
3
-Sasaki structure is uniquely determined by
\iota^{*}g=\pi^{*}\check{g}
and
\check{\xi}_{i}=\pi_{*}(\xi_{i}|_{\mu^{-1}(\{0\})})
.

We will focus on actions of
S^{1}
on the
3
-Sasaki sphere
S^{11}\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
via

\displaystyle z\cdot(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})=(z^{p_{1}}q_{1},z^{p_{2}}q_{2},z^{p_{3}}q_{3}).

In
[
BG08
, Theorem
13.7.6
]
the authors show that for pairwise coprime, positive integers
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
these actions satisfy the assumptions in
Theorem 4.6
and, thus, give rise to
3
-Sasaki manifolds
\mathcal{S}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
. If
p_{1}=p_{2}=p_{3}=1
this is exactly the homogeneous
3
-Sasaki Aloff-Wallach space
W^{1,1}
. Apart form this they are shown in
[
BG08
, Corollary
13.7.13
]
to be of cohomogeneity
1
or
2
.

In
[
Dea04
]
the author shows that under the assumption
\sqrt{2}\min p_{i}>\max p_{i}
a certain deformation of the
3
-Sasaki metric, corresponding to a
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation in our notation, admits positive sectional curvature. We make use of a key step of his showing that their underlying quaternionic Kähler orbifolds have positive sectional curvature,
[
Dea04
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 4.7

(
[
Dea04
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
be as before and
\mathcal{O}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
the underlying quaternionic Kähler orbifold. If
\sqrt{2}\min p_{i}>\max{p_{i}}
then
\mathcal{O}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
has positive sectional curvature.

In order to make the jump from positive sectional curvature to strongly positive curvature we make use of the fact that
\mathcal{O}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
is
4
-dimensional. In this dimension Thorpe proves the following,
[
Th71
, Corollary
4.2
]
.

Theorem 4.8

(
[
Th71
]
)
.

Let
V
be a
4
-dimensional vector space and
R
any algebraic curvature operator on
V
. If
\lambda
is the minimal sectional curvature of
R
then there is a unique
\omega\in\Lambda^{4}V
such that
\lambda
is the minimal eigenvalue of
R+\omega
.

We are finally ready to state our main theorem.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
be coprime integers with
\sqrt{2}\min p_{i}>\max p_{i}
. Then there is a
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation of
\mathcal{S}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
that has strongly positive curvature.

Proof.

Thorpe’s theorem proves that the orbifold
\mathcal{O}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
has not only positive sectional curvature but strongly positive curvature. Since we are in dimension
4
the form
\omega
is necessarily a multiple of the volume form
\omega=\nu_{p}\mathrm{dVol}
. As before the volume form has eigenspaces
\Lambda^{2}_{\pm}
where
\Lambda^{2}_{+}=\mathcal{Q}
and
\Lambda^{2}_{-}=\mathcal{Q}^{\perp}
. In particular,
\omega
is an adapted
4
-form with minimal eigenvalue
\nu=\min\nu_{p}
. The minimum exists since the orbifolds are quotients of compact spaces and, thus, compact themselves. All in all we may apply
Theorem 4.1
. Note that by
Remark 4.1
we obtain a
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation of
\mathcal{S}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
with
\alpha/\delta\gg 0
sufficiently big while not changing the metric
g_{\mathcal{O}}
on
\mathcal{O}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
.

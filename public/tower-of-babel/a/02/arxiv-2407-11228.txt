Existence of weak solutions for a volume-filling model of cell invasion into extracellular matrix
Rebecca M. Crossley, Jan-Frederik Pietschmann ,, Markus Schmidtchen

Abstract
We study the existence of weak solutions for a model of cell invasion into the extracellular matrix (ECM), consisting of a non-linear partial differential equation (PDE) for cell density coupled with an ordinary differential equation (ODE) for ECM density. The model includes cross-species density-dependent diffusion and proliferation terms, capturing the role of the ECM in supporting cells during invasion and preventing growth via volume-filling effects. The occurrence of cross-diffusion terms is a common theme in the system of interacting species with excluded-volume interactions. Additionally, ECM degradation by cells is included. We present an existence result for weak solutions, exploiting the partial gradient flow structure to overcome the non-regularising nature of the ODE. Furthermore, we present simulations that illustrate travelling wave solutions and investigate asymptotic behaviour as the ECM degradation rate tends to infinity.



1
Introduction

The aim of this work is to analyse a model for cell migration into the extracellular matrix (ECM) that consists of a system including a non-linear, degenerate parabolic, partial differential equation (PDE) for the density of cells, coupled to an ordinary differential equation (ODE) (yet for every point in space) for the ECM.

Mathematical models of collective cell migration are often implemented to understand how the number of cells within at least one population evolves over time.
However, existing models of this important biological phenomenon become increasingly complex by considering the impacts and influences of various environmental characteristics or additional populations, such as the availability of nutrients
[
20
,
32
,
33
,
40
]
, surrounding oxygen levels
[
10
]
, or physical structures such as the ECM
[
12
,
15
]
.

The ECM, in particular, is an intricate, dynamic network of molecules including elastin, collagen and laminin, among others, that varies in structure depending on its location.
The molecules within the ECM are meticulously organised into a complex meshwork that plays an important role in providing structural integrity and repair of tissues, as well as providing structural and biochemical support to cells during migration
[
34
]
.
An important mechanism of cell-ECM feedback is the degradation of ECM elements by surrounding cells through the use of matrix-degrading enzymes, such as membrane-bound and diffusive matrix metalloproteinases
[
46
]
.
Matrix-degrading enzymes target specific components of the ECM, such as collagen fibers, to degrade them, which, in turn, increases the space available for cells to move into during migration
[
4
,
24
,
37
]
.

There are a variety of mathematical approaches available for studying the dynamics of cells and the ECM during collective cell migration that are detailed in
[
12
]
.
However, the specific focus of this work is the following system of differential equations that was derived and non-dimensionalised in
[
13
]
to describe the evolution of a cell density,
u
, and the ECM,
m
, respectively, at a location
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, open and bounded, as well as
t\in(0,\infty)
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\bigg[(1-u-m)\nabla u+u\nabla(u+m)\bigg]+u(1-u-m),

(1a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial m}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\lambda mu.

(1b)

The system (
1
) is equipped with initial data assumed to verify

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{\mathrm{in}}(x),\qquad\text{ and }\qquad m(x,0)=m_{\mathrm{in}}(x),

where
u_{\mathrm{in}},\,m_{\mathrm{in}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfy
0\leq u_{\mathrm{in}},m_{\mathrm{in}}
and
u_{\mathrm{in}}+m_{\mathrm{in}}\leq 1
.
We have chosen to constrain the total mass above by one, in line with the non-dimensional carrying capacity of this model.
Furthermore, we prescribe no-flux boundary conditions for the cell density,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\bigg[(1-u-m)\nabla u+u\nabla(u+m)\bigg]\cdot{\bf{n}}=0\qquad\text{ on }\qquad\partial\Omega,

where
{\bf{n}}
denotes the outward normal vector on the boundary
\partial\Omega
, as we assume that cells are confined to
\Omega
.

This model follows simplifying assumptions, which are set out below and verified experimentally in
[
38
]
, that state that the timescale over which ECM degradation occurs is far larger than the timescale over which the matrix-degrading enzymes decay, so we can model that cells directly degrade the ECM via their membrane-bound matrix-degrading enzymes only.
As such, Eq. (
1b
) contains only one term that describes the non-dimensionalised degradation rate of ECM by surrounding cells as
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, which largely impacts the speed and structure of travelling wave type solutions observed in this model.

Eq. (
1a
) is a reaction-diffusion type PDE that relates closely to the Fisher-Kolmogorov-Petrovsky-Piskunov (Fisher-KPP) equation
[
19
,
25
]
, which is well-studied in a variety of contexts, including population models in mathematical biology
[
35
]
.
The first term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
1a
) describes the movement of cells down the gradient of the cell density, where movement is prevented by the presence of surrounding cells and ECM.
The second term models motion of the cells down the gradient of the total density of cells and ECM,
{u}+{m}
, and takes the form of an advective term.
Diffusion-advection-reaction differential equations with linear diffusion are well-studied in a variety of contexts, see, for example,
[
39
,
42
]
.
It is a result of the volume filling assumptions in the underlying individual based model that ensures that the corresponding continuum model includes two flux terms that describe motility, such as those previously studied in a variety of contexts, which include cross-diffusion
[
5
,
9
]
, drift
[
1
,
2
,
31
]
and non-local interaction terms
[
16
]
.
This is because the volume exclusion processes included prevent cell growth and motility in regions of space with higher density of cells and ECM.
From a mathematical perspective, we are therefore dealing with a cross-diffusion type of model.
The final term on the right hand side of Eq. (
1a
) captures cell proliferation, which, due to volume-filling effects, is also reduced by the presence of surrounding cells and ECM.

The mathematical analysis of multi-species cross-diffusion models is challenging as, on the one hand, due to the solution-dependent mobilities, such systems are often only degenerate parabolic, as in the current context, which impedes the usual regularising effects.
On the other hand, the coupling of several equations often prevents the use of maximum principles to obtain
L^{\infty}
-bounds.
This is particularly important for systems that involve volume exclusion processes, as in this case, since the upper bound on the densities is a central modelling assumption.
In the wider context of linear diffusion
[
26
]
, or stationary cross-diffusion
[
27
]
, variations of the maximum principle have been employed in order to obtain sufficient regularising bounds
[
28
,
45
]
. However, they either treat stationary equations or require regularity of solutions which, due to the fact that (
1a
) is only degenerate parabolic, is not available in our case.

However, one, by now established, approach to overcome these difficulties is the so-called boundedness by entropy methods
[
6
,
23
]
that can be applied if the system is (formally) an Otto-Wasserstein gradient flow
[
22
,
36
]
.
The basic idea is to transform the system into entropy variables, defined as variational derivatives of the entropy functional with respect to the unknown densities. Then, in many practically relevant cases, it turns out that the mapping between these and the original variables automatically enforces the desired bounds.
This technique has, in different variants, been successfully applied in, for example,
[
8
,
17
,
47
]
.

Another issue which is specific to the system of equations studied in this work is the fact that Eq. (
1b
), merely being an ODE, does not contain any diffusive terms and thus, no regularising effect for
m
can be expected. A similar coupling of PDEs and an ODE was previously analysed in the context of chemotaxis, see, for example,
[
11
,
29
,
44
]
.
Most importantly in our situation, the coupling also implies that we are not dealing with a formal Otto-Wasserstein gradient flow. A similar situation, yet for a model of tumour growth, was encountered in
[
7
]
.

As a consequence, the main difficulty in studying the aforementioned system stems from the fact that it cannot be cast into any standard gradient flow framework. Indeed, we first observe that the form of the flux inside the divergence in Eq. (
1a
) is exactly the same as for the volume filling model analysed in
[
6
]
.
The entropy functional that the authors used in their work, yet without self-diffusion for
m
, reads as

\mathcal{E}(u,m)=\int_{\Omega}u(\log(u)-1)+(1-\rho)(\log(1-\rho)-1)\mathrm{d}x,

(2)

with
\rho(x,t):=u(x,t)+m(x,t)
.
This allows us to rewrite Eq. (
1a
) in the form

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot\bigg[u(1-\rho)\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta u}\bigg]+u(1-\rho).

The form of the right-hand side of Eq. (1a) dictates an Otto-Wasserstein-type gradient flow, which, in turn, implies the functional form of the free energy with respect to
u
, up to a constant, possibly depending on
m
. However, the reaction term in Eq. (
1a
) disrupts this gradient flow structure as the equation is not mass-conserving.
Coupled to Eq. (
1b
) it is not clear in which sense the whole system could be interpreted as gradient flow.
Since the system does not fall into a remotely standard gradient flow framework that is often exploited in existence proofs.

In particular, we do not expect the entropy (Eq. (
2
)) to act as a Lyapunov functional.
Nevertheless, we can show that it grows at most linearly in time.
Then, we are able to extract regularity information from its dissipation which provides adequate compactness to establish the existence of solutions to the system.
These estimates are made rigorous by means of a time-discretistion and regularisation procedure.

The cornerstone of this analysis is the observation that there is a regularising effect for the ECM equation despite it only being an ODE.
Albeit not being a gradient flow, from the dissipation we obtain an estimate of the form

\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}({t_{n-1},t_{n}};L^{2}(\Omega))}-\|\nabla m\|^{2}_{L^{2}({t_{n-1},t_{n}};L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C,

in conjunction with the conditional regularising effect for the ECM density,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla m\rVert_{L^{\infty}({t_{n-1},t_{n}};L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\leq 2^{n}\lVert\nabla m_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\tau\lambda^{2}\Delta t\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}2^{n-i}\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}({t_{i},t_{i+1}};L^{2}(\Omega))},

on any time interval
[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
of length
\Delta t>0
, see Lemma
2.8
.
It requires a fine study to combine both estimates in a suitable way that allows extension to the whole time interval
[0,T]
.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}[(1-u-m)\nabla u+u\nabla(u+m)]\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u(1-u-m)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

(3)

The rest of the paper is organised as follows.

2
Proof of the main theorem

The first step of our proof consists of constructing a time discrete, regularised (both in the equations and the initial datum for the ECM equation) approximation to the system (
1
), which is, however, still non-linear. Therefore, we have to show the existence of iterates for this scheme by means of a fixed point argument. It turns out that a regularisation of the initial datum
m_{\mathrm{in}}
results in solutions satisfying the box constraints strictly. This allows us to obtain a time-discrete, yet rigorous, version of the entropy–entropy-dissipation inequality. Thus, using a careful construction to mitigate the lack of regularity in
m
, we eventually obtain bounds that are sufficient to pass to the limit and obtain the desired weak solution. Throughout our analysis we will use the letter
C
to denote constants that may change from line to line.

2.1
Time discretisation and regularisation

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and discretise time such that
(0,T]
has sub-intervals of the form

(0,T]=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\big((k-1)\tau,k\tau\big],

where
\tau=\frac{T}{N}
.
We write the recursive sequence

\displaystyle\frac{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}{\tau}

\displaystyle=\tau{(\Delta w_{k}-w_{k})}+\nabla\cdot\big(u_{k}(1-\rho_{k})\nabla w_{k}\big)+u_{k}(1-\rho_{k}),

(4a)

\displaystyle\frac{m_{k}-m_{k-1}}{\tau}

\displaystyle=-\lambda m_{k}u_{k},

(4b)

where
\rho_{k}:=u_{k}+m_{k}
,

m_{0}:=\max(\tau,\min(m_{\mathrm{in}},1-\tau)),\quad\text{and}\quad u_{0}=u_{\mathrm{in}}.

Furthermore, we recall that

\displaystyle w(u,m)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}}{\delta u}=\log(u)-\log(1-\rho),

In order to show the existence of weak solutions, we define the set

{\mathcal{A}:=\left\{(u,m)\in(L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{2}:\,0\leq u,\,m\leq 1,\,0\leq\rho=u+m\leq 1\right\}.}

Lemma 2.1

Given

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\tau+\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{\rho}))\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi+\tau w\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left[\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{\rho})-\frac{\tilde{u}-u_{k-1}}{\tau}\right]\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C,

where the constant

In addition, the operator
S_{1}:\mathcal{A}\to L^{2}(\Omega)

) is continuous and compact.

Proof.

As
\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{\rho})\geq 0

, the existence of a unique solution

For the continuity of

, consider a sequence
(\tilde{u}_{n},\tilde{m}_{n})\in\mathcal{A}
such that
(\tilde{u}_{n},\tilde{m}_{m})\to(\tilde{u},\tilde{m})\in\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\tau+\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{\rho})|\nabla(w-w_{n})|^{2}+\tau|w-w_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\Omega}[\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{\rho})-\tilde{u}_{n}(1-\tilde{\rho}_{n})]\nabla w_{n}\cdot\nabla(w-w_{n})-\frac{\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{n}}{\tau}(w-w_{n})\mathrm{d}x.

As both
(\tilde{u}_{n},\tilde{m}_{n})\in\mathcal{A}
and
(\tilde{u},\tilde{m})\in\mathcal{A}
, elementary calculations show that the following estimate holds:

|\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{\rho})-\tilde{u}_{n}(1-\tilde{\rho}_{n})|\leq 2|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{n}|+|\tilde{m}-\tilde{m}_{n}|.

Additionally, we can use that
0\leq\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{\rho})\leq 1
and estimate the left-hand side in Eq. (

\displaystyle\tau\|w-w_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}[\tilde{u}(1-\tilde{\rho})-\tilde{u}_{n}(1-\tilde{\rho}_{n})]\nabla w_{n}\cdot\nabla(w-w_{n})-\frac{\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{n}}{\tau}(w-w_{n})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(2\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{m}-\tilde{m}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|\nabla w_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla(w-w_{n})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{\sqrt{|\Omega|}}{\tau}\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|w-w_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C^{\prime}\left(\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{m}-\tilde{m}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\|w-w_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

where
C^{\prime}>0
only depends on
\tau
,
\Omega
and

comes from Eq. (

Thus, as
(\tilde{u}_{n},\tilde{m}_{n})\to(\tilde{u},\tilde{m})
in

, we conclude that

in

.
Finally, the compactness of

then follows from the compactness of the embedding

.
∎

Our next goal is to recover a new pair

from

\displaystyle u=(1-m)\frac{e^{w}}{1+e^{w}}=:a_{w}(1-m).

Inserting this relation into Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{m-m_{k-1}}{\tau}=-\lambda um=-\lambda a_{w}m(1-m)

Lemma 2.2
.

Given
m_{k-1}
that satisfies
0\leq m_{k-1}\leq 1
, and for

, Eq. (

Additionally, the operator

, which maps

Proof.

To establish the existence of a unique solution

to Eq. (

\mathcal{B}=\{z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\;:\;0\leq z\leq 1\},

and the mapping

As

\displaystyle\|K(z_{1})-K(z_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\tau\lambda a_{w}\|m_{k-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

Theorem 2.3
.

For given

Due to the results of Lemma

Thus, an application of Schauder’s fixed point theorem yields the existence of a fixed point

To show that the fixed-point has

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla m_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\lVert\nabla w_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla m_{k-1}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

Theorem 2.4

(Uniform upper and lower bounds on

)
.

Let
(u_{k},m_{k})
be solutions provided by Theorem

The strict bounds in Theorem

Proof.

Starting from
(u_{0},m_{0})
, we construct

which solves

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\tau+u_{1}(1-\rho_{1}))\nabla w_{1}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\tau w_{1}\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left[u_{1}(1-\rho_{1})-\frac{u_{1}-u_{0}}{\tau}\right]\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x.

As the right-hand side is bounded in

, say, by the constant

, depending on

\displaystyle\lVert w_{1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\tau}:=\frac{\tilde{C}_{\tau}}{\tau}

Thes upper bound bounds follow by using

as test functions in Eq. (

\displaystyle\tau\int_{\Omega}(w_{1}-C_{\tau})_{+}^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}(\tau+u_{1}(1-\rho_{1}))\left|\nabla(w_{1}-C_{\tau})_{+}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left[u_{1}(1-\rho_{1})-\frac{u_{1}-u_{0}}{\tau}\right](w_{1}-C_{\tau})_{+}\;\mathrm{d}x-\tau\int_{\Omega}C_{\tau}(w_{1}-C_{\tau})_{+}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{C}_{\tau}-\tau C_{\tau})(w_{1}-C_{\tau})_{+}\mathrm{d}x=0,

as
\tilde{C}_{\tau}=\tau C_{\tau}
. The lower bound follows analogously.

Note that then

\displaystyle a_{w_{1}}=\frac{e^{w_{1}}}{1+e^{w_{1}}}\in\left[e^{-C_{\tau}},\frac{1}{1+e^{-C_{\tau}}}\right].

(14)

In addition, note that
m_{1}
is related to
w_{1}
via Eq. (
11
)

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}-m_{0}}{\tau}=-\lambda a_{w_{1}}m_{1}(1-m_{1}).

Thus, on the one hand, we have

\frac{m_{1}-m_{0}}{\tau}\leq 0,

i.e.
m_{1}\leq m_{0}\leq 1-\tau
. Conversely, we have, as
a_{w}\leq 1
, that

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}-m_{0}}{\tau}

\displaystyle=-\lambda a_{w_{1}}m_{1}(1-m_{1})\geq-\lambda m_{1},

which implies

m_{1}\geq\frac{\tau}{(1+\tau\lambda)}>0.

Recalling Eq. (
10
) then yields

\displaystyle u_{1}=a_{w_{1}}(1-m_{1})\in(0,1),

owing to the fact that
m_{1}\in(0,1)
and
a_{w_{1}}\in(0,1)
. For

\rho_{1}=u_{1}+m_{1}=a_{w_{1}}+m_{1}(1-a_{w_{1}})

this implies the bounds

\displaystyle 0<e^{-C_{\tau}}+\frac{\tau}{(1+\tau\lambda)}\leq a_{w_{1}}+m_{1}(1-a_{w_{1}})=\rho_{1}

as well as

\displaystyle\rho_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{w_{1}}+m_{1}(1-a_{w_{1}})\leq a_{w_{1}}+(1-\tau)(1-a_{w_{1}})

\displaystyle=1-\tau(1-a_{w_{1}})\leq 1-\tau\left(1-\frac{1}{1+e^{-C_{\tau}}}\right)<1.

Iterating this procedure, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\tau^{k}}{(1+\tau\lambda)^{k}}\leq m_{k}\leq 1-\tau,

as well as

\displaystyle e^{-C_{\tau}}\leq u_{k}\leq 1-\frac{\tau^{k}}{(1+\tau\lambda)^{k}},

and

\displaystyle e^{-C_{\tau}}+\frac{\tau^{k}}{(1+\tau\lambda)^{k}}\leq\rho_{k}\leq 1-\tau\left(1-\frac{1}{1+e^{-C_{\tau}}}\right),

where
C_{\tau}\to\infty
as
\tau\to 0
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

The fixed point
(u_{k},m_{k})\in\mathcal{A}\cap(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
constructed above is a weak solution to Eqs. (
4a
) and (
4b
).

Proof.

In view of the bounds provided by Theorem
2.4
, we can invert Eq. (
10
) and obtain
w_{k}=\log(u_{k})-\log(1-\rho_{k})
, which allows the identification of
(u_{k},m_{k})
as a weak solution of Eqs. (
4a
) and (
4b
).
∎

2.2
A priori estimates

Let
(u_{k},m_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}\,\cap\,(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
be the sequence obtained from the implicit Euler approximation, cf. Eq. (
4
).
This section is dedicated to deriving estimates independent of the time step size,
\tau>0
, that ultimately provide sufficient compactness to obtain weak solutions to the continuous system, Eq. (
1
).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(u_{k},m_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}\cap(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
as defined in Theorem
2.4
. Then, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following discrete entropy estimate holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})\right)+\tau\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}+|w_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq C-\int_{\Omega}u_{k}(1-u_{k}-m_{k})|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(15)

where
C>0
only depends on
\Omega
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C+\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})\right)+\|\nabla m_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(16)

with
C=C(\tau)>0
independent of
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Owing to the strict upper and lower bounds provided by Theorem
2.4
, the logarithmic terms appearing in the derivative of the entropy are well defined. Thus, we can use the joint convexity of the energy to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\log u_{k}-\log(1-u_{k}-m_{k})\right](u_{k}-u_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\log(1-u_{k}-m_{k})(m_{k}-m_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\log u_{k}-\log(1-u_{k}-m_{k})\right](u_{k}-u_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x,

where the last term was discarded due to its sign. Using the definition of the entropy variable, Eq. (
5
), this results in

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})\leq\int_{\Omega}w_{k}(u_{k}-u_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x.

Due to the
H^{1}(\Omega)
-regularity of
w_{k}
, we may use it as a test functions in Eq. (
4
) to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\tau\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}+|w_{k}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\nabla w_{k}\cdot(u_{k}(1-u_{k}-m_{k})\nabla w_{k})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}w_{k}u_{k}(1-u_{k}-m_{k})\mathrm{d}x.

Integrating by parts, and using that
|\Omega|<\infty
as well as
0\leq u_{k},m_{k}\leq 1
and the definition of
w_{k}
, the inequality further simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C-\tau\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}+|w_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}u_{k}(1-u_{k}-m_{k})|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

where
C>0
is independent of
k,\ell,\tau
.
Using the definition of
w_{k}
, cf. Eq (
5
), we further estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{k}(1-u_{k}-m_{k})|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{\Omega}(1-\rho_{k})\frac{|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}}{u_{k}}+2\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla(u_{k}+m_{k})+u_{k}\frac{|\nabla(1-\rho_{k})|^{2}}{1-\rho_{k}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\int_{\Omega}2|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}-2|\nabla u_{k}||\nabla m_{k}|\;\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}-|\nabla m_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Inserting this estimate into the entropy inequality above yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C+\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})\right)+\|\nabla m_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

∎

Before proceeding, let us introduce the piecewise constant interpolations associated to the iterates of the implicit Euler method.
For
v\in\{u,m,w\}
, we define

\displaystyle v_{\tau}(x,t):=v_{k}(x),

whenever
t\in(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
, for all
k\geq 1
and
x\in\Omega
.

Corollary 2.7

(
H^{1}
-estimate for
w_{\tau}
)
.

Let
(u_{k},m_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}\cap(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
and
(w_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
be the associated entropy variables defined in Eq. (
5
). Then

\displaystyle\tau^{1/2}\|w_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C,

where
w_{\tau}
denotes the corresponding piecewise constant interpolation and the constant
C>0
only depends on
\Omega
and
T>0
.

Proof.

From Eq. (
15
) we get

\displaystyle\tau\left(\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|w_{k}\|_{L(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\leq C+\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})\right),

which, upon integrating over
(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
and summing from
k=1,\ldots,N_{T}
, gives

\displaystyle\tau\left(\|\nabla w_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|w_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq CT+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})\right).

Since the sum on the right-hand side is telescopic, the estimate simplifies further to

\displaystyle\tau\left(\|\nabla w_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|w_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\right)\leq CT+\mathcal{E}(u_{0},m_{0})-\mathcal{E}(u_{N_{T}},m_{N_{T}}).

Since
0\leq u_{k},m_{k}\leq 1
and
|\Omega|\leq C
, we obtain the following uniform estimate

\displaystyle\tau^{1/2}\|w_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C,

independent of
\tau>0
.
∎

Lemma 2.8

(Conditional estimate for
\nabla m
)
.

Let
(u_{k},m_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}\cap(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
be the solution to the implicit Euler approximation, Eq. (
4
).
Then, for any
n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the conditional estimate

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla m_{n\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\lVert\nabla m_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}\ell\sum_{{k=0}}^{n-1}2^{n-k}\phi_{k}.

where

\phi_{k}=\sum_{i=k\ell+1}^{(k+1)\ell}\tau\lVert\nabla u_{i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(17)

Proof.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. Recalling Eq. (
6
), we can write, for
r=1,\,\dots,\,\ell,
that

\displaystyle m_{j+r}

\displaystyle=m_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\dfrac{1}{1+\tau\lambda u_{j+i}}.

Taking the gradient of this expression, we find that

\displaystyle\nabla m_{j+r}

\displaystyle=\nabla m_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\dfrac{1}{1+\tau\lambda u_{j+i}}-m_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\dfrac{1}{1+\tau\lambda u_{j+i}}\right)\dfrac{\tau\lambda\nabla u_{j+i}}{(1+\tau\lambda u_{j+i})^{2}}.

Finally, we pass to the norm and obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla m_{j+r}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\nabla m_{j}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\tau\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lVert\nabla u_{j+i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(18)

having used the fact that
0\leq u_{k},m_{k}\leq 1
.
Squaring both sides yields

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq r\leq\ell-1}\lVert\nabla m_{j+r}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert\nabla m_{j}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\lambda^{2}\tau^{2}\ell\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lVert\nabla u_{j+i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(19)

Next, we set
j=n\ell
, upon which the preceding estimate becomes

\displaystyle\sup_{n\ell\leq k\leq(n+1)\ell}\lVert\nabla m_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert\nabla m_{n\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\lambda^{2}\tau^{2}\ell\phi_{n},

(20)

where

\phi_{n}=\sum_{k=n\ell+1}^{(n+1)\ell}\tau\lVert\nabla u_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

If we now consider Eq. (
20
), we can write

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla m_{n\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(n-1)\ell\leq k\leq n\ell}\lVert\nabla m_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 2\lVert\nabla m_{(n-1)\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\lambda^{2}\tau^{2}\ell\phi_{n-1},

and substitute in Eq. (
20
) iteratively to find

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla m_{n\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\lVert\nabla m_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}\ell\sum_{{k=0}}^{n-1}2^{n-k}\phi_{k}.

∎

Let us note that the definition of the quantity
\phi_{k}
already shows that the
H^{1}
-control of
m
depends on the
H^{1}
-control of
u
, hence the name ‘conditional estimate’.
We will use it to derive a uniform
H^{1}
-bound for
u
such that, a fortiori,
m\in H^{1}
, unconditionally.

Lemma 2.9

(
L^{2}
-estimate for
\nabla u_{\tau}
)
.

Let
(u_{k},m_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}\cap(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
be the solution to the implicit Euler approximation, Eq. (
4
), and let
u_{\tau}
be the piecewise constant interpolation associated to
(u_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
.
Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\leq C,

where
C>0
is independent of
\tau>0
.

Proof.

Let us recall Eq. (
16
) in Proposition
2.6
:

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C+\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})\right)+\|\nabla m_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Summing from
k=n\ell+1
to
k=(n+1)\ell
and multiplying by
\tau>0
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n\ell+1}^{(n+1)\ell}\tau\lVert\nabla u_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq C\ell\tau+\sum_{k=n\ell+1}^{(n+1)\ell}\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{k-1},m_{k-1})-\mathcal{E}(u_{k},m_{k})\right)+\sum_{k=n\ell+1}^{(n+1)\ell}\tau\lVert\nabla m_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq C\ell\tau+\left(\mathcal{E}(u_{n\ell},m_{n\ell})-\mathcal{E}(u_{(n+1)\ell},m_{(n+1)\ell})\right)+\sum_{k=n\ell+1}^{(n+1)\ell}\tau\lVert\nabla m_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq C(1+\ell\tau)+\sum_{k=n\ell+1}^{(n+1)\ell}\tau\lVert\nabla m_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

using the fact that the entropy can be bounded since
0\leq u_{k}+m_{k}\leq 1
and the domain is bounded.
Thus, having passed to the supremum on the right-hand side, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n\ell+1}^{(n+1)\ell}\tau\lVert\nabla u_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\ell\tau)+\ell\tau\sup_{n\ell+1\leq k\leq(n+1)\ell}\lVert\nabla m_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Hence, recalling the definition of
\phi
(cf. Eq. (
17
)), we find

\displaystyle\phi_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\ell\tau)+2\ell\tau\left(\lVert\nabla m_{n\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\ell\lambda^{2}\phi_{n}\right),

where we also used Eq. (
19
).
Since both sides contain the term
\phi_{n}
, we rearrange and get

\displaystyle\phi_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C(1+\ell\tau)+2\ell\tau\lVert\nabla m_{n\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{1-2\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}\ell^{2}},

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C(1+\ell\tau)}{1-2\tau^{2}\ell^{2}\lambda^{2}}+\dfrac{2\ell\tau}{1-2\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}\ell^{2}}\lVert\nabla m_{n\ell}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega}^{2},

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C(1+\ell\tau)}{1-2\tau^{2}\ell^{2}\lambda^{2}}+\dfrac{2\ell\tau}{1-2\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}\ell^{2}}\left(2^{n}\lVert\nabla m_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}\ell\sum_{{k=0}}^{n-1}2^{n-k}\phi_{k}\right),

having used Lemma
2.8
. Thus,

\displaystyle\phi_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C(1+\ell\tau)}{1-2\tau^{2}\ell^{2}\lambda^{2}}+\dfrac{2\ell\tau}{1-2\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}\ell^{2}}2^{n}\lVert\nabla m_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\dfrac{2\tau^{3}\lambda^{2}\ell^{2}}{1-2\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}\ell^{2}}\sum_{{k=0}}^{n-1}2^{n-k}\phi_{k}.

Setting
\Delta t:=\ell\tau
for
\ell>1
, the estimate becomes

\phi_{n}\leq\dfrac{C(1+\Delta t)}{1-2{\Delta t}^{2}\lambda^{2}}+\dfrac{2\Delta t}{1-2{\Delta t}^{2}\lambda^{2}}2^{n}\lVert\nabla m_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\dfrac{2\tau{\Delta t}^{2}\lambda^{2}}{1-2{\Delta t}^{2}\lambda^{2}}\sum_{{k=0}}^{n-1}2^{n-k}\phi_{k}.

Hence, for any
\tau>0
, we choose
\ell=\ell(\tau)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Delta t\leq 1/(2\lambda)
and
(\ell+1)\tau\geq 1/(2\lambda)
.
This way, we obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\Delta t)+\tau\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{n-k}\phi_{k}.

Using a discrete version of Gronwall’s lemma, we find that

\sup_{0\leq n\leq N}\phi_{n}\leq Ce^{\sum_{k=1}^{N}2^{N-k}}\leq\bar{C_{1}}<\infty,

where
\bar{C}_{1}>0
.
It is crucial to highlight that
\Delta t\nrightarrow 0
, as
\tau\to 0
.
Thus

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\phi_{n\ell}\leq N\bar{C_{1}}<\infty,

where
N:=\lceil T/\Delta t\rceil
does not go to infinity as
\tau\to 0
, thereby providing the desired uniform bound.
As a result,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{(N+1)l-1}\tau\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\tau\sum_{k=nl}^{(n+1)l-1}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\phi_{nl}\leq C<\infty,

where
C>0
does not depend on
\tau>0
.
∎

Corollary 2.10

(
L^{2}
-estimate for
\nabla m
)
.

Let
(u_{k},m_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}\cap(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
be the solution to the implicit Euler approximation (Eq. (
4
)) and let
m_{\tau}
be the piecewise constant interpolation associated with
(m_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
.
Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla m_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\leq C,

where
C>0
is independent of
\tau>0
.

Proof.

Revisiting Eq. (
18
), we have

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla m_{r}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lVert\nabla m_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\tau^{2}\lambda^{2}r\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lVert\nabla u_{i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C+2\tau\lambda^{2}N_{T}\lVert\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2},

having used the initial bound on
\nabla m_{0}.

Multiplying by
\tau
and summing from
r=0
to
N_{T}
, we get

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{r=0}^{N_{T}}\lVert\nabla m_{r}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(N_{T}+1)\tau\lVert\nabla m_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\lambda^{2}N_{T}(N_{T}+1)\tau^{2}\lVert\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C,

where
C>0
is independent of
\tau.

∎

Next, let us address the time regularity of
u
and
m
. To this end, we introduce the notation

d_{\tau}v:=\dfrac{v(x,t+\tau)-v(x,t)}{\tau},

for
v\in\{u,m\}
.

Lemma 2.11

(Time regularity for
u_{\tau}
,
m_{\tau}
)
.

Let
(u_{k},m_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}\cap(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
be the solution to the implicit Euler approximation (Eq. (
4
)) and let
u_{\tau}
be the piecewise constant interpolation associated with
(u_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
.
Then, there holds

\lVert d_{\tau}u_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime})}\leq C,

and

\lVert d_{\tau}m_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C,

where
C>0
is independent of
\tau>0
.

Proof.

From Eq. (
4a
) and employing the notation above, we see

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}d_{\tau}u_{\tau}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\Big\{\tau{(\nabla w_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\varphi+w_{\tau}\varphi)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\big[(1-u_{\tau}-m_{\tau})\nabla u_{\tau}+u_{\tau}\nabla(u_{\tau}+m_{\tau})\big]\cdot\nabla\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-u_{\tau}(1-u_{\tau}-m_{\tau})\varphi\Big\}\mathrm{d}x,

(21)

for any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Passing to the modulus and using Cauchy-Schwartz, we may estimate

\displaystyle|\langle d_{\tau}u_{\tau},\varphi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\tau\left(\lVert\nabla w_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\left((1-m_{\tau})\nabla u_{\tau}+u_{\tau}\nabla m_{\tau}\right)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert u_{\tau}(1-u_{\tau}-m_{\tau})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

whence

\displaystyle|\langle d_{\tau}u_{\tau},\varphi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\left(\|w_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+C_{1}\left(\lVert\nabla u_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla m_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)+C_{2}\right),

having used the fact that
u_{\tau},m_{\tau}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{A}\cap(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2})
. Upon dividing by
\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
and passing to the supremum,

\displaystyle\lVert d_{\tau}u_{\tau}\rVert_{(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}+C_{1}\|w_{\tau}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+C_{2}\left(\lVert\nabla u_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\nabla m_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Squaring and integrating over
[0,T]
yields

\displaystyle\lVert d_{\tau}u_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\left(1+\|w_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}^{2}+\left(\lVert\nabla u_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\lVert\nabla m_{\tau}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C,

where we used Lemma
2.9
and Corollary
2.10
. In particular, note that the constant
C>0
is independent of
\tau>0
. The regularity result for
d_{\tau}m
follows directly from Eq. (
4b
) and the uniform bounds on
u_{\tau},m_{\tau}
, which completes the proof.
∎

2.3
Existence of weak solutions

Having established the a priori estimates, let us now show the existence of convergent subsequences whose limits we identify as weak solutions in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

The bounds provided by Lemma
2.9
and Corollary
2.10
in conjunction with the Banach-Alaoglu theorem yield the existence of subsequences and two functions
\nabla u,\nabla m\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, such that

•

\nabla u_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\nabla u
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,

•

\nabla m_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\nabla m
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,

where we did not relabel the subsequences. Moreover, by the uniform bounds of Lemma

, Lemma

u_{\tau}\rightarrow u
in

,

•

again, up to subsequences. Finally, from Lemma

•

d_{\tau}u_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}u
in
L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime})
,

d_{\tau}m_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}m
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,

up to a subsequence. Indeed, the limits can be identified as follows. If
d_{\tau}u_{\tau}\to\chi
, then, for
\xi(t)\phi(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\int_{\Omega}d_{\tau}u_{\tau}\xi(t)\phi(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\tau}(t+\tau)-u_{\tau}(t)}{\tau}\xi(t)\phi(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

Upon a change of variables,
s=t+\tau
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\frac{u_{\tau}(t+\tau)-u_{\tau}(t)}{\tau}\xi(t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\int_{\tau}^{T}\frac{u_{\tau}(x,t)}{\tau}\xi(t-\tau)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\frac{u_{\tau}(x,t)}{\tau}\xi(t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\int_{\tau}^{T-\tau}u_{\tau}\frac{\xi(t-\tau)-\xi(t)}{\tau}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\int_{T-\tau}^{T}u_{\tau}(x,t)\xi(t-\tau)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\int_{0}^{\tau}u_{\tau}(x,t)\xi(t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\longrightarrow-\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\int_{0}^{T}u(x,t)\xi^{\prime}(t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x,

i.e.

in the sense of distributions. Replacing

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{\chi}(t)\xi(t)\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}m(t)\xi^{\prime}(t)\mathrm{d}t,

and thus
m^{\prime}=\tilde{\chi}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us revisit Eq. (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}d_{\tau}u_{\tau}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\tau\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi+w\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left((1-m_{\tau})\nabla u_{\tau}+u_{\tau}\nabla m_{\tau}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u_{\tau}(1-u_{\tau}-m_{\tau})\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

First let us note that the term premultiplied by
\tau

2.7

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left((1-m)\nabla u+u\nabla m\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u(1-u-m)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T))
which is dense in

(u,m)
is a weak solution to Eq. (

1.1
. Similarly, we can pass to the limit in the equation for the ECM, Eq. (
1b

,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}m\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}um\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

for any

, having used the same approximation procedure of the test function.

The a priori estimates (

2.9

Finally, the compactness is sufficient to conclude that the weak solution satisfy the initial data, using in addition that

3
A numerical exploration of large ECM degradation rates

In this section, we explore solutions to the system (
1
) numerically, using simulations subject to no-flux boundary conditions and the following initial conditions:

u({\bf{x}},0)=\begin{cases}1,\qquad&$if$\qquad|{\bf{x}}|<1,\\
0,\qquad&$if$\qquad|{\bf{x}}|\geq 1,\end{cases}

(22)

\displaystyle m({\bf{x}},0)=\begin{cases}0,\qquad&$if$\qquad|{\bf{x}}|<1,\\
m_{0},\qquad&$if$\qquad|{\bf{x}}|\geq 1.\end{cases}

(23)

As in
[
13
]
, the system (
1
) is solved in one dimension on the domain
x\in[0,L]
, where
L\geq 200
is chosen sufficiently largely to ensure a full travelling wave type profile had evolved without the impact of boundary conditions.
In two dimensions, we solve the system (
1
) numerically on the domain
{\bf{x}}=(x,y)
where
x,y\in[-5,5]
.
The spatial discretisation used is
\Delta x=0.1
, where we employ the method of lines to discretise physical space before integrating in time using Python’s built-in integrator
scipy.intergrate.solve_ivp
, which uses an explicit Runga-Kutta integration method of order 5 and time step
\Delta t=1.

This specification of
\Delta t,\,\Delta x
satisfies the CFL condition for this system of equations (
1
), given by

\Delta t\leq\dfrac{(\Delta x)^{2}}{2\max\left(\lvert 1-u-m\rvert,\lvert u\rvert\right)}.

The discretisation employed at spatial point
i
is

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigg[D\frac{\partial a}{\partial x}\bigg]\approx\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{2}}\bigg[(D_{i-1}+D_{i})a_{i-1}-(D_{i-1}+2D_{i}+D_{i+1})a_{i}+(D_{i}+D_{i+1})a_{i+1}\bigg].

When simulating the system of equations (
1
) on a fixed domain, subject to zero flux boundary conditions and initial conditions (
22
) and (
23
), we observe solutions in both one and two spatial dimensions that we have a travelling wave type profile, whose far-field conditions are determined by the steady states of the system of equations (
1
):

(u,\,m)=(1,\,0),\,(0,\,m_{0}).

In the travelling wave type profile, the cell density,
u
, decreases monotonically from one to zero, as the cells diffuse into available space and proliferate up to the carrying capacity behind the wave, driving invasion.
The ECM density,
m
, increases monotonically between zero (where it is completely degraded by cells in the same spatial position as the ECM) and
m_{0}
(where no degradation has occurred as no cells are present here yet, ahead of the wave).

One key feature of the profiles in cell and ECM densities is the overlap between the wave fronts.
For low ECM degradation rates, we observe a non-negligible region of overlap in the cell density and ECM profiles.
Alternatively, as
\lambda
increases, the very low density of cells at the front of the non-compactly supported cell density profile (in the exponential tail) have a larger impact on the ECM.
This is because they are able to degrade the ECM locally at a much higher rate and therefore the remaining density of ECM is significantly reduced, resulting in a much smaller overlap in the travelling wave type profiles of cell and ECM densities.
Indeed, in extremely high ECM degradation rates, a gap appears between the cell and ECM density wave fronts, typical of those observed in acid-mediated tumour invasion models, such as those by Gatenby and Gawlinski and others
[
20
,
21
,
30
]
.
It is also clear from Figures
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
that increasing the ECM degradation rate impacts the shape of the ECM density travelling wave type profile.
As
\lambda
increases, the ECM profile displays a sharper transition between zero and
m_{0}
around the wave front.

If we instead consider a setup where the initial condition for the ECM is inhomogeneous across the space ahead of the cells, we observe a less smooth profile in the ECM density.

By examining the ECM (plotted on the left, in red), we can see that small, stochastic differences in the initial ECM density develop into larger regions of disparity in the ECM profile as it is degraded by invading cells, such that the degraded front in the ECM density varies azimuthally during a two dimensional simulation.
In contrast to this, the radial migration of the cell density (plotted on the right, in blue) smooths out the azimuthal variation in the ECM density profile and thus a smooth invading cell population is observed.
This is likely due to the higher cell densities, and a greater impact can be observed when the ECM degradation rate is larger.

To investigate whether this averaging out among the invading cell density is always observed, we returned to one dimension and considered an oscillatory initial condition for the ECM.
When oscillations are small in amplitude or high in frequency, similar behaviour to the random ECM setup in two dimensions was observed -

In contrast, when we simulated an initial condition for the ECM with a larger amplitude and lower frequency of oscillations, we notice that there were constant profile solutions, however the speed of invasion varied through time and space (see Figure

As the ECM degradation rate increases, the differences in the speed of the travelling wave type profiles decreases, and only marginal changes can be observed over time.

As the cells move through regions of low ECM density, the speed of invasion increases, and then, in regions of higher ECM density, the speed of invasion decreases again.

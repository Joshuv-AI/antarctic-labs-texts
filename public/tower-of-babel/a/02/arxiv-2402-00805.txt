HIGHER STATIONARITY AND DERIVED TOPOLOGIES ON 𝒫 κ ​ ( A ) \mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
M. Catalina Torres

Abstract
. Let κ \kappa be a regular limit cardinal, κ ⊆ A \kappa\subseteq A . We study a notion of n n -s-stationarity on 𝒫 κ ​ ( A ) \mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A}) . We construct a sequence of topologies ⟨ τ 0 , τ 1 , … ⟩ \langle\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\dots\rangle on 𝒫 κ ​ ( A ) \mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A}) characterising the simultaneous reflection of a pair of n n -s-stationary sets in terms of elements in the base of τ n \tau_{n} . This result constitutes a complete generalisation to the context of 𝒫 κ ​ ( A ) \mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A}) of Bagaria’s prior characterisation of n n -simultaneous reflection in terms of derived topologies on ordinals.



1.
Introduction

The study of combinatorial properties of
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\lambda:=\{x\subseteq\lambda:|x|<\kappa\}
where
\kappa
denotes an uncountable regular cardinal and
\kappa\leq\lambda
, has a long history. It originated considering natural generalisations of combinatorial properties on an infinite cardinal
\kappa
within the context of large cardinal notions (see
[
15
,
16
,
21
]
). For example, strongly compact and supercompact cardinals can be seen as strengthenings of weakly compact and measurable cardinals, respectively, by extending certain properties from a single cardinal,
\kappa
, to
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\lambda
. This work later lead to the study of various related topics, including ideals, filters, stationary sets, and stationary reflection on
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\lambda
(see, e.g.,
[
17
,
7
,
8
,
13
,
14
,
19
]
). In this paper we investigate some generalisations of reflection properties from the case of
\kappa
to the case of
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\lambda
, with the aim of yielding compelling results that potentially exhibit significantly higher levels of consistency strength.

In
[
1
]
, Bagaria introduced the following iterated notion of stationary reflection for a given limit ordinal
\alpha
:
A\subseteq\alpha
is
0
-stationary
in
\alpha
if and only if it is unbounded in
\alpha
. For
\xi>0
,
A\subseteq\alpha
is
\xi
-stationary in
\alpha
if and only if for every
\zeta<\xi
and every
S

\zeta
-stationary in
\alpha
, there is a limit ordinal
\beta<\alpha
such that
S\cap\beta
is
\zeta
-stationary in
\beta
. A regular cardinal
\kappa
is
\xi
-stationary if and only if
\kappa
is
\xi
-stationary in
\kappa
. It is easy to see that a subset
S\subseteq\alpha
is
1
-stationary in
\alpha
if and only if
S
is stationary in
\alpha
. Thus, an ordinal
\alpha
is
2
-stationary if and only if it reflects stationary sets.
In
[

, Bagaria, Magidor and Sakai showed that in the constructible universe,L, this iterated form of stationarity was tightly related with the notion of indescribability. They proved that in

, a regular cardinal is

-stationary if and only if it is
\Pi_{n}^{1}
indescribable. Later on, in
[
3
]
, Bagaria revisited
n
-stationarity on ordinals, this time in the stronger form of

-simultaneous stationarity. Given a limit ordinal
\alpha
:
A\subseteq\alpha
is
0
-simultaneously-stationary
(
0
-s-stationary for short) in
\alpha
if and only if it is unbounded in
\alpha
. For
\xi>0
,
A\subseteq\alpha
is
\xi
-simultaneously-stationary (
\xi
-s-stationary for short) in

if and only if for every
\zeta<\xi
and every

\zeta

such that

are both

-stationary in
\beta
. As shown in
[
3
]

-simultaneous stationarity is characterised in terms of the non-discreteness of derived topologies on ordinals.

Given a topological space
(X,\tau)
, the Cantor derivative operator is the map
d_{\tau}:\mathcal{P}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(X)
such that

d_{\tau}(A):=\{x\in X:x\text{ is a limit point of }A\text{ in }\tau\}.

For an ordinal
\delta
, the set
\mathcal{B}_{0}:=\{0\}\cup\{(\alpha,\beta):\alpha<\beta\leq\delta\}
constitutes a base for a topology
\tau_{0}
in
\delta
, namely the interval topology. In
[

Bagaria constructed an increasing sequence of topologies

on

by means of successive applications of Cantor’s derivative operator on the topological space

. The sets

are taken as new open sets at step

is defined to be the topology generated by
\mathcal{B}_{\xi+1}:=\mathcal{B}_{\xi}\cup\{d_{\xi}(A):A\subseteq\delta\}
, and

when

Bagaria proved various properties of

acting on sets of ordinals, particularly on simultaneous stationary sets. He showed that for all

,

d_{\xi}(A)=\{\alpha<\delta:A\text{ is }\xi\text{-s-stationary in }\alpha\}.

This result led to two of the main results of his paper:

Theorem 2.11,
[
3
]
. For every
\xi
, an ordinal
\alpha<\delta
is not isolated in the
\tau_{\xi}
topology on

if and only if

-s-stationary in

Theorem 3.1,
[
3
]
. For every

-s-stationary in

if and only if

is a proper ideal.

The investigation into higher stationarity on ordinals of

,

, H. Brickhill, S. Fuchino and H. Sakai proposed a definition of

-stationarity in

, where

. Additionally, they suggested that the concept of indescribability in

). In

, the authors also suggested an investigation into the consistency strength of

-stationarity in

In this paper, our focus lies on exploring the definition of

]
concerning derived topologies on ordinals to the context of

. These results are summarised in Theorems

to the case of

In the last section, we present a sufficient condition for

-stationary and

Notation and framework

Everywhere in the sequel, if not specified otherwise,
\kappa
denotes a regular limit cardinal, and

denotes a set such that

. Recall that

. In

,

,

\subseteq
-cofinal)

(3)

is

\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
iff

\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
.

(4)

is

iff for any

club in
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
,

.

Originally, Jech employed the term
``

"
for definition (1), however, in the present work, we opt for the terminology

is
``
\subseteq
-cofinal
"
or simply

is
``

"
, to avoid confusion with the property that for any

. While in the ordinal case these properties happen to be equivalent, in the context of

The following are some well known facts that can be found easily in the literature
[
17
,
18
,
20
]
. For every

, the sets

x{\uparrow}\mathrel{:=}\{y\in\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A}):x\subseteq y\}\ \text{ and }\ \mathcal{C}_{\kappa}:=\{x\in\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A}):x\cap\kappa\text{ is a cardinal}\}

are club subsets of
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
, recall that for

to be a club we need that

is a limit cardinal. A set

is called directed if for any

, there is a

such that

. A subset

of

is closed if and only if for every directed set

of cardinality

(See

). A set

is unbounded (or closed, or stationary) in the sense of

. Club subsets of
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
generate a filter, the

\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
and we denote its corresponding dual ideal by

.

Finally, recall that, given a topological space
\langle X,\tau\rangle
,

and

,

is a limit point of

in the topology

if and only if for every open set

such that

. Also,
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
is a base for a topology in

if and only if (1)

, and (2) if

.

As mentioned in the introduction, Bagaria
[
3
]
, employed the symbol "

" to represent the Cantor derivative operator, which extracts from a given set the set of its limit points in a specific topology. However, for the sake of our presentation, we adopt the symbol "
\partial
" for this operation. More precisely, for a topological space

and a subset

, we define the operator
\partial_{\tau}:\mathcal{P}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(X)
by

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}(T):=\{x\in T:x\text{ is a limit point of }T\text{ in the topology }\tau\}.

This choice of notation allows us to reserve the symbol "

" for denoting the set of points for where the higher stationary of some

is reflected. Ultimately, our goal is to equate this set with

.

3.
Higher stationary subsets of
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})

Bagaria’s definition of higher stationarity can be understood as an iterative process, akin to the reflection of certain sets. It happens to be precisely an iteration of the notion of stationarity in ordinals, because the following equivalence holds:
S
is a stationary in an ordinal
\alpha
of uncountable cofinality if and only if for all unbounded subset
T
of
\alpha
, there is some
\beta\in S
such that

is unbounded in
\beta
. This is,
S
is a stationary in

if and only if

is
1
-stationary in
\alpha
. Similarly, the notion of

-stationarity in
\alpha
aligns with the notion of reflection of stationary sets in
\alpha

[
3
]
, primarily emerges from a novel approach: the iterative reflection of specific sets.

Reflection of stationary sets in
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
, where
A
is an ordinal, has also been extensively studied by authors such as Jech, Sakai, and Shelah
[
25
,
19
,
14
,
24
,
12
]
. Since any form of reflection in

involves at least two parameters, namely

and

, several definitions have been proposed. The proposal of extending the

made in

]
, to the context of
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}({A})
is, however, quite novel. This approach was recently introduced by H. Brickhill, S. Fuchino, and H. Sakai, as presented in
[

]
. This particular concept will be our focus, as it closely relates to the notion from
[

]

Theoretical Study on Anisotropic Magnetoresistance Effects of Arbitrary Directions of Current and Magnetization for Ferromagnets: Application to Transverse Anisotropic Magnetoresistance Effect
Satoshi Kokado 1 and Masakiyo Tsunoda 2,3

Abstract
We develop a theory of the anisotropic magnetoresistance (AMR) effects of arbitrary directions of current and magnetization for ferromagnets. Here, we use the electron scattering theory with the s s – s s and s s – d d scattering processes, where s s is the conduction electron state and d d is the localized d states. The resistivity due to electron scattering is expressed by the probability density of the d states of the current direction. The d states are numerically obtained by applying the exact diagonalization method to the Hamiltonian of the d states with the exchange field, crystal field, and spin–orbit interaction. Using the theory, we investigate the transverse AMR (TAMR) effect for strong ferromagnets with a crystal field of cubic or tetragonal symmetry. The cubic systems exhibit the fourfold symmetric TAMR effect, whereas the tetragonal systems show the twofold and fourfold symmetric TAMR effect. On the basis of the above results, we also comment on the experimental results of the TAMR effect for Fe 4 N.

2.1
Hamiltonian

To obtain the localized d states,
we give the Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
of the localized d states
of a single atom in a ferromagnet with
orbital energies,
an exchange field,
and a spin–orbit interaction.
The Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
is expressed as

\displaystyle{\cal H}={\cal H}_{\rm orbital}+{\cal H}_{\rm ex}+{\cal H}_{\rm so},

(4)

where

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm orbital}=\sum_{\sigma=+,-}\Bigg(E_{xy}|xy,\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)\rangle\langle xy,\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)|+E_{yz}|yz,\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)\rangle\langle yz,\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)|

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21504pt+E_{xz}|xz,\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)\rangle\langle xz,\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)|

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21504pt+E_{x^{2}-y^{2}}|x^{2}-y^{2},\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)\rangle\langle x^{2}-y^{2},\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)|

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21504pt+E_{3z^{2}-r^{2}}|3z^{2}-r^{2},\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)\rangle\langle 3z^{2}-r^{2},\chi_{\sigma}(\theta,\phi)|\Bigg),

(5)

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm ex}=-{\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}},

(6)

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm so}=\lambda(L_{x}S_{x}+L_{y}S_{y})+\lambda^{\prime}L_{z}S_{z},

(7)

with

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}=(S_{x},S_{y},S_{z}),

(8)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$L$}}=(L_{x},L_{y},L_{z}),

(9)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$H$}}=H\left(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta\right).

(10)

\lambda^{\prime}

The different symbols
\lambda

\lambda^{\prime}

The spin quantum number

5
Comment

On the basis of the above-mentioned results,
we offer a comment on
the experimental results of
the temperature,
T
, dependences of
C_{2}
and
C_{4}
in
TAMR
(\theta)

of Eq. (
2
)
for Fe
4
N.
[
21
]

Here, Fe
4
N is considered to be
a strong ferromagnet.
[
23
,
12
,
14
]

In addition, the parameters in Sect.
4

are appropriate for Fe
4
N.
[
32
]

The noteworthy experimental result is
the enhancement of
|C_{2}|
and
|C_{4}|

with a decrease in temperature for
T\lesssim 35
K,
i.e.,
(i)
C_{2}=0
and
C_{4}=-0.005
at
T\sim 35
K
and (ii)
C_{2}=-0.02
and
C_{4}=-0.01
at
T=5
K.
[
21
]

Result (i)
agrees
fairly well with

C_{2}=0
and
C_{4}=-0.0048
evaluated
for the
d\varepsilon+d\gamma
model
of
D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}/D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}=1.5

with a crystal field of “cubic” symmetry (see Table
2
).
In contrast, result (ii)
corresponds well with

C_{2}=-0.014
and
C_{4}=-0.0096
evaluated
for the
d\varepsilon+d\gamma
model
of
D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}/D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}=1.5

with a crystal field of “tetragonal” symmetry (see Table
3
).
Note here that
this
D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}/D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}
(=1.5)
is comparable to

1.1\lesssim D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}/D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}\lesssim 1.4

evaluated from our theoretical analysis of
the experimental result of the in-plane AMR effect for

T\leq 35
K (see Fig. 11 of Erratum II in Ref.
\citen
Kokado3).
From the above result, we predict that
the tetragonal distortion may proceed
with decreasing temperature for

T\lesssim 35
K.

The tetragonal distortion has already been predicted
also in our analysis of
the experimental results of the in-plane AMR ratio
for Fe
4
N,
[
14
]

where
the experimental results show
the
appearance of
C_{4}

for
T\leq 35
K.
[
7
]

Concretely,
by using the electron scattering theory
based on the second-order perturbation theory,
we showed that
a model with a crystal field of tetragonal symmetry
can reproduce the appearance of
C_{4}
.
[
14
]

On the other hand,
Yahagi
et al.

investigated
the fourfold symmetric AMR effect
for a cubic single-crystal ferromagnetic model
by using
the Kubo formula
and the multi-orbital d-impurity Anderson model,
where
the d states have the spin–orbit interaction and
crystal field of cubic symmetry.
[
24
]

They found that

C_{4}
in the in-plane AMR ratio
appears even for the cubic system
as a result of the fourth-order perturbation
with respect to the spin–orbit interaction.
Here,
the splitting of the d states
due to the spin–orbit interaction
is responsible for the appearance of
C_{4}
.
Furthermore,
by performing the numerical (i.e., nonperturbative) calculation for
TAMR(
\theta
) of the cubic system,
they showed that
C_{4}
appears
by the same mechanism as
the above-mentioned
C_{4}
in the in-plane AMR ratio.
Simultaneously, they confirmed the absence of
C_{2}
in TAMR(
\theta
).
The absence of
C_{2}

is
certainly valid from the viewpoint of the symmetry of the cubic crystal.
In addition,
the absence of
C_{2}

for the cubic system is confirmed also in our present study
based on the exact diagonalization method (see Sect.
4.2.2
).

We emphasize, however, that
the experimental result of TAMR(
\theta
) for Fe
4
N shows

C_{2}\neq 0
for
T\lesssim 35
K.
[
21
]

This
C_{2}\neq 0
is actually obtained
for the tetragonal system
with the finite
\delta
of Eq. (
66
)
in our present study
(see Sect.
4.2.3
).
Therefore, we guess that
the tetragonal distortion may be necessary
to explain
the experimental results of
both TAMR(
\theta
) and the in-plane AMR ratio for Fe
4
N.

6
Conclusion

We developed a theory of
the anisotropic magnetoresistance effects
of arbitrary directions of

I

and

M

for ferromagnets.
Here, we used the electron scattering theory
with
s
–
d
scattering processes.
The
s
–
d
scattering means
that the conduction electron is scattered into the localized d states
by a nonmagnetic impurity.
The resistivity due to the
s
–
d
scattering
directly reflects
the probability density of the d states
of the
I
direction.
The d states are numerically obtained by
applying the exact diagonalization method
to the Hamiltonian of the d states
with the exchange field, crystal field, and spin–orbit interaction.
Using this theory,
we investigated the TAMR effect
for strong ferromagnets
with a crystal field of cubic or tetragonal symmetry.
The cubic system exhibits the fourfold TAMR effect,
whereas
the tetragonal system shows
the twofold and fourfold TAMR effect.
In addition,
the cubic or tetragonal system has

{\rm TAMR}(\theta)\leq 0

for
D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}/D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}<1
,

{\rm TAMR}(\theta)=0
at
D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}/D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}=1
,
and

{\rm TAMR}(\theta)\geq 0

for
D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}/D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}>1
.
We also found
contradictory behavior of TAMR(
\theta
)
between
the
d\varepsilon
model with

D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}\neq 0
and
D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}=0

and
the
d\gamma
model with

D_{\varepsilon,-}^{(d)}=0
and
D_{\gamma,-}^{(d)}\neq 0
.
Such behavior appears under the condition for
the probability density of the d states
of the
I
direction.
Finally, on the basis of
the calculation results,
we commented on the experimental results of
the TAMR effect for Fe
4
N, i.e.,
the enhancement of
|C_{2}|
and
|C_{4}|

with a decrease in temperature.
We predicted that
the tetragonal distortion may proceed
with decreasing temperature.

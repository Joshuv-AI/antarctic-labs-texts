Correlation function and the inverse problem in the B ​ D BD interaction
Hai-Peng Li, Jing-Yu Yi, Chu-Wen Xiao, De-Liang Yao, Wei-Hong Liang, Eulogio Oset

Abstract
We study the correlation functions of the B 0 ​ D + , B + ​ D 0 B^{0}D^{+},B^{+}D^{0} system, which develops a bound state of approximately 40 ​ MeV 40\textrm{ MeV} , using inputs consistent with the T c ​ c ​ ( 3875 ) T_{cc}(3875) state. Then we address the inverse problem starting from these correlation functions to determine the scattering observables related to the system, including the existence of the bound state and its molecular nature. The important output of the approach is the uncertainty with which these observables can be obtained, considering errors in the B 0 ​ D + , B + ​ D 0 B^{0}D^{+},B^{+}D^{0} correlation functions typical of current values in present correlation functions. We find that it is possible to obtain scattering lengths and effective ranges with relative high precision and the existence of a bound state. Although the pole position is obtained with errors of the order of 50 % 50\% of the binding energy, the molecular probability of the state is obtained with a very small error of the order of 6 % 6\% . All these findings can serve as motivation to perform such measurements in future runs of high energy hadron collisions.

If

The finding of the

;

The information and arguments on heavy quark symmetry used in Ref.
Sakai et al. 2017
consolidated their results.
With confidence in the predictions of Ref.
Sakai et al. 2017

First, we reproduce the result of Ref.

Next, assuming that these correlation functions correspond to actual data, we address the inverse problem of determining from them the value of the scattering observables, scattering length and effective range for these two channels; the existence of a bound state below the threshold; the molecular probability of each of the
D^{0}B^{+}

D^{+}B^{0}

II
Formalism

II.1
Interaction of the
D^{0}B^{+}
,
D^{+}B^{0}
channels

The details can be found in Ref.
Sakai et al. 2017
;
hence, we do not cover them here. Light vector meson are exchanged between the
B
and
D
components, as shown in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Vector exchange between the
D^{0}B^{+}
and
D^{+}B^{0}
components (in brackets, the four momentum of each particle).

We can see that the heavy quarks are spectators in the exchange of light vector mesons.
Furthermore, assuming
m_{\rho}=m_{\omega}
, the interaction for the
B^{+}D^{0}(1)
,
B^{0}D^{+}(2)
channels is given by

V_{ij}=-\frac{1}{4f^{2}}\;C_{ij}\;(p_{1}+p_{3})\cdot(p_{2}+p_{4});\quad f=93\ \text{MeV},

(1)

with
C_{ij}
the element of matrix
C
,

C=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\\
\end{pmatrix},

(2)

and projected over
S
-wave

(p_{1}+p_{3})\cdot(p_{2}+p_{4})\to\frac{1}{2}\left[3s-2(m^{2}_{B}+m^{2}_{D})-\frac{(m_{B}^{2}-m_{D}^{2})^{2}}{s}\right].

(3)

The scattering matrix is then given by

T=[1-VG]^{-1}V,

(4)

where
G={\rm diag}(G_{1},G_{2})
, with
G_{i}
being the
BD
meson loop function regularized with a cutoff

G_{i}(\sqrt{s})=\int_{|\vec{q}\,|<q_{\rm max}}\dfrac{{\rm d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\dfrac{\omega_{1}(q)+\omega_{2}(q)}{2\,\omega_{1}(q)\,\omega_{2}(q)}\;\dfrac{1}{s-[\omega_{1}(q)+\omega_{2}(q)]^{2}+i\varepsilon},

(5)

where
\omega_{1}(q)=\sqrt{\vec{q}^{\;2}+m_{i}^{2}}
,
\omega_{2}(q)=\sqrt{\vec{q}^{\;2}+M_{i}^{2}}
, and
m_{i},M_{i}
are the masses of the
D
and
B
mesons in channel
i
.
With the isospin states
(B^{+},B^{0})
,
(D^{+},-D^{0})
, the
I=0
combination is given by

\ket{BD, I=0}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{B^{+}D^{0}+B^{0}D^{+}},

(6)

with which we can obtain

\matrixelement{BD, I=0}{V}{BD, I=0}=\frac{1}{2}(V_{11}+V_{22}+2V_{12})=2V_{11},

(7)

which is the result obtained in Ref.
Sakai et al. 2017
, with
V_{ij}
given by Eqs. (
1
), (
2
), (
3
). The
I=1
combination is given by

\ket{BD, I=1,I_{3}=0}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{B^{+}D^{0}-B^{0}D^{+}}.

(8)

We reproduce the results of Ref.
Sakai et al. 2017
using the cutoff regularization with an extra form factor (Eq. (27) from Ref.
Sakai et al. 2017
)
stemming from the
S
-wave projection of a vector meson exchange.
However, practically identical results are obtained by ignoring this form factor and decreasing
q_{\text{max}}
.
To match the formalism of the correlation functions of Ref.
Vidana et al. 2023
, we ignore this form factor and take
q_{\text{max}}
of the order of
420\textrm{ MeV}
, as used in Ref.
Feijoo et al. 2021
, to obtain the binding of the related
T_{cc}
state.

II.2
Scattering observables

We can evaluate the scattering length
a
,
r_{0}
for the
B^{+}D^{0}
and
B^{0}D^{+}
channels by recalling the relationship between our
T
matrix and that used in quantum mechanics
Gamermann et al. 2010

T=-8\pi\sqrt{s}\;f^{QM}\simeq-8\pi\sqrt{s}\;\frac{1}{-\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{2}r_{0}k^{2}-ik},

(9)

with

k=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}(s,m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})}{2\sqrt{s}},

(10)

from which we easily find

-\frac{1}{a_{i}}=\left.\left(-8\pi\sqrt{s}\;T_{ii}^{-1}\right)\right|_{s_{{\rm th},i}},

(11)

r_{0,i}=\left[\frac{2\sqrt{s}}{\mu_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(-8\pi\sqrt{s}\;T_{ii}^{-1}+ik_{i}\right)\right]_{s_{{\rm th},i}},

(12)

where
\mu_{i}
is the reduced mass in channel
i
, and
s_{{\rm th},i}
and
k_{i}
are the square of the threshold mass and the center of mass momenta of the mesons for channel
i
, respectively.

II.3
Couplings and probabilities

We find that there is a pole, which is below the threshold of the two channels and hence corresponds to a bound state.
The couplings are obtained from the
T
matrix in the vicinity of the pole

T_{ij}=\frac{g_{i}\,g_{j}}{s-s_{p}},

(13)

where
s_{p}
is the square of the mass at the pole. Thus

g_{1}^{2}=\lim_{s\to s_{p}}(s-s_{p})\;T_{11},

(14)

g_{1}\,g_{j}=\lim_{s\to s_{p}}(s-s_{p})\;T_{1j},

(15)

which determine the relative sign of
g_{2}
with respect to
g_{1}
.
Once the couplings are evaluated, we calculate the molecular probabilities of the
B^{+}D^{0}
and
B^{0}D^{+}
channels, as in Refs.
Gamermann et al. 2010
;
Hyodo et al. 2012
;
Sekihara et al. 2015

\mathcal{P}_{i}=-g_{i}^{2}\left.\frac{\partial G_{i}}{\partial s}\right|_{s=s_{p}}.

(16)

Another magnitude of relevance is the wave function at the origin in coordinate space given by
Gamermann et al. 2010

\psi_{i}(r=0)=\left.g_{i}\,G_{i}\right|_{s_{{\rm th},i}}.

(17)

II.4
Correlation functions

We follow the formalism of Ref.
Vidana et al. 2023
and write the correlation functions for the two channels as

\displaystyle C_{B^{+}D^{0}}(p_{D^{0}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+4\,\pi\,\theta(q_{\rm max}-p_{D^{0}})\,\int dr\,r^{2}S_{12}(r)\cdot

(18)

\displaystyle\left\{\left|j_{0}(p_{D^{0}}\,r)+T_{B^{+}D^{0},B^{+}D^{0}}(E)\;\tilde{G}^{(B^{+}D^{0})}(r;E)\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\;\left.+\left|T_{B^{0}D^{+},B^{+}D^{0}}(E)\;\tilde{G}^{(B^{0}D^{+})}(r;E)\right|^{2}-j_{0}^{2}(p_{D^{0}}\,r)\right\},

\displaystyle C_{B^{0}D^{+}}(p_{D^{+}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+4\,\pi\,\theta(q_{\rm max}-p_{D^{+}})\,\int dr\,r^{2}S_{12}(r)\cdot

(19)

\displaystyle\left\{\left|j_{0}(p_{D^{+}}\,r)+T_{B^{0}D^{+},B^{0}D^{+}}(E)\;\tilde{G}^{(B^{0}D^{+})}(r;E)\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\;\left.+\left|T_{B^{+}D^{0},B^{0}D^{+}}(E)\;\tilde{G}^{(B^{+}D^{0})}(r;E)\right|^{2}-j_{0}^{2}(p_{D^{+}}\,r)\right\},

where
p_{i}
is the momentum of the particles in the rest frame of the pair,

p_{i}=\dfrac{\lambda^{1/2}(s,m_{i}^{2},M_{i}^{2})}{2\,\sqrt{s}},

(20)

S_{12}(r)
is the source function,
parameterized as a Gaussian normalized to
1
,

S_{12}(r)=\dfrac{1}{(\sqrt{4\pi}\,R)^{3}}\;e^{-(r^{2}/4R^{2})},

(21)

and the
\tilde{G}^{(i)}(r;E)
function is defined as

\tilde{G}^{(i)}(r;E)=\int\dfrac{{\rm d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\dfrac{\omega_{1}(q)+\omega_{2}(q)}{2\,\omega_{1}(q)\,\omega_{2}(q)}\;\dfrac{j_{0}(q\,r)}{s-[\omega_{1}(q)+\omega_{2}(q)]^{2}+i\varepsilon},

(22)

where
j_{0}(q\,r)
is the spherical Bessel function, and
E=\sqrt{s}=\sqrt{m_{i}^{2}+\vec{p}_{i}^{\;2}}+\sqrt{M_{i}^{2}+\vec{p}_{i}^{\;2}}
.

III
Inverse problem: Model independent analysis of the correlation functions

Here, we assume that the correlation functions have already been measured and attempt to extract the maximum information available using a general framework in which no model assumptions are made.
To perform the test, we use the correlation function with the model described in the previous sections, assuming errors at the order of
\pm 0.02
, which are slightly larger than those obtained in present measurements of correlation functions.

We begin by assuming that there is an interaction between the coupled channels given by

V=\begin{pmatrix}V_{11}&V_{12}\\
V_{12}&V_{22}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(23)

where
V_{ij}
are unknown potentials to be determined, and the
T
matrix is given by Eq. (
4
), using the
G
function of Eq. (
5
) with an unknown
q_{\rm max}
.
We make no assumption on the isospin structure of a possible bound state but assume that the interaction is isospin symmetric, which implies, according to Eqs. (
6
),(
7
), and(
8
), that

\matrixelement{BD, I=0}{V}{BD, I=1}=\frac{1}{2}(V_{11}-V_{22})=0,

(24)

V_{22}=V_{11}.

(25)

In addition, to consider possible sources of interaction originating from channels neglected in our approach, we introduce several energy dependent terms, as discussed in Refs.
Aceti et al. 2014
;
Hyodo 2013
and studied in Refs.
Ikeno et al. 2023
;
Albaladejo et al. 2023b
;
Feijoo et al. 2024
:

V_{11}=V_{11}^{\prime}+\frac{\alpha}{m_{V}^{2}}(s-s_{{\rm th},1}),

(26)

V_{12}=V_{12}^{\prime}+\frac{\beta}{m_{V}^{2}}(s-s_{{\rm th},1}),

(27)

where the factor
m^{2}_{V}
(with
m_{V}=800\textrm{ MeV}
) is introduced to make
\alpha,\beta
dimensionless.
Then we have
4
free parameters for the interaction, as well as
q_{\rm max}
and
R
, which is a total of
6
parameters to fit the two correlation functions.
One must be aware that there are strong correlations between these parameters because the input used to obtain the correlation functions corresponds to an interaction at
I=0
; hence what matters is the combination
V_{11}+V_{12}
.
This means that the values we obtain for the parameters in fits to the pseudodata are not too meaningful, only the values of the observables obtained from them are significant.
In order to deal with these correlations we use the bootstrap or resampling method
Press et al. 1992
;
Efron and Tibshirani 1986
;
Albaladejo et al. 2016
, generating random centroids of the data with a Gaussian distribution weight and performing a large number of fits to the data with the new centroids and same errors.
After each fit, the values of the observables are evaluated and the average and dispersion for each are calculated.

V
Conclusions

We address the problem of evaluating the correlation functions for the
BD
system using input extracted from a successful study of the
T_{cc}(3875)
state.
We take two channels,
B^{0}D^{+}
and
B^{+}D^{0}
, the small mass differences between which induce visible differences in the correlation functions.
The system also develops a bound state of approximately 40 MeV.
Once this is achieved, we address the inverse problem of obtaining the observables associated with this system starting from the correlation functions, assuming errors as in current measurements.
Although we obtain results compatible with those obtained from the original model, an important new result is the uncertainty by which we can obtain the observables of the system.
We obtain the size of the source with a precision of approximately
4\%
.
We also determine that there is a bound state using the data of the correlation functions above the threshold of the channels, although with an uncertainty of approximately
50\%
of the binding energy.
However, remarkably, we can determine the molecular nature of the obtained state with a good precision of approximately
6\%
.
The scattering lengths of the two channels are obtained with good precision and significant values, and the effective ranges are also obtained but with smaller precision.
All these results indicate that the measurement of these correlation functions in the future will allow us to obtain valuable information on the
BD
interaction and the bound states associated with this interaction.
This study could serve as motivation to carry such measurements in the future.

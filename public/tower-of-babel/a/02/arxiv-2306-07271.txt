Area-minimizing surfaces with fractal singular sets: prevalence, moduli space, and refinements on strata
Zhenhua Liu

Abstract
. This paper is a continuation of our work in [ 30 ] , about a conjecture of Almgren on area-minimizing surfaces with fractal singular sets. First, we prove that area-minimizing surfaces with fractal singular sets are prevalent on the homology level on general manifolds. They exist even in metrics arbitrarily close to the flat metric on a torus. Second, we partially determine the moduli space of area-minimizing currents near the ones with fractal singular set that we constructed in this and previous work. Around strata of codimension at least three, the fractal self-intersections deform into transverse immersions under generic perturbations of metric. The result is sharp and we construct concrete example to show that our fractal singularities do not necessarily completely dissolve under generic perturbations. Finally, we improve the results in [ 30 ] to give examples with different strata limiting to each other and refine the first stratum to fractal subsets of finite combinatorial graphs.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\ast
denote the Hodge star on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
. Then for any constant
m
-form
\phi
on , we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{comass}\phi=\operatorname{comass}\ast\phi.

Proof.

Every such
\phi
can be written as a finite sum

\displaystyle\phi=\sum_{j}a_{j}X_{j}^{\ast},

with
a_{j}
nonzero real numbers and
X_{j}
oriented
m
-dimensional coordinate planes.

Let
Y
be any oriented
m
-plane, and
Y^{\perp}
be its orthogonal complement, oriented so that

\displaystyle Y\wedge Y^{\perp}=\mathbb{R}^{m+n}.

This implies that

\displaystyle(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})^{\ast}=Y^{\ast}\wedge(Y^{\perp})^{\ast}.

If use an orthonormal coordinate adapted to
Y,Y^{\perp},
then coordinate calculation yields

\displaystyle\ast Y^{\ast}=(Y^{\perp})^{\ast}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\ast\phi=\sum_{j}a_{j}(Y^{\perp})^{\ast},

by definition of Hodge star. Thus, by the determinant definition of simple forms, we have

\displaystyle\phi(Y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}a_{j}X_{j}^{\ast}(Y)=\sum_{j}a_{j}\langle X_{j}^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\rangle,

\displaystyle\ast\phi(Y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}a_{j}(X_{j}^{\perp})^{\ast}(Y^{\perp})=\sum_{j}a_{j}\langle(X_{j}^{\perp})^{\ast},(Y^{\perp})^{\ast}\rangle=\sum_{j}a_{j}\langle\ast X_{j}^{\ast},\ast Y^{\ast}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}a_{j}\langle\ast X_{j}^{\ast}\wedge\ast\ast Y^{\ast},(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})^{\ast}\rangle=\sum_{j}a_{j}(-1)^{mn}\langle\ast X_{j}^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast},(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})^{\ast}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}a_{j}\langle Y^{\ast}\wedge\ast X_{j}^{\ast},(\mathbb{R}^{m+n})^{\ast}\rangle=\sum_{j}a_{j}\langle Y^{\ast},\ast\ast X_{j}^{\ast}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{mn}\sum_{j}a_{j}\langle X_{j}^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{mn}\phi(Y).

By definition of comass, the result is immediate.
∎

Lemma 2.6

Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{comass}_{g^{a}h^{b}}\phi\leq(\operatorname{comass}_{g}\phi)^{a}(\operatorname{comass}_{h}\phi)^{b}

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{comass}_{g^{a}h^{b}}(\phi)=

\displaystyle 1/\min\{\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{g^{a}b^{b}}|\phi(V)=1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/\min\{(\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{g})^{a}(\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{g})^{b}|\phi(V)=1\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{\min\{\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{g}|\phi(V)=1\}}\bigg)^{a}\bigg(\frac{1}{\min\{\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{h}^{b}|\phi(V)=1\}}\bigg)^{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\operatorname{comass}_{g}\phi)^{a}(\operatorname{comass}_{h}\phi)^{b}.

defined by orthogonal projection into
X_{j}.
It is straightforward to verify that
P_{j}P_{i}=P_{i}P_{j}
(due to orthogonality of the planes) and
P_{j}^{\dagger}=P_{j}
(direct consequence of orthogonal projection). Thus, we have a finite set of commuting Hermitian matrices with real entries. Then spectral theorem and simultaneous diagonlizability results give the orthonormal basis
\{x_{l}\}
.

5.2.
The calibration form

Since constant forms projects down to well-defined smooth forms on
\mathbb{T}^{m+n},
we deduce that
X_{j}^{\ast}=dx_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{j_{d}}
calibrates
\mathbb{T}_{j}.

Equip
N
with an arbitrary smooth metric

h_{N}

. Without loss of generality, we can assume that there exists a smooth domain
U
containing
N_{1},\cdots,N_{k},
so that the normal bundle exponential map of each
N_{j}
maps injectively onto
U.
Let
\omega_{j}
be the volume form of
N_{j}
and
\pi_{j}
the nearest distance projection to
N_{j}.
Define

\displaystyle\phi=\sum_{j}X_{j}^{\ast}\wedge\pi_{j}^{\ast}\omega_{j}.

By construction,
d\phi=0
(since each
X_{j}^{\ast}
is constant and
d\pi_{j}^{\ast}\omega_{j}=\pi_{j}^{\ast}(d\omega_{j})=0.
)

5.3.
Prototype metric

We can write the metric on
\mathbb{T}^{d+c}
as

\displaystyle\delta=\sum_{j}dx_{j}^{2}.

Our aim is to find smooth functions
\langle_{1},\cdots,\langle_{d+c},
so that in the metric

(5.1)

\displaystyle g=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}dx_{j}^{2}+h_{N}.

At any point
p

\displaystyle e_{1}=\lambda^{-1}_{1}\partial_{1},\cdots,e_{d+c}=\lambda^{-1}_{d+c}\partial_{d+c},\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{m+n}

forms an orthonormal basis in
g,
where
\partial_{j}
is the coordinate vector associated with
x_{j},

\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{m+n}
is an arbitrary orthonormal basis of
\Pi_{N}(p),
with
\Pi_{N}
the projection on to the
N
factor. The dual basis is

\displaystyle e_{1}^{\ast}=\lambda_{1}dx_{1},\cdots,e_{d+c}^{\ast}=\lambda_{d+c}dx_{d+c},\nu_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,\nu_{m+n}^{\prime},

where
\nu_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,\nu_{m+n}^{\prime}
is the dual basis to
\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{m+n}
in
N.

Thus we can write

(5.2)

\displaystyle\phi=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\operatorname{comass}_{N}\pi^{\ast}_{j}\omega_{j}\lambda^{-1}_{j_{1}}\cdots\lambda^{-1}_{j_{d}})e_{j_{1}}^{\ast}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{j_{d}}^{\ast}\wedge\frac{\pi^{\ast}_{j}\omega_{j}}{\operatorname{comass}_{N}\pi^{\ast}_{j}\omega_{j}}.

Since
\pi_{j}^{\ast}\omega_{j}
is a simple form, its comass is just its norm, i.e.,

\displaystyle\operatorname{comass}_{N}\pi_{j}^{\ast}\omega_{j}=\sqrt{h_{N}(\pi_{j}^{\ast}\omega_{j},\pi_{j}^{\ast}\omega_{j})}.

Thus, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{\ast}_{j}\omega_{j}}{\operatorname{comass}_{N}\pi^{\ast}_{j}\omega_{j}}=B_{j}^{\ast},

where
B_{j}
is a smooth section of
n
-dimensional planes in
U\subset N.
Thus, if the coefficient for every term in (
5.2
) is precisely
1,
then Lemma
2.5
would imply that
\operatorname{comass}_{g}\phi=1.
Thus, we need to solve the system formed by simultaneously for all
j,

\sum_{l=1}^{d}\log\lambda_{j_{l}}=\log\operatorname{comass}_{N}\pi_{j}^{\ast}\omega_{j}.

Note that if we regard
\log\lambda_{j_{l}}
as unknowns, then this is a linear system. The
j
-th row of the corresponding matrix has
0
in the coordinate direction not in
X_{j}
and
1
in the coordinate directions contained in
X_{j}.
We can explicitly reduce the system to a determined system as follows. First, one can take out all rows with
1
only, then take out all rows with
0
only. (This corresponds to setting
\lambda_{l}=1
for
\partial_{l}
either contained in all
X_{j}
or none of
X_{j}.
Note that for
k\geq 2,
all
\partial_{l}
is contained in at least one
X_{j}.
) Then every column left has one and only one
0
, with
1
in the rest entries, since the orthogonal complements of
X_{j}
are linearly independent. Note that for each column, there exist precisely
c
columns equal to it, again by linear independence of orthogonal complements to
X_{j}.
Then one can replace all equal columns with a single column. (This means that if
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{c}}
spans the orthogonal complement to
X_{j}
, then we set
\lambda_{i_{1}}=\cdots=\lambda_{i_{c}}.
) After all the operations, we are left with a matrix consisting of nonequal columns that have only one
0
entry and all the rest entries are
1.
Moreover, there are no rows with only
1
. Otherwise, revert the operations, we would get an
X_{j},
whose orthogonal complement intersects the orthogonal complement of other
X_{i},
a contradiction. This implies that each row consists of only one entry of
0
and all other entries are one. Thus, up to rearranging columns and rows, the matrix can be written as
vv^{T}-\textnormal{id},
with
v=(1,1,\cdots,1).
By direct calculation or the matrix determinant lemma, we deduce that
\det(vv^{T}-\textnormal{id})=k-1.
Thus, the reduced system is a determined linear system and has a unique solution. Then we just revert back the operations to get our
\{\lambda_{l}\}.
The process gives
c\log\lambda_{l}
either
0
or depending linearly on
\operatorname{comass}_{N}\pi_{j}^{\ast}\omega_{j}.
Thus, we can ensure that
\lambda_{j}
is smoothly defined on
\mathbb{T}^{d+c}\times U\subset\mathbb{T}^{d+c}\times N.

To sum it, we have constructed a smooth metric
g
and a closed form
\phi,
both defined on
\mathbb{T}^{d+c}\times U,
that calibrate
T=\sum_{j}\mathbb{T}_{j}\times N_{j}.

Gluing constructions to finish the proof

Just apply Lemma 2.7 in
[
31
]
and we are done.

6.1.
Transition of special Lagrangian form

First, let us do a transition construction between the special Lagrangian form and the form we use.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists a smooth metric
\overline{g}
and a smooth calibration form
\overline{\psi}

-axis, so that up to changing the

We will give the proof in the following two subsubsections. One involves modifying the form to delete or create small

6.1.1.
Step 1:creating and deleting

We will show how to do the construction on

for
x_{3}>t_{2}+\frac{1}{2}(t_{3}-t_{2}).
Define

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}=

\displaystyle d\bigg(x_{3}(dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}-dy_{1}\wedge dy_{2})-y_{3}\sigma(dx_{1}\wedge dy_{2}+dy_{1}\wedge dx_{2})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}-dy_{1}\wedge dy_{2})\wedge dx_{3}-\sigma(dx_{1}\wedge dy_{2}+dy_{1}\wedge dx_{2})\wedge dy_{3}

\displaystyle-y_{3}\sigma^{\prime}(dx_{1}\wedge dy_{2}+dy_{1}\wedge dx_{2})\wedge dx_{3}.

By construction

By 4.2 The torus Lemma in
[
18
]
, the comass of

\displaystyle\max_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3}\in[-\pi,\pi)}\overline{\psi}\bigg((\cos\tau_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}+\sin\tau_{1}\partial_{y_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\cos\tau_{3}\partial_{x_{3}}+\sin\tau_{3}\partial_{y_{3}})\bigg).

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}\bigg((\cos\tau_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}+\sin\tau_{1}\partial_{y_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\cos\tau_{3}\partial_{x_{3}}+\sin\tau_{3}\partial_{y_{3}})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\cos\tau_{3}-\sigma\sin(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\sin\tau_{3}-y_{3}\sigma^{\prime}\sin(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\cos\tau_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\cos\tau_{3}-\sin(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})(\sigma\sin\tau_{3}+y_{3}\sigma^{\prime}\cos\tau_{3})

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\cos\tau_{3})^{2}+(\sigma\sin\tau_{3}+y_{3}\sigma^{\prime}\cos\tau_{3})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\cos\tau_{3})^{2}+\sigma^{2}(\sin\tau_{3})^{2}+2y_{3}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\sin\tau_{3}\cos\tau_{3}+(y_{3}\sigma^{\prime})^{2}(\cos\tau_{3})^{2}}

\displaystyle\sqrt{(1+(y_{3}\sigma^{\prime})^{2}-\sigma^{2})\frac{\cos(2\tau_{3})+1}{2}+y_{3}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\sin(2\tau_{3})+\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1+(y_{3}\sigma^{\prime})^{2}-\sigma^{2}}{2}\cos(2\tau_{3})+y_{3}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\sin(2\tau_{3})+\frac{1+(y_{3}\sigma^{\prime})^{2}+\sigma^{2}}{2}}

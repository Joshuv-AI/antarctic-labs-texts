1.
Introduction

The study of the unavoidable substructures in graphs of high chromatic number is a well known area of graph theory.
A graph can have high chromatic number due to the presence of a large clique so it is natural to ask for which families
\mathcal{H}
of graphs does there exist a function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
with the property that any graph with clique number at most
k
and chromatic number at least
f(k)
must contain an induced subgraph isomorphic to a graph
H\in\mathcal{H}
.

More generally, let
\mathcal{H}
be a hereditary class of graphs then we say
\mathcal{H}
is
\chi
-bounded if there is a function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that for every
G\in\mathcal{H}
satisfies
\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
, where
\omega(G)
is the order of the largest clique in
G
. Finally we say
\mathcal{H}
is polynomially
\chi
-bounded if
f
can be taken to be a polynomial.

As usual, given a family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
, we say
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free if there is no induced subgraph of
G
isomorphic to a graph in
\mathcal{F}
.
A classical result of Erdős states that all finite families
\mathcal{F}
for which the class of
\mathcal{F}
-free graphs is
\chi
-bounded must contain a tree and it is a long standing open conjecture due Gyárfás
[
13
]
and independently by Sumner
[
28
]
that the converse holds. Namely, that for every
T
, the family of
T
-free graphs is
\chi
-bounded.

When the family of excluded graphs is not finite the situation seems to be more delicate. A remarkable result of Scott and Seymour
[
23
]
(building on previous work with Chudnovsky and Spirkl
[
6
,
7
]
) confirms in a very strong form several conjectures of Gyárfás
[
14
]
. It says that for every
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
the family of graphs without an induced cycle of length congruent with
m
(modulo
n
) is
\chi
-bounded. One might then be tempted to conjecture that the class of graphs avoiding all induced subdivisions of a graph
H
is
\chi
-bounded but this is false as shown by Pawlik, Kozik, Krawczyk, Lasoń, Micek, Trotter, and Walczak
[
22
]
who constructed for every
k\geq 2
a family of line segments in the plane for which its
intersection graph
is triangle-free and has chromatic number greater than
k
. It is not hard to see that any
proper
(i.e. all subdivided paths have length at least
2
) induced subdivision of a non-planar graph cannot be represented as an intersection graph of line segments in the plane.

A lovely result of Briański, Davies, and Walczak
[
4
]
(extending ideas of Carbonero, Hompe, Moore, and Spirkl
[
5
]
) shows there are
\chi
-bounded families of graphs for which the growth rate of
f
is arbitrarily high which sparks the broader question of when a
\chi
-bounded family is polynomially
\chi
-bounded. This question has attracted a lot of attention (see e.g.
[
25
]
) but a full classification seems to be out of reach.
Somewhat surprisingly, we do not even know whether the family of graphs avoiding an induced fixed path of length
t\geq 5
is polynomially
\chi
-bounded (as noted by Trotignon and Pham
[
29
]
).

A similar notion to
\chi
-boundedness, but with average degree instead of chromatic number was recently introduced. Intuitively, while a class is
\chi
-bounded if cliques are the only thing that can force large chromatic number, it is “degree-bounded” if, instead, balanced bicliques are the only thing that can force large average degree.
Formally, we say that a hereditary family
\mathcal{F}
is
degree-bounded
if there exists a function
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that for every
G\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
d(G)\leq g(\tau(G))
, where we write
d(G)
for the average degree of
G
and
\tau(G)
for the
biclique number
of
G
, which is the largest integer
s
so that
G
has a (not necessarily induced) copy of
K_{s,s}
. Any such function
g
is called a
degree-bounding function
for the class.

An important result in this area due to Kühn and Osthus
[
18
]
states that for every graph
H
, the class of graphs which do not contain an induced subdivision of
H
is degree-bounded.

Theorem 1.1

(Kühn and Osthus)
.

For every graph
H
and integer
s
, there is an integer
p(s,H)
such that every graph
G
without a
K_{s,s}
and with average degree at least
p(s,H)
contains an induced subdivision of
H
.

Their bounds for
p(s,H)
are roughly triply exponential in
s
, for fixed
H
. A natural conjecture raised by Bonamy et al.
[
2
, Conjecture 33]
asserts that actually
p(s,H)
could be taken to be a polynomial in
s
. Some partial results towards this conjecture are known. Indeed, Scott, Seymour, and Spirkl
[
26
]
established a quantitative strengthening of a result of Kierstead and Penrice
[
16
]
, by showing that the class of
T
-induced-free graphs is polynomially degree bounded (which of course implies a polynomial upper bound for
p(s,T)
). This in turn generalized another theorem of Bonamy, Bousquet, Pilipczuk, Rzążewski,
Thomassé and Walczak
[
2
]
, who proved the same result when
T
is a path.

Our first theorem confirms this conjecture, in a very strong form.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each integer
h
, and all large
s
the following holds.

Let
G
be a graph with
d(G)\geq s^{500h^{2}}
. Then
G
either contains a (not necessarily induced)
K_{s,s}
or an induced proper subdivision of
K_{h}
. In fact, we ensure this subdivision is balanced (meaning each edge of
K_{h}
is replaced by a path of some common length,
\ell
).

By taking a random graph
G\sim G(N,p)
on
N:=s^{h^{2}/100}
vertices (for
1
1

1

Occasionally we will write ‘
x\ggg y
’ to informally say that
x
is sufficiently big compared to
y
.

s\ggg h
) with
p=1-\frac{h^{2}\log s}{s}\geq 1/2
, we have with positive probability that
d(G)\geq s^{h^{2}/100}/4
and
G
does not contain a
K_{s,s}
nor an independent set of size
h^{2}/4
(which implies there is no proper induced subdivision of a
K_{h}
). This shows the theorem is tight as a function of
s
. It is also clear that any proper induced subdivision of a
K_{h}
contains an induced subdivision of every graph
H
with
|H|\leq h
.

In other words, Theorem
1.2
says that the class of graphs without an induced subdivision of a fixed graph
H
is degree-bounded with a polynomial degree-bounding function. It is therefore natural to ask whether the same phenomenon holds for every such class i.e. whether every degree-bounded class of graphs has a polynomial degree-bounding function. In a very recent paper
[
10
]
the authors together with Du, McCarty and Scott proved that every degree-bounded family of graphs is essentially exponentially degree-bounded very much in contrast to the
\chi
-boundedness case.
More precisely, it was shown that for every hereditary degree-bounded class of graphs
\mathcal{F}
, there exists a constant
C_{\mathcal{F}}
so that
(C_{\mathcal{F}})^{s^{3}}
is a degree-bounding function for
\mathcal{F}
.

Our strongest result establishes a polynomial bound for the degree-bounding function of every degree-bounded hereditary class of graphs which follows straightforwardly from the following stronger statement.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix any
k\geq 2
and suppose
s
is sufficiently large. Any graph
G
with average degree
\geq s^{5000k^{4}}
either contains a
K_{s,s}
(not necessarily induced) or an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
with no
C_{4}
and
d(G)\geq k
.

Remark
.

The exponent ‘
5000k^{4}
’ is of roughly the right shape (being polynomial in
k
). Indeed, by considering
G\sim G(s^{k^{2}/100},1-1/s^{9/10})
for large enough
s
, one gets that the exponent must be at least
k^{2}/100
.

We turn now to a recent question asked by James Davies
[
9
]
on
\chi
-boundedness.

Problem 1.4
.

For
t\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{C}
be the family of graphs of without an induced subdivision of
K_{2,t}
(not necessarily proper). Is this family polynomially
\chi
-bounded?

An affirmative answer follows as a simple corollary of Theorem
1.2
. Indeed, let
\omega\coloneqq\omega(G)
. We shall show that for every
G\in\mathcal{C}
,
d(G)={(t\omega)}^{O(t^{2})}
. Observe that if
G
contains a
K_{\omega+1,{(t\omega(G))}^{t}}
then by Ramsey’ Theorem, we must have an independent edge joined to an independent set of size
t
which forms an induced
K_{2,t}
. If not, applying Theorem
1.2
with
H=K_{2,t}
and
s=(t\omega)^{t}
, we must have an induced subdivision of
K_{2,t}
, as required.

We can actually show a much stronger result which answers Problem
1.4
with essentially tight bounds.

As before, we say a subdivision is proper if every edge is subdivided at least once and further we say a subdivision is balanced if all paths have the same length.

Theorem 1.5
.

For every
s,t\in\mathbb{N}
with
s\leq t
, the following holds for all large
k
. Let
G
be a graph with
d(G)\geq k^{4000t}
, then either:

•

G
contains a
k
-clique;

•

G
contains an induced proper balanced subdivision of
K_{h}
, with
h\geq d(G)^{1/(10^{9}s)}
;

•

G
contains an induced
K_{s,t}
.

Remark
.

We did not try to optimize the constants ‘
4000
’ and ‘
1/10^{9}
’, here (nor the constants ‘
500
’ and ‘
5000
’ from our last two theorems).

We note that this result is
essentially
sharp (up to the constant in the exponent) when
k\ggg t
. Indeed, if
G
is a graph without
k
-cliques or independent sets of size
t
, then it will also lack an induced proper subdivision of
K_{t}
; and a simple probabilistic construction shows that there exist such
G
with average degree
\geq k^{\Omega(t)}
(cf.
[
10
, Proof of Corollary 6.9]
). Meanwhile, if
G
does not contain
K_{s,s}
as a subgraph, then it is
K_{2s}
-free and
K_{s,s}
-free, and if it lacks independent sets of size
h
it will lack a proper subdivision of
K_{h}
; another probabilistic construction shows that there are such graphs with average degree
\geq h^{\Omega(s)}
(cf.
[
10
]
).

Also, the above result becomes false if we remove either of the first two bullets (by either taking
G
to be an arbitrarily large clique, or a graph with no
C_{4}
of arbitrarily large degree). Moreover, if we removed the last bullet, then we know that
G
could be triangle-free with arbitrarily high average degree and without an induced proper subdivision of
K_{5}
(due to
[
22
]
).

Remark
.

In independent contemporaneous work
[
3
]
, Bourneouf, Bucić, Cook and Davies have obtained some results that overlap with those proved here. Firstly, they proved a version of Theorem
1.2
(cf.
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
), but the dependence on
H
is not established. Secondly, they showed a reduction (cf.
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
) which made some partial progress towards our main theorem (Theorem
1.3
).
Lastly, Lemma
7.1
, a key lemma of ours, is a quantitatively explicit version of
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
.

While both this paper and
[
3
]
build upon previous techniques of
[
10
]
, the intermediate steps are quite different.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout this paper, for positive integer
n
we write
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Given a multigraph
G
, we denote
d(G)
to be the average degree of
G
(i.e.
d(G)\coloneqq\frac{2|E(G)|}{|G|}
). Similarly for
s
-uniform multihypergraphs
\mathcal{H}
we write
d(\mathcal{H}):=\frac{s|E(G)|}{|V(\mathcal{H})|}
.

For a family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
, we say
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free if for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
,
G
does not contain any copy of
F
as an induced subgraph.
As usual, we denote
\omega(G)
to be the size of the largest clique in
G
and
e(G):=|E(G)|
. Moreover, for a subset
B\subset V(G)
, we let

d_{B}(x)=|N_{B}(x)|
where
N_{B}(x)=\{y\in B:y\in N(x)\}
. Finally, we denote
G[A,B]
to be the bipartite graph induced between
A
and
B
.

2.2.
Tools

We shall need the classical Ramsey’s Theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

R(s,k)\leq\binom{s+k}{s}=O_{s}(k^{s})
.

Next we recall a standard consequence of dependent random choice.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be graph with
n
vertices, and consider some integer
s\geq 1
.

If
\#(x\in V(G):d(x)\geq n^{1-1/100s})\geq\sqrt{n}
, then
G
has a set
S
of
n^{1/3}-1
vertices so that any
s
-subset
S^{\prime}\subset S
has a common neighborhood of size at least
n^{0.9}
.

Proof.

Let
S_{*}:=\{x\in V(G):d(x)\geq n^{1-1/100s}\}
. Now pick vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{10s}\sim V(G)
uniformly at random (independently).

Take
S_{0}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{10s}N(v_{i})
. For
x\in S_{*}
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(x\in S_{0})=(d(x)/n)^{10s}\geq n^{-1/10}
, thus
\mathbb{E}[|S_{0}|]\geq|S_{*}|n^{-1/10}\geq n^{1/3}
. Meanwhile, we have that

\mathbb{E}[\#(x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\in S_{0}^{s}:|N(x_{1})\cap\dots\cap N(x_{s})|\leq n^{0.9})]\leq n^{s}(n^{0.9}/n)^{10s}=1.

Deleting a vertex from such bad
s
-tuple gives the result.
∎

Putting these together yields the following supersaturation result.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
t\geq s\geq 2
. For all sufficiently large
k
with respect to
s,t
the following holds.
Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices which is
K_{s,t}
-free and contains no
K_{k}
. Then,
G
has at least
\Omega_{s}(n^{s})
independent sets of size
s
, provided
n\geq k^{100t}
.

Proof.

If
\frac{e(G)}{\binom{n}{2}}\leq 1/s^{2}
, then sampling a
s
-subset
S\in\binom{V(G)}{s}
uniformly at random, we will have that

\mathbb{E}[e(G[S])]\leq\frac{\binom{s}{2}}{s^{2}}<1/2.

Thus, with probability
\geq 1/2
,
S
is an independent set. This implies that there are at least
\frac{1}{2}\binom{n}{s}\geq\frac{n^{s}}{3(s!)}
independent sets (assuming
n
is sufficiently large).

Now suppose this was not the case. Then, for sufficiently large
n
, we must have that
e(G)\geq 2n^{2-1/100s}
. This easily implies that
\#(x\in V(G):d(x)\geq n^{1-1/100s})\geq\sqrt{n}
. Applying Lemma
2.2
, we can find
S\subset V(G)
with
|S|\geq n^{1/4}
, so that every
s
-subset
S^{\prime}\subset S
has a common neighborhood of size at least
\sqrt{n}
.

Meanwhile, by Ramsey’s Theorem, and the assumption
n\geq k^{100t}
, we have that every set of
n^{1/4}
vertices either has a
k
-clique or independent set of size
t
. Thus, we can find an independent set
I\subset S
of size
s\leq t
(since
G
is assumed to be
K_{k}
-free). Then,
|N(I)|\geq\sqrt{n}
, so we can find an independent set
J\subset N(I)
of size
t
. But then
G[I\cup J]\cong K_{s,t}
, contradicting the assumption that
G
was
K_{s,t}
-free.
∎

3.
Auxiliary cleaning lemmas

3.1.
Key dichotomy

We require a lemma from
[
15
]
. We recreate their proof for completeness.

Definition 3.1
.

We say a bipartite graph
\Gamma=(A,B,E)
is
L
-almost-biregular if:
d_{\Gamma}(a)\leq L|E|/|A|
for all
a\in A
, and
d_{\Gamma}(b)\leq L|E|/|B|
for all
b\in B
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
15
, Lemma 3.7]
)
.

Let
\Gamma=(A,B,E)
be
L
-almost-biregular. Then
\Gamma
has an induced subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
with with
d(\Gamma^{\prime})\geq d(\Gamma)/4
and
\Delta(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq 24Ld(\Gamma^{\prime})
.

Proof.

If
E=\varnothing
, there is nothing to prove (we may take
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma)
. Supposing otherwise, we now have that
L\frac{|E|}{|A|},L\frac{|E|}{|B|}\geq 1
.

We may assume
|A|\leq|B|
. Let
p=|A|/|B|
. We take a random subset
B^{\prime}\subset B
by adding each
b\in B
independently with probability
p
. We take
A^{\prime}\subset A
to be the set of
a\in A
where
|N_{\Gamma}(v)\cap B^{\prime}|\leq 1+2p(d_{\Gamma}(v)-1)
. We shall take
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]
, and show this works with positive probability.

By construction, we have
\Delta(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq 1+2L\frac{|E|}{|B|}\leq 3L\frac{|E|}{|B|}
. Indeed, each vertex
a\in A^{\prime}
has degree at most
1+2pL|E|/|A|\leq 3L|E|/|B|
, and each vertex
b\in B^{\prime}
has degree at most
d_{\Gamma}(b)\leq L|E|/|B|
.

Consider any
e=ab\in E
, and let
U=(N_{\Gamma}(a)\setminus\{b\})\cap B^{\prime}
. Applying Markov’s inequality, we see that

\mathbb{P}(a\in A^{\prime}|b\in B^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}(|U|\leq 2\mathbb{E}[|U|])>1/2.

It follows that

\mathbb{E}[e(\Gamma^{\prime})]=\sum_{e=ab\in E}\mathbb{P}(b\in B^{\prime})\mathbb{P}(a\in A^{\prime}|b\in B^{\prime})>p|E|/2.

Also, it is obvious that
\mathbb{E}[|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|]\leq|A|+\mathbb{E}[|B^{\prime}|]=2|A|
.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[4e(\Gamma^{\prime})-\frac{|E|}{|B|}(|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|)]

\displaystyle>4(p|E|/2)-\frac{|E|}{|B|}2|A|=0.

Consequently, we can choose
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
such that the LHS is positive, and so
\Gamma^{\prime}
is non-empty with

d(\Gamma^{\prime})\geq 2(\frac{|E|}{4|B|})\geq d(\Gamma)/4,

and, as
\Delta(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq 3L|E|/|B|
,

12Le(\Gamma^{\prime})\geq\Delta(\Gamma^{\prime})(|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|)

which implies that
\Delta(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq 24Ld(\Gamma^{\prime})

as desired.
∎

Lemma
3.2
allows us to ‘bootstrap’ almost-regular graphs as follows.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
L\geq 2
be a constant and let
G
be a
n
-vertex graph with average degree
d(G)\geq d
. Furthermore, suppose that
\Delta(G)\leq Ld
. Then
G
has an induced subgraph
H\subset G
where
\Delta(H)=O(d(H)\cdot\log^{2}(L))
and
d(H)=\Omega(\frac{d}{\log^{2}(L)})
.

Remark
.

We may and will assume
L
is large enough so that
2\log(L)\geq\log(L)+\log\log(L)+7
(recall that all logarithms are in base
2
).
This is simply a straightforward modification of an argument that appeared in the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 3.4]
. We need a slightly broader range of parameters for the current paper, so we have presented them here.

This result will be black-boxed to prove our key “dichotomy result” (Lemma
3.4
), which is a minor generalization of
[
10
, Lemma 3.4]
. Slightly sharper versions of Lemma
3.3
may be possible by using more involved choices of parameters, in which case we hope the more streamlined presentation will be helpful in future applications.

Proof.

First, we may and will assume
\delta(G)\geq d/2
and that
G
is
d
-degenerate, otherwise we may pass to a subgraph with higher average degree. Write
V:=V(G)
.

We now split the vertices in
V
into
V_{i}=\{x\in V:2^{i-2}d\leq d(x)<2^{i-1}d\}
, for
i\in[\log(L)+1]
.
By pigeonhole principle, there is some
i\in[\log(L)+1]
, for which

\sum_{x\in V_{i}}d(x)\geq\frac{dn}{\log(L)+1}\geq\frac{dn}{2\log(L)}.

We fix one such
i
.

For technical reasons, we shall take a random subset of
V_{i}
uniformly at random. Let
F\subset V_{i}
be such a random set.
Moreover, let
F^{\prime}\subset F
be the set of vertices
x\in F
which send at least
d(x)/2
edges to
V\setminus F
.
It is easy to see that
\mathbb{P}[x\in F^{\prime}]\geq\mathbb{P}[x\in F]/2=\frac{1}{4}
. Hence, we may fix an outcome where
|F^{\prime}|\geq\mathbb{E}[|F^{\prime}|]\geq|V_{i}|/4
and therefore
e(F^{\prime},V\setminus F^{\prime})\geq\frac{\sum_{x\in V_{i}}d(x)}{2\cdot 4}\geq\frac{nd}{16\log(L)}
.

Now, because
G
is
d
-degenerate, we can find some
F^{\prime\prime}\subset F^{\prime}
such that
\Delta(G[F^{\prime\prime}])\leq 4d

The proof of our main result shall be split into two cases. Either we can find an induced subgraph of

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

Consider an

(1)

(2)

There is an induced subgraph

where
\Delta(H)=O(d(G[H])\cdot\log^{2}(L))
and

Proof.

Let
A_{\text{heavy}}=\{x\in V(G)|d(x)\geq Ld\}

2
2

2

Perhaps a more apt term is ‘not-too-abnormally-heavy’, rather than ‘light’, but we opt for the latter term for the sake of brevity.

A_{\text{light}}=V(G)\setminus A_{\text{heavy}}

3.2.
The subdichotomy

Now, in the case where we are almost-regular we show how to further clean our graph.

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix a graph
F
and
\varepsilon,\delta>0
with
\varepsilon<\delta/2
. Suppose that
d
is sufficiently large with respect to
\varepsilon,\delta
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
d(G)\geq d
and
\Delta(G)\leq d^{1+\varepsilon}
. Furthermore, suppose that
G
has less than
nd^{|V(F)|-1-\delta}
copies of
F
(not necessarily induced). Then
G
contains an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
which does not contain
F
as a subgraph, such that
d(G^{\prime})\geq d^{\delta/(10|V(F)|)}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of subgraphs
F^{\prime}\subset G
with
F^{\prime}\cong F
. Also for convenience write
v_{0}:=|V(F)|
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of pairs
(e,F^{\prime})
such that
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
and
e\in E(G)
is an edge with exactly one vertex in
V(F^{\prime})
. Clearly, we have
|\mathcal{S}|\leq v_{0}d^{1+\varepsilon}|\mathcal{C}|<v_{0}nd^{v_{0}-\delta/2}
.

Now set
\delta^{\prime}:=\frac{\delta}{5v_{0}}
, and consider a random set
V^{\prime}\subset V(G)
where each vertex is included with probability
p:=d^{\delta^{\prime}-1}
(independently). We let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the set of
F\in\mathcal{F}
where
V(F)\subset V^{\prime}
.

Finally we consider

X:=\mathbb{E}[e(G[V^{\prime}])],\quad Y:=\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|],\quad Z:=\mathbb{E}[\#\{(e,F^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}:e\in E(G[V^{\prime}])\text{ and }F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\}].

Let
G^{\prime}
be the induced subgraph on vertex set
V^{\prime}\setminus\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}V(F)
. It is clear that
e(G^{\prime})\geq X-\binom{v_{0}}{2}Y-Z
, and that
G^{\prime}
will not have
F
as a subgraph.

Now, we simply observe that

\mathbb{E}[X]\geq n(d/2)p^{2}=\frac{1}{2}d^{\delta^{\prime}}(np),\quad\mathbb{E}[Y]=|\mathcal{F}|p^{v_{0}}\leq d^{v_{0}\delta^{\prime}-\delta}(np)\quad{\text{ and }}\quad\mathbb{E}[Z]=|\mathcal{S}|p^{v_{0}+1}\leq v_{0}d^{(v_{0}+1)\delta^{\prime}-\delta/2}(np).

Thus (noting that
(v_{0}+1)\delta^{\prime}<\delta/3
, and assuming
d
is sufficiently large),
Y,Z
become lower order terms and we get

\mathbb{E}[X-\binom{v_{0}}{2}Y-Z]\geq d^{\delta^{\prime}/2}(np)=d^{\delta^{\prime}/2}\mathbb{E}[|V^{\prime}|],

whence there is some outcome where
e(G^{\prime})\geq d^{\delta^{\prime}/2}|V(G^{\prime})|
, giving us our desired subgraph.
∎

4.
Using the subdichotomy

In this short section, we collect some useful corollaries of our cleaning lemmas. These will allow us to pass to a favorable outcome if the edges are noticeably concentrated in some local area of
G
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
and assume
d
is sufficiently large with respect to
\varepsilon
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with

which does not have any
C_{4}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{4}
be the set of

. If

.
Next, since

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
, and suppose

is sufficiently large with respect to
\varepsilon
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph, without an independent set of size

either has an induced subgraph

on

By Túran’s Theorem, we may assume

, otherwise we have an independent set of size

. Applying the dichotomy from Lemma

with

, whence the first outcome cannot happen, which means we can pass to an induced subgraph

5.
Finding and using subdivisions

5.1.
A reduction

Definition 5.1
.

Given a multihypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
, we define the
1
-subdivision of
\mathcal{H}
to be the bipartite graph
\Gamma
with bipartition
A,B
, having bijections
\phi_{A}:A\to E,\phi_{B}:B\to V
, so that

N_{\Gamma}(a)=\{b\in B:\phi_{B}(b)\in\phi_{A}(a)\}

for each
a\in A
.

By definition, it is obvious that the following property holds.

Observation 5.2
.

Let
G
be the
1
-subdivision of a multihypergraph
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a subhypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
(obtained by removing some edges and vertices), then the
1
-subdivision of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph of
G
.

This is quite helpful, since it allows us to reduce problems about finding induced subgraphs in
G
into problems about finding (not necessarily induced) subgraphs inside the auxiliary graph
H
(which are understood much better).

We show now how to find a
1
-subdivision of a
simple

1
-subdivision of an
s
-uniform hypergraph of high enough average degree, provided the hyperedges do not clump too much.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

and

be a

-subdivision of a
s
-uniform multihypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, with

. Suppose

-free, then
H
contains an induced subgraph
H^{\prime}
which is a
1
-subdivision of a simple graph
G
with average degree
\Omega_{s,t}(d^{1/(s-1)})

Proof.

We will first remove some hyperedges from
\mathcal{H}
, to obtain a “cleaner” hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{*}
. This corresponds to deleting vertices from
H
.

First, since
H
is

-free, no hyperedge

Next, by randomly partitioning the vertices of

s

.

Finally, pass to a choice of

where

for

d_{\mathcal{H}^{***}}(v)\geq d^{\prime}:=\frac{d(\mathcal{H}^{**})}{s}=\Omega_{s,t}(d)

.

We may and will assume
|V_{s}^{\prime}|\geq|V_{i}^{\prime}|
, for all

Clearly, we have that

). Consider the graph
G
with vertex set
V(G)\coloneqq V^{\prime}_{s}\cup V^{\prime}_{i}
where
(x_{s},x_{i})\in E(G)

.

Without loss generality, we may assume that

For

\mathbb{P}(x\in B)\leq\mathbb{P}(x\in B_{0})=p

Understanding

(1)

\mathbb{P}(x\in A)=\Theta_{s}(P_{x})

(2)

\mathbb{P}(x\in A\text{ and }y\in A)\ \leq O_{s}(P_{x}P_{y}).

From here, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(y\in A_{0}|\mathcal{E}_{x})

7.1.

We proceed as in the proof of Theorem

First, we need an analogue of Lemma

Lemma 7.1
.

Fix a bipartite graph

Remark
.

This result can be viewed as a

Erdős-Hajnal result (in the same spirit as a result of Fox and Sudakov
[

). Our proof gives better bounds (
[

) and has a weaker assumption (they require that

Proof.

Write
a:=|A|
, and
\Delta:=\Delta(H)
.

First, note that we can find

with

. By Lemma

, we must have a set

We need now a simple observation.

has the same distribution as

8.
Proof of the polychotomy propositions

8.1.
Big picture

The purpose of this section is to establish Propositions
7.3
and
7.7

We first need a “model result”, in the same vein as to Lemma

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
d,D,k\geq 2
and suppose

is sufficiently large with respect to
D
and

.

Let
G
be a graph which is
d
-degenerate with a bi-partition into disjoint vertex sets

so that the following holds:

•

|A|>d^{D+1}|B|
;

•

;

•

for each

.

Remark
.

The first three bullets are essentially what we get after applying Claim

(in the proof of Lemma

does not have any copy of

Proof.

Let

be as in the statement above.

with maximum out-degree at most

.

Now, let

be a random subset of

with probability

(independently). Let

be the set of

and

be the set of

It is clear that

Using that

. Therefore, we have

(using that

Whence

and so there is some outcome with

.
Taking

gives the desired graph. Indeed, clearly this is the 1-subdivision of some

with

∎

To finish, we show that we can always reduce to this case, unless one of the three forbidden bullets happens.

We delay the details to the upcoming subsection (Subsection

8.2.
Establishing the last proposition

This section is devoted to proving Proposition
8.2
. After appealing to results like Claim
5.6
and Lemma
4.1
, we will morally reduce to the following problem. Consider
x\in A_{0}
with
N(x)\subset B
being an independent set of size
d
, and with
|N(x)\cap N(x^{\prime})|
not being “too large” for any other
x^{\prime}\in A_{0}\setminus\{x\}
. Here, we would like to find some
I_{x}\in\binom{N(x)}{D}
where
|I_{x}\cap N(x^{\prime})|<k
for each

, or otherwise find an induced subgraph

isomorphic to the
1
-subdivision of

.

We handle the task of finding

or a copy of

via a “shattering lemma” (Lemma
8.4
), which is inspired by the notion of VC-dimension. Although a more quantitative shattering result follows by applying the well-known Sauer-Shelah lemma, we choose to give a simple self-contained proof in the spirit of Lemma
7.1
.

Given a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}([n])
, we say that
\mathcal{F}

k
-shatters a set
R\subset[n]
if for every
S\in\binom{R}{k}
, there is
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that
R\cap F=S
.

We start with an easy preliminary observation.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
r\geq k\geq 2
and
n\geq 10r^{2}
, and suppose
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}([n])
has the property that for every
S\in\binom{[n]}{k}
, there exists some
F\in\mathcal{F}
with
F\supset S
. Furthermore, suppose that
|F|\leq n/r^{k+1}
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Then, there is some
r
-subset
R\subset[n]
which is
k
-shattered by
F
.

Proof.

For each subset
S\in\binom{[n]}{k}
, we assign some
F_{S}\in\mathcal{F}
so that
S\subset F_{S}
(if there are multiple choices, pick one arbitrarily).

Now, let
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in[n]
be chosen uniformly at random (with repetitions allowed). We have that
\mathbb{P}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\text{ are all distinct})\geq 1-\binom{r}{2}\frac{1}{n}\geq 9/10
.

Next, for
e\in\binom{[r]}{k}
, we bound the probability of the event,
\mathcal{E}_{e}
, that
(x_{i})_{i\in e}
are all distinct and
x_{\ell}\in F_{\{x_{i}:i\in e\}}
for some other
\ell\in[r]\setminus e
. Since
|F_{\{x_{i}:i\in e\}}|\leq n/r^{k+1}
, and each
x_{i}
is chosen uniformly at random, a union bound gives
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{e})\leq(r-k)/r^{k+1}\leq 1/r^{k}
. Summing over the
e
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{e}\text{ holds for some }e)\leq\binom{r}{k}\frac{1}{r^{k}}\leq 1/2
.

Thus, with positive probability
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
are all distinct, and none of the events
\mathcal{E}_{e}
happen. Which means that
R:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
is a set of size
r
which is
s
-shattered by
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

We now simply apply the hypergraph Ramsey theorem (which says that for every
k,h,D
, there exists some finite
N
so that every
2
-coloring of
\binom{[N]}{k}
either has a
h
-set
R
where
\binom{R}{k}
is monochromatic in the first color, or a
D
-set
B
where
\binom{B}{k}
is monochromatic in the second color) to finish.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
D,h,k\geq 2
. Then, there exists
\delta:=\delta(D,h,s)>0
so that the following holds for all sufficiently large

.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}([n])
where
F<\delta n
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
, and which does not
k
-shatter a set of size
h
. Then there exists a set
I\subset[n]
of size
D
where
|I\cap F|<k
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
N
be the 2-color Ramsey number of
K_{h}^{(k)}
and
K_{D}^{(k)}
and let
\delta:=1/N^{k+1}
.

Now consider a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}([n])
where
|F|<\delta n
. Define
\mathcal{G}\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
to be the set of all
S\in\binom{[n]}{k}
such that
S\not\subset F
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
. As
n
is large, we have that
\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{G}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
8.3
(with

), thus we can find some set

of size

which is
k
-shattered by
\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{G}
.

For each
k
-subset

, we color it red if
S=F\cap X
for some

, and otherwise we color it blue. Note that if

is colored blue, we must have that
S\in\mathcal{G}
. By our choice of

, we can either find a set

where all

\mathcal{F}

s
-shatters a set of size

), or a set

where all

-subsets are blue. By assumption

does not exist, so we are done taking

(we cannot have

for any

, as this would mean

-subset).
∎

Finally we deduce Proposition

.

.

To find the

Let

9.
Concluding Remarks

We have shown that every degree-bounded class of graphs is polynomially degree-bounded.
It could be interesting to classify all degree-bounded classes with a degree bounding function which grows linearly.

Now, regarding
\chi
-bounded classes of graphs our knowledge is much more limited. Recall, we do not even know whether for every
T
, the class of
T
-free graphs is
\chi
-bounded. This has only been verified for very specific trees (see e.g.
[
25
]
) which includes all paths.
We conjecture that actually for every path
P
, the class of
P
-free graphs is
polynomially

\chi
-bounded.

Conjecture 9.1
.

For every
k
, there is
C_{k}>0
such that every graph
G
with
\chi(G)\geq\omega(G)^{C_{k}}
contains an induced path of length
k
.

Scott, Seymour and Spirkl
[
27
]
showed recently that the class
\mathcal{C}
of
P_{5}
-free graphs is
\chi
-bounded where
f(x)=x^{\log_{2}(x)}
is a
\chi
-binding function for
\mathcal{C}
.

Recall Theorem
1.5
states that if the average degree of a graph
G
is sufficiently large compared with its clique number then
G
must either contain an induced complete bipartite graph or an induced subdivision of every small graph but we cannot ensure either of the structures by itself.

But what happens if we replace average degree with chromatic number? We observe again that neither of these structures could individually be forced, as there are graphs of arbitrarily high chromatic number and girth at least
5
and triangle-free graphs with arbitrarily high chromatic number and no induced subdivision of the
1
-subdivision of
K_{5}

[
22
]
.
Scott and Seymour
[
24
]
showed the family of graphs without an induced subdivision of a
(\ell,t)
-theta graph i.e. a graph consisting of two vertices joined by
t
-vertex disjoint paths of length at least
\ell
is
\chi
-bounded. We conjecture that actually avoiding the family of long balanced subdivisions of
K_{2,t}
is enough to guarantee
\chi
-boundedness.

Conjecture 9.2
.

For every
\ell
and
t
, there is
f_{\ell,t}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that every graph
G
without an induced copy of a
t
-theta graph where all paths are of the same length at least
\ell
has
\chi(G)\leq f_{\ell,t}(\omega(G))
.

Maybe
f_{\ell,t}
could even be taken to be polynomial, but this is not known even for cycles (the case of
t=2
), since a positive answer would resolve Conjecture
9.1
.

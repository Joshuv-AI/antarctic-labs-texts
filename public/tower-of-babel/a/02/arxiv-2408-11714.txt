Abstract
. We describe the behaviour of a free reduced plane projective curve with respect to the deletion, respectively addition, of a smooth conic. These results apply in particular to conic-line arrangements. We present some obstructions to the geometry and combinatorics of a free reduced curve, generalizing results known a priori only for free projective line arrangements.

14H50 (Primary); 14F06, 14C17 (Secondary)

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{2}\mathbb{C}
. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is the union of a finite number of lines and smooth conics,
{\mathcal{C}}
is called a
conic-line arrangement
, in short, a CL-arrangement.
One important motivation to study CL-arrangements is to explore generalizations in this direction of the theory of hyperplane arrangements, as CL-arrangements are a natural generalization of arrangements of projective lines.

Addition-deletion

and
deletion-restriction

type results provide important inductive tools to approach various problems concerning arrangements of hyperplanes, for instance to study the freeness property. Among them, we mention the classical addition-deletion theorem of Terao (
[
21
]
) and the more recent division theorem (
[
2
]
) and combinatorial deletion theorem (
[
3
]
) of Abe. For curves, Schenck-Terao-Yoshinaga prove in
[
19
]
an addition-type result, that concerns the addition of a smooth curve, under some quasihomogeneity conditions.

An emblematic and long standing open problem in the theory of arrangements of hyperplanes is Terao’s Conjecture that predicts the combinatorial nature of the algebraic freeness property. Despite attracting considerable interest over the years, the conjecture is still open, even for arrangements of projective lines, i.e., in the
3
-dimensional case. This has generated an intense research on the topics of freeness and freeness-adjacent properties, such as the property of being plus-one generated, introduced in
[
4
]
.

CL-arrangements too, and more generally, reduced curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, were in recent years persistently studied in relation to the freeness property (see for instance
[
20
,
19
,
6
,
17
]
). Freeness for curves proves to be an even more complicated subject, compared to its manifestation in the field of hyperplane arrangements. As Schenck-Tohăneanu prove in
[
20
]
, freeness is not determined by the combinatorics of the curve (combinatorics of a curve as defined, for instance, in
[
10
]
).

In this note we investigate the effect, on the freeness property, of adding a smooth conic to a free reduced curve, respectively of deleting a smooth conic from a free reduced curve. Incidentally, these addition-deletion type results (Theorems
3.14
and
3.16
) give an effective way to construct new free and plus-one generated examples of curves.

In particular we give an answer to an open question from
[
14
]
about the type of CL-arrangement that can appear by deleting a smooth conic from a free CL-arrangement. We formulate necessary and sufficient conditions for the resulting arrangement to be free or plus-one generated, in Theorem
3.2
.
Given a free CL-arrangement, under some quasihomogeneity assumptions, we show that one just has to count the points of intersection of the arrangement to the conic to decide if the result of the deletion of the conic from the arrangement is free, plus-one generated, or neither. Moreover, we describe precisely the type of curve (from the point of view of a minimal resolution of its associated derivations module) we obtain in this third case, see Corollary
3.3
and Remark
3.4
.

As for the addition of a smooth conic to a CL-arrangement, the fact that it can produce a whole range of results, even in the quasihomogeneity hypothesis, was already known, and it is illustrated by
[
14
, Example 2.8]
. However, we give new insight on how the addition behaves, specifically we give the precise conditions for the result of the addition to be free, plus-one generated or neither, see Theorem
3.7
. We effectively describe a minimal resolution of the derivations module of the resulting curve in this third case, in Corollary
3.8
and Remark
3.9
.

The main ingredients for the proofs of Theorems
3.2
,
3.7
are found in Schenck-Tohăneanu’s paper
[
20
]
. However, the authors are interested in
[
20
]
only in the situation when the addition or deletion of a smooth conic applied to a free CL-arrangement results in a free CL-arrangement (see
[
20
, Theorem 3.4]
), under a quasihomogeneity assumption. We take this a step further and we characterize completely the possible results to the addition or deletion of a smooth conic, starting from a free CL-arrangement. A more recent result from
[
12
]
, Theorem
2.3
, also comes into play.

Moreover, we show that the results concerning CL-arrangements, Theorems
3.2
,
3.7
and Corollaries
3.5
,
3.10
hold in a more general hypothesis, i.e. for reduced curves, with or without the quasihomogeneity assumption. More precisely, they hold for triples of reduced curves with respect to a smooth conic (see Section
3
for the definition of a triple with respect to a smooth conic). For quasihomogeneous triples of reduced curves the statements are identical to the ones for quasihomogeneous triples of CL-arrangements. Dropping the quasihomogeneity assumption leads to some adjustments in the initial statements, see Theorems
3.14
,
3.16
and Theorem
3.18
.

To put the problem in context, we recall that recent results from
[
11
]
(see also
[
15
]
) show that, when
applied to a free CL-arrangement, any of the two operations of deletion, respectively addition, of a
line
from (respectively to) a CL-arrangement produces either a free or a plus-one generated CL-arrangement.

These types of results, but stated for arrangements of hyperplanes, appeared first in the work of Abe (
[
4
]
), where the notion of
plus-one generated
was introduced. As seen in
[
4
]
, addition and deletion of a hyperplane behave perfectly symmetrical in a
3
-dimensional vector space: both procedures give rise to a free or a plus-one generated arrangement. But in ambient spaces of higher dimension only the deletion works the same way, in full generality. The addition of a hyperplane to a free arrangement in spaces of dimension higher than
3
can produce an arrangement which is neither free nor plus-one generated (see
[
4
, Example 7.5]
). In
[
5
]
, Abe-Denham introduce, for logarithmic forms, an analogue notion to plus-one generated,
dual plus-one generated
, with respect to which the addition of a hyperplane to a hyperplane arrangement, in arbitrary dimension, behaves nicely. That is, it produces as a result either a free or a dual plus-one generated arrangement. The two notions, plus-one generated and dual plus-one generated, are equivalent in a
3
-dimensional vector space.

We present in Theorems
2.6
and
3.18

some geometric and combinatoric implications of the freeness property for reduced curves.
In particular, for a CL-arrangement, we obtain obstructions on its restrictions to lines and smooth conics and also obstructions on its intersection to lines and smooth conics that are not elements in the arrangement.

Notice that Theorem
2.6
is a generalization to free reduced curves of a result of Abe for free projective line arrangements from
[
1
]
(recalled in Theorem
2.4
), concerning the number of points of the restriction to a line of the arrangement, respectively the number of points of the intersection of the arrangement to a line not contained in the arrangement.

In Theorem
2.8
we formulate similar geometric obstructions induced by the plus-one generated property. With this we generalize a result on projective line arrangements from
[
8
]
, recalled in Theorem
2.5
.

We conclude in Section
4
with a series of examples that reflect the variety of situations from Theorems
3.2
,
3.7
and their generalized versions, Theorems
3.14
and
3.16
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us briefly recall the notion of freeness for curves.
Denote
S:=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
and let

Der(S)\coloneqq S\partial_{x}\oplus S\partial_{y}\oplus S\partial_{z}

be the module of
S
-derivations.
To a reduced curve
{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
, defined as the zero set of the homogeneous polynomial
f_{{\mathcal{C}}}\in S
, one associates the module of logarithmic derivations

D({\mathcal{C}})\coloneqq\{\theta\in Der(S)\;|\;\theta(f_{{\mathcal{C}}})\in Sf_{{\mathcal{C}}}\}.

{\mathcal{C}}
is called
free
if
D({\mathcal{C}})
is free as an
S
-module. The degrees of a minimal set of homogeneous generators of
D({\mathcal{C}})
(which are independent of the choice of the generators themselves) constitute the
exponents
of the free curve
{\mathcal{C}}
.
The Euler derivation
\theta_{E}=x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}+z\partial_{z}
is always an element of
D({\mathcal{C}})
, so
1
is always among the exponents.

Moreover, there is a decomposition of the module of logarithmic derivations as a direct sum of modules

D({\mathcal{C}})=D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})\oplus S\theta_{E}

where

D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})\coloneqq\{\theta\in Der(S)\;|\;\theta(f_{{\mathcal{C}}})=0\}.

So
{\mathcal{C}}
is free if and only if the
S
-module
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
is free. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is free with exponents
(1,a,b)
then
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
is freely generated by two homogeneous derivations of degrees
a,b
. We will omit the exponent of
{\mathcal{C}}
corresponding to the Euler derivation and simply state that
{\mathcal{C}}
is free with exponents
(a,b)
.

In
[
4
, Definition 1.1]
Abe introduces, for hyperplane arrangements in general, a notion close to and strongly related to freeness. A
plus-one generated
arrangement of hyperplanes is characterized by the existence of a very simple resolution for the associated module of logarithmic derivations, a natural step away from the resolution of a free module. Later on, in
[
12
]
, Dimca-Sticlaru extend this definition to reduced plane projective curves, via their associated derivations modules:

Definition 2.1
.

A reduced curve
{\mathcal{C}}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is called plus-one generated with exponents
(a,b)
and level
\ell
if
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
admits a minimal resolution of the form:

0\rightarrow S(-\ell-1)\rightarrow S(-\ell)\oplus S(-b)\oplus S(-a)\rightarrow D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})\rightarrow 0

This definition of the plus-one generated property is at first glance slightly different from the definition given by the authors in
[
12
]
. We clarify in Proposition
2.2
that the two definitions actually coincide.

Let us recall some general facts about the derivations module
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
associated to a reduced degree
\mathit{d}
curve
{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
. It is a rank
2
, graded, reflexive,
S
-module, of projective dimension
pd_{S}(D_{0}({\mathcal{C}}))\leq 2
, so a minimal resolution is of type:

(1)

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-2}S[-e_{i}]\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}S[-d_{i}]\rightarrow D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})\rightarrow 0

Following the convention established in
[
12
]
we call the ordered multiset

(2)

(d_{1}\leq\dots\leq d_{r})

the (generalized) exponents
of
{\mathcal{C}}
, where
d_{1}\geq 1
. These are the ordered degrees of the generators from a minimal set of homogeneous generators of
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
, and do not depend on the choice of the generators.

With this notion of generalized exponents defined for any reduced curve, let us prove that a plus-one generated curve
{\mathcal{C}}
with exponents
(a,b)
and level
\ell
as in Definition
2.1
means precisely a curve with generalized exponents
(a,b,\ell)
such that
a+b=\deg({\mathcal{C}})
. This latter characterisation of the plus-one generated property was employed as definition in
[
12
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

A curve
{\mathcal{C}}
is plus-one generated with exponents
(a,b)
and level
\ell
if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}
has generalized exponents
(a,b,\ell)
and
a+b=\deg({\mathcal{C}})
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be plus-one generated. Denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
the sheafification of the
S
-module of logarithmic derivations
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
. It is known that
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
is in fact a locally free sheaf, i.e. a vector bundle.
Consider the exact sequence of vector bundles induced by the sheafification of the exact sequence of graded modules from Definition
2.1
:

(3)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1-\ell)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-\ell)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-b)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-a)\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow 0

There is a well known formula that connects the Chern polynomials of the terms of an exact sequence of vector bundles, see for instance
[
13
, Section 3]
. Using this formula we compute the first Chern number of
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
,

c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}})=1-a-b.

Since, for reduced curves in general,
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}})=1-\deg({\mathcal{C}})
, it follows that the plus-one generated property implies

a+b=\deg({\mathcal{C}}).

Conversely, let us assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a curve with generalized exponents
(a,b,\ell)
such that
a+b=\deg({\mathcal{C}})
. A minimal free resolution for
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
is of the following type:

0\rightarrow S(-e)\rightarrow S(-\ell)\oplus S(-b)\oplus S(-a)\rightarrow D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})\rightarrow 0

Just as before, pass to sheaves in the above exact sequence to compute
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}})
and then use the equality
a+b=\deg({\mathcal{C}})
to conclude

e=\ell+1.

∎

A very useful criterion to decide when a curve
{\mathcal{C}}
is free or plus-one generated involves the generalized exponents from
2
, see
[
12
, Theorem 2.3]
:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a reduced curve of degree
\mathit{d}
. Then, with the notations from (
1
):

(1)

{\mathcal{C}}
is free if and only if
d_{1}+d_{2}=d-1
.

(2)

{\mathcal{C}}
is plus-one generated if and only if
d_{1}+d_{2}=d
.

(3)

{\mathcal{C}}
is neither free nor plus-one generated if and only if
d_{1}+d_{2}>d
.

For arrangements of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, freeness imposes constraints on the number of points of the restriction to an arbitrary line in the arrangement, and also on the number of intersection points of the arrangement to a an arbitrary line not contained in the arrangement.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
1
]
)

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be a free arrangement in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with exponents
(a,b)
,
a\leq b
.

(1)

Let
H\in{\pazocal{A}}
and
{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\coloneqq{\pazocal{A}}\setminus\{H\}
. Then either
|{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\cap H|\leq a+1
or
|{\pazocal{A}}^{\prime}\cap H|=b+1
.

(2)

Let

be a line such that

. Then
|{\pazocal{A}}\cap L|=a+1
or
|{\pazocal{A}}\cap L|\geq b+1
.

We generalize the above result, proving that similar obstructions hold for the geometry of free reduced curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, in Theorem

.

Likewise, it is known that the plus-one generated property imposes constraints on the cardinal of the restriction to an arbitrary line in the arrangement.

Theorem 2.5
.

(
[
8
]
)

Let
{\pazocal{A}}
be a plus-one generated arrangement in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with exponents
(a,b),\;a\leq b,
and level

,
H\in{\pazocal{A}}

.

We generalize Theorem
2.5
, showing that the plus-one generated property for curves induces the same type of geometric obstructions as for projective line arrangements, in Theorem

.

To state and prove the above mentioned results, Theorems
2.6
and
2.8
, we need to recall, in our particular setting, the definition of an invariant associated to an isolated hypersurface singularity, introduced in
[
11
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an arbitrary reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
p\in Sing({\mathcal{C}})
. Denote by
\mu({\mathcal{C}},p),\;\tau({\mathcal{C}},p)
, the Milnor number, respectively, the Tjurina number, of the singularity
p
. Let

\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},p)\coloneqq\mu({\mathcal{C}},p)-\tau({\mathcal{C}},p)

be the measure of the defect from quasihomogeneity of
p\in Sing({\mathcal{C}})

(it is known that
p
is quasihomogeneous if and only if the Milnor and Tjurina numbers are equal, i.e.
p
quasihomogeneous is equivalent to
\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},p)=0
, see
[
18
]
). In general, the inequality

\mu({\mathcal{C}},p)\geq\tau({\mathcal{C}},p)
holds, i.e.

\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},p)\geq 0.

If
{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\;{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
are two reduced curves with no common irreducible components and
p\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, denote

\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{p}\coloneqq\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{2},p)-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1},p)

and

\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{2})\coloneqq\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}_{1},{\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{p}.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a reduced free curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with exponents
(a,b),a\leq b
.

(1)

Let
L
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a line which is an irreducible component of
{\mathcal{C}}
, i.e.
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cup L
, where
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is the union of the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{C}}
, other than
L
. Then either
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|\leq a+1-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},L)
or
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|=b+1-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},L)
.

(2)

Let
L
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a line which is not an irreducible component of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then either
|{\mathcal{C}}\cap L|=a+1-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},L)
or
|{\mathcal{C}}\cap L|\geq b+1-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}},L)
.

Proof.

Immediately from
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

We formulate in Theorem
3.18
geometric obstructions of the same flavour for free reduced curves, involving the restriction of the curve to one of its smooth conics (as irreducible component), respectively the intersection of the curve to an arbitrary smooth conic which is not an irreducible component of the curve.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a plus-one generated curve with exponents
(a,b),\;a\leq b,
and level
l
, and
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
a line which is an irreducible component of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be the union of the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{C}}
, other than
L
. Then either
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|+\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},L)\leq a+1
or
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|+\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},L)\in\{b,b+1,l+1\}
.

Proof.

Recall that, by
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
(extending and based on
[
19
, Theorem 1.6]
), we have an exact sequence of vector bundles:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}(-1)\overset{\alpha_{L}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{E}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},L))\rightarrow 0.

In particular, we have a surjective map

\mathcal{E}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},L)).

Tensor this map by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, to obtain again a surjection,

\mathcal{E}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},L)).

By
[
15
, Theorem 2.8]
, we know the possible splitting types onto an arbitrary line of the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
associated to a plus-one generated curve
{\mathcal{C}}
. More precisely,
\mathcal{E}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}
must be one of the three:
\mathcal{O}_{L}(-a)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-b)
,
\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-a)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}(-b)
or
\mathcal{O}_{L}(-l)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}(l+1-a-b)
.

Then the same argument as in the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 2.5]
proves our claim.
∎

3.
Main results

We start by looking at CL-arrangements, which were our initial object of study for this note, as they naturally extend line arrangements. Another motivation to start with the class of CL-arrangements is that the proofs of our statements concerning CL-arrangements are then easily extended to reduced curves.

Let

{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcup_{i}C_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j}L_{j}

be the decomposition into irreducible components of a CL-arrangement
{\mathcal{C}}
, where
C_{i}
’s are smooth conics and
L_{j}
’s lines.

We will generally follow the definitions and constructions from
[
20
]
.

All conics referred to in this section are considered to be
smooth
conics.

For an arbitrary smooth conic
C=C_{i_{0}}
in the arrangement
{\mathcal{C}}
define
the associated triple

({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime})

with respect to the conic
C
by

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{C}}\setminus\{C_{i_{0}}\}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\neq i_{0}}C_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j}L_{j}

and

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap C.

We will sometimes write
L\in{\mathcal{C}}
, respectively
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
, meaning the line
L
, respectively the conic
C
, are irreducible components of the CL-arrangement
{\mathcal{C}}
. We will use the same convention if
C,L
are irreducible components of a curve
{\mathcal{C}}
.

If
{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
have only quasihomogeneous singularities, we will call
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime})
a
quasihomogeneous triple
.

Assume without loss of generality that the (smooth) conic
C
is defined by the homogeneous degree
2
polynomial
f_{C}=y^{2}-xz
.
Denote

k\coloneqq|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime}|=|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cap C|.

Denote moreover by
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}},{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}
the sheafification of the
S
-modules of logarithmic derivations
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}})
, respectively
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
. They are locally free sheaves, i.e. rank
2
vector bundles.

By
[
20
, Proposition 3.7]
, we have the short exact sequence:

(4)

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}(-2)\overset{\cdot f_{C}}{\rightarrow}{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow i_{*}{\pazocal O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-k)\rightarrow 0

where
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is parametrized by
[s:t]
and
i:\mathbb{P}^{1}\xrightarrow{[s^{2}:st:t^{2}]}\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
i
being the inclusion of the conic
C
into
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

For a quasihomogeneous triple
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime})
, when
{\mathcal{C}}
is free, from
[
20
, Lemma 3.6, Proposition 3.7 and the proof of Lemma 3.10]
one can extract formulas for the Hilbert series of the logarithmic derivations module
D_{0}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
, in terms of
k
and of the exponents of the free CL-arrangement
{\mathcal{C}}
, see Proposition
3.1
. The computations in
[
20
]
are based on the existence of the short exact sequence of sheaves (
4
).

Since
[
20
, Lemma 3.10]
gives the recipe for computing the Hilbert series for the dual
{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}
of
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}
, one has to take into account the relation between a rank
2
vector bundle and its dual, which holds for an arbitrary rank
2
vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
:

{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\simeq{\mathcal{E}}(-c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}))

where
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})
is the first Chern number of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Finally, recall that, for a reduced curve
{\mathcal{C}}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,

c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}})=1-\deg({\mathcal{C}}).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime})
be a quasihomogeneous triple with respect to the conic
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
{\mathcal{C}}
is free with exponents
(a,b)
. Let
k=|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime}|
and
\deg({\mathcal{C}})=d
.

If
k=2m
, then

HS(D_{0}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}))(t)=\frac{t^{a}+t^{b}+t^{d-3-m}-t^{d-1-m}}{(1-t)^{3}}.

If
k=2m+1
, then

HS(D_{0}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}))(t)=\frac{t^{a}+t^{b}+2t^{d-3-m}-2t^{d-2-m}}{(1-t)^{3}}.

Theorem 3.2

3.1.
From CL-arrangements to reduced curves

The definition of quasihomogeneous triple extends easily from CL-arrangements to curves. Let us recall here, for convenience, this particular instance of
[
19
, Definition 1.5]

Definition 3.13
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is a reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that

Then, by

(12)

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}(-2)\overset{\cdot f_{C}}{\rightarrow}{\mathcal{E}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\rightarrow i_{*}{\pazocal O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime}|)\rightarrow 0

where

Our main results can be extended verbatim to this generalized version of quasihomogeneous triples, as defined in
3.13

Theorems
3.2
,
3.7
and their corollaries actually hold as stated in this new, more general setting. The proofs are basically the same, but using instead of (
4

There is one argument from the original proof that may need justification in this new context. At some point, in
[
20
, Lemmas 3.8, 3.10]
the fact that a derivations module

is isomorphic to the module of sections of the sheafification of

is used. This is true for derivations modules associated to reduced curves in general, not only for derivations modules associated to CL-arrangements, see for instance
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Quantitative reducibility of Gevrey quasi-periodic cocycles and its applications
Xianzhe Li

Abstract
. We establish a quantitative version of strong almost reducibility result for sl ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathrm{sl}(2,{\mathbb{R}}) quasi-periodic cocycle close to a constant in Gevrey class. We prove that, for the quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators with small Gevrey potentials, the length of spectral gaps decays sub-exponentially with respect to its labelling, the long range duality operator has pure point spectrum with sub-exponentially decaying eigenfunctions for almost all phases and the spectrum is an interval for discrete Schrödinger operator acting on ℤ d {\mathbb{Z}}^{d} with small separable potentials. All these results are based on a refined KAM scheme, and thus are perturbative.

Introduction

We consider the quasi-periodic Schrödinger operator on

:

(1.1)

where

{\mathbb{Z}}^{b}

(

) is the

, and

are called

, the

respectively. We will always assume that

is rationally independent, thus the spectrum of

\Sigma_{v,\alpha}

with no isolated points, and independent on

1.1.
Quantitative almost reducibility

The reducibility theory (consult section
2.2
for related concepts) has attracted much attention due to its importance in connecting dynamical systems and the spectral theory.
The pioneering work was due to Dinaburg-Sinai
[
15
]
. They first used classical KAM theory to obtain positive measure energies has reducibility for one-dimensional continuous quasi-periodic Schrödinger equation, provided small analytic potential. Eliasson
[
16
]
made a breakthrough by proving the reducibility for almost all energies and the almost reducibility for all energies provided the frequency is Diophantine and the potential is small and analytic, using a crucial resonance-cancelation technique introduced by Moser-Pöschel
[
27
]
.
The non-perturbative reducibility, i.e., the smallness of the perturbations does not depend on the Diophantine constant, was established by Hou-You
[
21
]
(see also Avila-Fayad-Krikorian
[
3
]
).
Recently, Leguil-You-Zhao-Zhou
[
24
]
obtained a
strong
version of quantitative almost reducibility result, i.e., the strips of width not going to zero. Almost reducibility have many interesting spectral applications, curious readers are invited to consult
[
38
]
.

In all the above almost reducibility results, the perturbations are all assumed to be analytic. In this paper, we extend these results to Gevrey class (consult section
2.1
). Recall that

\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
Diophantine
if there exist
\gamma^{-1}>1
,
\tau>d
, such that
\alpha\in\mathrm{DC}_{d}(\gamma,\tau)
,
where

\mathrm{DC}_{d}(\gamma,\tau):=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\inf_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\langle n,x\rangle-j|\geq\frac{\gamma}{|n|^{\tau}},0\neq n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}.

Let
\mathrm{DC}_{d}:=\bigcup_{\gamma^{-1}>1,\tau>d}\mathrm{DC}_{d}(\gamma,\tau)
, then
\mathrm{DC}_{d}
has full Lebesgue measure. Then we can state our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
r_{0}>0
. Assume
\alpha\in\mathrm{DC}_{d}(\gamma,\tau)
,
A_{0}\in\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
,
f_{0}\in G^{\nu}_{r_{0}}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathrm{sl(2,{\mathbb{R}})})
, with
0<\nu<1
. Then for any
r\in(0,r_{0})
, there exists
\epsilon_{*}
depending on
\gamma,\tau,d,\nu,r,r_{0},\lVert A_{0}\rVert
, such that if
|f_{0}|_{r_{0}}<\epsilon_{*}
, then the system
(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})
is strong almost reducible with the “width”
r
.

More detailed quantitative estimates will be given in section 4. As a comparison, we mention that though the strong almost reducibility for Gevrey class have been proved by Chavaudret
[
12
]
in continuous analogy. We remark that our result provides more quantitative estimates, which plays a key role in the following applications to quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators with Gevrey potentials.

1.2.
Estimates on spectral gaps

Recall that the bounded connected component of
{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\Sigma_{v,\alpha}
is called the
spectral gap
, from the mathematical perspective, gap estimates is a core problem in the study of the spectral theory of quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators, since it can reflect the topological structure of the spectrum.

For
b=1
, the famous Gap-Labelling theorem
[
22
]
states that for any spectral gap
G
, there exists a unique
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that
2\rho\equiv\langle k,\alpha\rangle\mod{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
\rho
is its fibered rotation number (consult section
2.2
). That is, all the spectral gaps can be labelled by integer vectors: we
denote by
G_{k}(v)=(E^{-}_{k},E^{+}_{k})
the gap with label
k\neq 0
.
When
E^{-}_{k}=E^{+}_{k}
, we
say the gap is collapsed.
We also set
E_{0}^{-}:=\inf\Sigma_{v,\alpha},E_{0}^{+}:=\sup\Sigma_{v,\alpha},
and

G_{0}(v):=(-\infty,E_{0}^{-})\cup(E_{0}^{+},\infty)
. The consideration of the estimates of gaps, may go back to a question that whether the spectrum is a Cantor set for the almost Mathieu operator (AMO), for which
v(\cdot)=2\lambda\cos 2\pi(\cdot)
,
\lambda\neq 1
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash{\mathbb{Q}}
. This is so-called
Ten Martini Problem
stated by Barry Simon after an offer of Mark Kac in 1981
[
35
]
. After works of many mathematicians, this problem was finally solved by Avila-Jitomirskaya
[
4
]
. The
Dry Ten Martini Problem
further conjectures that all gaps
G_{k}
labeled by the Gap-Labelling theorem are open, for AMO case. This problem was partially resolved in
[
31
,
4
,
26
,
5
]
. Moreover, Leguil-You-Zhao-Zhou
[
24
]
obtained the exponential asymptotic behavior on the gaps of noncritical AMO recently, which gives a quantitative estimate on their size.

It was conjectured that
one-dimensional quasi-periodic Schrödinger operator generically has Cantor spectrum
[
34
]
. For partial advances we refer to
[
37
]
.
Then people are also curious about how large are the gaps. There are many works along this line: For analytic small potentials, Moser-Pöschel
[
27
]
proved that
G_{k}(v)
is exponentially small with respect to
|k|
provided that
|k|
large enough and
\langle k,\alpha\rangle
not too close to other
\langle m,\alpha\rangle
; Amor
[
1
]
proved the all gaps
G_{k}(v)
are sub-exponentially small with respect to
|k|
for small analytic potentials and Diophantine
\alpha
; Damanik-Goldstein
[
13
]
proved the decay rate
|G_{k}(v)|\leq 2\lVert v\rVert_{r_{0}}e^{-\pi r_{0}|k|}
for sufficiently small
v\in C^{\omega}_{r_{0}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
. Later on, Leguil-You-Zhao-Zhou
[
24
]
improved the decay rate as
|G_{k}(v)|\leq\lVert v\rVert_{r_{0}}^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{-2\pi r|k|}
, where
r\in(0,r_{0})
can be arbitrary close to
r_{0}
. For weakly coupled quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators with partial Liouville frequencies, see also the result by Liu-Shi
[
25
]
, the size of the spectral gaps decays exponentially.

If the regularity of
v
is weaker than analytic, one can not expect an exponential decay rate
[
13
]
.
In finite differentiable regime, Cai-Wang
[
11
]
showed that the gap lengths decay polynomially. In this paper, we improve this result to sub-exponential decay for small Gevrey potentials.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
r_{0}>0
. Assume that
\alpha\in\mathrm{DC}_{d}(\gamma,\tau)
,
v\in G^{\nu}_{r_{0}}(\mathbb{T}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})

.
For any

\epsilon_{*}=\epsilon_{*}(\gamma,\tau,d,\nu,r_{0},r)>0

, the

, denoted by

, has the following estimate

|G_{k}(v)|\leq\epsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-r|2\pi k|^{\nu}}.

One should not expect all gaps are open for general quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators
[
19
]
, thus different from the AMO case, in general it is impossible to give any non-trivial lower bound of gap length.

If

If

showed that for any fixed Diophantine

1.4.
Interval spectrum

In the study of spectral theory, an interesting question to ask is how complex the spectrum is. If we consider the topological structure of a compact set, there are two extremes, one is the interval, one is the Cantor set. Interval is simple, intuitive, while the Cantor set is complex, counterintuitive. For
b=1
, it is known that if the frequency
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
, the spectrum of
H_{v,\alpha,\theta}
is a disjoint union of at most finite compact intervals. Furthermore, Bethe–Sommerfeld Conjecture states that for
b\geq 2
, the spectrum of the
b
-dimensional Schrödinger operator with periodic potential contains only finitely many gaps, partial advances see
[
30
,
20
]
. But for quasi-periodic case, the spectrum contains only finitely many gaps is a novel phenomenon. For
b=1
, Goldstein-Schlag-Voda
[
19
]
showed that for multi-frequency case, the spectrum can be a single interval. While for
b\geq 2
, things become easier. It is known that the spectrum of separable
b
-dimensional Schrödinger operators are sums of the spectrum of single 1-dimensional operators (consult section
2.3
). Thus if we assume the potential
v
of
H_{v,\alpha,\theta}
is separable potentials, intuitively, even though the spectrum of 1-dimensional operators is a Cantor set, if those gaps in every Cantor set have good control, then their sums can still produce an interval, thus it is natural to expect a condition such that the interval spectrum occurs.
The famous Newhouse Gap Lemma
[
28
]
provides a tool to link the gap information for a single Cantor set and their sums, thus our gap estimates can be applied to show the interval spectrum. This approach has been studied by Takase
[
36
]
, who showed that if any 1-dimensional Schrödinger operator with analytic potential frequency-dependent close to constants, then their sums is an interval. We extend this work to infinite differentiable regime, that is, the following result:

Theorem 1.4
.

For separable
b
-dimensional discrete Schroödinger operators
\hat{H}
generated by one dimensional quasi-periodic Gevrey-class Schrödinger operators

(1.3)

\hat{H}=\Delta+\sum_{j=1}^{b}v_{j}(n_{j}\alpha_{j}+\theta_{j})\delta_{nn^{\prime}},

where
v_{j}\in G_{r_{0}}^{\nu}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})\text{ with }0<\nu<1
, for
j=1,\cdots,b
. Assume
\alpha_{j}\in\mathrm{DC}_{d}(\gamma,\tau)
, then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\gamma,\tau,d,\nu,r_{0})>0
, such that if

0<|v_{j}|<\varepsilon\text{ \ \ for }j=1,\cdots,b,

the spectrum of
\hat{H}
is an interval.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1.4
is necessarily perturbative due to
[
8
]
.

2.1.
Gevrey functions

Recall that a function

,
|k|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|k_{i}|

,

is analytic. In this paper, we will set

|f|_{r}=|f|_{\nu,r}:=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(k)|e^{r|2\pi k|^{\nu}}<\infty,\ 0<\nu<1,

where
r>0
is called the “width”, and denote by
G^{\nu}_{r}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},*)
the set of these
*
-valued functions (
*
will usually denote
{\mathbb{R}},\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})

Remark 2.1
.

If

2.2.
Quasi-periodic cocycle and reducibility

In this subsection, we recall some basic concepts in quasi-periodic dynamics.

For given
A\in C^{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}))
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
(1,\alpha)
rationally independent, the quasi-periodic
cocycle
is defined as

(\alpha,A):\left\{\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}&&&\to&&{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\\
(\theta,v)&&&\mapsto&&(\theta+\alpha,A(\theta)v)\end{aligned}\right..

Denote
(\alpha,A)^{n}=(n\alpha,\mathcal{A}_{n})
, where

\mathcal{A}_{n}(\theta):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &A(\theta+(n-1)\alpha)\cdots A(\theta+\alpha)A(\theta),&&n\geq 0\\
&A^{-1}(\theta+n\alpha)A^{-1}(\theta+(n+1)\alpha)\cdots A^{-1}(\theta-\alpha),&&n<0\end{aligned}\right..

Then the
Lyapunov exponent
is well-defined by

L(\alpha,A):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\ln\lVert\mathcal{A}_{n}(\theta)\rVert\mathrm{d}\theta.

The cocycle
(\alpha,A)
is
uniformly hyperbolic
if for any
\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, there exists a continuous splitting
\mathbb{C}^{2}=E^{s}(\theta)\oplus E^{u}(\theta)
such that for every
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{A}_{n}(\theta)v\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-cn}\lvert n\rvert,

\displaystyle v\in E^{s}(\theta),

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{A}_{n}(\theta)^{-1}v\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-cn}\lvert n\rvert,

\displaystyle v\in E^{u}(\theta+n\alpha),

for some constants
C,c>0
, i.e., the splitting is invariant by the dynamics.
If we consider the eigenvalue equation
H_{v,\alpha,\theta}u=Eu
, then we can induce a
Schrödinger cocycle
, denoted by
(\alpha,S_{E}^{v})
:

(2.1)

\begin{pmatrix}u_{n+1}\\
u_{n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}E-v(\theta+n\alpha)&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{n}\\
u_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}.

It is well-known that
E\notin\Sigma_{v,\alpha}
if and only if
(\alpha,S_{E}^{v})
is uniformly hyperbolic.

Assume
A\in C^{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}))
is homotopic to the identity, then there exist
\psi:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
u:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

A(x)\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\cos 2\pi y\\
\sin 2\pi y\end{pmatrix}=u(x,y)\begin{pmatrix}\cos 2\pi(y+\psi(x,y))\\
\sin 2\pi(y+\psi(x,y))\end{pmatrix}.

The function
\psi
is called a
lift
of
A
. Let
\mu
be any probability measure on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}
which is invariant by the continuous map
T:(x,y)\mapsto(x+\alpha,y+\psi(x,y))
, projecting over Lebesgue measure on the first coordinate.
Then the number

\rho(\alpha,A)=\int\psi\ \mathrm{d}\mu\mod{\mathbb{Z}}

does not depend on the choices of
\psi
and
\mu
and is called the
fibered rotation number
of
(\alpha,A)
, see
[
22
]
. Given
\phi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, let
R_{\phi}:=\begin{pmatrix}\cos 2\pi\phi&\sin 2\pi\phi\\
\cos 2\pi\phi&\cos 2\pi\phi\end{pmatrix}
. It is immediate from the definition that

(2.2)

|\rho(\alpha,A)-\phi|<\lVert A-R_{\phi}\rVert_{0},

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{0}
denotes the
C^{0}
norm.
Any
B:2{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is homotopic to
\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to R_{\frac{\langle n,\theta\rangle}{2}}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
called the

of
B
, denoted by
\deg B=n
.
Given two real cocycles
(\alpha,A)
and

, we say they are

to each other if there exists
B\in C^{0}(2{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}))
such that

B(\theta+\alpha)^{-1}A(\theta)B(\theta)=A^{\prime}(\theta).

The fibered rotation number has the following property:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
23
]
)
.

Assume
(\alpha,A)
is conjugated to
(\alpha,A^{\prime})
by
B\in C^{0}(2\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}))
with
\deg B=n
, then we have

(2.3)

\rho(\alpha,A)=\rho(\alpha,A^{\prime})+\frac{\langle n,\alpha\rangle}{2}.

The cocycle
(\alpha,A)
is said to be (

-Gevrey)

if it can be conjugated to a constant cocycle, that is, there exist

It is said to be

In Hou-You

For any

K\subset{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}

(\mathcal{S}_{K}f)(\theta):=\sum_{k\in K}\widehat{f}(k)e^{\mathrm{i}2\pi\langle k,\alpha\rangle}.

Given
\eta>0
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
A\in\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Recall that there is an isomorphism from
\mathrm{sl}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
to
\mathrm{su}(1,1)
, which is given by

with

M=\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{i}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-\mathrm{i}\\
1&\mathrm{i}\end{pmatrix}.

More precisely, a direct computation leads to

(3.1)

M\begin{pmatrix}x&y+z\\
y-z&-x\end{pmatrix}M^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{i}z&x-\mathrm{i}y\\
x+\mathrm{i}y&-\mathrm{i}z\end{pmatrix}.

Assume that the two eigenvalues of
A
are
e^{\pm\mathrm{i}2\pi\xi}
, let

\Phi:=\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\backslash\{0\}:|e^{\mathrm{i}2\pi(\langle n,\alpha\rangle\pm 2\xi)}-1|\geq\eta,|e^{\mathrm{i}2\pi\langle n,\alpha\rangle}-1|\geq\eta\}

and
\Phi^{c}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}-\Phi
. Define the following subspace,

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{r}^{\nu\,(nre)}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{i}\mathcal{S}_{\Phi}f&\mathcal{S}_{\Phi}g\\
\mathcal{S}_{\Phi}\bar{g}&-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{S}_{\Phi}f\end{pmatrix}\right|f\in G^{\nu}_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}),g\in G^{\nu}_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{C})\right\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{r}^{\nu\,(re)}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{i}\mathcal{S}_{\Phi^{c}}f&\mathcal{S}_{\Phi^{c}}g\\
\mathcal{S}_{\Phi^{c}}\bar{g}&-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{S}_{\Phi^{c}}f\end{pmatrix}\right|f\in G^{\nu}_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}),g\in G^{\nu}_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{C})\right\},

and also define
G_{r}^{\nu\,(nre)}(\eta)=M^{-1}\widetilde{G}_{r}^{\nu\,(nre)}(\eta)M
,
G_{r}^{\nu\,(re)}(\eta)=M^{-1}\widetilde{G}_{r}^{\nu\,(re)}(\eta)M
, we call
G_{r}^{\nu\,(nre)}(\eta)
the non-resonant space and
G_{r}^{\nu\,(re)}(\eta)
the resonant space, all these subspaces above are Banach space. It follows that

\displaystyle G^{\nu}_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathrm{su(1,1)})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{G}_{r}^{\nu\,(re)}(\eta)\oplus\widetilde{G}_{r}^{\nu\,(nre)}(\eta),

\displaystyle G^{\nu}_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathrm{sl(2,{\mathbb{R}})})

\displaystyle=G_{r}^{\nu\,(re)}(\eta)\oplus G_{r}^{\nu\,(nre)}(\eta).

Under this decomposition, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
A\in\mathrm{SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
, there exist numerical constants
c_{1},D_{1}
such that if

\epsilon\leq c_{1}\lVert A\rVert^{-D_{1}}\ \text{and}\ \ \eta\geq\|A\|^{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{9}},

then for any

with

and

such that

e^{-Y(\theta+\alpha)}(Ae^{f(\theta)})e^{Y(\theta)}=Ae^{f^{(re)}(\theta)},

with
|Y|_{r}\leq\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
|f^{(re)}|_{r}\leq 2\epsilon
.

Remark 3.1

The analytic version of this result has appeared in

10

For

Lemma 4.1

For

Assume

4.2.
KAM scheme

Now we consider the initial cocycle
(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})
. Denote its rotation number by
\rho(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})
, Eliasson
[
16
]
proved that if
\rho(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})
is
Diophantine
with respect to
\alpha
, that is, there exist
\kappa^{-1}>1
,
\tau>d
such that
\rho\in\mathrm{DC}_{\alpha}^{d}(\kappa,\tau)
, where

\mathrm{DC}_{\alpha}^{d}(\kappa,\tau)=\{\phi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:|2\phi-\langle m,\alpha\rangle|_{{\mathbb{T}}}\geq\frac{\kappa}{(1+|m|)^{\tau}}\},

or
rational
with respect to
\alpha
(i.e.,
2\rho(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})\equiv\langle k,\alpha\rangle\mod{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
), then the system
(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})
is reducible, thus is full measure reducible, for small analytic potentials. Here we will focus on a quantitative version of full measure reducibility.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given
r_{0}>0
. Assume
\alpha\in\mathrm{DC}_{d}(\gamma,\tau)
,
A_{0}\in\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
,

, there exists
\bar{c}=\bar{c}(\gamma,\tau,d,\nu)>0
and a numerical constant
\bar{D}
, such that if

(4.9)

|f_{0}|_{r_{0}}=\epsilon_{0}<\epsilon_{*}:=\bar{c}\lVert A_{0}\rVert^{-\bar{D}}(r_{0}-r)^{\bar{D}\nu\tau},

the following holds:

\mathrm{(i)}

The system
(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})
is strong almost reducible with the “width”
r
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

If
\rho(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})
is rational w.r.t.
\alpha
, then there exist
\widetilde{Z}\in G^{\nu}_{r}(2\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}))
and
A\in\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
such that

(4.10)

\widetilde{Z}(\theta+\alpha)^{-1}A_{0}e^{f_{0}(\theta)}\widetilde{Z}(\theta)=A,

with estimates

|\widetilde{Z}|_{r^{\prime\prime}}\leq D_{1}|k|^{\tau}e^{2r^{\prime\prime}(\pi|k|)^{{\nu}}},\forall r^{\prime\prime}\in(0,r],\ D_{1}=D_{1}(\gamma,\tau,\lVert A_{0}\rVert,r_{0},d)>0.

Moreover, if
(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})
is not uniformly hyperbolic,
then
A
can be chosen as

\begin{pmatrix}1&\varphi\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ with }|\varphi|\leq\epsilon_{0}^{\frac{3}{5}}e^{-r|2\pi k|^{{\nu}}}.

\mathrm{(iii)}

If
\rho(\alpha,A_{0}e^{f_{0}})\in\bigcup_{\gamma^{\prime}>0}\mathrm{DC}_{\alpha}^{d}(\gamma^{\prime},\tau)
,
then there exist
B\in G^{\nu}_{r}(2{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}))
,
A\in\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
such that

(4.11)

B(\theta+\alpha)^{-1}A_{0}e^{f_{0}(\theta)}B(\theta)=A,\quad\rho(\alpha,A)\neq 0,

with estimates
|B|_{r}\leq\Gamma_{1}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma,\tau,d,\nu,r,r_{0},\lVert A_{0}\rVert)
,
|\deg B|\leq\Gamma_{2}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma,\tau,d,\nu,r,r_{0},\lVert A_{0}\rVert)
.

Proof.

We prove (i) by induction.

1). Take
r_{0},r,\epsilon_{*}
such that the first step holds.

and the subadditivity of

\displaystyle|u_{m}^{\phi}(n)|

\displaystyle=|u_{m}^{\phi+\langle m^{\prime},\alpha\rangle}(n-m^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{2}e^{-r|2\pi|^{\nu}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|n_{i}-m^{\prime}_{i}|)^{\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{2}e^{-r|2\pi|^{\nu}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|n_{i}|-|m^{\prime}_{i}|)^{\nu}}

for

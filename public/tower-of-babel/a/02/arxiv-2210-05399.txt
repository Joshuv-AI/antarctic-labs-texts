A note on weight systems which are quantum states
Carlo Collari

Abstract
. A result of Corfield, Sati, and Schreiber asserts that 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} -weight systems associated to the defining representation are quantum states. In this short note we extend this result to all 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} -weight systems corresponding to labelling by symmetric and exterior powers of the defining representation.

1.
Introduction

Weight systems and chord diagrams are central objects in the study of finite-type invariants, and Chern-Simons theory, in different contexts.
Universal Vassiliev-type invariants typically take values in a space of diagrams – e.g. Jacobi, chord, etc. – and weight systems are used to recover specific invariants from the universal ones.
General references for these topics are, for instance,
[
2
,
6
,
9
]
.

To study finite type invariant for braids, rather than links, a relevant space of diagrams is the space of horizontal chord diagrams.
Recently, horizontal chord diagrams appeared as natural objects in the study of a mathematical framework for quantum physics of branes
[
3
,
5
]
.
Roughly speaking, an horizontal chord diagram on
N
-strands is given by
N
lines oriented upwards (
strands
or
Wilson lines
), and a number (possibly none) of horizontal segments (
chords
) joining pairs of strands – cf. Figure
1
.(a).
One advantage of horizontal chord diagrams with respect to multi-circle chord diagrams is that they can be endowed with a natural algebraic structure.
The algebra of horizontal chord diagrams
\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}
is defined as the algebra generated by (formal) complex linear combinations of horizontal chord diagrams, where the multiplication is given by stacking two diagrams one on top of the other (cf. Figure
1
.(b)), modulo the infinitesimal pure braid relations (see Figure
2
). Further, we have an anti-linear involution
\star
on
\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}
which acts on each horizontal chord diagram by reversing the direction of the strands, and reflecting the page along an horizontal axis. This involution endows
\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}
with the structure of complex
\star
-algebra (Definition
4
).
In
[
5
]
(see also
[
3
, Section 4]
), the
\star
-algebra of horizontal chord diagrams
\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}
was interpreted as "higher observables" on certain brane moduli.
This interpretation is coherent with some expected effects in the quantum theory of branes.

Given a
\star
-algebra of observables
\mathscr{O}
, a
quantum state
(or, simply,
state
) is linear map
\varphi:\mathscr{O}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\varphi(x\cdot x^{\star})\geq 0
, for all
x\in\mathscr{O}
, and
\varphi(1_{\mathscr{O}})>0
.
A
weight system
on horizontal chord diagrams is, by definition, a (complex) linear function from
\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}
to
\mathbb{C}
.
Since horizontal chord diagrams can be interpreted as observables, it is natural to ask the following;

Question

(
[
3
, Question 1.1]
)
.

Which weight systems on horizontal chord diagrams are quantum states?

This question, which finds its motivation theoretical physics, is also quite interesting from the mathematical viewpoint. In particular, we may restrict ourselves to the case of Lie algebra weight systems associated to a labelling of the Wilson lines by (finite-dimensional) irreducible representations.
These are used to define quantum link invariants from the Kontsevich integral.

In
[
3
]
it was shown that the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight systems associated to the defining representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, which are generators of sorts for all weight systems on
\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}
, are indeed quantum states.
The proof of
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
is conceptually simple and exploits a interesting subtle relation between these weight systems and certain distance kernels in Cayley graphs.
The aim of this note is to extend the main result of
[
3
]
to
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight systems associated to more general sets of labellings.
Recall that an irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-representation is identified by a Young diagram and a complex number. In this paper we are only concerned with the case when this complex number is
1
. We call these representations
Young diagram representations
.

Theorem 1
.

All
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight systems associated to labelling by symmetric and/or exterior powers of the standard representation are quantum states.

It is well known that the symmetric powers of the standard representation are all the irreducible Young diagram representations of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
.

Corollary 2
.

All
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
-weight systems corresponding to irreducible Young diagram representations are quantum states.

The fundamental representations of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
are the given by exterior powers of the standard representation, which gives the next corollary.

Corollary 3
.

All
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight systems corresponding to any labelling given by fundamental representations are quantum states.

The paper is organised as follows: in Section 2 we recall some basic definitions and properties of
\star
-algebras, horizontal chord diagrams, representations of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, and Young symmetrisers.
In Section 3 we prove our main result.

2.1.
The
\star
-algebra of horizontal chord diagrams

We start with a definition.

Definition 4
.

Given a commutative ring
C
endowed with a ring involution
\overline{\cdot}:C\to C
.
A
\star
-algebra
, or
involutive algebra
, over
C
is a unital associative
C
-algebra
\mathscr{O}
together with an involution
\star:\mathscr{O}\to\mathscr{O}
, such that:

(A1)

(1_{\mathscr{O}})^{\star}=1_{\mathscr{O}}
;

(A2)

(z\cdot a+w\cdot b)^{\star}=\overline{z}\cdot a^{\star}+\overline{w}\cdot b^{\star}
, for all
z,w\in C
and
a,b\in\mathscr{O}
;

(A3)

(ab)^{\star}=b^{\star}a^{\star}
, for all
a,b\in\mathscr{O}
.

A
morphism of
\star
-algebras
is a morphism of algebras which commutes with the involution.

A first class of examples of
\star
-algebras is given by group rings.

Example 5
.

Given a group
G
, the group ring
\mathbb{C}[G]
has a natural structure of
\star
-algebra given by setting

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}\cdot g_{i}\right)^{\star}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\overline{z}_{i}\cdot g_{i}^{-1},

for all
z_{1},...,z_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and
g_{1},...,g_{k}\in G
.

In the next example we present a formal definition of the
\star

Example 6

An

N

is an element of the free monoid

generated by the pairs

\uparrow_{N}
.
We also consider the

\mathscr{D}^{\rm h}_{0}=\{\uparrow_{0}\}
.
This monoid has a natural involution
⋆

[(i_{1},j_{1})\circ\cdots\circ(i_{r},j_{r})]^{\star}=(i_{r},j_{r})\circ\cdots\circ(i_{1},j_{1}).

The

N

is given by the quotient algebra

\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}\coloneqq\frac{\mathbb{C}[\mathscr{D}^{\rm h}_{N}]}{I},

where

is the ideal generated by elements of the form

(2T)

(i,j)\circ(k,l)-(k,l)\circ(i,j),\qquad 1\leq i<j<k<l\leq N,

and of the form

(4T)

(i,j)\circ(i,k)+(i,j)\circ(j,k)-(i,k)\circ(i,j)-(j,k)\circ(i,j),\quad 1\leq i<j<k\leq N,

Finally, define the

\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}=\bigoplus_{N\geq 0}\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}

Extending

This involution has actually a deeper and more abstract interpretation as the antipode of an Hopf algebra associated to the homology of some loop spaces – cf.

For small values of

2.2.
Horizontal chord diagrams, permutations, and standard weight systems

To each horizontal chord diagram
C=(i_{r},j_{r})\circ\cdots\circ(i_{1},j_{1})\in\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}
there is a naturally associated permutation:

(1)

\sigma(C)=\tau_{i_{r},j_{r}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{i_{1},j_{1}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{N},

where
\tau_{i,j}
denotes the transposition of
i
and
j
.
Recall, from Example
5
, that the group ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{N}]
has a natural structure of
\star
-algebra.
Indeed, we have the following;

Proposition 7
.

The map
\sigma:\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}\to\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{N}]:C\mapsto\sigma(C)
is a morphism of
\star
-algebras.

Proof.

We have to prove that
\sigma
is a well-defined ring homomorphism. That is we have to show that the images of elements in Equations (
2T
) and (
4T
) are trivial.
This is immediate for the elements in Equation (
2T
).
On the other hand, a direct computation shows that

\sigma((i,j)\circ(i,k))=\sigma((j,k)\circ(i,j))=(j\ k\ i)

and that

\sigma((i,j)\circ(j,k))=\sigma((i,k)\circ(i,j))=(i\ k\ j).

Since
\sigma
commutes with
\star
, this concludes the proof.
∎

Following
[
1
, Section 2.2]
, the
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}

standard

C\in\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}
the integer

(2)

W_{\tt st}(C)=w_{\tt st}(\sigma(C))\coloneqq n^{\text{number of cycles in }\sigma(C)}\ ,

extended by
\mathbb{C}
-linearity. We remark that here the number of cycles in a permutation also includes the trivial cycles (i.e. the fixed points).

2.3.
Tensor splitting

Another important operation on
\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}
is what we call tensor splitting.
Intuitively, the
\underline{i}
-tensor splitting, with
\underline{i}=(i_{1},...,i_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, is obtained by replacing the
r
-th strand with
i_{r}
parallel strands, and replacing each chord with the sum of all possible “liftings” of said chord in the new horizontal chord diagram. More formally, we have the following definition.

Definition 8

(
[
1
, Definition 2.2]
)
.

Let
\underline{i}=(i_{1},...,i_{N})
be a
N
-tuple of positive integers.
The
\underline{i}
-tensor splitting
is the ring homomorphism

\Delta^{\underline{i}}:\>\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}\to\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{\ell_{N+1}(\underline{i})}:\>(j,k)\mapsto\sum_{r=\ell_{j}(\underline{i})+1}^{\ell_{j+1}(\underline{i})}\sum_{s=\ell_{k}(\underline{i})+1}^{\ell_{k+1}(\underline{i})}(r,s)

where
\ell_{1}(\underline{i})=0
and
\ell_{r}(\underline{i})=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{r-1}
, for
0<r\leq N+1
.

Let us see in an example the effect of the tensor splitting.

Example 9
.

Denote
C=(2,3)\circ(1,3)\in\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{3}
, we explicitly compute some tensor splittings in this case; for instance, we have that

\Delta^{(1,2,1)}(C)=(2,4)\circ(1,4)+(3,4)\circ(1,4)\in\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{4},

and also that

\Delta^{(3,1,1)}(C)=(4,5)\circ(1,5)+(4,5)\circ(2,5)+(4,5)\circ(3,5)\in\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{5}.

Note that
\Delta^{\underline{1}}
, where
\underline{1}=(1,...,1)
, is just the identity map. We conclude this subsection with the following, useful, observation;

Remark 10
.

The tensor splitting
\Delta^{\underline{i}}:\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}\to\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{\ell_{N+1}(\underline{i})}
is a morphism of
\star
-algebras.

2.4.

. The image of the action of

on

is called the

corresponding to
\lambda
, and is denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

The restriction of
(\rho_{\tt st}^{\mathfrak{sl}})^{\otimes k}
to the Weyl module

associated to

is isomorphic to the irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
with highest weight

, denoted by
\rho^{\mathfrak{sl}}_{\lambda}
.
We can extend

to a representation of

. For

, consider the representation

\rho_{z}(g+k{\rm Id}_{\mathbb{C}})[x]=zkx,\quad\forall g\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n},\ k,x\in\mathbb{C}\,.

Each irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}\langle{\rm Id}_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is isomorphic to a tensor product of the form
\rho_{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{z}
– see
[

– where the tensor product of two representations, say
\rho
and

(\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime})(g)[v_{1}\otimes v_{2}]=\rho(g)[v_{1}]\otimes v_{2}+v_{1}\otimes\rho^{\prime}(g)[v_{2}].

We refer to the Young diagram representation

simply as
\lambda
.
The symmetric and exterior power of the defining representation are associated to the partitions of the form

3.
Proof of the main result

In
[
3
]
it was proven that the standard
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
weight system is a quantum state. The proof goes as follows: first, one observes that
W_{\tt st}
factors as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{N}]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{\tt st}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{\tt st}}

where
\sigma
is the morphism of
\star
-algebras defined in Subsection
2.2
.
The key observation made in
[
3
]
is that
w_{\tt st}
is the kernel of the Cayley distance function, which is a class functions (i.e. it is constant on conjugacy classes). Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of class functions on the symmetric group are known – cf.
[
3
, Section 3]
and references therein. So one concludes by observing that these eigenvalues are non-negative for all possible
n
. This proves that
w_{\tt st}
is a quantum state, and the main result of
[
3
]
is now a consequence of the following easy lemma.

Lemma 22
.

Let
\mathscr{O}
and
\mathscr{O}^{\prime}
be
\star
-algebras.
If
\phi:\mathscr{O}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}
is a state, and
f:\mathscr{O}\to\mathscr{O}^{\prime}
is a morphism of
\star
-algebras, then
\phi\circ f
is a state.

We want to use a similar reasoning to prove our main result. We know that
W_{\underline{\rho}}
factors as the following composition:

\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}\overset{\Delta_{|{\underline{\rho}}|}}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{|{\underline{\rho}}|}\overset{\sigma}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{|{\underline{\rho}}|}]\overset{c_{\underline{\rho}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{|{\underline{\rho}}|}]\overset{w_{\tt st}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{C}\ .

While the map
\sigma\circ\Delta_{|\underline{\rho}|}
is a morphism of
\star
-algebras, the map
c_{\underline{\rho}}
is not – for any choice of
{\underline{\rho}}
which is not
{\tt st}
. Therefore, we cannot conclude using Lemma
22
.
Nonetheless, we can show that the composition
w_{\tt st}\circ c_{{\underline{\rho}}}
is a state for suitable choices of
{\underline{\rho}}
, proving our main result.

Definition 23
.

Let
\mathscr{O}
be a
\star
-algebra, and
x\in\mathscr{O}
. A linear function
\phi:\mathscr{O}\to\mathbb{C}
is
class-like with respect to
x
if
\phi(xy)=\phi(yx)
, for each
y\in\mathscr{O}
.

Linear functions arising from class functions are class-like functions

Example 24
.

If
f:\mathfrak{S}_{m}\to\mathbb{C}
is a class function (i.e. it is constant in each conjugacy class), then

\phi\left(\sum_{i}z_{i}\sigma_{i}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{i}z_{i}f(\sigma_{i})\ ,

is class-like for all
c
.
In fact, given
c=\sum_{j}x_{j}\eta_{j}
and
x=\sum_{i}z_{i}\sigma_{i}
, we have:

\phi\left(cx\right)=\sum_{i,j}x_{j}z_{i}f(\eta_{j}\sigma_{i})=\sum_{i,j}z_{i}x_{j}f(\sigma_{i}\eta_{j})=\phi\left(xc\right)\ .

States which are class-like with respect to special elements can be used to produce new states, as shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 25
.

Let
c\in\mathscr{O}
be such that
c^{2}=\alpha c
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and
c^{\star}=c
. If a state
\phi:\mathscr{O}\to\mathbb{C}
is class-like with respect to
c
, then
\psi=\phi\circ(c\cdot)
is also a state.

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathscr{O}
, then

\psi(aa^{\star})=\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\phi(c^{2}aa^{\star})=\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\phi(caa^{\star}c)=\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\phi(caa^{\star}c^{\star})=\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\phi(ca(ca)^{\star})\geq 0.

∎

We remark that the hypothesis
c=c^{\star}
plays a key role in the above lemma. Now, we are ready to prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
{\underline{\rho}}=(\lambda^{1},...,\lambda^{N})
be a labelling such that each
\lambda_{i}
is either a symmetric or an exterior power of the standard representation.
Since
W_{\underline{\rho}}(\uparrow_{N})>0
, see Remark
10
, we only have to prove that
W_{\underline{\rho}}(CC^{\star})\geq 0
, for each
C\in\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}
.
We recall that the following triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{A}^{\rm h}_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{|\underline{\rho}|}]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma\circ\Delta^{(|\lambda^{1}|,...,|\lambda^{N}|)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{\tt st}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{\tt st}\circ(c_{\underline{\rho}}\cdot)}

is commutative.
From Proposition
7

\sigma\circ\Delta_{\rho}
is a morphism of
\star

is a state it is sufficient to prove that

Note that

Furthermore, since each

Since

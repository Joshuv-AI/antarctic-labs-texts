Efficient Channel Estimation for RIS-Aided MIMO Communications with Unitary Approximate Message Passing
Yabo Guo, Peng Sun, Zhengdao Yuan, Chongwen Huang, Qinghua Guo, Zhongyong Wang, Chau Yuen

Abstract
Reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) is very promising for wireless networks to achieve high energy efficiency, extended coverage, improved capacity, massive connectivity, etc. To unleash the full potentials of RIS-aided communications, acquiring accurate channel state information is crucial, which however is very challenging. For RIS-aided multiple-input and multiple-output (MIMO) communications, the existing channel estimation methods have computational complexity growing rapidly with the number of RIS units N N (e.g., in the order of N 2 N^{2} or N 3 N^{3} ) and/or have special requirements on the matrices involved (e.g., the matrices need to be sparse for algorithm convergence to achieve satisfactory performance), which hinder their applications. In this work, instead of using the conventional signal model in the literature, we derive a new signal model obtained through proper vectorization and reduction operations. Then, leveraging the unitary approximate message passing (UAMP), we develop a more efficient channel estimator that has complexity linear with N N and does not have special requirements on the relevant matrices, thanks to the robustness of UAMP. These facilitate the applications of the proposed algorithm to a general RIS-aided MIMO system with a larger N N . Moreover, extensive numerical results show that the proposed estimator delivers much better performance and/or requires significantly less number of training symbols, thereby leading to notable reductions in both training overhead and latency.

II
System Model and Problem Formulation

Fig. 1:
Illustration of RIS-aided MIMO uplink transmission.

We consider RIS-aided MIMO uplink transmission as shown in Fig.
1
, where the BS equipped with
M
antennas receives signals from
K
users. A RIS with
N
passive reflecting elements is equipped between the BS and users, each user equipped with a single antenna (the extension to multiple antennas is straightforward).
The RIS is attached to the facade of a building in the vicinity of users. Due to the highly attenuation caused by unfavorable propagation environments such as tall buildings, the direct propagation path between the BS and users is neglected
[
29
]
.

We denote the channel matrix between the BS and the RIS by
\bm{G}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}
, and use
\bm{H}\triangleq[\bm{h}_{1}^{u},\ldots,\bm{h}_{K}^{u}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times K}
to represent the channel matrix between the RIS and the
K
users, where
\bm{h}_{k}^{u}
denote the channel vector from the
k
-th user to the RIS. With the
l
-th (
l=1,2,\ldots,L
) RIS phase configuration, the
K
consecutive received signals
\bm{Y}_{l}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K}
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{Y}_{l}=\bm{G}\textbf{Diag}(\bm{\Phi}_{l,:})\bm{H}\bm{X}+\bm{W}_{l},

(1)

where
\bm{\Phi}_{l,:}
is the
l
-th row of the RIS phase matrix
\bm{\Phi}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times N}
(part of the RIS phase configurations) and
\bm{W}_{l}
models the zero mean complex additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) with precision
\beta
(i.e., variance
\beta^{-1}
), and
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times K}
denotes the transmitted orthogonal training matrix from the users, i.e.,
\bm{X}\bm{X}^{H}=\bm{I}_{K}
. Right-multiplying both sides of
(
1
)
by
\bm{X}^{H}
leads to

\displaystyle\bm{\tilde{Y}}_{l}=\bm{G}\textbf{Diag}(\bm{\Phi}_{l,:})\bm{H}+\bm{\tilde{W}}_{l},

(2)

where
\bm{\tilde{Y}}_{l}\triangleq\bm{Y}_{l}\bm{X}^{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K}
, and
\bm{\tilde{W}}_{l}\triangleq\bm{W}_{l}\bm{X}^{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K}
.
As
\bm{X}
is a unitary matrix, entries of
\bm{\tilde{W}}_{l}
are still zero-mean white Gaussian with the same noise precision
\beta
.

During the training process,
L
configurations of the RIS are used, leading to
L
matrices
\{\bm{\tilde{Y}}_{l},l=1,...,L\}
, based which we aim to estimate the channel matrices. As the number of RIS unit
N
can be large, low complexity of channel estimators is crucial for practical implementation. The cubic or quadratic complexity (in terms of
N
) of existing channel estimation algorithms can be a concern in their applications. During the training process, the RIS undergoes
L
configurations. In order to reduce the training overhead and communication latency, a small
L
is desirable.
We address the above challenges by reformulating the RIS channel estimation to a structured signal recovery problem with a new signal model and develop an efficient message passing algorithm. In particular, we do not make any special requirements on the matrices
\bm{G}
,
\bm{H}
or
\bm{\Phi}
.

III
Problem Reformulation and Factor Graph Representation

III-A

New Model for RIS Channel Estimation

Instead of using model
(
2
)
directly, we reformulate it to a new model through vectorization and reduction, which leads to a structured signal recovery problem. Vectorizing
(
2
)
leads to

\displaystyle\textbf{vec}(\bm{\tilde{Y}}_{l})=(\bm{H}^{T}\otimes\bm{G})\textbf{vec}\left(\textbf{Diag}(\bm{\Phi}_{l,:})\right)+\textbf{vec}(\bm{\tilde{W}}_{l}),

(3)

where the vector

(4)

and

donates the
n

\bm{\Phi}_{l,:}
.
Here we note that, the vector

\textbf{vec}\left(\textbf{Diag}(\bm{\Phi}_{l,:})\right)
are separated by all-zero vector
\mathbf{0}_{N}
. This can be exploited to significantly reduce the dimension of
(
3
)

\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\Phi}^{T}_{l,:}=(\bm{H}^{T}\otimes\bm{G})\textbf{vec}\left(\textbf{Diag}(\bm{\Phi}_{l,:})\right)

\bm{\tilde{w}}_{l}\triangleq\textbf{vec}(\bm{\tilde{W}}_{l})\in\mathbb{C}^{KM\times 1}

By stacking

\bm{\tilde{Y}}=\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\Phi}^{T}+\bm{\tilde{W}}
,
which is rewritten as

\bm{Y}=\bm{\Phi}\bm{S}+\bm{W}.

(6)

where
\bm{Y}=\bm{\tilde{Y}}^{T}
,
\bm{W}=\bm{\tilde{W}}^{T}
and

\bm{S}=\bm{\tilde{S}}^{T}=(\bm{H}^{T}\odot\bm{G})^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times KM}.

(7)

Now we can see from (

\bm{S}
, which admits the structure (

\bm{S}

\bm{H}

\bm{G}

IV
UAMP Based Message Passing Algorithm For RIS Channel Estimation

In this section, we develop an efficient message passing algorithm for approximate inference, where UAMP is incorporated to deal with the most computationally intensive part of message passing, which is crucial to achieving low complexity while with high robustness.
The message passing algorithm carries out an iterative process, where each iteration involves a forward message passing process and backward message passing process in the graph shown in Fig.
2
or Fig.
3
.
We use
m_{A\rightarrow B}(x)
to denote a message passed from node
A
to node
B
, which is a function of
x
. For Gaussian messages, the arrows above its mean and variance indicate the direction of the message passing. In addition, we use
b(x)
to denote the belief of a variable
x
. Note that, if a forward computation requires backward messages, the relevant messages in the previous iteration is used by default.

IV-A

Forward Message Passing

In the forward direction, according to the rules of variational message passing
[
35
]
, we have

\displaystyle m_{f_{\bm{r}_{j}}\rightarrow\bm{z}_{j}}(\bm{z}_{j})

\displaystyle\propto\exp\left\{\int_{\beta}\mathfrak{b}(\beta)\log f_{\bm{r}_{j}}\right\}

\displaystyle\propto\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{z}_{j};\bm{r}_{j},\hat{\beta}^{-1}\right),

(11)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(\beta)

\displaystyle\propto m_{f_{\bm{r}_{j}}\rightarrow\beta}(\beta)f_{\beta}

\displaystyle\propto\beta^{LJ-1}\exp\left\{\sum_{j=1}-\beta\left(\|\bm{r}_{j}-\hat{\bm{z}_{j}}\|^{2}+\mathbf{1}^{T}\bm{\nu}_{\bm{z}_{j}}\right)\right\},

(12)

and

\hat{\beta}=\int_{\beta}\beta\mathfrak{b}(\beta)=\frac{LJ}{\sum_{j=1}(\|\bm{r}_{j}-\hat{\bm{z}_{j}}\|^{2}+\mathbf{1}_{L}^{T}\bm{\nu}_{\bm{z}_{j}})}.

(13)

It is noted that in the above equation,
\hat{\bm{z}}_{j}
and the message
m_{f_{\bm{r}_{j}}\rightarrow\beta}(\beta)
are required to compute
\hat{\beta}
, which are obtained from the last iteration and their computations are delayed to (
71
) and (
74
).

The Gaussian form of the message in (
IV-A
) suggests the following model

\bm{r}_{j}=\bm{z}_{j}+\bm{w}_{j},

(14)

where the noise
\bm{w}_{j}
is Gaussian with mean zero and precision
\hat{\beta}
. This allows seamless integration with the forward recursion of UAMP. Here, assume that the mean and variance of
\bm{s}_{j}
are available, i.e.,
\hat{\bm{s}}_{j}
and
\nu_{\bm{s}_{j}}

have a common variance
\nu_{\bm{s}_{j}}
, and the corresponding computation will be detailed later. Following UAMP, we define a vector
\bm{\psi}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times 1}
as

\bm{\psi}=|\bm{\Psi}|^{2}\bm{1}_{N}.

(15)

Then we calculate two vectors
\bm{\nu_{p_{j}}}
and
\bm{p}_{j}
as

\bm{\nu_{p_{j}}}=\bm{\psi}\nu_{\bm{{s}}_{j}},

(16)

\bm{p}_{j}=\bm{\Psi}\hat{{\bm{s}}}_{j}-\bm{\nu_{p_{j}}}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{j},

(17)

where
\bm{\mu}_{j}
is a vector that is computed in last iteration. According to UAMP, we update the intermediate vectors
\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\mu}_{j}}
and
\bm{\mu}_{j}
by

\displaystyle\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\mu}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{L}./(\bm{\nu}_{\bm{p}_{j}}+\hat{\beta}^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{L}),

(18)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{j}

\displaystyle=\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\mu}_{j}}\cdot(\bm{r}_{j}-\bm{p}_{j}),

(19)

Then compute vectors
\bm{\nu}_{\bm{q}_{j}}
and

with

\displaystyle\bm{\nu}_{\bm{q}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{N}./|\bm{\Psi}^{H}|^{2}\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\mu}_{j}},

(20)

\displaystyle\bm{q}_{j}

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{s}}_{j}+\bm{\nu}_{\bm{q}_{j}}.\bm{\Psi}^{H}\bm{\mu}_{j},

(21)

The message
\bm{q}_{j}
and

are the mean and variance of

. According to the belief propagation derivation of (U)AMP, we have

m_{\bm{s}_{j}\rightarrow f_{\bm{s}}}(\bm{s}_{j})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{s}_{j};\bm{q}_{j},\textbf{Diag}(\bm{\nu}_{\bm{q}_{j}})).

(22)

Stack

and

into matrices as

IV-B

Backward message passing

Next, we elaborate the backward message passing. The backward message from
h_{k,n}
to
f_{\tilde{s}_{m,k,n}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle m_{h_{k,n}\rightarrow f_{\tilde{s}_{m,k,n}}}\left(h_{k,n}\right)=\frac{\mathfrak{b}\left(h_{k,n}\right)}{m_{f_{\tilde{s}_{m,k,n}\rightarrow h_{k,n}}}\left(h_{k,n}\right)},

(53)

They are represented collectively as
m_{\bm{h}_{n}\rightarrow f_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n}}}\left(\bm{h}_{n}\right)
, which is Gaussian with mean
\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{h}}_{n}
and variance
\textbf{Diag}(\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{h}_{n}})
. With the factor graph shown in Fig.
4
, the mean and variance can be computed as

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{h}_{n}}=\left(\left(\mathbf{1}_{K}./\bm{\nu}_{\bm{h}_{n}}\right)\otimes\mathbf{1}_{M}-\mathbf{1}_{J}./\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{h}_{n}}\right)^{.-1},

(54)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{h}}_{n}=\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{h}_{n}}\cdot\left((\hat{\bm{h}}_{n}./\bm{\nu}_{\bm{h}_{n}})\otimes\mathbf{1}_{M}-\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{h}}_{n}./\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{h}_{n}}\right),

(55)

where
\bm{\nu}_{\bm{h}_{n}}=[\nu_{h_{1,n}},\ldots,\nu_{h_{K,n}}]^{T}
,
\hat{\bm{h}}_{n}=[\hat{h}_{1,n},\ldots,\hat{h}_{K,n}]^{T}
,
\left[\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{h}_{n}}\right]_{(k-1)M+m}=\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{h_{m,k,n}}
and
\left[{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{h}}_{n}}\right]_{(k-1)M+m}=\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{h}_{m,k,n}
.

Similarly, the message
m_{\bm{g}_{n}\rightarrow f_{\bm{s}_{n}}}\left(\bm{g}_{n}\right)
is also Gaussian with mean
\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{g}}_{n}
and variance
\textbf{Diag}(\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{g}_{n}})
, which can be computed as

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{g}_{n}}=\left(\mathbf{1}_{K}\otimes\left(\mathbf{1}_{M}./\bm{\nu}_{\bm{g}_{n}}\right)-\mathbf{1}_{J}./\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{g}_{n}}\right)^{.-1},

(56)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{g}}_{n}=\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{g}_{n}}\cdot\left(\mathbf{1}_{K}\otimes(\hat{\bm{g}}_{n}./\bm{\nu}_{\bm{g}_{n}})-\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{g}}_{n}./\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{g}_{n}}\right),

(57)

where
\bm{\nu}_{\bm{g}_{n}}=[{\nu}_{{g}_{1,n}},\ldots,{\nu}_{{g}_{M,n}}]^{T}
,
\hat{\bm{g}}_{n}=[\hat{g}_{1,n},\ldots,\hat{g}_{M,n}]^{T}
,
\left[\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{g}_{n}}\right]_{(m-1)K+k}=\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\nu}_{g_{m,k,n}}
and
\left[{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{\bm{g}}_{n}}\right]_{(m-1)K+k}=\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\rightarrow}{g}_{m,k,n}
. Then, the backward message
m_{f_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n}}\rightarrow\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n}}(\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n};\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\tilde{s}}}_{n},\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n}})
with

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\tilde{s}}}_{n}=\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{h}}_{n}\cdot\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{g}}_{n},

(58)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n}}=|\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{h}}_{n}|^{2}\cdot\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{g}_{n}}+\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{h}_{n}}\cdot|\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{g}}_{n}|^{2}+\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{h}_{n}}\cdot\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{g}_{n}},

(59)

\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n}}=[\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{1,n}}^{T},\ldots,\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{K,n}}^{T}]^{T}

Following (U)AMP, the backward message is combined with message

\displaystyle\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{k,n}}=\left(1/\nu_{\bm{q}_{k,n}}\mathbf{1}_{M}+\mathbf{1}_{M}./\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{k,n}}\right)^{.-1},

(60)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\tilde{s}}}_{k,n}=\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{k,n}}\cdot\left(1/\nu_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{k,n}}\hat{\bm{q}}_{k,n}+\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\tilde{s}}}_{k,n}./\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\leftarrow}{\bm{\nu}}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{k,n}}\right),

(61)

Stack
\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{k,n}}
and

\displaystyle\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{n}}=[\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{1,n}}^{T},\ldots,\bm{\nu}_{\bm{\tilde{s}}_{K,n}}^{T}]^{T},

(64)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\tilde{s}}}_{n}=[\hat{\bm{\tilde{s}}}_{1,n}^{T},\ldots,\hat{\bm{\tilde{s}}}_{K,n}^{T}]^{T}.

(65)

Further, we can obtain

and
\hat{\bm{S}}=\hat{\bm{\tilde{S}}}^{T}
with

\displaystyle\bm{\nu}_{\bm{S}}=[\bm{\nu}_{{\bm{s}}_{1}},\ldots,\bm{\nu}_{{\bm{s}}_{J}}],

(66)

\displaystyle{\nu}_{\bm{s}_{j}}=\langle{\bm{\nu}_{\bm{s}_{j}}}\rangle,

(67)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{S}}=[\hat{\bm{s}}_{1},\ldots,\hat{{\bm{s}}}_{J}],

(68)

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{s}}}_{j}=[\hat{s}_{j,1},\ldots,\hat{s}_{j,N}],

(69)

According to the belief propagation derivation of (U)AMP, it holds that

m_{\bm{z}_{j}\rightarrow f_{\bm{r}_{j}}}(\bm{z}_{j})=m_{f_{\bm{z}_{j}}\rightarrow\bm{z}_{j}}(\bm{z}_{j})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{z}_{j};\bm{p}_{j},\textbf{Diag}(\bm{\nu_{p_{j}}})),

(70)

where
\bm{p}_{j}
and
\bm{\nu_{p_{j}}}
are computed in the forward direction. It is noted that the factor node

connects the variable node
\beta

can be expressed as

(71)

where

is the approximate marginal of
\bm{z}_{j}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(\bm{z}_{j})

\displaystyle\propto m_{f_{\bm{r}_{j}}\rightarrow\bm{z}_{j}}(\bm{z}_{j})m_{\bm{z}_{j}\rightarrow f_{\bm{r}_{j}}}(\bm{z}_{j})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{z}_{j};\hat{\bm{z}}_{j},\textbf{Diag}\left(\bm{\nu}_{\bm{z}_{j}}\right)\right),

(72)

where

\bm{\nu}_{\bm{z}_{j}}=\mathbf{1}_{L}./\left(\mathbf{1}_{L}\cdot/\bm{\nu}_{\bm{p}_{j}}+\hat{\beta}\mathbf{1}_{L}\right),

(73)

\hat{\bm{z}}_{j}=\bm{\nu}_{\bm{z}_{j}}\cdot\left(\bm{p}_{j}\cdot/\bm{\nu}_{\bm{p}_{j}}+\hat{\beta}\bm{r}_{j}\right),

(74)

with
\hat{\beta}
being the approximate a posteriori mean of the noise precision that is obtained with (
13

m_{f_{\bm{r}_{j}}\rightarrow\bm{z}_{j}}(\bm{z}_{j})
is required, which is Gaussian, i.e.,
m_{f_{\bm{r}_{j}}\rightarrow\bm{z}_{j}}(\bm{z}_{j})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{z}_{j};\bm{r}_{j},\hat{\beta}^{-1})
, and its derivation is shown in (
IV-A

(75)

This is the end of backward message passing.

and unitary transformation, and the complexity is
\mathcal{O}(NL^{2})
.
It noted that the SVD can be carried out offline and there is no matrix inversion involved in Algorithm
1
. Also note that the formulated problem is a multiple measurement vector one. The complexity of the proposed algorithm is dominated by the computation of
\bm{p}
in step 1, which requires
\mathcal{O}(NLKM+LKM)
, and the computations of
\nu_{\bm{q}}
and
\bm{q}
in step 6, which require
\mathcal{O}(NLKM+NKM)
and
\mathcal{O}(NLKM)
, respectively. It can be shown that the overall complexity of the algorithm is
\mathcal{O}(NLKM)+\mathcal{O}(NKM)
per iteration.
As we consider a general RIS-aided MIMO system without any special requirements on the (channel or RIS phase) matrices, the most relevant algorithms for comparison are the ALS-based algorithm and VAMP-based algorithm in
[
29
]
. The complexity of the ALS-based algorithm is
\mathcal{O}(NLKM+L(M+K)N^{2})+\mathcal{O}(N^{3})
per iteration, and that of the VAMP-based algorithm is
\mathcal{O}((M+K)N^{3}+(M+K)N^{2})
per iteration. It is noted that
N
is the number of RIS units, which can be much larger than
M
,
K
and
L
. From the analysis, we can see that the complexity of the proposed algorithm, which is linear with
N
, is significantly smaller than that of the ALS or VAMP-based algorithm.

In this section, we derive the CRLB for the RIS channel estimation, which is used to serve as another performance benchmark, besides the ALS and VAMP-based algorithms.

We firstly rewrite the system model as

\displaystyle\bm{\tilde{Y}}=(\bm{H}^{T}\odot\bm{G})\bm{\Phi}^{T}+\bm{\tilde{W}},

(76)

and define a complex parameter
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{C}^{2N(M+K)\times 1}

\bm{H}
and
\bm{G}

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}\triangleq\left[\bm{h}_{1}^{T},\ldots,\bm{h}_{N}^{T},\bm{g}_{1}^{T},\ldots,\bm{g}_{N}^{T},\bm{h}_{1}^{H},\ldots,\bm{h}_{N}^{H},\bm{g}_{1}^{H},\ldots,\bm{g}_{N}^{H}\right]^{T}.

(77)

The likelihood function of

\displaystyle p(\bm{\tilde{Y}};\bm{\theta})\!=\!\left(\pi\sigma^{2}\right)^{-KML}\!\exp\!\left\{\!-{\sigma^{-2}}\sum_{l=1}^{L}\left\|\bm{\tilde{y}}_{l}\!-\!(\bm{H}^{T}\!\odot\!\bm{G})\bm{\Phi}^{T}_{l,:}\right\|^{2}\right\}\!,

(78)

\displaystyle\ln(p(\bm{\tilde{Y}};\bm{\theta}))\!\!=\!\!-KML\ln\left(\!\pi\sigma^{2}\right)\!\!-\!\!{\sigma^{-2}}\sum_{l=1}^{L}\left\|\bm{\tilde{y}}_{l}\!-\!(\bm{H}^{T}\!\!\odot\!\bm{G})\bm{\Phi}^{T}_{l,:}\right\|^{2}\!\!,

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have addressed the issue of channel estimation in RIS-aided MIMO communications. Through vectorization and reduction, we obtain a new signal model for channel estimation, based on which a message passing based algorithm is developed, leveraging UAMP. Compared to the state-of-the-art algorithms, the proposed algorithm does not have any special requirements on the matrices involved, and it shows significant advantages in computational complexity, estimation performance and training overhead.
Extensive numerical results demonstrate the merits of the proposed algorithm.

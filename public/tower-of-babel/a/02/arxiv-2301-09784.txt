On Kähler Ricci shrinker surfaces
Yu Li, and, Bing Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we prove that any Kähler Ricci shrinker surface has bounded sectional curvature. By combining this estimate with previous research by numerous authors, we achieve a complete classification of all Kähler Ricci shrinker surfaces.

Introduction

A Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)
is a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
coupled with a smooth function
f
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}+\text{Hess}\,f=\frac{1}{2}g,

(1.1)

where the potential function
f
is normalized so that

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=f.

(1.2)

Ricci shrinkers play a fundamental role in the study of singularities of the Ricci flow. For the type-I Ricci flow, it was proved by Enders-Müller-Topping
[
28
]
that any proper blowup sequence converges smoothly to a non-trivial Ricci shrinker. In general, Ricci shrinkers provide crucial insights into the ancient solutions that arise from blowup sequences of Ricci flows.

In the case that
\dim M=n\leq 3
, the classification of all Ricci shrinkers has been fully established (cf.
[
31
]
[
56
]
[
60
]
[
12
]
, etc.). The complete list consists of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
S^{2}
,
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
S^{3}
,
S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, as well as their corresponding quotients. Consequently, the natural focus of study shifts to the classification of Ricci shrinkers when
n=4
. Significant advancements have been made in this direction over the past decade. The majority of classification results, to the best of our knowledge, require certain assumptions on the curvature (cf.
[
11
]
[

]
[

[

[

, etc.). In this paper, we drop all curvature assumptions in the Kähler category.

A Ricci shrinker
(M,g,f)
is called a Kähler Ricci shrinker if it admits a Kähler structure
J
such that
\nabla f
is a holomorphic vector field.
In complex coordinate charts, this means

\displaystyle R_{i\bar{j}}+f_{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{2}g_{i\bar{j}},\quad f_{ij}=f_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}=0.

Kähler manifolds of complex dimension
2
are often referred to as Kähler surfaces. This paper focuses on Kähler Ricci shrinker surfaces
(M^{2},g,J,f)
, where
2
denotes the complex dimension (by a slight abuse of notation).

Assuming bounded curvature, a classification of such surfaces was previously obtained by Bamler-Cifarelli-Conlon-Deruelle
[
5
]
, building upon the foundational work of Munteanu-Wang
[
55
]
and Cifarelli
[
17
]
. However, it remains unclear whether the bounded-curvature assumption is necessary.

In this paper, we remove the bounded-curvature assumption and establish a complete classification of all Kähler Ricci shrinker surfaces. (See also Theorem B of
[
5
]
for comparison.)

Theorem 1.1

(
Complete classification of Kähler Ricci shrinker surfaces
)
.

Let
(M^{2},g,J,f)
be a Kähler Ricci shrinker surface. Then
(M^{2},g,J,f)
is biholomorphic-isometric to one of the following manifolds.

(i)

A closed Fano surface with its unique Kähler Ricci shrinker metric;

(ii)

The Gaussian soliton
(\mathbb{C}^{2},g_{E})
;

(iii)

The FIK shrinker, constructed by Feldman-Ilmanen-Knopf
[
29
]
on the blowup of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
;

(iv)

The standard
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c})
;

(v)

The BCCD shrinker, abstractly constructed by Bamler-Cifarelli-Conlon-Deruelle
[
5
]
on the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
.

Let us briefly discuss the development and background of this classification.
We consider two cases for the complete manifold
M
: closed and non-compact. We will discuss each case separately.

(I)
M
is closed.

Since
M
is closed, we have
c_{1}(M,J)=\frac{1}{2\pi}[\mathrm{Rc}]>0
, which implies that
(M,J)
is a del Pezzo surface—a smooth Fano variety of dimension two. The classification of del Pezzo surfaces was established a long time ago (cf.
[

[

[

(M,J)

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

\text{Bl}_{k}\mathbb{P}^{2}

k

\mathbb{P}^{2}
in general position, with
0\leq k\leq 8
. Here, “general position” means that no three points are collinear, no six points lie on the same conic, and no eight points lie on a cubic curve with a double point at one of them. For
\text{Bl}_{k}\mathbb{P}^{2}
with
5\leq k\leq 8
, the configuration of these
k

, the potential function

is constant, then

[

\text{Bl}_{2}\mathbb{P}^{2}=\text{Bl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})

\text{Bl}_{2}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the existence of such a metric was established by Wang-Zhu

In sum, each del Pezzo surface admits a unique Kähler Ricci shrinker metric.

(II)
M
is noncompact.

The classification of Kähler Ricci shrinker surfaces is also known when the scalar curvature of
M
is bounded, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sup_{M}R<+\infty.

(1.3)

In fact, the curvature estimate established by Munteanu and Wang
[
53
, Theorem 1.1]
implies that under the condition (
1.3
),
M
has bounded Riemannian curvature:

\displaystyle\sup_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}|<+\infty.

(1.4)

In view of (
1.3
), the potential function
f
does not have critical points outside a compact set, and the flow lines of
\nabla f
can be parametrized by the level sets of
f
. Consequently, two possibilities arise: either the scalar curvature tends to zero along each flow line, or there exists a flow line along which the scalar curvature approaches a positive number.

By combining the curvature estimate (
1.4
) with the Kähler condition, Cifarelli, Conlon, and Deruelle
[
25
]
have demonstrated that the aforementioned dichotomy can be expressed more simply as: either
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x)=0
or
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x)=1
.

\bullet
If
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x)=0
, then
M
is the Gaussian metric on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
or the FIK metric on
\text{Bl}_{1}\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

This result is established by Conlon, Deruelle, and Sun (cf.
[
26
]
). Here, we provide a brief outline of the key points for the readers’ convenience. By applying the result of Munteanu and Wang
[
53
, Theorem 5.1]
, it is deduced that in this case, the Riemannian curvature decays quadratically, and
M
approaches an asymptotic metric cone with a smooth link. In Conlon, Deruelle, and Sun’s work (cf.
[
26
, Theorem E (3)]
), rigidity from complex analytic geometry is employed to demonstrate that the limiting cone must be biholomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Thus, by the adjunction formula, it follows that
M
is biholomorphic to either
\mathbb{C}^{2}
or
\text{Bl}_{1}\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In both cases, they establish that
g
possesses
U(2)
-symmetry and prove the uniqueness of the shrinker metric through the work of Feldman, Ilmanen, and Knopf
[
29
]
. In the former case,
g
is isometric to the Gaussian shrinker metric, while in the latter case,
g
is isometric to the Feldman-Ilmanen-Knopf metric (cf.
[
29
]
).

\bullet
If
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x)=1
, then
M
is the standard
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
, or the BCCD metric on
\text{Bl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C})
.

The classification of Kähler Ricci shrinker surfaces with
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x)=1
is completed in the works of Cifarelli-Conlon-Deruelle
[
25
]
and Bamler-Cifarelli-Conlon-Deruelle
[
5
]
. In Cifarelli-Conlon-Deruelle
[
25
]
, it is established that
M
is biholomorphic to either
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
or
\text{Bl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C})
. Moreover, in the former case, they prove that the metric must be isometric to the standard metric on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
. However, in the latter case, while uniqueness is established, the existence of the metric is still conjectural (cf. Conjecture 1.1 of
[
25
]
). In
[
5
]
, Bamler, Cifarelli, Conlon, Deruelle study the blowup limit of a Kähler Ricci flow with toric symmetry, which corresponds to a non-compact Kähler Ricci shrinker. Utilizing divisor rigidity and toric symmetry (cf.
[
19
]
[
20
]
), it is determined that the infinity of this shrinker is
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
, and it contains an exceptional curve. Therefore, it must correspond to the conjectural Kähler Ricci shrinker on
\text{Bl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C})
. It is an interesting problem to find also a traditional construction by solving PDE, similar to the cases
\text{Bl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
and
\text{Bl}_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
.

From the preceding discussion, it is evident that the only remaining component needed to complete the classification is the scalar curvature estimate (
1.3
) on each Kähler Ricci shrinker surface. This estimate is accomplished by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(
Main result
)
.

Any Kähler Ricci shrinker surface has bounded scalar curvature and consequently bounded sectional curvature.

Bounding the scalar curvature is a significant problem in Kähler geometry. One notable contribution is made by G. Perelman, who established a uniform scalar curvature bound along the Kähler Ricci flow on Fano manifolds (cf.
[
64
]
).

Theorem
1.2
presents a similar phenomenon to Perelman’s scalar curvature bound, albeit with different background and proof techniques in our specific setting. It is worth noting that the scalar curvature bound in Theorem
1.2
assumes a complex dimension of
2
, although we conjecture that this assumption may be unnecessary.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on the idea that if the scalar curvature tends to infinity, it does so uniformly. This, in turn, leads to the divergence of the scalar curvature average, which is a contradiction. The uniform behavior of the scalar curvature necessitates a thorough understanding of the geometry in the vicinity of points with high scalar curvature. This understanding is achieved through the following canonical neighborhood theorem.

Theorem 1.3

(
Canonical Neighborhood Theorem
)
.

For any positive constants
A,\epsilon
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exist positive constants
\sigma=\sigma(\epsilon,A)
and
L=L(\epsilon,\delta,A)
satisfying the following property.

Let
(M^{2},g,J,f)
be a Kähler Ricci shrinker surface with
\boldsymbol{\mu}(g)\geq-A
. Then the following statements hold for
x\in M
.

(A)

If
R(x)\geq\delta f(x)
and
f(x)\geq L
, then
(M,g,x)
is
\epsilon
-close to a pointed Kähler steady soliton orbifold with isolated singularities. Moreover,
R(y)\geq L^{-1}f(x)
for any
y\in B(x,\epsilon^{-1}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x))
.

(B)

If
R(x)+R^{-1}(x)\leq\sigma f(x)
, then
(M,g,J,x)
is
\epsilon
-close to the Kähler Ricci flow
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}(t):=g_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(t)\times g_{E},J_{c})
.

Theorem
1.3
serves as the technical core of this article. It is important to note that this result holds uniformly. The quantity
\boldsymbol{\mu}(g)
represents Perelman’s functional (cf. equation (
2.2
)).

The notion of being
\epsilon
-close to a Kähler steady soliton orbifold or
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}(t),J_{c})
can be found in Definition
3.7
and Definition
2.4
, respectively. In essence, it implies that a neighborhood around the base point is close to a Kähler steady soliton orbifold in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense or close to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
in the smooth sense, upon appropriate rescaling.

These two situations are distinguished by whether the ratio
R/f
at the base point is small. To provide an overview of the proof of Theorem
1.3
, although at the cost of sacrificing some rigorous definitions, we can summarize it as follows.

Outline of proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Note that both parts (A) and (B) can be reformulated as the weak-compactness of Kähler Ricci shrinkers after appropriate rescaling. Part (A) states that for any sequence of points
x_{i}
tending to infinity, with the ratio
R(x_{i})/f_{i}(x_{i})
bounded from below, rescaling the sequence by the scalar curvature
R(x_{i})
leads to convergence of the pointed space sequence at
x_{i}
to a steady soliton orbifold in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology. Furthermore, this convergence can be enhanced to the smooth topology away from singularities. Part (B) demonstrates that if
x_{i}
tends to infinity and
(R(x_{i})+R^{-1}(x_{i}))/f_{i}(x_{i})
tends to zero, then a suitably rescaled spacetime converges to a cylindrical Kähler Ricci flow in the smooth topology.

It is worth noting that there are weak-compactness theories for solitons available in the literature, such as those presented in
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
41
]
, and
[
4
]
. However, none of these theories can be directly applied in the current context. Significant improvements of these weak-compactness theories are necessary to derive meaningful geometric conclusions.

The proof of part (A) is an improvement of the earlier weak-compactness theories in
[
33
]
and
[
41
]
, where
f_{i}(x_{i})
are uniformly bounded and the scales remain fixed. In the current scenario, we are addressing the case where
f_{i}(x_{i})
tends to infinity and the scales involved are very small. Due to this scale consideration, the uniform no-local-collapsing theorem proved in
[
46
]
plays a crucial role in our proof. Additionally, we modify the local conformal transformation technique from
[
41
]
to achieve bootstrapping and improve regularity.

The proof of part (B) is more involved. Our proof utilizes the
\mathbb{F}
-compactness developed by Bamler. However, the
\mathbb{F}
-compactness typically deals with a weak topology, similar to how the compactness in the mean curvature flow theory is handled by the Brakke flow
[
7
]
. To improve the convergence in the smooth topology, significantly more effort is required.

To achieve this, we rely on the following key ingredients:

•

The first is the local scalar curvature estimate, which originates from our previous work
[
41
]
. This estimate ensures control over points with bounded reduced distance (in the sense of Perelman
[
61
]
) to given points or related
H
-center in the terminology of
[
2
]
.

•

The second is obtaining a splitting direction of the limiting flow, thus reducing the dimension of the flow by one. The splitting direction comes from
\nabla f
after proper rescaling. It is important to note that we analyze the estimate of
\nabla f
in spacetime, rather than solely on a fixed time slice.

•

The third is to demonstrate that the limiting flow corresponds to a conventional ancient Ricci flow solution. By combining the symmetry induced by
J
with the tangent flow argument, we are able to eliminate the singularities in the limiting flow. Additionally, employing a localized maximum principle argument similar to the one used in
[
18
]
, we establish that the limiting flow possesses nonnegative sectional curvature.

Using Toponogov’s comparison geometry, as exemplified in techniques employed in
[
61
]
, we deduce that the limiting flow has bounded sectional curvature on each time slice. Consequently, the flow can be classified as a conventional ancient Kähler Ricci flow.

•

The fourth involves the classification of ancient Kähler Ricci flow solutions of complex dimension two, as developed by the first-named author and their collaborator
[
24
]
. It is established that any non-flat ancient Kähler Ricci flow solution with an
\mathbb{R}
-factor splitting must be
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}(t),J_{c})
, which represents the canonical ancient solution on the cylinder
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
.

The proof of part (B) becomes relatively straightforward when considering the key ingredients described above. We start with the given sequence of pointed Kähler Ricci shrinkers
(M_{i},g_{i},J_{i},f_{i},x_{i})
and study the associated pointed Kähler Ricci flows
(M_{i},g_{i}(t),J_{i},(x_{i},0))_{t\leq 0}
.

Treating the pointed Kähler Ricci flow as a metric flow coupled with the conjugate heat flow from
(x_{i},0)
, we appropriately rescale the metric flow pairs to demonstrate
\mathbb{F}
-convergence to a limiting metric flow pair (cf. Theorem
A.10
). However, the limiting metric flow typically contains singularities, and the
\mathbb{F}
-convergence is weaker than Gromov-Hausdorff convergence.

To eliminate the singular set and improve the convergence to the smooth topology, we utilize the local scalar curvature estimate, which partially ensures compatibility between
\mathbb{F}
-convergence and pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. Additionally, considering the sufficiently small ratio
R/f
, we construct a splitting direction induced by
\nabla f_{i}
on the limiting metric flow.

Once we establish that the limiting metric flow exhibits a line splitting, we utilize the Kähler structure and various techniques to prove that the singular set is empty. This further confirms that the limiting metric flow corresponds to a conventional ancient Kähler Ricci flow solution with nonnegative bisectional curvature. In particular, the limiting metric flow is identified as
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}(t))
, and the convergence is smooth.
∎

From the aforementioned discussion, it is evident that part (B) can be interpreted as a theorem focused on removing singularities and improving regularity. This approach follows a classical strategy employed in various theories. For instance, in the context of mean curvature flow theory, a similar strategy is employed in
[
44
]
. By utilizing Brakke flow compactness, the central aspect of the proof in
[
44
]
involves enhancing convergence to be smooth away from a curve. Subsequently,
L
-stability is applied to eliminate the singular curve and ensure regularity.

Based on Theorem
1.3
, we are now prepared to provide a more detailed explanation of the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Outline of proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Consider a complete Kähler Ricci shrinker surface
(M,g,f,J,p)
, where
p
is a point at which
f
attains its minimum value. The crucial step is to examine the behavior of the scalar curvature
R
along the flow
\phi^{s}
generated by
\nabla f/|\nabla f|^{2}
.

Suppose
\Sigma
is a connected component of some level set
\Sigma(s_{0})
of
f
. If the scalar curvature
R
remains smaller than
1
on
\phi^{s}(\Sigma)
for all
s\geq s_{0}
, then the union of these level sets,
\bigcup_{s\geq s_{0}}\phi^{s}(\Sigma)
, forms an end of the Kähler Ricci shrinker. Since the end is unique (cf. Theorem
5.1
), this implies that the scalar curvature
R
is bounded.

Alternatively, if the scalar curvature
R
exceeds
1
by a detectably small amount at a certain point on
\Sigma
, we can demonstrate that any point on
\Sigma
along the flow
\phi^{s}
will eventually satisfy the conditions of Theorem
1.3
(A). Consequently, outside a compact set, the scalar curvature is comparable to
f
.

It is worth noting that as
s
approaches infinity, the volume of the ball
B(p,\sqrt{s})
is at least
\epsilon\sqrt{s}
; see Proposition
5.3
and the relevant reference. Consequently, the average of the scalar curvature over
B(p,\sqrt{s})
tends to infinity, which contradicts the integration of the equation
R+\Delta f=2
.
∎

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 discusses the elementary properties of Ricci shrinkers and their associated Ricci flows. In Section 3, we prove part (A) of the canonical neighborhood theorem, which is modeled on a steady Ricci soliton orbifold.
Section 4 focuses on part (B) of the canonical neighborhood theorem, which is modeled on the canonical ancient Kähler Ricci flow solution on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
. Building upon the canonical neighborhood theorem, we establish the proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section 5.

Preliminaries

A Kähler Ricci shrinker
(M^{m},g,J,f)
is a Ricci shrinker on a Kähler manifold
(M^{m},g,J)
, where we denote the complex structure by
J
, the complex dimension by
m
and the real dimension by
n=2m
. Under local holomorphic coordinates, (
1.1
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle R_{i\bar{j}}+f_{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{2}g_{i\bar{j}},\quad f_{ij}=f_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}=0.

(2.1)

It is well known from (
2.1
) that
J\nabla f
is a Killing and real holomorphic vector field on
M
.

For any Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)
, the scalar curvature
R\geq 0

[
18
, Corollary
2.5
]
and
R>0
unless
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to the Gaussian soliton
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{E})
, by the strong maximum principle.

With the normalization (
1.2
), the entropy is defined as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}=\boldsymbol{\mu}(g)\coloneqq\log\int\frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\,dV.

(2.2)

Note that
e^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is uniformly comparable to the volume of the unit ball
B(p,1)
, see
[
41
, Lemma
2.5
]
.
It was proved in
[
15
]
(see also
[
46
, Proposition
3
]
) that
\boldsymbol{\mu}
agrees with Perelman’s celebrated entropy functional
\boldsymbol{\mu}(g,1)
; see
[
61
]
,
[
70
]
.
In fact,
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is the optimal log-Sobolev constant for all scales; see
[
46
, Theorem
1
]
. For the detailed discussions of
\boldsymbol{\mu}
-functional on Ricci shrinkers and its properties, we refer the readers to
[
46
, Section
5
]
.

For the potential function
f
, we have the following fundamental estimates.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
13
]

[
32
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker. Then there exists a point
p\in M
where
f
attains its infimum, and
f
satisfies the quadratic growth estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(d(x,p)-5n\right)^{2}_{+}\leq f(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(d(x,p)+\sqrt{2n}\right)^{2}

for all
x\in M
, where
a_{+}:=\max\{0,a\}
.

We will always denote a minimum point of
f
by
p
, which is regarded as the Ricci shrinker’s base point. Moreover, Lemma
2.1
implies that

is almost

. For simplicity, we define
\Sigma(s,t):=\{x\in M\mid s\leq f(x)\leq t\}
and
\Sigma(t):=\Sigma(t,t)
.

Recall that any Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)
can be considered as a self-similar solution to the Ricci flow. Let
{\psi^{t}}:M\to M
be a family of diffeomorphisms generated by
\dfrac{1}{1-t}\nabla f
and
\psi^{0}=\text{id}
.
In other words, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\psi^{t}}(x)=\frac{1}{1-t}\nabla f\left({\psi^{t}}(x)\right).

(2.3)

It is well known that the rescaled pull-back metric
g(t)\coloneqq(1-t)(\psi^{t})^{*}g
satisfies the Ricci flow equation for any
-\infty<t<1

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g=-2\mathrm{Rc}_{g(t)}\quad\text{and}\quad g(0)=g.

(2.4)

Moreover, for any
-\infty<t<1
, the pull-back function
F(t):=(1-t)(\psi^{t})^{*}f
satisfies the following identities

\displaystyle(1-t)\mathrm{Rc}_{g(t)}+\text{Hess}_{g(t)}\,F(t)=\frac{1}{2}g(t),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F=-(1-t)R_{g(t)},

(2.6)

\displaystyle(1-t)^{2}R_{g(t)}+|\nabla_{g(t)}F|^{2}_{g(t)}=F.

(2.7)

We refer the readers to
[
46
, Section 2]
for the proof of the above identities.

Definition 2.2
.

For any Ricci shrinker, the Ricci flow defined in (
2.4
) is called the associated Ricci flow. Any time-shifting and rescaling of the associated Ricci flow is called the Ricci flow induced by the Ricci shrinker.

Example 2.3

(Standard model)
.

The standard product
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}:=g_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\times g_{E},J_{c},f_{c})
is a Kähler Ricci shrinker with scalar curvature to be identically
1
and the potential function
f_{c}=|z|^{2}/4+1
, where

is the holomorphic coordinate of the
\mathbb{C}
-factor.
J_{c}
denotes the unique complex structure compatible with

. The associated Ricci flow is
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}(t))_{t\in(-\infty,1)}
, where

\displaystyle g_{c}(t)=(1-t)g_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\times g_{E},\quad g_{c}(0)=g_{c}.

The scalar curvature evolves under the Ricci flow by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}R=R^{2}.

(2.8)

Definition 2.4

(

By taking a subsequence if necessary, it follows from Theorem

4
Canonical neighborhood theorem—part B

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be the Ricci flow induced by a Ricci shrinker (cf. Definition

) in
\mathcal{M}(A)
for

\displaystyle\square H(\cdot,\cdot,y,s)=0,\quad\lim_{t\to s^{+}}H(\cdot,t,y,s)=\delta_{y}

\displaystyle\square^{*}H(x,t,\cdot,\cdot)=0,\quad\lim_{s\to t^{-}}H(x,t,\cdot,s)=\delta_{x},

In particular,

H

Definition 4.1
.

Given a constant

H
-center of

\displaystyle\emph{\text{Var}}_{t}(\delta_{z},v_{x_{0},t_{0};t})\leq H(t_{0}-t),

\displaystyle\emph{\text{Var}}_{t}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\iint d_{t}^{2}(x,y)\,d\mu_{1}(x)d\mu_{2}(y).

It is proved in
[
47
, Proposition 3.12]
that for any

and

such that

. Moreover, any two

-centers

The following result ensures that the conjugate heat kernel measure is concentrated around an

[
47

Proposition 4.2
.

If
(z,t)
is an
H
-center of
(x_{0},t_{0})
, then for any

\displaystyle v_{x_{0},t_{0};t}\left(B_{t}(z,\sqrt{LH(t_{0}-t)})\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{L}.

Now, we have the following heat kernel upper bound estimate from
[
47
, Theorem 1.3]

Theorem 4.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,s)\leq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{s}^{2}(z,y)}{(4+\epsilon)(t-s)}\right),

where
(z,s)
is an
H_{n}
-center of
(x,t)\in M\times I
.

From Theorem

.

It follows from

everywhere outside a compact set.

We proceed to prove Theorem
1.2
, which we restate here.

Theorem 5.4
.

Any noncompact Kähler Ricci shrinker surface
(M^{2},g,J,f)
has bounded scalar curvature.

Proof.

We assume
(M,g,f)\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{A})
for some constant
\bar{A}>0
. We fix a small constant
\epsilon>0
to be determined later, and set

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}:=\min\{\sigma(\epsilon,\bar{A}),1/1000\},\quad\bar{L}:=\max\{L(\epsilon,\bar{\sigma}/2,\bar{A}),1000\bar{\sigma}^{-1}\},

(5.3)

where
\sigma
and
L
are functions in Theorem
1.3
.

Now, we define the following subsets of
\Sigma(\bar{L},\infty)
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&A\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Sigma(\bar{L},\infty)\mid R(x)\geq\bar{\sigma}f(x)/2\right\},\\
&B\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Sigma(\bar{L},\infty)\mid R(x)<\bar{\sigma}f(x)/2\quad\text{and}\quad R^{-1}(x)<\bar{\sigma}f(x)/2\right\},\\
&C\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Sigma(\bar{L},\infty)\mid R^{-1}(x)\geq\bar{\sigma}f(x)/2\right\}.\end{cases}

It is clear that
\Sigma(\bar{L},\infty)=A\sqcup B\sqcup C
. Moreover, it follows from Theorem
1.3
that for any
x\in A
,
(M,g,x)
is
\epsilon
-close to a pointed Kähler steady soliton orbifold. On the other hand, for any
x\in B
,
(M,g,J,x)
is
\epsilon
-close to
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}(t),J_{c})
.

For any
x\in M
, we set
x^{t}:=\psi^{t}(x)
for
t<1
, where
\psi^{t}
is the family of diffeomorphisms in (
2.3
). We fix a small constant
0<\delta\ll 1
and let
\epsilon
depend on it. Moreover, we set
\eta_{\epsilon}
to be a positive function such that
\eta_{\epsilon}\to 0
if
\epsilon\to 0
. In the following,
\eta_{\epsilon}
may be different line by line.

Claim 1
: There exists a constant
\bar{t}=\bar{t}(\delta)\in(0,1)
such that for any
x\in B
with
R(x)\geq 1+\delta
, there exists a constant
t_{1}\leq\bar{t}
such that
x^{t}\in B
for any
t\in[0,t_{1})
and
x^{t_{1}}\in A

Proof of Claim 1
: Since
B
is an open set, there exists a largest constant
t_{1}\in(0,1]
such that
x^{t}\in B
for any
t\in[0,t_{1})
. By our definition,
(M,g,J,x^{t})
is
\epsilon
-close to
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}(t),J_{c})
for any
t\in[0,t_{1})
. Since the Ricci flow associated with the Ricci shrinker is self-similar, we conclude that
(M,g(t),J,(x,t))
is also
\epsilon
-close to
(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C},g_{c}(t),J_{c})
for any
t\in[0,t_{1})
.

Therefore, the evolution equation of the scalar curvature satisfies

(1-\eta_{\epsilon})R^{2}\leq\partial_{t}R=\Delta_{t}R+2|\mathrm{Rc}|^{2}\leq(1+\eta_{\epsilon})R^{2}.

(5.4)

for any
(x,t)
with
t\in[0,t_{1})
. Indeed, (
5.4
) holds, as
\partial_{t}R/R^{2}
is invariant under parabolic scaling, and the model space satisfies (
2.8
). In particular, we have

\displaystyle 1-\eta_{\epsilon}\leq\partial_{t}\left(-\frac{1}{R}\right)\leq 1+\eta_{\epsilon}.

Integrating the above inequality over time, we conclude that if
\epsilon
is sufficiently small,

R(x,t)\geq\frac{R(x)}{1-(1-\eta_{\epsilon})R(x)t}\geq\frac{1+\delta}{1-(1-\eta_{\epsilon})(1+\delta)t},

(5.5)

for any
t\in[0,t_{1})
. From (
5.5
), it is clear that
t_{1}<1/(1-\eta_{\epsilon})(1+\delta)<1/(1+\delta/2):=\bar{t}
for sufficiently small

. In addition, since

and
R(x)\geq 1+\delta\geq(1-\eta_{\epsilon})^{-1}
for
\epsilon

R(x^{t})\geq\frac{(1-t)R(x)}{1-(1-\eta_{\epsilon})R(x)t}\geq R(x)

(5.6)

for any

.
As

is increasing, it follows from (

) that for any

R(x^{t})f(x^{t})\geq R(x)f(x)>2\bar{\sigma}^{-1},

where the last inequality holds because

. However, by the definition of
t_{1}
,

is on the boundary of

.
Therefore, either

, or
R(x^{t_{1}})f(x^{t_{1}})=2\bar{\sigma}^{-1}

Consequently,
R(x^{t_{1}})=\bar{\sigma}f(x^{t_{1}})/2
and

Claim 2
: For any
x\in\Sigma(\bar{L},\infty)
with

, we have

for any

Proof of Claim 2
: Suppose the claim fails. There exists a constant

for any

and

Similarly, if

Thus

(5.7)

for any

R(y^{t})\geq\frac{(1-t)(1-\delta)}{1-(1+\eta_{\epsilon})(1-\delta)t}>1-\delta

for any

Claim 3

R(x^{t})\geq\bar{\sigma}f(x^{t})/4
for any

Proof of Claim 3

for any

R(y,t)\leq\frac{R(y)}{1-(1-\eta_{\epsilon})R(y)t}

for any

(a).
R^{-1}(y)<t_{4}:=\dfrac{t_{3}}{1-t_{3}}

In this case,

for some

\psi^{s}(\psi^{t}(z))=\psi^{t}(\psi^{s}(z))=\psi^{s+t-st}(z)

s,t<1

In addition,

Combining (

5.9

Since

.

(b).
R^{-1}(y)\geq t_{4}

In this case,

\frac{R(x)}{f(x)}\leq\frac{(1+t_{4})^{2}}{1+(1-\eta_{\epsilon})R(y)t_{4}}\frac{R(y)}{f(y)}\leq\frac{\bar{\sigma}(1+t_{4})^{2}}{4}.

Since
t_{4}\leq R^{-1}(y)\ll 1

5.12
) contradicts the fact that
R(x)\geq\bar{\sigma}f(x)/2
. Therefore, we have proved Claim 3.

From Sard’s theorem, there exists
s_{0}>\bar{L}
such that
\Sigma(s_{0})

.

is just a reparametrization of

. Here, if

of

for any

(or

For each

Case (i)

.

for all

.
Argued as in the proof of Theorem

Case (iii)
: There exist a constant

Case (iv)

Remark 5.5
.

As pointed out by the referee, the decomposition in (

Appendix A
Metric flows and
\mathbb{F}
-convergence

In this appendix, we state some theorems regarding the metric flows and
\mathbb{F}
-convergence. The theory of
\mathbb{F}
-convergence was originally established by Bamler for compact Ricci flows
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
In
[
47
]
, the theory is generalized by the authors to Ricci flows induced by Ricci shrinkers. For more detailed discussions, see
[
47
, Section 6]
.

We first recall the following definition of the metric flow from
[
3
, Definition 3.1]
.

Definition A.1

(Metric flow)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a subset.
A metric flow over
I
is a tuple of the form

(\mathcal{X},\mathfrak{t},(d_{t})_{t\in I},(v_{x;s})_{x\in\mathcal{X},s\in I,s\leq\mathfrak{t}(x)})

with the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{X}
is a set consisting of points.

(2)

\mathfrak{t}:\mathcal{X}\to I
is a map called time-function.
Its level sets
\mathcal{X}_{t}:=\mathfrak{t}^{-1}(t)
are called time-slices and the preimages
\mathcal{X}_{I^{\prime}}:=\mathfrak{t}^{-1}(I^{\prime})
,
I^{\prime}\subset I
, are called time-slabs.

(3)

(\mathcal{X}_{t},d_{t})
is a complete and separable metric space for all
t\in I
.

(4)

v_{x;s}
is a probability measure on
\mathcal{X}_{s}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
s\in I
,
s\leq\mathfrak{t}(x)
. For any
x\in\mathcal{X}
the family
(v_{x;s})_{s\in I,s\leq\mathfrak{t}(x)}
is called the conjugate heat kernel at
x
.

(5)

v_{x;\mathfrak{t}(x)}=\delta_{x}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

(6)

For all
s,t\in I
,
s<t
,
T\geq 0
and any measurable function
u_{s}:\mathcal{X}_{s}\to[0,1]
with the property that if
T>0
,
then
u_{s}=\Phi\circ f_{s}
for some
T^{-1/2}
-Lipschitz function
f_{s}:\mathcal{X}_{s}\to\mathbb{R}

(
if
T=0
, then there is no additional assumption on
u_{s}
)
, the following is true.
The function

u_{t}:\mathcal{X}_{t}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad x\longmapsto\int_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}u_{s}\,dv_{x;s}

is either constant or of the form
u_{t}=\Phi\circ f_{t}
, where
f_{t}:\mathcal{X}_{t}\to\mathbb{R}
is
(t-s+T)^{-1/2}
-Lipschitz. Here,
\Phi
is given by

\Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}(4\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^{2}/4}\,dt.

(7)

For any
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\in I
,
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}
,
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t_{3}}
we have the reproduction formula

v_{x;t_{1}}=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t_{2}}}v_{\cdot;t_{1}}dv_{x;t_{2}},

meaning that for any Borel set
S\subset\mathcal{X}_{t_{1}}

v_{x;t_{1}}(S)=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t_{2}}}v_{y;t_{1}}(S)dv_{x;t_{2}}(y).

Given a metric flow
\mathcal{X}
over
I
, we recall the following definition of the conjugate heat flow from
[
3
, Definition 3.13]
.

Definition A.2

(Conjugate heat flow)
.

A family of probability measures
(\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}_{t}))_{t\in I^{\prime}}
over
I^{\prime}\subset I
is called a conjugate heat flow if for all
s,t\in I^{\prime}
,
s\leq t
we have

\mu_{s}=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}\nu_{x;s}\,d\mu_{t}(x).

In particular, the conjugate heat kernel
(v_{x;s})_{s\in I,s\leq\mathfrak{t}(x)}
is a conjugate heat flow over
I^{\prime}=I\cap(-\infty,\mathfrak{t}(x))
.

Definition A.3

(
H
-Concentration)
.

Given a constant
H>0
, a metric flow
\mathcal{X}

Definition A.12

(Ricci flow spacetime)
.

An
n
-dimensional Ricci flow spacetime over an interval

is a tuple
(\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{t},\partial_{\mathfrak{t}},g)
with the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{M}
is an
(n+1)
-dimensional smooth manifold with smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}
, and
\mathcal{M}
is a disjoint union of smooth manifolds of dimension
n
.

(2)

\mathfrak{t}:\mathcal{M}\to I
is a smooth function without critical points. For any
t\in I
we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{t}:=\mathfrak{t}^{-1}(t)\subset\mathcal{M}
the time-
t
-slice of
\mathcal{M}
.

(3)

\mathfrak{t}(\partial\mathcal{M})\subset\partial I
.

(4)

\partial_{\mathfrak{t}}
is a smooth vector field on
\mathcal{M}
that satisfies
\partial_{\mathfrak{t}}\mathfrak{t}\equiv 1
.

(5)

g
is a smooth inner product on the spatial subbundle
\ker(d\mathfrak{t})\subset T\mathcal{M}
.
For any
t\in I
we denote by
g_{t}
the restriction of

to the time-
t
-slice
\mathcal{M}_{t}
.

(6)

(\mathcal{M}_{t},g_{t})
is connected for all
t\in I
.

(7)

g
satisfies the Ricci flow equation:
\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\mathfrak{t}}}g=-2Ric(g)
.
Here

denotes the symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor on
\ker(d\mathfrak{t})
that restricts to the Ricci tensor of
(\mathcal{M}_{t},g_{t})
for all
t\in I
.

Obviously, a conventional Ricci flow
(M,g(t))_{t\in I}
is a Ricci flow spacetime by setting
\mathcal{M}=M\times I
,
\mathfrak{t}
to be the projection on the time factor, and
\partial_{\mathfrak{t}}
to be the unit vector on
I
.

\displaystyle R_{g_{t}}(x)\geq-\frac{3}{t+T}

for any
t\in(-T,0]
and
x\in\mathcal{M}_{t}
. By letting
T\to\infty
, we conclude that
R_{g_{t}}\geq 0
on
\mathcal{M}
.

Now, we set
\lambda\geq\mu\geq\nu
to be the eigenvalues of the curvature operator. By using the same inductive argument as in
[
18
, Proposition 2.2]
, we conclude that for any integer
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\lambda+\mu+k\nu\geq\min\left\{-\frac{C_{k}}{t+T},-\frac{C_{k}}{(Ar)^{2}}\right\}

on
B_{g_{t}}(x_{t},A/2)
, for any
t\in(-T,0]
. Here,
A\gg Tr^{-2}
and
C_{k}
is a constant depending only on
k
. For the same reason as above, it is clear that

\displaystyle\lambda+\mu+k\nu\geq 0

on
\mathcal{M}
and hence
\nu\geq 0
.

In sum, we have shown that
(\mathcal{M}_{t},g_{t})
has nonnegative sectional curvature for all
t\in I
.
∎

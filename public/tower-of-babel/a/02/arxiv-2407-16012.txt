Data Processing Inequalities for the Quantum Guesswork
Ilyass Mejdoub, Julien Béguinot, and Olivier Rioul

Abstract
Quantum guesswork has emerged as a crucial measure in assessing the distinguishability of non-orthogonal quantum states. In this paper, we review and compare previous definitions of the quantum guesswork, and establish the pre and post data processing inequalities (DPIs) that are relevant for the guessing problem. We also derive improved lower and upper bounds on the quantum guesswork in terms of the Shannon entropy and Holevo’s information.



I
Introduction

The classical guessing game is as follows. Alice has access to the realization
X=x
of a discrete random variable
X\in\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
, and Bob asks questions of the form “is
X\!=\!x_{i}
?” to Alice until the answer is “yes”.
Both Alice and Bob know the alphabet
\mathcal{X}
and the probability distribution
p_{X}
of
X
.
Bob’s
guessing strategy
specifies the order of the successive guesses he makes until Alice confirms that Bob has found the correct value.
A sound strategy never asks twice for the same value of
x
; therefore, Bob’s strategy can be seen as
a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
of the alphabet of cardinality
n
, where
\sigma(1)
is the first guess,
\sigma(2)
is the second, and so on until the secret
x
is found in at most
n
trials.

The
guesswork
is the average number of Bob’s guesses under his strategy
\sigma
, given by

\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X)\triangleq{\sum_{i=1}^{n}}\,i\cdot p_{X}(\sigma(i)).

(1)

This quantity is minimized for Bob’s optimal strategy
\sigma^{*}
, such that the probabilities
p_{X}(\sigma^{*}(i))
are in descending order, yielding a so-called
guessing entropy
, introduced by Massey
[
1
]
:

G(X)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X)=\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{*}}(X)=\smash[b]{\sum_{i=1}^{n}}\,i\cdot p^{\downarrow}_{X}(x_{i})

(2)

where
p^{\downarrow}_{X}(x)
are the probabilities
p_{X}(x)
sorted in descending order.

Should Bob gain supplementary side information correlated with
X
, his resulting guesswork is, on average, lower. In the presence of some side information
Y
(taking values in an arbitrary alphabet
\mathcal{Y}
), Bob’s strategy depends on his observed value
Y=y
:

\sigma:y\in\mathcal{Y}\mapsto\sigma_{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}.

(3)

and the corresponding guesswork given observation
Y=y
is

\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y=y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,i\cdot p_{X|Y}(\sigma_{y}(i)|y).

(4)

Bob’s
conditional guesswork
is the average guesswork over all possible observations:

\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y)=\mathbb{E}_{Y}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,i\cdot p_{X|Y}(\sigma_{Y}(i)|Y).

(5)

Now for a given strategy, all observations
y\in\mathcal{Y}
such that
\sigma_{y}=\sigma
yield the same permutation
\sigma
, and regrouping terms in the sum gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,i\cdot p_{X,Y}(\sigma_{y}(i),y)

(6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,i\cdot p_{X,\sigma_{Y}}(\sigma(i),\sigma)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|\sigma_{Y}).

This means that for a fixed strategy
\sigma:y\in\mathcal{Y}\mapsto\sigma_{y}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
,
the conditional guesswork depends on the observation
Y

only through
\sigma_{Y}
.
As shown by Arikan
[
2
]
in 1996, the optimal strategy
\sigma^{*}_{y}
still consists in guessing in descending order of conditional probabilities
p_{X|Y}(x|y)
, and the
conditional guessing entropy
is the average guesswork for such an optimal strategy
\sigma^{*}
:

G(X|Y)\!=\!\min_{\sigma}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y)\!=\!\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{*}}(X|Y)\!=\!\mathbb{E}_{Y}\!\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,i\cdot p^{\downarrow}_{X|Y}(x_{i}|y)

(7)

where
p^{\downarrow}_{X|Y}(x|y)
are the probabilities
p_{X|Y}(x|y)
sorted in descending order.

If we now substitute the classical random variable
Y
with quantum side information, we get the notion of
quantum guesswork
introduced by Chen
et al.

[
3
]
as an optimization criterion for discriminating non-orthogonal quantum states. This constitutes an alternative to the conventional error probability criterion
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
Furthermore, as
mentioned in
[
10
, § IX]
, quantum guesswork can serve
as a security criterion in some quantum information processing
tasks such as certifying the safety of an exchanged key in an
imperfect implementation of a QKD protocol.

In this quantum guessing game,
Alice still has a random variable
X
whose probability distribution
p_{X}
and alphabet
\mathcal{X}
are known to both parties. Now, when Alice observes the realization
X=x
, she sends Bob a quantum state
\rho_{x}
.
The quantum ensemble

\mathcal{E}\triangleq\{(\rho_{x},p_{X}(x))\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}

(8)

is known to both Alice and Bob.
The quantum states
\{\rho_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}
can be either pure or mixed states and belong to the set of density matrices in some finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

Once again, Bob can only ask questions of the form “is
X\!=\!x
?”.
The only way for Bob to get any helpful information from the received state
\rho_{x}
is to eventually measure it. Bob can freely manipulate the state
\rho_{x}
, including applying unitary transformations or conducting measurements, to correctly infer the realization
x
.
The most general form of quantum measurement
[
11
,
12
]
is called POVM (positive operator valued measure) and is mathematically defined as a set
E=\{E_{y}\}_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}
of positive semi-definite operators which sum to the identity:

E_{y}\geqslant 0\text{ and }\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}E_{y}=I.

(9)

The POVM and the quantum ensemble used by Bob define a classical random variable
Y_{E}
taking values in
\mathcal{Y}
.
By Born’s rule
[
11
,
12
]
:

p_{Y_{E}|X}(y|x)=\Tr[E_{y}\rho_{x}].

(10)

so that
p_{Y_{E}}(y)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\Tr[E_{y}\rho_{x}]\cdot p_{X}(x)
.
While the channel
X\to\rho_{X}
is quantum, for a fixed POVM
E
the composition of channels

X\to\boxed{\text{Quantum Channel}}\to\rho_{X}\to\boxed{\mathrm{POVM}}\to Y_{E}

forms a classical channel. Hence, subject to the POVM
E
, we can define the quantum conditional guesswork for a given strategy
\sigma
as a classical conditional guesswork (
6
)

\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y_{E})=\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|\sigma_{Y_{E}})

(11)

The guesswork depends on
\rho_{x}
only through
Y_{E}
, and as above, it depends on
Y_{E}
only through
\sigma_{Y_{E}}
, according to the following Markov chain

X\to\rho_{X}\to\boxed{\mathrm{POVM}}\to Y_{E}\to\sigma_{Y_{E}}.

The quantum guessing entropy is now the minimum average guesswork, achieved for the optimal guess strategy
and
the optimal
measurement
strategy (through the choice of the POVM). Therefore, a natural definition is the following:

Definition 1
(Quantum Guesswork)

The
quantum conditional guessing entropy
of
X
(also named
quantum guesswork
) given a quantum ensemble
\mathcal{E}
is defined by

G(X|\mathcal{E})=\min_{\sigma,E}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y_{E})

The optimization is over the pair
(\sigma,E)
; however, it only depends on
\sigma_{Y_{E}}
so that the procedure can be simplified.

In the present literature on quantum guessing, the quantum guesswork
G(X|\mathcal{E})
was defined in two ways, either by minimizing a classical guessing entropy over the choice of a POVM
E

[
3
,
10
,
13
]
, or through it “operational” point of view advocated in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
The equivalence between these two definitions of quantum guesswork is perhaps known in certain circles but, to the best of our knowledge, has not yet been explicitly proved.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
II
, we restate the two previous definitions of quantum guesswork and formally show that they are equivalent to our Definition
1
. In addition, we review and explain anew known results about the measurement attaining the quantum guesswork. The main results of this paper are in Section
III
. We first establish the unitary invariance property (§
III-A
). We then generalize two data processing inequalities to the quantum setting (pre-DPI in §
III-C
and post-DPI in §
III-B
). Finally, in §

et al.

[
3
]

II-B

Quantum Guesswork: Chen et al.’s Definition
G_{1}(X|\mathcal{E})

The first previous definition of the quantum guesswork
G_{1}(X|\mathcal{E})
given in
[
3
,
10
,
13
]
is as follows.
From the joint distribution
p_{X}(x)p_{Y_{E}|X}(y|x)
of
X
and
Y_{E}
, we compute the (classical)
guessing entropy

G(X|Y_{E})
from the POVM
E
and the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
.
Bob can now only control the choice of the POVM to minimize his average number of guesses. Thus the quantum guesswork is here defined as

G_{1}(X|\mathcal{E})\triangleq\inf_{E}G(X|Y_{E}).

(13)

As shown in
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, the most general quantum measurement strategy Bob can use is either one of the following strategies:

•

perform a single measurement using an arbitrary POVM and then apply the classical guessing strategy;

•

perform a single measurement using a POVM indexed by permutations
\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}
specifying the order of the successive guesses;

•

perform successive measurements using POVMs indexed by the remaining possible values using Alice’s feedback to each of Bob’s guesses.

II-C

Quantum Guesswork: Dall’Arno et al.’s Definition
G_{2}(X|\mathcal{E})

The second previous definition of the quantum guesswork
G_{2}(X|\mathcal{E})
given in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
is as follows.
Without loss of generality, we can assume
\mathcal{X}=\{1,...,n\}
, and we restrict ourselves to special types of POVMs
{E}
indexed by
permutations
:

{E}=\{{E}_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}}

(14)

whose outcome is
operational
in the sense that it gives Bob directly its entire ranking of guesses in the form of a permutation
\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}
where
\sigma(i)
is Bob’s
i
-th guess.

The probability that the outcome of Bob’s measurement is
\sigma
and that his
i
-th guess is the correct one (i.e
\sigma(i)=x
) given that he received the state
\rho_{x}
is obtained by Born’s rule

p(\sigma,i)=\Tr[E_{\sigma}\cdot\rho_{\sigma(i)}]\cdot p_{X}(\sigma(i)).

(15)

If we marginalize over
\sigma
we get the probability that the
i
-th guess is correct given that the received state is
\rho_{x}
,

p(i)=\sum_{\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}}\Tr[E_{\sigma}\cdot\rho_{\sigma(i)}]\cdot p_{X}(\sigma(i)).

(16)

Bob’s guesswork for ensemble
\mathcal{E}
and POVM
E
is now

\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E)\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot p(i),

(17)

where the values
p(i)
are not necessarily in decreasing order, and the quantum guesswork is defined as its minimum over all POVMs
{E}=\{{E}_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}}
:

G_{2}(X|\mathcal{E})\triangleq\inf_{{E}}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E).

(18)

II-D

Equivalence Between Definitions
G_{1}(X|\mathcal{E})
and
G_{2}(X|\mathcal{E})

Theorem 1

Definitions of Chen
et al.
and Dall’Arno
et al.
are equivalent to our Definition
1
:

G_{1}(X|\mathcal{E})=G_{2}(X|\mathcal{E})=G(X|\mathcal{E}).

(19)

Proof:

By definition of conditional guessing entropy,

G(X|Y_{E})=\min_{\sigma}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|{Y_{E}})\!=\!\mathcal{G}_{\sigma^{*}}(X|{Y_{E}})

(20)

where the optimal strategy
\sigma^{*}
is such that for each
y
, the probabilities
p_{X|Y_{E}}(\sigma^{*}_{Y}(i)|y)
are sorted in descending order.
It follows that

G(X|\mathcal{E})=\min_{E}\min_{\sigma}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|{Y_{E}})=\min_{E}G(X|Y_{E})=G_{1}(X|\mathcal{E}).

(21)

Now let
\sigma:y\in\mathcal{Y}\mapsto\sigma_{y}
be Bob’s (not necessarily optimal) strategy and recall the Markov chain

ρ
_x →
[formula]
→Y_E →
σ
_Y_E

and the definition

\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y_{E})=\sum_{y}\sum_{i}\,i\cdot p_{X,Y_{E}}(\sigma_{y}(i),y)

(22)

where by Born’s rule,

p_{X,Y_{E}}(x,y)=p_{X}(x)\cdot\Tr[E_{y}\rho_{x}].

(23)

Again grouping terms in
y
corresponding to the same
\sigma_{y}=\sigma
, we have

p_{X,\sigma_{Y_{E}}}(x,\sigma)=p_{X}(x)\sum_{y\mid\sigma_{y}=\sigma}\Tr[E_{y}\rho_{x}]=p_{X}(x)\Tr[\tilde{E}_{\sigma}\rho_{x}]

(24)

where

\tilde{E}_{\sigma}=\sum_{y\mid\sigma_{y}=\sigma}E_{y}.

(25)

is another POVM
\widetilde{E}
, indexed by permutations
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
, since

\tilde{E}_{\sigma}\geqslant 0
and
\sum_{\sigma}\tilde{E}_{\sigma}=I
.
The output of a measurement is now a realization of a random permutation
\Sigma\triangleq Y_{\tilde{E}}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
, which directly gives Bob’s strategy, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y_{E})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Id}}(X|\Sigma)

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{i}\,i\cdot p_{X,\Sigma}(\sigma(i),\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{i}\,i\cdot p(i,\sigma)=\sum_{i}\,i\cdot p(i)

where
\mathrm{Id}
is the identity permutation since by construction,
\Sigma=\sigma\iff\sigma_{y_{\tilde{E}}}=\sigma
.
Thus, minimizing over
\tilde{E}
is the same as minimizing over both
E
and
\sigma
.
Therefore,

G(X|\mathcal{E})=\min_{E}\min_{\sigma}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(X|Y_{E})=\min_{\tilde{E}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Id}}(X|\Sigma)=G_{2}(X|\mathcal{E})

(27)

which ends the proof.
∎

II-E

Remarks on Attaining the Minimum

Dall’Arno et al.
[
14
]
found a closed form expression
G(X|\mathcal{E})
under some conditions on the quantum ensemble
\mathcal{E}
, using his definition
G_{2}(X|\mathcal{E})
above.
For convenience write
E
instead of
\tilde{E}
, and let

\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E)\triangleq\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Id}}(X|\Sigma)

(28)

to stress the dependence on the quantum ensemble
\mathcal{E}
and the POVM
E
.
With that definition, the objective function to be minimized over the space of POVMs is

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}}\Tr[E_{\sigma}\cdot\rho_{\sigma(i)}]\cdot p_{X}(\sigma(i))

(29)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\Tr[E_{\sigma}\cdot f_{\sigma}]

(30)

where

f_{\sigma}\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot p_{X}(\sigma(i))\rho_{\sigma(i)}
.
To simplify this expression consider its evaluation on
\overline{\sigma}
:

f_{\overline{\sigma}}=(n+1)\widehat{\rho}-f_{\sigma}

(31)

where

\widehat{\rho}\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{\sigma(i)}p_{X}(\sigma(i))
does not depend on
\sigma
.
Therefore, defining

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\triangleq 2f_{\sigma}-(n+1)\widehat{\rho}

(32)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(2t-n-1)p_{X}(\sigma(i))\cdot\rho_{\sigma(i)}.

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n+1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\Tr[E_{\sigma}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}]

(33)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n+1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\Tr[E_{\sigma}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\overline{\sigma}}]

since

\mathcal{E}_{\overline{\sigma}}=-\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 1

Let
A
be an Hermitian positive matrix. If
X
and
Y
are Hermitian matrices such that
X\leqslant Y
, then
\Tr[AX]\leqslant\Tr[AY]
.

Proof:

Let
Y-X=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\left|\varphi_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle\varphi_{i}\right|
be the spectral decomposition of
Y-X\geqslant 0
. Then

\displaystyle\Tr[AY]-\Tr[AX]

\displaystyle=\Tr[A(Y-X)]

(34)

\displaystyle=\Tr[A\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\left|\varphi_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle\varphi_{i}\right|]

(35)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\smash[b]{\Tr[\underbrace{\left\langle\varphi_{i}\right|A\left|\varphi_{i}\right\rangle}_{\geqslant 0}]}\geqslant 0\hskip 56.9055pt

∎

Applying Lemma
1
to
|\mathcal{E}_{\overline{\sigma}}|\geqslant\mathcal{E}_{\overline{\sigma}}
we obtain

\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E)\geqslant\dfrac{n+1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\Tr[E_{\sigma}\cdot|\mathcal{E}_{\overline{\sigma}}|]\\

(36)

where we have used Lemma
1
.
Now, if there exists
\sigma^{*}\in{\cal S}_{n}
such that
|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}|\geqslant|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}|
for all
\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}
,
then

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E)

\displaystyle\geqslant\dfrac{n+1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\Tr[E_{\sigma}\cdot|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}|]

(37)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n+1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Tr[|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}|]

since
\sum_{\sigma}E_{\sigma}=I
. Now

-|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}|=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}<0}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}>0}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}=E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\sigma^{*}}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}+E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\overline{\sigma^{*}}}\mathcal{E}_{\overline{\sigma^{*}}}

(38)

where
E^{\sigma^{*}}
is the POVM defined by

E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\sigma}=\begin{cases}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}<0}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}=0}&\text{if }\sigma\in\{\sigma^{*},\overline{\sigma^{*}}\},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(39)

Indeed, one easily checks that
E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\sigma}\geqslant 0
,
\sum_{\sigma}E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\sigma}=E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\sigma^{*}}+E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\overline{\sigma^{*}}}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}<0}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}=0}+\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\overline{\sigma^{*}}}<0}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\overline{\sigma^{*}}}=0}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}>0}+\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{{\sigma^{*}}}<0}+\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{{\sigma^{*}}}=0}=I
.
It follows that

\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E)\geqslant\dfrac{n+1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\Tr[E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\sigma}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}]

(40)

Hence
\mathcal{G}(X|\Sigma)
is minimized for
E=E^{\sigma^{*}}
.
This explains why this particular POVM achieves the minimum guessing work Dall’Arno et al.’s result:

Theorem 2
(
[
16
, Theorem 1]
)

if
\exists\sigma^{*}\in{\cal S}_{n}
s.t
\forall\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}

|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}|\geqslant|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}|
then

G(X|\mathcal{E})=\dfrac{n+1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma}\Tr[E^{\sigma^{*}}_{\sigma}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}]

(41)

This closed form formula is only valid under the condition
|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma^{*}}|\geqslant|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}|
. In particular, every ensemble made of uniformly distributed qubits satisfies this hypothesis
[
16
]
.
However since the Loewner order
A\geqslant B
is not total, the permutation
\sigma^{*}
in Theorem
2
does not always exist, and the problem of efficient computation of the quantum guesswork is still open for many classes of ensembles.

III
Data Processing Inequalities

III-A

Unitary Invariance

In the framework of the guessing game, if a unitary transformation is applied to the quantum ensemble, we expect the value of quantum guesswork to remain the same:

Theorem 3

Let
U
be a unitary matrix and define

U\mathcal{E}U^{\dagger}=\{U\rho_{x}U^{\dagger},p_{X}(x)\}_{x\in X}.

(42)

Then

G(X|\mathcal{E})=G(X|U\mathcal{E}U^{\dagger}).

(43)

Proof:

First observe that
\tilde{E}_{y}\triangleq U^{\dagger}E_{y}U
forms a POVM since

\sum_{y}\tilde{E}_{y}=U^{\dagger}\sum_{y}E_{y}U=U^{\dagger}U=I
.
From the identity

\Tr[E_{y}\cdot U\rho_{i}U^{\dagger}]=\Tr[U^{\dagger}E_{y}U\cdot\rho_{i}],

(44)

it is easily seen that

\mathcal{G}(U\mathcal{E}U^{\dagger},E)=\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},U^{\dagger}EU).

(45)

Therefore, since the map
E\mapsto\tilde{E}=U^{\dagger}EU
is one-to-one,

\displaystyle G(X|U\mathcal{E}U^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\inf_{E}\mathcal{G}(U\mathcal{E}U^{\dagger},E)

(46)

\displaystyle=\inf_{E}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},U^{\dagger}EU)

(47)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tilde{E}}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\tilde{E})

(48)

\displaystyle=G(X|\mathcal{E})\hskip 142.26378pt

∎

III-B

Post Data Processing Inequality

More generally, assume that a quantum channel is applied to the quantum ensemble.
We expect that guessing the label of the output of the quantum channel is more difficult than directly guessing the initial state. This is a generalization of the
data processing inequality
for guesswork
G(X|Y)
to the quantum case:

Theorem 4

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a quantum channel (a completely positive trace-preserving map) and define

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E})=\{\mathcal{N}(\rho_{x}),p_{X}(x)\}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}.

(49)

Then

G(X|\mathcal{E})\leqslant G(X|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}))

(50)

Proof:

Let

\mathcal{N}(\rho_{x})=\sum_{l=1}^{d}V_{l}\rho_{x}V_{l}^{\dagger}

(51)

be the operator-sum representation
[
11
, Theorem 4.4.1]
of
\mathcal{N}
, where

\sum_{l=1}^{d}V_{l}^{\dagger}V_{l}=I
.
Since

\Tr[E_{y}\cdot\mathcal{N}(\rho_{x_{i}})]=\Tr[E_{y}\cdot\sum_{l=1}^{d}V_{l}\rho_{x_{i}}V_{l}^{\dagger}]=\Tr[\sum_{l=1}^{d}V_{l}^{\dagger}E_{y}V_{l}\cdot\rho_{x_{i}}],

(52)

one has

\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}),E)=\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(E))

(53)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(E_{y})=\sum_{l=1}^{d}V_{l}^{\dagger}E_{y}V_{l}\geqslant 0
is a POVM since

\sum_{y}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(E_{y})=\sum_{l=1}^{d}V_{l}^{\dagger}{\sum_{y}E_{y}}V_{l}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}V_{l}^{\dagger}V_{l}=I.

(54)

Therefore,

\displaystyle G(X|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{E}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}),E)=\inf_{E}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(E))

(55)

\displaystyle\geqslant\inf_{E}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},E)

(56)

\displaystyle=G(X|\mathcal{E})\hskip 145.10922pt

∎

Remark 1

Note that equality does not hold in general since

is not always one-to-one.
However, one recovers Theorem
3

twice, once for the channel

and once for the inverse channel
\rho\mapsto U^{\dagger}\rho\,U
.

Proof:

III-D

From Classical to Quantum Guessing vs. Entropy

Closed-form solutions to the quantum guesswork problem are only known for some specific ensembles under certain conditions (geometrically uniform states
[
3
, Section 5]
, qubits under a uniform distribution
[
14
, Corollary 1]
[
16
, Section B]
). When closed-form solutions are not available, it may be desirable to obtain tight upper and lower bounds in terms of the Shannon entropy of
X
and quantities such as Holevo’s information, which can be computed from the quantum ensemble
\mathcal{E}
.
In the following, we generalize the approach of
[
3
]
to obtain lower and upper bounds from any type of classical inequalities between
H(X|Y)
and
G(X|Y)
.

Let
X
and
Y_{E}
be two random variables defined as in §
I
.
Accessible information
, denoted
I_{acc}(\mathcal{E})
, is the maximum amount of information that Bob can extract from his received state
\rho_{x}
about
X
, defined as

I_{acc}(\mathcal{E})\triangleq\sup_{E}I(X:Y_{E}).

(61)

There is no known formula to analytically solve the optimization problem in the definition of accessible information, but we have the following tight upper
[
21
]
and lower
[
22
]
bounds:

\Lambda(\mathcal{E})\leqslant I_{acc}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant\chi(\mathcal{E}).

(62)

Here
\chi(\mathcal{E})
(Holevo’s information) and
\Lambda(\mathcal{E})
are defined by

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=S\bigl(\sum_{x}p_{X}(x)\rho_{x}\bigr)-\sum_{x}p_{X}(x)S(\rho_{x})

(63)

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=Q\bigl(\sum_{x}p_{X}(x)\rho_{x}\bigr)-\sum_{x}p_{X}(x)Q(\rho_{x})

(64)

where
S
denotes the Von Neumann entropy and
Q
denotes the subentropy, a quantity introduced in
[
22
]
:

\displaystyle S(\rho)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\log(\lambda_{k})

(65)

\displaystyle Q(\rho)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k}\prod_{l\neq k}\dfrac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\lambda_{k}\log({\lambda_{k}})

(66)

where the
\lambda_{k}
’s are the eigenvalues of
\rho
.

Theorem 6

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be two non-decreasing functions such that for Shannon’s entropy and the classical guessing entropy,

\psi(H(X|Y))\geqslant G(X|Y)\geqslant\varphi(H(X|Y)).

(67)

Then for the quantum guesswork, we have

\psi(H(X)-\Lambda(\mathcal{E}))\geqslant G(X|\mathcal{E})\geqslant\varphi(H(X)-\chi(\mathcal{E})).

(68)

Proof:

For any POVM
E
, we have

\psi(H(X|Y_{E}))\geqslant G(X|Y_{E})\geqslant\varphi(H(X|Y_{E})).

(69)

Now, using the bounds in (
62
),

H(X)-\Lambda(\mathcal{E})\geqslant\inf_{E}H(X|Y_{E})\geqslant H(X)-\chi(\mathcal{E}).

(70)

Taking the infimum over
E
in (
69
) we obtain

\psi(H(X)-\Lambda(\mathcal{E}))\geqslant G(X|\mathcal{E})\geqslant\varphi(H(X)-\chi(\mathcal{E})).

(71)

since
\varphi
and
\psi
are non-decreasing.
∎

3

.
In this case McEliece-Yu inequality cannot be improved since it is achieved everywhere when the channel

n

Using the classical Massey inequality

G(X|\mathcal{E})\geqslant\dfrac{2^{H(X)-\chi(\mathcal{E})}}{e}+\dfrac{1}{2}.

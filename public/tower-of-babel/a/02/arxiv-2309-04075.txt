Semi-abelian categories arising from pseudo cluster tilting subcategories
Jian He, Jing He

Abstract
The notion of a pseudo cluster tilting subcategory 𝒳 \mathscr{X} in an extriangulated category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} is defined in this article. We prove that the quotient category 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} , obtained by factoring an extriangulated category by a pseudo cluster tilting subcategory, is a semi-abelian category. Furthermore, we also show that the quotient category 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} is an abelian category if and only if certain self-orthogonal conditions are satisfied. As an application, these results generalize the work of Xu and Zheng in the exact category. Keywords: extriangulated categories; semi-abelian categories; pseudo cluster tilting subcategory; cluster tilting subcategories; abelian categories 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10

Introduction

Nakaoka and Palu introduced the concept of extriangulated categories in their seminal work
[
8
]
. This notion simultaneously generalizes exact categories and triangulated categories. Exact categories, which include abelian categories, and extension-closed subcategories within extriangulated categories are considered as specific instances of extriangulated categories.
Moreover, there exist additional instances of extriangulated categories that do not fall within the categories of exact or triangulated, as documented in
[
8
,
12
,
4
]
.

Cluster tilting theory offers a method for constructing abelian categories from some triangulated categories and exact categories. Koenig and Zhu
[
5
]
established a comprehensive framework for transitioning from triangulated categories to abelian categories by factoring out cluster tilting subcategories.
Following this line of research, Demonet and Liu
[
2
]
subsequently demonstrated a similar result to that of Koenig and Zhu. Specifically, they presented a generalized framework for transitioning from exact categories to abelian categories by isolating cluster tilting subcategories.
Expanding on this work, Liu and Nakaoka
[
6
]
demonstrated that any quotient of an extriangulated category modulo a cluster tilting subcategory inherently carries an induced abelian structure. This result extends and generalizes both
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
and

[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Omega)
be an exact category with the exact structure
\Omega
. It is well known that the category
\Omega(\mathscr{C})
of conflations in
\mathscr{C}
forms an additive category. Furthermore, the category
\Omega(\mathscr{C})
possesses an exact structure, computed degree-wise. Let
S(\mathscr{C})
denote the full subcategory of
\Omega(\mathscr{C})
consisting of all splitting conflations.
Recently, in their work
[
10
]
, Xu and Zheng introduced the concept of a pseudo cluster tilting subcategory
\mathscr{X}
in an exact category
\mathscr{C}
. They demonstrated that
S(\mathscr{C})
always constitutes a pseudo cluster tilting subcategory of
\Omega(\mathscr{C}).
More generally, Xu and Zheng established that the quotient category
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
of an exact category by a pseudo cluster tilting subcategory is a semi-abelian category, with
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
becoming an abelian category if and only if certain self-orthogonal conditions are satisfied.
Motivated by these findings, a natural question arises: Can the results of Xu and Zheng
[
10
]
be extended within the framework of extriangulated categories? In this article, we provide an affirmative answer.

Consider an extriangulated category
\mathscr{C}
with a pseudo cluster tilting subcategory
\mathscr{X}
.
Our first main result establishes that the quotient category
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
, obtained by dividing an extriangulated category by a pseudo cluster tilting subcategory, becomes a semi-abelian category, as detailed in Theorem
3.9
. Our second main result demonstrates that
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
attains the status of an abelian category if and only if specific self-orthogonal conditions are satisfied, as outlined in Theorem
3.15
. These findings represent a broadening and generalization of the results initially presented by Xu and Zheng in
[
10
]
.
To further elucidate our main results, we provide illustrative examples.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we provide a review of essential definitions and facts necessary for our study. In Section 3, we present the proofs of the main results of this article.

In this section, when we say that

We recall from
[

an additive
\mathscr{C}
with kernels and cokernels is called
semi-abelian
if for any morphism

, the canonical morphism
\hat{f}\colon{\rm Coim}f\to{\rm Im}f
is both an epimorphism and a monomorphism. It is abelian if

The Chern-Weil-Lecomte characteristic map for L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras
Juan Sebastián Herrera-Carmona, Cristian Ortiz

Abstract
. In this paper we extend the Chern-Weil-Lecomte characteristic map to the setting of L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras. In this general framework, characteristic classes of L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra extensions are defined by means of the Chern-Weil-Lecomte map which takes values in the cohomology of an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra with coefficients in a representation up to homotopy. This general set up allows us to recover several known cohomology classes in a unified manner, including: the characteristic class of a Lie 2-algebra, the Ševera class of an exact Courant algebroid and the curvature 3-form of a gerbe with connective structure. We conclude by introducing a Chern-Weil map for principal 2-bundles over Lie groupoids.



1.
Introduction

Chern-Weil theory is a geometric setting which permits to construct invariants of principal bundles called characteristic classes. Given a principal
G
-bundle
P\to M
, the Chern-Weil map is an algebra morphism

cw:S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\Ad}\to H_{dR}(M),

which maps an invariant polynomial to a de Rham cohomology class defined in terms of the curvature of a principal connection.

In
[
36
]
, Lecomte introduced characteristic classes associated with any extension of Lie algebras
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
with kernel
\mathfrak{n}
, together with a Lie algebra representation
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{End}(V)
. The corresponding
Chern-Weil-Lecomte characteristic map
is defined as

cw:\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{n},V)^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}\to H^{ev}(\mathfrak{g},V),f\mapsto[f_{K_{h}}],

where
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{n},V)^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
is the space of symmetric linear functions
f:\mathfrak{n}^{\otimes k}\to V
which are
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant and
f_{K_{h}}\in\wedge^{ev}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes V
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg cocycle defined by evaluating
f
at a suitable wedge product of the curvature
K_{h}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{n}
of a splitting
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Just as in the classical case, the cohomology class
[f_{K_{h}}]
is independent of the splitting
[
36
]
.

The classical Chern-Weil morphism is recovered from this algebraic perspective by looking at the Atiyah extension of a principal bundle
P\to M
together with the canonical representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}(M)
of vector fields on the space of functions
C^{\infty}(M)
. Several authors have also generalized the Chern-Weil construction to a variety of situations, see for instance
[
1
,
3
,
17
,
31
,
35
]
.

In this paper we are concerned with a generalization of Lecomte’s construction to the framework of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. Introduced in
[
34
]
,
L_{\infty}
-algebras certain higher structures which generalize Lie algebras in the sense that the Jacobi identity holds up to higher order homotopies. These objects play an important role in deformation theory but also arise as the space of observables in multisymplectic geometry
[
47
]
. Other examples of higher structures include: Courant algebroids
[
37
]
, central to the study of generalized geometry, Lie groupoids and their associated differentiable stacks
[
6
]
as geometric objects which take care in a smooth way of singularities and quotients,
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes
[
8
]
as degree three cohomology analogues of line bundles, n-plectic structures
[
47
]
which arise in connection with classical field theories
[
49
]
, principal 2-bundles as the main objects of study in higher gauge theory
[
5
,
51
,
53
]
, among many others. All of the previous examples of higher structures have an associated extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras, hence it is natural to extend the Chern-Weil-Lecomte construction to this more general framework giving rise to characteristic classes which are invariants of the aforementioned higher structures.

In order to extend the Chern-Weil-Lecomte characteristic map to the setting of
L_{\infty}
-algebras, one needs to study the cohomology of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with values in a representation up to homotopy
[
43
,
45
]
. Representations up to homotopy of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
are also called
L_{\infty}
-modules in
[
33
]
, these are given by a graded vector space
\mathbb{V}
together with a suitable notion of action of
\mathfrak{g}
. It turns out that a representation up to homotopy has an associated cochain complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D)
which generalizes the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex defined by an ordinary Lie algebra representation. The corresponding cohomology
H(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
is called the
L_{\infty}
-cohomology
of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
\mathbb{V}
.

It is natural to investigate the invariance of
H(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
with respect to a suitable notion of morphism of representations up to homotopy. For that, we study functorial properties of
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
. It is well known that an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is equivalent to a degree one coderivation
d_{\mathfrak{g}}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
with
d^{2}_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
. Here
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
is the symmetric coalgebra of the graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. An
L_{\infty}
-morphism

F:(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{g}})\to(S(\mathfrak{h}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a coalgebra morphism which commutes with the coderivations. An
L_{\infty}
-morphism is completely determined by its projection onto
\mathfrak{h}[1]
, hence by a family of maps
F^{1}_{n}:S^{n}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{h}[1];\quad n\geq 1.
We say that
F
is
strict
if
F^{1}_{n}=0
for every
n\geq 2
. Every
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has an underlying cochain complex of vector spaces defined by the first order bracket
[\cdot]:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
given by the
L_{\infty}
-structure of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
F:(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{g}})\to(S(\mathfrak{h}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{h}})
is an
L_{\infty}
-morphism, then its
linear part

F^{1}_{1}:\mathfrak{g}[1]\to\mathfrak{h}[1]
is a cochain map between the underlying complexes. We say that
F
is an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism
if its linear part is a quasi-isomorphism between the underlying complexes.

One can see that a graded vector space
\mathbb{V}
which is a representation up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
inherits a canonical structure of cochain complex. Let
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{W}
be representations up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
, respectively. A
morphism of representations up to homotopy

\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{W}
is given by a pair
(F,f)
where
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra map and
f:\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{V}
is a degree preserving map satisfying a suitable equivariance condition, see Definition
3.4
. If both
F
and
f
are quasi-isomorphisms, we say that
(F,f)
is a
quasi-isomorphism
of representations up to homotopy.

It turns out that every morphism of representations up to homotopy induces a pullback
F^{*}:C(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
which is a cochain map, see Proposition
3.8
. Our first main result says that the invariance of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology is controlled by the linear part of a morphism of representations up to homotopy.

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{W}
be representations up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
, respectively. If
(F,f):\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{W}
is a quasi-isomorphism of representations up to homotopy, then

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{*}:C(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})}

is a quasi-isomorphism. Hence
H(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\cong H(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W})
.

We proceed now to state the
L_{\infty}
-version of the Chern-Weil-Lecomte map. For that, recall that an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras is a short exact sequence of strict
L_{\infty}
-morphisms

0\to\mathfrak{n}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}\to 0.

Suppose that
\mathbb{V}
is a representation up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
. We also see
\mathbb{V}
as a representation up to homotopy of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
defined by the pullback representation up to homotopy via
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
. One observes that
\mathfrak{n}[1]
inherits a canonical representation up to homotopy of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
, which extends naturally to any exterior power
\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1]
. Hence one considers the space
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
of all morphisms of representations up to homotopy. The following is our second main result.

Theorem
.

Let
K_{h}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
be the curvature of a section
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
of an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

and
\mathbb{V}
a representation up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
f\in\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
, then

(1)

the element
f_{K_{h}}=f\circ K^{\wedge k}_{h}
is a cocycle in the complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D_{\rho})
,

(2)

the cohomology class
[f_{K_{h}}]\in H^{\bullet+k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
is independent of the section
h
.

Here
K^{\wedge k}_{h}\in C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
is a suitable exterior power defined in terms of cochain products, see subsection
3.3
. As a consequence, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is a well defined map

cw:\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}\to H^{\bullet+k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V});f\mapsto[f_{K_{h}}]

called the
Chern-Weil-Lecomte map
associated with an
L_{\infty}
-algebra extension.

The cohomology classes
cw(f)=[f_{K_{h}}]\in H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
are invariants of an extension of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
together with the representation up to homotopy
\mathbb{V}
. This is the content of Theorem
4.9
.

An important class of
L_{\infty}
-algebras is given by
2-term

L_{\infty}
-algebras, that is, those whose underlying complex has length 2. As shown in
[
4
]
, 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras correspond to Lie 2-algebras, which are categorified Lie algebras. There are two different types of Lie 2-algebras called
strict
and
semi-strict
. The former are Lie 2-algebras in which the usual Jacobi identity is satisfied and they are equivalent to crossed modules of Lie algebras. Among the main examples of strict Lie 2-algebras, one encounters: multiplicative vector fields on a Lie groupoid
[
7
,
42
]
, the sections of an LA-groupoid
[
42
]
, infinitesimal symmetries of an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe
[
18
,
19
,
32
]
, among others. The latter are Lie 2-algebras in which the Jacobi identity holds up to a natural isomorphism and important examples include: sections of a Courant algebroid
[
48
]
, the Atiyah Lie 2-algebra associated with a closed 3-form
[
23
]
, the Poisson Lie 2-algebra of 2-plectic structures
[
47
]
and multiplicative forms on quasi-Poisson groupoids
[
15
]
.

We apply the Chern-Weil-Lecomte construction of characteristic classes of
L_{\infty}
-algebras to recover well known cohomology classes associated with a variety of structures, including: the characteristic class of any Lie 2-algebra, the Ševera class of an exact Courant algebroid and the 3-curvature form of an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe with connective structure.

Besides the aforementioned examples of characteristic classes defined via the
L_{\infty}
-version of the Chern-Weil-Lecomte map, we also apply our construction to introduce a Chern-Weil morphism for principal 2-bundles over Lie groupoids. Principal 2-bundles equipped with a 2-connection are the main objects of study in higher gauge theory
[
5
,
51
]
, which is a geometric setting in which parallel transport is defined not only along paths, but also along surfaces. The notion of principal 2-bundle considered in this paper can be thought of as a strict version of a stacky principal bundle as in
[
12
]
. Several notions of principal 2-bundles have been studied by different authors, including
[
53
,
51
,
14
]
. Also, in
[
35
]
it is introduced a Chern-Weil map for principal
G
-bundles over Lie groupoids, which are particular cases of principal 2-bundles in which the structural 2-group is the unit groupoid defined by a Lie group.

Given a Lie 2-group
\mathbb{G}
, a
principal 2-bundle
is given by a right Lie 2-group action
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
together with a Lie groupoid fibration
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{X}
which is
\mathbb{G}
-invariant and principal in the sense that
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{P}\times_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{P}
is a Lie groupoid isomorphism. A
2-connection
on
\mathbb{P}=(P_{1}\rightrightarrows P_{0})
is defined as a
\mathbb{g}
-valued multiplicative 1-form whose components are ordinary connections on
P_{1}
and
P_{0}
, respectively. Here
\mathbb{g}
denotes the strict Lie 2-algebra of the Lie 2-group
\mathbb{G}
. See Definition
5.31
. Let
\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}
be the crossed module associated with
\mathbb{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Then, every 2-connection can be written as
\theta=\bar{\theta}+s^{*}\theta_{0}
where
\bar{\theta}\in\Omega^{1}(P_{1},\mathfrak{h})
and
\theta_{0}\in\Omega^{1}(P_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0})
satisfying certain conditions.

The
curvature
of
\theta
is defined as the multiplicative 2-form
\Omega=d\theta+\frac{1}{2}[\theta,\theta]\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})
. Due to the multiplicativity of the curvature 2-form, one has
\Omega=\bar{\Omega}+s^{*}\Omega_{0}
where
\bar{\Omega}\in\Omega^{2}(P_{1},\mathfrak{h})
and
\Omega_{0}\in\Omega^{2}(P_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0})
is the curvature of the connection
\theta_{0}
. We introduce the following terminology.

Definition
.

Let
\theta=\bar{\theta}+s^{*}\theta_{0}
be a 2-connection on a principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
with curvature 2-form
\Omega=\bar{\Omega}+s^{*}\Omega_{0}
. A
curving
for
\theta
is a basic 2-form
B\in\Omega^{2}(P_{0},\mathfrak{h})
with:

(1)

\Omega_{0}+\partial\circ B=0
, and

(2)

\bar{\Omega}+(t^{*}-s^{*})(B)=0
.

A
connective structure
on a principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
is a pair
(\theta,B)
where
\theta
is a 2-connection on
\mathbb{P}
and
B
is a curving for
\theta
. The
curvature 3-form
of a connective structure
(\theta,B)
is the 3-form
\nabla^{\theta_{0}}B\in\Omega^{3}(P_{0},\mathfrak{h})
defined as the covariant derivative of
B
induced by
\theta_{0}
. In the special case of principal 2-bundles with
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightrightarrows\{*\})
, one recovers the usual notions of connective structure and curvature 3-form on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes.

Just as ordinary connections, a 2-connection on
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
is equivalent to a multiplicative splitting of the Atiyah sequence

0\to\Ad(\mathbb{P})\to{\mathrm{At}}(\mathbb{P})\to T\mathbb{X}\to 0.

This is a short exact sequence of LA-groupoids over
\mathbb{X}
. As shown in
[
42
]
, every LA-groupoid
\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{X}
has an associated strict Lie 2-algebra
\mathbb{L}(\mathbb{E})
of multiplicative sections. In particular, there is extension of Lie 2-algebras of multiplicative sections

0\to\mathbb{L}(\Ad(\mathbb{P}))\to\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{At}}(\mathbb{P}))\to\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X})\to 0.

called the
Atiyah extension
of
\mathbb{P}
. A 2-connection induces a splitting
h:\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X})\to\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{At}}(\mathbb{\mathbb{P}}))
of the Atiyah extension which is not necessarily an
L_{\infty}
-morphism. The curvature 3-form of a connective structure on a principal 2-bundle has the following geometric interpretation.

Theorem
.

Let
(\theta,B)
be a connective structure on a principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
. If the curvature 3-form vanishes, then the induced splitting
h:\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X})\to\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{At}}(\mathbb{\mathbb{P}}))
defined by the 2-connection
\theta
is a weak morphism of 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

This result generalizes a theorem by Krepski and Vaughan
[
32
]
in the setting of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes with connective structure.

We explain now our last main result. Let
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
be a principal 2-bundle. The 2-vector space of multiplicative functions on
\mathbb{F(X)}
is the one associated with the 2-term complex
C^{\infty}(X_{0})\to C^{\infty}_{mult}(\mathbb{X})
given by the truncation of the usual groupoid complex. The Lie derivative extends to a natural representation up to homotopy of the Lie 2-algebra of multiplicative vector fields
\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X})
on
\mathbb{F(X)}

[
26
]
. Combining this representation up to homotopy with the splitting
h:\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X})\to\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{At}}(\mathbb{\mathbb{P}}))
defined by a 2-connection on
\mathbb{P}
, one is led to the following version of the Chern-Weil map for principal 2-bundles.

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
be a principal 2-bundle equipped with a 2-connection. Let
h:\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X})\to\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{At}}(\mathbb{P}))
the induced splitting of the Atiyah extension of Lie 2-algebras. For every
k\geq 0
there exists a natural map

(1)

\displaystyle cw:\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathbb{L}(\Ad(\mathbb{P}))

\displaystyle[1],\mathbb{F(X)})^{\mathbb{L}(\mathrm{At}(\mathbb{P}))}\to H^{\bullet+k}(\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X}),\mathbb{F(X)})

\displaystyle f\mapsto[f\circ K^{k}_{h}],

where
K_{h}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X}),\mathbb{L}(\Ad(\mathbb{P}))[1])
is the curvature of
h
. The map
cw
is independent of the 2-connection.

It is natural to investigate the relation between the cohomology
H(\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X}),\mathbb{F(X)})
of the Lie 2-algebra of multiplicative vector fields
\mathbb{L}(T\mathbb{X})
with values in
\mathbb{F}(\mathbb{X})
and the de Rham cohomology
H_{dR}(\mathbb{X})
, which is defined as the total cohomology of the Bott-Shulman-Stasheff complex of the base Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
. We plan to investigate this question in a future work.

This work is organized as follows. In Section 2 we present the basics on
L_{\infty}
-algebras and their morphisms. In Section 3, we study the cohomology of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with coefficients in a representation up to homotopy. The main result of this section is Theorem
3.27
which says that the
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology with coefficients in a representation up to homotopy is invariant by quasi-isomorphisms of representations up to homotopy. In Section 4 we study extensions of
L_{\infty}
-algebras and we prove Theorem
4.8
which is the
L_{\infty}
-version of the Chern-Weil-Lecomte map and the main result of this section. We also discuss several examples of cohomology classes which can be described by means of the Chern-Weil-Lecomte map. In Section 5 we specialize our results to the setting of principal 2-bundles. We give a detailed presentation of principal 2-bundles, 2-connections and curvature. We show Theorem
5.43
which give an interpretation of the curvature 3-form of a connective structure on a principal 2-bundle as the failure of a splitting of the Atiyah extension to be a weak
L_{\infty}
-morphism. Then we state Theorem
5.45
which introduces a Chern-Weil map for principal 2-bundles over Lie groupoids.

2.
Background material

In this section we briefly recall the definition of
L_{\infty}
-algebras and their morphisms. We follow
[
45
]
closely.

2.1.
Graded vector spaces

Let
\mathbb{V}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
be a graded vector space. An element
v\in V_{n}
is called homogeneous of degree
n
and its degree is denoted by
|v|
. Given a second graded vector space
\mathbb{W}
, the space
\underline{\Hom}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W})
is the graded vector space whose homogeneous component of degree
p
is defined by

\underline{\Hom}^{p}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W}):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}(V_{n},W_{n+p}).

A linear map
f\in\underline{\Hom}^{p}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W})
is called homogeneous of degree
|f|=p
. A linear map
f:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{W}
is called degree preserving if it is homogeneous of degree zero. If
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{V}
we write
\underline{\End}(\mathbb{V}):=\underline{\Hom}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{V})
.

The tensor product
\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{W}
is the graded vector space with grading given by

(\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{W})_{n}:=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}V_{i}\otimes W_{j},

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, for every
n\geq 0
one has a graded vector space
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{V}
.

One can also define the tensor product of two homogeneous linear maps
f\in\underline{\Hom}^{p}(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{W})
and
g\in\underline{\Hom}^{q}(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\mathbb{W}^{\prime})
as the homogeneous linear map
f\otimes g\in\underline{\Hom}^{p+q}(\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\mathbb{W}\otimes\mathbb{W}^{\prime})
defined by

(2)

(f\otimes g)(v\otimes v^{\prime}):=(-1)^{|g||v|}f(v)\otimes g(v^{\prime}),

for any homogeneous elements
v\in\mathbb{V}
and
v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
. The tensor product of composable linear maps is composable and one has the following interchange law

(3)

(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})\circ(g_{1}\otimes g_{2})=(-1)^{|f_{2}||g_{1}|}(f_{1}\circ g_{1})\otimes(f_{2}\circ g_{2}),

where
f_{1},g_{1}
and
f_{2},g_{2}
are couples of composable homogeneous linear maps.

The graded vector space
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
carries a natural
\Sigma_{n}
-action by degree preserving maps
\hat{\epsilon}:\Sigma_{n}\to\underline{\End}(\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n})
, where
\Sigma_{n}
denotes the symmetric group in
n
-letters. For every transposition
\sigma_{i}=(i,i+1),1\leq i\leq n
the action is defined by

\hat{\epsilon}(\sigma_{i})(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n})=(-1)^{|v_{i}||v_{i+1}|}v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{i+1}\otimes v_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}

where
v_{i}\in\mathbb{V}
are homogeneous elements with
1\leq i\leq n
. The action of an arbitrary permutation
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
is defined as

(4)

\hat{\epsilon}(\sigma)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}):=\epsilon(\sigma)v_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots v_{\sigma(n)}

where
\epsilon(\sigma)
is the
Koszul sign
. Although the Koszul sign also depends on
v_{1},...,v_{n}
we write
\epsilon(\sigma)
to make our notation simpler. This action naturally extends to the tensor algebra
T(\mathbb{V}):=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
, which is equipped with the graded product given by concatenation. For every
n\geq 0
there is a graded subspace
I_{\epsilon}\subset\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
defined by

I_{\epsilon}:=\mathrm{span}\{v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}-\epsilon(\sigma)v_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma(n)};\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}\}.

The
n
-th symmetric power
of
\mathbb{V}
is defined by
S^{n}(\mathbb{V}):=\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}/I_{\epsilon}
. The
symmetric algebra
of
\mathbb{V}
is the free unital graded commutative algebra given by

S(\mathbb{V})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}S^{n}(\mathbb{V}):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}/I_{\epsilon}.

The canonical projection
\pi_{S}:T(\mathbb{V})\to S(\mathbb{V})
is a surjective linear degree preserving map, whose restriction to the fixed points
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathbb{V})\subset\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
of the action
\hat{\epsilon}:\Sigma_{n}\to\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n})
yields an isomorphism
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathbb{V})\cong S^{n}(\mathbb{V})
. For
v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\in\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
we write
v_{1}\vee\cdots\vee v_{n}:=\pi_{S}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})
and the graded product in the symmetric algebra is denoted
\mu_{S}:S(\mathbb{V})\otimes S(\mathbb{V})\to S(\mathbb{V})
. For a homogeneous element
v=v_{1}\vee\cdots\vee v_{n}
in
S^{n}(\mathbb{V})
its
weight
is the integer
n
and its
degree
is
|v|=|v_{1}|+\cdots+|v_{n}|
.

The
exterior algebra
of
\mathbb{V}
is defined in a similar manner by

\wedge\mathbb{V}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\wedge^{n}(\mathbb{V}),

where
\wedge^{n}(\mathbb{V}):=\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}/I_{\chi}
for every
n\geq 0
. Here
I_{\chi}\subset\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
is defined similarly by considering the skew-symmetric
\Sigma_{n}
-action on
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
defined on transpositions by

\hat{\chi}(\sigma_{i})(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n})=-(-1)^{|v_{i}||v_{i+1}|}v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{i+1}\otimes v_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}.

The tensor algebra
T(\mathbb{V})
carries the structure of a coassociative, cocommutative coalgebra with coproduct given by deconcatenation. It turns out that the symmetric algebra
S(\mathbb{V})
inherits a coalgebra structure which we recall now. A permutation
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
is called a
(k,n-k)
-
unshuffle
with
0\leq k\leq n
, if
\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(k)
and
\sigma(k+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n)
. The set of
(k,n-k)
-unshuffles is denoted by
Sh(k,n-k)
. The coproduct
\Delta_{S}:S(\mathbb{V})\to S(\mathbb{V})\otimes S(\mathbb{V})
is defined by the symmetric deconcatenation

(5)

\Delta_{S}(v_{1}\vee\cdots\vee v_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(k,n-k)}\epsilon(\sigma)(v_{\sigma(1)}\vee\cdots\vee v_{\sigma(k)})\otimes(v_{\sigma(k+1)}\vee\cdots\vee v_{\sigma(n)}).

This makes
S(\mathbb{V})
a coassociative and cocommutative coalgebra, which is also coaugmented with
S(\mathbb{V})=\mathbb{R}1\oplus\bar{S}(\mathbb{V})
where
\bar{S}(\mathbb{V}):=\oplus_{n\geq 1}S^{n}(\mathbb{V})
. The induced coproduct
\bar{\Delta}_{S}:\bar{S}(\mathbb{V})\to\bar{S}(\mathbb{V})\otimes\bar{S}(\mathbb{V})
is defined as
\Delta_{S}=1\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}+\bar{\Delta}_{S}+{\mathrm{Id}}\otimes 1
and it is referred to as the
reduced coproduct
.

2.2.
L_{\infty}
-algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
be a non-positively graded vector space. The structure of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra on
\mathfrak{g}
can be seen in three equivalent ways, either as higher Lie brackets, higher symmetric functions or coderivations of the coalgebra
S(\mathbb{V})
, respectively. Each of these perspectives will be explained below. For more details, the reader can see
[
34
,
33
,
40
]
and references therein.

An
L_{\infty}
-structure
on
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is given by a family of linear maps
[\cdot]^{k}:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g};k\geq 1
of degree
2-k
called
higher brackets
, with vanishing graded Jacobiator:

(6)

\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(i,n-i)}(-1)^{i(j-1)}\chi(\sigma)\left[\left[v_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{\sigma(i)}\right]^{i}\wedge v_{\sigma(i+1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{\sigma(n)}\right]^{j}=0,

for every
n\geq 1
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
is a pair
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot]^{\bullet})
given by a non-positively graded vector space equipped with an
L_{\infty}
-structure.

Let us see some examples of
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot]^{\bullet})
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. The degree one map
\partial:=[\cdot]^{1}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
\partial^{2}=0
. In particular, an
L_{\infty}
-algebra in which
[\cdot]^{k}=0
for every
k\geq 2
is the same that a cochain complex of vector spaces.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra in which
[\cdot]^{k}=0
for every
k\geq 3
. This is equivalent to having a structure of a differential graded Lie algebra (dgla for short) on the graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}
with bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:=[\cdot]^{2}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and differential
d:=[\cdot]^{1}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 2.4
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}
concentrated in degrees -1 and 0 is called 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra. In this case, the only not necessarily trivial brackets are
[\cdot]^{i}:\wedge^{i}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
with
i=1,2,3
. As shown in
[
4
]
, the category of 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras is equivalent to that of semistrict Lie 2-algebras. If
[\cdot]^{3}=0
, one recovers the notion of a strict Lie 2-algebra, which is also equivalent to having a crossed module of Lie algebras. That is, a cochain complex of Lie algebras
\partial:\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}
together with an action
D:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}_{-1})
satisfying the Peiffer identities:

(1)

D_{\partial(u)}v=\ad_{u}v

(2)

\partial(D_{x}u)=\ad_{x}\partial(u)

for every
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.

The
decálage
isomorphism
S^{n}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\cong(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g})[n],n\geq 1
(see
[
45
]
), yields an alternative description of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on a graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
. Namely, as a family of linear maps
\lambda_{k}:S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{g}[1],k\geq 1
of degree 1 satisfying

(7)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(i,n-i)}\epsilon(\sigma)\lambda_{n-i+1}(\lambda_{i}(v_{\sigma(1)}\vee\cdots\vee v_{\sigma(i)})\vee v_{\sigma(i+1)}\vee\cdots\vee v_{\sigma(n)})=0,

for every
n\geq 1
.

The family
\lambda_{k}:S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{g}[1];k\geq 1
yields a linear map
\lambda:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{g}[1]
via
\lambda=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}
. This data is equivalent to a degree 1 coderivation
d_{\lambda}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
defined by

(8)

d_{\lambda}:=\mu_{S}\circ(\lambda\otimes{\mathrm{Id}})\circ\Delta_{S}.

Also, the maps
\lambda_{k}:S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{g}[1]
satisfy (
7
) if and only if the induced coderivation squares to zero, i.e.
d_{\lambda}^{2}=0
.

Remark 2.5
.

The map
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]),\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathrm{Coder}(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]));\lambda\mapsto d_{\lambda}
as in (
8
) is an isomorphism. In particular, an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is equivalent to a degree one coderivation
d\in\mathrm{Coder}(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]))
with
d(1)=0
and
d^{2}=0
.

Accordingly, an
L_{\infty}
-algebra is denoted either by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot]^{\bullet}),(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
or
(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]),d)
.

2.3.
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphisms

Viewing an
L_{\infty}
-structure on a graded vector space as a degree 1 coderivation on its symmetric coalgebra allows us to introduce
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphisms in a simple manner.

Definition 2.6
.

An
L_{\infty}
-
morphism

F:(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{g}})\to(S(\mathfrak{h}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a coalgebra morphism which commutes with the differentials.

That is,
F:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
is a degree zero linear map with the following properties:

(9)

\displaystyle F\otimes F\circ\Delta_{S}=

\displaystyle\Delta_{S}\circ F

(10)

\displaystyle F\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}}=

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ F.

Any coalgebra morphism
F:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
is uniquely determined by its projection onto
\mathfrak{h}[1]
, therefore equivalent to a collection of degree zero linear maps

F^{1}_{n}:S^{n}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{h}[1];\quad n\geq 1.

An
L_{\infty}
-
morphism

F:(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{g}})\to(S(\mathfrak{h}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{h}})
is called
strict
if
F_{n}^{1}=0
for
n\geq 2
. This is equivalent to having
F=S(F^{1}_{1})
where

(11)

(SF^{1}_{1})(v_{1}\vee\cdots\vee v_{n})=F_{1}^{1}(v_{1})\vee\cdots\vee F_{1}^{1}(v_{n}),

is the canonical extension of
F^{1}_{1}:\mathfrak{g}[1]\to\mathfrak{h}[1]
. In this case, identity (
10
) can be written in terms of symmetric brackets. More precisely, if
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
d_{\mathfrak{h}}
are respectively induced by
\lambda^{\mathfrak{g}}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{g}[1]
and
\lambda^{\mathfrak{h}}:S(\mathfrak{h}[1])\to\mathfrak{h}[1]
as in (
8
), then (
10
) is equivalent to

(12)

\lambda^{\mathfrak{h}}\circ S(F^{1}_{1})=F^{1}_{1}\circ\lambda^{\mathfrak{g}}.

As shown in Example
2.2
, an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot]^{\bullet})
has an underlying complex of vector spaces with differential
\partial:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
where
\partial:=[\cdot]^{1}
. If
F
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism, then
F^{1}_{1}:\mathfrak{g}[1]\to\mathfrak{h}[1]
is a morphism between the underlying complexes. We say that
F
is an

L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism
if the induced map in cohomology
H(F_{1}^{1}):H(\mathfrak{g},\partial)\to H(\mathfrak{h},\partial)
is an isomorphism.

An important example of
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism is given by the minimal model of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot]^{\bullet})
is called
minimal
if
[\cdot]^{1}=0
. We say that it is
contractible
if
[\cdot]^{k}=0
for every
k\geq 2
and
H(\mathfrak{g},\partial)=0
where
\partial:=[\cdot]^{1}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is the underlying cochain complex.

Example 2.7

(
Kontsevich decomposition
)
.

Every
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to a direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{m}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{c}
of a minimal and a contractible
L_{\infty}
-algebra, see
[
30
]
. It turns out that
\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-isomorphic to its minimal component
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
, which is unique up to quasi-isomorphism and referred to as the
minimal model
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

The minimal model of a strict 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra can be easily described in terms of crossed modules. This is explained in the next example.

Example 2.8

(
Minimal model of 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras
)
.

As shown in
[
4
]
, minimal 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras are in one-to-one correspondence with quadruples
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},V,\rho,\theta)
where
\rho:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathrm{End}(V)
is a Lie algebra representation and
\theta\in Z^{3}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{0},V)
is a 3-cocycle in the corresponding Chevalley-Eilenberg complex. The correspondence assigns to every minimal 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}
the vector space
V:=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
together with the representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\otimes V\to V;(x,u)\mapsto[x,u]^{2}
and 3-cocycle
\theta:=[\cdot]^{3}
. See Thm. 55 in
[
4
]
. Isomorphic minimal 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras give rise to an isomorphism between the corresponding quadruples. In particular, there is a one-to-one correspondence between quasi-isomorphism classes of 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras and isomorphisms of quadruples
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},V,\rho,[\theta])
with
[\theta]\in H^{3}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{0},V)
. The cohomology class
[\theta]\in H^{3}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g}_{0},V)
is called the
characteristic class
of a 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Now we describe the minimal model of a
strict
2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, hence providing an explicit description of its characteristic class. For that, let
\partial:\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}
be the underlying crossed module of
\mathfrak{g}
and consider
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{0}
with
\mathfrak{h}_{-1}:=\ker(\partial)
and
\mathfrak{h}_{0}:=\mathrm{coker}(\partial)
. The choice of a splitting
h:\mathfrak{h}_{0}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}
of the canonical projection
\pi:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathfrak{h}_{0}
induces a quasi-isomorphism of complexes

(13)

(\iota,h):\big(0:\mathfrak{h}_{-1}\to\mathfrak{h}_{0}\big)\to\big(\partial:\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}\big).

This quasi-isomorphism of complexes is part of a
weak
quasi-isomorphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras
F:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
with
F^{1}_{1}=(\iota,h)
and
F^{1}_{2}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}_{0}\to\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
defined as
F^{1}_{2}=\sigma_{*}K_{h}
where
K_{h}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h}_{0}\to\mathrm{im}(\partial)
and
\sigma:\mathrm{im}(\partial)\to\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
is a splitting of the exact sequence
\ker(\partial)\to\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\to\mathrm{im}(\partial)
. The
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\mathfrak{h}
has bracket of order three given by

\theta=d_{h^{*}D}\sigma_{*}K_{h},

where
h^{*}D:\mathfrak{h}_{0}\times\mathfrak{h}_{-1}\to\mathfrak{h}_{-1};(\bar{x},u)\mapsto[h(\bar{x}),u]
is the pullback via
h
of the representation
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}_{-1})
. Hence, the characteristic class of the strict 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is

[d_{h^{*}D}\sigma_{*}K_{h}]\in H^{3}_{CE}(\mathrm{coker}(\partial),\ker(\partial)).

3.
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology

In this section we recall the notion of
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology with coefficients in a representation up to homotopy. For that we follow
[
45
]
closely. See also
[
33
]
.

3.1.
Representations up to homotopy

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with induced coderivation
d_{\lambda}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
as in (
8
). Given a graded vector space
\mathbb{V}
, the tensor product
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}
has a natural structure of left
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
-comodule defined by

\Delta_{\mathbb{V}}:=\Delta_{S}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V},

where
\Delta_{S}
denotes the symmetric coproduct in
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
as in (
5
).

Definition 3.1
.

A
representation up to homotopy (or ruth)
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathbb{V}
is a degree one coderivation
D:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}
satisfying:

i)

\Delta_{\mathbb{V}}\circ D=(d_{\lambda}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}}+{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes D)\circ\Delta_{\mathbb{V}}

ii)

D^{2}=0.

The set of all ruths of
(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
is denoted by
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
. An element in
\mathrm{Rep}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
is written
(\mathbb{V},D)
or simply
\mathbb{V}
. As shown in
[
45
]
, ruths can be equivalently described as actions of
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
on a graded vector space
\mathbb{V}
. More precisely, a representation up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathbb{V}
is equivalent to a linear map

(14)

\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V},

of degree one satisfying the following condition

(15)

\rho\circ(d_{\lambda}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}})+\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\rho)\circ(\Delta_{S}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}})=0.

In agreement with this perspective, a representation up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted by
(\mathbb{V},\rho)
or simply
\mathbb{V}
.

Remark 3.2
.

One can think of Definition
3.1
as a coalgebra version of the well-known fact that a Lie algebra representation is fully characterized by its Chevalley-Eilenberg differential. Also, Definition
3.1
resembles the notion of representation up to homotopy as in
[
2
]
and
[
24
]
. However, describing a representation up to homotopy as an action map
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
satisfying (
15
) is more geometric and we follow this perspective.

Example 3.3

(
Adjoint representation
)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. The
adjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the representation up to homotopy defined by the higher brackets

\ad:S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])\otimes\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]\to\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right],\quad\ad(x\otimes y):=\lambda(x\vee y).

Just as with Lie algebras and their representations, looking at ruths of a given
L_{\infty}
-algebra allows us to define two algebraic objects. Namely, its category of representations up to homotopy and the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology with coefficients in a representation up to homotopy. The former is described in what follows.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{V},\rho)
and
(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be two representations up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, respectively. A
ruth morphism

(F,f):(\mathbb{V},\rho)\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
is given by a couple of maps
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
which is an
L_{\infty}
-morphism and
f:\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{V}
a degree-preserving map satisfying

(16)

f\circ\rho^{\prime}\circ(F\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}^{\prime}})=\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes f).

In the particular case that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
and
F={\mathrm{Id}}
, we simply say that the map
f:\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{V}
is
ruth map
. One easily checks that if
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
are Lie algebras,
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
are usual representations, then Definition
3.4
recovers the known notion of morphism of Lie algebra representations.

One observes that ruth morphisms can be composed according to the next proposition, whose proof is a straightforward computation.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(\mathbb{V},\rho),(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
and
(\mathbb{V}^{\prime\prime},\rho^{\prime\prime})
be representations up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. If
(F,f):(\mathbb{V},\rho)\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
and
(F^{\prime},f^{\prime}):(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime\prime},\rho^{\prime\prime})
are ruths morphisms, then

(F^{\prime}\circ F,f\circ f^{\prime}):(\mathbb{V},\rho)\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime\prime},\rho^{\prime\prime}),

is a ruths morphism.

Given an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, its
category of representations up to homotopy
is the category
\mathcal{R}ep_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
whose objects are ruths of
\mathfrak{g}
and morphisms are defined as ruth maps.

One observes that an
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
yields a pullback
F^{*}:\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
. If
(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
is a ruth, the
pullback ruth

(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},F^{*}\rho^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
is defined as

(17)

F^{*}\rho^{\prime}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\quad F^{*}\rho^{\prime}:=\rho^{\prime}\circ(F\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}^{\prime}}).

Remark 3.6
.

Let
(\mathbb{V},\rho)
and
(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be representations up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, respectively. Any ruth morphism
(F,f):(\mathbb{V},\rho)\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
factors through a pair of ruth morphisms
(F,{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}^{\prime}}):(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},F^{*}\rho^{\prime})\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
and
({\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}},f):(\mathbb{V},\rho)\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},F^{*}\rho^{\prime})
. That is,
(F,f)=({\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}},f)\circ(F,{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}^{\prime}})
.

We proceed now to recall the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra together with a representation up to homotopy. For details, see
[
20
]
and
[
45
]
and the references therein. Let
(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
\mathbb{V}
a graded vector space. Consider the graded vector space

(18)

C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}):=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),

where
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})=\underline{\Hom}^{p}(S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),\mathbb{V})
. In other words
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}):=\underline{\Hom}(S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),\mathbb{V})
is the graded vector space of graded linear maps on
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
with values in
\mathbb{V}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{g}
has an associated coproduct
\Delta_{S}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
as in (
5
) and induced degree 1 coderivation
d_{\lambda}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
as in (
8
).

A representation up to homotopy
(\mathbb{V},\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
determines a cochain complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D_{\rho})
where the differential
D_{\rho}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
is given by

(19)

D_{\rho}\alpha:=\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{p}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda},

for every homogeneous element
\alpha\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
.

Definition 3.7
.

The cohomology
H(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
of the complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D_{\rho})
is called the
L_{\infty}
- algebra cohomology
of
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{V}
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra and
\mathbb{V}
is a usual representation, then the cochain complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D_{\rho})
coincides with the usual Chevalley-Eilenberg complex associated to a Lie algebra representation. In particular, the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of a Lie algebra is a particular instance of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology with coefficients in a ruth. For more details see
[
45
]
.

The
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology with coefficients is functorial as stated in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(F,f):(\mathbb{V},\rho)\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be a morphism between ruths of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
. The pullback map
F^{*}:C(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathbb{V}^{\prime})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V});\alpha\mapsto F^{*}\alpha:=f\circ\alpha\circ F
is a cochain map and hence induces a well-defined map
F^{*}:H(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathbb{V}^{\prime})\to H(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let us see that
F^{*}D_{\rho^{\prime}}=D_{\rho}F^{*}
. Indeed, if
\alpha
is a homogeneous element of degree
p
in
C(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W})
, then

\displaystyle F^{*}(D_{\rho^{\prime}}\alpha)=

\displaystyle f\circ(\rho^{\prime}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\left[1\right])}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{p}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda^{\prime}})\circ F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\rho^{\prime}({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}[1])}\otimes\alpha)(F\otimes F)\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{p}f\circ\alpha\circ F\circ d

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\rho^{\prime}(F\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}})({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}[1])}\otimes\alpha)({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}[1])}\otimes F)\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{p}(F^{*}\alpha)d

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes f)({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes F)\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{p}(F^{*}\alpha)d,\quad\text{by equation (\ref{eq:ruthmorphism}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes(F^{*}\alpha))\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{p}(F^{*}\alpha)d

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\rho}(F^{*}\alpha).

∎

3.2.
Tensor products

Recall that given graded vector spaces
\mathbb{V}_{1},\mathbb{V}_{2}
, the
twisting map
is defined by

(20)

T:\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{1};v\otimes w\mapsto(-1)^{|v||w|}w\otimes v.

The following result shows that representations up to homotopy have a natural tensor product operation.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
(\mathbb{V}_{1},\rho_{1}),(\mathbb{V}_{2},\rho_{2})\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
. The tensor product
\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}
inherits the structure of a representation up to homotopy given by

\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}:S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])\otimes(\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2})\to\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2},\quad\rho:=\rho_{1}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}_{2}}+({\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}_{1}}\otimes\rho_{2})\circ(T\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}_{2}})

where
T:S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])\otimes\mathbb{V}_{1}\to\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])
is the twisting map. More concretely, on homogeneous elements
x\in S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),v\in\mathbb{V}_{1}
and
w\in\mathbb{V}_{2}
one has

(21)

\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}(x\otimes v\otimes w)=\rho_{1}(x\otimes v)\otimes w+(-1)^{(|x|+1)|v|}v\otimes\rho_{2}(x\otimes w).

The proof of Theorem
3.9
is a straightforward but long computation which can be found in
[
Herrera
]
. The
tensor product ruth
of
(\mathbb{V}_{1},\rho_{1})
and
(\mathbb{V}_{2},\rho_{2})
is denoted by
(\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2},\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})
.

In particular, if
n\geq 2
and
(\mathbb{V},\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
then the
n^{th}
-tensor power
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
inherits the structure of a ruth defined as follows:

(22)

\rho^{\otimes n}:=\rho\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}^{\otimes n-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{n}({\mathrm{Id}}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\rho\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}^{\otimes n-k})\circ T^{k},

where
T^{k}:\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}\to\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n};k\geq 2
, is the degree preserving map

(23)

T^{k}:=({\mathrm{Id}}^{\otimes k-2}\otimes T\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}^{\otimes n-k})\circ T^{k-1},

and
T:\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V},v\otimes w\mapsto(-1)^{|v||w|}w\otimes v
is the twisting map. More concretely, on homogeneous elements
x\in S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
and
v_{1},...,v_{n}\in\mathbb{V}
one has

T^{k}(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})=(-1)^{|v_{1}|(\sum_{j=2}^{k}|v_{j}|)}v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes v_{1}\otimes v_{k-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}

and

\displaystyle\rho^{\otimes n}(x\otimes v_{1}\otimes

\displaystyle\cdots\otimes v_{n})=\rho(x\otimes v_{1})\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=2}^{n}(-1)^{(|x|+1)(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}|v_{j}|)}v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k-1}\otimes\rho(x\otimes v_{k})\otimes v_{k+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}.

3.3.
Cochain products

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
\mathbb{V}_{i};i=1,2,3
graded vector spaces. A degree preserving map
m:\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{3}
yields a degree zero linear map

(24)

\wedge_{m}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{1})\otimes C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{2})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{3}),

defined on homogeneous elements
\alpha\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{1})
and
\beta\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{2})
by

(25)

\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta:=m\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ\Delta_{S}.

The element
\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta\in C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{3})
is called the
cochain product
between
\alpha
and
\beta

along
m
.

We say that
m:\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{3}
is
symmetric
if
m=m\circ T
. Similarly, if
m=-m\circ T
one says that
m
is
skew-symmetric
. The following can be verified by a straightforward computation.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
m:\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{3}
a degree zero map with induced cochain product
\wedge_{m}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{1})\otimes C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{2})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{3})
. Then
\wedge_{m}
is symmetric (resp. skew-symmetric) if
m
is symmetric (resp. skew-symmetric).

Assume now that
(\mathbb{V}_{i},\rho_{i})\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g});i=1,2,3
are representations up to homotopy. It is reasonable to require
m:\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{3}
to be an equivariant map, that is

(26)

m\circ(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})=\rho_{3}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes m),

where
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
denotes the tensor product ruth on
\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}
as in Theorem
3.9
. In this case, the following Leibniz identity holds.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
(\mathbb{V}_{i},\rho_{i})\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
ruths with induced differentials
D_{\rho_{i}}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{i})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{i})
as in (
19
). If
m:\mathbb{V}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{3}
is equivariant, then

(27)

D_{\rho_{3}}(\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta)=(D_{\rho_{1}}\alpha)\wedge_{m}\beta+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\wedge_{m}(D_{\rho_{2}}\beta),

for every homogeneus elements
\alpha\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{1})
and
\beta\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{2})
.

Proof.

We start by computing the left hand side of (
27
). If
\alpha\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{1})
and
\beta\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{2})
are homogeneous, then using the definition of
D_{\rho_{i}}
together with the interchange law (
3
), one has

\displaystyle D_{\rho_{3}}(\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta)=

\displaystyle\rho_{3}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta)\circ\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta\circ d

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{3}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes(m\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ\Delta_{S}))\circ\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}m\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ\Delta_{S}\circ d

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{3}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes m)\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha\otimes\beta)\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\Delta_{S})\circ\Delta_{S}-

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}m\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes d+d\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])})\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\circ\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha\otimes\beta)\circ\Delta_{S}^{2}-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}m\circ(\alpha\otimes(\beta\circ d))\circ\Delta_{S}+

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}m\circ(-1)^{|\beta|}((\alpha\circ d)\otimes\beta)\circ\Delta_{S})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A+B

where

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle m\circ\big((\rho_{1}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha))\otimes\beta+({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\rho_{2})\circ(T\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])})\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha\otimes\beta)\big)\circ\Delta_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle m\circ(-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\alpha\otimes(\beta\circ d)-(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\alpha\circ d)\otimes\beta)\circ\Delta_{S}.

Since the coproduct is coassociative and cocommutative
\Delta_{S}^{2}=(\Delta_{S}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{s})\circ\Delta_{S}=({\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\Delta_{S})\circ\Delta_{S}
and
(T\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{s})\circ\Delta_{S}^{2}=\Delta_{S}^{2}
, one concludes

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle m\circ((\rho_{1}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\alpha))\otimes\beta\circ(\Delta_{S}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{s})\circ\Delta_{S}+({\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\rho_{2})\circ(\alpha\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\beta)\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\Delta_{S})\circ\Delta_{S})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\circ(((\rho_{1}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{S})\otimes\beta)\circ\Delta_{S}+(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\alpha\otimes(\rho_{2}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\beta)\circ\Delta_{S}))\circ\Delta_{S})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\circ(((\rho_{1}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{S})\otimes\beta)+(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\alpha\otimes(\rho_{2}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{s}\otimes\beta)\circ\Delta_{S})))\circ\Delta_{S}.

Now, one can easily see that

\displaystyle D_{\rho_{3}}(\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta)=

\displaystyle A+B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\rho_{1}}\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\wedge_{m}D_{\rho_{2}}\beta,

as claimed.
∎

It follows from Proposition
3.11
that the product (
24
) descends to a map in cohomology

(28)

\displaystyle\wedge_{m}:H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{1})\otimes

\displaystyle H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{2})\to H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{3})

\displaystyle[\alpha]\otimes

\displaystyle[\beta]\mapsto[\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta].

The following special instance of Proposition
3.11
will be relevant in section
4
. Let
\mathbb{V}
be a graded algebra with product
m:\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
and
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
is a representation up to homotopy. Using (
21
) one has that
m:\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
is a ruth map if and only if for every homogeneous elements
x\in S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
and
v,w\in\mathbb{V}
the following holds

(29)

(\rho\otimes\rho)(x\otimes m(v\otimes w))=m(\rho(x\otimes v)\otimes w)+(-1)^{(|x|+1)|v|}m(v\otimes\rho(x\otimes w)).

In other words, property (
29
) is equivalent to saying that
\mathfrak{g}
acts on
(\mathbb{V},m)
by graded derivations.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
(\mathbb{V},m)
a graded algebra. If
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
is an action by derivations, then
(H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),\wedge_{m})
is a graded algebra where
\wedge_{m}
is defined by (
28
)

(30)

\displaystyle\wedge_{m}:H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\otimes

\displaystyle H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})

\displaystyle[\alpha]\otimes

\displaystyle[\beta]\mapsto[\alpha\wedge_{m}\beta].

Let us see some examples of induced cochain products.

Example 3.13
.

Let
(\mathbb{V},\rho)
be a representation up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathbb{V}
. The identity map
{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}}:\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}
determines a cochain product map

C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\otimes C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}).

In this case, the cochain product of
\alpha,\beta\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
is denoted by
\alpha\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}\beta
. Note that this cochain product is neither symmetric nor skew-symmetric.

Example 3.14
.

Consider now a graded vector space
\mathbb{V}
. The evaluation map is given by

(31)

ev:\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V})\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V};T\otimes v\mapsto T(v).

Clearly (
31
) has degree zero, so it induces a product on cochains
\wedge_{ev}:C(\mathfrak{g},\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
.

Example 3.15
.

Let
(L,\left[\cdot,\cdot\right])
be a graded Lie algebra. That is,
L=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}
is a graded vector space and
\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]:L\otimes L\to L
is a degree zero linear map which is skew-symmetric and satisfies the graded Jacobi identity:

\left[x,\left[y,z\right]\right]=\left[y,\left[x,z\right]\right]+(-1)^{rs}\left[y,\left[x,z\right]\right].

If
\mathfrak{g}
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, the bracket
\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]:L\otimes L\to L
defines a cochain product
\wedge_{[\cdot,\cdot]}:C(\mathfrak{g},L)\otimes C(\mathfrak{g},L)\to C(\mathfrak{g},L)
which is skew-symmetric.

In particular, if
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],d)
is a dgla (see Example
2.3
), that is, the linear map
d:L\to L
has degree +1, it squares to zero
d^{2}=0
and

d(\left[x,y\right])=\left[dx,y\right]+(-1)^{|x|}\left[x,dy\right],

then the cochain product
\wedge_{[\cdot,\cdot]}:C(\mathfrak{g},L)\otimes C(\mathfrak{g},L)\to C(\mathfrak{g},L)
is well-defined too. As shown in Corollary
3.12
, if the space of coefficients of a ruth is a graded algebra, then such an additional structure descends to cohomology. The next result shows that if the space of coefficients is a dgla, then the corresponding space of
cochains
inherits the structure of a dgla.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with induced degree 1 coderivation
d_{\lambda}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
as in (
8
). Assume that
(L,\left[\cdot,\cdot\right])
is a graded Lie algebra, then
(C(\mathfrak{g},L),\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]})
is a graded Lie algebra. Moreover, if
(L,\left[\cdot,\cdot\right],d)
is a dgla, then
(C(\mathfrak{g},L),\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]},\textbf{d})
is dgla with differential given by

\textbf{d}(\alpha):=d\circ\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}

for every homogeneous element
\alpha\in C(\mathfrak{g},L)
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
3.10
that the product
\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}
is skew-symmetric, we will see that it also satisfies the Jacobi identity. For that, let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in C(\mathfrak{g},L)
be homogeneous elements, then:

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}(\beta\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\gamma)=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(\alpha\otimes\beta\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\gamma)\circ\Delta_{S}=\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(\alpha\otimes(\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\beta\otimes\gamma\circ\Delta))\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ({\mathrm{Id}}\otimes\left[\cdot,\cdot\right])\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\otimes\gamma\circ({\mathrm{Id}}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\otimes{\mathrm{Id}})\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\otimes\gamma\circ\Delta^{2}+\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ({\mathrm{Id}}\otimes\left[\cdot,\cdot\right])\circ(T\otimes{\mathrm{Id}})\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\otimes\gamma\circ\Delta^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\beta)\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\gamma+(-1)^{|\alpha||\beta|}\beta\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}(\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\gamma).

Assume now that
(L,\left[\cdot,\cdot\right],d)
is dgla. We will show that the map
d
is derivation of the product
\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}
with
\textbf{d}^{2}=0.
The derivation property follows by a direct computation on homogeneous elements
\alpha,\beta\in C(\mathfrak{g},L)
. In fact,

\displaystyle\textbf{d}(\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\beta)=

\displaystyle d\circ\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\beta-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\beta\circ d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\circ\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ\Delta-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ\Delta\circ d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(d\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}+{\mathrm{Id}}\otimes d)\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ\Delta-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ(d_{\lambda}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}+{\mathrm{Id}}\otimes d_{\lambda})\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(d\circ\alpha)\otimes\beta\circ\Delta+\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\otimes(d\circ\beta)\circ\Delta-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(\alpha\circ d_{\lambda})\otimes\beta\circ\Delta

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\otimes(\beta\circ d_{\lambda})\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(d\circ\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda})\otimes\beta\circ\Delta+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\otimes(d\circ\beta-(-1)^{|\beta|}\beta\circ d_{\lambda})\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{d}\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\beta+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\textbf{d}\beta

As
d^{2}=0
and
d^{2}_{\lambda}=0
, it follows that
\textbf{d}^{2}=0
as well.
∎

As for any dgla, elements of degree 1 has a well defined curvature.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],d)
a dgla. Let
C(\mathfrak{g},L)
be the dgla of Proposition
3.16
. If
\theta\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},L)
is an element of degree 1, its
curvature
is the element
\Omega_{\theta}\in C^{2}(\mathfrak{g},L)
defined by

\Omega_{\theta}=\textbf{d}\theta+\frac{1}{2}\theta\wedge_{[\cdot,\cdot]}\theta.

A
Maurer-Cartan
element in the dgla
C(\mathfrak{g},L)
is a homogeneous element
\theta\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},L)
whose curvature vanishes. That is,

\textbf{d}\theta+\frac{1}{2}\theta\wedge_{[\cdot,\cdot]}\theta=0.

The set of Maurer-Cartan elements in
C(\mathfrak{g},L)
is denoted
MC(C(\mathfrak{g},L))
.

Example 3.18
.

Every cochain complex
(\partial:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V})
has an associated dgla
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}):=\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V})
with bracket defined as

\left[S,T\right]=S\circ T-(-1)^{|S||T|}T\circ S,

and differential
\delta:\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V})\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V})
given by

\delta(T):=\left[\partial,T\right]=\partial\circ T-(-1)^{|T|}T\circ\partial.

In particular, if
(\mathfrak{g},\lambda)
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, then
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
is a dgla whose differential is given by

(32)

\bf{d}\alpha=[\partial,\alpha]-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda},

for every homogeneous element
\alpha\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
.

Definition 3.19
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
\mathbb{V}
a graded vector space. A cochain
\alpha\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
is called
reduced
if
\alpha(1)=0
. The set of reduced cochains is denoted by
\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
.

Recall that the symmetric coalgebra is coaugmented with
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])=\mathbb{R}\oplus\overline{S}(\mathfrak{g}[1])
, where

\overline{S}(\mathfrak{g}[1])=\oplus_{k\geq 1}S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}[1]).

The natural inclusion
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(\overline{S}(\mathfrak{g}[1]),\mathbb{V})\hookrightarrow C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
has image given by the space of reduced cochains. From now on we use the identification
\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(\overline{S}(\mathfrak{g}[1]),\mathbb{V})
.

Now, if
(\mathbb{V},\partial)
is a cochain complex, then the dgla
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V})
of Example
3.18
induces a dgla structure on
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
as in Proposition
3.16
. One observes that this dgla structure restricts to reduced cochains, yielding a dgla
\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
. It turns out that Maurer-Cartan elements in the dgla
\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
are precisely representations up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathbb{V}
. We proceed now to explain this relation and its consequences.

3.3.1.
Maurer-Cartan elements and ruths

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. If
\mathbb{V}
is a graded vector space, there is an isomorphism
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V},\mathbb{V})\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]),\underline{\mathrm{End}}\mathbb{V})
and hence a degree one map
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
is equivalent to having a degree one map
\partial:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V};v\mapsto\rho(1\otimes v)
, together with a degree one map
\overline{\rho}:\overline{S}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V})
. We refer to
\partial\in\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V})_{1}
and
\overline{\rho}\in\overline{C}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V}))
as the
components
of
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
with respect to the natural coagmentation of
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
.

As shown in
[
45
]
,
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
is a representation up to homotopy if and only if
\partial^{2}=0
and

(33)

\left[\partial,-\right]\circ\bar{\rho}+\bar{\rho}\circ d_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\bar{\rho}\otimes\bar{\rho}\circ\bar{\Delta}_{s}=0.

One immediately observes that (
33
) reads
\Omega_{\overline{\rho}}=0
where
\Omega_{\overline{\rho}}
is the curvature of
\overline{\rho}
as defined in
3.17
. In other words, ruths of
\mathfrak{g}
on the cochain complex
(\partial:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V})
are just Maurer-Cartan elements in the dgla
\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
.

Let us consider now the categories
L_{\infty}
-Alg and DGLA, respectively. Given a cochain complex
(\mathbb{V},\partial)
, there is a well-defined map on objects

(34)

\displaystyle L_{\infty}\text{-Alg}

\displaystyle\to\text{DGLA}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\mapsto\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))

We will show that this map is actually a functor. For that, we need some preliminary results about homotopies between
L_{\infty}
-morphisms.

Remark 3.20
.

A well-known result of Kontsevich
[
30
]
says that given an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
there exists an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism
G:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that the
L_{\infty}
-morphisms
F\circ G:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
and
G\circ F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
induce the identity map between the underlying cohomologies
H(\mathfrak{h},\partial_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
H(\mathfrak{g},\partial_{\mathfrak{g}})
, respectively. We refer to
G:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
as an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-inverse
of
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
.

We recall now the notion of homotopy between
L_{\infty}
-morphisms. Let
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}
be
L_{\infty}
-algebras with underlying differential graded coalgebras
(S(\mathfrak{g}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(S(\mathfrak{h}[1]),d_{\mathfrak{h}})
, respectively. Since there is no risk of confusion, we denote both the coproducts in
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
and
S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
simply by
\Delta_{S}
.

A
homotopy
between
L_{\infty}
-morphisms
F,G:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
is a degree -1 map
H:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
which satisfies

(35)

F-G=d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ H+H\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}}.

In this case we say that
F
and
G
are
homotopic
and we write
F\sim G
.

Given
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism, there exists an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism
G:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that
F\circ G\sim{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
G\circ F\sim{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. This was shown by Kajiura
[
29
]
in the case of
A_{\infty}
-morphisms. However, as mentioned in
[
29
]
, the very same proof and techniques apply to the setting of
L_{\infty}
-morphisms. Kajiura’s proof relies on the minimal model of an
A_{\infty}
-algebra, whose analogue for an
L_{\infty}
-algebra is given by Kontsevich decomposition of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra into minimal and contractible components
[
30
]
.

Remark 3.21
.

In what follows we use that any
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
has a quasi-inverse
G:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
and there exist homotopies

(36)

G\circ F-{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}=d_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ H+H\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}};\quad\text{and}\quad F\circ G-{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{h}}=d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ H^{\prime}+H^{\prime}\circ d_{\mathfrak{h}},

We are ready to show that (
34
) is actually a functor.

Theorem 3.22
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be two
L_{\infty}
-algebras. An
L_{\infty}
-morphism
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
induces a DGLA-morphism

F^{*}:\overline{C}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))\to\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V})),\quad F^{*}\alpha:=\alpha\circ F.

If
F
is an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism then
F^{*}
is a quasi-isomorphism of dgla’s.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\overline{C}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
be two homogeneous elements. Then

\displaystyle F^{*}[\alpha,\beta]=F^{*}(\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\beta)=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ\Delta_{S}\circ F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\otimes\beta\circ F\otimes F\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]\circ(\alpha\circ F)\otimes(\beta\circ F)\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{*}\alpha\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}F^{*}\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[F^{*}\alpha,F^{*}\beta]

showing that
F^{*}
preserves graded Lie brackets. Regarding the compatibility with the differentials, it follows from (
32
) that:

\displaystyle F^{*}(\textbf{d}\alpha)=

\displaystyle(\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda^{\mathfrak{h}}})\circ F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ(\alpha\circ F)-(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\alpha\circ F)\circ d_{\lambda^{\mathfrak{g}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ F^{*}\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}F^{*}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda^{\mathfrak{g}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{d}(F^{*}\alpha).

Hence,
F^{*}
is a morphism of dgla’s. Assume now that
F
is an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism and consider an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-inverse
G:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
together with homotopies as in (
36
). For every homogeneous element
\alpha\in\overline{C}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
one has

\displaystyle(F\circ G-{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{h}})^{*}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\alpha\circ(F\circ G-{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\circ(d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ H^{\prime}+H^{\prime}\circ d_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\circ H^{\prime}+\alpha\circ d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ H^{\prime}+(-1)^{|\alpha|}\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ\alpha\circ H^{\prime}+\alpha\circ H^{\prime}\circ d_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|\alpha|}H^{\prime*}(\textbf{d}\alpha)+(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ H^{\prime*}\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|-1}H^{\prime*}\alpha\circ d_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\textbf{d}(H^{\prime*}\alpha)-H^{\prime*}(\textbf{d}\alpha)).

As a consequence, in cohomology the map
G^{*}:\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))\to\overline{C}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{V}))
yields a left inverse of
F^{*}
. Similarly,
G^{*}
is a right inverse of
F^{*}
in cohomology. This shows that
F^{*}
is an isomorphism in cohomology, hence a quasi-isomorphism of dgla’s.
∎

3.4.
Invariance of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology

In this subsection we show that the
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology with coefficients in a representation up to homotopy is invariant under quasi-isomorphisms of ruths.

3.4.1.
Reduced complex

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra and
\mathbb{V}
a graded vector space. Let
\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
denote the set of reduced cochains.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
be a degree one map with associated components
\partial:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
and
\overline{\rho}:\overline{S}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V})
. If
\alpha\in\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
then
D_{\rho}\alpha\in\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
. Also, the codifferential
D_{\rho}
decomposes as

D_{\rho}\alpha=\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha+\partial\circ\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle D_{\rho}\alpha(1)=

\displaystyle\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{S}(1)-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)(1\otimes 1)-0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(1\otimes\alpha(1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial(\alpha(1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Consider now an element
x\in\overline{S}(\mathfrak{g}[1])
, then

\displaystyle D_{\rho}\alpha(x)=

\displaystyle\rho({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)(\overline{\Delta}(x)+1\otimes x+x\otimes 1)-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha(d_{\lambda}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)\overline{\Delta}(x)+\rho({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)(1\otimes x)+\rho({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)(x\otimes 1)-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha(d_{\lambda}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)\overline{\Delta}(x)+\partial(\alpha(x))+(-1)^{|\alpha||x|}\overline{\rho}_{x}(\alpha(1))-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha(d_{\lambda}(x))

and since
\overline{\rho}(1\otimes\alpha)\overline{\Delta}=\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha
, one concludes

D_{\rho}\alpha=\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha+\partial\circ\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}.

∎

Due to Lemma
3.23
the operator
D_{\rho}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
restricts to an operator
D_{\overline{\rho}}:\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
on reduced cochains and

(37)

D_{\overline{\rho}}\alpha=\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha+\partial\circ\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}.

If
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}
is not a representation up to homotopy, then
\overline{\rho}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\underline{\mathrm{End}}(\mathbb{V}))
is not a Maurer-Cartan element. However, one has the following result which is the analogue of the classical expression for the square of the covariant derivative of a linear connection.

Proposition 3.24
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
be a reduced cochain. Then the following holds

D_{\overline{\rho}}^{2}(\alpha)=\Omega_{\overline{\rho}}\wedge_{ev}(\alpha).

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
be a homogeneous element. It follows from (
37
) that

\displaystyle D^{2}_{\overline{\rho}}\alpha=

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}(D_{\overline{\rho}}\alpha)+\partial\circ(D_{\overline{\rho}}\alpha)-(-1)^{|\alpha|+1}D_{\overline{\rho}}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}(\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha)+\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}(\partial\circ\alpha)+\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}(-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda})+\partial\circ(\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha)+\partial\circ\partial\circ\alpha+

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\partial\circ\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}-(-1)^{|\alpha|+1}\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}-(-1)^{|\alpha|+1}\partial\circ\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}-(-1)^{|\alpha|+1}((-1)^{|\alpha|+1}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}\circ d_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A+B+C

where
A=\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}(\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha)
,
B=\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}(\partial\circ\alpha)+\partial\circ(\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha)
and
C=-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}(\alpha\circ d_{\lambda})+(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\overline{\rho}\wedge_{ev}\alpha)\circ d_{\lambda}
. Here we have used the identities
\partial^{2}=0
and
d^{2}_{\lambda}=0
.

A simple computation yields:

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\overline{\rho}\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\overline{\rho})\wedge_{ev}\alpha

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle\left(\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ\overline{\rho}\right)\wedge_{ev}\alpha

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle(\overline{\rho}\circ d_{\lambda})\wedge_{ev}\alpha

which together imply

\displaystyle D_{\overline{\rho}}^{2}\alpha=

\displaystyle(\frac{1}{2}\overline{\rho}\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\overline{\rho})\wedge_{ev}\alpha+\left(\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ\overline{\rho}\right)\wedge_{ev}\alpha+(\overline{\rho}\circ d)\wedge_{ev}\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\overline{\rho}\wedge_{\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]}\overline{\rho}+\left[\partial,\cdot\right]\circ\overline{\rho}+\overline{\rho}\circ d\right)\wedge_{ev}\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{\overline{\rho}}\wedge_{ev}\alpha

as desired.

∎

In particular, a representation up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
on a cochain complex
(\mathbb{V},\partial)
yields a cochain complex
(\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D_{\overline{\rho}})
called the
reduced complex
.

3.4.2.
The Canonical Spectral Sequence

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
L_{\infty}
algebra. The symmetric coalgebra
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
comes equipped with its primitive filtration

S_{\left[0\right]}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])=0,\quad S_{\left[1\right]}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])=\mathbb{R},\quad S_{\left[p\right]}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])=\bigoplus_{n<p}S^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),

for every
p>1
, which is clearly ascending and bounded below. Also, It is well-known that the primitive filtration is exhaustive and Hausdorff, that is

\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{\left[n\right]}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])=S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),\quad\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{\left[n\right]}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])=0,

respectively. It is easy to see that the degree one coderivation
d:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
induced by the
L_{\infty}
-structure preserves the filtration. Additionally, if
(\mathbb{V},\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
is a representation up to homotopy, then the cochain complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D_{\rho})
inherits a descending filtration defined by

F^{p}C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}):=\left\{\alpha:S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])\to\mathbb{V}\,|\,\alpha|_{S_{\left[p\right]}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])}=0\right\},

for every
p\geq 0
. As a consequence of Lemma
3.23
, one immediately observes that

D_{\rho}(F^{p}C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}))\subseteq F^{p}C^{\bullet+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}).

It is clear that
F^{0}C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})=C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
is given by the whole complex while
F^{1}C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})=\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
coincides with the complex of reduced cochains. In general, one has

\cdots F^{p+1}C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\subseteq F^{p}C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\overline{C}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\subseteq C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),

so the filtration is exhaustive. Also, one can see that this filtration is complete since

C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})/F^{n}C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\simeq\Hom(S_{[n]}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),\mathbb{V}),

which implies

\lim_{\longleftarrow}\Hom(S_{[n]}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),\mathbb{V})\simeq\Hom(S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),\mathbb{V})=C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}).

As a consequence, we have the following result about the spectral sequence associated to this filtration.

Proposition 3.25
.

The spectral sequence associated to the filtration
F_{\bullet}C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
weakly converges and

E^{p,q}_{1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{V})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{V}),

for every
p,q>0
.

Proof.

The descending filtration above is exhaustive and complete then the result follows by Theorem 10 Section 5.5 in
[
52
]
.

∎

The spectral sequence associated to the filtration
F_{\bullet}C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
of the complex
C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
starts with

E^{p,q}_{0}=F^{p}C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})/F^{p+1}C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\simeq\Hom^{p+q}(S^{p}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),\mathbb{V})

and
d_{0}:E^{p,q}_{0}\to E^{p,q+1}_{0}
is given by
\bar{D_{\rho}}
, for
\alpha\in\Hom^{p+q}(S^{p}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]),\mathbb{V})
we have

(38)

d_{0}\alpha=\partial\alpha-(-1)^{p+q}\alpha{d_{1}^{1}}^{\otimes},

where
{d_{1}^{1}}^{\otimes}
is the coderivation induced by
\lambda_{1}
. Explicitly for
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right],1\leq i\leq p
holds that

\displaystyle d_{0}\alpha(x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{p})=

\displaystyle\partial(\alpha(x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{p}))-\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(1,p-1)}(-1)^{p+q}\epsilon(\sigma)\alpha(\lambda_{1}(x_{\sigma(1)})\vee x_{\sigma(2)}\cdots\vee x_{\sigma(p)}).

We proceed now to show that the
L_{\infty}
-cohomology with coefficients in a ruth is invariant by ruth quasi-isomorphisms.

Definition 3.26
.

Let
(\mathbb{V},\rho)
and
(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be representations up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, respectively. A morphism
(F,f):(\mathbb{V},\rho)\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
of representations up to homotopy is called a
quasi-isomorphism
if
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism and
f:\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{V}
is a quasi-isomorphism between the underlying cochain complexes.

Let
F,G:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
be
L_{\infty}
-morphisms. A coderivation
along F and G
is a homogeneous map
D:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
satisfying

(39)

\Delta_{S}\circ D=(F\otimes D+D\otimes G)\circ\Delta_{S},

where by abuse of notation we denote
\Delta_{S}
the coproduct in
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
and
S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
. The
iterated coproduct
is the map
\Delta^{n}_{S}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])^{\otimes n}
given by
\Delta^{n}_{S}=(\Delta_{S}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}^{\otimes(n-1)})\circ\Delta_{S}
for
n\geq 1
and
\Delta^{0}_{S}:={\mathrm{Id}}
. A straightforward computation shows that if
D:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
is a coderivation along
F
and
G
, then the following holds:

(40)

\Delta^{n}_{S}\circ D=\big(\sum_{i+j=n}F^{\otimes i}\otimes D\otimes G^{\otimes j}\big)\circ\Delta^{n}_{S}.

Let
F:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
be an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism with a quasi-inverse
G:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}

[
30
]
. As observed in Remark
3.21
there are homotopies
H:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
and
H^{\prime}:S(\mathfrak{h}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{h}[1])
satisfying equations (
36
), see
[
29
]
. The construction of the previous homotopies shows that
H
is actually a coderivation along
G\circ F
and
{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. In particular, it follows from (
40
) that
H
preserves the primitive filtration of
S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
. A similar result holds for
H^{\prime}
.

Now we are ready to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.27
.

Let
(\mathbb{V},\rho)
and
(\mathbb{W},\tau)
be representations up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
, respectively. If
(F,f):(\mathbb{V},\rho)\to(\mathbb{W},\tau)
is a ruth quasi-isomorphism, then

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{*}:C(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})}

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

Note that as
F
is a coalgebra morphism it preserves the primitive filtration on
S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])
and
S(\mathfrak{h}\left[1\right])
then
F^{*}
preserves the filtration on the cochain complexes
C_{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W})
and
C_{\rho}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
. Thus,
F^{*}
induces a morphism between spectral sequences. Now we shall see that

F^{*}_{1}:E_{1}^{p,q}(\mathfrak{h};\mathbb{W})\to E_{1}^{p,q}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{V})

is a isomorphism. For this we take a
L_{\infty}
-homotopy inverse
T
for
F
, it exists by
[
29
, Thm.7.5]
. Note that by equation (
40
) a coderivation preserves the primitive filtration, then in particular for a homotopy coderivation
H
of
S(\mathfrak{h}\left[1\right])
the equation

F\circ T-{\mathrm{Id}}=Hd+dH

holds on
S^{p}(\mathfrak{h}\left[1\right])
for all
p\geq 1
. On the other hand, one has that for
p,q>0
the map
F_{0}^{*}:E_{0}^{p,q}(\mathfrak{h};\mathbb{W})\to E_{0}^{p,q}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{V})
depends only on
F_{1}^{1}
and
f
. Indeed, given
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right],1\leq i\leq p
and
\alpha\in E_{0}^{p,q}(\mathfrak{h};\mathbb{W})
, the following holds

F_{0}^{*}\alpha(x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{p})=f\alpha(F_{1}^{1}(x_{1})\vee F_{1}^{1}(x_{2})\vee\cdots\vee F_{1}^{1}(x_{p})).

Hence, if
t
is a homotopy inverse for
f
, then the map
(T,t):\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
although it is not a ruths morphism, it yields a well-defined map

T_{0}^{*}:E_{0}^{p,q}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{V})\to E_{0}^{p,q}(\mathfrak{h};\mathbb{W}),

for every
p,q>0
. Now it is straightforward verify that
T_{0}^{*}
is a homotopy left inverse for
F_{0}^{*},

\displaystyle T_{0}^{*}F_{0}^{*}\alpha=

\displaystyle({\mathrm{Id}}+h\partial+\partial h)\alpha({\mathrm{Id}}+H{d_{1}^{1}}^{\otimes}+{d_{1}^{1}}^{\otimes}H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha+d_{0}\left(h\alpha+h\alpha H{d_{1}^{1}}^{\otimes}+(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\alpha H+h\partial\alpha H)\right)

In a similar way we can see that
T_{0}^{*}
is a homotopy right inverse of
F_{0}^{*}
. Hence,
F_{1}^{*}
is an isomorphism, and therefore by the Eilenberg-Moore Comparison Theorem
[
52
, Thm 5.5.11]
we conclude that

H(F^{*}):H(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W})\to H(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})

is an isomorphism.
∎

The
L_{\infty}
-algebra cohomology is an invariant in the derived category of representations up to homotopy of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. This is the content of the following result, whose proof is straightforward from Theorem
3.27
.

Corollary 3.28
.

Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathfrak{h}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
be quasi-isomorphisms of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. Let
(\mathbb{V},\rho)
and
(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be ruths of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, respectively. If
g:\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{V}
is a quasi-isomorphism of complexes such that
(G,g):(\mathbb{V},F^{*}\rho)\to(\mathbb{V}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
is a quasi-isomorphism of ruths, then

H(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\cong H(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathbb{V}^{\prime}).

4.
Extensions of
L_{\infty}
-algebras and the Chern-Weil-Lecomte map

4.1.
Extensions of
L_{\infty}
-algebras

We start by recalling the notion of extension in the category of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. For more details see
[
41
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
45
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

An
extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras
is a short exact sequence of
L_{\infty}
-algebras
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

in which
\pi
and
\iota
are strict
L_{\infty}
-morphisms. In this case we say that
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is an extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{n}
.

As
\pi
is a strict
L_{\infty}
-morphism it is determined by its projection
\pi_{1}^{1}
onto
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. More precisely
\pi=S(\pi_{1}^{1})
where the right hand side of the equality is the symmetric extension of
\pi_{1}^{1}
defined in (
11
). As a consequence, a linear section
h_{1}^{1}:\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\left[1\right]
of
\pi_{1}^{1}
induces a coalgebra morphism
h=S(h_{1}^{1})
which is clearly a linear section of
\pi
.

In this section,
\mu:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\times S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
denotes the multiplication map in the symmetric algebra of
\mathfrak{g}[1]
and
\lambda:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{g}[1]
the linear map induced by the symmetric brackets
\lambda_{k}:S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{g}[1]
defining the
L_{\infty}
-structure of
\mathfrak{g}
, see (
7
). Similarly, the corresponding maps for
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
are denoted by
\hat{\mu}
,
\hat{\lambda}
and
\hat{\lambda_{k}}
, respectively.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
be a section of an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras (
4.1
).
For every
k\geq 1
the linear map

K_{h}^{k}:=\hat{\lambda}_{k}\circ h^{\otimes k}-h\circ\lambda_{k}:\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]^{\otimes k}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\left[1\right]

has the following properties:

(1)

K_{h}^{k}
is symmetric of degree 1, and

(2)

\mathrm{Im}(K_{h}^{k})\subseteq\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right]
.

Proof.

To prove
(1)
let
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},k\geq 1
be homogeneous elements. Then

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{k}\circ h^{\otimes k}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k})=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{k}(h(x_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes h(x_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma;h(x_{1}),\dots,h(x_{n}))\hat{\lambda}_{k}(h(x_{\sigma(1)}),\otimes\cdots\otimes h(x_{\sigma(k)}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})(\hat{\lambda}_{k}\circ h^{\otimes k})(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)}).

The last identity follows from the fact that
h
is a degree-preserving map, hence
\epsilon(\sigma;h(x_{1}),\dots,h(x_{n}))=\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then

\displaystyle K_{h}^{k}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n})=

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})(\hat{\lambda}_{k}\circ h^{\otimes k})(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)})

\displaystyle-h(\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\lambda_{k}(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})(\hat{\lambda}_{k}\circ h^{\otimes k}-h\circ\lambda_{k})(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})K_{h}^{k}(x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)}).

which shows that
K_{h}^{k}
is symmetric. As both
\lambda_{k}
and
\hat{\lambda}_{k}
have degree one and
h
has degree zero, then
K_{h}^{k}
has degree 1.

In order to show
(2)
, note that since
\pi:\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a strict
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism, then
\pi_{1}^{1}\circ\hat{\lambda}_{k}=\lambda_{k}\circ(\pi_{1}^{1})^{\otimes k}
. Hence

\displaystyle\pi_{1}^{1}(K_{h}^{k})=

\displaystyle\pi_{1}^{1}\circ\hat{\lambda}_{k}\circ h^{\otimes k}-\pi_{1}^{1}\circ h\circ\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\circ(\pi_{1}^{1})^{\otimes k}\circ h^{\otimes k}-\pi_{1}^{1}\circ h\circ\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\circ(\pi_{1}^{1}\circ h)^{\otimes k}-(\pi_{1}^{1}\circ h)\circ\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where we have used the interchange law (
3
) and the fact that
\pi_{1}^{1}\circ h={\mathrm{Id}}
.
Then, by exactness of the sequence, one has

\mathrm{Im}(K_{h}^{k})\subseteq\text{Ker}(\pi_{1}^{1})=\text{Im}(\iota)=\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right],

as desired.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
4.2
, the family of maps
K_{h}^{k}:S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])\to\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right];k\geq 1
defines a degree one linear map

(41)

K_{h}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}K_{h}^{k}:S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])\to\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right].

Definition 4.3
.

Let
h:\mathfrak{g}[1]\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1]
be a section of an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras as in
4.1
. The element
K_{h}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
is called the
curvature
of
h
. If
K_{h}=0
, then section
h
is called
flat
.

Remark 4.4
.

The curvature
K_{h}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
measures the failure of
h:\mathfrak{g}[1]\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1]
being an
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism, since
K_{h}=\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h)-h\circ\lambda
. In other words, a section
h:\mathfrak{g}[1]\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1]
is flat if and only if it is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right],\lambda)
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. A graded subspace
\mathfrak{h}\left[1\right]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}[1]
is an
ideal
if it is invariant under the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, see Example
3.3
. That is,

\lambda(x\vee y)\in\mathfrak{h}\left[1\right],

for every
x\in\mathfrak{h}\left[1\right]
and
y\in S(\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right])
.

Example 4.5
.

Given an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras as in
4.1
, the space
\text{Ker}(\pi_{1}^{1})=\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right]
is an ideal of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\left[1\right]
, since for
x\in\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right]
and
y\in S(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\left[1\right])
we have

\displaystyle\pi_{1}^{1}(\hat{\lambda}(x\vee y))=

\displaystyle\lambda(\pi_{1}^{1}(x)\vee\pi(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(0\vee\pi(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Hence,
\hat{\lambda}(x\vee y)\in\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right]
.

It follows from example
4.5
that the adjoint representation up to homotopy
\ad:S(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1])\otimes\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1]\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1]
restricts to a ruth
\ad|_{\mathfrak{n}}:S(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1])\otimes\mathfrak{n}[1]\to\mathfrak{n}[1]
. If
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is a section of (
4.1
) with zero curvature, then by applying the pullback functor
h^{*}:\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
defined in (
17
) one gets a ruth

(42)

\rho:=h^{*}(\ad|_{\mathfrak{n}}):S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathfrak{n}[1]\to\mathfrak{n}[1].

In particular, the
L_{\infty}
-algebra cochain complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1]),D_{\rho})
is well defined and
D^{2}_{\rho}=0
, see (
19
).

In general, given a section
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
its curvature does not necessarily vanish, hence
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is not an
L_{\infty}
-morphism in general. In spite of this, one can still consider the map
\rho=h^{*}(\ad|_{\mathfrak{n}})
as in (
42
) but it does not define a representation up to homotopy. In particular, the degree 1 map
D_{\rho}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
does not square to zero.

The maps
\rho:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathfrak{n}[1]\to\mathfrak{n}[1]
and
D_{\rho}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
are given by

(43)

\rho=\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h)\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{n}[1]});

and

(44)

D_{\rho}\alpha:=\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{p}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda},

for every homogeneous cochain
\alpha\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
.

We have the following version of the Bianchi identity.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
be a section of an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras (
4.1
) with curvature
K_{h}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
. Then, the following holds

D_{\rho}K_{h}=0.

Proof.

The proof is a long, but straightforward computation:

\displaystyle D_{\rho}K_{h}=

\displaystyle\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes K_{h})\circ\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{1}K_{h}\circ d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes(\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h))\circ\Delta_{S}-\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes h\circ\lambda)\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle+(\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h))\circ d_{\lambda}-(h\circ\lambda)\circ d_{\lambda}.

Since
\lambda\circ d_{\lambda}=0
, we conclude that
D_{\rho}K_{h}=A-B+C
, where

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes(\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h)))\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes(h\circ\lambda))\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h)\circ d_{\lambda}.

We proceed now to show that
A=0
. Indeed, since
\rho:=\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h)\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{n}[1]})
is defined by (
43
), one has

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h)\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{n}[1]})\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes(\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h)))\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h)\otimes(\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h))\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\hat{\lambda})\circ(S(h)\otimes S(h))\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\hat{\lambda})\circ(\Delta_{S}\otimes S(h))

where we have used the interchange law (
3
) twice and the fact that
S(h)
is a coalgebra morphism. But
\hat{\mu}=\hat{\mu}\circ T
and
\Delta_{S}=T\circ\Delta_{S}
, where
T
is the twisting map (
20
), so replacing these expressions above and using
d_{\hat{\lambda}}=\hat{\mu}\circ(\hat{\lambda}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1])})\circ\Delta_{S}
(see (
8
)), one concludes

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ{T\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1])}\otimes\hat{\lambda})\circ T}\circ\Delta_{S}\otimes S(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(\hat{\lambda}\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1])})\circ\Delta_{S}\circ S(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ d_{\hat{\lambda}}\circ S(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where we used
\hat{\lambda}\circ d_{\hat{\lambda}}=0
.

Regarding the term
B
, one can use the definition of
\rho
as above, which yields

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h)\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{n}[1]})\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes(h\circ\lambda))\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h)\otimes(h\circ\lambda))\circ\Delta_{S},

where we have used the interchange law (
3
).

Finally, we compute the term
C
. For that, using the definition of the coderivation
d_{\lambda}=\mu\circ(\lambda\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])})\circ\Delta_{S}
as in (
8
), we have

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h)\circ\mu\circ(\lambda\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])})\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h)\otimes S(h))\circ(\lambda\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])})\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ((S(h)\circ\lambda)\otimes S(h))\circ\Delta_{S}

where the interchange law was used in the last identity. Again, because
\hat{\mu}=\hat{\mu}\circ T
and
\Delta_{S}=T\circ\Delta_{S}
, where
T
is the twisting map (
20
), one gets

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ((S(h)\circ\lambda)\otimes S(h))\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ T\circ((h\circ\lambda)\otimes S(h))\circ T\circ\Delta_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h)\otimes(h\circ\lambda))\circ\Delta_{S},

showing that
B=C
. Hence,

\displaystyle D_{\rho}K_{h}=

\displaystyle A-B+C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

∎

Note that if
h_{0},h_{1}:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
are sections of (
4.1
), then
h_{1}-h_{0}\in\underline{\Hom}(\mathfrak{g}[1],\mathfrak{n}[1])
. In other words, the space of sections of (
4.1
) is an affine space. In particular, there is a one-parameter family of sections
h_{t}:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
defined as

h_{t}:=h_{0}+t(h_{1}-h_{0}),t\in[0,1].

Such a family of sections induces maps
\rho_{t}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\mathfrak{n}[1]\to\mathfrak{n}[1]
and
D_{\rho_{t}}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
given by (
43
) and (
44
), respectively. Let
K_{h_{t}}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
be the curvature of
h_{t}
. One has the cochain
\alpha\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
defined by

(45)

\alpha:=(h_{1}-h_{0})\circ\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}[1]},

where
\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}[1]}:S(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\mathfrak{g}[1]
is the canonical projection. It turns out that
\alpha
controls the variation of the one-parameter family of curvatures
K_{h_{t}}
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
h_{t}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}
be a one-parameter family of sections given by
h_{t}=h_{0}+t(h_{1}-h_{0}),t\in[0,1]
. Then

\frac{d}{dt}K_{h_{t}}=D_{\rho_{t}}\alpha,

with
\alpha\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
as in (
45
).

Proof.

For every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the variation
h_{t+\epsilon}=h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha
and its curvature
K_{h_{t+\epsilon}}
. Note that

\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}K_{h_{t+\epsilon}}=\frac{d}{dt}K_{h_{t}},

for every
t\in[0,1]
. We proceed now to compute the curvature
K_{h_{t+\epsilon}}
. If
c_{t,\epsilon}:=S(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)-S(h_{t})-S(\epsilon\alpha)
, then:

\displaystyle K_{h_{t+\epsilon}}=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h_{t+\epsilon})-h_{t+\epsilon}\circ\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ S(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)-(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)\circ\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ(S(h_{t})+S(\epsilon\alpha)+c_{t,\epsilon})-h_{t}\circ\lambda-\epsilon\alpha\circ\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{h_{t}}+(\hat{\lambda}\circ S(\epsilon\alpha)-\epsilon\alpha\circ\lambda)+\hat{\lambda}\circ c_{t,\epsilon}.

Since
\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}K_{h_{t}}=0
, we conclude

\frac{d}{dt}K_{h_{t}}=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}K_{h_{t+\epsilon}}=A+B,

where
A=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}(\hat{\lambda}\circ S(\epsilon\alpha)-\epsilon\alpha\circ\lambda)
and
B=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\hat{\lambda}\circ c_{t,\epsilon}.

Let us compute the term
A
in the expression above.

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\hat{\lambda}_{k}\circ t_{0}^{k}S^{k}(\alpha)-\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\epsilon\alpha\circ\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{1}\circ S^{1}(\alpha)\circ\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}[1]}-\alpha\circ\lambda,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{1}\circ\alpha-\alpha\circ\lambda,

Regarding the term
B
, one has

(46)

\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\hat{\lambda}\circ c_{t,\epsilon}=\sum_{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}\left(\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}c_{t,\epsilon}^{k}\right).

On the one hand

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}c_{t,\epsilon}^{k}=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}(S^{k}(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)-S^{k}(h_{t})-S^{k}(\epsilon\alpha))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}S^{k}(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)-\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}S^{k}(\epsilon\alpha),

where we have used
\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}S^{k}(h_{t})=0
. Note that

\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}S^{k}(\epsilon\alpha)=\begin{cases}S^{1}(\alpha);\quad k=1\\
0;\quad k\geq 2.\end{cases}

On the other hand, for homogeneous elements
x_{1},...,x_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}[1],
the following holds:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}S^{k}

\displaystyle(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)(x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{k})=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)x_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{t}(x_{1})\vee\cdots\vee h_{t}(x_{i-1})\vee\alpha(x_{i})\vee h_{t}(x_{i+1})\vee\cdots\vee h_{t}(x_{k}).

In other words,

\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}S^{k}(h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{t}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha\vee\cdots\vee h_{t},

and hence (
46
) reads

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\hat{\lambda}\circ c_{t,\epsilon}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{t}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha\vee\cdots\vee h_{t}\right)-\hat{\lambda}_{1}\circ\alpha\circ\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}[1]}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}K_{h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha}=

\displaystyle A+B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{1}\circ\alpha-\alpha\circ\lambda+\sum_{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{t}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha\vee\cdots\vee h_{t}\right)

\displaystyle-\hat{\lambda}_{1}\circ\alpha\circ\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}[1]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}(h_{t}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha\vee\cdots\vee h_{t})\right)-\alpha\circ\lambda.

Summing up, one has

(47)

\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}K_{h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha}=\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}(h_{t}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha\vee\cdots\vee h_{t})\right)-\alpha\circ\lambda.

The first term of the left hand side of (
47
) can be written as

(48)

\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}(h_{t}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha\vee\cdots\vee h_{t})\right)=\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h_{t})\otimes\hat{\alpha})\circ\Delta_{S},

where
\hat{\alpha}:=h_{1}-h_{0}
. Note that this immediately implies the statement of the proposition, namely:

\frac{d}{dt}K_{h_{t}}=D_{\rho_{t}}\alpha.

Indeed, the variation of the curvatures has the expression

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}K_{h_{t}}=\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}K_{h_{t}+\epsilon\alpha}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}(h_{t}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha\vee\cdots\vee h_{t})\right)-\alpha\circ\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{t}\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{S}-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\circ d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\rho_{t}}\alpha.

It only remains to show (
48
). To see this, recall that
\alpha=\hat{\alpha}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}[1]}
, therefore on homogeneous elements
x_{1},...,x_{k}\in S(\mathfrak{g}[1])
the following holds

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h_{t})\otimes\hat{\alpha})\Delta_{S}(x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{k})=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h_{t}))\left(\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(k-1,1)}\epsilon(\sigma)x_{\sigma(1)}\vee\cdots\vee x_{\sigma(k-1)}\otimes\alpha(x_{\sigma(k)})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(k-1,1)}\epsilon(\sigma)h_{t}(x_{\sigma(1)})\vee\cdots\vee h_{t}(x_{\sigma(k-1)})\otimes\alpha(x_{\sigma(k)})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(k-1,1)}\epsilon(\sigma)h_{t}(x_{\sigma(1)})\vee\cdots\vee h_{t}(x_{\sigma(k-1)})\vee\alpha(x_{\sigma(k)})\right).

Note that since

Sh(k-1,1)=\left\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{k}\,|\,\sigma(1)<\sigma(2)<\cdots<\sigma(k-1)\right\}

has precisely
k
elements, then

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}\circ\hat{\mu}\circ(S(h_{t})\otimes\hat{\alpha})\Delta_{S}(x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{k})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon(\sigma)h_{t}(x_{1})\vee\cdots\vee\alpha(x_{i})\vee\cdots h_{t}(x_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{\lambda}_{k}(h_{t}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha\vee\cdots\vee h_{t})\right)(x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{k}).

which shows (
48
).

∎

4.2.
Chern-Weil-Lecomte map

Let us consider an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

as in (
4.1
). Assume that
(\mathbb{V},\rho)
is a representation up to homotopy of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\pi:\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism, one gets a pullback ruth
(\mathbb{V},\pi^{*}\rho)
of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathbb{V}
. As we observed in Example
4.5
, the subspace
\mathfrak{n}[1]\subseteq\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1]
is an ideal and hence the adjoint ruth of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
restricts to a ruth
\ad|_{\mathfrak{n}}:S(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}[1])\otimes\mathfrak{n}[1]\to\mathfrak{n}[1]
, which extends to a ruth of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1]
for every
k\geq 1
. This induced representation is denoted
(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\wedge^{k}\ad|_{\mathfrak{n}})
for every
k\geq 1
. In this way, we end up with two ruths

(\mathbb{V},\pi^{*}\rho),(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\wedge^{k}\ad|_{\mathfrak{n}})\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}).

The corresponding space of ruths morphisms is denoted by

(49)

\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}:=\{f\in\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})\,|\,f\text{ ruth map}\}.

One easily observes that if
f\in\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
is a ruth map, then
f
is
\mathfrak{g}
-equivariant in the sense that

(50)

f\circ h^{*}(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}})=\rho\circ({\mathrm{Id}}_{S(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\otimes f),

where
h^{*}(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}}):=(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}})\circ(S(h)\otimes{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{n}[1]})
. It is worth noticing that, although
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is not an
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism, the map
h^{*}(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}})
is well defined but it is not a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Even though, the map
h^{*}(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}})
induces a degree 1 derivation
D_{h^{*}(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}})}:C(\mathfrak{g},\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1])\to C(\mathfrak{g},\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1])
which does not square to zero. In spite of this,
f:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1]\to\mathbb{V}
yields a pullback map
f^{*}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1])
and property (
50
) implies that the computations in Proposition
3.8
hold true, so

(51)

D_{\rho}\circ f^{*}=f^{*}\circ D_{h^{*}(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}})}.

We will see that elements in
\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
applied to the curvature of a section
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
of an extension (
4.1
) define
L_{\infty}
-cocycles in the cochain complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D_{\rho})
. For that, let
K_{h}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
be the curvature of a section
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
of (
4.1
). As seen in Example
3.13
, the identity map
{\mathrm{Id}}:\mathfrak{n}[1]\otimes\mathfrak{n}[1]\to\mathfrak{n}[1]\otimes\mathfrak{n}[1]
defines a cochain product
\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])\otimes C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])\to C(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1]\otimes\mathfrak{n}[1])
. In particular, when applied to the curvature one gets an element

K^{\wedge^{k}}_{h}:=K_{h}\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}\cdots\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}K_{h}\in C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1]^{\otimes k}),

for every
k\geq 1
. Now, if
f\in\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})_{\bullet}^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
, then

(52)

f_{K_{h}}:=f\circ K^{\wedge^{k}}_{h}\in C^{k+\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}).

Now we state the
L_{\infty}
-version of the Chern-Weil-Lecomte morphism, which is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
K_{h}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
be the curvature of a section
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
of an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

and
(\mathbb{V},\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
. If
f\in\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
, then

(1)

the element
f_{K_{h}}
given by (
52
) is a cocycle in the complex
(C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}),D_{\rho})
,

(2)

the cohomology class
[f_{K_{h}}]\in H^{\bullet+k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
does not depend of the section
h
.

Proof.

First we show that
f_{K_{h}}
is a cocycle, that is,
D_{\rho}f_{K_{h}}=0
. To see this, since
f:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1]\to\mathbb{V}
is a ruth map, the induced pullback
f^{*}:C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to C(\mathfrak{g},\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1])
commutes with the corresponding derivations, see (
51
). Also, by definition
f_{K_{h}}=f^{*}(\wedge^{k}K_{h})
then

D_{\rho}f_{K_{h}}=f^{*}D_{h^{*}(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}})}(\wedge^{k}K_{h}).

Using the Leibniz rule (see Prop.
3.11
) together with the Bianchi identity (see Theorem
4.6
) yields
D_{h^{*}(\ad^{\otimes k}|_{\mathfrak{n}})}(\wedge^{k}K_{h})=0
, which implies that
D_{\rho}f_{K_{h}}=0
.

Now we proceed to check
(2)
. We will show that if
h_{0},h_{1}:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
are sections, then
f_{K_{h_{1}}},f_{K_{h_{0}}}
are cohomologous. For that, consider the family of sections
h_{t}=h_{0}+t(h_{1}-h_{0})
with
t\in[0,1]
together with the corresponding cocycle
f_{K_{h_{t}}}\in C(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
as in (
52
). To see that
[f_{K_{h_{1}}}]=[f_{K_{h_{0}}}]
, it suffices to find
\beta_{t}\in C^{k-1+\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
satisfying

(53)

\frac{d}{dt}f_{K_{h_{t}}}=D_{\rho}\beta_{t}.

Indeed, if such a
\beta_{t}
exists, then

\displaystyle f_{K_{h_{1}}}-f_{K_{h_{0}}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}f_{K_{h_{t}}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}D_{\rho}\beta_{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\rho}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\beta_{t}dt\big)

We will show that (
53
) holds. In fact, by the curvature variation formula of Proposition
4.7
and the Leibniz rule in Proposition
3.11
, one has

(54)

\frac{d}{dt}f_{K_{h_{t}}}=f^{*}(\frac{d}{dt}K^{\wedge^{k}}_{h_{t}})\\
=\sum f^{*}(K_{h_{t}}\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}\cdots\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}D_{\rho_{t}}\alpha\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}\cdots\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}K_{h_{t}}).

A direct computation using the skew-symmetry of
f
gives rise to

f^{*}(K_{h_{t}}\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}\cdots\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}D_{\rho_{t}}\alpha\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}\cdots\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}K_{h_{t}})=f^{*}(D_{\rho_{t}}\alpha\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}K_{h_{t}}^{\wedge^{(k-1)}}).

As a consequence, identity (
54
) reads

(55)

\frac{d}{dt}f_{K_{h_{t}}}=kf^{*}(D_{\rho_{t}}\alpha\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}K_{h_{t}}^{\wedge^{(k-1)}}).

Define now
\beta_{t}:=kf^{*}(\alpha\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}K^{\wedge(k-1)}_{h_{t}})
. Then, using
D_{\rho}\circ f^{*}=f^{*}\circ D_{\rho_{t}}
and the Leinbiz rule for
D_{\rho_{t}}
as in Proposition (
3.11
), one gets

\displaystyle D_{\rho}\beta_{t}=

\displaystyle kf^{*}\big((D_{\rho_{t}}\alpha)\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}K^{\wedge^{(k-1)}}_{h_{t}}+\alpha\wedge_{{\mathrm{Id}}}D_{\rho_{t}}K^{\wedge^{(k-1)}}_{h_{t}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}f_{K_{h_{t}}},

where in the last identity we have used (
55
) together with the Bianchi identity as in Theorem
4.6
. This proves (
53
) and hence statement
(2)
as desired.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
4.8
, given an extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

together with a representation up to homotopy
(\mathbb{V},\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
, there is a well defined map

(56)

\displaystyle cw:\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],

\displaystyle\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}\to H^{\bullet+k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})

\displaystyle f\mapsto[f_{K_{h}}],

where
h:\mathfrak{g}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is any section. The map (
56
) is referred to as the
Chern-Weil-Lecomte
map of the extension.

4.3.
Naturality

In this section we show the naturality of the Chern-Weil-Lecomte morphism. More precisely, we will see that the pullback construction in the category of
L_{\infty}
-algebras induces a notion of pullback of extensions of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. If additionally, extensions come with representations up to homotopy, we study how the Chern-Weil-Lecomte morphism behaves with respect to these pullbacks.

As shown in
[
47
, Thm 5.9]
(see also
[
50
, Thm 2.1]
), given two
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphisms
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
and
T^{\prime}:\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{g}
one has a pullback
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{h}\times_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
which is an
L_{\infty}
-subalgebra of the direct sum
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
. We observe that this pullback construction extends to
L_{\infty}
-algebra extensions. Indeed, given
L_{\infty}
-algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{n}
, extensions (
4.1
) of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{n}
form a category
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
in which morphisms are given by commutative diagrams of
L_{\infty}
-morphisms:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Id}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Id}}}

We simply say that
\varphi:\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is a morphism of extensions
\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
. It is clear that every arrow in
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
is necessarily invertible.

Any
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
defines a pullback functor
T^{*}:\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})\to\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{n})
defined on objects by
T^{*}\hat{\mathfrak{g}}:=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
where
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}:=\mathfrak{h}\times_{\mathfrak{g}}\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the pullback defined by the maps
\pi:\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
and
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
. The pullback extension is depicted as in the next commutative diagram of
L_{\infty}
-algebras:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{h}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Id}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\bullet}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\bullet}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\bullet}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Id}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

As for any extension of
L_{\infty}
-algebras, the graded vector space
\mathfrak{n}
in (
4.3
) is an ideal of both
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
and its pullback
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
via
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
. In particular, one can see
\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1];k\geq 1
as a ruth in two different ways, namely
\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1]\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
and
\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1]\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
via the adjoint action. Additionally, if
(\mathbb{V},\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
and
(\mathbb{W},\tau)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{h})
are representations up to homotopy, then there are pullback ruths
(\mathbb{V},\pi^{*}\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathbb{W},\pi^{*}_{\bullet}\tau)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})
. It is reasonable to require
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
to be part of a ruth morphism, so we assume now that
(T,t):(\mathbb{W},\tau)\to(\mathbb{V},\rho)
is a ruths morphism, i.e.
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism and
t:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{W}
is a map of graded vector spaces satisfying condition (
16
). One can easily see that there is a well defined map

(57)

t^{\sharp}:\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}\to\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{W})^{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}};f\mapsto t\circ f

for every
k\geq 1
.

It is clear that every section
h:\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\left[1\right]
of
\pi
induces a section
\overline{h}:\mathfrak{h}[1]\to\hat{\mathfrak{h}}[1]
of the pullback extension (
4.3
), with the property:

(58)

T_{\bullet}\circ S(\overline{h})=S(h)\circ T.

In particular, for every
k\geq 1
there are Chern-Weil-Lecomte morphisms

cw:\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}\to H^{\bullet+k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\quad\text{ and }\quad cw:\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{W})^{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}\to H^{\bullet+k}(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W}),

which are related according to the next result.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
be an extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{n}
. Assume that
(\mathbb{V},\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
are
(\mathbb{W},\tau)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{h})
and
(T,t):(\mathbb{W},\tau)\to(\mathbb{V},\rho)
is a ruths morphism. Let
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{n})
be the associated pullback extension (
4.3
). Then the following diagram commutes

(59)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k+\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right],\mathbb{W})^{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k+\bullet}(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces cw}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t^{\sharp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces cw}

where
t^{\sharp}
is given by (
57
) and
T^{*}:H^{k+\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to H^{k+\bullet}(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W})
denotes the pullback map in
L_{\infty}
-cohomology.

Proof.

Let
h:\mathfrak{g}\left[1\right]\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\left[1\right]
be a linear section of
\pi:\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
with curvature
K_{h}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}[1])
. As observed above, there is an induced section
\overline{h}:\mathfrak{h}\left[1\right]\to\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\left[1\right]
of
\pi_{\bullet}:\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\to\mathfrak{h}
satisfying (
58
). Let
K_{\overline{h}}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{n}[1])
be its curvature. A simple computation shows that
T^{*}K_{h}=K_{\overline{h}}
.

For
f\in\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}[1],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}
, let us consider the cocycle
f_{K_{h}}:=f\circ K^{\wedge^{k}}_{h}\in C^{k+\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})
as in (
52
). Then:

\displaystyle T^{*}f_{K_{h}}=

\displaystyle f\circ K_{h}^{\wedge^{k}}\circ T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circ K_{h}\otimes\cdots\otimes K_{h}\circ\Delta^{k-1}\circ T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circ K_{h}\otimes\cdots\otimes K_{h}\circ T^{\otimes k}\circ\Delta^{k-1}

where in the last identity we have used that
T
is a coalgebra morphism. Due to the interchange law (
3
), one has

\displaystyle K_{h}\otimes\cdots\otimes K_{h}\circ T^{\otimes k}=

\displaystyle(K_{h}\circ T)^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(T^{*}K_{h})^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K^{\otimes k}_{\overline{h}}.

Hence we conclude

\displaystyle T^{*}f_{K_{h}}=

\displaystyle f\circ K^{\otimes k}_{\overline{h}}\circ\Delta^{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circ K_{\overline{h}}^{\wedge_{\otimes}k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{K_{\overline{h}}}.

As a consequence

\displaystyle\left(T^{*}\circ cw\right)(f)=

\displaystyle\left[T^{*}(f_{K_{h}})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[t\circ f_{K_{h}}\circ T\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[t\circ T^{*}f_{K_{h}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[t\circ f_{K_{\overline{h}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[t\circ f\circ K_{\overline{h}}^{\wedge_{\otimes}k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[t^{\sharp}(f)\circ K_{\overline{h}}^{\wedge_{\otimes}k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle cw(t^{\sharp}(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(cw\circ t^{\sharp})(f),

which shows that
T^{*}\circ cw=cw\circ t^{\sharp}
, as desired.
∎

Suppose now that
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
is an
L_{\infty}
-morphism and
(\mathbb{V},\rho)\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
. The pullback ruth
(T^{*}\rho,\mathbb{V})\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{h})
defined in (
17
) comes with a canonical ruth morphism
(T,{\mathrm{Id}}_{\mathbb{V}}):(\mathbb{V},T^{*}{\rho})\to(\mathbb{V},\rho)
. In particular, if
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g,\mathfrak{n}})
and
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{h,\mathfrak{n}})
is the pullback extension, then Theorem
4.9
gives rise to the following.

Corollary 4.10
.

If
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
is an
L_{\infty}
-quasi isomorphism, then the isomorphism
T^{*}:H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})\to H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h},T^{*}\mathbb{V})
intertwines Chern-Weil-Lecomte maps:

(60)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right],\mathbb{V})^{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k+\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{n}\left[1\right],\mathbb{W})^{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k+\bullet}(\mathfrak{h},\mathbb{W}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces cw}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Id}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces cw}

In other words, quasi-isomorphic
L_{\infty}
-algebras have the same characteristic classes defined via the Chern-Weil-Lecomte construction.

Remark 4.12
.

In the special case of extensions of semistrict Lie

5.
Chern-Weil map for principal 2-bundles

In this section we show how the Chern-Weil-Lecomte of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra extension can be applied to introduce a Chern-Weil map for principal 2-bundles over Lie groupoids.

5.1.
Principal 2-bundles over a Lie groupoid

We start by introducing some terminology that shall be used throughout the whole section. For a Lie 2-group we use the notation
\mathbb{G}=(G_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
and similarly a Lie groupoid will be denoted by
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0})
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Lie 2-group and
\mathbb{X}
be a Lie groupoid. A
right action
of
\mathbb{G}
on
\mathbb{X}
is a Lie groupoid morphism
a:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
which defines Lie group actions
a_{1}:X_{1}\times G_{1}\to X_{1}
and
a_{0}:X_{0}\times G_{0}\to X_{0}
on arrows and objects, respectively. Left actions are defined in a similar manner.

Right and left actions are denoted
\mathbb{X}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{X}
, respectively. Similarly,
xg
and
gx
denote the corresponding actions of
g\in\mathbb{G}
on
x\in\mathbb{X}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Given a right action of
\mathbb{G}
on
\mathbb{X}
, the structural maps of
\mathbb{X}
are equivariant with respect to the structural maps of
\mathbb{G}
. That is, if
g\in G_{1},g_{0}\in G_{0}
and
x\in X_{1},x_{0}\in X_{0}
, then

s_{X}(xg)=s_{X}(x)s_{G}(g),\quad t_{X}(xg)=t_{X}(x)t_{G}(g),\quad 1_{x_{0}g_{0}}=1_{x_{0}}1_{g_{0}}

and for every composable arrows
(x,y)\in X_{2}
and
(g,g^{\prime})\in G_{2}
the following interchange law is fulfilled

(64)

(x*y)(g*h)=(xg)*(yh)

where
x*y
and
g*h
denote the groupoid multiplication in
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{G}
, respectively.

Remark 5.3
.

It is worth observing that a right action
\mathbb{X}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
is not necessarily given by Lie groupoid automorphism. However, the Lie group
G_{0}
acts on
\mathbb{X}
by Lie groupoid automorphisms via
1_{g_{0}}:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}
for every
g_{0}\in G_{0}
. Also, every arrow
g\in G_{1}
defines a map
\tau_{g}:X_{0}\to X_{1};x_{0}\mapsto 1_{x_{0}}g
which is a natural isomorphism
\tau_{g}:1_{s(g)}\Rightarrow 1_{t(g)}
. Hence
\mathbb{X}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
can be seen as a 2-group morphism
\mathbb{G}\to 2\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{X})
where
2\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{X})
denotes the 2-group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{X}
.

Let us see some examples of Lie 2-group actions.

Example 5.4
.

A usual action of a Lie group
G
on a manifold
X
can be seen as an action of the Lie 2-group
G\rightrightarrows G
on the unit groupoid
X\rightrightarrows X
.

Example 5.5
.

Every Lie 2-group
\mathbb{G}
acts on itself by right translations. More generally, any Lie 2-subgroup
\mathbb{H}
of
\mathbb{G}
acts on
\mathbb{G}
by right translations.

Example 5.6
.

Any Lie 2-group
\mathbb{G}
acts on itself on the left by conjugation at both the level of arrows and the level of objects. This induces an action
\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}_{0})
on its Lie 2-algebra, called the
adjoint representation
of
\mathbb{G}
.

Example 5.7
.

A right action of
\mathbb{X}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
induces a right action
T\mathbb{X}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
on
the tangent groupoid

T\mathbb{X}=(TX_{1}\rightrightarrows TX_{0})
given by the tangent lift of the action of
G_{i}
on
X_{i}
, for
i=0,1
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0})
and
\mathbb{E}=(E_{1}\rightrightarrows E_{0})
be two Lie groupoids. A Lie groupoid morphism
\pi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{X}
is said to be a
fibration
if it satisfies the following conditions

(1)

the base map
\pi_{0}:E_{0}\to X_{0}
is a surjective submersion, and

(2)

the map
\tilde{\pi}:E_{1}\to X_{1}{}_{s}\kern-0.5pt{\times}_{\pi_{0}}E_{0},e\mapsto(\pi_{1}(e),s_{E}(e))
is a surjective submersion.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{X}
be a groupoid fibration together with a right action
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
. We say that the action is
principal along
\pi
if

(65)

\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{P}\times_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{P};(p,g)\mapsto(p,pg)

is a Lie groupoid isomorphism. In this case, we say that
\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{X}
is a
principal
\mathbb{G}
-2-bundle
.

Our main motivation to consider principal 2-bundles as in Definition
5.9
comes from the study of principal actions on differentiable stacks as in
[
12
]
, where principal actions of stacky Lie groupoids are studied. Since every Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1}\to X_{0})
defines a quotient stack
[X_{0}/X_{1}]

[
6
]
, principal 2-bundles
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
in the sense of Definition
5.9
define principal actions of the stacky Lie group
[G_{0}/G_{1}]
along the stack morphism
[P_{0}/P_{1}]\to[X_{0}/X_{1}]
induced by
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{X}
. In
[
12
]
, a stacky Lie group is not necessarily strict and actions are weak. Hence, the notion of principal 2-bundle considered here can be seen as a strict version of a stacky principal bundle as in
[
12
]
. Several notions of principal 2-bundles have been studied by different authors, in
[
53
]
weak 2-groups act in a principal manner along a map
\mathcal{P}\to M
between smooth 2-spaces. Here the base

is a manifold seen as a smooth 2-space. Similarly, in
[
51
]
, Waldorf studies principal 2-bundles as in Definition
5.9
with
\mathbb{X}
being the unit Lie groupoid of a manifold, however principality is required in a weaker sense. Also, principal 2-bundles as in Definition
5.9
are studied in
[
14
]
.

Example 5.10

(

)
.

Let

.

Example 5.11

(
Principal 2-bundles over manifolds
)
.

A
principal 2-bundle over a manifold
is a principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to(X\rightrightarrows X)
where the base space is the unit groupoid of a manifold
X
. A more general notion of principal 2-bundle over a manifold was introduced in
[
53
]
, where principality is defined in a weak sense, i.e. the map (
65
) is a Morita map rather than an isomorphism.

Example 5.12

(
Principal bundles over Lie groupoids
)
.

Let
\pi_{0}:P_{0}\to X_{0}
be a principal
G
-bundle with a left action of a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0})
along
\pi_{0}
which commutes with the right action of
G
. The transformation groupoid
\mathbb{P}:=X_{1}\ltimes P_{0}\rightrightarrows P_{0}
is a principal
\mathbb{G}
-2-bundle over
\mathbb{X}
with
\mathbb{G}=(G\rightrightarrows G)
defined as the unit groupoid of
G
. These kind of principal bundles are studied in
[
35
]
.

Example 5.13

(
Homogeneous Lie groupoids
)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Lie 2-group and
\mathbb{H}
a Lie 2-subgroup such that
H_{i}\subseteq G_{i}
is a closed subgroup for
i=0,1
. Then
\mathbb{G}
is a principal
\mathbb{H}
-2-bundle over the Lie groupoid,
G_{1}/H_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0}/H_{0}
.

Example 5.14

(
Pullbacks
)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{Y}\to\mathbb{X}
be a Lie groupoid morphism. If
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
is a principal 2-bundle, then the pullback bundles
\phi^{*}_{1}P_{1}\to Y_{1}
and
\phi^{*}_{0}P_{0}\to X_{0}
define a principal 2-bundle
\phi^{*}\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{Y}
called the
pullback
2-bundle of
\mathbb{P}
by
\phi:\mathbb{Y}\to\mathbb{X}
.

Example 5.15

(
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbes
)
.

Every surjective submersion
q:Y\to X
defines a Lie groupoid
Y\times_{X}Y\to Y
called the
submersion groupoid
. The nerve of the submersion groupoid yields a simplicial manifold
(Y_{n})_{n}
whose face maps are denoted by
d_{i}:Y_{n}\to Y_{n-1},i=0,1,...,n
. An
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe
over a manifold
X
is given by a triple
\mathcal{G}=(Y,P,\mu)
where
q:Y\to X
is a surjective submersion,
\pi:P\to Y\times_{X}Y
is a principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle and
\mu:d^{*}_{0}P\otimes d^{*}_{2}P\to d^{*}_{1}P
is a bundle isomorphism over
Y_{3}
called the
gerbe multiplication
, satisfying

d^{*}_{1}\mu\circ(d^{*}_{3}\mu\otimes{\mathrm{Id}})=d^{*}_{2}\mu\circ({\mathrm{Id}}\otimes d^{*}_{0}\mu),

over
Y_{4}
. One can see that an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe determines a principal 2-bundle with 2-group
\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightrightarrows\{*\}
. Indeed, there is a natural Lie groupoid
P\rightrightarrows Y
with source and target maps
s:=d_{0}\circ\pi
,
t:=d_{1}\circ\pi
and multiplication
m:P\times_{Y}P\to P
defined by

\mu((x,y,z),p_{1}\otimes p_{2}):=((x,y,z),m(p_{1},p_{2})).

One can see that the action of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
on
P
defines an action of the Lie 2-group
\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightrightarrows\{*\}
on
P\rightrightarrows Y
, making
\mathbb{P}:=(P\rightrightarrows Y)
a principal 2-bundle over the submersion groupoid
Y\times_{X}Y\to Y
. We refer to
\mathbb{P}\to Y\times_{X}Y
as the
principal 2-bundle underlying
the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe
\mathcal{G}
.

5.2.
Equivariant VB-groupoids and LA-groupoids

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0})
be a Lie groupoid. A
VB-groupoid

\pi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{X}
is a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{E}=(E_{1}\rightrightarrows E_{0})
where
E_{1}\to X_{1}
and
E_{0}\to X_{0}
are vector bundles and all the structural maps of
\mathbb{E}
are vector bundle morphisms over the corresponding structural maps of
\mathbb{X}
. An
LA-groupoid
is a VB-groupoid
\pi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{X}
where
E_{1}\to X_{1}
and
E_{0}\to X_{0}
are Lie algebroids and all the structural maps are Lie algebroid morphisms. See
[
39
]
for more details.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Lie 2-group. A VB-groupoid
\pi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{X}
is called
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant
if there is a left action
\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{E}
by vector bundle automorphisms. As a consequence,
E_{1}\to X_{1}
and
E_{0}\to X_{0}
are
G_{1}
-equivariant and
G_{0}
-equivariant, respectively. It follows from Remark
5.3
that
G_{0}\curvearrowright\mathbb{E}
is an action by VB-groupoid automorphisms. In a similar manner, one can introduce
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant LA-groupoids and in this case
G_{0}\curvearrowright\mathbb{E}
is an action by LA-groupoid automorphisms.

Example 5.16
.

Let
\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{X}
be a left action. Then the tangent LA-groupoid
T\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}
is
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant with the action defined in Example
5.7
.

Example 5.17
.

Let
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
be a principal 2-bundle. Let
\mathbb{g}=(\mathfrak{g_{1}}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g_{0}})
be the Lie 2-algebra of
\mathbb{G}
equipped with the adjoint representation of Example
5.6
. The trivial VB-groupoid
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}\to\mathbb{P}
is
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant. Viewing
P_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to P_{1}
and
P_{0}\times\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to P_{0}
as bundles of Lie algebras, then
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}\to\mathbb{P}
is actually a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant LA-groupoid.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{X}
be a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant VB-groupoid. If
G_{1}\curvearrowright E_{1}
and
G_{0}\curvearrowright E_{0}
are principal actions, then
G_{1}\curvearrowright X_{1}
and
G_{0}\curvearrowright X_{0}
are principal as well and both
E_{1}/G_{1}\to X_{1}/G_{1}
and
E_{0}/G_{0}\to X_{0}/G_{0}
are vector bundles. It is easy to see that in this case, all groupoid structures also descend to the quotient, yielding a quotient VB-groupoid
\overline{\pi}:\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}/\mathbb{G}
. A similar result holds for
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant LA-groupoids.

The following examples of quotients LA-groupoids associated to
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant ones are particularly important in this work. See also
[
14
]
, where both examples are discussed.

Example 5.18
.

Let us consider the
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant LA-groupoid
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}\to\mathbb{P}
of Example
5.17
. The corresponding quotient LA-groupoid is denoted
\Ad(\mathbb{P})\to\mathbb{X}
and referred to as the
adjoint LA-groupoid
of
\mathbb{P}
.

Example 5.19
.

Let
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}

5.3.
Connections on principal 2-bundles

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0})
be a Lie groupoid and consider its nerve
N\mathbb{X}=(X_{n})
with its usual structure of simplicial manifold. Here
X_{n}
denotes the space of
n
-simplices, which are strings of arrows
(x_{1},...,x_{n})
with
s(x_{i})=t(x_{i+1}),i=1,...,n-1
and face maps
d_{i}:X_{n+1}\to X_{n};i=1,...,n+1
. The simplicial structure gives rise to a complex
\delta:\Omega^{\bullet}(X_{n})\to\Omega^{\bullet}(X_{n+1})
defined as the alternating sum of pullbacks by the face maps. This differential does not change the form degree, but it increases the simplicial degree by +1. For instance,
\delta\eta=t^{*}\eta-s^{*}\eta
for every
\eta\in\Omega^{k}(X_{0})
and
\delta\omega=pr^{*}_{1}\omega-m^{*}\omega+pr^{*}_{2}\omega
if
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(X_{1})
, where
m,pr_{1},pr_{2}:X_{1}\times_{X_{0}}X_{1}\to X_{1}
denote the groupoid multiplication and the canonical projections, respectively.

The
Bott-Shulman-Stasheff complex
of
\mathbb{X}
is the double complex
\Omega^{\bullet}(X_{\bullet})
with vertical differential given by the usual de Rham differential of forms and horizontal differential defined by the simplicial structure. The
de Rham complex
of
\mathbb{X}
is the total complex:

C^{p}_{dR}(\mathbb{X}):=\bigoplus_{i+j=p}\Omega^{i}(X_{j}),

with differential
D\omega:=d\omega+(-1)^{j+1}\delta\omega
for every
\omega\in\Omega^{i}(X_{j})
.

A
multiplicative
k
-form
on
\mathbb{X}
is a
k
-form
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(X_{1})
which satisfies
\delta\omega=0
. That is,

(69)

m^{*}\omega=pr^{*}_{1}\omega+pr^{*}_{2}\omega.

As shown in
[
10
,
11
]
, a
k
-form
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(X_{1})
is multiplicative if and only if the induced bundle map
\omega:\oplus^{k-1}T\mathbb{X}\to T^{*}\mathbb{X}
is a Lie groupoid morphism.

Let
\eta\in\Omega^{k+1}_{cl}(X_{0})
be a closed form. We say that a multiplicative form
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(X_{1})
is
closed
with respect to
\eta
if
d\omega=t^{*}\eta-s^{*}\eta
. In other words, the element
(\omega+\eta)\in C^{k+1}_{dR}(\mathbb{X})
is a cocycle in the de Rham complex of
\mathbb{X}
.

Remark 5.26
.

A multiplicative
k
-form on
\mathbb{X}
should be thought of as a
k
-form of degree 1 on the quotient stack
[X_{0}/X_{1}]
since it yields a morphism of complexes
\bigoplus^{k-1}T_{\mathbb{X}}\to T^{*}_{\mathbb{X}}[1]
, where
T_{\mathbb{X}}:=(A_{\mathbb{X}}\to TX_{0})
is the tangent complex and
T^{*}_{\mathbb{X}}:=(T^{*}X_{0}\to A^{*}_{\mathbb{X}})
is the cotangent complex. Similarly, a multiplicative
k
-form on
\mathbb{X}
which is closed with respect to
\eta\in\Omega^{k+1}_{cl}(X_{0})
has to be thought of as a closed
k
-form on the quotient stack
[X_{0}/X_{1}]
. The special case of 2-forms arises in the context of Dirac structures
[
9
]
as well as in shifted symplectic geometry
[
44
]
, see also
[
13
]
.

A more general notion of multiplicative form with values in a VB-groupoid is considered in
[

]
. We are interested in the particular case of
\mathbb{g}
-valued multiplicative

-forms on a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
where
\mathbb{g}=(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}_{0})
is a Lie 2-algebra.

.

A
\mathbb{g}
-valued multiplicative

-form
on a Lie groupoid

is a

-form

such that

\omega_{0}:\oplus^{k}TX_{0}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}\times X_{0}
where
\omega_{0}\in\Omega^{k}(X_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0})
.

If

is the cochain complex associated with
\mathbb{g}
, then a
\mathbb{g}
-valued multiplicative
k
-form on
\mathbb{X}
is given by a pair of
k
-forms
\bar{\omega}\in\Omega^{k}(X_{1},\mathfrak{h})
and
\omega_{0}\in\Omega^{k}(X_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0})
with

(70)

\omega_{1}=\bar{\omega}+s^{*}\omega_{0},

and satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle m^{*}\bar{\omega}=

\displaystyle pr^{*}_{1}\bar{\omega}+pr^{*}_{2}\bar{\omega}

\displaystyle\partial\circ\bar{\omega}=

\displaystyle t^{*}\omega_{0}-s^{*}\omega_{0}.

For more details, see
[
22
]
.

Definition 5.28
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0})
be a Lie groupoid and
\eta\in\Omega^{k+1}_{cl}(X_{0},\mathfrak{h})
be a closed form. We say that a
\mathbb{g}
-valued multiplicative
k
-form
\omega_{1}=(\bar{\omega},s^{*}\omega_{0})

\displaystyle d\omega_{0}=

\displaystyle\partial\circ\eta

\displaystyle d\bar{\omega}=

\displaystyle(t^{*}\eta-s^{*}\eta)

Remark 5.29
.

A
\mathbb{g}
-valued multiplicative

should be thought of as a

. A similar interpretation holds for

Just as multiplicative

-cocycles in the de Rham complex of a Lie groupoid, closed

[

Let
\mathbb{g}
be a Lie 2-algebra with associated 2-term cochain complex
\partial:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}

\partial_{k}:\mathfrak{v}_{k}\to\mathfrak{v}_{k+1}
where
\partial_{-1}:=\partial:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{v}_{k}=0
for every

. Let
\mathbb{X}=(X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0})
be a Lie groupoid and consider the simplicial manifold given by the nerve

C^{p}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{g}):=\bigoplus_{i+j+k=p}\Omega^{i}(X_{j},\mathfrak{v}_{k}),

with differential

is given by a triple of differential forms:

\Phi^{a}\in\Omega^{p}(X_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0});\quad\Phi^{b}\in\Omega^{p}(X_{1},\mathfrak{h});\quad\Phi^{c}\in\Omega^{p+1}(X_{0},\mathfrak{h}),

such that

(73)

\displaystyle m^{*}\Phi^{b}=

\displaystyle pr^{*}_{1}\Phi^{b}+pr^{*}_{2}\Phi^{b}

\displaystyle\partial\circ\Phi^{b}=

\displaystyle t^{*}\Phi^{a}-s^{*}\Phi^{a}.

The component
\Phi^{c}
controls the differential of
\Phi
. More concretely, the differential
\mathbb{D}\Phi\in C^{p+1}_{dR}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{g})
is given by the triple
((\mathbb{D}\Phi)^{a},(\mathbb{D}\Phi)^{b},(\mathbb{D}\Phi)^{c})
with

(74)

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}\Phi)^{a}=

\displaystyle d\Phi^{a}-(-1)^{p}\partial\circ\Phi^{c}

(75)

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}\Phi)^{b}=

\displaystyle d\Phi^{b}-(-1)^{p}\delta_{0}\circ\Phi^{c}

(76)

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}\Phi)^{c}=

\displaystyle d\Phi^{c}

Remark 5.30
.

Note that

5.4.
The curvature of a 2-connection

Our main goal now is to define the curvature of a 2-connection. For that, we start by fixing the notation in the case of ordinary principal bundles.

Let
P\curvearrowleft G\to X
be a principal bundle with connection
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(P,\mathfrak{g})
. Let
\Omega(P,V)
be the space of
V
-valued differential forms on
P
where
V
is a vector space. The
covariant derivative
of
\omega\in\Omega(P,V)
is defined as
\nabla^{\theta}\omega:=(d\omega)^{h}
, the horizontal part of the usual exterior derivative. The
curvature
of a connection
\theta
is the 2-form
\Omega:=\nabla^{\theta}\theta\in\Omega^{2}(P,\mathfrak{g})
. The curvature 2-form can be written as

(78)

\Omega=d\theta+\frac{1}{2}[\theta,\theta].

If
\rho:G\to GL(V)
a representation, we say that
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(P,V)
is
basic
if it is horizontal and invariant in the sense that
R^{*}_{g}\omega=\rho_{g^{-1}}\omega
for every
g\in G
. We denote by
\Omega^{k}_{\rho}(P,V)
the space of basic
k
-forms on
P
with values in
V
. The space
\Omega^{k}_{\rho}(P,V)
identifies canonically with
\Omega^{k}(X,P\times_{G}V)
, the space of
k
-forms on
X
with values in the associated bundle
P\times_{G}V\to X
. The curvature of a connection is basic with respect to the adjoint representation, hence it can be seen as a 2-form on
X
with values in the adjoint bundle
\Ad(P)
.

For basic forms
\omega\in\Omega^{k}_{\rho}(P,V)
the covariant derivative has the following explicit formula:

(79)

\nabla^{\theta}\omega=d\omega+\rho_{*}(\theta\wedge\omega).

Here
\rho_{*}(\theta\wedge\omega)\in\Omega^{k+1}(P,V)
is defined as

\rho_{*}(\theta\wedge\omega)(U_{1},...,U_{k+1})=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{k+1}}(-1)^{\sigma}\rho_{*}(\theta(U_{\sigma(1)}))\omega(U_{\sigma(2)},...,U_{\sigma(k+1)}),

where
\rho_{*}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is the induced Lie algebra representation.

We proceed now to define the curvature of a 2-connection on a principal 2-bundle.

Definition 5.36
.

Let
\theta=\bar{\theta}+s^{*}\theta_{0}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})
be a 2-connection on a principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
. The
curvature
of
\theta
is the 2-form
\Omega:=d\theta+\frac{1}{2}[\theta,\theta]\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})
.

Remark 5.37
.

It is reasonable to define the curvature of a 2-connection
\theta=\bar{\theta}+s^{*}\theta_{0}
by looking at the expression (
78
) of each of its components
\bar{\theta}
and
\theta_{0}
. However, the 2-form
d\bar{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}[\bar{\theta},\bar{\theta}]\in\Omega^{2}(P_{1},\mathfrak{h})
fails to be multiplicative, hence it does not provide a good notion of curvature in the setting of principal bundles over Lie groupoids.

Note that the curvature
\Omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})
as in Definition
5.36
is a multiplicative 2-form. This follows immediately from the expression
\Omega=(d\theta)^{h}
combined with the fact that
d\theta
is multiplicative and the horizontal projection
TP\to H:=\Ker(\theta)
is a VB-groupoid morphism. As a consequence, one can write the curvature as

(80)

\Omega=\bar{\Omega}+s^{*}\Omega_{0},

where
\bar{\Omega}\in\Omega^{2}(P_{1},\mathfrak{h})
and
\Omega_{0}\in\Omega^{2}(P_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0})
. More concretely, it follows from Definition
5.36
that
\Omega_{0}=d\theta_{0}+\frac{1}{2}[\theta_{0},\theta_{0}]
is just the curvature of
\theta_{0}
, and

\bar{\Omega}=d\bar{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}[\bar{\theta},\bar{\theta}]+[s^{*}\theta_{0},\bar{\theta}].

Now we describe
\bar{\Omega}
in (
80
) in terms of the covariant derivative. For that, recall that the source map
P_{1}\to P_{0}
is a morphism of principal bundles with connections, hence the corresponding covariant derivatives are related by
\nabla^{\theta}\circ s^{*}=s^{*}\circ\nabla^{\theta_{0}}
. Since
\Omega=\nabla^{\theta}\theta
and
\theta=\bar{\theta}+s^{*}\theta_{0}
, one has

\displaystyle\Omega=

\displaystyle\nabla^{\theta}\bar{\theta}+s^{*}\nabla^{\theta_{0}}\theta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{\theta}\bar{\theta}+s^{*}\Omega_{0}.

The uniqueness of the decomposition (
80
) implies
\bar{\Omega}:=\nabla^{\theta}\bar{\theta}
. In particular, as a consequence of Bianchi identity
\nabla^{\theta}\Omega=0
, one has the following Bianchi identity for
\bar{\Omega}
:

(81)

\nabla^{\theta}\bar{\Omega}=0.

Remark 5.38
.

Let
\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})
be the curvature of a 2-connection
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})
. Since
\Omega=\bar{\Omega}+s^{*}\Omega_{0}
is basic with respect to
\pi:\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
, there exists a unique
multiplicative
2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{X},\Ad(\mathbb{P}))
with
\pi^{*}\omega=\Omega
. As shown in
[
22
]
, a VB-groupoid valued form is completely determined by its core and side components, which in the case of
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{X},\Ad(\mathbb{P}))
such a components are
\bar{\omega}\in\Omega^{2}(X_{1},t^{*}(P_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathfrak{h}))
and
\omega_{0}\in\Omega^{2}(X_{0},P_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathfrak{g}_{0})
, and

\omega=\bar{\omega}+s^{*}\omega_{0},

Since
\pi^{*}\omega=\Omega
, then
\pi^{*}_{0}\omega_{0}=\Omega_{0}
and
\pi^{*}_{1}\bar{\omega}=\bar{\Omega}
. Also, due to Bianchi identity (
81
)
\nabla^{\theta}\bar{\Omega}=0
, one has that
\nabla_{0}\bar{\omega}=0
where
\nabla_{0}
is a covariant exterior derivative defined by the induced connection on
t^{*}(P_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathfrak{h})\to X_{1}
.

It is useful to consider 2-connections with prescribed curvature. This is formalized by introducing the notion of a curving 2-form. Let
G_{0}\to H
be the crossed module associated to the Lie 2-group
\mathbb{G}
. One of the components of the crossed module is a Lie group morphism
\alpha:G_{0}\to\Aut(H)
which yields a Lie group representation
\tau:G_{0}\to GL(\mathfrak{h})
. In particular, one can look at basic forms
\Omega_{\tau}(P_{0},\mathfrak{h})
with respect to the representation
\tau
.

Definition 5.39
.

Let
\theta=\bar{\theta}+s^{*}\theta_{0}
be a 2-connection on a principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
with curvature
\Omega=\bar{\Omega}+s^{*}\Omega_{0}
. A
curving
for
\theta
is a basic 2-form
B\in\Omega^{2}_{\tau}(P_{0},\mathfrak{h})
with:

(1)

\Omega_{0}+\partial\circ B=0
, and

(2)

\bar{\Omega}+(t^{*}-s^{*})(B)=0
.

A
connective structure
on a principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
is a pair
(\theta,B)
where
\theta
is a 2-connection on
\mathbb{P}
and
B
is a curving for
\theta
. The
curvature 3-form
of a connective structure
(\theta,B)
is the 3-form
\nabla^{c}_{\theta_{0}}B\in\Omega^{3}(P_{0},\mathfrak{h})
. Due to (
79
), the curvature 3-form has the following expression

(82)

\nabla^{c}_{\theta_{0}}B=dB+\tau_{*}(\theta_{0}\wedge B),

where
\tau_{*}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
is the Lie algebra representation induced by
\tau:G_{0}\to GL(\mathfrak{h})
.

The terminology is motivated by the following example.

Example 5.40
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(Y,P,\mu)
be an
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-gerbe over a manifold

(\theta,B)
on the underlying principal 2-bundle associated to

is just a connection 1-form on

and

such that
\Omega=-(d^{*}_{1}-d^{*}_{0})B
, where
\Omega
is the curvature of

[

. In this case, the curvature 3-form is

Remark 5.41
.

As observed in Example
5.40
, the curvature 3-form of a connective structure on a gerbe
\mathcal{G}=(Y,P,\mu)
is a closed 3-form on the manifold
X
, which is the base of the submersion
q:Y\to X
. Regarding
X
as the quotient stack of of the submersion groupoid
Y\times_{X}Y\rightrightarrows Y
, the curvature 3-form can be seen as a 3-form on the stack
X=[Y/Y\times_{X}Y]
. For a general principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
, the curvature 3-form of a connective structure is an element in
\Omega^{3}(P_{0},\mathfrak{h})
. However, one can see the curvature 3-form as a suitable multiplicative 3-form on
\mathbb{X}
with values in the VB-groupoid
\Ad(\mathbb{P})
, which in the case of a regular groupoid
\mathbb{X}
yields a 3-form on the quotient stack
[X_{0}/X_{1}]
with values in
\Ad(\mathbb{P})
. We will explain this below.

As shown in
[
22
]
,
k
-forms on a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{Y}
with values in a
VB
-groupoid
\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{Y}
determine a cochain complex
\delta_{\mathbb{E}}:C^{\bullet,k}(\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{E})\to C^{\bullet,k}(\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{E})
whose 1-cocycles are precisely multiplicative
k
-forms. If
C\to Y_{0}
is the core bundle of
\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{Y}
, then
C^{0,k}(\mathbb{Y},\mathbb{E}):=\Omega^{k}(Y_{0},C)
. A degree zero cocycle is called
\mathbb{Y}
-invariant
.

In the case of a principal 2-bundle
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}
and the trivial VB-groupoid
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}
, one has the complex
\delta_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}}:C^{p,2}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})\to C^{p+1,2}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})
of
\mathbb{g}
-valued 2-forms on
\mathbb{P}
whose 1-cocycles are
\mathbb{g}
-valued multiplicative
2
-forms on
\mathbb{P}
. A connective structure
\tilde{\theta}:=(\theta,B)
on
\mathbb{P}
allows us to write the curvature

\Omega=-((t^{*}B-s^{*}B+s^{*}(\partial\circ B))=\delta_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}}B

as a
multiplicatively exact
2-form with values in
\mathbb{g}
. The groupoid morphism
\pi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{X}
defines a cochain map
\pi^{*}:C^{\bullet,2}(\mathbb{X},\Ad(\mathbb{P}))\to C^{\bullet,2}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})
, hence the multiplicative 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{X},\Ad(\mathbb{P}))
described in Remark
5.38
is also multiplicatively exact. Indeed,
\omega=\delta_{\Ad(\mathbb{P})}B_{0}
where
B_{0}\in\Omega^{2}(X_{0},P_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathfrak{h})
is the unique 2-form with
\pi^{*}_{0}B_{0}=B
.

Let
\nabla^{c}_{0}:\Omega(X_{0},P_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathfrak{h})\to\Omega(X_{0},P_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathfrak{h})
the covariant exterior derivative defined by the induced connection on
P_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathfrak{h}\to X_{0}

\nabla^{c}_{0}B_{0}\in\Omega^{3}(X_{0},P_{0}\times_{G_{0}}\mathfrak{h})
is
\mathbb{X}
-invariant, that is, it is a zero cocycle in the complex

. To see this, it suffices to check that
\pi^{*}(\delta_{\Ad(\mathbb{P})}\nabla^{c}_{0}B_{0})=0
for the cochain map
\pi^{*}:C^{\bullet,3}(\mathbb{X},\Ad(\mathbb{P}))\to C^{\bullet,3}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{g})

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\delta_{\Ad(\mathbb{P})}\nabla^{c}_{0}B_{0})=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}}(\pi^{*}_{0}\nabla^{c}_{0}B_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}}(\nabla^{c}_{\theta_{0}}\pi^{*}_{0}B_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}}(\nabla^{c}_{\theta_{0}}B)

where
\nabla^{c}_{\theta_{0}}
is the induced covariant exterior derivative on
P_{0}\times\mathfrak{h}
. But
\delta_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}}(\nabla^{c}_{\theta_{0}}B)=\nabla(\delta_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}}B)=0
because of Bianchi identity and
\delta_{\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{g}}B=\Omega

[\nabla^{c}_{0}B_{0}]\in H^{0,3}(\mathbb{X},\Ad(\mathbb{P}))
is an invariant of principal 2-bundles with connective structures.

on

5.4.1.
Flatness

A connective structure
(\theta,B)
on
\mathbb{P}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{X}

5.5.

In what follows, we will state and prove the main result of this section, namely the construction of the Chern-Weil morphism for principal 2-bundles with 2-connections. This relies on the general set up of Theorem

which gives rise to characteristic classes of extensions of

-algebras together with a representation up to homotopy.

Let

.

Let

\displaystyle cw:\underline{\Hom}^{\bullet}(\wedge^{k}\mathbb{L}(\Ad(\mathbb{P}))

One observes that since

Remark 5.46
.

Suppose that

is a curving for a 2-connection

is given by

K_{h}=-(t^{*}B-s^{*}B)-\partial\circ B.

The curvature 3-form

is not necessarily zero unless the horizontal lift

Let

\Phi:\phi^{*}\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{P}

\phi:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}^{\prime}

Abstract
. We explore the notion of Santaló point for the Holmes-Thompson boundary area of a convex body in a normed space. In the case where the norm is C 1 C^{1} , and in the case where unit ball and convex body coincide, we prove existence and uniqueness. When the normed space has a smooth positively curved unit ball, we exhibit a dual Santaló point expressed as an average of centroids of projections of the dual body.

1.
introduction

Several decades ago Santaló studied in
[
Santaló 1949
]
the functional

(1.1)

x\in\operatorname{int}(K)\mapsto|(K-x)^{\circ}|

associated to any convex body
K
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
A^{\circ}=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}\leq 1\,\,\forall x\in A\}
is the polar set of a convex body
A
. He found that this functional is proper and strictly convex. Consequently, there exists a unique minimizing point
s(K)
in the interior of
K
, known nowadays as the
Santaló point
of
K
. Along the way, Santaló computed the derivative of (
1.1
) and showed that

(1.2)

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}|(K-tv)^{\circ}|=(n+1)\left\langle c(K^{\circ}),v\right\rangle

where
c(K^{\circ})=\int_{K^{\circ}}x\,\,dx
is the centroid of the polar body
K^{\circ}
and
v
any vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore
s(K)
lies at the origin if and only if
c(K^{\circ})
lies at the origin. Determining the Santaló point of a given convex body can be a difficult question, so this characterization is particularly useful.

As the Lebesgue measure coincides with the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is natural to consider the following functional similar to (
1.1
)

(1.3)

x\in\operatorname{int}(K)\mapsto\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial(K-x)^{\circ}),

where
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
denotes the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and look for possible minimizing points.

More generally, let
V
be an
n
-dimensional real vector space. To any convex body
B
in
V
that contains the origin in its interior is associated a unique (asymmetric) norm
\|\cdot\|_{B}
whose unit ball is precisely
B
. The restriction of
\|\cdot\|_{B}
to any hypersurface defines a Finsler metric whose corresponding Holmes-Thompson
(n-1)
-volume (or area in short) will be denoted by
\mathscr{A}_{B}(\cdot)
. If
K
is a convex body in
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
, we can consider its dual body
K^{\ast}=\{p\in V^{\ast}\mid p(x)\leq 1\,\,\forall x\in K\}
as a convex body of the dual normed vector space
(V^{\ast},\|\cdot\|_{B^{\ast}})
and focus on the associated Holmes-Thompson area of its boundary sphere, that is the quantity
\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}(\partial K^{\ast})
. In the special case where
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
B
is the Euclidean unit ball, we have
\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}(\partial K^{\ast})=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial K^{\circ}).
Therefore functional (
1.3
) turns out to be a particular case of the functional

x\in\operatorname{int}(K)\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}(\partial(K-x)^{\ast}).

A minimizing point of this functional will be called a
Santaló point
of
K
for the Holmes-Thompson area in
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
.

In this paper we first prove existence and uniqueness of Santaló points for the Holmes-Thompson area in any normed vector space whose unit ball is of class
C^{1}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a convex body in a finite-dimensional real normed vector space
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
whose unit ball
B
is of class
C^{1}
. The functional

x\in\operatorname{int}(K)\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}(\partial(K-x)^{\ast})

is strictly convex and proper on the interior of
K
.

In particular there exists a unique minimizing point
\SS_{B}(K)\in\operatorname{int}(K)
for this functional.

In case the unit ball is no longer of class
C^{1}
, the above functional is still convex (and proper) on the interior of
K
, but strict convexity is no longer guaranteed. Therefore, Santaló points do exist but are no longer necessarily unique. In section
3
, our study goes far beyond the
C^{1}
-regularity, and we give precise necessary and sufficient conditions for the uniqueness. For example, when the unit ball
B
is a polytope there is always a convex body
K
with an infinite set of Santaló points, as illustrated in the concrete example
3.5
. On the bright side, uniqueness is always ensured without any regularity assumption in the special case where unit ball and convex body coincide. Using the duality formula
\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}(\partial K^{\ast})=\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial B)
discovered by
[
Holmes & Thompson 1979
]
, we restate this result as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
be any finite-dimensional real normed vector space.
The functional

x\in\operatorname{int}(B)\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{B-x}(\partial(B-x))

admits a unique minimizing point
\SS_{B}(B)
.

Rephrasing the theorem above, there exists a unique translation of the unit ball of a given normed vector space minimizing its own Holmes-Thompson boundary area.

In addition, by invariance properties of the Holmes-Thompson area, the map
B\to\SS_{B}(B)
is affinely equivariant, that is

\SS_{TB}(TB)=T(\SS_{B}(B))

for any invertible affine map
T
. Therefore this map defines a new affine-invariant point in the sense of
[
Grünbaum 1963
]
. See subsection
3.3
for more details.

In order to characterize
\SS_{B}(K)
, we study the first variation of the above functional. Assuming that the normed vector space
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
is
Minkowski
, i.e.
\partial B
is smooth and positively curved (that is, with strictly positive sectional curvature), we obtain an interesting formula closely ressembling (
1.2
) and involving some classical notions from affine differential geometry.

Given
x\in\partial B
, recall that the affine normal line is a canonical
1
-dimensional vector subspace
N_{x}\subset V
transverse to
T_{x}=T_{x}\partial B
, which behaves equivariantly under linear transformations of
\partial B
. We thus have the decompositions

V=T_{x}\oplus N_{x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad V^{*}=N_{x}^{\bot}\oplus T_{x}^{\bot}.

Let us consider the isomorphism
N_{x}^{\bot}\simeq T_{x}^{*}
given by
\phi\mapsto\phi|_{T_{x}}
, and the projection
\pi_{x}\colon V^{*}\to N_{x}^{\bot}
with kernel
\ker\pi_{x}=T_{x}^{\bot}
. Pick an arbitrary smooth measure
\mu
on
\partial B
and consider the Lebesgue measure
\mu_{x}
on
T_{x}
, and its dual measure
(\mu_{x})^{*}
on
T_{x}^{*}
. Let
\nu_{x}
be the Lebesgue measure on
N_{x}^{\bot}\simeq T_{x}^{*}
corresponding to
(\mu_{x})^{*}
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
K
be a convex body in a Minkowski space
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
. Using the previous notation, we define

\CC_{B}(K^{\ast})=\int_{\partial B}\left(\int_{\pi_{x}(K^{\ast})}\,\,q\,d\nu_{x}(q)\right)\,d\mu(x).

Note that the inner integral is the centroid of the projection
\pi_{x}(K^{\ast})
with respect to
\nu_{x}
and belongs to
V^{\ast}
. It is easy to see that
\CC_{B}(K^{\ast})
does not depend on the choice of
\mu
.

Our third main result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
K
be a convex body in a Minkowski space
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
.
Then for any
v\in V
we have

(1.4)

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}({\partial(K-tv)^{\ast}})=\frac{n+1}{\varepsilon_{n-1}}\left\langle\CC_{B}(K^{\ast}),v\right\rangle.

In particular
\SS_{B}(K)
lies at the origin of
V
if and only if
\CC_{B}(K^{\ast})
lies at the origin of
V^{\ast}
.

Here
\varepsilon_{n-1}
denotes the Euclidean volume of an
(n-1)
-dimensional unit Euclidean ball and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the canonical duality pairing. Note the similarity with formula (
1.2
).

In the particular case of a convex body in an Euclidean space, both theorems sum up into the following.

Corollary 1.5
.

Given a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a unique point
\SS(K)\in\operatorname{int}(K)
minimizing the functional

x\in\operatorname{int}(K)\mapsto\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial(K-x)^{\circ}).

Furthermore, this point lies at the origin if and only if the following average of centroids of orthogonal projections of its polar body

\CS(K^{\circ})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(\int_{\pi_{x^{\perp}}(K^{\circ})}\,\,y\,d\lambda(y)\right)\,d\sigma(x)

lies at the origin.

Here
\pi_{x^{\perp}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the hyperplane orthogonal to
x
, and
\sigma,\lambda
denote respectively the canonical measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and the Lebesgue measure on
x^{\perp}
induced by the standard Euclidean structure.

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we collect some material on the Holmes-Thompson volume, and present a Crofton formula for the Holmes-Thompson area of a hypersurface in a Minkowski space due to
[
Álvarez Paiva 1998
]
. This formula will be decisive to show (
1.4
). In section
3
we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. The next three sections are devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. The strategy consists of using the above mentioned Crofton formula which describes the Holmes-Thompson area of
\partial K^{*}
as an integral on the set of lines intersecting
K^{\ast}
. In order to compute its first variation under translations, we first use the boundary sphere
\partial B
to construct a parametrization space for the oriented affine lines of the dual vector space in terms of intersections of affine hyperplanes. We then show how to rewrite the measure involved in Álvarez Paiva’s formula in this new space. The effect of translations is then simple to describe which allows us to compute the first variation of the area. So after shortly recalling in section
4
some classical notions of equiaffine differential geometry, we define the parametrization space in section
5
and show how to rewrite the measure of lines in this new setup. In the last section
6
we compute the first variation of the Holmes-Thompson area and obtain formula (
1.4
).

2.
Background on the Holmes-Thompson notions of volume and area

In this section we collect some material on the Holmes-Thompson notions of volume and area. The two major tools we will use in the sequel are a Crofton type formula due to Álvarez Paiva and presented in Proposition
2.1
, and a duality formula due to Holmes & Thompson stated in Proposition
2.3
.

2.1.
Holmes-Thompson volume on continuous Finsler manifolds

Let
M
be a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
k
endowed with a continuous Finsler metric
F:TM\to[0,\infty)
, that is a continuous function which is positively homogenous of degree
1
outside the zero section and such that for all
x\in M
the subsets of
T_{x}M

D_{x}(M,F)\coloneqq\{v\in T_{x}M\mid F(x,v)\leq 1\}

are convex bodies containing the origin in their interior (or equivalently,
F
is a continuous function whose restriction to each tangent space
T_{x}M
is a norm, possibly asymmetric). For any measurable set
A\subset M
denote by
D^{\ast}(A,F)
the unit co-disc bundle over
A
consisting of the disjoint union of the dual bodies
D_{x}(M,F)^{\ast}\subset(T_{x}M)^{\ast}
for
x\in A
. The
Holmes-Thompson volume
of the measurable set
A
in
(M,F)
is then defined as the following integral:

\vol(A,F)={\frac{1}{k!\varepsilon_{k}}\,}\int_{D^{\ast}(A,F)}\omega_{M}^{k}

where
\omega_{M}
denotes the standard symplectic form on the cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}M
. If
\rho
denotes any non-vanishing
k
-density on
M
, then

\vol(A,F)=\int_{A}\frac{|D_{x}(M,F)^{\ast}|^{\ast}_{\rho_{x}}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\,\rho_{x}

where
|\cdot|^{\ast}_{\rho_{x}}
is the dual Lebesgue measure on
T_{x}^{\ast}M
of the Lebesgue measure
|\cdot|_{\rho_{x}}
on
T_{x}M
associated to
\rho
. More precisely,
|\cdot|_{\rho_{x}}
is the Lebesgue measure on
T_{x}M
normalized to give volume
1
on a parallelotope spanned by a base
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
of
T_{x}M
for which
\rho_{x}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})=1
, and
|\cdot|^{\ast}_{\rho_{x}}
is the Lebesgue measure on
T_{x}^{\ast}M
normalized to give volume
1
for the parallelotope spanned by the covectors
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in T_{x}^{\ast}M
forming a base which is dual to
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
.

2.2.
Holmes-Thompson volume and area in normed spaces

Let
{B}
be a convex body in an
n
-dimensional real vector space
V
that contains the origin as an interior point. The associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{B}
defines a continuous Finsler metric on the manifold
V
whose Holmes-Thompson volume satisfies for any compact set
A
the equality

\vol(A,\|\cdot\|_{B})=\frac{|A|\,|{B}^{\ast}|^{\ast}}{\varepsilon_{n}}

where
|\cdot|
and
|\cdot|^{\ast}
are respectively the measure and its dual measure associated to some translation-invariant density on
V
. We will denote shortly
\vol(A,\|\cdot\|_{B})
by
\vol_{B}(A)
.

In the case where
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
we find that
\vol_{B}(A)=|A|\,|{B}^{\circ}|/\varepsilon_{n}
. In particular the Finsler volume
\vol_{B}(B)
of the convex body
{B}
itself coincides with its volume product up to the constant
1/\varepsilon_{n}
.

It turns out that the associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{B}
also permits to define for any hypersurface
M\subset V
(and, more generally, for any smooth submanifold of
V
) a continuous Finsler metric
F_{B}:TM\to[0,\infty)
by simply setting
F_{B}(x,v)=\|v\|_{B}.
We will denote shortly
\vol(M,F_{B})
by
\mathscr{A}_{B}(M)
and speak of the
Holmes-Thompson area
of
M
. This notion of area extends to boundaries of convex bodies. For this recall that the set of singular boundary points (those for which the supporting hyperplane is not uniquely defined) of a convex body
{K}
in
V
is of
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure zero, see
[
Reidemeister 1921
]
. Therefore the formula

(2.1)

\mathscr{A}_{B}(M)=\int_{M}\frac{|(T_{x}M\cap{B})^{\ast}|^{\ast}_{\rho}}{\varepsilon_{n-1}}\,\rho(x)

where
\rho
denotes any translation-invariant
(n-1)
-density on
V
makes sense when
M=\partial{K}
is the boundary of a convex body
{K}
. Moreover, if
{K}_{n}\to{K}
is a sequence of smooth convex bodies that converges in the Hausdorff metric to a convex body
{K}
, then
\mathscr{A}_{B}(\partial{K}_{n})\to\mathscr{A}_{B}(\partial{K})
(see Remark
2.2
).

2.3.
Crofton formula for hypersurfaces in Minkowski spaces

In case the normed space
{(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})}
has smoothness and strict convexity properties, the Holmes-Thompson area of hypersurfaces admits a Crofton formula due to Alvarez-Paiva (cf.
[
Álvarez Paiva 1998
]
) that we present next. We refer to
[
Schneider 2006
]
for a very complete survey on the integral geometry of normed spaces.

Let
{B}
be a convex body of
V
that contains the origin as an interior point, and suppose
\partial B
is smooth and positively curved. Then the associated normed space
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
is said to be
Minkowski
. In that case its dual body also has smooth and positively curved boundary, and the dual Legendre transform

{\LL}^{\ast}:\partial{B}^{\ast}\to\partial{B}

is a well defined diffeomorphism. Recall that
{\LL}^{\ast}(p)
is defined as the unique
x\in\partial{B}
such that
p(x)=1
. Denote by
G_{+}(V)
the space of oriented affine lines in
V
whose elements are denoted by
x+\langle v\rangle_{+}
where
x
is a point in
V
and
\langle v\rangle_{+}
is the
1
-dimensional vector subspace of
V
spanned and oriented by a vector
v
. In particular according to this notation
x+\langle v\rangle_{+}=y+\langle w\rangle_{+}
if and only if there exists
(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
y=x+tv
and
w=sv
. Now consider the projection map

\displaystyle\pi:V\times\partial{B}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\to G_{+}(V)

\displaystyle(x,p)

\displaystyle\mapsto x+\langle{\LL}^{\ast}(p)\rangle_{+}.

Recall that
\omega_{V}
denotes the standard symplectic form of
T^{\ast}V
and denote by
i:V\times\partial{B}^{\ast}\hookrightarrow T^{\ast}V
the canonical inclusion. It is well known that there exists a unique symplectic form
\omega_{B}
on
G_{+}(V)
such that
\pi^{\ast}\omega_{B}=i^{\ast}\omega_{V}
, see
[
Arnold & Givental 1990
,
Besse 1978
]
. Here is the Crofton formula associated to this symplectic form.

Proposition 2.1

(Álvarez Paiva)
.

The Holmes-Thompson area of a compact immersed hypersurface
M
in a Minkowski space
(V,\|\cdot\|_{B})
satisfies the following formula:

\mathscr{A}_{B}(M)=\frac{1}{2\,(n-1)!\,\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{G_{+}(V)}\#(L\cap M)\,\omega_{B}^{n-1}.

Applying this formula to the boundary of a smooth convex
{K}
in
V
we obtain the following formula:

\mathscr{A}_{B}(\partial{K})=\frac{1}{(n-1)!\,\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{\{L\colon L\cap{K}\neq\emptyset\}}\omega_{B}^{n-1}.

By continuity we see that this formula still holds when the convex body
{K}
is not necessarily smooth.

Proposition
2.1
was first stated in
[
Álvarez Paiva 1998
]
(see
[
Álvarez & Fernandes 1998
, Theorem 3.1]
) more generally for immersed hypersurfaces in reversible Finsler manifolds whose space of oriented geodesics is a smooth manifold. We include the proof here for the reader’s convenience as well to check that it is still true in our context without the reversibility assumption.

Proof.

First note that if
H\subset V
is a linear subspace, then
({B}\cap H)^{\ast}=r_{H^{\ast}}({B}^{\ast})
where
r_{H^{\ast}}:V^{\ast}\to H^{\ast}
denotes the restriction morphism defined by
r_{H^{\ast}}(p)=p_{|H}
. Thus

\displaystyle D^{\ast}(M,F_{B})

\displaystyle=\{(x,p)\in T^{\ast}M\mid x\in M\,\text{and}\,\,p\in({B}\cap T_{x}M)^{\ast}\}

\displaystyle=\{(x,p)\in T^{\ast}M\mid x\in M\,\text{and}\,\,p\in r_{(T_{x}M)^{\ast}}({B}^{\ast})\}

\displaystyle=\{(x,p)\in T^{\ast}M\mid x\in M\,\text{and}\,\,p\in r_{(T_{x}M)^{\ast}}(\partial{B}^{\ast})\}

\displaystyle=P_{M}(M\times\partial{B}^{\ast})

where

\displaystyle P_{M}:M\times\partial{B}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\to D^{\ast}(M,F_{B})

\displaystyle(x,p)

\displaystyle\mapsto(x,p_{|T_{x}M}).

Therefore, by the coarea formula

(n-1)!\,\varepsilon_{n-1}\,\mathscr{A}_{B}(M)=\int_{D^{\ast}(M,F_{B})}\omega_{M}^{n-1}={\frac{1}{2}}\,\int_{M\times\partial{B}^{\ast}}|P^{\ast}_{M}\omega_{M}^{n-1}|.

Here
|\eta|
denotes the density given by the absolute value of a top differential form
\eta
.
Now observe that
P^{\ast}_{M}\omega_{M}=i^{\ast}\omega_{V}
(as both are the exterior derivative of the tautological one-form) which implies, using the identity
i^{\ast}\omega_{V}=\pi^{\ast}\omega_{B}
and the coarea formula again, that

2\,(n-1)!\,\varepsilon_{n-1}\,\mathscr{A}_{B}(M)=\int_{M\times\partial{B}^{\ast}}|\pi^{\ast}\omega_{B}^{n-1}|=\int_{G_{+}(V)}\#(L\cap M)\omega_{B}^{n-1}.

∎

Observe that we obtain by the way the following formula for Holmes-Thompson area:

(2.2)

\mathscr{A}_{B}(\partial{K})=\frac{1}{2\,(n-1)!\,\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{\partial{K}\times\partial{B}^{\ast}}|i^{\ast}\omega_{V}^{n-1}|.

Remark 2.2
.

We easily check from the above formula that if
{K}_{n},{B_{n}}
are sequences of convex bodies that converge in the Hausdorff metric to
{K,B}
respectively, then
\mathscr{A}_{B_{n}}(\partial{K}_{n})\to\mathscr{A}_{B}(\partial{K})
.

2.4.
Holmes-Thompson duality formula

To conclude this section let us recall the following duality principle for the Holmes-Thompson area of convex bodies.

Proposition 2.3

(Holmes & Thompson)
.

Let
{K}
and
{B}
be two convex bodies in
V
that contain the origin in their interior. Then

\mathscr{A}_{{B}^{\ast}}(\partial{K}^{\ast})=\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial{B}).

Proof.

This was first observed by Holmes and Thompson in
[
Holmes & Thompson 1979
]
. Here is a quick argument:

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial{B})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\,(n-1)!\,\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{\partial{B}\times\partial{K}^{\ast}}|\omega_{V}^{n-1}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\,(n-1)!\,\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{\partial{K}^{\ast}\times\partial({B}^{\ast})^{\ast}}|\omega_{V^{\ast}}^{n-1}|

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{{B}^{\ast}}(\partial{K}^{\ast}),

where we have abusively denoted
i^{\ast}\omega_{V}
by
\omega_{V}
.
∎

3.
Strict convexity and properness

In this section we prove both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
which we merge in a same statement as follows using Proposition
2.3
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
B
and
K
be two convex bodies of a finite-dimensional real vector space
V
. Suppose that
either (i)
\partial B
is of class
C^{1}
, or (ii)
K=B
.
The functional

x\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{K-x}(\partial B)

is strictly convex and proper on the interior of
K
.

In particular there exists a unique minimizing point
\SS_{B}(K)\in\operatorname{int}(K)
for the Holmes-Thompson area of the boundary sphere of
(K-x)^{\ast}
in
(V^{\ast},\|\cdot\|_{B^{\ast}})
.

We split the proof into two subsections. The first concerns the strict convexity of the functional above. The second proves the properness of the functional, using an isoperimetric inequality between Holmes-Thompson notions of volume and area. In a third subsection we explain why the map
B\mapsto\SS_{B}(B)
gives rise to an affine-invariant point. Finally, in the last subsection we further explore conditions which ensure the strict convexity beyond conditions (i) and (ii).

3.1.
Proof of the strict convexity

After fixing some isomorphism
V\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can use the standard Euclidean structure to identify the dual body
A^{\ast}
with the polar body
A^{\circ}
of any convex body
A
containing the origin as an interior point, and rewrite

(3.1)

\mathscr{A}_{K-x}(\partial B)=\int_{\partial B}\frac{|(T_{y}\partial B\cap(K-x))^{\circ}|_{n-1}}{\varepsilon_{n-1}}d\lambda_{n-1}(y)

using formula (
2.1
). Here we have denoted by

•

d\lambda_{n-1}
the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume element induced on hypersufaces by the Euclidean structure

•

|C|_{n-1}=\int_{C}d\lambda_{n-1}
the
(n-1)
-Hausdorff measure of any compact domain
C
contained in some hyperplane.

Recall that a point
y
in the boundary of a convex body
B
is said to be
regular
if there is a unique supporting hyperplane
y+H(y,B)
of
B
at
y
. The set
\operatorname{reg}{B}\subset\partial B
of regular points has
(n-1)
-Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\operatorname{reg}{B})=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial B)
(see
[
Reidemeister 1921
]
), and the map

y\in\operatorname{reg}{B}\mapsto H(y,B)\in\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is continuous (cf.
[
Schneider 2013
, Lemma 2.2.12]
). Here
\operatorname{Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the set of all vector
k
-planes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, the push-forward of the
(n-1)
-Hausdorff measure
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
by this map is well defined and we denote by
\mu_{\partial B}
this measure on
\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The measure
\mu_{\partial B}
coincides with the push-forward of the classical
surface area measure
of
B
(which is a measure on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
) using the standard two folding map
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Consider the functional

\displaystyle f_{K}:\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\operatorname{int}(K)

\displaystyle\to(0,\infty)

\displaystyle(H,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto|(H\cap(K-x))^{\circ}|_{n-1}

which is continuous as a composition of continuous maps.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
H\in\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the function
x\mapsto f_{K}(H,x)
is convex on
\operatorname{int}(K)
.

Proof.

By
[
Schneider 2013
, eqs. (1.52) & (1.53)]
, in an
m
-dimensional Euclidean vector space
(W,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, the Lebesgue measure of any convex body
C
can be computed using the formula

|C|_{m}={\frac{1}{m}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}(W)}\left(\frac{1}{h_{C^{\circ}}(u)}\right)^{m}d\lambda_{m-1}(u),

where
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}(W)
denotes the unit sphere in
(W,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
and
h_{C^{\circ}}
is the support function of the polar body
C^{\circ}
, defined for any
x\in W
by
h_{C^{\circ}}(x)=\max\{\langle x,y\rangle\mid y\in C^{\circ}\}
. Therefore

f_{K}(H,x)={\frac{1}{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}(H)}\left(\frac{1}{h_{H\cap(K-x)}(u)}\right)^{n-1}d\lambda_{n-2}(u).

Now observe that
\forall u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}(H)
the function
x\mapsto h_{H\cap(K-x)}(u)
is concave as for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
we have

\displaystyle h_{H\cap(K-\lambda x_{1}-(1-\lambda)x_{2})}(u)

\displaystyle=\max\{\langle u,z\rangle\mid z\in H\cap[\lambda(K-x_{1})+(1-\lambda)(K-x_{2})]\}

\displaystyle\geq\max\{\langle u,z\rangle\mid z\in\lambda[H\cap(K-x_{1})]+(1-\lambda)[H\cap(K-x_{2}))]\}

\displaystyle=\lambda h_{H\cap(K-x_{1})}(u)+(1-\lambda)h_{H\cap(K-x_{2})}(u).

The function
t\mapsto{1/t^{n-1}}
being strictly convex on
(0,\infty)
, we get that the function

x\mapsto\left(\frac{1}{h_{H\cap(K-x)}(u)}\right)^{n-1}

is convex. So
f_{K}(H,\cdot)
is also convex as an integral of convex functions.
∎

For any
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
let us define
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\subset\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the set of hyperplanes
H
such that

(3.2)

f_{K}(H,x_{1})+f_{K}(H,x_{2})-2\,f_{K}(H,(x_{1}+x_{2})/2)>0.

Observe that
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})
is an open set by continuity of
f_{K}
. Furthermore, for a fixed
H
, we know by
[
Santaló 1949
]
that for any
x_{0}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
the map
x\mapsto f_{K}(H,x+x_{0})
is strictly convex on
H\cap\operatorname{int}(K-x_{0})
. So in particular, we always have
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\emptyset
as it contains any hyperplane
H
containing
x_{2}-x_{1}
. Now let us say that
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\operatorname{int}(K)\times\operatorname{int}(K)\setminus\Delta
is a
cylindrical pair
for
K
if
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here
\Delta
denotes the diagonal subset. We denote by
\operatorname{Cylpair}(K)\subset\operatorname{int}(K)\times\operatorname{int}(K)\setminus\Delta
the subset of cylindrical pairs associated to
K
. The term cylindrical pair is justified by the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\operatorname{Cylpair}(K)
, we can find a hyperplane
H\in\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a convex body
C
of
H
such that

H\cap(K-x)=C

for any
x\in[x_{1},x_{2}]
.
Equivalently, we have that
(H+[x_{1},x_{2}])\cap K=C+[x_{1},x_{2}]
.

Proof.

Picking
H\notin\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})
we get that

f_{K}(H,\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2})=\lambda f_{K}(H,(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)f_{K}(H,x_{2})

for
\lambda=1/2
and thus for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
. It follows by the proof of Lemma
3.2
that
\forall u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}(H)
and
\forall\lambda\in[0,1]

h_{H\cap(K-x_{1})}(u)=h_{H\cap(K-\lambda x_{1}-(1-\lambda)x_{2})}(u).

Therefore there exists a convex body
C
of
H
such that
H\cap(K-z)=C
for any
z\in[x_{1},x_{2}]
. Equivalently, we have that
(H+[x_{1},x_{2}])\cap K=C+[x_{1},x_{2}]
.
∎

The following result describes how cylindrical pairs of
K
should interact with the support of
\mu_{\partial B}
to ensure strict convexity for the Holmes-Thompson area. Recall that the support
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
of a Borel measure
\mu
is the intersection of all closed sets of total measure.

Proposition 3.4
.

The map
x\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{K-x}(\partial B)
is strictly convex on
\operatorname{int}(K)
if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})\cap\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\emptyset
for every pair
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\operatorname{Cylpair}(K)
.

Observe that
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})\cap\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\emptyset
for every
(x_{1},x_{2})\notin\operatorname{Cylpair}(K)
as
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})=\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in this case. So we can replace “for every pair
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\operatorname{Cylpair}(K)
” by “for every
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
” in the proposition above.

Proof.

The integral of a non-negative continuous function against a Borel measure is positive if and only if the support of the measure meets the interior of the support of the function. Therefore, fixing
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
, we get that

\mathscr{A}_{K-x_{1}}(\partial B)+\mathscr{A}_{K-x_{2}}(\partial B)-2\,\mathscr{A}_{K-(x_{1}+x_{2})/2}(\partial B)\\
=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left[f_{K}(H,x_{1})+f_{K}(H,x_{2})-2\,f_{K}(H,(x_{1}+x_{2})/2)\right]d\mu_{\partial B}(H)

is positive if and only if
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})
meets
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the strict convexity part of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of the strict convexity in Theorem
3.1
.

(i) Suppose first that
\partial B
is of class
C^{1}
. Thus
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})=\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which implies that the map
x\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{K-x}(\partial B)
is always strictly convex on
\operatorname{int}(K)
according to Proposition
3.4
.

(ii) Suppose now that
K=B
.
By Proposition
3.4
we need to prove that
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})\cap\mathrm{Strcvx}_{B}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\emptyset
for every
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\operatorname{Cylpair}(B)
.

By Lemma
3.3
, given any
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\operatorname{Cylpair}(B)
, there exists
H\in\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
C
a convex body in
H
such that
(H+[x_{1},x_{2}])\cap B=C+[x_{1},x_{2}]
. Let
\mu_{\partial C}
be the push-forward measure of the surface area measure of
C
by the quotient map
S^{n-2}(H)\to\operatorname{Gr}_{n-2}(H)
. Take any
G\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial C})
, and consider the hyperplane
H^{\prime}=G\oplus\mathrm{span}(x_{2}-x_{1})
. Since
x_{2}-x_{1}\in H^{\prime}
, we know by [Santaló,1949] that
H^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Strcvx}_{B}(x_{1},x_{2})
. Let
N
be any open neighborhood of
H^{\prime}
in
\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let us prove that
\mu_{\partial B}(N)>0
.
Consider the map

\displaystyle\iota:\operatorname{Gr}_{n-2}(H)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle F\oplus\mathrm{span}(x_{2}-x_{1})

which is continuous.
Since
\iota^{-1}(N)
is open and contains
G\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial C})
we have
\mu_{\partial C}(\iota^{-1}(N))>0
. Let
U
be the set of regular points of
\partial B
with supporting hyperplane inside
N
, and let
V\subset\partial C
consist of the regular points of
\partial C
with supporting hyperplane in
\iota^{-1}(N)
. By
[
Rockafellar 1997
, Theorems 25.1 and 25.5]
, there exists an injective
C^{1}
-map
\varphi
defined on some measurable set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
and such that
\varphi(D)=V
.
Set

\displaystyle\bar{\varphi}:D\times]x_{1},x_{2}[

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle V+]x_{1},x_{2}[\subset U

\displaystyle(y,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\varphi(y)+x.

Observe that the Jacobians of
\varphi
and
\bar{\varphi}
satisfy the inequality
\mathrm{Jac}_{x}\,\bar{\varphi}\geq\mathrm{Jac}_{x}\,\varphi\cdot\sin\alpha

where
\alpha
denotes the angle between
x_{2}-x_{1}
and
H
.
Therefore we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\partial B}(N)=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(U)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{D\times]x_{1},x_{2}[}\mathrm{Jac}_{x}\bar{\varphi}

\displaystyle\geq|x_{2}-x_{1}|\cdot\sin\alpha\cdot\int_{D}\mathrm{Jac}_{x}\varphi

\displaystyle=|x_{2}-x_{1}|\cdot\sin(\alpha)\cdot\mathscr{H}^{n-2}(V)

\displaystyle=|x_{2}-x_{1}|\cdot\sin(\alpha)\cdot\mu_{\partial C}(\iota^{-1}(N))>0,

as claimed. This implies that
H^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})
. Since also
H^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Strcvx}_{B}(x_{1},x_{2})
, this proves strict convexity in case (ii).
∎

We now present an example of two convex bodies

Observe that

consists of the two directions
{x=y},{x=-y}
each of weight

3.2.
Proof of the properness

In order to prove Theorem
3.1
, it remains to show that the map is proper on the interior of
K
. This holds without any assumption on the convex body
B
, and easily follows from the well-known fact that the map
x\mapsto\vol_{K-x}(B)
is proper on the interior of
K
and the following generalization of the classical isoperimetric inequality to asymmetric Minkowski spaces.

Proposition 3.6

(Isoperimetric inequality for Holmes-Thompson volume)
.

Let
B
and
K
be two convex bodies of an
n
-dimensional real vector space
V
. Suppose that
K
contains the origin as an interior point. Then

\frac{\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial B)^{n}}{\vol_{K}(B)^{n-1}}\geq\frac{(4n)^{n}}{n!\varepsilon_{n}}.

This inequality is stated in
[
Thompson 1996
, Theorem 6.6.4]
in the case where
K
is symmetric. The proof straightforwardly generalizes to the non-symmetric case so we briefly survey the arguments.

Proof.

After fixing some isomorphism
V\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we identify the dual body
A^{\ast}
of any convex body
A
containing the origin as an interior point with its polar body
A^{\circ}
. First recall that (compare with
[
Thompson 1996
, Theorem 5.2.2]
)

\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial B)=n\cdot V(B[n-1],I_{K})

where
V
denotes the mixed volume and
I_{K}
the isoperimetrix convex body defined as the unique symmetric convex body with support function

(3.3)

h_{I_{K}}(u)=|\pi_{u^{\perp}}(K^{\circ})|/\varepsilon_{n-1}=|(K\cap u^{\perp})^{\circ}|/\varepsilon_{n-1}.

Observe that
I_{K}
is a zonoid according to
[
Schneider 2013
, Formula 5.80 and Theorem 3.5.3]
.

Next we check that

\frac{\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial B)^{n}}{\vol_{K}(B)^{n-1}}\geq\frac{\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial I_{K})^{n}}{\vol_{K}(I_{K})^{n-1}}.

Indeed as
\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial I_{K})=n|I_{K}|
and
\vol_{K}(B)=|B||K^{\circ}|/\varepsilon_{n}
, the above inequality is equivalent to

V(B[n-1],I_{K})^{n}\geq|I_{K}||B|^{n-1}

which directly follows from Minkowski’s inequality, see
[
Schneider 2013
, Theorem 7.2.1]
.

Now recall that the normalized isoperimetrix convex body
\tilde{I}_{K}
is defined as the unique dilated of the isoperimetrix satisfying
n\vol_{K}(\tilde{I}_{K})=\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial\tilde{I}_{K})
. Therefore

\frac{\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial B)^{n}}{\vol_{K}(B)^{n-1}}\geq\frac{\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial\tilde{I}_{K})^{n}}{\vol_{K}(\tilde{I}_{K})^{n-1}}=n^{n}\vol_{K}(\tilde{I}_{K})=n^{n}\frac{|K^{\circ}||\tilde{I}_{K}|}{\varepsilon_{n}}.

Besides it is easy to see that in fact
\tilde{I}_{K}=\varepsilon_{n}\cdot I_{K}/|K^{\circ}|
.
Using equation (
3.3
), we also see that
\varepsilon_{n-1}\cdot h_{I_{K}}
is precisely the support function of the projection body
\Pi K^{\circ}
. Consequently we find the identity
\tilde{I}_{K}=(\varepsilon_{n}/\varepsilon_{n-1}|K^{\circ}|)\cdot\Pi K^{\circ}
. Therefore

|(\tilde{I}_{K})^{\circ}|=\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{n-1}|K^{\circ}|}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{n}|(\Pi K^{\circ})^{\circ}|=\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{n-1}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)^{n}|K^{\circ}||K^{\circ}|^{n-1}|(\Pi K^{\circ})^{\circ}|,

which implies together with Petty’s projection inequality
[
Petty 1971
]
, see
[
Schneider 2013
, Formula 10.86]
, that

|(\tilde{I}_{K})^{\circ}|\leq|K^{\circ}|.

Finally

\frac{\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial B)^{n}}{\vol_{K}(B)^{n-1}}\geq n^{n}\frac{|(\tilde{I}_{K})^{\circ}||\tilde{I}_{K}|}{\varepsilon_{n}}\geq\frac{(4n)^{n}}{n!\varepsilon_{n}}

using Reisner optimal lower bound on the volume product for zonoids
[
Reisner 1985
]
.
∎

The properness statement in Theorem
3.1
is now a consequence of the following simple fact.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
B_{k}\to B
be a convergent sequence of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and assume that
0\in\operatorname{int}(B_{k})
for each
k
and
0\in\partial B
. Then
|B_{k}^{\circ}|\to\infty
.

Proof.

Given a convex body
K
, let us say that a parallelotope circumscribes
K
if
K\subset P
and every face of
P
is contained in a supporting hyperplane of
K
.
Without loss of generality assume that
B
is supported by a coordinate hyperplane at
0
. Let
P_{k}
(resp.
P
) be the parallelotope circumscribing
B_{k}
(resp.
B
), and with all faces parallel to the coordinate hyperplanes. Then
P_{n}^{\circ}
is a sequence of polytopes with vertices contained in the coordinate lines. One of these vertices tends to infinity while the others converge to points different from the origin. It follows that

|B_{n}^{\circ}|\geq|P_{n}^{\circ}|\to\infty.

∎

3.3.
A new affine-invariant point

Let us start by recalling the definition of an
affine-invariant point
(see
[
Grünbaum 1963
]
): this is a map
f:{\pazocal K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
{\pazocal K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the space of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the following two conditions

•

for every inversible affine map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every convex body
B\in{\pazocal K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one has
f(\Phi(B))=\Phi(f(B))
.

•

f
is continuous with respect to the Hausdorff metric.

We will prove the following.

Proposition 3.8
.

The map
f_{\SS}:{\pazocal K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by
f_{\SS}(B)=\SS_{B}(B)
is an affine-invariant point.

Proof.

Using (
2.2
) we easily check that given any pair
K,B\in{\pazocal K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have
\mathscr{A}_{LK}(\partial LB)=\mathscr{A}_{K}(\partial B)
for every invertible linear map
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. So if
T\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an invertible affine map, we get that

\mathscr{A}_{B-x}(\partial B)=\mathscr{A}_{TB-T(x)}(\partial\,TB)

for all
x\in\operatorname{int}(B)
. Our Santaló point thus satisfies the equality

\SS_{TB}(TB)=T(\SS_{B}(B))

from which we deduce that the map
f_{\SS}
fulfills the first condition.

In order to check the second condition, we argue as follows. First define a function

F_{\SS}:{\pazocal K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to]0,+\infty]

by setting
F_{\SS}(B,x)=\mathscr{A}_{B-x}(\partial B)
if
x\in\operatorname{int}(B)
and
F_{\SS}(B,x)=+\infty
otherwise. Observe that this function is continuous. Indeed, since
F_{\SS}(B,x)=F_{\SS}(B-x,0)
, we only need to check continuity with respect to
B
. As sequentially continuous maps defined on metric spaces are continuous, this follows from Remark
2.2
and Propositions
3.6
and
3.7
.

Given a convex body
B
, fix
K_{0}\in{\pazocal K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
B\subset\operatorname{int}(K_{0})
. By the Blaschke selection theorem, the subset
V:=\{K\in{\pazocal K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid K\subset K_{0}\}
is a compact neighbourhood of
B
in the Hausdorff topology (cf.
[
Schneider 2013
, Theorems 1.8.4 and 1.8.6]
). Let now
\{B_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of convex bodies in
V
converging to
B
, and consider the sequence
\{f_{\SS}(B_{n})\}_{n}
, which is contained in
K_{0}
as each
B_{n}\in V
. Let us show that
\{f_{\SS}(B_{n})\}_{n}
converges to
f_{\SS}(B)
. By contradiction, suppose that there exists an open neighborhood
N
of
f_{\SS}(B)
and a subsequence of
\{f_{\SS}(B_{n})\}_{n}
contained in
K_{0}\setminus N
. This set being compact, there exists a subsequence
\{f_{\SS}(B_{n_{k}})\}_{k}
converging to some point
x\neq f_{\SS}(B)
. Because
F_{\SS}(B_{n},\SS_{B_{n}}(B_{n}))\leq F_{\SS}(B_{n},y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we deduce by continuity that
F_{\SS}(B,x)\leq F_{\SS}(B,y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then both
x
and
f_{\SS}(B)
would be minimizers of the function
F_{\SS}(B,\cdot)
, thus contradicting Theorem
3.1
. Therefore the sequence
\{f_{\SS}(B_{n})\}_{n}
necessarily converges to
f_{\SS}(B)
. This proves the continuity of the map
f_{\SS}
and concludes the proof.
∎

3.4.
Further study of the strict convexity

Next, we describe an optimal property on
B
that ensures strict convexity for any convex body
K
.
For this, let us define for any
L\subset\operatorname{Gr}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the subset

\operatorname{Star}(L)\coloneqq\{H\in\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid L\subset H\}.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
B
be a convex body. The map
x\in\operatorname{int}(K)\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{K-x}(\partial B)
is strictly convex for any convex body
K
if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})\cap\operatorname{Star}(L)\neq\emptyset
for any
L\in\operatorname{Gr}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Recall that as already observed, for a fixed
H
and
K
, we know by
[
Santaló 1949
]
that for any
x_{0}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
the map
x\mapsto f_{K}(H,x)
is strictly convex on
(x_{0}+H)\cap\operatorname{int}(K)
. In particular, for any
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
, we have that
\operatorname{Star}(\operatorname{span}(x_{2}-x_{1}))\subset\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})
. So if
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})\cap\operatorname{Star}(L)\neq\emptyset
for any
L\in\operatorname{Gr}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we directly get that
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})\cap\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\emptyset
for any convex body
K
and
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
. By Proposition
3.4
, it ensures that the map
x\in\operatorname{int}(K)\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{K-x}(\partial B)
is always strictly convex.

Now suppose that
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})\cap\operatorname{Star}(L)=\emptyset
for some
L\in\operatorname{Gr}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Lemma 3.10
.

The family of open sets
\{\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\}_{(K,x_{1},x_{2})}
where
K
runs over convex bodies and
x_{1},x_{2}
are any pair of distinct points of
\operatorname{int}(K)
such that
\operatorname{span}(x_{2}-x_{1})=L
is an open neighborhood base of
\operatorname{Star}(L)
.

Proof of the Lemma.

Fix an open neighborhood
U
of
\operatorname{Star}(L)
in
\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We construct a convex body
K
such that for some
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
with
\operatorname{span}(x_{2}-x_{1})=L
we have
\operatorname{Star}(L)\subset\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\subset U
.

For this consider the (possibly empty) compact set
{U}^{c}
and let
\widetilde{K}
be any infinite cylinder with direction
L
, with a compact convex base and that contains
0
in its interior. Now fix
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a unit vector
v\in L
. As
{U}^{c}
is compact and disjoint from
\operatorname{Star}(L)
, we can consider a finite subcylinder
K
of
\widetilde{K}
delimited by two affine hyperplanes whose underlying directions are not in
\operatorname{Star}(L)
and such that
(H+\varepsilon v)\cap\widetilde{K}=(H+\varepsilon v)\cap K
for any
H\in{U}^{c}
and any
|\varepsilon|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. By taking
x_{1}=-\varepsilon_{0}v
and
x_{2}=\varepsilon_{0}v
we have that for any
H\in{U}^{c}
, the map
x\mapsto H\cap(K-x)
is constant on
[x_{1},x_{2}]
and so
H\notin\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})
.
∎

Lemma
3.10
applied to the open neighborhood
U\coloneqq{\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})}^{c}
implies that there exist
K
and
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\in\operatorname{int}(K)
such that
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\subset{\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial K})}^{c}
and by Proposition
3.4
we are done.
∎

To conclude this section, we describe an optimal property on
K
that ensures strict convexity for any convex body
B
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Then the map
x\mapsto\mathscr{A}_{K-x}(\partial B)
is strictly convex on
\operatorname{int}(K)
for any convex body
B
if and only if
K
has no cylindrical directions, that is
\operatorname{Cylpair}(K)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

The fact that the condition
\operatorname{Cylpair}(K)=\emptyset
is sufficient follows directly from
3.4
. To prove that it is also necessary, let
K
be a convex body with
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some cylindrical pair
(x_{1},x_{2})
. We will construct
B
such that
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{\partial B})\cap\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})=\emptyset
. For this we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.12
.

If
{\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})}^{c}
is nonempty, then it has a nonempty interior.

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.3
, if
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can find a hyperplane
H_{0}\in\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a convex body
C_{0}
of
H_{0}
such that
H_{0}\cap(K-x)=C_{0}
for any
x\in[x_{1},x_{2}]
. Fix any proper subinterval
[x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2}]
of
[x_{1},x_{2}]
, that is
[x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2}]\subset]x_{1},x_{2}[
. We can find an open neighbourhood
U
of
H_{0}
such that for any
H\in U
there is a convex body
C
of
H
such that
H\cap(K-x)=C
for any

. In particular
H\notin\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2})\subset\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})
which implies that
{\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})}^{c}
contains an open neighborhood of
H_{0}
.
∎

From this lemma, it follows that if
\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})\neq\operatorname{Gr}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
{\operatorname{Strcvx}_{K}(x_{1},x_{2})}^{c}
contains
n
hyperplanes defined by linearly independent covectors. So, for any parallelotope

whose face directions are precisely these

, and so we can conclude by Proposition

.
∎

4.
Equiaffine differential geometry

We now introduce classical notions from equiaffine geometry and some notations that will be used to prove Theorem
1.4
in the next two sections.

From here on, let us suppose the
n
-dimensional vector space
V
endowed with a non-trivial alternate multilinear
n
-form, which we denote by
\det
.
Let
M\subset V
be a hypersurface and take a vector field
\Xi
transverse to
M
. Then, for every pair of tangent vector fields
X,Y
on
M
, we can decompose the flat connection
\nabla
of
V
as

(4.1)

\nabla_{X}Y=\nabla^{M}_{X}Y+g(X,Y)\cdot\Xi

where
\nabla^{M}
is an affine torsion-free connection on
M
and
g
is a field of symmetric bilinear forms. The hypersurface
M
is said to be non-degenerate if
g
is nowhere degenerate, a condition that does not depend on
\Xi
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
Nomizu & Sasaki 1994
]
, Ch.II, Thm.3.1)
.

For each non-degenerate oriented hypersurface
M
, there is a unique transversal vector field
\,\Xi
, called equiaffine normal field or Blaschke’s normal field, such that

i)

\nabla_{v}\Xi\in T_{x}M
for every
x\in M
and
v\in T_{x}M,

ii)

the volume
(n-1)
-form
\alpha
associated to
g
satisfies

\alpha(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})=\det(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})

for every
x\in M
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}\in T_{x}M
.

The pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
and its volume form
\alpha
are called
equiaffine metric
and
equiaffine area measure
respectively. We assume from here on that
g
is Riemannian.

We will denote by
E=E(M)
the frame bundle of
M
whose fiber over
x\in M
is

E_{x}=\{(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})\in M\times V^{n-1}\colon\operatorname{span}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})=T_{x}M\}.

Given
(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})\in E
, items i) and ii) in Theorem
4.1
read

(4.2)

\det(\nabla\Xi,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})=0,\quad\det(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})^{2}=\det(g(\xi_{i},\xi_{j}))_{i,j}.

Definition 4.2
.

Given an oriented hypersurface
M\subset V
and
\xi\in E
, let for any
i,j=1,\ldots,n-1

L_{i,j}(\xi)\coloneqq\det(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}{X_{j}},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}),

where
X_{j}
is any tangent vector field with
{X_{j}}(x)=\xi_{j}
. It is easy to check that
L_{i,j}=L_{j,i}
and does not depend on
X_{j}
.
Let
L\in C^{\infty}(E)
be the function defined by

L(\xi)\coloneqq\det(L_{i,j}(\xi))_{i,j}.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
\xi=(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})\in E
we have

(4.3)

L(\xi)=\det(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})^{n+1}.

Proof.

We can assume
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}\}
to be positively oriented, as both sides of (
4.3
) are equally affected by a permutation. Indeed, the effect on the matrix
(L_{i,j})_{i,j}
of a transposition of
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1}
is a sign change of all entries
L_{i,j}
, and a simultaneous permutation of two lines and two columns.
From (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) we get

\displaystyle L_{k,l}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\det(\nabla_{\xi_{k}}X_{l},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})

(4.4)

\displaystyle=g(\xi_{k},\xi_{l})\det(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})

(4.5)

\displaystyle=g(\xi_{k},\xi_{l})\det(g(\xi_{i},\xi_{j}))_{i,j}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It follows using (
4.2
) again that

(4.6)

L(\xi)=\det(L_{k,l}(\xi))=\det(g(\xi_{k},\xi_{l}))_{k,l}^{1+\frac{n-1}{2}}=\det(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})^{n+1}.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

By (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), the equiaffine metric is simply given by

g(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})=|L(\xi)|^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}L_{i,j}(\xi).

In turn, the equiaffine normal vector can be obtained from
g
as
\Xi=\frac{1}{n-1}\Delta f
where
\Delta
is the Laplacian with respect to
g
and
f\colon M\to V
is the inclusion (cf.
[
Nomizu & Sasaki 1994
, Thm.6.5, Ch. II]
). We will not make use of this fact.

5.
Measure of lines in terms of hyperplanes

In this section we use the boundary sphere
\partial B
to construct a parametrization space for the oriented affine lines of the dual vector space in terms of intersections of affine hyperplanes. We then show how to rewrite the measure involved in Álvarez Paiva formula in this new space.

Recall that we have fixed some non-trivial alternate multilinear
n
-form det on the
n
-dimensional vector space
V
.

Let
B
be a convex body of
V
that contains the origin as an interior point, and suppose
\partial B
is smooth and positively curved. In particular its dual body is smooth, and the Legendre transform
{\LL}:\partial B\to\partial B^{*}

\ker\LL(x)=T_{x}\partial B\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\langle\LL(x),x\rangle=1,

\partial B

4.1

with the inward vector (i.e. as the boundary of

and

(see Definition

).

Consider a smooth local section

\displaystyle\zeta\colon U\subset\partial B

\displaystyle\to E(\partial B)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\zeta(x)=(x,\zeta_{1}(x),\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}(x)).

We define the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{n-1}\times U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(\partial B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{*}\times\partial B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{+}(V^{*})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

where

•

G_{+}(V^{*})
is the space of oriented affine lines in
V^{*}
introduced in section
2.3
;

•

for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{n-1}
and

•

is the affine line

•

for

•

\pi

It is easy to check that the previous diagram commutes.

Indeed observe that

and

for all

\pi\circ H(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})=p(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})+\langle{\LL}(x)\rangle_{+}=G(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}).

Recall that
G_{+}(V^{\ast})
is endowed with a symplectic form
\omega_{B^{\ast}}
(see section

). It will be convenient to consider on

the following associated volume element

\eta_{B^{\ast}}=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\omega_{B^{\ast}}^{n-1}

and take the corresponding orientation. Our goal in this section is to compute the pull-back form

. For this, we introduce the following
2
-forms.

Definition 5.1
.

Let

be the

-forms on

given by

{\omega_{i}}_{(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})}=-\det(d\pi_{i},d\pi_{i},\xi_{1}-\xi_{i},\ldots,\widehat{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}-\xi_{i}),

where

\displaystyle\pi_{i}:E(\partial B)

\displaystyle\to V

\displaystyle(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})

\displaystyle\mapsto\xi_{i}.

Using these

as follows.

Proposition 5.2
.

(5.1)

G^{*}\eta_{B^{\ast}}=\frac{(n-1)!}{L}\,\,\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1}.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of the proposition above.

5.1.
Technical lemmas

Let us begin with the following computation.

Lemma 5.3
.

(F^{\ast}G^{\ast}\eta_{B^{\ast}})_{(\lambda,x)}=\frac{(n-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{2}}\,\,d\lambda_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\lambda_{n-1}\wedge\zeta^{1}(x)\wedge\cdots\wedge\zeta^{n-1}(x)

where
\{\LL(x),\zeta^{1}(x),\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}(x)\}
is the dual basis of
\{x,\zeta_{1}(x),\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}(x)\}
.

Proof.

Note first that

F^{\ast}G^{\ast}\omega_{B^{\ast}}=F^{\ast}H^{\ast}\pi^{\ast}\omega_{B^{\ast}}=F^{\ast}H^{\ast}\omega_{V^{*}}

where we have abusively denoted
i^{\ast}\omega_{V^{\ast}}
by
\omega_{V^{\ast}}
(here
i
stands for the canonical inclusion
V^{\ast}\times\partial B\hookrightarrow V^{\ast}\times V
).

Fixing
x_{0}\in U
we write

\omega_{V^{*}}=x_{0}\wedge\LL(x_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\zeta_{i}(x_{0})\wedge\zeta^{i}(x_{0})

globally on
V^{*}\times V
.

Since
H\circ F(\lambda,x)=(p\circ F(\lambda,x),x)=(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\zeta^{i}(x)/\lambda_{i},x)
we have

d(H\circ F)_{(\lambda,x)}\left({\partial}/{\partial\lambda_{i}}\right)=\left(-\zeta^{i}(x)/{\lambda_{i}^{2}},0\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad d(H\circ F)_{(\lambda,x)}(\zeta_{i}(x))=(\ast,\zeta_{i}(x)).

Thus, modulo terms of the form
\zeta^{i}(x_{0})\wedge\zeta^{j}(x_{0})
,

\displaystyle(F^{*}H^{*}\omega_{V^{*}})_{(\lambda,x_{0})}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\omega_{V^{*}}(d(H\circ F)_{(\lambda,x_{0})}\left({\partial}/{\partial\lambda_{i}}\right),d(H\circ F)_{(\lambda,x_{0})}(\zeta_{i}(x_{0})))\,d\lambda_{i}\wedge\zeta^{i}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\,d\lambda_{i}\wedge\zeta^{i}(x_{0}).

The statement follows.
∎

Secondly we prove the following identity.

Lemma 5.4
.

F^{\ast}(\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1})_{(\lambda,x)}=L(\zeta(x))\,\,\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{n-1}\right)\,\,d\lambda_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\lambda_{n-1}\wedge\zeta^{1}(x)\wedge\cdots\wedge\zeta^{n-1}(x).

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in U
and put
\xi_{i}=\zeta_{i}(x_{0})
. Considering
E(\partial B)\subset V\times V^{n-1}
we have

\displaystyle dF_{(\lambda,x_{0})}\left(\partial/\partial\lambda_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=(0\,;\,0,\ldots,0,\xi_{j},0,\ldots,0),

and

\displaystyle dF_{(\lambda,x_{0})}(\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=(\xi_{j}\,;\,\lambda_{1}(d\zeta_{1})_{x_{0}}(\xi_{j}),\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}(d\zeta_{n-1})_{x_{0}}(\xi_{j})).

Thus

F^{*}\omega_{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{k}}\right)=0

for any

and

F^{*}\omega_{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}},\xi_{k}\right)=0

for any

such that
i\neq j
, while

\displaystyle F^{*}\omega_{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}},\xi_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=-\det(\xi_{i},\lambda_{i}(d\zeta_{i})_{x_{0}}(\xi_{j}),\lambda_{1}\xi_{1}-\lambda_{i}\xi_{i},\ldots,\widehat{\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}-\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}\xi_{n-1}-\lambda_{i}\xi_{i})

\displaystyle=-\det(\xi_{i},\lambda_{i}(d\zeta_{i})_{x_{0}}(\xi_{j}),\lambda_{1}\xi_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}\xi_{n-1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+1}\,\,\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}\right)\,\,\det(\nabla_{\xi_{j}}\zeta_{i},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}),

that is

F^{*}\omega_{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}},\xi_{j}\right)=(-1)^{i+1}\,\,\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}\right)\,\,L_{j,i}(x_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}).

Therefore, putting
L_{j,i}=L_{j,i}(x_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})
and

, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-10.00002ptF^{\ast}(\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1})_{(\lambda,x_{0})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-1}\,\,(-1)^{\frac{(n-1)n}{2}}\,\,\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{n-1}\right)\,\,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}L_{j,1}d\lambda_{1}\wedge\xi^{j}\right)\wedge\cdots\wedge\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}L_{j,n-1}d\lambda_{n-1}\wedge\xi^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{n-1}\right)\,\,d\lambda_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\lambda_{n-1}\wedge\left(\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n-1}}L_{j_{1},1}\cdots L_{j_{n-1,n-1}}\xi^{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi^{j_{n-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{n-1}\right)\,\,\det(L_{i,j})_{i,j}\,\,d\lambda_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\lambda_{n-1}\wedge\xi^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi^{n-1}

from which we get the announced formula.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

Let us denote

\Omega\coloneqq\frac{(n-1)!}{L}\,\,\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1}.

The proposition will follow easily after proving that

;

(2)

for all

.

Indeed, given

we can take a local section

of

such that

. In particular the map

associated to this section satisfies that

. Then we observe that

\operatorname{im}dF_{((1,\ldots,1),x)}\oplus\ker dG_{\xi}=T_{\xi}E(\partial B),

because

is a diffeomorphism according to Lemma

. Equality (

Let us check point (1).

Hence, by Lemma

we have

\displaystyle F^{\ast}\Omega

In order to check point (2), we easily verify that

Next we see that if

6.
First variation of the dual area under translations

Recall that we have chosen some non-trivial alternate multilinear
n
-form denoted by
\det

is a convex body containing the origin in its interior and has smooth and positively curved boundary (i.e. the normed vector space

.

In this section we prove Theorem

6.1.
Preliminaries

Let us begin with the following simple fact.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\xi=(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})\in E(\partial B)
and

. The affine line

is contained in the affine hyperplane

if and only if

belongs to
\mathrm{aff}(\xi)
, the affine hull of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}
.

Proof.

Recall that

G(\xi)=\{p\in V^{*}\colon\langle\xi_{1},p\rangle=\cdots=\langle\xi_{n-1},p\rangle=1\}=q+\langle\LL(x)\rangle

for some

. Hence

if and only if

and
\langle q,y\rangle=1
. This is equivalent to

with
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=\langle q,y\rangle=1
, which means that
y
is in the affine hull of the
\xi_{i}
.
∎

Given

, let
E_{v}
denote the set of
\xi\in E(\partial B)
such that
-v\notin\mathrm{aff}(\xi)
. Let
\overline{T}_{v}\colon E_{v}\to E(\partial B)
be given by

\overline{T}_{v}(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})=(x_{v},\xi_{1}+v,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}+v).

Here

is uniquely determined by the conditions that
\xi_{i}+v\in T_{x_{v}}\partial B
for all

and that the basis
\{x_{v},\xi_{1}+v,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}+v\}
is negatively oriented. Note that

ensures that
\xi_{1}+v,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}+v
are linearly independent.

Let
T_{v}\colon G(E_{v})\rightarrow G_{+}(V^{*})
be defined by

T_{v}(G(\xi))=G(\overline{T}_{v}(\xi)).

To prove that this is independent of
\xi
, assume
G(\xi)=G(\xi^{\prime})
for some
\xi^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},\xi_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}^{\prime})
. By Lemma

, each
\xi_{i}^{\prime}
is in the affine hull of
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}
.
Since
\xi_{i}^{\prime}+v
is clearly also in the affine hull of the
\xi_{j}+v
, we get by Lemma
6.1
that
G(\overline{T}_{v}(\xi))=G(\overline{T}_{v}(\xi^{\prime}))
up to orientation.
Since
v\mapsto x_{v}
is continuous, the orientations of
G(\overline{T}_{v}(\xi))
and
G(\overline{T}_{v}(\xi^{\prime}))
must agree or disagree for all
v
; and they coincide for
v=0
. This shows that
T_{v}
is well-defined.

Lemma 6.2
.

For
v\in V
and
L\in G_{+}(V^{*})
we have

(6.1)

L\cap(K-v)^{\ast}\neq\emptyset\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L\in G(E_{v})\mbox{ and }T_{v}(L)\cap K^{\ast}\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

Given
\zeta=(x,\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n-1})\in E(\partial B)
and a convex body
Q
in
V
, note that
G(\zeta)\cap Q^{\ast}\neq\emptyset
if and only if every hyperplane containing
G(\zeta)
intersects
Q^{*}
. By Lemma
6.1
this is equivalent to the fact that
H_{y}\cap Q^{\ast}\neq\emptyset
for every
y
in the affine hull of
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n-1}
. In turn, this is equivalent to
y\notin Q
for all such
y
.

Let now
L=G(\xi)
. Taking
\zeta=\xi
and
Q=K-v
we get that the left hand side of (
6.1
) holds if and only if
y\notin K-v
for every
y\in\mathrm{aff}(\xi)
. Since
0\in\operatorname{int}(K)
, this is equivalent to the fact that
-v\notin\mathrm{aff}(\xi)
and
z\notin K
for all
z\in\mathrm{aff}(\overline{T}_{v}(\xi))
.
Taking
\zeta=\overline{T}_{v}(\xi)
and
Q=K
this condition is equivalent to the right hand side of (
6.1
).
∎

Note that, by the first part of the previous proof, if
G(\xi)\cap K^{*}\neq\emptyset
and
v\in K
, then
\xi\in E_{-v}
. Then, by Proposition
2.1
and Lemma

, for
tv\in\operatorname{int}(K)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}(\partial(K-tv)^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{\{L\in G(E_{tv})\colon T_{tv}(L)\cap{K^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset\}}\eta_{B^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{\{T_{-tv}(L)\colon L\in G_{+}(V^{*}),L\cap{K^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset\}}\eta_{B^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!\varepsilon_{n-1}}\int_{\{L\in G_{+}(V^{*})\colon L\cap{K^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset\}}T_{-tv}^{\ast}\,\eta_{B^{\ast}}.

Consider the vector field
Z_{v}
on
E(\partial B)
given by

Z_{v}(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\overline{T}_{tv}(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}),

and recall that

G^{*}\eta_{B^{\ast}}=\Omega=\frac{(n-1)!}{L}\,\,\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1}.

Let now
\{U_{i}\}
be a finite cover of
\partial B
, take a local section
\zeta_{i}
of each
E(U_{i})
, and let
F_{i}\colon(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{n-1}\times U_{i}\to E(\partial B)
be the corresponding map. Since
G\circ F_{i}
is a diffeomorphism onto its image, taking a partition of unity
\rho_{i}
subordinate to

, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}(\partial(K-tv)^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!\varepsilon_{n-1}}\sum_{i}\int_{(G\circ F_{i})^{-1}(\{L\colon L\cap{K^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset\})}\rho_{i}\cdot F_{i}^{\ast}G^{\ast}T_{-tv}^{\ast}\,\eta_{B^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!\varepsilon_{n-1}}\sum_{i}\int_{(G\circ F_{i})^{-1}(\{L\colon L\cap{K^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset\})}\rho_{i}\cdot F_{i}^{\ast}\overline{T}_{-tv}^{\ast}\,\Omega

and we deduce that

Proposition 6.3
.

For any
\xi=(x,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})\in E(\partial B)
and any
v\in V

\LL_{Z_{v}}\Omega=-(n-1)!\,\,\frac{\LL_{Z_{v}}L}{L^{2}}\,\,\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1}

and

\LL_{Z_{v}}L(\xi)=(n+1)\,\,(\det(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}))^{n}\,\,\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}{\det(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i-1},v,\xi_{i+1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})}\right)

where
\Xi
is the interior equiaffine normal field of
\partial B
.

Proof.

The first equality follows from
\LL_{Z_{v}}\omega_{i}=0
. As for the second, by Proposition
4.3
we have

L(\overline{T}_{v}(\xi))=L(x_{tv},\xi_{1}+tv,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}+tv)=\det(\Xi(x_{tv}),\xi_{1}+tv,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}+tv)^{n+1}.

Hence

\LL_{Z_{v}}L(\xi)=(n+1)\,\,\det(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})^{n}\,\,\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\det(\Xi(x_{tv}),\xi_{1}+tv,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}+tv).

The proposition follows by noting that
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Xi(x_{tv})=\nabla_{X}\Xi(x)
with
X=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}x_{tv}
which according to the first identity of (
4.2
) implies that

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\det(\Xi(x_{tv}),\xi_{1}+tv,\ldots,\xi_{n-1}+tv)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\det\left(\Xi(x),\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i-1},v,\xi_{i+1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1}\right).

∎

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Take a local section
\zeta\colon U\to E(\partial B)
mapping each
x\in U
to a positive orthonormal basis
\{e_{1}(x),\ldots,e_{n-1}(x)\}
of
T_{x}\partial B
with respect to the Riemannian metric
g
. For all
x\in U
let
\{e^{1}(x),\ldots,e^{n-1}(x),\Xi^{*}(x)\}
denote the dual basis of
\{e_{1}(x),\ldots,e_{n-1}(x),\Xi(x)\}
. Since
\ker\Xi^{*}(x)=\operatorname{span}(e_{1}(x),\ldots,e_{n-1}(x))
, we have
\Xi^{*}(x)\in\langle\LL(x)\rangle
. Take the smooth measure
\mu=e^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{n-1}
on
U
. For each
x\in U
the dual measure
\nu_{x}
on
\ker\Xi(x)\simeq T^{*}_{x}\partial B
of the Lebesgue measure
\mu_{x}
is given by
\nu_{x}=e_{1}(x)\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n-1}(x)
.

First observe that if
F
denotes the map corresponding to
\zeta
we have

\displaystyle L\circ F(\lambda,x)

\displaystyle=L(x,\lambda_{1}e_{1}(x),\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}e_{n-1}(x))

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{n+1}\right)\,\,\det(\Xi(x),e_{1}(x),\ldots,e_{n-1}(x))^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{n+1}\right)

using the fact that
\{e_{1}(x),\ldots,e_{n-1}(x)\}
is a positive orthonormal basis together with point ii) in Theorem
4.1
, and that similarly

\LL_{Z_{v}}L\circ F(\lambda,x)=(n+1)\,\,\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{n+1}\right)\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\langle e^{i}(x),v\rangle

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the canonical duality pairing.
Furthermore by Lemma
5.4
the following holds:

F^{\ast}(\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1})_{(\lambda,x)}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{n-1}\right)\,\,d\lambda_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\lambda_{n-1}\wedge e^{1}(x)\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{n-1}(x).

Therefore, using Proposition
6.3
, we get the following formula:

F^{*}(\LL_{Z_{v}}\Omega)_{(\lambda,x)}=-\frac{(n+1)!}{n}\,\,\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}e^{i}(x),v\right\rangle\,\,\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\right)\,\,d\lambda_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\lambda_{n-1}\wedge d\mu(x).

Let us define the map

\displaystyle p_{x}:(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{n-1}

\displaystyle\to\ker\Xi(x)\subset V^{\ast}

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}e^{i}(x)

which is a diffeomorphism onto its image that satisfies

G\circ F(\lambda,x)=p_{x}(\lambda)+\langle\LL(x)\rangle_{+}.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle(G\circ F)^{-1}(\{L\colon L\cap{K^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset\})

\displaystyle=\{(\lambda,x)\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{n-1}\times U\colon G\circ F(\lambda,x)\cap{K^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset\}

\displaystyle=\{(\lambda,x)\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})^{n-1}\times U\colon p_{x}(\lambda)\in\pi_{x}({K^{\ast}})\}

where
\pi_{x}\colon V^{*}\to\ker\Xi(x)
is the linear projection with
\ker\pi_{x}=\langle\LL(x)\rangle
.

A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle(p^{-1}_{x})_{*}\,\nu_{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\right)\,\,d\lambda_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\lambda_{n-1}

as measures. Therefore

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\int_{(G\circ F)^{-1}(\{L\colon L\cap{K^{\ast}}\neq\emptyset\})}F^{*}\LL_{Z_{v}}\Omega

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n+1)!}{n}\int_{x\in U}\left(\int_{\lambda\in p_{x}^{-1}(\pi_{x}(K^{\ast}))}\langle p_{x}(\lambda),v\rangle\,\,d(p^{-1}_{x})_{*}(\nu_{x})\right)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n+1)!}{n}\int_{x\in U}\left(\int_{q\in\pi_{x}(K^{\ast})}\,\,\langle q,v\rangle\,d\nu_{x}(q)\right)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n+1)!}{n}\left\langle\int_{x\in U}\left(\int_{\pi_{x}(K^{\ast})}\,\,q\,d\nu_{x}(q)\right)\,d\mu(x),v\right\rangle.

Covering
\partial B
with local sections and using (
6.2
), we deduce the stated formula:

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\mathscr{A}_{B^{\ast}}(\partial(K-tv)^{\ast})=\frac{n+1}{\varepsilon_{n-1}}\left\langle\int_{x\in\partial B}\left(\int_{\pi_{x}(K^{\ast})}\,\,q\,d\nu_{x}(q)\right)\,d\mu(x),v\right\rangle.\qquad\qquad\square

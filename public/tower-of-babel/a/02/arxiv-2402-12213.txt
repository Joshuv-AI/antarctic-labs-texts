Representation formulas and far-field behavior of time-periodic incompressible viscous flow around a translating rigid body
Thomas Eiter, Ana Leonor Silvestre

Abstract
This paper is concerned with integral representations and asymptotic expansions of solutions to the time-periodic incompressible Navier-Stokes equations for fluid flow in the exterior of a rigid body that moves with constant velocity. Using the time-periodic Oseen fundamental solution, we derive representation formulas for solutions with suitable regularity. From these formulas, the decomposition of the velocity component of the fundamental solution into steady-state and purely periodic parts and their detailed decay rate in space, we deduce complete information on the asymptotic structure of the velocity and pressure fields.

Introduction

We are interested in time-periodic incompressible viscous flow around a translating rigid body
{\mathcal{O}}
. In a reference frame attached to the solid, the velocity
v=v(t,x)
and pressure
p=p(t,x)
of the fluid satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v-\nu\Delta v+\nabla p-\zeta\cdot\nabla v&=f&&\textrm{in }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\nabla\cdot v&=0&&\textrm{in }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega,\\
\displaystyle v&=v_{b}&&\textrm{on }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Sigma,\\
\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}v(t,x)&=0&&\textrm{for }t\in{\mathbb{T}},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is an exterior domain and
{\mathbb{T}}:={\raisebox{1.00006pt}{${\mathbb{R}}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{${\mathcal{T}}{\mathbb{Z}}$}\right.}
is the torus group related to the period
{\mathcal{T}}>0
of the motion. The fluid has constant viscosity
\nu>0
and is subject to a
{\mathcal{T}}
-periodic external force
f
.
The body
{\mathcal{O}}
undergoes a translational motion with constant velocity
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
,
and the fluid motion on its surface
\Sigma:=\partial\Omega
is prescribed via

{\mathcal{T}}
-periodic boundary values
v_{b}
,
which may describe a periodic boundary motion or flux through the boundary.

The stationary counterpart of (
1.1
) is a classical mathematical problem with well-known properties, which have been studied, for example, in
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
1
,
15
,
16
]
. In particular, the velocity field exhibits different asymptotic behavior inside and outside a wake region created by the translational motion of
\mathcal{O}
. This behavior is inherited from the steady Oseen fundamental solution. From the point of view of applications, a precise description of the far-field behavior of the flow can be very useful in numerical studies, for which a bounded computational domain is defined, and consequently, appropriate boundary conditions must be imposed on the outer boundary of the truncated domain. Such boundary conditions should be consistent with the asymptotic decay of solutions, as was shown in
[
2
,
3
]
.

The study of the time-periodic exterior problem (
1.1
) is more recent, see
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
23
,
24
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
22
,
9
,
12
]
for instance. In this paper, we exploit the mathematical properties of time-periodic Oseen fundamental solution, based on the results of
[
26
,
11
,
10
]
, wherein a modern mathematical setting was developed for the time-periodic Stokes and Oseen problems and extended to the exterior Navier-Stokes equations. The first step of our analysis is the integral representation obtained in Theorem
3.2
for
(v,p)
, where we followed
[
30
]
. Then, employing the decomposition of the velocity component of the fundamental solution into steady-state and purely periodic contributions, we deduce complete asymptotic expansions for the steady and purely periodic parts of the velocity and the pressure fields, the main results being stated in Theorem
3.3
.

Comparing our results with the cases
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, see
[
6
,
11
,
10
]
, or with the simpler case
v_{b}=\zeta
, addressed for example in
[
8
]
, we found that the
time-periodic boundary data
v_{b}

significantly influence the far-field behavior
of the velocity and of the pressure of the flow.
More precisely, in Theorem
3.6
we show that, if the total flux through the boundary is time-independent, the purely periodic part of the velocity as well as the pressure show a faster decay rate, the same as for the whole space problem and the corresponding fundamental solutions.
However, if the flux is time-dependent,
both quantities decay at a slower rate.

Structure of the paper.
Section
2
contains the notations for functions spaces, differential operators and distributions that are relevant for the mathematical formulation and analysis of the problem. We also recall the steady and time-periodic Oseen fundamental solutions and some results for their convolution with suitably decaying functions,
and we prepare a general result on estimates for certain convolution integrals.
In Section
3
, we present and discuss the main results of the paper. The linearized time-periodic problem is addressed in Section
4
. Firstly, we deduce integral representation formulas for the velocity and pressure of the linear problem using the time-periodic Oseen fundamental solution. Subsequently, a detailed analysis of the far-field behavior of solutions is carried out, exploiting the decomposition of the velocity fields into a time-independent part and a time-dependent part with mean value zero. The proofs of our main results are given in Section
5
.

2.2
Steady-state Stokes and Oseen fundamental solutions

It is well known that

{\mathrm{E}}(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi|x|}

(2.2)

is the fundamental solution of the Laplace operator in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, that is,
-\Delta{\mathrm{E}}=\delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

The fundamental solution of the classical Stokes system in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is the pair
(\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{0}^{\mathrm{ste}},{\mathrm{P}})\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{3\times 3}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{3}
given by (see, for example,
[
21
]
)

(\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{0}^{\mathrm{ste}},{\mathrm{P}})(x)=\left(\frac{1}{8\pi\nu|x|}\left({\mathds{I}}+\hat{x}\otimes\hat{x}\right),\frac{1}{4\pi|x|^{2}}\hat{x}\right).

In particular, the pressure component satisfies

{\mathrm{P}}(x)=-\nabla{\mathrm{E}}(x),

(2.3)

and one directly
deduces the estimate

\forall\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{3}\quad\exists C>0\quad\forall x\neq 0:\quad\big\lvert D^{\alpha}_{x}{\mathrm{P}}(x)\big\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-2-\lvert\alpha\rvert}.

(2.4)

The fundamental solution

(\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}},{\mathrm{P}})\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{3\times 3}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})^{3}

of the 3D Oseen system has the same pressure part (
2.3
),
and the velocity component is given by (see
[
21
,
16
,
30
]
)

\displaystyle\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi\nu|x|}\exp\left(-\frac{{s}_{\zeta}(x)}{\nu}\right){\mathds{I}}-\frac{1-\exp(-{s}_{\zeta}(x)/\nu)}{8\pi|x|{s}_{\zeta}(x)}\left({\mathds{I}}-\hat{x}\otimes\hat{x}\right)\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

(2.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{|\zeta|}{16\pi}\frac{1-\exp(-{s}_{\zeta}(x)/\nu)-\exp(-{s}_{\zeta}(x)/\nu){s}_{\zeta}(x)/\nu}{{s}_{\zeta}(x)^{2}}\left(\hat{x}+\hat{\zeta}\right)\otimes\left(\hat{x}+\hat{\zeta}\right),

where
{s}_{\zeta}(x):=\left[|\zeta||x|+(\zeta\cdot x)\right]/2.

It is subject to the pointwise estimate (see
[
16
]
)

\forall\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{3}\quad\forall\varepsilon>0\quad\exists C>0\quad\forall\lvert x\rvert\geq\varepsilon:\quad\big\lvert D^{\alpha}_{x}\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}(x)\big\rvert\leq C\big[\lvert x\rvert\big(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\big)\big]^{-1-\lvert\alpha\rvert/2}.

(2.6)

Observe that this estimate is anisotropic in space.
In particular, the decay rate depends on the direction
\zeta
,
and reflects the occurrence of a wake region in the flow behind the body. Hence, there is a significant difference between the velocity components of the Stokes and Oseen fundamental solutions.

The following lemma yields estimates on convolutions of
\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}

with functions with suitable decay. Throughout this work we will use the notation

\log_{+}(r)\coloneqq\max\{1,\log r\}\text{ for }r>0.

(2.7)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
A\in[2,\infty)
,
B\in[0,\infty)
,
M>0

such that

\lvert g(x)\rvert\leq M(1+\lvert x\rvert)^{-A}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-B}.

Then there exists

C=C(A,B,\zeta)>0

with the following properties:

1.

If
A+\min\{1,B\}>3
, then

\big\lvert\lvert\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}\rvert\ast g(x)\big\rvert\leq CM\big[(1+\lvert x\rvert)\big(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\big)\big]^{-1}.

2.

If
A+\min\{1,B\}>3
and
A+B\geq 7/2
, then

\big\lvert\lvert\nabla\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}\rvert\ast g(x)\big\rvert\leq CM\big[(1+\lvert x\rvert)\big(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\big)\big]^{-3/2}.

3.

If
A+\min\{1,B\}=3
and
A+B\geq 7/2
, then

\big\lvert\lvert\nabla\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}\rvert\ast g(x)\big\rvert\leq CM\big[(1+\lvert x\rvert)\big(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\big)\big]^{-3/2}\log_{+}\lvert x\rvert.

4.

If
A+B<3
, then

\big\lvert\lvert\nabla\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}\rvert\ast g(x)\big\rvert\leq CM(1+\lvert x\rvert)^{-(A+B)/2}\big(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x)\big)^{-(A+B-1)/2}.

Proof.

These are special cases of
[
25
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2]
,
see also
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

We next provide similar convolution estimates
for
\nabla{\mathrm{P}}
in a particular case.
However, since
\nabla{\mathrm{P}}
is strongly singular at the origin,
we exclude a neighborhood
from the domain of integration.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
M>0

such that

\lvert g(x)\rvert\leq M\big((1+\lvert x\rvert)(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))\big)^{-2}.

Let
R>0
and
\chi:=\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus B_{R}}
be the characteristic function
of the exterior to the ball
B_{R}
centered at the origin.
Then there exists

C=C(R,\zeta)>0

such that

\big\lvert\big(\chi\nabla{\mathrm{P}}\big)\ast g(x)\big\rvert\leq CM\lvert x\rvert^{-2}\min\big\{1,(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-2}\log_{+}\lvert x\rvert+\lvert x\rvert^{-1}\log_{+}\lvert x\rvert+(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-1}\big\}.

Proof.

We use (
2.4
)
and the decay of
g
to estimate

\displaystyle I(x)\coloneqq\big\lvert\big(\chi\nabla{\mathrm{P}}\big)\ast g(x)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus B_{R}(x)}\lvert x-y\rvert^{-3}\big((1+\lvert y\rvert)(1+{s}_{\zeta}(y))\big)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq CM\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{-3}\big((1+\lvert y\rvert)(1+{s}_{\zeta}(y))\big)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Following the calculations in
[
25
, Section 2]
,
we obtain

\displaystyle I(x)\leq CM\big(\lvert x\rvert^{-2}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-2}\log_{+}\lvert x\rvert+\lvert x\rvert^{-3}\log_{+}\lvert x\rvert+\lvert x\rvert^{-2}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-1}\big).

Alternatively, we can omit the anisotropic contributions
and directly estimate

I(x)\leq CM\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{-3}(1+\lvert y\rvert)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq CM\lvert x\rvert^{-2},

which gives an improved estimate along

\{x\in\Omega\,|\,{s}_{\zeta}(x)=0\}=\{x\in\Omega\,|\,x=\alpha\zeta,\,\alpha>0\}
.
In summary, we conclude the asserted estimate.
∎

2.3
Time-periodic fundamental solutions

In the time-periodic case, the fundamental solutions can be written as solutions to a system of partial differential equations on the group
G={\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Following
[
26
,
11
,
10
]
, the fundamental solution of the Stokes (
\zeta=0
) or Oseen (
\zeta\not=0
) equations is a pair
(\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta},{\mathrm{Q}})\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(G)^{3\times 3}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(G)^{3}
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}-\nu\Delta\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}+\nabla{\mathrm{Q}}-(\zeta\cdot\nabla)\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}&={\mathds{I}}\delta_{G}&&\text{in }G,\\
\nabla\cdot\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}&=0&&\text{in }G.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.8)

The pressure component is given by

{\mathrm{Q}}=\delta_{{\mathbb{T}}}\otimes{\mathrm{P}},

(2.9)

that is, we formally have
{\mathrm{Q}}(t,x)=\delta_{{\mathbb{T}}}(t){\mathrm{P}}(x)
,
where
\mathrm{P}
is the pressure of the fundamental solution in the steady cases, given in (
2.3
).
The velocity component can be split in the form

\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}=1_{{\mathbb{T}}}\otimes\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}+\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}},

(2.10)

with
\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}
the velocity steady part of the fundamental solution, already presented in the previous section, and
\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}
the purely periodic part of
\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}
,
which is given by

\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{G}\left[\frac{1-\delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(k)}{|\xi|^{2}+i\left(\frac{2\pi k}{\mathcal{T}}-\zeta\cdot\xi\right)}\left({\mathds{I}}-\hat{\xi}\otimes\hat{\xi}\right)\right],

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{G}:{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(G)\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\hat{G})
,
\hat{G}:={\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, is the Fourier transform on the group
G
.
For details on the definition of the Fourier transform on
G
and the spaces
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\hat{G})

of tempered distributions,
we refer to
[
11
]
.
We recall the following decay properties of
\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
. Then

\forall\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{3}\quad\forall\varepsilon>0\quad\exists C>0\quad\forall\lvert x\rvert\geq\varepsilon:\quad\big\lVert D^{\alpha}_{x}\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}(\cdot,x)\big\rVert_{L^{q}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3-\lvert\alpha\rvert}.

(2.11)

Proof.

See
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

In comparison with the pointwise estimate (
2.6
)
for the steady-state fundamental solution,
the estimate (
2.11
) is of higher order and homogeneous in space.
Therefore, the occurrence of a wake region behind the body
is mainly reflected in the steady-state part of the velocity field.

The following lemma can be used to derive pointwise estimates
for convolutions of the fundamental solution
\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}

and suitably decaying functions.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
A,M\in(0,\infty)

such that

\lvert g(t,x)\rvert\leq M(1+\lvert x\rvert)^{-A}.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists

C=C(A,\zeta,\mathcal{T},\varepsilon)>0

such that

\forall\lvert x\rvert\geq\varepsilon:\quad\big\lvert\left[\lvert\nabla\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}\rvert\ast_{G}g\right](t,x)\big\rvert\leq CM(1+\lvert x\rvert)^{-\min\{A,4\}}

(2.12)

and, if
A>3
,

\forall\lvert x\rvert\geq\varepsilon:\quad\big\lvert\left[\lvert\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}\rvert\ast_{G}g\right](t,x)\big\rvert\leq CM(1+\lvert x\rvert)^{-3}.

(2.13)

Proof.

See
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
.
∎

2.4
Anisotropic convolution estimates

In the next lemma we study convolutions of functions with anisotropic decay, which is expressed by
{s}_{\zeta}(x):=\left[|\zeta||x|+(\zeta\cdot x)\right]/2
. The characteristic function of the exterior domain
\Omega
will be denoted by
\chi_{\Omega}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}

and
\phi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}))

such that for some
\varepsilon>0
,
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
and
p\in(1,\infty]

\exists C>0\quad\forall\lvert x\rvert\geq\varepsilon:\quad\lVert\phi(\cdot,x)\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}.

Let
R>0
such that
\Sigma\subset B_{R}
.
Let
p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
, where
\infty^{\prime}:=1
,
and let
f
be given by

1.

f=\hat{f}\chi_{\Omega}
with

\hat{f}\in L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega)
,

\supp\hat{f}\subset{\mathbb{T}}\times B_{R}
, or

2.

f=\hat{f}\delta_{\Sigma}
with

\hat{f}\in L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Sigma)
,

and define,

\Lambda(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\hat{f}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y&\text{if }f=\hat{f}\chi_{\Omega},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\hat{f}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y)&\text{if }f=\hat{f}\delta_{\Sigma},\end{cases}\qquad\Xi(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}y\hat{f}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y&\text{if }f=\hat{f}\chi_{\Omega},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}y\hat{f}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y)&\text{if }f=\hat{f}\delta_{\Sigma},\end{cases}

and

M\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lVert\hat{f}\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega)}&\text{if }f=\hat{f}\chi_{\Omega},\\
\displaystyle\lVert\hat{f}\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Sigma)}&\text{if }f=\hat{f}\delta_{\Sigma}.\end{cases}

For
S>R+\varepsilon
there exists
C=C(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon,R,S,\zeta)>0
such that

\forall\lvert x\rvert\geq S:\quad\lvert[\phi\ast_{G}f](t,x)\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M.

If
\psi\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}))

is such that

\lvert\nabla\psi\rvert=\phi
,
then
there is
C=C(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon,R,S,\zeta)>0
such that

\forall\lvert x\rvert\geq S:\quad\big\lvert[\psi\ast_{G}f](t,x)-\big[\psi(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\Lambda\big](t)\big\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M.

If
\xi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\})

such that

\lvert\nabla^{2}\xi\rvert=\phi
,
then
there is
C=C(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon,R,S,\zeta)>0
such that

\forall\lvert x\rvert\geq S:\quad\big\lvert[\xi\ast_{G}f](t,x)-\big[\xi(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\Lambda\big](t)+\big[\nabla\xi(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\Xi\big](t)\big\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M.

In particular, if
\psi
,
\xi
and
f
are time-independent,
then also
\Lambda
and
\Xi
are time-independent and the previous estimates reduce to

\displaystyle\lvert[\phi\ast_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f](x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M,

\displaystyle\big\lvert[\psi\ast_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f](x)-\Lambda\psi(x)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M,

\displaystyle\big\lvert[\xi\ast_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f](x)-\Lambda\xi(x)+\Xi\cdot\nabla\xi(x)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M.

Proof.

We only treat the case
f=\hat{f}\chi_{\Omega}
here.
For
f=\hat{f}\delta_{\Sigma}
we can proceed along the same lines
by replacing volume integrals over
\Omega

with boundary integrals over
\Sigma
.

For
\lvert x\rvert\geq S>R+\varepsilon\geq R\geq\lvert y\rvert
and
\theta\in[0,1]

we have

\displaystyle\lvert x-\theta y\rvert\geq\lvert x\rvert-\theta\lvert y\rvert

\displaystyle\geq(1-\theta R/S)\lvert x\rvert\geq(1-R/S)\lvert x\rvert\geq S-R>\varepsilon,

\displaystyle(1+2\lvert\zeta\rvert R)(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x-\theta y))

\displaystyle\geq 1+2\lvert\zeta\rvert\lvert\theta y\rvert+{s}_{\zeta}(x-\theta y)\geq 1+{s}_{\zeta}(x).

In particular, this yields

\lvert x\rvert^{\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{\beta}\leq C\lvert x-\theta y\rvert^{\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x-\theta y))^{\beta}
.
Since
\supp\hat{f}\subset B_{R}
,
we thus obtain

\displaystyle\big\lvert[\phi\ast_{G}f](t,x)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\lvert x-y\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x-y))^{-\beta}\,\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lvert\hat{f}(s,y)\rvert^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M.

Furthermore, we use the fundamental theorem of calculus to deduce

\displaystyle\big\lvert[\psi\ast_{G}f](t,x)

\displaystyle-\big[\psi(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\big]\Lambda(t)\big\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigg\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Omega}\big(\psi(t-s,x-y)-\psi(t-s,x)\big)\hat{f}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigg\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}y\cdot\nabla\psi(t-s,x-\theta y)\hat{f}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq CR\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\lvert x-\theta y\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x-\theta y))^{-\beta}\,\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lvert\hat{f}(s,y)\rvert^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M.

Similarly, from Taylor’s Theorem we deduce

\displaystyle\big\lvert[\xi\ast_{G}f](t,x)

\displaystyle-\big[\xi(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\Lambda(t)\big]+\big[\nabla\xi(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\Xi\big](t)\big\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigg\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)(y\otimes y):\nabla^{2}\xi(x-\theta y)\hat{f}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq CR^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\lvert x-\theta y\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x-\theta y))^{-\beta}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lvert\hat{f}(s,y)\rvert^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))^{-\beta}M,

which completes the proof.
∎

To formulate suitable integrability properties of solutions, we recall the

Assuming

in
{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Omega}\big[-v\cdot\partial_{t}\varphi+\nabla v:\nabla\varphi-\zeta\cdot\nabla v\cdot\varphi+(v\cdot\nabla v)\cdot\varphi\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

For the derivation of the asymptotic decay rates of

\displaystyle\exists\,\kappa,\rho\in(1,\infty)\text{ with }\frac{2}{\rho}+\frac{3}{\kappa}<1:

\displaystyle\quad{\mathcal{P}_{\bot}}v\in L^{\rho}({\mathbb{T}};L^{\kappa}(\Omega)^{3}),

\displaystyle\exists\,\kappa,\rho\in(1,\infty)\text{ with }\frac{2}{\rho}+\frac{3}{\kappa}<2:

\displaystyle\quad\nabla{\mathcal{P}_{\bot}}v\in L^{\rho}({\mathbb{T}};L^{\kappa}(\Omega)^{3\times 3}).

(3.2)

Both assumptions increase the regularity of weak solutions

[
7

it is of the same order as the decay of

Therefore, (

does not yield a proper asymptotic expansion for

since a leading-order term is not identified.
Instead, if one considers only the linear boundary contributions that appear in

for the leading term,
then one arrives at (

which properly identifies

.

Obviously,
the assumption of constant total flux is also satisfied

.
In this case we have

,
and from Theorem

see

19

1

.

In the previous theorems, the assumption that

its gradient

However, bondedness with respect to time does not follow for the pressure

The proofs of these results will be provided in Section

4
The linearized time-periodic problem

In the present section, we derive representation formulas and study the asymptotic behavior of the linearized time-periodic problem that is obtained from the Navier-Stokes equations when the convective term
v\cdot\nabla v=\nabla\cdot(v\otimes v)
is neglected or replaced with
\nabla\cdot{\mathds{F}}
for a tensor field
\mathds{F}
. At this stage,
{\mathds{F}}
is know and satisfies suitable summability properties.

4.1
Representation formulas for solutions

Assume
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is an exterior domain
with
C^{2}
-boundary. For the whole section, let
f\in L^{r}({\mathbb{T}};L^{q}(\Omega)^{3})
,
{\mathds{F}}\in L^{r}({\mathbb{T}};W^{1,q}(\Omega)^{3\times 3})
and
v_{b}\in T_{r,q}({\mathbb{T}}\times\Sigma)

for some
q\in(1,2)
and
r\in(1,\infty)
,
and consider a solution
(v,p)
to the (linear) time-periodic Oseen problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v-\nu\Delta v+\nabla p-\zeta\cdot\nabla v&=f-\nabla\cdot{\mathds{F}}&&\textrm{in }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot v&=0&&\textrm{in }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega,\\
v&=v_{b}&&\textrm{on }{\mathbb{T}}\times\Sigma,\\
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}v(t,x)&=0&&\textrm{for }t\in{\mathbb{T}}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

Note that the existence of a solution
(v,p)
with
v=v_{0}+v_{\perp}
and

\displaystyle v_{0}

\displaystyle\in D^{2,q}(\Omega)^{3}\cap L^{2q/(2-q)}(\Omega)^{3},

\displaystyle v_{\perp}

\displaystyle\in W^{1,r}({\mathbb{T}};L^{q}(\Omega)^{3})\cap L^{r}({\mathbb{T}};W^{2,q}(\Omega)^{3}),

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\in L^{r}({\mathbb{T}};D^{1,q}(\Omega))

was shown in
[
14
, Theorem 4.7]
.
Moreover, the velocity field
v
is unique in this class,
while the pressure
p
is unique up to addition with a space-independent function.

In order to derive the integral representation formulas for the velocity and pressure,
we first transform the exterior problem (
4.1
) to a whole-space problem. To do so, we extend the velocity and pressure to
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
in the following way:

\widetilde{v}(t,x):=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\\
v(t,x)&\textrm{if }x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}\qquad\widetilde{p}(t,x):=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\\
p(t,x)&\textrm{if }x\in\overline{\Omega}.\end{cases}

(4.2)

In a first step we assume
{\mathds{F}}\equiv 0
. The next lemma is proved along the same lines as in the steady case
[

]

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the above assumptions on the data
and if

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\widetilde{v}-\nu\Delta\widetilde{v}+\nabla\widetilde{p}-\zeta\cdot\nabla\widetilde{v}&=F+\nu\nabla g&&\text{in }G,\\

where

(4.4)

\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}=\chi_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v.

\displaystyle\zeta\cdot\nabla\widetilde{v}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\Omega}(\zeta\cdot\nabla v)-(\zeta\cdot n)v_{b}\delta_{\Sigma},

\displaystyle 2{\mathds{D}}(\widetilde{v})

\displaystyle=\nabla\widetilde{v}+(\nabla\widetilde{v})^{\top}=2\chi_{\Omega}{\mathds{D}}(v)-v_{b}\otimes n\delta_{\Sigma}-n\otimes v_{b}\delta_{\Sigma},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\mathds{T}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{p})=

\displaystyle+\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}\ast_{G}\left[{\mathds{T}}(v,p)n\delta_{\Sigma}-(n^{\top}{\mathds{F}})\delta_{\Sigma}\right]-\left[\nabla(\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}\mathsf{e}_{i})\ast_{G}\left(\chi_{\Omega}{\mathds{F}}\right)\right]\mathsf{e}_{i}.

From here onwards, the arguments are similar to those of Theorem

and lead to the representations (

) and (

).
∎

4.2
Asymptotic expansion of the velocity and pressure fields

First, we consider the case
{\mathds{F}}\equiv 0
. Here, we use the functions
\Phi
and
\Psi

defined in (

), which depend on the boundary data

.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(v,p)
be the solution of system (
4.1
) with
{\mathds{F}}\equiv 0
,
and such that
f
has compact support. The asymptotic expansion at infinity of the velocity field

v
is of the form

\begin{split}v(t,x)&=\big[\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{lin}}\big](t)-\Phi(t){\mathrm{P}}(x)+\Psi(t)\cdot\nabla{\mathrm{P}}(x)+{\mathcal{R}}(t,x),\end{split}

(4.17)

where

\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{lin}}(t)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}f(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\Sigma}\left[{\mathds{T}}(v,p)(t,y)n(y)+(\zeta\cdot n)(y)v_{b}(t,y)\right]\mathrm{d}S(y),

(4.18)

and there is
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert{D_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{R}}_{0}(x)\rvert\leq C\big[\lvert x\rvert\,(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))\big]^{-3/2-\lvert\alpha\rvert/2},\qquad\lvert{D_{x}^{\beta}\mathcal{R}}_{\perp}(t,x)\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-4-\lvert\beta\rvert}

(4.19)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{3}
with
\lvert\beta\rvert\leq 2
,
and all
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega

with
\lvert x\rvert
sufficiently large.
In particular, asymptotic expansions for the steady-state part
v_{0}
and the purely periodic part
v_{\perp}
of
v
are given by

\displaystyle v_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}(x)\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{lin}}_{0}-\Phi_{0}{\mathrm{P}}(x)+{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(x),

(4.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{\perp}(t,x)&=\big[\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{lin}}_{\perp}\big](t)-\Phi_{\perp}(t){\mathrm{P}}(x)+\Psi_{\perp}(t)\cdot\nabla{\mathrm{P}}(x)+{\mathcal{R}}_{\perp}(t,x).\end{split}

(4.21)

The asymptotic expansion at infinity of the pressure is of the form

p(t,x)=p_{\infty}(t)+\Phi^{\prime}(t){\mathrm{E}}(x)+\big[\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{lin}}(t)+\zeta\Phi(t)-\Psi^{\prime}(t)\big]\cdot{\mathrm{P}}(x)+{\mathfrak{R}}(t,x)

(4.22)

for some
p_{\infty}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})

and

\lvert D_{x}^{\alpha}{\mathfrak{R}_{0}}(x)\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3-\lvert\alpha\rvert},\qquad\lvert D_{x}^{\beta}{\mathfrak{R}_{\perp}}(t,x)\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3-\lvert\beta\rvert}

(4.23)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{3}
with
\lvert\beta\rvert\leq 1
,
and all
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega
with
\lvert x\rvert
sufficiently large.

Proof.

We derive (
4.20
) and (
4.21
)
from the representation formula for
v
in Theorem
4.2
.
For the steady-state part
v_{0}={\mathcal{P}}v

we have

\displaystyle v_{0}(x)={\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(1)}(x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(2)}(x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(3)}(x)-\int_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{P}}v_{b}(y)\cdot n(y){\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y).

Since

\lvert\nabla\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}(x)\rvert\leq C\big(\lvert x\rvert(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))\big)^{-3/2}

for
\lvert x\rvert\geq\varepsilon
,
from Lemma
2.5

we obtain

\displaystyle\big\lvert{\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(1)}(x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(2)}(x)-\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}(x)\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{lin}}_{0}\big\rvert\leq C\big(\lvert x\rvert(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))\big)^{-3/2}

for
\lvert x\rvert
sufficiently large.
Lemma
2.5
further yields

\displaystyle\big\lvert{\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(3)}(x)\big\rvert\leq C\big(\lvert x\rvert(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))\big)^{-3/2}

for
\lvert x\rvert
sufficiently large.
Similarly, using Lemma
2.5
and
the identity
\lvert\nabla{\mathrm{P}}(x)\rvert=C\lvert x\rvert^{-3}
,
we deduce

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert\int_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{P}}v_{b}(y)\cdot n(y){\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y)-{\mathrm{P}}(x)\Phi_{0}\bigg\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3}

for
\lvert x\rvert
sufficiently large.
Collecting the last three estimates, we conclude (
4.20
)
with the asserted estimate of the remainder
\mathcal{R}_{0}
.
Since the purely periodic part
v_{\perp}={\mathcal{P}_{\bot}}v

satisfies

\displaystyle v_{\perp}(t,x)={\widetilde{u}}_{\perp}^{(1)}(t,x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{\perp}^{(2)}(t,x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{\perp}^{(3)}(t,x)-\int_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{P}_{\bot}}v_{b}(t,y)\cdot n(y){\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y),

we obtain in the same way that

\displaystyle\big\lvert{\widetilde{u}}_{\perp}^{(1)}(t,x)+{\widetilde{u}}^{(2)}_{\perp}(t,x)-\big[\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{F}_{v,\perp}\big](t)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-4},

\displaystyle\big\lvert{\widetilde{u}}_{\perp}^{(3)}(t,x)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-4},

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert\int_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{P}_{\bot}}v_{b}(t,y)\cdot n(y){\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y)-{\mathrm{P}}(x)\Phi_{\perp}(t)+\nabla{\mathrm{P}}(x)\Psi_{\perp}(t)\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-4}

for large
\lvert x\rvert

Summarizing, we obtain (
4.21
) with

\lvert{\mathcal{R}}_{\perp}(t,x)\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-4}
for large
\lvert x\rvert
.

Now let us turn to the asymptotic expansion (
4.22
) of the pressure.
We define

\displaystyle p^{(1)}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(t,x)\cdot{\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle p^{(2)}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}{\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\cdot{\mathds{T}}(v,p)n(y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}+\int_{\Sigma}\zeta\cdot n(y){\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\cdot v_{b}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\zeta\cdot{\mathrm{P}}(x-y)v_{b}(t,y)\cdot n(y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y},

\displaystyle p^{(3)}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}{\mathrm{E}}(x-y)\partial_{t}v_{b}(t,y)\cdot n(y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y},

so that
p(t,x)=p^{(1)}(t,x)+p^{(2)}(t,x)+p^{(3)}(t,x)+p_{\infty}(t)
.
Arguing as above,
we use

\lvert\nabla{\mathrm{P}}(x)\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3}

for

to obtain

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert p^{(1)}(t,x)-{\mathrm{P}}(x)\cdot\int_{\Omega}f(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3},

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert p^{(2)}(t,x)-{\mathrm{P}}(x)\cdot\int_{\Sigma}\left[{\mathds{T}}(v,p)(t,y)n(y)+(\zeta\cdot n)(y)v_{b}(t,y)\right]\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}-\Phi(t){\mathrm{P}}(x)\cdot\zeta\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3},

\displaystyle\bigg\lvert p^{(3)}(t,x)-\Phi^{\prime}(t){\mathrm{E}}(x)+\Psi^{\prime}(t)\cdot{\mathrm{P}}(x)\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3}

for
\lvert x\rvert
sufficiently large.
Collecting these estimates,
we arrive at (
4.22
)
with remainder term
\mathfrak{R}
satisfying (
4.23
) for
\alpha=\beta=0
.

To obtain (
4.19
) for
\lvert\alpha\rvert,\lvert\beta\rvert\leq 2
and
(
4.23
) for
\lvert\alpha\rvert,\lvert\beta\rvert\leq 1
,
we can argue in the same way.
For example, for the pressure
p
we thus have

\partial_{j}\mathfrak{R}(t,x)=\partial_{j}p(t,x)-\Phi^{\prime}(t)\partial_{j}E(x)-\big[\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{lin}}(t)+\zeta\Phi(t)-\Psi^{\prime}(t)\big]\cdot\partial_{j}{\mathrm{P}}(x)

for

,
and we can repeat the previous argument.
Moreover, since
(v_{0},p_{0})
satisfy the steady Oseen equations
and

has compact support,
elliptic regularity implies smoothness of
(v_{0},p_{0})
far from the boundary.
Therefore, the above argument is admissible for derivatives of

\mathcal{R}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{R}_{0}
of arbitrary order.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

By assuming more time regularity of the data

and
v_{b}

we could ensure higher-order regularity of the full solution
(v,p)

far from the boundary, that is, not only of the steady part.
Then the previous argument also yields decay rates

In the next theorem we treat the homogeneous boundary value problem containing solely a forcing of the form
\nabla\cdot{\mathds{F}}

{\mathds{F}}

Theorem 4.7
.

In system (
4.1
) assume
v_{b}\equiv 0
and

.
If

with

for some

then the asymptotic expansion at infinity of the velocity field

is of the form

\begin{split}v(t,x)&=\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\int_{\Sigma}\left({\mathds{T}}(v,p)n+n^{\top}{\mathds{F}}\right)\mathrm{d}S\right](t)+{\mathcal{R}}(t,x),\end{split}

for all

v_{0}

and the purely periodic part

\displaystyle v_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{ste}}(x)\int_{\Sigma}\left[{\mathcal{P}}{\mathds{T}}(v,p)(t,y)n(y)+{\mathcal{P}}n(y)^{\top}{\mathds{F}}(t,y)\right]\mathrm{d}S(y)+{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(x),

(4.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}v_{\perp}(t,x)&=\left[\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}^{\mathrm{per}}(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Sigma}\left[{\mathcal{P}}_{\perp}{\mathds{T}}(v,p)(t,y)n(y)+{\mathcal{P}}_{\perp}n(y)^{\top}{\mathds{F}}(t,y)\right]\mathrm{d}S(y)\right](t)+{\mathcal{R}}_{\perp}(t,x).\end{split}

for all
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega

with
\lvert x\rvert
sufficiently large.

Proof.

We use the representation formulas from Theorem
4.4
and split

into several parts.
Then

u(t,x):=\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}\ast_{G}\left[({\mathds{T}}(v,p)n\delta_{\Sigma}\right](t,x)=\left[\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}(\cdot,x)\ast_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Sigma}{\mathds{T}}(v,p)n\,\mathrm{d}S\right](t)+{\mathcal{R}}^{u}(t,x)

is treated as in the previous lemma.
For the part

\displaystyle\begin{split}w^{\Omega}(t,x)&:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Omega}\left[\nabla\left(\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}\mathsf{e}_{i}\right)(t-s,x-y)\right]:{\mathds{F}}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\,\mathsf{e}_{i}\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}w^{\Sigma}(t,x)&:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Sigma}\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathds{L}$}}_{\zeta}(x-y,t-s)n(y)^{\top}{\mathds{F}}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y)\mathrm{d}s\\

2.1

\displaystyle\lvert w_{0}^{\Omega}(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\lvert x\rvert(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))\big)^{-3/2}\log_{+}\lvert x\rvert,

\displaystyle\lvert w_{\perp}^{\Omega}(t,x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\min\{A,4\}}.

\displaystyle\big\lvert\mathcal{R}^{w^{\Sigma}}_{0}(x)\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\lvert x\rvert(1+{s}_{\zeta}(x))\big)^{-3/2},

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-4}

for all

with
\lvert x\rvert
sufficiently large.
Since we have

,
estimate (

Now let us address the pressure term.
We write (
4.16
) in the form
p=\mathfrak{p}-\mathfrak{q}
with

\mathfrak{q}(t,x)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\cdot(\nabla\cdot{\mathds{F}})(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.5

we obtain

\big\lvert\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{p}}(t,x)\big\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3}

(4.34)

for all
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{T}}\times\Omega
,
where we split
\mathfrak{R}=\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{p}}-\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{q}}

with

\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{q}}(t,x)=\mathfrak{q}(t,x)-{\mathrm{P}}(x)\cdot\int_{\Sigma}n(y)^{\top}{\mathds{F}}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y).

such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi(x-y){\mathrm{P}}(x-y)\cdot[\nabla\cdot{\mathds{F}}(t,y)]\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}[1-\chi(x-y)]\nabla{\mathrm{P}}(x-y):{\mathds{F}}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

2.5

we conclude

\bigg\lvert\mathfrak{q}_{4}(t,x)-{\mathrm{P}}(x)\cdot\int_{\Sigma}{\mathds{F}}(t,y)n(y)\,\mathrm{d}S(y)\bigg\rvert\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-3}

for
\lvert x\rvert

and (
4.34

we finally arrive at (

∎

To prove Theorem

the linear terms in the representation formulas can be handled
as in the proof of Theorem

.
However, new difficulties arise from the nonlinear terms.
To treat them,
we begin with the following decay property of the velocity gradient.

such that

For all

From (

Proof of Theorem

We begin with the treatment of the velocity field

Then

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle={\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(1)}(x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(2)}(x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{0}^{(3)}(x)\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

\displaystyle+{\widetilde{u}}_{\perp}^{(1)}(t,x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{\perp}^{(2)}(t,x)+{\widetilde{u}}_{\perp}^{(3)}(t,x)\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

Decomposing

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{u}(t,x)

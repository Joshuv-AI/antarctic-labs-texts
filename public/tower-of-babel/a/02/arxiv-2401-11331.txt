Abstract
The SU( N N ) principal chiral model is asymptotically free and integrable in 1 + 1 1+1 dimensions. In the large- N N limit, there is no scattering, but correlation functions are not those of a free field theory. We briefly review the derivation of form factors for local operators. Two-point functions for such operators are known exactly. The two-point function of scaling-field operators has the short-distance behavior expected from the renormalization group. We briefly discuss non-vacuum operator products. The ultimate goal is to derive the Lagrangian field theory from this axiomatic quantum-field-theory formalism.

The Lagrangian of the SU(

\displaystyle\langle...\Phi(x)....\rangle=Z^{-1/2}\langle...U(x)....\rangle,

2.1
Rules for Exact Form Factors

The form factor of a local operator
{\mathfrak{B}}(x)
is an expression of the form
given by

\displaystyle f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots;\theta_{n},q_{n})=\langle 0|{\mathfrak{B}}(0)|\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots;\theta_{n},q_{n}\rangle_{{\rm in}},

(10)

where
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}
are the external rapidities of excitations and
q_{1},\dots q_{n}
are the corresponding quantum numbers. Other
matrix elements, besides (
10

To determine form factors, it is convenient to use Smirnov’s axioms
[
4
]
,
[
5
]

1. Scattering Axiom

\displaystyle\!\!f\!\!

\!\!{}^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots;\theta_{i-1},q_{i-1};\theta_{i+1},q_{i+1};\theta_{i},q_{i};\theta_{i+2},q_{i+2};\dots;\theta_{n},q_{n})

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle S^{k_{i}k_{i+1}}_{q_{i}q_{i+1}}(\theta_{i}-\theta_{i+1})f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots;\theta_{i-1},q_{i-1};\theta_{i},q_{i};\theta_{i+1},q_{i+1};\theta_{i+2},q_{i+2}\dots;\theta_{n},q_{n}).\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

This axiom is a generalization of Watson’s theorem.

2. Periodicity Axiom

\displaystyle f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots;\theta_{n},q_{n})=f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{n}-2\pi{\rm i},q_{n};\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots;\theta_{n-1},q_{n-1})\;.

3. Annihilation-Pole Axiom

\displaystyle\!\!{\rm i}\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\left.{\rm Res}f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots;\theta_{n},q_{n})\right|_{\theta_{n}=\theta_{n-1}+\pi{\rm i}}=f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots,\theta_{n-2};q_{n-2})\,C_{q_{n-1}q_{n}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle S^{k_{n-1}k_{1}}_{t_{1}q_{1}}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{n-1})S^{t_{1}k_{2}}_{t_{2}q_{2}}(\theta_{2}-\theta_{n-1})\cdots S^{t_{n-4}k_{n-3}}_{t_{n-3}q_{n-3}}(\theta_{n-3}-\theta_{n-1})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle S^{k_{n-3}k_{n-2}}_{q_{n-1}q_{n-2}}(\theta_{n-2}-\theta_{n-1})f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},k_{1};\dots;\theta_{n-2},k_{n-2})\,C_{k_{n-1}q_{n}}\;,

where
C
is the charge-conjugation matrix. The normalization of the left-hand side
follows from the standard state normalization,
e.g.

<\!\!\theta|\theta^{\prime}\!\!>=4\pi\delta(\theta-\theta^{\prime})
. This axiom and the previous one are special cases of “generalized crossing”, discussed in detail
by Babujian et.al.
[

4. Lorentz-Invariance Axiom

If an operator
{\mathfrak{B}}(x)
carries Lorentz spin s (not the center-of-mass energy
squared

), the form
factors must
transform under a boost
\theta_{j}\rightarrow\theta_{j}+\alpha
for all

as

\displaystyle f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1}+\alpha,q_{1};\dots;\theta_{n}+\alpha,q_{n})=e^{{\rm s}\alpha}f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},q_{1};\dots;\theta_{n},q_{n})\;,

5. Bound-State Pole Axiom

The name says it all. Poles appear in the form factors, at differences of rapidities corresponding to the formation of bound states.

6. Minimality Axiom

In the spirit of the bootstrap, we assume form factors have as much analyticity as possible. It can sometimes be checked
that this is consistent with some perturbative method, which means either standard covariant
perturbation theory or
the
1/N
-expansion. In cases where we can find form factors, minimality stands up very well. If
a first guess for the form factor

\displaystyle f_{\rm minimal}^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})=f^{\mathfrak{B}}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})\frac{P_{n}(\{\cosh(\theta_{j}-\theta_{k})\})}{Q_{n}(\{\cosh(\theta_{j}-\theta_{k})\})}\;,

where

poles in form factors, except those required by Axiom 3. or
corresponding to bound states. For Axiom 3

\displaystyle\left.P_{n}\right|_{\theta_{n}=\theta_{n-1}+\pi{\rm i}}=P_{n-2}\;,\;\;\left.Q_{n}\right|_{\theta_{n}=\theta_{n-1}+\pi{\rm i}}=Q_{n-2}\;.

The overall normalization of form factors can often be found through physical or mathematical considerations.

Although the S matrix becomes unity as

On multi-graded Proj schemes
Arnaud Mayeux, Simon Riche

Abstract
We review the construction (due to Brenner–Schröer) of the Proj scheme associated with a ring graded by a finitely generated abelian group. This construction generalizes the well-known Grothendieck Proj construction for ℕ {\mathbb{N}} -graded rings; we extend some classical results (in particular, regarding quasi-coherent sheaves on such schemes) from the ℕ {\mathbb{N}} -graded setting to this general setting, and prove new results that make sense only in the general setting of Brenner–Schröer. Finally, we show that flag varieties of reductive groups, as well as some vector bundles over such varieties attached to representations of a Borel subgroup, can be naturally interpreted in this formalism.

Introduction

1.1
Proj schemes

As part of his refoundation of Algebraic Geometry during the second part of the twentieth century, A. Grothendieck introduced the Proj scheme of an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded ring. This generalization of the notion of projective space is natural in the framework of his theory of schemes. Grothendieck’s Proj construction has since been studied, taught, treated and used many thousands of times.
During the first years of the third millennium, H. Brenner and S. Schröer generalized Grothendieck’s Proj construction to rings graded by arbitrary finitely generated abelian groups. Surprisingly, this very nice generalization still seems to be relatively confidential,
although it has been recently used and studied in some references, including
[
Za19
,
KSU21
,
MRo24
]
. In this paper, we provide a slightly different perspective on the Brenner–Schröer Proj construction, and prove a couple of new results on this construction. In particular, we explain a generalization in this setting of Serre’s classical description of coherent sheaves on a projective space (or, more generally, a Proj scheme) as a Serre quotient of a category of graded modules over a graded ring, see e.g.
[
StP
,
Tag 01YR
]
.

To illustrate this theory,
we also explain how to describe flag varieties of reductive algebraic groups over algebraically closed fields, and vector bundles over such flag varieties associated with some representations of a Borel subgroup (including the Springer resolution) as the Proj scheme of a ring graded by characters of a maximal torus. The description of flag varieties as Proj schemes of
\mathbb{N}
-graded rings is classical, see e.g.
[
Wa
]
, but it requires a choice of a strictly dominant weight. In the multi-graded setting one does
not
make any such choice, hence gets a more canonical construction, which is sometimes useful. In fact, versions of this construction appear implicitly or explicitly (but without proper references) in various constructions in Geometric Representation Theory, including
[
AB09
,
ABG04
,
ARd16
]
, and it was one of our motivations for this work to make this construction completely explicit and rigorous. Note that
[
MS05
, Exercise 14.16]
indicates a special case of this construction.

Remark 1.1
.

Other Proj-like constructions appear in the literature, often in relation with various forms of “toric geometry,” and some versions of the results explained below can be found in such frameworks (see e.g.
[
Roh14
]
). What we find particularly satisfying in the Brenner–Schröer theory is its elementary, while very general, nature.

1.2
The Brenner–Schröer construction

Let us now introduce the ideas of our work more precisely. Recall that given a commutative ring
A
,
its (affine) spectrum
{\operatorname{Spec}}(A)
is defined as the set of prime ideals of
A
, endowed with a certain topology and a sheaf of rings. Most treatments of Grothendieck’s Proj construction proceed by analogy with this construction,
namely
by associating to a graded ring the set of its graded prime ideals, and endowing it with a topology and a sheaf of rings after (somewhat tedious) identifications of various sets. However,
Brenner–Schröer proceed differently in their generalization in
[
BS03
, §2]
: they start from
the fact that, if
M
is an abelian group, the datum of a grading on a commutative ring
A
is equivalent to the datum of an action of the associated diagonalizable group scheme
\mathrm{D}_{{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathbb{Z})}(M)
on
{\operatorname{Spec}}(A)
(see e.g.
[
SGA3
, Exp. I, §4.7.3]
), and then consider a certain open subset in a quotient ringed space constructed in terms of this action.
Brenner–Schröer’s Proj scheme is covered by some special affine open subschemes, which generalize similar special affine subschemes appearing in Grothendieck’s Proj construction. In case
M=\mathbb{Z}
and the negative-degree components in
A
are
0
, this recovers Grothendieck’s construction. (We will refer to this case as the “
\mathbb{N}
-graded case.”)

In the present treatment of the Proj construction, we put these “special affine subschemes” at the heart of the Proj construction. In fact, we give them a name: “potions.” Potions glue together to give birth to the Proj scheme thanks to a purely algebraic statement we call “the magic of potions.”
To use this statement and glue potion schemes, we have to assume
that
M
is finitely generated. (In the
\mathbb{N}
-graded case, this result is part, as an auxiliary lemma, of the justification of the tedious identifications mentioned before in most treatments of the Proj construction.)
Note that, in general,
the underlying set of the Proj scheme is
not
the set of graded prime ideals of the given graded ring;
it can be described as a set of graded ideals (see
[
BS03
, Remark 2.3]
), but we believe that this point of view is not really useful, and in any case it does not play any role in the present paper.
Note that
[
MS05
]
, already mentioned, provides some specific multi-graded Proj schemes (under the name spector) by glueing, cf.
[
MS05
, Definition 10.25]
. It seems that
[
BS03
]
and
[
MS05
]
are independent (each does not cite the other). Some other works related to multi-graded Proj schemes are cited in
[
BS03
,
Za19
,
KSU21
,
MRo24
]
, see e.g.
[
Rob98
,
Pe07
]
.

, and

Proof.

(
1
)
Let

be relevant. By Proposition

, such that the degrees

generate a subgroup of

of finite index. For

and
m_{i}^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}

(resp.

) generate a subgroup of

(resp.

) of finite index. For

(

Proposition 2.8

Let

be

-graded rings, and let

.

.

Now, assume that

Proposition 2.11

((Magic of potions))

Let

and

1.

We have a canonical homomorphism of potion rings

.

2.

Assume that

-algebras between

generated by the elements

3.

Assume that

is relevant and that

is finitely generated as submonoid of
(A,\times)
. The morphism of schemes

induced by the ring homomorphism in (

4.

Let

be nonzero relevant homogeneous elements of the same degree.
Then we have a canonical open immersion

]

Proof.

(
1
)
The desired morphism
is obtained as the degree-zero part of the canonical homomorphism of graded rings

.

(
2
)
Without loss of generality, we can (and will) assume that
S=\underline{S}
. Consider the localization
\big(A_{(S)}\big)_{\{\frac{t^{n_{t}}s_{t}^{\prime}}{s_{t}}:t\in T^{\prime}\}}

with respect to the homogeneous multiplicative subset generated by

, the universal property of localizations gives us a canonical homomorphism of

-algebras

in

Let now

\mathfrak{t}\in T

is surjective, and concludes the proof.

(
3
)
In case

(

Now by (

Remark 3.4

If

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Prim}(A):\forall t\in T,\,t\not\in\mathfrak{p}\}.
Let
\mathrm{Alex}(A)
be the set
\mathrm{Prim}(A)

Recall that the association

(for

) extends to a sheaf of rings on

, namely the structure sheaf

[
StP
,
Tag 01HU

(for

) extends to a sheaf of rings on

, refining

. In other words, considering the continuous Alexandroffication map

coincide with the pullback sheaf

? (See e.g.
[

,
Tag 008C
]
for the notation

(Boolean ring). In this case
\mathrm{Alex}(A)
is the discrete topology, so that while the global sections of
f_{p}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Spec}}(A)}
give
A
, the global sections of
f^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Spec}}(A)}
are products of
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
over
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
. It is well-known that
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
is in bijection with the Stone–Cech compactification of
{\mathbb{N}}
, which has ordinality strictly bigger than
{\mathbb{N}}
; as a consequence,
f_{p}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Spec}}(A)}\neq f^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Spec}}(A)}
.

Therefore, one cannot refine the structure sheaf of affine schemes (and the theory of schemes) in this way, using arbitrary localizations. So it is not possible to create a theory, similar to the theory of schemes in the framework of locally ringed space, allowing glueing along open subspaces in the Alexandroff–Zariski topology, which justifies Remark
1.2
. Note that Exercise 11.1 of
[
Sch19
]
says that one
can
produce a theory inspired by the theory of schemes using arbitrary localizations, but it is not a theory based on locally ringed spaces, and will not behave formally as scheme theory regarding glueings. Note that the theory of Analytic Spaces of
[
Sch19
]
uses sorts of quasi-compact coverings, so that one cannot define the Proj of an arbirary graded ring in this theory either. In the above geometric paradigms, it thus seems that the hypothesis that
A
is graded by a
finitely generated
abelian group cannot be removed without serious difficulties to define
{\operatorname{Proj}}
schemes.

If

3.2
First properties

By construction,

Lemma 3.5

The scheme
{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)
is quasi-separated.

For any

we have a canonical homomorphism

; these homomorphisms glue to a morphism of schemes

{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{0}).

(3.1)

For the next statement, note that if

is relevant, for any homogeneous

Lemma 3.6

Let

1.

We have

.

2.

If

3.

If

under the composition

D_{\dagger}(f)\hookrightarrow{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)\xrightarrow{\eqref{eqn:morph-Spec-A0}}{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{0}).

Proof.

(
1
)
We have

(

(

This follows similarly from Remark

(

(

By (

In particular, Lemma

The natural morphism of ringed spaces

Remark 3.9

Assume we are given a flat affine group scheme

3.3
Open subschemes defined by quasi-relevant elements

Recall the notion of quasi-
M
-relevant element of
A
from Definition

. If

is a nonzero
M
-quasi-relevant element, then we can write

where each

is a nonzero
M
-relevant element of degree

, and by Proposition

(

) we have an open immersion

{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(a)})\longrightarrow\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}D_{\dagger}(f_{i}).

By construction the right-hand side is an open subscheme in

, so we deduce an open immersion

{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(a)})\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A).

(3.5)

Lemma 3.10

The morphism (
3.5
) is canonical, i.e. it does not depend on the way
a
is written as a sum of relevant elements.

Proof.

Assume that
a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{j}
where the
f_{i}
’s and the
g_{j}
’s are nonzero and relevant, all of the same degree. What we have to prove is that the compositions

{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(a)})\to\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(f_{i})})\subset{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)\quad\text{and}\quad{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(a)})\to\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(g_{j})})\subset{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)

coincide. Here the first, resp. second, immersion is obtained using the open covering

{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(a)})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(af_{i})}),\quad\text{resp.}\quad{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(a)})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(ag_{j})})

and the natural open immersions
{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(af_{i})})\to{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(f_{i})})
, resp.
{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(ag_{i})})\to{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(g_{i})})
.
However, in
A_{(a)}
we have
\sum_{i,j}\frac{f_{i}g_{j}}{a^{2}}=1
, so that using similar considerations we can refine both of these decompositions into a decomposition

{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(a)})=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i\leqslant n\\
1\leqslant j\leqslant m\end{subarray}}{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(af_{i}g_{j})}),

and it is clear that both immersions can be obtained from this decomposition using the canonical open immersions
{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(af_{i}g_{j})})\to{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)
.
∎

Lemma
3.10
justifies that the image of (

) can be denoted

; this open scheme identifies canonically with
{\operatorname{Spec}}(A_{(a)})
. In case

is relevant, this coincides with the open subscheme denoted similarly above.

Corollary 3.11

Let
(a_{i}:i\in I)
be relevant elements in

.
If

A_{\dagger}\subset{\operatorname{Rad}}\left(\sum_{i\in I}A\cdot a_{i}\right),

then we have

{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)=\bigcup_{i\in I}D_{\dagger}(a_{i}).

Proof.

Of course we can assume that the

By (

is covered by the open subschemes

such that

3.4
More basic properties

We start with a functoriality property of the Proj scheme construction, which generalizes
[
StP
,
Tag 01MY
]
. For this we note that if
\Psi:A\to B
is a homomorphism of commutative
M
-graded rings and
S\subset A
is a homogeneous multiplicative subset, then so is
\Psi(S)
. Clearly,
\Psi(S)
is relevant if
S
is, and finitely generated as a submonoid of
(B,\times)
if
S
is finitely generated as a submonoid of
(A,\times)
. Hence
\Psi
induces a map
\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{F}_{B}
, which will also be denoted
\Psi
.

Proposition 3.13

((Functoriality of Proj))

Let
\Psi:A\to B
be a homomorphism of
M
-graded rings.
For any
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{F}_{A}

we have a canonical morphism of schemes
\mathrm{Proj}^{M}_{\Psi(\mathcal{F})}(B)\to\mathrm{Proj}^{M}_{\mathcal{F}}(A)
. Moreover, for any
S\in{\mathcal{F}}
we have

{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}_{\Psi({\mathcal{F}})}(B)\times_{{\operatorname{Proj}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{M}(A)}D_{\dagger}(S)=D_{\dagger}(\Psi(S));

(3.6)

in particular, the morphism is affine.

Proof.

The morphism is obtained by glueing from the morphisms induced by the canonical homomorphisms
A_{(S)}\to B_{(\Psi(S))}
for
S\in\mathcal{F}
.

Now fix
S\in\mathcal{F}
. From the definition of our morphism we see that we have an inclusion

D_{\dagger}(\Psi(S))\subset{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}_{\Psi({\mathcal{F}})}(B)\times_{{\operatorname{Proj}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{M}(A)}D_{\dagger}(S)

as open subschemes of
{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}_{\Psi({\mathcal{F}})}(B)
. On the other hand,
by definition
{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}_{\Psi({\mathcal{F}})}(B)
is covered by the open subschemes
D_{\dagger}(\Psi(T))
where
T
runs over
\mathcal{F}
, so that
{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}_{\Psi({\mathcal{F}})}(B)\times_{{\operatorname{Proj}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{M}(A)}D_{\dagger}(S)
is covered by the open subschemes

D_{\dagger}(\Psi(T))\times_{{\operatorname{Proj}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{M}(A)}D_{\dagger}(S)
.
Now the morphism
D_{\dagger}(\Psi(T))\to{\operatorname{Proj}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{M}(A)
factors through
D_{\dagger}(T)
, so that

D_{\dagger}(\Psi(T))\times_{{\operatorname{Proj}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{M}(A)}D_{\dagger}(S)=D_{\dagger}(\Psi(T))\times_{D_{\dagger}(T)}(D_{\dagger}(T)\cap D_{\dagger}(S)).

Using Proposition
2.11
(
2
) one sees that the right-hand side coincides with
D_{\dagger}(\Psi(ST))
; in particular it is contained in
D_{\dagger}(\Psi(S))
, which finishes the proof of (
3.6
).

The equalities (
3.6
) imply that our morphism is affine, see e.g.
[
StP
,
Tag 01S8
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.14

In the setting of Proposition
3.13
, assume that
\Psi:A\to B
is surjective. Then we have
\mathrm{Proj}^{M}_{\Psi(\mathcal{F}_{A})}(B)=\mathrm{Proj}^{M}(B)
, and the canonical morphism

{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(B)\to{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)

is a closed immersion.

Proof.

Let
S\in{\mathcal{F}}_{B}
. By definition, there exist
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\in\underline{S}
whose degrees generate a subgroup of
M
of finite index. Let
S^{\prime}\subset B
be the homogenenous multiplicative subset generated by
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}
, and let
S^{\prime\prime}=S\cdot S^{\prime}
. Then
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
are finitely generated relevant homogeneous multiplicative subsets of
B
, and by Remark
3.2
(
3
) we have
D_{\dagger}(S)=D_{\dagger}(S^{\prime\prime})
. We therefore have an embedding

D_{\dagger}(S)\to D_{\dagger}(S^{\prime})
. Now, choosing for each

a homogeneous preimage

of

in

and denoting by

the homogeneous multiplicative subset generated by

, it is clear that

is relevant and that

. Hence

, which implies that

is also contained in

Since the property of being a closed immersion is local on the target (see

]

The following example generalizes

,
Tag 01MI

Example 3.21

Let

It is a standard fact that, even in the

Proposition 3.22

-graded ring, or as an

{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)\cong{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A^{\prime})\cong{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M^{\prime}}(A^{\prime}).

We use the description of these schemes using (

If

is an

we have

for all

We now state a few immediate (but useful) consequences of Proposition

Corollary 3.23

Let

Let

Let

3.6
Proj schemes, blowups and dilatations

Let
A
be a ring and
\{J_{i}:i\in I\}
be a family of ideals of

parametrized by a finite set

{\operatorname{Bl}}_{\{J_{i}:i\in I\}}A=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}J_{i}^{\nu}
(see Example

[

, Definition 2.34]

Any family

for each

The goal of the present subsection is to study related connections and compatibilities between dilatations and these specific Proj schemes,
and to show that this allows to easily provide a proof of the existence of (projective) blowups, possibly with multiple centers. This section was announced in
[
Ma24
, § 3.9]
.
We start with a multi-centered version of
[
StP
,
Tag 0804
]
.

Lemma 3.25

In the setting above, the scheme
{\operatorname{Proj}}^{\mathbb{Z}^{I}}({\operatorname{Bl}}_{\{J_{i}:i\in I\}}A)
is covered by the open subschemes
D_{\dagger}(\{a_{i}:i\in I\})
where
\{a_{i}:i\in I\}
runs over the families such that
a_{i}\in J_{i}
for all
i\in I
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.11
, the lemma will follow if we prove that any non nilpotent relevant element in the
\mathbb{Z}^{I}
-graded ring
{\operatorname{Bl}}_{\{J_{i}:i\in I\}}A

belongs to the sum of the ideals
({\operatorname{Bl}}_{\{J_{i}:i\in I\}}A)\cdot\prod_{i}a_{i}
where
\{a_{i}:i\in I\}
runs over the families as above. Now it is clear that this sum is the sum of the graded components in
{\operatorname{Bl}}_{\{J_{i}:i\in I\}}A
associated with elements in
\mathbb{Z}^{I}
all of whose components are positive. The claim therefore follows from the observation that the degree of any non nilpotent relevant element in
{\operatorname{Bl}}_{\{J_{i}:i\in I\}}A
must have positive components, because if the
i

∎

We now work in a relative setting; we therefore fix a base scheme

Proof.

We proceed with the usual local notations from the beginning of the subsection.
Consider the collection of families

for some ring

In the following statement we use the notation of dilatations of schemes from

Proposition 3.29

((Complementary))

For all

is an effective Cartier divisor on

. Moreover, the composition
\mathrm{Bl}^{\{Y_{i}^{\prime}:i\in I\}}_{\{\varnothing:i\in I\}}X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}\to X

4
4

4

Recall that if

Proof.

This statement is local on

In this case,

Proposition 3.30

((Existence of blowups))

Let
\pi:T\to X
be a scheme such that, for all

,

is an effective Cartier divisor on

. Then there exists a unique morphism of
X
-schemes

. As a consequence, the pair consisting of

and the canonical morphism

is a blowup of
X
with center
\{Y_{i}:i\in I\}

Proof.

First we show that for
T
and
\pi
as in the statement, there exists at most one morphism of
X
-schemes
T\to X^{\prime}
. In fact, let
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:T\to X^{\prime}
be two morphisms of
X
-schemes. Denote by
U
the open complement of the sum of the effective Cartier divisors
T\times_{X}Y_{i}
; then
U
is scheme theoretically dense in
T
by
[
StP
,
Tag 07ZU
]
. By Lemma
3.27
,
the morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
is separated; as a consequence, the difference kernel
\mathrm{ker}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})
(see
[
GW20
, Definition 9.1]
) is a closed subscheme of

by
[
GW20
, Definition/Proposition 9.7]
.
Since

U\times_{X}Y_{i}=\varnothing

for all

, the universal property of dilatations (see
[

) implies that both
\phi_{|U}
and
\phi^{\prime}_{|U}
factor through
\mathrm{Bl}_{\{\varnothing:i\in I\}}^{\{Y_{i}^{\prime}:i\in I\}}X^{\prime}
. By Proposition
3.29
,
\mathrm{Bl}_{\{\varnothing:i\in I\}}^{\{Y_{i}^{\prime}:i\in I\}}X^{\prime}
is an open subscheme of

, so that this implies that

\phi=\phi^{\prime}
.

To prove existence we first consider the following setting. We assume that
X={\operatorname{Spec}}(A)
and

for some rings

and

, and denote by

the global sections of

, so that

, and by

the homomorphism of rings

for some non-zero-divisor

Let us first show the existence of a morphism of

.
The morphism

induces a canonical morphism of

-graded rings

\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}J^{\nu}\to\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}(f(J)R)^{\nu}

\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}(f(J)R)^{\nu}\cong R[\mathbb{N}^{I}]

So we get a canonical homomorphism of graded

\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}J^{\nu}\to R[\mathbb{N}^{I}].

By Example

3.13
this morphism provides a morphism of

from an open subscheme of

to

{\operatorname{Proj}}^{\mathbb{Z}^{I}}(R[\mathbb{N}^{I}])
coincides with its open subscheme associated with the relevant element corresponding to the element in

all of whose components are

, which corresponds to
\prod_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}\in\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{I}}(f(J)R)^{\nu}

In view of the construction of §

and Lemma

{\operatorname{Proj}}^{\mathbb{Z}^{I}}(R[\mathbb{N}^{I}])
is covered by the open subschemes

Now we prove the existence of a morphism of

4.3
The Springer resolution

We continue with the setting of §

Proof.

\bigoplus_{\alpha\in M}\Gamma({\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}(\alpha)).

(5.3)

Note that the morphism (

More generally, if

These maps define on

a structure of an

-graded sheaf of modules over (

), and on

-graded module over (

Fact 5.7

1.

There is a canonical morphism of
M
-graded rings

A\to\bigoplus_{\alpha\in M}\Gamma\Bigl({\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A),{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}(\alpha)\Bigr).

2.

For any graded

-module

5.3

For any

-module

Proposition 5.8

Let

, and let
\alpha\in M[\underline{S}]
.

1.

The sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}(\alpha)_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}

.

2.

For any
M
-graded

-module

\widetilde{Q}(\alpha)_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\longrightarrow\widetilde{Q(\alpha)}_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}

obtained by restriction from (

3.

For any
\alpha^{\prime}\in M
, the morphism

\left({\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}(\alpha)_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\right)\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{\dagger}(S)}}\left({\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}(\alpha^{\prime})_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\right)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}

4.

For any
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}
-module
{\mathcal{Q}}
and any

, the morphism

\left({\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}(\alpha)_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\right)\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{\dagger}(S)}}\left({\mathcal{Q}}(\alpha^{\prime})|_{D_{\dagger}(S)}\right)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}(\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}

Proof.

Since

, there exists an invertible element

of degree

(see Proposition

(

induces an isomorphism of

-modules

. We deduce (

Similarly, given a graded

∎

Lemma 5.9

For any
{\mathcal{Q}}\in{\mathsf{Mod}}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{Proj}^{M}(A)})
, there exists a canonical (in particular, functorial) morphism

\widetilde{\Gamma_{\bullet}({\mathcal{Q}})}\to{\mathcal{Q}}

in
{\mathsf{Mod}}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{Proj}^{M}(A)})
.

Proof.

Since
{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)
is covered (by definition) by the open subschemes
D_{\dagger}(S)
for
S\in{\mathcal{F}}_{A}
not containing
0
, to prove the lemma it suffices to construct morphisms

\widetilde{\Gamma_{\bullet}({\mathcal{Q}})}_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}

(5.6)

for such
S
, which coincide on intersections of such open subschemes. And given
S
, by basic properties of quasi-coherent sheaves on affine schemes (see
[
StP
,
Tag 01I7
]
), to define such a morphism it suffices to define a morphism of
A_{(S)}
-modules

\Gamma_{\bullet}({\mathcal{Q}})_{(S)}\to\Gamma(D_{\dagger}(S),{\mathcal{Q}}).

(5.7)

Now an element of
\Gamma_{\bullet}({\mathcal{Q}})_{(S)}
can be represented by a fraction
\frac{m}{s}
where
s\in S
and
m\in\Gamma_{\bullet}({\mathcal{Q}})_{\deg(s)}
, i.e.
m
is a global section of
{\mathcal{Q}}(\deg(s))
. Consider

m_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\in\Gamma(D_{\dagger}(S),{\mathcal{Q}}(\deg(s))).

The element
\frac{1}{s}\in A_{S}
has degree
-\deg(s)
, hence defines a section of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(S)}(-\deg(s))
on
D_{\dagger}(S)
, which we denote
s^{-1}
. Then the product
m_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\otimes s^{-1}
defines a global section of

{\mathcal{Q}}(\deg(s))_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\otimes_{D_{\dagger}(S)}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{Proj}}^{M}(A)}(-\deg(s))_{|D_{\dagger}(S)}\cong{\mathcal{Q}}_{|D_{\dagger}(S)},

where the isomorphism is provided by Proposition
5.8
(
4
).

One easily checks that this section is independent of the representation of the element of
\Gamma_{\bullet}({\mathcal{Q}})_{(S)}
as a fraction, hence that this process defines a map (
5.7
), and then that this map is a morphism of
A_{(S)}
-modules. One also easily sees that the associated morphisms (
5.6
) glue on the intersections of open subschemes
D_{\dagger}(S)
, hence provide the desired morphism of sheaves.
∎

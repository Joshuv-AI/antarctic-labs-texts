Nilpotent probability of compact groups
Alireza Abdollahi, Meisam Soleimani Malekan

Abstract
. Let k k be any positive integer and G G a compact (Hausdorff) group. Let 𝐧𝐩 k ​ ( G ) \mathbf{np}_{k}(G) denote the probability that k + 1 k+1 randomly chosen elements x 1 , … , x k + 1 x_{1},\dots,x_{k+1} satisfy [ x 1 , x 2 , … , x k + 1 ] = 1 [x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k+1}]=1 . We study the following problem: If 𝐧𝐩 k ​ ( G ) > 0 \mathbf{np}_{k}(G)>0 then, does there exist an open nilpotent subgroup of class at most k k ? The answer is positive for profinite groups and we give a new proof. We also prove that the connected component G 0 G^{0} of G G is abelian and there exists a closed normal nilpotent subgroup N N of class at most k k such that G 0 ​ N G^{0}N is open in G G .

2.
Relative nilpotent probability and submultiplicativity

We denote the set of continuous functions on a compact group
G
by
\mathcal{C}(G)
. If
N
is a closed normal subgroup of
G
, then
G/N
is a compact group, equipped with quotient topology. There exists a projection-like map
P:\mathcal{C}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(G/N)
defined by

Pf(\overline{g}):=\int_{N}f(gy)\,\text{d}y,

where
\text{d}y
stands for the Haar measure on
N
. We will use the following two simple lemmas repeatedly:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a compact group and
N
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
. Then, for all
f\in\mathcal{C}(G)

\int_{G}f(g)\,\text{d}g=\int_{G/N}Pf(\overline{g})\,\text{d}\overline{g}.

Proof.

The functional

I:\mathcal{C}(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad f\mapsto\int_{G/N}Pf(\overline{g})\,\text{d}\overline{g}

is nonzero, positive and left-invariant, also
I(1)=1
, so by the uniqueness of Haar integral the result holds.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We have an analog of Lemma
2.1
whenever
H
is a closed not necessarily normal subgroup, see
[
4
, (2.49) Theorem]
.

The Uniqueness of Harr measure has another achievement; consider a continuous epimorphism
\phi:G\rightarrow H
between compact groups. Then

I:\mathcal{C}(H)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad f\mapsto\int_{G}f\circ\phi(g)\,\text{d}g

defines a nonzero, positive and left-invariant functional. Aslo,
I(1)=1
, so we have

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\phi:G\rightarrow H
be a continuous epimorphism between compact groups. Then

\int_{H}f(h)\,\text{d}h=\int_{G}f\circ\phi(g)\,\text{d}g\quad(f\in\mathcal{C}(H)).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of a group
G
. Then
yC_{G}(x)\cap H
is either empty or a coset of
C_{H}(x)
, for all
x,y\in G
. In particular, we have
\mathbf{m}_{H}(yC_{G}(x)\cap H)\leq\mathbf{m}_{H}(C_{H}(x))
, for all
x,y\in G
.

Proof.

If
z\in C_{G}(x)\cap y^{-1}H
, then one can easily check that
C_{G}(x)\cap y^{-1}H=zC_{H}(x)
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of a compact group
G
. Then
\mathbf{np}(H;x,y)\leq\mathbf{cp}(H)
for all
x
and
y
in
G
.

Proof.

By the above lemma, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{np}(H;x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{H}1_{\mathcal{N}(H;x,y)}(\xi,\eta)\,\text{d}\eta\,\text{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\mathbf{m}_{H}(x^{-1}C_{G}(y\eta)\cap N)\,\text{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H}\mathbf{m}_{H}(C_{H}(y\eta))\,\text{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\mathbf{m}_{H}(y^{-1}C_{G}(\xi)\cap N)\,\text{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H}\mathbf{m}_{H}(C_{H}(\xi))\,\text{d}\xi=\mathbf{cp}(H).

∎

Remark 2.6
.

In
[
11
, Lemma 6.2.]
, using combinatorics methods, the authors have shown that for a normal subgroup
N
of a
finite
group
G
,

\mathbf{np}(N;x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\leq\mathbf{np}(N;\overbrace{1,\dotsc,1}^{n\text{-times}})

for all
x_{k}\in G
,
1\leq k\leq n
. We could not implement these methods in the case of infinite compact groups to have the above inequality for a closed normal subgroup
N
, and of course we do not need this generalization.

Now, we are going to prove that
\mathbf{np}(N;x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
is bounded away from 1, for all closed normal subgroup
N
of a compact group
G
which is not nilpotent of class at most
k
and all
x_{k}\in G
,
1\leq k\leq n
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of a compact group
G
. Then

\mathbf{np}(H;x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n+1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\mathbf{np}\left(\frac{H}{\mathbf{Z}\cap H};\overline{x_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{n}}\right)\right),

for all
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in G
, where
\mathbf{Z}
is the center of
G
, and
\overline{x}
denotes the image of
x
in
H/(\mathbf{Z}\cap H)
.

Proof.

Put
K:=\mathbf{Z}\cap H
,

X=\left\{\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n})\in H^{n}:[x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}y_{n}]\in K\right\}

and

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{H}{K};\overline{x_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{n}}\right).

Then one can easily verify that

(2.1)

\displaystyle 1_{X}(\mathbf{y}\mathbf{z})=1_{X}(\mathbf{y})\quad(\mathbf{y}\in H^{n},\mathbf{z}\in K^{n})

(
1_{X}
is the characteristic function of
X
) and

(2.2)

\displaystyle 1_{X}(\mathbf{y})=1\quad\text{if and only if}\quad 1_{\mathcal{N}}(\bar{\mathbf{y}})=1.

Now, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{np}(H;x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{n}}\mathbf{m}_{H}\left(x_{n+1}^{-1}C_{G}([x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}y_{n}])\cap H\right)\,\text{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H^{n}}\mathbf{m}_{H}\left(C_{H}([x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}y_{n}])\right)\,\text{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\mathbf{m}_{H}\left(C_{H}([x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}y_{n}])\right)\,\text{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\int_{H^{n}\setminus X}\mathbf{m}_{H}\left(C_{H}([x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}y_{n}])\right)\,\text{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{m}_{H^{n}}(X)+\frac{1}{2}(1-\mathbf{m}_{H^{n}}(X))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\mathbf{m}_{H^{n}}(X)\right).

The latter inequality holds because on
X^{c}
,
C_{G}([x_{1}y_{1},\dots,x_{n}y_{n}])
has index at least 2. We compute
\mathbf{m}_{H^{n}}(X)
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{H^{n}}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{n}}1_{X}(\mathbf{y})\,\text{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{n}/K^{n}}\int_{K^{n}}1_{X}(\mathbf{y}\mathbf{z})\,\text{d}\mathbf{z}\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}},\quad\text{by Lemma \ref{Gotosubgroups},}

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{n}/K^{n}}1_{X}(\mathbf{y})\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}},\quad\text{by (\ref{1})},

\displaystyle=\int_{(H/K)^{n}}1_{\mathcal{N}}(\bar{\mathbf{y}})\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}},\quad\text{by Lemma \ref{themeasureinquoitient}},

\displaystyle=\mathbf{np}\left(\frac{H}{\mathbf{Z}\cap H};\overline{x_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{n}}\right).

∎

By Theorem
2.7
we obtain the following characterization of closed and nilpotent normal subgroups of a compact group:

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of a compact group
G
. Then
H
is nilpotent of class at most
n
if and only if

\mathbf{np}(H;x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n+1})=1

for some
x_{k}\in G
,
1\leq k\leq n+1
.

Proof.

Only one direction needs proof.

Using induction on
n
, we prove the result. For
n=1
, assume that
\mathbf{np}(H;x,y)=1
for some
x,y\in G
, so by Proposition
2.5
,
\mathbf{cp}(H)=1
, whence implies that
H
is an abelian group.
Assuming that the result is valid for
n-1
, we prove its correctness for
n
. If

\mathbf{np}(H;x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n+1})=1

for some
x_{k}\in G
,
1\leq k\leq n+1
, then by Proposition
2.7
, we get

\mathbf{np}\left(\frac{H}{\mathbf{Z}\cap H};\overline{x_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{n}}\right)=1
. Now, by induction hypothesis,
\frac{H}{\mathbf{Z}\cap H}
is nilpotent of class at most
n-1
, so,
H
is nilpotent of class at most
n
.
∎

Definition 2.9
.

Let
H

. The
the relative

in

When

.

Since the Haar measure is left-invariant, one can easily verify that

\mathbf{np}_{k}(G)=\mathbf{np}(G;x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k+1})
for all

\mathbf{np}_{k}(G)
is nothing but the degree of

defined in
[

. Also, by Lemma

\mathbf{np}_{1,G}(H)=\mathbf{cp}(H)

.

Theorem 2.11

Let

By Theorem

Theorem 2.12
.

(

Let

. Then

Proof.

By

If
B
is a measurable subgroup of a compact group

, then
\mathbf{m}_{A}(B)=[A:B]^{-1}
, by convention on “
\frac{1}{\infty}=0
”. We have

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
G
be a compact group,
H
be a closed subgroup of
G
and
N
be a normal closed subgroup of
G
. Then

\mathbf{m}_{G}(H)=\mathbf{m}_{N}(N\cap H)\mathbf{m}_{G/N}\left(NH/N\right).

Proof.

The following is well known

[G:H]=[G:NH][NH:H]=[G/N:NH/N][N:N\cap H].

This along with the explanation before the lemma implies the result.
∎

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
G
be a compact group with closed subgroups
H
and closed normal subgroup
N
contained in
H
. Then
\mathbf{np}_{k,G}(H)\leq\mathbf{np}_{k,G/N}(H/N)\mathbf{np}_{k,G}(N)
.

Proof.

For
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k+1}\in G
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{np}(H;x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H^{k}}\mathbf{m}_{H}(C_{H}([x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k}]))\,\text{d}\mathbf{y}.

By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.13
,

\displaystyle\int_{H^{k}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{H}(C_{H}([x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k}]))\,\text{d}\mathbf{y}=\int_{H^{k}/N^{k}}\int_{N^{k}}\mathbf{m}_{H}(C_{H}([x_{1}y_{1}z_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k}z_{k}]))\,\text{d}\mathbf{z}\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{k}/N^{k}}\int_{N^{k}}\mathbf{m}_{N}(C_{N}([x_{1}y_{1}z_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k}z_{k}]))\mathbf{m}_{G/N}\left(NC_{H}([x_{1}y_{1}z_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k}z_{k}])/N\right)\,\text{d}\mathbf{z}\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{\leq}\int_{H^{k}/N^{k}}\int_{N^{k}}\mathbf{m}_{N}(C_{N}([x_{1}y_{1}z_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k}z_{k}]))\mathbf{m}_{H/N}\left(C_{H/N}([\overline{x_{1}y_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{k}y_{k}}])\right)\,\text{d}\mathbf{z}\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{k}/N^{k}}\mathbf{m}_{H/N}\left(C_{H/N}([\overline{x_{1}y_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{k}y_{k}}])\right)\left(\int_{N^{k}}\mathbf{m}_{N}(C_{N}([x_{1}y_{1}z_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k}z_{k}]))\,\text{d}\mathbf{z}\right)\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{k}/N^{k}}\mathbf{m}_{G/N}\left(C_{G/N}([\overline{x_{1}y_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{k}y_{k}}])\right)\mathbf{np}(N;x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k},1)\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{np}_{k,G}(N)\int_{(G/N)^{k}}\mathbf{m}_{G/N}\left(C_{G/N}([\overline{x_{1}}\overline{y_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{k}}\overline{y_{k}}])\right)\,\text{d}\bar{\mathbf{y}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{np}_{k,G}(N)\mathbf{np}_{k,G/N}(H/N).

The inequality (*) obtained by the inclusion

NC_{H}([x_{1}y_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}y_{k}])/N\subset C_{H/N}([\overline{x_{1}y_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{x_{k}y_{k}}]).

∎

As a corollary of the above theorem, we state:

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
N
be a closed normal subgroup of a compact group
G
. Then
\mathbf{np}_{k}(G)\leq\mathbf{np}_{k}(G/N)\mathbf{np}_{k,G}(N)
. In particular,
\mathbf{cp}(G)\leq\mathbf{cp}(G/N)\mathbf{cp}(N)
.

3.
Proofs of Main Results

Using Corollaries
2.15
and
2.11
, we prove the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a compact group and assume that
\mathbf{np}_{k}(G)>0
. Then the length of all closed normal series of
G
which its factors are not nilpotent of class at most
k
should be bounded.

Proof.

Let

1=G_{r+1}\leq G_{r}\leq\dots\leq G_{1}\leq G_{0}=G

be a closed normal series such that
G_{i}/G_{i+1}
,
0\leq i\leq r
, is not nilpotent of class at most
k
. Using the Theorem
2.14
repeatedly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{np}_{k}(G)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{np}_{k,G_{0}}(G_{r})\mathbf{np}_{k}(G_{0}/G_{r})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{np}_{k,G_{0}}(G_{r})\mathbf{np}_{k,G_{0}/G_{r}}(G_{r-1}/G_{r})\mathbf{np}_{k}(G_{0}/G_{r-1})

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=2}^{r+1}\mathbf{np}_{k,G_{0}/G_{i}}(G_{i-1}/G_{i}).

The Corollary
2.11
implies that the right hand side of the above inequality is at most

\left(1-\frac{3}{2^{k+1}}\right)^{r}
. All in all, we get

r<\frac{\ln\mathbf{np}_{k}(G)}{\ln\left(1-\frac{3}{2^{k+1}}\right)}.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a compact group with
\mathbf{np}_{k}(G)>0
. Assume that there exists a decreasing net
(H_{\alpha})
of closed normal subgroups of
G
such that
\bigcap_{\alpha}H_{\alpha}=1
. Then
H_{\alpha}
is nilpotent of class at most
k
for sufficiently large
\alpha
.

Proof.

If none of
H_{\alpha}
’s are nilpotent of class at most
k
, then we can construct an infinite closed normal series of
G
which its factors are not nilpotent of class at most
k
, violates Theorem
3.1
; indeed, suppose we have constructed a closed normal series,

(3.1)

G=H_{0}\geq H_{\alpha_{1}}\geq\dots\geq H_{\alpha_{n}}

such that its factors are not nilpotent of class at most
k
. Since
H_{\alpha_{n}}
is not
k
-step nilpotent, there exists
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k+1})
in
H_{\alpha_{n}}^{k+1}
such that
[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k+1}]\neq 1
. By the assumption, we can choose
H_{\alpha_{n+1}}
such that
[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k+1}]\not\in H_{\alpha_{n+1}}
, so
H_{\alpha_{n+1}}
can be added to (
3.2
).
∎

Let

Now we’re ready to show that the class

contains connected compact groups. A little preparation is needed before proving the later.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
be a compact group. Then
G
contains no nontrivial closed semi simple connected compact Lie subgroup

\mathbf{np}_{k,G}(H)>0

Proof.

Suppose

. Since
\mathcal{N}
is Zariski-closed and has positive measure, it should be equal to

should be nilpotent by Proposition

and hence trivial by semi simplicity.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G
be a connected compact group with
\mathbf{np}_{k}(G)>0
. Then
G
is an abelian group.

Proof.

Note first that simply connected compact groups are semi simple (

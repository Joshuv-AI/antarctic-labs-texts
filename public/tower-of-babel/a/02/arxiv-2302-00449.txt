Yangian symmetry applied to Quantum chromodynamics


Abstract
We review applications of Yangain symmetry to high-energy QCD phenomenology. Some basic facts about high-energy QCD are recalled, in particular the spinor-helicity form of scattering amplitudes, the scale evolution equations of deep-inelastic scattering structure functions and the high-energy asymptotics of scattering. As the working tool the Yangian symmetric correlators are introduced and constructed in the framework of the Yangian algebra of g ​ ℓ ​ ( n ) g\ell(n) type. We present the application to the tree scattering amplitudes and their iterative relation, to the parton splitting amplitudes and the kernels of the scale evolution equations of structure functions and to the equations describing the high-energy asymptotics of scattering.

Both the momenta and the polarization states of the scattering quarks and

The spinor components of massless momenta appear as products of the components
of a left and right spinor,

k_{\alpha,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\alpha}}}=\sigma_{\alpha,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\alpha}}}^{\mu}k_{\mu}=\lambda_{\alpha}\bar{\lambda}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\alpha}}}

(2.1)

The polarization vectors characterizing the helicity states

(2.2)

The spinor indices

K_{\alpha,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\alpha}}}=\partial_{\alpha}\bar{\partial}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\alpha}}},\ \ \ D=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}+\bar{\lambda}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\alpha}}}\bar{\partial}^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot}}{{\alpha}}}+2).

3.3
The Yang-Baxter operator

In the fundamental Yang-Baxter relation (
3.1
) the
R

the tensor product of fundamental
n

We shall obtain a more general
R
operator intertwining the tensor product
of JS type representations. It should obey the Yang-Baxter relation of the
form

R_{12}(u-v)L_{1}(u)L_{2}(v)=L_{1}(v)L_{2}(u)R_{12}(u-v),

(3.21)

(L_{1}(u))_{ab}=\delta_{ab}u+\partial^{1}_{a}x_{b}^{1},\ \ \ (L_{2}(u))_{ab}=\delta_{ab}u+\partial^{2}_{a}x_{b}^{2}.

The relation involves the matrix product
L_{1}(u)L_{2}(v)
.

R_{12}
is not a matrix, i.e. does not act on the fundamental representation
indices, but acts rather
on the two sets of canonical pairs
x_{a}^{1},\partial^{1}_{a}
and
x_{a}^{2},\partial^{2}_{a}
.

We read (
3.21
) as the defining relation of
R_{12}
and solve it
using the ansatz

R_{12}(u)=\int\mathrm{d}c\,\phi(c)e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}.

We calculate the action of the shift operator on the product of
L

matrices:

e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}\underline{\partial}_{1}=(\underline{\partial}_{1}+c\underline{\partial}_{2})e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})},\qquad e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}\mathbf{x}_{2}=(\mathbf{x}_{2}-c\mathbf{x}_{1})\ e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})},

e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}(L_{1}^{+}(u)L_{2}^{+}(v))_{ab}e^{c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}=\big(L_{1ac}^{+}(u)+c\partial^{2}_{ad}x^{1}_{dc}\big)\big(L_{2cb}^{+}(v)-c\partial^{2}_{cd}x^{1}_{db}\big)=

\big(L_{1}^{+}(v)L_{2}^{+}(u)\big)_{ab}+\big(L_{2}^{+}(0)-L_{1}^{+}(0)\big)_{ab}\{[u-v+c(\underline{x}_{1}\cdot\underline{\partial}_{2})]-c[u-v-1+c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})]\}.

The condition that the contribution of the second term
vanishes implies a differential equation
on
\phi(c)
, because

\int\mathrm{d}c\,\phi(c)\cdot c\cdot(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}=-\int\mathrm{d}c\,\phi(c)\cdot c\cdot\partial_{c}\,e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}=\int\mathrm{d}c\,\partial_{c}(c\phi(c))\,e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}.

In the last step we have assumed that the integration by parts is done
without boundary terms as it holds for closed contours.
Thus the condition on
\phi
is

\partial_{c}(c\phi(c))+(u-v)\phi(c)=0,

and it is solved by

\phi(c)=\frac{1}{c^{1+u-v}}.

The condition of vanishing of the third term can be written as

0=\partial_{c}(c^{2}\phi(c))+c(u-v-1)\phi(c)=c[\partial_{c}(c\phi(c))+(u-v)\phi(c)].

We see that it does not imply a further condition on
\phi(c)
.
Thus we have proved that

R_{12}(u)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}c}{c^{1+u}}e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}

(3.22)

obeys the Yang-Baxter relation provided the simple rule of integration by parts.
If the latter rule is different then the indicated procedure leads to the
appropriate modification.

Now we derive two Yang-Baxter relations for the projected
L
operators
(
3.12
). We notice that the shift operation included in

e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{1})e^{c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}=(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{1})+c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2}),\ \ e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}(\underline{x}_{2}\underline{\partial}_{2})e^{c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}=(\underline{x}_{2}\underline{\partial}_{2})-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2}).

By partial integration we obtain

\int dc\phi(c)c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}=-\int dc\phi(c)c\partial_{c}e^{-c(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{2})}=

In this way we have

R_{12}(u-v)\left(v+(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{1})\right)R_{12}^{-1}(u-v)=u+(\underline{x}_{1}\underline{\partial}_{1}),\ \ R_{12}(u-v)\left(u+(\underline{x}_{2}\underline{\partial}_{2})\right)R_{12}^{-1}(u-v)=v+(\underline{x}_{2}\underline{\partial}_{2})

R_{12}(u-v)\Pi_{1}(2\ell_{1})=\Pi_{1}(2\ell_{1}+u-v)R_{12}(u-v),\ \ \ R_{12}(u-v)\Pi_{2}(2\ell_{2})=\Pi_{2}(2\ell_{2}-u+v)R_{12}(u-v).

(3.23)

Two and three points

By one
R
operation we obtain the 2-point YSC

\Phi^{-+}_{12}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=R_{21}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{2})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\int\frac{dc_{12}}{c_{12}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{2}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2}).

Let us abbreviate in
\Phi
the dependence on the points
\mathbf{x}_{I}

by subscripts
I
.

We construct 3-point correlators starting from the trivial ones

\Phi_{123,0}^{--+}=\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{2}),\ \ \ \Phi_{123,0}^{-++}=\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}).

The corresponding monodromy is characterized by the set of parameters

\mathbf{u}

conveniently displayed by rows of
u_{I}
and
u^{+}_{I}
(
3.16
). We abbreviate here
parameters by their position indices
I
eventually supplemented by a superscript
+

for indicating the shift by
-n
.
In case
\Phi_{123,0}^{--+}
we have

\mathbf{u}^{--+(0)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&\\
1^{+}&2^{+}&3\end{pmatrix},

and in the case
\Phi_{123,0}^{-++}

\mathbf{u}^{-++(0)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&\\
1^{+}&2&3\end{pmatrix}.

By one
R
operation we obtain

\Phi_{123,1}^{--+}=R_{31}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{3})\Phi_{123,0}^{--+}=\int\frac{c_{13}}{c_{13}^{1+u^{+}_{1}-u_{3}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{13}\mathbf{x}_{3})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{2})

with

\mathbf{u}^{--+(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&\\
3&2^{+}&1^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

By a second
R
operation we obtain the completely connected correlator.

\Phi_{123,2}^{--+}=R_{32}u^{+}_{2}-u^{+}_{1})R_{31}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{3})\Phi_{123,0}^{--+}=\int\frac{dc_{13}}{c_{13}^{1+u^{+}_{1}-u_{3}}}\frac{dc_{23}}{c_{23}^{1+u^{+}_{2}-u^{+}_{1}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{13}\mathbf{x}_{3})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{2}-c_{23}\mathbf{x}_{3})

(3.32)

with

\mathbf{u}^{--+(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&\\
3&1^{+}&2^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.33)

For the other case we have

\Phi_{123,1}^{-++}=R_{31}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{3})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\int\frac{dc_{13}}{c_{13}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{3}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{13}\mathbf{x}_{3})

with

\mathbf{u}^{-++(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&\\
3&2&1^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

The completely connected correlator is obtained in the next step as

\Phi_{123,2}^{-++}=R_{21}(u_{3}-u_{2})R_{31}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{3})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\int\frac{dc_{13}}{c_{13}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{3}}}\frac{dc_{12}}{c_{12}^{1+u_{3}-u_{2}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2}-c_{13}\mathbf{x}_{3})

(3.34)

with

\mathbf{u}^{-++(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&\\
2&3&1^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.35)

3.5.2
Four points

We illustrate the construction of a connected 4-point YSC
in two ways, first by R operations
on the product of 2-point YSC and second by the convolution
of two 3-point YSC followed by a R operation.

We start from the product of 2-point YSC,

\Phi_{1234,2}=\Phi_{12}\Phi_{34}=R_{21}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{2})R_{43}(u_{3}^{+}-u_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3})=

\int\frac{dc_{12}dc_{34}}{c_{12}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{2}}c_{34}^{1+u_{3}^{+}-u_{4}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{34}\mathbf{x}_{4}).

The corresponding monodromy is characterised by the set of parameters

\mathbf{u}^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&4\\
2&1^{+}&4&3^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.36)

To obtain a completely connected 4-point YSC we act by
R_{23}R_{32}
.
The appropriate arguments are read off from (
3.36
).
The action by
R_{32}
results in

\Phi_{1234,3}=R_{32}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{4})\Phi_{1234,2}=

\int\frac{dc_{23}dc_{12}dc_{34}}{c_{23}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{4}}c_{12}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{2}}c_{34}^{1+u_{3}^{+}-u_{4}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2}-c_{12}c_{23}c_{34}\mathbf{x}_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{34}\mathbf{x}_{4}).

The substitution
c_{14}=c_{12}c_{23}c_{34}
leads to

\Phi_{1234,3}=\int\frac{dc_{23}dc_{12}dc_{34}}{c_{14}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{4}}c_{12}^{1+u_{4}-u_{2}}c_{34}^{1+u_{3}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2}-c_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{34}\mathbf{x}_{4}).

The monodromy parameters are

\mathbf{u}^{(3)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&4\\
2&4&1^{+}&3^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.37)

This correlator is still not completely connected.
The action by
R_{23}
leads to the completely connected correlator

\Phi_{1234,4}=\int\frac{dc_{32}dc_{23}dc_{12}dc_{34}}{c_{32}^{1+u_{2}-u_{3}}c_{14}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{4}}c_{12}^{1+u_{4}-u_{2}}c_{34}^{1+u_{3}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2}-c_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{32}\mathbf{x}_{2}-c_{34}\mathbf{x}_{4}),

with

\mathbf{u}^{(4)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&3&2&4\\
2&4&1^{+}&3^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.38)

Now we show how the same result is obtained by the convolution
method.

We consider the convolution of 3-point YSC (
3.34
) and
(
3.32
),

\int d\mathbf{x}_{0}\Phi_{120,2}^{-++}(\mathbf{u})\Phi_{034,2}^{--+}(\mathbf{v}).

The condition on the spectral parameters is obtained from (
3.19
)
taking into account the parameter sets (
3.35
) and (
3.33

v_{0}-n=u_{1}-n,u_{0}-n=v_{4}-n.

(3.39)

Thus we have

\int\frac{dc_{12}dc_{10}dc_{04}dc_{34}}{c_{12}^{1+v_{4}-u_{2}}c_{10}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-v_{4}}c_{04}^{1+u_{1}-n-v_{4}}c_{34}^{1+u_{2}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\widetilde{c}_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2}-\widetilde{c}_{10}c_{04}\mathbf{x}_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{34}\mathbf{x}_{4}).

The substitution
c_{14}=c_{10}c_{04}
leads to

\int\frac{dc_{04}}{c_{04}}\int\frac{dc_{12}dc_{14}dc_{34}}{c_{12}^{1+v_{4}-u_{2}}c_{14}^{1+u_{1}^{+}-v_{4}}c_{34}^{1+u_{2}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}}}\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\widetilde{c}_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2}-\widetilde{c}_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{34}\mathbf{x}_{4}).

The first integral can be regarded as an irrelevant factor.
The result can be represented as generated directly by three

R
operators as in the next-to-last step of
R
operations above

R_{32}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{4})R_{21}(u_{1}^{+}-u_{2})R_{43}(u_{3}^{+}-u_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3})

with the substitution
c_{14}=c_{12}c_{23}c_{34}
, i.e.
it coincides with
\Phi_{1234,3}
and the parameter set with (
3.37
).
As above the
completely connected correlator (
3.38
) is obtained by the action
of
R_{23}
. Thus the reconstruction of this 4-point YSC by convolution of
two 3-point YSC can be summarized as

\Phi_{1234,4}=R_{23}(u_{2}-u_{3})\int d\mathbf{x}_{0}\Phi_{120,2}^{-++}(\mathbf{u})\Phi_{034,2}^{--+}(\mathbf{v})|_{v|u},

(3.40)

where
v|u
advises the substitution (
3.39

The points of the correlators seem to be of two types marked
by signature
\pm
. Those
\mathbf{x}_{I}

entering the initial trivial correlator
\Phi_{...,0}
by
\delta(\mathbf{x}_{I})

we have marked by
-
and the others by
+

our examples above of 4-point YSC
carry the signature
-+-+

assignment is inessential. This holds in general, we show this in the case of 4 points.

The standard
-+-+
form

\Phi_{1234}^{-+-+}=\int dc\varphi^{-+-+}(c_{12},c_{14},c_{32},c_{34})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{12}\mathbf{x}_{2}-c_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{32}\mathbf{x}_{2}-c_{34}\mathbf{x}_{4})

\Phi_{1234}^{--++}=\int dc^{\prime}\varphi^{--++}(c^{\prime})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c^{\prime}_{13}\mathbf{x}_{3}-c^{\prime}_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{2}-c^{\prime}_{23}\mathbf{x}_{3}-c^{\prime}_{24}\mathbf{x}_{4})

\varphi^{--++}(c^{\prime}_{13},c^{\prime}_{14},c^{\prime}_{23},c^{\prime}_{24})=c_{23}^{\prime n-4}\varphi^{-+-+}(\frac{c^{\prime}_{13}}{c^{\prime}_{23}},\frac{c^{\prime}_{14}c^{\prime}_{23}-c^{\prime}_{24}c^{\prime}_{13}}{c^{\prime}_{23}},\frac{1}{c^{\prime}_{23}},-\frac{c^{\prime}_{24}}{c^{\prime}_{23}}).

(3.41)

Therefore the maximally connected correlator (
3.38
)
transforms to the
--++
form as

\Phi_{1234,4}^{--++}=\int\frac{dc^{\prime}_{13}dc^{\prime}_{14}dc^{\prime}_{23}dc^{\prime}_{24}}{(c^{\prime}_{13})^{1+u_{4}-u_{2}}(c^{\prime}_{24})^{1+u_{3}^{+}-u_{1}^{+}}(c^{\prime}_{14}c^{\prime}_{23}-c^{\prime}_{24}c^{\prime}_{13})^{1+u_{1}^{+}-u_{4}}}

(3.42)

\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c^{\prime}_{13}\mathbf{x}_{3}-c^{\prime}_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})\ \delta(\mathbf{x}_{2}-c^{\prime}_{23}\mathbf{x}_{3}-c^{\prime}_{24}\mathbf{x}_{4})

Both expressions
\Phi_{1234,4}^{-+-+}
and
\Phi_{1234,4}^{--++}

are equivalent, in particular they obey the YSC condition (
3.17
)
with the same monodromy of the parameters (
3.38
).
The latter expression (
3.42
) can be obtained directly from

\Phi_{1234,0}^{--++}=\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{2})

by
R
operations

\Phi_{1234,4}^{--++}=R_{12}(v_{1}-v_{2})R_{21}(v_{4}-v_{3})R_{32}(v_{2}^{+}-v_{3})R_{41}(v_{1}^{+}-v_{4})\ \delta(\mathbf{x}_{1})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{2})

(3.43)

and corresponds to the monodromy with the parameters
\mathbf{v}

abbreviated by the pattern

\mathbf{v}^{(4)}=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&3&4\\
3&4&2^{+}&1^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.44)

The parameters
\mathbf{u}^{(4)}
and
\mathbf{v}^{(4)}
are related as

u_{1}=v_{2},u_{2}=v_{3},u_{3}=v_{1},u_{4}=v_{4}

Indeed, r.h.s of (
3.43

\int\frac{dc_{21}dc_{12}dc_{23}dc_{14}}{c_{21}^{1+v_{1}-v_{2}}c_{12}^{1+v_{4}-v_{3}}c_{23}^{1+v_{1}^{+}-v_{3}}c_{14}^{1+v_{1}^{+}-v_{4}}}

\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c_{12}c_{23}\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})\delta(\mathbf{x}_{2}-(1+c_{12}c_{21}+1)c_{23}\mathbf{x}_{3}-c_{21}c_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})

The transformation to the standard
--++
form (
3.41

results in

\Phi_{1234,4}^{--++}=\int\frac{dc_{13}^{\prime}dc_{14}^{\prime}dc_{23}^{\prime}dc_{24}^{\prime}}{c_{13}^{\prime 1+v_{4}-v_{3}}c_{24}^{\prime 1+v_{1}-v_{2}}(c^{\prime}_{14}c^{\prime}_{23}-c^{\prime}_{24}c^{\prime}_{13})^{1+v_{2}^{+}-v_{4}}}

(3.45)

\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-c^{\prime}_{13}\mathbf{x}_{3}-c^{\prime}_{14}\mathbf{x}_{4})\ \delta(\mathbf{x}_{2}-c^{\prime}_{23}\mathbf{x}_{3}-c^{\prime}_{24}\mathbf{x}_{4})

with the monodromy parameters (
3.44

We shall use
\Phi_{1234;4}^{--++}
(
3.43

and

3.5.3
Crossing

We recall that
the parameters
u^{+}_{I},u_{I}
are related to the dilatation weight of the YSC
at the point
I
as
2\ell_{I}=u_{I}^{+}-u_{I}
. The set of weights in
a correlator
\Phi_{K,N}
obeys the constraint

\sum_{I=1}^{N}2\ell_{I}+nK=0.

(3.46)

The weights of the 4-point YSC
\Phi_{1234,4}
constructed above
are more restricted,

2\ell_{1}+2\ell_{3}+n=0,\ \ 2\ell_{2}+2\ell_{4}+n=0.

(3.47)

We intend to construct a 4-point YSC where such constraints connect
the points
1,4
and
2,3
instead.

For this we apply additional
R
operators to the YSC (
3.44
), (
3.45
),

R_{43}(v_{2}-v_{1})R_{34}(v_{3}-v_{4})\Phi_{1234;4}^{--++4}.

The action affects the arguments of the delta distributions
(
3.45
),

\delta(\mathbf{x}_{1}-(c_{13}-c_{14}\bar{c}_{42})\mathbf{x}_{3}-(c_{14}(1+\bar{c}_{43}\bar{c}_{34})-c_{13}\bar{c}_{34})\mathbf{x}_{4})\ \delta(\mathbf{x}_{2}-(c_{23}-c_{24}\bar{c}_{42})\mathbf{x}_{3}-(c_{24}(1+\bar{c}_{43}\bar{c}_{34})-c_{23}\bar{c}_{34})\mathbf{x}_{4}).

The standard form (
3.26
) is recovered by the transformation of the
variables
c_{I3},c_{I4}
to
\widetilde{c}_{I3},\widetilde{c}_{I4}

(
I=1,2
),

\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{c}_{I3}\\
\widetilde{c}_{I4}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\bar{c}_{43}\\
-\bar{c}_{34}&1+\bar{c}_{43}\bar{c}_{34}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{I3}\\
c_{I4}\\
\end{pmatrix},

by the matrix depending on the variables
\bar{c}_{34},\bar{c}_{43}
,
corresponding to the additional
R_{43},R_{34}
respectively.

We transform the correlator integrand
\varphi(c)
of
\Phi_{1234,4}^{--++}

(
3.45
) to the new variables

(\varphi(c)^{-1}\cdot\bar{c}_{34}^{1+v_{2}-v_{1}}\bar{c}_{43}^{1+v_{3}-v_{4}}=((1+\bar{c}_{43}\bar{c}_{34})\widetilde{c}_{13}\bar{c}_{43}\widetilde{c}_{14})^{1+v_{4}-v_{3}}\ (\bar{c}_{34}\widetilde{c}_{23}+\widetilde{c}_{24})^{1+v_{1}-v_{2}}

(\widetilde{c}_{13}\widetilde{c}_{24}-\widetilde{c}_{23}\widetilde{c}_{14})^{1+v_{2}^{+}-v_{4}}\cdot\bar{c}_{34}^{1+v_{2}-v_{1}}\bar{c}_{43}^{1+v_{3}-v_{4}}=

We do the integrals over
\bar{c}_{43},\bar{c}_{34}

\int\frac{d\bar{c}_{43}}{\bar{c}_{43}^{2}(\widetilde{c}_{14}+\frac{\widetilde{c}_{13}}{\bar{c}_{43}}(1+\bar{c}_{43}\bar{c}_{34}))^{1+v_{4}-v_{3}}}=\widetilde{c}_{14}^{-1-v_{4}+v_{3}}\int\frac{dc^{\prime}_{43}}{(1+\frac{\widetilde{c}_{13}}{\widetilde{c}_{14}}\bar{c}_{34}+c^{\prime}_{34}\frac{\widetilde{c}_{13}}{\widetilde{c}_{14}})^{1+v_{4}-v_{3}}}.

We have substituted

\frac{1}{\widetilde{c}_{13}\widetilde{c}_{14}^{v_{4}-v_{3}}}\int\frac{c_{43}^{\prime\prime}}{c_{43}^{\prime\prime 1+v_{4}-v_{3}}}.

Similarly, we substitute
c_{34}^{\prime}=\frac{\widetilde{c}_{24}}{\widetilde{c}_{23}\bar{c}_{34}}

\int\frac{d\bar{c}_{34}}{\bar{c}_{34}^{2}(\widetilde{c}_{23}+\frac{\widetilde{c}_{24}}{\bar{c}_{34}})^{1+v_{1}-v_{2}}}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{c}_{24}\widetilde{c}_{23}^{v_{1}-v_{2}}}\int\frac{c_{34}^{\prime}}{c_{43}^{\prime 1+v_{1}-v_{2}}}.

The integrals over
c_{43}^{\prime\prime},c_{34}^{\prime}
result
in undefined but irrelevant factors.
As the result the correlator

3.26
)
with the integrand
\widetilde{\varphi}

(3.48)

(3.49)

3.6
Integral operators from symmetric correlators

We consider
integral operators with YSC as kernels. Such operators
act in a highly symmetric way, defining
homomorphisms not only of the
g\ell(n)
Lie algebra action on the functions
(global symmetry) but of the
related Yangian algebra. Let us illustrate this in the case of
operators on functions of two points with 4-point YSC as kernels
[
79
]
.

We define the operator
Q
in action on
\psi(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})

as

Q\psi(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=\int d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1}d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2}\psi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2})\Phi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\mathbf{x}_{1})

(3.52)

with the YSC condition (
3.17
) ( relabelling

on the kernel

L_{2^{\prime}}(u_{2}^{\prime+},u_{2}^{\prime})L_{1^{\prime}}(u_{1}^{\prime+},u_{1}^{\prime})L_{2}(u_{2}^{+},u_{2})L_{1}(u_{1}^{+},u_{1})\Phi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\mathbf{x}_{1})=E(\mathbf{u})\Phi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\mathbf{x}_{1}).

The involved monodromy has the parameter pattern

\mathbf{u}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}2^{\prime}&1^{\prime}&2&1\\
2^{\prime+}&1^{\prime+}&2^{+}&1^{+}\end{pmatrix}.

We show that

obeys

L_{2}(u_{2}^{+},u_{2})L_{1}(u_{1}^{+},u_{1})Q=F((\mathbf{u}^{\prime})QL_{1}(u_{1}^{\prime}-n,u_{1}^{\prime+})L_{2}(u_{2}^{\prime}-n,u_{2}^{\prime+}),

(3.54)

F(\mathbf{u}^{\prime})=\frac{E(\mathbf{u}^{\prime})}{u_{1}^{\prime}(u_{1}^{\prime+}+1)u_{2}^{\prime}(u_{2}^{\prime+}+1)}.

Indeed by the YSC condition on the kernel,

L_{2}(u_{2}^{+},u_{2})L_{1}(u_{1}^{+},u_{1})Q=E(\mathbf{u})\int d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1}d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2}\psi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2})L^{-1}_{1^{\prime}}(u_{1}^{\prime+},u_{1}^{\prime})L^{-1}_{2^{\prime}}(u_{2}^{\prime+},u_{2}^{\prime})\Phi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\mathbf{x}_{1}).

We integrate by parts, assuming that the integration region is such that
no boundary contributions appear and the relations (
3.11
,
3.14
)
apply.

E(\mathbf{u}^{\prime})\int d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1}d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2}(L^{-1}_{1^{\prime}}(u_{1}^{\prime+},u_{1}^{\prime}))^{T}(L^{-1}_{2^{\prime}}(u_{2}^{\prime+},u_{2}^{\prime}))^{T}\psi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2})\Phi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\mathbf{x}_{1})=

\frac{E(\mathbf{u}^{\prime})}{u_{1}^{\prime}(u_{1}^{\prime+}+1)u_{2}^{\prime}(u_{2}^{\prime+}+1)}\int d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1}d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2}L_{1^{\prime}}(u_{1}^{\prime}-n,u_{1}^{\prime+})L_{2^{\prime}}(u_{2}^{\prime}-n,u_{2}^{\prime+})\psi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2})\Phi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{2},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\mathbf{x}_{1}).

The relation proven for arbitrary functions implies the operator relation
(
3.54
).

If

4.2

In sect. 3.2.1 and 3.5.2 we have shown in the particular case of three points

7
Conclusions

The L operator matrices with the Jordan-Schwinger presentation of
the
g\ell(n)
algebra as its matrix elements can be regarded as the
basis of our approach. The monodromies built as ordered matrix products of
L operators enter the definition of the YSC. The properties of the
L operators result in relations for YSC and in methods of their
construction.

By elementary canonical transformations different forms of the
L operators are obtained leading to different representation forms of the
YSC. The original JS form and the helicity form appear in the applications.

The R operators obeying the Yang-Baxter RLL relation
involving products of two such L operators play a central role.
A particular solution for such a R operator has the
form of a closed contour integral over a shift operator. It serves as the
tool of constructing YSC. Integral operators built by
particular 4-point YSC turn out to be solutions of the RLL relation.
In the case
n=2
the spectrum of operators obeying the RLL relation
and a further operator form of R have been studied and applied.

The L operators projected to the action on homogeneous functions depend on
two parameters, the order N monodromy composed of them and the
corresponding N-point YSC depend on the set of 2N parameters. We choose these
parameters in a form, where the action of the R operators implies permutations
of these parameters.

In the applications the monodromy parameters are related to the particle
types and helicities. The YSC with generic parameter values turn to
physically relevant objects by specification of the parameters.

We have shown that the 3-point amplitudes, the building blocks of
the iterative BCFW construction of tree amplitudes, are specifications of
3-point YSC. We have explained that this iterative construction is in fact
the convolution method of YSC construction. We have shown how
specifications of the parameters of 4-point YSC result in the
2\to 2

tree amplitudes of gluons and quarks.

We have seen that the parton splitting amplitudes, appearing in the
collinear limit from the 3-point amplitudes, are obtained from specifications
of 3-point YSC. The 4-point YSC define as kernels integral operators obeying
relations of the RLL type. The latter allow to derive the eigenvalues of
those operators. We have shown how by the specification of parameters in
4-point YSC and
integration contours the kernels of the Bjorken evolution equation
are obtained. This has been confirmed by showing that the corresponding
eigenvalues and the anomalous dimensions are proportional.

The reggeon triple vertex decomposes in a holomorphic and an anti-holomorphic
factor, each of which is obtained as a specification of 3-point YSC.
The R operator obeying the RLL relation with the L operators specified to
the normal coordinate form of the
g\ell(2)
case can be obtained
by the parameter permutation method and contains the (holomorphic part of)
BFKL hamiltonian as the first non-trivial term in its decomposition.
The integral operator form of this R operator is obtained by
specifying a 4-point YSC as its kernel. For the action on functions of
two points in the transverse plane the corresponding R operator
with two factors of such 4-point YSC as its kernel is constructed.
The dipole form of the BFKL hamiltonian appears in the
decomposition of the latter R operator.

Abstract
. Given a rough definably amenable rough approximate subgroup A A of a group in some first-order structure, there is a type-definable subgroup H H normalised by A A and contained in A 4 A^{4} of bounded index in ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle .

03C98, 11P70, 20A15

Introduction

In
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
, Hrushovski found a fundamental connexion between approximate subgroups and Lie groups. This result, now known as the Lie Model Theorem, was the starting point for the complete classification of finite approximate subgroups by Breuillard, Green and Tao
[
1
]
.

The proof of
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
first shows that, for any definable approximate subgroup
A
, under a certain definable amenability condition, there is a type-definable normal subgroup
S
of bounded index of
\langle A\rangle
contained in
A^{4}
. For this first fundamental step, Hrushovski proves a general Stabilizer Theorem
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
, working with an
S_{1}
ideal of definable sets which, in addition, is invariant under translations and automorphisms. Then, in
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
, this general Stabilizer Theorem is applied for the
S_{1}
ideal of sets of measure zero. However, in order for this ideal to become invariant under automorphisms, one needs to work with an expansion of the initial language.

In a joint work with Massicot, the second named author provided in
[
5
]
an alternative proof of Hrushovski’s Lie Model Theorem avoiding any expansion of the language. While Hrushovski’s original proof only uses the ideal of measure zero subsets, the argument in
[
5
]
fundamentally exploits the additivity of the measure.

In
[
6
]
, the first named author adapted
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
and
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
for hyperdefinable sets, providing in particular a generalisation of the Lie Model Theorem for rough approximate subgroups. This result was later used in
[
4
]
for the particular case of metric groups. The argument in
[
6
]
uses again a general version of the Stabilizer Theorem (now for hyperdefinable sets), and so the applications in
[
4
]
require again to work with an expansion of the initial language.

The aim of this paper is to adapt the alternative technique developed in
[
5
]
for the case studied in
[
4
]
. This is not a straightforward task — while the ideal used for the original Lie Model Theorem is directly given by a measure, the ideal in
[
4
]
is not associated with a measure at all. Instead, the ideal in
[
4
]
is built as the inf-limit of the zero ideals of a sequence of subadditive set functions. Hence, in
[
4
]
, we lack the additivity property that is fundamentally used in
[
5
]
. Fortunately, a careful analysis of the properties of these subadditive set functions shows that, although they are not completely additive, they still keep some rough additivity that suffices to conclude the proof.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1
.

Assume
A
is
T
-rough definably amenable with respect to
(\mu_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and suppose that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there is

such that
\mu_{i}(A^{m})<\infty
for all

. Then, there is a type-definable normal subgroup

of
\langle AT_{\omega}\rangle
of bounded index such that
T_{\omega}\subseteq N\subseteq A^{4}T_{\omega}
.

As a corollary, we get the following improved version of
[

:

1.
Rough Measures

Through this section, let
T\subseteq G
be a symmetric subset and
\mathcal{A}
some subalgebra of the boolean algebra of definable subsets of
G
containing
A
and closed under
T
-thickenings.

Definition 3
.

A
T
-rough measure
on
\mathcal{A}
is an increasing subadditive set function
\mu:\,\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
which is additive modulo
T
, i.e. for
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}
with
X\cap YT=\emptyset
we have
\mu(X\cup Y)=\mu(X)+\mu(Y)
. For a type-definable set
X
we put
\mu(X)\coloneqq\inf\{\mu(\bar{X})\mathrel{:}\bar{X}\supseteq X\mathrm{\ definable}\}
. We say that
\mu
is
normalised
at
A
if
\mu(A)=1
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mu
be a
T
-rough measure on
\mathcal{A}
. Then, for any
S_{0},\ldots,S_{n-1}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\mu\left(\textstyle{\bigcup_{i<n}}S_{i}\right)\geq\sum_{i<n}\mu(S_{i})-\sum_{\mathclap{i<j<n}}\min\{\mu(S_{i}\cap S_{j}T),\mu(S_{i}T\cap S_{j})\}.

Proof.

For
n<2
, there is nothing to prove. For
n=2
, by subadditivity,

\mu(S_{0})\leq\mu(S_{0}\setminus S_{1}T)+\mu(S_{0}\cap S_{1}T).

Hence, by additivity modulo
T
, we get

\displaystyle\mu(S_{0}\cup S_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\mu((S_{0}\setminus S_{1}T)\cup S_{1})=\mu(S_{0}\setminus S_{1}T)+\mu(S_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\mu(S_{0})+\mu(S_{1})-\mu(S_{0}\cap S_{1}T).

By symmetry,
\mu(S_{0}\cup S_{1})\geq\mu(S_{0})+\mu(S_{1})-\mu(S_{0}T\cap S_{1})
, and the result follows.

Suppose the assertion is true for
n
, and consider
S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
. Put

I=\{i<n\mathrel{:}\mu(S_{i}\cap S_{n}T)\leq\mu(S_{i}T\cap S_{n})\},

and set
X=\bigcup_{i\in I}S_{i}
and
X^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i\in n\setminus I}S_{i}
. Suppose

x\in X^{\prime}\cap(S_{n}\setminus X^{\prime}T)T
; say
x=yt
with
y\in S_{n}\setminus X^{\prime}T
and
t\in T
. Then,
y=xt^{-1}\in X^{\prime}T
, a contradiction. Therefore,
(X\cup X^{\prime})\cap(S_{n}\setminus X^{\prime}T)T\subseteq X\cap S_{n}T
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mu\left(\textstyle{\bigcup_{i\leq n}}S_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mu(X\cup X^{\prime}\cup(S_{n}\setminus X^{\prime}T))

\displaystyle\geq\mu(X\cup X^{\prime})+\mu(S_{n}\setminus X^{\prime}T)-\mu((X\cup X^{\prime})\cap(S_{n}\setminus X^{\prime}T)T)

\displaystyle\geq\mu(X\cup X^{\prime})+\mu(S_{n})-\mu(S_{n}\cap X^{\prime}T)-\mu(X\cap S_{n}T)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\leq n}\mu(S_{i})-\sum_{\mathclap{i<j<n}}\min\{\mu(S_{i}\cap S_{j}T),\mu(S_{i}T\cap S_{j})\}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\mathclap{i\in n\setminus I}}\mu(S_{i}T\cap S_{n})-\sum_{i\in I}\mu(S_{i}\cap S_{n}T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\leq n}\mu(S_{i})-\sum_{\mathclap{i<j\leq n}}\min\{\mu(S_{i}\cap S_{j}T),\mu(S_{i}T\cap S_{j})\}.

∎

Remark 5
.

The above lemma also holds for type-definable sets, i.e. bounded intersections of definable sets in
\mathcal{A}
, by taking limits.

Definition 6
.

Let
X

Note that, since

.

Lemma 7
.

If
Y
is
t
-thick in
X
, then
t
left translates of

cover

. Conversely, if
t
left translates of
Y
cover

, then
Y^{-1}Y
is
t
-thick in

.

Proof.

Take
Z\subseteq X
maximal such that
z_{1}^{-1}z_{2}\notin Y
for

with
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
. Since
Y
is

-thick,

exists and
|Z|\leq t
. Since

is maximal, for any

, there is

such that

; in other words,

.

Conversely, if
X\subseteq ZY
with

, by the pigeonhole principle, for any
g_{0},\ldots,g_{t}
in

, there are

, so

. Since

-thick

Lemma 8
.

Let
X
be a definable subset of
G
. An arbitrary intersection of type-definable subsets of
G
which are thick in
X
is again thick in
X
.

Proof.

Let us see first that a finite intersection of definable thick subsets in
X
is thick in
X
. If
Y\subseteq G
is definable and
t
-thick in
X
and
Y^{\prime}\subseteq G
is definable and
t^{\prime}
-thick in
X
, then the intersection
Y\cap Y^{\prime}
is
R(2,t+1,t^{\prime}+1)
-thick in
X
, where
R({\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}},{\scriptscriptstyle{\bullet}})
is the Ramsey number with respect to a
3
-colouring. Indeed,
Y\cap Y^{\prime}
is obviously definable and symmetric. Now, given any
Z
subset of
X
of size
R(2,t+1,t^{\prime}+1)
, colour the edges of the complete graph on
Z
by

•

\{z,z^{\prime}\}
is yellow if
z^{-1}z^{\prime}\in Y\cap Y^{\prime}
,

•

\{z,z^{\prime}\}
is blue if
z^{-1}z^{\prime}\notin Y
,

•

\{z,z^{\prime}\}
is red if
z^{-1}z^{\prime}\in Y\setminus Y^{\prime}
.

Since
Y
is
t
-thick,
Z
cannot contain a complete blue subgraph of size
t+1
and, as
Y^{\prime}
is
t^{\prime}
-thick,
Z
cannot contain a complete red subgraph of size
t^{\prime}+1
either. Thus, it must contain a yellow edge, i.e. there are
z,z^{\prime}\in Z
with
z\neq z^{\prime}
such that
z^{-1}z^{\prime}\in Y\cap Y^{\prime}
. As
Z
is arbitrary, we conclude that
Y\cap Y^{\prime}
is
R(2,t+1,t^{\prime}+1)
-thick in
X
. By induction, from the two case, we conclude that any finite intersection of definable thick subsets in
X
is again thick in
X
.

Now, let
\bigcap_{I}Y_{i}
be an intersection of type-definable subsets of
G
thick in
X
and
\bar{Y}
be a definable symmetric superset of
\bigcap_{I}Y_{i}
. By compactness, there are a finite
I_{0}\subseteq I
and definable symmetric supersets
\bar{Y}_{i}
of

for each

such that
\bigcap_{I_{0}}\bar{Y}_{i}\subseteq\bar{Y}
. As
\bigcap_{I_{0}}\bar{Y}_{i}
is thick in
X
, so is
\bar{Y}
; as
\bar{Y}
is arbitrary,
\bigcap_{I}Y_{i}
is thick in
X
.∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mu
be a
T
-rough measure on
\mathcal{A}
normalised at
A
. Suppose that
A^{2m}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\mu(A^{2m})<\infty
. Let
t>0
be an integer and
B\in\mathcal{A}
with
B\subseteq A^{m}
and
\mu(B)\geq 2\mu(A^{2m})/t
. Put

\mathrm{S}(B)=\left\{g\in A^{2m}\mathrel{:}\min\{\mu(B\cap gBT),\mu(gB\cap BT)\}\geq\frac{2\mu(A^{2m})}{t^{2}}\right\}.

Then,
\mathrm{S}(B)
is
t
-thick in
A^{m}
.

Note that
\mathrm{S}(B)
is symmetric, since
A
is symmetric and
\mu
is left invariant. Also, note that any
g\in G
with
B\cap gBT
or
gB\cap BT
non-empty must be in
BTB^{-1}
, so, in particular,
\mathrm{S}(B)\subseteq BTB^{-1}
.

Proof.

Suppose, aiming for a contradiction, that the conclusion is false, and consider a counter-example
(g_{i}\mathrel{:}i\leq t)
. Then,

\min\{\mu(g_{i}B\cap g_{j}BT),\mu(g_{i}BT\cap g_{j}B)\}<\frac{2\mu(A^{2m})}{t^{2}},

for all
0\leq i<j\leq t
. By Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle\mu(A^{2m})

\displaystyle\geq\mu\Big(\textstyle{\bigcup_{i\leq t}}\,g_{i}B\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\leq t}\mu(g_{i}B)-\sum_{\mathclap{0\leq i<j\leq t}}\min\{\mu(g_{i}B\cap g_{j}BT),\mu(g_{i}BT\cap g_{j}B)\}

\displaystyle>(t+1)\cdot\frac{2\mu(A^{2m})}{t}-\frac{(t+1)t}{2}\cdot\frac{2\mu(A^{2m})}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mu(A^{2m})\cdot\frac{t+1}{t}>\mu(A^{2m}),

a contradiction.
∎

G

T_{i+1}^{2}\subseteq T_{i}

T_{i+1}^{A}\subseteq T_{i}

Definition 10
.

We say that

-rough measure
\mu_{i}

Remark 11
.

We do not really need

which contains

Lemma 12
.

Let
P
be a partially ordered set and
f_{0},\ldots,f_{r-1}:\,P\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be increasing functions. Suppose there is
a>0
such that
f_{k}(x)\geq a
for all
x\in P
and each

. Then, for any
\varepsilon_{0},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r-1}>0
, there is
x\in P
such that
f_{k}(y)>(1-\varepsilon_{k})\cdot f_{k}(x)
for any
y\in P
with
y<x
and each
k<r
.

Proof.

Aiming for a contradiction, suppose otherwise. Then, we can recursively construct a decreasing sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
P
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is
i<r
with
f_{i}(x_{n+1})\leq(1-\varepsilon_{i})\cdot f_{i}(x_{n})
. Since
r\in\mathbb{N}
is finite, by the pigeonhole principle, there is
i<r
and
N\subseteq\mathbb{N}
infinite such that
f_{i}(x_{n+1})\leq(1-\varepsilon_{i})\cdot f_{i}(x_{n})
for any
n\in N
. Since
f_{i}
is monotonous, we get that
f_{i}(x_{m})\leq(1-\varepsilon_{i})\cdot f_{i}(x_{n})
for any
n\in N
and
m>n
. Let
n_{0}\coloneqq\min N
and
b\coloneqq f_{i}(x_{n_{0}})
. For any
n\in N
, put
\alpha\coloneqq|\{m\in N\mathrel{:}m<n\}|
. Then,

a\leq f_{i}(x_{n})\leq(1-\varepsilon_{i})^{\alpha}\cdot f_{i}(x_{n_{0}})=(1-\varepsilon_{i})^{\alpha}\cdot b,

getting a contradiction when
\alpha>\ln(a/b)/\ln(1-\varepsilon_{i})
. ∎

Proposition 13
.

Let
A\subseteq G
be

-rough definably amenable, and

and

\mu_{i}(A^{2m(r+1)})<\infty
for each

. Suppose

Proof.

For all

, define recursively on

\mathscr{T}^{t}_{i,j}

-thick in

. Note that

is symmetric.

By induction on

\{b\mathrel{:}X(b)\in\mathscr{T}^{t}_{i,j}\}

Next, we claim that, if

-thick in
A^{m}
and, by induction hypothesis,

, concluding that

.

Let

Definition 15

Let

a symmetric subset. We say that

-rough

-approximate

Remark 16
.

The notion of rough approximate subgroups has been studied by several authors before (e.g.

,
[

). Typically, rough approximate subgroups are only defined in the context of metric groups where

Assume

3.
Applications to Metric Groups

In this section we explain how to apply Theorem
14
to get Corollary
2
(i.e.
[
4
, Theorem 20]
). Recall that a
metric group
is a group
G
together with a metric
\mathrm{d}
invariant under left translations. We denote by
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
the closed ball of radius
r
at the identity. For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, a subset
A\subseteq G
is
(\ell,r)
-Lipschitz
if every right translation by an element of
A
is
\ell
-Lipschitz in
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
, i.e.
\mathrm{d}(xa,ya)\leq\ell\cdot\mathrm{d}(x,y)
for any
a\in A
and
x,y\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
. For a subset
X\subseteq G
, we define
\mathrm{N}_{r}(X)\coloneqq\max\{|Z|\mathrel{:}Z\subseteq X
and
\mathrm{d}(z,z^{\prime})>r
for all
z,z^{\prime}\in Z
with
z\neq z^{\prime}\}
.

Remark 19
.

Note that
\mathrm{N}_{r}
is a
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
-rough measure invariant under left translations. Indeed, as the metric is invariant under left translations, so is
\mathrm{N}_{r}
. If
Z\subseteq X\cup Y
is
r
-separated,
|Z|\leq|Z\cap X|+|Z\cap Y|
where
Z\cap X\subseteq X
and
Z\cap Y\subseteq Y
are
r
-separated, so
\mathrm{N}_{r}
is subadditive. Finally, if
X\cap Y\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}=\emptyset
, then
X
and
Y
are
r
-separated. As the union of two
r
-separated subsets which are
r
-separated is
r
-separated,
\mathrm{N}_{r}(X\cup Y)=\mathrm{N}_{r}(X)+\mathrm{N}_{r}(Y)
, so
\mathrm{N}_{r}
is
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
-rough additive.

Fix natural numbers
\ell
and
(K_{i}\mathrel{:}i\in\mathbb{N})
. Let
(G_{m},A_{m},r_{i,m})_{i\leq m\in\mathbb{N}}
be such that

(1)

G_{m}
is a metric group,

(2)

A_{m}
is an
(\ell,r_{0,m})
-Lipschitz symmetric subset,

(3)

r_{m}=(r_{i,m})_{i\leq m}
is a finite sequence of positive real numbers with

and

\mathrm{N}_{r_{i,m}}(A^{4}_{m})\leq K_{i}\cdot\mathrm{N}_{r_{i,m}}(A_{m})<\infty.

Consider the language of groups enriched with a predicate for
A_{m}
and predicates for
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{i,m}}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
— where
r_{i,m}=r_{m,m}
for

; we consider
(G_{m},X_{m},r_{i,m})_{i\leq m\in\mathbb{N}}
as a sequence of structures in this language. Pick a non-principal ultrafilter
\mathfrak{u}
of
\mathbb{N}
and let

be the corresponding ultraproduct — recall that this is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated
[
8
, Exercise 5.2.3]
. Set

and

Lemma 20

A

-rough definably amenable with respect to

∎

By Corollary

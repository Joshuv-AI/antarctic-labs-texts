p\in(0,1)

be the waiting time for the first run of
k
consecutive successes.
Also define
q=1-p

Then

geometric distribution of order
k

For

[

[

Its probability generating function (pgf) is
[

\mathscr{P}(s)=\frac{p^{k}s^{k}(1-ps)}{1-s+qp^{k}s^{k+1}}=\frac{p^{k}s^{k}}{1-qs(1+pk+\dots+k^{k-1}s^{k-1})}\,.

(1)

Its moments (and factorial moments) can be obtained from the derivatives of the pgf.
Our interest in the factorial moments.
Denote the
r^{th}
factorial moment by
\mu_{(r)}=\mathbb{E}[N_{k}(N_{k}-1)\dots(N_{k}-r+1)]

r=1,2,\dots
.
However, the derivatives of the pgf are tedious to compute.
In this note, we derive a simple expression for
\mu_{(r)}
in terms of the
probability mass function (pmf)
f_{k}(n)=P(N_{k}=n)

First we establish some notation and background information.
The pmf
f_{k}(n)

f_{k}(n)=qf_{k}(n-1)+pqf_{k}(n-2)+p^{2}qf_{k}(n-3)+\dots+p^{k-1}qf_{k}(n-k)\,.

(2)

The initial conditions are
f_{k}(n)=0
for
n\in[1,k-1]
and
f_{k}(k)=p^{k}
.
The auxiliary polynomial is

\mathscr{A}(z)=z^{k}-qz^{k-1}-qpz^{k-2}-\dots-qp^{k-1}\,.

(see eq. (

f_{k}(n)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{p^{k}}{k+1}\,\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}^{n-k}\,\frac{\lambda_{j}-p}{\lambda_{j}-k/(k+1)}&\qquad\displaystyle p\neq\frac{k}{k+1}\,,\\
\displaystyle\frac{p^{k}}{k+1}\,\bigl(\,2\lambda_{0}^{n-k}+\lambda_{1}^{n-k}+\dots+\lambda_{k-1}^{n-k}\bigr)&\qquad\displaystyle p=\frac{k}{k+1}\,.\end{cases}

Proposition 1
.

The
r^{th}
factorial moment
\mu_{(r)}
of the geometric distribution of order
k

with success parameter
p\in(0,1)
(and
q=1-p
) is given as follows, for
r\geq 1
.

\mu_{(r)}=r!\frac{f_{k}((r+1)k+r)}{(qp^{k})^{r+1}}\,.

(5)

Proof.

We treat the case
p\neq k/(k+1)
below.
The proof is essentially a matter of summing a geometric series and using the property that for any root,
\lambda_{j}^{k}(1-\lambda_{j})=p^{k}q
(see eq. (
3
) or
[
11
]
).
From the definition of the factorial moment of a discrete-valued random variable taking values
n=0,1,\dots
,

\begin{split}\mu_{(r)}&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}n(n-1)\dots(n-r+1)f_{k}(n)\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}n(n-1)\dots(n-r+1)\biggl(\frac{p^{k}}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}^{n-k}\,\frac{\lambda_{j}-p}{\lambda_{j}-k/(k+1)}\biggr)\\
&=\frac{p^{k}}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}^{r-k}\,\frac{\lambda_{j}-p}{\lambda_{j}-k/(k+1)}\biggl(\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}n(n-1)\dots(n-r+1)\lambda_{j}^{n-r}\biggr)\\
&=\frac{p^{k}}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\lambda_{j}-p}{\lambda_{j}-k/(k+1)}\frac{r!\lambda_{j}^{r-k}}{(1-\lambda_{j})^{r+1}}\\
&=r!\frac{p^{k}}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\lambda_{j}-p}{\lambda_{j}-k/(k+1)}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{kr+r}}{\lambda_{j}^{(r+1)k}(1-\lambda_{j})^{r+1}}\\
&=r!\frac{p^{k}}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\lambda_{j}-p}{\lambda_{j}-k/(k+1)}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{kr+r}}{(qp^{k})^{r+1}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\textrm{(because $\lambda_{j}^{k}(1-\lambda_{j})=p^{k}q$)}\\
&=r!\frac{1}{(qp^{k})^{r+1}}\frac{p^{k}}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}^{(kr+k+r)-k}\,\frac{\lambda_{j}-p}{\lambda_{j}-k/(k+1)}\\
&=r!\frac{f_{k}((r+1)k+r)}{(qp^{k})^{r+1}}\,.\end{split}

(6)

∎

Remark 2
.

Observe that the proof employs only the formal properties of the roots.
The explicit expressions for the roots are not required.

(7)

Hence we can state the following expression for the

An expression for the pmf which does not contain vanishing binomial coefficients was derived by the author as follows

f_{k}(n)=0

f_{k}(k)=p^{k}

\begin{split}f_{k}(n)&=qp^{k}-\sum_{m=2}^{\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k+1}\rfloor}(-1)^{m}p^{mk}q^{m-1}\binom{n-mk-1}{m-2}-\sum_{m=2}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{k+1}\rfloor}(-1)^{m}p^{mk}q^{m}\binom{n-mk-1}{m-1}\,.\end{split}

(9)

\begin{split}\mu_{(r)}&=\frac{r!}{(qp^{k})^{r+1}}\biggl\{qp^{k}-\sum_{m=2}^{r+1}(-1)^{m}p^{mk}q^{m-1}\binom{(r+1-m)k+r-1}{m-2}\\
&\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\sum_{m=2}^{r}(-1)^{m}p^{mk}q^{m}\binom{(r+1-m)k+r-1}{m-1}\biggr\}\,.\end{split}

Addendum 12/30/2023

It was stated above that for
m=1
, the first binomial coefficient in eq. (
7
) is zero, but this is not completely true.
Consider
n=k
.
A run of
k
consecutive successes can be obtained in
k
Bernoulli trials if and only if every trial is a success, hence the probability is
f_{k}(k)=p^{k}
.
Setting
n=k
in eq. (
7
) yields

\begin{split}f_{k}(k)&=\sum_{m=1}^{1}(-1)^{m-1}p^{mk}q^{m-1}\biggl[\binom{k-mk-1}{m-2}+q\binom{k-mk-1}{m-1}\biggr]\\
&=p^{k}\biggl[\binom{-1}{-1}+q\binom{-1}{0}\biggr]\\
&=p^{k}\,?\end{split}

(13)

We must interpret
\binom{-1}{-1}=1
on the grounds that
\binom{i}{i}=1
for all values of
i
(including negative
i
) and

\binom{-1}{0}=0
on the grounds that
\binom{i}{j}=0
if
j>i
(even if
j=0
).
Hence care must be exercised when using eqs. (
7
) and (
8
).
The binomial coefficients in eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) do not require such subtleties of interpretation.

Stochastic wave equation with heavy-tailed noise: Uniqueness of solutions and past light-cone property
Juan J. Jiménez

Abstract
In this article, we study the stochastic wave equation in spatial dimensions d ≤ 2 d\leq 2 with multiplicative Lévy noise that can have infinite p p -th moments. Using the past light-cone property of the wave equation, we prove the existence and uniqueness of a solution, considering only the p p -integrability of the Lévy measure ν \nu for the region corresponding to the small jumps of the noise. For d = 1 d=1 , there are no restrictions on ν \nu . For d = 2 d=2 , we assume that there exists a value p ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) p\in(0,2) for which ∫ { | z | ≤ 1 } | z | p ν ( d z ) < + ∞ \int_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{p}\nu(dz)<+\infty .

Let

A core principle used throughout this article is the fact that the fundamental solution
G_{t}
of the wave operator satisfies the following property: for any given point
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the function
(s,y)\mapsto G_{t-s}(x-y)
has support in the conic region

\mathcal{C}_{t,x}:=\{(s,y)\in[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,;\,|x-y|\leq t-s\}.

(11)

The region
\mathcal{C}_{t,x}
is called
the past light-cone
or
the domain of dependence
. In physics, the past light-cone illustrates causality, ensuring that the effects at a point are only due to sources within this cone. This ensures that solutions to the wave equation adhere to the principle of causality, i.e., the information or energy can only travel within the constraint set by the speed of wave propagation (see Theorem 14.1 of
[
22
]
).

The PLCP has also been used in
[
15
]
for the study of the stochastic wave equation in dimension
d=3
, driven by a colored Gaussian noise. Unlike the Gaussian noise, which typically influences the entire random field uniformly, a Lévy noise can introduce abrupt changes or jumps. This makes the analysis of dependencies and influences within the past light-cone crucial for understanding how waves propagate in a heavy-tailed random field.

Remark 2.2

For equation (

\int_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{p}\nu(dz)\leq\int_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{2}\nu(dz)<+\infty,\quad\text{for all }p\geq 2.

Thus, for
d=1
, we can apply Lemma
2.1
with
\mathcal{G}_{t}=G_{t}
, and choose any value
p\geq 2\vee q
. Conversely, for equation (
1
) in dimension
d=2
, we must impose condition (
19
), since
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t}^{p}(x)\,dx\,dt<+\infty
only holds for
p\in(0,2).

To proceed with the proofs of the main results, we need the following lemma; its proof can be found in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\beta_{1}>-1,\ldots,\beta_{n}>-1
,

\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{\beta_{j}}dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(\beta_{j}+1)}{\Gamma(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}+n+1)}t^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}+n},

where
T_{n}(t):=\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in(0,t)^{n}\,;\,t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}\}
and
t=t_{n+1}
.

We follow a strategy similar to
[
11
]
to establish the existence of a mild solution to the stochastic heat equation (
12
). More precisely, we first show that the stochastic wave equation driven by
\Lambda_{N}
,

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\sigma(u(t,x))\dot{\Lambda}_{N}(t,x),\quad&t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad\quad\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\end{cases}

(21)

has a unique mild solution
u^{(N)}
in
B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,
u^{(N)}
is the only (up to modifications) random field in
B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
satisfying

u^{(N)}(t,x)=w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(u^{(N)}(s,y))\Lambda_{N}(ds,dy).

(22)

We define the operator
\mathcal{T}_{N}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}
by

\mathcal{T}_{N}(\phi)(t,x):=w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(\phi(s,y))\Lambda_{N}(ds,dy),

(23)

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{P}
. By Lemma 6.6 of
[
12
]
, the random field
\mathcal{T}_{N}(\phi)
admits a predictable modification. We will always work with this modification.

Utilizing the compact support property of
G_{t}
, we establish the self-mapping property of
\mathcal{T}_{N}
in
B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
. Subsequently, the existence of a unique mild solution for (
21
) is a consequence of the Banach fixed-point theorem applied to the operator
\mathcal{T}_{N}
. It is relevant to highlight that this particular approach cannot be extended to address the uniqueness of a solution to equation (
12
). This limitation arises from the fact that the operator
\mathcal{J}_{N}
, as delineated in (
16
), is well-defined within the spaces
B^{p}
to
B_{\text{loc}}^{p}
, but lacks self-mapping attributes in
B_{\text{loc}}^{p}
, as mentioned in the previous section.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied. Then, for any fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, equation (
21
) has a unique (up to modifications) mild solution
u^{(N)}
that satisfies

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|x|\leq R}\mathbb{E}\left[|u^{(N)}(t,x)|^{p}\right]<+\infty,

for all
T>0
and
R>0
, where
p
is any arbitrary value such that
p\geq q\vee 2
if
d=1
, and
p
is the exponent from Assumption
2
if
d=2
.

Proof.

Step 1 (
\mathcal{T}_{N}
is a self-map in
B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
).
Note that for any
0\leq s<t\leq T
and
|x|\leq R
, we have:

\text{supp}(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot))\subseteq\overline{B_{T}(x)}\subset\overline{B_{T+R}(0)}.

(24)

Hence,

h(y)^{p-q}<C_{T}(1+|R|^{\gamma})\quad\text{for all }y\in\overline{B_{T+R}(0)},

(25)

where
\gamma=\eta(p-q)
. Then, by Lemma
2.1
, (
25
), and (
24
), for any
\phi\in B_{\text{loc}}^{p}
, we have:

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\Bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(\phi(s,y))\Lambda_{N}(ds,dy)\Bigg|^{p}\right]\\
&\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{p}(t-s,x-y)\,\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\phi(s,y)|^{p}\right]\right)h(y)^{p-q}dyds\\
&\leq C_{T}(1+|R|^{\gamma})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{p}(t-s,x-y)\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[|\phi(s,y)|^{p}\right]\right)dyds\\
&\leq C_{p,T,R}\left(1+\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\sup_{|y|\leq R+T}\mathbb{E}\left[|\phi(s,y)|^{p}\right]\right).\\
\end{split}

(26)

Therefore, by (
7
) and (
26
), we get:

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|x|\leq R}\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{T}_{N}(\phi)(t,x)|^{p}\right]<+\infty,\qquad\text{for all }T,R\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(27)

Step 2 (Convergence of the Picard iterations).
In this step, we consider the Picard iteration
u_{n}^{(N)}=\{u_{n}^{(N)}(t,x)\,;\,t\geq 0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
given by:
u_{0}^{(N)}(t,x):=\Psi_{0}(t,x)
, where
\Psi_{0}
is an arbitrary element of
B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
, and
u_{n}^{(N)}:=\mathcal{T}_{N}(u^{(N)}_{n-1})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,

u_{n}^{(N)}(t,x)=w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(u_{n-1}^{(N)}(s,y))\Lambda_{N}(ds,dy),\,\,\text{for }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(28)

By (
27
), it follows that
u_{n}^{(N)}\in B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by induction over
n
. Next, we will show that
\{u_{n}^{(N)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
. By Lemma
2.1
-(ii), we have:

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n}^{(N)}(t,x)-u_{n-1}^{(N)}(t,x)|^{p}\right]\\
&\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{p}(t-s,x-y)\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n-1}^{(N)}(s,y)-u_{n-2}^{(N)}(s,y)|^{p}\right]h(y)^{p-q}dyds.\end{split}

(29)

Iterating (
29
), we get:

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n}^{(N)}(t,x)-u_{n-1}^{(N)}(t,x)|^{p}\right]\\
\leq&C_{T}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{p}(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\\
&\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}h(x_{i})^{p-q}\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{1}^{(N)}(t_{1},x_{1})-\Psi_{0}(t_{1},x_{1})|^{p}\right]d\textbf{x}d\textbf{t},\\
\end{split}

(30)

where
\textbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
,
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and we set
t_{n+1}=t
and
x_{n+1}=x
. For a fixed
t
, note that the function
\mathbb{G}_{\textbf{t}}:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
given by

\mathbb{G}_{\textbf{t}}(\textbf{x}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{p}(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\mathds{1}_{T_{n}(t)}(\textbf{t}),

has support in the set

\{\textbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,;\,|x_{i+1}-x_{i}|\leq t_{i+1}-t_{i},\,\,\text{for }i=1,\ldots,n\}.

Hence, if
t\in[0,T]
and
|x|\leq R
, the integral in (
30
) can be restricted to the values
x
in the bounded set

\left\{\textbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,;\,|x_{i}|\leq R+T\,\,\text{for }i=1,\ldots,n\right\},

(31)

since

|x-x_{i}|\leq|x-x_{n}|+\sum_{k=i}^{n-1}|x_{k+1}-x_{k}|\leq(t-t_{n})+\sum_{k=i}^{n-1}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})=t-t_{i}\leq t<T.

(32)

Then, by (
31
), it follows that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n}^{(N)}(t,x)-u_{n-1}^{(N)}(t,x)|^{p}\right]\leq C_{T}^{n}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\sup_{|y|\leq R+T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{1}^{(N)}(t,x)|^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Psi_{0}(t,x)|^{p}\Big]\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{p}(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}h(x_{i})^{p-q}d\textbf{x}d\textbf{t}.\end{split}

(33)

On the restricted set given by (
31
), we have:

\prod_{i=1}^{n}h(x_{i})^{p-q}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big[1+(R+T)^{\eta}\Big]^{p-q}\leq C_{T}^{n}(1+|R|^{n\gamma}).

(34)

Hence, by (
33
) and (
34
), we obtain:

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n}^{(N)}(t,x)-u_{n-1}^{(N)}(t,x)|^{p}\right]&\leq C_{T,R}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{p}(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})d\textbf{x}d\textbf{t}\\
&:=C^{n}_{T,R}A_{n}^{(p)}(t).\\
\end{split}

(35)

Note that
A_{n}^{(p)}(t)
does not depend on
x
. If
p<1
, by (
20
) and Lemma
2.3
, we get:

A_{n}^{(p)}(t)=C_{p}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})^{\alpha}d\textbf{t}=C_{p}^{n}\frac{t^{(\alpha+1)n}}{\Gamma((\alpha+1)n+1)},

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on
p
, and

\alpha=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if $d=1,$}\\
2-p\quad&\text{if $d=2.$}\end{cases}

Hence,

\text{if $p<1$,}\quad\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}A_{n}^{(p)}(t)\leq\begin{cases}C_{p}^{n}\frac{T^{2n}}{(n!)^{2}}\quad&\text{if $d=1,$}\\
C_{p}^{n}\frac{T^{(3-p)n}}{(n!)^{3-p}}\quad&\text{if $d=2.$}\end{cases}

(36)

Assume that
p\geq 1
. If
d=1
, it holds that
G_{t}^{p}(x)=2^{1-p}G_{t}(x)
, so we can proceed in the same way as for
p<1
, which implies
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}A_{n}^{(p)}(t)\leq 2^{n(1-p)}C_{p}^{n}\frac{T^{2n}}{(n!)^{2}}
. If
d=2
, by (
20
), we have:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g_{p}(t,x)dx=c_{p}t^{2-p}+t\leq(c_{p}+T^{p-1})t^{2-p},\qquad\text{with}\quad c_{p}=\frac{(2\pi)^{1-p}}{2-p}.

Hence,
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}A_{n}^{(p)}(t)\leq(c_{p}+T^{p-1})^{n}C_{p}^{n}\frac{T^{(3-p)n}}{(n!)^{3-p}}
. Thus,

\text{if $p\geq 1$,}\quad\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}A_{n}^{(p)}(t)\leq\begin{cases}2^{n(1-p)}C_{p}^{n}\frac{T^{2n}}{(n!)^{2}}\quad&\text{if $d=1,$}\\
(c_{p}+T^{p-1})^{n}C_{p}^{n}\frac{T^{(3-p)n}}{(n!)^{3-p}}\quad&\text{if $d=2.$}\end{cases}

(37)

Therefore, for both cases
p<1
and
p\geq 1
, it holds

\sum_{n\geq 1}C_{T,R,p}^{n}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}A_{n}^{(p)}(t)<+\infty.

(38)

By (
35
) and (
38
),
\{u_{n}^{(N)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
. Hence, there exists an element
u^{(N)}\in B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
such that
u_{n}^{(N)}\xrightarrow{B^{p}_{\text{loc}}}u^{(N)}
as
n\to+\infty
.

Step 3 (Existence of the solution).
In this step, we verify that
u^{(N)}
satisfies (
22
). First, we apply Lemma
2.1
-(ii) with
\mathcal{G}_{t}=G_{t}
. Then,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)(\sigma(u_{n}^{(N)}(s,y))-\sigma(u^{(N)}(s,y)))\Lambda_{N}(ds,dy)\Big|^{p}\right]\\
&\leq\left\|\mathcal{I}^{(t,x)}(\sigma(u_{n}^{(N)})-\sigma(u^{(N)}))\right\|_{\Lambda_{N},p}^{p\vee 1}\\
&\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{p}(t-s,x-y)\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n}^{(N)}(s,y)-u^{(N)}(s,y)|^{p}\right]h(y)^{p-q}dyds\\
&\leq C_{T,R,p}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\sup_{|y|\leq T+R}\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n}^{(N)}(s,y)-u^{(N)}(s,y)|^{p}\right]\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{p}(t-s,x-y)dyds,\end{split}

(39)

we used (
24
) and (
25
) in the previous inequality. Now, if we let
n
approach infinity in (
39
), we find that for a fixed pair
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the expression
\mathcal{I}^{(t,x)}(\sigma(u_{n}^{(N)}))
converges to
\mathcal{I}^{(t,x)}(\sigma(u^{(N)}))
with the semi-norm
||\cdot||_{\Lambda_{N},p}^{p\vee 1}
. This convergence implies that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{T}_{N}(u_{n}^{(N)})(t,x)=\mathcal{T}_{N}(u^{(N)})(t,x)\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega).

Moreover, we have
u_{n}^{(N)}=\mathcal{T}_{N}(u_{n-1}^{(N)})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and the sequence
\{u_{n}^{(N)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
u^{(N)}
in the space
B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
as
n\to+\infty
. Therefore, we conclude that
u^{(N)}
satisfies (
22
).

Step 4 (Uniqueness of the solution).
Assume that there exists another process
v^{(N)}\in B^{p}_{\text{loc}}
that satisfies (
22
), i.e.,
\mathcal{T}_{N}(v^{(N)})=v^{(N)}
. Then, by Lemma
2.1
-(ii), we have:

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n}^{(N)}(t,x)-v^{(N)}(t,x)|^{p}\right]\\
&\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{p}(t-s,x-y)\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n-1}^{(N)}(s,y)-v^{(N)}(s,y)|^{p}\right]h(y)^{p-q}dsdy.\\
\end{split}

Iterating the inequality above as in (
30
), and following the same steps as in (
35
), we get:

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{n}^{(N)}(t,x)-v^{(N)}(t,x)|^{p}\right]\\
&\leq C_{p,T,R}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{p}(t_{i+1}-t_{i},x_{i+1}-x_{i})\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{1}^{(N)}(t_{1},x_{1})-v^{(N)}(t_{1},x_{1})|^{p}\right]d\textbf{x}d\textbf{t}\\
&\leq\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\sup_{|y|\leq R+T}\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{1}^{(N)}(s,y)-v^{(N)}(s,y)|^{p}\right]\,C_{p,T,R}^{n}A_{n}^{p}(t)\to 0,\end{split}

as
n\to+\infty
. Therefore,
u_{n}^{(N)}\to v^{(N)}
in
B_{\text{loc}}^{p}
as
n\to+\infty
. Alternatively,
u_{n}^{(N)}\to u^{(N)}
in
B_{\text{loc}}^{p}
as
n\to+\infty
, which implies
u^{(N)}=v^{(N)}
in
B_{\text{loc}}^{p}
.
∎

We can extend Theorem

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+f(t,x,u(t,x))+\sigma(t,x,u(t,x))\dot{\Lambda}(t,x),&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\

Assumption 3

3
Past light-cone property

In this section, we fix
T>0
and consider the stochastic wave equation in the interval
[0,T]
,

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\sigma(u(t,x))\dot{\Lambda}(t,x),\quad&t\in(0,T],\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,d\leq 2,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(53)

where
\sigma
is a globally Lipschitz function,
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
satisfy Assumption
1
. By exploiting the PLCP of the wave equation, we will show the existence and uniqueness of a solution to (
53
) without imposing the assumption of
q
-integrability over the large jumps:

\int_{\{|z|>1\}}|z|^{q}\nu(dz)<+\infty.

(54)

The PLCP has been extensively studied for hyperbolic PDEs in the literature. For instance, in
[
15
]
, it was proved that the solution of the stochastic wave equation, in dimension
d=3
, driven by a colored Gaussian noise remains invariant in a region of space, if the problem is restricted to that region. As in Section 6 of
[
15
]
, for a fixed region
D\in\mathfrak{B}
, we define the conic region

\mathcal{K}^{D}(s):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;;\;d(y,D)\leq T-s\},\quad\text{for any }s\in[0,T].

Clearly,
\mathcal{K}^{D}(0)=\bigcup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{K}^{D}(t)
.

As we do not impose condition (
54
), the stopping time
\tau_{N}
given by (
15
) may not be well-defined. Therefore, we consider another stopping time given by

\tau_{N}(D):=\inf\left\{t\in[0,T]\,;\,J([0,t]\times\mathcal{K}^{D}(0)\times\{|z|>N\})>0\right\},\quad N\in\mathbb{N},\;D\in\mathfrak{B}.

(55)

Note that
\tau_{N}(D)>0
a.s., and
\tau_{N}(D)<\tau_{N+1}(D)
a.s. for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
\tau_{N}(D)=+\infty
, for large
N
.

Additionally, for a fixed
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{D}
, note that
\text{supp}(H^{(t,x)})\subset[0,t]\times\mathcal{K}^{D}(0)
, where

H^{(t,x)}(s,y)=G_{t-s}(x-y)\mathds{1}_{\{t\geq s\}},

This implies that for a fixed
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{D}
, the value
u(t,x)
of the solution of (
4
) only depends on the values of
\Lambda
on
[0,T]\times\mathcal{K}^{D}(0)
. Thus, for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{D}
, if
\phi
is an integrable random field with respect to
\Lambda
(see (
83
)),

\begin{split}&\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau_{N}(D)]\!]}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(\phi(s,y))\Lambda(ds,dy)\\
&=\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau_{N}(D)]\!]}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(\phi(s,y))\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau_{N}(D)]\!]}(s)\overline{\Lambda}_{N}(ds,dy),\\
\end{split}

(56)

due to Lemma
A.5
, and the fact that

\Lambda([0,t]\times A)=\overline{\Lambda}_{N}([0,t]\times A)\quad\text{on}\quad\{t\leq\tau_{N}(D)\},

for all
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
A\subset\mathcal{K}^{D}(0).

The stopping times defined in (
55
) are analogous to those used in
[
1
,
13
]
for solving SPDEs driven by Lévy noise in bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For example, in
[
13
]
, it was proved that for any fixed
T>0
and
D\in\mathfrak{B}
, the stochastic heat equation on
D
,

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+\sigma(u(t,x))\dot{\Lambda}(t,x),\quad&t\in(0,T],\,x\in D,\\
u(t,x)=0,\quad&t\in[0,T],\,x\in\partial D,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad&x\in D,\end{cases}

(57)

has a unique solution
u=\{u(t,x)\;;\;t\in[0,T],\,x\in D\}
that satisfies

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in D}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u(t,x)\right|^{p}\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau^{\star}_{N}(D)]\!]}}(t)\right]<+\infty,

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Here,
\tau^{\star}_{N}(D)
is the stopping time defined by

\tau^{\star}_{N}(D):=\inf\left\{t\in[0,T]:\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{\{|z|>N\}}J(ds,dx,dz)>0\right\}.

However, the main issue associated with the use of
\tau^{\star}_{N}(D)
for SPDEs on the entire space (such as (
1
) and (
12
)) is that if
D=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\tau^{\star}_{N}(D)=0
almost surely for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. This happens because the region
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{|z|>N\}
may contain infinitely many points of
J
. By contrast, using the PLCP, we can construct a ”local solution”
u^{(D)}
of (
53
) on
[0,T]\times\overline{D}
using the stopping times given by (
55
). As these local solutions are consistent and agree almost surely on the same region in space, we can construct a mild solution to (
53
).

The resemblance between
\tau_{N}(D)
and
\tau^{\star}_{N}(D)
is not a coincidence. The primary result of this section shows that the solution
u
to (
53
) given by Theorem
3.1
satisfies
u^{(D)}(t,x)=u(t,x)
a.s. for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{D}
, where
u^{(D)}
is a predictable process satisfying the stochastic-integral equation:

u^{(D)}(t,x)=w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(u^{(D)}(s,y))\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{K}^{D}(s)}(y)\Lambda(ds,dy).

(58)

Note that the integrand of the stochastic integral on the right-hand side of equation (
58
) has support on
\mathcal{K}^{D}(0)
. Hence, we can solve (
58
) using
\tau_{N}(D)
similarly to solving SPDEs driven by Lévy noise in bounded domains.

Furthermore, since the method that we use to construct a solution to (
58
) essentially requires the same integrability condition on
\nu
as for solving SPDEs in bounded domains, it suffices to consider only the assumption on the small jumps
\int_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{p}\nu(dz)<+\infty
for some
p>0
. More precisely, we only need this assumption on the small jumps for the wave equation (
53
) in dimension
d=2
, while for dimension

).

Theorem 3.1
.

(a) If

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\overline{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u(t,x)\right|^{p}\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(D)]\!]}}(t)\right]<+\infty,\quad\text{for all }N\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }D\in\mathfrak{B}.

\mathbf{s}:=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n}),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{y}:=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}).

Additionally, note that

\int_{\tilde{T}_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{p}(s_{i-1}-s_{i},y_{i-1}-y_{i})\,d\mathbf{y}\,d\mathbf{s}=A_{n}^{(p)}(t),

By the triangle inequality, note that

B_{s_{i-1}-s_{i}}(y_{i-1})\subset\mathcal{K}^{A}(s_{i})\subset\mathcal{K}^{B}(s_{i}),\quad\text{for all $i=1,\ldots,n$}.

(70)

\begin{split}u^{(A,N)}_{n-k+1}(s_{k-1},y_{k-1})&=w(s_{k-1},y_{k-1})\\
&+\int_{0}^{s_{k-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{s_{k-1}-s_{k}}(y_{k-1}-y_{k})\sigma(u^{(A,N)}_{n-k}(s_{k},y_{k}))\overline{\Lambda}_{N}(ds_{k},dy_{k})\\
\end{split}

(71)

\begin{split}u^{(B,N)}_{n-k+1}(s_{k-1},y_{k-1})&=w(s_{k-1},y_{k-1})\\
&+\int_{0}^{s_{k-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{s_{k-1}-s_{k}}(y_{k-1}-y_{k})\sigma(u^{(B,N)}_{n-k}(s_{k},y_{k}))\overline{\Lambda}_{N}(ds_{k},dy_{k}).\\

for

Let

Y_{n,k}^{N}(s_{k},y_{k}):=(u^{(A,N)}_{n-k}(s_{k},y_{k})-u^{(B,N)}_{n-k}(s_{k},y_{k}))\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau_{N}(B)]\!]}(s_{k}),

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{n,k-1}^{N}(s_{k-1},y_{k-1})|^{p}\right]\\

Now, by (

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{n,0}^{N}(t,x)|^{p}\right]\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{p}(t-s_{1},x-y_{1})\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{n,1}^{N}(s_{1},y_{1})|^{p}\right]\,dy_{1}\,ds_{1},\end{split}

(75)

Therefore, by (

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|u^{(A,N)}_{n}(t,x)-u^{(B,N)}_{n}(t,x)\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau_{N}(B)]\!]}(t)|^{p}\right]\\

where

.

Step 3 (Global solution).

Let
u=\{u(t,x)\,;\,t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
be the random field defined by

u(t,x):=u^{(D_{k})}(t,x),\quad\text{if}\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times[-k,k]^{d},

\begin{split}&w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(u(s,y))\Lambda(ds,dy)\\

Step 4 (Uniqueness of the solution).

We assume that

is a mild solution of (
53
) that satisfies

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\overline{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[|v(t,x)|^{p}\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(D)]\!]}}(t)\right]<+\infty,

First, we define

V_{T,D}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d};|y|<T+R_{D}\},\quad\text{where $R_{D}=\sup_{x\in\overline{D}}|x|$}.

Note that for all

Also, recall that

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\overline{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[|(u(t,x)-v(t,x))\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(V_{T,D})]\!]}}(t)|^{p}\right]\\

&+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\overline{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[|(u_{n}^{(V_{T,D},N)}(t,x)-v(t,x))\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(V_{T,D})]\!]}}(t)|^{p}\right]\Bigg),\\
\end{split}

where

is the sequence in (

. The first term on the right hand side of the inequality above converges to 0 by (

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\overline{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[|(u_{n}^{(V_{T,D},N)}(t,x)-v(t,x))\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(V_{T,D})]\!]}}(t)|^{p}\right]\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to+\infty$,}

for a fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
(t,x,s_{1},y_{1},\ldots,s_{n},y_{n})\in\mathcal{A}^{(t,x)}
, using the same argument as in (

(78)

Then, by (

\begin{split}&\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(V_{T,D})]\!]}}(s_{k-1})\int_{0}^{s_{k-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{s_{k-1}-s_{k}}(y_{k-1}-y_{k})\sigma(v(s_{k},y_{k}))\Lambda(ds_{k},dy_{k})\\
=&\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(V_{T,D})]\!]}}(s_{k-1})\\
&\times\int_{0}^{s_{k-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{s_{k-1}-s_{k}}(y_{k-1}-y_{k})\sigma(v(s_{k},y_{k}))\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(V_{T,D})]\!]}}(s_{k})\overline{\Lambda}_{N}(ds_{k},dy_{k}),\\

(79)

for

Now, let us define,

W^{N}_{n,k}(s_{k},y_{k}):=(u^{(V_{T,D},N)}_{n-k}(s_{k},y_{k})-v(s_{k},y_{k}))\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau_{N}(V_{T,D})]\!]}(s_{k}),

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[|W_{n,k-1}^{N}(s_{k-1},y_{k-1})|^{p}\right]\\

for

\mathbb{E}\left[|W_{n,0}^{N}(t,x)|^{p}\right]\leq C_{T}^{n}\int_{\tilde{T}_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{p}(s_{i-1}-s_{i},y_{i-1}-y_{i})\mathbb{E}\left[|W_{n,n}^{N}(s_{n},y_{n})|^{p}\right]d\textbf{y}d\textbf{s}.

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\overline{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[|(u_{n}^{(V_{T,D},N)}(t,x)-v(t,x))\mathds{1}_{{[\![0,\tau_{N}(V_{T,D})]\!]}}(t)|^{p}\right]\\

Remark 3.2

Let

Appendix A
Stochastic integration

For the reader’s convenience, this section presents some basic elements of the theory of stochastic integration of
L^{p}
-random measures (see
[
8
,
9
]
), which are required to perform stochastic calculus with respect to heavy-tailed noise. There are different ways to define random measures. In this work, we follow the definition of
L^{p}
-random measures presented in
[
10
]
.

Definition A.1

(Definition 2.1 in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets in
\mathcal{P}
satisfying
\Omega_{k}\uparrow\tilde{\Omega}
. A map

M:\mathcal{P}_{M}=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{P}\Big|_{\Omega_{k}}\to L^{p}(\Omega)

is called an
L^{p}
-random measure
in
\tilde{\Omega}
if it satisfies:

i)

M(\emptyset)=0
a.s.,

ii)

For every sequence
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of pairwise disjoint sets in
\mathcal{P}_{M}
with
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
, we have

M\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}M(A_{i})\qquad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega).

iii)

For all
A\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
with
A\subset\Omega\times(t,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the random variable
M(A)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.

iv)

For all
A\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
F\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
, we have

M(A\cap(F\times(t,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))=\mathds{1}_{F}M(A\cap(\Omega\times(t,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))\quad a.s.

In
[
20
]
, a
Lévy basis
is defined as an infinitely divisible independently scattered random measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In
[
10
,
11
]
, the definition of
Lévy basis
has been modified to incorporate the theory of stochastic integration developed in
[
8
]
. In this work, we use the same definition of
Lévy basis
as in
[
10
,
11
]
. We recall this definition below.

Definition A.2
.

An
L^{0}
-random measure
\tilde{\Lambda}:\tilde{\mathcal{P}_{b}}\to L^{0}
is called a
Lévy basis
if it satisfies:

i)

Let
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pairwise disjoint sets in
\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
\{\tilde{\Lambda}(\Omega\times B_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of independent random variables. Additionally, if
B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies
B\subset(t,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, then
\tilde{\Lambda}(\Omega\times B)
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
.

ii)

For all
B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\tilde{\Lambda}(\Omega\times B)
has an infinitely divisible distribution.

iii)

For all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\tilde{\Lambda}(\Omega\times\{t\}\times[-k,k]^{d})=0
a.s.

Now, we extend the notion of pure-jump Lévy white noise
\Lambda=\{\Lambda(B);B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
defined for non-random Borel sets in (
2
) to a Lévy basis
\tilde{\Lambda}
in order to perform stochastic integration with respect to
\tilde{\Lambda}
. The details of this extension can be found in
[
10
,
17
,
18
]
.

Remark A.3
.

The noise
\Lambda
in (
2
) has an extension
\tilde{\Lambda}:\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{b}\to L^{0}
, that satisfies

\tilde{\Lambda}(\Omega\times B)=\Lambda(B)\qquad\text{for all $B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

(82)

This random measure
\tilde{\Lambda}
satisfies Definition
A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of simple predictable processes of the form
S=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}
for some
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
A_{i}\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
. Then, the stochastic integral of
S
with respect to
M
is given by:

I^{\Lambda}(S)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(t,x)M(dt,dx)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}M(A_{i}).

For any
p\geq 0
, the
Daniell mean
of a process
H=\{H(t,x);t\geq 0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
with respect to an
L^{p}
-random measure
M
is defined by

||H||_{M,p}=\sup_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda};|S|\leq|H|}||I^{M}(S)||_{p}.

A predictable process
H
is said to be
p
-integrable
with respect to
M
if there exists a sequence
\{S_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{S}
such that
||S_{n}-H||_{M,p}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
. In this case, the
stochastic integral
of
H
with respect to
M
is given by:

I^{M}(S)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}I^{M}(S_{n})\quad\text{in}\quad L^{p}(\Omega).

(83)

We define the space

L^{1,p}(M)=\Big\{\text{the set of }p\text{-integrable processes with respect to }M\Big\}.

In this definition, we omit writing
p
if
p=0
. Note that the map
I^{M}:L^{1,p}(M)\rightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
is a contraction. We include the following version of the dominated convergence theorem for stochastic integrals with respect to
M
.

Theorem A.4

(Theorem A.1 of
[
13
]
)
.

If
\{H_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of predictable processes that converges pointwise to
H
, and
|H_{n}|\leq H_{0}
for all
n\geq 1
and some
H_{0}\in L^{1,p}(M)
, then
H_{n},H\in L^{1,p}(M)
and
\|H-H_{n}\|_{M,p}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.

The following local property of the stochastic integral plays an important role in this article. While different versions of this property can be found in
[
8
,
9
,
18
]
, we include its proof here because we could not find a direct reference that addresses it with respect to a Lévy basis as in Definition
A.2
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\tilde{\Lambda}
be a Lévy basis and
H(t,x)
be a predictable process such that there exists an increasing sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of stopping times with
T_{N}\uparrow+\infty
a.s. for
N\to+\infty
, and
H(t,x)\mathds{1}_{[\![0,T_{N}]\!]}(t)
is
p
-integrable with respect to
\tilde{\Lambda}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for any stopping time
\tau
we have:

\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau]\!]}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H(s,y)\tilde{\Lambda}(ds,dy)=\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau]\!]}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H(s,y)\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau]\!]}(s)\tilde{\Lambda}(ds,dy).

(84)

Proof.

Step 1:
first, we prove (
84
) for an elementary stopping time
\tau
and
H(s,y)=\mathds{1}_{A}(s,y)
, with
A\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{b}
, i.e.,

\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau]\!]}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathds{1}_{A}(s,y)\tilde{\Lambda}(ds,dy)=\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau]\!]}(t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathds{1}_{A}(s,y)\mathds{1}_{[\![0,\tau]\!]}(s)\tilde{\Lambda}(ds,dy).

(85)

Let

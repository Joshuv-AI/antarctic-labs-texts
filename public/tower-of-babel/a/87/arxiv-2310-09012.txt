Weil pairing on twisted curves
Ashwin Deopurkar

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth, proper, connected curve of genus
g\geq 2
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two. We have the Weil pairing on
C
which is a perfect, alternating pairing on
\text{Pic}(C)[2]
. It is natural to consider this pairing as
C
varies in the moduli space
M_{g}
of smooth, proper curves. The natural object to consider is the moduli space

M_{g}^{\mathcal{O},2}=\left.\{(C,L,f)|f:L^{\otimes 2}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}\}\middle/\cong\right.

that parameterizes smooth, proper curves of genus
g
together with a
2\mbox{-torsion}
line bundle. The space
M_{g}^{\mathcal{O},2}
is a Deligne-Mumford stack that forms a finite étale cover of
M_{g}
of degree
2^{2g}
(See
[
1
]
). In this setting, we can realize the Weil pairing as a pairing on the fibers of
M_{g}^{\mathcal{O},2}
over
M_{g}
. A natural question to ask is whether we can extend the pairing on a compactification of
M_{g}
. We may consider the category
\overline{M_{g}}^{\mathcal{O},2}
that parameterizes stable curves together with a two torsion line bundle. It is an étale Deligne-Mumford stack over the space
\overline{M_{g}}
of stable curves; however, the morphism
{\overline{M_{g}}^{\mathcal{O},2}\to\overline{M_{g}}}
is not proper (See Example 1.1,
[
1
]
). Thus, we fail to obtain a finite étale cover of
\overline{M_{g}}
. This defect is rectified by Chiodo using the category of Abramovich and Vistoli’s twisted curves (See
[
1
]
). We have a compact moduli of twisted curves over which the two torsion line bundles form a finite, étale group sheaf.

In the present work, we extend the Weil pairing to the moduli of twisted curves. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a twisted curve with the coarse space
C
. Let
H
denote the subgroup of
\text{Pic}(C)[2]
of those line bundles which are trivial on the normalization of
C
. We prove that
H
is naturally Weil dual to the quotient
\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]/\text{Pic}(C)[2]
. Both
H
and
\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]/\text{Pic}(C)[2]
have a combinatorial description in terms of the dual graph of
\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, the Weil pairing between the two can be seen as a combinatorial integration-homology pairing on the dual graph (Theorem
5.7
).

In the last section, we look at the interplay of tropical and algebraic geometry that emerges when we have an arithmetic surface. We give an algebro-geometric argument to prove that the kernel of the tropical specialization is isotropic for the Weil pairing when the dual graph is totally degenerate (Proposition
7.9
). This was proved earlier using tropical methods (See
[
2
]
). Finally, we look at arithmetic surfaces where the special fiber is a twisted curve. We obtain a combinatorial consequence that the group of
r\mbox{-torsion}
points of the Picard group of a metric graph with unit edge lengths can be realized as divisors supported on a set which is the union of the original vertices of the graph and interior vertices that subdivide each non-separating edge into
r
equal parts (Proposition
7.10
).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field. For a finite group
G
, we denote by
\text{B}G
the quotient stack
\left[\text{Spec}(k)\middle/G\right]
. By
\mathbf{G}_{m,{X}}
we denote the sheaf of invertible functions on
X
on the étale site of
X
. We omit the subscript and simply write
\mathbf{G}_{m}
when the object is clear from the context. Similarly, by
\mu_{r}
we mean the sheaf of
r^{th}
roots of unity. We assume all stacks to be separated.

Let
M,N
be modules over a ring
R
. A pairing on
M\times N
is a
R\mbox{-bilinear}
map
{M\times N\xrightarrow{e(-,-)}R}
. By the kernel of a pairing in the first factor we mean the kernel of the associated map
{M\to\text{Hom}_{R}(N,R)}
. We say a pairing is perfect, if this map is an isomorphism. We say a pairing is non-degenerate if the kernel of the pairing in both the factors is zero. By a pairing on
M
, we mean a pairing on
{M\times M}
. We say a pairing on
M
is alternating if we have
{e(m,m)=0}
for every
m\in M
. It is straightforward to see that for an alternating pairing, we have
{e(m_{1},m_{2})=-e(m_{2},m_{1})}
. If we have a pairing on
M
, then a submodule
M^{\prime}\subset M
is said to be isotropic, if the pairing restricted to
M^{\prime}
is identically zero.

By a graph we mean a finite, undirected graph with a finite number of vertices and edges. We allow multiple edges between two vertices as well as loop edges.

Definition 2.1
.

A smooth orbifold curve of genus
g
over
k
is a connected, proper, smooth, tame Deligne-Mumford stack of dimension
1
over
k
which has trivial generic stabilizer and whose coarse space is a smooth, proper curve of genus
g
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{f}C}
be a smooth orbifold curve over
k
with coarse space
C
. It is known that
\mathcal{X}
is obtained from its coarse space by a finite number of root constructions (See chapter 10.3,
[
3
]
for root stacks). That is, we have

\mathcal{X}\cong C[p_{1}/d_{1}]\times_{C}C[p_{2}/d_{2}]\times_{C}\cdots\times_{C}C[p_{m}/d_{m}]

where
\{p_{i}\}
are closed points of
C
,
\{d_{i}\}
are positive integers coprime with the characteristic of
k
, and
C[p_{i}/d_{i}]
is the root stack with the stabilizer
\mathbb{Z}/d_{i}\mathbb{Z}
at
p_{i}
. We denote the stabilizer
\mathbb{Z}/d_{i}\mathbb{Z}
at
p_{i}
by
G_{p_{i}}
. The étale local structure of
\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{f}C
at a point
p_{i}
is given by:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\text{Spec}(k[t])\middle/G_{p_{i}}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Spec}(k[t^{d_{i}}])}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where the generator of
G_{p_{i}}
acts on
\text{Spec}(k[t])
by
t\to\zeta_{d_{i}}t
for some primitive
d_{i}^{th}
root
\zeta_{d_{i}}
of unity. For each point
p_{i}
with a non-trivial stabilizer, the orbifold admits a line bundle
L_{i}
together with an isomorphism of line bundles
{L_{i}^{\otimes d_{i}}\to f^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(p_{i}))}
. Like schematic proper curves, we have a notion of a degree of a line bundle on
\mathcal{X}
. However, the degree is not necessarily integral but takes values in
1/d\mathbb{Z}
, where
d
is the least common multiple of the numbers
\{d_{i}\}
. For example, the degree of
L_{i}
equals
1/d_{i}
. We have a closed immersion

{p_{i}\times_{C}\mathcal{X}\cong\text{B}G_{p_{i}}\xrightarrow{j}\mathcal{X}}
. The Picard group of
\text{B}G_{p_{i}}
is isomorphic to
\text{Hom}(G_{p_{i}},k^{*})\cong\mathbb{Z}/d_{i}\mathbb{Z}
and is generated by
j^{*}(L_{i})
. Thus we have a morphism
{{\theta}^{\mathcal{X}}_{p_{i}}:\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathbb{Z}/d_{i}\mathbb{Z}}
which maps a line bundle
L
to the unique integer
{\theta}^{\mathcal{X}}_{p_{i}}(L)
modulo
d_{i}
such that
{j^{*}(L)\cong j^{*}(L_{i})^{\otimes{\theta}^{\mathcal{X}}_{p_{i}}(L)}}
. We refer to
{\theta}^{\mathcal{X}}_{p_{i}}(L)
as the character of
L
at
p_{i}
. The Picard group of
\mathcal{X}
is described by the following exact sequence (See Corollary 4.15,
[
4
]
):

0\to\text{Pic}(C)\xrightarrow{f^{*}}\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})\xrightarrow{\oplus{\theta}^{\mathcal{X}}_{p_{i}}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{d_{i}\mathbb{Z}}\to 0.

Remark 2.2
.

For a line bundle
L
on
\mathcal{X}
, we have
\deg(L)\in 1/d\mathbb{Z}
and
{\deg(L)-\Sigma_{i=1}^{m}{\theta}^{\mathcal{X}}_{p_{i}}(L)}
is an integer (See Proposition 2.8,
[
1
]
).

We have
\text{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{G}_{m})\cong\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})
and
\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{G}_{m})=0
(See Corollary 4.15,
[
4
]
).

3.
Twisted curves

Definition 3.1
.

A (balanced) twisted curve of genus
g
over
k
is a connected, proper tame Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
over
k
of dimension one which admits a coarse space
C
that is a nodal curve of arithmetic genus
g
. The morphism
\mathcal{X}\to C
is an isomorphism except at the nodes of
C
where the étale local structure of
\mathcal{X}
is given by

\left[\text{Spec}(k[x,y]/xy)\middle/\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{l\mathbb{Z}}\right]

where the generator of
\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
acts by
x\to\zeta_{l}x,y\to\zeta_{l}^{-1}y
for some primitive
l^{th}
root
\zeta_{l}
of unity.

Remark 3.2
.

The last condition that
\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
acts by inverse characters on the two branches is called balancing condition but we omit this adjective because we always consider twisted curves which are balanced.

Let
\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{f}C
be a twisted curve of genus
g
with the coarse space
C
. Let
E
denote the set of nodal points of
C
. For a point
e
in
E
, let
G_{e}
denote the stabilizer of
e
. The normalization
{\nu:\widehat{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{X}}
is
{\nu:\widehat{\mathcal{X}}=\widehat{C}\times_{C}\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}}
where
{\widehat{C}\to C}
is the normalization of
C
. The stack
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
is a disjoint union of smooth orbifold curves as in Definition
2.1
. The points of
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
with non-trivial stabilizers are precisely those which lie above the nodes of
C
. The degree of a line bundle
L
on
\mathcal{X}
is defined as the sum of the degrees of
\nu^{*}(L)
on the connected components of the normalization
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
. For
e\in E
, we have a closed immersion
{e\times_{C}\mathcal{X}\cong BG_{e}\to\mathcal{X}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

(Proposition 5.2,
[
4
]
) The Picard group of
\mathcal{X}
is described by the following short exact sequence:

\displaystyle 0\to\text{Pic}(C)\to\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})\to\bigoplus_{e\in E}\text{Pic}(BG_{e})\to 0.

Moreover, for
e\in E
, the group
\text{Pic}(BG_{e})\cong\text{Hom}(G_{e},k^{*})
is a cyclic group of order
\lvert G_{e}\rvert
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
L
be a line bundle on
\mathcal{X}
. Then the degree of
L
is an integer.

Proof.

This essentially follows from the balancing conditions at the nodes (See Proposition 2.19,
[
1
]
). For a node
e
of
C
, we have a closed immersion
BG_{e}\to\mathcal{X}
. The fiber
{BG_{e}\times_{\mathcal{X}}\widehat{\mathcal{X}}}
of the normalization over
BG_{e}
is isomorphic to
BG_{e}\coprod BG_{e}
. The morphism
{BG_{e}\coprod BG_{e}\xrightarrow{\nu}BG_{e}}
differs on the two copies of
BG_{e}
by the automorphism corresponding to the inverse automorphism of the stabilizer
G_{e}
. Consequently, the characters of
\nu^{*}(L)
at the two pre-images of
e
are inverses of each other. Hence the total degree of
L
is an integer (See Remark
2.2
).

∎

Definition 3.5
.

The dual graph associated to
\mathcal{X}
is a graph that has a vertex for every irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}
. For every node in
\mathcal{X}
, there is an (undirected) edge which joins the vertices corresponding to the branches that the node belongs to.

We denote the dual graph of
\mathcal{X}
by
\Gamma
. We denote the vertex set of the dual graph by by
V
and the edge set is naturally parameterized by
E
.

Definition 3.6
.

By
C_{0}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
(resp.
C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
), we denote the free
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
vector space generated by the vertex (resp. edge) set of
\Gamma
. The boundary map
{C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\partial}C_{0}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})}
is defined by
{\partial(e=(v_{1},v_{2}))=v_{2}-v_{1}}
We denote the kernel of
\partial
by
\text{H}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
.

.

Assume that the stabilizers of the nodes of
\mathcal{X}
have even order. Then we have the following exact sequence (See Corollary 3.1,

We have the exact sequence as stated in Lemma

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial^{\prime}}

Definition 3.8
.

By a cycle in a graph we mean a sequence
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{l}\}
of distinct edges for which there exists a sequence of distinct vertices
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{l}\}
such that for
1\leq i\leq l-1
, the edge
e_{i}
joins the vertices
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
, and the last edge
e_{l}
joins the vertices
(v_{l},v_{1})
. We say an edge
e
is non-separating, if deleting
e
does not increase the number of connected components of the graph.

Remark 3.9
.

It is straightforward to see that a chain
{e_{1}+e_{2}+\ldots+e_{l}}
in
C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
is killed by the boundary map
{\partial:C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to C_{0}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})}
if and only if we can write the set
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{l}\}
as an edge-disjoint union of cycles. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
denote a graph obtained by deleting some edges from
\Gamma
. We may conclude from the previous observation that the natural injective map
{\text{H}_{1}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to\text{H}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})}
is an isomorphism if and only if
\Gamma^{\prime}
contains all the non-separating edges of
\Gamma
. When not all stabilizers are of even order, we have the exact sequence:

0\to\text{Pic}(C)[2]\to\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]\to\text{H}_{1}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to 0

where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is the graph obtained by deleting the edges of
\Gamma
that correspond to the nodes with odd stabilizers. This can easily be seen from the proof of the above Proposition
3.7
. Thus we have
{\lvert\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]\rvert=2^{2g}}
if and only if the stabilizers of the non-separating nodes have even order (See Theorem 3.9,
[
1
]
).

Definition 3.10
.

By
C^{0}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
(resp.
C^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
), we denote the vector space of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
valued functions on the vertex (resp. edge) set of
\Gamma
. The coboundary map
{C^{0}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\delta}C^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})}
is defined by
{\delta f(e=(v_{1},v_{2}))=f(v_{2})-f(v_{1})}
. We denote the cokernel of this map by
\text{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
.

Proposition 3.11
.

We set
{H=\{L\in\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]\,|\,\nu^{*}(L)\text{ is trivial on all components of }\widehat{\mathcal{X}}.\}}
. Then we have an isomorphism
{H\cong\text{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})}
.

Proof.

We note that if
{L\in H}
then the characters of
\nu^{*}(L)
at all the stacky points of
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
are trivial. Therefore,
L
is isomorphic to

for some line bundle

such that

pulls back to a trivial line bundle on each component of the normalization
\widehat{C}
. We have the following exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\left.(\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in V}k^{*})\middle/k^{*}\right.}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{e\in E}k^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{ ker }\left(\text{Pic}(C)\to\text{Pic}(\widehat{C})\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Since

is closed under taking square roots, we have
{\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},k^{*})\cong 0}
and
{\text{Ext}^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\left.(\bigoplus_{i\in V}k^{*})\middle/k^{*}\right.\right)\cong 0}
. After applying the functor

∎

4.
Azumaya algebras and Weil pairing

Let
X
be a quasi-compact, separated Deligne-Mumford stack. A matrix algebra on
X
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}\mbox{-algebra}
which is isomorphic to the endomorphism algebra of a finite, locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}\mbox{-module}
. An Azumaya algebra on
X
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra which is étale locally isomorphic to a matrix algebra (See
[
6
]
or
[
7
]
). An Azumaya algebra is said to be trivial if it is isomorphic to a matrix algebra.

Remark 4.1
.

Suppose
M
and
N
are two finite, locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}\mbox{-modules}
on
X
such that the endomorphisms algebras
\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(M,M)
and
\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(N,N)
are isomorphic. Then there exists a line bundle
L
on
X
such that
{M\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}L\cong N}
(See
[
8
,
Tag 0A2K
]
).

The multiplication operation on Azumaya algebras is given by the tensor product over
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Two Azumaya algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are said to be Brauer equivalent, if we have an isomorphism
{\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}T_{1}\cong\mathcal{B}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}T_{2}}
for some trivial Azumaya algebras
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. The Brauer group of
X
, which we denote
\text{Br}(X)
, is the group of equivalence classes of Azumaya algebras. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an Azumaya algebra which is locally free of rank
n^{2}
. We can regard
\mathcal{A}
as a
PGL_{n}
-torsor over
X
and therefore such Azumaya algebras are classified by the group
\text{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(X,PGL_{n})
(See
[
6
]
). From the exact sequence of sheaves:

1\to\mathbf{G}_{m}\to GL_{n}\to PGL_{n}\to 1

we obtain a group homomorphism
{\text{br}_{X}:\text{Br}(X)\to\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\mathbf{G}_{m})}
which is called the Brauer class map. The Brauer class of
\mathcal{A}
in
\text{Br}(X)
is killed by
n
(See
[
8
,
Tag 0A2L
]
). The Brauer class map is injective and its image is contained in the torsion part of
\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\mathbf{G}_{m})
(See
[
6
]
).

Assume that two is invertible on
X
and let
L_{1},L_{2}\in\text{Pic}(X)[2]
be two
2\mbox{-torsion}
line bundles on
X
. Then we define the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}
as follows. We set

\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\oplus(L_{1}\otimes L_{2}).

The multiplication on
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}
is defined by fixing isomorphisms
L_{1}^{2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
L_{2}^{2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and if
e_{1},e_{2}
are local sections
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
respectively, then we define
e_{1}\times e_{2}=-e_{2}\times e_{1}
as a section of
L_{1}\otimes L_{2}
. It is easy to see that this defines a matrix algebra whenever
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are trivial. Therefore
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}
is an Azumaya algebra which corresponds to a two torsion element in the Brauer group.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
f:Y\to X
is a morphism of quasi-compact Deligne-Mumford stacks. Let
L_{1},L_{2}\in\text{Pic}(X)[2]
be
2\mbox{-torsion}
line bundles on
X
. Then we have
{f^{*}\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}\cong\mathcal{A}_{\{f^{*}L_{1},f^{*}L_{2}\}}}
. Moreover, if
{\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}\cong\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(M,M)}
for some locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}\mbox{-module}

M
then we have
{\mathcal{A}_{\{f^{*}L_{1},f^{*}L_{2}\}}\cong\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(f^{*}M,f^{*}M)}
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a twisted curve over
k
on which
2
is invertible. We use Azumaya algebras to define the Weil pairing on
\mathcal{X}
. The key ingredient is the following:

Lemma 4.3
.

The cohomology group
\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{G}_{m})
is zero (See Proposition 5.2,
[
4
]
).

It follows from the above Lemma

that any Azumaya algebra on
\mathcal{X}
is trivial. Let
L_{1},L_{2}\in\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]
be two
2\mbox{-torsion}
line bundles on
\mathcal{X}
. Then we have
{\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}\cong\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}(M,M)}
for some locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\mbox{-module}
of rank two. We define the pairing
\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}
to be the degree of the line bundle
\wedge^{2}M
modulo two. We note that
M
is unique up to a twist (See Remark
4.1
) and therefore the partify of the degree of
\wedge^{2}M
is well-defined.

Definition 4.4
.

The Weil pairing on
\mathcal{X}
is the map
{\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]\times\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
as defined above. We denote this pairing by
\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}
.

Remark 4.5
.

When
\mathcal{X}
is a smooth, proper curve over
k
, we know that the Weil pairing as defined above coincides with the usual Weil pairing (See Lemma 2,
[
9
]
). Therefore we know in this case that the Weil pairing is bilinear, alternating and non-degenerate.

5.
Weil pairing on twisted curves

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a twisted curve over
k
of genus
g
on which two in invertible. A two torsion line bundle gives an étale double cover of
\mathcal{X}
which is also a twisted curve. We can use this double cover to compute the Weil pairing. For a smooth, proper curve, this idea is due to Mumford (See Lemma 2,
[
9
]
).

Situation 5.1
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\in\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]
be two
2\mbox{-torsion}
line bundles. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathcal{X}
be the étale double cover of
\mathcal{X}
given by
L_{1}
, and let
\tau
denote the involution of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
over
\mathcal{X}
. Let
G
denote the Galois group
\{id_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}},\tau\}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
over
\mathcal{X}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

In the above Situation
5.1
we have the following:

(1)

There exists a line bundle
P
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
such that

P\cong\tau^{*}P\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}\pi^{*}(L_{2}).

(2)

For any line bundle
P
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
such that the above holds, we have

\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}\equiv\text{ degree}(P)\text{ modulo 2}.

Proof.

We give a cohomological reasoning for the existence of
P
while the rest of the proof is identical to Mumford’s argument in
[
9
]
.
We have the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence for the Galois cover
{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathcal{X}}
that converges to
\text{H}^{p+q}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{G}_{m})
. The second page of the spectral sequence is as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}},\mathbf{G}_{m,{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}})^{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Pic}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})}^{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{1}(G,\text{Pic}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{2}(G,\text{Pic}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{G}_{m}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})^{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{1}(G,\mathbf{G}_{m}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{2}(G,\mathbf{G}_{m}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{3}(G,\mathbf{G}_{m}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d^{2}_{0,1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d^{2}_{1,1}}

Since
\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathcal{X},\mathbf{G}_{m})
is zero, we conclude that the map
d^{2}_{1,1}
is injective. The assignment
\tau\to L_{2}
defines a group homomorphism
G\xrightarrow{h}\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})
. The composite group homomorphism
G\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}\circ h}\text{Pic}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})
gives a
1\mbox{-cocycle}
with values in
\text{Pic}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})
and represents a group cohomology class in
\text{H}^{1}(G,\text{Pic}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}))
. We know that this group cohomology class lies in the kernel of the map
d^{2}_{1,1}
(See Proposition 6.17,
[
10
]
). Therefore, we conclude that the group cohomology class given by
\pi^{*}\circ h
is zero. In other words, there exists a line bundle
P
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
such that
{P\cong\tau^{*}P\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}\pi^{*}L_{2}}
(See Proposition 7.1,
[
10
]
). This proves the first part of the proposition.

Let
P
be any line bundle on
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
that satisfies the first part of the proposition. Let
V
denote the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\mbox{-module}

\pi_{*}P
. Since
\pi
is a finite, étale morphism of degree two, we conclude that
V
is a locally
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\mbox{-module}
of rank two. We claim that the Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}
is isomorphic to the matrix algebra
\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}(V,V)
. Since
P
is an
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}\mbox{-module}
, we have a map of sheaves
{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}\times P\to P}
which gives a map
{\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}})\times\pi_{*}(P)}\to\pi_{*}(P)
. Since
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}})\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\oplus L_{1}
, we thus have an action of
{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\oplus L_{1}}
on
V
. We choose an isomorphism
{\alpha:\pi^{*}L_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}P\to\tau^{*}P}
such that the composition:

P\cong\pi^{*}L_{2}\otimes\pi^{*}L_{2}\otimes P\xrightarrow{id\otimes\alpha}\pi^{*}L_{2}\otimes\tau^{*}P\xrightarrow{\tau^{*}\alpha}\tau^{*}(\tau^{*}P)\cong P

is identity. Then we have the map:

\pi^{*}L_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}\left(P\oplus\tau^{*}P\right)\cong\left(\pi^{*}L_{2}\otimes P\right)\oplus\left(\pi^{*}L_{2}\otimes\tau^{*}P\right)\xrightarrow{\alpha\oplus\tau^{*}\alpha}\tau^{*}P\oplus P\cong P\oplus\tau^{*}P.

We have
{\pi^{*}V\cong P\oplus\tau^{*}P}
and the adjoint of the above map gives a morphism
{L_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}V\to V}
that defines an action of
L_{2}
on
V
. Altogether, this makes
V
a module over the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\mbox{-algebra}

\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}
and gives an isomorphism
{\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}\cong\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}(V,V)}
(See Lemma 2,
[
9
]
). Finally, we observe that

\pi^{*}(\wedge^{2}V)\cong\wedge^{2}\pi^{*}(V)\cong P\otimes\tau^{*}P

and therefore we have

2\text{ deg}(\wedge^{2}V)=\text{ deg}(P)+\text{ deg}(\tau^{*}P)=2\text{ deg}(P).

We conclude that the degree of
\wedge^{2}V
equals the degree of
P
. This proves the second part of the proposition.

∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The Weil pairing on
\mathcal{X}
is bilinear and alternating.

Proof.

Let
L_{1},L_{2},L^{\prime}_{2}
be
2\mbox{-torsion}
line bundles on
\mathcal{X}
. It is clear from the definition that the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{2},L_{1}\}}
is the opposite algebra of
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}
. Therefore if
{\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}\cong\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}(M,M)}
for some locally free, finite
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\mbox{-module}

M
, then we have
{\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{2},L_{1}\}}\cong\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}(M^{*},M^{*})}
where
M^{*}
is the dual module
{\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}(M,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})}
. Since
{\wedge^{2}M^{*}}
is isomorphic to
{\left(\wedge^{2}M\right)^{-1}}
, we see that
{\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}=-\langle L_{2},L_{1}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}}
.

Let
{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathcal{X}}
be the étale double cover of
\mathcal{X}
defined by
L_{1}
as in Proposition
5.2
. Let
\tau
denote the non-trivial involution of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
over
\mathcal{X}
. Then by Proposition
5.2
there exist line bundles
P,P^{\prime}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
such that

\pi^{*}L_{2}\cong\tau^{*}P\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}P^{-1}\text{ and }\pi^{*}L^{\prime}_{2}\cong\tau^{*}P^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}P^{\prime-1}.

Then we see that

\tau^{*}(P\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}P^{\prime})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}(P\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}P^{\prime})^{-1}\cong L_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}L^{\prime}_{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle L_{1},L_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}L^{\prime}_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}=\deg(P\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}P^{\prime})=\deg(P)+\deg(P^{\prime})=\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}+\langle L_{1},L^{\prime}_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}

Thus the Weil pairing is linear is the second variable. Since we have observed that
{\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}=-\langle L_{2},L_{1}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}}
, we conclude that the pairing is bilinear. Finally, the pullback
\pi^{*}(L_{1})
is the trivial line bundle on
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
and therefore we conclude from Proposition
5.2
that
{\langle L_{1},L_{1}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}=0}
.

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

In Situation

, we have an exact sequence:

We claim that

. A consequence of the spectral sequence is that we have a long exact sequence in low degrees which reads as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Pic}}(\mathcal{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Pic}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})^{G}}

Theorem 5.7
.

In Situation
5.5
, assume that the stabilizers of the nodes of
\mathcal{X}
are of even order. Let

be the subgroup defined by

. Then the Weil pairing on

induces a pairing:

Moreover, with isomorphisms

and

(See Propositions

and

), the above pairing can realized as the graph pairing (as in Remark

\text{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\text{H}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\langle-,-\rangle_{\Gamma}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Let
L_{1}\in H
and

be

line bundles. Since

is trivial on the normalization, we have

for some

is trivial on each component of

(See Proposition

Thus we see that the Weil pairing restricted to

factors through a pairing

We use the second part of Proposition
5.2
to prove that the above pairing is identical to the graph pairing. Let

. Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{X}}}

and the components are glued together along nodes as prescribed by

are conjugate if they are interchanged by the involution
\tau

on

. Then for

, there are conjugate irreducible components
{\{\mathcal{X}_{i}^{a},\mathcal{X}_{i}^{b}\}}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
which are mapped isomorphically to
\mathcal{X}_{i}
, and there are nodes
e_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{b}
lying above
e_{i}
such that

\{\mathcal{X}_{1}^{a},e^{a}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}^{a},e^{a}_{2},\ldots,e^{a}_{l-1},\mathcal{X}_{l}^{a},e^{a}_{l},\mathcal{X}_{1}^{b},e^{b}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}^{b},\ldots,e_{l-1}^{b},\mathcal{X}_{l}^{b},e_{l}^{b},\mathcal{X}_{1}^{a}\}

is a cycle of length
2l
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
. As in the previous case, the line bundle
\pi^{*}(L_{2})
has trivial characters at all nodes except at
\{e_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{b}|1\leq i\leq n\}
where it has the non-trivial character of order two. Again by the same argument we used earlier, we can ensure that the line bundle
P
meets the conditions
1
and
2
of the previous case. Therefore the degree of
P
on any component that does not contain the nodes
{\{e^{a}_{i},e^{b}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}}
is integral. From the second condition, we conclude that the degree of
P
is integral on the components
{\{\mathcal{X}_{i}^{a}|2\leq i\leq l\}}
. Since the degree is constant on conjugate pairs, the degree of
P
is also integral on the components
{\{\mathcal{X}_{i}^{b}|2\leq i\leq n\}}
. Now, the character of
P
at
e^{a}_{l}
is trivial but
\pi^{*}(L_{2})
has the non-trivial character of order two at
e^{a}_{l}
. Hence the character of
P
at
e^{b}_{l}
must be the non-trivial element of order two. Therefore, we conclude that the degree of
P
on
\mathcal{X}_{1}^{a}
is a half-integer (See Remark
2.2
). In conclusion, the degree of
P
on any component of
{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}}
is integral and equal to its degree on the conjugate component except for the conjugate pair
\{\mathcal{X}_{1}^{a},\mathcal{X}_{1}^{b}\}
. The degree of
P
on
\mathcal{X}_{1}^{a}
equals its degree on
\mathcal{X}_{1}^{b}
and it is a half-integer. It follows that the total degree of
P
is odd, and hence we have
\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{X}}=1
.

∎

Remark 5.8
.

When not all stabilizers of the nodes in
\mathcal{X}
are of even order, the induced pairing on
{H\times\frac{\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]}{\text{Pic}(C)[2]}}

\text{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\text{H}_{1}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

as a natural subgroup of

(See Remark

).

Proposition 5.9
.

The Weil pairing on
\mathcal{X}
is non-degenerate if and only if the stabilizers of the non-separating nodes are of even order or equivalently if
\lvert\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]\rvert=2^{2g}
.

Proof.

We have the exact sequence:

0\to\text{Pic}(C)[2]\xrightarrow{f^{*}}\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]\to\text{H}_{1}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to 0

where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is the modified dual graph obtained by deleting the edges corresponding to the nodes with odd stabilizers (See Remark
3.9
). We regard

as a subgroup of
\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]
. Let

denote the subgroup of
\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]
consisting of those line bundles which are trivial on the normalization. Then we have

(See Proposition
3.11
). With the notation as in Situation
5.5
, we have the exact sequence:

0\to H\to\text{Pic}(C)[2]\to\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in V}\text{Pic}(C_{i})[2]\to 0.

We know that the Weil pairing is non-degenerate on each

which is a smooth, proper curve. Therefore, it follows from Proposition

that the kernel of the Weil pairing restricted to
{\text{Pic}(C)[2]}

has kernel
\text{Pic}(C)[2]
in the second factor (See Proposition
5.7
and Remark

). It follows that the Weil pairing on
\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]

{H\times\frac{\text{Pic}(\mathcal{X})[2]}{\text{Pic}(C)[2]}\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}

∎

6.
Weil pairing in a family

Let
B
be a strictly Heneslian local ring with the residue field
k
such that
\text{Spec}(B)
has a unique generic point which we denote by
\eta
. A special case of interest is when
B
is a complete discrete valuation ring. By a family of twisted curves over a scheme we mean the notion of twisted curves due to Abramovich and Vistoli as outlined by Olsson in
[
11
]
(Definition 1.2).

Situation 6.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{}\text{Spec}(B)}
be a family of twisted curves of genus
g\geq 2
. Let
{j:\mathscr{X}_{0}\to\mathscr{X}}
denote the special fiber and
{i:\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}}\to\mathscr{X}}
denote the generic fiber. Let
{C\to\text{Spec}(B)}
be the coarse space of
\mathscr{X}
and we denote its special fiber and the generic fiber by
C_{0}
and
C_{\eta}
, respectively.

Proposition 6.2
.

In Situation
6.1
, let
r
be a positive integer invertible on
\text{Spec}(B)
. Then we have the following:

(1)

The map
{\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X})[r]\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{0})[r]}

is bijective.

(2)

If
B
is a discrete valuation ring, then the map
{\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X})[r]\xrightarrow{i^{*}}\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}})[r]}
is injective.

(3)

The map
\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X})\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{0})
is surjective.

Proof.

These assertions essentially follow from Chiodo’s constructions in
[
1
]
. Let
F
be a line bundle on
\mathscr{X}
whose relative degree is a multiple of
r
. Then there is a Deligne-Mumford stack
{F^{1/r}\to\text{Spec}(B)}
that parameterizes
r^{th}
roots of the line bundle
F
. An object of
F^{1/r}
over a
B
-scheme
B^{\prime}
is a pair
(M,\lambda)
where
M
is a line bundle on
{\mathscr{X}_{B^{\prime}}=\mathscr{X}\times_{B}B^{\prime}}
and
{\lambda:M^{\otimes r}\to F_{B^{\prime}}}
is an isomorphism. The stack
F^{1/r}
is étale and separated over
\text{Spec}(B)
(Proposition 3.4,
[
1
]
). After rigidifying along the automorphisms given by the group sheaf
\mu_{r}
on
B
, we have a scheme
\overline{F^{1/r}}\to\text{Spec}(B)
whose fiber over a geometric point
q
is
{\{L\in\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{q})\lvert L^{\otimes r}\cong F_{q}\}}
. We refer to
[
1
]
(Definition 3.5) and references therein. The scheme
\overline{F^{1/r}}
is étale and separated over
\text{Spec}(B)
. Moreover, if all geometric fibers
\mathscr{X}_{q}
have
r^{2g}
roots of
F_{p}
in
\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{q})
, then
\overline{F^{1/r}}
is a finite, étale scheme over
\text{Spec}(B)
(Proposition 3.7,
[
1
]
).

We apply the above construction to the
r^{th}
roots of the trivial line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}
on
\mathscr{X}
. Since
B
is strictly Henselian, and the morphism
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}^{1/r}}\to\text{Spec}(B)
is étale and separated, any point in the closed fiber uniquely extends to a section (See
[
12
]
, Theorem 4.2, (c) and (d)). This proves part
1
. If
B
is a disecrete valuation ring then we apply the valuative criterion of separatedness to the separated morphism
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}^{1/r}}\to\text{Spec}(B)
. This proves part
2
.

Let
L_{0}\in\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{0})
be a line bundle on
\mathscr{X}_{0}
. We say
L_{0}
can be lifted to
\mathscr{X}
if it lies in the image of the map
j^{*}
. We first argue that if
L_{0}
is a pull back of a line bundle on the coarse space
C_{0}
of
\mathscr{X}_{0}
, then it can be lifted. A line bundle on the nodal curve
C_{0}
is given by a Cartier divisor. Such a divisor can be deformed to a Cartier divisor on the coarse space of
\mathscr{X}
. We argue this is as follows. Let
p
be a closed point in the smooth locus of
C_{0}
. Then there is a Zariski neighborhood
U\subset C
of
p
such that
U\to\text{Spec}(B)
is smooth. Since
B
is strictly Henselian and
U
is smooth over
B
, the section given by
p
over the closed point of
\text{Spec}(B)
can be extended to a section
\text{Spec}(B)\xrightarrow{s}U
. Since
U\to\text{Spec}(B)
is smooth, the section
s
gives a Cartier divisor in all geometric fibers of
C
. Therefore it is a Cartier divisor in
C
(See
[
8
,
Tag 062Y
]
). Thus, we see that the Cartier divisors on
C_{0}
can be lifted to Cartier divisors on
C
. Therefore, if
L_{0}\in\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{0})
is a pull back of a line bundle on
C_{0}
, then it can be lifted. In the general case, there is a positive integer
n
invertible on
\text{Spec}(B)
such that
L_{0}^{n}
is a pull back of a line bundle on
C_{0}
. Since
L_{0}^{n}
can be lifted, there exists a line bundle
L
on
\mathscr{X}
whose relative degree is a multiple of
n
, and which restricts to
L_{0}^{n}
on the special fiber. Then we have the scheme
\overline{L^{1/n}}
which is étale and separated over
\text{Spec}(B)
. Moreover,
\overline{L^{1/n}}
admits a section over the closed point given by
L_{0}
. Since
B
is Henselian, this section can be extended to a section over
\text{Spec}(B)
(See
[
12
]
, Theorem 4.2). In other words,
L_{0}
can be lifted to a line bundle on
\mathscr{X}
.

∎

Suppose in the Situation

Lemma 6.4

In Situation

We have the Kummer exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathscr{X},\mathbf{G}_{m,{\mathscr{X}}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathscr{X},\mu_{r})}

Proposition 6.5
.

In Situation
6.1
, suppose
2
is invertible on
\text{Spec}(B)
. Let
{L_{1},L_{2}\in\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X})[2]}
be two
2\mbox{-torsion}
line bundles on
\mathscr{X}
. Then we have equality of Weil pairings:
{\langle L_{1}|_{\mathscr{X}_{0}},L_{2}|_{\mathscr{X}_{0}}\rangle_{\mathscr{X}_{0}}=\langle L_{1}|_{\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}}},L_{2}|_{\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}}}\rangle_{\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}}}}
.

Proof.

The Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}
on
\mathscr{X}
corresponds to a
2\mbox{-torsion}
Brauer class in the cohomology group
\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathscr{X},\mathbf{G}_{m})
. From the above Lemma
6.4
, we conclude that
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},_{2}\}}
must be a matrix algebra. In other words, we have a finite, locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}\mbox{-module}

V
on
\mathscr{X}
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\{L_{1},L_{2}\}}\cong\mathop{\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}}}(V,V)
. Since the degree the line bundle

is constant on the fibers, we conclude that the Weil pairings are equal as claimed.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
Z
be a connected Noetherian scheme on which
2
is invertible. Let
\mathscr{X}\to Z
be a family of twisted curves over
Z
. Let
{L_{1},L_{2}\in\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X})[2]}
be
2\mbox{-torsion}
line bundles on
\mathscr{X}
. Then the Weil pairing
\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle_{\mathscr{X}_{p}}
is independent of the geometric fiber
\mathscr{X}_{p}
of
\mathscr{X}
over

.

Proof.

We may assume that
Z
is irreducible. Let
p
be a geometric point of
Z
lying over

. We have the stalk
{\mathcal{O}^{sh}_{Z,s}}
of the étale structure sheaf which is a strictly Henselian ring. Then
\mathscr{X}\times_{Z}\text{Spec}({\mathcal{O}^{sh}_{Z,s}})
is a family of twisted curves over
\text{Spec}({\mathcal{O}^{sh}_{Z,s}})
as in Situation

. From the above Proposition

we conclude that the Weil pairing on

is equal to the Weil pairing on the generic fiber.
∎

Consequently, we get a well-defined Weil pairing over the moduli of twisted curves. We can use deformation methods to give an alternate argument to conclude some of the properties of the Weil pairing which we proved directly in the previous section. The crucial tool is that we can deform a twisted curve to a smooth, proper curve.

Remark 6.7
.

We have a moduli space of twisted curves of genus
g
in which the space of smooth, proper curves forms an open, dense substack (See
[
1
]
). Let
\mathcal{X}
be a twisted curve over

with nodes

be the order of the stabilizer at the node

. Then

admits a versal deformation space given by
{R[z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m}]/(z_{1}^{l_{1}}-t_{1},z_{2}^{l_{2}}-t_{2},\ldots,z_{m}^{l_{m}}-t_{m})}

are parameters in

that define the nodes (See Theorem 2.13,

]
). A consequence of this is that there exists a family of twisted curves over a complete discrete valuation ring

whose special fiber is isomorphic to

and the generic fiber is a smooth, proper curve (See Appendix B,
[
13

for the case of schematic curves). Moreover, we can ensure that étale locally at a node

\left[\text{Spec}\left(B[z,w]/(zw-t)\right)\middle/G_{e}\right]\xrightarrow{x\to z^{l},y\to w^{l}}\text{Spec}(B[x,y]/(xy-t^{l}))

, and the cyclic group

acts via

.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a twisted cure of genus

over

. Then the Weil pairing on

is bilinear and alternating. If the non-separating nodes of

There exists a family of curves as in Situation
6.1
where

is a complete discrete valuation ring, the special fiber

, and the generic fiber is a smooth, proper curve. We know that the Weil pairing on

∎

7.
Connection with tropical geometry

In this section, we look at the
tropical specialization
of line bundles from curves to graphs which arises when we have an arithmetic surface. We refer to
[
13
]
for the notions and ideas in this regard. Let
B
be a complete discrete valuation ring with the residue field
k
and field of fractions
K
.

Definition 7.1
.

A semi-stable arithmetic surface over
B
is a proper, flat scheme over
\text{Spec}(B)
such that the generic fiber is a smooth, geometrically connected curve and the special fiber is a reduced curve with at worst nodal singularities. A strongly semi-stable arithmetic surface is a semi-stable arithmetic surface such that the irreducible components of the special fiber are smooth.

By a metric graph we mean a metric space obtained by viewing the edges of a graph as line segments of prescribed lengths (See Definition 1D,
[
13
]
). We have notions of divisors and principal divisors on a metric graph for which we refer to
[
13
]
and the references therein. The Picard group of a metric graph is the group of divisors modulo the principal divisors. We denote the degree zero Picard group by
\text{Pic}^{0}
and it is isomorphic to the real torus
\mathbb{R}^{g^{\prime}}/\mathbb{Z}^{g^{\prime}}
where
g^{\prime}
is the genus of the graph (See Theorem 2.8,
[
5
]
). If
\Gamma
is a graph, then the associated metric graph is obtained by viewing each edge in
\Gamma
as a line segment of length one. We denote it by
\Gamma
just the same.

Situation 7.2
.

Let
C
be a regular strongly semi-stable arithmetic surface over
\text{Spec}(B)
. Let
i:C_{\eta}\to C
denote the generic fiber and
j:C_{0}\to C
denote the special fiber. Let
\Gamma
denote the dual graph of
C_{0}
. We denote the geometric generic fiber by
C_{\overline{\eta}}
. Let
r
be a positive integer invertible on
\text{Spec}(B)
. Let
g^{\prime}
denote the genus of the graph
\Gamma
.

In the above Situation
7.2
, we have a degree preserving homomorphism
{\text{Pic}(C)\xrightarrow{\rho}\text{Pic}(\Gamma)}
. If
L\in\text{Pic}(C)
, then
\rho(L)
is a divisor supported on the vertices corresponding to the irreducible components of
C_{0}
and the corresponding coefficient is the degree of
L
restricted to the associated component. The Zariski closure of a Cartier divisor on
C_{\eta}
gives a Cartier divisor on
C
. Thus we get the
tropical specialization
map
{\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})\xrightarrow{trop}\text{Pic}(\Gamma)}
. The specialization map is compatible with finite field extensions of
K
and gives a map from the geometric Picard group
{\text{Pic}(C_{\overline{\eta}})\xrightarrow{trop}\text{Pic}(\Gamma)}
(See Section 2C,
[
13
]
). Moreover, the morphism
\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{\overline{\eta}})\to\text{Pic}^{0}(\Gamma)
is surjective onto the torsion points (or equivalently rational points) of
\text{Pic}^{0}(\Gamma)
(See Remark A.10,
[
13
]
).

Lemma 7.3
.

A line bundle
L_{\eta}\in\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})
lies in the kernel of the map
{\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})\xrightarrow{trop}\text{Pic}(\Gamma)}
if and only if there exists a line bundle

such that

and the degree of
j^{*}L
is zero on every irreducible component of
C_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
D
be the Zariski closure of a divisor on
C_{\eta}
that defines the line bundle
L_{\eta}
on
C_{\eta}
. Then
D
is a Cartier divisor on
C
. Moreover,
trop(L_{\eta})=0
precisely means that there exists a vertical divisor
D^{\prime}
on
C
such that
{L=\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(D^{\prime})}
satisfies the conditions stated in the lemma (See Section 2A,
[
13
]
for details).
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

In Situation
7.2
, we have the following:

(1)

\lvert{\text{Pic}}(C_{0})[r]\rvert=r^{2g-g^{\prime}}
.

(2)

For every line bundle
L_{0}
in
{\text{Pic}}(C_{0})[r]
, there exists a unique line bundle

in
{\text{Pic}}(C)[r]
such that
{j^{*}L\cong L_{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{C_{0}}
denote the normalization of

. Then we have an exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

(For

{\lvert{\text{Pic}}(C_{0})[r]\rvert=2^{2g-g^{\prime}}}

Lemma 7.5
.

The kernel of the morphism
\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{\eta})\xrightarrow{trop}\text{Pic}^{0}(\Gamma)
is
r\mbox{-divisible}
.

Proof.

Step I:
Let
\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{0})
denote the subgroup of
\text{Pic}(C_{0})
consisting of those line bundles whose degree is zero on each irreducible component. We first claim that
\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{0})
is
r\mbox{-divisible}
. If
C_{0}^{1},C_{0}^{2},\ldots,C_{0}^{n}
are the irreducible components of the normalization
\widehat{C_{0}}
, then we have the exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{ ker }\left(\text{Pic}(C_{0})\to\text{Pic}(\widehat{C_{0}})\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Pic}}^{0}(C_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{0}^{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

The group
\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{0}^{i})
is
r\mbox{-divisible}
for each
i
. We have a description of the kernel of the above map in the proof of Proposition
3.11
. Since
k^{*}
is closed under taking
r^{th}
roots, it follows that the kernel above is
r\mbox{-divisible}
. Therefore we conclude that
\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{0})
is
r\mbox{-divisible}
as well.

Step II:
Let
L_{\eta}\in\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{\eta})
be a line bundle on
C_{\eta}
such that
trop(L)
is zero. By Lemma
7.3
, there exists a line bundle
L\in\text{Pic}(C)
which restricts to
L_{\eta}
on
C_{\eta}
and such that the degree of
j^{*}L
on each irreducible component of
C_{0}
is zero. We have the scheme
\overline{L^{1/r}}
which is étale and separated over
\text{Spec}(B)
that parametrizes
r^{th}
roots of
L
(See the proof of Proposition
6.2
). Since
j^{*}L
belongs to
\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{0})
, we conclude from the first step that
\overline{L^{1/r}}
admits a section over the closed point of
\text{Spec}(B)
. Since
\overline{L^{1/r}}
is étale over
\text{Spec}(B)
, this section can be extended to all of
\text{Spec}(B)
. In other words,
L
admits an
r^{th}
root in
\text{Pic}(C)
whose degree on each irreducible component of
C_{0}
is zero. Consequently,
L_{\eta}
admits an
r^{th}
root in
\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})
which also lies in the kernel of the
trop
map.
∎

The map

We know that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Proposition 7.7
.

A line bundle
L_{\eta}
in
\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})[r]
lies in the kernel of the tropical specialization
{\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})\xrightarrow{trop}\text{Pic}(\Gamma)}
if and only if there exists a line bundle
L
in
\text{Pic}(C)[r]
such that
j^{*}(L)\cong L_{\eta}
.

Proof.

By counting, we see that the kernel of
{\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})[r]\xrightarrow{trop}\text{Pic}(\Gamma)[r]}
has cardinality at most
r^{2g-g^{\prime}}
. By Proposition
7.4
, we conclude that we have
{\lvert\text{Pic}(C)[r]\rvert=r^{2g-g^{\prime}}}
. By part
2
of Proposition
6.2
, we know that the map
{\text{Pic}(C)[r]\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})[r]}
is injective and by Lemma
7.3
, its image lies in the kernel of the
trop
map. This completes the proof.

∎

Remark 7.8
.

We can interpret the above Proposition
7.7
by saying that an
r\mbox{-torsion}
line bundle in
\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})
tropically specializes to zero if and only if it algebraically specializes (in the Zariski topology of the scheme
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{C}^{1/r}}
) to some
r\mbox{-torsion}
line bundle on the special fiber.

Proposition 7.9
.

Suppose in Situation
7.2
, we have
r=2
and that
C_{0}
is totally degenerate. That is, the irreducible components of
C_{0}
are all genus zero curves. Then the kernel of the map
{\text{Pic}^{0}(C_{\overline{\eta}})[2]\xrightarrow{trop}\text{Pic}^{0}(\Gamma)[2]}
is isotropic for the Weil pairing on

. (See Proposition 4.3,
[
2
]
).

Proof.

Since
C_{0}
is totally degenerate, the genus of the graph
\Gamma
is
g
and the Weil pairing on
\text{Pic}(C_{0})[2]
is identically zero (See Proposition
5.6
). By Proposition
7.4
, we have an isomorphism
{\text{Pic}(C)[2]\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\text{Pic}(C_{0})[2]}
. By Proposition

∎

Now we look at the situation when we have an arithmetic surface where the special fiber is a twisted curve. Suppose have a family of twisted curves as in Situation
6.1
, where
B
is a complete discrete valuation ring and the generic fiber
\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}}
is a smooth curve. The coarse space
C\to\text{Spec}(B)
is a semi-stable arithmetic surface. However, it is important to note that
C
is
not
regular at the nodes which are stacky. If
e
is a node of
C
whose stabilizer has order
r
, then an étale local neighborhood of
C
at
e
is isomorphic to
\text{Spec}(B[x,y]/(xy-t^{r}))
for some
t
in the maximal ideal of
B
(See part (ii), Proposition 2.2,
[
11
]
). Since
t^{r}
is not a uniformizer of
B
, we see that the
C
fails to be regular at
e
. Let
\pi
denote a uniformizer of
B
, and let
n
be the positive integer such that the ideals
(t)
and
(\pi^{n})
in
B
are equal. Then a scheme regular at
e
, is obtained by a sequence of blow ups that modify the special fiber by introducing
nr-1
number of rational bridges at
e
. By performing a similar operation of blow ups at all the nodes, we obtain a regular scheme
C^{reg}
.

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph of genus
g
with the vertex set
V
. We regard
\Gamma
as a metric graph where each edge is assigned length one. For each edge, we introduce
r-1
vertices placed equidistantly on it so that it is divided into
r
equal parts. Let
V^{reg}
be the union of
V
together with these vertices. Then there are
r^{2g}
linearly in-equivalent divisors supported on
V^{reg}
that form the subgroup
{\text{Pic}}^{0}(\Gamma)[r]
.

Proof.

We can conceive of a twisted curve
\mathscr{X}_{0}
over
k
of genus
g
such that the following holds:

•

The stabilizer of every node is a cyclic group of order
r
.

•

The dual graph of
\mathscr{X}_{0}
is isomorphic to
\Gamma
.

There exists a family of curves
\mathscr{X}
over a complete discrete valuation ring
B
as in Remark
6.7
where the special fiber is isomorphic to
\mathscr{X}_{0}
and the generic fiber is smooth. Let
{C\to\text{Spec}(B)}
denote the coarse space of
\mathscr{X}
. Since the stabilizers of the nodes are cyclic groups of order
r
, we have
\lvert\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{0})[r]\rvert=r^{2g}
(See Proposition
3.7
for
r=2
, and the general case is similar. See Corollary 3.1,
[
1
]
). We have
{\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X})[r]\cong\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{0})[r]}
and the pull back map
{\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X})[r]\to\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}})}
is injective (See Proposition
6.2
). Thus we have
{\lvert\text{Pic}(\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}})[r]\rvert=r^{2g}}
. The étale local structure of the coarse space
C
at the nodes is as outlined in Remark
6.7
. A consequence of this is that
C^{reg}
, which is obtained by a sequence of blowups as discussed above, has
r-1
rational bridges for each node of
C
. Therefore the dual graph of
C^{reg}
is isomorphic to
\Gamma
(up to scaling) but with a larger vertex set
V^{reg}
. The generic fiber of
C^{reg}
is isomorphic to
\mathscr{X}_{{\eta}}
and therefore we have
{\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})[r]=r^{2g}}
. Therefore, we deduce that the tropical specialization
{\text{Pic}(C_{\eta})[r]\xrightarrow{trop}\text{Pic}(\Gamma)[r]}
is surjective (See Corollary
7.6
). Since the image of this morphism is supported on
V^{reg}
, we conclude the result of the proposition.

∎

Remark 7.11
.

By having stabilizers only for the non-separating nodes, we see that the above Proposition
7.10
also holds when we only divide the non-separating edges into
r
equal parts (See Remark
3.9
). Also, we note that this is straightforward to prove combinatorially so that Proposition
7.10
serves more as a consistency check and another illustration of the interplay between combinatorial tropical geometry and algebraic geometry of curves.

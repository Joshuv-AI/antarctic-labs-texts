Stability estimates for nonlocal balance laws arising in traffic modelling
Felisia Angela Chiarello, Harold Deivi Contreras

Abstract
. This paper focuses on the proof of the stability of entropy weak solutions of a nonlocal balance law modelling vehicular traffic flow on a road with on- and off-ramps. The stability is obtained with respect to a kernel function in the source term. We get an estimate of the 𝐋 𝟏 − \mathbf{L^{1}}- dependence of the solution with respect to the initial datum, the on-ramp rate, the off-ramp rate and the mentioned kernel function. We also show numerical experiments in order to perform an optimization problem in traffic flow with on-ramps.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Aim

In traffic flow modeling, nonlocal conservation laws are intended to describe the behaviour of drivers that adapt their velocity with respect to what happens in front of them, see
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
]
; the classical LWR (Lighthill-Whitham
[
10
]
and Richards
[
13
]
) is not able to model this type of situation. For this reason, in order to generalise the LWR model in such a way to make it able to describe more realistic settings, in
[
2
]
a nonlocal balance law intended to model vehicular traffic flow on a main road wit on- and off-ramps is introduced and it is given by

(1.1)

\rho_{t}+(\rho v(\rho*\omega_{\eta}))_{x}=S_{\mathrm{on}}(\cdot,\cdot,\rho,\rho*\omega_{\eta,\delta})-S_{\mathrm{off}}(\cdot,\cdot,\rho),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where
S_{\mathrm{on}}
and
S_{\mathrm{off}}
represents the traffic flow entering and exiting through an on- and off-ramp respectively, and the convolution term in
S_{\mathrm{on}}
is defined as follows

(\rho*\omega_{\eta,\delta})(t,x)=\int_{x-\eta+\delta}^{x+\eta+\delta}\rho(t,y)\omega_{\eta,\delta}(y-x)\mathrm{d}y,

with
\eta\in[0,1]
and
\delta\in[-\eta,\eta].
Here, the parameter
\eta
represents the length of the support of the kernel function
\omega_{\eta,\delta}
, while
\delta
is the point at which the maximum of the kernel is attained.
This choice of the kernel function models the fact that drivers on the on-ramp can see what happens on backward and forward on the main road.

It is well known that on-ramp merging has a great impact on traffic efficiency, if one concentrates on highway
networks the reduction of the capacity is often due to on- and off-ramps, for this reason in this paper we are interested to study the dependence of solutions to (
1.1
) on the convolution kernel given in the source term
S_{\mathrm{on}}
. The strategies that we employ are inspired by the results obtained in
[
2
,
5
]
. In particular, we adopt the results about existence and uniqueness to (
1.1
) presented in
[
2
]
and we propose the analytical study of the dependence of solutions to (
1.1
) on the kernel function in the source term.

1.2.
Related work

In literature, the LWR model has been extended to include on and off-ramps by means of source and sink terms, see
[
6
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
]
. Starting from the model introduced in
[
11
]
, in
[
2
]
the authors introduce the new nonlocal balance law (
1.1
), in which the velocity function depends on a weighted mean of the downstream traffic density, in order to model the behaviour of drivers that adapt their velocity with respect to what happens in front of them; moreover the nonlocal source term is intended to model the fact that drivers on the on-ramp can see what happens on backward and forward on the main road.
Regarding the aferomentioned model the existence and uniqueness of entropy weak solution has been proved, specifically, approximating the problem using an Upwind-type numerical scheme and providing compactness estimates for the sequence of approximate solutions that allows to get the convergence and thus, the existence of solution. Furthemore, by using Kružkov’s doubling of variables technique the authors get an
\mathbf{L^{1}}
stability estimate depending on initial data, the on-ramp rate and the off-ramp rate.
On the other hand, motivated by control and optimisation problems, in
[
5
]
the authors study a conservation law with nonlocal flux function given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\left(f(t,x,\rho)v(\rho\ast\omega)(t,x)\right)=0.

The authors obtain an estimate of the dependence of the solution to (
1.2
) with respect to the kernel function, the velocity and the initial datum. The stability is obtained from an entropy condition through the doubling of variables technique.

2.
Mathematical model

Let us consider the equation (
1.1
) with terms
S_{\mathrm{on}}
and
S_{\mathrm{off}}
defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{on}}(t,x,\rho,\rho*\omega_{\eta,\delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{on}}(x)q_{\mathrm{on}}(t)\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\max}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\rho*\omega_{\eta,\delta}}{\rho_{\max}}\right),

(2.2)

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{off}}(t,x,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{off}}(x)q_{\mathrm{off}}(t)\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\max}},

with
\rho_{\max}=1
for the sake of simplicity, and we also endow the nonlocal traffic reaction model (
1.1
)-(
2.1
)-(
2.2
) with an initial condition as follows

(2.3)

\displaystyle\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x)\in\left(\mathbf{L^{1}}\cap\mathbf{BV}\right)(\mathbb{R};[0,{\rho_{\max}}]).

Let us assume the following assumptions on the parameters of model (
1.1
).

Assumptions 2.1
.

We assume

(
i
)

q_{\mathrm{on}}^{\mathrm{ramp}}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{+}),q_{\mathrm{off}}^{\mathrm{ramp}}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{+}).

(
ii
)

v\in\mathbf{C^{2}}([0,{\rho_{\max}}];\mathbb{R}^{+}),\ v^{\prime}(\rho)\leq 0,\,\rho\in[0,\rho_{\max}]
.

(
iii
)

\omega_{\eta}\in\mathbf{C^{1}}([0,\eta];\mathbb{R}^{+})\ \textup{with}\ \omega^{\prime}_{\eta}(x)\leq 0,\ \int_{0}^{\eta}\omega_{\eta}(x)\mathrm{d}x=1,\ \forall\eta>0
.

(
iv
)

\omega_{\eta,\delta}\in(\mathbf{C^{1}}\cap\mathbf{L^{1}})([\delta-\eta,\delta+\eta];\mathbb{R}^{+})\ \textup{with}\ \omega^{\prime}(x)_{\eta,\delta}\geq 0\ \text{for}\ x\in[\delta-\eta,0]
,

\omega^{\prime}(x)_{\eta,\delta}\leq 0\ \text{ for }x\in[0,\delta+\eta]
, and
\int_{\delta-\eta}^{\delta+\eta}\omega_{\eta,\delta}(x)\mathrm{d}x=1,\ \forall\eta>0.

We consider solutions in a weak sense as follows

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in(\mathbf{L^{1}}\cap\mathbf{BV})(\mathbb{R};[0,{\rho_{\max}}]).
We say that
\rho\in\mathbf{C}([0,T];\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R};[0,{\rho_{\max}}])),
with
\rho(t,\cdot)\in\mathbf{BV}(\mathbb{R};[0,{\rho_{\max}}])
for
t\in[0,T]
, is a weak solution to (
1.1
)-(
2.3
) if for any
\varphi\in\mathbf{C_{c}^{1}}([0,T[\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\rho\varphi_{t}+\rho V\varphi_{x}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}S_{\mathrm{on}}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}}S_{\mathrm{off}}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)\mathrm{d}x=0,

where
V(t,x)=v((\rho*\omega)(t,x))
.

Furthermore, we consider entropy weak solutions in Kružkov sense, i.e.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in(\mathbf{L^{1}}\cap\mathbf{BV})(\mathbb{R};[0,{\rho_{\max}}]).
We say that
\rho\in\mathbf{C}([0,T];\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R};[0,{\rho_{\max}}])),
with
\rho(t,\cdot)\in\mathbf{BV}(\mathbb{R};[0,{\rho_{\max}}])
for
t\in[0,T]
, is a entropy weak solution to (
1.1
) with initial datum
\rho_{0}
if for any
\varphi\in\mathbf{C_{c}^{1}}([0,T[\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
and for all
k\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|\rho-k|\varphi_{t}+\left|\rho-k\right|V\varphi_{x}-\sgn(\rho-k)kV_{x}\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\sgn(\rho-k)S_{\mathrm{on}}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}}\sgn(\rho-k)S_{\mathrm{off}}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\rho_{0}-k|\varphi(0,x)\mathrm{d}x\geq 0,

where
\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}
and
\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}
are the spatial positions of on-ramp and off-ramp on the main road, respectively.

3.
Main Result

Before giving the main results of this work, we first recall the main Theorem in
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in\left(\mathbf{L^{1}}\cap\mathbf{BV}\right)\left(\mathbb{R};[0,1]\right)
. Let the Assumptions
2.1
hold. Then, for all
T>0
, the problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution
\rho\in\mathbf{C^{0}}\left([0,T];\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])\right)
in the sense of Definition
2.2
. Moreover, the following estimates hold: for any
t\in[0,T]

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\|\rho(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\mathcal{R}_{1}(t),\\
0\leq\rho(t,x)\leq 1,\\
TV(\rho(t))\leq e^{t\mathcal{H}}\left(TV(\rho_{0})+t\mathcal{Q}_{T}\right),\end{array}

where

(3.1)

\ \begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{1}&=\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}^{\mathrm{ramp}}(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,t])}-\min_{x\in\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}^{\mathrm{ramp}}(\cdot)\rho(\cdot,x)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,t])}\\
&-\min_{x\in\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}}\left\|q_{\mathrm{off}}^{\mathrm{ramp}}(\cdot)\rho(\cdot,x)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,t])}\\
\end{split}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{2}\left(\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}+\left\|q_{\mathrm{off}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}+\left\|q_{\mathrm{off}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}+\omega_{\eta}(0)\mathcal{L}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\|v\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,1])}+\|v^{\prime}\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,1])}\right).

The following theorem is the main result of this work and it states the
\mathbf{L^{1}}
- Lipschitz continuous dependence of solutions to (
1.1
) on to the initial datum, the on-ramp rate, the off-ramp rate and the kernel function.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\rho
and
\tilde{\rho}
be two solutions to problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.2
, with initial data
\rho_{0},\ \tilde{\rho}_{0}\in\mathbf{L^{1}}\cap\mathbf{BV}\left(\mathbb{R};[0,1]\right)
, with on-ramp rates
q_{\mathrm{on}}
,
\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}}
, off-ramp rates
q_{\mathrm{off}}
,
\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{off}}
and kernel functions
{\omega}_{\eta,\delta},\,

\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}
, respectively. Assume
v\in\mathbf{C^{2}}\left([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{+}\right)
. Then, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho(t)-\tilde{\rho}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{C}T}\bigg(\left\|\rho_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}+{L}\left(\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,t])}+\left\|q_{\mathrm{off}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{off}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,T])}\right)

\displaystyle+r(T)\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\bigg),

where
r(T)
depends on the
\mathbf{L^{1}}-
norms of initial conditions, expected inflow flow of the on-ramp and expected output flow of the off-ramp and
\mathcal{C}
is defined as in
[
2
, (3.24)]
.

Proof.

Since
\rho
and
\tilde{\rho}
are entropy weak solutions to (
1.1
) then

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\rho_{t}+(\rho V(t,x))_{x}=S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\rho,R_{\mathrm{on}}\right)-S_{\mathrm{off}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{off}},\rho\right)\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\tilde{\rho}_{t}+(\tilde{\rho}\tilde{V}(t,x))_{x}={S}_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right)-S_{\mathrm{off}}\left(t,x,\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{off}},\rho\right)\\
\tilde{\rho}(0,x)=\tilde{\rho}_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

in a distributional sense, where
V(t,x)=v((\rho\ast\omega_{\eta})(t,x)),

R_{\mathrm{on}}=(\rho\ast\omega_{\eta,\delta})(t,x)
,
\tilde{V}(t,x)=v((\tilde{\rho}\ast\omega_{\eta})(t,x)),

\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}=(\tilde{\rho}\ast\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta})(t,x)
. Following the argument in
[
2
]
and using Kružkov’s doubling of variables technique we get

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\|\rho(T,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(T,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{off}}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{V}\right|\left|\partial_{x}\rho(t,x)\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{V}_{x}\right|\left|\rho(t,x)\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\rho,R_{\mathrm{on}}\right)-S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{off}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{off}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\rho\right)-S_{\mathrm{off}}\left(t,x,\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}},\tilde{\rho}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(R)-v(P),

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}v(R)-\partial_{x}v(P).

Except for the second term, all terms appearing at the right-hand side of (
3.7
) are computed as in
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}}\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{off}}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left\|{q}_{\mathrm{off}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}t+{L}\left\|q_{\mathrm{off}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{off}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,T])},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{V}\right|\left|\partial_{x}\rho(t,x)\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(0)\left\|v^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,1])}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}TV(\rho(t,\cdot))\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{V}_{x}\right|\left|\rho(t,x)\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}t.

Regarding the second term in (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\rho,R_{\mathrm{on}}\right)-S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left(\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{1}\right|+\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{2}\right|+\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{3}\right|\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\rho,R_{\mathrm{on}}\right)-S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\rho,\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\rho,\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right)-S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,q_{\mathrm{on}},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right)-S_{\mathrm{on}}\left(t,x,\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right).

We bound
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{2}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{3}
writing

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{2}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{3}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|q_{\mathrm{on}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{L}\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,T])}.

Next, we are going to bound
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{1}
term,

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{1}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{on}}q_{\mathrm{on}}\left(1-\rho\right)\left(\left(1-R_{\mathrm{on}}\right)-\left(1-\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}}\left|\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}-R_{\mathrm{on}}\right|,

thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{1}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}-R_{\mathrm{on}}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

and observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|{R}_{\mathrm{on}}-\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{on}}\right|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\omega_{\eta,\delta}\ast\rho-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\ast\tilde{\rho}\right|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\omega_{\eta,\delta}\ast\rho-\omega_{\eta,\delta}\ast\tilde{\rho}+\omega_{\eta,\delta}\ast\tilde{\rho}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\ast\tilde{\rho}\right|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\omega_{\eta,\delta}\ast\rho-\omega_{\eta,\delta}\ast\tilde{\rho}\right|+\left|\omega_{\eta,\delta}\ast\tilde{\rho}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\ast\tilde{\rho}\right|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\omega_{\eta,\delta}\ast\left(\rho-\tilde{\rho}\right)\right|+\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\left(\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\right)\ast\tilde{\rho}\right|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{on}})}\left\|\rho-\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{on}})}+\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{on}})}\left\|\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{on}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|\rho-\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\rho-\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathcal{R}_{1}(t),

since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{\eta,\delta}(x)\mathrm{d}x=1.
Here
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is defined as in (
3.1
). Then,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{on}}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{on}}^{1}\right|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathcal{R}_{1}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T])}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{R}_{1}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Therefore, the inequality (
3.7
) is equivalent to following inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\rho(T,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(T,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}+{L}\left(\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,t])}+\left\|q_{\mathrm{off}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{off}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,t])}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{C}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}t+\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{R}_{1}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{0}-\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}+{L}\left(\left\|q_{\mathrm{on}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{on}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,t])}+\left\|q_{\mathrm{off}}-\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{off}}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}([0,t])}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{C}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}t+r(T)\left\|\omega_{\eta,\delta}-\tilde{\omega}_{\eta,\delta}\right\|_{\mathbf{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R})}.

An application of Gronwall’s Lemma to the above inequality completes the proof.
∎

4.
Numerical examples

In this Section we use

\delta

\eta
in the kernel function
\omega_{\eta,\delta}.
According to the meaning of that kernel function, this is equivalent to find where a drivers located on the on-ramp should look in order to avoid the congestion on the main road. To this end, we consider one on-ramp with length
L=0.1
located from
x=1.0
until
x=1.1
, and we consider the following kernel functions

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 2\frac{\eta-x}{\eta^{2}}\chi_{[0,\eta]}(x),

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta,\delta}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta^{6}}\frac{16}{5\pi}\left(\eta^{2}-(x-\delta)^{2}\right)^{5/2}\chi_{[-\eta+\delta,\eta+\delta]}(x),

for convective and reactive terms, respectively, with
\eta=0.5
and
\delta\in[-\eta,\eta]
, for
x\in[-1,4]
at simulated time
T=6
and velocity function given by
v(\rho)=1-\rho
. We also consider
\Delta x=1/200
, a constant initial condition
\rho_{0}(x)=0.3
, and the rate of the on-ramp given by
q_{\mathrm{on}}(t)=1.2
. Following
[
5
]
, as a metric of traffic congestion we consider the two following functionals

\displaystyle J(T)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}|\partial_{x}\rho(t,\cdot)|\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\Psi(T;a,b)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(\rho(t,x))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

where

\displaystyle\varphi(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}0,&r<0.75,\\
10r-7.5,&0.75\leq r\leq 0.85,\\
1,&0.85<r\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

The functional J defined measures the integral with respect to time of the spatial total variation of the traffic density while
the functional
\Psi
measures the traffic congestion in the interval
[a,b]=[-1,4]

5.
Conclusions

In this work we proved the
\mathbf{L^{1}}-
stability of entropy weak solutions of a scalar nonlocal balance law with nonlocal source term arising in traffic modelling with on- and off-ramps introduced in
[
2
]
. We reached an estimate of the dependence of the solution with respect to the kernel function in the source term, the on-ramp rate, the off-ramp rate and the initial datum. The stability has been obtained from the entropy condition through the doubling of variables technique. Finally, following
[
5
]
, we have shown numerical simulations illustrating the dependencies above for two cost functionals derived from traffic flow applications.

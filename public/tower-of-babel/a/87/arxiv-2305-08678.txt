Tensor and direct extension of definable subcategories
Mike Prest

Abstract
Definable subcategories may be extended along a ring homomorphism directly, by using their defining conditions in the new module category, or by tensoring up with the new ring. We investigate what is preserved and reflected by these processes. On the way, we introduce the notion of being atomic with respect to a bimodule - a refinement of the Mittag-Leffler condition. Particular attention is given to the case where the ring homomorphism is an elementary embedding.



1
Introduction

A subcategory of a module category
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
is said to be definable if it is closed in
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
under direct products, directed colimits and pure submodules, equivalently if it is an additive axiomatisable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
. If
f:R\to S
is a homomorphism of rings, then there are two obvious ways of inducing a definable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
from a definable subcategory
{\cal D}
of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
:
direct extension
, which takes an axiomatisation of
{\cal D}
, transfers it along
f
and then applies this in
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
;
tensor extension
, which is the smallest definable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
which contains all modules of the form
M\otimes_{R}S_{S}
with
M\in{\cal D}
.

We consider in Sections
3
and
4
the effects of these extension processes, paying particular attention to tensor extension, indeed considering more generally the effect that tensoring with a bimodule
{}_{R}B_{S}
has on definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
.

This leads us to consider a strengthening of the relative Mittag-Leffler condition. Recall that a right
R
-module
M_{R}
is Mittag-Leffler if, for every set
\{\,_{R}L_{i}\}_{i}
of left
R
-modules, the natural map
M\otimes_{R}\prod_{i}L_{i}\to\prod_{i}\,M\otimes_{R}L_{i}
is monic. There is a relative version of the definition under which the modules
L_{i}
are restricted to those belonging to some definable subcategory of
R\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}
(the category of left
R
-modules), see Section
4.2
. If the
L_{i}
all are
(R,S)
-bimodules for some ring
S
, then that natural map, even if monic, need not, in the relativised case, be pure as an embedding of
S
-modules, see
4.21
. We characterise in
4.17
those modules
M
where tensoring with
M
does give a pure embedding of
S
-modules.

We consider various conditions under which we can say more about extending definable subcategories along a morphism of rings. In particular, in Section
5
we consider the case where
f:R\to S
is an elementary embedding of rings.

Subcategories are assumed to be full and closed under isomorphism. Rings
R
can be taken to be rings with one object but everything works with
R
a (skeletally) small preadditive category. In that more general case, elements of modules and variables in formulas are sorted which, in some arguments, would require more cumbersome notation. Therefore we present the results for the usual, 1-sorted ring, case, but the arguments work just as well over rings with many objects (see, for instance,
[
12
]
). The model theory that we use is summarised in the next section and always refers to the usual language for
R
-modules. One can see various other references for details about this, but we think that enough is said here to make the paper understandable without needing to refer elsewhere.

2.1
Pp formulas, pp-pairs and pp-types

A
pp formula
for right
R
-modules is an existentially quantified (homogeneous) system of
R
-linear equations, that is, a formula of the form

\exists\overline{y}\,(\overline{x}A+\overline{y}B=0),

where
A
and

are suitable-sized matrices with entries from
R
and tuples
\overline{x}
,
\overline{y}
of variables are read as row vectors (and
0
is a column vector). We unpack this compact notation to write the formula more explicitly as

\exists\overline{y}\,\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}r_{ij}+\sum_{k=1}^{t}y_{k}s_{kj}=0.

Here the
r_{ij}
and
s_{kj}
- the entries of
A
and
B
- are elements
1
1

1

precisely, function symbols standing for multiplication by those elements; for details of how the formal language is set up, see elsewhere
of
R
and
\bigwedge
is to
\wedge
(“and”) as
\sum
is to
+
; so this is a system of
m

R
-linear equations prefaced by a quantifier which has the effect of projecting (solution sets) along the
\overline{y}
-axis. The variables
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
are free to be substituted with values from some module. If we denote this formula by
\phi
, then we may write
\phi(\overline{x})
or
\phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
to display its free = unquantified variables.

Given such a pp formula
\phi
we have, in each module
M
, its
solution set
:

\phi(M)=\{\overline{a}\in M^{n}:\exists\overline{b}\in M^{t}\text{ such that }\overline{a}A+\overline{b}B=0\}.

We say that
\phi(M)
is a subgroup of
M^{n}

pp-definable
in
M
or, more briefly, a
pp-definable subgroup
of
M
. The notation
M\models\phi(\overline{a})
, which is more usual in model theory, is equivalent to
\overline{a}\in\phi(M)
.

If
M
is a submodule of
N
,
M\leq N
, then
M
is a
pure submodule
if, for every tuple
\overline{a}
from
M
, if
N\models\phi(\overline{a})
, then

. More generally, a

is a monomorphism whose image is pure in its codomain.

If
\psi

are formulas with the same free variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, then we write

if, for every module

\psi(M)\leq\phi(M)
, that is,
\psi(M)
is a subgroup of
\phi(M)
(pp-definable subgroups are modules over the centre of the ring

but are not, in general,
R
-submodules). Formulas which are equivalent in this preordering, for instance

\phi\wedge(\overline{x}=\overline{x})
, have the same solution set in every module and, in practice are identified, so we regard this as an ordering on (equivalence classes of) pp formulas. The resulting poset, denoted
{\rm pp}^{n}_{R}
, is a lattice, with meet given by conjunction,
\phi\wedge\psi
, and join given by
\phi+\psi
, where the latter is the pp formula
\exists\overline{x}_{1}\overline{x}_{2}\,(\phi(\overline{x}_{1})\,\wedge\,\psi(\overline{x}_{2})\,\wedge\,\overline{x}=\overline{x}_{1}+\overline{x}_{2})
. These define
\phi(M)\cap\psi(M)
, respectively
\phi(M)+\psi(M)
in any module
M
. One may note that the conjunction and sum of two pp formulas are not strictly of the form given in the definition of pp formula above but may be rewritten as equivalent formulas in that form, so we do refer to them as pp formulas.

A
pp-pair
is a pair
\psi\leq\phi
of pp-formulas, that is, where one implies the other. We notate such a pair as
\phi/\psi
because often we are interested in the factor groups
\phi(M)/\psi(M)
. We say that a pp-pair

\phi(M)=\psi(M)
; otherwise it is
open
on
M
.

The

is the set of all pp formulas that

satisfies in
M
; more generally for
n
-tuples:
2
2

2

The free variables
x_{1},\dots x_{n}
are just placeholders, so we keep them in the background in our notation whenever we can.

{\rm pp}^{M}(\overline{a})=\{\phi(\overline{x}):M\models\phi(\overline{a})\}.

We say that
\overline{a}
is a
realisation
of that pp-type
in

M
. Every set

filter

A pp-type

if there is a pp formula

; we write

and say that

Morphisms preserve pp formulas: if

{\rm pp}^{M}(\overline{a})\subseteq{\rm pp}^{N}(f\overline{a})

.

2.2
Pure-injectives and the Ziegler spectrum

A module
N
is
pure-injective
if it is injective over pure monomorphisms, equivalently if every embedding of
N
as a pure submodule is a split embedding.

The (right)
Ziegler spectrum
,
[
22
]
,
{\rm Zg}_{R}
of
R
is the topological space on the set,
{\rm pinj}_{R}
, of isomorphism classes of indecomposable pure-injective (right)
R
-modules and which has, for a basis of open sets, those of the form

(\phi/\psi)=\{N\in{\rm Zg}_{R}:\phi(N)/\psi(N)\neq 0\}.

If
{\cal T}
is any topological space then the lattice
{\cal O}({\cal T})
of open subsets of
{\cal T}
is a complete Heyting algebra (a complete lattice where meet distributes over arbitrary join), also referred to as a
frame
.

2.3
Definable subcategories of module categories

We say that
{\cal D}
is a
definable subcategory
of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
if it satisfies the following equivalent conditions, where a subcategory is
additive
if it is closed under finite direct sums and under direct summands.

Theorem 2.1
.

(see, e.g.,
[
11
, 3.4.7]
or
[
12
, 9.1]
) The following conditions on a subcategory
{\cal D}
of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
are equivalent:

(i)
{\cal D}
is additive and axiomatisable by some set of sentences in the language for
R
-modules;

(ii) there is a set
\Phi
of pp-pairs such that
{\cal D}=\{M\in{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R:\phi(M)=\psi(M)\,\,\forall\phi/\psi\in\Phi\}
;

(iii)
{\cal D}
is closed in
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
under direct products, directed colimits and pure submodules.

We say that
{\cal D}
is
defined
by
\Phi
in the situation of (ii) above.

A definable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
is also closed under images of pure epimorphisms and under pure-injective hulls (e.g.
[
11
, 3.4.8]
).

If
M
is a module then we denote by
\langle M\rangle
the definable subcategory
generated
by
M
- the smallest definable subcategory (of the ambient module category) containing
M
. Therefore
\langle M\rangle
consists of the class of modules
N
such that every pp-pair closed on
M
is closed on
N
:

\langle M\rangle=\{N\in{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R:\phi(M)=\psi(M)\implies\phi(N)=\psi(N)\,\,\forall\phi\geq\psi\text{ pp }\}.

Similar notation is used for the definable subcategory generated by a class of modules.
Every definable subcategory is generated by some, by no means unique,
M
. We say that two
R
-modules
M,N
are
definably equivalent
if they generate the same definable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
:
\langle M\rangle=\langle N\rangle
, equivalently if every pp-pair open on
M
is open on
N
and
vice versa
.

The set of definable subcategories of a module category
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
is closed under arbitrary intersection.

Lemma 2.2
.

(e.g. proof of
[
11
, 5.1.1]
) Suppose that
{\cal D}_{i}
,
i\in I
, are definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
, with
{\cal D}_{i}
being defined by
\Phi_{i}
. Then their intersection
\bigcap_{i}\,{\cal D}_{i}
is definable, being defined by
\bigcup_{i}\,\Phi_{i}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
{\cal D}_{i}
,
i\in I
, are definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
and let
\Phi_{i}
be the set of all pp-pairs closed on
{\cal D}_{i}
. Then the definable join
{\cal D}=\langle\bigcup_{i}\,{\cal D}_{i}\rangle
is defined by
\bigcap_{i}\,\Phi_{i}
.

Proof.
Certainly the category defined by
\Phi=\bigcap_{i}\,\Phi_{i}
contains each
{\cal D}_{i}
and hence contains
{\cal D}
(since that is the smallest definable subcategory which contains each
{\cal D}_{i}
). For the converse, if
{\cal D}
were strictly contained in the subcategory defined by
\Phi
, then there would be some pp-pair
\phi/\psi
not in
\Phi
but closed on
{\cal D}
. There is some
i
such that
\phi/\psi\notin\Phi_{i}
so, by definition of
\Phi_{i}
, there is some
D\in{\cal D}_{i}
such that
\phi/\psi
is open on
D
. But
D\in{\cal D}
and that is a contradiction. Therefore
\Phi
defines precisely
{\cal D}
.

\Box

Therefore the set of definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
, ordered by inclusion, is a complete lattice which we denote by
{\rm DefSub}_{R}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

(e.g.
[
11
, 5.1.1, 5.1.5]
) The lattice

of definable subcategories of

The first correspondence is

and, in the other direction, it takes a set of indecomposable pure-injective modules to the definable subcategory that they together generate. We write

for the set

given the relative topology induced from

The join of finitely many definable subcategories is described algebraically as follows.

Lemma 2.5

Suppose that

Lemma 2.8
.

If

2.4
Elementary duality

The (
elementary
)
dual
(
[
7
]
) of a pp formula
\phi

\exists\overline{y}\,(\overline{x}A+\overline{y}B=0)

for right modules is the pp formula
D\phi
for left modules which is

\exists\overline{z}\,(\overline{x}=A\overline{z}\,\wedge\,B\overline{z}=0).

For example, the dual of the annihilator formula
xr=0
for right
R
-modules is (to equivalence) the divisibility formula
r|x
, meaning
\exists y\,(x=ry)
, for left
R
-modules, and
vice versa
.

Theorem 2.9
.

(see
[
11
, §1.3.1]
) For each

, elementary duality
D(-)
of pp formulas defines an anti-isomorphism
{\rm pp}^{n}_{R}\simeq{\rm pp}^{n}_{R^{\rm op}}
.

In particular
D(\psi\wedge\phi)=D\psi+D\phi
,
D(\psi+\phi)=D\psi\,\wedge\,D\phi
and
D^{2}\phi=\phi
(where

is also used to denote elementary duality from left to right formulas).

If

is a definable subcategory of

, determined by closure of some set

)

]

is the same for any choice of

. We have, e.g.

If

(

2.5
Ultrapowers

If
I
is a set then an
ultrafilter
on
I
is a collection
{\cal U}
of subsets of
I
which is a filter in the lattice of subsets of
I
and which is such that, for every
J\subseteq I
, either
J\in{\cal U}
or
I\setminus J\in{\cal U}
.

Given a structure
X
- a ring, a module over a ring, … (precisely, a structure for some first order finitary language) - we can define the corresponding
ultrapower

X^{I}/{\cal U}
of
X
to be the structure whose underlying set is
X^{I}/\sim_{\cal U}
where
\sim_{\cal U}
is the equivalence relation on
X^{I}
given by
(a_{i})_{i}\sim_{\cal U}(b_{i})_{i}
iff
\{i\in I:a_{i}=b_{i}\}\in{\cal U}
. We denote the equivalence class of
(a_{i})_{i}
by
(a_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}
. The structure on
X^{I}/{\cal U}
is defined pointwise; let’s be more precise supposing that
X
is a ring. First though, we remark that the idea is that sets in
{\cal U}
are “large” so
\sim_{\cal U}
identifies elements of the product which are equal on a “large” set of coordinates.

If
X
is a ring, then we define addition on the ultrapower
X^{I}/{\cal U}
by
(a_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}+(b_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}=(a_{i}+b_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}
and similarly for multiplication. The identity and zero are
(1_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}
and
(0_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}
respectively. It follows easily from the definition of ultrafilter that the subset
Z\subseteq X^{I}
of elements which are
\sim_{\cal U}
-equivalent to
0
is a two-sided ideal of the ring
X^{I}
and that, as a set and as a ring,
X^{I}/Z
is exactly the factor ring
X^{I}/{\cal U}
.

Similarly for modules: if
M
is a right
R
-module, then
M^{I}/{\cal U}
is a right
R
-module with
(a_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}\cdot r
defined to be
(a_{i}r)_{i}/{\cal U}
. In fact, defining
(a_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}\cdot(r_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}=(a_{i}r_{i})_{i}/{\cal U}
makes
M^{I}/{\cal U}
a right
R^{I}/{\cal U}
-module with the
R
-action on
M^{I}/{\cal U}
arising
via
the diagonal embedding (see
5.1
)
R\to R^{I}/{\cal U}
.

3
Direct extension of definable subcategories

Suppose that
f:R\to S
is a morphism of rings. If
\phi
is a pp formula for
R
-modules, then we denote by
f_{\ast}\phi
the pp formula for
S
-modules obtained by replacing each ring element
r
appearing in
\phi
, by
fr
. Note that
f_{\ast}(\phi\wedge\psi)=f_{\ast}\phi\wedge f_{\ast}\psi
and
f_{\ast}(\phi+\psi)=f_{\ast}\phi+f_{\ast}\psi
, so this is a lattice homomorphism from
{\rm pp}^{n}_{R}
to
{\rm pp}^{n}_{S}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
11
, 3.2.7]
If

Corollary 3.2

So, in practice, particularly if

Let

Lemma 3.5

If

Proof.
If

and

For the converse, if

\Box

Example 3.6
.

If

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that

is a definable subcategory of

. Then

The first statement is immediate by

for

\Box

We give an algebraic description of

in

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
R\to S
is a ring homomorphism and

is a definable subcategory of

. If

is an elementary cogenerator for

is an elementary cogenerator for

Proof.
First we show that

is an elementary cogenerator for the definable category

that it generates.

Since

is pure-injective as an

-module it is pure-injective as an

-module (e.g.
[

, 4.3.7]

[

, 3.8]
, it is enough to show that every ultrapower of

purely embeds in a power of
N_{R}
. Consider an ultrapower

where
{\cal U}
is an ultrafilter on a set

. We can form the corresponding ultrapower

of the
S
-module

. Since

is an elementary cogenerator, there is an
S
-pure embedding
N^{I}/{\cal U}\to N^{\kappa}
for some

. Note that

is exactly
(N_{R})^{I}/{\cal U}
(form the ultrapower, then apply the module restriction functor
f^{\ast}
or apply

, then form the ultrapower; these are the same since the restriction functor has both a left and right adjoint so commutes with powers and directed colimits, hence (e.g.
[

Just for interest, we note a different, more model-theoretic, proof which uses a different characterisation of elementary cogenerators - as pure-injectives which realise every neg-isolated type (see
[

). So we have to show that if

-modules, which we can extend to a set

from

So

is an elementary cogenerator for

purely embeds in some power

\Box

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose that

{\cal C}|_{R}=\{M^{\prime}\in{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R:M^{\prime}\text{ is a pure submodule of }M_{R}\text{ for some }M\in{\cal C}\}.

That is, to obtain

under (

-)pure submodules.

Given a definable subcategory

of

Directly from the definitions we have the following.

Suppose that

is a ring homomorphism and that

is a definable subcategory of

A definable subcategory of

of the form

(from (

for some definable subcategory of

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose that
R\to S
is a ring homomorphism. The map
(-)^{S}:{\cal D}\mapsto{\cal D}^{S}
is a morphism from the lattice
{\rm DefSub}_{R}
of definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
to
{\rm DefSub}_{S}
.

The lattice of definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
directly induced from

is a sublattice of
{\rm DefSub}_{S}
. It is a complete lattice and its inclusion into
{\rm DefSub}_{S}
preserves infinite meets.

The map,
(-)^{S}:{\cal D}\mapsto{\cal D}^{S}
, restricted to the definable subcategories of
{\rm DefTr}^{S}_{R}
and corestricted to the directly induced (from

) definable subcategories of

is a lattice isomorphism.

Proof.
If
({\cal D}_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
are definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
, then their intersection
{\cal D}
is definable (
2.2
) and hence is the meet of these in the lattice of definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
. We have
M\in\bigcap_{\lambda}\,({\cal D}_{\lambda})^{S}
iff
M_{R}\in{\cal D}_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda
, iff
M_{R}\in{\cal D}
iff
M\in{\cal D}^{S}=\big(\bigcap_{\lambda}\,{\cal D}_{\lambda})^{S}
, so
(-)^{S}
commutes with arbitrary intersections.

Take two directly induced definable subcategories of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
, by
3.10
without loss of generality of the form
({\cal D}_{1})^{S}
and
({\cal D}_{2})^{S}
for some definable subcategories
{\cal D}_{1}
,
{\cal D}_{2}
contained in
{\rm DefTr}^{S}_{R}
. Let
N_{i}
be an elementary cogenerator for
({\cal D}_{i})^{S}
. Since, by
3.10
, we have
({\cal D}_{i})^{S}|_{R}={\cal D}_{i}
, it follows by

that

is an elementary cogenerator for

so, by

, also

is an elementary cogenerator for the join

. Since

is an elementary cogenerator for

, we see that

preserves finite joins.

If
{\cal D}_{1}\subseteq{\cal D}_{2}\subseteq{\rm DefTr}^{S}_{R}
have the same image under
(-)^{S}
and if
M_{2}\in{\cal D}_{2}
, then, since
{\cal D}_{2}\subseteq{\rm DefTr}^{S}_{R}
, there is, by
3.9

M_{S}\in({\cal D}_{2})^{S}
with

pure in

. By assumption

so

, showing that

.

\Box

Example 3.12
.

Take

,

It follows from

and

that direct extension is an embedding at the level of topology, that is, a frame embedding, from the Ziegler spectrum of

.

Corollary 3.14
.

If

is a morphism of rings, then direct extension of definable subcategories gives a morphism of frames

Extension along ring epimorphisms

We recall that if
f:R\to S
is an epimorphism of rings, then the situation is very straightforward. Indeed, the action of each element of
S
is pp-definable in terms of the actions of elements of

, so the model theory of
S
-modules is contained in that of
R
-modules, see
[
11
, 6.1.9]
. For instance, every (pp) formula for

-module, to a (pp) formula for

-modules. Indeed, restriction along

is a full and faithful embedding of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
into

and, see
[

, 5.5.4]
, the image of that embedding is a definable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
and so
{\cal D}^{S}={\cal D}\cap{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
for every definable subcategory
{\cal D}
of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
.

4
Tensor extension of definable subcategories

Suppose that
f:R\to S
is a ring homomorphism. Given a definable subcategory
{\cal D}
of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
, we may consider its

to
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
:

{\cal D}\otimes_{R}S=\langle\{M\otimes_{R}S:M\in{\cal D}\}\rangle

- the definable subcategory of

generated by what is obtained by applying
-\otimes_{R}S
to

.

Example 4.1
.

The class
\{M\otimes_{R}S:M\in{\cal D}\}
appearing above might not be a definable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}S
. Take

to be, for instance, the diagonal map
K\to K\times K
where

is a field, and take
{\cal D}
to be
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}K
. Then the modules in

all will be even-dimensional so will not include, for instance, the

-module

, which clearly is a direct summand of
(K,K)=K\otimes_{K}(K\times K)
.

Example 4.2
.

Closing the class
\{M\otimes_{R}S:M\in{\cal D}\}
under pure submodules is, in general, not enough to give a definable subcategory: take
R\to S
to be

and
{\cal D}
to be
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}K
. Then

is the category of free
K[X]
-modules which is closed under neither direct products nor directed colimits (by coherence of
K[X]
, its definable closure is the category of flat
K[X]
-modules, see e.g.
[
11
, 3.4.24]
).

This example also shows that if
N
is an elementary cogenerator for
{\cal D}
then

need not be an elementary cogenerator for

. For, in this example,

is not even pure-injective and, even if we replace the ring

by, say, its completion

at the ideal generated by

, that module, though pure-injective, is not an elementary cogenerator for the flat
\overline{K[X]_{(X)}}
-modules (the ring of quotients of
\overline{K[X]_{(X)}}
doesn’t embed as a module in any power of
\overline{K[X]_{(X)}}
).

Tensoring up with
S
preserves neither infinite join nor finite meet of definable categories.

Example 4.3
.

For join, take
f:R\to S
to be the inclusion

and let

be the definable subcategory generated by

. On the other hand, the torsionfree module

belongs to

and so

.

For meet, take

to be the surjection of rings

.

Depending on the type of morphism

and the properties of the (bi)module

, there may be little relation between

and

or between, say,

, whereas

.

4.1

First, we state Herzog’s characterisation in terms of pp formulas of when a tensor product is

Theorem 4.4

(Herzog’s Criterion

Lemma 4.16

If

is atomic with respect to every bimodule
{}_{R}L_{S}
in the definable subcategory of

generated by

.

More precisely, for each pp formula
\sigma
for right
S
-modules, and for each partitioned tuple
\overline{a}
from

for every

Proof.
Given a pp formula
\sigma
for right

from

be chosen for

\Box

Theorem 4.17
.

Suppose that
M_{R}
is a right
R
-module and that
{}_{R}B_{S}
is an
(R,S)
-bimodule. Then
M_{R}

Proof.
The proof is just as for the case where
\sigma

.

(
\Rightarrow
) Just to simplify notation, we recall that, to check purity, it is enough to consider only pp formulas
\sigma

[

, 2.1.6]
. We have already noted that the hypothesis implies that

\overline{a}

\prod_{\lambda}\,M\otimes_{R}L_{\lambda}\models\sigma((\overline{a}\otimes\overline{l}_{\lambda})_{\lambda})
and so

for each

\lambda

such that

. We need a uniform choice of

for each

and hence, by

(
\Leftarrow

\sigma
for right
S
-modules and a partitioned tuple
\overline{a}
from

\overline{l}^{\prime}
. Then, by

and hence, by

\phi\in{\rm pp}^{M}(\overline{a})
such that

. Therefore, looking at each coordinate, we see

for every

\Box

If the bimodule

It was already noted (parenthetically) in the definition at the start of this section that, by

Given an

Corollary 4.20

If

That follows from

Remark 4.21
.

In view of
4.20
, we should show that

being atomic with respect to a bimodule

is genuinely stronger than requiring that it be atomic with respect to the left module
{}_{R}L

and

is atomic with respect to

where

is a finitely presented left

-module.

To get examples, consider
{}_{R}L_{R}=\,_{R}R_{R}
. By
[
1
, 2.7]
,

is atomic with respect to
{}_{R}R
iff it is atomic with respect to all flat left
R
-modules iff for every finitely generated submodule

of

, the inclusion of

into

factors through a finitely presented module. On the other hand, if

is atomic with respect to every module of the form

with

finitely presented, then

is, by

, atomic with respect to

So, if

is right noetherian, then every module

but, unless

is right pure semisimple, not every such module is atomic with respect to

The conclusion of the next result is similar to that of

but the hypothesis is rather different.

Proposition 4.22
.

Suppose that
{}_{R}B
is a pure-projective left
R
-module with endomorphism ring
S
. Then for any set
(M_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
of right
R
-modules, the canonical map
f:(\prod_{\lambda}\,M_{\lambda})\otimes_{R}B_{S}\to\prod_{\lambda}\,(M_{\lambda}\otimes_{R}B_{S})
is a pure embedding of right
S
-modules.

Proof.
The map is an embedding because
{}_{R}B
is Mittag-Leffler by
4.12
. To check
S
-purity it is sufficient to show that, for every finitely presented left
S
-module

, the map

is monic. As seen in the proof of

, since
{}_{S}L
is finitely presented
\big(\prod_{\lambda}\,(M_{\lambda}\otimes_{R}B_{S})\big)\otimes_{S}L\simeq\prod_{\lambda}\,(M_{\lambda}\otimes_{R}B\otimes_{S}L)
so, to show that

is monic, it is sufficient to show that

is Mittag-Leffler.

Suppose first that
B
is a direct sum of finitely presented modules. Take a finite presentation

of

, so we have an exact sequence
{}_{R}B\otimes_{S}S^{m}\to\,_{R}B\otimes_{S}S^{n}\to\,_{R}B\otimes_{S}L\to 0
. Take any finite tuple
\overline{a}
from

. Since
B
is a direct sum of finitely presented modules, there is a finitely presented direct summand

of
B
such that
\overline{a}
is contained in

which, since
\otimes
commutes with direct sums, is a direct summand of

; therefore the pp-type of
\overline{a}
in

is equal to the pp-type of
\overline{a}
in

is a finitely presented left

in

,

is a Mittag-Leffler left

For the general case,
{}_{R}B
is a direct summand of a direct sum of finitely presented left

\Box

is from

\alpha\big((fr)_{j}/{\cal V}\big)=(r)_{i}/{\cal U}

are the constant sequences

, respectively
r
.

Let

There are various conditions under which

generate the same definable subcategory and hence are definably equivalent. For instance, we have the following from

Corollary 5.15
.

(
[
9
, Prop. 21]
) Suppose that
R\prec S
and that
M
is a right
R
-module. Assume either that
M_{R}
is Mittag-Leffler or that the bimodule
{}_{R}R_{R}
has the ascending chain condition on pp-definable subgroups. Then
M
and
M\otimes_{R}S_{R}
are definably equivalent

Proof.
We recall the proofs. In view of
5.9
we have to show that
M\otimes_{R}S_{R}
is in the definable subcategory generated by
M_{R}
. So suppose that
\sigma/\tau
is a pp-pair open on
M\otimes_{R}S_{R}
, say
\overline{a}\otimes\overline{s}
is in
\sigma(M\otimes_{R}S_{R})
but not in
\tau(M\otimes_{R}S_{R})
.

First suppose that
M_{R}
is Mittag-Leffler. Then there is a pp formula
\phi
such that
{\rm pp}^{M}(\overline{a})
is generated by
\phi
and hence, by
4.7
(2), such that
{}_{R}S_{R}\models(\sigma:\phi)(\overline{s})
and also such that
\overline{s}\notin(\tau:\phi)(_{R}S_{R})
. Then
{}_{R}S_{R}
satisfies the sentence (in the language of
(R,R)
-bimodules, and where “
\neg
” means “not”)

\exists\overline{y}\,\big((\sigma:\phi)(\overline{y})\wedge\neg(\tau:\phi)(\overline{y})\big).

Since
R
is an elementary subring of
S
, it also satisfies this sentence, so choose
\overline{r}
from
R
in
(\sigma:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})\setminus(\tau:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})
. So we have
M\simeq M\otimes_{R}R_{R}\models\sigma(\overline{a}\otimes\overline{r})
and also, by choice of
\phi
and
4.7
(2),
\overline{a}\otimes\overline{r}
is not in
\tau(M)
. That is,
\sigma/\tau
is open on
M
, as required.

In the second case,
M
is arbitrary but we have the acc on pp-definable subgroups of
{}_{R}R_{R}
. Consider the set of pp-definable subgroups of
{}_{R}R_{R}
of the form
(\tau:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})
with
\overline{a}\in\phi(M)
. Since the set of such
\phi
is closed under finite
\wedge
and since (from the definitions) we have
(\tau:\phi\wedge\phi^{\prime})\geq(\tau:\phi)+(\tau:\phi^{\prime})
, the set of those subgroups
(\tau:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})
with
\phi\in{\rm pp}^{M}(\overline{a})
is upwards-directed so, by assumption, has a maximal element
(\tau:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})
say. Since
{}_{R}S_{R}\models\sigma(\overline{a}\otimes\overline{s})
, there is
\phi^{\prime}\in{\rm pp}^{M}(\overline{a})
with
{}_{R}S_{R}\models(\tau:\phi)(\overline{s})
. Replacing each of
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
by
\phi\wedge\phi^{\prime}
, we may assume that
\phi=\phi^{\prime}
. As before, we therefore obtain
\overline{r}\in(\sigma:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})\setminus(\tau:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})
and so have
M\models\sigma(\overline{a}\cdot\overline{r})
(by
\overline{a}\cdot\overline{r}
we mean
\sum_{i}\,a_{i}r_{i}
). If we had
M=M\otimes_{R}R_{R}\models\tau(\overline{a}\otimes\overline{r})
, then there would be
\psi\in{\rm pp}^{M}(\overline{a})
with
\overline{r}\in(\tau:\psi)(_{R}R_{R})
; yet
\overline{r}\notin(\tau:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})
, contradicting maximality of
(\tau:\phi)(_{R}R_{R})
. Therefore
\overline{a}\cdot\overline{r}\notin\tau(M)
and so
\sigma/\tau
is open on
M
, as required.

The second hypothesis is satisfied if
R
is finitely generated as a module over a noetherian centre (since every pp-definable subgroup is a module over the centre of
R
). We have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.16
.

Suppose that
{}_{R}R_{R}
has the ascending chain condition on pp-definable subgroups and that
R\prec S
. Let
{\cal D}
be a definable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
. Then
({\cal D}\otimes_{R}S)|_{R}={\cal D}
, so
{\cal D}\otimes_{R}S\subseteq{\cal D}^{S}
.

Corollary 5.17
.

Suppose that
R
is a finite-dimensional algebra over a field and that
R\prec S
. Let
{\cal D}
be a definable subcategory of
{\rm Mod}\mbox{-}R
. Then
({\cal D}\otimes_{R}S)|_{R}={\cal D}
, so
{\cal D}\otimes_{R}S\subseteq{\cal D}^{S}
.

Proof.
If
S
is a finite-dimensional algebra, in particular if
S
is an ultrapower of
R
then, see
5.2
, this follows from
5.16
. In general
S
is just an elementary subring of an ultrapower
R^{\ast}=R^{I}/{\cal U}
of
R
. If, for such a ring
S
, there were

-bimodule pp formulas
\phi_{0}(\overline{x}),\phi_{1}(\overline{x}),\dots,\phi_{n}(\overline{x}),\dots
such that
\phi_{0}(_{S}S_{S})<\phi_{1}(_{S}S_{S})<\dots<\phi_{n}(_{S}S_{S})<\dots
were an infinite ascending chain then, for each

, we would have that

satisfies the sentence, from the proof of
5.8
, in the language of

-bimodules which says that

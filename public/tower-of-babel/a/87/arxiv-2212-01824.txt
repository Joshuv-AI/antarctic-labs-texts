A flow approach to the Orlicz-Minkowski problem for torsional rigidity Thanks: The authors were supported by NSFC, grant nos. 11971424 and 12031017.
Weimin Sheng, Ke Xue

Abstract
. In this paper the Orlicz-Minkowski problem for torsional rigidity, a generalization of the classical Minkowski problem, is studied. Using the flow method, we obtain a new existence result of solutions to this problem for general measures.

. Recall that the torsional rigidity of a convex body in

is described by Colesanti
[

(1.1)

\frac{1}{T(\Omega)}=\inf\Big\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\overline{\nabla}U|^{2}dX}{(\int_{\Omega}|U|dX)^{2}},U\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|U|dX>0\Big\},

where
\overline{\nabla}
is the gradient in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It has been shown in
[
18
]
that, there exists a unique function
U
such that

(1.2)

T(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}|\overline{\nabla}U|^{2}dX,

where
U
satisfies the boundary-value problem

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta U(X)=-2,X\in\mathring{\Omega},\\
\\
U(X)=0,\quad X\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

for a convex body
\Omega
we denote by
\mathring{\Omega}
its interior and
\Delta
the Lapalacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Colesanti and Fimiani in
[
18
]
built up the following variational formula of the torsional rigidity, for two arbitrary convex bodies
\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.4)

\frac{d}{dt}T(\Omega_{0}+t\Omega_{1})|_{t=0^{+}}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}h_{\Omega_{1}}(\nu_{\Omega_{0}}(X))|\overline{\nabla}U(X)|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(X)

where
h_{\Omega_{1}}
is the support function of
\Omega_{1}
,
\nu_{\Omega_{0}}
is the Gauss mappign of
\partial\Omega_{0}
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. By (
1.4
), the torsional measure
\mu_{tor}(\Omega,\cdot)
is defined on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
by

(1.5)

\mu_{tor}(\Omega,\eta)=\int_{\nu^{-1}_{\Omega}(\eta)}|\overline{\nabla}U(X)|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(X)=\int_{\eta}|\overline{\nabla}U(\nu^{-1}_{\Omega}(x))|^{2}dS_{\Omega}(x),

for every Borel subset
\eta
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Here
S_{\Omega}
is the surface area measure of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\nu^{-1}_{\Omega}(x)
is
the inverse of the Gauss map on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Furthermore, Dahlberg
[
21
]
revealed that
\overline{\nabla}U

has finite non-tangential limit

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
a.e. on
\partial\Omega

and
|\overline{\nabla}U|\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
without
smoothness assumption on
\Omega
. Hence, (
1.5
) is well-defined on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Colesanti and Fimiani
[
18
]
first proposed the Minkowski problem for torsional rigidity:

Given a finite Borel measure
\mu
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
under what the necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
,
does there exist a
convex body
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mu=\mu_{tor}(\Omega,\cdot)
?
They proved the existence and uniqueness up to translations of the solution
via a variational argument which was first proposed by Aleksandrov
[
1
,
2
]
.

The
L_{p}
version of Minkowski problem for torsional rigidity has
been studied, see for instance, Chen-Dai
[
14
]
for
p>1
and Hu-Liu
[
27
]
for
0<p<1
.
Recently, the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for torsional rigidity was extended to the
Orlicz case involving non-homogeneous terms by Li-Zhu
[
36
]
, which asks: given a
finite Borel measure
\mu
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, what are the necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
such that there exists a convex body
\Omega
with support function
h

so that

(1.6)

hd\mu_{tor}(\Omega,\cdot)=\tau\,\psi(h)d\mu.

where
\psi\in\mathcal{A}
, the class of continuous functions
\psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

(i)

\Psi(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}dt
exists for all
s>0
;

(ii)

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\Psi(s)=\infty
.

Hu, Liu and Ma
[
28
]
studied the existence of the Orlicz-Minkowski problem (
1.6
) for even data. They proved the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
28
]
Suppose
\psi\in\mathcal{A}
. If
\mu
is an even finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
which is not concentrated on any closed hemisphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then there exists an origin symmetric convex body
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}_{e}

such that (
1.6
) holds.

Here
\mathcal{K}_{e}
denotes the set of origin-symmetric convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Li and Zhu
[
36
]
studied the general (not necessarily even) Orlicz-Minkowski problem and obtained the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
36
]
Suppose
\psi\in\mathcal{A}
, and
s/\psi(s)
tends to
+\infty
as
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
. If
\mu
is a finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
which is not concentrated on any closed hemisphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then there exists a convex body
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}_{0}

such that (
1.6
) holds.

\mathcal{K}_{0}
denotes the set of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the origin in their interior.
When
\psi(s)=s^{p}
, we note that Theorem
1.1
includes the even
L_{p}
torsional-Minkowski problem for
p>0
, and Theorem
1.2
includes the general
L_{p}
torsional-Minkowski problem for
p>1
. There is no result about the general Orlicz-Minkowski problem which can include the general
L_{p}
torsional-Minkowski problem for
0<p<1
. Inspired by
[
37
]
, in this paper, we shall fill this gap. In
[
28
,
36
]
, the authors considered the convex body
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
,
but the weak solution to (
1.6
) may vanish somewhere, so the
associated convex body may contain the origin on its boundary.
Hence our primary goal is to extend the Orlicz-Minkowski problem to
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}
. We consider to study the extended set of
\psi
in the following ways.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be the class of continuous functions
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

(i)

\psi(s)\in\mathcal{A}
;

(ii)

\psi(0)=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\psi(s)=0
.

We get the following theorem:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
\psi\in\mathcal{B}
. If
\mu
is a finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
which is not concentrated on any closed hemisphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then there exists a convex body
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}
such that (
1.6
) holds.

We would like to mention that Theorem
1.3
holds for
\psi=s^{p}
with
p>0
. The proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on the study of a suitably designed parabolic flow and the use of approximation argument. The idea of using the parabolic flow comes from the fact that Problem (
1.6
) can be rewritten as a Monge-Ampère type equation on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Assume the pregiven measure
\mu
has a density
f
with respect to
\,dx
, (
1.6
) reduces to solve the following Monge-Ampère type equation on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ h|\overline{\nabla}U(hx+\nabla h)|^{2}\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)=\gamma f(x){\psi(h)},

where
\nabla
and
\nabla^{2}
are the gradient and Hessian operators with respect to an orthonormal frame on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
\gamma>0
is a constant,
I
is the identity matrix.

Let
f:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a smooth positive function, and
\Omega_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
be a convex body such that
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\partial\Omega_{0}
is a smooth and uniformly convex hypersurface. We consider the following curvature flow,

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial{X}}{\partial{t}}(x,t)&=\left(-f(\nu){\psi}(h)|\overline{\nabla}U(hx+\nabla h,t)|^{-2}K+\eta(t)h\right)\nu,\\
\\
X(x,0)&=X_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
X(\cdot,t):\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the embedding that parameterizes a family of convex hypersurfaces
\mathcal{M}_{t}
(in particular,
X_{0}
is the parametrization of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
),

\Omega_{t}
is the convex body inclosed by
\mathcal{M}_{t}
,
U(\cdot,t)
is the solution of (
1.3
) in
\Omega_{t}
,
K
denotes the Gauss curvature of
\Omega_{t}
at
X(x,t)
,
\nu
denotes the unit outer normal of
\Omega_{t}
at
X(x,t)
,
h
is the support function of
\Omega_{t}
, and

(1.9)

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f{\psi}(h)dx}{(n+2)T(\Omega_{t})}.

Assume the functions
f
and
\psi
are smooth enough. We show that, under the condition that

(1.10)

\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{s^{n}}{\psi(s)}=\infty,

the flow (
1.8
) deforms a smooth and uniformly convex hypersurface to a limit hypersurface satisfying (
1.7
). The key ingredient for such convergence is to establish a priori estimates; the
C^{0}
estimate in this case can be obtained by the maximum principle. However, when the condition (
1.10
) does not hold, i.e.

(1.11)

\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{s^{n}}{\psi(s)}<\infty,

the
C^{0}
estimate cannot be obtained by the maximum principle directly. In order to overcome this obstruction, we study a more careful designed flow replacing (
1.8
) with the function
\psi\in\mathcal{B}
replaced by a smooth function
\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined as follows:

(1.12)

\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi(s),&\textrm{if $s\geq 2\varepsilon$},\\
\\
s^{n+\varepsilon},&\textrm{if $0\leq s\leq\varepsilon$},\end{array}\right.

and
\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(s)\leq C_{0}
for
s\in(\varepsilon,2\varepsilon)
is chosen so that
\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}
is smooth on
[0,\infty)
and
\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(s)>0
for all
s\in(0,\infty)
, where
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
is a small number. Hereafter,

(1.13)

\displaystyle C_{0}=\max\{1,\max_{s\in[0,2]}\psi(s)\}.

In fact, we study the following curvature flow:

(1.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial{X_{\varepsilon}}}{\partial{t}}(x,t)&=\left(-f(\nu)\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(h_{\varepsilon})|\overline{\nabla}U_{\varepsilon}(h_{\varepsilon}x+\nabla h_{\varepsilon},t)|^{-2}K+\eta_{\varepsilon}(t)h_{\varepsilon}\right)\nu,\\
\\
X_{\varepsilon}(x,0)&=X_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
X_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t):\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
parameterizes convex hypersurface

\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
,

h_{\varepsilon}
denotes the support function of the convex body
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{t}

inclosed by
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
,
U_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)
is the solution of (
1.3
) in
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{t}
and

(1.15)

\displaystyle\eta_{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(h)dx}{(n+2)T(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{t})}.

We will show that
h_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)
is uniformly bounded from above in Lemma
3.1
. A positive uniform lower bound estimate for
h_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)
will be proved in Lemma
3.4
, and this argument relies on the construction for
\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.12
). By the
C^{0}
estimates we further obtain the higher order estimates and therefore show that flow (
1.14
) exists for all time. These, together with Lemma
2.8
, imply the existence of a sequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
such that
h_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t_{i})
converges to a positive and uniformly convex function
h_{\varepsilon,\infty}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
solving the equation below

(1.16)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ h|\overline{\nabla}U_{\varepsilon}(hx+\nabla h)|^{2}\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)=\gamma_{\varepsilon}f(x){\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(h)},

where
\gamma_{\varepsilon}>0
is a constant and
U_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
is the solution of (
1.3
) in
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\infty}
. Furthermore, we prove that there is a sequence of
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
such that
h_{\varepsilon_{i},\infty}
locally uniformly converges to a weak solution of (
1.7
). Throughout this paper, we say that
h\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, the set of function on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
with continuous second order derivative, is uniformly convex if the matrix
\nabla^{2}h+hI
is positively definite. Thus, the following theorem can be obtained, which provides solutions to (
1.7
)
when
d\mu=fd\sigma_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{B}
be a smooth function. Suppose that
\,d\mu(x)=f(x)\,dx
with smooth and positive function
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
The following statements hold:

(\mathrm{i})
If
\psi
satisfies (
1.10
), then one can find an
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
such that (
1.7
);

(\mathrm{ii})
If
\psi
does not satisfy (
1.10
), i.e. it satisfies (
1.11
), then one can find an
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}
such that (
1.6
).

For any general measure
\mu
, there is a sequence of measures
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
where
\,d\mu_{i}=f_{i}d\sigma_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
with
f_{i}
being smooth and strictly positive on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
such that
\mu_{i}
converges to
\mu
weakly.
Theorem
1.3
is then proved by the virtue of Theorem
1.4
and an approximation argument.

Next, we consider the Orlicz-Minkowski problem for torsional rigidity that includes the
L_{p}
torsional Minkowski problem for
p=0
, which is called the logarithmic torsional Minkowski problem.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the class of continuous functions
\psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

(i)

\Psi(s)=\int_{1}^{s}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}dt
exists for all
s>0
;

(ii)

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\Psi(s)=\infty
;

(iii)

For some
\widehat{C_{1}}>0
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Psi(|u\cdot x|)f(x)dx\geq-\widehat{C_{1}},\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

We can get the following theorem:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{C}
be a smooth function. Suppose that
\,d\mu(x)=f(x)\,dx
with
f
being even, smooth and strictly positive on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then there is a uniformly convex, even, smooth and positive solution to (
1.7
).

When
\psi(s)=1
, the Orlicz-Minkowski problem for torsional rigidity becomes the logarithmic torsional Minkowski problem, we claim that
\psi(s)=1\in\mathcal{C}
. Only need to show (iii) is true, to see this, we know
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log|x_{i}|du>-\infty
, where
(x_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,x_{n})
denotes the Euclidean coordinates.
The proof of the Theorem
1.5
relies on the flow (
1.8
). Eveness and
\psi\in\mathcal{C}
guarantee the
C^{0}
estimate.

2.
Preliminary and properties of the flows

2.1.
Convex Bodies

We say
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body if it is a compact convex set with nonempty interior.
Denote by
\mathcal{K}
the set of all convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing the origin.
Let
\mathcal{K}_{0}\subset\mathcal{K}
be the set of all convex bodies with the origin in their interiors,
\mathcal{K}_{e}
is the class of origin-symmetric convex bodies. For
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}
, define its radial function
r_{\Omega}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
and support function
h_{\Omega}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, respectively, by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ r_{\Omega}(x)=\max\{a\in\mathbb{R}:ax\in\Omega\}\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ h_{\Omega}(x)=\max\{x\cdot y,\,y\in\Omega\},\,\ \,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

where
x\cdot y
denotes the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}
, let
\partial\Omega
be its boundary. The Gauss map of
\partial\Omega
, denoted by
\nu_{\Omega}:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, is defined as follows: for
y\in\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle\nu_{\Omega}(y)=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:x\cdot y=h_{\Omega}(x)\}.

Let
\nu_{\Omega}^{-1}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\partial\Omega
be the reverse Gauss map such that

\displaystyle\nu_{\Omega}^{-1}(x)=\{y\in\partial\Omega:x\cdot y=h_{\Omega}(x)\},\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Denote by
\alpha_{\Omega}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
the radial Gauss image of
\Omega
. That is,

\alpha_{\Omega}(\xi)=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:x\in\nu_{\Omega}(r_{\Omega}(\xi)\xi)\},\ \ \xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Define
\alpha^{*}_{\Omega}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the reverse radial Gauss image of
\Omega
as follows: for any Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

(2.2)

\alpha^{*}_{\Omega}(E)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:r_{\Omega}(\xi)\xi\in\nu_{\Omega}^{-1}(E)\}.

We often omit the subscript
\Omega
in
r_{\Omega}
,
h_{\Omega}
,
\nu_{\Omega}
,
\nu_{\Omega}^{-1}
,
\alpha_{\Omega}
, and
\alpha_{\Omega}^{*}
if no confusion occurs.

Let us recall some basic notations. In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, sometimes, we also use
\langle x,y\rangle=x\cdot y
to denote the inner product of
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
|x|
means the Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
will be assumed to have a smooth local orthonormal frame field
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}
. By
\nabla
and
\nabla^{2}
, the gradient and Hessian operators with respect to
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. The surface area of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is denoted by
|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}
be a convex body containing the origin. Denote by
w_{\Omega}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
the width function of
\Omega
which can be formulated by

w_{\Omega}(x)=h_{\Omega}(x)+h_{\Omega}(-x).

We shall need
w_{\Omega}^{+}
and
w_{\Omega}^{-}
, the maximal and the minimal width of
\Omega
, respectively, which can be formulated by

(2.3)

\displaystyle w_{\Omega}^{+}=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\{h_{\Omega}(x)+h_{\Omega}(-x)\}\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ w_{\Omega}^{-}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\{h_{\Omega}(x)+h_{\Omega}(-x)\}.

The following result can be found in
[
11
]
for instance.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
be a convex body containing the origin in its interior,
h_{\Omega}
and
r_{\Omega}
be the support and radial functions of
\Omega
, and
x_{max}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\xi_{min}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be such that
h_{\Omega}(x_{max})=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{\Omega}(x)
and
r_{\Omega}(\xi_{min})=\min_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}r_{\Omega}(\xi)
. Then

\displaystyle\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{\Omega}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}r_{\Omega}(\xi)\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \min_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{\Omega}(x)=\min_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}r_{\Omega}(\xi),

\displaystyle h_{\Omega}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\langle x,x_{max}\rangle h_{\Omega}(x_{max})\ \ \ \mathrm{for\ all}\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

Lemma 4.2

Let

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

be a sequence of positive and smooth functions on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that the measures

converge to

weakly as

. By Theorem
1.4
, there are
\gamma_{j}>0
and
\Omega_{j}\in\mathcal{K}_{V}

such that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}\int_{\omega}\psi(h_{\Omega_{j}})\,d\mu_{j}(x)=\int_{\omega}\,\,h_{\Omega_{j}}\,d\mu_{tor}(\Omega_{j},x),

for any Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{j}\Psi(h_{\Omega_{j}})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{j}\Psi(h_{\mathbb{K}})dx:\mathbb{K}\in\mathcal{K}_{V}\Big\}.

Clearly, the constants
\gamma_{j}
can be calculated by

(4.16)

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}=\frac{(n+2)T(\Omega_{j})}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\psi(h_{\Omega_{j}})d\mu_{j}(x)}.

Let
{h_{j}}_{\max}=\displaystyle\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{{\Omega}_{j}}(x)
and
\bar{x}_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
satisfy that
{h_{j}}_{\max}=h_{{\Omega}_{j}}(\bar{x}_{j})
. Then

(4.17)

\displaystyle h_{{\Omega}_{j}}(x)\geq\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle{h_{j}}_{\max}\ \ \ \ \mathrm{for\ all}\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\ \mathrm{with}\ \langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle>0.

Denote by
\textbf{B}^{n}_{t}
the origin-symmetric Euclidean ball with radius
t>0
. Note that

(4.18)

\displaystyle T(\textbf{B}^{n}_{t})=\int_{\textbf{B}^{n}_{t}}|\overline{\nabla}U|^{2}dx,

and the function
t\mapsto T(\textbf{B}^{n}_{t})
is continuous and monotone increasing, then there exists a
t_{0}>0
such that
T(\textbf{B}^{n}_{t_{0}})=T(\Omega_{0})
. That is,
\textbf{B}^{n}_{t_{0}}\in\mathcal{K}_{V}
, which further yields, by (
4.15
), that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{j}\Psi(h_{\textbf{B}^{n}_{t_{0}}})dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f_{j}\Psi(h_{\Omega_{j}})dx.

Since
f_{j}dx\rightarrow\,d\mu
weakly and
\mu
is not concentrated on any closed hemisphere, there exists a constant
c_{\mu}>0
, such that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle_{+}f_{j}dx>c_{\mu}\ \ \ \mathrm{for\ all}\ j\in\mathbb{N},

where
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
. To this end, assume the contrary, there exists a subsequence of
j
(which will still be denoted by
j
) such that
x_{j}\rightarrow x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and

(4.21)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle x,x_{0}\rangle_{+}d\mu=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle_{+}f_{j}dx=0.

Consequently, one gets

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle x,x_{0}\rangle_{+}d\mu\geq\frac{\mu(\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,x_{0}\rangle>1/N\})}{N}.

This implies
\mu(\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,x_{0}\rangle>1/N\})=0
for all
N>1
, which contradicts with, after taking
N\rightarrow\infty
, the fact that
\mu
is not concentrated on any closed hemisphere.

By (
4.20
), for any
\zeta\in(0,1)
, one has

\displaystyle c_{\mu}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle_{+}f_{j}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle>\zeta\}}\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle_{+}f_{j}dx+\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle\leq\zeta\}}\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle_{+}f_{j}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle>\zeta\}}f_{j}dx+\zeta\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{j}dx,

since
0<\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle_{+}<1
.
Hence

(4.22)

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle>\zeta\}}f_{j}dx\geq c_{\mu}-\zeta\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{j}dx.

As
\,d\mu_{j}=f_{j}dx
converges to
\mu
weakly, one can choose
\zeta_{1}\in(0,1)
to be a small enough constant such that, for all

,

\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle>\zeta_{1}\}}f_{j}dx\geq c_{\mu}-\zeta_{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{j}dx>\frac{c_{\mu}}{2}.

Consequently, it follows that

(4.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Psi(h_{\Omega_{j}})f_{j}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle>\zeta_{1}\}}\Psi({h_{j}}_{\max}\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle)f_{j}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Psi(\zeta_{1}{h_{j}}_{\max})\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle x,\bar{x}_{j}\rangle>\zeta_{1}\}}f_{j}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\mu}}{2}\cdot\Psi(\zeta_{1}{h_{j}}_{\max}).

As
\,d\mu_{j}=f_{j}dx
converges to
\mu
weakly, (
4.19
) implies that for all

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{j}\Psi(h_{\Omega_{j}})dx\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f_{j}\Psi(h_{\textbf{B}^{n}_{t_{0}}})dx<\infty,

this together with (
4.23
) and the fact that
\Psi(s)\to\infty
as
s\to\infty
, one gets that
{h_{j}}_{\max}\leq C
, where we use
C>0
to denote a constant
which depends only on
\mu
and
\psi
, but it may change from line to line.

Since
\Omega_{j}\in\mathcal{K}_{V}
, using the same argument as in Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

1/C\leq w_{\Omega_{j}}^{-}\leq w_{\Omega_{j}}^{+}\leq C,\quad\forall j.

By (

) and the Blaschke selection theorem, we obtain the existence of a subsequence

(which will still be denoted by

It follows from (

∎

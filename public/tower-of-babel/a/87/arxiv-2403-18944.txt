The generators of the K K -groups of the sphere
Hermann Schulz-Baldes, Tom Stoiber

Abstract
This note presents an elementary iterative construction of the generators for the complex K K -groups K i ​ ( C ​ ( 𝕊 d ) ) K_{i}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{d})) of the d d -dimensional spheres. These generators are explicitly given as the restrictions of Dirac or Weyl Hamiltonians to the unit sphere. Connections to solid state physics are briefly elaborated on. Keywords: K K -groups, generators, connecting maps, Dirac Hamiltonian, Weyl Hamiltonian

Recall

Let us recall two methods used to compute generators of the

(i)

The stereographic projection

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

K_{*}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{d}))\,\cong\,K_{*}(C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\oplus K_{*}({\mathbb{C}})\;.

From the elementary standpoint taken here, this merely states that generators for the
K

and

are equally difficult to compute. Standard text books

compute the

(ii)

For

\pm 1

(iii)

Another way to obtain the generators is to resort to
KK
-theory which is known to provide representations of
K
-groups as well. One can then compute iteratively the Kasparov products
KK_{i}({\mathbb{C}},C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\otimes KK_{1}({\mathbb{C}},C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}))\to KK_{1-i}({\mathbb{C}},C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\otimes C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}))

KK_{1}({\mathbb{C}},C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}))

7

KK_{i}({\mathbb{C}},C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

The aim is thus to show that the Dirac and Weyl Hamiltonians in dimension

, when restricted to the sphere, are generators of the two non-trivial summands
{\mathbb{Z}}
in (

Proposition 1

For
d\geq 2
even, let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{d+1}
be a selfadjoint irreducible representation of the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{d+1}
. A generator of
\widetilde{K}_{0}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{d}))
is given by the selfadjoint unitary

x\,\in\,{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\,\mapsto\,Q_{d}^{W}(x)\,=\,\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}x_{j}\,\Gamma_{j}\hskip 16.38895pt\mbox{\rm(Weyl Hamiltonian)}\;.

For odd
d
, let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{d}
be a selfadjoint irreducible representation of the Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{d}
, a generator of
{K}_{1}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{d}))
is given by the unitary

x\,\in\,{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\,\mapsto\,U_{d}^{D}(x)\,=\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}\,\Gamma_{j}\,+\,\imath\,x_{d+1}{\bf 1}\hskip 16.38895pt\mbox{\rm(Dirac phase)}\;.

Let us note that odd
K
-groups can also be described by chiral selfadjoint unitaries. In this representation and for odd
d
, the above generator is given by
x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\mapsto Q_{d}^{D}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}x_{j}\,\Gamma_{j}
which indeed anti-commutes with the self-adjoint unitary
\Gamma_{d+2}=\imath^{\frac{d+2}{2}}\Gamma_{1}\cdots\Gamma_{d+1}
. Hence in the grading of
\Gamma_{d+2}
, one has
Q_{d}^{D}=\binom{\;0\;\;(U^{D}_{d})^{*}}{U^{D}_{d}\;\;\;\;0\;\;}
which is hence the Dirac Hamiltonian. Let us also remark that the equivalent of Proposition
1
for the van Daele
K
-groups of ”real” spheres was recently given in
[
8

using very different methods. More precisely, the generators can be given in an analogous form if certain reality conditions on the Clifford representation are enforced. Presumably this can also be derived using the methods of this article by simply implementing the symmetries in the connecting maps as in
[
3
,
6
]
.

The proof that the elements given in Proposition

are generators will be carried out inductively in the dimension

{K}_{1}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{1}))
is generated by
x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\mapsto U_{1}^{D}(x)=x_{1}+\imath x_{2}
. The iteration uses the connecting maps of the short exact sequence of C
∗

0\;\rightarrow\;C_{0}({\mathbb{D}}^{d+1})\;\overset{i}{\rightarrow}\;C(\overline{{\mathbb{D}}^{d+1}})\;\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}\;C({\mathbb{S}}^{d})\;\rightarrow\;0

(2)

Here the
d

\|\,.\,\|
as above) are given by

{\mathbb{D}}^{d}\;=\;\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;\|x\|<1\}\;,\hskip 16.38895pt\overline{{\mathbb{D}}^{d}}\;=\;\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;\|x\|\leq 1\}\;.

The exact sequence simply reflects that one has the disjoint decomposition
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}^{d+1}}={{\mathbb{D}}^{d+1}}\cup{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
. For the iterative construction, convenient forms of the connecting maps will be spelled out in Section
3

In non-commutative geometry, the generators of Proposition
1
are the restrictions to the unit sphere of the so-called dual Dirac or Clifford operators which represent the generator of
K_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
in the unbounded Fredholm picture of
K
-theory,
i.e.
under the isomorphism
K_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cong KK_{i}({\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. The connection of those with the generators of the sphere will be made more explicit in Section
7
. On the other hand, in solid state physics the generators are rather viewed as the restrictions to the unit sphere of the Fourier transforms of the Weyl and Dirac Hamiltonians on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
which provide a good local description of so-called Weyl points and Dirac points. In a solid state framework, a
K
-theoretic description of these points and an analysis of their stability apparently goes back to
[
7
]
. The importance of such points as topological phase transition points through semimetals has been stressed in many works (for a mathematical treatment, see
[
12
,
15
]
). In the remainder of the introduction, let us describe the solid state perspective on the generators in the physically most relevant dimensions
d=3
and
d=2
. Let us first consider the three-dimensional so-called Weyl Hamiltonian

H\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{3}{p_{j}\otimes\sigma_{j}}

where
\sigma_{j}
are the three Pauli matrices and the momentum operators are
p_{j}=-\imath\nabla_{j}
. Densely defined on a suitable Sobolev space,
H
is a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. It models a three-dimensional massless particle, a so-called Weyl fermion, and is popular as an effective model for certain quasi-particles in solid state physics. It is convenient to consider it in the Fourier-transformed picture and therefore instead interprete
p_{j}
as the (unbounded) multiplication operator by the
j
-th coordinate on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. In that way,
H
is multiplication by the matrix-valued function

h:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\;,\hskip 16.38895pth(x)\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sigma_{j}x_{j}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}x_{3}&x_{1}+\imath x_{2}\\
x_{1}-\imath x_{2}&-x_{3}\end{pmatrix}\;.

The pointwise spectrum is
\sigma(h(x))=\{-\sqrt{\lVert x\rVert},\sqrt{\lVert x\rVert}\}
and therefore there are two distinct sheets that meet at the point
x=0
. As is well-known in physics this band-crossing is stable: continuously modifying the function
h
in a neighborhood of
x=0
while keeping the asymptotic behavior fixed, the two sheets will always meet in at least one point. The reason for that is that the band-crossing carries a ”topological charge” given by the Chern number which prevents the opening of a gap in the spectrum. To understand this in terms of
K
-theory let us note that
h\rvert_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}
is a self-adjoint unitary and therefore defines a
K
-theory class
[h\rvert_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}]_{0}\in K_{0}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{2}))
. The exact sequence (
2
) for
d=2
links the interior of the sphere (the open disk
{\mathbb{D}}^{3}
) with its boundary
\partial{\mathbb{D}}^{3}={\mathbb{S}}^{2}
. From the associated
K
-theoretic six-term exact sequence one can read off (as we will do in Section
4
) that the connecting homomorphism
{\mbox{\rm Exp}}:K_{0}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{2}))\to K_{1}(C_{0}({\mathbb{D}}^{3}))
is a surjection which maps a generator of the reduced
K
-group
\widetilde{K}_{0}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{2}))
to a generator of
K_{1}(C_{0}({\mathbb{D}}^{3}))
. Now recall that the computation of
Exp
requires one to continuously extend
h\rvert_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}
to the interior of the disk and if the image under
Exp
is supposed to be non-trivial, then no such extension can have a spectral gap inside the interval
[-1,1]
. Therefore the phenomenology is consistent with the fact that
[h\rvert_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}]_{0}
is a non-trivial vector bundle on the sphere (and indeed this is the generator from Proposition
1
).

Due to
\widetilde{K}_{0}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{2}))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
, this procedure associates an integer-valued ”charge” to the band-crossing. In solid state physics a general three-dimensional translation-invariant Hamiltonian corresponds in momentum space to a function
h:X\to M_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
where
X
is either
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
or
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
and the spectrum as a function of
x\in X
could have multiple band-crossings
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
at spectral values
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. To each isolated band-crossing
x_{i}
, one can associate a charge in
K_{0}({\mathbb{S}}^{2})
. If one deforms
h
continuously in such a way that all band-crossings stay isolated (
i.e.
zero-dimensional), then it is only possible to eliminate non-trivial band-crossings and separate the corresponding bands by merging two or more of them in such a way that their charges add up to
0
. In this sense, Weyl points,
i.e.
band-crossings around which a Hamiltonian locally can be approximated by a Weyl Hamiltonian, are stable features of band structures.

In dimension
d=2
one can similarly construct an element of
K_{1}(C({\mathbb{S}}^{1}))
from the Dirac Hamiltonian

H=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sigma_{j}p_{j}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, which upon Fourier transform is again a multiplication operator by the matrix function

h:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\;,\hskip 16.38895pth(x)\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sigma_{j}x_{j}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{1}+\imath x_{2}\\
x_{1}-\imath x_{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\;.

As above, the spectrum consists of two sheets that meet at
x=0
. Such band-crossings that locally look like ”Dirac points” are not stable on their own, one merely needs to add a ”mass-term”
m\sigma_{3}
with
m\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
to open a gap in the spectrum. However, they do become stable if one enforces onto
H
an additional chiral symmetry,
i.e.
one requires it to anti-commute with a self-adjoint unitary matrix
J
which here one can choose to be
\sigma_{3}
. One can then associate to isolated band-crossings at energy zero a charge in odd
K
-theory
K_{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
and has similar stability properties under deformations as in the odd-dimensional case as long as the chiral symmetry is preserved.

2
Complex Clifford algebras

This section briefly recalls the definition and some well-known properties of the complex Clifford algebras. The complex Clifford algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{d}
of
d
generators is the universal
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra generated by selfadjoint elements
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{d}
satisfying the anti-commutation relation
\Gamma_{i}\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{j}\Gamma_{i}=2\,{\bf 1}\,\delta_{i,j}
. They admit
2^{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}
-dimensional irreducible representations, which are unique up to unitary equivalence for even
d
, but for odd
d
there are two equivalence classes distinguished by the sign in

\Gamma_{1}\cdots\Gamma_{d}\;=\;\pm\,\imath^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\;.

Indeed, the product is only fixed to commute with all generators, thus a scalar by Schur’s lemma, and to be a self-adjoint unitary or an anti-self-adjoint unitary depending on the parity of
\frac{d-1}{2}
. Irreducible matrix representations can be generated iteratively by starting with the Pauli matrices

\sigma_{1}\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\;,\hskip 8.19447pt\sigma_{2}\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}0&\imath\\
-\imath&0\end{pmatrix}\;,\hskip 8.19447pt\sigma_{3}\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\;,

of which
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
generate an irreducible representation of
{\mathbb{C}}_{2}
and
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
one of two irreducible representations of
{\mathbb{C}}_{3}
. The other can be obtained
e.g.
by permuting two of those generators. Given a representation
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{d-2})
of
{\mathbb{C}}_{d-2}
with
d>1
odd, one obtains generators of
{\mathbb{C}}_{d-1}
and
{\mathbb{C}}_{d}
by setting

\Gamma_{i}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma_{i}\\
\sigma_{i}&0\end{pmatrix}\hskip 8.19447pt\forall\;\,i=1,\ldots,d-2\,,\hskip 16.38895pt\Gamma_{d-1}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}0&\imath\\
-\imath&0\end{pmatrix}\;,\hskip 8.19447pt\Gamma_{d}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}{\bf 1}&0\\
0&-{\bf 1}\end{pmatrix}\;.

With those conventions one has for odd
d
the irreducible representation with
\Gamma_{1}\cdots\Gamma_{d}=\imath^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
, which will be called the left-handed convention (
e.g.
as in
[
15
]
). Note that in Proposition
1
both constructions use irreducible representations of an odd-dimensional Clifford algebra, but we do not specify the handedness.

Lemma 1

It is sufficient to prove
Proposition
1
under the additional assumption that the irreducible representation of
{\mathbb{C}}_{d}
respectively
{\mathbb{C}}_{d+1}
is precisely the one which was iteratively constructed above.

Proof.
If the representation is not left-handed one first applies the homeomorphism

{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\,\to\,{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\;,\hskip 8.19447pt(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\,\mapsto\,(-x_{1},x_{2}\ldots,x_{d})\;,

and replaces
\Gamma_{1}\mapsto-\Gamma_{1}
after which the generator
Q^{W}_{d}
respectively
U_{d}^{D}
again takes the same form, but is constructed from a left-handed representation, which is therefore unitarily equivalent to the one given above.

\Box

A different handedness of the representation corresponds to a relative minus sign of the generators, hence one needs to be careful with these conventions if it is relevant for the outcome of a computation.

7
Relation to the Fredholm picture

In this section, the generators of
K_{i}(C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}))
given in Proposition
1
are used to determine the unbounded generators of the isomorphic group
KK_{i}({\mathbb{C}},C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}))
.

Let us start out be explaining this isomorphism which is merely the so-called Fredholm picture of
K
-theory (see
[
2
, Section 17.5.1]
or
[
13
]
). It states that any class in
K_{0}({\cal A})
can be represented by a bounded adjointable operator
T
on a standard Hilbert
{\cal A}
-module
{\cal H}_{\cal A}={\cal A}\otimes{\cal H}
, with separable but possibly finite-dimensional Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{\cal A}
, satisfying the Fredholm conditions

{\bf 1}\,-\,T^{*}T\,,\;{\bf 1}\,-\,TT^{*}\;\in\;{\mathbb{K}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})\;.

(15)

For
K_{1}({\cal A})
, on top of the condition (
15
) one requires the representatives
T
to be self-adjoint. To be precise,
T
(together with the Hilbert module it acts on) defines a class in
KK_{i}({\mathbb{C}},{\cal A})
which is isomorphic to
K_{i}({\cal A})
via the maps

{\cal I}_{0}\;:\;[T]_{0}\,\mapsto\,{\rm Ind}([T+{\mathbb{K}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})]_{1})\;,\hskip 16.38895pt{\cal I}_{1}\;:\;[T]_{1}\,\mapsto\,{\mbox{\rm Exp}}([T+{\mathbb{K}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})]_{0})\;,

where the index and exponential map are w.r.t. the extension

0\;\rightarrow\;{\mathbb{K}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})\;\rightarrow\;{\mathbb{B}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})\;\rightarrow\;{\mathbb{B}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})/{\mathbb{K}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})\;\rightarrow\;0\;.

This is well-defined since
T+{\mathbb{K}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})
is a (self-adjoint) unitary and
K_{i}({\cal H}_{\cal A})\cong K_{i}({\cal A})
due to stability. Let us briefly recall why this is an isomorphism. One can assume that
{\cal H}
is some fixed infinite-dimensional Hilbert space (if
T
acts on a different Hilbert module
{\cal K}_{\cal A}
one can always extend
T
to
T\oplus{\bf 1}_{{\cal H}_{\cal A}}
and use the absorption theorem
{\cal K}_{\cal A}\oplus{\cal H}_{\cal A}\simeq{\cal H}_{\cal A}
). This gives an isomorphism of
KK_{i}({\mathbb{C}},{\cal A})
with
K_{1-i}({\mathbb{B}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})/{\mathbb{K}}({\cal H}_{\cal A}))
and since the
K
-groups of
{\mathbb{B}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})
are trivial, exponential and index map are both isomorphisms.

In the so-called unbounded picture one instead uses unbounded regular operators
T
with compact resolvent,
i.e.

T
and
T^{*}
are densely defined,
{\bf 1}+T^{*}T
has dense range and the Fredholm condition (
15
) is replaced by

({\bf 1}+T^{*}T)^{-1}\,,\;({\bf 1}+TT^{*})^{-1}\;\in\;{\mathbb{K}}({\cal H}_{\cal A})\;.

(16)

This is accomplished simply by replacing
T
with its bounded transform
F(T):=T({\bf 1}+T^{*}T)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, which is then a bounded operator that satisfies (
15
).

Let us now consider the case
{\cal A}=C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}_{d+1}
where
{\mathbb{C}}_{d+1}
is the complex Clifford algebra with
d+1
generators. Associated is the Hilbert module
{\cal H}_{\cal A}=C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
with
N
being the dimension of the Clifford representation. Now introduce, with the same notations as in Proposition
1
, the unbounded functions

x\,\in\,{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}\;\mapsto\;H^{W}_{d}(x)\,=\,\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}x_{j}\Gamma_{j}\in M_{N}({\mathbb{C}})\;,

for even
d
and

for odd
d
. These operators define unbounded multipliers on the Hilbert module

\lvert x\rvert^{2}
and

is a
C_{0}
-function and the compact operators on

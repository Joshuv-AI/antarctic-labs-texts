Performance Gaps in Multi-view Clustering under the Nested Matrix-Tensor Model
Hugo Lebeau, Mohamed El Amine Seddik, José Henrique de Morais Goulart

Abstract
We study the estimation of a planted signal hidden in a recently introduced nested matrix-tensor model, which is an extension of the classical spiked rank-one tensor model, motivated by multi-view clustering. Prior work has theoretically examined the performance of a tensor-based approach, which relies on finding a best rank-one approximation, a problem known to be computationally hard. A tractable alternative approach consists in computing instead the best rank-one (matrix) approximation of an unfolding of the observed tensor data, but its performance was hitherto unknown. We quantify here the performance gap between these two approaches, in particular by deriving the precise algorithmic threshold of the unfolding approach and demonstrating that it exhibits a BBP-type transition behavior ( Baik et al. 2005 ) . This work is therefore in line with recent contributions which deepen our understanding of why tensor-based methods surpass matrix-based methods in handling structured tensor data.

Introduction

In the age of artificial intelligence, handling vast amounts of data has become a fundamental aspect of machine learning tasks. Datasets are often high-dimensional and composed of multiple modes, such as various modalities, sensors, sources, types, or domains, naturally lending themselves to be represented as tensors.
Tensors offer a richer structure
compared to traditional one-dimensional vectors and two-dimensional matrices, making them increasingly relevant in various applications, including statistical learning and data analysis
(
Landsberg 2012
;
Sun et al. 2014
)
.

Yet, in the existing literature, there is a notable scarcity of theoretical studies that specifically address the performance gaps between tensor-based methods and traditional (matrix) spectral methods in the context of high-dimensional data analysis. While tensor methods have shown promise in various applications, including multi-view clustering, co-clustering, community detection, and latent variable modeling
(
Wu et al. 2019
;
Anandkumar et al. 2014
;
Papalexakis et al. 2012
;
Wang et al. 2023
)
, little attention has been devoted to rigorously quantifying the advantages and drawbacks of leveraging the hidden low-rank tensor structure.
Filling this gap by conducting a thorough theoretical analysis is crucial for gaining a deeper understanding of the practical implications and potential performance gains associated with tensor-based techniques.

In the specific case of multi-view clustering,
Seddik et al. 2023a
recently proposed a spectral tensor method and carried out a precise analysis of its performance in the large-dimensional limit.
Their method consists in computing a best rank-one (tensor) approximation of a
nested matrix-tensor model
, which, in particular, generalizes the classical

of

Although the above described model arises from a rather particular choice of view transformations

Related work.

In the machine learning literature, the notion of “view” is fairly general and models data whose form may differ but all represent the same object seen from different (and complementary) angles (for instance, multiple descriptors of an image, translations of a text or features of a webpage such as its hyperlinks, text and images). Various approaches have been considered to address multi-view clustering problems. For instance,
Nie et al. 2016
;
Nie et al. 2017b
;
Nie et al. 2017a
consider a graph-based model and construct a similarity matrix by integrating all views with a weighted sum before applying spectral clustering. Other approaches, relying upon a space-learning-based model
(
Wang et al. 2017
;
Zhang et al. 2017
;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Peng et al. 2019
)
, reconstruct the data in an ideal space where clustering is easy.
Zhang et al. 2019
suggest a method which is more suitable for large datasets by mixing binary coding and clustering. Tensor methods have also been considered:
Wu et al. 2019
propose an essential tensor learning approach for Markov chain-based multi-view spectral clustering. Furthermore,
Liu et al. 2021
;
Liu et al. 2023
design simple yet effective methods for multi-view clustering relying on multiple kernel
k
-means. Our work differs from these previous contributions in that it is focused on a tensor model having the specific form of equation
Nested Matrix-Tensor Model
. Even though this model corresponds to a rather particular case of the general setting given in equation
1
, our results represent a first step towards precisely understanding how tensor methods can contribute to addressing the latter.

Regarding the analysis of performance gaps between tensor- and matrix-based methods, we can mention the recent work by
Seddik et al. 2023b
, where the authors proposed a data model that consists of a Gaussian mixture assuming a low-rank tensor structure on the population means and further characterized the theoretical performance gap between a simple tensor-based method and a flattening-based method that neglects the low-rank structure. Their study has demonstrated that the tensor approach yields provably better performance compared to treating the data as mere vectors.

2.2

Example

With

The results presented below are derived using tools from the theory of large random matrices

Given a random symmetric matrix

2

3.2
Estimation with Weighted Mean

Before diving into the application of the previous results to multi-view clustering (where we will be interested in the estimation of the class labels contained in
{\bm{y}}
), we propose an analysis of a related matrix model corresponding to the optimal estimation of
{\bm{y}}
when
{\bm{z}}
is perfectly known. These results will give us an optimistic upper bound on the performance of the estimation of
{\bm{y}}
from
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}

In case
{\bm{z}}
is known,
{\bm{y}}
can be estimated with the following weighted mean of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
along mode
3

\bar{{\mathbf{T}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{3}}{z}_{k}{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}_{:,:,k}=\beta_{T}\beta_{M}{\bm{x}}{\bm{y}}^{\top}+\frac{\varsigma}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{N}}

Onatski et al. 2013

Löffler et al. 2020

Similarly to the previous spiked models, the rank-one information
\beta_{T}\beta_{M}{\bm{x}}{\bm{y}}^{\top}
induces the presence of an isolated eigenvalue in the spectrum of
\frac{1}{\varsigma^{2}}\bar{{\mathbf{T}}}^{\top}\bar{{\mathbf{T}}}

Theorem 4

(Spike Behavior)
.

If

{\bm{\mathsfit{X}}}=\left({\bm{\mu}}\bar{{\bm{y}}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{Z}}\right)\otimes{\bm{h}}+{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\textnormal{Z}}_{i,j}&\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}&{\mathcal{N}}\left(0,\frac{1}{p+n}\right)\\

(5)

i

5
Conclusion and Perspectives

Under the nested matrix-tensor model, we have precisely quantified the multi-view clustering performance achievable by the unfolding method and compared it with a previously studied tensor approach
(
Seddik et al. 2023a
)
. This analysis has showed the theoretical superiority of the latter in terms of clustering accuracy. In particular, the tensor approach can, in principle, recover the signal at a
\Theta(1)
signal-to-noise ratio, while the matrix approach needs this ratio to diverge as
n_{T}^{1/4}
. However, the tensor approach is based on an NP-hard formulation, and no efficient algorithm capable of succeeding at a
\Theta(1)
signal-to-noise ratio is currently known. In practice, for a sufficiently large ratio, one may combine these approaches by initializing a tensor rank-one approximation algorithm (such as power iteration) with the estimate given by the unfolding method. Overall, this work advances our understanding of tensor data processing through rigorous theoretical results. Finally, a natural question for future work is how to extend our results to more general view functions
f_{k}
.

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{e,f,g=1}^{n_{3},n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}{\textnormal{Q}}_{a,e}\left(\delta_{e,c}\delta_{f,d}{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{g,f}+{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{e,f}\delta_{g,c}\delta_{f,d}\right){\textnormal{Q}}_{g,b}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{a,b}}{\partial{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{c,d}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\left({\textnormal{Q}}_{a,c}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{d,b}+{\textnormal{Q}}_{c,b}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{d,a}\right).

∎

Proposition 4
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{W}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]=\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}+1\right){\mathbf{Q}}+s\left({\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}\right)\right]

(20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}\left({\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{1}{n_{T}}\left({\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right)\right)\right]=\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]

(21)

Proof.

We prove these two results with Stein’s lemma (Lemma
1
) and Lemma
2
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{W}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{i,k}{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{l,k}{\textnormal{Q}}_{l,j}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{l,k}}{\partial{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{i,k}}{\textnormal{Q}}_{l,j}+{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{l,k}\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{l,j}}{\partial{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{i,k}}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\delta_{l,i}\delta_{k,k}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\textnormal{Q}}_{l,j}\right]\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{l,k}\left({\textnormal{Q}}_{l,i}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{k,j}+{\textnormal{Q}}_{i,j}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{k,l}\right)\right]\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}+1\right){\mathbf{Q}}+s\left({\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right)\right]_{i,j}

where the last equality comes from
{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}={\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[{z}_{i}{m}_{k}{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{l,k}{\textnormal{Q}}_{l,j}\right]

\displaystyle=\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[{z}_{i}{m}_{k}\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{l,j}}{\partial{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{l,k}}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}\\
-\frac{1}{n_{T}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[{z}_{i}{m}_{k}\left({\textnormal{Q}}_{l,l}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{k,j}+{\textnormal{Q}}_{l,j}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{k,l}\right)\right]\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}-\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}\left({\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right)\right]_{i,j}.

∎

E.2
A First Matrix Equivalent

Since
{\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}{\mathbf{Q}}={\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}
,

\beta_{T}{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\mathbf{W}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}-s{\mathbf{Q}}={\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}

and, from equation
20
, we have

\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]+\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]-\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}+1\right){\mathbf{Q}}+s\left({\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}\right)\right]-s\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]={\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}.

Let us rearrange the terms

s\frac{n_{3}}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}}{n_{3}}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]+\left(s+\frac{n_{3}-n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{T}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]+{\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}=\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]-\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]

in order to see that we need to following rescaling to counteract the divergence of the spectrum of
{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}
,

\tilde{s}=\frac{n_{T}s-\left(n_{3}+n_{1}n_{2}\right)}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}},\qquad\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}(\tilde{s})=\left(\frac{n_{T}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}-\left(n_{3}+n_{1}n_{2}\right){\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}-\tilde{s}{\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}\right)^{-1}.

Hence, our equation becomes,

\left(\frac{n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\tilde{s}+\frac{n_{T}}{n_{1}n_{2}}+\frac{n_{T}}{n_{3}}\right)\frac{n_{3}}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}}{n_{3}}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]+\left(\tilde{s}+\frac{2n_{3}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]+{\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}\\
=\frac{\beta_{T}n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}+\left(\tilde{s}+\frac{n_{3}+n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}\right].

(22)

Moreover, equation
21
becomes,

\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}\left(\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\left(\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}+\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}\right)\right)\right]=\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right].

(23)

Therefore, denoting
\tilde{m}(\tilde{s})=\lim\frac{\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}(\tilde{s})}{n_{3}}
the Stieltjes transform of the limiting spectral distribution of
\frac{n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}-\frac{n_{3}+n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}{\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}
, the combination of equation
22
and equation
23
yield the following matrix equivalent,

\tilde{m}(\tilde{s})\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}+\tilde{s}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}+{\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}\longleftrightarrow\frac{\beta_{T}^{2}n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}.

(24)

At this point, we are still carrying the random term
\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}
in the RHS. This quantity is expected to concentrate rapidly around its mean
\beta_{M}^{2}+\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}}
. However, because
{\mathbf{M}}
and
\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}
are not independent, the RHS must be studied carefully.

E.3
Analysis of the RHS

Lemma 3
.

\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{a,b}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{c,d}}=-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\left(\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{a}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[c,d],b}+\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{b}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[c,d],a}\right).

Proof.

Since
\partial{\mathbf{Q}}=-{\mathbf{Q}}\partial\left({\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}\right){\mathbf{Q}}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{a,b}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{c,d}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{e,f,g,h=1}^{n_{3},n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}{\textnormal{Q}}_{a,e}\left(\frac{\partial{\mathsfit{T}}_{f,g,e}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{c,d}}{\mathsfit{T}}_{f,g,h}+{\mathsfit{T}}_{f,g,e}\frac{\partial{\mathsfit{T}}_{f,g,h}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{c,d}}\right){\textnormal{Q}}_{h,b}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{e,h=1}^{n_{3}}{\textnormal{Q}}_{a,e}\left({z}_{e}{\mathsfit{T}}_{c,d,h}+{\mathsfit{T}}_{c,d,e}{z}_{h}\right){\textnormal{Q}}_{h,b}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{a,b}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{c,d}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\left(\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{a}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[c,d],b}+\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{b}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[c,d],a}\right).

∎

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\beta_{M}{x}_{u}{y}_{v}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}\right)^{2}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\beta_{M}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}+2\beta_{M}{x}_{u}{y}_{v}\right){\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\beta_{M}^{2}+\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}+2\beta_{M}{x}_{u}{y}_{v}\right)\frac{\partial{\mathbf{Q}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}}\right]

where we have used Stein’s lemma (Lemma
1
) to derive the last equality. Hence, using Lemma
3
, it becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\left(\beta_{M}^{2}+\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}}\right)\right){\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta_{T}}{n_{M}}\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}+2\beta_{M}{x}_{u}{y}_{v}\right)\left(\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{i}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],j}+\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{j}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],i}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta_{T}}{n_{M}}\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(2{m}_{[u,v]}-\frac{{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\right)\left(\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{i}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],j}+\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{j}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],i}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}-2\frac{\beta_{T}}{n_{M}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+\left({\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right)^{\top}\right]_{i,j}\\
+\frac{\beta_{T}}{n_{M}\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}\left(\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{i}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],j}+\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{j}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],i}\right)\right]\end{multlined}

and, since
\beta_{T}{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}={\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\mathbf{W}}^{(3)}
and
{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}={\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}
, we have,

\beta_{T}{\bm{z}}{\bm{m}}^{\top}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}={\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\mathbf{W}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}.

Thus, our expression turns into

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\left(\beta_{M}^{2}+\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}}\right)\right){\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}-\frac{4}{n_{M}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]_{i,j}+\frac{2}{n_{M}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\left({\mathbf{W}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}+{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{W}}^{(3)\top}\right){\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}\\
+\frac{\beta_{T}}{n_{M}\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}\left(\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{i}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],j}+\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{j}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],i}\right)\right]\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{n_{M}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]_{i,j}+\frac{2}{n_{M}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\left[{\bm{A}}_{1}+{\bm{A}}_{1}^{\top}\right]_{i,j}+\frac{\beta_{T}}{n_{M}\sqrt{n_{M}}}\left[{\bm{A}}_{2}+{\bm{A}}_{2}^{\top}\right]_{i,j}

with

{\bm{A}}_{1}=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{W}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right],\qquad\left[{\bm{A}}_{2}\right]_{i,j}=\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]_{i}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{[u,v],j}\right].

Let us develop
{\bm{A}}_{1}
with Stein’s lemma (Lemma
1
) on
{\mathbf{W}}^{(3)}
.

\displaystyle\left[{\bm{A}}_{1}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{n_{3},n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{i,a}}{\partial{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{a,b}}{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{c,b}{\textnormal{Q}}_{c,j}+{\textnormal{Q}}_{i,a}\frac{\partial{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{c,b}}{\partial{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{a,b}}{\textnormal{Q}}_{c,j}+{\textnormal{Q}}_{i,a}{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{c,b}\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{c,j}}{\partial{\textnormal{W}}^{(3)}_{a,b}}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]_{i,j}\\
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{n_{3},n_{1}n_{2},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left({\textnormal{Q}}_{i,a}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{b,a}+{\textnormal{Q}}_{a,a}\left[{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{b,i}\right){\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{c,b}{\textnormal{Q}}_{c,j}\right]\\

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]_{i,j}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(2{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}\right){\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\Tr{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle\left[{\bm{A}}_{1}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]_{i,j}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}+2\right){\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}+2s{\mathbf{Q}}\left({\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}\right)\right]_{i,j}

since
{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}={\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}
.

Next, we develop
{\bm{A}}_{2}
with Stein’s lemma (Lemma
1
) on
{\mathbf{Z}}
.

\displaystyle\left[{\bm{A}}_{2}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v=1}^{n_{1},n_{2}}\sum_{a,b=1}^{n_{3},n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{i,a}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}}{z}_{a}{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{b,[u,v]}{\textnormal{Q}}_{b,j}+{\textnormal{Q}}_{i,a}{z}_{a}\frac{\partial{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{b,[u,v]}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}}{\textnormal{Q}}_{b,j}+{\textnormal{Q}}_{i,a}{z}_{a}{\textnormal{T}}^{(3)}_{b,[u,v]}\frac{\partial{\textnormal{Q}}_{b,j}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{u,v}}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\frac{\beta_{T}n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}\\

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\frac{\beta_{T}n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left({\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]{\mathbf{Q}}\right){\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}\\
-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\left({\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right)\right]_{i,j}\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\left[{\bm{A}}_{2}\right]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\frac{\beta_{T}n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}+2\right){\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]{\mathbf{Q}}\right]_{i,j}\\

since, again,
{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}{\mathbf{Q}}={\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}
.

Eventually, we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\left(\beta_{M}^{2}+\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]-\frac{4}{n_{M}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}+s{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]\\
+\frac{4n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]-\frac{4}{n_{M}n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}+2\right){\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}+2s{\mathbf{Q}}\left({\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}\right)\right]\\
+\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]-\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{M}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}+2\right){\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]{\mathbf{Q}}\right]\\
-\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{M}^{2}}s\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}+\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]-\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{M}^{2}}s\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\left(\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}}+\beta_{M}^{2}-\frac{4}{n_{M}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\right]-\frac{4}{n_{M}}\left(s+\frac{n_{3}-n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{T}}+\frac{2}{n_{T}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right]\\
-\frac{4n_{3}}{n_{M}n_{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}\frac{\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}}{n_{3}}\right]-\frac{8}{n_{M}n_{T}}s\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}^{3}\right]-\frac{8n_{3}}{n_{M}n_{T}}s\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\frac{\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}}{n_{3}}\right]\\
-\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{M}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}-n_{1}n_{2}+2\right){\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}+\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]{\mathbf{Q}}\right]\\
-\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{M}^{2}}s\mathbb{E}\left[n_{3}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\frac{\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}}{n_{3}}+\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}\right]{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\right].\end{multlined}

After rescaling, this becomes,

\displaystyle\frac{n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\left(\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{M}}+\beta_{M}^{2}-\frac{4}{n_{M}}\right)\frac{n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]-\frac{4}{n_{M}}\left(\tilde{s}+\frac{2\left(n_{3}+1\right)}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)\frac{n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}\right]\\
-\frac{4n_{T}}{n_{M}n_{1}n_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\frac{\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}}{n_{3}}\right]-\frac{8n_{T}}{n_{M}n_{1}n_{2}}\left(\tilde{s}+\frac{n_{3}+n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{n_{3}}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{3}+\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}\frac{\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}}{n_{3}}\right]\\
-\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}n_{T}^{2}}{n_{M}^{2}n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(n_{3}-n_{1}n_{2}+2\right)\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}+\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}\right]\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]\\
-\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}n_{T}^{2}}{n_{M}^{2}n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}\left(\tilde{s}+\frac{n_{3}+n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[n_{3}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\frac{\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}}{n_{3}}\right]\\
-\frac{2\beta_{T}^{2}n_{T}^{2}}{n_{M}^{2}n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}\left(\tilde{s}+\frac{n_{3}+n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}\right]\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}+\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}+\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\Theta\left(\sqrt{n_{T}}+\frac{\beta_{M}^{2}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}\right]\\
-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{n_{T}^{2}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\frac{\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}}{n_{3}}\right]-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{n_{T}^{2}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{3}\right]-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}\frac{\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}}{n_{3}}\right]\\
-\Theta\left(\frac{\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{T}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]-\Theta\left(\frac{\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{T}^{3}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}\right]\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right]\\
-\Theta\left(\frac{\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\frac{\Tr\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}}{n_{3}}\right]\\
-\Theta\left(\frac{\beta_{T}^{2}}{n_{T}^{2}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left[{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}\right]\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}+\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}+\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\right].\end{multlined}

Hence, as long as
\beta_{T}=o(n_{T}^{3/4})
, we have

\frac{\beta_{T}^{2}n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}\longleftrightarrow\beta_{T}^{2}\left(\frac{n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{3}}}+\frac{\beta_{M}^{2}n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}.

E.4
Deterministic Equivalent

Coming back to equation
24
, we are now allowed to write,

\tilde{m}(\tilde{s})\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}+\tilde{s}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}+{\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}\longleftrightarrow\beta_{T}^{2}\left(\frac{n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{3}}}+\frac{\beta_{M}^{2}n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right){\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}.

Hence, in order to prevent the RHS from either vanishing or exploding, we see that
\beta_{T}
must be
\Theta(n_{T}^{-1/4})
. In this case, Let
\varrho=\lim\beta_{T}^{2}\left(\frac{n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{3}}}+\frac{\beta_{M}^{2}n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)
.

Then, we can define
\bar{{\mathbf{Q}}}
, the deterministic equivalent of
\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}
such that,

\tilde{m}(\tilde{s})\bar{{\mathbf{Q}}}+\tilde{s}\bar{{\mathbf{Q}}}+{\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}=\varrho{\bm{z}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\bar{{\mathbf{Q}}}.

(25)

Remark
.

In fact, with the assumption
\beta_{M}=\Theta(1)
,
\beta_{T}^{2}\left(\frac{n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{3}}}+\frac{\beta_{M}^{2}n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}\right)\sim\beta_{T}^{2}\frac{n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{n_{3}}}
so we could have kept only this dominant term. However, for finite horizon considerations (as it is the case in practice), adding the
\Theta\left(\frac{\beta_{M}^{2}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\right)
term leads to slightly more precise predictions. Indeed, with the dominant term only, we consider a “worst-case scenario”
\beta_{M}=0
.

E.5
Limiting Spectral Distribution

According to the previous deterministic equivalent, the Stieltjes transform of the limiting spectral distribution of
\frac{n_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)}{\mathbf{T}}^{(3)\top}-\frac{n_{3}+n_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}}{\bm{I}}_{n_{3}}
is solution to

\tilde{m}^{2}(\tilde{s})+\tilde{s}\tilde{m}(\tilde{s})+1=0,

(26)

i.e., it is the standard semi-circle law.

Appendix F
Proof of Theorem
7

F.1
Isolated Eigenvalue

The asymptotic position
\tilde{\xi}
of the isolated eigenvalue (when it exists) is a singular point of
\bar{{\mathbf{Q}}}
. From equation
25
, it is therefore solution to

\tilde{m}(\tilde{\xi})+\tilde{\xi}=\varrho.

Following equation
26
,
\tilde{\xi}=-\tilde{m}(\tilde{\xi})-\frac{1}{\tilde{m}(\tilde{\xi})}
, thus,
\tilde{m}(\tilde{\xi})=-\frac{1}{\varrho}
. Injecting this in equation
26
yields

\tilde{\xi}=\varrho+\frac{1}{\varrho}.

(27)

F.2
Eigenvector Alignment

Following Cauchy’s integral formula,

\left\lvert{\bm{z}}^{\top}\hat{{\mathbf{z}}}\right\rvert^{2}=-\frac{1}{2i\pi}\oint_{\tilde{\gamma}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\tilde{{\mathbf{Q}}}(\tilde{s}){\bm{z}}~\mathrm{d}\tilde{s}

where
\tilde{\gamma}
is a positively-oriented complex contour circling around the isolated eigenvalue only. Hence, using the previously found deterministic equivalent
\bar{{\mathbf{Q}}}
, we can compute the asymptotic value
\zeta
of
\left\lvert{\bm{z}}^{\top}\hat{{\mathbf{z}}}\right\rvert^{2}
,

\zeta=-\frac{1}{2i\pi}\oint_{\tilde{\gamma}}{\bm{z}}^{\top}\bar{{\mathbf{Q}}}(\tilde{s}){\bm{z}}~\mathrm{d}\tilde{s}.

This reduces to residue calculus,

\zeta=\lim_{\tilde{s}\to\tilde{\xi}}\left(\tilde{s}-\tilde{\xi}\right)\left[\tilde{m}(\tilde{s})+\tilde{s}-\varrho\right]^{-1}=\frac{1}{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tilde{s}}\left[\tilde{m}(\tilde{s})+\tilde{s}-\varrho\right]_{\tilde{s}=\tilde{\xi}}}=\frac{1}{\tilde{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{\xi})+1}.

Differentiating equation
26
, we have,

\displaystyle 2\tilde{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{\xi})m(\tilde{\xi})+\tilde{m}(\tilde{\xi})+\tilde{\xi}\tilde{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{\xi})=0

\displaystyle\iff\tilde{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{\xi})=-\frac{\tilde{m}(\tilde{\xi})}{2m(\tilde{\xi})+\tilde{\xi}}

\displaystyle\iff\tilde{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{\xi})=\frac{1}{\varrho\tilde{\xi}-2}

\displaystyle\iff\tilde{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{\xi})=\frac{1}{\varrho^{2}-1}.

Hence, we conclude,

\zeta=1-\frac{1}{\varrho^{2}}.

(28)

A row

2.5.
The elementary symplectic Witt group
W_{E}(R)

If
\alpha\in M_{r}(R),\beta\in M_{s}(R)
are matrices
then
\alpha\perp\beta
denotes the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\beta\\
\end{bmatrix}\in M_{r+s}(R)
.
\psi_{1}
will denote
\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\\
\end{bmatrix}\in E_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and
\psi_{r}
is inductively defined by
\psi_{r}=\psi_{r-1}\perp\psi_{1}\in E_{2r}(\mathbb{Z})
, for
r\geq 2
.

A skew-symmetric matrix whose diagonal elements are zero is called an alternating matrix. If
\phi\in M_{2r}(R)
is
alternating then
\mbox{det}(\phi)=(\mbox{pf}(\phi))^{2}
where
pf
is a
polynomial (called the Pfaffian) in the matrix elements
with coefficients
\pm 1
. Note that we need to fix a sign in the choice of
pf
; so we insist

\mbox{pf}(\psi_{r})=1

for all
r
. For any
\alpha\in M_{2r}(R)
and any alternating matrix
\phi\in M_{2r}(R)
we have

\mbox{pf}(\alpha^{t}\phi\alpha)=\mbox{pf}(\phi)\mbox{det}(\alpha)
. For alternating matrices
\phi,\psi
it is easy to check that

\mbox{pf}(\phi\perp\psi)=(\mbox{pf}(\phi))(\mbox{pf}(\psi))
.

Two matrices
\alpha\in M_{2r}(R),\beta\in M_{2s}(R)
are said to be equivalent (w.r.t.
E(R)
) if there exists
a matrix
\varepsilon\in SL_{2(r+s+l)}(R)\bigcap E(R)
, such that
\alpha\perp\psi_{s+l}=\varepsilon^{t}(\beta\perp\psi_{r+l})\varepsilon,
for some
l
. Denote this by
\alpha\overset{E}{\sim}\beta
. Thus
\overset{E}{\sim}
is an
equivalence relation; denote by
[\alpha]
the orbit of
\alpha
under this relation.

It is easy to see
(
[
19
, p. 945]
)
that
\perp
induces the structure of an abelian group on the set of all
equivalence classes of alternating matrices with Pfaffian
1
; this group is called elementary symplectic Witt group
and is denoted by
W_{E}(R)
.

2.6.
The Suslin matrices

First recall the Suslin matrix
S_{r}(v,w)
. These were defined by Suslin in

(
[
15
, Section 5]
)
. We recall his inductive process : Let

, where

v_{1},w_{1}\in M_{1,r}(R)
. Set
S_{0}(v,w)=a_{0}
and set

S_{r}(v,w)=\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}I_{2^{r-1}}&S_{r-1}(v_{1},w_{1})\\
-S_{r-1}(w_{1},v_{1})^{T}&b_{0}I_{2^{r-1}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

The process is reversible and given a Suslin matrix

one can recover the associated rows
v,w
, i.e. the pair

(v,w)
.

In
[
17
]
, Suslin proves that if

, then by row and
column operations one can reduce

to a matrix
\beta_{r}(v,w)\in SL_{r+1}(R),
whose first row is
(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}^{2}\cdots,a_{r}^{r})
.
This in particular proves that rows of such type can be completed to an invertible matrix of determinant one. We call any such

\beta_{r}(v,w)
to be a compressed Suslin matrix.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring with
1,

\mbox{sdim}(R)\leq 2n-2,

\frac{1}{n!}\in R
and
n\geq 2.
Assume that

(1)

For all
v\in Um_{n+1}(R)
,
v\overset{E_{n+1}(R)}{\sim}(w_{0}^{n!},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}).

(2)

If
\beta_{n}(v,w)\in SL_{n+1}(R)\cap E(R),
then
[e_{1}\beta_{n}(v,w)]=[e_{1}].

(3)

SK_{1}(R)
has no
k
-torsion, if
\frac{1}{k}\in R.

Then, for some
w
with
\langle e_{1}\sigma,w\rangle=1,
the map

\displaystyle\varphi:

\displaystyle\frac{Um_{n+1}(R)}{E_{n+1}(R)}\longrightarrow SK_{1}(R)

\displaystyle[v]=[e_{1}\sigma]^{n!}\longmapsto[S_{n}(e_{1}\sigma,w)]

is a well-defined injective group homomorphism.

{{\bf Proof:}}

Well-defined:
In view of the hypothesis
(1)
, for any
v\in Um_{n+1}(R)
,
there exists
v^{\prime}\in Um_{n+1}(R)
, such that
[v]=\chi_{n!}([v^{\prime}]).
Since every
v^{\prime}\in Um_{n+1}(R)

is completable, so by Lemma
2.1
,
[v]=[e_{1}\sigma]^{n!},
for
some
\sigma\in SL_{n+1}(R).

If
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in Um_{n+1}(R)
be such that
[v]=[v^{\prime}]^{n!}=[v^{\prime\prime}]^{n!}.
By
hypothesis
(1)
, there exists
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in SL_{n+1}(R)
such that
[v]=[e_{1}\tau]^{n!}=[e_{1}\tau^{\prime}]^{n!}.

By

(
[
7
, Lemma 3.2]
)
,
[S_{n}(e_{1}\tau^{n!},w^{\prime})]=[S_{n}(e_{1}\tau^{\prime n!},w^{\prime\prime})].
Since
[v]\longmapsto[S_{n}(v,w)]

is a Mennicke symbol and using Lemma
2.1
, we have

[S_{n}(e_{1}\tau,w^{\prime})]^{n!}=[S_{n}(e_{1}\tau^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})]^{n!}.
Since
SK_{1}(R)
does not have any
(n!)
-torsion,
we have
[S_{n}(e_{1}\tau,w^{\prime})]=[S_{n}(e_{1}\tau^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})].

Therefore the map

\displaystyle\varphi:

\displaystyle\frac{Um_{n+1}(R)}{E_{n+1}(R)}\longrightarrow SK_{1}(R)

\displaystyle[v]=[e_{1}\sigma]^{n!}\longmapsto[S_{n}(e_{1}\sigma,w)]

for some
w\in Um_{n+1}(R)
with
(e_{1}\sigma)\cdot w^{t}=1,
is well-defined.

Injectivity:
Let
v=[e_{1}\sigma]^{n!}
be such that
[S_{n}(e_{1}\sigma,w)]=1
in
SK_{1}(R).
By

(
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
)
,
\beta_{n}(e_{1}\sigma,w)\in SL_{n+1}(R)\cap E(R).
By
hypothesis
(2),

[v]=[e_{1}\sigma]^{n!}=[e_{1}].
Thus
\phi
is injective.

Homomorphism:
Let
v,w\in Um_{n+1}(R)
be such that
[v]=[e_{1}\sigma]^{n!}
and
[w]=[e_{1}\tau]^{n!}
for
some matrices
\sigma,\tau\in SL_{n+1}(R).
In view of Mennicke-Newman lemma (Lemma
2.7
),
we may assume that

[e_{1}\sigma]=[a,x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]
and
[e_{1}\tau]=[b,x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}].
Now, in view of
Lemma
2.1
, we have
[e_{1}\sigma]^{n!}=[a^{n!},x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]
and

[e_{1}\tau]^{n!}=[b^{n!},x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}].
Thus by
(
[
9
, Theorem 3.16(iii)]
),
we have

[v]*[w]=[(ab)^{n!},x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}].

Now, by hypothesis
(1),
there exists
\tau^{\prime}\in SL_{n+1}(R)

be such that
[e_{1}\tau^{\prime}]=[ab,x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}].
Thus,

\displaystyle\varphi([v]*[w])

\displaystyle=[S_{n}(e_{1}\tau^{\prime},w^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=[S_{n}((ab,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),w^{\prime})].

Since
v\longmapsto S_{n}(v,w)
is a Mennicke symbol, we have

\displaystyle\varphi([v]*[w])

\displaystyle=[S_{n}((ab,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),w^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=[S_{n}((a,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),w_{1})][S_{n}((b,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),w_{2})]

\displaystyle=[S_{n}(e_{1}\sigma,w_{1})][S_{n}(e_{1}\tau,w_{2})]

\displaystyle=\varphi([v])\varphi([w]).

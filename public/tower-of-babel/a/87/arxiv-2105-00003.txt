Nu SPAN: A Proximal Average Network for Nonuniform Sparse Model — Application to Seismic Reflectivity Inversion
Swapnil Mache, Praveen Kumar Pokala, Kusala Rajendran, and Chandra Sekhar Seelamantula

Abstract
We solve the problem of sparse signal deconvolution in the context of seismic reflectivity inversion, which pertains to high-resolution recovery of the subsurface reflection coefficients. Our formulation employs a nonuniform, non-convex synthesis sparse model comprising a combination of convex and non-convex regularizers, which results in accurate approximations of the ℓ 0 \ell_{0} pseudo-norm. The resulting iterative algorithm requires the proximal average strategy. When unfolded, the iterations give rise to a learnable proximal average network architecture that can be optimized in a data-driven fashion. We demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed approach through numerical experiments on synthetic 1 1 -D seismic traces and 2 2 -D wedge models in comparison with the benchmark techniques. We also present validations considering the simulated Marmousi 2 2 model as well as real 3 3 -D seismic volume data acquired from the Penobscot 3 3 D survey off the coast of Nova Scotia, Canada.

We propose an optimization framework for seismic reflectivity inversion based on a composite sparse-prior, which comprises multiple weighted regularizers. The weights are allowed to be different for each component. Such a model is commonly referred to as a

II
Non-convex penalties and their weighted counterparts

The
\ell_{1}
penalty (Fig.
2
), which is the convex relaxation of the
\ell_{0}
penalty, is the most popular one for promoting sparsity while solving sparse linear inverse problems, owing to its convexity-preserving attribute, making the optimization problem computationally more tractable than
\ell_{0}
or non-convex regularization. However,
\ell_{1}
-regularization results in a biased estimate. In particular, it over-penalizes large-valued coefficients due to the constant shrinkage parameter. Non-convex penalties such as the minimax concave penalty (MCP) (Fig.
3
) and smoothly clipped absolute deviation (SCAD) (Fig.
4
) result in nearly unbiased estimates compared to the
\ell_{1}
penalty. Non-convex regularization overcomes the limitation of
\ell_{1}
, but the resulting optimization problem is quite challenging. Proximal operators are central to developing proximal gradient-descent methods for optimizing non-convex, nonsmooth sparse linear inverse problems. Further, the resulting optimization problem is solved via proximal methods since proximal operators of MCP and SCAD are computationally simple
[
18
,
23
]
. The proximal operator
\mathcal{P}_{g}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
associated with the penalty
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{g}(y,\theta_{g})=\arg\,\min_{x}\frac{1}{2}(x-y)^{2}+\lambda g(x),

(2)

where
\theta_{g}
denotes the parameters of
g
and
\lambda>0
.

\ell_{1}
penalty and its corresponding proximal operator, the soft-thresholding operator.

The scaled
\ell_{1}
penalty is defined as

g_{1}(x;\lambda)=\lambda\left|x\right|,

(3)

where the weight factor
\lambda
allows for adjusting the threshold value. The MCP
g_{2}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is defined as

\displaystyle g_{2}(x;\mu,\gamma)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle\mu|x|-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2\gamma},&|x|\leq\mu\gamma,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{2}\gamma}{2},&|x|\geq\mu\gamma,\end{array}\right.

(4)

where
\mu>0,\gamma>1
. The SCAD penalty
g_{3}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is defined as

\displaystyle g_{3}(x;\nu,a)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\nu|x|,&|x|\leq\nu,\\
\displaystyle\frac{2\nu a|x|-|x|^{2}-\nu^{2}}{2(a-1)},&\nu<|x|\leq\nu a,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\nu^{2}(a+1)}{2},&|x|>\nu a,\end{array}\right.

(5)

where

. The penalties used in this study and their proximal operators are shown in Table

III
Proximal Average Network for Reflectivity Inversion — Nonuniform Sparse Model (Type-
1
)

The sparse reflection coefficients
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are recovered from the observed noisy seismic trace
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
via optimization of the following composite-regularized cost:

\begin{split}\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{x},\,\{\omega_{i}\}}\,\Big\{\mathcal{J(\boldsymbol{x})}=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}{\left\|\boldsymbol{h}*\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\right\|}_{2}^{2}}_{f(\boldsymbol{x})}+\underbrace{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{i}{g_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})}}_{g(\boldsymbol{x})}\Big\}\\
\text{subject to}\,\,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\omega_{i}=1},0<\omega_{i}<1\;\forall{i},\end{split}

(6)

where
m
is the number of regularizers, and
\{\omega_{i}\}
are the weights assigned to the regularizers.

The composite objective function
\mathcal{J}
in (
6
), regularizer
g
and data-fidelity term
f
satisfy the following properties, which are essential for minimizing
\mathcal{J}
,

P1.

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\left(-\infty,\infty\right]
is proper, closed, and
L
-smooth, i.e.,
\|\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x})-\nabla f(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{2}\leq L\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}
.

P2.

g
is lower semi-continuous.

P3.

\mathcal{J}
is bounded from below, i.e., inf
\mathcal{J}>-\infty
.

Given a set of training samples
\{\boldsymbol{y}_{p},\boldsymbol{x}_{p}\},\,\,\forall p\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,N\}
, our objective is to design a deep-unrolled network that solves the optimization problem stated in Eq. (
6
).

Our algorithm is referred to as the nonuniform proximal-averaged thresholding algorithm (
Nu
PATA-
1
), which relies on Majorization-Minimization (MM)
[

]
and the proximal average strategy
[

,
27
,
43
]
. Further, we unfold the

PATA-
1

Nu
SPAN-
1
).

1

Due to P1, there exists
\eta<1/L
such that

is upper bounded locally by a quadratic expansion about
\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}
as follows:

f(\boldsymbol{x})\leq Q\left(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}\right),

(7)

where
Q(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})=f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})+\langle\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}),\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}\|_{2}^{2}

\mathcal{J}
at
\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}
is

\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\underbrace{Q(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})+g(\boldsymbol{x})}_{H(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})}.

(8)

The update for
\boldsymbol{x}
at the
(k+1)^{\text{th}}
iteration is given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\arg\,\min_{\boldsymbol{x}}H(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}),

\displaystyle=\arg\,\min_{\boldsymbol{x}}\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\|\boldsymbol{x}-\left(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}-\eta\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+g(\boldsymbol{x}).

(9)

The above problem does not have a closed-form solution. Hence, we consider an approximate variant of the problem corresponding to Eq. (
9
) based on the proximal average strategy:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{x}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\omega_{i}}{2\eta}{\left\|\boldsymbol{x}-\left(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}-\eta\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})\right)\right\|}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\omega_{i}g_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})},

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{i}\mathcal{P}_{g_{i}}(\mathbf{u}^{(k)}),

(10)

where
\mathbf{u}^{(k)}=\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}-\eta\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f(\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})

is the flipped version of
\boldsymbol{h}

1

\boldsymbol{x}^{(k+1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{i}\mathcal{P}_{g_{i}}(\mathbf{W}\boldsymbol{y}+\mathbf{S}\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}),

(11)

where
\mathbf{W}=(1/L)\mathop{\boldsymbol{h}^{\prime}}
and
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{I}-(1/L)\mathop{\boldsymbol{h}^{\prime}*\boldsymbol{h}}

[
28
]
are initialized as Toeplitz matrices. In the learning stage, they are dense and unstructured. Given training data that consists of a large number of independent and identically distributed examples
\{\boldsymbol{x}_{p},\boldsymbol{y}_{p}\}_{p=1}^{N}
and
Nu
SPAN-
1
with a fixed number of layers, we optimize the smooth
\ell_{1}

\boldsymbol{x}
and the prediction

\displaystyle\text{CC}=\frac{\langle{\boldsymbol{x}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\rangle-\langle{\boldsymbol{x}},{{\boldsymbol{1}}}\rangle\langle\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}},{{\boldsymbol{1}}}\rangle}{\sqrt{\left(\left\|{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-{\langle{\boldsymbol{x}},{{\boldsymbol{1}}}\rangle}^{2}\right)\left(\left\|\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-{\langle\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}},{{\boldsymbol{1}}}\rangle}^{2}\right)}},

VI
Conclusions

We considered the problem of seismic reflectivity inversion based on systematically learned non-convex sparse-prior. We proposed a nonuniform sparse model based on composite regularization and solved it based on a deep-unrolled architecture for prior learning given training data. Using the proposed framework, we solved the problem of seismic reflectivity inversion, where the challenge lies in improving the resolution to characterize the subsurface. The proposed techniques outperform benchmark reconstructions given by state-of-the-art techniques on synthetic, simulated, and real datasets in terms of objective metrics.

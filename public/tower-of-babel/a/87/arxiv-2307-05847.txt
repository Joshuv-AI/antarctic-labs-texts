Large deviations of conservative stochastic partial differential equations
Ping Chen, Tusheng Zhang

Abstract




Abstract:

In this paper, we establish a large deviation principle for the conservative stochastic partial differential equations, whose solutions are related to stochastic differential equations with interaction. The weak convergence method and the contraction principle in the theory of large deviations play an important role.

Key Words:

Large deviation principle; conservative stochastic partial differential equations; stochastic differential equations with interaction; weak convergence method.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification:
Primary 60H15, 60F10; Secondary 60G57.

1
Introduction

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
be fixed. In this paper, we study the small noise large deviation principle (LDP) of conservative stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs), which is written as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}&=\frac{1}{2}D^{2}:(A(t,\cdot,\mu_{t})\mu_{t})\mathrm{d}t-\nabla\cdot(V(t,\cdot,\mu_{t})\mu_{t})\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 8.50012pt-\int_{\Theta}\nabla\cdot(G(t,\cdot,\mu_{t},\theta)\mu_{t})W(\mathrm{d}\theta,\mathrm{d}t),\end{split}

(1.1)

where
\{W_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is a cylindrical Wiener process on some
L^{2}
space
L^{2}(\Theta,\vartheta)
defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. For the precise conditions on
A
,
V
and
G
, we refer the readers to Section
2
.

Conservative SPDEs arise as fluctuating continuum models for interacting particle system
[
11
]
, and have been used to describe the hydrodynamic large deviations of simple exclusion and zero range particle processes, see, for instance,
[
15
]
and
[
19
]
. In
[
8
]
, the authors obtained the well-posedness of conservative SPDEs with correlated noise. In
[
17
]
, the authors adopted a duality argument to establish the well-posedness of measure-valued solutions to the nonlinear, nonlocal stochastic Fokker-Planck equations. Additionally, motivated from fluid dynamics in vorticity form, the case of signed measure-valued solutions has been considered in
[
10
,
3
,
20
]
and the references therein. In a recent work
[
9
]
, the authors showed that under sufficiently nice initial distribution
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and appropriate conditions on the coefficients, the each solution to (
1.1
) was given as a superposition of solutions to the following stochastic differential equation (SDE) with interaction:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}X_{t}(x)&=V(t,X_{t}(x),\bar{\mu}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\Theta}G(t,X_{t}(x),\bar{\mu}_{t},\theta)W(\mathrm{d}\theta,\mathrm{d}t),\\
X_{0}(x)&=x,\hskip 8.50012pt\bar{\mu}_{t}=\mu_{0}\circ(X_{t}(\cdot))^{-1},\hskip 8.50012ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\hskip 8.50012ptt\in[0,T].\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

SDEs with interaction have been widely studied since the work by Dorogovtsev in
[
1
]
. In
[
18
]
, the authors studied the limit behavior of solutions to SDEs with interaction in one-dimensional case. This has been extended to two-demensional case in
[
16
]
. In
[
7
]
, the authors proved a Stroock-Varadhan-type support theorem for stochastic flows generated by SDEs with interaction. In
[
13
]
, the authors established the well-posedness of backward stochastic differential equations. We also refer to readers to
[
14
,
2
]
and references therein for further related works.

Large deviations principles (LDP) provide exponential estimates for the probability of the rare events in terms of some explicit rate function.
It has wide applications in statistics, statistic mechanics, stochastic dynamical systems, financial mathematics etc. The early framework was introduced by Varadhan in
[
22
]
, in which the small noise (also called Freidlin-Wentzell) LDP for finite dimensional diffusion processes were studied. The literature on large deviations is huge, and a list of them can be found, e.g., in
[
21
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish a Freidlin-Wentzell type LDP for the conservative SPDE (
1.1
). To obtain the LDP of the solutions, we first establish the LDP of the associated SDEs with interaction (
1.2
) and then use the contraction principle in the theory of large deviations. We will adopt the weak convergence method in large deviations introduced in
[
4
]
. More precisely, we will use the more convenient sufficient conditions given in the paper
[
6
]
.

The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the basic notations and assumptions, recall well-posedness results for the SDE with interaction (
1.2
) and conservative SPDE (
1.1
). Moreover, we characterize the trajectory space of the solutions of the SDEs with interaction (
1.2
). The trajectory space will be the space on which the large deviation is established. In Section
3
, we present the main results of the paper, and then outline how the results will be proved. Section
4
and Section
5
are devoted to the proof of the LDP for the SDE with interaction (
1.2
).

, we get (
4.10
).

Next, we prove that

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|x|\geq M}\frac{|Y^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)-Z^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)|^{m}}{(1+|x|^{1+\delta})^{m}}]=0.

(4.12)

By Lemma
4.1
, using an argument similar to that proving Lemma
2.1
shows that there exists a constant

C_{L,m,T,N,d}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}

\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{|x|\in[k,k+1)}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)-Z^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)|^{m}]\leq C_{L,m,T,N,d}(1+k)^{m}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|x|\geq M}\frac{|Y^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)-Z^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)|^{m}}{(1+|x|^{1+\delta})^{m}}]

(4.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{|x|\geq M}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{|Y^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)-Z^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)|^{m}}{(1+|x|^{1+\delta})^{m}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{k\geq M}\sup_{|x|\in[k,k+1)}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{|Y^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)-Z^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)|^{m}}{(1+|x|^{1+\delta})^{m}}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{|x|\in[k,k+1)}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{|Y^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)-Z^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)|^{m}}{(1+|x|^{1+\delta})^{m}}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}\frac{\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{|x|\in[k,k+1)}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)-Z^{\epsilon}_{t}(x)|^{m}]}{(1+k^{1+\delta})^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}\frac{C_{L,m,T,N,d}(1+k)^{m}}{(1+k^{1+\delta})^{m}}\leq C_{L,m,T,N,d}\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{{\delta}m}}.

Since
{\delta}m>1
, letting
M\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.13
), we get (
4.12
).

Now, we are in the position to complete the proof of the Proposition.
By (
4.9
) and (
4.12
), letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, and then letting
M\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.8
), we obtain

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}[\|Y^{\epsilon}-Z^{\epsilon}\|_{\infty,T}^{m}]=0.

The proof is complete.

∎

5
Verification of (LDP2)

In this section, we verify (LDP2), namely, we will prove the following result.

Proposition 5.1

Let Assumption
2.2
hold. For every
N<+\infty
and any family
\{h^{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}\subset\mathcal{H}^{N}
that converges weakly to some element
h
in
\mathcal{H}^{N}
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
,
X^{h^{\epsilon}}
converges to
X^{h}

in the space
C([0,T],C_{\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
X^{h}
solve equation (
3.2
), and
X^{h^{\epsilon}}
solve equation (
3.2
) with
h
replaced by
h^{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

For every
\epsilon>0
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
, using an argument similar to that proving (
4.8
) shows that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|}{1+|x|^{1+\delta}}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|x|\geq M}\frac{|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|}{1+|x|^{1+\delta}}\vee\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|x|<M}\frac{|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|}{1+|x|^{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon>0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|x|\geq M}\frac{|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|}{1+|x|^{1+\delta}}\vee\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|x|<M}|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}^{M}\vee I_{2}^{M,\epsilon}.

We first verify that

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}I_{1}^{M}=0.

(5.2)

By
(ii)
in Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}^{M}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|\geq M}\frac{C_{L,T,N}(1+|x|)}{1+|x|^{1+\delta}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{L,T,N}}{M^{\delta}}.

So letting
M\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain (
5.2
).

Next, we verify that for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}I_{2}^{M,\epsilon}=0.

(5.4)

By
(iv)
in Lemma
4.1
and using a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we only need to show that
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|=0.

(5.5)

For every
\epsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by Assumption
2.2
, Hölder inequality and the fact that

\{h^{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}\subset\mathcal{H}^{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|^{2}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3\Big\{(\int_{0}^{T}|V(s,X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s}(x),\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s})-V(s,X^{h}_{s}(x),\mu^{h}_{s})|\mathrm{d}s)^{2}

\displaystyle+(\int_{0}^{T}|\int_{\Theta}(G(s,X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s}(x),\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s},\theta)-G(s,X^{h}_{s}(x),\mu^{h}_{s},\theta))\cdot h^{\epsilon}(\theta,s)\vartheta(\mathrm{d}\theta)|\mathrm{d}s)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Theta}G(s,X^{h}_{s}(x),\mu^{h}_{s},\theta)\cdot(h^{\epsilon}(\theta,s)-h(\theta,s))\vartheta(\mathrm{d}\theta)\mathrm{d}s|^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3T\int_{0}^{T}|V(s,X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s}(x),\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s})-V(s,X^{h}_{s}(x),\mu^{h}_{s})|^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+3(\int_{0}^{T}\||G(s,X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s}(x),\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s},\cdot)-G(s,X^{h}_{s}(x),\mu^{h}_{s},\cdot)|\|_{\vartheta}^{2}\mathrm{d}s)\cdot(\int_{0}^{T}\|h^{\epsilon}(\cdot,s)\|_{\vartheta}^{2}\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle+3\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Theta}G(s,X^{h}_{s}(x),\mu^{h}_{s},\theta)\cdot(h^{\epsilon}(\theta,s)-h(\theta,s))\vartheta(\mathrm{d}\theta)\mathrm{d}s|^{2}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{L,T,N}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{r\in[0,s]}|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{r}(x)-X^{h}_{r}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s+C_{L,T,N}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s},\mu^{h}_{s})\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+3\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Theta}G(s,X^{h}_{s}(x),\mu^{h}_{s},\theta)\cdot(h^{\epsilon}(\theta,s)-h(\theta,s))\vartheta(\mathrm{d}\theta)\mathrm{d}s|^{2}.

For each
\epsilon>0
,
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

F_{\epsilon}(t,x):=|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Theta}G(s,X^{h}_{s}(x),\mu^{h}_{s},\theta)\cdot(h^{\epsilon}(\theta,s)-h(\theta,s))\vartheta(\mathrm{d}\theta)\mathrm{d}s|.

Then applying Gronwall’s lemma to (
5.6
) yields

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|^{2}\leq C_{L,T,N}(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s},\mu^{h}_{s})\mathrm{d}s+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}F_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x)).

(5.7)

Using the definition of the Wasserstein distance, we further estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t},\mu^{h}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|^{2}\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|^{2}\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{L,T,N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{s},\mu^{h}_{s})\mathrm{d}s+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}F_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x))\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{L,T,N}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{r},\mu^{h}_{r})\mathrm{d}s+C_{L,T,N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}F_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x)\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}x).

Hence, by Gronwall’s lemma

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t},\mu^{h}_{t})\leq C_{L,T,N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}F_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x)\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}x).

Combing the inequality above with (
5.7
), we obtain

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|^{2}\leq C_{L,T,N}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}F_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x)\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}x)+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}F_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x)).

(5.8)

Now, we prove that the right-hand side of the above inequality tends to
0
as
\epsilon
tending to
0
.
By Remark
2.1
, the definition of the Wasserstein distance and
(ii)
in Lemma
4.1
, we get that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\||G(t,X^{h}_{t}(x),\mu^{h}_{t},\cdot)|\|_{\vartheta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{L}(1+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{h}_{t}(x)|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu^{h}_{t},\delta_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{L}(1+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{h}_{t}(x)|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{h}_{t}(x)|^{2}\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{L,T,N}(1+|x|^{2}),

which implies that
|G(t,X^{h}_{t}(x),\mu^{h}_{t},\theta)|\in L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}(\Theta,\vartheta))
and for any
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle|F_{\epsilon}(t,x)-F_{\epsilon}(s,x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Theta}G(r,X^{h}_{r}(x),\mu^{h}_{r},\theta)\cdot(h^{\epsilon}(\theta,r)-h(\theta,r))\vartheta(\mathrm{d}\theta)\mathrm{d}r|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\int_{s}^{t}\||G(r,X^{h}_{r}(x),\mu^{h}_{r},\cdot)|\|_{\vartheta}^{2}\mathrm{d}r)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\int_{0}^{T}\|h^{\epsilon}(\cdot,r)-h(\cdot,r)\|_{\vartheta}^{2}\mathrm{d}r)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{L,T,N}(1+|x|)(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we have used the facts that
h^{\epsilon}
,
h\in\mathcal{H}^{N}
in the last step. In combination with the fact that
h^{\epsilon}
converges to
h
weakly in
L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}(\Theta,\vartheta))
, it follows that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}F_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x)=0.

(5.9)

Moreover, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}F_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x)\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}x)=0.

(5.10)

Therefore, by (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
5.8
), we get (
5.5
).

Finally, combing with (
5.2
) and (
5.4
), letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, and then letting
M\rightarrow\infty
in (
5.1
), we obtain

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|X^{h^{\epsilon}}_{t}(x)-X^{h}_{t}(x)|}{1+|x|^{1+\delta}}=0,

completing the proof.

∎

Haibin Wang, Guoyi Xu

Abstract
. We prove the sharp lower bound of the first Neumann eigenvalue for bounded convex planar domain in term of its diameter and width. Mathematics Subject Classification: 34B09, 35J25, 35J05.



1.
Introduction

Payne and Weinberger
[
18
]
firstly showed that for any convex domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with fixed diameter
=2
, its first Neumann eigenvalue
\mu_{1}(\Omega)
is at least
\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}
. Their method is by integral estimates and geometric cutting of the region, one key is the eigenvalue estimate for weighted ODE corresponding the region
\Omega
.

Li
[
15
]
and Li-Yau
[
14
]
applied the gradient estimates of eigenfunctions to study the lower bound of the first Neumann (Dirichlet) eigenvalue on manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature. Through a delicate refined gradient estimate of eigenfunctions, Zhong-Yang
[
23
]
firstly obtain the sharp lower bound of the first eigenvalue for compact Riemannian manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature. Zhong-Yang’s type gradient estimate of eigenfunction is closely related to the solution of suitable ODE (also see
[
12
]
). There is another proof the sharp lower bound of the first eigenvalue by two-points method in
[
2
]
.

Hang-Wang
[
7
]
firstly showed that
\mu_{1}(\Omega)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}
if and only if
\Omega
is isometric to
[-1,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. The sharp lower bound of the first Neumann eigenvalue and its rigidity later is obtained through optimal transport
[
11
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

We define the width of
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Width}(\Omega)=\min_{v\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{diam}(P_{v,t}\cap\Omega),

where
P_{v,t}\vcentcolon=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(x-tv)\cdot v=0\}
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(P_{v,t}\cap\Omega)\vcentcolon=\max_{x,y\in P_{v,t}\cap\Omega}|x-y|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

Furthermore, we define the projective width of
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{v}(\Omega)\vcentcolon=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot v=0,x=y+s\cdot v\ \text{for some}\ y\in\Omega,s\in\mathbb{R}\},\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n};

\displaystyle\mathscr{PW}(\Omega)=\min_{v\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\mathrm{diam}(\mathscr{P}_{v}(\Omega)),

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(\mathscr{P}_{v}(\Omega))\vcentcolon=\max_{x,y\in\mathscr{P}_{v}(\Omega)}|x-y|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

The main result of this paper is the following Theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

There are universal constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, such that for any convex domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)=2
and
\mathrm{Width}(\Omega)\in(0,C_{1})
; we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\Omega)\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}+C_{2}\cdot\mathrm{Width}(\Omega)^{2}.

Remark 1.3
.

If
\mathrm{Width}(\Omega)\geq C_{1}
, by compactness argument and the rigidity result from
[
7
]
, we can get (
1.1
). Therefore, the above Theorem confirms a conjecture of Fengbo Hang and Xiaodong Wang
[
7
]
(also see
[
20
]
).

In fact (
1.1
) can be obtained for convex domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)=2
, by compactness argument and the rigidity in
[
7
]
. The main contribution of this paper is a quantitative argument for (
1.1
) when the width is uniformly small with respect to the diameter of
\Omega
.

The order of width is sharp as
\mathrm{Width}(\Omega)\to 0
. We choose
\Omega=[0,\sqrt{4-\epsilon^{2}}]\times[0,\epsilon]
, then
\mu_{1}(\Omega)-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4(4-\epsilon^{2})}
.

Remark 1.4
.

The results of this paper are part of the first author’s Ph.D thesis. During the process of writing this paper, Amato, Bucur, Fragalà had posted their paper
[
1
]
on arXiv. Among other things, the above theorem is proved in a more general context in
[
1
]
by different method.

For the study of lower bound of the first Dirichlet eigenvalue, Hersch
[
8
]
showed
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\cdot\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\geq 0
, where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a convex region,
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\Omega
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)\vcentcolon=\sup_{x\in\Omega}d(x,\partial\Omega)
is the inscribed radius of
\Omega
.

Later, for convex region
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Mendez-Hernandez
[
17
]
(also see Protter
[
19
]
) gave a proof of the inequality

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\cdot\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\geq\frac{(n-1)\pi^{2}}{4}\cdot\Big(\frac{\mathrm{IR}(\Omega)}{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}\Big)^{2}.

For non-collapsing model with fixed volume, the sharp quantitative form of Faber-Krahn inequality is established in
[
3
]
.

For convex domain
\Omega\subset{\bf\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, Jerison
[
9
]
proved that the comparison inequality between the first two Dirichlet eigenvalues and the first two Dirichlet eigenvalues of corresponding weighted ODE, and some geometry invariant of the convex domain is involved in the inequalities. Also see
[
6
]
for a survey on the study of Dirichlet eigenvalues on thin tubes.

For Neumann eigenvalue of convex domain
\Omega\subset{\bf\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, Jerison
[
10
]
showed
the inequality between the first Neumann eigenvalues of
\Omega
and the first Neumann eigenvalue of corresponding weighted ODE. His method is again the comparison to the ordinary differential equation, which originates from
[
18
]
. Later Jerison’s result is generalized to higher dimension in
[
4
]
by discretizing the Neumann eigenfunction of Laplace operator. Zheng used the cross-sectional average of the eigenfunction, which can be viewed as continuous version of argument in
[
4
]
, to develop the refined comparison in
[
22
]
. Our argument of Theorem
1.2
follows this philosophy of comparison.

The first new thing we obtain is the sharp estimate of Neumann eigenvalue of the weighted ODE in term of the weight
h
, which is implied in the proof of Theorem
10.1
. It is a consequence of Liouville transform and the estimate of the rest term
\int_{0}^{d}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}(\zeta^{\prime})^{2}
, where
\zeta
is the corresponding Neumann eigenfunction of the weighted ODE. To obtain the estimate of the rest term, in Section
3
, we get the comparison estimate of
\zeta^{\prime}(x)
from the weighted ODE, which behaves like
\|x\|
after detailed analysis. Then the estimate of
\int_{0}^{d}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}(\zeta^{\prime})^{2}
is reduced to
\int_{0}^{d}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}
, which is obtained in Lemma
4.2
. The key Lemma
4.2
contributes the estimate of the major second term in the lower bound of Neumann eigenvalue, which relies on the fixed diameter and convex property of
\Omega
.

The second technical tool we establish, is the estimate of the error term
\eta
relating the PDE and weighted ODE, which is obtained in Section
5
. This estimate needs the refined gradient estimate for Neumann eigenfunction of
\Omega
, which is discussed in Section
2
. Then in Section
6
, we use the estimate of
\eta
and the estimate of weighted ODE, to control the difference between the modified cross-sectional average Laplace operator’s first Neumann eigenfunction and the first Neumann eigenfunction of weighted ODE.

Next we obtain a novel integral estimate of vertical (perpendicular to the axis corresponding to the weighted ODE) directional derivative of Neumann eigenfunction in Section
7
, which relies on the difference estimate in Section
6
. All the above argument hold for
n
-dim
\Omega
.

Our first result which holds only for
2
-dim
\Omega
is, the equations between width and projective width, the related boundary function equations after suitable choice of the
x
-axis. Those equations are established in Section
8
.

In Section
9
we get the improved integral estimate of
\eta\tilde{u}^{\prime}
for
2
-dim
\Omega
, which is almost the difference between Neumann eigenvalues of Laplace operator and the weighted ODE. They depends on the estimates in Section
7
and equations in Section
8
.

Finally in Section
10
, we use Liouville transform and Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, to control the error terms appearing in the calculation of eigenvalue by Rayleigh quotient and prove the main theorem.

2.
The refined gradient estimates

Notation 2.1
.

We define a special norm for

\displaystyle\|x\|\vcentcolon=\min\{x,d-x\},\quad\quad\quad\forall x\in[0,d].

For the first Neumann eigenfunction

\displaystyle-1\leq-k=\min u\leq u\leq 1=\max u,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}|Du|\leq\sqrt{\mu_{1}(\Omega)}\cdot\sqrt{1-u^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\mu_{1}(\Omega)}.

Recall the following formula on the second line of

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot 2\geq\pi+\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{(1-k)^{2}}{(1+k)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\psi^{2}(\theta)d\theta,

If

In the rest argument, we always assume that

There is a universal constant

Step (1)

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}=\Omega\cap\{x<s_{0}\},\quad\quad\quad u(x_{0})=\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega_{1}}}u(x).

3.
The weighted ODE

Define

The integral estimate of vertical directional derivatives

Lemma 7.1
.

There is a universal constant

Proof:

Then using Proposition
6.3
and (

), we have

2.2
and Lemma
6.1

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,\epsilon]\cup[d-\epsilon,d]}dx\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u_{x}^{2}|+|\zeta^{\prime 2}|+\int_{[\epsilon,d-\epsilon]}dx\int_{\Omega_{x}}|u_{x}^{2}-\zeta^{\prime 2}|

6.3

and

The inequality of eigenvalue and width

Theorem 10.1
.

There are universal constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, such that for any convex domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)=2
and
\mathrm{Width}(\Omega)\in(0,C_{1})
; we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\Omega)\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}+C_{2}\cdot\mathrm{Width}(\Omega)^{2}.

Remark 10.2
.

The transform
w=\sqrt{h}\zeta^{\prime}
is called Liouville transformation
[
16
]
.

Proof:

Step (1)
. Assume
10^{-2}<\tau_{0}<d-10^{-2}
, note that h is concave, then

\displaystyle\int_{10^{-2}}^{\tau_{0}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx\leq\frac{C}{\ep}\int_{10^{-2}}^{\tau_{0}}(h^{\prime})^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\ep}(h^{\prime}(10^{-2}))^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{10^{-2}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx\geq\int_{10^{-2}/2}^{10^{-2}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx\geq\frac{C}{\ep}\int_{10^{-2}/2}^{10^{-2}}(h^{\prime})^{2}\geq\frac{C}{\ep}(h^{\prime}(10^{-2}))^{2}

So we get
\int_{10^{-2}}^{\tau_{0}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{0}^{10^{-2}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx
.

Similarily we have
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{d-10^{-2}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{d-10^{-2}}^{d}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx
. Hence we get

(10.1)

\displaystyle\int_{[0,10^{-2}]\cup[d-10^{-2},d]}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx\geq C\cdot\int_{0}^{d}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx.

If
\tau_{0}>d-10^{-2}
,then

\displaystyle\int_{10^{-2}}^{d-10^{-2}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx\leq\frac{C}{\ep}\int_{10^{-2}}^{d-10^{-2}}(h^{\prime})^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\ep}(h^{\prime}(10^{-2}))^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{10^{-2}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx.

If
\tau_{0}<10^{-2}
, then
\int_{10^{-2}}^{d-10^{-2}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{d-10^{-2}}^{d}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\cdot\|x\|^{2}dx
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=[0,10^{-2}]\cup[d-10^{-2},d]
in the rest argument. From (
10.1
), Lemma
6.2
and Proposition
9.2
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}(N)-\frac{\int_{0}^{d}|\eta\tilde{u}^{\prime}|dx}{\int_{0}^{d}h\tilde{u}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}(N)-C\frac{\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big\{V(\Omega)\int_{0}^{d}\frac{|h^{\prime}|^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\int_{0}^{d}h\zeta^{2}}-C\epsilon^{3}

(10.2)

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}(N)-C\frac{\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big\{V(\Omega)\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{|h^{\prime}|^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\int_{0}^{d}h\zeta^{2}}-C\epsilon^{3}.

Step (2)
. From Lemma
6.1
, there is a universal constant
C>0
such that

(10.3)

\displaystyle|\zeta^{\prime}|(x)\geq C\|x\|,\quad\quad\forall x\in[0,10^{-2}]\cup[d-10^{-2},d].

Let
w=\sqrt{h}\zeta^{\prime}
, from
-(h\zeta^{\prime})^{\prime}=\mu_{1}(N)\cdot h\zeta
, we have

\displaystyle w^{\prime\prime}=-\mu_{1}(N)w+(\frac{3}{4}(\frac{h^{\prime}}{h})^{2}-\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{2h})w,\quad\quad and\quad\quad w(0)=w(d)=0.

Also we get

(10.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(N)=\frac{\int_{0}^{d}(w^{\prime}(t))^{2}+V\cdot w^{2}dt}{\int_{0}^{d}w(t)^{2}dt},\quad\quad V\vcentcolon=(\frac{3}{4}(\frac{h^{\prime}}{h})^{2}-\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{2h}).

By
h^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
, from (
10.2
), (
10.3
) and (
10.4
), using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\Omega)\geq\frac{\int_{0}^{d}(w^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h^{2}}\cdot w^{2}dt}{\int_{0}^{d}w^{2}dt}-C\frac{\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big\{V(\Omega)\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{|h^{\prime}|^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\int_{0}^{d}h\zeta^{2}}-C\epsilon^{3}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{d^{2}}+\frac{\int_{0}^{d}\frac{3}{4}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}(\zeta^{\prime})^{2}dx-C\cdot\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big\{V(\Omega)\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{|h^{\prime}|^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\int_{0}^{d}h(\zeta^{\prime})^{2}}-C\epsilon^{3}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{d^{2}}+\frac{C_{1}\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx-C\cdot\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big\{V(\Omega)\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{|h^{\prime}|^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\int_{0}^{d}h(\zeta^{\prime})^{2}}-C\epsilon^{3}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{d^{2}}+\frac{\frac{C_{1}}{2}\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx-C_{2}\cdot\epsilon^{2+\frac{1}{2}}V(\Omega)}{\int_{0}^{d}h(\zeta^{\prime})^{2}}-C\epsilon^{3}

(10.5)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{d^{2}}+C\frac{\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{|h^{\prime}|^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx}{V(\Omega)}-C\epsilon^{2+\frac{1}{2}}.

Step (3)
. If
d\leq 2-10^{-6n}\cdot\epsilon^{2}
, then the conclusion follows from (
10.5
). In the rest argument, we will assume
d\geq 2-10^{-6n}\cdot\epsilon^{2}
.

Similar to (
10.1
), we have

(10.6)

\displaystyle\int_{[0,10^{-2}]}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx\geq C\cdot\int_{[0,10^{-1}]}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx.

Therefore from Lemma
4.2
and (
10.6
), we get

(10.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx\geq C\cdot\int_{[0,10^{-1}]}\frac{(h^{\prime})^{2}}{h}\|x\|^{2}dx\geq CV(\Omega)\epsilon^{2}.

Putting (
10.7
) into (
10.5
), we get

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\Omega)\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}+\frac{CV(\Omega)\epsilon^{2}}{V(\Omega)}-C\epsilon^{2+\frac{1}{2}}\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}+C\epsilon^{2}.

∎

Appendix A
The eigenvalue and Rayleigh quotient

Lemma A.1
.

There exists
\varphi\in H-\{0\}
such that
\mathscr{R}(\varphi)=\min\limits_{f\in H-\{0\}}\mathscr{R}(f)
. Furthermore,
\varphi
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}\varphi^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}hdt=\mathscr{R}(\varphi)\int_{0}^{d}\varphi\cdot vhdt,\quad\quad\forall v\in W^{1,2}(0,d).

Proof.

Step (1)
. Denote
\mu=\min\limits_{f\in H-\{0\}}\mathscr{R}(f)=\frac{\int_{0}^{d}h(f^{\prime})^{2}}{\int_{0}^{d}hf^{2}}
. First assume
h\geq c>0
, choose
\varphi_{k}\in H
such that
\mathscr{R}(\varphi_{k})\to\mu,\mathscr{R}(\varphi_{k})\leq\mu+1
. Let
f_{k}=\frac{\varphi_{k}}{||\varphi_{k}||_{L^{2}(0,d)}}
. Then
||f_{k}||_{L^{2}}=1
, and

\int_{0}^{d}(f_{k}^{\prime})^{2}\leq\frac{1}{c}\int_{0}^{d}h(f_{k}^{\prime})^{2}\leq\frac{\mu+1}{c}\int_{0}^{d}h(f_{k})^{2}\leq C

By Sobolev Compactness Theorem and and Banach-Alaoglu Theorem, there exists
G\in L^{2},g\in L^{2}
, and
\{f_{n_{k}}\}\subset\{f_{k}\}
.

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|f_{n_{k}}-G|_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{d}(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}-g)v=0,\quad\forall v\in L^{2}

For any
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}-Gv^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{d}\lim_{k\to\infty}-f_{n_{k}}v^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{d}\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}v=\int_{0}^{d}gv.

Thus
G^{\prime}=g
and
G\in H-\{0\}
.

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\int h(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime})^{2}}{\int h(f_{n_{k}})^{2}}\geq\frac{\varliminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{d}h(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime})^{2}}{\int hG^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varliminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{d}h(G^{\prime})^{2}+2hG^{\prime}(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}-G^{\prime})}{\int hG^{2}}=\frac{\int_{0}^{d}h(G^{\prime})^{2}}{\int hG^{2}}

Let
0=\frac{d}{dt}\mathscr{R}(G+tv)\big|_{t=0}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}G^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}hdt=\mu\int_{0}^{d}G\cdot vhdt,\quad\quad\forall v\in H-\{0\}.

Note that the above equality holds for
v=c,c\in{\bf\mathbb{R}}
. Assume
f\in W^{1,2}(0,d)
and
f
is not constant function, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}G^{\prime}\cdot f^{\prime}hdt=\int_{0}^{d}G^{\prime}\cdot(f-\int_{0}^{d}hf)^{\prime}hdt=\mu\int_{0}^{d}G\cdot(f-\int_{0}^{d}hf)hdt=\mu\int_{0}^{d}G\cdot fhdt.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}G^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}hdt=\mu\int_{0}^{d}G\cdot vhdt,\quad\quad\forall v\in W^{1,2}(0,d).

Step (2)
. If
h\geq 0
, we choose
h_{k}=(h^{\frac{1}{n-1}}+1/k)^{n-1}
. For
h_{k}
, from
Step (1)
, we can find
G_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}G_{k}^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}h_{k}dt=\mu(k)\int_{0}^{d}G_{k}\cdot vh_{k}dt,\quad\quad\forall v\in W^{1,2}(0,d).

By regularity theory, we have
G_{k}\in C^{\infty}[0,d]
. Choose
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,d)
, we get
(h_{k}G_{k}^{\prime})^{\prime}+\mu(k)h_{k}G_{k}=0
. We assume
\sup_{x\in[0,d]}|G_{k}|=1,h^{\prime}(\tau_{0})=0
and note
h_{k}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
is concave, then

(A.1)

\displaystyle|G_{k}^{\prime}(x)|=\begin{cases}&=|\frac{1}{h_{k}(x)}\int_{0}^{x}\mu(k)h_{k}(s)G_{k}(s)|\leq Cx,\quad x\in[0,\tau_{0}]\\
&=|\frac{1}{h_{k}(x)}\int_{x}^{d}\mu(k)h_{k}(s)G_{k}(s)|\leq C(d-x),\quad x\in[\tau_{0},d]\end{cases}

So
\sup|G_{k}|_{C^{1}[0,d]}<C
.

Since
h_{k}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
is concave, then for
x<\tau_{0}
,
h_{k}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}(h_{k}^{\frac{1}{n-1}})^{\prime}\geq x(h_{k}^{\frac{1}{n-1}})^{\prime}(x)=xh_{k}^{\prime}(x)h_{k}^{\frac{1}{n-1}-1}(x)
. We get

(A.2)

\displaystyle|\frac{h_{k}^{\prime}(x)}{h_{k}(x)}|=\begin{cases}&=|\frac{h_{k}^{\prime}(x)}{\int_{0}^{x}h_{k}^{\prime}(s)ds}|\leq|\frac{h_{k}^{\prime}(x)}{h_{k}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}h_{k}^{\prime}(s)ds}|\leq\frac{h_{k}^{\prime}(x)}{xh_{k}^{\prime}(x)}\leq\frac{1}{x},\quad x\in(0,\tau_{0}]\\
&\leq\frac{1}{d-x},\quad x\in[\tau_{0},d)\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle|G_{k}^{\prime\prime}(x)|=|-\frac{h_{k}^{\prime}(x)}{h_{k}(x)}G_{k}^{\prime}(x)-\mu(k)G_{k}(x)|\leq|\frac{h_{k}^{\prime}(x)}{h_{k}(x)}G_{k}^{\prime}(x)|+C|G_{k}(x)|\leq C.

we have
|G_{k}|_{C^{2}[0,d]}<C
. By Arzela-Ascoli theorem we can choose
G_{n_{k}}\to\varphi
in
C^{1}[0,d]
norm for some
\varphi
. For any
f\in H-\{0\}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{d}hf^{\prime 2}}{\int_{0}^{d}hf^{2}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{d}h_{k}f^{\prime 2}}{\int_{0}^{d}h_{k}f^{2}}\geq\varlimsup_{k\to\infty}\mu(k)\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{d}h_{n_{k}}G_{n_{k}}^{\prime 2}}{\int_{0}^{d}h_{n_{k}}G_{n_{k}}^{2}}=\frac{\int_{0}^{d}h\varphi^{\prime 2}}{\int_{0}^{d}h\varphi^{2}}

Thus we have
\mu=\mathscr{R}(\varphi)
. Let
0=\frac{d}{dt}\mathscr{R}(\varphi+tv)\big|_{t=0}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}\varphi^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}hdt=\mathscr{R}(\varphi)\int_{0}^{d}\varphi\cdot vhdt,\quad\quad\forall v\in H-\{0\}.

Note that the above equality holds for
v=c,c\in{\bf\mathbb{R}}
. Assume
f\in W^{1,2}(0,d)
and
f
is not constant function, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}\varphi^{\prime}\cdot f^{\prime}hdt=\int_{0}^{d}\varphi^{\prime}\cdot(f-\int_{0}^{d}hf)^{\prime}hdt=\mu\int_{0}^{d}\varphi\cdot(f-\int_{0}^{d}hf)hdt=\mu\int_{0}^{d}\varphi\cdot fhdt.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}\varphi^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}hdt=\mu\int_{0}^{d}\varphi\cdot vhdt,\quad\quad\forall v\in W^{1,2}(0,d).

∎

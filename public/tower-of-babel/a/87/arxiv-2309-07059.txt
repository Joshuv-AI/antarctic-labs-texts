Absorbing Markov Decision Processes
François Dufour, Tomás Prieto-Rumeau

Abstract
In this paper, we study discrete-time absorbing Markov Decision Processes (MDP) with measurable state space and Borel action space with a given initial distribution. For such models, solutions to the characteristic equation that are not occupation measures may exist. Several necessary and sufficient conditions are provided to guarantee that any solution to the characteristic equation is an occupation measure. Under the so-called continuity-compactness conditions, it is shown that the set of occupation measures is compact in the weak-strong topology if and only if the model is uniformly absorbing. Finally, it is shown that the occupation measures are characterized by the characteristic equation and an additional condition. Several examples are provided to illustrate our results.



Keywords:
Markov decision processes; absorbing model; occupation measures, characteristic equation, phantom mesures, compactness of the set of occupation measures

91A10, 91A15.

Introduction

In this work, we consider a discrete-time absorbing Markov Decision Process (MDP) with measurable state space
\mathbf{X}
and Borel action space
\mathbf{A}
with a given initial distribution denoted by
\eta
.
We consider general measurable state spaces to cover models that are studied in game theory, see for example
[
15
]

and
[
7
]
for an analysis of absorbing non-zero sum Markov games.
An absorbing MDP is a special type of MDP where some measurable subset
\Delta
of the state space is considered as
absorbing
and lead to termination of the process.
This means that once the system enters in
\Delta
, it remains there indefinitely without any further transitions with a null reward or cost. The underlying assumption is that for any policy, the average time to enter
\Delta
is finite. For more details on this type of MDP, we refer to the following papers and books
[
2
,
9
,
10
,
18
]
and their references therein.

The objective of this paper is to study the properties of the solution set of the characteristic equation and of the set of occupation measures.
The notion of occupation measure is particularly important and plays a central role in solving constraint Markov decision processes, see for example the references

[
2
,
6
,
13
,
14
,
17
]
. It describes the expected amount of time spends by the state and action processes
in any measurable subset of
\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}
and under any policy. Any occupation measure satisfies the so-called characteristic equation which is of the form

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathbf{X}}(\cdot)=\eta(\cdot)+\int_{X\times A}R(\cdot|x,a)\mu(dx,da)

(1)

(see equation (
3
) for a more precise statement) where
\mu^{\mathbf{X}}
is the marginal of the measure
\mu
on
\mathbf{X}
,
\eta
is the initial distribution of the MDP and
R
is a kernel depending on the transition kernel of the MDP and on the absorbing set
\Delta
.

We will show that although the model is absorbing, there may exist solutions to the characteristic equation that are not occupation measures (see example
3.4
). These are called phantom solution to the characteristic equation (or simply phantom measures).
In order to avoid this pathological phenomenon, we will provide in Theorem
3.6
several necessary and sufficient conditions to ensure that there are no phantom measures, thereby guaranteeing that any solution to the characteristic equation is an occupation measure.
By strengthening our hypotheses, that is, assuming the so-called continuity-compactness conditions introduced by Schäl in
[
19
]
(see Condition (S) below), we will show
in our second main result (see Theorem
4.9
) that the set of occupation measures is compact in the weak-strong topology (see
[
4
]
) if and only if the model is uniformly absorbing. Finally, it will be shown in our last main result (see Theorem
4.5
)
that under Condition (S) the occupation measures are characterized by the characteristic equation and an additional condition.
This is the first notable difference with the discounted model, where the occupation measures are essentially characterized by a single equation of type (
1
).

Lemma 1.1
(Disintegration lemma)

be a Polish space. Let

(2)

2
The absorbing control model

The main goal of this section is to introduce the parameters defining the model with a brief presentation of the construction of the controlled process.
We also describe the notions of (uniformly) absorbing PDM and give the definition of an occupation measure, providing a first elementary property (see lemma
2.4
).

2.1
The control model.

We consider a stationary Markov controlled process
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{A},\{\mathbf{A}(x):x\in\mathbf{X}\},Q,\eta)

consisting of:

•

A measurable state space
\mathbf{X}
endowed with a
\sigma
-algebra
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}
.

•

A Borel space
\mathbf{A}
, representing the action space.

•

A family of nonempty measurable sets
\mathbf{A}(x)\subseteq\mathbf{A}
for
x\in\mathbf{X}
. The set
\mathbf{A}(x)
gives the available actions in state
x
.
Let
\mathbf{K}=\{(x,a)\in\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}:a\in\mathbf{A}(x)\}
be the family of feasible state-action pairs. We assume that
\mathbf{K}
is a measurable subset of

\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}
endowed with the
\sigma
-algebra
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}\otimes\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbf{A})
.

•

A stochastic kernel
Q
on
\mathbf{X}
given
\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}
, which stands for the transition probability function.

•

An initial distribution given by
\eta\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbf{X})
.

Additionally, we assume that a measurable set
\Delta\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}
is given. As we shall see later in Defintion
2.2
,
\Delta
will play the role of the
absorbing set
. The so-defined control model is denoted by
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\eta,\Delta)
where we make explicit the dependence on the initial distribution and the absorbing set.

The space of admissible histories of the controlled process up to time
n\in\mathbb{N}
is denoted by
\mathbf{H}_{n}
. It is defined recursively by

\mathbf{H}_{0}=\mathbf{X}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathbf{H}_{n}=\mathbf{H}_{n-1}\times\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{X}\quad\hbox{for $n\geq 1$},

all endowed with their corresponding product
\sigma
-algebras.
A control policy
\pi
is a sequence
\{\pi_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
of stochastic kernels on
\mathbf{A}
given
\mathbf{H}_{n}
, denoted by
\pi_{n}(da|h_{n})
, such that

\pi_{n}(\mathbf{A}(x_{n})|h_{n})=1\quad\hbox{for each $n\geq 0$ and $h_{n}=(x_{0},a_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{H}_{n}$}.

The set of all policies is denoted by
\mathbf{\Pi}
.

Let us denote by
\mathbf{M}
the set of stochastic kernels
\varphi
on
\mathbf{A}
given
\mathbf{X}
satisfying
\varphi(\mathbf{A}(x)|x)=1

for any
x\in\mathbf{X}
.
A policy
\pi=\{\pi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbf{\Pi}
is called a
stationary randomized policy
if there exists
\varphi\in\mathbf{M}
satisfying

\pi_{n}(\cdot|h_{n})=\varphi(\cdot|x_{n})\quad\hbox{ for any $h_{n}=(x_{0},a_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{H}_{n}$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

In such a case, we will write
\varphi
instead of
\pi
to emphasize that the corresponding stationary randomized policy
\pi
is generated by
\varphi
.
We denote by
\mathbf{\Pi}^{s}
the set of all stationary randomized policies.

A policy
\pi=\{\pi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbf{\Pi}
is called a
Markovian randomized policy
if there exists a sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbf{M}
satisfying

\pi_{n}(\cdot|h_{n})=\varphi_{n}(\cdot|x_{n})\quad\hbox{ for any $h_{n}=(x_{0},a_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{H}_{n}$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Let
\mathbf{\Pi}^{m}
be the set of all Markovian randomized policies. We have
\mathbf{\Pi}^{s}\subseteq\mathbf{\Pi}^{m}\subseteq\mathbf{\Pi}
.

The canonical space of all possible sample paths of the state-action process is

\mathbf{\Omega}=(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})^{\infty}
endowed with the product
\sigma
-algebra
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}
.
The coordinate projection functions from
\mathbf{\Omega}
to the state space
\mathbf{X}
, the action space
\mathbf{A}
, and
\mathbf{H}_{n}
for
n\geq 0
are respectively denoted by
X_{n}
,
A_{n}
, and
H_{n}
.
We will refer to
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as to state process and
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as the action process.
It is a well known result that for every policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
and any initial probability measure
\lambda
on
(\mathbf{X},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}})
there exists a unique probability
measure
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}
on
(\mathbf{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}})

such that
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}(\mathbf{K}^{\infty})=1
and such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}
, and
\Lambda\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbf{A})
we have

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}(X_{0}\in\Gamma)=\lambda(\Gamma)
,

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}(X_{n+1}\in\Gamma\mid H_{n},A_{n})=Q(\Gamma\mid X_{n},A_{n})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}(A_{n}\in\Lambda\mid H_{n})=\pi_{n}(\Lambda\mid H_{n})

with

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}
-probability one.
The expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}
is denoted by
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\pi}
.

Definition 2.1

The hitting time
T_{\Delta}
of the set
\Delta
is given by
T_{\Delta}:\mathbf{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
defined as

T_{\Delta}(x_{0},a_{0},x_{1},a_{1},\ldots)=\min\{n\geq 0:x_{n}\in\Delta\},

where the
\min
over the empty set is defined as
+\infty
.

Next we propose the definition of an absorbing control model.

Definition 2.2

Given an initial distribution
\lambda\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbf{X})
and
\Delta\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}
, we say that the control model

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\lambda,\Delta)
is absorbing if the conditions (a) and (b) below are satisfied, and we say that
it is uniformly absorbing if, additionally, condition (c) holds.

(a).

For every
(x,a)\in\Delta\times\mathbf{A}
we have
Q(\Delta|x,a)=1
.

(b).

For any
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
the expected hitting time
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\pi}[T_{\Delta}]
is finite.

(c).

The following limit holds:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}^{s}}\sum_{t=n}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}\{T_{\Delta}>t\}=0.

We define now the occupation measures of an absorbing control model
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\lambda,\Delta)
.

Definition 2.3

The occupation measure
\mu_{\lambda,\pi}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
of the control policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
is

\displaystyle\mu_{\lambda,\pi}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\pi}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{I}_{\{T_{\Delta}>t\}}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{\{(X_{t},A_{t})\in\Gamma\}}\Big]\quad\hbox{for $\Gamma\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}\otimes\mathfrak{B}(\mathbf{A})$.}

We note that the occupation measure
\mu_{\lambda,\pi}
takes into account the state-action process up to time
T_{\Delta}
and it does not count the time spent in
\Delta
: indeed,
\mu_{\lambda,\pi}(\Delta\times\mathbf{A})=0
.
We will consider the following sets of occupation measures

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}=\{\mu_{\lambda,\pi}:\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}\}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{m}_{\lambda}=\{\mu_{\lambda,\pi}:\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}^{m}\}
and also

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{s}_{\lambda}=\{\mu_{\lambda,\pi}:\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}^{s}\}
.

Lemma 2.4

For an absorbing control model
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\lambda,\Delta)
, the set
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
is bounded.

Proof.

Note that the total mass of the occupation measure
\mu_{\lambda,\pi}
is

\mu_{\lambda,\pi}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\pi}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{I}_{\{T_{\Delta}>t\}}\Big]=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\pi}\{T_{\Delta}>t\}=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\pi}[T_{\Delta}],

which is finite as a consequence of item (b) in Definition
2.2
. We also have
\sup_{\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\pi}[T_{\Delta}]<\infty
according to
[
8
, Sections 4.4 and 5.5]
for the special case of a Borel state space or Proposition 2.4(i) in
[
7
]
for the general case of a measurable state space. This shows that the set
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
is bounded.

\Box

3
The characteristic equations

In this section, we define the notion of phantom measure. We show that for absorbing MDP, phantom measures may exist.
In order to avoid this pathological phenomenon, we establish our first main result (see Theorem
3.6
) that provides several necessary and sufficient conditions to ensure that there are no phantom measures.

Definition 3.1

Given the absorbing control model
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\eta,\Delta)
,
we say
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
is a solution of the characteristic equations when

\mu(\mathbf{K}^{c})=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mu^{\mathbf{X}}=(\eta+\mu Q)\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}.

(3)

We denote by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
the family of all solutions of the characteristic equations.

Lemma 3.2

Suppose that the control model
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\eta,\Delta)
is absorbing. Given any
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
and
\sigma\in\mathbf{M}
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}=0\text{ for }\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}\text{-almost all }x\in\mathbf{X}.

Proof.

For notational convenience, let us introduce the functions
h_{t}
and
h
on
\mathbf{X}
taking values in
[0,1]
as

h_{t}(x)=\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}>t\}
and
h(x)=\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}
respectively.
Note that
h_{t}
vanishes on
\Delta
and also that
h_{t}
is a decreasing sequence of measurable functions which converges pointwise to

h(x)=\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}
.
To prove the result, we proceed by contradiction and suppose that
\int hd\mu^{\mathbf{X}}_{\eta,\pi}>0
. By definition of the occupation measure

\mu_{\eta,\pi}
and since
h
vanishes on
\Delta
, this implies that there exist some
s\geq 0
with
\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\pi}[h(X_{s})]>0
and, in particular, since
h_{t}\downarrow h
,

\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\pi}[h_{t}(X_{s})]=\infty.

(4)

Define the strategy
\gamma\in\mathbf{\Pi}
as follows:
\gamma_{k}=\pi_{k}
for
0\leq k\leq s-1
and

\gamma_{k}=\sigma
for every
k\geq s
(in case that
s=0
, this definition reduces to
\gamma=\sigma
).
Consequently, the distribution of
X_{s}
is the same under
\mathbb{P}_{\eta,\pi}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\eta,\gamma}
, and we have

\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\pi}[h_{t}(X_{s})]=\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\gamma}[h_{t}(X_{s})]=\mathbb{P}_{\eta,\gamma}\{T_{\Delta}>t+s\}.

Recalling (
4
), this implies that the series

\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\eta,\gamma}\{T_{\Delta}>t+s\}=\infty,

in contradiction with
\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\gamma}[T_{\Delta}]
being finite.
This gives the result.

\Box

Proposition 3.3

Suppose that the control model
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\eta,\Delta)
is absorbing.

(i).

Given any
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
, its occupation measure
\mu_{\eta,\pi}
satisfies the characteristic equations. Therefore, we have the inclusion
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\eta}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
.

(ii).

Given any
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
, there exists
\sigma\in\mathbf{M}
such that
\mu_{\eta,\pi}=\mu_{\eta,\sigma}
. Hence,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}^{s}_{\eta}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\eta}
.

Proof.
(i). We have already shown that
\mu_{\eta,\pi}
is a finite measure on
\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}
.
To prove the stated result, note that for any
B\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}}
we have

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathbf{X}}(B)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\eta,\pi}\{T_{\Delta}>t,X_{t}\in B\}=\eta(B-\Delta)+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\pi}\big[\mathbb{P}_{\eta,\pi}\{T_{\Delta}>t,X_{t}\in B\mid H_{t-1},A_{t-1}\}\big].

Observe now that for each
t\geq 1
the conditional probability within brackets vanishes on the set
\{T_{\Delta}\leq t-1\}
, and so

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathbf{X}}(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta(B-\Delta)+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\pi}\big[Q(B-\Delta\mid X_{t-1},A_{t-1})\cdot\mathbf{I}_{\{T_{\Delta}>t-1\}}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta(B-\Delta)+\int_{\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}}Q(B-\Delta|x,a)\mu(dx,da),

which can be equivalently written precisely as
\mu^{\mathbf{X}}=(\eta+\mu Q)\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
.
By construction of the state-action process, it is clear that
\mu(\mathbf{K}^{c})=0
. This shows that
\mu_{\eta,\pi}
indeed verifies the characteristic equations.

(ii). We will show that for any
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
there is some
\sigma\in\mathbf{M}
with
\mu_{\eta,\pi}=\mu_{\eta,\sigma}
.
Since
\mu_{\eta,\pi}
is in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
with
\mu_{\eta,\pi}(\mathbf{K}^{c})=0
, the measure
\mu_{\eta,\pi}
can be disintegrated as

\mu_{\eta,\pi}=\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}\otimes\sigma\quad\hbox{for some $\sigma\in\mathbf{M}$}.

(5)

It follows that the characteristic equation can be written

\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}=(\eta+\mu_{\eta,\pi}Q)\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}=(\eta+\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}Q_{\sigma})\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}.

Iterating this equation we obtain that for any
t\geq 0

\displaystyle\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}=\eta\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}(Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}})^{k}+\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}(Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}})^{t}.

(6)

We have, for any

and
t\geq 0

(Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}})^{t}(\mathbf{X}|x)=Q_{\sigma}^{t}(\Delta^{c}|x)=\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}>t\}

and
\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}>t\}\downarrow\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
for any
x\in\mathbf{X}
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
, we obtain that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}(Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}})^{t}(\mathbf{X})=0
.
Taking the limit as
t\rightarrow\infty
in (
6

\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}=\eta\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}})^{k}=\mu_{\eta,\sigma}^{\mathbf{X}}.

Once we have shown that the
\mathbf{X}
-marginals of the occupation measures of
\mu_{\eta,\pi}
and
\mu_{\eta,\sigma}
coincide, we conclude the result from (
5
).

\Box

The result in Proposition

does not exclude the possibility that there are measures in

illustrates the existence of phantom measures.

Example 3.4

Consider on

as initial distribution. A mesure

\displaystyle\nu(1)=\eta(1)+(1-\beta)\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\nu(j)

It will be shown in Theorem

This is the first notable difference with the discounted model, where the occupation measures are essentially characterized by a single equation of type
(

Definition 3.5

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\eta,\Delta)
be an absorbing control model.
Given a measure

we say that

is invariant for the kernel

when

\vartheta(\mathbf{K}^{c})=0

\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}=\vartheta Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}

Let us make some comments on this definition. Note that we are not excluding that

We call such a measure invariant because, by disintegration, there exists

satisfying

which can be written as

is an invariant measure for the substochastic kernel

Q_{\sigma}
on
\Delta^{c}
.

Theorem 3.6

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\eta,\Delta)
be absorbing.

(i).

A measure is in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
if and only if it can be decomposed as the sum of an occupation measure
\mu_{\eta,\sigma}
in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\eta}

and an invariant measure
\nu\otimes\sigma
for
Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
with
\sigma\in\mathbf{M}
and
\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbf{X})
.

(ii).

The following statements are equivalent.

(a).

The unique invariant measure for
Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
is the null measure on
\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A}
.

(b).

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\eta}
, i.e., there are no phantom measures.

(c).

The set
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
is bounded.

Proof.
(i).
Suppose that
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
is a solution of the characteristic equations. Proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we derive the existence of
\sigma\in\mathbf{M}
such that

\mu=\mu^{\mathbf{X}}\otimes\sigma
and

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathbf{X}}=\eta\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}(Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}})^{k}+\mu^{\mathbf{X}}(Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}})^{t}

(7)

for any
t\geq 1
. It follows that by taking the limit as
t\rightarrow\infty
that

\nu=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{\mathbf{X}}(Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}})^{t}=\mu^{\mathbf{X}}-\mu_{\eta,\sigma}^{\mathbf{X}}

defines a measure in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{A})
satisfying
\mu^{\mathbf{X}}=\mu_{\eta,\sigma}^{\mathbf{X}}+\nu
.
Recalling that

\mu^{\mathbf{X}}=\big(\eta+\mu^{\mathbf{X}}Q_{\sigma}\big)\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mu_{\eta,\sigma}^{\mathbf{X}}=\big(\eta+\mu_{\eta,\sigma}^{\mathbf{X}}Q_{\sigma}\big)\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}},

we get that

\nu=\nu Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
. The measure
\vartheta=\nu\otimes\sigma
satisfies the conditions in the statement of this proposition:
\mu=\mu_{\eta,\sigma}+\vartheta
.

Conversely, it is straightforward to check that the sum of an invariant measure and an occupation measure (which satisfies the characteristic equations) satisfies itself the characteristic equations and, hence, belongs to
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
. In fact, the sum of any measure in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
and an invariant measure lies in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
.

(ii). The implication
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
follows directly from item (i), while
(b)\Rightarrow(c)
is derived from Lemma
2.4
.
We prove

by contradiction and, hence, we suppose that there exists a non-null invariant measure
\vartheta

we have that

is also an invariant measure and so for any
\mu\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
and every

, which is not compatible with
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}_{\eta}
being bounded.

\Box

Our next result gives some insight on the behavior of phantom measures.

Proposition 3.7

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{M}}(\eta,\Delta)
is absorbing and let
\vartheta
be a non-null invariant measure for
Q\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
. For every
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
we have
\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}\perp\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}
.

Proof.

By disintegration of
\vartheta
there exists
\sigma\in\mathbf{M}
with

\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}=\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}Q_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
. Iterating this equation we obtain
\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}=\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}Q^{t}_{\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{\Delta^{c}}
for every
t\geq 1
and taking the limit as
t\rightarrow\infty
yields

\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{X})=\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}(\Delta^{c})=\int_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}(dx)

and so the set
B=\{x\in\mathbf{X}:\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}<1\}
satisfies
\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}(B)=0
.
By the Lebesgue decomposition theorem there exist two finite measures
\vartheta_{1}^{\mathbf{X}},\vartheta_{2}^{\mathbf{X}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbf{X})
with

\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}=\vartheta_{1}^{\mathbf{X}}+\vartheta_{2}^{\mathbf{X}}\quad\hbox{with $\vartheta_{1}^{\mathbf{X}}\ll\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}$ and
$\vartheta_{2}^{\mathbf{X}}\perp\mu_{\eta,\pi}^{\mathbf{X}}$}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{1}(dx)+\int_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{2}(dx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}\cdot\frac{d\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{1}}{d\mu^{\mathbf{X}}_{\eta,\pi}}\ \mu^{\mathbf{X}}_{\eta,\pi}(dx)+\int_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{2}(dx).

Applying Lemma
3.2
, we obtain that
\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}
vanishes
\mu^{\mathbf{X}}_{\eta,\pi}
-a.s. and so

\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{X})=\int_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{2}(dx).

Observe that
\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{2}(B)=0
and so

\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{X})=\int_{B^{c}}\mathbb{P}_{x,\sigma}\{T_{\Delta}=\infty\}\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{2}(dx)=\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{2}(B^{c})\ =\ \vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{2}(\mathbf{X}).

This shows that the measure
\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{1}
is null and, therefore,
\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}=\vartheta^{\mathbf{X}}_{2}
, showing the result.

\Box

This result establishes, loosely speaking, that the state process
\{X_{t}\}
under any policy
\pi\in\mathbf{\Pi}
never visits the support of a non-null invariant measure.

4
Compactness of occupation measures

In section, we introduce an additional hypothesis. It is assumed the so-called continuity-compactness conditions (see Condition (S) below).
In this context, we show in our second main result (see Theorem

Finally, it is shown that the occupation measures are characterized by the characteristic equation and an additional condition see our last main result (see Theorem

This represents a distinguishing factor from the discounted model, where occupation measures are fundamentally defined by a single characteristic equation.

Example 4.10

Consider the model defined with state and actions spaces

\mathbf{X}=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}:0\leq j\leq 2^{i}-1\}\cup\{\bar{x}\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathbf{A}=\{c,s\},

with

\mathbf{A}(x)=\{c,s\}
for
x\in\mathbb{N}\times\{0\}
and
\mathbf{A}(x)=\{s\}
otherwise.
The transition kernel is given by:

•

Q\big(\bar{x}\mid(i,0),c)\big)=\frac{1}{2}
and
Q\big((i+1,0)\mid(i,0),c)\big)=\frac{1}{2}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

Q\big((i,j+1)\mid(i,j),s\big)=1
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j<2^{i}-1
;

•

Q\big(\bar{x}\mid(i,2^{i}-1),s\big)=1
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

Q(\bar{x}\mid\bar{x},s)=1
.

In
[
9
, Example 3.13]
, it has been shown that this model is absorbing to
\Delta=\{\bar{x}\}
with initial distribution

\eta=\delta_{(0,0)}
but not uniformly absorbing.
Our objective is to illustrate by means of this example that the set of occupation measures
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\eta}
is not compact for the
ws
-topology, as can be derived from Theorem
4.9
.

Let us consider the sequence
\{\gamma_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
of nonrandomized stationary policies in
\mathbf{\Pi}^{s}
defined as follows. The policy
\gamma_{t}(da|x)
takes the action
c
for all states
(i,0)
for
0\leq i<t
and takes the action
s
for all states
(i,0)
with
i\geq t

If
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}_{\eta}
were relatively compact, then for any decreasing sequence of sets
\Gamma_{n}\downarrow\emptyset
we would have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\mu^{\mathbf{X}}_{\eta,\gamma_{t}}(\Gamma_{n})=0.

(16)

Let us consider

\Gamma_{n}=\big\{(i,j)\in\mathbf{X}:i\geq n\text{ and }1\leq j\leq 2^{i}-1\}\big\}

which indeed satisfies
\Gamma_{n}\downarrow\emptyset
. Observe that

\mathbb{P}_{\eta,\gamma_{n}}\{X_{n+j}=(n,j)\}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\quad\hbox{for $n\geq 1$

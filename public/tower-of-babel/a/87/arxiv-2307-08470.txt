The Dirichlet-to-Neumann Map for Poincaré–Einstein Fillings
Samuel Blitz ♢ , A. Rod Gover ♡ , Jarosław Kopiński ♣ , & Andrew Waldron ♠

Abstract
. We study the non-linear Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for the Poincaré–Einstein filling problem. For even dimensional manifolds we describe the range of this non-local map in terms of a natural rank two tensor along the boundary determined by the Poincaré–Einstein metric. This tensor is proportional to the variation of renormalized volume along a path of Poincaré–Einstein metrics. We show that it has an explicit formula and is the unique, natural conformal hypersurface invariant of transverse order equaling the boundary dimension. We also construct such conformally invariant Dirichlet-to-Neumann tensors for Poincaré–Einstein fillings for odd dimensional manifolds with conformally flat boundary. Keywords: Conformal geometry, Poincaré–Einstein manifolds, Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, renormalized volume.

53C18, 53A55, 53C21, 58J32.

1.
Introduction

A smooth Riemannian
d
-manifold
(M_{+},g^{o})

is said to be conformally compact if

M_{+}
is the interior of a smooth manifold with boundary
M
, and

g_{s}=s^{2}g^{o}

extends smoothly to a metric on
M=\overline{M_{+}}

for some (and so any)
smooth defining function
s\in C^{\infty}{M}
.
By the latter we mean that
{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}s
is nowhere vanishing along

\Sigma:=s^{-1}(0)=\partial M
.
Conformally compact manifolds for which the trace-free Ricci tensor vanishes,
i.e.

Ric^{{\hskip 0.85358pt}g^{o}}_{(ab)\circ}=0\,,

are termed
Poincaré–Einstein
.
Necessarily, Poincaré–Einstein manifolds have constant negative scalar curvature, which by convention, we choose to be
-d(d-1)
.
A classical example is the Poincaré ball

M_{+}=\big\{\vec{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}|\,|\vec{x}|^{{\hskip 0.85358pt}2}<1\big\}

with

g^{o}=\frac{4{\hskip 0.85358pt}|d\vec{x}|^{{\hskip 0.85358pt}2}}{\,(1-|\vec{x}|^{{\hskip 0.85358pt}2})^{2}}\,.

In addition to myriad mathematical applications, Poincaré–Einstein and conformally compact structures have played a central
rôle
in the geometry of physical models relating boundary field theories to bulk gravitational ones
(see for example
[
14
,
24
,
27
,
10
,
15
]

and
[
31
,
28
,
26
]
, respectively).

Since
s^{2}g^{o}
extends to the boundary for any defining function, a conformally compact structure canonically determines a conformal class of boundary metrics
\bm{c}_{\Sigma}
that are induced by a corresponding conformal class of metrics

\bm{c}
on
M
.
It turns out that the formal asymptotics of a Poincaré–Einstein metric
g^{o}
are an effective tool for the study of the conformal manifold
(\Sigma,\bm{c}_{\Sigma})
(see
[
14
,
15
]
). We say that a conformally compact structure for which

Ric^{g^{o}}_{(ab)\circ}=s^{k}Q_{(ab)\circ}\,,

for some smooth tensor
Q_{ab}
on
M^{d}
, is
asymptotically Poincaré–Einstein of order
k
and denote such data by
{\rm APE}_{k}^{d}
.
We often drop the label
d
when context makes it clear.
We also adopt the notation
{\mathcal{O}}(s^{k})
for the right hand side of the above display and other such quantities.
The case
k=d-2
is distinguished in the sense that
g_{s}
, modulo terms of order
{\mathcal{O}}(s^{d})
,
is no longer determined
solely by the boundary conformal manifold
(\Sigma,\bm{c}_{\Sigma})
.
This is exactly
when
the asymptotics of a global solution first
probe the interior structure of
M_{+}
.

A key global conformal invariant of an even dimensional Poincaré–Einstein structure
(M_{+},g^{o})

is its renormalized volume
[
28
,
22
]
, which arises as follows. Consider the one-parameter
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
family of Riemannian manifolds

M_{\varepsilon}=\{p\in M_{+}|s(p)>\varepsilon\}

equipped with the restriction of the metric
g^{o}
. Then the
renormalized volume
is given by

\operatorname{Vol}^{\rm ren}(M_{+},g^{o}):=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}^{n}}{{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\varepsilon^{n}}\Big(\varepsilon^{n}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}}{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\!\operatorname{Vol}^{g^{o}}\Big)\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\,.

For four-manifolds, a result of Anderson
[
3
]
is that

\operatorname{Vol}^{\rm ren}=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{3}\chi-\frac{1}{24}\int_{M_{+}}|W^{g^{o}}|^{2}\,{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\!\operatorname{Vol}^{g^{o}}\,,

where
W
denotes the Weyl tensor and
\chi
is the Euler characteristic of
M_{+}
. Moreover, Anderson also established that the functional gradient of the renormalized volume along a path of Poincaré–Einstein metrics is given by

\frac{1}{6}\mathring{\top}{\hskip 0.85358pt}C^{g}_{\hat{n}(ab)}\big|_{\Sigma}\,.

Here
C
denotes the Cotton tensor,
\hat{n}
is the inward unit normal of
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,g)
, and
\mathring{\top}

orthogonally projects
\Gamma(\odot^{2}T^{*}M|_{\Sigma})
to
\Gamma(\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}\Sigma)
(and also restricts sections to bundles over
\Sigma
when necessary).
Note that, since conformal transformations preserve angles (and metric tracelessness), this projection is independent of any choice of
g\in\bm{c}
.
Throughout, we use a
\circ
to denote
trace-free parts of a tensor.

Picking a metric
\bar{g}\in\bm{c}_{\Sigma}
, in a collar neighborhood of
\Sigma
there exists a special defining function
s=r

where
r(p)
is the minimal geodesic distance between
p
and
\Sigma

as measured by the metric
g_{r}=r^{2}g^{o}

[
14
]
.
In coordinates
(r,\vec{x})
for which
g_{r}(\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{x}})=0
, series in powers of
r
are termed
Fefferman–Graham
expansions. Returning to dimension
d=4
, the third Lie derivative of the
compactified metric

g_{r}
obeys

{\mathcal{L}}^{3}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}g_{r}\big|_{\Sigma}=-4\mathring{\top}C^{g_{r}}_{\hat{n}(ab)}\simeq:\operatorname{DN}_{ab}^{(4)}\,.

The above tensor is interesting, especially for general relativity (see for example
[
18
,
29
]
). This is partly signaled by the
fact that it is the first odd term in the Fefferman–Graham expansion of
g_{r}
.
Throughout this article we employ the symbol
\simeq
to denote
a representative of an equivalence class of tensors
that are
equal up to multiplication by a non-zero constant; and
:\simeq
when such an equivalence class is being defined this way. For simplicity we will often refer to an equivalence class such as
\operatorname{DN}^{(4)}
as if it were a single tensor.
As shown in
[
14
,
22
]
, the tensor
\operatorname{DN}^{(4)}
changes covariantly when computed with respect to an expansion in a new geodesic coordinate
r^{\prime}
corresponding to a different choice of
\bar{g}^{\prime}\in\bm{c}_{\Sigma}
.
In fact,

\mathring{\top}{\hskip 0.85358pt}C^{\Omega^{2}g}_{\hat{n}^{\prime}(ab)}\big|_{\Sigma}=\bar{\Omega}^{-1}\mathring{\top}{\hskip 0.85358pt}C^{g}_{\hat{n}(ab)}\big|_{\Sigma}\,,

where
\hat{n}^{\prime}=\bar{\Omega}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hat{n}
, for
0<\Omega\in C^{\infty}M
and
\bar{\Omega}:=\Omega|_{\Sigma}
, so long as
g\in\bm{c}
where
\bm{c}
is a conformal class of metrics defined by a Poincaré–Einstein structure (see
[
8
]
for the generalization to arbitrary conformal structures).
It therefore constitutes an example of what we shall later term a conformal hypersurface invariant of the conformal embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{c})
.
The tensor
\operatorname{DN}^{(4)}

defines a section of
\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[-1]
(the space of symmetric, rank 2, trace-free tensor-valued,
weight
-1
conformal densities; the latter are explained in Section
2
).
This section is defined independently of any pair
(g_{s},s)
with
s
defining, chosen such that
g^{o}=s^{-2}g_{s}
, and therefore
is an invariant of the Poincaré–Einstein structure.
However it is not determined by the local data of the boundary conformal class
\bm{c}_{\Sigma}
, again see
[
14
]
.
Knowledge of
\operatorname{DN}^{(4)}
requires information about the global Poincaré–Einstein metric
g^{o}
.
Our point is that this tensor
describes the range of the
global, non-linear, Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for Poincaré–Einstein metrics introduced by Graham in
[
23
]
.

As
\operatorname{DN}^{(4)}
depends on
g_{r}
through at most three derivatives with respect to a coordinate
r
transverse to
\Sigma
, it is an example of what we will term a
natural hypersurface
tensor of transverse order
3
.
A first result here is the existence of a unique natural tranverse order 5 tensor for
dimension
6
Poincaré–Einstein structures.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M_{+}^{6},g^{o})
be a Poincaré–Einstein structure with conformal infinity
\Sigma
.
Then the unique natural transverse order 5 section of
\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[-3]

determined by the
Poincaré–Einstein structure, is given, for a choice of
g\in\bm{c}
, by

\mathring{\top}\big((\nabla_{\hat{n}}+2H)B_{ab}\big)-4\bar{C}_{c(ab)}\bar{\nabla}^{c}H\,.

The detailed notions of transverse order and natural tensors are described in Section
3
.
In the above,
B_{ab}
denotes the Bach tensor,
H
is the mean curvature of the embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,g)
, and bars are used to denote objects intrinsic to the hypersurface
\Sigma
.
This theorem
is proved by exhaustion in Appendix
A
.

In arbitrary even dimensions
d
, the coefficient
({\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}})^{d-1}g_{r}\big|_{\Sigma}
in the Fefferman–Graham expansion of a Poincaré–Einstein metric also
changes covariantly when computed with respect to a different choice of
boundary metric representative
\bar{g}\in\bm{c}_{\Sigma}
,
but once again is not determined by the boundary data
(\Sigma,\bm{c}_{\Sigma})

[
22
]
. It therefore defines
an invariant of the
Poincaré–Einstein structure which
we term a
(Poincaré–Einstein) Dirichlet-to-Neumann tensor

\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}
. When it exists (meaning when the data of
(\Sigma,\bm{c}_{\Sigma})\hookrightarrow M^{d}
uniquely determines a corresponding Poincaré–Einstein structure, for example for boundary metrics suitably close to the round sphere
[
25
]
), the map

\bm{c}_{\Sigma}\mapsto\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}\in\Gamma(\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[3-d])

is termed the
(Poincaré–Einstein) Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
; see
[
23
]
. The linearization of this map is studied in
[
32
]
.
The data of the boundary conformal class of metrics
\bm{c}_{\Sigma}
and the tensor
\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}
determines the formal Fefferman–Graham asymptotics of
g_{r}
to all orders
[
14
]
.
The above definition extends to
odd dimension
d
structures in the case that the boundary
(\Sigma,\bm{c}_{\Sigma})
is conformally flat; see below.

A main point of this article is
to both give a simple characterization of these maps and, in particular, compute Dirichlet-to-Neumann tensors
\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}

for dimensions
d\geqslant 4
.
Critically, Dirichlet-to-Neumann tensors are natural and of transverse order
d-1
; see Proposition
3.1
.
Thus we establish higher dimensional analogs of Theorem
1.1
. This result
dovetails nicely
with a seminal physics conjecture of Deser and Schwimmer
[
13
]
that was proved by Alexakis
[
1
,
2
]
,
as well as work on renormalized volumes by Anderson
[
3
]
and Chang, Qing and Yang
[
10
]
. The latter work builds on the former ones to show that general even dimensional renormalized volumes are a sum of
an Euler characteristic term plus an integral over
M_{+}
whose integrand is a natural conformal invariant.

In principle, following Anderson, even dimensional Dirichlet-to-Neumann tensors
could be computed by varying renormalized volumes
[
3
]
.
The latter are intimately related to
Q
-curvature invariants, whose
complexity in terms of Riemannian invariants explodes in
eight and higher dimensions
[
19
]
, so a
more powerful method is required.
Luckily, tractor calculus methods, and in particular recent results on conformally invariant normal operators
[
20
]
and conformal fundamental forms
[
8
]
, yield a rather simple solution to this problem. This is a main theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M_{+}^{d},g^{o})
be an even-dimensional Poincaré–Einstein structure with conformal infinity
\Sigma
and
d\geqslant 6
.
For
d=6
, we have that

\operatorname{DN}^{(6)}\simeq{\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{q}^{*}\mathring{\top}\delta_{R}\,W\,.

For
d\geqslant 8
, we have that

\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}\simeq{\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{q}^{*}\mathring{\top}\delta_{\frac{d-6}{2},\frac{d-4}{2}}\,W\,.

Moreover,
\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}

has
leading transverse order term

\mathring{\top}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\scalebox{1.3}{$:$}\nabla_{\hat{n}}^{d-5}\scalebox{1.3}{$:$}{\hskip 0.85358pt}B_{ab}\,,

and is the
unique natural transverse order
(d-1)
section of
\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[3-d]
determined by the
Poincaré–Einstein structure.
Thus it is the functional gradient of the renormalized volume
along a path of Poincaré–Einstein metrics.

Here we have employed a normal ordering notation

\scalebox{1.3}{$:$}\nabla_{\hat{n}}^{\ell}\scalebox{1.3}{$:$}
for
\hat{n}^{a_{1}}\cdots\hat{n}^{a_{\ell}}\nabla_{a_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{a_{\ell}}

(the composition of operators
\nabla_{\hat{n}}\circ\nabla_{\hat{n}}
is ill-defined) while
the above tractor calculus notions and notations, including the
W
-tractor, are explained in Section
2
.

The recently developed conformal fundamental forms of
[
8
,
6
]
, designed for the study conformal embeddings
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{c})
, play a key
rôle
in establishing our main theorems for the case
d
is even.
Given such an embedding,
a
k^{\rm th}

conformal fundamental form

\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{\scalebox{.8}{$k$}}}}}
is any section of
\odot^{2}_{\circ}T^{*}\Sigma[3-k]
that is a natural conformal hypersurface invariant of transverse order
k-1
(in many contexts the integer
k
is denoted by Roman numerals). In the cases
2\leqslant k\leqslant d-1
, the
k^{\rm th}
conformal fundamental forms are obstructions to a
d
-dimensional conformally compact manifold being an
{\rm APE}_{d-3}
structure.
This generalizes an older result
[
30
,
17
]

that the hypersurface embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,s^{2}g^{o})
(for any defining function
s
) of a Poincaré–Einstein structure
(M_{+},g^{o})
is necessarily umbilic—so the trace-free second fundamental form
\hskip 1.13809pt\mathring{\rm{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.0pt}
vanishes. In fact, vanishing of all
conformal fundamental forms for

2\leqslant k\leqslant\ell
with
\ell\leqslant d-1

is in fact a both necessary and sufficient condition for a conformally compact structure to be

{\rm APE}_{\ell-2}
.

In dimension four a conformally invariant fourth fundamental form is
[
8
,
18
]

{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptV}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}_{ab}:=C_{\hat{n}(ab)}^{\top}-\bar{\nabla}^{c}W_{c(ab)\hat{n}}^{\top}+HW_{\hat{n}ab\hat{n}}\,.

For conformal embeddings arising from an
{\rm APE}_{1}
structure,

{\mathring{{\bf\rm I\hskip-0.56905ptV}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}{}=\operatorname{DN}^{(4)}
.
In this sense, on
{\rm APE}_{d-3}
structures, Dirichlet-to-Neumann tensors
\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}
could be viewed as conformal fundamental forms
\mathring{\underline{\overline{\rm{\scalebox{.8}{$d$}}}}}
. An interesting question is whether there are
d^{\rm th}
conformal fundamental forms for more general conformal embeddings.

The case of odd dimension-
d
Poincaré-Einstein structures is subtle. Firstly, no Anderson-type formula for the renormalized volume is available, and in general this quantity depends on a choice of boundary metric representative
\bar{g}\in\bm{c}_{\Sigma}
. Concordantly, there is an obstruction to smoothness of the Fefferman–Graham expansion.
For general boundary conformal structures, the coefficient
({\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}})^{d-1}g_{r}\big|_{\Sigma}
in the Fefferman–Graham expansion of a Poincaré–Einstein metric does
not
define a tensor-density
valued in
\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[3-d]

independently of the choice of
\bar{g}\in\bm{c}_{\Sigma}
.
One might hope for a canonical class of boundary metrics leading to a
Dirichlet-to-Neumann tensor.
The following theorem
is a positive answer
in dimension
d=5
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M_{+}^{5},g^{o})
be a Poincaré–Einstein structure with (locally) conformally flat conformal infinity
\Sigma
.
Then the unique natural transverse order 4 section of
\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[-2]
that is an invariant of the Poincaré–Einstein structure, is given, for a choice of
g\in\bm{c}_{o}
, by

B^{\top}_{(ab)\circ}\,.

Moreover, given a (locally) flat boundary metric representative,
and corresponding distance function
r
, this tensor is a non-zero multiple of the fourth Fefferman–Graham expansion coefficient
({\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}})^{4}g_{r}\big|_{\Sigma}
.

The above is an odd dimensional analog of Theorem
1.1
and the following is that of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M^{d}_{+},g^{o})
be an odd-dimensional Poincaré–Einstein structure with a (locally) conformally flat conformal infinity. Then if
d\geqslant 7
, the map

\bm{c}_{\Sigma}\mapsto\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}\in\Gamma(\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[3-d])

defined by the natural conformal hypersurface invariant

\operatorname{DN}^{(d)}\simeq\mathring{\top}\circ q^{*}\big(\big[\hat{n}^{a}\scalebox{1.3}{$:$}\nabla_{\hat{n}}^{d-4}\scalebox{1.3}{$:$}F_{ab}{}^{A}{}_{B}\big]\big|_{\Sigma}\big)

has as image a non-zero multiple of
the section of
\Gamma(\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[3-d])
given by the Fefferman–Graham coefficient

({\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}})^{d-1}g_{r}\big|_{\Sigma}\,.

1.1.
Conventions

Throughout we take
M
to be a smooth, for simplicity oriented,
d\geqslant 3
-dimensional manifold. The canonical
d
-form determined by a metric
g
is denoted
{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}{\rm Vol}^{g}
and may be used when integrating over
M
; this is normalized such that in local coordinates it gives the measure
\sqrt{\det g}\,{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}x^{1}\cdots{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}x^{d}
where
g
here denotes the matrix of metric components. The tangent, cotangent, and tensor bundles of
M
are denoted, respectively, by
TM
,
T^{*}M
, and
{\mathbb{T}}M
. Sections are often handled using an abstract index notation; for example
t^{ab}{}_{c}\in\Gamma(\otimes^{2}TM\otimes T^{*}M)
, and the contraction
v(\omega)
of a vector field
v
and one-form field
\omega
is denoted by
v^{a}\omega_{a}=v_{\omega}=\omega_{v}\in C^{\infty}M
. Throughout, unless otherwise specified, all structures are taken to be smooth. The inverse of a metric
g_{ab}\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}T^{*}M)
is denoted
g^{ab}
and is used to “raise indices” in the standard way, for example
\omega^{a}=g^{ab}\omega_{b}
. The symmetric trace-free part of a tensor
X_{ab}
is denoted
X_{(ab)\circ}:=\frac{1}{2}(X_{ab}+X_{ba})-\frac{1}{d}g_{ab}X_{c}{}^{c}\in\Gamma(\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}M)
.
Also, for a tensor
X_{abc\cdots}
, we denote
|X|_{g}^{2}:=X_{abc\cdots}X^{abc\cdots}
.
While we work solely in Riemannian signature, many results carry over
mutatis mutandis
to indefinite
metric signatures.

The Levi-Civita connection of a metric is denoted
\nabla^{g}
(or simply
\nabla
when context lends clarity).
The Riemann tensor
R
of
\nabla^{g}
is defined by

(R(x,y)z)^{a}:=\big((\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}-\nabla_{y}\nabla_{x}-\nabla_{[x,y]})z\big)^{a}=x^{c}y^{d}R_{cd}{}^{a}{}_{b}z^{b}=R_{xy}{}^{a}{}_{z}\in\Gamma(TM)\,,

where
x,y,z\in\Gamma(TM)
and
[x,y]
is the Lie bracket. The Weyl, Cotton and Bach tensors are given by

\displaystyle W_{abcd}

\displaystyle:=R_{abcd}-g_{ac}P_{bd}+g_{ad}P_{bc}+g_{bc}P_{ad}-g_{bd}P_{ac}\,,

\displaystyle C_{abc}

\displaystyle:=\nabla_{a}P_{bc}-\nabla_{b}P_{ac}\,,

\displaystyle B_{ab}

\displaystyle:=\Delta P_{ab}-\nabla^{c}\nabla_{a}P_{bc}+P^{cd}W_{acbd}\,,

where the Schouten tensor
P_{ab}
and its trace
J=P_{a}{}^{a}
are defined by the equation

Ric_{ab}:=R_{ca}{}^{c}{}_{b}=(d-2)P_{ab}+g_{ab}J
, and
\Delta:=\nabla^{a}\nabla_{a}
is the (negative) rough Laplacian.
Finally, note we will often place a symbol above an equals sign to qualify its domain of applicability, for example
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma}}{{=}}B
implies equality along the hypersurface
\Sigma
, or
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}B
denotes equality for the choice of metric
g
.

2.
Conformal Hypersurface Calculus

A
conformal manifold

(M,\bm{c})
is the data of a smooth manifold and a conformal class of metrics
\bm{c}
meaning, that if
g,g^{\prime}\in\bm{c}
then
g^{\prime}=\Omega^{2}g
for some
0<\Omega\in C^{\infty}M
. A
conformal hypersurface embedding

\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{c})
is a conformal manifold equipped with a smoothly embedded codimension 1 submanifold
\Sigma
. The data
(M,\bm{c})
may be viewed as a ray subbundle of
\odot^{2}T^{*}M
and in turn as an
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
-principal bundle. The group action
t\mapsto s^{-w}t
on
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
(corresponding to
g(p)\mapsto s^{2}g(p)
) for
w\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
determines an associated line bundle
{\mathcal{E}}M[w]
over
M
called a
weight
w
conformal density bundle
.
Given any vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}M
over
M
, we denote
{\mathcal{V}}M[w]:={\mathcal{V}}M\otimes{\mathcal{E}}M[w]
.
The tautological section
{\bm{g}}\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}T^{*}M[2])
determined by
\bm{c}
is called the
conformal metric
. We may equally well label a conformal manifold
(M,\bm{c})
by the pair
(M,{\bm{g}})
.
The
tractor bundle
is defined by (see
[
12
]
)

{\mathcal{T}}M:=\Big(\bigsqcup_{g\in\bm{c}}{\mathcal{T}}^{g}M\Big)\Big/\penalty\sim\,,

where

{\mathcal{T}}_{g}M:={\mathcal{E}}M[1]\oplus T^{*}M[1]\oplus{\mathcal{E}}M[-1]\,.

The above equivalence relation
\sim
on direct sum bundles is defined by the following relations on sections

\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}_{g^{\prime}})\ni(\tau,\mu+\Upsilon\tau,\rho-\Upsilon.\mu-\frac{1}{2}\Upsilon^{2}\tau)\sim(\tau,\mu,\rho)\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}_{g})\,,

for
\Upsilon:=d\log\Omega
. Here
\Upsilon.\mu:={\bm{g}}^{-1}(\Upsilon,\mu)
and
\Upsilon^{2}:={\bm{g}}^{-1}(\Upsilon,\Upsilon)
.
There is a canonical section
X
of
{\mathcal{T}}M[1]
given by

(0,0,1)\in{\mathcal{T}}_{g}M[1]\,,

for any
g\in\bm{c}
, which is termed the
canonical tractor
. Also, there is an indefinite, conformally invariant, bundle metric
h\in\Gamma(\odot^{2}{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M)
given, in an obvious matrix notation, by

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&{\bm{g}}^{-1}&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

and termed the
tractor metric
.
Sections
T\in\Gamma({\mathcal{T}}M)
are denoted in an abstract index notation by
T^{A}
, so the tractor metric is then
h_{AB}
and will be used to raised and lower tractor indices in the standard way. For example,
h_{AB}X^{A}X^{B}
is the zero section of
{\mathcal{E}}M[2]
.

On an oriented conformal hypersurface
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{c})
, there is a canonical section
N
of
{\mathcal{T}}M\big|_{\Sigma}
given, for any
g\in\bm{c}
, by

(0,\hat{n},-H)\in{\mathcal{T}}_{g}M\,,

where
\hat{n}
is the inward unit conormal of
g
and
H
its mean curvature (
i.e.
, averaged eigenvalues of the second fundamental form). This section is called the
normal tractor

[
4
]
and is key for the study of conformally embedded hypersurfaces.
The normal tractor and tractor metric allow us to define the tractor analog of the projection operator
\mathring{\top}

3.

Any Poincaré–Einstein structure

In fact, a slew of other natural conformal hypersurface invariants

We next need to define natural conformal hypersurface invariants.
First recall that a natural Riemannian invariant on a Riemannian manifold is any tensor-valued polynomial made from the metric, its inverse, the Riemann tensor, and covariant derivatives of the latter. Strictly, this is the class of “even invariants”, meaning those that are unaffected by a change of orientation. It is clear that this type of invariant is all that will be required in our context.

The above definition extends in the obvious way to a Riemannian manifold equipped with any set of scalar functions.
Now given a triple
(M,{\bm{g}},\sigma)

\sigma
is a defining density for
the conformally embedded hypersurface
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{c})

for

3.2.
Odd dimensional bulk

We first need to characterize conformal hypersurface invariants for (asymptotically) Poincaré–Einstein structures with conformally flat boundaries.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M^{d},{\bm{g}})

be a conformal hypersurface embedding where
(M^{d},{\bm{g}},\sigma)\in{\rm APE}_{k-2}
with
2\leqslant k\leqslant d-1
. Moreover, suppose that the boundary
(\Sigma,\bm{c}_{\Sigma})
is conformally flat. Then any natural conformal hypersurface invariant
I\in\Gamma({\mathbb{T}}\Sigma[w])
with transverse order
1\leqslant m\leqslant k-1
vanishes.

Proof.

First we choose a scale for which the boundary metric
\bar{g}=\bar{\delta}
is flat. Then, the corresponding Fefferman–Graham expansion of the compactified metric

of an
{\rm APE}_{k-2}
structure is

g_{r}={{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}}r^{2}+\bar{\delta}+r^{k}Y^{\bar{\delta}}

for some smooth, rank two, symmetric tensor

. This follows directly from
[
15
]
; see Equation (
3.7
)
below.
The statement now follows since the only local conformal invariants for a flat metric are those built from the metric itself, and that the order

term cannot contribute to invariants of transverse order strictly less than
k
. Vanishing in one scale implies the same for all other scales.
∎

Thus we learn that

So using

, we have that

X^{\bar{g}^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma}}{{=}}\,\bar{\Omega}^{3-d}X^{\bar{g}}\,.

Clearly
(d-1)!{\hskip 0.85358pt}X^{\bar{g}}|_{\Sigma}=({\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}})^{d-1}g_{r}\big|_{\Sigma}

is trace-free, we first note that the Poincaré–Einstein condition implies that (see for example

]

where
n:={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}r

\rho=-(\nabla^{a}n_{a}+rJ)/d
,
which in the scale
\sigma=[g_{r};r]
implies

0=-d\rho=\nabla.n+rJ^{g_{r}}\,.

With impunity, we may once again compute in the boundary flat metric
\bar{\delta}
scale for which

g_{r}={{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}}r^{2}+\bar{\delta}+r^{d-1}X^{\bar{\delta}}\,.

Then simple computations show

\nabla.n=\tfrac{d-1}{2}r^{d-2}\tr_{\bar{\delta}}X^{\bar{\delta}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{d-1})

and

rJ^{g_{r}}=-\tfrac{d-2}{2}r^{d-2}\tr_{\bar{\delta}}X^{\bar{\delta}}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{d-1})\,.

Hence the right hand side of Equation (

\tfrac{1}{2}\tr_{\bar{\delta}}X^{\bar{\delta}}

It only remains to show that

Certainly they are both natural conformal hypersurface invariants (naturalness of the Lie derivative expression follows by the same proof method as used in Theorem

Fefferman and Graham

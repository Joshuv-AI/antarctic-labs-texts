The Tolman-Ehrenfest effect for an ideal gas in a background of time-independent electric, magnetic, and gravitational fields
H. Arthur Weldon

Abstract
The statistical mechanics of an ideal gas of point particles moving in a time independent background metric with g 0 ​ j ≠ 0 g_{0j}\neq 0 is investigated. An explicit calculation shows that when there is no background electrostatic or magnetostatic field the thermodynamic pressure, energy density, and thermally averaged energy-momentum tensor depend on temperature and chemical potential only through the ratios T 0 / g 00 T_{0}/\sqrt{g_{00}} and μ 0 / g 00 \mu_{0}/\sqrt{g_{00}} . A background magnetostatic field does not change this, however with a background electrostatic field the previous results are multiplied by a factor exp ( − e A 0 / T 0 ) \exp(-eA_{0}/T_{0}) , which is an exception to the strict Tolman-Ehrenfest rule because the system is open.

Equations (

Appendix B
T^{\mu\nu}

B.1
Calculation of
T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm part}
from
H_{\rm part}

The energy-momentum tensor for the particles may be calculated using the Hilbert
variational principle:

\displaystyle-{\sqrt{g}\over 2}T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm part}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\delta\over\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x)}\int dx^{\prime 0}L_{\rm part}(x^{\prime})

(103)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})\bigg[{\partial L_{\rm part}\over\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\bigg]_{\partial_{0}x^{i},x^{i}}

where the metric variation is performed before the equations of motion are imposed on the particle
velocity and position.
The following steps depend three facts: (i)
L_{\rm part}
does not depend on the momentum
p_{j}
,
(ii)
L_{\rm part}=p_{j}\partial_{0}x^{j}-H_{\rm part}
and
p_{j}\partial_{0}x^{j}
does not depend on the metric,
(iii)
H_{\rm part}
does not depend on the velocity
\partial_{0}x^{i}
:

\displaystyle\bigg[{\partial L_{\rm part}\over\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\bigg]_{\partial_{0}x^{i},x^{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[{\partial L_{\rm part}\over\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\bigg]_{\partial_{0}x^{i},x^{i},p_{j}}

(104)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigg[{\partial H_{\rm part}\over\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\bigg]_{\partial_{0}x^{i},x^{i},p_{j}}

(105)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigg[{\partial H_{\rm part}\over\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\bigg]_{x^{i},p_{j}}.

(106)

Therefore

{\sqrt{g}\over 2}T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm part}(x)=\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})\bigg[{\partial H_{\rm part}\over\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\bigg]_{x^{i},p_{j}}.

(107)

The partition function for the particles

\ln Z_{\rm part}=\int{d^{3}xd^{3}p\over(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-\beta_{0}(H_{\rm part}-\mu_{0})}

(108)

has a variational derivative

{\delta\ln Z_{\rm part}\over\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x^{\prime})}=-\beta_{0}{\sqrt{g}\over 2}\int{d^{3}pd^{3}x\over(2\pi)^{3}}T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm part}e^{-\beta_{0}(H_{\rm part}-\mu_{0})}

(109)

This is the starting point of Sec. IV.

Explicit form for
T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm part}
.

It is not difficult to explicitly differentiate
H_{\rm part}
with respect to
g_{\mu\nu}

by considering the metric dependence of the covariant particle velocity
u_{\alpha}
.
From (
26
)
u_{j}
is independent of the metric because
p_{j}
and
A_{j}
are.
The derivative of
g^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}=1
with respect to
g^{\mu\nu}

yields

{\partial u_{0}\over\partial g^{\mu\nu}}=-{u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\over 2u^{0}}.

(110)

This gives the derivative of
H_{\rm part}=mu_{0}+eA_{0}
.
A change from the contravariant metric to the covariant metric gives

\sqrt{g}\,T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm part}=\delta^{3}({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})m{u^{\mu}u^{\nu}\over u^{0}}

(111)

as expected.

B.2

T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm field}
from
{\cal H}_{\rm field}

B.2.1
The canonical
{\cal H}_{\rm field}

Because
g_{0j}\neq 0
it is not trivial to compute
{\cal H}_{\rm field}
in terms of the canonical momenta
\pi^{j}
and the magnetic field
F_{jk}
.
The starting point is the Lagrange density

{\cal L}_{\rm field}=-{\sqrt{g}\over 4}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta},

(112)

which implies a canonical momentum

\pi^{j}={\partial{\cal L}_{\rm field}\over\partial(\partial_{0}A_{j})}=-\sqrt{g}F^{0j}

(113)

and the Hamiltonian density

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm field}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{j}\partial_{0}A_{j}+{\sqrt{g}\over 4}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}

(114)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{j}F_{0j}+{\sqrt{g}\over 4}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}+\pi^{j}\partial_{j}A_{0}.

To express this in terms of canonical variables requires the identity

g_{jk}\pi^{k}=-\sqrt{g}F^{0k}g_{kj}=-\sqrt{g}\,g^{0\mu}F_{\mu j}

(115)

which may be solved for
F_{0j}
:

F_{0j}={1\over g^{00}}\bigg[-{g_{jk}\pi^{k}\over\sqrt{g}}+F_{j\ell}g^{\ell 0}\bigg].

(116)

This allows the first term in (
114
) to be expressed in terms of momentum and covariant field strength.
For the second term in (
114
) use the quantity

c^{\alpha\mu}=g^{\alpha\mu}-{g^{\alpha 0}g^{\mu 0}\over g^{00}}

(117)

to obtain

F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}=F_{\alpha\beta}c^{\alpha\mu}c^{\beta\nu}F_{\mu\nu}+2{F^{0\alpha}g_{\alpha\mu}F^{0\mu}\over g^{00}}.

(118)

Because
c^{\alpha\mu}
is zero if either
\alpha=0
or
\mu=0
and
F^{0\alpha}
is zero if
\alpha=0

the identity simplifies to

F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}=F_{jk}c^{j\ell}c^{km}F_{\ell m}+2{F^{0j}g_{jk}F^{0k}\over g^{00}}.

(119)

This allows the second term in (
114
) to be expressed as

{\sqrt{g}\over 4}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}={\pi^{j}\pi^{k}g_{jk}\over 2g^{00}\sqrt{g}}+{\sqrt{g}\over 4}F_{jk}c^{j\ell}c^{km}F_{\ell m}.

(120)

The resulting Hamiltonian density in terms of canonical variables is therefore

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\rm field}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\pi^{j}\pi^{k}g_{jk}\over 2g^{00}\sqrt{g}}+{\pi^{j}F_{jk}g^{k0}\over g^{00}}

(121)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\sqrt{g}\over 4}F_{jk}F_{\ell m}c^{j\ell}c^{km}+\pi^{j}\partial_{j}A_{0}.

A simple check is that
\partial_{0}A{{}_{j}}=\partial{\cal H}_{\rm field}/\partial\pi_{j}
.

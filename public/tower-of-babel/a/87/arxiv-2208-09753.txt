A Modified Trapezoidal Rule for a Class of Weakly Singular Integrals in n n Dimensions
Senbao Jiang, Xiaofan Li

Abstract
In this paper we propose and analyze a general arbitrarily high-order modified trapezoidal rule for a class of weakly singular integrals of the forms I = ∫ ℝ n ϕ ⁡ ( x ) ​ s ​ ( x ) ​ d ⁡ x I=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi(x)s(x)\operatorname{d}\!{x} in n n dimensions, where ϕ ∈ C c N ​ ( ℝ n ) \phi\in C_{c}^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) for some sufficiently large N N and s s is the weakly singular kernel. The admissible class of weakly singular kernel requires s s satisfies dilation and symmetry properties and is large enough to contain functions of the form P ⁡ ( x ) | x | r \frac{P(x)}{|x|^{r}} where r > 0 r>0 and P ⁡ ( x ) P(x) is any monomials such that deg ⁡ P < r < deg ⁡ P + n \deg P<r<\deg P+n . The modified trapezoidal rule is the singularity-punctured trapezoidal rule added by correction terms involving the correction weights for grid points around singularity. Correction weights are determined by enforcing the quadrature rule exactly evaluates some monomials and solving corresponding linear systems. A long-standing difficulty of these type of methods is establishing the non-singularity of the linear system, despite strong numerical evidences. By using an algebraic-combinatorial argument, we show the non-singularity always holds and prove the general order of convergence of the modified quadrature rule. We present numerical experiments to validate the order of convergence.

Introduction

Numerical integration is a classic topic in numerical analysis and it is still an area of active research. Classical methods such as trapezoidal rule, Simpson rule and Gaussian quadrature have become integral part of standard textbooks, e.g.
[
10
]
. Many of these quadrature require some regularity of the integrand and therefore can be generalized to higher dimensions by iterated integral justified by Fubini’s theorem. It is not always the case for weakly singular integrals. There are many numerical methods for weakly singular integrals in low dimensions, i.e. one or two or three dimension. Among them, a class of methods based on modifying the trapezoidal rules are popular, see
[
12
,
3
,
8
,
1
,
9
,
8
,
11
,

]
.

Rokhlin
[
12
]
was first to propose singularity-corrected trapezoidal rule and Alpert
[
3
]
and Kapur and Rokhlin
[
8
]
further improved the method. Their quadrature rules are designed for functions of the form
f(x)=\phi(x)s(x)+\Psi(x)
or
f(x)=\phi(x)s(x)
in 1D, where
\phi(x),\Psi(x)
are regular functions and
s(x)
is singular function with isolated singularity such as
s(x)=|x|^{\gamma},\gamma>-1
or
s(x)=\log(x)
. Aguilar and Chen designed singularity-boundary-corrected trapezoidal rule for singular kernel

in 2D
[
1
]
and
s(x)=\nicefrac{{1}}{{|x|}}
in 3D
[
2
]
, in particular, their singularity correction is introducing correction terms in the vicinity of the singularity, involving correction weights and values of the regular part of the integrand. Correction weights are computed through enforcing the singularity-corrected trapezoidal rule exactly evaluate monomials up to certain degree. They observed high-order convergence of the rule but do not offer any proofs. Marin et al
[
11
]
proposed an increasingly high-order modified trapezoidal rule to weakly singular integrals
\int\phi(x)s(x)\operatorname{d}\!{x}
with sufficiently smooth part
\phi(x)
with compact support and singular parts
s(x)=|x|^{\gamma},\gamma>-1
in 1D and
s(x)=\nicefrac{{1}}{{|x|}}
in 2D and conducted rigorous analysis of order of convergence. Recently, Jiang and Li
[
6
]
further developed this method to weakly singular integrals
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi(x)s_{ij}(x)\operatorname{d}\!{x}
with
\phi(x)\in C_{c}^{2p+4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
s(x)=\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2+\alpha}}
where
1\leq i,j\leq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
. They proved the order of convergence is
2p+4-\alpha
where
p\geq 1
is associated with total number of correction weights. A further application to numerical fractional laplacian in 2D can be found at
[
7
]
. This type of methods have a common difficulty: proving the linear systems of correction weights have a unique solution. We refer this difficulty as
non-singularity problem
. One of the main convergence theorems in
[
11
]
is conditional upon the non-singularity. Jiang and Li
[
6
]
proposed an algebraic-combinatorial method to overcome their non-singularity problem in 2D, regardless of the number of the correction weights. Their method can be easily adapted to non-singularity problem of Marin et al
[
11
]
and hence the convergence theorem in
[
11
]

In this paper we generalize the modified trapezoidal rule in
[
6
]
to a class of weakly singular integrals in arbitrary
n

satisfying dilation property

[

We organize the paper as follows. In

2.2
Modified Trapezoidal Rule

By a weakly singular kernel
s
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
we mean there exists

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{h<|x|\leq 1}s(x)\operatorname{d}\!{x}=L.

In this paper we assume the weakly singular kernel
s
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and satisfies the following properties:

1.

The Dilation Property:
There exists a
\delta\in(0,n)
and a non-zero smooth function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle s(x)=|x|^{\delta-n}\rho\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

(1)

It follows immediately from
Eq.

1
that
s(hx)=h^{\delta-n}s(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
h>0
.

2.

The Symmetry Property:
There exists an integer
0\leq\kappa\leq n
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle s(x_{1},\cdots,x_{j},\cdots,x_{n})=\begin{cases}-s(x_{1},\cdots,-x_{j},\cdots,x_{n}),&1\leq j\leq\kappa\\
s(x_{1},\cdots,-x_{j},\cdots,x_{n}),&\kappa<j\leq n\end{cases}.

(2)

Remark 2.1
.

One can easily show that weakly singular kernels of the form
s(x)=\frac{x_{1}^{2m+1}x_{j}^{2k}}{|x|^{r}}

m,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

1<j\leq n

1
and
2

\frac{P(x)}{|x|^{r}}

is a monomial such that
\deg P<r<\deg P+n

1
and
2

We focus on weakly singular integrals of the form

\displaystyle I\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi(x)s(x)\operatorname{d}\!{x},

(3)

where
\phi\in C_{c}^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(4)

Let

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{p}[\phi\cdot s]\coloneqq T_{h}^{0}[\phi\cdot s]+h^{\delta}\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{M}_{n,p}}\bar{\omega}_{\beta}\phi(\beta h),

(5)

where
\bar{\omega}_{\beta}
’s are the correction weights to be defined and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n,p}=\left\{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:|\beta|_{1}\leq p,\prod_{1\leq j\leq\kappa}\beta_{j}\not=0\right\}.

(6)

Here,

\displaystyle\int_{(-h,h)^{n}}|\phi(x)|s(x)\ \operatorname{d}\!{x}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{B(0,h)}|\phi(x)|s(x)\ \operatorname{d}\!{x}\lesssim\int_{B(0,h)}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!{x}}{|x|^{n-\delta}}\ \lesssim\int_{0}^{h}r^{-1+\delta}\ \operatorname{d}\!{r}\sim h^{\delta},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\beta}

Remark 3.1

It is not hard to verify that if either

Let

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, for any integer
1\leq j\leq n
, there exist a constant
A>0
, depending on
j\text{ and }k
such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{j}^{k}s(x)|\leq\frac{A}{|x|^{n-\delta+k}}.

Proof.

By induction. Fix

. By the dilation property

1
, there exists a smooth function
\rho_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
s(x)=|x|^{\delta-n}\rho_{0}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)
for all

. Hence

\displaystyle|\partial_{j}^{0}s(x)|=|s(x)|\leq|x|^{\delta-n}\sup_{x\in S^{n-1}}|\rho_{0}(x)|.

Let

and assume there exists a smooth function
\rho_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{j}^{k}s(x)=|x|^{\delta-n-k}\rho_{k}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

(29)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{j}^{k+1}s(x)

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}\left\{|x|^{\delta-n-k}\rho_{k}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=|x|^{\delta-n-k-1}\left[(\delta-n-k)\frac{x_{j}}{|x|}+\left(\partial_{j}\rho_{k}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\rho_{k}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\coloneqq|x|^{\delta-n-k-1}\rho_{k+1}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right).

It is easy to see that

and

\displaystyle|\partial_{j}^{k}s(x)|\leq|x|^{\delta-n-k}\sup_{x\in S^{n-1}}|\rho_{k}(x)|,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

∎

\displaystyle=C\prod_{k=1}^{n}\big(\det{\boldsymbol{D}_{k}}\big)^{\binom{n}{k}}.

where

is a non-zero constant.

Proof.

Applying

4.3
to

, we get

\displaystyle\det\boldsymbol{K}=C\det\boldsymbol{D}_{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(\det\boldsymbol{D}_{n-k})^{\binom{n}{k}}=C\prod_{k=1}^{n}\big(\det{\boldsymbol{D}_{k}}\big)^{\binom{n}{k}}.

We are now focusing on the non-singularity of
\boldsymbol{D_{n}}

Definition 4.4
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the
falling factorial
is defined to be

\displaystyle(x)_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1,

\displaystyle(x)_{m}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{k=1}^{m}(x-k+1)

We only need to following elementary property of the falling factorial.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
m,M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M>m
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m}^{M}(j)_{m}=\frac{(M+1)_{m+1}}{m+1}.

Proof.

Let
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we note that

\displaystyle(j+1)_{k}-(j)_{k}=(j+1)(j)_{k-1}-(j-k+1)(j)_{k-1}=k(j)_{k-1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m}^{M}(j)_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{j=m}^{M}((j+1)_{m+1}-(j)_{m+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m+1}((M+1)_{m+1}-\underbrace{(m)_{m+1}}_{=0})=\frac{(M+1)_{m+1}}{m+1}

∎

We cite a corollary due to Ruiz
[
13
, Corollary 2]
.

Corollary 4.6
.

For all
n\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}{n\choose i}(x-i)^{n-j}=0,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

We establish an algebraic identity.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}:n\geq 2
, for all
0\leq k\leq n-2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{m}{n\choose m}{n-m\choose k}m=0.

(85)

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle LHS

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{m}\frac{n!m}{m!k!(n-m-k)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{k!}\sum_{m=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{m!(n-m-k)!}m={n\choose k}\sum_{m=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{m}\frac{(n-k)!}{m!(n-m-k)!}m

\displaystyle=-{n\choose k}\sum_{m=0}^{K}(-1)^{m+1}{K\choose m}m.\quad(\text{setting }K=n-k)

Invoking
Corollary

4.6
and substituting
n
by
K
,
i
by
m
and letting
x=0
,
j=n-1
, we obtain
LHS=0
.

We present a crucial enumeration lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
j,m,n\in\mathbb{N}:m>j
,

\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m)}(j)=n\mathcal{N}(n-1,m-j).

Proof.

Let
1\leq k\leq n
. We temporarily use the following notation in this proof:

\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m;j,k)=\{\xi\in\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m):\lambda(\xi,j)=k\},

and if
J\in\{1,\cdots,n\}:|J|=k
,

\mathcal{L}^{+}_{J}(n,m;j,k)=\{\xi\in\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m;j,k):\xi_{l}=j\ \forall l\in J,\xi_{l}\not=j\ \forall l\not\in J\}.

It is easy to see that there are
n\choose k
distinct
J
and there are bijections between
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{J}(n,m;j,k)
and
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\{1,\cdots,k\}}(n,m;j,k)
. Therefore, for each fixed
k
, choosing
J=\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m;j,k)}(j)

\displaystyle={n\choose k}k\Big|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:\xi_{l}=j\ \forall l\in J,\ \xi_{l}\not=j\ \forall l\not\in J,\ |\xi|_{1}=m\}\Big|

\displaystyle={n\choose k}k\Big|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k}:(\xi_{1}\not=j)\wedge\cdots\wedge(\xi_{n-k}\not=j),\ |\xi|_{1}=m-kj\}\Big|

\displaystyle={n\choose k}k\Big|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k}:\neg((\xi_{1}=j)\vee\cdots\vee(\xi_{n-k}=j)),\ |\xi|_{1}=m-kj\}\Big|

\displaystyle={n\choose k}k\Big[\big|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k}:|\xi|_{1}=m-kj\}\big|

\displaystyle-\big|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k}:(\xi_{1}=j)\vee\cdots\vee(\xi_{n-k}=j),\ |\xi|_{1}=m-kj\}\big|\Big].

(86)

By Exclusion-inclusion principle in naive set theory, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k}:(\xi_{1}=j)\vee\cdots\vee(\xi_{n-k}=j),\ |\xi|_{1}=m-kj\}\Big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{l+1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{l}\leq n-k}\big|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k}:\xi_{i_{1}}=\cdots=\xi_{i_{l}}=j,\ |\xi|_{1}=m-kj\}\big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{l+1}{n-k\choose l}\big|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k-l}:|\xi|_{1}=m-(k+l)j\}\big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{l+1}{n-k\choose l}\mathcal{N}(n-k-l,m-(k+l)j).

(87)

Combining
Eq.

86
and
Eq.

87
, we get

\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m;j,k)}(j)={n\choose k}k\left(\mathcal{N}(n-k,m-kj)+\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{l}{n-k\choose l}\mathcal{N}(n-k-l,m-(k+l)j)\right).

Using mutually disjoint decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m)=\bigsqcup_{k=0}^{n}\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m;j,k),

we therefore have,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m)}(j)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m;j,k)}(j)

(88)

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}k\mathcal{N}(n-k,m-kj)

(89)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}k\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{l}{n-k\choose l}\mathcal{N}(n-k-l,m-(k+l)j)

(90)

Rearranging the summation,
Eq.

89
can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}k\mathcal{N}(n-k,m-kj)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}(n-k)\mathcal{N}(k,m-(n-k)j),

(91)

and
Eq.

90
becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}k\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{l}{n-k\choose l}\mathcal{N}(n-k-l,m-(k+l)j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{s=1}^{n-t}(-1)^{s}{n\choose t}{n-t\choose s}t\ \mathcal{N}(n-t-s,m-(t+s)j)\quad(\text{changing }k\to t,\ l\to s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{s^{\prime}=0}^{n-t-1}(-1)^{n-t-s^{\prime}}{n\choose t}{n-t\choose n-t-s^{\prime}}t\ \mathcal{N}(s^{\prime},m-(n-s^{\prime})j)\quad(\text{changing }s\to n-t-s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime}=0}^{n-2}\sum_{t=0}^{n-s^{\prime}-1}(-1)^{n-t-s^{\prime}}{n\choose t}{n-t\choose s^{\prime}}t\ \mathcal{N}(s^{\prime},m-(n-s^{\prime})j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k-1}(-1)^{n-k-l}{n\choose l}{n-l\choose k}l\ \mathcal{N}(k,m-(n-k)j).\quad(\text{changing }s^{\prime}\to k,\ t\to l)

(92)

Therefore, combining
Eqs.

91
and
92
,
Eq.

88
becomes

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m)}(j)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}(n-k)\mathcal{N}(k,m-(n-k)j)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n-k}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{n-k-1}(-1)^{l}{n\choose l}{n-l\choose k}l\right]\mathcal{N}(k,m-(n-k)j)

\displaystyle=n\mathcal{N}(n-1,m-j)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{n-k}\underbrace{\left[\sum_{l=1}^{n-k}(-1)^{l}{n\choose l}{n-l\choose k}l\right]}_{=0}\mathcal{N}(k,m-(n-k)j),

(93)

where
Eq.

93
holds due to
Lemma

4.7
. As a result, we have

\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m)}(j)=n\mathcal{N}(n-1,m-j)

(94)

as desired.
∎

We now give a explicit formula for
\mathcal{N}(n,p)
for

Lemma 4.9
.

For all
n\geq 2
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathcal{N}(n,p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{n}(p-1)_{n-1}&p\geq n\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

(95)

where
C_{n}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}
.

Proof.

If
p<n
, then
\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,p)=\varnothing
and
\mathcal{N}(n,p)=0
. We focus on

. Let

,
\mathcal{N}(2,p)=p-1
is clear. We assume the lemma holds for
n
, we now prove the lemma holds for
n+1
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(n+1,p)

\displaystyle=|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1}:|\xi|_{1}=p\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{p-n}|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1}:\xi_{1}=l,\ |\xi|_{1}=p\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=n}^{p-1}|\{\xi\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:|\xi|_{1}=j\}|=\sum_{j=n}^{p-1}\mathcal{N}(n,j)

\displaystyle=C_{n}\sum_{j=n}^{p-1}(j-1)_{n-1}=C_{n}\sum_{j=n-1}^{p-2}(j)_{n-1}.

(96)

By
Lemma

4.5
,
Eq.

leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(n+1,p)=\frac{C_{n}}{n}[(p-1)_{n}-0]=C_{n+1}(p-1)_{n},

(97)

with
C_{n+1}=\frac{1}{n!}
.
∎

Remark 4.4

We come to the theorem addressing the non-singularity of matrix
\boldsymbol{D}_{n}
generated by
\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
that is the culmination of all labors. We will use a algebraic argument. To this end, let
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}:i=1,\cdots,p-n+1
, we define a matrix
\boldsymbol{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)|\times|\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)|}
by

\displaystyle E_{i,j}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-n+1})=\prod_{l=1}^{n}x_{\xi_{j,l}}^{\xi_{i,l}},

(98)

here
\xi_{i},\xi_{j}
are
i
-th and
j
-th elements in
\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
respectively.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
n,p\in\mathbb{N}:n\geq 2,\ p\geq n
.

\displaystyle\det{\boldsymbol{E}}=C\prod_{1\leq j\leq p-n+1}x_{j}^{\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq p-n+1}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)},

(99)

where
C
is s non-zero constant.

Proof.

Denote
F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-n+1})=\det{\boldsymbol{E}}
and
I_{n,p}^{+}=|\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)|
. Let us first show that for each
1\leq j\leq p-n+1
,
x_{j}
divides
F
and has multiplicity at least
\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)
. By construction of
\boldsymbol{E}
, substituting
0
into
x_{j}
we have at least one vanishing column. To see this, note that
(j,1,\cdots,1)\in\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
, so there is one column such that its
i
-th entry is of the form
x_{j}^{\xi_{i,1}}x_{1}^{\sum_{l>1}\xi_{i,l}}
, where
\xi_{i}
is the
i
-th element in
\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
, for all
1\leq i\leq I_{n,p}^{+}
. This column will vanish if
x_{j}=0
, we see that
F=0
and hence
x_{j}
divides
F
. To determine multiplicity, we only need to enumerate the total occurrence of
j
in the set
\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
, this is precisely
\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)
.

Similar reasoning applies to proving
x_{j}-x_{i}
divides
F
with multiplicity at least
\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)
for each pairs of integer
i,j
such that
1\leq i<j\leq p-n+1
. We claim that
\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)\leq\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(i)
. For each
\xi\in\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
such that
\lambda(\xi,j)>0
, replacing all
j
in
\xi
by
i
, we obtain a new vector
\eta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that
|\eta|_{1}<|\xi|_{1}\leq p
, so
\eta\in\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
and the total occurrence of
i
in
\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
is at least as much as that of
j
, i.e.,
\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)\leq\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(i)
. Now, substituting
x_{i}
into
x_{j}
we have two identical columns, which implies
x_{j}-x_{i}
divides
F
. Moreover, the multiplicity of
x_{j}-x_{i}
is at least
\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)
.

We proceed to determine the degree of
F
. Note that
F
is a homogeneous multi-variable polynomial. To see this, by construction of
\boldsymbol{E}
, polynomials in each row share same degree, namely
|\xi_{i}|_{1}
for row
i
, where
\xi_{i}
is the
i
-th element in
\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)
. By Leibniz formula for determinant

\displaystyle F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-n+1})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{I_{n,p}^{+}}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{I_{n,p}^{+}}E_{i,\sigma(i)},

where
S_{m}
is the permutation group on
\{1,\cdots,m\}
, we can deduce that each term in
F
shares the degree
\sum_{i=1}^{I_{n,p}^{+}}|\xi_{i}|_{1}
. To compute this sum, the mutually disjoint decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)=\bigsqcup_{k=n}^{p}\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,k),

gives rise to

\displaystyle\deg F=\sum_{k=n}^{p}k\mathcal{N}(n,k).

(100)

Using
Lemmas

4.5
and
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle\deg F

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n}^{p}k\mathcal{N}(n,k)=C_{n}\sum_{k=n}^{p}k(k-1)_{n-1}

\displaystyle=C_{n}\sum_{k=n}^{p}(k)_{n}=\frac{C_{n}}{n+1}(p+1)_{n+1}=\frac{(p+1)_{n+1}}{(n+1)(n-1)!}.

(101)

Denote

\displaystyle H(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-n+1})=\prod_{1\leq j\leq p-n+1}x_{j}^{\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq p-n+1}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)}.

(102)

Since
H
divides
F
, the proof is complete once we show
\deg F=\deg H
. We compute

\displaystyle\deg H

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{p-n+1}\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)+\sum_{j=2}^{p-n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{p-n+1}j\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j).

(103)

By
Lemmas

4.5
,
4.8
and
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}^{+}(n,p)}(j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=j+n-1}^{p}\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}^{+}(n,m)}(j)

\displaystyle=n\sum_{m=j+n-1}^{p}\mathcal{N}(n-1,m-j)=n\sum_{m=n-1}^{p-j}\mathcal{N}(n-1,m)

\displaystyle=nC_{n-1}\sum_{m=n-1}^{p-j}(m-1)_{n-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(p-j)_{n-1}}{(n-1)(n-2)!}=\frac{n}{(n-1)!}(p-j)_{n-1}.

(104)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\deg H=\frac{n}{(n-1)!}\sum_{j=1}^{p-n+1}j(p-j)_{n-1}.

(105)

We focus on the summation

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p-n+1}j(p-j)_{n-1}=-\sum_{j=1}^{p-n+1}[p-j+1-(p+1)](p-j)_{n-1}

\displaystyle=(p+1)\sum_{j=1}^{p-n+1}(p-j)_{n-1}-\sum_{j=1}^{p-n+1}(p-j+1)_{n}

\displaystyle=(p+1)\sum_{j=n-1}^{p-1}(j)_{n-1}-\sum_{j=n}^{p}(j)_{n}=\frac{(p+1)(p)_{n}}{n}-\frac{(p+1)_{n+1}}{n+1}=\frac{(p+1)_{n+1}}{n(n+1)}.

(106)

Substituting
Eq.

106
into
Eq.

105
, we therefore obtain

\displaystyle\deg H=\frac{n}{(n-1)!}\frac{(p+1)_{n+1}}{n(n+1)}=\frac{(p+1)_{n+1}}{(n+1)(n-1)!}=\deg F.

(107)

This concludes the proof.
∎

\mathcal{I}_{J_{k,l}}(n,p;0,k)
.

Proof.

We first show
\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{H}
. To see this, let
\xi_{i},\xi_{j}
be
i
-th and
j
-th elements in
\mathcal{I}(n,p)
, then by construction of
\boldsymbol{K}
in
Eq.

14
, we have

\displaystyle K_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{G}_{\xi_{j}}}\text{sgn}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\kappa}\beta_{j}\right)\beta^{2\xi_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}e_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{G}_{\xi_{j}}}\frac{\beta^{2\xi_{i}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{\kappa}|\beta_{j}|}=\frac{2^{n-\lambda(\xi_{j},0)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{\kappa}|\beta_{j}|}\xi_{j}^{2\xi_{i}}.

(113)

Hence the factorization follows.

We proceed to show the structure of
\boldsymbol{E}
. By
Eq.

113
and decomposition
Eq.

108
we know that
\boldsymbol{E}
is partitioned by sub-matrices generated by
\mathcal{I}(n,p;0,l)
and
\mathcal{I}(n,p;0,m)
where
l\not=m
, multiplied by
2^{n-m}
. It is suffices to show matrix
\boldsymbol{M}
generated by
\mathcal{I}(n,p;0,l)
and
\mathcal{I}(n,p;0,m)
where
l<m
is
\boldsymbol{0}
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{I}(n,p;0,l)
and
\eta\in\mathcal{I}(n,p;0,m)
, by
l<m
there exists
j\in\{K+1,\cdots,n\}
such that
\xi_{j}\not=0
but
\eta_{j}=0
, so

\displaystyle\eta^{2\xi}=\underbrace{\eta_{j}^{2\xi_{j}}}_{=0}\prod_{i\not=j}\eta_{i}^{2\xi_{i}}=0,

this proves
\boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{0}
and the upper-triangularity block structure of
\boldsymbol{E}
.

We are left to show
\boldsymbol{A}_{k},\ k=1,\cdots,n-\kappa
has the required structure. For each
k=1,\cdots,n-\kappa
, by decomposition
Eq.

109
we know that
\boldsymbol{A}_{k}
is further partitioned by sub-blocks generated by
\mathcal{I}_{J_{k,l}}(n,p;0,k)
and
\mathcal{I}_{J_{k,m}}(n,p;0,k)
where
l\not=m,\ l,m\in\{1,\cdots,\binom{n-\kappa}{k}\}
. Noting that
|J_{k,l}\triangle J_{k,m}|\geq 2
, hence by symmetry it is enough to show matrix
\boldsymbol{M}
generated by
\mathcal{I}_{J_{k,l}}(n,p;0,k)
and
\mathcal{I}_{J_{k,m}}(n,p;0,k)
is
\boldsymbol{0}
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{I}_{J_{k,l}}(n,p;0,k)
and
\eta\in\mathcal{I}_{J_{k,m}}(n,p;0,k)
, assume without loss of generality that
\xi_{1}\not=0
but
\eta_{1}=0
, then a general element in
\boldsymbol{M}
is

\displaystyle\eta^{2\xi}=\underbrace{\eta_{1}^{2\xi_{1}}}_{=0}\prod_{i\not=j}\eta_{i}^{2\xi_{i}}=0,

we have
\boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{0}
and this proves the block diagonal structure of
\boldsymbol{A}_{k}
.

6
Conclusion

We propose an arbitrarily high-order modified trapezoidal rules for a large class of weakly singular integrals with singular part satisfy the dilation property
Eq.

1
and symmetry property
Eq.

2
, and some sufficient smooth function
\phi
with compact support. The rule is a punctured-hole trapezoidal rule with correction terms. We have shown the order of accuracy of the modified quadrature given the number of correction layers. We focus on the error due to singularity in this paper. The rule can be combined with any boundary error correction for regular functions
\phi
without compact support to attain high-order convergence. The correction weights can be pre-computed and stored for future use. We tabulate the correction weights required for the two numerical examples with 20 correct digits. We provide theoretical guarantee that the rule works with arbitrary “correction-layers”
p
in arbitrary
n
dimensions, though additional treatment is necessary whenever large
p
is used (e.g.
p>10
), since numerical evidence suggests that linear systems
Eq.

16
become increasingly ill-conditioned for large
p
. For future works, one can further relax the smoothness criteria for
\phi
in
Theorem

3.1
. In addition, we can adapt the modified trapezoidal rule to other common weakly singular kernels that does not satisfy our hypotheses
Eqs.

1
and
2
, such as
x_{1}\log{(|x|)}
.

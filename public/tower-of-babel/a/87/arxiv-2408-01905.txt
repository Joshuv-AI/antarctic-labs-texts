Enhanced Sensing by Geometric Tuning of YIG Spheres: Noise Reduction, Signal Amplification and Directional Magnetic Field Detection
Zheng Liu, Ding-hui Xu, Yi-jia Yang, and Chang-shui Yu

Abstract
Noise suppression and directional signal enhancement are essential challenges in detecting weak magnetic fields in cavity electrodynamics systems. Traditional schemes struggle to reduce magnonic probe noise but lack directional sensing capabilities. We exploit an innovative and intrinsic squeezing mechanism by leveraging the geometric configuration of an anisotropic ellipsoidal yttrium iron garnet (YIG) sphere and its interaction with internal demagnetization fields. This mechanism can enhance magnetic field signals and suppress noise in the target direction while suppressing sensitivity in non-target directions to avoid disturbing the target direction, thus generating a directionally selective sensing scheme realizing high-precision detection in complex environments. In particular, the target-direction sensor performance can be optimized by adjusting the YIG sphere’s geometry (e.g., aspect ratio) without complex setups, ensuring high feasibility and scalability. Our approach offers greater flexibility and directionality by tuning the YIG sphere’s geometry than existing methods. This innovation provides a new approach for weak magnetic field detection in cavity magnonics systems, with potential applications in biomedical imaging, quantum sensing, precision measurement, and environmental monitoring.

This anisotropic response could have significant practical applications, such as designing noise-resistant quantum sensors, creating directional magnetic field mapping systems, or enabling advanced signal filtering. By leveraging this directional sensing capability, the system can achieve enhanced performance and versatility, paving the way for innovative designs in magnetic sensing technologies.

VI
DISCUSSION and CONCLUSIONS

In this paper, we propose a high-precision weak magnetic field sensing scheme based on geometric modulation and successfully realize magnon squeezing interactions using anisotropic yttrium iron garnet (YIG) spheres. Our study primarily addresses the challenges of signal amplification and noise suppression in directional weak magnetic field sensing within anisotropic cavity magnonic systems, particularly thermal noise from the input probe. To address this issue, we propose reshaping the YIG sphere from its conventional spherical geometry into an ellipsoidal shape, thereby inducing magnon squeezing. This geometric modification effectively adjusts the magnon mode’s effective frequency, providing a parametric amplification effect that enhances the signal response while suppressing additional quantum noise from the cavity field and magnon input thermal noise. We also analyze the impact of the orientation of the ellipsoid’s major axis on weak magnetic field sensing performance and propose the potential for directional sensing, which helps suppress undesired directional signals. This method significantly advances weak magnetic field sensing, offering a new pathway for substantially improving directional sensitivity.

In addition to its application in weak magnetic field sensing, cavity magnonic systems exhibit tremendous potential for dark matter detection. Specifically, interactions between axions and Standard Model particles can generate a pseudo-magnetic field, which magnons can detect. The resulting signal can be observed via optical readout techniques, as proposed in
[
85
]
. Recent advancements in applying cavity magnonic systems for weak magnetic field sensing
[
86
,
87
,
88
,
89
]
provide a robust platform for exploring their potential in dark matter detection. Future research will focus on leveraging quantum resources, such as entanglement, further to enhance the sensitivity of weak magnetic field sensing systems. These advancements can revolutionize weak magnetic field detection technologies, opening new frontiers in quantum sensing and fundamental physics research.

Appendix B
DERIVATION OF THE QUANTUM LANGEVIN EQUATION OF THE SQUEEZED MAGNON MODE

In this section, we present a detailed, rigorous derivation of the quantum Heisenberg-Langevin equation of the squeezed magnon. the total Hamiltonian is
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{M}+\hat{H}_{B}+\hat{H}_{M-B}
, as follows

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{M}

\displaystyle=\hbar\omega^{\prime}_{0}\hat{M}^{\dagger}\hat{M},

(40)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{B}

\displaystyle=\hbar\int d\omega\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)\hat{B}(\omega),

(41)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{M-B}

\displaystyle=\hbar\int d\omega g_{MB}(\omega)\left[\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{B}(\omega)+\hat{m}\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)\right],

(42)

where
\hat{H}_{M}
is the Hamiltonian of the squeezed magnon mode,

\hat{H}_{B}
is the Hamiltonian of the bath,

\hat{H}_{M-B}
is the interaction Hamiltonian between the original magnon mode and the environment thermal bath.
According to the squeezing transformation
\hat{m}=\cosh r_{m}\hat{M}-\sinh r_{m}\hat{M}^{\dagger}
, the
\hat{H}_{M-B}
can be given as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{M-B}

\displaystyle=\hbar\int d\omega g_{MB}(\omega)\left[\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{B}(\omega)+\hat{m}\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)\right]

\displaystyle=\hbar\int d\omega g_{MB}(\omega)[\hat{M}^{\dagger}\left(\cosh(r_{m})B(\omega)-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)\right)

\displaystyle+\hat{M}\left(\cosh(r_{m})\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{B}(\omega)\right)].

(43)

The Langevin equation is derived using the Heisenberg equations

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{M}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-i\omega^{\prime}_{0}\hat{M}-i\int d\omega g_{MB}(\omega)(\cosh(r_{m})\hat{B}(\omega)-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{B}(\omega)}{dt}

\displaystyle=-i\omega\hat{B}(\omega)-ig_{MB}(\omega)\left[\cosh(r_{m})\hat{M}-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{M}^{\dagger}\right].

(44)

Next, The formal solution to the
\hat{B}(\omega)(t)
,
\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)(t)
equation can be given as

\displaystyle\hat{B}(\omega)(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{B}(\omega)(0)e^{-i\omega t}-i\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,g_{MB}(\omega)[\cosh(r_{m})\hat{M}(\tau)-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)]e^{-i\omega(t-\tau)},

\displaystyle\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)(0)e^{i\omega t}+i\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,g_{MB}(\omega)\big[\cosh(r_{m})\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{M}(\tau)\big]e^{i\omega(t-\tau)}.

(45)

Substitute the formal solution for
\hat{B}(\omega)
,
\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)
into the equation for
\hat{M}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{M}}{dt}=

\displaystyle-i\omega^{\prime}_{0}\hat{M}-i\int d\omega g_{MB}(\omega)\Bigg\{\cosh(r_{m})\Bigg[\hat{B}(\omega)(0)e^{-i\omega t}-i\int_{0}^{t}d\tau g_{MB}(\omega)\big[\cosh(r_{m})\hat{M}(\tau)

\displaystyle-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)\big]e^{-i\omega(t-\tau)}\Bigg]-\sinh(r_{m})\Bigg[\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)(0)e^{i\omega t}+i\int_{0}^{t}d\tau g_{MB}(\omega)\big[\cosh(r_{m})\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)

\displaystyle-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{M}(\tau)\big]e^{i\omega(t-\tau)}\Bigg]\Bigg\}.

(46)

It can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{M}}{dt}=-i\omega^{\prime}_{0}\hat{M}-i\int d\omega g_{MB}(\omega)\Big[\cosh(r_{m})\hat{B}(\omega)(0)e^{-i\omega t}-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)(0)e^{i\omega t}\Big]-\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,K_{1}(t-\tau),

(47)

where the memory kernel
K_{1}(t-\tau)
is:

\displaystyle K_{1}(t-\tau)

\displaystyle=\cosh^{2}(r_{m})\int d\omega g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)e^{-i\omega(t-\tau)}\hat{M}(\tau)-\sinh^{2}(r_{m})\int d\omega g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)e^{i\omega(t-\tau)}\hat{M}(\tau)

\displaystyle-\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})\int d\omega g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)e^{-i\omega(t-\tau)}\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)

\displaystyle+\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})\int d\omega g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)e^{i\omega(t-\tau)}\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau).

(48)

To simplify, let us define

\displaystyle G^{+}(t-\tau)

\displaystyle=\int d\omega g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)e^{-i\omega(t-\tau)},

\displaystyle G^{-}(t-\tau)

\displaystyle=\int d\omega g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)e^{i\omega(t-\tau)}.

(49)

Using these expressions, the memory kernel can be rewritten as

\displaystyle K_{1}(t-\tau)

\displaystyle=\cosh^{2}(r_{m})G^{+}(t-\tau)\hat{M}(\tau)-\sinh^{2}(r_{m})G^{-}(t-\tau)\hat{M}(\tau)

\displaystyle-\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})G^{+}(t-\tau)\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)

\displaystyle+\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})G^{-}(t-\tau)\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau).

(50)

In most physical scenarios, the coupling strength squared,
g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)
, is symmetric concerning frequency. As a result, we have

\displaystyle G^{-}(t-\tau)=\big[G^{+}(t-\tau)\big]^{*}.

(51)

Substituting this symmetry into the expression gives

\displaystyle K_{1}(t-\tau)

\displaystyle=\cosh^{2}(r_{m})G^{+}(t-\tau)\hat{M}(\tau)-\sinh^{2}(r_{m})\big[G^{+}(t-\tau)\big]^{*}\hat{M}(\tau)

\displaystyle-\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})G^{+}(t-\tau)\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)

\displaystyle+\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})\big[G^{+}(t-\tau)\big]^{*}\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau).

(52)

Expand
G^{+}(t-\tau)
and
G^{-}(t-\tau)
as

\displaystyle G^{+}(t-\tau)=R(t-\tau)+iI(t-\tau),

(53)

where

R(t-\tau)=\operatorname{Re}[G^{+}(t-\tau)]\quad\text{and}\quad I(t-\tau)=\operatorname{Im}[G^{+}(t-\tau)],

represent the real and imaginary parts of
G^{+}(t-\tau)
, respectively.
Similarly, the conjugate is

\displaystyle\big[G^{+}(t-\tau)\big]^{*}=R(t-\tau)-iI(t-\tau).

(54)

Combining all terms, the simplified memory kernel becomes as

\displaystyle K_{1}(t-\tau)

\displaystyle=R(t-\tau)\big[\hat{M}(\tau)-2i\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})I(t-\tau)\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)\big]

\displaystyle\quad+iI(t-\tau)\big(\cosh^{2}(r_{m})+\sinh^{2}(r_{m})\big)\hat{M}(\tau),

(55)

For the real part
R(t-\tau)=\operatorname{Re}[G^{+}(t-\tau)]
, under the Markov approximation,
R(t-\tau)\approx\kappa_{m}\delta(t-\tau)
.
For the imaginary part
I(t-\tau)=\operatorname{Im}[G^{+}(t-\tau)]
, this term typically represents a frequency shift. If included, it can also be approximated as:

I(t-\tau)\approx\Delta\delta(t-\tau),

Where
\Delta
corresponds to an effective frequency shift from the imaginary part. Substituting the Markov approximation, the memory kernel becomes as

\displaystyle K_{1}(t-\tau)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{m}\delta(t-\tau)\hat{M}(\tau)

\displaystyle-2i\Delta\delta(t-\tau)\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})\hat{M}^{\dagger}(\tau)

\displaystyle\quad+i\Delta\delta(t-\tau)\big(\cosh^{2}(r_{m})+\sinh^{2}(r_{m})\big)\hat{M}(\tau).

(56)

The
\delta(t-\tau)
ensures that the memory kernel’s influence is instantaneous, affecting only the current time. Then, substituting the simplified memory kernel into the original Langevin equation as

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{M}}{dt}=-i\omega^{\prime}_{0}\hat{M}-\int_{0}^{t}d\tau K_{1}(t-\tau),

(57)

thus we yield

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{M}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-i\omega^{\prime}_{0}\hat{M}-(\kappa_{m}/2)\hat{M}(t)-i\Delta\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})\hat{M}^{\dagger}(t)

\displaystyle-i\Delta/2\big(\cosh^{2}(r_{m})+\sinh^{2}(r_{m})\big)\hat{M}(t)+F_{M}(t).

(58)

The above can also be simplified as

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{M}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-i\big(\omega^{\prime}_{0}+\frac{\Delta}{2}(2\cosh^{2}(r_{m})-1)\big)\hat{M}(t)-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\hat{M}(t)

\displaystyle-i\Delta\cosh(r_{m})\sinh(r_{m})\hat{M}^{\dagger}(t)+F_{M}(t),

(59)

where
F_{M}(t)
is the noise term, describing random fluctuations from the environment, which can give as

\displaystyle F_{M}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\int d\omega g_{MB}(\omega)[\cosh(r_{m})\hat{B}(\omega)(0)e^{-i\omega t}

\displaystyle-\sinh(r_{m})\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)(0)e^{i\omega t}].

(60)

Using the bath expectation value as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega)\hat{B}(\omega^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n(\omega)\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\langle\hat{B}(\omega)\hat{B}^{\dagger}(\omega^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n(\omega)+1)\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime}),

(61)

and assuming the coupling strength
g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)
is slowly varying, and the thermal occupation
n(\omega)
can be approximated by its value
\bar{n}_{M}
at a central frequency (
\omega^{\prime}_{0}
), the integral simplifies to

\displaystyle\int d\omega g_{MB}^{2}(\omega)n(\omega)e^{i\omega(t+t^{\prime})}\approx\kappa_{m}\bar{n}_{M}\delta(t+t^{\prime}),

(62)

Substituting this back, the correlation functions can be given as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{F}_{M}(t)\hat{F}_{M}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{m}\sinh(2r_{m})\left(\bar{n}_{M}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\langle\hat{F}_{M}(t)\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{m}\delta(t-t^{\prime})[\cosh(2r_{m})\bar{n}_{M}+\sinh^{2}(r_{m})+1].

(63)

In practical problems, the frequency shift effect can often be neglected, allowing us to ignore the
\Delta
term. The resulting Langevin equation is then given as

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{M}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i(\omega^{\prime}_{0}-i\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2})\hat{M}(t)+\hat{F}_{M}(t).

(64)

This equation characterizes the squeezed magnon mode dynamics of the system.

Next, we introduce the all correlation function for the input noise, which is defined as follows according to Equation (B24)

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}(t)\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle=\kappa_{m}[\cosh(2r_{m})\bar{n}_{M}+\sinh^{2}(r_{m})+1]\delta\left(t-t^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle=\kappa_{m}\sinh(2r_{m})(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)\delta\left(t-t^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}(t)\hat{F}_{M}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle=\kappa_{m}\sinh(2r_{m})(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)\delta\left(t-t^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{F}_{M}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle=\kappa_{m}[\cosh(2r_{m})\bar{n}_{M}+\sinh^{2}(r_{m})]\delta\left(t-t^{\prime}\right).

(65)

We also present the quadrature components of the amplitude and phase for the squeezed magnon mode, which can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle\hat{X}_{M}^{\rm in}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hat{M}_{in}^{\dagger}+\hat{M}_{in})/\sqrt{2},

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{M}^{\rm in}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hat{M}_{in}-\hat{M}_{in}^{\dagger})/\sqrt{2}i.

(66)

The correlation functions for the amplitude and phase quadrature components of the squeezed magnon mode are given as follows

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{X}_{M}^{\rm in}(t)\hat{X}_{M}^{\rm in}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}(t)\hat{F}_{\rm M}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}(t)\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{F}_{M}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\left[2\sinh(2r_{m})(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)+2\cosh(2r_{m})\bar{n}_{M}+\cosh(2r_{m})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\left[2\sinh(2r_{m})(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)+2\cosh(2r_{m})(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)\right]

\displaystyle=\kappa_{m}e^{2r_{m}}(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)\delta\left(t-t^{\prime}\right).

(67)

\displaystyle\left\langle\hat{P}_{M}^{\rm in}(t)\hat{P}_{M}^{\rm in}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}(t)\hat{F}_{\rm M}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle-\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}(t)\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle-\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{F}_{M}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{F}_{M}^{\dagger}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\left[2\sinh(2r_{m})(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)-2\cosh(2r_{m})\bar{n}_{M}-\cosh(2r_{m})\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\left[2\sinh(2r_{m})(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)-2\cosh(2r_{m})(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)\right]

\displaystyle=\kappa_{m}e^{-2r_{m}}(\bar{n}_{M}+1/2)\delta\left(t-t^{\prime}\right).

(68)

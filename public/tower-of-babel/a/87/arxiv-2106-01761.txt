Near Optimal Stochastic Algorithms for Finite-Sum Unbalanced Convex-Concave Minimax Optimization
Luo Luo, Guangzeng Xie Tong Zhang Zhihua Zhang

Abstract
This paper considers stochastic first-order algorithms for convex-concave minimax problems of the form min 𝐱 ⁡ max 𝐲 ⁡ f ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 𝐲 ) \min_{\bf x}\max_{\bf y}f(\bf x,\bf y) , where f f can be presented by the average of n n individual components which are L L -average smooth. For μ x \mu_{x} -strongly-convex- μ y \mu_{y} -strongly-concave setting, we propose a new stochastic algorithm which could find an ε \varepsilon -saddle point of the problem in 𝒪 ~ ​ ( n ⁡ ( n + κ x ) ​ ( n + κ y ) ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{n(\sqrt{n}+\kappa_{x})(\sqrt{n}+\kappa_{y})}\log(1/\varepsilon)) stochastic first-order complexity, where κ x ≜ L / μ x \kappa_{x}\triangleq L/\mu_{x} and κ y ≜ L / μ y \kappa_{y}\triangleq L/\mu_{y} . This upper bound is near optimal with respect to ε \varepsilon , n n , κ x \kappa_{x} and κ y \kappa_{y} simultaneously. In addition, the algorithm is easily implemented and works well in practice. Our methods can be extended to solve more general unbalanced convex-concave minimax problems and the corresponding upper complexity bounds are also near optimal.

Accelerating Unbalanced Convex-Concave Optimization

In this section, we first revisit loopless stochastic variance reduced extragradient (L-SVRE)
[
1
]
, and show it can be used to solve balanced strongly-convex-strongly-concave (SCSC) minimax with optimal SFO complexity.
Then we propose a Catalyst-type scheme to accelerate L-SVRE, deriving our new algorithm

(AL-SVRE) whose SFO upper bound is near optimal for different types of unbalanced concave-concave minimax problems.

Algorithm 1

\text{L-SVRE}~\left(\{f_{i}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\}_{i=1}^{n},({\bf{x}}_{0},{\bf{y}}_{0}),\tau,p,T\right)

1:

Initialize:

{\bf{w}}_{0}={\bf{z}}_{0}
.

2:

for

3:

5:

6:

{\bf{z}}_{k+1}={\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\bar{{\bf{z}}}_{k}-\tau[{\bf{g}}({\bf{w}}_{k})+{\bf{g}}_{i}({\bf{z}}_{k+1/2})-{\bf{g}}_{i}({\bf{w}}_{k})])

{\bf{x}}_{k}={\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\left(\tilde{\bf{x}}_{k}-\tau_{k}\nabla_{{\bf{x}}}F_{k}\big(\tilde{\bf{x}}_{k},\tilde{\bf{y}}_{k}\big)\right)

5:

{\bf{y}}_{k}={\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\left(\tilde{\bf{y}}_{k}+\tau_{k}\nabla_{{\bf{y}}}F_{k}\big(\tilde{\bf{x}}_{k},\tilde{\bf{y}}_{k}\big)\right)

6:

{\bf{u}}_{k}={\bf{x}}_{k}+\gamma({\bf{x}}_{k}-{\bf{x}}_{k-1})

7:

end for

8:

Output:

({\bf{x}}_{K},{\bf{y}}_{K})
.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
\mu_{x}=\mu_{y}=\mu
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is
L
-average smooth. Then Algorithm
1
with probability parameter
p=1/(2n)
and stepsize
\tau=1/(4\sqrt{n}L)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|{\bf{z}}_{k}-{\bf{z}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4\left\|{\bf{z}}_{0}-{\bf{z}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{4(n+2\sqrt{n}L/\mu)}\right)^{k}\!.

(5)

The result of inequality (

) means that L-SVRE could find
(\hat{\bf{x}},\hat{\bf{y}})
satisfying
{\mathbb{E}}[\left\|\hat{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\hat{\bf{y}}-{\bf{y}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}]\leq{\varepsilon}

.

3.2
Acceleration for Unbalanced SCSC Minimax

Note that the convergence result of L-SVRE is not perfect when the objective function is unbalanced. For example, we consider problem (
1
) in the case of
\kappa_{x}=\kappa>\sqrt{n}>\kappa_{y}={\mathcal{O}}(1)
. Then Theorem
2
cannot leverage the well-conditioned assumption on
{\bf{y}}
and leads to SFO upper bound
{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sqrt{n}\kappa\log(1/{\varepsilon}))\right)
, which is
worse than complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(n\sqrt{\kappa}\log^{3}(1/{\varepsilon}))
achieved by deterministic algorithms
[
22
]
.

Our key intuition to establish a better SFO algorithm for unbalanced SCSC minimax problems is taking the advantage of the optimality of L-SVRE in the balanced case. We present the new method AL-SVRE in Algorithm
2
, whose iteration applies L-SVRE to solve the following sub-problem

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}F_{k}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\triangleq f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{u}}_{k-1}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(6)

Since Theorem
2
only provides the convergence rate for L-SVRE by distance, we introduce additional projection gradient iterations (Lines 4-5 of Algorithm
2
) on the output of the sub-problem solver.
These steps help us control the accuracy with respect to primal and dual functions, which is helpful to establish the convergence results with respect to the duality gap in our main result Theorem
3
.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose the function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})\colon{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
(\mu_{x},\mu_{y})
-convex-concave and
L
-smooth. Denote the saddle point of
\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
by
({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})
and let the condition numbers of
f
be
\kappa_{x}=L/\mu_{x}
and
\kappa_{y}=L/\mu_{y}
. Assume that the point
(\hat{\bf{x}},\hat{\bf{y}})
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\hat{\bf{y}}-{\bf{y}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq{\varepsilon}.

We introduce

\displaystyle\tilde{\bf{x}}={\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\left(\hat{\bf{x}}-\eta\nabla_{{\bf{x}}}f(\hat{\bf{x}},\hat{\bf{y}})\right)~~\text{and}~~\tilde{\bf{y}}={\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}\left(\hat{\bf{y}}+\eta\nabla_{{\bf{y}}}f(\hat{\bf{x}},\hat{\bf{y}})\right),

then it holds that

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f(\tilde{\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})-f({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})\leq\left(\sqrt{2}(1+\eta L)+2(1+\eta L)^{2}+2\right)\kappa_{y}L{\varepsilon}+\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2\eta}

and

\displaystyle f({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})-\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}f({\bf{x}},\tilde{\bf{y}})\leq\left(\sqrt{2}(1+\eta L)+2(1+\eta L)^{2}+2\right)\kappa_{x}L{\varepsilon}+\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2\eta}.

The following lemma shows that we can upper bound the distance from given point to the saddle point by its primal-dual gap.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose the function
f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}):{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
(\mu_{x},\mu_{y})
-convex-concave and
L
-smooth. Denote the saddle point of
f
by
({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})
. Then for any
\hat{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}},\hat{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\left\|\hat{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\mu_{y}\left\|\hat{\bf{y}}-{\bf{y}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\left(\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f(\hat{\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})-\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}f({\bf{x}},\hat{\bf{y}})\right).

Then introduces four auxiliary quantities and provide their upper bounds, which are useful to the analyze the convergence of AL-SVRE.

Lemma 3
.

We use the notation of Algorithm
2
and denote
({\bf{x}}_{k}^{*},{\bf{y}}_{k}^{*})
is the saddle point of
F_{k}
and

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}=\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f({\bf{x}}_{0},{\bf{y}})-\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}_{0}).

Then, for each
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bf{x}}_{k}-{\bf{x}}_{k}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|{\bf{y}}_{k}-{\bf{y}}_{k}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq{\varepsilon}_{k},

(7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}F_{k}({\bf{x}}_{k},{\bf{y}})-F_{k}({\bf{x}}_{k}^{*},{\bf{y}}_{k}^{*})\right]\leq\frac{2\Delta_{f}}{9}(1-\rho)^{k},

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|{\bf{x}}_{k}-{\bf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2\delta_{k}}{\mu_{x}},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bf{x}}^{*}_{k}-{\bf{x}}^{*}_{k+1}\right\|_{2}^{2}\!+\!\left\|{\bf{y}}^{*}_{k}-{\bf{y}}^{*}_{k+1}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{72\beta\delta_{k-2}}{\mu_{x}\min\{\mu_{x},\mu_{y}\}},

(10)

where

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}_{k}\triangleq\frac{2\mu_{y}\Delta_{f}(1-\rho)^{k}}{3(L+\beta)(7(L+\beta)+2\sqrt{n}\mu_{y})}

and

\displaystyle\delta_{k}\triangleq\frac{8\Delta_{f}(1-\rho)^{k+1}}{(\sqrt{q}-\rho)^{2}}.

Based on above lemmas, we obtain the main result of this paper as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Running Algorithm
2
with
q=\mu_{x}/(\mu_{x}+\beta)
,
\beta\geq 0
,
p=1/2n
,
\tau_{k}=1/(4\sqrt{n}(L+\beta))
and

\displaystyle T_{k}=\Bigg\lceil 4\left(n+\frac{2\sqrt{n}(L+\beta)}{\min\{\mu_{x}+\beta,\mu_{y}\}}\right)\log\left(\!12\!\left(\frac{2}{1-\rho}\!+\!\frac{1728\beta(L+\beta)(7(L+\beta)+2\sqrt{n}\mu_{y}))}{\mu_{x}\mu_{y}\min\{\mu_{x},\mu_{y}\}(1-\rho)^{2}(\sqrt{q}-\rho)^{2}}\right)\right)\Bigg\rceil,

where
\rho<\sqrt{q}
,
then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f({\bf{x}}_{k},{\bf{y}})-\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}_{k})\right]\leq\frac{916\Delta_{f}\left(\kappa_{x}L+\sqrt{n}(L+\beta)\right)\kappa_{y}^{2}}{\mu_{x}(\sqrt{q}-\rho)^{2}}(1-\rho)^{k},

where
\Delta_{f}=\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f({\bf{x}}_{0},{\bf{y}})-\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}_{0})
.

Theorem
3
shows the
{\varepsilon}
-saddle point can be obtained by calling
K=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(\sqrt{(\mu_{x}{+}\beta)/\mu_{x}}\log(1/{\varepsilon})\big)
times AL-SVRE to solve the sub-problem. By minimizing the product of dominant terms (ignore logarithmic terms)
\sqrt{(\mu_{x}+\beta)/\mu_{x}}
and
\left(n+2\sqrt{n}(L+\beta)/\min\{\mu_{x}+\beta,\mu_{y}\}\right)
in
K
and
T_{k}
respectively, we decide the choice of
\beta
as follows (since we have suppose
\mu_{x}\leq\mu_{y}
)

\displaystyle\beta=\begin{cases}\mu_{y}-\mu_{x},~~&\text{if }\kappa_{y}\geq\sqrt{n},\\
L/\sqrt{n}-\mu_{x},~~&\text{if }\kappa_{x}>\sqrt{n}>\kappa_{y},\\
0,~~&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(11)

Combing the result of Theorem
3
and equation (
11
), we immediately obtain the following corollary, which implies AL-SVRE has near optimal SFO upper bound for unbalanced SCSC minimax optimization.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose Problem (
1
) satisfies
\mu_{x}<\mu_{y}
. If running AL-SVRE with the setting of Theorem
3
and letting
\beta
as (
11
),
\rho=0.5\sqrt{q}
and

\displaystyle K=\left\lceil\frac{2}{\sqrt{q}}\log\left(10992\sqrt{n}\Delta_{f}\kappa_{y}\kappa_{x}^{3}\right)\right\rceil,

then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{{\bf{y}}\in{\mathcal{Y}}}f({\bf{x}}_{K},{\bf{y}})-\min_{{\bf{x}}\in{\mathcal{X}}}f({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}_{K})\Big]\leq{\varepsilon}

with the number of SFO calls at most

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sqrt{n(\sqrt{n}+\kappa_{x})(\sqrt{n}+\kappa_{y})}\log(n\kappa_{x})\log\left(\frac{n\Delta_{f}\kappa_{y}\kappa_{x}}{{\varepsilon}}\right)\right).

The design of AL-SVRE aims to sufficiently take the advantage of AL-SVRE’s optimality for the balanced SCSC minimax problem. Hence, the choice of
\beta
makes the sub-problem (
6
) more balanced than the main problem (
1
). In contrast, previous catalyst-type methods
[
22
,
35
,
33
]
select a larger
\beta
to guarantee the sub-problem be well-conditioned in one of the variables. However, such strategy leads to that the sub-problem is still so unbalanced and we need two-loops of iterations to solve it. As a result, the implementation of the whole procedure is more complicated and almost cannot be used in practical.

Discussion

Note that
Tominin et al. 2021
’s work supposes each
f_{i}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
is
L_{i}
-smooth, that is

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\bf{z}}_{1})-\nabla f_{i}({\bf{z}}_{2})\right\|_{2}\leq L_{i}\left\|{\bf{z}}_{1}-{\bf{z}}_{2}\right\|_{2}

(12)

where
{\bf{z}}_{1}=({\bf{x}}_{1},{\bf{y}}_{1})
and
{\bf{z}}_{2}=({\bf{x}}_{2},{\bf{y}}_{2})
, which leads to the objective function
f({\bf{z}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}({\bf{z}})
is
L_{G}
-smooth with
L_{G}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}
.
Furthermore, we can verify that
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is
L_{A}
-average smooth, where
L_{A}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}^{2}}
(c.f. Lemma
11
).

Consider a simple class of functions
\{g_{i}({\bf{z}})\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that
g_{i}({\bf{z}})=\frac{\sqrt{n}L}{2}({\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}{\bf{z}})^{2}
, where
\{{\bf{e}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is the standard basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
It is easily to check that
g_{i}
is
\sqrt{n}L
-smooth and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\nabla g_{i}({\bf{z}}_{1})-\nabla g_{i}({\bf{z}}_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(nL^{2})\left\|{\bf{e}}_{i}{\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bf{z}}_{1}-{\bf{z}}_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}=L^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(z_{1,i}-z_{2,i})^{2}=L^{2}\left\|{\bf{z}}_{1}-{\bf{z}}_{2}\right\|_{2}^{2},

which implies that
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is
L
-average smooth.

Using notations of
Tominin et al. 2021
on
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, we have
L_{i}=\sqrt{n}L
and
L_{G}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}=\sqrt{n}L
. Hence, their smoothness parameter
L_{G}
is larger than the average-smoothness parameter
L
we used in this paper.

Conclusions

In this paper we have studied unbalanced convex-concave minimax problems with finite-sum structure. We have shown the optimality of L-SVRE for balanced SCSC minimax and proposed a near optimal algorithm AL-SVRE for unbalanced problems. AL-SVRE only contains two loops of iterations, making its implementation more simple and practical than the existing methods for unbalanced SCSC minimax. We have also extended our algorithm to more general convex-concave minimax problems and showed the near optimality.

It would be interesting to apply our ideas to solve specific convex-concave minimax with refined smoothness assumptions and specific bilinear settings
[
35
,
38
,
28
]
.
It is also possible to design accelerated variance reduced algorithms to solve unbalanced minimax problems in online setting.

Lemma 10

Lemma 12
.

is

-average-smooth, then

Proof.

And we provide the lower bound for finding approximate saddle point of Problem (

Theorem 5
.

For the parameter
L,\mu,n,{\varepsilon}
such that
L/\mu>2
and
{\varepsilon}<\frac{1}{8}
, the functions
\hat{f}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
and
\hat{f}_{i}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})
defined in Problem (
44
) satisfy
\hat{f}
is
(\mu,\mu)
-convex-concave and
\{\hat{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is
L
-average-smooth. Moreover, when we employ any SFO algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}
to solve the Problem (
44
), there holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bf{x}}^{(t)}-{\bf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|{\bf{y}}^{(t)}-{\bf{y}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]>{\varepsilon}~~~\text{ for }t\leq n/2.

Proof.

It is easily to check that

\displaystyle\hat{f}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})=\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|{\bf{x}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\hat{L}}{2\sqrt{n}}\left\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|{\bf{y}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\hat{L}}{2\sqrt{n}}\left\|{\bf{y}}-{\bf{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence
\hat{f}
is
(\mu,\mu)
-convex-concave and the saddle point
({\bf{x}}^{*},{\bf{y}}^{*})
of
\hat{f}
satisfies

\displaystyle{\bf{x}}^{*}={\bf{y}}^{*}=\frac{\hat{L}}{\hat{L}+\sqrt{n}\mu}{\bf{1}}.

Next, observe that

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bf{x}}}\hat{f}_{i}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})=

\displaystyle\mu{\bf{x}}+\sqrt{n}\hat{L}({\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}{\bf{x}}-1){\bf{e}}_{i},

(45)

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bf{y}}}\hat{f}_{i}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})=

\displaystyle-\mu{\bf{y}}-\sqrt{n}\hat{L}({\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}{\bf{y}}-1){\bf{e}}_{i}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\left\|\nabla_{{\bf{x}}}\hat{f}_{i}({\bf{x}}_{1},{\bf{y}}_{1})-\nabla_{{\bf{x}}}\hat{f}_{i}({\bf{x}}_{1},{\bf{y}}_{1})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\nabla_{{\bf{y}}}\hat{f}_{i}({\bf{x}}_{1},{\bf{y}}_{1})-\nabla_{{\bf{y}}}\hat{f}_{i}({\bf{x}}_{1},{\bf{y}}_{1})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\left\|\mu({\bf{x}}_{1}-{\bf{x}}_{2})+\sqrt{n}\hat{L}{\bf{e}}_{i}{\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bf{x}}_{1}-{\bf{x}}_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\mu({\bf{y}}_{1}-{\bf{y}}_{2})+\sqrt{n}\hat{L}{\bf{e}}_{i}{\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bf{y}}_{1}-{\bf{y}}_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(2\mu^{2}\left\|{\bf{x}}_{1}-{\bf{x}}_{2}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2n\hat{L}^{2}\left\|{\bf{e}}_{i}{\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bf{x}}_{1}-{\bf{x}}_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\mu^{2}\left\|{\bf{y}}_{1}-{\bf{y}}_{2}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2n\hat{L}^{2}\left\|{\bf{e}}_{i}{\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}({\bf{y}}_{1}-{\bf{y}}_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2(\hat{L}^{2}+\mu^{2})\left(\left\|{\bf{x}}_{1}-{\bf{x}}_{2}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|{\bf{y}}_{1}-{\bf{y}}_{2}\right\|_{2}\right)=L^{2}\left(\left\|{\bf{x}}_{1}-{\bf{x}}_{2}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|{\bf{y}}_{1}-{\bf{y}}_{2}\right\|_{2}\right),

where the first inequality is according to
\left\|{\bf{a}}+{\bf{b}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\left\|{\bf{a}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\left\|{\bf{b}}\right\|_{2}^{2}
and the last equation follows from

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|{\bf{e}}_{i}{\bf{e}}_{i}^{\top}{\bf{u}}\right\|_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}=\left\|{\bf{u}}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence,
\{\hat{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is
L
-average-smooth.

Moreover, by Equation (
45
) and definition of SFO algorithm, we know that

\displaystyle{\bf{x}}^{(t)},{\bf{y}}^{(t)}\in\spn\left\{{\bf{e}}_{i_{1}},\dots,{\bf{e}}_{i_{t}}\right\}.

Consequently, for
t\leq n/2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\left\|{\bf{x}}^{(t)}-{\bf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|{\bf{y}}^{(t)}-{\bf{y}}^{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(n-t)\cdot\frac{\hat{L}^{2}}{(\hat{L}+\sqrt{n}\mu)^{2}}\geq\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{\hat{L}^{2}}{2\hat{L}^{2}+2n\mu^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}\cdot\frac{L^{2}/2-\mu^{2}}{L^{2}-2\mu^{2}+2n\mu^{2}}=\frac{n}{4}\left(1-\frac{2n}{L^{2}/\mu^{2}+2n-2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{4(n+1)}\geq\frac{1}{8}>{\varepsilon},

where we have recalled that
\hat{L}^{2}=L^{2}/2-\mu^{2}
,
L/\mu>2
and
n\geq 1
.
∎

Combining Theorem
4

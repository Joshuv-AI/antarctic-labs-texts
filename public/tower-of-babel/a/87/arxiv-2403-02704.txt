Geometric Certificates for Deterministic Global Linear Convergence of Overparameterized Projected Gradient Descent

Introduction

Low-rank estimation problems in inverse problems, signal processing, and
machine learning frequently take the form

\min_{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:\,\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})\leq r}f(\mathbf{X}).

(1)

We assume that
f
is twice continuously differentiable and has an
unconstrained minimizer
\mathbf{X}_{*}
of rank
r_{*}
, with

1\leq r_{*}\leq r<n
. The matrix variable need not be symmetric or
positive semidefinite; the square formulation is only for simplicity.
The search rank
r
may exceed the intrinsic rank
r_{*}
.

Two standard approaches to (
1
) are Burer–Monteiro (BM)
factorization
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T}
, followed by optimization in the factors

[
7
,
22
,
33
]
,
and projected gradient descent (ProjGD), which takes a Euclidean gradient
step and truncates the result to rank
r

[
14
,
28
]
.
Ha, Liu, and Barber
[
13
]
identified a
fundamental connection: under an appropriate local step-size bound,
second-order stationary points of the factored objective yield ProjGD
fixed points. Their work includes results for overestimated search rank,
but its qualitative correspondence alone does not ensure that the

iterates
of ProjGD converge at a uniform linear rate.
Here we answer the question through an explicit geometric
certificate whose strength improves as
r/r_{*}
increases.

1.1
Main contribution: from a geometric certificate to ProjGD convergence

Write
\mathcal{M}_{r}=\{\mathbf{X}:\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})\leq r\}
,
and let
T(\mathbf{X})
denote the tangent space to the fixed-rank stratum
at a rank-
r
matrix
\mathbf{X}
. For a rank-
r
stationary point of (
1
),
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
. The first ingredient is
a rank-dependent normal-gradient certificate:

{\quad\frac{\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|}{\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})}\geq\frac{2L\mu}{L-\mu}\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}.\quad}

(2)

This holds whenever
0<\mu<L
and the objective has the required
rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted curvature. We note that for the case
L=\mu
, no nonoptimal rank-
r
stationary point exists.
The ratio on the left, together with the step size, determines whether a normal singular direction can displace one of the retained singular directions during rank-
r
projection of ProjGD. Indeed, for a
rank-
r
point, the ProjGD fixed-point criterion is

P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0,\qquad\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|\leq\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X}).

(3)

The two inequalities are incompatible for any
\mathbf{X}\neq\mathbf{X}_{*}
whenever

\eta>\sqrt{r_{*}/r}(L-\mu)/(2L\mu)
. As a result,
\mathbf{X}_{*}
is the unique fixed point of ProjGD. Proposition
4

formalizes this argument.

The stronger consequence is on the iterative update of ProjGD, rather than only its stationary points.
Theorem
6
proves that, when rank-
(r+r_{*})

restricted curvature condition is supplemented by rank-
2r
restricted
smoothness, each ProjGD step makes progress proportional to the
current distance to
\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Specifically, for

{\qquad\sqrt{\frac{r_{*}}{r}}\frac{L-\mu}{2L\mu}<\eta<\frac{1}{L},\qquad}

(4)

there exists an explicit
b>0
independent of
\mathbf{X}
such that for
\mathbf{X}^{+}=\mathcal{P}_{r}(\mathbf{X}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{X}))
,

\|\mathbf{X}^{+}-\mathbf{X}\|_{F}\geq b\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}.

This uniform estimate, established in the proof of
Theorem
6
, is the quantitative link between the
stationary-point certificate and convergence of the iterates.
Combining it with sufficient decrease estimation in Lemma
5
, there is an explicit

\gamma\in(0,1)
such that

f(\mathbf{X}^{(k)})-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\leq(1-\gamma)^{k}\bigl[f(\mathbf{X}^{(0)})-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\bigr].

(5)

The allowable interval in (
4
) is nonempty when

\frac{L}{\mu}<1+2\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}},\quad\text{equivalently}\quad\delta:=\frac{L-\mu}{L+\mu}<\frac{\sqrt{r/r_{*}}}{1+\sqrt{r/r_{*}}}.

(6)

Neither the step-size interval nor the linear rate depends on the
ratio
\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{X}_{*})/\sigma_{r_{*}}(\mathbf{X}_{*})
.

Our proof of (
2
) combines an estimation of the gradient on the
joint row and column spaces of
\mathbf{X}
and the singular values of
\mathbf{X}_{*}
with a sharp matrix
inequality. To pass from stationary points to the iterates of ProjGD, we distinguish two cases: a large tangential gradient directly forces a substantial update, while a small tangential gradient implies a sufficiently large normal component to prevent the rank projection from producing a negligible update. This step is
what turns the certificate into deterministic linear convergence.

1.2
Relationship with earlier guarantees

Ha, Liu, and Barber
[
13
]

established a fundamental connection between low-rank factorization
and projected gradient descent (ProjGD). Specifically, they showed
that every second-order stationary point
(U,V)
of the factorized
objective
f(UV^{\top})
corresponds to a ProjGD fixed point

X=UV^{\top}
for sufficiently small step sizes in Theorem 1.
Using this connection, they derived several optimality guarantees.
In particular, when the unconstrained minimizer
X_{*}
has rank

r_{*}\leq r
, their Theorem 2 guarantees that
X_{*}
is the unique
ProjGD fixed point under
L<2\mu
, for step sizes

1/(2\mu)<\eta\leq 1/L
.
Their results also cover rank overparameterization beyond this
near-isometry regime: Theorem 4 implies that, under restricted
strong convexity and smoothness,
r>(L/\mu)^{2}r_{*}
suffices
to guarantee that every ProjGD fixed point with step size

\eta=1/L
is globally optimal.

Our contribution is different in two respects.
First, we establish a quantitative, rank-dependent lower bound
on the normal component of the gradient at nonoptimal critical
points. This yields a sharper criterion for excluding nonoptimal
ProjGD fixed points, allowing

r>\frac{1}{4}(L/\mu-1)^{2}r_{*}
.
Second, we extend this geometric argument
to the actual ProjGD iterates. By establishing a uniform lower
bound on the size of each projected-gradient update, we prove
deterministic global linear convergence of ProjGD.
Thus, our main result concerns the convergence of the ProjGD algorithm
itself, rather than only the optimality of its fixed points.

The landscape of low-rank recovery under restricted isometry has also been
studied extensively. Earlier work established benign landscapes for
low-rank matrix sensing and related recovery problems

[
3
,
22
,
12
]
, with
extensions to more general low-rank objectives and nonlinear measurements

[
34
,
4
]
. Earlier sharp restricted-isometry
results include
[
29
,
32
]
.
Subsequent work obtained sharp rank-dependent guarantees
for positive-semidefinite problems

[
28
,
30
]
and guarantees for asymmetric, noisy,
and approximately stationary problems
[
31
]
.
Recently, McRae and Zhang
[
18
]
have already
proved the sharp asymmetric rank-overparameterized threshold and
provided matching examples.
Their work also treats nuclear-norm regularization and statistical
recovery, which are outside the scope of our present noiseless analysis.
Our Corollary
10
recovers this result by a
short, independent normal-gradient argument. We also retain an explicit quadratic example illustrating the
sharp obstruction and the corresponding step-size barrier.

Related work addresses singular-value projection and restricted
smoothness
[
14
,
9
,
8
]
.
For general closed, possibly nonconvex feasible sets,
Olikier and Waldspurger
[
20
]
show that
accumulation points of their backtracking projected-gradient method
are Bouligand stationary and, when the gradient is locally Lipschitz
continuous, proximally stationary. In the rank-constrained setting,
Theorem
6
uses restricted curvature to obtain a
uniform step-length bound and global linear convergence to
\mathbf{X}_{*}

for a fixed step size in (
4
).
Other approaches include optimization on fixed-rank manifolds

[
26
,
25
,
16
,
19
]

and factorized methods with preconditioning

[
27
,
24

.
These works provide complementary theoretical analyses and algorithmic approaches to low-rank optimization.

Problem setting and algorithms

2.1
Notation

For a matrix
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
of rank
q
, write its compact singular value decomposition as
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}^{T}
, where
\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times q},\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times q},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times q}
. We order its singular values nonincreasingly and set
\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{X})=0
for
j>q
. The map
\mathcal{P}_{r}(\mathbf{Y})
denotes any best Frobenius-norm approximation of
\mathbf{Y}
of rank at most
r
, obtained by truncating its singular value decomposition; if
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})\leq r
, then
\mathcal{P}_{r}(\mathbf{Y})=\mathbf{Y}
.

For a matrix
\mathbf{U}
with orthonormal columns,
\mathbf{U}^{\perp}
denotes an orthonormal basis of its orthogonal complement. Write
[\mathbf{Z}]_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{V}^{T}
. For a matrix
\mathbf{X}
of actual rank
q>0
,
T(\mathbf{X})=\{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times n_{2}},\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{1}}\}
denotes the tangent space to the set of rank-
q
matrices at
\mathbf{X}
. Its orthogonal projectors are

\displaystyle P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}^{T}\mathbf{Z}+\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}^{T}-\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}^{T}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}^{T},

\displaystyle P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}^{T})\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}^{T}).

These are the standard fixed-rank tangent projectors
[
2
]
. We write
\|\cdot\|
for the matrix spectral norm,
\|\cdot\|_{F}
for the Frobenius norm, and
\|\cdot\|_{*}
for the nuclear norm.

For
f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
, we write
\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
for its Euclidean gradient,
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
for its Riemannian gradient, and
\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{X})[\mathbf{E}]
for the directional derivative of
\nabla f
at
\mathbf{X}
in the direction
\mathbf{E}
. Thus
\langle\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{X})[\mathbf{E}],\mathbf{E}\rangle
is the second directional derivative of
t\mapsto f(\mathbf{X}+t\mathbf{E})
at
t=0
. We refer to
P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
as the
normal component of the Euclidean gradient
, or simply the
normal component of the gradient
when the context is clear.

For any
\mathbf{X}
with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})\leq r
, let
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{X}}=\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{col}(\mathbf{X}),\operatorname{col}(\mathbf{X}_{*}))
and
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{X}}=\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{row}(\mathbf{X}),\operatorname{row}(\mathbf{X}_{*}))
. Choose orthonormal basis matrices
\mathbf{U}_{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_{U}}
and
\mathbf{V}_{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_{V}}
for these subspaces, where
d_{U}=\dim\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{X}}
and
d_{V}=\dim\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{X}}
, and define

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}=\{\mathbf{U}_{+}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{V}_{+}^{T}:\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{U}\times d_{V}}\},\qquad P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}(\mathbf{Z})=\mathbf{U}_{+}\mathbf{U}_{+}^{T}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{V}_{+}\mathbf{V}_{+}^{T}.

The definition of
P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}
is independent of the chosen orthonormal bases. Every element of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}
has rank at most
r+r_{*}
because both joint subspaces have dimension at most
r+r_{*}
. Moreover
P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}
commutes with
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}
: the column and row spaces of
\mathbf{X}
are contained in
\mathcal{U}_{\mathbf{X}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{X}}
.

2.2
Assumptions

The geometric certificate uses a rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted second-derivative condition. For nonlinear objectives this is the natural second-order analogue of RIP; for matrix sensing it is standard low-rank RIP after normalization.

Assumption A1 (Rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted curvature / RIP-type condition).

The function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
is twice continuously differentiable. For every
\mathbf{X}
and every
\mathbf{E}
with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{E})\leq r+r_{*}
, constants
0<\mu\leq L
satisfy

\mu\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{X})[\mathbf{E}],\mathbf{E}\rangle\leq L\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}.

(7)

This implies that
f
has rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted
L
-smoothness and
\mu
-strong convexity. Define the normalized restricted-curvature constant

\delta:=\frac{L-\mu}{L+\mu}\in[0,1).
After scaling the objective by
2/(L+\mu)
, (
7
) becomes the normalized bound
(1-\delta)\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\langle\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{X})[\mathbf{E}],\mathbf{E}\rangle\leq(1+\delta)\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}
. If
f(\mathbf{X})=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*})\|_{2}^{2}
, this is rank-
(r+r_{*})
RIP for
\mathcal{A}
.

Assumption A1
′
(Additional rank-
2r
restricted smoothness for ProjGD).

Assumption A1 holds with constants
0<\mu\leq L
. In addition, for every
\mathbf{X}
and every
\mathbf{E}
with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{E})\leq 2r
, we assume

\langle\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{X})[\mathbf{E}],\mathbf{E}\rangle\leq L\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}.

Thus A1
′
imposes no rank-
2r
restricted strong-convexity condition: the lower bound with constant
\mu
is required only through rank
r+r_{*}
. The additional smoothness range is used to control the difference between consecutive rank-
r
iterates in the sufficient-decrease argument.

Throughout,
\mu
and
L
are the rank-
(r+r_{*})
curvature bounds from A1, with the same
L
also serving as a rank-
2r
smoothness bound;
\delta=(L-\mu)/(L+\mu)
is defined using these constants.

Assumption A2 (Low-rank unconstrained minimizer).

There exists an unconstrained minimizer
\mathbf{X}_{*}\in\operatorname*{arg\;min}_{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}f(\mathbf{X})
of rank
r_{*}
, with
1\leq r_{*}\leq r<n
. Thus
\nabla f(\mathbf{X}_{*})=0
. Under A1,
\mathbf{X}_{*}
is the unique minimizer over
\mathcal{M}_{r}
: the difference between any rank-
r
point and
\mathbf{X}_{*}
has rank at most
r+r_{*}
, so restricted strong convexity applies.

Assumption A3 (Nonempty ProjGD step-size interval).

Set
\rho=\sqrt{r/r_{*}}
. We assume

\delta<\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}.

(8)

Equivalently, this is

\frac{L}{\mu}<1+2\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}},\,\,\text{or}\,\,r>\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{L}{\mu}-1\right)^{2}r_{*}.

Discussion of the assumptions.

A1 is substantially weaker than global strong convexity/smoothness: high-rank directions are unrestricted. In underdetermined matrix sensing,
\mathcal{A}
may therefore have a large ambient nullspace while still satisfying A1. The certificate and its landscape consequence use only rank
r+r_{*}
, consistent with the order appearing in
[
30
,
31
,
18
]
. The ProjGD sufficient-decrease argument additionally requires rank-
2r
restricted smoothness under A1
′
, because consecutive rank-
r
iterates may differ by a matrix of rank as large as
2r
. Rank-
2r
restricted strong convexity is not needed. By contrast, the geometric certificate and landscape analysis use only rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted curvature.

2.3
Stationarity and rank-deficient boundary points

Let the constraint set of (
1
) be

\mathcal{M}_{r}=\{\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})\leq r\}.

For
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
, let
T(\mathbf{X})
be the tangent space defined in Section
2.1
. For a full-rank (
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})=r
) first-order stationary point of the constrained problem (
1
), referred to below as a
rank-
r
critical point
, the first-order necessary condition is

P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0.

We call such a critical point a
strict saddle
if there exists a smooth curve
\mathbf{X}(t)\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
through
\mathbf{X}
for which

\mathbf{X}(0)=\mathbf{X},\,\,\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}f(\mathbf{X}(t))\right|_{t=0}<0.

For a point of rank
q<r
, the fixed-rank tangent-space condition
is not the first-order optimality condition for
\mathcal{M}_{r}
: a rank-one
perturbation is feasible, so a local minimizer at such a point must
satisfy
\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
. Under A1–A2 the latter is possible
only at
\mathbf{X}_{*}
. At rank-deficient points, we show that any nonoptimal feasible point is neither a ProjGD fixed point nor a local minimizer.

2.4
Algorithms

Projected gradient descent (ProjGD) algorithm
The projected gradient descent algorithm treats (
1
) as a constrained optimization problem. It iteratively performs a gradient step and then projects the result to satisfy the constraints:

\mathbf{X}^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{P}_{r}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{X}^{(t)})\big),

(9)

where
\mathcal{P}_{r}
is the best rank-
r
approximation defined in Section
2.1
.

Factored gradient descent (FGD) algorithm

FGD
[
23
,
10
,
33
,
6
,
30
,
22
,
5
]
leverages a nonconvex approach introduced by
[
7
]
, where the original
n\times n
candidate matrix
\mathbf{X}
is factored into two smaller matrices
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T}
, with
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
. The FGD algorithm directly optimizes over these factor matrices

(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})
using gradient descent. Denoting the gradient with respect to

\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V},\mathbf{X}
by
\nabla_{\mathbf{U}},\nabla_{\mathbf{V}},\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}
, the FGD iteration is:

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}^{(t)}-\eta\nabla_{\mathbf{U}}f|_{\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}^{(t)}}=\mathbf{U}^{(t)}-\eta\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}f(\mathbf{X}^{(t)})\mathbf{V}^{(t)},

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{V}^{(t)}-\eta\nabla_{\mathbf{V}}f|_{\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}^{(t)}}=\mathbf{V}^{(t)}-\eta\big[\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}f(\mathbf{X}^{(t)})\big]^{T}\mathbf{U}^{(t)},

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}^{(t+1)}(\mathbf{V}^{(t+1)})^{T}.

In Section
6
we analyze gradient descent based on this factorization.

3
The geometric certificate for rank projection

We establish the quantitative normal-gradient certificate and explain how it excludes nonoptimal fixed points of the ProjGD algorithm. Section
4
upgrades this static obstruction to a uniform one-step progress bound and deterministic linear convergence.

3.1
The key inequality

The goal of this subsection is to convert the rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted-curvature assumption into a quantitative lower bound on the normal component of the Euclidean gradient. A difficulty is that A1 controls the second derivative only along low-rank directions. We therefore restrict the second-derivative operator to the joint row and column spaces generated by
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Every matrix in the resulting linear subspace
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}
has rank at most
r+r_{*}
, so A1 controls this compressed operator on the entire subspace. Lemma
1
gives the corresponding centered-gradient estimate.

Lemma 1

(Compressed centered-gradient estimate)
.

Assume A1–A2 and let
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})\leq r
. Put
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}
and let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}
be the joint row/column subspace defined in Section
2.1
. Then

\left\|P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})-\frac{L+\mu}{2}\mathbf{E}\right\|_{F}\leq\frac{L-\mu}{2}\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}.

(11)

Equivalently, with
c=(L+\mu)/2
and
\kappa_{0}=(L-\mu)/(L+\mu)
,

\left\|-\frac{1}{c}P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})+\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\right\|_{F}\leq\kappa_{0}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}.

The compression
P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}
in Lemma
1
is essential due to the restricted rank assumption. Although A1 need not control the second derivative in arbitrary high-rank directions, its restriction to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}
has spectrum in
[\mu,L]
because every direction in that linear subspace has rank at most
r+r_{*}
. Thus Lemma
1
is the restricted-curvature analogue of the usual centered-gradient estimate under global smoothness and strong convexity.

Lemma
2
is a purely algebraic inequality for a general matrix
\mathbf{Z}
, which plays the role of the gradient at a rank-
r
critical point of
f
. The factor
\sqrt{r/r_{*}}
makes the certificate stronger under overparameterization.

Lemma 2

(A matrix inequality)
.

Let
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})=r
,
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X}_{*})\leq r_{*}
,
1\leq r_{*}\leq r
, and
\mathbf{X}\neq\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Suppose
0<\kappa_{0}<1
and

P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\mathbf{Z}=0,\qquad\|\mathbf{Z}+\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}\leq\kappa_{0}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}.

(12)

Then

\|\mathbf{Z}\|\geq\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\frac{1-\kappa_{0}^{2}}{\kappa_{0}}\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X}).

Lemma
2
says that if a perturbation
\mathbf{Z}
makes a rank-
r
matrix
\mathbf{X}
substantially closer to a rank-
r_{*}
target
\mathbf{X}_{*}
and is orthogonal to
T(\mathbf{X})
, then
\mathbf{Z}
must contain a singular value large compared with
\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})
. The factor
\sqrt{r/r_{*}}
is the source of the improvement obtained from rank overparameterization.

The two preceding lemmas now combine to prove properties of a rank-
r
critical point. Lemma
1
says that the gradient
\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
(when restricted to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}
) points approximately in the error direction
\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}
, while Lemma
2
says that any
\mathbf{Z}
with this approximation property cannot be spectrally small. Their combination gives Proposition
3
, the quantitative core of the proof of this Section. It depends on restricted curvature parameters
L,\mu
and the rank ratio
r/r_{*}
; we first use it to rule out spurious ProjGD fixed points, then to control finite steps in Section
4
.

Proposition 3

(Normal-component lower bound at nonoptimal critical points)
.

Under Assumptions A1–A2, suppose
0<\mu<L
and let
\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
be a nonoptimal rank-
r
critical point, so
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
and
\mathbf{X}\neq\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Then

\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|\geq\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\,\frac{2L\mu}{L-\mu}\,\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X}).

(13)

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{G}=\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}=P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}\mathbf{G}
,
c=(L+\mu)/2
, and
\kappa_{0}=(L-\mu)/(L+\mu)
. Since
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\mathbf{G}=0
and
P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}
commutes with
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}
, we also have
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}=0
. Set
\mathbf{Z}=-\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}/c
. Lemma
1
gives

P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\mathbf{Z}=0,\qquad\|\mathbf{Z}+\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}\leq\kappa_{0}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}.

Applying Lemma
2
and using

\frac{1-\kappa_{0}^{2}}{\kappa_{0}}=\frac{4L\mu}{(L+\mu)(L-\mu)}

yields

\|P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|\geq\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\,\frac{2L\mu}{L-\mu}\,\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X}).

(14)

Because orthogonal compression
P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}
cannot increase spectral norm and
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\mathbf{G}=0
,

\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\mathbf{G}\|=\|\mathbf{G}\|\geq\|\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}\|,

which gives (
13
).
∎

Under Assumption A3, we have

\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\frac{2L\mu}{L-\mu}>L.

Under Assumption A3, the coefficient in (
13
)
exceeds
L
. As shown in the next subsection, this bound
excludes nonoptimal ProjGD fixed points for sufficiently
large step sizes and ensures a nonempty step-size interval
for the global convergence result in Section
4
.
The same bound is also used in Section
5
to rule out
nonoptimal local minima.

3.2
Fixed-point interpretation and the Ha–Liu–Barber connection

For
\eta>0
,
\mathbf{X}
a
ProjGD fixed point
if

\mathbf{X}\in\operatorname*{arg\;min}_{\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})\leq r}\|\mathbf{Y}-[\mathbf{X}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{X})]\|_{F}.

(15)

This definition allows ties in the truncated SVD.
The following characterization of ProjGD fixed points is due to
Ha, Liu, and Barber
[
13
]
.
Under restricted strong convexity and smoothness with
constants
\mu
and
L
, their Lemma 5 and Theorem 2
exclude nonoptimal fixed points when
L<2\mu
.
Our Proposition

provides a stronger,
rank-dependent lower bound on the normal component of
the gradient at nonoptimal critical points.
Combining this bound with the existing fixed-point
characterization, we obtain the less restrictive
sufficient condition

\frac{L}{\mu}<1+2\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}.

In particular, this allows
L<3\mu
when
r=r_{*}
,
with further improvements as the search rank

increases relative to
r_{*}
.

Proposition 4

(Fixed-point criterion and exclusion)
.

Assume A1–A2 and fix
\eta>0
.

1.

If
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})=r
, then
\mathbf{X}
is a ProjGD fixed point
if and only if

P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0,\qquad\eta\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|\leq\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X}).

(16)

If
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})<r
, then
\mathbf{X}
is a ProjGD fixed point if and
only if
\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
.

2.

If
L>\mu
, then, whenever

\eta>\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}:=\sqrt{\frac{r_{*}}{r}}\frac{L-\mu}{2L\mu},

(17)

\mathbf{X}_{*}
is the
only
ProjGD fixed point over

\mathcal{M}_{r}
. If
L=\mu
, the same conclusion holds for every

\eta>0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{G}=\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
.
If
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})=r
, the truncated-SVD characterization of best
rank-
r
approximations shows that
\mathbf{X}
is a best approximation of

\mathbf{X}-\eta\mathbf{G}
when
-\eta\mathbf{G}
lies in

T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}
and its largest singular value does not exceed

\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})
. This is (
16
).
If
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})<r
and
\mathbf{X}
is a best rank-
r
approximation of

\mathbf{X}-\eta\mathbf{G}
, then that latter matrix must equal
\mathbf{X}
:
a best approximation of rank less than
r
leaves no nonzero
singular value unretained. Thus
\mathbf{G}=0
; the converse is immediate.

For any rank-
r

\mathbf{X}\neq\mathbf{X}_{*}
, let
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Restricted
strong convexity along
\mathbf{E}
, whose rank is at most
r+r_{*}
, gives

\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{E}\rangle=\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{X})-\nabla f(\mathbf{X}_{*}),\mathbf{E}\rangle\geq\mu\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}>0.

(18)

Therefore a nonoptimal rank-deficient point is never fixed.
At rank
r
with
L>\mu
, if the first condition of
(
16
) holds, Proposition
3

implies

\eta\|\mathbf{G}\|\geq\eta(2L\mu/(L-\mu))\sqrt{r/r_{*}}\,\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})>\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})
whenever

\eta>\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}
. This contradicts the second condition.

If
L=\mu
, Lemma
1
gives

P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=L(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*})
.
A hypothetical nonoptimal rank-
r
stationary point would then satisfy

P_{T(\mathbf{X})}(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*})=0
. In an SVD coordinate system for
\mathbf{X}
,
this equality says
\mathbf{X}_{*}=\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{W}
with

\mathbf{W}\in T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}
. The two terms occupy orthogonal row and
column blocks, so

\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X}_{*})=r+\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{W})\geq r
.
Since
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X}_{*})=r_{*}\leq r
, necessarily

\mathbf{W}=0
and
\mathbf{X}_{*}=\mathbf{X}
, a contradiction.
Finally,
\nabla f(\mathbf{X}_{*})=0
, so
\mathbf{X}_{*}
is always fixed.
∎

Ha, Liu, and Barber
[
13

also obtain a guarantee for
overparameterized ranks. In our setting, their result

Proposition

is a statement about fixed point of ProjGD:

4
Deterministic global linear convergence of ProjGD

We extend the geometric certificate developed in Section
3
to establish a uniform lower bound on the size of each ProjGD update relative to the distance to
X_{*}
. Combined with a sufficient-decrease estimate, this yields deterministic global linear convergence.

4.1
Global convergence of projected gradient descent

The additional rank-
2r
restricted smoothness in
Assumption A1
′
is needed because the difference between
two consecutive ProjGD iterates can have rank as large
as
2r
. We first establish a sufficient-decrease
inequality and lower bounds on the size of each ProjGD
update. We then use the geometric certificate from
Section
3
to show that the update cannot be arbitrarily
small relative to the distance from
\mathbf{X}_{*}
.

Lemma 5

(Iterative decrease in functional value)
.

Assume A1
′
. Let
\mathbf{X}^{+}=\mathcal{P}_{r}(\mathbf{X}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{X}))
denote the result of one iteration of ProjGD applied to
\mathbf{X}
. If
\eta<1/L
, then

f(\mathbf{X})-f(\mathbf{X}^{+})\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\eta}-L\Big)\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}^{+}\|_{F}^{2}.

(19)

The term
\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}^{+}\|_{F}
in the RHS of (
19
) is bounded below by

\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}^{+}\|_{F}\geq\max\Big(\frac{\eta\|\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|-\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})}{2},\frac{\eta\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|_{F}}{\sqrt{10}}\Big)

(20)

Theorem 6

(Deterministic global linear convergence of ProjGD)
.

Under A1
′
–A3, consider the ProjGD recursion (
9
), allowing any choice of a best rank-
r
approximation at each step. For every initialization
\mathbf{X}^{(0)}
with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X}^{(0)})\leq r
, the iterates converge linearly to
\mathbf{X}_{*}
whenever the step size
\eta
satisfies

\sqrt{\frac{r_{*}}{r}}\frac{L-\mu}{2L\mu}<\eta<\frac{1}{L}.

(21)

Set
\kappa_{0}=(L-\mu)/(L+\mu)
and
\eta_{0}=\eta(L+\mu)/2
. Choose
\epsilon>0
so that
\hat{\kappa}_{0}=\kappa_{0}+\epsilon<1
and
\sqrt{r_{*}/r}\,\hat{\kappa}_{0}/(1-\hat{\kappa}_{0}^{2})<\eta_{0}
. Such an
\epsilon
exists by (

f(\mathbf{X}^{(k+1)})-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\leq\left[1-\left(\frac{1}{\eta L}-1\right)b^{2}\right]\bigl(f(\mathbf{X}^{(k)})-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\bigr),

(22)

where

b=\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon\eta_{0}}{\sqrt{10}},\,\frac{1-\hat{\kappa}_{0}}{2\sqrt{r}}\left(\eta_{0}-\sqrt{\frac{r_{*}}{r}}\frac{\hat{\kappa}_{0}}{1-\hat{\kappa}_{0}^{2}}\right)\right\}>0.

Proof.

The complete proof is given in Appendix
B
.
At an exact nonoptimal rank-
r
critical point, the certificate gives

\eta\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|>\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})
, which excludes a
fixed point but does not quantify the update at a nonstationary iterate.
The key step is to extend the argument to the uniform estimate

\|\mathbf{X}^{+}-\mathbf{X}\|_{F}\geq b\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F},\qquad\mathbf{X}^{+}=\mathcal{P}_{r}(\mathbf{X}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{X})).

(23)

Here
b>0
is the constant in the theorem, valid for every feasible

\mathbf{X}
and every choice of a best rank-
r
approximation. The estimate
is immediate at
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Otherwise, write

\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}
,
\mathbf{G}=\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
, and
c=(L+\mu)/2
.

If
\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\mathbf{G}\|_{F}/c\geq\epsilon\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}
, the tangent estimate
in Lemma
5
gives

\|\mathbf{X}^{+}-\mathbf{X}\|_{F}\geq(\epsilon\eta_{0}/\sqrt{10})\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}
.
Otherwise, set

\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}=-P_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}}\mathbf{G}/c
and

\mathbf{N}=P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}
. Lemma
1

and the small tangential component imply

\|\mathbf{N}+\mathbf{E}\|_{F}\leq(\kappa_{0}+\epsilon)\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}=\hat{\kappa}_{0}\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}.

At rank
r

certificate applies with
\kappa_{0}
replaced by
\hat{\kappa}_{0}

Lemma

bounds
\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})
in terms of

\|\mathbf{N}\|

\|\mathbf{N}\|\geq(1-\hat{\kappa}_{0})\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}/\sqrt{r}
without a rank

\mathbf{N}

\eta\|\mathbf{G}\|-\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})\geq\frac{1-\hat{\kappa}_{0}}{\sqrt{r}}\left(\eta_{0}-\sqrt{\frac{r_{*}}{r}}\frac{\hat{\kappa}_{0}}{1-\hat{\kappa}_{0}^{2}}\right)\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}.

The same bound holds when
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})<r
, since then

\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})=0
. The spectral estimate in
Lemma
5
gives the second branch in the definition
of
b

23
) in both cases.
Finally, combining (
23

and
f(\mathbf{X})-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\leq L\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}/2
gives
(

∎

Role of the step size.

The step size
\eta
plays two distinct roles in the
analysis of ProjGD:

•

Exclusion of nonoptimal fixed points.

For
\eta>\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}
, where

\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}=\sqrt{\frac{r_{*}}{r}}\frac{L-\mu}{2L\mu},

Proposition
4
guarantees that

\mathbf{X}_{*}
is the only ProjGD fixed point.
Thus, every nonoptimal feasible point must change
under a ProjGD update, although objective decrease
is not guaranteed by this condition alone.

•

Sufficient decrease.

For
0<\eta<1/L

f(\mathbf{X})-f(\mathbf{X}^{+})\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-L\right)\|\mathbf{X}^{+}-\mathbf{X}\|_{F}^{2}.

Hence, every nonzero ProjGD update strictly

only nonincrease is guaranteed.

•

Global linear convergence.

When
\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}<\eta<1/L
,
both conditions hold. Together with the uniform
step-length bound established in Theorem 6,
they yield deterministic global linear convergence
from every feasible initialization.

Theorem

convergence of ProjGD under rank-

restricted curvature
and rank-

restricted smoothness.
This goes beyond a landscape guarantee: even the absence

The key additional ingredient is a uniform lower bound
on the size of each ProjGD update relative to the

\mathbf{X}_{*}

Previous analyses of singular-value projection have

28

13

Related work by Zhang

5
Landscape consequences and sharpness

McRae and Zhang
[
18
]
established the
sharp rank-dependent threshold for the absence of spurious
local minima in asymmetric low-rank factorization, covering
matrix sensing and more general convex losses.
Here we recover the same threshold under Assumptions A1–A3,
which do not require global convexity.
We retain this result because it follows directly from our
normal-gradient certificate and provides a short alternative
proof that works directly with the asymmetric matrix,
without lifting it to a positive semidefinite formulation.

5.1
An alternative proof of the sharp threshold

We next convert the normal-component certificate in Proposition
3

into a statement about second-order behavior under the rank constraint. At a rank-
r
critical point, the first-order condition

P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0

implies that the Euclidean gradient lies entirely in
T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}
. The following lemma shows that if this normal component is sufficiently large relative to the smallest singular value of
\mathbf{X}
, then there is a rank-preserving curve through
\mathbf{X}
along which
f
has negative second derivative. Consequently,
\mathbf{X}
cannot be a local minimizer in
\mathcal{M}_{r}
.

Lemma 9

(Necessary condition for a local minimizer)
.

Assume A1. If
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})=r
,
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
, and

\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|>L\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X}),

then
\mathbf{X}
is not a local minimizer of
f
over matrices of rank
r
. More precisely, there exists a smooth curve
\mathbf{X}(t)\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
with
\mathbf{X}(0)=\mathbf{X}
and
\|\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)\|_{F}=1
such that

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}f(\mathbf{X}(t))\right|_{t=0}\leq L-\frac{\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|}{\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})}<0.

The proof, given in Appendix
E
, constructs this curve explicitly. The same construction is used in the theorem below.

Proposition
3
and Lemma
9
yield the following corollary, which recovers the known sharp threshold
[
18
]
. The direct matrix-coordinate argument supplies an alternative proof and a quantitative negative-curvature bound. Rank-deficient boundary points are treated separately.

Corollary 10

(Sharp asymmetric landscape threshold; alternative proof)
.

Under Assumptions A1–A3,
\mathbf{X}_{*}
is the unique local minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{M}_{r}
. If
L>\mu
, each nonoptimal rank-
r
critical point
\mathbf{X}
is a strict saddle: along a smooth rank-preserving curve
\mathbf{X}(t)\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
with
\mathbf{X}(0)=\mathbf{X}
and
\|\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)\|_{F}=1
satisfying

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}f(\mathbf{X}(t))\right|_{t=0}\leq L-\frac{\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|}{\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})}\leq L-\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\,\frac{2L\mu}{L-\mu}<0.

When
L=\mu
, there is no nonoptimal rank-
r
critical point.

Proof.

First suppose
0<\mu<L
and let
\mathbf{X}\neq\mathbf{X}_{*}
be a rank-
r
critical point. Proposition
3
gives

\frac{\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|}{\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})}\geq\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\frac{2L\mu}{L-\mu}>L.

The proof of Lemma
9
constructs a smooth rank-preserving curve
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(t)
with
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(0)=\mathbf{X}
,
\|\dot{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}}(0)\|_{F}^{2}=2
, and

f(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(t))-f(\mathbf{X})\leq\left(L-\frac{\|P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|}{\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})}\right)t^{2}+O(t^{4}).

Reparameterizing by
\mathbf{X}(t)=\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(t/\sqrt{2})
gives
\|\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)\|_{F}=1
and the displayed second-derivative bound. Thus
\mathbf{X}
is a strict saddle along a rank-preserving curve.
If
L=\mu
, the argument in the proof of
Proposition
4
rules out nonoptimal
rank-
r
critical points altogether.
Finally, let
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})=q<r
and
\mathbf{X}\neq\mathbf{X}_{*}
. By
(
18
),
\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\neq 0
.
Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
be top singular vectors of
\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
, with

\mathbf{u}^{T}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\mathbf{v}=\|\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\|>0
.
The curve
\mathbf{X}(t)=\mathbf{X}-t\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^{T}
has rank at most

q+1\leq r
and strictly decreases
f
for small
t>0
.
Thus no nonoptimal rank-deficient feasible point is a local minimizer,
and the corollary follows.

∎

Interpretation.

Corollary
10
reduces the exclusion of nonoptimal local minima to a comparison between the upper second-derivative scale
L
and the normal-component scale

\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\frac{2L\mu}{L-\mu}.

Increasing the search rank amplifies the latter by
\sqrt{r/r_{*}}
. The threshold occurs when this term exceeds
L
, equivalently
L/\mu<1+2\sqrt{r/r_{*}}
. For
r=r_{*}
this is
L/\mu<3
. The admissible restricted condition number increases with
r/r_{*}
, keeping in mind that the restricted constants can also vary with the rank at which they are measured.

For matrix sensing, the restricted-curvature constants become the RIP constants. The following specialization translates the alternative proof into the standard normalization. The sharp threshold itself and matching asymmetric examples are established by
[
18
]
.

Matrix-sensing specialization.

For the matrix-sensing objective

f(\mathbf{X})=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*})\|_{2}^{2}
,
the rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted isometry property
with constant
\delta
gives
\mu=1-\delta
and

L=1+\delta
. Consequently, Corollary
10

implies that
\mathbf{X}_{*}
is the unique local minimizer
whenever

\delta<\frac{\sqrt{r/r_{*}}}{1+\sqrt{r/r_{*}}}.

This recovers the known sharp asymmetric RIP threshold

[
18
]
through our
normal-gradient certificate.

Comparison with the asymmetric lifting result.

For asymmetric low-rank factorization, the earlier
result of Zhang
[
31
, Theorem 1.6]

implies, in the noiseless setting with exact
second-order stationarity, the sufficient RIP condition

\delta<\frac{\sqrt{r/r_{*}}}{2(1+\sqrt{r/r_{*}})}.
The factor of two arises from lifting the asymmetric
problem to a symmetric positive semidefinite (PSD)
formulation, which effectively doubles the RIP constant.
McRae and Zhang
[
18
]

subsequently closed this gap, establishing the sharp
threshold

\delta<\frac{\sqrt{r/r_{*}}}{1+\sqrt{r/r_{*}}},

and providing matching counterexamples for the
asymmetric setting.

The same sharp rank-dependent threshold was previously
established for PSD factorization in

[
30
]
.
Our Corollary
10
recovers this threshold
directly for asymmetric rank-constrained optimization,
without PSD lifting, and additionally provides an
explicit negative-curvature bound at every nonoptimal
rank-
r
critical point.
More importantly, the same geometric certificate is
used in Section
4
to establish
deterministic global linear convergence of ProjGD.

For nonlinear objectives, A1 is a restricted second-derivative condition rather than a quadratic measurement model. The proof uses only rank-
(r+r_{*})
curvature because the compressed second-derivative operator acts on a linear subspace all of whose elements have rank at most
r+r_{*}
. Related restricted-curvature landscape results include
[

,
28

; our emphasis is the certificate and its use in the algorithmic result, with the landscape statement as a consequence.

5.2
Sharpness of the geometric certificate and step-size threshold

The following quadratic example shows that both the normal-gradient bound and the lower step-size threshold for ProjGD are attained. It parallels known sharp asymmetric counterexamples
[
30
,
31
,
18
]
; we do not claim a new landscape threshold.

Partition the coordinates into blocks of sizes
r_{*}
,
r
, and
n-r-r_{*}
(the third block may be absent), and set

\mathbf{X}_{*}=\mbox{diag}(\mathbf{I}_{r_{*}},0,0),\qquad\tau(\mathbf{X})=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{X}_{11}-\mathbf{I}_{r_{*}})}{\sqrt{r_{*}}}+\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{X}_{22})}{\sqrt{r}}.

For
\kappa>1
, consider

f(\mathbf{X})=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\kappa-1}{4}\left[\tau(\mathbf{X})^{2}+\|\mathbf{X}_{12}+\mathbf{X}_{21}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}\right].

(25)

Proposition 11

(Sharpness of the certificate and step-size threshold)
.

Let
1\leq r_{*}\leq r
and
n\geq r+r_{*}
. The function in (

\mu=1
and

. Define

Then
P_{T(\bar{\mathbf{X}})}\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{X}})=0
and

\frac{\|P_{T(\bar{\mathbf{X}})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{X}})\|}{\sigma_{r}(\bar{\mathbf{X}})}=\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\frac{2\kappa}{\kappa-1}.

Moreover,

is a ProjGD fixed point for every

It is a strict local minimizer when
r<\frac{1}{4}(\kappa-1)^{2}r_{*}

a degenerate nonoptimal critical point at equality, and a strict saddle
when

The proof is given in Appendix

6
Connection with Burer–Monteiro factorization

6.1
Second-order points and ProjGD fixed points

Consider the factorized objective

h(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})=f(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T})
, where

\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
.
Ha, Liu, and Barber
[
13
, Theorem 1]

showed that if
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})
is a second-order stationary
point of

, then
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T}
is a ProjGD fixed point
for any step size
0<\eta\leq 1/\beta_{\mathrm{local}}(\mathbf{X})
,
where
\beta_{\mathrm{local}}(\mathbf{X})
denotes the local
smoothness constant over matrices of rank at most
r
.
This result applies to arbitrary search ranks
r
,
including the overparameterized case
r>r_{*}
.

Under Assumption A1
′
, we have

\beta_{\mathrm{local}}(\mathbf{X})\leq L
, so every
second-order stationary factor pair yields a ProjGD
fixed point for any
0<\eta\leq 1/L
.
Assumption A3 ensures that the interval

(\eta_{\mathrm{crit}},1/L)
is nonempty, and
Proposition
4
shows that

\mathbf{X}_{*}
is the unique ProjGD fixed point for

\eta>\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}
.
Combining these results gives the following corollary.

Corollary 12

(Factored second-order points via ProjGD)
.

Under A1
′
–A3, every second-order stationary point
(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})

of
h(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V})=f(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T})
satisfies

\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T}=\mathbf{X}_{*}
, including when
r>r_{*}
.

Proof.

Restricted
L
-smoothness through rank
2r
bounds the local
rank-constrained smoothness constant of

[
13
]
by
L
. Choose any
\eta
in the
nonempty interval (
21
). Their stationary-point
result implies that
\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T}
is a ProjGD fixed point at this

.
Proposition
4
forces
\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T}=\mathbf{X}_{*}
. This
argument is another way to recover a previously known type of
landscape conclusion; unlike Theorem
6
, it makes no
assertion about a deterministic global rate of factor gradient descent.
∎

6.2
Balanced factored gradient descent

As a complementary result, consider a balancing penalty on the asymmetric factors:

g(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R})=f(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T})+\frac{\lambda}{4}\big\|\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}\big\|_{F}^{2},\qquad\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}.

(26)

Related factored/variable-rank geometry appears in
[
13
,
17
]
. The following secondary theorem records a standard strict-saddle and almost-sure convergence implication for gradient descent applied to (
26
), with the required dynamical assumptions stated explicitly.

Theorem 13

(Strict saddles and almost-sure convergence for a balanced factorization)
.

Assume A1–A3 and let
\lambda>0
.

(a)

Every critical point
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R})
of
g
is balanced. It is a global minimizer if and only if
\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T}=\mathbf{X}_{*}
; every other critical point is a strict saddle.

(b)

Suppose in addition that
f
is real analytic. Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
be bounded, fix
\bar{c}>\sup_{\mathcal{S}}g
, and let
L_{g}
be a Lipschitz constant of
\nabla g
on a compact neighborhood of
\{g\leq\bar{c}\}
containing all points
\mathbf{z}-t\nabla g(\mathbf{z})
with
g(\mathbf{z})\leq\bar{c}
and
t\in[0,1]
. If
0<\eta<\min(1,1/L_{g})
and
(\mathbf{L}^{(0)},\mathbf{R}^{(0)})
is drawn from a distribution supported on
\mathcal{S}
and absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, then
\mathbf{L}^{(k)}(\mathbf{R}^{(k)})^{T}\to\mathbf{X}_{*}
almost surely.

Proof.

A complete proof is given in Appendix
D
. We record the two geometric cases that drive it. At a critical point, the first-order equations imply
\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}
and

\nabla f(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T})\mathbf{R}=0
,
\mathbf{L}^{T}\nabla f(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T})=0
. If
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T})=r
, the product satisfies
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
, and Corollary
10
supplies a rank-preserving direction with negative second derivative that lifts to the balanced factors. If the product is rank deficient, choose a common kernel vector
\mathbf{w}
and top singular vectors
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
of
-\nabla f(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T})
. Then

(\mathbf{L}+t\mathbf{a}\mathbf{w}^{T})(\mathbf{R}+t\mathbf{b}\mathbf{w}^{T})^{T}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T}+t^{2}\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}^{T},

while the balancing term remains unchanged to second order. Along the displayed factor-space curve,

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}g(\mathbf{z}(t))\right|_{t=0}=-2\sigma_{1}(\nabla f)<0.

Part (b) then follows from standard descent, measure-zero saddle avoidance, and analytic convergence; Appendix
D
supplies the compactness and dynamical details.
∎

The balanced-factor statement is secondary: unlike Theorem
6
for ProjGD, it does not yield a deterministic every-initialization convergence rate, and its almost-sure conclusion uses additional dynamical regularity.

7
Discussion

What the certificate adds to the fixed-point correspondence.

Ha, Liu, and Barber
[
13
]
show that
every second-order stationary point
(U,V)
of the factored
objective
f(UV^{\top})
yields a ProjGD fixed point

\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
for
0<\eta\leq 1/\beta_{\mathrm{local}}(\mathbf{X})
.
This implication alone does not quantify the progress of ProjGD
at a nonstationary iterate.
Our certificate (
13
) excludes nonoptimal rank-
r

critical points as ProjGD fixed points when

\eta>\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}
.
The proof of Theorem
6
extends this argument from
exact stationary points to arbitrary iterates.
A large tangential gradient relative to
\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}

directly gives a lower bound on iteratively progress:
Under the assumptions of Theorem
6
, these estimates
give

\|\mathbf{X}^{+}-\mathbf{X}\|_{F}\geq b\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F},\qquad\mathbf{X}^{+}=P_{r}(\mathbf{X}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{X})).

Thus the update cannot become arbitrarily small relative to
the current error, even as the iterates approach a rank-deficient
matrix.
Combining this estimate with

f(\mathbf{X})-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\leq(L/2)\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}^{2}
gives the uniform objective
contraction in Theorem
6
.

Rank overparameterization and the step-size window.

The lower endpoint

\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}=\sqrt{r_{*}/r}(L-\mu)/(2L\mu)
arises from
normal singular-value competition under hard projection, while

\eta<1/L
ensures descent under rank-
2r
restricted smoothness.
For
r=r_{*}
, the condition becomes
L/\mu<3
; for
r>r_{*}
,
the permissible restricted condition number grows as

1+2\sqrt{r/r_{*}}
. Thus rank overparameterization improves the
worst-case geometric certificate. The quadratic construction of
Proposition
11
shows why the lower step-size condition
is not simply a proof artifact. It does
not
imply that taking
an arbitrarily large
r
improves practical performance, because the
restricted curvature constants themselves depend on the tested rank.

Matrix geometry versus factor geometry.

The objective
f(\mathbf{X})=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}^{2}
has
identity ambient Hessian, but the induced factor objective

f(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T})
has curvature that may depend on the nonzero singular
values of
\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Plain factor-gradient methods can therefore
exhibit poor conditioning or sublinear behavior under rank
overspecification, whereas suitable preconditioned methods can
mitigate this effect

[
24
,
27
]
.
ProjGD operates in matrix coordinates: the rate in
Theorem
6
depends on
L,\mu,r,r_{*},\eta
but not
on
\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{X}_{*})/\sigma_{r_{*}}(\mathbf{X}_{*})
, unlike factorized methods.

Scope and limitations.

The deterministic global result assumes exact best rank-
r
projections,
a low-rank unconstrained minimizer, rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted curvature,
and rank-
2r
restricted smoothness. The rank-
(r+r_{*})
curvature
condition suffices for the certificate and landscape corollary, while
the stronger smoothness range is used for sufficient decrease in ProjGD.
In particular, standard matrix completion need not satisfy the
uniform rank-
2r
condition without further structure.
Noise, approximate projections, nonzero
\nabla f(\mathbf{X}_{*})
,
and approximate second-order stationarity are outside the present
theorem; some of these aspects are addressed in

[
31
,
18
]
by different methods.
Possible extensions include adaptive step sizes, inexact truncated
SVDs, and safeguarded switches between a low-cost Riemannian update
and a ProjGD update. Any such hybrid strategy requires its own
analysis at rank-deficient boundaries

[
16
,
19
]
.

8
Conclusions

We establish a sharp, rank-dependent lower bound on
P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
, the
normal component of the gradient, relative to the smallest
singular value at any nonoptimal rank-
r
critical point
\mathbf{X}
.
Under rank-
(r+r_{*})
restricted curvature, this certificate
excludes nonoptimal ProjGD fixed points whenever the step
size exceeds an explicit threshold.
More importantly, we extend the geometric argument to
establish a uniform lower
bound on the size of each ProjGD update relative to the
distance from
\mathbf{X}_{*}
.
Combined with sufficient decrease under the additional
rank-
2r
restricted smoothness assumption, this yields
deterministic global linear convergence of
ProjGD whenever

\sqrt{\frac{r_{*}}{r}}\frac{L-\mu}{2L\mu}<\eta<\frac{1}{L}.

This interval is nonempty when

L/\mu<1+2\sqrt{r/r_{*}}
, and the convergence rate
does not depend on the singular-value condition
number of
\mathbf{X}_{*}
, even when
r>r_{*}
.

Our analysis has a similar flavor on the fixed-point characterization
of Ha, Liu, and Barber
[
13
]
,
but goes beyond fixed-point analysis by establishing
a quantitative progress bound for the actual ProjGD
iterates. As a further consequence, the geometric
certificate recovers the known sharp asymmetric
landscape threshold
[
18
]

through a direct argument that also provides an
explicit negative-curvature bound at nonoptimal
critical points.

\mathbf{E}
also gives

f(\mathbf{X})-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\geq\mu\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}/2
, so objective convergence implies
\mathbf{X}^{(k)}\to\mathbf{X}_{*}
.
∎

B.1
Proof of Lemma
1

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}
and abbreviate
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{X}}
. Because
\mathbf{E}\in\mathcal{S}
and every element of
\mathcal{S}
has rank at most
r+r_{*}
, Assumption A1 implies that, for every
t\in[0,1]
, the compressed self-adjoint operator

\mathcal{H}_{t}=P_{\mathcal{S}}\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{X}_{*}+t\mathbf{E})\big|_{\mathcal{S}}

satisfies
\mu\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{S}}\preceq\mathcal{H}_{t}\preceq L\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{S}}
. Hence the averaged compressed second-derivative operator
\overline{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{H}_{t}\,dt
satisfies

\left\|\overline{\mathcal{H}}-\frac{L+\mu}{2}\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{F\to F}\leq\frac{L-\mu}{2}.

The fundamental theorem of calculus, followed by orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{S}
, gives

P_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl(\nabla f(\mathbf{X})-\nabla f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\bigr)=\overline{\mathcal{H}}[\mathbf{E}].

Since
\nabla f(\mathbf{X}_{*})=0
, applying the operator bound to
\mathbf{E}
proves (
11
).
∎

B.2
Proof of Lemma
2

Proof.

Put
a=1-\kappa_{0}^{2}>0
,
s=\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})
, and
h=\|\mathbf{Z}\|
. Completing the square in (
12
) gives

a\left\|\mathbf{X}+\frac{\mathbf{Z}}{a}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{\kappa_{0}^{2}}{a}\|\mathbf{Z}\|_{F}^{2}.

(33)

The normality condition puts
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Z}
in orthogonal row and column blocks. Thus the singular values of
\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{Z}/a
are the combined singular values of these two blocks. By Eckart–Young
[
11
]
, its best rank-
r_{*}
error is no larger than the error to
\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Choose such a truncation, breaking ties arbitrarily. Suppose it retains
j
singular values from the
\mathbf{Z}/a
block and
r_{*}-j
from the
\mathbf{X}
block. Let
S_{J}
be the sum of squares of the retained singular values of
\mathbf{Z}
, and
S_{J^{c}}
the sum of squares of the remaining ones. Since at least
r-r_{*}+j
singular values of
\mathbf{X}
are discarded, (
33
) implies

a^{2}(r-r_{*}+j)s^{2}+S_{J^{c}}\leq\kappa_{0}^{2}(S_{J}+S_{J^{c}}),\quad\text{hence}\quad a^{2}(r-r_{*}+j)s^{2}+aS_{J^{c}}\leq\kappa_{0}^{2}S_{J}.

If
j=0
and
r>r_{*}
, the left side contains the strictly positive term
a^{2}(r-r_{*})s^{2}
, which is impossible. If
j=0
and
r=r_{*}
, the inequality forces
\mathbf{Z}=0
, and (
12
) then forces
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}_{*}
, again a contradiction. Thus
1\leq j\leq r_{*}
, and
S_{J}\leq jh^{2}
yields

h^{2}\geq\frac{a^{2}}{\kappa_{0}^{2}}\frac{r-r_{*}+j}{j}s^{2}\geq\frac{a^{2}}{\kappa_{0}^{2}}\frac{r}{r_{*}}s^{2}.

Taking square roots proves the claim. No rank bound on
\mathbf{Z}
and no second-derivative assumption are needed for this purely matrix-theoretic step.
∎

Appendix C
Detailed proof of Proposition
11

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{W}=\mbox{diag}(\mathbf{I}_{r_{*}}/\sqrt{r_{*}},\mathbf{I}_{r}/\sqrt{r},0)
and

\mathbf{Z}_{ij}=\mathbf{e}_{i}\mathbf{e}_{j}^{T}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\mathbf{e}_{i}^{T}

for
1\leq i\leq r_{*}<j\leq r_{*}+r
. Then

\tau(\mathbf{X})=\langle\mathbf{W},\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\rangle
and

\|\mathbf{X}_{12}+\mathbf{X}_{21}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\langle\mathbf{Z}_{ij},\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\rangle^{2}
.
The matrices
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{Z}_{ij}
are mutually orthogonal, each with squared Frobenius norm
2
. Hence

\nabla^{2}f=\mathcal{I}+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\left(\mathbf{W}\otimes\mathbf{W}+\sum_{i,j}\mathbf{Z}_{ij}\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{ij}\right),

where
(\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{A})[\mathbf{E}]=\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{E}\rangle\mathbf{A}
.
Its eigenvalues are
1
and
\kappa
, proving A1 with
\mu=1
and
L=\kappa
. Since
f\geq 0=f(\mathbf{X}_{*})
, A2 also holds.

At
\bar{\mathbf{X}}
,
\tau(\bar{\mathbf{X}})=-2\sqrt{r_{*}}/(\kappa+1)
and

\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{X}})=-g\,\mbox{diag}(\mathbf{I}_{r_{*}},0,0),\qquad g=\frac{2\kappa}{\kappa+1}.

This gradient is normal to
T(\bar{\mathbf{X}})
, with spectral norm
g
, whereas
\sigma_{r}(\bar{\mathbf{X}})=s
. Thus the stated ratio equals
g/s
. Moreover,

\bar{\mathbf{X}}-\eta\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{X}})=\mbox{diag}(\eta g\mathbf{I}_{r_{*}},s\mathbf{I}_{r},0)
,
whose best rank-
r
approximations include
\bar{\mathbf{X}}
whenever
\eta g\leq s
, establishing the fixed-point claim, including the case of tied singular values.

For the local landscape, let
K
denote the union of the first and third coordinate blocks. Every feasible
\mathbf{X}
sufficiently near
\bar{\mathbf{X}}
has an invertible
(2,2)
block, and rank
r
then forces its Schur complement to vanish. Using local coordinates

\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{X}_{K2}
,
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{X}_{2K}
, and
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{X}_{22}-s\mathbf{I}_{r}
, we have

\mathbf{X}_{KK}=\mathbf{A}(s\mathbf{I}_{r}+\mathbf{C})^{-1}\mathbf{B}=\frac{1}{s}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}+O(\|(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C})\|^{3}).

Write
a_{ij}=(\mathbf{X}_{12})_{i,j-r_{*}}
and
b_{ji}=(\mathbf{X}_{21})_{j-r_{*},i}
. Since
\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{X}})=-g\,\mbox{diag}(\mathbf{I}_{r_{*}},0,0)
, the linear term in the Taylor expansion of
f
contributes

-(g/s)\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{X}_{12}\mathbf{X}_{21})
at quadratic order. Together with the Hessian terms, this yields

f(\mathbf{X})-f(\bar{\mathbf{X}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\leq r_{*}<j\leq r_{*}+r}\begin{pmatrix}a_{ij}\\
b_{ji}\end{pmatrix}^{\!T}\left(\mathbf{B}_{\kappa}-\frac{g}{s}\mathbf{J}\right)\begin{pmatrix}a_{ij}\\
b_{ji}\end{pmatrix}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\mathbf{X}_{32}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathbf{X}_{23}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathbf{C}\|_{F}^{2}\right)+\frac{\kappa-1}{4r}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{C})^{2}+O(\|(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C})\|^{3}),

where

\mathbf{B}_{\kappa}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\kappa+1&\kappa-1\\
\kappa-1&\kappa+1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{J}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Each
2\times 2
block has eigenvalues
\kappa-g/s
and
1+g/s
, while the remaining quadratic terms are positive definite. Since

\kappa-\frac{g}{s}=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-1}\left(\kappa-1-2\sqrt{\frac{r}{r_{*}}}\right),

the constrained Hessian is positive definite when
r<\frac{1}{4}(\kappa-1)^{2}r_{*}
, has a zero eigenvalue at equality, and has a negative eigenvalue when
r>\frac{1}{4}(\kappa-1)^{2}r_{*}
. This gives the respective local-minimum, degeneracy, and strict-saddle conclusions.
∎

Appendix D
Detailed proof of Theorem
13

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T}
and
\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}
. A direct computation gives

\nabla_{\mathbf{L}}g=\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\mathbf{R}+\lambda\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D},\qquad\nabla_{\mathbf{R}}g=\nabla f(\mathbf{X})^{T}\mathbf{L}-\lambda\mathbf{R}\mathbf{D}.

Step 1: critical points are balanced.
At a critical point,
0=\mathbf{L}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{L}}g-(\nabla_{\mathbf{R}}g)^{T}\mathbf{R}=\lambda(\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}+\mathbf{D}\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R})
. Taking the inner product with the symmetric matrix
\mathbf{D}
gives
\|\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathbf{R}\mathbf{D}\|_{F}^{2}=0
, so
\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{R}\mathbf{D}=0
. Then
\mathbf{D}\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{L}=(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D})^{T}\mathbf{L}=0
and similarly
\mathbf{D}\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}=0
, hence
\mathbf{D}^{2}=0
and
\mathbf{D}=0
. Consequently the critical-point equations reduce to

\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\mathbf{R}=0,\qquad\mathbf{L}^{T}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0.

(34)

Step 2: global minimizers.
Since
\mathbf{X}_{*}
is the unconstrained minimizer of
f
,
g(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R})\geq f(\mathbf{X})\geq f(\mathbf{X}_{*})
, with equality if and only if
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}_{*}
and
\mathbf{D}=0
. This value is attained, because
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X}_{*})=r_{*}\leq r
and
\mathbf{X}_{*}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{T}
admits the balanced factors
\mathbf{L}=[\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2},0]
,
\mathbf{R}=[\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{1/2},0]
. By Step 1, every critical point with
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}_{*}
is balanced and is therefore a global minimizer.

Step 3: critical points with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})=r
and
\mathbf{X}\neq\mathbf{X}_{*}
.
Then
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
have full column rank, and the column spaces of
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
are the column and row spaces of
\mathbf{X}
. By (
34
),
\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=[\nabla f(\mathbf{X})]_{\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{X}}^{\perp},\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{X}}^{\perp}}
, so
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
. Corollary
10
therefore gives a tangent direction
\xi\in T(\mathbf{X})
whose second-order variation under the rank constraint is negative. Since
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\mapsto\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}^{T}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{B}^{T}
maps onto
T(\mathbf{X})
, we may write
\xi=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}^{T}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{B}^{T}
. For a symmetric
\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, the pair
(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{K},-\mathbf{R}\mathbf{K})
satisfies
\mathbf{L}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{R}^{T}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{R}^{T}=0
, so
(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{K},\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{R}\mathbf{K})
still represents
\xi
. The first-order change of
\mathbf{D}
along
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is the symmetric matrix
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}=\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{R}
, and along
(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{K},-\mathbf{R}\mathbf{K})
it is
2(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{K}\mathbf{P})
with
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}\succ 0
. Since
\mathbf{K}\mapsto\mathbf{P}\mathbf{K}+\mathbf{K}\mathbf{P}
is invertible on symmetric matrices, we can choose
\mathbf{K}
so that the first-order change of
\mathbf{D}
along
(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime}):=(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{L}\mathbf{K},\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{R}\mathbf{K})
vanishes. Consider the straight factor-space curve
\mathbf{z}(t)=(\mathbf{L}+t\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{R}+t\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
. For small
|t|
, the product
\mathbf{X}(t)=(\mathbf{L}+t\mathbf{A}^{\prime})(\mathbf{R}+t\mathbf{B}^{\prime})^{T}
has rank
r
, satisfies
\mathbf{X}(0)=\mathbf{X}
and
\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)=\xi
, and the balancing term is
\frac{\lambda}{4}\|\mathbf{D}(t)\|_{F}^{2}=O(t^{4})
because
\mathbf{D}(0)=0
and
\dot{\mathbf{D}}(0)=0
. At a point satisfying
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
, the second derivative of
f
along rank-preserving curves with the same initial velocity has the same value; hence the choice of
\xi
from Corollary
10
gives

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}g(\mathbf{z}(t))\right|_{t=0}<0.

Thus
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R})
has a factor-space direction with negative second directional derivative.

Step 4: critical points with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})<r
and
\mathbf{X}\neq\mathbf{X}_{*}
.
Since
\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}
, we have
\ker\mathbf{L}=\ker\mathbf{R}
. This kernel is nontrivial, since otherwise
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
would have full column rank and
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})=r
. Let
\mathbf{w}
be a unit vector with
\mathbf{L}\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{R}\mathbf{w}=0
. Let
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{*}
. Since
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{E})\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X})+r_{*}<r+r_{*}
, Assumption A1 applied along the segment from
\mathbf{X}_{*}
to
\mathbf{X}
gives

\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{E}\rangle\geq\mu\|\mathbf{E}\|_{F}^{2}>0
, so
\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\neq 0
. Let
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
be a pair of unit top singular vectors of
-\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
, so that
\mathbf{a}^{T}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})\mathbf{b}=-\sigma_{1}(\nabla f(\mathbf{X}))
. By (
34
),
\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{a}=0
and
\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{b}=0
. Consider the curve
\mathbf{z}(t)=(\mathbf{L}+t\mathbf{a}\mathbf{w}^{T},\mathbf{R}+t\mathbf{b}\mathbf{w}^{T})
. Using
\mathbf{L}\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{R}\mathbf{w}=0
,

(\mathbf{L}+t\mathbf{a}\mathbf{w}^{T})(\mathbf{R}+t\mathbf{b}\mathbf{w}^{T})^{T}=\mathbf{X}+t^{2}\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}^{T},

and using
\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{b}=0
and
\|\mathbf{a}\|=\|\mathbf{b}\|=1
,

(\mathbf{L}+t\mathbf{a}\mathbf{w}^{T})^{T}(\mathbf{L}+t\mathbf{a}\mathbf{w}^{T})-(\mathbf{R}+t\mathbf{b}\mathbf{w}^{T})^{T}(\mathbf{R}+t\mathbf{b}\mathbf{w}^{T})=\mathbf{D}+t^{2}\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{T}-t^{2}\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{T}=0.

Hence
g(\mathbf{z}(t))=f(\mathbf{X}+t^{2}\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}^{T})=g(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{R})-\sigma_{1}(\nabla f(\mathbf{X}))\,t^{2}+O(t^{4})
, and therefore
\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}g(\mathbf{z}(t))\right|_{t=0}=-2\sigma_{1}(\nabla f(\mathbf{X}))<0
. This proves part (a).

Step 5: dynamics.
Let
\mathcal{G}=\{\mathbf{z}:g(\mathbf{z})<\bar{c}\}
, which is open. Its closure is compact: on it,
\frac{\mu}{2}\|\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T}-\mathbf{X}_{*}\|_{F}^{2}\leq f(\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T})-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})\leq\bar{c}-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})
and
\frac{\lambda}{4}\|\mathbf{D}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\bar{c}-f(\mathbf{X}_{*})
, while
\|\mathbf{L}^{T}\mathbf{L}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathbf{R}^{T}\mathbf{R}\|_{F}^{2}=\|\mathbf{D}\|_{F}^{2}+2\|\mathbf{L}\mathbf{R}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}
, so
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{R}
are bounded. For
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{G}
and
\eta<\min(1,1/L_{g})
, the segment from
\mathbf{z}
to
\mathbf{z}-\eta\nabla g(\mathbf{z})
lies in the set on which
\nabla g
is
L_{g}
-Lipschitz, so the descent lemma gives
g(\mathbf{z}-\eta\nabla g(\mathbf{z}))\leq g(\mathbf{z})-\frac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla g(\mathbf{z})\|^{2}
. Hence the gradient descent map
\phi(\mathbf{z})=\mathbf{z}-\eta\nabla g(\mathbf{z})
satisfies
\phi(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, and
\det D\phi=\det(I-\eta\nabla^{2}g)\neq 0
on
\mathcal{G}
because
\eta\|\nabla^{2}g\|\leq\eta L_{g}<1
there. At a strict saddle
\mathbf{z}^{*}
,
D\phi(\mathbf{z}^{*})
has the eigenvalue
1-\eta\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^{2}g(\mathbf{z}^{*}))>1
. By the standard measure-zero saddle-avoidance result for smooth maps on an invariant open set
[
15
,
21
]
, the set of initializations in
\mathcal{G}
whose iterates converge to a strict saddle has Lebesgue measure zero. On the other hand, since
f
is real analytic, so is
g
, and the iterates satisfy the strong descent condition
g(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})-g(\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)})\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla g(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})\|\,\|\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|
and stay in the compact closure of
\mathcal{G}
; since
\sum_{k}\|\nabla g(\mathbf{z}^{(k)})\|^{2}<\infty
, every accumulation point is a critical point of
g
, and by
[
1
]
the iterates converge to a single critical point of
g
. By part (a), with probability one this limit is a global minimizer, i.e.,
\mathbf{L}^{(k)}(\mathbf{R}^{(k)})^{T}\to\mathbf{X}_{*}
.
∎

Appendix E
Proof of Lemma
9

Proof.

Write
s=\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{X})>0
and
\mathbf{G}_{N}=P_{T(\mathbf{X})^{\perp}}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})
. Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
be a singular pair of
\mathbf{X}
for
s
, and choose unit singular vectors
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}
of
\mathbf{G}_{N}
so that
\mathbf{p}^{T}\mathbf{G}_{N}\mathbf{q}=\|\mathbf{G}_{N}\|
. They are orthogonal to the column and row spaces of
\mathbf{X}
. Define

\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(t)=\mathbf{X}+t(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{q}^{T}-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{v}^{T})-\frac{t^{2}}{s}\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}^{T}.

Replacing the term
s\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^{T}
in the SVD of
\mathbf{X}
by

(s\mathbf{u}-t\mathbf{p})(\mathbf{v}+(t/s)\mathbf{q})^{T}
shows that
\mathrm{rank}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(t))=r
for all sufficiently small
|t|
. Set
\boldsymbol{\Delta}(t)=\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(t)-\mathbf{X}
. The first-order condition
P_{T(\mathbf{X})}\nabla f(\mathbf{X})=0
and orthogonality give

\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{X}),\boldsymbol{\Delta}(t)\rangle=-\frac{\|\mathbf{G}_{N}\|}{s}t^{2},\qquad\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}(t)\|_{F}^{2}=2t^{2}+\frac{t^{4}}{s^{2}}.

Since
\mathrm{rank}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}(t))\leq 2\leq r+r_{*}
, Assumption A1 applies to the line segment in direction
\boldsymbol{\Delta}(t)
, and therefore

f(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(t))-f(\mathbf{X})\leq\left(L-\frac{\|\mathbf{G}_{N}\|}{s}\right)t^{2}+\frac{L}{2s^{2}}t^{4}.

Now reparameterize by

\mathbf{X}(t)=\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}(t/\sqrt{2}).

Then
\mathbf{X}(t)
is smooth and rank preserving for all sufficiently small
|t|
,
\mathbf{X}(0)=\mathbf{X}
, and

\|\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)\|_{F}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|\mathbf{u}\mathbf{q}^{T}-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{v}^{T}\|_{F}=1.

Moreover,

f(\mathbf{X}(t))-f(\mathbf{X})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(L-\frac{\|\mathbf{G}_{N}\|}{s}\right)t^{2}+\frac{L}{8s^{2}}t^{4}.

Because the first derivative at
t=0
vanishes, dividing by
t^{2}/2
and letting
t\to 0
gives

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}f(\mathbf{X}(t))\right|_{t=0}\leq L-\frac{\|\mathbf{G}_{N}\|}{s}<0.

Hence
\mathbf{X}
is not a local minimizer of
f
over matrices of rank
r
, and the quantitative claim in the lemma follows.

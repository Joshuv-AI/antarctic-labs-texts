Abstract
It is well-known that cyclic codes have efficient encoding and decoding algorithms. In recent years, antiprimitive BCH codes have attracted a lot of attention. The objective of this paper is to study BCH codes of this type over finite fields and analyse their parameters. Some lower bounds on the minimum distance of antiprimitive BCH codes are given. The BCH codes presented in this paper have good parameters in general, containing many optimal linear codes. In particular, two open problems about the minimum distance of BCH codes of this type are partially solved in this paper.

BCH code, cyclic code, coset leader, elementary symmetric polynomial.

Throughout this paper, let

{\mathcal{C}}

A very important class of linear codes is the class of

, which are invariant
under cyclic shifts

(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\longmapsto(c_{n-1},c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-2})

of the coordinates.
By identifying any vector
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}\in\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}{(x^{n}-1)},
any code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
corresponds to a subset of the principal quotient ring
\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}{(x^{n}-1)}.

Let

is a monic polynomial of smallest degree among all the generators of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then

, and

.

Let

\beta^{s}

with length
n

g_{(q,n,\delta,b)}(x)={\rm lcm}(\mathbb{M}_{\beta^{b}}(x),\mathbb{M}_{\beta^{b+1}}(x),\ldots,\mathbb{M}_{\beta^{b+\delta-2}}(x))

where the least common multiple is computed over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
When

{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,n,\delta,1)}
is called a

. If

If

Several important linear codes and bounds

For a vector

The

In particular,

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
[n,k,d]

. The

of
{\mathcal{C}}
over

{\mathcal{C}}|_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}=\{{\mathbf{c}}\in{\mathcal{C}}:{\mathbf{c}}\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{n}\}={\mathcal{C}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{r}^{n}.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear code of length

over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}|(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}^{T}=0~{\rm for~all}~\mathbf{y}\in{\mathcal{C}}\},

denotes the transposition of the vector
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}).
A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
is called

{\mathcal{C}}\bigcap{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. Let
f(x)=f_{h}x^{h}+f_{h-1}x^{h-1}+\cdots+f_{1}x+f_{0}

and

. A polynomial is

A code

•

An

The minimum distance of BCH codes has the following well-known bound.

Lemma 2

.

3
A necessary and sufficient condition for
0\leq a\leq q^{m}
being a coset leader

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{L}
denote the ring of integers modulo
L
.
Let
s
be an integer with
0\leq s<L
. The
q
-cyclotomic coset
of
s
is defined by

C_{s}=\{s,sq,sq^{2},\cdots,sq^{\ell_{s-1}}\}\,\,{\text{\,}\,mod\,\,L\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{L},}

where
\ell_{s}
is the smallest positive integer such that
s\equiv sq^{\ell_{s}}\pmod{L}
, and is the size of the
q
-cyclotomic coset.
The smallest integer in
C_{s}
is called the
coset leader
of
C_{s}
.

It is known that coset leaders provide information on the Bose distance and dimension of BCH codes. In this section, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for
0\leq a\leq q^{m}
being a coset leader. We call that
x
is a coset leader means that
x
is the coset leader of
C_{x}
modulo
n
. Firstly, we consider the case that
q
is an even prime power.

Proposition 6
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
q
be an even prime power. Let
1\leq i<m
,
l
and
h
be integers satisfying

1\leq l\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(q^{i}-1)q}{2(q+1)}\right\rfloor\,\,\text{and}\,\,-\frac{l(q^{m-i}-1)}{q^{i}+1}<h<\frac{l(q^{m-i}+1)}{q^{i}-1}.

Then
0\leq a\leq q^{m}
is a coset leader if and only if

0\leq a\leq\left\lfloor\frac{q^{m+1}+q}{2(q+1)}\right\rfloor
and
a\neq lq^{m-i}+h
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
a=0
is a coset leader. In the following, we only consider the case
0<a\leq q^{m}
.
By definition,
a
is not a coset leader if and only if
there exist an integer
0\leq b<a
and a positive integer
i
such that

aq^{i}\equiv b\pmod{n}.

(1)

Since
0\leq b<a\leq q^{m}
, we can assume that
1\leq i\leq 2m
.
Firstly, we consider the easy case
i=m
. Note that

aq^{m}\equiv-a\pmod{n},

then Eq.(
1
) can be written as

a+b\equiv 0\pmod{n},

which is the same as

a+b=n.

(2)

It is easily seen that Eq.(
2
) holds if and only if
\frac{q^{m}}{2}+1\leq a\leq q^{m}
since
b<a
,
then
a
is not a coset leader if
\frac{q^{m}}{2}+1\leq a\leq q^{m}
.

Hence, in order to obtain the desired claims,
we only need to consider a necessary and sufficient condition for
a
being not a coset leader when
0<a\leq\frac{q^{m}}{2}
and
i\neq m
.
Obviously, Eq.(
1
) is equivalent to

aq^{i}=b+nl_{0},

which is the same as

b+q^{m}l_{0}+l_{0}-aq^{i}=0,

(3)

where
l_{0}
is an integer. The proof will be carried out by distinguishing the following two cases.

Case 1
.
1\leq i<m
. In this case, Eq.(
3
) becomes

b=(a-l_{0}q^{m-i})q^{i}-l_{0}.

Since
b\geq 0
,
a
can be expressed as
a=l_{0}q^{m-i}+h_{0}
, where
h_{0}
is a positive integer.
Hence,
b=h_{0}q^{i}-l_{0}
. From
0\leq b<a\leq\frac{q^{m}}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}l_{0}q^{m-i}+h_{0}\leq\frac{q^{m}}{2},\\
h_{0}q^{i}-l_{0}\geq 0,\\
h_{0}q^{i}-l_{0}<l_{0}q^{m-i}+h_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Solving these inequalities, we obtain
1\leq l_{0}\leq\frac{q^{i}}{2}-1
and
1\leq h_{0}<\frac{l(q^{m-i}+1)}{q^{i}-1}
.
Then Eq.(
1
) holds if
a=l_{0}q^{m-i}+h
, where

1\leq l_{0}\leq\frac{q^{i}}{2}-1
and
1\leq h_{0}<\frac{l(q^{m-i}+1)}{q^{i}-1}
.

Case 2
.
m<i<2m
. It is clear that

aq^{i}\equiv-aq^{i-m}\pmod{n}.

Then
aq^{i}
can be expressed as
aq^{i}=t(q^{m}+1)-aq^{i-m}
, where

is a positive integer. Let
l_{1}=t-l_{0}
, then Eq.(
3
) becomes

b=nl_{1}-aq^{i-m}.

(5)

It is obvious that

since

.
By definition and Eq.(

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}a>nl_{1}-aq^{i-m},\\
nl_{1}-aq^{i-m}>0.\end{array}\right.

Solving these inequalities, the range of the value of

can be given as

\frac{l_{1}n}{q^{i-m}+1}<a<\frac{l_{1}n}{q^{i-m}}.

(7)

It is easily seen that

\frac{l_{1}n}{q^{i-m}+1}=\frac{l_{1}(q^{2m-i}(q^{i-m}+1)-q^{2m-i}+1)}{q^{i-m}+1}=l_{1}q^{2m-i}-\frac{l_{1}(q^{2m-i}-1)}{q^{i-m}+1}

(8)

and

(9)

Then we have
\left\lfloor l_{1}q^{2m-i}+\frac{l_{1}}{q^{i-m}}\right\rfloor\leq\frac{q^{m}}{2}

. Hence,
l_{1}\leq\frac{q^{i-m}}{2}
.

From the inequality in (
7
), Eqs.(
8
) and (
9
), we know that

can be written as

, where

1\leq l_{1}\leq\frac{q^{i-m}}{2}
and
0\leq h_{1}<\frac{l_{1}(q^{2m-i}-1)}{q^{i-m}+1}
.
Hence, Eq.(
1
) holds if
a=l_{1}q^{2m-i}-h_{1}
, where

1\leq l_{1}\leq\frac{q^{i-m}}{2}
and
0\leq h_{1}<\frac{l_{1}(q^{2m-i}-1)}{q^{i-m}+1}
.

Summarizing the discussions above, we know that

does not satisfy any of the following conditions:

(2)

and

if

satisfies Condition (II).
Hence, from the discussions above, we know that

is a coset leader if and only if

does not satisfy any of the following conditions:

•

;

•

, where

,

and

,

which is the same as that
a
is a coset leader if and only if

•

a\leq\left\lfloor\frac{q^{m+1}+q}{2(q+1)}\right\rfloor
;

•

a\neq lq^{m-i}+h
, where
1\leq i<m
,

1\leq l\leq\frac{q^{i}}{2}-1

Since

(10)

Solving the inequality in (

Example 7

4
The lower bounds on the minimum distance of antiprimitive BCH codes

In this section, our main task is to study the minimum distance of antiprimitive BCH codes. Let us start with two classes of important polynomials.

4.1
Two classes of important polynomials

We define
[l]:=\{1,2,\ldots,l\}
. The
multivariate homogeneous polynomial
of degree
r
in
l
variables
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l}
, written
P_{r,l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})
, is defined recursively by

P_{r,l}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})=x_{l}P_{r-1,l}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})+P_{r,l-1}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l-1});

P_{r,1}(x_{1})=x_{1}^{r};~~~~P_{0,l}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})\equiv 1;~~~~P_{-s,l}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})\equiv 0,~s>0.

Equally efficient notation is
P_{r,l}
.

The
elementary symmetric polynomial
(ESP for short) of degree
r
in
l
variables
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l}
, written
\sigma_{l,r}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})
, is defined by

\sigma_{l,r}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})=\sum\limits_{I\subseteq[l],\#I=r}\prod\limits_{j\in I}x_{j}.

An
m\times m
matrix

V_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m-1}}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&1&\cdots&1\\
a_{1}^{i_{1}}&a_{2}^{i_{1}}&\cdots&a_{m}^{i_{1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{1}^{{}^{i_{m-1}}}&a_{2}^{{}^{i_{m-1}}}&\cdots&a_{m}^{{}^{i_{m-1}}}\end{array}\right)

is called a
generalized Vandermonde matrix
, where the elements are all different, that is
s\neq t
implies
a_{s}\neq a_{t}.
Moreover, it is called a
Vandermonde matrix
, and written simply as
V(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})
, if
i_{1}=i_{2}-1=\cdots=i_{m-1}-(m-2)=1
.

The following lemma establishes a useful connection between the Vandermonde determinant and the generalized Vandermonde determinant.

Lemma 20
.

[
36
, Sec. 338]
[
39
]

Let
D_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})
denote the determinant of the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}P_{k_{j}-1,2}&P_{k_{j}-2,3}&\cdots&P_{k_{j}-m,m+1}\end{array}\right)_{1\leq j\leq m},

where
(P_{k_{j}-1,2}~~P_{k_{j}-2,3}~~\cdots~~P_{k_{j}-m,m+1})
denotes the
j
-th row of this matrix.
Then the determinant of
V_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})
is equal to

D_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})\det(V(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})).

Let
M_{\eta,l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l})
be the matrix defined as

M_{\eta,l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}x_{1}^{-l}&\cdots&x_{\eta}^{-l}&x_{\eta+1}^{-l}&\cdots&x_{\eta+l}^{-l}\\
x_{1}^{-l+1}&\cdots&x_{\eta}^{-l+1}&x_{\eta+1}^{-l+1}&\cdots&x_{\eta+l}^{-l+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{1}^{-1}&\cdots&x_{\eta}^{-1}&x_{\eta+1}^{-1}&\cdots&x_{\eta+l}^{-1}\\
x_{1}^{1}&\cdots&x_{\eta}^{1}&x_{\eta+1}^{1}&\cdots&x_{\eta+l}^{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{1}^{\eta}&\cdots&x_{\eta}^{\eta}&x_{\eta+1}^{\eta}&\cdots&x_{\eta+l}^{\eta}\end{array}\right)

with
1\leq l\leq\eta
and
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\eta+l}
being pairwise distinct.

According to the above auxiliary lemma, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 21
.

Let
l
be a positive integer and
1\leq l\leq\eta
. Then

\det(M_{\eta,l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l}))=\frac{\sigma_{\eta+l,\eta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l})}{\sigma_{\eta+l,\eta+l}^{l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l})}\det(V(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l})).

Proof.

Observe that

\det(M_{\eta,l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l}))=\frac{\det(V_{1,2,\ldots,l-1,l+1,\ldots,\eta+l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l}))}{\sigma_{\eta+l,\eta+l}^{l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l})}
. Lemma
20
implies that

\displaystyle\det(V_{1,2,\ldots,l-1,l+1,\ldots,\eta+l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l}))=

\displaystyle\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}P_{1,l+1}&P_{0,l+2}&0&\cdots&0\\
P_{2,l+1}&P_{1,l+2}&P_{0,l+3}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
P_{\eta-1,l+1}&P_{\eta-2,l+2}&P_{\eta-3,l+3}&\cdots&P_{0,l+\eta}\\
P_{\eta,l+1}&P_{\eta-1,l+2}&P_{\eta-2,l+3}&\cdots&P_{1,l+\eta}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\det(V(x_{1},\ldots,v_{\eta+l})).

It is easy to verify that
P_{0,l}=1
,
P_{1,l}=\sigma_{l,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})
and
P_{r,l}=\sum\limits_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{l}=r}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}^{i_{j}}
, where
i_{1},\ldots,i_{l}
are nonnegative integers.
The following equality

\det(V_{1,2,\ldots,l-1,l+1,\ldots,\eta+l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l}))=\sigma_{\eta+l,\eta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l}),

(17)

is proved by induction on
\eta
. The initial case
\eta=1
is trivial. When
\eta=2
,

\displaystyle\det\left(V_{1,\ldots,l-1,l+1,l+2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l+2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma_{l+1,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l+1})&1\\
P_{2,l+1}&\sigma_{l+2,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l+2})\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{l+1,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l+1})\sigma_{l+2,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l+2})-\sum\limits_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{l+1}=2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{l+1}x_{j}^{i_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{l+2,2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l+2}).

Assuming Eq.(
17
) holds with
\eta
replaced by
\eta-a
, where
a\in\{1,2,\ldots,\eta-1\}.
For the sake of convenience, let

\det(V_{1,2,\ldots,l-1,l+1,\ldots,\eta+l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\eta+l})):=F(\eta)

and let
\sigma_{k,l}
denote
\sigma_{k,l}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
in this proof.
It then follows from
F(\eta-a)=\sigma_{\eta-a+l,\eta-a}
that

\displaystyle F(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{1,l+\eta}F(\eta-1)-P_{2,l+\eta-1}F(\eta-2)+\ldots+(-1)^{\eta-2}P_{\eta-1,l+2}F(1)+(-1)^{\eta-1}P_{\eta,l+1}F(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{1,l+\eta}\sigma_{\eta-1+l,\eta-1}-P_{2,l+\eta-1}\sigma_{\eta-2+l,\eta-2}+\ldots+(-1)^{\eta-2}P_{\eta-1,l+2}\sigma_{l+1,1}+(-1)^{\eta-1}P_{\eta,l+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{\eta+l,\eta}.

The desired conclusion is therefore obtained.
∎

. By the definition of BCH codes, the generator polynomial of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q+1,\delta,1)}
is

.
It then follows from Delsarte’s theorem

that the trace expression of

is given by

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}_{(q,n,\delta,1)}=\{{\mathbf{c}}_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\eta})}:a_{1},\ldots,a_{\eta}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\}

where
{\mathbf{c}}_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\eta})}=({\rm{Tr}}_{q}^{q^{2}}(a_{1}\gamma^{i}+a_{2}\gamma^{2i}+\cdots+a_{\eta}\gamma^{\eta i}))_{i=0}^{q}
.
Define

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&\gamma^{1}&\gamma^{2}&\cdots&\gamma^{q}\\
1&\gamma^{2}&\gamma^{4}&\cdots&\gamma^{2q}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\

It is not difficult to check that

is a parity-check matrix of

If
{\mathbf{c}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{q+1})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q+1,\delta,1)}

, then there exists

=0
follows from

.

According to the BCH bound and the Singleton bound, we have that
\eta+1\leq d\leq 2\eta+1
.
Assume that

. Then there are
\eta+w
pairwise distinct elements

a_{s_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}\\
u_{1}^{2}\\
\vdots\\
u_{1}^{\eta}\\
\end{array}\right)+a_{s_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{2}\\
u_{2}^{2}\\
\vdots\\
u_{2}^{\eta}\\
\end{array}\right)+\cdots+a_{s_{\eta+w}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{\eta+w}\\
u_{\eta+w}^{2}\\

u_{\eta+w}^{\eta}\\
\end{array}\right)=0.

(19)

Eq.(

a_{s_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}^{-w}\\

u_{1}^{-1}\\

∎

Since

Corollary 23

Proof.

.
From Theorem

under the second condition. This completes the proof.

In other words,

4.3
The lower bounds on the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,b)}
for
m\geq 1

By the Delsarte theorem, we have that
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{m},q^{m}+1,\delta,b)}|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}={\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,b)}
. This equality is useful for deriving the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,b)}.

Let
H
be a parity-check matrix of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{m},q^{m}+1,\delta,b)}
. If there exists some vector
{\mathbf{c}}={(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q^{m}+1})}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{q^{m}+1}
with Hamming weight
i
such that
{\mathbf{c}}H^{T}=\mathbf{0}
, then the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,b)}
is no less than
i
. To some extent, it will help us to determine the minimum distance of some BCH codes with small designed distance
\delta
.

The following theorem improves the lower bound of Lemma
3
when
\delta\equiv 1~({\rm mod}~q)
.

Theorem 27
.

If
\delta\equiv 1~({\rm mod}~q)
and
n\geq 2\delta
, then

{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,0)}={\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta+1,0)}={\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,2(\delta-1),q^{m}-(\delta-2))}={\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,2\delta,q^{m}-(\delta-1))}
. Moreover, the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,0)}
has minimum distance
d\geq 2\delta
in this case.

Proof.

The proof of Theorem 17 in
[
30
]
shows that the generator polynomial
g_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,0)}(x)
has the zeroes
\beta^{i}
for all
i
in the set

\{q^{m}-(\delta-2),\ldots,q^{m}-2,q^{m}-1,0,1,2,\ldots,\delta-2\}.

It then follows that
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,0)}={\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,2(\delta-1),q^{m}-(\delta-2))}
for any positive integer
\delta\geq 2
.
Next, we only need to prove that
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{m},q^{m}+1,\delta,0)}|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}={\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{m},q^{m}+1,\delta+1,0)}|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.
In a manner similar to the proof of Theorem
22
, we need the parity-check matrix of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{l},q^{l}+1,\delta,0)}
when
\delta\equiv 1~({\rm mod}~q)
. For convenience, take
\delta=q^{j}t+1
for some nonnegative integers
j
and
t
, where
1<t<q
.
Let
\gamma=\beta^{-1}
. Then
\gamma
is a primitive
(q^{m}+1)
-th root of unity.
Define

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&\gamma&\gamma^{2}&\cdots&\gamma^{q^{m}}\\
1&\gamma^{2}&\gamma^{4}&\cdots&\gamma^{2q^{m}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&\gamma^{q^{j}t-1}&\gamma^{2(q^{j}t-1)}&\cdots&\gamma^{(q^{j}t-1){q^{m}}}\\
\end{array}\right).

It is easy to check that
H
is a parity-check matrix of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{m},q^{m}+1,q^{j}t+1,0)}
, and

{\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{m},q^{m}+1,q^{j}t+1,0)}|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=\{{\mathbf{c}}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q^{m}+1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{q^{m}+1}:{\mathbf{c}}H^{T}=\mathbf{0}\}.

Raising to the
q
-th power both sides of the equation
c_{1}+c_{2}\gamma^{q^{j-1}t}+c_{3}\gamma^{2q^{j-1}t}+\cdots+c_{q^{m}+1}\gamma^{q^{m}q^{j-1}t}=0
yields

c_{1}+c_{2}\gamma^{q^{j}t}+c_{3}\gamma^{2q^{j}t}+\cdots+c_{q^{m}+1}\gamma^{q^{m}q^{j}t}=0.

Let
\mathbf{w}=(1,\gamma^{q^{j}t},\gamma^{2q^{j}t},\cdots,\gamma^{q^{m}q^{j}t}).

It then follows that

{\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{m},q^{m}+1,q^{j}t+1,0)}|_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=\{{\mathbf{c}}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q^{m}+1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{q^{m}+1}:{\mathbf{c}}H_{1}^{T}=\mathbf{0}\},

where
H_{1}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}H\\
\mathbf{w}\\
\end{array}\right).
Since
H_{1}
is a parity-check matrix of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q^{m},q^{m}+1,q^{j}t+2,0)}
, the desired result is obtained.
∎

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
27
, and

.
Let
\sigma(x)=(x-u_{1})(x-u_{2})(x-u_{3})(x-u_{4})(x-u_{5})
. Similarly, we have
\sigma(x)=x^{5}+\sigma_{5,1},
which can not have
4
zeroes in
U_{q+1}
since
\gcd(q+1,5)=1.

Therefore,
d>5
when
m=4t
.

If
m\equiv 4~({\rm mod}~8)
, then
x^{82}-1|x^{3^{m}+1}-1
. There exists a six-term factor
f(x)=x^{16}-x^{15}+x^{11}+x^{5}-x+1
which satisfies
f(x)|x^{82}-1
and
f(\beta^{\frac{5(q+1)}{82}})=f(\beta^{\frac{5(q+1)}{41}})=0
, where
\beta
is a generator of
U_{q+1}
. The fact that
\beta^{\frac{5(q+1)}{82}}
and
\beta^{\frac{5(q+1)}{41}}
are roots of
f(x)
was checked by Magma.

This completes the proof.
∎

5
Conclusion

The main contributions of this paper are the following:

(1)

We provided a necessary and sufficient condition for
0\leq a\leq q^{m}
being a coset leader modulo
n
for any prime power
q
.

(2)

A lower bound on the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q+1,\delta,1)}
is given. Some improved lower bounds on the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,0)}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,q^{m}+1,\delta,b)}
are given.

(3)

Two open problems in
[
16
,
30
]
are partially solved in this paper. The method of using the ESPs
\sigma_{i,j}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j})
to study the minimum distance of BCH codes is novel and innovative.

(4)

According to the tables of best codes known in
[
21
]
, some of the proposed linear codes are optimal.

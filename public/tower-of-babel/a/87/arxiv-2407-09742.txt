Non-left-orderability of lattices in higher-rank semisimple Lie groups (after Deroin and Hurtado)
Dave Witte Morris

Abstract
. Let G G be a connected, semisimple, real Lie group with finite centre, with rank ℝ ⁡ G ≥ 2 \rank_{\mathbb{R}}G\geq 2 . B. Deroin and S. Hurtado recently proved the 30-year-old conjecture that no irreducible lattice in G G has a left-invariant total order. (Equivalently, they proved that no such lattice has a nontrivial, orientation-preserving action on the real line.) We will explain many of the main ideas of the proof, by using them to prove the analogous result for lattices in p p -adic semisimple groups. The p p -adic case is easier, because some of the technical issues do not arise.



1.
Introduction

Definition 1.1

(
[
16
, p. 7]
)
.

A group
\Gamma
is
left-orderable
if it has a left-invariant total order. This means there is a transitive binary relation
\prec
, such that, for all
a,b,c\in\Gamma
,

•

exactly one of the following is true:
a\prec b
, or
a=b
, or
a\succ b
,
and

•

if
a\prec b
, then
ca\prec cb
.

Understanding which groups are left-orderable is a longstanding problem in algebra
[
25
,
30
]
and is also important in topology
[
11
]
. The following result proves a conjecture that was suggested informally (at least for the case where
G
is simple) by the current author more than 30 years ago, and became official when it was proposed independently by É. Ghys
[
22
, p. 200]
in 1999.

Theorem 1.2

(Deroin-Hurtado
[
14
, Thm. 1.3]
)
.

If
\Gamma
is an irreducible lattice in a connected, semisimple, real Lie group
G
with finite centre, and
\rank_{\mathbb{R}}G\geq 2
, then
\Gamma
is not left-orderable.

Theorem

1.2
can be restated as follows:

Corollary 1.3

(Deroin-Hurtado
[
14
, Thm. 1.3]
)
.

Assume
\Gamma
is an irreducible lattice in a connected, semisimple, real Lie group
G
with finite centre, and
\rank_{\mathbb{R}}G\geq 2
. Then
\Gamma
has no nontrivial action on the real line by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms.

Proof.

Suppose
\Gamma
has a nontrivial, orientation-preserving action on
\mathbb{R}
, and let
N
be the kernel of the action. Then
\Gamma/N
has a faithful, orientation-preserving action on
\mathbb{R}
, so it is not difficult to see that
\Gamma/N
is left-orderable
[
23
, Thm. 6.8]
.

On the other hand, since no finite groups are left-orderable
[
16
, §1.4.1, p. 27]
, we know that
N
is a normal subgroup of infinite index, so the Margulis Normal Subgroup Theorem
[
29
, Thm. 17.1.1, p. 347]
tells us that
N
is contained in the centre of
G
. So
N
is normal in
G
. Then
\Gamma/N
is a left-orderable, irreducible lattice in the semisimple Lie group
G/N
. This contradicts
theorem

1.2
.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

If we do not require the homeomorphisms to be orientation-preserving, then there could be a nontrivial action: the map
x\mapsto-x
is a homeomorphism of order
2
, and it is possible that
\Gamma
has a nontrivial homomorphism to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. However, it follows from
theorem

1.2
that every action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{R}
(whether orientation-preserving or not) must be
virtually trivial
. This means that some finite-index subgroup of
\Gamma
acts trivially. (In fact, the subgroup will have index
1
or
2
.)

By combining
theorem

1.2
with the following result, Deroin and Hurtado also obtained a strong result for actions on the circle
S^{1}
, not only actions on
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1.5

(Ghys
[
22
]
, cf. Burger-Monod
[
9
, Cor. 1.5]
,
[
8
, Cor. 1.7]
)
.

Assume
\Gamma
is an irreducible lattice in a connected, semisimple, real Lie group
G
, and
\rank_{\mathbb{R}}G\geq 2
. If no almost-simple factor of
G
is locally isomorphic to
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, then every action of
\Gamma
on the circle
S^{1}
has a finite orbit.

Corollary 1.6

(Deroin-Hurtado
[
14
]
)
.

Assume
\Gamma
is an irreducible lattice in a connected, semisimple, real Lie group
G
, and
\rank_{\mathbb{R}}G\geq 2
. If no almost-simple factor of
G
is locally isomorphic to
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, then every action of
\Gamma
on the circle
S^{1}
is virtually trivial.

Proof.

By
theorem

1.5
, we may let
x
be a point whose orbit is finite. Then its stabilizer
\Gamma_{\!x}
is a finite-index subgroup that acts on
S^{1}\smallsetminus\{x\}
, which is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
. Then we see from
Remark

1.4
that a finite-index subgroup of
\Gamma_{\!x}
must act trivially.
∎

Remark 1.7
.

The group
\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
acts faithfully on
\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\simeq S^{1}
(by linear-fractional transformations). Therefore, if some almost-simple factor of
G
is locally isomorphic to
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, then every lattice in
G
has a highly nontrivial action on
S^{1}
(because there is a surjective homomorphism
G\to\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
). This shows that
corollary

1.6
would not be valid without the hypothesis that no factor is locally isomorphic to
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
.

For simplicity, the statements of the above results assume that the centre of
G
is finite. There are counterexamples without this assumption, but Deroin and Hurtado proved that all of them arise from the fact that the action of
\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
on
S^{1}
lifts to an orientation-preserving action of
\widetilde{\SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
(the universal cover of
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
) on
\mathbb{R}
. In particular:

Corollary 1.8

(Deroin-Hurtado
[
14
, Thm. 1.1 and §2]
)
.

Assume
G
is a connected, semisimple, real Lie group, and
\rank_{\mathbb{R}}G\geq 2
.
An irreducible lattice
\Gamma
in
G
is left-orderable if and only if it is torsion-free and there is a surjective homomorphism
G\to\widetilde{\SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 1.9
.

Theorem

1.2
is a huge advance over what was previously known. In particular:

(1)

There was not a single example of a cocompact, torsion-free lattice
\Gamma
(in a connected, semisimple Lie group), for which it was known that no finite-index subgroup of
\Gamma
is left-orderable.

(2)

For the noncocompact case, the theorem had been proved under the additional assumption that either

[
32
]
or

[
26
]
. However, the theorem was not known to be true even for the special case where

See

]
for expository discussions of actions of lattices (and related groups) on

, and see

.

2.
Preliminaries

2A.
Measure theory

Reminder 2.1
.

Let
\mu
be a measure on a separable, metrizable topological space
Y
.

(1)

We say that
\mu
is a
probability measure
if
\mu(Y)=1
.

(2)

The
support
of
\mu
is the (unique) smallest closed set whose complement has measure
0
.

(3)

\mu
is
atomic
if
\mu(B)=\sum_{b\in B}\mu\bigl(\{b\}\bigr)
for every Borel set
B
. Informally, this means that
\mu
is a sum of point masses.

(4)

A property is true
for
\mu
-a.e.
y\in Y
if the set of points where it is false has measure
0
. In this case, we may also say that the property is true
\mu
-a.e.
, or that is is true for
\mu
-a.e.
y\in Y
.

(5)

A measure
\mu^{\prime}
is
absolutely continuous
with respect to
\mu
if, for every Borel set
B
, such that
\mu(B)=0
, we also have
\mu^{\prime}(B)=0
.

(6)

If
\mu^{\prime}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
, then there is a non-negative measurable function
d\mu^{\prime}/d\mu
(called the
Radon-Nikodym derivative
of
\mu^{\prime}
with respect to
\mu
), such that
\mu^{\prime}=(d\mu^{\prime}/d\mu)\,\mu
, in the sense that, for every real-valued continuous function with compact support, we have

\int_{Y}f\,d\mu^{\prime}=\int_{Y}f(y)\,\frac{d\mu^{\prime}}{d\mu}(y)\,d\mu(y).

The Radon-Nikodym derivative is unique up to a set of
\mu
-measure
0
.

(7)

If two measures are absolutely continuous with respect to each other, then they are in the same
measure class
. (This is an equivalence relation.)

(8)

If
f\colon Y\to Z
is a continuous function to a topological space
Z
, then we define a measure
f_{*}\mu
on
Z
by

f_{*}\mu(B)=\mu\bigl(f^{-1}(B)\bigr).

2B.
Topological groups

Reminder 2.2
.

Assume
H
is a locally compact topological group.

•

H
is said to be
locally compact
if it has a nonempty, open subset whose closure is compact. All groups in this paper (including
G
,
K
,
\Gamma
, and
\mathbb{R}
) are locally compact (and second countable).

•

It is a basic fact in the theory of topological groups that every locally compact group has a
Haar measure
. This is a measure
m_{H}
on
H
, such that:

\circ

m_{H}
is left-invariant:
m_{H}(hB)=m_{H}(B)
for all
h\in H
and every Borel set
B
,

\circ

m_{H}
is locally finite:
m_{H}(C)<\infty
for every compact set
C
,
and

\circ

m_{H}
has full support:
m_{H}(U)>0
for every open set
U
.

Furthermore, the Haar measure is unique, up to a scalar multiple: if
\mu
is another Haar measure on
H
, then there is a constant
c>0
, such that
\mu(B)=c\,m_{H}(B)
for every Borel set
B
.

•

H
is
unimodular
if its Haar measure is bi-invariant. This means it is both left-invariant and right-invariant:
\mu_{H}(hB)=\mu_{H}(B)=\mu_{H}(Bh)
. Note that this implies that
\mu_{H}
is invariant under conjugation:
\mu_{H}(hBh^{-1})=\mu_{H}(B)
.

•

If
H
is either a semisimple Lie group or a discrete group or a compact group, then
H
is unimodular.

•

If
H
is compact, then
m_{H}(H)<\infty
(because the measure of every compact set is finite), and we usually choose the normalizing scalar to make
m_{H}
a probability measure.

•

If
H
is
not
compact, then
m_{H}(H)=\infty
, so it is not possible for the Haar measure on a noncompact group to be a probability measure.

Definition 2.3
.

Assume
G
is a unimodular topological group.

•

If
\Gamma
is any discrete subgroup of
G
, then any Haar measure
m_{G}
induces a well-defined
G
-invariant measure
m_{G/\Gamma}
on
G/\Gamma
by setting:

m_{G/\Gamma}(B)=m_{G}\bigl(\pi^{-1}(B)\cap\mathcal{F}\bigr),

where
\pi\colon G\to G/\Gamma
is the natural quotient map (
\pi(g)=g\Gamma
), and
\mathcal{F}
is a measurable set of coset representatives for
\Gamma
in
G
(or, in other words,
\mathcal{F}
is a
fundamental domain
for
\Gamma
in
G
).

•

A subgroup
\Gamma
of a locally compact topological group
G
is a
lattice
in
G
if

\Gamma
is discrete,
and
the quotient space
G/\Gamma
has finite measure (i.e.,
m_{G/\Gamma}(G/\Gamma)<\infty
).

•

A lattice
\Gamma
is
irreducible
if
\Gamma N
is dense in
G
, for every closed, noncompact, normal subgroup
N
of
G
.

•

A closed subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
is
cocompact
if
G/\Gamma
is compact.

Every discrete, cocompact subgroup is a lattice (because the Haar measure of every compact set is finite). The converse is not true in general, but the first part of the following result is a special case of the fact that it is true in
p
-adic groups:

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume
\Gamma
is an irreducible lattice in a
p
-adic semisimple Lie group
G
, such that
\rank_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}G\geq 2
. Then:

(1)

\Gamma
is cocompact
[
27
, Prop. IX.3.7, p. 313]
,

(2)

the commutator subgroup
[\Gamma,\Gamma]
has finite index in
\Gamma

[
27
, Thm. IX.5.4, p. 325]
,
and

(3)

if
\Gamma
acts on the circle
S^{1}
by homeomorphisms, then the action has a finite orbit
[
33
, Cor. 6.10]
.

2C.
Group actions

Definition 2.5
.

Assume

•

a topological group
H
acts by homeomorphisms on a metrizable topological space
Y
,

•

\mu_{H}
is a probability measure on
H
,
and

•

\mu_{Y}
is a probability measure on
Y
.

Then:

(1)

The action of
H
is
free
if
hy\neq y
for all nonidentity
h\in H
and all
y\in Y
.

(2)

The measure
\mu_{Y}
is
H
-invariant
if
h_{*}\mu_{Y}=\mu_{Y}
for all
h\in H
(where
h_{*}\mu_{Y}
was defined in
Reminder

2.1
(
8
)). In other words,
\mu_{Y}(hB)=\mu_{Y}(B)
, for every
h\in H
and every Borel set
B
.

(3)

An
H
-invariant measure
\mu_{Y}
is
ergodic
if, for every
H
-invariant Borel set
B
, either
\mu_{Y}(B)=0
, or
\mu_{Y}(Y\setminus B)=0
.

(4)

The convolution
\mu_{H}*\mu_{Y}
is the measure on
Y
defined by

\text{$\mu_{H}*\mu_{Y}=\int_{H}h_{*}\mu_{Y}\,d\mu_{H}(H)$,
\quad so \quad$(\mu_{H}*\mu_{Y})(B)=\int_{H}\mu_{Y}(h^{-1}B)\,d\mu_{H}(h)$}.

(5)

We say that
\mu_{Y}
is
\mu_{H}
-stationary
if
\mu_{H}*\mu_{Y}=\mu_{Y}
. More concretely, for every Borel set
B
, this means that we have

\mu_{Y}(B)=\int_{H}\mu_{Y}(h^{-1}B)\,d\mu_{H}(h).

In other words, the measure of every set is equal to the average of the measures of its translates.

(6)

If
\mu_{Y}
is
H
-invariant, then there is a unique probability measure
\eta
on the space
\mathcal{E}
of ergodic
H
-invariant measures on
Y
, such that

\mu_{Y}=\int_{\mathcal{E}}\xi\,d\eta(\xi).

(See
[
31
, Thm., p. 77]
, for example.)
Measures in the support of
\eta
are called
ergodic components
of
\mu_{Y}
.

(7)

A function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is
Lipschitz
if there is a constant
C\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

\text{$|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C\,|x-y|$ \quad for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$}.

2D.
Dynamical systems

Proposition 2.6

(cf.
[
17
, Cor. 4.2 and Thm. 4.4, pp. 98–99]
)
.

Assume
\mathbb{R}
acts continuously on a compact, metrizable space
Y
. Then there is at least one
\mathbb{R}
-invariant probability measure on
Y
that is ergodic.

Theorem 2.7

(Pointwise Ergodic Theorem
[
17
, Thm. 2.30, p. 44]
)
.

Assume a cyclic group
\langle g\rangle
acts by homeomorphisms on a metrizable space
Y
, and
\mu
is an ergodic
\langle g\rangle
-invariant probability measure on
Y
. Then, for any continuous function
f
on
Y
with compact support, and
\mu
-a.e.
y\in Y
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(g^{i}y)=\int_{Y}f\,d\mu.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
cocompact
, irreducible lattice in a
(
real or
p
-adic
)
semisimple Lie group
G
, and let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
. Then:

(1)

The
P
-invariant probability measure on
G/\Gamma
is unique
(
namely, the
G
-invariant measure
m_{G/\Gamma}
is the only
P
-invariant probability measure
)
.

(2)

G/\Gamma
has no closed, nonempty, proper,
P
-invariant subsets. In other words, for all
y\in G/\Gamma
, the orbit
Py
is dense in
G/\Gamma
.

Idea of proof.

Since
P
is parabolic, we know there is an
\mathbb{R}
-split semisimple element
a\in P
, such that

P=\bigl\{\,p\in G\mid\text{$\{\,a^{n}pa^{-n}\mid n\leq 0\,\}$ is bounded}\,\bigr\}.

By the Moore Ergodicity Theorem
[
29
, Exer. 11.2#11, p. 218]
, we also know that
a
is ergodic on
G/\Gamma
(with respect to the
G
-invariant probability measure
m_{G/\Gamma}
).

(
2
) Since
a
is ergodic, almost every
a
-orbit is dense. Hence, we can choose some
y^{\prime}\approx y
, such that
\{\,a^{n}y^{\prime}\mid n\geq 0\,\}
is dense in
G/\Gamma
.
Let

U_{a}^{-}=\bigl\{\,u\in G\mid a^{n}ua^{-n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{1}\,\}.

Then
U_{a}^{-}P
contains a neighbourhood of
\mathbf{1}
in
G
, so we may write
y^{\prime}=upy
with
u\in U_{a}^{-}
and
p\in P
.

For any
x\in G/\Gamma
, there is some large
n>0
, such that
a^{n}y^{\prime}\approx x
. Then

x\approx a^{n}y^{\prime}=a^{n}upy=a^{n}u^{-}a^{-n}\cdot a^{n}py\approx\mathbf{1}\cdot a^{n}py\in Py.

(
1
) Let
\mu
be an ergodic
P
-invariant probability measure on
G/\Gamma
, let
f
be a continuous function on
G/\Gamma
, and let

Y=\left\{\,y\in G/\Gamma\mathrel{\left|\vphantom{\left\{y\in G/\Gamma\mid\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(a^{i}y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{G/\Gamma}f\,d\mu\right\}}\right.}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(a^{i}y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{G/\Gamma}f\,d\mu\,\right\}.

We have
\mu(Y)=1
by the Pointwise Ergodic Theorem (
2.7
). Since
\mu
is
P
-invariant, this implies that for
\mu
-a.e.
u\in Y
and
m_{P}
-a.e.
p\in P
, we have
py\in Y
. Also, it is not difficult to see that
uy\in Y
for every
u\in U_{a}^{-}
. This easily implies that
m_{G/\Gamma}(Y)>0
. Hence, by the Pointwise Ergodic Theorem (
2.7
), there exists
y\in Y
, such that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(a^{i}y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{G/\Gamma}f\,dm_{G/\Gamma}.

So we must have
\int_{G/\Gamma}f\,d\mu=\int_{G/\Gamma}f\,dm_{G/\Gamma}
.
∎

Remark 3.4

(1)

Since we are assuming that (

We may assume that

Then

Definition 5.1

(Furstenberg

at certain other points.

Lemma 5.2

(
[

, p. 363]

Every
\mu_{G}
-harmonic function

For

\displaystyle f(kx)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(gkx)\,d\mu_{G}(g)

-harmonic)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(gx)\,d\mu_{G}(g)

(

is right

(

\displaystyle=f(x)

\displaystyle\text{($f$ is $\mu_{G}$-harmonic)}.\qed

Remark 5.3

Choose some

in
G

. (This means that, for every

n

\mu_{G}

We define a stopping time

Now, suppose

-harmonic.

Conversely, given an appropriate function on

-harmonic function that is defined on all of

(1)

D_{G}\colon G\times Z\to\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
. (This function is
\mu_{G}
-harmonic in the
G
-coordinate, and defined for
\mu_{Z}
-a.e.
z
.) Let

\widetilde{\mu_{X}}=D_{G}\cdot(m_{G}\times\mu_{Z}),

so
\widetilde{\mu_{X}}
is a measure on
G\times Z
that is in the measure class of
m_{G}\times\mu_{Z}
.

For any
g\in G
, the uniqueness of
D_{G}
implies that (
5.10
) still holds if we replace
D
with
D_{G}
, and replace
\lambda
with
g
.
Then for
\gamma\in\Gamma
(acting on the right via
(g,z)\mapsto(g\gamma,\gamma^{-1}z)
), we can use the fact that
d\gamma^{-1}_{*}\mu_{Z}/d\mu_{Z}=D_{G}(\gamma,z)
(see
lemma

3.25
) to conclude that

\frac{d\gamma_{*}\widetilde{\mu_{X}}}{d\widetilde{\mu_{X}}}(g,z)=\frac{D_{G}(g\gamma^{-1},\gamma z)}{D_{G}(g,z)}\cdot\frac{d\gamma_{*}m_{G}}{dm_{G}}(g)\cdot\frac{d\gamma^{-1}_{*}\mu_{Z}}{d\mu_{Z}}(z)=\frac{D_{G}(g\gamma^{-1},\gamma z)}{D_{G}(g,z)}\cdot 1\cdot D_{G}(\gamma,z)=1.

This means that
\widetilde{\mu_{X}}
is invariant for the action of
\Gamma
, so we can construct a well-defined measure
\mu_{X}
on
X
from
\widetilde{\mu_{X}}
, by restricting the quotient map
G\times Z\to X
to any fundamental domain of the action.
The harmonicity of
D
in the
G
-coordinate implies that
\mu_{X}
is
\mu_{G}
-stationary.

The advantage of this approach is that it shows
\mu_{X}
is in the same measure class as
m_{G}\times\mu_{Z}
(restricted to a fundamental domain).

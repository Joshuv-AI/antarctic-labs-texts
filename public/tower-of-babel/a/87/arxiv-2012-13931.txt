Local Well-posedness of the Free-Boundary Problem in Compressible Resistive Magnetohydrodynamics
Junyan Zhang

Abstract
We prove the local well-posedness in Sobolev spaces of the free-boundary problem for compressible inviscid resistive isentropic MHD system under the Rayleigh-Taylor physical sign condition, which describes the motion of a free-boundary compressible plasma in an electro-magnetic field with magnetic diffusion. We use Lagrangian coordinates and apply the tangential smoothing method introduced by Coutand-Shkoller [ 8 , 10 ] to construct the approximation solutions. One of the key observations is that the Christodoulou-Lindblad type elliptic estimate [ 6 ] together with magnetic diffusion not only gives the common control of magnetic field and fluid pressure directly, but also controls the Lorentz force which is a higher order term in the energy functional.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the 3D magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations with magnetic resistivity

\begin{cases}\rho D_{t}u=B\cdot\nabla B-\nabla P,~~P:=p+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}~~~&\text{in}~\mathcal{D};\\
D_{t}\rho+\rho\text{div }u=0~~~&\text{in}~\mathcal{D};\\
D_{t}B-\lambda\Delta B=B\cdot\nabla u-B\text{div }u,~~~&\text{in}~\mathcal{D};\\
\text{div }B=0~~~&\text{in}~\mathcal{D},\end{cases}

(1.1)

describing the motion of a compressible conducting fluid in an electro-magnetic field with magnetic diffusion,
\lambda>0
is the magnetic diffusivity constant.
\mathcal{D}:={\bigcup}_{0\leq t\leq T}\{t\}\times\mathcal{D}_{t}
and
\mathcal{D}_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the domain occupied by the conducting fluid whose boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}
moves with the velocity of the fluid.
\nabla:=(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}},\partial_{x_{3}})
is the standard spatial derivative and
\text{div }X:=\partial_{x_{\alpha}}X^{\alpha}
is the standard divergence for any vector field
X
. Throughout this paper,
X^{\alpha}=\delta^{\alpha\beta}X_{\beta}
for any vector field
X
, i.e., we use Einstein summation convention. The fluid velocity
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
, the magnetic field
B=(B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})
, the fluid density
\rho
, the pressure
p
and the domain
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
are to be determined.
D_{t}:=\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla
is the material derivative. Here we consider the isentropic case, and thus the fluid pressure
p=p(\rho)
should be a given strictly increasing smooth function of the density
\rho
.

1.1
Initial and boundary conditions and constraints

We consider the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
): Given a simply-connected bounded domain
\mathcal{D}_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the initial data
u_{0}
,
\rho_{0}
and
B_{0}
satisfying the constraints
\text{div }B_{0}=0
in
\mathcal{D}_{0}
and
B=\mathbf{0}
on
\partial\mathcal{D}_{0}
, we want to find a set
\mathcal{D}
, the vector field
u
, the magnetic field
B
, and the density
\rho
solving (
1.1
) satisfying the initial conditions:

\mathcal{D}_{0}=\{x:(0,x)\in\mathcal{D}\},\quad(u,B,\rho)=(u_{0},B_{0},\rho_{0}),\quad\text{in}\,\,\{0\}\times\mathcal{D}_{0}.

(1.2)

Thus we need to introduce the initial and boundary conditions. First, we require the following boundary conditions on the free boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}={\cup}_{0\leq t\leq T}\{t\}\times\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}
:

\begin{cases}D_{t}|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}\in T(\partial\mathcal{D})\\
p=0&\text{on}~\partial\mathcal{D},\\
B=\mathbf{0}&\text{on}~\partial\mathcal{D}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

Illustration on the boundary conditions

The first condition in (
1.3
) means that the boundary moves with the velocity of the fluid. It can be equivalently rewritten as:
V(\partial\mathcal{D}_{t})=u\cdot n
on
\partial\mathcal{D}
or
(1,u)
is tangent to
\partial\mathcal{D}
. The second condition in (
1.3
) means that outside the fluid region
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is the vacuum. The third boundary condition requires that the magnetic field vanishes on the boundary.

Remark
.

1.

In (
1.1
), the divergence-free condition of
B
is just a constraint for initial data and thus the system is not over-determined. Indeed, the original version of the third equation in (
1.1
) is
\partial_{t}B=-\nabla\times E
where
E:=-u\times B+\lambda j
is the electric field and
j:=\nabla\times B
is the current density. Taking divergence in this equation and using the continuity equation will give us
D_{t}(\rho^{-1}\text{div }B)=0
and thus
\text{div }B=0
is preserved if it initially holds. Throughout this manuscript, we will always use the heat equation of
B
to do estimates.

2.

For ideal MHD (
\lambda=0
), the boundary condition
B=\mathbf{0}
should NOT be an imposed conditions for the system, otherwise the system is over-determined. Instead, this is a direct result of propagation of the constraints on initial data. See also Hao-Luo
[
24
]
for details. However, for resistive MHD, the equation of
B
is a parabolic equation which has to be given a boundary condition as opposed to the ideal case. Such a condition will not make the system be over-determined. See also Wang-Xin
[
59
]
in the case of incompressible inviscid resistive MHD surface waves.

Related physical background

The free-boundary problem considered in this manuscript originates from the plasma-vacuum free-interface model, which is an important theoretic model both in laboratory and in astro-physical magnetohydrodynamics: The plasma is confined in a vacuum in which there is another magnetic field
\hat{B}
, and there is a free interface
\Gamma(t)
, moving with the motion of plasma, between the plasma region
\Omega_{+}(t)
and the vacuum region
\Omega_{-}(t)
. This model requires that (
1.1
) holds in the plasma region
\Omega_{+}(t)
and the pre-Maxwell system holds in vacuum
\Omega_{-}(t)
:

\text{curl }\hat{B}=\mathbf{0},~~~\text{div }\hat{B}=0.

(1.4)

On the interface
\Gamma(t)
, it is required that there is no jump for the pressure or the
normal components of magnetic fields
:

B\cdot n=\hat{B}\cdot n,~~~P:=p+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|\hat{B}|^{2}

(1.5)

where
n
is the exterior unit normal to
\Gamma(t)
. Note that for ideal MHD (
\lambda=0
) (
1.5
) should also be a constraint on initial data which propagates instead of an imposed boundary condition. For more details, we refer readers to Chapter 4, 6 in
[
19
]
.

Hence, the case considered in this manuscript is that the vacuum magnetic field
\hat{B}
vanishes plus the imposed condition
B=\mathbf{0}
on the boundary which is reasonable for resistive MHD. It characterizes the motion of an isolated plasma in an electro-magnetic field with magnetic diffusion.

Energy conservation/dissapation

The free-boundary compressible resistive MHD system together with boundary conditions (
1.3
) satisfies the following energy conservation/dissapation: Set
Q(\rho)=\int_{1}^{\rho}p(R)/R^{2}dR
, then we use (
1.1
) to get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho|u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho Q(\rho)\,dx\right)

(1.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho u\cdot D_{t}u\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}B\cdot D_{t}B\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho D_{t}Q(\rho)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho D_{t}(1/\rho)|B|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}u\cdot(B\cdot\nabla B)\,dx-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}u\cdot\nabla P\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}B\cdot(B\cdot\nabla u)\,dx-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\text{div }u\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}p(\rho)\frac{D_{t}\rho}{\rho}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\frac{D_{t}\rho}{\rho}|B|^{2}\,dx.

Integrating by part in the first term in the last equality, this term will cancel with
\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}B\cdot(B\cdot\partial u)\,dx
because the boundary term and the other interior term vanishes due to
B\cdot n=0
and
\text{div }B=0
respectively. Here
n
is the exterior unit normal vector to
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}
. Also we integrate by parts in the second term and then use the continuity equation to get

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}u\cdot\nabla P\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}P\text{div }u\,dx-\underbrace{\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}}(u\cdot N)PdS}_{=0}=-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}p\frac{D_{t}\rho}{\rho}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\text{div }u\,dx

(1.7)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}p\frac{D_{t}\rho}{\rho}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\text{div }u\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\text{div }u\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}p\frac{D_{t}\rho}{\rho}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\text{div }u\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\frac{D_{t}\rho}{\rho}|B|^{2}\,dx.

Summing up (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), one can get the energy conservation for the free-boundary ideal compressible MHD:

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho|u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho Q(\rho)\,dx\right)=0.

(1.8)

Also one can see this energy conservation coincides with the analogue for the free-boundary compressible Euler’s equations in Lindblad-Luo
[
36
]
.

For the resistive compressible MHD as stated in (
1.1
), there will be one extra dissipation term, and one can integrate by part to get the energy dissipation.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho|u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\rho Q(\rho)\,dx\right)

(1.9)

\displaystyle=0+\lambda\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}B\cdot\Delta B\,dx=-\lambda\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|\nabla B|^{2}\,dx<0.

Equation of state: Isentropic liquid

Since
p=p(\rho)
and
p|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}=0
, we know the fluid density also has to be a constant
\bar{\rho_{0}}\geq 0
on the boundary. We assume
\bar{\rho_{0}}>0
, corresponding to the case of liquid as opposed to gas. Hence

p(\bar{\rho_{0}})=0,~~p^{\prime}(\rho)>0,~~\text{for }\rho\geq\bar{\rho_{0}}.

(1.10)

Physical constraints

Next we impose the following natural conditions on
\rho^{\prime}(p)
for some fixed constant
A_{0}>1
. See also Luo
[
37
]
.

A_{0}^{-1}\leq|\rho^{(m)}(p)|\leq A_{0},\text{ and }~A_{0}^{-1}|\rho^{\prime}(p)|^{m}\leq|\rho^{(m)}(p)|\leq A_{0}|\rho^{\prime}(p)|^{m},~~for~1\leq m\leq 5.

(1.11)

We also need to impose the Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition

-\nabla_{n}P\geq c_{0}>0~~on~\partial\mathcal{D}_{t},

(1.12)

where ,
c_{0}>0
is a constant and
P:=p+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}
is the total pressure. When
B=0
, Ebin
[
15
]
proved the ill-posedness of the free-boundary incompressible Euler equations without Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition. For the free-boundary MHD equations, (
1.12
) is also necessary: Hao-Luo
[
25
]
proved that the free-boundary problem of 2D incompressible MHD equations is ill-posed when (
1.12
) fails. We also note that (
1.12
) is only required for initial data and it propagates in a short time interval because one can prove it is
C_{t,x}^{0,1/4}
Hölder continuous by using Morrey’s embedding. See Luo-Zhang
[
38
]
and Zhang
[
65
]
.

Compatibility conditions on initial data

To make the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) be well-posed, the initial data has to satisfy certain compatibility conditions on the boundary. In fact, the continuity equation implies that
\text{div }v|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}=0
and thus we have to require
p_{0}|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{0}}
=0 and
\text{div }v_{0}|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{0}}
=0. Also the constraints on the magnetic field
\text{div }B=0
and
B|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}=\mathbf{0}
requires that
\text{div }B_{0}=0
and
B_{0}|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{0}}=\mathbf{0}
. Furthermore, we define the
k
-th(
k\geq 0
) order compatibility condition as follows:

D_{t}^{j}p|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{0}}=0,~~D_{t}^{j}B|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{0}}=\mathbf{0}~~\text{at time }t=0~~\forall 0\leq j\leq k.

(1.13)

Such initial data has been constructed in the author’s previous paper
[
65
]
.

In this manuscript, we prove the local well-posedness of the free-boundary compressible resistive MHD system under Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition: System (
1.1
) with initial-boundary condition (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) and physical conditions (
1.10
)-(
1.12
).

Free-boundary MHD equations: Compressible case

The study of free-boundary compressible MHD is even more delicate due to the extra coupling between pressure wave and magnetic field. For compressible ideal MHD (
\lambda=0
), there is one derivative loss in the curl estimates, and thus a loss of normal derivative. We note that analogous loss does not appear in tangential estimates. The reason is that taking curl eliminates the symmetry enjoyed by the equations. Briefly speaking, one can recall the derivation of energy conservation that the term
-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}|B|^{2}\text{div }u
is cancelled by part of
-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}u\cdot\nabla P
. But taking the curl eliminates the counterpart of
-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}u\cdot\nabla P

before
such cancellation is produced because of
\text{curl }\nabla P=0
. On the other hand, if we taking tangential derivatives instead of curl, then analogous cancellation is still preserved. We recommend readers to read Section 1.5 in the author’s previous work
[
65
]
for detailed discussion.

Before further discussion on the free-boundary problem in compressible MHD, let us first review the results for the fixed-domain problems. The compressible ideal MHD system in a fixed domain is a quasi-linear symmetric hyperbolic system with characteristic boundary conditions
[
47
]
. The loss of normal derivatives seems necessary, especially when the uniform Lopatinskii condition fails for the linearized counterpart. On the one hand, Ohno-Shirota
[
44
]
constructed explicit counter-examples to the local existence in standard Sobolev space
H^{l}(l\geq 2)
for the linearized problem. On the other hand, Chen Shu-Xing
[
5
]
first introduced the anisotropic Sobolev spaces
H_{*}^{m}
to compensate the normal derivative loss for characteristic boundary problems. With the help of such funtional spaces, Yanagisawa-Matsumura
[
64
]
established the first LWP result for compressible ideal MHD. Later, the LWP result was improved by Secchi
[
46
,
47
]
such that there is no regularity loss from initial data to solution, both are in
H_{*}^{m}
for
m\geq 16
. However, it is still difficult to generalized Secchi’s results to free-boundary case due to the extra derivative loss brought by free boundary.

Although there is derivative loss even for the linearized problem due to the failure of uniform Lopatinskii condition, the tame estimates, which allows a fixed order loss of derivatives, can still be established in anisotropic Sobolev space. Recently, Trakhinin-Wang
[
57
]
proved the LWP for free-boundary compressible ideal MHD under Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition by using such method and Nash-Moser iteration. However, a suitable energy estimates without loss of regularity cannot be established by Nash-Moser iteration. To resolve such problem, we observe that the magnetic resistivity exactly compensates the derivative loss mentioned above. With the help of this observation, the author
[
65
]
recently proved the Christodoulou-Lindblad type a priori estimates in standard Sobolev spaces and incompressible limit for the free-boundary compressible resistive MHD system. As a contiuation, the presenting manuscript deals with the local well-posedness of this problem. Note that it is still quite non-trivial to pass from a priori bound to local existence for a free-boundary problem: Direct iteration and fixed-point argument often fails due to the lack of regularity of free boundary. Once the local well-posedness is established, the next possible goal is to consider the vanishing resistivity limit, though it seems quite difficult now.

As for the full plasma-vacuum model in compressible ideal MHD, Secchi-Trakhinin
[
48
]
proved the LWP under the non-collinearity condition
|B\times\hat{B}|\geq c_{0}>0
. The study of plasma-vacuum model in compressible ideal MHD under Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition is still open. See Trakhinin
[
56
]
for more detailed discussion.

1.3
Reformulation in Lagrangian coordinates

We use Lagrangian coordinates to reduce free-boundary problem to a fixed domain. We assume
\Omega:=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(0,1)
to be the reference doamin. The coordinates on
\Omega
is
y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
. We define
\eta:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathcal{D}
as the flow map of velocity field
u
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta(t,y)=u(t,\eta(t,y)),\quad\eta(0,y)=y.

(1.14)

By chain rule, it is easy to see that
D_{t}
becomes
\partial_{t}
in the
(t,y)
coordinates and the free-boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}
becomes fixed (
\Gamma=\mathbb{T}^{2}
). We introduce the Lagrangian varaibles as follows:
v(t,y):=u(t,\eta(t,y))
,
b(t,y):=B(t,\eta(t,y))
,
q(t,y):=Q(t,\eta(t,y))
, and
R(t,y):=\rho(t,\eta(t,y))
.

Let
\partial=\partial_{y}
be the spatial derivative in the Lagrangian coordinates. We introduce the cofactor matrix
a=[\partial\eta]^{-1}
, specifically
a^{\mu\alpha}=a^{\mu}_{\alpha}:=\frac{\partial y^{\mu}}{\partial\eta^{\alpha}}
. In terms of
\eta,v,b,q,R
, the system (
1.1
)-(
1.12
) becomes

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta=v&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
R\partial_{t}v=\left(b\cdot\nabla_{a}\right)b-\nabla_{a}Q&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
R^{\prime}(q)\partial_{t}q+R\text{div}\,_{a}v=0&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
\partial_{t}b-\lambda\Delta_{a}b=\left(b\cdot\nabla_{a}\right)v-b\text{div}\,_{a}v&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
\text{div}\,_{a}b=0&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
\partial_{t}|_{[0,T]\times\Gamma}\in T([0,T]\times\Gamma)\\
p=0,~~B=\mathbf{0}&~~~\text{on}\,[0,T]\times\Gamma\\
-\partial_{3}Q_{0}|_{\Gamma}\geq c_{0}>0\\
(\eta,v,b,q,R)=(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0},\rho_{0}).\end{cases}

(1.15)

Here,
\nabla_{a}^{\alpha}=a^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}
and
\text{div}\,_{a}X=\nabla_{a}\cdot X=a^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}X_{\alpha}
denote the covariant derivative and divergence in Lagrangian coordinates (or say Eulerian derivative/divergence). In the manuscript, we adopt the convention that the Greek indices range over
1,2,3
, and the Latin indices range over
1
and
2
. In addition, since
\eta(0,\cdot)=\text{Id}
, we have
a(0,\cdot)=I
, where
I
is the identity matrix, and
u_{0},B_{0},p_{0}
and
v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}
agree respectively. Furthermore, let
J:=\det[\partial\eta]
and
A:=Ja
. Then we have the Piola’s identity

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu\alpha}=0

(1.16)

And
J
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}J=Ja^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}v_{\alpha},

(1.17)

which together with
R^{\prime}(q)\partial_{t}q+R\text{div}\,_{a}v=0
gives that
\rho_{0}=RJ
. Therefore,
R
can be fully expressed in terms of
\eta
and
\rho_{0}
, and thus the initial-boundary value problem can be expressed as follows:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta=v&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
\rho_{0}J^{-1}\partial_{t}v=\left(b\cdot\nabla_{a}\right)b-\nabla_{a}Q&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
\frac{JR^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q+\text{div}\,_{a}v=0&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
\partial_{t}b-\lambda\Delta_{a}b=\left(b\cdot\nabla_{a}\right)v-b\text{div}\,_{a}v&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
\text{div}\,_{a}b=0&~~~\text{in}\,[0,T]\times\Omega\\
\partial_{t}|_{[0,T]\times\Gamma}\in T([0,T]\times\Gamma)\\
p=0,~~B=\mathbf{0}&~~~\text{on}\,[0,T]\times\Gamma\\
-\partial_{3}Q|_{\Gamma}\geq c_{0}>0&~~~\text{on}\,\{t=0\}\\
(\eta,v,b,q)=(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}).\end{cases}

(1.18)

Our result is

.

1.4
Strategy of the proof

1.4.1
Necessity of tangential smoothing

Since there have been results
[
65
]
on the a priori bound of this system, it is natural to consider the fixed point argument and Picard iteration to construct the solution to (
1.18
). Specifically, one starts with trivial solution
(\eta^{(0)},v^{(0)},b^{(0)},q^{(0)})=(\text{Id},\mathbf{0,0},0)
, and inductively define
(\eta^{(n+1)},v^{(n+1)},b^{(n+1)},q^{(n+1)})
by the following linearized system provided that
\{(\eta^{(k)},v^{(k)},b^{(k)},q^{(k)})\}_{0\leq k\leq n}
are given. (Note that the div-free condition for magnetic field is not needed in the nonlinear estimates.)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta^{(n+1)}=v^{(n+1)}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\rho_{0}}{J^{(n)}}\partial_{t}v^{(n+1)}=(b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{a^{(n)}})b^{(n+1)}-\nabla_{a^{(n)}}Q^{(n+1)},~~Q^{(n+1)}=q^{(n+1)}+\frac{1}{2}|b^{(n+1)}|^{2}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{J^{(n)}R^{\prime}(q^{(n)})}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q^{(n+1)}+\text{div }_{a^{(n)}}v^{(n+1)}=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b^{(n+1)}-\Delta_{a^{(n)}}b^{(n+1)}=(b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{a^{(n)}})v^{(n+1)}-b^{(n)}\text{div }_{a^{(n)}}v^{(n+1)},&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
q^{(n+1)}=0,~b^{(n+1)}=\mathbf{0}&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\\
(\eta^{(n+1)},v^{(n+1)},b^{(n+1)},q^{(n+1)})|_{\{t=0\}}=(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}).\end{cases}

(1.24)

Then we need to

•

Construct the solution to the linearized system (
1.24
).

•

Prove uniform-in-
n
a priori estimates of (
1.24
).

•

Prove
\{(\eta^{(n)},v^{(n)},b^{(n)},q^{(n)})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly.

The first step is usually done by standard fixed point argument. While in the second step, we need to invoke the elliptic estimates
[
6
]
Lemma
2.5
to control
\nabla_{a}b
and
\nabla_{a}q
which is necessary for us to construct suitable functional space in step 1:

\left\|\nabla_{a^{(n)}}b^{(n+1)}\right\|_{4}\lesssim P(\|\eta^{(n)}\|_{4})\left(\|\Delta_{a}b^{(n+1)}\|_{3}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\eta^{(n)}\|_{4}\|b\|_{4}\right).

We find that the
\eta
loses one tangential derivative, which motivates us to regularise the flow map in tangential directions. This method was first introduced by Coutand-Shkoller
[
8
,
10
]
and then widely used in numerous works on the study of free-boundary hydrodynamics. One can define
\tilde{\eta}:=\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}\eta
and replace
a
by
\tilde{a}:=[\partial\tilde{\eta}]^{-1}
where
\Lambda_{\kappa}
is the standard convolution mollifier in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since the region is
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(0,1)
, the boundary only appears in normal directions and thus the mollifier is always well-defined. After introducing the tangential smoothing, we need to investigate the following nonlinear
\kappa
-approximation system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta=v&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}v=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b-\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q,~~Q=q+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q+\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b-\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v,&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
q=0,~b=\mathbf{0}&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\\
-\partial_{3}Q|_{t=0}\geq c_{0}>0&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\\
(\eta,v,b,q)|_{\{t=0\}}=(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}).\end{cases}

(1.25)

Specifically, we need to

1.

Establish nonlinear a priori estimates uniform in
\kappa>0
.

2.

Construct the unique strong solution to the nonlinear
\kappa
-approximation system for each fixed
\kappa>0
.

1.4.2
A priori estimates of the smoothed approximation system

Magnetic field and Lorentz force: Elliptic estimates

The ideas of deriving the a priori estimates are quite different from ideal MHD case. For ideal MHD, the magnetic field can be written as
b=J^{-1}(b_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta
, which should be controlled together with the same derivatives of
v=\partial_{t}\eta
, and higher order terms are expected to vanish due to subtle cancellation. However, the magnetic resistivity yields a direct control of the Lorentz force
(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b
as a source term. First, we notice that
b
vanishes on the boundary and so does the Lorentz force. Therefore, Lemma
2.5
can be directly applied to
(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b
in the following way:

\left\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right\|_{4}\approx\left\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\right\|_{3}\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\left(\left\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\right\|_{3}\left\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right\|_{3}\right).

Then we invoke the heat equation of
b
to replace the Laplacian term by first order derivative and thus control the Lorentz force by lower order terms. Similar processes can be applied to the time derivatives of Lorentz force and the space-time derivatives of magnetic field
b
.

Remark
.

We can control
\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\|_{4}
by elliptic estimates as above, but it is NOT possible to control the
H^{5}
-norm of
b
even if one has the diffusion effect on the magnetic field. Indeed, if we use elliptic estimates to control
\|b\|_{5}
, then

\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}b\|_{4}\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{4})(\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b\|_{3}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{4}\|b\|_{4}),

where
\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{4}
is out of control. In other words, the regularity of the free surface is not enough to enhance the full spatial regularity of
b
.

Velocity and pressure: Div-curl and tangential estimates

The second and third equations of the nonlinear
\kappa
-system above present the following relations if we temporarily omit the coefficients
\rho_{0}J
and
R^{\prime}(q)
:

\partial\partial_{t}^{k}q\approx\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{k}q\approx-\partial_{t}^{k+1}v+\partial_{t}^{k}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b-\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right),

and

\partial\partial_{t}^{k}v\xrightarrow{\text{div }}\partial_{t}^{k}\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v+\text{curl}+\text{boundary}\approx\partial_{t}^{k+1}q+\text{curl}+\text{boundary}.

Again we note that, it is exactly the curl part that denies the possibility of an energy control in standard Soboelv spaces for compressible ideal MHD. However, for resistive MHD, the contribution of curl part can be directly controlled by taking
\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}
in the second equation of (
1.25
) because
\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q)=0
and
\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b
has been directly controlled. As for the boundary term, we are able to further reduce to interior tangential estimates and divergence estimates by using normal trace Lemma
2.2
. Since the terms containing magnetic field
b
can be reduced to lower order with the help of magnetic diffusion, the procedure above allows us to trade one spatial derivative by one time derivative:

\displaystyle\partial^{4}q\to\partial^{3}\partial_{t}v\xrightarrow{\text{div }}\partial^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}q\to\partial\partial_{t}^{3}v

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{div }}\partial_{t}^{4}q

\displaystyle\partial^{3}\partial_{t}q\to\partial^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}v\xrightarrow{\text{div }}\partial\partial_{t}^{3}q

\displaystyle\to\partial_{t}^{4}v,

which reduces to the estimates of full time derivatives.

In the tangential estimates, if the tangential derivatives
\mathfrak{D}^{4}
contain at least one time derivative, then the energy estimates can be derived by direct computation because
[\mathfrak{D}^{4},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}]\partial_{\mu}Q
can be controlled. However, when
\mathfrak{D}^{4}=\overline{\partial}{}^{4}
contains no time derivative, that commutator is out of control due to
\overline{\partial}{}^{4}\tilde{a}\approx\overline{\partial}{}^{r}\partial\tilde{\eta}\cdot\partial\tilde{\eta}
. In the study of Euler equations, one can integrate
\overline{\partial}{}^{1/2}
by parts and use the enhanced regularity
\|\eta\|_{9/2}
to control this commutator, but here the presence of magnetic field destroys such property. The reason is that MHD system does not preserve the irrotational assumption propagated from initial data, and thus it is not possible for the flow map to get enhanced regularity. Our method is to introduce the Alinhac good unknowns: For a function
f
, we define
\mathbf{f}:=\overline{\partial}{}^{4}f-\overline{\partial}{}^{4}\eta\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}f
to be the Alinhac good unknown of
f
when commuting
\overline{\partial}{}^{4}
with
\nabla_{\tilde{a}}
. Under this setting, we have that

\overline{\partial}{}^{4}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}f)=\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\mathbf{f}+\text{ controllable commutators}.

Such crucial fact was first observed by Alinhac
[
3
]
, and then widely used in the study of first-order quasilinear hyperbolic system. In the study of fluid mechanics, there are also many applications in the study of incompressible inviscid fluids, e.g.,
[
42
,
58
,
22
]
and so on, but not widely used in the compressible case
[
40
]
.

Now we apply
\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}
(instead of
\overline{\partial}{}^{4}
, will be explained later) to the second equation of (
1.25
) and define
\mathbf{V}=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}v-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}v
and
\mathbf{Q}=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}Q-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q
to be the Alinhac good unknowns for
v
and
Q=q+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}
. Then we have

\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}\mathbf{V}+\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\mathbf{Q}=\underbrace{\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)+[\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1},\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}]\partial_{t}v_{\alpha}+\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}v)+C(Q)}_{\mathbf{F}},

subject to

\mathbf{Q}=-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{3\beta}\partial_{3}Q~~~on~\Gamma.

and

\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\cdot\mathbf{V}=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v)-C^{\alpha}(v_{\alpha})~~~in~\Omega.

Taking
L^{2}
inner product with
\mathbf{V}
, and integrating by parts, the interior term is

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{Q}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v)\,dy\,dt.

Plugging
Q=q+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}
and
\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\approx-\partial_{t}q
into this integral, we are able to get an energy term

-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\left|\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}q\right|^{2}\,dy

plus controllable terms. The boundary term reads

I_{0}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\tilde{a}^{3\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\partial_{t}\eta_{\alpha}-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}v_{\alpha})\,dS\,dt.

The first term produces the Taylor sign term contributing to the boundary term in
E_{\kappa}(t)
after commuting a
\Lambda_{\kappa}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\tilde{a}^{3\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\partial_{t}\eta_{\alpha}\,dS\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q\left|\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\Lambda_{\kappa}\eta_{\alpha}\right|^{2}\,dS\bigg|^{T}_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q\tilde{a}^{3\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\Lambda_{\kappa}\eta_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{3\gamma}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}v_{\gamma}\tilde{a}^{i\alpha}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\Lambda_{\kappa}\eta_{\alpha}\,dS-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\tilde{a}^{3\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\gamma}\tilde{a}^{i\gamma}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}v_{\alpha}\,dS

When
\kappa=0
, the two terms in the second line automatically cancel, while for the smoothed version such structure no longer holds. However, inspired by a remarkable observation in Gu-Wang
[
22
]
, we notice that these two terms can be cancelled if we introduce a correction term

\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\tilde{a}^{3\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\psi\,dS,

where
\psi
is defined by

\begin{cases}\Delta\psi=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi=\overline{\Delta}{}^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\left(\overline{\Delta}{}\eta_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}v-\overline{\Delta}{}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}\eta_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}v\right)&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\end{cases}

(1.26)

where
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}
denotes the standard Littlewood-Paley projection in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
which removes the zero-frequency part.
\overline{\Delta}{}:=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
denotes the tangential Laplacian operator. Therefore, it suffices to replace
\partial_{t}\eta=v
by
\partial_{t}\eta=v+\psi
to produce such an extra cancellation. The structure of
\psi
also illustrates why we have to replace
\overline{\partial}{}^{r}
by
\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}
.

Common control of higher order wave and heat equation

In the previous estimates, we still need to control the
r
-th time derivatives of
\partial_{t}b,(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b,\partial_{t}q,\nabla_{\tilde{a}}q
, which exactly come from the energy functionals of
r
-th time-differentiated heat eqution of
b

\partial_{t}b-\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v

and wave equation of
q

\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}^{2}q-\Delta_{\tilde{a}}q=b\cdot\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b+w_{0},

where
w_{0}
consists of first order derivatives terms. It is easy to observe that the Laplacian term in RHS of wave equation can be replaced by terms of first-order derivative with the help of magnetic resistivity. In other words, the magnetic resistivity compensate the derivative loss in the wave equation of
q
. Therefore, standard energy estimates of heat and wave equations give the control of
H_{\kappa}(T)
and
W_{\kappa}(T)
. Note that
\partial_{t}
is tangential on the boundary. Integrating by parts does not produce any boundary term for the time differentiated equations. Therefore, we are able to close the a priori estimates.

1.4.3
Linearisation and Picard iteration

Now, it remains to prove the local existence of the solution to the nonlinear
\kappa
-approximation system. The proof is standard linearisation and Picard iteration argument. We start with the trivial solution
(\eta^{(0)},v^{(0)},b^{(0)},q^{(0)})=(\eta^{(1)},v^{(1)},b^{(1)},q^{(1)})=(\text{Id},0,0,0)
. Inductively, given
\{(\eta^{(k)},v^{(k)},b^{(k)},q^{(k)})\}_{0\leq k\leq n}
for some given
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we define
(\eta^{(n+1)},v^{(n+1)},b^{(n+1)},q^{(n+1)})
by linearisation around
a^{(n)}:=[\partial\eta^{(n)}]^{-1}
.

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta^{(n+1)}=v^{(n+1)}+\psi^{(n)}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\rho_{0}}{\tilde{J}^{(n)}}\partial_{t}v^{(n+1)}=(b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}})b^{(n+1)}-\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}Q^{(n+1)},~~Q^{(n+1)}=q^{(n+1)}+\frac{1}{2}|b^{(n+1)}|^{2}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\tilde{J}^{(n)}R^{\prime}(q^{(n)})}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q^{(n+1)}+\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}v^{(n+1)}=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b^{(n+1)}-\Delta_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}b^{(n+1)}=(b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}})v^{(n+1)}-b^{(n)}\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}v^{(n+1)},&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
q^{(n+1)}=0,~b^{(n+1)}=\mathbf{0}&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\\
(\eta^{(n+1)},v^{(n+1)},b^{(n+1)},q^{(n+1)})|_{\{t=0\}}=(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}).\end{cases}

(1.27)

Note that the directional derivative
(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})
should be given by the
n
-th solution instead of
(n+1)
-th, otherwise the linearisation is not complete and the construction of the solution to the linearized system is much more difficult in the fixed-point argument.

The construction of the solution to the linearized system needs the following function space in the fixed-point argument:

Define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X}^{r}}
by

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{r}}^{2}:=\sum_{m=0}^{r}\sum_{k+l=m}\left\|\partial_{t}^{k}\partial^{l}f\right\|_{0}^{2}

and a Banach space on
[0,T]\times\Omega

\mathbf{X}(M,T):=\left\{\left(\xi,w,h,\pi\right)\bigg|\left(\xi,w,h,\pi\right)\bigg|_{t=0}=\left(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}\right),\left\|\left(\xi,w,h,\pi\right)\right\|_{\mathbf{X}}\leq M\right\}

where

\left\|\left(\xi,w,h,\pi\right)\right\|_{\mathbf{X}}:=\left\|\left(\xi,\partial_{t}\xi,w,h,\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}}h,\pi,\partial_{t}\pi,\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}}\pi\right)\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\mathbf{X}^{4}}+\|\partial_{t}^{5}h\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}},

and
\mathring{a}
denotes
a^{(n)}
. Here
(\xi,w,h,\pi)
are the corresponding variables of
(\eta,v,b,q)
.

We notice that, unlike the nonlinear estimates, the
H^{4}
-norm of
\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}}\pi
is necessary because one has to use
v(T)=v_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}v
to bound the
H^{4}
-norm of
v
. Such term should be estimated by means of elliptic estimates Lemma
2.5

\|\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}}q\|_{4}\lesssim P(\|\mathring{\tilde{{\eta}}}\|_{4})(\|\Delta_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}}q\|_{3}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\mathring{\tilde{{\eta}}}\|_{4}\|q\|_{4}).

With the help of tangential smoothing, we are able to control
\|\overline{\partial}{}\mathring{\tilde{{\eta}}}\|_{4}
by sacrificing a
\kappa^{-1}
: This is not allowed in the nonlinear estimates due to the
\kappa
-independence requirement, however, in the linearisation part
\kappa>0
is fixed. Therefore, mimicing the previous proof of nonlinear a priori estimates, we are able to

•

Prove the local well-posedness of the solution to the linearised system (
1.27
)

•

Establish uniform-in-
n
estimates of (
1.27
)

•

Finish the iteration scheme to produce the solution to nonlinear
\kappa
-approximation system.

This finalizes the whole proof.

As stated in the introduction, we are going to use the tangential smoothing to construct the approximate solutions. Here we list the definition and basic properties which are repeatedly used in this paper. Let
\zeta=\zeta(y_{1},y_{2})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a standard cut-off function such that
\text{Spt }\zeta=\overline{B(0,1)}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2},~~0\leq\zeta\leq 1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\zeta=1
. The corresponding dilation is

\zeta_{\kappa}(y_{1},y_{2})=\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\zeta\left(\frac{y_{1}}{\kappa},\frac{y_{2}}{\kappa}\right),~~\kappa>0.

Now we define

\Lambda_{\kappa}f(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\zeta_{\kappa}(y_{1}-z_{1},y_{2}-z_{2})f(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\,dz_{1}\,dz_{2}.

(2.2)

The following lemma records the basic properties of tangential smoothing.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Regularity and Commutator estimates)
For
\kappa>0
, we have

(1) The following regularity estimates:

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{\kappa}f\|_{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{s},~~\forall s\geq 0;

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\kappa}f|_{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|_{s},~~\forall s\geq-0.5;

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\overline{\partial}{}\Lambda_{\kappa}f|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa^{-s}|f|_{1-s},~~\forall s\in[0,1];

(2.5)

\displaystyle|f-\Lambda_{\kappa}f|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\kappa}|\overline{\partial}{}f|_{1/2}.

(2.6)

(2) Commutator estimates: Define the commutator
[\Lambda_{\kappa},f]g:=\Lambda_{\kappa}(fg)-f\Lambda_{\kappa}(g)
. Then it satisfies

\displaystyle|[\Lambda_{\kappa},f]g|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|_{L^{\infty}}|g|_{0},

(2.7)

\displaystyle|[\Lambda_{\kappa},f]\overline{\partial}{}g|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|_{W^{1,\infty}}|g|_{0},

(2.8)

\displaystyle|[\Lambda_{\kappa},f]\overline{\partial}{}g|_{0.5}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|_{W^{1,\infty}}|g|_{0.5}.

(2.9)

Proof.

(1): The estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) follows directly from the definition (
2.2
) and the basic properties of convolution. (
2.6
) is derived by using Sobolev embedding and Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle|f-\Lambda_{\kappa}f|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\cap B(0,\kappa)}\zeta_{\kappa}(z)(f(y-z)-f(y))\,dz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\zeta_{\kappa}|_{L^{4/3}}|\kappa\overline{\partial}{}f|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\kappa}|\zeta|_{L^{4/3}}|f|_{1.5}.

(2): The first three estimates can be found in Lemma 5.1 in Coutand-Shkoller
[
9
]
. To prove the fourth one, we note that

\overline{\partial}{}([\Lambda_{\kappa},f]g)=\Lambda_{\kappa}(\overline{\partial}{}f\overline{\partial}{}g)+\Lambda_{\kappa}(f\overline{\partial}{}^{2}g)-\overline{\partial}{}f\Lambda_{\kappa}\overline{\partial}{}g-f\Lambda_{\kappa}\overline{\partial}{}^{2}g=[\Lambda_{\kappa},\overline{\partial}{}f]\overline{\partial}{}g+[\Lambda_{\kappa},f]\overline{\partial}{}^{2}g.

From (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) we know

|\overline{\partial}{}[\Lambda_{\kappa},f]g|_{0}\lesssim|\overline{\partial}{}f|_{L^{\infty}}|\overline{\partial}{}g|_{0}+|f|_{W^{1,\infty}}|\overline{\partial}{}g|_{0}\lesssim|f|_{W^{1,\infty}}|g|_{1}.

(2.10)

Therefore (
2.9
) follows from the interpolation of (
2.8
) and (
2.10
).
∎

3
A priori estimates of the nonlinear approximation system

For
\kappa>0
, we introduce the nonlinear
\kappa
-approximation system.

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta=v+\psi&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}v=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b-\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q,~~Q=q+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q+\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b-\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v,&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
q=0,~b=\mathbf{0}&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\\
(\eta,v,b,q)|_{\{t=0\}}=(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}).\end{cases}

(3.1)

Here,
\Omega:=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(0,1)
is the reference domain,
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(\{0\}\cup\{1\})
is the boundary. The notation
\tilde{\eta}
is the smoothed version of the flow map
\eta
defined by
\tilde{\eta}:=\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}\eta
,
\tilde{a}:=(\partial\tilde{\eta})^{-1}
is the cofactor matrix of
[\partial\tilde{\eta}]
, and
\tilde{J}:=\det[\partial\tilde{\eta}]
is the Jacobian determinant. The term
\psi=\psi(\eta,v)
is a correction term which solves the Laplacian equation

\begin{cases}\Delta\psi=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi=\overline{\Delta}{}^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\left(\overline{\Delta}{}\eta_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}v-\overline{\Delta}{}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}\eta_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}v\right)&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\end{cases}

(3.2)

where
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}
denotes the standard Littlewood-Paley projection in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
which removes the zero-frequency part.
\overline{\Delta}{}:=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
denotes the tangential Laplacian operator.

Remark
.

1.

The correction term
\psi\to 0
as
\kappa\to 0
. We introduce such a term to eliminate the higher order boundary terms which appears in the tangential estimates of
v
. These higher order boundary terms are zero when
\kappa=0
but are out of control when
\kappa>0
.

2.

The Littlewood-Paley projection is necessary here because we will repeatedly use

|\overline{\Delta}{}^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}f|_{s}\approx|\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}f|_{{H}^{s-2}}\approx|f|_{\dot{H}^{s-2}},

which can be proved by using Bernstein inequality.

3.

The initial data is the same of origin system because the compatibility conditions stay unchanged after mollification by
\tilde{a}(0)=a(0)=\text{Id}
. Such initial data has been constructed in the author’s previous paper
[
65
]
, so these steps are omitted in this manuscript.

4.

The precise form of the commutators in the remaining context can be found in Section 4.4 in the author’s previous work
[
65
]
. Details are omitted in the presenting manuscript.

Now, we define the energy functional of (
3.1
)

\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}(T):=E_{\kappa}(T)+H_{\kappa}(T)+W_{\kappa}(T)+\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}\left(\left(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\right)b\right)\right\|_{k}^{2},

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}(T):=\left\|\eta\right\|_{4}^{2}+\left|\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{4}\Lambda_{\kappa}\eta_{\alpha}\right|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{4}\left(\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}b\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}q\right\|_{k}^{2}\right),

(3.4)

\displaystyle H_{\kappa}(T)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{t}^{5}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\left\|\partial_{t}^{4}b\right\|_{1}^{2},

(3.5)

\displaystyle W_{\kappa}(T)

\displaystyle:=\left\|\partial_{t}^{5}q\right\|_{0}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{4}q\right\|_{1}^{2}

(3.6)

denote the energy functional of fluid, higher order heat equation of
b
, and wave equation of
q
, respectively.

The context of this section is the uniform-in-
\kappa
a priori estimates of (
3.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists some
T>0
independent of
\kappa
, such that the energy functional
E_{\kappa}
satisfies

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}(t)\leq P(\|v_{0}\|_{4},\|b_{0}\|_{5},\|q_{0}\|_{4},\|\rho_{0}\|_{4}),

(3.7)

provided the following assumptions hold for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle-\partial_{3}Q(t)\geq c_{0}/2~~~

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{J}(t)-1\|_{3}+\|\text{Id}-\tilde{a}(t)\|_{3}\leq\epsilon~~~

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega.

(3.9)

Remark
.

The a priori assumptions can be easily justified once the energy bounds are established by using
\tilde{a}(T)-\text{Id}=\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}\tilde{a}=\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{a}:\partial_{t}\partial\tilde{\eta}:\tilde{a}\,dt
and the smallness of

4.2
.

In Section

, we will prove the local well-posedness of (
3.1

\kappa

. Therefore, the uniform-in-
\kappa

to (

in this manuscript.

3.2
Estimates of the magnetic field

For ideal MHD, the magnetic field can be written as
b=J^{-1}(b_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta
, which should be controlled together with the same derivatives of
v=\partial_{t}\eta
, and higher order terms are expected to vanish due to subtle cancellation. But for resistive MHD, the magnetic diffusion, together with the boundary condition
b=\mathbf{0}
, allows us to control the higher order term
\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b
directly, and
(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b
(Lorentz force) with the help of Christodoulou-Lindblad type elliptic estimates Lemma
2.5
.

3.2.1
Control of
\partial_{t}^{k}b
when
k\leq 2

First we estimate
\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}b\|_{k}
. Notice that, when
k\geq 1
, we have

\partial^{k-1}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{4-k}b=\partial^{k-1}(\tilde{a}^{\mu}_{\alpha}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{t}^{4-k}b)+\partial^{k-1}((\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}-\tilde{a}^{\mu}_{\alpha})\partial_{\mu}\partial_{t}^{4-k}b),

which gives

\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}b\|_{k}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}b\|_{k-1}^{2}+\|\text{Id}-\tilde{a}\|_{k-1}^{2}\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}b\|_{k}^{2}\leq\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}b\|_{k-1}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}b\|_{k}^{2}.

Here the
\epsilon
-term can be absorbed into the LHS. When
k=1,2
,
\|\text{Id}-\tilde{a}\|_{k-1}
should be replaced by
\|\text{Id}-\tilde{a}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Therefore, we have that for
1\leq k\leq 4
,
\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}b\|_{k}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}(\partial_{t}^{4-k}b)\|_{k-1}
, which motivates us to use Christodoulou-Lindblad elliptic estimates Lemma
2.5
.

Applying Lemma
2.5
to
b
, we have

\|b\|_{4}\approx\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}b\|_{3}\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})(\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b\|_{2}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\|b\|_{3})

(3.33)

Invoking the heat equation
\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b=\partial_{t}b-(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v+b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v
, we have

\displaystyle\|b\|_{4}\lesssim

\displaystyle P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\left(\|\partial_{t}b\|_{2}+\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v\|_{2}+\|b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\|_{2}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\|b\|_{3}\right)

(3.34)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})P\left(\|\partial_{t}b\|_{2},\|b\|_{2},\|u\|_{3}\right)+P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\|b\|_{3}

The first term
P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})P\left(\|\partial_{t}b\|_{2},\|b\|_{2},\|u\|_{3}\right)
can be directly controlled by
\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt
. For the second term, we notice that
\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{0}+\|\partial^{3}\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{0}
, and
\overline{\partial}{}_{i}\eta_{\alpha}|_{t=0}=\delta_{i\alpha}
,
\partial^{3}\overline{\partial}{}\eta|_{t=0}=0
, so

\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\,dt.

Plugging this into the second term
\left(\|\overline{\partial}{}\eta\|_{0}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\partial\tilde{\eta}\|_{2}\right)P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\|b\|_{3}
, we know

\displaystyle\left(\|\overline{\partial}{}\eta\|_{0}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\partial\tilde{\eta}\|_{2}\right)P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\|b\|_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\|b\|_{3}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\,dt+P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\left(\|b_{0}\|_{3}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}b\|_{3}\,dt\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(t))\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt.

Therefore, (
3.34
) becomes

\|b\|_{4}\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(t))\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt

(3.35)

Since
\partial_{t}
is tangential on
\Gamma
,
\partial_{t}b
also vanishes on the boundary. Applying elliptic estimates as in (
3.33
), we get

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}b\|_{3}

\displaystyle\approx\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}b\|_{2}\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{2})\left(\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}b\|_{1}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}b\|_{2}\right)

(3.36)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{2}b\|_{2}

\displaystyle\approx\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{2}b\|_{1}\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{2})\left(\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{2}b\|_{0}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}^{2}b\|_{1}\right).

(3.37)

Taking time derivatives in the heat equation of
b
, we have

\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{k}b=\partial_{t}^{k+1}b-\partial_{t}^{k}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\right),

of which the RHS is of one less derivative than LHS. Therefore, we are able to control
\|\partial_{t}b\|_{3},\|\partial_{t}^{2}b\|_{2}
in the same way as (
3.35
):

\|\partial_{t}b\|_{3}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}b\|_{2}\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(t))\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt

(3.38)

3.2.2
Control of
\partial_{t}^{3}b
: Heat equation

Note that
\|\partial_{t}^{3}b\|_{1}\approx\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\|_{0}
is a part of the energy of 3-rd order time-differentiated heat equation

\partial_{t}^{4}b-\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b=\partial_{t}^{3}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v)-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\right)+[\partial_{t}^{3},\Delta_{\tilde{a}}]b,

of which the RHS only contain terms with
\leq 4
derivatives.

Taking
L^{2}
inner product with
\tilde{J}\partial_{t}^{4}b
, integrating in
y\in\Omega
and
t\in[0,T]
, and then integrating by parts, one has

\displaystyle LHS=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left|\partial_{t}^{4}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{4}b\cdot\tilde{J}\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left|\partial_{t}^{4}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4}b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left|\partial_{t}^{4}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\right|^{2}\,dy\bigg|^{T}_{0}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\tilde{J}\left|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left[\nabla_{\tilde{a}},\partial_{t}\right]\partial_{t}^{3}b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle RHS=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{4}b\cdot\left(\partial_{t}^{3}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v)-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\right)+[\partial_{t}^{3},\Delta_{\tilde{a}}]b\right)\,dy\,dt

Therefore, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left|\partial_{t}^{4}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b(T)\right|^{2}\,dy

(3.39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b(0)\right|^{2}\,dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\tilde{J}\left|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{J}\left[\nabla_{\tilde{a}},\partial_{t}\right]\partial_{t}^{3}b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{4}b\cdot\left(\partial_{t}^{3}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v)-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\right)+[\partial_{t}^{3},\Delta_{\tilde{a}}]b\right)\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt,

which gives the
H^{1}
control of
\partial_{t}^{3}b
.

3.2.3
Control of
\partial_{t}^{4}b
: Higher order estimates needed

There are two ways to control
\|\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}
. One way is to use Poincaré’s inequality

\|\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{1}\approx\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}

(3.40)

due to
\partial_{t}^{4}b|_{\Gamma}=0

Another way is direct computation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}^{4}b(0)\|_{0}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}^{4}b\cdot\partial_{t}^{5}b\,dt

(3.41)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{5}b\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4}b\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}+\epsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{t}^{5}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{t}^{4}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{t}^{5}b\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt.

From (
3.40
) and (
3.41
), we find that either
\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}^{2}
or
\|\partial_{t}^{5}b\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}
is required to control
\|\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}^{2}
. On the other hand, we notice that these two terms exactly come from the energy functional of 4-th time-differentiated heat equation of
b
:

\partial_{t}^{5}b-\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4}b=\left[\partial_{t}^{4},\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\right]b+\partial_{t}^{4}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\right).

The energy estimate cannot be controlled in the same way as in Section
3.2.2
because the RHS of this heat equation contains 5-th order derivatives. Instead, we will seek for a common control of
b
and
p
via the heat equation and wave equation. This part will be postponed to Section
3.4
.

3.2.4
Estimates of Lorentz force

Later on we will see both the estimates of
u
and common control of higher order heat and wave equations require the control of 5-th derivatives of magnetic field, all of which are actually 4-th space-time derivatives of Lorentz force
(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b
. Notice that
b=0
on the boundary implies
(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b
also vanishes on
\Gamma
. Therefore, we can apply the elliptic estimate Lemma
2.5
to
(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b
.

We start with
\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\|_{4}
. Similarly as in (
3.33
), we have

\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\|_{4}\approx\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{3}\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\left(\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{2}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\|_{3}\right)

(3.42)

The second term
P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{3})\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\|_{3}
can again be controlled by
\mathcal{P}_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(T))\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt
by writting
\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{3}
as in (
3.35
). For the first term, we invoke the heat equation of
b
to get

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)=

\displaystyle(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})\left(\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b\right)+[\Delta_{\tilde{a}},b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}]b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})\left(\partial_{t}b-(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v+b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\right)+[\Delta_{\tilde{a}},b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}]b,

of which the RHS only contains terms with
\leq 2
derivatives. So we have

\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)\|_{2}\lesssim P(E_{\kappa}(T)),

and thus

\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\|_{4}\lesssim P(E_{\kappa}(T))+\mathcal{P}_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(T))\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt.

(3.43)

When
k=1,2
,
\|\partial_{t}^{k}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{4-k}
can be controlled in the same way as (
3.36
), (
3.37
) and (
3.38
):

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{3}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{2}

(3.44)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{2})\left(\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{1}+\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{2}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{2})\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{2}\left(\|\partial_{t}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{2})\left(\|\partial_{t}(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})(\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b)\|_{1}+\|[\Delta_{\tilde{a}},\partial_{t}(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})]b\|_{1}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})\Delta_{\tilde{a}}b\|_{0}+\|[\Delta_{\tilde{a}},\partial_{t}^{2}(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})]b\|_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{P}_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(T))\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle P(E_{\kappa}(T))+\mathcal{P}_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(T))\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt.

When
k=3
, we have
\partial(\partial_{t}^{3}(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})\partial\partial_{t}^{3}b+[\partial\partial_{t}^{3},b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}]b
, where the commutator only contains the terms of
\leq 4
-th order derivative, so

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{3}(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\|_{1}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})\partial\partial_{t}^{3}b\|_{0}+\|[\partial\partial_{t}^{3},b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}]b\|_{0}

(3.45)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|b\|_{2}\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\|_{1}+P(E_{\kappa}(T))

Then by elliptic estimates Lemma
2.5
and the heat equation,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\|_{1}\lesssim

\displaystyle P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{2})(\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}b\|_{0}+\|\overline{\partial}{}\tilde{\eta}\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}^{3}b\|_{1})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{2})\left(\|\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}+\|\partial_{t}^{3}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})v-b\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v)\|_{0}+\|[\Delta_{\tilde{a}},\partial_{t}^{3}]b\|_{0}\right)+P(E_{\kappa}(T))

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle P(E_{\kappa}(T))

Similarly, for
k=4
, we have
\partial_{t}^{4}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})\partial_{t}^{4}b+[\partial_{t}^{4},b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}]b
, where the commutator only contains the terms of
\leq 4
-th order derivative, so

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{4}(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\|_{0}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}+\|[\partial_{t}^{4},b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}]b\|_{0}

(3.46)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|b\|_{2}\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(T)),

where the term
\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4}b\|_{0}^{2}
is exactly part of the energy functional
H_{\kappa}(T)
of 4-th time-differentiated heat equation.

Summing up (
3.43
), (
3.44
), (
3.45
) and (
3.46
), we get the estimates of Lorentz force

\sum_{k=0}^{4}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\right\|_{k}^{2}\lesssim\left\|b\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4}b\right\|_{0}^{2}+P(E_{\kappa}(T))+\mathcal{P}_{0}+P(E_{\kappa}(T))\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(T))\,dt

(3.47)

Therefore, we find that the estimates of Lorentz force are again reduced to the control of higher order heat equation.

3.3
Estimates of the velocity and the pressure

In this part we control the space-time Sobolev norm of
v
and
q
. We first apply the Hodge-type div-curl decomposition (Lemma
2.4
) to
v
(and its time derivatives). The curl part can be directly controlled by the counterpart of Lorentz force. The boundary term can be reduced to interior tangential estimates by using Sobolev trace Lemma. The divergence part together with the estimates of
q
can be reduced to the control of full time derivatives, which is also part of tangential estimates. One should keep in mind that, we no longer seek for subtle cancellation to eliminate higher order terms as what was done for ideal MHD, no matter in curl or tangential estimates. Instead, those higher order terms (with 5 derivatives) can be controlled either by Lorentz force, or by the combination of heat equation and wave equation, i.e.,
H_{\kappa}(T)
and
W_{\kappa}(T)
.

First we recall Lemma
2.4
:
\forall s\geq 1
and any sufficiently regular vector field
X
, we have

\|X\|_{s}\lesssim\|X\|_{0}+\|\text{div }X\|_{s-1}+\|\text{curl }X\|_{s-1}+|X\cdot N|_{s-1/2}.

Let
X=v,\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}^{2}v,\partial_{t}^{3}v
and
s=4,3,2,1
respectively. We have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{0}+\|\text{div }v\|_{3}+\|\text{curl }v\|_{3}+|v\cdot N|_{3.5}

(3.48)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v\|_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}v\|_{0}+\|\text{div }\partial_{t}v\|_{2}+\|\text{curl }\partial_{t}v\|_{2}+|\partial_{t}v\cdot N|_{2.5}

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{0}+\|\text{div }\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{1}+\|\text{curl }\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{1}+|\partial_{t}^{2}v\cdot N|_{1.5}

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{0}+\|\text{div }\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{0}+\|\text{curl }\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{0}+|\partial_{t}^{3}v\cdot N|_{0.5}.

First, the
L^{2}
-norms are of lower order. The
L^{2}
-norm of
v
has been controlled in the energy dissapation. While for
\|\partial_{t}v\|_{0},\|\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{0}
and
\|\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{0}
, we commute
\partial_{t}
through
\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}v=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b-\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q
and obtain

\|\partial_{t}v(T)\|_{0}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}v(T)\|_{0}+\|\partial_{t}^{3}v(T)\|_{0}\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt

(3.49)

3.3.1
Boundary estimates: Reduced to tangential estimates

The boundary part of div-curl decomposition can be reduced to the interior tangential estimates by invoking the normal trace Lemma
2.2

|\overline{\partial}{}v^{3}|_{2.5}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}{}^{4}v\|_{0}+\|\text{div }v\|_{3}.

(3.50)

Similarly we have for
1\leq k\leq 3

|\partial_{t}^{k}v^{3}|_{3.5-k}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}{}^{4-k}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{0}+\|\text{div }\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{3-k}

(3.51)

3.3.2
Curl control: Reduced to Lorentz force

By the a priori assumption (
3.9
), we can estimate the Lagrangian vorticity via Eulerian vorticity plus a small error, for
1\leq k\leq 4

\|\text{curl }\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\|_{k-1}^{2}\lesssim\|\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\|_{k-1}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\|_{k}^{2}

(3.52)

Taking
\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}
in
\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}v=(b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b-\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q
, we have

\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}v=\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)+\left[\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t},\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\right]v,

(3.53)

where the commutator only contains first order derivative of
v,\rho,\partial_{t}\eta
.

Taking derivative
\partial_{t}^{4-k}\partial^{k-1}
in (
3.53
), we get the differentiated equation of
\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}v
:

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle(\partial^{k-1}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}v)=\partial_{t}^{4-k}\partial^{k-1}\text{curl }((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)

(3.54)

\displaystyle\underbrace{+\partial_{t}^{4-k}\partial^{k-1}\left(\left[\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t},\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\right]v\right)+[\partial_{t}^{4-k}\partial^{k-1},\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}]\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}v+\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}\partial^{k-1}([\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}},\partial_{t}^{4-k}]v)}_{F_{k}}

then taking
L^{2}
-inner product with
\partial^{k-1}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}v
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\left|\partial^{k-1}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}v(T)\right|^{2}\,dy-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\left|\partial^{k-1}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}v(0)\right|^{2}\,dy

(3.55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\left(\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\right)\left|\partial^{k-1}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial^{k-1}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\cdot\partial^{k-1}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b)\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial^{k-1}\text{curl}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\cdot F_{k}\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\left(\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\right\|_{k}^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\right\|_{k}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)\right\|_{k}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\right\|_{k}\left\|F_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon T\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)\right\|_{k}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt.

Here we used the fact that all terms in
F_{k}
are of
\leq 4
derivatives, and thus can be controlled by
P(E_{\kappa}(t))
.

3.3.3
Divergence Control: Reduction to full time derivatives

Before going into detailed proof, we briefly describe the procedure of such reduction. The second and third equations of (
3.1
) give us the following relations if we omit the coefficients
\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}
and
\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}
:

\partial\partial_{t}^{k}q\approx\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{k}q\approx-\partial_{t}^{k+1}v+\partial_{t}^{k}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b-\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right),

and

\partial\partial_{t}^{k}v\xrightarrow{\text{div }}\partial_{t}^{k}\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v+\text{curl}+\text{boundary}\sim\partial_{t}^{k+1}q+\text{curl}+\text{boundary}.

Since the terms containing magnetic field
b
can be reduced to lower order with the help of magnetic diffusion, the procedure above allows us to control
\text{div }v
by
\partial_{t}q
, and control
\partial q
by
\partial_{t}v
. In other words, we are able to trade one spatial derivative by one time derivative, and finally reduce the control to the full time derivative estimates. See the following

\displaystyle\partial^{4}q\xrightarrow{\eqref{nonlinearkk}}\partial^{3}\partial_{t}v\xrightarrow{\text{div }}\partial^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}q\xrightarrow{\eqref{nonlinearkk}}\partial\partial_{t}^{3}v

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{div }}\partial_{t}^{4}q

(3.56)

\displaystyle\partial^{3}\partial_{t}q\xrightarrow{\eqref{nonlinearkk}}\partial^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}v\xrightarrow{\text{div }}\partial\partial_{t}^{3}q

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\eqref{nonlinearkk}}\partial_{t}^{4}v.

Step 1: Reduce
q
to
\partial_{t}v

First we investigate
\|\partial_{t}^{3}q\|_{1}
. We take
\partial_{t}^{3}
in the second equation in (
3.1
) to get

\partial\partial_{t}^{3}q=\partial_{t}^{3}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}q)+\nabla_{I-\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}q=-\partial_{t}^{3}\left(\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}v\right)+\partial_{t}^{3}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\tilde{a}}|b|^{2}\right)+\nabla_{I-\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}q,

where we have
Therefore,
\partial_{t}^{3}q
is estimated as

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}^{3}q\right\|_{1}\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon\left\|\partial_{t}^{3}q\right\|_{1}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{3}(\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}v)\right\|_{0}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{3}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\tilde{a}}|b|^{2}\right)\right\|_{0}

(3.57)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon\left\|\partial_{t}^{3}q\right\|_{1}+\left\|\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4}v\right\|_{0}+\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt+L.O.T.,

where
\epsilon>0
can be chosen suitably small in order for being absorbed by LHS. The
\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt
comes from the magnetic field according to Section
3.2
.

Similarly as in the derivation of (
3.57
), we get the following estimates

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{2}q\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{1}+\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt+L.O.T.

(3.58)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}q\|_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{2}+\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt+L.O.T.

(3.59)

\displaystyle\|q\|_{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}v\|_{3}+\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt+L.O.T.

(3.60)

Step 2: Divergence estimates of
v

The Eulerian divergence is
\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}X=\text{div }X+(\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}-\delta^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}X_{\alpha}
, which together with (
3.9
) implies

\displaystyle\forall s>2.5

\displaystyle:~\|\text{div }X\|_{s-1}\lesssim\|\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}X\|_{s-1}+\|I-\tilde{a}\|_{s-1}\|X\|_{s}\lesssim\|\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}X\|_{s-1}+\epsilon\|X\|_{s}

(3.61)

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq s\leq 2.5

\displaystyle:~\|\text{div }X\|_{s-1}\lesssim\|\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}X\|_{s-1}+\|I-\tilde{a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|X\|_{s}\lesssim\|\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}X\|_{s-1}+\epsilon\|X\|_{s}.

The
\epsilon
-terms can be absorbed by
\|X\|_{s}
on LHS by choosing
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small. So it suffices to estimate the Eulerian divergence which satisfies
\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v=-\frac{R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q
. Taking time derivatives in this equation, we get

\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{k}v=-\partial_{t}^{k}\left(\frac{R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q\right)-[\partial_{t}^{k},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}]\partial_{\mu}v_{\alpha}\approx\frac{R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}^{k+1}q-[\partial_{t}^{k},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}]\partial_{\mu}v_{\alpha},~~k=0,1,2,3.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v\|_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}q\|_{3}+L.O.T.

(3.62)

\displaystyle\|\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}v\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}q\|_{2}+L.O.T.

\displaystyle\|\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}^{3}q\|_{1}+L.O.T.

\displaystyle\|\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}^{4}q\|_{0}+L.O.T.

Combining (
3.61
) and (
3.62
), by choosing
\epsilon>0
in (
3.61
) to be suitably small, we know the divergence estimates are all be reduced to one more time derivative of
q
:

\displaystyle\|\text{div }v\|_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|v\|_{4}+\|R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}q\|_{3}+L.O.T.

(3.63)

\displaystyle\|\text{div }\partial_{t}v\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|v_{t}\|_{3}+\|R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}q\|_{2}+L.O.T.

(3.64)

\displaystyle\|\text{div }\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|\partial_{t}^{2}v\|_{2}+\|R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}^{3}q\|_{1}+L.O.T.

(3.65)

\displaystyle\|\text{div }\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|\partial_{t}^{3}v\|_{1}+\|R^{\prime}(q)\tilde{J}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{t}^{4}q\|_{0}+L.O.T.

(3.66)

Combining (
3.57
)-(
3.60
). (
3.63
)-(
3.66
) with the previous analysis of curl and boundary estimates, the control of
\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}q\|_{k}
and
\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}v\|_{0}
are reduced to
\|\partial_{t}^{4}v\|_{0}
and
\|\partial_{t}^{4}q\|_{0}
together with the tangential estimates of
v
.

3.3.4
Tangential space-time derivative estimates

Denote
\mathfrak{D}=\overline{\partial}{}
or
\partial_{t}
. First we consider the case
\mathfrak{D}^{4}=\partial_{t}^{4},\partial_{t}^{3}\overline{\partial}{},\partial_{t}^{2}\overline{\partial}{}^{2},\partial_{t}\overline{\partial}{}^{3}
, i.e., there are at least one time derivative in the four tangential derivatives.

Direct computation gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\left|\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\right|^{2}\,dy\bigg|^{T}_{0}

(3.67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}(\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}v)\cdot\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}\left(\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\right)\left|\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathfrak{D}^{4},\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\right]\partial_{t}v\cdot\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\,dy\,dt}_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q)\cdot\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\,dy\,dt+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\,dy\,dt}_{L_{2}}+L_{1},

where
L_{1}
can be directtly bounded by
\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt
, and
L_{2}
can be controlled by the Lorentz force

L_{2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\right\|_{0}\left\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)\right\|_{0}\,dt\lesssim\epsilon\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)\right\|_{0}^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\right\|_{0}^{2}\,dt.

For the first term, we first commute
\mathfrak{D}^{4}
with
\nabla_{\tilde{a}}
, then integrate
\nabla_{\tilde{a}}
by parts to get

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q)\cdot\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\,dy\,dt

(3.68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\mathfrak{D}^{4}Q\cdot\mathfrak{D}^{4}v\,dy\,dt+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathfrak{D}^{4},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}\right]\partial_{\mu}Q\cdot\mathfrak{D}^{4}v_{\alpha}\,dy\,dt}_{L_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\underbrace{\mathfrak{D}^{4}Q}_{=0}\mathfrak{D}^{4}v_{\alpha}\,dS\,dt+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}Q\mathfrak{D}^{4}(\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v)\,dy\,dt}_{K_{1}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}Q\cdot\left[\mathfrak{D}^{4},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}\right]\partial_{\mu}v_{\alpha}\,dy\,dt}_{L_{4}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{\mu}\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}\mathfrak{D}^{4}Q\mathfrak{D}^{4}v_{\alpha}}_{L_{5}}.

Notice that,
\mathfrak{D}^{4}=\mathfrak{D}^{3}\partial_{t}
now contains at least one time derivative, and
\tilde{a}\sim\partial\tilde{\eta}\cdot\partial\tilde{\eta}
, so by the estimates correction term
\psi
, we know the
L^{2}
-norm of
\mathfrak{D}^{4}\tilde{a}\approx\mathfrak{D}^{3}\partial\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\partial\tilde{\eta}+L.O.T.
can be controlled by
P(E_{\kappa}(t))
, and thus
L_{3},L_{4}
can be controlled by
\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt
. The term
L_{5}
is also directly bounded by
\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt
.

Next we plug
\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v=-\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q
and
Q=q+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}
into
K_{1}
to get

\displaystyle K_{1}=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}q\mathfrak{D}^{4}\left(\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q\right)\,dy\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right)\mathfrak{D}^{4}\left(\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q\right)\,dy\,dt

(3.69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\left|\mathfrak{D}^{4}q(t)\right|^{2}\,dy\bigg|^{T}_{0}\underbrace{-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{D}^{4}Q\cdot\left[\mathfrak{D}^{4},\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\right]\partial_{t}q\,dy\,dt}_{L_{6}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\mathfrak{D}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right)\mathfrak{D}^{4}\partial_{t}q\,dy\,dt}_{K_{2}}.

From the computation above, we find that the energy term
\|\partial_{t}^{4}q\|_{0}^{2}
automatically appears if
\mathfrak{D}^{4}=\partial_{t}^{4}
. The commutator term
L_{6}
can be directly bounded by
\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt
. The term
K_{2}
satisfies

K_{2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right)\right\|_{0}\cdot\left\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}\partial_{t}q\right\|_{0}\,dt\lesssim\epsilon\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}\partial_{t}q\right\|_{0}^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt,

(3.70)

therefore we need the energy functional of 4-th time differentiated wave equation of
q
and elliptic estimates Lemma
2.5
to bound
K_{2}
. This will also be postponed to Section
3.4
.

3.3.5
Tangential spatial derivative estimates: Alinhac good unknowns

When
\mathfrak{D}^{4}=\overline{\partial}{}^{4}
, the above analysis no longer works due to
[\overline{\partial}{}^{4},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}]\partial_{\mu}f
being out of control. According to the discussion in Section 1.4, we introduce the Alinhac good unknowns. In specific, we replace
\overline{\partial}{}^{4}
by
\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}
due to the special structure of correction term
\psi
. Then for any function
f
and its corresponding Alinhac good unknown

\mathbf{f}:=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}f-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}f,

the following equality holds

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}f)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}f)+(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}f+[\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}f)-\overline{\partial}{}\overline{\Delta}{}(\tilde{a}^{\mu\gamma}\overline{\partial}{}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{\gamma}\tilde{a}^{\beta\alpha})\partial_{\mu}f+[\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}f)-\tilde{a}^{\beta\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\gamma}\tilde{a}^{\mu\gamma}\partial_{\mu}f-([\overline{\partial}{}\overline{\Delta}{},\tilde{a}^{\mu\gamma}\tilde{a}^{\beta\alpha}]\overline{\partial}{}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{\gamma})\partial_{\mu}f+[\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}f-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\eta_{\gamma}\tilde{a}^{\mu\gamma}\partial_{\mu}f)}_{=\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}}+\underbrace{\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\eta_{\gamma}\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\gamma}f)-([\overline{\partial}{}\overline{\Delta}{},\tilde{a}^{\mu\gamma}\tilde{a}^{\beta\alpha}]\overline{\partial}{}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{\gamma})\partial_{\mu}f+[\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]}_{=:C^{\alpha}(f)},

where
[\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta},g,h]:=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(gh)-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(g)h-g\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(h)
.
A direct computation yields that

\displaystyle\|\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\eta_{\gamma}\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\gamma}f)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{4}\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\gamma}f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\;

\displaystyle\|([\overline{\partial}{}\overline{\Delta}{},\tilde{a}^{\mu\gamma}\tilde{a}^{\beta\alpha}]\overline{\partial}{}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{\gamma})\partial_{\mu}f\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|[\overline{\partial}{}\overline{\Delta}{},\tilde{a}^{\mu\gamma}\tilde{a}^{\beta\alpha}]\overline{\partial}{}\partial_{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{\gamma}\|_{0}\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{4})\|f\|_{3}

\displaystyle\|[\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta},\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{4})\|f\|_{4}.

Therefore, Alinhac good unknown enjoys the following important properties:

\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}f)=\nabla_{\tilde{a}}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}+C^{\alpha}(f)

(3.71)

with

\|C^{\alpha}(f)\|\lesssim P(\|\tilde{\eta}\|_{4})\|f\|_{4}.

(3.72)

For (
3.1
), we define
\mathbf{V}=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}v-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}v
and
\mathbf{Q}=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}Q-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q
to be the Alinhac good unknowns for
v
and
Q=q+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}
. Taking
\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}
in the second equation of (
3.1
), we get

\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}\mathbf{V}+\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\mathbf{Q}=\underbrace{\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)+[\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1},\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}]\partial_{t}v_{\alpha}+\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\partial_{t}(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}v)+C(Q)}_{\mathbf{F}},

(3.73)

subject to

\mathbf{Q}=-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{3\beta}\partial_{3}Q~~~on~\Gamma.

(3.74)

and

\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\cdot\mathbf{V}=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v)-C^{\alpha}(v_{\alpha})~~~in~\Omega.

(3.75)

Taking
L^{2}
inner product with
\mathbf{V}
and time integral, we have

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\tilde{J}^{-1}\left|\mathbf{V}(T)\right|^{2}\,dy=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\left|\mathbf{V}(0)\right|^{2}\,dy-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{V}\,dy\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}\partial_{t}\tilde{J}^{-1}\left|\mathbf{V}\right|^{2}+\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{V}\,dy\,dt.

(3.76)

By (
3.71
)-(
3.72
) and direct computation, we know the last term on RHS can be directly controlled:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}\partial_{t}\tilde{J}^{-1}\left|\mathbf{V}\right|^{2}+\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{V}\,dy\,dt\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt+\epsilon\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left((b\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b\right)\right\|_{0}^{2}\,dt.

(3.77)

We integrate
\nabla_{\tilde{a}}
by parts to get

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{V}\,dy\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha}\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\,dy\,dt

(3.78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\mathbf{Q}(\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\mathbf{V}_{\alpha})\,dS\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{Q}(\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\cdot\mathbf{V})\,dy\,dt\underbrace{+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{\mu}\tilde{a}^{\mu\alpha})\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\,dy\,dt}_{J_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{3\beta}\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\,dS\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{Q}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v)\,dy\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{Q}C^{\alpha}(v_{\alpha})\,dy\,dt+J_{1}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{0}+I_{1}+J_{2}+J_{1}.

The term
J_{1},J_{2}
can be directly controlled by
\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt
and we omit the details. Let us first investigate
I_{1}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{Q}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v)\,dy\,dt
. Invoking
\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v=-\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{Q}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}(\text{div}_{\tilde{a}}v)\,dy\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}q+\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right)-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q\right)\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left(-\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q\right)

(3.79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}q\cdot\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left(-\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q\right)\,dy\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left(-\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q\right)\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}\right)\cdot\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left(-\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q\right)\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{11}+I_{12}+I_{13}.

The term
I_{11}
and
I_{13}
can be similarly computed as in
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
(
3.70
):

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle\lesssim-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\left|\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}q\right|^{2}\,dy\bigg|^{T}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt,

(3.80)

\displaystyle I_{13}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\partial_{t}q\right\|_{0}^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt.

(3.81)

One can see that
I_{11}
has been controlled, while
I_{13}
requires the control of 5-th order wave equation of
q
to absorb that
\epsilon
-term. This will again be postponed in Section
3.4
. For
I_{12}
, we just need to integrate
\partial_{t}
by parts

\displaystyle I_{12}=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\left(\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q\right)\,dy\,dt

(3.82)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}q\,dy\bigg|^{t=T}_{t=0}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}q\partial_{t}(\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\tilde{\eta}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q)\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{J}R^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\left|\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}q(T)\right|^{2}\,dy+\frac{1}{8\epsilon}\left(\|\tilde{\eta}(T)\|_{4}^{4}+\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}Q(T)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\right)+\mathcal{P}_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{\kappa}(t))\,dt.

Here in the last step we use
\epsilon
-Young’s inequality to deal with the first term in the second line. The second term can be directly controlled by using the estimates of
\|\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}\|_{4}
and we skip the details.

It remains to control the boundary integral
I_{0}
. Plugging
\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}=\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}v_{\alpha}-\overline{\partial}^{2}\overline{\Delta}\eta\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}}v_{\alpha}
into

4.2
Construction of the solution to the linearized approximation system

This part provides a fixed-point argument of constructing the solution to the linearized system (
4.2
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta=v+\mathring{\psi}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\rho_{0}\mathring{\tilde{{J}}}^{-1}\partial_{t}v=(\mathring{b}\cdot\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}})b-\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}}Q,~~Q=q+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\

Define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X}^{r}}

\displaystyle\Xi:\mathbf{X}(M,T)

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{X}(M,T)

\displaystyle\left(\xi,w,h,\pi\right)

1.

\eta(0)=

2.

Define

\frac{\rho_{0}}{\tilde{J}^{(n)}}\partial_{t}v^{(n+1)}=(b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{a^{(n)}})b^{(n+1)}-\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}Q^{(n+1)},~~Q^{(n+1)}=q^{(n+1)}+\frac{1}{2}|b^{(n+1)}|^{2}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\tilde{J}^{(n)}R^{\prime}(q^{(n)})}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q^{(n+1)}+\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}v^{(n+1)}=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b^{(n+1)}-\Delta_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}b^{(n+1)}=(b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{a^{(n)}})v^{(n+1)}-b^{(n)}\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}v^{(n+1)},&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
q^{(n+1)}=0,~b^{(n+1)}=\mathbf{0}&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\\
(\eta^{(n+1)},v^{(n+1)},b^{(n+1)},q^{(n+1)})|_{\{t=0\}}=(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}).\end{cases}

This part shows the Picard-type iteration of the sequence
\{(\eta^{(n)},v^{(n)},b^{(n)},q^{(n)})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
which gives a subsequential limit
(\eta,v,b,q)
converging in
H^{3}
-norm. Such limit
(\eta,v,b,q)
exactly solves the nonlinear
\kappa
-approximation problem (
3.1
).

Define
[\eta]^{(n)}:=\eta^{(n+1)}-\eta^{(n)},~[v]^{(n)}:=v^{(n+1)}-v^{(n)},~[b]^{(n)}:=b^{(n+1)}-b^{(n)},~[q]^{(n)}:=q^{(n+1)}-q^{(n)},
and
[a]^{(n)}:=a^{(n)}-a^{(n-1)},~[A]^{(n)}:=A^{(n)}-A^{(n-1)},~[\psi]^{(n)}:=\psi^{(n)}-\psi^{(n-1)}
. Then these quantities satisfy the following system consisting of:

The equation of momentum

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\partial_{t}[v]^{(n)}=

\displaystyle\left(b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{A}^{(n)}}\right)\left[b\right]^{(n)}-\nabla_{\tilde{A}^{(n)}}[Q]^{(n)}

(4.41)

\displaystyle+b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{[\tilde{A}]^{(n)}}b^{(n)}+[b]^{(n-1)}\cdot\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{{A}}}^{(n-1)}}b^{(n)}-\nabla_{\tilde{A}^{(n)}}Q^{(n)}.

Continuity equation:

r^{(n)}\partial_{t}[q]^{(n)}+\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[v]^{(n)}=-\text{div }_{[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}}v^{(n)}+[r]^{(n)}\partial_{t}q^{(n)},

(4.42)

here
r^{(n)}:=\mathring{\tilde{{J}}}^{(n)}R^{\prime}(q^{(n)})/\rho_{0}
.

Equation of magnetic field:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}[b]^{(n)}-\Delta_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[b]^{(n)}=

\displaystyle(b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}})[v]^{(n)}-b^{(n)}\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[v]^{(n)}

(4.43)

\displaystyle+b^{(n)}\cdot\nabla_{[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}}v^{(n)}-b^{(n)}\text{div }_{[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}}v^{(n)}

\displaystyle+[b]^{(n-1)}\cdot\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{{a}}}^{(n-1)}}v^{(n)}-[b]^{(n-1)}\text{div }_{\mathring{\tilde{{a}}}^{(n-1)}}v^{(n)}

\displaystyle+\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}\left(\nabla_{[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}}b^{(n)}\right)+\text{div }_{[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}}\left(\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{{a}}}^{(n-1)}}b^{(n)}\right).

The initial data of
([\eta],[v],[b],[q])=(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0},0)
. The boundary conditions are

[b]^{(n)}=\mathbf{0},[q]^{(n)}=0.

(4.44)

Define the energy functional

[\mathcal{E}]^{(n)}(T):=[E]^{(n)}(T)+[H]^{(n)}(T)+[W]^{(n)}(T)+\sum_{k=0}^{3}\left\|\partial_{t}^{3-k}\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[b]^{(n)}\right\|_{k}^{2},

(4.45)

where

\displaystyle[E]^{(n)}(T):=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{3}\left(\left\|\partial_{t}^{3-k}[v]^{(n)}\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{3-k}[b]^{(n)}\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{3-k}[q]^{(n)}\right\|_{k}^{2}\right),

(4.46)

\displaystyle[H]^{(n)}(T):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4}[b]^{n}\right\|_{0}^{2}\,dt+\left\|\partial_{t}^{3}\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[b]^{(n)}\right\|_{0}^{2},

(4.47)

\displaystyle[W]^{(n)}(T):=

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}^{4}[q]^{(n)}\right\|_{0}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{3}\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[q]^{(n)}\right\|_{0}^{2}.

(4.48)

The conclusion is

Proposition 4.3
.

For
n
sufficiently large and
T_{\kappa}>0
suitably small, we have that
\forall T\in[0,T_{\kappa}]

[E]^{(n)}(T)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left([E]^{(n-1)}(T)+[E]^{(n-2)}(T)\right).

\square

By Proposition
4.3
, we know
[E]^{(n)}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}P_{\kappa}(\mathcal{P}_{0})
, and thus yields the limit for each fixed
\kappa>0
:

\left(\eta^{(n)},v^{(n)},b^{(n)},q^{(n)}\right)\xrightarrow{\text{converge strongly}}(\eta(\kappa),v(\kappa),b(\kappa),q(\kappa))~~~as~n\to\infty.

Such limit exactly solves the nonlinear approximation system
3.1
.

Corollary 4.4
.

The limit
(\eta(\kappa),v(\kappa),b(\kappa),q(\kappa))
gotten in Proposition
4.3
is the unique strong solution to the nonlinear approximation system (
3.1
) and satisfies the energy estimates in
[0,T_{\kappa}]

\sup_{0\leq T\leq T_{\kappa}}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\kappa}(T)\leq 2\left(\|v_{0}\|_{4}^{2}+\|b_{0}\|_{5}^{2}+\|q_{0}\|_{4}^{2}\right),

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\kappa}(T):=\widetilde{E}_{\kappa}(T)+\widetilde{H}_{\kappa}(T)+\widetilde{W}_{\kappa}(T)+\sum_{k=0}^{4}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}\left((b(\kappa)\cdot\nabla_{\tilde{a}})b(\kappa)\right)\right\|_{k}^{2},

(4.49)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\kappa}(T):=

\displaystyle\left\|\eta\right\|_{4}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{4}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}v(\kappa)\right\|_{k}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{4}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}b(\kappa)\right\|_{k}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{4}\left\|\partial_{t}^{4-k}q(\kappa)\right\|_{k}^{2}

(4.50)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\kappa}(T):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{t}^{5}b(\kappa)\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\left\|\partial_{t}^{4}b(\kappa)\right\|_{1}^{2}

(4.51)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{\kappa}(T):=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{4}\left\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}}\partial_{t}^{4-k}q(\kappa)\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{5}q(\kappa)\right\|_{0}^{2}.

(4.52)

\square

The proof process is nearly the same as in the a priori estimates part, so we do not write all details here but still state the main steps.

Step 1: Correction Terms

First we estimate the coefficients and correction terms.

[\psi]^{(n)}
satisfies
-\Delta[\psi]^{(n)}=0
with the boundary condition

\displaystyle[\psi]^{(n)}=\overline{\Delta}{}^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}\bigg(

\displaystyle\overline{\Delta}{}[\eta]^{(n-1)}_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{(n)i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}v^{(n)}+\overline{\partial}{}\eta^{(n-1)}_{\beta}[\tilde{a}]^{(n)i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}v^{(n)}+\overline{\partial}{}\eta^{(n-1)}_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{(n-1)i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}[v]^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle-\overline{\Delta}{}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}[\eta]^{(n-1)}_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{(n)i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}v^{(n)}-\overline{\Delta}{}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}\eta^{(n-1)}_{\beta}[\tilde{a}]^{(n)i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}v^{(n)}-\overline{\Delta}{}\Lambda_{\kappa}^{2}\eta^{(n-1)}_{\beta}\tilde{a}^{(n-1)i\beta}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}[v]^{(n-1)}\bigg).

By the standard elliptic estimates, we have the control for
[\psi]^{(n)}

\|[\psi]^{(n)}\|_{3}^{2}\lesssim|[\psi]^{(n)}|_{2.5}\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{0}\left(\|[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{3}^{2}+\|[v]^{(n-1)}\|_{2}^{2}+\|[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}\|_{1}^{2}\right).

(4.53)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle[a]^{(n)\mu\nu}(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}(a^{(n)\mu\nu}-a^{(n-1)\mu\nu})\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}[a]^{(n)\mu\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\partial_{t}\eta^{(n)}_{\gamma}a^{(n)\beta\nu}+a^{(n-1)\mu\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\partial_{t}[\eta]^{(n-1)}_{\gamma}a^{(n)\beta\nu}+a^{(n-1)\mu\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\partial_{t}\eta^{(n-1)}_{\gamma}[a]^{(n)\beta\nu},

which gives

\|[a]^{(n)}(T)\|_{2}\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\|[a]^{(n)}(t)\|_{2}^{2}(\|[v]^{(n-1)}\|_{3}+\|[\psi]^{(n-1)}\|_{3}))\,dt.

(4.54)

Therefore we get

\sup_{[0,T]}\|[a]^{(n)}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{0}T^{2}\left(\|[a]^{(n)},[a]^{(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}}+\|[v]^{(n-1)},[v]^{(n-2)},[\eta]^{(n-2)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{3}}^{2}\right),

(4.55)

and the bound for
[\eta]
via
\partial_{t}[\eta]^{(n)}=[v]^{(n)}+[\psi]^{(n)}
:

\sup_{[0,T]}\|[\eta]^{(n)}\|_{3}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{0}T^{2}\left(\|[a]^{(n)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|[v]^{(n)},[v]^{(n-1)},[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{3}}^{2}\right)

(4.56)

Similar as in Lemma
3.2
, one can get estimates for the time derivatives of
[\eta]
and
[\psi]

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{t}\psi]^{(n)}\|_{3}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}\left(\|[a]^{(n)}\|_{2}^{2}+\|[\partial_{t}v]^{(n-1)}\|_{2}^{2}+\|[v]^{(n-1)},[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{3}^{2}\right)

(4.57)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{t}^{2}\psi]^{(n)}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}\left(\|[a]^{(n)}\|_{2}^{2}+\|[\partial_{t}^{2}v]^{(n-1)}\|_{1}^{2}+\|[\partial_{t}v]^{(n-1)}\|_{2}^{2}+\|[v]^{(n-1)},[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{3}^{2}\right)

(4.58)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{t}^{3}\psi]^{(n)}\|_{1}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}\left(\|[a]^{(n)}\|_{2}^{2}+\|[\partial_{t}^{2}v]^{(n-1)}\|_{1}^{2}+\|[\partial_{t}^{3}v]^{(n-1)}\|_{0}^{2}+\|[\partial_{t}v]^{(n-1)}\|_{2}^{2}+\|[v]^{(n-1)},[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{3}^{2}\right)

(4.59)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{t}\eta]^{(n)}\|_{3}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}T^{2}\left(\|[a]^{(n)},[\partial_{t}v]^{(n)},[\partial_{t}v]^{(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|[v]^{(n)},[v]^{(n-1)},[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{3}}^{2}\right)

(4.60)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{t}^{2}\eta]^{(n)}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}T^{2}\left(\|[\partial_{t}^{2}v]^{(n),(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|[a]^{(n)},[\partial_{t}v]^{(n),(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}}+\|[v]^{(n),(n-1)},[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{3}}^{2}\right)

(4.61)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{t}^{3}\eta]^{(n)}\|_{1}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}\left(\|[\partial_{t}^{2}v]^{(n),(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|[a]^{(n)},[\partial_{t}v]^{(n),(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|[v]^{(n),(n-1)},[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{3}}^{2}\right).

(4.62)

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{t}^{4}\eta]^{(n)}\|_{0}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}\bigg(\|[\partial_{t}^{3}v]^{(n,n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}+\|[\partial_{t}^{2}v]^{(n,n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|[a]^{(n)},[\partial_{t}v]^{(n,n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}

(4.63)

\displaystyle+\|[v]^{(n,n-1)},[\eta]^{(n-1)}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{3}}^{2}\bigg).

(4.64)

Step 2: Magnetic field and Lorentz force

The first step is still the elliptic estimates of
[b]^{(n)}
. We show an example of
\|[b]^{(n)}\|_{3}

\|[b]^{(n)}\|_{3}\lesssim P(\|\mathring{\tilde{{\eta}}}^{(n)}\|_{2})\left(\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[b]^{(n)}\|_{1}+P(\|\overline{\partial}{}\mathring{\tilde{{\eta}}}\|_{2})\|[b]^{(n)}\|_{2}\right).

One can still use the heat equation (
4.43
) to eliminate the Laplacian terms, but now we have two more higher order terms when “[
\cdot
]” falls on
\text{div}_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}}
or
\mathring{\tilde{{a}}}

\mathcal{E}^{(n-1,n,n+1)}
and thus by
\mathcal{P}_{0}
. In specific, such terms are

\left\|\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}\left(\nabla_{[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}}b^{(n)}\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\text{div }_{[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}}\left(\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{{a}}}^{(n-1)}}b^{(n)}\right)\right\|_{1}.

The leading order part in these two terms can be written as
[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}
times the top order derivatives (4-th order) of

which has been controlled uniformly in
n
in Proposition
4.1
. For example,

\left\|\text{div }_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}\left(\nabla_{[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}}b^{(n)}\right)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\left\|[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}\right\|_{2}\left\|b^{(n)}\right\|_{4}\times\cdots\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{0}\left\|[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}\right\|_{2}

Therefore, the control of
[b]^{(n)}
can be controlled in the same manner as before. Similar estimates hold for
\partial_{t}[b]^{(n)}
. The control of
\|\partial_{t}^{2}[b]\|_{1}
and
\|\partial_{t}^{3}[b]\|_{0}
is reduced to the estimates of heat equation (
4.43
). The proof is the same as Section
3.2
so we omit it here.

The Lorentz force is controlled in a silimar way. For example,

\left\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[b]^{(n)}\right\|_{3}\lesssim P\left(\|\mathring{\tilde{{\eta}}}^{(n)}\|_{3}\right)\left(\left\|\Delta_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[b]^{(n)}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\overline{\partial}{}\mathring{\tilde{{\eta}}}^{(n)}\right\|_{3}\left\|[b]^{(n)}\right\|_{3}\right)

We again use the heat equation (
4.43
) to eliminate the Laplacian term, and the extra terms can be controlled in the same way as above. (Note that
\|\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{a}}}b\|_{4}
is controlled in Proposition
4.1
). Therefore,

\left\|\nabla_{\tilde{a}^{(n)}}[b]^{(n)}\right\|_{3}\lesssim\kappa^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{0}\left\|[\tilde{a}]^{(n)}\right\|_{2}.

Similar estimates hold for the time derivatives of Lorentz force.

Step 3: Div-Curl estimates

The control of

and

. The equation of

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\partial_{t}\text{curl }_{\tilde{A}^{(n)}}[v]^{(n)}=

\displaystyle+\text{curl }_{\tilde{A}^{(n)}}\left([b]^{(n-1)}\cdot\nabla_{\mathring{\tilde{{A}}}^{(n-1)}}b^{(n)}+b^{(n-1)}\cdot\nabla_{[\tilde{A}]^{(n)}}b^{(n)}-\nabla_{[\tilde{A}]^{(n)}}Q^{(n)}\right)

Invoking the divergence equation (

Similarly as in Section

5
Local well-posedness of the original system

As stated in Corollary
4.4
, the local well-posedness of the nonlinear approximation system (
3.1
) is established in an
\kappa
-dependent time interval
[0,T_{\kappa}]
. Combining the uniform-in-
\kappa
nonlinear a priori estimates Proposition
3.1
, we know that there exists a
\kappa
-
independent
time
T_{1}>0
, such that the local existence of the solution
(\eta,v,b,q)
to the original equation (
1.18
) holds in
[0,T_{1}]
by letting
\kappa\to 0
. It remains to prove the uniqueness of the solution. Let us recall the original equation first

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta=v&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\rho_{0}J^{-1}\partial_{t}v=(b\cdot\nabla_{a})b-\nabla_{a}Q,~~Q=q+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2}&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\frac{JR^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}q+\text{div }_{a}v=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}b-\Delta_{a}b=(b\cdot\nabla_{a})v-b\text{div }_{a}v,&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
\text{div }_{a}b=0&~~~\text{in }\Omega,\\
q=0,~b=\mathbf{0},~-\partial_{3}Q|_{t=0}\geq c_{0}>0&~~~\text{on }\Gamma,\\
(\eta,v,b,q)|_{\{t=0\}}=(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0}).\end{cases}

Suppose
(\eta^{i},v^{i}.b^{i},q^{i}),~i=1,2
solves (
1.18
) with the same initial data
(\text{Id},v_{0},b_{0},q_{0})
. Then we consider the system of
([\eta],[v],[b],[q])
by setting
[f]:=f^{1}-f^{2}
. Then we have

The flow map:

\partial_{t}[\eta]=[v].

The momentum equation:

\rho_{0}(J^{1})^{-1}\partial_{t}[v]=(b^{1}\cdot\nabla_{a^{1}})[b]-\nabla_{a^{1}}[Q]-\rho_{0}[J^{-1}]\partial_{t}v^{2}+(b^{1}\cdot\nabla_{[a]})b^{2}+[b]\cdot\nabla_{a^{2}}b^{2}-\nabla_{[a]}Q^{2}.

The continuity equation:

\frac{J^{1}R^{\prime}(q^{1})}{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}[q]+\text{div }_{a^{1}}[v]=\left[\frac{JR^{\prime}(q)}{\rho_{0}}\right]\partial_{t}q^{2}-\text{div }_{[a]}v^{2}~~~\text{in }\Omega.

The equation of magnetic field:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}[b]-\Delta_{a^{1}}[b]=

\displaystyle(b^{1}\cdot\nabla_{a^{1}})[v]-b\text{div }_{a}[v]

\displaystyle+\text{div }_{a^{1}}\left(\nabla_{[a]}b^{2}\right)+\text{div }_{[a]}\left(\nabla_{a^{2}}b^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+(b^{1}\cdot\nabla_{[a]})v^{2}+([b]\cdot\nabla_{a^{2}})v^{2}

\displaystyle-b^{1}\text{div }_{[a]}v^{2}-[b]\text{div }_{a^{2}}v^{2},

and

\text{div }_{a^{1}}[b]=-\text{div }_{[a]}b^{2}.

The boundary conditions:

[q]=0,~[b]=\mathbf{0},~-\partial_{3}Q_{1}\text{ and }-\partial_{3}Q_{2}|_{t=0}\geq c_{0}>0,

and zero initial data.

Define the energy functional

[\mathcal{E}](T):=[E](T)+[H](T)+[W](T)+\left\|\partial_{t}^{2-k}\left(\left(b^{1}\cdot\nabla_{a^{1}}\right)[b]\right)\right\|_{k}^{2},

where

\displaystyle[E](t)

\displaystyle:=\left\|[\eta]\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left|\tilde{a}^{3\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{2}[\eta]_{\alpha}\right|_{0}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\left(\left\|\partial_{t}^{2-k}[v]\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{2-k}[b]\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{2-k}[q]\right\|_{k}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle[H](T)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{t}^{3}[b]\right|^{2}\,dy\,dt+\left\|\partial_{t}^{2}[b]\right\|_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle[W](T)

\displaystyle:=\left\|\partial_{t}^{3}[q]\right\|_{0}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{2}[q]\right\|_{1}^{2}.

The energy estimate of
[\mathcal{E}]
is almost the same as
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}
except that
[Q]
no longer satisfies Taylor sign condition. So what we need to do is to investigate the boundary integral

\int_{\Gamma}[\mathbf{Q}](a^{1})^{3\alpha}[\mathbf{V}]_{\alpha}\,dS,

where we define the Alinhac good unknowns

\mathbf{V}^{i}=\overline{\partial}{}^{2}v^{i}-\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\eta^{i}\cdot\nabla_{a^{i}}v^{i},~~\mathbf{Q}^{i}=\overline{\partial}{}^{2}Q^{i}-\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\eta^{i}\cdot\nabla_{a^{i}}Q^{i},

and

[\mathbf{V}]:=\mathbf{V}^{1}-\mathbf{V}^{2},~~[\mathbf{Q}]:=\mathbf{Q}^{1}-\mathbf{Q}^{2}.

The boundary terms then becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}[\mathbf{Q}](a^{1})^{3\alpha}[\mathbf{V}]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}[Q]\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\eta^{2}_{\beta}(a^{2})^{3\beta}(a^{2})^{3\alpha}[\mathbf{V}]_{\alpha}\,dS-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q^{1}(\overline{\partial}{}^{2}[\eta]_{\beta}(a^{1})^{3\beta}+\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\eta^{2}_{\beta}[a]^{3\beta})(a^{1})^{3\alpha}[\mathbf{V}]_{\alpha}\,dS

\displaystyle\lesssim-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q^{1}|(a^{1})^{3\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{2}[\eta]_{\alpha}|_{0}^{2}\,dS

\displaystyle~~~~-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q^{1}(a^{1})^{3\gamma}\overline{\partial}{}^{2}[\eta]_{\gamma}(\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\eta^{2}_{\beta}[a]^{\mu\beta}\partial_{\mu}v^{1}_{\alpha}-\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\eta^{2}_{\beta}(a^{2})^{\mu\beta}\partial_{\mu}[v]_{\alpha})(a^{1})^{3\alpha}\,dS

\displaystyle~~~~-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{3}Q^{1}(\overline{\partial}{}^{2}[\eta]_{\beta}(a^{1})^{3\beta}+\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\eta^{2}_{\beta}[a]^{3\beta})(a^{1})^{3\alpha}[\mathbf{V}]_{\alpha}\,dS

\displaystyle\lesssim-\frac{c_{0}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Gamma}|(a^{1})^{3\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{2}[\eta]_{\alpha}|_{0}^{2}\,dS+P(\text{initial data})P([\mathcal{E}](t)).

Here in the second step we use the precise formula of
[\mathbf{V}]
, and in the third step we use Taylor sign condition for
Q^{1}
. Thus similarly we get

\sup_{t\in[0,T_{1}]}[\mathcal{E}](t)\leq\text{initial data}+\int_{0}^{T_{0}}P([\mathcal{E}](t))\,dt.

Since the initial data of the system of
([\eta],[v],[b],[q])
is 0, we know
[\mathcal{E}](t)=0
for all
t\in[0,T_{1}]
.

Conclusively, the local well-posedness of the free-boundary compressible resistive MHD system (
1.1
) is established in Lagrangian coordinates (
1.18
) with Sobolev initial data.

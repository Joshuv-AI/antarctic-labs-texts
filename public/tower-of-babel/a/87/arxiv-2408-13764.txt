Strichartz estimates for orthonormal functions and probabilistic
convergence of density functions of compact operators on manifolds

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we establish
some Strichartz estimates for orthonormal functions and their applications as well as
probabilistic convergence of density
functions related to some compact operators on manifolds.
Firstly, we present the suitable bound of

\int_{a\leq|s|\leq b}e^{isx}s^{-1+i\beta}ds(\beta\in\mbox{\bf R},a\geq 0,0<b\leq\infty),

\int_{a\leq s\leq b}e^{isx}s^{-1+i\beta}ds(\beta\in\mbox{\bf R},\beta\neq 0,a,b\in\mbox{\bf R})
,
which extends the result of Page 204 of
Vega (Restriction theorems and the Schrödinger multiplier
on the torus.
Partial differential equations
with minimal smoothness and applications, 199-211, 1992.).
Secondly, we prove that
\left|\beta\int_{a}^{b}e^{isx}s^{-1+i\beta}ds\right|\leq C(1+e^{-\pi\beta})(1+|\beta|)^{2}(\beta\in\mbox{\bf R},a,b\in\mbox{\bf R}),

which extends Lemma 1 of Bez et al.
(Forum of Mathematics, Sigma, 9(2021), 1-52).
Thirdly, we establish some Strichartz estimates for orthonormal
functions
and Schatten bound with space-time norms
related to elliptic operator and non-elliptic operator on
\mbox{\bf R}^{d},

which extend the result of Theorems 8, 9 of
Frank and Sabin (Amer. J. Math. 139(2017), 1649-1691.);
we also establish some Strichartz estimates for orthonormal functions
and Schatten bound with space-time norms on
\mbox{\bf T}^{d}
related to
non-elliptic operator.
Fourthly, we establish the Strichartz estimates for
orthonormal functions related to Boussinesq operator
on
R
.
Fifthly, we
establish the convergence
result of some compact operators
and nonlinear
part of the solution to
some operator equations in Schatten norms.
Finally, inspired by the work of Hadama and Yamamoto
(Probabilistic Strichartz
estimates in Schatten classes and
their applications to Hartree equation, arxiv:2311.02713v1.),
for
\gamma_{0}\in\mathfrak{S}^{2}
which is the Hilbert-Schmidt class,
we establish the probabilistic convergence
of density functions of some compact operators on

\mbox{\bf R}^{d},\mbox{\bf T}^{d},\Theta=\left\{x\in\mbox{\bf R}^{3}:|x|<1\right\}
with full
randomization, which improves the result of Corollary 1.2 of
Bez et al. (Selecta Math. 26(2020), 24 pp) with
x\in\mbox{\bf R}

in the probabilistic sense.

Keywords
:Strichartz estimates for orthonormal
functions; Suitable bound of some complex integrals; Probabilistic
convergence of density functions with full randomization

Introduction

We begin by studying the infinite system of some dispersive
equation with Hartree-type nonlinearity in the following form:

\displaystyle i\frac{du_{j}}{dt}=P(D)u_{j}+(w*\rho)u_{j},j\in\mbox{\bf N}^{+},x\in\mbox{\bf R}^{d},t\in I\subset\mbox{\bf R},

(1.1)

\displaystyle u_{j}(x,0)=f_{j}(x).

(1.2)

Here
f_{j}
forms an orthonormal family in
L^{2}(\mbox{\bf R}^{d})
,
w(x):\mbox{\bf R}^{d}\rightarrow\mbox{\bf R}
,

\rho=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{+\infty}|u_{j}|^{2}
and

P(D)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle P(D)f(x)=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}P(\xi)\mathscr{F}_{x}(\xi)d\xi,D=\frac{1}{i}(\partial_{x_{1}},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\partial_{x_{d}}),

(1.3)

where
x\cdot\xi=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}\xi_{j}
.
By using a direct computation, we derive the operator valued form
of (
1.1
)

\displaystyle i\frac{d\gamma(t)}{dt}=[P(D)+w*\rho_{\gamma},\gamma],

(1.4)

\displaystyle\gamma(0)=\gamma_{0}.

(1.5)

Here
\gamma(t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}|u_{j}\rangle\langle u_{j}|
is
bounded and self-adjoint operator
on
L^{2}(\mbox{\bf R}^{d})
,
\rho_{\gamma}

is the density function of
\gamma

When

1.4

\gamma_{0}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|f_{j}\rangle\langle f_{j}|

\displaystyle\gamma(t):=e^{-itP(D)}\gamma_{0}e^{itP(D)}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{-itP(D)}f_{j}\rangle\langle e^{-itP(D)}f_{j}|.

(1.6)

Here we have used Dirac’s notation for the rank-one operator

|u\rangle\langle v|

f\longrightarrow\langle v,f\rangle u=u\int_{I}\overline{v}fdx
,
where
I\subset\mbox{\bf R}^{d}.

Because the
f_{j}(j\in\mbox{\bf N}^{+})
form an orthonormal system,

\lambda_{j}
are precisely the
eigenvalues of the operator
\gamma
.
By using a direct computation, we derive the kernel of
\gamma(t)
as
follows:

\displaystyle K(x,y,t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}(e^{-itP(D)}f_{j})(x,t)(\overline{e^{-itP(D)}f_{j}})(y,t).

(1.7)

Thus, the density function of
\gamma(t)
is
\rho_{\gamma(t)}=K(x,x,t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{-itP(D)}f_{j}|^{2}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle Trace(\gamma(t))=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}^{d}}K(x,x,t)dx=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}^{d}}|e^{-itP(D)}f_{j}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}.

(1.8)

One of our goal in this paper is to establish the Strichartz estimates
for density function of
\gamma(t)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}|e^{-itP(D)}f_{j}(x,t)|^{2}\right\|_{L_{t\in I_{1}}^{p}L_{x\in I_{2}}^{q}}=\left\|\rho_{\gamma(t)}\right\|_{L_{t\in I_{1}}^{p}L_{x\in I_{2}}^{q}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{I_{1}}\left(\int_{I_{2}}\left|\rho_{\gamma(t)}\right|^{q}dx\right)^{p/q}dt\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq C\left\|\gamma(t)\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}}(1\leq\alpha<\infty),

(1.9)

which is the duality form of

\displaystyle\left\|We^{-itP(D)}(e^{-itP(D)})^{*}\overline{W}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}\leq C\|W\|_{L_{t\in I_{1}}^{2p^{\prime}}L_{x\in I_{2}}^{2q^{\prime}}}^{2}.

\lambda_{j}(j\in\mbox{\bf N}^{+})
are eigenvalues of
\gamma(t)

and we use the fact that

\displaystyle\left[Trace(|\gamma(t)|^{\alpha})\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\left[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\left\|\gamma(t)\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}}(1\leq\alpha<\infty),

[
54

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
b\in\mbox{\bf R}.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho^{-1+ib}d\rho\right]\phi(b)db

\displaystyle=2\pi\phi(0)=2\pi\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\delta(b)\phi(b)db.

in the sense of distribution.
Here

is the Dirac function and
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mbox{\bf R})

Proof.
We present two methods to prove Lemma 3.1.
We present the first method to prove Lemma 3.1.
For
b\in\mbox{\bf R},
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho^{-1+ib}d\rho\right]\phi(b)db

\displaystyle=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho^{ib}d\ln\rho\ \right]\phi(b)db\quad\underline{\underline{t=\ln\rho}}\ \

\displaystyle=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{ibt}dt\right]\phi(b)db=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{ibt}dt\right]\phi(b)db

\displaystyle=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left[\lim\limits_{\alpha\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{ibt}e^{-\alpha|t|}dt\right]\phi(b)db

\displaystyle=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left[\lim\limits_{\alpha\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{ibt}e^{-\alpha t}dt+\lim\limits_{\alpha\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{ibt}e^{\alpha t}dt\right]\phi(b)db

\displaystyle=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left[\lim\limits_{\alpha\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha^{2}+b^{2}}\right]\phi(b)db=2\pi\phi(0).

(3.2)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho^{-1+ib}d\rho=\lim\limits_{\alpha\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha^{2}+b^{2}}=2\pi\delta(b)

(3.3)

in the sense of distribution.
Now we present the second method to prove Lemma 3.1.
For any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mbox{\bf R})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho^{-1+ib}d\rho\right)\phi db\ \ \underline{\underline{t=\ln\rho}}\ \ \int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\left(\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}e^{ibt}dt\right)\phi db

\displaystyle=\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}e^{ibt}\phi dbdt=2\pi\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}(\mathscr{F}\phi)(-t)dt\ \underline{\underline{t=-s}}\ \

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{\mbox{\scriptsize\bf R}}(\mathscr{F}\phi)(s)ds=2\pi\phi(0).

(3.4)

From (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho^{-1+ib}d\rho=2\pi\delta(b)

in the sense of distribution.

This completes the proof of Lemma 3.1.

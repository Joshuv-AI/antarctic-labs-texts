Abstract
. We study a categorical condition on relations, which is a categorical formulation of Jónsson’s characterisation of congruence distributive varieties. Categories satisfying these conditions need not be varieties; for instance, the dual of the categories of topological spaces, ordered sets, G G -sets, and the dual of any (pre)topos all provide us with examples.

1.
Introduction

A variety of universal algebras
\mathbb{V}
is
congruence distributive

[
24
]
if the congruence lattice on any algebra in
\mathbb{V}
is a distributive lattice. These varieties occupy an extremal situation in universal algebra created by the modular commutator, namely, when it is as large as possible. On the other end, when it is as small as possible, we have the abelian varieties — a topic which categorical algebra is well suited for. However, there has been comparably less attention given to congruence distributivity, from the categorical point of view. One possible reason for this, is that there are several categorical conditions arising from equivalent characterisations of congruence distributivity for varieties, each with their advantages and disadvantages. Moreover, these categorical conditions are not necessarily equivalent, as are their varietal counterparts.

Congruences in a variety of universal algebras are just internal equivalence relations in the variety, so the most direct categorical distributivity condition is the notion of an equivalence distributive category given in
[
14
]
: a finitely complete category
\mathbb{C}
is called
equivalence distributive
when the meet semilattice
\mathsf{Eq}(A)
of equivalence relations on
A
is a distributive lattice: for any
R,S,T\in\mathsf{Eq}(A)
, we have

(1)

R\wedge(S\vee T)=(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T).

However, this notion has several drawbacks. For one thing, its definition relies on the existence of suprema of equivalence relations — a condition not guaranteed by familiar categorical contexts such as finite completeness, regularity
[
1
]
, or even Barr-exactness
[
1
]
. Moreover, the definition of equivalence distributive category excludes categories such the variety of (distributive) lattices equipped with an term of countable arity (see
[
21
]
). However, this category is categorically quite similar to the category of lattices — the prototypical example of a congruence distributive variety.

A condition which does not involve such an inductive requirement as the existence of suprema of equivalence relations, is a categorical version of the so called
trapezoid lemma

[
9

15

\mathbb{V}

p_{i}(x,x,y)=p_{i+1}(x,x,y),&\text{for $i$ even},\\

p_{n}(y,x,x)=x,&\text{if $n$ odd}.\\

(R\circ S)\wedge T\leqslant(R\wedge(T\circ S^{\mathsf{op}}))\circ S.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be a regular category satisfying the trapezoid lemma. Given any equivalence relations
R,S,T
on any object
A
in
\mathbb{C}
such that
S\circ T\leqslant R\circ S
and
R\wedge S\leqslant T
, then
T
permutes with
S
.

Proof.

Given the relations as in the statement, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}S\circ T&\leqslant(R\circ S)\wedge(S\circ T)&\text{(assumption)}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Freyd}}}{{\leqslant}}(R\wedge(S\circ T\circ S^{\mathsf{op}}))\circ S&\\
&=(R\wedge(S\circ T\circ S))\circ S&\text{(S is symmetric)}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{TpL}}}{{\leqslant}}T\circ S.\end{array}

∎

Recall that a regular category
\mathbb{C}
is called
factor permutable

[
12
]
if for any equivalence relation
E
on an object
A
we have
E\circ\mathsf{Eq}(p)=\mathsf{Eq}(p)\circ E
where
p:A\rightarrow X
is a product projection of
A
.

Proposition 3.4
.

A regular category
\mathbb{C}
satisfying the trapezoid lemma is factor permutable.

Proof.

Given any product diagram

we note that
\mathsf{Eq}(p_{1})\wedge\mathsf{Eq}(p_{2})=\Delta_{A}
and
\mathsf{Eq}(p_{1})\circ\mathsf{Eq}(p_{2})=\nabla_{A}
, which denotes the largest equivalence relation on
A
. Now given any equivalence relation
E
on
A
and applying Proposition
3.3
in the case
R=\mathsf{Eq}(p_{1})
and
S=\mathsf{Eq}(p_{2})
and
T=E
, we get the desired result.
∎

3.5.
Jónsson categories

In this subsection we introduce the notion of a Jónsson category of order
n
as a categorical generalisation of varieties admitting
n
Jónsson terms for
n\geqslant 1
. A usual procedure to obtain such kind of generalisation is to consider a characteristic property on relations of the variety being studied, when such a property can be expressed categorically. We note here that a general class of properties which allow for the translation of Mal’tsev conditions into their corresponding categorical conditions has been given in
[
22
]
. In particular, it was shown in that paper how the
linear Mal’tsev conditions
(see
[
32
]
) can be interpreted as a property determined by an extended matrix of variables.

Definition 3.6
.

A regular category
\mathbb{C}
is said to be a
Jónsson category of order
n
(where
n\in\mathbb{N})
if for any equivalence relations
R,S,T
on any object
A
of
\mathbb{C}
, we have

(7)

R\wedge(S\circ T)\leqslant(R\wedge S,R\wedge T)_{n+1}.

Remark 3.7
.

A variety of universal algebras
\mathbb{V}
admits
n
Jónsson terms if and only if it is a Jónsson category of order
n
. The proof of this is implicitly in
[
24
]
, and for this reason we omit it.

Remark 3.8
.

A Jónsson category of order 1 is precisely a regular majority category (see Theorem 3.1 in
[
18
]
). Note also, that the notion of majority category
[
16
]
does not require the base category to be regular and has been defined as a property which may be formulated with respect to any category.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be a regular category. The following statements are equivalent for
n\geqslant 1
:

(i)

\mathbb{C}
is a Jónsson category of order
n
;

(ii)

Given an equivalence relation
R
and reflexive relations
S,T
on any object
A
of
\mathbb{C}
, we have

(8)

R\wedge(S\circ T)\leqslant\left(\,(R\wedge S^{\mathsf{op}})\circ(R\wedge S),(R\wedge T)\circ(R\wedge T^{\mathsf{op}})\,\right)_{n+1}.

The property (ii) in the theorem above has been extracted from
[
24
]
(see equation (4) therein). The proof of the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) is straightforward by using the symmetry and transitivity of
S
and
T
. Moreover, for
n=1
, the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) follow from Theorem 3.1 in
[
18
]
. The proof of the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) for the general case when
n\geqslant 2
is a bit technical. For this reason, we decided to postpone its proof to the end of this work (Section
3.20
), while focussing now on the nice consequences and properties that can be extracted from Theorem
3.9
.

Corollary 3.10
.

If
\mathbb{C}
is a Jónsson category of order
n
, then it is a Jónsson category of order
k
, for any
k\geqslant n
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Definition
3.6
and the fact that
(R\wedge S,R\wedge T)_{n+1}\leqslant(R\wedge S,R\wedge T)_{k+1}
, since
R,S
and
T
are reflexive relations.
∎

Under the assumption that the base category
\mathbb{C}
is
n
-permutable (so that suprema of equivalence relations on a same object
A
exist in
\mathbb{C}
, so that
\mathsf{Eq}(A)
is a lattice), we shall prove in Proposition
3.12
that the notion of equivalence distributive category coincides with that of a Jónsson category (of order
n-1
).

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be an
n
-permutable category (
n\geqslant 2
) and a Jónsson category of any order. Given
R,S,T\in\mathsf{Eq}(A)
, for some object
A
of
\mathbb{C}
, we have

R\wedge(S,T)_{j}\leqslant(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T),\;\text{for any $j\geqslant 1$}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbb{C}
is a Jónsson category of order
k\geqslant 1
. We can suppose that
k\geqslant n-1
by Corollary
3.10
. Since
k\geqslant n-1
, then
\mathbb{C}
is also
(k+1)
-permutable. For
j=1
, the inequality is obvious. We prove the above inequality by induction on
j\geqslant 2
. For
j=2
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}R\wedge(S,T)_{2}&=R\wedge(S\circ T)&\\
&\leqslant(\,R\wedge S,R\wedge T\,)_{k+1}&\text{(by Definition\penalty\ \ref{definition: Jonsson Category})}\\
&=(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T)&\text{($\,(k+1)$-permutability)}.\end{array}

Suppose now that
R\wedge(S,T)_{j}\leqslant(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T)
, for some
j\geqslant 2
. When
j
is odd

\begin{array}[]{ll}R\wedge(S,T)_{j+1}=R\wedge((S,T)_{j}\circ T)&\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Jonsson pp for vars}}}{{\leqslant}}(\,(R\wedge(S,T)_{j})\circ(R\wedge(S,T)_{j}),R\wedge T\,)_{k+1}&\text{($(S,T)_{j}^{\mathsf{op}}=(S,T)_{j}$, $T^{\mathsf{op}}=T$)}\\
\leqslant(\,((R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T))\circ((R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T)),R\wedge T\,)_{k+1}&\text{(induction assumption)}\\
=(\,(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T),R\wedge T\,)_{k+1}&\text{($(R\!\wedge\!S)\!\vee\!(R\!\wedge\!T)$ transitive)}\\
=((R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T))\vee(R\wedge T)&\text{($\,(k+1)$-permutability)}\\
=(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T).\end{array}

When
j
is even

\begin{array}[]{ll}R\wedge(S,T)_{j+1}=R\wedge((S,T)_{j}\circ S)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Jonsson pp for vars}}}{{\leqslant}}(\,(R\wedge(T,S)_{j})\circ(R\wedge(S,T)_{j}),R\wedge S\,)_{k+1},&\text{($(S,T)_{j}^{\mathsf{op}}=(T,S)_{j}$, $T^{\mathsf{op}}=T$)}\\
\leqslant(\,((R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T))\circ((R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T)),R\wedge S\,)_{k+1}&\text{(induction assumption)}\\
=(\,(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T),R\wedge S\,)_{k+1}&\text{($(R\!\wedge\!S)\!\vee\!(R\!\wedge\!T)$ transitive)}\\
=((R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T))\vee(R\wedge S)&\text{($\,(k+1)$-permutability)}\\
=(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T).\end{array}

∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be an
n
-permutable category (
n\geqslant 2
). Then
\mathbb{C}
is equivalence distributive if and only
\mathbb{C}
is a Jónsson category (of order
n-1
).

Proof.

Note that for
n=2
, the statement claims the known fact: a regular Mal’tsev category is equivalence distributive if and only if it is a majority category (Corollary 3.2 in
[
18
]
).

Let
R,S,T\in\mathsf{Eq}(A)
, for some object
A
of
\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that
\mathbb{C}
is a Jónsson category (of any order, in this implication). By Proposition
3.11
we get

R\wedge(S\vee T)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{join in n-permutable cat}}}{{=}}R\wedge(S,T)_{n}\leqslant(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T).

Conversely, if
\mathbb{C}
is equivalence distributive, then

R\wedge(S\circ T)\leqslant R\wedge(S,T)_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{join in n-permutable cat}}}{{=}}R\wedge(S\vee T)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{distributivity}}}{{=}}(R\wedge S)\vee(R\wedge T)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{join in n-permutable cat}}}{{=}}(R\wedge S,R\wedge T)_{n}.

By Definition
3.6
,
\mathbb{C}
is a Jónsson category of order
n-1
.
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

If
\mathbb{C}
is a Jónsson category of any order, then
\mathbb{C}
satisfies the trapezoid lemma.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbb{C}
is a Jónsson category of order
n
. Let
R,S,T\in\mathsf{Eq}(A)
, for some object
A
of
\mathbb{C}
, such that
R\wedge S\leqslant T
. We may apply (
7
) to get that

R\wedge(S\circ T)\leqslant(R\wedge S,R\wedge T)_{n+1}\leqslant T,

the last inequality following from the property that both

and

are contained in

. Similarly, we have that
R\wedge(T\circ S)\leqslant T
. We prove (

)

\begin{array}[]{ll}R\wedge((S\circ T)\circ S)\\
\leqslant((R\wedge(T\circ S))\circ(R\wedge(S\circ T)),R\wedge S)_{n+1}&\text{(by \eqref{Jonsson pp for vars}, $(S\circ T)^{\mathsf{op}}=T\circ S$, $S^{\mathsf{op}}=S$)}\\
\leqslant T.\\
\end{array}

∎

Recall from
[

]
that a variety of universal algebras

satisfies the

if for every three congruences

on any algebra

in

the a diagrammatic condition similar to that of (

. Any regular Mal’tsev or Goursat category satisfy the shifting lemma

that regular Mal’tsev or Goursat categories can be characterised by stronger variations of the shifting lemma. The shifting lemma is implied by the trapezoid lemma (even for finitely complete categories), so that every Jónsson category

is necessarily a Gumm category (by Proposition

that binary products commute with coequalisers locally (in the sense of

Remark 3.14
.

There are examples of Gumm categories that are not Jónsson categories. Indeed, any regular Mal’tsev category which is not equivalence distributive, is not a Jónsson category of any order. This follows from the proof of Proposition

of abelian groups,

Definition 3.15
.

Let
A
be an object in a category
\mathbb{C}
, then a
factor relation

F
on
A
is any equivalence relation on
A
of the form
\mathsf{Eq}(p)
where

is a product diagram. The

will be called a

.

Proposition 3.16
.

For any Jónsson category
\mathbb{C}
of any order, given
F,S,T\in\mathsf{Eq}(A)
where
F
is a factor relation, we have

(F\circ S)\wedge(F\circ T)=F\circ(S\wedge T).

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbb{C}
is Jónsson of order
n
for some
n\geqslant 1
. Note that since
\mathbb{C}
is factor permutable, the relation
F\circ S
is an equivalence relation, so that we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}(F\circ S)\wedge(F\circ T)\\
\leqslant((F\circ S)\wedge F,(F\circ S)\wedge T)_{n+1}&\text{(by \eqref{Jonsson pp})}\\
\leqslant(F,(F\wedge T,S\wedge T)_{n+1})_{n+1}&\text{($(F\circ S)\wedge F\leqslant F$, \eqref{Jonsson pp} applied to $(F\circ S)\wedge T$)}\\
\leqslant(F,(F,S\wedge T)_{n+1})_{n+1}&\text{($F\wedge T\leqslant F$)}\\
=F\circ(S\wedge T),&\text{($F\circ(S\wedge T)=(S\wedge T)\circ F$ and $F$ is transitive)}\\
\end{array}

The inclusion
F\circ(S\wedge T)\leqslant(F\circ S)\wedge(F\circ T)
is trivial.
∎

Proposition 3.17
.

If
\mathbb{C}
is a Barr-exact Jónsson category of any order, then the set of factor relations
\mathsf{F}(A)
, for any object
A
of
\mathbb{C}
, forms a Boolean algebra under
\wedge
and
\circ
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be Jónsson of order
n\geqslant 1
and suppose that
F,G\in\mathsf{F}(A)
. Then we show that
F\wedge G
and
F^{\prime}\circ G^{\prime}
are complementary factor relations. By (
7
), we have

(F\wedge G)\wedge(F^{\prime}\circ G^{\prime})\leqslant(F\wedge G\wedge F^{\prime},F\wedge G\wedge G^{\prime})_{n+1}\leqslant(F\wedge F^{\prime},G\wedge G^{\prime})_{n+1}=\Delta_{A},

since
F\wedge F^{\prime}=\Delta_{A}=G\wedge G^{\prime}
, as complementary factor relations.
Moreover, applying Proposition
3.16
we have

(F\wedge G)\circ(F^{\prime}\circ G^{\prime})=((F\wedge G)\circ F^{\prime})\circ G^{\prime}=((F\circ F^{\prime})\wedge(G\circ F^{\prime}))\circ G^{\prime}=G\circ F^{\prime}\circ G^{\prime}=\nabla_{A},

since
F\circ F^{\prime}=\nabla_{A}=G\circ G^{\prime}
, as complementary factor relations. Therefore we have that
F\wedge G
and
F^{\prime}\circ G^{\prime}
complementary factor relations, so that
\mathsf{F}(A)
is closed under
\wedge
and
\circ
, and then by Proposition
3.16
it is a complemented distributive lattice under these terms.
∎

The above proposition shows that in any Barr-exact Jónsson category, every object with global support is

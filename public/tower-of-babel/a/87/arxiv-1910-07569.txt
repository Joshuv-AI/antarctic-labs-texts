Level lowering on Siegel modular threefold of paramodular level
Haining Wang

Abstract
. In this article we prove several level lowering results for cuspidal automorphic representations occurring in the cohomology of the Siegel modular threefold with paramodular level structure by adapting a method of Ribet in his proof of the Serre’s epsilon conjecture. The proof is purely geometric and relies on the description of the supersingular locus of certain quaternionic unitary Shimura variety and an arithmetic level raising result on this Shimura variety. The heart of the proof is a comparison of the dimension of the space of vanishing cycles on the Siegel modular threefold with paramodular level structure with that on the quaternionic unitary Shimura variety.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivations

In this article we prove three level lowering principles for Galois representations attached to cuspidal automorphic representation of the symplectic similitude group
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
of degree
4
over
\mathbb{Q}
. These results are inspired by the celebrated work of Ribet on Serre’s epsilon conjecture
[
Rib90
]
,
[
Rib91
]
. To motivate our results, we first recall these results in the setting of modular forms. Let
l\geq 3
be a prime and we fix an isomorphism
\iota_{l}:\mathbb{C}\cong\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
. Denote by
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. Let

\rho:\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k)

be an absolutely irreducible representation of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
valued in a finite field
k
of characteristic
l
. Suppose that
\rho
is modular of level
\mathrm{N}
which means
\rho
comes as the residual representation of the
\lambda
-adic representation

\rho_{f,\lambda}:\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(E_{\lambda})

attached to a weight
2
newform
f
of level
\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{N})
. Here
E
is the Hecke field of
f
and
\lambda
is the place in
E
over
l
induced by
\iota_{l}
. Let
p
be a prime which divides
\mathrm{N}
exactly once and suppose that
\rho
is unramified at
p
. Then Serre
[
Ser87
]
conjectured that
\rho
is modular of level
\mathrm{N}/p
. This conjecture is known as the Serre’s epsilon conjecture. The following theorem is the main result of
[
Rib90
]
.

Theorem

(Serre’s epsilon conjecture)
.

Assume
\rho
is modular of level
\mathrm{N}
and is unramified at
p
with
p\mid\mid\mathrm{N}
. Then
\rho
is modular of level
\mathrm{N}/p
if
\mathrm{N}
is prime to
l
.

The proof of the above theorem in fact breaks into two parts. First one proves the following theorem which is known as the Mazur’s principle.

Theorem

(Mazur’s principle)
.

Assume
\rho
is modular of level
\mathrm{N}
and unramified at
p
with
p\mid\mid\mathrm{N}
. Then
\rho
is modular of level
\mathrm{N}/p
if
\mathrm{N}
is prime to
l
and
p\not\equiv 1\mod l
.

The assumption that
p\not\equiv 1\mod l
means that the two Frobenius eigenvalues of
\rho
at
p
are distinct modulo
l
. In this case, the geometric argument simplifies significantly and one can prove this theorem by analyzing the bad reduction of a suitable modular curve. To treat the case when
p\equiv 1\mod l
, one chooses an auxiliary prime
q
such that
q\not\equiv 1\mod l
and raise the level of
f
to
\Gamma_{0}(q\mathrm{N})
. Then one can regard
f
as a modular form on the indefinite quaternion algebra
\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{B}_{pq}
that is ramified at
pq
by the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence. Then the so-called
(p,q)
-switch trick allows one to drop the level at
p
and the Mazur’s principle allows one to drop the level at
q
. The original proof in
[
Rib90
]
relies on certain multiplicity one results on the Galois representations appearing in the torison points of the Jacobian of the modular and Shimura curves which are hard to generalize to other Shimura varieties. The reliance on these multiplicity one results are lifted in
[
Rib91
]
and in the same article Ribet proves the following theorem which we will refer to as the Ribet’s principle.

Theorem

(Ribet’s principle)
.

Let
\mathrm{N}
be a positive integer. Let
p,q
be two distinct primes which are coprime to
l\mathrm{N}
. Assume
\rho
is modular of level
pq\mathrm{N}
. Assume that

\rho
is unramified at
p
but ramified at
q
. Then
\rho
is modular of level
q\mathrm{N}
.

The existence of the auxiliary prime
q
allows Ribet to use the forementioned
(p,q)
-switch trick in a simplified way. The crucial observation for the
(p,q)
-switch trick is that the singular locus of a Shimura curve associated to a suitable indefinite quaternion algebra and the singular locus of a suitable modular curve can be parametrized by the same discrete Shimura set. We make this observation more precise now. Let
\mathbb{A}
be the ring of adèles over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{B}_{pq}
be an indefinite quaternion algebra with discriminant
pq
. Let
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})
(for the moment) be the Shimura curve associated to
\mathrm{B}
with level
\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{N})
and let
\mathrm{X}_{0}(pq)
be the usual modular curve with
\Gamma_{0}(pq\mathrm{N})
level structure. Consider the special fiber

\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})
of
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})
at
p
. The singular locus of
\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})
can be identified with the double coset

\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\overline{\mathrm{B}})=\overline{\mathrm{B}}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\overline{\mathrm{B}}^{\times}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty)})/\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(pq)\mathrm{U}_{0}(\mathrm{N})

where
\overline{\mathrm{B}}=\mathrm{B}_{q\infty}
is the definite quaternion algebra with discriminant
q
,
\mathrm{U}_{0}(\mathrm{N})
is the tame level structure corresponding to
\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{N})
and
\mathrm{U}^{\prime}(pq)={\rm{Iw}}(p)\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{\mathrm{D}^{\prime}}
where
{\rm{Iw}}(p)
is the Iwahori subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{D}^{\prime}}
is the maximal order of
\mathrm{D}^{\prime}=\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{q}
. On the other hand, the singular locus of
\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{0}(pq)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
at the prime
q
can be identified with the same double coset. Therefore the vanishing cycles on
\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
and on
\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{0}(pq)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
can be identified. The starting point of this article is the observation that the same phenomenon happens for the paramodular Siegel threefold and its quaternionic analogue.

1.2.
Main results

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A})
which is of general type in the sense of Arthur
[
Art04
]
and whose weight is
(k_{1},k_{2})
satisfying
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 3
and
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\mod 2
. Then one can attach a Galois representation

\rho_{\pi,\lambda}:\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(E_{\lambda})

for a finite extension
E_{\lambda}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
. We will denote by

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}:\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(k)

its associated residual representation valued in the residue field
k
of
E_{\lambda}
. In fact, this Galois representation is realized in the middle degree cohomology of the Siegel modular threefold. More precisely, it is realized in
\Hom_{\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty)})}(\pi_{f},\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{V}))
where
\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{V})=\varinjlim_{\mathrm{U}}\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{U}}\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{V})
is the compact support cohomology of the Siegel modular threefold indexed by the open compact subgroups
\mathrm{U}
of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty)})
and
\mathbb{V}
is a suitable étale local system which we will make it more precise in the main body of the article. Recall that there are two maximal parahoric subgroups of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
up to isomorphism, one is known as the hyperspecial subgroup which we will denote by
\mathrm{H}
and the other is known as the paramodular subgroup which we will denote by
\mathrm{Pa}(p)
.
Suppose that the local component of
\pi
at
p
is paraspherical and ramified which means that
\pi
is of type
\mathrm{IIa}
in the table of Schmidt
[
Schm05
]
. We will let
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{Pa}(p)\mathrm{U}^{(p)}
where
\mathrm{U}^{(p)}\subset\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty p)})
such that
\mathrm{U}
is a neat open compact subgroup such that
\pi^{\mathrm{U}}\neq 0
. Let
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)=\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{U}}
be the Siegel modular threefold with paramodular level at
p
. Let
\mathbb{T}
be a suitable Hecke algebra acting on the middle degree cohomology of
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)
and
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal in
\mathbb{T}
corresponding to
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
. Our first result is the analogue of Mazur’s principle for Galois representations occurring in the middle degree cohomology of
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A})
which is of general type and whose weight is
(k_{1},k_{2})
satisfying
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 3
and
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\mod 2
. We assume that
l+1\geq k_{1}+k_{2}
. Let
p
be a prime distinct from
l
and such that
p\not\equiv 1\mod l
. Let
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{Pa}(p)\mathrm{U}^{p}\subset\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty p)})
be a neat open compact subgroup such that
\pi^{\mathrm{U}}\neq 0
. We assume that
\pi_{p}
is ramified of type
\mathrm{IIa}
in the table of Schmidt. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal associated to
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
. Suppose the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
satisfies the following assumptions.

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is unramified at
p
;

(2)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is absolutely irreducible and the image of
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{l})
;

(3)

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is concentrated in degree
3
;

(4)

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k
is semisimple as a Galois module.

Then there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation
\breve{\pi}
with the same weight as
\pi
such that

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}\cong\overline{\rho}_{\breve{\pi},\lambda}

and such that
\breve{\pi}_{p}
is an unramified principal series.

We remark that in a pioneering work
[
VH19
]
, van Hoften was able to prove the Mazur’s principle in this setting with different assumptions. In particular we are not relying on the vanishing results of Lan–Suh
[
LS13
]
and thus we allow more general weights for the étale local system
\mathbb{V}
. In addition, we do not require that the Frobenius eigenvalues of
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
at
p
to be distinct instead we only assume that
p\not\equiv 1\mod l
. However we introduce the additional assumption that
\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k
is semisimple. The assumption is the analogue of the main result of
[
BLR91
]
. The proof of
[
BLR91
]
relies on the fact the representation of interest is two dimensional and therefore their methods do not generalize directly to our setting. We also introduce the additional assumption
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is concentrated in degree
3
. This holds true if we impose further conditions on the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
or on the weights of
\mathbb{V}
. We will discuss this point in Remark
6.2
. The most important step in proving the Mazur’s principle is to show that the cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
vanishes. For this, we follow the strategy of
[
VH19
]
closely. Comparing to our work
[
Wang22
]
in quaternionic setting, the reason for this vanishing seems to be the lack of Tate cycles coming from the supersingular locus. Indeed, the supersingular locus of
\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)
is one dimensional and thus can not contribute to the degree
2
cohomology. This is in contrast to the case of the quaternionic unitary Shimura variety which we will discuss later.

Next we state our result on Ribet’s principle. For this, let
p,q
be two distinct primes which are different from
l
but now
p
could be congruent to
1
modulo
l
. We let
\mathrm{U}^{pq}
be an open compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty pq)})
such that
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{Pa}(p)\mathrm{Pa}(q)\mathrm{U}^{(pq)}
is a neat open compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty)})
such that
\pi^{\mathrm{U}}\neq 0
. We consider the Siegel modular threefold
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)=\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{U}}
with paramodular level at
p
and
q
. We will refer to following theorem as the Ribet’s principle.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A})
of general type and whose weight is
(k_{1},k_{2})
satisfying
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 3
and
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\mod 2
. We assume that
l+1\geq k_{1}+k_{2}
. Let
p,q
be two distinct primes different from
l
. Let
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{Pa}(p)\mathrm{Pa}(q)\mathrm{U}^{pq}
be a neat open compact subgroup such that
\pi^{\mathrm{U}}\neq 0
. We assume that
\pi_{p}
and
\pi_{q}
are both ramified of type
\mathrm{IIa}
. Suppose the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
satisfies the following assumptions.

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is unramified at
p
with generic Hecke parameters;

(2)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is ramified at
q
;

(3)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is absolutely irreducible and
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{l})
;

(4)

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
are concentrated in degree
3
;

(5)

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k
is a semisimple Galois module.

Then there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation
\breve{\pi}
with the same weight as
\pi
and such that

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}\cong\overline{\rho}_{\breve{\pi},\lambda}

and
\breve{\pi}_{p}
is an unramified principal series.

Although we allow
p\equiv 1\mod l
, we need to impose a mild assumption called generic on the Hecke parameters of
\pi
at
p
. The precise definition of this assumption will be given in Definition
8.2
. The proof of this theorem relies heavily on the interplay between the geometry of the Siegel modular threefold
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)
and its quaternionic analogue
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})
. Here the quaternion algebra
\mathrm{B}
is again the indefinite quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
with discriminant
pq
as in the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-case but
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})
is now the Shimura variety associated to the quaternionic unitary group
\mathrm{GU}_{2}(\mathrm{B})
of degree
2
. We will rely on the description of the supersingular locus of
\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
in
[
Wang19a
]
which is also obtained by Oki
[
Oki22
]
independently. In particular the starting point of this work is the observation that the singular locus of
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
and the singular locus
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
are parametrized by the same discrete Shimura set and therefore one can identify the space of vanishing cycles on
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
and the space of vanishing cycles on
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
. Under this observation, we assume that the level of
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
can not be lowered and we compare the dimension of the space of vanishing cycles on
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)
and
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})
at the primes
p
and
q
to derive a contradiction. We will also use the fact that in this case the Tate cycles coming from the supersingular locus indeed generate the space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
once the Hecke parameters of
\pi
is generic. This is in contrast to the situation of
\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)
mentioned before. Indeed, the supersingular locus of
\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})
is two dimensional and hence does contribute to
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V})
.

Note that the auxiliary assumption that
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is ramified at
q
simplify the situation significantly but can be removed once we impose further assumptions on the residual Galois representation. This is the content of the main level lowering theorem in this article.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A})
of general type and whose weight is
(k_{1},k_{2})
with
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 3
and
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\mod 2
. Suppose
l+1\geq k_{1}+k_{2}
. Let
p
be a prime different from
l
. Let
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{Pa}(p)\mathrm{U}^{(p)}
with
\mathrm{U}^{(p)}\subset\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty p)})
such that
\mathrm{U}
is a neat open compact subgroup such that
\pi^{\mathrm{U}}\neq 0
. Suppose that
\pi_{p}
is ramified of type
\mathrm{IIa}
. Suppose also that the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
satisfies the following assumptions.

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is unramified with generic Hecke parameters at
p
;

(2)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is rigid as in Definition
9.1
;

(3)

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
are concentrated in degree
i=3
;

(4)

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k
is a semisimple Galois module;

(5)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is absolutely irreducible and the image of
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{l})
.

Then there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation
\breve{\pi}
of general type with the same weight as
\pi
such that

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}\cong\overline{\rho}_{\breve{\pi},\lambda}

and
\breve{\pi}_{p}
is an unramified principal series.

To prove this theorem, first we exploit the level raising theorem in
[
Wang22
, Theorem 9.9]
to obtain an automorphic representation which is congruent to
\pi
and occurs in the cohomology of
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})
as well as in the cohomology of

\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)
. Then we proceed similarly as in the proof of the Ribet’s principle to calculate the dimensions of the spaces of vanishing cycles at
p
and
q
for these Shimura varieties. This calculation is complicated by the fact that the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
corresponding to
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
occurs both in the space of
q
-newforms and
q
-oldforms. Fortunately, our main arithmetic level raising result on
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})

[
Wang22
]
which is finer than the level raising theorem exactly enables us to overcome this difficulty.

1.3.
Survey of level lowering results

We survey some results in the literature on level lowering theorems. We do not attempt to exhaust all the results in this direction but only those the author is aware of.

•

In the case of Hilbert modular forms, Jarvis
[
Jar99
]
proved Mazur’s principle for Shimura curves over totally real field and Rajaei
[
Raj01
]
generalizes Ribet’s method in this setting. See also the work of Fujiwara
[
Fuji06
]
in this direction. Note that there are also other techniques to prove level lowering results for Hilbert modular forms, notably the work of Skinner–Wiles
[
SW01
]
proves a potential level lowering theorem.

•

In the setting of Kottwitz–Harris–Taylor type Shimura varieties, the Mazur’s principle is proved for the
\mathrm{U}(2,1)
-case by Helm in
[
Hel06
]
and by Boyer
[
Boy19
]
for the
\mathrm{U}(n-1,1)
-case.

•

In the setting of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
, Sorensen
[
Sor09a
]
is able to prove a potential level lowering result using the techniques of
[
SW01
]
. More precisely, he establishes a congruence between representations that admits Iwahori fixed vectors and certain tamely ramified principal series.

•

Using techniques from modularity lifting theorems, one can also prove level raising and level lowering theorems. For automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
over totally real fields, one can deduce level lowering results from the main theorem of
[
GG12
]
. See also
[
Gee11
]
for the case of Hilbert modular forms and the case for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

Nearby and vanishing cycles

Let

&&cb^{-1}&\ast\\
&&&ca^{-1}\\

This is irreducible if and only if none of the characters

is the normalized valuation such that

]

]

of

Let

3.6.
Cohomology of Siegel modular threefold

In this subsection, we define the Siegel modular threefold and various cohomology theories related to automorphic forms. Let

\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
-stable

. We review the construction of an étale local system

on

We denote its associated

-local system by

-local system by

•

The

The

The

5.
Cohomological vanishing for paramodular Siegel threefold

In order to prove the Mazur’s principle, we need to analyze the Picard–Lefschetz formula for the nearby cycle cohomology

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V})).

For this purpose, we will prove a vanishing result for the non-middle-degree cohomology of the special fiber
\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)
when localized at a non-Eisenstein maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{T}
. This result essentially follows from the work of van Hoften in
[
VH19
]
but we give a slightly different exposition. We define a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
to be non-Eisenstein if the natural map

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}

is an isomorphism for
\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{H}}
or
\mathrm{X}=\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)
.

Remark 5.1
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{m}
is associated to a residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
. It is known from
[
MT14
, Corollary 3]
that if
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
satisfies the assumption that the image of
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{l})
and that
l+1\geq k_{1}+k_{2}
for the weight
(k_{1},k_{2})
of
\pi
. Then
\mathfrak{m}
is indeed non-Eisenstein.

We also impose the following assumption on the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
throughout this section.

The cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(p)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{H}}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
are both concentrated in the middle degree.

This assumption can be verified in the situations discussed in Remark
6.2
.

over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in this case. In fact, let
\mathrm{C}=\mathsf{N}^{\tau=1}
with
\tau=\Pi^{-1}\mathrm{F}
equipped with the restriction of the alternating form

to
\mathrm{C}
. Then we have

We consider the Rapoport–Zink space

for the group

. This is the set valued functor on

which assigns each

•

•

•

\rho_{\mathsf{X}}:\mathbb{X}\times_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\overline{S}\rightarrow\mathsf{X}\times_{S}\overline{S}
is a quasi-isogeny where
\overline{S}
is the special fiber of

at

This functor
\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{B})
is representable by a formal scheme locally formally of finite type over
\mathrm{Spf}(\mathrm{W}_{0})

\mathrm{J}_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Again
\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{B})
can be decomposed into connected components

(7.5)

\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{B})=\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{M}^{(i)}(\mathrm{B})

where
\mathcal{M}^{(i)}(\mathrm{B})
classifies those

the reduced subscheme of the formal scheme

\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{B})
can be described by the Bruhat–Tits stratification. A
vertex lattice

L
in the symplectic space
\mathrm{C}
is by definition a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice
L\subset\mathrm{C}
such that

pL^{\vee}\subset L\subset L^{\vee}

where

is the integral dual of

in
\mathrm{C}
. Let

be the set of vertex lattices of type

, they are by definition given by

\mathcal{L}_{\{0\}}=\{\text{vertex lattice $L_{0}$ of type $0$ in $\mathrm{C}$: }pL^{\vee}_{0}\subset^{4}L_{0}\subset^{0}L^{\vee}_{0}\}
;

\mathcal{L}_{\{2\}}=\{\text{vertex lattice $L_{2}$ of type $2$ in $\mathrm{C}$: }pL^{\vee}_{2}\subset^{0}L_{2}\subset^{4}L^{\vee}_{2}\}

\mathcal{L}_{\{1\}}=\{\text{vertex lattice $L_{1}$ of type $1$ in $\mathrm{C}$: }pL^{\vee}_{1}\subset^{2}L_{1}\subset^{2}L^{\vee}_{1}\}

Each vertex lattice gives rise to a maximal parahoric subgroup in

by taking the stabilizer of the lattice.

•

A vertex lattice
L_{0}
of type

corresponds to a hyperspecial subgroup
\mathrm{K}_{\{0\}}
;

•

A vertex lattice
L_{2}
of type
2
corresponds to a hyperspecial subgroup
\mathrm{K}_{\{2\}}
. It is conjugate to
\mathrm{K}_{\{0\}}
. When we need to identify them, we write them commonly as
\mathrm{H}

•

A vertex lattices
L_{1}
of type

;

•

A pair
(L_{0},L_{2})
of vertex lattices of type

and type

\mathrm{Sie}(p)
;

•

A pair
(L_{0},L_{1})
of vertex lattices of type
0
and
1
corresponds to the Klingen parahoric subgroup
\mathrm{Kl}(p)
.

Each vertex lattice gives rise to a
lattice stratum
which is a projective subscheme of
\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{B})
, see
[

, Theorem 5.1]
.

•

For a vertex lattice

of type

\mathrm{Z}^{p}_{3}\mathrm{Z}_{0}-\mathrm{Z}^{p}_{0}\mathrm{Z}_{3}+\mathrm{Z}^{p}_{2}\mathrm{Z}_{1}-\mathrm{Z}^{p}_{1}\mathrm{Z}_{2}=0

for a projective coordinate
[\mathrm{Z}_{0}:\mathrm{Z}_{1}:\mathrm{Z}_{2}:\mathrm{Z}_{3}]
of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
;

•

For a vertex lattice
L_{2}
of type
2

\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{B})\{L_{2}\}
is a projective surface of the form

\mathrm{Z}^{p}_{3}\mathrm{Z}_{0}-\mathrm{Z}^{p}_{0}\mathrm{Z}_{3}+\mathrm{Z}^{p}_{2}\mathrm{Z}_{1}-\mathrm{Z}^{p}_{1}\mathrm{Z}_{2}=0

for a projective coordinate

For a vertex lattice

of type

\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{B})\{L_{1}\}
consists of a superspecial point.

Therefore there are two types of irreducible components of
\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{B})

The Rapoport–Zink uniformization theorem

in this case gives rise to an isomorphism

which ramifies at

Since

Proposition 7.7
.

We have the following description of the supersingular locus

and the singular locus

\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})
.

(1)

\overline{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{ss}}(\mathrm{B})
can be parametrized by the union of the two Shimura sets

and
\mathrm{Z}_{\{2\}}(\overline{\mathrm{B}})

(2)

\Sigma_{p}(\mathrm{B})

\mathrm{Z}_{\{1\}}(\overline{\mathrm{B}})
.

Recall there are two kinds of irreducible components of
\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{B})
and they correspond to vertex lattices of type
0
and vertex lattice of type

. Therefore by (

) the irreducible components of type

One then sees that this is exactly

\mathrm{Z}_{\{0\}}(\overline{\mathrm{B}})=\mathrm{G}(\overline{\mathrm{B}})(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathrm{G}(\overline{\mathrm{B}})(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty)})/\mathrm{K}_{\{0\}}\mathrm{U}^{p}.

One then sees that this is exactly

One then sees that this is exactly

8.
Ribet’s principle for paramodular Siegel threefold

8.1.
The Ribet’s principle

Now we come to state the second main theorem of this article which we call the Ribet’s principle for the paramodular Siegel threefold. We will make some similar assumptions as in the Mazur’s principle concerning the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
. We fix a
\mu\in\mathrm{X}^{\ast}(\mathrm{T})
and denote
\mathbb{V}_{\mu}
simply by
\mathbb{V}
.

Assumption 8.1
.

We will make the following assumptions throughout this section.

(1)

The residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is absolutely irreducible and the image of
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{l})
.

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal associated to
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
as explained in (
3.15
), we assume that

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}

and

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}

vanishes unless
i=3
.

(3)

The cohomology group

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k

is semisimple as a Galois module. This implies that we have an identification of Galois modules

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k=\overline{\rho}^{\oplus s}_{\pi,\lambda}

for some positive number
s
.

We introduce the following notion of generic Hecke parameters at
p
. This will be used when we apply some geometric results in
[
Wang22
]
.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal corresponding to
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
. Then we say
\mathfrak{m}
is generic at
p
if the Hecke parameters
[\alpha_{p},\beta_{p},p^{3}\beta^{-1},p^{3}\alpha^{-1}_{p}]
of
\pi_{p}
satisfy

\alpha_{p}+\beta_{p}+p^{3}\alpha^{-1}_{p}+p^{3}\beta^{-1}_{p}\pm 2p(p+1)\not\equiv 0\mod\lambda.

If this is the case, we also say
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}|_{\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is generic.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A})
which is of general type with weight
(k_{1},k_{2})
satisfying
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 3
and
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\mod 2
. Let
p,q
be two distinct primes different from
l
. Let
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{Pa}(p)\mathrm{Pa}(q)\mathrm{U}^{(pq)}
with
\mathrm{U}^{(pq)}\subset\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{\infty pq})
such that
\mathrm{U}
is a neat open compact subgroup such that
\pi^{\mathrm{U}}\neq 0
. Suppose that
\pi_{p}
and
\pi_{q}
are both ramified of type
\mathrm{IIa}
in the table of Schmidt. Suppose also that the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
satisfies the following assumptions:

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
satisfies Assumption
8.1
;

(2)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}|_{\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is unramified and generic;

(3)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}|_{\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{q}}}
is ramified.

Then there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation
\breve{\pi}
of general type with the same weight as
\pi
such that

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}\cong\overline{\rho}_{\breve{\pi},\lambda}

and such that
\breve{\pi}_{p}
is an unramified principal series.

8.3.
Proof of Ribet’s principle

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal corresponding to
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
as in (

). Then by our semisimple assumption, we have

(8.8)

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k=\overline{\rho}^{\oplus s}_{\pi,\lambda}

for some positive integer

. By the global Jacquet–Langlands correspondence for
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}

[
RW19
]
, we have a non-canonical injection

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{V}_{E_{\lambda}})\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V}_{E_{\lambda}}).

The subspace
\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{V}_{E_{\lambda}})
is preserved by the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
, the Atkin–Lehner operators
\mathrm{u}_{p},\mathrm{u}_{q}
and the Galois action. By Assumption
8.1
, the cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{V})
and
\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})
are both torsion free. Thus the above injection upgrades to

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{V})\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V}).

Therefore the semisimple assumption of
\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})\otimes k
also implies that of
\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k
and thus

(8.9)

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k=\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}^{\oplus t}

for some non-negative integer
t
. Note that

is positive in our case, since in the decomposition

(8.10)

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes E_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\vec{\pi}}\rho^{m(\vec{\pi})}_{\vec{\pi},\lambda}

where
\vec{\pi}
runs through all the representations that is congruent to
\pi
, there is at least one
\vec{\pi}
such that
\vec{\pi}_{p}
and
\vec{\pi}_{q}
are both ramified. Our strategy is to prove the theorem by contradiction
and therefore we assume that all the representation
\vec{\pi}
appear in the following equations

(8.11)

\begin{split}&\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes E_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\vec{\pi}}\rho^{n(\vec{\pi})}_{\vec{\pi},\lambda}\\
&\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes E_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\vec{\pi}}\rho^{m(\vec{\pi})}_{\vec{\pi},\lambda}\\
\end{split}

are
ramified
at
p
. As we have assumed that
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is ramified at
q
, all
\vec{\pi}_{q}
is necessarily ramified. Therefore it follows that in particular
t
in (
8.9
) is positive.

Proposition 8.12
.

Suppose that all the
\vec{\pi}
are ramified in (
8.11
). The map
\mathrm{ca}

\mathrm{ca}:\mathrm{H}^{3}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}))_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}(\mathrm{B})}\mathrm{R}^{3}\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k

localized at
\mathfrak{m}
is surjective and by duality the map
\mathrm{coca}

\mathrm{coca}:\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}(\mathrm{B})}\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\{\sigma\}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}))_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}))_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k

localized at
\mathfrak{m}
is injective.

Proof.

Note that the kernel of

\mathrm{coca}:\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}(\mathrm{B})}\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\{\sigma\}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}))_{\mathfrak{m}}\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}))_{\mathfrak{m}},

is given by
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
and we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{H}}(\overline{\mathrm{B}}),\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\oplus\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{H}}(\overline{\mathrm{B}}),\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}

by Theorem

.
But the right hand side supports only unramified representations and therefore vanishes by our assumption in the statement of this proposition. Since

\mathrm{coca}:\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}(\mathrm{B})}\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\{\sigma\}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}))_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}))_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k

\mathrm{H}^{4}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(\mathrm{B}),\mathbb{V}_{k})_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Note that, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{4}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V}_{k})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{4}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V}_{k})

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}^{\mathrm{sm}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V}_{k})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V}_{k})

by considering the excision long exact sequence. Then Poincaré duality for

implies that
\mathrm{H}^{4}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\mathbb{V}_{k})_{\mathfrak{m}}

∎

Proof of Theorem
8.3
.

Recall that in Corollary
7.11
, we have proved that
\Sigma_{p}(\mathrm{B})
and
\Sigma_{q}(\mathrm{Pa}(pq))
can be identified. Thus we have
s=2t
by the previous two propositions. Note also that if we switch the role of
p
and
q
in the previous discussions, we will obtain that

\begin{split}&\dim_{k}\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}(\mathrm{Pa}(pq))}\mathrm{R}^{3}\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k=2s;\\
&\dim_{k}\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{q}(\mathrm{B})}\mathrm{R}^{3}\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k=t.\\
\end{split}

By Corollary

9.2.
The main level lowering theorem

The goal of this subsection is to prove the following theorem which is an analogue of Serre’s epsilon conjecture for
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
and is the main level lowering theorem in this article.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A})
of general type which is of weight
(k_{1},k_{2})
satisfying
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 3
and
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\mod 2
. Suppose
l+1\geq k_{1}+k_{2}
. Let
p
be a prime different from
l
. Let
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{Pa}(p)\mathrm{U}^{(p)}
with
\mathrm{U}^{(p)}\subset\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}^{(\infty p)})
such that
\mathrm{U}
is a neat open compact subgroup such that
\pi^{\mathrm{U}}\neq 0
. Suppose that
\pi_{p}
is ramified of type
\mathrm{IIa}
in the table of Schmidt. Suppose also that the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
satisfies the following assumptions.

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
satisfies Assumption
8.1
;

(2)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is rigid for
(\Sigma_{\mathrm{min}},\Sigma_{\mathrm{lr}})
as in Definition
9.1
;

(3)

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}
is absolutely irreducible and the image of
\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{l})
;

Then there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation
\breve{\pi}
of general type with the same weight as
\pi
such that

\overline{\rho}_{\pi,\lambda}\cong\overline{\rho}_{\breve{\pi},\lambda}

and
\breve{\pi}_{p}
is an unramified principal series representation.

The strategy to prove this theorem is similar to that of Ribet’s proof of the original epsilon conjecture. First by using Chebotarev density theorem and the large image assumption, we can find a prime
q
which is level raising special for
\pi
. We apply Corollary
9.4
to

Proposition 9.9
.

We have the following equalities

\dim_{k}\bigoplus\limits_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{q}(\mathrm{Pa}(pq))}\mathrm{R}^{3}\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k=s.

and

\dim_{k}\bigoplus\limits_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{q}(\mathrm{B})}\mathrm{R}^{3}\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k=3t

Proof.

To prove the first equality, we consider the specialization exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{3}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{3}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}))_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{q}(\mathrm{Pa}(pq))}\mathrm{R}^{3}\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k\rightarrow 0.

Note that
q
is a level raising special prime, the Hecke parameters of
\pi
are distinct modulo
\lambda
, thus the Frobenius eigenvalues are distinct modulo
\lambda
. Since the three Frobenius eigenvalues already appear in
\mathrm{H}^{3}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{Pa}}(pq)\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k
, the dimension equality follows.

We now prove the second equality. The same argument as above proves the equality

\dim_{k}\ker(\alpha)_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k=t.

By definition and torsion freeness of
\mathrm{H}^{4}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}
in
[
Wang22
, Corollary 9.8]
, we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\ker(\alpha)\otimes k\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{q}(\mathrm{B})}\mathrm{R}^{3}\Phi_{\sigma}(\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{4}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{V})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes k\rightarrow 0.

Since we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{4}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{V}_{k})_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{H}}({\mathrm{B}^{\prime}}),\mathbb{V}_{k})^{\oplus 2}_{\mathfrak{m}}

by
[
Wang22
, Corollary 9.8]
and our main result of arithmetic level raising gives the isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sin}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{2}},\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}_{k})(1))_{\mathfrak{m}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{H}}({\mathrm{B}^{\prime}}),\mathbb{V}_{k}),

note that the role of the two primes
p
and
q
are switched in the statement of Theorem
9.3
.
A simple calculation shows

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{sin}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{2}},\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{\mathrm{X}}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathrm{R}\Psi(\mathbb{V}_{k})(1))_{\mathfrak{m}})

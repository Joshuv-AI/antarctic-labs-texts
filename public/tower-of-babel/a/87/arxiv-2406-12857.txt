Jean-François Delmas, Dylan Dronnier, Pierre-André Zitt

Abstract
. We discuss transformations on matrices that preserve the effective spectrum and/or the effective spectral radius.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
15A18, 15A86

1.
Effective spectral radius of a matrix

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}=\{n>0\,\colon\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.
For
K
a square matrix of size
n
, let
\sspec(K)
and

\rho(K)=\max\{|\lambda|\,\colon\lambda\in\sspec(K)\}
denote
the spectrum and spectral radius of
K
. By the Perron-Frobenius theorem, if
K
has nonnegative entries, then

\rho(K)
is an eigenvalue and thus belongs to

\spec(K)
. For
\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
,
let
\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
denote the diagonal matrix with entries given by

\eta
.
We now define the effective spectrum and
effective spectral radius functions associated to
K
.

Definition 1
.

Let
K
be a square matrix of size
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. The effective spectrum

\spec[K]

and effective spectral radius
R_{e}[K]
functions are defined
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
by:

\spec[K](\eta)=\sspec(K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta))\quad\text{and}\quad R_{e}[K](\eta)=\rho(K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta))\quad\text{for}\quad\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}.

Trivially, two matrices with the same
effective spectrum have the same effective spectral radius.

Motivated by the quantitative effect of vaccination strategies on the
reproduction number in epidemic models, see

[
2
, Theorem 7.4]
or

[
3
]
in a more general
framework, we give in
[
4
]

examples of transformations on positive compact operators that leave
the effective spectral radius and effective spectrum functions
invariant (mainly transposition and diagonal similarity, see below).
The aim of this note is to explore further this invariance property in a
finite dimensional setting, that is, for matrices.

Our main result is a characterization of the equality between
effective spectrum, shown in Section
2
.
Building in
particular on results from
[
6
,
7
]
, we then give
in Section
3
sufficient
conditions for two matrices to have the same effective spectral
radius, and show that they are necessary under various additional
assumptions.

2.
Equivalent conditions for equality

Let us first define some notation.
For
\alpha
and
\beta
non-empty subsets of
\{1,...,n\}
we denote
by
K[\alpha,\beta]
the sub-matrix of
K
obtained by keeping the
lines in
\alpha
and the columns in
\beta
, and let

K[\alpha]=K[\alpha,\alpha]
.
The determinant of
K[\alpha]
is
called a
principal minor
of
K
, its
size
is the cardinal of

\alpha
. It is elementary to check that the characteristic polynomial
of
K
may be written as:

(1)

\chi_{K}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}c_{n-k}t^{k},

where
c_{0}=1
and, for

j\geq 1
,
c_{j}
is the sum of all principal minors of size
j
of
K
.

We now give equivalent conditions
for the effective spectrum and effective spectral radius for two
matrices to be equal.

Theorem 2.1

(Effective spectrum and principal minors)
.

Let
K
and
\tilde{K}
be square matrices of the same size
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
nonnegative entries. The following are equivalent.

(1)

The functions
R_{e}[K]
and
R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
coincide on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
.

(2)

The functions
R_{e}[K]
and
R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
coincide on
\{0,1\}^{n}
.

(3)

The functions
\spec[K]
and
\spec[\tilde{K}]
coincide on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
.

(4)

The functions
\spec[K]
and

\spec[\tilde{K}]
coincide on
\{0,1\}^{n}
.

(5)

All principal minors of
K
and
\tilde{K}
coincide.

For simplicity, we write
\mathscr{E}(n)=\{0,1\}^{n}
.

Proof.

Clearly
3
\implies
1
\implies
2
, and
3
\implies
4
\implies
2
.

Let us check that
5
implies
3
. Assume that all principal
minors of
K
and
\tilde{K}
coincide.
For any vector
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any set
of indices
\alpha
, by multi-linearity of the determinant, we get:

\det\Big((K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta))[\alpha]\Big)=\Big(\prod_{i\in\alpha}\eta_{i}\Big)\det\left(K[\alpha]\right).

Consequently, all principal minors of
K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
and

\tilde{K}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
coincide. By (
1
) this
implies that
K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
and
\tilde{K}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
have the same
spectrum. Thus, Point

3
holds.

Therefore, it is enough to prove that
2
implies

5
. The proof is an induction on the dimension.
The result is clear in dimension
1
. Assume that it holds for
any square matrix with nonnegative entries of dimension smaller
than or equal to
n
. Let
K
and
\tilde{K}
be two square matrices of
dimension
n+1
with nonnegative entries, and assume that
R_{e}[K]
and
R_{e}[\tilde{K}]

coincide on
\mathscr{E}(n+1)
. For any non-empty

\alpha\subset\{1,...,n+1\}
, let
\eta_{\alpha}
be the column
vector
(\mathbb{1}_{\alpha}(i),1\leq i\leq n+1)
, that is, with 1
in position
\alpha
and 0 otherwise. Notice that for
any matrix
K^{\prime}
:

R_{e}[K^{\prime}](\eta_{\alpha})=\rho(K^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta_{\alpha}))=\rho(K^{\prime}[\alpha]).

Fix
\alpha\subset\{1,...,n+1\}
nonempty, with
\alpha\neq\{1,...,n+1\}
. Let
\beta\subset\alpha
and set
\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}=(\mathbb{1}_{\beta}(i),i\in\alpha)
.
We have:

(2)

\displaystyle R_{e}[K^{\prime}[\alpha]](\tilde{\eta}_{\beta})=\rho(K^{\prime}[\alpha]\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\tilde{\eta}_{\beta}))=\rho(K^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta_{\alpha})\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta_{\beta}))

\displaystyle=\rho(K^{\prime}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta_{\beta}))

\displaystyle=R_{e}[K^{\prime}](\eta_{\beta}).

Since
\eta_{\beta}\in\mathscr{E}(n+1)
, we get

R_{e}[K](\eta_{\beta})=R_{e}[\tilde{K}](\eta_{\beta})
for all

\beta\subset\alpha
. We deduce from (
2
) that

R_{e}[K[\alpha]]=R_{e}[\tilde{K}[\alpha]]
on
\mathscr{E}(\card\alpha)
. By
the induction hypothesis the principal minors of
K[\alpha]
and

\tilde{K}[\alpha]
are equal, that is all principal minors of size less
than or equal to
n
of
K
and
\tilde{K}
coincide. It remains to check that
the determinants are the same. Since all principal minors of size less
than or equal to
n
coincide, we deduce
from (
1
) that:

(3)

\chi_{K}(t)-\det(K)=\chi_{\tilde{K}}(t)-\det(\tilde{K}).

Since
K
and
K^{\prime}
have nonnegative entries, by Perron-Frobenius
theorem, their spectral radius
\rho(K)=R_{e}[K](\mathbb{1})
and
\rho(K^{\prime})=R_{e}[K^{\prime}](\mathbb{1})

is also an eigenvalue, and thus a root of their characteristic
polynomial. As
R_{e}[K](\mathbb{1})=R_{e}[K^{\prime}](\mathbb{1})
, we deduce
from (
3
) that
\det(K)=\det(\tilde{K})
. This ends
the proof of the induction step.
∎

According to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have that
5
implies
3
and thus
1
,
2
and
4

without assuming that the entries are nonnegative. We first
investigate whether
1
from Theorem
2.1

implies
5
when the entries of the matrices have
generals signs. Notice that
R_{e}[K]=R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
automatically
implies
K
and
\tilde{K}
have the same entries on the diagonal up to
their sign (evaluate the effective spectral radii on
\eta
with only
one non-zero component). So in order for the equality of the
effective spectral radii of
K
and
\tilde{K}
to imply the equality
of all principal minors, it is necessary to assume that the two
matrices have the same sign on their diagonal, that is,

\sgn(K_{ii})=\sgn(\tilde{K}_{ii})
for all indices
i
. It is however
not enough, see next example and lemma.

Example 2

(Same effective spectral radii do not imply same
principal minors in general)
.

Consider the following two matrices:

K=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{K}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

We have
R_{e}[K]=R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, but, even if
all the principal minors of
size 1 coincide, the principal minor of size two is different.

The key point is in fact the
number of zeroes on the diagonal.

Lemma 3
.

Let
K
and
\tilde{K}
be square matrices of the same size
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with the same sign on their diagonal
and having at most one zero term in their diagonal.
If
R_{e}[K]=R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
(on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
), then all principal minors of
K
and
\tilde{K}
coincide.

Proof.

Mimicking the proof by
induction of
2

\implies
5
from
Theorem
2.1
and assuming
1
, we get by
induction that (
3
) holds for
K
and
\tilde{K}
as well
as for
K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
and
\tilde{K}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
with

\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
(this amounts to multiply all terms
in (
3
) by
\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\eta_{i}
):

\chi_{K\eta}(t)-\det(K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta))=\chi_{\tilde{K}\eta}(t)-\det(\tilde{K}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)).

As there is at most one zero on the diagonal, without loss of
generality (multiplying
K
and
\tilde{K}
by
-1
and using a
permutation of the canonical bassis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
if necessary), we can
assume that
K_{11}=\tilde{K}_{11}=a>0
. Taking

\eta=(1,\varepsilon,\ldots,\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon>0
small
enough, we deduce that the spectral radius of
K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)

(resp.
\tilde{K}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
) is also a simple eigenvalue of

K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
(resp.
\tilde{K}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)
). As

R_{e}[K](\eta)=R_{e}[\tilde{K}](\eta)
, we deduce that

\det(K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta))=\det(\tilde{K}\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta))
and thus

\det(K)=\det(\tilde{K})
. Thus, by induction, all the minors of
K

and
\tilde{K}
coincide.
∎

We now check that
2
from
Theorem
2.1
does not imply
1

or
5
when the entries of the matrices have generals
signs (even with positive entries on the diagonal).

Example 4

(Same effective spectral radii on Boolean vectors do not imply same
effective spectral radius)
.

Consider the
following two matrices:

K=\begin{pmatrix}1&\beta\\
\beta&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{K}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\gamma\\
\gamma&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\gamma>0
and
\beta=\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}-1
. The eigenvalues
of
K
are
\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}
and
2-\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}
; the
eigenvalues of
\tilde{K}
are
1\pm\gamma i
. In particular, the
two matrices have the same spectral radius
\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}
. The
functions
R_{e}[K]
and
R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
clearly coincide on
\mathscr{E}(2)
.
Since
\det(K)\neq\det(\tilde{K})
, we deduce that all the principal
minors of
K
and
\tilde{K}
do not coincide, and thus

R_{e}[K]\neq R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
thanks to Lemma
3
.

3.
Matrices with the same effective spectral radius

Let us first recall a few notions.
The matrix
K
is

irreducible
if
K[\alpha,\alpha^{c}]\neq 0
for all subsets

\alpha
such that
\alpha
and
\alpha^{c}
are non-empty.
The non-empty subset
\alpha
is irreducible for
K

if
K[\alpha]
is irreducible.
Let
\mathscr{A}(K)
be the family of
maximal irreducible sets for the inclusion, and consider the matrix

K^{\mathscr{A}}
given by:

K^{\mathscr{A}}_{ij}=K_{ij}\quad\text{if $i,j\in\alpha$ for some $\alpha\in\mathscr{A}(K)$,}\quad\text{and
$K^{\mathscr{A}}_{ij}=0$ otherwise.}

The elements of
\mathscr{A}(K)
corresponds to the atoms of
K
in
[
4
]
.
The map
K\mapsto K^{\mathscr{A}}
is not linear.

The matrix

K
is
completely reducible
if
K[\alpha,\alpha^{c}]=0
implies

K[\alpha^{c},\alpha]=0
whenever
\alpha
and
\alpha^{c}
are non-empty,
or equivalently if
K=K^{\mathscr{A}}
.
We have the following graph interpretation: consider the oriented graph
G=(V,E)
with
V=\{1,\ldots,n\}

and
ij\in E
, that is
ij
is an oriented edge of
G
, if and only if

K_{ij}\neq 0
. Then the matrix
K
is irreducible if for any choice of
vertices
i,j\in V
there is an oriented path from
i
to
j
; the
matrix
K
is completely reducible if for any vertices
i,j\in V
there
is an oriented path from
i
to
j
if and only if there is an oriented
path from
j
to
i
.

Recall the matrix
K
is
diagonally similar
to a
matrix
\tilde{K}
if there exists a non singular real diagonal matrix

D
such that
K=D\cdot\tilde{K}\cdot D^{-1}
. Notice that if
K
and

\tilde{K}
have nonnegative entries one can assume without loss of
generality that
D
is also nonnegative.
We recall the following well known result (see

[
4
, Lemma 3.1 and Corrolary 5.4]
in the infinite dimensional setting). For

\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, we denote
\mathbb{1}_{\{\eta>0\}}
the vector whose
i
-th
component is
\mathbb{1}_{\{\eta_{i}>0\}}
.

Lemma 5

(Sufficient conditions for equality of effective spectrum)
.

Let
K
and
\tilde{K}
be square matrices of the same
size
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with nonnegative entries. We have:

(1)

\spec[K]=\spec[K^{\top}]=\spec[K^{\mathscr{A}}]
.

(2)

If
K
and
\tilde{K}
are diagonally similar, then
\spec[K]=\spec[\tilde{K}]
.

(3)

For
\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, we have:

\displaystyle\spec[K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)]=\spec[\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)\cdot K]

\displaystyle=\spec[\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbb{1}_{\{\eta>0\}})\cdot K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)]

\displaystyle=\spec[\mathrm{Diag}(\eta)\cdot K\cdot\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbb{1}_{\{\eta>0\}})].

We now try to find necessary conditions for equality of effective spectra.
In other words, we would like to see if there are others transformations
of matrices that leave the effective spectrum invariant.
Following
[
1
]

we introduce the notion of clan.

Definition 6

(Clans and clan-free matrix)
.

Let
K
be a square matrix of size
n
. A subset
\alpha
of
\{1,...,n\}
is
a
clan
if it satisfies
2\leq\mathrm{Card}(\alpha)\leq n-2
, and the submatrices
K[\alpha,\alpha^{c}]
and
K[\alpha^{c},\alpha]
have rank at most
1
.
The matrix
K
is
clan-free
if there exists no clan.

Remark 7
.

A square matrix of size
n\in\{1,2,3\}
is automatically clan-free.

The following proposition gathers known results on necessary conditions
for equality of principal minors, and therefore of effective spectrum.

Proposition 8
.

Let
K
and
\tilde{K}
be square matrices of the same size with
nonnegative entries, and the same effective spectrum, that is,

R_{e}[K]=R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
.

(1)

If
K
and
\tilde{K}
are symmetric,
then
\tilde{K}=K
.

(2)

If
K
is symmetric, then
\tilde{K}^{\mathscr{A}}
is diagonally similar
to
K
.

(3)

If
K
is irreducible and clan-free,
then
\tilde{K}
is diagonally similar to
K
or to
K^{\top}
.

Proof.

Thanks to Theorem
2.1
, the
principal minors of
K
and
\tilde{K}
coincide. The results then
follow directly from
[
5
, Theorem 3.5]
, for the symmetric
case,
[
6
, Theorem 3]
for the irreducible case when

n\leq 3
(by Remark
7
, there can be no clan
in this case), and
[
7
, Theorem 1]
for the clan-free case
when
n\geq 4
.
∎

Finally, as a corollary of Theorem
2.1
, we show that
the clan-free assumption is needed and get
an additional sufficient condition for equality.
Assume that
\alpha=\{1,...,m\}
is a clan for
K
(and thus
2\leq m\leq n-2
). Then, there exists vectors
v
,
w
of size
m
,
and
b
,
c
of size
n-m
such that
K
may be written in block form as:

(4)

K=\begin{pmatrix}A&vb^{\top}\\
cw^{\top}&B\end{pmatrix}.

The choice of
v,w,b,c
is not unique in general. We say that:

(5)

\tilde{K}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{\top}&wb^{\top}\\
cv^{\top}&B\end{pmatrix}

is a
partial transpose
of
K
(note that the partial transpose is
not unique in general).

Remark 9
.

Such transformations have been considered in the special case
where
v=w
in
[
7
, Lemma 5]
; see also
[
1
]
where a similar
transformation called
clan reversal
is introduced for skew symmetric matrices.

Proposition 10
.

If
K
is not clan-free, then we have
R_{e}[K]=R_{e}[\tilde{K}]
for
any partial transpose
\tilde{K}
of
K
.

Proof.

To prove Point
10
, suppose that
K
has a
clan
\alpha
, and let
\tilde{K}
be a partial transpose of
K
,
so that
K
and
\tilde{K}
may be given by (
4
) and
(
5
).
For any
\lambda\notin\spec(B)
, using a classical formula
for
determinants of block matrices, we get:

\displaystyle\det(K-\lambda I)

\displaystyle=\det(A-\lambda I-vb^{\top}(B-\lambda I)^{-1}cw^{\top})\det(B-\lambda I),

\displaystyle\det(\tilde{K}-\lambda I)

\displaystyle=\det(A^{\top}-\lambda I-wb^{\top}(B-\lambda I)^{-1}cv^{\top})\det(B-\lambda I)

\displaystyle=\det(A-\lambda I-vc^{\top}((B-\lambda I)^{-1})^{\top}bw^{\top})\det(B-\lambda I).

Since
b^{\top}(B-\lambda I)^{-1}c
is a one-dimensional matrix, it is equal to its
transpose, so that
\det(K-\lambda I)=\det(\tilde{K}-\lambda I)

are
equal for all
\lambda\notin\spec(B)
, and thus for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

by continuity.
Consequently, the matrices
K
and
\tilde{K}
have the same
spectrum.
For
any
\beta
, it is easily seen that
K[\beta]
and
\tilde{K}[\beta]

are partial transposes of each other, so that
K[\beta]
and

\tilde{K}[\beta]
also have the same spectrum, and in
particular
the same spectral radius. Therefore
R_{e}[K]
and
R_{e}[\tilde{K}]

coincide as
1
and
2
are equivalent
in Theorem
2.1
.
∎

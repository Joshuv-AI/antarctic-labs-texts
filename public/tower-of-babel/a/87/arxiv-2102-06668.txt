Numerical analysis of a model of two phase compressible fluid flow
Eduard Feireisl, Mădălina Petcu, Bangwei She

Abstract
We consider a model of a binary mixture of two immiscible compressible fluids. We propose a numerical scheme and discuss its basic properties: Stability, consistency, convergence. The convergence is established via the method of generalized weak solutions combined with the weak–strong uniqueness principle.

1
Introduction

We consider a binary mixture of two immiscible compressible fluids. There are several ways how to model such a system. Here, we consider the model introduced in
[
13
]
based on the phase field approach:

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})&=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho)&={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}({\nabla_{x}}{\bf u})-{\rm div}_{x}\left(\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\otimes\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right)+\nabla_{x}F(\mathfrak{c}),\\
\partial_{t}\mathfrak{c}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}&=\mu,\end{split}

(1.1)

where

\begin{split}\mu&=\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-F^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c}),\\
\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})&=\nu\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{d}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\right)+\lambda{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I},\ \nu>0,\ \lambda\geq 0.\end{split}

(1.2)

The system (
1.1
), (
1.2
) is a variant of the general phase field approach, where the density of the mixture is
represented by a single scalar function
\varrho
, the joint velocity is
{\bf u}
, and the concentration difference of the two phases is the order
parameter
\mathfrak{c}
, the evolution of which is governed by the Allen–Cahn equation.

The energy of the system is

E(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c})=\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}|^{2}+P(\varrho)+F(\mathfrak{c}),

where
P
is the pressure potential and
F
is the Ginzburg-Landau potential. The pressure potential
is related to the pressure
p
as

P^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho-P(\varrho)=p(\varrho).

Moreover, we suppose

\begin{split}&p\in C[0,\infty)\cap C^{\infty}(0,\infty),\\
&p^{\prime}(\varrho)>0\ \mbox{for}\ \varrho>0,\ \liminf_{\varrho\to\infty}p^{\prime}(\varrho)>0,\ p(\varrho)\leq c\left(1+P(\varrho)\right)\ \mbox{for all}\ \varrho\geq 0.\end{split}

(1.3)

The Ginzburg–Landau potential takes the form

F(\mathfrak{c})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&(\mathfrak{c}+1)^{2},\quad{\text{on}}\quad\mathfrak{c}\leq-1,\\
&\frac{1}{4}(\mathfrak{c}^{2}-1)^{2},\quad{\text{ on}}\quad-1\leq\mathfrak{c}\leq 1,\\
&(\mathfrak{c}-1)^{2},\quad{\text{on}}\quad\mathfrak{c}>1.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

Note that
F
coincides with the more standard double well potential
F(\mathfrak{c})=\frac{1}{4}(\mathfrak{c}^{2}-1)^{2}

in the physically relevant area
\mathfrak{c}\in[-1,1]
. All results of this paper remain valid
for
F
of the form
F(\mathfrak{c})=\lambda\mathfrak{c}^{2}+W(\mathfrak{c})
,
\lambda>0
, with
W\in\mathcal{C}^{2}\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
W
and
W^{\prime}
are (globally) Lipschitz functions.

We consider either the simplified periodic boundary conditions, where the physical domain can be identified with the flat torus

\Omega=\mathcal{T}^{d}=\left([-1,1]|_{\{-1,1\}}\right)^{d},\ {d}=2,3,

(1.5a)

or the Dirichlet boundary conditions

{\bf u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \mathfrak{c}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\ \mbox{a bounded domain.}

(1.5b)

In comparison with more complex models proposed by Blesgen
[
4
]
or Anderson et al.
[
3
]
, the present model
is much simpler to facilitate numerical analysis. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt in the context of mixtures of
compressible
fluids.

If the boundary conditions (
1.5
) are imposed, the total energy is a Lyapunov function,

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}|^{2}+P(\varrho)+F(\mathfrak{c})\right]\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-F^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c})|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}=0.

(1.6)

Our goal in the present paper is

•

to propose a numerical scheme to solve (
1.1
), (
1.2
), with the boundary conditions (
1.5
);

•

to show stability estimates and consistency of the scheme;

•

to show convergence of numerical approximations to a regular solution as long as it exists.

The strategy is to identify a large class of generalized solutions to the problem that goes beyond the standard framework of weak solutions. These are the so called
dissipative weak solutions
similar to those introduced in the monograph
[
10
]
. Although
dissipative weak solutions are more general objects than the weak solutions, they still comply with the weak–strong uniqueness principle.
A dissipative weak solution coincides with the strong solution originating from the same initial data. Then we show that any sequence
of numerical solutions that is stable and consistent converges to a dissipative weak solution. Applying the weak–strong uniqueness principle, we finally prove unconditional convergence to the strong solution, provided the latter exists.

2
Dissipative weak solutions

In accordance with the choice of the boundary conditions (
1.5
), we will use the same symbol
\Omega
to denote
either the flat torus
\mathcal{T}^{d}
or a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The symbol
\mathcal{M}(\Omega;X)
denotes the set of (Radon)
measures on
\Omega
ranging in a (finite–dimensional) space
X
,
\mathcal{M}^{+}
is the cone of non–negative scalar–valued measures.

The anticipated integrability properties of dissipative solutions are in agreement with the energy balance (
1.6
):

\begin{split}\sqrt{\varrho}{\bf u}&\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})),\ \mathfrak{c}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),\ P(\varrho)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))\\
{\bf u}&\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})),\ \mathfrak{c}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\Omega)).\end{split}

(2.1)

Definition 2.1

(Dissipative weak solution)
.

A trio
\{\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\}
is called
dissipative weak solution
of the problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
) in

(0,T)\times\Omega
, with the boundary
conditions (
1.5a
) (or (
1.5b
)) and the initial conditions

\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ \varrho{\bf u}(0,\cdot)=(\varrho{\bf u})_{0},\ \mathfrak{c}(0,\cdot)=\mathfrak{c}_{0},

if the following is satisfied:

•

Regularity.
The solution belongs to the class (
2.1
). Moreover,

\varrho\geq 0\ \mbox{a.a. in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega.

•

Equation of continuity.

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\varphi(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}

(2.2)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
.

•

Momentum equation.

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}&\int_{\Omega}\Big[\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\phi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}+p(\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\Big]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}+(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-F^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c}))\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{\Omega}(\varrho{\bf u})_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}:{\rm d}\mathfrak{R}(t){\rm d}t\end{split}

(2.3)

for any
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where

\mathfrak{R}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})).

In the case of the Dirichlet boundary conditions (
1.5b
), we also require

{\bf u}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})).

•

Allen–Cahn equation for the concentration difference.

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mathfrak{c}\partial_{t}\varphi-{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\varphi\Big]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t&=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mu\varphi\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}_{0}\varphi(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x},\\
\mu&=\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-F^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c})\end{split}

(2.4)

for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\Omega)
. If (
1.5b
) is imposed, we require

\mathfrak{c}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)).

•

Energy balance.

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}|^{2}+P(\varrho)+F(\mathfrak{c})\right](\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-F^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c})|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{\Omega}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{|(\varrho{\bf u})_{0}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}_{0}|^{2}+P(\varrho_{0})+F(\mathfrak{c}_{0})\right]\,{\rm d}{x}\end{split}

(2.5)

for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
, where

\mathfrak{E}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{M}^{+}({\overline{\Omega}})).

•

Defect compatibility.

|\mathfrak{R}(\tau)|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)

(2.6)

for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

Remark 2.2
.

The inequality (
2.6
) can be interpreted that there exists a constant
\Lambda>0
such that

\Big(\Lambda\mathfrak{E}\mathbb{I}\pm\mathfrak{R}\Big)(\tau)\geq 0,

meaning

\int_{\Omega}\phi\xi\otimes\xi:{\rm d}\left(\Lambda\mathfrak{E}(\tau)\pm\mathfrak{R}\right)(\tau)\geq 0

for any
\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)
,
\phi\geq 0
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The measure
\mathfrak{R}
can be viewed as the sum of a concentration and oscillation defects related to the non–linearities in
the momentum balance. Similarly, the measure
\mathfrak{E}
results from the defect in due to possible “anomalous” energy dissipation. The compatibility property (
2.6
) is absolutely crucial for the weak–strong uniqueness principle. A more elaborate
discussion concerning this approach can be found in the monograph
[
10
]
.

3
Relative energy inequality

The
relative energy
is a non-negative quantity that represents a “distance” between
a dissipative weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.1
and any trio of suitable smooth test functions.
It can be also interpreted as the Bregman distance generated by the energy functional, cf. Sprung
[
22
]
.
In this section we derive a differential inequality satisfied by the relative energy. Later we use it to evaluate the distance between a dissipative weak solution for the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and a more regular one. This technique, introduced by Dafermos in
[
6
]
, was largely used in order to prove weak-strong uniqueness for the solutions of different types of partial differential equations (see for example
[
8
,
15
,
23
]
) as well as to study certain singular limits as for example incompressible, inviscid limits of compressible, viscous fluids (see
[
12
,
20
,
21
]
).

Let
R
,
R>0
,
{\bf U}
, and
\mathfrak{C}
be arbitrary continuously differentiable functions. We define the
relative energy
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\left(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\ \Big|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf U}|^{2}+(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}|^{2}+P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(R)(\varrho-R)-P(R)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

In contrast with its counterpart introduced in
[
13
]
, the present relative energy is augmented
by the term
(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})^{2}
to compensate the absence of Poincaré inequality in the periodic case.

In order to facilitate the computations, we can decompose the relative energy
\mathcal{E}
as follows:

\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\ \Big|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{5}I_{j},

with

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+P(\varrho)+\mathfrak{c}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}|^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x},\quad I_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho\left[\frac{1}{2}|{\bf U}|^{2}-P^{\prime}(R)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x},

\displaystyle I_{3}=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x},\quad I_{4}=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}+2\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x},

\displaystyle I_{5}=\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathfrak{C}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}|^{2}+p(R)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x},{\textrm{ where }}\ p(R)=P^{\prime}(R)R-P(R).

Let us now state and prove the main result of this section:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c})
be a dissipative weak solution to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), (
1.5
), in the sense specified in Definition
2.1
.

Then, for any continuously differentiable functions satisfying

R\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega),\ R>0,\ {\bf U}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \ \mathfrak{C},\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C},\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega),

and, in the case of the Dirichlet boundary conditions, also

{\bf U}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\ \mathfrak{C}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

the following relative energy inequality holds:

\begin{split}&\left[\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,\mathfrak{c},{\bf u}\ \Big|R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U}\right)\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}{\bf U}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)+\mu^{2}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)\\
&\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\mu-2\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U})W(\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(R-\varrho)\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\cdot({\bf U}-{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\varrho)-\mathfrak{c}^{2}){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}:{\rm d}\mathfrak{R}(t)}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mu-{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c})(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}_{t}-2\mathfrak{C}_{t})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\end{split}

(3.1)

for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

Since a dissipative weak solution satisfies the energy inequality (
2.5
), the term
I_{1}
from the relative energy is estimated as follows:

\begin{split}\left[I_{1}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}&=\left[\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+P(\varrho)+\mathfrak{c}^{2}+W(\mathfrak{c})+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}|^{2}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}-\left[\int_{\Omega}W(\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&\leq-\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\mu^{2}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\left[\int_{\Omega}W(\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau},\end{split}

(3.2)

where
W(\mathfrak{c})=F(\mathfrak{c})-\mathfrak{c}^{2}
.

For the last term in (
3.2
), we use the Allen-Cahn equation for the concentration
\mathfrak{c}
and we get:

\begin{split}&\left[\int_{\Omega}W(\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}W(\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\mathfrak{c}W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mu-{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c})W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\mu-2\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}W(\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\mu-2\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\ W(\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.3)

The estimates for
I_{2}
are obtained testing the continuity equation by
\varphi=\dfrac{1}{2}|{\bf U}|^{2}-P^{\prime}(R)
. Remark that the choice of
\varphi
is possible thanks to the regularity of the functions
R
and
{\bf U}
. We obtain:

\begin{split}[I_{2}]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho\Big({\bf U}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}-\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R)\Big)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\Big(\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf U}\cdot{\bf U}-\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)\Big)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.4)

Adding the inequalities for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, we obtain:

\begin{split}[I_{1}+&I_{2}]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\mu^{2}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\\
&\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\mu-2\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\ W(\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\varrho{\bf U}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}-\varrho\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R))\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot(\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf U}\cdot{\bf U}-\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R))\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.5)

We also test the momentum equation by
{\bf U}
and get:

\begin{split}[I_{3}]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf{U}}+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\cdot{\bf u}+\big(p(\varrho)-\mathfrak{c}^{2}-W(\mathfrak{c})\big){\rm div}_{x}\bf{U}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\nabla_{x}{\bf U}:{\rm d}\ \mathfrak{R}(t)}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.6)

Summing (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have:

\begin{split}[I_{1}&+I_{2}+I_{3}]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)+\mu^{2}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\mu-2\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U})W(\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\cdot({\bf U}-{\bf u})\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R)+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\varrho)-\mathfrak{c}^{2}){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}:{\rm d}\mathfrak{R}(t){\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.7)

For the last two terms in the relative energy, we use the Allen-Cahn equation. Thus:

\begin{split}[I_{4}]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}&=-\left[\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}+2\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\left[\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C}\right]\mathfrak{c}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\left[\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C}\right]\mathfrak{c}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C}\right]\partial_{t}\mathfrak{c}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\left[\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C}\right]\mathfrak{c}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C}\right]\left[\mu-{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,\end{split}

(3.8)

and

\begin{split}[I_{5}]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}&=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{C}\ \Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}_{t}-2\mathfrak{C}\ \mathfrak{C}_{t})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}R\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.9)

thanks to the equality
\partial_{t}p(R)=\partial_{t}(P^{\prime}(R)R-P(R))=R\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R)
.

Adding (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) to (
3.7
), we obtain the desired relative energy inequality.

∎

4
Weak-strong uniqueness

In this section we prove that a dissipative weak solution and a strong solution for the compressible Navier-Stokes-Allen-Cahn problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), (
1.5a
)/(
1.5b
), both emanating from the same initial data, coincide on the life span of the strong solution. More exactly, we prove the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let the initial data
(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},\mathfrak{c}_{0})
be given such that the initial energy is finite

\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{|{\bf m}_{0}|^{2}}{\varrho_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}_{0}|^{2}+P(\varrho_{0})+F(\mathfrak{c}_{0})\right]\,{\rm d}{x}<\infty.

Let
(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c})
be a dissipative weak solution of the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), (
1.5a
)/(
1.5b
) in
(0,T)\times\Omega
in the sense of Definition
2.1
, with the initial data
(\varrho_{0},{\bf m}_{0},\mathfrak{c}_{0})
. Suppose that
(R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})
is a strong solution of the same problem belonging to the class:

\begin{split}&\inf_{(0,T)\times\Omega}R>0,\,R\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}),\\
&{\bf U}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}),\>{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\in C([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
&\mathfrak{C},\ \nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C},\ \Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}),\end{split}

(4.1)

and such that

R(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\,R(0,\cdot){\bf U}(0,\cdot)={\bf m}_{0},\,\mathfrak{C}(0,\cdot)=\mathfrak{c}_{0}.

Then

\varrho=R,\,{\bf u}={\bf U},\,\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{C}{\textrm{ in }}(0,T)\times\Omega,

and

\mathfrak{E}=\mathfrak{R}=0.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to test the relative energy inequality by the strong solution and use the Gronwall inequality in order to obtain the desired result. Let us proceed by considering the following terms from the relative energy inequality:

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\cdot({\bf U}-{\bf u})\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot R\partial_{t}{\bf U}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\cdot({\bf U}-{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\cdot({\bf U}-{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{4}J_{i}.

For
J_{1}+J_{2}
we use the equation of momentum and get:

\begin{split}J_{1}+J_{2}=&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\left[{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})-{\rm div}_{x}\left(\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\otimes\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right)-{\bf U}\partial_{t}R\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot{\rm div}_{x}\left(R{\bf U}\otimes{\bf U}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(p(R)-\mathfrak{C}^{2}-W(\mathfrak{C})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
=&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\left[\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}+R\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf U}\cdot{\bf U}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho-R}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(p(R)-\mathfrak{C}^{2}-W(\mathfrak{C})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.2)

which implies that:

\begin{split}&J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}=\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho-R}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(p(R)-\mathfrak{C}^{2}-W(\mathfrak{C})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.3)

The last term in
J
can be easily estimated as follows:

|J_{4}|\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}|\nabla_{x}{\bf U}|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})(t){\rm d}t.

Using exactly the same arguments as in
[
8
]
, we also estimate:

\begin{split}\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho-R}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot{\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\leq&\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\right):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+c(\delta)\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})(t){\rm d}t,\end{split}

(4.4)

for any
\delta>0
, where
c(\delta)
is a positive constant depending on
\delta
and on certain norms of
R
and
{\bf U}
. The estimate is based on the Korn-Poincaré inequality
(see e.g.
[
12
]
):

\int_{\Omega}\left|{\bf u}-{\bf U}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{x}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq c_{kp}\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\right):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)+\varrho\left|{\bf u}-{\bf U}\right|^{2}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}.

(4.5)

The last term in (
4.3
) we split it:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(p(R)-\mathfrak{C}^{2}-W(\mathfrak{C})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(p(R)-\mathfrak{C}^{2}-W(\mathfrak{C})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho-R}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(p(R)-\mathfrak{C}^{2}-W(\mathfrak{C})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.6)

Using the fact that
R
and
\mathfrak{C}
are regular enough, we can bound

\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varrho-R}{R}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(p(R)-\mathfrak{C}^{2}-W(\mathfrak{C})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\leq c(R,\mathfrak{C})\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\varrho-R||{\bf U}-{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,

(4.7)

where
c(R,\mathfrak{C})
is a positive constant depending on the norms of
R
and
\mathfrak{C}
.

Using the same arguments as in
[
8
]
and
[
13
]
, we now introduce the following cut-off function:

\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\infty),\,0\leq\Psi\leq 1,\ \Psi\equiv 1\ \mbox{in}\ [\delta,\frac{1}{\delta}],

where
\delta
is chosen so small that

R(t,x)\in[2\delta,\frac{1}{2\delta}]\ \mbox{for all}\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}.

For any function
h\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, we set the following splitting

h=h_{{\rm ess}}+h_{\rm res},\ h_{{\rm ess}}=\Psi(\varrho)h,\ h_{\rm res}=(1-\Psi(\varrho))h.

We can thus continue to estimate in (
4.7
) as:

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\varrho-R||{\bf U}-{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\varrho-R|_{\rm ess}|{\bf U}-{\bf u}|_{\rm ess}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\varrho-R|_{\rm res}|{\bf U}-{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,

(4.8)

where we used the fact that:

\left|[\varrho-r]_{\rm ess}\right|\ |{\bf U}-{\bf u}|=\sqrt{[\varrho-r]^{2}_{\rm ess}}\sqrt{|{\bf U}-{\bf u}|^{2}_{\rm ess}}.

(4.9)

It can be easily checked that

P(\varrho)-P(r)(\varrho-r)-P(r)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}(\varrho-r)^{2}_{\rm ess}+(1+\varrho)_{\rm res}\,.

(4.10)

We can thus write

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,{\bf u},c\ \Big|\ r,{\bf U},C\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\int_{\Omega}\left(|{\bf u}-{\bf U}|^{2}_{\rm ess}+[\varrho-r]^{2}_{\rm ess}+1_{\rm res}+\varrho_{\rm res}\right)\,{\rm d}{x},\end{split}

(4.11)

which allows us to conclude that:

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\varrho-R|_{\rm ess}|{\bf U}-{\bf u}|_{\rm ess}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,{\bf u},c\ \Big|\ r,{\bf U},C\right).

We also know that:

\left|[\varrho-r]_{\rm res}\right|\ |{\bf U}-{\bf u}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1_{\rm res}|{\bf U}-{\bf u}|+\sqrt{\varrho_{\rm res}}\sqrt{\varrho}|{\bf U}-{\bf u}|,

(4.12)

which implies:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|[\varrho-r]_{\rm res}\right|\ |{\bf U}-{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq c(\delta)\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}1_{\rm res}+\varrho_{\rm res}+\varrho|{\bf u}-{\bf U}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\delta\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\right):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\,{\rm d}{x},

where we use again the Korn-Poincaré inequality (
4.5
).

Gathering all these estimates, we obtain the following inequality:

\begin{split}&\left[\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,\mathfrak{c},{\bf u}\ \Big|R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U}\right)\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[(1-2\delta)\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}{\bf U}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)+\mu^{2}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)\\
&\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\mu-2\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U})W(\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varrho}{R}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(R-\varrho)\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}p(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\left[\mathfrak{C}^{2}+W(\mathfrak{C})\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(p(\varrho)-\mathfrak{c}^{2}){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}\bf{U}:{\rm d}\mathfrak{R}(t){\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mu-{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c})(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}_{t}-2\mathfrak{C}_{t})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+c(\delta,R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U})\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,\mathfrak{c},{\bf u}\ \Big|R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U}\right)(t){\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.13)

Now, notice that the continuity equation for
R
and
{\bf U}
also implies that

\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R)+{\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)+RP^{\prime\prime}(R){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}=0,

(4.14)

with
RP^{\prime\prime}(R)=p^{\prime}(R)
.

Using (
4.14
), we can handle the terms related to the elastic pressure
P
:

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}p(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(R-\varrho)\partial_{t}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}(p(R)-p(\varrho)-(R-\varrho)p^{\prime}(R))\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}p(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(R-\varrho)({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}R{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.15)

For the first term from the right hand side of (
4.15
) we have the following bound:

\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}(p(R)-p(\varrho)-(R-\varrho)p^{\prime}(R))\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,\mathfrak{c},{\bf u}\ \Big|R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U}\right)(t){\rm d}t,

(4.16)

where we used the fact that:

\big|p(R)-p^{\prime}(R)(R-\varrho)-p(\varrho)\big|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\big|P(\varrho)-P^{\prime}(R)(\varrho-R)-P(R)\big|.

(4.17)

The second and the last term from the right hand side of (
4.15
) cancel since
\nabla_{x}p(R)=R\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)
and the third term is estimated as in (
4.7
):

\begin{split}&\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(R-\varrho)({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(R)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|R-\varrho||{\bf U}-{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\right):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+c(\delta,R,{\bf U})\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})(t){\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.18)

Returning to (
4.13
), we obtain:

\begin{split}&\left[\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,\mathfrak{c},{\bf u}\ \Big|R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U}\right)\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[(1-3\delta)\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}{\bf U}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)+\mu^{2}\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)\\
&\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\mu-2\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U})W(\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varrho-R}{R}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}W(\mathfrak{C})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}^{2}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}:{\rm d}\mathfrak{R}(t){\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}_{t}-2\mathfrak{C}_{t})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+c(\delta,R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U})\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,\mathfrak{c},{\bf u}\ \Big|R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U}\right)(t){\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.19)

We first remark that:

\begin{split}&\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U})W(\mathfrak{c})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}W(\mathfrak{C})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\\
&=\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U})(W(\mathfrak{c})-W(\mathfrak{C}))\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}||\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\right):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+c(\delta)\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})(t){\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.20)

Since
R
and
\mathfrak{C}
are regular, we can also write:

\begin{split}\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varrho-R}{R}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\varrho-R||{\bf u}-{\bf U}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,\end{split}

(4.21)

and the right hand side we estimate identically as in (
4.7
).

Using the equation for the concentration
\mathfrak{C}
, we have:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}_{t}-2\mathfrak{C}_{t})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})-2(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})\right]\left[\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C}-W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{C})\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C}){\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+2\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{C}-\mathfrak{c}){\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.22)

Gathering the following terms, we also obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla_{x}c\otimes\nabla_{x}c-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}c|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right):\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}){\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}(\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}-\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c})\cdot({\bf u}-{\bf U})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf U}\cdot(\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}-\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c})(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}(\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}-\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c})\cdot({\bf u}-{\bf U})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}{\bf U}:\left[\nabla_{x}(\mathfrak{C}-\mathfrak{c})\otimes\nabla_{x}(\mathfrak{C}-\mathfrak{c})-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}(\mathfrak{C}-\mathfrak{c})|^{2}\mathbb{I}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,

terms that can be also bounded by:

\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\right):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+c(\delta,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})(t){\rm d}t.

We also gather the following convective terms:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf U}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}^{2}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+2\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{C}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-2\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C}){\bf U}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}(\mathfrak{C}-\mathfrak{c})^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+2\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\bf u}-{\bf U})\cdot(\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{C})\mathfrak{C}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf U})\right):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+c(\delta,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})(t){\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.23)

The remaining terms give, after elementary manipulations:

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{c}-\mu-2\mathfrak{c})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C})\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})-2(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})\right]\left[\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C}-W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{C})\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})-2(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})\right]\left[\mu-\Delta_{x}\mathfrak{C}-2\mathfrak{C}-W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{C})\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})-2(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})-2(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})\right]\left[W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c})-W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{C})\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,\end{split}

(4.24)

where we can bound the last term in (
4.24
) by:

\begin{split}&\Big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})-2(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})\right]\left[W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c})-W^{\prime}(\mathfrak{C})\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})-2(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.25)

Gathering all these estimates in (
4.19
) and taking
\delta
small enough, we obtain:

\begin{split}&\left[\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\ \Big|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C}\right)\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}{\bf U}):\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\nabla_{x}\bf{U}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta_{x}(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})-2(\mathfrak{c}-\mathfrak{C})|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}:{\rm d}\mathfrak{R}(t){\rm d}t+c(\delta,R,\mathfrak{C},{\bf U})\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\ \Big|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C}\right)(t){\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.26)

Using the defect compatibility hypothesis (
2.6
) (see also Remark
2.2
), we also have:

\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}:{\rm d}\mathfrak{R}(t)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\big|\leq\Lambda\|\nabla_{x}{\bf{U}}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times N})}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}}(t){\rm d}t.

(4.27)

Using (
4.27
) with (
4.26
), the relative energy inequality reduces to:

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\ \Big|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C}\right)(\tau)&+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(\tau)\leq\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho_{0},{\bf u}_{0},\mathfrak{c}_{0}\ \Big|R(0,\cdot),{\bf U}(0,\cdot),\mathfrak{C}(0,\cdot)\right)\\
&+c(\delta,R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C})\int_{0}^{\tau}\left[\mathcal{E}\left(\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\ \Big|R,{\bf U},\mathfrak{C}\right)(t)+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(t)\right]{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(4.28)

Applying the Gronwall lemma, we finally obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

5
Convergence of a numerical approximation

In this section we propose a combined discontinuous Galerkin (DG) – finite element (FE) method for the approximation of the Navier–Stokes–Allen–Cahn system (
1.1
). Specifically, for the Navier–Stokes part, we adopt the method studied by Karper
[
17
]
as well as Feireisl and Lukačová
[
11
]
. For the Allen-Cahn part, we take a discontinuous Galerkin approximation.
The main purpose is to analyze the convergence of DG-FE method using the theoretical study built in the previous sections.
For the sake of simplicity, we restrict ourselves to the space periodic boundary conditions, meaning
\Omega=\mathcal{T}^{d}
.

Moreover, we strengthen the hypothesis (
1.3
) concerning the structural properties of the pressure. Here and hereafter we suppose that

\begin{split}&p\in C[0,\infty)\cap C^{2}(0,\infty),\ p(0)=0,\ p^{\prime}(\varrho)>0\ \mbox{for}\ \varrho>0;\\
&\mbox{the pressure potential}\ P\ \mbox{determined by}\ P^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho-P(\varrho)=p(\varrho)\ \mbox{satisfies}\ P(0)=0,\\
&\mbox{and}\ P-\underline{a}p,\ \overline{a}p-P\ \mbox{are convex functions
for certain constants}\ \underline{a}>0,\ \overline{a}>0.\end{split}

(5.1)

As shown in
[
1
, Section 2.1.1]
, hypothesis (
5.1
) implies that there exists
\gamma>1
such that

P(\varrho)\geq a\varrho^{\gamma}\ \mbox{for some}\ a>0\ \mbox{and all}\ \varrho\geq 1.

(5.2)

5.1

We begin by introducing the notations. We write

for a generic positive constant

\Delta t

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

Mesh.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{h}

\Omega\equiv\left([-1,1]|_{\{-1,1\}}\right)^{d}

•

We denote

|K|

•

•

Periodic boundary conditions frequently appear in the mathematical physical problems. Their numerical realization is often more complicated than that of Dirichlet or Neumann type boundary conditions. We realize the periodicity by the following definition.

Definition 5.1

(Periodic mesh)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulation of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
P^{L}_{i}
(resp.
P^{R}_{i}
),
i=1,\dots,d
, be the set of vertices that forms the edges on the left (resp. right) boundary of
\Omega
in the
i^{\rm th}
direction of the Cartesian coordinates. We say
\mathcal{T}
is periodic mesh if the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

For any vertex
P\in P^{L}_{i}
, there exists a dual vertex
P^{*}\in P^{R}_{i}
such that
x_{P^{*}}-x_{P}=\ell_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}
, where
\mathbf{e}_{i}
is the
i^{\rm th}
basis vector of the Cartesian coordinates and
\ell_{i}
is the length of the domain
\Omega
in the
i^{\rm th}-
direction.

2.

For all
i=1,\dots,d
, the vertices
P\in P^{L}_{i}
and their dual
P^{*}\in P^{R}_{i}
are treated as the same degree of freedom.

For a piecewise (elementwise) continuous function
v
we define

\displaystyle v^{\rm out}(x)=\lim_{\delta\to 0+}v(x+\delta{\bf n}),\quad v^{\rm in}(x)=\lim_{\delta\to 0+}v(x-\delta{\bf n}),

\displaystyle\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{v\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}(x)=\frac{v^{\rm in}(x)+v^{\rm out}(x)}{2},\quad\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket(x)=v^{\rm out}(x)-v^{\rm in}(x)

whenever
x\in\sigma\in\mathcal{E}
. Note that our jump operator has an opposite sign with respect to the classical discontinuous Galerkin setting
[
7
]
.

Function spaces.

Let
{\cal P}^{\ell}_{d}(K)
be the space of polynomials of degree not greater than
\ell
on
K
for
d-
dimensional vector-valued functions. We introduce the following function spaces:

\displaystyle Q_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in L^{1}(\Omega)|v_{K}\in\mathcal{P}^{0}_{1}(K)\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{{\bf v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)|{\bf v}_{K}\in\mathcal{P}^{1}_{d}(K)\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T};\int_{\sigma}\left\llbracket{\bf v}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S_{x}=0\;\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{E}\right\},

\displaystyle X_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)|v_{K}\in\mathcal{P}^{1}_{1}(K)\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

associated with the following projection operators

\Pi_{h}^{Q}:\,L^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow Q_{h},\qquad\Pi_{h}^{V}:\,W^{1,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbf{V}_{h},\qquad\Pi_{h}^{X}:\,W^{2,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow X_{h}.

Moreover, we introduce the space

W_{h}:=\left\{v\in X_{h}\ \middle|\ \int_{\Omega}v\,{\rm d}{x}=0\right\}

along with the projection operator
\Pi^{W}_{h}
constructed by Kay et al.
[
18
]
,
enjoying the following properties, see
[
18
, Section 2, formula (2.20)]

\|\Pi^{W}_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq h\lvert\lVert\Pi^{W}_{h}v-v\rVert\rvert\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lvert\lVert\Pi^{W}_{h}v-v\rVert\rvert\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}{h^{1-\beta}\|v\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}}

(5.3)

for any
v\in W^{2,2}(\Omega).
Here we have introduced the broken norm

\lvert\lVert v\rVert\rvert^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{K}|\nabla_{h}v|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+h\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\nabla_{h}v\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}+{\frac{1}{h^{1+\beta}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}},\quad\mbox{with }\beta>0.

Note that the operator
\Pi_{h}^{Q}
can be explicitly written as

\Pi_{h}^{Q}\phi=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\frac{1_{K}(x)}{|K|}\int_{K}\phi\,{\rm d}{x},\quad 1_{K}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in K,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We shall frequently use the notation
\widehat{\phi}=\Pi_{h}^{Q}\phi
.
Hereafter, for any
K\in\mathcal{T}
we denote:

\nabla_{h}v|_{K}=\nabla_{x}v|_{K},\quad{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}|_{K}={\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}|_{K}

for any
v\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\cup X_{h}
,
{\bf u}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
.

Further, we introduce the bilinear form

B(v,w)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}v\cdot\nabla_{h}w\,{\rm d}{x}{+}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left(\left\llbracket w\right\rrbracket{\bf n}\cdot\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\nabla_{h}v\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}{+}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket{\bf n}\cdot\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\nabla_{h}w\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}{+}{\frac{1}{h^{1+\beta}}}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket\left\llbracket w\right\rrbracket\right)\,{\rm d}S_{x}.

It is easy to check

B(v,v-w)=\frac{1}{2}\lvert\lVert v\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lvert\lVert w\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\lVert v-w\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}.

(5.4)

Next, we introduce a norm on
W_{h}
(seminorm on
X_{h}
),

\displaystyle\lvert\lVert v\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}={\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{K}|\nabla_{h}v|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\frac{1}{h^{1+\beta}}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}.

(5.5)

Observe that the seminorms
\lvert\lVert\cdot\rVert\rvert_{B}
and
\lvert\lVert\cdot\rVert\rvert
are equivalent on
X_{h}
with a constant independent of
h
.
Consequently, by means of the Riesz representation theorem, there exists a unique
\Delta_{h}v\in W_{h}
such that

-\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v\;w\,{\rm d}{x}=B(v,w)\quad\mbox{for any }w\in W_{h}.

(5.6)

Here we may replace the test function space
W_{h}
by
X_{h}
as
X_{h}=W_{h}\oplus{\rm span}\{1\}
and
w\equiv 1
satisfies (
5.6
).

Lemma 5.2

(Closed graph lemma)
.

Suppose that
v_{h}(t)\in X_{h}
for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
,

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\lvert\lVert v_{h}\rVert\rvert_{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,

and

\ v_{h}\to v\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\ \Delta_{h}v_{h}\to\widetilde{\Delta_{x}v}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

Then

\widetilde{\Delta_{x}v}=\Delta_{x}v\ \mbox{in}\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Proof.

Our goal is to show

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\Delta_{x}v}w\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v_{h}w\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}v\Delta_{x}w\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t

for any
w\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\Omega))
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\int_{\Omega}w\,{\rm d}{x}=0
for a.a.
t
.

We have

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v_{h}w\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v_{h}(w-\Pi^{W}_{h}w)\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v_{h}\Pi^{W}_{h}w\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,

where, by virtue of (
5.3
),

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v_{h}(w-\Pi^{W}_{h}w)\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ h{\to 0}.

Next, in accordance with (
5.6
),

-\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v_{h}\Pi^{W}_{h}w\,{\rm d}{x}=B(v_{h},\Pi^{W}_{h}w)=B(v_{h},\Pi^{W}_{h}w-w)+B(v_{h},w),

where, by direct manipulation,

B(v_{h},w)=-\int_{\Omega}v_{h}\Delta_{x}w\,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{and, in particular,}\ \int_{0}^{T}B(v_{h},w){\rm d}t\to-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}v\Delta_{x}w\,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{as}\ h\to 0.

Thus it is enough to show

\int_{0}^{T}B(v_{h},\Pi^{W}_{h}w-w){\rm d}t\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ h\to 0.

It follows from Cauchy–Schwartz inequality that

|B(v_{h},\Pi^{W}_{h}w-w)|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\||v_{h}\||\ \||\Pi^{W}_{h}w-w\||.

As
v_{h}\in X_{h}
, we have

\||v_{h}\||\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\lvert\lVert v_{h}\rVert\rvert_{B}

and the desired conclusion follows from (
5.3
).

∎

Lemma 5.3

(Compactness Lemma)
.

Suppose that
v_{h}(t)\in X_{h}
for a.a.
t\in(0,T)
,

v_{h}(t)\to v\ \mbox{(strongly) in}\ L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\ \Delta_{h}v_{h}(t)\to\Delta_{x}v\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

and

\nabla_{h}v_{h}\to\nabla_{x}v\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})).

Then

\nabla_{h}v_{h}\to\nabla_{x}v\ \mbox{(strongly) in}\ L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})).

Proof.

In view of the weak lower semi–continuity and convexity of the
L^{2}
-norm, it is enough to show

\limsup_{h\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}v_{h}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.

To see this, we write

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}v_{h}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}=-\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v_{h}v_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left(2\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket{\bf n}\cdot\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\nabla_{h}v_{h}\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}+\frac{1}{h^{1+\beta}}\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}S_{x}.

On one hand, thanks to our hypotheses,

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}v_{h}v_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\to-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{x}vv\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}v|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t.

On the other hand,

\left|\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}2\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket{\bf n}\cdot\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\nabla_{h}v_{h}\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}\,{\rm d}S_{x}\right|\leq\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\frac{1}{h^{1+\beta}}\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}+ch^{\beta}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}v_{h}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}.

As
\beta>0
, we get the desired conclusion.
∎

Diffusive upwind flux.

Given the velocity field
{\bf v}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
, the upwind flux for any function
r\in Q_{h}
is specified at each face
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}
by

\displaystyle{\rm Up}[r,{\bf v}]|_{\sigma}=r^{{\rm up}}{\bf v}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}=r^{\rm in}[{\bf v}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}]^{+}+r^{\rm out}[{\bf v}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}]^{-}=\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{r\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}\ {\bf v}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}-\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}|\left\llbracket r\right\rrbracket,

where

{\bf v}_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{\sigma}{\bf v}\,{\rm d}S_{x},\quad[f]^{\pm}=\frac{f\pm|f|}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad r^{{\rm up}}=\begin{cases}r^{\rm in}&\mbox{if}\ {\bf v}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}\geq 0,\\
r^{\rm out}&\mbox{if}\ {\bf v}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}<0.\end{cases}

Furthermore, we consider a diffusive numerical flux function of the following form

\displaystyle{\rm F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(r,{\bf v})={\rm Up}[r,{\bf v}]-h^{\varepsilon}\left\llbracket r\right\rrbracket,\,\varepsilon>0.

(5.7)

When
{\bf r}
is a vector function, e.g.
{\bf r}=\varrho{\bf u}
in the momentum equation, we write the above numerical flux as

\textbf{F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho{\bf u},{\bf v})\equiv\big({\rm F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho u_{1},{\bf v}),\ldots,{\rm F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho u_{d},{\bf v})\big)^{T}\mbox{ and }{\textbf{Up}}(\varrho{\bf u},{\bf v})\equiv\big({\rm Up}(\varrho u_{1},{\bf v}),\ldots,{\rm Up}(\varrho u_{d},{\bf v})\big)^{T}.

Time discretization.

For a given time step
\Delta t\approx h>0
,
we denote the approximation of a function
v_{h}
at time
t^{k}=k\Delta t
by
v_{h}^{k}
for
k=1,\ldots,N_{T}(=T/\Delta t)
.
Then, we introduce the piecewise constant extension of discrete values,

v_{h}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}v_{h}^{k}1_{I^{k}}\mbox{ with }I^{k}=((k-1)\Delta t,k\Delta t].

(5.8)

Furthermore, we approximate the time derivative by the backward Euler method

D_{t}v_{h}(t)=\frac{v_{h}(t)-v_{h}(t-\Delta t)}{\Delta t}\;\forall\;t\in(0,T],i.e.,\quad D_{t}v_{h}^{k}=\frac{v_{h}^{k}-v_{h}^{k-1}}{\Delta t}\mbox{ for all }k=1,\dots,N_{T}.

Useful estimates.

We recall some basic inequalities used in the numerical analysis. First, thanks to Taylor’s theorem, it is obvious for
\phi,\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{2}(\Omega)
that

\begin{split}\lVert\phi-\Pi_{h}^{Q}\phi\rVert_{L^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{s}\lVert\phi\rVert_{C^{s}},\>\lVert\boldsymbol{\phi}-\Pi_{h}^{V}\boldsymbol{\phi}\rVert_{L^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{s}\lVert\phi\rVert_{C^{s}},\\
\lVert\phi-\Pi_{h}^{X}\phi\rVert_{L^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{s}\lVert\phi\rVert_{C^{s}},\ 1<p\leq\infty,\>\mbox{ for }s=1,2.\end{split}

(5.9)

Next, we report a discrete analogous of the Poincaré–Sobolev type inequality (see
[
10
, Theorem 17]
for a similar result):

Lemma 5.4

(Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
r\geq 0
be a function defined on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

0<c_{M}\leq\int_{\Omega}r\,{\rm d}{x},\mbox{ and }\int_{\Omega}r^{\gamma}\,{\rm d}{x}\leq c_{E}\mbox{ for }\gamma>1,

where
c_{M}
and
c_{E}
are some positive constants.
Then the following Poincaré-Sobolev type inequality holds true

\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}c\lVert\nabla_{h}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\int_{\Omega}r|\Pi_{a}v_{h}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}

(5.10)

for any
v_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\cup X_{h}
, and
1\leq q\leq 6
for
d=3
,
1\leq q<\infty
for
d=2
,
where the constant
c
depends on
c_{M}
and
c_{E}
but not on the mesh parameter and
\Pi_{a}\in\{1,\Pi_{h}^{Q}\}
.
In particular, setting
r=1
yields

\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\lVert\nabla_{h}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\lVert v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(5.11)

The following lemma shall be useful in the analysis of the energy stability.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\varrho_{h}\in Q_{h}
and
{\bf u}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
it holds

\begin{split}&\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left(\textbf{F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}},{\bf u}_{h})\cdot\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\right\rrbracket-\textbf{F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho_{h},{\bf u}_{h})\left\llbracket\frac{1}{2}|\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}|^{2}\right\rrbracket\right)\,{\rm d}S_{x}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\varrho_{h}^{{\rm up}}\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert{\bf u}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}\right\rvert\,{\rm d}S_{x}-h^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\varrho_{h}\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x},\end{split}

(5.12)

where we have denoted
{\bf u}_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{\sigma}{\bf u}_{h}\,{\rm d}S_{x}
.

The proof is analogous to
[
10
, Lemma 8.1]
. For completeness, we attach the proof in Appendix
A.1
.

5.2
A mixed discontinuous Galerkin – finite element method

Now we are ready to introduce a combined discontinuous Galerkin (DG) – finite element (FE) method for the approximation of Navier–Stokes–Allen–Cahn system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with the periodic boundary conditions (
1.5a
).

Definition 5.6

(DG-FE method)
.

Let
(\varrho_{h}^{0},{\bf u}_{h}^{0},\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{0})=(\Pi_{h}^{Q}\varrho_{0},\Pi_{h}^{V}{\bf u}_{0},\Pi_{h}^{X}\mathfrak{c}_{0})
be the initial data. We say
(\varrho_{h},{\bf u}_{h},\mathfrak{c}_{h})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{T}}(\varrho_{h}^{k},{\bf u}_{h}^{k},\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})1_{I^{k}}
is a DG-FE approximation of the Navier–Stokes–Allen–Cahn system (
1.1
)–(
1.5a
) if the triple
(\varrho_{h}^{k},{\bf u}_{h}^{k},\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\in Q_{h}\times\mathbf{V}_{h}\times X_{h}
satisfies
the following system of algebraic equations with the space periodic boundary conditions for all
k=1,\dots,N_{T}
:

\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\varrho_{h}^{k}\phi_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}{\rm F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho_{h}^{k},{\bf u}_{h}^{k})\left\llbracket\phi_{h}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S_{x}=0,\quad\mbox{for all }\phi_{h}\in Q_{h};

(5.13a)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}D_{t}(\varrho_{h}^{k}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\textbf{F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho_{h}^{k}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}},{\bf u}_{h}^{k})\cdot\left\llbracket\widehat{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S_{x}+\nu\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}:\nabla_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}\\
&+\eta\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}{\rm div}_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}p_{h}^{k}{\rm div}_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}(f_{h}^{k}-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x},\quad\mbox{for all }\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h};\end{split}

(5.13b)

\int_{\Omega}(D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\psi_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\big(\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}-f_{h}^{k}\big)\psi_{h}\,{\rm d}{x},\quad\mbox{for all }\psi_{h}\in X_{h};

(5.13c)

where
\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\in W_{h}
is defined according to (
5.6
),

-\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\;\varphi_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}=B(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k},\varphi_{h})\quad\mbox{for all }\varphi_{h}\in W_{h}.

(5.13d)

Here,
p^{k}_{h}=p(\rho^{k}_{h})
,
\eta=\frac{d-2}{d}\nu+\lambda>0
and

f_{h}^{k}=\begin{cases}2(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+1)&\text{if }\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\in(-\infty,-1),\\
(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})^{3}-\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k-1}&\text{if }\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\in[-1,1].\\
2(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}-1)&\text{if }\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\in(1,\infty).\end{cases}

(5.14)

Remark 5.7
.

Note that
f_{h}^{k}
is an approximation of
f\equiv F^{\prime}(\mathfrak{c})
at time
t^{k}
.
The idea in defining
f_{h}
is that we split the convex and concave parts of
F(\mathfrak{c}_{h})
and respectively approximate them implicitly and explicitly in time. Such kind of splitting shall be helpful in deriving the energy stability, see the proof of Theorem
5.10
below.

5.3
Stability

In this section we show the stability of the DG-FE method, including the positivity of density, conservation of mass and the energy stability.

5.3.1
Basic properties of the DG-FE scheme

Before stating the stability, let us show some fundamental properties of the DG-FE scheme (
5.13
).

Conservation of mass.

Setting
\phi_{h}=1
in the density method (
5.13a
) we get

\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\varrho_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}=0
,
which implies for all
k=1,2,\ldots,N_{T}
that

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{k-1}\,{\rm d}{x}=\dots=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{0}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\Pi_{h}^{Q}\varrho_{0}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{0}\,{\rm d}{x}.

Internal energy balance.

We recall the discrete internal energy balance from
[
9
, Section 4.1]
or
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
. Indeed, testing the scheme for the density (
5.13a
) by
\phi_{h}=P^{\prime}(\varrho_{h}^{k})
gives rise to the following lemma.

Lemma 5.8

(Discrete internal energy balance)
.

Let
(\varrho_{h}^{k},{\bf u}_{h}^{k})\in Q_{h}\times\mathbf{V}_{h}
satisfy the discrete continuity equation (
5.13a
) for any
k\in\{1,\dots,N_{T}\}
. Then, there exist
\xi\in{\rm co}\{\varrho_{h}^{k-1},\varrho_{h}^{k}\}
and
\zeta\in{\rm co}\{\varrho_{K}^{k},\varrho_{L}^{k}\}
for any
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}
such that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}D_{t}P(\varrho_{h}^{k})\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho_{h}^{k}){\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}\\
&=-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{\Omega}P^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|D_{t}\varrho_{h}^{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}P^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)\left\llbracket\varrho_{h}^{k}\right\rrbracket^{2}\left(h^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}_{\sigma}^{k}\cdot{\bf n}|\right)\,{\rm d}S_{x}\leq 0.\end{split}

(5.15)

Lemma 5.9

(Existence of a solution and positivity of density)
.

Given
\varrho_{0}>0
. For every
k=1,\dots,N_{T}
there exists a solution
(\varrho_{h}^{k},{\bf u}_{h}^{k},\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\in Q_{h}\times\mathbf{V}_{h}\times X_{h}
to the DG-FE scheme (
5.13
).
Moreover, any solution to (
5.13
) preserves the positivity of the density, i.e.
\varrho_{h}^{k}>0
for any
k=1,\dots,N_{T}
.

The proof can be done analogously as
[
10
, Lemma 11.3]
. For completeness, we give the proof in Appendix
A.2
.

5.3.2
Energy estimates

Now, we are ready to derive the discrete counterpart of the total energy balance (
1.6
).

Theorem 5.10

(Discrete energy balance)
.

Let
(\varrho_{h},{\bf u}_{h},\mathfrak{c}_{h})
be a solution of the DG-FE method (
5.13
). Then we have the following energy estimate

\begin{split}&D_{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{h}^{k}\left\lvert\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}+P(\varrho_{h}^{k})\right)\,{\rm d}{x}+D_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}F(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\,{\rm d}{x}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}\right)\\
&+\nu\lVert\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\eta\lVert{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-D_{\rm num}^{k},\end{split}

(5.16)

where
D_{\rm num}^{k}\geq 0
is the numerical dissipation

\begin{split}D_{\rm num}^{k}&=\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{k-1}|D_{t}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}(\varrho_{h}^{k})^{\rm up}\left\lvert{\bf u}_{\sigma}^{k}\cdot{\bf n}\right\rvert\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}+h^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\varrho_{h}^{k}\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}\\
&\quad+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{\Omega}P^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|D_{t}\varrho_{h}^{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}P^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)\left\llbracket\varrho_{h}^{k}\right\rrbracket^{2}\left(h^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}_{\sigma}^{k}\cdot{\bf n}|\right)\,{\rm d}S_{x}\\
&\quad+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\lvert\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Delta t}{2}\big(1+1_{|\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}|>1}+3(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k,*})^{2}1_{|\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}|\leq 1}\big)\left\lvert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}{x},\end{split}

(5.17)

where
\zeta\in{\rm co}\{\varrho_{K}^{k},\varrho_{L}^{k}\}
for any
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}
,
\xi\in{\rm co}\{\varrho_{h}^{k-1},\varrho_{h}^{k}\}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k,*}\in{\rm co}\{\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k-1},\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\}
.

Proof.

First, setting
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}={\bf u}_{h}^{k}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
in (
5.13b
) we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}D_{t}(\varrho_{h}^{k}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}})\cdot{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}+\nu\lVert\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\eta\lVert{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\textbf{F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho_{h}^{k}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}},{\bf u}_{h}^{k})\cdot\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S_{x}+\int_{\Omega}p_{h}^{k}{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}(f_{h}^{k}-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\cdot{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}.\end{split}

(5.18)

Next, letting
\phi_{h}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}\in Q_{h}
in (
5.13a
) we find

\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\varrho_{h}^{k}\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}{\rm F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}[\varrho_{h}^{k},{\bf u}_{h}^{k}]\left\llbracket\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S_{x}.

(5.19)

Subtracting (
5.19
) from (
5.18
) we derive

\begin{split}&D_{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{h}^{k}\left\lvert\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\nu\lVert\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\eta\lVert{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&=-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{k-1}\left\lvert D_{t}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\varrho_{h}^{k,{\rm up}}\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert{\bf u}^{k}_{\sigma}\cdot{\bf n}\right\rvert\,{\rm d}S_{x}\\
&\quad-h^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\varrho_{h}^{k}\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}+\int_{\Omega}p_{h}^{k}{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}(f_{h}^{k}-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\cdot{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}.\end{split}

(5.20)

where we have used (
5.12
) and the following identity

\int_{\Omega}\left(D_{t}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}})^{k}\cdot{\bf u}_{h}^{k}-D_{t}\varrho_{h}^{k}\frac{\left\lvert\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}}{2}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\left(D_{t}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{h}^{k}\left\lvert\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}\Big)+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\varrho_{h}^{k-1}\left\lvert D_{t}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}.

Further, by setting
\psi_{h}=D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\in X_{h}
in (
5.13c
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}-f_{h}^{k})(D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\,{\rm d}{x}

(5.21)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}-f_{h}^{k}){\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}-B(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k},D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})-\int_{\Omega}f_{h}^{k}D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}-f_{h}^{k}){\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}-\frac{1}{2}D_{t}\lvert\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\lvert\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(D_{t}F(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\big(1+1_{|\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}|>1}+3(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k,*})^{2}1_{|\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}|\leq 1}\big)\left\lvert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\right\rvert^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}{x},

where we have used (
5.4
) and the following two applications of Taylor’s theory

\displaystyle 2\left(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\pm 1\right)D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}

\displaystyle=D_{t}(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\pm 1)^{2}+\Delta t(D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})^{2}=D_{t}F(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})+\Delta t(D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})^{2},\quad\mbox{ for }|\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}|\geq 1,

\displaystyle\left((\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})^{3}-\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k-1}\right)D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}D_{t}(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})^{4}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}3(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k,*})^{2}\left\lvert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\right\rvert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}D_{t}(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})^{2}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\left\lvert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=D_{t}F(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\big(3(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k,*})^{2}+1\big)(D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})^{2},\quad\mbox{ for }|\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}|\leq 1.

Here
\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k,*}\in{\rm co}\{\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k-1},\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\}
is a Taylor remainder term.

Finally, combining (
5.20
) and (
5.21
) together with (
5.15
), we complete the proof, i.e.,

\displaystyle D_{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{h}^{k}\left\lvert\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rvert^{2}+P(\varrho_{h}^{k})\right)\,{\rm d}{x}+D_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}F(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\,{\rm d}{x}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\nu\lVert\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\eta\lVert{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{k-1}|D_{t}{\bf u}_{h}^{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}(\varrho_{h}^{k})^{\rm up}\left\lvert{\bf u}_{\sigma}^{k}\cdot{\bf n}\right\rvert\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}-h^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\left\{\hskip-1.99997pt\left\{\varrho_{h}^{k}\right\}\hskip-1.99997pt\right\}\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{k}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}S_{x}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{\Omega}P^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|D_{t}\varrho_{h}^{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}P^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)\left\llbracket\varrho_{h}^{k}\right\rrbracket^{2}\left(h^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}_{\sigma}^{k}\cdot{\bf n}|\right)\,{\rm d}S_{x}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\lvert\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert\rvert_{B}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\big(1+1_{|\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}|>1}+3(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k,*})^{2}1_{|\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}|\leq 1}\big)\Delta t\left\lvert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-D_{\rm num}^{k}.

∎

Uniform bounds.

From the energy estimates we derive the following uniform bounds.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
(\varrho_{h},{\bf u}_{h},\mathfrak{c}_{h})
be a solution to the scheme (
5.13
) for
\gamma>1
.
Then, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\lVert\varrho_{h}|\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}|^{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\quad\lVert\varrho_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\gamma}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\quad\lVert\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,

(5.22a)

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\quad\lVert{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\quad\lVert{\bf u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,

(5.22b)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\lvert\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{h}(t)\rVert\rvert_{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\quad\lVert f_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\approx\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\lVert F(\mathfrak{c}_{h})\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,

(5.22c)

\displaystyle\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\lvert\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert\rvert_{B}+\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\quad\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}+{\bf u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,

(5.22d)

\displaystyle\lVert\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\quad\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{3/2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1.

(5.22e)

where
\lvert\lVert\cdot\rVert\rvert
and
\lvert\lVert\cdot\rVert\rvert_{B}
are defined in (
5.5
),

p\in[1,\infty)
if
d=2
or
p\in[1,6]
if
d=3
.

Proof.

Applying the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality Lemma
5.4
to the energy estimates stated in Theorem
5.10
we directly obtain the estimates (
5.22a
)–(
5.22d
).
We are left with the proof of (
5.22e
).
First, we set
\psi_{h}=\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}
in (
5.13c
) to get

\displaystyle\lVert\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k})\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}f_{h}^{k}\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}+{\bf u}_{h}^{k}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert f_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then we observe the first estimate of (
5.22e
) after
recalling the bounds
\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}+{\bf u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1
and
\lVert f_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1
.

Next, due to the uniform bound
\lVert{\bf u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{3/2}}\leq\lVert{\bf u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{6}}\lVert\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1
and the second estimate of (
5.22d
) we proves the second estimate of (
5.22e
),
which completes the proof.
∎

5.4
Consistency

Next step towards the convergence of the approximate solutions is the consistency of the numerical scheme. In particular, we require the numerical solution to satisfy the weak formulation of the continuous problem up to residual terms vanishing for
h\to 0.

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
(\varrho_{h},{\bf u}_{h},\mathfrak{c}_{h})
be a solution of the approximate problem (
5.13
) on the time interval
[0,T]
with
\Delta t\approx h
,
\gamma>4d/(1+3d)
and the artificial diffusion coefficient
\varepsilon
satisfies

\varepsilon>0\mbox{ if }\gamma\geq 2\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\varepsilon\in(0,2\gamma-1-d/3)\mbox{ if }\gamma\in(4d/(1+3d),2).

(5.23)

Then

-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{0}\phi(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{h}\partial_{t}\phi+\varrho_{h}{\bf u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}e_{1,h}(t,\phi){\rm d}t,

(5.24a)

for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{2}([0,T)\times\Omega)
with
\|e_{1,h}(\cdot,\phi)\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
;

\begin{split}-&\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{0}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{0}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\phi}+\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\otimes{\bf u}_{h}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}+p_{h}{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&-\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\eta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\big(f_{h}-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\big)\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}e_{2,h}(t,\boldsymbol{\phi}){\rm d}t\end{split}

(5.24b)

for any
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{2}_{c}([0,T)\times{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|e_{2,h}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\phi})\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
;

-\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{0}\psi(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathfrak{c}_{h}\partial_{t}\psi-{\bf u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\psi+(\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}-f_{h})\psi\right)\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}e_{3,h}(t,\psi){\rm d}t,

(5.24c)

for any
\psi\in C_{c}^{1}([0,T)\times\Omega)
with

for some
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

Step 1: proof of (

Recalling the first estimate of

Step 2: proof of (

Recalling the second estimate of
[
10
, Theorem 13.2]
, we know there exists a positive constant
\alpha
such that

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\sigma}\textbf{F}_{\varepsilon}^{{\rm up}}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}},{\bf u}_{h})\cdot\left\llbracket\widehat{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S_{x}{\rm d}t\\
&+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}:\nabla_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\eta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}{\rm div}_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}p_{h}{\rm div}_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\\
&=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{0}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{0}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\phi}+\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\otimes{\bf u}_{h}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}+p_{h}{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,\\
&\quad+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\eta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+ch^{\alpha}\end{split}

(5.25)

for any
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{2}_{c}([0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

with
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{V}\boldsymbol{\phi}
,
where

and on the initial energy of the problem.

) we are left to treat the consistency of

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(f_{h}-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h})\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\phi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{2}\lVert f_{h}-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\lVert\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\lVert\boldsymbol{\phi}\rVert_{C^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{2},

(5.26)

where we have used (

), and the first estimate in (

Obviously, combining (

\begin{split}-&\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{0}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}^{0}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\phi}+\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}\otimes{\bf u}_{h}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}+p_{h}{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t,\\
&-\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\eta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}_{h}{\bf u}_{h}{\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(f_{h}-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h})\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}\,{\rm d}{x}+ch^{\alpha}\end{split}

, where the positive constant

Step 3: proof of (

Let
\psi_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{X}\psi

be the test function in (
5.13c
) for
\psi\in C_{c}^{1}([0,T)\times\Omega)

5.22c
)–(
5.22e
) we first calculate

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\big((D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}+{\bf u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h})-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}+f_{h}\big)(\psi_{h}-\psi)\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t\right\rvert

(5.27)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert(D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}+{\bf u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h})-\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}+f_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\lVert\psi_{h}-\psi\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h\left(\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}+{\bf u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}+\lVert\Delta_{h}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}+\lVert f_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\right)\lVert\psi\rVert_{C^{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h.

Next, we rewrite the term
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\psi\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t
as

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\psi\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}(\psi-\psi^{k-1})\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\psi^{k-1}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

(5.28)

For the term
I_{1}
we have by Taylor’s theory and the estimate (

) that

\displaystyle\left\lvert I_{1}\right\rvert\leq\lVert D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}L^{3/2}}\Delta t\lVert\psi\rVert_{C^{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\Delta t.

I_{2}
in the following way

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\psi^{k-1}\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\psi^{k-1}\,{\rm d}{x}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\int_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}-\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k-1})\psi^{k-1}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{k}(\psi^{k}-\psi^{k-1})\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{N_{T}}\underbrace{\psi^{N_{T}}}_{=0}\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{0}\psi^{0}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}_{h}\partial_{t}\psi\ \,{\rm d}{x}{\rm d}t-\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{c}_{h}^{0}\psi^{0}\,{\rm d}{x}.

Substituting the above relation together with the estimate of the term

5.5
Convergence

In this subsection, we prove the final result, that is the convergence of the numerical solutions resulting from the DG–FE method.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
(\varrho_{h},{\bf u}_{h},\mathfrak{c}_{h})
be a solution of the DG–FE method (
5.13
), with
\Delta t\approx h
,
\gamma>3/2
for
d=3
and
\gamma>8/7
for
d=2
,
the artificial diffusion coefficient
\varepsilon
satisfies (
5.23
),
and the initial data satisfying

\varrho_{0}\in L^{\gamma}(\Omega),\ \varrho_{0}>0,\ {\bf u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \mathfrak{c}_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega).

1.

Then, for a suitable subsequence,

\begin{split}\varrho_{h}&\to\varrho\ \mbox{weakly-(*) in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),\\
{\bf u}_{h}&\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\
\mathfrak{c}_{h}&\to\mathfrak{c}\ \mbox{weakly-(*) in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\end{split}

(5.30)

where
\{\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\}
is a dissipative weak solution of the Navier–Stokes–Allen–Cahn system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

2.

In addition, suppose that the Navier–Stokes–Allen–Cahn system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
with the initial data
(\varrho_{0},{\bf u}_{0},\mathfrak{c}_{0})

5.30

\{\varrho,{\bf u},\mathfrak{c}\}

Proof.

From the energy estimates (
5.16
) (see also Lemma
5.11
) and the Closed Graph Lemma

we deduce that at least for suitable subsequences,

\displaystyle\varrho_{h}

\displaystyle\to\varrho\ \mbox{weakly-(*) in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),\ \varrho\geq 0,

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{h},\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}

\displaystyle\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle\nabla_{h}{\bf u}_{h}

\displaystyle\to\nabla_{x}{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\quad\mbox{where}\ \mathbf{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})),

\displaystyle\varrho_{h}{\bf u}_{h},\varrho_{h}\widehat{{\bf u}_{h}}

17

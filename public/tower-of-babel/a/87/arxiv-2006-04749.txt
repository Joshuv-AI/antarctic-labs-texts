Ring of flows of one-dimensional differential equations
Ronald Orozco López

Abstract
In this article the ring of flows of autonomous differential equations of order one on integral domains is constructed. First we build the autonomous ring 𝔄 ⁡ ( H R ⁡ [ [ x ] ] ) \mathfrak{A}(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]) and then its structure is studied. Next, we build the ring of formal exponential generating series of the ring 𝔄 ⁡ ( H R ⁡ [ [ x ] ] ) \mathfrak{A}(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]) , where it is possible to find solutions of differential equations of order one when the vector field of the system can be decomposed in sum or product of functions.



Keywords:
Hurwitz ring, autonomous operator, autonomous ring, flows ring

Mathematics Subject Classification:
47H99, 16W99, 34A34, 11B83

1
Introducción

A very important problem in the theory of autonomous differential equations is to find exact solutions of these. In the one-dimensional case the solutions of
\phi^{\prime}=f(\phi)

can be found by using the method of separation of variables. However, it is not always possible to find explicit solutions to these equations by this method. In this article we propose a different method that allows not only to find solutions to these equations but also to give a ring structure to the set of one-dimensional dynamic systems. This type of solutions are found if the vector field
f(x)
is taken from the Hurwitz ring of power series
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
, where
R
is a integral domain.

In article [5] we make a first approach to this type of solutions. In particular we find an analytical solution to the autonomous equation
\phi^{(k)}=f(\phi)
of order
k
in terms of autonomous polynomials. In this paper the equations will be limited to order one.

Autonomous polynomials are constructed using Bell polynomials. Bell polynomials are a very useful tool in mathematics to represent the
n
-th derivative of the composition of functions (see [3]). Indeed, let
f
and
g
be two analytic functions with representation
in power series
\sum a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
and
\sum b_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
, respectively, with
a_{n},b_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

f(g(x))=f(b_{0})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Y_{n}(b_{1},...,b_{n};a_{1},...,a_{n})\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

(1)

where
Y_{n}
is the
n
-th Bell polynomial. For example, it is well known that

\displaystyle Y_{1}(b_{1};a_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}b_{1},

\displaystyle Y_{2}(b_{1},b_{2};a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle Y_{3}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3};a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}b_{3}+a_{2}(3b_{1}b_{2})+a_{3}b_{1}^{3}.

Bell’s polynomials can be expressed explicitly by using the formula of Faá di Bruno ([3])

Y_{n}(b_{1},...,b_{n};a_{1},...,a_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}B_{n,k}a_{k},

(2)

where

B_{n,k}=\sum_{|p(n)|=k}\frac{n!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\cdots j_{n}!}\left[\frac{b_{1}}{1!}\right]^{j_{1}}\left[\frac{b_{2}}{2!}\right]^{j_{2}}\cdots\left[\frac{b_{n}}{n!}\right]^{j_{n}},

(3)

and the sum runs over all partitions
p(n)
of
n
, that is,
n=j_{1}+2j_{2}+\cdots+nj_{n}
,
j_{h}
denotes the number of parts of size
h
y
|p(n)|=j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{n}
is the length
of the partition
p(n)
.

Suppose
f
with power series representation and let

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}=f(u)\\
u(0)=x\end{cases}

(4)

be the autonomous differential equation with initial value problem.

In [5] autonomous polynomials were introduced, which are the coefficients of the analytical solution of the equation (
4
) expressed as a function of the initial value in the following way

\begin{cases}A_{1}(f(x))=f(x),\\
A_{n+1}(f(x),f^{\prime}(x),...,f^{(n)}(x))=Y_{n}(A_{1}(f(x)),...,A_{n}(f(x));f^{\prime}(x),...,f^{(n)}(x))\end{cases}

(5)

From (
5
) we can see that each autonomous polynomial
A_{n}
is defined on the sequence
(f^{(n)}(x))_{n\geq 0}
of derivatives of
f(x)\in C_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, where
C_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

is the set of all smoothness functions in
\mathbb{R}
. In this article, autonomous differential equations of order one with functions in
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
, the Hurwitz ring of exponential generating functions, are studied. The article is divided as follows. In the second section the ring
(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]],+,\cdot)
and its structure are studied. In the third section we define the autonomous operator and construct the autonomous ring
(\mathfrak{A}(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]),\boxplus,\odot)
that will serve as the basis for defining operations with flows of dynamical systems. Dynamic systems on rings are defined in section four. In this section it is proved that the flow of a dynamic system is a
R
-module acting on the phase space of the system. Finally, in section five the ring of generating functions of sequences in the autonomous ring is constructed. With this ring it is possible to define the one-dimensionals flows ring with which it will be possible to define flow operations. That is, we can decompose the flow of a dynamic system into simpler flows.

Throughout this paper
R
will be a ring of characteristic zero. Also,
\mathbb{N}
will be the monoid of non-negative integers and
\mathbb{N}_{1}
will be the semigroup of positive integers.

2
The Hurwitz ring
(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]],+,\cdot)

Throughout this paper let
(R,+,\cdot)
denote an integral domain. Let
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}=\{(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}:a_{n}\in R\}
denote the set of sequences with elements in the ring
R
. Take
\textbf{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\textbf{b}=(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}
. Define the sum in
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}
as

\textbf{a}+\textbf{b}=(a_{n}+b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\cdot
denote the Hadamard product of
a
and
b
given by
\textbf{a}\cdot\textbf{b}=(a_{n}\cdot b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, that is, the product in
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}
is a componentwise product. Then
(H_{R},+,\cdot)
is a ring with unit element
\textbf{1}=(1,1,1,...)
. An element
a
is invertible in
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}
with respect to the product
\cdot
if and only if
a_{n}\in R^{*}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Now let
*
denote the Hurwitz product in
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}
as

\textbf{a}\ast\textbf{b}=\left(\sum_{h=0}^{n}\binom{h}{n}a_{h}b_{n-h}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

(6)

Then
(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R},+,\ast)
is a ring with unit element
\textbf{e}=(1,0,0,...)
. An element

\textbf{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}
is invertible with respect to
\ast
if and only if

a_{0}\in R^{*}
. Let

\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}^{*}=\{\textbf{a}^{-1}:\textbf{a}\in\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}\}

denote the set of invertible elements in
(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R},+,*)
, where
\textbf{a}^{-1}=\textbf{b}=(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with

\begin{cases}b_{0}=a_{0}^{-1}\\
b_{n}=-a_{0}^{-1}\sum_{h=1}^{n}\binom{n}{h}a_{h}b_{n-h},\ n\geq 1.\end{cases}

(7)

Now denote
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
the set of formal power series of the form
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
with coefficients in
R
. It is clear that

(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]],+,\cdot)
is a ring with sum and product of ordinary series, that is,

f(x)+g(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(a_{n}+b_{n})\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

f(x)\cdot g(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}b_{n-k}\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

where
f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
,

g(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\in\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
. The ring
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
is
known as ring of Hurwitz of power series (veáse [4]).

Let
\rho_{x}:(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R},+,*)\rightarrow(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]],+,\cdot)
be an isomorphism given by

\rho_{x}(\textbf{a})=\rho_{x}((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=f(x).

If
\rho_{x}(\textbf{a})=f(x)
y
\rho_{x}(\textbf{b})=g(x)
, then

\rho_{x}(\textbf{a}\ast\textbf{b})=\rho_{x}(\textbf{a})\cdot\rho_{x}(\textbf{b})

and

\rho_{x}(\textbf{a}^{-1})=\frac{1}{\rho_{x}(\textbf{a})}=(\rho_{x}(\textbf{a}))^{-1}

Let
\delta
be a derivation on
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}
define by

\delta((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}})=(a_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

with
\delta(\textbf{a}*\textbf{b})=\delta(\textbf{a})\ast\textbf{b}+\textbf{a}*\delta(\textbf{b})

and let
\delta_{x}\equiv\frac{d}{dx}
be a derivation on
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
given by

\delta_{x}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{\delta_{x}x^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n+1}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

Then

\delta_{x}\rho_{x}(\textbf{a})=\rho_{x}(\delta\textbf{a}),

(8)

that is, the following diagram commutes

\textstyle{\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{x}}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\textstyle{\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{x}}
\textstyle{\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta_{x}}
\textstyle{\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]}

(9)

It is easy to see that
\rho_{x}(\mathop{\mbox{{Ker}}}\nolimits\delta)=\mathop{\mbox{{Ker}}}\nolimits\delta_{x}
, that is, the constant ring of

\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
is the image by
\rho_{x}
of the constant ring of
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}
.

3
The Ring Autonomous
(\mathfrak{A}(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]),\boxplus,\odot)

In this section the autonomous ring is constructed. This is a ring of sequences of autonomous polynomials defined on the ring
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
. We first define the autonomous operator and study its properties. We then use this operator to construct the binary operations of the autonomous ring. We start with the following definition

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{S}
be the ring of sequences on the ring
S=\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
. We define the

autonomous operator

\mathfrak{A}
as the linear map

\mathfrak{A}:\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]\rightarrow\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{S}
defined by

\mathfrak{A}(f(x))=(A_{0}(f(x)),A_{1}(f(x)),A_{2}(f(x)),A_{3}(f(x)),...)

(10)

where the
A_{n}
are recursively defined by

\displaystyle A_{0}(f(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x,

\displaystyle A_{1}(f(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x),

\displaystyle A_{n+1}(f(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{n}(A_{1}(f(x)),A_{2}(f(x))...,A_{n}(f(x));\delta^{1}f(x),...,\delta^{n}f(x)),

n\geq 1
.

The polynomials
A_{n}
in the indeterminate
\delta^{0}f,\delta^{1}f,...,\delta^{n-1}f
will be called
autonomous polynomials
.

Some autonomous polynomials
A_{n}
are

\displaystyle A_{2}(f(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1}(f(x))\delta^{1}f(x)=f(x)\delta f(x),

\displaystyle A_{3}(f(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{2}(f(x))\delta f(x)+A_{1}^{2}(f(x))\delta^{2}f(x),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)(\delta f(x))^{2}+f^{2}(x)\delta^{2}f(x),

\displaystyle A_{4}(f(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{3}(f(x))\delta^{1}f(x)+3A_{1}(f(x))A_{2}(f(x))\delta^{2}f(x)+A_{1}^{3}(f(x))\delta^{3}f(x),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\delta f(x))^{3}+4f^{2}(x)\delta^{1}f(x)\delta^{2}f(x)+f^{3}(x)\delta^{3}f(x).

In the following Lemma we will see how to construct the autonomous polynomial
A_{n}(f(x))
from the polynomial
A_{n-1}(f(x))

Lemma 1
.

Take
f(x)\in\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
. Then

A_{n+1}(f(x))=f(x)\delta(A_{n}(f(x))),

(11)

con
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

By definition
A_{2}(f)=f\delta(f)
. Now suppose by induction hypothesis that

A_{n}(f)=f\delta(A_{n-1}(f))
. Put
A_{n}\equiv A_{n}(f)
. On the one hand, we have

\displaystyle A_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta fA_{n}+\delta^{2}f\{A_{1}A_{n-1}+A_{2}A_{n-2}+\cdots\}+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(f)f\delta(A_{n-1})+\delta^{2}(f)\{ff\delta(A_{n-2})+f\delta(A_{1})A_{n-2}+\cdots\}+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f[\delta(f)\delta(A_{n-1})+\delta^{2}(f)\{f\delta(A_{n-2})+\delta(A_{1})A_{n-2}+\cdots\}+\cdots]

On the other hand,

\displaystyle f\delta(A_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\delta(\delta(f)A_{n-1}+\delta(f)\{A_{1}A_{n-2}+A_{2}A_{n-3}+\cdots\}+\cdots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f[\delta(f)\delta(A_{n-1})+\delta^{2}(f)A_{n-1}+\delta^{2}(f)\delta(A_{1})A_{n-2}+\delta^{2}(f)A_{1}\delta(A_{n-2})+\cdots]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f[\delta(f)\delta(A_{n-1})+\delta^{2}(f)\{f\delta(A_{n-2})+\delta(A_{1})A_{n-2}+\cdots\}+\cdots]

Now by comparing the results we obtain
A_{n+1}=f\delta(A_{n})
.
∎

From the above we obtain the following corollary

Corollary 1
.

Take
f(x)\in\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
. Then

A_{n}(f(x))=\overbrace{f(x)\delta(\cdots f(x)\delta(f(x)))}^{n}.

(12)

Now we will define operations in
\mathfrak{A}(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]])
that allow to give it a ring structure.

Definition 2
.

We define the sum
\boxplus
in
\mathfrak{A}(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]])
as follows

\mathfrak{A}(f)\boxplus\mathfrak{A}(g)=(B_{n}(f,g))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

where

\displaystyle B_{0}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x

(14)

\displaystyle B_{1}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1}(f)+A_{2}(g)

(15)

\displaystyle B_{n}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}(f)+A_{n}(g)+H_{n}(f,g),\ \ \ n\geq 2

(16)

where

is a polynomial in the variables

and

fulfilling

(17)

Lemma 2
.

Take
f(x),g(x)\in\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]]
. Then

\mathfrak{A}(f(x))\boxplus\mathfrak{A}(g(x))=\mathfrak{A}(f(x)+g(x))

(18)

Proof.

Finding the autonomous polynomials for the sum

we obtain

A_{n}(f+g)=A_{n}(f)+A_{n}(g)+H_{n}(f,g).

(19)

where
H_{n}(f,g)
is a polynomial in the variables
f,f^{\prime},...,f^{(n-1)}
and

g,g^{\prime},...,g^{(n-1)}
. If
H_{n}(f,g)
satisfies (
17
) and if we use the Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle A_{n+1}(f+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f+g)\delta A_{n}(f+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f+g)\delta[A_{n}(f)+A_{n}(g)+H_{n}(f,g)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\delta A_{n}(f)+g\delta A_{n}(g)+f\delta A_{n}(g)

\displaystyle+g\delta A_{n}(f)+(f+g)H_{n}(f,g)

\displaystyle A_{n+1}(f)+A_{n+1}(g)+H_{n+1}(f,g)

Then our affirmation is followed.

We want to find the additive inverse of the element
\mathfrak{A}(f)
in
\mathfrak{A}(\mathop{\mbox{{H}}}\nolimits_{R}[[x]])

Definition 3
.

Take
a\in R-\{0\}
. Let

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(R)
denote the set of all expansions
\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(a)
,

\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(R)

\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(a)\cdot\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(b)=\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(ab)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(a)\cdot(\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(b)\ast\mathop{\mbox{{exp}}}\nolimits(c))

The right cancellation property for certain classes of dendroidal anodynes
Miguel Barata

Abstract
. We generalize a previous result of Stevenson [ 9 ] to the category of dendroidal sets, yielding the right cancellation property of dendroidal inner anodynes within the class of normal monomorphisms. As an application of this property, we show how to construct a symmetric monoidal ∞ \infty -category 𝖤𝗇𝗏 ​ ( X ) ⊗ \mathsf{Env}(X)^{\otimes} from a dendroidal ∞ \infty -operad X X , in a way that generalizes the symmetric monoidal envelope of a coloured operad.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this note is to give a generalization of the right cancellation property of inner anodynes in the category of simplicial set
\mathsf{sSets}
to the category of dendroidal sets
\mathsf{dSets}
. We register here the central definition of this work.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
be classes of morphisms of a category
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{M}
is contained in
\mathcal{N}
. Then we say that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the
right cancellation property
within
\mathcal{N}
if given any string of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}

A\xrightarrow{i}B\xrightarrow{j}C

such that
i,j,ji
are in
\mathcal{N}
and additionally both
i
and
ji
are in
\mathcal{M}
, then
j
is also in
\mathcal{M}
.

A knowledge of which classes of simplicial morphisms satisfy the right cancellation property seems to be of particular interest, for instance to simplify certain conbinatorial arguments in
\mathsf{sSets}
. As an example of this phenomenon, one has the well-known result
[
3
, Prop. 5.34]
in simplicial homotopy theory which guarantees that, for any saturated class of simplicial morphisms
\mathcal{M}
satisfying the right cancellation property within the monomorphisms, if all the spine inclusion are in
\mathcal{M}
, then actually all inner anodynes are in
\mathcal{M}
. The upshot of such a result is that, under the circumstances described, one can often argue with spine inclusions in place of inner horn inclusions, which tends to be combinatorially easier to do.

For the sake of easy reference, we register below the simplicial statement we are aiming to generalize.

Theorem 1.2
.

The class of inner anodynes in
\mathsf{sSets}
satisfies the right cancellation property within the class of simplicial monomorphisms.

The first account and proof of Theorem
1.2
appears in
[
9
]
as Theorem 1.5 and seems to have gone mostly unnoticed in the literature on simplicial sets. In contrast, the right cancellation property for simplicial left and right anodynes is more well-known: it was already present in Joyal’s work on simplicial sets and quasicategories, for instance.

In what concerns the proof of the right cancellation property for simplicial left and right anodyne maps, the interested reader can check
[
6
, Cor. 4.1.2.2]
and
[
3
, Cor. 8.80]
for instance. In any case, we want to mention that these proofs are not entirely trivial, and rely on certain properties of left and right anodynes deduced from the existence of the contravariant and covariant model structures on the slice categories
\mathsf{sSets}_{/V}
. In contrast, this argument cannot be followed through in the inner anodyne scenario, due to the corresponding analogues of the contravariant and covariant model structures not existing. In this sense, Stevenson’s proof turns to be a better approach: it actually also works (and simplifies somewhat) if one wants to show the right cancellation property for left and right anodynes.

As we have already mentioned, our intention is to generalize Stevenson’s result to the context of dendroidal sets. This is a category of presheaves on the tree category
\mathsf{\Omega}
, first introduced by Moerdijk and Weiss in
[
8
]
, and this category play a role in the theory of operads analogous to the one played by
\mathsf{sSets}
in category theory. After giving a brief account in Section 2 of some notation and results on dendroidal sets, we will dedicate Section 3 to proving the following generalization of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Inner/leaf/root anodynes in
\mathsf{dSets}
satisfy the right cancellation property within the class of normal monomorphisms.

Here the classes of leaf and root anodynes play a similar role in the dendroidal formalism as that of left and right anodynes in
\mathsf{sSets}
, respectively. We remark that the right cancellation property for leaf anodynes is already known to hold: for instance, one can easily adapt the simplicial argument presented in
[
3
]
to the dendroidal context, using the existence of the version of the covariant model structure for the overcategories
\mathsf{dSets}_{/V}
. However, the version of Theorem
1.3
about inner and root anodynes doesn’t seem to be present in the literature
1
1

1

We note that, for the simplicial context, the result for left anodynes immediately implies the result for right anodynes, due to the existence of the involution
\mathsf{\Delta}\to\mathsf{\Delta}
which reverses the order of each poset. However, there is no parallel in
\mathsf{\Omega}
of this involution, and therefore the leaf anodyne result doesn’t imply the root anodyne one.
.

In Section 4 we give an application of the right cancellation property of dendroidal inner anodynes. We explain how one can assign to a dendroidal
\infty
-operad
X
a simplicial set
\mathsf{Env}(X)^{\otimes}
which recovers the usual construction of the symmetric monoidal envelope of a coloured operad. The exact result we will show is the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a dendroidal set. Then there exists a simplicial set
\mathsf{Env}(X)^{\otimes}
satisfying the following properties:

(a)

If
X
is an
\infty
-operad, then
\mathsf{Env}(X)^{\otimes}
is a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category in the sense of Lurie.

(b)

If
\mathbf{P}
is a coloured operad and
N\mathbf{P}\in\mathsf{dSets}
is the corresponding dendroidal nerve, then
\mathsf{Env}(N\mathbf{P})^{\otimes}
is the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category associated to the symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{Env}(\mathbf{P})
corresponding to the symmetric monoidal envelope of
\mathbf{P}
.

The main technical hurdle for proving this result will be handled in Section 4.1, where we use the right cancellation property of dendroidal inner anodynes to investigate a certain category of forests
\mathbb{F}
constructed from
\mathsf{Fin}_{\ast}
. Finally, in Section 4.2 we will use these dendroidal considerations to construct
\mathsf{Env}(X)^{\otimes}
and prove Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Some recollections about dendroidal sets

Before embarking on proving Theorem

The tree category

\diamond

\diamond

\diamond

The set of edges incident to a vertex

of

, and the remaining edges, which are pairwise independent in the ordering above, are the

of

. In the scenario when

admits no incoming edges, we say that the vertex

is a

.

\diamond

Equipped with this new formalism, one can easily transfer some of the notions from the homotopy theory of simplicial sets to the world of dendroidal sets. Let us point out some of these analogous constructions:

\diamond

\diamond

\diamond

\diamond

In the theory of simplicial sets, a necessary and crucial step in analysing the behaviour of the different classes of anodyne maps is to consider how these interact with the cartesian product in

{-}\otimes{-}:\mathsf{dSets}\times\mathsf{dSets}\rightarrow\mathsf{dSets}.

This is defined on representables via the formula

The dendroidal tensor product takes part in an adjunction, which, for any

\mathsf{dSets}(X\otimes A,Y)\cong\mathsf{dSets}(X,\mathsf{Hom}(A,Y)).

Remark 3.2
.

One can deduce Stevenson’s original simplicial version of Theorem
1.3
from the dendroidal variation we just presented. Indeed, if

is a string of simplicial monomorphisms with
ji
and
i
inner anodynes, then applying
\iota_{!}
to it yields a string of dendroidal sets

\iota_{!}A\xlongrightarrow{\iota_{!}i}\iota_{!}B\xlongrightarrow{\iota_{!}j}\iota_{!}C.

All the monomorphisms in questions are normal (being normal is meaningless in this context), and the dendroidal maps
\iota_{!}i
and

are inner anodynes. Therefore, by right cancellation so is

, which is equivalent to saying

is a simplicial inner anodyne. This argument also holds for the other two classes of anodynes we discussed.

4.
An application: the symmetric monoidal envelope of a dendroidal
\mathbf{\infty}
-operad

In this section we will give an application of the right cancellation property for dendroidal inner anodynes in order to explain how one can associate to any dendroidal set
X
a simplicial set
\mathsf{Env}(X)^{\otimes}
which, when applied to the dendroidal nerve of a coloured operad
X=N\mathbf{P}
, should recover the symmetric monoidal envelope of the operad, which we recall below. Here we will write
\underline{n}=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, which we often see also as the poset
\{1<2<\cdots<n\}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a coloured operad. We define the
symmetric monoidal envelope of
\mathbf{P}
is the symmetric monoidal category
(\mathsf{Env}(\mathbf{P}),\boxplus,\emptyset)
described by the following:

\diamond

The objects are the strings
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
of colours of
\mathbf{P}
, for
n\geq 0
. The empty string is respresented by
\emptyset
.

\diamond

A morphism

(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\longrightarrow(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})

is determined by a pair
(f,\{p_{j}:j\in\underline{m}\})
, where
f\colon\underline{n}\to\underline{m}
is a function of finite sets. For each
j\in\underline{m}
,
p_{j}
is an operation in
\mathbf{P}
with output
d_{j}
, and input set
\{c_{i}:f(i)=j\}\subseteq\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\}
ordered according to
\underline{n}
. Composition comes from the operadic composition of
\mathbf{P}
.

\diamond

The tensor product is given on objects via concatenation

(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\boxplus(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},d_{1},\dots,d_{m}),

with unit given by the empty tuple.

Remark 4.2
.

The symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{Env}(\mathbf{P})
has appeared under many different names throughout the years. They initially appeared under the name of PROP in
[
5
]
, connected to the study of cohomology operations by Adams and Mac Lane. In this work we are working with the notion of a
coloured
PROP, which is the one also appearing in the work of Boardman and Vogt in
[
1
]
, but not the one used by Hackney and Robertson in
[
4

.

For a great survey on the history of PROPs see
[

, and for more on coloured operads
[

F
(here we will again ignore the action of the face maps on the labelling data):

\diamond

\partial_{0}F
is obtained by removing the
\times
symbol from trees 2 and 3, and applying the root face to tree 4.

\diamond

\partial_{1}F
is obtained by removing the
\times
symbol from tree 1, applying the root face to trees 2 and 3, and contracting
w
in tree 4. When the root face is applied to trees 2 and 3, the symbol
\times
propagates to the newly-obtained trees.

\diamond

\partial_{2}F
is obtained by applying the root face to tree 1, contracting
x,y
and
z
in trees 2 and 3, and removing the stump in tree 4.

\diamond

\partial_{3}F
is obtained by applying the leaf faces in trees 1, 2 and 3.

It is not difficult to deduce from this the general pattern of the face maps on a generic element of
N\mathbb{F}_{k}
, by removing and contracting certain "layers" of the associated forest.

From now on we will ignore the information on a
k
-simplex of
N\mathbb{F}
given by the labelling, and instead think of such a simplex as a certain forest
F
. In particular, this means that we can see each simplex in
N\mathbb{F}
as a dendroidal set, as we explained at the start of the section. Before delving into the technical results we will need, we will present some useful definitions.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
F
a
k
-simplex in
N\mathbb{F}
.

(a)

We say a component tree
T
of
F
is
uprooted
if its graphical representation corresponds to a tree containing the symbol
\times
.

(b)

We write
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}F\subseteq F
for the dendroidal subset given by the union of the faces
\partial_{0}F\cup\partial_{k}F\subseteq F
.

(c)

For
0\leq j\leq n
, we write
\Lambda^{j}F\subseteq F
for the dendroidal set given by the union of all faces
\partial_{i}F
such that
i\neq j
.

The next two lemmas will be the main technical results that will be used in the next section. We point out that here is where we will make heavy use of the right cancellation property for inner anodynes, which lets us avoid complicated combinatorial arguments about the dendroidal category.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
F\in N\mathbb{F}_{k}
. Then the dendroidal inclusion
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}F\to F
is inner anodyne.

Proof.

Let us start by making some simplifying assumptions on
F
:

\diamond

We can assume that
F
is a tree
T
since the action of the face maps
\partial_{0}
and
\partial_{k}
can be restricted to each component of
F
.

\diamond

We can further assume that
T
is not uprooted: we would then have the equality
\partial_{0}T=T
as dendroidal sets and therefore
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}T=T
.

\diamond

If
k=2
then
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}T\to T
is the spine inclusion of
T
, which is always inner anodyne by
[
3
, Lem. 6.37]
. Therefore we may additionally assume
k\geq 3
.

Before discussing the general case, we will first exemplify what happens when
k=3
: writing
T
as a grafting
C_{n}\circ(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
for
n\geq 0
, then the dendroidal set
\partial_{0}F
is
T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{k}
, and therefore the inclusion
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}T\to T
will correspond to

j\colon T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{n}\cup\partial_{3}T\longrightarrow T,

where we note that the trees
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
and
\partial_{3}T
are elements of
N\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We can now apply
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}
to each of the components of the domain of
j
, leading to the string of composable maps

\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}^{2}T:=\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}T_{n}\cup\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}(\partial_{3}T)\xlongrightarrow{i}T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{n}\cup\partial_{3}T\xlongrightarrow{j}T.

The dendroidal inclusion
ji\colon\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}^{2}(T)\to T
is inner anodyne, since it coincides with the spine inclusion of
T
. Moreover,
i
is also inner anodyne by the induction hypothesis. We can now apply the right cancellation property for inner anodynes to conclude that
j
is also inner anodyne, as we wanted to show.

For the general case, we first note that we can extend the previous definition of
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}^{2}T
to arbitrary higher powers
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}^{n}T
by inductively applying the operator
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}
to each component of
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}^{n-1}T
. For a general
T\in N\mathbb{F}_{k}
, we consider now the dendroidal inclusions

\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}^{k-1}T\longrightarrow\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}T\longrightarrow T.

We see that the composite is again just the spine inclusion, and
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}^{k-1}T\rightarrow\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}T
is inner anodyne by the induction hypothesis. Consequently, another application of Theorem
1.3
finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
F\in N\mathbb{F}_{k}
. Then, for each
0<j<k
, the horn inclusion
\Lambda^{j}F\to F
is dendroidal inner anodyne.

Proof.

The case when
k=2
is exactly Lemma
4.5
, so we can assume that
k\geq 3
from now on. Furthermore, by the type of arguments as at the start of the previous lemma, we can additionally assume that
F
has no uprooted components.

For
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
, set
\partial_{\leq\ell}F\subseteq\Lambda^{j}F
to be the dendroidal subset obtained from
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}F
by attaching the faces
\partial_{i}F
, with
0\leq i\leq\ell
and
i\neq j
, which leads to a filtration

\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}F=\partial_{\leq 0}F\subseteq\partial_{\leq 1}F\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\partial_{\leq k-1}F=\Lambda^{j}F.

It suffices to show that each stage of this filtration is inner anodyne: indeed, the previous lemma together with the right cancellation property for inner anodynes applied to
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}F\to\Lambda^{j}F\to F
will then finish the proof.

The first stage of the filtration can be represented via the following pushout square in
\mathsf{dSets}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}\partial_{1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\partial_{1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\partial_{\leq 1}F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

The left map is inner anodyne by Lemma
4.5
and therefore so is
\partial_{\mathrm{rl}}F\to\partial_{\leq 1}F
. For the higher stages of the filtration we have instead the description below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\partial_{\leq i-1}\left(\partial_{i}F\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\partial_{\leq i-1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\partial_{i}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\partial_{\leq i}F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Since
\partial_{i}F\in N\mathbb{F}_{k-1}
, we conclude that
\partial_{\leq i-1}\left(\partial_{i}F\right)\to\partial_{i}F
is inner anodyne, by the induction hypothesis on both the height
k
of the forest and on the stage of the filtration
i
. By the closure of inner anodynes under pushouts, the same holds for
\partial_{\leq i-1}F\to\partial_{\leq i}F
, as we wanted to show.
∎

4.2.
The symmetric monoidal envelope of a dendroidal
\infty
-operad

The reason for introducing and discussing the category
\mathbb{F}
in the previous section is that it will help us encode the symmetric monoidal structure of
\mathsf{Env}(\mathbf{P})
. However, this will not be enough for our purposes, since we also need to keep track of the colours of our operad. Instead, we will consider a slightly more elaborate version of
\mathbb{F}
, which we introduce now.

Definition 4.7
.

The category of
decorated forests

\mathbb{F}^{\text{dec}}
is the category given by the following data:

\diamond

An object is given by an
n

(M_{1},\ldots,M_{n})
for
n\geq 0
, where each
M_{i}
is the forest
k_{i}\cdot\eta
for some integer
k_{i}\geq 0
. Such an
n
-tuple partitions the edges of the forest
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}
.

\diamond

A morphism in
\mathbb{F}^{\text{dec}}

(L_{1},\ldots,L_{\ell})\xlongrightarrow{(\beta,F)}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{m})

is given by a function of finite pointed sets
\beta\colon\langle\ell\rangle\to\langle m\rangle
, together with a forest
F
which is a morphism of
\mathbb{F}
. Additionally, these must interact with each other in the following way:

(a)

The root edges of
F
are partitioned according to the
M_{i}
’s, and all the remaining edges of
F
(these are the leaves of the corollas and the edges of the uprooted components) are partitioned according to the
L_{j}
’s.

(b)

The set of leaves over the edges in
M_{i}
is the union of the edges contained in the
L_{j}
’s satisfying
\beta(j)=i
.

The identity morphism will correspond to seeting
\beta
to be the corresponding identity function, and
F
is a forest of only 1-corollas.

\diamond

The composition of the morphisms

(L_{1},\ldots,L_{\ell})\xlongrightarrow{(\beta_{1},F_{1})}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{m})\xlongrightarrow{(\beta_{2},F_{2})}(N_{1},\ldots,N_{n})

is the pair
(\beta_{2}\beta_{1},F_{2}F_{1})
, where
F_{2}F_{1}
comes from the composition in
\mathbb{F}
, see Remark
4.8
for a clarification on how the grafting is done.

Remark 4.8
.

As we have done before, when defining the morphisms in
\mathbb{F}^{\text{dec}}
we have omitted the information regarding the function
\mathsf{input}
that labels the inputs of
F
. However, the set up of

gives a canonical choice of such a labelling: indeed, the leaves of

form the forest of edges

, together with the usual order of the indexing set

\mathbb{F}^{\text{dec}}
.

Definition 4.10

as a simplicial set over the nerve of finite pointed sets via the composite of

\mathsf{Env}(X)^{\otimes}\longrightarrow N\mathbb{F}^{\text{dec}}\xlongrightarrow{\mathsf{part}}N\mathsf{Fin}_{\ast},

\mathsf{Env}(X)^{\otimes}\to N\mathbb{F}^{\text{dec}}

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a coloured operad. Then
\mathsf{Env}(N\mathbf{P})^{\otimes}
is the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category associated to the (strict) symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{Env}(\mathbf{P})
.

Proof.

One can easily describe the associated symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{Env}(N\mathbf{P})^{\otimes}_{\langle 1\rangle}
from Definition
4.10
(note that since we are working over the object
\langle 1\rangle
, the partition information does not exist):

\diamond

The set of objects of
\mathsf{Env}(N\mathbf{P})^{\otimes}_{\langle 1\rangle}
is the coproduct
\coprod_{n\geq 0}N\mathbf{P}_{\eta}^{\times n},

and consequently an object is just a tuple of colours
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
of
\mathbf{P}
, for
n\geq 0
.

\diamond

A morphism
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})\to(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
will be given by an
n
-tuple

(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in X_{C_{k_{1}}}\times\cdots\times X_{C_{k_{n}}},

for integers
k_{i}\geq 0
satisfying
\sum_{i}k_{i}=m
. Moreover, the
c_{i}
’s and
d_{j}
’s are the values of
X
on the leaves and roots of these corollas, respectively.

This can be equivalently encoded by a function of sets
f\colon\underline{m}\to\underline{n}
where
f(i)=j
if
c_{i}
and
d_{j}
belong to the same corolla, and operations
p_{j}
in
\mathbf{P}
with output
d_{j}
and inputs given by the
c_{i}
’s satisfying
f(i)=j
ordered according to
\underline{n}
.

\diamond

By the proof of part (c) of Proposition
4.11
, the monoidal structure is induced by the concatenation of forests
(F,G)\mapsto F\oplus G
. On
\mathsf{Env}(N\mathbf{P})^{\otimes}_{\langle 1\rangle}
this corresponds to the concatenation of tuples of colours.

A quick comparison with Definition
4.1
readily shows that this coincides with
\mathsf{Env}(\mathbf{P})
. It is also clear that
\mathsf{Env}(N\mathbf{P})^{\otimes}
is the usual construction of a symmetric monoidal
\infty

∎

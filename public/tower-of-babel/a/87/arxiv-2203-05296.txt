A new proof of a theorem of Tietze
Daniel Duverney, Iekata Shiokawa

Abstract
. We give a new proof of Tietze Theorem on the convergence of infinite semi-regular continued fractions.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
11A55

The purpose of this note is to give a new proof of Theorem
1

below, known as
Tietze Theorem
[
4
]
. By Theorem
1
,
any infinite semi-regular continued fraction (
[
5
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
2
]
) is convergent.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that the infinite continued fraction

(1)

\alpha:=b_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}

satisfies the following conditions:

(2)

a_{n}\in\left\{-1,1\right\},\qquad b_{n}\in\left[1,+\infty\right[,\qquad b_{n}+a_{n+1}\geq 1\qquad\left(n\geq 1\right).

Then
\alpha
is convergent.

Tietze original proof of Theorem
1
(1911) rested on a geometrical
representation of the convergents
p_{n}/q_{n}
as points
(p_{n},q_{n})
in
the plane
[
5
]
. Shortly after (1913), Oskar Perron gave a different
proof by using continuants
[
4
]
. Almost 100 years later, Keith Matthews
challenged Alan Offer to find a proof of Theorem
1
avoiding the
use of continuants. In
[
3
]
, Offer succeeded in doing so in the proof
of Lemma
3
below, but not in the rest of the proof. In this note we
develop Offer’s ideas and give a new simple proof of Tietze Theorem without
using at all continuants.

Let
\alpha
be the continued fraction defined by (
1
). As usual, we
define for
n\geq 1

(3)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}p_{-1}=1,&p_{0}=b_{0},&p_{n}=b_{n}p_{n-1}+a_{n}p_{n-2},\\
q_{-1}=0,&q_{0}=1,&q_{n}=b_{n}q_{n-1}+a_{n}q_{n-2}.\end{array}\right.

It is well known that

(4)

b_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\qquad\left(n\geq 1\right),

and an easy induction using (
3
) shows that

(5)

p_{n}q_{n-1}-p_{n-1}q_{n}=\left(-1\right)^{n-1}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}\qquad\left(n\geq 1\right),

which yields immediately

(6)

\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}=b_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k-1}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}}{q_{k-1}q_{k}}.

First we prove Theorem
1
in the following very special case,
connected to negative continued fractions
[
2
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that
a_{n}=-1
for all
n\geq 1.
Then the continued
fraction (
1
) is well defined and convergent.

Proof.

We follow
[
1
]
. By (
3
) and (
2
) we have for
n\geq 0

q_{n+1}-q_{n}=\left(b_{n+1}-1\right)q_{n}-q_{n-1}\geq q_{n}-q_{n-1}\geq\ldots\geq q_{0}-q_{-1}=1.

Hence
q_{n}\geq n+1
for all
n\geq 0,
which proves that the continued
fraction (
1
) is well defined and convergent by (
6
).
∎

We now return to the general case. Since
q_{n+1}<q_{n}
when
\left(a_{n+1},b_{n+1}\right)=\left(-1,1\right),
the above proof cannot be used.

Lemma 2
.

Let
q_{n}
be defined by (
3
). We have for
n\geq 0

(7)

q_{n}\geq 1,\quad q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq 1.

Proof.

By following Offer
[
3
, Lemma 1]
, we first observe that

(8)

q_{n+1}+a_{n+2}q_{n}=\left(b_{n+1}+a_{n+2}\right)q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\quad\left(n\geq 0\right).

Hence by induction
q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq q_{0}+a_{1}q_{-1}=1
for all

n\geq 0,
which yields

(9)

q_{n+1}=b_{n+1}q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq 1\quad\left(n\geq 0\right).

Therefore (
7
) holds.
∎

Lemma
2
shows that the convergents of the continued fraction
\alpha

defined by (
1
) and (
2
) are well defined since
q_{n}\geq 1

for all
n\geq 0.\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt

The following lemma is proved in
[
4
]
by using continuants and in

[
3
]
without using them. We give here a new proof, also without using continuants.

Lemma 3
.

Let
q_{n}
be defined by (
3
). Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n}=\infty.

Proof.

As observed in
[
3
, Formula (3)]
, it is clear by (
8
) and
(
9
) that

(10)

q_{n}\geq q_{m}+a_{m+1}q_{m-1}\geq 1\qquad\left(n>m\geq 1\right).

Assume that there exists
A\geq 1
such that
1\leq q_{n}\leq A
for infinitely
many
n.
Then there exists
B\geq 1
and an increasing sequence
n_{k}
such
that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}q_{n_{k}}=B.
Let
C\geq 1
be the least real
number having this property. By (
10
) with
n=n_{k+1}
and
m=n_{k}
, we
have for all large
k

C+\frac{1}{3}\geq C-\frac{1}{3}+a_{n_{k}+1}q_{n_{k}-1}\quad\text{and}\quad C+\frac{1}{3}+a_{n_{k}+1}q_{n_{k}-1}\geq 1.

This yields immediately
a_{n_{k}+1}=-1,
and therefore
q_{n_{k}-1}\leq C-2/3
for all large
k.
This contradicts the minimality of
C
and proves
Lemma
3
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Define for
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 1

(11)

x_{n,k}:=\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{n+2}}{b_{n+2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{n+k}}{b_{n+k}}.

Then for
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 1

(12)

0<x_{n,k}\leq 1\text{ if }a_{n+1}=1,\qquad-1\leq x_{n,k}<0\text{ if }a_{n+1}=-1.

Proof.

The proof follows
[
2
, Proposition 3]
and is by induction on
k.
If

k=1,
then
x_{n,1}=a_{n+1}/b_{n+1}
and so (
2
) implies
(
12
) for
k=1
and any
n\geq 0.
Assume that (
12
) holds for
some
k\geq 1
and any
n\geq 0.
Then by (
11
) we have

(13)

x_{n,k+1}=\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}+x_{n+1,k}},

where for any
n\geq 0

(14)

0<x_{n+1,k}\leq 1\text{ if }a_{n+2}=1,\qquad-1\leq x_{n+1,k}<0\text{ if
}a_{n+2}=-1.

If
b_{n+1}\geq 2,
then by (
14
)

(15)

b_{n+1}+x_{n+1,k}\geq 1.

Otherwise, we have
b_{n+1}<2
and therefore
a_{n+2}=1
by (
2
), and
so we find again (
15
) by the first inequality in (
14
).
Hence (
13
) and (
15
) imply

0<x_{n,k+1}\leq 1\text{ if }a_{n+1}=1,\qquad-1\leq x_{n,k+1}<0\text{ if
}a_{n+1}=-1,

which proves (
12
) by induction.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
x_{n,k}
be defined by (
11
). Then

(16)

\frac{p_{n+k}}{q_{n+k}}=\frac{p_{n}+x_{n,k}p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+x_{n,k}q_{n-1}}\qquad\left(n\geq 0,k\geq 1\right).

Proof.

By induction on
n.
Clearly (
16
) is true for
n=0
and every

k\geq 1
since

\frac{p_{0}+x_{0,k}p_{-1}}{q_{0}+x_{0,k}q_{-1}}=b_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{k}}{b_{k}}=\frac{p_{k}}{q_{k}}.

Assume that (
16
) is true for a given
n\geq 0
and all
k\geq 1.
By
(
13
), we see that

\frac{p_{n+1+k}}{q_{n+1+k}}=\frac{p_{n+k+1}}{q_{n+k+1}}=\frac{p_{n}+x_{n,k+1}p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+x_{n,k+1}q_{n-1}}=\frac{p_{n}\left(b_{n+1}+x_{n+1,k}\right)+a_{n+1}p_{n-1}}{q_{n}\left(b_{n+1}+x_{n+1,k}\right)+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}},

which proves (
16
) by induction.

∎

From Lemmas
5
and
4
we deduce immediately that

(17)

\left|\frac{p_{n+k}}{q_{n+k}}-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}\left|q_{n}+x_{n,k}q_{n-1}\right|}\qquad\left(n\geq 0,k\geq 1\right).

Now we prove Tietze Theorem. By Lemma
1
, we can assume that there
exist infinitely many
m
such that
a_{m+1}=1.
For every large
n,
let

m=m(n)
be the greatest integer less than
n
such that
a_{m+1}=1.
Clearly

m
tends to infinity with
n.
For every large
n
and every
k\geq 1,
we
have by (
17
)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{p_{n+k}}{q_{n+k}}-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{p_{n+k}}{q_{n+k}}-\frac{p_{m}}{q_{m}}\right|+\left|\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}-\frac{p_{m}}{q_{m}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q_{m}\left|q_{m}+x_{m,n+k-m}q_{m-1}\right|}+\frac{1}{q_{m}\left|q_{m}+x_{m,n-m}q_{m-1}\right|}.

However
x_{m,n+k-m}>0
and
x_{m,n-m}>0
by Lemma
4
. Hence

\left|\frac{p_{n+k}}{q_{n+k}}-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{2}{q_{m\left(n\right)}^{2}}

for all large
n
and all
k\geq 1.
As
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{m\left(n\right)}=\infty
by Lemma
3
,
p_{n}/q_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence,
which proves Tietze Theorem.

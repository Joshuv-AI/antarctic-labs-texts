Sprays on Hamel-Funk Functions Model
Guojun Yang

Abstract
Hamel functions of a spray play an important role in the study of the projective metrizability of the concerned spray, and Funk functions are special Hamel functions. A Finsler metric is a special Hamel function of the spray induced by the metric itself and a Funk metric is a special Funk function of a Minkowski spray. In this paper, we study sprays on a Hamel or Funk function model. Firstly, we give some basic properties of a Hamel or Funk function of a spray and some curvature properties of a Hamel or Funk function in projective relations. We use the Funk metric to construct a family of sprays and obtain some of their curvature properties and their metrizability conditions. Secondly, we consider the existence of Funk functions on certain spray manifold. We prove that there exist local Funk functions on a R-flat spray manifold, and on certain projectively flat Berwald spray manifolds, we construct a multitude of nonzero Funk functions. Finally, we introduce a new class of sprays called Hamel or Funk sprays associated to given sprays and Hamel or Funk functions. We obtain some special properties of a Hamel or Funk spray of scalar curvature, especially on its metrizability and a special form of its Riemann curvature. Keywords: Spray, Finsler Metric, Metrizability, Scalar (Isotropic, Constant) Curvature, Hamel-Funk Function, Hamel-Funk Spray 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 53C60, 53B40

Introduction

Spray geometry studies the properties of sprays on a manifold,
and it is closely related to Finsler geometry. Every Finsler
metric induces a natural spray but there are a lot of sprays which
cannot be induced by any Finsler metric (
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
16
,
17
]
). A spray
{\bf G}
on a manifold
M
defines a
special vector filed on a conical region
\mathcal{C}
of

TM\setminus\{0\}
, and it naturally defines the integral curves and the
projections of the integral curves onto the manifold
M
are
called geodesics. For deep understanding of spray geometry, it is
important to develop more basic curvatures, to study some classes of sprays with
special geometric properties and to investigate the (projective) metrizablity
of a spray under certain curvature conditions.

Many basic curvatures, such as Riemann curvature, Ricci curvature,
Weyl curvature, Berwald curvature and Douglas curvature, appearing
in Finsler geometry, are actually defined in spray geometry via
the spray coefficients. With a fixed volume form, the S-curvature,
originally introduced in Finsler geometry by Z. Shen in

[
12
]
, can also be generalized to spray geometry. The
notions for a Finsler metric of scalar flag curvature, isotropic
flag curvature and constant flag curvature, already have the
corresponding generalization in spray geometry, and they are
respectively called a spray of scalar curvature, isotropic
curvature and constant curvature. A spray of scalar curvature
first appears in Shen’s book
[
14
]
(therein, it is
originally called an isotropic spray). In
[
9
]
, B. Li and Z.
Shen introduce a spray of isotropic curvature. The present author
introduces a spray of constant curvature in
[
17
]
. Further
studies show that these curvatures or curvature characters play an
important role in spray geometry.

Among all sprays, some special classes of sprays are helpful in
understanding the geometric structures of a general spray
(
[
4
,
8
,
17
]
). A Berald spray means that its spray
coefficients are polynomials and every Berwald metric induces a
Berwald spray. A (locally) projectively flat spray means its
geodesics are (local) straight lines, and it’s the Hilbert’s
Fourth Problem to study the structure of a (locally) projectively
flat spray, especially when the spray is induced by a Finsler
metric. These sprays and sprays of scalar curvature (including
isotropic, constant curvature) have some special good properties
and deserve further studies.

The metrizability problem for a spray
{\bf G}
seeks for a
Finsler metric whose spray is just
G
, and the projective
metrizability of a spray
G
aims to look for a Finsler
metric projectively related to
G
. So a natural question is
to determine whether a given spray is (projectively)
Finsler-metrizable or not under certain curvature conditions.
Quite a few papers concentrate on this problem (
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
16
,
17
]
). For instance, any spray
of scalar curvature is locally projectively metrizable (
[

,
3
]
); the metrizability for a spray of scalar curvature with
nonzero Ricci curvature is solved (
[
4
]
);
the local structure is determined
for a projectively flat Berwald spray of isotropic curvature to be
metrizable (
[
17
]
).

In this paper, we are going to study some geometric properties of
Hamel-Funk functions and Hamel-Funk sprays. The notion of Funk
function first appears in Shen’s book
[
14
]
. Hamel-Funk
sprays are a new class of sprays we introduce in this paper.

Let

be a spray on a manifold

A Hamel or

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional manifold. A conical region

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(M)

means

\mathcal{C}_{x}:=\mathcal{C}\cap T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}

\lambda y\in\mathcal{C}_{x}

\lambda>0,y\in\mathcal{C}_{x}

is a smooth

on a conical region

{\bf G}=y^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}-2G^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}},

The Riemann curvature tensor

R^{i}_{\ k}:=2\partial_{k}G^{i}-y^{j}(\partial_{j}\dot{\partial_{k}}G^{i})+2G^{j}(\dot{\partial_{j}}\dot{\partial_{k}}G^{i})-(\dot{\partial_{j}}G^{i})(\dot{\partial_{k}}G^{j}),

(5)

\partial_{k}:=\partial/\partial x^{k},\dot{\partial}_{k}:=\partial/\partial y^{k}
. The

. A spray

is said to be

is said to be of

(

The three notions are natural generalizations for a Finsler metric
of scalar/isotropic/constant flag curvature in Finsler geometry.

A spray

G^{i}_{hjk}:=\dot{\partial}_{h}\dot{\partial}_{j}\dot{\partial}_{k}G^{i}=0

\bar{G}^{i}=G^{i}+Py^{i}

A spray

is said to be locally projectively flat if

6
Hamel-Funk Sprays

In this section we introduce a new spray associated to a spray
and a Hamel function, and then study some properties of this new
spray.

For a given spray
G
and a nonzero homogeneous function
Q

of degree one, we consider a spray
{\bf\bar{G}}
defined by

\bar{G}^{i}:=G^{i}+\frac{Q_{;0}}{2Q}y^{i}.

(49)

When
{\bf G},Q
satisfies certain good conditions,
{\bf\bar{G}}
is metrizable (Theorem
1.3
below). So in general
case,
{\bf\bar{G}}
is pre-metrizable. For short,
{\bf\bar{G}}
is called a PM-spray, and
{\bf G},Q
are respectively
called the adjoint spray and adjoint function of
{\bf\bar{G}}
.

Lemma 6.1

Each spray in the projective class
Proj({\bf G})
of a spray
G
is a local PM-spray
{\bf\bar{G}}
defined by (
49
) for
some
Q
.

Proof :
For an arbitrary spray
{\bf\bar{G}}\in Proj({\bf G})
, we have
\bar{G}^{i}=G^{i}+Py^{i}
for some function
P
. Consider
the following system of PDEs with the unknown function
Q
:

\bar{Y}(Q)=2PQ,\ \ \ \ \ Y(Q)=Q,\ \ \ \ \big(\bar{Y}:=y^{i}\delta_{i},\ \ Y=y^{i}\dot{\partial}y^{i}\big).

(50)

The integral condition of (
50
) is given by

[\bar{Y},Y](Q)=\bar{Y}Y(Q)-Y\bar{Y}(Q).

Applying
[\bar{Y},Y]=-\bar{Y}
and (
50
), it is easy to
verify that the above integral condition is satisfied. So
(
50
) has a local solution
Q
. Q.E.D.

In Lemma
6.1
,
Q
is generally not unique. For instance,
letting

G^{i}:=0,\ \ \ \ \ P:=-\frac{x^{1}y^{1}+x^{2}y^{2}}{1+(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}},\ \ \ \ \ Q:=\frac{f(\frac{y^{2}}{y^{1}},\frac{x^{2}y^{1}-x^{1}y^{2}}{y^{1}})}{1+(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}\ y^{1},

(51)

where
f=f(s,t)
is some function.
Then
Q
satisfies (
50
).

Lemma 6.2

A PM-spray is projectively
invariant with a fixed adjoint function.

Proof :
Let
{\bf\bar{G}}_{1},{\bf\bar{G}}_{2}
be two
PM-sprays associated to
({\bf G}_{1},Q),({\bf G}_{2},Q)

respectively, where
{\bf G}_{1}
and
{\bf G}_{2}
are projectively
related with
G_{1}^{i}=G_{2}^{i}+Py^{i}
. By a direct computation, we have
(see (
29
))

Q_{|i}=Q_{;i}-(PQ)_{.i},\ \ \ \ Q_{|0}=Q_{;0}-2PQ,

where
|
and
;
denote the horizontal derivatives of
{\bf G}_{1}
and
{\bf G}_{2}
respectively. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{G}^{i}_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182pt=G^{i}_{1}+\frac{Q_{|0}}{2Q}y^{i}=G_{2}^{i}+Py^{i}+\frac{Q_{;0}-2PQ}{2Q}y^{i}

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182pt=G^{i}_{2}+\frac{Q_{;0}}{2Q}y^{i}=\bar{G}^{i}_{2},

which completes the proof. Q.E.D.

Lemma
6.2
is similar to the result of the spray defined
by the S-curvature (
[

]
). For a PM-spray associated to

({\bf G},Q)

Definition 6.3

It is clear that the Funk spray

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{.i;0}=Q\lambda_{.i;0}+Q_{;0}\lambda_{.i}+\lambda Q_{.i;0}=Q\lambda_{.i;0}+Q_{;0}\lambda_{.i}+\lambda Q_{;i},

where in the third formula above we have used the assumptions

\lambda_{;0}=0
and
Q_{.i;0}=Q_{;i}
. Therefore,
\bar{Q}
is a
Hamel function if and only if

Q\lambda_{;i}+\lambda Q_{;i}=Q\lambda_{.i;0}+Q_{;0}\lambda_{.i}+\lambda Q_{;i},

which reduces to the first formula in (
60
) since

\lambda_{.i;0}=\lambda_{;0.i}-\lambda_{;i}=-\lambda_{;i}
.
Conversely, if the first formula in (
60
) holds, it is
easy to get
\lambda_{;0}=0
by contracting this formula by
y^{i}
.
Further, the above proof has shown that
\bar{Q}
is Hamel
function.

Now under the spray
{\bf\bar{G}}
, a direct computation gives

\lambda_{\bar{;}i}=\lambda_{;i}-\frac{Q_{;0}}{2Q}\lambda_{.i},

which implies
\lambda_{\bar{;}i}=0\Longleftrightarrow 2Q\lambda_{;i}=Q_{;0}\lambda_{.i}
.
Q.E.D.

Introduction

In the paper
[
20

maximal subgroups project
, Michael Aschbacher introduced eight classes
C_{1}
-
C_{8}
8 of subgroups of finite simple classical groups. The groups from these classes are ”obvious” maximal subgroups of a finite classical groups. To be precise, each subgroup from an Aschbacher class either is maximal itself or is contained in a maximal subgroup that in its turn either also belongs to an Aschbacher class or can be constructed by a certain explicit procedure.

Nikolai Vavilov defined five classes of “large” subgroups of the Chevalley groups (including exceptional ones) over arbitrary rings (see
[
17
]
for details). Although these
subgroups are not maximal, he conjectured that they are sufficiently large for the corresponding overgroup lattice to admit a description. One of these classes is the class of
subsystem subgroups (the definition will be given in Subsection 2.1).

Overgroups of (elementary) subsystem subgroups of the general linear group were studied in the papers
[

,

.In that case, subsystem subgroups are precisely block-diagonal subgroups. This results were generalized to orthogonal and symplectic groups under the assumption
2\in R^{*}
in the thesis of Nikolai Vavilov (see also
[
7

,

and

The problem we discuss in the present paper is to describe the overgroups of subsystem
subgroups of exceptional groups (over a commutative ring). This problem was posed
in

[

By a standard description we mean
”sandwich classification”
. Let
G
be an abstract
group, and let
\mathcal{L}
be a certain lattice of its subgroups. The lattice
\mathcal{L}
admits sandwich classification if it is a disjoint union of ”sandwiches”:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\bigsqcup_{i}L(F_{i},N_{i})\text{,}

\displaystyle L(F_{i},N_{i})=\{H\colon F_{i}\leqslant H\leqslant N_{i}\}\text{,}

where
i
runs through some index set, and
F_{i}
is a normal subgroup of
N_{i}
for all
i
. To study such a lattice, it suffices to study the quotients
N_{i}/F_{i}
. In
[
14
]
it was conjectured that the lattice of subgroups of a Chevalley group that contain a sufficiently large subsystem subgroup admits sandwich classification.

In the present paper, we prove a sandwich classification theorem for the embeddings
A_{l-1}\leqslant D_{l}
(where
l\geqslant 5
),
D_{5}\leqslant E_{6}
, and
E_{6}\leqslant E_{7}

all three cases simultaneously. In all these cases, the corresponding subsystem subgroup
is (up to a torus) a Levi subgroup

is a fundamental root such that its coefficient in the decomposition of a maximal root is equal to one. In that case, the level
is determined by two ideals. One can add to this list the embedding

, for which
the main result follows, of course, from the paper
[
3
]
. The fact that in all these cases the corresponding unipotent radical is Abelian simplifies our problem from a technical point of view.

Overgroup description for the embeding

is a special case of the results
obtained in
[

and

2.2.
Affine schemes

The functor
G(\Phi,-)
from the category of rings to the category
of groups is an affine group scheme (a Chevalley–Demazure scheme). This means that
its composition with the forgetful functor to the category of sets is representable, i.e.,

G(\Phi,R)=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\mathbb{Z}[G],R)\text{.}

The ring
\mathbb{Z}[G]
here is called the
ring of regular functions
on the scheme
G(\Phi,-)
.

An element
g_{\gen}\in G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z}[G])
, that corresponds to the identity ring homomorphism is
called the
generic element
of the scheme
G(\Phi,-)
. This element has a universal property:
for any ring
R
and for any
g\in G(\Phi,R)
, there exists a unique ring homomorphism

f\colon\mathbb{Z}[G]\to R

such that
f_{*}(g_{\gen})=g
. For details about application of the method of generic element
to the problems similar to our problem see the paper of Alexei Stepanov
[
32
]
. We will
use this method in the second part of Lemma
32
to avoid problems with zero divisors of
the ring
R
.

Later, we will define the scheme
\mathfrak{R}(-)
of the root type elements and introduce a
similar notation for it.

The identity element of the group
G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z})
corresponds to the augmentation homomorphism

\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{Z}[G]\to\mathbb{Z}\text{.}

Its kernel is called the
augmentation
ideal. We denote it by
I_{\aug}
.

2.3.
Representation
V

Let
\Lambda
be the set of weights of the representation

. A basis
of
V
V is indexed by the weights from

The order we fixed on the system

Root elements of the group

x_{\alpha}(\xi)v^{\lambda}=\begin{cases}v^{\lambda}+v^{\lambda+\alpha}c_{\lambda\alpha}\xi\quad&\text{if }\lambda+\alpha\in\Lambda\text{,}\\
v^{\lambda}\quad&\text{if }\lambda+\alpha\notin\Lambda\text{,}\end{cases}

where the structure constants

(Matsumoto’s lemma
[

]
,
[

]
). Moreover, we choose

to be a crystal basis, i.e.,

if

. The
existence of such a basis was proved in
[
34
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
9
]
.

Therefore, the weight diagram describes the action of

on the module

completely. This action has a unique extension that is an algebraic action of the group

.

Weights from
\Lambda

]
). To describe

For any pair of weights

Lemma 5

The coefficient of

Proof.

Lemma 6

The set
\Phi\setminus\Delta
has exactly two

-orbits

Proof.

We prove that if a root

One can obtain the maximal root from the root

Corollary 2
.

(to Theorem
2
) Let
R
be a finitely generated ring, and let
\sigma=(I^{+},I^{-})
be a pair of ideals of
R
. Then there exists a natural number
N
such that

D^{N}N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)=E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\text{.}

Proof.

In the cases of the first type, the statement follows from Theorem
2
and Lemma
18
. We consider the case of the second type.

By Lemma
18
, it suffices to prove the inclusion

\left[N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right),N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)\right]\leqslant G(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\text{.}

We prove the inclusion

\left[N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right),N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)\right]\leqslant G(\Phi,\Delta,R,(R,I^{-}))\text{,}

The proof of the inclusion into
G(\Phi,\Delta,R,(I^{+},R))
is similar (the form
q
from Lemma
21
should be transferred to the dual representation via the isomorphism described in Lemma
20
).

Replacing the ring by its quotient
R/I^{-}
, we may assume that
I^{-}=(0)
. Therefore,
we need to prove the inclusion

\left[N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right),N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)\right]\leqslant P\text{.}

Since the ring
R
is finitely generated, it is Noetherian; hence it is a direct product of rings with connected spectra. Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume that the spectrum of
R
is connected.

Case 1
.
I^{+}\neq(0)
.

We claim that in this case, even a stronger inclusion holds:

N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)\leqslant P\text{.}

Indeed, let

g\in N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)
. Then
g^{-1}\in N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)
, and by Theorem
2
we have

g_{\lambda,\lambda_{0}}\in I^{-}=(0)\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}\}\text{.}

Next, consider the form
q
from Lemma
21
. Since it annihilates the vectors from the
orbit of the highest weight vector, the coefficients
q_{\lambda,\lambda}
(
=q(v^{\lambda})
) vanish; hence, using that
g_{*,\lambda_{0}}=g_{\lambda,\lambda_{0}}v^{\lambda_{0}}+g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}v^{-\lambda_{0}}
, we obtain

g_{\lambda,\lambda_{0}}g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}=\pm q(g_{*,\lambda_{0}})=0\text{.}

The pair
g_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}
,
g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}
is unimodular. Hence every prime ideal of the ring
R
contains exactly one of the elements
g_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}
and
g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}
. Since the spectrum of the ring
R
is
connected, one of those elements is invertible, and the other (since
g_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}=0
) is
equal to zero. By Theorem
2
we have the inclusion

g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}I^{+}\subseteq I^{-}=0\text{.}

Hence the element
g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}
is not invertible, i.e., it is equal to zero. Therefore,
g\in P
.

Case 2
.
I^{+}=(0)
.

First we assume that
g\in N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)
. Then
g^{-1}\in N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)
, and by Theorem
2
we have:

\displaystyle g_{\lambda,\lambda_{0}}\in I^{-}=(0)\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}\}\text{,}

\displaystyle g_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}\in I^{+}=(0)\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}\}\text{.}

The same is true for
g^{-1}
; hence for
\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}\}
, we have

g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda}=h(g_{*,\lambda},v^{\lambda_{0}})=h(gv^{\lambda},v^{\lambda_{0}})=h(v^{\lambda},g^{-1}v^{\lambda_{0}})=(g^{-1})_{-\lambda,\lambda_{0}}=0\text{,}

where

is a form occurring in Lemma
20
. Similarly, we deduce that

g_{\lambda,-\lambda_{0}}=0\text{.}

Therefore, the submodule generated by the vectors
v^{\lambda_{0}}
and
v^{-\lambda_{0}}
, is invariant with respect to the action of the group
N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)
, and the element

acts on it by the
following matrix:

\begin{pmatrix}g_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}&g_{\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}}\\
g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}&g_{-\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}}\end{pmatrix}\text{.}

Arguing as in the first case, we see that each column and each row of this matrix contain
an invertible entry and a zero entry. Therefore, this matrix can be of one of the following
shapes:

\begin{pmatrix}g_{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}&0\\
0&g_{-\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}}\end{pmatrix}\text{or}\begin{pmatrix}0&g_{\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}}\\
g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}&0\end{pmatrix}\text{.}

If

g\in\left[N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right),N_{G(\Phi,R)}\left(E(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)\right)\right]\text{,}

then the matrix has the first shape, i.e.
g_{-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}}=0
and

.
∎

8.
Root type elements

Let
\mathfrak{R}(-)
be the smallest closed subscheme (over
\mathbb{Z}
) in
G(\Phi,-)
, such that for any
R
and any

we have

x_{\alpha_{1}}(1)^{h}\in\mathfrak{R}(R)\text{.}

Obviously, one can replace
\alpha_{1}
with any other root because all elements

are conjugate by elements of the Weyl group.

The elements of
\mathfrak{R}(R)
are what we call the

Let
\mathbb{Z}[G]
be the ring of regular functions on the scheme
G(\Phi,-)
, and let
\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{R}]
be the ring of regular functions on the scheme
\mathfrak{R}(-)
. By definition, the second ring is a quotient of the first one. However, using the following lemma, we will also view the ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{R}]
as a subring of
\mathbb{Z}[G]
.

We denote by
g_{\gen}\in G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z}[G])
and
r_{\gen}\in\mathfrak{R}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{R}])
the generic elements of the corresponding schemes.

Lemma 22
.

There exists an injective map

i\colon Z[\mathfrak{R}]\to\mathbb{Z}[G]

such that

i(r_{\gen})=x_{\alpha_{1}}(1)^{g_{\gen}}\text{.}

Proof.

The collection of maps

\displaystyle G(\Phi,R)\to\mathfrak{R}(R)

\displaystyle g\mapsto x_{\alpha_{1}}(1)^{g}

gives a morphism of schemes
G(\Phi,-)\to\mathfrak{R}(-)
. Take
i
to be the corresponding homomorphism of rings of regular functions. The relation
i(r_{\gen})=x_{\alpha_{1}}(1)^{g_{\gen}}
is true in this
case by definition. It remains to prove that the homomorphism
i
is injective.

Let
I=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits i
, and let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}(-)
be the subscheme of
\mathfrak{R}(-)
determined by the ideal
I
. Then by the definition of
I
we have

x_{\alpha_{1}}(1)^{g_{\gen}}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathbb{Z}[G])\text{.}

Hence by the universal property of
g_{gen}
, we have

x_{\alpha_{1}}(1)^{h}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}(R)\text{.}

for any ring
R
and any
h\in G(\Phi,R)
. However,
\mathfrak{R}(-)
) is the
smallest subscheme with this property. Hence
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}(-)
coincides with
\mathfrak{R}(-)
, i.e.
I=0
.
∎

We provide several important examples of root type elements.

Lemma 23
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring; then the following holds

(a)

x_{\alpha}(t)\in\mathfrak{R}(R)
for any
\alpha\in\Phi
,
t\in R

(b)

(c)

\alpha

\beta\in\Phi

(a)

It suffices to consider the case where

). We
need to check that the element
x_{\alpha}(t)
satisfies certain equations. Since the ring
\mathbb{Z}[t]
is embeded into the ring

, we can replace one with another. In the

G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])
the element

is conjugate to the element

(b)

It suffices to consider the element

(r_{\gen})^{g_{\gen}}\in G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{R}]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[G])\text{.}

As before, it suffices to map our ring to another ring injectively with the image of the element

(and hence of the element

(c)

As before, it suffices to consider the ring

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\

0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\

Lemma 34

Let

stabilizes the line spanned by the covector

Further since

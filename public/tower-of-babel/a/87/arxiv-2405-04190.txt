On the Euler characteristic of the commutative graph complex and the top weight cohomology of ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g}
Michael Borinsky with an appendix by Don Zagier

Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic formula for the Euler characteristic of Kontsevich’s commutative graph complex. This formula implies that the total amount of commutative graph homology grows super-exponentially with the rank and, via a theorem of Chan, Galatius, and Payne, that the dimension of the top weight cohomology of the moduli space of curves, ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} , grows super-exponentially with the genus g g .



1.
Introduction

A
graph

G
is a one-dimensional CW complex. It has
rank

g
if its fundamental group is a free group of rank
g
,
\pi_{1}(G)\cong F_{g}
, and we call it
admissible
if it has no vertices of degree
0
,
1
, and
2
.
A graph is
even-orientable
if it has no automorphism inducing an odd permutation on its edges.

Kontsevich defined various chain complexes that are generated by families of graphs
[
Kon93
]
.
The arguably simplest of these
graph complexes
,
\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g}
, is freely generated over
{\mathbb{Q}}
by all isomorphism classes of connected, admissible, and even-orientable graphs of rank
g
. Each generator is oriented by fixing an ordering of the graph’s edges. The
k
-chains in the subspace
C_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})\subset\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g}
are rational linear combinations of such graphs with exactly
k
edges and the boundary maps,

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\;:\;C_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})\;\rightarrow\;C_{k-1}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})\,,\quad G\mapsto\sum\nolimits_{e}\pm\,G/e\,,

map a graph generator to the alternating sum of its edge contractions. The sign in front of
G/e
is the product of the parity of
e
in the ordering of
G
’s edges and the sign of the permutation that maps
G
’s edge ordering to
G/e
’s.

Even commutative graph homology
is the quotient

H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})\;=\;\ker\partial_{k}\,/\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}.

It has various applications, such as deformation quantization
[
Kon97
,
DRW15
]
, the group cohomology of automorphism groups of free groups
[
CV03
]
,
Drinfeld’s theory of quasi-Hopf algebras
[
Wil15
]
, and the cohomology of moduli spaces of curves
[
CGP21
]
(see §
1.1
).

The Euler characteristic of such a graph complex is the alternating
sum of its Betti numbers,

\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim C_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
. As the dimension of the chain space
C_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
is the number of isomorphism classes of connected, admissible, and even-orientable graphs of rank
g
with
k
edges, the computation of
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
is the combinatorial problem of counting such graphs and taking the alternating sum of their numbers over
k
.

For two sequences
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots
and
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots
the asymptotic notation ‘
a_{g}\sim b_{g}
as
g\rightarrow\infty
’ means that
\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}a_{g}/b_{g}=1.
The main result of this article is an asymptotic expression for
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
:

Theorem 1.1
.

The Euler characteristic,
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
, behaves as

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})\;\sim\;\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{g}{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{g^{3}}}\,\left(\frac{g}{2\pi e}\right)^{g}&\text{ if $g$ is even}\\
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{g}\,\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi g}{4}}-\frac{\pi g}{4}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right)\,e^{\sqrt{\frac{\pi g}{4}}}\,\left(\frac{g}{2\pi e}\right)^{\frac{g}{2}}&\text{ if $g$ is odd}\end{cases}\qquad\text{as }g\rightarrow\infty\,.

This asymptotic behavior of the Euler characteristic implies lower bounds for the total amount of even commutative graph homology,
H(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})=\bigoplus_{k}H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g}):

Theorem 1.2
.

For every positive
C<(2\pi e)^{-1}=0.0585...,

and all but finitely many
g\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\dim H(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})\;>\;\begin{cases}(Cg)^{g}&\text{ if $g$ is even}\\
(Cg)^{g/2}&\text{ if $g$ is odd}\end{cases}

In the even
g
case, the estimate above follows directly from Theorem
1.1
.
The odd
g
case is nontrivial and relies on a bound for the
irrationality measure
of
\pi
.
By Theorem
1.2
, the number of homology classes in
\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g}
grows
super-exponentially
with
g
. Because of the wildly alternating signs in Theorem
1.1
, many of these classes must have odd degrees.
Both results refute the expectation formulated in
[
Kon93
, §7.B]
that the
orbifold
and the classical Euler characteristic behave similarly for large
g
, as the former vanishes for all odd
g
(see also
[
BV24
, §3]
).

The homology
H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
vanishes outside the degree range
2g\leq k\leq 3g-3
. The upper bound is due to the lack of admissible graphs of rank
g
with more than
3g-3
edges.
The lower bound was proven by Willwacher, who also showed that
the bottom degree homology
H_{2g}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
is isomorphic to the degree
g
subspace of the
Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra
,
\mathfrak{grt}_{1}

[
Wil15
]
. It is a theorem of Brown that the free Lie algebra with one generator in each odd degree
\geq 3
injects into
\mathfrak{grt}_{1}

[
Bro12
]
.
Counting the number of generators of this free Lie algebra interpreted as a graded vector space shows that at least an exponentially growing amount of classes is concentrated in the bottom degree
2g
of
H(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
.

Khoroshkin, Willwacher, and Živković proved that even graph homology, summed over all ranks and degrees larger than the bottom degree
2g
, i.e.,
\bigoplus_{g\geq 2,k>2g}H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
, is infinite-dimensional
[
KWZ17
, Cor. 1]
. Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
strengthen this result. For instance, they directly imply that the dimension of
\bigoplus_{k}H^{2k+1}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{4g+2})
grows super-exponentially with
g
.
Also, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
answer a related question posed after
[
KWZ17
, Cor. 6]
in the negative.
Moreover, Theorem
1.2
(negatively) answers Brown’s question of whether a specific map into
H(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
is an isomorphism
[
Bro21
, Conj. 2.5 and §2.11]
, as the domain of this map is not large enough to match the super-exponentially growing dimension of the image.
Further, Theorem
1.2
implies the same bound on the dimension of the top weight cohomology of the
handlebody group

[
HP23
]
.

1.1.
Implications on the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g}

Due to a recent result by Chan, Galatius, and Payne
[
CGP21
]
, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
provide new information on the cohomology of the moduli space of curves
\mathcal{M}_{g}.

According to Deligne’s theory of mixed Hodge structures, the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
admits a
weight grading
in addition to its grading by degree. The
j
-th weight-graded piece is the subspace
\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{j}H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})\subset H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}}).

Chan, Galatius, and Payne’s result establishes an isomorphism between the
top weight
(i.e., the
(6g-6)
-th weight-graded piece of the) cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
and the homology of
\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g}.

Translated to our degree conventions, they proved

\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{6g-6}H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H_{6g-6-k}(\mathcal{GC}^{g}_{+})

[
CGP21
, Thms. 1.2 and 1.3]
.
This result shed new light on an older problem: In 1986, Harer and Zagier
[
HZ86
]
proved a formula for the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
,
\chi(\mathcal{M}_{g})=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}}),
which implies
an inequality of the shape

\dim H(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})>([\text{positive constant}]\cdot g)^{2g}

for all
g\geq 2
.
It is an open problem to explain the nature of this super-exponentially growing amount of classes.
The only previously known family, the
tautological classes
,
grows less than exponentially with
g
.
Chan, Galatius, and Payne combined their insight on the top weight cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
with Brown and Willwacher’s theorems on
\mathcal{GC}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{grt}_{1}
to prove the existence of another
exponentially growing
family of classes in the next-to-top degree
H^{4g-6}(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})

[
CGP21
, Thms. 1.1 and 2.7]
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

add a positive and a negative twist to this story:
Together with Chan, Galatius, and Payne’s result, Theorem
1.2
implies that the dimension of the top weight cohomology
\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{6g-6}H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})
grows super-exponentially with
g
:

Theorem 1.3
.

The
top weight Euler characteristic

\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{6g-6}H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})
exhibits the same large
g
asymptotic behavior as
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
in Theorem
1.1
and
\dim\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{6g-6}H(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})
respects the same lower bounds as
\dim H(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
in Theorem
1.2
.

So, potentially, Chan, Galatius, and Payne’s result can explain many more classes than the exponentially growing family in degree
4g-6
.
However, the growth rate of the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g}
, which is dominated by a
g^{g}
or
g^{\frac{g}{2}}
term, is insufficient to explain the even larger growth rate of the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, which is dominated by a
g^{2g}
term.
Further, as the Euler characteristic is additive over direct sums,
Theorem
1.1
, in combination with the Chan–Galatius–Payne and Harer–Zagier results, implies that the total dimension of the rational, non-top weight cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
,
\bigoplus_{j=0}^{6g-8}\mathrm{Gr}_{j}^{W}H(\mathcal{M}_{g};{\mathbb{Q}})
, also grows super-exponentially with
g
.

1.2.
The odd commutative graph complex

Kontsevich defined his graph complexes in two flavors:
The
odd
and the
even
.
1
1

1

In the original article
[
Kon93
]
, the even/odd nomenclature was used in the opposite way. We agree with most recent publications that use the present convention.

An
odd orientation
of a graph
G
is given by an ordering of its edges together with
bases for its zeroth and first homology
H_{0}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H_{1}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
A given automorphism
\alpha
of a graph induces a permutation
\alpha_{E}
on
G
’s set of edges
and automorphisms
\alpha_{H_{0}}:H_{0}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow H_{0}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
\alpha_{H_{1}}:H_{1}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow H_{1}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
A graph is
odd-orientable
if it has no automorphism
\alpha

for which
{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})\cdot\det(\alpha_{H_{0}})\cdot\det(\alpha_{H_{1}})=-1
.
The
odd commutative graph complex
,
\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g}
, is defined analogously to the even graph complex described above.
Here, generators are graded by the number of vertices instead of the edge number.
So, connected, odd-oriented graphs of genus
g
with
k
vertices span the space of
k
-chains
C_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})
.
The boundary maps,
\partial_{k}:C_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})\rightarrow C_{k-1}(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})
are again defined on generators by
G\mapsto\sum\nolimits_{e}\pm G/e
, where the sign in front of
G/e
is the parity of
e
in the edge ordering times the sign of the permutation that translates from
G
’s edge ordering to
G/e
’s and the determinants of the canonical isomorphisms that map the bases of
H_{0}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H_{1}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})

to the chosen bases of
H_{0}(G/e,{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H_{1}(G/e,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Odd commutative graph homology
H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})
has support in the degree range
1\leq k\leq 2g-2
,
as admissible graphs at rank
g
have at most
2g-2
vertices.

We will prove that the Euler characteristic of the odd commutative complex,
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})
, behaves similarly to the even one up to a sign:

Theorem 1.4
.

\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})\sim-\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
as
g\rightarrow\infty
.
Moreover,
\dim H(\mathcal{GC}^{g}_{-})
respects the same lower bounds as
\dim H(\mathcal{GC}^{g}_{+})
in Theorem
1.2
.

The odd commutative graph complex was studied extensively due to its role in knot theory and finite type invariants (see, e.g.,
[
BN95
]
). Many classes are known in the top-degree
2g-2
due to a relation to Chern–Simons theory. It is conjectured that odd commutative graph cohomology in this degree forms a specific commutative algebra
[
Vog11
]
.

Aside from showing that the total amount of odd graph homology grows rapidly with
g
, Theorem
1.4
explains an observation in
[
WZ15
]
on the similarity of the Euler characteristics of
\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g}
and
\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g}
: Asymptotically, they are equal up to a sign.

In many articles,
\mathcal{GC}_{+}
is denoted as
\mathcal{GC}_{0}
, or it appears as the, up to degree-shifts equivalent,
\mathcal{GC}_{2}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{GC}_{-}
corresponds to
\mathcal{GC}_{1}
in the conventional notation. Here, we use the
\pm
-notation to avoid repetition, as our statements on
\mathcal{GC}_{+}
and
\mathcal{GC}_{-}
often only differ by signs.

1.3.
Generating functions for commutative graph complex Euler characteristics

We will prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
after establishing
generating functions
for

\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
and
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})
.

Let
\mu
denote the
Möbius function
, defined recursively by

\mu(1)=1
and
0=\sum_{d|m}\mu(d)
for all integers
m\geq 2
.
For any integer
k\geq 1
, we write
M_{k}(z)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{d|k}\mu(d)z^{k/d}
,
L_{k}(z)=\log(kM_{k}(z)/z^{k})

and
\delta_{2|k}=(1+(-1)^{k})/2
.
The
Bernoulli numbers
,
B_{m}
, are defined by

{x}/({e^{x}-1})=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{B_{m}}\frac{x^{m}}{m!}
.

Each member of the following family of power series is meant to be expanded in terms of
1/z
,

(1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}^{\pm}(z)\;=\;\pm\left((1-L_{k}(z))\,M_{k}(z)\;-\;\frac{z^{k}}{k}\;+\;\frac{\delta_{2|k}}{2k}\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,L_{k}(z)\;\mp\;\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{m+1}}{m(m+1)}\,\frac{1}{M_{k}(z)^{m}}\,.

We write
\lceil q\rceil
for the smallest integer
\geq q
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For all
k\geq 1
, we have
\Psi_{k}^{\pm}(z)\in z^{-\lceil k/6\rceil}{\mathbb{Q}}[[\frac{1}{z}]]
and the identity in
{\mathbb{Q}}[[\frac{1}{z}]]
holds,

\sum_{g\geq 2}\,\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g})\,z^{1-g}\;=\;\sum_{k,\ell\geq 1}\,\frac{\mu(\ell)}{\ell}\,\Psi^{\pm}_{k}(z^{\ell})\,.

Generating functions for the Euler characteristic of
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g})
were previously obtained by Willwacher and Živković
[
WZ15
]
. Their formulas do not seem amenable to an asymptotic analysis. Tsopméné and Turchin extended this formula to hold for graph complexes where the graphs are allowed to have
leaves (also called legs or hairs)

[
STT18
]
.
Chan, Faber, Galatius, and Payne
[
CFGP23
]
proved a formula for the
\SG_{n}
-equivariant top weight Euler characteristic of the moduli space of curves with
n
marked points,
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
.
This formula, which also does not seem amenable to an asymptotic analysis, was conjectured in 2008 in an unpublished letter by Zagier based on computations by Faber. This letter, which also explains the relationship between Theorem
1.5
and the formula in
[
CFGP23
]
, is adapted to the present context in Appendix
A
.

1.4.
Kontsevich’s other graph complexes

In addition to the commutative graph complex,
Kontsevich defined the
associative

and the
Lie
graph complex
[
Kon93
]
.
The former is generated by oriented

ribbon
graphs, i.e., graphs with a cyclic ordering attached
to each vertex.
Getzler and Kapranov obtained a generating function for the Euler characteristic of this graph complex
[
GK98
, Theorem 9.18]
that is remarkably similar to the formula in Theorem
1.5
.
Let
\chi(\mathcal{AGC}^{g})
denote the Euler characteristic of the associative graph complex.
Applying the methods developed in the present article to Getzler and Kapranov’s result (see §
9.1
) gives

Theorem 1.6
.

\chi(\mathcal{AGC}^{g})\sim-\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
as
g\rightarrow\infty
and associative graph homology
\dim H(\mathcal{AGC}^{g})
respects the same lower bounds as
\dim H(\mathcal{GC}^{g}_{+})
in Theorem
1.2
.

The Lie graph complex computes the rational group cohomology of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
, the outer automorphism group of the free group, and the cohomology of the moduli space of graphs. Vogtmann and the author proved that the associated Euler characteristic behaves like
\chi({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g}))\sim-e^{-1/4}(g/e)^{g}/(g\log g)^{2}
as
g\rightarrow\infty

[
BV23
]
,
which is strikingly different from the oscillatory behavior exhibited by the other graph complexes.
The results of the present paper hence complete the analysis of the Euler characteristics of Kontsevich’s original graph complexes.

2.
The oscillating asymptotic behavior of
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g})
and the irrationality of
\pi

The magnitude of the Euler characteristic,
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
, gives a lower bound for the total dimension of the homology
\sum_{k}\dim H_{k}(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})
(and for
\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g}
and
\mathcal{AGC}^{g}
analogously).
In the even
g
case, Theorem
1.1
implies
the even
g
case of Theorem
1.2
:
It follows from Theorem
1.1
that for any positive constant
C<\sqrt{8\pi}

the inequality

|\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g})|>Cg^{-3/2}(g/(2\pi e))^{g}

holds for all but finitely many even
g\geq 2
.
Theorem
1.2
follows as for all positive constants
C^{\prime}<1
,
the inequality
Cg^{-3/2}>(C^{\prime})^{g}
holds for all but finitely many
g\geq 2
.

The odd
g
case is more complicated, as the cosine in the statement of Theorem
1.1
can become arbitrarily small.
However, known bounds on the measure of the irrationality of
\pi

imply that the cosine cannot become so small that it cancels the super-exponential growth rate from the
g^{g/2}
term.
The following paragraphs will explain this mechanism.

According to a theorem of Mahler
[
Mah53
]
, there is a number
\mu
, the
irrationality measure of
\pi
, such that the inequality

\left|\pi-{p}/{q}\right|>{q^{-\mu^{*}}}
holds for all numbers
\mu^{*}>\mu
and all but finitely many pairs of integers
p,q
.
In fact, we may assume that
\mu\leq 7.103...

[
ZZ20
]
.
We can use this property of
\pi
to prove the following lower bound on the cosine in the statement of Theorem
1.1
:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mu^{*}
be strictly larger than the irrationality measure
\mu
of
\pi
, then

\left|\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi g}{4}}\;-\;\frac{\pi g}{4}\;-\;\frac{\pi}{8}\right)\right|\;>\;g^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu^{*}}\text{ for all but finitely many integers $g\geq 2$}\,.

Proof.

For any given
g
, let
k_{g}
be the unique integer
that minimizes the magnitude of
\mathcal{R}_{g}
in

\sqrt{\frac{\pi g}{4}}\;=\;\frac{\pi k_{g}}{8}\;+\;\mathcal{R}_{g}\,.

It follows that
|\mathcal{R}_{g}|<\frac{\pi}{16}
.
If
m=k_{g}-2g-1
is not an integer of the form
8n+4
for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
then
|\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi g}{4}}-\frac{\pi g}{4}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right)|=|\cos(\frac{\pi m}{8}+\mathcal{R}_{g})|=|\sin(\pi\frac{m-4}{8}+\mathcal{R}_{g})|\geq\sin(\frac{\pi}{16})
.
So, the statement is only nontrivial if
m=8n+4
for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, which we will assume.
Using this with the inequality
|\sin(x)|\geq|x|(1-\frac{|x|}{\pi})
,
valid for all
|x|\leq\pi
, gives

|\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi g}{4}}-\frac{\pi g}{4}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right)|=|\sin\left(\mathcal{R}_{g}\right)|\geq|\mathcal{R}_{g}|(1-\frac{|\mathcal{R}_{g}|}{\pi})\geq\frac{15}{16}|\mathcal{R}_{g}|
.
It remains to prove a lower bound on
|\mathcal{R}_{g}|
.
The bounds on the irrationality measure of
\pi

discussed above imply

|\pi-16{g}/{k_{g}^{2}}|>{k_{g}^{-2\mu}}

for all but finitely many
g
.
By definition of
\mathcal{R}_{g}
,

|\pi-16{g}/{k_{g}^{2}}|=|16{\mathcal{R}_{g}}/k_{g}+8^{2}{\mathcal{R}_{g}^{2}}/({\pi k_{g}^{2}})|\leq 20{|\mathcal{R}_{g}|}/{k_{g}}.

Hence,

|\mathcal{R}_{g}|>\frac{1}{20}{k_{g}^{1-2\mu}}
for almost all
g
.
Using

k_{g}>\frac{8}{\pi}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi g}{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)

and

\mu^{*}>\mu

gives the statement.
∎

3.
Connected and disconnected graphs

All graphs in this article are admissible. So, we will drop this adjective from now on.
Let
\mathbf{G}_{+}
(
\mathbf{G}_{-}
) be the set of isomorphism classes of even(-odd)-orientable graphs
and
\mathbf{cG}_{+}
(
\mathbf{cG}_{-}
) be the subsets of such graphs that are connected.
As explained in the introduction, the elements of
\mathbf{cG}_{\pm}
generate the complexes
\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g}
, whose Euler characteristics are signed sums over these elements:

Proposition 3.1
.

With
|E_{G}|
and
|V_{G}|
the number of edges and vertices of
G
,

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g})

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathbf{cG}_{+}\\
\rank(G)=g\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|E_{G}|}

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g})

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathbf{cG}_{-}\\
\rank(G)=g\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|V_{G}|}\,,

where we sum over all connected graphs with fixed rank and the respective orientability.

Only connected graphs generate the graph complexes
\mathcal{GC}_{+}^{g}
and
\mathcal{GC}_{-}^{g}
, but it is easier to evaluate expressions like the two above if we sum over all, possibly disconnected, graphs. Further, it is convenient to index such graphs by their Euler characteristic and define numbers for all
n\geq 0
,

(2)

\displaystyle\chi^{+}_{n}

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathbf{G}_{+}\\
\chi(G)=-n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|E_{G}|}

\displaystyle\chi^{-}_{n}

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathbf{G}_{-}\\
\chi(G)=-n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|V_{G}|}\,,

where we sum over all graphs with fixed Euler characteristic
\chi(G)=|E_{G}|-|V_{G}|
.
Admissibility of a graph implies that
2|E_{G}|\geq 3|V_{G}|
.
Hence,
\chi(G)<0
if
G
is non-empty, and there are only finitely many graphs
G
with specific
\chi(G)=-n
. So, the sequences above are well-defined, and
\chi^{\pm}_{0}=1
due to the empty graph for which
\chi(G)=0
.

The following statement translates between the connected and disconnected case.

Proposition 3.2
.

In the ring of power series
{\mathbb{Q}}[[\frac{1}{z}]]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\;\chi^{\pm}_{n}\;z^{-n}\;=\;\prod_{g\geq 2}\;\left(\frac{1}{1-z^{1-g}}\right)^{\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g})}\,.

The proof is a variant of the one for Theorem 2.5 in
[
BV23
]
:

Proof.

Each disconnected even/odd-orientable graph can be written as a unique disjoint union of connected even/odd-orientable graphs. However, not every such disjoint union is necessarily an even/odd-orientable graph.
If a disconnected graph contains two copies of the same connected graph, it has an automorphism that interchanges these components. This automorphism may flip the graph’s orientation, making it non-orientable.
For even orientability, this happens if a disconnected graph,
G^{\prime}
, contains two copies of the same graph,
G
, with an odd number of edges. The automorphism that switches the two components induces an odd permutation on the edge set of the whole graph, which is thereby not even-orientable.
For the odd-orientability, the same situation arises if a disconnected graph,
G^{\prime}
, contains two copies of a graph
G
with the sum
|E_{G}|+\dim H_{0}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})+\dim H_{1}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})
being an odd number, as then, with
\alpha
the automorphism of
G^{\prime}
that switches the two copies of
G
, we have
{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})\det(\alpha_{H_{0}})\det(\alpha_{H_{1}})=-1
.

Let
\deg^{+}(G)=|E_{G}|
and
\deg^{-}(G)=|V_{G}|
and note that

\deg^{-}(G)=|E_{G}|+\chi(G)=|E_{G}|+\dim H_{0}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})-\dim H_{1}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})
has the same parity as
|E_{G}|+\dim H_{0}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})+\dim H_{1}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
It follows from the last paragraph’s discussion that
each member of
\mathbf{G}_{\pm}
is a sequence of connected graphs in which graphs
G
with even
\deg^{\pm}(G)
may appear any number of times and
graphs with odd
\deg^{\pm}(G)
may appear at most once. So, the following product expands to a sum with one term for each
G^{\prime}\in\mathbf{G}_{\pm}
weighted by
(-1)^{\deg^{\pm}(G^{\prime})}z^{\chi(G^{\prime})}
:

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathbf{cG}_{\pm}\\
\deg^{\pm}(G)\text{ is even}\end{subarray}}\left(1\;+\;z^{\chi(G)}\;+\;z^{2\chi(G)}\;+\;\cdots\right)\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathbf{cG}_{\pm}\\
\deg^{\pm}(G)\text{ is odd}\end{subarray}}\left(1\;-\;z^{\chi(G)}\right)\,.

Also, use Proposition
3.1
,
\chi(G)=1-\rank(G)
if
G
is connected, and
\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}=\frac{1}{1-x}
.
∎

4.
Half-edge labeled graphs

A
set partition
of a finite set
S
is a set
P=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{r}\}
of non-empty and mutually disjoint subsets of
S
such that
B_{1}\cup\ldots\cup B_{r}=S
. The elements
B_{i}\in P
are called
blocks
. A bijection
\alpha:S\rightarrow S
fixes the partition if there is an
induced permutation of the blocks

\alpha_{P}\in\SG_{r}
such that
\alpha(B_{i})=B_{\alpha_{P}(i)}
for all
i
.
For a given
\alpha
that fixes
P
, the induced permutation
\alpha_{P}
on the blocks is unique.
We use the convention that the empty set has one (empty) set partition, which is fixed by the unique bijection between empty sets.

For given
s\geq 0
and
\alpha\in\SG_{2s}
, let
\mathcal{E}(\alpha)
be the set of set partitions of
[2s]
that are fixed by
\alpha
and have
s
blocks of size
2
.
Given
s,r\geq 0
and
\alpha\in\SG_{2s}
, let
\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)
be the set of set partitions of
[2s]
that are fixed by
\alpha
and have exactly
r
blocks of size
\geq 3
.
Further, for given
E\in\mathcal{E}(\alpha)
and
V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)
,
we write
\alpha_{E}
and
\alpha_{V}
for the permutations that
\alpha
induces on the blocks of
E
and
V
.

This terminology allows us to write the number of
isomorphism classes of even/odd-orientable graphs with a fixed number of edges and vertices as signed sums over set partitions.

Theorem 4.1
.

For all
s,r\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\{G\in\mathbf{G}_{+}:|E_{G}|=s,|V_{G}|=r\}\right|

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{1}{(2s)!}\,\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\sum_{(E,V)\in\mathcal{E}(\alpha)\times\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})\,,\text{ and }

\displaystyle\left|\{G\in\mathbf{G}_{-}:|E_{G}|=s,|V_{G}|=r\}\right|

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{1}{(2s)!}\,\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\sum_{(E,V)\in\mathcal{E}(\alpha)\times\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha)\cdot{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{V})\,.

The proof of this theorem will occupy the remainder of this section.
The first step is to interpret the Cartesian product
\mathcal{E}(\alpha)\times\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)
in the sums above as the set of
half-edge labeled graphs
with
r
vertices that have
\alpha
as an automorphism.
We do so in the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a set
S
, an
S
-
labeled graph
is a pair
\Gamma=(E,V)
of two set partitions
E,V
of
S
such that all blocks of
E
have size
2
and all blocks of
V
have size
\geq 3
.
The blocks of
E
and
V
are the
edges

and
vertices
of
\Gamma
, respectively.
An automorphism of an
S
-labeled graph is a bijection
S\rightarrow S
, which fixes the partitions
E
and
V
.
These bijections form a group
{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)
.

It is easy to see that giving a graph with
s
edges together with a labeling of its edges’ end-points (or half-edges) with integers from the set
[2s]=\{1,\ldots,2s\}
is equivalent to giving a
[2s]
-labeled graph. The following lemma quantifies the number of
[2s]
-labeled graphs we can construct for a given isomorphism class of graphs.

Lemma 4.3
.

Each isomorphism class of graphs with
s
edges can be represented by exactly
(2s)!/|{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)|

[2s]
-labeled graphs where
\Gamma
can be any such latter graph.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{lab}(G)
be the set of
[2s]
-labeled representatives of the isomorphism class
G
. The symmetric group
\SG_{2s}
acts transitively on
\mathrm{lab}(G)
by permuting the labels.
By Definition
4.2
, the stabilizer of an element
\Gamma\in\mathrm{lab}(G)
is the group
{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)
.
Hence,
|\SG_{2m}|=|\mathrm{lab}(G)||{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)|
.
∎

The sign factors in the statement of Theorem
4.1
make the non-orientable graphs cancel out. In the even-orientability case, this mechanism is straightforward:

Lemma 4.4
.

For an
[2s]
-labeled graph
\Gamma
, the sum

\sum_{\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})

evaluates to
|{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)|
if
\Gamma

is even-orientable and to
0
otherwise.

Proof.

The graph
\Gamma
is even-orientable if it has no automorphism
\alpha
for which
{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})=-1
. In that case,
\sum_{\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})=|{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)|
.
The map
\xi:{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)\rightarrow{\operatorname{GL}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}})
given by
\xi:\alpha\mapsto{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})
is a group homomorphism. If
\Gamma
is not even-orientable, then
\xi
is surjective and

\sum_{\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)}{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})=\sum_{\alpha\in\xi^{-1}(1)}1-\sum_{\alpha\in\xi^{-1}(-1)}1=|\ker(\xi)|-|\ker(\xi)|=0.

∎

For the odd-orientation we need an additional lemma,
which for given
[2s]
-labeled graph
\Gamma

and automorphism
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)\subset\SG_{2s}

relates the signs of the induced permutations
\alpha_{E}
and

\alpha_{V}
on the edge and vertex set of
\Gamma

with the determinants of the automorphisms
that
\alpha
induces on
\Gamma
’s homology,
\alpha_{H_{k}}:H_{k}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow H_{k}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{Z}})
with

Lemma 4.5
.

For a pair
(\Gamma,\alpha)
with the induced permutations/automorphisms as above, we have

{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha)\cdot{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{V})\;=\;{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})\cdot\det(\alpha_{H_{0}})\cdot\det(\alpha_{H_{1}})\,.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{G}
be the barycentric subdivision of
\Gamma
.
That means
\widetilde{G}
is the graph, homeomorphic to
\Gamma
, that we construct from
\Gamma
by adding a new two-valent vertex in the middle of each edge of
\Gamma

\widetilde{E}
of
\widetilde{G}
correspond to the half-edges of
\Gamma
and
\widetilde{G}

\widetilde{V}

\Gamma
’s edges and vertices. The graph
\widetilde{G}
is a simplicial complex with a canonical orientation on its edges: we can point each edge towards the adjacent two-valent vertex. Hence, we have a canonical boundary map,
\partial:{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}

{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}
. The vector spaces
{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}
come with canonical non-degenerate bilinear forms given, e.g., by
\langle e,e\rangle=1
and
\langle e,e^{\prime}\rangle=0
for generators
e\neq e^{\prime}\in\widetilde{E}
.
So, we can decompose the chain spaces using the orthogonal complement, giving canonical isomorphisms
{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}\rightarrow\ker\partial\oplus(\ker\partial)^{\perp}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}\rightarrow(\operatorname{im}\partial)\oplus(\operatorname{im}\partial)^{\perp}
.
We get a chain of canonical isomorphisms,

H_{1}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}\rightarrow H_{1}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus(\operatorname{im}\partial)\oplus(\operatorname{im}\partial)^{\perp}\rightarrow\ker\partial\oplus(\ker\partial)^{\perp}\oplus H_{0}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}\oplus H_{0}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}}),

where we also used the identification of
\ker\partial
with
H_{1}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})
and the natural isomorphism
(\operatorname{im}\partial)^{\perp}\rightarrow H_{0}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}}).

Every
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(\Gamma)
induces an automorphism of
\widetilde{G}
and thereby automorphisms
on any of the above vector spaces, which we denote with the respective space in the subscript, e.g.,
\alpha_{{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}}:{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}
.
Due to the chain of isomorphisms above, we have

\det(\alpha_{H_{1}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}})=\det(\alpha_{{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}\oplus H_{0}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})}).

The determinants factor over direct sums, so

\det(\alpha_{H_{1}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})})\cdot\det(\alpha_{{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}})\;=\;\det(\alpha_{{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}})\cdot\det(\alpha_{H_{0}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})})\,.

Each automorphism fixes
\widetilde{G}
’s edges’ orientation, so
\alpha_{{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}}

acts by permuting the basis vectors of
{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}
corresponding to half-edges of
\Gamma
. Hence,
\det(\alpha_{{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{E}})={\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha)
. Also,

\alpha_{{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}}
permutes the vertices of
\widetilde{G}
corresponding to edges and vertices of
\Gamma
, so
\det(\alpha_{{\mathbb{Q}}\widetilde{V}})={\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{E})\cdot{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{V})
.
Further, the automorphisms
\alpha_{H_{0}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})}
and
\alpha_{H_{1}(\widetilde{G};{\mathbb{Q}})}
can be identified with
automorphisms of
H_{0}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H_{1}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{Z}})
, respectively. The statement follows from
{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{V})^{2}=1
.
∎

For an

Proof.

After using Lemma

\displaystyle V(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\;=\;\exp\Biggl(\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{x_{k}}{k}\Biggr)\,-\,1\,-\,x_{1}\,-\,\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{2}\,-\,\frac{x_{2}}{2}\;=\;\frac{x_{1}^{3}}{6}\,+\,\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{2}\,+\,\frac{x_{3}}{3}\,+\,\frac{x_{1}^{4}}{24}\,+\ldots

.

We have the following identities of power series in

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{x^{\alpha}}{(2s)!}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}\eta^{c(\alpha_{E})}

To prove this proposition, we first need the following lemma on the number of permutations that fix a given set partition while acting transitively on its set of blocks.
It is a variant of a classic statement going back to Pólya.
It can also be interpreted as a statement on relations of
character polynomials of symmetric group representations

[

Lemma 5.2
.

For a set partition

be the set of permutations of

For every pair

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

We will construct a bijection between

As

(V,\alpha)

\alpha\in\SG_{2s}
and a set partition
V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)
for some
r\geq 0
.
For any
(V,\alpha)\in VA_{s}
, we write
r(V)
for the number of blocks of
V
and
c(\alpha_{V})
for the number of cycles of the induced permutation
\alpha_{V}
. By the argument from above, we get

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{(V,\alpha)\in VA_{s}}\lambda^{r(V)}\,x^{\alpha}\,\phi^{c(\alpha_{V})}\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 0}\phi^{k}\sum_{\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\}\in\Pi^{k}([2s])}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\geq 3\end{subarray}}\sum_{\widetilde{V}\subset\Pi_{d}(B_{i})}\lambda^{r(\widetilde{V})}\sum_{\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Cyc}(\widetilde{V})}x^{\widetilde{\alpha}}\Biggr).

Corollary
5.3
implies that

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{i}|!}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\geq 3\end{subarray}}\sum_{\widetilde{V}\subset\Pi_{d}(B_{i})}\lambda^{r(\widetilde{V})}\sum_{\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Cyc}(\widetilde{V})}x^{\widetilde{\alpha}}\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\geq 3,r\geq 1\\
\text{s.t. }d\cdot r=|B_{i}|\end{subarray}}\lambda^{r}\,\frac{1}{r\cdot(d!)}\sum_{\beta\in\SG_{d}}x^{[r\cdot\beta]}\,.

For
d\geq 3
and integers

such that
c_{1}+2c_{2}+3c_{3}+\ldots=d
,
there are precisely

permutations
with exactly
c_{1}
1-cycles,
c_{2}
2-cycles, and so on. Hence,

\sum_{d\geq 3}\,\frac{1}{d!}\,\sum_{\beta\in\SG_{d}}\,x^{[r\cdot\beta]}\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1},c_{2},\ldots\geq 0\\
\text{s.t. }\sum_{k}kc_{k}\geq 3\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{x_{r\cdot k}^{c_{k}}}{k^{c_{k}}c_{k}!}\;=\;\exp\Biggl(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{x_{\ell\cdot r}}{\ell}\Biggr)\;-\;1\;-\;x_{r}\;-\;\frac{x_{r}^{2}}{2}\;-\;\frac{x_{2r}}{2}\,.

Using this together with Eqs. (

), (
5
),
and Lemma

gives the statement.
∎

We have now finished the purely combinatorial part of the argument
that will lead to the proof of Theorem

. The following three sections will relate the mainly discrete objects discussed above with analytic ones. Thanks to the additional flexibility provided by the analytic tools, we can prove the relatively simple formula in Theorem

.

6.
Partition functions for graph complex Euler characteristics

In this section, we define families of integrals whose asymptotic expansions in a specific limit encode the numbers
\chi_{n}^{\pm}
from Eq. (
2
).
For fixed
n\geq 1
, we define the following functions in
z,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. By analogy to quantum mechanical systems, we call them
action
functions:

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{S}^{+}_{n}(z,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})&\;=\;\phantom{-}\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k}}{k}\Biggl(\exp\Biggl(i\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{x_{k\ell}}{\ell}\Biggr)\;-\;1\;-\;ix_{k}\Biggr)\\
\mathcal{S}^{-}_{n}(z,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})&\;=\;-\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k}}{k}\Biggl(\exp\Biggl(\phantom{i}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{x_{k\ell}}{\ell}\Biggr)\;-\;1\;-\;\phantom{i}x_{k}\Biggr),\end{aligned}

where
\lfloor q\rfloor
is the largest integer
\leq q
.
Each such action function gives a
partition function
via the following integral expression that depends on
z\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and a bounded subset
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
:

(7)

\displaystyle I^{\pm}_{n}(z,D)\;=\;\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\exp\left(\pm\frac{\delta_{2|k}}{2k}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\right)\,\int_{D}\,\exp\left(\mathcal{S}^{\pm}_{n}(z,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}\,.

These integrals exist because
D
is bounded, and the integrand is an entire function in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.

We will use the big-
\mathcal{O}
notation to formulate asymptotic statements.
Let
h:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
be a positive function defined on positive reals.
The notation
`\mathcal{O}(h(z))
as
z\rightarrow\infty
’ denotes the set of all functions
f(z)
, for which
\limsup_{z\rightarrow\infty}|f(z)|/h(z)<\infty
.
The notation ‘
f(z)=g(z)+\mathcal{O}(h(z))\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty
’ means that
f(z)-g(z)\in\mathcal{O}(h(z))
as
z\rightarrow\infty
.

Let
B_{n}(\varepsilon)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|/(k\varepsilon^{k})\leq 1\}
and recall the definition of the sequences
\chi^{\pm}_{n}
in Eq. (
2
).
The main result of this section is that for a specific set of integration domains, the large
z
asymptotic expansion of the integrals
I^{\pm}_{n}(z,D)

encodes the integers
\chi^{\pm}_{n}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
, and any
z
-dependent family,
D(z)
, of subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which fulfills

B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/8)\subset D(z)\subset B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/2),
the integral
I^{\pm}_{n}(z,D(z))
behaves asymptotically as

\displaystyle I^{\pm}_{n}(z,D(z))\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/12\rfloor}\chi^{\pm}_{k}~z^{-k}\;+\;\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{n+1}{12}})\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty\,.

The proof of this theorem will occupy the remainder of this section. We will ensure that, for large
z
, the integrals are well-approximated by specific Gaussian integrals. After establishing bounds on this approximation’s accuracy, we can compute the
z\rightarrow\infty
asymptotic expansion via slight perturbations of the original Gaussian integral. In the finite-dimensional case, this strategy is called
Laplace method
. Here, a significant technical challenge is the dimension of the integrals, which increases with
n
.

For any
z\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
, we define the following complex-valued Gaussian measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

(8)

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{\pm}(z)\;=\;\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{z^{k}}{2k}\left(x_{k}-\rho^{\pm}\cdot\delta_{2|k}\cdot z^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right)^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}\,,

where
\rho^{+}=i
and
\rho^{-}=-1
and we recall that
\delta_{2|k}=(1+(-1)^{k})/2
.

The following proposition can be interpreted as an equivariant version of
Wick
’s theorem, a standard statement in quantum field theory.
Getzler and Kapranov obtained similar statements in a formal power series context (see,
[
GK98
, §8.16]
).

Proposition 6.2
.

Fix
z>0
and integers
n,s
such that
n\geq 1
and
s\geq 0
.
If
n\geq 2s+1
and

\alpha\in\SG_{2s+1}
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(ix)^{\alpha}\mu_{n}^{+}(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}x^{\alpha}\mu_{n}^{-}(z)=0.

If
n\geq 2s
and

\alpha\in\SG_{2s}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(ix)^{\alpha}\,\mu_{n}^{+}(z)

\displaystyle\;=\;z^{-s}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}(-1)^{c(\alpha_{E})}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}x^{\alpha}\,\mu_{n}^{-}(z)

\displaystyle\;=\;z^{-s}\,{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha)\,|\mathcal{E}(\alpha)|\,,

where
(ix)^{\alpha}
is the monomial
x^{\alpha}
with each instance of
x_{k}
replaced by
ix_{k}
.

Proof.

We will first prove that for any
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we have

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f^{+}&\;:=\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left(i\sum_{k=1}^{n}z^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{x_{k}y_{k}}{k}\right)\,\mu_{n}^{+}(z)\;=\;\exp\left(-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{y_{k}^{2}+y_{2k}}{2k}\right)\\
f^{-}&\;:=\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left(\phantom{i}\sum_{k=1}^{n}z^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{x_{k}y_{k}}{k}\right)\,\mu_{n}^{-}(z)\;=\;\exp\left(\phantom{-}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{y_{k}^{2}-y_{2k}}{2k}\right)\,,\end{aligned}

where we agree that
y_{k}=0
whenever
k>n
.
From the definition of
\mu^{\pm}_{n}
in Eq. (
8
), we get

\displaystyle f^{\pm}\;=\;\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left(A_{k}^{\pm}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\mathrm{d}x_{k}\right)\,,\quad\text{where }

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} A_{k}^{+}&=\;-\frac{z^{k}}{2k}\left(x_{k}-i\delta_{2|k}\;z^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right)^{2}+iz^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{x_{k}y_{k}}{k}\;=\;-\frac{z^{k}}{2k}\left(x_{k}-i\delta_{2|k}\;z^{-\frac{k}{2}}-iy_{k}z^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{y_{k}^{2}}{2k}-\frac{\delta_{2|k}y_{k}}{k}\,,\\
A_{k}^{-}&=\;-\frac{z^{k}}{2k}\left(x_{k}+\phantom{i}\delta_{2|k}\;z^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right)^{2}+\phantom{i}z^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{x_{k}y_{k}}{k}\;=\;-\frac{z^{k}}{2k}\left(x_{k}+\phantom{i}\delta_{2|k}\;z^{-\frac{k}{2}}-\phantom{i}y_{k}z^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{y_{k}^{2}}{2k}-\frac{\delta_{2|k}y_{k}}{k}\,.\end{aligned}

Eq. (
9
) follows from the formula

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-a(x+c)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=\sqrt{\pi/a}
,
which holds for all
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
a>0
.

If
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\geq 0
with
\sum_{k}kc_{k}=m
,
there are
m!/(\prod_{k\geq 1}k^{c_{k}}c_{k}!)
permutations with exactly
c_{1}

1
-cycles,
c_{2}

2
-cycles, and so on. Recall our convention that
y_{k}=0
for
k>n
, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{m}}\frac{(ix)^{\alpha}y^{\alpha}}{m!}\;=\;\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\geq 0\\
c_{1}+2c_{2}+\ldots=m\end{subarray}}\prod_{k\geq 1}\frac{(ix_{k}y_{k})^{c_{k}}}{k^{c_{k}}c_{k}!}\;=\;\exp\left(i\sum_{k=1}^{n}z^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{x_{k}y_{k}}{k}\right).

Using this together with Eq. (
9
) and Proposition
5.1
, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{m}}\frac{(ix)^{\alpha}y^{\alpha}}{m!}\right)\,\mu_{n}^{+}(z)\;=\;\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{y^{\alpha}}{(2s)!}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}(-1)^{c(\alpha_{E})}\,.

The integrand on the left-hand side is an entire function in the
y
-variables.
So, both sides agree coefficient-wise in
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
.
Also, each coefficient in front of
y^{\alpha}
only depends on the cycle type of
\alpha
.
The first stated equation follows. For the second one, observe that due to
Proposition
5.1
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{y_{k}^{2}-y_{2k}}{2k}\right)\;=\;\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}(-1)^{\sum_{k}c_{2k}(\alpha)}\,\frac{y^{\alpha}}{(2s)!}\,|\mathcal{E}(\alpha)|\;=\;\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha)\,\frac{y^{\alpha}}{(2s)!}\,|\mathcal{E}(\alpha)|\,,

where
c_{2k}(\alpha)
is the number of
2k
-cycles of
\alpha
.
∎

The following corollary tells us how to extract the numbers of graphs out of Gaussian integrals.

Corollary 6.3
.

Fix
z>0
and integers
n,s,r
such that
n\geq 1
and
s\geq 0
.
For all

,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Biggl(\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{(ix)^{\alpha}}{(2s)!}|\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)|\Biggr)\,\mu_{n}^{+}(z)

\displaystyle\;=\;(-1)^{s}z^{-s}\,\left|\{G\in\mathbf{G}_{+}:|E_{G}|=s,|V_{G}|=r\}\right|\,,\text{ and }

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Biggl(\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{x^{\alpha}}{(2s)!}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)}(-1)^{c(\alpha_{V})}\Biggr)\,\mu_{n}^{-}(z)

\displaystyle\;=\;(-1)^{r}z^{-s}\,\left|\{G\in\mathbf{G}_{-}:|E_{G}|=s,|V_{G}|=r\}\right|\,.

Proof.

Using Proposition
6.2
, we find that the left-hand sides are equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{1}{(2s)!}|\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)|\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}(-1)^{c(\alpha_{E})},\text{ and }\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{1}{(2s)!}\,{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha)\,|\mathcal{E}(\alpha)|\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)}(-1)^{c(\alpha_{V})}\text{ respectively.}

The statement follows from Theorem

because, for any permutation

, the

(-1)^{c(\alpha)}=(-1)^{n}\,{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha)
.
∎

To apply these methods to the integral expressions in Eq. (

,

Lemma 6.4
.

If

,

\displaystyle\Biggl|\exp\Biggl(\phi\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k}V(x_{k},x_{2k},\ldots)\Biggr)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\geq 0\\
2s<R\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{x^{\alpha}\lambda^{r}}{(2s)!}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)}\phi^{c(\alpha_{V})}\Biggr|\;\leq\;\left(\frac{8}{7}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2^{12}}{z}\right)^{R/12}\,.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
, the expression inside the absolute function on the left-hand side is

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{R}\;:=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\geq 0\\
2s\geq R\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{x^{\alpha}\lambda^{r}}{(2s)!}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)}\phi^{c(\alpha_{V})}\,,

which we can estimate using
|\phi|=1
and the required bounds on
\lambda
and
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{R}|\;\leq\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\geq 0\\
2s\geq R\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{z^{-\frac{5}{6}s}\,z^{r}}{(2s)!}\,|V_{r}(\alpha)|\,.

Recall that
\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)
only contains
partitions of
[2s]
into
r
blocks, each of size
\geq 3
.
Thus, the sum only has support if
r\leq 2s/3
. Hence, in the sum

z^{-\frac{5}{6}s+r}\leq z^{-\frac{5}{6}s+\frac{2}{3}s}=z^{-\frac{1}{6}s}
.
Moreover, it follows from
2s\geq R
and
z\geq 2^{12}

that

. Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{R}|\;\leq\;\left(\frac{2^{12}}{z}\right)^{R/12}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\geq 0\\
2s\geq R\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{2^{-2s}}{(2s)!}\,|V_{r}(\alpha)|\,.

After dropping the requirement that
2s\geq R
in the sum, we may apply Proposition
5.1
to get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{R}|\;\leq\;\left(\frac{2^{12}}{z}\right)^{{R}/{12}}\exp\Biggl(|\phi|\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}V\left(2^{-k},2^{-2k},\ldots\right)\Biggr)\;=\;\left(\frac{2^{12}}{z}\right)^{{R}/{12}}\exp\Biggl(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\frac{2^{-3k}}{1-2^{-k}}\Biggr)\,,

where we used Eq. (
3
). The statement
follows as

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\frac{2^{-3k}}{1-2^{-k}}\leq\frac{1}{1-2^{-1}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{2^{-3k}}{k}=2\log\frac{8}{7}
.
∎

Recall that for
\varepsilon>0
, we defined
B_{n}(\varepsilon)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|/(k\varepsilon^{k})\leq 1\}

Lemma 6.5
.

For each
n\geq 0
,
there is a constant
C_{n}>0

such that for all

,

, and

, which fulfill

and

, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{n}(\varepsilon)}\left|x^{\alpha}\,\mu^{\pm}(z)\right|\;\leq\;C_{n}\,z^{-s}\,e^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{z}}\,.

Here, the vertical bars mean that we take the modulus of the
complex-valued integrand. For instance with
\mu^{\pm}(z)=f^{\pm}(z,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}
,
|\mu^{\pm}(z)|=|f^{\pm}(z,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}
.

Proof.

We start by proving three inequalities. For all integers
n\geq k\geq 1
,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
z\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
,
and a complex number
\rho^{\pm}\in{\mathbb{C}}
of modulus
1
,
it follows directly that

(10)

\displaystyle\left|\exp\left(-\;\frac{z^{k}}{2k}\left(x_{k}\;-\;\rho^{\pm}\cdot\delta_{2|k}\cdot z^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\right|\;\leq\;\exp\Biggl(-\;\frac{z^{k}}{2k}x_{k}^{2}\;+\;\frac{z^{\frac{k}{2}}}{k}|x_{k}|\;+\;\frac{1}{2k}\Biggr)\,.

For any given non-negative integer
s
with
2s\leq n
and
\alpha\in\SG_{2s}
, we have,

(11)

\displaystyle|x^{\alpha}|\;\leq\;(2s)!\,\left(\frac{e^{2}}{z}\right)^{s}\,\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z^{\frac{k}{2}}|x_{k}|}{k}\right)\,,

To prove this second inequality observe that
k\geq 1\Rightarrow\log k\leq k
,
\frac{|x|^{k}}{k!}\leq e^{|x|}
and therefore

\displaystyle|x_{k}|^{c_{k}}\;=\;z^{-\frac{kc_{k}}{2}}\,e^{c_{k}\log k}\,\biggl(\frac{z^{\frac{k}{2}}|x_{k}|}{k}\biggr)^{c_{k}}\;\leq\;\left(\frac{e^{2}}{z}\right)^{\frac{kc_{k}}{2}}\,c_{k}!\,\exp\biggl(\frac{z^{\frac{k}{2}}|x_{k}|}{k}\biggr)\,.

Eq. (
11

|x^{\alpha}|=\prod_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|^{c_{k}}
, where
c_{k}
is the number of
k
-cycles of
\alpha

because
a!b!\leq(a+b)!
for all non-negative integers

, and
\sum_{k}c_{k}\leq\sum_{k}kc_{k}=2s
.

For all
\varepsilon,z>0

\displaystyle 0\;<\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z^{\frac{k}{2}}\,|x_{k}|}{k}\;-\;\varepsilon\sqrt{z}\,,

which holds, because

as we require

, Eq. (
12
) follows.

We can apply the inequalities Eq. (

), (

), and (

) to the definition of
\mu^{\pm}(z)
in Eq. (

) and find that for

,

, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{n}(\varepsilon)}x^{\alpha}\mu^{\pm}(z)\right|\;\leq\;(2s)!\,\left(\frac{e^{2}}{z}\right)^{s}\,e^{-\gamma\varepsilon\sqrt{z}}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{n}(\varepsilon)}\frac{\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}(|x_{k}|,z,\gamma)\right)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n},

where

) with the arbitrary non-negative

We can write the integrand on the right-hand side as an integral over the positive orthant, which we can extend to a purely Gaussian integral over

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{n}(\varepsilon)}\frac{\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}(|x_{k}|,z,\gamma)\right)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}\setminus B_{n}(\varepsilon)}\frac{\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}(x_{k},z,\gamma)\right)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}(x_{k},z,\gamma)\right)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(2+\gamma)^{2}+1}{2k}\right),

where we used the formula
\sqrt{\pi/a}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-a(x+b)^{2}}\mathrm{d}x
that holds for all

and

in the last step.
The statement follows
as

and by choosing

.

Corollary 6.6
.

For each

,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{n}(\varepsilon)}\Biggl|\Biggl(\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\geq 0\\

Proof.

Use Lemma
6.5
, that
\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)
is only non-empty if
r\leq\frac{2s}{3}
and
z\geq 1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

We will only discuss the even case in detail. The odd case follows analogously.
We fix
n\geq 1
and a
z
-dependent family of subsets
D(z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that fulfills
B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/8)\subset D(z)\subset B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/2).

We define
A^{+}_{n}(z)
to be the generating polynomial of signed even-orientable graphs with Euler characteristic bounded from below by
-n/6
, i.e., with
\chi^{+}_{n}
from Eq. (
2
),

\displaystyle A^{+}_{n}(z)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{6}\rfloor}\chi^{+}_{k}\,z^{-k}\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\geq 0\\
r-s\geq-n/6\end{subarray}}(-1)^{s}\,z^{r-s}\,\left|\{G\in\mathbf{G}_{+}:|E_{G}|=s,|V_{G}|=r\}\right|\,.

Recall that an (admissible) graph
G
fulfills
2|E_{G}|\geq 3|V_{G}|
.
Hence, the Euler characteristic
\chi(G)=|V_{G}|-|E_{G}|
is negative for every non-empty
G
, and the right-most sum above only has support if
2s\geq 3r
. In the sum, we therefore have
s-r\leq\frac{1}{3}s\leq n/6\Rightarrow 2s\leq n
.
Hence,

\displaystyle A^{+}_{n}(z)\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,r\geq 0\\
2s\leq n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{s}\,z^{r-s}\,\left|\{G\in\mathbf{G}_{+}:|E_{G}|=s,|V_{G}|=r\}\right|\;+\;\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{n}(z)\,,

where the error term is a (finite) sum over graphs that have at most
n/2
edges but whose Euler characteristic is smaller than
-n/6
. Hence,
\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{n}(z)\in z^{-\lceil n/6\rceil-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[z^{-1}]
, and
\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{n}(z)\in\mathcal{O}(z^{-\lceil n/6\rceil-1})
as
z\rightarrow\infty
.
Using Corollary
6.3
, we write the expression above as an integral,

\displaystyle A^{+}_{n}(z)\;=\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Biggl(\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\geq 0\\
2s\leq n\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{(ix)^{\alpha}z^{r}}{(2s)!}\,|\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)|\Biggr)\,\mu_{n}^{+}(z)\;+\;\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{n}(z)\,,

whose integration to a domain we may restrict to
D(z)
,

\displaystyle A^{+}_{n}(z)\;=\;\int_{D(z)}\Biggl(\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\geq 0\\
2s\leq n\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha\in\SG_{2s}}\frac{(ix)^{\alpha}z^{r}}{(2s)!}\,|\mathcal{V}_{r}(\alpha)|\Biggr)\,\mu_{n}^{+}(z)\;+\;\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{n}(z)\;+\;\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{n}(z)\,.

Observe that if
\varepsilon(z)=z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/8

and
z\geq 2^{36}
, then
\varepsilon(z)^{2}z=z^{\frac{1}{6}}/8^{2}\geq 2^{6}/8^{2}=1
.
So, as
D(z)\supset B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/8)
, we may use Corollary
6.6
to estimate the error term,

|\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{n}(z)|\leq C^{\prime}_{n}\exp(-z^{\frac{1}{12}}/8)
.

Recall the definition of
V
in Eq. (
3
) and its relation to the sum under the integral sign above due to Proposition
5.1
.
For any
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/2)
,
we have
|x_{k}|\leq k2^{-k}z^{-\frac{5}{12}k}\leq z^{-\frac{5}{12}k}

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. So, if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in D(z)\subset B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/2)
and
z\geq 2^{12}
, then the conditions for Lemma
6.4
are fulfilled, and it is reasonable to write

\displaystyle A^{+}_{n}(z)=\int_{D(z)}\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k}}{k}V(ix_{k},ix_{2k},\ldots,ix_{\lfloor n\rfloor_{k}},0,\ldots)\right)\mu_{n}^{+}(z)+\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{n}(z)+\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{n}(z)+\mathcal{R}^{(3)}_{n}(z),

where
\lfloor n\rfloor_{k}
denotes the largest multiple of
k
that does not exceed
n
. Lemma
6.4
provides an estimate of the third error term
|\mathcal{R}^{(3)}_{n}(z)|\leq\left(\frac{8}{7}\right)^{2}\left({2^{12}/z}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{12}}

for
z\geq 2^{12}
.

By expanding the exponents in the definitions of
V
in Eq. (
3
) and the Gaussian measure
\mu_{n}^{+}(z)
in Eq. (
8
) while using the definition of the action function
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{+}
in Eq. (
6
), we recover the partition function
I^{+}_{n}(z,D)
as defined in Eq. (
7
), in the integral above. Hence,

\displaystyle A^{+}_{n}(z)\;=\;I^{+}_{n}(z,D(z))\;+\;\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{n}(z)\;+\;\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{n}(z)\;+\;\mathcal{R}^{(3)}_{n}(z)\,.

The statements follow as the dominant error
term for
z\rightarrow\infty
is
\mathcal{R}^{(3)}_{n}(z)
, which falls off as
z^{-\frac{n+1}{12}}
for large
z
,
and by definition,

A^{+}_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{6}\rfloor}\chi^{+}_{k}z^{-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{12}\rfloor}\chi^{+}_{k}z^{-k}+\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{n+1}{12}})
as

.
∎

7.
A variable transformation

This section will show that a specific choice for the integration domain
D
for the partition function (
7
) results in a convenient simplification. These simplified partition functions indexed by
n
and dependent on two parameters,
z
and
\varepsilon
, will be denoted as
J_{n}^{\pm}(z,\varepsilon)
.

Recall that we defined
M_{k}(z)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{d|k}\mu(d)z^{k/d}
in Section
1.3
. Again, we define two
action
-type functions, which depend on an integer index
k\geq 1
and variables
z,y
,

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} s_{k}^{+}(z,y)&\;=\;\phantom{-}\;\frac{z^{k}}{k}\left(e^{iy}\;-\;1\right)\;-\;iyM_{k}(z)\\
s_{k}^{-}(z,y)&\;=\;-\;\frac{z^{k}}{k}\left(e^{\phantom{i}y}\;-\;1\right)\;+\;\phantom{i}yM_{k}(z)\,.\end{aligned}

The functions
J_{n}^{\pm}
are defined as

(14)

\displaystyle J_{n}^{\pm}(z,\varepsilon)\;=\;\prod_{k=1}^{n}\biggl(\exp\Bigl(\pm\,\frac{\delta_{2|k}}{2k}\Bigr)\,\int_{-k\varepsilon^{k}}^{k\varepsilon^{k}}\exp\left(s_{k}^{\pm}(z,y)\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\biggr)\,.

To relate the integrals
I^{\pm}_{n}
from Eq. (
7
)
with the ones above, we define a linear map

\iota_{n}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

such that
y=\iota_{n}(x)
is expressed coordinate-wise as

y_{k}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{x_{k\cdot\ell}}{\ell}
.
The map
\iota_{n}
is bijective, and the inverse transformation

x=\iota_{n}^{-1}(y)
is given coordinate-wise by

x_{k}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\mu(\ell)\frac{y_{k\cdot\ell}}{\ell}
.

For each
\varepsilon>0
, let
K_{n}(\varepsilon)=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|y_{k}|\leq k\varepsilon^{k}\text{ for }k\in\{1,\ldots n\}\}
. As
K_{n}(\varepsilon)
is a convex set, its preimage,
\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))
, is too.
Further, it is easy to see that triangular matrices with
1
’s on the diagonal represent
\iota_{n}
and
\iota_{n}^{-1}
. Therefore,
\det(\iota_{n})=\det(\iota_{n}^{-1})=1
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Fix
n\geq 1
, and let

D(\varepsilon)=\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
then
J^{\pm}_{n}(z,\varepsilon)=I^{\pm}_{n}(z,D(\varepsilon))
.

Proof.

To recover Eq. (
14
),
change variables to

y_{k}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{x_{\ell k}}{\ell}

in the partition integral Eq. (
7
). As
\det(\iota_{n})=1
, the Jacobian is trivial. The only nontrivial computation is

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k}}{k}x_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k}}{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\mu(\ell)\frac{y_{k\ell}}{\ell}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{k}M_{k}(z)
, where we interchanged the order of summation.
∎

The main technical result of this section is that the choice of integration domain
D(\varepsilon)=\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))
also fulfills the requirements of Theorem
6.1
with a specific choice for
\varepsilon
.

Theorem 7.2
.

For all
n\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{3}
,
we have
B_{n}(\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon)\subset\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))\subset B_{n}(3\varepsilon)
.

Corollary 7.3
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle J^{\pm}_{n}(z,z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/6)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/12\rfloor}\chi^{\pm}_{k}\,z^{-k}\;+\;\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{n+1}{12}})\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty\,.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon(z)=z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/6
and

D(z)=\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon(z)))
. Then,
by Theorem
7.2
,

B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/8)\subset D(z)\subset B_{n}(z^{-\frac{5}{12}}/2)
.
So, the statement follows from
Proposition
7.1
and Theorem
6.1
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
7.2
will occupy the remainder of this section.

Lemma 7.4
.

If
n\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{3}
and
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\iota^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))
, then
|x_{k}|\leq 2k\varepsilon^{k}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

Suppose
n
,
\varepsilon
, and
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
are given as required.
The definitions of
\iota_{n}
and
K_{n}(\varepsilon)
imply that

\left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{x_{k\cdot\ell}}{\ell}\right|\leq k\varepsilon^{k}
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
It follows that

(15)

\displaystyle|x_{k}|\;\leq\;k\varepsilon^{k}\;+\;\sum_{\ell=2}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{|x_{k\cdot\ell}|}{\ell}\text{ for all }k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\,.

We will use this inequality to prove the lemma via induction.
First, observe that Eq. (
15
) implies
|x_{n}|\leq n\varepsilon^{n}\leq 2n\varepsilon^{n}
.
Assume that for given
m\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have

|x_{k}|\leq 2k\varepsilon^{k}
for all
k\in\{m,\ldots,n\}
.
It follows from Eq. (
15
) that for all
k
with
\frac{m}{2}\leq k\leq n
,

|x_{k}|\leq k\varepsilon^{k}+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{|x_{k\cdot\ell}|}{\ell}\leq k\varepsilon^{k}+2k\sum_{\ell=2}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\varepsilon^{k\ell}=k\varepsilon^{k}\left(1+2\sum_{\ell=2}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\varepsilon^{k(\ell-1)}\right)\leq k\varepsilon^{k}\left(1+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k\ell}\right),

where we used the assumption in the second step.
Using
\varepsilon^{k}\leq\frac{1}{3}
and
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{\ell}}=\frac{1}{2}
proves that
|x_{k}|\leq 2k\varepsilon^{k}
for all
k
with
\frac{m}{2}\leq k\leq n
, which inductively implies the statement.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.2
.

We start by proving
B_{n}(\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon)\subset\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))
.
Fix
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in B_{n}(\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon)
which means that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|x_{k}|}{k(3\varepsilon/4)^{k}}\leq 1
implying

|x_{k}|\leq k(3\varepsilon/4)^{k}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Hence,

\left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{x_{k\cdot\ell}}{\ell}\right|\leq k\sum_{\ell=1}^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}(3\varepsilon/4)^{k\ell}\leq k\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(3\varepsilon/4)^{k\ell}=k\frac{(3\varepsilon/4)^{k}}{1-(3\varepsilon/4)^{k}}

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The inclusion
B_{n}(3\varepsilon/4)\subset\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))
follows, as
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{3}
implies

(\frac{3}{4})^{k}/(1-(3\varepsilon/4)^{k})\leq(\frac{3}{4})^{k}\frac{4}{3}\leq 1.

To prove
\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))\subset B_{n}(3\varepsilon)
,
fix

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\iota_{n}^{-1}(K_{n}(\varepsilon))
, which, by Lemma
7.4
, implies

|x_{k}|\leq 2k\varepsilon^{k}
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and therefore

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|x_{k}|}{k(3\varepsilon)^{k}}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{3^{k}}\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{k}}=1.

∎

8.
Evaluating the partition functions

This section will prove Theorem
1.5
using the previously established asymptotic expansions.
The strategy is to use a known asymptotic expansion for the classical
\Gamma
function to find a more explicit expression for the
z\rightarrow\infty
asymptotic expansion of the integrals in Eq. (
14
). Corollary
7.3
then relates this more explicit asymptotic expression to the numbers
\chi_{n}^{\pm}
.
It is convenient to define the following notation for the part of the expression in Eq. (
1
) without the Bernoulli numbers:

(16)

\displaystyle G_{k}^{\pm}(z)\;=\;\pm\;\left((1\;-\;L_{k}(z))\,M_{k}(z)\;-\;\frac{z^{k}}{k}\;+\;\frac{\delta_{2|k}}{2k}\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,L_{k}(z)

We will first prove the first part of Theorem
1.5
, which states that
\Psi_{k}^{\pm}(z)\in z^{-\lceil\frac{k}{6}\rceil}{\mathbb{Q}}[[\frac{1}{z}]]
. The following two lemmas imply it.

Lemma 8.1
.

If
k\geq 1
,
u\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
|u|\leq\frac{1}{4}
, then

|ku^{k}M_{k}(\frac{1}{u})|\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Recall that
M_{k}(z)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{d|k}\mu(d)z^{\frac{k}{d}}

and hence
ku^{k}M_{k}(1/u)=1+\sum_{d|k,d\neq 1}\mu(d)u^{k(1-\frac{1}{d})}
.
Because

\mu(1)=1
,
|\mu(d)|\leq 1
, and
|u|\leq 1
,
we have

|ku^{k}M_{k}(1/u)|\geq 1-k|u|^{\frac{k}{2}}
.
For all
k\geq 1
, the inequality
k\leq 2^{k-1}
holds.
Hence,
if
|u|\leq\frac{1}{4}
, then

k|u|^{\frac{k}{2}}\leq\frac{k}{2^{k}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

For
k\geq 1
, the function
u\mapsto G_{k}^{\pm}(1/u)
is
holomorphic in the disc
\{u\in{\mathbb{C}}:|u|\leq\frac{1}{8}\}
. Moreover,
there is a constant
C
such that

|G_{k}^{\pm}(1/u)|\leq C|u|^{\lceil\frac{k}{6}\rceil}
for all
u
inside that domain.

Proof.

By Lemma
8.1
,
u\mapsto L_{k}(1/u)=\log(ku^{k}M_{k}(1/u))
defines an
holomorphic function for all
|u|\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
It follows from Eq. (
16
) that

u\mapsto G_{k}^{\pm}(1/u)
is also holomorphic for all
|u|\leq\frac{1}{4}

with the possible exception of
u=0
due to the
M_{k}(1/u)
term, which has a pole at
u=0
.
We will prove that
G_{k}^{\pm}(1/u)
is regular at
u=0
.
Let
W_{k}=1-{1}/{(ku^{k}M_{k}(1/u))}
and observe that

W_{k}\;=\;-\;\delta_{2|k}u^{\frac{k}{2}}\;+\;\frac{(ku^{k}M_{k}(\frac{1}{u})-1+\delta_{2|k}u^{\frac{k}{2}})+\delta_{2|k}u^{\frac{k}{2}}\left(ku^{k}M_{k}(\frac{1}{u})-1\right)}{ku^{k}M_{k}(\frac{1}{u})}\,.

The denominator is bounded from below by
\frac{1}{2}
for all
|u|\leq\frac{1}{8}\leq\frac{1}{4}

due to Lemma
8.1
.
The numerator is a sum of two polynomials in
u^{\lceil\frac{2k}{3}\rceil}{\mathbb{Q}}[u]
whose coefficients are
\pm 1
.
Hence,

|W_{k}+\delta_{2|k}\,u^{\frac{k}{2}}|\;\leq\;2u^{\lceil\frac{2k}{3}\rceil}(2+2u+2u^{2}+\ldots)\;\leq\;4u^{\lceil\frac{2k}{3}\rceil}\frac{1}{1-u}\;\leq\;\frac{4}{1-2^{-3}}\,u^{\lceil\frac{2k}{3}\rceil}\;\leq\;\frac{32}{7}\,u^{\lceil\frac{2k}{3}\rceil}\,,

and similarly

|W_{k}|\leq u^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}(1+4u+4u^{2}\ldots)\leq\frac{11}{7}u^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}.

Abbreviating
\ell_{m}(W_{k}):=\frac{1}{W_{k}^{m}}\left(\log\frac{1}{1-W_{k}}-\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\frac{W_{k}^{n}}{n}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{W_{k}^{n}}{n+m}
allows
us to write

\displaystyle G_{k}^{\pm}(1/u)\;=\;\mp\;\frac{u^{-k}}{k}\,W_{k}^{3}\,\frac{\ell_{3}(W_{k})+\frac{1}{2}}{1-W_{k}}\;\mp\;\frac{u^{-k}}{2k}\left(W_{k}+\delta_{2|k}\,u^{\frac{k}{2}}\right)\left(W_{k}-\delta_{2|k}\,u^{\frac{k}{2}}\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{2}W_{k}\,\ell_{1}(W_{k})\,.

Each summand is regular at
u=0
and falls off at least like
u^{\lceil\frac{k}{6}\rceil}
for
u\rightarrow 0
by the estimates for
W_{k}
above.
Moreover, it follows from
k\geq 1,|u|\leq\frac{1}{8}
and
|W_{k}|\leq\frac{11}{7}u^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}\leq\frac{11}{7\sqrt{8}}<\frac{3}{4}

that

|\ell_{m}(W_{k})|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|W_{k}|^{n}}{n+m}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|W_{k}|^{n}=\frac{1}{1-|W_{k}|}\leq 4
for all
m,k\geq 1
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle|G_{k}^{\pm}(1/u)|\;\leq\;4\,\left(4+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\frac{|u|^{-k}}{k}\,|W_{k}|^{3}\;+\;\frac{|u|^{-k}}{2k}\,\left|W_{k}+\delta_{2|k}\,u^{\frac{k}{2}}\right|\left(|W_{k}|+|u|^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}\right)\;+\;\frac{4}{2}|W_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{18}{k}\,\left(\frac{11}{7}\right)^{3}|u|^{\lceil\frac{3k}{2}\rceil-k}\;+\;\frac{\frac{32}{7}\cdot(\frac{11}{7}+1)}{2k}|u|^{\lceil\frac{2k}{3}\rceil+\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil-k}\;+\;2\frac{11}{7}\,|u|^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}\;\leq\;\frac{14534}{343}\,|u|^{\lceil\frac{k}{6}\rceil}\,.\qed

To prove the expression for the Euler characteristics
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g})
in the statement of Theorem
1.5
, we define two integrals depending on three parameters,

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Q^{+}(z,\varepsilon,u)&\;=\;\int_{-\varepsilon+iu}^{\varepsilon+iu}e^{z(e^{iy}-1-iy)}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2\pi/z}},&Q^{-}(z,\varepsilon,u)&\;=\;\int_{-\varepsilon-u}^{\varepsilon-u}e^{-z(e^{y}-1-y)}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2\pi/z}}\,,\end{aligned}

because the integrands are entire functions in

, these integrals do not depend on the integration path and are well-defined for all
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and

.
The proof of Theorem
1.5
relies on the following two statements on the functions

.

Proposition 8.3
.

If
z,\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
, and

with
|u|+|\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\prime}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\leq\frac{1}{5}

\displaystyle\left|Q^{\pm}(z,\varepsilon^{\prime},u)-Q^{\pm}(z,\varepsilon,0)\right|\;\leq\;\frac{2}{5}\,e^{e}\,\frac{\exp(-\frac{7}{32}z\varepsilon^{2})}{\sqrt{2\pi/z}}\,.

The second proposition is the origin of the Bernoulli number,

, in Theorem

.

Proposition 8.4
.

If
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
, then for all

,

\displaystyle Q^{\pm}(z,\xi\cdot z^{-\frac{5}{12}},0)\;=\;\exp\biggl(\mp\,\sum_{m=1}^{R-1}\frac{B_{m+1}}{m(m+1)}z^{-m}\biggr)\;+\;\mathcal{O}(z^{-R})\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty.

Before we dive into the proofs of these last two statements,
we use them to prove Theorem
1.5
:

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By definition of
M_{k}(z)
,
1/M_{k}(z)^{m}\in z^{-km}{\mathbb{Q}}[[\frac{1}{z}]]
and, by Lemma

,
G^{\pm}_{k}(z)\in z^{-\lceil\frac{k}{6}\rceil}{\mathbb{Q}}[[\frac{1}{z}]]

\Psi_{k}^{\pm}(z)\in z^{-\lceil\frac{k}{6}\rceil}{\mathbb{Q}}[[\frac{1}{z}]]
.
To prove the formula for
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g})
, we write the integrals in Eq. (
14
) in terms of the
functions
Q^{\pm}
.
Fix
k\geq 1
and assume that
z
is sufficiently large such that
M_{k}(z)>0
.
For each
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
,

(18)

\displaystyle\exp\Bigl(\pm\frac{\delta_{2|k}}{2k}\Bigr)\int_{-\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\exp({s_{k}^{\pm}(z,y)})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\sqrt{2\pi k/z^{k}}}\;=\;\exp\left({G^{\pm}_{k}(z)}\right)\,Q^{\pm}(M_{k}(z),\varepsilon^{\prime},L_{k}(z))\,,

which follows from the definition of

in
Eq. (

in Eq. (

), of
G^{\pm}_{k}
in Eq. (
16
), and by shifting the integration variable

in the

case and

in the

We now specialize to

and also define
\varepsilon(z)=k^{\frac{17}{12}}{M_{k}}(z)^{-\frac{5}{12}}/6^{k}
.
Because

as

, we have

\varepsilon(z)=kz^{-\frac{5}{12}k}/6^{k}+\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{11}{12}k})
.
Moreover,

and hence
\varepsilon^{\prime}(z)-\varepsilon(z)=\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{11}{12}k})
as

.
Similarly, it follows that

and

.
Therefore, for all sufficiently large

Lemma 8.8

9.
Asymptotic analysis of the generating functions

This section will prove the asymptotic statements in Theorems
1.1
,
1.4
, and
1.6
. The proofs will be based on the generating function from Theorem
1.5
. Asymptotically, only the term involving the Bernoulli numbers in Eq. (
1
) will contribute, and within that term, only summands with
\ell=1,k\in\{1,2\}
and
m\rightarrow\infty
turn out to be significant.

The super-exponential growth rate of the magnitude of the Euler characteristics is due to the rapid growth of the magnitude of the Bernoulli numbers. We can read off this growth rate from Euler’s classical formula, which relates the Bernoulli numbers to the even values of the Riemann
\zeta
-function,
\zeta(n)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{n}}
:

(22)

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle\;=\;(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,2\,\frac{n!}{(2\pi)^{n}}\,\zeta(n)\quad\text{ for all even }n\geq 2\,.

For odd
n>1
, the numbers
B_{n}
vanish.

We will fix a notation for the coefficients of the part of Eq. (
1
) that involves the Bernoulli numbers. Let
\xi_{n,k}^{\pm}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
denote the coefficients defined via the following power series in
{\mathbb{Q}}[[\frac{1}{z}]]
,

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n,k}^{\pm}\,z^{-n}\;=\;\mp\,\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{m+1}}{m(m+1)}\,\frac{1}{M_{k}(z)^{m}}\text{ for all }k\geq 1\,.

Because
M_{k}(z)
has
\frac{1}{k}z^{k}
as its leading monomial,
\xi_{n,k}^{\pm}\neq 0
only if
n\geq k
.

We can estimate the coefficients
\xi_{n,k}^{\pm}
uniformly over
n
and
k
:

Lemma 9.1
.

There is a constant
C
such that
for all
n\geq k\geq 1
,

|\xi_{n,k}^{\pm}|\leq C4^{n}(\lfloor n/k\rfloor-1)!
.

Proof.

We define the expansion coefficients
a_{n}^{(k,m)}
such that
A^{(k,m)}(u)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}^{(k,m)}u^{n}=(ku^{k}M_{k}(1/u))^{-m}
.
It follows from Lemma
8.1
that the functions
A^{(k,m)}:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
are holomorphic in the disc
|u|\leq\frac{1}{4}
, where they are bounded by

|A^{(k,m)}(u)|\leq 2^{m}
. Hence, by Cauchy’s inequality,

|a_{n}^{(k,m)}|\leq 4^{n}\sup_{|u|=\frac{1}{4}}|A^{(k,m)}(u)|\leq 2^{2n+m}
.
Writing
\xi_{n,k}^{\pm}
in terms of the
a_{n}^{(k,m)}
gives

\xi_{n,k}^{\pm}\;=\;\mp\,\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}\frac{B_{m+1}}{m(m+1)}\,k^{m}\,a^{(k,m)}_{n-km}\,.

As
\zeta(n)
is decreasing with
n
, it follows immediately from Eq. (
22
) that

\left|\frac{B_{n+1}}{n(n+1)}\right|\leq\frac{2\zeta(2)}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}(n-1)!
for all
n\geq 1
.
After using this fact and the bound on
a_{n}^{(k,m)}
, we get for all
n\geq k\geq 1
,

|\xi_{n,k}^{\pm}|\;\leq\;4^{n}\,\frac{\zeta(2)}{\pi}\,\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}\left(\frac{k}{2^{2k}\pi}\right)^{m}\,(m-1)!\;\leq\;4^{n}\,\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\right\rfloor-1\right)!\,\frac{\zeta(2)}{\pi}\,\frac{1}{\pi-1}\,,

where we used
k\leq 2^{2k}
for all
k\geq 1
and
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{1/\pi^{m}}={1/(\pi-1)}
.
∎

Thanks to this estimate and our knowledge of the coefficients of the non-Bernoulli terms of Eq. (
1
) from Lemma
8.2
, we can prove that the dominant asymptotic behavior of the numbers
\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{g})
comes from only three terms in the double sum over
\ell
and
k
in Theorem
1.5
:

Proposition 9.2
.

\chi(\mathcal{GC}_{\pm}^{n+1})=\xi^{\pm}_{n,1}+\xi^{\pm}_{n,2}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2|n}\,\xi^{\pm}_{\frac{n}{2},1}+\mathcal{O}(4^{n}(\lfloor n/3\rfloor+1)!)
.

Proof.

Let
h^{\pm}_{n}
denote the expansion coefficients of

G^{\pm}(z)=\sum_{k,\ell\geq 1}\frac{\mu(\ell)}{\ell}G^{\pm}_{k}(z^{\ell})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h^{\pm}_{n}z^{-n}.

By Lemma
8.2
,

G_{k}^{\pm}(1/u)

is holomorphic in
u
and bounded by
|G_{k}^{\pm}(1/u)|\leq\frac{14534}{343}|u|^{\lceil\frac{k}{6}\rceil}

in a disc of size
\frac{1}{8}
around the origin.
The double sum

Lemma 9.5

Let

For all

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{g(z,y,\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}y\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{z}}\,e^{\frac{1}{8}\alpha^{2}}\;+\;\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{3}{4}})\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty\,.

Proof.

From Lemma
8.9
, we get

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{g(z,y,\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}y\;=\;\int_{-z^{-\frac{5}{12}}}^{z^{-\frac{5}{12}}}e^{g(z,y,\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}y\;+\;\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\exp(-z^{\frac{1}{6}}))\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty\,.

For
y\in[-z^{-\frac{5}{12}},z^{-\frac{5}{12}}]
and large

we can approximate the exponent

by a shifted quadric,
i.e.,
g(z,y,\alpha)=-z\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\sqrt{z}y+\mathcal{R}(z,y,\alpha)

\sum_{n\geq k}\frac{|y|^{n}}{n!}\leq\frac{1}{k!}|y|^{k}e^{|y|}

|\mathcal{R}(z,y,\alpha)|\;\leq\;\frac{z|y|^{3}}{3!}\,e^{|y|}\;+\;|\alpha|\,\sqrt{z}\,\frac{(|y|/2)^{2}}{2!}\,e^{|y|/2}\;\leq\;e\,\frac{z^{-\frac{1}{4}}}{6}\;+\;\sqrt{e}\,|\alpha|\,\frac{z^{-\frac{1}{3}}}{8}\text{ if }|y|\;\leq\;z^{-\frac{5}{12}}\;\leq\;1\,.

Using also
|\exp(\mathcal{R}(z,y,\alpha))-1|\leq\frac{|\mathcal{R}(z,y,\alpha)|}{1-|\mathcal{R}(z,y,\alpha)|}\in\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{1}{4}})
as

results in

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{g(z,y,\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}y\;=\;\int_{-z^{-\frac{5}{12}}}^{z^{-\frac{5}{12}}}e^{-z\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\sqrt{z}y}\,\mathrm{d}y\cdot\bigl(1\;+\;\mathcal{O}(z^{-\frac{1}{4}})\bigr)\,.

If
z
is large enough such that

and hence
\frac{z\cdot y^{2}}{4}\geq\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|\sqrt{z}|y|
for all

, then

\int_{z^{-\frac{5}{12}}}^{\infty}e^{-z\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\sqrt{z}|y|}\mathrm{d}y\,\leq\,\int_{z^{-\frac{5}{12}}}^{\infty}e^{-z\frac{y^{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|\sqrt{z}|y|-z\frac{y^{2}}{4}}\mathrm{d}y\,\leq\,\int_{z^{-\frac{5}{12}}}^{\infty}e^{-z\frac{y^{2}}{4}}\mathrm{d}y\,\in\,\mathcal{O}\Bigl(z^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-z^{\frac{1}{6}}/4}\Bigr)\,,

by Lemma
8.6
.
Hence, we may complete the Gaussian integral, evaluate it, and obtain,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-z\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\sqrt{z}y}\,\mathrm{d}y\;=\;\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z}{2}\left(y-\frac{\alpha}{2\sqrt{z}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\alpha^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{z}}e^{\frac{1}{8}\alpha^{2}}\,.\qed

We will use this integral to evaluate the following sum,
which relates closely to

Lemma 9.6
.

For all
A\in{\mathbb{C}}
that do not fall on the negative real axis,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}A^{\ell}\,\frac{(n+\ell-1)!}{(2\ell)!}\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2n}}\,n^{n}\,e^{-n+\sqrt{An}+\frac{A}{8}}\,\bigl(1\;+\;\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{1}{4}})\bigr),

where we take the square root of
A
with a positive real part.

Proof.

We denote the left-hand side by
f_{n}(A)
.
Completing the sum over
\ell
and using the integral formula for the
\Gamma

\Gamma(m)=(m-1)!=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{m}e^{-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}
, valid for all integers

f_{n}(A)\;=\;\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}A^{\ell}\,\frac{(n+\ell-1)!}{(2\ell)!}\;+\;\mathcal{R}_{n}(A)\;=\;\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{A^{\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n+\ell}\,e^{-t}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\;+\;\mathcal{R}_{n}(A)\,.

Because
(2n+2\ell)!\geq(2n+\ell)!\ell!
, we get the following bound on the remainder term,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{n}(A)|\leq\sum_{\ell=n}^{\infty}|A|^{\ell}\frac{(n+\ell-1)!}{(2\ell)!}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}|A|^{\ell+n}\frac{(2n+\ell-1)!}{(2n+2\ell)!}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}|A|^{\ell+n}\frac{(2n+\ell-1)!}{(2n+\ell)!\ell!}\leq|A|^{n}e^{|A|}.

We use the identity
\frac{1}{2}(e^{\sqrt{x}}+e^{-\sqrt{x}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}(1-(-1)^{\ell})\frac{\sqrt{x}^{\ell}}{\ell!}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{x^{\ell}}{(2\ell)!}

to perform the sum in our expression for

and get

\displaystyle f_{n}(A)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}\,\left(e^{\sqrt{At}}+e^{-\sqrt{At}}\right)\,e^{-t}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\;+\;\mathcal{R}_{n}(A)\,.

The integral above is absolutely convergent for all positive values of
n
and all
A\in{\mathbb{C}}
. So, exchanging
sum and integral is justified. We will find the large-
n
asymptotic expansion of each summand above.
With the change of variables

, the integrals in the sum become

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}\,e^{\pm\sqrt{At}-t}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\;=\;n^{n}\,e^{-n\pm\sqrt{An}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{g(n,y,\pm\sqrt{A})}\,\mathrm{d}y\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n}}\,n^{n}\,e^{-n\pm\sqrt{An}+\frac{A}{8}}\,\bigl(1+\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{1}{4}})\bigr),

where

and we used Lemma
9.5

As we assume that
\sqrt{A}
is not purely imaginary,
only the square root of

with a positive real part significantly contributes in the limit

∎

Appendix A
On the
\SG_{n}
-equivariant top weight Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

The following appendix is a reproduction of a 2008 letter by Don Zagier to Carel Faber with a couple of modifications to fit the context of the present paper.
The letter discusses the
\SG_{n}
-equivariant
top weight Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
.
In it, Zagier used computations by Faber to conjecture a first, rather complicated formula for this Euler characteristic. Chan, Faber, Galatius, and Payne recently proved this conjectured formula as their Theorem 1.1 in
[
CFGP23
]
(see their Remark 1.9 for further details on this formula’s history).
In his letter, Zagier continues to derive a second, simpler formula.
This second, previously unpublished formula can easily be seen to reduce to the even (i.e., the
\mathcal{GC}_{+}
) case of Theorem
1.5
if one sets
n=0
.

To provide further context for the following letter, we briefly explain the interpretation of both formulas in relation to
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
.
The top weight cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
can be decomposed into irreducible representations of the symmetric group
\SG_{n}
that acts by permuting the marked points:

\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{6g-6+2n}H^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n};{\mathbb{Q}})\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}\,c^{k}_{\lambda}\,V_{\lambda}\qquad\text{ for all }k\in\{0,1,\ldots\},

where
c^{k}_{\lambda}
is the multiplicity of the irreducible representation
V_{\lambda}
indexed by the integer partition
\lambda
of size
n
. The
\SG_{n}
-equivariant top weight Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is the element of the ring of formal symmetric power series,
\widehat{\Lambda}=\lim_{\leftarrow n}{\mathbb{Q}}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]]^{\SG_{n}}
, given by
A_{g}=\sum_{k,\lambda}(-1)^{k}c^{k}_{\lambda}\,s_{\lambda},

where
s_{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{\SG_{n}}
is the Schur polynomial associated to
\lambda.

A key observation made by Carel Faber in 2008 is that, for
g\geq 2
,
A_{g}
is a rational function in
P_{i}=1+p_{i}

with
p_{i}=\sum_{j}x_{j}^{i}\in\widehat{\Lambda}
.

Formulas for the
\SG_{n}
-equivariant top weight Euler characteristics of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

appendix by Don Zagier

Rule for the coefficients

For
2\leq g\leq 8
Faber found explicit formulas
giving
A_{g}
as an element of the ring
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{Q}[P_{1}^{\pm 1},P_{2}^{\pm 1},\dots]
, homogeneous of degree
1-g
,
where
\,\deg(P_{i})=i
, e.g.

A_{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{P_{1}}{P_{2}}-\,\frac{1}{12}\,\frac{1}{P_{1}}\,-\,\frac{1}{6}\,\frac{P_{1}^{2}}{P_{3}}\,-\,\frac{1}{12}\,\frac{P_{1}^{3}}{P_{2}^{2}}\,-\,\frac{1}{6}\,\frac{P_{2}P_{3}}{P_{6}}\,.

By studying these formulas, one can eventually guess the rule: the monomials occurring in
A_{g}
are those of the form

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{P_{d_{1}}^{a_{1}}\,\cdots\,P_{d_{s}}^{a_{s}}}{P_{m}^{k}}

with
m
,
k
,
d_{i}
and
a_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq s
) satisfying

(26)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&m\geq 1,\quad 0<d_{1}<\cdots<d_{s}<m,\quad d_{i}|m,\\
&k\geq 1,\quad a_{i}\geq 1,\quad r:=k+1-a_{1}-\cdots-a_{s}\geq 0,\\
&km-a_{1}d_{1}-\cdots-a_{s}d_{s}=g-1\,,\end{cases}

and the coefficient of the monomial (
25
) is equal to

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-r}(k-1)!\,B_{r}}{r!\,a_{1}!\,\cdots\,a_{s}!}\;\mu(m/d_{1})^{a_{1}}\cdots\mu(m/d_{s})^{a_{s}}\;m^{r-1}\!\prod_{p|(m,d_{1},\dots,d_{s})}\Bigl(1\,-\,\frac{1}{p^{r}}\Bigr)\,,

where
B_{r}
denotes the
r
th Bernoulli number. Notice that the coefficient (
26
) vanishes if
r
is odd and greater than 1, or if any
m/d_{i}
is not square-free,
or if
r=0
and the numbers
m,d_{1},\dots,d_{s}
have a nontrivial common factor, so that not all of the monomials (
25
)
subject to the conditions (
26
) actually occur.

The formula for
A_{g}
described in equations (
25
)–(
27
) was recently proven to hold for all
g\geq 2
(Theorem 1.1 of
[
CFGP23
]
).

Formula for the generating function

If we write the product
\prod_{p|(m,d_{1},\dots,d_{s})}(1-p^{-r})
in (
27
) as
\sum_{d|(m,d_{1},\dots,d_{s})}\mu(d)/d^{r}

and furthermore denote
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{s}
by
a
and observe that the multinomial coefficient
a!/a_{1}!\cdots a_{s}!

counts the number of ways of writing the monomial
P_{d_{1}}^{a_{1}}\cdots P_{d_{s}}^{a_{s}}
as an ordered product

P_{\ell_{1}}\cdots P_{\ell_{a}}
with
\ell_{i}|m
, we can express the rule given above in terms of the generating function

(28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\;=\;-\,\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{P_{1}^{\,2}}{P_{2}}\,+\,\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}A_{g}\,\hbar^{g-1}\;\in\;\mathcal{R}[[\hbar]]

as

(29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\;=\;\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{d|m}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,\,r\geq 0\\
a+r\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{a+1}(a+r-2)!\,B_{r}}{r!\,a!}\,\bigl(\frac{m}{d}\bigr)^{r-1}\,\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell|m\\
d|\ell\\
\ell\neq m\end{subarray}}\mu\bigl(\frac{m}{\ell}\bigr)\,\frac{P_{\ell}}{\hbar^{\ell}}\biggr)^{a}\,\biggl(\frac{\hbar^{m}}{P_{m}}\biggr)^{a+r-1}\,.

This formula can be rewritten nicely in the following way. Define

\displaystyle H(x,y)\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,\,r\geq 0\\
a+r\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{a+1}(a+r-2)!\,B_{r}}{r!\,a!}\,x^{r-1}\,y^{a}\quad\in\;\frac{1}{x}\,\mathbb{Q}[[x,y]]\,,

so that (
29
) becomes

(30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\;=\;\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{d|m}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\,H\biggl(\frac{m}{d}\,\frac{\hbar^{m}}{P_{m}},\;\frac{\hbar^{m}}{P_{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell|m\\
d|\ell\\
\ell\neq m\end{subarray}}\mu\bigl(\frac{m}{\ell}\bigr)\,\frac{P_{\ell}}{\hbar^{\ell}}\biggr)\,.

The Laurent series
H(x,y)
can be expressed in terms of functions of a single variable by the formula

(31)

\displaystyle H(x,y)\;=\;\frac{y\,-\,(1+y)\log(1+y)}{x}\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\log(1+y)\,-\,\mathcal{B}\Bigl(\frac{x}{1+y}\Bigr)\,,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x)\;=\;\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{r}}{r(r-1)}\,x^{r-1}\;=\;\frac{x}{12}\,-\,\frac{x^{3}}{360}\,+\,\frac{x^{5}}{1260}\,-\,\cdots\quad\in\mathbb{Q}[[x]]

(essentially the generating function of virtual Euler characteristics of moduli spaces of curves).
The power series
\mathcal{B}(x)
occurs in Stirling’s formula,

\displaystyle\log\Gamma(z)\,\sim\,\Bigl(z-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)\log z\,-\,z\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)\,+\,\mathcal{B}\Bigl(\frac{1}{z}\Bigr)\qquad(z\to\infty)\,,

and, as a consequence of this, or of the standard recursion formula for the Bernoulli numbers (see

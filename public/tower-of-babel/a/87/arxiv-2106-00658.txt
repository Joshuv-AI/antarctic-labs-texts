Abstract
This paper considers feedback methods for ensemble reachability of parameter-dependent linear systems ( A ⁡ ( θ ) , B ⁡ ( θ ) ) (A(\theta),B(\theta)) , where the parameter θ \theta is varying over a compact Jordan arc in the complex plane. Recently, pointwise testable sufficient conditions for uniform ensemble reachability have been developed. Beside the necessity of pointwise reachablility these conditions put restrictions on the spectra of the matrices A ⁡ ( θ ) A(\theta) and the Hermite indices of the pair ( A ⁡ ( θ ) , B ⁡ ( θ ) ) (A(\theta),B(\theta)) . In this paper we show that these conditions can be ensured by applying a suitable feedback transformation if the pair ( A ⁡ ( θ ) , B ⁡ ( θ ) ) (A(\theta),B(\theta)) is pointwise reachable and it Kronecker indices are independent from the parameter.

Introduction

An emerging field in mathematical systems and control theory refers to the task of controlling a large, potentially infinite, number of states, or systems, using a single input function or a single feedback controller. Main goals of a control theory for such problems is to establish fundamental system theoretic methods and results in the context, i.e., to prove the existence of parameter independent open-loop and closed-loop controllers, develop methods for constructing them and tackle relevant system theoretic tasks. This is a huge area and the term
ensemble control
has been established to refer to this area of research, cf.
[
9

.

Ensembles arise in statistical approaches to linear systems, where the states are replaced by probability density functions. The design of controllers that morph one probability density function into another one then become control problems of the Fokker-Planck equation or the Liouville transport equations, cf.
[

44

]
.

Ensemble control also embraces the situation of uncertainties in the model parameters. In this cases the task is to control a parameter-dependent system with a single or a few open-loop inputs which are independent from the unknown
model parameters
[
29
]
. Recently, there has been much interest in motion control problems for spatio-temporal systems and infinite platoons of vehicles
[

,

,

,
33
]
. Using Fourier-transform techniques, such control systems can be identified with parameter-dependent families of linear systems, cf.
[

A recent observation from the control of probability densities is that the range of achievable tasks becomes much wider by using a mixture of open-loop and feedback controllers. In

In the context of parameter-dependent systems and ensemble reachability, however, feedback methods have not been addressed so far. This paper devoted to this problem. In our analysis embraces the Hermite and the Kronecker indices. 2
Problem statement, notation and known results

In this paper we consider the reachability properties of parameter-dependent linear systems with the additional constraint that the open-loop control input is independent of the parameter. To investigate continuous-time systems

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}x(t,\theta)

\displaystyle=A(\theta)x(t,\theta)+B(\theta)u(t)

(1)

and discrete-time systems

\displaystyle x_{t+1}(\theta)

\displaystyle=A(\theta)x_{t}(\theta)+B(\theta)u_{t}

(2)

along the same lines the initial condition is in both cases
x(0,\theta)=x_{0}(\theta)=0
for each parameter
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
. The parameter space
\mathbf{P}
is assumed to be a Jordan arc in the complex plane, i.e.
\mathbf{P}
is the image of a continuous and bijective function defined on a compact interval. The matrix-valued functions
A:\mathbf{P}\mapsto\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and
B:\mathbf{P}\mapsto\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}
are assumed to be continuous and we will use the short notation
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
to express this. Moreover, we denote by
C_{n}(\mathbf{P})
the space of continuous functions from
\mathbf{P}
to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The time domain is
\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the discrete-time case or
[0,\infty)
in the continuous-time case, and the inputs are
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}\left([0,\infty),\mathbb{C}^{m}\right)
or
u=(u_{0},u_{1},\dots),u_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, respectively. Let

\displaystyle\varphi(T,0,u)(\theta)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{A(\theta)(T-s)}B(\theta)u(s)\mathrm{d}s

and

\displaystyle\varphi(T,0,u)(\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}A(\theta)^{k}B(\theta)u_{T-1-k}

denote the solutions of (
1
) and (
2
), respectively.

The central notion of reachability that will be considered in this paper is as follows. A pair
(A,B)
is called
uniformly ensemble reachable (from zero)
, if for any
f\in C_{n}(\mathbf{P})
and any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
T>0
and
u\in L^{1}_{m}([0,T])
or
u=(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{T-1}),\ u_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi(T,0,u)-f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbf{P}}\|\varphi(T,0,u)(\theta)-f(\theta)\|<\varepsilon.

We note that the notion ensemble reachability coincides with approximate reachability for the infinite-dimensional system, cf.
[
15
]
. Also, we note that in continuous-time ensemble reachability is independent from the final time, i.e. if it holds for some
T>0
is also holds for every
T>0
, cf.
[
16
,
39
]
. Thus, for continuous-time systems ensemble reachability is equivalent to complete ensemble reachability, i.e. for every
x_{0},f\in C_{n}(\mathbf{P})
, for every
\varepsilon>0
and for every
T>0
there is an input
u\in L^{1}_{m}([0,T])
such that
\|\varphi(T,x_{0},u)-f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
.
Also we recall that exact ensemble reachability (i.e.
\varepsilon=0
) is never possible if the parameter space is infinite. In contrast, if
\mathbf{P}=\{\theta_{1},...,\theta_{N}\}
is finite, ensemble reachability boils down to the classical finite-dimensional reachability of the corresponding parallel connection defined by the pair

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A(\theta_{1})&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&A(\theta_{N})\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}B(\theta_{1})\\
\vdots\\
B(\theta_{N})\end{pmatrix}

Problem statement
: In this paper we explore how the application of feedback methods enlarges the class of the parameter-dependent linear systems that are uniformly ensemble reachable. We consider inputs of the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=F(\theta)x(t,\theta)+u(t),\quad F\in C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P}).

and aim at conditions on
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
guaranteeing the existence of a feedback
F\in C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
and an open-loop input
u
such that the mixed open-loop and feedback controlled pair
(A+BF,B)
is uniformly ensemble reachable.

In recent years, some effort has been spent to derive necessary and sufficient conditions for uniform ensemble reachability that are verifiable just in terms of the matrices
A(\theta)
and
B(\theta)
, cf.
[
15
,
30
]
. Exemplary, we recall the following set of sufficient conditions, cf.
[
15
, Corollary 4]
.

In the case that
\mathbf{P}
is a Jordan arc a pair
(A,B)
is uniformly ensemble reachable if the following conditions are satisfied:

(N1)

(A(\theta),B(\theta))
is reachable for all
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
.

(N2)

For all distinct parameters
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}
, the spectra
\sigma\big(A(\theta)\big)
and
\sigma\big(A(\theta^{\prime})\big)
are disjoint.

(S)

For each
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
, the eigenvalues of
A(\theta)
are simple.

(H)

The Hermite indices
h_{1}(\theta),\dots,h_{m}(\theta)
of
(A(\theta),B(\theta))
are independent of
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
.

Before we recall the definition of the Hermite indices, some comments are in order. Condition (N1) is also necessary for uniform ensemble reachability. Moreover, for single-input systems condition (N2) is also necessary for uniform ensemble reachability.
The label for condition (S2) is chosen such that the notation is consistent with the labeling in
[
34
]
.

Next we recall relevant lists of indices from finite-dimensional linear control theory, cf.
[
27
]
. Let
(A,B)\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}
be a reachable pair, i.e.

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}B&AB&\cdots&A^{n-1}B\end{pmatrix}=n.

Let
b_{i}
denote the
i
th column of
B
. Selecting from left to right the first linear independent vectors

\displaystyle b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{m},Ab_{1},...,Ab_{m},A^{2}b_{1},...,A^{2}b_{m},\ldots,A^{n-1}b_{1},\ldots,A^{n-1}b_{m}

(3)

one obtains a list of basis vectors of the reachability subspace as

\displaystyle b_{1},\ldots,A^{\kappa_{1}-1}b_{1},\ldots,b_{m},\ldots,A^{\kappa_{m}-1}b_{m}.

The integers
\kappa(A,B)=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{m})
are called the
Kronecker indices
of
(A,B)
, where
\kappa_{i}:=0
if the vector
b_{i}
has not been selected.
Associated to the Kronecker indices
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{m})
we define the pair
(A_{\kappa},B_{\kappa})
given by

\displaystyle A_{\kappa}=\begin{pmatrix}{A_{\kappa_{1}}}&{0}&{\cdots}&{0}\\
{0}&{A_{{\kappa_{2}}}}&{\cdots}&{0}\\
{\vdots}&{\vdots}&{\ddots}&{\vdots}\\
{0}&{0}&{\cdots}&{A_{\kappa_{m}}}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ and }\quad B_{\kappa}=\begin{pmatrix}{b_{\kappa_{1}}}&{0}&{\cdots}&{0}\\
{0}&{b_{\kappa_{2}}}&{\cdots}&{0}\\
{\vdots}&{\vdots}&{\ddots}&{\vdots}\\
{0}&{0}&{\cdots}&{b_{\kappa_{m}}}\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where for
\kappa_{i}\geq 1
the blocks
A_{\kappa_{i}}
have size
k_{i}\times k_{i}
and the
b_{\kappa_{i}}
are columns of size
k_{i}
and have the following form

\displaystyle A_{\kappa_{i}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ c c c c c }{0}&{0}&{0}&{\dots}&{0}\\
{1}&{0}&{0}&{\dots}&{0}\\
{\vdots}&{\ddots}&{\ddots}&{\ddots}&{\vdots}\\
{0}&{\ddots}&{1}&{0}&{0}\\
{0}&{0}&{\ldots}&1&{0}\end{array}\right),\quad b_{k_{i}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ c }{1}\\
{0}\\
{\vdots}\\
{0}\\
{0}\end{array}\right)

and if
\kappa_{i}=0
the block
A_{\kappa_{i}}
is absent and
b_{\kappa_{i}}=0
.

Besides, selecting from left to right the first independent vectors

\displaystyle b_{1},Ab_{1},...,A^{n-1}b_{1},b_{2},Ab_{2},...,A^{n-1}b_{2},...,b_{m},Ab_{m},\ldots,A^{n-1}b_{m}

one obtains another list of basis vectors of the reachability subspace

\displaystyle b_{1},\ldots,A^{h_{1}-1}b_{1},\ldots,b_{m},\ldots,A^{h_{m}-1}b_{m}.

The integers
{h}(A,B)=(h_{1},\dots,h_{m})
are called the
Hermite indices
, where
h_{i}:=0
if the vector
b_{i}
has not been selected.
Recall that a pair
(A,B)
is reachable if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\kappa_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{j}=n.

Furthermore, we briefly adapt the notion of feedback equivalence from finite-dimensional linear systems to the present context of parameter-dependent linear systems. For a more comprehensive exposition, we refer to
[
18
, Sections 6.1 and 6.3]
,
[
36
, Section 5.2]
.

A triple
(T,F,S)\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{m,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{m,m}(\mathbf{P})
is called a
restricted feedback transformation
if it satisfies
T(\theta)\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
S(\theta)\in\mathbf{U}_{m}
for every
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
, i.e.
T(\theta)
is invertible and
U(\theta)
is upper triangular so that all diagonal entries are
1
. The term restricted refers to the requirement that
S(\theta)\in\mathbf{U}_{m}
. In contrast,
(T,F,S)\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{m,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{m,m}(\mathbf{P})
would be called a
feedback transformation
if
T(\theta)\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
S(\theta)\in\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
for every
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
.

In the following, for
M\in C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
and
N\in C_{m,p}(\mathbf{P})
we will write
M\,N
as a short notation for
M(\theta)N(\theta)
for every
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
. Moreover, the set of a restricted feedback transformations defines the
restricted feedback group

\mathscr{F}_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
, where the composition of two elements
(T_{1},F_{1},S_{1})
and
(T_{2},F_{2},S_{2})
is given by

\displaystyle\left(T_{1},F_{1},S_{1}\right)\circ\left(T_{2},F_{2},S_{2}\right)=\left(T_{1}T_{2},F_{1}T_{2}+S_{1}F_{2},S_{1}S_{2}\right).

Further, the neutral element of the restricted feedback group
\mathscr{F}_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
is
\left(I_{n},0_{n,m},I_{m}\right)

and the inverse of an element
(T,F,S)
is given by
\left(T^{-1},-S^{-1}FT^{-1},S^{-1}\right)
.
The restricted feedback group
\mathscr{F}_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
acts on a pair
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
as follows

\displaystyle(A,B)\quad\mapsto_{(T,F,S)}\quad\left(T(A-BS^{-1}F)T^{-1},TBS^{-1}\right)

For notational convenience we will write

\displaystyle(T,F,S)\cdot(A,B):=\left(T(A-BS^{-1}F)T^{-1},TBS^{-1}\right).

Two pairs
(A_{1},B_{1}),(A_{2},B_{2})
in
C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
are called
restricted feedback equivalent on

\mathbf{P}
, denoted by
\sim_{\mathbf{P}}
, if there exists
(T,F,S)\in\mathscr{F}_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
such that

\displaystyle(A_{2},B_{2})=(T,F,S)\cdot(A_{1},B_{1}).

Note that
(A_{1},B_{1})\sim_{\mathbf{P}}(A_{2},B_{2})
if and only if there is a restricted feedback transformation
(T,F,S)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}TA_{1}-A_{2}T&=B_{2}F\\
TB_{1}&=B_{2}S.\end{split}

(5)

Also, we recall that the Kronecker indices
\kappa(A,B)
are invariant under restricted feedback transformations, cf.
[
18
, Lemma 6.16]
, i.e. for all

(T,F,S)\in\mathscr{F}_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
it holds

\displaystyle\kappa(A,B)(\theta)=\kappa\left(T(A+BF)T^{-1},TBS^{-1}\right)(\theta).

For future use we recap the following well-known result.

Lemma 1

Let
T\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})
and suppose that
T(\theta)=\big(t_{1}(\theta),\cdots,t_{n}(\theta)\big)\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for every
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
.

(a)

Then the mapping
\theta\mapsto T(\theta)^{-1}
is continuous.

(b)

Let
v\colon\mathbf{P}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be continuous. Then, the coordinates
\alpha_{1}(\theta),\dots,\alpha_{n}(\theta)
of
v(\theta)
with respect to the basis
t_{1}(\theta),\dots,t_{n}(\theta)
depend continuously on
\theta
.

3
Feedback Equivalence for parameter-dependent linear systems and canonical forms

In this section we show that every pointwise reachable pair
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
with constant Kronecker indices is restricted feedback equivalent to
(A_{\kappa},B_{\kappa})
. This result will be used in the proof of Theorem
3
and might be of independent interest. The proof follows the exposition in
[
18
]
, where the finite-dimensional case is treated. The main step in the subsequent proof will be to conclude that the constructed restricted feedback transformation in
[
18
, Proof of Theorem 6.18]
is continuous in the parameter.

Theorem 1

Suppose that
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
has constant Kronecker indices
\kappa(A,B)(\theta)=(\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{m})
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_{i}=n
, then
(A,B)\sim_{\mathbf{P}}(A_{\kappa},B_{\kappa})
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa(A,B)=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{m})
denote the constant Kronecker indices. We will construct a suitable feedback transformation
(T,F,S)\in\mathscr{F}_{n,m}(\mathbf{P})
in four steps.

Step 1
: First we apply a transformation of the form
(I,0,U)
. By definition and since
(A,B)
is pointwise reachable, it holds that

\displaystyle b_{1}(\theta),...,A(\theta)^{\kappa_{1}-1}b(\theta)_{1},b(\theta)_{2},...,A(\theta)^{\kappa_{2}-1}b(\theta)_{2},...,b(\theta)_{m},...,A(\theta)^{\kappa_{m}-1}b(\theta)_{m}

is a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for each
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
. By construction, for every
i=1,...,m
there are functions
\alpha_{ij}\colon\mathbf{P}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\beta_{ijl}\colon\mathbf{P}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle{A(\theta)^{\kappa_{i}}b(\theta)_{i}}={\sum_{j<i}}\alpha_{ji}(\theta)A(\theta)^{\kappa_{i}}b(\theta)_{j}+\sum_{l=1}^{\kappa_{i}}A(\theta)^{l-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{ijl}(\theta)b(\theta)_{j}

(6)

Since the functions
\theta\mapsto A(\theta)^{l}b(\theta)_{i}
are continuous for every
l=0,1,2,...
and
i=1,...,m
, by Lemma
1
(b) we have that
\alpha_{ji}\in C(\mathbf{P})
and
\beta_{ijl}\in C(\mathbf{P})
. Then, we define the continuous upper-triangular matrix

\displaystyle U(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\alpha_{12}(\theta)&-\alpha_{13}(\theta)&\cdots&-\alpha_{1m}(\theta)\\
0&1&-\alpha_{23}(\theta)&\cdots&-\alpha_{2m}(\theta)\\
\vdots&0&1&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&-\alpha_{(m-1)\,m}(\theta)\\
0&&&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then, for
\tilde{B}(\theta):=B(\theta)U(\theta)
the columns of
\tilde{B}
and
B
are related as follows

\displaystyle{\tilde{b}(\theta)_{i}}=b(\theta)_{i}-\sum_{j<i}\alpha_{ji}(\theta)b(\theta)_{j},\quad i=1,\ldots,m,

or equivalently

\displaystyle{b(\theta)_{i}}=\tilde{b}(\theta)_{i}-\sum_{j<i}\tilde{\alpha}_{ji}(\theta)\tilde{b}(\theta)_{j},

where
\tilde{\alpha}_{ji}(\theta)
denote the entries of
U^{-1}
. Note that
\tilde{\alpha}_{ji}\colon\mathbf{P}\to\mathbb{C}
are continuous functions. For each
i=1,...,m
it follows from (

\displaystyle\begin{split}A(\theta)^{\kappa_{i}}{\tilde{b}(\theta)_{i}}&=\sum_{l=1}^{\kappa_{i}}A(\theta)^{l-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\beta}_{ijl}(\theta){b(\theta)_{j}}\\
&=\sum_{l=1}^{\kappa_{i}}A(\theta)^{l-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\beta}_{ijl}(\theta)\left(\tilde{b}(\theta)_{j}-\sum_{\mu<j}\tilde{\alpha}_{\mu j}\tilde{b}(\theta)_{\mu}\right)\\
&=\sum_{l=1}^{\kappa_{i}}A(\theta)^{l-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tilde{\beta}}_{ijl}(\theta){\tilde{b}(\theta)_{j}},\end{split}

(7)

with
\tilde{\beta}_{ijl}\in C(\mathbf{P})
. To see that these functions are continuous, observe that the
\tilde{\beta}_{ijl}
are compositions of the continuous functions
\tilde{\alpha}_{ji}
and
{\beta}_{ijl}
.

Step 2
: In this step we will construct a continuous transformation
(T^{-1},0,I)
so that the
0
- and
1
-entries are at the right places. To this end, for
i=1,\dots,m
and
l:=2,\dots\kappa_{i}
we define continuous vectors

\displaystyle\mathrm{v}_{1i}(\theta)

\displaystyle:={\tilde{b}(\theta)_{i}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{v}_{li}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=A(\theta)^{l-1}{\tilde{b}}(\theta)_{i}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{l-1}A(\theta)^{l-1-\mu}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{\beta}_{ij(\kappa_{i}+1-\mu)}(\theta)\tilde{b}(\theta)_{j}

and the transformation

\displaystyle T(\theta):=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{v}_{11}(\theta)&\cdots&\mathrm{v}_{\kappa_{1}1}(\theta)&\mathrm{v}_{12}(\theta)&\cdots&\mathrm{v}_{\kappa_{2}2}(\theta)&\cdots&\mathrm{v}_{1m}(\theta)&\cdots&\mathrm{v}_{\kappa_{m}m}(\theta)\end{pmatrix}.

To see that
T(\theta)
is continuously invertible, by Lemma

(b) it suffices to show that for every
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
the columns of
T(\theta)
define a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Indeed, fix
\theta^{*}\in\mathbf{P}
and let

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}:=\operatorname{span}\{\mathrm{v}_{li}(\theta^{*})\,|\,i=1,...,m,\,l=1,...,\kappa_{i}\}.

Since
(A(\theta^{*}),B(\theta^{*}))
is reachable the claim follows by verifying that
\mathscr{X}
is

To ease notation, we drop the dependence on
\theta^{*}
and shortly write

\gamma_{il}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{\beta}_{ijl}\tilde{b}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{\beta}_{ijl}\mathrm{v}_{1j}\in\mathscr{X}

for a moment. Then, for
i=1,...,m
and
l<\kappa_{i}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}A\mathrm{v}_{li}&=A^{(l+1)-1}\tilde{b}_{i}-A\left(A^{l-2}\gamma_{i\kappa_{i}}+A^{l-3}\gamma_{i(\kappa_{i}-1)}+\cdots\gamma_{i(\kappa_{i}-l)}\right)-\gamma_{i(\kappa_{i}-(l+1))}+\gamma_{i(\kappa_{i}-(l+1))}\\

(8)

For

we use the same reasoning as above together with
(

4
Main Results

The main results of the paper explore the possibility to derive uniformly ensemble reachable systems by using a mixture of open-loop inputs and feedback controllers. We begin with the single-input case. In this case there is only one Hermite index and it is equal to
n
if condition (N1) holds.
Also, the conditions (N2) and (S2) put restrictions on the spectra of the matrices
A(\theta)
. Under the assumption that the pairs are reachable for every parameter, the spectra of the matrices can be assigned arbitrarily by the the Pole-Shifting Theorem, cf.
[
18
, Theorem 6.23]
. We obtain the following result.

Theorem 2

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a Jordan arc and assume that
(A,b)\in C_{n,n}(\mathbf{P})\times C_{n}(\mathbf{P})
is pointwise reachable. Then, there is a continuous feedback
f\in C_{1,n}(\mathbf{P})
such that the feedback pair
(A+bf,b)
is uniformly ensemble reachable.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{P}
is Jordan arc, there is a continuous and injective function
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbf{P}
. Then, for
1\leq l\leq k<n
we define the injective mappings

\displaystyle\lambda_{l}(\theta):=e^{2\pi i\,\left(\gamma^{-1}(\theta)\tfrac{l-1}{k}+(1-\gamma^{-1}(\theta))\left(\tfrac{l}{k}-\tfrac{1}{k+1}\right)\right)}\in\partial\mathbb{D}.

and
k<l\leq n

\displaystyle\lambda_{l}(\theta):=(l+1)-\gamma^{-1}(\theta)\in\mathbb{R}.

Consequently, for all
k\neq l
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{l}(\mathbf{P})\cap\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{P})=\emptyset.

Next we define the family of monic polynomials
(p_{\theta})_{\theta\in\mathbf{P}}
by

\displaystyle p_{\theta}(z):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\lambda_{i}(\theta)).

Then, by Ackermann’s Formula
[
18
, Theorem 6.20]
, the family

\displaystyle f(\theta):=(0,\dots,0,1)R(A(\theta),b(\theta))^{-1}p_{\theta}(A(\theta))

of state-feedback, where
R(A(\theta),b(\theta))=\begin{pmatrix}b(\theta)&A(\theta)b(\theta)&\cdots&A(\theta)^{n-1}b(\theta)\end{pmatrix}
is the reachability matrix, satisfies

\displaystyle\det(zI-A(\theta)+b(\theta)f(\theta))=p_{\theta}(z).

Thus, the spectral conditions (N2) and (S2) are fulfilled and it remains to prove that
\theta\mapsto F(\theta)
is continuous on
\mathbf{P}
. Since
A(\theta)
is continuous and
p_{\theta}
is a polynomial,
\theta\mapsto p_{\theta}(A(\theta))
is also continuous. Furthermore the reachability matrix
R(A(\theta),b(\theta))
is continuous and invertible for every
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
. So, by Lemma
1
(a) its inverse is also continuous. Hence,
F(\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathbf{P}
. Finally we note that for single-input systems condition (N1) implies condition (H). This shows the assertion.
∎

\tilde{f}_{1}
and
\tilde{f}_{2}
, respectively, i.e.

\displaystyle q_{n}(z):=B_{n,\tilde{f}_{2}}(z)\quad\text{ and }\quad r_{n}(z):=B_{n,\tilde{f}_{1}}(z).

Then, by
[
24
, Theorem 1]
we have

\|p_{n}(A_{k})b_{g}-f\|_{\infty}\leq\tfrac{1}{g(-\theta^{*})}\,\left\|p_{n}\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&h_{k}(\theta)\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\right)\binom{1}{0}-\tfrac{1}{g}\binom{f_{2}}{f_{1}}\right\|_{\infty}\\
\leq\tfrac{1}{g(-\theta^{*})}\,\max_{i=1,2}|B_{n,\tilde{f}_{i}}(z)-\tilde{f}_{i}(z)|_{\infty}\leq\tfrac{1}{g(-\theta^{*})}\,\max_{i=1,2}\,\left\{4M_{\tilde{f}_{i}}+\frac{k\,c_{g,\mathbf{P}}L_{\tilde{f}_{i}}}{2}\right\}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\\
\leq\tfrac{1}{g(-\theta^{*})}\,\left(\frac{4M_{f}}{\min_{\theta\in\mathbf{P}}|g(\theta)|}+\,\frac{k\,c_{g,\mathbf{P}}}{2\min_{\theta\in\mathbf{P}}|kg^{\prime}(\theta)-2\theta|}\left(M_{f}\,\frac{L_{g}}{m_{g}^{2}}+\frac{L_{f}}{m_{g}}\right)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}

for
n\geq 3
. This shows the assertion.
∎

The latter error bound shows that the approximation is getting better the larger the feedback gain is. In terms of the eigenvalues of the matrices
A_{k}(\theta)
it can be observed that the eigenvalue arcs
\theta\mapsto\sqrt{kg(\theta)-\theta^{2}}
and
\theta\mapsto-\sqrt{kg(\theta)-\theta^{2}}
are located on the positive and the negative real line, respectively, and the gap between them is increasing with the feedback gain
k
. Also, we emphasize that for pure open-loop inputs, i.e.
k=0
, the pair
(A_{0},b_{g})
is not uniformly ensemble reachable over the parameter set
\mathbf{P}=[-\theta^{*},\theta^{*}]
. This corresponds to the fact that the traces of the eigenvalue arcs
\theta\mapsto i\theta
and
\theta\mapsto-i\theta
coincide. By using a feedback gain
k\in\mathbb{R}
the traces can be separated and the assumptions in
[
15
, Theorem 3.1.1 (c)]
are satisfied such that the pair
(A_{k},b_{g})
is uniformly ensemble reachability.

Sobolev spaces via chains in metric measure spaces
Emanuele Caputo, Nicola Cavallucci

Abstract
. We define the chain Sobolev space on a possibly non-complete metric measure space in terms of chain upper gradients. In this context, ε \varepsilon -chains are a finite collection of points with distance at most ε \varepsilon between consecutive points. They play the role of discrete versions of curves. Chain upper gradients are defined accordingly and the chain Sobolev space is defined by letting the size parameter ε \varepsilon going to zero. In the complete setting, we prove that the chain Sobolev space is equal to the classical notions of Sobolev spaces in terms of relaxation of upper gradients or of the local Lipschitz constant of Lipschitz functions. The proof of this fact is inspired by a recent technique developed by Eriksson-Bique in [ EB23 ] . In the possible non-complete setting, we prove that the chain Sobolev space is equal to the one defined via relaxation of the local Lipschitz constant of Lipschitz functions, while in general they are different from the one defined via upper gradients along curves. We apply the theory developed in the paper to prove equivalent formulations of the Poincaré inequality in terms of pointwise estimates involving ε \varepsilon -upper gradients, lower bounds on modulus of chains connecting points and size of separating sets measured with the Minkowski content in the non-complete setting. Along the way, we discuss the notion of weak ε \varepsilon -upper gradients and asymmetric notions of integral along chains.

1.
Introduction

A fundamental research direction in analysis on metric spaces is the development of calculus with Sobolev functions and Lipschitz functions defined on metric measure spaces
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
.
After the work of Shanmuganlingam
[
Sha00
]
, the Sobolev seminorm of a Borel function
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
is the infimum of the
L^{p}({\rm X})
-norms of all upper gradients of
u
, where a function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
is an upper gradient of
u
if the following weak version of fundamental theorem of calculus holds for every rectifiable curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
:

|u(\gamma_{1})-u(\gamma_{0})|\leq\int_{0}^{1}g(\gamma_{t})|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

We refer the reader to the classical textbook
[
HKST15
]
.

Another classical approach in the subject concerning the case
p>1
is based on a relaxation procedure of appropriate functionals with respect to the
L^{p}({\rm X})
-topology, playing the role of the Dirichlet energy. In Section
2.1
we will recall these relaxation procedures in detail.
On one side, Cheeger in
[
Che99
]
considered the relaxation of the
L^{p}({\rm X})
-norm of upper gradients. Here we denote the corresponding Banach space by
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
. On the other hand, Ambrosio, Gigli and Savaré in
[
AGS13
,
AGS14
]
studied the relaxation of the
L^{p}({\rm X})
-norm of the local Lipschitz constant
{\rm lip}\,u
of Lipschitz functions
u
. The corresponding Banach space is denoted by
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
. For details on these spaces we refer to Section
3
.

Other approaches are available, like the ones defined via integrations along test plans
[
AGS13
,
AGS14
]
, but this approach will not be used in this work. We refer the reader to the recent survey
[
AILP24
]
.

The main results of
[
AGS13
,
ADM14
,
EB23
,
LP24
]
show that the spaces
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
coincide for
p\geq 1
, if the metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete. However, for non complete metric spaces they can be different, see Example
3.4
.

This work comes from the following question: is it possible to show that
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
is equal to a space obtained via relaxation in terms of a suitable notion of upper gradients when the metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is not assumed to be complete?

The answer is affirmative and leads to an alternative definition of Sobolev or
BV
spaces, expressed in terms of chains instead of curves. We recall that a
\varepsilon
-chain, for
\varepsilon>0
, is a finite collection of points
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
such that
{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})\leq\varepsilon
for every
i=0,\ldots,N-1
.

The integration along rectifiable paths into Shanmugalingam’s definition is replaced by integration along
\varepsilon
-chains. With this analogy in mind, a function
g
is a
\varepsilon
-upper gradient of
u
provided that

|u(q_{N})-u(q_{0})|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{g(q_{i})+g(q_{i+1})}{2}{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})=:\int_{{\sf c}}g

for every
\varepsilon
-chain
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
. This can be seen as a discrete analogue of the integral along a rectifiable path, see Proposition
4.4
for a precise statement. The set of all
\varepsilon
-upper gradients of
u
is denoted by
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
.
The corresponding functional is

(1)

{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}\colon L^{p}({\rm X})\to[0,+\infty],\quad u\mapsto\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf\left\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\right\}.

The Banach space obtained by relaxation of
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}
is denoted by
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})
.

The first result shows the equivalence of the spaces introduced so far, if the metric space is complete.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space such that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete. Then

(2)

H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})=H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})=H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})

and

(3)

\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})}=\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})}=\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})}

for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
.

The second result, which answers the aforementioned question, establishes the equality between
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})
also for non complete metric spaces. It is obtained from Theorem
1.1
and the fact that
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})=H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}(\bar{{\rm X}})
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})=H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})
, where
\bar{{\rm X}}
is the metric completion of
{\rm X}
, see Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
6.9
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space, not necessarily complete. Then

(4)

H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})=H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})

and

(5)

\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})}=\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})}

for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
.

Other possible notions of integral along chains are considered in the paper, leading to the definition of the same space.

1.1.
The proof of the main results

The proof of Theorem
1.1

is inspired by the approximation method developed by Eriksson-Bique in
[
EB23
]
. It is not apriori clear the relation between
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
. Therefore we introduce an auxiliary space, that we denote as
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
and it is defined as the domain of finiteness of the lower semicontinuous envelop of the following energy

(6)

{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}\colon L^{p}({\rm X})\to[0,+\infty],\quad u\mapsto\begin{cases}\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\lim}\inf\left\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\cap{\rm Lip}({\rm X})\right\}&\text{if }u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The space is normed with the sum of the
L^{p}
-norm and the relaxation of the energy functional above.
The functions for which
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}
is finite play the role of regular functions, being Lipschitz and with Lipschitz upper gradients. They form a regular class of functions for which one could hope to get density in energy in
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
, in full generality.

On one side, one easily gets

(7)

H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})\qquad\text{and}\qquad H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})

and that the inclusions are
1
-Lipschitz.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is achieved into two steps, respectively proving that the reverse inclusions in (
7
) hold and are
1
-Lipschitz.

To do both, we follow the mentioned approximation scheme in
[
EB23
]
, that we briefly recall, in a simplified form, for reader’s convenience in the case of the proof of the inclusion
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
. For every given
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
, proceed using the following steps.

(Step 1)

Reduce the proof to the case where
u
is bounded, with bounded support and nonnegative.

(Step 2)

For every upper gradient along curves
g
, define Lipschitz functions
g_{j}
that converge to
g
pointwise and in
L^{p}({\rm X})
.

(Step 3)

Define the functions

u_{j}(x):=\inf\left\{u(q_{0})+\int_{\sf c}g_{j}\,:\,{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\text{ is a }\frac{1}{j}\text{-chain such that }q_{N}=x\right\}.

Prove that
u_{j}
is Lipschitz, has bounded support and it has
g_{j}
as
\frac{1}{j}
-upper gradient.

(Step 4)

Conclude the proof showing that
u_{j}
converges to
u
in
L^{p}({\rm X})
via a contradiction argument. The contradiction is obtained by violating the fact that
g
is an upper gradient of
u
.

So, in order to prove that
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
one could follow this scheme with minor modifications. However, there are technical issues in adapting the proof to show that
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
, that we briefly present in a moment. That is why we will give a different proof of
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
too, that can be easily adapted to show
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
.

The main problem is related to the reduction in Step 1.
As stated, this is a consequence of the well known locality property of the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient along curves, which can be derived from a Leibniz rule. The same locality property for
p
-weak
\varepsilon
-upper gradients does not hold, and it is also not clear if it is true in a limit sense for
\varepsilon
going to zero (see Remark
6.5
). However, a weak Leibniz rule for
\varepsilon
-chain upper gradients holds (see Proposition
6.8
). Using it we are able to reduce the proof, also for
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})
, to bounded functions with bounded support, but
not necessarily nonnegative
.

This difference creates two additional difficulties in the scheme sketched above. The first one is that the approximating functions
u_{j}
are not necessarily with bounded support. This requires an additional cutoff argument.
The second difference involves the core of the proof, namely the contradiction argument in Step 4. We need to analyze separately three different cases, one of which is the only one that needed to be considered in
[
EB23
]
. For more details we refer to Step 8 of the proof of Theorem
6.4
.

Moreover, the adaptation of the proof to the inclusion
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
requires an additional care in the contradiction argument in Step 4. This is due to the fact that every
\varepsilon
-upper gradient, that takes extended values, satisfies the upper gradient inequality along many curves but not necessarily along all of them, see Proposition
4.4
.

1.2.
Application to characterizations of Poincaré inequality

A standard consequence of theorems like Theorem
1.2
is the equivalence between apriori different formulations of the Poincaré inequality. Tailoring such a discussion in our case, we have the equivalence between the Poincaré inequality formulated for couples
(u,{\rm lip}\,u)
with
u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
and
(u,g)
where
u
is Borel and
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
for some
\varepsilon>0
, see Corollary
7.1
. This result holds also in non-complete metric space. While in the complete setting the conditions above are also equivalent to the Poincaré inequality formulated for couples
(u,g)
where
u
is Borel and
g
is an upper gradient of
u
, this is no more true if the metric measure space is not complete, see Remark
7.2
.

The Poincaré inequality on complete and doubling metric measure spaces can be characterized in at least three ways: Heinonen’s pointwise estimates in
[
Hei01
]
, Keith’s modulus estimates in
[
Kei03
]
and via energy of separating sets for
p=1
in
[
CC24b
]
.
We reinterpret these characterizations also for non-complete spaces in terms of chains respectively in Sections
7.1
,
7.2
and
7.3
. To treat the analogue of Keith’s estimate we need to introduce the notion of modulus of a family of chains and to study its basic properties. This is done in Section
5
.

Interestingly, the approach via chains allows to improve a result concerning upper gradients along curves by relaxing the assumptions on the metric measure space. Indeed in Proposition
7.6
and Remark
7.7
we prove the equivalence of the validity of the following Heinonen’s pointwise estimates at a fixed couple of points
x,y\in{\rm X}
:

|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\leq C{\sf d}(x,y)^{p-1}\int({\rm lip}\,u)^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}\qquad\text{for all }u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X})

and

|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\leq C{\sf d}(x,y)^{p-1}\int g^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}\qquad\text{for all $u$ Borel and $g$ upper gradient of $u$.}

Here
\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}
is the measure that is absolutely continuous with respect to the reference measure with density being the truncated Riesz potential with poles
x
and
y
(see Section
7.1
).
This result was proved by the authors in
[
CC24a
, Theorem A.3]
, under the additional assumption of local quasiconvexity of the metric space. The use of chains allows to remove this additional assumption. It is likely that similar improvements can be performed in other situations.

1.3.
Structure of the paper

Section
2
contains general facts about measure theory, curves and chains on metric spaces. In Section
3
we recall the definition of Sobolev spaces via a relaxation approach. In Section
4
we define chain upper gradients and we derive basic properties and relations with the classical upper gradients along curves. Section
5
introduces the notions of modulus of a family of chains and of weak chain upper gradient. Similarities and differences with the theory of weak upper gradients along curves are shown. In Section
6
we define the Sobolev spaces via chain upper gradients. In Section
6.1
we prove the main results, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
Section
7
contains equivalent formulations of Poincaré inequality in the possibly non-complete setting in terms of chains.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. With this term, we mean that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a separable metric space which is not necessarily complete and
\mathfrak{m}
is a non-trivial outer measure which is Radon and finite on bounded sets. If
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is a metric measure space in the sense of
[
HKST15
, p.62]
, i.e it is a triple such that

({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete and separable and
\mathfrak{m}
is a non-trivial outer measure which is Borel regular and finite on bounded sets, then
\mathfrak{m}
is Radon by
[
HKST15
, Proposition 3.3.44]
, and so
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is a metric measure space in the sense above. This is false if
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is not complete.
If a triple
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is such
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is separable and
\mathfrak{m}
is a non-trivial outer measure which is finite on bounded sets, then
\mathfrak{m}
is Radon if and only if the space
(\bar{{\rm X}},\bar{{\sf d}},\bar{\mathfrak{m}})
is again a metric measure space as defined above (cp.
[
HKST15
, Proposition 3.3.46]
: the proof still works with our assumption of finiteness on bounded set in place of local finiteness), where
(\bar{{\rm X}},\bar{{\sf d}})
denotes the completion of
({\rm X},{\sf d})
and
\bar{\mathfrak{m}}
is the outer measure
\bar{\mathfrak{m}}(E):=\mathfrak{m}(E\cap{\rm X})
for every
E\subseteq\bar{{\rm X}}
.

A metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is said to be doubling if there exists
C_{D}\geq 1
such that

(8)

\mathfrak{m}(B_{2r}(x))\leq C_{D}\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))\qquad\text{ for all }x\in{\rm X},r>0.

If
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is doubling and
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete, then
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a proper metric space, i.e. every closed bounded set is compact.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})
the space of functions
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\int|u|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}<\infty
, and by
L^{p}({\rm X})
its quotient by the equivalence relation that identifies two functions if they agree
\mathfrak{m}
-almost everywhere. The
L^{p}({\rm X})
norm will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}
. The class of Lipschitz functions on
{\rm X}
is denoted by
{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
.

A function
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded if there exists
M\geq 0
such that
|u|\leq M
. It has bounded support if there exists a bounded subset
B\subseteq{\rm X}
such that
u\equiv 0
on
{\rm X}\setminus B
. The slope of
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
at size
\varepsilon>0
is defined as

{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}u(x):=\sup_{y\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{{\sf d}(y,x)}.

If
\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
then
{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}u\leq{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}u
. The local Lipschitz constant of
u
is defined as

{\rm lip}\,u(x):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}u(x)=\inf_{\varepsilon>0}{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}u(x).

2.1.
Relaxation of functionals

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
{\rm F}\colon\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})\to[0,+\infty]
be a functional such that

(a)

{\rm F}(0)=0
,

(b)

{\rm F}(u+v)\leq{\rm F}(u)+{\rm F}(v)
,

(c)

{\rm F}(\lambda u)=|\lambda|{\rm F}(u)

for every
u,v\in\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. By definition, the relaxation of
{\rm F}
is the biggest functional
\tilde{\rm F}\colon\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})\to[0,+\infty]
which is lower semicontinuous with respect to the
L^{p}({\rm X})
-norm and such that
\tilde{\rm F}\leq{\rm F}
. A concrete description of
\tilde{\rm F}
is given by

(9)

\tilde{\rm F}(u)=\inf\left\{\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}{\rm F}(u_{j})\,:\,u_{j}\underset{L^{p}({\rm X})}{\longrightarrow}u\right\}.

By the definition above,
\tilde{\rm F}
induces a functional on
L^{p}({\rm X})
which is lower semicontinuous and satisfies properties (a), (b) and (c) above. Therefore one can define the space

(10)

H^{1,p}_{{\rm F}}({\rm X}):=\left\{u\in L^{p}({\rm X})\,:\,\tilde{\rm F}(u)<+\infty\right\}

endowed with the norm

(11)

\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm F}}({\rm X})}^{p}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}^{p}+\tilde{\rm F}(u)^{p}.

The normed space
(H^{1,p}_{{\rm F}}({\rm X}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm F}}({\rm X})})
is a Banach space since
\tilde{\rm F}
is
L^{p}({\rm X})
-lower semicontinuous.
Indeed, given a
H^{1,p}_{{\rm F}}({\rm X})
-Cauchy sequence
u_{j}
, that is also
L^{p}({\rm X})
-Cauchy, we can extract an
L^{p}({\rm X})
-limit
u
. By lower semicontinuity,
{\rm F}(u-u_{k})\leq\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}{\rm F}(u_{j}-u_{k})
for every
k
, thus

0\leq\varlimsup_{k\to+\infty}{\rm F}(u-u_{k})\leq\varlimsup_{k\to+\infty}\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}{\rm F}(u_{j}-u_{k})=0

where the last equality follows by
\{u_{j}\}
being
H^{1,p}_{{\rm F}}({\rm X})
-Cauchy. Throughout the whole paper we will consider several functionals
{\rm F}
, where it can be readily checked that they always satisfy properties (a), (b) and (c) above.

2.2.
Curves

A curve is a continuous function
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to{\rm X}
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
. The starting and final point of
\gamma
are respectively
\alpha(\gamma):=\gamma(a)
and
\omega(\gamma):=\gamma(b)
.
The length of a curve
\gamma
is defined as

(12)

\ell(\gamma):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}{\sf d}(\gamma_{t_{i}},\gamma_{t_{i+1}})\,:\,a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=b,\,N\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

A curve of finite length is called rectifiable. Every rectifiable curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to{\rm X}
admits a reparametrization
s_{\gamma}\colon[0,\ell(\gamma)]\to[a,b]
by arc-length. This means that the curve
\gamma^{\prime}:=\gamma\circ s_{\gamma}\colon[0,\ell(\gamma)]\to{\rm X}
satisfies
\ell(\gamma^{\prime}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[0,t]}$})=t
for every
t\in[0,\ell(\gamma)]
.
Given two points
x,y\in{\rm X}
, we denote by
\Gamma_{x,y}
the set of rectifiable curves
\gamma
with
\alpha(\gamma)=x
and
\omega(\gamma)=y
.

The integral of a Borel function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
over a rectifiable curve
\gamma
is

(13)

\int_{\gamma}g:=\int_{0}^{\ell(\gamma)}g(\gamma(s_{\gamma}(t)))\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

2.3.
Chains

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space and
\varepsilon>0
.
A
\varepsilon
-chain is a finite collection of points
\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
such that
{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})\leq\varepsilon
. The set of all
\varepsilon
-chains of
{\rm X}
is denoted by
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}({\rm X})
. The set of all chains of
{\rm X}
is
\mathscr{C}({\rm X}):=\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}({\rm X})
. When the context is clear we simply write
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathscr{C}
.
More generally, if
E
is a subset of
{\rm X}
, we set
\mathscr{C}(E):=\{{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}\,:\,q_{i}\in E\text{ for some }0\leq i\leq N\}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}(E):=\mathscr{C}(E)\cap\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}
. Notice that the two definitions of
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}({\rm X})
and
\mathscr{C}({\rm X})
are consistent.

The first and last points of
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}
are respectively
\alpha({\sf c}):=q_{0}
and
\omega({\sf c})=q_{N}.

The concatenation of
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon},{\sf c}^{\prime}=\{q_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=0}^{N^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
such that
\omega({\sf c})=\alpha({\sf c}^{\prime})
is defined as

(14)

{\sf c}\star{\sf c}^{\prime}=\{q_{0},\ldots,q_{N}=q_{0}^{\prime},q_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,q_{N^{\prime}}^{\prime}\}.

Notice that
{\sf c}\star{\sf c}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon\vee\varepsilon^{\prime}}
and
\alpha({\sf c}\star{\sf c}^{\prime})=\alpha({\sf c})
,
\omega({\sf c}\star{\sf c}^{\prime})=\omega({\sf c}^{\prime})
. The inverse of a chain
{\sf c}=\{q_{0},\ldots,q_{N}\}\in\mathscr{C}
is
-{\sf c}:=\{q_{N},\ldots,q_{0}\}
.
If
x,y\in{\rm X}
are two points then we set
\mathscr{C}_{x,y}:=\{{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}\,:\,\alpha({\sf c})=x,\omega({\sf c})=y\}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}_{x,y}:=\mathscr{C}_{x,y}\cap\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}
.

A metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is said to be
\varepsilon
-chain connected if
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}_{x,y}\neq\emptyset
for every
x,y\in{\rm X}
. A metric space can be decomposed in
\varepsilon
-chain connected components in the following way. Given two points
x,y\in{\rm X}
we say that
x\sim_{\varepsilon}y
if and only if
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}_{x,y}\neq\emptyset
. This defines an equivalence relation on
{\rm X}
. Such a relation partitions
{\rm X}
into a family of sets
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. If
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is separable, it can be readily checked that the set of indices
I
is countable. Moreover, every set
A_{i}
is
\varepsilon
-chain connected: it is called a
\varepsilon
-chain connected component of
{\rm X}
.
By definition, every
\varepsilon
-chain connected component is both open and closed.
Moreover, we have that

(15)

{\sf d}(A_{i},A_{j})\geq\varepsilon,\qquad\text{if }i\neq j.

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon
and let
{\rm X}=\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}A_{i}^{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}A_{i}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
, be the two decompositions where
\{A_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}
and
\{A_{i}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}
are respectively the
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-chain connected components of
{\rm X}
. Then, for every
i\in I_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
there exists
j\in I_{\varepsilon}
such that
A_{i}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\subseteq A_{j}^{\varepsilon}
. The set of
\varepsilon
-chain connected components of
{\rm X}
is denoted by
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}\textup{-cc}({\rm X})
.

Given
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}
and a function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
we define

(16)

\int_{{{\sf c}}}g:=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{g(q_{i})+g(q_{i+1})}{2}{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1}).

For every function
g
and every two chains
{\sf c},{\sf c}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
it holds

(17)

\int_{{\sf c}\star{\sf c}^{\prime}}g=\int_{\sf c}g+\int_{{\sf c}^{\prime}}g,\qquad\int_{-{\sf c}}g=\int_{\sf c}g.

Moreover the integral over a fixed chain
{\sf c}
is linear, i.e.
\int_{\sf c}ag+bh=a\int_{\sf c}g+b\int_{\sf c}h
for every
g,h\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
and every
a,b\geq 0
.

The length of a chain
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}
is
\ell({\sf c}):=\int_{{\sf c}}1=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})
.

Remark 2.1
.

There is not a canonical way to define the integral over a chain. Let
\lambda\in[0,1]
. If
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
we set
[a,b]_{\lambda}:=\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b
. Given
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}
, a function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we define the
\lambda
-integral of
g
over
{\sf c}
as

(18)

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.41553pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.2593pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}\int_{{{\sf c}}}g:=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}[g(q_{i}),g(q_{i+1})]_{\lambda}{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1}).

When
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
we recover the definition in (
16
), while for
\lambda=1
we find the expression used in
[
EB23
]
.
The
\lambda
-integral is linear in the sense above and it satisfies the first equality of (
17
). The second equality of (
17
) becomes

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.41553pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.2593pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}\int_{-{\sf c}}g=\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 11.92366pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1-\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.92366pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1-\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 12.1795pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1-\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.02327pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1-\lambda}}}}\int_{\sf c}g
for every
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
and every
{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}
. In the paper we will present the results for the
\frac{1}{2}
-integral, for simplicity, and we will briefly comment on how it works for different values of
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

2.4.
Convergence of chains to curves

We recall the notion of convergence of a sequence of chains to a curve defined in
[
EB23
]
. Given a chain
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
we define the set of interpolating times as
(t_{0},\ldots,t_{N})
by
t_{0}=0
and
t_{i}=\frac{\ell(\{q_{0},\ldots,q_{i}\})}{\ell({\sf c})}
. Then we define the function
\gamma_{{\sf c}}\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
piecewisely defined by
[t_{i},t_{i+1})\ni t\mapsto q_{i}
, for
i=0,\ldots,N
. We say that a sequence of chains
\{{\sf c}_{j}\}_{j}
converges to a curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
if
\{\gamma_{{\sf c}_{j}}\}_{j}
converges uniformly to
\gamma
as
j
goes to
+\infty
. We have the following compactness result for complete metric spaces.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
EB23
, Lemma 2.18]
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a complete metric space. Let
K_{j}\subseteq{\rm X}
be an increasing sequence of compact subsets of
{\rm X}
and let
h_{j}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\min\{j{\sf d}(x,K_{i}),1\}
. Let
M,L,\Delta>0
be constants. Let
{\sf c}_{j}=\{q_{0}^{j},\ldots,q_{N_{j}}^{j}\}\in\mathscr{C}^{\frac{1}{j}}({\rm X})
be chains such that:

(i)

\ell({\sf c}_{j})\leq L
for every
j
;

(ii)

{\rm Diam}({\sf c}_{j}):=\max\{{\sf d}(q,q^{\prime})\,:\,q,q^{\prime}\in{\sf c}_{j}\}\geq\Delta
for every
j
;

(iii)

\sum_{m=0}^{N_{j}-1}h_{j}(q_{m}^{j}){\sf d}(q_{m}^{j},q_{m+1}^{j})\leq M
for every
j
.

Then there exists a subsequence of
\{{\sf c}_{j}\}_{j}
that converges to a curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Condition (iii) in Proposition
2.2
is exactly
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 4.48613pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 4.48613pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.61693pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.4607pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{1}}}}\int_{{\sf c}_{j}}h_{j}\leq M
, for every
j
. The proof of
[
EB23
, Lemma 2.18]
can be straightforwardly modified replacing (iii) with

(iii)
λ

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.41553pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.2593pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}\int_{{\sf c}_{j}}h_{j}\leq M
for every
j
,

for every
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

The next goal is to compare the integral of a function along a sequence of chains
\{{\sf c}_{j}\}_{j}
converging to a curve
\gamma
with the integral of the same function on
\gamma
. To this aim, we need the following approximation of Lebesgue integral by Riemann sums. A classical reference is
[
Doo90
, Pag. 63]
, but we adopt a strategy very close to the proof in
[
CGP21
, Prop. 3.18]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f\colon[0,\ell]\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be an integrable function such that
f(0),f(\ell)<\infty
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[0,1]
we set

(19)

\displaystyle R_{t}(f,n):=\frac{f(0)+f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)}{2}\cdot\ell\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\frac{f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+i}{n}\right)\right)+f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+(i+1)}{n}\right)\right)}{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+n-1}{n}\right)\right)+f(\ell)}{2}\cdot\ell\left(\frac{1-t}{n}\right).

Then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left|R_{t}(f,n)-\int_{0}^{\ell}f(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0.

Remark 2.5
.

The quantity
R_{t}(f,n)
should be thought as a Riemann sum associated to the partition
0\leq\ell\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)<\ldots<\ell\left(\frac{t+n-1}{n}\right)\leq\ell
of
[0,\ell]
. The difference is in the average of
f
evaluated at two successive points of the partition instead of the value of
f
at every point of the partition. This is due to our definition of integral along chains.
The statement above implies that, up to subsequence,
R_{t}(f,n)\to\int_{0}^{\ell}f(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s
for a.e.
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

For an integrable function
f\colon[0,\ell]\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
we define the auxiliary quantity

(20)

R_{t}^{\prime}(f,n):=\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\frac{f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+i}{n}\right)\right)+f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+(i+1)}{n}\right)\right)}{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{n},

which is the middle term in the definition of
R_{t}(f,n)
. First of all we estimate

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|R_{t}(f,n)-R_{t}^{\prime}(f,n)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{f(0)+f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)}{2}\right|\ +\left|\frac{f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+n-1}{n}\right)\right)+f(\ell)}{2}\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell}{2n}\left(f(0)+f(\ell)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\ell}{n}}f(u)\,{\mathrm{d}}u+\int_{\ell\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)}^{\ell}f(u)\,{\mathrm{d}}u\right),

and the last two terms go to
0
as
n
goes to
\infty
, respectively because
f
assume finite values at
0
and
\ell
and dominated convergence. We now set

\displaystyle D(f,n):=\int_{0}^{1}\left|R_{t}^{\prime}(f,n)-\int_{0}^{\ell}f(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

If
f\in C^{0}([0,\ell])
we have

(22)

D(f,n)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\int_{\ell\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)}^{\ell\left(\frac{i+1}{n}\right)}\left|\frac{f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+i}{n}\right)\right)-f(s)}{2}\right|+\left|\frac{f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+i+1}{n}\right)\right)-f(s)}{2}\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathrm{d}}t\leq\ell\varepsilon.

for
n>n(f,\varepsilon)
, by uniform continuity of
f
on
[0,\ell]
.
Finally, given two Borel functions
f
and
f^{\prime}
, we estimate

(23)

\displaystyle|D(f,n)-D(f^{\prime},n)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|R_{t}^{\prime}(f,n)-R_{t}^{\prime}(f^{\prime},n)|\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{1}\left|\int_{0}^{\ell}(f-f^{\prime})(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\frac{(f-f^{\prime})\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+i}{n}\right)\right)+(f-f^{\prime})\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+(i+1)}{n}\right)\right)}{2}\cdot\frac{\ell}{n}\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,\ell)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\int_{0}^{1}|f-f^{\prime}|\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+i}{n}\right)\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\int_{0}^{1}|f-f^{\prime}|\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+i}{n}\right)\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle+\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,\ell)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\int_{\ell(\frac{i}{n})}^{\ell(\frac{i+1}{n})}|f-f^{\prime}|\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\int_{\ell(\frac{i+1}{n})}^{\ell(\frac{i+2}{n})}|f-f^{\prime}|\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm L}^{1}(0,\ell)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,\ell)}.

By approximating
f\in L^{1}([0,\ell])
in
L^{1}
-norm with a sequence
\{f_{j}\}\subseteq C^{0}([0,\ell])
, applying the triangular inequality, (
22
) and (
23
) we conclude that
\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}D(f,n)=0
. This, together with (
21
), proves the claim.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Given
\lambda\in[0,1]
, one can prove the equivalent of Proposition
2.4
for the approximation

(24)

\displaystyle R_{t}^{\lambda}(f,n):=\left[f(0),f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right)\right]_{\lambda}\cdot\ell\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\left[f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+i}{n}\right)\right),f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+(i+1)}{n}\right)\right)\right]_{\lambda}\cdot\frac{\ell}{n}

\displaystyle+\left[f\left(\ell\left(\frac{t+n-1}{n}\right)\right),f(\ell)\right]_{\lambda}\cdot\ell\left(\frac{1-t}{n}\right),

where
f\colon[0,\ell]\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is integrable and such that
f(0),f(\ell)<\infty
. When
\lambda=1
, this is exactly the statement of
[
Doo90
, Pag.63]
.

On one side we can adapt the proof of
[
EB23
, Lemma 2.19]
to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space.
Let
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
be a lower semicontinuous function. Let
g_{j}\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty)
be a sequence of continuous functions such that
g_{j}(x)\nearrow g(x)
for every
x\in{\rm X}
. Let
\{{\sf c}_{j}\}_{j}
be a sequence of chains with
\sup_{j}\ell({\sf c}_{j})<\infty
and converging to a curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to{\rm X}
. Then

\int_{\gamma}g\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leq\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{{\sf c}_{j}}g_{j}.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the one of
[
EB23
, Lemma 2.19]
, where he uses the notion of
\lambda
-integral along chains with parameter
\lambda=1
.
We just need to modify it for the
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
-integral as we did for the proof of Proposition
2.4
with respect to the original proof in
[
Doo90
]
.
∎

On the other hand we have the next result.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space, let
\gamma\colon[0,L]\to{\rm X}
be a curve parametrized by arc-length and let
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
be such that
g(\alpha(\gamma)),g(\omega(\gamma))<\infty
and
\int_{\gamma}g<+\infty
. For
t\in[0,1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

(25)

{\sf c}_{t,n}:=\left\{\gamma(0),\gamma\left(L\left(\frac{t}{n}\right)\right),\gamma\left(L\left(\frac{t+1}{n}\right)\right),\ldots,\gamma\left(L\left(\frac{t+n-1}{n}\right)\right),\gamma(L)\right\}\in\mathscr{C}^{\frac{L}{n}}.

Then, there exists
t\in[0,1]
and a subsequence
n_{j}
such that

\int_{\gamma}g\geq\varlimsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{{\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}}g.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
2.4
to the function
h=g\circ\gamma
, which is integrable by assumption and satisfies
h(0),h(L)<\infty
. In particular, there exists
t\in[0,1]
and a subsequence
n_{j}
such that

(26)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}R_{t}(h,n_{j})=\int_{0}^{L}h(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s

as noted in Remark
2.5
.
For every
j
we compute

(27)

\displaystyle R_{t}(h,n_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{h(0)+h\left(L\left(\frac{t}{n_{j}}\right)\right)}{2}\cdot L\left(\frac{t}{n_{j}}\right)+\sum_{i=0}^{n_{j}-2}\frac{h\left(L\left(\frac{t+i}{n_{j}}\right)\right)+h\left(L\left(\frac{t+(i+1)}{n_{j}}\right)\right)}{2}\cdot\frac{L}{n_{j}}

(28)

\displaystyle+\frac{h\left(L\left(\frac{t+n_{j}-1}{n_{j}}\right)\right)+h(L)}{2}\cdot L\left(\frac{1-t}{n_{j}}\right)

(29)

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\gamma(0))+g\left(\gamma\left(L\left(\frac{t}{n_{j}}\right)\right)\right)}{2}\cdot\ell\left(\gamma\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{\left[0,L\left(\frac{t}{n_{j}}\right)\right]}$}\right)

(30)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{n_{j}-2}\frac{g\left(\gamma\left(L\left(\frac{t+i}{n_{j}}\right)\right)\right)+g\left(\gamma\left(L\left(\frac{t+(i+1)}{n_{j}}\right)\right)\right)}{2}\cdot\ell\left(\gamma\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{\left[L\left(\frac{t+i}{n_{j}}\right),L\left(\frac{t+(i+1)}{n_{j}}\right)\right]}$}\right)

(31)

\displaystyle+\frac{g\left(\gamma\left(L\left(\frac{t+n_{j}-1}{n_{j}}\right)\right)\right)+g(\gamma(L))}{2}\cdot\ell\left(\gamma\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{\left[L\left(\frac{1-t}{n_{j}}\right),1\right]}$}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}}g

where we used in the last inequality that
{\sf d}(\gamma(a),\gamma(b))\leq\ell(\gamma\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{[a,b]}$})
for every
a,b\in[0,L]
.
∎

.

Combining Lemma

The proofs of Lemma

3.
Sobolev and
BV
spaces à la Cheeger and Ambrosio-Gigli-Savaré

In this section we recall the definitions of two functionals that have been used by Cheeger (
[
Che99
]
) and Ambrosio-Gigli-Savaré (
[
AGS13
,
AGS14
]
) to define Sobolev spaces, for
p>1
, and
BV
spaces, for
p=1
, via relaxation.

Let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Borel function. A function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
is an upper gradient of
u
, and we write
g\in{\rm UG}(u)
, if

(34)

|u(\omega(\gamma))-u(\alpha(\gamma))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g

for every rectifiable curve
\gamma
.

Cheeger considered the functional

(35)

{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}\colon\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})\to[0,+\infty],\quad u\mapsto\inf\left\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\in{\rm UG}(u)\right\},

with the usual convention that the infimum over an empty set is
+\infty
. The relaxation of
{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}
is then

(36)

\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)=\inf\left\{\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}\inf_{g\in{\rm UG}(u_{j})}\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,u_{j}\underset{L^{p}({\rm X})}{\longrightarrow}u\right\}.

We denote the associated Banach space defined as in (
10
) by
(H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})})
.

Remark 3.1
.

If
p>1
the space
(H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})})
is isometric to the
p
-Newtonian-Sobolev space, see
[
Sha00
, Theorem 4.10]
. Instead the space
(H^{1,1}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,1}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})})
can be used as a possible definition of the space of
BV
functions (equivalent to other ones in literature when
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete by
[
ADM14
]
), which generally strictly contains the
1
-Newtonian-Sobolev space.

Ambrosio, Gigli and Savaré defined the functional

(37)

{\rm F}_{{\rm AGS}}\colon\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})\to[0,+\infty],\quad u\mapsto\begin{cases}\|{\rm lip}\,u\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}&\text{ if }u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X});\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

The relaxation of
{\rm F}_{{\rm AGS}}
is then

(38)

\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm AGS}}(u)=\inf\left\{\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}\|{\rm lip}\,u_{j}\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,u_{j}\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X})\text{ and }u_{j}\underset{L^{p}({\rm X})}{\longrightarrow}u\right\}.

We denote the associated Banach space defined as in (
10
) by
(H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})})
.

It is known that if
u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
then
{\rm lip}\,u\in{\rm UG}(u)
(see for instance
[
HKST15
, Lemma 6.2.6]
). This gives immediately that
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm AGS}}(u)\geq\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
. When the metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete, the several proofs of density in energy of Lipschitz functions (see
[
AGS13
,
ADM14
,
EB23
,
LP24
]
) say that
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm AGS}}(u)=\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
. We summarize these well known results in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. Then
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
with
\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})}
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
. Moreover, if
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete then
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})=H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
with
\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})}=\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})}
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
.

The last part of the statement cannot hold without the completeness assumption. The motivation is the following: the space
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
does not change if we take the completion of
{\rm X}
, while
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
is not preserved.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
(\bar{{\rm X}},\bar{{\sf d}},\bar{\mathfrak{m}})
be its completion. Then the identity map
\iota\colon L^{p}({\rm X})\to L^{p}(\bar{{\rm X}})
induces an isometry between
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}(\bar{{\rm X}})
.

Proof.

Given
u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
, there exists a unique extension
\bar{u}\in{\rm Lip}(\bar{{\rm X}})
. For every
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in{\rm X}
, we have

\sup_{y\in(B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap{\rm X})\setminus\{x\}}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{{\sf d}(y,x)}=\sup_{y\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus\{x\}}\frac{|\bar{u}(y)-\bar{u}(x)|}{\bar{{\sf d}}(y,x)},

where the balls are in
(\bar{{\rm X}},\bar{{\sf d}})
. By denoting with a superscript the space in which the local Lipschitz constant is computed, we have that

{\rm lip}^{\rm X}u(x)={\rm lip}^{\bar{{\rm X}}}\bar{u}(x)

for every
x\in{\rm X}
. This in particular implies that

\|{\rm lip}^{\rm X}u\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}=\|{\rm lip}^{\bar{{\rm X}}}\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}(\bar{{\rm X}})}.

Moreover, if a sequence of functions
u_{j}\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
converges to
u
in
L^{p}({\rm X})
then the extensions
\bar{u}_{j}\in{\rm Lip}(\bar{{\rm X}})
converge to
\iota(u)
in
L^{p}(\bar{{\rm X}})
as well, since
\bar{\mathfrak{m}}
is concentrated on
{\rm X}
.
Thus
\iota(H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X}))\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}(\bar{{\rm X}})
and
\|\iota(u)\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}(\bar{{\rm X}})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})}
. On the other hand the operator
r\colon H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}(\bar{{\rm X}})\to H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
induced by the restriction from
\bar{{\rm X}}
to
{\rm X}
is linear,
1
-Lipschitz and satisfies
r\circ\iota=\iota\circ r=\text{id}
, thus concluding the proof.
∎

Example 3.4
.

In the simple example of
{\rm X}:=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
endowed with the Euclidean distance and the Lebesgue measure, we have
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})\neq H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
. Indeed from one side we have, by Proposition
3.3
, that
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})\cong H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}(\mathbb{R})
, and the latter is the classical Sobolev space on
\mathbb{R}
for
p>1
and the classical space of functions with bounded variations on
\mathbb{R}
for
p=1
, while
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})\cong L^{p}({\rm X})\cong L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
since there are no nonconstant curves in
{\rm X}
and so the constant function
0
is an upper gradient of every
L^{p}({\rm X})
function.

Remark 3.5
.

Example
3.4
shows that
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})\neq H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}(\bar{{\rm X}})
in general. The two spaces are the same if for instance the
p
-capacity of
\bar{{\rm X}}\setminus{\rm X}
, namely
{\rm Cap}_{p}(\bar{{\rm X}}\setminus{\rm X})
, is zero. For the definition of
p
-capacity we refer to
[
HKST15
, Chapter 7]
. The following is a (non-exhaustive) list of papers studying sufficient conditions that ensure that
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})=H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}(\bar{{\rm X}})
:
[
HKST15
,
Kos99
,
KST00
,
Lah23
]
. They are expressed in terms of capacity or porosity-type conditions.

4.
Chain upper gradients

The goal of the next sections is to recover a description of the space
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
in the sense of Proposition
3.2
, even when
{\rm X}
is not complete. This is possible if we replace upper gradients with chain upper gradients.

Let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon>0
. A Borel function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
is a
\varepsilon
-upper gradient of
u
, and we write
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
, if for all
{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}
it holds

(39)

|u(\omega({\sf c}))-u(\alpha({\sf c}))|\leq\int_{\mathsf{c}}g.

The definition of
\varepsilon
-upper gradient is very sensitive to the value of the function at every point. Sometimes it is preferable to impose some regularity on the function. With this in mind we consider the class of Lipschitz
\varepsilon
-upper gradients of
u
, namely
{\rm LUG}^{\varepsilon}(u):={\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\cap{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\varepsilon>0
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. A Borel function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
is a
(\varepsilon,\lambda)
-upper gradient of
u
, and we write
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u)
, if for all
{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}
it holds

(40)

u(\omega({\sf c}))-u(\alpha({\sf c}))\leq\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.41553pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.2593pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}\int_{\mathsf{c}}g.

In the symmetric case, i.e. when
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
, this is equivalent to (
39
). We also set
{\rm LUG}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u):={\rm UG}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u)\cap{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
.

Remark 4.2
.

The
\varepsilon
-upper gradient condition can be tested on nonconstant chains with two elements. Namely, a function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
is a
\varepsilon
-upper gradient of
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
if and only if for every chain
\{x,y\}
,
x,y\in{\rm X}
,
x\neq y
with
{\sf d}(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
, it holds that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\frac{g(x)+g(y)}{2}{\sf d}(x,y)=\int_{\{x,y\}}g.

One implication is obvious. For the other one we fix a chain
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
and we compute

|u(q_{N})-u(q_{0})|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}|u(q_{i})-u(q_{i+1})|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{\{q_{i},q_{i+1}\}}g=\int_{\sf c}g.

A similar conclusion holds for
(\varepsilon,\lambda)
-upper gradients, for every
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

The next lemma shows that the slope at level
\varepsilon
is always a
\varepsilon
-upper gradient. On the other hand, the local Lipschitz constant is smaller than every upper semicontinuous (in particular every Lipschitz)
\varepsilon
-upper gradient.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space and let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
. Then
{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}u\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, for every
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
it holds
{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}u(x)\leq\sup_{B_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x)}g
for every
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon
. Finally, if
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
is upper semicontinuous then
{\rm lip}\,u\leq g
.

Proof.

By Remark
4.2
it suffices to consider
{\sf c}=\{q_{0},q_{1}\}
. Then

(41)

\displaystyle|u(q_{1})-u(q_{0})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|u(q_{1})-u(q_{0})|}{{\sf d}(q_{0},q_{1})}+\frac{|u(q_{1})-u(q_{0})|}{{\sf d}(q_{0},q_{1})}\right){\sf d}(q_{0},q_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}u(q_{0})+{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}u(q_{1})}{2}{\sf d}(q_{0},q_{1})=\int_{\mathsf{c}}{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}u.

This proves the first part of the statement.

We move to the second part. Fix
x\in{\rm X}
and consider
y\in{\rm X}
such that
{\sf d}(x,y)\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
with
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon
. Since
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
and
\{x,y\}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}
, we have

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq{\sf d}(x,y)\,\frac{g(x)+g(y)}{2}\leq{\sf d}(x,y)\,\sup_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}g.

By taking the supremum over
y\in B_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x)
the second conclusion follows. Taking the limit as
\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0
and using the uppersemicontinuity of
g
, we conclude also the third part.
∎

On the other hand, every
\varepsilon
-upper gradient
g
satisfies the upper gradient inequality along every curve with endpoints in the set
\{g<\infty\}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space and let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
. If
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
for some
\varepsilon>0
, then

(42)

|u(\omega(\gamma))-u(\alpha(\gamma))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g

for every rectifiable curve
\gamma
such that
\omega(\gamma),\alpha(\gamma)\in\{g<+\infty\}
. In particular if
g
takes only finite values then
g\in{\rm UG}(u)
.

Remark 4.5
.

The inequality (
42
) does not hold in general for curves whose endpoints belong to the set
\{g=+\infty\}
.
Indeed let us consider
u=\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}
, i.e. the characteristic function of the set of rational numbers in
\mathbb{R}
. The function
g\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty]
,
g(x)=+\infty
if
x\in\mathbb{Q}
and
g(x)=0
otherwise, belongs to
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
for every
\varepsilon>0
. However it does not belong to
{\rm UG}(u)
since
\int_{\gamma}g=0
for every rectifiable curve
\gamma
of
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Assume by contradiction that there exists a rectifiable curve, that we can assume parametrized by arc length
\gamma\colon[0,L]\to{\rm X}
, with
L:=\ell(\gamma)
, such that

(43)

\int_{0}^{L}(g\circ\gamma)(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s=\int_{\gamma}g<|u(\omega(\gamma))-u(\alpha(\gamma))|<\infty

and
g(\alpha(\gamma)),g(\omega(\gamma))<\infty
.
By Proposition
2.8
we can find a subsequence
n_{j}
such that

\int_{\gamma}g\geq\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{{\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}}g.

This, together with (
43
), implies the existence of a
\frac{L}{n_{j}}
-chain
{\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}
with same endpoints of
\gamma
such that

\int_{{\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}}g<|u(\omega(\gamma))-u(\alpha(\gamma))|=|u(\alpha({\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}))-u(\omega({\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}))|.

This proves that
g\notin{\rm UG}^{\frac{L}{n_{j}}}(u)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The results of this section remain true if we consider the
\lambda
-integral and
(\varepsilon,\lambda)
-upper gradients, for every
\lambda\in[0,1]
. The proof of Proposition
4.4
follows by Remark
2.10
.

5.
p
-weak
\varepsilon
-upper gradients

In the classical theory of Sobolev spaces, one weakens the definition of upper gradients along curves by requiring that (
34
) holds for
\textup{Mod}_{p}
-almost every curve. The definition of the outer measure
\textup{Mod}_{p}
will be recalled in Section
7
. In this section we will give a similar definition for chain upper gradients and we will show similarities and differences with the classical setting of curves.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
p\geq 1
. The
(\varepsilon,p)
-modulus of a family of chains
{\sf C}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
is

{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}):=\inf\left\{\int\rho^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}:\,\rho\in{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}({{\sf C}})\right\}

where
{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}({{\sf C}})=\left\{\rho\geq 0\,:\,\rho\text{ Borel, }\int_{{\sf c}}\rho\geq 1\text{ for every }{\sf c}\in{{\sf C}}\cap\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}\right\}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
p\geq 1
. Then
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}
is an outer measure.

Proof.

The only non trivial property to be proven is
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}{\sf C}_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}_{i})
for
\{{\sf C}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
. We assume that the right hand side is finite, otherwise there is nothing to prove.
We fix
\delta>0
and we choose
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i}
such that
\sum_{i}\eta_{i}\leq\delta
.
We take
\rho_{i}\in{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}_{i})
such that

\int\rho_{i}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}_{i})+\eta_{i}
.
The function
\rho:=(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\rho_{i})^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
satisfies
\rho\in{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}{\sf C}_{i})
. Therefore

{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}{\sf C}_{i}\right)\leq\int\rho^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int\rho_{i}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}_{i})+\delta.

Taking the limit as
\delta
converges to
0
, we get the thesis.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The
(\varepsilon,p)
-modulus is concentrated on
\varepsilon
-chains in the following sense.
Let
{\sf C}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
and let
{\sf C}^{\varepsilon}:={\sf C}\cap\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}
. Then
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}\setminus{\sf C}^{\varepsilon})=0
since the function
\rho=0
belongs to
{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}({{\sf C}}\setminus{{\sf C}}^{\varepsilon})
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. Given a function
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
, we say that a Borel function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
is a
p
-weak
\varepsilon
-upper gradient of
u
and we write
g\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
if

|u(\omega({\sf c}))-u(\alpha({\sf c}))|\leq\int_{{\sf c}}g\qquad\text{ for }{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}\text{-a.e. chain.}

In particular if
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
then
g\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
for every
p\geq 1
.

Remark 5.3
.

For
\lambda\in[0,1]
one can define the
(\varepsilon,\lambda,p)
-modulus of a family of chains
{\sf C}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
by

{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}({\sf C}):=\inf\left\{\int\rho^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}:\,\rho\in{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}({{\sf C}})\right\}

where
{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}({{\sf C}})=\left\{\rho\geq 0\,:\,\rho\text{ Borel, }\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.41553pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.2593pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}\int_{{\sf c}}\rho\geq 1\text{ for every }{\sf c}\in{{\sf C}}\cap\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}\right\}
. This is still an outer measure which is concentrated on
\varepsilon
-chains. A Borel function
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
is a
p
-weak
(\varepsilon,\lambda)
-upper gradient of
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
if

u(\omega({\sf c}))-u(\alpha({\sf c}))\leq\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.24307pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.41553pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.2593pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\int}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\lambda}}}}\int_{{\sf c}}g\qquad\text{ for }{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}\text{-a.e. chain.}

The set of
p
-weak
(\varepsilon,\lambda)
-upper gradient of
u
is denoted by
{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u)
. It holds
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u)\subseteq{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u)
.

The set of
p
-integrable
p
-weak
\varepsilon
-upper gradients is closed under
L^{p}({\rm X})
-convergence. This can be seen as a consequence of an appropriate version of Fuglede’s Lemma in this context.

Proposition 5.4

(Fuglede’s lemma for chains)
.

Let
g_{j}
be a sequence of Borel functions that converges in
L^{p}({\rm X})
. Then there is a subsequence
g_{j_{k}}
with the following property: if
g
is any Borel representative of the
L^{p}({\rm X})
-limit of
g_{j}
then

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{{\sf c}}|g_{j_{k}}-g|=0

for
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}
-a.e. chain.

In the chain case this result is easier to prove and it is a consequence of the following easy but important fact.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
E\subseteq{\rm X}
,
\varepsilon>0
,
p\geq 1
. If
\mathfrak{m}(E)=0
then
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}(E))=0
.

Proof.

We write

\mathscr{C}(E)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{\sf C}_{k}
, where

{\sf C}_{k}:=\left\{{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}(E)\,:\,\min_{0\leq i<N}{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})\geq\frac{1}{k}\right\}.

By Proposition
5.1
,
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}
is an outer measure. So it is enough to prove that
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}_{k})=0
for every
k
. The function
\rho=2k\cdot\chi_{E}
belongs to Adm
{}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}_{k})
. Therefore
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C}_{k})\leq\int\rho^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}=(2k)^{p}\mathfrak{m}(E)=0
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The previous lemma differs to the classical case in which the modulus is defined in terms of rectifiable curves. Given a Borel set
E
with
\mathfrak{m}(E)=0
,
{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma(E))=0
if and only if
{\rm Cap}_{p}(E)=0
, see
[
HKST15
, Prop. 7.2.8]
, where
\Gamma(E)
denotes the family of curves intersecting
E
. In other words, given a
\mathfrak{m}
-null set with positive capacity, the
p
-modulus of the curves hitting this set is positive. This does not happen in the case of chains, as Lemma
5.5
shows.
On the other hand, the same proof of Lemma
5.5
shows in the case of curves that the
p
-modulus of the set of curves spending a positive time in
E
is zero, if
\mathfrak{m}(E)=0
. In this case
{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma(E))
is concentrated on the family of curves spending time zero in
E
(see also
[
HKST15
, Lemma 5.2.15]
).

Remark 5.7
.

Lemma
5.5
implies that if
g\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
and
h
is a function such that
\mathfrak{m}(\{g\neq h\})=0
, then
h\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
as well.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Since
g_{j}
converges to
g
in
L^{p}({\rm X})
then we can find a subsequence
g_{j_{k}}
which converges to
g
pointwise almost everywhere. This means that we can find a set
E\subseteq{\rm X}
with
\mathfrak{m}(E)=0
such that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}g_{j_{k}}(x)=g(x)
for every
x\in{\rm X}\setminus E
. Let us consider the set of chains
\mathscr{C}(E)
which has
(\varepsilon,p)
-modulus
0
by Lemma
5.5
. We claim that for every chain which is not in
\mathscr{C}(E)
we have
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\sf c}|g_{j_{k}}-g|=0
.
Let us fix a chain
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
which is not in
\mathscr{C}(E)
.
Then
q_{i}\notin E
for every
0\leq i\leq N
. In particular
\lim_{k\to+\infty}g_{j_{k}}(q_{i})=g(q_{i})
for
0\leq i\leq N
. Therefore

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\sf c}|g_{j_{k}}-g|=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{|g_{j_{k}}(q_{i})-g(q_{i})|+|g_{j_{k}}(q_{i+1})-g(q_{i+1})|}{2}{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})=0.

∎

Remark 5.8
.

Lemma
5.5
is true for
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}
, for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, with the same proof. An alternative argument is to observe that
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}
and
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon,\lambda^{\prime}}
are mutually absolutely continuous if
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in(0,1)
.
However, if
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
, Lemma
5.5
is no more true. Indeed, if
\mathbb{Q}
is the set of rational numbers in
\mathbb{R}
, then
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{Q}))=+\infty
for
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
. This follows from the fact that every admissible function has to be equal to
+\infty
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
. On the other hand, the same proof as above shows that given
E\subseteq{\rm X}
such that
\mathfrak{m}(E)=0
, then

{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon,1}(\{{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}\,:\,q_{i}\in E\text{ for some }i=0,\ldots,N-1\})=0

and

{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon,0}(\{{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}\,:\,q_{i}\in E\text{ for some }i=1,\ldots,N\})=0.

This is enough for adapting the proof of Proposition
5.4
to
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
.

As a consequence we prove that the set
{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)\cap L^{p}({\rm X})
is closed in
L^{p}({\rm X})
. Actually a stronger statement, that is the chain counterpart of
[
HKST15
, Proposition 6.3.30]
, holds. Notice that in
[
HKST15
, Proposition 6.3.30]
is required the convergence of
u_{j}
to
u

{\rm Cap}_{p}
-a.e.  while in our result it is enough to consider convergence
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
u_{j}\to u
pointwise
\mathfrak{m}
-almost everywhere, let
g_{j}\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u_{j})
and suppose
g_{j}\to g
in
L^{p}({\rm X})
. Then
g\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
.

Proof.

Let
E
be the set of points of
{\rm X}
where the convergence of
u_{j}
to
u
does not hold. By Proposition
5.4
we can extract a further subsequence, not relabeled, and a set of chains
{\sf C}
such that
\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\sf c}g_{j}=\int_{\sf c}g
for every
{\sf c}\in{\sf C}
and with
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}\setminus{\sf C})=0
. Since
g_{j}\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u_{j})
we can find set of chains
{{\sf C}}_{j}
such that
|u_{j}(\omega({\sf c}))-u_{j}(\alpha({\sf c}))|\leq\int_{\sf c}g_{j}
for every
{\sf c}\in{{\sf C}}_{j}
and such that
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}\setminus{{\sf C}}_{j})=0
. The set of chains
{{\sf C}}^{\prime}=\left({{\sf C}}\cap\bigcap_{j\in\mathbb{N}}{{\sf C}}_{j}\right)\setminus\mathscr{C}(E)
still satisfies
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}\setminus{\sf C}^{\prime})=0
, because of Proposition
5.1
, since
\mathscr{C}\setminus{{\sf C}}^{\prime}=\mathscr{C}(E)\cup(\mathscr{C}\setminus{{\sf C}})\cup\bigcup_{j}(\mathscr{C}\setminus{{\sf C}}_{j})
.

For every
{\sf c}\in{{\sf C}}^{\prime}
we have

|u(\omega({\sf c}))-u(\alpha({\sf c}))|=\lim_{j\to+\infty}|u_{j}(\omega({\sf c}))-u_{j}(\alpha({\sf c}))|\leq\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\sf c}g_{j}=\int_{\sf c}g.

This shows that
g\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

Proposition
5.9
remains true for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, while it is not clear if it holds for

. However it is still true, and the proof is the same, that

-topology.

As a consequence we can find a

\varepsilon

Proposition 5.11
.

The set
{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)\cap L^{p}({\rm X})
is a closed, convex subset of
L^{p}({\rm X})
. If not empty, it contains an element of minimal
L^{p}({\rm X})
-norm. If
p>1
such element is unique.

Proof.

We already showed in Proposition
5.9
that
{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
is closed, while its convexity is trivial. The existence of an element of minimal norm, i.e. the existence of a projection of
0\in L^{p}({\rm X})
on
{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
, follows directly. The uniqueness statement for
p>1
is a consequence of the strict convexity of the norm of
L^{p}({\rm X})
for
p>1
.
∎

On the other hand the minimal norm can be computed also using true
\varepsilon
-upper gradients because of the next result.

Proposition 5.12
.

The set
{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)\cap L^{p}({\rm X})
is the
L^{p}({\rm X})
-closure of
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.9
we know that
{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)\cap L^{p}({\rm X})
is closed in
L^{p}({\rm X})
and therefore it contains the
L^{p}({\rm X})
-closure of
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
. Let
g\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)
. Let
{\sf C}
be a family of chains such that
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}\setminus{\sf C})=0
and such that
|u(\omega({\sf c}))-u(\alpha({\sf c}))|\leq\int_{\sf c}g
for every
{\sf c}\in{\sf C}

such that
\int\rho_{j}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq 2^{-jp}

\rho=\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}\rho_{j}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\in\text{Adm}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}\setminus\sf C)

for every

converges to

.
∎

Example 5.13

(Non uniqueness of minimal weak chain upper gradient)
.

If

there are infinitely many elements of minimal norm in

.

We define the following two sequences of real numbers:
x_{n}=n
,

, for

.
Let
{\rm X}
be the countable set

{\rm X}

and

Hence, its

For every
\mu\in[0,1]
the function

defined as

2n(1-\mu)&x=y_{n}\text{ for }n\geq\varepsilon^{-1}\\

we have

\varepsilon
-chain upper gradients of minimal

-norm, by Proposition

.

6.
The chain Sobolev spaces

In this section we introduce two new functionals and we study their relaxations. The two functionals are

(45)

\displaystyle{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}\colon\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})\to[0,+\infty],

\displaystyle u\mapsto\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf\left\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\right\},

\displaystyle{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}\colon\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})\to[0,+\infty],

\displaystyle u\mapsto\begin{cases}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf\left\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\in{\rm LUG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\right\}&\text{if }u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the infimum of an empty set is
+\infty
.
By Proposition
5.12
,
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}
can be equivalently defined by
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf\left\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\in{\rm WUG}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(u)\right\}
. The limits in the definitions exist because the arguments are decreasing functions. Indeed if
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon
then every
\varepsilon
-upper gradient is also a
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-upper gradient. The two functionals satisfy properties (a),(b) and (c) of Section
2.1
. The less trivial property, which is (c), is consequence of the symmetric property in (
17
) that implies that
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)={\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(-u)
for every Borel function
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
.

The relaxations of the functionals above are denoted respectively by
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}
and
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}
. The associated Banach spaces are respectively
(H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})})
and
(H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})})
. Since
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)\geq{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u)
for every
u\in\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})
, we have that

H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X}),

and the inclusion is
1
-Lipschitz.

Remark 6.1
.

The domain of
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}
, namely the set of functions in
\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})
that admits an
L^{p}({\rm X})
-integrable
\varepsilon
-upper gradient for some
\varepsilon>0
, is clearly larger than the domain of
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}
, but it is also bigger than the set of functions (not necessarily Lipschitz) that admit
p
-integrable, Lipschitz,
\varepsilon
-upper gradients for some
\varepsilon>0
. Indeed, it contains functions that are highly non-regular, as the next example shows. Let
u=\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the characteristic function of the rational numbers on
\mathbb{R}
. It can be readily checked that
{\rm LUG}^{\varepsilon}(u)=\emptyset
, thus it does not admit a
p
-integrable, Lipschitz,
\varepsilon
-upper gradient for every
\varepsilon>0
, while
g\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]
,
g=\infty\cdot\chi_{\mathbb{Q}}
belongs to
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
. Since
\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=0
we have that
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u)=0
.

In order to familiarize with the definition of
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}
we compute explicitly this space in the case of a snowflake of a metric measure space. In view of Theorem
1.1
and the well known fact that there are no rectifiable curves in such spaces, hence
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})=L^{p}({\rm X})
, we know that it must hold that
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})=L^{p}({\rm X})
too.

Example 6.2

(Snowflaking of a metric space
({\rm X},{\sf d})
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d})
be a metric space and
0<\alpha<1
. We consider
({\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha})
, where
{\sf d}^{\alpha}(x,y):=({\sf d}(x,y))^{\alpha}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a Borel measure on
({\rm X},{\sf d})
(so a Borel measure on
({\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha})
too)
and consider
\varepsilon>0
. Notice that
{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}({\rm X},{\sf d})
if and only if
{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}({\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha})
.
We claim that, if
g
is a
\varepsilon
-upper gradient of
u
on
({\rm X},{\sf d})
, then
\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}g
is a
\varepsilon^{\alpha}
-upper gradient of
u
on
({\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha})
.
Indeed, for a chain
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
such that
{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})\leq\varepsilon
we have

(46)

\displaystyle|u(\omega({\sf c}))-u(\alpha({\sf c}))|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\frac{g(q_{i})+g(q_{i+1})}{2}\,{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})

(47)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{g(q_{i})+g(q_{i+1})}{2}\,{\sf d}^{\alpha}(q_{i},q_{i+1})\,{\sf d}^{1-\alpha}(q_{i},q_{i+1})

(48)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\frac{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}g(q_{i})+\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}g(q_{i+1})}{2}\,{\sf d}^{\alpha}(q_{i},q_{i+1}).

Therefore, for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
, we have

(49)

\displaystyle{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}^{{\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha},\mathfrak{m}}(u)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf\left\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\text{ is a }\varepsilon^{\alpha}\text{-upper gradient of }u\text{ in }({\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\inf\left\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\text{ is a }\varepsilon\text{-upper gradient of }u\text{ in }({\rm X},{\sf d})\right\}.

In particular, since every function
u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X},{\sf d})
with bounded support has a chain upper gradient, namely
{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}(u)
, which is in
L^{p}({\rm X})
, then
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}^{{\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha},\mathfrak{m}}(u)=0
for all such functions. Therefore
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}^{{\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha},\mathfrak{m}}(u)=0
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
since the class of Lipschitz functions (w.r.t.
{\sf d}
) with bounded support is dense in
L^{p}({\rm X})
. Thus
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X},{\sf d}^{\alpha},\mathfrak{m})=L^{p}({\rm X})
.

6.1.
Proof of the main results

The goal of this section is to compare the space
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})
with
H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm AGS}}({\rm X})
, with the help of
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space. Then

(50)

H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})

and

(51)

\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})}

for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
.

Proof.

Proposition
4.4
implies that
{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)\leq{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)
for every
u\in\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})
and this concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space such that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete. Then

(52)

H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})=H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})

and

(53)

\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})}=\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm curve}}({\rm X})}

for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
.

Remark 6.5
.

The proof of Theorem
6.4
can be done following word by word the proof of
[
EB23
, Theorem 1.1]
, with very few modifications, like the obvious one due to our definition of integral along chains, that requires Lemma
2.7
in place of
[
EB23
, Lemma 2.19]
. However, we are not able to use this scheme of demonstration in order to prove the next Theorem
6.7
. For this reason we propose a proof of Theorem
6.4
which is still inspired to the one of
[
EB23
, Theorem 1.1]
, but that can be easily modified to prove Theorem
6.7
. The main difference relies on the simplifications procedures: while we are able to reduce the proof to bounded functions with bounded support, in Theorem
6.7
we are not able to restrict the study to nonnegative functions. This is due to the fact that the
p
-minimal
\varepsilon
-weak upper gradients are not local in any suitable sense. In particular is not clear how to show that
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u)=\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u_{+})+\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u_{-})
, where
u_{+}
and
u_{-}
are the positive and negative part of
u
. Notice that, a posteriori, this has to be true because of Theorems
6.7
and
6.4
, since
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)=\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u_{+})+\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u_{-})
.

Proof of Theorem
6.4
.

For simplicity we divide the proof in steps.

Step 1.
It is enough to prove that for every bounded function
u
with bounded support it holds
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)\leq{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)
.
Indeed, by
[
HKST15
, Proposition 7.1.35]
, for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
such that
{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)<\infty
we can find a sequence of bounded functions
u_{j}
with bounded support such that
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{p}({\rm X})
and
\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u_{j})\leq{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)
. Therefore

\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)\leq\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u_{j})\leq\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u_{j})\leq{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u),

because of the lower semicontinuity of
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}
. Since
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}
is the biggest lower semicontinuous functional which is smaller than or equal to
{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}
, we infer that
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)\leq\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
such that
{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)<\infty
. The thesis for an arbitrary
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
follows directly by the definitions of
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)
and
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)
.

In the following we fix
x_{0}\in{\rm X}
and we assume that
u\colon{\rm X}\to[-M,M]
and that there exists
R\geq 3
such that
u\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{{\rm X}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}$}=0
.

Step 2.
We claim that it is enough to prove the following statement. For every upper gradient
g\in{\rm UG}(u)
and for every
\eta>0
there exists another upper gradient
g_{\eta}\in{\rm UG}(u)
such that

(54)

\|g-g_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}<\eta

and with the following property. For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
there exist functions
u_{\eta,j}\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g_{\eta,j}\in{\rm LUG}^{\frac{1}{j}}(u_{\eta,j})
such that

(55)

\varlimsup_{j\to+\infty}\|u_{\eta,j}-u\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\leq\eta\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{j\to+\infty}\|g_{\eta,j}-g_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}=0

Indeed, if the claim is true, then we have

(56)

\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u_{\eta,j})\leq\varliminf_{j\to+\infty}\|g_{\eta,j}\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}=\|g_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}+\eta

for every
\eta>0
, where we used (
54
) and (
55
). By a diagonal argument we deduce that
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)\leq\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}
. By the arbitrariness of
g\in{\rm UG}(u)
we infer that
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)\leq{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)
, which is the statement we had to prove from Step 1.

In the remaining steps we will prove the claim of Step 2. In order to simplify the proof we notice that it is enough to prove the statement for upper gradients
g\in{\rm UG}(u)
that are lower semicontinuous and such that
g\equiv 0
on
{\rm X}\setminus B_{2R}(x_{0})
. The first assertion follows by Vitali-Carathéodory Theorem (cp.
[
HKST15
, page 108]
), while the second one follows by truncation: for every
g\in{\rm UG}(u)
, the truncated function
g\cdot\chi_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}
is still an upper gradient of
u
since
u\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{{\rm X}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}$}=0
and it is smaller than the original one. In the sequel we assume that
g
has these properties.

Step 3:
For every
g
as above, we define
g_{\eta}
. By Lusin’s Theorem and the fact that
\mathfrak{m}
is Radon, we can find compact sets
K_{j}\subseteq B_{2R}(x_{0})
such that
\mathfrak{m}(B_{2R}(x_{0})\setminus K_{j})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2^{-j}\eta
and so that
u\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{K_{j}}$}
is continuous. We can suppose
K_{j}\subseteq K_{j+1}
for every
j
. Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
be so that
\mathfrak{m}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))^{\frac{1}{p}}\sigma\leq\frac{\eta}{2}
.
Define

(57)

g_{\eta}(x):=g(x)+\sigma\chi_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\chi_{B_{2R}(x_{0})\setminus K_{i}}(x).

The function
g_{\eta}
is still lower semicontinuous and belongs to
{\rm UG}(u)
, since it is bigger than
g
. Moreover,
g_{\eta}\equiv 0
on
{\rm X}\setminus B_{2R}(x_{0})
.
We show that (
54
) holds. Indeed

(58)

\|g-g_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\leq\sigma\mathfrak{m}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))^{\frac{1}{p}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{m}(B_{2R}(x_{0})\setminus K_{i})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\eta.

In the next step we define auxiliary functions
\hat{g}_{\eta,j}
. Later we will slightly modify these functions in order to define the
g_{\eta,j}
’s of Step 2.

Step 4.
We proceed to the definition of
\hat{g}_{\eta,j}
. Let
g_{j}
be an increasing sequence of bounded Lipschitz functions such that
g_{j}\nearrow g
pointwise, whose existence is guaranteed for instance by
[
HKST15
, Corollary 4.2.3]
. Define
\psi_{j}(x):=\max\{0,\min\{1,j(2R-{\sf d}(x_{0},x))\}\}
. Observe that
\psi_{j}
is
j
-Lipschitz, that
\psi_{j}\leq\chi_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}
, that
\psi_{j}\equiv 1
on
B_{2R-\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})
and that
\psi_{j}\to\chi_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}
pointwise as
j\to+\infty
.
We define

(59)

\hat{g}_{\eta,j}(x)=g_{j}(x)+\sigma\psi_{j}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\min\{j{\sf d}(x,K_{i}),\psi_{j}(x)\}.

By definition,
\hat{g}_{\eta,j}
is Lipschitz and bounded. Moreover,
\hat{g}_{\eta,j}\leq g_{\eta}
for every
j
and
\hat{g}_{\eta,j}\nearrow g_{\eta}
pointwise as
j\to+\infty
.

We define auxiliary functions
\hat{u}_{\eta,j}
. In Step 6 will modify them in order to define the functions
u_{\eta,j}
required by Step 2.

Step 5.
We define
\hat{u}_{\eta,j}
. We choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
so that
\mathfrak{m}(B_{2R}(x_{0})\setminus K_{N})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq(2M)^{-1}\eta
. We define the closed set

. Since
u\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{K_{N}}$}
and
u\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}$}

Theorem 6.7

Let

Before doing that we need a version of the Leibniz rule for chain upper gradients.

Proposition 6.8

(Leibniz rule)
.

Let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
be Borel and
\varphi\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
.
For every
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
, we have

(74)

|u|\,{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi+Q_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u\varphi),

where
Q_{\varepsilon}\varphi(x):=\sup_{y\in\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(x)}|\varphi|(y)
. In particular, for every
u\in\mathcal{L}^{p}({\rm X})
it holds that

(75)

{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u\varphi)\leq\left(\int|u|^{p}({\rm lip}\,\varphi)^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\rm X})}\,{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u).

Proof.

Let
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
. We verify (
74
). Given
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}
, we compute

(76)

\displaystyle|

\displaystyle(u\varphi)(\omega({\sf c}))-(u\varphi)(\alpha({\sf c}))|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}|(u\varphi)(q_{i+1})-(u\varphi)(q_{i})|

(77)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\bigg|u(q_{i+1})\varphi(q_{i+1})-\frac{1}{2}u(q_{i+1})\varphi(q_{i})+\frac{1}{2}u(q_{i+1})\varphi(q_{i})

(78)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{2}u(q_{i})\varphi(q_{i+1})+\frac{1}{2}u(q_{i})\varphi(q_{i+1})-u(q_{i})\varphi(q_{i})\bigg|

(79)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}|u(q_{i+1})|{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi(q_{i+1})+\frac{1}{2}|u(q_{i})|{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi(q_{i})\right)\,{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})+\frac{1}{2}|\varphi(q_{i+1})||u(q_{i+1})-u(q_{i})|

(80)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}|\varphi(q_{i})||u(q_{i+1})-u(q_{i})|

(81)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\sf c}}|u|\,{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{4}(|\varphi(q_{i})|+|\varphi(q_{i+1})|)(g(q_{i})+g(q_{i+1})){\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})

(82)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\sf c}}|u|\,{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{(Q_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,g)(q_{i})+(Q_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,g)(q_{i+1})}{2}{\sf d}(q_{i},q_{i+1})=\int_{{\sf c}}(|u|\,{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi+Q_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,g).

Now, to estimate
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(\varphi\,u)
, we compute

\displaystyle\inf\{\|h\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}:\,h\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u\varphi)\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\{\||u|\,{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi+Q_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\{\||u|\,{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}+\|Q_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}\,:\,g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int|u|^{p}\,{\rm sl}_{\varepsilon}\varphi^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\rm X})}\inf\{\|g\|_{L^{p}({\rm X})}:\,g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\}

where we used that
\|Q_{\varepsilon}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\rm X})}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\rm X})}
.
By taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
and using the fact that
\varphi
is Lipschitz, dominated convergence and the definition of
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(\cdot)
, we get the conclusion.
∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
(\bar{{\rm X}},\bar{{\sf d}},\bar{\mathfrak{m}})
be its completion. Then the identity map
\iota\colon L^{p}({\rm X})\to L^{p}(\bar{{\rm X}})
induces isometries between
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})

H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\bar{{\rm X}})
be any representative of
\iota(u)
.
Since
\varepsilon
-chains in
{\rm X}
are
\varepsilon
-chains in
\bar{{\rm X}}
, restrictions of elements in
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u_{0})
belong to
{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon}(u)
and the property of being Lipschitz is preserved. Thus,
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})\subseteq\iota(H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X}))
and
\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X})}\leq\|\iota(u)\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})}
for every

, and similarly
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})\subseteq\iota(H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X}))
and
\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})}\leq\|\iota(u)\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})}
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
.

For the other inequality we proceed in two different ways. If
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})\cap{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
and
g\in{\rm LUG}^{\varepsilon}(u)\cap L^{p}({\rm X})
, then we consider the Lipschitz extensions
\bar{u}
,
\bar{g}
of
u
and
g
on
\bar{X}
. We claim that
\bar{g}\in{\rm LUG}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\bar{u})\cap L^{p}({\rm X})
. Indeed, given a chain
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\bar{{\rm X}})

{\sf c}_{j}=\{q_{i}^{j}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}({\rm X})
such that

converges to
q_{i}
for every

. By continuity of
\bar{g}
and
\bar{u}

\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(\iota(u))=\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(\bar{u})\leq{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(u)
for every

. This is enough to conclude that
\iota(H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X}))\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})
and
\|\iota(u)\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}({\rm X})}
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
.

For the remaining inequality we recall that in the definition of
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(u)
the infimum of the

-norms can be taken among the

-weak
\varepsilon
-upper gradients of

. Moreover, since

, then

, where

\iota(H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}({\rm X}))\subseteq H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}(\bar{{\rm X}})
and

6.2.
Comments on the main results with the
\lambda
-integral

If one considers
(\varepsilon,\lambda)
-upper gradients instead of
\varepsilon
-upper gradients, for
\lambda\in[0,1]
, one defines natural variants of the functionals
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}
and
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}
, denoted by
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}^{\lambda}
and
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}^{\lambda}
. Their relaxations are
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}^{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}^{\lambda}
. Let us outline some differences.

For
\lambda\neq\frac{1}{2}
, the symmetric property in (
17
) does not hold, see Remark
2.1
. Therefore, it is not obvious that
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}^{\lambda}
and
{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}^{\lambda}
satisfy property (c) of Section
2.1
. This is due to the fact that it is not true in general that if
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(u)
then
g\in{\rm UG}^{\varepsilon,\lambda}(-u)
when
\lambda\neq\frac{1}{2}
. However the same proofs of Theorems
6.4
and
6.7
show that
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip}}}^{\lambda}(u)=\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm curve}}(u)=\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}^{\lambda}(u)
for every
u\in L^{p}({\rm X})
, if
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete. In particular, a posteriori,
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C}}}^{\lambda}
and
\tilde{\rm F}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},{\rm Lip}}}^{\lambda}
are seminorms, when
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete and the related Sobolev spaces are denoted by
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\,{\rm Lip},\lambda}}
and
H^{1,p}_{{\rm\mathscr{C},\lambda}}
. There are some subtleties to be taken into consideration.

First, the proof of Proposition
6.8
holds for every
\lambda\in[0,1]
under the additional assumption that
\varphi\geq 0

We recall the notion of the Poincaré inequality that we now discuss. Let

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left|u-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}u\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\leq Cr\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{\lambda r}(x)}g^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Proof.

Let us consider the first equivalence. The if implication is trivial by Lemma

7.2.
Keith’s characterization via chains

The Poincaré inequality with upper gradients can be characterized via modulus estimates, see
[
Kei03
, Theorem 2]
and
[
CC24a
, Proposition A.1]
. We will show a similar statement for chains. Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of Borel functions on
{\rm X}
.
Given a family of chains
{\sf C}
, the
(\varepsilon,p)
-modulus of
{\sf C}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
is defined as

{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{\varepsilon}^{p}({\sf C},\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{m}):=\inf\left\{\int\rho^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\,:\,\rho\in{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C})\cap\mathcal{F}\right\},

where we recall that

{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}({{\sf C}})=\left\{\rho\geq 0\,:\,\rho\text{ Borel, }\int_{{\sf c}}\rho\geq 1\text{ for every }{\sf c}\in{{\sf C}}\cap\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}\right\}.

If
\mathcal{F}
is closed under finite sums, the same proof of Proposition
5.1
shows that the assignment
{\sf C}\mapsto{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}({\sf C},\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{m})
satisfies

(101)

{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\sf C}_{i},\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{m}\right)\leq\sum_{i\in I}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}\left({\sf C}_{i},\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{m}\right)

for a finite set of indices
I
. In general it does not define an outer measure. Notice that
(0,+\infty)\ni\varepsilon\mapsto{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{\varepsilon}^{p}({\sf C},\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{m})\in[0,+\infty]
is non-decreasing.

We also recall the definition of the
p
-modulus of a family of curves. Let
\Gamma
be a family of curves and let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of Borel functions. Then

\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma,\mathcal{F},\mathfrak{m}):=\inf\left\{\int\rho^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\,:\,\rho\in{\rm Adm}(\Gamma)\cap\mathcal{F}\right\},

where
{\rm Adm}(\Gamma):=\{\rho\colon{\rm X}\to[0,+\infty]\,:\,\int_{\gamma}\rho\geq 1\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\}
. If
\mathcal{F}
is the class of all Borel functions, then we simply write
\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma,\mathfrak{m})
.

For the next result we define
\mathcal{F}_{x,y}:=\{g\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,g\text{ Borel and }g(x),g(y)<+\infty\}
, for
x,y\in{\rm X}
.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a doubling metric measure space such that
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is complete,
x,y\in{\rm X}
and
L\geq 1
. Then

\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},\mathcal{F}_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}).

Proof.

During this proof we use the notation
\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon,\Lambda}_{x,y}
to denote the family of chains
{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}_{x,y}
such that
\ell({\sf c})\leq\Lambda{\sf d}(x,y)
.
In the same way,
\Gamma_{x,y}^{\Lambda}
denotes the family of rectifiable curves with
\alpha(\gamma)=x
,
\omega(\gamma)=y
and
\ell(\gamma)\leq\Lambda{\sf d}(x,y)
.
We want to show

(102)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})\leq\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}).

We fix
\delta>0
. The same proof of
[
CC24b
, Lemma A.2]
, together with (
101
), shows that we can find
\Lambda\geq 1
such that
{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})\leq{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon,\Lambda}_{x,y},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})+\delta
.
We consider the compact family of curves
\Gamma_{x,y}^{\Lambda}
.
By
[
Kei03
, Proposition 6]
we have
\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y}^{\Lambda},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})=\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y}^{\Lambda},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})
.
Let
\rho\in{\rm Adm}(\Gamma_{x,y}^{\Lambda})\cap{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
.
We claim that

(103)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf_{{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon,\Lambda}_{x,y}}\int_{\sf c}\rho\geq 1.

Assuming the claim holds true, this implies that for every
\eta>0
there exists
\varepsilon_{\eta}>0
such that if
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{\eta}
then
(1+\eta)\rho\in{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon,\Lambda}_{x,y})
. Hence

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon,\Lambda}_{x,y},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})\leq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\int(1+\eta)\rho^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}=\int\rho^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}.

By the arbitrariness of
\rho
, we would get

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})\leq\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y}^{\Lambda},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})+\delta\leq\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})+\delta

By taking
\delta\to 0
we would conclude that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})\leq\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}).

We prove the claim. Suppose (
103
) is not true. Then there exists
\eta>0
and chains
{\sf c}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon,\Lambda}_{x,y}
such that
\int_{{\sf c}_{\varepsilon}}\rho<1-\eta
, for every
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. We are in position to apply Proposition
2.2
in order to find a curve
\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}^{\Lambda}
such that
{\sf c}_{\varepsilon}
subconverges to
\gamma
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. By Lemma
2.7
we have

\int_{\gamma}\rho\leq\varliminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\sf c}_{\varepsilon}}\rho<1-\eta,

which is a contradiction to the fact that
\rho\in{\rm Adm}(\Gamma_{x,y}^{\Lambda})
. This concludes the proof of the claim.

The inequality

{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},\mathcal{F}_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})\leq{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},{\rm Lip}({\rm X}),\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})

holds trivially for every
\varepsilon>0
. It remains to show that

(104)

\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},\mathcal{F}_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}).

Let
\rho\in{\rm Adm}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y})\cap\mathcal{F}_{x,y}
. We claim that
\rho\in{\rm Adm}(\Gamma_{x,y})
.
Let
\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}
. If
\int_{\gamma}\rho=+\infty
there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, applying Proposition
2.8
, we have that

\int_{\gamma}\rho\geq\varlimsup_{j\to+\infty}\int_{{\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}}\rho\geq 1,

for
{\sf c}_{t,n_{j}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\frac{1}{n_{j}}}_{x,y}
defined therein.
By the arbitrariness of
\rho
we have

\textup{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})\leq{\mathscr{C}}\text{-}{\rm Mod}_{p}^{\varepsilon}(\mathscr{C}_{x,y},\mathcal{F}_{x,y},\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}),

for every
\varepsilon>0
. By taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain (
104
) and we conclude the proof.

∎

7.3.
Energy of separating sets via chains

In the case
p=1
we can extend our characterizations in
[
CC24a
]
to possibly non complete metric spaces.
We need the notion of chain width of a given set
A\subset{\rm X}
, which is

(106)

\mathscr{C}\textup{-}0pt_{x,y}(A):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf_{{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}_{x,y}}\int_{{\sf c}}\chi_{A}.

We also need to recall the notion of separating set and of Minkowski content. Given
x,y\in{\rm X}
we say that a set
\Omega\subseteq{\rm X}
is separating if it is closed,
x
belongs to the interior of
\Omega
and
y
belongs to
\Omega^{c}
. The family of separating sets between
x
and
y
is denoted by
{\rm SS}_{\textup{top}}(x,y)
.

Given a subset
A
of a metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
, we define its Minkowski content by

\mathfrak{m}^{+}(A):=\varliminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(A)\setminus A)}{r}.

The following theorem is the chain version of
[
CC24a
, Theorem 1.4]
and it is suited for the case
p=1
.

Theorem 7.11
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a doubling metric measure space. Let
x,y\in{\rm X}
. Then the following conditions are quantitatively equivalent:

(i)

there exist
C>0
,
L\geq 1
such that (
92
) holds for every
u\in{\rm Lip}({\rm X})
;

(ii)

there exist
C>0
,
L\geq 1
such that for every
A\subseteq{\rm X}
it holds

(107)

\mathscr{C}\textup{-}0pt_{x,y}(A)\leq C\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}(A).

(iii)

there exist
c>0
,
L\geq 1
such that for every
\Omega\in{\rm SS}_{\textup{top}}(x,y)
it holds
(\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})^{+}(\Omega)\geq c
.

Proof.

If (i) holds then item (ii) of Proposition
7.6
holds. Applying it to the Borel function
g=\chi_{A}
, where
A\subseteq{\rm X}
is Borel, we get that

\mathscr{C}\textup{-}0pt_{x,y}(A)\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\sf c}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}_{x,y}\\
\ell({\sf c})\leq C{\sf d}(x,y)\end{subarray}}\int_{{\sf c}}\chi_{A}\leq C\int\chi_{A}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}=C\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}(A).

This shows (ii).

We now assume (ii) and we consider
\Omega\in{\rm SS}_{\rm top}(x,y)
. Let
0<r<\min\{{\sf d}(x,\partial\Omega),{\sf d}(y,\partial\Omega)\}
. For
\varepsilon<r
, let
{\sf c}=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\in\mathscr{C}^{\varepsilon}_{x,y}
and let
{\sf c}^{\prime}=(q_{m},\ldots,q_{M})
be a maximal subchain such that
q_{i}\in B_{r}(\Omega)\setminus\Omega
for every
i=m,\ldots,M
. Therefore we have

\int_{{\sf c}}\chi_{B_{r}(\Omega)\setminus\Omega}\geq\int_{{\sf c}^{\prime}}\chi_{B_{r}(\Omega)\setminus\Omega}\geq r-2\varepsilon,

by maximality of
{\sf c}^{\prime}
. By taking the limit for
\varepsilon
going to zero we find

(108)

\mathscr{C}\textup{-}0pt_{x,y}(B_{r}(\Omega)\setminus\Omega)\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}(r-2\varepsilon)=r.

Hence we compute

(\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})^{+}(\Omega)=\varliminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}(B_{r}(\Omega)\setminus\Omega)}{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:estimate_cwidth_below}}}{{\geq}}\varliminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}(B_{r}(\Omega)\setminus\Omega)}{\mathscr{C}\textup{-}0pt_{x,y}(B_{r}(\Omega)\setminus\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{C}.

This proves (iii).

It remains only to prove (iii) implies (i). This is the proof of the last implication in
[
CC24b
, Thm. 6.1]
that we report for completeness. Let
u\colon{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
,
u\geq 0
be a bounded Lipschitz function and let
x,y\in{\rm X}
. We can assume that
u(x)<u(y)
otherwise there is nothing to prove. The sets
\Omega_{t}:=\{u\geq t\}
belong to
{\rm SS}_{\text{top}}(x,y)
for all
t\in(u(x),u(y))
. So we can apply the coarea inequality for the Minkowski content (see
[
ADMG17
, Lemma 3.2]
) with respect to the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}
to get

c\,|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\int_{u(x)}^{u(y)}(\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L})^{+}(\{u\geq t\})\,{\mathrm{d}}t\leq\int_{\rm X}{\rm lip}\,u\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{x,y}^{L}.

Therefore item (i) follows with
C=1/c
for Lipschitz, nonnegative, bounded functions. A standard approximation argument gives the same estimate for all Lipschitz functions.
∎

Abstract
To study diffusion processes on the p-Wasserstein space 𝒫 p \mathscr{P}_{p} for p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) p\in[1,\infty) over a separable, reflexive Banach space X X , we present a criterion on the quasi-regularity of Dirichlet forms in L 2 ​ ( 𝒫 p , Λ ) L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda) for a reference probability Λ \Lambda on 𝒫 p \mathscr{P}_{p} . It is formulated in terms of an upper bound condition with the uniform norm of the intrinsic derivative. We find a versatile class of quasi-regular local Dirichlet forms on 𝒫 p \mathscr{P}_{p} by using images of Dirichlet forms on the tangent space L p ( X → X , μ 0 ) L^{p}(X\rightarrow X,\mu_{0}) at a reference point μ 0 ∈ 𝒫 p \mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{p} . The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck type Dirichlet form and process on 𝒫 2 \mathscr{P}_{2} are an important example in this class. We derive an L 2 L^{2} -estimate for the corresponding heat kernel and an integration by parts formula for the invariant measure.

Introduction

As a crucial topic in the crossed field of probability theory, optimal transport and partial differential equations, stochastic analysis on the Wasserstein space
has received much attention. Some measure-valued diffusion processes have been constructed by using the theory of Dirichlet forms, see
[
18
,
21
,
12
,
28
,
24
,
29
]
and references therein.
The pre-Dirichlet forms are defined by integrating a square field operator with respect to a reference Borel probability measure
\Lambda
on a topological space, whose points are measures over a Riemannian manifold, or
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The square field operators are determined by the intrinsic or extrinsic derivatives, which describe the stochastic motion and birth-death of particles respectively.
In order to establish the integration by parts formula ensuring the closability of the pre-Dirichlet form, the selection of reference measures
\Lambda
found in the literature are typically supported on the class of singular measures. Hence, these do not provide natural options when looking for a suitable substitute for a volume measure or a Gaussian measure on the set of probability measures.
On the other hand, for stochastic analysis on the Wasserstein space, it is essential to construct a diffusion process which plays a role of Brownian motion in finite-dimensions,
or the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (O-U for short) process on a separable Hilbert space.
This has been a long standing open problem due to the lack of a volume or Gaussian measure on such a state space, which could serve as an invariant measure.
As a solution to this problem,
[
23
]
presents a general technique to construct an abundance of Gaussian-like probability measures on
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
together with the related O-U type Dirichlet forms. The construction is very natural as the Gaussian measure and the related Dirichlet form are obtained as images of the corresponding objects from the tangent space
T_{\mu_{0},2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu_{0})
at a fixed element
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The results of this article ensure the existence of a versatile class of diffusion processes on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
,
p\in[1,2]
, whose Dirichlet forms are of gradient type.
The OU type process on
\mathscr{P}_{2}
is an important example. We derive an
L^{2}
-estimate for the corresponding heat kernel and an integration by parts formula for the Gaussian-like measure on
\mathscr{P}_{2}
.

The main idea behind the construction of an OU type process on
\mathscr{P}_{2}
is based on the following fact from the theory of optimal transport, which can be found in
[
31
]
or
[
4
]
, for example.
The set
\mathscr{P}_{2}
coincides with the image set of

\Psi:T_{\mu_{0},2}\ni h\mapsto\mu_{0}\circ h^{-1}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}.

The map
\Psi
is
1
-Lipschitz continuous with respect to
the
2
-Wasserstein distance

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}\,\text{\rm{d}}\pi(x,y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2},

where
\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of all couplings of
\mu
and
\nu
.
Now, let
G
be a non-degenerate Gaussian measure on the Hilbert space
T_{\mu_{0},2}
with trace-class
covariance operator
A^{-1}
, where
(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
is a positive definite self-adjoint operator in
T_{\mu_{0},2}
. The associated O-U process on
T_{\mu_{0},2}
is generated by

L^{\text{OU}}u(h):=\Delta u(h)-\langle A\nabla u(h),h\rangle_{T_{\mu_{0},2}},\quad h\in T_{\mu_{0},2},\,u\in\mathscr{D}(L^{\text{OU}})\subseteq L^{2}(T_{\mu_{0},2},G).

(1.1)

Here,
\nabla
and
\Delta
denote the gradient and Laplacian on
T_{\mu_{0},2}
respectively.
The O-U process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on the tangent space can be constructed as the mild solution of the corresponding semi-linear SPDE, i.e.

X_{t}=\text{\rm{e}}^{-At}X_{0}+\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-(t-s)A}\text{\rm{d}}W_{s},\ \ t\geq 0,

where
W_{t}
is the standard cylindrical Brownian motion on
T_{\mu_{0},2}
(see e.g.
[
10
, Chap. 6]
).
The associated O-U Dirichlet form
(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}))
is the closure of

\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}(f,g):=\int_{T_{\mu_{0},2}}\langle\nabla f(\phi),\nabla g(\phi)\rangle_{T_{\mu_{0},2}}\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi),\qquad f,g\in C_{b}^{1}(T_{\mu_{0},2}).

Now, under the map
\Psi
, the image of the Gaussian measure
G
gives a reference measure

N_{G}:=G\circ\Psi^{-1}

on
\mathscr{P}_{2}
, which is called
the Gaussian measure induced by
G
.
The
\Psi
-image
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
of
(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}))
is a symmetric conservative local Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{2},N_{G})

satisfying

\mathscr{E}(u,v)=\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}\langle Du(\mu),Dv(\mu)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)}\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu),\qquad u,v\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2}),

(1.2)

where
D
is the intrinsic derivative on
\mathscr{P}_{2}
, which is first introduced in
[
1
]

on the configuration space over Riemannian manifolds, and see
[
5
]
or Definition
2.2
below for the class
C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p}),p\geq 1.

The form
\mathscr{E}
in (
1.2
) has the same type as the O-U Dirichlet form on a Hilbert space. Moreover, as shown in
[
23
]
, it inherits several nice properties from
\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}
,
including the log-Sobolev inequality and compactness of its semigroup.
However, the question of quasi-regularity and hence the existence of a Markov process on
\mathscr{P}_{2}
associated with
\mathscr{E}
is still open up to now.

It is worth pointing out that the choice of
\mu_{0}
in this regard dose not matter.
Since the set

\big\{N_{G}=G\circ\Psi^{-1}:G\text{ is a Gaussian on }T_{\mu_{0},2}\big\}

is invariant under change of reference point
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
, as long as
\mu_{0}
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure (see Remark

below), the family of Gaussian-like measures and corresponding O-U type processes on
\mathscr{P}_{2}
do not depend on
\mu_{0}
.
The generator of
\mathscr{E}
can be formally represented as the intrinsic Laplacian with a drift (see
[
23
]
) and the reference measure
N_{G}
satisfies an integration by parts formula (see Sect
4.2
below) in
analogy to the corresponding formula for the Gaussian
G
.
So, we refer to
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
as O-U type Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{2},N_{G})
.

Since quasi-regularity is the key to construct Markov processes using Dirichlet forms, the existence of the corresponding OU type stochastic process is still an open problem.
As such a process is of wide interest, we prove a handy, general criterion (see Theorem
2.1
below) for the quasi-regularity of Dirichlet forms on the Wasserstein space over a Banach space
X
,
which in particular applies to the above example, but also in more general situations.
The framework in this article allows to construct diffusion processes on the

p
-Wasserstein space
\mathscr{P}_{p}
over a separable, reflexive Banach space for
p\in[1,\infty)
. Application to the O-U type process for
p=2
in case

is a Hilbert space serve as a typical and highly relevant example.
The proof of quasi-regularity is inspired by the methods developed in
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
.
The latter of these two only presumes a Polish state space.
Nevertheless, application of the techniques and verification of conditions require a detailed analysis which takes into account the special nature and properties of the metric respectively topology involved.
An adaptation to the
p
-Wasserstein distance, as realized in this article, is completely new.
For the configuration space equipped with the vague topology, a similar result has been achieved in
[
19
]
.
Regarding the weak topology on the set of Borel probability measures over a Polish space,
[
21
]
provides a quasi-regularity result. The latter, however, focuses on Dirichlet forms linked to the extrinsic derivative instead of the intrinsic. Our main results, Theorems
2.1
&
3.1
, relating to the intrinsic derivative and the
p
-Wasserstein distance,
show quasi-regularity for a wide class of Dirichlet forms with state space
\mathscr{P}_{p}
.
The methods of this survey should also be applicable for the Wasserstein space over non-linear metric spaces like Riemannian manifolds. To save space we leave this for a future study.

Throughout this text, let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a separable, reflexive Banach space and
\mathscr{P}
be the space of probability measures on
X
. For fixed
p\in[1,\infty)
, we consider the
p
-Wasserstein space

\mathscr{P}_{p}:=\big\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}:\ \mu(\|\cdot\|_{X}^{p})<\infty\big\}.

(1.3)

As stated in
[
31
, Thm. 6.18]
, the
p
-Wasserstein distance

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\bigg(\int_{X\times X}\|x-y\|_{X}^{p}\,\text{\rm{d}}\pi(x,y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{p},

(1.4)

yields a complete, separable metric on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
.
It is worth mentioning, that the metric space
(\mathscr{P}_{p},\mathbb{W}_{p})
is not locally compact, not even in case
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The subsequent list sums up our main results.

•

We provide a general quasi-regularity condition for a Dirichlet form

in

for a given reference probability
\Lambda
on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
:
If

has a dense subset of quasi-continuous functions and moreover for all differentiable functions

satisfies the inequality

\mathscr{E}(f,f)\leq C\sup_{\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}}\|Df(\mu)\|^{2}_{L^{p^{*}}(X\rightarrow X^{*},\mu)}

and

, then

is quasi-regular on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
.

•

Fixing

on

and quasi-regular

In case

is a diffusion form, then

is as well, i.e. a local Dirichlet form with square-field operator

where

is a field over

of bounded, symmetric operators,
and
\mathscr{E}
is associated to a conservative diffusion process on

.

•

For the choices

and

, we

and satisfies

The outline of this article is as follows:
In Section
2
, we address the property of quasi-regularity for Dirichlet forms in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda)
and develop mild criterion which involves the uniform norm of the intrinsic derivative. In Section
3

2
Quasi-regular Dirichlet forms on
\mathscr{P}_{p}

We first recall some notions on Dirichlet forms which can be found in
[
20
]
.

Let
(E,\rho)
be a Polish space and
\Lambda
be a probability measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{B}(E)
.
A Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
on
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
is a densely defined, closed bilinear form, which is Markovian, see for instance
[
20
, Chapt. I]
.
As a convention in this text, to have a simpler and more convenient notation, a measurable function
f
on
(E,\mathscr{B}(E))
is identified with its
\Lambda
-equivalence class of measurable functions.
For example, we write
f\in L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
or
f\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
if its class has the respective property.
In this article, only Dirichlet forms which are symmetric and conservative, i.e.
\boldsymbol{1}_{E}\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
with
\mathscr{E}(\boldsymbol{1}_{E},\boldsymbol{1}_{E})=0
, play a role.
So, by convention, if we refer to a generic Dirichlet form in an abstract context, then symmetry and conservativeness are always assumed.
We denote by
\Lambda(f)
the integral of a function
f
with respect to the measure
\Lambda
and set

\mathscr{E}_{1}(f,g):=\Lambda(fg)+\mathscr{E}(f,g),\quad f,g\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}).

For an open set
O\subseteq E
the
1
-Capacity associated to
\mathscr{E}
is defined as

{\rm Cap}_{1}(O):=\inf\big\{\mathscr{E}_{1}(f,f)\,:\,f\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}),\,f(z)\geq 1\text{ for }\Lambda\text{-a.e.~}z\in O\big\}

with the convention of
\inf(\emptyset):=\infty
. For an arbitrary set
A\subseteq E
, let

{\rm Cap}_{1}(A):=\inf\big\{{\rm Cap}_{1}(O)\,:\,A\subseteq O,\,O\,\text{is\ an open set in }E\big\}.

An
\mathscr{E}
-nest (or nest for short) is a sequence of closed subsets
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
E
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rm Cap}_{1}(E\setminus K_{n})=0.

A measurable function
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called quasi-continuous, if there exists a nest
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the restriction
f|_{K_{n}}
is continuous for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
A sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of measurable functions is said to converge quasi-uniformly to a function
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, if there exists a nest
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the sequence of restricted functions
f_{k}|_{K_{n}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, converge to
f|_{K_{n}}
uniformly on
K_{n}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If a property, which an element
z\in E
either has or doesn’t, holds for all
z
in the complement of a set
N\subseteq E
with
\textnormal{Cap}_{1}(N)=0
, then this property is said to hold quasi-everywhere (q.-e.) on
E
.

The following Definition is equivalent to
[
20
, Def. IV.3.1]
in the conservative case.

Definition 2.1
.

The Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
is called quasi-regular, if the following three conditions are met.

1)

There exists an
\mathscr{E}
-nest of compact sets (i.e.
\textnormal{Cap}_{1}(\,\cdot\,)
is tight).

2)

The Hilbert space
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
has a dense subspace consisting of quasi-continuous functions.

3)

There exists
N\subseteq E
with
\textnormal{Cap}_{1}(N)=0
and a
sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
of quasi-continuous functions which
separate points in
E\setminus N
, i.e. for any two different points
z_{1},z_{2}\in E\setminus N
there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{i}(z_{1})\neq f_{i}(z_{2})
.

2.1
A criterion of the quasi-regularity

From now on, we consider
(E,\rho)=(\mathscr{P}_{p},\mathbb{W}_{p})
given in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) for some
p\in[1,\infty)

(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
in

, we look at the class
C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
defined as follows by using the intrinsic derivative. This derivative is first introduced in
[
1
]
on the configuration space over a Riemannian manifold, and has been extended in
[
5
]
to

Let

be the dual space of
X
, i.e.
X^{*}
is the Banach space of bounded linear functionals
X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. We write

\phantom{}{}_{X^{*}}\langle x^{\prime},x\rangle_{X}:=x^{\prime}(x),\ \ x^{\prime}\in X^{*},\ x\in X.

Let
p^{*}=\frac{p}{p-1}
which is
\infty
if
p=1
. The tangent space of
\mathscr{P}_{p}
at a point
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
is
defined as

T_{\mu,p}:=L^{p}(X\rightarrow X,\mu).

By virtue of
[
13

, its dual space
T_{\mu,p}^{*}
can be identified with the space

L^{p^{*}}(X\rightarrow X^{*},\mu)
. Accordingly, we write

\phantom{}{}_{T_{\mu,p}^{*}}\langle\phi^{\prime},\phi\rangle_{T_{\mu,p}}:=\int_{X}\phantom{}{}_{X^{*}}\langle\phi^{\prime}(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{X}\,\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x),\ \ \phi^{\prime}\in T_{\mu,p}^{*},\ \phi\in T_{\mu,p}.

Let
id
denote the identity function
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f
be a continuous function on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
.

•

f
is called intrinsically differentiable,
if for every
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
,

T_{\mu,p}\ni\phi\mapsto D_{\phi}f(\mu):=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(\mu\circ(id+\varepsilon\phi)^{-1})-f(\mu)}{\varepsilon}

is a bounded linear functional, and the intrinsic derivative of
f
at
\mu
is defined as the unique element
Df(\mu)\in T_{\mu,p}^{*}
such that

D_{\phi}f(\mu)=\phantom{}_{T_{\mu,p}^{*}}\langle Df(\mu),\phi\rangle_{T_{\mu,p}}:={\int_{X}}\phantom{}_{X^{*}}\langle Df(\mu)(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{X}\,\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x),\ \ \phi\in T_{\mu,p}.

•

We denote
f\in C^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p}),
if
f
is intrinsically differentiable such that

\lim_{\|\phi\|_{T_{\mu,p}}\downarrow 0}\frac{|f(\mu\circ(id+\phi)^{-1})-f(\mu)-D_{\phi}f(\mu)|}{\|\phi\|_{T_{\mu,p}}}=0,\ \ \mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p},

and
Df(\mu)(x)
has a continuous version in
(\mu,x)\in\mathscr{P}_{p}\times X,
i.e. there exists a continuous map
g:\mathscr{P}_{p}\times X\rightarrow X^{*}

g(\mu,\,\cdot\,)
is a
\mu

Df(\mu)
for each
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
. In this case, we always take

to be its continuous version, which is unique.

•

We write
f\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathscr{P}_{p}),
if
f\in C^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
and
|f|+\|Df\|_{X^{*}}
is bounded on
\mathscr{P}_{p}\times X.

A typical subspace of
C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
is the class of cylindrical functions introduced as follows.
First, we recall the continuously differentiable cylindrical functions on the Banach space
X
:

\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(X):=\big\{g(x_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,x_{n}^{\prime}):\,\ n\in\mathbb{N},\,x_{i}^{\prime}\in X^{*},\,g\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big\},

where each
x_{i}^{\prime}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a bounded linear functional.
It is well known that each

\psi:=g(x_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,x_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(X)

, since

\nabla\psi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}g)\big(\phantom{}_{X^{*}}{\langle}x_{1}^{\prime},x{\rangle}_{X},\cdots,\phantom{}_{X^{*}}{\langle}x_{n}^{\prime},x{\rangle}_{X}\big)x_{i}^{\prime},\quad\ x\in X.

Next, we consider the space of continuously differentiable cylindrical functions:

\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}):=\big\{\mathscr{P}\ni\mu\mapsto g(\mu(\psi_{1}),\cdots,\mu(\psi_{n}))\,:\,\ n\in\mathbb{N},\,\psi_{i}\in\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(X),\,g\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big\}.

(2.1)

It is clear that for a function
f\in\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P})
with
f(\mu):=g(\mu(\psi_{1}),\cdots,\mu(\psi_{n}))
the map
\mathscr{P}_{p}\ni\mu\mapsto f(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}
, is an element of
C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
with

Df(\mu)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}g)(\mu(\psi_{1}),\cdots,\mu(\psi_{n}))\nabla\psi_{i}(x)\in X^{*},\quad(\mu,x)\in\mathscr{P}_{p}\times X.

(2.2)

In the following, we write
\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
for the restrictions of functions in
\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P})
to
\mathscr{P}_{p}
.
We will see that for any probability measure
\Lambda
on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
,
\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
is dense in

(Lemma

Theorem 2.1
.

A Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda)
is quasi-regular if it satisfies the following two conditions:

(C_{1})

\mathscr{F}C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
and there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)

(C_{2})

The Hilbert space

has a dense subspace consisting of quasi-continuous functions.

We would like to indicate that the conditions in Theorem

When the Dirichlet form

is constructed as the closure (i.e. smallest closed extension) of a bilinear form defined on

This together with (

,

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\sup_{\mu\in K_{n}^{(1)}}\mu\big(\|\,\cdot\,\|_{X}^{p}\boldsymbol{1}_{[R_{k},\infty)}(\|\,\cdot\,\|_{X}\big)\big)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\sup_{\mu\in K_{n}^{(1)}}\mu\big(\gamma_{k}(\|\,\cdot\,\|_{X})\big)=0.

Thus, (1) holds for
\mathscr{A}=K_{n}^{(1)}
as desired.

(b) Construction of
\{K_{n}^{(2)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.
Let

be a countable dense subset of

, and for each

, let

Again,

is the function from (
2.3
) with

. Noting that

decreases to

as

,
by Proposition
2.5
and
the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{E}(u_{k},u_{k})<\infty,\ \ \lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Lambda(|u_{k}|^{2})=0.

So, as shown above
[
20
, Lemma I.2.12]
and
[
20
, Proposition III.3.5]
imply the existence of a subsequence of

which converges to zero quasi-uniformly. The arguments, which include the strong convergence w.r.t.
\mathscr{E}_{1}^{1/2}
of the Césaro means for a suitable subsequence together with the fact that
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
is a decreasing in
k
, are completely analogous to step (a). Hence, there exists a nest
\{K_{n}^{(2)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for each

the following property holds:

\text{For }\varepsilon>0\text{ there exists }\{k_{m}\}_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\text{ with }\sup_{\mu\in K_{n}^{(2)}}u_{k_{m}}(\mu)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{m2^{m}},\ \ m\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.11)

satisfies property (2) for fixed

. Let

,
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary and

be chosen according to (

). Since for

and

it holds

\chi_{1}(s)\geq r\quad\text{if and only if}\quad s\geq r,

we estimate

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\Big\{x\in X\,:\,\min\big(\big\{\|\,x-y_{i}\|_{X}\,:\,i=1,\dots,k_{m}\big\}\geq\frac{1}{m}\Big\}\Big)

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\mu\Big(\Big\{x\in X\,:\,\chi_{1}\big(\min\big\{\|\,x-y_{i}\|_{X}\,:\,i=1,\dots,k_{m}\big\}\big)\geq\frac{1}{m}\Big\}\Big)

3
Quasi-regular image Dirichlet forms on
\mathscr{P}_{p}

In this section, we prove the quasi-regularity for image Dirichlet forms on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
under the map

\Psi:T_{\mu_{0},p}\ni\phi\mapsto\mu_{0}\circ\phi^{-1}\in\mathscr{P}_{p}

(3.1)

from the tangent space
T_{\mu_{0},p}
for a fixed element
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
.
To shorten notation, we set

T_{0}:=T_{\mu_{0},p}=L^{p}(X\rightarrow X,\mu_{0}),\ \ T_{0}^{*}:=T_{\mu_{0},p}^{*}=L^{p^{*}}(X\rightarrow X^{*},\mu_{0}).

The map
\Psi
is Lipschitz continuous, since

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{p}(\Psi(\phi_{1}),\Psi(\phi_{2}))^{p}\leq\int_{X\times X}\|x-y\|_{X}^{p}\,\text{\rm{d}}\pi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\|\phi_{1}(x)-\phi_{2}(x)\|_{X}^{p}\,\text{\rm{d}}\mu_{0}(x)=\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{T_{0}}^{p},\ \ \phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in T_{0}.

In the case where
X
is a separable Hilbert space and
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
is absolutely continuous with respect to a non-degenerate Gaussian measure,
the theory of optimal transport provides that

\Psi
is surjective and that for any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
, there exists a unique
\phi_{\mu}\in T_{0}
such that

\mu_{0}\circ\phi_{\mu}^{-1}=\mu\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{0},\mu)=\|\textnormal{id}-\phi_{\mu}\|_{T_{0}}

(see
[
4
, Theorem 6.2.10]
).
Here,
\phi_{\mu}
is called the optimal map from
\mu_{0}
to
\mu.

In particular, this holds if
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure.

Let
\Lambda_{0}
be a probability measure on
T_{0}
. Then the image measure

\Lambda:=\Lambda_{0}\circ\Psi^{-1}

is a probability measure on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
. The map

L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda)\ni u\mapsto u\circ\Psi\in L^{2}(T_{0},\Lambda_{0})

is isometric, i.e.

\Lambda(uv)=\Lambda_{0}\big((u\circ\Psi)(v\circ\Psi)\big),\ \ \ u,v\in L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda).

(3.2)

We would like to infer that, if
\Psi
is surjective, then choosing
\Lambda_{0}
such that

\Lambda_{0}(U)>0\quad\text{for any non-empty open set}\quad U\subseteq T_{0}

results in
\Lambda
bearing the analogous property, i.e. it takes a strictly positive value on each non-empty open set in
\mathscr{P}_{p}
. We refer to this property of
\Lambda
(resp.
\Lambda_{0}
) as full
topological support. An example for such a measure is found in Section
4.1
.
In general, since the topology of
\mathscr{P}_{p}
is second countable and in particular strongly Lindelöf, the support of a measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra is well-defined. For a measurable function
u:\mathscr{P}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
we set
{\rm supp}[u]:={\rm supp}[|u|\Lambda]
.

In the following, a class of quasi-regular Dirichlet forms in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda)
are constructed by using the image of Dirichlet forms in
L^{2}(T_{0},\Lambda)
. This provides quasi-regular local Dirichlet forms associated with diffusion processes on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
.

3.1
Main result

From here on, the Dirichlet forms we consider are assumed to be symmetric. Let

(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}))
be a symmetric Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(T_{0},\Lambda_{0})
, where
\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}})
contains the following defined class
C_{b}^{1}(T_{0})
of functions on
T_{0}
.

Definition 3.1
.

C_{b}^{1}(T_{0})
consists of all bounded Fréchet differentiable functions
f
on
T_{0}

with

T_{0}\ni\phi\mapsto\nabla f(\phi)\in L^{1}(X\rightarrow X^{*},\mu_{0})

continuous, and

\sup_{\phi\in T_{0}}\|\nabla f(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(X\rightarrow X^{*},\mu_{0})}<\infty.

Note that by the dominated convergence theorem, if
f\in C_{b}^{1}(T_{0})
then

T_{0}\ni\phi\mapsto\nabla f(\phi)\in L^{q}(X\rightarrow X^{*},\mu_{0})

is continuous for all
q\in[1,\infty).

The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda_{0}
be Borel probability measure on
T_{0}
, and let
(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}))
be a symmetric Dirichlet form
in
L^{2}(T_{0},\Lambda_{0})
such that
C_{b}^{1}(T_{0})\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}).

We have the following assertions.

(1)

It holds

C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})\circ\Psi:=\big\{u\circ\Psi\,:\,u\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})\big\}\subseteq C_{b}^{1}(T_{0}),

(3.3)

and the bilinear form

\mathscr{E}(u,v):=\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}(u\circ\Psi,v\circ\Psi),\ \ u,v\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})

is closable in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda)
, where
\Lambda:=\Lambda_{0}\circ\Psi^{-1}
. Its closure

(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
is a Dirichlet form satisfying

\begin{split}&\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\circ\Psi:=\big\{u\circ\Psi:\ u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\big\}\subseteq\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}),\\
&\mathscr{E}(u,v)=\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}(u\circ\Psi,v\circ\Psi),\ \ u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}).\end{split}

(3.4)

(2)

If the generator
(\widetilde{L},\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{L}))
of
(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}))
has purely discrete spectrum, let
\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be all eigenvalues of
-\widetilde{L}
listed in the increasing order with multiplicities.
Then the generator
(L,\mathscr{D}(L))
of
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
also has purely discrete spectrum, and eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of
-L
listed in the same order satisfies
\lambda_{n}\geq\sigma_{n},n\geq 1.

If moreover
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\sigma_{n}t}<\infty
for
t>0
, then the diffusion semigroup
P_{t}:=\text{\rm{e}}^{tL}
has heat kernel
p_{t}(\mu,\nu)
with respect to
\Lambda
satisfying

\int_{\mathscr{P}_{p}\times\mathscr{P}_{p}}p_{t}(\mu,\nu)^{2}\Lambda(\text{\rm{d}}\mu)\Lambda(\text{\rm{d}}\nu)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\lambda_{n}t}<\infty,\ \ t>0.

(3)

If there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}(f,f)\leq C\sup_{\phi\in T_{0}}\|\nabla f(\phi)\|_{T_{0}^{*}}^{2},\ \ f\in C_{b}^{1}(T_{0}),

(3.5)

then
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
is quasi-regular.

To prove (
3.3
), we need the following chain rule.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
u\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
, then

u\circ\Psi\in C_{b}^{1}(T_{0})
with

\nabla(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)=Du(\Psi(\phi))\circ\phi,\quad\phi\in T_{0}.

In particular,

\big\|\nabla(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)\big\|_{T_{0}^{*}}=\big\|Du(\Psi(\phi))\big\|_{T_{\Psi(\phi),p}^{*}},\quad\phi\in T_{0}.

Proof.

Let
\phi,\xi\in T_{0}
. On the probability space
(X,\mathscr{B}(X),\mu_{0})

[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
implies

\displaystyle(u\circ\Psi)(\phi+\xi)-(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}\varepsilon}(u\circ\Psi)(\phi+\varepsilon\xi)\,\text{\rm{d}}\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\phantom{}{}_{T_{0}^{*}}{\big\langle}Du(\Psi(\phi+\varepsilon\xi))\circ(\phi+\varepsilon\xi),\xi{\big\rangle}_{T_{0}}.

Combining this with the boundedness and continuity of
Df
on
\mathscr{P}_{p}\times X
, we may apply the dominated convergence theorem to deduce

\lim_{\|\xi\|_{T_{0}}\downarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{(u\circ\Psi)(\phi+\xi)-(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)-\phantom{}_{T_{0}^{*}}{\big\langle}Du(\Psi(\phi))\circ\phi,\xi{\big\rangle}_{T_{0}}}{\|\xi\|_{T_{0}}}\bigg|=0,

(3.6)

hence
u\circ\Psi
is Fréchet differentiable on
T_{0}
with derivative

\nabla(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)=Du(\Psi(\phi))\circ\phi\in T_{0}^{*}

(3.7)

satisfying

\|\nabla(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(X\rightarrow X^{*},\mu_{0})}\leq\|Du\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Finally, let
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n}\subseteq T_{0}
and
\phi\in T_{0}
such that as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|_{T_{0}}:=\bigg(\int_{X}\|\phi_{n}-\phi\|_{X}^{p}\text{\rm{d}}\mu_{0}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\rightarrow 0.

By the continuity of
\Psi:T_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{P}_{p}
, and the boundedness and continuity of
Du:\mathscr{P}_{p}\times X\rightarrow X^{*}
, we may apply the dominated convergence theorem to derive

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}\|Du(\Psi(\phi_{n}))(\phi_{n})-Du(\Psi(\phi))(\phi)\|_{X^{*}}\text{\rm{d}}\mu_{0}=0,

which
together with (
3.7
) yields the continuity of
T_{0}\ni\phi\mapsto\nabla u(\phi)\in L^{1}(X\rightarrow X^{*},\mu_{0})
.

∎

3.2
Local Dirichlet forms and diffusion processes

In classic theory, the Dirichlet form for a symmetric diffusion process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is of gradient type

\mathscr{E}(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle Q\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda

for a nice probability measure
\Lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a diffusion coefficient
Q=(q_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
.

In the following, we consider the case
X=H
for a separable Hilbert space
H
and develop an analogous concept for the state space
\mathscr{P}_{p}
,
p\in[1,2]
.
First, we fix a symmetric bounded linear operator
Q
on
L^{2}(T_{0}\rightarrow T_{\mu_{0},2},\Lambda_{0})
such that there is a decomposition
Q=\{Q_{\phi}\}_{\phi\in T_{0}}
with
Q_{\phi}:T_{\mu_{0},2}\rightarrow T_{\mu_{0},2}
and

QV(\phi):=Q_{\phi}(V(\phi))\quad\text{for}\quad V\in L^{2}(T_{0}\rightarrow T_{\mu_{0},2},\Lambda_{0}).

(3.8)

By Definition
3.1
we have
Q\nabla f\in L^{2}(T_{0}\rightarrow T_{\mu_{0},2},\Lambda_{0})
for
f\in C_{b}^{1}(T_{0})
.
We assume that

\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}(f,g)=\int_{T_{0}}\langle Q_{\phi}(\nabla f(\phi)),\nabla g(\phi)\rangle_{T_{\mu_{0},2}}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(\phi),\qquad f,g\in C_{b}^{1}(T_{0}),

(3.9)

is a well-defined and closable bilinear form in
L^{2}(T_{0},\Lambda_{0})
.
Let

\mathscr{E}(u,v):=\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}(u\circ\Psi,v\circ\Psi),\quad u,v\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p}).

(3.10)

Remark 3.3
.

The form in (
3.10
) is closable in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda)
and its closure
(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
is quasi-regular by Theorem
3.4
.
Moreover, we note that

L^{p^{*}}(H\rightarrow H,\mu)\subseteq L^{2}(H\rightarrow H,\mu)\subseteq L^{p}(H\rightarrow H,\mu),

i.e.
T_{\mu,p}^{*}\subseteq T_{\mu,2}\subseteq T_{\mu,p}
for
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
, since
p\in[1,2]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

If (
3.9
) is well-defined and closable in
L^{2}(T_{0},\Lambda_{0})
, then

(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
as defined in (
3.10
) and Remark
3.3
is a local, quasi-regular Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{p},\Lambda)
. Moreover, there are bounded symmetric operators
B_{\mu}:T_{\mu,2}\rightarrow T_{\mu,2}
,
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
,
such that the representation

\mathscr{E}(u,v)=\int_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}\Gamma(u,v)(\mu)\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)\quad\text{for }u,v\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})

holds with square-field operator

\Gamma(u,v)(\mu)=\langle Du(\mu),B_{\mu}(Dv(\mu))\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}.

(3.11)

Proof.

In part (a) we show the claimed representation for
\mathscr{E}
. In (b) the local property of
\mathscr{E}
is addressed.

(a) The direct integral
{\textstyle\int}^{\oplus}_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}T_{\mu,2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)
contains all
\Lambda
-classes of measurable vector fields
V(\mu)\in T_{\mu,2}
,
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
, such that

\int_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}\|V(\mu)\|_{T_{\mu},2}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)<\infty
(see
[
14
,
30
]
). The inner product is given by

\int_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}{\langle V(\mu),W(\mu)\rangle}_{T_{\mu},2}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)
for
V,W\in{\textstyle\int}^{\oplus}_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}T_{\mu,2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)
.
The pull-back operator

PV(\phi):=V(\Psi(\phi))\circ\phi,\quad\phi\in T_{0},

linearly maps an element
V\in{\textstyle\int}^{\oplus}_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}T_{\mu,2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)
to
L^{2}(T_{0}\rightarrow T_{\mu_{0},2},\Lambda_{0})
.
It has an adjoint operator
P^{*}:L^{2}(T_{0}\rightarrow T_{\mu_{0},2},\Lambda_{0})\rightarrow{\textstyle\int}^{\oplus}_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}T_{\mu,2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)

and we can define
B:=P^{*}QP
, a symmetric, bounded linear operator on
{\textstyle\int}^{\oplus}_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}T_{\mu,2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)
.
For
V,W\in{\textstyle\int}^{\oplus}_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}T_{\mu,2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)
and
\xi\in C_{b}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
, by definition

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}\langle B(\xi V)(\mu),W(\mu)\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu)=\int_{T_{0}}\langle QP(\xi V)(\phi),PW(\phi)\rangle_{T_{\mu_{0},2}}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}}\big\langle Q_{\phi}\big[\xi(\mu_{0}\circ\phi^{-1})V(\mu_{0}\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi\big],W(\mu_{0}\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi\big\rangle_{T_{\mu_{0},2}}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}}\xi(\mu_{0}\circ\phi^{-1})\big\langle Q_{\phi}\big[V(\mu_{0}\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi\big],W(\mu_{0}\circ\phi^{-1})\circ\phi\big\rangle_{T_{\mu_{0},2}}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}}\langle Q(PV)(\phi),P(\xi W)(\phi)\rangle_{T_{\mu_{0},2}}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{0}(\phi)=\int_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}\langle\xi(\mu)BV(\mu),W(\mu)\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu),

i.e.
B
commutes with multiplication by
\xi
.
Hence, there exists a decomposition
B=\{B_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}}
, where
B_{\mu}:T_{\mu,2}\rightarrow T_{\mu,2}
is a symmetric bounded operator (see
[
14
, Sect. II.2.5]
) and

BV(\mu)=B_{\mu}(V(\mu)),\qquad\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p},\,V\in{\textstyle\int}^{\oplus}_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}T_{\mu,2}\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda(\mu).

Using Lemma
3.2

,

for given

t>0
,

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}p_{t}^{n}(x,y)^{2}\text{\rm{d}}m_{n}(x)\text{\rm{d}}m_{n}(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-2k\alpha_{n}t}\\
&\leq 1+\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\alpha_{n}t}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\alpha_{n}ts}\text{\rm{d}}s\leq 1+\frac{2\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\alpha_{n}t}}{(2\alpha_{n}t)\land 1}.\end{split}

(4.2)

Noting that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{-1}<\infty
implies

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log\Big(1+\frac{2\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\alpha_{n}t}}{(2\alpha_{n}t)\land 1}\Big)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\alpha_{n}t}}{(2\alpha_{n}t)\land 1}<\infty,

we conclude that

p_{t}^{\infty}({\bf x},{\bf y}):=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{t}^{n}(x_{n},y_{n}),\ \ {\bf x}=(x_{n}),{\bf y}=(y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}

is a well defined measurable function in
L^{2}(m^{\infty}\times m^{\infty})
, where
m^{\infty}:=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}m_{n}
, and

\int p_{t}^{\infty}({\bf x},{\bf y})^{2}\text{\rm{d}}m^{\infty}({\bf x})\text{\rm{d}}m^{\infty}({\bf y})=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}p_{t}^{n}(x,y)^{2}\text{\rm{d}}m_{n}(x)\text{\rm{d}}m_{n}(y)\leq\tilde{\xi}_{t},

holds for

\tilde{\xi}_{t}:=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(1+\frac{2\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\alpha_{n}t}}{(2\alpha_{n}t)\land 1}\Big)<\infty,\ \ t>0.

Let
\widetilde{T}_{t}
be the O-U semigroup associated with
(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}))
. Then for every
t>0
,
\widetilde{T}_{t}
has the following density with respect to
G
:

\widetilde{p}_{t}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})=p_{t}^{\infty}({\bf x}(\phi),{\bf x}(\phi^{\prime})),\ \ {\bf x}(\phi):=(\langle\phi,\phi_{n}\rangle_{T_{0}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

so that by the spectral representation, see for instance
[
11
]
, the eigenvalues
\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
-\widetilde{L}
satisfies

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-2t\sigma_{n}}=\int_{T_{0}\times T_{0}}\widetilde{p}_{t}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})^{2}\,\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)\,\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi^{\prime})\leq\tilde{\xi}_{t}.

Then the desired assertion is implied by Theorem
3.1
(2).
∎

4.2
Partial integration for
N_{G}

We continue in the setting of Section
4.1
with
G
,
{\{\phi_{n}\}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
N_{G}
as above.
By Lemma
3.6
there exist
\phi_{\mu,n}
for
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
such that
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
,
G
-a.e.
\phi\in T_{0}
and
\xi\in T_{\Psi(\phi),2}
we have

\partial_{\phi_{n}}(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)=(D_{\phi_{\Psi(\phi)},n}u)(\Psi(\phi))

and

\langle\phi_{n},\xi\circ\phi\rangle_{T_{0}}=\langle\phi_{\Psi(\phi),n}\circ\phi,\xi\circ\phi\rangle_{T_{0}}.

We now state a partial integration formula for
N_{G}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

(i) For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
u,v\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
it holds

\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}u(\mu)v(\mu)\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)=-\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}u(\mu)\big(D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}v(\mu)-\alpha_{n}v(\mu)\langle\phi_{\mu,n},id\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\big)\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu).

(ii) Let
u\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
and
V(\mu)=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\xi_{n}(\mu)\phi_{\mu,n}
,
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
, for given
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
\xi_{n}\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
.
It holds

\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}\langle Du(\mu),V(\mu)\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)=-\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}u(\mu)\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{M}D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}\xi_{n}(\mu)-\alpha_{n}\langle\phi_{\mu,n},id\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\xi_{n}(\mu)\Big)\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu).

Proof.

(i) On the one hand, we have

\int_{T_{0}}\partial_{\phi_{n}}(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)(v\circ\Psi)(\phi)\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)=-\int_{T_{0}}(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)\big(\partial_{\phi_{n}}(v\circ\Psi)(\phi)-\alpha_{n}(v\circ\Psi)(\phi)\langle\phi_{n},\phi\rangle_{T_{0}}\big)\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)

(4.3)

by construction of
G
. On the other hand, by Lemma
3.6
it holds

\displaystyle\int_{T_{0}}\partial_{\phi_{n}}(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)(v\circ\Psi)(\phi)\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}}D_{\phi_{\Psi(\phi),n}}u(\Psi(\phi))v(\Psi(\phi))\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}u(\mu)v(\mu)\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)

and

\int_{T_{0}}(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)\big(\partial_{\phi_{n}}(v\circ\Psi)(\phi)-\alpha_{n}(v\circ\Psi)(\phi)\langle\phi_{n},\phi\rangle_{T_{0}}\big)\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)=\\
\int_{T_{0}}(u\circ\Psi)(\phi)\big(D_{\phi_{\Psi(\phi),n}}u(\Psi(\phi))-\alpha_{n}(v\circ\Psi)(\phi)\langle\phi_{\Psi(\phi),n},id\rangle_{\Psi(\phi)}\big)\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)\\
=\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}u(\mu)\big(D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}v(\mu)-\alpha_{n}v(\mu)\langle\phi_{\mu,n},id\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\big)\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu).

(4.4)

(ii) Again by Lemma
3.6
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}\langle Du(\mu),V(\mu)\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int_{T_{0}}\xi_{n}(\Psi(\phi))\langle Du(\Psi(\phi))\circ\phi,\phi_{\Psi(\phi),n}\circ\phi\rangle_{T_{0}}\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int_{T_{0}}\xi_{n}(\Psi(\phi))\langle Du(\Psi(\phi))\circ\phi,\phi_{n}\rangle_{T_{0}}\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi).

Applying (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}\langle Du(\mu),V(\mu)\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}u(\mu)\big(D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}\xi_{n}(\mu)-\alpha_{n}\xi_{n}(\mu)\langle\phi_{\mu,n},id\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\big)\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)

as claimed.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Let
u\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
. For
V\in{\textstyle\int}^{\oplus}_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}T_{\mu,2}\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)
we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}\langle D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}u(\mu)\phi_{\mu,n},V(\mu)\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{T_{0}}\langle\partial_{\phi_{n}}u(\Psi(\phi))\phi_{n},V(\Psi(\phi))\circ\phi\rangle_{T_{0}}\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)\\
=\int_{T_{0}}\langle\nabla(u\circ\Psi)(\phi),V(\Psi(\phi))\circ\phi\rangle_{T_{0}}\text{\rm{d}}G(\phi)=\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}\langle Du(\mu),V(\mu)\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\text{\rm{d}}N_{G}(\mu)

and hence

Du(\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}u(\mu)\phi_{\mu,n},\qquad N_{G}\textnormal{-a.e.~}\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We define
\partial_{n}u:\mu\mapsto D_{\phi_{\mu,n}}u(\mu)\in\mathbb{R}
for
u\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})

and if
\partial_{n}u\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2})
we set

L_{n}u:=\partial_{n}\partial_{n}u(\mu)-\alpha_{n}\langle\phi_{\mu,n},id\rangle_{T_{\mu,2}}\partial_{n}u(\mu).

As a consequence of Lemma
4.3
(ii), for such
u
, the operator
L_{n}
coincides with generator of the
n
-th component form, i.e. the closure of

\mathscr{E}^{n}(u,v):=\int_{\mathscr{P}_{2}}\partial_{n}u\partial_{n}v\text{\rm{d}}N_{G},\qquad u,v\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}),

in
L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{2},N_{G})
.
By the partial integration formula for
N_{G}
stated in Lemma
4.3
(ii)
and a straight-forward approximation, we can give an explicit expression for the generator

of the Markov semigroup considered in Corollary
4.2
,
on a certain set of functions.

Corollary 4.5

If

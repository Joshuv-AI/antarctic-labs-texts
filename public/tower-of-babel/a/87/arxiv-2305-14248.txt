Improved rates of convergence for the multivariate Central Limit Theorem in Wasserstein distance
Thomas Bonis

Abstract
We provide new bounds for the rate of convergence of the multivariate Central Limit Theorem in Wasserstein distances of order p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . In particular, we obtain what we conjecture to be the asymptotically optimal rate whenever the density of the summands admits a non-zero continuous component and has a non-zero third moment.



1
Introduction and main result

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. random variables drawn from a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and such that
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}X_{1}^{T}]=I_{d}
. By the Central Limit Theorem, we know that the measure
\mu_{n}
of
S_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
converges to the
d
-dimensional standard normal distribution
\gamma
. In this work, we wish to quantify this convergence for the family of Wasserstein distances of order
p\geq 2
, defined between any two measures
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

W_{p}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})^{p}=\inf_{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|y-x\|^{p}\,d\pi(x,y),

where
\pi
has marginals
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
and
\|\cdot\|
is the traditional Euclidean norm.

In recent years, multiple works provided non-asymptotic bounds for
W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)
. For instance as long as
\mathbb{E}[\|X_{1}\|^{4}]<\infty
, Theorem 1
[
2
]
gives

W_{2}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{d}\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}X_{1}^{T}\|X_{1}\|^{2}]\|_{HS}}{n}},

(1)

where
C>0
and
\|\cdot\|_{HS}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm. Similar results were also obtained for other
W_{p}
distances
[
2
,
5
]
. However, this bound is not sharp with respect to the dimension. Indeed, if
X_{1}
has i.i.d. components, (
1
) scales with
d^{3/4}
while an optimal bound would scale with
\sqrt{d}
.
Sharper bounds have been obtained under additional assumptions on the measure
\mu
. For instance, if
\mu
satisfies a Poincaré inequality with constant
K\geq 1
, Theorem 4.1
[
3
]
gives

W_{2}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\leq C\sqrt{\frac{(K-1)d}{n}}

(2)

and similar results have been obtained for any
W_{p}
distances with
p\geq 1
in Theorem 1.2
[
6
]
under the additional assumption that
\mu
is log-concave. As a consequence of (
2
), if
\mu
is log-concave then it admits a Poincaré constant
K\leq C\sqrt{\log d}
for some
C>0

[
8
]
and if the Kannan-Lovász-Simonovits isoperimetric conjecture is true then
K\leq C
. Finally, for uniformly log-concave measures, the optimal dependency on
\sqrt{d}
is obtained in Theorem 3.4
[
7
]
without any further assumptions.

Some insight on the conditions required to obtain this optimal dependency on the dimension in a more general case can be obtained from Proposition 1.2
[
13
]
which states that, if
X_{1}
takes value in the lattice
h\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
h>0
, then

\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{n}W_{2}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\geq\frac{\sqrt{d}h}{4}.

In particular, if
h
is of order
\sqrt{d}
then

\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{n}W_{2}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\geq Cd
. Therefore, if one wants
W_{2}(\mu_{n},\gamma)
to scale with the square root of the dimension, one would require
h
to be independent of
d
, or
X_{1}
to not be lattice-distributed. Such a result does not come as surprising in the light of known asymptotic results obtained in the univariate setting. Indeed, according to Theorem 1.2
[
12
]
, if
X_{1}
takes values in
\{a+kh\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
for some
a\in\mathbb{R},h>0
and has a finite moment of order
p+2
with
p\in]1,2]
, then

\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{n}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)=\frac{1}{6}\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{3}](Z^{2}-1)+hU\|_{p},

(3)

where
Z\sim\gamma,U
is a uniform random variable on
[-1/2,1/2]
independent of
Z
and
\|\cdot\|_{p}=\mathbb{E}[\|\cdot\|^{p}]^{1/p}
. On the other hand, as long as
X_{1}
is not lattice-distributed, one has

\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{n}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)=\frac{1}{6}\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{3}](Z^{2}-1)\|_{p}.

(4)

Furthermore, faster rates of convergence have been obtained for all
p\geq 1
whenever the first moments of
\mu
and
\gamma
are equal and
\mu
satisfies the Cramer’s condition
[
1
]
.

One can thus expect the rate of convergence for the central limit theorem in Wasserstein distance in a high-dimensional setting to not only be determined by the moments of
X_{1}
but to also depend on whether the measure is lattice-distributed. In other words, along with the large-scale behaviour of
\mu
, described by its moments, we expect a tight bound on
W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)
to include a term corresponding to the small-scale behaviour of
\mu
. In this work, we provide a first instance of such a result in the multidimensional setting. In particular, we obtain the following asymptotic bound.

Corollary 1
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. centered random variables drawn from a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with identity covariance matrix and finite moment of order
p+2
. Suppose there exists
h>0
such that the matrix

\mathbb{E}[(X_{2}-X_{1})(X_{2}-X_{1})^{T}1_{\|X_{2}-X_{1}\|\leq h}]

is positive-definite. Then, the measure
\mu_{n}
of
S_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
verifies

\sqrt{n}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{\otimes 3}](Z^{\otimes 2}-I_{d})\|_{p}+Cph\sqrt{d}+C_{d,p,\mu}\mathcal{O}\left(\log(n)^{-1/p}\right),

where
C>0
is a generic constant,
C_{d,p,\mu}
is a constant depending on
d,p
and
\mu
,
Z
is drawn from the
d
-dimensional standard normal distribution
\gamma
and
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{\otimes 3}](Z^{\otimes 2}-I_{d})
is a vector whose
i
-th coordinate is given by

(\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{\otimes 3}](Z^{\otimes 2}-I_{d}))_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{j,k}\mathbb{E}[(X_{1})_{i}(X_{1})_{j}(X_{1})_{k}](Z_{j}Z_{k}-1_{j=k}).

Furthermore, if
\mu
has a non-zero absolutely continuous component with respect to the Lebesgues measure then,

\sqrt{n}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{\otimes 3}](Z^{\otimes 2}-I_{d})\|_{p}+C_{d,p,\mu}o\left(1\right).

(5)

Whenever
\mu
admits a non-zero continuous component and has a non-zero third moment, we conjecture our result to be asymptotically optimal as it is a natural multidimensional generalization of (
4
). In particular, if
X_{1}
has i.i.d. components we recover the correct dependency on
\sqrt{d}
since, by Lemma
3
,

\sqrt{n}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[(X_{1})_{1}^{3}]\sqrt{(p-1)d}}{3\sqrt{2}}+C_{d,p,\mu}o\left(1\right).

Our bound is also asymptotically sharper than known existing bounds.
Indeed, using Lemma
3
and Lemma
6
, we obtain

\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{\otimes 3}](Z^{\otimes 2}-I_{d})\|_{p}\leq\sqrt{2(p-1)\sqrt{d}\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}X_{1}^{T}\|X_{1}\|^{2}]\|_{HS}},

thus recovering (
1
) in the asymptotic setting. In particular, this means that if
\|X_{1}\|\leq M
almost surely then, for any
p\geq 1
,

\sqrt{n}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\leq M\sqrt{\frac{(p-1)d}{18}}+C_{d,p,\mu}o\left(1\right).

Remark that this bound scales with at least
d
as
M
must be of order at least
\sqrt{d}
. On the other hand, if
\mu
admits a Stein kernel
\tau
as defined in
[
9
]
, combining Lemmas
3
and
7
gives

\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{\otimes 3}](Z^{\otimes 2}-I_{d})\|_{p}\leq 2\sqrt{2(p-1)\mathbb{E}[\|\tau-I_{d}\|^{2}]}.

Hence, following the work of
[
3
]
, if
\mu
admits a Poincaré constant
K\geq 1
we can generalize (
2
) to all
p\geq 1
:

\sqrt{n}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2(p-1)(K-1)d}}{3}+C_{d,p,\mu}o\left(1\right).

Let us note that, asymptotically, this bound depends only on
\sqrt{p-1}
, thus improving on the bound obtained in Theorem 1.2
[
6
]
which scales with
p^{2}
while lifting the requirement for
\mu
to be log-concave.

For lattice-distributed measures, our bound is close to matching a multidimensional equivalent of (
3
) but still requires improvements. However, obtaining the optimal rate of convergence for discrete but non lattice-distributed random variables is still an open issue. In any case, let us note that the remainder term is likely sub-optimal.

Corollary
1
is derived from a non-asymptotic bound obtained in Theorem
1
which also deals with non-identically distributed random variables.
Our result is derived through refinements on a variant of Stein’s method used in
[
2
]
which might be of interest in other contexts.

(x_{j})_{j\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{k}}
.

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

x^{\otimes k}

(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}

\forall j\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{k},(x^{\otimes k})_{j}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{j_{i}}.

x,y\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}

<x,y>
the Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product between
x
and

defined by

<x,y>=\sum_{i\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{k}}x_{i}y_{i},

x\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes(k+1)}

, let
xy
be the vector defined by

\forall i\in\{1,\dots,d\},(xy)_{i}=\sum_{j\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{k}}x_{i,j}y_{j}.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}

\phi
with partial derivatives of order
k
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\nabla^{k}\phi(x)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k}
the
k
-th gradient of
\phi
at
x
:

\forall j\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{k},(\nabla^{k}\phi(x))_{j}=\frac{\partial^{k}\phi}{\partial x_{j_{1}}\dots\partial x_{j_{k}}}(x).

k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
H_{k}
be the
d
-dimensional Hermite polynomial, defined by

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},H_{k}(x)=(-1)^{k}e^{\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2}}\nabla^{k}e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2}}.

X
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

, that is

3
Main Result

Let
n>0
and
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
be independent centered random variables on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
W=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
has identity covariance matrix and
\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|<1
. We denote by
\nu
the measure of
W
.
For any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, let
D_{i}=W^{\prime}_{i}-W_{i}
, where
W^{\prime}_{i}
is an independent copy of
W_{i}
.
Let us define a set of features describing the large-scale behaviour of the variables
(W_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
:

•

\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n\},\xi_{i}=-\log(\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|)
;

•

\forall q>0,L_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\|W_{i}\|^{q}]
;

•

\forall q>2,N_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{q}(1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\geq\xi_{i}\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|^{2/3}}+\xi_{i}^{-1})^{q/2-1}]
;

•

N^{\prime}_{4}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|W_{i}\|\|D_{i}\|]\|}{\sqrt{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\xi_{i}}}
.

Now, for any
\beta>0
, let
D_{i,\beta}=D_{i}1_{\|D_{i}\|\leq\beta}
. If
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i,\beta}^{\otimes 2}\right]
is positive-definite, we consider the following small-scale feature:

\forall q\geq 0,\beta_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i,\beta}^{\otimes 2}\right]^{-1}D_{i,\beta}\right\|^{q}\right].

Theorem 1
.

Let
p\geq 2
such that
p\leq\min_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|^{-1}
and suppose
L_{p+2}<\infty
. Let
\epsilon\coloneqq\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|^{2/3}
. If there exists
0<\beta<\sqrt{\epsilon}
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i,\beta}^{\otimes 2}\right]
is positive-definite, then, for any
q,r>p
such that
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}
, we have

W_{p}(\nu,\gamma)\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]H_{2}(Z)\|_{p}}{6}+C\left(\beta p\sqrt{d}+\frac{(2r-1)^{3/2}\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]\|W_{q}(\nu,\gamma)}{\sqrt{(p-1)\epsilon}}\right)\\
+Cp\left(\epsilon\left(\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{d})+p\left(\beta_{p}^{1/p}+L_{p}^{1/p}\right)\right)+\sqrt{N_{4}}+(pN_{p+2})^{1/p}+N^{\prime}_{4}\right).

In order to prove Corollary
1
from this result, we take

\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n\},W_{i}=\frac{X_{i}}{\sqrt{n}}

and
\beta=\frac{h}{\sqrt{n}}
and let us assume
n
is sufficiently large so that
\beta<\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{\otimes 2}]\|^{2/3}}{n^{2/3}}=\frac{d^{1/3}}{n^{2/3}}
. In the following, we denote by
C
a positive constant depending on properties of
\mu
but independent of
n
.
First, we have

\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{d})+p\left(\beta_{p}^{1/p}+L_{p}^{1/p}\right)\leq C

and, since
\epsilon=\frac{C}{n^{2/3}}
,

\epsilon\left(\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{d})+p\left(\beta_{p}^{1/p}+L_{p}^{1/p}\right)\right)\leq\frac{C}{n^{2/3}}.

Then,

N^{\prime}_{4}=\frac{C}{\sqrt{n\log(n)}}

and, since we have
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}n\epsilon\xi_{1}=+\infty
,

p\sqrt{N_{4}}+p^{1+1/p}N_{p+2}^{1/p}=o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\log(n)^{1/p}}\right).

Furthermore, since
X_{1}
has finite moment of order
p+2
, we can use Theorem 6 from
[
2
]
to obtain

W_{p+1/2}(\mu_{n},\gamma)\leq\frac{C}{n^{1/2-1/4p}}\leq\frac{C}{n^{3/8}}.

Thus, since
\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]\|=\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}
,

\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]\|W_{p+1/2}(\mu_{n},\gamma)}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\leq\frac{C}{n^{13/24}}.

which concludes the proof whenever
\mu
does not admit an absolutely continuous component with respect to the Lebesgues measure. If it does, let us denote by
\mu_{c}
this continuous component.
For any
h>0
, there must exist a ball
\mathcal{B}
with radius
h
and non-zero mass for
\mu_{c}
. Remark that

\int_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}(x^{\prime}-x)^{\otimes 2}d\mu_{c}(x)d\mu_{c}(x^{\prime})

must be positive definite. Otherwise the dimension of the support of
\mu_{c}
on this ball would be lower than
d
which is impossible since
\mu_{c}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgues measure. Thus,

\forall h,q>0,\beta_{q}(h)=\frac{1}{n^{q/2-1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[(X^{\prime}_{i}-X_{i})^{\otimes 2}1_{\|X^{\prime}_{i}-X_{i}\|\leq h}\right]^{-1}(X^{\prime}_{i}-X_{i})1_{\|X^{\prime}_{i}-X_{i}\|\leq h}\right\|^{q}\right]

must be finite.
Therefore, for
n
sufficiently large, we can take
h_{n}
as

h_{n}=\inf\left\{h\geq\frac{1}{n}:\sqrt{\beta_{2}(h)}+\beta_{p}(h)^{1/p}\leq n^{1/7}\right\}.

Then
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{n}=0
and

\epsilon\left(\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\beta_{2}(h_{n})}+\sqrt{d})+p\left(\beta_{p}(h_{n})^{1/p}+L_{p}^{1/p}\right)\right)=O\left(n^{1/7-2/3}\right)=o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

which yields the desired result.

Remark 1
.

Note that we restricted ourselves to the existence of a moment of order
p+2
for the summands to simplify computations. Let us note that one could only consider existence of a moment of order
p+l
with
l<2
only in order to obtain the rate
o\left(n^{-1/2+1/p-l/2p}\log(n)^{-l/2p}\right)
for the i.i.d. case which would slightly improve on Theorem 6
[
2
]
in which the rate
O\left(n^{-1/2+1/p-l/2p}\right)
was obtained.
Our approach would also be able to deal with varying moment assumption where each variable
W_{i}
admits a finite moment of order
p+l_{i}
for non identically distributed summands.

4
Diffusion interpolation approach

Let
p>0
and
W
be a random variable drawn from a measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In the following, we assume
\nu
admits a density
h
with respect to the Gaussian measure which is both bounded and with bounded gradient. These additional assumptions can later be lifted to obtain Theorem
1
using approximation arguments similar to those developed in Section 8
[
2
]
.

Let
t>0
and let us consider the random variable
F_{t}:=e^{-t}W+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}Z
, where
Z
is a random variable drawn from the
d
-dimensional standard Gaussian measure
\gamma
and independent of
W
. We denote by
\nu_{t}
the measure of
F_{t}
. Due to our assumptions on
h
,
\nu_{t}
admits a smooth density
h_{t}
with respect to
\gamma
. We can thus consider the score function of
F_{t}
defined by

\rho_{t}\coloneqq\nabla\log h_{t}(F_{t}).

Then, by Equation (3.8)
[
9
]
, we have

W_{p}(\nu,\nu_{t})\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\,dt

and, since
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{t}=\gamma
,

W_{p}(\nu,\gamma)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\,dt.

We are thus left with bounding
\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}
for all
t\geq 0
.

One can first remark that this score function verifies the following formula (see e.g. Lemma IV.1
[
10
]
):

\rho_{t}=e^{-t}\mathbb{E}\left[W-\frac{Z}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}\mid F_{t}\right]\text{ a.s.},

(6)

where
\Delta(t)\coloneqq e^{2t}-1
. A first, somewhat trivial, bound on
\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}
can then be obtained by applying Jensen’s and the triangular inequalities:

\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq e^{-t}\left(\|\mathbb{E}[W\mid F_{t}]\|_{p}+\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid F_{t}]\|_{p}}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}\right)\leq e^{-t}\left(\|W\|_{p}+\frac{\|Z\|_{p}}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}\right).

(7)

Note that this bound can still be nearly optimal for small values of
t
. Indeed, whenever
W
takes values in
h\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, one has, for small enough values of
t<<h
,

W_{2}(\nu,\nu_{t})\approx\|F_{t}-W\|_{2}=(1-e^{-t})\left\|W\right\|_{2}+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}\left\|Z\right\|_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-t}\left(\|W\|_{2}+\frac{\|Z\|_{2}}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}\right)\,dt.

However, for continuous measures
\nu
or for higher values of
t
, it is usually possible to obtain better bounds on
\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}
. For instance, (
1
) is obtained by combining (
7
) with another bound on
\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}
which holds for large values of
t
. A similar approach was used in
[
5
]
to provide quantitative results for normal approximation in various frameworks such as Wiener chaos or homogeneous sums. In this work, we refine this approach by using three different bounds: (
7
) for small values of
t
, a bound for medium values of
t
highlighting the small-scale behaviour of the measure
\nu
and a last bound for larger values of
t
which depends on the large-scale structure of
\nu
through its moments.

5
Bounding
\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}

5.1
Small times

Let
p\geq 2
and let
W=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
where the
(W_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
are centered and independent random variables on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite moment of order
p
. If
\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 2}]=I_{d}
, there exists
C>0
such that

\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\Psi_{1}(t)\coloneqq Ce^{-t}\left(\sqrt{dp}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}\right)+pL_{p}^{1/p}\right).

(8)

Indeed,
since the
(W_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
are independent and centered, we can use Rosenthal’s inequality (see Lemma
2
) to obtain

\|W\|_{p}\leq C\left(\sqrt{dp}+pL_{p}^{1/p}\right).

On the other hand, by Lemma
3
,

\|Z\|_{p}\leq\sqrt{d(p-1)}.

Injecting these bounds into (
7
) then yields (
8
).

5.2
Medium times

When looking at (
7
), we can see that, for small values of
t
, the main contributor of this bound is
\|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid F_{t}]\|_{p}/\sqrt{\Delta(t)}
. In the previous Section, we upper bounded this quantity somewhat crudely by using Jensen’s inequality. In this Section, we establish a sharper bound on

\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}
by proving a variant of Proposition 6.1
[
5
]
leveraging the small scale features of
W
. We start by covering the more general exchangeable pair framework, a standard framework for applying Stein’s method, before tackling the specific Central Limit Theorem case.

5.2.1
Exchangeable pairs framework

Proposition 1
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
(W,W^{\prime})
be a pair of random variables on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
(W,W^{\prime})
and
(W^{\prime},W)
follow the same law. For any
t\geq 0
, let
\eta_{p}(t)=\Delta(t)/(p-1)
and
D_{t}=(W^{\prime}-W)1_{\|W^{\prime}-W\|^{2}\leq\eta_{p}(t)}
.
For any
0<s<t
such that
\mathbb{E}[D_{s}^{\otimes 2}]
is positive-definite, we have

\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq e^{-t}\left(\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}+W\mid W]\|_{p}+\frac{C}{\sqrt{\eta_{p}(t)}}\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\mid W]-\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}]\|_{p}+\frac{C\sqrt{d}\eta_{p}(s)}{\eta_{p}(t)^{3/2}}\right),

where
C>0
is a generic constant,
\Lambda_{s}=\mathbb{E}[D_{s}^{\otimes 2}]^{-1}
and
\Gamma_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{s}D_{s}^{\otimes 2}
.

The proof of this result mostly follows the proof of Proposition 6.1
[
5
]
.

Proof.

Let
0<s<t
and let

\tau_{t}=\left(\Lambda_{s}D_{s}+\frac{\Gamma_{s}Z}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{(\Gamma_{s}\otimes D_{s}^{\otimes(k-1)})H_{k}(Z)}{\Delta(t)^{k/2}}\right),

with
a_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}-\frac{1}{4(k-2)!}
.
A small modification of Lemma 6.5
[
4
]
(see also the proof of Lemma
1
) gives

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{t}\mid F_{t}]=0.

Therefore,

\rho_{t}=\rho_{t}+e^{-t}\mathbb{E}[\tau_{t}\mid F_{t}]

and using (
6
) along with the triangle inequality yields

e^{t}\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\left\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}+W\mid F_{t}]\right\|_{p}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Gamma_{s}-I_{d}\right)Z\mid F_{t}\right]\right\|_{p}\\
+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{\Delta(t)^{k/2}}\left\|\mathbb{E}[(\Gamma_{s}\otimes D_{s}^{\otimes(k-1)})H_{k}(Z)\mid F_{t}]\right\|_{p}.

Then, since
Z
and
W
are independent, we have, by Jensen’s inequality,

e^{t}\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}+W\mid W]\|_{p}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}\left\|\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma_{s}\mid W\right]-I_{d}\right)Z\right\|_{p}\\
+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{\Delta(t)^{k/2}}\left\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\otimes D_{s}^{\otimes(k-1)}\mid W]H_{k}(Z)\right\|_{p}

and, by Lemma
3
,

e^{t}\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}+W\mid W]\|_{p}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta_{p}(t)}}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma_{s}\mid W\right]-I_{d}\right\|_{p}\\
+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}\sqrt{k!}}{\eta_{p}(t)^{k/2}}\left\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\otimes D_{s}^{\otimes(k-1)}\mid W]\right\|_{p}.

Since
\Gamma_{s}
is positive-definite, we have, for any
k\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\otimes D_{s}^{\otimes(k-1)}\mid W]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\|D_{s}\|^{k-1}\mid W]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{p}(s)^{(k-1)/2}\left\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\mid W]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{p}(s)^{(k-1)/2}\left(\left\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\mid W]-I_{d}\right\|_{p}+\|I_{d}\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{p}(s)^{(k-1)/2}\left(\left\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\mid W]-I_{d}\right\|_{p}+\sqrt{d}\right).

Thus, since
\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}a_{k}\sqrt{k!}<\infty
and
\eta_{p}(s)\leq\eta_{p}(t)
, we obtain that there exists
C>0
such that

e^{t}\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}+W\mid W]\|_{p}+\frac{C}{\sqrt{\eta_{p}(t)}}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma_{s}\mid W\right]-I_{d}\right\|_{p}+\frac{C\sqrt{d}\eta_{p}(s)}{\eta_{p}(t)^{3/2}}.

Finally, one can remark that, by definition of
\Gamma_{s}
,

\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}]=I_{d},

concluding the proof.
∎

5.2.2
Sum of independent variables

Proposition 2
.

Let
W=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
where the
(W_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
are independent random variables on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite moment of order
p\geq 2
. For any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\beta>0
, let
D_{i,\beta}=(W^{\prime}_{i}-W_{i})1_{\|D_{i}\|\leq\beta}
where
W^{\prime}_{i}
is an independent copy of
W_{i}
. Suppose there exists
\beta>0
such that

\Lambda_{\beta}^{-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[D_{i,\beta}^{\otimes 2}]

is positive-definite.
Then, for any
t
such that
\Delta(t)\geq(p-1)\beta^{2}
, there exists
C>0
such that

\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\Psi_{2}(t)\coloneqq C\left(\sqrt{p}\left(\sqrt{\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{L_{2}}\right)+p\left(\beta_{p}^{1/p}+L_{p}^{1/p}\right)+\frac{p^{3/2}\sqrt{d}\beta^{2}}{\Delta(t)^{3/2}}\right),

where

\forall q\geq 0,\beta_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}\right\|^{q}\right]

and

\forall q\geq 0,L_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|W_{i}\right\|^{q}\right].

In the following, we denote by
C
a generic positive constant.
Let
s
be such that
\Delta(s)=(p-1)\beta^{2}
and let
t>s
. Let
W^{\prime}=W+(W^{\prime}_{I}-W_{I})
where
I
is a uniform random variable on
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Since
(W,W^{\prime})
and
(W^{\prime},W)
follow the same law, we can apply Proposition
1
to obtain

e^{t}\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}\mid W]\|_{p}+\|W\|_{p}+\sqrt{\frac{p-1}{\Delta(t)}}\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\mid W]-\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}]\|_{p}+C\frac{(p-1)^{3/2}\sqrt{d}\beta^{2}}{\Delta(t)^{3/2}},

with
\Lambda_{s}=n\Lambda_{\beta}
.
First, following the proof of (
8
), we have

\|W\|_{p}\leq C\left(\sqrt{pL_{2}}+pL_{p}^{1/p}\right).

Then, by definition of
D_{s}
and since
I
is independent of
W
,

\mathbb{E}[D_{s}\mid W]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[D_{i,\beta}\mid W].

Hence,

\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}\mid W]\|_{p}=\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[D_{i,\beta}\mid W\right]\right\|_{p}

and, by Jensen’s inequality,

\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}\mid W]\|_{p}\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}\right\|_{p}.

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Since
W^{\prime}_{i}
and
W_{i}
are independent,
we have

\mathbb{E}[D_{i,\beta}]=0.

We can thus apply Rosenthal’s inequality (see Lemma
2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{s}D_{s}\mid W]\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+Cp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}\right\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sqrt{p\beta_{2}}+p\beta_{p}^{1/p}\right).

Similarly,

\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\mid W]-\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}]\|_{p}\leq C\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}^{\otimes 2}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
+Cp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}^{\otimes 2}\right\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

and, since
\|D_{i,\beta}\|\leq\beta\leq\sqrt{\Delta(t)/(p-1)}
, we can use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to obtain

\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}^{\otimes 2}\right\|\leq\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}\right\|\left\|D_{i,\beta}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{\Delta(t)}{p-1}}\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}\right\|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{p-1}{\Delta(t)}}\|\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}\mid W]-\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{s}]\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+Cp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\beta}D_{i,\beta}\right\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sqrt{p\beta_{2}}+p\beta_{p}^{1/p}\right)

which concludes the proof.

5.3
Large times

Finally, we are left with bounding
\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}
for ”large” values of
t
by using large-scale features of the
(W_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
. In practice, we improve on Proposition 6.1
[
5
]
. However, while this result was derived in the general exchangeable pairs framework, our improvements require dealing with sums of independent random variables and thus to the Central Limit Theorem case.

Proposition 3
.

Suppose
W=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
where the
(W_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
are centered independent random variables on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite moment of order
p+2
such that
\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 2}]=I_{d}
. There exists
C>0
such that for any
p<q\leq p+2
and
r
verifying
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}
and any
t
such that
\Delta(t)>(p-1)\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|
, we have

\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\Psi_{3}(t)\coloneqq\frac{e^{-3t}\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]H_{2}(Z)\|_{p}}{2}+\frac{C\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]\|W_{q}(\nu,\gamma)}{\eta_{2r}(t)^{3/2}}\\
+C\left(\sqrt{\frac{pN_{4}(t)}{\eta_{p}(t)}}+p\left(\frac{N_{p+2}(t)}{\eta_{p}(t)}\right)^{1/p}\right)+\frac{N^{\prime}_{4}(t)}{\eta_{p}(t)},

where

•

\eta_{p}(t)=\frac{\Delta(t)}{p-1}
;

•

\xi_{i}(t)=\log\left(\frac{\eta_{p}(t)}{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|}\right)
;

•

\forall q>2,N_{q}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{q}(1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\geq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}+\xi_{i}(t)^{-1})^{q/2-1}]}{\xi_{i}(t)}
;

•

N^{\prime}_{4}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|W_{i}\|\|D_{i}\|]\|}{\sqrt{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|}\xi_{i}(t)^{3/2}}
.

For any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and any
t>0
, let
D_{i,t}=D_{i}1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\leq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}
.
Let us first rewrite
\rho_{t}
with the help of the following result.

Lemma 1
.

For any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the quantity

\tau_{i,t}=\mathbb{E}\left[D_{i,t}\mid F_{t}\right]+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[(W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes D_{i,t}^{\otimes k})H_{k}(Z)\mid F_{t}]}{k!\Delta(t)^{k/2}}

verifies

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{i,t}\mid F_{t}]=0.

Proof.

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and let
\phi
be a smooth test function.
Since
\Phi:x\rightarrow\mathbb{E}[\phi(e^{-t}x+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}Z)]
is real analytic (see e.g. Lemma 1
[
2
]
or Lemma 6.4
[
5
]
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\phi(F_{t}+e^{-t}D_{i,t})1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\leq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-kt}}{k!}\mathbb{E}\left[W^{\prime}_{i}\left<D_{i,t}^{\otimes k},\nabla^{k}\phi(F_{t})\right>\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-kt}}{k!}\mathbb{E}\left[(W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes D_{i,t}^{\otimes k})\nabla^{k}\phi(F_{t})\right].

Thus, by performing multiple integrations by parts with respect to the Gaussian measure (see e.g. Equation (16)
[
2
]
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\phi(F_{t}+e^{-t}D_{i,t})1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\leq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[(W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes D_{i,t}^{\otimes k})H_{k}(Z)\phi(F_{t})\right]}{k!\Delta(t)^{k/2}}.

Finally, since
W_{i}
and
W^{\prime}_{i}
are independent and identically distributed, we have

\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\phi(F_{t}+e^{-t}D_{i,t})1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\leq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}]=\mathbb{E}[W_{i}\phi(F_{t})1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\leq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}]

concluding the proof.
∎

We are now ready to start the proof of Proposition
3
. Using Lemma
1
, we obtain

\rho_{t}=\rho_{t}+e^{-t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\tau_{i,t}\mid F_{t}].

Then, since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]=I_{d}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 3}]=\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]
, we can write

\rho_{t}=\frac{e^{-t}\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]\mathbb{E}[H_{2}(Z)\mid F_{t}]}{2\Delta(t)}+e^{-t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[W_{i}-\frac{\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]}{\sqrt{\Delta(t)}}Z-\frac{\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 3}]}{2\Delta(t)}H_{2}(Z)+\tau_{i,t}\mid F_{t}\right].

Thus, combining the triangle inequality, Jensen’s inequality and Lemma
3
, we obtain

\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\frac{e^{-t}|\|\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]H_{2}(Z)\mid F_{t}]\|_{p}}{2\Delta(t)}+e^{-t}A(t),

where

A(t)=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{k,i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right]^{1/p}

with

•

A_{0,i}=\mathbb{E}[D_{i,t}+W_{i}\mid W_{i}]
;

•

\forall k\in\{1,2\},A_{k,i}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes D_{i,t}^{\otimes k}\mid W_{i}]-\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes(k+1)}]}{\sqrt{k!}\eta_{p}(t)^{k/2}}
;

•

\forall k>2,A_{k,i}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes D_{i,t}^{\otimes k}\mid W_{i}]}{\sqrt{k!}\eta_{p}(t)^{k/2}}
.

First, by Lemmas
4
and
5
, we have

\frac{e^{-t}|\|\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]H_{2}(Z)\mid F_{t}]\|_{p}}{2\Delta(t)}\leq\frac{e^{-3t}}{2}\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]H_{2}(Z)\|_{p}+\frac{C\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]\|W_{q}(\nu,\gamma)}{\eta_{2r}(t)^{3/2}}

where
q>p
and
r
is such that

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}.

Let
\bar{D}_{i,t}=D_{i}1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\geq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}
.
In order to deal with
A(t)
, let us first remark that, since
\mathbb{E}[W_{i}]=0
and since
W^{\prime}_{i}
and
W_{i}
are independent, we have

A_{0,i}=\mathbb{E}[D_{i,t}+W_{i}\mid W_{i}]=\mathbb{E}[D_{i,t}-D_{i}\mid W_{i}]=-\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{i,t}\mid W_{i}].

And similarly,

A_{1,i}=-\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t}\mid W_{i}]}{\sqrt{\eta_{p}(t)}}.

Let us also note that

\mathbb{E}[A_{2,i}]=-\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t}^{\otimes 2}]}{2\eta_{p}(t)}.

Then, viewing
A(t)
as the
p
-norm of an infinite-dimensional vector, we can apply Rosenthal’s inequality (see Lemma
2
) to obtain

\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\mathbb{E}[A_{k,i}]\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|B_{k,i}\|^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}\\
+Cp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|B_{k,i}\|^{2}\right)^{p/2}\right]\right)^{1/p},

where

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq n,B_{k,i}=\begin{cases}A_{k,i}\text{ if $k\neq 2$}\\
\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes D_{i,t}^{\otimes 2}\mid W_{i}]}{\sqrt{2}\eta_{p}(t)}\text{ if $k=2$}.\end{cases}

Let us conclude the proof by bounding these quantities.

5.3.1
Bounding the first term

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
First, let us note that since
W_{i}
and
W^{\prime}_{i}
are independent,

\mathbb{E}[A_{0,i}]=\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{i,t}]=0.

On the other hand, since
\mathbb{E}[W_{i}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]=I_{d}
, we have
\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t}]=-\mathbb{E}[W_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t}]
. Hence,

\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t}]=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{i,t}^{\otimes 2}]}{2}

and

\|\mathbb{E}[A_{1,i}]\|^{2}=\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{i,t}^{\otimes 2}]\|^{2}}{4\eta_{p}(t)}\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|D_{i}\|^{2}]\|^{2}}{4\eta_{p}(t)^{3}\xi_{i}(t)^{2}}\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|D_{i}\|\|W_{i}\|]\|^{2}}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{3}}\xi_{i}(t).

On the other hand,

\|\mathbb{E}[A_{2,i}]\|^{2}\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{i,t}^{\otimes 2}\|W_{i}\|]\|^{2}}{\eta_{p}(t)^{2}}\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|D_{i}\|\|W_{i}\|]\|^{2}}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{3}}\frac{\xi_{i}(t)^{2}}{2}.

Finally, for any
k\geq 3
,

\|\mathbb{E}[A_{k,i}]\|^{2}\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i,t}^{\otimes 2}\|D_{i,t}\|^{k-2}\|W_{i}\|]\|^{2}}{k!\eta_{p}(t)^{k}}\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|D_{i}\|\|W_{i}\|]\|^{2}}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{3}}\frac{\xi_{i}(t)^{k}}{k!}.

Therefore, by definition of
\xi_{i}(t)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\mathbb{E}[A_{k,i}]\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|D_{i}\|\|W_{i}\|]\|^{2}}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\xi_{i}(t)^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|D_{i}\|\|W_{i}\|]\|^{2}}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{3}}e^{\xi_{i}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[D_{i}^{\otimes 2}\|D_{i}\|\|W_{i}\|]\|^{2}}{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\eta_{p}(t)^{2}\xi_{i}(t)^{3}}

and thus

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\mathbb{E}[A_{k,i}]\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{N^{\prime}_{4}(t)}{\eta_{p}(t)}.

5.3.2
Bounding the last two terms

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,n\},q\in[2,p]
.
First, by Jensen’ inequality and by definition of
\bar{D}_{i,t}
,

\|B_{0,i}\|^{2}\leq\|\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{i,t}\mid W_{i}]\|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{D}_{i,t}\|^{2}\mid W_{i}]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{D}_{i,t}\|^{2+4/q}\mid W_{i}]}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{2/q}}.

Let
W^{\prime\prime}_{i}
and
\bar{D}^{\prime}_{i,t}
be a conditionally independent copies of
W^{\prime}_{i}
and
\bar{D}_{i,t}
with respect to
W_{i}
. We have

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t}\mid W_{i}]\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\left<W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t},W^{\prime\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}^{\prime}_{i,t}\right>\mid W_{i}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\left<W^{\prime}_{i},W^{\prime\prime}_{i}\right>\left<\bar{D}_{i,t},\bar{D}^{\prime}_{i,t}\right>\mid W_{i}]

and, by Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t}\mid W_{i}]\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\left<W^{\prime}_{i},W^{\prime\prime}_{i}\right>^{2}\mid W_{i}]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}[\left<\bar{D}_{i,t},\bar{D}^{\prime}_{i,t}\right>^{2}\mid W_{i}]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\left<W_{i}^{\prime\otimes 2},W_{i}^{\prime\prime\otimes 2}\right>\mid W_{i}]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}[\left<\bar{D}_{i,t}^{\otimes 2},\bar{D}_{i,t}^{\prime\otimes 2}\right>\mid W_{i}]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\prime\otimes 2}\mid W_{i}]\|\|\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{i,t}^{\otimes 2}\mid W_{i}]\|.

Since
W^{\prime}_{i}
is independent of
W
,
\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\prime\otimes 2}\mid W_{i}]\|=\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\prime\otimes 2}]\|=\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|
. Thus,

\displaystyle\|B_{1,i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\bar{D}_{i,t}\mid W_{i}]\|^{2}}{\eta_{p}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{D}_{i,t}\|^{2}\mid W_{i}]}{\eta_{p}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{2+4/q}\mid W_{i}]}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{1+2/q}}\xi_{i}(t).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|B_{2,i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\prime}_{i}\otimes D_{i,t}^{\otimes 2}\mid W_{i}]\|^{2}}{2\eta_{p}(t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i,t}\|^{4}\mid W_{i}]}{2\eta_{p}(t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{2+4/q}\mid W_{i}]}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{1+2/q}}\frac{\xi^{2}_{i}(t)}{2}.

Finally, for any
k\geq 3
,

\|B_{k,i}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i,t}\|^{2k}\mid W_{i}]}{k!\eta_{p}(t)^{k}}\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{2+4/q}\mid W_{i}]}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{1+2/q}}\frac{\xi_{i}(t)^{k}}{k!}.

Combining both these bounds yields, by definition of
\xi_{i}(t)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|B_{k,i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{2+4/q}\mid W_{i}]}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{1+2/q}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\xi_{i}(t)}{k!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{2+4/q}\mid W_{i}]}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{1+2/q}}e^{\xi_{i}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{2+4/q}\mid W_{i}]}{\xi_{i}(t)^{1+2/q}\eta_{p}(t)^{2/q}}

Thus,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|B_{k,i}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{2+4/q}(1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\geq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}+\xi_{i}(t)^{-1})\mid W_{i}]}{(\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t))^{2/q}}

and, by Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|B_{k,i}\|^{2}\right)^{q/2}\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|D_{i}\|^{q+2}(1_{\|D_{i}\|^{2}\geq\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}+\xi_{i}(t)^{-1})^{q/2}]}{\eta_{p}(t)\xi_{i}(t)}.

Therefore,

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|B_{k,i}\|^{2}\right)^{q/2}\right]\right)^{1/q}\leq\left(\frac{N_{q+2}(t)}{\eta_{p}(t)}\right)^{1/q}.

5.4
Combining times

We are now ready to conclude the proof of Theorem
1
. Let
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
such that
\eta_{p}(\epsilon_{1})\coloneqq\frac{\Delta(\epsilon_{1})}{p-1}=\beta^{2}
and
\epsilon_{2}
be such that
\eta_{p}(\epsilon_{2})\coloneqq\frac{\Delta(\epsilon_{2})}{p-1}=\epsilon\coloneqq\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|^{2/3}
. Remark that, by assumption,
\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon_{2}
. In the following computations, we will rely on the fact that
\Delta(t)\geq 2t
. By (
8
) and Propositions
2
and
3
we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\rho_{t}\|_{p}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{1}}\Psi_{1}(t)\,dt+\int_{\epsilon_{1}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\Psi_{2}(t)\,dt+\int_{\epsilon_{2}}^{\infty}\Psi_{3}(t)\,dt.

First, since
\beta^{2}<\epsilon

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{1}}\Psi_{1}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\left(p\sqrt{\eta_{p}(\epsilon_{1})d}+p\eta_{p}(\epsilon_{1})\left(\sqrt{pd}+pL_{p}^{1/p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(p\beta\sqrt{d}+p\epsilon\left(\sqrt{pd}+pL_{p}^{1/p}\right)\right).

Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon_{1}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\Psi_{2}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq Cp\eta_{p}(\epsilon_{2})\left(\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{d})+p\left(\beta_{p}^{1/p}+L_{p}^{1/p}\right)\right)+\frac{Cp\beta^{2}\sqrt{d}}{\sqrt{\eta_{p}(\epsilon_{1})}}

\displaystyle\leq Cp\epsilon\left(\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{d})+p\left(\beta_{p}^{1/p}+L_{p}^{1/p}\right)\right)+Cp\beta\sqrt{d}.

Finally, let
t\geq\epsilon_{2}
. Since
\eta_{p}(t)\geq\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|^{2/3}
, we have, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\},\xi_{i}(t)\geq C\xi_{i}
. On the other hand, since
p\leq\min_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\|\mathbb{E}[W_{i}^{\otimes 2}]\|^{-1}
, we have
-\log(\epsilon_{2})\leq-\log(\eta_{p}(\epsilon_{2}))-\log(p-1)\leq C\xi_{i}
. Hence, since
N_{4}(.),N_{p+2}(.)
and
N^{\prime}_{4}(.)
are decreasing functions,

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon_{2}}^{\infty}\Psi_{3}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]H_{2}(Z)\|_{p}}{6}+\frac{C(2r-1)^{3/2}\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]\|W_{q}(\nu,\gamma)}{\sqrt{(p-1)\eta_{p}(\epsilon_{2})}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+Cp\left(\sqrt{N_{4}(\epsilon_{2})}+(pN_{p+2}(\epsilon_{2}))^{1/p}-N^{\prime}_{4}(\epsilon_{2})\log(\epsilon_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]H_{2}(Z)\|_{p}}{6}+\frac{C(2r-1)^{3/2}\|\mathbb{E}[W^{\otimes 3}]\|W_{q}(\nu,\gamma)}{\sqrt{(p-1)\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+Cp\left(\sqrt{N_{4}}+(pN_{p+2})^{1/p}+N^{\prime}_{4}\right).

which concludes the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 2

(Rosenthal inequality, Theorem 5.2
[
11
]
)
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for any
p\geq 2
and any independent random variables
(U_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
with finite moment of order
p
taking values in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we have

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}-\mathbb{E}[U_{i}]\right\|_{\mathcal{H},p}\leq C\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H},2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+Cp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H},p}^{p}\right)^{1/p},

where, for any random variable
X
taking values in
\mathcal{H}
and any
q>0
,

\|X\|_{\mathcal{H},q}=\mathbb{E}[\|X\|^{q}_{\mathcal{H}}].

Proof.

By Theorem 5.2
[
11
]
,

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}-\mathbb{E}[U_{i}]\right\|_{\mathcal{H},p}\leq C\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-\mathbb{E}[U_{i}]\|_{\mathcal{H},2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+Cp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-\mathbb{E}[U_{i}]\|_{\mathcal{H},p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Now for
q\in[2,p]
, combining the triangle and Jensen’s inequalities yields

\|U_{i}-\mathbb{E}[U_{i}]\|_{\mathcal{H},q}\leq\|U_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H},q}+\|\mathbb{E}[U_{i}]\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 2\|U_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H},q},

concluding the proof.

∎

Lemma 3

(Lemma 3

]
)
.

Let
Z

d
-dimensional standard normal random variable. For any
p\geq 2
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
M\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes k+1}

Lemma 4
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z
be three random variables on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
Z
is drawn from the Gaussian measure
\gamma
and is independent from
(X,Y)
. Let
q>p\geq 2
and suppose that
X
and
Y
have finite moment of order
q
. Then, for any
k\geq 0
and any
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}^{k}
,

\|\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid X+Z]-\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid Y+Z]\|_{p}\leq C\sqrt{(2r-1)^{k+1}(k+1)!}\|Y-X\|_{q},

where
C>0
is a generic constant,
H_{i}=(H_{k})_{i}
and
r
is such that
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon=Y-X
.
We have

\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid X+Z]=\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid Y+Z+\epsilon]

and

\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid Y+Z]-\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid X+Z]=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid Y+Z+t\epsilon]\,dt.

Let us denote the density of
Z
by
f_{\gamma}
and the measure of
(Y,\epsilon)
by
\mu
. For any
t\in[0,1]
, let

f(t)=\int(-1)^{k}\nabla_{i}f_{\gamma}\left(Z+Y-y^{\prime}+t(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})\right)d\mu(y^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}),

where
\nabla_{i}\cdot=(\nabla^{k}\cdot)_{i}
.
We then have

f^{\prime}(t)=\int\left<\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime},(-1)^{k}\nabla\nabla_{i}f_{\gamma}\left(Z+Y-y^{\prime}+t(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})\right)\right>d\mu(y^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}).

Similarly, letting

g(t)=\int f_{\gamma}\left(Z+Y-y^{\prime}+t(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})\right)d\mu(y^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}),

we have

g^{\prime}(t)=\int\left<\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime},\nabla f_{\gamma}\left(Z+Y-y^{\prime}+t(\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})\right)\right>d\mu(y^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime}).

By definition of the conditional expectation,

•

\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}=\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid Y+Z+t\epsilon]
;

•

\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}=\mathbb{E}[\left<\epsilon,Z\right>\mid Y+Z+t\epsilon]-\left<\epsilon,\mathbb{E}[Z\mid Y+Z+t\epsilon]\right>
and

•

\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}=\mathbb{E}[\left<\epsilon,H_{i+1}(Z)\right>\mid Y+Z+t\epsilon]-\left<\epsilon,\mathbb{E}[H_{i+1}(Z)\mid Y+Z+t\epsilon]\right>
,

where
H_{i+1}(x)=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\nabla\nabla_{i}f_{\gamma}(x)}{f_{\gamma}(x)}
.
Therefore, letting
G_{t}=Y+Z+t\epsilon
, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid G_{t}]=\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)^{\prime}(t)=\left<\epsilon,\mathbb{E}[Z\mid G_{t}]\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid G_{t}]-\mathbb{E}[H_{i+1}(Z)\mid G_{t}]\right>\\
-\left(\mathbb{E}[\left<\epsilon,Z\right>\mid G_{t}]\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid G_{t}]-\mathbb{E}[\left<\epsilon,H_{i+1}(Z)\right>\mid G_{t}]\right).

Applying the triangle inequality along with Cauchy-Schwarz, Holder’s and Jensen’s inequalities then yields

\left\|\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid G_{t}]\right\|_{p}\leq C\|\epsilon\|_{q}(\|H_{i+1}(Z)\|_{r}+\|Z\|_{2r}\|H_{i}(Z)\|_{2r}),

where

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}.

Finally applying Lemma
3
yields

\left\|\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[H_{i}(Z)\mid Y+Z+t\epsilon]\right\|_{p}\leq C\sqrt{(2r-1)^{(k+1)}(k+1)!}\|\epsilon\|_{q},

concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
Y
and
Z
be two independent standard normal random variables on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, for any
k\geq 1
and any
\alpha>0
, we have

\mathbb{E}[H_{k}(Z)\mid\alpha Y+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}Z]=(1-\alpha^{2})^{k/2}H_{k}(\alpha Y+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}Z).

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a smooth function with compact support. By performing multiple integrations by parts with respect to the Gaussian measure (see e.g. Equation (16)
[
2
]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[H_{k}(Z)\mid\alpha Y+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}Z]

\displaystyle\phi(\alpha Y+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}Z)\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[H_{k}(Z)\phi(\alpha Y+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}Z)]

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha^{2})^{k/2}\mathbb{E}[\nabla^{k}\phi(\alpha Y+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}Z)]

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha^{2})^{k/2}\mathbb{E}[H_{k}(\alpha Y+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}Z)\phi(\alpha Y+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}Z)],

concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
X
be a random variable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with identity covariance matrix. Then,

\|\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 3}]\|^{2}\leq\sqrt{d}\|\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 2}\|X\|^{2}]\|.

Proof.

Let
X^{\prime}
be an independent copy of
X
. By Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 3}]\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left<X^{\otimes 3},X^{\prime\otimes 3}\right>\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left<X,X^{\prime}\right>^{3}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left<X,X^{\prime}\right>^{2}\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left<X,X^{\prime}\right>^{4}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left<X^{\otimes 2},X^{\prime\otimes 2}\right>\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left<X^{\otimes 2},X^{\prime\otimes 2}\right>\|X\|^{2}\|X^{\prime}\|^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 2}]\|\|\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 2}\|X\|^{2}]\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{E}[I_{d}]\|\|\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 2}\|X\|^{2}]\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{d}\|\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 2}\|X\|^{2}]\|.

∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
X
be a centered random variable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with identity covariance matrix and suppose there exists
\tau:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes 2}
such that, for any smooth test function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}[\left<X,\phi(X)\right>]=\mathbb{E}[\left<\tau(X),\nabla\phi(X)\right>].

(9)

Then

\|\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 3}]\|\leq 2\mathbb{E}[\|\tau(X)-I_{d}\|^{2}]^{1/2}.

Proof.

The proof follows the proof of Equation (3.14)
[
8
]
, except we use (
9
) in place of the Poincaré inequality.

Let
B=\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 3}]
. We have

\|B\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left<X,BX^{\otimes 2}\right>\right].

By definition of
\tau
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|B\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left<\tau(X),\nabla(BX^{\otimes 2})\right>\right]

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\left[\left<\tau(X),BX\right>\right],

where

(BX)_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{i,j,k}X_{k}.

Since
X
is centered, we have
\mathbb{E}[BX]=0
and

\|B\|^{2}=2\mathbb{E}\left[\left<\tau(X)-I_{d},BX\right>\right].

Finally, by Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality and since
\mathbb{E}[X^{\otimes 2}]=I_{d}
,

\displaystyle\|B\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\tau(X)-I_{d}\|^{2}]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}[\|BX\|^{2}]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|B\|\mathbb{E}[\|\tau(X)-I_{d}\|^{2}]^{1/2},

concluding the proof.
∎

Renormalized charged scalar current in the Reissner-Nordström-de Sitter spacetime
Christiane Klein, Jochen Zahn

Abstract
We perform a Hadamard point-split renormalization of the current density j ν j_{\nu} of a charged scalar field in a Reissner-Nordström-de Sitter spacetime. We compute numerically the expectation values of the components j r j_{r} and j t j_{t} in the Unruh state in the exterior region and study their dependence on the field parameters and the position.



I
Introduction

In astrophysical contexts, it is expected that any charged black hole (BH) will quickly lose its charge by accreting matter of the opposite charge. But even an isolated charged BH loses its charge by quantum effects.
The discharge of charged BHs by Hawking radiation or pair creation due to the electromagnetic background field has already been anticipated by Hawking himself in his seminal work
Hawking 1975
. This effect has been studied further, for example, by
Zaumen 1974
;
Carter 1974
;
Damour and Ruffini 1975
;
Gibbons 1975
;
Hiscock and Weems 1990
;
Herman and Hiscock 1994
;
Herman and Hiscock 1996
;
Khriplovich 1999
;
Gabriel 2000
;
Chen et al. 2012
;
Kim 2014
;
Johnson 2020
;
Ong 2019
;
Ong 2021
;
Balakumar et al. 2020
. These works typically focus on the discharge in the exterior region and, as far as quantitative results are concerned, on large field masses and charges. While this is certainly the most physically interesting phenomenon and parameter range, we think that there are still interesting open questions. Here, we want to answer a few of them.

The study of discharge in the exterior region typically proceeds either by considering pair creation (for example using Schwinger’s pair creation rate
Schwinger 1951
), or by computing the expectation value of the current
j_{r}
. By charge conservation, this quantity is determined by its value at an arbitrary position in stationary and rotationally symmetric states. We compute it numerically at the event horizon in the Unruh state, albeit only for small mass
\mu
and charge
q
of the field and a large cosmological constant
\Lambda
.
We find an exponential decay in the mass and a proportionality
\sim q^{2}
for small
q
.

Another potentially interesting observable, which to the best of our knowledge has not yet been considered in the literature, is the current component
j_{t}
, representing, up to a negative factor, the (vacuum polarization) charge density, as measured by a stationary observer. Certainly, for astrophysical BHs and realistic field masses, one expects this polarization charge density to decay rapidly, as for QED in an external Coulomb potential (exponentially according to Uehling’s result linear in the external potential
Uehling 1935
or subexponentially when treating the external potential nonperturbatively
Mohr et al. 1998
). It would be nice to verify this intuition. We make first steps in this direction, as we calculate a finite counterterm from a Hadamard point-split renormalization. We also compute the vacuum polarization numerically in the Unruh state, again in the regime of small mass
\mu
and charge
q
of the field and a large cosmological constant
\Lambda
.

The inclusion of a cosmological constant, i.e., working on a Reissner-Nordström-de Sitter (RNdS) instead of a Reissner-Nordström (RN) spacetime, goes beyond previous work (focusing on the asymptotically flat case) and has further advantages: numerical calculations are easier, as the corresponding mode equation has only regular singular points (instead of an irregular singular point at
\infty
for RN). This allows for an efficient numerical scheme
Hollands et al. 2020a
, which works best if the cosmological and event horizons are of the same order of magnitude (corresponding to a large cosmological constant). Furthermore, (quantum) fields on RNdS have attracted considerable attention in recent years, due to the possibility of violations of strong cosmic censorship (sCC)
Penrose 1974
on such spacetimes
Cardoso et al. 2018a
;
Dias et al. 2019
;
Cardoso et al. 2018b
.

Our results are also relevant regarding the ongoing debate about sCC. The sCC conjecture roughly states that at the inner horizon of a charged or rotating black hole, like
\mathcal{CH}^{R}
in Fig.
1
, local observables such as the current of a charged scalar field will diverge. This prevents any observer from crossing the inner horizon and entering the region beyond it, where determinism breaks down. It was suspected long ago
Birrell and Davies 1978
and recently shown
Hollands et al. 2020b
that the stress tensor of a real scalar field diverges in a universal way at the inner horizon. The divergence is stronger than that of classical fields and strong enough to ensure the validity of sCC.

In the context of charged BHs, the consideration of charged fields is natural. In view of the sCC conjecture, it is then interesting to study the behavior of the expectation value of the current density
j_{\nu}
near the inner horizon. Our results on the point-split renormalization of the current, which are also valid in the interior region, are used in
Klein et al. 2021
to do this.

The study of charged scalars on RNdS is made slightly more complicated by the fact that an instability is present in the regime of small mass
\mu
and charge
q
of the field and small cosmological constant
\Lambda

Zhu et al. 2014
;
Konoplya and Zhidenko 2014
;
Dias et al. 2019
;
Hod 2018
. As far as our general results on the point-split renormalization of the current density in the Unruh state are concerned, we assume that we are in a parameter range in which the instability is absent. For our concrete computations, we choose parameters such that the instability is avoided (this is one of the reasons for choosing a large cosmological constant
\Lambda
).

Let us comment on the relation to previous similar work. In
Herman and Hiscock 1996
, an expression for the Hadamard point-split renormalization of the current in general spacetimes and external background on-shell electromagnetic fields is derived (though not evaluated). A divergent counterterm from Hadamard point splitting is obtained, in contrast to our result of a finite counterterm. As explained below, this seems to be due to a sign mistake in
Herman and Hiscock 1996
. Reference
Balakumar and Winstanley 2020
has derived a prescription for the calculation of the renormalized current density of a charged scalar field in a curved spacetime with a background electromagnetic field via Hadamard point splitting, but only applies it to show that the renormalized current satisfies current conservation. Recently, the expectation value of
j_{r}
was computed in the massless case for the Boulware state in a RN spacetime
Balakumar et al. 2020
. We go beyond these results in that we allow for a nonzero mass and cosmological constant, use the physically more relevant Unruh state, and also compute the expectation value of the component
j_{t}
, related to the charge density.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the dynamics of the scalar field and the Unruh vacuum. These are used in Sec.
III
to derive a point-splitting regularized expression for the current in the Unruh state. The parametrix for the current is determined in Sec.
IV
. We present numerical results in Sec.
V
and conclude in Sec.
VI
.

Throughout, we work in units
\hbar=c=G=4\pi\epsilon_{0}=1
.

III
The regularized current in the Unruh state

The observable that we focus on in this work is the current density
j_{\nu}
, which is classically given by

\displaystyle j_{\nu}(x)=iq\left(\Phi(x)D_{\nu}^{*}\Phi^{*}(x)-\Phi^{*}(x)D_{\nu}\Phi(x)\right)\,

(13)

with
D^{*}_{\nu}={\partial}_{\nu}+iqA_{\nu}
.
In the quantum theory, this needs to be renormalized, as it is quadratic and local in the field. We do this using Hadamard point-split renormalization, which is local and (gauge) covariant
Hollands and Wald 2001
;
Hollands and Wald 2015
;
Zahn 2014
. The expectation value of a renormalized field monomial
D_{\alpha}\Phi(x)D^{*}_{\beta}\Phi^{*}(x)
, with multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
in a Hadamard state
\Psi
, is defined as

\langle D_{\alpha}\Phi(x)D^{*}_{\beta}\Phi^{*}(x)\rangle_{\Psi}^{\mathrm{ren}}=\lim\limits_{x^{\prime}\to x}D_{\alpha}(D^{\prime}_{\beta})^{*}\left(\langle\Phi(x)\Phi^{*}(x^{\prime})\rangle_{\Psi}-H(x,x^{\prime})\right)\,.

(14)

Here,
H(x,x^{\prime})
is the Hadamard parametrix of the wave operator
D_{\nu}D^{\nu}-\mu^{2}
, which is discussed in detail in the following section. For a Hadamard state
\Psi
, the expression in brackets on the right-hand side (r.h.s.) is smooth, so the limit of coinciding points is well defined and gauge invariant (before the limit of coinciding points is taken, the expression on the r.h.s. is of course not gauge invariant).

Hadamard point-split renormalization is not the unique local and gauge covariant renormalization scheme. There are in general finite renormalization ambiguities. However, for the case of the current, any two locally gauge covariant renormalization schemes yielding a conserved current
j_{\mu}
differ, at most, by multiple of the current
J_{\mu}
responsible for the external electromagnetic field
Zahn 2014
;
Balakumar and Winstanley 2020
, corresponding to a renormalization of the charge of the field. In the present case, the external current
J_{\mu}
vanishes, so that Hadamard point-split renormalization yields a unique result. For some applications of this renormalization procedure in the context of external electromagnetic potentials or curved spacetimes, we refer to
Schlemmer and Zahn 2015
;
Wernersson and Zahn 2021
and
Hollands and Wald 2015
;
Zilberman et al. 2020
;
Hollands et al. 2020b
.

In this section, we focus on the evaluation of the first term on the r.h.s. of (
14

\langle j_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}=iq\left\langle\left\{\Phi(x_{1}),\partial_{\nu}\Phi^{*}(x_{2})\right\}-\left\{\Phi^{*}(x_{1}),\partial_{\nu}\Phi(x_{2})\right\}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}.

(15)

\{A,B\}=\tfrac{1}{2}(AB+BA)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}(\overline{\alpha_{-\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}}}\alpha_{-\omega\omega^{\prime\prime}}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}+\overline{\alpha_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}}}\alpha_{\omega\omega^{\prime\prime}}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}^{\prime}})=4\pi^{2}C_{\lambda\mathrm{N}\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\delta(\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime})\,,

(23)

where the
C_{\lambda\mathrm{N}\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})
are real functions given by

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{in},{\mathrm{I}},{\mathrm{I}}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\coth\left(\pi\tfrac{\omega}{\kappa_{c}}\right)\omega^{-1}

(24a)

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{up},{\mathrm{I}},{\mathrm{I}}}(\omega)=C_{\mathrm{up},{\mathrm{II}},{\mathrm{II}}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\coth\left(\pi\tfrac{\omega}{\kappa_{+}}\right)\omega^{-1}

(24b)

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{up},{\mathrm{I}},{\mathrm{II}}}(\omega)=C_{\mathrm{up},{\mathrm{II}},{\mathrm{I}}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\left[\omega\sinh\left(\pi\tfrac{\omega}{\kappa_{+}}\right)\right]^{-1}\,,

(24c)

and all other
C_{\lambda\mathrm{N}\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}(\omega)
vanish.
Inserting this back in (
20
), we find

\displaystyle\langle j_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{4\pi r_{1}r_{2}}\sum\limits_{\ell,m,\lambda,\mathrm{N}\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}\omega\ \Imag\bigg[C_{\lambda\mathrm{N}\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}(\omega)\overline{Y_{\ell m}}(\theta_{1},\phi_{1})Y_{\ell m}(\theta_{2},\phi_{2})\times

(25)

\displaystyle\times\overline{\tilde{h}_{\omega\ell}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}}}(r_{1},t_{1})\left(\partial_{\nu}\tilde{h}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}_{\omega\ell}(r_{2},t_{2})-\frac{1}{r_{2}}\tilde{h}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}_{\omega\ell}(r_{2},t_{2})\partial_{\nu}r_{2}\right)\bigg]\,.

So far, we have not specified the point splitting we apply. We use a
\theta
- splitting as introduced in
Levi and Ori 2016
: We identify
r_{2}=r_{1}
,
\phi_{2}=\phi_{1}
, but set
\theta_{2}=\theta_{1}+\epsilon=\theta+\epsilon
and in addition
t_{2}=t_{1}+\delta=t+\delta
for the regularization of the
\omega
-integral. Note that we first take the limit
\delta\to 0
, before we take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
. This allows us to write

\sum\limits_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\overline{Y_{\ell m}}(\theta,\phi)Y_{\ell m}(\theta+\epsilon,\phi)=\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\cos\epsilon)\,

(26)

for the
\omega
-independent part of the regularized current in (
25
).

Let us now take a closer look at the integral over
\omega
. Consider first the part with the derivative acting on
r
. In the coincidence limit, only the imaginary part of
\partial_{\nu}r
will contribute to the integral.
However,
\partial_{\nu}r
is real. Hence this part does not contribute to the integral in the coincidence limit, and we can drop it. The remaining integrals over
\omega
are of the form

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda,\mathrm{N},\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}\omega\ C_{\lambda\mathrm{N}\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}(\omega)\Imag\left[\overline{\tilde{h}_{\omega\ell}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}}}(r,t)\partial_{\nu}\tilde{h}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}_{\omega\ell}(r,t+\delta)\right]\,.

(27)

The splitting in
t
will lead to an oscillatory term of the form
e^{-i\delta(\omega-\omega_{g})}
, where
\omega_{g}
is the frequency shift between the gauge in which the modes are defined and the one in which they are evaluated. To be able to take the limit
\delta\to 0
later, we will shift the integrals by
\omega_{g}
.
This means that the integral involving the
\mathrm{in}{\mathrm{I}}
-type Boulware mode will be shifted by
\omega_{r,\mathrm{in}}=qQ(r^{-1}-r_{c}^{-1})
and the one involving the
\mathrm{up}{\mathrm{I}}
- or
\mathrm{up}{\mathrm{II}}
-type mode will be shifted by
\omega_{r,\mathrm{up}}=qQ(r^{-1}-r_{+}^{-1})
.

Next, consider the leading order in the large
|\omega|
-limit. There, (
6a
) becomes

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r_{*}}^{2}+\omega^{2}\right]H_{\omega\ell}(r_{*})=0\,

(28)

so that the Boulware modes become plane waves. Then, the leading order contribution to the integrand of (
27
), evaluated in region
{\mathrm{I}}
, is given by

\displaystyle\coth\left(\pi\tfrac{\omega}{\kappa_{c}}\right)\left(\partial_{\nu}t+\partial_{\nu}r_{*}\right)\cos(\delta\omega)+\coth\left(\pi\tfrac{\omega}{\kappa_{+}}\right)\left(\partial_{\nu}t-\partial_{\nu}r_{*}\right)\cos(\delta\omega)\,.

(29)

This integrand is antisymmetric in
\omega
, and the contributions from
\omega\to\infty
and
\omega\to-\infty
cancel. Similar results also hold for the integrand in
{\mathrm{II}}
. The results for situations close to the present one
Yafaev 2010
, as well as the numerical results obtained with this formula, indicate that this cancellation is indeed strong enough to make the integral converge, even when the coincidence limit is taken. Assuming this to be true, we can take the limit
\delta\to 0
, and hence

\displaystyle\langle j_{\nu}(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum\limits_{\ell}\frac{q(2\ell+1)}{16\pi^{2}r^{2}}P_{\ell}(\cos\epsilon)\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\omega\sum\limits_{\lambda,\mathrm{N},\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}\left(C_{\lambda\mathrm{N}\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}(\omega+\omega_{r,\lambda})\Imag\left[\overline{\tilde{h}_{(\omega+\omega_{r,\lambda})\ell}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}}}(r,t)\partial_{\nu}\tilde{h}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}_{(\omega+\omega_{r,\lambda})\ell}(r,t)\right]+\omega\leftrightarrow-\omega\right).

(30)

From numerical computations, it turns out that this expression is already finite in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
. This is further confirmed by the calculation of the second term in (
14
), in the combination relevant for the current, which must also be, and indeed is, finite.

IV
The calculation of the parametrix

We now turn to the discussion of the second term on the r.h.s. of (
14
). In four dimensions, the Hadamard parametrix can be written as

\displaystyle H(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\left[\frac{U(x,x^{\prime})}{\sigma_{\epsilon}(x,x^{\prime})}+\sum\limits_{n}V_{n}(x,x^{\prime})\sigma^{n}(x,x^{\prime})\ln\left(\frac{\sigma_{\epsilon}(x,x^{\prime})}{K^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

(31)

Here
K
is an arbitrary scale, and
\sigma_{\epsilon}(x,x^{\prime})
is Synge’s world function
Poisson et al. 2011
equipped with an
i\epsilon
-prescription, which is only relevant for causally related
x
,
x^{\prime}
(and is thus irrelevant in the
\theta
-splitting that we are employing). The so-called Hadamard coefficients,
U(x,x^{\prime})
and
V_{n}(x,x^{\prime})
, are smooth functions, which are determined in a local and (gauge) covariant way and fulfill

\displaystyle\overline{U(x,x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=U(x^{\prime},x),

\displaystyle\overline{V_{n}(x,x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=V_{n}(x^{\prime},x).

(32)

The sum in (
31
) does not converge in general, but for the application in the Hadamard point split (
14
), only a finite number of terms is relevant (whose number depends on the number of derivatives in the field monomial, i.e.,
|\alpha|
and
|\beta|
). From the requirement that the application of
D_{\nu}D^{\nu}-\mu^{2}
yields a smooth function, one derives the transport equations

\displaystyle\left[\sigma^{\nu}D_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\Box\sigma-2\right]U

\displaystyle=0,

(33a)

\displaystyle 2\left[\sigma^{\nu}D_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\Box\sigma-1\right]V_{0}

\displaystyle=-\left[D_{\nu}D^{\nu}-\mu^{2}\right]U,

(33b)

\displaystyle 2(n+1)\left[\sigma^{\nu}D_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\Box\sigma+n\right]V_{n+1}

\displaystyle=-[D_{\nu}D^{\nu}-\mu^{2}]V_{n}.

(33c)

Here we used the notation
\sigma^{\nu}=\nabla^{\nu}\sigma
. That the leading short-distance singularity is as in the flat case is ensured by the initial condition
U(x,x)=1
. This determines
U(x,x^{\prime})
uniquely as

U(x,x^{\prime})=\Delta^{\tfrac{1}{2}}(x,x^{\prime})P(x,x^{\prime})\,,

(34)

where
\Delta(x,x^{\prime})
is the Van Vleck-Morette determinant
Poisson et al. 2011
, and
P(x,x^{\prime})
is the parallel transport with respect to
D_{\nu}
along the geodesic from
x^{\prime}
to
x
(which can be assumed to be unique by restricting
x^{\prime}
to a suitably small neighborhood of
x
), i.e., solving
\sigma^{\nu}D_{\nu}P=0
with the initial condition
U(x,x)=1
. The higher order Hadamard coefficients are then determined recursively.

For the application in Hadamard point splitting (
14
), only the coinciding point limit of a suitable number of derivatives of the Hadamard coefficients is relevant. More concretely, for the point-split renormalization of the current, we need to determine all the divergent and finite contributions to the coinciding point limit
x^{\prime}\to x
of
\partial^{\prime}_{\nu}\Imag[H(x,x^{\prime})]
, where we used (
32
). For this, we perform a Taylor expansion of the imaginary part of the Hadamard coefficients, in the form

\displaystyle F(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=F^{(0)}(x)+F^{(1)}_{\alpha}(x)\sigma^{\alpha}(x,x^{\prime})+F^{(2)}_{\alpha\beta}(x)\sigma^{\alpha}(x,x^{\prime})\sigma^{\beta}(x,x^{\prime})+\dots

(35)

Evaluating successively higher covariant derivatives of
U
,
V_{n}
in the limit of coinciding points, one obtains from the transport equations (
33
) the following results
Balakumar and Winstanley 2020
:

\displaystyle\Imag\left(U^{(1)}_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=qA_{\alpha}

(36a)

\displaystyle\Imag\left(U^{(2)}_{\alpha\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{2}\nabla_{(\alpha}A_{\beta)}

(36b)

\displaystyle\Imag\left(U^{(3)}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{6}\Real\left(D_{(\alpha}D_{\beta}A_{\gamma)}\right)+\frac{q}{12}A_{(\alpha}R_{\beta\gamma)}

(36c)

\displaystyle\Imag\left(V_{0\alpha}^{(1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{2}\left[\mu^{2}-\frac{1}{6}R\right]A_{\alpha}-\frac{q}{12}\nabla^{\nu}F_{\nu\alpha}

(36d)

\displaystyle\Imag\left(V_{0\alpha\beta}^{(2)}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{4}\left[\mu^{2}-\frac{1}{6}R\right]\Real\left(D_{(\alpha}A_{\beta)}\right)+\frac{q}{24}A_{(\alpha}\nabla_{\beta)}R-\frac{q}{24}\nabla_{(\alpha}\nabla^{\nu}F_{\beta)\nu}

(36e)

\displaystyle\Imag\left(V^{(0)}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=0\,,

(36f)

where symmetrization with respect to the indices in round brackets is understood.
In addition, we will need an expansion of
\sigma^{\mu}
in terms of the angular separation
\epsilon
. For this, we use the expansion given in
Ottewill and Wardell 2009
, and find

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{2}\epsilon^{2}-\frac{fr^{2}}{24}\epsilon^{4}+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{6}\right)

(37a)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\theta}

\displaystyle=-r^{2}\epsilon+\frac{fr^{2}}{6}\epsilon^{3}+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{4}\right)

(37b)

\displaystyle\sigma_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{2}\epsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{4}\right)

(37c)

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}

\displaystyle\approx\sigma_{\varphi}=0+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{4}\right)\,.

(37d)

Taking into account that we are in a gauge such that
A(x)=A(x^{\prime})=0
, we can now put these ingredients together and compute the coinciding point limit of
\partial^{\prime}_{\nu}\Imag[H(x,x^{\prime})]
in the
\theta
-splitting, which is the relevant quantity for our purpose of renormalizing the current density. We find that the divergent parts cancel, consistent with our finding in the previous section, and for the finite part we obtain

-2q\partial^{\prime}_{\nu}\Imag\left[H(x,x^{\prime})\right]=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{q^{2}Qf(r)}{6r^{3}}\delta^{t}_{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\,.

(38)

We note that this term is only relevant for the
t
-component of the current and vanishes on the horizons. Similar finite contributions from Hadamard point splitting frequently occur in the renormalization of the current density (see the discussion and examples in
Schlemmer and Zahn 2015
;
Wernersson and Zahn 2021
).
Comparing this expression to the result of
Herman and Hiscock 1996
, where a divergence in the coinciding point limit is found, we note that there is an error in the calculation of
Herman and Hiscock 1996
: in their formula for the current, Eq. (8), the difference rather than the sum of contributions with derivatives acting on the first and second variable
should be taken. If this is taken into account, the results do agree.

Note also that the parametrix satisfies the condition for the conservation of the current, as can be shown by an explicit calculation in the
(t,r_{*},\theta,\phi)
coordinates. Employing that by spherical symmetry and stationarity the angular components of the current must vanish,
j_{\theta}=j_{\phi}=0
, and that it must be time-independent, the current conservation
\nabla_{\nu}j^{\nu}=0
reduces to
\partial_{r_{*}}(r^{2}j_{r_{*}})=0

Balakumar et al. 2020
. Since the parametrix only affects the
t
-component of the current and is time-independent, the current is conserved if the expression without the parametrix yields a conserved current.

Finally, our expression for the renormalized current is given by subtracting the Hadamard parametrix from the result of the previous section. Since this parametrix is finite, and supported by the numerical results, we take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
. We find

\displaystyle\langle j_{\nu}(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\ell}\frac{q(2\ell+1)}{16\pi^{2}r^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\omega\sum\limits_{\lambda,\mathrm{N},\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}\left(C_{\lambda\mathrm{N}\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}(\omega+\omega_{r,\lambda})\Imag\left[\overline{\tilde{h}_{(\omega+\omega_{r,\lambda})\ell}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}}}(r,t)\partial_{\nu}\tilde{h}^{\lambda\mathrm{N}^{\prime}}_{(\omega+\omega_{r,\lambda})\ell}(r,t)\right]+\omega\leftrightarrow-\omega\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{q^{2}Qf(r)}{6r^{3}}\delta^{t}_{\nu}.

(39)

V
Numerical results

While there is no analytic solution for the functions
\tilde{h}^{\lambda N}_{\omega\ell}(r_{*},t)
, they can be approximated numerically. This allows us, for example, to calculate the
v
-component of the current on the event horizon. Here, we can use the asymptotic behavior of the Boulware modes (
21
)
to get an expression in terms of scattering coefficients. We find

\displaystyle\langle j_{v}\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\ell}\frac{q(2\ell+1)}{16\pi^{2}r^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\omega\ \left(F(\omega)+F(-\omega)\right)

(40a)

\displaystyle F(\omega)

\displaystyle=\coth\left(\pi\tfrac{\omega+\omega_{{\mathrm{I}}}}{\kappa_{c}}\right)\left(1-|\mathcal{R}_{\omega\ell}|^{2}\right)+\coth\left(\pi\tfrac{\omega}{\kappa_{+}}\right)|\mathcal{R}_{\omega\ell}|^{2}\,.

(40b)

Due to the fact that the radial part of the Klein-Gordon equation can be brought into the form of a Heun equation in the conformally coupled case,
\mu^{2}=2\Lambda/3
,
Suzuki et al. 1999
;
Hollands et al. 2020a
, this case can be dealt with most efficiently. However, more general masses can be handled as well
Hollands et al. 2020a
: one can obtain solutions to (
6a
) which are defined in the neighborhood of one of the three horizons. Comparing these solutions on the overlap of their domains, one can then compute the scattering coefficients. In this way, the scattering coefficients and the integrand in (
40a
) or (
IV
) are computed for different values of
\omega
and
\ell
up to some maximal values, beyond which the contributions become negligibly small. The integral is finally estimated using a Riemann sum. The sum is also used to estimate the error of this procedure. For more details, see
Hollands et al. 2020a
.

Note that in order to achieve a good performance of the code, we restrict ourselves to small charges
qQ
and masses
\mu^{2}
of the scalar field. As discussed above, we thus need to choose the cosmological constant
\Lambda
large enough in order to avoid the instability parameter region. Equation (46) in
Dias et al. 2019
gives a condition to determine whether the instability appears for a chosen set of spacetime parameters. We checked this condition and find no instability in the parameter range we consider.

VI
Conclusion

We derived a formula for the current of a charged scalar field in the RNdS spacetime, using point splitting and Hadamard renormalization. We found that the Hadamard parametrix only contributes a finite term, in contrast to results obtained previously
Herman and Hiscock 1996
. In addition, this term vanishes on all horizons. We obtained an explicit expression for the current of the charged scalar in the RNdS spacetime, which can also be used in the interior of the BH, as was done in
Klein et al. 2021
. We tested the result by evaluating it numerically. We evaluated the
v
-component of the current on
\mathcal{H}^{R}
in the Unruh state and found an exponential decay in the mass
\mu
, as well as a quadratic dependence on the charge
q
of the field for small
q
. We also calculated the
r_{*}
-component of the current in the exterior region of the spacetime to test current conservation, and found it to hold. Finally,
\langle j_{t}\rangle_{\mathrm{U}}
, related to vacuum polarization, was computed inside region
{\mathrm{I}}
, with results compatible with those obtained on the horizons bounding region
{\mathrm{I}}
.

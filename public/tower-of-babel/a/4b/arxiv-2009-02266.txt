Strong positivity for the skein algebras of the 4 4 -punctured sphere and of the 1 1 -punctured torus
Pierrick Bousseau

Abstract
. The Kauffman bracket skein algebra is a quantization of the algebra of regular functions on the S ​ L 2 SL_{2} character variety of a topological surface. We realize the skein algebra of the 4 4 -punctured sphere as the output of a mirror symmetry construction based on higher genus Gromov-Witten theory and applied to a complex cubic surface. Using this result, we prove the positivity of the structure constants of the bracelets basis for the skein algebras of the 4 4 -punctured sphere and of the 1 1 -punctured torus. This connection between topology of the 4 4 -punctured sphere and enumerative geometry of curves in cubic surfaces is a mathematical manifestation of the existence of dual descriptions in string/M-theory for the 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 N f = 4 N_{f}=4 S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) gauge theory.

Introduction

In this paper, we address questions
in low-dimensional topology using algebraic and geometric methods inspired by mirror symmetry.
More precisely, we prove results on the topology
of simple closed curves on the
4
-punctured sphere and the
1
-punctured torus by studying
the a priori unrelated problem of counting holomorphic maps
from Riemann surfaces to complex cubic surfaces.
We present our results on positive bases
for the skein algebras of the
4
-punctured sphere and
1
-punctured torus in Section

1.1
. We give a survey of the
proof, based on enumerative algebraic geometry, in Section
1.2
.
Motivations from theoretical physics are briefly discussed in
Section
1.3
.

that can be isotoped to identical embeddings except within the neighborhood shown,
where the framing is vertical, i.e. pointing out to the reader.
The Kauffman bracket skein module was
introduced independently by Przytycki

[
Prz91
]
and Turaev
[
Tur88
]
as an extension to general

3
-manifolds of the variant of the Jones polynomial

[
Jon85
]
given by the Kauffman bracket polynomial for framed links in the

3
-sphere
[
Kau87
]
. In the general context of skein modules
attached to arbitrary ribbon categories
[
GJS19
]
,
the Kauffman bracket skein module is
associated to the ribbon category of finite-dimensional representations
of the
SL_{2}
quantum group.

Given an oriented
2
-manifold
\mathbb{S}
,
one can define a natural algebra structure on the
Kauffmann bracket skein module of the
3
-manifold

\mathbb{M}\coloneqq\mathbb{S}\times(-1,1)
:
given two framed links
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
in
\mathbb{S}\times(-1,1)
,
and viewing the interval
(-1,1)
as a vertical direction, the product
L_{1}L_{2}

is defined by placing
L_{1}
on top of
L_{2}
.
We denote by
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S})

the resulting associative
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-algebra with unit.
The skein algebra

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S})
is in general non-commutative.

We consider the case where
\mathbb{S}
is the complement

\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}

of a finite number
\ell
of points in a compact oriented 2-manifold of genus
g
.
A multicurve on
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
is the union of finitely many disjoint compact connected embedded
1
-dimensional
submanifolds of
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
such that none of them bounds a disc in
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
.
Identifying

\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
with
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}\times(-1,1)
,
a multicurve on
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
endowed with the vertical framing naturally defined
a framed link in

\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}\times(-1,1)
. By a result of
Przytycki
[
Prz06
, Theorem IX.7.1]
,
isotopy classes of multicurves form a basis of

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
as
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-module.

1.1.2.
Positivity of the bracelets basis of
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
and
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})

Dylan Thurston introduced in
[
Thu14
]

a different basis
\mathbf{B}_{T}
of
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
,
called the
bracelets basis and defined as follows.
Let
T_{n}(x)
be the Chebyshev polynomials defined by

(2)

T_{0}(x)=1\,,T_{1}(x)=x\,,T_{2}(x)=x^{2}-2\,,\,\text{and for every}\,\,n\geq 2,\,\,T_{n+1}(x)=xT_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x)\,.

Writing
x=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}
, we have

T_{n}(x)=\lambda^{n}+\lambda^{-n}
for every

n\geq 1
.
Given an isotopy class
\gamma
of multicurve on
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
,
one can uniquely write
\gamma
in
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
as

\gamma=\gamma_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{r}^{n_{r}}

where
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r}
are all distinct isotopy classes
of connected multicurves
and
n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and we define

(3)

\mathbf{T}(\gamma)\coloneqq T_{n_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\cdots T_{n_{r}}(\gamma_{r})\,.

As the leading term of
T_{n}(x)
is
x^{n}
,
the set

\mathbf{B}_{T}

of all
\mathbf{T}(\gamma)
, for
\gamma
isotopy class of multicurve,
is a
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-linear basis of

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
. If
\gamma
is a connected multicurve,

\gamma^{n}
is the class of
n
disjoint isotopic copies of
\gamma
,
whereas
T_{n}(\gamma)
is the class of a connected bracelet
made of
n
isotopic copies of
\gamma
(see
[
Thu14
, Proposition 4.4]
),
hence the name of bracelets basis for
\mathbf{B}_{T}
.

In
[
Thu14
, Conjecture 4.20]
, Dylan Thurston made
the remarkable positivity conjecture that the structure constants of
the bracelets basis, which are a priori in

\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
, in fact belong to

\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[A^{\pm}]
. He proved in
[
Thu14
, Theorem 1]

that the conjecture holds after setting
A=1
.
In the present paper, we prove

[
Thu14
, Conjecture 4.20]
in the case of the

4
-punctured sphere
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
, that is,

g=0
and
\ell=4
, and the
1
-punctured torus
\mathbb{S}_{1,1}
, that is,

g=1
and
\ell=1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The structure constants for the bracelets basis of the skein algebras
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
and

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
of the
4
-punctured sphere
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
and of the

1
-punctured torus
\mathbb{S}_{1,1}
belong to
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[A^{\pm}]
.
In other words, for every
x
and
y
in
\mathbf{B}_{T}
, the product
xy
in the skein algebra
is a linear combination with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[A^{\pm}]
of elements of

\mathbf{B}_{T}
.

[
Thu14
, Conjecture 4.20]
was previously known
in the following cases:

(1)

For
g=0
and
\ell\leq 3
, the skein algebra
is a commutative polynomial algebra, more precisely,
we have
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,0})=\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
,

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,1})=\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
,

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,2})=\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][x]
, and

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,3})=\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][x,y,z]
, and so

[
Thu14
, Conjecture 4.20]
follows directly
from the identity
T_{m}(x)T_{n}(x)=T_{m+n}(x)+T_{|m-n|}(x)
.

(2)

For
g=1
and
\ell=0
. For every
p=(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
write
\gamma_{p}
for the isotopy class of

\gcd(a,b)
disjoint copies of connected multicurves with
homology class
\frac{1}{\gcd(a,b)}(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}=H_{1}(\mathbb{S}_{1,0},\mathbb{Z})
.
Frohman and Gelca proved in
[
FG00
]

the identity

\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{(a,b)})\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{(c,d)})=A^{ad-bc}\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{(a+c,b+d)})+A^{-ad+bc}\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{(a-c,b-d)})\,.

[
Thu14
, Conjecture 4.20]
follows because the bracelets basis of

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,0})
is made of
monomials in the variables
\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p})
.

The cases
(g,\ell)=(0,4)
and

(g,\ell)=(1,1)
treated by Theorem

1.1
are the first examples
of a proof of
[
Thu14
, Conjecture 4.20]
in a situation where no
simple closed formula for the structure constants of the bracelets basis seems to exist.

A conceptual approach to the general case of

[
Thu14
, Conjecture 4.20]

would be to construct a monoidal categorification of the skein algebras
\operatorname{Sk}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})

and a categorification of the bracelets basis.
First steps towards this goal are described by Queffelec and Wedrich in
[
QW18
]
.
We do not follow this path to prove Theorem
1.1
.
Rather, one should view Theorem
1.1

as providing further non-trivial evidence that such monoidal categorification should exist.

For
\ell>0
, there is a more refined positivity conjecture,

[
Thu14
, Conjecture 4.21]
, involving the so-called bands basis.
We do not adress this conjecture in the present paper.
General constraints on possible positive bases of skein algebras are discussed by Lê

[
L1̂8a
]
and
Lê, Thurston, and Yu

[
LTY19
]
.

1.1.3.
A stronger positivity result for
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

We will in fact prove a positivity result
for
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
stronger than Theorem

1.1
and conjectured by
Bakshi, Mukherjee, Przytycki, Silvero and Wang
in
[
BMP+18
, Conjecture 4.10 (1)]
.
For
1\leq j\leq 4
, let
p_{j}
be the punctures of

\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
, and
a_{j}
the isotopy class of connected peripheral curves around
p_{j}
,
that is, bounding a
1
-punctured disc with puncture
p_{j}
. The peripheral curves

a_{j}
are in the center of the skein algebra
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
, and so
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
is naturally a

\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}]
-module.

We fix a decomposition of
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
into
two pairs of pants, glued along a connected multicurve

\delta
of
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}

separating the four punctures into the pairs
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
,
p_{4}
.
Isotopy classes of multicurves on

\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
without peripheral components can
then be classified by their Dehn-Thurston coordinates with respect to
\delta

[
MR579
,
PH92
]
.
For every
p=(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
m\geq 0
if
n=0
,
there exists a unique isotopy class
\gamma_{p}
of multicurves without peripheral components,
such that, the minimal number of intersection points
of a multicurve of class
\gamma_{p}
with
\delta
is
2n
,
and such that the twisting number of
\gamma_{p}
around
\delta
is
m
.
As a special case of a theorem of Dehn,
the map

p\mapsto\gamma_{p}
defines a bijection between

(4)

B(\mathbb{Z})\coloneqq\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,\,\,|\,m\geq 0\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,n=0\}

and the set of isotopy classes of multicurves on

\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
without peripheral components,
see
[
PH92
, Theorem 1.2.1]
.
For example,
\gamma_{(0,0)}
is the isotopy class of the empty multicurve,

\gamma_{(1,0)}
is the isotopy class of
\delta
, and a multicurve of class

\gamma_{p}
with
p=(m,n)
has
\gcd(m,n)
connected components.
Equivalently, if
p=(m,n)
with
m
and
n
coprime, and
if we realize

\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
as the quotient
of the four-punctured torus
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}))/\mathbb{Z}^{2}

by the involution
x\mapsto-x
, then

\gamma_{p}
is the class of the image
in
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
of a straight line of slope
n/m
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z})
(e.g. see
[
FM12
, Proposition 2.6]
).
As isotopy classes of multicurves form a basis of the skein algebra as

\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-module, the set

\{\gamma_{p}\}_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}
is a basis of
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
as

\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}]
-module.

For every
p_{1},p_{2},p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
,
we define structure constants
C_{p_{1},p_{2}}^{\mathbb{S}_{0,4},p}\in\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}]
by

(5)

\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p_{1}})\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p_{2}})=\sum_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}C_{p_{1},p_{2}}^{\mathbb{S}_{0,4},p}\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p})\,.

Following
[
BMP+18
]
, we introduce the notation

(6)

R_{1,0}\coloneqq a_{1}a_{2}+a_{3}a_{4}\,,\,\,\,R_{0,1}\coloneqq a_{1}a_{3}+a_{2}a_{4}\,,\,\,\,R_{1,1}\coloneqq a_{1}a_{4}+a_{2}a_{3}\,,

(7)

y\coloneqq a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}+a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}+a_{4}^{2}+(A^{2}-A^{-2})^{2}\,.

The following Theorem
1.2

is our main result and proves Conjecture 4.10(1) of

[
BMP+18
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
p_{1},p_{2},p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

(8)

C^{\mathbb{S}_{0,4},p}_{p_{1},p_{2}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[A^{\pm}][R_{1,0},R_{0,1},R_{1,1},y]\,.

As we will see at the end of Section

,

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

1.1.4.
A stronger positivity result for
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})

Let
\eta
be the isotopy class of connected peripheral curves around the puncture of
\mathbb{S}_{1,1}
.
As
\eta
is in the center of
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
,
the skein algebra
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
is naturally a
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][\eta]
-module.
Isotopy classes multicurves
on
\mathbb{S}_{1,1}
without peripheral components are classified by their homology classes,
which are well-defined up to sign.
Fixing a basis of homology, we get a bijection
p\mapsto\gamma_{p}
between
B(\mathbb{Z})

and the set of isotopy classes of multicurves on
\mathbb{S}_{1,1}
without peripheral components.
For example, multicurve of class
\gamma_{p}
with
p=(m,n)
has
\gcd(m,n)
components.
As isotopy classes of multicurves form a basis of the skein algebra as
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-module,
the set

\{\gamma_{p}\}_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}
is a basis of
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
as

\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][\eta]
-module.
For every
p_{1},p_{2},p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
, we define structure constants
C_{p_{1},p_{2}}^{\mathbb{S}_{1,1},p}\in\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][\eta]
by

(9)

\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p_{1}})\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p_{2}})=\sum_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}C_{p_{1},p_{2}}^{\mathbb{S}_{1,1},p}\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p})\,.

We write

(10)

z\coloneqq A^{2}+A^{-2}+\eta\,.

Note that
z
is the deformation parameter from
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,0})
to

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
:
indeed, closing the puncture means setting
\eta=-A^{2}-A^{-2}
, that is

.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
p_{1},p_{2},p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

(11)

C^{\mathbb{S}_{1,1},p}_{p_{1},p_{2}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[A^{\pm}][z]\,.

As we will see at the end of Section
3.2
,
it is elementary to check that Theorem
1.3
implies Theorem

1.1
for
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
.

1.1.5.
Strong positivity for the quantum cluster algebras
\mathcal{X}^{q}_{PGL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{0,4}}
and
\mathcal{X}^{q}_{PGL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{1,1}}

We can appply our positivity result on the skein algebras
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

and
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
, Theorem
1.2
,
to prove a similar positivity result
for the quantum cluster algebras
\mathcal{X}^{q}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{0,4}}

and
\mathcal{X}^{q}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{1,1}}
.

For every punctured surface
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
with
\ell>0
,
Fock and Goncharov introduced in
[
FG06
]

the cluster varieties
\mathcal{A}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}

and
\mathcal{X}_{PSL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}
:
\mathcal{A}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}

is a moduli space of decorated
SL_{2}
-local system on
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
,
and
\mathcal{X}_{PSL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}
is a moduli space of
framed
PSL_{2}
-local systems on
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}

and both admit a cluster structure. Fock and Goncharov constructed a “duality map”

(12)

\mathbb{I}\colon\mathcal{A}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}_{PSL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}})

from the set
\mathcal{A}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})

of integral tropical points of
\mathcal{A}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}

to the algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}_{PSL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}})

of regular functions on
\mathcal{X}_{PSL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}
.
They proved that
\{\mathbb{I}(l)\}_{l\in\mathcal{A}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})}

is a basis of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}_{PSL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}})

(
[
FG06
, Theorem 12.3]
)
with positive structure constants (
[
FG06
, Theorem 12.2]
).

The cluster variety
\mathcal{X}_{PGL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}
admits a natural Poisson structure,
which can be canonically quantized using the cluster structure
to produce a quantum cluster algebra
\mathcal{X}^{q}_{PSL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}

[
FG09
]
.
Fock and Goncharov conjectured in
[
FG06
, Conjecture 12.4]

1.2.
Structure of the proof: quantum scattering diagrams and curve counting

We will prove Theorems
1.2

and
1.3

by giving an algorithm which computes the structure constants for the bracelets basis of

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

and
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
, and makes manifest their positivity properties.
This algorithm is based on the notion of quantum broken lines
defined by a quantum scattering diagram.

1.2.1.
Quantum scattering diagrams, quantum broken lines and quantum theta functions

Scattering diagrams and broken lines are algebraic and
combinatorial objects playing a key role in the Gross-Siebert
approach to mirror symmetry. Scattering diagrams were introduced by Gross and Siebert

[
GS11
]
, following early insights of Kontsevich and Soibelman

[
KS06
]
. Broken lines were introduced by Gross

[
Gro10
]
, studied by Carl, Pumperla and Siebert
[
CPS10
]
,
and discussed in a quite general context by Gross, Hacking, and Siebert
[
GHS16
]
.
Given an integral affine manifold with singularities
B
,
a scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}^{\operatorname{cl}}
is a collection of codimension
1
integral affine subspaces of
B

called walls and which are decorated by power series. A broken line is a continuous picewise integral affine
line in
B
which bends when crossing
walls of
\mathfrak{D}^{\operatorname{cl}}
.
When the scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}^{\operatorname{cl}}
is so called consistent,
one can construct a commutative associative algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}^{\operatorname{cl}}}
, coming with a basis

\{\vartheta_{p}^{\operatorname{cl}}\}_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}
of so-called theta functions indexed by integral points
B(\mathbb{Z})
of
B
,
and whose structure constants are determined explicitly in terms of the broken lines.

Scattering diagrams and broken lines have
q
-deformed versions, that we
refer to as quantum scattering diagrams and quantum broken lines.
Quantum scattering diagrams were considered by Kontsevich and Soibelman

[
KS06
,
Soi09
,
KS13
]
,
and Filippini and Stoppa

[
FS15
]
. Quantum broken lines were studied by
Mandel
[
Man15
]
and the author

[
Bou20
]
. Given a consistent quantum scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}
,
one can construct an associative
non-necessarily commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}}
, coming with a basis

\{\vartheta_{p}\}_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}
of so-called quantum theta functions,
and whose structure constants are determined explicitly in terms of the quantum broken lines.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2

and
1.3
, we will first define explicit quantum scattering diagrams
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}

and
\mathfrak{D}_{1,1}
over the integral affine manifold with singularities
B=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\langle-\operatorname{id}\,\rangle
and prove that they are consistent.
We will then show that
the algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}

and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{1,1}}
are respectively isomorphic to the skein algebras

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
and
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
, and that the bases of quantum theta functions
agree with the bracelets bases.
The positivity of the structure constants
will follow from the description in terms of quantum broken lines
and from the explicit definitions of
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}

and
\mathfrak{D}_{1,1}
.
As the results for the
1
-punctured torus
\mathbb{S}_{1,1}
will follow from those
for
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
by specialization and change of variables,
we focus on the case of the
4
-punctured sphere
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
.
There are two results to show: the consistency of
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}

(Theorem
3.7
), and the identification of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}
with

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
matching the basis of quantum theta functions
with the bracelets basis (Theorem
3.8
).

1.2.2.
Consistent quantum scattering diagrams from curve counting

We will prove the consistency of
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}
by
showing that
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}

arises from the enumerative geometry
of holomorphic curves in complex cubic surfaces.
It is a general expectation from mirror symmetry
that one should obtain consistent scattering diagrams by counting genus
0

holomorphic curves in log Calabi-Yau varieties,
see the work of Gross, Pandharipande and Siebert

[
GPS10
]
and Gross, Hacking
and Keel
[
GHK15
]
in dimension
2
, and Gross and Siebert

[
GS18
]
, Keel and Yu

[
KY19
]
, and Argüz and Gross

[
AG20
]
in higher dimensions.
Given a maximal log Calabi-Yau variety

(Y,D)
,
that is, the pair of a smooth projective variety

Y
over
\mathbb{C}
and of an anticanonical normal crossing divisor
D

with a
0
-dimensional stratum,
one can construct a consistent canonical scattering diagram

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\operatorname{cl}}
by counting holomorphic maps from genus
0

holomorphic curves to
Y
whose images intersect
D
at a single point

[
GHK15
,
GS18
]
.
More precisely, these counts of holomorphic curves
are defined using logarithmic Gromov-Witten theory
[
GS13
,
AC14
]
.
The corresponding algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\operatorname{cl}}}
is then
the algebra of functions on the family of varieties mirror to
(Y,D)
. Heuristically, the integral affine manifold with singularities
B
containing
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\operatorname{cl}}

should be the basis of a special Lagrangian torus fibration on
the complement of
D
in
Y

[
SYZ96
,
Aur07
]
.

For
(Y,D)
a maximal log Calabi-Yau surface,
we explained in
[
Bou20
]
how to construct
a consistent canonical quantum scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}

in terms of log Gromov-Witten counts
of holomorphic maps from higher genus holomorphic curves to
Y

whose images intersect
D
at a single point.
The corresponding non-commutative algebra

\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}}
is a deformation quantization
of the mirror family of holomorphic symplectic surfaces constructed in
[
GHK15
]
.
The main idea of the present paper is to apply the framework of
[
Bou20
]

for
Y
a smooth cubic surface and
D
a triangle of lines on
Y
.
Before giving more details, we need to review the
general relation between skein algebras and character varieties.

1.2.3.
Skein algebras and character varieties

Let
\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
be the
SL_{2}
-character variety of the

\ell
-punctured genus
g
surface

\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
. This is an affine variety of finite type over

\mathbb{Z}
obtained as affine GIT quotient by the
SL_{2}

conjugation action of the affine variety of representations of the fundamental group

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
into
SL_{2}
.
The character variety
\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}
admits a natural Poisson structure.

Setting

A=-e^{\frac{i\hbar}{4}}
, the skein algebra

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
defines a deformation quantization
of the Poisson variety
\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
.
If
\gamma
is a multicurve on
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}

with connected components
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r}
,
then, the map sending a representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})\rightarrow SL_{2}
to
\prod_{j=1}^{r}(-\operatorname{tr}(\rho(\gamma_{j})))

defines a regular
function
f_{\gamma}

on

\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
.
The map
\gamma\mapsto f_{\gamma}
defines a ring isomorphism between the
specialization

\operatorname{Sk}_{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
of the skein algebra at
A=-1

and the ring of regular functions of
\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
.
If
\gamma
is a connected multicurve on

\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
, then the building blocks

T_{n}(\gamma)
of the bracelets basis are
quantizations of the functions
\rho\mapsto-\operatorname{tr}(\rho(\gamma)^{n})
on

\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
.

The general idea of a connection between skein algebras
and quantization goes back to Turaev
[
Tur91
]
.
Bullock

[
Bul97
]
and Przytycki and Sikora with a different
proof
[
PS00
]
showed that
\gamma\mapsto f_{\gamma}

defines a ring isomorphism between the quotient of
\operatorname{Sk}_{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})

by its nilradical and the ring of regular functions of
\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
.
The fact that the nilradical of
\operatorname{Sk}_{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
is trivial
was shown by Charles and Marché for
\ell=0

[
CM12
, Theorem 1.2]
,
and by Przytycki and Sikora

[
PS19
]
in general.

1.2.4.
Curve counting in cubic surfaces

It is classically known that the
SL_{2}
-character variety

\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

of the
4
-punctured sphere
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
can be described
explicitly as a
4
-parameters family of affine cubic surfaces:
original 19
th
century sources are
[
Vog89
,
Fri96
]
,
[
FK65
, II, Eq.(9), p298]
and more recent references
include
[
Mag80
,
Jim82
,
BG99
,
Gol09
,
GT10
]
.
Recently, Gross, Hacking, Keel and Siebert
[
GHKS19
]

proved that this family of cubic surfaces
is the result of the general mirror construction
of
[
GHK15
]

for maximal log Calabi-Yau surfaces applied
to a pair
(Y,D)
, where
Y
is a smooth projective cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}

and
D
is a triangle of lines on
Y
.
In other words, they showed that the algebra obtained
from the consistent canonical scattering diagram defined by counting genus
0

holomorphic curves in
(Y,D)
is exactly the algebra of regular functions on
\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
.

Thus, we now have two ways to produce a deformation quantization of

\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
and it is natural to compare them:
either consider the skein algebra
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
,
or consider the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}}

obtained from the consistent canonical quantum scattering diagram

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}
defined in
[
Bou20
]
by counting higher genus holomorphic curves in

(Y,D)
.

First of all, we will compute explicitly the quantum scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}
.
It involves computing higher genus log Gromov-Witten invariants of
(Y,D)
.
The corresponding calculation in genus
0
was done in

[
GHKS19
]
: exploiting a
large
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
group of birational automorphisms of
(Y,D)
,
Gross, Hacking, Keel and Siebert showed that the genus
0

calculation can be reduced to genus
0
multiple covers of
8
lines and
2
conics in

(Y,D)
. Following the same strategy,
we will prove that the higher genus calculation reduces to higher genus multiple covers of the same
8
lines and
2
conics.
The contribution of multiple covers of lines is fairly standard
but the contribution of multiple covers of the conics is more intricate
and we will use our previous work

[
Bou18
]
on higher genus log Gromov-Witten
invariants of log Calabi-Yau surfaces to evaluate it.
At the end of the day, we can phrase the result
as stating that
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}
is equal (after an appropriate specialization of variables)
to the explicit quantum scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}
.
As
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}
is consistent by
[
Bou20
]
,
this proves the consistency of
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}
.

1.2.5.
Comparison of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}
and
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

Once we know that the quantum scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}
is consistent,
we have the corresponding algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}
,
with its basis of quantum theta functions

\{\vartheta_{p}\}_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}

and structure constants expressed in terms of quantum broken lines.
It remains to construct an isomorphism of algebras

\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
matching the bracelets basis
\{\mathbf{\gamma}_{p}\}_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}
and the basis of quantum theta functions
\{\vartheta_{p}\}_{p\in B(\mathbb{Z})}
,
i.e. such that
\varphi(\vartheta_{p})=\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p})
for every
p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
.

By explicit computations with quantum broken lines in
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}
,
we will obtain an explicit presentation of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}

by generators and relations as a family of non-commutative cubic surfaces (Theorem
6.13
).
On the other hand, it was known since Bullock and Przytycki
[
BP00
]

that the description of
\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
as a family of cubic surfaces
deforms into a presentation of the skein algebra

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
as a family of non-commutative cubic surfaces
(Theorem
6.12
). Comparing these two families
of non-commutative cubic surfaces, we
will define an isomorphism of algebras

\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
.

Finally, we will have to prove that

\varphi(\vartheta_{p})=\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p})
for every
p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
. We will first prove it
for
p=(k,0)
by some explicit computation of quantum broken lines.
In particular, we will see how the recursion relation
(
2
)
defining the Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
naturally arises
from drawing quantum broken lines. To prove the general result,
we will check explicitly that
\varphi
intertwines the natural action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
via the mapping class group of
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
,
with an action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}

coming from a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-symmetry of
the quantum scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}
.
This ends our summary of the proof.

We remark that by taking the classical limit
of the statement that
\varphi(\vartheta_{p})=\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p})
for every
p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
,
we obtain that the classical theta functions
\vartheta_{p}^{\operatorname{cl}}
constructed in

[
GHK15
,
GHKS19
]

agree with the trace functions

\rho\mapsto-\operatorname{tr}(\rho(\gamma_{p_{prim}})^{k})
on the character variety

\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
, where
p=kp_{prim}
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
p_{prim}\in B(\mathbb{Z})
primitive (Corollary
3.9
).

1.2.6.
More on non-commutative cubic surfaces

We briefly comment about works related to an essential ingredient of the proof
of our main result: the presentation of
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
as a family of non-commutative cubic surfaces.
This non-commutative cubic equation has appeared in quite a number of contexts.
The present paper provides one more: the non-commutative cubic surface appears
for us as a quantum mirror in the sense of
[
Bou20
]

and as the result of calculations in higher genus log Gromov-Witten theory.

The quantization of the family of affine cubic surfaces
\operatorname{Ch}_{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

from the point of view of quantum Teichmüller theory has been studied
by Chekhov and Mazzocco
[
CM10
, Eq. (3.20)-(3.24)]
,
and by Hiatt
[
Hia10
]
.
Quantization from the cluster point of view has been
discussed by Hikami
[
Hik19b
, Eq. (7.2)-(7.3)]
.
The general relation between skein algebras and the quantum Teichmüller/cluster points of view
follows from the existence of the quantum trace map of Bonahon and Wong

[
BW11
]
(see also
[
L1̂8b
]
).

The skein algebra
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
is
isomorphic to the spherical double affine Hecke algebra (DAHA) of type

(C^{\vee}_{1},C_{1})
defined in

[
Sah99
,
NS04
,
Sto03
]
.
The explicit connection between the spherical DAHA of type
(C^{\vee}_{1},C_{1})

and the quantization of cubic surfaces was established by Oblomkov

[
Obl04
]
. Terwilliger
[
Ter13
, Proposition 16.4]

wrote down an explicit presentation of the spherical
DAHA of type
(C^{\vee}_{1},C_{1})
from which the isomorphism with
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
is clear.
A much earlier appearance of the non-commutative cubic surface
is the Askey-Wilson algebra
AW(3)

of Zhedanov
[
Zhe91
]
.
A comparison between
AW(3)
and the spherical DAHA of type
(C^{\vee}_{1},C_{1})

was done by Koornwinder
[
Koo08
]
.
More details on the relation between the skein and DAHA points of view can be found in

[
BS16
, Section 2]
,
[
BS18
, Section 2]
,
[
Hik19a
]
.

Skein algebras can also be considered in the framework of
SL_{2}
-factorization homology.
Explicit presentations of
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
and

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{1,1})
as non-commutative cubic surfaces
are recovered using this point of view by Cooke

[
Coo18
]
.

1.3.
Line operators and BPS spectrum of the
\mathcal{N}=2

N_{f}=4

SU(2)
gauge theory

In this section, which can be ignored by a
purely mathematically minded reader, we briefly discuss the
string/M-theoretic motivation for a connection between the skein algebra

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
and the enumerative geometry of curves in cubic surfaces.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a four-dimensional quantum field theory
with
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry. Such theory has in general
an interesting dynamics connecting its short-distance behaviour (UV) with its long-distance behaviour (IR).
The IR behaviour of
\mathcal{T}
is largely determined by its Seiberg-Witten geometry

\nu\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow B

[
SW94a
,
SW94b
]
, described as follows.
The special Kähler manifold with singularities
B

is the Coulomb branch of the moduli space of vacua of
\mathcal{T}
on

\mathbb{R}^{1,3}
. The hyperkähler manifold
\mathcal{M}
is the
Coulomb branch of the moduli space of vacua of
\mathcal{T}
on

\mathbb{R}^{1,2}\times S^{1}
. The map
\nu
is a complex integrable system, that is,

\nu
is holomorphic with respect to a specific complex structure

I
on
\mathcal{M}
, and the fibers of
\nu
are
holomorphic Lagrangian with respect to the

I
-holomorphic symplectic form. General fibers of
\nu

endowed with the complex structure
I
are abelian varieties.

Due to supersymmetry, particular sectors of
\mathcal{T}
have remarkable protections against arbitrary quantum corrections and so can be often computed exactly.
Examples of such protected sectors are the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}
of
\frac{1}{2}
BPS line operators and the
spectrum of BPS 1-particle states.
The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}
depends only on the UV behaviour of
\mathcal{T}
.
By wrapping around
S^{1}
, a line operator on
\mathbb{R}^{1,3}
becomes a local operator on
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
, and so its expectation value can be viewed as a function on
\mathcal{M}
.
In fact,

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}
is an algebra of functions on
\mathcal{M}
which are holomorphic for a complex structure
J
on
\mathcal{M}
with respect to which
\nu
is a special Lagrangian fibration.
By contrast, the BPS spectrum depends on a choice of vacuum
u\in B

and changes discontinuously along real codimension-one walls in
B
.

Gaiotto, Moore and Neitzke
[
GMN13
]

described how to construct a non-commutative deformation

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}^{q}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}
by twisting correlation functions
by rotations in the plane transverse to the line operators.
They also explained that, given a choice of vacuum
u\in B
,
line operators have expansions in terms of IR line operators
with coefficients given by counts of framed BPS states.
These expansions depend discontinuously on
u
: they jump when the spectrum
of framed BPS states jumps by forming bound states with (unframed) BPS states.

The same
\mathcal{N}=2
theory can often
be engineered
in several ways in string/M-theory. Given a punctured Riemann surface
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
,
one obtains a
\mathcal{N}=2
theory
\mathcal{T}_{g,\ell}
by compactifying on
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}

the six-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,0)
superconformal field theory of type
A_{1}

living, at low energy and after decoupling of gravity,
on two coincident
M5
-branes in
M
-theory
[
Gai12
]
.
The corresponding Seiberg-Witten geometry
\nu\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow B

is the Hitchin fibration on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}
of semistable
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles on

\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
(with regular singularities and given residues at the punctures).
By non-abelian Hodge theory,
\mathcal{M}
with its complex structure
J

is isomorphic to the

SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character variety of
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}

(with given conjugacy classes around the punctures).
The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}
of line operators is identified with the algebra of regular functions on the

SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character variety, and the non-commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{T}}^{q}
is
identified with the skein algebra

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
, which is physically realized
as the algebra of loop operators in quantum Liouville theory on

\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}

[
DGOT10
]
.
Explicit discussions of the families of non-commutative cubic surfaces
describing
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
and

3.3.
Recovering the skein algebra of the closed torus

As reviewed in Section

3.18
gives a description of

\mathfrak{D}_{1,1}
. Setting

in the Definition
3.12
of

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{can}}
, we obtain a trivial scattering diagrams
whose all quantum rays
\rho=(p_{\rho},f_{\rho})
have

. In particular, no broken line can bend
in such scattering diagram, and the structure constants become extremely simple.

Let

action on

straight going to infinity parallel

and
\gamma_{2}
straight going to infinity parallel to

, and the one
with
\gamma_{1}
straight going to infinity parallel
to

and
\gamma_{2}
straight going to infinity parallel to
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}p_{1}
.
Therefore, applying Equation(

\vartheta_{p_{1}}\vartheta_{p_{2}}=A^{\det(p_{1},p_{2})}\vartheta_{p_{1}+p_{2}}+A^{-\det(p_{1},p_{2})}\vartheta_{p_{1}-p_{2}}\,,

that is, we recover the product-to-sum formula

[
FG00
]

(see also
[
QR15
]

Note that in the limit where the scattering diagram on

[

3.4.
Application to quantum cluster algebras

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
,
that is, that the positivity of the structure constants
of the bracelets basis of the skein algebra
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})

implies the positivity of the structure constants
defined by the quantum duality map
\hat{\mathbb{I}}
of

[
AK17
]
. We use the notations
introduced in Section
1.1.5
.

The quantum duality map
\hat{\mathbb{I}}
is defined in

[
AK17
]
using the quantum
trace map of Bonahon and Wong

[
BW11
]
. Given an ideal triangulation
T
of
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}
,
there is a corresponding quantum trace map,
which is an injective algebra morphism

\operatorname{Tr}_{T}\colon\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})\longrightarrow\hat{\mathcal{Z}}_{T}\,,

where
\hat{\mathcal{Z}}_{T}
is the square root Chekhov-Fock algebra.

The set of tropical points
\mathcal{A}_{SL_{2},\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}}(\mathbb{Z}^{t})

is the set of
even integral laminations on
\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell}

[
FG06
]
.
For every

,
we can write uniquely

l=\sum_{j}k_{j}l_{j}

where the
l_{j}
are connected multicurves
with distinct homotopy classes,

k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
if
l_{j}
is not peripheral, and

k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
if
l_{j}
is peripheral.
By Definition 3.11 of

[
AK17
]
, we have

\hat{\mathbb{I}}(l)=\prod_{j}\hat{\mathbb{I}}(k_{j}l_{j})
.
Therefore, to prove Theorem
1.4
,
it is enough
to prove the positivity of the structure constants
appearing in the products of the form

\hat{\mathbb{I}}(kl)\hat{\mathbb{I}}(k^{\prime}l^{\prime})

where
l
and
l^{\prime}
are connected multicurves,

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
(resp.
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
)
if
l
(resp.
l^{\prime}
) is not peripheral,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
(resp.
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
) if
l
(resp.
l^{\prime}
)
is peripheral.

By Lemma 3.25 of
[
AK17
]
, for
l
a
peripheral connected multicurve,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
and
l^{\prime}
a lamination, we have

\hat{\mathbb{I}}(kl)\hat{\mathbb{I}}(l^{\prime})=\hat{\mathbb{I}}(kl+l^{\prime})
.
It follows that it is enough to prove the positivity
of the structure constants appearing in the products of the form

\hat{\mathbb{I}}(kl)\hat{\mathbb{I}}(k^{\prime}l^{\prime})

where
l
and
l^{\prime}
are
non-peripheral connected multicurves and
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

So, let us consider
l
and
l^{\prime}
non-peripheral
connected multicurves and
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
By Definitions 3.4 and 3.8 of

[
AK17
]
,we have

\hat{\mathbb{I}}(kl)=\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(T_{k}(l))

and
\hat{\mathbb{I}}(k^{\prime}l^{\prime})=\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(T_{k^{\prime}}(l^{\prime}))
.
Therefore, assuming the positivity
of the structure constants of the bracelets basis of

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{g,\ell})
, we have

(48)

\hat{\mathbb{I}}(kl)\hat{\mathbb{I}}(k^{\prime}l^{\prime})=\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(T_{k}(l))\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(T_{k^{\prime}}(l^{\prime}))=\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(T_{k}(l)T_{k^{\prime}}(l^{\prime}))=\sum_{\gamma}C_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}^{\gamma}\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(\mathbf{T}(\gamma))\,,

where the sum is over finitely many multicurves
\gamma
and

C_{kl,kl^{\prime}}^{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[A^{\pm}]
.
Write

\gamma=\gamma_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{r}^{n_{r}}

with
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r}

all distinct isotopy classes of connected multicurves,

\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{s}
non-peripheral and

\gamma_{s+1},\cdots,\gamma_{r}
peripheral, and

.
We have

(49)

\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(\mathbf{T}(\gamma))=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(T_{n_{j}}(\gamma_{j}))=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\hat{\mathbb{I}}(n_{j}\gamma_{j})\prod_{k=s+1}^{r}(\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(\gamma_{k}))^{n_{k}}\,.

For
s+1\leq k\leq r
,
\gamma_{k}
is peripheral, and so
by Lemma 3.24 of
[
AK17
]
, we have

(50)

\hat{\mathbb{I}}(\gamma_{k})+\hat{\mathbb{I}}(-\gamma_{k})=\operatorname{Tr}_{T}(\gamma_{k})\,.

Therefore, using again Lemma 3.25 of
[
AK17
]
, we have

(51)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{I}}(kl)\hat{\mathbb{I}}(k^{\prime}l^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}C_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}^{\gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\hat{\mathbb{I}}(n_{j}\gamma_{j})\prod_{k=s+1}^{r}(\hat{\mathbb{I}}(\gamma_{k})+\hat{\mathbb{I}}(-\gamma_{k}))^{n_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}C_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}^{\gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\hat{\mathbb{I}}(n_{j}\gamma_{j})\prod_{k=s+1}^{r}\left(\sum_{a=0}^{n_{k}}\binom{n_{k}}{a}\hat{\mathbb{I}}((2a-n_{k})\gamma_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}C_{kl,k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}^{\gamma}\sum_{a_{s+1}=0}^{n_{s+1}}\cdots\sum_{a_{r}=0}^{n_{r}}\binom{n_{s+1}}{a_{s+1}}\cdots\binom{n_{r}}{a_{r}}\hat{\mathbb{I}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}n_{j}\gamma_{j}+\sum_{k=s+1}^{r}(2a_{k}-n_{k})\gamma_{k}\right)\,,

and so, under our assumption that

, the structure constants of

\hat{\mathbb{I}}
belong to
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[A^{\pm}]
. On the other hand,
by Theorem 1.2 of
[
AK17
]
, these structure constants belong to

\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm 2}]=\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]
.
Thus, the structure constants of

\hat{\mathbb{I}}
belong to
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]

and this proves Theorem
1.4
.

The pair

In particular, the three two-dimensional cones

We refer to

Let

Theorem 6.14
.

For every
p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

(134)

\varphi(\vartheta_{p})=\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{p})\,.

Proof.

We first prove that, for every
k\geq 0
, we have

(135)

\varphi(\vartheta_{kv_{1}})=\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{kp_{1}})\,.

The isotopy class
\gamma_{kp_{1}}
is the class of
k

disjoint curves isotopic to
\gamma_{1}
,
and so
\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{kp_{1}})=T_{k}(\gamma_{kp_{1}})
.
Recall that the Chebyshev polynomials

T_{k}(x)
are defined by
T_{0}(x)=1
,
T_{1}(x)=x
,
T_{2}(x)=x^{2}-2
,
and for every
k\geq 2
,

T_{k+1}(x)=xT_{k}(x)-T_{k-1}(x)
.

We prove that
\varphi(\vartheta_{kv_{1}})=\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{kp_{1}})
for every
k\geq 0
by induction on
k
.
The result holds trivially for
k=0
as
\vartheta_{0}=1
and
\mathbf{T}(\gamma_{0})=1)
.
It holds for
k=1
by construction of
\varphi
:

\varphi(\vartheta_{v_{1}})=\gamma_{v_{1}}=T_{1}(\gamma_{v_{1}})
.
It also holds for
k=2
: using Lemma

5.2
, we have

(136)

\varphi(\vartheta_{2v_{1}})=\varphi(\vartheta_{v_{1}}^{2}-2)=\varphi(\vartheta_{v_{1}})^{2}-2=\gamma_{v_{1}}^{2}-2=T_{2}(\gamma_{v_{1}})\,.

Let
k\geq 2
and assume that the result holds for all
k^{\prime}\leq k
.
Then, using
Lemma
5.3
, we have

(137)

\displaystyle\varphi(\vartheta_{(k+1)v_{1}})

\displaystyle=\varphi(\vartheta_{v_{1}}\vartheta_{kv_{1}}-\vartheta_{(k-1)v_{1}})=\varphi(\vartheta_{v_{1}})\varphi(\vartheta_{kv_{1}})-\varphi(\vartheta_{(k-1)v_{1}})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{v_{1}}T_{k}(\gamma_{v_{1}})-T_{k-1}(\gamma_{v_{1}})=T_{k+1}(\gamma_{v_{1}})\,,

and so the result holds for
k+1
.

We now explain how to deduce the result for general
p\in B(\mathbb{Z})

from the result for
p=kv_{1}
using
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-symmetry.
In order to simplify the notation, we write
R
for

\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}][R_{1,0},R_{0,1},R_{1,1},y]
.
Recall from the proof of Theorem
6.10
that

PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts through its finite quotient
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
on

R

by
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-algebra automorphisms permuting

R_{1,0}
,
R_{0,1}
,
R_{1,1}
, and fixing
y
.
We define below actions of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on

\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}
and
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
lifting the action on
R
.

For every
M\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we define a lift
\Psi_{M}
to
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}

of the action of
M
on
R
by

(138)

\Psi_{M}(\vartheta_{p})\coloneqq\vartheta_{Mp}\,,

where
p\mapsto Mp
is the action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
B(\mathbb{Z})
.
We claim that
\Psi_{M}
is an automorphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}
as
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-algebra.
Indeed, the Definition
3.5
of
\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}

has the following manifest
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-symmetry:
for every
M\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
p=(m,n)\in B(\mathbb{Z})

with
m
and
n
coprime,
the function attached to the quantum ray
\rho_{Mp}
is obtained
by applying the action of
M\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
R

to the coefficients of the function attached to the quantum ray

\rho_{p}
. The compatibility of
\Psi_{M}
with the product structure of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}

then follows from the Definition
2.13
of the product of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}

in terms of quantum broken lines for

\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}
. Thus,
M\mapsto\Psi_{M}
defines an action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on

\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}
by automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-algebras lifting the action on
R
.

On the other hand, given the geometric definition of the skein algebra,
there is a natural action of the mapping class group
MCG(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

of
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
on
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
by
automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm}]
-algebras.
Recall that the mapping class group is the group of isotopy classes of
orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms.
The mapping class group
MCG(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

contains a natural subgroup isomorphic to
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
which is coming from the description of
\mathbb{S}_{0,4}
as a quotient of a
4
-punctured torus
by an involution, and from the fact that
the mapping class group of the torus is
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
In fact
MCG(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
is a semi-direct product of

PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(see e.g. Section
2.2.5 of
[
FM12
]
).
The action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on

\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
is reviewed at the beginning of
Section 4 of
[
BMP+18
]
: this action

M\mapsto\Phi_{M}
lifts the action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
R
and satisfies

(139)

\Phi_{M}(\gamma_{p})=\gamma_{Mp}\,,

for every
M\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
p\in B(\mathbb{Z})
.

We claim that
\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})

intertwines between the actions
\Psi
and
\Phi
of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on

\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}
and
\operatorname{Sk}_{A}(\mathbb{S}_{0,4})
, that is

(140)

\varphi\circ\Psi_{M}=\Phi_{M}\circ\varphi

for every
M\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
It is enough to check it for the generators
S
and
T
of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
given in (

The result is clear for

,

,

,
and so

follows by combining Equations

138
) and (

Similarly, we have

,

for

But we need an extra argument for

one needs to show that

in
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{D}_{0,4}}
by Lemma

in

[
BMP+18

.

We can now end the proof of Theorem

Let

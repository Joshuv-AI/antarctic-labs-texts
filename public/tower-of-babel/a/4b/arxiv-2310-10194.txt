Regularizing Effect for a Class of Maxwell-Schrödinger Systems
Ayana Pinheiro de Castro Santana, Luís Henrique de Miranda

Abstract
In this paper we prove existence and regularity of weak solutions for the following system { − div ( M ( x ) ∇ u ) + g ( x , u , v ) = f in Ω − div ( M ( x ) ∇ v ) = h ( x , u , v ) in Ω u = v = 0 on ∂ Ω , \displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mbox{div}(M(x)\nabla u)+g(x,u,v)=f\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega\\ -\mbox{div}(M(x)\nabla v)=h(x,u,v)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega\\ \ \ \ \ \ u=v=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \partial\Omega,\end{cases} where Ω \Omega is an open bounded subset of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , for N > 2 N>2 , f ∈ L m ​ ( Ω ) f\in L^{m}(\Omega) , m > 1 m>1 and g g , h h are two Carathéodory functions. We prove that under appropriate conditions on g g and h h , there exist solutions which escape the predicted regularity by the classical Stampacchia’s theory causing the so-called regularizing effect. 1.2
Main results

Our main contributions are twofold. First we consider nonlinearities which are more general and second we address the regularizing effect in the case where there is the presence of a second parameter of coupling on the nonlinearities, as it was conjectured in
[
13
]
. However, as it turned out, we discover the presence of a ramification on the gain of regularity depending on the interplay between the data and the coupling parameters, below the known results for systems related to (
P

1.5

In the literature, we say that there exist regularizing effects in a solution of a problem or a system, whenever its regularity escapes the predicted one according to the standard Stampacchia or Calderón–Zygmund theories. In order to summarize this justification, we introduce the following definition on “regularized solutions”.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
F\in L^{m}(\Omega)
where
1\leqslant m<\frac{N}{2}

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\mbox{div}(M(x)\nabla w)=F(x).

where

b)

If

where

is Sobolev regularized.

Despite that the justification of the latter definition is tacit, for the convenience of the reader we explain it. Indeed, for instance, we know by Stamppachia’s classical regularity theory that, if

with

1.1

then once again from Stampacchia’s theory if

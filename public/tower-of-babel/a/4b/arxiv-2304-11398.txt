On the relation between decay and regularity of solutions to the modified Korteweg-de Vries equation
Alexander Muñoz, Ademir Pastor

Abstract
. This work is devoted to study the relation between the regularity index and the decay for solutions of the modified Korteweg-de Vries equation in weighted Sobolev spaces. We show if a solution belongs to a Sobolev space and has a large polynomial decay, compared to the regularity, then actually it is more regular than initially considered.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35Q53, 35R25

1.
Introduction

The existence of solutions to nonlinear dispersive equations have been studied for several years as a direct application of techniques in which the Fourier transform is one of the main ingredients.
The best suited space in terms of Fourier transform is the well known Schwartz class in which functions display both, high regularity and fast decay. This relation is also presented by the Fourier transform in which derivatives in space become weights in the frequency space.

One natural question that arises is if a certain decay is preserved by the flow of a dispersive model. Here, we will investigate the relation between the regularity and the decay of solutions. Particular attention will be given to the following Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u+u^{k}\partial_{x}u=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(x,t)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
k=1,2
. If
k=1
we have the Kortweg-de Vries (KdV) equation and in case
k=2
we obtain the modified KdV (mKdV) equation. The KdV equation was derived in
[
12
]
as a model describing the propagation of waves in one dimensional dispersive media. This equation has been widely studied in the literature, see for instance
[
2
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
and the references therein.

Our main interest here will be on the mKdV equation. Local and global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geqslant 1/4
, for such equation was obtained in
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
. Recently, this index was pushed down in
[
5
]
, where the authors established the (sharp) global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, for
s>-1/2
in the sense that the solution map extends uniquely from Schwartz space
to a continuous map in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
.

Denote with
U(t)f
the unitary group associated to the linear part of (
1.1
), that is,

U(t)f(x):=\left(e^{it\xi^{3}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\right)^{\vee}(x).

(1.2)

Taking into account the Duhamel formulation of solutions

u(x,t)=U(t)u_{0}(x)-\int_{0}^{t}U(t-t^{\prime})u(x,t^{\prime})^{k}\partial_{x}u(x,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(1.3)

to study expressions of the form
|x|^{b}u(x,t)
it is necessary to understand the relation between the unitary group
U(t)
and the weights
|x|^{b}
. In
[
9
]
, using the operators
\Gamma_{t}:=x-3t\partial_{x}^{2}
and
L:=\partial_{t}+\partial_{x}^{3}
, Kato showed that
[\Gamma_{t},L]=0
and used this to note that both,
U(t)xu_{0}
and
\Gamma_{t}U(t)u_{0}
, are solutions of the problem

\begin{cases}Lu=0,\\
u(0)=xu_{0}.\end{cases}

Therefore, it follows that
U(t)xu_{0}=\Gamma_{t}U(t)u_{0}
. The latter can be translated into the formula

xU(t)u_{0}(x)=U(t)xu_{0}(x)+3tU(t)(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{0})(x).

(1.4)

From this, it is expected that the relation between the regularity index
s
and the decay
b
of a solution, at least in the case where these parameters are integer numbers, is
s\geqslant 2b
.

Recently, in
[
4
]
, using one version of the Stein derivative (which does not directly depend on the Fourier transform), the formula in (
1.4
) was generalized to weights with fractional powers. It was proven that for
b\in(0,1)
we have

|x|^{b}U(t)u_{0}(x)=U(t)(|x|^{b}u_{0})(x)+U(t)\{\Phi_{t,b}(\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi))\}^{\vee}(x),

(1.5)

where the residual term
\{\Phi_{t,b}(\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi))\}^{\vee}
is well behaved in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
in the sense detailed in Theorem
2.2
below. Then, we can also expect the relation
s\geqslant 2b
for weights with fractional powers.

For a formulation analogous to (
1.5
) for a wide family of dispersive models and several dimensions we refer the reader to Theorem 1.1 in
[
13
]
.

The main topic of this work is then to establish the fact the relation
s\geqslant 2b
is optimal for the mKdV equation in the sense that if the solution has a decay that exceeds
s/2
, say,
b=s/2+\varepsilon
, then actually the solution is more regular than initially considered. In particular, the index of regularity would be
s+2\varepsilon
. This will be cleared up in the statement of our main result and its consequences. (See for instance Corollary
3.1
below).

The first result in this direction is due to Isaza, Linares and Ponce
[
8
]
for the KdV equation. It reads as follows:

Theorem A
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
be the global solution of the IVP (
1.1
) with
k=1
. If there exist
\alpha>0
and two different times
t_{0},\ t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

|x|^{\alpha}u(x,t_{0}),\ |x|^{\alpha}u(x,t_{1})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

then
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{R}))
.

An application of the same principle used in the proof of Theorem
A
to the fifth-order KdV equation

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{5}u+u\partial_{x}u=0

was done by Bustamante, Jiménez and Mejía in
[
1
]
.

The proof of Theorem
A
consists in a bootstrap argument depending on the size of
\alpha
. In each step the authors perform energy estimates in an accurate way to obtain decay for the solution and its derivative. For instance, in the first step (assuming
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
) they established that for almost every
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
it follows that
\partial_{x}u(t)\langle x\rangle^{\alpha-1/2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
,
u(t)\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. By considering
f:=\langle x\rangle^{\alpha-1/2}u(t^{*})
, for some
t^{*}\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
, the latter implies that
Jf
and
\langle x\rangle^{1/2}f
are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Using interpolation, the following bound was obtained:

\left\|J^{\theta}\left(\langle x\rangle^{(1-\theta)\frac{1}{2}}f\right)\right\|_{2}\leqslant c\|Jf\|_{2}^{\theta}\|\langle x\rangle^{1/2}f\|_{2}^{1-\theta},\ \ \ \ \theta\in(0,1).

(1.6)

By requiring that
(1-\theta)/2+\alpha-1/2=0
, that is,
\theta=2\alpha
they proved that
u(t^{*})\in H^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
. The idea is then to repeat the above procedure in each step with an interval of length
1/2
for
\alpha
.

1.1.
Main Result

Based on the proof of Theorem
A
and taking into account some ideas from Theorem 1.6 in
[
1
]
, we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
v_{0}\in H^{1/4}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
v\in C\left([0,T];H^{1/4}(\mathbb{R})\right)
be the solution of the IVP (
1.1
) with
k=2
provided by Theorem
2.5
. Assume there exist
t_{0},t_{1}\in[0,T]
with
t_{0}<t_{1}
and
\alpha>0
such that

|x|^{\alpha}v(t_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\mbox{ and }|x|^{\alpha}v(t_{1})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

(1.7)

Then
v\in C\left([0,T];H^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\right)
.

Note that in Theorem
1.1
when
\alpha\in(0,1/8]
there is no gain of extra regularity. This is consistent with the weighted local theory in which the solution persists in
H^{1/4}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2b}dx)
whenever
b\leqslant 1/8
. Another way to formulate Theorem
1.1
is to replace (
1.7
) with

|x|^{1/4+\alpha}v(t_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\mbox{ and }|x|^{1/4+\alpha}v(t_{1})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

and the conclusion
v\in C\left([0,T];H^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\right)
with
v\in C\left([0,T];H^{\frac{1}{4}+2\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\right)
instead.

Remark 1
.

We may interpret Theorem
1.1
as an ill-posedness result in the following sense: assume
v_{0}
belongs to
Y_{b}:=\left(H^{1/4}(\mathbb{R})\setminus H^{1/4^{+}}(\mathbb{R})\right)\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2b}dx)
, for
b\in\mathbb{R}
with
b>1/8
, then the corresponding solution of the mKdV equation does not belong to
Y_{b}
.

In
[
8
]
the authors suggested that the proof of Theorem
A
extends to the mKdV. It appears that some adjustments to the bootstrap argument are required to rise according to the size of
\alpha
. For instance, if
\alpha>1/2
the same choice of
\theta
in (
1.6
) might not be the best due to the constrain
\theta\leqslant 1
. Moreover, regardless of the choice of
\theta
, the most regular scenario for
J^{\theta}
leads to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

This concern exhibits the needing of increase the regularity over
f
, that is, to get a higher estimate than
J^{1}f
; while the role of
\alpha
is re-escalated to fit
(1-\theta)/2+\tilde{\alpha}-1/2=0
(for some
\tilde{\alpha}
) with
\theta\in(0,1)
. One natural way of doing this is as follows: in case
\alpha\in(\frac{r}{2},\frac{r+1}{2}]
we can take
\partial_{x}^{r}
to the mKdV equation and prove that
\partial_{x}^{r+1}u(t)\langle x\rangle^{\tilde{\alpha}-1/2}
is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha-r/2\in(0,1/2]
.
By setting
f:=\langle x\rangle^{\tilde{\alpha}-1/2}u(t^{*})
it might be seen that
J^{r+1}f
and
\langle x\rangle^{1/2}f
are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Interpolating as in (
1.6
) we would get

\left\|J^{(r+1)\theta}\left(\langle x\rangle^{(1-\theta)\frac{1}{2}}f\right)\right\|_{2}\leqslant c\|J^{r+1}f\|_{2}^{\theta}\|\langle x\rangle^{1/2}f\|_{2}^{1-\theta},\ \ \ \ \theta\in(0,1).

From the condition
(1-\theta)/2+\tilde{\alpha}-1/2=0
we obtain
\theta=2\alpha-r
, which is in
(0,1)
. Unfortunately this interpolation will not lead to the best estimate for
J^{s}u
because
(r+1)\theta
is less than or equal to the expected gain of regularity of
2\alpha
and only attains it when
\alpha=(r+1)/2
.

Given that only an increase in the regularity does not resolve on its own, in the proof of our main theorem we not only increase the spatial derivatives of
u
but we also consider some decay for them. We manage to prove that in the general case
\alpha\in(\frac{r}{2},\frac{r+1}{2}]
, for almost every
t
in a subinterval of
[t_{0},t_{1}]
we have

\langle x\rangle^{\tilde{\alpha}-1/2}\partial_{x}^{r+1}u(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\langle x\rangle^{\tilde{\alpha}}\partial_{x}^{r}u(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

(1.8)

By considering now
f:=\langle x\rangle^{\tilde{\alpha}-1/2}\partial_{x}^{r}u(t^{*})
(for some
t^{*}\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
) it is noted that
Jf
and
\langle x\rangle^{1/2}f
are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore interpolating analogously to (
1.6
) with
\theta=2\tilde{\alpha}
we would obtain
J^{2\tilde{\alpha}}\partial_{x}^{r}u(t^{*})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, which is to say
u(t^{*})\in H^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we introduce some notation and give the preliminary theory in order to prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
3
we breakdown the proof and enunciate some consequences.

2.1.
Notation

We use

to denote several constants that may vary from line to line. Dependence on parameters is indicated using subscripts. We write

when there exists a constant

such that

. For a real number

we denote with

the quantity

for an arbitrary small

.

The set

with respect to the measure

. The norm in

for the classical fractional derivative

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof is presented in four cases, in which the first three are required to be done explicitly because of technical details involving the size of the truncated weights with respect to the regularity of the solution. The fourth case provides a construction of the solution in a general setting.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Without loss of generality we assume
t_{0}=0
.

3.1.
Case
\alpha\in(0,1/2]

Let
\{v_{0m}\}_{m}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a sequence converging to
v_{0}
in
H^{1/4}(\mathbb{R})
. Denote with
v_{m}(\cdot)\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
the corresponding solution provided by Theorem
2.5
with initial data
v_{0m}
. By regularity of the data-solution map we can assume all the
v_{m}
’s are defined in
[0,T]
with
v_{m}
converging to
v
in
C\left([0,T];H^{1/4}(\mathbb{R})\right)
.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

\tilde{\tilde{\phi}}_{0,N}^{\alpha}(x):=\begin{cases}\langle x\rangle^{2\alpha}-1\hskip 14.22636ptx\in[0,N],\\
(2N)^{2\alpha}\hskip 25.60747ptx\in[3N,\infty).\end{cases}

Let
\tilde{\phi}_{0,N}^{\alpha}
be a smooth regularization of
\tilde{\tilde{\phi}}_{0,N}^{\alpha}
, defined in
[0,\infty)
, such that for
j=1,2,\dots
we have
\left|\partial_{x}^{j}\tilde{\phi}_{0,N}^{\alpha}\right|\leqslant c
with
c
independent of
N
. Set
\phi_{N}\equiv\phi_{0,N}^{\alpha}
to be the odd extension of
\tilde{\phi}_{0,N}^{\alpha}
, that is,

\phi_{N}(x):=\begin{cases}\tilde{\phi}_{0,N}^{\alpha}(x)\hskip 31.29802ptx\in[0,\infty],\\
-\tilde{\phi}_{0,N}^{\alpha}(-x)\hskip 14.22636ptx\in(-\infty,0).\end{cases}

(3.1)

Note that
\phi_{N}^{\prime}\geqslant 0
.

Take the mKdV equation for
v_{m}
and multiply it by
v_{m}\phi_{N}
. After integration by parts in space we get:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int v_{m}^{2}\phi_{N}dx+\frac{3}{2}\int(\partial_{x}v_{m})^{2}\phi_{N}^{\prime}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int v_{m}^{2}\phi_{N}^{(3)}dx-\frac{1}{4}\int v_{m}^{4}\phi_{N}^{\prime}dx=0.

(3.2)

Integrating over
[0,t_{1}]
we get:

\int v_{m}^{2}(t_{1})\phi_{N}dx-\int v_{0m}^{2}\phi_{N}dx+3\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int(\partial_{x}v_{m})^{2}\phi_{N}^{\prime}dxdt-\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int v_{m}^{2}\phi_{N}^{(3)}dxdt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int v_{m}^{4}\phi_{N}^{\prime}dxdt=0.

(3.3)

For
m
large enough we have

\left|-\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int v_{m}^{2}\phi_{N}^{(3)}dxdt\right|\leqslant c\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int v^{2}_{m}dxdt\leqslant c\|v_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{x{t_{1}}}}^{2}\leqslant c\|v_{0m}\|_{L^{2}_{xt_{1}}}^{2}\leqslant 2ct_{1}\|v_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

(3.4)

Also,

\left|-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int v^{4}_{m}\phi_{n}^{\prime}dxdt\right|\leqslant c\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int v_{m}^{4}dxdt\leqslant ct_{1}\|v_{m}\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{x}L^{\infty}_{t_{1}}}\leqslant c\|v_{m}\|_{L^{4}_{x}L^{\infty}_{t_{1}}}^{4}\leqslant 2\|v\|_{L^{4}_{x}L^{\infty}_{t_{1}}}^{4}.

(3.5)

From (
3.3
)-(
3.5
) we get that for
m\gg 1
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int(\partial_{x}v_{m})^{2}\phi_{N}^{\prime}dxdt&\leqslant c(\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{2}+\|v\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{x}L^{\infty}_{t_{1}}})+\|v_{0}\phi_{N}^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|v(t_{1})\phi_{N}^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\\
&\leqslant c(\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{2}+\|v\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{x}L^{\infty}_{t_{1}}}+\|\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}v(t_{1})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}).\end{split}

(3.6)

Thus

\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int(\partial_{x}v_{m})^{2}\phi_{N}^{\prime}dxdt\leqslant M,

(3.7)

where
M>0
depends on
\|v_{0}\|_{Z_{1/4,\alpha}}
,
\|v(t_{1})\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
and
\|v\|_{L^{4}_{x}L^{\infty}_{t_{1}}}
.

We claim that, for any fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the left-hand side of (
3.6
) actually converges to

\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\phi_{N}^{\prime}dxdt

as

. Indeed,

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int\left[(\partial_{x}v_{m})^{2}-(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\right]\phi_{N}^{\prime}dxdt\right|&\leqslant c\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{[-3N,3N]}|\partial_{x}v_{m}-\partial_{x}v||\partial_{x}v_{m}+\partial_{x}v|dxdt\\
&\hskip-28.45274pt\leqslant c\|\partial_{x}v_{m}-\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{t_{1}}L^{2}_{x}[-3N,3N]}\|\partial_{x}v_{m}+\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{2}_{t_{1}}L^{2}_{x}[-3N,3N]}\\
&\hskip-28.45274pt\leqslant c_{N}\|\partial_{x}v_{m}-\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t_{1}}}\left(\|\partial_{x}v_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t_{1}}}+\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t_{1}}}\right)\\
&\hskip-8.53581pt\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{}0,\end{split}

(3.8)

where we used (
2.8
) together with the continuous dependence on the initial data.

In view of (
3.7
), we therefore have

\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int(\partial_{x}v)\phi_{N}^{\prime}dxdt\leqslant M.

Since
\phi_{N}^{\prime}
is even and for

(3.9)

Moreover,

(3.10)

From (
3.9
) and (
3.10

m\gg 1
,

\begin{split}|B_{k,j}|&\leqslant c\|\langle x\rangle^{\beta-n/2}w_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xt_{1}}}\|\langle x\rangle^{\beta-n/2}\partial_{x}^{n+1-k}v_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{xt_{1}}}\|\partial_{x}^{j}v_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x{t_{1}}}}\|\partial_{x}^{k-j}v_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x{t_{1}}}}\\
&\leqslant c\|v_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t_{1}}H^{n}}\|\langle x\rangle^{\beta-n/2}w_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xt_{1}}}\|\langle x\rangle^{\beta-n/2}\partial_{x}^{n+1-k}v_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{xt_{1}}}\\
&\leqslant M.\end{split}

With these estimates of the nonlinear term in hand, it can be seen (as in (
3.2
)-(
3.10
)) that

\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int(\partial_{x}^{n+1}v)^{2}\langle x\rangle^{2\beta-(n+1)}dxdt<\infty,

which implies

\langle x\rangle^{\beta-(n+1)/2}\partial_{x}^{n+1}v(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\mbox{ for almost every }t\in[0,t_{1}],

(3.61)

which establishes our claim. From (
3.59
) and (
3.61
) the inductive step follows, and the proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

Note that, under minor modifications, the proof of Theorem
1.1
remains valid for
v_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for any
s\geqslant 1/4
. In fact, the following corollary is a direct consequence of this fact.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
v_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geqslant 1/4
. Let
v
be the global in-time solution provided by Theorem
2.6
. Assume there exist
t_{0},t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha>0
such that

|x|^{\frac{s}{2}+\alpha}v(t_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\mbox{ and }|x|^{\frac{s}{2}+\alpha}v(t_{1})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Then
v\in C\left([-T,T];H^{s+2\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\right)
, for any
T>0
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume
T>\max\{|t_{0}|,|t_{1}|\}
. Note that
u_{0}\in H^{{\frac{1}{4}}}(\mathbb{R})
with the respective solution
v\in C([-T,T];H^{{\frac{1}{4}}}(\mathbb{R}))
so that
|x|^{\tilde{\alpha}}v(t_{i})
are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for
i=0,1
and
\tilde{\alpha}=\frac{s}{2}+\alpha>0
. By Theorem
1.1
we obtain that
v\in C([-T,T];H^{2\tilde{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}))
. The result then follows since
2\tilde{\alpha}=s+2\alpha
.
∎

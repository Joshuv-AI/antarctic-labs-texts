Introduction

Our goal is to derive, from elementary identities, some presumably new Fibonacci and Lucas identities including binomial coefficients. The research is a continuation of the recent works by Adegoke
[
1
,
2
]
and Adegoke et al.
[
3
]
.
The results are similar to those found in the classical articles of Carlitz and Ferns
[
5
]
, Hoggatt et al.
[
6
]
, Layman
[
9
]
, Carlitz
[
4
]
and Long
[
10
]
.

Recall that the Fibonacci numbers
F_{j}
and the Lucas numbers
L_{j}
are defined, for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, through the recurrence relations

F_{j}=F_{j-1}+F_{j-2},\quad j\geq 2,\quad F_{0}=0,\,\,F_{1}=1,

L_{j}=L_{j-1}+L_{j-2},\quad j\geq 2,\quad L_{0}=2,\,\,L_{1}=1,

with

F_{-j}=(-1)^{j-1}F_{j},\qquad L_{-j}=(-1)^{j}L_{j}.

Throughout this paper, we denote the golden ratio by
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
and write
\beta=-\frac{1}{\alpha}
,
so that
\alpha\beta=-1
and
\alpha+\beta=1
. Binet formulas for the Fibonacci and Lucas numbers are

F_{j}=\frac{{\alpha^{j}-\beta^{j}}}{{\alpha-\beta}},\quad L_{j}=\alpha^{j}+\beta^{j},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

(1)

Koshy
[
7
]
and Vajda
[
11
]
have written excellent books dealing with Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.

Here are a couple of results to whet the readers appetite for reading on:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{2n-1}{2k-1}F_{2k-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}F_{2n-1}L_{n-1}L_{n},&\text{$n$ odd;}\\
\frac{5}{2}F_{2n-1}F_{n-1}F_{n},&\text{$n$ even,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\binom{2n-1}{2k-1}L_{2k-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(L_{4n-2}-L_{2n-1}),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2n}{2k}F_{3k+r}F_{3k+s}

\displaystyle=2^{2n-1}F_{n}F_{3n+r+s},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n}/{2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}{2^{n-2k+1}}F_{2k+s}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{s+1}F_{2n-s}+5^{{n}/{2}}F_{n+s},&\text{\rm$n$ even;}\\
(-1)^{s+1}F_{2n-s}+5^{({n-1})/{2}}L_{n+s},&\text{\rm$n$ odd.}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{2n}{2k}L_{j(2rk+s)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sqrt{5^{n}}F_{jr}^{n}L_{jrn+js}\cos\big(2n\arctan(\alpha^{jr})\big),&\text{\rm$jr$ odd;}\\
L_{jr}^{n}L_{jrn+js}\cos\big(2n\arctan(\alpha^{jr})\big),&\text{\rm$jr$ even, $n$ even;}\\
\sqrt{5}L_{jr}^{n}F_{jrn+js}\cos\big(2n\arctan(\alpha^{jr})\big),&\text{\rm$jr$ even, $n$ odd.}\end{cases}

Required identities

Lemma 1

(K. Adegoke
[
1
]
)
.

For real or complex
z
, let a given well-behaved
function
h(z)
have in its domain the representation
h(z)=\sum\limits_{k=c_{1}}^{c_{2}}{g(k)z^{f(k)}}
, where
f(k)
and
g(k)
are given real sequences and
-\infty\leq c_{1}\leq c_{2}\leq\infty
. Let
j
be an integer. Then

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=c_{1}}^{c_{2}}{g(k)z^{f(k)}F_{jf(k)}}

\displaystyle=h(\alpha^{j}z)-h(\beta^{j}z),

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=c_{1}}^{c_{2}}{g(k)z^{f(k)}L_{jf(k)}}

\displaystyle=h(\alpha^{j}z)+h(\beta^{j}z).

(3)

Lemma 2

(S. Vajda
[
11
]
)
.

For integers
r
and
s
,

\displaystyle F_{r+s}+(-1)^{s}F_{r-s}=L_{s}F_{r},\qquad\quad F_{r+s}-(-1)^{s}F_{r-s}=F_{s}L_{r},

(4)

\displaystyle L_{r+s}+(-1)^{s}L_{r-s}=L_{s}L_{r},\qquad\quad L_{r+s}-(-1)^{s}L_{r-s}=5F_{s}F_{r}.

(5)

If
u
and
v
are integers having the same parity, then identities (
4
) and (
5
) can be put in the useful versions:

\displaystyle F_{u}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\frac{u-v}{2}}F_{v}=L_{\frac{u-v}{2}}F_{\frac{u+v}{2}},\qquad\quad F_{u}-(-1)^{\frac{u-v}{2}}F_{v}=F_{\frac{u-v}{2}}L_{\frac{u+v}{2}},

\displaystyle L_{u}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\frac{u-v}{2}}L_{v}=L_{\frac{u-v}{2}}L_{\frac{u+v}{2}},\qquad\quad L_{u}-(-1)^{\frac{u-v}{2}}L_{v}=5F_{\frac{u-v}{2}}F_{\frac{u+v}{2}},

and

\displaystyle F_{u}+F_{v}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}L_{\frac{u-v}{2}}F_{\frac{u+v}{2}},&\text{$\frac{u-v}{2}$ even;}\\
F_{\frac{u-v}{2}}L_{\frac{u+v}{2}},&\text{$\frac{u-v}{2}$ odd,}\end{cases}\qquad\quad F_{u}-F_{v}=\begin{cases}L_{\frac{u-v}{2}}F_{\frac{u+v}{2}},&\text{$\frac{u-v}{2}$ odd;}\\
F_{\frac{u-v}{2}}L_{\frac{u+v}{2}},&\text{$\frac{u-v}{2}$ even,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle L_{u}+L_{v}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}L_{\frac{u-v}{2}}L_{\frac{u+v}{2}},&\text{$\frac{u-v}{2}$ even;}\\
5F_{\frac{u-v}{2}}F_{\frac{u+v}{2}},&\text{$\frac{u-v}{2}$ odd,}\end{cases}\qquad\quad L_{u}-L_{v}=\begin{cases}L_{\frac{u-v}{2}}L_{\frac{u+v}{2}},&\text{$\frac{u-v}{2}$ odd;}\\
5F_{\frac{u-v}{2}}F_{\frac{u+v}{2}},&\text{$\frac{u-v}{2}$ even.}\end{cases}

Lemma 3

(K. Adegoke
[
1
]
)
.

For
p
and
q
integers,

\displaystyle 1+(-1)^{p}\alpha^{2q}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{p}\alpha^{q}\sqrt{5}F_{q},&\text{\rm$p-q$ odd;}\\
(-1)^{p}\alpha^{q}L_{q},&\text{\rm$p-q$ even,}\end{cases}

(6)

\displaystyle 1-(-1)^{p}\alpha^{2q}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{p-1}\alpha^{q}L_{q},&\text{\rm$p-q$ odd;}\\
(-1)^{p-1}\alpha^{q}\sqrt{5}F_{q},&\text{\rm$p-q$ even.}\end{cases}

(7)

The following formulas could be easily derived from the Binet formulas (

).

Lemma 4
.

For
p
and
q
integers,

\displaystyle(-1)^{q}+\alpha^{2q}=\alpha^{q}L_{q},\qquad\quad(-1)^{q}-\alpha^{2q}=-\sqrt{5}\alpha^{q}F_{q},

(8)

\displaystyle(-1)^{q}+\beta^{2q}=\beta^{q}L_{q},\qquad\quad(-1)^{q}-\beta^{2q}=\sqrt{5}\beta^{q}.

(9)

Lemma 5

(V.E. Hoggatt, Jr. et al.
[

]
)
.

For
p
and
q
integers,

\displaystyle L_{p+q}-L_{p}\alpha^{q}=-\sqrt{5}\beta^{p}F_{q},\qquad\quad L_{p+q}-L_{p}\beta^{q}=\sqrt{5}\alpha^{p}F_{q},

\displaystyle F_{p+q}-F_{p}\alpha^{q}=\beta^{p}F_{q},\qquad\qquad\quad F_{p+q}-F_{p}\beta^{q}=\alpha^{p}F_{q}.

Lemma 6
.

We have

\displaystyle 1-\alpha=\beta,\qquad 1-\beta=\alpha,\qquad 1+\alpha=\alpha^{2},\qquad 1+\beta=\beta^{2},

(10)

\displaystyle 1-\alpha^{3}=-2\alpha,\qquad 1-\beta^{3}=-2\beta,\qquad 1+\alpha^{3}=2\alpha^{2},\qquad 1+\beta^{3}=2\beta^{2},

(11)

\displaystyle 1-2\alpha=-\sqrt{5},\qquad 1-2\beta=\sqrt{5},\qquad 1+2\alpha=\alpha^{3},\qquad 1+2\beta=\beta^{3},

(12)

\displaystyle 2-\alpha=\beta^{2},\qquad 2-\beta=\alpha^{2},\qquad 2+\alpha=\alpha\sqrt{5},\qquad 2+\beta=-\beta\sqrt{5},

(13)

\displaystyle 1-\alpha^{3}\sqrt{5}=-2\alpha^{3},\quad\,1-\beta^{3}\sqrt{5}=4\beta^{2},\quad\,1+\alpha^{3}\sqrt{5}=4\alpha^{2},\quad\,1+\beta^{3}\sqrt{5}=-2\beta^{3},

(14)

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}-\alpha^{3}=-2,\qquad\sqrt{5}-\beta^{3}=-4\beta,\qquad\sqrt{5}+\alpha^{3}=4\alpha,\,\quad\sqrt{5}+\beta^{3}=2,

(15)

\displaystyle 3-\alpha^{3}=2\beta,\qquad 3-\beta^{3}=2\alpha,\qquad 3+\alpha^{3}=2\alpha\sqrt{5},\qquad 3+\beta^{3}=-2\beta\sqrt{5},

(16)

\displaystyle 1-3\alpha^{3}=-2\alpha^{2}\sqrt{5},\qquad 1-3\beta^{3}=2\beta^{2}\sqrt{5},\qquad 1+3\alpha^{3}=2\alpha^{4},\qquad 1+3\beta^{3}=2\beta^{4}.

(17)

Proof.

Each identity is obtained by making appropriate substitutions for
p
and
q
in the identities given in Lemma
5

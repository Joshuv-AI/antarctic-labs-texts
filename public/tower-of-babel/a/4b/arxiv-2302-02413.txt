Abstract
. In this work we investigate a class of degenerate Schrödinger equations associated to degenerate elliptic operators with irregular potentials on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} by introducing a suitable Hörmander metric g g and a g g -weight m m . We establish the well-posedness for the corresponding degenerate Schrödinger and degenerate parabolic equations. When the subelliticity is available on the degenerate elliptic operator we deduce spectral properties for a class of degenerate Hamiltonians. We also study the L p L^{p} mapping properties for operators with symbols in the S ⁡ ( m − β , g ) S(m^{-\beta},g) classes in the spirit of classical Fefferman’s L p L^{p} -bounds for the ( ρ , δ ) (\rho,\delta) calculus. Finally, within our S ⁡ ( m , g ) S(m,g) -classes, sharp L p L^{p} -estimates and Schatten properties for Schrödinger operators for Hörmander sums of squares are also investigated.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Outline

In this work we study a class of degenerate Schrödinger equations corresponding to a Hamiltonian:
\mathcal{H}_{V}=a_{2}(x,D)+V(x)
, where
a_{2}(x,D)
is a second order degenerate elliptic operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the potential
V
is a real-valued measurable function of quadratic order at
\infty
. Our analysis covers the case where the principal symbol
a_{2}(x,\xi)
of the operator
a_{2}(x,D)
which we assume to be positive belongs to the Kohn-Nirenberg class of second order, that is, it satisfies estimates of the type

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a_{2}(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{2-|\alpha|},\,\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.1)

Note that by the spectral theory of second-order self-adjoint operators, the positivity condition
a_{2}(x,\xi)\geq 0
also covers the case of differential operators given by

a_{2}(x,D)=-\sum a_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}f+\mbox{ lower order terms},\quad\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.2)

with the coefficients
a_{ij}\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, belonging to the space of real-valued smooth functions which are uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, together with all their derivatives, and so that
A(x)=(a_{ij}(x))
is a positive semi-definite matrix for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Then, our setting also includes Hörmander sub-Laplacians

a_{2}(x,D)=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}^{2}

that are sums of squares of real vector fields. For a differential operator
a_{2}(x,D)
whose symbol is as in (
1.1
), we introduce a metric
g
on the phase-space defined by

g_{X}(dx,d\xi):=\frac{(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}+|x|^{2})}{m(x,\xi)}\left(dx^{2}+\frac{d\xi^{2}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)\,,

(1.3)

where

m(x,\xi)=a(x,\xi)+\langle(x,\xi)\rangle,

(1.4)

with

a(x,\xi)=a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2},\,\,X=(x,\xi),\,\,\langle(x,\xi)\rangle=(1+|x|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The metric and the weight above are sharp with respect to the Hörmander conditions: temperateness, slow continuity, and the uncertainty principle, that defines the
S(m,g)
class that contains the Schrödinger operators
a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}.

After establishing that we are in a suitable
S(m,g)
pseudodifferential calculus, i.e, the metric
g
is indeed a Hörmander metric and
m
is a
g
-weight. We consider
V
to be a real-valued Borel function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for appropriate constants
C,C_{1},C_{2}>0
,
|V(x)|\leq C|x|^{2},
for a.e.
|x|\geq C_{1}
and
V(x)\geq-C_{2}
, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, by using the machinery of the Weyl-Hörmander calculus, we:

•

obtain the well-posedness of the following Cauchy problem on the Sobolev space
H(M,g)
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}i\partial_{t}u&=\mathcal{H}_{V}u,\\
u(0)&=f.\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

•

We analyse the well posedness for degenerate parabolic equations of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}u&=\mathcal{H}u,\\
u(0)&=f,\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
The Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
is considered to be irregular.

•

We establish the
L^{p}
-boundedness theory for the
S(m^{-\frac{n\varepsilon}{2}},g)
-classes with the metric
g
and the weight
m
as in (
1.3
) and in (
1.4
), respectively. Moreover, we give the sharp index
\varepsilon
in the case where
a_{2}(x,D)
is the principal symbol of a Hörmander sub-Laplacian. Consequently, we obtain the
L^{p}
-boundedness of negative powers for our degenerate Schrödinger type operators in that case.

•

We investigate the membership of the operators in the classes
S(m^{-\mu},g)
to the Schatten-von Neumann classes
S_{r}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).
Consequently, the information about the distribution of the eigenvalues for a class of Schrödinger type operators is obtained, as well as the rate of growth of their eigenvalues.

2.
Preliminaries: Weyl-Hörmander calculus

In this section we summarise some preliminary topics about the Weyl quantisation and the Weyl-Hörmander calculus. For this we will follow Hörmander
[
52
, Section 18.5]
and Chapter 2 of
[
62
]
.

The Weyl quantisation of
a\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the operator
a(x,D):\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined in the weak sense by the symmetric expression

\displaystyle a^{w}(x,D)u(x):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}a((x+y)/2,\xi)u(y)dyd\xi,\,\,u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The Weyl quantisation of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is the special case of the
\tau
-quantisation of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
defined for
0\leq\tau\leq 1,
by

a^{\tau}(x,D)f(x):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}a(\tau x+(1-\tau)y,\xi)u(y)dyd\xi,

also defined in the weak sense. So, the Weyl quantisation of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is
(1/2)
-quantisation of
a(\cdot,\cdot),
i.e.
a^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,D)=a^{w}(x,D).
The
1
-quantisation (Kohn-Nirenberg quantisation) of
a(\cdot,\cdot),
recovers the usual definition of the pseudo-differential operator associated to the ‘symbol’
a(\cdot,\cdot):

a(x,D)u(x)\equiv a^{1}(x,D)u(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle}a(x,\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)d\xi,

where the equivalence of both representations is a consequence of the fact that the Fourier transform is an isomorphism on the Schwartz space implying that
\widehat{u}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
where by abuse of notation we have written
a(x,\xi)\widehat{u}:=a(x,\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)
which makes sense if e.g. the distribution
a(\cdot,\cdot)
agrees with a locally integrable function. For
a,b\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
and
0\leq\tau,\tau^{\prime}\leq 1,
we have the equality
a^{\tau}(x,D)=b^{\tau^{\prime}}(x,D)
if and only if

a(x,\xi)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-2\pi i\langle\xi-\eta,z\rangle}b(x+(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)z,\eta)d\eta,

which means that we always can pass from one quantisation to other one by using the semigroup
(J_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}},
defined by

J_{t}a(x,\xi):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-2\pi i\langle\xi-\eta,z\rangle}b(x+tz,\eta)dyd\eta,

which satisfies
J_{t+s}=J_{t}J_{s},

t,s\in\mathbb{R}.
In particular, for the Weyl quantisation and the Kohn-Nirenberg quantisation of
a
, respectively, we have

a(x,D)=(J_{1/2}a)^{w}(x,D),\quad\,a^{w}(x,D)=(J_{-1/2}a)(x,D).

Remark 2.1

(The adjoint of the Weyl quantisation)
.

The following remarkable property:
a^{w}(x,D)^{*}=\overline{a}^{w}(x,D),
implies that
a^{w}(x,D)^{*}={a}^{w}(x,D)
for real valued symbols and it is one of the reasons why Hermann Weyl introduced this quantisation for the purposes of quantum mechanics (see Hörmander
[
52
, page 151]
). The other important property is its symplectic invariance that we will not discuss here.

Remark 2.2

(Composition in the Weyl quantisation)
.

Let us write
X=(x,\xi),
(or
Y=(y,\eta)
etc.) for points on the phase space
\mathbb{R}^{2n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\xi},
(resp.
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\eta}).

The composition
a^{w}\circ b^{w}(x,D)\equiv a^{w}(x,D)\circ b^{w}(x,D)
in the Weyl quantisation is related with the symplectic structure of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}=T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, for

a,b\in S(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let us define

(a\#b)(X):=\frac{1}{\pi^{{2n}}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{-2i\sigma(X-Y_{1},X-Y_{2})}a(Y_{1})b(Y_{2})dY_{1}dY_{2},

where
\sigma(X,Y):=\langle y,\xi\rangle-\langle x,\eta\rangle
is the symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}.
Then, in terms of the operation
\#,
we have
a^{w}(x,D)\circ b^{\omega}(x,D)=(a\#b)^{\omega}(x,D)
.

In order to introduce the Weyl-Hörmander calculus we need the notion of a Hörmander metric.
One reason for this, is that if we want to study the properties of some operator
L,
we can to associate to
L
this kind of metric.

Definition 2.3

(Hörmander’s metric)
.

For
X\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, let
g_{X}(\cdot)
be a positive definite quadratic form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. We say that

g(\cdot)
is a Hörmander metric if the following three conditions are satisfied:

i.

Continuity or slowness.
There exist a constant
C>0
such that

g_{X}(X-Y)\leq C^{-1}\Longrightarrow\left(\frac{g_{X}(T)}{g_{Y}(T)}\right)^{\pm 1}\leq C,

for all
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
T\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\{0\}
.

ii.

Uncertainty principle.
In terms of the symplectic form
\sigma(Y,Z):=\langle z,\eta\rangle-\langle y,\zeta\rangle
,
we define

g_{X}^{\sigma}(T):=\sup_{W\neq 0}\frac{\sigma(T,W)^{2}}{g_{X}(W)}.

(2.1)

We say that
g
satisfies the
uncertainty principle

if

\lambda_{g}(X)=\inf_{T\neq 0}\left(\frac{g_{X}^{\sigma}(T)}{g_{X}(T)}\right)^{1/2}\geq 1,

for
all
X,T\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

iii.

Temperateness.
We say that
g
is temperate if there exist
\overline{C}>0
and
J\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left(\frac{g_{X}(T)}{g_{Y}(T)}\right)^{\pm 1}\leq\overline{C}(1+g_{Y}^{\sigma}(X-Y))^{J},

for all
X,Y,T\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Let
g
be a Hörmander metric.
The uncertainty parameter
or
the Planck function
associated to
g
is defined by

h_{g}(X)^{2}=\sup_{T\neq 0}\frac{g_{X}(T)}{g_{X}^{\sigma}(T)}.

Note that since
h_{g}(X)=(\lambda_{g}(X))^{-1}
, the uncertainty principle can be translated into the condition

h_{g}(X)\leq 1.

Definition 2.4

(g-weight)
.

Let
M:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a function. Then,

•

we say that

M
is
g
-
continuous
if there exists
\tilde{C}>0

such that

g_{X}(X-Y)\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{C}}\Longrightarrow\left(\frac{M(X)}{M(Y)}\right)^{\pm 1}\leq\tilde{C}\,.

for all
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

•

we say that

M
is
g
-
temperate
if there exist
\tilde{C}>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}

such that

\left(\frac{M(X)}{M(Y)}\right)^{\pm 1}\leq\tilde{C}(1+g_{Y}^{\sigma}(X-Y))^{N}\,,

for all
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

We will say that
M
is a
g
-
weight
if it is
g
-continuous and
g
-temperate.

We can now define the classes of symbols adapted to a Hörmander metric
g
and a
g
-weight
M
.

Definition 2.5
.

For a Hörmander metric
g
and a
g
-weight
M
, the class
S(M,g)
consists of all smooth functions
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
C_{k}>0
, such that for all

X,T_{1},...,T_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
we have

|\sigma^{(k)}(X;T_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes T_{k})|\leq C_{k}M(X)\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_{X}^{1/2}(T_{i})\,.

(2.2)

The notation
\sigma^{(k)}
stands for the
k^{th}
derivative of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{(k)}(X;T_{1},...,T_{k})
denotes the
k^{th}
derivative tensor of
a
at
X
in the directions
T_{1},...,T_{k}
.
For
\sigma\in S(M,g)
we denote by
\parallel\sigma\parallel_{k,S(M,g)}
the minimum
C_{k}
satisfying the above inequality. The class
S(M,g)
becomes a Fréchet space endowed with the family of seminorms
\parallel\cdot\parallel_{k,S(M,g)}
.

4.
Some applications to well-posedness for degenerate Schrödinger and degenerate parabolic equations

We are now going to establish some implications in the

and Sobolev spaces

adapted to our degenerate harmonic oscillators has other consequences, regarding the well-posedness for degenerate Schrödinger equations and degenerate parabolic equations in the

-setting. We introduce the following appropriate class of potentials on

Definition 4.1
.

We will denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the class of Borel functions

satisying the following conditions:

(V1)

\displaystyle\mbox{There exist }C,C_{1}>0\mbox{ such that }|V(x)|\leq C|x|^{2},\mbox{ for a.e. }|x|\geq C_{1}.

(V2)

\displaystyle\mbox{There exists }C_{2}>0\mbox{ such that }V(x)\geq-C_{2},\mbox{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It is worth to point out that no regularity assumption has been imposed on
V
as is customary in most of the main theorems on the classical spectral theory of Schrödinger operators where the conditions are more likely to be on the integrability (
[
5
]
,
[
48
]
). We will consider Hamiltonians of the form
\mathcal{H}_{V}=a_{2}(x,D)+V

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
a_{2}(x,D)\in\mbox{Diff }_{+}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be formally self-adjoint,
V\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

the Hörmander metric and
m
the
g
-weight associated to
a_{2}(x,D)
as in (
3.1
). Then,
-i\mathcal{H}_{V}
is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}-
group of unitary operators. Consequently, the following Cauchy problem for the corresponding degenerate Schrödinger equation is well-posed on
L^{2}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}i\partial_{t}u&=\mathcal{H}_{V}u,\\
u(0)&=f.\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

Furthermore, if

is a sum of squares of real vector fields, then
\mathcal{H}=-(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2})
is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}-
semigroup (degenerate harmonic semigroup) of contractions and the corresponding degenerate parabolic equation is well-posed on

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}u&=\mathcal{H}u,\\
u(0)&=f.\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

Proof.

We first state the boundedness of the potential operator. Indeed, we first note that since
|x|^{2}\in S(m,g)
, we have that
|x|^{2}:H(m,g)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded. From (V1) and (V2), we have
|V(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2})
, for a.e.

. Since
m\geq 1
, it is now clear that

\lvert|V(x)u\rvert|_{L^{2}}\leq C\lvert|u\rvert|_{H(m,g)},

(4.3)

for all

.

On the other hand, the operator
a_{2}(x,D)+V(x)
is symmetric, Lemma
3.7
, Theorem
2.6
and the inequality (
4.3
) imply that the operator
a_{2}(x,D)+V(x):H(m,g)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

, applying Fefferman-Phong theorem
there exists

such that

\mbox{Re}\langle a_{2}(x,D)v,v\rangle\geq-\frac{C}{2}\lvert|v\rvert|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Hence

Thus, by using (V2) there exists

is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}-
semigroup of contractions and hence the corresponding degenerate parabolic equation (
4.2
) is well-posed.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

We recall that the simultaneous study of the Hamiltonian as an infinitesimal generator for a Schrödinger semigroup as
for Heat semigroup has been an active field of research in the last decades as it was well pointed out by B. Simon in
[
76
]
. For instance, when looking at the
L^{p}
properties of the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{H}=-\Delta+V(x)
, if
\mathcal{H}\psi=E\psi
and
\psi\in L^{2}
, one observes that since
e^{-t\mathcal{H}}\psi=e^{-tE}\psi
, then
\psi\in e^{-t\mathcal{H}}(L^{2})
. Thus, a mapping property such as
e^{-t\mathcal{H}}:L^{2}\rightarrow L^{\infty}
can be used to deduce that
\psi
belongs also to
L^{\infty}
. The works of B. Simon and R. Carmona on Schrödinger semigroups have deeply influenced the research on these semigroups, their applications are diverse and the literature is currently huge.
See for instance
[
1
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
77
]
,
[
75
]
,
[
53
]
,
[
57
]
, for more accounts on this matter. Thus, the Schrödinger semigroups are relevant for the study of
L^{p}
properties of eigenfunctions of Schrödinger operators and especially for the so-called
ground states
.

As a corollary of our previous
L^{2}
estimates, we can extend the well-posedness for the positive powers of
\mathcal{H}
, in the case where

a_{2}(x,D)=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}^{2}

is a sum of squares of real vector fields, where the family of vector fields satisfies the Hörmander condition of order
2
. A reason for considering a sub-Laplacian associated to a system of vector fields satisfying the Hörmander condition of order 2 is that in this case the geometry of the balls associated to the Carnót-Carathéodory distance of the Hörmander system is very well understood from the fundamental work of Nagel, Stein and Wainger
[
69
]
, see Remark
6.1
.

On the other hand, thanks to the Fefferman-Phong inequality, we can define the positive powers of
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}
, and we have the membership
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}\in OpS(m^{\beta},g)
. Moreover, the maximal hypoellipticity of
a_{2}(x,D)
guarantees that the Sobolev space
H(m^{\beta},g)
is characterised by the condition
(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+C)^{\beta}u\in L^{2}
. Hence,
(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+C)^{\beta}
is semi-bounded from below
and consequently we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\beta>0.
Let

a_{2}(x,D)=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}^{2}

be a sum of squares of real vector fields, where
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
satisfy the Hörmander condition of order
2
,
g
the Hörmander metric and
m
the
g
-weight associated to
a_{2}(x,D)
as in (
3.1
). Then, there exists
C>0
such that
\mathcal{H}_{C}=a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+C
is semibounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
-i\mathcal{H}_{C}
is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}-
semigroup. Consequently, the following Cauchy problem for the corresponding degenerate Schrödinger equation is well-posed on
L^{2}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}i\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\beta}u&=0,\\
u(0)&=f.\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

Furthermore, the operator

\mathcal{H}=-(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}),

is the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}-
semigroup (degenerate harmonic semigroup) of contractions and the corresponding degenerate parabolic equation is well-posed on
L^{2}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{H}^{\beta}u&=0,\\
u(0)&=f.\end{array}\right.

(4.6)

In order to go further with our analysis we need the property of
geodesic temperateness
for our metric
g
. This property allows us to obtain the invertibilitiy of the Hamiltonian as a pseudodifferential operator. This important notion was introduced by Bony and Chemin in
[
8
]
, originally calling it
strong temperateness
. We start by recalling its definition, see
[
62
]
,
[
9
]
.

Let
d^{\sigma}(X,Y)
be the geodesic distance for the Riemannian metric
g^{\sigma}
between
X
and
Y
, where
g^{\sigma}
is given by (
2.1
).

Definition 4.5
.

A Hörmander metric
g
is called
geodesically temperate
if it is temperate and if there exist
C,N>0
such that

\frac{g_{Y}}{g_{X}}\leq C(1+d^{\sigma}(X,Y))^{N}.

(4.7)

It is known (c.f.
[
9
]
) that the condition (
4.7
) is equivalent to : there exist
C,N>0
such that

C^{-1}(1+d^{\sigma}(X,Y))^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq(1+g^{\sigma}(X-Y))\leq C(1+d^{\sigma}(X,Y))^{N}.

(4.8)

Since our Hörmander metric is symmetrically split, it follows that it is geodesically temperate by a result of Bony (Cf. Theorem 5 (i),
[
9
]
).
In what follows we require the following definition. We record that a second order differential operator
P(x,D)
is subelliptic of order
0<\tau\leq 2,
if it satisfies the
a-priori estimate

\lVert v\rVert_{H^{\tau}}\leq C_{1}(\lVert P(x,D)v\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}}),\,\,\,\forall v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(4.9)

where
H^{\tau}
denotes the standard Sobolev space of order
\tau
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
a_{2}(x,D)\in\mbox{Diff }_{+}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be formally self-adjoint and subelliptic with order of subellipticity
0<\tau\leq 2
,
g
the Hörmander metric and
m
the
g
-weight associated to
a_{2}(x,D)
as in (
3.1
). Then, there exist
C>0
such that the operator

\mathcal{H}_{C}=a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+C:H(m,g)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

is an isomorphism with inverse a pseudodifferential operator

\mathcal{H}_{C}^{-1}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow H(m,g),

with symbol in
S(m^{-1},g)
.

Proof.

The
a priori
estimate (
4.4
) and the self-adjointness of
\mathcal{H}_{C}
gives the surjectivity. On the other hand, the subellipticity of
a_{2}(x,D)
gives

\lVert v\rVert_{H^{\tau}}\leq C_{1}(\lVert(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2})v\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}}),\,\,\,\forall v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(4.10)

By the
a priori
estimate (
4.4
) again and (
4.10
), there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{\tau}}\leq C_{2}\lVert\mathcal{H}_{C}u\rVert_{L^{2}},\,\,\,\forall u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(4.11)

Now, an analogous argument to the one for the proof of Lemma 3.3 in
[
25
]
, splitting the phase-space within an elliptic and a subelliptic zones with respect to
g
give us

\|u\|_{H(m,g)}\leq C_{2}\|\mathcal{H}_{C}u\|_{L^{2}},\,\,\,\forall u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Hence
\mathcal{H}_{C}:H(m,g)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is injective. Therefore, an application of Corollary 7.7 in
[
8
]
concludes the proof.
∎

-

and

-

In this section we consider our analysis of pseudo-differential operators associated to the degenerate class of operators
S(m,g)
where
g
is the metric in (
3.1
).

5.1.
L^{\infty}
-
BMO
and
H^{1}
-
L^{1}
boundedness

The next lemma will be essential for us in order to estimate an optimal fractional power of the weight
m(x,\xi)
, which determines a suitable class for the
L^{p}
-boundedness. The
L^{\infty}
-
BMO
boundedness will be reached by analysing a partition of unity of the symbol of interest. The estimates for each piece is obtained from the next lemma which is an extension of a classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{3}{4}
and
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\varepsilon<1,
and let
q\in S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon},g)
be supported in

R\leq a(x,\xi)+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R
for
R>1
. Then, for all
l>n/4
we have

\lvert|q(x,D)f\rvert|_{\infty}\leq C\lvert|q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\frac{n\varepsilon}{2}},g)}\lvert|f\rvert|_{L^{\infty}},

where the constant
C
is independent of
q
and
f
.

Proof.

Let
q\in S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon},g)
be supported in

\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:R\leq a(x,\xi)+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R\},

for

R>1
fixed. We observe that

\displaystyle q(x,D)f(x)=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-n}\int\int e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}q\left(x,\xi\right)f(y)dyd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\hat{q}_{x}(y-x)f(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{x}*f(x),

where
\hat{q}_{x}
is the Fourier transform

q_{x}(\xi)=q(x,\xi)
with respect to the variable
\xi
.
We obtain

|q(x,D)f(x)|\leq||\hat{q}_{x}\rvert|_{L^{1}}||f\rvert|_{L^{\infty}},\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It is then enough to prove that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
l>n/4

||\hat{q}_{x}\rvert|_{L^{1}}\leq C\lvert|q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon},g)}.

Let us consider
b>0
to be fixed below. By applying the
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|y|<b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cb^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\,\int\limits_{|y|<b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cb^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\,\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|q(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C||q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon},g)}b^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\,\int\limits_{a(x,\xi)+\langle x,\xi\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-n\varepsilon}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C||q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon},g)}b^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\,\int\limits_{\langle\xi\rangle\leq 3R}\langle\xi\rangle^{-n\varepsilon}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Now, by choosing
b=R^{\varepsilon-1}
, we obtain

\int\limits_{|y|<b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\leq C||q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon},g)}.

On the other hand, for the analysis of the integral
\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy,
we first note that for an integer
k>\frac{n}{4}
, we have
k>\frac{n}{4}\geq n(1-\varepsilon)
, since
\varepsilon\geq\frac{3}{4}
. Hence

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\leq

\displaystyle Cb^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{y\geq b}|y|^{2k}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cb^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla_{\xi}^{k}q(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and the Plancherel theorem implies the inequality

\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\leq C\lvert|q\rvert|_{k;S(m^{-\frac{n\varepsilon}{2}},g)}b^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.1)

In order to estimate the last integral, we first note that
there exists
C>1
such that for all
(x,\xi)
:

a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq C(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}+|x|^{2}).

We split the argument into two cases
:

|x|^{2}\leq\frac{R}{2C}
and
|x|^{2}>\frac{R}{2C}
.

If
|x|^{2}\leq\frac{R}{2C}
, we get

R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq C(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}+|x|^{2})\leq C\langle\xi\rangle^{2}+\frac{R}{2}.

Hence
\frac{R}{2}\leq C\langle\xi\rangle^{2}.

Thus

b^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\leq Cb^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{R\leq 2C\langle\xi\rangle^{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{-n\varepsilon-k}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq CR^{(\varepsilon-1)(\frac{n}{2}-k)+\frac{1}{4}(n(1-\varepsilon)-k)}\leq C.

To see the last inequality, we note that

(\varepsilon-1)(\frac{n}{2}-k)+\frac{1}{4}(n(1-\varepsilon)-k)=\frac{n}{4}(\varepsilon-1)+k(\frac{3}{4}-\varepsilon).

Since
\varepsilon\geq\frac{3}{4}
, we obtain the desired bound.

On the other hand, if
|x|^{2}>\frac{R}{2C}
, we have

-|x|^{2}<-\frac{R}{2C}
and

\langle\xi\rangle\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R-|x|^{2}\leq 3R-\frac{R}{2C}=\frac{(6C-1)}{2C}R.

Since
6C-1>0
and by using the fact that
|x|^{2}>\frac{R}{2C}
, we have
\langle x\rangle^{-k}\leq C^{\prime}R^{-\frac{k}{2}}
and we obtain

\displaystyle b^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=

\displaystyle b^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{\langle\xi\rangle\leq C^{\prime}R}m^{-n\varepsilon}(x,\xi)m^{-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{(\frac{n}{2}-k)(\varepsilon-1)}\left(\,\int\limits_{\langle\xi\rangle\leq C^{\prime}R}\langle\xi\rangle^{-n\varepsilon}\langle x\rangle^{-2k}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{(\frac{n}{2}-k)(\varepsilon-1)}\langle x\rangle^{-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{\langle\xi\rangle\leq C^{\prime}R}\langle\xi\rangle^{-n\varepsilon}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{(\frac{n}{2}-k)(\varepsilon-1)}R^{-\frac{k}{2}}R^{\frac{n}{2}(1-\varepsilon)}.

By observing that

R^{(\frac{n}{2}-k)(\varepsilon-1)+\frac{n}{2}(1-\varepsilon)-\frac{k}{2}}=R^{k(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)}\leq C,

since
\varepsilon\geq\frac{3}{4}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, the desired estimate follows.
∎

We can now state the
L^{\infty}
-
BMO
bounds as a consequence of our
L^{\infty}
-
L^{\infty}
estimates.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\frac{3}{4}\leq\beta<1,
and let
\sigma(x,D)\in\textnormal{Op}(S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)).
Then, for
\ell>\frac{n}{4},
we have

\|\sigma(x,D)f\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\sigma(x,\xi)\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where the constant
C>0,
is independent of
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
So,
\sigma(x,D):L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
extends to a bounded operator.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
and let us denote
A=\sigma(x,D).
Let us fix a ball
B(x_{0},r)
where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
and
r>0.
We will prove that there exists a constant
C>0,
independent of
f
and
r,
such that

\frac{1}{|B(x_{0},r)|}\int\limits_{B(x_{0},r)}|Af(x)-(Af)_{B(x_{0},r)}|dx\leqslant C\|\sigma\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(5.2)

where

(Af)_{B(x_{0},r)}:=\frac{1}{|B(x_{0},r)|}\int\limits_{B(x_{0},r)}Af(x)dx.

We will split to
\sigma
as the sum of two symbols

\sigma(x,\xi)=\sigma^{0}(x,\xi)+\sigma^{1}(x,\xi),\,\,\,\sigma^{j}(x,\xi)\in S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g),\,j=0,1,

in a such way that both,
\sigma^{0}(x,\xi)
and
\sigma^{1}(x,\xi),
satisfy the estimate

\|\sigma^{j}\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}\leqslant C_{j,\ell}\|\sigma\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)},\,\,j=0,1,\,\ell\geqslant 1.

(5.3)

Now, if
A^{j}:=\textnormal{Op}(\sigma^{j}),
for
j=0,1,
then
A=A^{0}+A^{1}
on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
and we only need to prove that

\frac{1}{|B(x_{0},r)|}\int\limits_{B(x_{0},r)}|A^{j}f(x)-(A^{j}f)_{B(x_{0},r)}|dx\leqslant C\|\sigma^{j}\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(5.4)

Then, if we prove (
5.3
), we can deduce (
5.2
). Now, we proceed to prove the existence of
\sigma^{0}
and
\sigma^{1}
satisfying the requested properties.
Let us define

\sigma^{0}(x,\xi)=\sigma(x,\xi)\tilde{\gamma}(x,\xi),\textnormal{ }\sigma^{1}(x,\xi)=\sigma(x,\xi)-\sigma^{0}(x,\xi),\,(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(5.5)

where

\tilde{\gamma}(x,\xi):=\gamma(r\times m(x,\xi))=\gamma(r\times(a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle x,\xi\rangle)),

and
\gamma\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}),
is a smooth function supported in
\{t:|t|\leqslant 1\},
satisfying
\gamma(t)=1,
for
|t|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

In order to prove (
5.3
) let us proceed as follows. Since
\tilde{\gamma}(x,\xi)
is a cut-off function supported in the set
\{(x,\xi):a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle x,\xi\rangle\leq r^{-1}\},
in this region, the symbol
\sigma^{0}(x,\xi)=\sigma(x,\xi)\tilde{\gamma}(x,\xi)
also satisfies the
S(m,g)
-estimates in (
2.2
) but probably depending on
r>0
. To remove this dependence in
r>0,
let us use the natural Fréchet topology on
S(m,g)
induced by its family of seminorms. This is the smalles topology on
S(m,g)
which makes these seminorms continuous. Indeed, we have that

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|\sigma(x,\xi)\gamma(r\times(a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle x,\xi\rangle))\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}=\|\sigma(x,\xi)\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)},

and

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\|\sigma(x,\xi)\gamma(r\times(a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle x,\xi\rangle))\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}=0.

In consequence, there exists
0<\varkappa<1,
such that for all
r\in(0,\varkappa)\cup[\varkappa^{-1},\infty),
we have that

\|\sigma(x,\xi)\gamma(r\times(a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle x,\xi\rangle))\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}\leq 2\|\sigma(x,\xi)\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}.

On the other hand, the function

r\in[\varkappa,\varkappa^{-1}]\mapsto\sigma(x,\xi)\gamma(r\times(a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle x,\xi\rangle))\in S(m,g),

(5.6)

is continuous, and since
S(m,g)
is a complete topological space, there exists
r_{0}\in[\varkappa,\varkappa^{-1}]
such that

\displaystyle\max_{r\in[\varkappa,\varkappa^{-1}]}\|\sigma(x,\xi)\gamma(r\times(a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle x,\xi\rangle))\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}

\displaystyle=\|\sigma(x,\xi)\gamma(r_{0}\times(a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle x,\xi\rangle))\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}\lesssim_{r_{0}}\|\sigma(x,\xi)\|_{\ell,S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\beta},g)}.

Since the previous estimate depends on
r_{0},
and then on
\varkappa,
but it is independent of
r>0,
the estimates above prove the estimate in (
5.3
).

To estimate the integral

I_{0}:=\frac{1}{|B(x_{0},r)|}\int\limits_{B(x_{0},r)}|A^{0}f(x)-(A^{0}f)_{B(x_{0},r)}|dx,

we will use the mean value theorem. Indeed, observe that

\displaystyle|A^{0}f(x)-A^{0}f(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{0}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\partial_{x_{k}}A^{0}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|x-y|

\displaystyle\leqslant Cr\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\partial_{x_{k}}A^{0}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(5.7)

In order to estimate the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
\partial_{x_{k}}A^{0}f,
first let us observe that the symbol of
\partial_{x_{k}}A^{0}=\textnormal{Op}(\sigma^{\prime}_{k})
is given by

\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(x,\xi):=2\pi i\xi_{k}\sigma^{0}(x,\xi)+(\partial_{x_{k}}\sigma^{0}(x,\xi)).

(5.8)

Indeed, the Leibniz law gives

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}A^{0}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{x_{k}}(e^{i2\pi x\xi}\sigma^{0}(x,\xi))\widehat{f}(\xi))\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}([\partial_{x_{k}}(e^{i2\pi x\xi})\sigma^{0}(x,\xi)+e^{i2\pi x\xi}\partial_{x_{k}}\sigma^{0}(x,\xi))]\widehat{f}(\xi))\,d\xi\,,

and we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}A^{0}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}([e^{i2\pi x\xi}2\pi i\xi_{k}\sigma^{0}(x,\xi)+e^{i2\pi x\xi}\partial_{x_{k}}\sigma^{0}(x,\xi))]\widehat{f}(\xi)),

which proves (
5.8
). By using a suitable partition of unity we will decompose
\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(x,\xi)
in the following way,

\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\rho_{j,k}(x,\xi).

To construct the family of operators
\rho_{j,k}(x,\xi)
we will proceed as follows. We choose a smooth real function
\eta
satisfying
\eta(t)\equiv 0
for
|t|\leqslant 1
and
\eta(t)\equiv 1
for
|t|\geqslant 2.
Set

\rho(t)=\eta(t)-\eta(\frac{t}{2}).

The support of
\rho
satisfies
\textnormal{supp}(\rho)\subset[1,4].
One can check that

1=\eta(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\rho(2^{j}t),\,\,\,\,\textnormal{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Indeed,

\eta(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\rho(2^{j}t)=\eta(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\eta(2^{j}t)-\eta(2^{j-1}t)\right)=\eta(2^{\ell}t)\rightarrow 1,\,\,\ell\rightarrow\infty.

For
t=rm(x,\xi)
we have

1=\eta(rm(x,\xi))+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\rho(2^{j}rm(x,\xi)).

Observe that

\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(x,\xi)=\eta(rm(x,\xi))\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(x,\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\rho(2^{j}rm(x,\xi))\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(x,\xi).

Because
\eta(rm(x,\xi))\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(x,\xi)=0,
in view that the support of
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\eta

\displaystyle I_{0}:

\frac{1}{|B(x_{0},r)|}\int\limits_{B(x_{0},r)}|A^{1}[\phi f](x)|dx\leqslant\frac{1}{|B(x_{0},r)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\int\limits_{B(x_{0},r)}|A^{1}[\phi f](x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, we will estimate

(5.11)

(5.14)

The exponent

5.1

Lemma 5.5
.

Let us consider the metric
g
given in (
3.1
) with
a_{2}(x,\xi)=\xi_{1}^{2}+\tilde{x_{1}}^{2}\xi_{2}^{2},

(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
being the symbol of the degenerate elliptic operator

a_{2}(x,D):=-(\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\tilde{x}_{1}^{2}\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}).

Let us consider the
g
-weight
m(X)=\xi_{1}^{2}+\tilde{x_{1}}^{2}\xi_{2}^{2}+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\in S(m,g).
Let
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{5}{12}
and
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\varepsilon<1.
Consider a symbol
q\in S(m^{-\varepsilon},g)
supported in

R\leq a(x,\xi)+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R
for
R>1
. Then, for all
l>1
we have

\lvert|q(x,D)f\rvert|_{\infty}\leq C\lvert|q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\varepsilon},g)}\lvert|f\rvert|_{L^{\infty}},

where the constant
C
is independent of
q
and of
f
.

Proof.

Wee proceed like in Lemma
5.1
.
Let
q\in S(m^{-\varepsilon},g)
be supported in

\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:R\leq a(x,\xi)+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R\},

for

R>1
fixed.
It is then enough to prove that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
l>1,

||\hat{q}_{x}\rvert|_{L^{1}}\leq C\lvert|q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\varepsilon},g)}.

Let us consider
b=R^{\varepsilon-1}.
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives the estimate

\int\limits_{|y|<b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\leq C||q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\varepsilon},g)}.

On the other hand, for the integral
\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy,
we first note that for an integer
k>1
, we have that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\leq

\displaystyle Cb^{1-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{y\geq b}|y|^{2k}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cb^{1-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla_{\xi}^{k}q(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\lvert|q\rvert|_{k;S(m^{-\varepsilon},g)}b^{1-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-2\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In order to estimate the last integral we split into two cases
:

|x|^{2}\leq\frac{R}{2C}
and
|x|^{2}>\frac{R}{2C}
.

If
|x|^{2}\leq\frac{R}{2C}
, we get

R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\lesssim C(\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|+|x|^{2}).

Since in the subelliptic zone
R\lesssim\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|,
we have that

\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|\lesssim a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle=m(x,\xi),

we can compare

\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-2\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\lesssim\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\lesssim\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}\\
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}>0\end{subarray}}(\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|)^{-2\varepsilon-k}d\xi.

By making the changes of variables

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=(\sqrt{R}u_{1},Ru_{2}^{2}),\,\,u_{1},u_{2}>0,

we can estimate

\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\lesssim\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}\\
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}>0\end{subarray}}(\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|)^{-2\varepsilon-k}d\xi\lesssim\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\lesssim u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\\
u_{1},u_{2}>0\end{subarray}}(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2})^{-2\varepsilon-k}R^{-2\varepsilon-k}R^{\frac{3}{2}}u_{2}du_{1}du_{2}=R^{-2\varepsilon-k+\frac{3}{2}}I,

where the integral

\displaystyle I=\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\lesssim u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\\
u_{1},u_{2}>0\end{subarray}}(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2})^{-2\varepsilon-k}u_{2}du_{1}du_{2}<\infty

(5.15)

converges since when
k>3-2\varepsilon.
So, putting all the previous estimates together we deduce that

\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\lesssim R^{(\varepsilon-1)(1-k)-\varepsilon-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{3}{4}}\leq C.

To see the last estimate note that
R>1
and that

(\varepsilon-1)(1-k)-\varepsilon-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{3}{4}=k(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{4}\leq 0,

(5.16)

if for example
k
is the sharp parameter in the convergence of
I
in (
5.15
), namely,
k=3,
from which the inequality

3(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{4}\leq 0,

from then we require that
\varepsilon\geq 5/12.

On the other hand, if
|x|^{2}>\frac{R}{2C}
, we have

-|x|^{2}<-\frac{R}{2C}
and

\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+\langle\xi\rangle\leq 3R-|x|^{2}\leq 3R-\frac{R}{2C}=\frac{(6C-1)}{2C}R.

Since
6C-1>0
and by using the fact that
|x|^{2}>\frac{R}{2C}
, we have
\langle x\rangle^{-k}\leq C^{\prime}R^{-\frac{k}{2}}
and we obtain

b^{1-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-2\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=b^{1-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|\leq C^{\prime}R}m^{-2\varepsilon}(x,\xi)m^{-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\leq b^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\int\limits_{\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|\leq C^{\prime}R}(\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|)^{-2\varepsilon}\langle x\rangle^{-2k}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\leq R^{(\varepsilon-1)(1-k)-\frac{k}{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|\leq C^{\prime}R}(\xi_{1}^{2}+|\xi_{2}|)^{-2\varepsilon}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\lesssim R^{(\varepsilon-1)(1-k)-\frac{k}{2}}\left(\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\lesssim 1\\
u_{1},u_{2}>0\end{subarray}}(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2})^{-2\varepsilon}R^{-2\varepsilon}R^{\frac{3}{2}}u_{2}du_{1}du_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

=R^{(\varepsilon-1)(1-k)-\frac{k}{2}}R^{-\varepsilon+\frac{3}{4}}\sqrt{J},

where

J=\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\lesssim 1\\
u_{1},u_{2}>0\end{subarray}}(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2})^{-2\varepsilon}u_{2}du_{1}du_{2}\lesssim\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}\lesssim 1\\
u_{1},u_{2}>0\end{subarray}}(u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2})^{-2\varepsilon}du_{1}du_{2}

is convergent for any
\varepsilon
such that

that is when
\varepsilon<1.
So, in the case where
|x|^{2}>\frac{R}{2C},
this analysis shows that

\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\lesssim R^{(\varepsilon-1)(1-k)-\frac{k}{2}}R^{-\varepsilon+\frac{3}{4}}\leq C,

when e.g.
k=3,
in view of the inequality

(\varepsilon-1)(1-k)-\frac{k}{2}-\varepsilon+\frac{3}{4}=3(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{4}\leq 0

for any
\varepsilon\geq 5/12.

The proof of the lemma is complete.

Regarding the improved exponent below

Remark 6.1

With the notation in Remark

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}:=\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{k}\}
be a family of real vector fields on

satisfying the Hörmander condition of order

Let

be the Hörmander sub-Laplacian

(6.4)

and let us consider the metric

and the

defined as in (
3.1
) and (

), respectively. Let
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{Q}{2n}
and let

and

Proof.

We only have to prove the lemma in the borderline case

The result for the other values of

follows from the inclusion properties of the

-classes. Let us improve as necessary the proof given for Lemma
5.1
. Let

be supported in

for

fixed. In view of the convolution identity

\displaystyle q(x,D)f(x)=

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{x}*f(x),

where
\hat{q}_{x}
is the Fourier transform

with respect to the variable
\xi,

we obtain

|q(x,D)f(x)|\leq||\hat{q}_{x}\rvert|_{L^{1}}||f\rvert|_{L^{\infty}},\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It is then enough to prove that for all

and

||\hat{q}_{x}\rvert|_{L^{1}}\leq C\lvert|q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon},g)}.

Let us consider

to be fixed below. We can follow the same argument as in the proof of Lemma

to deduce by the use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in order to obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|y|<b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\leq C||q\rvert|_{l;S(m^{-\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon},g)}b^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\,\int\limits_{\langle\xi\rangle\leq 3R}\langle\xi\rangle^{-n\varepsilon}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, by choosing

On the other hand, for the integral

we first note that for an integer

\displaystyle Cb^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{y\geq b}|y|^{2k}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cb^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla_{\xi}^{k}q(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

the Plancherel theorem implies the inequality

\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy\leq C\lvert|q\rvert|_{k;S(m^{-n\varepsilon},g)}b^{\frac{n}{2}-k}\left(\,\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.5)

Let
X_{j}(x,\xi)
be the principal symbol of the vector-field
X_{j},

1\leq j\leq k.
Then

a_{2}(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}(x,\xi)^{2}

(6.6)

is the principal symbol of

For any

in view of Remark

we can find a positive-definite matrix

in such a way that

1\leq\ell\leq n}a_{j,\ell}(x)\xi_{j}\xi_{\ell},\,

and with range

Then, there exists a non-singular matrix

such that
\det(\theta(x))=1
and with

(6.7)

where

1\leq j\leq r(x),
are the positive eigenvalues of the matrix

By defining

(6.8)

\|\overline{\xi}\|:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{0}}\overline{\xi}_{j}^{4}+\sum_{j=r_{0}+1}^{n}\overline{\xi}_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\,,

(6.9)

we use the following family of dilations

(6.10)

Observe that

The properties of

give the lower bound

(6.11)

In order to estimate the integral in (

If

\displaystyle\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)\leq 3R-|x|^{2}-\langle X\rangle}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\theta(x)^{-1}\overline{\xi})}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\theta(x)^{-1}\overline{\xi})|\det(\theta(x)^{-1})|d\overline{\xi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{0}}\overline{\xi}_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=r_{0}+1}^{r(x)}\lambda_{j}(x)|\overline{\xi}_{j}|^{2}+|\overline{\xi}|\right)^{-n\varepsilon-k}d\overline{\xi},

where

gives the inequality

\displaystyle\int\limits_{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R}m^{-n\varepsilon-k}(x,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{R}^{Q-2(n\varepsilon+k)}\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{0}}\overline{\xi^{\prime}}_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=r_{0}+1}^{r(x)}R\lambda_{j}(x)|\overline{\xi^{\prime}}_{j}|^{2}+|\overline{\xi^{\prime}}|\right)^{-n\varepsilon-k}d\overline{\xi^{\prime}},

where

Let us consider the set
B=\{j:\,r_{0}\leq j\leq r(x)\textnormal{ and }R\lambda_{j}(x)\geq 1\}.
Let us apply the change of variables
\overline{\xi}^{\prime}\mapsto\overline{\xi},
defined by

\overline{\xi}_{j}:=\overline{\xi}_{j}^{\prime},\,j\notin B,

and

\overline{\xi}_{j}:=\delta_{\sqrt{R\lambda_{j}(x)}}\overline{\xi}_{j}^{\prime},\,j\in B.

Since

for
j\in B,

the Jacobian of this change is

=\Pi_{j\in B}({R\lambda_{j}(x)})^{-|B|}d\overline{\xi}.

The eigenvalue conditions

imply that

|\overline{\xi}|\geq|\overline{\xi}^{\prime}|\geq C\sum_{i=r(x)+1}^{n}|\overline{\xi}^{\prime}_{i}|,\textnormal{ for some }C>0,

and taking into account that

for any

we have the estimate

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{0}}\overline{\xi^{\prime}}_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=r_{0}+1}^{r(x)}R\lambda_{j}(x)|\overline{\xi^{\prime}}_{j}|^{2}+|\overline{\xi^{\prime}}|\right)^{-n\varepsilon-k}d\overline{\xi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{0}}\overline{\xi^{\prime}}_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=r_{0}+1}^{r(x)}R\lambda_{j}(x)|\overline{\xi^{\prime}}_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{i=r(x)+1}^{n}|\overline{\xi}^{\prime}_{i}|\right)^{-n\varepsilon-k}d\overline{\xi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{0}}|\overline{\xi}_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{j=r_{0}+1}^{r(x)}|{\overline{\xi}}_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{i=r(x)+1}^{n}|\overline{\xi}_{i}|\right)^{-n\varepsilon-k}d\overline{\xi}

where

Then, we can compare

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{0}}|\overline{\xi}_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{j=r_{0}+1}^{r(x)}|{\overline{\xi}}_{j}|^{2}+\sum_{i=r(x)+1}^{n}|\overline{\xi}_{i}|\right)^{-n\varepsilon-k}d\overline{\xi}

when

\lesssim C\|q\|_{k;S(m^{-n\varepsilon},g)}R^{(\varepsilon-1)(\frac{n}{2}-k)}\left(\sqrt{R}^{Q-2\left(n\varepsilon+k\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\lesssim C\|q\|_{k;S(m^{-n\varepsilon},g)}R^{(\varepsilon-1)(\frac{n}{2}-k)+\frac{1}{4}({Q-2\left(n\varepsilon+k\right))}}.

So, to estimate the norm
\int\limits_{|y|\geq b}|\hat{q}_{x}(y)|dy
with a bound independent of

we require the following two conditions

(6.12)

and

(\varepsilon-1)(\frac{n}{2}-k)+\frac{1}{4}({Q-2\left(n\varepsilon+k\right))}\leq 0.

(6.13)

If

for

In this section we present some aspects of the spectral analysis of the Hamiltonians of the form (

[

[

Let

\mathbb{C}
endowed with an inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)

By the spectral theorem there exists an orthonormal basis for

. Let

of the operator
T

Definition 7.1
.

A compact operator
T
on a Hilbert space
H
into
H
belongs to the Schatten-von Neumann class
\mathit{S}_{p}(H)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, if

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(s_{k}(T))^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\,.

(7.1)

The sum (
7.1
) is the norm of
T
in
\mathit{S}_{p}
denoted by
\parallel\cdot\parallel_{\mathit{S}_{p}}
.

Remark 7.2
.

If we endow
\mathit{S}_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, with the norm
\parallel\cdot\parallel_{\mathit{S}_{p}}
,
then
\mathit{S}_{p}
becomes a Banach space.

As a particular case of the spaces
\mathit{S}_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
\mathit{S}_{2}
is the space of
Hilbert-Schmidt
operators. Moreover, the Schatten-von Neumann classes are nested and we have

\mathit{S}_{p}\subset\mathit{S}_{q}\,,\quad\text{if}\quad 1\leq p\leq q<\infty\,.

The analogy between the Schatten-von Neumann classes of operators and functions in the Lebesgue spaces
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the context of Weyl-Hörmander calculus, is given in the following proposition by Toft; see Remark 6.4
[
82
]
.

Proposition 7.3
.

We have the following:

(A)

If
p\in[1,\infty]
, then
S(m,g)\subset s_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
if and only if
m\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(B)

If
p\in[1,\infty]
,
a\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})\cap S(m,g)
and
h_{g}^{N/2}m\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
for some
N\geq 0
, then
a\in s_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
,

where we have denoted by
s_{\tau,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
the set of all symbols in
\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
a^{\tau}(x,D)
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, belongs to
S_{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

The theorem below is an application of Proposition
7.3
to the Hörmander classes associated with the Hamiltonians of the form (
1.7
) that we consider here, for the special case of sum of squares.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\mathbb{X}:=\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{k}\}
be a family of real vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the Hörmander condition of order
2
. Let
a_{2}(x,D)
be the Hörmander sub-Laplacian

a_{2}(x,D)=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}^{2},

(7.2)

and let us consider the metric
g
and the
g
-weight
m
defined as in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), respectively.
Then we have

m^{-\mu}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})\,,

for some
0<r<\infty
, provided that

\mu>\frac{Q}{r}=\frac{2n-r_{0}}{r}.

(7.3)

Consequently, for such choices of
\mu
and for
a\in S(m^{-\mu},g)
we have

a^{\tau}(x,D)\in\mathit{S}_{r}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\,,\quad\text{for all}\quad\tau\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Proof.

In order to estimate the norm
\|m^{-\mu}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})},
let us take a strategy from harmonic analysis and let us make a suitable triadic decomposition of the phase space. To do this we decompose
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
in triadic sectors

A_{R}:=\{R\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3R\}

(7.4)

where
R=3^{k}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}.
Also, note that as
m(x,\xi)\geq 1,
only the annulus
A_{3^{k}}
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
will contribute to the estimate of the norm
\|m^{-\mu}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}.
Indeed, by using Remark
6.5
with
s=\mu r/2>Q/2
(from which we have that
\mu r>Q\geq n
) we can estimate the norm
\|m^{-\mu}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}
as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m(x,\xi)^{-\mu r}\,dxd\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\{3^{k}\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3^{k+1}\}}m(x,\xi)^{-\mu r/2}m(x,\xi)^{-\mu r/2}dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x\rangle\leq 3^{k+1}\}}\int\limits_{\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:3^{k}\leq a_{2}(x,\xi)+|x|^{2}+\langle X\rangle\leq 3^{k+1}\}}m(x,\xi)^{-\mu r/2}m(x,\xi)^{-\mu r/2}\,d\xi\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\{\langle x\rangle\leq 3^{k+1}\}}\left(|x|^{2}\right)^{-\mu r/2}dx\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\{3^{k}\leq a_{2}(y,\xi)+|y|^{2}+\langle(y,\xi)\rangle\leq 3^{k+1}\}}m(y,\xi)^{-\mu r/2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}3^{-k\left(\mu r-n\right)}\int\limits_{\{\langle x\rangle\leq 1\}}\langle x\rangle^{-\mu r}dx\sqrt{3}^{k(Q-\mu r)}\int\limits_{\{\xi^{\prime}:\sum_{j=1}^{r(y)}{\xi^{\prime}}_{j}^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|\geq 1\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r_{0}}\overline{\xi^{\prime}}_{j}^{2}+||\overline{\xi^{\prime}}||\right)^{-\mu r}d\overline{\xi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}3^{-k\left(\mu r-n\right)}\sqrt{3}^{k(Q-\mu r)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}3^{-k\left(n-\mu r\right)}3^{k({Q}/{2}-\mu r/2)}.

Since

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}3^{-k\left(n-\mu r/2\right)}3^{k({Q}/{2}-\mu r/2)}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}3^{k/2({Q}-\mu r)}<\infty
we have proved that the condition
\mu>Q/r
implies that
m^{-\mu}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
and then an application of condition
(A)
in Proposition
7.3
finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

We note that the condition in (
7.3
) is sharp. Indeed, by testing in the elliptic case
r_{0}=n
taking the Laplacian and
r=2
, the condition (
7.3
) coincides with the well-known sharp condition
\mu>\frac{n}{2}
for the Hilbert-Schmidt class.

We now derive some consequences on the rate of growth of eigenvalues for the operator
m(x,D)=a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+J_{\langle X\rangle}
, where
a_{2}(x,D)
is as in Theorem
7.4
and
J_{\langle X\rangle}=(-\Delta+|x|^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. To do so, we will apply Theorem
7.4
to the negative powers of
m(x,D)
, then we get the rate of decay of eigenvalues and consequently the rate of growth.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
a_{2}(x,D)
be a Hörmander sub-Laplacian
as in Theorem
7.4
. Then
m(x,D)=a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+J_{\langle X\rangle}
satisfies

m(x,D)^{-\mu}\in\mathit{S}_{r}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\,,

provided
\mu>\frac{2n-r_{0}}{r}.

Moreover, the sequence of eigenvalues of
\lambda_{j}(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+J_{\langle X\rangle})
of
a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+J_{\langle X\rangle}
has a growth of order at least

j^{\frac{1}{r}},\mbox{ as }j\rightarrow\infty,

(7.5)

provided
r>2n-r_{0}
. Consequently, the sequence of eigenvalues of
\lambda_{j}(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2})
has a growth at least as (
7.5
) for
r>2n-r_{0}
.

Proof.

If
\mu>\frac{2n-r_{0}}{r},
the membership of
m(x,D)^{-\mu}
into the class
\mathit{S}_{r}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is an immediate consequence of Theorem
7.4
. On the other hand, we note that from the case
\mu=1
, we can deduce the rate of decay of the eigenvalues of
m(x,D)^{-1}
, obtaining for
r>2n-r_{0}

\lambda_{j}(m(x,D)^{-1})=\os(j^{-\frac{1}{r}}),\,\mbox{ as }j\rightarrow\infty\,.

(7.6)

From this we will obtain the following estimate for the rate of growth of the eigenvalues of
m(x,D)=a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+J_{\langle X\rangle}:\\

For every
L\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
L_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

Lj^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\lambda_{j}(m(x,D)),\,\,\mbox{ for }j\geq L_{0}.

(7.7)

Thus, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2}+J_{\langle X\rangle})
have a growth of order at least

j^{\frac{1}{r}},\mbox{ as }j\rightarrow\infty,

(7.8)

provided
r>2n-r_{0}
.

For the last part, we observe that since
2n-r_{0}=n+n-r_{0}\geq n
and the eigenvalues of
J_{\langle X\rangle}=(-\Delta+|x|^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}
are of order
j^{\frac{1}{r}}
for
r>n
. It follows that the eigenvalues of
\lambda_{j}(a_{2}(x,D)+|x|^{2})
have a growth at least as (
7.5
) for
r>2n-r_{0}
.
∎

In order to obtain (
7.6
) one can apply the Weyl inequality below which relates the singular values
s_{n}(T)
and the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}(T)
for a compact operator
T
on a complex separable Hilbert space:

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{n}(T)|^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(T)^{p},\quad p>0.

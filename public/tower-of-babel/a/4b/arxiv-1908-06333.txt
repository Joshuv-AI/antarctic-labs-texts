Asymptotic enumeration of linear hypergraphs with given number of vertices and edges

Abstract
For n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , let r = r ⁡ ( n ) ≥ 3 r=r(n)\geq 3 be an integer. A hypergraph is r r -uniform if each edge is a set of r r vertices, and is said to be linear if two edges intersect in at most one vertex. In this paper, the number of linear r r -uniform hypergraphs on n → ∞ n\to\infty vertices is determined asymptotically when the number of edges is m ⁡ ( n ) = o ⁡ ( r − 3 ​ n 3 2 ) m(n)=o(r^{-3}n^{\frac{3}{2}}) . As one application, we find the probability of linearity for the independent-edge model of random r r -uniform hypergraph when the expected number of edges is o ⁡ ( r − 3 ​ n 3 2 ) o(r^{-3}n^{\frac{3}{2}}) . We also find the probability that a random r r -uniform linear hypergraph with a given number of edges contains a given subhypergraph.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
r^{-2}n\leq m=o(r^{-3}n^{\frac{3}{2}})
.
Let

and

be the set of

[n]
of which no two vertices belong to
the same edge of
H
, where

and

Suppose that

Proof.

We will use inclusion-exclusion. Let

be the
event that a
t

[n]
contains two vertices
i
and
j

e
.
Thus, we have

\binom{n}{t}-\sum_{\{e,\{i,j\}\}}|A_{e(i,j)}|\leq|N_{t}|\leq\binom{n}{t}-\sum_{\{e,\{i,j\}\}}|A_{e(i,j)}|+\sum_{\{e,\{i,j\}\}\neq\{e^{\prime},\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\}}|A_{e(i,j)}\cap A_{e^{\prime}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}|.

Clearly,
|A_{e(i,j)}|=\binom{n-2}{t-2}
for each edge
e

and
\{i,j\}\subset e
. We have
|N_{t}|\geq\binom{n}{t}-\binom{r}{2}(m-\xi)\binom{n-2}{t-2}
.

Now we consider the upper bound. For the case
e=e^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\{e,\{i,j\}\}\neq\{e^{\prime},\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\}}

\displaystyle|A_{e(i,j)}\cap A_{e^{\prime}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}|

\displaystyle=(m-\xi)\binom{r}{3}\binom{3}{2}\binom{n-3}{t-3}+\frac{1}{2}(m-\xi)\binom{r}{4}\binom{4}{2}\binom{n-4}{t-4}

\displaystyle=O\biggl((m-\xi)r^{3}\binom{n-3}{t-3}\biggr).

(3.2)

For the case
e\neq e^{\prime}
and

, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\{e,\{i,j\}\}\neq\{e^{\prime},\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\}}

\displaystyle|A_{e(i,j)}\cap A_{e^{\prime}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}|

\displaystyle=O\biggl(\binom{m-\xi}{2}\binom{r}{2}^{2}\binom{n-4}{t-4}\biggr)

\displaystyle=O\biggl((m-\xi)^{2}r^{4}\binom{n-4}{t-4}\biggr).

(3.3)

For the case
e\neq e^{\prime}
and
|\{i,j\}\cap\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}|=1
, by Remark
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\{e,\{i,j\}\}\neq\{e^{\prime},\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\}}

\displaystyle|A_{e(i,j)}\cap A_{e^{\prime}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in[n]}\binom{\deg(v)}{2}(r-1)^{2}\binom{n-3}{t-3}

\displaystyle=O\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{r^{3}(m-\xi)n\log(r^{-2}n)+r^{5}(m-\xi)^{2}}{n}\Bigr)\binom{n-3}{t-3}\biggr).

(3.4)

For the case
e\neq e^{\prime}
and
\{i,j\}=\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}
, since there are at most
M_{s}
Type-
s

clusters with
1\leq s\leq 4
in
H
, as the equation shown in (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\{e,\{i,j\}\}\neq\{e^{\prime},\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\}}

\displaystyle|A_{e(i,j)}\cap A_{e^{\prime}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}|

\displaystyle=O\biggl(\sum_{s=1}^{4}M_{s}\binom{n-2}{t-2}\biggr)

\displaystyle=O\biggl(\Bigl(\log(r^{-2}n)+\frac{r^{4}(m-\xi)^{2}}{n^{2}}\Bigr)\binom{n-2}{t-2}\biggr).

(3.5)

By equations (
3
)–(
3
) and the assumption

, we have

\sum_{\{e,\{i,j\}\}\neq\{e^{\prime},\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\}}|A_{e(i,j)}\cap A_{e^{\prime}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}|=O\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{r^{3}mn\log(r^{-2}n)+r^{5}m^{2}}{n}\Bigr)\binom{n-3}{t-3}\biggr).

Thus, we complete the proof of Lemma

by

\frac{\binom{n-3}{t-3}}{\binom{n}{t}}=\frac{[t]_{3}}{[n]_{3}}=O\Bigl(\frac{r^{3}}{n^{3}}\Bigr),\frac{\binom{r}{2}\xi\binom{n-2}{t-2}}{\binom{n}{t}}=O\Bigl(\frac{r^{4}}{n^{2}}\Bigr)=O\Bigl(\frac{r^{8}m^{2}}{n^{4}}\Bigr),

because

,

The switching method relies on the fact that the ratio of the sizes of the two
parts of a bipartite graph is reciprocal to the ratio of their average degrees.
For our purposes we need a generalization as given in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with vertex sets
A
and
B
, where
A=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
with
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\emptyset
and

B=B_{1}\cup B_{2}
with
B_{1}\cap B_{2}=\emptyset
. Let

d_{min}^{A_{1}}
and
d_{min}^{B_{1}}
be the minimum degrees of vertices in
A_{1}
and
B_{1}
, respectively.
Let
d_{max}^{A}
and
d_{max}^{B}
be the maximum degrees of vertices in
A
and
B
, respectively. Then

\frac{d_{min}^{B_{1}}}{d_{max}^{A}}\biggl(1+\frac{|A_{2}|}{|A_{1}|}\biggr)^{\!\!-1}\leq\frac{|A_{1}|}{|B_{1}|}\leq\frac{d_{max}^{B}}{d_{min}^{A_{1}}}\biggl(1+\frac{|B_{2}|}{|B_{1}|}\biggr).

Proof.

Let
E
be the set of edges between
A_{1}
and
B_{1}
in
G
. We have

\displaystyle|A_{1}|d_{min}^{A_{1}}-|B_{2}|d_{max}^{B}\leq|E|

\displaystyle\leq|A_{1}|d_{max}^{A},~~\text{and}

\displaystyle|B_{1}|d_{min}^{B_{1}}-|A_{2}|d_{max}^{A}\leq|E|

\displaystyle\leq|B_{1}|d_{max}^{B}.

Combining these inequalities, we have

\frac{|A_{1}|d_{min}^{A_{1}}-|B_{2}|d_{max}^{B}}{|B_{1}|d_{max}^{B}}\leq 1

which gives the upper bound on
\frac{|A_{1}|}{|B_{1}|}
, and

\frac{|B_{1}|d_{min}^{B_{1}}-|A_{2}|d_{max}^{A}}{|A_{1}|d_{max}^{A}}\leq 1

which gives the lower bound.
∎

\displaystyle{\qquad}+(d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3})\biggl[\binom{n-1}{3r-4}-\binom{r}{2}m\binom{n-3}{3r-6}\biggr]

\displaystyle{\qquad}+d_{4}\biggl[\binom{n-1}{2r-2}-\binom{r}{2}m\binom{n-3}{2r-4}\biggr]

\displaystyle>d\biggl[\binom{n-1}{r}-\binom{r}{2}m\binom{n-3}{r-2}\biggr].

Assume

for some integer

. Define the
set

as the switching
strategies inverse to the above to increase the degree of

from

to

.
Clearly,
|\mathcal{S}_{v}(d-1\to d)|\leq m\binom{n-1}{r-1}
.

Thus, we have

\frac{\mathbb{P}[\deg(v)=d]}{\mathbb{P}[\deg(v)=d-1]}=\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{v}(d-1\to d)|}{|\mathcal{S}_{v}(d\to d-1)|}<\frac{m\binom{n-1}{r-1}}{d\bigl[\binom{n-1}{r}-\binom{r}{2}m\binom{n-2}{r-2}\bigr]}=\frac{rm}{dn}\Bigl(1+O\Bigl(\frac{r^{4}m}{n^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr),

after which we complete the proof as in Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to prove Ein–Lazarsfeld’s conjecture on asymptotic vanishing of syzygies of algebraic varieties. This result, together with Ein–Lazarsfeld’s asymptotic nonvanishing theorem, describes the overall picture of asymptotic behaviors of the minimal free resolutions of the graded section rings of line bundles on a projective variety as the positivity of the line bundles grows. Previously, Raicu reduced the problem to the case of products of three projective spaces, and we resolve this case here.



1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of arbitrary characteristic.
Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n
, and
L
be a very ample line bundle on
X
which
gives rise to an embedding

X\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbb{P}^{r},

where
r=h^{0}(X,L)-1
.
Denote by
S
the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Fix a coherent sheaf
B
on
X
, and let

R=R(X,B;L):=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{m})

be the graded section
S
-module of
B
associated to
L
. By the Hilbert syzygy theorem,
R
has a minimal free resolution

\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\cdots}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces E_{r}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces 0}

where

E_{p}=\bigoplus_{q}K_{p,q}(X,B;L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}S(-p-q).

The
Koszul cohomology
group
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)
is the space of
p
-th syzygies of weight
q
.
When
B=\mathscr{O}_{X}
, we set
K_{p,q}(X,L):=K_{p,q}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X};L)
.
After the pioneering work of Green
[
21
,
22
]
, there has been a considerable amount of work to understand vanishing and nonvanishing of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)
.

We say that
L
satisfies the
property
N_{k}
if
K_{0,1}(X,L)=0
and
K_{p,q}(X,L)=0
for
0\leq p\leq k
and
q\geq 2
. The property
N_{0}
means that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is projectively normal, and the property
N_{1}
means that the defining ideal of
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
is generated by quadratic polynomials. Thus the property
N_{k}
provides a natural framework to generalize classical results on defining equations of algebraic varieties to the results on their syzygies. Along this line, Green proved that if
X
is a smooth projective complex curve of genus
g
and
\deg L\geq 2g+1+k
, then
L
satisfies the property
N_{k}
(see
[
21
, Theorem (4.a.1)]
). Green’s celebrated theorem has stimulated further work in this direction, and several analogous statements for higher dimensional algebraic varieties have been established, e.g.
[
7
,
8
,
11
,
16
,
20
,
22
,
27
]
. On the other hand, Green–Lazarsfeld
[
23
,
24
]
and Ottaviani–Paoletti
[
26
]
called attention to the failure of the property
N_{k}
. The main result of
[
26
]
asserts that

K_{p,2}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d))\neq 0~~\text{ for $3d-2\leq p\leq r_{d}-2$},

where
r_{d}=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d))-1
. In particular,
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d)
does not satisfy the property
N_{3d-2}
. As
r_{d}\approx d^{2}/2
, the property
N_{k}
for
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d)
describes only a small fraction of the syzygies of the
d
-th Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Eisenbud–Green–Hulek–Popescu observed in
[
18
, Proposition 3.4]
that a similar phenomenon occurs for other smooth projective surfaces, and Ein–Lazarsfeld proved in
[
12
, Theorem A]
that this always happens for all smooth projective varieties.

It is an interesting problem to describe the overall asymptotic behaviors of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)
as the positivity of
L
grows. This type of question was first suggested by Green
[
21
, Problem 5.13]
and also considered by Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
11
, Problem 4.4]
. To set the stage for asymptotic syzygies of algebraic varieties, assume that
X
is smooth and
B
is a line bundle, and let

L_{d}:=\mathscr{O}_{X}(dA+P)~~\text{ for an integer $d\geq 1$,}

where
A
is an ample divisor and
P
is an arbitrary divisor on
X
. We suppose that
d
is sufficiently large, so in particular,
L_{d}
is very ample. Put
r_{d}:=h^{0}(X,L_{d})-1
. Elementary considerations of Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity show that

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})=0~~\text{ for $q\geq n+2$}.

When
q=0
or
n+1
,
[
12
, Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.2]
state that

\begin{array}[]{l}K_{p,0}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~\Longleftrightarrow~0\leq p\leq h^{0}(B)-1;\\
K_{p,n+1}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~\Longleftrightarrow~r_{d}-n-h^{0}(X,K_{X}-B)+1\leq p\leq r_{d}-n.\end{array}

The main issue is then to study vanishing and nonvanishing of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
for
1\leq q\leq n
.
Motivated by the nonvanishing results of
[
18
,
26
]
, Ein–Lazarsfeld established the
asymptotic nonvanishing theorem
in
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
: For each
1\leq q\leq n
, there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that if
d
is sufficiently large, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~~\text{ for $C_{1}d^{q-1}\leq p\leq r_{d}-C_{2}d^{n-1}$}.

If furthermore
H^{i}(X,B)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0~~\text{ for $C_{1}d^{q-1}\leq p\leq r_{d}-C_{2}d^{n-q}$};

in this case with
P=0
, a quick proof is provided in
[
10
]
with effective range of
p
. It is worth noting that Zhou adapted the arguments in
[
12
]
to show that the asymptotic nonvanishing theorem also holds for singular projective varieties with a relaxed assumption on
B
(see
[
12
, Remark 4.2]
). The influential paper
[
12
]
opens the door to research on the asymptotic behaviors of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
for
d
increasing. We refer to
[
14
]
for survey on recent progress on asymptotic syzygies of algebraic varieties.

It is very natural to ask whether the
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
vanish for the values of
p
outside the range in the statement of the asymptotic nonvanishing theorem. Ein–Lazarsfeld conjectured that this is indeed the case for
p<O(d^{q-1})
(see
[
12
, Conjecture 7.1]
,
[
14
, Conjecture 1.10]
). We confirm this
asymptotic vanishing conjecture
in a more general setting.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n
, and
B
be a coherent sheaf on
X
. Fix an ample divisor
A
and an arbitrary divisor
P
on
X
, and put
L_{d}:=\mathscr{O}_{X}(dA+P)
for an integer
d\geq 1
.
For each
1\leq q\leq n+1
, there is a constant
C>0
depending on
X,A,B,P
such that if
d
is sufficiently large, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq Cd^{q-1}$}.

We give some remarks on vanishing of
K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})
for large
p
. For simplicity, we assume that
X
is smooth and
B
is a vector bundle. If
H^{i}(X,B)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, then the duality theorem (cf.
[
12
, Proposition 3.5]
,
[
21
, Theorem 2.c.6]
) says that

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})=K_{r_{d}-n-p,n+1-q}(X,B^{*}\otimes\omega_{X};L_{d})^{*}.

Then our asymptotic vanishing theorem implies the following: For each
1\leq q\leq n
, there is a constant
C>0
such that if
d
is sufficiently large, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})=0~~\text{ for $p\geq r_{d}-Cd^{n-q}$}.

However, if
H^{q-1}(X,B)\neq 0
for some
2\leq q\leq n
, then
K_{r_{d}-q+1,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0
for large
d
(see
[
12
, Remark 5.3]
). When
X
is a smooth projective complex curve and
B
is a line bundle, vanishing of weight-one syzygies
K_{p,1}(X,B;L_{d})
for large
p
is determined by the duality theorem and
[
13
, Theorem B]
(see also
[
29
]
). This implies Green–Lazarsfeld’s gonality conjecture, and a higher dimensional generalization is treated in the work of Ein–Lazarsfeld–Yang
[
15
]
.

Shortly after the asymptotic vanishing conjecture was proposed, Raicu showed in the appendix of
[
28
]
that the general case of the conjecture follows from the case of products of three projective spaces. The case that
q=1
in Theorem
1.1
is trivial. To prove Theorem
1.1
, it is more than enough to establish the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer,
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k},d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
be positive integers, and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
be integers. Set

\begin{array}[]{l}X:=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}},~B:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(b_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}}}(b_{k}),~L:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(d_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}}}(d_{k}),\end{array}

and
b:=\min\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\},~d:=\min\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}
.
For each
2\leq q\leq n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}+1
, if
d+b\geq 0
, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k}!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})$}.

As
K_{p,q+1}(X,B;L)=K_{p,q}(X,B+L;L)
, it is reasonable to assume that
b<d
in Theorem
1.2
. But we do not need this assumption for the proof.

We give a sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.2
for the Veronese case.
Let
M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)}
be the kernel of the evaluation map
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\to\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
. It is well known that

K_{p,q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))=H^{q-1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\wedge^{p+q-1}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)}(d))~~\text{ for $p\geq 0$ and $q\geq 2$}.

The main idea is to work on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
instead of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
via the finite map
\sigma\colon\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
given by
(\xi,z)\mapsto\xi+z
, where
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is regarded as the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\sigma
is the universal family. Note that
\sigma_{*}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n-1))=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{\oplus n}
. For each
2\leq q\leq n+1
, the problem is equivalent to showing that

H^{q-1}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\wedge^{p+q-1}\sigma^{*}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d)\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d+n-1))=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq O(d^{q-1})$}.

An advantage of working on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is that we can use the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-n)\longrightarrow\sigma^{*}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)}\longrightarrow M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d)}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d)\longrightarrow 0,

which provides a way to proceed by induction on
n
.
By considering the natural filtration of
\wedge^{p+q-1}\sigma^{*}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)}
, we reduce the problem to proving that

H^{q-1}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\wedge^{i}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d)}(d)\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i}))=0~~\text{ for $0\leq i\leq O(d^{q-1})$},

where
a_{i}=id-(p+q-1-i)n+d+n-1
.
By induction on
n
, we can assume that
H^{j}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\wedge^{i}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d)}(d))=0
for
0\leq i\leq O(d^{j})
and
j=q-2,q-1
. By the Künneth formula, it is sufficient to check that

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i}))=0~~\text{ when $i\geq O(d^{q-2})$}.

But we have

a_{i}=id+(d+2n+in-qn)-1-pn\geq O(d^{q-1})-1-pn\geq-1

as soon as
0\leq p\leq O(d^{q-1})
. Thus
K_{p,q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))=0
for this range of
p
. The same argument works for the general Segre–Veronese case.

There has been a great deal of attention to the syzygies of Veronese or Segre–Veronese embeddings, e.g.
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
19
,
25
,
26
,
28
,
30
]
. The syzygies of these varieties have connections to representation theory and combinatorics. It would be exceedingly interesting to know whether the method of the present paper could make progress on the study of the Veronese or Segre–Veronese syzygies.

The paper is organized as follows. After reviewing basic necessary facts in Section
2
, we prove Theorem
1.2
in Section
3
, where we also show Theorem
1.1
following Raicu’s argument in
[
28
]
. Section
4
is devoted to presenting some open problems on asymptotic syzygies of algebraic varieties.

2.
Preliminaries

We collect basic facts which are used to prove the main theorems of the paper.

2.1.
Koszul Cohomology

Let
X
be a projective variety,
B
be a coherent sheaf on
X
, and
L
be a very ample line bundle on
X
, which gives an embedding

X\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbb{P}^{r}.

Let
S:=S(H^{0}(X,L))=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}S^{m}H^{0}(X,L)
be the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, and

R=R(X,B;L):=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{m})

be the graded section
S
-module of
B
associated to
L
.
Denote by
S_{+}\subseteq S
the irrelevant maximal ideal, and define the
Koszul cohomology group
to be

K_{p,q}(X,B;L):=\Tor_{p}^{S}(R,S/S_{+})_{p+q}.

Then
R
has a minimal free resolution

\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\cdots}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces E_{r}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces 0}

where

E_{p}=\bigoplus_{q}K_{p,q}(X,B;L)\otimes_{\Bbbk}S(-p-q).

Notice that
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)
is the vector space of
p
-th syzygies of weight
q
and it is the cohomology of the Koszul-type complex

\begin{array}[]{l}\wedge^{p+1}H^{0}(X,L)\otimes H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{q-1})\longrightarrow\wedge^{p}H^{0}(X,L)\otimes H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{q})\\[5.0pt]
~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }~\text{ }\longrightarrow\wedge^{p-1}H^{0}(X,L)\otimes H^{0}(X,B\otimes L^{q+1}).\end{array}

When
B=\mathscr{O}_{X}
, we set
K_{p,q}(X,L):=K_{p,q}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X};L)
.

Now, let
L
be a globally generated line bundle on a projective variety
X
.
Consider the evaluation map

\ev\colon H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\longrightarrow L,

which is surjective since
L
is globally generated. Denote by
M_{L}
the kernel bundle of the evaluation map
\ev
. Then we obtain a short exact sequence of vector bundles on
X
:

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow M_{L}\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0.

We use the following well-known fact to compute the Koszul cohomology group.

Proposition 2.1

(cf.
[
12
, Corollary 3.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety,
B
be a coherent sheaf on
X
, and
L
be a very ample line bundle on
X
.
Assume that
H^{i}(X,B\otimes L^{m})=0
for
i>0
and
m>0
. Fix
q\geq 2
. Then we have

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=H^{q-1}(X,\wedge^{p+q-1}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L)~\text{ for $p\geq 0$}.

In particular,
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0
for
0\leq p\leq p_{0}
if and only if

H^{q-1}(X,\wedge^{j}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L)=0~~\text{ for $0\leq j\leq p_{0}+q-1$}.

Proof.

By taking wedge product of (
2.1
), we have a short exact sequence

(2.2)

0\longrightarrow\wedge^{p+1}M_{L}\longrightarrow\wedge^{p+1}H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\wedge^{p}M_{L}\otimes L\longrightarrow 0.

By using the Koszul-type complex and chasing through the diagram, we see that

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=H^{1}(X,\wedge^{p+1}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L^{q-1})~~\text{ for $p\geq 0$ and $q\geq 2$}.

See
[
3
, Section 2.1]
or
[
11
, Section 1]
for the complete proof. Now, from (
2.2
), we find that

H^{1}(X,\wedge^{p+1}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L^{q-1})=H^{2}(X,\wedge^{p+2}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L^{q-2})=\cdots=H^{q-1}(X,\wedge^{p+q-1}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L),

so the first assertion holds. Thus
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0
for
0\leq p\leq p_{0}
if and only if

H^{q-1}(X,\wedge^{j}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L)=0~~\text{ for $q-1\leq j\leq p_{0}+q-1$}.

But we get from (
2.2
) that

H^{q-1}(X,\wedge^{i}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L)=H^{q-2}(X,\wedge^{i-1}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L^{2})=\cdots=H^{q-i-1}(X,B\otimes L^{i+1})=0

for any
0\leq i\leq q-2
. Thus the second assertion holds.
∎

We refer to
[
3
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
21
]
for more details on syzygies and Koszul cohomology.

2.2.
Filtrations for Wedge Products

For a short exact sequence
0\to U\to V\to W\to 0
of vector bundles on a projective variety
X
and an integer
k\geq 1
, there is a natural filtration

(2.3)

0=F^{0}\subseteq F^{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F^{k}\subseteq F^{k+1}=\wedge^{k}V

such that

F^{p+1}/F^{p}=\wedge^{k-p}U\otimes\wedge^{p}W~~\text{ for all $0\leq p\leq k$. }

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0\to U\to V\to W\to 0
be a short exact sequence of vector bundles on a projective variety
X
. If
H^{q}(X,\wedge^{k-p}U\otimes\wedge^{p}W)=0
for all
0\leq p\leq k
, then
H^{q}(X,\wedge^{k}V)=0
.

Proof.

By considering the natural filtration (
2.3
), we immediately obtain the lemma.
∎

2.3.
Divided and Symmetric Powers

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\Bbbk
. For an integer
n\geq 1
, the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
naturally acts on the tensor power
T^{n}V:=V^{\otimes n}
by permuting the factors.
The
divided power
of
V
is the subspace

D^{n}V:=\{\omega\in T^{n}V\mid\sigma(\omega)=\omega~\text{for all $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}$}\}\subseteq T^{n}V,

while the
symmetric power

S^{n}V
of
V
is the quotient of
T^{n}V
by the subspace spanned by
\sigma(\omega)-\omega
for all
\omega\in T^{n}V
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. When
n=0
, we set
T^{0}V=D^{0}V=S^{0}V=\Bbbk
. We have a natural identification

D^{n}V=(S^{n}V^{*})^{*}.

By composing the inclusion of
D^{n}V
into
T^{n}V
with the projection onto
S^{n}V
, we have a natural map
D^{n}V\to S^{n}V
. This map is an isomorphism in characteristic zero, but it may be neither injective nor surjective in general. We can also define divided and symmetric powers of vector bundles on projective varieties. We refer to
[
2
, Section 3]
for more details.

Let
0\to U\to V\to W\to 0
be a short exact sequence of vector bundles on a projective variety
X
with
\rank W=1
. Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}(V^{*})\to X
be the natural projection. Note that
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}(-\mathbb{P}(U^{*}))=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}(-1)\otimes\pi^{*}W^{*}
. For
k\geq 0
, we have a short exact sequence on
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
:

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}(k)\otimes\pi^{*}W^{*}\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}(k+1)\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(U^{*})}(k+1)\longrightarrow 0.

By applying
\pi_{*}
, we get a short exact sequence on
X
:

0\longrightarrow S^{k}V^{*}\otimes W^{*}\longrightarrow S^{k+1}V^{*}\longrightarrow S^{k+1}U^{*}\longrightarrow 0.

This construction was suggested by Lawrence Ein, and a purely algebraic construction of this kind of exact sequence can be found in
[
1
, Corollary V.1.15]
.
By taking the dual, we obtain a short exact sequence on
X
:

(2.4)

0\longrightarrow D^{k+1}U\longrightarrow D^{k+1}V\longrightarrow D^{k}V\otimes W\longrightarrow 0.

We remark that
S^{k+1}V\to S^{k}V\otimes W
may not be surjective in positive characteristic.

Now, let
C
be a smooth projective curve, and
L
be a line bundle on
C
. For an integer
k\geq 0
, the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{k+1}
naturally acts on the
(k+1)
-th ordinary product
C^{k+1}
of
C
by permuting the components, and the line bundle

L^{\boxtimes k+1}:=\underbrace{L\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L}_{\text{$k+1$ times}}

on
C^{k+1}
descends to a line bundle
T_{k+1}(L)
on the
(k+1)
-th symmetric product
C_{k+1}
of
C
(see
[
16
, Subsection 3.1]
). Note that

H^{0}(C_{k+1},T_{k+1}(L))=H^{0}(C^{k+1},L^{\boxtimes k+1})^{\mathfrak{S}_{k+1}}=D^{k+1}H^{0}(C,L).

If
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
L=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d)
with
d\geq 1
, then
(\mathbb{P}^{1})_{n}=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
T_{n}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d))=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
. By an arbitrary characteristic version of Hermite reciprocity (see
[
2
, Remark 3.2]
), we have

\begin{array}[]{l}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},T_{n}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d)))~=~D^{n}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d))~=~D^{n}(S^{d}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)))\\[5.0pt]
~~=~S^{d}(D^{n}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)))~=~S^{d}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},T_{n}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1))).\end{array}

2.4.
Tautological Bundles on Projective Spaces

Recall that
\sigma\colon\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
is the finite map of degree
n
given by
(\xi,z)\mapsto\xi+z
by viewing
\mathbb{P}^{n}
as the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For any integer
k
, the
tautological bundle
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is defined as

E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}:=\sigma_{*}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)),

which is a vector bundle of rank
n
. The tautological bundles on symmetric products of curves play an important role in the study of secant varieties of curves (see
[
16
]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}
is splitting if and only if
-1\leq k\leq n-1
. In this case,

E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{\oplus k+1}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)^{\oplus n-1-k}.

Proof.

Since
\sigma\colon\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a finite map, we have

H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{n},E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}(m))=H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(m)\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(m+k)).

for any
i\geq 0
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The Künneth formula shows that

\begin{array}[]{l}H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(m)\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(m+k))\\[5.0pt]
=\big(H^{i-1}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(m))\otimes H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(m+k))\big)\oplus\big(H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(m))\otimes H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(m+k))\big).\end{array}

Then we see that

\begin{array}[]{l}H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{n},E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}(m))=0~~\text{ for $2\leq i\leq n-2$ and $m\in\mathbb{Z}$},\\[5.0pt]
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}(m))=0~\text{for $m\in\mathbb{Z}$}~\Longleftrightarrow~k\geq-1,\\[5.0pt]
H^{n-1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}(m))=0~\text{for $m\in\mathbb{Z}$}~\Longleftrightarrow~k\leq n-1.\end{array}

By the Horrocks criterion, the first assertion of the lemma follows. Now, we observe that
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)})=k+1
, and
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}(1))=n(k+2)=(n+1)(k+1)+n-1-k
. This implies the second assertion of the lemma.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The following alternative approach to Lemma
2.3
was suggested by Lawrence Ein. Let
D_{n}
be the image of the injective map
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
given by
(\xi,z)\mapsto(\xi+z,z)
. Note that
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-D_{n})=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-n)
. For any integer
k
, we have a short exact sequence on
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k-n)\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)\longrightarrow 0.

Let
p\colon\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
be the projection to the first component.
When
-1\leq k\leq n-1
, by applying
p_{*}
, we obtain a short exact sequence on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
:

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k))\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\longrightarrow E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}\longrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k-n))\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)\longrightarrow 0.

Thus
E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}=(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k))\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})\oplus(H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k-n))\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1))
. When
k\leq-2
or
k\geq n
, it is easy to check that
E_{n,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)}
is not splitting.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
Y
be a projective variety, and
\sigma\colon Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
be the finite map given by
(y,\xi,z)\mapsto(y,\xi+z)
. If
M
is a vector bundle on
Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
, then

H^{q}(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n},M)=0~\Longleftrightarrow~H^{q}(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\sigma^{*}M\otimes(\mathscr{O}_{Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n-1)))=0

for any
q\geq 0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
and the projection formula,

\sigma_{*}\big(\sigma^{*}M\otimes(\mathscr{O}_{Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n-1))\big)=M^{\oplus n}
.
Since
\sigma
is a finite map, the assertion immediately follows.
∎

3.2.
Case of Product of Projective Spaces

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Recall that

\begin{array}[]{l}X=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}},~B=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(b_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}}}(b_{k}),~L=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(d_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}}}(d_{k}),\end{array}

and
b=\min\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\},~d=\min\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}
. Fix
2\leq q\leq n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}+1
. Our aim is to show that if
d+b\geq 0
, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k}!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})$.}

We put

Y:=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k-1}},~B_{Y}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(b_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{k-1}}}(b_{k-1}),~L_{Y}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(d_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{k-1}}}(d_{k-1}),

and
n:=n_{k}
. Then

X=Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n},~B=B_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(b_{k}),~L=L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d_{k}).

As
md+b\geq 0
for any
m>0
, we have

H^{i}(X,B\otimes L^{m})=0~~\text{ for $i>0$ and $m>0$}.

By Proposition
2.1
, for
p\geq 0
, we have

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=H^{q-1}\big(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n},\wedge^{p+q-1}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}+B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d_{k}+b_{k}))\big).

We proceed by induction on
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}
. If
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=1
, then
q=2
and the problem is to check the cohomology vanishing

H^{1}\big(\mathbb{P}^{1},\wedge^{p+1}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d)}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d+b)\big)=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq d+b$.}

As
\wedge^{p+1}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d)}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d+b)=\bigoplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d+b-p-1)
and
d+b-p-1\geq-1
, the desired cohomology vanishing immediately follows.

Assume that
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}\geq 2
. Fix
0\leq p\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k}!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})
. By Lemma
2.5
, it is sufficient to show the cohomology vanishing on
Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

H^{q-1}\big(\wedge^{p+q-1}\sigma^{*}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}+B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d_{k}+b_{k}+n-1))\big)=0,

where
\sigma\colon Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
is the finite map given by
(y,\xi,z)\mapsto(y,\xi+z)
.
By considering the short exact sequence (
3.1
) and applying Lemma
2.2
to
\wedge^{p+q-1}\sigma^{*}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d_{k})}
, we can reduce the problem to proving the following:

H^{q-1}\big(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},(\wedge^{i}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}+B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k})))\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i})\big)=0

for
0\leq i\leq p+q-1~(\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k}!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})+q-1)
, where

a_{i}:=id_{k}-(p+q-1-i)n+d_{k}+b_{k}+n-1=i(d_{k}+n)+d_{k}+b_{k}+2n-qn-1-pn.

By the Künneth formula, it is equivalent to showing that

(3.2)

H^{q-1}\big(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\wedge^{i}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}+B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k}))\big)\otimes H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i}))=0

and

(3.3)

H^{q-2}\big(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\wedge^{i}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}+B_{Y}))\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k})\big)\otimes H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i}))=0

for
0\leq i\leq p+q-1
.

By induction and Proposition
2.1
, we can assume that

(3.4)

H^{q-1}\big(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\wedge^{i}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}+B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k}))\big)=0

for
0\leq i\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k-1}!(n_{k}-1)!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})+q-1
when
q\geq 2
, and

(3.5)

H^{q-2}\big(Y\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\wedge^{i}M_{L_{Y}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k})}\otimes((L_{Y}+B_{Y})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d_{k}+b_{k}))\big)=0

for
0\leq i\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k-1}!(n_{k}-1)!)(d^{q-2}+bd^{q-3})+q-2
when
q\geq 3
.
Observe that (
3.2
) immediately follows from (
3.4
). It only remains to check (
3.3
). If
q=2
, then

a_{i}=i(d_{k}+n)+d_{k}+b_{k}-1-np\geq d_{k}+b_{k}-1-np\geq d+b-1-np\geq-1

since
p\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k}!)(d+b)\leq(1/n)(d+b)
. Thus

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i}))=0,

so the cohomology vanishing (
3.3
) holds for
q=2
. Next, we consider the case that
q\geq 3
. If
0\leq i\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k-1}!(n_{k}-1)!)(d^{q-2}+bd^{q-3})+q-2
, then (
3.3
) immediately follows from (
3.5
). If
i\geq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k-1}!(n_{k}-1)!)(d^{q-2}+bd^{q-3})+q-1
, then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}a_{i}&=&id_{k}+\underbrace{(d_{k}+b_{k}+2n+in-qn)}_{\geq 0}-1-np\\
&\geq&id-1-np\\
&\geq&(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k-1}!(n-1)!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})-1-np\\
&\geq&-1\end{array}

since
p\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k-1}!n!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})
. Thus

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i}))=0,

so the cohomology vanishing (
3.3
) holds in this case as well. We have shown (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), and they imply
K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0
as desired. We complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

3.3.
General Case

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.1
. As we mentioned in the introduction, Raicu proved that Theorem

can be deduced from Theorem

for the case

(see

Recall that

Our aim is to show that for each

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq Cd^{q-1}$}.

We can choose integers

with

such that

(3.6)

a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are depending only on

.
As

is sufficiently large, we can find integers

such that

and

. Let

. Then

, and
L_{d}=\mathscr{O}_{X}(d_{1}A_{1}+d_{2}A_{2}+d_{3}A_{3})
. Note that
d_{1}<d_{3}<d_{2}
.

Next, consider the commutative diagrams

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,A_{1})\times\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,A_{2})\times\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,A_{3})=:\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{3}}=Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{r}:=\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,L_{d})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}H^{0}(Y,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(d_{1})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}}(d_{2})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{3}}}(d_{3}))=:\mathbb{P}^{N}.}

Clearly,
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}
are depending only on
X,A,P
.
Notice that
\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
by the surjectivity of (
3.6
). We can regard
B
as a coherent sheaf on
Y,~\mathbb{P}^{r},
and
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. The syzygies of
B
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
are the syzygies of
B
on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
tensoring with a Koszul complex of linear forms.
By letting

L_{Y}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(d_{1})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}}(d_{2})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{3}}}(d_{3}),

we see that

(3.7)

\min\{p\mid K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0\}=\min\{p\mid K_{p,q}(Y,B;L_{Y})\neq 0\}.

Let
S:=S(H^{0}(X,A_{1})\oplus H^{0}(X,A_{2})\oplus H^{0}(X,A_{3}))
be the total coordinate ring of
Y=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{3}}
with the usual
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
-grading. Then

M:=\bigoplus_{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{\geq 0}}H^{0}(Y,B\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(a_{1})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}}(a_{2})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{3}}}(a_{3}))

is a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Consider the minimal free resolution of
M
:

\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\cdots}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces E_{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where

E_{i}=\bigoplus_{b^{j}=(b_{1}^{j},b_{2}^{j},b_{3}^{j})\in S_{i}}F_{i,b^{j}}\otimes S(b^{j})

for some finite dimensional vector space

over
\Bbbk
and finite subsets

. Let

B_{Y}^{j}:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(b_{1}^{j})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{2}}}(b_{2}^{j})\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{3}}}(b_{3}^{j})~~\text{ and }~~b:=\min\{b_{1}^{j},b_{2}^{j},b_{3}^{j}\mid b^{j}\in S_{i},0\leq i\leq m\}.

Note that

is depending only on
X,A,B,P
.
Now, fix
2\leq q\leq n+1~(\leq n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+1)
. By Theorem
1.2
, for
0\leq i\leq m
and
b^{j}\in S_{i}
, we have

(3.8)

K_{p-i,q+i}(Y,B_{Y}^{j};L_{Y})=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq(1/n_{1}!n_{2}!n_{3}!)(d_{1}^{q+i-1}+bd_{1}^{q+i-2})+i$}.

Then (

) and

, Theorem A.1]
(cf.
[

) show that

(3.9)

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})=K_{p,q}(Y,B;L_{Y})=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq(1/n_{1}!n_{2}!n_{3}!)(d_{1}^{q-1}+bd_{1}^{q-2})$}.

Recall that the numbers

are depending only on

but not on

. Since

grows linearly in

and

is sufficiently large, Theorem

follows.

.

Instead of applying

4.
Open Problems

In this section, we discuss some open problems and conjectures. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
, and
B
be a coherent sheaf on
X
. Fix an ample divisor
A
and an arbitrary divisor
P
on
X
, and put
L_{d}:=\mathscr{O}_{X}(dA+P)
for an integer
d\geq 1
.

For each
2\leq q\leq n+1
, it would be extremely interesting to find an explicit constant
c>0
in terms of
X,A,B,P
, and
q,d
such that if
d
is sufficiently large, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})=0
for
0\leq p\leq c
and
K_{c+1,q}(X,B;L_{d})\neq 0
.

However, this problem is already very difficult for
q=2
. A generalization of Mukai’s conjecture (cf.
[
11
, Conjecture 4.2]
) asks whether the property
N_{d}
holds for
K_{X}+(n+2+d)A
when
X
is a smooth projective complex variety. But it is widely open even when
n=2
and
d=0
. Moreover, Fujita’s conjecture, which predicts that
K_{X}+(n+2+d)A
is very ample for
d\geq 0
, is unknown when
n\geq 3
. However, when
A
is very ample, Ein–Lazarsfeld established in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
that

K_{p,q}(X,K_{X}+(n+1+d)A)=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq d$ and $q\geq 2$}.

It is reasonable to expect extending this result for
q\geq 3
.

Problem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
n
, and
A
be a very ample divisor on
X
.
For each
2\leq q\leq n+1
and
d\geq 0
, find an explicit polynomial
P(x)
of degree
q-1
such that

K_{p,q}(X,K_{X}+(n+1+d)A)=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq P(d)$}

One can also consider the effective asymptotic vanishing problem for the syzygies of products of projective spaces.

Problem 4.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer,
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k},d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
be positive integers, and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
be integers. Set

\begin{array}[]{l}X:=\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}},~B:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(b_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}}}(b_{k}),~L:=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{1}}}(d_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n_{k}}}(d_{k}),\end{array}

For each
2\leq q\leq n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}+1
, find a constant
c>0
in terms of
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k},d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
, and
q
such that if
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
are sufficiently large, then

(4.1)

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0
for
0\leq p\leq c
and
K_{c+1,q}(X,B;L)\neq 0
.

In Theorem
1.2
, we prove that if
d+b\geq 0
, then

K_{p,q}(X,B;L)=0~~\text{ for $0\leq p\leq(1/n_{1}!\cdots n_{k}!)(d^{q-1}+bd^{q-2})$},

where
b:=\min\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\},~d:=\min\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\}
. By a small modification of the proof, we can improve the bound on
p
, but our method only gives a bound on
p
depending on dimensions
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
. It is expected that the constant
c
in Problem
4.2
is independent of
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
. It would be also interesting to find a constant
d>0
in terms of
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k},n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
, and
q
such that if
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\geq d
, then (
4.1
) holds. When
k=1
, there is a precise prediction on
c
and
d
(see
[
12
, Conjecture 7.5]
,
[
14
, Conjecture 2.3]
).

Conjecture 4.3

(Ein–Lazarsfeld)
.

Fix
n\geq 1
,
b\geq 0
, and
0\leq q\leq n
. If
d\geq b+q+1
, then

K_{p,q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(b);\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))=0~~\text{ for }~0\leq p\leq{d+q\choose q}-{d-b-1\choose q}-q-1.

Notice that the conjecture gives the precise vanishing range because Ein–Erman–Lazarsfeld
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
(see also
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
) proved that
K_{p,q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(b);\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))\neq 0
for all

{d+q\choose q}-{d-b-1\choose q}-q\leq p\leq{d+n\choose n}+{d+n-q\choose n-q}-{n+b\choose q+b}-q-1,

In
[
26
]
, Ottaviani–Paoletti conjectured that if
n\geq 3,d\geq 3
, then
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
satisfies the property
N_{3d-3}
. They also consider the cases that
n\leq 2
or
d\leq 2
, but these cases are already settled. By
[
26
, Theorem 1.6]
, the property
N_{3d-3}
for
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
is implied by that
K_{p,2}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))=0
for
0\leq p\leq 3d-3
. Thus Conjecture
4.3
for
b=0
and
q=2
is equivalent to Ottaviani–Paoletti’s conjecture. At this moment, we only know that
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
satisfies the property
N_{d+1}
by Bruns–Conca–Römer
[
7
]
, and a small change of the proof of Theorem
1.2
yields that
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(d)
satisfies the property
N_{d+2}
. A new idea might be needed to solve Conjecture
4.3
.

It is also a fascinating problem to study the asymptotic behavior of the
Betti numbers

k_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d}):=\dim K_{p,q}(X,B;L_{d})

when
d
is sufficiently large (see
[
12
, Problem 7.3]
). In this direction, Ein–Erman–Lazarsfeld conjectured that for each
1\leq q\leq n
, the Betti numbers
k_{p,q}(X,L_{d})
converge to a normal distribution (see
[
9
, Conjecture B]
,
[
14
, Conjecture 3.2]
). This normal distribution conjecture has not been verified even for
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and it seems that the conjecture is already very challenging for Veronese embeddings (cf.
[
5
,
6
]
).

Conjecture 4.4

(Ein–Erman–Lazarsfeld)
.

Fix
n\geq 1
and
1\leq q\leq n
. Then there is a normalizing function
F_{q}(d)
such that

F_{q}(d)\cdot k_{p_{d},q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))\longrightarrow e^{-a^{2}/2}

as
d\to\infty
and
p_{d}\to r_{d}/2+a\sqrt{r_{d}}/2
.

Notice that

k_{p,q}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d))=\frac{1}{n}\cdot h^{q}\big(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\wedge^{p+q}\sigma^{*}M_{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)}\otimes(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n-1))\big)

for
p\geq 0
and
1\leq q\leq n
. It is tempting to wonder if there is a clever way to compute this Betti number.

We refer to
[
6
]
and
[
12
,
14
]
for more problems and conjectures on syzygies of Veronese embeddings and asymptotic syzygies of algebraic varieties, respectively.

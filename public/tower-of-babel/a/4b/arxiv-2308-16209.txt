A New Generating Function for Hermite Polynomials
Manouchehr Amiri

Abstract
This paper presents a new generating function for Hermite polynomials of one variable in the form of g ⁡ ( x , t ) = ∑ n = 0 ∞ t n ​ H n e ​ ( x ) g(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x) and reveals its connection with incomplete gamma function. Recurrence relations of Hermite polynomials are derived by a new generating function. A definition for two-variable Hermite generating function based on this generating function has been proposed.



Keywords—
Hermite polynomials, Generating function, Incomplete gamma function, Error function

A New Generating Function

In this section, a new generating function for one-variable Hermite polynomials is introduced. The recurrence relations and connections to Incomplete gamma function, Error function and cumulative distribution function are discussed in the subsections.

2.1
New Generating Function for one-variable Hermite Polynomials

Known generating function for Hermite polynomials of one variable is represented as:

\sum_{0}^{\infty}H^{e}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{xt-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}

(1)

Where
H^{e}_{n}(x)=He_{n}(x)
is denoted as probabilistic Hermite polynomials. This generating function contains a factorial term
\frac{1}{n!}
. We present a new generating function without the factorial term in the related sum.
Probabilistic Hermite polynomials
H^{e}_{n}(x)
are presented as:

H^{e}_{n}(x)=e^{\frac{-D^{2}}{2}}x^{n}

(2)

Where
D=\frac{d}{dx}
. We apply this operator equation to obtain a generating function for
H^{e}_{n}(x)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R},t\in\mathbb{R}-\left\{0\right\},n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}

A generating function for Hermite polynomials is:

g(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t^{2}}}e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})

(3)

Proof.

Acting operator
O=e^{\frac{-\partial^{2}_{x}}{2}}
from equation (2) on the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{1-xt}
, yields a generating function for
H_{n}^{e}(x)
:

g(x,t)=e^{\frac{-\partial^{2}_{x}}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}x^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H_{n}^{e}(x)=e^{\frac{-\partial^{2}_{x}}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{1-xt}\right)

(4)

The convergence of
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{1-xt}
requires
\left|xt\right|<1
or
0<1-xt<2

Expanding the right side of equation (4) gives:

g(x,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{t^{2j}(1-xt)^{-2j-1}(2j)!}{2^{j}j!}

(5)

With respect to the identity
\frac{(2j)!}{2^{j}j!}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}(-2)^{j}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-j)}
and denoting
y=t^{-1}(1-xt)
we have:

g(x,t)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{(1-xt)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\frac{y^{2}}{2})^{-j}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-j)^{-1}

(6)

By the identity for
n-th
derivative of
z^{s}
when
n\in\mathbb{N}
we get

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}z^{s}=\frac{\Gamma(s+1)}{\Gamma(s+1-n)}z^{s-n}

(7)

For
s=-\frac{1}{2}
we have:

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-n)}z^{-\frac{1}{2}-n}

(8)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})z^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-n}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-n)}

(9)

The strict condition for convergence of this formal series is
\left|z\right|>1
. Rewriting equation (9) by replacing
j=n
on the right side, yields:

z^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})z^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-j}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-j)}

(10)

By changing the variable
\frac{y^{2}}{2}=z
and with respect to
1-xt>0
, for
z^{-\frac{1}{2}}
we have:

z^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{2}\frac{\left|t\right|}{1-xt}=\frac{\sqrt{2t^{2}}}{1-xt}

(11)

By this equation and comparing equation (10) with (6) and applying the identity
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}
, equation (10) becomes:

z^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{2t^{2}}g(x,t)

(12)

The left side of equation (12) can be calculated as follows:

z^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{(1-\frac{d}{dz})}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(13)

Then by the following integral identity we get

\frac{1}{(1-\frac{d}{dz})}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}=-e^{z}\int e^{-z}z^{-\frac{1}{2}}dz=-e^{z}\left[-\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},z)\right]

(14)

By Calculating the integral, the equation (12) becomes:

g(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t^{2}}}e^{z}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},z)

(15)

The term
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},z)
on the right side is incomplete gamma function. By writing
z
in terms of
t
and
x
we obtain:

g(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t^{2}}}e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})

(16)

∎

This is a new generating function for Hermite polynomials. As a test for validation of equation (16) at the limit
t\to 0
, by knowing the limit:

\lim_{t\to 0}g(x,t)=\lim_{t\to 0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=1

(17)

We can verify the limit of the right side of (16) when
t\to 0
.

\lim_{t\to 0}g(x,t)=\lim_{t\to 0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t^{2}}}e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})=1

(18)

For validation of equation (18), this limit was calculated by online calculator for some values of
\lim_{t\to 0}g(x,t)
. Calculations of equation (15) for
z=1200
and
z=2000
while
t
is small and
x=1
, result in 0.994 and 0.997 for
g(x,t)
respectively. This intuitively verifies the limit in equation (18).

Remark

Due to the term
\sqrt{2}\left|t\right|=\sqrt{2t^{2}}
in the generating function (16), the derivative of the generating function could be calculated for
t>0
and
t<0
separately. The results of both are the same for derivation of recurrence relations that are discussed in the next section, thus the recurrence relations have been derived for
t>0
.

2.2
Derivation of Recurrence Relations

In this section we derive the Recurrence relations of Hermite polynomials by generating function introduced in the previous section. Considering the remark in previous section, we calculate the partial derivatives of the right side of the generating function
g(x,t)
in equation (16) respect to
t
and
x
respectively. Due to to the derivative relation for
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},z)
:

\frac{d}{dz}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2},z\right)=-z^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-z}

(19)

Derivative of right side of equation (16) respect to
t
gives:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(x,t)=-\frac{1}{t}g(x,t)-\frac{1-xt}{t^{3}}g(x,t)+\frac{1}{t^{3}}

(20)

Derivative of same equation respect to
x
yields:

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}g(x,t)=-\frac{1-xt}{t}g(x,t)+\frac{1}{t}

(21)

Substitution of
g(x,t)
in equation (20) by the summation definition of equation (3) yields the following:

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-\frac{1-xt}{t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{t}

(22)

Thus, we get:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\frac{d}{dx}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)+x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{t}

(23)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\frac{d}{dx}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n-1}H^{e}_{n}(x)+x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{t}

(24)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\frac{d}{dx}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-\frac{1}{t}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n+1}(x)+x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{t}

(25)

Equating the coefficients of
t^{n}
on both sides gives:

\frac{d}{dx}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-H^{e}_{n+1}(x)+xH^{e}_{n}(x)

(26)

This is one of the recurrence relation of Hermite polynomials.

Substitution of
g(x,t)
in equation (20) by the summation definition of equation (3) yields the following:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)-\frac{1-xt}{t^{3}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{t^{3}}

(27)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nt^{n-1}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n-1}H^{e}_{n}(x)-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)+\frac{x}{t^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{t^{3}}

(28)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nt^{n-1}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n-1}H^{e}_{n}(x)-\frac{1}{t^{3}}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n-3}H^{e}_{n}(x)+x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n-2}H^{e}_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{t^{3}}

(29)

Multiplying both sides of Equations (29) by
t^{2}
gives:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)t^{n+1}H^{e}_{n}(x)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n-1}H^{e}_{n}(x)+x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)

(30)

Equating the coefficients of
t^{n}
on both sides gives:

nH^{e}_{n-1}(x)=-H^{e}_{n+1}(x)+xH^{e}_{n}(x)

(31)

This is another recurrence relation of Hermite polynomials.

2.3
Associated Partial Differential Equation of Generating Function

Multiplying both sides of Equations (20) by
t^{2}
gives:

t^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(x,t)=-tg(x,t)-\frac{1-xt}{t}g(x,t)+\frac{1}{t}

(32)

By comparing the equations (21) and (32) we have:

t^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(x,t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}g(x,t)-tg(x,t)

(33)

Performing the same derivatives on the generating function that has been defined as a sum in equation (3), yields the same relation in equation (33):

t^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)-t\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)

(34)

This verifies the generating function equation
introduced in equation (16).

2.4
Solutions to Partial Differential Equations Derived from Generating Functions

We show that the solutions to partial differential equation (32) encompass the form of the generating function introduced in equation (3).

The auxiliary system of equations for first order partial differential equation introduced in equation (33) is presented as:

\frac{dt}{t^{2}}=-dx=-\frac{dg(x,t)}{tg(x,t)}

(35)

Integrating the first two with the t auxiliary equations yields two independent first integrals as follows:

\psi_{1}=-\frac{1}{t}+x=c_{1}\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\psi_{2}=g(x,t)t=c_{2}

(36)

Thus the integral of equation (33) reads as:

\Phi(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=0

(37)

Where
\Phi
is an arbitrary function.
As a solution for
g(x,t)
we may obtain:

tg(x,t)=\varphi(-\frac{1}{t}+x)

(38)

Therefor the general solution may read as:

g(x,t)=\frac{1}{t}\varphi(-\frac{1}{t}+x)

(39)

This solution is compatible with the form of new generating function in equation (3).

The differential equation (33) associated to new generating function, differs from the associated differential equation of presented generating function in equation (1). Repeating the same derivatives respect to
t
and
x
for generating function presented in equation (1):

G(x,t)=\sum_{0}^{\infty}H^{e}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{xt-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}

(40)

yields the relations:

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G(x,t)=tG(x,t),\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(x,t)=(x-t)G(x,t)

(41)

These equations result in the partial differential equation:

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G(x,t)+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(x,t)=xG(x,t)

(42)

This differs from the differential equation presented for
g(x,t)
in equation (33).

The solution to this partial differential equation through its auxiliary equations reads as:

\frac{dx}{1}=\frac{dt}{1}=\frac{dG(x,t)}{xG(x,t)}

(43)

\psi_{1}=x-t=c_{1},\hskip 9.24994pt\psi_{2}=G(x,t)e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}=c_{2}

(44)

Thus for general solution
\Phi(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=0
we have:

G(x,t)e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}=\varphi(x-t)

(45)

By choosing:

\varphi(x-t)=e^{-\frac{(x-t)^{2}}{2}}

(46)

The solution reads as:

G(x,t)e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}=e^{-\frac{(x-t)^{2}}{2}}

(47)

Or:

G(x,t)=e^{xt-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}

(48)

This is the generating function in equation (1).

2.5
Relation to Error Function and Cumulative Distribution Function

Respect to equation (16) and the identity for complementary Error function:

erfc(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},x^{2})

(49)

We obtain:

g(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}\left|t\right|}e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}erfc(\frac{1-xt}{\sqrt{2}\left|t\right|})

(50)

Respect to relations between cumulative probability of a normal distribution (CDF) and error function we have:

\Phi(x,\mu,\sigma)=1-\frac{1}{2}\left(erfc\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right)\right)

(51)

By the identity
erfc(-x)=2-erfc(x)
we get:

\Phi(x,\mu,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\left(erfc\left(\frac{\mu-x}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right)\right)

(52)

Let
\sigma=1
and
\mu=\frac{1}{t}
, thus by equation (50) we obtain:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\right)^{n}H^{e}_{n}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{H^{e}_{n}(x)}{\mu^{n}}=\sqrt{2\pi}\mu\Phi(x,\mu,1)

(53)

The last equation implies:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{H^{e}_{n}(x)}{\mu^{n+1}}=\sqrt{2\pi}\Phi(x,\mu,1)

(54)

With the identity
{\mathbb{E}\left[H^{e}_{n}(x)\right]}=\mu^{n}
, equation (54) becomes:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{E}\left[H^{e}_{n}(x)\right]}}{\mu^{n+1}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu}=\sqrt{2\pi}{\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi(x,\mu,1)\right]}=\infty

(55)

Squaring both sides of equation (54) yields:

\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{H^{e}_{n}(x)H^{e}_{m}(x)}{\mu^{n+m+2}}=2\pi\Phi^{2}(x,\mu,1)

(56)

The equations(50),(54),(55) and (56) are new relations which hold right for Hermite polynomials.

3
New Generating Function for Bivariate Hermite Polynomials

Theorem 2
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R},s,t\in\mathbb{R}-\left\{0\right\},m,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}

The generating functions for product of Hermite polynomials and Bivariate Hermite polynomials are respectively as follows:

g(s,t,x,y)=\frac{1}{2\left|ts\right|}e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}e^{\frac{(1-ys)^{2}}{2s^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-ys)^{2}}{2s^{2}})

(57)

g(x,y,t,s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{n}s^{m}H_{n,m}^{e}(\mathrm{M};x,y)=\frac{1}{2\left|t^{\prime}s^{\prime}\right|}e^{\frac{(1-xt^{\prime})^{2}}{2t^{\prime 2}}}e^{\frac{(1-ys^{\prime})^{2}}{2s^{\prime 2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt^{\prime})^{2}}{2t^{\prime 2}})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-ys^{\prime})^{2}}{2s^{\prime 2}})

(58)

Proof.

Respect to equation (3) we have:

g(x,y,t,s)=g(x,t)g(y,s)=\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}t^{n}s^{m}H^{e}_{n}(x)H^{e}_{m}(y)=\frac{1}{2\left|ts\right|}e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}e^{\frac{(1-ys)^{2}}{2s^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-ys)^{2}}{2s^{2}})

(59)

This is a simple product of two Hermite polynomials. For a general definition of the 2-dimensional Hermite polynomial which is based on the generating function defined in equation (3), first we introduce the 2-dimensional Hermite polynomials as defined in
[
11
]
:

g(s,t,x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{n}s^{m}H_{n,m}^{e}(\mathrm{M};x,y)

(60)

Where
\mathrm{M}
is a matrix with real entries:

\mathrm{M}=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}

(61)

That transforms the vector
(t,s)
to
(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})
from the right side:

(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})=(t,s)\mathrm{M}\Rightarrow t^{\prime}=at+cs\hskip 9.24994pts^{\prime}=bt+ds

(62)

Thus, the new proposed 2-dimensional generating function is defined by the equation:

g(x,y,t,s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{n}s^{m}H_{n,m}^{e}(\mathrm{M};x,y)=\frac{1}{2\left|t^{\prime}s^{\prime}\right|}e^{\frac{(1-xt^{\prime})^{2}}{2t^{\prime 2}}}e^{\frac{(1-ys^{\prime})^{2}}{2s^{\prime 2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt^{\prime})^{2}}{2t^{\prime 2}})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-ys^{\prime})^{2}}{2s^{\prime 2}})

(63)

For special case with
\mathrm{M}=\textbf{I}
we have,
s^{\prime}=s
and
t^{\prime}=t
, thus the equation (63) reduces to the simpler form in equation (59):

g(x,y,t,s)=g(x,t)g(y,s)

(64)

∎

4
Contour Integral Representation

In this section, the contour integral representation for new generating function is derived. Taking into account the formal generating function introduced in equation (1), the contour integral representation of Hermite polynomials for
t,x\in\mathbb{C}
reads as
[
1
]
:

H^{e}_{n}(x)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}\frac{e^{xt-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}}{t^{n+1}}dt

(65)

Where
C
is a simple closed contour in complex plane encircles
t=0
as the sole singular point of the integrand.
The numerator of the integrand is recognized as the ordinary generating function for Hermite polynomial that was introduced in equation (1). We introduce an alternative contour integral for Hermite polynomials based on new generating function in equation (16).

Theorem 3
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{C},t\in\mathbb{C}-\left\{0\right\}
. A contour integral representation for Hermite polynomials is :

H^{e}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\pi i}\oint_{\gamma}\frac{e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})}{t^{n+2}}dt

(66)

Where
\gamma
is defined as the simple closed curve (contour) with center at
t=0
.

Proof.

By taking
n-th
derivatives with respect to
t
for the generating function in Equation (16) and calculating it at
t\rightarrow 0
we obtain:

n!H^{e}_{n}(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}g(x,t)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t^{2}}}e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})\right]

(67)

Let
\gamma
be a simple closed contour in complex plane . If the complex function
f(z)
is analytic on a region containing
\gamma
and its interior, the Cauchy integral for
n-th
derivative of
f(z)
at a point
z_{0}
inside the contour
\gamma
is presented as
[
10
]
:

f^{\left(n\right)}(z_{0})=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma}\frac{f(z)}{\left(z-z_{0}\right)^{n+1}}dz

(68)

Where
\gamma
is a simple closed contour containing
z_{0}
. In the case where
z=0
is a singular point of
f(z)
, the integral could be written at the limit
{z_{0}\to 0}
as follows :

\lim_{z_{0}\to 0}f^{\left(n\right)}(z_{0})=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\lim_{z_{0}\to 0}\oint_{\gamma}\frac{f(z)}{\left(z-z_{0}\right)^{n+1}}dz=\oint_{\gamma}\frac{f(z)}{z^{n+1}}dz

(69)

Thus respect to the Taylor expansion coefficients of generating
function on the right side of (67) and replacing
f(z)
by
g(x,t)
, and
z
by
t
for fixed
x
, the Cauchy contour integral formula for
n-th
derivative, yields:

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}g(x,t)=\frac{n!}{2\sqrt{2}\pi i}\oint_{\gamma}\frac{e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})}{t^{n+2}}dt

(70)

Thus, with respect to equation (67) we have:

H^{e}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\pi i}\oint_{\gamma}\frac{e^{\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2},\frac{(1-xt)^{2}}{2t^{2}})}{t^{n+2}}dt

(71)

∎

This is a new contour integral representation for Hermite polynomial over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
.

5
Conclusion

Applying the operational method to derive a new generating function for Hermite polynomials reveals its connection with incomplete gamma function and a sum without integer factorials, i.e.
n!
. The close relation between incomplete gamma function and error function results in new relations between this functions and cumulative distribution function and Hermite generating function. A definition for two-variable Hermite generating function based on the new generating function has been proposed. An alternative contour integral representation for Hermite polynomials has been derived. By more investigations, this new generating function may lead to more hidden relations between Hermite polynomial and other special functions.

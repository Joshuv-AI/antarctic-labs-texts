Abstract
A derangement k k -representation of a graph G G is a map π \pi of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) to the symmetric group S k S_{k} , such that for any two vertices v v and u u of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) , v v and u u are adjacent if and only if π ​ ( v ) ​ ( i ) ≠ π ​ ( u ) ​ ( i ) \pi(v)(i)\neq\pi(u)(i) for each i ∈ { 1 , 2 , 3 , … , k } i\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,k\} . Introduction

All graphs we consider in this paper are simple, finite, and undirected. For a graph
G

There are many geometric and algebraic representations of graphs, such as intersection graphs, interval graphs
[
11
]
, orthogonal latin square graphs
[

is a graph formed from a family of finite sets

for each set

E(G)=\{\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\mid i\neq j,\ S_{i}\cap S_{j}\neq\emptyset\}
. The intersection number of a graph

is the minimum total number of elements in a representation of

as an intersection graph of finite sets.
In 1966, Erdos et al. proved that the intersection number of
G
is at most
\frac{n^{2}}{4}
, where

is the order of
G

[
7
]
.

Replacing the finite sets by open intervals, the interval number of a graph

, denoted by

, is the minimum
t
such that
G
is the intersection graph of sets consisting of at most
t
intervals on the real line
[
11
]
. Such a description of

is called a
t
-interval representation of

An orthogonal latin square graph (OLSG) is one in which the vertices are latin squares of the same order and on the same symbols, and two vertices are adjacent if and only if the corresponding latin squares are orthogonal
[
8
]
. Erdos and Evans proved that any finite graph can be realized as an orthogonal latin square graph
[
6
]
.

A Cayley graph is a graph on a group
G
with connection set

satisfying

and
S=S^{-1}
, denoted by

, such that the vertices are the elements of

and there is an edge joining

and

if and only if

for some

[
2
]
. This concept was introduced by Arthur Cayley in

[
4
]
. We know that any Cayley graph is a vertex-transitive graph and so the family of Cayley graphs is a proper subfamily of the family of all graphs. But Babai and Sos in a probabilistic approach showed that if

is a finite graph, then every sufficiently large group has a Cayley graph containing an induced subgraph isomorphic to

, precisely if
X
is a finite graph of order

and

is a group of order at least

, then

[

]
. Furthermore, in group theory, the Cayley theorem states that every group

is isomorphic to a subgroup of a symmetric group
[
4
]
. Therefore the study of Cayley graphs on symmetric groups has great importance.

The main objective of this work is to provide an algebraic approach for finding a Cayley graph containing an induced subgraph that is isomorphic to any given graph

. To show that we first, introduce a new representation of graphs using derangements. A permutation on a finite set

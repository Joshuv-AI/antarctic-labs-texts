Relation between local density and density relaxation near glass transition in a glass forming binary mixture
D. C. Thakur, Sandeep Kushawah, Jalim Singh, Anna Varughese, Prasanth P. Jose

Abstract
Many investigations shed light on various correlations between structure and dynamics in supercooled liquids; however, a general relation between structure and dynamics remains elusive. This molecular dynamics simulation study identifies the interrelationship between the growth of the highest peak of the radial distribution function g ⁡ ( r ) g(r) , variation in the radial force from this peak, and the slowdown of the density relaxation in the supercooled states of a model binary glass former. From the microscopic string-like motion in supercooled liquids, we argue that the surface density on a spherical shell ρ l ​ o ​ c \rho_{loc} around a reference particle at the highest peak of g ⁡ ( r ) g(r) can represent the free volume available for motion. We further show from these arguments and simulations that density relaxation time τ α \tau_{\alpha} and ρ l ​ o ​ c \rho_{loc} are connected, τ α ∝ exp ⁡ ( ρ l ​ o ​ c / ( ρ 0 − ρ l ​ o ​ c ) ) \tau_{\alpha}\propto\exp{(\rho_{loc}/(\rho_{0}-\rho_{loc}))} , where dynamics diverge at ρ 0 \rho_{0} . This relation is similar to the Vogel–Fulcher–Tammann (VFT) relation in the supercooled liquids, thus giving insight into the structural origin of the VFT as jamming of particles in a channel of density relaxation.



Liquids undergo glass transition or acquire solid-like rigidity with marginal structural
changes when compressed or cooled at a faster rate than structural relaxation.
These solid-like domains are due to the transient cages
that resist density relaxation. Recent experiments on two-dimensional (2D)
colloids show molecular-cages have higher local density

size fluctuation facilitates the density relaxation

a microscopic theory of dense liquids can shed light on the relationship between
the structure of molecular cages and slowing down the density relaxation.
A perturbation theory of dense Lennard-Jones (LJ) liquids by Weeks, Chandler,
and Andersen (WCA) propose that repulsive interactions govern the structure
of dense liquids
Weeks et al. 1971

follows the same

density
\rho=

1.8.
Studies also show that the relaxation dynamics become identical when the

A new approach to formal moduli problems
Brice Le Grignou Victor Roca i Lucio

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to introduce a framework for formal moduli problems, using new methods coming from operadic calculus. We construct an adjunction between formal moduli problems over some type of algebras and their Koszul dual algebras, in any characteristic. This adjunction is an equivalence if and only if some algebras are equivalent to their completions. We give a concrete homological criterion for it. It gives us a new proof of the celebrated Lurie–Pridham theorem, as well as of many other generalizations of it.

Introduction

The idea that every infinitesimal deformation problem is encoded by a differential graded Lie algebra has a long history. It dates back to the works of K. Kodaira and D. Spenser on the deformation of complex structure on a manifold
[
KS58
]
, and slowly became a guiding principle with the insights of P. Deligne, V. Drinfeld and many others. We refer to
[
Toë17
,
CG21
]
for comprehensive surveys. Nevertheless, it was only recently that J. Lurie and J. Pridham (independently) were able to translate it into a precise mathematical statement.

Theorem

(
[
Lur11
,
Pri10
]
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic zero. There is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories between the category of formal moduli problems and the category of dg Lie algebras over
\Bbbk
.

Here the notion of a formal moduli problem corresponds to a formalization of the idea of an "infinitesimal deformation problem". A formal moduli problem is a functor from
\Bbbk
-augmented dg Artinian
\Bbbk
-algebras with residue field
\Bbbk
to spaces, which preserves certain homotopy pullbacks. Informally speaking, these dg Artinian
\Bbbk
-algebras correspond to infinitesimal neighbourhoods of a
\Bbbk
-point of an algebro-geometric object, for instance a moduli space. A formal moduli problem therefore encodes the formal neighbourhood of this
\Bbbk
-point via a "functor of points" perspective.

The Lurie–Pridham theorem has been since then generalized in many directions. For example, B. Hennion generalized it to the case where the base field is a connective dg unital commutative
\Bbbk
-algebra
A
with finite dimensional homology, see
[
Hen18
]
. Formal moduli problems then correspond to formal neighbourhoods of a
\Bbbk
-point in a algebro-geometric object over
A
, and these are encoded by dg Lie algebras in dg
A
-modules. More generally, J. Nuiten showed in
[
Nui19
]
that formal neighbourhoods of more general geometric objects inside a moduli space are encoded by dg Lie
algebroids
.

D. Calaque, R. Campos and J. Nuiten showed in
[
CCN22
]
that the fact that dg Lie algebras are the ones appearing on the other side of the correspondence is a particular instance of Koszul duality for operads. This idea can be traced back to the original approach of J. Lurie in
[
Lur11
, Sections 3 and 4]
. One can define formal moduli problems in a more general setting than just dg Artinian commutative algebras, defining it over Artinian algebras over any operad
\mathcal{P}
. They showed that for certain operads satisfying a technical condition, called
splendid
operads, the
\infty
-category of formal moduli problems over
\mathcal{P}
-algebras is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of dg algebras over a Koszul dual operad.

Another direction of generalizations is given when one considers positive characteristic base fields. In this case, the naive analogue of the Lurie–Pridham theorem is false, as dg Lie algebras a notoriously badly behaved in positive characteristic. Nevertheless, L. Brantner and A. Mathew showed in
[
BM19
]
that the
\infty
-category of formal moduli problems is still equivalence to the
\infty
-category of algebras over a monad (defined
\infty
-categorically). Algebras over this monad are called
partition Lie algebras
. L. Brantner, R. Campos and J. Nuiten later showed in
[
BCN21
]
that this
\infty
-category can be obtained as the localization of a semi-model category of divided power algebras over an operad. This in particular gives
point-set
models for these partitions Lie algebras. There are two versions of these theorems, depending on whether one considers
spectral
or
derived
approaches; we will only focus on the spectral case from now on.

Let us say a few words about the proofs of these theorems. First, it is quite noticeable that all of these proofs rely on the same underlying machinery developed in
[
Lur11
]
. We will give a brief overview of the key elements in the original case, but all the other cases are to some extent similar. The first idea is to relate dg commutative algebras and dg Lie algebra using some kind of Koszul duality. In this case, it can be done by considering the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex (which is a dg cocommutative coalgebra) and then taking the linear dual. This gives a functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{Com}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}^{\mathsf{op}}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{Com}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]}

between the
\infty
-category of dg Lie algebras and the
\infty
-category of dg commutative algebras. Using that
\widehat{\mathrm{CE}}
commutes with (homotopy) limits, one can then deduce that it must have an
\infty
-categorical left adjoint, which is often denoted by
\mathfrak{D}
. This allows one to construct a functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi:\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Fun}\left(\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{Art}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}],\mathcal{S}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[A\mapsto\mathrm{Map}(\mathfrak{D}(A),\mathfrak{g})\right]\penalty\ ,}

where
\mathcal{S}
denotes the
\infty
-category of spaces. Here is where one needs to prove various key assertions:

(1)

The functor
\mathfrak{D}(-)
preserves (homotopy) pullback, and therefore the image of
\Psi
lands in the
\infty
-category of formal moduli problems.

(2)

The functor
\Psi
is conservative and preserves sifted (homotopy) colimits. Since it is accessible and preserves all limits, it admits a left adjoint
\Phi
.

(3)

The functor
\Phi
is fully faithful, that is, the unit
\mathrm{id}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\Psi\circ\Phi
is a weak-equivalence.

If these assertions are true, then the adjunction
\Phi\dashv\Psi
is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories. Indeed, using the fact that the unit of adjunction is a weak-equivalence and that
\Phi
is conservative, we can deduce that the counit is also a weak-equivalence, and therefore that the adjunction is an equivalence.

The first point is in general non-trivial. The second point is somewhat easier, as it can be directly checked on
\mathrm{T}\circ\Psi
, where
\mathrm{T}
is the tangent space of a formal moduli problem. The hardest assertion to show is the third one. It can be shown, using hypercoverings, that any formal moduli problem
F
is weakly-equivalent to a the geometric realization of a simplicial object
X_{\bullet}
, where
X_{n}
is pro-representable. Using the fact that
\Psi
commutes with sifted (homotopy) colimits, one can then reduce to the pro-representable case. Using the fact that
\Psi
commutes with filtered (homotopy) colimits, one can further reduce to the representable case. So the third point can be reduced to showing that for any dg Artinian algebra
A
,

\eta_{A}:A\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{CE}}\penalty\ \mathfrak{D}(A)

is a quasi-isomorphism. Even then, there are still quite non-trivial computations to be done. In fact, it is rather the counit of this adjunction which is shown to be a quasi-isomorphism for dg Lie algebras which are called
good
(which we call cellular). And good dg Lie algebras are shown to be equivalent to dg Artinian algebras and thus the above unit of adjunction is also an equivalence.

Explaining the method.
The main goal of this paper is to propose a new approach to formal moduli problems. Before stating the general results obtained in this paper, let us illustrate our new method in the case of the Lurie–Pridham. The first ingredient is using the duality square constructed in
[
RiL22
]
, which contains the operadic calculus methods that will be used in the proof. In order to study formal moduli problems, it does not matter if we define them in terms of the
\infty
-category of dg commutative Artinian algebras or in terms the
\infty
-category of Artinian
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-algebras (algebras commutative up to homotopy). We consider the following commutative square of Quillen adjunctions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}^{\mathsf{conil}}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathcal{C}_{\infty}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{abs}\penalty\ \mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}\penalty\ ,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\circ}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega^{\mathsf{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Omega}}

Here the bar-cobar adjunction

and the complete bar-cobar adjunction

are both instance of the Koszul duality which generalize the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex. Vertical adjunctions are given in each case by the linear dual functor
(-)^{*}
and its adjoint. These adjunctions become Quillen adjunctions when one considers the left (resp. right) transferred model structure on dg
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-algebras (resp. coalgebras) from dg modules, and then further transfers it along the bar-cobar adjunctions. Notice that on the bottom right corner, the category of dg
absolute
Lie algebras appears. This is a new type of algebraic structure introduced in
[
GL22
]
, where infinite sums of operations have a well-defined image without assuming an underlying topology. These objects are encoded as algebras over a
cooperad
instead of an operad.

Now, using the vertical left linear duality adjunction, it is quite simple to show that Artinian
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-algebras are equivalent to coArtinian
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-coalgebras, since the linear duality adjunction induces an equivalence on objects with finite dimensional homology. This first step allows us to define formal moduli problems purely in terms of coalgebras, which coincides with the philosophy of
[
Hin01
]
that states that coalgebras should be regarded as formal derived spaces. Then, using the complete bar-cobar adjunction, which is a Quillen equivalence, it is straightforward to show that coArtinian
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-coalgebras correspond to
cellular
dg absolute Lie algebras. This gives equivalences of
\infty
-categories

\mathsf{Art}\penalty\ \mathcal{C}_{\infty}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]\simeq\mathsf{coArt}\penalty\ \mathcal{C}_{\infty}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]\simeq\mathsf{Cell}\penalty\ \mathsf{abs}\penalty\ \mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}\penalty\ [\mathrm{W}^{-1}]\penalty\ .

The
\infty
-category of formal moduli problems is given by functors on Artinian
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
-algebras that respect certain pullbacks. Therefore it is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of pre-sheaves on cellular dg absolute Lie algebras which respect certain pushouts. This highlights the important role that absolute algebras play in deformation theory.

Finally, we use the presentation of the
\infty
-category of algebras over a monad in dg modules obtained in
[
Gri20
]
to further reduce the initial question. In
op.cit
, the first author showed that the
\infty
-category of algebras over a monad which preserves sifted (homotopy) colimits is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of functors from cellular algebras to spaces which preserve certain pushouts. In our case, it gives that

\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]\simeq\mathsf{Fun}^{\amalg}\left(\mathsf{Cell}\penalty\ \mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]^{\mathsf{op}},\mathcal{S}\right)\penalty\ ,

that is, the
\infty
-category of dg Lie algebras up to quasi-isomorphisms is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of pre-sheaves on cellular Lie algebras that preserve certain pushouts. This entails that formal moduli problems are equivalent to dg Lie algebras if and only if the absolute envelope functor

\mathrm{Ab}:\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\longrightarrow\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{abs}\penalty\ \mathsf{Lie}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}^{\mathsf{comp}}

from dg Lie algebras to dg absolute Lie algebras induces an equivalence between the

-categories of cellular Lie algebras and of cellular absolute Lie algebras. This amounts to the natural inclusion

of the free dg Lie algebra into the free completed dg Lie algebra being a quasi-isomorphism for any finite dimensional dg module

concentrated in degrees
\leq-1
. It follows from a straightforward observation: since there are only a finite number of non-zero terms in each degree, the direct sum coincides with the product and
\eta
is an isomorphism. This straightforward observation is the only non-formal step in our approach to formal moduli problem statements.

\Bbbk
be a field of any characteristic. Our first ingredient will be the generalization of the homotopical operadic calculus to the positive characteristic case obtained in

Definition 3

(Quasi-planar dg cooperad)

A

It is called

of it is the colimit of a cocontinuous directed diagram of pseudo-planar dg conilpotent cooperads

\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{C}^{(0)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{(1)}\longrightarrow\cdots\mathcal{C}^{(i)}\longrightarrow\cdots

indexed by an ordinal

is an arity-wise degree-wise injection and such that

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{(i+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}\mathcal{C}^{(i+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{C}^{(i+1)}}

factors through
\overline{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{C}^{(i)}

(2)

\mathcal{C}^{(i+1)}
to

In other words, the differential of

In full generality, the operadic cobar construction

[

If we restrict to conilpotent

Notation
.

We will denote by

(
[

.

Let

Remark 3

In

Hence, by Proposition

Over a field of characteristic zero,

1.4.
The coalgebraic point of view of formal moduli problems

The goal of this section is to define formal moduli problems exclusively in terms of coalgebras. When one considers formal moduli problems over
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras, this point of view is very close to the one expressed in
[

]
: indeed, it shows that
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebras can be thought as

. From this point of view, formal moduli problems are defined as functors that assign to some "elementary" derived formal stacks a space, together with a "gluing condition".

The dg operad
\Omega\mathcal{C}
is cofibrant, thus it is coadmissible. This means that the category of dg
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebras admits a
left-transferred model structure
from dg modules, where weak-equivalences are given by quasi-isomorphisms and where cofibrations are given by degree-wise monomorphisms.

Let

be the augmentation morphisms. It induces a Quillen adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \Omega\mathcal{C}\text{-}\mathsf{coalg}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathsf{mod}\penalty\ ,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{prim}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{triv}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

where the functor

is given by primitive elements of a dg

-coalgebra and the functor

endows any dg module with the structure of a trivial
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra structure. In particular, the dg modules

can be endowed with a trivial
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-coalgebra structure for all

\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 6

(coArtinian

-coalgebras)
.

The

-category of

-category of dg

-coalgebras such that

The

See
[

-modules is a tame equivalence.

4.
Tempered cooperads

For the rest of this section let
\mathcal{C}
be a
0
-reduced quasi-planar conilpotent dg cooperads. We give a powerful criterion that turns the functor relating the
\infty
-categories of cellular
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and cellular
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras an equivalence. In its essence, it holds if the homology of
\mathcal{C}
is concentrated in increasingly positive homological degrees. The reason behind is that cellular
\mathcal{C}
-algebras and cellular
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras are equivalent if a graded direct sum is equivalent to a graded direct product, which will be true if there are finitely many non-trivial elements in each homological degree.

4.1.
Reduction to the free case

Recall that by Theorem
4
, in order to have an equivalence of
\infty
-categories between formal moduli problems over
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras and dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras, it is enough to check that the (derived) unit of the
\mathrm{Ab}\dashv\mathrm{Res}
is a quasi-isomorphism for every strictly cellular dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebra. It is in fact possible to reduce even further to the case of free dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras.

Proposition 9
.

As the conilpotent dg cooperad
\mathcal{C}
is quasi-planar and
0
-reduced, the following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

For every strictly cellular dg
\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
-algebra the unit of the
\mathrm{Ab}\dashv\mathrm{Res}
adjunction is a quasi-isomorphism.

(2)

For every free dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by a finite dimensional dg module in non-positve degrees, the unit of the
\mathrm{Ab}\dashv\mathrm{Res}
adjunction is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

The first point
(1)
clearly implies the second point
(2)
. Let
\left(\mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V,d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V}+d_{2}\right)
be a strictly cellular dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebra. We will first reduce to the case where
d_{2}
is concentrated in arity one, that is
d_{2}(V)\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{*}(1)\otimes V
, before reducing to the case where
d_{2}
is trivial, that is, when the stricly cellular algebra is in fact free.

We consider the following decreasing exhaustive filtrations

\mathrm{F}_{m}\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\geq m}\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\penalty\ ,\quad\quad\mathrm{F}_{m}\penalty\ (V)^{\mathcal{C}}\coloneqq\prod_{n\geq m}\mathrm{Hom}\left(\mathcal{C}(n),V^{\otimes n}\right)^{{\mathbb{S}_{n}}}\penalty\ ,

for all
m\geq 0
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is
0
-reduced, both filtrations are stable by the differentials. The unit map is given by the natural inclusion

\eta:\bigoplus_{n\geq m}\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\rightarrowtail\prod_{n\geq m}\mathrm{Hom}\left(\mathcal{C}(n),V^{\otimes n}\right)^{{\mathbb{S}_{n}}}

preserves these filtrations. Notice that
\eta
induces an isomorphism at the first page of the induced spectral sequences. Indeed, since
V
is a finite dimensional dg module, there is an isomorphism

\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\cong\mathrm{Hom}\left(\mathcal{C}(n),V^{\otimes n}\right)^{{\mathbb{S}_{n}}}\penalty\ .

So in order to prove that
\eta
is a quasi-isomorphism, it is enough to show that both spectral sequences strongly converge. The filtration on the infinite product is complete and Hausdorff, therefore it strongly converges.

Let us show that the spectral sequence induced by the filtration
\mathrm{F}_{m}\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V
is bounded at page two. We suppose that the unit of adjunction is a quasi-isomorphism for
(\mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V,d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V}+d_{2}^{\leq 1})
, where
d_{2}^{\leq 1}
is the differential
d_{2}
truncated at arity one. This unit is given in homology by

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{H}_{*}\left(\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}},d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V}+d_{2}^{\leq 1}\right)\rightarrowtail\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{H}_{*}\left(\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}},d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V}+d_{2}^{\leq 1}\right)\penalty\ .

It is an isomorphism if and only if for every
k
in
\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a
m_{k}
such that

\mathrm{H}_{k}\left(\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}},d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V}+d_{2}^{\leq 1}\right)=0\penalty\ ,

for all
n\geq m_{k}
. This implies that the spectral sequence induced by the filtration
\mathrm{F}_{m}\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V
is bounded at page two.

We are left to show that if the unit map is a quasi-isomorphism for
(\mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V,d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V})
, then it implies that is a quasi-isomorphism for
(\mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V,d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V}+d_{2}^{\leq 1})
. It suffices to show that if

\mathrm{H}_{k}\left(\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}},d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V}\right)=0\quad\text{then}\quad\mathrm{H}_{k}\left(\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}},d_{\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V}+d_{2}^{\leq 1}\right)=0\penalty\ .

Recall that
V
is endowed with an exhaustive
finite
filtration
V(i)
since
\mathcal{C}^{*}\circ V
is strictly cellular. We consider the following increasing filtration

\mathrm{F}_{i}\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{C}(n)^{*}\otimes\left(\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{l}=i}V(i_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes V(i_{l})\right)\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\penalty\ .

The filtration is finite, therefore the spectral sequence associated it is strongly convergent. Using this spectral sequence, it is straightforward to obtain the desired vanishing result for
\mathrm{H}_{k}
.
∎

4.2.
Tempered cooperads

Definition 15

(Tempered cooperad)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a quasi-planar
0
-reduced conilpotent dg cooperad. It is
tempered
if for all
\kappa\geq 0
, there exist an
n_{\kappa}
such that, for all
n\geq n_{\kappa}
and
k\leq\kappa
,
\mathrm{H}_{k}(\mathcal{C}(n))=0
.

Remark 15
.

Equivalently, for all
\kappa\geq 0
, there exist an
n_{\kappa}
such that, for all
n\geq n_{\kappa}
and
k\geq\kappa
,
\mathrm{H}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\ast}(n))=0
. Essentially, it means that the homology of
\mathcal{C}
is concentrated in increasingly bigger degrees as the arity goes to infinity.

Remark 16

(Comparison with splendid operads)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic zero and let
\mathcal{P}
be a connective
splendid
dg operad in the sense of
[
CCN22
]
. One can always transfer an
\infty
-operad structure onto
\mathrm{H}\mathcal{P}
, and there is a weak-equivalence of conilpotent dg cooperads
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{P}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{H}\mathcal{P}
. Here
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{H}\mathcal{P}
is a tempered conilpotent dg cooperad concentrated in degrees
\geq 1
. This gives a tempered model for any splendid dg operad.

On the other hand, if a conilpotent dg cooperad
\mathcal{C}
is
tempered
, then one can check that the dg operad
\Omega\mathcal{C}
is splendid. See
[
CCN22
, Variant 5.18]
for more details. Notice that
op.cit.
uses a
cohomological
convention.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a tempered
0
-reduced quasi-planar conilpotent dg cooperad and let
n\geq 0
. Suppose that
\mathrm{H}_{*}(\mathcal{C}(n))
is concentrated in degrees
>\kappa
. Let
V
be a finite dimensional dg module concentrated in degrees
\leq 0
. The homology

\mathrm{H}_{*}\left(\mathrm{Hom}\left(\mathcal{C}(n),V^{\otimes n}\right)^{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\right)

is concentrated in degrees
<-\kappa
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\tau_{\kappa+1}(\mathcal{C}(n))
the "intelligent" truncation of
\mathcal{C}(n)
at degree
\kappa+1
given by

\tau_{\kappa+1}(\mathcal{C}(n))_{k}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{C}(n)_{k}\text{ if }k\geq\kappa+1\penalty\ ,\\
0\text{ if }k\leq\kappa\penalty\ ,\\
\mathrm{Ker}(d:\mathcal{C}(n)_{\kappa+1}\to\mathcal{C}(n)_{\kappa+1})\text{ if }k=\kappa+1\penalty\ .\end{cases}

There is a quasi-isomorphism

\tau_{\kappa+1}(\mathcal{C}(n))\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathcal{C}(n)\penalty\ .

The
\mathbb S_{n}
-module
\mathcal{C}(n)
is
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-projective since
\mathcal{C}
is quasi-planar. In general
\tau_{\kappa+1}\mathcal{C}(n)
is not a
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-projective any more. Nevertheless, we can take a cofibrant resolution
P
of it in the model category of dg
\mathbb S_{n}
-module in degrees strictly above
\kappa
endowed with the projective model structure. It is also cofibrant when seen as an unbounded dg
\mathbb S_{n}
-module for the projective model strucutre. Thus we get a composite quasi-isomorphism of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-projective dg
\mathbb S_{n}
-modules
P\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathcal{C}(n)
.

The model category of dg
\mathbb S_{n}
-module with the projective model structure is homotopically enriched-tensored-cotensored over the dg
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-modules with the injective model structure. By applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}\left(-,V^{\otimes n}\right)
we get a quasi-isomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}\left(\mathcal{C}(n),V^{\otimes n}\right)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathrm{Hom}\left(P,V^{\otimes n}\right)

of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-injective dg
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-modules. This give a quasi-isomorphism between their respective
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-invariants since these also compute the homotopy invariants. But in degree
-\kappa
and above, the dg module
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\left(P,V^{\otimes n}\right)
is zero, therefore so is its homology.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a tempered
0
-reduced quasi-planar conilpotent dg cooperad. The canonical adjunction of
\infty
-categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{FMP}_{\Omega\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \mathcal{C}^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]\penalty\ ,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

between the
\infty
-categories of formal moduli problems over
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-algebras and the
\infty
-category of dg
\mathcal{C}^{*}
-algebras is an equivalence.

Proof.

As a consequence of Lemma

, the (derived) unit map is a quasi-isomorphism for every dg
\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
-algebra freely generated by a finite dimensionsal dg module in non positive degrees. We conclude by Proposition

.
∎

If

5.2.
Partition Lie algebras

Let
\Bbbk
be field of any characteristic. We consider formal moduli problems over an
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{\infty}
operad, that is, an operad which is a
\mathbb{S}
-projective resolution of the operad
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{om}
. This corresponds to spectral formal moduli problems over
\mathrm{H}\Bbbk
; we emphasize that do not consider the
derived
version here.

It was shown by L. Brantner and A. Mathew in
[
BM19
]
that the
\infty
-category of these formal moduli problems is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of
(spectral) partition Lie algebras
. The
\infty
-category of partition Lie algebras was first defined to be the
\infty
-category of algebras over a monad. The question of whether this
\infty
-category could admit a simple presentation in terms of an algebraic
1
-category localized at some class of equivalences was settled by L. Brantner, R. Campos and J. Nuiten in
[
BCN21
]
.

Theorem 9

(
[
BCN21
, Proposition 4.40]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{E}
be a connected
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{\infty}
-operad. There is a canonical equivalence

\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \Omega(\mathbb{E}^{*})\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]\simeq\mathcal{L}ie^{\pi}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}

between the
\infty
-categories of dg
\Omega(\mathbb{E}^{*})
-algebras and the
\infty
-category of partition Lie algebras.

Therefore, the semi-model category of dg
\Omega(\mathbb{E}^{*})
-algebras is a model for partition Lie algebras, and
a fortiori
, their localized
\infty
-category is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of formal moduli problems of
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{\infty}
-algebras over a field
\Bbbk
of any characteristic.

On the other hand, the methods introduced in this paper give a direct proof that there is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories between the
\infty
-category of formal moduli problems of
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{\infty}
-algebras and the
\infty
-category of dg
\Omega(\mathbb{E}^{*})
-algebras. For that, we start by choosing a specific model, the non-unital Barratt-Eccles dg operad
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
.

Lemma 12
.

The conilpotent dg cooperad
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})
is quasi-planar and tempered.

Proof.

For the fact that
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})
is quasi-planar, see
[
GRiL23
, Section 2]
. It follows from
[
Fre04
, Theorem 6.8]
that the homology of
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})
is the underlying
\mathbb{S}
-module of the shifted Lie cooperad
s\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}^{*}
.
∎

Theorem 10
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
be the non-unital Barratt-Eccles dg operad. There is a canonical equivalence

\mathsf{FMP}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}}\simeq\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \Omega((\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})^{*})\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]\penalty\ .

between the
\infty
-categories of dg
\Omega((\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})^{*})
-algebras and the
\infty
-category of formal moduli problems of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
-algebras.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
6
and Lemma
12
.
∎

This proof has the advantage of directly providing point-set models.

Remark 18
.

The result of Theorem
10
can be subsequently extended to other connected models for of
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{\infty}
-operads by the homotopy-invariance of the
\infty
-categories considered.

Remark 19
.

The results of
[
BM19
]
(resp. of
[
BCN21
]
) have an extra level of generality with respect to our current work since their results are also valid when
\Bbbk
is a complete local ring (resp. a coherent
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring). We nevertheless expect that the methods introduced in this paper can be generalized to a similar setting.

5.3.
\mathbb{E}_{k}
formal moduli problems

Over a field of any characteristic
\Bbbk
, one recovers the correspondence between formal moduli problems over
\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}
-algebras and
\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}
-algebras, where
\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}
is a model for the non-unital little
k
-cubes operad. For
k=1
, this gives that formal moduli problems of non-unital associative algebras are encoded by non-unital dg associative algebras. See
[
Lur11
, Section 3 and 4]
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
be the non-unital Barratt-Eccles dg operad. It admits a filtration of sub dg operads

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{1}\rightarrowtail\cdots\rightarrowtail\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}\rightarrowtail\cdots\rightarrowtail\mathrm{colim}_{k}\penalty\ \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}\cong\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}\penalty\ ,

where each
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}
is a model for
\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}
. See
[
BF04
, Section 1.6]
for more details on this.

Lemma 13
.

Let
k\geq 1
. The conilpotent dg cooperad
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}
is tempered and quasi-planar.

Proof.

Let us notice that for any
k\geq 1
, every
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}(n)
is a quasi-free dg
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-module. Therefore the underlying conilpotent graded cooperad
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}
is planar. Filtering by the homological degree of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}
together with the weight of rooted trees, gives the quasi-planar filtration on
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}
. See
[
GRiL23
, Section 2]
for an analogue filtration.

It was shown in
[
Fre11
]
that the homology of
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}
is given by the underlying
\mathbb{S}
-module of the
k
-fold suspension of the conilpotent dg cooperad
(k-1)\text{-}\mathcal{G}\mathrm{erst}^{*}
, which is the linear dual cooperad of the operad encoding non-unital
(k-1)
-Gerstenhaber algebras. It follows directly that
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}
is tempered.
∎

Theorem 11
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}
the sub dg operad of the non-unital Barratt-Eccles dg operad encoding
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}
-algebras. There is a canonical equivalence

\mathsf{FMP}_{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}}\simeq\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \Omega((\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k})^{*})\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]\penalty\ .

between the
\infty
-categories of dg
\Omega((\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k})^{*})
-algebras and of formal moduli problems over
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}_{k}
-algebras.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
6
and Lemma
13
.
∎

Remark 20
.

The result of Theorem
11
can be subsequently extended to other connected models for of
\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nu}}
-operads by the homotopy-invariance of the
\infty
-categories considered.

\prod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}(\mathcal{C}(n),\mathcal{P}(n))\penalty\ ,

\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}
is not
\mathbb{S}

\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
is the non-unital Barratt-Eccles operad.

Lemma 14
.

The conilpotent dg cooperad
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{P}erm\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})
is tempered.

Proof.

It follows from
[
CV06
, Theorem 1.13]
that the homology of
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})
(which is quasi-isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm})
) is the underlying
\mathbb{S}
-module of the shifted pre-Lie cooperad
s\mathrm{pre}\text{-}\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}^{*}
. Thus it clearly satisfies the tempered condition.
∎

Theorem 12
.

There is a canonical equivalence

\mathsf{FMP}_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}\penalty\ \otimes\penalty\ \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}}\simeq\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ \Omega(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})^{*})\text{-}\mathsf{alg}\penalty\ [\mathrm{Q.iso}^{-1}]\penalty\ .

between the
\infty
-categories of dg
\Omega(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})^{*})
-algebras and of formal moduli problems over
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}\penalty\ \otimes\penalty\ \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
-algebras.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
6
and Lemma
14
.
∎

Remark 21
.

Adapting the arguments of
[
BCN21
, Proposition 4.51]
, one can show that
\mathcal{S}\mathrm{urj}^{*}\otimes s\mathrm{pre}\text{-}\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}
is also a model for
\Omega(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}})^{*})
, where
\mathcal{S}\mathrm{urj}^{*}
is the dual of the surjections cooperad constructed in the appendix of
op.cit
.

In order to define the analogue of the functor
\mathrm{Def}_{\Omega\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}}^{0}
in positive characteristic, one can replace dg operads by some kinds of operads up to homotopy ; for instance dg algebras over the coloured operads
\mathcal{O}\mathrm{p}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
(where
\mathcal{O}\mathrm{p}
is the coloured operad that encodes non-unital dg operads). These dg operads up to homotopy are tensored over
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
-algebras. For two operads
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}
, we can then define a formal moduli problem over
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{erm}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}
-algebras in an analogous way

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{\mathsf{dg}\penalty\ {\mathcal{O}\mathrm{p}\penalty\ \otimes\penalty\ \mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{nu}}}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}\left(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{P}\otimes A\right)\penalty\ .}

Replacing
\mathcal{Q}
by the cobar construction
\Omega\mathcal{C}
of a quasi-planar conilpotent dg cooperad
\mathcal{C}
, the convolution construction between
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{P}
should encode this formal moduli problem, and therefore should be endowed with a dg partition pre-Lie algebra structure that extends the classical pre-Lie structure.

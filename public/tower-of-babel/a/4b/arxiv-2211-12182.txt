The categorical DT/PT correspondence and quasi-BPS categories for local surfaces
Tudor Pădurariu and Yukinobu Toda

Abstract
. We construct semiorthogonal decompositions of Donaldson-Thomas (DT) categories for reduced curve classes on local surfaces into products of quasi-BPS categories and Pandharipande-Thomas (PT) categories, giving a categorical analogue of the numerical DT/PT correspondence for Calabi-Yau 3-folds. The main ingredient is a categorical wall-crossing formula for DT/PT quivers (which appear as Ext-quivers in the DT/PT wall-crossing) proved in our previous paper. We also study quasi-BPS categories of points on local surfaces and propose conjectural computations of their K-theory analogous to formulas already known for the three dimensional affine space.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The DT/PT correspondence for Calabi-Yau
3-folds

For a Calabi-Yau 3-fold
X
, the DT/PT
correspondence is a formula which relates
rank one Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants
counting
one-dimensional closed subschemes in
X

with Pandharipande-Thomas (PT) invariants
counting stable pairs on
X
.
The PT invariants provide a geometric description of the contribution of sheaf counting invariants in the
Maulik–Nekrasov–Okounkov–Pandharipande conjecture
[
MNOP06
]
.
More precisely,
for a curve class
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we denote by

(1.1)

\displaystyle I_{X}(\beta,n),\ (\mbox{resp.~}P_{X}(\beta,n))

the moduli space of (compactly supported) closed
subschemes
C\subset X
with
[C]=\beta
,

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C})=n
,
(resp. stable
pairs
(F,s)
with
[F]=\beta
,
\chi(F)=n
).
By taking the integration
of the Behrend constructible functions on the spaces (
1.1
),
we obtain the DT invariant

\mathrm{DT}_{\beta,n}\in\mathbb{Z}

and the PT invariant

\mathrm{PT}_{\beta,n}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The DT/PT correspondence is the formula

(1.2)

\text{DT}_{\beta,n}=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\text{DT}_{0,k}\cdot\text{PT}_{\beta,n-k}.

The above formula was conjectured in
[
PT09
]
,
its unweighted version
was proved in
[
Tod10
,
ST11
]

using Joyce’s motivic Hall algebra machinery
[
Joy06
,
Joy07a
,
Joy07b
,
Joy08
]
, and its version incorporating the Behrend function was proved in
[
Bri11
]
(also see
[
Tod20a
]
)
using the work of
Joyce-Song
[
JS12
]

(see also
[
KS
]
).

The purpose of this paper is to prove a categorical version of (
1.2
) for
X
a local surface, see Theorem
1.1
. In
[
Toda
]
, the second author defined dg-categories categorifying DT and PT invariants for local surfaces,
which are total spaces of canonical
line bundles on surfaces.
For reduced curve classes, we construct semiorthogonal decompositions of DT categories with summands which are products of quasi-BPS categories on points on
X
and PT categories. By taking the Grothendieck groups of the categories appearing in these semiorthogonal decompositions, we obtain a K-theoretic version of (
1.2
).

1.2.
The categorical DT/PT correspondence

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface. The local surface is the
following non-compact Calabi-Yau 3-fold

\displaystyle X:=\mathrm{Tot}_{S}(K_{S}).

Take
\beta\in H_{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})

and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In
[
Toda
, Definition 4.2.1]
, the second author defined
dg-categories

(1.3)

\mathcal{DT}_{X}(\beta,n)\text{ and }\mathcal{PT}_{X}(\beta,n).

These categories may be regarded as a gluing of
categories of graded matrix factorizations of
super-potentials whose critical loci are
locally isomorphic to

I_{X}(\beta,n)
and
P_{X}(\beta,n)
,
respectively.
The dg-categories (
1.3
) should
recover DT/PT invariants by
taking their periodic cyclic homologies,
see
[
Efi18
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
Toda
, Subsection 3.3]
, especially
[
Toda
, Conjecture 3.3.4]
.
The DT/PT categories (
1.3
)
are constructed as quotients of

D^{b}(\mathfrak{M}^{{\dagger}})
, where
\mathfrak{M}^{{\dagger}}

is a certain derived open substack of
the moduli stack of pairs
(F,s)
, where

F\in\operatorname{Coh}(S)
is at most one-dimensional and

s\colon\mathcal{O}_{S}\to F

is a section, by the subcategory of
objects whose singular supports
[
AG15
]
are
contained in unstable loci. We will
review their constructions in Subsections
2.5.4

and
5.1
.

We say that
\beta
is a reduced class
if any effective divisor on
S
with class

\beta
is a reduced divisor.
In this case, the second author constructed
a fully-faithful functor from the PT category to the
DT category, see
[
Toda
, Theorem 1.4.5]
.
The main theorem of this paper is
the following, which refines the
above fully-faithful functor to
a semiorthogonal decomposition:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\beta
is a reduced class.
Then there is a semiorthogonal decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{DT}_{X}(\beta,n)=\left\langle\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{T}_{X}(d_{i})_{v_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{PT}_{X}(\beta,n^{\prime})\,\Big|-1<\frac{v_{1}}{d_{1}}<\cdots<\frac{v_{k}}{d_{k}}\leqslant 0\right\rangle,

where the right hand side is after all partitions
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}+n^{\prime}=n
.

There is also a version for arbitrary classes
\beta
and for the DT/PT categories of reduced supports
over
S
, see Remark
5.10
.
In the above,
\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}
is the quasi-BPS category of
d
points and weight
w
of
X
, see Subsection
1.3
for more details.

The main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the
local categorical DT/PT correspondence
for DT/PT quivers proved in
[
PTc
, Theorem 1.1]
.
The wall crossing between ideal sheaves and stable pairs (on an arbitrary Calabi-Yau
3
-fold) can be described using Ext quivers with super-potential
[
Tod18
]
, and all these Ext quivers can be constructed from
DT/PT quivers, see Subsection
3.1
. We also make use of the Koszul equivalence
[
Isi13
]
between coherent sheaves on a Koszul (quasi-smooth) stack and a category of matrix factorizations, the window categories of Segal
[
Seg11
]
, Halpern-Leistner
[
HL15
]
, and Ballard–Favero–Katzarkov
[
BFK19
]
, the noncommutative resolutions of Špenko–Van den Bergh
[
ŠVdB17
]
, and categorical Hall algebras of surfaces
[
PS
]
and of quivers with potential
[
Păda
]
.

We expect that the semiorthogonal decomposition in
Theorem
1.1

also holds for an arbitrary class

\beta
.
The reason we restrict to a reduced class is that,
in this case,
one can take
\mathfrak{M}^{{\dagger}}

to be the derived moduli stack

\mathfrak{T}_{S}(\beta,n)

of pairs
(F,s)

such that
s
has at most zero-dimensional
cokernel. Then its classical
truncation
\mathcal{T}_{S}(\beta,n)

admits a good moduli space

\mathcal{T}_{S}(\beta,n)\to T_{S}(\beta,n),

and we can reduce the statement to be proved to the analogous formal
local statements on
T_{S}(\beta,n)
.
For an arbitrary class
\beta
,
we cannot find such a
\mathfrak{M}^{{\dagger}}

admitting a good moduli space in general.
Alternatively,
we expect an
argument reducing to a formal local statement on

T_{X}(\beta,n)
, but the local structure of
the categories (
1.3
) over
T_{X}(\beta,n)

is hard to investigate and
we can prove Theorem
1.1
only for reduced
classes at this moment.

For
\beta=0
, we obtain the following semiorthogonal decomposition of the DT category of points on
X
in quasi-BPS categories:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. There is a semiorthogonal decomposition

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{DT}_{X}(0,n)=\left\langle\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{T}_{X}(d_{i})_{v_{i}}\,\Big|-1<\frac{v_{1}}{d_{1}}<\cdots<\frac{v_{k}}{d_{k}}\leqslant 0\right\rangle,

where the right hand side contains all partitions
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}=n
.

We proved Corollary
1.2
for
\mathbb{C}^{3}
in
[
PTa
, Theorem 1.1]
. We explain in Subsection
1.3
that Corollary
1.2
is a categorical analogue of the computation of DT invariants of points on a
3
-fold of Behrend–Fantechi
[
BF08
]
.
By taking the Grothendieck groups of the categories in Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
, we obtain an analogue of (
1.2
) in K-theory:

Corollary 1.3
.

There is an isomorphism

(1.5)

K(\mathcal{DT}_{X}(\beta,n))\cong\bigoplus_{n^{\prime}\leqslant n}K\left(\mathcal{DT}_{X}(0,n-n^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{PT}_{X}(\beta,n^{\prime})\right).

1.3.
Quasi-BPS categories

Let
S
be a smooth (not necessarily projective) surface.
For the local surface
X=\mathrm{Tot}_{S}(K_{S})
, the first author defined quasi-BPS categories
\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}
of
d
points and weight
w
in
[
Pădb
]
. We briefly explain the relation of these categories to (numerical, cohomological) BPS (also called Gopakumar-Vafa) invariants.

There are semiorthogonal decompositions of the Hall algebra of points on a surface of Porta–Sala
[
PS
]
with summands which are products of quasi-BPS categories
[
Pădb
, Theorem 1.1]
. This is a categorical analogue of the Davison–Meinhardt PBW theorem for the cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) of a quiver with potential
[
DM20
]
which says that the CoHA is generated by the (cohomological) BPS spaces of the quiver with potential.

The numerical BPS invariants are expected to be fundamental enumerative invariants which determine DT/PT invariants and Gromow-Witten invariants
[
PT14
, Section 2 and a half]
.
The BPS invariants of
d
points on
X
are given by

(1.6)

\Omega_{X,d}=-\chi_{c}(S),

where
\chi_{c}(S)
is the Euler characteristic of the compactly supported cohomology of
S
.
It is an interesting problem to compute the K-theory of quasi-BPS categories.
In
[
PTa
]
, we discussed two K-theoretic computations for quasi-BPS categories of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
: of localized equivariant K-theory (with respect to the two dimensional Calabi-Yau torus of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
) and of topological K-theory.

Assume
S
is a toric surface. Let
T
be the two dimensional torus acting on
S
. Let
\mathbb{K}:=K_{0}(BT)
,
\mathbb{F}:=\mathrm{Frac}\,\mathbb{K}
, and
K_{T}\left(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}\right)_{\mathbb{F}}:=K_{T}\left(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{F}
. The following is the analogue of
[
PTa
, Theorem 4.12]
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
S
be a toric surface, let
X=\mathrm{Tot}_{S}(K_{S})
, let
(d,w)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
, and let
n=\gcd(d,w)
. Then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}K_{T}\left(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}\right)_{\mathbb{F}}=\chi_{c}(\mathrm{Hilb}(S,n)).

We next discuss topological K-theoretic computations.
For a dg-category
\mathcal{D}
, consider its topological K-theory
K_{i}^{\rm{top}}(\mathcal{D})

defined by Blanc
[
Bla16
]
, which
is two-periodic by its construction
[
Bla16
, Definition 1.6]
.
Let

\chi_{K}(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}):=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{0}^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w})_{\mathbb{Q}}-\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

We propose the following K-theoretic computations, see
[
PTa
, Corollary 5.13]
for the case of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
S
be a smooth surface, let
X=\mathrm{Tot}_{S}(K_{S})
,
let
(d,w)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
, and let
n=\gcd(d,w)
. Then

\chi_{K}(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w})=\chi_{c}(\mathrm{Hilb}(S,n)).

When
\gcd(d,w)=1
, we regard
\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}
as categorifications of the BPS invariants
\Omega_{X,d}
, for example Conjecture
1.5
implies that
\chi_{K}(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w})=|\Omega_{X,d}|
.
Further, when
\gcd(d,w)=1
, we show (using
[
PTb
, Theorem 1.1]
) that objects in
\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}
are supported over the small diagonal
X\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(X)
, which is a categorical analogue of Davison’s support lemma
[
Dav
, Lemma 4.2, Theorem 5.1]
.

For
\gcd(d,w)>1
, the Euler characteristic of the topological K-theory of
\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}
may be different from
|\Omega_{X,d}|
.
In
[
PTb
]
, we constructed a coproduct on the K-theory of quasi-BPS categories of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
of fixed slope and we used it to define a K-theoretic version of BPS invariants for all
(d,w)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
. We do not know if this is possible for a general local surface
X
.

Let
(d,w)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(d,w)=n
.
Similarly to our expectations for
S=\mathbb{C}^{2}
from
[
PTa
]
,
[
PTb
]
, we believe the category
\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}
is related to the category
\mathrm{MF}^{\mathrm{gr}}(X^{\times d}/\mathfrak{S}_{d},0)
of zero matrix factorizations on the orbifold
X^{\times d}/\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.

1.4.
Computations of K-theoretic DT invariants via quasi-BPS categories

We revisit the computation of DT invariants from BPS invariants.
By
[
BF08
]
, there is an equality

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\geqslant 0}\mathrm{DT}_{X,d}\cdot q^{d}=\prod_{d\geqslant 1}(1-(-q)^{d})^{d\Omega_{X,d}}=\prod_{d\geqslant 1}(1-(-q)^{d})^{-d\chi_{c}(S)}.

In the spirit of
[
PTa
]
,
[
PTb
, Subsection 5.5]
, we regard Corollary
1.2
as a (partial) categorical analogue of the above formula.
To obtain a K-theoretic version of (
1.7
) from Theorem
1.2
, one needs a K-theoretic computation of quasi-BPS categories as in Conjecture
1.5
. More precisely, assuming Conjecture
1.5
and the following Künneth formula

(1.8)

\chi_{K}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{T}_{X}(d_{i})_{v_{i}}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{K}(\mathbb{T}_{X}(d_{i})_{v_{i}}),

we obtain from Corollary
1.2
the following computation of the Euler characteristic of the topological K-theory of DT categories of points on a local surface:

(1.9)

\sum_{d\geqslant 0}\chi_{K}(\mathcal{DT}_{X}(0,d))\cdot q^{d}=\prod_{d\geqslant 1}(1-q^{d})^{-d\chi_{c}(S)},

see for example
[
PTa
, Corollary 4.13]
,
[
PTb
, Subsection 5.5]
. We discuss an analogous statement for the localized equivariant K-theory of toric surfaces in Remark
5.12
.

2.3.
The Koszul equivalence

Let
Y
be a smooth affine scheme with an action of a reductive group
G
, let
\mathcal{Y}=Y/G
, and let
V
be a
G
-equivariant vector bundle on
Y
. Let
\mathbb{C}^{*}
act on the fibers of
V
with weight
2
and consider
s\in\Gamma(Y,V)
a section of
V
of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-weight
2
.
It induces a map
\partial\colon V^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.
Let
s^{-1}(0)
be the derived zero locus of

with ring of regular functions

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{s^{-1}(0)}:=\mathcal{O}_{Y}\left[V^{\vee}[1];\partial\right].

Consider the quotient

(2.4)

\mathscr{P}:=s^{-1}(0)/G.

We call
\mathscr{P}

(Y,V,s,G)
.
We denote by

The section

Consider the category of graded matrix factorizations
\text{MF}^{\text{gr}}\left(\mathscr{V}^{\vee},f\right)
with respect to the group
\mathbb{C}^{*}
mentioned above. The Koszul duality equivalence, also called dimensional reduction in the literature, says the following:

Theorem 2.1

(
[

,

2.4.

2.4.1.
Attracting stacks

Let
Y
be an affine variety with an action of a reductive group
G
. Let
\lambda
be a cocharacter of
G
.
Let
G^{\lambda}
and
G^{\lambda\geqslant 0}
be the Levi and parabolic groups associated to
\lambda
. Let
Y^{\lambda}\subset Y
be the closed subvariety of
\lambda
-fixed points.
Consider the attracting variety

Y^{\lambda\geqslant 0}:=\{y\in Y|\,\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)\cdot y\in Y^{\lambda}\}\subset Y.

Consider the attracting and fixed stacks

(2.7)

\mathscr{Z}:=Y^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}\xleftarrow{q}\mathscr{S}:=Y^{\lambda\geqslant 0}/G^{\lambda\geqslant 0}\xrightarrow{p}\mathcal{Y}.

The map
p
is proper.
Kempf-Ness strata are connected components of certain attracting stacks
\mathscr{S}
, and the map
p
restricted to a Kempf-Ness stratum is a closed immersion, see
[
HL15
, Section 2.1]
.
The attracting stacks also appear in the definition of Hall algebras
[
Păda
]
(for
Y
an affine space), where the Hall product is induced by the functor

(2.8)

\ast:=p_{*}q^{*}\colon D^{b}(\mathscr{Z})\to D^{b}(\mathcal{Y}).

In this case, the map
p
may not be a closed immersion.

Let
T\subset G
be a maximal torus and

\lambda
is a cocharacter
\lambda\colon\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\to T
.
For a
G
-representation
Y
,
the attracting variety

Y^{\lambda\geqslant 0}\subset Y

coincides with the sub
T
-representation
generated by weights which pair non-negatively with
\lambda
.
We denote by

\langle\lambda,Y^{\lambda\geqslant 0}\rangle:=\langle\lambda,\det Y^{\lambda\geqslant 0}\rangle
,
where
\det Y^{\lambda\geqslant 0}
is the sum of
T
-weights of
Y^{\lambda\geqslant 0}
.

2.4.2.
The definition of window categories

Let
Y
be an affine variety with an action of a reductive group
G
and a linearization
\mathscr{L}
. Consider the stacks

j\colon\mathcal{Y}^{\mathscr{L}\text{-ss}}:=Y^{\mathscr{L}\text{-ss}}/G\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}:=Y/G.

We review the construction of window categories of
D^{b}(\mathcal{Y})
which are equivalent to
D^{b}(\mathcal{Y}^{\mathscr{L}\text{-ss}})
via the restriction map, due to
Segal
[
Seg11
]
, Halpern-Leistner
[
HL15
]
, and Ballard–Favero–Katzarkov
[
BFK19
]
. We follow the presentation from
[
HL15
]
.

By also fixing a Weyl-invariant norm on the
cocharacter lattice,
the unstable locus
\mathcal{Y}\setminus\mathcal{Y}^{\mathscr{L}\text{-ss}}
has a stratification in Kempf-Ness strata
\mathscr{S}_{i}
for
i\in I
a finite ordered set:

\mathcal{Y}\setminus\mathcal{Y}^{\mathscr{L}\text{-ss}}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\mathscr{S}_{i}.

A Kempf-Ness stratum
\mathscr{S}_{i}
is the attracting stack
in
\mathcal{Y}\setminus\sqcup_{j<i}\mathcal{S}_{j}

for
a cocharacter
\lambda_{i}
, with the fixed stack
\mathscr{Z}_{i}:=\mathscr{S}_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}
.
Let
N_{\mathscr{S}_{i}/\mathcal{Y}}
be the normal bundle of
\mathscr{S}_{i}
in
\mathcal{Y}
.
Define the width of the window categories

\eta_{i}:=\left\langle\lambda_{i},N^{\vee}_{\mathscr{S}_{i}/\mathcal{Y}}|_{\mathscr{Z}_{i}}\right\rangle.

For a choice of real numbers
w=(w_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
, define the category

(2.9)

\mathbb{G}_{w}:=\{\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}(\mathcal{Y})\text{ such that }\mathrm{wt}_{\lambda_{i}}(\mathcal{F}|_{\mathscr{Z}_{i}})\subset[w_{i},w_{i}+\eta_{i})\text{ for all }i\in I\}.

In the above,
\mathrm{wt}_{\lambda_{i}}(\mathcal{F}|_{\mathscr{Z}_{i}})

is the set of
\lambda_{i}
-weights on
\mathcal{F}|_{\mathscr{Z}_{i}}
.
Then
[
HL15
, Theorem 2.10]

says that the restriction functor

j^{*}
induces an equivalence of categories:

(2.10)

j^{*}\colon\mathbb{G}_{w}\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}\big(\mathcal{Y}^{\mathscr{L}\text{-ss}}\big)

for any choice of real numbers
w=(w_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
.

We discuss a slight extension of the above theorem for matrix factorizations. Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{C}
be a regular function and recall the definition of
\mathrm{MF}(\mathbb{G}_{w},f)
from Subsection
2.2.1
. Then there is an equivalence

(2.11)

j^{*}\colon\mathrm{MF}(\mathbb{G}_{w},f)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{MF}\big(\mathcal{Y}^{\mathscr{L}\text{-ss}},f\big).

The analogous statement holds for categories of graded matrix factorizations.

For later use, we also generalize the above notation and construction.
Let
d=d^{(1)}+\cdots+d^{(m)}
and set

G=\prod_{j=1}^{m}GL(d^{(j)})
.
Its maximal torus is

, and we
denote the simple roots of

by

\beta_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,\beta_{d^{(j)}}^{(j)}
.
We write

(3.1)

\displaystyle\chi=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\chi^{(j)}\in\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}M(d^{(j)})_{\mathbb{R}}.

We say that
\chi
is

-dominant if each
\chi^{(j)}
is dominant.
Let
\rho^{(j)}
be half the sum of positive roots of

.
We set

, which is half the sum of
positive roots of

.

3.2.2.
The Špenko–Van den Bergh construction

We explain a construction of (twisted) noncommutative
resolutions of singularities

of the coarse quotient space

due to Špenko–Van den Bergh
[

]
.
Define the polytope
\textbf{W}(d)
as

(3.2)

\textbf{W}(d):=\frac{3}{2}\text{sum}[0,\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}]+\mathbb{R}\tau_{d}\subset M(d)_{\mathbb{R}},

where the Minkowski sum is after all
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d
.
For

,
consider the hyperplane:

(3.3)

For

, denote by

the subcategory of

consisting of objects of

with respect to the diagonal
cocharacter

of

.
There is a direct sum decomposition

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathcal{X}(d))=\bigoplus_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}D^{b}(\mathcal{X}(d))_{w}.

We define the dg-subcategories

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}(d)\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{X}(d)),\ (\mbox{resp. }\mathbb{M}(d)_{w}\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{X}(d))_{w})

to be generated
by the vector bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}(d)}\otimes\Gamma_{GL(d)}(\chi)
, where
\chi
is a dominant weight of

such that

(3.4)

\chi+\rho\in\textbf{W}(d),\ (\mbox{resp. }\chi+\rho\in\textbf{W}(d)_{w}).

Note that
\mathbb{M}(d)

decomposes into the direct sum of
\mathbb{M}(d)_{w}

for
w\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Alternatively, the category
\mathbb{M}(d)_{w}

is generated by the vector bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}(d)}\otimes\Gamma

for
\Gamma
a
GL(d)
-representation such that
the
T(d)
-weights of
\Gamma
are contained in the set

\nabla_{w}
defined by:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\chi\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,\Big|-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\lambda}\leqslant\langle\lambda,\chi\rangle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\lambda}\mbox{ for all }\lambda\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\to T(d)\right\}+w\tau_{d},

where

,
see
[
HLS20
, Lemma 2.9]
.

3.2.3.
Quasi-BPS categories via matrix factorizations

Consider the regular function

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\,W_{d}\colon\mathcal{X}(d)\to\mathbb{C},\,(X,Y,Z)\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}\left(X[Y,Z]\right)

Let

3.3.
The local categorical DT/PT correspondence

Let
Q^{af,N}
be the quiver obtained by adding
N
-loops at the vertex
0

of the DT/PT quiver
Q^{af}
.
Then the moduli stack of representations of
Q^{af,N}

of dimension
(1,d)
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d)=\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathcal{X}^{af}(1,d)=(\mathbb{C}^{N}\times R^{af}(1,d))/GL(d)

where
GL(d)
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
trivially.
Let
\widetilde{W}
be a super-potential on
Q^{af,N}
satisfying

\widetilde{W}|_{Q}=X[Y,Z],

where

Q\subset Q^{af,N}
is the full subquiver consisting
of the vertex
\{1\}
, i.e. it is the triple loop quiver.
Then we have the function

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\widetilde{W}_{d}\colon\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d)\to\mathbb{C}.

Let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\to T(d)
be the cocharacter given by

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=(\overbrace{t^{k},\ldots,t^{k}}^{d_{1}},\ldots,\overbrace{t,\ldots,t}^{d_{k}},\overbrace{1,\ldots,1}^{d^{\prime}}).

We have the diagram of attracting and fixed stacks

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d)^{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q_{\lambda}}}{{\leftarrow}}\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d)^{\lambda\geqslant 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{\lambda}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d)

where the fixed stack is

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d)^{\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{X}(d_{i})\times\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d^{\prime}).

The pull-back/push-forward with respect to the diagram (
3.13
)
gives the categorical Hall product for DT/PT quivers with super-potentials, see
[
Păda
, Section 3]
:

(3.14)

\displaystyle\ast=q_{\lambda\ast}p_{\lambda}^{\ast}\colon\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}(d_{i}),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d_{i}})\boxtimes

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d^{\prime}),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\widetilde{W}_{d^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\widetilde{W}_{d}).

Let
\delta=\mu\chi_{0}=\mu\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta_{i}
for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. We define the
subcategories

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{a}(1,d;\delta)\subset\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\widetilde{W}_{d}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}^{a}(1,d;\delta)\subset\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\widetilde{W}_{d})

of matrix factorizations whose factors are generated by

\Gamma_{GL(d)}(\chi)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}^{af,N}(1,d)}

for
\chi
a dominant weight of
T(d)
satisfying

\displaystyle\chi+\rho+\delta\in\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{sum}[0,\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}]+\frac{a}{2}\mathrm{sum}[-\beta_{k},\beta_{k}]+\mathrm{sum}[-\beta_{k},0],

\displaystyle\chi+\rho+\delta\in\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{sum}[0,\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}]+\frac{a}{2}\mathrm{sum}(-\beta_{k},\beta_{k}]

respectively, where
1\leqslant i,j,k\leqslant d
.

Let
R
be the following set

R=\{(d_{i},v_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\mid d_{i}\in\mathbb{N},v_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We define a subset
O\subset R\times R
which will be used to compare summands in the semiorthogonal decomposition of Theorem
3.2
and
Theorem
1.1
, see Subsection
2.1
.
This order is a particular example of an order from
[
PTc
, Subsection 3.2.3]
, which depends on a choice of
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A pair of elements
S=(d_{i},v_{i})_{i=1}^{k}

and
S^{\prime}=(d_{i}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}

in
R

is an element of
O\subset R\times R
if either

•

\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}>\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}v_{i}^{\prime}
, or

•

\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}v_{i}^{\prime}

and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}<\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}d_{i}^{\prime}
, or

•

\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}v_{i}^{\prime}
,
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}d_{i}^{\prime}
, and
(S,S^{\prime})

is in the set
O
from
[
PTa
, Subsection 3.4]
.

The following is the local categorical DT/PT correspondence in terms of the
above subcategories, see
[
PTc
, Proposition 3.12, Corollary 3.14]
:

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
2\mu l\notin\mathbb{Z}
for
1\leqslant l\leqslant d
.
There is a semiorthogonal decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{a}(1,d;\delta)=\left\langle\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{S}(d_{i})_{w_{i}}\boxtimes\mathbb{F}(1,d^{\prime};\delta^{\prime})\right\rangle.

The right hand side is after all
d^{\prime}\leqslant d
, partitions
(d_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
of
d-d^{\prime}
, and integers
(w_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
such that for

(3.15)

v_{i}:=w_{i}+d_{i}\left(d^{\prime}+\sum_{j>i}d_{j}-\sum_{j<i}d_{j}\right),

we have

(3.16)

-1-\mu-\frac{a}{2}<\frac{v_{1}}{d_{1}}<\cdots<\frac{v_{k}}{d_{k}}<-\mu-\frac{a}{2}.

In the above, we let
\delta^{\prime}:=\left(\mu-d+d^{\prime}\right)\sum_{i>d-d^{\prime}}^{d}\beta_{i}
.
Moreover, each fully-faithful functor

\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{S}(d_{i})_{w_{i}}\boxtimes\mathbb{F}(1,d^{\prime};\delta^{\prime})\to\mathbb{E}^{a}(1,d;\delta)

is given by the restriction of the categorical Hall product (
3.14
).

The order of the semiorthogonal summands is
discussed in
[
PTc
, Subsection 3.2.3]
and depends on
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, let
\mu=-a/2-\varepsilon
. Then the order is
that of
(d_{i},v_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\in R

in Definition
3.1
, see also Subsection
2.1
.

The analogous conclusion holds if we replace
\mathbb{C}^{N}
by an open subset in all the constructions and statements above, and also for graded categories of matrix factorizations.

Remark 3.3
.

One also obtains similar semiorthogonal decompositions for different super-potentials
\widetilde{W}
. Assume
N=0
and
\widetilde{W}=0
.
Let
\chi_{0}\colon GL(V)\to\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
be the determinant character

,
and define the following DT and PT spaces for
the quivers
Q^{af}

defined by the GIT quotient stacks (which are smooth quasi-projective varieties):

\displaystyle I^{a}(d):=\mathcal{X}^{af}(1,d)^{\chi_{0}\text{-ss}},\ P^{a}(d):=\mathcal{X}^{af}(1,d)^{\chi^{-1}_{0}\text{-ss}}.

Using the window theorem, we obtain the local categorical DT/PT correspondence
[

, which says that there is a semiorthogonal decomposition

where the right hand side is indexed as in Theorem

.

4.
Quasi-BPS categories for points on a surface

In this section, we introduce quasi-BPS categories for points on surfaces

4.1.
The definition of quasi-BPS category

Let
S

\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d)
the derived moduli stack of

of length
d
.
The derived stack
\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{S}(d)\to\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(S)

Moreover, the stack
\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d)
is
symmetric, see
[
Toda
, Lemma 5.4.1]
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}\in D^{b}(S\times\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d))
be the universal zero-dimensional sheaf.
We define the following line bundle on

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}:=\mathrm{det}(p_{\mathfrak{M}\ast}\mathcal{Q})\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d)),

where

is
the natural projection.
Recall the definition of an intrinsic window subcategory for
a quasi-smooth and symmetric derived stack, see Definition
2.5
.
We define the quasi-BPS category as follows:

Definition 4.1
.

For
(d,w)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
,
we define the quasi-BPS category

\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}\subset D^{b}(\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d))
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w}:=\mathbb{W}_{\delta=w\mathscr{L}/d}^{\rm{int}}\subset D^{b}(\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d)).

We see that the above definition coincides with the
one introduced in
[
Pădb
]
.
Let us take a point
p=\sum_{j=1}^{m}d^{(j)}x^{(j)}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(S)
for distinct
points

\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}_{S}(d)_{p}
the formal fiber of
\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d)
at
p

Let

be distinct points

Then there is an equivalence of derived stacks

\displaystyle\iota_{p}\colon\widehat{\mathscr{C}}(d)_{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}_{S}(d)_{p}.

Let
\mathbb{T}(d)_{w}\subset D^{b}(\mathscr{C}(d))
be the quasi-BPS category
from Subsection
3.2.4
and define

\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(d)_{w,q}\subset D^{b}(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}(d)_{q})

The following lemma

Proof.

The defining property of the intrinsic window
subcategory is local on the good moduli space, so

\mathcal{E}\in D^{b}(\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d))

if and only if,

in

where

By the presentation invariance of the intrinsic window subcategory,

[

\mathcal{F}_{q}:=\iota_{p}^{\ast}\left(\mathcal{E}\big|_{\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}_{S}(d)_{p}}\right)

We have the closed immersion

\displaystyle j_{q}\colon\widehat{\mathscr{C}}(d)_{q}=\mu_{q}^{-1}(0)/GL(d)\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}(d)_{q},

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{\mathscr{V}}|_{\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}(d)_{q}}=\left(\mathfrak{gl}(d)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}(d)_{q}}\to\mathfrak{gl}(d)^{\oplus 3}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}(d)_{q}}\right).

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\lambda^{\ast}j_{q\ast}\mathcal{F}_{q})\subset\left[-\langle\lambda,\mathfrak{gl}(d)^{\lambda>0}\rangle,\langle\lambda,\mathfrak{gl}(d)^{\lambda>0}\rangle\right]+w\tau_{d},

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{X}(d)_{w,p}\subset D^{b}(\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}_{S}(d)_{p})

where

Lemma 4.3

An object
\mathcal{E}\in D^{b}(\mathfrak{M}_{S}(d))_{w}

Remark 5.10
.

It is possible to obtain a version of Theorem

The definitions of the DT/PT categories for reduced curves are similar to Definition

We consider the (finite type) open substack
\mathfrak{T}^{\mathrm{red}}_{S}(\beta,n)\subset\mathfrak{T}_{S}(\beta,n)

such that
F
has reduced support.
The classical truncation
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{red}}_{S}(\beta,n)

\mathrm{Chow}^{\beta}(S)^{\mathrm{red}}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(S)

.

By the argument in
[

, Lemma 5.5.4]
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{red}}_{X}(\beta,n):=t_{0}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{T}^{\mathrm{red}}_{S}(\beta,n)}[-1])

is the moduli stack of pairs
(\mathcal{O}_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\to}}E)
,
where

has compact support,

with reduced supports over

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{DT}^{\mathrm{red}}_{X}(\beta,n):=D^{b}(\mathfrak{T}^{\mathrm{red}}_{S}(\beta,n))/\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{Z}_{I}^{\mathrm{red}}},

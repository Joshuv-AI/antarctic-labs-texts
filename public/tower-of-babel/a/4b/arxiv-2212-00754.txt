On scalar-type standing-wave solutions to systems of nonlinear Schrödinger equations
Satoshi MASAKI

Abstract
. In this article, we study the standing-wave solutions to a class of systems of nonlinear Schrödinger equations. Our target is all the standard forms of the NLS systems, with two unknowns, that have a common linear part and cubic gauge-invariant nonlinearities and that yield a Hamiltonian with a coercive kinetic-energy part. We give a necessary and sufficient condition on the existence of the ground state. Further, we give a characterization of the shape of the ground state. It will turn out that the ground states are scalar-type, i.e., multiples of a constant vector and a scalar function. We further give a sufficient condition on the existence of excited states of the same form. The stability and the instability of the ground states are also studied. To this end, we introduce an abstract treatment on the study of scalar-type standing-wave solution that applies to a wide class of NLS systems with homogeneous energy-subcritical nonlinearity. By the argument, some previous results are reproduced.

1.
Introduction

This article studies the standing-wave solutions (solitons)
to a class of systems of nonlinear Schrödinger equations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The specific systems we have in our mind are the following:

(1)

Type 1, decoupled systems:

(NLS1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{1}&=\alpha|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1},\\
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{2}&=\beta|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,0,1\}
satisfy
\alpha\geqslant\beta
.

(2)

Type 2, systems with componentwise mass-conservation:

(NLS2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{1}&=\alpha|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}+\sigma(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{1},\\
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{2}&=\beta|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}+\sigma(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha\geqslant\beta
and
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}
.

(3)

Type 3:

(NLS3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{1}&=(3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})(2|u_{2}|^{2}u_{1}+u_{2}^{2}\overline{u_{1}})+r(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{1},\\
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{2}&=(3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})(2|u_{1}|^{2}u_{2}+u_{1}^{2}\overline{u_{2}})+r(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha_{2}\geqslant 0
,
\alpha_{1}^{2}\neq\alpha_{2}^{2}
,
\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=1
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

(4)

Type 4:

(NLS4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{1}&=(3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+2\alpha_{3})|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})(2|u_{2}|^{2}u_{1}+u_{2}^{2}\overline{u_{1}})\\
&\quad+r(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{1},\\
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{2}&=(3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\alpha_{3})|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})(2|u_{1}|^{2}u_{2}+u_{1}^{2}\overline{u_{2}})\\
&\quad+r(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\alpha_{2}\geqslant 0
,
\alpha_{3}>0
,
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}
,

\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2}=1
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

(5)

Type 5:

(NLS5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{1}&=(3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+2\alpha_{3}\cos\eta)|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})(2|u_{2}|^{2}u_{1}+u_{2}^{2}\overline{u_{1}})\\
&\quad+\alpha_{3}\sin\eta(2|u_{1}|^{2}u_{2}+u_{1}^{2}\overline{u_{2}}+|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2})+r(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{1},\\
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{2}&=(3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2\alpha_{3}\cos\eta)|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})(2|u_{1}|^{2}u_{2}+u_{1}^{2}\overline{u_{2}})\\
&\quad+\alpha_{3}\sin\eta(2|u_{2}|^{2}u_{1}+u_{2}^{2}\overline{u_{1}}+|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1})+r(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\alpha_{2}>0
,
\alpha_{3}>0
,
\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2}=1
,
\eta\in(0,\pi)
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
.
We assume
\alpha_{1}>0
if
\eta>\pi/2
.

Note that the systems of (
NLS3
) and (
NLS4
) are degenerate cases
\alpha_{3}=0
and
\eta=0

of (
NLS5
), respectively.

By a standard argument, the well-posedness of the above systems is established in
(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
for
d\leqslant 4
(See
[
6
]
, for instance).
These systems possess three conserved quantities.
One is
mass
(or
charge
)

(1.1)

M(u_{1},u_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|u_{1}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|u_{2}|^{2}\right)dx

and the others two are
momentum

(1.2)

P(u_{1},u_{2})=\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\overline{u_{1}}\nabla u_{1}+\overline{u_{2}}\nabla u_{2}\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(|\hat{u_{1}}|^{2}+|\hat{u_{2}}|^{2})d\xi

and
energy

(1.3)

E(u_{1},u_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}g(u_{1},u_{2})\right)dx,

where
g
is a suitable quartic homogeneous polynomial given by the nonlinearity.

In
[
24
,
23
,
22
]
, the author and his collaborators give the classification of cubic systems which possess at least one mass-like conserved quantity.
According to the classification, one sees that the above (
NLS1
)–(
NLS5
) are the complete list of the standard forms (or the canonical forms)
of the systems which have gauge-invariant cubic nonlinearities and which
admit the conserved energy with a coercive kinetic-energy part.
See Theorem
A.1
for more detail.

The main purpose of the present article is to study the standing-wave solutions (solitons) to the above systems.
It will turn out that the ground state solutions, i.e., the least action solutions, to the above systems are
scalar-type
.
Here, we say a vector-valued function is
scalar-type
if the function is given as a multiple of a constant vector and a scalar function.
There is a number of literature on the study of standing waves of the NLS systems (see
[
1
,
19
,
29
,
18
,
14
]
and references therein).
Some of the above systems are already studied.

In this article, we introduce a unified treatment to study the
scalar-type
solutions of the following
elliptic system

(gE
ω
)

-\Delta u_{j}+\omega u_{j}=-F_{j}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})\qquad(j=1,2,\dots,N),

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

d\geqslant 1
,
N\geqslant 2
,
\omega>0
,
u_{j}
are complex-valued unknowns, and
F_{j}
are nonlinearities.
Let us emphasize that the parameter
\omega>0
is independent of
j
.
This restriction is made because it is a natural requirement for the presence of standing-wave solutions to NLS systems without a constant-type linear potential such as (
NLS1
)–(
NLS5
) (See Remark
1.9
).
As
\omega
reflects the decay speed of the components at the spatial infinity, it is quite natural to expect that
(
gEω
) possesses scalar-type solutions.
Except for the equality of the parameter
\omega
, our argument applies to a wide range of systems.
Roughly speaking, we make only two assumptions on nonlinearity:
One is the existence of a simple Hamiltonian structure (i.e., the existence of a Hamiltonian with the normalized kinetic-energy part), and the other is the homogeneity of the function that determines the nonlinear part of the Hamiltonian.
The precise assumption on the nonlinearity is given below in Assumption
1
.
We give a simple criterion on the existence/nonexistence of the ground state and its shape (Theorem
1.2
).
That of scalar-type excited states is also established (Theorem
1.3
).

As is well known, solutions to the nonlinear elliptic system of the form (
gEω
)
have a connection between standing wave solutions to an NLS system.
Fix a vector
{\bf n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{N}
and consider the NLS system

(gNLS)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(i\partial_{t}+n_{j}\Delta)u_{j}=n_{j}F_{j}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})\qquad(j=1,2,\dots,N),\\
&(u_{1}(0),u_{2}(0),\dots,u_{N}(0))=(u_{1,0},u_{2,0},\dots,u_{N,0})\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N},\end{aligned}\right.

where
{\bf u}(t)=(u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t),\dots,u_{N}(t))
,

(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+d}
,
d\geqslant 1
, and
N\geqslant 2
.
To link (
gEω
) and (
gNLS
), we need one more assumption on the nonlinearity (Assumption
2
).
This is a generalized version of the gauge-invariance property. The vector
{\bf n}
is given in the assumption.
The systems (
NLS1
)–(
NLS5
) are obtained with the choice
N=2
and
{\bf n}=(1,1)
.
Here we consider general
{\bf n}
because we would compare our result with previous results on the scalar-type solutions.
In particular, we will find that our theorems reproduce results in
[
CO
]
.

1.1.
Scalar-type solutions to the nonlinear elliptic systems

We first consider the nonlinear elliptic system (
gEω
).
Let us make the assumption on the nonlinearity clear.

Assumption 1
.

The nonlinearity is given by the formula

(1.4)

F_{j}:=\tfrac{2}{p}\partial_{\overline{z_{j}}}g

for
j=1,2,\dots,N
from a function
g:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
which is twice continuously differentiable as a function
\mathbb{R}^{2N}\to\mathbb{C}

and is homogeneous of degree
p
:

(1.5)

g(rz_{1},rz_{2},\dots,rz_{N})=r^{p}g(z_{1},z_{2}.\dots,z_{N})

for all
(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
r\geqslant 0
. Here,
p
satisfies the energy subcriticality condition

(1.6)

p\in(2,2^{*}),

where
2^{*}=\infty
for
d=1,2
and

2^{*}=2d/(d-2)
for
d\geqslant 3
.

We remark that the
{z}
-derivative and the

\overline{z}
-derivative of a function
f(z)
are given by
\partial_{{z}}f=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{y})\tilde{f}
and

\partial_{\overline{z}}f=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}+i\partial_{y})\tilde{f}
, respectively, with the identifications
z=x+iy
and
f(z)=\tilde{f}(x,y)
. Since
g
is real-valued, one has
\partial_{\overline{z_{j}}}g=\overline{\partial_{z_{j}}g}
.
This gives us an alternative representation of the nonlinearity:

(1.7)

F_{j}=\tfrac{1}{p}(\overline{\partial_{{z_{j}}}g}+\partial_{\overline{z_{j}}}g)=\tfrac{2}{p}\overline{\partial_{{z_{j}}}g}.

By differentiating the both sides of (
1.5
) by
\overline{z_{j}}
and applying the chain rule, we obtain

(1.8)

F_{j}(rz_{1},rz_{2},\dots,rz_{N})=r^{p-1}F_{j}(z_{1},z_{2}.\dots,z_{N})

for all
(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
r\geqslant 0
.

For
\omega>0
,
let
S_{\omega}
be the action functional on
(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
given by

(1.9)

S_{\omega}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N}):=E(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})+\omega M(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})

with

(1.10)

E(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{p}g(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})dx

and

(1.11)

M(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}|u_{j}|^{2}dx,

where
g
is the function given in Assumption
1
.
Note that
{\bf u}\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
solves (
gEω
) if and only if it is a critical point
S_{\omega}
,
i.e.,
S^{\prime}_{\omega}({\bf u})=0
.

Definition 1.1
.

For
\omega>0
, let

\mathcal{A}_{\omega}:=\{\Phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{N})\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}\ |\ \Phi\neq 0,\,S^{\prime}_{\omega}(\Phi)=0\}

be the set of solutions to the elliptic system (
gEω
)
and let

\mathcal{G}_{\omega}:=\left\{\Phi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\ \middle|\ S_{\omega}(\Phi)=\inf_{\Psi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}}S_{\omega}(\Psi)\right\}

be the set of ground states. Further, we let
\mathcal{A}=\cup_{\omega>0}\mathcal{A}_{\omega}

and
\mathcal{G}=\cup_{\omega>0}\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
.
We call an element of
\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{G}
an excited state.

Let
Q\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the positive radial solution to the elliptic equation

-\Delta Q+Q=Q^{p-1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For
\omega>0
and
a>0
, we denote

Q_{\omega,a}:=(\omega/a)^{1/(p-2)}Q(\sqrt{\omega}\cdot).

Note that
\omega>0
is a scale parameter and
a>0
is an amplitude parameter.
Obviously,
Q_{\omega,a}
is a positive radial solution to

(1.12)

-\Delta Q_{\omega,a}+\omega Q_{\omega,a}=aQ_{\omega,a}^{p-1}.

Let

\partial B:=\{(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\ |\ |z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{N}|^{2}=1\}

be the boundary of the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
For
{\bf w}=(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{N})\in\partial B
,
\omega>0
,
and
a>0
, we define

(1.13)

\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,a):=\{{\bf w}Q_{\omega,a}(\cdot-y)=(w_{1}Q_{\omega,a}(\cdot-y),w_{2}Q_{\omega,a}(\cdot-y),\dots,w_{N}Q_{\omega,a}(\cdot-y))\ |\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

This is a set of scalar-type functions.

Set

(1.14)

g_{\min}=\min_{(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\partial B}g(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})

and

(1.15)

T_{0}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\partial B\ |\ g(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})=g_{\min}\}.

Our main result on the ground states
is formulated as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose Assumption
1
.
If
g_{\min}<0
then

\mathcal{G}_{\omega}=\bigcup_{{\bf w}\in T_{0}}\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,-g_{\min})

for all
\omega>0
.
If
g_{\min}\geqslant 0
then
\mathcal{A}=\emptyset
, i.e., there is no nontrivial solution to (
gEω
) for all
\omega>0
.

We remark that the theorem tells us that
g_{\min}<0
is a necessary and sufficient condition of the existence of the ground states.
The existence of the ground state is studied in
[
2
,
5
,
30
]
in an abstract setting.
Our result gives a precise description of the shape of the ground states.
This kind of characterization is introduced in
[
9
]
to study some specific systems of the form (
gEω
).

Let us next turn to the excited states.
We have the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose Assumption
1
.
Let
{\bf w}\in\partial B
and
a>0
.

\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,a)\subset\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
holds for all
\omega>0

if and only if

{\bf w}\in\partial B

is a critical point of
g|_{\partial B}
and
a=-g({\bf w})>0
.

The theorem implies that if
g|_{\partial B}
has a negative critical value other than its minimum value then (
gEω
) admits scalar-type excited states.
Our theorems reveal that the shape of the scalar-type solutions to (
gEω
)
is well described in the language of the function
g
, which is the novelty of our theorems.

Remark 1.4
.

We remark that a homogeneous function satisfying (
1.5
) is identified with a pair of the degree
p

and the function on
\partial B
. Indeed, for a function
g
satisfying (
1.5
),
the pair
(p,g|_{\partial B})
is uniquely specified obviously.
On the other hand, for the prescribed pair
(q,h)
, where
q>0
and
h:\partial B\to\mathbb{R}
, the function

\mathbb{C}^{N}\ni z\mapsto|z|^{q}h(\frac{z}{|z|})\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies (
1.5
).
The important quantity
g_{\min}
and set
T_{0}
given in (
1.14
) and (
1.15
), respectively, are defined only by the function part.

Remark 1.5
.

If
d\geqslant 2
then the nonlinear elliptic equation
-\Delta Q+Q=|Q|^{p-2}Q
admits sign-changing solutions.
If the function
g
satisfies the relation
g(-{\bf z})=g({\bf z})
for all
{\bf z}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}

then
the scalar-type functions given by the sign-changing solutions instead of the positive solution
Q

are also contained in
\mathcal{A}
. They are all excited states. See Remark
3.1
below.
As seen in the next subsection, the function
g
corresponding to the systems (
NLS1
)–(
NLS5
) satisfies the assumption.

It is known that the ground states are related to the optimizer of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
This is true also in our case.
Let us introduce a Gagliardo-Nirenberg-type inequality adopted in the current context. Besides its own interest, this inequality is used below in the study of the instability of the ground state.

Theorem 1.6

(A sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg-type inequality)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
. Suppose
g_{\min}<0
.
It holds for all
{\bf u}\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
that

-G({\bf u})\leqslant C_{\mathrm{GN}}M({\bf u})^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{d(p-2)}{4}}H({\bf u})^{\frac{d(p-2)}{4}},

where the constant
C_{\mathrm{GN}}
is given by

C_{\mathrm{GN}}=(\tfrac{2}{p-2})^{\frac{p}{2}}(\tfrac{2}{d})^{\frac{d(p-2)}{4}}(d-\tfrac{d-2}{2}p)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\|Q\|_{2}^{2-p}(-g_{\min}).

Further, the equality holds if and only if
{\bf u}
is a constant multiple of an element of
\mathcal{G}
.

1.2.
Linking with NLS systems and stability/instability of the ground states

Let us now turn to the study of standing-wave solutions to (
gNLS
).
Let us introduce the notion of an
H^{1}
-solution to (
gNLS
).

Definition 1.7
.

We say an
N
-tuple of functions
{\bf u}(t)=(u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t),\dots,u_{N}(t))
be an
H^{1}
-solution to (
gNLS
)
on an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
,
0\in\overline{I}
,
if
{\bf u}
belongs to

(C(I,H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{q_{d}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I,W^{1,r_{d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))^{N}

and satisfies

u_{j}(t)=e^{itn_{j}\Delta}u_{j,0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}n_{j}{F}_{j}({\bf u}(s))ds\qquad(j=1,2,\dotsm,N)

on
I
. Here,
(q_{1},r_{1})=(4,\infty)
,
(q_{2},r_{2})=(\frac{2p}{p-2},p)
, and
(q_{d},r_{d})=(2,2^{*})
for
d\geqslant 3
.
Let
I_{\max}=(T_{\min},T_{\max})
denote the maximal lifespan of the solution.
A soluiton extended to that on its maximal lifespan is called a maximal-lifespan solution.
We say a solution is global if
I_{\max}=\mathbb{R}
.

The local well-posedness of (
gNLS
) in the
H^{1}
-framework is established under Assumption
1

by a standard fixed point argument with Strichartz’ estimates (See e.g.
[
6
]
for details).
The solutions have the conserved
energy
defined in (
1.10
).
The energy conservation can be seen by means of the identity

\operatorname{Re}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{F}_{j}\overline{\partial_{t}u_{j}}=\partial_{t}(\tfrac{1}{p}{g}({\bf u})).

To relate the nonlinear elliptic system (
gEω
) and the nonlinear Schrodinger system,
we make one more assumption.

Assumption 2
.

There exists a vector
{\bf n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{N}
such that
the function
g
satisfies the gauge condition

(1.16)

g(e^{in_{1}\theta}z_{1},e^{in_{2}\theta}z_{2},\dots,e^{in_{N}\theta}z_{N})=g(z_{1},z_{2}.\dots,z_{N})

for all
(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

One easily sees that (
1.16
) yields

(1.17)

F_{j}(e^{in_{1}\theta}z_{1},e^{in_{2}\theta}z_{2},\dots,e^{in_{N}\theta}z_{N})=e^{in_{j}\theta}F_{j}(z_{1},z_{2}.\dots,z_{N})

for all
(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

If Assumption
2
is fulfilled with the same vector as in (
gNLS
)
then an
H^{1}
-solution
{\bf u}(t)
to (
gNLS
) also conserves the

mass
(or
charge
)
M({\bf u})
defined in (
1.11
).
Note that the conservation of
mass is verified with the property
\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j}\operatorname{Im}(F_{j}({\bf z})\overline{z_{j}})=0
for all
{\bf z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, which follows by differentiating (
1.16
) by
\theta
at
\theta=0
.

In the rest of this subsection, we suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied and consider
(
gNLS
) with the same vector
{\bf n}
given in Assumption
2
.

Theorem 1.8

(Standing wave solutions)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
, with the same vector
{\bf n}
as in (
gNLS
).
Let
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
be the set of solutions to (
gEω
) with the nonlinearity
{F}_{j}
.
If
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
then

(e^{in_{1}\omega t}\phi_{1},e^{in_{2}\omega t}\phi_{2},\dots,e^{in_{N}\omega t}\phi_{N})

is a global
H^{1}
-solution to (
gNLS
).

Remark 1.9
.

The equality of the parameter
\omega>0
in (
gEω
) corresponds to the
absence of a constant-type linear potential in (
gNLS
).
Namely,
consider

(gNLS’)

(i\partial_{t}+n_{j}\Delta-n_{j}b_{j})u_{j}=n_{j}F_{j}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})\qquad(j=1,2,\dots,N),

where
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Suppose that
F_{j}
satisfies Assumption
1
and
2
, with the same vector
{\bf n}
as in the equation.
Note that a suitable application of the gauge transform
u_{j}(t,x)\mapsto e^{in_{j}\theta t}u_{j}(t,x)
allows us to assume
\min_{j\in[1,N]}b_{j}=0
.
Then, a soliton solution to the system takes the form

Let us turn to the study of the stability/instability of the ground states.
The notion of the stability of the ground state in this article is as follows:

Definition 1.10

(Orbital stability/instability)
.

Let
\omega>0
.
We say the family of the ground states
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}

is stable if
for any
\varepsilon>0

\delta>0
such that if
{\bf u}_{0}\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
satisfies

\inf_{\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}}\|{\bf u}_{0}-\Phi\|_{(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}}\leqslant\delta

then the corresponding

-solution

to (

(1.18)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\inf_{\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}}\|{\bf u}(t)-\Phi\|_{(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}}\leqslant\varepsilon.

We say the family of the ground states
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}

is unstable if it is not stable.

A new ingredient is a variant of rearrangement (See the proof of Lemma

It is inspired by a treatment of homogeneous functions in

We remark that a similar treatment is used in
[

,

]

We let
s_{p}:=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}
and

1.6
) reads as
s_{p}\in(0,\min(1,\frac{d}{2}))
or

Let us introduce few more functionals. Let

H({\bf u})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\lVert\nabla u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad G({\bf u})=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g({\bf u})dx.

Note that the energy functional is written as
E=H+G
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\omega>0
, let us introduce a functional

K_{\omega}({\bf u}):=2H({\bf u})+2\omega M({\bf u})+pG({\bf u}).

and a set

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}:=\{{\bf u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,K_{\omega}({\bf u})=0\}.

Note that the functional
K_{\omega}
has an alternative expression

K_{\omega}({\bf u})=\left.\frac{d}{da}S_{\omega}(a{\bf u})\right\rvert_{a=1}.

Hence, if
{\bf u}
is a critical point of
S_{\omega}
then
K_{\omega}({\bf u})=0
.
This shows

(2.1)

\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\omega}.

If
g_{\min}\geqslant 0
then

holds and so
K_{\omega}({\bf u})>0
for
{\bf u}\neq 0
.
This implies
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=\mathcal{A}_{\omega}=\emptyset
for all
\omega>0
.
Namely, (
gEω
) does no have any nontrivial

Let us assume

in what follows.
Let us observe the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
is not empty.

Proof.

Since
g_{\min}<0
, there exists
{\bf w}\in\partial B
such that

.
Hence, one has

G({\bf w}Q)=\frac{1}{p}g({\bf w})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(x)^{p}dx<0.

Note that

{K}_{\omega}(c{\bf w}Q)=c^{2}(\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\omega\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+c^{p}pG({\bf w}Q)
.
As
p>2
, there exists

such that
{K}_{\omega}(c{\bf w}Q)=0
.
∎

We consider the following minimization problem:

(2.2)

\mathfrak{I}(\omega):=\inf\{S_{\omega}({\bf u})\ |\ {\bf u}\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\}.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
\omega>0
,
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The finiteness follows from Lemma
2.2
.
Pick
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
.
Let us show that
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
is positive.
Let
\rho(x)=(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|u_{j}|^{2})^{1/2}\geqslant 0
.
If
\rho(x)>0
then

Hence, using the fact that

, we obtain

-G({\bf u})\leqslant\tfrac{|g_{\min}|}{p}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}_{x}\ell^{2}_{j}}^{p}\lesssim_{N}M({\bf u})^{\frac{p}{2}(1-s_{p})}H({\bf u})^{\frac{p}{2}s_{p}}\lesssim(H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u}))^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Therefore, there exists a constant

such that

{\bf u}\in K_{\omega}
then one has

H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u})=-\tfrac{p}{2}G({\bf u})\leqslant C(H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u}))^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Thus,
S_{\omega}({\bf u})=(1-\frac{2}{p})(H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u}))\gtrsim 1
for any
{\bf u}\in K_{\omega}
.
We obtain the conclusion.
∎

There is another characterization of
\mathfrak{I}(\omega).

Lemma 2.4
.

It holds that

\mathfrak{I}(\omega)=(1-\tfrac{2}{p})\inf\{H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u})\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,K_{\omega}({\bf u})\leqslant 0\}

Proof.

Denote the right hand side

.
Pick a nonzero

.
Then, it holds that

S_{\omega}({\bf u})=(1-\tfrac{2}{p})(H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u}))\geqslant\tilde{\mathfrak{I}}(\omega).

Taking infimum with respect to such
{\bf u}
, we obtain
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)\geqslant\tilde{\mathfrak{I}}(\omega)
.
On the other hand, fix a nonzero
{\bf v}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
so that

K_{\omega}({\bf v})\leqslant 0
. Then, looking at the curve
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni c\mapsto K_{\omega}(c{\bf v})\in\mathbb{R}
, one sees that there exists
c_{0}\in(0,1]
such that

K_{\omega}(c_{0}{\bf v})=0
. It holds that

H({\bf v})+\omega M({\bf v})\geqslant H(c_{0}{\bf v})+\omega M(c_{0}{\bf v})=\tfrac{p}{p-2}S_{\omega}(c_{0}{\bf v})\geqslant\tfrac{p}{p-2}\mathfrak{I}(\omega).

Taking infimum with respect to such
{\bf v}
, we obtain
\tilde{\mathfrak{I}}(\omega)\geqslant\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
.
Thus, the equality holds.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
{\bf u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
satisfies
K_{\omega}({\bf u})<0
then

-\frac{p-2}{2}G({\bf u})>\mathfrak{I}(\omega).

Proof.

As in the previous lemma, consider the curve
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni c\mapsto K_{\omega}(c{\bf u})\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
K_{\omega}({\bf u})<0
then
there exists
c_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

K_{\omega}(c_{0}{\bf u})=0
.
Since
{\bf u}\neq 0
,

-G({\bf u})>-G(c_{0}{\bf u})=\tfrac{2}{p}(H(c_{0}{\bf u})+\omega M(c_{0}{\bf u}))\geqslant\tfrac{2}{p}(1-\tfrac{2}{p})^{-1}\mathfrak{I}(\omega)

follows from the preceding lemma.
∎

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
be the set of minimizers to
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
:

(2.3)

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}:=\{\Phi\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\ |\ S_{\omega}(\Phi)=\mathfrak{I}(\omega)\}.

We next show that a minimizer exist, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\neq\emptyset
.
There is a direct approach to the existence based on Theorem
1.3
and the rearrangement argument used in the proof of Lemma
2.8
, below. However, we prove this by a standard compactness argument since we use it in the proof of the stability result (Theorem
1.11
).

Lemma 2.6
.

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
is not empty. Namely, there exists a minimizer to
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
.

Proof.

One can choose a minimizing sequence
\{{\bf u}_{n}\}_{n}=\{(u_{1,n},u_{2,n},\dots,u_{N,n})\}_{n}\subset(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}

such that

K_{\omega}({\bf u}_{n})=0
and

S_{\omega}({\bf u}_{n})\in[\mathfrak{I}(\omega),\mathfrak{I}(\omega)+\tfrac{1}{n}].

Notice that
K_{\omega}({\bf u}_{n})=0
gives us

H({\bf u}_{n})+\omega M({\bf u}_{n})=\tfrac{p}{p-2}S_{\omega}({\bf u}_{n})\leqslant\tfrac{p}{p-2}(\mathfrak{I}(\omega)+\tfrac{1}{n})\leqslant\tfrac{p}{p-2}(\mathfrak{I}(\omega)+1).

Hence,
\{{\bf u}_{n}\}_{n}
is a bounded sequence in
(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
.

By extracting a subsequence if necessary, we suppose that the limits

M_{*}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}M({\bf u}_{n})
,
H_{*}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}H({\bf u}_{n})
, and
G_{*}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}G({\bf u}_{n})

exist. Then,

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}K_{\omega}({\bf u}_{n})=2H_{*}+2\omega M_{*}+pG_{*}

and

\mathfrak{I}(\omega)=H_{*}+\omega M_{*}+G_{*}=(1-\tfrac{2}{p})(H_{*}+\omega M_{*})=(1-\tfrac{p}{2})G_{*}>0

hold.

We apply the profile decomposition to
\{{\bf u}_{n}\}_{n}
with errors in
(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
.
Then, up to a subsequence, there exist
J_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}

and, for
1\leqslant j\leqslant J_{0}
,

\Phi_{j}=(\phi_{1,j},\phi_{2,j},\dots,\phi_{N,j})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
,
y_{n}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
{\bf r}_{n}^{J}=(r_{1,n}^{J},r_{2,n}^{J},\dots,r_{N,n}^{J})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
such that,
for each
J\leqslant J_{0}
,

u_{k,n}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\phi_{k,j}(\cdot-{y_{n}^{j}})+r_{k,n}^{J}\quad(k=1,2,\dots,N)

for
n\geqslant 1
. Further, for any
1\leqslant j_{1}<j_{2}\leqslant J_{0}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}|y_{n}^{j_{1}}-y_{n}^{j_{2}}|=\infty.

One has

(r_{1,n}^{J},r_{2,n}^{J},\dots,r_{N,n}^{J})(\cdot+{y_{n}^{j}})\rightharpoonup(0,0,\dots,0)\quad\text{in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}

as
n\to\infty
for any
j\leqslant J
and

\lim_{J\to J_{0}}\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|r_{k,n}^{J}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

We have the decoupling inequalities

M_{*}\geqslant\sum_{j=1}^{J_{0}}M(\Phi_{j})\quad\text{and}\quad H_{*}\geqslant\sum_{j=1}^{J_{0}}H(\Phi_{j}).

Further,

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}M({\bf r}_{n}^{J})\leqslant M_{*},\quad\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}H({\bf r}_{n}^{J})\leqslant H_{*}

for all
J\geqslant 1
.
Let us claim

(2.4)

G_{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{J_{0}}G(\Phi_{j}).

Indeed, for each fixed
J\leqslant J_{0}
finite, one sees from (
1.8
) that

\left|G({\bf u}_{n})-G\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Phi_{j}(\cdot-{y_{n}^{j}})\right)\right|=\left|-\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\theta}G({\bf u}_{n}-\theta{\bf r}_{n}^{J})d\theta\right|\lesssim(\|{\bf u}_{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\|{\bf r}_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{p}})^{p-1}\|{\bf r}_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{p}}.

Notice that the implicit constant is independent of
J
.
Further, by the mutual orthogonality of
\{y_{n}^{j}\}_{n}
, one finds

\lim_{n\to\infty}G\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Phi_{j}(\cdot-{y_{n}^{j}})\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}G(\Phi_{j}).

This is justified, for instance, by approximating each
\phi_{k,j}
by functions with a compact support in the
(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
-topology.
Hence, combining these two estimates and using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, one obtains

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\left|G({\bf u}_{n})-\sum_{j=1}^{J}G(\Phi_{j})\right|\lesssim(M_{*}+H_{*})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\|{\bf r}_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{p}}\to 0

as
J\to J_{0}
.
This shows the claim.

If
J_{0}=0
then
G_{*}=0
follows from (
2.4
). Hence,
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)=0
. This contradicts with
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)>0
.

If
J_{0}\geqslant 2
then
0<H(\Phi_{j})+\omega M(\Phi_{j})<H_{*}+\omega M_{*}=\frac{p}{p-2}\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
for all
j\in[1,J_{0}]
.
Hence, by means of Lemma
2.4
, we have

K_{\omega}(\Phi_{j})>0
for all
j\in[1,J_{0}]
.
Then,

0=2H_{*}+2\omega M_{*}+pG_{*}\geqslant 2\sum_{j=1}^{J_{0}}H(\Phi_{j})+2\omega\sum_{j=1}^{J_{0}}M(\Phi_{j})+p\sum_{j=1}^{J_{0}}G(\Phi_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{J_{0}}K_{\omega}(\Phi_{j})>0,

which is a contradiction.

Thus, we have
J_{0}=1
. Then,
in one hand, we have
H(\Phi_{1})+\omega M(\Phi_{1})\leqslant H_{*}+\omega M_{*}
.
On the other hand, since

0=2H_{*}+2\omega M_{*}+pG_{*}\geqslant K_{\omega}(\Phi_{1})
,
we see from Lemma
2.4
that

H_{*}+\omega M_{*}=\frac{p}{p-2}\mathfrak{I}(\omega)\leqslant H(\Phi_{1})+\omega M(\Phi_{1})
.
Thus,
H(\Phi_{1})+\omega M(\Phi_{1})=H_{*}+\omega M_{*}
.
This also shows
K_{\omega}(\Phi_{1})=2H_{*}+2\omega M_{*}+pG_{*}=0

and
S_{\omega}(\Phi_{1})=H_{*}+\omega M_{*}+G_{*}=\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
.
Thus,
\Phi_{1}
is a minimizer to
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}=\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
. In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\subset(C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap(\cap_{2\leqslant q<\infty}W^{3,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))^{N}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
.
Since
\Phi\in\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
minimizes
S_{\omega}
under the constraint
K_{\omega}=0
, one sees from
Lagrange’s multiplier theorem that there exists
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that

S_{\omega}^{\prime}(\Phi)+\mu K^{\prime}_{\omega}(\Phi)=0
.
Then,

0=K_{\omega}(\Phi)=\left.\frac{d}{da}S_{\omega}(a\Phi)\right\rvert_{a=1}=(S^{\prime}_{\omega}(\Phi),\Phi)_{(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}}=-\mu(K^{\prime}_{\omega}(\Phi),\Phi)_{(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}}.

Since

\displaystyle(K^{\prime}_{\omega}(\Phi),\Phi)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}}={}

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{da}K_{\omega}(a{\bf u})\right\rvert_{a=1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 4H(\Phi)+4\omega M(\Phi)+p^{2}G(\Phi)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2(2-p)(H(\Phi)+\omega M(\Phi))>0,

we see that
\mu=0
. Hence,
S^{\prime}(\Phi)=0
. This implies
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
.
Further, recalling (
2.1
), one obtains
S_{\omega}(\Phi)=\mathfrak{I}(\omega)\leqslant S_{\omega}(\Psi)

for all
\Psi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
.
This implies that
\Psi\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
.
Thus
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
.

We remark that the above argument shows the identity

\inf_{\Psi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}}S_{\omega}(\Psi)=\mathfrak{I}(\omega).

This immediately shows the other relation
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
.
Indeed, any
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
satisfies

S_{\omega}(\Phi)=\inf_{\Psi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}}S_{\omega}(\Psi)=\mathfrak{I}(\omega)

and
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
.

The regularity property
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\subset(C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap(\cap_{2\leqslant q<\infty}W^{3,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))^{N}

follows by a standard argument (See
[
6
]
*Theorem 8.1.1, for instance).
∎

The following lemma completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.8
.

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}=\bigcup_{{\bf w}\in T_{0}}\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,-g_{\min})
.
Further,

\mathfrak{I}(\omega)=\tfrac{1}{2(1-s_{c})}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(-g_{\min})^{s_{c}-\frac{d}{2}}}\omega^{1-s_{c}}
.

The proof is essentially the same as in
[
9
]
*Theorem 1.
Here we reorganize the argument.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1
.
We shall prove
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\subset\bigcup_{{\bf w}\in T_{0}}\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,-g_{\min})
in the first two steps.
Fix
\omega>0
.
Pick
{\bf u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})\in\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
.
Let us first establish

(2.5)

\operatorname{Im}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{u_{j}}\nabla u_{j}=0,\quad u_{j_{1}}\nabla u_{j_{2}}-u_{j_{2}}\nabla u_{j_{1}}=0

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all
j_{1},j_{2}\in[1,N]
.
Pick
{\bf w}=(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{N})\in T_{0}
and
define
\tilde{\bf u}=(\tilde{u}_{1},\tilde{u}_{2},\dots,\tilde{u}_{N})\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
by

\tilde{u}_{j}(x)=w_{j}\rho(x)\quad(j=1,2,\dots,N),\quad\rho(x)=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|u_{j}(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Then, one has

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\tilde{u}_{j}(x)|^{2}=\rho(x)^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|u_{j}(x)|^{2}.

This shows
M(\tilde{\bf u})=M({\bf u})
.
Further, thanks to the homogeneity of
g
, one has for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\rho(x)>0
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle g({\bf u}(x))=\rho(x)^{p}g\left(\frac{{\bf u}(x)}{\rho(x)}\right)\geqslant{}\rho(x)^{p}g_{\min}={}\rho(x)^{p}g({\bf w})=g(\tilde{u}(x)),

where we have used the fact that
{\bf u}/\rho\in\partial B
to obtain the inequality.
Hence,
G({\bf u})\geqslant G(\tilde{\bf u})
.
Moreover, one has

\displaystyle|\nabla\rho|={}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\rho}\left|\operatorname{Re}{\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}}\overline{u_{j}}\nabla u_{j}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\rho}\left|{\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}}\overline{u_{j}}\nabla u_{j}\right|=\sqrt{{\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}-{(2\rho^{2})}^{-1}{\textstyle\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}}|u_{j_{1}}\nabla u_{j_{2}}-u_{j_{2}}\nabla u_{j_{1}}|^{2}}.

Hence, one sees that
H(\tilde{\bf u})\leqslant H({\bf u})
(See, e.g.
[
17
]
*Theorem 7.8, for rigorous treatment).
In view of the above inequality,
H(\tilde{\bf u})=H({\bf u})
gives us the desired conclusion (
2.5
).

Combining the above estimates, we have
K_{\omega}(\tilde{\bf u})\leqslant K_{\omega}({\bf u})=0
and hence

\tfrac{p-2}{p}\mathfrak{I}(\omega)\leqslant H(\tilde{\bf u})+\omega M(\tilde{\bf u})\leqslant H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u})=\tfrac{p-2}{p}\mathfrak{I}(\omega)

by definition of Lemma
2.4
.
Together with
M(\tilde{\bf u})=M({\bf u})
, one obtains

H(\tilde{\bf u})=H({\bf u})
as desired.

Moreover, this implies
\tilde{\bf u}\in\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
and hence
K_{\omega}(\tilde{\bf u})=0
.
Therefore,
G({\bf u})=G(\tilde{\bf u})
.
Hence, by the virtue of (
2.6
), we obtain

(2.7)

\tfrac{\bf u(x)}{\rho(x)}\in T_{0}

as long as
\rho(x)>0
.

Step 2.
We next show that
{\bf u}\in\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,-g_{\min})

for some
{\bf w}\in T_{0}
.
For
j\in[1,N]
,
let
\Gamma_{j}
be the set of all connected components of
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ |\ u_{j}(x)\neq 0\}
.
Note that
\Gamma_{j}
is well-defined since
u_{j}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Note that
\cup_{j=1}^{N}\Gamma_{j}
is not empty since
{\bf u}\neq 0
.

Pick
j_{0}\in[1,N]
such that
\Gamma_{j_{0}}\neq\emptyset
.
For simplicity, we assume
j_{0}=1
.
Pick
\Omega\in\Gamma_{1}
.
Thanks to the latter identity of (
2.5
), for each
j\in[2,N]
, there exists a constant
\Lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
u_{j}=\Lambda_{j}u_{1}
on
\Omega
(See the proof of Theorem 7.8 of
[
17
]
, for instance).
Substituting this relation to the former identity of (
2.5
), we obtain
\operatorname{Im}(\overline{u_{1}}\nabla u_{1})=0
on
\Omega
, which implies that there exist
a\in\mathbb{R}
and a nonnegative function
\phi(x)
such that

u_{1}=e^{ia}\phi
on
\Omega
. Hence, we obtain

(2.8)

u_{j}=w_{0,j}r_{0}\phi

for
j\in[1,N]
, where
r_{0}=(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Lambda_{j}|^{2})^{1/2}
and

w_{0,j}:=e^{ia}r_{0}^{-1}\Lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with the convention
\Lambda_{1}=1
.
Further, we denote
{\bf w}_{0}=(w_{0,1},w_{0,2},\dots,w_{0,N})\in\partial B
.
By (
2.7
),
{\bf w}_{0}\in T_{0}
.
By means of (
1.17
), we have

F_{1}({\bf u})=\phi^{p-1}r_{0}^{p-1}F_{1}({\bf w}_{0})

on
\Omega
. Since
{\bf u}\in A_{\omega}
, we have
-\Delta u_{1}+\omega u_{1}=-F_{1}({\bf u})
. Substituting the above formula, we obtain

-\Delta\phi+\omega\phi=\lambda\phi^{p-1},\quad\lambda:=-e^{-ia}r_{0}^{p-1}F_{1}({\bf w}_{0})

on
\Omega
. Since
\phi
is positive on
\Omega
,
one sees from the equation that
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\psi
be the zero extension of
\phi|_{\Omega}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since
\phi
is positive on
\Omega
and vanishes on the boundary of
\Omega
,

\psi
satisfies
-\Delta\psi+\omega\psi=\lambda\psi^{p-1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hence,
\psi=\lambda(-\Delta+\omega)^{-1}\psi^{p-1}
holds.
Since
\psi\neq 0
, one has
\lambda\neq 0
.
Further,
since the Bessel potential, the integral kernel of
(-\Delta+\omega)^{-1}
, is positive everywhere but the origin and since
\psi\geqslant 0
, we see that
(-\Delta+\omega)^{-1}\psi^{p-1}
is positive everywhere.
This shows
\lambda>0
and
\psi
is also positive everywhere.
By definition of
\psi
, this implies that
\phi
is also positive on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Thus the expression (
2.8
) is valid on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since
\phi
is a positive solution to

-\Delta\phi+\omega\phi=\lambda\phi^{p-1}
(
\lambda>0
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
if
d\geqslant 2
then
it is radially symmetric up to space translation (see
[
11
]
) and hence

\phi=Q_{\omega,\lambda}(\cdot-y)
for some
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
16
]
).
This shows

(2.9)

u_{j}(x)=w_{0,j}r_{0}Q_{\omega,\lambda}(x-y)=w_{0,j}Q_{\omega,r_{0}^{-2}\lambda}(x-y)

Hence, we conclude that

{\bf u}\in\mathcal{R}({\bf w}_{0},\omega,r_{0}^{-2}\lambda)\subset\bigcup_{{\bf w}\in T_{0}}\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,r_{0}^{-2}\lambda).

To complete the proof of this step, let us prove that
r_{0}^{-2}\lambda=-g_{\min}
.
In one hand, by using
{\bf w}_{0}\in\partial B
and
the fact that
Q
satisfies
\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{s_{p}}{1-s_{p}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
,
one has

2(H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u}))=\tfrac{1}{(1-s_{p})(r_{0}^{-2}\lambda)^{2/(p-2)}}\omega^{1-s_{c}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

On the other hand, noting that
{\bf w}_{0}\in T_{0}
and

Hence, we obtain the desired identity
from
K_{\omega}({\bf u})=0
.

Step 3
. We complete the proof.
As
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\subset\bigcup_{{\bf w}\in T_{0}}\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,-g_{\min})
,
we see that

\mathfrak{I}(\omega)=S_{\omega}({\bf u})=(1-\tfrac{2}{p})(H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u}))=\tfrac{1}{2(1-s_{c})}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(-g_{\min})^{s_{c}-\frac{d}{2}}\omega^{1-s_{c}}

by substituting one element in
{\bf u}\in\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
.
One then sees that any element in
\bigcup_{{\bf w}\in T_{0}}\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,-g_{\min})

gives the same value of
S_{\omega}
and belongs to
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}

and hence that any element in
\bigcup_{{\bf w}\in T_{0}}\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,-g_{\min})

is a minimizer to
\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
.
Thus, we obtain the opposite inclusion relation

\bigcup_{{\bf w}\in T_{0}}\mathcal{R}({\bf w},\omega,-g_{\min})\subset\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
.
∎

We finish this section with the proof of the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg-type inequality.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

The inequality is obvious when
{\bf u}=0
. Hence, we let
{\bf u}\neq 0
.
It suffices to prove that the best constant is attained by elements in
\mathcal{G}
.
Let us define

\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{GN}}:=\sup\left\{\tfrac{-G({\bf u})}{M({\bf u})^{\frac{p(1-s_{p})}{2}}H({\bf u})^{\frac{ps_{p}}{2}}}\ \middle|\ {\bf u}\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

We will show that
\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{GN}}=C_{\mathrm{GN}}
.

Let us begin with the proof of

\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{GN}}\leqslant C_{\mathrm{GN}}
.
Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, one sees that
\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{GN}}
is finite.
Further, mimicking the proof of Lemma
2.2
, one finds that
\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{GN}}>0
.
Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a nonzero
{\bf v}\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
such that

0<\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{GN}}-\varepsilon\leqslant\tfrac{-G({\bf v})}{M({\bf v})^{\frac{p(1-s_{p})}{2}}H({\bf v})^{\frac{ps_{p}}{2}}}=\tfrac{-G({\bf v})}{M({\bf v})^{\frac{2(1-s_{c})}{d-2s_{c}}}H({\bf v})^{\frac{d}{d-2s_{c}}}}.

Now, we take
c_{0}>0
so that
H(c_{0}{\bf v})=-\frac{d}{d-2s_{c}}G(c_{0}{\bf v})
. This is possible because the left hand side is of the form
ac_{0}^{2}
and the right hand side is of the form
bc_{0}^{p}
for some constants
a,b>0
.
We further let
\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda}=c_{0}\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}-s_{c}}{\bf v}(\lambda\cdot)
with
\lambda>0
to be chosen later.
It follows that

M(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=\lambda^{-2s_{c}}M(c_{0}{\bf v}),\quad H(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=\lambda^{2(1-s_{c})}H(c_{0}{\bf v}),\quad G(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=\lambda^{2(1-s_{c})}G(c_{0}{\bf v}).

These imply that
H(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=-\frac{d}{d-2s_{c}}G(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})
for any
\lambda>0
.
Further, since

K_{1}(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=2\lambda^{-2s_{c}}(-\tfrac{2(1-s_{c})}{d}\lambda^{2}H(c_{0}{\bf v})+M(c_{0}{\bf v})),

there exists unique
\lambda>0
such that
K_{1}(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=0
.
We fix this
\lambda
.
Then,
H(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=-\frac{d}{d-2s_{c}}G(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})
and
K_{1}(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=0
give us

S_{1}(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=\tfrac{2}{d}H(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=\tfrac{1}{1-s_{c}}M(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})=-\tfrac{2}{d-2s_{c}}G(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda}).

Further, one has
S_{1}(\tilde{\bf v}_{\lambda})\geqslant\mathfrak{I}(1)
by definition of
\mathfrak{I}(1)
.
Using these relations, one has

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{GN}}-\varepsilon\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\tfrac{-G({\bf v})}{M({\bf v})^{\frac{2(1-s_{c})}{d-2s_{c}}}H({\bf v})^{\frac{d}{d-2s_{c}}}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\tfrac{-G(c_{0}{\bf v})}{M(c_{0}{\bf v})^{\frac{2(1-s_{c})}{d-2s_{c}}}H(c_{0}{\bf v})^{\frac{d}{d-2s_{c}}}}

5.
The Potential-well structure in the mass-supercritical case

In this section, we establish another variational characterization of the ground states in the mass-supercritical
case
p>2+4/d
.
The characterization is useful in the proof of the instability.
We do not need Assumption
2
nor any restriction on
d
and
p
in this section.
Recall that
s_{p}:=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}
and
s_{c}:=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p-2}
. Note that
p>2+4/d
corresponds to
s_{c}>0
.

We introduce a functional

V({\bf u})=2H({\bf u})+\tfrac{2d}{d-2s_{c}}G({\bf u}),

which is the
L^{2}
-scaling derivative of
E({\bf u})
or of
S_{\omega}({\bf u})
.
Further, we introduce

\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\omega):=\inf\{\tfrac{2s_{c}}{d}H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u})\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,V({\bf u})\leqslant 0\}

for
\omega>0

and

\mathfrak{I}_{3}:=\inf\{H({\bf u})M({\bf u})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,V({\bf u})\leqslant 0\}.

We remark that if
{\bf u}\neq 0
satisfies
G({\bf u})<0
then
V(c{\bf u})<0
for large
c>0
.
Further, the assumption
p>2+\frac{4}{d}
assures
s_{c}>0
.
Hence,
\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\omega)
and
\mathfrak{I}_{3}
are well-defined and are positive and finite.

The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 5.1

(Potential well structure)
.

Suppose that
p>2+\frac{4}{d}
.
Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}
.
For
\delta\in(0,1)
there exists
\tilde{\delta}=\tilde{\delta}(d,p,\delta)>0
such that
if
{\bf u}\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{N}
is nonzero and satisfies

E({\bf u})M({\bf u})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}\leqslant(1-\delta)E(\Phi)M(\Phi)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}

then the followings are true:

(1)

V({\bf u})\neq 0
;

(2)

if
V({\bf u})>0
then

H({\bf u})M({\bf u})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}<\mathfrak{I}_{3}
and
V({\bf u})\geqslant\tilde{\delta}H({\bf u})
;

(3)

if
V({\bf u})<0
then

H({\bf u})M({\bf u})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}>\mathfrak{I}_{3}
and

V({\bf u})M({\bf u})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}\leqslant-\tilde{\delta}\mathfrak{I}_{3}.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}
.
A computation shows that

E(\Phi)M(\Phi)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}=\tfrac{s_{c}}{1-s_{c}}(\tfrac{1}{2}(-g_{\min})^{s_{c}-\frac{d}{2}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{s_{c}}}.

In particular, this quantity is independent of the choice of
\Phi
.
Further, it will turn out that

\mathfrak{I}_{3}=H(\Phi)M(\Phi)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}=\tfrac{d}{2(1-s_{c})}(\tfrac{1}{2}(-g_{\min})^{s_{c}-\frac{d}{2}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{s_{c}}}.

To prove the theorem, let us begin with the study of
\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\omega)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
\omega>0
,
\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\omega)=\mathfrak{I}(\omega)
. Further, the minimizer to
\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\omega)

Proof.

Let us first claim that

\mathfrak{I}_{2}(\omega)=\inf\{S_{\omega}({\bf u})\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,V({\bf u})=0\}.

\tfrac{2s_{c}}{d}H({\bf u})+\omega M({\bf u})=S_{\omega}({\bf u})-\frac{d-2s_{c}}{2d}V({\bf u})

\displaystyle\inf\{S_{\omega}({\bf u})-\tfrac{d-2s_{c}}{2d}V({\bf u})\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,V({\bf u})=0\}=\inf\{S_{\omega}({\bf u})\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,V({\bf u})=0\}.

Taking the infimum with respect to
{\bf u}
, we obtain the desired inequality.

We next claim

\inf\{S_{\omega}({\bf u})\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,V({\bf u})=0\}=\inf\{S_{\omega}({\bf u})\ |\ {\bf u}\neq 0,\,V({\bf u})=0,\,H({\bf u})=\tfrac{d}{2(1-s_{c})}\omega M({\bf u})\}.

{\bf u}\neq 0
such that

Taking the infimum with respect to
{\bf u}
, we obtain the desired inequality.

Lemma 5.4

By substituting an element

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We first remark that

Fix

V({\bf u}_{0})\leqslant-\tilde{\delta}\mathfrak{I}_{3}M({\bf u}_{0})^{-\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}

{\bf u}(t)

gNLS

Due to a direct computation.

Gevrey regularity for the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation with localized structural damping
Matteo Caggio and Filippo Dell’Oro

Abstract
. We study a Euler-Bernoulli beam equation with localized discontinuous structural damping. As our main result, we prove that the associated C 0 C_{0} -semigroup ( S ⁡ ( t ) ) t ≥ 0 (S(t))_{t\geq 0} is of Gevrey class δ > 24 \delta>24 for t > 0 t>0 , hence immediately differentiable. Moreover, we show that ( S ⁡ ( t ) ) t ≥ 0 (S(t))_{t\geq 0} is exponentially stable.

1.
Introduction

We analyze the regularity and the stability properties of the
C_{0}
-semigroup associated to
a Euler-Bernoulli beam equation with localized discontinuous structural damping.
Denoting by
\ell>0
the length of the beam and fixing
0<\ell_{0}<\ell
,
the corresponding model reads

(1.1)

u_{tt}(x,t)+u_{xxxx}(x,t)-(a(x)u_{tx}(x,t))_{x}=0,

where
x\in(0,\ell_{0})\cup(\ell_{0},\ell)
and
t>0
.
The unknown
u(x,t)
represents the vertical deflection of the beam
with respect to its reference configuration, while
a(x)
is the damping function.
We assume that
a(x)
has the form

a(x)=\begin{cases}0\quad\,\,\,{\rm if}\,\,\,x\in(0,\ell_{0}),\\
1\quad\,\,\,{\rm if}\,\,\,x\in(\ell_{0},\ell),\end{cases}

meaning that the dissipation is localized on the interval

(\ell_{0},\ell)
.
We also prescribe the following transmission conditions at the interface

(1.2)

\begin{cases}u(\ell_{0}^{-},t)=u(\ell_{0}^{+},t),\qquad\quad\,\,\,\,u_{x}(\ell_{0}^{-},t)=u_{x}(\ell_{0}^{+},t),\\
u_{xx}(\ell_{0}^{-},t)=u_{xx}(\ell_{0}^{+},t),\qquad u_{xxx}(\ell_{0}^{-},t)=u_{xxx}(\ell_{0}^{+},t)-u_{tx}(\ell_{0}^{+},t).\end{cases}

Finally, we impose the clamped boundary conditions

(1.3)

u(0,t)=u_{x}(0,t)=u(\ell,t)=u_{x}(\ell,t)=0,

and the initial conditions

(1.4)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\qquad u_{t}(x,0)=U_{0}(x),

where
u_{0},U_{0}
are assigned data.

The analysis of solutions to one-dimensional and multi-dimensional Euler-Bernoulli
equations with different damping mechanisms is a classical topic in PDEs.
The reader may consult for instance
[
5
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
and many references therein for
an overview of the relevant literature. The specific problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) treated here
has been previously studied in
[
8
]
, actually in a more general higher-dimensional version,
to which we refer for detailed physical motivations.
In that paper, it was shown that the associated contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup is exponentially stable
provided that the damping function is supported near the whole boundary of the spatial domain.
The methodology of
[
8
]
consists in proving that the resolvent of the semigroup generator
along the imaginary axis is bounded, which allows to apply the classical
Gearhart-Prüss theorem
[
10
,
19
]
and deduce the exponential
stability. The case when the damping functions is supported near an arbitrary small portion of the boundary
has been recently analyzed in
[
2
]
under different boundary conditions
(hinged rather than clamped). By means of suitable Carleman estimates, the authors of
[
2
]

proved that the resolvent of the semigroup generator
along the imaginary axis grows at most at an exponential rate. Combined
with a well-known result of Burq
[
6
]
, this leads to a logarithmic (semiuniform) decay of the semigroup.
Other works related to Euler-Bernoulli
equations with locally distributed structural damping include
[
4
,
22
]
.

The aim of present paper is to analyze both the stability and the
regularity properties of the contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup

(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
associated to (
1.1
)-(
1.4
). As remarked in
[
17
]
,
such a semigroup is not analytic. Still, we show here that
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}

is of Gevrey class
\delta>24
for
t>0
. In particular,
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
turns out to be immediately differentiable,
which implies an instantaneous smoothing effect on the initial data. Moreover, the spectrum determined growth condition
(otherwise called the linear stability property) is satisfied, meaning that the growth bound of the semigroup
coincides with the spectral bound of its generator.
Finally, we prove that
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is exponentially stable.
Note that in our case the damping function
a(x)
vanishes on
(0,\ell_{0})
, and thus
it is not supported near the whole boundary of the spatial domain
(0,\ell)
. For this reason,
the exponential stability of
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
cannot be deduced directly from the results of
[
8
]
.
The method used here to prove the aforementioned Gevrey regularity of

(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
consists in showing that the resolvent of the semigroup generator
along the imaginary axis decays at a proper polynomial rate, which permits
to reach the desired conclusion by invoking an abstract result of Taylor
[
21
]
.

In order to gain a better understanding of our results, we shall compare them with
the analogous ones obtained for the case of the Kelvin-Voigt damping, namely, when the damping
term and the leading elastic term have the same spatial order (four).
As shown in
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
, the
C_{0}
-semigroup associated to
the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation with localized discontinuous Kelvin-Voigt damping is exponentially stable but not
analytic. Still, it has been recently proved in
[
20
]
that such a semigroup is of Gevrey class
\delta>8
.
The difference between the two Gevrey orders of regularity (order
8
for the Kelvin-Voigt damping and order
24

for the structural damping) reflects the fact
that the structural damping is weaker than the Kelvin-Voigt one.
Indeed, the Gevrey order is a way to “measure” the divergence degree of the power series expansion of a non-analytic
function (the larger the order, the “more divergent” the power expansion).
From the technical viewpoint, the worse regularizing effect of the structural damping
represents the main difficulty with respect to the analysis
carried out in
[
17
,
20
]
.
More precisely, one needs a proper estimate of the solution
in the damped region of the spatial domain, which in our case
translates into an estimate of
u
in the Sobolev space
H^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell)
.
Such an estimate can be obtained basically for free for the Kelvin-Voigt damping,
but this is not the case for the structural damping treated here.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next Section
2
we rewrite the problem
as a coupled PDE system in two unknown variables
v
and
w
. In the subsequent
Section
3
we recast such a system as
an abstract Cauchy problem and we prove the existence of the contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup

(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
.
We also show that the spectrum of the semigroup generator is contained in the open
left half-plane
{\mathbb{C}}^{-}
, meaning in particular that the imaginary axis is spectrum free.
In Section
4
we state the main Theorem
4.4

concerning the Gevrey regularity of
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
, together with the corollaries
concerning the differentiability, the spectrum determined growth condition and the exponential stability.
The final Section
5
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Rewriting the problem

Splitting the unknown variable
u
as

u(x,t)=\begin{cases}v(x,t)\quad\,\,\,\,{\rm if}\,\,\,x\in(0,\ell_{0})\\
w(x,t)\quad\,\,\,{\rm if}\,\,\,x\in(\ell_{0},\ell),\end{cases}

we rewrite equation (
1.1
) as the coupled PDE system (in the sequel we shall write
v
and
w

instead of
v(x,t)
and
w(x,t)
to avoid cumbersome notation)

(2.1)

\begin{cases}v_{tt}+v_{xxxx}=0\qquad&{\rm in}\,\,\,(0,\ell_{0})\times(0,\infty),\\
w_{tt}+w_{xxxx}-w_{txx}=0\qquad&{\rm in}\,\,\,(\ell_{0},\ell)\times(0,\infty).\end{cases}

The transmission conditions (
1.2
) take the form

(2.2)

\begin{cases}v(\ell_{0},t)=w(\ell_{0},t),\qquad\quad\,\,\,\,v_{x}(\ell_{0},t)=w_{x}(\ell_{0},t),\\
v_{xx}(\ell_{0},t)=w_{xx}(\ell_{0},t),\qquad v_{xxx}(\ell_{0},t)=w_{xxx}(\ell_{0},t)-w_{tx}(\ell_{0},t),\end{cases}

while the boundary conditions (
1.3
) read

(2.3)

v(0,t)=v_{x}(0,t)=w(\ell,t)=w_{x}(\ell,t)=0.

Finally, the initial conditions (
1.4
) can be written in terms of
v
and
w
as

(2.4)

\begin{cases}v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\qquad\,\,\,v_{t}(x,0)=V_{0}(x),\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),\qquad w_{t}(x,0)=W_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where the functions
v_{0},V_{0},w_{0},W_{0}
are related to
u_{0}
and
U_{0}
in an obvious way.

2.2.
Functional setting

We consider the (complex) Hilbert spaces

\displaystyle H^{2}_{l}(0,\ell_{0})

\displaystyle=\{v\in H^{2}(0,\ell_{0}):v(0)=v_{x}(0)=0\},

\displaystyle H^{2}_{r}(\ell_{0},\ell)

\displaystyle=\{w\in H^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell):w(\ell)=w_{x}(\ell)=0\},

equipped with the norms

\|v\|_{H^{2}_{l}(0,\ell_{0})}=\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|w\|_{H^{2}_{r}(\ell_{0},\ell)}=\|w_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell)}.

Introducing the variable
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(v,V,w,W)
, the
state space associated to problem (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) is the (complex) Hilbert space

{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{{\boldsymbol{z}}\in H^{2}_{l}(0,\ell_{0})\times L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})\times H^{2}_{r}(\ell_{0},\ell)\times L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell)\,\,\Big|\,\,\begin{matrix}v(\ell_{0})=w(\ell_{0})\\
v_{x}(\ell_{0})=w_{x}(\ell_{0})\end{matrix}\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}=\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{2}+\|V\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{2}+\|w_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell)}^{2}+\|W\|_{L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell)}^{2}.

2.3.
Two technical tools

The following functional inequalities
(see e.g.
[
1
, Ch. 5]
) will be crucial for our purposes.

Lemma 2.1

(Interpolation)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval and let
0\leq j<k<m
.
Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\|f\|_{H^{k}(I)}\leq c\|f\|_{H^{j}(I)}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{H^{m}(I)}^{\theta}\qquad\forall f\in H^{m}(I),

where
\theta=(k-j)/(m-j)
.

Lemma 2.2

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded interval.
Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{1/2}\|f\|_{H^{1}(I)}^{1/2}\qquad\forall f\in H^{1}(I).

2.4.
General agreements

Throughout the paper, the Hölder and Poincaré inequalities
will be tacitly used several times.
Given
X,Y\geq 0
, we will also employ the Young inequality (often without explicit mention)

(2.5)

X^{\,2/p}\,Y^{\,2/q}\leq X^{2}+Y^{2}\qquad\text{where}\qquad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

Finally, we denote by
L({\mathcal{H}})
the space of bounded linear operators on
{\mathcal{H}}
.

3.
The Semigroup and Its Generator

Setting
{\boldsymbol{z}}(t)=(v(t),V(t),w(t),W(t))^{\rm T}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
we may rewrite problem (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) as

\begin{cases}{\boldsymbol{z}}^{\prime}(t)={\mathbb{A}}{\boldsymbol{z}}(t),\quad t>0,\\
{\boldsymbol{z}}(0)={\boldsymbol{z}}_{0},\end{cases}

where
{\boldsymbol{z}}_{0}=(v_{0},V_{0},w_{0},W_{0})^{\rm T}\in{\mathcal{H}}
and the
operator
{\mathbb{A}}:{{D}}({\mathbb{A}})\subset{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is defined as

{\mathbb{A}}\left(\begin{matrix}v\\
\vskip 2.84526pt\cr V\\
w\\
\vskip 3.69885pt\cr W\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}V\\
-v_{xxxx}\\
\vskip 4.2679pt\cr W\\
-w_{xxxx}+W_{xx}\end{matrix}\right)

with domain

{{D}}({\mathbb{A}})=\left\{{\boldsymbol{z}}\in{\mathcal{H}}\left|\,\,\begin{matrix}v\in H^{4}(0,\ell_{0})\\
V\in H^{2}_{l}(0,\ell_{0})\\
w\in H^{4}(\ell_{0},\ell)\\
W\in H^{2}_{r}(\ell_{0},\ell)\\
V(\ell_{0})=W(\ell_{0})\\
V_{x}(\ell_{0})=W_{x}(\ell_{0})\\
v_{xx}(\ell_{0})=w_{xx}(\ell_{0})\\
v_{xxx}(\ell_{0})=w_{xxx}(\ell_{0})-W_{x}(\ell_{0})\\
\end{matrix}\right.\right\}.

Denoting by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}
the inner product associated to
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}
,
a direct calculation yields

(3.1)

\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle{\mathbb{A}}{\boldsymbol{z}},{\boldsymbol{z}}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}}=-\|W_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell)}^{2}\leq 0,\quad\,\,\forall{\boldsymbol{z}}\in{{D}}({\mathbb{A}}).

In particular, this tells that
{\mathbb{A}}
is a dissipative operator. Further properties of
{\mathbb{A}}

are contained in the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

The operator
{\mathbb{A}}
is bijective and the inverse operator
{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}
is compact.

Proof.

First, we show that
{\mathbb{A}}
is bijective. To this end,
for every
\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}=(\hat{v},\hat{V},\hat{w},\hat{W})^{\rm T}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
we prove that the equation
{\mathbb{A}}{\boldsymbol{z}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}

has a unique solution
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(v,V,w,W)^{\rm T}\in D({\mathbb{A}})
.
Writing the equation in components, we obtain the system

(3.2)

\displaystyle V=\hat{v}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,H^{2}_{l}(0,\ell_{0}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle-v_{xxxx}=\hat{V}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,L^{2}(0,\ell_{0}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle W=\hat{w}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,H^{2}_{r}(\ell_{0},\ell),

(3.5)

\displaystyle-w_{xxxx}+W_{xx}=\hat{W}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell).

From (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) we immediately learn that

V=\hat{v}\in H^{2}_{l}(0,\ell_{0})W=\hat{w}\in H^{2}_{r}(\ell_{0},\ell).

Since
\hat{v}(\ell_{0})=\hat{w}(\ell_{0})
and
\hat{v}_{x}(\ell_{0})=\hat{w}_{x}(\ell_{0})
, we also have
the equalities

V(\ell_{0})=W(\ell_{0})
and
V_{x}(\ell_{0})=W_{x}(\ell_{0})
.
For
x\in(0,\ell_{0})
, the general solution of
(
3.3
) with the boundary conditions
v(0)=v_{x}(0)=0
is given by

v(x)=a_{1}x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+p(x),

where
a_{1},a_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and

p(x)=-\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{y}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{s}\hat{V}(\tau)\,d\tau dsdrdy.

Similarly, for
x\in(\ell_{0},\ell)
, the general solution of (
3.5
) with
W=\hat{w}
and
the boundary conditions
w(\ell)=w_{x}(\ell)=0
is given by

w(x)=a_{3}(x-\ell)^{3}+a_{4}(x-\ell)^{2}+q(x),

where
a_{3},a_{4}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and

q(x)=\int_{\ell}^{x}\int_{\ell}^{y}\int_{\ell}^{r}\int_{\ell}^{s}[\hat{w}_{xx}(\tau)-\hat{W}(\tau)]\,d\tau dsdrdy.

Note that
v\in H^{4}(0,\ell_{0})\cap H^{2}_{l}(0,\ell_{0})

and
w\in H^{4}(\ell_{0},\ell)\cap H^{2}_{r}(\ell_{0},\ell)
.
In addition, there exists exactly
one choice of the constants
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}
such that

(3.6)

\begin{cases}v(\ell_{0})=w(\ell_{0}),\\
v_{x}(\ell_{0})=w_{x}(\ell_{0}),\\
v_{xx}(\ell_{0})=w_{xx}(\ell_{0}),\\
v_{xxx}(\ell_{0})=w_{xxx}(\ell_{0})-W_{x}(\ell_{0}).\end{cases}

Indeed, introducing the matrix

{\bf M}=\begin{pmatrix}\ell_{0}^{3}&\ell_{0}^{2}&-(\ell_{0}-\ell)^{3}&-(\ell_{0}-\ell)^{2}\\[1.99997pt]
3\ell_{0}^{2}&2\ell_{0}&-3(\ell_{0}-\ell)^{2}&-2(\ell_{0}-\ell)\\[1.99997pt]
6\ell_{0}&2&-6(\ell_{0}-\ell)&-2\\[1.99997pt]
6&0&-6&0\end{pmatrix}

and the vectors

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})^{\rm T},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{b}=(q(\ell_{0})-p(\ell_{0}),q_{x}(\ell_{0})-p_{x}(\ell_{0}),q_{xx}(\ell_{0})-p_{xx}(\ell_{0}),q_{xxx}(\ell_{0})-p_{xxx}(\ell_{0})-\hat{w}_{x}(\ell_{0}))^{\rm T},

we rewrite (
3.6
) as

{\bf M}\hskip 0.28453pt\boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{b}
.
Direct calculations show that
{\rm Det}({\bf M})=12\ell^{4}\neq 0
, meaning that
there is exactly one solution
\boldsymbol{a}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
.
We conclude that
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(v,V,w,W)^{\rm T}\in D({\mathbb{A}})
is the desired unique solution to the equation
{\mathbb{A}}{\boldsymbol{z}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}
.

At this point, we multiply in
L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})
equation (
3.2
)
by
V
and equation (
3.3
)
by
v
. Similarly, we multiply in
L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell)
equation (
3.4
)
by
W
and equation (
3.5
)
by
w
. Adding the resulting identities and after a straightforward calculation, we find
the bound

\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq c\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}},

for some structural constant
c>0
.
The latter ensures that the inverse operator
{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}
is bounded, hence
{\mathbb{A}}
is closed.
Finally, it is not difficult to check that
D({\mathbb{A}})
is compactly embedded into
{\mathcal{H}}
, so that
the operator
{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}
is compact.
∎

In the light of Lemma
3.1
, we infer that

0
belongs to the resolvent set
\rho({\mathbb{A}})
of
{\mathbb{A}}
. Being
\rho({\mathbb{A}})
an open set,
this implies that the operator
\lambda-{\mathbb{A}}
is surjective
for
\lambda>0
small enough. In turn,

{\mathbb{A}}
is densely defined
[
9
, Corollary II.3.20]
and, due to
the Lumer-Phillips theorem, it generates of a contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup

(S(t))_{t\geq 0}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}.

We end the section by proving a spectral property of
{\mathbb{A}}
that will be crucial
in the sequel.

Lemma 3.2
.

The spectrum
\sigma({\mathbb{A}})
of
{\mathbb{A}}
is contained in the open left half-plane
{\mathbb{C}}^{-}
. In particular,
the imaginary axis
i\mathbb{R}
is spectrum free.

Proof.

Being
{\mathbb{A}}
the infinitesimal generator
of a contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup, it follows from the Hille-Yosida theorem that
\sigma({\mathbb{A}})
is
contained in the closed left half-plane. Since
we know from Lemma
3.1
that
{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}
is a compact operator,

\sigma({\mathbb{A}})
consists entirely of isolated eigenvalues (see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 6.29]
).
We are left to show that there are no purely imaginary eigenvalues of
{\mathbb{A}}
.
To this end, assume that
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(v,V,w,W)^{\rm T}\in D({\mathbb{A}})
satisfies

(3.7)

i\lambda{\boldsymbol{z}}-{\mathbb{A}}{\boldsymbol{z}}=0

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\neq 0

(recall that
0\notin\sigma({\mathbb{A}})
). Then, we get the system

(3.8)

\displaystyle i\lambda v-V=0,

(3.9)

\displaystyle i\lambda V+v_{xxxx}=0,

(3.10)

\displaystyle i\lambda w-W=0,

(3.11)

\displaystyle i\lambda W+w_{xxxx}-W_{xx}=0.

Taking the inner product in
{\mathcal{H}}
of (
3.7
) with
{\boldsymbol{z}}

and invoking (
3.1
), we see at once that
W_{x}=0
, yielding in turn
W=0
. Thus,
it follows from
(
3.10
)
that
w=0
as well. Next, substituting (
3.8
) into (
3.9
),
we infer that
v
fulfills

v_{xxxx}=\lambda^{2}v.

For
x\in(0,\ell_{0})
, the general solution of
the equation above with the boundary conditions
v(0)=v_{x}(0)=0
is given by

v(x)=a_{1}{\rm e}^{i|\lambda|^{1/2}x}+a_{2}{\rm e}^{-i|\lambda|^{1/2}x}+\alpha(a_{1},a_{2}){\rm e}^{|\lambda|^{1/2}x}+\beta(a_{1},a_{2}){\rm e}^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}x}

where
a_{1},a_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and

\alpha(a_{1},a_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}[a_{1}+a_{2}+i(a_{1}-a_{2})],\quad\,\,\,\beta(a_{1},a_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}[a_{1}+a_{2}-i(a_{1}-a_{2})].

Since
w(\ell_{0})=w_{x}(\ell_{0})=w_{xx}(\ell_{0})=w_{xxx}(\ell_{0})-W_{x}(\ell_{0})=0
,
and recalling that
{\boldsymbol{z}}\in D({\mathbb{A}})
,
the function
v
is subjected to the further constraints

v(\ell_{0})=v_{x}(\ell_{0})=v_{xx}(\ell_{0})=v_{xxx}(\ell_{0})=0
. It is straightforward
to check that these conditions
force
v=0
. Finally, equation
(
3.8
) ensures that
V=0
.
∎

4.
The Gevrey Class

In this section, we state the main result of the article concerning the Gevrey
class of the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
generated by the operator
{\mathbb{A}}
.
Let us recall the definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\delta>1
and
t_{0}\geq 0
be given. A
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}

is said to be of
Gevrey class
\delta
for
t>t_{0}
if

(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is infinitely differentiable for
t>t_{0}
and
for all compact
K\subset(t_{0},\infty)
and all
\theta>0

there is a constant
C=C(K,\theta)>0
such that

\|S^{(n)}(t)\|_{L({\mathcal{H}})}\leq C\theta^{n}(n!)^{\delta}

for all
t\in K
and
n=0,1,2,\ldots
. Here,

S^{(n)}(t)
denotes the
n
-th derivative of
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Recall that if
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is infinitely differentiable for
t>t_{0}

then
\|S^{(n)}(t)\|_{L({\mathcal{H}})}
is continuous
for all
t>t_{0}
and
n=0,1,2,\ldots
(see e.g.
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
).

Remark 4.3
.

The regularity properties of Gevrey semigroups are somewhat “between”
that of differentiable semigroups and analytic semigroups. Indeed,
the bounds on the derivatives of
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
are
stronger than the ones corresponding to differentiability
but weaker than the ones corresponding to
analyticity (see e.g.
[
7
,
18
,
21
]
).

Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 4.4
.

The
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}

is of Gevrey class
\delta>24
for
t>0
.

In particular,
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
turns out to be immediately differentiable.
As a consequence,
the spectrum determined growth condition is satisfied (see e.g.
[
9
, p. 281]
), namely,
the spectral bound of
{\mathbb{A}}

\sigma_{*}=\sup\{\mathfrak{Re\,}(\zeta):\zeta\in\sigma({\mathbb{A}})\}

coincides with the growth bound of
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}

\omega_{*}=\inf\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}:\,\,\|S(t)\|_{L({\mathcal{H}})}\leq Me^{\omega t}\,\text{ for some }M=M(\omega)\geq 1\}.

Moreover, being
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
immediately
differentiable (hence eventually norm-continuos), it follows from the inclusion

\sigma({\mathbb{A}})\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{-}
ensured by Lemma
3.2
that
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}

is exponentially stable, i.e.
\omega_{*}<0
(see e.g. the proof of
[
3
, Prop. 2.7]
).
We summarize this discussion in the next result.

Corollary 4.5
.

The
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is immediately differentiable
and the spectrum determined
growth condition is satisfied.
Moreover,
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is exponentially stable.

Proof of Theorem

The argument is based on the following well-known abstract criterion,

[

Lemma 5.1

(Taylor)
.

Assume that
\sigma({\mathbb{A}})\cap i\mathbb{R}=\emptyset
. If there exists

such that

\limsup_{|\lambda|\to\infty}|\lambda|^{\mu}\|(i\lambda-{\mathbb{A}})^{-1}\|_{L({\mathcal{H}})}<\infty,

then
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is of Gevrey class
\delta>1/\mu
for
t>0
.

Since Lemma
3.2
ensures that
\sigma({\mathbb{A}})\cap{{i}}\mathbb{R}=\emptyset
,
we only need to show the validity of condition (
5.1
) with
\mu=1/24
. To this end,
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and

\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}=(\hat{v},\hat{V},\hat{w},\hat{W})^{\rm T}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
we consider the resolvent equation

i\lambda{\boldsymbol{z}}-{\mathbb{A}}{\boldsymbol{z}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{z}},

and we denote by
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(v,V,w,W)^{\rm T}\in{{D}}({\mathbb{A}})
its unique solution.
Taking the inner product in
{\mathcal{H}}
with
{\boldsymbol{z}}

and exploiting (
3.1
), we immediately get

\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle i\lambda{\boldsymbol{z}}-{\mathbb{A}}{\boldsymbol{z}},{\boldsymbol{z}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\|W_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell)}^{2}=\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle\hat{\boldsymbol{z}},{\boldsymbol{z}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

The latter yields

Next, writing the resolvent equation componentwise, we obtain the system

\displaystyle i\lambda v-V=\hat{v}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,H^{2}_{l}(0,\ell_{0}),

(5.4)

\displaystyle i\lambda V+v_{xxxx}=\hat{V}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,L^{2}(0,\ell_{0}),

(5.5)

\displaystyle i\lambda w-W=\hat{w}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,H^{2}_{r}(\ell_{0},\ell),

(5.6)

\displaystyle i\lambda W+w_{xxxx}-W_{xx}=\hat{W}

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,L^{2}(\ell_{0},\ell).

In the sequel, we always denote by

a generic constant

of the problem (hence independent of
\lambda

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
|\lambda|
sufficiently large the inequality

|v_{xx}(0)|\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}|v_{xxx}(0)|+\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big]

holds for some structural constant
c>0
independent of
\lambda
.

Proof.

The argument follows very closely the one of
[
11
, Lemma 3.10]

(see also
[
17
,
20
]
).
Nevertheless, for the reader’s convenience, we report here the full proof.

Calling
\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=v_{xx}(x)-|\lambda|v(x)
and combining
(
5.3
) with (
5.4
), we easily find

\varphi_{\lambda xx}+|\lambda|\varphi_{\lambda}=i\lambda\hat{v}+\hat{V}.

For
x\in(0,\ell_{0})
and
\lambda\neq 0
, the solution of the equation above with the boundary conditions

\varphi_{\lambda}(0)=v_{xx}(0)
and
\varphi_{\lambda x}(0)=v_{xxx}(0)
can be written as

\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{2|\lambda|^{1/2}}\big[J_{1\lambda}(x)+J_{2\lambda}(x)+iJ_{3\lambda}(x)+iJ_{4\lambda}(x)\big]

having set

\displaystyle J_{1\lambda}(x)=\big[|\lambda|^{1/2}v_{xx}(0)-iv_{xxx}(0)\big]e^{i|\lambda|^{1/2}x},

\displaystyle J_{2\lambda}(x)=\big[|\lambda|^{1/2}v_{xx}(0)+iv_{xxx}(0)\big]e^{-i|\lambda|^{1/2}x},

\displaystyle J_{3\lambda}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}e^{i|\lambda|^{1/2}(s-x)}[i\lambda\hat{v}(s)+\hat{V}(s)]ds,

\displaystyle J_{4\lambda}(x)=-\int_{0}^{x}e^{-i|\lambda|^{1/2}(s-x)}[i\lambda\hat{v}(s)+\hat{V}(s)]ds.

Introducing the further variable
\psi_{\lambda}(x)=v_{x}(x)+|\lambda|^{1/2}v(x)
,
we infer that

\psi_{\lambda x}-|\lambda|^{1/2}\psi_{\lambda}=\varphi_{\lambda}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=e^{|\lambda|^{1/2}(x-\ell_{0})}\psi_{\lambda}(\ell_{0})-\int_{x}^{\ell_{0}}e^{|\lambda|^{1/2}(x-s)}\varphi_{\lambda}(s)ds

\displaystyle=e^{|\lambda|^{1/2}(x-\ell_{0})}\psi_{\lambda}(\ell_{0})-\int_{x}^{\ell_{0}}\frac{e^{|\lambda|^{1/2}(x-s)}}{2|\lambda|^{1/2}}\big[J_{1\lambda}(s)+J_{2\lambda}(s)+iJ_{3\lambda}(s)+iJ_{4\lambda}(s)\big]ds.

Choosing
x=0
in the expression above and noting that
\psi_{\lambda}(0)=0
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}\big[J_{1\lambda}(s)+J_{2\lambda}(s)\big]ds

\displaystyle=2|\lambda|^{1/2}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}\psi_{\lambda}(\ell_{0})-i\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}\big[J_{3\lambda}(s)+J_{4\lambda}(s)\big]ds.

At this point, a straightforward computation yields the identity

\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}\big[J_{1\lambda}(s)+J_{2\lambda}(s)\big]ds=K_{\lambda}+v_{xx}(0)+\frac{v_{xxx}(0)}{|\lambda|^{1/2}},

where

K_{\lambda}=\frac{J_{1\lambda}(0)e^{(i-1)|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}}{(i-1)|\lambda|^{1/2}}-\frac{J_{2\lambda}(0)e^{-(i+1)|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}}{(i+1)|\lambda|^{1/2}}.

As a consequence, we have the bound

\displaystyle|v_{xx}(0)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}|v_{xxx}(0)|+|K_{\lambda}|+2|\lambda|^{1/2}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}|\psi_{\lambda}(\ell_{0})|

\displaystyle\quad+\Big|\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}\big[J_{3\lambda}(s)+J_{4\lambda}(s)\big]ds\Big|.

For every
|\lambda|
sufficiently large, it is readily seen that

|K_{\lambda}|\leq ce^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}|v_{xx}(0)|+\frac{ce^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}|v_{xxx}(0)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|v_{xx}(0)|+\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}|v_{xxx}(0)|.

Moreover, invoking the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we also find

\displaystyle 2|\lambda|^{1/2}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}|\psi_{\lambda}(\ell_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}\big[\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\ell_{0})}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\ell_{0})}\big]

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}},

for every
|\lambda|
sufficiently large.
This leads to

\displaystyle|v_{xx}(0)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}|v_{xxx}(0)|+\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\Big|\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}\big[J_{3\lambda}(s)+J_{4\lambda}(s)\big]ds\Big|.

In order to finish the proof, we are left to show that

(5.15)

\Big|\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}\big[J_{3\lambda}(s)+J_{4\lambda}(s)\big]ds\Big|\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}

for every
|\lambda|
sufficiently large. To this end, integrating by parts with

\hat{v}(0)=\hat{v}_{x}(0)=0
, we compute

\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}J_{3\lambda}(s)ds=P_{1\lambda}+P_{2\lambda}+P_{3\lambda},

having set

\displaystyle P_{1\lambda}=-\frac{e^{-(i+1)|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}}{(i+1)|\lambda|^{1/2}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{i|\lambda|^{1/2}s}[i\lambda\hat{v}(s)+\hat{V}(s)]ds,

\displaystyle P_{2\lambda}=-\frac{i\lambda}{(i+1)|\lambda|}\big[e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}\hat{v}(\ell_{0})+\frac{e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}\hat{v}_{x}(\ell_{0})\big],

\displaystyle P_{3\lambda}=\frac{i\lambda}{(i+1)|\lambda|^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}\,\hat{v}_{xx}(s)ds+\frac{1}{(i+1)|\lambda|^{1/2}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}\,\hat{V}(s)ds.

Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and the Hölder inequality,
it is not difficult to check that for every
|\lambda|
sufficiently large

\displaystyle|P_{1\lambda}|\leq c|\lambda|^{1/2}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}},

\displaystyle|P_{2\lambda}|\leq ce^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}\big[\|\hat{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\ell_{0})}+\|\hat{v}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\ell_{0})}\big]\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}},

\displaystyle|P_{3\lambda}|\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\big[1-e^{-2|\lambda|^{1/2}\ell_{0}}\big]^{1/2}\big[\|\hat{v}_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}+\|\hat{V}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\big]\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Therefore, we arrive at

\Big|\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}J_{3\lambda}(s)ds\Big|\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

By the same token, we also have

\Big|\int_{0}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-|\lambda|^{1/2}s}J_{4\lambda}(s)ds\Big|\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/4}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}},

and (
5.15
) follows.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For every
|\lambda|\geq 1
the inequality

|v_{xxx}(0)|^{2}\leq c|\lambda|^{5/6}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big]

.

Proof.

Multiplying in

equation (

)

v_{xxx}
and taking the real part, we obtain

\mathfrak{Re\,}{{i}}\lambda\langle V,v_{xxx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}+\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle v_{xxxx},v_{xxx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}=\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle\hat{V},v_{xxx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}.

After an elementary calculation, and with the aid of (
5.3
), we find (the
horizontal bar stands for the complex conjugate)

\mathfrak{Re\,}{{i}}\lambda\langle V,v_{xxx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}=\mathfrak{Re\,}\big[{{i}}\lambda V(\ell_{0})\overline{v_{xx}(\ell_{0})}\big]+\frac{1}{2}|V_{x}(\ell_{0})|^{2}+\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle V_{x},\hat{v}_{xx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}.

In addition, it is readily seen that

\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle v_{xxxx},v_{xxx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}=\frac{1}{2}|v_{xxx}(\ell_{0})|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|v_{xxx}(0)|^{2}.

Therefore, we get the bound

\displaystyle|v_{xxx}(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c|v_{xxx}(\ell_{0})|^{2}+c|\lambda||V(\ell_{0})||v_{xx}(\ell_{0})|+c|V_{x}(\ell_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+c\|V_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|v_{xxx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}.

We now estimate for
|\lambda|\geq 1
the terms appearing in the right-hand side above.
First we observe that, since

v_{xxx}(\ell_{0})=w_{xxx}(\ell_{0})-W_{x}(\ell_{0})
, an exploitation (
5.10
) and (
5.12
) entails

|v_{xxx}(\ell_{0})|^{2}\leq c|\lambda|^{5/6}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big].

Recalling also that
V(\ell_{0})=W(\ell_{0})
and
v_{xx}(\ell_{0})=w_{xx}(\ell_{0})
, using (
5.9
) and (
5.11
)
we get

\displaystyle|\lambda||V(\ell_{0})||v_{xx}(\ell_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|^{3/4}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|^{3/4}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big].

Moreover, since
V_{x}(\ell_{0})=W_{x}(\ell_{0})
, in the light of (
5.10
)
we have

|V_{x}(\ell_{0})|^{2}\leq c|\lambda|^{2/3}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big].

Next, interpolating between
L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})
and

H^{2}(0,\ell_{0})
and owing to (
5.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|V_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|V\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{1/2}\|V_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{1/2}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|^{1/2}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{1/2}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{3/2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|^{1/2}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big].

Similarly, interpolating between
H^{2}(0,\ell_{0})
and

H^{4}(0,\ell_{0})
and using (
5.4
), we find

\displaystyle\|v_{xxx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+c\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{1/2}\|v_{xxxx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{1/2}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|^{1/2}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{1/2}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{3/2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|^{1/2}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big].

Collecting all the estimates above, and noting
that the biggest power is
|\lambda|^{5/6}
, we arrive at the desired conclusion.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

For every
|\lambda|
sufficiently large the inequality

\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{2}+\|V\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{2}\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/12}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\big]

holds for some structural constant
c>0
independent of
\lambda
.

Proof.

Setting
q(x)=x-\ell_{0}
for
x\in(0,\ell_{0})
, we
multiply in
L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})
equation (
5.4
)
by
qv_{x}
. Taking the real part of the resulting identity, we obtain

\mathfrak{Re\,}{{i}}\lambda\langle V,qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}+\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle v_{xxxx},qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}=\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle\hat{V},qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}.

Exploiting (
5.3
) and after an elementary calculation, we find

\mathfrak{Re\,}{{i}}\lambda\langle V,qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}=\frac{1}{2}\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}-\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle V,q\hat{v}_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}.

Moreover, we compute (as before the horizontal bar stands for the complex conjugate)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle v_{xxxx},qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}=-\mathfrak{Re\,}\big[v_{xx}(\ell_{0})\overline{v_{x}(\ell_{0})}\big]+\frac{3}{2}\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{2}-\frac{\ell_{0}}{2}|v_{xx}(0)|^{2}.

As a consequence, we find

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big\|V\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell_{0}}{2}|v_{xx}(0)|^{2}+\mathfrak{Re\,}\big[v_{xx}(\ell_{0})\overline{v_{x}(\ell_{0})}\big]+\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle\hat{V},qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}+\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle V,q\hat{v}_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}.

Collecting Lemmas
5.4
and
5.5
, it is readily seen that
for every
|\lambda|
sufficiently large

\frac{\ell_{0}}{2}|v_{xx}(0)|^{2}\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|}|v_{xxx}(0)|^{2}+\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{3/2}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big]\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/6}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big].

Recalling that

v_{xx}(\ell_{0})=w_{xx}(\ell_{0})
, and exploiting
the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality together with (
5.11
), we also obtain

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{Re\,}\big[v_{xx}(\ell_{0})\overline{v_{x}(\ell_{0})}\big]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/12}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\big]\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\ell_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/12}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\big]\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/12}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\big].

Therefore, we arrive at the bound

(5.16)

\displaystyle\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{2}+\|V\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/12}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\big]+|\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle\hat{V},qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}|+|\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle V,q\hat{v}_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}|.

Next, in the light of (
5.3
), we estimate

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle\hat{V},qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|}\big[\|V_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}+\|\hat{v}_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\big]\|\hat{V}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|}\|V_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\frac{c}{|\lambda|}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}.

Interpolating between
L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})
and

H^{2}(0,\ell_{0})
and invoking again (
5.3
), we also have

\|V_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\leq c\|V\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{1/2}\|V_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{1/2}\leq c|\lambda|^{1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+c\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{1/2}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{1/2}.

In conclusion, we find

(5.17)

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle\hat{V},qv_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{1/2}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{3/2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big].

At this point, owing to (
5.4
), we compute

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle V,q\hat{v}_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda|}|\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle v_{xxxx},q\hat{v}_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\big|+\frac{c}{|\lambda|}\|\hat{V}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{v}_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\lambda|}|\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle v_{xxx},\hat{v}_{x}+q\hat{v}_{xx}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}|+\frac{c}{|\lambda|}\|\hat{V}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{v}_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|}\|v_{xxx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{c}{|\lambda|}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}.

Interpolating between
H^{2}(0,\ell_{0})
and

H^{4}(0,\ell_{0})
and exploiting again (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|v_{xxx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}+c\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{1/2}\|v_{xxxx}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|^{1/2}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{1/2}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{1/2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq c|\lambda|^{1/2}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big].

Therefore, we arrive at

|\mathfrak{Re\,}\langle V,q\hat{v}_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell_{0})}|\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\big]\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/2}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\big].

Plugging (
5.17
) and the estimate above into (
5.16
) we reach the thesis.
∎

We are now in a position to complete the proof of Theorem
4.4
. Indeed, collecting Lemmas
5.2
and

5.6
, we infer that

\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/12}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\big]\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/12}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

for every
|\lambda|
sufficiently large. Therefore

\|{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\frac{c}{|\lambda|^{1/24}}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\|_{\mathcal{H}},

which in turns yields (recall that
\sigma({\mathbb{A}})\cap{{i}}\mathbb{R}=\emptyset
)

\limsup_{|\lambda|\to\infty}|\lambda|^{1/24}\|(i\lambda-{\mathbb{A}})^{-1}\|_{L({\mathcal{H}})}<\infty.

The latter is nothing but (
5.1
) with
\mu=1/24
, as desired.
∎

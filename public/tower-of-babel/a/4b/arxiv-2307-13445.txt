Ekedahl-Oort types of stable curves
Dušan Dragutinović

Abstract
. We extend Moonen’s definition of Ekedahl-Oort types of smooth curves in terms of Hasse-Witt triples to all stable curves and show that it matches Ekedahl and van der Geer’s definition of Ekedahl-Oort types of their generalized Jacobians as semi-abelian varieties. Using this intrinsic insight, we can compute the dimensions of certain Ekedahl-Oort loci of curves and generalize some previously known results about the dimensions of the p ​ - ​ rank p\text{-}\mathrm{rank} and a a -number loci of curves.

2.
Ekedahl-Oort types of abelian varieties

In this section, we first recall the definition of the Ekedahl-Oort type of an arbitrary principally polarized abelian variety following
[
Oor01
]
and
[
vdG99
]
. Then, we present its equivalent form in terms of the Dieudonné modules and discuss Moonen’s work on Hasse-Witt triples from
[
Moo22
]
. We define the Ekedahl-Oort type of a smooth curve as the Ekedahl-Oort type of its Jacobian and comment on equivalent ways of defining it. Finally, we comment on when an Ekedahl-Oort locus is contained in the closure of the other one in terms of elements of the Weyl group; we use this in Section
5
to explain the technical condition appearing in Theorem
5.2
.

Let
A
be a
g
-dimensional principally polarized abelian variety over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
, and let
\sigma:k\to k
be the Frobenius morphism on
k
. Its first de Rham cohomology
H^{1}_{dR}(A)
is equipped with
\sigma
- and
\sigma^{-1}
- linear operators
F
and
V
, such that
FV=VF=0
and they are adjoints with respect to the symplectic form
\left\langle\text{ },\right\rangle
on
H^{1}_{dR}(A)
, i.e.,
\left\langle V\omega,\omega^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle\omega,F\omega^{\prime}\right\rangle.

For any subspace
N\subseteq H_{dR}^{1}(A)
, it holds that
(VN)^{\perp}=F^{-1}(N^{\perp})
. Starting from the filtration
0\subset H^{1}_{dR}(A)
and adding the repeated images of
V
as well as their orthogonal complements gives us the so-called
canonical filtration

0\subset N_{1}^{\prime}\subset\ldots\subset N_{r}^{\prime}\subset N_{r+1}^{\prime}\subset\ldots\subset N_{2r}^{\prime}=H^{1}_{dR}(A)

which stabilizes, and by construction satisfies
N_{r}^{\prime}=V(N_{2r}^{\prime})
and
N_{r-i}^{\prime\perp}=N_{r+i}^{\prime}
for any
i
; see also
[
EvdG09
, Section 3.2]
. One can further refine this to a
final filtration
by choosing a maximal filtration that is stable under
V
and
\perp
,

0\subset N_{1}\subset\ldots\subset N_{g}\subset N_{g+1}\subset\ldots\subset N_{2g}=H^{1}_{dR}(A),

i.e., such that
V(N_{2g})=N_{g}
and
N_{g-i}^{\perp}=N_{g+i}
for all
i
.

The
final type
of
A
is the increasing surjective map
\nu:\{0,1,\ldots,2g\}\to\{0,1,\ldots,g\}
defined by

(2.1)

\nu(i)=\dim_{k}V(N_{i})

and
\nu(0)=0
satisfying
\nu(2g-i)=\nu(i)-i+g
for
0\leq i\leq g
. Using these properties, we can see that
\nu
is determined by its values
\nu(i)
for
1\leq i\leq g
, and hence, we will write it as
\nu=(\nu(1),\ldots,\nu(g))
(and say that
\nu
is of degree
g
). The final type of
A
is unique.

An alternative combinatorial description of the same property uses Young diagrams
\mu=\mu(A)=[\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{n}]
with
g\geq\mu_{1}>\ldots\mu_{n}>0
defined in terms of
\nu=\nu(A)
by

(2.2)

\mu_{j}=\#\{i:1\leq i\leq g\text{ and }j\leq i-\nu(i)\}.

We can read off the
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}
and the
a
-number of
A
from
\mu(A)
as

p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(A)=g-\mu_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad a(A)=n.

From this, one can also deduce the basic property

1\leq p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(A)+a(A)\leq g.

By the Ekedahl-Oort type of
A
, we refer to its Young diagram
\mu(A)
. Considering all
A
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
such that
\mu(A)=\mu
defines the locally closed subsets
Z_{\mu}
of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
called the Ekedahl-Oort strata. It is well known by
[
Oor01
, Corollary 11.2]
that
Z_{\mu}
is pure of codimension

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}

in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
. By
[
Oor01
, Proposition 11.1]
, if we introduce a partial ordering by

\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]\geq\mu^{\prime}=[\mu^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{m}]\text{ if }n\leq m\text{ and }\mu_{i}\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n,

for any
g
as long as
g\geq\max\{\mu_{1},\mu_{1}^{\prime}\}
, we get

(2.3)

\mu^{\prime}\leq\mu\quad\implies Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}\text{ in }\mathcal{A}_{g}.

The reverse implication does not hold in general; see
[
Oor01
, 14.3]
or
[
EvdG09
, page 611]
. We comment more on the relations between the Ekedahl-Oort strata in Section
2.2
.

A polarized Dieudonné module over
k
is a quadruple
(M,F,V,b)
, where

•

M
is a
2g
-dimensional
k
-vector space;

•

F:M\to M
is a
\sigma
-linear operator, while
V:M\to M
is a
\sigma^{-1}
-linear operator;

•

\ker(F)=\mathrm{im}(V)
and
\ker(V)=\mathrm{im}(F)
;

•

b:M\times M\to k
is a polarization, i.e., a non-degenerate alternating bilinear form such that
b(F(x),y)=b(x,V(y))^{p}
for all
x,y\in M
.

If we denote by
\mathbb{D}_{A}=\mathbb{D}(A[p])
the (polarized) Dieudonné module of
A[p]
,
[
Oda69
, Section 5]
gives us that

\mathbb{D}(A[p])\cong H_{dR}^{1}(A).

More precisely, we take
M
to be
H_{dR}^{1}(A)
as a
k
-vector space,
F
and
V
the considered operators on
H_{dR}^{1}(A)
, while the polarization
b
is defined by
\left\langle\text{ },\right\rangle
. The mentioned equivalence and what we presented above give us that we can refer to
\mathbb{D}_{A}=(M,F,V,b)
as the Ekedahl-Oort type of
A
.

An important notion that Moonen introduced is the
Hasse-Witt triple
over a perfect field
K
of characteristic
p>0
, which is a triple
(Q,\Phi,\Psi)
such that

•

Q
is a finite-dimensional
K
-vector space;

•

\Phi:Q\to Q
is a
\sigma
-linear map;

•

\Psi:\ker(\Phi)\overset{\cong}{\to}\mathrm{im}(\Phi)^{\perp}
is a
\sigma
-linear bijective map;

where
\mathrm{im}(\Phi)^{\perp}=\{\lambda\in Q^{\vee}:\lambda(q)=0\text{ for all }q\in\mathrm{im}(\Phi)\}
and
\sigma:K\to K
is the Frobenius morphism on
K
. Here, we choose
K=k
and recall the following result.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Moo22
, Theorem 2.8]
)
.

There is a bijection

\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{ isomorphism classes of }\\
(M,F,V,b),\text{ }\dim_{k}(M)=2g\end{matrix}\right\}\longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{matrix}\text{ isomorphism classes of }\\
(Q,\Phi,\Psi),\text{ }\dim_{k}(Q)=g\end{matrix}\right\},

given by
(M,F,V,b)\mapsto(Q,\Phi,\Psi)
where
Q=M/\ker F
,
\Phi=\bar{F}:Q\to Q
is the map induced by
F
, and
\Psi(x)=b(-,F(x))
.

Proof.

See
[
Moo22
, 2.5 and 2.6]
for constructing this bijection and its inverse.

2.1.
Ekedahl-Oort types of smooth curves

Let
C
be a smooth curve over
k
and let
\mathcal{J}_{C}
be its Jacobian. Using the above, we define the Ekedahl-Oort type of
C
either as the isomorphism class of the Dieudonné module
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{J}_{C}[p])
or as the Young diagram

\mu(C)=\mu(\mathcal{J}_{C}).

We also say
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(C)=p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{J}_{C})
and
a(C)=a(\mathcal{J}_{C})
. Let us mention below some other equivalent ways to define the Ekedahl-Oort type of
C
.

Note that by
[
Oda69
, Section 5]
, we have
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{J}_{C}[p])\cong H_{dR}^{1}(\mathcal{J}_{C})\cong H_{dR}^{1}(C).
Furthermore, by, e.g.,
[
KT18
, Proposition 3.1]
, we have the Hodge-de Rham short exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(C,\Omega_{C}^{1})\to H_{dR}^{1}(C)\to H^{1}({C,\mathcal{O}_{C}})\to 0,

and the operator
V
on
H_{dR}^{1}(C)
essentially acts as the Cartier operator
\mathcal{C}
on
H^{0}(C,\Omega_{C}^{1})
, i.e.,
V(H_{dR}^{1}(C))=H^{0}(C,\Omega_{C}^{1})
and
V\mid_{H^{0}(C,\Omega_{C}^{1})}=\mathcal{C}
. Using the preceding definitions, we can naturally define the Ekedahl-Oort type of
C
using
V
and
\perp
.

Lastly, as Moonen observed in
[
Moo22
]
, we can take
Q
to be
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
,
\Phi
to be the Frobenius operator
F_{C}
on
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
, and take
\Psi
as above to define the Ekedahl-Oort type of
C
via the Hasse-Witt triple
(Q,\Phi,\Psi)
using the equivalence from Theorem
2.1
.

2.2.
On the Weyl group and the inclusion of Ekedahl-Oort loci

Let
S_{g}
be the symmetric group on
g
letters, let
W_{g}
be the Weyl group defined by

W_{g}=\{\omega\in S_{2g}:\omega(i)+\omega(2g+1-i)=2g+1\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots,g\}\cong S_{g}\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{g}.

The length of an element
\omega\in W_{g}
is the number

l(\omega)=\#\{i<j\leq g:\omega(i)>\omega(j)\}+\#\{1\leq j\leq g:\omega(i)+\omega(j)>2g+1\}.

For any
\omega\in W_{g}
,
\omega(i)
determines
\omega(2g+1-i)
for
1\leq i\leq g
, so we can write
\omega=[a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{g}]
if
\omega(i)=a_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq g
.
Let
\omega=[a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{g}]
and
\omega^{\prime}=[b_{1}b_{2}\ldots b_{g}]
. Using
[
EvdG09
, Lemma 2.1]
, we write
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}
, which is the Bruhat-Chevalley order, if for all
1\leq i\leq d\leq g
, the
i
th largest element of
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
is
\leq
the
i
th largest element of
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}\}
.

Let
W_{I}=\{\omega\in W_{g}:\omega\{1,2,\ldots,g\}=\{1,2,\ldots,g\}\}\cong S_{g}
be a subgroup of
W_{g}
and note that
|W_{g}|/|W_{I}|=2^{g}
. If we denote by
{}^{I}W
and
W^{I}
the sets of minimal length coset representatives of
W_{I}\backslash W_{g}
and
W_{g}/W_{I}
, the map
\omega\mapsto\omega^{-1}
is an order preserving bijection of
{}^{I}W
and
W^{I}
by
[
PWZ11
, page 260]
(or
[
Wed05
, Lemma 1.5]
). There is a one-to-one correspondence between the cosets in
W_{I}\backslash W_{g}
and the final types
\nu
(of degree
g
). It is
induced by the map
\omega\mapsto\nu_{\omega}=(\nu_{\omega}(1),\ldots,\nu_{\omega}(g))
, with
\nu_{\omega}(i)=i-\#\{a\leq g:\omega^{-1}(a)\leq i\}
, which depends only on the cosets; see
[
EvdG09
, page 575]
for more details. For a Young diagram
\mu
associated to

On Hasse-Witt partitions

Let

In the rest of this section, to the objects parametrized by

On Hasse-Witt partitions and

Let

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

be a smooth curve of genus

over

be its Jacobian. If

is defined as above and
\delta(\mathcal{J}_{C})

, then

.

Proof.

Denote by

its Hasse-Witt matrix, i.e., the matrix of Frobenius

on

and

its Cartier-Manin matrix, i.e., the matrix of the Cartier operator
\mathcal{C}
acting on

as in Section

4.
Going to the boundary

In this section, we first define the Hasse-Witt partition and then the Ekedahl-Oort type of an arbitrary semi-abelian variety or an arbitrary stable curve in terms of Hasse-Witt triples. We carefully prove that these definitions are equivalent to the ones in the literature.

4.1.
Hasse-Witt partitions of stable curves

Let first
A
be a semi-abelian variety of dimension
g
that is an extension of an abelian variety
A_{0}
by a torus of toric rank
l
. In
[
EvdG09
, Section 5]
, Ekedahl and van der Geer defined the final type
\nu_{A}
of

by

(4.1)

\nu_{A}(i+l)=\nu_{A_{0}}(i)+l,\quad\text{for any}\quad 0\leq i\leq g-l

(and

for

Essentially, this introduces the Ekedahl-Oort loci on

that they study.
Here, we define the Hasse-Witt partition of

as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

that is an extension of an abelian variety

by a torus of toric rank

is

, where

By (

Recall that in Section

for

either a stable curve or a finite union of smooth curves. We are interested in the latter case because we would like to compute the Hasse-Witt partition (and later the Ekedahl-Oort type) of the normalization of a stable curve, which does not have to be connected. Furthermore, if

is a union of smooth curves

for

Below, we show that the Hasse-Witt partitions of a stable curve

and its (generalized) Jacobian

match. Furthermore, we find that the Hasse-Witt partition of

coincides with the one of its normalization. Namely, we have the following results.

Proposition 4.2
.

Proof.

Let

be a stable curve with

be its normalization. Similarly as in

]
, we get the short exact sequence of sheaves

of

. Taking the long exact sequence in cohomology, we get

. Even more is true: consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{C}}

Ekedahl-Oort types of stable curves

by a torus of toric rank

follows as well.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Let us point out that the information about the loops of a stable curve (or equivalently, the toric part of its generalized Jacobian) in the Hasse-Witt triple is fully described by the operator

]
.

Remark 4.8
.

Let
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}
be a principally polarized abelian variety, e.g.,

, as in the proof of Theorem

.

For any

, let

and let

equals

5.
Inductive results

Using what was observed in Section
4
, we get an inductive result telling us how the dimensions of components of certain
\overline{Z}_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+1}
that intersect the divisor
\Delta_{0}
depend on the dimensions of the components of
\overline{Z}_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. We present it below and show how it generalizes some well-known inductive results about the
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}
and
a
-number stratifications of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.

Throughout this section, we use that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
and
\mathcal{A}_{g}
are smooth stacks so that the codimension of an intersection of two of their closed subvarieties is the sum of the codimensions at most. We denote
j(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{ct})=\mathcal{J}_{g}
and
j(\mathcal{M}_{g})=\mathcal{J}_{g}^{0}\subset\mathcal{J}_{g}.
Furthermore, we denote by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
the moduli space of
n
-pointed stable curves of genus
g
, and we use
\kappa
as the notation for any of the finite clutching morphisms; see
[
Knu83
, Section 3]
. For example,

\kappa_{1,1}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g^{\prime},1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+g^{\prime}}

is obtained by identifying the labeled points on a stable genus-
g
curve
C
and a stable genus-
g^{\prime}
curve
C^{\prime}
, while

\kappa_{2}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g-1,2}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}

is obtained by identifying the labeled points on a stable genus-
(g-1)
curve
C
.
For any
n\geq 1
, by
Z_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
we denote the inverse image of
Z_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
under the forgetful morphism
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
and we write
Z_{\mu}\mathcal{M}_{g}=Z_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\cap\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Fix
g\geq 2
and let
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
be two Young diagrams. We have seen in Section
2.2
that we can deduce whether there is an inclusion
Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}
of the Ekedahl-Oort loci in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
by looking at the combinatorial description given by (
2.4
). This description involves certain relations between elements in the Weyl group
W_{g}
(and the so-called shuffles). By
[
EvdG09
, page 599]
, there is an injective group homomorphism
\rho:W_{g}\to W_{g+1}
, which respects the Bruhat-Chevalley order and the shuffling. Therefore, it respects (
2.4
), so we can see that

Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
implies
Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g+1}
for any
g
. On the other hand, let us consider the condition that
Young diagram
\mu
satisfies

(5.1)

Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}\text{ in }\mathcal{A}_{g+1}\implies Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}\text{ in }\mathcal{A}_{g}\text{ for each }\mu^{\prime}=[\mu_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mu_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime}]\text{ with }\mu_{1}^{\prime}<g+1.

Note that (
2.4
) essentially depends on
g
and the structure of
W_{g}
. Namely, the condition that
Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g+1}
is given by a relation in
W_{g+1}
involving shuffles of elements in
W_{g+1}
, while the condition
Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is described in terms of the elements of
W_{g}
. Therefore, (
5.1
) is, a priori, non-trivial.
Because of this, to present Theorem
5.2
in full generality, we have to be careful and consider only the situation when this condition holds.
Below, we mention some cases when (
5.1
) is automatically satisfied.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that (
5.1
) is satisfied for all
\mu
such that
\overline{Z}_{\mu}=\cup_{\mu^{\prime}\leq\mu}Z_{\mu^{\prime}}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g+1}
with
g\geq\mu_{1}
; see (
2.3
).
Using
[
Oor01
, Proposition 12.5]
, we find that for any Young diagram

(5.2)

\mu=[\mu_{1},\underbrace{\mu_{2},\mu_{2}-1,\ldots,2,1}_{\mu_{2}}]

with
\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}\geq 0
and for any
g\geq\mu_{1}
, it holds that
\overline{Z}_{\mu}=\cup_{\mu^{\prime}\leq\mu}Z_{\mu^{\prime}}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
.
This follows from the fact that
\overline{Z}_{\mu}
defines the closed locus of principally polarized abelian varieties with
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}\leq g-\mu_{1}
and
a
-number
\geq 1+\mu_{2}
; for more details, see
[
Oor74
, Corollary 1.5]
and
[
Kob75
, page 44]
. Therefore, for
\mu
as in
(
5.2
)
, technical condition (
5.1
) is automatically satisfied for any

, as long as
g\geq\mu_{1}
.

Now that we commented on the technical conditions appearing in it, we can present our second main result, which relies on the intrinsic nature of Definition
4.5
and Theorem
4.6
, and shows the full potential of technique used in the proof of
[
FvdG04
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

For any
g\geq\mu_{1}
, let
\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]
be a Young diagram such that (
5.1
), and let
d
be the number defined by
3g-3-d=\max\{\dim\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma^{\prime}\text{ a component of }\overline{Z}_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\}
. If
\Gamma
is a component of
\overline{Z}_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+1}
that meets
\Delta_{0}
, the codimension of
\Gamma
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+1}
is at least
d
.

Proof.

Let
C
be any stable curve that corresponds to a point of
\Gamma\cap\Delta_{0}\neq\varnothing
and denote by
\mu^{\prime}=[\mu_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mu_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime}]
its Ekedahl-Oort type. Note that
\mu^{\prime}
satisfies
Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g+1}
.
Furthermore, recall that
\Delta_{0}=\kappa_{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2})
and note that
\mu^{\prime}_{1}<g+1
since the
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}
of a curve lying in
\Delta_{0}
has to be at least
1
, while
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(C)=g+1-\mu_{1}^{\prime}
.

Now, Theorem
4.6
implies that the normalization of any such singular curve

with Ekedahl-Oort type
\mu^{\prime}
has Ekedahl-Oort type
\mu^{\prime}
as well. By the assumption, i.e., by (

),
Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}\text{ in }\mathcal{A}_{g+1}
implies

in

, and therefore,

\Gamma\cap\Delta_{0}\subseteq\kappa_{2}(\overline{Z}_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}).

In particular, this gives us that

\dim\Gamma\leq\dim({\Gamma}\cap\Delta_{0})+1\leq(3g-3-d+2)+1=3(g+1)-3-d.

In other words, the codimension of
\Gamma
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g+1}
is at least

.
∎

In the remarks below, we further comment on the preceding theorem.

Remark 5.3
.

Note that

in Theorem

is

satisfied if

. This follows from the Diaz-Looijenga bound and we discuss this in Proposition

might be empty.

Remark 5.4
.

We have the following observations about the number

from Theorem

(1)

In principle, we could formulate Theorem

in a way that it holds for any Young diagram

(2)

in

in Theorem

, instead of choosing

to be the smallest codimension of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
from the set of all components
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\overline{Z}_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.

Remark 5.5
.

Note that Theorem

from the introduction follows from Theorem
5.2
for the choice of

as in (

, we clarify why such a

satisfies condition (

Let

with

at most

As a consequence of the preceding theorem, we reprove some results about the

Corollary 5.6
.

The following results hold.

(1)

[

, Theorem 2.3]

and

(2)

[

Proof.

For part

, if

For part

. Part

gives us that the codimension of

∎

Let us now see an application of how one can compute the exact dimensions of certain components of

Corollary 5.7
.

Let

, and

be integers such that

, let

and that for all

\Gamma

\overline{Z}_{\mu}\mathcal{M}_{g}

in
V_{g-3}\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Proof.

The non-emptiness follows from Remark

[

, while parts

and
[

, using

Another way to prove these claims assuming the non-emptiness of the considered loci is to use that the

-number
\geq 2
locus of

and the
a
-number
\geq 2
locus of

are, respectively, equal to

as we noted above. Now, dimension computations and Proposition

tell us that any component

of these Ekedahl-Oort loci satisfies the conditions of Corollary

to

Let
Z_{[4,3]}\mathcal{H}_{4}
be the locus of smooth hyperelliptic curves of genus
4
over

with Ekedahl-Oort type
[4,3]
. In Theorem

, we present the criterion for computing dimensions of certain loci
\Gamma
in
\mathcal{M}_{4}
such that

in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
. We show it below in case

with

is completely contained in the supersingular locus of

.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\mathrm{char}(k)=p>2
. If the locus of smooth hyperelliptic curves of genus
4
over
k
with Ekedahl-Oort type
[4,3]
is non-empty and finite, then all components of
V_{0}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
(and thus of
V_{g-4}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
) have generic
a
-number
1
. Moreover, then
Z_{\mu}\mathcal{M}_{4}
is pure of codimension
\sum\mu_{i}
in
\mathcal{M}_{4}
if non-empty, for any
\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]
with
\mu>[4,3]
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a Young diagram with
\mu>[4,3]
. Since
\mathcal{J}_{4}
is of codimension
1
in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
and
\mathcal{A}_{4}
is smooth,

\overline{Z}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{J}_{4}
is either pure of the expected codimension in
\mathcal{J}_{4}
, or it is of codimension one less, irreducible by
[
EvdG09
, Theorem 11.5]
, and thus coincides with
\overline{Z}_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
.

Assume the latter is true and let
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be components of
\overline{Z}_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
and
\overline{Z}_{[4,3]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
containing a point of
Z_{[4,3]}\mathcal{H}_{4}
which corresponds to a smooth hyperelliptic curve
C
with Ekedahl-Oort type
[4,3]
, and such that
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
. By assumption, we get that
j(\Gamma\cap\mathcal{M}_{g}^{ct})=\overline{Z}_{\mu}
and that
\dim\Gamma^{\prime}=3
since
j(\Gamma^{\prime})
is a component of the locus
\overline{Z}_{[4,3]}
, which is pure of dimension
3
. Since it is non-empty, any component of the locus
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
must be of dimension at least
3-2=1
. However, as we noted, there is a component of
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
which is
0
-dimensional, namely, the one consisting of the point corresponding to the curve
C
.

As before, the result for
V_{0}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
follows for the choice
\mu=[4,1]
and we conclude that every component of
V_{g-4}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
has generic
a
-number
1
for any
g\geq 4
using Corollary
5.6
(2).
∎

Remark 5.12
.

The proof of Theorem

in principle works in any characteristic. However, by Lemma

it turns out there are no smooth curves of genus

with Ekedahl-Oort type

in characteristic

is empty.

6.

In this section, we present some results about the Ekedahl-Oort stratification of the moduli spaces of genus-

in characteristics

For

Lemma 6.1

(
[

, Corollary 6.6]
)
.

In characteristic

By Remark

Lemma 6.2
.

In characteristic

, there is a smooth genus
4
curve with Ekedahl-Oort type
[3,2]
.

Proof.

From the list of all genus
4

(up to an

-isomorphism) made by Xarles in

, we find the hyperelliptic curve

and

, where

.
∎

With a proof similar to the one of Lemma
6.1
, we prove below a slight generalization of that claim.

Proposition 6.3
.

In characteristic
p=2
, for any Young diagram
\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]
with
\mu>[4,3]
,
Z_{\mu}\mathcal{M}_{4}
is non-empty and is pure of codimension
\sum\mu_{i}
in
\mathcal{M}_{4}
. For any other Young diagram
\mu
, namely
\mu=[4,3]
or
\mu\leq[3,2,1]

Proof.

In characteristic
2
,
Z_{\mu}\mathcal{M}_{4}
is non-empty for
\mu\in\{[4],[4,1],[4,2]\}
by Lemma
6.1
, for
\mu=[3,2]
by Lemma

, and for the Young diagrams
\mu
with
\mu>[3,2]
by Remark
5.8
. On the other hand,
Z_{\mu}\mathcal{M}_{4}
is empty for
\mu=[3,2,1]
by
[
Zho19
, Theorem 1.1]
and for the remaining ones, which do not satisfy

.

Fix
\mu>[4,3]
and note that
\overline{Z}_{\mu}
is irreducible in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
by
[

, Theorem 11.5]
. Again using that
\mathcal{A}_{4}
is a smooth stack and
\mathcal{J}_{4}

\mathcal{A}_{4}

of

. If the latter is true for a component

. This is not possible since

. Namely,
Z_{[4,3]}\cap\mathcal{J}_{4}^{0}=\varnothing
and the locus of Jacobians of singular curves with Ekedahl-Oort type

is at most
2

. This implies the result.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

In characteristic

, there is a smooth genus

[3,2]
, for any

.

Proof.

,

is non-empty and is pure of the expected codimension

. Therefore,
[

, Theorem 6.4]

∎

Let us now consider the case

as we do in the following example.

Example 6.5
.

Any hyperelliptic genus-
4
curve
C
over an algebraically closed field
k
with characteristic
\neq 2
can be written in the following normal form

y^{2}=f(x)=x^{9}+a_{8}x^{8}+\ldots+a_{2}x^{2}+x,

with
a_{i}\in k
, for
2\leq i\leq 8
, obtained by choosing
0
and
\infty
to be the branch points and by scaling. Let here
\mathrm{char}(k)=3
, when we can write the Hasse-Witt matrix of
C
in quite a simple form:

\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&a_{5}&a_{8}&0\\
1&a_{4}&a_{7}&0\\
0&a_{3}&a_{6}&1\\
0&a_{2}&a_{5}&a_{8}\end{pmatrix}.

The condition that
C
has Ekedahl-Oort type
[4,3]
is equivalent to the fact that its Hasse-Witt partition is
\delta=22
, i.e., that

\mathrm{rank}_{k}H=2\quad\text{and}\quad HH^{(3)}=0.

It is not hard to check that the first condition is equivalent to
a_{2}=a_{5}=a_{8}=0
using that the discriminant of
f
should be non-zero, while the second one gives us
a_{3}=a_{4}=a_{6}=a_{7}=0
. Hence, there is a unique hyperelliptic curve

y^{2}=x^{9}+x

with Ekedahl-Oort type
[4,3]
up to isomorphism. In particular, there is a zero-dimensional component of

\overline{Z}_{[4,3]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}\cap\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{4},

where
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{4}
is the (closed) locus of stable hyperelliptic curves of genus
4
, which is of codimension
4-2=2
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
.

.

In characteristic
p=3
, for any Young diagram
\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]

5.11
finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

In any characteristic

, all the components of

\overline{Z}_{[2]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3}

On the other hand, note that we cannot use the Diaz-Looijenga bound to obtain the result for

[

, Theorem 11.5]
, which implies that

\overline{Z}_{[2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3}
has only one component

that is generically smooth and

has a non-empty intersection with

. All the other components of

In the following two examples, we find the upper bounds on the dimensions of the components of
\overline{Z}_{[3,2]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
and
\overline{Z}_{[3,2]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{5}

and comment on special cases. We note that the type

gives us the first case when there are components of

-dimensional locus.

Example 6.9

(Ekedahl-Oort type
\left[3,2\right]
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}
.)
.

Let
\mu=[3,2]
. In this example, we give an upper bound on

for any component
\Gamma

\overline{Z}_{\mu}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4}

.

Because of the remark that there is a component of

.

In Proposition

(Ekedahl-Oort type

. Using this observation and Theorem

, we can first find some upper bounds on the dimensions of the components of
\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{5}
and then of
\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{6}
in a similar manner:
\dim\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{5}\leq 7
and
\dim\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{6}\leq 10.
Namely, if
\Gamma
is a component
\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{5}
with
\Gamma\cap\Delta_{0}\neq\varnothing
(resp.
\Gamma\cap\Delta_{0}=\varnothing
) it follows
\dim\Gamma\leq 7
by Theorem
5.2
(resp. by the Diaz-Looijenga bound in Remark
5.4
). Similarly, if
\Gamma
is a component
\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{6}
with
\Gamma\cap\Delta_{0}\neq\varnothing
(resp.
\Gamma\cap\Delta_{0}=\varnothing
) it follows
\dim\Gamma\leq 10
by Theorem
5.2
(resp.
\dim\Gamma\leq 9
by the Diaz-Looijenga bound).

By the Diaz-Looijenga bound, every component of
\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, for
g\geq 7
, has a non-empty intersection with
\Delta_{0}
. Therefore, Theorem
5.2
and the preceding computations imply that for any
g\geq 3
, it holds that

\dim\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\leq 3g-8.

Finally, let us denote by
T_{a}\mathcal{M}_{g}
the locus of smooth curves of genus
g
with
a
-number
\geq a
. We sum up some of the conclusions we made.

Corollary 6.13
.

In any characteristic
p>0
and for any
g\geq 3
, we have
\dim T_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}=3g-4
,
\dim T_{2}\mathcal{M}_{g}=3g-6
, and
\dim T_{3}\mathcal{M}_{g}\leq 3g-8
.

Proof.

The conclusion for
T_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}=V_{g-1}\mathcal{M}_{g}
follows from
[
FvdG04
, Theorem 2.3]
(here Corollary
5.6
(1)). The upper bound for
\dim T_{2}\mathcal{M}_{g}
is given by
[
Pri09
, Corollary 4.5]
, while the lower bound is discussed in Corollary
5.10
(1). Lastly, the conclusion
\dim T_{3}\mathcal{M}_{g}\leq 3g-8
follows from Example
6.12
since
T_{3}\mathcal{M}_{g}=\overline{Z}_{[3,2,1]}\mathcal{M}_{g}
.
∎

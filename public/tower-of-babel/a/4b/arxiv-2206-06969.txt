Using Ultrapowers to Compare Continuous Structures
H. Jerome Keisler

Abstract
. In 1967 the author introduced a pre-ordering of all first order complete theories where T is lower than U if it is easier for an ultrapower of a model of T T than an ultrapower of a model of U U to be saturated. In a long series of recent papers, Malliaris and Shelah showed that this pre-ordering is very rich and gives a useful way of classifying simple theories. In this paper we investigate the analogous pre-ordering in continuous model theory.



1.
Introduction

The results in this paper bring together two research projects that began in the 1960’s, the so-called “Keisler order”
\trianglelefteq

Both of these projects were mostly dormant for four decades, and then suddenly
blossomed into active and highly successful programs during the last decade.

In the paper [Ke67], the relation

The modern treatment of continuous model theory deals with metric structures.
A surprisingly large part of first order model theory can be generalized to the continuous case.
Many notions and results in first order model theory have analogues for metric structures, including ultrapowers and

In recent years, the model theory of metric structures has had exciting mathematical applications in analysis.

In this paper we study the analogue of the relation

Some care is needed in our terminology, because we will be comparing metric theories with possibly different vocabularies.

Historically, continuous model theory for real-valued structures was introduced by C. C. Chang and the author in 1966 in the monograph [CK66]. Four decades later, the 2008 paper [BBHU08] introduced the modern treatment of continuous model theory for metric structures.
In [CK66] the real-valued structures had an equality predicate. In the current treatment, the real-valued structures do not have equality, and are the continuous analogue of
first order structures without equality. Metric structures are the continuous analogue of first order structures with equality, with the distance predicate playing the
role of equality.

The ultraproduct construction for first order structures was introduced by Łoś [Lo55]. Frayne, Morel and Scott [FMS62] used ultraproducts modulo regular ultrafilters.
The first order analogue of the result A appeared in Keisler [Ke67], and the first order analogues of B and C appeared in Shelah [Sh78].
For the next three decades, there was no further progress on the relation
\trianglelefteq
on first order theories until a breakthrough by Malliaris in [Ma09].
In that paper, Malliaris introduced notions, such as the distribution of a set of formulas in an ultraproduct, that were key to
subsequent work on the
\trianglelefteq
-order. The main result in [Ma09]
showed that an ultrapower of a first order structure modulo a regular
\mathcal{D}
is
\lambda^{+}
-saturated if, for every first order formula
\varphi
, the ultrapower is
\lambda^{+}
-saturated for
\varphi
-types.

The first order analogues of the results D, E, and F appeared in the following papers.
D: Malliaris and Shelah [MS16a]; E: Malliaris [Ma12]; and F: Malliaris and Shelah [MS13].
Those results show that the relation
\trianglelefteq
on first order theories also looks like Figure 1 (with
T^{*}_{feq}
instead of
T^{R}_{rg}
). The proofs of the first order results required the construction of ultrafilters with properties like being good.
Kunen’s method of constructing ultrafilters via independent families of sets played a key role.
Good ultrafilters were introduced in [Ke64] and constructed assuming the generalized continuum hypothesis (GCH). In 1972, Kunen [Ku72]
used independent families of sets to construct good ultrafilters without the GCH.

The work of Malliaris and Shelah involved two parallel lines of research: the construction of ultrafilters with special set-theoretic properties,
and the interplay between the
\trianglelefteq
relation and model-theoretic properties of first order theories.

The results in this paper are obtained by extending the second of the above lines of research to metric theories.
Along the way we obtain other results
about metric structures that may be of interest in their own right. Stability and indiscernible sequences in metric theories
have been studied extensively in many papers (see Section
6
below).
However, as by-products of his proof of the results B and C above for first order theories, Shelah in [Sh78] proved many additional results about indiscernible sequences and the finite cover property,
which are generalized to the continuous case here.
Metric theories with the property TP
2
and the independence property have been studied in the paper [BY13]. The notion of an SOP
2
metric theory here may be new.

In the continuous model theory literature, a metric signature is usually held fixed and the ultraproduct is introduced for families of metric structures with the
same metric signature.
To prove the results in this paper we will need a more general ultraproduct construction, for families of real-valued structures with the same vocabulary.
That leads us to consider the
\trianglelefteq
relation between theories of arbitrary real-valued structures, rather than just between metric theories.

It turns out that the pre-ordering
\trianglelefteq
is essentially the same in that case.
For a real-valued structure
\mathcal{M}
with vocabulary
V
,
\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})
is again defined as the set of all
V
-sentences with truth value
0
in
\mathcal{M}
,
and we call
\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})
a continuous theory with vocabulary
V
. We have

\{\mbox{first order theories}\}\subsetneqq\{\mbox{metric theories}\}\subsetneqq\{\mbox{continuous theories}\}.

We define the relation
T\trianglelefteq U
for continuous theories in the same way as for metric theories.
Theorem 3.6 below is a stronger version of result A above, showing that
T\trianglelefteq T
for every continuous theory
T
, so any two real-valued models
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
of
T

(metric or not) are saturated by the same regular ultrafilters. Theorem 5.1 below will show that every continuous theory is
\trianglelefteq
-equivalent to a metric theory. We may therefore
identify the
\trianglelefteq
-equivalence classes of continuous theories with the
\trianglelefteq
-equivalence classes of metric theories in a way that preserves the
\trianglelefteq
relation.

We now review some results of Malliaris and Shelah about first order theories.
In

Theorem 4.4

\prod^{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{M}_{t}

\lambda^{+}
-saturated.

Our proof of Theorem

Fact 6.6

.

Convention 6.7
.

Hereafter, when
T
is a stable theory,
\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hbox to0.0pt{$$}\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hbox to0.0pt{$$}\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\kern 4.53473pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hbox to0.0pt{$$}\hss}\lower 2.71246pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 4.53473pt}{\kern 3.95836pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hbox to0.0pt{$$}\hss}\lower 1.93747pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 3.95836pt}}
will denote the unique stable independence relation for
T
.

By a proper tuple we mean a tuple of distinct elements.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. A set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}^{n}
is called
B
-indiscernible if every two proper tuples of elements of
\mathcal{I}
of the same length
have the same type over
B
. A sequence
\mathcal{I}=\langle\vec{a}_{h}\rangle_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements of
\mathfrak{M}^{n}
is
B
-indiscernible if every two
strictly increasing finite subsequences of
\mathcal{I}
of the same length have the same type over
B
.
Indiscernible means
\emptyset
-indiscernible.

Lemma 6.8
.

Suppose

is stable. Then every

-indiscernible sequence is a
B
-indiscernible set.

Proof.

The proof is the same as the proof of the corresponding result in first order logic (see, for example,
Proposition 7.1 in [Pi83]), but using the continuous notion of a stable formula in Definition
6.2

instead of the first order notion of a stable formula.

The next lemma is a continuous analogue of Definition III.1.5 and Lemma III.1.7 in [Sh78].

Lemma 6.9
.

Suppose
T
is stable,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}^{n}
is an infinite indiscernible set, and
B\subseteq\mathfrak{M}
is small.
There is a unique type
\operatorname{Av}(\mathcal{I},B)\in S_{k}(B)
such that for each formula
\psi(\vec{x},B)
with
|\vec{x}|=n
and
\varepsilon>0
,
for all but finitely many
\vec{a}\in\mathcal{I}
we have

|\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(\vec{a},B)-\operatorname{Av}(\mathcal{I},B)^{\psi}|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Consider a formula
\psi(\vec{x},B)
. We have
\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(\vec{x},B)=\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(\vec{x},\vec{b})
for some finite
\vec{b}\subseteq B
.
It suffices to show that there is a unique value
t(\psi)\in[0,1]
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
, for all but finitely many
\vec{a}\in\mathcal{I}
we have

|\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(|\vec{a},\vec{b})-t(\psi)|<\varepsilon,

because then

\operatorname{Av}(\mathcal{I},B)=\{\psi(\vec{x},\vec{b})\colon t(\psi)=0\}

has the required property.
It is clear that there is at most one such value
t(\psi)
.

Let is call a set
X\subseteq[0,1]

broad
if
X
is a closed interval, and
\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(\vec{a},\vec{b})\in X
for all but finitely many
\vec{a}\in\mathcal{I}
.
Note that any intersection of finitely many broad sets is broad.

Claim
6.9
.1:
Suppose
0\leq s<r\leq 1
.
Then at least one of the intervals
[0,r],[s,1]
is broad.

Proof of Claim
6.9
.1. Assume not. Then there are infinitely many
\vec{a}\in\mathcal{I}
such that
\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(\vec{a},\vec{b})<s
, and infinitely many
\vec{c}\in\mathcal{I}

such that
\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(\vec{c},\vec{b})>r
.
Hence there is an infinite subsequence
\langle\vec{c}_{h}\rangle_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{I}
such that for all even
h\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(\vec{c}_{h},\vec{b})<s
, and for all odd
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\psi^{\mathfrak{M}}(\vec{c}_{j},\vec{b})>r
. Taking
\vec{b}_{h}=\vec{b}
for all
h
, we see that

\psi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
is unstable in
T
. But since
T
is stable by hypothesis,
\psi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
is stable in
T

by Fact
6.6
. This proves Claim
6.9
.1.

Claim
6.9
.2:
For each positive
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a broad set
X_{k}
of length
\leq 2/k
.

Proof of Claim
6.9
.2. There is a least integer
m_{0}\leq k
such that
[0,m_{0}/k]
is broad, and a greatest integer
m_{1}\leq k

such that
[m_{1}/k,1]
is broad. Then the set

X_{k}=[0,m_{0}/k]\cap[m_{1}/k,1]=[m_{1}/k,m_{0}/k]

is broad, but neither of the sets
[0,(m_{0}-1)/k]
,
[(m_{1}+1)/k,1]
is broad. By Claim
6.9
.1, we cannot have
m_{0}-1>m_{1}+1
. Hence
m_{0}\leq m_{1}+2
, so
the interval
X_{k}
has length
\leq 2/k
. This proves Claim
6.9
.2.

Since
[0,1]
is compact,

\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{k}
is non-empty, and contains exactly one point
t\in[0,1]
. The result now follows with
t(\psi)=t
.
∎

Corollary 6.10
.

Suppose

is stable,

(ii) By Lemma

8.
\trianglelefteq
-maximal theories and SOP
2

Shelah [Sh78] introduced a family of “strict order properties” of first order theories, including the property SOP
2
.
Malliaris and Shelah [MS16a] proved that every first order theory with SOP
2
is maximal in

(\mathbb{F},\trianglelefteq)
, and conjectured that the converse also holds. In this section we
introduce a metric analogue SOP
2
, and show that metric theories that
that have SOP
2
are maximal in
(\mathbb{M},\trianglelefteq)
.

We refer to [Sh78], [MS15], and the expository article [Co12] for background about SOP
2
and related notions in first order logic.

We now introduce some notation for working with binary trees. We identify each
n\in\mathbb{N}
with the set
n=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
.
Let
\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}}
be the set of finite sequences of elements of
\{0,1\}
, let
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of infinite sequences of elements of
\{0,1\}
,
and let
\{0,1\}^{n}
be the set of
n
-tuples of elements of
\{0,1\}
.
Thus
\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}}=\bigcup_{n}\{0,1\}^{n}
.
We regard the elements
\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}}
as nodes of the full binary tree, and regard the elements of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
as branches of the full binary tree.
For
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\sigma|n=\langle\sigma(k)\rangle_{k<n}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
.
For
\mu,\nu\in\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}}
, note that
\mu\subseteq\nu
iff
\mu
is an initial segment of
\nu
.
We call
\mu,\nu

comparable
if
\mu\subseteq\nu
or
\nu\subseteq\mu
, and
incomparable
otherwise.

Definition 8.1
.

A metric theory
T
has SOP
2
if there is a formula
\theta(\vec{x},\vec{z})
, called an SOP
2
formula for
T
,
such that in some model
\mathcal{M}\models T
there are
|\vec{z}|
-tuples

\langle\vec{b}_{\mu}\rangle_{\mu\in\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}}}
such that for each
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1)

\mathcal{M}\models(\inf_{\vec{x}})\max\{\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{\sigma|k})\colon k<n\}=0,

but for each incomparable pair
\mu,\nu\in\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}}
we have

(2)

\mathcal{M}\models(\inf_{\vec{x}})\max(\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{\mu}),\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{\nu}))=1.

Remark 8.2
.

A first order theory has SOP
2
in the above sense if and only if it has SOP
2
in the sense of [Sh78].

Corollary 8.3
.

T
has SOP
2
if and only if for some
0\leq s<r\leq 1
, Definition
8.1
holds with (1) and (2) replaced by

(3)

\mathcal{M}\models(\inf_{\vec{x}})\max\{\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{\sigma|k})\colon k<n\}\leq s,

and

(4)

\mathcal{M}\models(\inf_{\vec{x}})\max(\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{\mu}),\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{\nu}))\geq r.

We call
\theta
an SOP
2
-formula with bounds
(s,r)
in
T
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Corollary
6.3
.
∎

Fact 8.4
.

(Malliaris and Shelah [MS16a], Theorem 11.11). Every first order theory with SOP
2
is maximal in
(\mathbb{F},\trianglelefteq)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{bt}
be the first order structure
(\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}},\subseteq)
(a full binary tree). The first order theory
\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{K}_{bt})
has SOP
2
.
We will need the following characterization of good regular ultrafilters, which was shown and used in [MS16a] to prove Fact
8.4
.

Fact 8.5
.

(By Theorem 10.26 and Lemma 11.6 in [MS16a])
A regular ultrafilter
\mathcal{D}
is good if and only if in the pre-ultrapower
\mathcal{K}_{bt}^{\mathcal{D}}
, every pairwise comparable set of cardinality
\leq|I|

has an upper bound.

Theorem 8.6
.

Every metric theory
T
with SOP
2
is maximal in
(\mathbb{M},\trianglelefteq)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}\in\operatorname{Sat}(T)
.
Then
\mathcal{D}
is a regular ultrafilter over a set

, and for every model
\mathcal{M}
of
T
, the pre-ultrapower
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}
is
|I|^{+}
saturated.
By Lemma
3.10
(ii), it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{D}
is good.
Suppose
A
is a pairwise comparable subset of
\mathcal{K}_{bt}^{\mathcal{D}}
of cardinality
\leq|I|
. By Fact
8.5
above, it is enough to
show that the set of formulas
\Gamma=\{a\subseteq u\colon a\in A\}
is satisfiable in
\mathcal{K}_{bt}^{\mathcal{D}}
.
Since
\mathcal{K}_{bt}^{\mathcal{D}}\equiv\mathcal{K}_{bt}
, every finite pairwise comparable subset of
\mathcal{K}_{bt}^{\mathcal{D}}
has a greatest element, so
\Gamma
is finitely satisfiable in

\mathcal{K}_{bt}^{\mathcal{D}}
.

There is an SOP
2
-formula
\theta(\vec{x},\vec{z})
for
T
, a model
\mathcal{M}
of
T
, and
|\vec{z}|
-tuples

\langle\vec{b}_{\mu}\rangle_{\mu\in\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}}}
in
\mathcal{M}
that satisfy Conditions (1) and (2) of Definition
8.1
.
For each
a\in A
, let
\vec{b}_{a}
be the
|\vec{z}|
-tuple in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}
such that for each
t\in I
,
\vec{b}_{a}[t]=\vec{b}_{a[t]}
.
Consider a finite
A_{0}\subseteq A
. By the Łoś Theorem, there is a set
X_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
such that for each
t\in X_{0}
the set
\{a[t]\colon a\in A_{0}\}

is pairwise comparable in
\mathcal{K}_{bt}
. By Condition (1), for each

\mathcal{M}\models(\inf_{\vec{x}})\max\{\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{a}[t])\colon a\in A_{0}\}=0,

so by the Łoś Theorem,

\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}\models(\inf_{\vec{x}})\max\{\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{a})\colon a\in A_{0}\}=0.

By Remark
2.26
,
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}
is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated, so there exists
\vec{x}
in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}
such that

\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}\models\max\{\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{a})\colon a\in A_{0}\}=0.

Thus the set of formulas

\Gamma^{\prime}=\{\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{a})\colon a\in A\}

is finitely satisfiable in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}
is
|I|^{+}
-saturated, there is a tuple
\vec{x}

that satisfies
\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
regularize
\mathcal{D}
, so
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
,
|\mathcal{X}|=|I|
, and each

belongs to only finitely many
X\in\mathcal{X}
.
Pick an injective function

.
Consider an element
t\in I
. Let

be the finite set of

that “matter” for

. Let

By Condition (2), any two elements of

are comparable in
\{0,1\}^{<\mathbb{N}}
. Let

B_{t}
in
\mathcal{K}_{bt}

(with the convention that

is the empty sequence if

is empty). Then

.

Fix an element

. Let

By the Łoś Theorem and the fact that
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}\models\theta(\vec{x},\vec{b}_{a})=0
, we have

. For each

we have

\mathcal{K}_{bt}\models a[t]\subseteq u[t]
, so by the Łoś Theorem again,
\mathcal{K}_{bt}^{\mathcal{D}}\models a\subseteq u
. Therefore

satisfies
\Gamma
in
\mathcal{K}_{bt}^{\mathcal{D}}
,
and the proof is complete.
∎

9.
\trianglelefteq
-minimal unstable theories and the independence property

The theory
T_{rg}
of the random (or Rado) graph is the first order theory with a single binary relation
R
whose axioms say that
R
is symmetric and reflexive, and that for any two
disjoint finite sets of elements
X,Y
there is an element
z
such that
R(z,x)\wedge\neg R(z,y)
for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
.
T_{rg}

is
\aleph_{0}
-categorical, simple, unstable, and has quantifier elimination.

In Theorem
9.7
below, we show that
T_{rg}

is
\trianglelefteq
-minimal among all unstable metric theories.
It will follow that the equivalence class of
T_{rg}
is the third element of
(\mathbb{M},\trianglelefteq)
and is not maximal in
(\mathbb{M},\trianglelefteq)
.
This is a continuous analogue of the result of Malliaris that
T_{rg}
is
\trianglelefteq
-minimal among all unstable first order theories (Fact
9.6
below).

Shelah, in Theorem II.4.7 of [Sh78], proved that a first order theory is unstable if and only if it has the strict order property or the independence property.
Dzamonja and Shelah [DS04] proved that a first order theory with the strict order property has SOP
2
, so a first order theory is unstable if and
only if it has either SOP
2
or the independence property.
In [BY09], Ben Yaacov introduced the continuous analogue of the independence property, which is equivalent to Definition
9.1
below.
We will show in Theorem
9.5
below that a metric theory is unstable if and only if it has either the SOP
2

or the independence property.

Definition 9.1
.

T
has the
independence property
if there is a formula
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
and a model
\mathcal{M}
of
T
such that
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
there are
|\vec{x}|
-tuples
\langle\vec{a}_{Z}\rangle_{Z\subseteq m}
and
|\vec{y}|
-tuples
\langle\vec{b}_{n}\rangle_{n<m}

such that for all
n<m
and
Z\subseteq m
,

n\in Z\Rightarrow\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{a}_{Z},\vec{b}_{n})=0,\qquad n\notin Z\Rightarrow\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{a}_{Z},\vec{b}_{n})=1.

We also say that
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
has the independence property for
T
(in
\mathcal{M}
).

Remark 9.2
.

If
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
has the independence property for
T
, then for every
\aleph_{1}
-saturated model
\mathcal{M}
of
T
,

\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
has the independence property for
T
in
\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 9.3
.

T
has the independence property if and only if there exist
0\leq s<r\leq 1
and a formula
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})

such that Definition
9.1
holds with the displayed formulas replaced by

n\in Z\Rightarrow\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{a}_{Z},\vec{b}_{n})\leq s,\qquad n\notin Z\Rightarrow\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{a}_{Z},\vec{b}_{n})\geq r.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Corollary
6.3
.
∎

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
\aleph_{1}
-saturated model of
T
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

T
has the independence property.

(ii)

There are real numbers
0\leq s<r\leq 1
, a formula
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
,
a tuple
\vec{a}
in
\mathcal{M}
, and an indiscernible sequence
\langle\vec{b}_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{M}
such that

\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{a},\vec{b}_{n})\leq s
for all even
n
, and
\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{a},\vec{b}_{n})\geq r
for all odd

.

(iii)

There is a formula
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
, a tuple
\vec{a}
in
\mathcal{M}
, and an indiscernible sequence
\langle\vec{b}_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{M}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\vec{a},\vec{b}_{n})
does not exist.

Proof.

It is trivial that (ii) implies (iii). If (iii) holds, then it is easily seen that (ii) holds with
\langle\vec{b}_{n}\rangle_{n]\in\mathbb{N}}
replaced by
some infinite subsequence of
\langle\vec{b}_{n}\rangle_{n]\in\mathbb{N}}
. Assume (i). Then there exist

and a formula
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
satisfying the conditions
of Lemma
9.3
. By
\aleph_{1}
-saturation and Ramsey’s theorem, (ii) holds.

Assume (ii), and let

,
\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})
,
\vec{a}
, and

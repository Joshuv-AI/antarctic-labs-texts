Abstract
. We compute the exact relaxation and Λ \Lambda -convex hull of the kinematic dynamo equations and show that they coincide. We also find the relaxation in the stationary case.



1.
Introduction

Dynamo theory
studies the mechanisms by which electrically conducting fluids generate and sustain the magnetic fields of celestial bodies
[
2
,
8
]
. In typical astrophysical applications, the magnetic Reynolds number is very large, and so one often studies the idealised limit of a perfect conductor
[
8
, p. 9]
. We also assume perfect conductivity in this paper. Dynamo theory is divided into two parts.

In the simpler
kinematic dynamo model
, the interaction of the magnetic field and the fluid is described via the induction equation and Gauss’s law for magnetism,

(1)

\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}+\nabla\times(\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{u})=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0.

In (
1
),
\mathbf{B}
is the magnetic field and
\mathbf{u}
is the fluid velocity. Sometimes, the incompressibility condition
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
is considered as a part of the kinematic model. In the
nonlinear dynamo model
, (
1
) is extended to the full ideal incompressible MHD (magnetohydrodynamic) equations by adding the Cauchy Momentum equation with Lorentz force and incompressibility,

(2)

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}=-\nabla p+(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\times\mathbf{B},\hskip 20.00003pt\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0.

The kinematic dynamo equations (
1
) also allow one to study very general
\mathbf{u}
regardless of constitutive laws, compressibility, viscosity, external forcing and so on
[
14
]
. Besides ideal or viscous MHD, one can e.g. set
\mathbf{u}
to be the electron fluid velocity in Hall MHD
[
13
]
or the velocity field in Moffatt’s magnetic relaxation equations
[
4
,
25
]
or magneto-friction
[
29
]
.

From a mathematical viewpoint, kinematic dynamo equations are also a useful toy model for MHD. While the highly difficult analysis of the back-propagation of the Lorentz force on the fluid is avoided, some of the salient features of the MHD model are still retained. In particular, one of the classical conserved quantities of MHD, magnetic helicity
\mathcal{H}_{M}(t)\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{A}(x,t)\cdot\mathbf{B}(x,t)\,\textup{d}x
, is conserved by weak solutions
\mathbf{B},\mathbf{u}\in L^{3}_{t,x}
of (
1
) e.g. under periodic boundary conditions on
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{3}
(see
[
16
,
17
]
for more information). Magnetic helicity constrains the dynamics, in particular via
Arnold’s inequality

\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\mathbf{B}(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}\,\textup{d}x\geq c\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{A}(x,t)\cdot\mathbf{B}(x,t)\,\textup{d}x
which keeps magnetic energy bounded away from zero if the initial field
\mathbf{B}_{0}
has non-zero magnetic helicity. By
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
, conservation even extends to a macroscopically averaged version, the so-called
relaxation
of (
1
). In the relaxation, solutions of (
1
) are replaced by weak limits of solutions, as we next make precise.

In the
Tartar framework

[
28
]
, the kinematic dynamo equations are decoupled into the conservation laws

(3)

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}+\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=0

(
Gauss’s law for magnetism
and
Faraday’s law of induction
) and the constitutive law
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{u}
(
ideal Ohm’s law
) which is codified into the condition that
(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{E})
takes values in the
constraint set

K=\{(B,u,E):E=B\times u\}
. One can also encode more information into the constraint set; one can e.g. prescribe kinetic and magnetic energy densities by letting
r,s>0
and considering the normalised set

K_{r,s}=\{(B,u,E)\colon E=B\times u,\,\left\lvert u\right\rvert=r,\,\left\lvert B\right\rvert=s\}.

The
relaxation of
K_{r,s}
can be defined as the smallest set
\widetilde{K_{r,s}}
such that whenever solutions of (
3
) take values in
K_{r,s}
, their weak limits take values in
\widetilde{K_{r,s}}
. Another, potentially larger (but in our case equal) variant is the smallest superset of
K_{r,s}
that is closed under weak convergence for solutions of (
3
), essentially following Tartar
[
28
]
. The relaxation describes macroscopic averages of solutions of (
1
)
[
28
]
.

We discuss the motivation for studying the relaxation of (
1
). The bare fields
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{u}
can be neither observed
[
1
]
nor predicted
[
9
]
effectively, and so most turbulence theories concentrate on various averaged quantities which are much better reproducible
[
9
]
. Lax
[
22
]
has suggested weak limits as a possible deterministic substitute for ensemble averaging, and the idea was developed further in
[
3
]
. For a recent systematic review on the topic see
[
12
]
. In the relaxation, the averaging is performed at all length scales simultaneously, whereas e.g. coarse-graining of MHD via spatial filtering
[
1
]
leads to subscale stresses which depend on the filtering resolution and need to be modelled (e.g. by eddy viscosity models
[
9
, p. 401]
).

The relaxation gives geometric insights which remain hidden in conventional averaging methods. As an example, in the kinematic dynamo equations, the orthogonality
\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{E}=0
extends to the relaxation even though the constitutive law
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{u}
breaks down; this plays a key role in showing that
L^{3}_{t,x}
is the optimal integrability under which magnetic helicity is conserved
[
16
]
. It is, at any rate, a challenging problem to find suitable physically motivated selection criteria which pick a unique
subsolution
(i.e. a triple
(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{E})
which solves (
1
) and take values in
\widetilde{K_{r,s}}
) under given initial/boundary (or other) conditions. For such a criterion in the case of the Muskat problem with flat interface (”maximal mixing”) see
[
23
,
26
,
27
]
.

The relaxation is also used in convex integration, a mathematical technique which produces a weak solution from a subsolution via an
h
-principle
[
11
]
. The use of convex integration in fluid mechanics was pioneered by De Lellis and Székelyhidi in
[
10
]
. By a refined quantitative version of the
h
-principle one can produce solutions whose averages are dictated by the subsolution (a rigorous statement was proved by Castro, Faraco and Mengual in
[
6
]
). Furthermore, instabilities in fluid dynamics, which result in a turbulent evolution intractable with the classical theory, have been successfully modelled by these techniques; see for example
[
5
,
7
]
for Rayleigh-Taylor under IPM,
[
19
,
20
]
for Rayleigh-Taylor under inhomogeneous Euler,
[
18
,
24
]
for Kelvin-Helmoltz and the references contained therein.

Precise information on the relaxation is imporant in identifying the boundary/initial conditions under which convex integration can be run in the Tartar framework
[
27
]
. It is a natural meta-conjecture in fluid dynamics that the relaxation coincides with the
\Lambda
-convex hull

K_{r,s}^{\Lambda}
, i.e., the set of points which cannot be separated from
K_{r,s}
by a
\Lambda
-convex function; see §
2
for the relevant definitions. We prove the conjecture in the case of the kinematic dynamo equations. Theorem
1
is also a step towards computing the exact relaxation of the full MHD system; a qualitative characterisation was given in
[
15
, Theorem 6.7]
.

Theorem 1
.

The relaxation, first laminate and
\Lambda
-convex hull of the kinematic dynamo equations coincide and can be written as

\displaystyle K_{r,s}^{\Lambda}=

\displaystyle\{(B,u,E)\colon\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leq r,\;\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq s,

(4)

\displaystyle B\cdot E=0,\;\left\lvert E-B\times u\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{(r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2})(s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2})}\}.

To close the introduction, we mention that (
1
) also gives the relaxation of kinematic dynamo equations in the stationary and incompressible cases (as the wave cone remains unchanged). However, in the incompressible, stationary case, the wave cone (and,
a fortiori
, the relaxation) are strictly smaller. We compute the corresponding relaxation in §
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall some definitions from the theory of differential inclusions and refer to
[
21
]
for more information. The
wave cone

\Lambda
consists of the directions
(\bar{B},\bar{u},\bar{E})\in\mathbb{R}^{9}
such that plane waves
(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{E})(\mathbf{x},t)\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}h((\mathbf{x},t)\cdot\xi)(\bar{B},\bar{u},\bar{E})
,
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, solve (
3
) for some
\xi\in(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}
. The wave cone of the (non-stationary as well as stationary) kinematic dynamo equations has been computed in
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
:

Proposition 2
.

\Lambda=\{(B,u,E)\colon B\cdot E=0\}
.

Given any compact set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the
laminates

C^{k,\Lambda}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, of
C
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle C^{0,\Lambda}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}C,

\displaystyle C^{k+1,\Lambda}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\{(\lambda z_{1}+(1-\lambda)z_{2}\colon z_{1},z_{2}\in C^{k,\Lambda},\,z_{1}-z_{2}\in\Lambda,\,\lambda\in[0,1]\}.

The
lamination convex hull
of
C
is defined as

C^{lc,\Lambda}\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\cup_{k=0}^{\infty}C^{k,\Lambda}.

Recall also that a function
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\Lambda
-convex
if
t\mapsto G(z_{0}+tz)\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex for every
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
z\in\Lambda
. The
\Lambda
-convex hull

C^{\Lambda}
consists of points
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that cannot be separated from
C
by a
\Lambda
-convex function. More precisely,
z\notin C^{\Lambda}
if and only if there exists a
\Lambda
-convex function
G
such that
G|_{C}\leq 0
but
G(z)>0
. We have
C^{\Lambda}\supset C^{lc,\Lambda}
.

3.
The proof of Theorem
1

We prove Theorem
1
through a series of lemmas, starting by the inclusion ”
\subset
” in (
1
). For this we need the following
\Lambda
-affine and convex, and hence also
\Lambda
-convex, functions.

Lemma 3
.

The function

G_{1}(B,u,E)\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}B\cdot E

is
\Lambda
-affine and vanishes in
K_{r,s}^{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

The
\Lambda
-affinity follows directly from Proposition
2
. Since
G_{1}
vanishes in
K_{r,s}
, it also vanishes in
K_{r,s}^{\Lambda}
.
∎

In Theorem
1
, the
\Lambda
-affine function
G_{1}
gives the correct bounds for the directions of the vector
E-B\times u
, and to bound the length we need the following convex function.

Lemma 4
.

The function

G_{2}(B,u,E)\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\max_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}[\alpha(\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}-r^{2})+(1-\alpha)(\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}-s^{2})+2\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\left\lvert B\times u-E\right\rvert]

is convex and vanishes in
K_{r,s}
. (Thus,
G_{2}\leq 0
in
K_{r,s}^{\Lambda}
.)

Proof.

Clearly
G_{2}|_{K_{r,s}}=0
. Denoting
G_{2}=\max_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}H_{\alpha}
, it then suffices to fix
\alpha\in[0,1]
and show that
H_{\alpha}
is convex. Fix
z_{0}=(B_{0},u_{0},E_{0})
and
z=(B,u,E)
. Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(z_{0}+tz)-H_{\alpha}(z_{0})

\displaystyle\geq ct+(\alpha\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}+(1-\alpha)\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}-2\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\left\lvert B\times u\right\rvert)t^{2}\geq ct

for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
which is independent of
t
, and so
H_{\alpha}
is convex.
∎

The functions from Lemmas
3
and
4
yield the inclusion ”
\subset
” in (
1
):

Lemma 5
.

Every triple
(B,u,E)\in K_{r,s}^{\Lambda}
satisfies

(5)

\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leq r,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq s,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\lvert B\times u-E\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{(r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2})(s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2})},\hskip 10.00002ptB\cdot E=0.

Proof.

Suppose
(B,u,E)\in K_{r,s}^{\Lambda}
. Now
\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}-r^{2}\leq G_{2}(B,u,E)\leq 0
and
\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}-s^{2}\leq G_{2}(B,u,E)\leq 0
. For the third inequality note that if
\left\lvert B\right\rvert=r
, then Proposition
4
gives, for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\left\lvert B\times u-E\right\rvert\leq[\alpha/(1-\alpha)]^{1/2}(r^{2}-\left\lvert b\right\rvert^{2})
, so that
\left\lvert B\times u-E\right\rvert=0
. The case
\left\lvert u\right\rvert=s
is similar, and so we assume that
\left\lvert B\right\rvert<r
and
\left\lvert u\right\rvert<s
. Choose
\alpha=(s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2})/(s^{2}+r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2})
in Proposition
4
. Now
G_{\alpha}(B,u,E)\leq 0
, that is,
\left\lvert B\times u-E\right\rvert\leq[(r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2})(s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2})]^{1/2}
. The equality
B\cdot E=0
follows immediately from Proposition
3
.
∎

Note that by the convexity of
G_{2}
and Jensen’s inequality, the three inequalities in (
5
) also extend to various other averaging processes (whereas the imporant equality
B\cdot E=0
, which is at the core of magnetic helicity conservation
[
17
]
, breaks down).

Our next task is to prove the inclusion ”
\supset
” in (
1
), i.e., the sharpness of the upper bound (
5
). We start with the case
E=B\times u
.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leq r
and
\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq s
. Then
(B,u,B\times u)\in K_{r,s}^{1,\Lambda}
.

Proof.

Choose
\bar{B},\bar{u}\in\textup{span}\{B,u\}^{\perp}
with
\bar{B}\times\bar{u}=0
,
\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}=r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}
and
\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{2}=s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle(B,u,B\times u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(B+\bar{B},u+\bar{u},(B+\bar{B})\times(u+\bar{u}))

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(B-\bar{B},u-\bar{u},(B-\bar{B})\times(u-\bar{u}))\mathrel{=\mkern-0.25mu:}\frac{z_{1}+z_{2}}{2}

and
z_{1}-z_{2}=(\bar{B},\bar{u},B\times\bar{u}+\bar{B}\times u)\in\Lambda
.
∎

The case
\left\lvert B\right\rvert<r
,
\left\lvert u\right\rvert<s
,
E-B\times u\neq 0
requires more work. In Lemma
7
we give a somewhat explicit characterisation of the first laminate
K^{1,\Lambda}_{r,s}
; the proof of formula (
1
) is then completed by showing that whenever
E\perp B
is as indicated in Theorem
1
, we can write
E=B\times u+\bar{B}\times\bar{u}/[\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert]
for some
\bar{B},\bar{u}
satisfying (
7
)–(
9
).

Lemma 7
.

Suppose
\left\lvert B\right\rvert<r
and
\left\lvert u\right\rvert<s
. Then
(B,u,E)\in K^{1,\Lambda}_{r,s}
if and only if there exist
\bar{B},\bar{u}\neq 0
such that

(6)

\displaystyle E=B\times u+\sqrt{(r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2})(s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2})}\frac{\bar{B}\times\bar{u}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert},

(7)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}=\frac{r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}}{s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}}\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{2}=4(r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}),

(8)

\displaystyle\left\lvert B\right\rvert\cos\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}=\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}}{s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\cos\alpha_{u,\bar{u}},

(9)

\displaystyle B\cdot\bar{B}\times\bar{u}=0,

where we set
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\sin\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}=\left\lvert B\right\rvert\cos\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}=0
if
B=0
and similarly for
u
.

Proof.

A general
\Lambda
-convex combination of two elements of
K_{r,s}
, with
\left\lvert B\right\rvert<r
and
\left\lvert u\right\rvert<s
, is of the form

(10)

\displaystyle\lambda V_{1}+\mu V_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda(B+\mu\bar{B},u+\mu\bar{u},(B+\mu\bar{B})\times(u+\mu\bar{u}))

\displaystyle+\mu(B-\lambda\bar{B},u-\lambda\bar{u},(B-\lambda\bar{B})\times(u-\lambda\bar{u}))

\displaystyle=(B,u,B\times u+\lambda\mu\bar{B}\times\bar{u}),

where
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
,
\lambda+\mu=1
,

(11)

\left\lvert B+\mu\bar{B}\right\rvert=r,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\lvert B-\lambda\bar{B}\right\rvert=r,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\lvert u+\mu\bar{u}\right\rvert=s,\hskip 10.00002pt\left\lvert u-\lambda\bar{u}\right\rvert=s,\hskip 10.00002ptB\cdot\bar{B}\times\bar{u}=0.

(In particular,
\bar{B},\bar{u}\neq 0
.)

We intend to show that (
11
) is equivalent to (
7
)–(
9
). We first assume that (
11
) holds and aim to prove (
7
). Squaring and subtracting on both sides of the first two equations in (
11
) and moving terms (and recalling that
\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2}=\lambda-\mu
),

(12)

2B\cdot\bar{B}=(\lambda-\mu)\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}=(2\lambda-1)\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}.\hskip 20.00003pt\Longrightarrow\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{B\cdot\bar{B}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}.

We next rewrite

\displaystyle r^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert B+\mu\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}=\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}-(2B\cdot\bar{B}-\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2})\frac{B\cdot\bar{B}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}+\frac{(2B\cdot\bar{B}-\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2})^{2}}{4\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}

(13)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{4}-4(B\cdot\bar{B})^{2}}{4\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}

and similarly
\lambda=1/2+u\cdot\bar{u}/\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{2}
and
s^{2}=\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}+[\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{4}-4(u\cdot\bar{u})^{2}]/[4\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{2}]
. We conclude that

(14)

\frac{r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}=\frac{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{4}-4(B\cdot\bar{B})^{2}}{4\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{4}}=\lambda\mu=\frac{\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{4}-4(u\cdot\bar{u})^{2}}{4\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{4}}=\frac{s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{2}}.

Now (
14
) immediately implies the first equality in (
7
), and the second one follows from (
3
):

\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}=4\left(r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{(B\cdot\bar{B})^{2}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}\right)=4(r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}).

Finally, (
8
) follows from (
12
), the equality
\lambda=1/2+u\cdot\bar{u}/\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert^{2}
and (
14
):

\left\lvert B\right\rvert\cos\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}=\frac{B\cdot\bar{B}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert}=\frac{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert}\frac{u\cdot\bar{u}}{\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert}=\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}}{s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\cos\alpha_{u,\bar{u}}.

Conversely, if (
7
)–(
9
) hold, we intend to show that (
11
) holds with the choice
\lambda=1/2+B\cdot\bar{B}/\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}
. First note that
\lambda\in[0,1]
since (
7
) yields

\frac{\left\lvert B\cdot\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}=\frac{\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}\leq\frac{r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert^{2}}=\frac{1}{4}.

Then (
3
) holds, and similarly
\left\lvert B-\lambda\bar{B}\right\rvert=r
and
\left\lvert u+\mu\bar{u}\right\rvert=\left\lvert u-\lambda\bar{u}\right\rvert=s
. The identity (
6
) is obtained by noting that (
14
) yields

\lambda\mu=\frac{\sqrt{(r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2})(s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2})}}{\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert}.

∎

Recall that our aim is to prove (
1
). When
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leq r
,
\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq s
and
\bar{E}\in\bar{B}(0,1)\setminus\{0\}
with
B\cdot\bar{E}=0
, our aim is, therefore, to find
\bar{B}
and
\bar{u}
satisfying
\bar{B}\times\bar{u}/(\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert)=\bar{E}
along with (
7
)–(
9
).

Proof of (
1
).

Suppose first
B\neq 0
. Now
\bar{B},\bar{u}\neq 0
satisfy
\bar{B}\times\bar{u}/[|\bar{B}||\bar{u}|]=\bar{E}
if and only if

(15)

\bar{B},\bar{u}\in\textup{span}\{B,B\times\bar{E}\},\hskip 20.00003pt\sin\alpha_{\bar{B},\bar{u}}=|\bar{E}|.

We therefore consider pairs
(\bar{B},\bar{u})
which satisfy (
15
). Given a direction
\bar{B}/\left\lvert\bar{B}\right\rvert
, note that
\bar{u}/\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert
is uniquely fixed by (
15
) once we choose
\alpha_{\bar{B},\bar{u}}\in[0,2\pi)
to be minimal. Also note that
\{B,\bar{E},B\times\bar{E}\}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Our task is to show that (
8
) can be satisfied simultaneously with
\sin\alpha_{\bar{B},\bar{u}}=\left\lvert\bar{E}\right\rvert
; then (
7
) is obtained simply by scaling
\bar{B}
and
\bar{u}
and (
9
) holds automatically since
\bar{B},\bar{u}\in\textup{span}\{B,B\times\bar{E}\}
.

Aiming to solve (
8
), we define
G\colon[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{R}
by

G(\alpha_{B,\bar{B}})\mathrel{:\mkern-0.25mu=}\left\lvert B\right\rvert\cos\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}-\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}-\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{2}}{s^{2}-\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{2}}}\left\lvert u\right\rvert\cos\alpha_{u,\bar{u}}.

Denoting
\alpha_{B,\bar{B}_{1}}=\pi/2
and
\alpha_{B,\bar{B}_{2}}=3\pi/2
, we have
\cos\alpha_{B,\bar{B}_{1}}=\cos\alpha_{B,\bar{B}_{2}}=0
and
\bar{u}_{2}/|\bar{u}_{2}|=-\bar{u}_{1}/|\bar{u}_{1}|
. Therefore,
G(\pi/2)=-G(3\pi/2)
. By Bolzano’s theorem,
G(\alpha_{B,\bar{B}})=0
for some
\alpha_{B,\bar{B}}\in[\pi/2,3\pi/2]
, that is, (
8
) holds.

We finish the proof by covering the case
B=0
. We choose
\bar{u}\in\{u,\bar{E}\}^{\perp}\neq\{0\}
and then select
\bar{B}\neq 0
such that
\bar{B}\times\bar{u}/(|\bar{B}|\left\lvert\bar{u}\right\rvert)=\bar{E}
. Again, (
8
) is satisfied, (
7
) follows by scaling and (
9

∎

4.
The relaxation of the stationary model under incompressibility

The
\Lambda
-convex hull
K_{r,s}^{\Lambda}
remains unchanged if we incorporate the incompressibility condition
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
or consider stationary solutions; in each case, the wave cone is given by Proposition
2
. For completeness, we also compute the
\Lambda
-convex hull for the stationary kinematic dynamo equations under incompressibility. In this case, the wave cone, which we denote by
\Lambda_{s}
, is smaller.

Proposition 8
.

\Lambda_{s}=\{(B,u,E)\colon B\cdot E=u\cdot E=0\}
.

Proof.

First suppose
B\cdot E=u\cdot E=0
. Our aim is to find
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(16)

B\cdot\xi=u\cdot\xi=0,\hskip 20.00003ptE\times\xi=0.

First, if
B\times u\neq 0
, then
\{B,u,B\times u\}
is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Thus
B\cdot E=u\cdot E=0
implies
E=k\,B\times u
for some
k\in\mathbb{R}
, so that we can choose
\xi=B\times u
. If
B\times u=0
and
E\neq 0
, we set
\xi=E
. If
B\times u=E=0
, we choose any
\xi\in\{B,u\}^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
.

Suppose then
(B,u,E)\in\Lambda
so that (
16
) holds for some
\xi\neq 0
. Now
E\times\xi=0
yields
E=k\xi
for some
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus
B\cdot E=u\cdot E=0
by (
16
).
∎

The relaxation, first laminate and
\Lambda_{s}
-convex hull of the stationary kinematic dynamo equations under incompressibility coincide and can be written as

\displaystyle K_{r,s}^{\Lambda_{s}}=

\displaystyle\{(B,u,E)\colon\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leq r,\;\left\lvert u\right\rvert\leq s,

When
\left\lvert B\right\rvert\leq r

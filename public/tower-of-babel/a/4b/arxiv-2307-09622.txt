Nonlinear elliptic eigenvalue problems in cylindrical domains becoming unbounded in one direction
Rama Rawat, Haripada Roy, Prosenjit Roy

Abstract
. The aim of this work is to characterize the asymptotic behaviour of the first eigenfunction of the generalised p p -Laplace operator with mixed (Dirichlet and Neumann) boundary conditions in cylindrical domains when the length of the cylindrical domains tends to infinity. This generalises an earlier work of Chipot et.al. [Asymptotics of eigenstates of elliptic problems with mixed boundary data on domains tending to infinity. Asymptot. Anal., 85(3-4):199–227, 2013] where the linear case p = 2 p=2 is studied. Asymptotic behavior of all the higher eigenvalues of the linear case and the second eigenvalues of general case (using topological degree) for such problems is also studied.

Introduction

The asymptotic analysis of problems set in cylindrical domains which become unbounded in one or several directions for various partial differential equations has been carried out by several authors since the pioneering work of Chipot
[
13
]
. In this paper, we revisit one of these problems which is set in cylindrical domains becoming unbounded in one direction for the generalised p-Laplacian with mixed boundary conditions. This continues the work in
[
13
]
, which concerns the asymptotic analysis of the linear case
p=2
with mixed (Dirichlet-Neumann) boundary conditions. We now briefly describe the setting.

Consider
\Omega_{\ell}=(-\ell,\ell)\times\omega
, where
\ell

\omega
is an open bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
n\geq 2

\Omega_{\ell}
orthogonal to

A_{12}^{t}(X_{2})&A_{22}(X_{2})\end{pmatrix},\ X_{2}\in\omega,

where

is an

( precise definitions are given in next section). For

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\textrm{div}\left(|A(X_{2})\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}A(X_{2})\nabla u_{\ell}\right)=\lambda_{D}(\Omega_{\ell})|u_{\ell}|^{p-2}u_{\ell}\ \textrm{in}\ \Omega_{\ell},\\

(1.2)

We now describe the problem which will be our main focus in this paper. Consider the following eigenvalue problem for the generalised p-Laplacian with mixed (Dirichlet and Neumann) boundary conditions:

\begin{cases}-\textrm{div}\left(|A(X_{2})\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}A(X_{2})\nabla u_{\ell}\right)=\lambda_{M}(\Omega_{\ell})|u_{\ell}|^{p-2}u_{\ell}\ \textrm{in}\ \Omega_{\ell},\\

2.
Preliminaries

Let
V
be a Banach space. Define the collection

\mathcal{A}=\left\{A\subset V:A\ \mathrm{is}\ \mathrm{closed}\ \mathrm{and}\ A=-A\right\}

of all closed and symmetric subsets of
V
. Let
\gamma:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{0,\infty\}
be defined by

\gamma(A)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{inf}\left\{m:\exists\,h:A\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\},h\ \mathrm{is}\ \mathrm{continuous}\ \mathrm{and}\ h(-u)=-h(u)\right\}\\
\infty,\ \mathrm{if}\ \{\cdots\}=\phi,\ \mathrm{in}\ \mathrm{particular\,if}\ A\ni 0,\end{cases}

for
A\neq\phi
and
\gamma(\phi)=0
.
\gamma(A)
is called the Krasnoselskii’s genus of the set
A
. We summarise some important properties of the Krasnoselskii’s genus in the following :

Proposition 2.1
.

(i)

\gamma
is a monotone sub-additive continuous map and
\gamma(A)
is finite for any compact symmetric subset of
V\setminus\{0\}
.

(ii)
Let
\Omega
be any bounded symmetric neighborhood of the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then
\gamma(\partial\Omega)=m
.

(iii)
Let
A
be a compact symmetric subset of a Hilbert space
V
with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}
and let
\gamma(A)=m<\infty
. Then
A
contains at least
m
mutually orthogonal vectors, i.e.
\exists u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m}\in A
such that
(u_{j},u_{k})_{V}=0
for
j\neq k
.

Suppose
E
is an even functional on
V
i.e.,
E(-u)=E(u)
,
u\in V
. A sequence
(u_{m})
is called a Palais-Smale sequence for
E
, if
|E(u_{m})|\leq c
uniformly on
m
, while
\lVert DE(u_{m})\rVert\rightarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, where
DE(u)
is the Fréchet derivative of the functional
E
at
u
.
E
is said to satisfy the Palais-Smale (P.S) condition if any Palais-Smale sequence has a strongly convergent subsequence. The following theorem assures the existence of the critical points and characterizes the critical values of
E
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(i)
Suppose
E
is an even functional on a complete symmetric
C^{1,1}
-manifold
M\subset V\setminus\{0\}
of some Banach space
V
. Also suppose
E
satisfies (P.S) condition and is bounded from below on
M
.
Let
\hat{\gamma}(M)=\mathrm{sup}\left\{\gamma(K):K\subset M\ \mathrm{compact}\ \mathrm{and}\ \mathrm{symmetric}\right\}.
Then the functional
E
possesses at least
\hat{\gamma}(M)\leq\infty
pairs of critical points.

(ii)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be defined as above. For any
k\leq\hat{\gamma}(M)
consider the family

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{A}:A\subset M,\gamma(A)\geq k\right\}.

If

\beta_{k}=\underset{A\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}{\mathrm{inf}}\,\underset{u\in A}{\mathrm{sup}}\,E(u)

is finite, then
\beta_{k}
is a critical value of
E
.

]

\int_{\omega}|A_{22}\nabla_{X_{2}}u\cdot\nabla_{X_{2}}u|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}A\nabla_{X_{2}}u\cdot\nabla_{X_{2}}v=\mu(\omega)\int_{\omega}|u|^{p-2}uv,\ \forall v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega).\end{cases}

The following characterization of the first eigenvalues

of (

) and
\mu^{1}(\omega)
of (

) is well known:

(2.6)

\lambda_{D}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\ell}),u\neq 0}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|u|^{p}}.

(2.7)

\mu^{1}(\omega)=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega),u\neq 0}\frac{\int_{\omega}|A_{22}\nabla_{X_{2}}u\cdot\nabla_{X_{2}}u|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\int_{\omega}|u|^{p}}=\frac{\int_{\omega}|A_{22}\nabla_{X_{2}}W\cdot\nabla_{X_{2}}W|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\int_{\omega}|W|^{p}}.

It is also a known fact (see

) that both

and

are simple and the corresponding eigenfunctions do not change sign. Here

is the first normalised non-negative eigenfunction of (
2.5
), considered earlier in Theorem
2
.

The corresponding weak form of the mixed boundary value problem (
1.4
) is the following:

(2.8)

\begin{cases}u\in V(\Omega_{\ell}),\\
\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=\lambda_{M}(\Omega_{\ell})\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|u|^{p-2}uv,\ \forall v\in V(\Omega_{\ell})\end{cases}

and the first eigenvalue is given in terms of the Rayleigh quotient as

(2.9)

\lambda_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})=\inf_{u\in V(\Omega_{\ell}),u\neq 0}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|u|^{p}}

In this case as well, the eigenvalue
\lambda_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})
is simple and the corresponding eigenfunction
u_{\ell}
have constant sign.

3.
Main result on the asymptotic behavior of the first eigenfunction

Proof of Theorem

1.1
.

Let
0<\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell-1
. Define the function
\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}=\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}(x_{1})

Clearly,
\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\ell})

2.6

\lambda_{D}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{p}|u_{\ell}|^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla(\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell})\cdot\nabla(\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell})|^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}+2\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}+u_{\ell}^{2}A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{2}A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}+A\nabla(\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}^{2})\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}.

On R.H.S we use the inequality
(a+b)^{q}\leq a^{q}+q2^{q-1}(b^{q}+a^{q-1}b)
for

q\geq 1

(3.1)

\lambda_{D}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{p}|u_{\ell}|^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{p}\\
+\frac{p}{2}C_{0}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\left(|A\nabla(\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}^{2})\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}+|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla(\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}^{2})\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|\right),

where

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{p}\\

Using the fact that
u_{\ell}
is the first eigenfunction of (
2.8

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{p}\leq\lambda_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{p}|u_{\ell}|^{p}+p\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{p-1}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}||u_{\ell}|.

Now we estimate the second integral on R.H.S of (
3.1
).

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla(\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}^{2})\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|2\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}+u_{\ell}^{2}A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{0}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\frac{p}{2}}|u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}+C_{0}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|u_{\ell}|^{p}.

Finally the last integral on R.H.S of (
3.1
) gives

(3.4)

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla(\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}^{2})\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|\\
=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|2\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}u_{\ell}A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}+u_{\ell}^{2}A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|\\
\leq 2\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}||u_{\ell}|+\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u_{\ell}^{2}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|.

Combining (
3.1
), (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) and using

(3.5)

\left(\lambda_{D}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})-\lambda_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})\right)\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}^{p}|u_{\ell}|^{p}\leq p\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}||u_{\ell}|\,+\\
pC_{0}^{3}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\,+\frac{p}{2}C_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|u_{\ell}|^{p}\,+\\
pC_{0}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}||u_{\ell}|\,+\frac{p}{2}C_{0}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|u_{\ell}^{2}

I_{3}=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|u_{\ell}|^{p},

I_{4}=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}\cdot\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|A\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|u_{\ell}^{2}

and
C_{1}=(1+C_{0})
,

,
C_{3}=\frac{1}{2}C_{0}^{2}
and

By the Hölder’s inequality, and then by the Poincaré inequality (

I_{2}\leq\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|A\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|\nabla\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\lVert A\rVert_{\infty}^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{D_{\ell^{\prime}}}|\nabla u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}|u_{\ell}|^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Using the Poincaré inequality (

3.8

\int_{\Omega_{\infty}^{+}}|A\nabla v_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla v_{\epsilon}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-p\epsilon x_{1}}\right)\Big[\mu^{1}(\omega)+\epsilon C\int_{\omega}|A_{22}\nabla_{X_{2}}W\cdot\nabla_{X_{2}}W|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\\

\tilde{\lambda}_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell}^{\pm})=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}^{\pm}}|A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}:u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\ell}^{\pm}),\int_{\Omega_{\ell}^{\pm}}|u|^{p}=1,u=0\ \textrm{on}\ \gamma_{\ell}^{\pm}\cup\Gamma_{\ell}^{\pm}\right\}.

in the half cylinders

\Omega_{\ell}^{+}=(0,\ell)\times\omega\ \mathrm{and}\ \Omega_{\ell}^{-}=(-\ell,0)\times\omega,

We have

Proof.

It is enough to prove (

A similar argument shows

,
\forall

\ell>0

The following space

(4.8)

V_{s}(\Omega_{\infty}^{+})=\left\{u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{\infty}^{+})\cap V(\Omega_{\infty}^{+}):\exists M=M(u)>0\ \textrm{such that}\ u=0\ \textrm{on}\ (M,\infty)\times\omega\right\}

is clearly dense in

Define

and let
\epsilon>0

(4.9)

\lVert\tilde{u}-\tilde{v_{n}}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty}^{+})}\leq\lVert\tilde{u}-v_{n}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty}^{+})}+\lVert v_{n}-\frac{v_{n}}{\lVert v_{n}\rVert_{L^{p}}}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty}^{+})}\\
=\lVert\tilde{u}-v_{n}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty}^{+})}+\left|1-\frac{1}{\lVert v_{n}\rVert_{L^{p}}}\right|\lVert v_{n}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\infty}^{+})}<\epsilon.

|a^{p}-b^{p}|\leq 2p|a-b|\mathrm{max}\left\{a^{p-1},b^{p-1}\right\}

I_{n}=\left|\int_{\Omega_{\infty}^{+}}\left(|A\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}|^{\frac{p}{2}}-|A\nabla\tilde{v_{n}}\cdot\nabla\tilde{v_{n}}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)\right|\\

Using (
4.9

(4.12)

a_{11}G_{\epsilon}^{2}-2(A_{12}\cdot\nabla W)G_{\epsilon}+A_{22}\nabla W\cdot\nabla W\rightarrow A_{22}\nabla W\cdot\nabla W-\frac{A_{12}\cdot\nabla W}{a_{11}}

as

The following inequality holds for any two vectors

|b|^{q}\geq|a|^{q}+q\langle|a|^{q-2}a,b-a\rangle.

Using this we obtain

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}^{-}}|v_{\ell}^{\epsilon}|^{p}=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}^{-}}|W(X_{2})-x_{1}\rho_{\ell}(X_{2})G_{\epsilon}(X_{2})|^{p}\\
\geq\int_{\Omega_{\ell}^{-}}|W(X_{2})|^{p}-p\int_{\Omega_{\ell}^{-}}|W(X_{2})|^{p-1}x_{1}\rho_{\ell}(X_{2})G_{\epsilon}(X_{2})=\ell+p\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\int_{\omega}|W|^{p-1}\rho_{\ell}G_{\epsilon}.

M_{\ell}=\left\{u\in V(\Omega_{\ell}):\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u^{2}=1\right\}

of the Banach space
V(\Omega_{\ell})
and with the even functional

E_{\ell}(u)=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u

on
M_{\ell}
. Then
\beta_{k}(\Omega_{\ell})=\lambda_{M}^{k}(\Omega_{\ell})
, where
\lambda_{M}^{k}(\Omega_{\ell})
is the
k
-th eigenvalue of the problem (
1.4
) for
p=2
.

Proof.

Let
\left\{u_{\ell}^{1},u_{\ell}^{2},\cdots u_{\ell}^{k}\right\}
be the first
k
normalised eigenfunctions of the problem (
1.4
). It is well known that
u_{\ell}^{i}
’s are mutually orthogonal and

(6.1)

\lambda_{M}^{k}(\Omega_{\ell})=\underset{\underset{||u||_{L^{2}}=1}{u\in\mathrm{sp}\{u_{\ell}^{1},\cdots u_{\ell}^{k}\}}}{\mathrm{max}}\,E_{\ell}(u)=\underset{\underset{\mathrm{dim}V=k}{V\subset V(\Omega_{\ell})}}{\mathrm{min}}\,\underset{\underset{||u||_{L^{2}}=1}{u\in V}}{\mathrm{max}}\,E_{\ell}(u).

Consider the subspace
V_{0}=\mathrm{sp}\left\{u_{\ell}^{1},u_{\ell}^{2},\cdots,u_{\ell}^{k}\right\}
of
V(\Omega_{\ell})
. Define a closed symmetric subset
A_{0}
of
M_{\ell}
as

A_{0}=V_{0}\cap M_{\ell}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}u_{\ell}^{i}:\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}u_{\ell}^{i}\right)^{2}=1\right\}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}u_{\ell}^{i}:\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}^{2}=1\right\}.

Clearly,
V_{0}\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
A_{0}
is homeomorphic to the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{k},
and therefore Proposition
2.1
gives
\gamma(A_{0})=k
. Now, definition of
\beta_{k}(\Omega_{\ell})
gives

\beta_{k}(\Omega_{\ell})\leq\underset{u\in A_{0}}{\mathrm{sup}}\,E_{\ell}(u)=\underset{\underset{||u||_{L^{2}}=1}{u\in\mathrm{sp}\{u_{\ell}^{1},\cdots u_{\ell}^{k}\}}}{\mathrm{sup}}\,E_{\ell}(u)=\lambda_{M}^{k}(\Omega_{\ell}).

For the reverse inequality, first we fix
\epsilon>0
.
\exists

A_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
such that
\underset{u\in A_{0}}{\mathrm{sup}}E_{\ell}(u)<\beta_{k}(\Omega_{\ell})+\epsilon
. Clearly by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
),
(u,v)_{A}=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla v
is an equivalent inner product on
V(\Omega_{\ell})
. By Propotion
2.1
, let
\{w_{1},w_{2},\cdots w_{k}\}
be a set of orthogonal (w.r.t
(\cdot,\cdot)_{A}
)
vectors in
A_{0}
.

Let
V_{0}=\mathrm{sp}\left\{w_{1},w_{2},\cdots w_{k}\right\}
. For any
u=\alpha_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}w_{k}\in V_{0}
with
||u||_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})}=1
, we have

(6.2)

E_{\ell}(u)=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}w_{i})\cdot\nabla(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}w_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}^{2}E_{\ell}(w_{i})\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq k}{\mathrm{max}}E_{\ell}(w_{i}).

Hence from (
6.2
)

(6.3)

\lambda_{M}^{k}(\Omega_{\ell})\leq\underset{\underset{||u||_{L^{2}}=1}{u\in V_{0}}}{\mathrm{max}}E_{\ell}(u)\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq k}{\mathrm{max}}\,E_{\ell}(w_{i})\leq\underset{u\in A_{0}}{\mathrm{sup}}\,E_{\ell}(u)<\beta_{k}(\Omega_{\ell})+\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, this completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2

For

and Remark

together gives

Again we have

,
\forall\ell_{1}\leq\ell
by considering the test function

on

and

on

, where

is the eigenfunction corresponding to

(see Remark
2
). Let

. Then
\exists

\ell_{0}>0
such that for
1\leq i\leq k

(6.5)

\tilde{\lambda}_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\frac{i}{k}\ell}^{+})<\lambda_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})+\epsilon,\quad\forall\ \frac{\ell}{k}>\ell_{0}.

For

and

, we define the following functions on

:

(6.6)

h_{i}^{-}(x_{1},X_{2})=\begin{cases}\tilde{u}_{\frac{i}{k}\ell}^{+}(x_{1}+\ell,X_{2})&\text{on $\Omega_{\ell}^{-}\setminus\Omega_{(1-\frac{i}{k})\ell}^{-}$}\\

(6.7)

h_{i}^{+}(x_{1},X_{2})=\begin{cases}\tilde{u}_{\frac{i}{k}\ell}^{-}(x_{1}-\ell,X_{2})&\text{on $\Omega_{\ell}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{(1-\frac{i}{k})\ell}^{+}$}\\

. Clearly

. Now for any

(6.8)

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u=\sum_{j=1}^{2k}\alpha_{j}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla u_{j}\cdot\nabla u_{j}+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 2k}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla u_{j}.

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla u_{j}\cdot\nabla u_{j}=\begin{cases}\tilde{\lambda}_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\frac{j+1}{2k}\ell}^{+})\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u_{j}^{2}&\text{if $j$ is odd,}\\
\tilde{\lambda}_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\frac{j}{2k}\ell}^{+})\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u_{j}^{2}&\text{if $j$ is even,}\end{cases}

and for

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla u_{j}=\begin{cases}\tilde{\lambda}_{M}^{1}(\Omega_{\frac{j+1}{2k}\ell}^{+})\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u_{i}u_{j}&\text{if $j$ is odd,}\\

Using (

Mean curvature flow from conical singularities
Otis Chodosh, J. M. Daniels-Holgate, Felix Schulze

Abstract
. We prove Ilmanen’s resolution of point singularities conjecture by establishing short-time smoothness of the level set flow of a smooth hypersurface with isolated conical singularities. This shows how the mean curvature flow evolves through asymptotically conical singularities. Precisely, we prove that the level set flow of a smooth hypersurface M n ⊂ ℝ n + 1 M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} , 2 ≤ n ≤ 6 2\leq n\leq 6 , with an isolated conical singularity is modeled on the level set flow of the cone. In particular, the flow fattens (instantaneously) if and only if the level set flow of the cone fattens.

1.2.
Strategy of proof

Optimistically, one might hope that the resolution of a conical singularity is always modeled on expanders, just as tangent flows are always modeled on self-shrinkers. Indeed, one might expect a forward monotonicity formula would control the forward blow-ups (the (subsequential) weak limits of
\lambda^{2}M(\lambda^{-1}t)
as
\lambda\to\infty
) but there appear to be serious issues to make this rigorous in the setting of isolated conical singularities (cf.
[
Ilm95a
, p. 25]
). We do note that in the setting of flows coming out of cones, Bernstein–Wang have obtained a version of forwards monotonicity
[
BW22c
]
(generalizing to the dynamical setting the relative expander entropy of Deruelle–Schulze
[
DS20
]
) and Chen
[
Che22a
]
has constructed non-self-expanding flows from cones. However, it remains unclear if/how monotonicity based methods could prove that forward blowups of outermost flows are outermost expanders (or even that they are smooth).

In this article we take a completely different approach (avoiding forwards monotonicity entirely). Instead, we find barriers that push the outermost flows onto the outermost expanders in the forward blowup limit. A closely related construction proves uniqueness of two flows with the same outermost expander blowup limit. The construction of these barriers combines two key spectral properties of an outermost expander
\Sigma
:

(1)

The outermost expander minimizes weighted area to the outside, so the linearized expander operator (cf. (
2.4
)) is non-negative
L_{\Sigma}\geq 0
. In particular, there is a positive eigenfunction
\phi_{3R}
on
\Sigma\cap B_{3R}(\mathbf{0})
with positive eigenvalue
\mu_{3R}>0
.

(2)

The outermost expander is the one-sided limit of expanders asymptotic to nearby cones, which yields a positive Jacobi field
L_{\Sigma}v=0
with
v
growing linearly at infinity.

The “interior” barrier is then formed by taking the graph over
\Sigma

f:=v+\alpha\phi_{3R}
. Because
L_{\Sigma}f=-\alpha\mu\phi_{3R}
this can be seen to be a strict barrier in

\Sigma

To prove that the flow fattens if the cone fattens (Theorem

In particular, this proves that the outermost flows have forward blowup at

2.4.
Density, Huisken’s monotonicity, and entropy

For
X_{0}=(\mathbf{x}_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
we consider the (
n
-dimensional) backwards heat kernel based at
X_{0}
:

(2.1)

\rho_{X_{0}}(\mathbf{x},t):=(4\pi(t_{0}-t))^{-\frac{n}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|^{2}}{4(t_{0}-t)}\right)

for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},t<t_{0}
. For
\mathcal{M}
a Brakke flow defined on
[T_{0},\infty)
,
t_{0}>T_{0}
and
0<r\leq\sqrt{T_{0}-t_{0}}
, we set

\Theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X_{0},r):=\int\rho_{X_{0}}(\mathbf{x},t_{0}-r^{2})d\mu(t_{0}-r^{2}).

Huisken’s monotonicity formula
[
Hui90
,
Ilm95b
]
implies that
r\mapsto\Theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X_{0},r)
is non-decreasing (and constant only for a shrinking self-shrinker centered at
X_{0}
). In particular we can define the density of
\mathcal{M}
at such
X_{0}
by

\Theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X_{0}):=\lim_{r\searrow 0}\Theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X_{0},r).

We call an integral Brakke flow
\mathcal{M}

unit-regular
if
\mathcal{M}
is smooth in a forwards-backwards space-time neighborhood of any space-time point
X
with
\Theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X)=1
. Note that we can then write
\sing\mathcal{M}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}:\Theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X)>1\}
. Note that by
[
SW20
, Theorem 4.2]
the class of unit-regular integral Brakke flows is closed under the convergence of Brakke flows. Furthermore, combining
[
SW20
, Lemma 4.1]
and
[
Whi05
]
it follows that there is
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, depending only on dimension, such that every point
X\in\sing\mathcal{M}
has
\Theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X)\geq 1+\varepsilon_{0}
. Upper semi-continuity of density then implies that
\sing\mathcal{M}
is closed.

2.5.
Cones and Self-Expanders

Consider
S\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
a smooth, embedded, closed hypersurface. We then call the cone over
S
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(S)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
smooth
. We say that
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a
smooth hypersurface with a conical singularity at
\mathbf{x}_{0}
modelled on the cone
\mathcal{C}
if:

(1)

M\backslash\{\mathbf{x}_{0}\}
is a smooth (embedded) hypersurface,

(2)

\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\rho\cdot(M-\mathbf{x}_{0})=\mathcal{C}
,

where the convergence is in
C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\backslash\{0\})
. Note that a hypersurface with conical singularities is admissible and smoothable in the sense of Section
2.3
, see also
[
CCMS24a
, Appendix E]
.

Similarly, we say that a hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is
(smoothly) asymptotic
to
\mathcal{C}
if

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\rho\cdot M=\mathcal{C}

in
C^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\backslash\{0\})
.

A natural class of solutions to mean curvature flow, starting from an initial (smooth) cone
\mathcal{C}
, are
self-similarly expanding solutions
, i.e. solutions given by

(2.2)

t\mapsto\sqrt{t}\cdot\Sigma

for
t>0
, where
\Sigma
is asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
. These solutions are invariant under parabolic rescalings
forward in time
. The condition that (
2.2
) is a mean curvature flow yields an elliptic equation for
\Sigma
, given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma}(\mathbf{x})-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}=0.

We call
\Sigma
a
self-expander
and denote the corresponding immortal solution to mean curvature flow by
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}
. Alternatively, self-expanders are critical points (under compact perturbations) of the expander functional

\mathcal{E}(M)=\int_{M}e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\,.

We call a self-expander
\Sigma
stable if the second variation of
\mathcal{E}
is non-negative under compact perturbations, i.e. if

\int_{\Sigma}\varphi(-L_{\Sigma}\varphi)\,e^{\frac{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}{4}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\geq 0

for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
, where
L_{\Sigma}
is the corresponding Jacobi operator given by

(2.4)

L_{\Sigma}=\Delta_{\Sigma}+\frac{\mathbf{x}}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}-\frac{1}{2}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}.

Note that a stable expander
\Sigma
becomes strictly stable when restricting to any compact subset
K\subset\Sigma
. Denoting
\Sigma_{R}:=\Sigma\cap B_{R}(\mathbf{0})
for
R\in(0,\infty)
, this implies that there exists a positive first eigenfunction
\phi_{R}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma_{R})
(unique up to scaling) solving

(2.5)

\begin{cases}L_{\Sigma}\phi_{R}+\mu_{R}\phi_{R}=0\ &\mathrm{in}\ \Sigma_{R}\\
\phi_{R}=0\ &\mathrm{on}\ \partial\Sigma_{R}\,,\end{cases}

where
R\mapsto\mu_{R}>0
is monotonically decreasing in
R
. We will scale such that
\int_{\Sigma_{R}}\phi_{R}^{2}e^{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}/4}=1
, ensuring that
\phi_{R}
is unique.

Linearising the expander equation (
2.3
) yields solutions to the linearized equation, i.e. functions
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that
L_{\Sigma}u=0
. We call such a function a
Jacobi field
.

We further recall the following decay estimate.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Din20
, Lemma 5.3]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
denote an expander asymptotic to a smooth cone. Then there is
R>0
sufficiently large so that
\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(\mathbf{0})
can be written as a normal graph over
\mathcal{C}
with the graphical height function
\sigma=o(|\mathbf{x}|^{-1})

as
\mathbf{x}\to\infty
.

This improves the trivial
\sigma=o(|\mathbf{x}|)
estimate via comparison with large spheres.

2.6.
The level set flow of a cone and the outermost expanders

For a smooth cone
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(S)
with
\mathcal{C}=\partial W
for
W
a closed set, we define
\Gap(\mathcal{C})
to be the level set flow of the cone
\mathcal{C}
at time
t=1
. Since the level set flow is unique, and
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under scaling, it follows that the level set flow of
\mathcal{C}
is given by
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}\cdot\Gap(\mathcal{C})
for
t\in(0,\infty)
.

The analogous statement to Proposition
2.2
holds also for the level-set flow of smooth hypercones, see
[
CCMS24a
, Theorem E.2]
. Furthermore, in
[
CCMS24a
, Theorem 8.21]
it was shown that the outermost/innermost flows from a cone (in low dimensions) are modelled on smooth expanders, minizing the expander functional
\mathcal{E}
from the outside. (For
n=2
smoothness had been shown by Ilmanen
[
Ilm95a
]
.) We will refer to these as the

. We summarize these facts as follows:

Theorem 2.4

(
[
Ilm95a
,
CCMS24a
]
)
.

For
2\leq n\leq 6
, let
\mathcal{C}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth cone. Then, there are smooth expanders
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
, smoothly asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
. The expanders
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
describe the level set flow of
\mathcal{C}
in the following sense:

•

If the level set flow of
\mathcal{C}
does not fatten, then
\Gap(\mathcal{C})=\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Sigma
.

•

If the level set flow of
\mathcal{C}
does fatten, then
\Sigma\cap\Sigma^{\prime}=\emptyset
and
\Gap(\mathcal{C})
is the region between
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
, i.e.
\partial\!\Gap(\mathcal{C})=\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Finally,
\Sigma
minimizes the expander functional
\mathcal{E}
to the outside (relative to
W
) on compact sets. Similarly,
\Sigma^{\prime}
minimizes
\mathcal{E}
to the inside.

Note that the property that
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
minimize
\mathcal{E}
from one side implies that both
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
are stable expanders. Furthermore, for
n\geq 7
,
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
could

have singular set of dimension

. We say that the

\mathcal{C}
are smooth
if the singular set is empty (so this always holds for

\mathcal{C}
are

carries over to prove the remaining assertions in the theorem.

Let
\Omega=W\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(S)

be the smooth unit normal vectorfield to

in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
pointing to the outside of
\Omega

\varepsilon>0

(S_{s})_{-\varepsilon<s<\varepsilon}
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that

and

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial s}S_{s}\Big|_{s=0}=\psi\cdot\nu_{S}\,.

We consider the cones
\mathcal{C}_{s}:=\mathcal{C}(S_{s})
and the corresponding outermost expanders
\Sigma_{s},\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}
. Note that by construction of the outermost flows of
\mathcal{C}_{s}
it follows that for
s>t
the outermost expander
\Sigma_{s}
lies strictly to the outside (with respect to
W
) of
\Sigma_{t}
and
\Sigma_{s}\to\Sigma_{t}
smoothly for

. Similarly for
s<t

\Sigma^{\prime}_{t}

and
\Sigma^{\prime}_{t}\to\Sigma^{\prime}_{s}

.

We denote with
\pi_{\Gamma}
the composition of the closest point projection onto
\mathcal{C}(S)
composed with the radial projection

of the cone onto its link. This is well defined on the cone over a neighborhood of

in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The next lemma then follows from the above discussion together with

and the strong maximum principle.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\psi:S\to\mathbb{R}
be a positive, smooth function. Then there is a positive Jacobi field

on
\Sigma

|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{\ell}v|=O(r^{1-\ell})

for

2.7.
Forward rescaled flow

Given a (smooth) mean curvature flow
(0,T)\ni t\mapsto\mathcal{M}(t)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
one obtains a solution to
forward rescaled mean curvature flow
by considering the rescaling

(-\infty,\log(T))\ni\tau\mapsto\tilde{M}(\tau):=e^{-\tau/2}\mathcal{M}(e^{\tau})\,,

which satisfies the evolution equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\tau}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{\tilde{M}(\tau)}(\mathbf{x})-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}.

Note that expanders are the stationary points of this evolution.

We consider

\mathbf{0}

\mathcal{C}

\partial W=\partial W^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}

\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\rho U=W

\mathcal{M}_{i},\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{i}

\mathcal{M}(0)=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(0)=\mathcal{H}^{n}\lfloor M

Theorem 4.1
.

The flow
\mathcal{M}

In particular, this shows that if

.

There is

It suffices to consider
\mathcal{M}

with

By the work of Hershkovits–White as recalled in Proposition

, so
\check{\mathcal{M}}
agrees with

.
∎

Proof of Theorem

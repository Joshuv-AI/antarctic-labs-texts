On the initial value of PageRank
Krishanu Deyasi

Abstract
Google employs PageRank to rank web pages, determining the order in which search results are presented to users based on their queries. PageRank is primarily utilized for directed networks, although there are instances where it is also applied to undirected networks. In this paper, we have applied PageRank to undirected networks, showing that a vertex’s PageRank relies on its initial value, often referred to as an intrinsic, non-network contribution. We have analytically proved that when the initial value of vertices is either proportional to their degrees or set to zero, the PageRank values of the vertices become directly proportional to their degrees. Simulated and empirical data are employed to bolster our research findings. Additionally, we have investigated the impact of initial values on PageRank localization.

Introduction

Complex network tools have been used in different fields from social, technological and

Various centrality metrics have been utilized to identify pivotal vertices within networks, including degree centrality, eigenvector centrality, Katz centrality, vertex betweenness centrality, edge betweenness centrality, and closeness centrality

PageRank stands out as another crucial metric utilized in network applications such as information retrieval, link prediction, and community detection

;

.
In 1998, Brin and Page introduced the PageRank algorithm to rank web pages within their search engine
Brin and Page 1998
. The PageRank score

, of a vertex
i

n

x_{i}=\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}\frac{x_{j}}{k_{j}}+(1-\alpha)\beta_{i}.

Here,

).

.

;
Pretto 2002
;

;

. But the significance of
\beta_{i}

We have done another important study regarding the improvement of PageRank of a web page when connected to a high PageRank web page. This
is similar to the analysis of whether the neighbor of a high PageRank web page also gets a

Another way it can be thought of is whether the PageRank

Localization of the eigenvector is a common

II
Theoretical study

In the matrix form, the Equation (
1
) can be written as:

\mathbf{x}=\alpha\mathbf{AD}^{-1}\mathbf{x}+(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\beta},

(2)

where
\mathbf{D}
is the diagonal matrix with elements
D_{ii}=k_{i}
and

\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{n})^{T}
.
Equation (
2
) can be written as:

\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{I}-\alpha\mathbf{AD}^{-1})^{-1}(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\beta}.

(3)

Since
|||\alpha\mathbf{AD}^{-1}|||_{\infty}=|\alpha||||\mathbf{AD}^{-1}|||_{\infty}=\alpha<1,
Equation (
3
) can be written as the sum of an infinite series
(
Horn and Johnson 2012
, p. 350)
:

\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\right)^{i}(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\beta}.

(4)

Here
\mathbf{AD^{-1}}=\mathbf{P}
is a column stochastic matrix. Now we will show that if the network is undirected and connected, then
\mathbf{P}
is diagonalizable.

Note that
\mathbf{D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}}
is a symmetric matrix, so there exists an invertible matrix
\mathbf{R}
and a diagonal matrix
\mathbf{B}
such that

\begin{split}\mathbf{D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}}&=\mathbf{RBR^{-1}}\\
\mathbf{A}&=\mathbf{D^{1/2}RBR^{-1}D^{1/2}}\end{split}

(5)

Now,

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}&=\mathbf{AD^{-1}}\\
&=\mathbf{D^{1/2}RBR^{-1}D^{1/2}D^{-1}}~~~~~~~~~~~\text{[from Equation \ref{equ5a}]}\\
&=\mathbf{D^{1/2}RBR^{-1}D^{-1/2}}\\
&=\mathbf{D^{1/2}RB(D^{1/2}R)^{-1}}\\
&=\mathbf{QBQ^{-1}},~~~~~~~~~~~\text{where $\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{D^{1/2}R}$}\end{split}

(6)

Hence the proof.

Then
(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\beta}
can be written as a linear combination of the
eigenvectors
\mathbf{w}_{j}
of
\mathbf{P}
, as

(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\beta}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{c}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}.

(7)

Then the Equation (
4
) reduces to

\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\mathbf{P}^{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{c}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\right).

(8)

Let
1=\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\dots\geq\mu_{n}\geq-1
be the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{P}

(
Van Mieghem 2011
, p. 64)
corresponding to the
eigenvectors
\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{n}
, respectively. Then

\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{c}_{j}\mu_{j}^{i}\mathbf{w}_{j}\right).

(9)

After simplifying, we get

\mathbf{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\alpha\mu_{j}\right)^{i}\right)\mathbf{w}_{j}.

(10)

Because
|\alpha\mu_{j}|<1~\forall j=1,2,\dots,n
, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbf{x}&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\frac{1}{1-\alpha\mu_{j}}\mathbf{w}_{j}.\\
\end{split}

(11)

Therefore, the PageRank vector
\mathbf{x}
of a network is the linear sum of eigenvectors of
\mathbf{P}
. As
\mathbf{P}
is a non-negative matrix so by the Perron–Frobenius theorem
\mathbf{w}_{1}
will be the vector having entries non-negative entries
(
Horn and Johnson 2012
, p. 529)
. In particular,
\mathbf{w}_{1}
is proportional to the degree of the network
(
Van Mieghem 2011
, p. 63)
.

Remark 1
.

If the value of
\boldsymbol{\beta}
is proportional to degrees, then Equation
(
7
) becomes:

(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\beta}=c_{1}\mathbf{w}_{1}.

(12)

Therefore, Equation (
11
) reduces to

\mathbf{x}=\frac{c_{1}}{1-\alpha}\mathbf{w}_{1}.

(13)

Hence, PageRank centrality is proportional to the degree of the network.

Remark 2
.

PageRank can also be quantified successively with an initial estimation of
\mathbf{x}
from Equation (
2
).
Equation (
2
) will be,

\mathbf{x}_{1}=\alpha\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{0}+(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{\beta},

(14)

where
\mathbf{x}_{0}
is any non-negative vector (
(
Newman 2010
, p. 170)
).
If the initial value
\boldsymbol{\beta}
is zero, then from Equation (
14
), we get

\mathbf{x}_{1}=\alpha\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{0}.

(15)

After
t
iterative steps, we have

\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\left(\alpha\mathbf{P}\right)^{t}\mathbf{x}_{0}.

(16)

Now, writing
\mathbf{x}_{0}
as a linear combination of the eigenvectors
\mathbf{w}_{j}
of
\mathbf{P}
, we get

\mathbf{x}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{w}_{i},

(17)

for some appropriate choice of
c_{i}^{\prime}
. Then

\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\alpha^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{\prime}\mu_{i}^{t}\mathbf{w}_{i}.

(18)

Since
|\mu_{i}|<1
for all
i\neq 1
, we get
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\to c_{1}^{\prime}\alpha^{t}\mathbf{w}_{1}
in the limit
t\to\infty
.
As
c^{\prime}_{1}
and
\alpha
are constants and
\mathbf{w}_{1}
is proportional to degree, it follows that PageRank
centrality is proportional to the degree centrality.

IV
Localization of PageRank centrality

Localization of a vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})^{T}
means most of the weight of the vector
\mathbf{v}
consists of only a few of the elements of the vector
\mathbf{v}
. On the other hand, if the weights of the entries of the vector
\mathbf{v}
are evenly distributed over all the entries, then the vector
\mathbf{v}
is called a delocalized vector.

The extent of localization can be measured by calculating the inverse participation ratio (IPR)
Kawamoto 2016
;
Martin et al. 2014
.
The IPR of a PageRank vector
\mathbf{v}
is calculated as:

\text{IPR}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{4}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{2})^{2}}.

(19)

When IPR is of order
O(1)
as
n\to\infty
, the PageRank vector
\mathbf{v}

V
Discussion and conclusion

In this work, we have shown that PageRank centrality depends on its initial value which is also called intrinsic, non-network
contribution of the vertices. We have proved that if the initial value of PageRank is proportional to the degree of vertex then the PageRank score becomes proportional to
the degree of the vertex. Grolmusz
Grolmusz 2015
made a similar conclusion. We have also shown that if the
initial value of PageRank is zero, then it also becomes
proportional to the vertex degree of the network. Our numerical simulation on the
four model networks (ER random network, WS small-world network, BA scale-free network and duplication-divergence network) shows that the PageRank scores are more
resilient in WS small-world, BA scale-free and duplication-divergence networks than ER random network upon changing the initial value of PageRank. The instability of PageRank in the ER random network arises from its adherence to the Poisson distribution when the average degree of vertices is significantly less than the total number of nodes in the network. Otherwise, the network follows the binomial distribution
Barabási 2016
;
Barabási and Albert 1999
. Hence, the likelihood of encountering high-degree nodes in the ER random network is diminished
Barabási and Albert 1999
. PageRank in the WS small-world network is stable. The rationale we have identified is that following the rewiring of edges in the regular ring lattice to form a small-world network, the degree of each node undergoes minimal change
Barabási and Albert 1999
. Therefore, altering the initial value of PageRank does not influence the PageRank of the WS small-world network. PageRank in the BA scale-free network exhibits high stability thanks to the power law degree distribution, which increases the likelihood of encountering high-degree nodes
Barabási and Albert 1999
. In this context, it is worth noting that PageRank remains stable in the scale-free network even when the network’s edges are rewired
Ghoshal and Barabási 2011
. PageRank in the duplication-divergence network is also stable as the duplication-divergence network also follows power-law degree distribution for the retention probability
\sigma=0.4

Ispolatov et al. 2005
.
Among all real-world networks studied
here, electric, email, blog, PGP, and P2P networks, PageRank values are unstable when the initial value of PageRank is either inversely proportional to
the vertex degree or equal to one. This is because, across all these networks, the likelihood of encountering high-degree nodes is low (Figure
16
). On the other hand, PageRank on the Internet
network is highly stable for all the initial values
\boldsymbol{\beta}
because the chances of getting the high degree are high (Figure
16
).

We have shown that the PageRank centrality can show localization
and the extent of localization depends on the initial value. None of the four synthetic networks we have examined exhibit PageRank localization. Only the Internet network, among the six real-world networks, demonstrates PageRank localization. In synthetic networks,
we have seen that the IPR value of PageRank increases with the increment of

\boldsymbol{\beta}
for ER random network, WS small-world network and BA scale-free network. We did not observe such a correlation in the duplication-divergence network or any of the real-world networks examined in this study.
As there are high correlations between degree and PageRank in all synthetic and real networks, consequently the extent of localization for degree and PageRank of all the networks have similar magnitude (Table
1
). The localization of PageRank in the Internet network can be attributed to the existence of hub nodes, which are vertices with exceptionally high degrees (Figure
16
). In contrast, the other real-world networks we have examined lack any hub nodes (Figure
16
). Thus, the presence of a hub vertex in a network fosters the localization of PageRank, rather than impacting its initial value.

In conclusion, we see that the initial value of PageRank can influence the PageRank score, and consequently the ranking of the web page. So specific and careful choice of initial value may enhance PageRank. The initial value of a web page can be given based on the textual relevance of the search query
(
Newman 2010
, p. 177)
. Accordingly, one can push up PageRank of their web page by giving more relevant words on their web page. This could yield favorable results for web pages with low degrees, as there’s no influence on the ranking of highly ranked pages. The initial value of PageRank may slightly affect the inverse participation ratio, but it lacks the ability to influence the localization in PageRank centrality. Localization in PageRank centrality occurs when the network contains hub nodes.

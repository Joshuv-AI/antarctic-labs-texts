Introduction

Let

F(x,y)=\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}(\delta_{k}x-\gamma_{k}y)

be a binary form of degree
n\geq 1
, with complex coefficients, written as a product of
n
linear forms in
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
. Let

h_{F}=\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\sqrt{|\gamma_{k}|^{2}+|\delta_{k}|^{2}}

denote the
height
of
F
. We define the
fundamental region
(also known as the
fundamental domain
)
\mathcal{D}_{F}
of
F
as

\mathcal{D}_{F}=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon|F(x,y)|\leq 1\right\}.

The area
A_{F}
of the fundamental region
\mathcal{D}_{F}
plays an important role in number theory. Consider the special case when
F\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
is a binary form of degree
n\geq 3
and nonzero discriminant
D_{F}
. In 1933, Mahler
[
4
]
proved that the number of solutions
Z_{F}(m)
to the
Thue inequality

|F(x,y)|\leq m

can be approximated by
A_{F}m^{2/n}
, provided that
F
is irreducible over the rationals (here
m
is a non-negative integer). More precisely, he proved the existence of a positive real number
c_{F}
, which depends only on
F
, such that

\left|Z_{F}(m)-A_{F}m^{2/n}\right|\leq c_{F}m^{1/(n-1)}.

In 2019, Stewart and Xiao
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
proved that the number of integers
R_{F}(m)
of absolute value at most
m
which are represented by
F
is asymptotic to
W_{F}A_{F}m^{2/n}
, where
W_{F}
is a positive rational number that can be computed in terms of the
automorphism group
of
F
(see
[
6
]
for the definition of the automorphism group). Thus it is interesting to investigate how large and how small can
A_{F}
be. Motivated by this question we succeeded in proving the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F
be a binary form with complex coefficients of degree
n\geq 1
, with exactly
r
roots on the real projective line
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, counting multiplicity. Then

h_{F}^{2/n}A_{F}\geq\left(2^{1+(r/n)}\right)\pi.

(1)

For complex numbers
x
and
y
with positive real parts, let

B(x,y)=2\int\limits_{0}^{\pi/2}(\sin\theta)^{2x-1}(\cos\theta)^{2y-1}d\theta

(2)

denote the
beta function
, written in its trigonometric form. The lower bound for
A_{F}
given in Theorem
1.1
complements the upper bound for
A_{F}
found by Bean
[
1
, Theorem 1]
. Bean’s result asserts that for any binary form with complex coefficients having degree
n\geq 3
and nonzero discriminant
D_{F}
the following inequality holds:

|D_{F}|^{1/n(n-1)}A_{F}\leq 3B\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3}\right).

One of the ingredients in our proof is Jensen’s inequality, which states that

\varphi\left(\frac{1}{b-a}\int\limits_{a}^{b}f(x)dx\right)\geq\frac{1}{b-a}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\varphi(f(x))dx

(3)

for every concave function
\varphi(x)
and every real-valued function
f(x)
that is Lebesgue-integrable on an interval
[a,b]
. Another important ingredient is the polar formula for the computation of
A_{F}
. Since the curve
|F(x,y)|=1
can be expressed in polar form as

r(\theta)=\frac{1}{\left|F(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\right|^{1/n}},

from calculus we know that the area
\mathcal{D}_{F}
bounded by this curve can be computed as follows:

A_{F}=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}(\theta)d\theta=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{\left|F(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\right|^{2/n}}.

(4)

With this formula one can also notice that
A_{cF}=|c|^{-2/n}A_{F}
for any nonzero complex number
c
. Since
h_{cF}=|c|h_{F}
, we can see that the quantity
h_{F}^{2/n}A_{F}
is invariant under scaling by a nonzero complex number, i.e.,

h_{cF}^{2/n}A_{cF}=\left(|c|h_{F}\right)^{2/n}\left(|c|^{-2/n}A_{F}\right)=h_{F}^{2/n}A_{F}.

When
r=0
or
r=n
the right-hand side of the inequality (
1
) becomes independent of both
r
and
n
. In the case
r=n
there does exist a family of binary forms
F_{n,n}
such that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(h_{F_{n,n}}A_{F_{n,n}}\right)=4\pi.

This family was studied by Bean and Laugesen
[
3
]
and a binary form
F_{n,n}
is defined by

F_{n,n}(x,y)=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(x\sin\frac{k\pi}{n}-y\cos\frac{k\pi}{n}\right).

In this case, for
n\geq 3
we have

h_{F_{n,n}}=1\quad\text{and}\quad A_{F_{n,n}}=4^{1-1/n}B\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{2}\right).

In the case
r=0
the family of binary forms
F_{n,0}
defined as

F_{n,0}(x,y)=(x-iy)^{n}

satisfies
h_{F_{n,0}}^{2/n}A_{F_{n,0}}=2\pi
for every positive integer

. To see that this is the case, note that
h_{F_{n,0}}=2^{n/2}

|F(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)|=|(\cos\theta-i\sin\theta)^{n}|=\left|\exp\left(-\theta i\right)^{n}\right|=\left|\exp(-n\theta i)\right|=1.

Thus it follows from (
4

A_{F_{n,0}}=\pi
. The family
F_{n,0}
has a rather special property that the quantity
h_{F}^{2/\deg F}A_{F}
attains its smallest value when
F=cF_{n,0}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and nonzero

.

Auxiliary Results

In this section we prove two auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log(\sin(x))dx=-\frac{\pi}{2}\log 2
.

Proof.

Denote the left-hand side by
I
. Then

I=\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log(\sin(x))dx=\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-x\right)\right)dx=\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log(\cos(x))dx,

so

\displaystyle 2I

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log(\sin(x))dx+\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log(\cos(x))dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log(\sin(x)\cos(x))dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log\left(\frac{\sin(2x)}{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\log(\sin(2x))dx-\frac{\pi}{2}\log 2

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log(\sin x)dx-\frac{\pi}{2}\log 2

\displaystyle=I-\frac{\pi}{2}\log 2.

Hence
I=-\frac{\pi}{2}\log 2
. ∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For every real number
\beta
,

\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log((\sin\beta\cos\theta)^{2}+(\cos\beta\sin\theta)^{2})\ d\theta\leq-\pi\log 2.

Furthermore, the equality is attained if and only if
\beta=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{\pi}{2}k
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
I(\beta)
denote the left-hand side. Then it follows from Jensen’s inequality (
3
) with
\varphi(x)=\log x
that

\displaystyle I(\beta)

\displaystyle\leq\pi\log\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\left(\sin^{2}\beta\cos^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\beta\sin^{2}\theta\right)\ d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\log\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\sin^{2}\beta\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\cos^{2}\theta\ d\theta+\cos^{2}\beta\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta\ d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\log\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\sin^{2}\beta\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}+\cos^{2}\beta\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\pi\log 2.

Since the function
\log x
is strictly convex, the equality holds if and only if

\log((\sin\beta\cos\theta)^{2}+(\cos\beta\sin\theta)^{2})

(6)

is constant on
(0,\pi)
. This means that for any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(0,\pi)
,

\displaystyle\log((\sin\beta\cos\theta_{1})^{2}+(\cos\beta\sin\theta_{1})^{2})

\displaystyle=\log((\sin\beta\cos\theta_{2})^{2}+(\cos\beta\sin\theta_{2})^{2}),

\displaystyle(\sin\beta\cos\theta_{1})^{2}+(\cos\beta\sin\theta_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle=(\sin\beta\cos\theta_{2})^{2}+(\cos\beta\sin\theta_{2})^{2}.

We can pick
\theta_{1}=\frac{\pi}{6}
and
\theta_{2}=\frac{2\pi}{3}
to obtain:

\displaystyle\left(\sin\beta\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\cos\beta\cdot\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sin\beta\cdot-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\cos\beta\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\sin^{2}\beta+\frac{1}{4}\cos^{2}\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sin^{2}\beta+\frac{3}{4}\cos^{2}\beta,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cos^{2}\beta,

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\beta

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\beta.

This happens if and only if
\beta=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{\pi}{2}k
for some integer
k
. Plugging this value of
\beta
into (
6
), we see that the resulting function is constant and equal to
-\log 2
. Hence
I\left(\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{\pi}{2}k\right)=-\pi\log 2
for every integer
k
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Suppose that the binary form
F(x,y)
has exactly
r
roots
(\gamma_{j}\colon\delta_{j})
on
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, counting multiplicity. Write

F(x,y)=\prod\limits_{j=0}^{r-1}(\delta_{j}x-\gamma_{j}y)\prod\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}(\delta_{k}x-\gamma_{k}y),

where
\delta_{j},\gamma_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta_{k},\gamma_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
. Notice that we can rewrite
F
as follows:

F(x,y)=h_{F}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{r-1}(\delta_{j}^{\prime}x-\gamma_{j}^{\prime}y)\prod\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}(\delta_{k}^{\prime}x-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}y),

where we now have

(\gamma_{j}^{\prime})^{2}+(\delta_{j}^{\prime})^{2}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\left|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\right|^{2}=1.

In particular, notice that for
j=0,1,\ldots,r-1
each point
(\gamma_{j}^{\prime},\delta_{j}^{\prime})
lies on the unit circle, so
(\gamma_{j}^{\prime},\delta_{j}^{\prime})=(\cos\alpha_{j},\sin\alpha_{j})
for some
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. Since

\displaystyle F(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)

\displaystyle=h_{F}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{r-1}\sin\left(\theta-\alpha_{j}\right)\prod\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}(\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta),

we can use the formula (
4
) to compute
A_{F}
:

\displaystyle A_{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{|F(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)|^{2/n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h_{F}^{2/n}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt[n]{\prod\limits_{j=0}^{r-1}\csc^{2}\left(\theta-\alpha_{j}\right)}\cdot\sqrt[n]{\prod\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|^{-2}}\ d\theta.

Since the integrand has period
\pi
, we conclude that

h_{F}^{2/n}A_{F}=\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\sqrt[n]{\prod\limits_{j=0}^{r-1}\csc^{2}\left(\theta-\alpha_{j}\right)}\cdot\sqrt[n]{\prod\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|^{-2}}\ d\theta.

By Jensen’s inequality (
3
) with
\varphi(x)=\log x
,

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{h_{F}^{2/n}A_{F}}{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log\left(\sqrt[n]{\prod\limits_{j=0}^{r-1}\csc^{2}\left(\theta-\alpha_{j}\right)}\cdot\sqrt[n]{\prod\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|^{-2}}\right)\ d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\pi}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log(\csc^{2}(\theta-\alpha_{j}))d\theta+\frac{1}{n\pi}\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log\left(|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|^{-2}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{n\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log(\csc^{2}(\theta))d\theta+\frac{1}{n\pi}\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log\left(|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|^{-2}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\text{Lemma \ref{lem:logsin}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{n}\log 4+\frac{1}{n\pi}I,

where

I=\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log\left(|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|^{-2}\right)d\theta.

It remains to prove that
I\geq(n-r)\pi\log 2
. Since
\left|\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\left|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\right|^{2}=1
, there exist real numbers
\beta_{k}
such that

|\gamma_{k}^{\prime}|=\cos\beta_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad|\delta_{k}^{\prime}|=\sin\beta_{k}.

Notice that

\displaystyle|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|\cdot|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos(\pi-\theta)-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin(\pi-\theta)|

\displaystyle=|(\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta)^{2}-(\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta)^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta\right|^{2}+\left|\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\sin\beta_{k}\cos\theta)^{2}+(\cos\beta_{k}\sin\theta)^{2},

so

(|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta||\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos(\pi-\theta)-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin(\pi-\theta)|)^{-2}\geq((\sin\beta_{k}\cos\theta)^{2}+(\cos\beta_{k}\sin\theta)^{2})^{-2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle 2I

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log\left(|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|^{-2}\right)d\theta+\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log\left(|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos(\pi-\theta)-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin(\pi-\theta)|^{-2}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log\left(|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos\theta-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin\theta|\cdot|\delta_{k}^{\prime}\cos(\pi-\theta)-\gamma_{k}^{\prime}\sin(\pi-\theta)|\right)^{-2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{k=r}^{n-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\log((\sin\beta_{k}\cos\theta)^{2}+(\cos\beta_{k}\sin\theta)^{2})^{-2}d\theta

\displaystyle\text{Lemma \ref{lem:bound}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\geq 2(n-r)\pi\log 2.

In summary, we proved that

\log\left(\frac{h_{F}^{2/n}A_{F}}{\pi}\right)\geq\frac{r}{n}\log 4+\frac{n-r}{n}\log 2.

Exponentiation on both sides yields the desired inequality
h_{F}^{2/n}A_{F}\geq\left(2^{1+(r/n)}\right)\pi
.

4
The Family
F_{n,r}

In this section we derive formulas for the height and the area of the fundamental region of a binary form
F_{n,r}
defined in (
5
). By definition, the height of
F_{n,r}
is given by
h_{F_{n,r}}=2^{(n-r)/2}
. To compute
A_{F_{n,r}}
, notice that for
r\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\left|F_{n,r}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(\cos\theta-i\sin\theta)^{n-r}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{r}\left(\cos\theta\sin\frac{k\pi}{r}-\sin\theta\cos\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{r}\sin\left(\theta-\frac{k\pi}{r}\right)\right|=\left|2^{-(r-1)}\sin(r\theta)\right|,

where the last equality follows from the identity
\sin(r\theta)=2^{r-1}\prod_{k=1}^{r}\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{r}-\theta\right)
(see, for example,
[
5
, Section 2]
). By (
4
),

\displaystyle A_{F_{n,r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt[n]{4^{r-1}\csc^{2}(r\theta)}d\theta

\displaystyle=2^{2(r-1)/n-1}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt[n]{\csc^{2}(r\theta)}d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{2(r-1)/n-1}}{r}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi r}\sqrt[n]{\csc^{2}\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{2(r-1)/n-1}}{r}\cdot 4r\int\limits_{0}^{\pi/2}(\sin\theta)^{-\frac{2}{n}}d\theta.

Now, for
n=1,2
the above integral diverges, while for
n\geq 3
it follows from (
2
) that

A_{F_{n,r}}=2^{2(r-1)/n}\cdot 2\int\limits_{0}^{\pi/2}(\sin\theta)^{-\frac{2}{n}}d\theta=2^{2(r-1)/n}B\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{2}\right).

Thus,

h_{F_{n,r}}^{2/n}A_{F_{n,r}}=\begin{cases}2\pi&\text{if $r=0$,}\\
\infty&\text{if $n=1,2$ and $r>0$,}\\
2^{1+(r-2)/n}B\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{if $n>2$ and $r>0$.}\end{cases}

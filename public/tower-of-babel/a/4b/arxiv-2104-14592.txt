Abstract
. A linear system of difference equations and a nonlinear perturbation are considered. We propose sufficient conditions to ensure that the homeomorphism of topological equivalence between them is actually a C 1 C^{1} diffeomorphism. These conditions consider that the linear part satisfies a dichotomy on the positive half-line, while the perturbation satisfies boundness and Lipschitzness conditions, and anothers that are tailored for our goal. The family of dichotomies that we study consider (but are not limited to) the exponential and nonuniform exponential dichotomies. We discuss the possibility of extending this result to a higher class of differentiability.

1.
Introduction

The concept of topological equivalence between two systems, linear and nonlinear, has played an important role in the study of differential equations, mainly due to the difficulty to find analytical solutions for them. It has been more fructiferous to study a linear, simpler system and from it obtain information about the nonlinear original system. Some facts in this analysis are the search for bounded solutions and characterization of their asymptotic behaviour. The method consists in establishing a continuous correspondence between solutions of both systems.

The problem of linearization of the flow of solutions of ordinary autonomous differential equations began in the decade of 1960 with the classical P. Hartman’s theorem
[
13
]
, which ensures the existence of a local homeomorphism between a nonlinear system and and its linearization around a fixed point, under the assumption that the linear system satisfies an hyperbolicity condition. During the next decade the result was generalized to the construction of a global homeomorphism thanks to C. Pugh
[
26
]
and A. Reinfeld
[
27
]
.

The extension of the previous result is due to result of K. J. Palmer
[
18
]
, whom in 1973 studied a couple of continuous systems given by:

(1.1)

\dot{x}=A(t)x

(1.2)

\dot{y}=A(t)y+f(t,y).

He used the exponential dichotomy property associated to the nonautonomous linear system, which mimics the hyperbolocity of the autonomous framework, namely, establish the existence of stable and unstable manifolds for solutions. Moreover, Palmer’s result considers a family of nonlinear perturbations for the system (
1.2
) that satisfy smallness and Lipschitz properties. Palmer’s result was the first to establish the topological equivalence (a more precise definition will be provided later) between the systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). However, most often a exponential dichotomy represents highly restrictive hypothesis, which is why during the next decade
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
the same author generalized his result to consider a wider family of dichotomies and perturbations.

Contemporaneously to K. J. Palmer, it was already being developed the analogous theory in the discrete framework (we refer the reader to C. Coffman
[
8
]
). These contributions were retaken in lately 80’s and early 90’s, when G. Papaschinopoulos’s work (
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
) was very prominent and helped to characterize dichotomies and asymptotic behavior of solutions.

In the decade of 1990 many authors came back to study K. J. Palmer’s problem in its continuous version, admitting wider hypothesis. Among them we recall the contributions of Z. Lin and Y-X. Lin
[
15
]
, L. Jiang
[
14
]
and J.L. Shi and K.Q. Xiong
[

]
.

In relation to new dichotomies, M. Pinto and R. Naulin (
[

]
,
[

]
) stand out for introducing the concept of
(h,k)-
dichotomies. We also highlight L. Barreira and C. Valls (
[

Since 2015, authors Á. Castañeda and G. Robledo

Other authors such as D. Dragi

Novelty of this work
. Our work follows the results initiated by Á. Castañeda and G. Robledo in

[

In the third section, we propose conditions that allows us to prove that K.J. Palmer’s homeomorphism, which we previously studied, is a

In the fourth section we study a generalization of the previous conditions in order to obtain higher order derivatives. Finally, in the last section, we propose an algebraic set of functions and an operator that can be inductively applied in order to find conditions for arbitrary high order derivatives.

2.
Preliminaries

In this work, we study the following nonautonomous discrete systems:

(2.1)

x(k+1)=A(k)x(k)

(2.2)

y(k+1)=A(k)y(k)+f(k,y(k)),

where
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathcal{M}_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})

) and (

) that pass through

Furthermore, we will consider the following properties:

A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to M_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})

\max\left\{\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(k)\right\rVert},\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}(k)\right\rVert}\right\}=M.

(d1)

The system (
2.1

there are two invariant complementary projectors

and

such that
P(n)+Q(n)=I

, a non-negative sequence

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi(k,n)P(n)\right\rVert}\leq D(n)\left({\frac{h(k)}{h(n)}}\right),&\forall k\geq n\geq 0\\

(d2)

(d4)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(k,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma(j)\leq q<1\text{ for every }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

(d5)

The sequence

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}(\ell)\gamma(\ell)\right\rVert}<1\text{ for every }\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

(d6)

The map

and its derivates respect to

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(k,u)\right\rVert}<+\infty

For each fixed

Remark 2.1
.

The transition matrix for (
2.1
) is the matrix function
\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathcal{M}_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})
given by:

\Phi(k,n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}A(k-1)A(k-2)\cdots A(n)&\text{if }&k>n\\
\\
I&\text{ if }&k=n\\
\\
A^{-1}(k)A^{-1}(k+1)\cdots A^{-1}(n-1)&\text{if }&k<n.\end{array}\right.

Remark 2.2
.

Projectors
P(\cdot)
and
Q(\cdot)
have been called invariant, which means that they are under the assumption that for every
k,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
they satisfy:

P(k)\Phi(k,n)=\Phi(k,n)P(n)\text{ and }Q(k)\Phi(k,n)=\Phi(k,n)Q(n).

Remark 2.3
.

Green’s operator for the system (
2.1
) asociated to the dichotomy
(d1)
is the matrix function
\mathcal{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathcal{M}_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})
given by:

\mathcal{G}(k,n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi(k,n)P(n)&\forall k\geq n\geq 0\\
\\
-\Phi(k,n)Q(n)&\forall 0\leq k<n.\end{array}\right.

And it is easily deduced that it satisfies:

\mathcal{G}(k+1,n)=A(k)\mathcal{G}(k,n)\text{ and }\mathcal{G}(n,n)=I+A(n-1)\mathcal{G}(n-1,n).

Remark 2.4
.

Condition
(d5)
was used in
[

]
in order to ensure that solutions for the system (
2.2
) can be uniquely backwards continued.

However, we have noted that some authors (
[
10
]
, for example) assume that this backwards continuation can be uniquely found even without this condition.

Now, we recall the concept of topological equivalence for discrete systems.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a interval of integer numbers. The systems (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are
J
-topologically equivalent if there is a function
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}J\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies:

i)

If
x(k)
is a solution of (
2.1
), then
H[k,x(k)]
is a solution of (
2.2
).

ii)

H(k,u)-u
is bounded over
J\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

iii)

For each fixed
k\in J
, the map
u\mapsto H(k,u)
is an homeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Moreover, the function
u\mapsto G(k,u)=H^{-1}(k,u)
satisfies ii) and iii) and maps solutions of (
2.2
) into solutions of (
2.1
).

However, as it was explained before, it turns out to be interesting to study which properties can be deduced when studying the differentiability of such homeomorphism. In the discrete case the characterization is even better than in the continuous framework, because as a difference equation may be understood as a dynamical system where a discrete group acts on the space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the space can be split on fibers, and so as soon as we find a diffeomorphism associated to one of such fibers, the other can be replicated inductively. This idea motivates the following definition:

Definition 2.6
.

The systems (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are
C^{r}
-topologically equivalent on
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
if they are topologically equivalent on
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
u\mapsto H(k,u)
is a diffeomorphism of class
C^{r}
, with
r\geq 1
for each fixed
k\geq 0
.

3.
Diffeomorphism of discrete topological equivalence under a dichotomy

In this section, we study the differentiability properties of the function
\eta\mapsto w^{*}(0;(m,\eta)),
in order to obtain that the topological equivalence is of class
C^{1}.

Lemma 3.1
.

If conditions
(d0)-(d7)
hold, then
\eta\mapsto w^{*}(0;(m,\eta))
is a
C^{1}
map.

Proof.

Fix
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
(\delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a properly convergent to zero sequence. Choose and fix
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we define

\psi_{n}(j)=\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\frac{f(j,y(j,m,\eta+\delta_{n}))-f(j,y(j,m,\eta))-\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(j,y(j,m,\eta))\frac{\partial y}{\partial\eta}(j,m,\eta)\delta_{n}}{|\delta_{n}|},

note that as
\eta\mapsto y(j,m,\eta)
is continuous, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}y(j,m,\eta+\delta_{n})=y(j,m,\eta)

(d6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{n}(j)=0.

In the proof of Theorem
2.7

7
]

\mathcal{C}_{m}(j)={\prod_{i=j}^{m-1}}\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}(i)\right\rVert}}{1-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}(i)\gamma(i)\right\rVert}}.

Thus, for

On the other hand, we know

\displaystyle y(m+1,m,\eta)=A(m)\eta+f(m,\eta),

\displaystyle y(m+1,m,\eta)-y(m+1,m,\tilde{\eta})=A(m)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})+f(m,\eta)-f(m,\tilde{\eta}).

So, we conclude

\displaystyle|y(m+1,m,\eta)-y(m+1,m,\tilde{\eta})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(m)\right\rVert}|\eta-\tilde{\eta}|+|f(m,\eta)-f(m,\tilde{\eta})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(m)\right\rVert}+\gamma(m)\right)|\eta-\tilde{\eta}|.

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}(j)|

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\right\rVert}\frac{|f(j,y(j,m,\eta+\delta_{n}))-f(j,y(j,m,\eta))|+\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(j,y(j,m,\eta))\frac{\partial y}{\partial\eta}(j,m,\eta)\delta_{n}\right|}{|\delta_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\right\rVert}\frac{\gamma(j)|y(j,m,\eta+\delta_{n})-y(j,m,\eta)|+\gamma(j)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial y}{\partial\eta}(j,m,\eta)\right\rVert}|\delta_{n}|}{|\delta_{n}|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma(j)\left(\frac{|y(j,m,\eta+\delta_{n})-y(j,m,\eta)|}{|\delta_{n}|}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial y}{\partial\eta}(j,m,\eta)\right\rVert}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma(j)\mathcal{A}_{m}(j).

On the other hand, by using
(d4)
and
(d7)
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma(j)\mathcal{A}_{m}(j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma(j)\mathcal{C}_{m}(j)+\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma(j)\mathcal{B}_{m}(j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma(j)\max_{i<m}\mathcal{C}_{m}(i)+\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}D(j+1)h(j+1)\gamma(j)\mathcal{B}_{m}(j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q\max_{i<m}\mathcal{C}_{m}(i)+\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}D(j+1)h(j+1)\gamma(j)\mathcal{B}_{m}(j)<+\infty.

So finally, using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{w^{*}(0;(m,\eta+\delta_{n}))-w^{*}(0;(m,\eta))+\left[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(j,y(j,m,\eta))\frac{\partial y}{\partial\eta}(j,m,\eta)\right]\delta_{n}}{|\delta_{n}|}&\\
\\
&=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(j)&\\
\\
&=-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{n}(j)\right)=0,&\end{array}

which implies
\eta\mapsto w^{*}(0;(m,\eta))
is differentiable.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

If conditions
(d0)-(d7)
hold, and
(d6)
is satisfied with
r=1
, then (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are
C^{1}
-topologically equivalent on
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.7
we know they are topologically equivalent. By Lemma
3.1

\eta\mapsto w^{*}(0;(k,\eta))
is
C^{1}
differentiable for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and, as stated in Remark
2.10
,
\eta\mapsto y(0,k,\eta)
is as well. By using (
2
) allows us to conclude
\eta\mapsto G(k,\eta)
is
C^{1}
differentiable.

Furthermore, as
G
is a topological equivalence, we know
\xi\mapsto G(k,\xi)-\xi
is bounded and so
G(k,\xi)\to\infty
when
|\xi|\to\infty
. This, combined with the previous expression, implies by Corollary 2.1 of
[
25
]
, that
\xi\mapsto G(k,\xi)
is a diffeomorphism. Moreover, as
G(k,H(k,\xi))=\xi
, we have

\frac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(k,H(k,\xi))\frac{\partial H}{\partial\xi}(k,\xi)=I,

and so
\frac{\partial H}{\partial\xi}(k,\xi)=\left[\frac{\partial G}{\partial\xi}(k,H(k,\xi))\right]^{-1}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose systems (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) satisfy
(d0)
and
(d6)
with
r=1
. Moreover, suppose
(d1)
is fulfilled with a classical exponential dichotomy, which means,
P
and
Q
are complementary constant projectors,
D(n)=D>0
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
h(n)=\theta^{n}
, with
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
(d2)
is fulfilled with
\gamma(n)=\gamma>0
and
\mu(n)=\mu>0
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then, if
M\gamma<1
,
\frac{D\gamma}{1-\theta}<1
and
\theta(M+\gamma)<1
, the systems (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are
C^{1}
-topologically equivalent on
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Proof.

Note that for an arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have:

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}(n)\gamma\right\rVert}\leq M\gamma<1,

hence
(d5)
is satisfied. On the other hand, for an arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(k,j+1)\right\rVert}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(k,j+1)\right\rVert}\mu+\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(k,j+1)\right\rVert}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi(k,j+1)P\right\rVert}\mu+\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi(k,j+1)Q\right\rVert}\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\theta^{k}}{\theta^{j+1}}D\mu+\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{j+1}}{\theta^{k}}D\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta^{k-1}D\mu\frac{1-\left(\frac{1}{\theta}\right)^{k}}{1-\frac{1}{\theta}}+\theta D\mu\frac{1}{1-\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D\mu\frac{1+\theta-\theta^{k}}{1-\theta}\leq\frac{D\mu}{1-\theta}.

Thus
(d3)
is satisfied. Analogously

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(k,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma\leq\frac{D\gamma}{1-\theta}<1.

Hence
(d4)
is satisfied. Finally, for an arbitrary
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}}D\gamma\left(h(j+1)\left[{\prod_{p=m}^{j-1}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(p)\right\rVert}+\gamma\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}}D\gamma\left(\theta^{j+1}\left[{\prod_{p=m}^{j-1}}M+\gamma\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D\gamma{\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}}\theta^{j+1}(M+\gamma)^{j-m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D\gamma\theta}{(M+\gamma)^{m}}{\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}}\left(\theta(M+\gamma)\right)^{j}<\infty,

hence
(d7)
is satisfied. So, by applying Theorem
3.2
, the systems are
C^{1}
-topologically equivalent on
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The conditions imposed in corollary

,

and

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose conditions
(d0)
and
(d5)
are satisfied. Also, consider system (
2.1
) admits a nonuniform exponential dichotomy, which means there are two complementary invariant projectors
P(\cdot)
and
Q(\cdot)
and constants
C,\lambda,\varepsilon>0
such that:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi(k,n)P(n)\right\rVert}\leq Ce^{-\lambda(k-n)+\varepsilon n},&\forall k\geq n\geq 0\\
\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi(k,n)Q(n)\right\rVert}\leq Ce^{\lambda(k-n)+\varepsilon n},&\forall 0\leq k\leq n.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, suppose that for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

u\mapsto f(k,u)
is a
C^{1}
function such that
u\mapsto\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(k,u)
is a bounded map that satisfies:

(3.3)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(k,u)\right\rVert}\leq\nu e^{-\varepsilon(k+1)}

and

(3.4)

|f(k,u)|\leq\kappa e^{-\tau(k+1)}

for given
\nu,\kappa>0
and
\tau>\varepsilon-\lambda
. Then, if
Me^{-\lambda}<1
, for sufficiently small
\nu>0
, systems (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are
C^{1}
-topologically equivalent on
\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

Proof.

Condition
(d1)
is easily verified with
D(n)=Ce^{\varepsilon n}
and
h(n)=e^{-\lambda n}
. Note that using generalized intermediate point Theorem, condition (
3.3
) implies:

|f(k,y)-f(k,\tilde{y})|\leq\nu e^{-\varepsilon(k+1)}|y-\tilde{y}|,

so condition
(d2)
is verified with
\gamma(k)=\nu e^{-\varepsilon(k+1)}
and
\mu(k)=\kappa e^{-\tau(k+1)}
. Hence condition
(d3)
is immediately satisfied and, provided
\nu
is sufficiently small, so is
(d4)
in a similar fashion as in the previous Corollary. Condition
(d6)
is immediately satisfied by hypothesis, with
r=1
. Now, denote

\Psi_{m}(j)={\prod_{p=m}^{j-1}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(p)\right\rVert}+\gamma(p).

It is easy to see
m\geq n
implies
\Psi_{m}(j)\leq\Psi_{n}(j)
, hence for a fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
:

\Psi_{m}(j)\leq\Psi_{0}(j)\leq\left(M+\nu e^{-\varepsilon}\right)^{j}

So we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}D(j+1)h(j+1)\gamma(j)\Psi_{m}(j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}D(j+1)h(j+1)\gamma(j)\Psi_{0}(j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\nu e^{-\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left[e^{-\lambda}\left(M+\nu e^{-\varepsilon}\right)\right]^{j},

which, as
Me^{-\lambda}<1
, is finite provided
\nu
is sufficiently small, so condition
(d7)
is satisfied. Applying Theorem
3.2
, the corollary is proved.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Note that for condition (

) to be satisfied, we only have

to be bigger than
\varepsilon-\lambda
, regardless of the sign of such

. This could mean that, in the case

, this dichotomy even admits a non bounded perturbation with exponential growth, it just has to have a increasing ratio smaller than the decay ratio of the dichotomy.

Remark 3.7
.

Corollary
3.5
has been inspired by
[
10
, Theorem 2]
. Our approach and that result consider conditions
(d0)
and (
3.3
), the same dichotomy and both results work for

sufficiently small. Furthermore, we imposed the extra hypothesis

and conditions (
3.4
) and
(d5)
. However, as stated on Remark

, we think this last hypothesis may be dropped.

Moreover, our result gives

Example 3.8

\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\tilde{\Phi}(k,n)\tilde{Q}(n)\right\rVert}\leq\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert E(n-1)\right\rVert}\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}c_{j}^{-1},&\forall 0\leq k\leq n,\end{array}\right.

hence, setting

and

,

follows. Note that
(d2)
and
(d6)
follow immediately from our hypothesis, while

is satisfied in the same fashion as in Example

.

Denote

It is easy to see

implies

, hence for a fixed

:

\tilde{\Psi}_{m}(j)\leq\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(j)\leq\prod_{p=1}^{j-1}\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}c_{p}+\gamma_{p}.

So we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}D(j+1)h(j+1)\gamma(j)\tilde{\Psi}_{m}(j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}D(j+1)h(j+1)\gamma(j)\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert E(j)\right\rVert}\left(\prod_{p=1}^{j}c_{p}^{-1}\right)\gamma_{j}\left(\prod_{p=1}^{j-1}\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}c_{p}+\gamma_{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}c_{j}^{-1}\left(\prod_{p=1}^{j-1}\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}+\gamma_{p}c_{p}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}\left(\prod_{p=1}^{j-1}\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}+\gamma_{p}\right).

As

, there exist

such that

for every

. Hence, applying condition (

), it is easy to see

is fulfilled. Finally

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(k,j+1)\right\rVert}\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{-}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{h(k)}{h(j+1)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert E(j-1)\right\rVert}\gamma_{j}+\mathscr{E}^{-}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{h(j+1)}{h(k)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert E(j-1)\right\rVert}\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\prod_{p=j+1}^{k-1}c_{p}^{-1}\right)\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle+\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{p=k}^{j}c_{p}^{-1}\right)\gamma_{j}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{-}\mathscr{E}^{+}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert}_{1}<1.

Hence proving
(d4)
and
(d3)
with
\mu_{j}=\gamma_{j}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert g\right\rVert}_{\infty}
. By using Theorem

our claim follows.

Remark 3.12
.

As we mentioned in the introduction, conditions
(d0)-(d6)
have been adopted several times by many authors in order to study the topological equivalence between systems (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). Hence, the novelty of this work is the condition
(d7)
and its implications.

Namely, in Corollary
3.3
the fact that this condition is satisfied relies mainly (as explained on Remark
3.4
) on the properties of the exponential dichotomy, which gives a evolution ratio for the solutions that allows
(d7)
to be fulfilled. Meanwhile, in Examples
3.8
and
3.10
, this condition is achieved by a perturbation that is small enough and has a fast enough decay ratio, and actually we completely ignore the help that the dichotomy may have presented. Finally, in Corollary
3.5
and Example
3.11
, the condition
(d7)
is achieved through a combination of the contributions of both the perturbation and the dichotomy.

This shows that there are many possibilities to achieve this condition and hence our main result, Theorem
3.2
, is applicable in many different situations.

4.
Higher order derivatives

In order to study higher order derivatives of the homeomorphism of topological equivalence, we once again consider the expression (
2
). Taking in account Remark
2.10
, the map
\eta\mapsto y(0,m,\eta)
is of class
C^{r}
(
r\geq 1
) for every fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
if conditions
(d0)-(d6)
are satisfied; hence the class of differentiability of the homeomorphism relies on the differentiability of the map
\eta\mapsto w^{*}(0;(m,\eta))
.

In this section we attempt to find conditions that generalize
(d7)
in order to obtain higher order derivatives for the homeomorphism.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose conditions
(d0)-(d7)
hold, where
(d6)
is fulfilled with
r=2
. Also, there are functions
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\pi_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that:

(4.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial u^{2}}(j,u)\right\rVert}\leq\Gamma(j)\text{ , for every }j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

and

(4.2)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}y}{\partial\eta^{2}}(j,m,\eta)\right\rVert}\leq\pi_{m}(j)\text{ , for every }j\geq m.

Finally, suppose that for each fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the sequences satisfy:

(4.3)

{\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}}\left(D(j+1)h(j+1)\left\{\pi_{m}(j)\gamma(j)+\Gamma(j)\left[{\prod_{p=m}^{j-1}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(p)\right\rVert}+\gamma(p)\right]^{2}\right\}\right)<+\infty.

Then
\eta\mapsto w^{*}(0;(m,\eta))
is a
C^{2}
map.

Proof.

From the proof of Lemma
3.1
it is easy to see that for every fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

\frac{\partial w^{*}(0;(m,\eta))}{\partial\eta}=-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(j,y(j,m,\eta))\frac{\partial y}{\partial\eta}(j,m,\eta).

Hence, in order to prove the differentiability of this map, it is enough to find a summable function that uniformly (respect to
\eta
) dominates

\mathcal{G}(0,j+1)\left[\frac{\partial^{2}y}{\partial\eta^{2}}(j,m,\eta)\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(j,y(j,m,\eta))+\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial u^{2}}(j,y(j,m,\eta))\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial\eta}(j,m,\eta)\right)^{2}\right]

for every
j\geq 0
.

This fact is verified by hypothesis. Therefore, we apply the same strategy as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

If conditions
(d0)-(d7)
hold,
(d6)
is satisfied with

, and conditions of Lemma
4.1
are satisfied, then (

) and (

) are

-topologically equivalent on

.

Note that Theorem
4.2
follows easily in the same fashion as the proof of Theorem

.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose conditions
(d0)
and
(d5)
are satisfied. Also, consider system (
2.1
) admits a nonuniform exponential dichotomy, which means there are two complementary invariant projectors

and

and constants

\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi(k,n)Q(n)\right\rVert}\leq Ce^{\lambda(k-n)+\varepsilon n},&\forall 0\leq k\leq n.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, suppose that for every

|f(k,u)|\leq\kappa e^{-\tau(k+1)},

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(k,u)\right\rVert}\leq\nu e^{-\varepsilon(k+1)}

, for sufficiently small

Proof.

By Corollary

It is easy to see that (

{\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}}\left(D(j+1)h(j+1)\Gamma(j)\left[{\prod_{p=m}^{j-1}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(p)\right\rVert}+\gamma(p)\right]^{2}\right)<+\infty.

5.
Discussion

Suppose that conditions
(d0)-(d6)
are verified. Suppose as well there are functions
\Gamma_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\pi_{s,m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
1\leq s\leq r
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that:

(5.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{s}f}{\partial u^{s}}(j,u)\right\rVert}\leq\Gamma_{s}(j)\text{ , for every }j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

and

(5.2)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{s}y}{\partial\eta^{s}}(j,m,\eta)\right\rVert}\leq\pi_{s,m}(j)\text{ , for every }j\geq m.

Chose a fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and consider the set of functions

\mathfrak{S}_{m}=\left\{\Gamma_{s}\prod_{k=1}^{r}\pi_{k,m}^{e_{k}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1\leq s\leq r,e_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}_{0}\right\}.

Consider the module
\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{m}]
and define the
\mathbb{Z}-
linear map
\mathbb{D}_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{m}]\to\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{m}]
given by

•

\mathbb{D}_{m}(\Gamma_{s})=\Gamma_{s+1}\pi_{1,m}
, for every
1\leq s<r.

•

\mathbb{D}_{m}(\pi_{s,m})=\pi_{s+1,m}
, for every
1\leq s<r.

•

\mathbb{D}_{m}(fg)=\mathbb{D}_{m}(f)g+f\mathbb{D}_{m}(g)
, for every
f,g\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{m}]
such that
fg\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{m}].

In order to simplify notations, suppose the existence of a certain function
\Gamma_{0}
such that
\mathbb{D}_{m}(\Gamma_{0})(j)=\Gamma_{1}(j)\pi_{1,m}(j)
. Under these conditions it is easy to see:

•

\mathbb{D}^{2}_{m}(\Gamma_{0})(j)=\Gamma_{2}(j)\pi_{1,m}(j)^{2}+\Gamma_{1}(j)\pi_{2,m}(j).

•

\mathbb{D}^{3}_{m}(\Gamma_{0})(j)=\Gamma_{3}(j)\pi_{1,m}(j)^{3}+3\Gamma_{2}(j)\pi_{2,m}(j)\pi_{1,m}(j)+\Gamma_{1}(j)\pi_{3,m}(j).

•

\mathbb{D}^{4}_{m}(\Gamma_{0})(j)=\Gamma_{4}(j)\pi_{1,m}(j)^{4}+6\Gamma_{3}(j)\pi_{2,m}(j)\pi_{1,m}(j)^{2}+4\Gamma_{2}(j)\pi_{3,m}(j)\pi_{1,m}(j)+\Gamma_{1}(j)\pi_{4,m}(j)
.

And inductively it is easy to calculate higher powers of
\mathbb{D}_{m}
applied to
\Gamma_{0}
. With this in mind, we would like to introduce the following condition:

(DIF,r)
Suppose conditions
(d0)-(d6)
are verified. Suppose as well there are functions
\Gamma_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\pi_{s,m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
1\leq s\leq r
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
that satisfy (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) respectively and are such that for every fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}
it is verified

{\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}}D(j+1)h(j+1)\mathbb{D}^{s}_{m}(\Gamma_{0})(j)<+\infty\text{ , for every }1\leq s\leq r.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that condition
(d7)
is a particular case of
(DIF,r)
, namely
(DIF,1)
, with
\Gamma_{1}(j)=\gamma(j)
and
\pi_{1,m}(j)=\prod_{p=m}^{j-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(p)\right\rVert}+\gamma(p)
. Similarly, the conditions on Lemma (
4.1
) can be summarized as
(DIF,2)
.

Moreover, condition
(d3)
is equivalent to
(DIF,0)
, defining
j\mapsto\Gamma_{0}(j)
as a function that uniformly (over
u
) dominates
j\mapsto f(j,u)
, or, in the notations of
(d3)
,
\Gamma_{0}(j)=\mu(j)
. This makes sense, because a
C^{0}
class of differentiability for a diffeomorphism corresponds to an homeomorphism, which is verified by considering
(d3)
(in addition with
(d0)-(d5)
).

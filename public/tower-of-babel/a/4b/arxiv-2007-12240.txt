Nonreciprocal surface magnetoelastic dynamics
Tao Yu

Abstract
Motivated by recent experiments, we investigate the nonreciprocal magnetoelastic interaction between the surface acoustic phonons of dielectric non-magnetic substrates and magnons of proximity nanomagnets. The magnetization dynamics exerts rotating forces at the edges of the nanomagnet that causes the nonreciprocal interaction with surface phonons due to its rotation-momentum locking. This coupling induces the nonreciprocity of the surface phonon transmission and a nearly complete phonon diode effect by several (tens of) magnetic nanowires of high (ordinary) magnetic quality. Phase-sensitive microwave transmission is also nonreciprocal that can pick up clear signals of the coherent phonons excited by magnetization dynamics. Nonreciprocal pumping of phonons by precessing magnetization is predicted using Landauer-Büttiker formalism.

Magnetoelastic coupling

The magnetoelastic coupling Hamiltonian generally depends on the crystal symmetry of the material

]
. Here we adopt the simplest form that describes a wide class of material

\hat{H}^{(a)}_{c}=\frac{1}{M_{s}^{2}}\int d{\bf r}\left(B_{\parallel}\sum_{i}M_{i}^{2}\varepsilon_{ii}+B_{\perp}\sum_{i\neq j}M_{i}M_{j}\varepsilon_{ij}\right),

(1)

where

are the magnetoelastic constants, and

denotes the strain tensor in terms of the displacement field

For the Rayleigh SAWs propagating perpendicular to the wire with momentum

and only

1

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(a)}_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sin\varphi}{M_{s}}\int d{\bf r}\left(B_{\parallel}\cos\varphi m_{y^{\prime}}\varepsilon_{yy}+B_{\perp}m_{x^{\prime}}\varepsilon_{xy}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2\sin\varphi\cos\varphi}{M_{s}}{B_{\parallel}Ld}m_{y^{\prime}}\left(u_{y}|_{\frac{w}{2}+y_{l}}-u_{y}|_{-\frac{w}{2}+y_{l}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin\varphi}{M_{s}}B_{\perp}Ldm_{x^{\prime}}\left(u_{x}|_{\frac{w}{2}+y_{l}}-u_{x}|_{-\frac{w}{2}+y_{l}}\right),

(2)

where
y_{l}
is the center coordinate of the
l
-th nanomagnet and

is the length of the nanowire.
Here we have assumed that the nanowire is sufficiently thin such that the displacements at its top and bottom surface are identical and hence have no net contribution to the magnetoelastic coupling.

Classically, we obtain the forces, by

\displaystyle F_{y}|_{y=\frac{w}{2}+y_{l}}=2\sin\varphi\cos\varphi B_{\parallel}Ldm_{y^{\prime}}/M_{s},

II.2
Magnetorotation coupling

Magnetocrystalline and shape anisotropies can contribute to the magnetorotation coupling in terms of antisymmetric tensor
\omega_{ij}=(\partial_{j}u_{i}-\partial_{i}u_{j})/2

[
57

58

\omega_{xz}
and
\omega_{yz}

-direction.

We first consider the perpendicular anisotropy with the easy axis along the

-direction

\hat{H}_{c}^{(b)}=-\frac{2K_{1}}{M_{s}^{2}}\int d{\bf r}M_{x}({\bf r})\big[M_{y}\omega_{yx}({\bf r})+M_{z}\omega_{zx}({\bf r})\big],

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{c}^{(b)}

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{1}}{M_{s}}\sin\varphi m_{x^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\partial u_{x}}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial u_{y}}{\partial x}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\varphi}{M_{s}}K_{1}Ldm_{x^{\prime}}\left(u_{x}|_{\frac{w}{2}+y_{l}}-u_{x}|_{-\frac{w}{2}+y_{l}}\right),

II.3
Coupling Hamiltonian

The polarization of the rotating forces follows that of the Kittel magnon. The Kittel mode is linearly polarized under the weak applied magnetic field and the induced force is not rotating. As only a circularly polarized magnon favors the nonreciprocity, we assume that a large magnetic field

\displaystyle g_{k,l}

\displaystyle=i\sin\varphi\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{M_{s}\rho c_{r}}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{w}}\sin\left(\frac{kw}{2}\right)e^{iky_{l}}\xi_{P}

We see that the coupling tends to vanish when

\displaystyle\cos\varphi_{c}=\frac{\tilde{B}_{\perp}}{B_{\parallel}}\frac{a}{1+b^{2}}.

Such conclusion agrees with the classical description in which the rotating force
|F_{x}|=|F_{y}|(1+b^{2})/(2a)
matches the phonon chirality. Without the magnetoelastic coupling, the magnetorotation coupling itself cannot cause nonreciprocity in the thin wire configuration.

III
Phonon diode effect

The nonreciprocal couplings are detectable by transmission spectra of phonon as the scattering cross section, for the phonon with opposite momenta, can be different, i.e., a diode effect for surface acoustic phonon
[
24
;
25
]
. The phonon transmission should be tunable by the number of proximity nanomagnet. Therefore, not restricting to one nanomagnet, we generally calculate the phonon scattering matrix in the presence of
N
parallel magnetic nanowires by using the scattering theory (Sec.
III.1
)
[
60
;
59
;
51
]
. The distance of the neighbouring wires is assumed to be much larger than the wire width such that the dipolar interaction between wires can be safely disregarded. In this case, every wire can be treated to be isolated. With many wires, the magnons in different wires interact with each other through virtual exchange of surface phonon; they form collective modes. We show that the phonon reflection are determined by these collective modes (Sec.
III.2
).

III.1
Phonon scattering matrix

The scattering amplitude between state
\ket{k}
to state
\ket{k^{\prime}}
is calculated by the
T
-matrix
[
60
;
59
;
51
]

\displaystyle T_{k^{\prime}k}

\displaystyle=\langle k^{\prime}|\hat{T}|k\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\omega_{k}-\omega_{k^{\prime}}+i\eta_{k^{\prime}}}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}g^{*}_{k^{\prime},l}G_{N,ll^{\prime}}(\omega_{k})g_{k,l^{\prime}},

(9)

where
\eta_{k^{\prime}}
is the damping broadening of phonon.
The summation on the magnet index
\{l=1,2,\cdots,N\}
implies that the scattering from state
\ket{k}
to state
\ket{k^{\prime}}
experiences all possible scattering paths such that the phonon transmission results from the net interference. Note that the magnets are not isolated as they interact with each other via the exchange of phonons. Also, different from the static scatters, the magnets are dynamical such that they absorb and emit the phonons. All these dynamics is encoded in the magnon Green function
G_{N}|_{ll^{\prime}}
that stands for the propagator of magnon from wire
l^{\prime}
to
l
. It is calculated to be
[
51
;
50
;
52
]

\left(G^{-1}_{N}(\omega)\right)_{ll^{\prime}}=(\omega-\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K})\delta_{ll^{\prime}}-\Sigma_{ll^{\prime}}(\omega),

(10)

where
\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K}=\omega_{{\rm K}}-i\alpha_{G}\omega_{{\rm K}}
with the Gilbert damping parameterized by the Gilbert coefficient
\alpha_{G}
, and
\Sigma
is the self-energy matrix of magnons due to their collective interactions with phonons with elements

\displaystyle\Sigma_{ll^{\prime}}(\omega)=\sum_{k^{\prime}}\frac{g_{k^{\prime},l}g^{*}_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}}{\omega-\omega_{k^{\prime}}+i\eta_{k^{\prime}}}.

(11)

The Green function is the basis for calculating the coherent and incoherent dynamics below.

When
\eta_{k}\rightarrow 0_{+}
for the high-quality elastic substrate such as GGG, these elements are calculated to be

\displaystyle\Sigma_{ll}(\omega)=-\frac{i}{2c_{r}}\left(|g_{k_{*},l}|^{2}+|g_{-k_{*},l}|^{2}\right)=-i\Gamma_{l}(\omega),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{l<l^{\prime}}(\omega)=-\frac{i}{c_{r}}\tilde{g}_{k_{*},l}\tilde{g}^{*}_{k_{*},l^{\prime}}e^{ik_{*}|y_{l}-y_{l^{\prime}}|}=-i\Gamma_{R,ll^{\prime}}(\omega),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{l>l^{\prime}}(\omega)=-\frac{i}{c_{r}}\tilde{g}_{-k_{*},l}\tilde{g}^{*}_{-k_{*},l^{\prime}}e^{ik_{*}|y_{l}-y_{l^{\prime}}|}=-i\Gamma_{L,ll^{\prime}}(\omega),

(12)

where

and
\tilde{g}_{k,l}=g_{k,l}e^{-iky_{l}}
.
The diagonal elements
\Sigma_{ll}(\omega_{k})
represent the broadening of magnons modes by pumping phonons. While the off-diagonal self-energies
\Sigma_{l\neq l^{\prime}}(\omega_{k})
imply that the magnets interact with each other mediated by the surface phonons.
Thus, the magnon Green function is represented by
G_{N}(\omega)=\big(\omega-{\cal H}_{N}(\omega)\big)^{-1}
, where the matrix

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{N}(\omega)

\displaystyle\equiv\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K}

\displaystyle-i\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{1}(\omega)&\Gamma_{L,21}(\omega)&\cdots&\Gamma_{L,N1}(\omega)\\
\Gamma_{R,12}(\omega)&\Gamma_{2}(\omega)&\cdots&\Gamma_{L,N2}(\omega)\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\Gamma_{R,1N}(\omega)&\Gamma_{R,2N}(\omega)&\cdots&\Gamma_{N}(\omega)\end{pmatrix}

(13)

is interpreted by a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian that describes the dissipatively-coupled magnons
[

;

;
32
;
27
]
. Note that the coupling constant
\Gamma(\omega)
still depends on the frequency.
Then from Eq. (

{T}
-matrix becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{k^{\prime}k}&=\delta_{k^{\prime}k}+\frac{1}{\omega_{k}-\omega_{k^{\prime}}+i\eta}{\cal M}_{k^{\prime}}^{*}G_{N}(\omega_{k}){\cal M}_{k}^{T}\end{split},

(14)

with

.

When propagating through magnetic nanowire arrays, the surface phonon of momentum

\lim_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\psi_{t}(y)=\sum_{k^{\prime}}\langle y|k^{\prime}\rangle T_{k^{\prime}k}

G_{N=1}(\omega)=1/({\omega-\omega_{\rm K}-\Sigma(\omega)})

with magnon self-energy

\Sigma(\omega)=\sum_{k^{\prime}}|g_{k^{\prime}}|^{2}/\left(\omega-\omega_{k^{\prime}}+i\eta_{k^{\prime}}\right)=-{i}(|g_{k}|^{2}+|g_{-k}|^{2})/(2c_{r})
. We obtain the scattering matrix,

\displaystyle S_{21}(k)=e^{ikD}\left(1-\frac{i}{c_{r}}\frac{|g_{k}|^{2}}{\omega_{k}-\omega_{\rm K}+i\alpha_{G}\omega_{\rm K}-\Sigma(\omega_{k})}\right),

\displaystyle S_{11}(k)=-\frac{i}{c_{r}}\frac{g_{k}g_{-k}^{*}}{\omega_{k}-\omega_{\rm K}+i\alpha_{G}\omega_{\rm K}-\Sigma(\omega_{k})}.

(16)

It is seen that when the magnon-phonon coupling is complete nonreciprocal, we always have
S_{11}(k)=0
no matter
g_{k}=0
or
g_{-k}=0
; there is no reflection. Nevertheless, the transmission of phonon with opposite momenta depends on the nonreciprocity. At the resonance with
\omega_{k}=\omega_{\rm K}
,

\displaystyle S_{21}(k)=\left\{\begin{matrix}e^{ikL},\\
\xi e^{ikL},\end{matrix}\right.\quad\begin{matrix}{\rm when}~~g_{k}=0,\\
~{\rm when}~~g_{-k}=0,\end{matrix}

(17)

where

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{2\alpha_{G}\omega_{\rm K}-|g_{k}|^{2}/c_{r}}{2\alpha_{G}\omega_{\rm K}+|g_{k}|^{2}/c_{r}}

(18)

modulates the amplitude of the transmitted waves. Generally,
\xi<1
as
\alpha_{G}>0
, implying a suppression of transmission. Nevertheless, a negative
\alpha_{G}
, i.e., a gain
[
65
;
64
;
66
;
67
]
, can amplify the phonons as
|\xi|>1
. When
g_{k}=0
, the nanomagnet is not excited at all and the propagating SAWs of momentum
k
only accumulate a propagation phase
kD
. While when
g_{-k}=0
, the SAWs are first absorbed and then emitted by the nanomagnet, resulting in a double dissipative phase shift
\pi/2
. This can be observed by a wire with a high magnetic quality
2\alpha_{G}\omega_{\rm K}\ll|g_{k}|^{2}/c_{r}
, leading to
\xi=e^{i\pi}
, i.e., without amplitude suppression but a pure phase shift. When
2\alpha_{G}\omega_{\rm K}\rightarrow|g_{k}|^{2}/c_{r}
, the transmission tends to be zero and the energy accumulates in the magnet
[
46
]
. Finally, when
2\alpha_{G}\omega_{\rm K}\gg|g_{k}|^{2}/c_{r}
,
\xi\rightarrow 1
and the modulation vanishes and the phonon diode effect is very small.

As the magnon damping breaks the unitarity of the phonon scattering matrix, the phonon suffers from the “resistivity” when passing through the magnets. III.2
Phonon resistivity by collective magnon modes

Although the phonon is assumed to have a small damping here, the magnon damping can be large. The conversion of phonon to magnon then suffers from a large damping that brings a resistivity. Experimentally, this can correspond to a short propagation length for phonon that scales with the number of magnet. To this end, we generally calculate the phonon transmission through many (identical) magnetic wires.

We express the phonon scattering matrix by the collective modes of magnons via the eigenvectors of the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
{\cal H}_{N}(\omega_{k})

[
36
]
. Assuming the right eigenvectors of
{\cal H}_{N}(\omega_{k})
are
\psi_{\zeta}
with corresponding eigenvalue
\nu_{\zeta}
,
{\cal H}_{N}(\omega_{k})\psi_{\zeta}=\nu_{\zeta}\psi_{\zeta}
. Here,
\zeta=\{1,\cdots,N\}
labels the collective modes. We also define the right eigenvectors
\phi_{\zeta}
of
{\cal H}^{\dagger}_{N}(\omega_{k})
with corresponding eigenvalue
\nu_{\zeta}^{*}
. The eigenvectors satisfy the orthornormal conditions with
\psi^{\dagger}_{\zeta}\phi_{\zeta^{\prime}}=\delta_{\zeta\zeta^{\prime}}
and
\phi_{\zeta}^{\dagger}\psi_{\zeta^{\prime}}=\delta_{\zeta\zeta^{\prime}}

[
36
]
. Thus, the magnon (retarded) Green function is found to be

\displaystyle G^{r}_{N}(\omega)=\sum_{\zeta}\psi_{\zeta}\phi_{\zeta}^{\dagger}\frac{1}{\omega-\nu_{\zeta}},

(19)

which is determined by the collective modes of the wires. Therefore, the net effect of many wires is not a simple summation of that of single wire, which would bring a modulation factor
\xi^{N}
, but may lead to different features. The phonon transmission can thus demonstrate the existence and information of the collective mode of many magnetic wires. Many properties of the collective mode were addressed in our previous works
[
36
;
46
]
.

We numerically diagonalize the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian and calculate the phonon transmission through a magnetic array with distance
\delta
between the neighbouring wires. Such an array was commonly employed to excite short-wavelength spin waves on top of a magnetic film
[
30
;
53
]
. By taking
\delta=3.2\pi/k
and the other parameters in the last subsection, we show the improvement of the diode effect by many magnetic wires. Figure
4
is a plot of the phonon transmission
|S_{21}(\omega_{k})|
at the critical angles with
|S_{12}(\omega_{k})|=1
when the damping of magnetic wire is large with
\alpha_{G}=5\times 10^{-3}
. Although the transmission is still very large with one magnetic wire, it tends to be zero rapidly with tens of wires. The frequency window of the filtering also increases with the increase of the wire number, i.e., a broadband nonreciprocity, as indicated by the dashed arrows in the figure, implying the advantage of many-wire configuration. The suppression of the phonon transmission is not as rapid as
\xi^{N}
-law with the increase of magnet number, indicating that it is the collective modes that play roles in the filtering. Note that the phonon reflection is zero in this complete nonreciprocal case, implying that the phonons are damped by exciting the magnon collective modes as the total number of magnon and phonon is conserved in the linear regime.

Phase-sensitive microwave transmission

The phase-sensitive microwave scattering matrix is also an efficient way to detect the nonreciprocity
[
30
;
32
]
. We again formulate a general case with
N
parallel magnetic wires. We consider the microwave excites the
i
-th wire and the radiated microwave is read out above the
j
-th wire. Such a setup was realized experimentally with two wires by narrow striplines on top of the wires
[

]
. Following our previous works
[
30
;
36
;

]
, the equation of motion of the magnons augmented by the microwave input leads to
[
63
;
62
]

\displaystyle-i\omega\hat{B}(\omega)=-i\hat{H}_{N}(\omega)\hat{B}(\omega)-\sqrt{\kappa_{p}}\hat{P}_{\rm in}(\omega),

(20)

where
\hat{B}=(\hat{\beta}_{1},\hat{\beta}_{2},\cdots,\hat{\beta}_{N})^{T}
and
\kappa_{p}
is the dissipative damping of magnon by microwave radiation, which we assume to be much smaller than the intrinsic Gilbert damping.
\hat{P}_{\rm in}
is the input of microwave photon in which we assume the
i
-th wire is excited by the local active stripline and the element is
(\hat{P}_{\rm in})_{l}=\delta_{li}\hat{p}_{\rm in}
. The local input microwaves, actually, excite all the magnetic wires as they are coupled through virtual exchange of the surface phonons. These excited wires can radiate out the microwaves that can be detected by the passive stripline. We monitor the microwave at the
j
-th wire with the photon output
[
63
;
62
;
31
]

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{\rm out}(\omega)=\hat{p}_{\rm in}(\omega)\delta_{ij}+\sqrt{\kappa_{p}}\hat{\beta}_{j}(\omega).

(21)

The magnon excitation at the
j
-th wire is represented by magnon Green function from Eq. (
20
),

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{j}(\omega)=i\sqrt{\kappa_{p}}(G_{N}(\omega))_{ji}\hat{p}_{\rm in}(\omega).

(22)

When
j=i
, we obtain the microwave reflection at the
i
-th wire,

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{ii}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{p}_{\rm out}(\omega)/\hat{p}_{\rm in}(\omega)=1-i\kappa_{p}(G_{N}(\omega))_{ii}

\displaystyle=1-i\kappa_{p}\sum_{\zeta=1}^{N}\frac{\left(\psi_{\zeta}(\omega)\right)_{i}\left(\phi_{\zeta}^{\dagger}(\omega)\right)_{i}}{\omega-\nu_{\zeta}},

(23)

which reads out the diagonal term of the magnon Green function. While when
j\neq i
, we obtain the microwave transmission from the
i
-th wire to the
j
-th one,

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{ji}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-i\kappa_{p}(G_{N}(\omega))_{ji}

\displaystyle=-i\kappa_{p}\sum_{\zeta=1}^{N}\frac{\left(\psi_{\zeta}(\omega)\right)_{j}\left(\phi_{\zeta}^{\dagger}(\omega)\right)_{i}}{\omega-\nu_{\zeta}},

(24)

which is expressed by the off-diagonal term of the magnon Green function. Therefore, an ergodic detection of all the microwave reflection and transmission can give the whole magnon Green function, whose inverse gives all the terms of the magnon Hamiltonian that contains rich information.

The simplest experimental setup employs two identical wires

\displaystyle G_{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\omega-\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K}+i\Gamma_{1}(\omega))^{2}+\Gamma_{R,12}(\omega)\Gamma_{L,21}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\times\left(\begin{matrix}\omega-\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K}+i\Gamma_{1}(\omega)&-i\Gamma_{L,21}(\omega)\\
-i\Gamma_{R,12}(\omega)&\omega-\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K}+i\Gamma_{1}(\omega)\end{matrix}\right).

(25)

Accordingly, the microwave reflection and transmission read

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{11}(\omega)=1-\frac{i\kappa_{p}(\omega-\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K}+i\Gamma_{1}(\omega))}{(\omega-\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K}+i\Gamma_{1}(\omega))^{2}+\Gamma_{L,21}(\omega)\Gamma_{R,12}(\omega)},

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{21}(\omega)=-\frac{\kappa_{p}\Gamma_{R,12}(\omega)}{(\omega-\tilde{\omega}_{\rm K}+i\Gamma_{1}(\omega))^{2}+\Gamma_{L,21}(\omega)\Gamma_{R,12}(\omega)},

recovering our previous results

]

\Gamma_{R,12}(\omega)=(|g_{k_{\ast}}|^{2}/c_{r})e^{i\omega|y_{2}-y_{1}|/c_{r}}

\Gamma_{L,21}=0
reads

(27)

which oscillates with the microwave frequency. The frequency difference between two neighboring peak of

is

\displaystyle\Delta\omega=2\pi c_{r}/|y_{2}-y_{1}|,

(28)

which is sensitive to the wire distance and phonon group velocity.

V
Nonreciprocal phonon pumping

In the presence of the nonreciprocal magnetoelastic coupling, the injected phonon from a precessing magnetization that travels with group velocity
c_{r}
forms opposite currents at the two sides of the nanowires, i.e., a chiral pumping effect that was considered recently in magnonics and photonics in the presence of one to many magnets
[
28
;
29
;
30
;
31
;
35
;
36
;
42
]
. In this part, we extend the formalism in terms of the Green function to calculate the chiral phonon pumping and make a connection to the phonon scattering matrix.

As the group velocity of surface phonon does not depend on the momentum, the current
I_{L/R}
in the
\pm\hat{\bf y}
-direction is solely determined by the total injection rate
{\cal P}_{R/L}
for the left- and right-moving phonons with

\displaystyle I_{R/L}={\cal P}_{R/L}c_{r}.

(29)

The corresponding phonon injection rate depends on the nonequilibrium distributions and can be generally calculated by Landauer-Büttiker formula
[
49
;
50
;
51
;
52
]
. The injection rates read

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{R(L)}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\left\langle\left[\hat{H},\sum_{l}\hat{\beta}_{l}^{\dagger}\hat{\beta}_{l}\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2{\rm Re}\left(\sum_{l}\sum_{k>0(<0)}g_{k,l}^{*}G^{<}_{lk}(t,t)\right),

(30)

which is evaluated by the Green functions

G^{<}_{lk}(t,t^{\prime})\equiv-i\left\langle\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\hat{\beta}_{l}(t)\right\rangle,

and

G^{<}_{kl}(t,t^{\prime})\equiv-i\left\langle\hat{\beta}_{l}(t^{\prime})\hat{b}_{k}(t)\right\rangle.

The “lesser” Green function can be calculated by invoking the time-ordered Green function

G^{t}_{lk}(t,t^{\prime})=-i\theta(t-t^{\prime})\langle\hat{\beta}_{l}(t)\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\rangle-i\theta(t^{\prime}-t)\langle\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\hat{\beta}_{l}(t)\rangle,

which evolves according to the coupled Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{c}
:

\Big(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}-\omega_{k}\Big)G^{t}_{lk}(t,t^{\prime})=\sum_{l^{\prime}}g_{k,l^{\prime}}{G}_{ll^{\prime}}^{t}(t,t^{\prime}),

(31)

where we define the time-ordered Green function for the Kittel magnons

{G}^{t}_{ll^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})=-i\theta(t-t^{\prime})\langle\hat{\beta}_{l}(t)\hat{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\rangle-i\theta(t^{\prime}-t)\langle\hat{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\hat{\beta}_{l}(t)\rangle.

Then through the operator
-i{\partial}/{\partial t^{\prime}}-\omega_{k}\equiv[{\cal G}^{t}_{k}(t^{\prime})]^{-1}
, we express

G_{lk}^{t}(t,t^{\prime})=\sum_{l^{\prime}}\int dt_{1}g_{k,l^{\prime}}{G}_{ll^{\prime}}^{t}(t,t_{1}){\cal G}_{k}^{t}(t_{1},t^{\prime}).

(32)

The time-contoured Green function shares the same Feynman rule as the time-ordered one, which allows us to
use Langreth theorem
[
52
]
to obtain the “lesser” Green function (in the frequency space)

G_{lk}^{<}(\omega)=\sum_{l^{\prime}}g_{k,l^{\prime}}\big[{G}_{ll^{\prime}}^{r}(\omega){\cal G}^{<}_{k}(\omega)+{G}_{ll^{\prime}}^{<}(\omega){\cal G}_{k}^{a}(\omega)\big].

(33)

Thus, the pumping rate is calculated to be

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{R(L)}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{k>0(<0)}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}{\rm Re}({g_{k,l}^{*}G^{r}_{ll^{\prime}}(\omega)}g_{k,l^{\prime}}{\cal G}^{<}_{k}(\omega)

\displaystyle+g_{k,l}^{*}G^{<}_{ll^{\prime}}(\omega)g_{k,l^{\prime}}{\cal G}^{a}_{k}(\omega)).

(34)

With complete nonreciprocity, one of
{\cal P}_{R}
and
{\cal P}_{L}
vanishes, indicating that the pumped phonon flows unidirectionally in half of the elastic substrate.

For the phonon, the “lesser” and advanced Green functions are given by
{\cal G}_{k}^{<}(\omega)=2\pi if(\omega)\delta(\omega-\omega_{k})
, with
f(\omega)=1/(e^{\omega/(k_{B}T)}-1)
being the Bose-Einstein distribution at temperature
T
, and
{\cal G}_{k}^{a}(\omega)={1}/({\omega-\omega_{k}-i\eta_{k}})
, respectively. The retarded Green function of magnons
G_{N}^{r}(\omega)
is given by Eq. (
19
), while the advanced Green function
G_{N}^{a}(\omega)=G_{N}^{r*}(\omega)
. These two Green function defines the spectra function
{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)=i(G^{r}_{N}(\omega)-G_{N}^{a}(\omega))
, and, from fluctuation-dissipation theorem, we have
[
52
]

\displaystyle G_{ll^{\prime}}^{<}(\omega)

\displaystyle=iF(\omega){\mathcal{A}}_{ll^{\prime}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-F(\omega)\left(G_{N}^{r}(\omega)-G_{N}^{r*}(\omega)\right)_{ll^{\prime}},

(35)

where
F(\omega)
parameterizes the magnon nonequlibrium distribution. Accordingly, we can demonstrate
{\cal I}(\omega)=\sum_{ll^{\prime}}g_{k,l}^{*}G_{ll^{\prime}}^{<}(\omega)g_{k,l^{\prime}}
is purely imaginary as
{\cal I}^{*}(\omega)=-{\mathcal{I}}(\omega)
. The phonon injection rates then read

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{R(L)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k>0(<0)}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}{\rm Re}\left(ig_{k,l}^{*}G_{ll^{\prime}}^{r}(\omega_{k})g_{k,l^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\times(F(\omega_{k})-f(\omega_{k}))

\displaystyle-\sum_{k>0(<0)}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}{\rm Re}\left(ig_{k,l}^{*}G_{ll^{\prime}}^{r}(\omega_{k})g_{k,l^{\prime}}\right)f(\omega_{k})

\displaystyle-\sum_{k>0(<0)}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}{\rm Re}\left(ig_{k,l}^{*}G_{ll^{\prime}}^{r*}(\omega_{k})g_{k,l^{\prime}}\right)F(\omega_{k}).

(36)

At the thermal equilibrium,
F(\omega_{k})=f(\omega_{k})
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{R(L)}
vanishes. The injection rate is closely related to the phonon transmission as
\sum_{ll^{\prime}}ig_{k,l}^{*}G_{ll^{\prime}}^{r}(\omega_{k})g_{k,l^{\prime}}=c_{r}(1-S_{21}(\omega_{k}))
from Eq. (
15
).

Usually, the injection rate with one magnetic nanowire is not large, and we may envision the injection rate can be improved with many nanowires.
Therefore, we are particularly interested in the scaling relation with respect to the number of the nanowire.
To this end, we adopt, for simplicity, a monochromatic microwave of frequency
\omega_{\rm K}
to resonantly excite the magnetic wires and
F(\omega_{k})\rightarrow f(\omega_{k})+\delta f\delta(\omega_{k}-\omega_{\rm K})
, with which Eq. (
36
) is reduced to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{R(L)}=({1}/{c_{r}}){\mathcal{T}}_{R(L)}\delta f,

(37)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}{\rm Re}\left(ig_{k_{*},l}^{*}(G_{ll^{\prime}}^{r}(\omega_{\rm K})-G_{ll^{\prime}}^{r*}(\omega_{\rm K}))g_{k_{*},l^{\prime}}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{ll^{\prime}}{\rm Re}\left(ig_{-k_{*},l}^{*}(G_{ll^{\prime}}^{r}(\omega_{\rm K})-G_{ll^{\prime}}^{r*}(\omega_{\rm K}))g_{-k_{*},l^{\prime}}\right).

(38)

Again, we employ the Ni nanowire on top of GGG with the same parameters as in Figs.
4
and
5
and tune the magnetic field to the critical angle such that
{\mathcal{T}}_{L}=0
.
Figure
6
shows the approximate linear scaling of
{\cal T}_{R}
with respect to the nanowire number, suggesting an large injection rate with a nanowire array of high magnetic quality.

VI
Discussion and summary

In conclusion, we formulate the nonreciprocal phonon transmission by acoustic pumping of magnon and its inverse process, the chiral pumping of phonon by ferromagnetic resonance. The model device we consider is one to many magnetic nanowires on top of a high-quality acoustic insulator rather than the extended magnetic film. We employ nanowire with thickness much smaller than the wavelength of the SAWs, in which situation the effect from the shear strain on the upper and lower surface cancels, different from that of thick films
[
19
;
68
]
. Therefore, in the wire setup the nonreciprocity comes from the edge effect that is sensitive to the wire width and is strong when the wire width is comparable to the SAW wavelength. Both processes, associated with the phonon diode effect and unidirectional phonon current in half space, have high efficiency when the magnetic quality of the wire is high, but the efficiency is significantly enhanced by increasing the number of wire that allows to use material with ordinary magnetic quality to realize similar functionalities. The microwave transmission mediated by two remote magnetic wires that interact by virtual exchange of phonons is phase sensitive and hence can be used to detect, e.g., the phonon group velocity and wire distance, which could be a unique way to measure the coherent phonon propagation.

The nonreciprocal magnon-phonon interaction comes from the chirality of magnon and rotation-momentum locking of surface phonon. Classically, we calculate the rotating forces at the edge of nanomagnet and show its relation with the magnon chirality. We use a quantum formalism and employ the Green function method to universally describe the chiral dynamics between magnon and other quasiparticles including photon
[
35
;
36
]
, other magnon
[
28
;
29
;
30
;
31
]
, electron
[
42
]
and phonon. We demonstrate the non-Hermitian interaction between wires lead to the collective motion of magnons that has influence on the phonon diode effect, microwave transmission, and chiral phonon pumping.

Magnetization dynamics can control the propagation of surface phonon in gigahertz frequency, much higher than the electric control in megahertz frequency
[
69
;
70
]
.
Replacing the magnetic nanowire by the various nanomagnet configurations such as the nano-disks is an interesting extension. As addressed in the text, the gain by a negative Gilbert damping
[
65
;
64
;
66
;
67
]
can add amplification functionality on the basis of the nonreciprocity that could be useful in the future applications in the logic device.
An inserted heavy metal between the nanomagnet and acoustic insulator may induce the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction that influences the nonreciprocity as well, which could be a possiblity to improve the magnetoelasitic nonreciprocity
[
25
;
71
;
72
]
.

(39)

where
q=\sqrt{k^{2}-k_{l}^{2}}
and
s=\sqrt{k^{2}-k_{t}^{2}}
with

k_{l}=\omega_{k}\sqrt{\rho/(\lambda+2\mu)}
and
k_{t}=\omega_{k}\sqrt{\rho/\mu}
being the wave vectors for longitudinal and transverse bulk waves, respectively. Here,
\mu
and
\lambda
are the elastic Lamé constants,
\omega_{k}=c_{r}|k|
represents the eigenfrequency of Rayleigh SAWs with velocity
c_{r}
, and
\varphi_{k}
is a normalization constant. The opposite relative phase of the displacement field
{\mathrm{Arg}}(\psi_{y}/\psi_{x})|_{x=0}=\pm i
for left- and right-propagating waves indicates the rotation-momentum locking of SAWs.
The displacement field
(\hat{u}_{x},\,\hat{u}_{y})
is quantized by the eigenmodes
\boldsymbol{\psi}(k)
and phonon operators
\hat{b}_{k}(t)

\hat{\mathbf{u}}(x,y,t)=\sum_{k}\left[\boldsymbol{\psi}(x,y,k)\hat{b}_{k}(t)+\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\ast}(x,y,k)\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}(t)\right].

(40)

The mode amplitudes
\boldsymbol{\psi}
are then normalized to recover the elastic
Hamiltonian of Rayleigh SAWs with

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{e}}=\rho\int d\boldsymbol{r}\,\dot{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{2}(x,z,t)=\sum_{k}\hbar\omega_{k}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k},

(41)

leading to the normalization condition

\int_{-\infty}^{0}dx\left(|\psi_{x}|^{2}+|\psi_{y}|^{2}\right)=\frac{\hbar}{2\rho L\omega_{k}}.

(42)

We then obtain the normalization factor

\varphi_{k}=\frac{1}{|k|}\frac{1+b^{2}}{2a(1-b^{2})}\sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{\rho Lc_{r}}}\,\xi_{P},

(43)

where the factor

\xi_{\rm P}=\frac{a(1-b^{2})}{1+b^{2}}\left(\frac{1+a^{2}}{2a}+\frac{2a(a-2b)}{b(1+b^{2})}\right)^{-1/2},

(44)

with dimensionless material constants

\begin{split}a&=q/\left|k\right|=\sqrt{1-(c_{r}/c_{l})^{2}},\\
b&=s/\left|k\right|=\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}.\end{split}

(45)

Here
c_{r}=\eta\sqrt{\mu/\rho}
and
c_{l}=\sqrt{(\lambda+2\mu)/\rho}
are, respectively, the sound velocities of the surface and longitudinal bulk waves.

For the magnetic nanowire, we focus on the case with a large applied magnetic field
H_{0}
to saturate the magnetization along the
z^{\prime}
-axis (see Fig.
1
). In the wire
\{xyz\}
-coordinate,
m_{x}=m_{x^{\prime}}
,
m_{y}=m_{y^{\prime}}\cos\varphi+m_{z^{\prime}}\sin\varphi
and
m_{z}=-m_{y^{\prime}}\sin\varphi+m_{z^{\prime}}\cos\varphi
.
Focusing on the Kittel magnon,

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{m}}=\mu_{0}\int d\boldsymbol{r}\,\left(\hat{m}_{z^{\prime}}H_{0}+\frac{1}{2}N_{xx}\hat{m}_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}N_{yy}\hat{m}_{y}^{2}\right),

(46)

where
\mu_{0}
is the vacuum permeability, and
N_{xx}\simeq w/(d+w)
and
N_{yy}\simeq d/(d+w)
are the demagnetization constants with the nanowire width
w
and thickness
d

[
29
;
46
]
. Here the demagnetization factors are treated to be uniform across the wire by disregarding their spatial variation at the edges of nanowires.
With the magnetic field applied opposite to the
\hat{\bf z}^{\prime}
-direction (Fig.
1
in the main text), the magnetization is not parallel to the applied magnetic field due to the demagnetization field. We thus assume that the magnetization is opposite to the
\tilde{\bf z}^{\prime}
-direction, with an angle
\theta
with respect to the
-\hat{\bf z}^{\prime}
-direction, as shown in Fig.
7
.

Figure 7:
Configuration of equilibrium magnetization under an applied magnetic field of general direction.

The free-energy density in the static situation reads

\displaystyle f_{m}=-\mu_{0}M_{s}H_{0}\cos\theta+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}N_{yy}(M_{s}\sin(\varphi-\theta))^{2}.

(47)

The minimum of the free energy
df_{m}/d\theta=0
leads to

\displaystyle\theta=N_{yy}\sin\varphi\cos\varphi M_{s}/H_{0},

(48)

when
H_{0}\gg M_{s}
, which is the situation we focus on.
With knowing the static configuration, the effective magnetic field in the dynamical situation reads

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=-H_{0}\cos\theta\tilde{\bf z}^{\prime}-H_{0}\sin\theta\tilde{\bf y}^{\prime}-N_{xx}m_{x^{\prime}}\tilde{\bf x}^{\prime}

\displaystyle-N_{yy}(m_{y^{\prime}}\cos\varphi^{\prime}+m_{z^{\prime}}\sin\varphi^{\prime})(\tilde{\bf y}^{\prime}\cos\varphi^{\prime}+\tilde{\bf z}^{\prime}\sin\varphi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\approx[-H_{0}-N_{yy}(m_{y^{\prime}}\sin\varphi\cos\varphi-M_{s}\sin^{2}\varphi)]\tilde{\bf z}^{\prime}

\displaystyle-N_{xx}m_{x^{\prime}}\tilde{\bf x}^{\prime}-N_{yy}m_{y^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\varphi\tilde{\bf y}^{\prime},

(49)

in which
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi-\theta
and we have used Eq. (
48
) in the second step.
With this magnetic field, the Landau-Lifshitz equation
d{\bf m}/dt=-\mu_{0}\gamma{\bf m}\times{\bf H}_{\rm eff}
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{x^{\prime}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\mu_{0}\gamma(-H_{0}-M_{s}N_{yy}\cos^{2}\varphi+N_{yy}M_{s}\sin^{2}\varphi)m_{y^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{y^{\prime}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\mu_{0}\gamma(H_{0}-N_{xx}M_{s}-N_{yy}M_{s}\sin^{2}\varphi)m_{x^{\prime}},

(50)

from which we can find the Kittel frequency

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm K}

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{(H_{0}+M_{s}N_{yy}\cos(2\varphi))}

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{(H_{0}+N_{xx}M_{s}-N_{yy}M_{s}\sin^{2}\varphi)}

\displaystyle\approx\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0},

(51)

which generally exists when
H_{0}\gg M_{s}
.

Our simple nanowire configuration is different from surface-modulated magnonic crystals or ferromagnetic nanogratings, where the Kittel mode is reported to exist in a region of magnetization direction with
\varphi\lesssim\pi/3

[
73
;
74
;
75
]
. In the latter cases, the demagnetization field could develop a more complicated distribution due to the special geomerty of magnetizations.

The transverse magnetization is then quantized by the Kittel-magnon operator

\hat{\beta}(t)
with wave function
\tilde{m}_{\chi=\{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\}}({\bf r})
:

\hat{\mathbf{m}}_{\chi}({\bf r})=-\sqrt{2\gamma\hbar M_{s}}\left(\tilde{m}_{\chi}({\bf r})\hat{\beta}(t)+\tilde{m}_{\chi}^{\ast}({\bf r})\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}(t)\right).

(52)

The magnon amplitudes
\tilde{m}_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}
satisfy the normalization condition
[
28
]

\int d\mathbf{r}\left(\tilde{m}_{x^{\prime}}({\bf r})\tilde{m}_{y^{\prime}}^{\ast}({\bf r})-\tilde{m}_{x^{\prime}}^{\ast}({\bf r})\tilde{m}_{y^{\prime}}({\bf r})\right)=-\frac{i}{2}.

(53)

With a large
H_{0}\gg M_{s}
, the magnon is circularly polarized with
\tilde{m}_{y^{\prime}}=i\tilde{m}_{x^{\prime}}
such that

\tilde{m}_{x^{\prime}}\simeq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Lwd}},\quad\tilde{m}_{y^{\prime}}\simeq\frac{i}{2\sqrt{Lwd}},

(54)

and
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{m}}=\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{K}}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{\beta}
with frequency
\omega_{\rm K}\simeq\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}
.

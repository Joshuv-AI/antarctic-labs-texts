Condenser capacities and capacitary potentials for unbounded sets, and global p p\mspace{1mu} -harmonic Green functions on metric spaces
Anders Björn

Abstract




Abstract
.
We study the condenser capacity
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)
on
unbounded
open sets
\Omega
in
a proper connected metric space
X

equipped with a locally doubling measure
supporting a local
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality, where
1<p<\infty
.
Using a new definition of capacitary potentials,
we show that
\capp_{p}
is countably subadditive and that it is a
Choquet capacity.
We next obtain formulas for the capacity
of superlevel sets for the capacitary potential.
These are then used to show that

p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic Green functions exist in an unbounded domain
\Omega
if and only if
either
X
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-hyperbolic or the Sobolev capacity
{C_{p}}(X\setminus\Omega)>0
.
As an application, we deduce new results for Perron solutions and boundary regularity
for the Dirichlet boundary value problem for
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions
in unbounded open sets.

Key words and phrases
:
boundary regularity,
condenser capacity,
capacitary potential,
Dirichlet problem,
locally doubling measure,
global
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic Green function,
metric space,
Perron solution,

p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic function,

p\mspace{1mu}
-hyperbolic space,
local Poincaré inequality,
singular function.

Introduction

It is well known that every domain
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, has
a
Green function
,
i.e a solution of
the Poisson equation
-\Delta u=\delta_{x_{0}}
in
\Omega

with zero boundary data on
\partial\Omega
and
at
\infty
when
\Omega
is unbounded.
Here
\delta_{x_{0}}
is the Dirac distribution at
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
For
n=2
, Green functions exist only in domains
\Omega

that are
Greenian
,
a property equivalent to the complement
\mathbf{R}^{2}\setminus\Omega
being a nonpolar set,
see e.g. Armitage–Gardiner
[
4
, Theorem 5.3.8]
or
Doob
[
28
, Theorems 1.V.6 and 1.VII.7]
.
In particular, the plane itself is not Greenian.

This distinction between the plane and higher-dimensional spaces is
reflected in many other phenomena, such as the (non)existence of nonnegative
nonconstant superharmonic functions or
the recurrent/transient behaviour of the Brownian motion, and can be
captured by the notions of
parabolic
and
hyperbolic
spaces.
An analogue in the setting of nonlinear PDEs, such as for
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions,
is that for all
n>p>1
, the
p\mspace{1mu}
-Green function

u(x)=C_{n,p}|x-x_{0}|^{(p-n)/(p-1)},

with a suitable constant
C_{n,p}>0
, satisfies

\Delta_{p}u:=\Div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=-\delta_{x_{0}}\quad\text{in }\mathbf{R}^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x)=0,

while there is no such
p\mspace{1mu}
-Green function for
n\leq p
.
Green functions are useful tools for PDEs
and together with potentials play an important role
in classical potential theory.

In this paper, we study the above notions for unbounded sets
in rather general metric measure spaces.
More precisely, let for the rest of the introduction (unless
said otherwise)

X
be
a proper connected metric space
equipped with a locally doubling measure
\mu

supporting a local
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality, where
1<p<\infty
.

\Omega
will always denote a nonempty open set.
Weighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
equipped with a
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissible weight
as in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
34
]
,
and (sub)Riemannian manifolds, as in
Coulhon–Holopainen–Saloff-Coste
[
26
]

and Holopainen
[
37
]
,
[
38
]
, are included as special cases.
So are
Heisenberg and Carnot groups,
vector fields satisfying the Hörmander condition,
and many other situations,
see Hajłasz–Koskela
[
31
, Sections 10–13]
.

In the generality of metric spaces,
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic
functions are
defined as continuous minimizers of the
p\mspace{1mu}
-energy integral

\int g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu,

(1.1)

where

is the minimal
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient of

.
The associated superminimizers then give rise to
p\mspace{1mu}
-superharmonic
functions,
see Section
4

Our results and proofs
also apply if the

-harmonicity is replaced by

-harmonicity, cf. Cheeger
[
24
, Remark 7.19 and pp. 434, 462]

In that case, the

-weak upper gradient

By the discussion in

-energy integral

Our approach does not require Cheeger’s differentiable structure and is based solely on

Theorem 1.2

Assume that

\Omega

while for unbounded

,

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega):=\lim_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E\cap B(x_{0},j),\Omega),

where

is an arbitrary point,
see Definition

Capacitary potentials for
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)

are a useful tool in our investigations.
When

is bounded, they are defined as (regularized)
admissible functions minimizing the

-energy
(

For unbounded

.
We therefore give a new definition (

) of capacitary

using superharmonic functions.
It coincides with the older definition
for bounded
\Omega
but exists also for unbounded

and
\Omega
.
A different construction for compact

in Riemannian manifolds
using exhaustions by bounded sets
appears in Holopainen
[
37

.
We include noncompact

in our investigations.

Even though the capacitary potential
Q^{\Omega}_{E}
need not be an
admissible function for the capacity,
its energy is always equal to

provided that

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)<\infty
,
see Propositions

and

.
However, it can happen that the energy of

is
finite even when the capacity is infinite,
see Examples

and
6.8
.
In fact,
Q^{X}_{X}\equiv 1
regardless of whether
\capp_{p}(X,X)=0
or
\capp_{p}(X,X)=\infty

(these are the only possibilities by Proposition

).

On (sub)Riemannian manifolds, condenser capacities were
used in connection with Green functions in

[
37
]
,
[

]
and

The outline of the paper is as follows:
In Section

.

(

2
Upper gradients and Newtonian spaces

We assume throughout the paper
that
1\leq p<\infty
and that

X=(X,d,\mu)
is a metric space equipped
with a metric
d
and a positive complete Borel measure
\mu

such that
0<\mu(B)<\infty
for all
balls
B\subset X
.
We also fix a point
x_{0}\in X
, write
B_{r}=B(x_{0},r)
,
and assume that
\Omega\subset X
is a nonempty open set.
In this and the next section we do not impose any other assumptions
on the space or the measure.
Additional standing
assumptions are added at the beginning of Section
4
.

In this section, we introduce
the necessary metric space concepts used in this paper.
Proofs of the results in
this section can be found in the monographs
Björn–Björn
[
7
]
and
Heinonen–Koskela–Shanmugalingam–Tyson
[
36
]
.
Note that it follows from the
assumption
0<\mu(B)<\infty
for all balls
B

that
X
is separable.

A
curve
is a continuous mapping from an interval,
and a
rectifiable
curve is a curve with finite length.
We will only consider curves which are nonconstant, compact and rectifiable.
A curve can thus be parameterized by its arc length
ds
.
A property holds for
p\mspace{1mu}
-almost every curve

if the curve family
\Gamma
for which it fails has zero
p\mspace{1mu}
-modulus,
i.e. there is
\rho\in L^{p}(X)
such that

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds=\infty
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Following Heinonen–Koskela
[
35
]
and Koskela–MacManus
[
50
]

we define
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradients.

Definition 2.1
.

A measurable function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient

of
f:X\to{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}:=[-\infty,\infty]

if for
p\mspace{1mu}
-almost all curves

\gamma:[0,l_{\gamma}]\to X
,

|f(\gamma(0))-f(\gamma(l_{\gamma}))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds,

where the left-hand side is
\infty

whenever at least one of the
terms therein is infinite.

If
f
has a
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient in
L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X)
, then
it has a
minimal
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient

g_{f}\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X)

in the sense that

g_{f}\leq g
a.e. for every
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient
g\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X)
of
f
.
The Newtonian Sobolev space on a metric space
X
was
introduced as follows by
Shanmugalingam
[
54
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For measurable
f
, let

\|f\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}=\biggl(\int_{X}|f|^{p}\,d\mu+\inf_{g}\int_{X}g^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p},

where the infimum is taken over all
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradients of
f
.
The
Newtonian space
on
X
is

N^{1,p}(X)=\{f:\|f\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}<\infty\}.

The space
N^{1,p}(X)/{\sim}
, where
f\sim h
if and only if
\|f-h\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}=0
,
is a Banach space and a lattice.
The
Dirichlet space

D^{p}(X)
is the collection of all
measurable functions on
X

that have a
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient in
L^{p}(X)
.
In this paper, it is convenient to
assume that functions in
N^{1,p}
and
D^{p}

are defined everywhere (with values in
{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
),
not just up to an equivalence class in the corresponding function space.

We say that
f\in N^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(X)
if
for every
x\in X
there exists a ball
B_{x}\ni x
such that

f\in N^{1,p}(B_{x})
.
If
f,h\in N^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(X)
,
then
g_{f}=g_{h}
a.e. in
\{x\in X:f(x)=h(x)\}
.
In particular
g_{\min\{f,c\}}=g_{f}\mathbbm{1}_{\{f<c\}}
a.e. in
X

for
c\in\mathbf{R}
, where
\mathbbm{1}
denotes the characteristic function.
For a measurable set
E\subset X
, the
spaces
N^{1,p}(E)
and
N^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(E)
are defined by
considering
(E,d|_{E},\mu|_{E})
as a metric space in its own right.

The
Sobolev capacity
of an arbitrary set
E\subset X
is

{C_{p}}(E)=\inf_{f}\|f\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}^{p},

(2.1)

where the infimum is taken over all
f\in N^{1,p}(X)
such that

f\geq 1
on
E
.
It is easy to see that the Sobolev capacity is countably subadditive.
For further properties, see
e.g.
[
7
]
, Eriksson-Bique–Poggi-Corradini
[
29
]

and Heinonen–Koskela–Shanmugalingam–Tyson
[
36
]
.

A property holds
quasieverywhere
(q.e.) if the set of points for which it fails
has Sobolev capacity zero.
The Sobolev capacity is the correct gauge
for distinguishing between two Newtonian functions:

h\sim f
if and only if
h=f
q.e.
Moreover, if
f,h\in N^{1,p}(X)
and
f=h
a.e., then
f=h
q.e.

For a ball
B=B(x,r)
with centre
x
and radius
r
, we let

\lambda B=B(x,\lambda r)
. In metric spaces
it can happen that balls with different centres or
radii denote the same set.
We will, however,
make the convention that a ball
B
comes with a predetermined
centre and radius.
All balls are assumed to be open in this paper.
By
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
we mean the closure of the open ball

,
not the (possibly larger) set
\{y:d(x,y)\leq r\}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The measure

is
doubling within
\Omega

if there is

such that
\mu(2B)\leq C\mu(B)
for all balls

.

Similarly, the

-Poincaré inequality holds within

if there are constants

and

such that for all balls

all integrable functions

-weak upper gradients

\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{B}|u-u_{B}|\,d\mu\leq Cr_{B}\biggl(\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{\lambda B}g^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p},

where
u_{B}:=\mathop{\mathchoice{\kern 1.28333pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.78336pt}{\kern 1.16664pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.50002pt}\kern-4.66666pt}{\kern 1.02428pt\vbox{\hrule width=3.07292pt}\kern-4.09721pt}{\kern 0.96526pt\vbox{\hrule width=2.89586pt}\kern-3.86111pt}}\mathopen{}\int_{B}u\,d\mu:=\int_{B}u\,d\mu/\mu(B)
.

Each of these properties is called
local
if
for every
x\in X
there is

such that the property holds within
B(x,r)
.
If a property holds within
\Omega=X
, then it is called

.

In
\mathbf{R}^{n}
equipped with a globally doubling measure

,

-Poincaré inequality with

-admissibility

]

]
.
Moreover, in this case
g_{u}=|\nabla u|
a.e. if

,
where
\nabla u
is the gradient from
[
34
]
.

The space
X
is
proper
if
all closed bounded subsets are compact.
A

is a nonempty connected open set.
As usual, we write
f_{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}=\max\{f,0\}
.
In this paper, a continuous function is always assumed
to be real-valued (as opposed to
{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
-valued).
As usual, by
E\Subset\Omega
we mean that
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}

.

We will need the following
D^{p}
-version of
Corollary 6.3 in

]

Lemma 2.4

Assume that

Let

3
The condenser capacity

Recall the standing assumptions from the beginning of Section
2
.
We define the condenser capacity in the following way.
It is also called “variational” (e.g. in
[
7
]
)
and “relative” in various parts of the literature.

Definition 3.1
.

The
condenser capacity
of a bounded set

E\subset\Omega
with respect to (a possibly unbounded)
\Omega
is

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)=\inf_{u}\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu,

(3.1)

where the infimum is taken over all
u\in N^{1,p}(X)

such that
\mathbbm{1}_{E}\leq u\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}
.
We call such
u

admissible
for

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)
.
If no such function
u
exists then
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)=\infty
.
We also let
\capp_{p}(\varnothing,\varnothing)=0
.
If
E\subset\Omega
is unbounded, we define

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E\cap B_{j},\Omega).

(3.2)

It is easy to see that the limit in (
3.2
)
does not depend on the choice of
x_{0}
in
B_{j}=B(x_{0},j)
.

There are other condenser capacities appearing in the
nonlinear metric space literature,
such as
\capp_{D^{p}}
, which is defined for disjoint sets
E
and
F
by

\capp_{D^{p}}(E,F)=\inf_{u}\int_{X}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu,

with the infimum taken over all functions
u\in D^{p}(X)
with
u=1
on
E

and
u=0
on
F
.
Without the measurability requirement for
u\in D^{p}(X)
,
the corresponding capacity
\overline{\capp}_{p}(E,F)

was defined in
Heinonen–Koskela
[
35
, (2.12)]
.
They showed that it
can equivalently be defined using the
p\mspace{1mu}
-modulus as

\overline{\capp}_{p}(E,F)=\Mod_{p}(E,F):=\inf_{\rho}\int_{X}\rho^{p}\,d\mu,

where the infimum is taken over all
Borel functions
\rho\geq 0
such that

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds\geq 1
for every
curve
\gamma
in
X
with one end point in
E
and the other in
F
,
see
[
35
, Proposition 2.17]
.
(Their proof of this identity holds for arbitrary
E
and
F
.)
These condenser capacities have also been studied
by e.g. Kallunki–Shanmugalingam
[
41
]
,
Björn–Björn–Shanmugalingam
[
20
]

and Eriksson-Bique–Poggi-Corradini
[
29
]
.

For closed
E
and
F
in a locally complete space
X
,
it was shown in
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
that

\overline{\capp}_{p}(E,F)=\capp_{D^{p}}(E,F)=\capp_{p}^{\Lip}(E,F),

where
\capp_{p}^{\Lip}(E,F)
is defined as
\capp_{D^{p}}(E,F)
but
using only locally Lipschitz functions
u
.
(The separability assumption in
[
29
]

is redundant since they assume (as here) that balls have positive and
finite measure.)

The papers
[
20
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
41
]

only consider condenser capacities for closed sets, and do not discuss
such properties as those in Theorems
1.5
and
1.6

for condenser capacities.

When
\Omega
is bounded, we have
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)=\capp_{D^{p}}(E,X\setminus\Omega)

for arbitrary
E\subset\Omega
,
but this is not at all true for unbounded
\Omega
, see Example
3.4
below.
In particular, if
\Omega=X
then

\capp_{D^{p}}(E,X\setminus\Omega)=\capp_{D^{p}}(E,\varnothing)=0
for every
E
,
so
\capp_{D^{p}}
is not well adapted
for studying global Green functions,
which are normalized by (
1.3
).
In the rest of the paper (except for Example
3.4
),
we therefore do not consider such capacities.

The basic properties of
\capp_{p}
are quite well understood for bounded
\Omega
,
see e.g. Björn–Björn
[
7
, Section 6.3]
and
[
8
]
, as well as
Costea
[
25
]
, Lehrbäck
[
52
]
and
Martio
[
53
]

with slightly different definitions.
But there seem to be few such studies
for unbounded
\Omega
in metric spaces.
In weighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
(with a somewhat different definition of
\capp_{p}
),
this was done in
Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
34
, Chapter 2]
.

That the Sobolev capacity (
2.1
) is
a Choquet (and in particular outer) capacity
has been shown under various assumptions, most recently by
Eriksson-Bique–Poggi-Corradini
[
29
, Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.6]

only assuming that
X
is locally complete
(in addition to the assumptions from the beginning of Section
2
).

In general metric spaces, the zero sets for the
Sobolev capacity (
2.1
)
determine the equivalence classes in
the Newtonian space
N^{1,p}(X)

\varepsilon>0
.
Then
V:=\{x\in\Omega:v(x)>\varepsilon\}
is bounded
since

\limsup_{x\to\infty}v(x)\leq\mathbbm{1}_{E}(\infty)=0\quad\text{if $\Omega$ is unbounded}.

Let
R>1/\varepsilon
be such that
B_{R}\Supset V
.
Set
G=\Omega\cap B_{R}
(
G=\Omega
if
\Omega
is bounded)
and
E^{\prime}=E\cap B_{R}
.
Since
v
is subharmonic and upper semicontinuous, we have

v-\varepsilon\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime}}}(G)
and hence
v-\varepsilon\leq{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{G}\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime}}
.

Let
I_{G}
be the set of irregular boundary points for
G
.
It follows from Lemma
5.6
that

E^{\prime}\setminus I_{G}\subset E^{\prime}\setminus I
,
and from the Kellogg property (Theorem
5.2
) that

{C_{p}}(I_{G})=0
.
Note that
\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime}}
is lower semicontinuous on
\partial G
.
Proposition 7.3
in Björn–Björn–Shanmugalingam
[
17
]

(or
[
7
, Proposition 10.32]
),
applied on the bounded set
G
to the boundary data

f=-\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime}}
and
f+h=-\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime}\setminus I_{G}}
,
together with Lemma
4.3
, then implies

v-\varepsilon\leq{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{G}\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime}}=P_{G}\mathbbm{1}_{E^{\prime}\setminus I_{G}}\leq{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}\quad\text{in }G.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
(and thus
R\to\infty
) gives

v\leq{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Taking the supremum over all
v\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E}}(\Omega)

shows that
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\leq{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}
,
which together with (
4.2
) and (
5.1
)
concludes the proof.
∎

6
Capacitary potentials

If
\Omega
is bounded
and
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)<\infty
, then there exists
u\in N^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

such that
\mathbbm{1}_{E}\leq u\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}
q.e. in
\Omega
and

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu.

(6.1)

If in addition
{C_{p}}(X\setminus\Omega)>0
, then
u

can be obtained as the unique
lsc-regularized solution

of the
{\cal K}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E},0}(\Omega)
-obstacle problem, i.e. it satisfies

u=u^{*}
in the sense of (
4.1
) and

\int_{\Omega}g^{p}_{u}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}g^{p}_{v}\,d\mu\quad\text{for all $v\in N^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)$ with $v\geq\mathbbm{1}_{E}$ q.e.},

see
[
7
, Definition 11.15 and Lemma 11.19]
.
When extended by
0
outside
\Omega
, this function
is called the
capacitary potential
for
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)
.
It is superharmonic and a superminimizer in
\Omega
.

After a modification on the set
\{x\in E:u(x)<1\}
, the
solution
u
of the
{\cal K}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E},0}(\Omega)
-obstacle problem
is also admissible for
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)
.
In fact, minimizing admissible functions, i.e. those fulfilling (
6.1
),
exist under much weaker assumptions than here, provided that
\Omega
is bounded,
see Björn–Björn
[
9
, Theorem 4.2 and Remark 5.6]
.
(The same proof applies also when
\Omega
is unbounded and
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
.)

In contrast, the following result shows that when
\Omega
is unbounded there need
not exist any minimizing admissible function for
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume that
X
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic and that
\mu(X)=\infty
.
Then

\capp_{p}(E,X)=0\quad\text{for every set $E\subset X$.}

(6.2)

Moreover, if
{C_{p}}(E)>0
then there is
no admissible function
u
for
\capp_{p}(E,X)
with

\int_{X}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu=0
, i.e. the infimum in (
3.1
)
is not attained for
\capp_{p}(E,X)
.

That (
6.2
) holds follows directly from Definitions
3.1

and
4.7
.

If there was
an admissible function for
\capp_{p}(E,X)
with

\int_{X}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu=0
, then by the local
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality
and the connectedness of
X
, it would be constant a.e. (and thus q.e. by Proposition 1.59 in
[
7
]
).
Since
{C_{p}}(E)>0
, it would follow
that
u=1
a.e. in
X
, which is impossible
as
\mu(X)=\infty
and
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
.
∎

Despite the fact that there may not be any minimizing admissible function,
our
next aim is to construct suitable
capacitary potentials also for unbounded
\Omega
.
(As a by-product we obtain them also when
X
is bounded and

{C_{p}}(X\setminus\Omega)=0
.)
For any open
\Omega
, we define the
capacitary potential
as

\displaystyle Q^{\Omega}_{E}(x)=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\inf\{\psi(x):\psi\in\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}\},&\text{if }x\in\Omega,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(6.3)

\displaystyle\quad\text{where }\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}=\{\psi:\psi\text{ is superharmonic in $\Omega$ and $\psi\geq\mathbbm{1}_{E}$ q.e.}\}.

This is a special case of the
Q
-balayage studied in
Björn–Björn–Mäkäläinen–Parviainen
[
15
]
,
where the function in (
6.3
)
is denoted

Q^{1}_{E}=Q^{\mathbbm{1}_{E}}=\widehat{Q}^{1}_{E}=\widehat{Q}^{\mathbbm{1}_{E}}

with
\Omega
implicit.
Because of the q.e.-inequality in (
6.3
), this definition
differs from the usual balayage and potential definition in e.g. Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
34
, Chapter 8]
.
In particular, here we do not need to lsc-regularize the infimum in (
6.3
).
By Theorem 4.4 in
[
15
]
,
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\in\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}
,
and it is therefore the minimal superharmonic function in
\Omega

which is
\geq\mathbbm{1}_{E}
q.e. in
\Omega
.
It is convenient to have
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\equiv 0
on
X\setminus\Omega
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}\subset\dots\subset\Omega=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{j}

be open sets.

If
E_{1}\subset E_{2}\subset\dots\subset E=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}E_{j}

and
E_{j}\subset\Omega_{j}
,
j=1,2,\dots
,
then

Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}\nearrow Q^{\Omega}_{E}\quad\text{everywhere in }X,\text{ as }j\to\infty.

In particular, if
E_{1}\subset E_{2}
,
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}

and
E_{j}\subset\Omega_{j}
,
j=1,2
,
then
Q^{\Omega_{1}}_{E_{1}}\leq Q^{\Omega_{2}}_{E_{2}}
.

The restriction of any function in
\Psi^{\Omega_{j+1}}_{E_{j+1}}

belongs to
\Psi^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}
and thus

Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}\leq Q^{\Omega_{j+1}}_{E_{j+1}}
.
Similarly,
Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}\leq Q^{\Omega}_{E}
.
So the limit
u:=\lim_{j\to\infty}Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}
exists and

u\leq Q^{\Omega}_{E}
.

For the reverse inequality, note first that

u
is a bounded increasing limit of superharmonic functions
and thus itself superharmonic in
\Omega
,
by Lemma 7.1 in Kinnunen–Martio
[
46
]

(or
[
7
, Theorem 9.27]
) and
[
7
, Proposition 9.21]
.
Moreover,

u\geq\mathbbm{1}_{E_{j}}
q.e. for each
j=1,2,\dots
, and
thus
u\geq\mathbbm{1}_{E}
q.e.
Hence
u\in\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}

and
u=0
outside
\Omega
, and so
u\geq Q^{\Omega}_{E}
.
∎

Next we
show that our definition
of capacitary potentials agrees with the one in
(
6.1
)
when that one is defined.
For
E\Subset\Omega
this follows from Theorem 5.3 in
Björn–Björn–Mäkäläinen–Parviainen
[
15
]
.

Proposition 6.3
.

If
\Omega
is bounded,
{C_{p}}(X\setminus\Omega)>0
and
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)<\infty
, then

Q^{\Omega}_{E}
is the unique
lsc-regularized solution of the
{\cal K}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E},0}(\Omega)
-obstacle problem
as in (
6.1
).

Let
u_{j}
be the unique lsc-regularized solution
of the
{\cal K}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E_{j}},0}(\Omega)
-obstacle problem, where

E_{j}=\{x\in E:\dist(x,X\setminus\Omega)\geq 1/j\}\Subset\Omega,\quad j=1,2,\dots.

By Lemma
6.2
and
[
15
, Theorem 5.3]
,

Q^{\Omega}_{E}=\lim_{j\to\infty}Q^{\Omega}_{E_{j}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}.

Lemma
2.4
and (
6.1
) then imply that

\int_{X}g^{p}_{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}\,d\mu\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}g^{p}_{u_{j}}\,d\mu=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega)\leq\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)<\infty.

Hence
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and so

Q^{\Omega}_{E}\in{\cal K}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E},0}(\Omega)
.
Thus, in view of (
6.1
),
Q^{\Omega}_{E}
is an
lsc-regularized solution of the
{\cal K}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E},0}(\Omega)
-obstacle problem,
which is unique by Theorem 8.27 in
[
7
]
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

If
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)<\infty
, then
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\in D^{p}(X)
,

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)=\int_{X}g_{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}^{p}\,d\mu

and
Q^{\Omega}_{E}
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic in
\Omega\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
.
If moreover
E\neq\Omega
is relatively closed in
\Omega
,
and
\partial^{*}\Omega\neq\varnothing
or
{C_{p}}(E)>0
, then

Q^{\Omega}_{E}={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E}=P_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}\quad\text{in }\Omega\setminus E,

(6.4)

where
I\subset\partial^{*}(\Omega\setminus E)

is the set of irregular boundary points for
\Omega\setminus E
.

Example 6.5
.

If
\partial^{*}\Omega=\varnothing
then
X
must be bounded and
\Omega=X
.
If in addition
E
is closed and
{C_{p}}(E)=0
,
then
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\equiv 0
but (
6.4
) never holds:
If
E=\{x\}
, then
I=\varnothing
and

P_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}=P_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\equiv 1,

see Example
4.5
.
If
E
contains at least two points,
then it follows from Proposition
11.2
below that

I=E
,

P_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\equiv 1.

Finally, if
E=\varnothing
, then the Perron solutions in (
6.4
)
are not even defined.

Let
E_{j}=E\cap{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}
,
j=1,2,\dots
.
By Lemma
3.3
there is
R_{j}>j
such that

\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega_{j})<\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega)+1/j,\quad\text{where }\Omega_{j}=\Omega\cap B_{R_{j}}.

We may assume that
R_{j+1}\geq R_{j}
for
j=1,2,\dots
.
By definition (
3.2
) and monotonicity,

\displaystyle\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega_{j})\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}(\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega)+1/j)=\capp_{p}(E,\Omega).

Hence, by Proposition
6.3
and (
6.1
),

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega_{j})=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}g^{p}_{v_{j}}\,d\mu,\quad\text{where }v_{j}:=Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}.

Moreover, by Lemma
6.2
,

v_{j}\nearrow Q^{\Omega}_{E}
.
Thus by Lemma
2.4
,

\int_{X}g^{p}_{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}\,d\mu\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}g^{p}_{v_{j}}\,d\mu=\capp_{p}(E,\Omega).

To prove the opposite inequality, Lemma
4.6

provides us with convex combinations

u_{j}=\sum_{k=j}^{N_{j}}\lambda_{j,k}v_{k},\quad\text{where $0\leq\lambda_{j,k}\leq 1$ and
$\sum_{k=j}^{N_{j}}\lambda_{j,k}=1$,}

such that
\|g_{u_{j}}-g_{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}\|_{L^{p}(X)}\to 0
,
as
j\to\infty
.
It follows from Proposition
6.3
that

u_{j}
is admissible for
\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega_{N_{j}})
,
from which it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{X}g_{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu\geq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega_{N_{j}})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{j},\Omega)=\capp_{p}(E,\Omega).

The
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonicity of
Q^{\Omega}_{E}

in
\Omega\setminus{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}
follows directly from Theorem 6.1 in
Björn–Björn–Mäkäläinen–Parviainen
[
15
]
.

Next, assume that
E
is relatively closed in
\Omega
,
with
\partial^{*}\Omega\neq\varnothing
or
{C_{p}}(E)>0
.
Consider first the case when
{C_{p}}(E)=0
.
Then
Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}\equiv 0

for all
j
and hence also
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\equiv 0
.
Since
{C_{p}}(E)=0
, every
v\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E}}(\Omega\setminus E)

has a subharmonic extension
\tilde{v}
to
\Omega
, by
Theorem 6.3 in Björn
[
6
]
(or
[
7
, Theorem 12.3]
).
Using that
\partial^{*}\Omega\neq\varnothing
and

\limsup_{\Omega\setminus E\ni y\to x}v(y)\leq 0\quad\text{for all }x\in\partial^{*}\Omega\subset\partial^{*}(\Omega\setminus E),

together with the usc-regularity
of
\tilde{v}
, the empty interior of
E

and the maximum principle for subharmonic functions,
we conclude that
\tilde{v}\leq 0
, and so

{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\equiv 0
in
\Omega\setminus E
.
The last identity in (
6.4
)
follows from Lemma
5.7
.

Finally, consider the case when
{C_{p}}(E)>0
.
Since
Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}\in N^{1,p}(X)
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic in
the bounded open set
\Omega_{j}\setminus E_{j}
,
it is the unique solution of the Dirichlet problem with itself as boundary data.
More precisely,
Corollary 5.7 and Theorem 5.1
in Björn–Björn–Shanmugalingam
[
17
]

(or
[
7
, Corollary 10.16 and Theorem 10.15]
)
imply that

Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}=P_{\Omega_{j}\setminus E_{j}}Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}=P_{\Omega_{j}\setminus E_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{j}}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{j}\setminus E_{j},\quad j=1,2,\dots,

(6.5)

where in the second equality we have also used that
Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}=\mathbbm{1}_{E_{j}}

q.e. on
\partial(\Omega_{j}\setminus E_{j})
.

Let
I
be the set of irregular boundary points for
\Omega\setminus E
.
Since

{C_{p}}(X\setminus(\Omega\setminus E))\geq{C_{p}}(E)>0,

Lemma
5.6
implies that
every

is also
regular for
\Omega_{k}\setminus E_{k}
when
k>d(x,x_{0})
.
For such
x
and

, we have by
Lemma
6.2
, (
6.5
),
Proposition
5.3
1
\Rightarrow
4

and the continuity of
\mathbbm{1}_{E_{k}}|_{\partial(\Omega_{k}\setminus E_{k})}
at
x

that

\liminf_{\Omega\setminus E\ni y\to x}Q^{\Omega}_{E}(y)\geq\liminf_{\Omega_{k}\setminus E_{k}\ni y\to x}Q^{\Omega_{k}}_{E_{k}}(y)=\liminf_{\Omega_{k}\setminus E_{k}\ni y\to x}P_{\Omega_{k}\setminus E_{k}}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{k}}(y)=1.

Since also
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\geq 0
,
we get that
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}}(\Omega\setminus E)
.
Noting also that
E\cap\partial(\Omega\setminus E)
is relatively open in
\partial(\Omega\setminus E)
,
Lemma
5.7
implies that

Q^{\Omega}_{E}\geq P_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E}.

(6.6)

Next, for
j=1,2,\dots
, Lemma
4.3
with
G_{1}=\Omega_{j}\setminus E_{j}

and
G_{2}=\Omega\setminus E
implies that

{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\geq{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{\Omega_{j}\setminus E_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{j}}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{j}\setminus E.

Hence, using also (
6.5
) and (
6.6
),

Q^{\Omega}_{E}\geq P_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E\setminus I}={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{\Omega\setminus E}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\geq{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}_{\Omega_{j}\setminus E_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{j}}=Q^{\Omega_{j}}_{E_{j}}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{j}\setminus E.

Letting

and using Lemma
6.2
shows
(
6.4
).
∎

When
E
is bounded or
X
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic,
the following equivalence holds.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
E\subset\Omega
and assume that
E
is bounded

Example 6.8

For a perhaps more illuminating example let

Let

Proposition 7.3

Let

Example 7.4
.

Let

,

, and

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=[-1,0)\times[-1,1],

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=((-2,2)\times(-2,2))\setminus\{(0,0)\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}

\displaystyle=\Omega\setminus([0,1/j]\times[-1/j,1/j]),

In this case,

.

Proposition 7.8
.

8
Capacity of superlevel sets

Superlevel set identities are part of the definition of Green functions,
through (
1.3
).
We shall now study superlevel sets for capacitary potentials.
They will be used in Section
10

in our study of Green functions.

Recall from (
6.3
) that the
capacitary potential for
E
in
\Omega
is

\displaystyle Q^{\Omega}_{E}(x)=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\inf\{\psi(x):\psi\in\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}\},&\text{if }x\in\Omega,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\quad\text{where }\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}=\{\psi:\psi\text{ is superharmonic in $\Omega$ and $\psi\geq\mathbbm{1}_{E}$ q.e.}\},

and that
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\in\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}
.

In this section we fix
E\subset\Omega
, let
0\leq a\leq 1

and consider the superlevel sets

\Omega_{a}=\{x\in\Omega:Q^{\Omega}_{E}>a\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega^{a}=\{x\in\Omega:Q^{\Omega}_{E}\geq a\}.

(8.1)

The set
\Omega_{a}
is open since
the potential
Q^{\Omega}_{E}
is superharmonic, and thus
lower semicontinuous, in
\Omega
.
If the potential
Q^{\Omega}_{E}
is continuous in
\Omega
, then
\Omega^{a}
is relatively
closed in
\Omega
.
Note that
\Omega_{0}=\Omega
whenever
\Omega
is connected and

{C_{p}}(E)>0
, by the minimum principle for
superharmonic functions,
while
\Omega_{0}=\Omega_{a}=\Omega^{a}=\varnothing
for all
0<a\leq 1
if
{C_{p}}(E)=0
.
Clearly,
\Omega^{0}=\Omega
for all
E\subset\Omega
.
Also, recall that
\capp_{p}(\varnothing,\varnothing)=0
.

Lemma 8.1
.

\displaystyle Q^{\Omega_{a}}_{E\cap\Omega_{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q^{\Omega}_{E}-a}{1-a}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega_{a},

\displaystyle\text{if\/ }0\leq a<1,

\displaystyle Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega_{a}}=Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}

\displaystyle=\min\biggl\{1,\frac{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}{a}\biggr\}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\text{if\/ }0<a<1.

(8.2)

Remark 8.2
.

The proof below of (
8.2
)
uses fine continuity of superharmonic functions,
proved in Björn
[
22
]
and Korte
[
49
]
,
together with a pasting lemma for fine superminimizers
from Björn–Björn–Latvala
[
11
, Lemma 5.7]
.
This is essential also when
E
is compact.

However, if the potential
Q^{\Omega}_{E}
is continuous, as will be
the case in our investigations of singular and Green functions,
these fine potential theoretic tools can be replaced by metric
continuity and
the
standard pasting
Lemma
4.1
.
This somewhat simplifies the proof.

Let
\psi\in\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}
and
0\leq a<1
.
Then
\psi\geq Q^{\Omega}_{E}>a
in the superlevel set
\Omega_{a}
and thus

(\psi-a)/(1-a)\in\Psi^{\Omega_{a}}_{E\cap\Omega_{a}}
.
Taking the infimum over all such
\psi
shows that

Q^{\Omega_{a}}_{E\cap\Omega_{a}}\leq\frac{Q^{\Omega}_{E}-a}{1-a}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{a}.

Conversely, let

v=\begin{cases}a+(1-a)Q^{\Omega_{a}}_{E\cap\Omega_{a}}&\text{in }\Omega_{a},\\
Q^{\Omega}_{E}&\text{in }\Omega\setminus\Omega_{a}.\end{cases}

By the first part of the proof,
v\leq Q^{\Omega}_{E}
.
Since
Q^{\Omega}_{E}\leq a
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{a}
, we see that
v
is lower
semicontinuous.
Hence, by the pasting Lemma
4.1
,

v
is superharmonic in
\Omega
.
By definition,
v\geq 1
q.e. in
E
,
and thus
v\in\Psi^{\Omega}_{E}
,
i.e.
v\geq Q^{\Omega}_{E}
.
This proves the first identity in the statement.

For the second identity, consider
0<a<1
. First
note that the inequality

Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}\leq\min\biggl\{1,\frac{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}{a}\biggr\}\quad\text{in }\Omega

(8.3)

follows immediately from the definition (
6.3
)
of
Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}
since the right-hand side belongs to
\Psi^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}
.

Conversely,
Q^{\Omega}_{E}
is superharmonic
in
\Omega
and thus finely continuous, by Björn
[
22
, Theorem 4.4]

or Korte
[
49
, Theorem 4.3]
(or
[
7
, Theorem 11.38]
).
Thus, the set
\Omega\setminus\Omega^{a}
is finely open.
Let

v=\begin{cases}Q^{\Omega}_{E}&\text{in }\Omega^{a},\\
aQ^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}=\min\{aQ^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}},Q^{\Omega}_{E}\}&\text{in }\Omega\setminus\Omega^{a},\end{cases}

and note that
v=aQ^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}+(Q^{\Omega}_{E}-a)_{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}
q.e. in
\Omega
.
Hence
v\in N^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.
It then follows from the pasting lemma
for fine superminimizers in
Björn–Björn–Latvala
[
11
, Lemma 5.7]
that
v

is a fine superminimizer in
\Omega
.
Since
\Omega
is open,
v
is a standard superminimizer in
\Omega

by Corollary 5.6 in
[
11
]
.
Its lsc-regularization
v^{*}
, defined by (

)
and q.e. equal to

, is then superharmonic in
\Omega

and admissible for the definition of
Q^{\Omega}_{E}
.
In particular, this gives

aQ^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}=v^{*}\geq Q^{\Omega}_{E}\quad\text{q.e.\ in }\Omega\setminus\Omega^{a}.

Hence

Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}\geq\min\biggl\{1,\frac{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}{a}\biggr\}\quad\text{q.e.\ in }\Omega,

and since both functions are superharmonic in
\Omega
(and thus
lsc-regularized), it follows that the inequality holds everywhere.
Together with (
8.3
) this shows that

Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}=\min\biggl\{1,\frac{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}{a}\biggr\}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Finally, we see that

Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}\geq Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega_{a}}\geq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}}Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a+\varepsilon}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}}\min\biggl\{1,\frac{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}{a+\varepsilon}\biggr\}=Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega^{a}}.\qed

Theorem 8.3
.

The following hold for the superlevel sets
in (
8.1
)
:

1.

\capp_{p}(E\cap\Omega_{a},\Omega_{a})=(1-a)^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)
if
0\leq a<1
.

2.

\capp_{p}(\Omega_{a},\Omega)=a^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)
if
0<a<1
.

3.

\capp_{p}(\Omega^{a},\Omega)=a^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)
if
0<a\leq 1
.

For bounded
\Omega
, the capacitary identities in Theorem
8.3

and Corollary

were obtained in
Aikawa–Björn–Björn–Shanmugalingam
[
1
, Proposition 4.1]

and Björn–Björn–Lehrbäck
[
12
, Theorem 3.3 and Lemma 3.4]
.
See Holopainen
[
37
, Lemma 3.8]
and
Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
34
, p. 118]
for earlier results
for bounded subsets of manifolds and weighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
In
[
12
]
,
these identities were subsequently used to study Green functions on bounded domains
in metric spaces.

First, note that
3
follows from
2
: Indeed,
2
and

\capp_{p}(\Omega^{a},\Omega)\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}}\capp_{p}(\Omega_{a-\varepsilon},\Omega),\quad 0<a\leq 1,

yield the
\leq
-inequality in
3
.
The
\geq
-inequality follows from
2
and

\Omega_{a}\subset\Omega^{a}
when
a<1
, while
Proposition
3.2
and
{C_{p}}(E\setminus\Omega^{1})=0
give

\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)\leq\capp_{p}(\Omega^{1},\Omega)
for
a=1
.

Next, if
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)=\infty
then the left-hand sides in

1
–
2
are clearly bounded from below by

\capp_{p}(E\cap\Omega_{a},\Omega)=\infty,

since
{C_{p}}(E\setminus\Omega_{a})=0
when
0\leq a<1
.
So assume that
\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)<\infty
and
0\leq a<1
.

We begin by proving the
\leq
-inequalities.
For
j,k=2,3,\dots
, let

E_{k}=E\cap B_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad G_{j}=\Omega\cap B_{j+1}.

Fix
k
for the moment. Since
E_{k}
is bounded and

\capp_{p}(E_{k},\Omega)\leq\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)<\infty
,
there is an admissible function for
\capp_{p}(E_{k},\Omega)
.
Multiplying it by a suitable cutoff function shows that

\capp_{p}(E_{k},G_{j})<\infty\quad\text{for }j\geq k.

(8.4)

Let

G^{k}_{j,a}=\{x\in G_{j}:Q^{G_{j}}_{E_{k}}>a\}\quad\text{for }j=k,k+1,\dots.

By Lemma
6.2
,
Q^{G_{j}}_{E_{k}}\nearrow Q^{\Omega}_{E_{k}}

as
j\to\infty
,
while
Q^{\Omega}_{E_{k}}\nearrow Q^{\Omega}_{E}
and

Q^{G_{k}}_{E_{k}}\nearrow Q^{\Omega}_{E}
as
k\to\infty
. Hence

\Omega^{k}_{a}:=\{x\in\Omega:Q^{\Omega}_{E_{k}}>a\}=\bigcup_{j=k}^{\infty}G^{k}_{j,a}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{a}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\Omega^{k}_{a}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}G^{k}_{k,a}.

(8.5)

As
{C_{p}}(E_{k}\setminus G^{k}_{j,a})=0

for all
j\geq k
, it follows from
Proposition
7.3
and
Björn–Björn–Lehrbäck
[

, Lemma 3.4]
that

\displaystyle\capp_{p}(E_{k}\cap G^{k}_{k,a},\Omega^{k}_{a})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{k}\cap G^{k}_{k,a},G^{k}_{j,a})=\lim_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{k}\cap G^{k}_{j,a},G^{k}_{j,a})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}(1-a)^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E_{k},G_{j})=(1-a)^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E_{k},\Omega),

(8.6)

where all the involved capacities are finite because
of (
8.4
) and the monotonicity of capacity.
Now, letting
k\to\infty
and using (
8.5
),
(
8.6
) and Proposition
7.7

yields

\displaystyle\capp_{p}(E\cap\Omega_{a},\Omega_{a})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\capp_{p}(E_{k}\cap G^{k}_{k,a},\Omega^{k}_{a})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}(1-a)^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E_{k},\Omega)=(1-a)^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E,\Omega).

This shows the
\leq
-inequality in
1
.

In
2
, we proceed similarly, assuming that
0<a<1
.
For each fixed
k
and
j\geq k
,
Lemma 3.4 in
[
12
]

implies that

\capp_{p}(G^{k}_{j,a},\Omega)\leq\capp_{p}(G^{k}_{j,a},G_{j})=a^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E_{k},G_{j}).

Together with Theorem
1.6
1
,
Lemma
3.3
(or Proposition
7.3
)
and (
8.5
), this yields

\capp_{p}(\Omega^{k}_{a},\Omega)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\capp_{p}(G^{k}_{j,a},\Omega)\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}a^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E_{k},G_{j})=a^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E_{k},\Omega)

and

\capp_{p}(\Omega_{a},\Omega)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\capp_{p}(\Omega^{k}_{a},\Omega)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}a^{1-p}\capp_{p}(E_{k},\Omega)=\capp_{p}(E,\Omega),

which
proves the
\leq
-inequality in
2
.

–

, we use

6.4

and Lemma

, together with the already proved

-inequalities, as follows:
(When

in

to obtain the third equality.)

\displaystyle\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}g_{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{a}}g_{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}^{p}\,d\mu+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{a}}g_{Q^{\Omega}_{E}}^{p}\,d\mu

(8.7)

\displaystyle=(1-a)^{p}\int_{\Omega_{a}}g_{Q^{\Omega_{a}}_{E\cap\Omega_{a}}}^{p}\,d\mu+a^{p}\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{a}}g_{Q^{\Omega}_{\Omega_{a}}}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq(1-a)\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)+a\capp_{p}(E,\Omega)

.

.

.

Remark 11.8
.

For bounded
\Omega
, the earliest perturbation result
for Perron solutions in the nonlinear case that we are aware of is
by Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
44
]
on unweighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
1<p\leq n
.
It is somewhat hidden in that paper so we seize the opportunity to point
out how it follows from their results.
These arguments apply also to so-called
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic functions.

Statement.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a nonempty bounded open set and
1<p\leq n
.
Let
f\in C(\partial\Omega)
and let
h:\partial\Omega\to\mathbf{R}
be a bounded function
with

{C_{p}}(\supp h)=0
.
Then
P(f+h)=Pf
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
I_{\Omega}
be the set of irregular boundary points.
Then
{C_{p}}(I_{\Omega})=0
, by the Kellogg property,
which had been obtained in this situation by
Kilpeläinen
[
43
, Corollary 5.6]
.
Hence
{C_{p}}(E)=0
for
E:=I_{\Omega}\cup\supp h
.

Lemma 2.17 in
[
43
]

shows that

\lim_{\Omega\ni y\to x}{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}(f+h)(y)=f(x)\quad\text{for every }x\in\partial\Omega\setminus E.

Hence
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}(f+h)
is a “generalized solution”
of the Dirichlet problem with boundary data

in the sense of

[
44
, p. 40]
for

\mathcal{V}=\{u:u\text{ is superharmonic and bounded from below in }\Omega\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}=\{E\}.

Moreover, it was shown in
[
43
, Theorem 1.5]
that regular boundary
points are “
\mathcal{V}
-exposed” in the sense of
[
44
, Section 4]
.
It then follows from the uniqueness theorem

[
44
, Proposition 5.2]
that
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}(f+h)=Pf
.
Similarly
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle P}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{P}}}(f+h)=Pf
.
∎

Using uniform convergence, as in the proof of
Proposition

, one can
in the above statement allow

to be any bounded function such that

.

Proposition 11.9
.

Assume that
X
is unbounded,
{C_{p}}(X\setminus\Omega)=0

\partial^{*}\Omega

Then all finite boundary points of

are irregular

First, we show that

.

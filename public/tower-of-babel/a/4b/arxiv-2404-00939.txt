Abstract
. This is a survey of Rational Homotopy Theory, intended for a Mathematical Physics readership.



Introduction

Rational Homotopy Theory (RHT) is a story of success in Algebraic
Topology. It sacrifices the all-time favorite homotopy equivalence
relation on topological spaces for the weaker rational homotopy
equivalence, ignoring all the torsion in homotopy and homology groups,
and provides a complete algebraic invariant of the rational
equivalence class of a good enough space
X
, the isomorphism class of
a differential graded (dg-) commutative algebra (DGCA)
M(X)
, called
the Sullivan minimal model of
X
: two such spaces are rationally
equivalent if and only if their Sullivan minimal models are
isomorphic. Moreover, with suitable model-category structures, the
homotopy category of good enough DGCAs becomes equivalent to the
rational homotopy category of good enough spaces. Another feature of
the DGCA
M(X)
is that it is rather easily computable, making the
invariant
M(X)
an effective tool of topology. A parallel,
historically earlier algebraic model of a space
X
is given by a
differential graded Lie algebra (DGLA)
Q(X)
, called the Quillen
minimal model, and provides practically the same features. Koszul
duality between the commutative and Lie operads,
[
GK94
]
, brings the
two models together.

Perhaps, one of the most spectacular mathematical applications of RHT
is the result of D. Sullivan and M. Vigué-Poirrier

[
VPS76
,
FOT08
]
which states that a
sufficiently general simply connected Riemannian manifold has
infinitely many closed geodesics. RHT has made a strong appearance in
Mathematical Physics via H. Sati’s
Hypothesis H
, which states,
roughly speaking, that the Sullivan minimal model of the 4-sphere

S^{4}
captures the dynamics of fields in M-theory in the low-energy
(UV) limit, also known as 11d supergravity

[
Sat18
]
. Hypothesis H progresses to the dimensional
reductions of M-theory with its dynamics described by the iterated
cyclic loop space
\mathcal{L}_{c}^{k}S^{4}
of the 4-sphere
[
FSS17
,
FSS20
,
FSS21
,
GS21
,
SV23b
,
SV23a
]
.

In this survey, we review the main ideas and results of RHT. RHT
cannot complain about the lack of surveys, see

[
Hes07
,
Ber12
,
Men15
,
Moe17
,
Wan19
,
Hol21
]
. What
distinguishes this survey from the variety of previous ones is that
it, first of all, keeps an eye on Mathematical Physics. A novel
feature of this survey is that more recent developments which engage

L_{\infty}
-algebras in lieu of DGLAs that are free as graded Lie algebras
are put forward as Quillen minimal models. Another novelty, which may
get a rise out of seasoned rational homotopy theorists, is the use of
the graded symmetric algebra notation
S(V)
in lieu of
\wedge V
, as
well as swapping the standard abbreviation CDGA for DGCA. The reason
for the first deviation from the standard convention is that in the
category of graded vector spaces, the graded exterior algebra is not
free as a graded commutative algebra and not isomorphic to the graded
symmetric algebra. The reason for the second deviation from the
standards is that the placement of modifiers may be commutative but
not necessarily associative: D(GC)A
\neq
(DG)CA
=
C(DG)A. Whatever
abbreviation the practitioners use, they always mean D(GC)A in this
context.

Some novelties in exposition do not mean novelties in the content:
this article presents only known results. The only possible exception,
which may appear for the first time in written form, is
Example

4.3
on
the Chern-Simons interpretation of the “phantom” 3-form
g_{3}
on the
sphere
S^{2}
. And even that is not due to the author of the survey.

aside a number of remarkable developments of RHT, such as, for

. Likewise, we do not pretend to cover all

Conventions
: When we use the term “space,” we will mean a
topological space. Moreover, we will assume all spaces to be

path-connected
unless stated otherwise. All manifolds will be
smooth (
C^{\infty}
) by default. In graded algebra,
\lvert a\rvert
will
denote the degree of an element
a
.

1.
Rational homotopy category of spaces

A continuous map
f:X\to Y
between topological spaces is called a

rational
(
homotopy
)
equivalence
if it induces an
isomorphism on rational homology:
f_{*}:H_{\bullet}(X;\mathbb{Q})\to H_{\bullet}(Y;\mathbb{Q})
. (Compare this to a stronger notion of weak
equivalence: to compare apples to apples and oranges to oranges, let
us say a continuous map
f:X\to Y
of simply connected spaces is
called a
weak
(
homotopy
)
equivalence
if it induces an
isomorphism
f_{*}:H_{\bullet}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{\bullet}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
on
integral homology.) We say that two spaces
X
and
Y
are

rationally equivalent
if they may be connected by a sequence of
rational equivalences
X\leftarrow X_{1}\to\dots\leftarrow X_{n}\rightarrow Y
. The equivalence class of a space
X
up to rational
equivalence is called the
rational homotopy type of

X
. If we
add formal inverses of rational equivalences to the category of
spaces, i.e., we localize the category with respect to rational
equivalences, we obtain the
rational homotopy category of
spaces
.

Example 1.1
.

The unique map
\mathbb{R}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\to\{*\}
from the real projective plane to
a singleton is a rational equivalence, because the homology groups

H_{n}(\mathbb{R}{\mathbb{P}}^{2};\mathbb{Z})
,
n>0
, are all torsion.

Example 1.2
.

Let
S^{2n-1}
,
n\geq 1
, be an odd-dimensional sphere and
K(\mathbb{Z},2n-1)
be the Eilenberg-MacLane space. Let
S^{2n-1}\to K(\mathbb{Z},2n-1)

be a map realizing a generator of
\pi_{2n-1}(K(\mathbb{Z},2n-1))=\mathbb{Z}
. This map is a rational equivalence, because it induces an
isomorphism on rational homotopy groups:

\pi_{i}(S^{2n-1})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\pi_{i}(K(\mathbb{Z},2n-1))\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q},&\text{if $i=2n-1$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

cf. the remark at the end of the second paragraph of

Section

6
. Thus,
S^{2n-1}
is a
rational Eilenberg-MacLane
space
. The same argument does not work for even-dimensional spheres

S^{2n}
, because
S^{2n}
has an extra rational homotopy group in
degree
4n-1
:

\pi_{i}(S^{2n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q},&\text{if $i=2n$ or $4n-1$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

2.
Differential graded commutative algebras

RHT assigns an algebraic model, namely a DGCA, see below, to a
space. Such an algebra is supposed to be large enough to carry all the
information about the rational homotopy type of the space. The theory
works further to replace this large DGCA with a much smaller model,
called a minimal Sullivan model. The prototypical example of an
excessively large model is the de Rham algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
of
a manifold
X
. In this case, the ground field has to be
extended to
\mathbb{R}
, and we are dealing with the
real
, rather
than rational,
homotopy type
of
X
.

A
differential graded commutative algebra
(
DGCA
)
over a
field
k
is a graded associative
k
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{n}
with a multiplication

\displaystyle A\otimes A

\displaystyle\to A,

\displaystyle a\otimes b

\displaystyle\mapsto ab,

which respects the grading:
A^{m}\otimes A^{n}\to A^{m+n}
, is

graded commutative
:

ba=(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}ab,

has a unit
1\in A
, and has a differential
d:A\to A
, a

k
-linear operator of degree 1 such that
d^{2}=0
and
it is a (graded)
derivation
of
A
:

d(ab)=(da)b+(-1)^{\lvert a\rvert}a(db).

The derivation property, also known as the
Leibniz rule
,
implies that the differential kills the constants:
d(1)=0
. A DGCA

homomorphism
is a linear map
A\to B
which respects the DGCA
structure: the multiplication, unit, grading, and differential. In
this survey, a DGCA will by default be nonnegatively graded (
A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A^{n}
) over a field
k
of characteristic 0.

A DGCA
A
is called
(
homologically
)
connected
if
A^{0}=k

(respectively,
H^{0}(A)=k
). It is called
(
homologically
)

simply connected
if
A^{0}=k
and
A^{1}=0
, (respectively,
H^{0}(A)=k
and
H^{1}(A)=0
).

A DGCA
(S(V),d)
is called
semifree
, if it is based on the
free graded commutative algebra
S(V)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}S^{n}(V)
on
a positively graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{n>0}V^{n}
.

3.
Minimal Sullivan models of DGCAs

RHT provides a minimal Sullivan model for each good enough DGCA. A

Sullivan algebra
is a semifree DGCA
(S(V),d)
such that the
differential
d
satisfies the following nilpotency condition, also
known as the
Sullivan condition
: there exists a filtration of
the generating space
V
by graded subspaces:

0=V(0)\subset V(1)\subset\dots

such that
V=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}V(n)
and
d(V(n))\subset S(V(n-1))
for all
n\geq 1
. A Sullivan algebra is automatically
connected.

A semifree DGCA
(S(V),d)
is called
minimal
if
d(V)\subset S^{\geq 2}(V)
.

Every simply connected minimal DGCA
S(V)
is necessarily Sullivan:
the filtration associated with the grading does the job. Indeed, we
have
V^{1}=0
and for

V(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{2\leq i\leq n}V^{i},\qquad n\geq 0,

the Sullivan nilpotency condition follows from
d(V^{n})\subset S^{\geq 2}(V)^{n+1}\subset S(V(n-1))
for all
n\geq 1
.

A DGCA
quasi-isomorphism
is a DGCA homomorphism
A\to B
which
induces and isomorphism on the cohomology of the differentials

H^{\bullet}(A,d_{A})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{\bullet}(B,d_{B})
. In this
case we write
A\xrightarrow{\qis}B
and say that
A
(or
A\xrightarrow{\qis}B
, to be more precise) is a
model
of

B
. Sullivan and minimal Sullivan models play an important role in
RHT for a variety of reasons, mostly related to the fact that they are
cofibrant DGCAs in a suitable model-category structure, see

Section

6
.

Theorem 1

(
[
FHT01
, Theorems 14.11-14.12]
)
.

(1)

Every quasi-isomorphism between minimal Sullivan algebras is an
isomorphism.

(2)

Every homologically connected DGCA has a minimal Sullivan model,
unique up to isomorphism.

We conclude this section with a structure theorem for Sullivan
algebras.

Theorem 2

(
[
FHT01
, Theorem 14.9]
)
.

Every connected Sullivan algebra is isomorphic to the tensor product
of a minimal Sullivan algebra and a semifree contractible algebra

S(U\oplus dU)
, where
U
is a positively graded vector space.

4.
Sullivan minimal models of spaces

Every manifold
X
has a DGCA associated to it, the de Rham
algebra
(\Omega^{\bullet}(X),d_{\dR})
. This is quite a familiar a
DGCA over the reals
\mathbb{R}
whose cohomology is the de Rham cohomology

H^{\bullet}(X;\mathbb{R})
. Sullivan
[
Sul77
]
has generalized this
construction to work for an arbitrary topological space
X
and
produce a DGCA
A_{\PL}(X)
over the rationals
\mathbb{Q}
whose cohomology
is isomorphic to the rational cohomology of
X
:

H^{\bullet}(A_{\PL}(X))\cong H^{\bullet}(X;\mathbb{Q}).

The DGCA
A_{\PL}(X)
, called the polynomial de Rham algebra or the
algebra of polynomial differential forms on
X
, is defined in

Section

4.2
. By definition, a Sullivan minimal model
M(X)
of a
path-connected space
X
is a minimal Sullivan model of
A_{\PL}(X)
. Since
M(X)
is defined uniquely up to isomorphism, it is
usually called
the
Sullivan minimal model of
X
. One has

(1)

H^{\bullet}(M(X))\cong H^{\bullet}(X;\mathbb{Q})

and

(2)

M(X)^{>0}/(M(X)^{>0})^{2}\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\pi_{\bullet}(X),\mathbb{Q}).

This implies that
M(X)\cong S(\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\pi_{\bullet}(X),\mathbb{Q}))
.

4.1.
Real algebraic models of manifolds

If
X
is a connected manifold, then
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
is
quasi-isomorphic to
A_{\PL}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
and defines the real
homotopy type of
X
,
[
FOT08
, Section 2.4.1]
. The

Sullivan minimal model
M_{\mathbb{R}}(X)
of
X
over
\mathbb{R}
is then the
minimal Sullivan model of
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
. Let us compute the real
homotopy type, i.e., the Sullivan minimal model over the reals, of the
sphere
S^{n}
for every
n\geq 1
. The Sullivan minimal model of
S^{n}

over
\mathbb{Q}
will be exactly the same, except that the ground field will
be changed to
\mathbb{Q}
.

Example 4.1

(The real homotopy type of an odd-dimensional
sphere)
.

We claim that the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{R}[g_{n}]\cong\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}g_{n}
in a variable
g_{n}
of degree
n
with differential
determined by
dg_{n}=0
is a Sullivan minimal model of

S^{n}
. Indeed, choose a differential
n
-form
\omega_{n}
on
S^{n}

generating its cohomology
H^{n}(S^{n};\mathbb{R})
and define an algebra
homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}[g_{n}]

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{\bullet}(S^{n}),

\displaystyle g_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto\omega_{n},

which obviously respects the grading and differentials. Moreover, it
is clear it is a quasi-isomorphism.
\mathbb{R}[g_{n}]
is trivially
Sullivan and minimal. We conclude that
M_{\mathbb{R}}(S^{n})=\mathbb{R}[g_{n}]
with

\lvert g_{n}\rvert=n
and
d=0
for
n
odd.

Example 4.2

(The real and rational homotopy type of an even-dimensional
sphere)
.

If
n\geq 2
is even, then arguing similarly, we observe that the
cohomology algebra
(\mathbb{R}[g_{n}]/(g_{n}^{2}),0)
, admits a
quasi-isomorphism to the de Rham algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(S^{n})
. The
problem is that
(\mathbb{R}[g_{n}]/(g_{n}^{2}),0)
is not Sullivan, as it is
not even semifree. If we drop the relation
g_{n}^{2}=0
to gain
freeness, we will acquire cohomology in each degree which is a
multiple of
n
. To kill the cohomology class
g_{n}^{2}
, we introduce
a new generator
g_{2n-1}
of degree
2n-1
with
dg_{2n-1}=g_{n}^{2}

and get the semifree resolution
\mathbb{R}[g_{n},g_{2n-1}]\xrightarrow{\qis}\mathbb{R}[g_{n}]/(g_{n}^{2})
with
dg_{n}=0
and
dg_{2n-1}=g^{2}_{n}
of
(\mathbb{R}[g_{n}]/(g_{n}^{2}),0)
. This semifree
resolution is simply connected and minimal and thereby
Sullivan. Thus,
M_{\mathbb{R}}(S^{n})=\mathbb{R}[g_{n},g_{2n-1}]
with
\lvert g_{n}\rvert=n
,
\lvert g_{2n-1}\rvert=2n-1
, and
dg_{n}=0
,
dg_{2n-1}=g_{n}^{2}

for
n
odd. Note that we could have set
dg_{2n-1}=cg^{2}_{n}
for
any nonzero
c\in\mathbb{R}
. The resulting DGCA would be isomorphic to
the one with
c=1
. The choice of
c=-1/2
looks more like the
Maurer-Cartan equation,
dg_{2n-1}+\tfrac{1}{2}g^{2}_{n}=0
, and is
particularly common in physics, see
[
Sat18
]
.

Note that in both examples, the cohomology algebra is quasi-isomorphic
to the de Rham algebra. In such cases, we say that the manifold and
its de Rham algebra are
formal
. For a general DGCA
A
or space

X
, we say it is
formal
, if
A
or, respectively,

A_{\PL}(X)
, is quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology DGCA (with the
zero differential). Another famous class of formal spaces are compact
Kähler manifolds, as per the Deligne-Griffiths-Morgan-Sullivan
theorem
[
DGMS75
]
.

Example 4.3

(Realization of
g_{3}
for
S^{2}
as a Chern-Simons form)
.

For an even number
n\geq 2
, one may wonder where the element

g_{2n-1}
for the sphere
S^{n}
lives. It is certainly not there in
the cohomology or even the de Rham algebra of
S^{n}
, as both vanish in
degree
2n-1
. M. Kapranov has suggested that
g_{3}
for
S^{2}
may be
realized as a Chern-Simons form on
X=\mathbb{C}{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}\setminus\mathbb{C}{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty-2}
, which is another model of
S^{2}
:
X=\{[z_{0}:z_{1}:z_{2}:\dots]\in\mathbb{C}{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}\mid z_{0}\neq 0\text{ or }z_{1}\neq 0\}
deformation retracts to the subspace
0=z_{2}=z_{3}=\dots
,
which is homeomorphic to
\{[z_{0}:z_{1}]\mid z_{0}\neq 0\text{ or }z_{1}\neq 0\}=\mathbb{C}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\cong S^{2}
. Let
\omega_{2}
be the

curvature
form of the Chern connection associated to the
standard Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{O}(1)
, the dual tautological line
bundle on
\mathbb{C}{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
. The cohomology class of
\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\omega_{2}
is the first Chern class
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))
of the line bundle

\mathcal{O}(1)
. The cohomology class of
\omega_{2}
freely generates the real
cohomology
\mathbb{R}[\omega_{2}]
of
\mathbb{C}{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
and no power of

\omega_{2}
vanishes on the dense open
X\subset\mathbb{C}{\mathbb{P}}^{\infty}
. However, the cohomology class of
\omega_{2}\wedge\omega_{2}
in
H^{4}(X;\mathbb{R})\cong H^{4}(S^{2};\mathbb{R})=0
vanishes and hence
there exists a 3-form
\omega_{3}
on
X
such that
d\omega_{3}=\omega_{2}\wedge\omega_{2}
. In this sense,
\omega_{3}
is the
rudimentary, abelian version of a Chern-Simons form, being associated
to a connection on a complex line bundle, rather than an

\SU(n)
-bundle for
n\geq 2
. Note that the whole DGCA
M(S^{2})=\mathbb{R}[g_{2},g_{3}]
may be realized as the sub-DGCA
\mathbb{R}[\omega_{2},\omega_{3}]
of the de Rham algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
, which is
another algebraic model of
S^{2}\sim X
:

M(S^{2})=\mathbb{R}[g_{2},g_{3}]\cong\mathbb{R}[\omega_{2},\omega_{3}]\xhookrightarrow{\qis}\Omega^{\bullet}(X).

4.2.
Rational algebraic models of spaces

Here we define the polynomial de Rham algebra
A_{\PL}(X)
for a
topological space
X
. We will do it by considering the simplicial set

\Sing(X)
of singular simplices of
X
and gluing the algebra out of
polynomial differential forms on each singular simplex.

The
polynomial de Rham algebra
or the
algebra of
polynomial differential forms on the standard
n
-simplex

\Delta^{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(t_{0},\dots,t_{n})\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}~\Bigg|~\sum_{i=0}^{n}t_{i}=1\text{ and }t_{i}\geq 0\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,n\right\}

is the DGCA

\Omega^{\bullet}([n])\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{Q}[t_{0},...,t_{n},dt_{0},...,dt_{n}]/(\sum t_{i}-1,\sum dt_{i}).

Varying
n
, we get a simplicial DGCA,

\Omega^{\bullet}
. For any simplicial set
K
, define the

polynomial de Rham algebra of
K
as

A_{\PL}(K)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom_{\sSet}(K,\Omega^{\bullet}).

Here
\Hom_{\sSet}
stands for the set of morphisms of simplicial
sets, i.e., maps of sets
K([n])\to\Omega^{\bullet}([n])
for each
n\geq 0
respecting the
face and degeneracy maps, or equivalently, defining natural
transformations between the corresponding functors
K\colon\thinspace\Delta\to\mathbf{Set}
and
\Omega^{\bullet}\colon\thinspace\Delta\to\mathbf{Set}
on the
simplex category
\Delta
, where
\mathbf{Set}
is the category of sets.
The structure of a DGCA on
A_{\PL}(K)
comes from that on each

\Omega^{\bullet}([n])
. Finally, using the simplicial set

\Sing(X)
of singular simplices of a space, we define the

polynomial de Rham algebra of a space
X
:

A_{\PL}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom_{\sSet}(\Sing(X),\Omega^{\bullet}).

For a path-connected space
X
, this DGCA is connected, called a

(
rational
)
model of
X
, and its minimal Sullivan model is
called the
Sullivan minimal model
M(X)
of
X
. The minimal
Sullivan model of a space is unique up to isomorphism. For good enough
spaces, (the isomorphism class of) the Sullivan minimal model
determines the space up to rational equivalence, that is to say,

X\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}Y\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad M(X)\cong M(Y).

Good enough spaces include spaces of finite
\mathbb{Q}
-type which are
simply connected, or, more generally, nilpotent, see definitions in

Section

7
. For example, if the Sullivan minimal model of a simply
connected space is
\mathbb{Q}[g_{4},g_{7}]
with
\lvert g_{4}\rvert=4
,
\lvert g_{7}\rvert=7
, and
dg_{4}=0
,
dg_{7}=-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{4}^{2}
, we may rest assured
that this space is rationally equivalent to the sphere
S^{4}
, see

Example

4.2
.

4.3.
Realizations

In the previous section, we associated a DGCA to every topological
space. We did that in two steps, going first from spaces to simplicial
sets and then to DGCAs.
Here we will discuss a construction that produces a topological space
for a given DGCA, also in two steps. Let
A
be a nonnegatively graded
DGCA. Its
simplicial realization
is
the simplicial set

K_{\SR}(A)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom_{\DGCA}(A,\Omega^{\bullet}),

where
\DGCA
is the category of nonnegatively graded DGCAs.
This construction actually defines a (contravariant) functor
K_{\SR}\colon\thinspace\DGCA\to\sSet
, which turns out to be left adjoint for the
(contravariant) polynomial de Rham functor
A_{\PL}\colon\thinspace\sSet\to\DGCA
of the previous section. More about this is in

Section

6
, in which we discuss model categories.

To pass to topological spaces, we can further apply the functor of

geometric realization
:

5.
Rationalization

A rationalization may be viewed as a rational topological model of a
space, categorically dual to the Sullivan models considered above. A
path-connected space is called
rational
if the
\mathbb{Z}
-module

H_{n}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space for each
n\geq 1
. (This means
unique divisibility of each element by natural numbers.) A

rationalization
of a path-connected space
X
is a rational
space
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
along with a rational equivalence
X\to X_{\mathbb{Q}}
. A
rationalization of a simply connected space is unique up to weak
homotopy equivalence and thereby called
the
rationalization.
In a suitable model-category structure in which the weak equivalences
are the rational homotopy equivalences, the rationalization
X_{\mathbb{Q}}
is
a fibrant replacement of
X
, see more on model categories below. We
will present a construction of rationalization in
Remark

7.1
.

6.
Model categories

W. G. Dwyer
[
Dwy08
]
taught us that ideally a homotopy
theory is determined just by defining the class of weak equivalences
without invoking other ingredients of a model category. Thus, if the
reader prefers to keep the technicalities at bay, they may wish to pay
attention only to the classes of weak equivalences in our model
categories.

A
model-category
structure on a category is characterized by
the choice of three classes of morphisms satisfying certain
properties, see
[
DS95
,
Hov99
]
: weak equivalences,
fibrations, and cofibrations. These definitions are modeled upon the
classes of homotopy equivalences, Hurewicz fibrations, and
cofibrations in the category of topological spaces, but this is not
the most commonly used model-category structure on topological spaces
and not the one we will use here. The
standard model category

\Top
of topological spaces
selects the following three classes of
morphisms:

•

weak equivalences
: weak homotopy equivalences;

•

fibrations
: Serre fibrations;

•

cofibrations
: continuous maps which have the left lifting
property (LLP) with respect to Serre fibrations which are weak
equivalences.

Here we use a more general notion of a weak homotopy equivalence than
the one defined earlier in
Section

1
for simply connected spaces:

For general spaces, we say that
f:X\to Y
is a
weak

(
homotopy
)
equivalence
if the induced map
f_{*}:\pi_{n}(X,x)\to\pi_{n}(Y,f(x))
is an isomorphism for each
n\geq 0
.

For simply connected spaces, this notion is equivalent to the one
which substitutes homology for homotopy, because of the relative
Hurewicz theorem, see the argument in
[
Hat02
, Corollary 4.33]
.

On our “intermediary” category,
\sSet
of simplicial sets, we
impose a model category structure as follows:

•

weak equivalences
: simplicial maps whose geometric
realization is a weak homotopy equivalence;

•

cofibrations
: simplicial inclusions;

•

fibrations
: simplicial maps which have the right lifting (RLP)
property with respect to cofibrations which are weak equivalences.

Fibrations in
\sSet
are known as
Kan fibrations
and may be
characterized by the RLP just with respect to inclusions of horns into
the simplicial representations of simplices, somewhat similar to Serre
fibrations.

Finally, the
model category
\DGCA
of DGCAs is the category of
(by default, nonnegatively graded and over
\mathbb{Q}
) DGCAs with

•

quasi-isomorphisms as
weak equivalences
;

•

surjective homomorphisms as
fibrations
;

•

homomorphisms which have the LLP with respect to fibrations
which are weak equivalences as
cofibrations
.

The axioms of a model category guarantee that every model category
possesses an initial object and a terminal object. An object
Q
is
called
cofibrant
if the unique morphism from the initial object
to
Q
is a cofibration. An object
R
is called
fibrant
if the
unique morphism from
R
to the final object is a fibration. It is a
fact that space is fibrant, every simplicial set is cofibrant, and
every DGCA is fibrant. A CW complex is an example of a cofibrant
topological space. Fibrant simplicial sets are exactly the
Kan
complexes
; they are characterized by the horn-filling
condition. Sullivan algebras are known to be cofibrant.

The
homotopy category
of a model category
{\mathcal{M}}
is the
localization
\Ho({\mathcal{M}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{M}}[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
of
{\mathcal{M}}
with respect to
the class
{\mathcal{W}}
of weak equivalences. The localization formally adds
inverses of weak equivalences to the morphisms of
{\mathcal{M}}
. One can
develop the notion of
homotopy between morphisms
in a model
category and show that the homotopy category
\Ho({\mathcal{M}})
is equivalent
to the category whose objects are fibrant cofibrant objects of
{\mathcal{M}}

and whose morphisms are homotopy classes of morphisms of
{\mathcal{M}}
,
cf.
[
Hol21
, Theorem 3.29]
.

Theorem 3

(
[
Hov99
, Proposition 3.1.5]
,

)
.

The left derived functor is defined by taking a cofibrant replacement

. We still need to replace maps between

The

the functors between the original model categories establish an
equivalence of their homotopy categories. This means that the derived
functors are not only adjoint, but also full and faithful. All in all,
this implies that homotopy theory in
\Top
is the “same” as in

\sSet
. Choose whichever is more convenient!

7.
The fundamental theorem of RHT

The fundamental theorem of RHT in a dream world would have said that
the functor

of spatial realization and the polynomial de
Rham algebra functor
A_{\PL}
establish a Quillen equivalence between
the categories
\Top
and
\DGCA

work, because the spatial realization functor

is neither a left, nor a right adjoint functor. There
are also other technicalities which creep in the way, such as the
finiteness and nilpotency conditions. This is another realization, in
addition to the geometric, simplicial, and spatial ones: this time, it
is a sad realization. For this reason, we completely abandon the
category of spaces for the rest of this section and work with the
category of simplicial sets instead.

simplicial set is of

if its rational homology
is finite-dimensional in each degree.

algebras are known to be cofibrant

if it is

8.
Quillen minimal models of spaces

The advantage of Sullivan models over historically earlier Quillen’s
models derives from the fact that Sullivan models are more
user-friendly: they are based on more familiar constructions, such as
algebras and differential forms, both known since the late 19th
century. Quillen minimal models use coalgebras and free Lie algebras,
or in modern interpretation,
L_{\infty}
-algebras, all of which are
less ingrained in the brain of a practitioner and are viewed as less
user-friendly by the general public.

8.1.
Quillen’s DGLA model
\lambda X
of a space
X

In the foundational paper
[
Qui69
]
, Quillen defines a
sequence of left and right Quillen equivalences, carefully avoiding
calling them that way. At the end of the day, these functors assign to
a simply connected topological space
X
a differential graded Lie
algebra (DGLA)
\lambda X
,

X\mapsto\lambda X,

defining a functor

(5)

\lambda:\Top_{1}\to\DGLA_{0},

where
\Top_{1}
is the category of simply connected spaces and
\DGLA_{0}

is the category of
positively graded DGLAs

L=\bigoplus_{n>0}L_{n}
, with the bracket
[-,-]:L_{m}\otimes L_{n}\to L_{m+n}
being
graded antisymmetric,

[y,x]=-(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert\cdot\lvert y\rvert}[x,y],

and satisfying a graded version of the Jacobi identity, with a
derivation of degree
-1
:

d[x,y]=[dx,y]+(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert}[x,dy].

Quillen constructs
\lambda X
as the following mouthful:

\lambda X=N\mathcal{P}\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}G\Sing(X),

where
G
associates Kan’s loop group to a simplicial set, a
simplicial version of the based loop space,
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}G
is a
completed group algebra,
\mathcal{P}
takes the simplicial Lie algebra of
primitive elements, and
N
maps simplicial Lie algebras to DGLAs by
taking the normalized chains.

Then Quillen uses a similar machinery to that of DGCAs to define the
minimal model of a (positively graded) DGLA and shows that its
isomorphism class determines the rational homotopy type of
X
. Here
we will use the same
\lambda X
but assign to it a minimal

L_{\infty}
-algebra
Q(X)
, rather than a minimal DGLA. We will explain
in which way this is more in line with Sullivan’s approach.

8.2.
L_{\infty}
-Algebras

First, we need a number of algebraic definitions and constructions. A

L_{\infty}
-algebra
is a graded vector space
L=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}
with a codifferential
D
on the
cofree
coaugemented cocommutative coalgebra
, i.e., the graded symmetric
coalgebra

C(L):=S(L[-1])=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}S^{n}(L[-1]),

where
L[-1]=\bigoplus_{n\geq 2}L[-1]_{n}
with
L[-1]_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L_{n-1}
is the suspension of
L
. The comultiplication
\Delta:S(L[-1])\to S(L[-1])\otimes S(L[-1])
is the standard shuffle
comultiplication, which is coassociative and graded cocommutative. The
coaugmentation is the coalgebra homomorphism
k=S^{0}(L[-1])\hookrightarrow S(L[-1])
and the
codifferential

D:S(L[-1])\to S(L[-1])
is a graded degree-
(-1)
coderivation,

\Delta(D(x))=D(x_{(1)})\otimes x_{(2)}+(-1)^{\lvert x_{(1)}\rvert}x_{(1)}\otimes D(x_{(2)}),

using
Sweedler’s notation

x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Delta(x)
, such that
D(1)=0
and
D^{2}=0
. The codifferential on a
cofree coalgebra is determined by the projection onto the cogenerating
space
L[-1]
:

S(L[-1])\xrightarrow{D}S(L[-1])\to L[-1],

and the restriction of this projection to
S^{n}(L[-1])
is the

n
-ary bracket

l_{n}
of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
:

l_{n}:S^{n}(L[-1])\hookrightarrow S(L[-1])\xrightarrow{D}S(L[-1])\to L[-1],\qquad n\geq 1.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a generalization of a DGLA: a DGLA
(L,[-,-],d)

may be regarded as an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with
l_{1}=d
,
l_{2}=[-,-]
, and

l_{n}=0
for
n\geq 0
. The condition
D^{2}=0
is equivalent to the
graded Jacobi equation for the bracket
[-,-]
and the derivation
property of
d
, along with the equation
d^{2}=0
. When higher
brackets
l_{n}
are present, the equation
D^{2}
gives the Jacobi
identity for
l_{2}
up to homotopy given by
l_{3}
, and similar higher
Jacobi identities. The unary bracket
l_{1}
is in fact a linear map
L\to L
of degree
-1
such that
(l_{1})^{2}=0
, i.e., a differential on
the underlying graded vector space, denoted by
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}l_{1}
.

An
L_{\infty}
-homomorphism

L_{1}\to L_{2}
is defined as a
homomorphism of the corresponding coaugmented dg-coalgebras
C(L_{1})\to C(L_{2})
. An
L_{\infty}
-isomorphism
is an invertible

L_{\infty}
-homomorphism, and an
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism
is an

L_{\infty}
-homomorphism which induces an isomorphism on homology of the
differentials
d
on
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
.

By definition, the functor
C
, sometimes called the

Chevalley-Eilenberg functor
, establishes an equivalence of
categories

(6)

C:\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\DGCC^{\operatorname{cf}}_{1},

where
\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{0}
is the category of
positively graded

L_{\infty}
-algebras with
L_{\infty}
-morphisms
and
\DGCC^{\operatorname{cf}}_{1}

is the category of
cofree coaugmented nonnegatively graded
dg-cocommutative coalgebras which are simply connected
, those
with
C_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
and
C_{1}=0
. The restrictions on grading can be
relaxed to nonnegatively graded
L_{\infty}
-algebras and connected
nonnegatively graded DGCCs, or completely removed in an obvious way,
but we will be mostly interested in the equivalence (
6
).

8.3.
Minimal
L_{\infty}
-models

We say that an
L_{\infty}
-algebra is
minimal
, if
d=0
. We say
that an
L_{\infty}
-algebra is
contractible
if
l_{n}=0
for
n\geq 2
and
H_{\bullet}(L,d)=0
for the homology of
d
. Note that this
is not an
L_{\infty}
-invariant notion, but it is invariant under

linear
L_{\infty}
-isomorphisms
, i.e., those which are given by
linear maps of the corresponding coalgebras. An
L_{\infty}
-model
of
an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L^{\prime}
with an

L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism
L^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\qis}L
. As in the theory of
minimal Sullivan models, minimal
L_{\infty}
-models play a crucial role.

The theory of minimal
L_{\infty}
-models for
L_{\infty}
-algebras, developed by
M. Kontsevich in a couple of impressionistic brush strokes

[
Kon03
]
, is parallel to the theory of minimal Sullivan
models for DGCAs, but actually simpler.

The following theorem is parallel to
Theorem

2
for DGCAs.

Theorem 7

(
[
Kon03
, Lemma 4.9]
,
[
BFM13
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

Every
L_{\infty}
-algebra is
L_{\infty}
-isomorphic to the direct sum of
a minimal
L_{\infty}
-algebra and a contractible one.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is also simpler than that of its DGCA
version. If the fact that in Kontsevich’s paper

[
Kon03
]
, it takes only seven lines is not
convincing, let us indicate the idea. Since we are working over a
field, the underlying complex (i.e., the dg-space)
(L,d)
of an

L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
is isomorphic to the direct sum of its homology

H_{\bullet}(L,d)
and an acyclic complex. The resulting projection of
the complex
(L,d)
onto its homology will then be a chain homotopy
equivalence. Then the homotopy transfer theorem, also known as the
homological perturbation lemma, which uses the fact that the

L_{\infty}
-operad is cofibrant in a suitable model category of
operads, see for example,
[
Ber12
, Theorem 1.23]
, gives an

L_{\infty}
structure on
H_{\bullet}(L,d)
so that the inclusion to

L
and projection onto
H_{\bullet}(L,d)
are

L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphisms.
∎

This theorem and a rather obvious inverse mapping theorem

[
Kon03
, Section 4.1]
, which says that an

L_{\infty}
-homomorphism whose linear part is an isomorphism of graded
vector spaces is an
L_{\infty}
-isomorphism, immediately implies the
following analogue of
Theorem

1
.

Theorem 8

(
[
Kon03
, Section 4.5.1]
,
[
BFM13
, Theorem 2.7]
)
.

(1)

Every
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphism between minimal
L_{\infty}
-algebras
is an
L_{\infty}
-isomorphism.

(2)

Every DGLA has a minimal
L_{\infty}
-model, unique up to isomorphism.

8.4.
The
L_{\infty}
version of the fundamental theorem of RHT

Now back to spaces. Let us use the functor (
5
) constructed
by Quillen. Given a simply connected space
X
, let us call a minimal

L_{\infty}
-model
Q(X)\xrightarrow{\qis}\lambda X
of the DGLA
\lambda X
the
Quillen minimal model
of
X
. It is defined up to

L_{\infty}
-isomorphism by the theorem above. Note that traditionally, the
Quillen model is defined differently: it is a DGLA which is free as a
graded Lie algebra and with a decomposable differential, see for
example,
[
BS23
, Section 1.9]
. In this way, Quillen’s
original approach is also parallel to Sullivan’s theory, but the
structure Theorems
2
and
7
suggest that Koszul
duality between DGCAs and
L_{\infty}
-algebras may be a better alternative
to the parallel between semifree DGCAs and semifree DGLAs.

The following analogue of
Theorem

5
works under weaker assumptions,
except for simple connectivity, and deals with covariant, rather than
contravariant functors. Consider the composition of functors

\Top_{1}\xrightarrow{\lambda}\DGLA_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{0}\xrightarrow{C}\DGCC_{1},

where
\DGCC_{1}
is the category of
simply connected coaugmented
nonnegatively graded dg-cocommutative coalgebras
. Even though
Quillen does not include in his work the category
\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{0}
, we know
it is equivalent to the full subcategory
\DGCC^{\operatorname{cf}}_{1}

of cofree objects in the category
\DGCC_{1}
.

Theorem 9

(
[
Qui69
, Theorem I]
)
.

The functor
\lambda
composed with the functors
\DGLA_{0}\to\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{0}\linebreak\to\DGCC_{1}
induces equivalences of categories

\Ho_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Top_{1})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ho(\DGLA_{0})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ho(\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{0})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ho(\DGCC_{1}),

where
\Ho_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Top_{1})
is the localization of the category
\Top_{1}
by
rational equivalences and, in the other cases,
\Ho
stands for the
localization of the corresponding category with respect to
quasi-isomorphisms.

This theorem implies that the isomorphism class of the Quillen minimal
model
Q(X)
is a complete invariant of the rational homotopy type of
a simply connected space
X
: such spaces
X
and
Y
are rationally
equivalent if and only if their Quillen minimal models
Q(X)
and

Q(Y)
are
L_{\infty}
-isomorphic.

One feature of Quillen’s functor
\lambda
is that the rational
homology of the DGLA
\lambda X
is naturally isomorphic to the
rational homotopy of a simply connected space
X
, with a shift:

H_{\bullet}(\lambda X,d)\cong\pi_{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[1].

Therefore, the shifted rational homotopy groups
\pi_{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[1]
acquire a graded Lie bracket. Quillen shows that this
bracket is the Whitehead product
[
Qui69
, Theorem I]
.

By definition, the Quillen minimal model
Q(X)
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
based on the graded
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space

Q(X)\cong H_{\bullet}(\lambda X,d)\cong\pi_{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[1],

cf. (
1
) and (
2
) for the Sullivan minimal
model. The binary bracket of this
L_{\infty}
structure is the Whitehead
product, while the higher
L_{\infty}
brackets have been shown to be the
higher Whitehead products, see
[
BBMFM17
]
.

8.5.
Relation to Sullivan’s theory

For a simply connected space
X
of finite
\mathbb{Q}
-type, the graded
linear dual
C(Q(X))^{*}
of the dg-cocommutative coalgebra

C(Q(X))
of the Quillen minimal model
Q(X)
is a DGCA. The fact that
the differential on the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
Q(X)
vanishes implies that the
linear part of the differential, the dual of
l_{1}
, on
C(Q(X))^{*}

vanishes, i.e., the differential is decomposable. Also, under the
finiteness assumption, the graded commutative algebra
C(Q(X))^{*}
is
isomorphic to
S(Q(X)^{*})
. This implies that
C(Q(X))^{*}
is a minimal
Sullivan algebra. Thus, a space
X
of the above type gets two minimal
Sullivan algebras:
C(Q(X))^{*}
and the Sullivan minimal model

M(X)
. Two natural constructions rarely happen to be different. This
is not the case here, either, as the following interpretation of
Majewski’s result shows.

Theorem 10

(Majewski
[
Maj00
, Theorem 4.90]
, cf.
[
BFM13
, Remark 2.9]
and
[
BS23
, Remark 1.40]
)
.

The two minimal Sullivan algebras associated to a simply connected
space of finite
\mathbb{Q}
-type,
C(Q(X))^{*}
and the Sullivan minimal
model
M(X)
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Under the assumptions, the graded dual
M(X)^{*}\cong S(\pi_{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q})
, see (
2
), is a dg-cocommutative
coalgebra, just like
C(Q(X))\cong S(\pi_{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q})
. The corresponding
L_{\infty}
structures on
\pi_{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[1]
are
L_{\infty}
-isomorphic. Indeed, the first one is

L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphic to its double bar construction, which is the
bar construction
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}(M(X))
of
M(X)
(the free Lie algebra on

M(X)^{*}[1]
with the differential incorporating the DGCA structure on

M(X)
). By the very definition of
Q(X)
, the second
L_{\infty}
structure
on
\pi_{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[1]
is
L_{\infty}
-quasi-isomorphic to

\lambda X
, which is quasi-isomorphic as a DGLA to Quillen’s original
minimal model
L_{X}
of
X
(the minimal semifree DGLA model of

\lambda X
). Majewski
[
Maj00
, Theorem 4.90]
proves that the
DGLAs
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}(M(X))
and
L_{X}
are quasi-isomorphic. Therefore, so are
the two
L_{\infty}
structures on
\pi_{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[1]
. Since
both are minimal, they must be
L_{\infty}
-isomorphic by
Theorem

8
. By
definition of an
L_{\infty}
-isomorphism, this means the corresponding
dg-cocommutative coalgebras
M(X)^{*}
and
C(Q(X))
are
isomorphic. Thus, the DGCAs
M(X)
and
C(Q(X))^{*}
are isomorphic.
∎

8.6.
The nerve of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra

Here we will deal with nonnegatively graded
L_{\infty}
-algebras for
simplicity. The more general case can be treated by considering
completed
L_{\infty}
-algebras, see
[
BS23
]
. As these form a
category important for the RHT of simply connected and nilpotent
spaces, it will be general enough for our purposes. Given a
nonegatively graded
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
, let us define the
nerve
,
also known as the
Maurer-Cartan space
, see
[
Hin97
,
Get09
,
BS23
]
,

(7)

\MC(L):=\left\{x\in(\Omega^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}L)_{-1}\mid dx+\tfrac{1}{2!}l_{2}(x,x)+\tfrac{1}{3!}l_{3}(x,x,x)+\dots=0\right\},

where
\Omega^{\bullet}
is the simplicial DGCA of
Section

4.2

with the opposite grading
\Omega_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Omega^{-n}
, so that the de
Rham differential takes degree
-1
. Under the grading assumptions,
the equation (
7
), for every element
x\in(\Omega^{\bullet}([n])\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}L)_{-1}
, will in fact contain at
most finitely many nonzero terms, because the degree of the de Rham
factors of

will have to be strictly negative, and the ideal of
forms of nonzero degree in the polynomial de Rham algebra of the
standard
n
-simplex is nilpotent.

The nerve gives a functor
\MC:\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{-1}\to\sSet
, which,
combined with the geometric realization, gives a functor

\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{-1}\xrightarrow{\MC}\sSet\xrightarrow{\lvert-\rvert}\Top,

where
\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{-1}
is the category of nonnegatively graded

L_{\infty}
-algebras. Restricting to positively graded
L_{\infty}
-algebras, we
get a functor

\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{0}\to\Top_{1},

which induces an inverse of the equivalence

\Ho_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Top_{1})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ho(\mathbf{L_{\infty}}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}_{0})

of
Theorem

9
at least for simply connected spaces of finite
type and positively graded
L_{\infty}
-algebras of finite type
(finite-dimensional in each degree), see
[
Ber15
, Proposition
6.1]
.

As a consequence, we get an isomorphism of graded vector spaces

(8)

\pi_{\bullet}(\lvert\MC(L)\rvert)\otimes\mathbb{Q}[1]\cong H_{\bullet}(L,d)

for a positively graded
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
of finite type, see

[
Ber15
, Theorem 1.1]
. If
L
is minimal, it is the
Quillen minimal model of
\lvert\MC(L)\rvert
and its homology coincides
with the algebra,
H_{\bullet}(L,d)=L
, and acquires the structure of
an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. In this case, (
8
) is an isomorphism
of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. This follows from
Theorem

10
.

9.
Some physical applications

9.1.
Hypothesis H

Sati
[
Sat18
]
made a remarkable observation that the equations
of motion (EOMs) of supergravity in an 11-dimensional (
d=11
)
spacetime are essentially the equations for the differential in the
Sullivan minimal model of
S^{4}
, see
Example

4.2
. Supergravity is the
low-energy, infrared limit of M-theory, which is supposed to be the
“mother of them all:” by combining S- and T-dualities, one can
arrive at type I, IIA, and IIB string theories as well as various
flavors of heterotic string theories. Dimensional reductions of
d=11

supergravity lead to supergravity theories in lower dimensions.

The EOMs of
d=11
supergravity are the equations

(9)

\displaystyle dG_{4}=0,\qquad dG_{7}=-\frac{1}{2}G_{4}\wedge G_{4},

\displaystyle G_{7}=*G_{4}

on two fields
G_{4}
and
G_{7}
, which are just differential forms on
the 11-dimensional spacetime
X
:

G_{4}\in\Omega^{4}(X),\qquad G_{7}\in\Omega^{7}(X).

Here
*
is the Hodge star, derived from a given metric on

X
. Returning to the following Sullivan minimal model of
S^{4}
:

M_{\mathbb{R}}(S^{4})=\mathbb{R}[g_{4},g_{7}]
with
\lvert g_{4}\rvert=4
,
\lvert g_{7}\rvert=7
,
and
dg_{4}=0
,
dg_{7}=-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{4}^{2}
, we see that there is a
DGCA homomorphism

(10)

\displaystyle M_{\mathbb{R}}(S^{4})

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{\bullet}(X),

\displaystyle g_{4}

\displaystyle\mapsto G_{4},

\displaystyle g_{7}

\displaystyle\mapsto G_{7}.

Moreover, the RHT of the four-sphere encompasses the topological part
(
9
) of the EOMs, called the
duality-symmetric
EOMs
. The equation
G_{7}=*G_{4}
is important, but it is geometric,
rather than topological. In a way, the four-sphere carries in its
rational homotopy type information about the duality-symmetric

9.2.
Mysterious Triality and cyclic loop spaces of
S^{4}

Mysterious Duality has been tantalizing the world of mathematical
physics since 2001, when A. Iqbal, A. Neitzke, and C. Vafa

[
INV01
]
discovered a convincing, yet mysterious correspondence
between certain symmetry patterns in the geometry of del Pezzo
surfaces, see
[
Man86
]
, and in toroidal compactifications of
M-theory, both governed by the root system series
E_{k}
.

In the context of algebraic geometry, the
E_{k}
root system appears
out of intersection theory on del Pezzo surfaces by a simple algebraic
argument: given a
Lorentzian
,
i.e
., of index
(1,k)
,
lattice of rank
k+1
with
k\leq 8
, let
-K
be the vector
-K=(3,-1,-1,\dots,-1)
with respect to a Lorentzian basis; then the
set of lattice vectors of inner square
-2
and orthogonal to
-K

form a root system that may be shown to be the root system of type

E_{k}
. What is remarkable in the geometry of del Pezzo surfaces is
that all this data is natural: the lattice is the second cohomology
group
H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{k};\mathbb{Z})
of the del Pezzo surface
\mathbb{B}_{k}
, the
Lorentzian inner product is the intersection form, and
-K
is the
anticanonical class, the first Chern class of the holomorphic tangent
bundle of
\mathbb{B}_{k}

In
[
SV23b
]

iterated cyclic loop spaces of
4

S^{4}

S^{4},\mathcal{L}_{c}S^{4},\mathcal{L}_{c}^{2}S^{4},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{c}^{8}S^{4}.

We assume that if

on this list happens not to be

S^{1}\to Z
and slightly abuse notation by using the same symbol

\mathcal{L}_{c}Z

Here is how the

to the infinitesimal symmetries of the Sullivan minimal model

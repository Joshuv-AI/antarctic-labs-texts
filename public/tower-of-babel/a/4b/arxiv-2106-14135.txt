Yuri Shimizu

Abstract
Cosheaves are a dual notion of sheaves. In this paper, we prove existence of a dual of sheafifications, called cosheafifications , in the ∞ \infty -category theory. We also prove that the ∞ \infty -category of ∞ \infty -cosheaves is presentable and equivalent to an ∞ \infty -category of left adjoint functors.



Introduction

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a small category equipped with a pretopology
J
and
\mathcal{V}
be a category.
Recall that a functor
\mathscr{G}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{V}
is called a
precosheaf
on
\mathcal{X}
.
It is called a
cosheaf
if the diagram

\coprod_{j,k\in I}\mathscr{G}(U_{j}\times_{U}U_{j})\rightrightarrows\coprod_{i\in I}\mathscr{G}(U_{i})\to\mathscr{G}(U)

(0.1)

is a coequalizer in
\mathcal{V}
for all
\{U_{i}\to U\}_{i}\in J
.
In other words, a cosheaf is a
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{op}}
-valued sheaf.
This is a dual notion of sheaves.
For example, the zeroth homology functor on the category of open sets is a cosheaf of abelian groups.
We write
\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
for the category of
\mathcal{V}
-valued precosheaves on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
for its full subcategory consisting of cosheaves.
In
[
Pra16
]
, Prasolov proved that the inclusion
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
admits a
right
adjoint, called the cosheafification functor. This is a dual notion of sheafification, which is a
left
adjoint functor of the natural inclusion.

In this paper, we develop a basic theory of cosheaves in
\infty
-category,
and prove the existence of cosheafifications in the
\infty
-categorical situation.
Let
\mathcal{X}
be a small
\infty
-site and
\mathcal{V}
be a symmetric closed monoidal
\infty
-category which is presentable.
We define
\mathcal{V}
-valued cosheaves on
\mathcal{X}
by using Čech nerves instead of coequalizer diagrams (Definition
3.1
).
The reason that we use Čech nerves is to define cosheaves as a dual of sheaves in the sense of Lurie
[
Lur09
]
.
It turns out that this definition is indeed a generalization of cosheaves in
1
-category (see Remark
3.6
).

Our main result is summarized as follows.

Theorem 1

(see Theorem
3.4
)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{V}
satisfy the conditions (
2.2
) and (
3.1
). Then

(1)
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
admits a right adjoint,

(2)
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is presentable, and

(3)
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of left adjoint functors from
\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
to
\mathcal{V}
.

The right adjoint functor constructed in (1) gives the
\infty
-cosheafification.
The conditions (
2.2
) and (
3.1
) are satisfied in various cases including the following
(cf. Examples
2.11
and
3.5
):
with
\mathcal{X}
arbitrary,

•

\mathcal{V}
the
\infty
-category
\mathbb{S}
of
\infty
-groupoids,

•

\mathcal{V}
the full subcategory of
\mathbb{S}
consisting of
n
-truncated objects for
n\geq 0
,

•

\mathcal{V}
the connective part of the
\infty
-derived category of a commutative ring,

•

\mathcal{V}
the
\infty
-category of connective spectra.

In
[
BF06
]
, Bunge-Funk proved that for a site
\mathcal{X}
and an elementary topos
\mathcal{V}
,
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is equivalent to the category of cocontinuous functors from
\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
to
\mathcal{V}
. Theorem
1
(3) implies Bunge-Funk’s result in the case
\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Set}
.

This paper is organized as follows. In §
1
, we give technical preliminaries from the
\infty
-category theory. In §
2
, we prove that the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is equivalent to the
\infty
-category of left adjoint functors from

to
\mathcal{V}
under the condition (

). In §

1
Right non-degenerate
\infty
-bifunctors

In this section, we give technical preliminaries from the theory of
\infty
-categories. For
\infty
-categories
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, we write
[\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}]^{\mathrm{L}}
(resp.
[\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}]^{\mathrm{R}}
) for the full subcategory of
[\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}]
consisting of left (resp. right) adjoint functors. We refer to
[
Lur09
]
for the basic theory of
\infty
-categories.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{V}
be
\infty
-categories. A bifunctor
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}
is called
right non-degenerate
, if the induced functor
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}\to[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V}]
is fully faithful and its essential image coincides with
[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}
.

Throughout this section, we fix a right non-degenerate bifunctor
\left\langle{},{}\right\rangle:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}
.

Lemma 1.2
.

If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{V}
are presentable, then so is
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
.

Proof.

This follows from the equivalence of
\infty
-categories
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}\cong[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}
and
[
Lur09
, Prop. 5.5.3.8]
.
∎

We write
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}
for the functor
\left\langle{-},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{V}
. Recall that there exists a canonical equivalence of
\infty
-categories

\varepsilon:[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\left([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{C}]^{\mathrm{R}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}

such that
\varepsilon F
is right adjoint to
F
for all
F\in[\mathcal{C},\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}
(see
[
Lur09
, Prop. 5.2.6.2]
and its proof). Then we write
\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}=\varepsilon(\mathscr{G}^{\dagger})
for
\mathscr{G}\in\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. We write
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
for the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
consisting of
\mathscr{G}\in\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
such that
\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v\in\mathcal{D}
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
. We call
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}

the dual of

\mathcal{D}
with respect to
\left\langle{},{}\right\rangle
.

The following property says that localizations of
\mathcal{C}
induce a right-nondegenerate bifunctor.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a reflective full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

(1) The restriction
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}
of
\left\langle{},{}\right\rangle
is right non-degenerate.

(2) If
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{V}
are presentable, then
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
is coreflective in
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
.

Proof.

(1) For
\mathscr{G}\in\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
, the functor
\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{C}
induces
\mathscr{G}_{\ddagger}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{D}
. Since
\mathscr{G}_{\ddagger}
is right adjoint to
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}|_{\mathcal{D}}
, we obtain
(-)_{\ddagger}:\mathcal{D}^{\vee}\to([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{C}]^{\mathrm{R}})^{\mathrm{op}}
. By the
\infty
-commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\mathcal{D}@>{(-)_{\ddagger}}>{}>\left([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{D}]^{\mathrm{R}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\mathcal{C}@>{}>{\cong}>\left([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{C}]^{\mathrm{R}}\right)^{\mathrm{op}},\end{CD}

the functor
(-)_{\ddagger}
is fully faithful. The composition

{G^{\prime}:\mathcal{V}\xrightarrow{G}\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}}

is contained in
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{C}]^{\mathrm{R}}
for all
G\in[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{D}]^{\mathrm{R}}
. Thus there exists
\mathscr{G}\in\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
such that
\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}
is equivalent to
G^{\prime}
in
([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{C}]^{\mathrm{R}})^{\mathrm{op}}
. Then we have
\mathscr{G}_{\ddagger}\cong G
. This means that the functor
(-)_{\ddagger}
is essentially surjective and thus equivalence of
\infty
-categories. Therefore,
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}
induces an equivalence of
\infty
-categories
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\cong}([\mathcal{V},\mathcal{D}]^{\mathrm{R}})^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\cong}[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}
.

(2) By Lemma
1.2
,
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
are presentable. Thus by
[
Lur09
, Cor. 5.5.2.9]
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
is closed under small colimits in
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
. Since limits of right adjoint functors are computed objectwise and
\mathcal{D}
is closed under small limits in
\mathcal{C}
, the functor
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{D}]^{\mathrm{R}}\to[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{C}]^{\mathrm{R}}
preserves all small limits. Therefore, the assertion follows from the equivalences
(\mathcal{C}^{\vee})^{\mathrm{op}}\cong[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{C}]^{\mathrm{R}}
and
(\mathcal{D}^{\vee})^{\mathrm{op}}\cong[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{D}]^{\mathrm{R}}
.
∎

Next, we consider the dual of the localization of
\mathcal{C}
by a set of morphisms
S
. Let
\mathcal{C}^{S\mathchar 45\mathrm{loc}}
be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of
S
-local objects (see
[
Lur09
, Def. 5.5.4.1]
). We write
\mathcal{C}_{S\mathchar 45\mathrm{col}^{\vee}}
for the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
consisting of objects
\mathscr{G}
such that for every
f:\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
in
S
, the induced morphism

\left\langle{f},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle:\left\langle{\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle\to\left\langle{\mathscr{F}^{\prime}},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle

is an equivalence in
\mathcal{V}
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
S
be a set of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
. Then
(\mathcal{C}^{S\mathchar 45\mathrm{loc}})^{\vee}=\mathcal{C}_{S\mathchar 45\mathrm{col}^{\vee}}
.

Proof.

Let
f:\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
be a morphism in
\mathcal{C}
and let
\mathscr{G}\in\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
. It suffices to show that the following two conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The morphism
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}f:\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
is an equivalence in
\mathcal{V}
.

(2)

The morphism
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(f,\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v):\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime},\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v)\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v)
is an equivalence in
\mathbb{S}
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
.

By the
\infty
-Yoneda lemma, the condition (
1
) is equivalent to the following.

(
1
’)

The morphism
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}f,v):\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\mathscr{F},v)\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\mathscr{F}^{\prime},v)
is an equivalence in
\mathbb{S}
for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
.

Since
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}
is left adjoint to
\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}
, the equivalence
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}f,v)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(f,\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v)
shows that (
1
’)
\Leftrightarrow
(
2
).
∎

Remark 1.6
.

When
\mathcal{C}
is presentable and
S
is small,
\mathcal{C}^{S\mathchar 45\mathrm{loc}}
is reflective and can be regarded as the localization
S^{-1}\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{C}
by
S
(see
[
Lur09
, Prop. 5.5.4.2 and 5.5.4.15]
). Therefore, we obtain a right non-degenerate bifunctor
S^{-1}\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}_{S\mathchar 45\mathrm{col}^{\vee}}\to\mathcal{V}
by Proposition
1.4
.

2
Pairing between presheaves and precosheaves

Throughout this section, we fix a small simplicial set
\mathcal{X}
and (the underlying
\infty
-category of) a symmetric closed monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{V}
which is complete and cocomplete. We write
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})=[\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{V}]
and
\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})=[\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}]
. Our aim of this section is to give a canonical right non-degenerate bifunctor
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\times\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{V}
. Let
\otimes
,
\mathscr{H}om
and
1
l
denote the monidal product, the internal hom and the monoidal unit of
\mathcal{V}
, respectively. Let
r_{*}
be the covariant functor
\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{S}
represented by
1
l
and
r^{*}
be the functor
\mathbb{S}\to\mathcal{V}
defined by
S_{\bullet}\mapsto\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}(S_{\bullet}\to\{\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}\}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V})
. The pair
(r^{*},r_{*})
is a variation of free-forgetful adjunctions.

Lemma 2.1
.

The functor
r_{*}
is right adjoint to
r^{*}
.

Proof.

For each
S_{\bullet}\in\mathbb{S}
,
v\in\mathcal{V}
and
n\geq 0
, there exist equivalences

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(r^{*}S_{n},v)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}\left(\prod_{s\in S_{n}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l},v\right)\cong\coprod_{s\in S_{n}}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l},v)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{S}}(S_{n},r_{*}v).

Since
S_{\bullet}
is equivalent to the colimit of the functor
\Delta\to\mathbb{S}
,
[n]\mapsto S_{n}
, and
r^{*}
preserves colimits, we obtain an equivalence

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(r^{*}S_{\bullet},v)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{S}}(S_{\bullet},r_{*}v)

which is functorial in both
S_{\bullet}
and
v
.
∎

Let
\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}
be the
\infty
-Yoneda embedding
\mathcal{X}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
. By Lemma
2.1
,
r^{*}
and
r_{*}
induce an adjunction

\overline{r}:\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})\rightleftarrows\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}):\underline{r}.

We define
\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}
as the composition
\overline{r}\circ\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}
. We give examples of
r^{*}
and
r_{*}
for some
\mathcal{V}
.

Example 2.2
.

(1) Assume that
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{S}
equipped with the cartesian monoidal structure. Then
r^{*}
and
r_{*}
are equivalent to the identity. Thus we obtain that
\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}\cong\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}
.

(2) Assume that
\mathcal{V}
is the full subcategory category
\mathbb{S}_{\leq n}
of
\mathbb{S}
consisting of
n
-truncated objects for
n\geq 0
. Then
r_{*}
is the inclusion
\mathbb{S}_{\leq n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}
and
r^{*}
is the truncation functor.

(3) Let
\Lambda
be a commutative unital ring. Assume that
\mathcal{V}
is the derived
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)
of
\Lambda
(see
[
Lur12
]
). Recall that
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)
has a monoidal structure induced by derived tensor products. Under this monoidal structure, we obtain that
r_{*}(C_{\bullet})\cong\mathrm{DK}(\tau_{\geq 0}C_{\bullet})
for each
C_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)
, where
\mathrm{DK}
means the Dold-Kan correspondence and
\tau_{\geq 0}
means the truncation. Thus
r^{*}
coincides with the functor of singular chain complexes.

(4) Assume that
\mathcal{V}
is the stable
\infty
-category of spectra
\mathbf{Sp}
. Then
\mathcal{V}
has a symmetric monoidal structure given by smash products (see
[
Lur12
, §4.8.2]
). Since
1
l
is the sphere spectrum,
r^{*}
is the suspension
{\Sigma^{\infty}:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbf{Sp}}
and
r_{*}
is the looping
\Omega^{\infty}:\mathbf{Sp}\to\mathbb{S}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists an equivalence
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}\cong r_{*}\circ\mathscr{H}om
in
[\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{V},\mathbb{S}]
.

Proof.

For each
v,w\in\mathcal{V}
, there exist equivalences

r_{*}\mathscr{H}om(v,w)=\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l},\mathscr{H}om(v,w))\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v\otimes\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l},w)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,w)

which are functorial in both
v
and
w
.
∎

For a bifunctor of
\infty
-categories
F:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, we write
\int_{c\in\mathcal{C}}F(c,c)
(resp.
\int^{c\in\mathcal{C}}F(c,c)
) for the
\infty
-categorical end (resp. coend) of
F
introduced by Glasman
[
Gla16
, Def. 2.2]
. Since ends (resp. coends) are defined as a limit (resp. colimit), these commute with right (resp. left) adjoint functors. We define a bifunctor
\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}:\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{V}
by

\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime})=\int_{U\in\mathcal{X}}\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{F}(U),\mathscr{F}^{\prime}(U))

for
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
. The next lemma is a variation of Yoneda’s lemma for
\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

In
[\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]
, the functor
(U,\mathscr{F})\mapsto\mathscr{F}(U)
is equivalent to

(U,\mathscr{F})\mapsto\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U),\mathscr{F}).

Proof.

Since ends are defined as a limit, these commute with hom-functors. Thus we obtain an equivalence

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U),\mathscr{F}))\cong\int_{V\in\mathcal{X}}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{H}om(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)(V),\mathscr{F}(V)))

for each
U\in\mathcal{X}
,
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
and
v\in\mathcal{V}
. We have

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{H}om(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)(V),\mathscr{F}(V)))

\displaystyle\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v\otimes\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)(V),\mathscr{F}(V))

\displaystyle\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)(V),\mathscr{H}om(v,\mathscr{F}(V))).

By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
, we also have

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)(V),\mathscr{H}om(v,\mathscr{F}(V)))

\displaystyle\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{S}}(\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}(U)(V),r_{*}\mathscr{H}om(v,\mathscr{F}(V)))

\displaystyle\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{S}}(\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}(U)(V),\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{F}(V))).

Hence, we obtain

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{H}om(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U),\mathscr{F}))\cong\int_{V\in\mathcal{X}}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{S}}(\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}(U)(V),\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{F}(V))).

The right-hand side is equivalent to
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})}(\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}(U),\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{F}(-)))
by
[
Gla16
, Prop. 2.3]
. Therefore, the
\infty
-Yoneda lemma in
\mathcal{X}
gives an equivalence

\int_{V\in\mathcal{X}}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}(U)(V),\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{F}(V)))\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{F}(U)).

Applying the
\infty
-Yoneda lemma in
\mathcal{V}
to the equivalence

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{H}om(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U),\mathscr{F}))\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{F}(U)),

we obtain the assertion.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

We define a bifunctor
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle:\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\times\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{V}
by

\left\langle{\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle=\int^{U\in\mathcal{X}}\mathscr{F}(U)\otimes\mathscr{G}(U)

(2.1)

for
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
and
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
. We write
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}
for the functor
\left\langle{-},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle:\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{V}
, and
\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}
for the functor
\mathcal{V}\to\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
defined by
v\mapsto\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{G}(-),v)
.

We are interested in the non-degeneracy of the paring
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle
, for which our main result is Proposition
2.10
. We first prepare some lemmas.

Lemma 2.6
.

In
[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}]
, the functor

(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G},v)\mapsto\mathscr{H}om(\left\langle{\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle,v)

is equivalent to

(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G},v)\mapsto\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v).

Proof.

For each
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
,
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
and
v\in\mathcal{V}
, there exist equivalences

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}om(\left\langle{\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle,v)

\displaystyle\cong\int_{U\in\mathcal{X}}\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{F}(U)\otimes\mathscr{G}(U),v)

\displaystyle\cong\int_{U\in\mathcal{X}}\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{F}(U),\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{G}(U),v))

\displaystyle=\int_{U\in\mathcal{X}}\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{F}(U),(\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v)(U))

\displaystyle=\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v)

which are functorial in
\mathscr{F}
,
\mathscr{G}
and
v
.
∎

By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.6
, we see that
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}
is left adjoint to
\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}
. This means that the pairing (
2.1
) induces a functor
(-)^{\dagger}:\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\to[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}
. Let
\mathrm{Res}
be the functor
[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]\to\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
defined by
F\mapsto F\circ\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}
. We prove that
(-)^{\dagger}
is an essential section of
\mathrm{Res}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The composition

\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\xrightarrow{(-)^{\dagger}}[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Res}}\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})

is equivalent to the identity of
\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
. In other words, we have

\left\langle{\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle\cong\mathscr{G}(U)

which is functorial in
U\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
.

Proof.

For each
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
and
v\in\mathcal{V}
, there exist equivalences

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\circ\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(-),v)\cong\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(-),\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v)\cong(\mathscr{G}_{\dagger}v)(-)=\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{G}(-),v)

by Lemmas
2.6
and
2.4
. Thus Lemma
2.3
gives

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}\circ\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(-),v)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathscr{G}(-),v).

Therefore, the
\infty
-Yoneda lemma in
\mathcal{V}
leads to a functorial equivalence
\mathrm{Res}(\mathscr{G}^{\dagger})\cong\mathscr{G}
.
∎

We prove a variation of the co-Yoneda lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

In
[\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]
, the functor
(V,\mathscr{F})\mapsto\mathscr{F}(V)
is equivalent to

V\mapsto\int^{U\in\mathcal{X}}\mathscr{F}(U)\otimes\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)(V)

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})=\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{V})
and
\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})=\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{V})
, Lemma
2.7
for
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}
gives a desired equivalence.
∎

We write
[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\spadesuit}
for the full subcategory of
[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]
consisting of functors
F
such that the composition

\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\times\mathcal{V}\xrightarrow{(F,\mathrm{id})}\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\xrightarrow{\mathscr{H}om}\mathcal{V}

is equivalent to

\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\times\mathcal{V}\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{id},G)}\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\times\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\xrightarrow{\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}

in
[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\times\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]
. Lemma
2.3
shows that
[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\spadesuit}\subseteq[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

A functor
F:\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{V}
belongs to
[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\spadesuit}
if and only if the following diagram is
\infty
-commutative:

\begin{CD}\mathcal{V}\times\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})@>{\otimes}>{}>\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\\
@V{(\mathrm{id},F)}V{}V@V{}V{F}V\\
\mathcal{V}\times\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})@>{}>{\otimes}>\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}).\end{CD}

Proof.

For
v,w\in\mathcal{V}
,
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
and an adjoint pair
{F:\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\rightleftarrows\mathcal{V}:G}
, we have
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v\otimes F(\mathscr{F}),w)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{H}om(F(\mathscr{F}),w))
and

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(F(v\otimes\mathscr{F}),w)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v\otimes\mathscr{F},G(w))\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{V}}(v,\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(\mathscr{F},G(w))).

Thus the assertion follows from the
\infty
-Yoneda lemma in
\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Now we can prove our non-degeneracy result on the pairing
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle
.

Proposition 2.10
.

The functor
(-)^{\dagger}:\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\to[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\spadesuit}
is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories and
\mathrm{Res}
gives its inverse. In particular, when

[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\spadesuit}=[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}

(2.2)

holds, the pairing
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle
is right non-degenerate.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
, it suffices to show that the composition

[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Res}}\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\xrightarrow{(-)^{\dagger}}[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}

is equivalent to the identity of
[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\spadesuit}
. For
F\in[\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}
and
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
, we have

\displaystyle F(\mathscr{F})

\displaystyle\cong F\left(\int^{U\in\mathcal{X}}\mathscr{F}(U)\otimes\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)(-)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\int^{U\in\mathcal{X}}F\left(\mathscr{F}(U)\otimes\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)(-)\right)

by Lemma
2.8
. We also have

\mathrm{Res}(F)^{\dagger}(\mathscr{F})=\int^{U\in\mathcal{X}}\mathscr{F}(U)\otimes F\left(\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)\right)

by the definition of
\mathrm{Res}(F)
. Thus the assertion follows from Lemma
2.9
.
∎

Finally, we give some examples of
\mathcal{V}
satisfying the condition (
2.2
).

Example 2.11
.

(1) Clearly,

and

).

(2) For a commutative unital ring

3
Proof of the main result

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a small
\infty
-category equipped with a Grothendieck topology
J
in the sense of
[
Lur09
, Def. 6.2.2.1]
and
\mathcal{V}
be a presentable symmetric closed monoidal
\infty
-category. In this section we prove the main result stated in the introduction. We first give a definition of
\mathcal{V}
-valued (co)sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
. Recall that a family of morphisms
\{U_{i}\to X\}
is called a
covering family
of
X\in\mathcal{X}
if the smallest sieve of
\mathcal{X}_{/X}
containing
\{U_{i}\to X\}
is a covering sieve. For each covering family
\mathcal{U}_{0}
, we write
\check{C}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0}):\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
for its Čech nerve and
\check{C}^{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0})=\overline{r}\circ\check{C}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0})
.

We define sheaves and cosheaves by using Čech nerves, instead of more familiar definition via (co)equalizer diagrams.
It seems that this is more suitable for the theory of
\infty
-categories as developed in
[
Lur09
]
.
The two definitions agree (Remark
3.6
).

Definition 3.1
.

(1) A presheaf
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is called a
sheaf
if for each covering family
\mathcal{U}_{0}
of
\mathcal{X}
, the composition

\Delta\xrightarrow{\check{C}^{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(-,\mathscr{F})}\mathcal{V}

is a limit diagram in
\mathcal{V}
. We write
\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
for the full subcategory of
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
consisting of sheaves.

(2) A precosheaf
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is called a
cosheaf
if for each covering family
\mathcal{U}_{0}
of
\mathcal{X}
, the composition

\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\check{C}^{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0})}\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\xrightarrow{\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}}\mathcal{V}

is a colimit diagram in
\mathcal{V}
. We write
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
for the full subcategory of
\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
consisting of cosheaves.

Remark 3.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
has all pullbacks. Then a Čech nerve of a covering family of
\mathcal{X}
can be regarded as a simplicial object in
\mathcal{X}
. Thus by Lemma
2.4
,
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is a sheaf if and only if
\mathscr{F}\circ\check{C}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0})^{\mathrm{op}}
is a limit diagram for every covering family
\mathcal{U}_{0}
of
\mathcal{X}
. Similarly,
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is a cosheaf if and only if
\mathscr{G}\circ\check{C}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0})
is a colimit diagram for every
\mathcal{U}_{0}
by Lemma
2.7
.

For a monomorphism
i_{\mathcal{U}}:j(\mathcal{U})\to\mathrm{Y}^{\infty}
associated with a covering sieve
\mathcal{U}
(see
[
Lur09
, Prop. 6.2.2.5]
), we write
i^{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{U}}=\overline{r}(i_{\mathcal{U}})
and
\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{U})=\overline{r}(j(\mathcal{U}))
. We write
S_{\mathcal{V}}=\{i^{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{U}}\in\mathcal{M}or(\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}))\ |\ \mathcal{U}\in J\}
. The following lemma assures that our definition of sheaves coincides with that of
[
Lur09
, Def. 6.2.2.6]
when
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{S}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

A presheaf
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is a sheaf if and only if the morphism
\mathscr{H}om^{\prime}(i^{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathscr{F})
is an equivalence in
\mathcal{V}
for all
i^{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathcal{U}}\in S_{\mathcal{V}}
. Moreover, when
r_{*}
is conservative, i.e., it reflects equivalences, then
\mathscr{F}
is a sheaf if and only if it is
S_{\mathcal{V}}
-local.

Proof.

Let
\Delta^{\geq 1}
for the full subcategory of
\Delta
consisting of
[n]
for
n\geq 1
. Since
\overline{r}
preserves colimits, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}\check{C}^{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0})|_{\Delta^{\geq 1}}\cong\overline{r}(\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}\check{C}(\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}_{0})|_{\Delta^{\geq 1}})\cong\overline{r}(j(\mathcal{U}))=\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{U}).

Thus the first half of the assertion follows from Lemma
2.4
and the second half follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Now we prove the main result.
This can be applied to the
\infty
-categories in the following Example
3.5
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{V}
satisfy (
2.2
) and also the following condition:

\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})=\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})^{S_{\mathcal{V}}\mathchar 45\mathrm{loc}}.

(3.1)

Then the following hold.

(1) Under the pairing (
2.1
), we have
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})=\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})^{\vee}
.

(2) The
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is presentable.

(3) The
\infty
-category
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is presentable.

(4) The inclusion
\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
admits a left adjoint.

(5) The inclusion
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
admits a right adjoint.

(6) There exists an equivalence of
\infty
-categories
\mathrm{CSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})\cong[\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{V}]^{\mathrm{L}}
.

Proof.

The assertion (2) follows from Lemma
3.3
and
[
Lur09
, Prop. 5.5.4.15]
. Moreover, (1) and (2) imply (3)-(6). Indeed, since
\mathrm{Sh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is closed under limits in
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
, (4) follows from (2) and
[
Lur09
, Cor. 5.5.2.9]
. Therefore, (3), (5) and (6) follow from Lemma
1.2
and Proposition
1.4
. Thus it suffices to prove (1). Since
\mathscr{G}^{\dagger}
preserves colimits, a precosheaf
\mathscr{G}\in\mathrm{PCSh}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
is a cosheaf if and only if the canonical morphism

\left\langle{\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{U})},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle\to\left\langle{\mathrm{Y}^{\mathcal{V}}(U)},{\mathscr{G}}\right\rangle

is an equivalence in
\mathcal{V}
by Lemma
2.7
. Thus we obtain (1) by Proposition
1.5
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Recall that if
\mathcal{X}
is a
1
-category and
\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Set}
, cosheaves are usually defined via coequalizer diagrams (cf. (
0.1
)). Theorem
3.4
(1) says that our Definition
3.1
(2) is equivalent to this more familiar definition for such
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
. Indeed, we can prove that the category of cosheaves defined by coequalizer diagrams is the dual of the category of sheaves by a similar proof. Similarly, our definition is also a generalization of ordinary cosheaves of modules.

Remark 3.7
.

In the context of
\infty
-topos theory, sheaves on a simplicial set
\mathcal{X}
are sometime defined as an object of an
\infty
-topos
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{X}
. Note that there exists an
\infty
-topos which is not the
\infty
-category of sheaves on a Grothendieck topology on
\mathcal{X}
, unlike the ordinary topos theory. Then Theorem
3.4
suggests a definition of cosheaves in such a situation. Namely, we can define a cosheaf on
\mathcal{T}
as an object of the dual
\mathcal{T}^{\vee}
, or equivalently, a left adjoint functor
\mathcal{T}\to\mathbb{S}
.

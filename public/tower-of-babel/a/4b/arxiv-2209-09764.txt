I
Introduction

For a symmetric energy-momentum tensor
T^{\mu\nu}
the dilation current
S^{\mu}=T^{\mu\lambda}x_{\lambda}
and the four
conformal currents
K^{\alpha\mu}=x^{2}T^{\alpha\mu}-2x^{\alpha}T^{\mu\lambda}x_{\lambda}
are conserved if the energy-momentum
tensor is traceless:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}S^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu}

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}K^{\alpha\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2x^{\alpha}T^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu}.

The classical energy-momentum tensor for QCD with massless fermions is traceless but quantum corrections introduce
a renormalization scale that spoils the conservation of scale and conformal currents and renders the
trace nonzero
Coleman
.

The trace of the thermally averaged energy-momentum tensor is

\langle T^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu}\rangle=u-3P
where
u=\langle T^{0}_{\;\;0}\rangle
is the energy density
and
P=-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\langle T^{j}_{\;\;j}\rangle/3
is the pressure. The relation

\exp(\beta PV)=Z={\rm Tr}\{e^{-\beta H}\}

between the pressure and the
partition function implies that

{\partial\over\partial\beta}(\beta P)=-{\langle H\rangle\over V}=-u,

or equivalently

T{\partial P\over\partial T}=u+P.

The trace of the energy-momentum tensor becomes

\langle T^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu}\rangle=u-3P=T{\partial P\over\partial T}-4P.

(1)

For non-Abelian gauge fields with massless fermions the pressure has the form

P=T^{4}\Phi(g_{M},M/T),

(2)

where
M
is the renormalization scale. From (
1
) the trace of the energy-momentum tensor is

\langle T^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu}\rangle=T^{5}{\partial\Phi\over\partial T}.

(3)

One would expect the calculation of
\Phi
to be primary and the trace anomaly only an afterthought.
However with the theorem of Drummond, Horgan, Landshoff, and Rebhan
Landshoff
that

\langle T^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu}\rangle=\beta(g_{M}){\partial P\over\partial g_{M}}

(4)

the anomaly becomes predictive in that the combination of (
3
) and (
4
) gives

T{\partial\Phi\over\partial T}=\beta(g_{M}){\partial\Phi\over\partial g_{M}},

(5)

which is Eq. (3.11) of Drummond et al
Landshoff
.

Note that (
4
) is similar to the zero temperature operator identity

T^{\mu}_{\;\;\mu}=\beta(g_{M})\partial{\cal L}/\partial g_{M}
.

Sec. II shows how Eq. (
5
) ensures that
P
is independent of the renormalization scale
M
and requires
P
to have the
structure shown in the abstract. Sec. III tests the recursion relation using known results for
\phi_{0}(g_{M})
from perturbation theory
to calculate the three known terms in
\phi_{1}(g_{M})
and the only known term of
\phi_{2}(g_{M})
and illustrates how to

Prescribed mean curvature flow for noncompact hypersurfaces in Lorentz manifolds
Luen-Fai Tam

Abstract
. Motivated by previous study on mean curvature flow and prescribed mean curvature flow on spatially compact space or asymptotically flat spacetime, in this work we will find sufficient conditions for the short time existence of prescribed mean curvature flow on a Lorentz manifold with a smooth time function starting from a complete noncompact spacelike hypersurface. Long time existence and convergence will also be discussed. Results will be applied to study some prescribed mean curvature flows inside the future of the origin in the Minkowski spacetime. Examples of spacetime related to the existence and convergence results near the future null infinity of the Schwarzschild spacetime are also discussed.

Let us discuss some of the assumptions in the above theorems. In Theorem
1.1
, unlike the case of complete Riemannian manifold, condition (i) in general may not be true, unless, for example, the spacetime is timelike geodesically complete. Condition (ii) is to ensure that the level sets

In Theorem
1.2
, the condition on

is used to obtain estimate of the tilt factor which has been derived by Ecker
[

means that longtime existence is true if we have height estimates, which can be obtained if there exist suitable barrier surfaces. In Theorem

will converge exponentially fast as in
[

. (iii) can be replaced by the conditions that

is monotone and

for all unit timelike vector. In Theorem

is convergent as maps. We only consider the convergence of hypersurfaces in terms of Hausdorff distance as in

. Moreover, it is unclear if the limit surface is complete. In case,

, then we do not need to assume

. In this case, this is the result in
[
8
]
. We should remark by changing the time orientation, dual results are true for Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
. See Remark

for more details.

By the above results, in order to use prescribed mean curvature flow to obtain prescribed mean curvature hypersurfaces, under suitable assumptions, it is sufficient to obtain height estimates. This can be done if suitable barrier surfaces exist. See Corollary
4.1
and Proposition

for examples. Also, under additional assumptions on the foliation in

, result similar to the result for maximal surface in asymptotically flat spacetime in

]
might also true for positive constant mean curvature cut in asymptotically Schwarzschild spacetime which was introduced and studied in

.

2.
Short time existence

2.1.
Setup and preliminary results

Before we prove the short time existence Theorem
1.1
, we need some preparation. Let
(N,G)
,
{\overline{\nabla}}
,
\,{\overline{\mathrm{Rm}}}
,
\tau
,
{\alpha}
,
{\mathbf{T}}
,
G_{E}
be as in section
1
.
First, it is obvious that
G_{E}
depends on
\tau
and will be called the reference Riemannian metric with respect to
\tau
.
If
\tau^{\prime}
is another time function with corresponding future pointing unit normal
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
, and let
G_{E}^{\prime}
be the reference Riemannian metric with respect to
\tau^{\prime}
, then
G_{E},G_{E}^{\prime}
are different. However, if

-\langle\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\rangle
(which is bounded from below by 1) is uniformly bounded from above then they will be equivalent by the following result which is well-known and has been used in
[
3
]
, see
[
10
]
for example:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(V^{n+1},G)
be a Lorentz vector space. Choose a fixed time orientation. Let
{\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
be two future unit time like unit vectors with dual one forms
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
. Consider the Euclidean metrics

G_{E}=G+2\omega\otimes\omega,G_{E}^{\prime}=G+2\omega^{\prime}\otimes\omega^{\prime}
. Let
B
be a tensor on
V
. Then the norms
|||B|||_{G_{E}},|||B|||_{G_{E}^{\prime}}
are equivalent. In fact, if
v=-\langle{\mathbf{T}},{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}\rangle
, and
B
is a
(k,l)
tensor, then

|||B|||_{G_{E}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq(n+1)^{k+l}\left(4v^{2}\right)^{k+l}|||B|||_{G_{E}}^{2}.

Proof.

We sketch the proof. Let
e_{0}={\mathbf{T}},e_{0}^{\prime}={\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
. By choosing an orthonormal basis for the intersection of
e_{0}^{\perp}
and
(e_{0}^{\prime})^{\perp}
, which are the subspaces perpendicular to
e_{0},e_{0}^{\prime}
respectively, we may find
e_{1},e_{1}^{\prime}
and
e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
so that
e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}
and
e_{0}^{\prime},e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}

are orthonormal. It is easy to see that

e_{0}=\cosh\lambda\,e_{0}^{\prime}+\sinh\lambda\,e_{1}^{\prime};\ e_{1}=\sinh\lambda\,e_{0}^{\prime}+\cosh\lambda\,e_{1}^{\prime}

where
v=\cosh\lambda
.
Let
w
be a vector so that

w=\sum_{{\alpha}=0}^{n}a_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}=(a_{0}\cosh\lambda+a_{1}\sinh\lambda)\,e_{0}^{\prime}+(a_{0}\sinh\lambda+a_{1}\cosh\lambda)\,e_{1}^{\prime}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}e_{i}.

Hence

\begin{split}|||w|||^{2}_{G_{E}^{\prime}}=&(a_{0}\cosh\lambda+a_{1}\sinh\lambda)^{2}+(a_{0}\sinh\lambda+a_{1}\cosh\lambda)^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\\
\leq&2(\cosh^{2}\lambda+\sinh^{2}\lambda)\sum_{{\alpha}=0}^{n}a_{\alpha}^{2}\\
\leq&4v^{2}|||w|||^{2}_{G_{E}}.\end{split}

Similarly,
|||w|||^{2}_{G_{E}}\leq 4v^{2}|||w|||^{2}_{G_{E}^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, take a
(0,2)
tensor
B
for example. Let
e_{\alpha}^{\prime}
be orthonormal with
e_{0}^{\prime}={\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
.

\begin{split}|||B|||_{G_{E}^{\prime}}^{2}=&\sum_{{\alpha},{\beta}}B^{2}(e_{\alpha}^{\prime},e_{\beta}^{\prime})\\
\leq&\sum_{{\alpha},{\beta}}|||B|||_{G_{E}}^{2}|||e_{\alpha}^{\prime}|||_{G_{E}}^{2}|||e_{\beta}^{\prime}|||_{G_{E}}^{2}\\
\leq&16v^{4}(n+1)^{2}|||B|||_{G_{E}}^{2}.\end{split}

The general case can be proved similarly.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
|||{\overline{\nabla}}{\mathbf{T}}|||_{G_{E}}\leq c
in

Let

Proof.

In the following, we will denote

.

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

|||{\overline{\nabla}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}{\mathbf{T}}|||\leq|||\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|||\,|||{\overline{\nabla}}{\mathbf{T}}|||\leq c|||\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|||.

Since

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v\right|=\left|\langle{\overline{\nabla}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}{\mathbf{T}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rangle\right|\leq|||{\overline{\nabla}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}{\mathbf{T}}|||\,|||\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|||\leq 2cv^{2}.

So if
|t|\leq\frac{1}{4cv_{0}}

.

∎

Lemma 2.3

Assume

With this setting,
{\bf F}:M\times[0,S_{0}]\to N
,
S_{0}>0
, is a prescribed mean curvature flow starting from
\mathbf{X}_{0}:M\to N
if it satisfies (
1.2
)
such that
{\bf F}(\cdot,s)
is a spacelike immersion, with mean curvature vector
\mathbf{H}={\vec{\nu}}H
where
H
is the mean curvature,
{\vec{\nu}}
is the future timelike unit normal, and
\mathcal{H}
is a function on
N
. The following fact is well-known.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
{\bf F}
be a solution of the prescribed mean curvature flow (
1.2
). Suppose in the setting of Assumption
2.1
there is a smooth family of diffeomorphisms
\phi(p,s)
,
0\leq s\leq S_{0}
of
M
with
\phi(\cdot,0)
being the identity map, and a smooth function
w(p,s)
on
M\times[0,S_{0}]
so that in this setting

{\bf F}(p,s)=\mathbf{X}(w(\phi(p,s),s),\phi(p,s))=(w(\phi(p,s),s),\phi(p,s))

with
|w|<a
, where
a
is as in Assumption
2.1
and
\mathbf{X}
is as in (
2.1
). Then
w
satisfies:

(2.10)

\frac{\partial w}{\partial s}=-\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t},{\vec{\nu}}\rangle^{-1}(H-\mathcal{H})

in
M\times[0,S_{0}]
and
w(p,0)=0
.

Proof.

Since
|w|<a
so that
\mathbf{X}(w(\phi(p,s),s),\phi(p,s))
is well-defined. In local coordinates
x^{i}
of
M
in the domain and local coordinates
y^{i}
of the image of
\phi
,

\begin{split}\partial_{s}{\bf F}=&\frac{\partial y^{j}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}+(\frac{\partial w}{\partial y^{j}}\frac{\partial y^{j}}{\partial s}+w_{s})\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\\
=&\frac{\partial y^{j}}{\partial s}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial y^{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)+w_{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.\end{split}

Here
w_{s}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial s}
as a function of in the form
w(p,s)
. For fixed
s
,
\phi(p,s)
is a diffeomorphism of
M
and so
{\bf F}_{*}({\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}})
is tangential for all
i
. Since

{\bf F}_{*}({\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}})=\frac{\partial y^{k}}{\partial x^{i}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial y^{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right),

and
\mathbf{y}=\phi(\mathbf{x},s)
is a diffeomorphism, we have

\left(\partial_{s}{\bf F}\right)^{\perp}=(w_{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t})^{\perp}

where
\perp
is the projection to the normal of immersion given by
{\bf F}^{s}={\bf F}(\cdot,s)
.

So the first term in the above is tangential. Hence

w_{s}\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t},{\vec{\nu}}\rangle=\langle\partial_{s}{\bf F},{\vec{\nu}}\rangle=-(H-\mathcal{H}).

From this the result follows because
-\langle{\vec{\nu}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rangle\geq 1
.
∎

The following is also well-known.

Lemma 2.9
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, suppose
w
is a smooth function on
M\times[0,S_{0}]
such that
|w|<a
,
w(p,0)=0
, and
w\in C^{2+\sigma,1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(M\times[0,S_{0}])
such that
1-|\nabla w|^{2}\geq\lambda^{-2}
uniformly for some constant
\lambda>0
. Here
\nabla
is the gradient on the slice
\{t\}\times M
. Let
\widetilde{\bf F}(p,s)=\mathbf{X}(p,w(p,s))
. Then the
\widetilde{\bf F}(\cdot,s)
is an immersion for all
s
. Suppose
w
satisfies (
2.10
) with mean curvature vector
\mathbf{H}={\vec{\nu}}H
with
\mathcal{H}
being bounded.
Then one can find a smooth family of diffeomorphisms
\phi(p,s)
,
0\leq s\leq S_{0}
of
M
with
\phi(\cdot,0)
being the identity map such that

{\bf F}(p,s)=\widetilde{\bf F}(\phi(p,s),w(\phi(p,s),s))

is a smooth solution to (
1.2
).

Proof.

Denote
\widetilde{\bf F}(\cdot,s)
by
\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}(\cdot)
. Let
y^{i}
be local coordinates of
M
so that
y^{i},t
are local coordinates of
N=(-a,a)\times M
. We may assume that
y^{i}
are quasi local coordinates. Then
\widetilde{\bf F}
is of the form

\widetilde{\bf F}(\mathbf{y},s)=(\mathbf{y},w(\mathbf{y},s)).

Let

e_{i}=:\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}_{*}(\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}})=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}+w_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
where
w_{i}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial y^{i}}
. So
\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle=g_{ij}(\mathbf{y},t)|_{t=w}-w_{i}w_{j}
. Let
\xi_{i},1\leq i\leq n
be real numbers and let
\xi^{i}=g^{ik}\xi_{k}
. Then

\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle\xi^{i}\xi^{j}=\xi^{i}\xi_{i}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\xi^{i}\right)^{2}.

So

\xi^{i}\xi_{i}\geq\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\geq\xi^{i}\xi_{i}(1-|\nabla w|^{2})\geq\lambda^{-2}\xi^{i}\xi_{i}.

Hence the graph
\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}
is spacelike with induced metric uniformly equivalent to
g(\mathbf{y},t)|_{t=w}
at
\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}(\mathbf{y})
, and hence is equivalent to
g(\mathbf{y},0)
by Lemma
2.3
. From this one can see that there is a smooth family of vector fields

V(\mathbf{y},s)
on
M

s\in[0,S_{0}]
such that
\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}_{*}(V)=\left(\partial_{s}\widetilde{\bf F}\right)^{\top}
which is the tangential component of
\partial_{s}\widetilde{\bf F}
to the graph of
\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}
. To be more precise, let
{\vec{\nu}}
be the future timelike unit normal of the graph of
\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}
,

\partial_{s}\widetilde{\bf F}=w_{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=(w_{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t})^{\top}-w_{s}\langle{\vec{\nu}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rangle{\vec{\nu}}=(w_{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t})^{\top}+(H-\mathcal{H}){\vec{\nu}}

because
w
satisfies (
2.10
). Now
(w_{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t})^{T}(\mathbf{y},s)=v^{i}(\mathbf{y},s)e_{i}
where
e_{i}
are tangent to the graph of
\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}
as before. Then
V=v^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}
. It is easy to see that
V
is smooth in
\mathbf{y},s
. Let
\phi(p,s)
be the integral curve of
-V
so that
\phi(\cdot,0)
is the identity map. We claim that
|V|_{g(0)}
is uniformly bounded. If the claim is true, then
\phi
can be defined on
M\times[0,S_{0}]
. In this case, let

{\bf F}(p,s)=\widetilde{\bf F}(q,w(q,s))|_{q=\phi(p,s)}.

Then
{\bf F}
is smooth,
{\bf F}(p,0)=\mathbf{X}_{0}(p)
because
w(p,0)=0
. Moreover,

\begin{split}\partial_{s}{\bf F}=&\widetilde{\bf F}^{s}_{*}(\partial_{s}\phi(p,s))+(\partial_{s}\widetilde{\bf F})^{\top}+(H-\mathcal{H}){\vec{\nu}}\\
=&(H-\mathcal{H}){\vec{\nu}}.\end{split}

The lemma follows provided we can prove the claim that
||V||_{g(0)}
is bounded. To prove the claim, let
e_{i}
be as before,

\begin{split}\langle(\partial_{s}\widetilde{\bf F})^{\top},e_{i}\rangle=&w_{i}w_{s}\end{split}

which is uniformly bounded because
w\in C^{2+\sigma,1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(M\times[0,S_{0}])
,
H
is given by (
2.3
),
1-|\nabla w|^{2}\geq\lambda^{-2}
, and
\mathcal{H}
is uniformly bounded. Here we have also used Lemma
2.7
. Since the metric
\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle
is uniformly equivalent to
g_{ij}(0)
, this implies the claim is true.

∎

Hence in order to find short time existence of the prescribed mean curvature flow (
1.2
), it is sufficient to find
w
satisfying the conditions in the above lemma on
M\times[0,S_{0}]
for some
S_{0}>0
.
We will use the inverse theorem on
C^{1}
maps on Banach spaces. We proceed as in
[
7
]
(see also
[
6
]
) using the argument outlined in
[
17
]
. Consider the Banach space

C^{2+\sigma,1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}_{0}(M\times[0,S_{0}])=\{f\in C^{2+\sigma,1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(M\times[0,S_{0}])|\ \ f(\cdot,0)=0\}

for some
0<\sigma<1
and
S_{0}
will be chosen later. If there is no confusion, we will drop
M\times[0,S_{0}]
in the definition of Hölder spaces.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the open set in
C_{0}^{2+\sigma,1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(M\times[0,S_{0}])

consisting functions
f
such that

(2.11)

\sup_{M\times[0,S_{0}]}|f|<a\ \mathrm{and}\ \inf_{M\times[0,S_{0}]}(1-|\nabla f|^{2})>\frac{1}{2}

where
|\nabla f|^{2}
is the norm of the gradient with respect to
g(t)
. More precisely in local coordinates
x^{i}
of
M
,

|\nabla f|^{2}(\mathbf{x},s)=f^{i}f_{i}

where
f_{i}=\partial_{i}f=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}f
and
f^{i}(\mathbf{x},s)=g^{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)|_{t=f(\mathbf{x},s)}f_{i}(\mathbf{x},s)
.

We want to define a suitable map
\mathcal{F}
from
\mathcal{B}
to
C^{\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}(M\times[0,S_{0}]
. Let
w\in\mathcal{B}
consider the graph of
w
in
N
which is identified with
(-a,a)\times M
with metric
G=-dt^{2}+g(t)
. The graph of
w(\cdot,s)
is given by
(\mathbf{x},w(\mathbf{x},s))\in(-a,a)\times M
,
\mathbf{x}\in M
. This is well defined because
|w|<a
. The graph is spacelike because
1-|\nabla w|^{2}>\frac{1}{2}
. For fixed
s
, the future pointing unit normal is given by

{\vec{\nu}}(w)=\displaystyle{\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\nabla w}{(1-|\nabla w|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}}.

The mean curvature vector is
\mathbf{H}=H{\vec{\nu}}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a smooth function in
(-a,a)\times M
. Define

(2.12)

\mathcal{F}(w)=\frac{\partial w}{\partial s}+\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t},{\vec{\nu}}(w)\rangle^{-1}(H-\mathcal{H})=\frac{\partial w}{\partial s}-(1-|\nabla w|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(H-\mathcal{H})

for
w\in\mathcal{B}
. In the following, it is more convenient to use the quasi local coordinates to express the map. By (
2.3
), we have

(2.13)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(w)=&\frac{\partial w}{\partial s}-\left(g^{ij}+\frac{w^{i}w^{j}}{1-|\nabla w|^{2}}\right)w_{;ij}-\frac{w_{i}w_{j}}{(1-|\nabla w|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial_{t}g^{ij}\\
&-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\partial_{t}g_{ij}+(1-|\nabla w|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}\\
=&\frac{\partial w}{\partial s}-\left(g^{ij}+\frac{w^{i}w^{j}}{1-|\nabla w|^{2}}\right)\left(w_{ij}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}w_{k}\right)-\frac{w_{i}w_{j}}{(1-|\nabla w|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial_{t}g^{ij}\\
&-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\partial_{t}g_{ij}+(1-|\nabla w|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{H}.\end{split}

For any function
f(t,\mathbf{x})
in the above it is understood the function will be evaluated at
t=w
. Here
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
is the Christoffel symbols of
g
with respect to the quasi local coordinates.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose
|||{\overline{\nabla}}^{k}\mathcal{H}|||_{\Omega}
is uniformly bounded for
k=0,1
, then
\mathcal{F}(w)\in C^{\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}
for
w\in\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Let
\xi_{p}:D(r)\to B_{p}(r)
be quasi local coordinate neighborhood. By Lemma
2.7
all the derivatives of the components of
\xi_{p}^{*}(g(t))
and its inverse with respect to
t
and the quasi local coordinates
x^{i}
are uniformly bounded independent of
p
and for
|t|<a
. Moreover, the
C^{2+\sigma,1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}
norm of
\xi^{*}(w)
in
D(r)\times[0,S_{0}]
is uniformly bounded independent of
p
. In order to prove the lemma, it is sufficient to prove that the
C^{\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}
norm in
D(r)\times[0,S_{0}]
of each term of the last expression in (
2.13
) is uniformly bounded, where
w
is understood to be
\xi^{*}_{p}(w)
and
g
is understood to be
\xi^{*}_{p}(g)
etc. By the assumption on
w
, the
C^{2\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}
norms of
w_{i},w_{ij}
are uniformly bounded. On the other hand, since the first derivative of
g_{ij}
in
x^{i},t
are uniformly bounded and since
w_{i},w_{s}
are uniformly bounded, we conclude that the
C^{\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}
norms of
g_{ij}(w(\mathbf{x},s),\mathbf{x})
are uniformly bounded. Similarly, the
C^{\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}
norms of
g^{ij},\partial_{t}g_{ij},\partial_{t}g^{ij},\Gamma_{ij}^{k},g
are uniformly bounded evaluated at
t=w(\mathbf{x},s)
. By the assumption on
w
and Lemma
2.7
,
w^{i}=g^{ik}w_{k}
has uniformly bounded
C^{\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}
norm.
Since
1-|\nabla w|^{2}>\frac{1}{2}
, one can see that for any
c
, the
C^{2\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}
norm of
(1-|\nabla w|^{2})^{c}=(1-w^{i}w_{i})^{c}
are uniformly bounded. Since
|||{\overline{\nabla}}^{k}\mathcal{H}|||_{\Omega}
is uniformly bounded for
k=0,1
,
\mathcal{H}(w(\mathbf{x},s),\mathbf{x})
is in
C^{\sigma,\frac{\sigma}{2}}
by Lemmas
2.3
and
2.7
. From these, we conclude that the lemma is true.
∎

Next we want to compute the differential of
{\mathcal{F}}
. Let
v(\mathbf{x},s)
be a function on
M\times[0,S_{0}]
with
|v|<a
. For any family of tensors

in
\mathbf{x}
for
(t,\mathbf{x})\in(-a,a)\times M

\,{}^{v}T(\mathbf{x},s)=:T(v(\mathbf{x},s),\mathbf{x}).

For examples:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v^{i}=\,^{v}g^{ij}v_{j}\\
v_{;ij}=v_{ij}-\,^{v}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}v_{k}.\\
\end{array}\right.

We will compute

at

in quasi local coordinates

in
M\times[0,S_{0}]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

In addition to Assumption
2.1
, suppose

are uniformly bounded for all

. Then in the quasi local coordinates, for any

(2.14)

D\mathcal{F}_{v}(\phi)=\phi_{s}-\,^{v}a^{ij}\,\phi_{ij}+\,^{v}b^{k}\,\phi_{k}+\,^{v}c\,\phi

where

(2.15)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\,{}^{v}a^{ij}=&\,{}^{v}g^{ij}+(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{-1}v^{i}v^{j};\\
\,{}^{v}b^{k}=&\displaystyle{-\frac{2(\,^{v}g^{ki}v^{j}(1-|\nabla v|^{2})+v^{i}v^{j}v^{k})(v_{ij}-\,^{v}\Gamma_{ij}^{m}v_{m})}{(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{2}}+\left(\,{}^{v}g^{ij}+\frac{v^{i}v^{j}}{1-|\nabla v|^{2}}\right)\,^{v}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}}\\
&2\displaystyle{-\bigg[\frac{\,{}^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}v^{k}+(1-|\nabla v|^{2})\,^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ik}v_{i}+v^{k}((1-|\nabla v|^{2})\,^{v}\mathcal{H}}{(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigg]}\\
\,{}^{v}c=&\displaystyle{-\bigg[\ ^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ij}+\frac{2\ ^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ik}v_{k}}{1-|\nabla v|^{2}}+\frac{v^{i}v^{j}\ {}^{v}\partial_{t}g^{lm}v_{l}v_{m}}{(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{2}}\bigg](v_{ij}-\,^{v}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}v_{k})}\\
&\displaystyle{+\left(\,{}^{v}g^{ij}+\frac{v^{i}v^{j}}{1-|\nabla v|^{2}}\right)\ ^{v}\partial_{t}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}v_{k}}\displaystyle{-\bigg[\frac{\ {}^{v}\partial_{t}g^{lm}v_{l}v_{m}\,^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ij}}{(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{\,{}^{v}\partial_{t}^{2}g^{ij}}{(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg]v_{i}v_{j}}\\
&\displaystyle{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\,{}^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ij}\,{}^{v}\partial_{t}g_{ij}+\,^{v}g^{ij}\,{}^{v}\partial_{t}^{2}g_{ij}\right)-\frac{\ {}^{v}\partial_{t}g^{lm}v_{l}v_{m}\,^{v}\mathcal{H}}{(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{}^{v}\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}}\\

{\mathcal{F}}
is
C^{1}
in
\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Fix
v\in\mathcal{B}
. Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{2+{\alpha},1+\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}
. Here and below the Höder spaces are defined on
M\times[0,S_{0}]
. Then for
|\epsilon|
small,

\mathcal{F}(v+\epsilon\phi)

|\epsilon|

v_{\epsilon}=:v+\epsilon\phi\in\mathcal{B}
.
Then at
\epsilon=0

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}(v+\epsilon\phi)_{s}=\phi_{s}}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\left(\,{}^{v_{\epsilon}}g^{ij}\right)=\phi\ ^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ij}}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\left(\,{}^{v_{\epsilon}}\partial_{t}g^{ij}\right)=\phi\ ^{v}\partial_{t}^{2}g^{ij}}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}v_{\epsilon}^{i}=\phi\ ^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ik}v_{k}+\phi_{k}\ ^{v}g^{ki}}\\

In the following

means
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\epsilon}|_{\epsilon=0}

\begin{split}&\left[\left(g^{ij}+\frac{v^{i}v^{j}}{1-|\nabla v|^{2}}\right)\left(v_{ij}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}v_{k}\right)\right]^{\prime}\\

\begin{split}\left(\frac{1}{(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial_{t}g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}\right)^{\prime}=&\frac{\phi\ ^{v}\partial_{t}g^{lm}v_{l}v_{m}+2\phi_{k}v^{k}}{(1-|\nabla v|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,^{v}\partial_{t}g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}\\

Let
D{\mathcal{F}}_{v}(\phi)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}{\mathcal{F}}(v+\epsilon\phi)|_{\epsilon=0}
. Then

D{\mathcal{F}}_{v}(\phi)=\phi_{s}-\,^{v}a^{ij}\phi_{ij}+\,^{v}b^{k}\phi_{k}+\,^{v}c\phi,

where

and
\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}

D(r)
. From this

\,{}^{v}a^{ij},\,^{v}b^{k},\,^{v}c

is continuous in

. Here we have used the fact that
(1-|\nabla w|^{2})>\frac{1}{2}
for all

We claim that
D{\mathcal{F}}_{v}
is the differential of
{\mathcal{F}}
at
v
. Let
v\in\mathcal{B}
be fixed and

Appendix A
Hölder spaces

We begin by explicitly defining the local Hölder norms used in the definition parabolic Hölder spaces. Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
k\geq 0
be an integer and
0<\sigma<1
, for
s_{0}>0
, the
C^{2k+\sigma,k+\frac{\sigma}{2}}
norm on
\Omega_{s_{0}}=\Omega\times[0,s_{0}]
for functions
f(\mathbf{x},s)
is defined as:

\begin{split}||f||_{2k+\sigma,k+\frac{\sigma}{2};\Omega_{s_{0}}}=&\sum_{|{\alpha}|+2r=0}^{2k}\sup_{\Omega}\left|\partial^{r}_{s}\partial^{\alpha}f\right|\\
&+\sum_{|{\alpha}|+2r=2k}\sup_{(\mathbf{x},s)\neq(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\Omega_{s_{0}}}\frac{\left|\partial^{r}_{s}\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x},s)-\partial^{r}_{s}\partial^{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},s^{\prime})\right|}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|^{\sigma}+|s-s^{\prime}|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}\end{split}

where

\partial^{r}_{s}\partial^{\alpha}u=\frac{\partial^{r+|{\alpha}|}u}{\partial s^{r}\partial x^{\alpha}}

and
{\alpha}
is a multi-index. If there is no confusion on the domain of definition of
f
, we will simply write the norm as
||f||_{2k+\sigma,k+\frac{\sigma}{2}}
.

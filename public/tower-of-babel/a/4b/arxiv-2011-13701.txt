Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to give a novel generalization of the Leibnitz numbers derived from application of the Beta function to the modification for the Bernstein basis functions. We also give some properties of the Leibnitz numbers with the aid of their generating functions derived from the Volkenborn integral on the set of p p -adic integers. We also give some novel identities and relations involving the Leibnitz numbers, the Daehee numbers, the Changhee numbers, inverse binomial coefficients, and combinatorial sums. Finally, by coding computation formula for the generalization of the Leibnitz numbers in Mathematica 12.0 with their implementation, we compute few values of these numbers with their tables. Finally, by using the applications of Volkenborn integral to Mahler coefficients, we derive some novel formulas involving the Leibnitz numbers. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 05A15, 11B65, 11B83, 11S80, 26C05, 33B15. Keywords and phrases. Leibnitz numbers, Daehee numbers, Stirling numbers, Special numbers and polynomials, Generating functions, Beta function, Bernstein basis functions, Volkenborn integral, p p -adic integers.

It is known by many researchers, who work on the subject of special numbers and their applications in recent years, that this subject has became among the leading topics of mathematics and especially analytic number theory. The
Leibnitz numbers
, known as the famous German mathematician Gottfried Wilhelm Leibnitz (1646 - 1716), are considered in this paper. These numbers, which have rarely been addressed until now, are studied by using the techniques of generating functions and their Volkenborn integral representation in this paper. These numbers are also closely related to the

(2)

(3)

\mathcal{G}_{l}\left(t,u\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}(t)u^{n}.

Generalized Leibnitz numbers

In order to achieve the results of this section, we let

(26)

f\left(x\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\binom{x}{n}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{K)},

where
\binom{x}{n}=\frac{x_{(n)}}{n!}
denotes the Mahler coefficients. Applying
the Volkenborn integral to the function
f\left(x\right)
in terms of the
Mahler coefficients
\binom{x}{n}
, we have the following well-known formula:

(27)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(x\right)d\mu_{1}\left(x\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}a_{n},

(
cf
.
[
21
, p. 168-Proposition 55.3]
).

In order to give generating function (
2
), we apply the Volkenborn
integral to the following uniformly differential function on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
:

f\left(x,u;t\right)=\left(1-u\right)^{x}\left(1-tu\right)^{x},

where
u
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Substituting the above function into the following well-known integral
equation:

\int\limits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f(x+1)d\mu_{1}\left(x\right)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f(x)d\mu_{1}\left(x\right)+f^{\prime}(k)

(
cf
.
[
21
]
), we get

(28)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(1-u\right)^{x}\left(1-tu\right)^{x}d\mu_{1}\left(x\right)=\frac{\log\left[\left(1-u\right)\left(1-tu\right)\right]}{\left(1-u\right)\left(1-tu\right)-1}.

Consequently, the function on the right of equation (
28
) gives the
generating function given in equation (
2
) for the Leibnitz numbers.

Combining (
28
) with the binomial series

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{(n)}\frac{u^{n}}{n!}=\left(1+u\right)^{x},

and using (
27
), we obtain

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{u^{n}}{n!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}t^{n-k}\int\limits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}x_{(k)}x_{(n-k)}d\mu_{1}\left(x\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\boldsymbol{l}\left(n,k\right)t^{k}u^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients of
u^{n}
on both sides of the above equation
yields the following theorem:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then we have

(29)

\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}t^{n-k}\int\limits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}x_{(k)}x_{(n-k)}d\mu_{1}\left(x\right)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\boldsymbol{l}\left(n,k\right)t^{k}.

Combining (
29
) with the following formula:

\int\limits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}x_{(n)}x_{(m)}d\mu_{1}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+n-k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{k!(n+m-k)!}{n+m-k+1}

where
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(
cf
.
[
35
]
), we arrive at the
following theorem:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then we have

(30)

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\boldsymbol{l}\left(n,k\right)t^{k}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\left(-1\right)^{j}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-k}{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{j!\left(n-j\right)!}{n-j+1}t^{n-k}.

Substituting
t=1
into (
30
), we get the following corollary:

Corollary 5.3
.

(31)

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\boldsymbol{l}\left(n,k\right)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\left(-1\right)^{j}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-k}{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{j!\left(n-j\right)!}{n-j+1}.

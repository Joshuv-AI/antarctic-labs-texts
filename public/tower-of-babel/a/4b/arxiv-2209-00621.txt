Combinatorial decomposition theorem for Hitchin systems via zonotopes
Mirko Mauri, Luca Migliorini and Roberto Pagaria

Abstract
. We determine the summands of the decomposition theorem for the Hitchin system for GL n \operatorname{GL}_{n} , in arbitrary degree, over the locus of reduced spectral curves. The key ingredient is an equivariant formula for lattice point counts in graphical zonotopes.



1.
Introduction

Intersection cohomology is a topological invariant well-adapted to investigate the symmetries of singular spaces. Nowadays, it is an established tool in Hodge theory, but also in representation theory and combinatorics. For instance, the symmetries of the
h
-polynomial of a polytope can be explained in terms of dualities of the intersection cohomology of the associated toric variety; see
[
16
,
6
]
. Via the geometric Satake correspondence, the irreducible representations of a reductive group
G
can be realized as the intersection cohomology of affine Schubert varieties for the Langlands dual group
{{}^{L}}G
; see
[
18
,
31
]
.
Another example is the calculation of the Frobenius character of the Orlik–Terao algebra by using the intersection cohomology ring of an hypertoric variety; see
[
7
,
33
,
37
]
.
Viceversa, the intersection cohomology of toric varieties is controlled by the combinatorics of their moment polytopes, as one may expect given the combinatorial nature of these varieties; see again
[
16
]
.

It may sound less obvious that the intersection cohomology of moduli spaces with no a priori combinatorial origin, like spaces of Higgs bundles, admits a combinatorial characterization. In this paper, we show how to obtain cohomological information for these spaces via lattice point count in rational polytopes called zonotopes.

1.1.
Decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration

We consider the
Dolbeault moduli space

M(n,d)
parametrising semistable Higgs bundles
(\mathcal{E},\phi)
on a compact Riemann surface
C
with
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})=n
, and
\deg(\mathcal{E})=d
; see §
7
. One can argue that the entire study of nonabelian Hodge theory may be thought of as the study of the
geometry of
M(n,d)
. Dolbeault moduli spaces play a central role in various other fields of mathematics
like mirror symmetry, Teichmüller theory, knot theory and in the geometric Langlands program.

A key feature of the geometry of
M(n,d)
is that it is endowed with a projective fibration called
Hitchin fibration

\chi(n,d)\colon{M}(n,d)\to A_{n}\coloneqq\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}^{\otimes i})

which assigns to
(\mathcal{E},\phi)
the characteristic polynomial
\mathrm{char}(\phi)
of the Higgs field
\phi
; see again §
7
. Set
A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}\subseteq A_{n}
the locus of reduced characteristic polynomial, and
M(n,d)^{\mathrm{red}}\coloneqq\chi^{-1}(n,d)(A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n})
.

The intersection cohomology of
{M}(n,d)
, denoted
I\!H^{*}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})
, is the global cohomology of the intersection complex
\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})
on
{M}(n,d)
, or of
the direct image complex
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})
on the base
A_{n}
. The advantage of the second complex is that we may decompose it in smaller complexes called
Ngô strings
. On the reduced locus
A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}
, the Ngô strings are indexed by a subset of partitions
\underline{n}
of
n
, and they are denoted
\mathscr{S}(\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d))
. Any string
\mathscr{S}(\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d))
is a combination of the cohomology of an abelian group scheme independent of
d
, and a local system
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
depending on
d
; see §
9
.

These local systems
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
should be considered as the combinatorial part of the intersection cohomology of
{M}(n,d)
: a partition
\underline{n}
corresponds to a choice of a zonotope in
\mathbb{R}^{|\underline{n}|}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{|\underline{n}|}\otimes\mathbb{R}
, and the degree
d
determines a translation of the zonotope. The local system
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
is a subrepresentation of a group of automorphisms of the zonotope on the lattice points contained in its translate.

The determination of these representations is a subtle task, and involves a delicate combination of combinatorics and algebraic geometry that we carried out in this paper. In §
1.4
and
[
28
, §1]
we hint at the geometric significance of the result. Here we summarize the main output, and discuss it in detail in the next section.

Theorem 1.1

(Effective decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration)
.

For any partition
\underline{n}=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\}
of
n
, let
S_{\underline{n}}\subseteq A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}
be the closure of the locus the characteristic polynomials
\mathrm{char}(\phi)
whose irreducible factors have degree
n_{i}
, and
g_{\underline{n}}:\mathscr{A}^{\times}_{\underline{n}}\to S^{\times}_{\underline{n}}
be the relative Jacobian of the simultaneous normalization of the spectral curves of equation
\mathrm{char}(\phi)=0
, with
\mathrm{char}(\phi)
in a dense open set
S^{\times}_{\underline{n}}\subseteq S^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}=S_{\underline{n}}\setminus\bigcup_{\underline{m}\leq\underline{n}}S_{\underline{m}}
. Denote by
\operatorname{Stab}(\underline{n})
the subgroup of the symmetric group on
r
elements stabilizing
\underline{n}
.

Then there is an isomorphism in the bounded derived category of algebraic mixed Hodge modules
D^{b}MHM_{\text{alg}}(A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n})
, or in the bounded derived category
D^{b}(A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n})
of
\mathbb{Q}
-constructible
complexes on
A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}
(ignoring the
Tate shifts):

(1)

R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M(n,d)},\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}\simeq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{n}\vdash\,n\\
d\text{-integral}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{IC}(S_{\underline{n}},Rg_{\underline{n},*}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathscr{A}^{\times}_{\underline{n}}}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d))[-2\mathrm{codim}S_{\underline{n}}](-\mathrm{codim}S_{\underline{n}}),

where
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
is a polarizable variation of pure Hodge structures of weight zero and of Hodge-Tate type on
S^{\times}_{\underline{n}}
.

The monodromy of
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
actually factors through a representation of the stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}(\underline{n})
, i.e. for any
a\in S^{\times}_{\underline{n}}
we have

\pi_{1}(S^{\times}_{\underline{n}})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(S^{\circ}_{\underline{n}})\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Stab}(\underline{n})\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Aut}(\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)_{a});

see
9.3
. The
\operatorname{Stab}(\underline{n})
-representation admits a clear combinatorial characterization as described in
1.6
, and its rank is computed in
1.8
.

1.2.
Supports and combinatorial local systems of the Hitchin fibration

In order to explain and prove
1.1
, we first introduce the notion of
d
-integral partition.

Definition 1.2
.

A partition
\underline{n}=\{n_{i}\}
of
n=\sum_{i}n_{i}
is
d
-integral
if
dn_{i}/n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 1.3
.

If
\gcd(n,e)=1
, the only
e
-integral partition of
n
is the trivial one, i.e.
\{n\}
. On the contrary, any partitions of
n
is
0
-integral.

Such partitions arise naturally while studying the singularities of
M(n,d)^{\mathrm{red}}
. Indeed, a singular point of
M(n,d)^{\mathrm{red}}
corresponds to a strictly polystable Higgs bundle
(E_{1},\phi_{1})\oplus\ldots\oplus(E_{r},\phi_{r})
with
r>1
, where
(\mathcal{E}_{i},\phi_{i})
are distinct stable Higgs bundles of slope
\deg E_{i}/\operatorname{rank}E_{i}=d/n
. In fact,
\{\operatorname{rank}E_{i}\}
is a
d
-integral partition of
n
. Let
M^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}(d)
be the locus of polystable Higgs bundles of multirank
\underline{n}
. Then the space
M(n,d)^{\mathrm{red}}
admits a Whitney stratification by partition type

M(n,d)^{\mathrm{red}}=\bigsqcup_{\underline{n}}M^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}(d),

where the sum runs over the
d
-integral partitions of
n
; see also
[
28
, §1.1]
.

For any partition
\underline{n}=\{n_{i}\}
of
n
, let
S_{\underline{n}}\subseteq A_{n}
be the closure of the polynomials whose irreducible factors have degree
n_{i}
; see also §
9
. In
[
28
, Cor. 3.8]
, the first and second authors showed that the supports of the Ngô strings of
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}
ranges among the subvarieties
S_{\underline{n}}
; see
9.1
. Here we determine which of them actually supports a non-zero Ngô string.

Theorem 1.4

(Supports in arbitrary degree)
.

The supports of
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}
are all and only the subvarieties
S_{\underline{n}}
such that
\underline{n}
is not a
d
-integral partition of
n
.

Proof.

It follows from
1.6
,
3.11
, and
3.12
.
∎

Note that the closure of the images of
M^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}(d)
are all and only the subvarieties
S_{\underline{n}}
such that
\underline{n}
is indeed
d
-integral. Therefore, we have the following dichotomy: either the subvariety
S_{\underline{n}}
has a Hodge-theoretic interpretation as summand of
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})
, or it admits a description in terms of the singularities of
M(n,d)
.

Therefore,
1.3
implies that we have two extreme cases: the coprime and the zero case. If
\gcd(n,e)=1
,
M(n,e)
is smooth and
R\chi(n,e)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,e),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}
has the maximal number of supports as the degree varies; see also
[
28
, §1.1]
. On the contrary, when
d=0
,
M(n,0)
is singular with the maximal number of strata in the stratification by partition type, but no proper summand of
R\chi(n,0)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,0),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}
. We could informally say that, despite their different origin, there exists a
conservation law
between summands of the decomposition theorem and singular strata as the degree
d
varies. In particular, we obtain an alternative proof of
[
28
, Thm 1.4]
, which is actually a key step in the proof of
1.4
and
1.8
.

Theorem 1.5

(Full support in degree zero)
.

The complex
R\chi(n,0)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,0),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}
has full support, i.e.

R\chi(n,0)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,0),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}\simeq\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{Q}_{A_{n}})|_{A_{n}^{\mathrm{red}}}.

If
\gcd(n,e)=1
, the rank of
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(e)
has been interpreted as the cohomology of a cographic matroid in
[
12
, Thm 6.11]
, and as the top cohomology of a symplectic resolution of a normal slice of
M^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}(0)
in
[
28
, Thm 1.1]
. Here we propose a new combinatorial description that holds in arbitrary degree. The monodromy of the local system
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
is a representation of
\operatorname{Stab}(\underline{n})
, i.e. the subgroup of the symmetric group on
\ell(\underline{n})=r
elements stabilizing
\underline{n}
. If
\underline{n}=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\}\vdash n
, set

(2)

\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)\coloneqq\bigg(d\frac{n_{1}}{n},\ldots,d\frac{n_{r}}{n}\bigg)\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}.

Then the poset of non-
\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)
-integral partition of the set
\underline{n}
, denoted
\overline{\Pi}_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)}
(cf
2.4
and
2.6
), admits an action of
\operatorname{Stab}(\underline{n})
.

Theorem 1.6

(Combinatorial characterization of
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
)
.

Let
\underline{n}
be a non-trivial partition of
n
.
The local system
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
can be identified as
\operatorname{Stab}(\underline{n})
-representation with the top reduced homology of the order complex of the poset
\overline{\Pi}_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)}
, twisted by the sign representation

\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)\simeq\sgn\otimes\widetilde{H}_{\ell(\underline{n})-3}(\Delta(\overline{\Pi}_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)})).

In particular, the rank of
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
is the number of spheres of the order complex
\Delta(\overline{\Pi}_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)})
.

Note that the order complex
\Delta(\overline{\Pi}_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)})
is homotopic equivalent to a wedge of spheres. This follows from the LEX-shellability of the poset
\hat{\Pi}_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)}
; see
3.2
,
3.6
and
3.9
.

Theorem 1.7

(
3.6
; LEX-shellability)
.

The poset
\hat{\Pi}_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)}
is LEX-shellable.

If
\gcd(n,e)=1
, the poset
\Pi_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(e)}
is simply the poset of all partitions of
[r]
. Such poset appears in
[
12
, Lemma 6.18]
as the lattice of flats of a cographic matroid, and it was indeed used to compute the cohomology of the matroid. Note however that in arbitrary degree the poset
\Pi_{\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)}
may fail to be semimodular, so in general there is no associated matroid.

The Hodge-to-singular correspondence in
[
28
]
provides a recursive formula to determine the rank of
\mathcal{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
as explained in
[
28
, §10.1]
.
Due to the new characterization

, we can now provide a closed formula.

Theorem 1.8

(
3.11
; Formula for
\operatorname{rk}\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
)
.

The rank of the local system
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
is

(3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda=\{\lambda_{i}\}\vdash[r]\\
\lambda\;\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)\textnormal{-integral}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell(\lambda)-1}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}(\lvert\lambda_{i}\rvert-1)!,

where the sum runs over the
\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)
-integral partitions of the set
\underline{n}
; see
2.4
.

By direct inspection, we show that the sum in (

\omega
is an integral vector. Otherwise, we prove that

contains at least a sphere, which by

. These facts together gives

1.3.
Strategy

The key input for the proof of the previous statements is that any local system
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
arises from a graphical zonotope.

According to the decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration (
9.1
), the local system
\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d)
are direct summands of
R^{2c(n,g)}\chi(n,d)_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{{M}(n,d)}
. The stalk of
R^{2c(n,g)}\chi(n,d)_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{{M}(n,d)}
at the point
a\in A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}
has a basis indexed by the irreducible components of
\chi(n,d)^{-1}(a)
. Via the BNR-correspondence,
\chi(n,d)^{-1}(a)
is a compactified Jacobian parametrising semistable rank 1 torsion free sheaves on the spectral curve
C_{a}
; see §
7
. In particular, the irreducible components of the compactified Jacobian of a reduced spectral curve are indexed by the degrees of the restrictions of stable line
bundles to the irreducible components of
C_{a}
; see
7.6
. The stability condition imposes numerical inequalities on the multidegree of a line bundle on
C_{a}
, which are defining equations for the graphical zonotope
Z_{\Gamma}+\omega
for a suitable graph
\Gamma
and translation vector
\omega
; see
7.8
.

\chi(n,0)
A_{n}

\chi(n,1)
A_{n}

Figure 1
.
The number of irreducible components of the Hitchin fibration
\chi(n,d)\colon{M}(n,d)\to A_{n}
is the number of lattice points in the interior of a graphical zonotope translated by a vector depending on the degree. The picture does not correspond to any particular Dolbeault moduli space: the fibers are usually higher dimensional, and the components much more numerous - we shave them off for illustrative purposes.

A crucial ingredient is the following combinatorial version of the decomposition theorem.

Theorem 1.9

(
6.4
; Combinatorial decomposition theorem)
.

For any graph
\Gamma
and for any
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
-invariant vector
\omega\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}
,
\mathcal{C}(Z_{\Gamma}+\omega)
is the rational vector space with basis the set of integral points of the interior of the zonotope
Z_{\Gamma}+\omega
. The following
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
-representations are isomorphic

(4)

\mathcal{C}(Z_{\Gamma}+\omega)=\mathcal{C}(Z_{\Gamma})\oplus\bigoplus_{\underline{S}\in\mathcal{L}_{\omega}/\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}\Ind_{\operatorname{Stab}(\underline{S})}^{\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}\big(\alpha_{\underline{S}}\otimes\widetilde{H}_{\ell(\underline{S})-3}(\Delta(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega,\geq\underline{S}}))\otimes\mathcal{C}(Z_{\Gamma_{\underline{S}}})\big).

We refer to §
6
for details about the notation. Here we emphasis the formal analogy between (
1
) and (
4
). Indeed, in the same way as
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}
is a direct sum of Ngô strings
\mathscr{S}(\mathscr{L}_{\underline{n}}(d))
indexed by
d
-integral partitions
\underline{n}
of
n
, the number of lattice points in a translated zonotope is the sum of the lattice points contained in the faces of some non-translated faces of the zonotopes corresponding to
d
-integral partitions too.

1.4.
Other work and broad context

1.1
is a refinement of the celebrated Ngô support theorem
[
34
, §7]
. Let
A^{\mathrm{ell}}_{n}
be the locus of irreducible characteristic polynomials. In our context, Ngô support theorem asserts that
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{ell}}_{n}}
has full support, and it is independent of the degree
d
. In hindsight, we can now say that
A^{\mathrm{ell}}_{n}
is the bigger open set of
A_{n}
avoiding proper summands of
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})
as the degree
d
varies. If
\gcd(n,d)=1
,
1.1
is then the main result of
[
12
]
. It is not clear however how the explicit computation of the Cattani–Kaplan–Schmidt complex in
[
12
, §5]
can be adapted in the singular setting, so here we pursue a different strategy.

As pointed out in
[
28
, §1.2]
,
1.1
implies that
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})|_{A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}}
depends only on the
\gcd(n,d)
, and so
I\!H^{*}(M(n,d)^{\mathrm{red}},\mathbb{Q})
does. The independence of
H^{*}(M(n,e),\mathbb{Q})
for
\gcd(n,e)=1
has already been proved using vanishing cycle techniques in
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
or positive characteristic methods in
[
13
, Theorem 0.1]
. It is worth remarking that the authors of
[
13
]
actually prove the stronger statement that the cohomology ring itself is independent of the degree.

In the singular case, the dependence of
I\!H^{*}(M(n,d),\mathbb{Q})
on the degree has been investigated in
[
28
, Corollary 1.9]
, and settled unconditionally in
[
10
, Corollary 14.9]
, by studying the geometry and representation theory of local singularities of
{M}(n,d)
. The viewpoint of our paper is instead global and combinatorial. Observe also that
[
10
]
does not prescribe the shape of the summands of
R\chi(n,d)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,d),\mathbb{Q})
. After
1.1
, the main open conjecture is whether there are summands supported on the complement of
A^{\mathrm{red}}_{n}
. As a corollary of
[
10
]
, it suffices to check it in degree zero, and this is indeed equivalent to the following generalization of
1.5
.

Conjecture 1.10

(Full support conjecture)
.

The complex
R\chi(n,0)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,0),\mathbb{Q})
has full support on the whole basis of the Hitchin fibration.

In other words, we ask whether the complex
R\chi(n,0)_{*}\operatorname{IC}({M}(n,0),\mathbb{Q})
is determined by its restriction to any open subsets of
A_{n}
, in particular the locus over which the Hitchin fibration is smooth. A positive answer to the full support conjecture would imply that
I\!H^{*}({M}(n,0),\mathbb{Q})
should be regarded as the primitive indecomposable building block of the cohomology of any other Dolbeault moduli space
{M}(n,d)
.

The key geometric fact used in this paper is the characterization of the fibres of the Hitchin fibration as compactified Jacobian. In fact, the same proof of
1.1
works if we replace the Hitchin system with other families of compactifid Jacobian, e.g. the Mukai systems on moduli space
of sheaves on K3 or abelian surfaces of Picard rank one; cf
[
28
, §10.3]
. In particular, we obtain the following equivariant description of the irreducible components of compactified Jacobians, which is a result of independent interest, and generalizes a classical result by Raynaud
[
40
, Prop. 8.1.2]
and Melo–Rapagnetta–Viviani
[
29
, Cor. 5.14]
for fine compactified Jacobian. We refer to §
7
for details on the terminology.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
X=\bigcup_{i\in I}X_{i}
be a nodal curve with smooth irreducible components and dual graph
\Gamma(X)
, and
X^{\prime}\to X
be a partial normalization of
X
. Note that the restriction
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\operatorname{Pic}(X^{\prime})
is always surjective, and the kernel is an algebraic torus of dimension
H^{1}(\Gamma(X),\Gamma(X^{\prime}),\mathbb{Z})
. We say that
X
has no tails if the dual graph
\Gamma(X)
does not collapse onto any proper subgraph, or equivalently
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\not\simeq\operatorname{Pic}(X^{\prime})
for any partial normalization
X^{\prime}
of
X
.

(Irreducible components of compactified Jacobians)

Let

, i.e.

As

Outline

The paper is divided into two parts: a combinatorial half (from §
3
to §
6
) and an algebro-geometric half (from §
7
to §
10
).

•

In §
2
we fix the notation of partitions, flats and posets used all over the text.

•

In §
3
we prove the LEX-shellability of some posets of flats, see
3.6
, and compute the Betti numbers of the order complex
\Delta(\overline{\Pi}_{\omega})
; see
3.11
. This are combinatorial results of independent interest.

•

In §
4
we recall a formula due to Ardila, Beck and McWhirter to compute the number of lattice points in the interior of a translated zonotope; see
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
. We apply Möbius inversion to obtain a numerical version of the combinatorial decomposition theorem, namely
4.5
.

•

In §
5
we specialize the previous count to graphical zonotopes of a complete graph with multiple edges, and realize that the same combinatorial pattern govern both the coefficients of the lattice point count formula and the numbers of spheres of the order complex
\Delta(\overline{\Pi}_{\omega})
; see
5.3
.

•

In §
6
we promote the previous numerical identities to an isomorphism of representations. This is the most technical and delicate part of the paper. We achieve this by comparing characters, which in turns boils down to compute lattice points of fixed loci of automorphisms in graphical zonotope. In general, the fixed loci are no longer graphical zonotopes, and this is why in §
4
we work with arbitrary zonotopes.

•

In §
7
we establish the relation between irreducible components of the fibers of the Hitchin fibrations and lattice point count in graphical zonotopes as described in the Strategy
1.3
.

•

After an analysis of the stalks of Ngô strings in §
9
, we can finally prove
1.5
and
1.6
in §
10
.

2.1.
Partitions of a set or of an integer

Definition 2.1
.

Let
I
be a finite set. A
partition
of
I
of length
\ell(\underline{S})=l
is a set
\underline{S}\coloneqq\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{l}\}
of
l
disjoint subsets
S_{i}\subseteq I
such that
I=\bigsqcup^{l}_{i=1}S_{i}
.

Let
\underline{S}^{\prime}
be another partition of
I
. We say that
\underline{S}\leq\underline{S}^{\prime}
if any
S^{\prime}_{j}
is the union of some
S_{i}
.

The
poset of partitions
of
I
is denoted
(\Pi(I),<)
, or simply by
\Pi
. The minimum
\hat{0}
of
\Pi
is the collection of the singletons of
I
, while the maximum
\hat{1}
is the trivial partition
\{I\}
consisting of the single block
I
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. A
partition

\underline{n}
of
n
of length
\ell(\underline{n})=r
is a set
\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\}
of positive integers such that
\sum^{r}_{i=1}n_{i}=n
.

Let
\underline{m}
be another partition of
n
of length
s
. We say that
\underline{n}\geq\underline{m}
, if there exists a partition
\underline{S}
of length
r
of the set
\underline{m}
such that

\underline{n}=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\},\qquad\underline{m}=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{s}\},\qquad n_{i}=\sum_{j\in S_{i}}m_{j}.

In particular, denote
\underline{n}_{i}
the partition
\{m_{j}:j\in S_{i}\}
of
n_{i}
.

Flats of a graph

Let

.

A

2.3.
Posets

Definition 2.6
.

A poset is
bounded
if it contains a least and greatest element
\hat{0}
and
\hat{1}
. For a bounded poset
\mathcal{P}
we denote by
\overline{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{P}\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
and

Definition 2.7
.

Given a poset
(\mathcal{P},<)
and
x,y\in\mathcal{P}
, the symbol

denotes a
covering relation
and it means that
x<y
and there is no

such that

Recall that the

\mu
of a finite poset
\mathcal{P}
assigns an integer to each interval

such that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{P}
we have

\sum_{x\leq z\leq y}\mu(z,y)=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if }x=y,\\

3.
Shellability of certain posets of partitions

3.1.
LEX-shellability

Let
\omega=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The main result of this section is the LEX-shellability of the poset
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}
of non-
\omega
-integral partitions; see
2.4
. The notion of
LEX-shellability was first introduced in
[
23
]
; see also
[
24
, §12.2.3]
.
For the convenience of the reader, we recall here the definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a graded poset, and
E(\mathcal{P})=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{P}|\,x\lessdot y\}
be the set of covering relations of
\mathcal{P}
.

An
edge labelling
of
\mathcal{P}
is a function
l\colon E(\mathcal{P})\to\Lambda
, where
\Lambda
is a poset. For any maximal chain
c=(x=x_{0}\lessdot x_{1}\lessdot\ldots\lessdot x_{k-1}\lessdot x_{k}=y)
we consider the associated
k
-uple
l(c)\coloneqq(l(x_{0}\lessdot x_{1}),l(x_{1}\lessdot x_{2}),\ldots,l(x_{k-1}\lessdot x_{k}))\in\Lambda^{k}
.
A chain
c
is less than a chain
d
if
l(c)
lexicographically precedes
l(d)
.

Definition 3.2
.

An edge labelling is a
LEX-labelling
if the following condition holds: for any interval
[x,t]
, any maximal chain
c
in
[x,t]
, and any
y,z\in c
, with
x<y<z<t
, if
c_{|[x,z]}
and
c_{|[y,t]}
are lexicographically least in
[x,z]
and
[y,t]
respectively, then
c
is lexicographical least in
[x,t]
.

A poset that admits a LEX-labelling is
LEX-shellable
.

In
3.4
we define an edge labelling of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}(\Gamma)
, and then prove that it is indeed a LEX-labelling.

.

If the graph
\Gamma

\omega

Proof.

\Gamma

is

\omega

∎

Let

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with
r
vertices, and
\underline{S}<\underline{U}
be two flats.
Then
E(T({\underline{U}}))\subset E(T({\underline{S}}))
. Moreover, if
a\in E(T({\underline{S}}))
then
E(T({\underline{S}\vee a}))=E(T({\underline{S}}))\setminus\{a\}
and

\underline{F}({\underline{S}\vee a})=\underline{F}({\underline{S}})\vee a
.

Proof.

The image of
E(T({\underline{S}}))
under the contraction
\Gamma^{\underline{S}}\to\Gamma^{\underline{U}}
is a spanning subgraph. In particular, the minimal spanning forest of
\Gamma^{\underline{U}}
can be identified with a subset of

E(T({\underline{S}\vee a}))=E(T({\underline{S}}))\setminus\{a\}
holds.
Notice that
\mathcal{L}(T({\underline{S}\vee a}))=\mathcal{L}(T({\underline{S}}))_{\geq a}

\underline{F}({\underline{S}\vee a})\geq\underline{F}({\underline{S}})\vee a

\underline{F}({\underline{S}\vee a})\leq\underline{F}({\underline{S}})\vee a

Theorem 3.6
.

The labelling
l
is a LEX-labelling for
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}
. In particular,
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}
is LEX-shellable.

Proof.

By
[
24
, Proposition 12.13]
, it is enough to check the LEX-labelling condition for any
x\lessdot y\lessdot z<t
.
Let
c
be a maximal chain in
[x,t]
such that
c_{|[x,z]}
and
c_{|[y,t]}
are lexicographically least.
Replacing
\Gamma
with
\Gamma_{t}^{x}
we may assume
t=\hat{1}
and
x=\hat{0}
.

Let
a=l(x\lessdot y)
and
b=l(y\lessdot z)
, so that
l(c)=(a,b,\dots)
.
Since
c_{|[y,t]}
is lexicographically least then
b\in E(T(y))\subset E(T(x))
.
We have
x\lessdot(x\vee b)\lessdot z
and since
c_{|[x,z]}
is lexicographically least then
a\prec_{x}l(x\lessdot x\vee b)\preceq_{x}b
. In particular
a\in E(T(x))
.

By contradiction, we assume that the chain
c
is not lexicographically least in
[x,t]
.
Hence there exists
d\prec_{x}a
and
d\in E(T(x))
.
Notice that
\{a,b,d\}
is an independent set of distinct edges in
\Gamma^{x}
because contained in
T(x)
, i.e. the poset
[x,x\vee a\vee b\vee d]
is Boolean of rank
3
.

Note that
x\vee a\vee d
cannot be
\omega
-integral. Otherwise,
\underline{F}(x)\leq x\vee a\vee d
, and
d\leq\underline{F}(x)
since
y=x\vee a
is not
\omega
-integral.
The conditions
d\in E(T(x))_{\leq\underline{F}}
,
b\in E(T(x))
and
d\prec_{x}b
would imply
b\in E(T(x))_{\geq\underline{F}}
, so
x\vee b\leq\underline{F}(x)\leq x\vee a\vee d
, which is a contradiction since
[x,x\vee a\vee b\vee d]
is Boolean.

Therefore,
y\vee d=x\vee a\vee d\in\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
and we are assuming that it is different from
z=x\vee a\vee b
.
Since
c_{|[y,t]}
is lexicographically least, we have
b\prec_{y}l(y\lessdot y\vee d)\preceq_{y}d
. Moreover,
b\leq\underline{F}(y)=\underline{F}({x\vee a})=\underline{F}(x)\vee a
if and only if
b\leq\underline{F}(x)
, and the same holds for
d
.
This fact, together the inequalities
d\prec_{x}b
and
b\prec_{y}d
, gives a contradiction.
∎

3.2.
Homotopy type of the order complex of non-
\omega
-integral partitions

Recall that the
order complex

\Delta(\mathcal{P})
associated to a poset
\mathcal{P}
is a simplicial complex whose vertices are in correspondence with the set underlying
\mathcal{P}
, and whose faces corresponds to finite chains in
\mathcal{P}
.

Recall that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}={\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
. We denote by
\widetilde{H}_{k}(T)
the reduced
k
-homology group of the topological space
T
and by
\widetilde{\chi}(T)
the reduced Euler characteristic.

Definition 3.8
.

Given an edge labelling of a poset, a saturated
chain
c
is
mediocre
if for any
x,y,z\in c
such that
x\lessdot y\lessdot z
, the chain

c|_{[x,z]}
is not lexicographically least in
[x,z]
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
r>2
. The order complex
\Delta(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})
has the homotopy type of the wedge of
(r-3)
-spheres.
The number of mediocre maximal chains in
\Pi_{\omega}
equals the number of
(r-3)
-spheres in
\Delta(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})
, i.e. the rank of the reduced homology group
\widetilde{H}_{r-3}(\Delta(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}))
.

Proof.

It follows from
3.6
and the general properties of LEX-shellability
[
24
, Theorem 12.15]
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

If
r=2
,
\Delta(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega})
is the empty set, but since
\tilde{H}_{-1}(\emptyset)=\mathbb{Z}
we still have
-\mu(\hat{0},\hat{1})=1=\operatorname{rk}\tilde{H}_{-1}(\emptyset)
.

.

The following equalities hold for

:

\operatorname{rk}\widetilde{H}_{r-3}(\Delta(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}))=|\widetilde{\chi}(\Delta(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\omega}))|=(-1)^{r-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\omega}}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}(\hat{0},\lambda)

Moreover, if

\lambda\;\omega\textnormal{-integral}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\lvert\lambda\rvert-1}\prod_{i=1}^{\lvert\lambda\rvert}(\lvert\lambda_{i}\rvert-1)!.

Proof.

The first equality follows from

and
3.10
.
For the second equality,

4.
Lattice points in zonotopes

In this section, we recall the definition of zonotope, and we provide the key formula for the number of lattice points in the interior of its translates; see
4.5
.

Recall that the Minkowski sum of several subsets
A,\ldots,B\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
is the locus of sums of vectors that belong to these subsets

A+\ldots+B:=\{a+\ldots+b\,|\,a\in A,\ldots,b\in B\}.

If
A,\ldots,B
are convex and/or lattice polytopes, then so is their Minkowski sum.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
U
be a finite set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{r}\setminus\{0\}
. The
zonotope
generated by
U
is the Minkowski sum of line segments
[0,u]
with
u\in U
, i.e.

Z(U)\coloneqq\sum_{u\in U}[0,u].

The zonotope
Z(U)
decomposes in the disjoint union of translates of
the half-open parallelepipeds

\sum_{u\in W}[0,u)

spanned by the linearly independent subsets
W\subseteq U
; see
[
43
, Theorem 54]
. This observation allows to compute the number of lattice points in the interior of a translate of
Z(U)
in
4.4
, as proved in
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
.

To state the result, we first adapt the definitions of §
2.2
to the present context, and recall the definition of Ehrhart quasi-polynomial.

Definition 4.2
.

A
flat
of
U
of rank
k
is a maximal subset of
U
spanning a vector subspace of rank
k
.
The poset of flats of
U
is denoted
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(U)
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}
be the standard lattice, and
\omega
be a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Then a flat
S\subseteq U
is
\omega
-integral
if the the affine subspace obtained by translating the vector space spanned by
S
by the translation vector
\omega
contains lattice points, i.e.
(\omega+\langle S\rangle)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}\neq\emptyset
. The poset of
\omega
-integral flats is denoted
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}=\mathcal{L}_{\omega}(U)
.

Given a set
W
of independent vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}
, the
relative volume
of the parallelepiped generated by
W
, denoted
\rvol(W)
, is the volume of the parallelepiped in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
normalized by the volume of a basis of the lattice
\langle W\rangle\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, where
\langle W\rangle
is the linear span of
W
in
\mathbb{Q}^{r}
. Alternatively, it is the index of the sublattice generated by
W
in
\langle W\rangle\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.

Definition 4.3
.

The
Ehrhart quasi-polynomial

L_{P}(t)\coloneqq\left|tP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}\right|

counts the number of lattice points in the dilated polytope
tP
.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
1
, Proposition 3.1]

Let
U\subset\mathbb{Z}^{r}
be a finite set of integer vectors,
\omega\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}
a rational vector, and
Z(U)
be the zonotope generated by
U
.
Then the Ehrhart quasi-polynomial of
Z(U)+\omega
is

L_{Z(U)+\omega}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\subseteq U\\
W\textnormal{ independent}\end{subarray}}\delta_{W,\omega}(t)\rvol(W)t^{\lvert W\rvert},

where

\delta_{W,\omega}(t)=\begin{cases}1&\textnormal{if }(t\omega+\langle W\rangle)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}\neq\emptyset,\\
0&\textnormal{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Given any polytope
P
we denote by
\mathrm{Int}(P)
the interior of
P
in its linear span. The number of lattice points contained in the interior of the polytope
P
is denoted

\displaystyle C(P)\coloneqq\left|\mathrm{Int}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}\right|.

By Ehrhart-Macdonald reciprocity
[
25
]
,
C(P)
is a value of the Ehrhart quasi-polynomial

L_{P}(t)
up to sign, namely

C(P)=(-1)^{\dim(P)}L_{P}(-1).

If
P
is a translated zonotope
Z(U)+\omega
, then Möbius inversion applied to
4.4
gives the combinatorial identity (
5
) and (
9
).

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{Z}^{r}
be a finite set of integer vectors,
\omega\in\mathbb{Q}^{r}
a rational vector, and
Z(U)
be the zonotope generated by
U
. Then we have

(5)

C(Z(U)+\omega)=\sum_{{S}\in\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{{T}\in\mathcal{L}_{\geq{S}}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\omega}}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}({S},{T})\right)C(Z({S})).

Proof.

From
4.4
we write

L_{Z(U)+\omega}(t)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}}\delta_{S,\omega}(t)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\subseteq S\textnormal{ independent}\\
\langle W\rangle=\langle S\rangle\end{subarray}}\rvol(W)\right)t^{\operatorname{rk}(S)}

where

\delta_{{S},\omega}(t)=\begin{cases}1&\textnormal{if }(t\omega+\langle S\rangle)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{r}\neq\emptyset,\\
0&\textnormal{otherwise,}\end{cases}

because the function
\delta_{W,\omega}(t)
does not depend on
W
but only on the flat
\langle W\rangle=\langle S\rangle
.

Let us define

Q_{{S}}(t)\coloneqq t^{\operatorname{rk}(S)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\subseteq S\textnormal{ independent}\\
\langle W\rangle=\langle S\rangle\end{subarray}}\rvol(W)

and notice that it is independent on
\omega
.
For any flat
{T}\in\mathcal{L}
we write

L_{Z(T)+\omega}(t)=\sum_{{S}\leq{T}}\delta_{{S},\omega}(t)Q_{{S}}(t).

We specialize to the case
\omega=0
so that
\delta_{{S},0}=1
, and we apply the Möbius inversion formula to obtain

Q_{{T}}(t)=\sum_{{S}\leq{T}}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}({S},{T})L_{Z(S)}(t).

Therefore, for any
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
we have

\displaystyle L_{Z(U)+\omega}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{T}\in\mathcal{L}}\delta_{{T},\omega}(t)\left(\sum_{{S}\leq{T}}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}({S},{T})L_{Z(S)}(t)\right)=\sum_{{S}\in\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{{T}\geq{S}}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}({S},{T})\delta_{{T},\omega}(t)\right)L_{Z(S)}(t).

Specializing at
t=-1
we have
\delta_{{T},\omega}(-1)=1
if and only if
{T}
is
\omega
-integral, and we obtain

\displaystyle C(Z(U)+\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{S}\in\mathcal{L}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{T}\geq{S}\\
{T}\,\omega\textnormal{-integral}\end{subarray}}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}({S},{T})\right)C(Z(S)).

This completes the proof.
∎

The subvariety parametrising
\underline{q}^{a}
-stable line bundles on the spectral curve
C_{a}
is dense in
\chi(n,d)^{-1}(a)
;

(2)

The irreducible components of
\chi(n,d)^{-1}(a)
are indexed by the degrees of the restrictions of the
\underline{q}^{a}
-stable line bundles to the irreducible components of
C_{a}
.

(3)

The number of irreducible components of
\chi(n,d)^{-1}(a)
is constant for
a\in S^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}
.

Proof.

The statement (
1
) holds for reduced curves with respect to a general polarization by
[
29
, Cor. 2.20]
. We show (
1
) for the (not necessarily general) canonical polarization
\underline{q}^{a}
, via a small perturbation of the polarization
\underline{q}^{a}
to a general one, denoted
\underline{q}^{\prime}
. Indeed,
\underline{q}^{\prime}
-stable sheaves are
\underline{q}^{a}
-semistable, and by the formation of GIT quotients there exists a specialization map

\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{\prime}}(C_{a})\to\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})

which is an isomorphism over the locus of
\underline{q}^{a}
-stable sheaves. Since
\underline{q}^{\prime}
is general, the locus of
\underline{q}^{\prime}
-stable line bundles is open and dense in
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{\prime}}(C_{a})
by
[
29
, Cor. 2.20]
. It is then sufficient to prove that the
\underline{q}^{a}
-stable locus is dense in
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})
. To this end, note that its complement
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})^{\mathrm{sing}}
is the intersection of
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})\simeq\chi^{-1}(n,d)(a)
with the singular locus of
M(n,d)
. Any irreducible component of
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})
is Lagrangian in
M(n,d)
and so of dimension
\dim M(n,d)/2
. On the other hand,
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})^{\mathrm{sing}}
is a Lagrangian subvariety of the singular locus of
M(n,d)
by
[
27
, Thm 3.1]
and
[
26
]
, hence

\dim\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})^{\mathrm{sing}}=\dim\mathrm{Sing}(M(n,d))/2<\dim M(n,d)/2.

We obtain that
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})^{\mathrm{sing}}
does not contain any irreducible component of
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})
, which concludes the proof of (
1
).

Now let
\mathcal{L}
be a
\underline{q}^{a}
-stable line bundle on
C_{a}
, and
J(C_{a},\deg_{\bullet}(\mathcal{L}))
be the Jacobian of line bundles
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
on
C_{a}
with
\deg_{Y}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\deg_{Y}(\mathcal{L})
for all
Y\subseteq C_{a}
, which is an irreducible variety. Since any line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
deformation equivalent to
\mathcal{L}
is
\underline{q}^{a}
-stable, we have
J(C_{a},\deg_{\bullet}(\mathcal{L}))\subset\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})
. Because the two spaces have the same dimension, the closure of
J(C_{a},\deg_{\bullet}(\mathcal{L}))
is an irreducible component of
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})
. This implies (
2
).

Statement (
3
) now follows from (
2
) and the fact that the stability of a line bundle is determined by numerical inequalities involving the degree of the irreducible components of
C_{a}
, and the sheaf of these irreducible components is locally constant on
S^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}
.
∎

Theorem 7.8

(Hitchin system and zonotopes)
.

Let

. The following
\operatorname{Aut}(\underline{n})
-representations are isomorphic

\displaystyle(R^{2c(n,g)}\chi(n,d)_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{{M}(n,d)})_{a}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{C}(Z_{\Gamma[\underline{n}]}+\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)).

In particular, the number of irreducible components of the fibre
\chi(n,d)^{-1}(a)
equals the number of lattice points contained in the zonotope
Z_{\Gamma[{\underline{n}}]}+\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)
, in symbols

\displaystyle\dim(R^{2c(n,g)}\chi(n,d)_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{{M}(n,d)})_{a}

\displaystyle=C(Z_{\Gamma[\underline{n}]}+\omega_{\underline{n}}(d)).

Proof.

By
7.6
we can suppose that the spectral curve
C_{a}=\bigcup_{i\in I}C_{i}
is nodal with smooth irreducible components. Denote by
C_{K}=\bigcup_{i\in K}C_{i}\subseteq C_{a}
the subcurve corresponding to
K\subseteq I
. Pick a
\underline{q}^{a}
-stable line bundle
\mathcal{I}
in
\overline{J}_{\underline{q}^{a}}(C_{a})
, and let
\mathcal{I}_{K}
be its restriction to
C_{K}
. By pushing forward from the normalization of
C_{a}
, we have the standard short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{I}_{K}\to\bigoplus_{k\in K}\mathcal{I}_{k}\to\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}J=\{i,j\}\subseteq K,\\
|J|=2\end{subarray}}\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}\cap C_{j}}\to 0.

The stability condition reads

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\chi(\mathcal{I}_{i})-\sum_{J=\{i,j\}\subseteq I,|J|=2}|C_{i}\cap C_{j}|=\chi(\mathcal{I})=d+n(1-g),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in K}\chi(\mathcal{I}_{i})-\sum_{J=\{i,j\}\subseteq K,|J|=2}|C_{i}\cap C_{j}|=\chi(\mathcal{I}_{K})>[d+n(1-g)]\sum_{k\in K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}.

For any pair
J=\{i,j\}\subseteq I
, the number of edges of
\Gamma(C_{a})
between the vertices corresponding to
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
, denoted

, equals
|C_{i}\cap C_{j}|
. Set
x_{i}\coloneqq\chi(\mathcal{I}_{i})-(1-g)n_{i}
, and
z_{K}=\sum_{J\subseteq K}y_{J}
. We can rewrite the previous system of equality and inequalities as

\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}=z_{I}+d,\quad\sum_{i\in K}x_{i}>z_{K}+\sum_{k\in K}\frac{dn_{k}}{n}\quad\forall K\subset I.

As a result, the possible multidegrees of
\mathcal{I}
are in correspondence with the lattice points of the zonotope
Z_{\Gamma(C_{a})}+\omega
by (
6
), and the bijection is
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma[{\underline{n}}])
-equivariant. ∎

9.
Ngô strings for the Hitchin system

In this section we recall how the cohomology of the Dolbeault moduli space

decomposes in Ngô strings, and we carefully study the stalk of these strings at the general point of their support.

For any partition
\underline{n}
of
n
of length
r
, we define
S_{\underline{n}}
as the image of the multiplication map

(24)

\mathrm{mult}_{\underline{n}}:A_{\underline{n}}\coloneqq\prod^{r}_{i=1}A_{n_{i}}\to A_{n}

given by
\mathrm{mult}_{\underline{n}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})=\prod^{n}_{i=1}a_{i}
. There exists a partition
\underline{S}(\underline{n})=\{S(\underline{n})_{k}\}^{s}_{k=1}
of
[r]
, maximal with respect to the order

, with the property that there exists
i(k)\in S(\underline{n})_{k}
such that

for all

. In other words, we group together the

which are equal. Then the multiplication

factors as follows

A_{\underline{n}}\xrightarrow{\eta_{\underline{n}}}A_{\underline{S}(\underline{n})}\coloneqq\prod^{t}_{k=1}\mathrm{Sym}^{|S(\underline{n})_{k}|}A_{n_{i(k)}}\xrightarrow{\nu_{\underline{n}}}S_{\underline{n}}.

The map

is the normalization of

, since

is a finite map and

is generically 1:1 with normal domain. In particular, the composition is étale over

with Galois group the automorphism
\mathrm{Stab}(\underline{n})\coloneqq\prod^{n}_{i=1}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha_{i}}
of the partition

stabilizing
\underline{n}
, or equivalently the automorphism group

of the general spectral curve in

.

We define also the following spaces:

(i)

is the locus parametrising reducible nodal curves having smooth irreducible components of degree

over

;

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

Theorem 9.1

(Ngô strings from Hitchin system)
.

[
28
, Thm 3.6, Thm 3.11]

Lemma 9.4

Proposition 9.5

1.
Introduction

In 1992 Móricz and Siddiqi investigated the rate of the approximation by Nörlund means of Walsh-Fourier series
[
16
]
. For Nörlund means
with monotone weights they gave a sufficient condition which provide Nörlund
means for having convergence in
L_{p}
norm (
1\leq p<\infty
) and
C_{W}

norm.

The result of
[
16
]
was extended by Fridli, Manchanda and
Siddiqi
[
3
]
for dyadic martingale Hardy spaces and dyadic
homogeneous Banach spaces . Recently, the theorem of Móricz and Siddiqi was
generalized for
\Theta
-means of Walsh-Fourier series in
L_{p}
spaces (
1\leq p<\infty
) and
C_{W}

[
1
]
.

The theorems mentioned above are related to the approximation of the Nörlund
means which in turn is related to the uniformly boundedness of the
corresponding operators of the Nörlund means. To study of almost everywhere
convergence of Nörlund means is connected to the study of the boundedness of
the maximum operators corresponding to the Nörlund means.

The first result with respect to the a.e. convergence of the Walsh-Fejér
means is due to Fine
[
2
]
. Later, Schipp
[
18
]
showed that the maximal operator of the Walsh-Fejér
means is of weak type (1, 1), from which the a. e. convergence follows by
standard argument
[
15
]
. Schipp result implies
by interpolation also the boundedness of
\sup\limits_{n}\left|\sigma_{n}^{1}\right|:L_{p}\rightarrow L_{p}\,\left(1<p\leq\infty\right)
. This fails to hold for
p=1
but Fujii
[
4
]
proved that
\sup\limits_{n}\left|\sigma_{n}^{1}\right|
is bounded from the
dyadic Hardy space
H_{1}(\mathbb{I})
to the space
L_{1}(\mathbb{I})
(see
also Simon
[
20
]
). Fujii’s theorem was extended by Weisz
[
25
]
. In particular, Weisz
[
25
]
proved that
the maximum operator is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to
the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{I}),
when
p>1/2
. The essence of the condition
p>1/2
was proved by the author
[
9
]
. If the
\{q_{k}\}
is an
non-decreasing sequence then it can be proved that the following inequality
occurs (see Persson, Tephnadze, Wall
[
17
]
),

(1)

\sup\limits_{n}\left|t_{n}\right|\leq c\sup\limits_{n}\left|\sigma_{n}\right|,

where by
t_{n}
is denoted Nörlund means of Walsh-Fourier series. From (
1
) it follows that the maximum operator
\sup\limits_{n}\left|t_{n}\right|
is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to
the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{I}),
when
p>1/2
.

The situation is different when the sequence
\{q_{k}\}
is decreasing. Let
us cite the following two cases:

•

say
q_{k}=A_{k}^{\alpha-1},\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
, where

A_{0}^{\alpha}=1,\,\,A_{n}^{\alpha}=\frac{\left(\alpha+1\right)\cdots\left(\alpha+n\right)}{n!}.

then it is easy to see that
\{q_{k}\}
is decreasing and at the same time
the operator
\sup\limits_{n}\left|\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}\right|\,\,\,\,\left(0<\alpha<1\right)
is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{I}),
when
p>1/\left(1+\alpha\right)
(see Weisz
[
26
]
);

•

Assume that
q_{k}=1/k
. Then the sequence is decreasing, but the
maximum operator is not bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to
the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{I})
by any
p\in(0,1]
(see
[
11
]
).

Therefore, Nörlund means with non-increasing weights can be divided into two
groups:

•

Nörlund means with non-increasing weights, whose corresponding maximum
operator is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{I})
for some
p\in(0,1]
;

•

Nörlund means with non-increasing weights that are not bounded from
the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{I})
by any

p\in(0,1]
.

The presented paper will be proved the necessary and sufficient conditions
in order maximal operator of Nörlund means with non-increasing weights to be
bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{I})
. It also follows from the established theorem that the
boundedness of maximal operator of Nörlund means with non-increasing weights
from the Hardy space
H_{1}(\mathbb{I})
to the space
L_{1}(\mathbb{I})

is equivalent to the type
\left(\infty,\infty\right)
.

2.
Walsh Functions

We denote the set of non-negative integers by
\mathbb{N}
. By a dyadic
interval in
\mathbb{I}:=[0,1)
we mean one of the form
I\left(l,k\right):=\left[\frac{l-1}{2^{k}},\frac{l}{2^{k}}\right)
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<l\leq 2^{k}
. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in[0,1),
let
I_{k}(x)
denote the dyadic interval of length
2^{-k}
which contains the
point
x
. We also use the notation
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right)\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right),\overline{I}_{k}\left(x\right):=\mathbb{I}\backslash I_{k}\left(x\right)
. Let

x=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}2^{-\left(n+1\right)}

be the dyadic expansion of
x\in\mathbb{I}
, where
x_{n}=0
or
1
and if
x
is a dyadic rational number we choose the expansion which terminates in
0^{\prime}
s.

For any given
n\in\mathbb{N}
it is possible to write
n
uniquely as

n=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}\left(n\right)2^{k},

where
\varepsilon_{k}\left(n\right)=0
or
1
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
This expression will be called the binary expansion of
n
and the numbers
\varepsilon_{k}\left(n\right)
will be called the binary coefficients of
n
. Let us introduce for
1\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
the notation
\left|n\right|:=\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}\mathbf{:}\varepsilon_{j}\left(n\right)\neq 0\}
, that is
2^{\left|n\right|}\leq n<2^{\left|n\right|+1}.

Let us set the
n
th
\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
Walsh-Paley function
at point
x\in\mathbb{I}
as:

w_{n}\left(x\right)=\left(-1\right)^{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{j}\left(n\right)x_{j}}.

Let us denote the logical addition on
\mathbb{I}
by
\dotplus
. That is,
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{I}

x\dotplus y:=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|x_{n}-y_{n}\right|2^{-\left(n+1\right)}.

The
n
th Walsh-Dirichlet kernel is defined by

D_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}w_{k}\left(x\right).

Recall that
[
14
,
19
]

(2)

D_{2^{n}}\left(x\right)=2^{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}\left(x\right),

where
\mathbf{1}_{E}
is the characteristic function of the set
E
.

As usual, denote by
L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
the set of measurable
functions defined on
\mathbb{I}
, for which

\left\|f\right\|_{1}:=\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}\left|f\left(t\right)\right|dt<\infty\text{.}

Let
f\in L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
. The partial sums of the
Walsh-Fourier series are defined as follows:

S_{M}\left(f;x\right):=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{M-1}\widehat{f}\left(i\right)w_{i}\left(x\right),

where the number

\widehat{f}\left(i\right)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}f\left(t\right)w_{i}\left(t\right)dt

is said to be the
i
th Walsh-Fourier coefficient of the function
f.
Let us set
E_{n}\left(f;x\right)=S_{2^{n}}\left(f;x\right)
. The
maximal function is defined by

E^{\ast}\left(f;x\right)=\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n}\left(f;x\right).

3.
Walsh-Nörlund means

Let us set
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers. We
define the
n
th Nörlund mean of the Walsh-Fourier series by

(3)

t_{n}(f;x):=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{n-k}S_{k}(f;x),

where
Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{k}

(n\geq 1).
It is always assumed that
q_{0}>0
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q_{n}=\infty
. In this case, the
summability method generated by the sequence
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
is regular
(see
[
16
]
) if and only if

(4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=0.

The Nörlund kernels are defined by

{F}_{n}(t):=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{n-k}D_{k}(t).

The Fejér means and kernels are

\sigma_{n}(f,x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}(f,x),\quad K_{n}(t):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}D_{k}(t).

It is easily seen that the means
t_{n}(f)
and
\sigma_{n}(f)
can be got
by convolution of
f
with the kernels
F_{n}
and
K_{n}
. That is,

\displaystyle t_{n}(f,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}f(x\dotplus t){\ F}_{n}(t)dt=\left(f\ast F_{n}\right)\left(x\right),\quad

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(f,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}f(x\dotplus t)K_{n}(t)dt=\left(f\ast K_{n}\right)\left(x\right).

It is well-known that
L_{1}
norm of Fejér kernels are uniformly bounded,
that is

(5)

\|K_{n}\|_{1}\leq c\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{.}

Yano estimated the value of
c
and he have
c=2

[
27
]
. Recently,
in paper (see
[
23
]
) it was shown that the exact value of
c
is
\frac{17}{15}
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
e_{j}:=2^{-j-1}
. Then

(6)

K_{2^{n}}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(2^{-n}D_{2^{n}}\left(x\right)+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}2^{j-n}D_{2^{n}}\left(x\dotplus e_{j}\right)\right).

The proof can be found in

.

.

Let
n=2^{n_{1}}+2^{n_{2}}+\cdots+2^{n_{r}}
with
n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}\geq 0
. Let us set
n^{(0)}:=n
and
n^{(i)}:=n^{(i-1)}-2^{n_{i}}
(
i=1.\ldots,r-1
),
n^{(r)}:=0
. Then the

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:F_{n,1}+F_{n,2}.

Theorem GN2
.

Let
\{q_{k}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be a sequence of
non-negative numbers. If the sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is
monotone non-increasing (in sign
q_{k}\downarrow
). Then

(8)

\|F_{n}\|_{1}\sim\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{|n|}|\varepsilon_{k}(n)-\varepsilon_{k+1}(n)|Q_{2^{k}}.

The proof of Theorems GN1 and GN2 can be found in
[

]
.

Using Abel’s transformation we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n_{j}}-1}q_{k+n^{(j)}}D_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n_{j}}-2}\left(q_{k+n^{(j)}}-q_{k+n^{(j)}+1}\right)kK_{k}

\displaystyle+q_{n^{(j-1)}-1}{(2^{n_{j}}-1})K_{2^{n_{j}}-1}.

Thus, we get

(9)

\displaystyle F_{n,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}}{Q_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n_{j}}-2}w_{n^{(j-1)}}w_{2^{n_{j}}-1}\left(q_{k+n^{(j)}}-q_{k+n^{(j)}+1}\right)kK_{k}

\displaystyle+\frac{w_{n}}{Q_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_{n^{(j-1)}}w_{2^{n_{j}}-1}q_{n^{(j-1)}-1}{(2^{n_{j}}-1})K_{2^{n_{j}}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:F_{n,2}^{\left(1\right)}+F_{n,2}^{\left(2\right)}.

Lemma 1
.

Let
p\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right]
. Then

\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}\sup\limits_{1\leq n\leq 2^{N}}\left(n\left|K_{n}\right|\right)^{p}\leq c_{p}2^{N\left(2p-1\right)}.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Let
p=1
. Since (see
[
19
]
)

(10)

n\left|K_{n}\left(x\right)\right|\leq c\sum\limits_{s=0}^{|n|}2^{s}K_{2^{s}}\left(x\right)

from (
5
) we have

\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}\sup\limits_{1\leq n\leq 2^{N}}\left(n\left|K_{n}\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx\leq c\sum\limits_{s=0}^{N}2^{s}\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}K_{2^{s}}\left(x\right)dx\leq c2^{N}.

Let
1/2<p<1
. Applying the inequality

\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\right)^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{p}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left(a_{k}\geq 0,\,\,\,\,0<p\leq 1\right)

and (see
[

]

\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}\left(2^{s}K_{2^{s}}\left(x\right)\right)^{p}dx\leq c_{p}2^{s\left(2p-1\right)},1/2<p<1

we get

\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}\sup\limits_{1\leq n\leq 2^{N}}\left(n\left|K_{n}\left(x\right)\right|\right)^{p}dx\leq c_{p}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{N}\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}\left(2^{s}K_{2^{s}}\left(x\right)\right)^{p}dx\leq c_{p}2^{N\left(2p-1\right)}.

1
is proved.

5.
Dyadic Hardy Spaces

The norm (or quasinorm) of the space
L_{p}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
is
defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}:=\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\,\,\,\,\left(0<p<+\infty\right).

In case
p=\infty
, by
L_{p}(\mathbb{I})
we mean
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{I})
,
endoved with the supremum norm.

The space weak-
L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
consists of all measurable
functions
f
for which

\left\|f\right\|_{\text{weak}-L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)}:=\sup\limits_{\lambda>0}\lambda\left|\left(\left|f\right|>\lambda\right)\right|<+\infty.

Let
f\in L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
. For
0<p<\infty
the Hardy
space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
consists all functions for which

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}:=\left\|E^{\ast}\left(f\right)\right\|_{p}<\infty.

A bounded measurable function
a
is a p-atom, if there exists a
dyadic interval
I
, such that

a)
\int\limits_{I}a=0
;

b)
\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left|I\right|^{-1/p}
;

c) supp
a\subset I
.

An operator
T
be called p-quasi-local if there exist a constant
c_{p}>0

such that for every p-atom
a

\int\limits_{\mathbb{I}\backslash I}|Ta|^{p}\leq c_{p}<\infty,

where
I
is the support of the atom. We shall need the following

Theorem W1
.

Suppose that the operator
T
is
\sigma
-sublinear and
p
-quasi-local for each
0<p\leq 1
. If
T
is bounded from
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{I})
to
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{I})
, then

\left\|Tf\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{p}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(f\in H_{p}\left(\mathbb{I}\right))

for every
0<p<\infty

.\,
In particular for
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{I})
, it
holds

\left\|Tf\right\|_{weak\_L_{1}(\mathbb{I})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{1}.

Theorem W2
.

If a sublinear operator is bounded from
H_{p_{0}}(\mathbb{I})
to
L_{p_{0}(\mathbb{I})}
and from
L_{p_{1}}(\mathbb{I})
to
L_{p_{1}}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)\left(p_{0}\leq 1<p_{1}\leq\infty\right)
then it is also bounded from
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to
L_{p}(\mathbb{I})
if
p_{0}<p<p_{1}
.

The proofs of Theorems W1 and W2 can be found in
[
24
]
.

6.
Maximal Operators of Walsh-Nörlund means

The goal of this paragraph is to investigate the boundedness of the maximal
operators of the Walsh-Nörlund means on the dyadic Hardy spaces. More
precisely, to find the necessary and sufficient conditions for the maximal
operator of the Walsh-Nörlund means to be bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{I})
to the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{I})
for fixed
p\in(0,1]
.

Let us first prove that if the condition

(11)

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{|n|}|\varepsilon_{k}(n)-\varepsilon_{k+1}(n)|Q_{2^{k}}=\infty

is fulfilled, then the boundedness of the maximum operator from the Hardy
space
H_{1}(\mathbb{I})
to the space
L_{1}(\mathbb{I})
does not occur.
Moreover, we prove that the following is valid

Theorem 1
.

Let
\left\{m_{A}:A\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
be a subsequence
for which the condition

\sup\limits_{A\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{Q_{m_{A}}}\sum_{k=1}^{|m_{A}|}|\varepsilon_{k}(m_{A})-\varepsilon_{k+1}(m_{A})|Q_{2^{k}}=\infty

holds.  The operator
t_{m_{A}}\left(f\right)
is not uniformly
bounded from the dyadic Hardy spaces
H_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
to the
space
L_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Set

f_{A}:=D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|+1}}-D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}.

Then it is easy to see that

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|S_{2^{n}}\left(f_{A}\right)\right|=D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}

and consequently,

\left\|f_{A}\right\|_{H_{1}}=\left\|\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|S_{2^{n}}\left(f_{A}\right)\right|\right\|_{1}=\left\|D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}\right\|_{1}=1.

Set

m_{A}=2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}+q_{A},

where

q_{A}:=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\left|m_{A}\right|-1}\varepsilon_{j}\left(m_{A}\right)2^{j}.

Then we can write

t_{m_{A}}\left(f_{A}\right)=\frac{1}{Q_{m_{A}}}\sum\limits_{k=2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}+1}^{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}+q_{A}-1}q_{m_{A}-k}S_{k}\left(f_{A}\right).

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle S_{k}\left(f_{A}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{k}\left(D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|+1}}-D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|+1}}\left(D_{k}\right)-S_{k}\left(D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{k}-D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}},2^{|m_{A}|}<k\leq m_{A}\text{.}

Hence, we have

\displaystyle t_{m_{A}}\left(f_{A}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{m_{A}}}\sum\limits_{k=2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}+1}^{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}+q_{A}}q_{m_{A}-k}\left(D_{k}-D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{m_{A}}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{q_{A}}q_{q_{A}-k}\left(D_{k+2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}-D_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{2^{\left|m_{A}\right|}}}{Q_{m_{A}}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{q_{A}}q_{q_{A}-k}D_{k}.

From the condition of Theorem
1
and by (
8
) we
conclude that

\sup\limits_{A\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|t_{m_{A}}\left(f_{A}\right)\right\|_{1}=\sup\limits_{A\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|F_{q_{A}}\right\|_{1}=\infty.

Theorem
1
is proved.
∎

Now, we prove that the maximal operator of Walsh-Nörlund means with
non-increasing weights can not be bounded from the Hardy space
H_{1/2}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
to the space
L_{1/2}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
. Based on the interpolation Theorem W2, the maximum operator of
Walsh-Nörlund means with non-increasing weights can not be bounded from the
Hardy space
H_{p}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
to the space
L_{p}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
when
p<1/2
(see Persson, Tephnadze, Wall
[
17
]
).

Theorem 2
.

The maximal operator of Walsh-Nörlund means with
non-increasing weights can not be bounded from the Hardy space
H_{1/2}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
to the space
L_{1/2}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Set

f_{n}:=D_{2^{n+1}}-D_{2^{n}}.

Then it is easy to see that

\sup\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\left|S_{2^{m}}\left(f_{n}\right)\right|=D_{2^{n}}

and consequently,

(12)

\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{H_{p}}=\left\|\sup\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\left|S_{2^{m}}\left(f_{n}\right)\right|\right\|_{p}=\left\|D_{2^{n}}\right\|_{p}=2^{n\left(1-1/p\right)}.

Let
s<n
. Then we can write

\displaystyle t_{2^{n}+2^{s}}\left(f_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{2^{n}+2^{s}}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2^{n}+2^{s}}q_{2^{n}+2^{s}-j}S_{j}\left(f_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{2^{n}+2^{s}}}\sum\limits_{j=2^{n}+1}^{2^{n}+2^{s}}q_{2^{n}+2^{s}-j}S_{j}\left(D_{2^{n+1}}-D_{2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{2^{n}+2^{s}}}\sum\limits_{j=2^{n}+1}^{2^{n}+2^{s}}q_{2^{n}+2^{s}-j}\left(S_{2^{n+1}}\left(D_{j}\right)-S_{j}\left(D_{2^{n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{2^{n}+2^{s}}}\sum\limits_{j=2^{n}+1}^{2^{n}+2^{s}}q_{2^{n}+2^{s}-j}\left(D_{j}-D_{2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{N\left(1-p\right)}}{Q_{2^{N}}^{p}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}2^{j\left(p\left(\alpha+1\right)-1\right)}

we conclude that

\sup\limits_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{2^{N\left(1-p\right)}}{Q_{2^{N}}^{p}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}Q_{2^{j}}^{p}2^{j\left(p-1\right)}<\infty\iff\left(p>\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\right).

Consequently, we have the following two conditions are equivalent:

The maximal operator

is bounded from the dyadic Hardy

H_{p}\left(\mathbb{I}\right)
to the space

p>\frac{1}{1+\alpha}.

Polynomial complex Ginzburg-Landau equations in almost periodic spaces
Agustín Besteiro

Abstract
We consider complex Ginzburg-Landau equations with a polynomial nonlinearity in the real line. We use splitting-methods to prove well-posedness for a subset of almost periodic spaces. Specifically, we prove that if the initial condition has multiples of an irrational phase, then the solution of the equation maintains those same phases.



keywords
Well-posedness, Almost periodic spaces, Lie–Trotter method.

We introduce some definitions and preliminary results.

Definition 2.1
.

We define
C_{\textrm{u}}(\mathbb{R})
as
the set of uniformly continuous and bounded functions on

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|u(x)|.

We define the set of almost periodic functions

\displaystyle P(\mathbb{R})=\{u\in C_{u}(\mathbb{R}):\ u=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}a_{j}e^{ix\lambda_{j}}:\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}\}

Definition 2.3

We define the following subset of almost periodic functions,

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})=\{u\in C_{u}(\mathbb{R}):\ u=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}a_{j}e^{ixj\lambda};\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|a_{j}|<\infty\ :\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{A_{\lambda}}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|a_{j}|.

(3)

Theorem 2.4
.

A_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})
is a Banach space.

Proof 2.5
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a Cauchy sequence such that
u_{n}\in A_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})\subset P(\mathbb{R})
. Then
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence, that is,
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{A_{\lambda}}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|a^{n}_{j}|\leq K
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Additionally,
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a convergent sequence in
P(\mathbb{R})
, i.e.
u_{n}\to u
with
u\in P(\mathbb{R})
and
\left\|u\right\|_{A_{\lambda}}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|a_{j}|
. Therefore, we have that
\sum^{N}_{j=1}|a_{j}|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum^{N}_{j=1}|a^{n}_{j}|\leq K
, which implies that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum^{N}_{j=1}|a_{j}|=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|a_{j}|\leq K.

On the other hand, we can define an inner product for
u,v\in P(\mathbb{R}
):

\displaystyle\langle u(x),v(x)\rangle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}u(x)v(x)dx.

In particular, we have a normalized orthogonal system in the following sense (see
[
Bohr2018
]
):

\displaystyle\langle e^{i\lambda_{1}x},e^{-i\lambda_{2}x}\rangle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{i\lambda_{1}x}e^{-i\lambda_{2}x}=\begin{cases}0&\text{, for }\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}\\
1&\text{, for }\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}.\end{cases}

For
u\in P(\mathbb{R}),\ u=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}e^{i\lambda_{j}x}
, we have,

\displaystyle\langle u(x),e^{-i\lambda_{j}x}\rangle=a_{j}.

If
u_{n}\in A_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})
is a Cauchy sequence, then

\displaystyle a_{j}^{n}=\langle u_{n}(x),e^{-ixj\lambda}\rangle\ \xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\ \langle u(x),e^{-ixj\lambda}\rangle=a_{j}.

As (
2
) and (
3
) are met then

.

\displaystyle|(U(t)u)(x)-u(x)|\leq{}

\displaystyle{}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t}(y)|u(x-y)-u(x)|dy

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{}\int_{|y|<\delta}G_{t}(y)|u(x-y)-u(x)|dy+\int_{|y|\geq\delta}G_{t}(y)|u(x-y)-u(x)|dy.

For

\displaystyle V_{h,k+1}

\displaystyle=U(h)W_{h,k},

\displaystyle W_{h,k+1}

We claim that

Clearly, the assertion is true for

If

2.10

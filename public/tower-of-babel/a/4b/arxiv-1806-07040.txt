Defective and Clustered Choosability of Sparse Graphs

Abstract
. An (improper) graph colouring has defect d d if each monochromatic subgraph has maximum degree at most d d , and has clustering c c if each monochromatic component has at most c c vertices. Introduction

This paper studies improper colourings of sparse graphs, where sparsity is measured by the following standard definition. The
maximum average degree
of a graph
G

\mad(G)

H
of
G
, of the average degree of
H
. We consider improper colourings with bounded monochromatic degree or with bounded monochromatic components, for graph classes with bounded maximum average degree.
We now formalise these ideas. A

of a graph

is a function that assigns a colour to each vertex. In a coloured graph

monochromatic subgraph
of

is the spanning subgraph consisting of those edges whose endpoints have the same colour. A colouring has

is adjacent to at most
k

. A colouring has

k

0
or clustering
1

Our focus is on minimising the number of colours, with small defect or small clustering as a secondary goal. This viewpoint leads to the following definitions. The
defective chromatic number
of a graph class
\mathcal{G}
is the minimum integer

such that for some integer

, every graph in
\mathcal{G}
is
k
-colourable with defect

. The

of a graph class

is the minimum integer

such that for some integer

\mathcal{G}
is
k
-colourable with clustering

The above definitions extend in the obvious way to list-colourings and choosability. A
list-assignment
for a graph

is a function

of colours to each vertex
v\in V(G)
.
A list-assignment

is a

-list-assignment

|L(v)|\geqslant k

.
An

is assigned a colour in

Define

-colouring with defect

for every

-list-assignment

of

. Similarly,

has an
L
-colouring with clustering

-list-assignment

n\geqslant 3
), implying
\mad(G,4tg-8t+1)<6t+\frac{3}{2}
. Hence,
8
implies the following improvement to this upper bound.

Theorem 26
.

For
g\geqslant 0
and
t\geqslant 1
, every graph with
g
-thickness
t
is
(4t+1)
-choosable with clustering

\max\{\lceil\frac{4tg-8t}{4t+1}\rceil,\,228t+6\}
.

This result highlights the utility of considering
\mad(G,n_{0})
.

9.
Stack and Queue Layouts

This section applies our results to graphs with given stack- or queue-number. Again, previous results for clustered colouring do not work for graphs with given stack- or queue-number since they can contain expanders
[
19
]
, and thus do not have sub-linear separators.

A
k
-stack layout
of a graph
G
consists of a linear ordering

v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of
V(G)
and a partition
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
of
E(G)

such that no two edges in
E_{i}
cross with respect to
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}

for each
i\in[1,k]
. Here edges
v_{a}v_{b}
and
v_{c}v_{d}

cross

if
a<c<b<d
. A graph is a
k
-stack graph
if it has a

k
-stack layout. The
stack-number
of a graph
G
is the
minimum integer
k
for which
G
is a
k
-stack graph. Stack layouts
are also called
book embeddings
, and stack-number is also
called
book-thickness
,
fixed outer-thickness
and

page-number
.
Dujmović and Wood 2004
showed that the maximum chromatic
number of
k
-stack graphs is in
\{2k,2k+1,2k+2\}
.

A
k
-queue layout
of a graph
G
consists of a linear ordering

v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of
V(G)
and a partition
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
of
E(G)

such that no two edges in
E_{i}
are nested with respect to

v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
for each
i\in[1,k]
. Here edges
v_{a}v_{b}
and
v_{c}v_{d}

are
nested
if
a<c<d<b
. The
queue-number
of a graph
G

is the minimum integer
k
for which
G
has a
k
-queue layout. A
graph is a
k
-queue graph
if it has a
k
-queue layout.

Dujmović and Wood 2004
showed that the maximum chromatic number of
k
-queue
graphs is in the range
[2k+1,4k]
.

Consider clustered colourings of
k
-stack and
k
-queue graphs.

Wood 2018
noted
the clustered chromatic number of the class of
k
-stack graphs is in

[k+2,2k+2]
, and that
the clustered chromatic number of the class of
k
-queue graphs is in

[k+1,4k]
.
The lower bounds come from standard examples, and the upper bounds hold since
every
k
-stack graph has maximum average degree less than
2k+2
,
and every
k
-queue graph has maximum average degree less than
4k
.

4
,
5
and
6
thus imply the
following improved upper bounds:

Theorem 27
.

Every
k
-stack graph is:

•

\lfloor\frac{3k+4}{2}\rfloor
-choosable with defect
1
, and thus
with clustering
2
.

•

\lfloor\frac{7k+11}{5}\rfloor
-choosable with clustering
9
.

•

\lfloor\frac{4k+6}{3}\rfloor
-choosable with clustering at most
76k+53
.

Theorem 28
.

Every
k
-queue graph is:

•

3k
-choosable with defect
1
, and thus with clustering
2
.

•

\lfloor\frac{14k+4}{5}\rfloor
-choosable with clustering
9
.

•

\lfloor\frac{8k+2}{3}\rfloor
-choosable with clustering at most
152k-13
.

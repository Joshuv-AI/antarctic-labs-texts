Spectral analysis on dissimilarity between turbulent momentum and heat transfers in plane Couette turbulence Preprint: AIP/123-QED
T. Kawata, T. Tsukahara

Abstract
Nonlinear interaction between different scales in turbulence results in both interscale and spatial transport of turbulent energy, and the role of such scale interactions in turbulent heat transfer mechanism is also of practical importance from the engineering viewpoint. In the present study, we investigate a turbulent plane Couette flow with passive-scalar heat transfer at the Prandtl number 0.71, in order to address the similarity/difference between the scale interactions in the velocity and temperature fields. The constant-temperature-difference boundary condition is used so that the mean velocity and temperature profiles are similar, and then the roles of interscale and spatial transports are compared for the spectral transport budgets of the turbulent energies and temperature-related statistics. We show that turbulent heat transfer occurs at relatively small streamwise length scales compared to momentum transfer, although molecular diffusion is more significant in the temperature field as the Prandtl number is less than 1. Detailed analysis on the transport budgets of the temperature-related spectra shows that scale interactions in temperature field supply more energy to small scales than those in velocity field. Such significant temperature cascade leads to more energetic temperature fluctuation at small scales, which eventually results in the dissimilarity between the spectra of turbulent heat and momentum transfers.



I
Introduction

Nonlinear interaction between different scales is the important aspect of turbulence, by which turbulent energy is transported across different scales and spatial diffusions of momentum and heat are enhanced. In wall turbulence at certainly high Reynolds numbers, the flow involves relatively small-scale coherent structures in the near-wall region and the large-scale structures located in the logarithmic region or further out (see, for example, Ref.
Smits, McKeon, and Marusic 2011
and references therein), and nonlinear interaction between such inner and outer structures has also been pointed out in a number of studied in the last decades
Mathis, Hutchins, and Marusic 2009
;
Bernardini and Pirozzoli 2011
;
Pirozzoli, Bernardini, and Orlandi 2011
;
Dogan et al. 2019
.

Motivated by such observations of coherent structures and their interactions, there has been intensive efforts in recent years to investigate the scale interactions in wall turbulence by quantifying turbulent energy transfers between different scales
Cimarelli et al. 2016
;
Mizuno 2016
;
Mamatsashvili et al. 2016
;
Cho, Hwang, and Choi 2018
;
Kawata and Alfredsson 2018
;
Bauer, von Kameke, and Wagner 2019
;
Hamba 2019
;
Lee and Moser 2019
;
Motoori and Goto 2019
;
Motoori and Goto 2020
;
Chan, Schlatter, and Chin 2021
;
Chiarini et al. 2021
;
Doohan, Wills, and Hwang 2021
;
Kawata and Tsukahara 2021
. Interestingly, both forward (from larger to smaller scales) and inverse (from smaller to larger scales) turbulent energy cascades have been observed in these studies, and the relations of such interscale energy transfers to the dynamics of coherent structures, such as the self-sustaining cycles of each inner and outer structure
Cho, Hwang, and Choi 2018
;
Hamba 2019
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Kawata and Tsukahara 2021
, the inner-outer interactions
Cho, Hwang, and Choi 2018
;
Kawata and Alfredsson 2018
;
Kawata and Tsukahara 2021
;
Doohan, Wills, and Hwang 2021
;
Chiarini et al. 2021
;
Mäteling and Schröder 2022
;
Chan et al. 2022
;
Zhou, Xu, and Jiménez 2022
, merging/splitting of wall-attached hair-pin vortices
Chan, Schlatter, and Chin 2021
, etc. have been discussed.

Although only the interscale energy transfer was focused on in the earlier studies mentioned above, the effect of nonlinear scale interactions to enhance spatial diffusions is also an important feature of turbulence, particularly in terms of engineering point of view. One of the most desired controls is the suppressing momentum transfer with enhancing heat transfer at the same time, but such dissimilar control of turbulence is usually difficult as the heat transfer is basically passive to the turbulent fluid motions and therefore there is a certain analogy between the turbulent heat and momentum transfers.

Some earlier studies, however, demonstrated the possibility of the dissimilar control of turbulent heat and momentum transfers. Hasegawa & Kasagi
Hasegawa and Kasagi 2011
and Yamamoto
et al
.
Yamamoto, Hasegawa, and Kasagi 2013
introduced suboptimal control theory and found that the dissimilar control works even in flows where the averaged energy and momentum transport equations have an identical form, and the optimal input mode exhibits a streamwise traveling-wave-like property. Successful dissimilar controls by traveling-wave-like input from the wall have been actually reported by some numerical studies using blowing/suction from the wall
(
Higashi, Mamori, and Fukagata 2011
;
Kaithakkal, Kametani, and Hasegawa 2020
;
Mamori et al. 2021
)
or wall deformation
Uchino, Mamori, and Fukagata 2017
. The essential mechanism of such dissimilar transfer control is the different sensitivities of velocity and temperature fields to the control input from the wall, which results from the fact that velocity is a divergence-free vector quantity whereas temperature is a scalar
Hasegawa and Kasagi 2011
. Deepening our understanding of such differences in the behaviours of velocity and temperature fields may allow us to further develop the dissimilar flow control schemes.

Despite numerous studies on wall turbulence with passive-scalar temperature field reported in the past including aforementioned attempts for the dissimilar flow control, less attention has been paid to the spectra and cascades of fluctuating temperature (or passive scalar). Among such few studies, Antonia & Abe
Antonia and Abe 2009
compares the spectra of the turbulent kinetic energy and the scalar temperature fluctuation in turbulent channel flows and reported no significant difference between those energy spectra, but their dissipation spectra display qualitatively different behaviours. Hane
et al
.
Hane et al. 2006
simulated a turbulent plane Couette flow with heat transfer and showed that the spectra of the streamwise turbulent energy and temperature fluctuation indicate their peaks at different streamwise wavelengths. These studies suggest that the scales dominating the heat and momentum transfers may differ in spite of the inherent analogy. As the turbulent heat and momentum transfers are the results of fluid motions in a wide range of scale, investigating their mechanisms scale-by-scale should help us, for example, develop new control strategies for heat transfer enhancement.

In the present study, we investigate the transport equation budgets of the temperature-related spectra based on the direct numerical simulation data of wall turbulence with passive-scalar temperature field, with a particular focus on the roles of the interscale and spatial transports at each scale. Turbulent plane Couette flow is chosen for the test case, because very-large-scale structures filling up the entire channel appear even at relatively low Reynolds numbers
Bech et al. 1995
;
Komminaho, Lundbladh, and Johansson 1996
;
Kitoh, Nakabayashi, and Nishimura 2005
;
Tsukahara, Kawamura, and Shingai 2006
;
Kitoh and Umeki 2008
;
Avsarkisov et al. 2015
;
Orlandi, Bernardini, and Pirozzoli 2015
;
Lee and Moser 2018
and therefore the scale separation between the inner and outer structures is relatively clear as compared to other canonical wall-bounded flow configurations at similar Reynolds numbers. The constant-temperature-difference boundary condition is applied to the temperature field so that the mean velocity and temperature profiles are similar to each other, and then fluctuating velocity and temperature fields are compared focusing on similarity/difference in the interscale and spatial energy transports caused by scale interactions.

II.2
Spectral Analysis on the Reynolds-Stress Transport and Turbulent Heat Transfers

In the present study, the spectral transports of the temperature fluctuation
\left\langle\theta^{2}\right\rangle
and the turbulent heat fluxes
\left\langle u_{i}\theta\right\rangle
are analysed. The transport equations of the temperature-related spectra are derived similarly to those of the Reynolds stress spectra. In this section, the derivation of the transport equations of the Reynolds stress spectra is briefly reviewed and that for the temperature-related turbulence statistics are defined. In the following, the instantaneous velocities in
x
-,
y
- and
z
-directions are denoted as
\tilde{u}=U+u
,
\tilde{v}=v
, and
\tilde{w}=w
, and the instantaneous temperature is also decomposed as
\tilde{\theta}=\varTheta+\theta
. The uppercase letters
U=\left\langle\tilde{u}\right\rangle
and
\varTheta=\left\langle\tilde{\theta}\right\rangle
are the mean streamwise velocity and temperature (here,
\left\langle\right\rangle
stands for averaging in
x
- and
z
-directions and in time), respectively, and the lowercase letters without the tilde represent the fluctuations around the mean values. Note here
\left\langle\tilde{v}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tilde{w}\right\rangle=0
.

II.2.1
Transport of Reynolds stresses

The transport equation of the Reynolds stress
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}\right\rangle
is expressed as

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+U_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\right)\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}\right\rangle=P_{ij}-\varepsilon_{ij}+\Phi_{ij}+D^{\nu}_{ij}+D^{t}_{ij},

(5a)

where the terms on the right-hand side are the production (
P_{ij}
), the viscous dissipation (
\varepsilon_{ij}
), the pressure work (
\Phi_{ij}
), the viscous diffusion (
D^{\nu}_{ij}
), and the turbulent diffusion (
D^{t}_{ij}
) terms, which are defined as

\displaystyle P_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle u_{i}u_{k}\right\rangle\frac{\partial U_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}-\left\langle u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle\frac{\partial U_{i}}{\partial x_{k}},

(5b)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{Re_{\mathrm{w}}}\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle,

(5c)

\displaystyle\Phi_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}u_{j}\right\rangle-\left\langle\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{j}}u_{i}\right\rangle,

(5d)

\displaystyle D^{\nu}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Re_{\mathrm{w}}}\frac{\partial^{2}\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}\right\rangle}{\partial x_{k}^{2}},

(5e)

\displaystyle D^{t}_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle}{\partial x_{k}}.

(5f)

The pressure-work term
\Phi_{ij}
may be decomposed into the pressure-strain correlation
\Pi_{ij}
and the pressure diffusion
D^{p}_{ij}
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{ij}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{ij}+D^{p}_{ij},

(6a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{where}\quad\Pi_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left\langle p\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\right\rangle,

(6b)

\displaystyle D^{p}_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\left\langle u_{j}p\right\rangle\delta_{ik}+\left\langle u_{i}p\right\rangle\delta_{jk}\right).

(6c)

Here,
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker’s delta.

Now, we consider a decomposition of the fluctuating components into their large- and small-scale parts

\displaystyle{\bf u}={\bf u}^{L}+{\bf u}^{S},\quad\theta=\theta^{L}+\theta^{S},

(7)

where the cross correlations between the large- and small-scale part of any quantities are zero:

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{j}^{S}\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{j}^{L}u_{i}^{S}\right\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\left\langle\theta^{L}\theta^{S}\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{i}^{L}\theta^{S}\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{i}^{S}\theta^{L}\right\rangle=0.

Such a decomposition is possible based on orthogonal mode decompositions such as the Fourier mode decomposition and the proper-orthogonal-mode decomposition. We adopt a decomposition based on the streamwise or spanwise Fourier modes at a certain cutoff wavenumber
k_{c}
. By the decomposition the Reynolds stress
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}\right\rangle
is decomposed simply into their large- and small-scale parts as

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{j}^{L}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{S}u_{j}^{S}\right\rangle.

(8)

With a similar manner to the “full” Reynolds stress equation (
5
), one can obtain the transport equations of the large- and small-scale parts of
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}\right\rangle
(the details of the derivation are found in Refs.
Kawata and Alfredsson 2019
;
Kawata and Tsukahara 2021
) as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{j}^{L}\right\rangle}{{\rm d}t}=P^{L}_{ij}-\varepsilon^{L}_{ij}+\Phi^{L}_{ij}+D^{\nu,L}_{ij}+D^{t,L}_{ij}-Tr_{ij},

(9a)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\left\langle u_{i}^{S}u_{j}^{S}\right\rangle}{{\rm d}t}=P^{S}_{ij}-\varepsilon^{S}_{ij}+\Phi^{S}_{ij}+D^{\nu,S}_{ij}+D^{t,S}_{ij}+Tr_{ij}.

(9b)

Here,
{\rm d}/{\rm d}t=\partial/\partial t+U_{k}\partial/\partial x_{k}
, and the terms on the right-hand sides of Eqs. (
9
a, b) are the large- and small-scale parts of their counterparts in Eq. (
5
), except for
Tr_{ij}
. In particular, the first four terms in Eq. (
5
) (i.e., the production, the viscous dissipation, the pressure work, and the viscous diffusion) are simply decomposed into their large- and small-scale parts similarly in Eq. (
8
): those in Eq. (
9
a) are defined as in Eq. (
5
b–e) with
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
replaced by
u_{i}^{L}
and
u_{j}^{L}
(for those in Eq. (
9
b), replaced by
u_{i}^{S}
and
u_{j}^{S}
).

On the other hand, the turbulent diffusion is decomposed as

\displaystyle D^{t,L}=-\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}}{\partial x_{k}},\quad D^{t,S}=-\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}}{\partial x_{k}},

(10)

where
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}
and
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}
are the large- and small-scale part of the triple velocity correlation
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle
defined as

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}=\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{j}^{L}u_{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{S}u_{j}^{L}u_{k}^{S}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{j}^{S}u_{k}^{S}\right\rangle,

(11a)

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}=\left\langle u_{i}^{S}u_{j}^{S}u_{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{j}^{S}u_{k}^{L}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{S}u_{j}^{L}u_{k}^{L}\right\rangle.

(11b)

The last term in Eqs. (
9
a, b),
Tr_{ij}
, is defined as

\displaystyle Tr_{ij}=-\left\langle u_{i}^{S}u_{k}^{S}\frac{\partial u_{j}^{L}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle-\left\langle u_{j}^{S}u_{k}^{S}\frac{\partial u_{i}^{L}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{k}^{L}\frac{\partial u_{j}^{S}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{j}^{L}u_{k}^{L}\frac{\partial u_{i}^{S}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle,

(12)

and as can be seen from Eqs.(
9
a, b) this term appears in both equations with opposite signs, which means that
Tr_{ij}
represents the energy exchange between the large- and small-scale velocity fields across the cutoff wavenumber
k_{c}
.

It is worth pointing out here that the turbulent transport terms (
D^{t,L}_{ij}
and
D^{t,S}_{ij}
) and the interscale energy flux term (
Tr_{ij}
) includes both the large- and small-scale part of fluctuating velocities, which is in contrast to the other terms in the the transport equations consisting of only either
{\bf u}^{L}
or
{\bf u}^{S}
. This is because both the turbulent transport and the interscale energy flux terms are the third-order moments of fluctuating velocities (and the velocity gradients), while the other terms are of the second moments. As
D^{t,L}_{ij}
,
D^{t,S}_{ij}
, and
Tr_{ij}
include both large- and small-scale parts of the velocity field, these terms can be interpreted to represent the energy-transport effects by interactions between different scales: the turbulent transport (
D^{t,L}_{ij}
and
D^{t,S}_{ij}
) indicates the energy transport in physical space caused by scale interactions, while the interscale energy flux (
Tr_{ij}
) is the energy transport between different scales.

As the large-scale part of the Reynolds stresses
\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{j}^{L}\right\rangle
and the Reynolds stress spectra
E_{ij}
are related as

\displaystyle E_{ij}=\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u^{L}_{j}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{c}}\left(=-\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}^{S}u_{j}^{S}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{c}}\right),

(13)

the transport equations of the Reynolds-stress spectra can, therefore, be derived by differentiating both sides of Eq. (
9
a) with respect to
k_{c}
as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}E_{ij}}{{\rm d}t}=pr_{ij}-\xi_{ij}+\phi_{ij}+d^{\nu}_{ij}+d^{t}_{ij}+tr_{ij},

(14)

where the terms on the right-hand side are the
k_{c}
-derivatives of the counterparts in Eq. (
9
a). The first five terms represent the spectra of the corresponding terms of Eq.(
5
a): the spectra of the production (
pr_{ij}
), the viscous dissipation (
\xi_{ij}
), the pressure-work (
\phi_{ij}
), the viscous diffusion
d^{\nu}_{ij}
, and the turbulent transport
d^{t}_{ij}
. The interscale transport term
tr_{ij}
represents the energy gain/loss by the interscale flux
Tr_{ij}
at each wavenumebr. In particular, the spectra of the production (
pr_{ij}
) and the viscous diffusion (
d^{\nu}_{ij}
) are expressed based on the Reynolds stress spectra as

\displaystyle pr_{ij}

\displaystyle=-E_{ik}\frac{\partial U_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}-E_{jk}\frac{\partial U_{i}}{\partial x_{k}},

(15a)

\displaystyle d^{\nu}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Re_{\mathrm{w}}}\frac{\partial^{2}E_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}^{2}}.

(15b)

According to Eq. (
6
), the pressure-work spectrum
\phi_{ij}
may be decomposed into the spectra of
\Pi_{ij}
and
D^{p}_{ij}
as

\displaystyle\phi_{ij}=\pi_{ij}+d^{p}_{ij}.

(16)

Here,
\pi_{ij}
is the cospectrum between the pressure and the strain rate, representing the inter-component energy transfers between different components of velocity fluctuation at each scale, and
d^{p}_{ij}
consists of the velocity-pressure cospectra, representing spatial energy transport by pressure fluctuation at each scale.

The turbulent spatial transport
d^{t}_{ij}
and the interscale transport
tr_{ij}
, which are of particular interest in the present study, are respectively defined as

\displaystyle d^{t}_{ij}=-\frac{\partial E_{ijk}}{\partial x_{k}},\quad\quad tr_{ij}=-\frac{\partial Tr_{ij}}{\partial k_{c}}.

(17a)

Here,
E_{ijk}
is the spectra of triple velocity correlations
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle
defined as

\displaystyle E_{ijk}=\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}}{\partial k_{c}}\left(=-\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}}{\partial k_{c}}\right),

(17b)

similarly to the definition of the Reynolds stress spectra, Eq. (
13
). As the physical meaning of
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\right\rangle
is the spatial transport flux of the Reynolds stress
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}\right\rangle
in the
x_{k}
-direction caused by the velocity fluctuation
u_{k}
, their spectra
E_{ijk}
represent the spatial flux of the Reynolds stress
\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}\right\rangle
in the
x_{k}
-direction at each wavenumber. Again, note that the spatial transport represented by
E_{ijk}
is caused by interactions between different scales as indicated by Eqs. (
11
).

II.2.2
Transport of temperature fluctuation and velocity-temperature correlations

Now we derive the spectral transport equations of the temperature-related turbulence statistics. The transport equation of the temperature fluctuation
\left\langle\theta^{2}\right\rangle
is written similarly to the Reynolds stress equation (
5
) as

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+U_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\right)\left\langle\theta^{2}\right\rangle=P_{\theta\theta}-\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}+D^{\nu}_{\theta\theta}+D^{t}_{\theta\theta},

(18a)

with the production (
P_{\theta\theta}
), the viscous dissipation (
\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
), the viscous diffusion (
D^{\nu}_{\theta\theta}
), and the turbulent transport (
D^{t}_{\theta\theta}
) terms defined as

\displaystyle P_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\theta u_{k}\right\rangle\frac{\partial\varTheta}{\partial x_{k}},

(18b)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{Re_{\mathrm{w}}Pr}\left\langle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle,

(18c)

\displaystyle D^{\nu}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Re_{\mathrm{w}}Pr}\frac{\partial^{2}\left\langle\theta^{2}\right\rangle}{\partial x_{k}^{2}},

(18d)

\displaystyle D^{t}_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\left\langle\theta^{2}u_{k}\right\rangle}{\partial x_{k}}.

(18e)

It is noteworthy here that any term corresponding to
\Phi_{ij}
in Eq. (
5
a) does not exist in Eq. (
18
a), since the energy equation (
2
) does not have pressure-related term.

The transport equations of the turbulent heat fluxes
\left\langle u_{i}\theta\right\rangle
are also similarly obtained as

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+U_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\right)\left\langle u_{i}\theta\right\rangle=P_{i\theta}-\varepsilon_{i\theta}+\Phi_{i\theta}+D^{\nu}_{i\theta}+D^{t}_{i\theta},

(19a)

with the production (
P_{i\theta}
), the viscous dissipation (
\varepsilon_{i\theta}
), the pressure-gradient-temperature correlations (
\Phi_{i\theta}
), the viscous diffusion (
D^{\nu}_{i\theta}
) and the turbulent transport (
D^{t}_{i\theta}
) terms:

\displaystyle P_{i\theta}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle u_{k}\theta\right\rangle\frac{\partial U_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}-\left\langle u_{i}u_{k}\right\rangle\frac{\partial\varTheta}{\partial x_{k}},

(19b)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Re_{\mathrm{w}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{Pr}\right)\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle,

(19c)

\displaystyle\Phi_{i\theta}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}\theta\right\rangle,

(19d)

\displaystyle D^{\nu}_{i\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Re_{\mathrm{w}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}\theta\right\rangle+\frac{1}{Pr}\left\langle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x_{k}}u_{i}\right\rangle\right),

(19e)

\displaystyle D^{t}_{i\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}\theta u_{k}\right\rangle}{\partial x_{k}}.

(19f)

Based on the decomposition given by Eq. (
7
), the temperature fluctuation and the turbulent heat fluxes are also split into their large- and small-scale parts, similarly to the Reynolds stress decomposition (
8
), as

\displaystyle\left\langle\theta^{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\theta^{L}\theta^{L}\right\rangle+\left\langle\theta^{S}\theta^{S}\right\rangle,

(20a)

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}\theta\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{i}^{L}\theta^{L}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{S}\theta^{S}\right\rangle,

(20b)

and therefore the transport equations of their large- and small-scale parts can be derived similarly to Eqs. (
9
). Then, these large- and small-scale parts of the temperature-related statistics are related to their spectra, similarly to Eq. (
13
), as

\displaystyle E_{\theta\theta}=\frac{\partial\left\langle\theta^{L}\theta^{L}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{c}},\quad E_{i\theta}=\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}^{L}\theta^{L}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{c}},

(21)

and hence the transport equations of these temperature-related spectra can also be obtained by differentiating the equations of
\left\langle\theta^{L}\theta^{L}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle u_{i}^{L}\theta^{L}\right\rangle
. The transport equation of the temperature-fluctuation spectrum
E_{\theta\theta}
is expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}E_{\theta\theta}}{{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle=pr_{\theta\theta}-\xi_{\theta\theta}+d^{\nu}_{\theta\theta}+d^{t}_{\theta\theta}+tr_{\theta\theta}

(22)

with the terms on the right-hand side representing the spectra of the counterparts in Eq. (
18
).
The interscale and the turbulent spatial transport terms are defined as

\displaystyle tr_{\theta\theta}=-\frac{\partial Tr_{\theta\theta}}{\partial k_{c}},\quad\quad d^{t}_{\theta\theta}=-\frac{\partial E_{\theta\theta k}}{\partial x_{k}},

(23a)

where
Tr_{\theta\theta}
and
E_{\theta\theta k}
are the interscale and the spectral spatial fluxes of the temperature fluctuation defined as

\displaystyle Tr_{\theta\theta}

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\theta^{S}u_{k}^{S}\frac{\partial\theta^{L}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle-2\left\langle\theta^{L}u_{k}^{L}\frac{\partial\theta^{S}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle,

(23b)

\displaystyle E_{\theta\theta k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\left\langle\theta^{2}u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}}{\partial k_{c}}=-\frac{\partial\left\langle\theta^{2}u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}}{\partial k_{c}},

(23c)

with
\left\langle\theta^{2}u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}
and
\left\langle\theta^{2}u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}
being the large- and small-scale parts of the spatial flux of temperature fluctuation
\left\langle\theta^{2}u_{k}\right\rangle
:

\displaystyle\left\langle\theta^{2}u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\theta^{L}\theta^{L}u_{k}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\theta^{L}\theta^{S}u_{k}^{S}\right\rangle,

(23d)

\displaystyle\left\langle\theta^{2}u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\theta^{S}\theta^{S}u_{k}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\theta^{L}\theta^{S}u_{k}^{L}\right\rangle.

(23e)

Similarly, the transport equation of the turbulent heat flux spectrum
E_{i\theta}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}E_{i\theta}}{{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle=pr_{i\theta}-\xi_{i\theta}+\phi_{i\theta}+d^{\nu}_{i\theta}+d^{t}_{i\theta}+tr_{i\theta},

(24)

and the terms on the right-hand side (except
tr_{i\theta}
) are, again, the spectra of their counterparts in Eq. (
19
).
The interscale and the turbulent spatial transport terms are defined in the same manners as in other spectral transport equations:

\displaystyle tr_{i\theta}=-\frac{\partial Tr_{i\theta}}{\partial k_{c}},\quad\quad d^{t}_{i\theta}=-\frac{\partial E_{i\theta k}}{\partial x_{k}},

(25a)

where the interscale flux
Tr_{i\theta}
and the spectral spatial flux
E_{i\theta k}
are respectively defined as

\displaystyle Tr_{i\theta}=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\theta^{S}u_{k}^{S}\frac{\partial u_{i}^{L}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle-\left\langle u_{i}^{S}u_{k}^{S}\frac{\partial\theta^{L}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\theta^{L}u_{k}^{L}\frac{\partial u_{i}^{S}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{L}u_{k}^{L}\frac{\partial\theta^{S}}{\partial x_{k}}\right\rangle,

(25b)

\displaystyle E_{i\theta k}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}\theta u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}}{\partial k_{c}}=-\frac{\partial\left\langle u_{i}\theta u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}}{\partial k_{c}}.

(25c)

Here,
\left\langle u_{i}\theta u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}
and
\left\langle u_{i}\theta u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}
are the large- and small-scale parts of
\left\langle u_{i}\theta u_{k}\right\rangle
defined as

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}\theta u_{k}\right\rangle^{L}=\left\langle u_{i}^{L}\theta^{L}u_{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{L}\theta^{S}u_{k}^{S}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{S}\theta^{L}u_{k}^{S}\right\rangle,

(25d)

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}\theta u_{k}\right\rangle^{S}=\left\langle u_{i}^{S}\theta^{S}u_{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{S}\theta^{L}u_{k}^{L}\right\rangle+\left\langle u_{i}^{L}\theta^{S}u_{k}^{L}\right\rangle.

(25e)

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{w}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{Re_{\mathrm{w}}}}_{\tau_{\mathrm{w,lam}}}+\underbrace{\int^{1}_{0}(-\left\langle uv\right\rangle)\mathrm{d}y}_{\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{w}}},

(27a)

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{w}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{PrRe_{\mathrm{w}}}}_{q_{\mathrm{w,lam}}}+\underbrace{\int^{1}_{0}(-\left\langle v\theta\right\rangle)\mathrm{d}y}_{\Delta q_{\mathrm{w}}},

and
q_{\mathrm{w,lam}}

27
) are the Fukagata-Iwamoto-Kasagi identity
(
Fukagata, Iwamoto, and Kasagi 2002
)
for the plane Couette configuration. Taking the streamwise one-dimensional spectra
E^{x}_{-uv}
and
E^{x}_{-v\theta}
for example,

and
\Delta q_{\mathrm{w}}
are expressed in terms of
E^{x}_{-uv}
and

\displaystyle\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{w}}

\displaystyle=\int^{1}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}E^{x}_{-uv}(y,k_{x})\mathrm{d}k_{x}\mathrm{d}y=\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathcal{E}^{x}_{-uv}(k_{x})\mathrm{d}k_{x},

(28a)

\displaystyle\Delta q_{\mathrm{w}}

\displaystyle=\int^{1}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}E^{x}_{-v\theta}(y,k_{x})\mathrm{d}k_{x}\mathrm{d}y=\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathcal{E}^{x}_{-v\theta}(k_{x})\mathrm{d}k_{x},

(28b)

where
\mathcal{E}^{x}_{-uv}
and
\mathcal{E}^{x}_{-v\theta}
are the spectra of turbulent momentum and heat transfers
integrated across the channel:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{x}_{-uv}=\int^{1}_{0}E^{x}_{-uv}(y,k_{x})\mathrm{d}y,

(28c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{x}_{-v\theta}=\int^{1}_{0}E^{x}_{-v\theta}(y,k_{x})\mathrm{d}y.

(28d)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm D}\mathcal{E}^{x}_{-uv}}{{\rm D}t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{x}_{-uv}-\varXi^{x}_{-uv}+\mathcal{W}^{x}_{-uv}+\mathcal{T}^{x}_{-uv},

(33a)

where
\mathcal{P}^{x}_{-uv}
,

V
Conclusion

In the present study, a turbulent plane Couette flow with passive-scalar heat transfer has been simulated in order to investigate the interscale and spatial transports of the temperature-related statistics in comparisons with the corresponding transports of the turbulent kinetic energy and the Reynolds shear stress. Due to the similar boundary conditions in the velocity and temperature fields, the profiles of the mean streamwise velocity and the mean temperature are similar to each other, and the productions of the turbulent energy and the temperature fluctuation, therefore, also have similar profiles. In such a configuration, the distributions of the spectra and their transport budgets have been investigated in details.

It has been found that the temperature fluctuation occurs at somewhat smaller streamwise length scales than the velocity fluctuations, although though the Prandtl number is smaller than 1 and therefore the effect of molecular diffusion is more significant in the temperature field. The turbulent heat flux spectrum is also found to be distributed in smaller streamwise wavelength range than the Reynolds shear stress cospectrum, indicating a certain scale-by-scale dissimilarity between the turbulent momentum and heat transfers. The transport budget analysis of the temperature-related spectra reveals that the interscale energy transport of the temperature fluctuation is notably significant compared to that of the turbulent kinetic energy at smallest streamwise wavelengths. Among various terms comprising the interscale energy transport, the term representing the effect by the streamwise temperature gradient at small scales is found to dominate the transport of the temperature fluctuation to the smallest wavelength range, while the counterpart in the turbulent kinetic energy transport plays only a minor role in the energy transport budget. Such difference between the interscale transports of the turbulent kinetic energy and the temperature fluctuation is attributable to the different distributions of their spectra at small scales. The same tendency is also found in the interscale transports of the Reynolds shear stress and the turbulent heat flux, which results in the spectral dissimilarity between the turbulent heat and momentum transfers.

Entanglement and maximal violation of the CHSH inequality in a system of two spins j j : a novel construction and further observations
G. Peruzzo, S. P. Sorella

Abstract
We study the CHSH inequality for a system of two spin j j particles, for generic j j . The CHSH operator is constructed using a set of unitary, Hermitian operators { A 1 , A 2 , B 1 , B 2 } \left\{A_{1},A_{2},B_{1},B_{2}\right\} . The expectation value of the CHSH operator is analyzed for the singlet state | ψ s ⟩ \left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle . Being | ψ s ⟩ \left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle an entangled state, a violation of the CHSH inequality compatible with Tsirelson’s bound is found. Although the construction employed here differs from that of [ 1 ] , full agreement is recovered.

Introduction

In Quantum Mechanics, the general absence of an objective reality prior
to a measurement leads to conflicts with the principles of relativity,
as locality. For instance, if we have a composed system and, according
to Quantum Mechanics, its observable quantities are not defined before
measuring them, a measurement done in some part of the system necessarily
affects instantaneously the other parts, so as to keep the
very strong correlations which arise due to the phenomenon of entanglement.
This somehow nonlocal feature of Quantum Mechanics was used by Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen
(EPR)
[
2
]
to argue that Quantum Mechanics was an incomplete theory, despite of being logically sound.

Many physicists, as
[
3
]
, tried to supplement the Quantum Mechanics framework
with a set of local hidden variables which, once known, would provide a complete deterministic description as opposed to a probabilistic one. This long debate between supporters of Quantum Mechanics and supporters
of what is usually called local realism received a pivotal contribution from
John S. Bell
[
4
,
5
]
, who found, through Bell’s inequalities, a way
to test both points of view. One of the beauties of Bell’s work is
its generality that allows us to test, in principle, the local realism
in any instance. Afterwards, Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt
(CHSH)
[
6
,
7
,
8
,

]
proposed a generalized version of Bell’s idea more
suitable for direct experiments. A violation of Bell’s inequalities or CHSH
inequalities means that nature cannot be described by a local realistic
model. In fact, Quantum Mechanics predicts a violation of Bell’s
inequalities or CHSH inequalities in, basically, any physical entangled system,
like the two-particles system used by EPR to refute Quantum Mechanics.
Today, despite the high sophistication needed to test Bell’s or
CHSH inequalities experimentally, see, for instance,

Quantum Mechanics remains a solid, robust and consistent framework to handle the quantum world.

2
Construction of the operators
A_{i}
and
B_{i}

Let us consider a two spins
j
system. The Hilbert space,

\mathcal{H}
, is spanned by the tensor product of the orthonormal
states
\left\{\left|m\right\rangle;\,-j\leq m\leq j\right\}
,
that is by the set

\displaystyle\left\{\left|m\right\rangle\otimes\left|n\right\rangle;\,-j\leq m,n\leq j\right\}.

(1)

which means that
{\rm dimension}\;\mathcal{H}=\left(2j+1\right)^{2}
. In order to construct a CHSH operator we define
a set of unitary, Hermitian operators

\displaystyle\left\{A_{1},\,A_{2},\,B_{1},\,B_{2}\right\}

(2)

by their action on the basis (
1
):

\displaystyle A_{i}\left|-m\right\rangle\otimes\left|n\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{m}}\left|m\right\rangle\otimes\left|n\right\rangle,\quad\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{m}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{-m}=-\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{m}

(3)

and

\displaystyle B_{i}\left|m\right\rangle\otimes\left|-n\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{n}}\left|m\right\rangle\otimes\left|n\right\rangle,\quad\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{n}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{-n}=-\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{n}

(4)

for all
-j\leq m,n\leq j
.

Note that
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
change only one state of the tensor product, which means that they could have been defined as
A_{i}\otimes I
and
I\otimes B_{i}
. One can easily check that
A_{i}

and
B_{i}
are unitary, Hermitian operators,
i.e.

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{i},

\displaystyle B_{i}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{i},

(5)

and

\displaystyle A_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I,

\displaystyle B_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I.

(6)

Moreover, from the definitions (
3
) and (
4
)
it follows that

\left[A_{i},B_{j}\right]=0.

(7)

Each operator,
A_{i}
or
B_{j}
, is characterized by a set of
real parameters,
\alpha_{i}\equiv\left\{\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{m};\,-j\leq m\leq j\right\}

or
\beta\equiv\left\{\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{m};\,-j\leq m\leq j\right\}
,
being
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{j}
completely independent.
This feature makes
A_{i}
and
B_{j}
compatible with one of the main requirements
used to derive Bell’s inequality:
the experimental arrangement
to measure
A_{i}
is totally independent from the arrangement
to measure
B_{j}
. This requirement is important because it allows
to make two causal,
i.e.
space-like separated independent measurements of
A_{i}
and
B_{j}
. Following Bell’s methodology,
it is then possible to test in this model if the results predicted
by the Quantum Mechanics formalism are compatible with some
local
realistic theory
, like a theory based on local hidden variables.

Expectation value of the CHSH operator in the singlet state

We introduce the CHSH operator,
\mathcal{O}_{CHSH}
, in its traditional
form, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{CHSH}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(A_{1}+A_{2}\right)B_{1}+\left(A_{1}-A_{2}\right)B_{2}.

(8)

By construction,
\mathcal{O}_{CHSH}
is Hermitian,
\mathcal{O}_{CHSH}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{O}_{CHSH}
.

From the point of view of the local realism, one would expect

\displaystyle-2\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{CHSH}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2.

(9)

Relation (
9
) is the Bell-Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt
inequality. Now, we evaluate the expectation value of (
8
)
in the singlet state
\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle
, defined as
1

1

\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle
such

\displaystyle S_{x}\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle S_{y}\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle S_{z}\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(10)

where
\vec{S}=\vec{S}_{1}+\vec{S}_{2}
is the total spin. As a consequence
of (
10
),
\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle
is
rotational invariant.

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(2j+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{m=-j}^{j}\left(-1\right)^{j-m}\left|m\right\rangle\otimes\left|-m\right\rangle.

(11)

One can check that
\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle
is a normalized
state,
\left\langle\psi_{s}|\psi_{s}\right\rangle=1

\displaystyle A_{i}B_{j}\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(2j+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{m=-j}^{j}\left(-1\right)^{j-m}e^{-i\left(\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{m}+\left(\beta_{j}\right)_{m}\right)}\left|-m\right\rangle\otimes\left|m\right\rangle,

(12)

we have the following expectation value

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi_{s}\right|A_{i}B_{j}\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(2j+1\right)}\sum_{n=-j}^{j}\sum_{m=-j}^{j}\left(-1\right)^{j-n}\left(-1\right)^{j-m}e^{-i\left(\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{m}+\left(\beta_{j}\right)_{m}\right)}\delta_{n,-m}\delta_{-n,m}

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{2j}}{\left(2j+1\right)}\sum_{m=-j}^{j}e^{-i\left(\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{m}+\left(\beta_{j}\right)_{m}\right)}.

Therefore, we get for the CHSH operator the following result

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi_{s}\right|\mathcal{O}_{CHSH}\left|\psi_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{2j}}{\left(2j+1\right)}\sum_{m=-j}^{j}\left[e^{-i\left(\left(\alpha_{1}\right)_{m}+\left(\beta_{1}\right)_{m}\right)}+e^{-i\left(\left(\alpha_{2}\right)_{m}+\left(\beta_{1}\right)_{m}\right)}+e^{-i\left(\left(\alpha_{1}\right)_{m}+\left(\beta_{2}\right)_{m}\right)}-e^{-i\left(\left(\alpha_{2}\right)_{m}+\left(\beta_{2}\right)_{m}\right)}\right]

It is useful now to distinguish the two cases:

Conclusions

In this work we have employed the setup outlined in
[

to construct the four operators
\{A_{1},\,A_{2},\,B_{1},\,B_{2}\}
entering the CHSH inequality in the case of a pair of spin

[
15
]

In particular, in the present case, the results of
[
1
]

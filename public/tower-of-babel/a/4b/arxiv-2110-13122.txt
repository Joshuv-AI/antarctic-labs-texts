1.
Introduction

In this study we establish several new cardinality bounds for Hausdorff topological spaces involving the weak Lindelöf degree and related cardinal invariants. Recall that the
weak Lindelöf degree

wL(X)
of a space
X
is the least infinite cardinal

\kappa
such that every open cover of
X
has a
\kappa
-subfamily with dense union. When
wL(X)=\aleph_{0}
, the space
X
is called
weakly Lindelöf
. Note that
wL(X)\leq c(X)
for any space
X
, where
c(X)
is the cellularity of
X
.

By finding bounds for the cellularity
c(X)
of an extremally disconnected space
X
, we show in Corollary
that
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)\pi\chi(X)\psi(X)}
, for any extremally disconnected space
X
, where
\psi(X)
is the
pseudocharacter of
X
and
\pi\chi(X)
is the
\pi
-character of
X
. (A Hausdorff topological space
X
is
extremally disconnected
if the closure of every open subset of
X
is open.) In Corollary
the related bound

2^{wL(X)\pi\chi(X)}
is given for the cardinality of any power homogeneous extremally disconnected space. Recall that a space
X
is
homogeneous
if for every
x,y\in X
there exists a homeomorphism
h:X\to Y
such that
h(x)=y
.

X
is
power homogeneous
if there exists a cardinal
\kappa
such that
X^{\kappa}
is homogeneous.

In Section
we explore cardinal inequalities for spaces with
G_{\delta}
-diagonals of rank 2, and related notions (see Section
for definitions). We recall that Buzyakova in
[
11
]
showed that any ccc space with a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal has cardinality not
exceeding
2^{\aleph_{0}}
. (Note that every space
X
with a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal is a Urysohn space i.e. a space such that every two distinct points can be separated by disjoint closed neighborhoods.)
Buzyakova’s inequality was extended to higher cardinalities by Gotchev in
[
16
]
as follows: If
X
is a Urysohn space, then
|X|\leq 2^{c(X)\overline{\Delta}(X)}
, where
\overline{\Delta}(X)
is the regular diagonal degree of
X
. We recall that
the
regular diagonal degree

\overline{\Delta}(X)
of a Urysohn space
X
is the minimal infinite cardinal
\kappa
such that
X
has a regular
G_{\kappa}
-diagonal. By replacing
c(X)
with
wL(X)
, in the same paper, Gotchev proved that
if
X
is a Urysohn space, then
|X|\leq 2^{\overline{\Delta}(X)\cdot 2^{wL(X)}}
. Therefore, every weakly Lindelöf space with a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal has cardinality at most
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
.
Bella in
[
8
]
showed that a ccc space with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2 has cardinality at most
2^{\aleph_{0}}
. In relation to these results in Theorem
we show that if
X
is an almost
Lindelöf space with a strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2, then
|X|\leq 2^{\aleph_{0}}
. We recall that the
almost Lindelöf degree
of a space
X
, denoted
aL(X)
, is the smallest infinite cardinal
\kappa
such that for every open cover

\mathcal{V}
of
X
, there is a subcollection
\mathcal{V}_{0}
of
\mathcal{V}
such that
|\mathcal{V}_{0}|\leq\kappa
and
\{\overline{V}:V\in\mathcal{V}_{0}\}
covers
X
. When
aL(X)=\aleph_{0}
, the space
X
is called
almost Lindelöf
.

Recall that a space
X
has
countable discrete cellularity
(cdc) if every discrete family of open sets in
X
is countable. We show in Theorem

that if
X
is a star-cdc space with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 3, then
|X|\leq 2^{\aleph_{0}}
, and in Theorem
that any normal star-cdc space
X
with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2 satisfies
|X|\leq 2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

In 1978, Bell, Ginsburg, and Woods showed in
[
6
]
that
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)\chi(X)}
for any normal
T_{1}
-space
X
, where
\chi(X)
is the character of the space
X
.
It is still an open question of Bell, Ginsburg and Woods if the inequality
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)\chi(X)}
holds true for regular,
T_{1}
-spaces
[
6
]
and even for Tychonoff spaces
[
9
]
.
Therefore, it is interesting to know if there are other classes of spaces for which Bell, Ginsburg and Woods’ inequality is true. Here we mention several such classes of spaces closely related to our results in Section
. For results related to
Bell, Ginsburg, and Woods’ question using topological games see
[
4
]
.

Gotchev proved in
[
16
]
that for any Urysohn space
X
,
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\overline{\Delta}(X)\chi(X)}
. Therefore, if
X
is a space with a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\chi(X)}
. Notice that this inequality improves significantly the
above-mentioned Bell, Ginsburg and Woods’ inequality for the class of normal spaces with regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonals.
(To see that take a discrete space
Y
with cardinality of the continuum
\mathfrak{c}
. Then
\chi(Y)=\omega
,
wL(Y)=\mathfrak{c}
and according to Bell, Ginsburg and Woods’ inequality
|Y|\leq 2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, while Gotchev’s inequality gives
|Y|\leq\mathfrak{c}^{\omega}=\mathfrak{c}
.)

Bella and Carlson in
[
9
]
found more classes of spaces
X
for which Bell, Ginsburg and Woods’ inequality holds true, including locally compact, locally Lindelöf, locally ccc, regular locally normal
T_{1}
-spaces (
[
9
, Theorem 2.12]
),
and also quasiregular or Urysohn spaces with a dense set of points with neighborhoods that are
H
-closed, normal, Lindelöf, or ccc (
[
9
, Corollary 2.7]
).

In
[
6
]
, Bell, Ginsburg, and Woods constructed an example of a zero-dimentional Hausdorff (and therefore Tychonoff) space
Y
such that
|Y|>2^{wL(Y)t(Y)\psi(Y)}
and asked if the inequality
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)t(X)\psi(X)}
is true for every
normal
T_{1}
-space
X
. While this question, in its full generality, is still open, partial answers are known. For example, in
[
9
]
, it was shown that
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)t(X)\psi(X)}
for any
regular
T_{1}
-space with a
\pi
-base whose elements have compact closures. We give an improvement of this result in Theorem
where we show that
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)t(X)\psi_{c}(X)}
for any
Hausdorff space with a
\pi
-base whose elements have compact closures. (Note that in regular spaces the pseudocharacter
\psi(X)
is equal to the closed pseudocharacter
\psi_{c}(X)
).

In Section
we also show that even the stronger inequality
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}
is true when the Hausdorff space
X
is locally
H
-closed (Theorem
) or locally Lindelöf (Theorem
).
As corollaries of these results we obtain refinements of some of the results in
[
9
]
mentioned above similar to Gotchev’s inequality for spaces with regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonals.
For example, while in the paper
[
9
]
it was proved that
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)\psi(X)}
, for any locally compact Hausdorff space, we show in Corollary
that in fact
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\psi(X)}
for such spaces.
Similarly, we prove in Theorem
that if
X
is a locally compact power homogeneous Hausdorff space, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{t(X)}
. This improves the inequality
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)t(X)}
proved in
[
9
]
under the same hypotheses.

In this work we assume all spaces are Hausdorff. See
[
15
]
or
[
18
]
for any undefined notions.

2.
A cardinal inequality for extremally disconnected spaces

We establish bounds for the cardinality of any extremally disconnected space in Corollay
and Corollay
. This is a consequence of the following cardinal inequalities for the cellularity
c(X)
of an extremally disconnected space.
We recall that a family
\mathcal{V}
of non-empty open sets in a space
X
is a local
\pi
-base at a point
x\in X
if for every open neighborhood
U
of
x
there is
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that
V\subset U
. The minimal infinite cardinal
\kappa
such that
for each
x\in X
there is a collection
\{V(\eta,x):\eta<\kappa\}
of non-empty open subsets of
X
which is a local
\pi
-base for
x
is called
\pi
-character
of
X
and is denoted by
\pi\chi(X)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be an extremally disconnected space. Then

(a)

c(X)\leq wL(X)t(X)
and

(b)

c(X)\leq wL(X)\pi\chi(X)
.

Proof.

For (a), let
\kappa=wL(X)t(X)
and for (b) let
\kappa=wL(X)\pi\chi(X)
. In either case suppose
c(X)\geq\kappa^{+}
.
Then there exists a cellular family of cardinality at least
\kappa^{+}
which is contained in a maximal cellular family

\mathcal{C}
. We have
|\mathcal{C}|\geq\kappa^{+}
and
X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}}
.

Let
D=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}}\backslash\bigcup\mathcal{C}
. If
D=\emptyset
, then for every
x\in X
there is
C_{x}\in\mathcal{C}
such that

x\in C_{x}
. Then for each
x\in X
we let
\mathcal{C}_{x}=\{C_{x}\}
. Now let
D\neq\emptyset
and let
x\in D
.

For (a), there exists
A_{x}\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{C}
such that
|A_{x}|\leq\kappa
and
x\in\overline{A_{x}}
. For every
a\in A_{x}
there
exists
C(x,a)\in\mathcal{C}
such that
a\in C(x,a)
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{x}=\{C(x,a):a\in A_{x}\}
. Note that

|\mathcal{C}_{x}|\leq|A_{x}|\leq\kappa
and
x\in\overline{A_{x}}\subseteq\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}
.

For (b), let
\mathcal{P}
be a local
\pi
-base at
x
such that
|\mathcal{P}|\leq\kappa
. Since
X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}}
, for every

U\in\mathcal{P}
there exists
C_{U}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
C_{U}\cap U\neq\varnothing
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{x}=\{C_{U}:U\in\mathcal{P}\}
and note
that
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\kappa
. It is straightfoward to see that
x\in\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}
.

In either case (a) or (b), we see from the above that for every
x\in D
there exists
\mathcal{C}_{x}\in[\mathcal{C}]^{\leq\kappa}

such that
x\in\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}
. As each
\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}
is open in the extremally disconnected space
X
, we have
that
\mathcal{C}\cup\left\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}:x\in D\right\}
is an open cover of
X
. Therefore there exist

\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\mathcal{C}]^{\leq\kappa}
and
B\in[D]^{\leq\kappa}
such that

\bigcup\mathcal{B}\cup\bigcup\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}:x\in B\}
is dense in
X
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{B}\cup\{\mathcal{C}_{x}:x\in B\}
. Note that
|\mathcal{D}|\leq\kappa
. Let
C\in\mathcal{C}\backslash\mathcal{D}
. Then

C\cap\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{B}}\neq\varnothing
or
C\cap\overline{\bigcup_{x\in B}\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}}\neq\varnothing
. If

C\cap\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{B}}\neq\varnothing
, then
C\cap(\bigcup\mathcal{B})\neq\varnothing
. This is a contradiction as
\mathcal{C}
is a pairwise
disjoint family. If
C\cap\overline{\bigcup_{x\in B}\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}}\neq\varnothing
, then

C\cap\left(\bigcup_{x\in B}\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}\right)\neq\varnothing
. Then there exists
x\in B
such that

C\cap\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x}}\neq\varnothing
, and thus
C\cap(\bigcup\mathcal{C}_{x})\neq\varnothing
. This is also a contradiction. Therefore

c(X)\leq\kappa
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

If
X
is extremally disconnected, then
|X|\leq\pi\chi(X)^{wL(X)t(X)\psi_{c}(X)}
.

Proof.

It was shown by Sun in
[
24
]
that if
X
is a Hausdorff space, then

|X|\leq\pi\chi(X)^{c(X)\psi_{c}(X)}
. Using Theorem
(a) we can replace in that inequality
c(X)
with
wL(X)t(X)
to obtain the required inequality.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

If
X
is extremally disconnected, then
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)\pi\chi(X)\psi(X)}
.

Proof.

As
X
is extremally disconnected and Hausdorff, we have that the semiregularization
X_{s}
of
X
is extremally
disconnected and
T_{3}
. (See, for example, 6P(3) in
[
21
]
). It was shown by Šapirovskiĭ in
[
23
]

that
|Y|\leq\pi\chi(Y)^{c(Y)\psi(Y)}
for any
T_{3}
-space
Y
. In addition, it was shown in
[
12
]
that

c(Y)=c(Y_{s})
and
\pi\chi(Y_{s})\leq\pi\chi(Y)
for any Hausdorff space
Y
. Using Theorem
(b), the
aforementioned facts, and that
\psi(Y_{s})\leq\psi(Y)
for any Hausdorff space, we have

\displaystyle|X|=|X_{s}|

\displaystyle\leq\pi\chi(X_{s})^{c(X_{s})\psi(X_{s})}

\displaystyle\leq\pi\chi(X)^{c(X)\psi(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\pi\chi(X)^{wL(X)\pi\chi(X)\psi(X)}

\displaystyle=2^{wL(X)\pi\chi(X)\psi(X)}.

∎

It was shown in
[
13
]
that
|X|\leq 2^{c(X)\pi\chi(X)}
for every power homogeneous Hausdorff space
X
. Using Theorem
(b) above we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
X
is an extremally disconnected, power homogeneous space, then
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)\pi\chi(X)}
.

Recall that a space
X
is
H-closed
if every open cover of
X
has a finite subfamily with dense union. We observe
that it follows from Corollary
that an
H
-closed, extremally disconnected, power homogeneous space has
cardinality at most
2^{\pi\chi(X)}
. However, it was shown in
[
12
, Theorem 3.3]
that an infinite
H
-closed
extremally disconnected space cannot be power homogeneous. This latter result is an extension of a result of
Kunen
[
19
]
that an infinite compact
F
-space is not power homogeneous.

A Tychonoff space is
basically disconnected
if the closure of each of its cozero sets is open. It is clear that every extremally disconnected Tychonoff space is basically disconnected, and that every basically disconnected space is an
F
-space.

Theorem 2.5
.

A weakly Lindelöf basically disconnected Tychonoff space of countable tightness has countable cellularity.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a family
\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
of pairwise disjoint non-empty open sets. Of course, we
may assume that each
U_{\alpha}
is a cozero set. As the collection of cozero sets is closed under countable unions and
X

is basically disconnected, for every
\alpha<\omega_{1}
the set
V_{\alpha}=\overline{\bigcup\{U_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}}
is clopen.
Moreover, the countable tightness of
X
guarantees that even the set
V=\bigcup\{V_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
is clopen.
Thus
V
is weakly Lindelöf as that property is hereditary on regular-closed sets. However, it is straightforward to see that
no countable subfamily of the cover
\{V_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
has dense union in
V
, which is a contradiction.
∎

It is stated in Problem 6L(8) in
[
21
]
that if a Tychonoff space has countable cellularity, then it is an
F
-space if and
only if it is extremally disconnected. As basically disconnected spaces are
F
-spaces, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

A weakly Lindelöf Tychonoff space of countable tightness is basically disconnected if and only if it is extremally disconnected.

3.
Cardinal inequalities for spaces with a (strong)
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2

Given a family of sets
\mathcal{B}
and a set
A
in a space
X
, the star of
\mathcal{B}
around
A
is the set

St(A,\mathcal{B})=\bigcup\{B\in\mathcal{B}:A\cap B\neq\varnothing\}
. If
A=\{a\}
we write
St(a,\mathcal{B})
.

A space
X
has a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal
(
strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal
) provided that there exists a sequence

\{\mathcal{U}_{n}:n<\omega\}
of open covers of
X
such that for every
x\in X
we have

\bigcap\{St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n}):n<\omega\}=\{x\}

\left(\bigcap\{\overline{St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n})}:n<\omega\}=\{x\}\right)
.

A space
X
has a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2
(
strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2
) provided that
there exists a sequence
\{\mathcal{U}_{n}:n<\omega\}
of open covers of
X
such that

\bigcap\{St(St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n}),\mathcal{U}_{n}):n<\omega\}=\{x\}

\left(\bigcap\{\overline{St(St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n}),\mathcal{U}_{n})}:n<\omega\}=\{x\}\right)
, whenever
x\in X
.
Similarly, for every natural number
n
, spaces with
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank
n

(
strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank
n
) are defined .

X
has a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal if the diagonal of
X
is the intersection of countably many closed neighborhoods in

X\times X
. Equivalently,
X
has a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal if there exists a sequence
\{\mathcal{U}_{n}:n<\omega\}
of
open covers of
X
such that if
x,y\in X
,
x\neq y
, then there is
n<\omega
and sets
V_{x},V_{y}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}

containing
x
and
y
, respectively, such that
V_{y}\cap\overline{St(V_{x},\mathcal{U}_{n})}=\emptyset

[
25
]
.

If a space has a strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2, then it has a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2 and a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal. On the other hand, both regular and rank 2
G_{\delta}
-diagonal imply strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal.

The two intermediate notions seems to play a parallel role. For instance, two remarkable facts are:

Theorem 3.1

(Buzyakova,
[
11
]
)
.

A ccc space with a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal has cardinality not exceeding
2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Bella,
[
8
]
)
.

A ccc space with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2 has cardinality not exceeding
2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

The above two results make the following questions very interesting:

Question 3.3
.

(a)
Is the cardinality of an almost Lindelöf space with a regular
G_{\delta}
-diagonal at most
2^{\aleph_{0}}
?

(b)
Is the cardinality of an almost Lindelöf space with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2 at most
2^{\aleph_{0}}
?

In relation to Question
(b) we can prove the following theorem. For its proof we need to introduce some
notation and to recall one well-known result.

The symbol
[X]^{2}
indicates the collection of all two-point subsets of
X
. The countable version of the Erdös-Rado
Theorem is the following statement:

Lemma 3.4
.

If
X
is a set with
|X|>2^{\aleph_{0}}
and
[X]^{2}=\bigcup\{F_{n}:n\in\omega\}
, then there exists an uncountable set

S\subseteq X
and an integer
n_{0}
such that
[S]^{2}\subseteq F_{n_{0}}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

If
X
is an almost Lindelöf space with a strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2, then
|X|\leq 2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\mathcal{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\}
be a sequence of open covers witnessing that
X
has a strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank
2 and suppose that
|X|>2^{\aleph_{0}}
. Put
F_{n}=\{\{x,y\}\in[X]^{2}:y\notin\overline{St(St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n}),\mathcal{U}_{n})\}}
. (Note
that
y\notin\overline{St(St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n}),\mathcal{U}_{n})}
if and only if
x\notin\overline{St(St(y,\mathcal{U}_{n}),\mathcal{U}_{n})}
.)
Since
[X]^{2}=\bigcup\{F_{n}:n<\omega\}
, by Lemma
there is an uncountable set
S\subseteq X
and an integer
n_{0}

such that
[S]^{2}\subseteq F_{n_{0}}
. Let
\mathcal{V}=\{St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n_{0}}):x\in S\}
. It is straightforward to verify that

\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}}\subset\bigcup\{St(St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n_{0}}),\mathcal{U}_{n_{0}}):x\in S\}
. Then

\{St(St(x,\mathcal{U}_{n_{0}}),\mathcal{U}_{n_{0}}):x\in S\}\cup(X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}})
is an open cover of
X
which does not
contain a countable subfamily
\mathcal{V}_{0}
with the property that
X=\bigcup\{\overline{V}:V\in\mathcal{V}_{0}\}
. Since this contradicts the
fact that
X
is almost Lindelöf, the proof is completed.
∎

In relation to the previous theorem we ask the following question:

Question 3.6
.

Is the cardinality of a weakly Lindelöf space with a strong
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2 at most
2^{\aleph_{0}}
?

Recently Gotchev obtained the following result:

Theorem 3.7

(

)
.

A weakly Lindelöf space with a regular

.

The latter result suggests the following:

Question 3.8
.

Is the cardinality of a weakly Lindelöf space with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2 at most
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
?

Here we will show that for the class of normal spaces even a stronger inequality is true (see Corollary
).

Notice that not every space with a

-diagonal of rank 2 has a regular

-diagonal. For example, every
Moore space has a

-diagonal of rank 2

]
, while not every Moore space has a regular

-diagonal

[
17

. Also, by a theorem of McArthur

every pseudocompact space with a regular

Question 3.9

Does there exist a normal space with a
G_{\delta}
-diagonal of rank 2 which does not have a regular

G_{\delta}
-diagonal?

The converse implication remains fully open:

Question 3.10

(
[
3
]
)

Is it true that every space with a regular

We recall that a collection of non-empty open sets in

is a
discrete cellular family
if every

has an open
neighborhood which intersects at most one of the members of the family.
A space

has
countable discrete cellularity
(briefly, cdc) if every discrete family of open sets in
X
is
countable. We give two proofs that every weakly Lindelöf space is cdc.

Lemma 3.11
.

Every weakly Lindelöf space is cdc.

Proof.

First proof:

Let
X
be a weakly Lindelöf space and suppose that there exists a discrete family of open sets

\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is discrete, for every
x\in X
we can find an open
neighborhood
V_{x}
such that
V_{x}
intersects at most one of the members of
\mathcal{U}
. Therefore

\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}}=\bigcup\{\overline{U_{\alpha}}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
, hence
\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}}
is a regular closed subset
of
X
. For every
\alpha<\omega_{1}
and every
x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\setminus U_{\alpha}
we fix a non-empty open set

V_{x}
such that
V_{x}\cap U_{\beta}=\emptyset
for every
\beta\neq\alpha
. Let
\mathcal{V}
be the family of all such sets

V_{x}
. Then
\mathcal{U}\cup\mathcal{V}
is an open cover of
\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}}
which clearly does not have a countable
subcolection which is dense in
\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}}
– contradiction.

Second proof:
Let
X
be a weakly Lindelöf space and suppose that there exists a discrete family of open sets

\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is discrete,

\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}}=\bigcup\{\overline{U_{\alpha}}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
. Even more, if

\mathcal{U}_{\beta}=\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1},\alpha\neq\beta\}
, then

\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\beta}}=\bigcup\{\overline{U_{\alpha}}:\alpha<\omega_{1},\alpha\neq\beta\}
and

\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\beta}}\cap\overline{U_{\beta}}=\emptyset
.
Then for each
\beta<\omega_{1}
, the set

is open and contains
\overline{U_{\beta}}
.
Thus
\{X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\beta}}:\beta<\omega_{1}\}
is an open cover of
X
which clearly does not have a
countable subcolection which is dense in
X
– contradiction.
∎

Now let

be a topological space and
\kappa=wL(X)

Let

Using the cardinal function

4.
A few improved cardinality bounds

We begin with some significant improvements of the cardinality bounds given in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
9
, Theorem 2.12]
)
.

Let
X
be a space which is locally
H
-closed, locally Lindelöf, locally ccc, or regular and locally normal.
Then
|X|\leq 2^{\chi(X)wL(X)}
.

As in
[
9
]
, for a property
\mathcal{P}
of a space X, we say X is
locally

\mathcal{P}
if every point in X has a neighborhood with property
\mathcal{P}
.

In the proofs of some of the following theorems we will use the fact that if
X
is a Hausdorff space, then

|X|\leq d(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}
(see
[
7
]
).

Theorem 4.2
.

If
X
is locally
H
-closed, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}
.

Proof.

For every
x\in X
there exists an open set
U_{x}
such that
x\in U_{x}
and
\overline{U}_{x}
is
H
-closed. It follows from
the Dow-Porter bound
[
14
]
for
H
-closed spaces that for every
x\in X
,
|U_{x}|\leq|\overline{U}_{x}|\leq 2^{\psi_{c}(\overline{U}_{x})}\leq 2^{\psi_{c}(X)}
.
Clearly
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{x}:x\in X\}
is an open cover of
X
. Then there exists
\mathcal{V}\in[\mathcal{U}]^{\leq wL(X)}
such that

X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}}
. Thus
\bigcup\mathcal{V}
is dense in
X
and
|\bigcup\mathcal{V}|\leq wL(X)\cdot 2^{\psi_{c}(X)}\leq wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)}
. As
X
is Hausdorff, we have

\displaystyle|X|\leq d(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}\leq|\bigcup\mathcal{V}|^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)}\right)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}

\displaystyle=wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If
X
is locally
H
-closed, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\chi(X)}
.

If
X
is locally compact, we have the following corollary, which improves Corollary 2.13 in
[
9
]
. Recall that

\chi(X)=\psi(X)=\psi_{c}(X)
for locally compact spaces and that
t(X)\leq\chi(X)
for every space
X
.

Corollary 4.4
.

If
X
is locally compact, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\psi(X)}=wL(X)^{\chi(X)}
.

The following two theorems have proofs that follow a similar structure.

Theorem 4.5
.

If
X
is locally Lindelöf, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}
.

Proof.

For every
x\in X
fix a neighborhood
F_{x}
of
x
which is Lindelöf. Since
X
is Hausdorff and
\psi(X)
and
t(X)
are monotone functions, for every
x\in X
we have

|\mathrm{Int}(F_{x})|\leq|F_{x}|\leq 2^{L(F_{x})\psi(F_{x})t(F_{x})}\leq 2^{\psi(X)t(X)}\leq 2^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}
.
Clearly
\mathcal{U}=\{\mathrm{Int}(F_{x}):x\in X\}
is an open cover of
X
.
Hence, there exists
\mathcal{V}\in[\mathcal{U}]^{\leq wL(X)}
such that
X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}}
.
Thus
\bigcup\mathcal{V}
is dense in
X
and
|\bigcup\mathcal{V}|\leq wL(X)\cdot 2^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}\leq wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}
. As
X
is Hausdorff, we have

\displaystyle|X|\leq d(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}\leq|\bigcup\mathcal{V}|^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}\right)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}

\displaystyle=wL(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}.

∎

Corollary 4.6
.

If
X
is locally Lindelöf, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\chi(X)}
.

In the proof of the following theorem we use Sun’s inequality that if
X
is a Hausdorff space, then

|X|\leq\pi\chi(X)^{c(X)\psi_{c}(X)}
(see
[
24
]
).

Theorem 4.7
.

If
X
is locally ccc, then

|X|\leq(wL(X)\pi\chi(X))^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}.

Proof.

For every
x\in X
fix a neighborhood
F_{x}
of
x
which is ccc.
Since
X
is Hausdorff,
\psi_{c}(X)
is monotone, and
c(X)
and
\pi\chi(X)
are monotone with respect to open sets, for every
x\in X
we have

|\mathrm{Int}(F_{x})|\leq\pi\chi(\mathrm{Int}(F_{x}))^{c(\mathrm{Int}(F_{x}))\psi_{c}(\mathrm{Int}(F_{x}))}\leq\pi\chi(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)}
.
Clearly
\mathcal{U}=\{\mathrm{Int}(F_{x}):x\in X\}
is an open cover of
X
.
Hence, there exists
\mathcal{V}\in[\mathcal{U}]^{\leq wL(X)}
such that
X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}}
.
Thus
\bigcup\mathcal{V}
is dense in
X
and
|\bigcup\mathcal{V}|\leq wL(X)\cdot\pi\chi(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)}\leq(wL(X)\pi\chi(X))^{\psi_{c}(X)}
. As
X
is Hausdorff, we have

\displaystyle|X|\leq d(X)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}\leq|\bigcup\mathcal{V}|^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(wL(X)\pi\chi(X))^{\psi_{c}(X)}\right)^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}

\displaystyle=(wL(X)\pi\chi(X))^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)}.

∎

Corollary 4.8
.

If
X
is locally ccc, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{\chi(X)}
.

In the power homogeneous (PH) setting, it was shown in Corollary 3.8 in
[
9
]
that if
X
is locally compact and PH, then
|X|\leq 2^{wL(X)t(X)}
, an extension of De la Vega’s Theorem for compact homogeneous spaces. The following
theorem improves that corollary.

Theorem 4.9
.

If
X
is a locally compact, power homogeneous space, then
|X|\leq wL(X)^{t(X)}
.

Proof.

For every
x\in X
there exists an open set
U_{x}
such that
x\in U_{x}
and
\overline{U}_{x}
is compact. Using Theorem 3.6 from
[
9
]
,
which states that if
X
is a power homogeneous space and
U\subseteq X
is a non-empty open set, then
|U|\leq 2^{L(\overline{U})t(X)pct(X)}
,
we conclude that for each
x\in X
,
|U_{x}|\leq 2^{t(X)}
. (Note that
pct(X)
is countable as
X
is locally
compact). Then
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{x}:x\in X\}
is an open cover of
X
. Hence, there exists
\mathcal{V}\in[\mathcal{U}]^{\leq wL(X)}

such that
X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}}
. Thus
\bigcup\mathcal{V}
is dense in
X
and

|\bigcup\mathcal{V}|\leq wL(X)\cdot 2^{t(X)}\leq wL(X)^{t(X)}
.

It was shown by Ridderbos in
[
22
]
that the cardinality of a power homogeneous Hausdorff space is at most

d(X)^{\pi\chi(X)}
. After noting that in our case
\pi\chi(X)\leq t(X)pct(X)=t(X)
, we conclude that

|X|\leq d(X)^{\pi\chi(X)}\leq|\bigcup\mathcal{V}|^{t(X)}\leq\left(wL(X)^{t(X)}\right)^{t(X)}=wL(X)^{t(X)}.

∎

We note that the proof of the above theorem is in fact simpler than the proof of Corollary 3.8 in
[
9
]
. The proof of
that corollary there fundamentally relies on the main theorem in that paper (Theorem 2.3). However, the proof above does
not.

Our next theorem shows that Bella–Carlson’s inequality
|X|\leq 2^{\psi(X)t(X)wL(X)}
, which was proved in
[
9
]

for every regular
T_{1}
-space
X
with a
\pi
-base whose elements have compact closures, is also valid for Hausdorff
spaces satisfying the same condition but after replacing in it
\psi(X)
with
\psi_{c}(X)
. (We recall that a
\pi
-base

for a space
X
is a collection
\mathcal{V}
of non-empty open sets in
X
such that if
U
is any non-empty open set in
X
,
then there exists
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that
V\subset U
.)

First we make the following observation.

Lemma 4.10
.

If
X
is a space with a
\pi
-base
\mathcal{B}
whose elements have compact closures and
F\subsetneq X
is
closed, then there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\overline{B}\cap F=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Since
F
is closed and
X\setminus F\neq\emptyset
, there is
B_{1}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B_{1}\subseteq X\setminus F
.
If
\overline{B_{1}}\cap F=\emptyset
, then
B=B_{1}
. If
\overline{B_{1}}\cap F\neq\emptyset
, then clearly

\overline{B_{1}}\cap F\subset\overline{B_{1}}\setminus B_{1}
. Let
L=\overline{B_{1}}\setminus B_{1}
and
y\in B_{1}
.
Then
L
is closed in
\overline{B_{1}}
and since

\overline{B_{1}}
is compact and Hausdorff there is an open set
B_{2}
in
\overline{B_{1}}
such that
y\in B_{2}
and

\overline{B_{2}}\cap L=\emptyset
. Then
B_{2}\subset B_{1}
and since
B_{1}
is open in
X
,
B_{2}
is also open in
X
.
Since
\mathcal{B}
is a
\pi
-base in
X
, there is a non-empty open set
B_{3}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B_{3}\subset B_{2}
. Clearly,

\overline{B_{3}}\cap F=\emptyset
and we can choose
B=B_{3}
.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

If
X
is a space with a
\pi
-base whose elements have compact closures, then

|X|\leq 2^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)wL(X)}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa=\psi_{c}(X)t(X)wL(X)
and let
\mathcal{B}
be a
\pi
-base of non-empty open sets with compact closures.
Notice that for each
B\in\mathcal{B}
if
\psi(\overline{B})
is the pseudocharacter of the space
\overline{B}
, then we have

\psi(\overline{B})\leq\psi_{c}(X)
and since
\overline{B}
is compact and Hausdorff, we have

\overline{B}\leq 2^{\psi(\overline{B})}\leq 2^{\kappa}
. Since
\psi_{c}(X)\leq\kappa
, for each
x\in X
we can fix a collection
\mathcal{V}_{x}

of open neighborhoods of
x
such that
|\mathcal{V}_{x}|\leq\kappa
and
\bigcap\{\overline{V}:V\in\mathcal{V}_{x}\}=\{x\}
. Without
loss of generality we may assume that each
\mathcal{V}_{x}
is closed under finite intersections.

We will construct by transfinite reqursion a non-decreasing chain of open sets
\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}
such that

(1)

\overline{U_{\alpha}}\leq 2^{\kappa}
for every
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
, and

(2)

if
X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\neq\emptyset
for some

\mathcal{M}\in[\bigcup\{\mathcal{V}_{x}:x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\}]^{\leq\kappa}
,
then there is
B_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B_{\mathcal{M}}\subset U_{\alpha+1}\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}
.

Let
B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}
be arbitrary. We set
U_{0}=B_{0}
. Then
|\overline{U_{0}}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
.
If
\beta=\alpha+1
, for some
\alpha
, then for every

\mathcal{M}\in[\bigcup\{\mathcal{V}_{x}:x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\}]^{\leq\kappa}
such that
X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\neq\emptyset
,
we choose
B_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B_{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}
.
We define
U_{\beta}=U_{\alpha}\cup\bigcup\{B_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathcal{M}\in[\bigcup\{\mathcal{V}_{x}:x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\}]^{\leq\kappa},X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\neq\emptyset\}
. Therefore, since
X
is Hausdorff, we have

|\overline{U_{\beta}}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
. If
\beta<\kappa^{+}
is a limit ordinal we let

U_{\beta}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\beta}U_{\alpha}
. Then clearly
|U_{\beta}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
, hence

|\overline{U_{\beta}}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
.

Let
F=\bigcup\{\overline{U_{\alpha}}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}
. Then
|F|\leq 2^{\kappa}
.
Since
t(X)\leq\kappa
,
F
is closed and therefore
F=\overline{\bigcup\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}}
.
Thus,
F
is a regular-closed set.

We will show that
X=F
. Suppose that
X\neq F
. Since
F
is closed and
\mathcal{B}
is a
\pi
-base, there is
B\in\mathcal{B}

such that
B\subset X\setminus F
and
\overline{B}\cap F=\emptyset
(Lemma
).
Then for every
x\in F
and
y\in\overline{B}
there is

V_{x}(y)\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
such that
y\notin\overline{V_{x}(y)}
. Therefore, using the compactness of
\overline{B}
and the fact that each

\mathcal{V}_{x}
is closed under finite intersections, for every
x\in F
we can find

V_{x}\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
such that
\overline{V_{x}}\cap\overline{B}=\emptyset
; hence
\overline{B}\cap V_{x}=\emptyset
.
Clearly
\{V_{x}:x\in F\}
is an open cover of
F
. Since
wL(X)
is monotone with respect to regular-closed sets, there
exists
\mathcal{M}\in\{V_{x}:x\in F\}^{\leq\kappa}
such that

. Then there
exists
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
such that
\mathcal{M}\in[\bigcup\{\mathcal{V}_{x}:x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\}]^{\leq\kappa}
.
As
\overline{B}\cap\bigcup\mathcal{M}=\emptyset
it follows that
B\subset X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}
, hence

X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\neq\emptyset
. Thus, there exists
B_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathcal{B}
such that

\emptyset\neq B_{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq U_{\alpha+1}\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\subseteq F\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}=\emptyset
.
Since this is a contradiction, we conclude that
X=F
and the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

In relation to spaces with
\pi
-bases whose elements have compact closures we can make also the following observation for which we need to
recall the following definitions: The
\theta
-closure
of a set
A
in a space
X
, denoted by
\mathrm{cl}_{\theta}(A)
, is the set of all points

x\in X
such that for every open neighborhood
U
of
x
we have
\overline{U}\cap A\neq\emptyset
;
A
is
called
\theta
-dense
in

if
\mathrm{cl}_{\theta}(A)=X
; and
the
\theta
-density of a space
X
is
d_{\theta}(X)=\min\{|A|:A\subset X,\mathrm{cl}_{\theta}(A)=X\}
.

Theorem 4.16
.

If
X
is a space with a
\pi
-base whose elements have compact closures, then
d(X)=d_{\theta}(X)
.

Proof.

If
D\subseteq X
is dense in
X
, then
D
is
\theta
-dense. Therefore
d_{\theta}(X)\leq d(X)
.

Now let
D
be
\theta
-dense in
X
and suppose that
D
is not dense in
X
. Then
\overline{D}\neq X
. Since
\overline{D}
is a
closed subset of
X
, if follows from Lemma
that there exists element
B
of the
\pi
-base such that

\overline{B}\cap\overline{D}=\emptyset
. If
x\in B
, then
x\notin\mathrm{cl}_{\theta}(D)
– contradiction. Thus
D
is dense in
X

and therefore

.
∎

Since
H
-closedness is a natural generalization of compactness, the following question is natural.

Question 4.17
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space with a
\pi
-base
\mathcal{B}
such that
\overline{B}
is
H
-closed for every
B\in\mathcal{B}
. Is it
true that
|X|\leq 2^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)wL(X)}
?

In relation to the above question we can prove the following:

Theorem 4.18
.

If
X
is a Hausdorff space with a
\pi
-base whose elements have
H
-closed closures, then

d_{\theta}(X)\leq 2^{\psi_{c}(X)t(X)wL(X)}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa=\psi_{c}(X)t(X)wL(X)
and let
\mathcal{B}
be a
\pi
-base of non-empty open sets with
H
-closed closures.
Notice that for each
B\in\mathcal{B}
if
\psi_{c}(\overline{B})
is the closed pseudocharacter of the space
\overline{B}
and since
\overline{B}

is
H
-closed, we have
\overline{B}\leq 2^{\psi_{c}(\overline{B})}\leq 2^{\kappa}
. Since
\psi_{c}(X)\leq\kappa
, for each
x\in X
we can fix a
collection
\mathcal{V}_{x}
of open neighborhoods of
x
such that
|\mathcal{V}_{x}|\leq\kappa
and

\bigcap\{\overline{V}:V\in\mathcal{V}_{x}\}=\{x\}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that each
\mathcal{V}_{x}
is closed under
finite intersections.

We will construct by transfinite recursion a non-decreasing chain of open sets
\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}
such that

(1)

\overline{U_{\alpha}}\leq 2^{\kappa}
for every
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
, and

(2)

if
X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\neq\emptyset
for some

\mathcal{M}\in[\bigcup\{\mathcal{V}_{x}:x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\}]^{\leq\kappa}
,
then there is
B_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B_{\mathcal{M}}\subset U_{\alpha+1}\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}
.

Let
B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}
be arbitrary. We set
U_{0}=B_{0}
. Then
|\overline{U_{0}}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
.
If
\beta=\alpha+1
, for some
\alpha
, then for every

\mathcal{M}\in[\bigcup\{\mathcal{V}_{x}:x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\}]^{\leq\kappa}
such that
X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\neq\emptyset
,
we choose
B_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B_{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}
.
We define
U_{\beta}=U_{\alpha}\cup\bigcup\{B_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathcal{M}\in[\bigcup\{\mathcal{V}_{x}:x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\}]^{\leq\kappa},X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\neq\emptyset\}
. Therefore, since
X
is Hausdorff, we have

|\overline{U_{\beta}}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
. If
\beta<\kappa^{+}
is a limit ordinal we let

U_{\beta}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\beta}U_{\alpha}
. Then clearly
|U_{\beta}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
, hence

|\overline{U_{\beta}}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
.

Let
F=\bigcup\{\overline{U_{\alpha}}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}
. Then
|F|\leq 2^{\kappa}
.
Since
t(X)\leq\kappa
,
F
is closed and therefore
F=\overline{\bigcup\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}}
.
Thus,
F
is a regular-closed set.

We will show that
X=\mathrm{cl}_{\theta}(F)
. Suppose that
X\neq\mathrm{cl}_{\theta}(F)
and let

y_{0}\in X\setminus\mathrm{cl}_{\theta}(F)
. Then there is a neighborhood
U
of
y_{0}
such that

\overline{U}\cap F=\emptyset
and since
\mathcal{B}
is a
\pi
-base, there is
B\in\mathcal{B}

such that
B\subset U
, hence
\overline{B}\cap F=\emptyset
.
Then for every
x\in F
and
y\in\overline{B}
there is

V_{x}(y)\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
such that
y\notin\overline{V_{x}(y)}
. Therefore, using the
H
-closedness of
\overline{B}
and the fact that each

\mathcal{V}_{x}
is closed under finite intersections, for every
x\in F
we can find

V_{x}\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
such that
\overline{V_{x}}\cap\overline{B}=\emptyset
; hence
\overline{B}\cap V_{x}=\emptyset
.
Clearly
\{V_{x}:x\in F\}
is an open cover of
F
. Since
wL(X)
is monotone with respect to regular-closed sets, there
exists
\mathcal{M}\in\{V_{x}:x\in F\}^{\leq\kappa}
such that
F\subseteq\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}
. Then there
exists
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
such that
\mathcal{M}\in[\bigcup\{\mathcal{V}_{x}:x\in\overline{U_{\alpha}}\}]^{\leq\kappa}
.
As
\overline{B}\cap\bigcup\mathcal{M}=\emptyset
it follows that
B\subset X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}
, hence

X\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\neq\emptyset
. Thus, there exists
B_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathcal{B}
such that

\emptyset\neq B_{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq U_{\alpha+1}\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}\subseteq F\setminus\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{M}}=\emptyset
.
Since this is a contradiction, we conclude that
X=\mathrm{cl}_{\theta}(F)
and the proof is completed.
∎

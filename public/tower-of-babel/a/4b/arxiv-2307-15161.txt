Graph morphisms and exhaustion of curve graphs of low-genus surfaces
Jesús Hernández Hernández

Abstract
This work is the extension of the results by the author in [ 7 ] and [ 6 ] for low-genus surfaces. Let S S be an orientable, connected surface of finite topological type, with genus g ≤ 2 g\leq 2 , empty boundary, and complexity at least 2 2 ; as a complement of the results of [ 6 ] , we prove that any graph endomorphism of the curve graph of S S is actually an automorphism. Also, as a complement of the results in [ 6 ] we prove that under mild conditions on the complexity of the underlying surfaces any graph morphism between curve graphs is induced by a homeomorphism of the surfaces. To prove these results, we construct a finite subgraph whose union of iterated rigid expansions is the curve graph 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) \mathcal{C}(S) . The sets constructed, and the method of rigid expansion, are closely related to Aramayona and Leiniger’s finite rigid sets in [ 2 ] . Similarly to [ 7 ] , a consequence of our proof is that Aramayona and Leininger’s rigid set also exhausts the curve graph via rigid expansions, and the combinatorial rigidity results follow as an immediate consequence, based on the results in [ 6 ] . 2020 AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 57K20. Keywords: Curve graph, low-genus surface, rigid expansions, graph morphisms.



Introduction

In this work we suppose
S_{g,n}
is an orientable surface of finte topological type, with genus
g\geq 0
,
n\geq 0
punctures, and empty boundary. The
(extended) mapping class group of

S_{g,n}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(S_{g,n})
is the group of isotopy classes of self-homeomorphisms of
S_{g,n}
.

In 1979 (see
[
5
]
) Harvey introduced what is now one of the main tools for the study of
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(S_{g,n})
: the
curve graph of

S_{g,n}
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
, is the simplicial graph whose vertices are isotopy classes of (essential simple closed) curves on
S_{g,n}
, where two vertices span an edge if the corresponding isotopy classes are different and have disjoint representatives.

There is a natural action of
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(S_{g,n})
on
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
by simplicial automorphisms. It is a well-known result by Ivanov (see
[
13
]
), Korkmaz (see
[
14
]
), and Luo (see
[
15
]
), that if
(g,n)\neq(1,2)
, then all the simplicial automorphisms of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
are induced by homeomorphisms of
S_{g,n}
.

This result was later extended by Schackleton (see
[
16
]
) to simplicial maps that are locally injective (recall that a simplicial map is locally injective if its restriction to the star of any vertex, is injective).

Afterwards, Aramayona and Leininger proved in
[
1
]
that there exist finite subgraphs of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
with this same property, i.e. there exists a finite subgraph
\mathfrak{X}(S_{g,n})<\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
, such that all locally injective maps
\mathfrak{X}(S_{g,n})\to\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
are restrictions to
\mathfrak{X}(S_{g,n})
of an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
. Additionally, in
[
2
]
they call subgraphs with this property
rigid
, and prove (using geometric methods) that there exists an (set-theoretically) increasing sequence of rigid subgraphs of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
whose union is
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
.

Note that this last result is
not trivial
since not any supergraph of a rigid subgraph is rigid.

Later on, we reproved this result (see
[
7
]
) using combinatorial methods (first introduced in
[
2
]
). The main technique used in
[
7
]
is called
rigid expansions
:

In the curve graph, a curve
\alpha
is
uniquely determined
by a set of curves
A
if
\alpha\notin A
and
\alpha
is the unique curve on
S_{g,n}
that is disjoint from every element in
A
. Given a subgraph
Y<\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
, the
first rigid expansion of

Y
, denoted by
Y^{1}
, is the subgraph induced by all the curves in
Y
along with all the curves uniquely determined by subsets of the vertex set of
Y
. The
n
-th rigid expansion of

Y
(denoted by
Y^{n}
) is defined recursively, and we define
Y^{\omega}
as the union of
Y^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. See Section
1
for more details.

In
[
2
]
it is proved that if
X
is rigid, then
X^{n}
is rigid for all
n\geq 1
, and in
[
7
]
we proved that if
g\geq 3
, then
\mathfrak{X}(S_{g,n})^{\omega}=\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
. The majority of this work is about extending this result to all surfaces with
\kappa(S_{g,n})\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=3g-3+n\geq 2
. To do this, we first prove there exists a convenient finite subgraph
\mathscr{Y}(S_{g,n})
that exhausts the curve graph via rigid expansions.

Theorem A
.

Let
S_{g,n}
be an orientable, connected surface of finite topological type, with genus
g\leq 2
,
n\geq 0
punctures, and empty boundary. If
\kappa(S_{g,n})\geq 2
, then there exists a finite subgraph
\mathscr{Y}(S_{g,n})
of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
whose union of iterated rigid expansions is equal to
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
.

Afterwards, we use this result to prove that
\mathfrak{X}(S_{g,n})
also exhausts the curve graph, with the methods varying depending on the genus of the surface.

Theorem B
.

Let
S_{g,n}
be an orientable, connected surface of finite topological type and empty boundary. If
\kappa(S_{g,n})\geq 2
, then
\mathfrak{X}(S_{g,n})^{\omega}=\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
.

These results for the case
g\geq 3
, were used by the author in
[
6
]
to prove that if
S_{1}=S_{g_{1},n_{1}}
,
S_{2}=S_{g_{2},n_{2}}
with
g_{1}\geq 3
and the complexity of
S_{2}
bounded above by the complexity of
S_{1}
, then any graph morphism between
\mathcal{C}(S_{1})
and
\mathcal{C}(S_{2})
is induced by a homeomorphism
S_{1}\rightarrow S_{2}
.

Recently, in
[
12
]
and
[
11
]
, Irmak extended this result for the case of endomorphisms, and proved that any endomorphism of the curve graph of
S_{g,n}
with
\kappa(S_{g,n})\geq 2
is induced by a homeomorphism of
S_{g,n}
.

In Section
5
we give a short proof of this result using Theorem
B
and the results from
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
.

Theorem C
.

Let
S_{g,n}
be an orientable, connected surface of finite topological type, with empty boundary, and
\kappa(S_{g,n})\geq 2
. Let also
\varphi:\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
be a graph (endo)morphism. Then
\varphi
is an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
.

Also in Section
5
we give an extension of the results in
[
16
]
and
[
6
]
, proving that with a restriction on the complexity of surfaces
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, and with
S_{1}\neq S_{0,7}
, then any graph morphism between the respective curve graphs, is induced by a homeomorphism between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
.

Theorem D
.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be two orientable, connected surfaces of finite topological type, with empty boundary, such that
\kappa(S_{1})\geq\kappa(S_{2})\geq 2
, and
S_{1}
is not homeomorphic to a 7-punctured sphere. Let also
\varphi:\mathcal{C}(S_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(S_{2})
be a graph morphism. Then,
S_{1}
is homeomorphic to
S_{2}
and
\varphi
is induced by a homeomorphism
S_{1}\to S_{2}
.

Note that, due to Theorem
C
, it is sufficient to prove topological rigidity, i.e. under the hypothesis of Theorem
D
,
S_{1}
is homeomorphic to
S_{2}
(see Theorem
5.4
).

Also, the lower bound on the complexity of Theorem
C
is sharp. Indeed, if
\kappa(S_{g,n})=1
, there exist infinitely many graph endomorphisms of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
that are surjective, but are NOT injective; thus, they cannot be induced by an automorphism.

As well, the lower bound on the complexity of Theorem
D
is also sharp. Indeed,
\mathcal{C}(S_{0,5})
and
\mathcal{C}(S_{0,6})
are isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}(S_{1,2})
and
\mathcal{C}(S_{2,0})
, respectively.

1
Preliminaries

Suppose
S_{g,n}
is an orientable, connected surface of finite topological type with empty boundary, genus
g\leq 2
,
n\geq 0
punctures, and such that its complexity
\kappa(S_{g,n})=3g-3+n
is at least
3
. The
mapping class group of

S_{g,n}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g,n})
, is the group of all orientation-preserving self-homeomorphisms of
S_{g,n}
.

In this work, a
curve on

S_{g,n}
is a topological embedding of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
into the surface. We often abuse notation and call “curve” the embedding, its image on
S_{g,n}
, or its isotopy class. The meaning is clear with the context.

A curve on
S_{g,n}
is
essential
if it is neither homotopic to a point, nor to the boundary curve of a neighbourhood of a puncture. Hereafter we assume every curve to be essential unless otherwise stated. See Figure
1
for examples.

The curves on
S_{g,n}
can be classified as follows: A curve
\alpha
on
S_{g,n}
is
separating
if
S_{g,n}\backslash\{\alpha\}
is disconnected, and it is
non-separating
otherwise. A special case of a separating curve is when
S_{g,n}\backslash\{\alpha\}
has a connected component homeomorphic to
S_{0,3}
, in which case we say
\alpha
is an
outer curve
. See Figure
1
for examples.

Figure 1:
Examples of curves on surfaces. The red curves are non-essential, while the rest are essential. The green curves are outer curves, the orange curves are separating non-outer curves, and the blue curve is non-separating.

Note that there exist non-separating curves on
S_{g,n}
if and only if
S_{g,n}
has positive genus.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
two (isotopy classes of) curves on
S_{g,n}
. The
(geometric) intersection number of

\alpha

and

\beta
, denoted by
i(\alpha,\beta)
, is defined as follows:

i(\alpha,\beta)=\min\{|a\cap b|:a\in\alpha,b\in\beta\}.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two curves on
S_{g,n}
. In this work, we say that
\alpha
is
disjoint
from
\beta
if
i(\alpha,\beta)=0

and

\alpha\neq\beta
.

The
curve graph of

S_{g,n}
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
, is the abstract simplicial graph whose vertices are the isotopy classes of essential curves on
S_{g,n}
, and two vertices span an edge if the corresponding curves are disjoint. We often abuse notation and denote by
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
both the curve graph and its vertex set.

1.1
Rigid expansions

In
[
6
]
we gave a general description for the concept of a rigid subgraph and rigid expansions of abstract simplicial graphs. Here however, we give the definitions focused on the case of the curve graph.

Given a curve
\alpha
on
S_{g,n}
, we define the set
\mathrm{adj}(\alpha)
as the set of all curves on
S
that are disjoint from
\alpha
. Note that
\alpha\notin\mathrm{adj}(\alpha)
.

We say a curve
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
is
uniquely determined by

A\subset\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
, denoted by
\alpha=\langle A\rangle
, if
\alpha
is the unique curve on
S_{g,n}
that is disjoint from every element in
A
, i.e.

\{\alpha\}=\bigcap_{\gamma\in A}\mathrm{adj}(\gamma).

See Figure
2
for examples.

Figure 2:
In both examples the blue curve is uniquely determined by the set of red curves.

Let
Y<\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
be a induced subgraph of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
, and let
V(Y)
denote its vertex set. We define the
first rigid expansion of

Y
, denoted by
Y^{1}
as the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
whose vertex set if the following:

V(Y^{1})\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=V(Y)\cup\{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n}):\alpha=\langle A\rangle,\text{ for some }A\subset V(Y)\}.

We then define the
(n+1)
-th rigid expansion as
(Y^{n})^{1}
, and
Y^{\omega}
as the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
induced by the following vertex set:

V(Y^{\omega})\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}V(Y^{n}).

Recall that a graph morphism between simplicial graphs is a map between the corresponding vertex sets that also maps edges to edges.

The following theorem is Theorem B in
[
6
]
for the special case of the curve graph, using the terminology here presented:

Theorem 1.1

(B in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
Y<\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
be an induced subgraph, and
\varphi:Y^{\omega}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
be a graph morphism such that there exists an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
(if
(g,n)\neq(1,2)
it is a possibly orientation-reversing homeomorphism of
S_{g,n}
)
h:\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
with
h|_{Y}=\varphi|_{Y}
. Then,
h|_{Y^{\omega}}=\varphi
.

Now, given a curve
\alpha
on
S_{g,n}
, we define the star of
\alpha
, denoted by
\mathrm{star}(\alpha)
, as the subgraph whose vertex set is
\{\alpha\}\cup\mathrm{adj}(\alpha)
, and the edges are those from
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
that have
\alpha
as one of its endpoints.

A locally injective map
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
is a simplicial map whose restriction to
\mathrm{star}(\alpha)
is injective for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
.

Now, a induced subgraph
X<\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
is called
rigid
if every locally injective map from
X
to
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
is induced by an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
(again, if
(g,n)\neq(1,2)
it becomes a homeomorphism of
S_{g,n}
).

Note that not every supergraph of a rigid subgraph is rigid (see Proposition 3.2 in
[
2
]
). However, any rigid expansion of a rigid subgraph is rigid (see Proposition 3.5 in
[
2
]
).

The following corollary is Corollary C in
[
6
]
for the special case of the curve graph, and it comes as a direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
and the definition of rigidity:

Corollary 1.2

(C in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
X<\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
be a rigid subgraph, and
\varphi:X^{\omega}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
be an edge-preserving map such that
\varphi|_{X}
is locally injective. Then
\varphi
is the restriction to
X^{\omega}
of an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}(S_{g,n})
(if
(g,n)\neq(1,2)
, it is a homeomorphism of
S_{g,n}
).

Lemma 2.2

are elements in
\mathscr{Y}(S)^{\omega}
; then we argue that any curve on
S
is uniquely determined by a finite set of non-separating curves and outer curves, thus obtaining the desired result.

First part:
Let
\gamma
be either an outer curve or a non-separating curve on
S
. Recall that all outer curves on
S
have the same topological type, and it is the same case for all non-separating curves. Then, there exists a curve
\delta\in\mathscr{Y}(S)
and a homeomorphism
h\in\mathrm{Mod}(S)
such that
h(\delta)=\gamma
. Since
\mathscr{G}
is a symmetric generating set of
\mathrm{Mod}(S)
, there exist elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in\mathscr{G}
and powers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 0
such that

f_{1}^{n_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{k}^{n_{k}}(\delta)=\gamma.

By an iterated use of Lemma
3.2
, this implies that
\gamma\in\mathscr{Y}(S)^{6(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k})}
. Thus, every outer curve and every non-separating curve on
S
is an element of
\mathscr{Y}(S)^{\omega}
.

Second part:
Let
\gamma
be a separating curve on
S
that is not an outer curve. The topological type of
\gamma
is then determined the punctures it bounds. Thus, up to homeomorphism,
\gamma
is as in the example on Figure
21
(right). Then, there exist finite sets
N
and
O
of non-separating and outer curves respectively, that uniquely determine
\gamma
. See Figure
21
for an example. The first part of this proof implies that there exists
k\geq 0
such that
N\cup O\subset\mathscr{Y}(S)^{k}
. Thus,
\gamma\in\mathscr{Y}(S)^{k+1}
and therefore every curve on
S
that is neither an outer curve nor a non-separating curve, is also an element of
\mathscr{Y}(S)^{\omega}
.

In this section we prove Theorems

To prove Theorems

and

, we first need the following definitions and lemmas.

Let

be such that
\kappa(S)=3g-3+n\geq 2

to a complete subgraph. Note that

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
S_{1}=S_{g_{1},n_{1}}
and
S_{2}=S_{g_{2},n_{2}}
be such that

, and let
\varphi:\mathcal{C}(S_{1})\to\mathcal{C}(S_{2})

maps multicurves to multicurves of the same cardinality. Moreover,
\kappa(S_{1})\leq\kappa(S_{2})

Proof.

The first part follows directly from
\varphi
being a graph morphism, and a multicurve
M
being a complete graph in
\mathcal{C}(S_{1})
. The second part follows from a pants decomposition

of

having maximal cardinality, and the first part of the lemma.
∎

Remark 5.2

Note that this lemma implies that if

S_{1}
would then be mapped to pants decompositions of
S_{2}

Let
\alpha

\alpha

Now, let

(respectively

[
8
]
,
[
10
]

[

[

Let

. This implies that every
\alpha_{i}
with
i\neq 0
is mapped to an essential curve in
A
(that is not a boundary curve); moreover, since for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
we have that
\alpha_{i}
is a non-separating curve, it follows that
f(\alpha_{i})
cannot be a separating curve of
A
. Thus, for all
i\neq 0
,
f(\alpha_{i})
and “
f(\alpha_{0})
” bound a
k
-punctured annulus in
A
for some
k>0
. Hence (up to relabelling), for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
,
f(\alpha_{i})
and
f(\alpha_{i+1})
bound a once-punctured annulus. This implies that (up to relabelling) for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
,
\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}\}
is a peripheral pair, and then
P
is (up to homeomorphism) the pants decomposition from Figure
52
, for which
\mathcal{A}(P)
is a cycle graph.

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

Since this theorem was already proved by the author in the case
g_{1}\geq 3
(Lemma 3.16 in
[
6
]
), let
g_{1}\leq 2
. Then, we divide the proof into three cases depending on the genus of
S_{1}
:

Case

g_{1}=0
: Let
P
be a pants decomposition of
S_{1}
. Since
g_{1}=0
, this implies that
P
is composed solely of separating curves. By Remark
5.2
and Lemmas
5.6
and
5.9
, we have that
\varphi(P)
is a pants decomposition of
S_{2}
composed solely of separating curves, which is only possible if
g_{2}=0
. The result follows from the fact that
\kappa(S_{1})=\kappa(S_{2})
and the Classification of Surfaces.

Case

g_{1}=1
: Let
P
be a pants decomposition of
S_{1}
composed solely of non-separating curves. By Lemma
5.10
we have that
\mathcal{A}(P)
is a cycle graph. This is preserved by
\varphi
due to Lemma
5.5
. Hence, by Remark
5.2
and Lemmas
5.5
and
5.7
,
\varphi(P)
is a pants decomposition of
S_{2}
composed solely of non-separating curves, such that
\mathcal{A}(\varphi(P))
is a cycle graph. Then, by Lemma
5.10
,
g_{2}=1
. As before, the result follows from the fact that
\kappa(S_{1})=\kappa(S_{2})
and the Classification of Surfaces.

Case

g_{1}=2
: Let
P
be a pants decomposition composed solely of non-separating curves, whose adjacency graph is not a cycle graph by Lemma
5.10
. By Remark
5.2
and Lemmas
5.5
and
5.7
, we have that
\varphi(P)
is a pants decomposition of
S_{2}
composed solely of non-separating curves, with
\mathcal{A}(\varphi(P))
not a cycle graph. This implies that
g_{2}\geq 2=g_{1}
. Since
\kappa(S_{1})=\kappa(S_{2})
, this implies that
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
.

If
n_{1}=1
, the result then follows from
\kappa(S_{1})=\kappa(S_{2})
and the Classification of Surfaces.

If
n_{1}>1
, let
M
be a multicurve of cardinality
\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{2}\rfloor
, composed solely of outer curves. By Lemmas
5.1
and
5.9
,
\varphi(M)
is a multicurve of
S_{2}
with cardinality
\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{2}\rfloor
and composed solely of outer curves. This implies that
2\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{2}\rfloor\leq n_{2}
. Thus, either
n_{1}=n_{2}
, or
n_{1}=n_{2}+1
; but the latter case leads us to a contradiction when substituting in
\kappa(S_{1})=\kappa(S_{2})
. Therefore
n_{1}=n_{2}
and the result follows from the fact that
\kappa(S_{1})=\kappa(S_{2})
and the Classification of Surfaces.
∎

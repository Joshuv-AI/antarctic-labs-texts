Abstract
. For non-negative integers a , b , a,b, and n n , let N ⁡ ( a , b , n ) N(a,b;n) be the number of representations of n n as a sum of squares with coefficients 1 1 or 3 3 ( a a of ones and b b of threes). Let N ∗ ​ ( a , b , n ) N^{*}(a,b;n) be the number of representations of n n as a sum of odd squares with coefficients 1 1 or 3 3 ( a a of ones and b b of threes). We have that N ∗ ​ ( a , b , 8 ​ n + a + 3 ​ b ) N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b) is the number of representations of n n as a sum of triangular numbers with coefficients 1 1 or 3 3 ( a a of ones and b b of threes). It is known that for a a and b b satisfying 1 ≤ a + 3 ​ b ≤ 7 1\leq a+3b\leq 7 , we have N ∗ ​ ( a , b , 8 ​ n + a + 3 ​ b ) = 2 2 + ( a 4 ) + a ​ b ​ N ​ ( a , b , 8 ​ n + a + 3 ​ b ) N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b)=\frac{2}{2+{a\choose 4}+ab}N(a,b;8n+a+3b) and for a a and b b satisfying a + 3 ​ b = 8 a+3b=8 , we have N ∗ ​ ( a , b , 8 ​ n + a + 3 ​ b ) = 2 2 + ( a 4 ) + a ​ b ​ ( N ⁡ ( a , b , 8 ​ n + a + 3 ​ b ) − N ⁡ ( a , b , ( 8 ​ n + a + 3 ​ b ) / 4 ) ) . N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b)=\frac{2}{2+{a\choose 4}+ab}\left(N(a,b;8n+a+3b)-N(a,b;(8n+a+3b)/4)\right). Such identities are not known for a + 3 ​ b > 8 a+3b>8 . In this paper, for general a a and b b with a + b a+b even, we prove asymptotic equivalence of formulas similar to the above, as n → ∞ n\rightarrow\infty . One of our main results extends a theorem of Bateman, Datskovsky, and Knopp where the case b = 0 b=0 and general a a was considered. Our approach is different from Bateman-Datskovsky-Knopp’s proof where the circle method and singular series were used. We achieve our results by explicitly computing the Eisenstein components of the generating functions of N ∗ ​ ( a , b , 8 ​ n + a + 3 ​ b ) N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b) and N ⁡ ( a , b , 8 ​ n + a + 3 ​ b ) N(a,b;8n+a+3b) . The method we use is robust and can be adapted in studying the asymptotics of other representation numbers with general coefficients.

1.
Introduction and main results

For
a,b,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, with
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, let

\displaystyle N(a,b;n):=\#\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{a},y_{1},\ldots,y_{b})\in\mathbb{Z}^{a+b};n=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{a}^{2}+3y_{1}^{2}+\cdots+3y_{b}^{2}\}

and
let
N^{*}(a,b;n)
be the numbers of representations of
n
by the quadratic form

x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{a}^{2}+3y_{1}^{2}+\cdots+3y_{b}^{2}

under the extra condition that
x_{i}
’s and
y_{j}
’s are odd integers. We let
N(a,b;n)
and
N^{*}(a,b;n)=0
if
n\not\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Observe that

N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b):=\#\left\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{a},y_{1},\ldots,y_{b})\in\mathbb{Z}^{a+b};~\right.\\
n=\frac{x_{1}(x_{1}-1)}{2}+\cdots+\frac{x_{a}(x_{a}-1)}{2}\left.+3\frac{y_{1}(y_{1}-1)}{2}+\cdots+3\frac{y_{b}(y_{b}-1)}{2}\right\}.

Thus,
N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b)
is the number of representations of
n
as a sum of triangular numbers with coefficients
1
or
3
(
a
of ones and
b
of threes). In this paper we are inspired by the existing results, in
[
ACH
]
,
[
C4
]
,
[
C7
]
,
[
C12
]
,
[
C10
]
, and
[
SunPaper
]
, to examine the ratio of
N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b)
to
N(a,b;8n+a+3b)
.

In
[
C10
]
, Bateman and Knopp, using elementary observations and Jacobi’s four square theorem, showed that for
1\leq a\leq 7
we have

\displaystyle N^{*}(a,0;8n+a)=\frac{2}{2+{a\choose 4}}N(a,0;8n+a).

(1.1)

This result was rediscovered independently in 2002 by Barrucand, Cooper and Hirschhorn
[
BCH
]
and proved by employing generating functions. A combinatorial proof is given in 2004 by Cooper and Hirschhorn in
[
CH
]
.

In
[
C12
]
, Bateman, Datskovsky and Knopp revisited the identity (
1.1
) from a different point of view. From the Hardy-Littlewood circle method
it is known, for
3\leq a\leq 8
, that

N(a,0;n)=\frac{\pi^{a/2}}{\Gamma(a/2)}n^{a/2-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}^{(a)}(n),

(1.2)

where the series in the above identity is the so called
singular series
(see
[
C12
, page 69]
) for the definition of
A_{k}^{(a)}(n)
). A similar formula also holds for
N^{*}(a,0;8n+a+3b)
. By employing the multiplicativity of the singular series and computing the local component at
2
, in
[
C12
]
it is shown that

\frac{N^{*}(a,0;8n+a)}{N(a,0;8n+a)}=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{2^{a-2}+2^{(a-2)/2}\cos(\pi a/4)+1}&if~a=3,4,5,6,7,\\
\frac{7\cdot 2^{3\nu}}{256\cdot 2^{3\nu}-480}&if~a=8,\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
2^{\nu}
is the largest power of
2
dividing
8n+8
(see
[
C12
, Formulas (6.5) and (6.7)]
). Observe that the formula in (
1.3
) for
3\leq a\leq 7
coincides with (
1.1
).
In fact,

\frac{2}{2^{a-2}+2^{(a-2)/2}\cos(\pi a/4)+1}=\frac{2}{2+{a\choose 4}},

for
1\leq a\leq 7
, however the left-hand side is strictly smaller than the right-hand side for
a\geq 8
.
On the other hand the appearance of
\nu
in (
1.3
), for the case
a=8
, shows that the ratio
{N^{*}(8,0;8n+8)}/{N(8,0;8n+8)}
is not a constant for all positive integer
n
. More generally, in
[
C12
]
by employing the theory of modular forms of weight
a/2
and appropriate multiplier system on the group
\Gamma_{0}(64)
, it is shown that the ratio
{N^{*}(a,0;8n+a)}/{N(a,0;8n+a)}
is never constant for
a>7
, neither there exists a non-negative integer
M
such that
{N^{*}(a,0;8n+a)}/{N(a,0;8n+a)}

stays constant
for all
n\geq M
.
Interestingly though, for
a>8
, the asymptotic identity

N(a,0;n)=\frac{\pi^{a/2}}{\Gamma(a/2)}n^{a/2-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}^{(a)}(n)+O(n^{a/4})

holds, where the main term is the same function as the right-hand side of (
1.2
) (see
[
C12
, formula (6.1)]
). Now the computation of the local component of the singular series at prime
2
reveals that for
a>8
where
8\nmid a
, relations similar to (
1.3
)
hold at the limit.
More precisely, Theorem 3 in
[
C12
]
states that, for each
a>8
where
a\not\equiv 0\pmod{8}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N^{*}(a,0;8n+a)}{N(a,0;8n+a)}=\frac{2}{2^{a-2}+2^{(a-2)/2}\cos(\pi a/4)+1}

(1.4)

and notes that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N^{*}(a,0;8n+a)}{N(a,0;8n+a)}

(1.5)

does not exist when
a\equiv 0\pmod{8}
.

In
[
ACH
]
, Adiga, Cooper and Han considered the more general problem of the relation between representation numbers
N({\lambda};8n+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m})
and
N^{*}({\bf\lambda};8n+\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m})
in which
{\bf\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})
corresponds to a partition of a positive integer not exceeding
7
,
N({\bf\lambda};n)
denotes the number of representations of
n
by the quadratic form
\lambda_{1}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}x_{m}^{2}
, and
N^{*}({\bf\lambda};n)
is defined similarly with extra condition that
x_{i}
’s are odd. Their result in our special case
\lambda_{i}\in\{1,3\}
states that for non-negative integers
a
and
b
satisfying
1\leq a+3b\leq 7
, we have

\displaystyle N^{*}(a,b;{8n+a+3b})=\frac{2}{2+{a\choose 4}+ab}N(a,b;8n+a+3b).

(1.6)

The cases corresponding to partitions of
8
for
N^{*}
and for a related function to
N
(denoted by
\widetilde{N}
) were considered by Baruah, Cooper and Hirschhorn in
[
C4
]
.
Let
\widetilde{N}(a,b;n)
be the number of representations of the integer
n
as a sum of squares with coefficients
1
or
3
(
a
of ones and
b
of threes) and at least one odd component. We see that

\widetilde{N}(a,b;8n+a+3b)=N(a,b;8n+a+3b)-N(a,b;(8n+a+3b)/4).

(1.7)

Since

\widetilde{N}(a,b;8n+a+3b)=N(a,b;8n+a+3b)

if
a+3b
is not a multiple of
4
, we use the notation
\widetilde{N}(a,b;8n+a+3b)
only if
a+3b\equiv 0
(mod 4).
In the special case
\lambda_{i}\in\{1,3\}
, Theorem 1.4 of
[
C4
]
implies that
for non-negative integers
a
and
b
satisfying
a+3b=8
, we have

\displaystyle N^{*}(a,b;{8n+a+3b})=\frac{2}{2+{a\choose 4}+ab}\widetilde{N}(a,b;8n+a+3b).

(1.8)

The above kind of exact identities between
N^{*}
and
N
or between
N^{*}
and
\widetilde{N}
are very rare. In fact, our first result in this paper proves that the above identities together with the ones listed in Lemma
3.1
are the only instances of such relations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be such that
a+b\equiv 0~({\rm mod}~2)
. The following statements hold.

(i)

We have

N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b)=C_{a,b}N(a,b;8n+a+3b)

(1.9)

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and for a rational constant
C_{a,b}
, depending on
a
and
b
, if and only if

(a,b)\in S=\{(0,2),(0,4),(0,6),(1,1),(1,3),(2,0),(3,1),(4,0),(6,0)\}.

(ii)

If
a+3b\equiv 0
(mod
4
), then

{N^{*}}(a,b;8n+a+3b)=\widetilde{C}_{a,b}{\widetilde{N}}(a,b;8n+a+3b)

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and for a rational
\widetilde{C}_{a,b}
, depending on
a
and
b
, if and only if

(a,b)\in\widetilde{S}=\{(0,4),(0,8),(1,1),(1,5),(2,2),(4,0),(5,1),(8,0)\}.

In view of Theorem
1.1
,
it is natural to study these representation functions asymptotically, for fixed integer parameters
a
and
b
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
We prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<a\in\mathbb{N}
and
b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be such that
a+b\equiv 0\pmod{2}
and assume
a+b\geq 4
. The following assertions hold:

(i)

If

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N^{*}(a,b;{8n+a+3b})}{N(a,b;8n+a+3b)}={\frac{2}{\displaystyle 2^{a+b-2}+(-1)^{b}2^{(a+b-2)/2}\cos(\pi(a+3b)/4)+1}.}

(1.10)

(ii)

Let
a+3b\equiv 0\pmod{8}
and let
\nu\geq 3
be the largest power of

in the prime decomposition of

. Then, for fixed

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\rightarrow\infty\\
2^{\nu}\|8n+a+3b\end{subarray}}\frac{N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b)}{N(a,b;8n+a+3b)}={\frac{2\delta(a,b,\nu)}{\displaystyle 2^{a+b-2}+(-1)^{b}2^{(a+b-2)/2}},}

\delta(a,b,\nu):=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=\nu-1}^{\nu}(-1)^{bj}2^{j(a+b-2)/2}}{\displaystyle-2+\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}(-1)^{bj}2^{j(a+b-2)/2}}.

(1.11)

(iii)

If
a+3b\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N^{*}(a,b;{8n+a+3b})}{\widetilde{N}(a,b;8n+a+3b)}=\frac{2}{\displaystyle 2^{a+b-2}+(-1)^{b}2^{(a+b-2)/2}\cos(\pi(a+3b)/4)}.

(1.12)

We next describe the method we use in proving Theorems

and

An important ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.5

is an explicit construction of the Eisenstein parts of the generating functions of
N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b)

and

, based on a method devised and employed in
[

]
,
[

]
, and
[

. In being able to compute the Eisenstein components explicitly,
we can explain here the underlying phenomenon using as little as possible technical details, see Section

Let us define

\displaystyle\varphi(z):=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{m^{2}},~\Psi(z):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}q^{m(m-1)/2+1/8},\mbox{ and }\Psi_{8}(z):=\Psi(8z).

(1.15)

Then the generating functions for

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}N(a,b;n)q^{n}=\varphi^{a}(z)\varphi^{b}(3z)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}N^{*}(a,b;8n+a+3b)q^{8n+a+3b}=2^{a+b}{\Psi_{8}^{a}(z)\Psi_{8}^{b}(3z)},

(1.17)

respectively.
In Section
4
we use the theory of modular forms to write

\displaystyle\Psi_{8}^{a}(z)\Psi_{8}^{b}(3z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}q^{n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}q^{n},

\displaystyle\varphi^{a}(z)\varphi^{b}(3z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n}q^{n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}^{\prime}q^{n},

where
\alpha_{n}
and

will be given explicitly in terms of the generalized divisor function
\sigma_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;n)
, associated to certain integer
k
and certain Dirichlet characters
\epsilon
and

\gamma_{n}=O_{\epsilon}(n^{(a+b-2)/4+\epsilon})\mbox{ and }\gamma^{\prime}_{n}=O_{\epsilon}(n^{(a+b-2)/4+\epsilon})

(1.18)

for any

(See Section

for the exact definitions of

,
\beta_{n}
,
\gamma_{n}
, and

. Note that

[8n+a+3b]f_{N^{*}}^{E}=\alpha_{8n+a+3b},~[8n+a+3b]f_{N}^{E}=\beta_{8n+a+3b}/2^{a+b},\\

Lemma 6.1

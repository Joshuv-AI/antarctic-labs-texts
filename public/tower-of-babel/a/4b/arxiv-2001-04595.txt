Local and global analyticity for a generalized Camassa-Holm system
Hideshi Yamane

Abstract
. We solve the analytic Cauchy problem for the generalized two-component Camassa-Holm system introduced by R. M. Chen and Y. Liu. We show the existence of a unique local/global-in-time analytic solution under certain conditions. This is the first result about global analyticity for a Camassa-Holm-like system. The method of proof is basically that developed by Barostichi, Himonas and Petronilho. The main differences of their proof and ours are twofold: (i) the system of Chen and Liu is not symmetic in the two unknowns and our estimates are not trivial generalization of those in their articles, (ii) we have simplified their argument by using fewer function spaces and the main result is stated in a simple and natural way. AMS subject classification: 35A01, 35A10, 35G55, 35Q35



Introduction

We consider the generalized two-component CH system

(0.1)

\begin{cases}&m_{t}-\alpha u_{x}+\beta(2mu_{x}+um_{x})+3(1-\beta)uu_{x}+\rho\rho_{x}=0,\,m=u-u_{xx},\\
&\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0.\end{cases}

It was introduced in
[
6

as a model of shallow water waves

\begin{cases}&u_{t}-u_{txx}-\alpha u_{x}+3uu_{x}-\beta(2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx})+\rho\rho_{x}=0,\\

Here it is assumed that

It is natural to introduce

The system (

(0.3)

(0.4)

We recall some background. The original Camassa-Holm equation

u_{t}-u_{xxt}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}

\begin{cases}&m_{t}-Au_{x}+2mu_{x}+um_{x}+\rho\rho_{x}=0,\,m=u-u_{xx},\\

The Camassa-Holm equation and its variants, including the ones above,

The analyticity of solutions is relevant since the equations model

analyticity of water waves. In
[

,
2
,

\mu

and similar equations. In the present paper, we use similar methods
in the study of the two-component system (
0.4

lies in the fact that (

and
v
. It is of higher order in

[
3

the authors introduced the quantity

(k=1,2,\dots,m+1)
,
b_{k}=e^{k\sigma}\|v^{(k)}\|_{2}/k!

(k=0,1,\dots,m)
,
and
A,\tilde{A,}B,\tilde{B}
are defined as in Proposition
19
,
then

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma}A\tilde{A}^{2}\leq 2\sqrt{2}e^{\sigma}\sqrt{\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(1)}(u)}\,\partial_{\sigma}\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(1)}(u)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}B\tilde{B}\leq 2e^{\sigma}\sqrt{\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(2)}(v)}\,\sqrt{\partial_{\sigma}\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(1)}(u)\cdot\partial_{\sigma}\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(2)}(v)},

\displaystyle A\tilde{B}^{2}=\sqrt{2}e^{\sigma}\sqrt{\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(1)}(u)}\,\partial_{\sigma}\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(2)}(v).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma}A^{2}=2\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(1)}(u),

\displaystyle e^{-2\sigma}\tilde{A}^{2}=\sum_{k=2}^{m+1}\frac{ke^{2(k-1)\sigma}}{k!^{2}}\|u^{(k)}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k=2}^{m+1}\frac{2(k-1)e^{2(k-1)\sigma}}{k!^{2}}\|u^{(k)}\|_{2}^{2}=2\partial_{\sigma}\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(1)}(u).

Moreover, we have

B^{2}=2\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(2)}(v),\;\tilde{B}^{2}=\partial_{\sigma}\Phi_{\sigma,m}^{(2)}(v).\qed

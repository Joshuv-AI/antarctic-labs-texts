Extrapolation for Large-batch Training in Deep Learning
Tao Lin, Lingjing Kong, Sebastian U. Stich, Martin Jaggi

Abstract
Deep learning networks are typically trained by Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) methods that iteratively improve the model parameters by estimating a gradient on a very small fraction of the training data. A major roadblock faced when increasing the batch size to a substantial fraction of the training data for improving training time is the persistent degradation in performance (generalization gap). To address this issue, recent work propose to add small perturbations to the model parameters when computing the stochastic gradients and report improved generalization performance due to smoothing effects. However, this approach is poorly understood; it requires often model-specific noise and fine-tuning. To alleviate these drawbacks, we propose to use instead computationally efficient extrapolation (extragradient) to stabilize the optimization trajectory while still benefiting from smoothing to avoid sharp minima. This principled approach is well grounded from an optimization perspective and we show that a host of variations can be covered in a unified framework that we propose. We prove the convergence of this novel scheme and rigorously evaluate its empirical performance on ResNet, LSTM, and Transformer. We demonstrate that in a variety of experiments the scheme allows scaling to much larger batch sizes than before whilst reaching or surpassing SOTA accuracy.

Introduction

The workhorse training algorithm for most machine learning applications—including deep learning—is Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD).
Recently, data parallelism has emerged in deep learning, where large-batch
(
Goyal et al. 2017
)
is used to reduce the gradient computation rounds so as to accelerate the training.
However, in practice, these large-batch variants suffer from severe issues of test quality loss
(
Shallue et al. 2018
;
McCandlish et al. 2018
;
Golmant et al. 2018
)
, which voids gained computational advantage.
While still not completely understood, recent research has linked part of this loss of efficiency to the existence of sharp minima. In contrast, landscapes with flat minima have in empirical studies shown generalization benefits
(
Keskar et al. 2016
;
Yao et al. 2018
;
Lin et al. 2020
)
, though this topic is still actively debated
(
Dinh et al. 2017
)
.

Another line of research tries to understand general deep learning training from an optimization perspective,
in terms of the optimization trajectory in the loss surface
(
Neyshabur 2017
;
Jastrzebski et al. 2020
)
.

Golatkar et al. 2019
empirically show that regularization techniques only affect early learning dynamics (initial optimization phase) but matter little in the final phase of training (converging to a local minimum),
similar to the critical initial learning phase described in
(
Achille et al. 2019
)
and the break-even point analysis on the entire optimization trajectory of
Jastrzebski et al. 2020
.

These new insights are consistent with empirically developed techniques for the SOTA large-batch training.
For example, gradual learning rate warmup for the first few epochs
(
Goyal et al. 2017
;
You et al. 2019
)
is often used;
and in local SGD
(
Lin et al. 2020
)
for better generalization, stochastic noise injection is only applied
after
the first phase of training (post-local SGD).

These discussions on optimization and generalization motivate us to answer the following questions when using or developing large-batch techniques for better training:

Does the proposed technique improve (initial) optimization, or help to converge to a better local minimum, or both?
How and when should we apply the technique?

In this paper, we first revisit the classical smoothing idea (which was recently attributed to avoiding sharp minima in deep learning
(
Wen et al. 2018
;
Haruki et al. 2019
)
) from optimization perspective.
We then propose a computational efficient local extragradient method as a way of smoothing for distributed large-batch training, referred to as
extrap-SGD
;
we further extend it to a general framework (extrapolated SGD) for distributed training.
We thoroughly evaluate our method on diverse tasks to understand how and when it improves the training in practice.
Our empirical results justify the benefits of
extrap-SGD
, and explain the effects of smoothing ill-conditioned loss landscapes as to exhibit more well-behaved regions, which contain multiple solutions of good generalization properties
(
Garipov et al. 2018
)
.
We show the importance of using
extrap-SGD
in the critical initial optimization phase, for the later convergence to better local minima;
the conjecture is verified by the combination with post-local SGD, which achieves SOTA large-batch training.
Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

•

We propose
extrap-SGD
and extend it to a unified framework (extrapolated SGD) for distributed large-batch training.
Extensive empirical results on three benchmarking tasks justify the effects of accelerated optimization and better generalization.

•

We provide convergence analysis for methods in the proposed framework, as well as the SOTA large batch training method (i.e. mini-batch SGD with Nesterov momentum).
Our analysis explains the large batch optimization inefficiency (diminishing linear speedup) observed in previous empirical work.

Related Work

Large-batch training.

The test performance degradation (often denoted as
generalization gap
) caused by large batch training has recently drawn significant attention
(
Keskar et al. 2016
;
Hoffer et al. 2017
;
Shallue et al. 2018
;
Masters & Luschi 2018
)
.

Hoffer et al. 2017
argue that the generalization gap in some cases can be closed by increasing training iterations and adjusting the learning rate proportional to the square root of the batch size.

Goyal et al. 2017
argue the poor test performance is due to the optimization issue; they try to bridge the generalization gap with the heuristics of linear scale-up of the learning rate during training or during a warmup phase.

You et al. 2017
propose Layer-wise Adaptive Rate Scaling (LARS) for better optimization and scaling to larger mini-batch sizes; but the generalization gap does not vanish.

Lin et al. 2020
further propose post-local SGD on top of these optimization techniques to inject stochastic noise (to mimic the training dynamics of small batch SGD) during the later training phase.

In addition to the techniques developed for improving optimization and generalization, the optimization ineffectiveness (in terms of required training steps-to-target performance) of large-batch training has been observed.

Shallue et al. 2018
;
McCandlish et al. 2018
;
Golmant et al. 2018
empirically demonstrate the existence of diminishing linear speedup region across different domains and architectures.
Such a limit is also theoretically characterized in
(
Ma et al. 2017
;
Yin et al. 2017
)
for mini-batch SGD in the convex setting.

Smoothing the “sharp minima”.

Some research links generalization performance to flatness of minima.
Entropy SGD
(
Chaudhari et al. 2017
)
proposes Langevin dynamics in the inner optimizer loop to smoothen out sharp valleys of the loss landscape.
From the perspective of large-batch training,
Wen et al. 2018
perform “sequential averaging” over models perturbed by isotropic noise, as a way to combat sharp minima.

Haruki et al. 2019
claim that injecting different anisotropic stochastic noises on local workers can smoothen sharper minima.
However, the claimed “sharper minima” is debatable
(
Dinh et al. 2017
)
;
it is also unclear whether the improved results
are due to obtained flatter local minima or improved initial optimization brought by smoothing.
We defer detailed discussion to Section
3.2
and
5.2
.

Smoothing in classical optimization.

Randomized smoothing has a long history in the optimization literature, see e.g.
Nesterov 2011
;
Duchi et al. 2012
;
Scaman et al. 2018

which show that a faster convergence rates can be achieved by convolving non-smooth convex functions with Gaussian noise.
In contrast to non-smooth convex functions, we focus on
the smooth non-convex functions, motivated by deep neural networks.

Extragradient methods and optimization stability.

Another useful building block from optimization is the extragradient method,
which is a well-known technique
to stabilize the training at each iteration by approximating the implicit update.
The method was first introduced in
(
Korpelevich 1976
)

and extended to many variants, e.g. mirror-prox
(
Nemirovski 2004
)
,
Optimistic Mirror Descent (OMD)
(
Juditsky et al. 2011
)
(using past gradient information),
extragradient method with Nesterov momentum
(
Diakonikolas & Orecchia 2017
)
.
Recently its stochastic variants have found new applications in machine learning,
e.g., Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) training
(
Daskalakis et al. 2017
;
Gidel et al. 2018
;
Chavdarova et al. 2019
;
Mishchenko et al. 2019
)
,
and low bit model training
(
Leng et al. 2017
)
.

On the theoretical side, several papers analyze the convergence of stochastic variants of extragradient.

Juditsky et al. 2011
study stochastic mirror-prox under restrictive assumptions.

Xu et al. 2019
analyze stochastic extragradient in a more general non-convex setting and demonstrate tighter upper bounds than mini-batch SGD,
when using mini-batch size
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
.

Mishchenko et al. 2019
revisit and slightly extend the stochastic extragradient for better implicit update approximation.
However, their work focuses on min-max GAN training
and argues the stochastic extragradient method might not be better than SGD for traditional function minimizations tasks.
Our work is the first that combines the idea of Nesterov momentum and extragradient (from past information)
for stochastic optimization in the setting of distributed training.

Optimization with Extrapolation

Problem Setting and Notation.
We consider sum-structured optimization problems of the form

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\,,

where
\mathbf{x}
denotes the parameters of the model (neural networks in our case),
and
f_{i}
denotes the loss function of the
i
-th (out of
N
) training data examples.
To introduce our notation, we recall a standard update of mini-batch SGD at iteration
t
, computed on
K
devices:

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}:=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\gamma_{t}\left[\frac{1}{KB}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{k}_{t}}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]\,.

(1)

Here
\mathcal{I}^{k}_{t}
denotes a subset of the training points selected on device
k
(typically selected uniformly at random) and we denote by
B:=\left\lvert\mathcal{I}^{k}_{t}\right\rvert
the local mini-batch size and by
\gamma_{t}
the step-size (learning rate).

3.1
Accelerated (Stochastic) Local Extragradient for Distributed Training

Motivated by the idea of randomized smoothing in the classic optimization literature (as for reducing the Lipschitz constant of the gradient),
we here introduce the novel idea of using extragradient locally, as a way of smoothing loss surface, for efficient distributed large-batch training.

The original idea of extrapolation (or extragradient
(
Korpelevich 1976
)
)
was developed to stabilize optimization dynamics on saddle-point problems for a single worker, such as e.g. in GAN training
(
Gidel et al. 2018
;
Chavdarova et al. 2019
)
.
The idea is to compute the gradient at an extrapolated point, different from the current point from which the update will be performed:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textstyle\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\gamma\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\,,\qquad\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}-\gamma\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\,.\end{split}

This step is intrinsically different from the well-known and widely used accelerated method (i.e. Nesterov momentum):

\textstyle\begin{split}\textstyle&\textstyle\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}\,,\mathbf{v}_{t+1}=u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\gamma\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\,,\\
\textstyle&\textstyle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\gamma\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\,,\end{split}

where here
1>u\geq 0
denotes the momentum parameter.
The key difference lies in the lookahead step for the gradient computation of these two methods.

Considering different extrapolated local models (with Nesterov momentum) under the distributed training,

extrap-SGD
combines the effects of randomized smoothing and the stabilized optimization dynamics through extrapolation.
Our
extrap-SGD
follows the idea of extrapolating from the past
(currently only used for single worker training
(
Gidel et al. 2018
;
Daskalakis et al. 2017
)
),
as a means to avoid additional cost for gradients used to form an extrapolation point.
The
extrap-SGD
method is detailed in Algorithm

3.2
Unified Extrapolation Framework

Our Algorithm
1
can be extended to a more general extrapolation framework for distributed training,
by using diverse extrapolation choices
\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}
.
It is achieved by replacing line 2 in Algorithm
1

by
\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{4}}^{k}\!=\!\mathbf{x}_{t}-\hat{\gamma}\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}
.
We denote our framework as
extrapolated SGD
,
covering different choices of noise
\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}
(e.g. Gaussian noise, uniform noise, and stochastic gradient noise)
and
extrap-SGD
(past mini-batch gradients).
We detail some choices of
\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}
below and use them as our close baselines for
extrap-SGD
:

•

\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}
as a form of isotropic noise.
The noise can be sampled from e.g. an isotropic Gaussian or uniform distribution.
Following the idea of
Li et al. 2017
,
the strength of noises added to a filter can be linearly scaled by the
l_{2}
norm of the filter,
instead of fixing a constant perturbation strength over different layers.
Formally, the scaled noise
\mathbf{\zeta}^{k}_{t}
of
j
-th filter at layer
i
on worker
k
follows

\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{t,i,j}\right\rVert\cdot\hat{\mathbf{\zeta}}^{k}_{t,i,j}/\bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{\zeta}}^{k}_{t,i,j}\bigr\|
.
A similar idea was proposed in SmoothOut
(
Wen et al. 2018
)
,
corresponding to letting
\mathbf{\zeta}^{k}_{t}:=\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}
in our framework.

•

\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}
as a form of anisotropic noise.

Kleinberg et al. 2018
interpret sequential SGD updates as GD with stochastic gradient noise convolution over update steps.
This motivates to use stochastic gradient noise for smoothing (similarly proposed in
Haruki et al. 2019
),
thus
\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}
can be chosen as:

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{k}_{t}}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}^{k})-\frac{1}{KB}\sum_{k}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{k}_{t}}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}^{k})\,.

The side effects of these noise extrapolation variants are the training setup sensitivity, causing the hyperparameter tuning difficulty and limited practical applications.
For example, the isotropic noise requires to manually design the noise distribution for each model and dataset;
the anisotropic noise distribution will be dynamically varied by different choices of the number of workers, the local mini-batch size, and the objective of the learning task
(
Zhang et al. 2019
)
.

Despite the existence of variants
(
Wen et al. 2018
;
Haruki et al. 2019
)
and their reported empirical results

4
Theoretical Analysis of Nesterov Momentum and
extrap-SGD

We now turn to the theoretical convergence analysis, i.e. we derive an upper bound on the number of iterations to find an
approximate solution with small gradient norm.

Following the convention in distributed stochastic optimization, We denote by
f^{\star}
a lower bound on the values of
f(\mathbf{x})
and use the following assumptions:

Assumption 1

(Unbiased Stochastic Gradients)
.

\forall i\in[N],t\in[T]
, it holds

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})
.

Assumption 2

(Bounded Gradient Variance)
.

\exists\sigma^{2}>0,\forall i\in[N],t\in[T]
, s.t.

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2}
.

Here
\sigma^{2}
quantifies the variance of stochastic gradients at each local worker
and we assume workers access IID training dataset (e.g., data center setting).

Assumption 3

(Lipschitz Gradient)
.

\exists L>0
, s.t.
\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},i\in[N]
,
the objective function
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following condition

\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{y})\right\rVert\leq L\left\lVert\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\rVert
.

4.1
Analysis for Mini-batch SGD (with Nesterov Momentum)

In this section we first recall the known convergence guarantees for mini-batch SGD (without momentum) on non-convex functions for later reference, and
then derive new guarantees for mini-batch SGD with Nesterov momentum.

Theorem 4.1

(Convergence of stochastic distributed mini-batch SGD for non-convex functions
(
Ghadimi & Lan 2016
)
)
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
3
, after
T
mini-batch gradient updates, each using
KB
samples, the mini-batch SGD returns an iterate
\mathbf{x}
satisfying

\displaystyle\textstyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{L\left(f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star}\right)}{T}+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{L\left(f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star}\right)}}{\sqrt{KBT}}\right)\,.

The second term in the rate is asymptotically dominant
as long as
KB\!=\!\mathcal{O}\bigl(\frac{\sigma^{2}T}{L\left(f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star}\right)}\bigr)
.
In this regime, increasing the mini-batch size gives a linear speedup, as
T\!=\!\mathcal{O}\bigl(\frac{\sigma^{2}L\left(f(\mathbf{x})-f^{\star}\right)}{KB\epsilon^{2}}\bigr)
decreases in
KB
, as similarly pointed out by
Wang & Srebro 2017
.
However, when we increase the mini-batch size beyond this critical point, the first term dominates the rate and increasing the mini-batch size further will have less impact on the convergence.
This phenomenon has also been empirically verified in deep learning applications
(
Shallue et al. 2018
)
.

In practice, mini-batch SGD with Nesterov momentum
(
Nesterov 1983
)
is the state-of-the-art deep learning training scheme.
However, previous theoretical analysis normally relies on the strong assumption of the bounded mini-batch gradients
(
Yan et al. 2018
)
.
Here we provide a better convergence analysis without such an assumption
for distributed mini-batch SGD with Nesterov momentum following closely
Yu et al. 2019
.
The convergence rate is detailed in Theorem
4.2
,
and for the proof details we refer to Section
A
of Appendix
I
.

Theorem 4.2

(Convergence of mini-batch SGD with Nesterov momentum for non-convex functions)
.

Under Assumption
1
–
3
,
for mini-batch SGD with Nesterov momentum, i.e.,

\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\!=\!\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}
,

\mathbf{v}_{t+1}\!=\!u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\frac{\gamma}{KB}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})
,

\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\!=\!\mathbf{x}_{t}+\mathbf{v}_{t+1}
,
we can show that for optimally tuned stepsize (cf. Lemma
A.4
)
\gamma\leq\frac{2(1-u)^{2}}{L(u^{3}+1)}
,
it holds

\textstyle\begin{split}\textstyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert^{2}\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{Lr_{0}(u^{3}+1)}{T(1-u)}+\sqrt{\frac{2Lr_{0}\sigma^{2}}{KBT(1-u)}}\right)\,,\end{split}

where here
\mathbf{x}
denotes a uniformly at random selected
\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}
iterate, i.e.
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\bigl\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\bigr\|^{2}
,
and
r_{0}:=f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star}
.

The second term is asymptotically dominant as long as

KB=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(1-u)}{(u^{3}+1)^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{2}T}{L(f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star})}\right)

and for small
K
we can achieve the linear speedup where
T=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{L\sigma^{2}(f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star})}{(1-u)KB\epsilon^{2}}\right)
.

Remark 4.3
.

Yu et al. 2019

argue that a linear speedup
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{KT})
for SGD with the local Nesterov momentumcan be achieved.
However, this claim is only valid for large
T
and stepsize
\gamma=\sqrt{K/T}
, cf.
(
Yu et al. 2019
, Cor. 1)
.
We here tune the stepsize differently and show a tighter bound that holds for all
T
,
providing a better critical mini-batch size analysis (diminishing linear speedup in terms of optimization) for mini-batch SGD with Nesterov momentum
(
Shallue et al. 2018
)
.

Theorem 4.8

6
Conclusion

In this work, we adopt the idea of randomized smoothing to distributed training and propose
extrap-SGD
to perform extrapolation with past local mini-batch gradients.
The idea further extends to a unified framework, covering multiple noise extrapolation variants.
We provide convergence guarantees for methods within this framework,
and empirically justify the remarkable benefits of our methods on image classification, language modeling and neural machine translation tasks.
We further investigate and understand the properties of our methods;
the algorithms smoothen the ill-conditioned loss landscape for faster optimization and biases the optimization trajectory to well-conditioned regions.
These insights further motivate us to combine our methods with post-local SGD for SOTA large-batch training.

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1})-f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})\right]&\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}),\;\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\overset{(a)}{=}{\mathbb{E}}\left[-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})\,,\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\left[-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})+\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\,,\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\left[-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\,,\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\,,\end{split}

where we use Lemma
A.1
for
(a)
.

We can note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\,,\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle\\
&\qquad=\left\langle-\frac{\sqrt{1-u}}{\sqrt{L}u^{3/2}}\left(\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right)\,,\frac{\gamma\sqrt{L}u^{3/2}}{(1-u)^{3/2}}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle\\
&\qquad\overset{(b)}{\leq}\frac{1-u}{2Lu^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}Lu^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\,,\end{split}

where
(b)
follows the basic inequality
\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2}
.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1})-f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})\right]\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1-u}{2Lu^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}Lu^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{(1-u)L}{2u^{3}}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}Lu^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\left[(-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}+\frac{\gamma^{2}Lu^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}})\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{(1-u)L}{2u^{3}}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\,.\end{split}

Taking sum over t and averaging by
\frac{1}{T}
yields:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1})-f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})\right]=\frac{1}{T}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{T})-f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{0})\right]\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}+\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\right)\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{(1-u)L}{2u^{3}}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\overset{(c)}{\leq}\left(-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}+\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\right)\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{(1-u)L\gamma^{2}}{2u^{3}}\frac{u^{4}}{(1-u)^{4}}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\overset{(d)}{\leq}\left(-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}+\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\right)\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}+\frac{Lu\gamma^{2}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{BK}\right)\\
&=\left(-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}+\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}+\frac{Lu\gamma^{2}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\right)\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}+\frac{Lu\gamma^{2}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\right)\frac{\sigma^{2}}{BK}\,,\end{split}

where the inequality
(c)
comes from Lemma
A.2
.
For (d), by using
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert X\right\rVert^{2}\right]={\text{var}}\left[X\right]+\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}\left[X\right]\right\rVert^{2}
and the fact
that
{\text{var}}\left[\sum_{i}X_{i}\right]=\sum_{i}{\text{var}}\left[X_{i}\right]
if
X_{i}
’s are independent , we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]&={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{B^{2}K^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}{\text{var}}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}\right]+\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}\right]\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{BK}\sigma^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\,.\end{split}

Note that the coefficient of the gradient norm of

-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}+\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}+\frac{Lu\gamma^{2}}{2(1-u)^{3}}

can be simplified as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left(-1+\frac{L\gamma u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{2}}+\frac{L\gamma}{2(1-u)}+\frac{Lu\gamma}{2(1-u)^{2}}\right)=\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left(-1+\frac{L\gamma(u^{3}+1)}{2(1-u)^{2}}\right)\,.\end{split}

By rearranging, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}&\leq\frac{1}{T\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left(1-\frac{L\gamma(u^{3}+1)}{2(1-u)^{2}}\right)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{0})-f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{T})\right]+\frac{\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}+\frac{Lu\gamma^{2}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\right)}{\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left(1-\frac{L\gamma(u^{3}+1)}{2(1-u)^{2}}\right)}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{BK}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{T\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\left(1-\frac{L\gamma(u^{3}+1)}{2(1-u)^{2}}\right)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star}\right]+\frac{\left(\frac{\gamma L}{2(1-u)}+\frac{Lu\gamma}{2(1-u)^{2}}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{L\gamma(u^{3}+1)}{2(1-u)^{2}}\right)}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{BK}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{1-\frac{L\gamma(u^{3}+1)}{2(1-u)^{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{T\frac{\gamma}{1-u}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star}\right]+\frac{\gamma L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{BK}\right)\,,\end{split}

where we have to make the overall coefficient of the RHS positive,
i.e.,
\gamma\leq\frac{2(1-u)^{2}}{L(u^{3}+1)}
.
∎

Lemma A.4

(Lemma 13 of
Stich & Karimireddy 2019
)
.

For every non-negative sequence
\{r_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and any parameters
d\geq 0,c\geq 0,T\geq 0
,
there exists a constant
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{d}
, such that for constant stepsizes
\{\gamma_{t}=\gamma\}_{t\geq 0}
it holds

\displaystyle\textstyle\Psi_{T}:=\frac{1}{T+1}\sum_{t=0}^{T}\left(\frac{r_{t}}{\gamma_{t}}-\frac{r_{t+1}}{\gamma_{t}}+c\gamma_{t}\right)\leq\frac{dr_{0}}{\gamma(T+1)}+c\gamma\,.

Corollary A.5
.

Consider the Theorem
4.2

and Lemma
A.4

with
\gamma\leq\frac{2(1-u)^{2}}{L(u^{3}+1)}
,
we can rewrite the convergence rate of Theorem
4.2
as

\textstyle\begin{split}\textstyle{\mathbb{E}}{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{Lr_{0}(u^{3}+1)}{T(1-u)}+\sqrt{\frac{2Lr_{0}\sigma^{2}}{KBT(1-u)}}\right)\,.\end{split}

Proof of Corollary
A.5
.

We first simplify the notations and constraints in Theorem
4.2
by considering

\Psi_{T}:=\frac{1}{T\gamma}r_{0}+\frac{\gamma L\sigma^{2}}{KB}
,
where
r_{0}:=f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f^{\star}
and
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L}
.
Following the techniques in the proof of the Lemma 13 in
Stich & Karimireddy 2019
,
we consider two cases:
(1) if
\frac{r_{0}KB}{L\sigma^{2}T}\leq\frac{1}{L^{2}}
,
then we choose the stepsize
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}KB}{L\sigma^{2}T}}

and get
\Psi_{T}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{Lr_{0}\sigma^{2}}{KBT}}
;
(2) if
\frac{r_{0}KB}{L\sigma^{2}T}>\frac{1}{L^{2}}
,
then we choose
\gamma=\frac{1}{L}
and get

\Psi_{T}\leq\frac{Lr_{0}}{T}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{KB}\leq\frac{Lr_{0}}{T}+\frac{Lr_{0}}{T}
.
These two bounds together show that
\Psi_{T}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{Lr_{0}\sigma^{2}}{KBT}}+2\frac{Lr_{0}}{T}
.

We then evaluate the exact case of Theorem
4.2
by considering

\Psi_{T}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{T\frac{\gamma}{1-u}}r_{0}+\frac{\gamma L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{KB}
.
Similarly, we have the potential
\gamma
to minimize
\Psi_{T}^{\prime}
,
where
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{2r_{0}KB(1-u)^{3}}{L\sigma^{2}T}}

and
\gamma=\frac{2(1-u)^{2}}{L(u^{3}+1)}
.
Thus, by considering two cases as in previous paragraph, we have

\Psi_{T}^{\prime}\leq\frac{Lr_{0}(u^{3}+1)}{T(1-u)}+\sqrt{\frac{2Lr_{0}\sigma^{2}}{KBT(1-u)}}
.
∎

Appendix B
Nonconvex proof for
extrap-SGD

In the main text we propose to reuse past local gradients (with mini-batch size
B
) for extrapolation.
For the convergence analysis illustrated in this section, we consider a more general extrapolation framework.

Recall that a general form of
extrap-SGD
can be defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{4}}=\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}\,,\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}^{k}=\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}\,,\mathbf{v}_{t+1}=u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\frac{\gamma}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\,,\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}+\mathbf{v}_{t+1}\,,\end{split}

(7)

where
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{\xi}_{0,i}^{k}=\mathbf{0}
.
The
\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}
indicates the possibility of using the samples from the subset of
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}
,
and
b=\left\lvert\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}\right\rvert
.
The
\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}
could be either fresh local gradient
\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t})
,
or old local gradient
\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})
.
The later choice corresponds to Algorithm
1
demonstrated in the main paper.
Furthermore we could choose
\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}:=\zeta_{t}^{k}

as various types of i.i.d. noise e.g. Gaussian noise, uniform noise and stochastic noise as discussed in Section
3.2
.
For ease of exposition, we adopt the following notations:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{bK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}\,,\qquad\mathbf{g}_{t,i}^{k}:=\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t}^{k})\,,\qquad\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t}:=\frac{1}{bK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t,i}^{k}\,,\qquad\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{1}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}^{k}\,.

(8)

We follow the idea of using virtual sequence
(
Yu et al. 2019
;
Stich & Karimireddy 2019
)

and define two auxiliary sequences for our own setup (
7
):

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\,,

(9)

and

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbf{x}_{0}&\textrm{if $t=0$}\\
\frac{1}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-u\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1})&\textrm{if $t\geq 1$}\end{array}\right.\,.

Following the definition of the virtual sequence in (
9
),
the update scheme in (
7
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}&=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}^{k}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t,i}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}\right)=\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{bK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}=\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\hat{\gamma}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}\,,\\
\mathbf{v}_{t+1}&=u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\frac{\gamma}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}^{k}=u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\,,\\
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}&=\mathbf{x}_{t}+\mathbf{v}_{t+1}\,,\end{split}

(12)

We have the following three lemmas:

Lemma B.1
.

Consider the sequence
\{\bar{\mathbf{y}}\}
in (
B
) and for
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}=-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Proof.

For the case
t=0
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}&=\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{1}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{0}\\
&=\frac{1}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\frac{3}{2}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{1}\\
&=\frac{1}{1-u}(u\mathbf{v}_{1}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\gamma}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{1})+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{1}\\

For the case

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}&=\frac{1}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{3}{2}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})-\frac{u}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-u\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}+u\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1})\\

&\qquad+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-u\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}+u\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1})\\
&=\frac{1}{1-u}(u\mathbf{v}_{t+1}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t+1})-\frac{u}{1-u}(u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})-\frac{u\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1}-\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t})\\

&=\frac{1}{1-u}(u\mathbf{v}_{t+1}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})-\frac{u}{1-u}(u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})\\
&=-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{u}{1-u}\mathbf{v}_{t+1}-\frac{u}{1-u}(u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}-\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})\\
&=-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\,.\end{split}

∎

Lemma B.2
.

For
T\geq 2
and
\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}
defined in (
9
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq(1+\beta)\frac{u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\hat{\gamma}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right],\,\forall\beta>0\,.

If we reuse past local stochastic gradients with mini-batch size
B
, i.e.

\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{0}&\mathrm{if}\;t=0\\
\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}&\mathrm{if}\;t\geq 1\end{cases}\,,

by choosing
\beta=1
and
\hat{\gamma}\leq\frac{u^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}}\gamma
,
we obtain the simplified expression:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\frac{1}{BK}T\sigma^{2}+\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,.

Proof.

The proof starts from

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}&=\frac{u}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-u\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1})\\
&=\frac{u}{1-u}(u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\hat{\gamma}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1}-\hat{\gamma}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t})+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-u\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1})\\
&=\frac{u^{2}}{1-u}\mathbf{v}_{t}-\frac{u\gamma}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{u\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1}-\frac{u\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-\frac{\hat{\gamma}u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t-1}\\
&=\frac{u^{2}}{1-u}\mathbf{v}_{t}-\frac{u\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}\\
&=\frac{u^{2}}{1-u}\mathbf{v}_{t}+\hat{\gamma}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}\,.\end{split}

Thus, we have

\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\frac{u^{2}}{1-u}\mathbf{v}_{t}+\hat{\gamma}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1+\beta)\frac{u^{4}}{(1-u)^{2}}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\hat{\gamma}^{2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}
,
where
\left\lVert\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1+\beta)\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert^{2}+(1+\beta^{-1})\left\lVert\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2}
for
\beta>0
.

Summing over
\{0,\ldots,T-1\}
, we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1+\beta)\frac{u^{4}}{(1-u)^{2}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\hat{\gamma}^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

We define

s_{t}:=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}u^{t-i-1}=\frac{1-u^{t}}{1-u}\leq\frac{1}{1-u}
. Noticing
\mathbf{v}_{0}=0
and
T\geq 2
,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}&=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&=\gamma^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{t}u^{t-i}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}=\gamma^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}s_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\frac{u^{t-i-1}}{s_{t}}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&\leq\gamma^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}s_{t}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\frac{u^{t-i-1}}{s_{t}}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}=\gamma^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}s_{t}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}u^{t-i-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1-u}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}u^{t-i-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1-u}\sum_{i=0}^{T-2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\sum_{t=i+1}^{T-1}u^{t-i-1}\\
&\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1-u}\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}u^{t}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\,,\end{split}

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1+\beta)\frac{u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}+(1+\frac{1}{\beta})\hat{\gamma}^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Further if we reuse the past local gradients with mini-batch
B
, i.e.

\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{0}&\mathrm{if}\;t=0\\
\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}&\mathrm{if}\;t\geq 1\end{cases}\,
,
and choose
\beta=1
and
\hat{\gamma}\leq\frac{u^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}}\gamma
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

By using the fact that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert X\right\rVert^{2}\right]={\text{var}}\left[X\right]+\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}\left[X\right]\right\rVert^{2}
and
that
{\text{var}}\left[\sum_{i}X_{i}\right]=\sum_{i}{\text{var}}\left[X_{i}\right]
if
X_{i}
’s are independent , we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]&={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{B^{2}K^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}{\text{var}}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}^{k}\right]+\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}^{k}\right]\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{BK}\sigma^{2}+\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,,\end{split}

where we rely on Assumption
1
,
2
.
Thus,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(\frac{1}{BK}\sigma^{2}+\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\frac{1}{BK}T\sigma^{2}+\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,.

∎

(13)

\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{0}&\mathrm{if}\;t=0\\
\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}&\mathrm{if}\;t\geq 1\end{cases}\,,

with

being i.i.d.,
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\right]=0
and
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{\zeta}^{k}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\hat{\sigma}^{2}
,
we have
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq 2\hat{\gamma}^{2}\hat{\sigma}^{2}

Proof.

By definition, for

.

For

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\hat{\gamma}\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\hat{\gamma}^{2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\end{split}

If we reuse past local gradients i.e.
\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}=\mathbf{g}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}^{k}
,
then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\hat{\gamma}^{2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right]&={\mathbb{E}}\left[\hat{\gamma}^{2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})+\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})-\mathbf{g}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}^{k}\right\rVert\right]\\
&\leq 2\hat{\gamma}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-\frac{1}{2}})-\mathbf{g}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)\right]\\
&\overset{(a)}{\leq}2\hat{\gamma}^{2}(\frac{1}{Kb}+\frac{1}{b})\sigma^{2}\leq\frac{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}}{b}\sigma^{2}\,,\end{split}

where (a) is from the Assumption
2
and the independence between data samples.

Alternatively if we use i.i.d. noise, i.e.

\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{0}&\mathrm{if}\;t=0\\
\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}&\mathrm{if}\;t\geq 1\end{cases}\,,

with
\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}
being i.i.d.,
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\right]=0
and
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{\zeta}^{k}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\hat{\sigma}^{2}
,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\hat{\gamma}^{2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{\xi}}_{t}-\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{\xi}_{t,i}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right]&=\hat{\gamma}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&=\hat{\gamma}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{2}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\langle\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{i},\,\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\rangle\right]\\
&=\hat{\gamma}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{K^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\lVert\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{2}{K}\left\lVert\mathbf{\zeta}_{t}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\leq\hat{\gamma}^{2}(\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\frac{1}{K}\hat{\sigma}^{2})\leq 2\hat{\gamma}^{2}\hat{\sigma}^{2}\,.\end{split}

Combining these yields the lemma.

Theorem B.4
.

Consider the update rule of
extrap-SGD
with notations defined in (
8
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{4}}=\mathbf{x}_{t}+u\mathbf{v}_{t}\,,\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}^{k}=\mathbf{x}_{t+\frac{1}{4}}-\hat{\gamma}\mathbf{g}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}^{k}\,,\mathbf{v}_{t+1}=u\mathbf{v}_{t}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\,,\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}+\mathbf{v}_{t+1}\,.\end{split}

We define
\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}

as well as the following virtual sequence

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbf{x}_{0}&\textrm{if $t=0$}\\
\frac{1}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\gamma u}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{1-u}(\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{1}{2}}-u\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t-\frac{3}{2}})&\textrm{if $t\geq 1$}\end{array}\right.\,,

where
\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{-\frac{1}{2}}=0
by default.

Under Assumption
1
-
3
,
the convergence rate of
\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}
for a non-convex function follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\frac{2(1-u)}{\gamma T}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{0})-f^{\star}\right]+\left(\frac{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}L^{2}}{B}+\frac{\gamma L(1+3u)}{(1-u)^{2}BK}\right)\sigma^{2}\,,\end{split}

where
\hat{\gamma}\leq\frac{u^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}}\gamma
and
\gamma\leq\frac{(1-u)^{2}}{L(1+3u+u^{3})}
.

Proof.

We start from the standard smoothness inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1})-f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})\right]&\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}),\;\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\overset{(1)}{=}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}),\;-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})+\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}),\;-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&=\underbrace{{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}),\;-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle\right]}_{(a)}+\underbrace{{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}),\;-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle\right]}_{(b)}\\
&\qquad+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\underbrace{{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]}_{(c)}\,,\end{split}

where we use Lemma
B.1
for (1).

For (a), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}),\;-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle\right]\\
&\qquad\overset{(2)}{\leq}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1-u}{2u^{3}L}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\qquad\overset{(3)}{\leq}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{(1-u)L}{2u^{3}}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\,,\end{split}

where the inequality (2) is due to the basic inequality
\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\beta}\left\lVert\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2}

with
\beta=\frac{1-u}{Lu^{3}}
and (3) is from Assumption
3
.

For (b), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}),\;-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle\right]\\
&=-\frac{\gamma}{1-u}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}),\;\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rangle\right]\\
&\overset{(4)}{=}-\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\overset{(5)}{\leq}-\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{L^{2}}{K}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\right]\\
&\overset{(6)}{\leq}-\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]-\frac{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}L^{2}}{B}\sigma^{2}\right)\,,\end{split}

where the equality (4) comes from
\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2}\right)
,
the inequality (5) is due to Assumption
3

and (6) is from Lemma
B.3
.

For (c), by using
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert X\right\rVert^{2}\right]={\text{var}}\left[X\right]+\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}\left[X\right]\right\rVert^{2}
and the fact
that
{\text{var}}\left[\sum_{i}X_{i}\right]=\sum_{i}{\text{var}}\left[X_{i}\right]
if
X_{i}
’s are independent , we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]&={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}^{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{B^{2}K^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}{\text{var}}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}^{k}\right]+\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{BK}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}^{k}}\mathbf{g}_{t+\frac{1}{2},i}^{k}\right]\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\\
&\overset{(7)}{\leq}\frac{1}{BK}\sigma^{2}+\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,,\end{split}

where (7) follows from Assumption
2
.

Thus the smoothness inequality becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t+1})-f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t})\right]&\leq\frac{(1-u)L}{2u^{3}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+\left(\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}-\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}\right){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\qquad-\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]+\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}\frac{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}L^{2}}{B}\sigma^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{BK}\sigma^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\right)\\
&=\frac{(1-u)L}{2u^{3}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{t}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}\right]+\left(\frac{L\gamma^{2}u^{3}}{2(1-u)^{3}}-\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\right){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]\\
&\qquad-\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]+\left(\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}\frac{4\hat{\gamma}^{2}L^{2}}{B}+\frac{\gamma^{2}L}{2(1-u)^{2}}\frac{1}{BK}\right)\sigma^{2}\,.\end{split}

By rearranging, summing over
t
, and diving both sides by
\frac{\gamma}{2(1-u)}
, we have

Through Lemma

, we have

thus,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert^{2}\right]&\leq\frac{2(1-u)}{\gamma}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{0})-f(\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{T})\right]+\frac{(1-u)^{2}L}{\gamma u^{3}}\left(\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\frac{1}{BK}T\sigma^{2}+\frac{4u^{4}\gamma^{2}}{(1-u)^{4}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{k}_{t+\frac{1}{2}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\\

∎

Quasi Yamabe Solitons on 3-Dimensional Contact Metric Manifolds with Q ​ φ = φ ​ Q Q\varphi=\varphi Q
V. Venkatesha and H. Aruna Kumara

Abstract
In this paper we initiate the study of quasi Yamabe soliton on 3-dimensional contact metric manifold with Q ​ φ = φ ​ Q Q\varphi=\varphi Q and prove that if a 3-dimensional contact metric manifold M M such that Q ​ φ = φ ​ Q Q\varphi=\varphi Q admits a quasi Yamabe soliton with non-zero soliton vector field V V being point-wise collinear with the Reeb vector field ξ \xi , then V V is a constant multiple of ξ \xi , the scalar curvature is constant and the manifold is Sasakian. Moreover, V V is Killing. Finally, we prove that if M M is a 3-dimensional compact contact metric manifold such that Q ​ φ = φ ​ Q Q\varphi=\varphi Q endowed with a quasi Yamabe soliton, then either M M is flat or soliton is trivial.

Introduction

The concept of Yamabe flow was introduced by Hamilton at the same time as the Ricci flow
[
Hamilton
]
as a tool to construct Yamabe metrics on compact Riemannian manifold. On a smooth Riemannian manifold, Yamabe flow can be defined as the evolution of the Riemannian metric
g_{0}
in time
t
to
g=g(t)
by means of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g=-rg,\quad g(0)=g_{0},

where
r
denotes the scalar curvature corresponds to
g
. The significance of Yamabe flow lies in the fact that it is a natural geometric deformation to metrics of constant scalar curvature. The Yamabe flow is equivalent to Ricci flow when the dimension is 2. However in dimension greater than 2, the Yamabe flow and Ricci flow are different in their properties, since the first one preserves the conformal class of the metric but the Ricci flow does not in general. A Yamabe solitons corresponds to self-simillar solution of the Yamabe flow and it is defined on Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
by a vector field
V
satisfying the equation
[
Barbosa
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{V}g=(r-\lambda)g,

(1)

where
L_{V}
denotes the Lie derivative in the direction of the vector field
V
and
\lambda
is a real number. A Yamabe soliton is said to be expanding, steady,
or shrinking, respectively, if
\lambda<0
,
\lambda=0
, or
\lambda>0
. In
[
Chen
]
, Chen and Deshmukh introduce the concept of quasi Yamabe soliton. A smooth Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to be a quasi Yamabe soliton if it admits a vector field
V
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{V}g=(r-\lambda)g+\frac{1}{m}V^{\#}\otimes V^{\#},

(2)

for some constant
m
, where
V^{\#}
is the dual 1-form of
V
. The vector field
V
is called a soliton vector field for quasi Yamabe soliton. Note that if
m\rightarrow\infty
, the equation (
2
) defines a Yamabe soliton. So, the quasi Yamabe solitons are natural extension of the Yamabe solitons. When the vector field
V
is gradient of smooth function
f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the equation (
2
) becomes

\displaystyle Hessf=(r-\lambda)g+\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df,

(3)

where
Hessf
denotes the Hessian of
f
and in this case
f
is called potential function of the quasi Yamabe soliton and
g
is called quasi Yamabe gradient soliton and this concept was introduced by Huang and Li
[
Huang
]
. When
f
is constant function, we say that
(M,g)
is a trivial quasi Yamabe gradient soliton.
There are several works on Yamabe flow and quasi Yamabe flow by Barbosa and Ribeiro
[
Barbosa
]
, Brendle
[
Brendle1
]
, Bruchard et al.
[
Bruchard
]
, Hsu
[
Hsu1
]
, Daskalopoulos and Sesum
[
Daskalopoulos
]
, Neto and Pina
[
Neto

, Ma and Cheng
[
Ma
]
, Ma and Miquel
[
Ma1
]
, Cao et al.
[
Cao
]
, Blaga
[
Blaga
]
, Chen and Deshmukh
[
Chen
]
, Pirhadi and Razavi
[
Pirhadi
]
, Wang
[
Wang
]
, Yang and Zhang
[
Yang
]
and many others. In the background of contact geometry, Yamabe solitons were first studied by Sharma
[
Sharma
]
in 3-dimensional Sasakian manifold. Then these are studied by Wang
[
YWang
]
, Suh and Mandal
[
Suh
]
and Erken

Erken

Recently, the present first author with Naik studied the Yamabe soliton on 3-dimensional contact metric manifold with
Q\varphi=\varphi Q
in
[
Venkatesha

and generalized the result of Sharma
[
Sharma
]

Q
commutes with the fundamental collineation
\varphi

Q\varphi=\varphi Q
and first we prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

If a 3-dimensional contact metric manifold
M
such that
Q\varphi=\varphi Q
admits a quasi Yamabe soliton with non-zero soliton vector field
V
being point-wise collinear with the Reeb vector field
\xi
, then
V
is a constant multiple of
\xi
, the scalar curvature is constant and the manifold is Sasakian. Moreover, the soliton vector field is Killing.

Next, we consider a 3-dimensional compact contact metric manifold with
Q\varphi=\varphi Q
and prove

Theorem 1.2
.

If a 3-dimensional compact contact metric manifold
M
with
Q\varphi=\varphi Q
admits a quasi Yamabe soliton, then
M
is a flat or soliton is trivial.

(6)

\displaystyle trl=Ric(\xi,\xi)=2n-trh^{2},

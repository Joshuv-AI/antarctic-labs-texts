Massive Scalar Perturbation of Extremal Rotating Braneworld Black hole: Superradiant Stability Analysis
Shauvik Biswas

Abstract
We analyse the superradiant stability of braneworld extremal Kerr and Kerr-Newman black holes under massive scalar perturbation. These black hole solutions differ from their four dimensional counterpart by the presence of a tidal charge, which unlike electric charge, can take both positive and negative values and carries the signature of extra dimensions. We consider the perturbation of the brane geometry by a massive scalar field. From the radial equation of motion of the scalar field, we have found the effective potential felt by the field. For superradiant stability, there should not be any trapping well of the effective potential outside of the horizon. Using this condition we have specified the parameter space of superradiant stability which depends on parameters of both black hole and perturbation. In the case of Kerr-like extremal braneworld black holes, we have obtained a bound on tidal charge for superradiant stability under a massive scalar perturbation. Similarly, for slowly rotating Kerr-Newman like extremal braneworld black holes, we have obtained a lower bound of the product of mass to charge ratio of the black hole and scalar field, for which the system is superradiantly stable.

A brief introduction to the braneworld scenario and solution of the effective gravitational equation on the brane

In the braneworld model, we describe four dimensional spacetime as embedded in five dimensional spacetime, with a spacelike extra dimension. The non-gravitational physics of matter fields are confined in the four dimensional space time; it is called brane. On the other hand, only gravity can probe the entire five dimensional space-time, known as bulk. People would like to know whether one can have a brane localized black hole. 3.1
Superradiant Stability Analysis

In order to find superradiantly stable parameter space of the braneworld extermal Kerr black hole, we define a new radial function
[
31
]

\psi_{lm}=\sqrt{\Delta}R_{lm}
. Using this radial function in

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\psi_{lm}}{dr^{2}}+(\omega^{2}-V_{K})\psi_{lm}=0~,

(26)

where

\displaystyle V_{K}

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}-\frac{U_{K}}{\Delta^{2}}

(27)

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}-\frac{\{\omega(r^{2}+a^{2})-am\}^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}-\frac{\{2am\omega-\mu^{2}(r^{2}+a^{2})-K_{lm}\}}{\Delta}~.

(28)

Here we have used the fact, that for extremal black holes
M^{2}=a^{2}-Q
.

The asymptotic and near horizon behaviour of the above potential is given by

\displaystyle V_{K}\sim\begin{cases}\mu^{2}+\frac{2M}{r}(\mu^{2}-2\omega^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{r^{2}})&\text{for}\ \quad r\to\infty\\
\\
-\infty&\text{for}\quad r\to r_{+}.\end{cases}

(29)

On the other hand
V^{\prime}_{K}(r\rightarrow\infty)\rightarrow\frac{2M}{r^{2}}(-\mu^{2}+2\omega^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{r^{3}})
. As explained in
1
, for superradiant stability under massive scalar perturbation, there should not be any trapping well outside the horizon for the effective potential

[
31
,
32
,
33
]

using a new radial coordinate

\displaystyle V^{\prime}_{K}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(Az^{3}+Bz^{2}+Cz+D)}{-z^{6}}\equiv\frac{zf(z)}{-z^{6}}~,

(30)

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=2M(\mu^{2}-2\omega^{2})~,

(31)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=2K_{lm}+2\mu^{2}a^{2}+2\mu^{2}M^{2}-12\omega^{2}M^{2}-4\omega^{2}a^{2}~,

(32)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=12M\omega ma-12M\omega^{2}(M^{2}+a^{2})~,

(33)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=-4[\omega(M^{2}+a^{2})-ma]^{2}~.

(34)

Here we have used the fact, that for extremal black holes

. In the above expressions

At the position of maxima or minima

has only one positive root and

can have only one maxima outside the horizon. To specify the parameter region consistent with this requirement, we analyze the sign of the roots of the

[
53
]
. If

,

and

are the three roots of the cubic equation

, then according to this theorem:

\displaystyle z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3}=-\frac{B}{A}~,

(35)

\displaystyle z_{1}z_{2}+z_{2}z_{3}+z_{3}z_{1}=\frac{C}{A}~,

(36)

\displaystyle z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}=-\frac{D}{A}~.

(37)

From the expression of the coefficients of

it is clear that

. Now suppose

, then from

we can see that

. Then from

.
In this case we are left with the possibility of having two positive roots and one negative root. That is, with the condition

one can not avoid the possibility of forming trapping well outside the horizon. On the other hand, if we let

, then the product of the roots become positive. In this case, we can choose our parameters to have only one positive (say

) and two negative roots (say

According to the asymptotic behaviour of the effective potential, this implies

So we have reduced our job to a problem of finding the parameter region where

\displaystyle B>0~.

Now using superradiance condition

Since

3.2
Bound On Tidal Charge

To hold the inequality

\displaystyle Q\geq-a^{2}.

(42)

Also we must have

\displaystyle-a^{2}\leq Q<a^{2}.

This serves as a bound on tidal charge. When the inequalities

and the inequality

As stated in

here

Using the same argument presented in

the solution of the above differential equation can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\Psi(t,r,\theta,\phi)=\sum_{lm}R_{lm}(r)S_{lm}(\theta)e^{im\phi}e^{-i\omega t}~,

here

One can do a similar analysis as we have done in

\\
e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{c})r_{*}}&\text{for}\quad r_{*}\to-\infty~,\end{cases}

where

From the above, it is clear that to get an exponentially decaying bound state at spatial infinity we need

\displaystyle\mu^{2}>\omega^{2}.

(50)

In the case of charged rotating black holes the superradiance condition

\displaystyle 0<\omega<\omega_{c}~.

(51)

4.1
Superradiant Stability Analysis

To analyze the superradiant stability of the braneworld extremal Kerr-Newman black hole we follow the same steps presented in
3.1
. Using a new radial function
[
31
]

\psi_{lm}=\sqrt{\Delta}R_{lm}
, one can put the radial equation
47
in the form,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\psi_{lm}}{dr^{2}}+(\omega^{2}-V_{KN})\psi_{lm}=0~,

(52)

where

\displaystyle V_{KN}

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}-\frac{U_{KN}}{\Delta^{2}}

(53)

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}-\frac{\{\omega(r^{2}+a^{2})-am-rq\beta\}^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}-\frac{\{2am\omega-\mu^{2}(r^{2}+a^{2})-K_{lm}\}}{\Delta}~.

(54)

While deriving

we have used the fact, that for charged extremal black holes
M^{2}=a^{2}+\beta^{2}-Q
.

The asymptotic and near horizon behaviour of the effective potential
V_{KN}(r)
is,

\displaystyle V_{KN}\sim\begin{cases}\mu^{2}+\frac{2M(\mu^{2}-2\omega^{2})+2q\beta\omega}{r}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{r^{2}})&\text{for}\ \quad r\to\infty\\
\\
-\infty&\text{for}\quad r\to r_{+}.\end{cases}

(55)

On the other hand
V^{\prime}_{KN}(r\rightarrow\infty)\rightarrow\frac{-2M(\mu^{2}-\omega^{2})-2\omega(q\beta-M\omega)}{r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{r^{3}})
. For superradiant stability under charged massive scalar perturbation, there should not be any trapping well outside the horizon for the effective potential
V_{KN}

[
31
,
32
,
33
]
. As in
3.1
, we can implement this condition by demanding

1.

V^{\prime}_{KN}(r\rightarrow\infty)<0
,

2.

V_{KN}(r)
should have only one maxima outside the horizon.

If the bound state condition
50
holds then the first condition always holds when

\displaystyle\omega<\frac{q\beta}{M}\quad(q\beta\neq 0)~.

(56)

To fulfill the second condition, we compute the derivative of the effective potential
V_{KN}(r)
using new radial coordinate
z=r-M

\displaystyle V^{\prime}_{KN}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(Ez^{3}+Fz^{2}+Gz+H)}{-z^{6}}\equiv\frac{zh(z)}{-z^{6}}~,

(57)

here

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=2[\omega(q\beta-M\omega)+M(\mu^{2}-\omega^{2})]~,

(58)

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=2[\mu^{2}(M^{2}+a^{2})-2\omega\{\omega(M^{2}+a^{2})-qM\beta\}+K_{lm}-(q\beta-2M\omega)^{2}]~,

(59)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=-6(2M\omega-q\beta)\{\omega(M^{2}+a^{2})-am-qM\beta\}~,

(60)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=-4(M^{2}+a^{2})^{2}(\omega-\omega_{c})^{2}~.

(61)

In the above expressions
M^{2}=a^{2}+\beta^{2}-Q
. As argued in
3.1
, the effective potential
V_{KN}(r)
will have only one maxima outside horizon if the equation

has only one positive root. If

,

are the three roots of the cubic equation

\displaystyle z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3}=-\frac{F}{E}~,

(62)

\displaystyle z_{1}z_{2}+z_{2}z_{3}+z_{3}z_{1}=\frac{G}{E}~,

(63)

\displaystyle z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}=-\frac{H}{E}~.

(64)

From the asymptotic behaviour of the effective potential

\displaystyle F>0~.

(66)

Now using the lower bound of
K_{lm}

The function

5
Discussions

In the above analysis, we have found superradiantly stable parameter regime of braneworld extremal Kerr and Kerr-Newman black holes under massive scalar perturbation. In our calculations, we have assumed that both the superradiance condition and the bound state condition hold simultaneously. For superradiantly stable parameter space, there should not be any trapping well of the effective potential outside the horizon. Otherwise, the trapped modes will be superradiantly amplified, this leads to the instability of the system. It is the crucial point of our analysis. The radial part of the Klein-Gordon equation is the main object of our concern. After choosing a suitable variable we have reduced the radial equation in the Schrödinger form. From this equation, we have determined the effective potential felt by the scalar field. Next, one should choose the parameters in a way such that there remain only one maxima (and no local minima) of the effective potential outside the horizon. This requirement is implemented by demanding that asymptotically the effective potential should behave like decreasing function and there should remain only one positive root of derivative of effective potential outside the horizon. Using this argument and Vieta’s theorem, we have obtained some inequalities involving both black hole parameters and perturbation parameters. These inequalities together with the superradiance condition and bound state condition specify the parameter space of stability. For massive scalar perturbation of braneworld Kerr-black holes, we have obtained a bound on tidal charge. On the other hand for charged massive scalar perturbation of slowly rotating braneworld extremal Kerr-Newman black holes, we have obtained a lower bound of the product of mass to charge ratio of black hole and scalar field.

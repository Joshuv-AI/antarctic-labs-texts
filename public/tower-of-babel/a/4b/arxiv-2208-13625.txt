A new approach to the study of spacelike submanifolds in a spherical Robertson-Walker spacetime: characterization of the stationary spacelike submanifolds as an application


Abstract
A natural one codimension isometric embedding of each ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -dimensional spherical Robertson-Walker (RW) spacetime I × f 𝕊 n I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n} in ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -dimensional Lorentz-Minkowski spacetime 𝕃 n + 2 \mathbb{L}^{n+2} permits to contemplate I × f 𝕊 n I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n} as a rotation Lorentzian hypersurface of 𝕃 n + 2 \mathbb{L}^{n+2} . After a detailed study of such Lorentzian hypersurfaces, any k k -dimensional spacelike submanifold of such an RW spacetime can be contemplated as a spacelike submanifold of 𝕃 n + 2 \mathbb{L}^{n+2} . Then, we use that situation to study k k -dimensional stationary (i.e., of zero mean curvature vector field) spacelike submanifolds of the RW spacetime. In particular, we prove a wide extension of the Lorentzian version of the classical Takahashi theorem, giving a characterization of stationary spacelike submanifolds of I × f 𝕊 n I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n} when contemplating them as spacelike submanifolds of 𝕃 n + 2 \mathbb{L}^{n+2} .

A(V)=-\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}N(t(s),x(s))=

\hskip 51.21495pt-a\frac{r^{\prime}(t_{0})}{r(t_{0})}\beta(t_{0})\Big(r^{\prime}(t_{0})r(t_{0}),x_{0}\Big)

(23)

\hskip 76.82243pt+\alpha(t_{0})\Big(a[r^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})r(t_{0})+r^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}],v\Big).

First of all, suppose that
\langle T(t_{0},x_{0}),V\rangle=0
. Then, from
ar(t_{0})=r^{\prime}(t_{0})\langle x_{0},v\rangle=ar^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}r(t_{0})
we obtain
a=0
. Hence, equation (
23
)
reduces to

A(V)=\alpha(t_{0})V.

Now, for the case
V=T(t_{0},x_{0})
, we have from (
14
) and (
23
),

\hskip-79.66771ptA(T(t_{0},x_{0}))=\frac{-r^{\prime}(t_{0})}{r(t_{0})\sqrt{1-r^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}}\beta(t_{0})\Big(r^{\prime}(t_{0})r(t_{0}),x_{0}\Big)

\hskip 5.69054pt-\beta(t_{0})\sqrt{1-r^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}\Big(1,0\Big)+\alpha(t_{0})T(t_{0},x_{0})=

\hskip 96.73936pt=-\beta(t_{0})\Big(\frac{r^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}{\sqrt{1-r^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}}+\sqrt{1-r^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}},\frac{r^{\prime}(t_{0})}{r(t_{0})\sqrt{1-r^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}}x_{0}\Big)

+\alpha(t_{0})T(t_{0},x_{0})=(\alpha(t_{0})-\beta(t_{0}))T(t_{0},x_{0})\,,

which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Now we are in position to analyze the extrinsic geometry of
Q(r)
. First, let us observe that, obviously,
Q(r)
is not totally geodesic for any admissible function
r
. On the other hand, Lemma
3.1
says that
Q(r)
is quasiumbilical
[
7
]
. Observe that thanks to (
21
) we have

\begin{cases}A(T)=\big(\alpha(t)-\beta(t)\big)T,and\;\\
A(V)=\alpha(t)V,\;\text{for any}\;V\in T_{(t,x)}Q(r)\;\text{orthogonal to}\;T(t,x).\end{cases}

Note that
A
is diagonalizable at any point (recall that a self-adjoint operator respect to a Lorentzian scalar product is not necessarily diagonalizable).
Therefore,
Q(r)
is totally umbilical if and only if
\beta(t)=0
everywhere on
Q(r)
, i.e., if and only if the function
r
satisfies the differential equation
r(t)r^{\prime\prime}(t)+r^{\prime}(t)^{2}=1
or equivalently
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\,r(t)^{2}=2
. Consequently,

r(t)^{2}=t^{2}+at+b
,
where
b=r(0)^{2}>0
since
r>0
, and
a=2r(0)r^{\prime}(0)
with
a^{2}<4b
, making use of
|r^{\prime}|<1
.
Summarizing, we have obtained that
Q(r)
is totally umbilical if and only if

r(t)=\sqrt{t^{2}+at+b}\,,\;\text{where the constants satisfy}\;b>0\;\text{and}\;a^{2}<4b.

(24)

Remark 3.3
.

If
Q(r)
is totally umbilical then
\alpha(t,x)=\frac{-2}{\sqrt{4b-a^{2}}}
, and, therefore, the Weingarten operator is
A=\frac{-2}{\sqrt{4b-a^{2}}}\,I
, where
I
denotes the identity operator, everywhere. In this case, the Gauss equation of
Q(r)
in
\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
gives for the curvature tensor
R
of
Q(r)
, the following expression
R(X,Y)Z=\big(\frac{4}{4b-a^{2}}\big)\{\langle Y,Z\rangle X-\langle X,Z\rangle Y\}
, i.e.,
Q(r)
has sectional curvature
\frac{4}{4b-a^{2}}
, indeed,
Q(r)
is, up to a translation, the
(n+1)
-dimensional De Sitter spacetime
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{1}(\frac{\sqrt{4b-a^{2}}}{2})
.

From Lemma
3.1
, the mean curvature function of
Q(r)
with respect to
N
,
H:=\frac{1}{n+1}\,\text{trace}(A)
, satisfies

H(t,x)=\alpha(t)-\frac{\beta(t)}{n+1}=-\frac{n(1-r^{\prime}(t)^{2})+r^{\prime\prime}(t)r(t)}{(n+1)r(t)\big(1-r^{\prime}(t)^{2}\big)^{3/2}}\,.

(25)

Remark 3.4
.

In the case
n=1
, formula (
25
) agrees, up the sign of
H
due to our choice of
N
, with
[
11
, eq. (8)]
. Therefore, if
H
is constant then

\frac{r(t)}{\sqrt{1-r^{\prime}(t)^{2}}}+r(t)^{2}H=\text{constant}\,,

for any
t\in J
, i.e., we have
[
11
, eq. (9)]
. However, for
n>1
no extension of this fact holds, and we have to make a different reasoning.

Now, taking into account

\alpha^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{r^{\prime}(t)}{r(t)}\,\beta(t),

we get from (
25
)

Corollary 3.5
.

The Lorentzian hypersurface
Q(r)
of
\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
has constant mean curvature if and only if there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\beta(t)=\frac{c}{r^{n+1}(t)}\,,

(26)

equivalently

\frac{r(t)^{n}\big(r^{\prime\prime}(t)r(t)+r^{\prime}(t)^{2}-1\big)}{(1-r^{\prime}(t)^{2})^{3/2}}=c

(27)

for all
t\in J
. In this case, we have

\alpha(t)=\frac{c}{(n+1)r^{n+1}(t)}+H\,.

(28)

Remark 3.6
.

Clearly, for the choice
c=0
we have the totally umbilical case. On the other hand, for each
c<0
we have that
r=\sqrt[n]{-c}
is a solution of (
27
) giving
Q(r)=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}(r)
which has constant mean curvature but it is not totally umbilical.

Extending
[
9
]
we are going to explore which Lorentzian hypersurfaces of the family
Q(r)

have the property that the principal curvature in the axial direction is a constant multiple of the common value of the principal curvatures in the rotational directions.

The Lorentzian hypersurface
Q(r)
of
\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
has proportional principal curvatures when there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lambda\,\alpha(t)=\alpha(t)-\beta(t),

(29)

for all
t\in J
.
If
Q(r)
has proportional principal curvatures, then it is totally umbilical when
\lambda=1
and, using (
25
), it has zero mean curvature when
\lambda=-n
.

As a direct consequence of formulae (
22
) we have,

Proposition 3.7
.

The Lorentzian hypersurface
Q(r)
of
\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
has proportional principal curvatures, i.e., it satisfies
(\ref{ppc_def})
if and only if

r(t)r^{\prime\prime}(t)=\lambda(1-r^{\prime}(t)^{2})

(30)

for all
t\in J
.

Remark 3.8
.

An analogous family of differential equations to (
30
) appears in
[
9
]
in the study of hypersurfaces of revolution with proportional principal curvatures in Euclidean spaces, giving the corresponding solutions in
[
9
, Thm. 1]
.
In our setting, the solutions of (
30
) for some choices of
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
do not provide Lorentzian hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
.
Indeed for the choice
\lambda=-1
the set of such solutions of (
30
) is given by

r(t)=\frac{1}{b}\sinh(bt+c)

where
b\neq 0
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>-c/b
. Since the condition
r^{\prime}(t)^{2}<1
, for all
t\in J
does not hold, the corresponding hypersurface does not inherit
a Lorentzian metric from
\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
.

4
Stationary spacelike submanifolds

Let
\Psi:M^{k}\to I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a spacelike immersion of a spherical RW spacetime
I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
. After identifying
\Psi
to
\psi\circ\Psi
, for a suitable admissible function
r
, we can consider

\Psi:M^{k}\to Q(r)\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+2}.

(31)

Now, let
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
be the second fundamental forms of
\Psi:M^{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
and
\Psi:M^{k}\to Q(r)
, respectively. From (
19
) we have

\sigma(X,Y)=\tilde{\sigma}(X,Y)\,+\,\langle AX,Y\rangle N\,,

(32)

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{k})
, where
N
, given in (
18
), is the unit normal vector field of
Q(r)
in
\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
and
A
its corresponding Weingarten operator (
21
). Consequently, the respective mean curvature vector fields

\mathbf{H}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}
are related by

\mathbf{H}=\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}\,+\,\frac{1}{k}\,\sum_{i=1}^{k}\,\langle AX_{i},X_{i}\rangle N\,,

(33)

where
\{X_{i}\}
is a local orthonormal basis of tangent vector fields to
M^{k}
.
Then, a direct computation from Lemma
3.1
gives
that
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}=0
if and only if

\mathbf{H}=\Big(\alpha(\Psi_{0})\,+\,\frac{\beta(\Psi_{0})}{k}\,\|T^{\top}\|^{2}\Big)\,N,

(34)

where
T^{\top}
denotes the tangential component of
T
on
M^{k}
.

By means of the Beltrami equation (
1
), we have then

\Delta\Psi=\Big(k\,\alpha(\Psi_{0})\,+\,\beta(\Psi_{0})\,\|T^{\top}\|^{2}\Big)\,N\,.

(35)

Now, for any local orthonormal tangent frame
\{X_{1},...,X_{k}\}
, we compute

\displaystyle\|T^{\top}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big\langle\Big(1,\frac{r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})}{r(\Psi_{0})}\Psi_{1},\dots,\frac{r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})}{r(\Psi_{0})}\Psi_{n+1}\Big),\Big(X_{i}(\Psi_{0}),\dots,X_{i}(\Psi_{n+1})\Big)\Big\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big(-X_{i}(\Psi_{0})+\frac{r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})}{r(\Psi_{0})}\Psi_{1}X_{i}(\Psi_{1})+\dots+\frac{r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})}{r(\Psi_{0})}\Psi_{n+1}X_{i}(\Psi_{n+1})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big(-X_{i}(\Psi_{0})+r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}X_{i}(\Psi_{0})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2})\sum_{i=1}^{k}(X_{i}(\Psi_{0}))^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2})\|\nabla\Psi_{0}\|^{2}.

Therefore, we get

k\,\alpha(\Psi_{0})+\beta(\Psi_{0})\,\|T^{\top}\|^{2}=\alpha(\Psi_{0})\,\Big[k-\Big(r^{\prime\prime}(\Psi_{0})r(\Psi_{0})+r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}-1\Big)\|\nabla\Psi_{0}\|^{2}\Big]\,.

We summarize previous computations as follows,

Proposition 4.1
.

A spacelike immersion
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to Q(r)
is stationary if and only if

\Delta\Psi=\alpha(\Psi_{0})\,\Big[k-\Big(r^{\prime\prime}(\Psi_{0})r(\Psi_{0})+r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}-1\Big)\|\nabla\Psi_{0}\|^{2}\Big]\,N.

(36)

Equivalently,
\Psi
is stationary if and only if

\Delta\Psi+q_{\Psi_{0}}\,\mathbf{P}=0,

as announced in
(\ref{120322A})
, where
\mathbf{P}
is the vector field along the immersion
\Psi
given in
(\ref{110322A})

and

q_{\Psi_{0}}
the function on
M^{k}
given in
(\ref{110322B})
.

Remark 4.2
.

The previous result gives rise to the following system of elliptic partial differential equations for the components of the spacelike immersion
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{n+1}
,

\begin{cases}\Delta\Psi_{0}+q_{\Psi_{0}}\,r(\Psi_{0})\,r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})=0,\\[7.11317pt]
\Delta\Psi_{i}+q_{\Psi_{0}}\,\Psi_{i}=0,\quad\quad i=1,2,\dots,n+1.\end{cases}

Assume
r(t)=\sqrt{1+t^{2}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In this case
Q(r)
is isometric to the unitary De Sitter spacetime
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{1}
. A spacelike immersion
\Psi\colon M^{k}\rightarrow Q(r)\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
is stationary if and only if

\Delta\Psi+k\,\Psi=0.

(37)

On the other hand, if
r(t)=1
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then
Q(1)
is isometric to the static Einstein spacetime
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Hence, as a direct consequence of Proposition
4.1
, we obtain,

Corollary 4.3
.

A spacelike immersion
\Psi\colon M^{k}\rightarrow Q(1)\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
is stationary if and only if

\Delta\Psi_{0}=0,\quad\Delta\Psi_{i}+(k+\|\nabla\Psi_{0}\|^{2})\Psi_{i}=0,\quad i=1,2,\dots,n+1.

(38)

As an immediate consequence, the only compact stationary spacelike submanifolds in
Q(1)
are the stationary submanifolds of a slice
\{t_{0}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\equiv\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Given a stationary spacelike immersion
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to Q(r)
, if the function

r(\Psi_{0})^{2}
on
M^{k}
attains a local maximum value at
x\in M^{k}
then
q_{\Psi_{0}}(x)>0
.

Proof.

In fact, this follows from
\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\Psi_{j}^{2}=r(\Psi_{0})^{2}
that gives

\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta r(\Psi_{0})^{2}+q_{\Psi_{0}}r(\Psi_{0})^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\|\nabla\Psi_{j}\|^{2}>0,

using Remark
4.2
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to Q(r)
be a stationary compact spacelike immersion. Assume
q_{\Psi_{0}}>0
and
r^{\prime}\leq 0
or
r^{\prime}\geq 0
. Then,
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to Q(r)
factors through a slice
\Psi_{0}=\mathrm{cte}
with
r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})=0.
Therefore,
q_{\psi_{0}}
is a positive constant and
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to Q(r)
realizes a stationary immersion in a totally geodesic slice of
Q(r)
, which is isometric to an
n
-dimensional round sphere of radius
r(\Psi_{0})
. In particular, there is no compact stationary spacelike submanifold in
Q(r)
which is contained in a slab where
r^{\prime}<0
or
r^{\prime}>0.

Proof.

From Remark
4.2
, we have

\int_{M^{k}}q_{\Psi_{0}}r(\Psi_{0})r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})d\mu_{g}=0,

where
d\mu_{g}
denotes the canonical measure associated to the induced metric.
Therefore, we get
r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})=0
and Remark
4.2
implies that
\Delta\Psi_{0}=0
. The compactness of
M^{k}
shows that
\Psi_{0}=\mathrm{cte}
, and
r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})=0
implies that the corresponding slice is totally geodesic in
Q(r)
. Now, equation (
7
) reduces to

\Delta\Psi_{i}+\frac{k}{r(\Psi_{0})^{2}}\Psi_{i}=0,\quad i=1,2,\dots,n+1,

and now we call the aforementioned Takahashi result
[
17
, Thm. 3]
to end the proof.
∎

In order to provide a physical interpretation to the assumptions in Corollary
4.5
, let us
recall that for a given reference frame
U
in a spacetime
\overline{M}
in terminology of
[
15
, Def. 2.3.1]
, the observers in
U
are
spreading out
(resp.
coming together
) if div
(U)>0
(resp. div
(U)<0
)
[
15
, p. 58]
. Thus, for the observers in
\overline{M}
their universe is
expanding
(resp.
contracting
). In the case
\overline{M}=I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
U=\partial_{t}
, the co-moving reference frame, we have div
(\partial_{t})=n\,f^{\prime}/f
. Therefore, the spherical RW spacetime
I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
is expanding (resp. contracting) (for co-moving observers) if
f^{\prime}(t)>0
(resp.
f^{\prime}(t)<0
) for all
t\in I
.

On the other hand, a spacetime
\overline{M}
obeys the
Null Convergent Condition
if its Ricci tensor satisfies
\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,X)\geq 0
for all null tangent vector
X
. This assumption is a necessary mathematical condition that holds from the physical fact that gravity attracts on average. Moreover, it also holds that if the spacetime obeys the Einstein equation (with zero cosmological constant) for suitable stress-energy tensors. In the case
\overline{M}=I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the
Null Convergence Condition holds if and only if

f^{2}(\log f)^{\prime\prime}\leq 1,

(39)

(see
[
2
]
, for instance).

Now, take into account, one more time, that the spherical Robertson-Walker spacetime
I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
is isometric to
Q(r)
by means of (
5
). Consequently, from equations in (
12
), we get

r^{\prime\prime}(t)r(t)+r^{\prime}(t)^{2}-1=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(s)f(s)-f^{\prime}(s)^{2}-1}{(1+f^{\prime}(s)^{2})^{2}},

for
h(s)=t
, and the Null Convergence Condition implies that
q_{\Psi_{0}}\geq 0
holds for every stationary spacelike immersion in
Q(r)
. Moreover, we would like to point out that
\overline{M}=I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
is Einstein if and only if

r^{\prime\prime}(t)r(t)+r^{\prime}(t)^{2}-1=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(s)f(s)-f^{\prime}(s)^{2}-1}{(1+f^{\prime}(s)^{2})^{2}}=0.

(see
[
3
]
). But,
I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
is Einstein if and only if it has (positive) sectional curvature
[
3
, Table]
. Note that
Q(r)
must be totally umbilical in
\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
(see Remark
3.2
).

Summarizing, when
Q(r)
satisfies the Null Convergence Condition, then for every
k
-dimensional stationary spacelike immersion
\Psi
in
Q(r)
, we have,

q_{\Psi_{0}}\geq k\,\alpha(\Psi_{0})^{2}

with equality whenever
Q(r)
has (positive) constant sectional curvature (hence,
Q(r)
is an open portion of a De Sitter spacetime).

As a consequence of previous discussion, we have,

Corollary 4.6
.

There is no stationary compact spacelike submanifold in a expanding or contracting spherical RW spacetime
I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
satisfying the Null Convergence Condition.

Proof.

Let us argue by contradiction. Suppose there exists a stationary compact spacelike immersion satisfying in a such t spherical RW spacetime
I\times_{f}\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Now Corollary
4.5
can be applied since
f^{\prime}
is signed and
f^{\prime\prime}(s)f(s)-f^{\prime}(s)^{2}\leq 1
, therefore, we should have
f^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})=0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

We finish the paper with the following result that completes the main result as announced in the end of introduction.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
r
be an admisible function and
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
any spacelike immersion with
q_{\Psi_{0}}>0
.
If
\Psi
satisfies
(\ref{120322A})
, then
\Psi
realizes a stationary spacelike immersion in
Q(r)
.

Proof.

Taking into account Proposition
4.1
, we only need to show that
\Psi(M^{k})\subset Q(r)
.
In fact, from (
7
) and (
1
), we have

k\mathbf{H}+q_{\Psi_{0}}\mathbf{P}=0,

(40)

that implies that vector field
\mathbf{P}
along the spacelike immersion
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
(
8
) is normal everywhere.

Now, the Weingarten formula for
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
and the normal vector field
\mathbf{P}
gives

\nabla^{0}_{v}\mathbf{P}=-A_{\mathbf{P}}(v)+\nabla^{\perp}_{v}\mathbf{P}.

(41)

We compute the left hand side of (
41
) for every
v=(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{n+1})\in T_{x}M^{k}
, obtaining

\nabla^{0}_{v}\mathbf{P}=\Big(\,\Big[r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0}(x))^{2}+r(\Psi_{0}(x))r^{\prime\prime}(\Psi_{0}(x))\Big]v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n+1}\Big)

\hskip 36.98857pt=v+v_{0}\Big[r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0}(x))^{2}+r(\Psi_{0}(x))r^{\prime\prime}(\Psi_{0}(x))-1\Big]\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\Big|_{\Psi(x)}}.

(42)

Now, recall that for every
a\in\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
, the vector field
a^{\top}=\nabla\langle\Psi,a\rangle\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{k})
. Here the superscript
\top
denotes the tangent part of
a\in\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
along the immersion
\Psi\colon M^{k}\to\mathbb{L}^{n+2}
.

In particular, we get

\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\Big|_{\Psi(x)}}\Big)^{\top}=\nabla\langle\Psi,e_{0}\rangle=-\nabla\Psi_{0},

and therefore, from equations (
42
) and (
41
) we have

A_{\mathbf{P}}=-\mathrm{Id}+\Big[r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}+r(\Psi_{0})r^{\prime\prime}(\Psi_{0})-1\Big]\,d\Psi_{0}\otimes\nabla\Psi_{0}\,.

(43)

Hence, we can directly compute

\mathrm{trace}(A_{\mathbf{P}})=-k+\Big[r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}+r(\Psi_{0})r^{\prime\prime}(\Psi_{0})-1\Big]\|\nabla\Psi_{0}\|^{2}=-\frac{q_{\Psi_{0}}}{\alpha(\Psi_{0})^{2}}\,,

(44)

and by means of (
40
), we get

\mathrm{trace}(A_{\mathbf{H}})=k\|\mathbf{H}\|^{2}=\frac{q^{2}_{\Psi_{0}}}{k\,\alpha(\Psi_{0})^{2}}\,.

(45)

On the other hand, formula (
40
) also gives that

\|\mathbf{H}\|^{2}=\frac{q_{\psi_{0}}^{2}}{k^{2}}\|\mathbf{P}\|^{2}=\frac{q^{2}_{\Psi_{0}}}{k^{2}}\Big(-r(\Psi_{0})^{2}r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\Psi_{j}^{2}\Big)\,.

(46)

The above two formulae (
45
) and (
46
) imply

-r(\Psi_{0})^{2}r^{\prime}(\Psi_{0})^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\Psi_{j}^{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha(\Psi_{0})^{2}},

which ends the proof.

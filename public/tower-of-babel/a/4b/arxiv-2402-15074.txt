Interpretation of Inaccessible Sets in Martin-Löf Type Theory with One Mahlo Universe Thanks: This work is supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number JP21K12822.
Yuta Takahashi ORCID:0000-0002-5214-7077

Abstract
. Martin-Löf type theory 𝐌𝐋𝐓𝐓 \mathbf{MLTT} was extended by Setzer with the so-called Mahlo universe types. The extension of 𝐌𝐋𝐓𝐓 \mathbf{MLTT} with one Mahlo universe is called 𝐌𝐋𝐌 \mathbf{MLM} and was introduced to develop a variant of 𝐌𝐋𝐓𝐓 \mathbf{MLTT} equipped with an analogue of a large cardinal. Another instance of constructive systems extended with an analogue of a large set was formulated in the context of Aczel’s constructive set theory: 𝐂𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{CZF} . Rathjen, Griffor and Palmgren extended 𝐂𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{CZF} with inaccessible sets of all transfinite orders. While Rathjen proved that this extended system of 𝐂𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{CZF} is interpretable in an extension of 𝐌𝐋𝐌 \mathbf{MLM} with one usual universe type above the Mahlo universe, it is unknown whether it can be interpreted by the Mahlo universe without a universe type above it. We extend 𝐌𝐋𝐌 \mathbf{MLM} not by a universe type but by the accessibility predicate, and show that 𝐂𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{CZF} with inaccessible sets can be interpreted in 𝐌𝐋𝐌 \mathbf{MLM} with the accessibility predicate. Our interpretation of this extension of 𝐂𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{CZF} is the same as that of Rathjen, Griffor and Palmgren formulated by 𝐌𝐋𝐓𝐓 \mathbf{MLTT} with second-order universe operators, except that we construct the inaccessible sets by using the Mahlo universe and the accessibility predicate. We formalised the main part of our interpretation in the proof assistant Agda.



1.
Introduction

Background and Aim

Martin-Löf type theory
\mathbf{MLTT}
was extended by Setzer
[
26
,
28
]
with the so-called Mahlo universe types to provide a variant of intensional
\mathbf{MLTT}
equipped with an analogue of a large cardinal. Specifically, a Mahlo universe
\mathbb{M}
has a reflection property similar to that of weakly Mahlo cardinals
[
16
]
(see also,
e.g.
,
[
7
]
): for any function
f
of type
\Sigma_{(x:\mathbb{M})}(\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}x\to\mathbb{M})\to\Sigma_{(x:\mathbb{M})}(\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}x\to\mathbb{M})
, where
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}
is the decoding function for
\mathbb{M}
, the universe
\mathbb{M}
has the subuniverse which is closed under
f
and denoted by
\mathrm{U}_{f}
. The resulting system,
\mathbf{MLM}
, is thus an instance of a constructive system extended with an analogue of weakly Mahlo cardinals. Setzer’s purpose of introducing
\mathbf{MLM}
is to obtain an extension of
\mathbf{MLTT}
whose proof-theoretic ordinal is slightly greater than that of
\mathbf{KPM}
,
i.e.
, Kripke-Platek set theory with one recursively Mahlo ordinal, where the proof-theoretic ordinals of a system enable measurement of the proof-theoretic strength. The proof-theoretic ordinal of
\mathbf{MLM}
was determined by the results of
[
26
,
28
]
.

Another instance of constructive systems extended with an analogue of weakly Mahlo cardinals was developed in the context of Aczel’s constructive set theory:
\mathbf{CZF}

[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Rathjen
[
21
]
formulated the system
\mathbf{CZFM}
by extending
\mathbf{CZF}
with the axiom asserting the existence of one Mahlo set. He then verified that
\mathbf{CZFM}
is interpretable in an extension of
\mathbf{MLM}
which has one usual Tarski universe above the Mahlo universe
\mathbb{M}
. Though he calls this extension
\mathbf{MLM}
, Setzer’s
\mathbf{MLM}
does not have any universe above
\mathbb{M}
and so we call this extension
\mathbf{MLM}^{+}
to distinguish it from
\mathbf{MLM}
. The main purpose of introducing
\mathbf{CZFM}
is a proof-theoretic one, as in the case of Mahlo universes. The interpretation of
\mathbf{CZFM}
in
\mathbf{MLM}^{+}
implies that
\mathbf{CZFM}
has at most the proof-theoretic strength of
\mathbf{MLM}^{+}
.

On the other hand, Rathjen, Griffor and Palmgren
[
22
]
introduced the system
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
, which is an extension of
\mathbf{CZF}
with the inaccessible sets of all transfinite orders
[
16
]
. They also introduced an extension of
\mathbf{MLTT}
with second-order universe operators called
\mathbf{MLQ}
, where the base theory of
\mathbf{MLQ}
is extensional
\mathbf{MLTT}
in
[
17
]
. Then, Rathjen, Griffor and Palmgren showed that
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
has at most the proof-theoretic strength of
\mathbf{MLQ}
by verifying that
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
is interpretable in
\mathbf{MLQ}
. As to the strength of large sets concerned,
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
is weaker than
\mathbf{CZFM}
: this claim is verified by Rathjen in
[
21
]
, where he proved that
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
is interpretable in
\mathbf{CZFM}
.

In summary, Rathjen
[
21
]
have shown that
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
can be interpreted in
\mathbf{MLM}^{+}
by proving that
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
and
\mathbf{CZFM}
are interpretable in
\mathbf{CZFM}
and
\mathbf{MLM}^{+}
, respectively. However, it is unknown whether a universe above the Mahlo universe
\mathbb{M}
is indispensable for the interpretation of
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
in
\mathbf{MLTT}
with the Mahlo universe
\mathbb{M}
. Rathjen used the universe above
\mathbb{M}
to define the type of Aczel’s iterative sets, which is a model of
\mathbf{CZFM}
in
\mathbf{MLM}^{+}
. So the universe above
\mathbb{M}
is crucial to his argument, and adding one universe type makes a system stronger as shown by Setzer
[
25
,
24
]
. Are the inaccessible sets of
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
interpretable by
\mathbb{M}
without a universe above it?

In this paper, we show that
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
is interpretable in
\mathbf{MLM}
with the accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
: one can dispense with a universe above the Mahlo universe, but the trade-off is the use of
\mathrm{Acc}
. The accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
provides the transfinite induction principle on Aczel’s iterative sets. This predicate can be defined in Dybjer-Setzer’s theory of induction-recursion
[
11
]
, which is a finite axiomatisation of Dybjer’s general schema of simultaneous inductive-recursive definitions
[
8
]
. Specifically, one can define
\mathrm{Acc}
by indexed inductive definition. To the best of our knowledge,
\mathbf{MLTT}
without the schema of indexed inductive definition is not sufficient to define indexed inductive types such as
\mathrm{Acc}
, though some progress was made by,
e.g.
, Hugunin
[
15
]
. We thus add
\mathrm{Acc}
as a base type to
\mathbf{MLM}
, while we show in Appendix
B
that
extensional

\mathbf{MLTT}
in
[
17
]
can define
\mathrm{Acc}
. So, in the extensional setting,
\mathrm{Acc}
is not necessary in our interpretation of
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
as in that of
[
22
]
.

Contrary to Rathjen’s elegant but “top-down” argument, the Mahlo universe
\mathbb{M}
together with
\mathrm{Acc}
enables to provide a “bottom-up” argument to interpret
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
. Below we first define a higher-order (in fact, second-order) universe operator using the reflection property of
\mathbb{M}
in
\mathbf{MLM}
. Next, the transfinite hierarchy of Aczel’s iterative sets is constructed by iterating this universe operator along the accessibility given by
\mathrm{Acc}
. Finally, we verify that these sets are indeed type-theoretic counterparts of inaccessible sets.

Moreover, we formalised the main part of our proof in the proof assistant Agda
[
5
]
. The Mahlo universe
\mathbb{M}
is simulated by the
external
Mahlo universe in Agda: the external Mahlo universe is a proof-theoretically weak variant of
\mathbb{M}
, and can be defined with induction-recursion as Dybjer and Setzer have shown
[
10
]
. So Agda is equipped with the external Mahlo universe because Agda supports even indexed induction-recursion, which is a generalisation of induction-recursion. This means that Agda also has the accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
. At the end of Section
3.2
, we will comment on the part of our proof which is not formalised yet.

Note that neither our argument in this paper nor our formalisation postulates extensional principles such as the rules of extensional identity types, except that Appendix
B
derives all inference rules for
\mathrm{Acc}
by using extensional
\mathbf{MLTT}
in
[
17
]
. The Agda code for the formalisation is available online
[
29
]
. The clickable symbol
Link
, which is attached to some statements in the main part of our proof, will open the Agda code in
[
29
]
corresponding to those statements.

Related Work

The interpretation of
\mathbf{CZF}
in
\mathbf{MLTT}
was first discussed in
[
1
]
. Here Aczel set out
\mathbf{CZF}
and interpreted it in the fragment
\mathbf{ML_{1}V}
of
\mathbf{MLTT}
, which has dependent function types (i.e.,
\Pi
-types), dependent pair types (i.e.,
\Sigma
-types), disjoint union types, the empty type, the unit type, the natural number type, the universe type and an instance of
\mathrm{W}
-types. Iterative sets in
\mathbf{CZF}
were interpreted by this instance of
\mathrm{W}
-types. Later, the set-theoretic axiom of dependent choice was interpreted in the extension of
\mathbf{ML_{1}V}
by extensional identity types (
[
2
]
). Moreover, the regular extension axiom (
\mathbf{REA}
), which states that any set is a subset of a regular set, was interpreted by further adding
\mathrm{W}
-types (
[
3
]
).

Griffor and Rathjen
[
13
]
showed that
\mathbf{CZF}
and
\mathbf{ML_{1}V}
have the same proof-theoretic strength and there is a fragment of
\mathbf{MLTT}
with the same proof-theoretic strength as
\mathbf{CZF}+\mathbf{REA}
. Setzer
[
25
,
24
]
determined the proof-theoretic strength of the fragment of
\mathbf{MLTT}
obtained from
\mathbf{ML_{1}V}
by adding intensional identity types and
\mathrm{W}
-types: he computed its proof-theoretic ordinal without appealing to Aczel’s interpretation of
\mathbf{CZF}
in
\mathbf{MLTT}
.

As aforementioned, Rathjen, Griffor and Palmgren
[
22
]
introduced the extension
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
of
\mathbf{CZF}
by the axiom asserting the existence of inaccessible sets of all transfinite orders. The type system in which
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
was interpreted is
\mathbf{MLQ}
, which is obtained from extensional
\mathbf{MLTT}
in
[
17
]
by adding the inductive type
\mathrm{Q}
and universe type
\mathrm{M}
:
\mathrm{Q}
is an inductive type of codes for operators which provides universes closed under universe operators constructed previously, and
\mathrm{M}
is a universe closed under the operators whose codes belong to
\mathrm{Q}
. Our higher-order universe operator is inspired by the one defined with the types
\mathrm{Q}
and
\mathrm{M}
, and our interpretation of
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
is the same as that of
[
22
]
, except for the interpretation of inaccessible sets. While
\mathbf{MLM}
is proof-theoretically stronger than
\mathbf{MLQ}
, it is an open question whether the intensional variant of
\mathbf{MLQ}
can be interpreted in
\mathbf{MLM}
. There is a difficulty in simulating the type of
codes
for operators of
\mathbf{MLQ}
by
\mathbf{MLM}
because such a type of codes for higher-order objects is absent from
\mathbf{MLM}
. Rathjen
[
20
]
introduced the fragment
\mathbf{MLF}
of
\mathbf{MLQ}
and showed that two weaker variants of
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
have the same proof-theoretic strength as
\mathbf{MLF}
.

The relationship between
\mathbf{MLTT}
and
\mathbf{CZF}
in the context of an analogue for weakly Mahlo cardinals has already been examined by Rathjen
[
21
]
, as shown above. He introduced the notion of a Mahlo set into
\mathbf{CZF}
and verified that the resulting system
\mathbf{CZFM}
is interpretable in
\mathbf{MLM}^{+}
, which is an extension of
\mathbf{MLM}
with one Tarski universe above the Mahlo universe. He also showed that
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
is interpretable in
\mathbf{CZFM}
, but this result is not explicitly stated and so we briefly recall it in Section
3.1
.

Contributions

Our contributions are summarised as follows:

(1)

The principal contribution of this paper is the construction of inaccessible sets of transfinite orders in
\mathbf{MLM}
with the accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
. To describe this interpretation, we use both the reflection property of
\mathbb{M}
and the accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
. We define a higher-order universe operator with the reflection property of
\mathbb{M}
and iterate this operator along the accessibility given by
\mathrm{Acc}
.

(2)

A secondary contribution is the Agda formalisation of the main proof for the result above. We utilise indexed induction-recursion in Agda for formalisation: the Mahlo universe of
\mathbf{MLM}
is simulated by the external Mahlo universe defined with induction-recursion, and the predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
is defined by using indexed inductive definition.

Organisation of the Paper

In Section
2
, we describe the system
\mathbf{MLM}
and define a higher-order universe operator using the reflection property of the Mahlo universe
\mathbb{M}
. Section
3
first provides a brief introduction to
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
and then shows that
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
is interpretable in
\mathbf{MLM}
with the accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
. Specifically, we iterate the application of our higher-order operator along the accessibility given by
\mathrm{Acc}
to construct the hierarchy of inaccessible sets in
\mathbf{MLM}
.

{exa}

[Super Universe]
A super universe, which is a universe closed under a universe operator, can be defined by reflecting a function with a parameter. Put

2.2.
Construction of A Higher-Order Universe Operator

Following Palmgren
[
19
]
, we define the type
\mathrm{O}
of
first-order operators
and the type
\mathrm{Fam}(\mathrm{O})
of
families of first-order operators
as

\mathrm{O}:=\mathrm{Fam}(\mathbb{M})\to\mathrm{Fam}(\mathbb{M}),\qquad\qquad\mathrm{Fam}(\mathrm{O}):=\Sigma_{(x:\mathbb{M})}(\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(x)\to\mathrm{O}).

For instance, let
f
be of type
\mathrm{N}_{3}\to\mathrm{O}
. Then,
(\widehat{\mathrm{N}_{3}}_{\mathbb{M}},f):\mathrm{Fam}(\mathrm{O})
is a family of first-order operators which consists of the operators
f\;0_{3},f\;1_{3},f\;2_{3}:\mathrm{O}
. We note that
\mathrm{O}
and
\mathrm{Fam}(\mathrm{O})
are just the lowest fragment of Palmgren’s higher-order operators defined in
[
19
]
: he introduced the notion of
n

n
.

We define the higher-order operator
\mathrm{u}^{\mathbb{M}}:\mathrm{Fam}(\mathrm{O})\to\mathrm{O}
by using the reflection property of
\mathbb{M}
. Typically, this operator takes a family of universe operators as an input, and returns a universe operator which provides a universe closed under all of the operators in this family. So an output of
\mathrm{u}^{\mathbb{M}}
can be its input again, and this process will be iterated transfinitely to construct the type-theoretic counterparts of inaccessible sets.

{defi}
[Operator
\mathrm{u}^{\mathbb{M}}
,
Link
]
The higher-order operator
\mathrm{u}^{\mathbb{M}}:\mathrm{Fam}(\mathrm{O})\to\mathrm{O}
is defined as follows. Let
(z,v):\mathrm{Fam}(\mathrm{O})
and
(x,y):\mathrm{Fam}(\mathbb{M})
be given. Then, by using
+\mathsf{el}
repeatedly, one can define

h^{\mathbb{M}}:\Pi_{(w:\mathrm{Fam}(\mathbb{M}))}\Bigl(\mathrm{N}_{1}+\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(x)+\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(z)+\Sigma_{(w^{\prime}:\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(z))}\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(\mathsf{p}_{1}(v\>w^{\prime}\>w))\to\mathbb{M}\Bigr)

such that

•

h^{\mathbb{M}}\>w\>(\mathsf{i}\mathsf{i}\mathsf{i}\>x_{1})=x

,

•

with
x_{2}:\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(x)
,

•

h^{\mathbb{M}}\>w\>(\mathsf{i}\mathsf{j}\>y_{1})=\mathsf{p}_{1}(v\>y_{1}\>w)
with
y_{1}:\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(z)
,

•

h^{\mathbb{M}}\>w\>(\mathsf{j}(y_{1},z_{1}))=\mathsf{p}_{2}(v\>y_{1}\>w)\>z_{1}
with
y_{1}:\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(z)
and

Next, put

f^{\mathbb{M}}[z,v,x,y]:=\lambda w.(\widehat{\mathrm{N}_{1}}_{\mathbb{M}}\>\widehat{+}_{\mathbb{M}}\>x\>\widehat{+}_{\mathbb{M}}\>z\>\widehat{+}_{\mathbb{M}}\>\widehat{\Sigma}_{\mathbb{M}}(z,\lambda w^{\prime}.\mathsf{p}_{1}(v\>w^{\prime}\>w)),\>h^{\mathbb{M}}\>w).

Then, we define

Roughly speaking,

whose index set is

That is,

•

x:\mathbb{M}
, where
x_{1}
is of type
\mathrm{N}_{1}
,

•

h^{\mathbb{M}}\>w\>(\mathsf{i}\mathsf{i}\mathsf{j}\>x_{2})
is the small type

given by the family
y:\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{M}}(x)\to\mathbb{M}
of small types and the index

,

•

h^{\mathbb{M}}\>w\>(\mathsf{i}\mathsf{j}\>y_{1})
is the small type

of first-order operators, the index

and

,

•

In particular, the two small types

For comparison, we show that a super universe can be defined by using the operator

3.
Inaccessible Sets in the Mahlo Universe of
\mathbf{MLM}

Below we first recall Aczel’s constructive set theory
\mathbf{CZF}
and then present Rathjen-Griffor-Palmgren’s extension of
\mathbf{CZF}
by inaccessible sets of all transfinite orders (Section
3.1
). Next, we prove our main theorem that inaccessible sets of
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
are interpretable in
\mathbf{MLM}
with the accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
(Section
3.2
).

3.1.
\mathbf{CZF}
with Inaccessible Sets of All Transfinite Orders

The language of
\mathbf{CZF}
is a usual first-order language with the equality
=
and the membership relation
\in
except that the restricted quantifiers
\forall x\in y
and
\exists x\in y
are primitive symbols. The symbol
\in
is the only non-logical symbol in this language. The underlying logic of
\mathbf{CZF}
is intuitionistic logic with equality, and the following set-theoretic axioms are postulated (
cf.

[
4
]
).

Pairing:

\forall a\forall b\exists c\forall x(x\in c\leftrightarrow x=a\lor x=b)

Union:

\forall a\exists b\forall x(x\in b\leftrightarrow\exists y\in a(x\in y))

Infinity:

\exists a(\exists x(x\in a)\land\forall x\in a\exists y\in a(x\in y))

Extensionality:

\forall a\forall b(\forall x(x\in a\leftrightarrow x\in b)\to a=b)

Set Induction:

For any formula
\varphi[x]
,

\forall a(\forall x\in a\varphi[x]\to\varphi[a])\to\forall a\varphi[a].

Restricted Separation:

For any restricted formula
\varphi[x]
,

\forall a\exists b\forall x(x\in b\leftrightarrow x\in a\land\varphi[x]),

where a formula is
restricted
if all of its quantifiers are restricted.

Subset Collection:

For any formula
\psi[x,y,u]
,

\displaystyle\forall a\forall b\exists c\forall u

\displaystyle(\forall x\in a\exists y\in b\psi[x,y,u]\to\exists z\in c(\forall x\in a\exists y\in z\psi[x,y,u]\land\forall y\in z\exists x\in a\psi[x,y,u])).

Strong Collection:

For any formula
\psi[x,y]
,

\forall a(\forall x\in a\exists y\psi[x,y]\to\exists b(\forall x\in a\exists y\in b\psi[x,y]\land\forall y\in b\exists x\in a\psi[x,y])).

Informally, one may suppose that the range of bound variables in
\mathbf{CZF}
forms a collection of objects. We call these objects Aczel’s
iterative sets
or
sets
shortly.

To state the axiom that there are inaccessible sets of all transfinite orders, we need to define the notion of regular set. For any set
a
,
a
is
transitive
if
\forall x\in a\forall y\in x(y\in a)
holds.

{defi}
[Regular Sets]
A set
a
is regular if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

a
is inhabited, i.e.,
\exists y(y\in a)
,

(2)

a
is transitive,

(3)

\forall b\in a\forall R(R\subseteq b\times a\land\forall x\in b\exists y(\langle x,y\rangle\in R)\to

\exists c\in a(\forall x\in b\exists y\in c(\langle x,y\rangle\in R)\land\forall y\in c\exists x\in b(\langle x,y\rangle\in R))
.

The notion of inaccessible set is captured by
set-inaccessibility
.

{defi}

A set
a
is set-inaccessible if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

a
is regular,

(2)

\forall x\in a\exists y\in a(x\subseteq y\land y\text{ is regular})
,

(3)

a
is a model of
\mathbf{CZF}
, i.e., the structure
\langle a,\in\upharpoonright(a\times a)\rangle
is a model of
\mathbf{CZF}
.

As in classical Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory, one can talk about
classes
in
\mathbf{CZF}
. A class
A
is said to be
unbounded in
a class
B
if
\forall x\in B\exists y\in A(x\in y\land y\in B)
holds. Let
\mathsf{TC}(a)
be the
transitive closure
of a set
a
with the following property (see
[
4
, Proposition 4.2]
):

\mathsf{TC}(a)=a\cup\bigcup\{\mathsf{TC}(x)\mid x\in a\}.

Inaccessible sets of transfinite orders are defined along the hierarchy of Aczel’s iterative sets.

{defi}

Let
a
and
b
be sets, then
b
is
a
-set-inaccessible if
b
is set-inaccessible and there is a family
(x_{c})_{c\in\mathsf{TC}(a)}
of sets such that for any
c\in\mathsf{TC}(a)
,

•

x_{c}
is unbounded in
b
,

•

x_{c}
consists of sets which are set-inaccessible,

•

for any
y\in x_{c}
and any
d\in\mathsf{TC}(c)
,
x_{d}
is unbounded in
y
.

Informally speaking, a set
a
represents a transfinite “order” in the hierarchy of iterative sets, so
a
-set-inaccessibles can be considered as inaccessible sets of order
a
. Note that
x_{d}
in the third condition is well-defined because for any sets

and

, if

and

hold then

holds. This claim is proved by
\mathsf{TC}
-induction (see
[
4
, Proposition 4.3]
) on

c\in a
holds, then we have

by

. If
c\in\bigcup\{\mathsf{TC}(x)\mid x\in a\}
holds, then

is an element of
\mathsf{TC}(x)
for some

. By the hypothesis of
\mathsf{TC}
-induction, we have

and so

holds.

The first lemma below follows from the definition of
a
-set-inaccessibility, and the second lemma follows from the first (

). For readers’ convenience, we recall their proofs below.

Lemma 1

(
\mathbf{CZF}
)
.

For any sets

and

, if

is
b
-set-inaccessible and

a
is

-set-inaccessible.

.

By assumption, there is a family
(x_{d})_{d\in\mathsf{TC}(b)}
of sets witnessing the

-set-inaccessibility of

(y_{d})_{d\in\mathsf{TC}(c)}
of sets as follows: for any

, we have
d\in\mathsf{TC}(b)
, so we put

. It immediately follows that
y_{d}
is unbounded in

y_{d}
consists of sets which are set-inaccessible. Let

and

holds and so

is unbounded in

.

(

)

is
c
-set-inaccessible. Since

consists of sets which are

If

also holds. To show that the class of

-set-inaccessibles is unbounded in

-set-inaccessible.

Conversely, suppose that for all

The system

As mentioned in Section

Let

{defi}

(

The following theorem is a straightforward adaptation of

\mathbf{CZF}

.

By induction on

. Let

[Transitive Closure,

4.
Concluding Remark

In this paper, we showed that the system
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
of Aczel’s constructive set theory with inaccessible sets is interpretable in a variant of
\mathbf{MLTT}
equipped with a Mahlo universe and accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
. Our type-theoretic counterparts of inaccessible sets were constructed by the transfinite iteration of a higher-order universe operator whose definition used the reflection property of the Mahlo universe. The accessibility predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
provided a recursion principle to iterate this operator. Moreover, we formalised the main part of this interpretation in Agda. For this purpose, the support of indexed induction-recursion in Agda is crucial: the Mahlo universe was simulated by the external Mahlo universe defined with induction-recursion, and the predicate
\mathrm{Acc}
was defined by indexed inductive definition.

A future research direction is to investigate the extent to which the transfinite hierarchy of inaccessible sets can be extended in
\mathbf{MLTT}
. In set-theoretic terms, the diagonalisation of this hierarchy gives the class
\{a\mid\text{$a$ is $a$-set-inaccessible}\}
(
cf.

[
23
]
). Members of this class can be considered as “fixed points” of an increasing sequence from inaccessible sets. It would be interesting to examine whether
\mathbf{MLM}
can construct an element of
\mathbb{V}
corresponding to a member of this class and, if possible, whether such elements of
\mathbb{V}
are unbounded in
\mathbb{V}
.

Another direction is to revisit our construction of inaccessible sets in
\mathbf{MLM}
from the viewpoint of homotopy type theory (HoTT). In
[
30
]
, Aczel’s interpretation of
\mathbf{CZF}
in
\mathbf{MLTT}
was refined: the type of Aczel’s iterative sets was defined as a higher inductive type
\mathbb{V}_{h}
. In addition to its ordinary introduction rule (
i.e.
, its point constructor rule), which is the same as that of
\mathbb{V}
, the higher inductive type
\mathbb{V}_{h}
also has path constructor rules. Although it is unknown whether the axioms
Strong Collection
and
Subset Collection
of
\mathbf{CZF}
hold in this refined interpretation, several interesting results on this interpretation have already been obtained (see,
e.g.
,
[
6
]
). Later, a general framework for interpreting constructive set theory into HoTT was proposed in
[
12
]
. Importantly, all axioms of
\mathbf{CZF}
hold in some interpretations of this framework; therefore, it would provide a HoTT framework to revisit our interpretation of
\mathbf{CZF}_{\pi}
in
\mathbf{MLM}
with
\mathrm{Acc}
.

MagneticTB: A package for tight-binding model of magnetic and non-magnetic materials
Zeying Zhang, Zhi-Ming Yu, Gui-Bin Liu, Yugui Yao

Abstract
We present a Mathematica program package MagneticTB, which can generate the tight-binding model for arbitrary magnetic space group. The only input parameters in MagneticTB are the (magnetic) space group number and the orbital information in each Wyckoff positions. Some useful functions including getting the matrix expression for symmetry operators, manipulating the energy band structure by parameters and interfacing with other software are also developed. MagneticTB can help to investigate the physical properties in both magnetic and non-magnetic system, especially for topological properties. 1
Introduction

Tight-binding method is a powerful tool to investigate the novel properties
in condensed mater physics

[
Wallace 1947
,
Löwdin 1950
,
Slater and Koster 1954
,
Goringe et al. 1997
,
Wieder et al. 2018
]
.
Compared with first-principles method, tight-binding method can greatly
simplify calculations. Moreover, after considering (magnetic) space
group symmetry, the tight-binding model can give more reliable results.
For example, in topological materials, symmetry plays
an important role to protect the topological properties, such as
Z_{2}

topological insulator protected by time reversal symmetry
[
Kane and Mele 2005
]
,
topological crystalline insulators and topological nodal semimetals
protected by space group symmetries
[
Fu 2011
,
Burkov et al. 2011
]

and magnetic topological crystalline insulator protected by magnetic
space group symmetries, i.e. the combination of space group operations
and time reversal
[
Fang et al. 2013
,
Liu 2013
]
.

At present, a lot of researchers using symmetry adopted tight-binding
model to investigate physical properties of electronic system
[
Egorov et al. 1968
,
Kuznetsov and Men 1978
,
Ku et al. 2002
,
Liu et al. 2013
,
Wieder and Kane 2016
,
Wang et al. 2016
,
Song et al. 2017
,
Zhang et al. 2018
,
Gresch et al. 2018
,
Koshino et al. 2018
,
Yu et al. 2019
]
.
However, most of the software packages are mainly focused on the tight-binding
model for non-magnetic materials
[
Di Martino et al. 1999
,
Groth 2014
,
Supka et al. 2017
,
Nakhaee et al. 2020
,
Klymenko et al. 2021
]

and only a few of them can be used to construct the symmetry adopted
tight-binding model automatically. For the first-principles level,
Wannier90 can generate the Wannier-tight-binding model by interfacing
with first-principles software, but the symmetry adopted Wannier function
can not be applied to other first-principles software (such as VASP,
ABINIT) except Quantum-Espresso
[
Mostofi et al. 2008
,
Giannozzi et al. 2009
,
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
,
Gonze et al. 2009
]
.
FPLO can generate symmetry adopted tight-binding model with proper
parameters for given structures
[
Koepernik and Eschrig 1999
]
.
Meanwhile, both Wannier90 and FPLO do not support magnetic symmetry.
For the model level, GTPack, Qsymm and MathemaTB can generate the
tight-binding model with space group symmetry but does
not support magnetic symmetry directly
[
Hergert and Geilhufe 2018
,
Varjas et al. 2018
,
Jacobse 2019
]
.
WannierTools can do the symmetrization of non-magnetic tight-binding
model but cannot generate the tight-binding model by itself
[
Wu et al. 2018
]
.

Therefore, it is necessary to develop a package which can construct
the tight-binding model with magnetic space group symmetry automatically.
Here we introduce a software package: MagneticTB, a tool for generating
the tight-binding model for the system with arbitrary magnetic space
group. The required input information of this package is only the
magnetic space group number and the orbital information in each Wyckoff
positions. It can help to investigate the physical properties of the
given symmetry. We also present some useful functions including get
the matrix expression for symmetry operators, manipulate the band
structure by parameters and interface with other software.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, we
give an introduction of symmetry adopted tight-binding methods. In
Sec.
3
, The usage of MagneticTB are given,
including how to install and run MagneticTB. In Sec.
4
,
we give three concrete examples, such examples show the specific capabilities
of the MagneticTB. Finally, conclusions are given.

2
Symmetry adopted tight-binding method

In periodic system, the bases
of tight-binding model can be written as Bloch sum
[
Egorov et al. 1968
]

\psi_{lm\bm{k}}^{n}(\bm{r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\bm{R}_{j}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l}^{n})}\varphi_{m}^{n}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{j}-\bm{d}_{l}^{n})

(1)

where
N
is the number of unit cells in the crystal,
\bm{R}_{j}

is the translation vector of the Bravais lattice,
\bm{d}_{l}^{n}

is the position of
n
-th Wyckoff position’s
l
-th atom in the
unit cell (for each
Q
and
\bm{d}_{l}^{n}
there
exists one and only one pair of
\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n}
and
R^{\prime}
which satisfies

Q\bm{d}_{l}^{n}=\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n}+R^{\prime}
where
Q
is arbitrary group element in the (magnetic)
space group and
R^{\prime}
is a lattice vector),

\varphi_{m}^{n}(\bm{r})

is the
m
-th atomic orbital
basis for
position
\bm{d}_{l}^{n}
but
located at coordinate origin. Then Eq. (
1
) satisfies
the Bloch theorem
\psi_{lm\bm{k}}^{n}(\bm{r}+\bm{R}_{j})=e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{j}}\psi_{lm\bm{k}}^{n}(\bm{r})
.
The tight-binding Hamiltonian can be written as:

\begin{split}H_{lml^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{nn^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=&\sum_{\bm{R}_{j}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}-\bm{d}_{l}^{n})}E_{mm^{\prime}}(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})\\
E_{mm^{\prime}}(\bm{d}_{j}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})=&\braket{\varphi_{m}^{n}(\bm{r}-\bm{d}_{l}^{n})|\hat{H}|\varphi_{m^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}(\bm{r}-\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}-\bm{R}_{j})}\end{split}

(2)

for simplicity, we rewrite the atomic orbitals
in vector form:
\varPhi^{n}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{j}-\bm{d}_{l}^{n})=\{\varphi_{m}^{n}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{j}-\bm{d}_{l}^{n})\},(m=1,...,M_{n})
.
Then
E_{mm^{\prime}}(\bm{d}_{j},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}})

(m=1,...,M_{n};\,m^{\prime}=1,\dots,M_{n^{\prime}})
form an

M_{n}\times M_{n^{\prime}}
matrix:

E(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})=\braket{\varPhi^{n}(\bm{r}-\bm{d}_{l}^{n})|\hat{H}|\varPhi^{n^{\prime}}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{j}-\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})}

(3)

Figure 1:
(a) Sketch of relationship between
E(\bm{d}_{j}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})

and
E(Q\bm{d}_{j}^{n},Q(\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}))
,
in this example we set
Q=C_{4}{\cal T}
,
\varPhi^{1}(\bm{r})=\{s\}

locate at
\bm{d}_{l}^{1}=(0,0)
,
\varPhi^{2}(\bm{r})=\{p_{x},p_{y}\}

locate at
\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{2}=(\lambda,0)(\lambda\neq 0)
.
Then we have
D^{1}(C_{4}{\cal T})=1,D^{2}(C_{4}{\cal T})=-i\sigma_{y}
,
and
E(Q\bm{d}_{l}^{1},Q\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{2})=E^{*}(\bm{d}_{l}^{1},\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{2})\times(-i\sigma_{y})
.
(b) Workflow of MagneticTB.

Then
the Hamiltonian can be rewritten as:

H_{ll^{\prime}}^{nn^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=\sum_{\bm{R}_{j}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}-\bm{d}_{l}^{n})}E(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})

(4)

E(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})

is the hopping matrix between
n
-th Wyckoff position’s
l
-th atom
to
n^{\prime}
-th Wyckoff position’s
l^{\prime}
-th atom. When the lattice is
in invariant under some symmetry
E(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})

may ne not independent
for arbitrary
\bm{d}_{l}^{n}
and
\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}
.
Fortunately, for symmetry operation

Q
, group representation theory gives
us explicit expression for the relationship between
E(Q\bm{d}_{j}^{n},Q(\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}))

and
E(\bm{d}_{j}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})
.

For the case that symmetry operation
does not contain the time
reversal
{\cal T}
, i.e.
Q=\{R|\bm{t}\}
, where
R

and
\bm{t}
are
the rotation and translation
part of
Q
respectively,
we have

E(Q\bm{d}_{l}^{n},Q(\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n}))=D^{n}(R)E(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{l}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})D^{n^{\prime}\dagger}(R)

(5)

For the case that operation
Q
contains time
reversal symmetry
{\cal T}
,
i.e.
Q=\{R|\bm{t}\}{\cal T}
,
we have

E(Q\bm{d}_{l}^{n},Q(\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n}))=D^{n}(R{\cal T})E^{*}(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})D^{n^{\prime}\dagger}(R{\cal T})

(6)

Where
D^{n}(R)(D^{n}(R{\cal T}))
are the
M_{n}\times M_{n}
representation
matrices of
R(R{\cal T})
under
atomic orbital bases

\varPhi^{n}(\bm{r})
(not necessarily irreducible representations),

E^{*}(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})

is complex conjugate of
E(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})

(see Fig.
1
for example). It is clear that for spinless
cases with time reversal symmetry
{\cal T}
, when the basis functions
are real,
D({\cal T})
is equal to identity matrix, indicating
that
E(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})

are real matrices.

The next step is to get the analytical expressions of
D^{n}(R)(D^{n}(RA))
.
For a fixed
n
, we don’t have to worry about mixing superscripts
of
n
in
D^{n}(R)

because transformations can only occur under the same
n
. So we
temporarily use
D(R)
rather than
D^{n}(R)
in this step. Consider
the following four cases:

i.

Spinless system and
Q
does not contain
{\cal T}
.

ii.

Spinless system and
Q
contain
{\cal T}
.

iii.

Spinful system and
Q
does not contain
{\cal T}
.

iv.

Spinful system and
Q
contain
{\cal T}
.

In case. i,
D(R)
can be obtained simply by solve the linear
equation
[
Cloizeaux 1963
]

\hat{R}\varPhi(\bm{r})=\varPhi(R^{-1}\bm{r})=\varPhi(\bm{r})D(R)

(7)

in which
\hat{R}
is
the function operator for the rotation
R
. In case. ii, we define
\overline{\varPhi}(\bm{r})=\hat{{\cal T}}\varPhi(\bm{r})
,
for spinless system
{\cal T}={\cal K}
, hence,
\overline{\varPhi}(\bm{r})=\varPhi^{*}(\bm{r})

and then solve the linear
equation

\hat{R}\hat{{\cal T}}\varPhi(\bm{r})=\overline{\varPhi}(R^{-1}\bm{r})=\varPhi(\bm{r})D(R{\cal T})

(8)

In case. iii, since the spin matrix is orbital independent we define
the basis function as

\varPhi^{s}(\bm{r})={\{\varPhi(\bm{r})\uparrow,\varPhi(\bm{r})\downarrow\}}

(9)

The
two spinors
\uparrow=(1,0)^{T}
, and
\downarrow=(0,1)^{T}
,and under the
rotation
\hat{R}

they are transformed according
to

\hat{R}(\uparrow,\downarrow)=(\uparrow,\downarrow)D^{\frac{1}{2}}(R)

(10)

For
proper rotation
R
,
D^{\frac{1}{2}}(R)=\text{exp}(-\frac{1}{2}i\alpha\bm{n}\cdot\hat{\bm{\sigma}})
,
where
\alpha
is the rotation angle of
R
,
\bm{n}
is
the unit vector along rotation axis, for improper rotation
S
,
R=IS
,

I
is the inversion symmetry,
D^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)=D^{\frac{1}{2}}(R)

[
LANDAU and LIFSHITZ 1977
,
Liu et al. 2021
]
. Then
D(R)

can be obtained by solving the linear
equation

\hat{R}\varPhi^{s}(\bm{r})=\varPhi^{s}(\bm{r})D(R)

(11)

Case. (iv) is similar to case. (ii), the only difference is replace
the time reversal operator
{\cal T}={\cal K}
by
{\cal T}=i\hat{\sigma}_{y}{\cal K}

and consider the spin rotation matrices. The above four cases cover
all the possibilities of
D(R)
and
D(R{\cal T})
.

Then the
operator (or representation
matrix) for
Q
can be
defined as

P_{ll^{\prime}}^{nn^{\prime}}(Q)=\begin{cases}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\tilde{\delta}_{\bm{d}_{l}^{n},Q\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}}D^{n}(R)&\text{ Q does not contain ${\cal T}$}\\
\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\tilde{\delta}_{\bm{d}_{l}^{n},Q\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}}D^{n}(R{\cal T})&\text{ Q contains ${\cal T}$}\end{cases}

(12)

where
\tilde{\delta}_{\bm{d}_{l}^{n},Q\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}}

is equal to 1 only when

d_{l}^{n}
and
Qd_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}
differ by a lattice vector and to
0 otherwise (it can also be written as
\tilde{\delta}_{\bm{d}_{l}^{n},Q\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}}=\delta_{\bm{d}_{l}^{n},Q\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}+\bm{R}_{s}}

if a suitable lattice vector
\bm{R}_{s}
is choosen). The Hamiltonian under constraint of
Q
can
be written as

P(Q)^{-1}H(\bm{k})P(Q)=H(R^{-1}\bm{k})

(13)

for
Q=\{R|\bm{t}\}
, and

P(Q)^{-1}H(\bm{k})P(Q)=H^{*}(-R^{-1}\bm{k})

(14)

for
Q=\{R|\bm{t}\}{\cal T}
[See

A
for proof of Eqs.(
5
–
6
)
and Eqs.(
13
–
14
)).
Eqs.(
13
–
14
)
are key point to generate the symmetry adopted tight-binding model.
In MagneticTB we first generate the Hamiltonian with only translation
symmetry and then use Eqs.(
13
–
14
)
to the simplify the
Hamiltonian (see Fig.
1
(b))
for the workflow of MagneticTB].
The database for tight-binding model of 1651 magnetic space group
can be found in our later work
[
Zhang and et. al. 2021
]
.

3.2.3
IO module

In MagnetTB, one can get the tight-binding model for
magnetic system. However, MagneticTB
do not calculate
the other properties
(such as surface states, finding the gap-less point and so on)
directly, since it will generally cost too much computing resources.
It is better to do such heavy calculations by Fortran, Python or C.
Therefore we develop
hopp
function to convert the symmetry
adopted tight-binding model to "wannier90_hr.dat" format, which
is convenient to interface with WannierTools
[
Wu et al. 2018
]
,
Z2Pack
[
Gresch et al. 2017
]
, PythTB
[
Yusufaly et al. 2017
]

and our home-made
package Wannflow
[
Zhang et al. 2018
,
Li et al. 2018
]
.
The "wannier90_hr.dat" in Wannier90 use the following convention

[
Mostofi et al. 2008
]
, namely conventions II:

\begin{split}\tilde{\psi}_{lm\bm{k}}^{n}(\bm{r})&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\bm{R}_{j}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{j}}\varphi_{lm}^{n}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{j}-\bm{d}_{l}^{n})\\
\tilde{H}_{lml^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{nn^{\prime}}(\bm{k})&=\sum_{\bm{R}_{j}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{j}}E_{mm^{\prime}}(\bm{d}_{l}^{n},\bm{R}_{j}+\bm{d}_{l^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}})\end{split}

(15)

which is different form MagneticTB in Eq. (
2
), the relationship
between two conventions is

\begin{split}\tilde{H}(\bm{k})=&V(\bm{k})H(\bm{k})V^{\dagger}(\bm{k})\\
V_{ll^{\prime}}^{nn^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=&e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{d}_{l}^{n}}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\end{split}

(16)

In MagneticTB (convention I) the operation
matrix defined in Eq. (
12
) is
\bm{k}
independent
while the Hamiltonian is non-periodic by shifting the reciprocal vector

\bm{G}

\begin{split}H(\bm{k}+\bm{G})=&V^{\dagger}(\bm{G})H(\bm{k})V(\bm{G})\end{split}

(17)

By contrast, in conventions II the Hamiltonian is periodic. i.e
\tilde{H}(\bm{k}+\bm{G})=\tilde{H}(\bm{k})
.
The format of
hopp
function is

⬇

hopp
[
Hamiltonian
,
parameters
]

See
section.
4
for concrete example. One can also use

symmhamII
[
ham
]
to generate the Wolfram expression for
Hamiltonian in convention II. Notice that

hopp
function (but not
symmhamII
) is only
applied
to the output of
symham
function, and
that expressions explicitly including

Sin
or
Cos
may not work well.
Be
careful to use it.

5
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have developed a software package to
generate the symmetry-adopted tight-binding model for arbitrary magnetic
space group. The input parameters for MagneticTB are clear and easy
to set and both spinless and spinful Hamiltonian can be generated
automatically. Besides, some useful functions such as manipulating
the band structure, interfacing with other software are implemented,
which can be used for further study on the magnetic systems. Moreover,
MagneticTB can not only be used to investigate physical properties
of electronic systems, but also be used to study photonics,
ultracold, acoustic and mechanical systems
[
Ozawa et al. 2019
,
Cooper et al. 2019
,
Ma et al. 2019
]
.
Finally, an exciting direction for future is to apply the magnetic
field for the tight-binding
model in MagneticTB
[
Peierls 1933
]
.

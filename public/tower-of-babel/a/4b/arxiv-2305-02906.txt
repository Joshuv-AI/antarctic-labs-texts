Optics for Premonoidal Categories
James Hefford, Mario Román

Abstract
We further the theory of optics or “circuits-with-holes” to encompass premonoidal categories: monoidal categories without the interchange law. Every premonoidal category gives rise to an effectful category (i.e. a generalised Freyd-category) given by the embedding of the monoidal subcategory of central morphisms. We introduce “pro-effectful” categories and show that optics for premonoidal categories exhibit this structure. Pro-effectful categories are the non-representable versions of effectful categories, akin to the generalisation of monoidal to promonoidal categories. We extend a classical result of Day to this setting, showing an equivalence between pro-effectful structures on a category and effectful structures on its free tight cocompletion. We also demonstrate that pro-effectful categories are equivalent to prostrong promonads.

Binoidal categories give us the necessary machinery to define premonoidal categories.

Definition 3

(Premonoidal Category)
.

A premonoidal category

Definition 5

(Premonoidal Functor)
.

A premonoidal functor
F:\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{D}
between premonoidal categories is a functor which preserves the centre pieces of
\mathcal{C}
such that for all centre pieces
\iota:U(a,b)\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}(a,b)
, composition with
F
gives a centre piece
U(a,b)\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathcal{C}(a,b)\xrightarrow{F_{a,b}}\mathcal{D}(Fa,Fb)
at
(Fa,Fb)
in
\mathcal{D}

Furthermore
F
must preserve the premonoidal structure up to natural transformations
Fa\otimes Fb\xrightarrow{}F(a\otimes b)
and
i\xrightarrow{}Fi
subject to coherence conditions like those for a monoidal functor.
A premonoidal functor is strict when these transformations are identities.

Definition 6

(Effectful Category)
.

An effectful category consists of a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}_{0}
, a premonoidal category
\mathcal{C}_{1}
with the same objects as
\mathcal{C}_{0}
and a strict, identity on objects, premonoidal functor
J:\mathcal{C}_{0}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

Remark
.

If
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is cartesian, then an effectful category is known as a
Freyd category

[
46
,
35
]
.
At least one good reason to relax from requiring cartesian structure to arbitrary monoidal structure concerns applications of effectful categories outside of computer science.
For instance, there has been interest in effectful structure for models of spacetime and quantum theory where products are not the canonical way of taking joint systems
[
17
,
27
]
, and in the study of Petri nets
[

]
.

Effectful categories can be seen as particular instances of actegories
[
35
]
- that is, a category with an action by a monoidal category
[

,
12
]
.
An effectful category
J:\mathcal{C}_{0}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}_{1}
specifies a left and right
\mathcal{C}_{0}

\mathcal{C}_{1}
, making
\mathcal{C}_{1}
into a
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-
\mathcal{C}_{0}
-biactegory.
These actions

and
\rtimes:\mathcal{C}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{0}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}_{1}
, are special because they preserve the canonical actions of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
on itself, i.e.

extends the action

and
J\boxtimes=\rtimes(J\otimes 1)

Effectful categories are also precisely the same thing as strong promonads
[
28
,
25
,
44
]

Given a strong promonad

.
The free functor

then constitutes an effectful category.
Conversely, given an effectful category
J:\mathcal{C}_{0}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}_{1}
, there is a promonad
\mathcal{C}_{1}(J-,J-):\mathcal{C}_{0}\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\joinrel\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{0}
which can be shown to be strong with strengths induced by the action of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
on
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

In this section, we show that effectful categories are pseudomonoids in the category of

In doing so we place effectful categories on the same footing as monoidal and promonoidal categories, showing that they are representations of the same underlying algebraic data.

3
Closed Effectful Categories

Now that we have a thorough understanding of effectful categories, we can start to work towards their “pro-” analogue.
To start, recall that a promonoidal category is equivalently a
closed
monoidal presheaf category.
This suggests we should turn our attention to the closure of effectful categories, which will be the focus of this section.

Power gave the following definition of closure for effectful categories, where there is still an adjunction between tensoring and the internal-hom, but only for the centre
[
37

In Section

Analogously to the case of monoidal categories where, in order for the presheaf category

To define these categories we need firstly to study the class of profunctors which factor through the tight cocompletion.
This will be the aim of this section.

To define pro-effectful categories we would like to replace the functors of a effectful category with profunctors, but we have a problem: we cannot consider arbitrary

Finally, we note some connections between pro-effectful categories and the premulticategories of Staton and Levy
[
46
]
, which generalise multicategories by dropping the interchange law.
Just as how promonoidal categories are examples of (co)multicategories
[
21
]
, pro-effectful categories are examples of (co)premulticategories.
Given a pro-effectful category
J:\mathcal{C}_{0}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}_{1}
, there is a co-premulticategory
\mathfrak{C}
with objects given by those of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.
For
a,b\in\mathfrak{C}
the class of arrows is given by
\mathfrak{C}(a;b):=\mathcal{C}_{1}(a,b)
and for
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{C}
the class of arrows is given by
\mathfrak{C}(a;b,c):=P_{1}(a,b,c)
.
The rest of the classes of arrows are defined inductively.

It is worth noting that there exist examples of pro-effectful categories which provide non-degenerate examples of premulticategories where the interchange law does not hold (in contrast to promonoidal and monoidal categories which are multicategories) and where the “tensor” is not representable (in contrast to monoidal and premonoidal categories).
For instance, the premonoidal optics introduced in the next section are an example of such a category.

Extended Higher Herglotz function II Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 11M06; Secondary 33E20. Keywords and phrases. Herglotz function, functional equations, Ramanujan’s lost notebook, Dirichlet character
Rajat Gupta and Rahul Kumar

Abstract
. Very recently, Radchenko and Zagier revived the theory of Herglotz functions. The main goal of the article is to show that one of the formulas on page 220 of Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook actually lives in the realms of this theory. As a consequence of our general theorem, we derive an interesting identity analogous to Ramanujan’s formula for ζ ⁡ ( 2 ​ m + 1 ) \zeta(2m+1) . We also introduce a character analogue of the Herglotz function and initiate its theory by obtaining an elegant functional equation governed by it.



1.
Introduction

In his seminal work on the Kronecker limit formula for a real quadratic field, Zagier
[
19
]
found an interesting function which is now known as the Herglotz function. It is defined by

\displaystyle F(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(nx)-\log(nx)}{n},\qquad x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0],

(1.1)

where
\psi(x):=\Gamma^{\prime}(x)/\Gamma(x)
is the digamma function. Prior to Zagier, a function quite similar to
F(x)
is also appeared in the work of Herglotz
[
10
]
. This is why Radchenko and Zagier call it the
Herglotz function
in
[
14
]
. Zagier establishes beautiful functional equations for
F(x)
, namely,
[
19
, Equations (7.4), (7.8)]
, for
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0]
,

\displaystyle F(x)-F(x+1)-F\left(\frac{x}{x+1}\right)

\displaystyle=-F(1)+\textup{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+x}\right),

\displaystyle F(x)+F\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=2F(1)+\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}(x)-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6x}(x-1)^{2},

(1.2)

where
\mathrm{Li}_{2}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n}/n^{2}
is dilogarithm function and
[
19
, Equation (7.12)]

F(1)=-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-\gamma_{1}.

Here
\gamma
and
\gamma_{1}
denote the Euler and first Stieltjes constants respectively. In the same paper
[
19
, Section 8]
, Zagier used these functional equations to prove Meyer’s theorem
[
13
]
.

Recently, Radchenko and Zagier
[
14
]
studied
F(x)
extensively and unearthed a myriad of connections that
F(x)
has with other areas of number theory. They provided many exciting properties of
F(x)
, including functional equations, special values at rational or quadratic irrational arguments, connections with Stark’s conjecture and with 1-cocycles for the modular group PSL
(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

Many generalizations of
F(x)
or its variants have been studied in the literature. Vlasenko and Zagier
[
18
]
, in their work on higher Kronecker limit formula, considered the study of the higher Herglotz function:

\displaystyle F_{k}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(nx)}{n^{k}},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N},\ k>1,\ x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0].

(1.3)

They found the following functional equations for
F_{k}(x)

[
18
, Equations (11), (12)]
:

\displaystyle F_{k}(x)+(-x)^{k-1}F_{k}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=-\gamma\zeta(k)\left(1+(-x)^{k-1}\right)-\sum_{r=2}^{k-1}\zeta(r)\zeta(k+1-r)(-x)^{r-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\zeta(k+1)\left((-x)^{k}-\frac{1}{x}\right),

(1.4)

and
1
1

1

The interpretation of
\zeta(1,k-1)
given in
[
18
, p. 28]
has a minus sign missing in front of the whole term, that is, the correct form is
-\left(\zeta(k-1,1)+\zeta(k)-\gamma\zeta(k-1)\right)
.

\displaystyle F_{k}(x)-F_{k}(x+1)+(-x)^{k-1}F_{k}\left(\frac{x+1}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=(-x)^{k-1}\left(\zeta(k,1)+\zeta(k+1)-\gamma\zeta(k)\right)-\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\zeta(k+1-r,r)(-x)^{r-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\zeta(k+1)\left(\frac{(-x)^{k}}{x+1}-\frac{1}{x}\right),

where,
\zeta(k)
is the Riemann zeta function and

\zeta(m,n):=\sum_{p>q>0}\frac{1}{p^{m}q^{n}}\hskip 14.22636pt(m\geq 2,n\geq 1).

Vlasenko and Zagier
[
18
, p. 54, Section 3]
also dealt with another twisted extension of (
1.3
) to treat the zeta functions of ray classes. Ishibashi studied
j^{th}
order Herglotz function to find an explicit representation of the Laurent series coefficients of the zeta function associated to indefinite quadratic forms
[
11
, Theorem 3]
.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
be a periodic function with period
M
. Masri
[
12
]
defined the following
L
-series generalization of
F(x)
:

\displaystyle F(\alpha,s,x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha(n)\left(\psi(nx)-\log(nx)\right)}{n^{s}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>0,\ x>0,

and provided its analytic continuation and evaluated it at integer points.

Very recently, the authors along with Dixit
[
7
]
studied the
extended higher Herglotz function

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{k,N}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(n^{N}x)-\log(n^{N}x)}{n^{k}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0],

where
k
and
N
are positive real numbers such that
k+N>1
. Note that the function
\mathscr{F}_{k,N}(x)
subsumes Herglotz function
F(x)
as well as Vlasenko and Zagier’s higher Herglotz function
F_{k}(x)
. One of the nice functional equations which
\mathscr{F}_{k,N}(x)
satisfies is

\displaystyle x^{\frac{1-k}{N}}\mathscr{F}_{k,N}(x)-\frac{(-1)^{k}}{N}\sum_{j=-(N-1)}^{(N-1)}{\vphantom{\sum}}{}^{\prime\prime}e^{i\pi j(k-1)/N}\mathscr{F}_{\frac{N+k-1}{N},\frac{1}{N}}\left(\frac{e^{-\frac{i\pi j}{N}}}{x^{1/N}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{N}\frac{\zeta\left(1+\frac{k-1}{N}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{N}(k-1)\right)}+x^{\frac{1-k}{N}}\left(-\left(\gamma+\log x\right)\zeta(k)+N\zeta^{\prime}(k)\right)-\frac{1}{x^{\frac{N+k-1}{N}}}\zeta(k+N)+\mathscr{B}(k,N,x),

(1.5)

where
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1<k\leq N
and
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0]
and

\mathscr{B}(k,N,x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{k+N+1}x^{1/N}\zeta\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)&\mbox{if }k=N\\
0&\mbox{if }k\neq N\end{array}\right..

Here and throughout this paper, the notation
\sum_{j=-(N-1)}^{(N-1)}{\vphantom{\sum}}{}^{\prime\prime}
denotes a sum over over
j=-(N-1),-(N-3),\cdots,N-3,N-1
. For many other nice properties of
\mathscr{F}_{k,N}(x)
and non-trivial applications of its functional equations, we refer the reader to
[
7
]
.

In the last section of their paper, the present authors with Dixit
[
7
]
posed several questions. Here in this article, we answer some of those questions affirmatively. Before we do this though, we record an elegant formula from Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook on page 220.
If

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\psi(x)+\frac{1}{2x}-\log x,

then for
\alpha,\beta
positive such that
\alpha\beta=1
, we have
[
16

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\gamma-\log(2\pi\alpha)}{2\alpha}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi(n\alpha)\right\}=\sqrt{\beta}\left\{\frac{\gamma-\log(2\pi\beta)}{2\beta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi(n\beta)\right\}.

(1.6)

He also provided the following surprising integral representation which is equal to either sides of (
1.6
):

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\Xi\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{-1+it}{4}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{\cos\left(\frac{t}{2}\log\alpha\right)}{1+t^{2}}dt,

(1.7)

where
\Xi(t)
denotes Riemann’s function defined by
[
17
, p. 16, Equations (2.1.14), (2.1.12)]

\displaystyle\Xi(t)

\displaystyle:=\xi(\tfrac{1}{2}+it),

and

\displaystyle\xi(s)

\displaystyle:=\frac{s}{2}(s-1)\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}s}\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}s)\zeta(s).

The proof of the above formula can be found in
[
3
]
. The above transformation and various other transformations of its type have generated interesting mathematics. We refer the reader to recent papers
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
, and references therein for the extensive literature related to this formula.

It was commented in
[
7
, p. 27]
that “
it seems that (
1.6
) lives in the realms of the theory of Herglotz function
” by observing the similarity between the definition of
F(x)
in (

) and series appearing on both sides of (

1.6

2.1
, and
2.4
).

Note that the functional equation (
1
) is valid only when
1<k\leq N
. It was also asked in
[
7
, Section 5, p. 28]
if there exists a functional equation for
\mathscr{F}_{k,N}(x)
where
k
is greater than
N
? We answer this question affirmatively in Theorem

.

Although
\mathscr{F}_{k,N}(x)
is a generalization of Valsenko-Zagier’s higher Herglotz function
\mathscr{F}_{k}(x)
, the functional equation of
\mathscr{F}_{k}(x)
in (

1
) as a special case due to the restriction
1<k\leq N
. This problem is now circumvented through our Theorem

To achieve our goals, we work with the following version of the extended higher Herglotz function:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{k,N}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\left(\psi\left(n^{N}x\right)-\log\left(n^{N}x\right)+\frac{1}{2n^{N}x}\right),

(1.8)

for
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0],\ \mathrm{and}\ k+2N>1
. The series is absolutely and uniformly convergent for
k+2N>1
since
[
1
, p. 259, formula 6.3.18]

\psi(x)\sim\log(x)-\frac{1}{2x}-\frac{1}{12x^{2}}+\cdots,\qquad\mathrm{as}\ x\to\infty,\ |\mathrm{arg}(x)|<\pi.

Note that for
k=N=1
,
\mathfrak{F}_{k,N}(x)
reduces to the function
F^{*}(x)
:

\displaystyle F^{*}(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left(\psi\left(nx\right)-\log\left(nx\right)+\frac{1}{2nx}\right).

The function
F^{*}(x)
was used by Radchenko and Zagier
[
14
, p. 17, Section 7.3]
to reveal the cocycle nature of the Herglotz function
F(x)
by finding an Eichler-type integral for it.

The Herglotz function
F(x)
is a special case of
\mathfrak{F}_{k,N}(x)
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{1,1}(x)=F(x)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12x}.

Also,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{k,N}(x)=\mathscr{F}_{k,N}(x)+\frac{1}{2x}\zeta(k+N).

Remark 1.1
.

Ramanujan’s formula (
1.6
) can be rephrased in terms of
\mathfrak{F}_{k,1}(x)
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{k,1}(x)-\frac{1}{x}\mathfrak{F}_{k,1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma-\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{x}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2x}\left(\gamma-\log(2\pi x)\right).

(1.9)

Thus Ramanujan understood the importance of such a function. Therefore, it would not be unfair to say that Ramanujan studied Hergltoz-type functions even before Herglotz and Zagier. Further, he went beyond the functional equation to find a surprising intergal representation for this Herglotz-type function which involves the Riemann
\Xi
-function.

The next objective of this paper is to initiate the theory of a character analogue of the Herglotz function. We define it by

\displaystyle F_{k}(x,\chi):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)\psi(nx,\chi)}{n^{k}},\quad x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0]

(1.10)

here and throughout the article
\chi(n)
is a primitive, nonprincipal character modulo
d
,
k
is a non-negative integer, and

\displaystyle\psi(x,\chi):=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(k)}{k+x}.

For a real character
\chi
, the function
\psi(n,\chi)
is equivalent to the character analogue
of the digamma function obtained by the logarithmic differentiation of the Weierstrass product form of the character analogue of the gamma function for real
characters introduced by Berndt
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle\Gamma(s,\chi):=e^{-sL(1,\chi)}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{s}{n}\right)^{-\chi(n)}e^{s\chi(n)/n},\hskip 14.22636pts\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{Z}_{<0}.

Note that the series in (
1.10
) is convergent for any
k\geq 0
since
[
5
, p. 334, Corollary 4.4]

\displaystyle\psi(x,\chi)\sim-\frac{L(0,\chi)}{x}-\chi(-1)\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{j}(\overline{\chi})}{jx^{j}},\quad|\mathrm{arg}(x)|<\pi,

as
x\to\infty
. Here
B_{j}(\chi)
is the generalized Bernoulli numbers
[
2
, p. 426]
.

2.1.
Extension of the formula on page 220 of Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{k,1}(x)=(-1)^{k}x^{k-1}\left\{\mathfrak{F}_{k,1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)-\mathcal{B}_{k}(x)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}(x):

\displaystyle=\zeta(k)\left(\log(x)-\gamma\right)+\zeta^{\prime}(k)+(-1)^{k}x^{1-k}\left((\log(x)+\gamma)\zeta(k)-\zeta^{\prime}(k)\right)+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2x^{k}}\zeta(k+1)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{x}{2}\zeta(1+k)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-2}(-1)^{j-1}\zeta(j+1)\zeta(k-j)x^{-j}.

.

It is easy to see that Ramanujan’s formula (

An immediate consequence of the above theorem is the following beautiful symmetric formula.

2.2.
Character analogue of the Herglotz function

For a non-principal Dirichlet character
\chi(n)
, let
L(s,\chi)
denote the Dirichlet
L
-function defined by

\displaystyle L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}

for Re
(s)>1

The two-term functional equation governed by
F_{k}(x,\chi)
is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
F_{k}(x,\chi)
be defined in (
1.10
). Let
\chi(n)
be a primitive, nonprincipal character modulo
d
. Then

and

(x)>0

\displaystyle F_{k}(x,\chi)-(-1)^{k}x^{k-1}F_{k}\left(x,\frac{1}{\chi}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-x)^{k-j-1}L(1+j,\chi)L(-j+k,\chi).

Now to state our next theorem, we define

\displaystyle b:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if }\chi(-1)=1\\
1&\mbox{if }\chi(-1)=-1.\end{array}\right.

Then the
\Xi(s,\chi)
can be defined as

\displaystyle\Xi(t,\chi):=\xi\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi\right),

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\xi(s,\chi):=\left(\frac{\pi}{d}\right)^{-(s+b)/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+b}{2}\right)L(s,\chi).

(2.5)

Our next theorem gives an elegant modular relation along with the integral comprising
\Xi(t,\chi)
-function.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\chi(n)
be a primitive, nonprincipal character modulo
d
. Further assume
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)>0,\ \mathrm{Re}(\beta)>0
such that
\alpha\beta=1
, then

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}F_{0}(\alpha,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\beta}F_{0}(\beta,\chi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{\pi}{d}\right)^{b+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{it}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{it}{4}+\frac{b}{2}\right)}\right|^{2}\Xi\left(-\frac{t}{2},\chi\right)\Xi\left(\frac{t}{2},\chi\right)\cos\left(\frac{t}{2}\log\alpha\right)dt.

The above theorem reduces to
[
5

depending on the parity of the character.

Path Dependent Feynman–Kac Formula for Forward Backward Stochastic Volterra Integral Equations
Hanxiao Wang, Jiongmin Yong and Jianfeng Zhang

Abstract




Abstract.

This paper is concerned with the relationship between forward-backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (FBSVIEs, for short) and a system of (non-local in time) path dependent partial differential equations (PPDEs, for short). Due to the nature of Volterra type equations, the usual flow property (or semigroup property) does not hold.
Inspired by Viens–Zhang
[
61
]
and Wang–Yong
[
66
]
, auxiliary processes are introduced so that the flow property of adapted solutions to the FBSVIEs is recovered in a suitable sense, and thus the functional Itô formula is applicable. Having achieved this stage, a natural PPDE is found so that the adapted solution of the backward SVIEs admits a representation in terms of the solution to the forward SVIE via the solution to a PPDE.
On the other hand, the solution of the PPDE admits a representation in terms of adapted solution to the (path dependent) FBSVIE, which is referred to as a Feynman–Kac formula. This leads to the existence and uniqueness of a classical solution to the PPDE, under smoothness conditions on the coefficients of the FBSVIEs. Further, when the smoothness conditions are relaxed with the backward component of FBSVIE being one-dimensional, a new (and suitable) notion of viscosity solution is introduced for the PPDE, for which a comparison principle of the viscosity solutions is established, leading to the uniqueness of the viscosity solution. Finally, some results have been extended to coupled FBSVIEs and type-II BSVIEs, and a representation formula for the path derivatives of PPDE solution is obtained by a closer investigation of linear FBSVIEs.

Keywords.
Forward-backward stochastic Volterra integral equation,
path dependent partial differential equation, Feynman–Kac formula,
viscosity solution, comparison principle.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete filtered probability space,
W
a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion,
\mathbb{F}\equiv\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
the natural filtration generated by
W
augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
{\cal F}
, and
T>0
a fixed time horizon. Consider the following (decoupled)
forward-backward stochastic differential equation
(FBSDE, for short): given initial data
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle X^{t,x}_{s}=x+\int_{t}^{s}b(r,X^{t,x}_{r})dr+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(r,X^{t,x}_{r})dW_{r},\\
\displaystyle Y^{t,x}_{s}=g(X^{t,x}_{T})+\int_{s}^{T}f(r,X^{t,x}_{r},Y^{t,x}_{r},Z^{t,x}_{r})dr-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{t,x}_{r}dW_{r},\end{array}\right.s\in[t,T],

(1.1)

where the coefficients
b,\sigma,f,g
are deterministic functions. Such an FBSDE is associated with the following terminal value problem of a
partial differential equation
(PDE, for short):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)+{1\over 2}\hbox{\rm tr$\,$}\big[\partial_{xx}^{2}u(t,x)\sigma(t,x)\sigma^{\top}(t,x)\big]+\partial_{x}u(t,x)b(t,x)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+f(t,x,u(t,x),\partial_{x}u(t,x)\sigma(t,x)\big)=0,\qquad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle u(T,x)=g(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

By the seminal works Peng
[
49
]
and Pardoux–Peng
[
48
]
, we have the nonlinear
Feynman–Kac formula
, representing the viscosity solution to PDE (
1.2
) by the adapted solution to FBSDE (
1.1
):

u(t,x)=Y^{t,x}_{t},\qquad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.3)

and on the other hand the adapted solution
(Y^{t,x},Z^{t,x})
to the
backward stochastic differential equation
(BSDE, for short), namely the second equation in (
1.1
), has the following
representation formula
via the solution to PDE (
1.2
):

Y^{t,x}_{s}=u(s,X_{s}^{t,x}),\qquad Z^{t,x}_{s}=\partial_{x}u(s,X^{t,x}_{s})\sigma(s,X^{t,x}_{s}),\qquad s\in[t,T],

(1.4)

provided
u
is smooth. The key for this PDE approach is the
flow property
, also called
semigroup property
and can be viewed as a type of
time consistency
, of the FBSDE. That is,

X^{t,x}_{r}=X^{s,X^{t,x}_{s}}_{r},\quad Y^{t,x}_{s}=Y^{r,u(r,\cdot);t,x}_{s},\quad Z^{t,x}_{s}=Z^{r,u(r,\cdot);t,x}_{s},\qquad t\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T,

(1.5)

where
(Y^{r,u(r,\cdot);t,x},Z^{r,u(r,\cdot);t,x})
is the solution to the BSDE on
[t,r]
with terminal condition
Y_{r}=u(r,X^{t,x}_{r})
. We remark that this approach remains effective for coupled FBSDEs (namely
b,\sigma
may depend on
(Y,Z)
), see Ma–Protter–Yong
[
45
]
, and even for more general situations, where
u
plays the role of the
decoupling field
for the forward-backward equations.

In this paper, our objective is to consider the following decoupled
forward-backward stochastic Volterra integral equation
(FBSVIE, for short) with solution triple
(X_{t},Y_{t},Z^{t}_{r})
,
0\leqslant t\leqslant r\leqslant T
:

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle X_{t}={\bf x}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,r,X_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,r,X_{r})dW_{r},\\
\displaystyle Y_{t}=g(t,X_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}f(t,r,X_{r},Y_{r},Z_{r}^{t})dr-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{r}^{t}dW_{r},\end{array}\right.\quad t\in[0,T].

(1.6)

Here the coefficients
b,\sigma,f
involve two time variables; the initial condition is a continuous path
{\bf x}\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
; and the terminal condition

depends on

{\bf x}=0
,

,

[

[

]
, El Euch–Rosenbaum
[

]
, and Viens–Zhang
[

]
. On the other hand, BSVIE has become a popular tool for studying many problems in mathematical finance. For examples, Di Persio
[
18
]
on stochastic differential utility, Yong
[
70
]
, Wang–Yong
[

]
and Agram
[

on dynamic risk measures, Kromer–Overbeck
[
39
]
on dynamic capital allocations, Wang–Sun–Yong
[
63

on equilibrium recursive utility and equilibrium dynamic risk measures, to mention a few. More interestingly, in recent years, time-inconsistent problems have attracted many researchers’ attention. Among others, the time-inconsistency could be caused by the time-preferences of the decision-makers, which can be described by non-exponential discounting. See the seminal paper by Strotz

Our goal of this paper is to extend the PDE approach to FBSVIEs. This on one hand will help us to understand the structure of FBSVIEs, and on the other hand is helpful for numerical computation of these equations. As mentioned, the PDE approach is based on the flow property of the equations. Unfortunately, due to the two time variable structure, neither FSVIE nor BSVIE satisfies the flow property in the standard sense: for
0\leqslant t<s\leqslant T
,

X_{s}\neq X_{t}+\!\!\int_{t}^{s}\!b(s,r,X_{r})dr\!+\!\int_{t}^{s}\!\sigma(s,r,X_{r})dW_{r},\quad\displaystyle Y_{t}\neq Y_{s}+\!\int_{t}^{s}\!f(t,r,X_{r},Y_{r},Z_{r}^{t})dr-\!\int_{t}^{s}\!Z_{r}^{t}dW_{r}.

Our work is built on Viens–Zhang
[
61
]
, Yong
[
73
]
and Wang–Yong
[
66
]
. By introducing auxiliary two time variable processes
\tilde{X}^{s}_{t}
,
\tilde{Y}^{t}_{s}
, see (
2.6
) and (
2.11
) below,
[
61
]
recovers the flow property of the FSVIE in certain sense, and
[
73
,
66
]
recover the flow property of the BSVIE. We remark that in
[
61
]
the backward equation is a standard BSDE, while in
[
73
,
66
]
the forward equation is a standard SDE. Putting together allows us to adopt the PDE approach to FBSVIE (
1.6
). We note that the associated PDE will intrinsically depend on the paths of
\tilde{X}^{[t,T]}_{t}
, and thus it becomes a
path dependent PDE
(PPDE, for short). Then, with a little extra effort, we can actually handle path dependent FBSVIEs, namely
b,\sigma,f,g
depend on the paths of
X
, as we will do in the paper. We shall emphasize though, even for the state dependent case (
1.6
), our results in the paper are new.

To be precise, we shall introduce a two-time variable function
U(t,s,{\bf x});

0\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T
,
{\bf x}\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which satisfies the PPDE with terminal condition
U(t,T,{\bf x})=g(t,{\bf x})
:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\partial_{s}U(t,s,{\bf x})+{1\over 2}\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,s,{\bf x}),\big(\sigma^{s,{\bf x}}_{[s,T]},\sigma^{s,{\bf x}}_{[s,T]}\big)\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{{\bf x}}U(t,s,{\bf x}),b^{s,{\bf x}}_{[s,T]}\big\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle+f\big(t,s,{\bf x},U(s,s,{\bf x}),\big\langle\partial_{{\bf x}}U(t,s,{\bf x}),\sigma^{s,{\bf x}}_{[s,T]}\big\rangle\big)=0.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\partial_{\bf x}U,\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U
are the first order and second order Fréchet derivatives with respect to the perturbation of
{\bf x}_{[s,T]}
, and for
\varphi=b,\sigma
,
\varphi^{s,{\bf x}}_{[s,T]}
refers to the path
\{\varphi(r,s,{\bf x})\}_{r\in[s,T]}
. Then we have the following relationship which extends (
1.4
): denoting
\hat{X}^{t}_{r}:=X_{r}1_{[0,t)}(r)+\tilde{X}^{r}_{t}1_{[t,T]}(r)
,

\displaystyle Y_{t}=U\big(t,t,\hat{X}^{t}\big),\quad Z^{t}_{s}=\big\langle\partial_{{\bf x}}U(t,s,\hat{X}^{s}),\,\sigma^{s,\hat{X}^{s}}_{[s,T]}\big\rangle,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{Y}^{t}_{s}=U(t,s,\hat{X}^{s}),

(1.10)

and similarly we can extend (
1.3
) to this case, see (
3.6
) below, and thus establish the Feyman–Kac formula for (
1
). Besides the key flow property, a crucial tool in this analysis is the functional Itô formula, initiated by Dupire
[
21
]
in standard SDE setting and extended to the SVIE setting by
[
61
]
. The PPDE (
1
) has several important features:

\bullet
The state variable
{\bf x}
has a continuous path on
[0,T]
, and thus is infinite dimensional.

\bullet

U
depends on two time variables
(t,s)
. In particular, the equation at
(t,s,{\bf x})
involves the value
U(s,s,{\bf x})
, and thus is non-local in the first time variable
t
.

\bullet
Alternatively, noting that (
1
) does not involve derivatives with respect to the first time variable
t
, then we may view
t
as a parameter instead of a variable. That is, we may view (
1
) as a system of PPDEs with parameter
t
and solution
\{U(t,\cdot,\cdot)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
. Then this is an (uncountably) infinite dimensional system of PPDEs which are self interacted through the diagonal term
U(s,s,{\bf x})
.

We next prove the existence of classical solutions to PPDE (
1
), provided the coefficients are smooth enough in an appropriate sense, and thus establish the above connection between PPDE (
1
) and FBSVIE (
1.6
) rigorously. We remark that Peng–Wang
[
52
]
obtained the classical solution in the form
u(t,{\bf x}_{[0,t]})
for a PPDE corresponding to PDE (
1.2
), associated with the path dependent version of the FBSDE (
1.1
). Our result generalizes
[
52
]
in several aspects. First, in
[
52
]

u(t,{\bf x}_{[0,t]})
depends on the path only up to
t
, in particular the path derivative
\partial_{\bf x}u
there involves only the perturbation of
{\bf x}_{t}
and thus is actually a finite dimensional derivative, while our path derivative is indeed a Fréchet derivative. Next,
u
is finite dimensional, while as mentioned (
1
) can be viewed as an infinite dimensional system. Moreover, when restricted to the state dependent case, the PPDE in
[
52
]
reduces back to the standard PDE (
1.2
), but (
1
) has the same features that both the state
{\bf x}
and the solution
U
are infinite dimensional. We also obtain a representation formula for the path derivative
\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x})
, which is interesting in its own right and is new in the literature.

The more challenging part is the viscosity solution theory for PPDE (
1
), in the case that
Y
is scalar but the coefficients are less smooth. Note that the state space
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
is not locally compact, so the standard viscosity solution theory of Crandall–Ishii–Lions
[
16
]
does not work here. Moreover, we have some intrinsic adaptiveness requirement on the dependence of the path, which prevents us from applying the viscosity solution theory in infinite dimensional space, see e.g. Crandall–Lions
[
17
]
, Li–Yong
[
42
]
, and Fabbri–Gozzi–Swiech
[
28
]
. One exception in this direction is Ren–Rosestolato
[
56
]
, which however requires some stronger type of regularity and is overall still not satisfactory for our purpose. We shall follow the approach proposed by Ekren–Keller–Touzi–Zhang
[
23
]
, where the pointwise optimization in
[
16
]
is replaced with an optimal stopping problem under certain nonlinear expectation, and thus the comparison principle can be obtained without requiring the local compactness of the state space. Our PPDE (
1
) has two major differences from
[
23
]
. First, the nonlinear expectation used in
[
23
]
relies on a family of semi-martinagle measures, while our state process
X
is not a semi-martingale. Second, the PPDE in
[
23
]
is one dimensional and the comparison principle for classical solutions (if they exist) is quite straightforward, but as mentioned PPDE (
1
) is non-local (or viewed as infinite dimensional), and in fact the comparison principle fails in general even for classical solutions. Nevertheless, we shall propose a new notion of viscosity solution to PPDE (
1
) and establish its well-posedness, including the comparison principle, under an additional assumption that
f
is nondecreasing in
y
. We note that this monotonicity condition is essentially the
proper
condition required in
[
16
]
for elliptic equations. For a standard parabolic equation like (
1.2
), this condition is redundant because it is implied from the Lipschitz condition by a standard change variable argument. However, the change variable argument fails for (
1
) because of its non-local structure. We also note that Wang–Yong
[
65
]
proved the comparison principle for BSVIEs under the same monotonicity condition. As in the standard literature, since viscosity solution is a local notion (even with some non-local feature here), its comparison principle is much more challenging.

Finally, we investigate briefly two more general FBSVIEs, the coupled FBSVIE (with
b,\sigma
depending on
Y
) and the type-II BSVIEs, and extend the representation formula in these cases. The more detailed studies on these equations are left for interested readers. We note particularly that our new representation formula for
\partial_{\bf x}U
relies on a linear type-II BSVIE. For this purpose, we establish a duality result for linear path dependent FSVIE which covers the corresponding results in Yong
[
69
,
71
]
and Peng–Yang
[
53
]
, and provide an explicit solution for linear BSVIEs which generalizes the result of Hu–Øksendal
[
36
]
.

Preliminaries

Let
T>0
be the time horizon,
\Omega:=C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
the canonical space,
W
the canonical process (namely
W(\omega)=\omega
),
\mathbb{P}
the Wiener measure (namely
W
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion under
\mathbb{P}
), and
\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{F}^{W}
augmented with the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets. Denote

\begin{array}[]{c}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{T}=[0,T],\qquad\mathbb{T}^{2}=[0,T]\times[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{2}_{-}=\big\{(t,s)\bigm|0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T\big\},\qquad\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}=\big\{(t,s)\bigm|0\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T\big\}.\end{array}

Here “
-
” indicates the left neighborhood of
t
, and “
+
” indicates the right neighborhood of
t
. For any Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
(say,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, etc.), let

\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\varphi:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable, }\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi(s)|^{p}ds<\infty\Big\}.

Our state space is
\mathbb{X}:=C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, equipped with the uniform norm:

\|{\bf x}\|=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|{\bf x}_{t}|,\qquad\forall{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}.

(2.1)

In this section we review and present some basic results concerning forward and backward SVIEs,
including a continuous-norm estimate for the adapted solution to a class of BSVIEs.
Moreover, among other things, we shall introduce two auxiliary processes
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
so that the flow property of the adapted solutions can be established in an extended sense. It turns out that such a property will play an essential role in proving the relation between FBSVIEs and PPDEs.

Before going further, we make a convention which will be used in the rest of the paper.
For any map
\varphi:\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{H}\times\Omega\to\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}
,
where
\mathbb{H}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}
are any Euclidean spaces (could be
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, etc.),
we simply say that
\varphi
is
progressively measurable
if

\varphi(t,r,{\bf x},h,\omega)=\varphi(t,r,{\bf x}_{r\land\cdot},h,\omega_{r\land\cdot}),\quad\forall(t,r,{\bf x},h,\omega)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{H}\times\Omega,

(2.2)

and the above map is measurable. In the above
\mathbb{T}^{2}
can be replaced by
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\pm}
; also some independent variables can be absent.

2.1
The well-posedness and flow property of FSVIEs

Given
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}
, consider an FSVIE:

\displaystyle X_{t}={\bf x}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,r,X_{\cdot})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,r,X_{\cdot})dW_{r},\quad t\in\mathbb{T}.

(2.3)

We shall assume the following.

Assumption 2.1
.

The map
(b,\sigma):\mathbb{T}^{2}_{-}\times\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
is progressively measurable satisfying:

(i)

For some constant
C_{0}>0
,
|b(t,r,{\bf 0})|+|\sigma(t,r,{\bf 0})|\leqslant C_{0}
for all
(t,r)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{-}.

(ii)

The map
{\bf x}\mapsto(b(t,r,{\bf x}),\sigma(t,r,{\bf x}))
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous under the norm
\|\cdot\|
.

(iii)

The map
t\mapsto(b(t,r,{\bf x}),\sigma(t,r,{\bf x}))
is differentiable, with
\partial_{t}b
and
\partial_{t}\sigma
also satisfying the conditions as in (i) and (ii).

The following result follows from Ruan–Zhang
[
58
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Under
Assumption
2.1
, FSVIE (
2.3
) admits a unique strong solution
X
such that
X
is continuous in
t
and the following estimate holds true: for any
p>1
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\|X\|^{p}\big]\leqslant C_{p}\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{p}\big].

(2.4)

We remark that due to the first time variable
t
in
\sigma
,
one cannot directly apply the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequalities in the Volterra setting. Assumption
2.1
(iii) helps us to get around that. In the state dependent case:
\sigma=\sigma(t,r,X_{r})
(
b
can be path dependent, although in the literature, typically, it is also state dependent), the well-posedness of (
2.3
) follows from standard arguments, see e.g. Berger–Mizel
[
8
]
. The pathwise continuity of
X
as well as the norm estimate (
2.4
) hold true for
b,\sigma
satisfying weaker continuity in the spirit of (
2.9
) below. The arguments are similar to those of Proposition
2.4
below and we skip the details. It will be interesting to see if it is possible to weaken
Assumption
2.1
(iii) in the path dependent case.

Note that
X
is neither a Markov process nor a semimartingale. Even worse, in general the flow property fails in the following sense: for fixed
t
,

\displaystyle X_{s}\neq X_{t}+\int_{t}^{s}b(s,r,X_{\cdot})dr+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(s,r,X_{\cdot})dW_{r},\quad s\in(t,T].

(2.5)

One may refer to this as the
time-inconsistency
. To overcome this deficiency, Viens–Zhang
[
61
]
introduced an auxiliary process with two time variables:

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{s}_{t}={\bf x}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,r,X_{\cdot})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,r,X_{\cdot})dW_{r},\quad(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}.

(2.6)

This process enjoys the following nice properties:

\bullet
For fixed
s
, the process
[0,s]\ni t\mapsto\widetilde{X}^{s}_{t}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-semimartingale with
\widetilde{X}^{t}_{t}=X_{t}
;

\bullet
For fixed
t
, the process
[t,T]\ni s\mapsto\widetilde{X}^{s}_{t}
is
{\cal F}_{t}
-measurable and continuous;

\bullet
The
flow property
holds in the following sense: for any
\mathbb{F}
-stopping time
\tau
,

\displaystyle X_{s}=\widetilde{X}^{s}_{\tau}+\int_{\tau}^{s}b(s,r,X_{\cdot})dr+\int_{\tau}^{s}\sigma(s,r,X_{\cdot})dW_{r},\quad s\in[\tau,T].

(2.7)

We remark that, in the state dependent case as in (
1.6
), (
2.7
) implies

X_{s}=\widetilde{X}^{s}_{t}+\int_{t}^{s}b(s,r,X_{r})dr+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(s,r,X_{r})dW_{r},\quad(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}.

One can easily see that, conditional on
\widetilde{X}^{[t,T]}_{t}
,
X_{[0,t)}
and
X_{(t,T]}
are conditionally independent. So this can be viewed as a generalized Markov property.

2.2
The well-posedness and flow property of BSVIEs

Consider the following path dependent BSVIE:

\displaystyle Y_{t}=g(t,X_{\cdot})+\int_{t}^{T}f(t,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r},Z^{t}_{r})dr-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\quad t\in\mathbb{T},

(2.8)

where
Y
is
m
-dimensional and hence
Z
is
(m\times d)
-dimensional. We shall assume

Assumption 2.3
.

The map
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is progressively measurable and the map
g:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable satisfying:

(i)

For some constant
C_{0}>0
, it holds

|f(t,r,{\bf x},0,0)|+|g(t,{\bf x})|\leqslant C_{0}\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|\big],\qquad\forall(t,r,{\bf x})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X};

(ii)

The map
(y,z)\mapsto f(t,r,{\bf x},y,z)
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous;

(iii)

The map
t\mapsto(f(t,r,{\bf x},y,z),g(t,{\bf x}))
is locally uniformly continuous in the following sense: for some modulus of continuity function
\rho
,

\displaystyle|f(t-\delta,r,{\bf x},y,z)-f(t,r,{\bf x},y,z)|+|g(t-\delta,{\bf x})-g(t,{\bf x})|

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|+|y|+|z|\big]\rho(\delta),\quad\forall(t,r,{\bf x},y,z)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},\,\delta\in[0,t].

Proposition 2.4
.

Under
Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
, BSVIE (
2.8
) admits a unique strong solution
(Y,Z)
such that
Y
is continuous in
t
and the following estimate holds true: for each
p>1
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|Y_{t}|^{p}\Big]+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{t}_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{{p\over 2}}\Big]\leqslant C_{p}\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{p}\big].

(2.10)

The proof of the well-posedness of BSVIE (
2.8
) is standard and could be found in Yong
[
71
]
, where the pathwise continuity of
Y
was proved for a more general BSVIE, but under much stronger technical conditions. Our arguments for the pathwise continuity and the above estimate (
2.10
) seems to be new in the literature. We note that (
2.9
) is much weaker than Assumption
2.1
(iii), because
f
is state dependent on
(Y,Z)
. To facilitate the proof, we introduce the following standard BSDE parameterized by
t\in\mathbb{T}
with adapted solution
(\widetilde{Y}^{t},\widetilde{Z}^{t})
:

\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}=g(t,X_{\cdot})+\int_{s}^{T}f(t\land r,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r},\widetilde{Z}^{t}_{r})dr-\int_{s}^{T}\widetilde{Z}^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\quad s\in[0,T].

(2.11)

Proof.

First, from
[
71
]
we know (
2.8
) admits a unique solution
(Y,Z)
such that
Y\in\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
Z^{t}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})
. Compare (
2.8
) with the following linear BSDE on
[t,T]
:

\widehat{Y}^{t}_{s}=g(t,X_{\cdot})+\int_{s}^{T}f(t\land r,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r},Z^{t}_{r})dr-\int_{s}^{T}\widehat{Z}^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\quad s\in[t,T].

It is obvious that
\widehat{Y}^{t}_{t}=Y_{t}
and
\widehat{Z}^{t}_{r}=Z^{t}_{r}
. This implies that
(\widehat{Y}^{t},\widehat{Z}^{t})
satisfies (
2.11
). Then we have

\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{t}=Y_{t},\quad\widetilde{Z}^{t}_{r}=Z^{t}_{r},\quad(t,r)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}.

(2.12)

Next, by (
2.4
), (
2.9
) and the standard BSDE arguments, we have

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant T}|\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z^{t}_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\leqslant C_{p}\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{p}\big];\\
\displaystyle|\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{t^{\prime}}_{s}|\leqslant C_{p}\Big[1+\big(\mathbb{E}_{s}[\|X\|^{p}]\big)^{1\over p}\Big]\rho(|t-t^{\prime}|),~\hbox{\rm a.s.},\quad\forall t,t^{\prime},s\in\mathbb{T}.\end{array}\right.

(2.13)

Note that
\widetilde{Y}_{s}^{t}
,
\widetilde{Y}_{s}^{t^{\prime}}
, and
\mathbb{E}_{s}[\|X\|^{p}]
are pathwise continuous in
s
, then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\mathbb{T}}|\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{t^{\prime}}_{s}|\leqslant C_{p}\Big[1+\sup_{s\in\mathbb{T}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{s}[\|X\|^{p}]\big)^{1\over p}\Big]\rho(|t-t^{\prime}|),~\hbox{\rm a.s.},\quad\forall t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}.

(2.14)

Note that, by (standard) Doob’s maximum inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}_{s}[\|X\|^{p}]\Big]\leqslant C_{p}\Big(\mathbb{E}[\|X\|^{2p}]\Big)^{1\over 2}\leqslant C_{p}\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{p}\big]<\infty.

Let
\{t_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 1}
be the rationals in
[0,T]
.
There exits an
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega
such that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{1})=1
,
\widetilde{Y}^{t_{i}}_{s}(\omega)
is continuous in
s
, and

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\mathbb{T}}|\widetilde{Y}^{t_{i}}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{t_{j}}_{s}|(\omega)\leqslant C_{p}(\omega)\rho(|t_{i}-t_{j}|),\quad\forall(i,j),~\forall\omega\in\Omega_{1},\\
\displaystyle\mbox{where}\quad C_{p}(\omega):=\Big[1+\sup_{s\in\mathbb{T}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{s}[\|X\|^{p}]\big)^{1\over p}\Big](\omega)<\infty,\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega_{1}.\end{array}\right.

For any
t\in\mathbb{T}
, by (
2.14
), there exists an
\Omega^{t}\subset\Omega
such that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{t})=1
and

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\mathbb{T}}|\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{t_{j}}_{s}|(\omega)\leqslant C_{p}(\omega)\rho(|t-t_{j}|),\quad\forall j,~\forall\omega\in\Omega^{t}\cap\Omega_{1}.

(2.18)

For any
(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, we define

\displaystyle\bar{Y}^{t}_{s}(\omega):=\limsup_{t_{j}\to t}\widetilde{Y}^{t_{j}}_{s}(\omega),\quad\omega\in\Omega.

(2.19)

By (
2.2
) we see that the above
\limsup
is actually a limit for
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
. Then, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
,

\displaystyle\bar{Y}^{t}_{s}(\omega)~\mbox{is continuous in $s$, }\quad\sup_{s\in\mathbb{T}}|\bar{Y}^{t}_{s}-\bar{Y}^{t^{\prime}}_{s}|(\omega)\leqslant C_{p}(\omega)\rho(|t-t^{\prime}|),\quad\forall t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}.

(2.20)

So
\bar{Y}
is (uniformly) continuous in
(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
. Moreover, by (
2.18
) we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}(\omega)=\bar{Y}^{t}_{s}(\omega),\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{T},~\forall\omega\in\Omega^{t}\cap\Omega_{1}.

(2.21)

Since
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{t}\cap\Omega_{1})=1
, so
\bar{Y}
is a desired version of
\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}
,
and thus, by always considering this version,
\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}
is jointly continuous in
(t,s)
, a.s.
In particular, this implies that
Y_{t}=\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{t}
is continuous in
t
, a.s.

Finally, applying the standard BSDE estimates on (
2.11
) we have

|Y_{t}|^{p}=|\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{t}|^{p}\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[1+\|X\|^{p}+\int_{t}^{T}|Y_{r}|^{p}dr\Big],\quad\hbox{\rm a.s.}

Since
t\mapsto Y_{t}
is continuous almost surely, we obtain from the Doob’s maximum inequality that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|Y_{t}|^{p}\Big]\leqslant C_{p}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[1+\|X\|^{2p}+\int_{0}^{T}|Y_{r}|^{2p}dr\Big]\Big)^{1\over 2}\leqslant C_{p}\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{p}\big],

where the second inequality thanks to (
2.4
) and the first line of (
2.13
). This, together with the first line of (
2.13
) again, implies (
2.10
).

Similar to the forward case, in general the flow property fails in the following sense: for fixed
s
,

Y_{t}\neq Y_{s}+\int_{t}^{s}f(t,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r},Z^{t}_{r})dr-\int_{t}^{s}Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\quad t\in[0,s).

(2.22)

However, we may recover the flow property by utilizing the auxiliary process
\widetilde{Y}
:

Y_{t}=\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}+\int_{t}^{s}f(t,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r},Z^{t}_{r})dr-\int_{t}^{s}Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\quad t\in[0,s].

(2.23)

2.3
The functional Itô formula

The materials in this subsection follow from Viens–Zhang
[
61
]
. Recall
\mathbb{X}:=C(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and define

\begin{array}[]{c}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{t}:=C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n});\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{X}}:=D(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\equiv\Big\{{\bf x}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|{\bf x}\hbox{ is right-continuous with left-limits}\Big\}.\end{array}

(2.24)

Clearly,
\mathbb{X}
is a subset of
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
. Also, hereafter, for any
\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{t}
, we automatically extend it to be zero on
[0,t)
, still denote it by
\eta
. Then
\mathbb{X}_{t}\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
. Next, we define

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{X},\quad\widehat{\Lambda}:=\Big\{(t,{\bf x})\in\mathbb{T}\times\widehat{\mathbb{X}}:{\bf x}|_{[t,T]}\in\mathbb{X}_{t}\Big\},\quad\displaystyle{\bf d}\big((t,{\bf x}),(t^{\prime},{\bf x}^{\prime})\big):=|t-t^{\prime}|+\|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}\|,

with
\|{\bf x}\|=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}|{\bf x}_{t}|
for
{\bf x}\in\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
.
It can be shown that
{\bf d}
is a metric under which
\widehat{\Lambda}
is a complete metric space. Now, let
C^{0}(\widehat{\Lambda})
denote the set of all functions
u:\widehat{\Lambda}\to\mathbb{R}
which are continuous under
{\bf d}
. For any
u\in C^{0}(\widehat{\Lambda})
and given
(t,{\bf x})\in\widehat{\Lambda}
, define

\partial_{t}u(t,{\bf x})=\lim_{\delta\mathop{\downarrow}0}{u(t+\delta,{\bf x})-u(t,{\bf x})\over\delta},

(2.25)

provided the limit exists, and define
\partial_{\bf x}u(t,{\bf x})
as the Fréchet derivative with respect to
{\bf x}|_{[t,T]}
, namely
\partial_{\bf x}u(t,{\bf x}):\mathbb{X}_{t}\to\mathbb{R}
is the linear functional satisfying the following:

u(t,{\bf x}+\eta)-u(t,{\bf x})=\langle\partial_{\bf x}u(t,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle+o(\|\eta\|),\qquad\forall\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{t}.

(2.26)

It is clear that this is equal to the Gâteux derivative:

\langle\partial_{\bf x}u(t,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{u(t,{\bf x}+\varepsilon\eta)-u(t,{\bf x})\over\varepsilon},\qquad\forall\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{t}.

(2.27)

Similarly we define the second order derivative
\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}u(t,{\bf x})
as a bilinear functional on
\mathbb{X}_{t}\times\mathbb{X}_{t}
:

\langle\partial_{\bf x}u(t,{\bf x}+\eta),\eta^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\partial_{\bf x}u(t,{\bf x}),\eta^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}u(t,{\bf x}),(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\rangle+o(\|\eta\|),\qquad\forall\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}_{t}.

(2.28)

Definition 2.5
.

Let
C^{1,2}_{+}(\widehat{\Lambda})
denote the set of
u\in C^{0}(\widehat{\Lambda})
such that
\partial_{t}u,\partial_{\bf x}u,\partial_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}^{2}u
exist on
\mathbb{T}\times\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
and satisfy:

(i)

There exist constants
\kappa>0
and
C>0
such that, for any
(t,{\bf x})
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}u(t,{\bf x})|+\!\!\!\!\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{t},\,\|\eta\|\leqslant 1}\!\!\!\!|\langle\partial_{\bf x}u(t,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle|+\!\!\!\!\sup_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}_{t},\,\|\eta\|,\|\eta^{\prime}\|\leqslant 1}\!\!\!\!|\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}u(t,{\bf x}),(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\rangle|\leqslant C[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{\kappa}].

(ii)

For any
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}
,
\partial_{t}u(t,{\bf x})
,
\langle\partial_{\bf x}u(t,{\bf x}),\eta|_{[t,T]}\rangle
,
\langle\partial_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}u(t,{\bf x}),(\eta|_{[t,T]},\eta^{\prime}|_{[t,T]})\rangle
are continuous in
(t,{\bf x})
, where the continuity in
t
always means right-continuity.

(iii)

There exist
\kappa>0
and a modulus of continuity function
\rho
such that:

\big|\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}u(t,{\bf x})-\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}u(t,{\bf x}^{\prime}),(\eta,\eta)\rangle\big|\leqslant\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{\kappa}+\|{\bf x}^{\prime}\|^{\kappa}\big]\|\eta\|^{2}\rho(\|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}\|).

We remark that the function
u
will be involved in some backward equations, so both in (

t

\eta|_{[t,T]}
and in all the arguments in

\Lambda

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}u_{1}(t,{\bf x})=\partial_{t}u_{2}(t,{\bf x}),\quad\langle\partial_{\bf x}u_{1}(t,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle=\langle\partial_{\bf x}u_{2}(t,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}u_{1}(t,{\bf x}),(\eta,\eta)\rangle=\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}u_{2}(t,{\bf x}),(\eta,\eta)\rangle.\end{array}\right.

Let

2.4

{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable satisfying:

\check{I}_{p}^{p}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|\check{f}(t,r,\cdot,0,0)|dr\Big)^{p}+|\check{g}(t,\cdot)|^{p}\Big]<\infty,

(2.36)

and the map
(y,z)\mapsto\check{f}(t,r,\omega,y,z)
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous.

From Shi–Wang–Yong
[
59
]
, we have the following standard result.

Proposition 2.11
.

Under
Assumption
2.10
, BSVIE (
2.35
) admits a unique strong solution
(\check{Y},\check{Z})
such that
the following estimate holds true:

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\check{Y}_{t}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|\check{Z}^{t}_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{{p\over 2}}\Big]\leqslant C_{p}\check{I}_{p}^{p},\quad\mbox{where $\check{I}_{p}^{p}$ is defined in \eqref{Ip2}}.

(2.37)

Another important property of BSVIEs is the following comparison principle, due to Wang–Yong
[
65
]
. For
y,\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we say
y\leqslant\tilde{y}
if
y_{i}\leqslant\tilde{y}_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,m
.

Proposition 2.12
.

For
k=1,2
, let
\check{f}^{k},\check{g}^{k}
satisfy
Assumption
2.10
and
(\check{Y}^{k},\check{Z}^{k})
be the solutions to the corresponding BSVIE (
2.35
).
Assume
\check{f}^{1}\leqslant\check{f}^{2}
and
\check{g}^{1}\leqslant\check{g}^{2}
. Assume further that, either for
k=1
or
k=2
,
\check{f}^{k}
is nondecreasing in
y
(in the componentwise sense), and
\check{f}^{k}_{i}
does not depend on
z_{j}
, for
i\neq j
, where
z_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the
j
-th row of
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
. Then we have
\check{Y}^{1}_{t}\leqslant\check{Y}^{2}_{t}
,
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
, a.s.

3
The Path Dependent Feynman–Kac Formula

In this section, we are going to establish the relations between PPDEs and FBSVIEs.

3.1
From PPDE to FBSVIE

Recall the FSVIEs (
2.3
)–(
2.6
) and BSVIEs (
2.8
)–(
2.11
).
Recall (
1
), let us introduce the following system of PPDEs:

\left\{\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle{\cal L}U(t,s,{\bf x}):=\partial_{s}U(t,s,{\bf x})+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,s,{\bf x}),(\sigma^{s,{\bf x}},\sigma^{s,{\bf x}})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}),b^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+f\big(t,s,{\bf x},U(s,s,{\bf x}),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}),\sigma^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle\big)=0,\quad(t,s,{\bf x})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle U(t,T,{\bf x})=g(t,{\bf x}),\qquad(t,{\bf x})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{X},\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where, for
\varphi=b,\sigma
,
\varphi^{s,{\bf x}}
is defined by (
2.30
). As we see in (
2.31
),
{\bf x}
will correspond to
X\oplus_{s}\widetilde{X}_{s}
, rather than
X
.
However, due to the required adaptiveness, one has

\varphi^{s,X\oplus_{s}\widetilde{X}_{s}}_{r}=\varphi(r,s,X\oplus_{s}\widetilde{X}_{s})=\varphi(r,s,X)=\varphi^{s,X}_{r},~~r\in[s,T],\quad\hbox{for}~\varphi=b,\sigma.

We emphasize that the derivatives in
{\cal L}U(t,s,{\bf x})
are with respect to
(s,{\bf x})
only. As mentioned in Introduction, since (
3.1
) involves the diagonal value
U(s,s,{\bf x})
, it is non-local in the first time variable
t
. Alternatively, if we view
t
as a parameter rather than an independent variable, then (
3.1
) is an (uncountably) infinite dimensional PPDE system self-interacted through
U(s,s,{\bf x})
.

We call
U\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X})
a
classical solution
to the PPDE (
3.1
)
if
U(t,\cdot)\in C^{1,2}_{+}([t,T]\times\mathbb{X})
for all
t\in\mathbb{T}
,
where
C^{1,2}_{+}([t,T]\times\mathbb{X})
is defined in the spirit of Definitions
2.5
and
2.6
, but restrict to
s\in[t,T]
only, and (
3.1
) is satisfied in the classical sense.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under
Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
,
if the PPDE (
3.1
) has a classical solution
U
, then, for any
(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}
and recalling the
\widehat{X}
in (
2.31
),

\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}=U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\quad Y_{t}=U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{t}),\quad Z^{t}_{s}=\widetilde{Z}^{t}_{s}=\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\,\sigma^{s,X}\rangle.

(3.2)

Proof.

Fix
t
. Applying the functional Itô formula (
2.31
) to
U(t,\cdot,\widehat{X}^{\cdot})
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle dU(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})=\partial_{s}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})ds+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),b^{s,X}\rangle ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\,(\sigma^{s,X},\sigma^{s,X})\rangle ds+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\sigma^{s,X}\rangle dW_{s}.\end{array}

Since
U
satisfies the PPDE (
3.1
), the above implies that

dU(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})=-f\big(t,s,X,U(s,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\sigma^{s,X}\rangle\big)ds+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\sigma^{s,X}\rangle dW_{s}.

(3.3)

Note that
U(t,T,X)=g(t,T,X)
, integrating (
3.3
) over
[t,T]
we have:

\displaystyle U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{t})

\displaystyle=g(t,T,X)+\int_{t}^{T}f\big(t,r,X,U(r,r,\widehat{X}^{r}),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,r,\widehat{X}^{r}),\sigma^{r,X}\rangle\big)dr

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t}^{T}\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,r,\widehat{X}^{r}),\sigma^{r,X}\rangle dW_{r}.

That is,
(\widehat{Y}_{t},\,\widehat{Z}^{t}_{s}):=(U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{t}),\,\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\sigma^{s,X}\rangle)
satisfies BSVIE (
2.8
).
Then, from the uniqueness of BSVIEs we obtain
\widehat{Y}=Y
and
\widehat{Z}=Z
, hence the last two formulae in (
3.2
).
Moreover, by substituting these into (
3.3
), we have

dU(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})=-f(t,s,X,Y_{s},Z^{t}_{s})ds+Z^{t}_{s}dW_{s}.

This clearly implies the first formula in (
3.2
).

3.2
From FBSVIE to PPDE

In this subsection we proceed with the opposite direction: constructing
U(t,s,{\bf x})
by using FBSVIEs.
We emphasize again that the
{\bf x}
here corresponds to
X\oplus_{s}\widetilde{X}_{s}
.

First, for any
(s,{\bf x})\in\Lambda
, denote
X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}:={\bf x}_{l}
,
l\in[0,s]
, and consider the following FBSVIE:

\left.\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}={\bf x}_{l}+\int_{s}^{l}b(l,r,X_{\cdot}^{s,{\bf x}})dr+\int_{s}^{l}\sigma(l,r,X_{\cdot}^{s,{\bf x}})dW_{r};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}=g(l,X_{\cdot}^{s,{\bf x}})+\int_{l}^{T}f(l,r,X_{\cdot}^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r})dr-\int_{l}^{T}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}dW_{r},\end{array}\right.\quad l\in[s,T].

(3.4)

Next, given
(t,s,{\bf x})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}
, consider the following standard FBSDE:

\left.\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{s,{\bf x}}_{r,l}:={\bf x}_{l}+\int_{s}^{r}b(l,r^{\prime},X_{\cdot}^{s,{\bf x}})dr^{\prime}+\int_{s}^{r}\sigma(l,r^{\prime},X_{\cdot}^{s,{\bf x}})dW_{r^{\prime}},\quad s\leqslant r\leqslant l\leqslant T;\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{l}=g(t,X_{\cdot}^{s,{\bf x}})+\int_{l}^{T}f(t,r,X_{\cdot}^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r})dr-\int_{l}^{T}\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}dW_{r},\quad l\in[s,T].\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

We now define

U(t,s,{\bf x}):=\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{s},\quad(t,s,{\bf x})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}.

(3.6)

It is obvious that
\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{l}
is
\sigma(W_{r}-W_{s},r\in[s,l])
-measurable,
so the above
U(t,s,{\bf x})
is deterministic.

Theorem 3.2
.

Under
Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
,
if the function
U
defined by (
3.6
) is continuous in all variables and,
for any fixed
t
,
\partial_{\bf x}U(t,\cdot),\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,\cdot)
exist and satisfy the requirements in
Definition
2.5
(in the sense of
Definition
2.6
),
then
U
is a classical solution of the PPDE (
3.1
).

Proof.

First, note that
U(s,s,{\bf x})=\widetilde{Y}^{s,s,{\bf x}}_{s}=Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{s}
.
For any
s\leqslant r\leqslant l\leqslant T
, by (
2.7
) we have

X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}=X^{r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}}_{l},\quad\mbox{where}\quad\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}_{r^{\prime}}:=\big(X^{s,{\bf x}}\oplus_{r}\widetilde{X}^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}\big)_{r^{\prime}}:=X^{s,{\bf x}}_{r^{\prime}}{\bf 1}_{[0,r)}(r^{\prime})+\widetilde{X}^{s,{\bf x}}_{r,r^{\prime}}{\bf 1}_{[r,T]}(r^{\prime}).

(3.7)

Then by the uniqueness of BSVIEs and BSDEs we have

Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}=U(r,r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}),\quad\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}=U(t,r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}).

(3.8)

We next establish the representation for
\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}
:

\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}=\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}),\sigma^{r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}}\rangle,\quad\hbox{\rm a.s.},\quad s\leqslant r\leqslant T.

(3.9)

Fix
\delta>0
and let
s=s_{0}<\cdots<s_{n}=T
be such that
\Delta s_{i}:=s_{i}-s_{i-1}\leqslant\delta
.
Denote

Y^{n}_{r}:=U(t,s_{i+1},\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}),\quad Z^{n}_{r}:=\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s_{i+1},\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}),\sigma^{r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}}\rangle,\quad r\in[s_{i},s_{i+1}].

Note that
(Y^{n}_{r},Z^{n}_{r})
is
{\cal F}_{r}
-measurable.
Fix
t
and then apply the functional Itô formula (
2.31
) to
U(t,s_{i+1},\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},\cdot})
(with time variable fixed),
we get

dY^{n}_{r}=\Big[{1\over 2}\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,s_{i+1},\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}),\,(\sigma^{r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}},\sigma^{r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}})\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s_{i+1},\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}),b^{r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}}\big\rangle\Big]dr+Z^{n}_{r}dW_{r}.

Denote
\Delta Y^{n}_{r}:=Y^{n}_{r}-\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}
,
\Delta Z^{n}_{r}:=Z^{n}_{r}-\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}
.
Note that
\Delta Y^{n}_{s_{i+1}}=0
, and

\displaystyle d\Delta Y^{n}_{r}=\widehat{f}(t,r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{n}_{r}+\Delta Z^{n}_{r})dr+\Delta Z^{n}_{r}dW_{r},\quad\mbox{where}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(t,r,\widehat{\bf x},y,z):={1\over 2}\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,s_{i+1},\widehat{\bf x}),\,(\sigma^{r,\widehat{\bf x}},\sigma^{r,\widehat{\bf x}})\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s_{i+1},\widehat{\bf x}),b^{r,\widehat{\bf x}}\big\rangle+f(t,r,\widehat{\bf x},y,z).

By standard BSDE arguments we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s_{i}\leqslant r\leqslant s_{i+1}}|\Delta Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}+\int_{s_{i}}^{s_{i+1}}|\Delta Z^{n}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big]

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{s_{i}}^{s_{i+1}}\big|\widehat{f}(t,r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{n}_{r})\big|dr\Big)^{2}\Big]\leqslant C\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{4+2\kappa}\big]\delta\Delta s_{i+1},

where
\kappa
is the generic order of polynomial growth in Definition
2.5
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{s_{i}}^{s_{i+1}}\Big|\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}-\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s_{i+1},\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}),\sigma^{r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}}\rangle\Big|^{2}dr\Big]\leqslant C\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{4+2\kappa}\big]\delta.

Send
\delta\to 0
, by the (right) continuity of
\partial_{\bf x}U
we obtain (
3.9
).

Moreover, set
s_{1}:=s+\delta
, by (
3.10
) again we have

U(t,s+\delta,{\bf x})-U(t,s,{\bf x})=\Delta Y^{n}_{s}=-\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\bar{f}(t,r,X^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{n}_{r})dr\Big]-R(\delta),

(3.12)

where

\displaystyle\bar{f}(t,r,\widehat{\bf x},y,z):={1\over 2}\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,r,\widehat{\bf x}),(\sigma^{r,\widehat{\bf x}},\sigma^{r,\widehat{\bf x}})\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,r,\widehat{\bf x}),b^{r,\widehat{\bf x}}\big\rangle+f(t,r,\widehat{\bf x},y,z),

\displaystyle R(\delta):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\big[\widehat{f}(t,r,X^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{n}_{r}+\Delta Z^{n}_{r})-\widehat{f}(t,r,X^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{n}_{r})\big]dr

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\big[\widehat{f}(t,r,X^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{n}_{r})-\bar{f}(t,r,X^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{n}_{r})\big]dr\Big].

By (
3.11
) and the regularity of
U
we have

\displaystyle|R(\delta)|^{2}\leqslant C\delta\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{s+\delta}|\Delta Z^{n}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big]+o(\delta^{2})\leqslant C\big[1+\|{\bf x}\|^{4+2\kappa}\big]\delta^{3}+o(\delta^{2}).

Divide the both sides of (
3.12
) by
\delta
and send
\delta\to 0
,
by the desired continuity we see that
\partial_{s}U(t,s,{\bf x})
exists and

\displaystyle-\partial_{s}U(t,s,{\bf x})=\bar{f}\big(t,s,{\bf x},U(s,s,{\bf x}),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}),\sigma^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle\big)

\displaystyle\quad={1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,s,{\bf x}),(\sigma^{s,{\bf x}},\sigma^{s,{\bf x}})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}),b^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle+f(t,s,{\bf x},U(s,s,{\bf x}),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}),\sigma^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle).

This implies that
\partial_{s}U(t,s,{\bf x})
has the desired regularity and
{\cal L}U(t,s,{\bf x})=0
.

Finally, clearly
U(t,T,{\bf x})=\widetilde{Y}^{t,T,{\bf x}}_{T}=g(t,T,{\bf x})
, thus
U
is a classical solution of PPDE (
3.1
).

3.3
Classical solutions of the PPDE

In this subsection, we provide some sufficient conditions so that the function
U
defined by (
3.6
) has the desired regularity
and thus is the unique classical solution of the PPDE (
3.1
).

We first note that, since the derivatives of
U
involve càdlàg paths,
we shall assume the coefficients
b,\sigma,f,g
can be extended to
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
,
and we will use the same notations.
The derivatives of
f
with respect to
(y,z)

and those of
(b,\sigma,f,g)
with respect to the first time variable
t
are in the standard sense, while those with respect to the second time variable will not be needed.
Given an adapted function
\varphi:\hat{\Lambda}\to\mathbb{R}
,
the derivative with respect to
{\bf x}
is the Fréchet derivative as a linear operator on
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
:

\varphi(t,{\bf x}+\eta)-\varphi(t,{\bf x})=\langle D\varphi(t,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle+o(\|\eta\|).

(3.13)

We emphasize that in (
2.26
) and (
2.28
) the perturbation path
\eta
is on
[t,T]
,
while here
\eta
is on
[0,T]
(actually on
[0,t]
due to the adaptedness).
Similarly we define
D^{2}\varphi
as a bilinear operator on
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
.

We say
D\varphi
is bounded if
|\langle D\varphi(t,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle|\leqslant C\|\eta\|
for all
(t,{\bf x},\eta)\in\widehat{\Lambda}\times\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
,
and
D\varphi
is continuous if, for any
\eta\in\widehat{\mathbb{X}}
, the mapping
(t,{\bf x})\in\widehat{\Lambda}\mapsto\langle D\varphi(t,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle
is continuous.
Similarly
D^{2}\varphi
is bounded if
|\langle D^{2}\varphi(t,{\bf x}),(\eta^{\prime},\eta)\rangle|\leqslant C\|\eta^{\prime}\|\|\eta\|
and continuous if
(t,{\bf x})\in\widehat{\Lambda}\mapsto\langle D^{2}\varphi(t,{\bf x}),(\eta^{\prime},\eta)\rangle
is continuous.
When the mapping
{\bf x}\in\widehat{\mathbb{X}}\mapsto D\varphi(t,{\bf x})
is continuous, one can easily show that

\displaystyle\varphi(t,{\bf x}+\eta)-\varphi(t,{\bf x})=\int_{0}^{1}\langle D\varphi(t,{\bf x}+\theta\eta),\eta\rangle d\theta.

(3.14)

Moreover, we may switch the order of differentiation:
\partial_{t}D\varphi=D\partial_{t}\varphi
, if one of them is continuous.

Assumption 3.3
.

Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
hold, and the dependence of
b,\sigma,f,g
on
{\bf x}
can be extended to
\widehat{\Lambda}
, stilled using the same notations, such that

(i)

For
\varphi=b,\sigma,g
,
\varphi
is twice differentiable in
{\bf x}
with bounded derivatives, and
D^{2}\varphi
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous in
{\bf x}
;

(ii)

f
is jointly twice differentiable in
({\bf x},y,z)
with bounded derivatives, and the second order derivatives are uniformly Lipschitz continuous in
({\bf x},y,z)
;

(iii)

b,\sigma
are differentiable in the first time variable
t
, and
\partial_{t}b,\partial_{t}\sigma
satisfy the requirements in (i).

Theorem 3.4
.

Under
Assumption
3.3
, the function
U
defined by (
3.6
) is the unique classical solution of PPDE (
3.1
).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
, it suffices to verify the required regularities of
U
.
We shall repeatedly apply Propositions
2.9
and
2.11
.
In the proof we may abuse the notations
\Delta X
,
\check{b}
etc, and we may omit the variable
\omega
.
We proceed in three steps.

Step 1.
In this step we show that

\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}),\eta\rangle=\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{s},\quad(s,{\bf x})\in\widehat{\Lambda},~t\leqslant s,~\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{s},

(3.15)

where
(\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}},\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}},\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}})
solve the following linear system with random coefficients on
[s,T]
: denoting
\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}:=0
for
l\in[0,s]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}=\eta_{l}+\int_{s}^{l}\langle Db(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle dr+\int_{s}^{l}\langle D\sigma(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle dW_{r};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}=\langle Dg(l,X^{s,{\bf x}}),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle-\int_{l}^{T}\nabla_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}dW_{r}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{l}^{T}\Big[\langle Df(\cdot),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle+\partial_{y}f(\cdot)\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}+\partial_{z}f(\cdot)\nabla_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}\Big](l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r})dr;\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{l}=\langle Dg(t,X^{s,{\bf x}}),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle-\int_{l}^{T}\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}dW_{r}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{l}^{T}\Big[\langle Df(\cdot),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle+\partial_{y}f(\cdot)\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}+\partial_{z}f(\cdot)\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}\Big](t,r,X^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r})dr.\end{array}

(3.16)

Indeed, first by Propositions
2.9
and
2.11
, and by standard BSDE arguments (see, e.g.,
[
75
, Chapter 4]
) we see that the above system (
3.16
) is wellposed, and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\|^{p}\big]\leqslant C_{p}\|\eta\|^{p};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sup_{l\in[s,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{l}^{T}|\nabla_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\|^{p}\big]\leqslant C_{p}\|\eta\|^{p};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{l\in[s,T]}|\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{l}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{s}^{T}|\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\|^{p}\big]+C_{p}\sup_{l\in[s,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[|\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}|^{p}\big]\leqslant C_{p}\|\eta\|^{p}.\end{array}

(3.17)

Next, denote

\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}:=X^{s,{\bf x}+\eta}-X^{s,{\bf x}}-\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}},\quad\Delta_{\eta}\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}:=\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}+\eta}-\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}-\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}},

and similarly for
\Delta_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}},\,\Delta_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}},\,\Delta_{\eta}\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}
.
Then
\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}=0
for
l\in[0,s]
, and for
l\in[s,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}=\int_{s}^{l}\check{b}(l,r,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})dr+\int_{s}^{l}\check{\sigma}(l,r,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})dW_{r};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\Delta_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}=\check{g}(l,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})+\int_{l}^{T}\check{f}(l,r,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}},\Delta_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},\Delta_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r})dr-\int_{l}^{T}\Delta_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}dW_{r};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\Delta_{\eta}\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{l}=\check{g}(t,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})+\!\!\int_{l}^{T}\check{f}(t,r,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}},\Delta_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},\Delta_{\eta}\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r})dr-\!\!\int_{l}^{T}\Delta_{\eta}\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}dW_{r},\end{array}

(3.18)

where, for
\varphi=b,\sigma,g
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\check{\varphi}(l,r,{\bf x}^{\prime}):=\varphi(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}+\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}+{\bf x}^{\prime})-\varphi(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}})-\langle D\varphi(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle;\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\check{f}(l,r,{\bf x}^{\prime},y,z):=f(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}+\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}+{\bf x}^{\prime},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}+\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}+y,Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}+\nabla_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}+z)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad-\Big[f(\cdot)+\langle Df(\cdot),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle+\partial_{y}f(\cdot)\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}+\partial_{z}f(\cdot)\nabla_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}\Big](l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}},Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}).\end{array}

Again by Propositions
2.9
and
2.11
, recalling (
3.14
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}\|^{p}\big]\leqslant C_{p}\sup_{(l,r)}\sum_{\varphi=b,\sigma}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\check{\varphi}(l,r,{\bf 0})|^{p}+|\partial_{t}\check{\varphi}(l,r,{\bf 0})|^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\sup_{(l,r)}\sum_{\varphi=b,\sigma}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\big\langle D\varphi(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}+\theta\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})-D\varphi(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\big\rangle d\theta\Big|^{p}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}\big[\big\langle D\varphi(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}+\theta\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})-D\varphi(l,r,X^{s,{\bf x}}),\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\big\rangle\big]d\theta\Big|^{p}\bigg]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\|^{2p}\big]\leqslant C_{p}\|\eta\|^{2p},\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sup_{l\in[s,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Delta_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{l}^{T}|\Delta_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\sup_{l\in[s,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\check{g}(l,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})|^{p}+\Big(\int_{l}^{T}|\check{f}(l,r,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}},0,0)|dr\Big)^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\sup_{l\in[s,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\check{g}(l,{\bf 0})|^{p}+\Big(\int_{l}^{T}|\check{f}(l,r,{\bf 0},0,0)|dr\Big)^{p}+\|\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\|^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\sup_{l\in[s,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{l}^{T}\big(\|\nabla_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\|+|\nabla_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}|+|\nabla_{\eta}Z^{l,s,{\bf x}}_{r}|\big)^{2}dr\Big)^{p}+\|\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}\|^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\|\eta\|^{2p}.\end{array}

Then it follows from standard BSDE arguments that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{l\in[s,T]}|\Delta_{\eta}\widetilde{Y}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{l}|^{p}+\Big(\int_{s}^{T}|\Delta_{\eta}\widetilde{Z}^{t,s,{\bf x}}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big)^{p\over 2}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\check{g}(t,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})|^{p}+\Big(\int_{s}^{T}|\check{f}(t,r,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}},\Delta_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r},0)|dr\Big)^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\check{g}(t,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}})|^{p}+\Big(\int_{s}^{T}\big(|\check{f}(t,r,\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}},0,0)|+|\Delta_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}|\big)dr\Big)^{p}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C_{p}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Delta_{\eta}X^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}\|^{p}\big]+C_{p}\sup_{l\in[s,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta_{\eta}Y^{s,{\bf x}}_{l}|^{p}\big]\leqslant C_{p}\|\eta\|^{2p}.\end{array}

In particular, taking

which exactly means (
3.15

Throughout this section, we shall assume the following.

Assumption 4.1

\displaystyle+f\big(t,s,{\bf x},U(s,s,{\bf x}),\langle\partial_{\bf x}\phi(s,{\bf x}),\sigma^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle\big).

We first have the consistency result.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume
U\in C^{0}_{b}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X})

.
Then

is a classical subsolution of PPDE (

3.1

Proof.

We first assume
U
is an
L
-viscosity subsolution for some

Clearly
U_{t}\in\underline{{\cal A}}^{L}U(t,s,{\bf x})
. Then
{\cal L}U(t,s,{\bf x})={\cal L}_{U}U_{t}(t,s,{\bf x})\geqslant 0
,
which implies that

is a classical subsoution.

On the other hand, assume
U
is a classical subsolution.
For any
\phi\in\underline{{\cal A}}^{L_{0}}U(t,s,{\bf x})
with the corresponding
\textsc{h}\in{\cal T}_{s}^{+}
, applying the functional Itô formula we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\phi(r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})=\Big[\partial_{r}\phi+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}\phi,(\sigma^{\cdot},\sigma^{\cdot})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}\phi,b^{\cdot}\rangle\Big](r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dr+\langle\partial_{\bf x}\phi,\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle(r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dW_{r}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=\big[{\cal L}_{U}\phi(t,\cdot)-f\big(t,\cdot,U(r,\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}\phi,\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)\big](r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dr+\langle\partial_{\bf x}\phi,\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle(r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dW_{r}.\end{array}

(4.9)

Similarly we have

dU_{t}(r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})=\big[{\cal L}U(t,\cdot)-f\big(t,\cdot,U(r,\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{t},\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)\big](r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dr+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{t},\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle(r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dW_{r}.

Denote

\Delta Y_{r}:=[\phi-U_{t}](r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}),\quad\Delta Z_{r}:=\langle\partial_{\bf x}[\phi-U_{t}],\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle(r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r}).

Then

d[\Delta Y_{r}]=\big[{\cal L}_{U}\phi-{\cal L}U\big](t,r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dr-\theta_{r}\Delta Z_{r}dr+\Delta Z_{r}dW_{r},

for some
|\theta|\leqslant L_{0}
. This implies that

d(M^{\theta}_{r}\Delta Y_{r})=M^{\theta}_{r}\big[{\cal L}_{U}\phi-{\cal L}U\big](t,r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dr+M^{\theta}_{r}\Delta Z_{r}dW_{r},

where

is defined by (

). Then, for any
\tau\leqslant\textsc{h}
, by (
4.4
) (or (
4.5
)) we have

0\leqslant\mathbb{E}\big[M^{\theta}_{\tau}\Delta Y_{\tau}-M^{\theta}_{s}\Delta Y_{s}\big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{\tau}M^{\theta}_{r}\big[{\cal L}_{U}\phi-{\cal L}U\big](t,r,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},r})dr\Big].

Since

is arbitrary and

is continuous, using the fact that

, we have

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\big[{\cal L}_{U}\phi-{\cal L}U\big](t,s,{\bf x})\leqslant{\cal L}_{U}\phi(t,s,{\bf x}),

implying the viscosity subsolution property.

The key for the viscosity solution theory is the following partial comparison principle.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
Assumption
4.1
hold and
U_{1}
(resp.
U_{2}
) be a viscosity subsolution (resp. supersolution) of PPDE (
3.1
).
Assume
U_{1}(t,T,{\bf x})\leqslant U_{2}(t,T,{\bf x})
for all
(t,{\bf x})\in\Lambda
. If one of
U_{1},U_{2}
is smooth, then
U_{1}\leqslant U_{2}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
U_{2}
is a classical supersolution.
Fix
\delta>0
which will be specified later.
We shall first prove
U_{1}(t,s,{\bf x})\leqslant U_{2}(t,s,{\bf x})
whenever
s\in[T-\delta,T]
.
Assume by contradiction that

c:=\sup_{s\in[T-\delta,T],\,t\in[0,s],\,{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}}[U_{1}-U_{2}](t,s,{\bf x})>0.

(4.10)

Then there exists desired
(t_{0},s_{0},{\bf x}^{0})
such that
[U_{1}-U_{2}](t_{0},s_{0},{\bf x}^{0})\geqslant{c\over 2}>0
. Fix
t_{0}
and denote

V(s,{\bf x}):=[U_{1}-U_{2}](t_{0},s,{\bf x})-{c\over 4(T-s_{0})}[T-s],\quad\psi(s,{\bf x}):=\sup_{\tau\in{\cal T}_{s}}\sup_{\theta\in{\cal U}^{L_{0}}_{s}}\mathbb{E}[M^{\theta}_{\tau}V(\tau,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},\tau})],

(4.11)

where
\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},\tau}
and

are defined by (
3.7
) and (
4.3
), respectively. Similar to Lemma
4.2
,
\psi
is bounded and uniformly continuous in
(s,{\bf x})
.
Moreover, by standard BSDE results (see
[
75
]
, for example),

{\cal Y}_{s}:=\psi(s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s})
is the solution to the following reflected BSDE:

\left.\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{s}=V(T,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},T})+L_{0}\int_{s}^{T}|{\cal Z}_{r}|dr-\int_{s}^{T}{\cal Z}_{r}dW_{r}+K_{T}-K_{s};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{s}\geqslant V(s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s}),\quad\big[{\cal Y}_{s}-V(s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s})\big]dK_{s}=0.\end{array}\right.

(4.12)

Denote

\tau^{*}:=\inf\big\{s\geqslant s_{0}:{\cal Y}_{s}=V(s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s})\big\}.

(4.13)

Then
dK_{s}=0
for
s\in[s_{0},\tau^{*}]
. From (
4.11
), we note

\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{s_{0}}\geqslant V(s_{0},\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s_{0}})=V(s_{0},{\bf x}^{0})=[U_{1}-U_{2}](t_{0},s_{0},{\bf x}^{0})-{c\over 4}\geqslant{c\over 4}>0,

(4.14)

\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{T}=[U_{1}-U_{2}](t_{0},T,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},T})\leqslant 0.

(4.15)

Then it is clear that
\mathbb{P}(\tau^{*}<T)>0
. Indeed, if
\mathbb{P}(\tau^{*}<T)=0
, we have
dK_{s}\equiv 0
and the reflected BSDE (
4.12
) becomes a standard BSDE.
Then the terminal condition (
4.15
) implies
{\cal Y}_{s_{0}}\leqslant 0
, which contradicts (
4.14
).
Therefore, there exists
\omega^{*}\in\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\tau^{*}(\omega^{*})<T\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi(s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})=V(s^{*},{\bf x}^{*}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad s^{*}:=\tau^{*}(\omega^{*}),\quad{\bf x}^{*}:=\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s^{*}}(\omega^{*}).

Now define

\displaystyle\phi(s,{\bf x}):=U_{2}(t_{0},s,{\bf x})+{c\over 4(T-s_{0})}(T-s)+\psi(s^{*},{\bf x}^{*}).

Then
\phi\in C^{1,2}_{+}([s_{0},T]\times\mathbb{X})
,
\phi(s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})=U_{1}(t_{0},s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})
, and, for any
\theta\in{\cal U}^{L_{0}}_{s^{*}}
and any
\tau\in{\cal T}_{s^{*}}
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[M^{\theta}_{\tau}\big[\phi-U_{1}(t_{0},\cdot)\big](s,\widehat{X}^{s^{*},{\bf x}^{*},s})\Big]=\psi(s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})-\mathbb{E}\big[M^{\theta}_{\tau}V(s,\widehat{X}^{s^{*},{\bf x}^{*},s})\big]\geqslant 0.

(4.16)

That is,
\phi\in\underline{{\cal A}}^{L_{0}}U_{1}(t_{0},s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})
, and thus by the viscosity subsolution property of
U_{1}
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\ {\cal L}_{U_{1}}\phi(t_{0},s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})

(4.17)

\displaystyle=\ \Big[\partial_{s}\phi+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}\phi,(\sigma^{\cdot},\sigma^{\cdot})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}\phi,b^{\cdot}\rangle+f\big(t_{0},\cdot,U_{1}(s^{*},\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}\phi,\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)\Big](s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})

\displaystyle=\ -{c\over 4(T-s_{0})}+\Big[\partial_{s}U_{2}(t_{0},\cdot)+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U_{2}(t_{0},\cdot),(\sigma^{\cdot},\sigma^{\cdot})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{2}(t_{0},\cdot),b^{\cdot}\rangle

\displaystyle U_{1}(s^{*},s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})\leqslant U_{2}(s^{*},s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})+c.

Let

denote the Lipschitz constant of

with respect to

.
Then, by Assumption

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-{c\over 4\delta}+\Big[\partial_{s}U_{2}(t_{0},\cdot)+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U_{2}(t_{0},\cdot),(\sigma^{\cdot},\sigma^{\cdot})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{2}(t_{0},\cdot),b^{\cdot}\rangle

\displaystyle+f\big(t_{0},\cdot,U_{2}(s^{*},\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{2}(t_{0},\cdot),\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)\Big](s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})+Lc

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal L}U_{2}(t_{0},s^{*},{\bf x}^{*})-{c\over 4\delta}+Lc\leqslant Lc-{c\over 4\delta},

thanks to the supersolution property of

. Set

, we obtain the desired contradiction, and hence

.

Now consider the PPDE (

) on
[0,T-\delta]

over the whole space.

Theorem 4.7

satisfy the requirements in

on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we shall assume
U_{1}(t,T,{\bf x})\leqslant g(t,{\bf x})
and prove only
U_{1}\leqslant U
, where
U
is defined by (
3.6
). We shall approximate
(f,g)
by

(f_{n},g_{n})
which satisfy Assumption

.
However, since

is a path, the standard mollification does not work and the approximations may not be uniform in terms of

. In particular, we may not have

for all
(t,{\bf x})\in\Lambda
.
Therefore, instead of directly applying the partial comparison principle, we will follow its arguments.
As in Theorem

, it suffices to prove
U_{1}(t,s,{\bf x})\leqslant U(t,s,{\bf x})
for

, where

.
Assume by contradiction that, for some
s_{0}\in[T-\delta,T]
,
t_{0}\leqslant s_{0}
, and
{\bf x}^{0}\in\mathbb{X}
,

c:=\sup_{s\in[T-\delta,T],t\in[0,s],{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}}[U_{1}-U](t,s,{\bf x})>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad[U_{1}-U](t_{0},s_{0},{\bf x}^{0})\geqslant{c\over 2}.

(4.18)

We now mollify
(f,g)
. By first discretizing
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}
,
one can easily construct

such that, for each

satisfy Assumption
3.3

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}_{+}^{2}}\sup_{y,z}\big|[f_{n}-f](t,s,{\bf x},y,z)\big|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big|[g_{n}-g](t,T,{\bf x})\big|\leqslant C\Big[{1\over n}+OSC_{1\over n}({\bf x})\Big],

(4.19)

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\quad OSC_{\delta}({\bf x}):=\sup_{|t-s|\leqslant\delta}|{\bf x}_{t}-{\bf x}_{s}|.

By Theorem
3.4
, the PPDE (
3.1
) with coefficients
(b,\sigma,f_{n},g_{n})
has a classical solution
U_{n}
.
As in (

), fix
t_{0}
and denote

V_{n}(s,{\bf x}):=[U_{1}-U_{n}](t_{0},s,{\bf x})-{c\over 4(T-s_{0})}[T-s],\quad\psi_{n}(s,{\bf x}):=\sup_{\tau\in{\cal T}_{s}}\sup_{\theta\in{\cal U}^{L_{0}}_{s}}\mathbb{E}[M^{\theta}_{\tau}V_{n}(\tau,\widehat{X}^{s,{\bf x},\tau})].

(4.20)

Denote further
{\cal Y}^{n}_{s}:=\psi_{n}(s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s})
. Then (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) become:

\left.\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle{\cal Y}^{n}_{s}=V_{n}(T,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},T})+L_{0}\int_{s}^{T}|{\cal Z}^{n}_{r}|dr-\int_{s}^{T}{\cal Z}^{n}_{r}dW_{r}+K^{n}_{T}-K^{n}_{s};\\
\displaystyle{\cal Y}^{n}_{s}\geqslant V_{n}(s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s}),\quad\big[{\cal Y}^{n}_{s}-V_{n}(s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s})\big]dK^{n}_{s}=0;\\
\displaystyle\tau^{*}_{n}:=\inf\big\{s\geqslant s_{0}:{\cal Y}^{n}_{s}=V_{n}(s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s})\big\}.\end{array}\right.

(4.21)

Note that

\displaystyle{\cal Y}^{n}_{s_{0}}\geqslant V_{n}(s_{0},\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s_{0}})=V_{n}(s_{0},{\bf x}^{0})=[U_{1}-U_{n}](s_{0},{\bf x}^{0})-{c\over 4}\geqslant{c\over 4}-[U-U_{n}](s_{0},{\bf x}^{0}),

\displaystyle{\cal Y}^{n}_{T}=[U_{1}-U_{n}](t_{0},T,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},T})\leqslant[U-U_{n}](t_{0},T,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},T}).

By (
4.19
) and noting that
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}
, one can easily show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[|OSC_{1\over n}(\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},\tau})|^{2}+|U-U_{n}|^{2}(t_{0},\tau,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},\tau})\Big]=0,\quad\mbox{for any}~\tau\in{\cal T}_{s_{0}}.

(4.22)

In particular, for any
\varepsilon>0
small, this implies that, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle{\cal Y}^{n}_{s_{0}}\geqslant{c\over 8}>0\quad\mbox{ and}\quad\mathbb{E}\big[|{\cal Y}^{n}_{T}|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{{\cal Y}^{n}_{T}\geqslant 0\}}\big]\leqslant\varepsilon.

Denote
\theta^{n}_{s}:=L_{0}{\rm sign}({\cal Z}^{n}_{s})
.
Note that
dK^{n}_{s}=0
for
s\in[s_{0},\tau^{*}_{n}]
, then
{\cal Y}^{n}_{s_{0}}=\mathbb{E}\big[M^{\theta^{n}}_{\tau^{*}_{n}}{\cal Y}^{n}_{\tau^{*}_{n}}\big]
. Thus

\displaystyle{c\over 8}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle{\cal Y}^{n}_{s_{0}}=\mathbb{E}\Big[M^{\theta^{n}}_{\tau^{*}_{n}}{\cal Y}^{n}_{\tau^{*}_{n}}{\bf 1}_{\{\tau^{*}_{n}<T\}}+M^{\theta^{n}}_{T}{\cal Y}^{n}_{T}{\bf 1}_{\{\tau^{*}_{n}=T\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[M^{\theta^{n}}_{\tau^{*}_{n}}{\cal Y}^{n}_{\tau^{*}_{n}}{\bf 1}_{\{\tau^{*}_{n}<T\}}+M^{\theta^{n}}_{T}{\cal Y}^{n}_{T}{\bf 1}_{\{\tau^{*}_{n}=T\}}{\bf 1}_{\{{\cal Y}^{n}_{T}\geqslant 0\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Big[\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(\tau^{*}_{n}<T)}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Big].

Then, for
\varepsilon>0
small, we have

\mathbb{P}(\tau^{*}_{n}<T)\geqslant{c^{2}\over C},\quad\mbox{for all $n$ large enough}.

(4.23)

Moreover, by (
4.19
) and (
4.22
), we have
\mathbb{E}[|\Delta_{n}|^{2}]\leqslant\varepsilon^{3}
for
n
large enough, where

\Delta_{n}:=\sup_{s\in[s_{0},T],t\in[0,s]}\sup_{y,z}|f_{n}-f|(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s},y,z)+|U-U_{n}|(t_{0},\tau^{*}_{n},\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},\tau^{*}_{n}}),

(4.24)

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Delta_{n}>\varepsilon)\leqslant{1\over\varepsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta_{n}|^{2}]\leqslant\varepsilon.

Together with (
4.23
), for
\varepsilon<{c^{2}\over C}
, we have
\mathbb{P}(\{\tau^{*}_{n}<T\}\cap\{\Delta_{n}\leqslant\varepsilon\})>0
, for all
n
large enough.
Therefore, there exists
\omega^{*}_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tau^{*}(\omega^{*}_{n})<T,~\Delta_{n}(\omega^{*}_{n})\leqslant\varepsilon,~\mbox{and}~\psi_{n}(s_{n}^{*},{\bf x}^{*}_{n})=V_{n}(s^{*}_{n},{\bf x}^{*}_{n}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mbox{where}\quad s^{*}_{n}:=\tau^{*}_{n}(\omega^{*}_{n}),~{\bf x}^{*}_{n}:=\widehat{X}^{s_{0},{\bf x}^{0},s^{*}_{n}}(\omega^{*}_{n}).

Now define

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(s,{\bf x}):=U_{n}(t_{0},s,{\bf x})+{c\over 4(T-s_{0})}(T-s)+\psi_{n}(s^{*}_{n},{\bf x}^{*}_{n}).

Similar to (
4.16
)–(
4.17
), we have
\phi_{n}\in\underline{{\cal A}}^{L_{0}}U_{1}(t_{0},s_{n}^{*},{\bf x}_{n}^{*})
and then,
recalling that
T-s_{0}\leqslant\delta={1\over 8L}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-2Lc+\Big[\partial_{s}U_{n}(t_{0},\cdot)+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U_{n}(t_{0},\cdot),(\sigma^{\cdot},\sigma^{\cdot})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{n}(t_{0},\cdot),b^{\cdot}\rangle

\displaystyle+f\big(t_{0},\cdot,U_{1}(s^{*}_{n},\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{n}(t_{0},\cdot),\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)\Big](s^{*}_{n},{\bf x}^{*}_{n}).

Since
U_{n}
is a classical solution of the corresponding PPDE, this implies

\displaystyle 2Lc\leqslant\Big[f\big(t_{0},\cdot,U_{1}(s^{*}_{n},\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{n}(t_{0},\cdot),\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)-f_{n}\big(t_{0},\cdot,U_{n}(s^{*}_{n},\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{n}(t_{0},\cdot),\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)\Big](s^{*}_{n},{\bf x}^{*}_{n}).

Then, by (
4.24
) and recalling
\Delta_{n}(\omega^{*}_{n})\leqslant\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle 2Lc\leqslant\Big[f\big(t_{0},\cdot,U_{1}(s^{*}_{n},\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{n}(t_{0},\cdot),\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)-f\big(t_{0},\cdot,U(s^{*}_{n},\cdot),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U_{n}(t_{0},\cdot),\sigma^{\cdot}\rangle\big)\Big](s^{*}_{n},{\bf x}^{*}_{n})+C\varepsilon.

Note further that (
4.18
) leads to
U_{1}(s^{*}_{n},s^{*}_{n},{\bf x}^{*}_{n})\leqslant U(s^{*}_{n},s^{*}_{n},{\bf x}^{*}_{n})+c
,
and since
f
is Lipschitz continuous and nondecreasing in
y
, we have
2Lc\leqslant Lc+C\varepsilon
and thus
Lc\leqslant C\varepsilon
.
This is a desired contradiction since
\varepsilon
can be arbitrarily small, thus
U_{1}(t,s,{\bf x})\leqslant U(t,s,{\bf x})
whenever
s\in[T-\delta,T]
.

Now similar to the end of Theorem
4.6
, we can show that
U_{1}\leqslant U
over the whole space.

Remark 4.8
.

In Theorem
4.7
, the assumptions imposed on
b,\sigma
are somewhat strong.

(i) In
[
23
]
and the subsequent works
[
24
,
25
]
, general semi-martingale measures are used to define the corresponding set of test functions
\underline{{\cal A}}^{L}U
. In this paper
X
is not a semi-martingale and inside
U
we need to use
\widehat{X}
, so in (
4.4
) we are using the exact process
\widehat{X}
. Consequently, we are not allowed to mollify
(b,\sigma)
, which will change the process
\widehat{X}
. Therefore, we assume
b,\sigma
are smooth so that, together with mollified
(f_{n},g_{n})
, the corresponding PPDE has a classical solution
U_{n}
. It will be desirable to allow the
\widehat{X}
in (
4.4
) to have more general distributions, in the spirit of
[
24
,
25
]
. Then it may become possible to mollify
(b,\sigma)
, and even to allow
b,\sigma
to depend on some controls. We shall leave this to future research.

(ii) If
X\equiv B^{H}
is a fractional Brownian motion (with the Hurst parameter
H\neq{1\over 2}
),
namely
b\equiv 0
and
\sigma(t,r,{\bf x})\equiv\sigma(t,r)
, following our arguments we may prove our results without Assumption
2.1
(iii) and
Assumption
3.3
(iii). Thus, in the setting that the randomness of
f,g
comes from some fractional Brownian motions,
the viscosity theory of the corresponding PPDEs still holds true.

5
Coupled Forward Backward SVIEs and Type-II BSVIEs

In this section, we investigate briefly two more general BSVIEs.

5.1
Coupled FBSVIEs

We now consider the following coupled FBSVIEs:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle X_{t}={\bf x}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r})dW_{r},\quad t\in\mathbb{T},\\
\displaystyle Y_{t}=g(t,X_{\cdot})+\int_{t}^{T}f(t,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r},Z^{t}_{r})dr-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\quad t\in\mathbb{T},\end{array}\right.

(5.1)

and the associated PPDE:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\partial_{s}U(t,s,{\bf x})+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,s,{\bf x}),(\widehat{\sigma}^{s,{\bf x}},\widehat{\sigma}^{s,{\bf x}})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}),\widehat{b}^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad+f\big(t,s,{\bf x},U(s,s,{\bf x}),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}),\widehat{\sigma}^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle\big)=0,\quad(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}_{+}^{2},\,{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle U(t,T,{\bf x})=g(t,T,{\bf x}),\quad t\in\mathbb{T},\,{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X},\end{array}\right.

(5.2)

where, for
\varphi=b,\sigma
,
\widehat{\varphi}^{s,{\bf x}}_{r}:=\varphi(r,s,{\bf x}_{\cdot},U(s,s,{\bf x}))
,
r\in[s,T]
.

When
T
is small, Hamaguchi
[
33
]
proved the well-posedness of FBSVIE (
5.1
) with the forward being a SDE. Following Ma–Protter–Yong
[
45
]
, in this subsection we prove the well-posedness of (
5.1
) for arbitrary
T
, provided PPDE (
5.2
) has a classical solution. The existence of such classical solution, as well as the well-posedness of (
5.1
) in general, remains a challenging problem and we shall leave it for future research. For simplicity, in the following result we do not specify the precise technical conditions.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
b,\sigma,f,g
are sufficiently smooth with all the related derivatives being bounded. If PPDE (
5.2
) has a classical solution
U
with bounded
\partial_{\bf x}U
, then the coupled FBSVIE (
5.1
) admits a unique strong solution
(X,Y,Z)
and the following representation holds:

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle Y_{t}=U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{t}),\quad Z^{t}_{s}=\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\,\widehat{\sigma}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}}\rangle,\quad(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}_{+}^{2},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mbox{where}\quad\widehat{X}^{t}:=X\oplus_{t}\widetilde{X}_{t},\quad\widetilde{X}^{s}_{t}:={\bf x}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r})dW_{r}.\end{array}\right.

(5.3)

Proof.

We proceed in two steps. Fix an arbitrary
T
.

Step 1.
We first show the existence. Let
\delta>0
be a small number which will be specified later. Introduce a mapping
\Phi
on
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,\delta];\mathbb{R}^{m})
by
\Phi({\bf y}):=Y^{\bf y}
, where, for any
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,\delta];\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle X^{\bf y}_{t}={\bf x}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,r,X^{\bf y}_{\cdot},{\bf y}_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,r,X^{\bf y}_{\cdot},{\bf y}_{r})dW_{r},\quad t\in[0,\delta];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{{\bf y},s}_{t}={\bf x}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,r,X^{\bf y}_{\cdot},{\bf y}_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,r,X^{\bf y}_{\cdot},{\bf y}_{r})dW_{r},\quad s\in[t,T];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}^{{\bf y},t}:=X^{\bf y}\oplus_{t}\widetilde{X}^{\bf y}_{t},\quad Y^{\bf y}_{t}:=U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{{\bf y},t}),\quad t\in[0,\delta].\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

We emphasize that here we do not need to assume
T\leqslant\delta
. We shall show that
\Phi
is a contraction mapping when
\delta
is small enough.

Indeed, let
{\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,\delta];\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Denote
\Delta{\bf y}:={\bf y}-{\bf y}^{\prime}
,
\Delta X:=X^{\bf y}-X^{{\bf y}^{\prime}}
, and similarly for the other notations. First applying Proposition
2.9
one can easily have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\delta}|\Delta X_{t}|^{2}\Big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\delta}|\Delta{\bf y}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big].

(5.5)

Then by standard SDE estimates we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta\widetilde{X}^{s}_{t}|^{2}\big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\delta}|\Delta{\bf y}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big],\quad t\in[0,\delta],s\in[t,T].

Moreover, since
\partial_{t}b,\partial_{t}\sigma
satisfy the desired regularity, following the arguments in
[
58
]
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|\Delta\widetilde{X}^{s}_{t}|^{2}\Big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\delta}|\Delta{\bf y}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big],\quad t\in[0,\delta].

This, together with (
5.5
), implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|\Delta\widehat{X}^{t}_{s}|^{2}\Big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\delta}|\Delta{\bf y}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big],\quad t\in[0,\delta].

Therefore, since
\partial_{\bf x}U
is bounded,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y_{t}|^{2}]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta\widehat{X}^{t}\|^{2}]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\delta}|\Delta{\bf y}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big],\quad t\in[0,\delta],

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\delta}|\Delta Y_{t}|^{2}dt\Big]\leqslant C\delta\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\delta}|\Delta{\bf y}_{t}|^{2}dt\Big].

Choose
\delta:={1\over 2C}
, we see that
\Phi
is a contraction mapping. Consequently,
\Phi
has a unique fixed point
{\bf y}^{*}
. Denote
X^{*}_{t}:=X^{{\bf y}^{*}}_{t},\widetilde{X}^{*,s}_{t}:=\widetilde{X}^{{\bf y}^{*},s}_{t}
,
t\in[0,\delta]
,
s\in[t,T]
.

Next, we introduce another mapping
\Phi_{2}
on
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([\delta,2\delta];\mathbb{R}^{m})
by
\Phi_{2}({\bf y}):=Y^{\bf y}
, where, by abusing the notations, for any
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([\delta,2\delta];\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle X^{\bf y}_{t}=\widetilde{X}^{*,t}_{\delta}+\int_{\delta}^{t}b(t,r,X^{*}\oplus_{\delta}X^{\bf y}_{\cdot},{\bf y}_{r})dr+\int_{\delta}^{t}\sigma(t,r,X^{*}\oplus_{t}X^{\bf y}_{\cdot},{\bf y}_{r})dW_{r},\quad t\in[\delta,2\delta];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{{\bf y},s}_{t}=\widetilde{X}^{*,s}_{\delta}+\int_{\delta}^{t}b(s,r,X^{*}\oplus_{\delta}X^{\bf y}_{\cdot},{\bf y}_{r})dr+\int_{\delta}^{t}\sigma(s,r,X^{*}\oplus_{\delta}X^{\bf y}_{\cdot},{\bf y}_{r})dW_{r},\quad s\in[t,T];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}^{{\bf y},t}:=X^{*}\oplus_{\delta}X^{\bf y}\oplus_{t}\widetilde{X}^{\bf y}_{t},\quad Y^{\bf y}_{t}:=U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{{\bf y},t}),\quad t\in[\delta,2\delta].\end{array}\right.

(5.6)

Following the same arguments we can show that
\Phi_{2}
is also a contraction mapping, and thus we may extend the unique fixed point
{\bf y}^{*}
to
[0,2\delta]
. Repeat the arguments we will obtain a fixed point
{\bf y}^{*}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle X^{*}_{t}={\bf x}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,r,X^{*}_{\cdot},{\bf y}^{*}_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,r,X^{*}_{\cdot},{\bf y}^{*}_{r})dW_{r},\quad t\in[0,T];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{*,s}_{t}={\bf x}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,r,X^{*}_{\cdot},{\bf y}^{*}_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,r,X^{*}_{\cdot},{\bf y}^{*}_{r})dW_{r},\quad s\in[t,T];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{X}^{*,t}:=X^{*}\oplus_{t}\widetilde{X}^{*}_{t},\quad{\bf y}^{*}_{t}:=U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{*,t}),\quad t\in[0,T].\end{array}\right.

(5.7)

Now applying the functional Itô formula (
2.31
) on
U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{*,t})
and utilizing the PPDE (
5.2
), one can easily see that
(X^{*},{\bf y}^{*})
satisfy FBSVIE (
5.1
) and the representation (
5.3
) holds true.

Step 2.
We next show the uniqueness. Let
(X,Y,Z)
be an arbitrary solution, and
\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y},\widehat{X}
be defined in an obvious way:
\widehat{X}^{t}:=X\oplus_{t}\widetilde{X}_{t}
, and

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{s}_{t}={\bf x}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r})dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r})dW_{r},\\
\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}=g(t,X_{\cdot})+\int_{s}^{T}f(t,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r},Z^{t}_{r})dr-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\end{array}\right.\quad(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+}.

Now denote
\varphi^{s,({\bf x},y)}_{r}:=\varphi(r,s,{\bf x},y)
,
r\in[s,T]
, for
\varphi=b,\sigma
, and

\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{t}:=U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{t}),\quad\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}:=U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\quad{\cal Z}^{t}_{s}:=\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s}),\sigma^{s,(X,Y_{s})}\rangle;

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal Y}:={\cal Y}-Y,\quad\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}:=\widetilde{\cal Y}-\widetilde{Y},\quad\Delta{\cal Z}:={\cal Z}-Z.

Applying the functional Itô formula (
2.31
) and then utilizing the PPDE (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle d\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal Z}^{t}_{s}dW_{s}+\Big[\partial_{s}U+{1\over 2}\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U,(\sigma^{s,X,Y_{s}},\sigma^{s,X,Y_{s}})\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{{\bf x}}U,b^{s,X,Y_{s}}\big\rangle\Big](t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal Z}^{t}_{s}dW_{s}+\Big[{1\over 2}\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U,\,(\sigma^{s,X,Y_{s}},\sigma^{s,X,Y_{s}})\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{{\bf x}}U,b^{s,X,Y_{s}}\big\rangle\Big](t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})ds

\displaystyle-\Big[{1\over 2}\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U,(\widehat{\sigma}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}},\widehat{\sigma}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}})\big\rangle+\big\langle\partial_{\bf x}U,\widehat{b}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}}\big\rangle+f\big(\cdot,{\cal Y}_{s},\langle\partial_{\bf x}U,\widehat{\sigma}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}}\rangle\big)\Big](t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})ds.

Then

\displaystyle d\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}=\Delta{\cal Z}^{t}_{s}dW_{s}+{1\over 2}\Big[\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U,(\sigma^{s,X,Y_{s}},\sigma^{s,X,Y_{s}})\big\rangle-\big\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U,(\widehat{\sigma}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}},\widehat{\sigma}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}})\big\rangle\Big](t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})ds

\displaystyle\quad+\big\langle\partial_{{\bf x}}U,b^{s,X,Y_{s}}-\widehat{b}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}}\big\rangle(t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})ds+\Big[f\big(\cdot,Y_{s},Z^{t}_{s}\big)-f\big(\cdot,{\cal Y}_{s},\langle\partial_{\bf x}U,\widehat{\sigma}^{s,\widehat{X}^{s}}\rangle\big)\Big](t,s,\widehat{X}^{s})ds.

Noting that
\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{T}=0
, by standard BSDE arguments, this implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}|^{2}+\int_{s}^{T}|\Delta{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{T}\Big(|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{r}|^{2}+\|\sigma^{r,X,Y_{r}}-\widehat{\sigma}^{r,\widehat{X}^{r}}\|^{2}+\|b^{r,X,Y_{r}}-\widehat{b}^{r,\widehat{X}^{r}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\Delta{\cal Y}_{r}|^{2}+|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{r}||Z^{t}_{r}-\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,r,\widehat{X}^{r}),\widehat{\sigma}^{r,\widehat{X}^{r}}\rangle|\Big)dr\Big].

Note that, for
\varphi=b,\sigma
,

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{r,X,Y_{r}}-\widehat{\varphi}^{r,\widehat{X}^{r}}\|=\sup_{l\in[r,T]}|\varphi(l,r,X,Y_{r})-\varphi(l,r,X,U(r,r,\widehat{X}^{r}))|\leqslant C|\Delta{\cal Y}_{r}|;

\displaystyle|Z^{t}_{r}-\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,r,\widehat{X}^{r}),\widehat{\sigma}^{r,\widehat{X}^{r}}\rangle|\leqslant|\Delta{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}|+\big|\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,r,\widehat{X}^{r}),\sigma^{r,X,Y_{r}}-\widehat{\sigma}^{r,\widehat{X}^{r}}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant|\Delta{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}|+C\|\sigma^{r,X,Y_{r}}-\widehat{\sigma}^{r,\widehat{X}^{r}}\|\leqslant|\Delta{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}|+C|\Delta{\cal Y}_{r}|.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}|^{2}+\int_{s}^{T}|\Delta{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{T}\big[C|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{r}|^{2}+C|\Delta{\cal Y}_{r}|^{2}+C|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{r}||{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}|\big]dr\Big],

which implies:

\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}|^{2}\big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}|^{2}+{1\over 2}\int_{s}^{T}|\Delta{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{T}\big[|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{r}|^{2}+|\Delta{\cal Y}_{r}|^{2}\big]dr\Big].

(5.9)

Now applying the Grönwall inequality we obtain

\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}|^{2}\big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{T}|\Delta{\cal Y}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big],\quad s\in[t,T].

(5.10)

Set
s=t
at above, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta{\cal Y}_{t}|^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[|\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{t}|^{2}\big]\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|\Delta{\cal Y}_{r}|^{2}dr\Big],\quad t\in[0,T].

Apply the Grönwall inequality again, we have
\Delta{\cal Y}=0
. Plug this into (
5.10
) and then to (
5.9
), we see that
\Delta\widetilde{\cal Y}=\Delta{\cal Z}=0
.
In particular, this implies that
{\bf y}:=Y
is a fixed point in Step 1. By the uniqueness of the fixed point, we see that
Y
is unique, which implies immediately that
X
and
Z
are also unique.

5.2
Type-II BSVIEs

Let
X
be the solution to FSVIE (
2.3
). In this subsection we consider the following type-II BSVIE:

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle Y_{t}=g(t,X_{\cdot})+\int_{t}^{T}f(t,r,X_{\cdot},Y_{r},Z^{t}_{r},Z^{r}_{t})dr-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\\
\displaystyle Y_{t}=\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]+\int_{0}^{t}Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r},\end{array}\right.\quad t\in\mathbb{T}.

(5.11)

We note that here
f
depends on both
Z^{t}_{r}
and
Z^{r}_{t}
, where
Z^{r}_{t}
for
t\leqslant r
is determined by the martingale representation of
Y_{r}
, as in the second line of (
5.11
). The
\mathbb{F}
-adapted solution to (
5.11
) is called an
M-solution
, with
M
referring to martingale. By Yong
[
71
]
, under suitable conditions BSVIE (
5.11
) admits a unique
M
-solution. Inspired by Wang–Yong
[
66
]
, we introduce the following PPDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{s}V(t,s,{\bf x})+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}V(t,s,{\bf x}),(\sigma^{s,{\bf x}},\sigma^{s,{\bf x}})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}V(t,s,{\bf x}),b^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle=0,\quad(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{-},\\
&\partial_{s}U(t,s,{\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U(t,s,{\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x}),(\sigma^{s,{\bf x}},\sigma^{s,{\bf x}})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x}),b^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle\\
&\quad+\negthinspace f\big(t,s,{\bf x},U(s,s,{\bf x},{\bf x}),\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x}),\sigma^{s,{\bf x}}\rangle,\langle\partial_{\bf x}V(s,t,{\bf x}^{\prime}),\sigma^{t,{\bf x}^{\prime}}\rangle\big)=0,\quad(t,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+},\\
&V(t,t,{\bf x})=U(t,t,{\bf x},{\bf x}),\quad U(t,T,{\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x})=g(t,{\bf x}_{\cdot}),\quad t\in\mathbb{T},~{\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.12)

Note that here
V:\mathbb{T}_{-}^{2}\times\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
U:\mathbb{T}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume
b,\sigma,f,g
are sufficiently smooth with all the related derivatives being bounded, and let
X,\tilde{X},\widehat{X}
be determined by FSVIE (
2.3
) in the obvious sense. Assume PPDE (
5.12
) has a classical solution
(V,U)
with bounded derivatives. Then the unique
M
-solution of BSVIE (
5.11
) satisfies: for any
0\leqslant r\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T
,

Y_{t}=U(t,t,\widehat{X}^{t},\widehat{X}^{t}),\quad Z^{t}_{s}=\langle\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{t},\widehat{X}^{s}),\sigma^{s,X}\rangle,\quad Z^{t}_{r}=\langle\partial_{\bf x}V(t,r,\widehat{X}^{r}),\sigma^{r,X}\rangle.

(5.13)

Proof.

Define
Y,Z
as in (
5.13
) and
\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}:=U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{t},\widehat{X}^{s})
,
\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{r}=V(t,r,\widehat{X}^{r})
for
0\leqslant r\leqslant t\leqslant s\leqslant T
. We shall verify that they satisfy BSVIE (
5.11
).

First, fix
t
, and apply functional Itô formula (
2.31
) on
V(t,r,\widehat{X}^{r});r\in[0,t]
, we have

\displaystyle d\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{r}=\Big[\partial_{r}V+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}V,(\sigma^{r,X},\sigma^{r,X})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}V,b^{r,X}\rangle\Big](t,r,\widehat{X}^{r})ds+Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r}=Z^{t}_{r}dW_{r},

where the second equality is due to (
5.12
). Since
\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{t}=Y_{t}
, this verifies the second line of (
5.11
).

Next, fix
(t,\widehat{X}^{t})
, and apply functional Itô formula (
2.31
) on
U(t,s,\widehat{X}^{t},\widehat{X}^{s});s\in[t,T]
, we have

\displaystyle d\widetilde{Y}^{t}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\partial_{s}U+{1\over 2}\langle\partial^{2}_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}U,(\sigma^{s,X},\sigma^{s,X})\rangle+\langle\partial_{\bf x}U,b^{s,X}\rangle\Big](t,s,\widehat{X}^{t},\widehat{X}^{s})ds+Z^{t}_{s}dW_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(t,s,Y_{s},Z^{t}_{s},Z^{s}_{t})ds+Z^{t}_{s}dW_{s},

where the second equality is also due to (
5.12
). This verifies the first line of (
5.11
) immediately.

6
Probabilistic representation of
\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x})

In this section, we shall investigate linear FBSVIEs more closely and then use it to obtain an explicit representation formula for
\partial_{\bf x}U(t,s,{\bf x})
.

6.1
A duality result for linear FSVIE

For the ease of presentation, in the rest of the paper we restrict to one dimensional processes only.
However, all our results hold true in the multiple dimensional situation, and we provide a multiple dimensional setting in Remark
6.4
below.

Note that the dual space of
C([0,T])
consists of signed measures on
[0,T]
(see
[
74
]
, p.119, for example).
That is, for a continuous linear mapping
\Phi:C([0,T])\to\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a unique function
\check{F}
on
[0,T]
with finite variation such that
\langle\Phi,\eta\rangle=\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}\check{F}(dt)
.
Then we may view the
Db,D\sigma
in (
3.16
) as signed measures.
For this purpose, in this subsection we consider the following FSVIE:

{\cal X}_{t}=\eta_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{b}(t,s,dr)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{\sigma}(t,s,dr)dW_{s}.

(6.1)

Here
\check{b},\check{\sigma}:(t,s,r,\omega)\in\mathbb{T}_{-}^{3}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are progressively measurable,
the adaptedness of
\omega
is with respect to the second time variable
s
,
and the dependence on the third time variable
r
is right continuous with finite variation. We are interested in the term

\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{g}(dr)\Big],

(6.2)

where
\check{g}:\mathbb{T}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is
{\cal F}_{T}
measurable in
\omega
and right continuous and finite variated in
r
.
Our goal is to find a finite variated function
r\in\mathbb{T}\mapsto\widetilde{\cal Y}(r,0)
such that the following duality principle holds:

\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal X}_{r}\check{g}(dr)\Big]=\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{r}\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,0).

(6.3)

This will give us an explicit representation for the linear mapping
\check{F}
:

\Phi=\check{F}(\cdot)=\widetilde{\cal Y}(\cdot,0).

(6.4)

We shall approach the problem dynamically. Define, for
0\leqslant t\leqslant r\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\cal X}^{r}_{t}:=\eta_{r}+\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{s\wedge t}{\cal X}_{l}~\check{b}(r,s,dl)ds+\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{s\wedge t}{\cal X}_{l}~\check{\sigma}(r,s,dl)dW_{s}.

(6.5)

Note that
\eta_{r}=\tilde{X}^{r}_{0}
. In light of (
6.3
), we want to find
\widetilde{\cal Y}
such that

\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}{\cal X}_{r}\check{g}(dr)\Big]=\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{\cal X}^{r}_{t}\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,t),\quad t\in[0,T].

(6.6)

For this purpose, we introduce the following type-II BSVIE:

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{t}=\check{g}(t)-\int_{t\leqslant l\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\check{b}(r,s,dl)\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\check{\sigma}(r,s,dl){\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds-\int_{t}^{T}{\cal Z}(t,s)dW_{s};\\
\displaystyle\widetilde{\cal Y}(t,s)={\cal Y}_{t}-\int_{s}^{t}{\cal Z}(t,r)dW_{r},\quad 0\leqslant s\leqslant t.\end{array}\right.

(6.7)

We emphasize that, for fixed
t
,

\bullet
The mapping
s\in[0,t]\to\widetilde{\cal Y}(t,s)
is an
\mathbb{F}
-martingale;

\bullet
The mappings
s\in[t,T]\to\big(\widetilde{\cal Y}(s,t),{\cal Z}(s,t)\big)
are
{\cal F}_{t}
-measurable and finite variated.

The second requirement above, of course, will add difficulty for the existence of solutions, which we shall leave for future research.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y},\widetilde{\cal Y},{\cal Z}
be the solution to (
6.1
) and (
6.7
), respectively.
Then (
6.6
) holds, and in particular (
6.3
) holds.

Proof.

We shall only prove (
6.3
), the arguments for (
6.6
) are similar.

Since
\eta
is continuous, by taking time partitions we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}\widetilde{\cal Y}(dt,0)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\big[\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i+1},0)-\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i},0)\big]\eta_{t_{i}}.

Now fix a time partition with large
N
, by the second line of (
6.7
), we see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}\widetilde{\cal Y}(dt,0)\approx\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\big[\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i+1},0)-\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i},0)\big]\eta_{t_{i}}=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[{\cal Y}_{t_{i+1}}-{\cal Y}_{t_{i}}\big]\eta_{t_{i}}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[{\cal Y}_{t_{i+1}}-{\cal Y}_{t_{i}}\big]\big[{\cal X}_{t_{i}}-\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{b}(t_{i},s,dr)ds-\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{\sigma}(t_{i},s,dr)dW_{s}\big]\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[{\cal Y}_{t_{i+1}}-{\cal Y}_{t_{i}}\big]{\cal X}_{t_{i}}-\big[{\cal Y}(t_{i+1},t_{i})-{\cal Y}(t_{i},t_{i})\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\big[\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{b}(t_{i},s,dr)ds+\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{\sigma}(t_{i},s,dr)dW_{s}\big]\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[{\cal Y}_{t_{i+1}}-{\cal Y}_{t_{i}}\big]{\cal X}_{t_{i}}-\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big[\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i+1},s)-\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i},s)\big]\big[\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{b}(t_{i},s,dr)\big]ds

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad-\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big[{\cal Z}(t_{i+1},s)-{\cal Z}(t_{i},s)\big]\big[\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{\sigma}(t_{i},s,dr)\big]ds\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\approx\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[{\cal Y}_{t_{k+1}}-{\cal Y}_{t_{k}}\big]{\cal X}_{t_{k}}\Big]-{T\over n}\sum_{0\leqslant k<j<i\leqslant N-1}\Big[\big[\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i+1},t_{j})-\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i},t_{j})\big]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\big[\check{b}(t_{i},t_{j},t_{k+1})-\check{b}(t_{i},t_{j},t_{k})\big]+\big[{\cal Z}(t_{i+1},t_{j})-{\cal Z}(t_{i},t_{j})\big]\big[\check{\sigma}(t_{i},t_{j},t_{k+1})-\check{\sigma}(t_{i},t_{j},t_{k})\big]\Big]{\cal X}_{t_{k}}.

Here and in the sequel, we are using
\approx
to denote a difference of
o(1)
term when
N\to\infty
. Then

\left.\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}\widetilde{\cal Y}(dt,0)-\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal X}_{t}\check{g}(dt)\Big]\\
\displaystyle\quad\approx\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}\widetilde{\cal Y}(dt,0)-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big[\check{g}(t_{k+1})-\check{g}(t_{k})\big]{\cal X}_{t_{k}}\Big]\approx\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[{\cal X}_{t_{k}}I_{k}\big],\end{array}\right.

(6.8)

where, for each
k
,

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}\leqslant l\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\check{b}(r,s,dl)\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\check{\sigma}(r,s,dl){\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{k+1}\leqslant l\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\check{b}(r,s,dl)\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\check{\sigma}(r,s,dl){\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds

\displaystyle-{T\over n}\sum_{k<j<i\leqslant N-1}\Big[\big[\check{b}(t_{i},t_{j},t_{k+1})-\check{b}(t_{i},t_{j},t_{k})\big]\big[\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i+1},t_{j})-\widetilde{\cal Y}(t_{i},t_{j})\big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\big[\check{\sigma}(t_{i},t_{j},t_{k+1})-\check{\sigma}(t_{i},t_{j},t_{k})\big]\big[{\cal Z}(t_{i+1},t_{j})-{\cal Z}(t_{i},t_{j})\big]\Big].

One may easily check that

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\big[\check{b}(r,s,s)-\check{b}(r,s,t_{k})\big]\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\big[\check{\sigma}(r,s,s)-\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k})\big]{\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{k+1}\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\big[\check{b}(r,s,s)-\check{b}(r,s,t_{k+1})\big]\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\big[\check{\sigma}(r,s,s)-\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k+1})\big]{\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{k+1}\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\big[\check{b}(r,s,t_{k+1})-\check{b}(r,s,t_{k})\big]\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\big[\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k+1})-\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k})\big]{\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\big[\check{b}(r,s,s)-\check{b}(r,s,t_{k})\big]\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\big[\check{\sigma}(r,s,s)-\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k})\big]{\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{k+1}\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\big[\check{b}(r,s,s)-\check{b}(r,s,t_{k})\big]\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\big[\check{\sigma}(r,s,s)-\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k})\big]{\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big[\big[\check{b}(r,s,s)-\check{b}(r,s,t_{k})\big]\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\big[\check{\sigma}(r,s,s)-\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k})\big]{\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds.

Substituting the above into (
6.8
) implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}\widetilde{\cal Y}(dt,0)-\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal X}_{t}\check{g}(dt)\Big]\approx\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big[\big[\check{b}(r,s,s)-\check{b}(r,s,t_{k})\big]\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)

(6.9)

\displaystyle+\big[\check{\sigma}(r,s,s)-\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k})\big]{\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds{\cal X}_{t_{k}}\Big].

Using the fact that the finite variated function is a.e continuous, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{t_{k}\uparrow s}\big[\check{b}(r,s,s)-\check{b}(r,s,t_{k})\big]=\lim_{t_{k}\uparrow s}\big[\check{\sigma}(r,s,s)-\check{\sigma}(r,s,t_{k})\big]=0,\quad\mbox{for a.e. $s$}.

Then from (
6.9
) we see that (
6.3
) holds true by letting
N\to\infty
.

Remark 6.2
.

In the state dependent case, the measures are degenerate:

\displaystyle\check{b}(t,s,dr)=b(t,s)\delta_{s}(r),\quad\check{\sigma}(t,s,dr)=\sigma(t,s)\delta_{s}(r),\quad\check{g}(dr)=g(r)dr,

with the Dirac measure
\delta
being defined by

\delta_{s}{\bf x}={\bf x}_{s},\quad s\in[0,T].~{\bf x}\in C([0,T]).

Then (
6.1
) and (
6.7
) become

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\cal X}_{t}=\eta_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s){\cal X}_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s){\cal X}_{s}dW_{s};\\
\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{t}=\check{g}(t)-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\Big[b(r,s)\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\sigma(r,s){\cal Z}(dr,s)\Big]ds-\int_{t}^{T}{\cal Z}(t,s)dW_{s};\\
\displaystyle\widetilde{\cal Y}(t,s)={\cal Y}_{t}-\int_{s}^{t}{\cal Z}(t,r)dW_{r},\quad 0\leqslant s\leqslant t.\end{array}\right.

(6.10)

Let
(Y,Z)
denote the solution to the following type-II BSVIE:

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle Y_{t}=g(t)+\int_{t}^{T}[b(s,t)Y_{s}+\sigma(s,t)Z(s,t)]ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW_{s};\\
\displaystyle Y_{t}=\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]+\int_{0}^{t}Z(t,s)dW_{s}.\end{array}\right.

(6.11)

We can easily check that

\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{t}={\cal Y}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}Y_{r}dr,\quad\widetilde{\cal Y}(t,s)={\cal Y}_{s}+\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{s}[Y_{r}]dr,\quad{\cal Z}(t,s)=\int_{s}^{t}Z(r,s)dr,\quad 0\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T

satisfy the BSVIE in (
6.10
). Then (
6.3
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}g(t){\cal X}_{t}dt\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\cal X}_{t}\check{g}(dt)\Big]=\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}\widetilde{\cal Y}(dt,0)=\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]dt=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}Y_{t}dt\Big].

This is exactly the duality in Yong
[
69
,
71
]
.
So our result here is a generalization of these works.

Remark 6.3
.

Our result also generalizes the duality between delayed SDEs and anticipated BSDEs in Peng–Yang
[
53
]
. Let
(X,Y,Z)
denote the solution to the following equations:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dX^{\xi}_{s}=(\mu_{s}X^{\xi}_{s}+\bar{\mu}_{s-\theta}X^{\xi}_{s-\theta})ds+(\sigma_{s}X^{\xi}_{s}+\sigma_{s-\theta}X^{\xi}_{s-\theta})dW_{s},\quad s\in[t,T+\theta],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X_{t}=\xi,\quad X_{s}=0,\quad s\in[t-\theta,t];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle-dY_{s}=\big(\mu_{s}Y_{s}+\bar{\mu}_{s}\mathbb{E}_{s}[Y_{s+\theta}]+\sigma_{s}Z_{s}+\sigma_{s}\mathbb{E}_{s}[Z_{s+\theta}]+l_{s}\big)ds-Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y_{s}=Q_{s},~~Z_{s}=P_{s},\quad s\in[T,T+\theta],\end{array}\right.

(6.12)

where
\theta>0
is a fixed delay time.
From
[
53
, Theorem 2.1]
we get the duality

\langle Y_{t},\xi\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[X^{\xi}_{T}Q_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}X^{\xi}_{s}l_{s}ds+\int_{T}^{T+\theta}(Q_{s}\bar{\mu}_{s-\theta}+P_{s}\bar{\sigma}_{s-\theta})X^{\xi}_{s-\theta}ds\Big]:=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}X^{\xi}_{s}\check{g}(ds)\Big],

(6.13)

which shows that
Y_{t}
is an explicit representation of the linear functional
\xi\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[\int_{t}^{T}X^{\xi}_{s}\check{g}(ds)\big]
.
Since FSVIE (
6.1
) is more general than the delayed SDE in (
6.12
),
we can also use Theorem
6.1
to give such an explicit representation for
\xi\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[\int_{t}^{T}X^{\xi}_{s}\check{g}(ds)\big]
.
Indeed, take

\displaystyle\check{b}(s,dr)=\mu_{s}\delta_{s}(r)+\bar{\mu}_{s-\theta}\delta_{s-\theta}(r),\quad\check{\sigma}(s,dr)=\sigma(s)\delta_{s}(r)+\bar{\sigma}_{s-\theta}\delta_{s-\theta}(r),\quad s\in[t+\theta,T],

\displaystyle\check{b}(s,dr)=\mu_{s}\delta_{s}(r),\quad\check{\sigma}(s,dr)=\sigma(s)\delta_{s}(r),\quad s\in[t,t+\theta],

\displaystyle\check{g}(dr)=l(r)dr+\mathbb{E}_{r}[Q_{r+\theta}\bar{\mu}_{r}+P_{r+\theta}\bar{\sigma}_{r}]1_{[T-\theta,T]}(r)dr+Q(T)\delta_{T}(r).

Note that
\check{b}(\tau,s,dr),\check{\sigma}(\tau,s,dr)
are independent of
\tau
, the corresponding BSVIE (
6.7
) reads

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{\tau}=\check{g}(\tau)-\int_{\tau}^{T}\mu_{s}[\widetilde{\cal Y}(T,s)-\widetilde{\cal Y}(s,s)]ds-\int_{\tau+\theta}^{T}\bar{\mu}_{s-\theta}[\widetilde{\cal Y}(T,s)-\widetilde{\cal Y}(s,s)]ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{\tau}^{T}\sigma_{s}[{\cal Z}(T,s)-{\cal Z}_{s}]ds-\int_{\tau+\theta}^{T}\bar{\sigma}_{s-\theta}[{\cal Z}(T,s)-{\cal Z}_{s}]ds-\int_{\tau}^{T}{\cal Z}_{s}dW_{s};\\
\displaystyle\widetilde{\cal Y}(\tau,s)={\cal Y}_{\tau}-\int_{s}^{\tau}{\cal Z}(\tau,r)dW_{r},\quad 0\leqslant s\leqslant\tau.\end{array}\right.

Then it is easy to check

\displaystyle Y_{t}={\cal Y}(T,t)-{\cal Y}_{t},\quad Z_{t}={\cal Z}(T,t)-{\cal Z}_{t},

\displaystyle\langle{\cal Y}(T,t)-{\cal Y}_{t},\,\xi\rangle=\langle Y_{t},\,\xi\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}X^{\xi}_{s}\check{g}(ds)\Big].

Thus Theorem
6.1
covers the duality in
[
53
]
.

Remark 6.4
.

The duality (
6.6
) still holds true in the multidimensional case, where the FSVIE (
6.1
) and type-II BSVIE (
6.7
) become

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\cal X}_{t}=\eta_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{b}(t,s,dr)ds+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}{\cal X}_{r}~\check{\sigma}^{j}(t,s,dr)dW^{j}_{s};\\
\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{t}=\check{g}(t)-\int_{t\leqslant l\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T}\Big[\check{b}(r,s,dl)\widetilde{\cal Y}(dr,s)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\check{\sigma}^{j}(r,s,dl){\cal Z}^{j}(dr,s)\Big]ds-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{t}^{T}{\cal Z}^{j}(t,s)dW_{s}^{j};\\
\displaystyle\widetilde{\cal Y}(t,s)={\cal Y}_{t}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{t}{\cal Z}^{j}(t,r)dW^{j}_{r},\quad 0\leqslant s\leqslant t,\end{array}\right.

with
\check{b},\check{\sigma}^{j}:(t,s,r,\omega)\in\mathbb{T}_{-}^{3}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\check{g}:\mathbb{T}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
being proper maps.

6.2
An explicit solution for linear BSVIEs

In this subsection we investigate the following linear BSVIE:

{\cal Y}_{t}=\xi_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\big[\alpha(t,r){\cal Y}_{r}+\beta(t,r){\cal Z}^{t}_{r}\big]dr-\int_{t}^{T}{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}dW_{r},

(6.14)

where
\xi:\mathbb{T}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha,\beta:\mathbb{T}_{+}^{2}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are progressively measurable (omitting the variable
\omega
).

Proposition 6.5
.

Assume
\alpha,\beta
are bounded and
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{t}|^{2}]<\infty
. Then

{\cal Y}_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[M^{t}_{T}\xi_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\Gamma(t,r)M^{r}_{T}\xi_{r}dr\Big],

(6.15)

where
M
is the solution to the following SDE:

dM^{t}_{r}=M^{t}_{r}\beta(t,r)dW_{r},~~(t,r)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{+};\quad M_{t}^{t}=I_{m},

(6.16)

and

\Gamma(t,s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{n}(t,s),~~K_{1}(t,s):=M^{t}_{s}\alpha(t,s),~~K_{n+1}(t,s):=\int_{t}^{s}K_{1}(t,r)K_{n}(r,s)dr.

(6.17)

Proof.

First, by Proposition
2.11
we see that (
6.14
) is wellposed. Next, since
\alpha,\beta
are bounded, it is clear that
\mathbb{E}_{t}[|K_{1}(t,s)|^{2}]\leqslant C_{0}<\infty
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[|K_{n+1}(t,s)|^{2}]\leqslant(s-t)\int_{t}^{s}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[|K_{1}(t,r)|^{2}\mathbb{E}_{r}[|K_{n}(r,s)|^{2}]\Big]dr.

Then by induction one can easily show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[|K_{n+1}(t,s)|^{2}]\leqslant{C_{0}^{n+1}(s-t)^{2n}\over(2n-1)!!},\quad\mbox{and thus}\quad\mathbb{E}_{t}[|\Gamma(t,s)|^{2}]\leqslant C<\infty.

We now let
(\widetilde{\cal Y},\widetilde{\cal Z})
satisfy the following BSDE:

\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}=\xi_{t}+\int_{s}^{T}\big[\alpha(t,r){\cal Y}_{r}+\beta(t,r)\widetilde{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}\big]dr-\int_{s}^{T}\widetilde{\cal Z}^{t}_{r}dW_{r}.

(6.18)

Then

{\cal Y}_{t}=\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{t},\quad{\cal Z}^{t}_{s}=\widetilde{\cal Z}^{t}_{s}.

Apply Itô formula to the mapping
s\mapsto M^{t}_{s}\widetilde{\cal Y}^{t}_{s}
on
[t,T]
, we get

{\cal Y}_{t}=\widetilde{\cal Y}_{t}^{t}=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[M^{t}_{T}\xi_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}M^{t}_{s}\alpha(t,s){\cal Y}_{s}ds\Big].

(6.19)

Moreover, note that

\Gamma(t,s)=K_{1}(t,s)+\int_{t}^{s}K_{1}(t,r)\Gamma(r,s)dr.

Then

\displaystyle{\cal Y}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[M^{t}_{T}\xi_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}K_{1}(t,s)\mathbb{E}_{s}\big[M^{s}_{T}\xi_{s}+\int_{s}^{T}\Gamma(s,r)M^{r}_{T}\xi_{r}dr\big]ds\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[M^{t}_{T}\xi_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}K_{1}(t,s)M^{s}_{T}\xi_{s}ds+\int_{t}^{T}K_{1}(t,s)\int_{s}^{T}\Gamma(s,r)M^{r}_{T}\xi_{r}drds\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[M^{t}_{T}\xi_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}K_{1}(t,s)M^{s}_{T}\xi_{s}ds+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{t}^{r}K_{1}(t,s)\Gamma(s,r)dsM^{r}_{T}\xi_{r}dr\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[M^{t}_{T}\xi_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\big[K_{1}(t,s)+\int_{t}^{s}K_{1}(t,r)\Gamma(r,s)dr\big]M^{s}_{T}\xi_{s}ds\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[M^{t}_{T}\xi_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\Gamma(t,s)M^{s}_{T}\xi_{s}ds\Big]={\cal Y}_{t}.

This implies that
({\cal Y},{\cal Z})
satisfy (
6.14
). The result then follows from the uniqueness of (
6.14
).

Remark 6.6
.

The representation (
6.15
) of
{\cal Y}
is exactly the so-called
variation of constants formula
for linear BSVIEs.
A similar result was first obtained by Hu–Øksendal
[
36
]
for the linear BSVIEs driven by a Brownian motion and a compensated Poisson random measure. However, in
[
36
]
the coefficients
\alpha,\beta
are assumed to be deterministic functions and
\beta(t,r)\equiv\beta(r)
is required to be independent of
t
. Thus our result is a generalized version of
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
.

In this subsection we assume Assumption

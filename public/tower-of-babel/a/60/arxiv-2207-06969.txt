Revisiting the magnetic and crystal structure of multiferroic KNiPO 4
Alexandre Pages, Jian-Rui Soh, Marjaneh Jafari Fesharaki, Henrik M. Ronnow, Hossein Ahmadvand

Abstract
The magnetic, dielectric and structural properties of type-I multiferroic KNiPO 4 have been investigated by neutron powder diffraction, magnetization, dielectric and high temperature synchrotron-XRD measurements. Below the Néel transition of T N T_{\mathrm{N}} = 25 K, KNiPO 4 displays a weakly non-collinear antiferromagnetic (AFM) structure with the orientation of the Ni 2+ magnetic moments mainly along a a axis. The compound crystallizes in the polar orthorhombic P ​ n ​ a ​ 2 1 Pna2_{1} space group at room temperature. A second-order structural phase transition corresponding to the onset ferroelectricity is observed at around T C ∼ T_{\mathrm{C}}\sim 594(3) ∘ C, above which the crystal structure of KNiPO 4 adopts the centrosymmetric P ​ n ​ m ​ a Pnma space group. The compound also displays another structural phase transition at T 0 ∼ T_{\mathrm{0}}\sim 469 – 488 ∘ C, with a first-order character, which is attributed to the rearrangement of oxygen ligands, resulting in a change in the nickel ion co-ordination from four to five.



I
Introduction

Multiferroic materials, which display a strong coupling between magnetic and ferroelectric order, have received sustained scientific interest over the last decade and hold considerable promise for a wide range of technological applications. In these magnetoelectric multiferroics, time-reversal symmetry (TRS) and spatial inversion symmetry (IS) are spontaneously broken and a magnetoelectric coupling between the magnetic and ferroelectric orders is possible. Considering the prevalence of materials with either magnetic or ferroelectric order in devices (e.g. ferroelectric capacitors, transducers and actuators and ferromagnetic sensors and permanent magnets), using materials where both orders are coupled is the next natural step in technological development
Cano et al. 2021
.

An example of a magnetoelectric multiferroic material where the magnetic and ferroelectric orders are coupled is KNiPO
4

Kurkin et al. 2003
;
Leskovets et al. 2002
. At room temperature, the orthorhombic unit cell of KNiPO
4

space group, where the broken IS gives rise to a spontaneous ferroelectric order
Fischer et al. 1994

= 25 K, signalling the breaking of TRS
Fischer et al. 1994

et al.

reported that below
T_{\mathrm{N}}

4
are non-zero (namely,
\alpha_{21}
and
\alpha_{12}
), indicating that the magnetic and ferroelectric orders in KNiPO
4
are coupled. However, some questions regarding the nature of TRS and IS breaking remain, which we consider in turn.

In a powder neutron diffraction study of KNiPO

As seen above, there has been a considerable effort to characterize KNiPO

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we present a comprehensive study of the magnetic and crystal structure of KNiPO
4
. Magnetization measurements show an antiferromagnetic order below
T_{\mathrm{N}}
=25(1) K, with a slight spin-flop behavior at low temperatures. Neutron diffraction at
T
= 1.5 K, reveal a weakly non-collinear structure in the
Pna2_{1}
space group, with magnetic propagation vector
k
= 0. The value of effective moment of Ni
2+
ions was found to be 3.27 and 3.87
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
from the magnetic susceptibility and neutron diffraction measurements, respectively, which state that the orbital momentum lies in between the unquenched and quenched states.

The crystal structure of KNiPO
4
was studied by synchrotron radiation, in the temperature range of
T
= 20 to 750
∘
C, in order to characterize the structural transitions. Refinement of the data suggests anisotropic changes in the unit cell of KNiPO
4
, where the most drastic change was observed in
c

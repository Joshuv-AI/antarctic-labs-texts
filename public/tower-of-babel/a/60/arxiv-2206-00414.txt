An analytical and computational study of the incompressible Toner-Tu Equations Journal: Physica D
John D. Gibbon, Kolluru Venkata Kiran, Nadia Bihari Padhan, Rahul Pandit

Abstract
The incompressible Toner-Tu (ITT) partial differential equations (PDEs) are an important example of a set of active-fluid PDEs. While they share certain properties with the Navier-Stokes equations (NSEs), such as the same scaling invariance, there are also important differences. The NSEs are usually considered in either the decaying or the additively forced cases, whereas the ITT equations have no additive forcing. Instead, they include a linear, activity term α ​ 𝒖 \alpha\mbox{\boldmath$u$} ( 𝒖 u is the velocity field) which pumps energy into the system, but also a negative 𝒖 ​ | 𝒖 | 2 \mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2} -term which provides a platform for either frozen or statistically steady states. Taken together, these differences make the ITT equations an intriguing candidate for study using a combination of PDE analysis and pseudo-spectral direct numerical simulations (DNSs). In the d = 2 d=2 case, we have established global regularity of solutions, but we have also shown the existence of bounded hierarchies of weighted, time-averaged norms of both higher derivatives and higher moments of the velocity field. Similar bounded hierarchies for Leray-type weak solutions have also been established in the d = 3 d=3 case. We present results for these norms from our DNSs in both d = 2 d=2 and d = 3 d=3 , and contrast them with their Navier-Stokes counterparts.

2
Dimensionless equations

The standard form of the incompressible Toner-Tu (ITT) equations is given by
TT1995
,
TTR2005
,
RP2020
:

\left(\partial_{t}+\lambda\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\right)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p=\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-\beta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}\,.

(2.1)

The fixed parameters
\alpha,\,\beta
are positive and the velocity field
u
satisfies the incompressibility condition
\mbox{div}\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0
.
\beta
has the dimension
TL^{-2}\equiv[\nu^{-1}]
,
\alpha
is a frequency and
\lambda
is a dimensionless parameter. The domain is taken to be a periodic box
[0,\,L]^{d}_{per}
. We leave remarks until §
3
on the literature involving generalizations of this system to a
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2\delta}
nonlinear term.

The first step is to introduce a typical velocity field
U_{0}
for which we have two definitions:

U_{0}=\sqrt{\alpha/\beta}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad U_{0}=\nu/L\,.

(2.2)

Then primed dimensionless variables are defined thus :

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{\prime}=L^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\,;\qquad t^{\prime}=U_{0}L^{-1}t\,;\qquad\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\prime}=\lambda U_{0}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\,;\qquad p^{\prime}=\lambda U_{0}^{-2}p\,.

(2.3)

This transforms (
2.1
) into the dimensionless ITT equations (dropping the primes) which, from now on, will be the form used in this paper :

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\right)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+{\rm Re}_{\nu}^{-1}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-{\rm Re}_{\beta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}\,,

(2.4)

together with the incompressibility condition
\mbox{div}\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0
. These operate on the unit periodic box
V_{d}=[0,\,1]^{d}
. The two Reynolds numbers
{\rm Re}_{\nu}
and
{\rm Re}_{\beta}
are defined as follows :

\displaystyle{\rm Re}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{U_{0}L}{\nu}\,,\qquad{\rm Re}_{\beta}=\frac{\beta U_{0}L}{\lambda^{2}}\,,

(2.5)

taken together with the dimensionless frequency
\alpha_{0}=L\alpha U_{0}^{-1}
. The second choice of
U_{0}
corresponds to
{\rm Re}_{\nu}=1
.

Invariant scaling, time averages and length scales

The incompressible NSEs possess the following well-known and powerful invariant scaling property involving an arbitrary parameter
\ell
:

\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{\prime}=\ell^{-1}x;\quad t^{\prime}=\ell^{-2}t;\quad\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\ell^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\prime};

(3.1)

which means that these equations are valid at every scale. The effect of this invariance is to scale the norms
\|\nabla^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2m}

\|\nabla^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2m}=\left(\int_{V_{d}}|\nabla^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2m}dV_{d}\right)^{1/2m}

in the following way:

\|\nabla^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2m}=\ell^{-1/\alpha_{m,n,d}}\|\nabla^{{}^{\prime}n}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\prime}\|_{2m}\,,

where
\alpha_{n,m,d}
is defined by

The

In keeping with standard Navier-Stokes notation, we define
n

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{V_{d}}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}dV_{d}\,;

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{V_{d}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}dV_{d}=\int_{V_{d}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}|^{2}dV_{d}\,.

-term

Thus, equilibration of the right hand side occurs at

6.2
Regularity : exponential bounds in
d=2
dimensions

The difference between the
2d
and
3d
NSEs lies in the absence of the vortex stretching term
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
, which is zero when
d=2
. For the ITT equations (
2.4
), the evolution equation for the vorticity is

\left(\partial_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}=\alpha_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}+{\rm Re}_{\nu}^{-1}\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}-{\rm Re}_{\beta}\,\mbox{curl}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}\right)

(6.11)

in which the
\mbox{curl}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}\right)
-term appears to create another form of vortex stretching. Specifically we have

\displaystyle{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}}\dot{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}H_{1}-{\rm Re}_{\nu}^{-1}H_{2}-{\rm Re}_{\beta}\int_{V}\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\cdot\mbox{curl}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2})dV

(6.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}H_{1}-{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\rm Re}_{\nu}^{-1}H_{2}+{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\rm Re}_{\beta}^{2}{\rm Re}_{\nu}\int_{V}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{6}dV\,,

where we have integrated by parts and have then used a Hölder inequality. Then we use a Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{6}\leq c\,\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2}^{{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{3}}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{4}^{{\scriptstyle\frac{2}{3}}}\,,

(6.13)

which can be re-expressed as

\int_{V}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{6}dV\leq c\,H_{1}\int_{V}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{4}dV\,.

(6.14)

Thus we can write

{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}}\dot{H}_{1}\leq\left(\alpha_{0}+{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\rm Re}_{\beta}^{2}{\rm Re}_{\nu}\int_{V}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{4}\,dV\right)H_{1}\,,

(6.15)

whence we can use the extra piece of information afforded to us by the bounded time integral expressed in (
4.9
). Thus (
6.15
) can be integrated to become

\displaystyle H_{1}(T)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle H_{1}(0)\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left(\alpha_{0}+c\,{\rm Re}_{\beta}^{2}{\rm Re}_{\nu}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{4}^{4}\right)\,d\tau\right\}

(6.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle H_{1}(0)\exp\left\{\alpha_{0}\left(1+c\,{\rm Re}_{\beta}^{2}{\rm Re}_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{2}\right)T\right\}\,,

which is finite for every finite
T
. Control over the
H_{1}
-norm establishes global regularity in this
2d
case but not a global attractor, which requires a uniform bound for all
t
.

8
Conclusions

We have married the two approaches of the analysis of solutions of the ITT equations through the estimation of weighted time averages, together with the results of numerical simulations. To achieve this we have invoked the similar scaling properties between the ITT equations and the NSEs : see §
3
. There are, however, two important differences. Usually the NSEs are considered either in the decaying or the additively forced case, whereas the ITT equations have no additive forcing but instead possess a linear-activity term
\alpha_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
which, in effect, pumps energy into the system. Dynamically the effect of this term, together with the negative cubic term, creates a platform for either temporally frozen solutions or statistically steady states. Furthermore, it is shown in §
6.2
that the greatest contrast with the
2d
NSEs lies at the level of the vorticity equation. Whereas the absence of vortex stretching in
2d
NSEs lies at the root of the regularity of its solutions, the
\mbox{curl}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}\right)
-term in the ITT equations appears to recreate another form of vortex stretching. However, the extra piece of information afforded to us is the bounded time average of the
L^{4}

Appendix A
Differential Inequalities

The most widely used class of differential inequalities that generalize the Sobolev inequalities are called Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities
Adams1975
. In their most general form in integer
d
dimensions (
d=1,2,3
) they can be expressed as

\|\nabla^{j}u\|_{p}\leq c\,\|\nabla^{n}u\|_{r}^{a}\|u\|_{q}^{1-a}\,,

(A.1)

where
0\leq j<n
and
1<p,\,r,\,q\leq\infty
. The exponent
a
can be calculated by dimensional analysis and thus must satisfy

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{j}{d}+a\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{n}{d}\right)+\frac{1-a}{q}\,,

(A.2)

where
j/n\leq a<1
. (
A.2
) holds on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. With periodic boundary conditions, there are
L^{2}
additional terms there are lower-order corrections to our estimates, which will be ignored. In the following :

1.

The standard Leray-Hopf weak-solution machinery has been used, which has been derived for the NSEs in
FGT1981
.

2.

We will use the convention that the constants designated as
c
,
c_{m}
or
c_{n,m}
are generic in the sense that they may differ from line to line.

\displaystyle\left<\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2m}^{\alpha_{0,m}}\right>_{T}

\displaystyle c\,\left<\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2}^{a\alpha_{0,m}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{4}^{(1-a)\alpha_{0,m}}\right>_{T}

(C.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\,\left<Q_{2,1}^{3a\alpha_{0,m}/2}\left(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{4}^{4}\right)^{{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{4}}(1-a)\alpha_{0,m}}\right>_{T}\,,

where
\alpha_{0,m}=\frac{2m}{2m-3}
. Then

\left<\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2m}^{\alpha_{0,m}}\right>_{T}\leq c\,\left<Q_{2,1}\right>_{T}^{3a\alpha_{0,m}/2}\left<\left(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{4}^{4}\right)^{\frac{{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{4}}(1-a)\alpha_{0,m}}{1-3a\alpha_{0,m}/2}}\right>_{T}^{1-3a\alpha_{0,m}/2}\,.

(C.26)

Given
a
and
\alpha_{0,m}
, it can easily be checked that the exponent of
\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{4}^{4}
inside the average is unity. Thus, because
\left<\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{4}^{4}\right>_{T}\leq c\,\mathcal{A}_{0}^{2}
and with the help of (
C.16
), (
C.26
) becomes

\displaystyle\left<\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2m}^{\alpha_{0,m}}\right>_{T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,(\alpha_{0}{\rm Re}_{\nu}^{2})^{3a\alpha_{0,m}/2}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{2-3a\alpha_{0,m}}

(C.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}^{3a\alpha_{0,m}/2}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{2-3a\alpha_{0,m}}{\rm Re}_{\nu}^{3a\alpha_{0,m}}\,.

In fact
3a\alpha_{0,m}/2=\frac{9(m-2)}{5(2m-3)}
and so
1-3a\alpha_{0,m}/2=\frac{m+3}{5(2m-3)}
,
whence

\left<\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{2m}^{\alpha_{0,m}}\right>_{T}\leq c\,\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\frac{2(m+3)}{5(2m-3)}}\left(\alpha_{0}{\rm Re}_{\nu}^{2}\right)^{\frac{9(m-2)}{5(2m-3)}}\qquad m>2\,,

(C.28)

as advertised in (
7.4
). In the limit
m\to\infty
, we find that

\displaystyle\left<\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{\infty}\right>_{T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,\mathcal{A}_{0}^{1/5}\left(\alpha_{0}{\rm Re}_{\nu}^{2}\right)^{9/10}\,,

(C.29)

as advertised in (
7.5
).

An adaptive gradient method for computing generalized tensor eigenpairs
Gaohang Yu, Zefeng Yu, Yi Xu, Yisheng Song

Abstract
High order tensor arises more and more often in signal processing, data analysis, higher-order statistics, as well as imaging sciences. In this paper, an adaptive gradient (AG) method is presented for generalized tensor eigenpairs. Global convergence and linear convergence rate are established under some suitable conditions. Numerical results are reported to illustrate the efficiency of the proposed method. Comparing with the GEAP method, an adaptive shifted power method proposed by Tamara G. Kolda and Jackson R. Mayo [SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl., 35 (2014), pp. 1563-1581], the AG method is much faster and could reach the largest eigenpair with a higher probability. Keywords: Higher order Tensor, Eigenvalue, Eigenvector, Gradient method, Power method.

(\mathcal{A}x^{m-1})_{i}=\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}.

(1)

(2)

In this paper, we consider an adaptive gradient method for solving the following nonlinear programming problem:

\max f(x)=\frac{\mathcal{A}x^{m}}{\mathcal{B}x^{m}}\ \ \mbox{subject to}\ \ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

(5)

Where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
denote the unit sphere, i.e.,
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\|x\|^{2}=1\}
,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm.
By some simple calculations, we can get its gradient and Hessian, as follows
[
21
,
10
]
:

g(x)\equiv\nabla f(x)=\frac{m}{\mathcal{B}x^{m}}(\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}-\frac{\mathcal{A}x^{m}}{\mathcal{B}x^{m}}\mathcal{B}x^{m-1}),

(6)

and its Hessian is

\displaystyle H(x)\equiv

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{m(m-1)\mathcal{A}x^{m-2}}{\mathcal{B}x^{m}}-\frac{m(m-1)\mathcal{A}x^{m}\mathcal{B}x^{m-2}+m^{2}(\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}\circledcirc\mathcal{B}x^{m-1})}{(\mathcal{B}x^{m})^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{m^{2}\mathcal{A}x^{m}(\mathcal{B}x^{m-1}\circledcirc\mathcal{B}x^{m-1})}{(\mathcal{B}x^{m})^{3}},

where
x\circledcirc y=xy^{T}+yx^{T}
, and
\mathcal{A}x^{m-2}
is a matrix with its component as

(\mathcal{A}x^{m-2})_{i_{1}i_{2}}=\sum_{i_{3},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\cdots i_{m}}x_{i_{3}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ i_{1},i_{2}=1,\cdots,n.

According to (
6
), we can derive an important property for the nonlinear programming problem (
5
) that the gradient
g(x)
is located in the tangent plane of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
at
x

[
10
]
, since

x^{T}g(x)=\frac{m}{\mathcal{B}x^{m}}(x^{T}\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}-\frac{\mathcal{A}x^{m}}{\mathcal{B}x^{m}}x^{T}\mathcal{B}x^{m-1})=0.

(7)

Let
\overline{x}
is a constrained stationary point of (
5
), i.e., that

\langle g(\overline{x}),x-\overline{x}\rangle\leq 0\ \ \mbox{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Then we can claim that
every constrained stationary point
of (
5
)
must be a stationary point
of
f(x)
since

\langle g(\overline{x}),x\rangle\leq 0
should be hold for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.
Otherwise, if
\|g(\overline{x})\|\neq 0
, we could choose
x=\frac{g(\overline{x})}{\|g(\overline{x})\|}
, and then
\|g(\overline{x})\|\leq 0
.

Suppose
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and denote
\lambda=\frac{\mathcal{A}x^{m}}{\mathcal{B}x^{m}}
. By
g(x)=0
, we know that any KKT point of (
5
) will be a solution of the system of equations (
3
).

If the gradient

at

is a generalized eigenvalue and the vector

2
Some exist gradient methods for tensor eigenpairs

2.1
Gradient method with fixed stepsize–power method

The symmetric higher-order power method (S-HOPM) was introduced by De Lathauwer, De Moor, and Vandewalle
[
23
]
for solving the following optimization problem:

\max f(x)=\mathcal{A}x^{m}\ \ \mbox{subject to}\ \ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

(8)

This problem is equivalent to finding the largest Z-eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}

[
34
]
and is related to finding the best symmetric rank-1 approximation of a symmetric tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{S}^{[m,n]}

[
23
]
.

Algorithm 1: Symmetric higher order power Method (S-HOPM)
[
23
]

Given a tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{S}^{[m,n]}
,
an initial unit iterate
x_{0}
. Let
\lambda_{0}=\mathcal{A}x_{0}^{m}.

for

k=0,1,\ldots

do

1:

\hat{x}_{k+1}\leftarrow\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-1}

2:

x_{k+1}\leftarrow\hat{x}_{k+1}/\|\hat{x}_{k+1}\|

3:

\lambda_{k+1}=\mathcal{A}x_{k+1}^{m}

End for

The cost per iteration of power method is
O(mn^{m})
, mainly for computing
\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-1}
. Let
g(x_{k})=\nabla f(x_{k})=\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-1}
. Set
d_{k}=g(x_{k})-x_{k}
,
\hat{x}_{k+1}=x_{k}+d_{k}
, then the main iteration could be reformulated as
x_{k+1}=\frac{x_{k}+d_{k}}{\|x_{k}+d_{k}\|}
, which is a projected gradient method with unit-stepsize. Kofidis and Regalia
[
20
]
pointed out that S-HOPM method can not guarantee to converge. By using convexity theory, they show that S-HOPM method could be convergent for even-order tensors under the convexity assumption on
\mathcal{A}x^{m}
. For general symmetric tensors, a shifted S-HOPM (SS-HOPM) method was proposed by Kolda and Mayo
[
22
]
for computing Z-eigenpairs. One shortcoming of SS-HOPM is that its performance depended on choosing an appropriate shift. Recently, Kolda and Mayo extended SS-HOPM for computing generalized tensor eigenpairs, called GEAP method which is an adaptive, monotonically convergent, shifted power method for generalized tensor eigenpairs (
3
). They showed that GEAP method is much faster than the SS-HOPM method due to its adaptive shift choice.

Algorithm 2: GEAP Method
[
21
]

Given tensors
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{S}^{[m,n]}
and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{[m,n]}
, and an initial guess
\hat{x}_{0}
. Let
\beta=1
if we want to find the local maxima; otherwise, let
\beta=-1
for seeking local minima. Let
\tau>0
be the tolerance on being positive/negative definite.

for

k=0,1,\ldots

do

1:
Precompute
\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-2}
,
\mathcal{B}x_{k}^{m-2}
,
\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-1}
,
\mathcal{B}x_{k}^{m-1}
,
\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m}
,
\mathcal{B}x_{k}^{m}

2:

\lambda_{k}=\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m}/\mathcal{B}x_{k}^{m}

3:

H_{k}\leftarrow H(x_{k})=\nabla^{2}f(x_{k})

4:

\alpha_{k}\leftarrow\beta\max\{0,(\tau-\lambda_{min}(\beta H_{k}))/m

5:

\hat{x}_{k+1}\leftarrow\beta(\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-1}-\lambda_{k}\mathcal{B}x_{k}^{m-1}+(\alpha_{k}+\lambda_{k})\mathcal{B}x_{k}^{m}x_{k})

6:

x_{k+1}\leftarrow\hat{x}_{k+1}/\|\hat{x}_{k+1}\|

End for

In
[
28
]
, Ng, Qi and Zhou proposed a power method for finding the largest H-eigenvalue of irreducible nonnegative tensors. It is proved in
[
6
]
that NQZ’s power method is convergent for primitive nonnegative tensors. Further, Zhang et. al
[
51

52

established its linear convergence result and presented some updated version for essentially positive tensors and weakly positive tensors, respectively.
However, similarly to the case of Matrix, when the largest eigenvalue is close to the second dominant eigenvalue, the convergence of power method will be very slow
[
11
]
.

2.2
Gradient method with optimal stepsize

In
[
15
]
, Hao, Cui and Dai proposed a sequential subspace projection method (SSPM) for Z-eigenvalue of symmetric tensors. In each iteration of SSPM method, one need to solve the following 2-dimensional subproblem:

\max_{x\in span\{x_{k},\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-1}\}}f(x)=\mathcal{A}x^{m}\ \ \mbox{subject to}\ \ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

(9)

Let
g_{k}\triangleq\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-1}
. The point in
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap span\{x_{k},g_{k}\}
can be expressed as

x(\alpha)=\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}x_{k}+\alpha g_{k},\ \ -\frac{1}{\|g_{k}\|}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{\|g_{k}\|}.

If
\alpha_{k}\equiv\frac{1}{\|g_{k}\|}
, then SSPM method will reduce to the power method.
For simplicity, if
\alpha_{k}\neq\frac{1}{\|g_{k}\|}
, the iterate can be expressed as
x(\alpha)=x_{k}+\sigma g_{k}
with
\sigma=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}}
. In order to solve (
9
), one need to solve a equation like
\nabla f(x_{k}+\sigma g_{k})^{T}g_{k}=(\mathcal{A}(x_{k}+\sigma g_{k})^{m-1})^{T}g_{k}=0
. For each iteration, the computational cost of SSPM method is
m
times than that of power method. As shown in
[
15
]
, the main computational cost of SSPM is the tensor-vector multiplications
\mathcal{A}x_{k}^{m-1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{k}
(defined in
[
15
]
), which requires
O(mn^{m})
operations and
O(m^{2}n^{m})
operations, respectively.

3
Inexact gradient method

Indicated by the idea in
[
15
]
, we can present a gradient method with optimal stepsize for computing the generalized tensor eigenpairs problem (
5
). But we don’t want to present it here, since the computational cost per iterate is more expensive than Power method.
In this section, we firstly present the following inexact gradient method, and then establish its global convergence and linear convergence results under some suitable assumptions.

Algorithm 3: Adaptive Gradient (AG) method for (
5
)

Given tensors
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{S}^{[m,n]}
and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{[m,n]}
, an initial unit iterate
x_{0}
, parameter
\rho\in(0,1)
. Let
\epsilon>0
be the tolerance. Set k=0; Calculate gradient
g(x_{k})
.

While

\|g(x_{k})\|>\epsilon

do

1:
Generate a stepsize
\frac{1}{\|g_{k}\|}\geq\alpha_{k}>0
such that
x_{k}(\alpha)=\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}x_{k}+\alpha g_{k}
satisfying

f(x_{k}(\alpha))\geq f(x_{k})+\rho\alpha\|g(x_{k})\|^{2}

(10)

2:
Update the iterate
x_{k+1}=x_{k}(\alpha_{k})
, calculate
g(x_{k+1})
.

End while

It is clear that
x_{k+1}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap span\{x_{k},g_{k}\}
. Moreover, by using (
7
), we can show the first-order gain per iterate is
g_{k}^{T}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})=\alpha_{k}\|g_{k}\|^{2}
. Since the spherical feasible region
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is compact,
\mathcal{B}x^{m}
is positive and bounds away from zero, we can get that all the functions and gradients of the objective (
5
) at feasible points are bounded
[
10
]
, i.e., there exists a constant
M>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

|f(x)|\leq M,\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \|g(x)\|\leq M.

(11)

The following theorem indicates that the Algorithm 3 is convergent to the KKT point of the problem (
5
). The constructive proof is motivated by the idea in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2

Suppose that the gradient
g(x)
is Lipschitz continuous on the unit shpere. Let
\{x_{k}\}
is generated by Algorithm 3. Then the inexact curvilinear search condition defined in (
10
) is well-defined and there exists a positive constant
c>0
such that

f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})\geq c\|g_{k}\|^{2}.

(12)

Furthermore,

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{k}\|=0.

Proof.
Firstly, we have

x_{k}^{\prime}(\alpha)=\frac{-\alpha\|g_{k}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}}x_{k}+g_{k}.

(13)

Furthermore, we can obtain

\frac{\mbox{d}f(x_{k}(\alpha))}{\mbox{d}\alpha}|_{\alpha=0}=g(x_{k}(\alpha))^{T}x_{k}^{\prime}(\alpha)|_{\alpha=0}=\|g_{k}\|^{2}.

(14)

Let
d_{k}(\alpha)=x_{k}(\alpha)-x_{k}
, using (
7
), we can derived that for any constant
\rho\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive scalar
\bar{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\|g_{k}\|}
such that for all

\alpha\in(0,\bar{\alpha}]
,

f(x_{k}(\alpha))-f(x_{k})\geq\rho\langle g_{k},d_{k}(\alpha)\rangle=\rho\alpha\|g(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Considering the gap between
f(x_{k}(\alpha))
and
f(x_{k})
, similarly to the proof of Lemma 4.2 in
[
15
]
, we can get

\begin{split}f(x_{k}(\alpha))-f(x_{k})=&\int_{0}^{\alpha}g(x_{k}(t))^{T}x^{\prime}_{k}(t)dt\\
=&\alpha g(x_{k}(0))^{T}x^{\prime}_{k}(0)+\int_{0}^{\alpha}g(x_{k}(t))^{T}[x^{\prime}_{k}(t)-x^{\prime}_{k}(0)]dt\\
&+\int_{0}^{\alpha}[g(x_{k}(t))-g(x_{k}(0))]^{T}x^{\prime}_{k}(0)dt\\
&(\mbox{Using (\ref{eq:dxk}), (\ref{eq:derivefxa}), and Lipschitz condition})\\
\geq&\alpha\|g_{k}\|^{2}-M\int_{0}^{\alpha}\|x^{\prime}_{k}(t)-x^{\prime}_{k}(0)\|dt-L\|g_{k}\|\int_{0}^{\alpha}\|x_{k}(t)-x_{k}(0)\|dt\\
\geq&\alpha\|g_{k}\|^{2}-M\|g_{k}\|^{2}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{t}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}}dt-L\|g_{k}\|\int_{0}^{\alpha}\sqrt{2-2\sqrt{1-t^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}}dt\\
\end{split}

Without loss of generality, assume that
\alpha_{k}\leq\tilde{\alpha}\triangleq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\|g_{k}\|}
, then for
t\leq\alpha\leq\tilde{\alpha}
, we have

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tilde{\alpha}^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}}=2,

and

\sqrt{2-2\sqrt{1-t^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}}}\leq 2t\|g_{k}\|.

So, we can obtain that

f(x_{k}(\alpha))-f(x_{k})\geq(\alpha-M\alpha^{2}-L\alpha^{2})\|g_{k}\|^{2}=(1-M\alpha-L\alpha)\alpha\|g_{k}\|^{2}.

(15)

Set
\bar{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2(M+L)}
, we have
1>1-M\alpha-L\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all
\alpha\in(0,\bar{\alpha}]
. It follows from (
15
) that (
10
) holds for all
\alpha\in(0,\bar{\alpha}]
with
\rho=\frac{1}{2}
.
So, by using a backward strategy in curvilinear search, one can claim that the stepsize
\alpha_{k}
is bounded from below. That is to say,
there exists a positive constant
c
(e.g.
c=\frac{1}{4(M+L)}
) such that (
12
) holds. As
f(x)
is bounded on the unit sphere, by (
12
), it is easy to prove that
\sum_{k}\|g_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty
. Therefore,
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{k}\|=0;
namely, the Algorithm 3 is globally convergent.
\quad\Box

In the rest of this section, we would like to establish the linear convergence rate of the Algorithm 3 under the assumption of second order sufficient condition. For convenience, rewrite (
5
) as

\max f(x)=\frac{\mathcal{A}x^{m}}{\mathcal{B}x^{m}}\ \ \mbox{subject to}\ \ c(x)=x^{T}x-1=0.

(16)

The Lagrangian function is
L(x,\mu)=f(x)-\mu c(x),
and its gradient and Hessian are

\nabla_{x}L(x,\mu)=\nabla f(x)-\mu x,\mbox{and}\ \nabla_{x}^{2}L(x,\mu)=\nabla^{2}f(x)-\mu I_{n}.

At the KKT point
(x^{*},\mu^{*})
, we have
\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\mu^{*})=0.
So,
\nabla f(x^{*})^{T}x^{*}-\mu^{*}\|x^{*}\|^{2}=0
. By (
7
), we know
\mu^{*}=\nabla f(x^{*})^{T}x^{*}=0.
We can formulate the sufficient conditions for
x^{*}
being a strict local maximizer of (
16
) as:
g(x^{*})=0
and

v^{T}H(x^{*})v<0,\forall v\in\nabla c(x^{*})^{\bot}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

(17)

where
g(x)=\nabla f(x),\ H(x)=\nabla^{2}f(x)
, and
x^{\bot}\equiv\{y\in\Re^{n}|x\perp y\}.

By Theorem
2
, the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
generated by Algorithm 3 is convergent to a KKT point
x^{*}
with
g(x^{*})=0
. If we further assume that the assumption of second sufficient condition (
17
) holds at
x^{*}
, then the following linear convergence theorem could be established for Algorithm 3.

Theorem 3

Let
\{x_{k}\}
is generated by Algorithm 3. Suppose that the gradient
g(x)
is Lipschitz continuous on the unit sphere and the second sufficient condition (
17
) holds at the KKT point
x^{*}
. Then
\{f(x_{k})\}
converges to
f(x^{*})
linearly.

Proof.
In order to show
\{f(x_{k})\}
converges to
f(x^{*})
linearly, we need to prove

0<\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k+1})}{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})}=1-\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})}{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})}<1.

(18)

To end of this, we firstly deduce that
f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})>0
. Project
x_{k}-x^{*}
on the orthogonal space of
\nabla c(x^{*})=2x^{*}
, we get

v_{k}=x_{k}-x^{*}-((x_{k}-x^{*})^{T}\nabla c(x^{*}))\nabla c(x^{*})=x_{k}-x^{*}-4((x_{k}-x^{*})^{T}x^{*})x^{*}.

It is clear that
v_{k}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, since
\|x_{k}\|^{2}=\|x^{*}\|^{2}=1
. Notice that
-(x_{k}-x^{*})^{T}x^{*}=1-x_{k}^{T}x^{*}=\frac{1}{2}(x_{k}-x^{*})^{T}(x_{k}-x^{*})
,
we can obtain that

x_{k}-x^{*}=v_{k}-2\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}x^{*}=v_{k}+O(\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}).

By the Taylor expansion, and the second order sufficient condition (
17
), we have

\begin{split}f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})=&-\frac{1}{2}(x_{k}-x^{*})^{T}H(x^{*})(x_{k}-x^{*})+o(\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2})\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}(v_{k}+O(\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}))^{T}H(x^{*})(v_{k}+O(\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}))+o(\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2})\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}v_{k}^{T}H(x^{*})v_{k}+o(\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2})>0.\\
\end{split}

Secondly, let us consider the following limitation:

\begin{split}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|g_{k}\|^{2}}{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})}=&\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|H(x^{*})(x_{k}-x^{*})\|^{2}}{-\frac{1}{2}v_{k}^{T}H(x^{*})v_{k}}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2\|H(x^{*})v_{k}\|^{2}}{-v_{k}^{T}H(x^{*})v_{k}}.\\
\end{split}

Let
U\in\Re^{n\times(n-1)}
be the orthogonal complement of the vector
\nabla c(x^{*})
. For any
v\in\nabla c(x^{*})^{\bot}
, there exist
w\in\Re^{n-1}
such that
v=Uw.
Suppose that the Cholesky decomposition of the positive definite matrix
-U^{T}H(x^{*})U
is
D^{T}D
. Denoting
y=Dw
. Notice that
\|U\|_{2}=\sqrt{\|U^{T}U\|}=1,
we can derive that:

\begin{split}\frac{2\|H(x^{*})v\|^{2}}{-v^{T}H(x^{*})v}=&\frac{2\|H(x^{*})Uw\|^{2}}{-w^{T}U^{T}H(x^{*})Uw}\\
=&\frac{2\|U\|\|H(x^{*})Uw\|^{2}}{-w^{T}U^{T}H(x^{*})Uw}\\
\geq&\frac{2\|U^{T}H(x^{*})Uw\|^{2}}{-w^{T}U^{T}H(x^{*})Uw}\\
=&\frac{-2y^{T}U^{T}H(x^{*})Uy}{y^{T}y}\\
\geq&2\lambda_{n-1},\end{split}

where
\lambda_{n-1}
is the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix
-U^{T}H(x^{*})U
. Therefore, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|g_{k}\|^{2}}{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})}\geq 2\lambda_{n-1}.

(19)

It follows from (
12
) and (
19
) that

\begin{split}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k+1})}{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})}=&1-\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})}{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})}\\
\leq&1-\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{c\|g_{k}\|^{2}}{f(x^{*})-f(x_{k})}\\
\leq&1-2c\lambda_{n-1}\\
\leq&1.\end{split}

The proof is completed.
\quad\Box

In this section, we present some numerical results to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed adaptive gradient (AG) method, which was compared

]
.

[
1

.
We set the parameter
\rho=0.001
and initial guess of the stepsize
\alpha
in (
10
) is generated by
\min(\frac{1}{\|g_{k}\|},\frac{\|\triangle x_{k}\|}{\|\triangle g_{k}\|})
. If this initial guess can not satisfy the line search condition (
10
), then we truncate it as
\alpha=0.5*\alpha
, and try it again. Generally, once or twice is enough in our experiments.

In all numerical experiments, we stop the iterates once

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced an adaptive gradient (AG) method for generalized tensor eigenpairs, which could be viewed as an inexact version of the gradient method with optimal stepsize for finding Z-eigenvalues of tensor in
[
15
]
. What we have done is to use an inexact curvilinear search condition to replace the constraint on optimal stepsize. So, the computational complexity of AG method is much cheaper than SSPM method in
[
15
]
. Global convergence and linear convergence rate are established for the AG method for computing generalized eigenpairs of symmetric tensor. Some numerical experiments illustrated that the AG method is faster than GEAP and could reach the largest eigenpair with a higher probability.

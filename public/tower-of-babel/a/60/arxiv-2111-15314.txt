Implementation of the algorithm for constructing homogeneous approximations of nonlinear control systems
Grigory SKLYAR, Pavel BARKHAYEV, Svetlana IGNATOVICH, Viktor RUSAKOV

Abstract
We present a “calculator” for constructing a homogeneous approximation of nonlinear control systems, which is based on the algebraic approach developed by the authors in their previous papers. This approach mainly uses linear algebraic and combinatorial tools, so, it is perfectly adapted to computer realization. We describe the algorithm and discuss its capabilities and limitations. We present its implementation as a web application and show by example how this app works. Keywords: nonlinear control systems, homogeneous approximation, nonlinear power moments, free algebra, algorithm for constructing an approximating system.



1
Introduction

When studying properties of dynamical systems or problems of control design, the first step commonly used in mechanics and engineering is to linearize the systems under consideration. Namely, the nonlinear system is substituted by a linear one, which is nevertheless close enough to the original one, so that the results obtained for the linear system are applicable to the original problem. A brilliant example of such a method is the Lyapunov stability theory.

However, in many cases it is impossible to choose an appropriate linear system. As a simple example, let us consider a two-input driftless control system

\dot{x}=X_{1}(x)u_{1}+X_{2}(x)u_{2},

(1)

where
X_{1}(x)
and
X_{2}(x)
are smooth vector fields defined in a domain of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
. Suppose that this system is locally controllable at some point
x^{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, i.e., any point from a neighborhood
U(x^{0})
can be reached from
x(0)=x^{0}
by a piecewise continuous control. However, its linearization, which should be of the form

\dot{x}=b_{1}u_{1}+b_{2}u_{2},

where
b_{1},b_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are constant vectors, necessarily is uncontrollable. Thus, any linear system looses the most important property of the original nonlinear system, therefore, hardly can be considered as its approximation.

In such a case, one can try to use an appropriate
nonlinear
system as an approximation. It is natural to expect that an approximating system, being nonlinear, has a simpler structure than the original one. Deep and long-term studies in this direction were carried out by several researches, who analyzed different problems. But despite the diversity of approaches, in the end all these authors came to the same concept of a so-called
homogeneous approximation
. We mention several important original publications
[
6
,
4
,
35
,
10
,
1
,
3
,
36
]
; this list is undoubtedly far from being complete. The results obtained were summarized in
[
11
]
and
[
2
]
. In the above works, the differential geometry language and tools were used.

Another way to introduce a nonlinear approximation was related to the interpretation of nonlinear control systems as series of iterated integrals. This idea was originated by M. Fliess
[
7
,
9
]
, who proposed to apply Chen series

[
5
]
to control problems. The approach uses the tools of free algebras
[
24
]
to study control systems
[
8
,
18
,
16
,
14
]
.

In the mid 90s of the last century, two of the authors of the present paper, Grigory Sklyar and Svetlana Ignatovich, considered another approximation problem, namely, approximation in the sense of time optimality
[
25
,
26
]
.
They proposed a way for analysis of the time optimal problem for nonlinear systems based on algebraic methods
[
28
]
. The main goal was to generalize the moment approach, which proved to be an effective tool in the linear setting
[
19
,
20
]
. As a result, they gave an explicit construction of approximating systems in the sense of time optimality using the free algebras language. It turned out that in essence, from the algebraic point of view, the approximation in the sense of time optimality and the homogeneous approximation are similar concepts though related to different free algebras
[
27
,
30
,

]
. In general, algebraic approach proved to be very natural and allowed a deeper understanding of nonlinear systems

[

It turns out that the proposed construction, in addition to its theoretical significance, has the following advantage, which is essential for computer implementation: it mainly consists of purely linear algebraic and combinatorial steps and, therefore, promises to become a universal tool adapted to arbitrary control-affine systems. However, in all their previous papers, Grigory Sklyar and Svetlana Ignatovich did not bring the approach to practical calculations, although they perfectly understood the possibilities provided by the algorithmic nature of the method. Another author of the present paper, Pavel Barkhayev, being deeply involved in the matter, initialized this work and created the first version of the algorithm realization. When the last author, Viktor Rusakov, joined, the computer program was improved and, finally, a “calculator” of homogeneous approximations was obtained.

The main goal of the paper is to present and discuss the algorithm for constructing a homogeneous approximation based on the mentioned algebraic approach, its implementation, capabilities and limitations.

Concluding the introduction section, we illustrate possible advantages of a homogeneous approximation by a very simple example. Let us consider a stabilization problem for a rotating rigid body

\dot{x}_{1}=\alpha_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+u_{1},\ \dot{x}_{2}=\alpha_{2}x_{1}x_{3}+u_{2},\ \dot{x}_{3}=\alpha_{3}x_{1}x_{2},

(2)

where
\alpha_{3}\neq 0
. In the paper

, time-varying feedback controls were proposed, which stabilize the equilibrium
x=0

The system (

2
Algebraic approach to the homogeneous approximation problem

By definition, a homogeneous approximation is of a local character, so, we consider control systems in a neighborhood of a given point; without loss of generality we assume this point to be the origin. The algorithm we present in this paper is applicable in two situations: (i) the system is autonomous, control-linear and the starting point is fixed,

\dot{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}(x)u_{i},\ x(0)=0;

(5)

(ii) the system is non-autonomous, control-affine and the end point, which is an equilibrium of the system, is fixed,

\dot{x}=a(t,x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}(t,x)u_{i},\ a(t,0)\equiv 0,\ x(\theta)=0.

(6)

In this paper we concentrate on the second case, where we assume
m=1
for simplicity, though, the main ideas for both cases are similar. A description of the algorithm for systems (
5
) can be found in
[
32
]
.

In the following subsections, we briefly outline our approach, which was first described in
[
28
]
.

2.1
Series representation of control systems

We consider a non-autonomous single-input system of the form

\dot{x}=a(t,x)+b(t,x)u,\ \ a(t,0)\equiv 0,

(7)

where
a(t,x)
,
b(t,x)
are analytic in a neighborhood of the origin in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, and we are interested in trajectories ending at the origin, i.e.,
x(\theta)=0
, for some time moment
\theta>0
. The condition
a(t,0)\equiv 0
means that the origin is an equilibrium of the system. The set of admissible controls is defined as

u\in B^{\theta}=\{u(t)\in L_{\infty}[0,\theta]:|u(t)|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e.}\}.

(8)

Then there exists
T>0
such that for any
\theta\in(0,T)
and any
u\in B^{\theta}
the solution of the differential equation
\dot{x}=a(t,x)+b(t,x)u(t)
satisfying the end condition
{x(\theta)=0}
is well defined for
t\in[0,\theta]
. Therefore, the system (
7
) defines the operator
S_{a,b}
, which maps a pair
(\theta,u)
to the starting point,

S_{a,b}(\theta,u)=x^{0},\ \ \theta\in(0,T),\ u\in B^{\theta}.

The operator
S_{a,b}
admits the following representation in the form of a series

S_{a,b}(\theta,u)=\sum_{k\geq 1,m_{i}\geq 0}v_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}(\theta,u),

(9)

where

\begin{array}[]{l}\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}(\theta,u)=\\
\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\theta}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{\tau_{k-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\tau_{i}^{m_{i}}u(\tau_{i}))d\tau_{k}\cdots d\tau_{1}\end{array}

(10)

are
nonlinear power moments
and
v_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are constant vector coefficients. A precise formula for
v_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}
is given below, see (
20
).

Since we are interested in local analysis, we assume that
\theta
is small. Admissible controls are from the set (
8
), hence, the following natural asymptotic equivalence can be adopted for nonlinear power moments (
10
):

\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}(\theta,u)\sim\theta^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k}+k},

(11)

i.e., the number
m=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k}+k
can be considered as an
order
of the integral
\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}(\theta,u)
.

This order naturally defines the homogeneity property, which allows us to introduce a homogeneous approximation of the system (
7
) in terms of its series.

Definition 2.1

Suppose that, after some coordinate transformation in the system (
7
), the series
S_{a,b}(\theta,u)
takes the form

(S_{a,b}(\theta,u))_{k}=z_{k}(\theta,u)+\rho_{k}(\theta,u),\ k=1,\ldots,n,

(12)

where

(i)

z_{k}(\theta,u)
is a sum of integrals (
10
) of order
w_{k}
,

(ii)

\rho_{k}(\theta,u)
contains integrals (
10
) of order greater than
w_{k}
,

(iii)

the system

\dot{x}=\widehat{a}(t,x)+\widehat{b}(t,x)u,

(13)

which corresponds to the series

(S_{\widehat{a},\widehat{b}}(\theta,u))_{k}=z_{k}(\theta,u),\ k=1,\ldots,n,

is controllable.

Then (
13
) is called a homogeneous approximation of the system (
7
).

It might seem that the problem is to find a coordinate transformation taking the series to the form (
12
). However, an approximation itself is defined by
z_{k}(\theta,u)
, which are linear combinations of integrals (
10
). The elements
z_{k}(\theta,u)
can be found directly, without finding an appropriate transformation. Moreover, only basic linear algebraic and simple combinatorial tools can be used, as is shown in the next subsections.

2.2
Algebra of nonlinear power moments

It turns out that the linear span of nonlinear power moments (
10
) can be considered as a free associative algebra

{\mathcal{A}}=\mathrm{Lin}\{\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}:m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\geq 0,\ k\geq 1\},

equipped with the (formal) multiplication defined on basis elements as

\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\xi_{n_{1}\ldots n_{r}}=\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}n_{1}\ldots n_{r}}.

The series
S_{a,b}
defines the linear map
v:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by

v(\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}})=v_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}.

The asymptotic property (
11
) leads to a
canonical grading structure
in the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
defined as

{\mathcal{A}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{A}}^{m},

(14)

where

{\mathcal{A}}^{m}=\mathrm{Lin}\{\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}:m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k}+k=m\}

are the subspaces of homogeneous elements of order
m
. Below we write
\mathrm{ord}(z)=m
if
z\in{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
. We emphasize that subspaces
{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
are finite-dimensional.

The free associative algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
is closely related to the free Lie algebra generated by the elements
\xi_{m}
,
m\geq 0
, with the Lie bracket operation
[\ell_{1},\ell_{2}]=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}-\ell_{2}\ell_{1}
; we denote this free Lie algebra by
{\mathcal{L}}
. The Lie algebra
{\mathcal{L}}
is graded as well,

{\mathcal{L}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{L}}^{m},\ \ \ {\mathcal{L}}^{m}={\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{m}.

Omitting details, which can be found in

[
31
]
,
we mention two properties of the map
v
.

1) Below we assume that the system (
7
) is locally accessible, i.e., the Rashevsky-Chow condition is satisfied. This can be expressed as

v({\mathcal{L}})={\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(15)

2) The series (
9
) is “realizable”, i.e., it corresponds to a control system. In our case this requirement reduces to the following property

\mbox{if }\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}\mbox{ and }v(\ell)=0,\mbox{ then }v(\ell z)=0\mbox{ for any }z\in{\mathcal{A}}.

(16)

An analytic change of coordinates in the system (
7
) can be expressed as a transformation over the series
S_{a,b}
. In order to find a result of such a transformation, one needs to find products of integrals (
10
). The product of two integrals corresponds to a
shuffle
operation in the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, which can be defined recursively as

\xi_{m_{1}}\shuffle\xi_{n_{1}}=\xi_{m_{1}n_{1}}+\xi_{n_{1}m_{1}},

\xi_{m_{1}}\shuffle\xi_{n_{1}\ldots n_{r}}=\xi_{m_{1}n_{1}\ldots n_{r}}+\xi_{n_{1}}(\xi_{m_{1}}\shuffle\xi_{n_{2}\ldots n_{r}}),

\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\shuffle\xi_{n_{1}\ldots n_{r}}=

=\xi_{m_{1}}(\xi_{m_{2}\ldots m_{k}}\shuffle\xi_{n_{1}\ldots n_{r}})+\xi_{n_{1}}(\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\shuffle\xi_{n_{2}\ldots n_{r}}).

For example, the product

\xi_{m_{1}}(u,\theta)\cdot\xi_{n_{1}}(u,\theta)=\int_{0}^{\theta}\tau_{1}^{m_{1}}u(\tau_{1})d\tau_{1}\cdot\int_{0}^{\theta}\tau_{2}^{n_{1}}u(\tau_{2})d\tau_{2}

equals the integral of the function
\tau_{1}^{m_{1}}\tau_{2}^{n_{1}}u(\tau_{1})u(\tau_{2})
over the square
\{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}):0\leq\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\leq\theta\}
, which can be written out as a sum of two integrals of the same function over the triangles
\{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}):0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq\theta\}
and
\{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}):0\leq\tau_{2}\leq\tau_{1}\leq\theta\}
. Therefore,

\xi_{m_{1}}(\theta,u)\cdot\xi_{n_{1}}(\theta,u)=\xi_{m_{1}n_{1}}(\theta,u)+\xi_{n_{1}m_{1}}(\theta,u),

which corresponds to the shuffle product in
{\mathcal{A}}
.

2.3
Finding a series representation for a homogeneous approximation

Now let us return to Definition
2.1
. Justifying the terms “homogeneous” and “approximation”, it also gives a clue to finding a homogeneous approximation. Actually, one can start with the series
S_{a,b}
and transform it, aiming to separate the “main part” with respect to the order in
{\mathcal{A}}
. The goal is to find a set of homogeneous elements
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
such that the “series”
S=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})^{\top}
is realizable and accessible, i.e., satisfied conditions 1) and 2) mentioned above (see (
15
) and (
16
)). Desired elements can be obtained step by step; many examples can be found in

[
34
]
.

However, one can obtain elements
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
explicitly, without such a brutal force way. For a given series
S_{a,b}
of the form (
9
) or, what is the same, for the linear map
v
satisfying conditions 1) and 2), we introduce the linear subspaces

{\mathcal{P}}^{m}=\{\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}^{m}:v(\ell)\in v({\mathcal{L}}^{1}+\cdots+{\mathcal{L}}^{m-1})\},\ m\geq 1,

and denote

{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{P}}^{m}.

It can be shown that
{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b}
is a Lie subalgebra of
{\mathcal{L}}
; by definition, it is graded and has codimension
n
in
{\mathcal{L}}
. We call it
a core Lie subalgebra
of the system (
7
). Also we introduce the following graded
right ideal
generated by
{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b}
,

{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}=\mathrm{Lin}\{\ell z:\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b},z\in{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathbb{R}}\}.

Finally, we introduce the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
in the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
declaring elements
\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}
to form an orthonormal basis. Below we denote by
\widetilde{z}
the orthogonal projection of an element
z
onto the subspace
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
, i.e., onto the orthogonal complement to the subspace
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}
.

Theorem 2.2

For a given system (
7
), let us choose
n
homogeneous elements
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}\in{\mathcal{L}}
such that

\mathrm{Lin}\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}\}+{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b}={\mathcal{L}}.

Then the elements
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
from Definition
2.1
can be chosen as
z_{k}=\widetilde{\ell}_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
. Moreover, all possible sets of such elements can be obtained from the mentioned elements by polynomial transformations that preserve the order.

For the proof, examples and comments, see

[
28
]
,
[
34
]
.

Thus, the homogeneous approximation is, in essence, unique and is defined by the core Lie subalgebra
{\mathcal{L}}_{a,b}
. Moreover, finding a series
S_{\widehat{a},\widehat{b}}
for the homogeneous approximation can be carried out in a purely algebraic way.

2.4
Constructing an approximating system: general case

First, we notice that the system (
13
) is not unique. However, if
\widehat{a}(t,x)\equiv 0
, then
\widehat{b}(t,x)
is defined uniquely. We can construct components
\widehat{b}_{i}(t,x)
of the vector function
\widehat{b}(t,x)=(\widehat{b}_{1}(t,x),\ldots,\widehat{b}_{n}(t,x))^{\top}
one by one in the following way.

According to Definition
2.1
, the element
z_{k}=\widetilde{\ell}_{k}
is of order
w_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
. To start with, one can show that
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}=\alpha_{1}\xi_{w_{1}-1}
, where
\alpha_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is nonzero. Then we set
\widehat{b}_{1}(t,x)=-\alpha_{1}t^{w_{1}-1}
. Further we act by induction. For any
i=2,\ldots,n
, we write the element
z_{i}=\widetilde{\ell}_{i}
in the form

\widetilde{\ell}_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{w_{i}-2}y_{j}\xi_{j}+\alpha_{i}\xi_{w_{i}-1},\ y_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{w_{i}-j-1},\ \alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}.

One can show that
y_{j}
are shuffle polynomials of the elements
\widetilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}
. This follows form the fact that
y_{j}\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
and elements
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{\shuffle q_{1}}\shuffle\cdots\shuffle\widetilde{\ell}_{n}^{\shuffle q_{n}}
form a basis of the subspace
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
. Here and below we use the notation for the “shuffle power”:
\ell^{\shuffle q}=\ell\shuffle\cdots\shuffle\ell
(
q
times). Therefore,

y_{j}=p_{j}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}),\ 0\leq j\leq w_{i}-2,

where
p_{j}
denotes a shuffle polynomial,

p_{j}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1})=\sum\beta^{j}_{q_{1}\ldots q_{i-1}}\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{\shuffle q_{1}}\shuffle\cdots\shuffle\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}^{\shuffle q_{i-1}}

and the sum is taken over all integers
q_{1},\ldots,q_{i-1}\geq 0
such that
w_{1}q_{1}+\cdots+w_{i-1}q_{i-1}=w_{i}-j-1
. Then we set

\widehat{b}_{i}(t,x)=-\sum_{j=0}^{w_{i}-2}P_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})t^{j}-\alpha_{i}t^{w_{i}-1},

where

P_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})=\sum\beta^{j}_{q_{1}\ldots q_{i-1}}x_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots x_{i-1}^{q_{i-1}}.

So, the polynomial
P_{j}
in real variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}
is constructed as follows: for any monomial
x_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots x_{i-1}^{q_{i-1}}
, its coefficient is the same as the coefficient of the monomial
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{\shuffle q_{1}}\shuffle\cdots\shuffle\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}^{\shuffle q_{i-1}}
in the shuffle polynomial
p_{j}
.

2.5
Constructing an approximating system: autonomous case

If the initial system is autonomous, i.e., has the form

\dot{x}=a(x)+b(x)u,\ \ a(0)=0,

(17)

then a homogeneous approximation can also be chosen as an autonomous system, which is uniquely defined. It can be constructed as follows.

Let us introduce a
derivation

\varphi
in the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
defined by

\varphi(\xi_{0})=0,\ \varphi(\xi_{m})=m\xi_{m-1},\ m\geq 1.

Recall that a derivation in the algebra is a linear map satisfying the Leibniz product rule. Hence,

\varphi(\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}})=\sum_{i:m_{i}\geq 1}m_{i}\xi_{m_{1}\ldots(m_{i}-1)\ldots m_{k}}.

One can show that for the autonomous system (
17
), if
z\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
, then
\varphi(z)\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
.

Denote by
\psi
a linear map in
{\mathcal{A}}
defined as

\psi(\xi_{0})=1,\ \ \psi(\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k-1}0})=\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k-1}}

and
\psi(\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}})=0
if
m_{k}\geq 1
. Again, if
z\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
and
\mathrm{ord}(z)\geq 2
, then
\psi(z)\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
.

We notice that if
z\in{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
, then
\varphi(z)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{m-1}
and
\psi(z)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{m-1}
if
m\geq 2
.

Now we are ready to construct the autonomous homogeneous approximation

\dot{x}=\widehat{a}(x)+\widehat{b}(x)u,\ \ \widehat{a}(0)=0,

for the system (
17
). First, one can show that
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}=\alpha_{1}\xi_{0}
, where
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
; we set
\widehat{a}_{1}(x)=0
,
\widehat{b}_{1}(x)=-\alpha_{1}
. Then we act by induction. For any
i=2,\ldots,n
, we consider the elements
\varphi(\widetilde{\ell}_{i})
and
\psi(\widetilde{\ell}_{i})
; since they belong to
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
, they can be written in the form

\varphi(\widetilde{\ell}_{i})=p_{1,i}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}),\ \psi(\widetilde{\ell}_{i})=p_{2,i}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}),

where
p_{1,i}
and
p_{2,i}
are shuffle polynomials,

p_{j,i}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1})=\sum\beta^{j}_{q_{1}\ldots q_{i-1}}\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{\shuffle q_{1}}\shuffle\cdots\shuffle\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}^{\shuffle q_{i-1}},

j=1,2
, and the sums are taken over all integers
q_{1},\ldots,q_{i-1}\geq 0
such that
w_{1}q_{1}+\cdots+w_{i-1}q_{i-1}=w_{i}-1
. Then we set

\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{a}_{i}(x)=-P_{1,i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}),\\
\widehat{b}_{i}(x)=-P_{2,i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}),\end{array}

where

P_{j,i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})=\sum\beta^{j}_{q_{1}\ldots q_{i-1}}x_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots x_{i-1}^{q_{i-1}}.

Thus, the polynomial
P_{j,i}
in real variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}
is constructed so that for any monomial
x_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots x_{i-1}^{q_{i-1}}
, its coefficient is the same as the coefficient of the monomial
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{\shuffle q_{1}}\shuffle\cdots\shuffle\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}^{\shuffle q_{i-1}}
in the shuffle polynomial
p_{j,i}
.

3
The algorithm

The pipeline of the algorithm for constructing a homogeneous approximation for a given affine control system may be described naturally in a step-by-step manner. In what follows we give a detailed description of the algorithm splitted into nine steps.

We illustrate all the steps by the following example:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}_{1}=-u\cos x_{1},\\
\dot{x}_{2}=-\sin^{2}x_{1}+t^{2}u,\\
\dot{x}_{3}=2x_{1}^{2}\sin t-x_{2}u,\end{array}\right.

(18)

i.e., for
n=3
,
a(t,x)=(0,-\sin^{2}x_{1},2x_{1}^{2}\sin t)^{T}
,
b(t,x)=(-\cos x_{1},t^{2},-x_{2})^{T}
.

Step 1.
Construction of the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
.

We fix
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and for any
m=1,\ldots,N
we construct the graded subspace
{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
, i.e., we find all the sequences
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\}
of length

satisfying the condition
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k}=m-k
(admissible sequences). The corresponding nonlinear power moments
\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}
belong to
{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
.

To find these sequences, one may consider all
{(m-k+1)}
-ary numbers with no more than
k
digits and check the sum of the digits. We note that
\dim{\mathcal{A}}^{m}=2^{m-1}
.

Step 2.
Construction of the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{L}}
.

Lie elements of the form

[\xi_{m_{1}},[\ldots[\xi_{m_{k-1}},\xi_{m_{k}}]\ldots]],\quad k\geq 1,m_{i}\geq 0

(19)

are called right normed elements
[
24
,
17
]
.
It is known that the set of all right normed elements is complete in the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{L}}
and does not form a basis. However, discarding linearly dependent elements from the set of right normed elements, one can construct a basis of
{\mathcal{L}}
.

Along with the canonical grading (
14
) in
{\mathcal{A}}
, we consider the
length grading

{\mathcal{A}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{B}}^{k},

where
{\mathcal{B}}^{k}=\mathrm{Lin}\{\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}:m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\geq 0\}
. We note that every right normed element is homogeneous in the sense that it belongs to some subspace
{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
as well as to some subspace
{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
. This means that a basis of
{\mathcal{L}}
may be constructed independently in every single subspace
{{\mathcal{A}}^{m}\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{k}}
,
m,k\geq 1
.

For any
m=1,\ldots,N
(the number
N
has been chosen at Step 1) and
k\leq m
we consider admissible sequences
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\}
such that
\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{m}\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
and construct the corresponding right normed elements (
19
).

To find linearly independent elements, we consider a realization of the subspaces
{\mathcal{A}}^{m}\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
,
q=\dim({\mathcal{A}}^{m}\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{k})
. Namely, let
F_{m,k}
be a bijection from the set of all elements
\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{m}\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
to the set of unit vectors
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
, and we extend it by linearity to the mapping from
{\mathcal{A}}^{m}\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
. Further, we represent right normed elements from
{\mathcal{A}}^{m}\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{k}
as sums of
\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}
and apply
F_{m,k}
.
The obtained vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
are being checked for linear independence (adding one by one), and we discard linearly dependent vectors.

We store the basis of the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{L}}
as the set of sequences
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\}
and as the corresponding vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
.

Further we refer to the obtained set of linearly independent right normed elements as
\{g_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
, assuming that
\mathrm{ord}(g_{i})\leq\mathrm{ord}(g_{j})
as
i<j
.

and

R_{a}
,
R_{b}

\mathrm{ad}_{R_{a}}^{j}

R_{b}

\mathrm{ad}_{R_{a}}^{0}R_{b}=R_{b},\quad\mathrm{ad}_{R_{a}}^{j}R_{b}=[R_{a},\mathrm{ad}_{R_{a}}^{j-1}R_{b}],\ j\geq 1,

(20)

where

(see, e.g.,

]
).

We implement the differential operators

,

by using symbolic differentiation by

and

, and the operators

are implemented recursively. We note that the usage of recursion may be avoided, however in this case we would have to store a set of functions.

For any

and any

we apply the operators

iteratively to obtain

.

Step 5.
Finding the first (by order)
n
Lie elements
\{\ell_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
with linearly independent vector coefficients and the generating set of the right ideal
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}
.

Successively for
m=1,\ldots,N
we divide the basis Lie elements
\{g_{i}\}\subset{\mathcal{L}}^{m}
into two groups as follows.

At the beginning, both groups are empty and we put
g_{1}
to the first group if
v(g_{1})\not=0
and to the second one otherwise. In the latter case we proceed until we find
g_{i}
such that
v(g_{i})\not=0
and set it to the first group. The first group is non-empty now.

General step: suppose there are
k
(
k<n
) elements in the first group denoted by

\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k}
, and we examine the basis Lie element
g_{i}
with
\mathrm{ord}(g_{i})=m
.

If
v(g_{i})\not\in\mathrm{Lin}\{v(\ell_{1}),\ldots,v(\ell_{k})\}
, then we add
g_{i}
to the first group and denote
\ell_{k+1}=g_{i}
.

If not, then, if
v(g_{i})\in v({\mathcal{L}}^{1}+\cdots+{\mathcal{L}}^{m-1})
, we add
g_{i}
to the second group.

If not again, we find a linear combination of elements
\ell_{j},\ldots,\ell_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{m}
such that
v(g_{i}+c_{j}\ell_{j}+\cdots+c_{k}\ell_{k})\in v({\mathcal{L}}^{1}+\cdots+{\mathcal{L}}^{m-1})
and add this linear combination to the second group.

We perform this splitting until the first group consists of
n
elements and then until all
g_{i}
such that
\mathrm{ord}(g_{i})=\mathrm{ord}(\ell_{n})
are being checked.

We denote the elements of the second group by
\{d_{j}\}
; below we use them to construct the right ideal
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}
.

For our example (
18
) we get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\ell_{1}=g_{1},&\ell_{2}=g_{3},&\ell_{3}=g_{6}\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{llll}d_{1}=g_{2},&d_{2}=g_{4}+2g_{3},&d_{3}=g_{5},&d_{4}=g_{7}+6g_{6}.\end{array}

Let us explain the last step, i.e., the choice of
d_{4}
. For
g_{7}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{4}
we see that
v(g_{7})\in\mathrm{Lin}\{v(\ell_{1}),v(\ell_{2}),v(\ell_{3})\}
, however,
v(g_{7})\not\in v({\mathcal{L}}^{1}+{\mathcal{L}}^{2}+{\mathcal{L}}^{3})=\mathrm{Lin}\{v(\ell_{1}),v(\ell_{2})\}
. Then we find that
v(g_{7}+6\ell_{3})\in\mathrm{Lin}\{v(\ell_{1}),v(\ell_{2})\}
(more specifically,
v(g_{7}+6\ell_{3})=0
), therefore, we set
d_{4}=g_{7}+6\ell_{3}=g_{7}+6g_{6}
.

Step 6.
Construction of the right ideal
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}
.

For every
m\in\{\mathrm{ord}(\ell_{i}):i=1,\ldots,n\}
we construct the intersection
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
of the right ideal with the subspace of elements of order
m
.
To this end, we take every
d_{j}
such that
\mathrm{ord}(d_{j})\leq m
and construct the set

D_{j}^{m}=\{d_{j}\xi_{s_{1}\ldots s_{k}}:\;\xi_{s_{1}\ldots s_{k}}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{r},\ r=m-\mathrm{ord}(d_{j})\}

if
\mathrm{ord}(d_{j})<m
and
D_{j}^{m}=\{d_{j}\}
if
\mathrm{ord}(d_{j})=m
.
Due to construction one has

{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{m}=\mathrm{Lin}\{D_{j}^{m}:\mathrm{ord}(d_{j})\leq m\}.

Further we find the representation of the obtained elements
\{D_{j}^{m}:\mathrm{ord}(d_{j})\leq m\}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
,
q=\dim{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
, considering them as linear combinations of basis elements
\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}
. Similarly to Step 1, we fix a bijection
F_{m}
from the set of all elements
\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
to the set of unit vectors
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
,
q=\dim{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
. Then, for any element
d_{j}\xi_{s_{1}\ldots s_{k}}\in D_{j}^{m}
, we express
F_{m}(d_{j}\xi_{s_{1}\ldots s_{k}})=c_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+c_{q}e_{q}
and compose a matrix
J_{m}
with rows
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q})
.

It may happen that the rank of the matrix
J_{m}
is not full.
E.g., suppose that
\xi_{0},\xi_{1}\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}
, then necessarily
[\xi_{0},\xi_{1}]\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}
. If at the previous step we denoted
d_{1}=\xi_{0}
,
d_{2}=\xi_{1}
,
d_{3}=[\xi_{0},\xi_{1}]
, then
d_{3}\in D_{3}^{3}
is a linear combination of elements from
D_{1}^{3}
and
D_{2}^{3}
(namely, of
d_{1}\xi_{1}
and
d_{2}\xi_{0}
). In this case the row of
J_{3}
corresponding to
d_{3}
linearly depends on the rows corresponding to
d_{1}\xi_{1}
and
d_{2}\xi_{0}
.

It is crucial for the next step that
J_{m}
is of full rank, so we discard linearly dependent rows from it.

For the system (
18
),
\{\mathrm{ord}(\ell_{i}):i=1,\ldots,n\}=\{1,3,4\}
, and we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{1}&=&\{0\},\\
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{3}&=&\mathrm{Lin}\{D_{1}^{3},D_{2}^{3}\},\\
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{4}&=&\mathrm{Lin}\{D_{1}^{4},D_{2}^{4},D_{3}^{4},D_{4}^{4}\},\end{array}

where

\begin{array}[]{rcl}D_{1}^{3}&=&\{d_{1}\xi_{0}\}=\{\xi_{10}\},\\
D_{2}^{3}&=&\{d_{2}\}=\{\xi_{01}-\xi_{10}+2\xi_{2}\},\\
D_{1}^{4}&=&\{d_{1}\xi_{00},d_{1}\xi_{1}\}=\{\xi_{100},\xi_{11}\},\\
D_{2}^{4}&=&\{d_{2}\xi_{0}\}=\{\xi_{010}-\xi_{100}+2\xi_{20}\},\\
D_{3}^{4}&=&\{d_{3}\}=\{\xi_{3}\},\\
D_{4}^{4}&=&\{d_{4}\}=\{2\xi_{010}-\xi_{001}-\xi_{100}+6\xi_{02}-6\xi_{20}\}.\end{array}

For
m=3
, considering the bijection
F_{3}:{\mathcal{A}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
that maps
\{\xi_{2},\xi_{01},\xi_{10},\xi_{000}\}
to

For
m=4

Step 7.
Construction of the projections of the elements
\{\ell_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
onto the orthogonal complement to the right ideal
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}
(the main part of the series).

For every
i=1,\ldots,n
we construct the projection of
\ell_{i}
onto
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
, which we denote by
\widetilde{\ell}_{i}
.

Let
\mathrm{ord}(\ell_{i})=m
. Then, actually, we construct the projection of
\ell_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
onto
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
. Let us consider the bijection
F_{m}:{\mathcal{A}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
used at Step 6. Since
\widetilde{\ell}_{i}\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}^{\perp}
, we get
J_{m}F_{m}(\widetilde{\ell}_{i})=0
.

Let us denote
y_{i}=\ell_{i}-\widetilde{\ell}_{i}\in{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
. Then
F_{m}(y_{i})
equals a linear combination of the (transposed) rows of the matrix
J_{m}
, i.e.,
F_{m}(y_{i})=J_{m}^{T}x_{i}
for some column vector
x_{i}
. On the other hand,
J_{m}F_{m}(y_{i})=J_{m}F_{m}(\ell_{i}-\widetilde{\ell}_{i})=J_{m}F_{m}(\ell_{i})
.

Therefore, we get the following system of linear equations for
x_{i}

J_{m}J_{m}^{T}x_{i}=J_{m}F_{m}(\ell_{i}).

(21)

The matrix
J_{m}
is of full rank, hence, the system (
21
) has a unique solution. Having found it, we then calculate
F_{m}(y_{i})=J_{m}^{T}x_{i}
, i.e., express
y_{i}
as a linear combination of basis elements of
{\mathcal{J}}_{a,b}\cap{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
(collected in
D_{j}^{m}
). Then we find
\widetilde{\ell}_{i}=\ell_{i}-y_{i}
.

For the system (

) we have
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}=\ell_{1}=\xi_{0}
since

. Further, for
\ell_{2}

F_{3}
and the matrix

from the previous step, we find

as a solution of the the equation (

), which takes the form

Thus,

equals

Step 8.
Construction of the non-autonomous approximating system.

We follow the procedure described in Subsection
2.4
. First we implement recursively the shuffle product for basis elements:

\xi_{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}}\shuffle\xi_{n_{1}\ldots n_{r}}.

The result is a homogeneous sum of basis elements and we represent it as a vector in the corresponding space
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
,
q=2^{\sum m_{i}+\sum n_{j}+k+r-1}
. Then we extend by linearity the shuffle operation on arbitrary homogeneous elements of the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Further for every
i=1,\ldots,n
we construct the function
\widehat{b}_{i}(t,x)
based on the representation of
\widetilde{\ell}_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
:

\widetilde{\ell}_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}y_{j}\xi_{j}+\alpha_{i}\xi_{m-1}.

We represent each
y_{j}
as a shuffle polynomial with respect to
\widetilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}
. To find all shuffle products of
\{\widetilde{\ell}_{j}\}
of the same order
m-j-1
, we use the method similar to one described at the Step 1.

Let us apply this procedure to the system (
18
). Since
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}=\xi_{0}
, we have
\widehat{b}_{1}(t,x)=-1
. Further,

\widetilde{\ell}_{2}={\textstyle\frac{1}{5}}\xi_{2}-{\textstyle\frac{2}{5}}\xi_{0}\xi_{1}={\textstyle\frac{1}{5}}\xi_{2}-{\textstyle\frac{2}{5}}\widetilde{\ell}_{1}\xi_{1},

\widehat{b}_{2}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{5}t^{2}+\frac{2}{5}tx_{1}
.
Finally,

\widetilde{\ell}_{3}={\textstyle\frac{3}{19}}\xi_{0}\xi_{2}+{\textstyle\frac{8}{57}}\underbrace{\xi_{00}}_{=\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{\shuffle 2}}\xi_{1}+{\textstyle\frac{23}{285}}(\underbrace{\xi_{2}-2\xi_{01}}_{=5\widetilde{\ell}_{2}})\xi_{0}

Step 9.
Construction of the autonomous approximating system.

We follow the procedure described in Subsection
2.5
. To this end, we implement the linear maps
\varphi
and
\psi
in the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}

and use the shuffle product implemented at the previous step.

For every
i=1,\ldots,n
we find
\varphi(\widetilde{\ell}_{i})
,
\psi(\widetilde{\ell}_{i})

and represent them as shuffle polynomials with respect to
\widetilde{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\ell}_{i-1}
if possible. It may happen that the corresponding system for
\varphi(\widetilde{\ell}_{i})
is unsolvable, which means that there is no autonomous approximating system.
Again, to find all shuffle products of
\{\widetilde{\ell}_{j}\}
of a given order, we use the method similar to one described at Step 1.

Applying this procedure to the system (
18
), we see that the autonomous approximating system does not exist since

\varphi(\widetilde{\ell}_{2})=\frac{2}{5}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{00})
cannot be represented as a shuffle polynomial with respect to
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}=\xi_{0}
.

Let us change the system (
18
) a little bit by adding the term
-2tx_{1}
to the second equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}_{1}=-u\cos x_{1},\\
\dot{x}_{2}=-\sin^{2}x_{1}-2tx_{1}+t^{2}u,\\
\dot{x}_{3}=2x_{1}^{2}\sin t-x_{2}u.\end{array}\right.

(23)

For this system,
v(\xi_{2})=0
and

\ell_{1}=\xi_{0},\quad\ell_{2}=[\xi_{0},\xi_{1}],\quad\ell_{3}=[\xi_{0},\xi_{2}].

Applying the described steps to the system (
23
), we construct the following autonomous approximation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}_{1}=-u,\\
\dot{x}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2},\\
\dot{x}_{3}=\frac{1}{27}x_{1}^{3}-\frac{10}{9}x_{2}+\frac{4}{9}x_{2}u.\end{array}\right.

4
Implementation of the algorithm and perspectives

The described above functionality and the pipeline of the algorithm were implemented as a package and a web application by using Python programming language. Here are the links:

the web application:

https://happycontrol.pythonanywhere.com/
,

the package:

https://github.com/ViktorRusakov/happy-control
.

The web application was built with the Django framework and has the following functionality. First, one has to choose system’s dimension and type of the problem to be solved. Then one enters coefficients of the system by using LaTeX text formatting (and possibly the point in which neighborhood the system is considered). The output is a pdf file with detailed calculations similar to the ones from the example given in the previous section. In Fig.
2
we give a screenshot of the application running.

Figure 2:
Screenshot of the web application: setting a system for approximation

The functionality on which the application is built, was implemented as an open source package, so it is possible to use it both as an end-to-end solution or as particular functions.

The core packages used for calculations are SymPy and NumPy:

•

SymPy is used for symbolic calculations when constructing series representation of control system (Step 3 requires differentiation operation) and when printing out nice formatted output pdf files;

•

NumPy is used for numerical calculations: finding linearly independent Lie elements, their projections, etc. Basically all matrix operations were done with NumPy.

The most computationally demanding are Steps 7, 8 and 9 since they require solving high dimensional systems of linear equations. Since values of
\dim{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
grow as
2^{m-1}
, one can conclude that the “depth” of a system, i.e., the order of the last linearly independent Lie element
\mathrm{ord}(\ell_{n})
, determines the complexity and time of computations rather than dimension
n
of a system.

The present-day implementation of the algorithm possesses the following restrictions on the depth of systems: the graded subspaces and the basis of the Lie algebra were constructed for orders up to
15
; the web application can handle in real time systems with
{\mathrm{ord}(\ell_{n})\leq 9}
; by using the functionality of the package one can construct approximations for systems with
{\mathrm{ord}(\ell_{n})\leq 13}
on an “average” local computer in a “reasonable” time.

As possible developments of the proposed algorithm and its implementation we consider the following:

1)

optimization of the present implementation to be able to construct approximations for “deeper” systems;

2)

implementation of the algorithm for the case of multi-dimensional control (for that, optimization is even more crucial since dimensions of graded subspaces for a system with
k
controls grows as
(k+1)^{m-1}
);

3)

applying the algorithm as an effective tool for analysis of certain specific classes of systems, in particular, Goursat systems, see, e.g.,
[
23
]
.

5
Physical example: Goursat systems and their homogeneous approximations

Let us consider a driftless control system (
1
) defined by two vector fields and introduce the following notation for the “derived flag”

\begin{array}[]{l}D^{1}=\mathrm{Lin}\{X_{1},X_{2}\},\\
D^{k+1}=D^{k}+[D^{k},D^{k}],\ k\geq 1,\end{array}

where
[D^{k},D^{k}]=\mathrm{Lin}\{[Y,Z]:Y,Z\in D^{k}\}
and
[\cdot,\cdot]
stands for the Lie bracket of vector fields. By definition, (
1
) is called a Goursat system if
\dim(D^{k})=k+1
for
k=1,\ldots,n-1
. A convenient coordinate description was found in
[
21
]
: it turned out that, in appropriate local coordinates, vector fields
X_{1}(x)
and
X_{2}(x)
are polynomial and can include constant parameters.

These systems have an important application discovered by F. Jean
[
15
]
: it relates to a kinematic model of a car pulling
n
trailers. Roughly speaking, variables here are orientation angles of the car and the trailers. If the car is not perpendicular to the first trailer and none of the consecutive trailers are perpendicular to each other, then the position is non-singular. In this case, constant parameters vanish, and all non-singular positions are described by a unique system (of a chained form). Otherwise, different singularities arise; a thorough research was made in
[
15
]
.

The study of possible homogeneous approximations of Goursat systems, which describe a possible local behavior of the mentioned kinematic model, is very difficult in general; the main question here is if constant parameters mentioned above are included in a homogeneous approximation. A deep investigation was carried out by P. Mormul
[
22
,
23
]
, who performed a lot of sophisticated calculations applying very special properties of Goursat systems. Actually, Goursat systems were our main motivation in developing an implementation of the algorithm presented above.

The described above restrictions on the depth of systems for the current implementation of the algorithm applies to Goursat systems as well. One of the “deepest” system for which we were able to calculate a homogeneous approximation is GGSGSG (written in a standard coding for Goursat systems, we refer to
[
15
]
,
[
23
]
for more details): this is a system of dimension 8 and Lie brackets up to length 12 are involved. However, we believe that based on the special structure of Goursat systems, the implementation of the algorithm can be significantly optimized, and we will be able to find homogeneous approximations for Goursat systems of higher dimension.

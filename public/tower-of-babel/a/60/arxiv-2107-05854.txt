Exploring the change of semiconductor hole mass under Coulomb scattering
Shiue-Yuan Shiau, Monique Combescot

Abstract
Semiconductor valence holes are known to have heavy and light effective masses; but the consequence of this mass difference on Coulomb scatterings has been considered intractable and thus ignored up to now. The reason is that the heavy/light index is quantized along the hole momentum that changes in a Coulomb scattering; so, a heavy hole can turn light, depending on the scattering angle. This mass change has never been taken into account in many-body problems, and a single “average” hole mass has been used instead. In order to study the missed consequences of this crude approximation, the first necessary step is to determine the Coulomb scatterings with valence holes in a precise way. We here derive these scatterings from scratch, starting from the threefold valence-electron spatial level, all the way through the spin-orbit splitting, the Kohn-Luttinger effective Hamiltonian, its spherical approximation, and the phase factors that appear when turning from valence electron to hole operators, that is, all the points of semiconductor physics that render valence holes so different from a naïve positive charge.

Getting rid of the warping, which removes the crystal axes from the electron dispersion relation, is already a big simplification toward handling valence electrons. Still, these axes do not completely disappear from the problem due to Coulomb interaction. Here enters a “miracle”, that most people take as granted without well-established derivation: the Coulomb scatterings for

By contrast, the crystal axes do not appear in the heavy and light valence states because they are eigenstates of a

{\bf k}

The fact that the Coulomb interaction is not diagonal between valence electron eigenstates has a dramatic consequence on many-body effects involving these electrons: because the electron wave vector
{\bf k}
changes in a Coulomb process, so changes the valence electron state along with its mass. The heavy-light hole mass difference has been deemed intractable in many-body problems. This is why, up to now, all many-body effects in bulk semiconductors have been derived by adopting

;
MC1972b
;

Up to now, there is no satisfactory treatment to this mass problem. Note that the situation is different in quantum wells because the heavy-hole and light-hole energies are split by confinement

Our motivation for deriving the Coulomb scatterings between heavy and light holes is to reconsider the validity of using a single hole mass in many-body problems, and to provide some mathematical support to the widely-used average hole mass value. The validity of using a single mass most probably depends on the problem at hand. A first question surely is related to the exciton

The very first step to investigate the consequences of using an average hole mass in bulk many-body effects—which still is an open problem—is to control how a heavy valence electron stays heavy or turns light under Coulomb scatterings. This requires to go back to the microscopic form of the Coulomb interaction in semiconductors

\displaystyle 1-\frac{3}{4}\sin^{2}\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}=P_{HH}=1-P_{LH}

\displaystyle=P_{LL}=1-P_{HL}\hskip 2.84544pt

(1)

Unfortunately, the microscopic scatterings between heavy and light states, which are the relevant quantities to derive many-body effects, are not as simple as these probabilities.

Before going further, we wish to comment on using notations borrowed from atomic physics when dealing with semiconductors. Because semiconductor electrons are in a periodic potential, they do not have the orbital angular momentum
\hat{{\bf L}}={\bf r}\times\hat{{\bf p}}
specific to systems with spherical symmetry
VAKu
, with state parity depending on orbital quantum number
\ell
as
(-1)^{\ell}
. A naïve extension of atomic notations to periodic systems can lead to incorrect results, as for example seen from the fact that the lowest spatial conduction level in a cubic semiconductor is called S, while all conduction levels are odd in parity; in the same way, the highest spatial valence level is called P, while all valence levels are even. So, atomic notations are valid to label level degeneracy—here nondegenerate and threefold—but are not valid for state parity, which plays a crucial role in many electronic transition problems.

Actually, it is possible to define a vector operator
\hat{\mathbfcal{L}}
from the potential
\mathcal{V}({\bf r})
felt by the electrons,

\nabla\mathcal{V}({\bf r})\times\hat{{\bf p}}\equiv\tilde{\lambda}\,\hat{\mathbfcal{L}}

(2)

that reduces to
\hat{{\bf L}}
when
\mathcal{V}({\bf r})
depends on
|{\bf r}|
only, as for spherical symmetry, but differs from
\hat{{\bf L}}
otherwise. For cubic symmetry, the commutation relations of the
\hat{\mathbfcal{L}}
components are the same as those for
(\hat{L}_{x},\hat{L}_{y},\hat{L}_{z})
, provided we align the arbitrary Cartesian axes
({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})
of the spherical system along the cubic crystal axes. When spin is introduced, the matrix representations of the resulting
\hat{\mathbfcal{J}}=\hat{\mathbfcal{L}}+\hat{{\bf S}}
operator are identical to those of the
\hat{{\bf J}}=\hat{{\bf L}}+\hat{{\bf S}}
operator, provided we again align the axes for
(\hat{J}_{x},\hat{J}_{y},\hat{J}_{z})
along the cubic crystal axes.

Because of identical commutation relations for
(\hat{\mathbfcal{L}},\hat{{\bf L}})
or
(\hat{\mathbfcal{J}},\hat{{\bf J}})
components, we could be led to call angular momentum
\hat{\mathbfcal{J}}
as well as
\hat{\mathbfcal{L}}
. However, being aware of the lack of spherical symmetry that
\hat{\mathbfcal{L}}
has and the aforementioned parity problem, we here prefer to securely derive all results from scratch, and only at the end draw connections with the angular momentum formalism. This is the price to pay for not using group theory, as commonly done in semiconductor physics.

II
Coulomb scatterings between heavy and light electrons

II.1
Without spin-orbit coupling

In the absence of spin-orbit interaction, the kinetic part of the valence electron Hamiltonian reads

\sum_{\bf k}\sum_{\mu=(x,y,z)}\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}\varepsilon_{v,{\bf k}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mu,s}\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k};\mu,s}

(39)

where
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mu,s}
creates a valence electron with wave vector
{\bf k}
and spin
s
in one of the three spatial states
\mu
, the wave function of this electron,
\langle{\bf r}|v,{\bf k};\mu\rangle
, being given in Eq. (
3
).

The repulsive Coulomb interaction between valence and conduction electrons reads in terms of their creation operators as

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{cv}=\sum_{{\bf q}\not=\bf 0}v_{\bf q}\sum_{{\bf k}_{1}{\bf k}_{2}}\,\,\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}\,\,\sum_{\mu_{2}=(x,y,z)}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(40)

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{c,{\bf k}_{1}+{\bf q};s_{1}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k}_{2}-{\bf q};\mu_{2},s_{2}}\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k}_{2};\mu_{2},s_{2}}\hat{a}_{c,{\bf k}_{1};s_{1}}

with
v_{\bf q}\simeq 4\pi e^{2}/L^{3}\epsilon_{sc}q^{2}
in the small wave-vector transfer limit. Note that the electrons keep their quantum indices,
s
or
(\mu,s)
, in this direct Coulomb process.

II.2
With spin-orbit coupling

The problem is more complicated when the spin-orbit interaction is included because the spin-orbit eigenstates
|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{\bf k}
depend on the
{\bf k}
direction. This fundamentally means that when the
{\bf k}
wave vector of the electron changes, as in a Coulomb process, its eigenstates also change.

The kinetic part of the valence electron Hamiltonian in the spherical approximation,
\hat{H}^{(\rm sph)}_{\bf k}
, is diagonal in its eigenstate basis
|v,{\bf k}\rangle\otimes|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{\bf k}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\bf k}\sum_{\mathcal{J}=\pm 3/2}\varepsilon^{(H)}_{v,k}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}}\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(41)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bf k}\sum_{\mathcal{J}=\pm 1/2}\varepsilon^{(L)}_{v,k}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}}\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}}

where
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}}
creates a valence electron with wave vector
{\bf k}
in the
\mathcal{J}=\pm 3/2
spin-orbit level for heavy electrons and in the
\mathcal{J}=\pm 1/2
level for light electrons,

|v,{\bf k}\rangle\otimes|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{\bf k}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}}|vac\rangle

(42)

where
|vac\rangle
denotes the vacuum state.

By contrast, the Coulomb part is not diagonal in the
|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{\bf k}
basis: a heavy valence electron can scatter into a heavy-mass state but also into a light-mass state. To derive these scatterings in a secure way, let us go step by step from the operator
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mu,s}
that appears in Eq. (
40
), to the succession of operators
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{L}_{z},s_{z}}
,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{z}}
, and ultimately to
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}}
that appears in Eq. (
41
).

From Eq. (
8
) that leads to

\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\pm 1_{z}}=\frac{\mp i\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{x}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{y}}{\sqrt{2}}\qquad\qquad\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{0_{z}}=i\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{z}

(43)

we can check that

\sum_{\mathcal{L}=(\pm 1,0)}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{L}_{z}}\hat{a}_{\mathcal{L}_{z}}=\sum_{\mu=(x,y,z)}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\hat{a}_{\mu}

(44)

Next, we introduce the spin and use Eq. (
6
) to write the creation operators for spin-orbit eigenstates as

\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\pm\frac{3}{2}_{z}}&=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\pm 1_{z};\pm\frac{1}{2}_{z}}\\
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\pm\frac{1}{2}_{z}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\pm 1_{z};\mp\frac{1}{2}_{z}}+\sqrt{2}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{0_{z};\pm\frac{1}{2}_{z}}\right)\\
\hat{a}^{\prime\dagger}_{\pm\frac{1}{2}_{z}}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\sqrt{2}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\pm 1_{z};\mp\frac{1}{2}_{z}}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{0_{z};\pm\frac{1}{2}_{z}}\right)

(45)

These relations fulfill

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{J}=(\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2)}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{J}_{z}}\hat{a}_{\mathcal{J}_{z}}+\sum_{\mathcal{J}=\pm 1/2}\hat{a}^{\prime\dagger}_{\mathcal{J}_{z}}\hat{a}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{z}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(46)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{L}=(\pm 1,0)}\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{L}_{z},s_{z}}\hat{a}_{\mathcal{L}_{z},s_{z}}

In the following, we will drop the split-off twofold subband with creation operators
\hat{a}^{\prime\dagger}_{\mathcal{J}_{z}}
and concentrate on the fourfold level with creation operators
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{J}_{z}}
for
\mathcal{J}=(\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2)
(see Fig.

Using the above relation, we can rewrite the
\hat{V}_{cv}
Coulomb interaction between conduction and valence electrons given in Eq. (
40

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{cv}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf k}_{1}{\bf k}_{2}}\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}\not={\bf k}_{2}}v_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}-{\bf k}_{2}}\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{J}=(\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2)}\!\!\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{c,{\bf k}_{1}+{\bf k}_{2}-{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2};s_{z}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2};\mathcal{J}_{z}}\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k}_{2};\mathcal{J}_{z}}\hat{a}_{c,{\bf k}_{1};s_{z}}

|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{z}
spin-orbit basis, but these states do not have a well-defined kinetic energy within the spherical Hamiltonian,
\hat{H}_{\bf k}^{(\rm sph)}

By noting that

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{z}}|vac\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|v,{\bf k}\rangle\otimes|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{z}

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|v,{\bf k}\rangle\otimes\!\!\!\sum_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}=(\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2)}\!\!\!|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\rangle_{\bf k}\,\,{}_{\bf k}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}=(\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2)}\!\!\!{}_{\bf k}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{z}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{\bf k}}|vac\rangle

to
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}}
, through

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{J}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k}^{\prime};\mathcal{J}_{z}}\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}_{z}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(49)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k}^{\prime};\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k};\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}_{{\bf k}}}\sum_{\mathcal{J}}{}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{z}\,\,{}_{z}\langle\mathcal{J}|\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{{\bf k}}

with the
\mathcal{J}
sum reducing to the scalar product
{}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{{\bf k}}
given in Eq. (
25
). So, the
\hat{V}_{cv}
interaction ends by reading in terms of heavy and light valence electrons as

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{cv}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf k}_{1}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{2}}\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}\not={\bf k}_{2}}v_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}-{\bf k}_{2}}

(50)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}\,\,\,\,\sum_{(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{J}^{\prime})=(\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2)}{}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{{\bf k}_{2}}

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{c;{\bf k}_{1}+{\bf k}_{2}-{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2};s_{z}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{v,{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2};\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}}}\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k}_{2};\mathcal{J}_{{\bf k}_{2}}}\hat{a}_{c;{\bf k}_{1};s_{z}}

A few points about this interaction are worth noting:

— For
{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}
parallel to
{\bf k}_{2}
, the scalar product

is equal to

— For
{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}

{\bf k}_{2}

III
From valence electrons to holes

The last step is to turn from valence electrons to holes. Because tricky phase factors appear due to level degeneracy, let us repeat the procedure all over again.

III.1
Procedure

The change from valence electron destruction operator to hole creation operator in a cubic crystal is based on two fundamental relations in the spatial and spin subspaces, namely

\hat{a}_{v,{\bf k};\mu}=\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k},\mu}\qquad\qquad\hat{a}_{\pm\frac{1}{2}}=\pm\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\mp\frac{1}{2}}

(51)

Equation (
43
) then readily gives

\hat{a}_{\pm 1_{z}}=\frac{\pm i\hat{a}_{x}+\hat{a}_{y}}{\sqrt{2}}=\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\mp 1_{z}}\qquad\qquad\hat{a}_{0_{z}}=-\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{0_{z}}

(52)

When used into Eq. (
6
), we obtain

\hat{a}_{\pm\frac{3}{2}_{z}}=\hat{a}_{\pm 1_{z},\pm\frac{1}{2}_{z}}=\pm\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\mp 1_{z},\mp\frac{1}{2}_{z}}=\pm\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\mp\frac{3}{2}_{z}}

(53)

In the same way,

\hat{a}_{\pm\frac{1}{2}_{z}}=\mp\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\mp\frac{1}{2}_{z}}\qquad\hat{a}^{\prime}_{\pm\frac{1}{2}_{z}}=\mp\hat{b}^{\prime\dagger}_{\mp\frac{1}{2}_{z}}

(54)

III.2
Coulomb potential in terms of holes

Since the Coulomb interaction between conduction and valence electrons, as given in Eq. (
47
), is diagonal in the
\mathcal{J}_{z}
index, the phase factors that would appear in Eq. (
50
) cancel. As
\hat{b}_{-{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}_{2}}=-\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}_{2}}\hat{b}_{-{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}}
since
{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}\not={\bf k}_{2}
, we find that this interaction, when written in terms of electrons and holes, becomes
footnote3

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{cv}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{{\bf k}_{1}{\bf k}_{2}}\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}\not={\bf k}_{2}}v_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}-{\bf k}_{2}}

(55)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}\,\sum_{\mathcal{J}=(\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2)}\!\!\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}_{1}+{\bf k}_{2}-{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2},s}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2},\mathcal{J}_{z}}\hat{b}_{{\bf k}_{2},\mathcal{J}_{z}}\hat{a}_{{\bf k}_{1},s}

The last step is to turn to heavy and light holes, following the procedure we have used for heavy and light valence electrons, that is,

\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k},\mathcal{J}_{z}}=\sum_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\,\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k},\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{\bf k}}\,\,{}_{\bf k}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{z}

(56)

By summing over
\mathcal{J}
, we end with the Coulomb interaction between electron and heavy or light hole reading

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{eh}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{{\bf k}_{1}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{2}}\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}\not={\bf k}_{2}}v_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}-{\bf k}_{2}}

(57)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}\,\,\sum_{(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{J}^{\prime})=(\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2)}{}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{{\bf k}_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}_{1}+{\bf k}_{2}-{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2},s}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2},\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}_{2}}}\hat{b}_{{\bf k}_{2},\mathcal{J}_{{\bf k}_{2}}}\hat{a}_{{\bf k}_{1},s}

This interaction is visualized in the diagram of Fig.
2
. The minus sign evidences that it is attractive, as expected between electrons and holes, but not diagonal between heavy and light holes, except for processes in which
{\bf k}^{\prime}
is parallel or antiparallel to
{\bf k}
.

IV
Probabilities from heavy to light holes

The above equation (
57
) shows that a heavy hole
\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}=3\eta/2
can keep its spin-orbit index or scatter into the other heavy hole state
\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}=-3\eta/2
. It can also scatter into one of the two light hole states
\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}=\pm 1/2
. As mentioned below Eq. (
38
), the squared modulus of the overlap
{}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{{\bf k}}
can be physically understood as the probability to go from
|\mathcal{J}\rangle_{{\bf k}}
to
|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\rangle_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}
. The probability for the
\mathcal{J}_{\bf k}=3\eta/2
hole to stay heavy thus reads

\displaystyle P_{HH}{=}\sum_{\eta^{\prime}=\pm 1}\big|{}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\langle 3\eta^{\prime}/2|3\eta/2\rangle_{{\bf k}}\big|^{2}{=}\cos^{6}\frac{\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}}{2}+\sin^{6}\frac{\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}}{2}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{3}{4}\sin^{2}\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}\hskip 139.41832pt

(58)

with
\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}
being the
({\bf k}^{\prime},{\bf k})
angle, while the probability for the
3\eta/2
heavy hole to turn light is

\displaystyle P_{LH}=\sum_{\eta^{\prime}=\pm 1}\big|{}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\langle\eta^{\prime}/2|3\eta/2\rangle_{{\bf k}}\big|^{2}\hskip 85.35826pt

\displaystyle=3\cos^{4}\frac{\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}}{2}+3\sin^{4}\frac{\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}}{2}\cos^{2}\frac{\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\sin^{2}\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}{\bf k}}\hskip 139.41832pt

(59)

In the same way, the probability for the
\eta/2

V
Conclusion

In view of the notorious complexity of many-body effects in semiconductor physics, it has been common practice to start with an electron-hole Hamiltonian that is free from the crystal axes (spherical approximation), and to moreover take a single hole mass. These approximations are the ones that lead to exciton looking very much like a hydrogen atom and that renders many-body effects in semiconductors possible to handle.

The existence of a hole mass difference has a dramatic consequence on Coulomb scatterings because these scatterings are not diagonal with respect to heavy and light holes. As a direct consequence, a heavy hole can turn light or vice versa, depending on the deflection angle in the scattering process at hand. In the present work, we give a detailed derivation of
the microscopic Coulomb scatterings between an electron and a heavy or light hole. This groundwork is the necessary first step toward exploring new many-body effects resulting from the difference in heavy and light hole masses. We expect the Coulomb-mediated channel allowing heavy-light hole transition to have an impact on the hole population distributions, the formation of mixed heavy-light-hole excitons, the dephasing in quantum beat phenomena

From the breaking working of Anderson

4

The operator

\displaystyle\ C(d,r_{1})L_{k}^{-\frac{r_{1}}{20}+2d}|\psi(n_{\star})|+C(d,r_{1},r)L_{k}^{-\frac{r_{1}}{20}-\frac{r}{2}+\frac{3}{2}d}|\psi(n_{\star})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C(d,r_{1},r)L_{k}^{-\frac{r_{1}}{20}+2d}|\psi(n_{\star})|.

Recalling (
3.2
)-(
3.4
), one has that

-\frac{r}{2}+\tau^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}s_{0}+\frac{3}{2}d<-\frac{r}{25},\quad-\frac{r_{1}}{20}+2d<-\frac{r}{25}.

Hence, for large enough
k
(depending on
d
and
r
),

|\psi(n_{\star})|\leq L_{k}^{-\frac{r}{30}}|\psi(n_{\star})|<|\psi(n_{\star})|,

which is impossible. Therefore
\Lambda_{L_{k}}(n_{\star})
is
(E,1/2)
-bad for all
k\geq k_{1}(d,r)
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Now we can give the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
2.6
,
\mathrm{H}_{\omega}
has power-law localization for
\mathbb{P}
almost all
\omega\in\Omega
. This means that there exists
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega
,
\mu(\Omega_{1})=1
so that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
,
\sigma_{c}(\mathrm{H}_{\omega})=\emptyset
and for all eigenvalues
E_{j,\omega}
, the corresponding eigenfunction
\psi_{j,\omega}
is
\ell^{2}
and satisfies (
2.6
) with
\|\psi_{j,\omega}\|=1
.

Our ultimate goal is to control the constant
C_{j,\omega}
in (
2.6
). More precisely, for each
0<\gamma\leq r/400
, we try to show that
n_{j,\omega}
can be chosen so that
C_{j,\omega}
grows slower than
|n_{j,\omega}|^{\epsilon^{\prime}\gamma}
for
1/3\leq\epsilon^{\prime}<1/2
.

The outline of the proof is a little complicated, for the convenience of reader, we will divide the main proof into three parts. Firstly, we fix
L_{0}=\underline{L}_{0}
,
\lambda_{0},\ \eta
and
I=[E_{0}-\eta,E_{0}+\eta]
in Theorem
2.5
. Recalling Theorem
2.5
, we have for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\ \exists}\ E\in I,\ {\rm s.t.\ both}\ \Lambda_{L_{k}}(m)\ {\rm and}\ \Lambda_{L_{k}}(n)\ {\rm are\ }(E,\delta)\text{-{\rm{bad}}})\leq L_{k}^{-2p}

for all
|m-n|>2L_{k}
, where
L_{k+1}=[L_{k}^{\alpha}]
and
L_{0}\gg 1
.

Step one
.
For any
k\geq 0
, we define the set

A_{k+1}(n_{0})=\Lambda_{2L_{k+1}}(n_{0})\setminus\Lambda_{L_{k}}(n_{0})

and the event:

(3.5)

{\mathbf{E}}_{k}(n_{0})=\{\exists E,\ \exists n\in A_{k+1}(n_{0}),\text{ s.t.\ both\ }\Lambda_{L_{k}}(n_{0})\text{ and }\Lambda_{L_{k}}(n)\text{ are }(E,1/2)\text{-{bad}}\}.

From Theorem
2.5
,

\mathbb{P}({\mathbf{E}}_{k}(n_{0}))\leq\sum_{n\in A_{k+1}(n_{0})}L_{k}^{-2p}\leq C(d)(4L_{k}^{\alpha}+1)^{d}L_{k}^{-2p}\leq C(d)L_{k}^{-2p+\alpha d}.

For
1/3\leq\epsilon^{\prime}<1/2
, we define

F_{k}=\bigcup_{|n_{0}|\leq L_{k+1}^{1/\epsilon^{\prime}}}\mathbf{E}_{k}(n_{0}).

Then

\mathbb{P}(F_{k})\leq\sum_{|n_{0}|\leq L_{k+1}^{1/\epsilon^{\prime}}}\mathbb{P}(E_{k}(n_{0}))\leq C(d,\epsilon^{\prime})L_{k}^{-2p+\alpha d(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\prime}})},

where
p
and
\alpha
are defined in (
3.2
). It is easy to verified that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(F_{k})<\infty
. Then, the Borel-Cantelli lemma implies that

\mathbb{P}\left(\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\bigcup_{k\geq m}F_{k}\right)=0,

so that the set

\Omega_{2}=\{\omega\in\Omega_{1}:\ \exists\tilde{k}_{1}=\tilde{k}_{1}(p,d,\epsilon^{\prime}),\text{ s.t. }\forall k\geq\tilde{k}_{1},\ \omega\notin F_{k}\}

has full measure.

Now pick
\omega\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
, which will be kept fixed throughout the rest of the proof. Let
\psi_{j,\omega}
be the eigenfunction of energy
E_{j,\omega}
, and
n_{j,\omega}
be a point where
|\psi_{j,\omega}(n_{j,\omega})|
is maximal. Note that such a point exists, since
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
and
\psi_{j,\omega}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Furthermore, we define the integers

(3.6)

\hat{k}_{2}(\epsilon^{\prime},m)=\min\{k\geq 0\text{ such that }|m|^{\epsilon^{\prime}}<L_{k+1}\},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

and

(3.7)

\bar{k}_{2}=\bar{k}_{2}(p,d,\epsilon^{\prime},n_{j,\omega})=\max\{\tilde{k}_{1},\hat{k}_{2}(\epsilon^{\prime},n_{j,\omega})\}.

For any
k\geq\bar{k}_{2}
, we see that
\omega\notin E_{k}(n_{j,\omega})
from the definition of
F_{k}
. By (
3.5
),
\forall k\geq\bar{k}_{2}
and
\forall n\in A_{k+1}(n_{j,\omega})
, either
\Lambda_{L_{k}}(n_{j,\omega})
or
\Lambda_{L_{k}}(n)
is
(E_{j,\omega},1/2)
-good. Applying Lemma
3.2
, there is an integer

k_{2}=\max\{k_{1},\bar{k}_{2}\}=\max\{k_{1},\tilde{k}_{2},\hat{k}_{2}\},

where
k_{1}={k}_{1}(d,r)
and
\tilde{k}_{2}:=\tilde{k}_{2}(p,d,r,\epsilon^{\prime})
does not depend on
j
, such that for any energy
E_{j,\omega}
,

\Lambda_{L_{k}}(n)\text{ is }(E_{j,\omega},1/2)\text{-good},\quad\forall n\in A_{k+1}(n_{j,\omega}),\quad\forall k\geq k_{2}.

Step two
. Let us apply the Possion’s identity (
2.5
) at the point
n\in A_{k+1}(n_{j,\omega})
. Similarly to the proof of Lemma
3.2
, and recalling (
3.2
)-(
3.4
), for any
k\geq k_{2}
, one has that

\displaystyle|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{L_{k}(n)},\atop n^{\prime\prime}\notin\Lambda_{L_{k}(n)}}|G_{\Lambda_{L_{k}}(n)}(E_{j,\omega})(n,n^{\prime})|\cdot|n^{\prime}-n^{\prime\prime}|^{-r}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n^{\prime\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq C(r,d,s_{0})L_{k}^{-\frac{r}{2}+\tau^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}s_{0}+\frac{3d}{2}}+C(d,r,r_{1})L_{k}^{-\frac{r_{1}}{20}+2d}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,r)L_{k}^{-\frac{r}{25}}.

Set

(3.8)

\widetilde{A}_{k+1}(n)=\Lambda_{\frac{8}{5}L_{k+1}}(n)\setminus\Lambda_{\frac{4}{3}L_{k}}(n)\subset A_{k+1}(n).

If
n\in\widetilde{A}_{k+1}(n_{j,\omega})
, one has that
L_{k}\geq(\frac{5}{8}|n-n_{j,\omega}|)^{\frac{1}{6}}
, and

|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq C(d,r)|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\frac{r}{150}}.

Hence, one can find
k_{3}=\max\{\tilde{k}_{3},k_{2}\}
, where
\tilde{k}_{3}=\tilde{k}_{3}(d,r)
is independent of
j
, such that

|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\frac{r}{160}},\qquad\forall k\geq{k}_{3}.

Then for any
0<\gamma\leq r/160
,

|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma},\quad\forall n\in\widetilde{A}_{k+1}(n_{j,\omega}),\quad\forall k\geq k_{3}.

Since
\bigcup\limits_{k\geq k_{3}}\widetilde{A}_{k+1}(n_{j,\omega})=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ |n-n_{j,\omega}|>\frac{4}{3}L_{k_{3}}\}
, there exists
k_{4}=\max\{\tilde{k}_{4},k_{3}\}
, where
\tilde{k}_{4}
does not depend on
j
, such that

|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma},\qquad\forall n:|n-n_{j,\omega}|\geq L_{k_{4}}.

Step three
. Using the fact that
|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, one has

(3.9)

|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq C(\epsilon^{\prime},\gamma)L_{k_{4}}^{\gamma}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma},\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Now, we try to control the
j
-dependence of the constant
L_{k_{4}}^{\gamma}
. Note that the only
j
-dependence of
k_{4}
comes from
\hat{k}_{2}(\epsilon^{\prime},n_{j,\omega})
. Suppose
\sup\{|n_{j,\omega}|\}<\infty
, then
k_{4}
can be chosen
j
-independently, so that we actually obtain a uniform localization for the all energy state

|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq C(\epsilon^{\prime},\gamma)|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

But the following Lemma
3.3
contradicts this first possibility. So, in fact,
\sup\{|n_{j,\omega}|\}=\infty
, and for
j
sufficiently large, one has

k_{4}=\hat{k}_{2}(\epsilon^{\prime},n_{j,\omega}),

and recalling the definition of
\hat{k}_{2}(\epsilon^{\prime},n_{j,\omega})
in (
3.6
),

L_{k_{4}}\leq|n_{j,\omega}|^{\epsilon^{\prime}}.

Inserting this in (
3.9
) yields the announced result. Theorem
3.1
is proved.
∎

We also need to control the growth of
|n_{j,\omega}|
with
j
, which is given by the following preliminary lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
n_{j,\omega}
are defined in Theorem
3.1
. Then one can order
|n_{j,\omega}|
in increasing order such that for
j
larger enough,

|n_{j,\omega}|\geq C|j|^{\frac{1}{d}}.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.1
,
\{\psi_{j,\omega}:j=1,2,\cdots\}
is a complete normalized orthogonal system of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and each
n_{j,\omega}
is chosen so that
|\psi_{j,\omega}(n_{j,\omega})|=\sup\{|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
.
Therefore we have

(3.10)

\ \ \ \ \ \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}=1,\qquad\forall j=1,2,\cdots,

(3.11)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}=1,\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Fix
0<\varepsilon<1
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, by using Theorem
3.1
and (
2.7
), one has that

\displaystyle\sum_{|n-n_{j,\omega}|\geq\varepsilon L}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\epsilon^{\prime},\gamma)\sum_{|n-n_{j,\omega}|\geq\varepsilon L}|n_{j,\omega}|^{2\epsilon^{\prime}\gamma}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,\epsilon^{\prime},\gamma)\varepsilon^{-\gamma+\frac{d}{2}}|n_{j,\omega}|^{2\epsilon^{\prime}\gamma}L^{-\gamma+\frac{d}{2}}.

Assume
|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L
. Then

\sum_{|n|\geq(1+\varepsilon)L}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}\leq\sum_{|n-n_{j,\omega}|\geq\varepsilon L}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}\leq C(d,\epsilon^{\prime},\gamma)\varepsilon^{-\gamma+\frac{d}{2}}L^{-\gamma+2\epsilon^{\prime}\gamma+\frac{d}{2}}.

From (
3.11
),

\displaystyle(2(1+\varepsilon)L+1)^{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}\atop|n|\leq(1+\varepsilon)L}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}\geq\sum_{j:|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L\atop|n|\leq(1+\varepsilon)L}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\#\{j:|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L\}\min_{j:|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L}\sum_{|n|\leq(1+\varepsilon)L}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\#\{j:|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L\}\left(1-\max_{j:|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L}\sum_{|n|\geq(1+\varepsilon)L}|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\#\{j:|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L\}(1-C(d,\epsilon^{\prime},\gamma)\varepsilon^{-\gamma+\frac{d}{2}}L^{-\gamma+2\epsilon^{\prime}\gamma+\frac{d}{2}}).

Choose
\varepsilon=1/2
,
\epsilon^{\prime}=1/3
and
\gamma=r/160
. Since
r\geq 331d
, we have
-\gamma+2\epsilon^{\prime}\gamma+\frac{d}{2}\leq-\frac{3}{16}d
. Then there exists
L_{0}=L_{0}(d,r)
large enough, such that

(3.12)

\#\{j:|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L\}\leq C(d)L^{d},\quad\forall L\geq L_{0},

where
C(d)
is independent of
L
and
j
. This tells us that
L\geq L_{0}
,
N(L)=\#\{j:|n_{j,\omega}|\leq L\}
is finite and we can reorder the eigenfunctions so
|n_{j,\omega}|
is increasing. Therefore, one has

|n_{j,\omega}|\geq c(d)j^{\frac{1}{d}},

for
j
large enough (depending on
d
and
r
).
∎

Finally, we can give the complete proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\phi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
be such that, for some constant
C_{\phi}>0
and
\theta\geq r/160
,
|\phi(n)|\leq C_{\phi}|n|^{-\theta}
. We have to bound
\|X^{q/2}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{H}_{\omega}t}\phi\|
, for some
q>0
and all
t>0
. Since
e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{H}_{\omega}t}\phi=\sum_{j}e^{-iE_{j,\omega}t}\langle\phi,\ \psi_{j,\omega}\rangle\psi_{j,\omega}
and
\|e^{-i\mathrm{H}_{\omega}t}\phi\|=\|\phi\|
, one has that

\displaystyle\|X^{q/2}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{H}_{\omega}t}\phi\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle X^{q}e^{-\mathrm{iH}_{\omega}t}\phi,e^{-\mathrm{iH}_{\omega}t}\phi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}|\langle\phi,\psi_{j,\omega}\rangle|\ |\langle X^{q}\psi_{j,\omega},\ e^{-\mathrm{iH}_{\omega}t}\phi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi\|\sum_{j}|\langle\phi,\ \psi_{j,\omega}\rangle|\|X^{q}\psi_{j,\omega}\|.

From Theorem
3.1
, we can choose
\epsilon^{\prime}=1/3
. Then there exists a constant
C(\gamma)>0
such that

(3.13)

|\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq C(\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{\frac{\gamma}{3}}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma},\quad\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

One has that

\|X^{q}\psi_{j,\omega}\|^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n^{q}\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|^{2}\leq C(\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{\frac{2\gamma}{3}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n|^{2q}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-2\gamma},

where

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|n|^{2q}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|n-n_{j,\omega}|\geq 2|n_{j,\omega}|}|n|^{2q}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-2\gamma}+\sum_{|n-n_{j,\omega}|<2|n_{j,\omega}|}|n|^{2q}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,q,\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma+q+\frac{d}{2}}+C(d,q)|n_{j,\omega}|^{2q+d}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,q,\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{2q+d}.

Therefore

\|X^{q}\psi_{j,\omega}\|\leq C(d,r,q,\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{q+\frac{\gamma}{3}+\frac{d}{2}}.

Moreover, from the assumption of
\phi
, we have

|\langle\phi,\psi_{j,\omega}\rangle|\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\phi(n)||\psi_{j,\omega}(n)|\leq C_{\phi}(\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{\frac{\gamma}{3}}\sum_{n}|n|^{-\theta}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma}

where

\displaystyle\sum_{n}|n|^{-\theta}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|n-n_{j,\omega}|<|n_{j,\omega}|/2}|n|^{-\theta}+\sum_{|n_{j,\omega}|\leq|n-n_{j,\omega}|\leq 2|n_{j,\omega}|}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle+\sum_{|n-n_{j,\omega}|\geq 2|n_{j,\omega}|}|n|^{-\theta}|n-n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,\theta)|n_{j,\omega}|^{-\theta+d}+C(d,\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma+d}+C(d,\theta,\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{-\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{d}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,\theta,\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{-\gamma+d}.

Therefore

|\langle\phi,\psi_{j,\omega}\rangle|\leq C_{\phi}(d,\theta,\gamma)|n_{j,\omega}|^{-\frac{2\gamma}{3}+d}.

Choose
0<q\leq\gamma/10
and
\gamma=r/160
. Since
r\geq 1800d
, we have
-\frac{\gamma}{3}+q+\frac{3d}{2}<-\frac{11d}{10}
. Recalling Lemma
3.3
, one has that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}|\langle\phi,\ \psi_{j,\omega}\rangle|\|X^{q}\psi_{j,\omega}\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}(d,r,q,\theta)\sum_{j}|n_{j,\omega}|^{-\frac{\gamma}{3}+q+\frac{3d}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}(d,r,q,\theta)\sum_{j}|n_{j,\omega}|^{-\frac{11d}{10}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\phi}(d,r,q,\theta)\sum_{j}|j|^{-\frac{11}{10}}\leq C_{\phi}(d,r,q,\theta).

Therefore

\|X^{q/2}e^{-\mathrm{iH}_{\omega}t}\phi\|^{2}\leq C_{\phi}(d,r,q,\theta),\quad\forall t\geq 0.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is finished.
∎

Torsion in Griffiths Groups
Theodosis Alexandrou

Abstract
. We show that for any integer n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 there is a smooth complex projective variety X X of dimension 5 5 whose third Griffiths group Griff 3 ⁡ ( X ) \operatorname{Griff}^{3}(X) contains infinitely many torsion elements of order n n . This generalises a recent theorem of Schreieder who proved the result for n = 2 n=2 .

1.
Introduction

The Griffiths group
\operatorname{Griff}^{i}(X)
of a smooth complex projective variety
X
is the group of nullhomologous codimension
i
cycles modulo algebraic equivalence. For divisors and zero-cycles these two equivalence relations of algebraic cycles coincide and so
\operatorname{Griff}^{i}(X)
is interesting only if
2\leq i\leq\dim X-1
.

The terminology of this group is due to the famous example of Griffiths
[
Gri69
]
, who showed that
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(X)
is not a torsion group for a very general quintic
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Clemens
[
Cle83
]
proved later that in this case
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(X)
has in fact infinite rank. Further examples of infinite dimensionality of
\operatorname{Griff}^{i}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
were constructed by Nori
[
No93
]
and Voisin
[
Voi00
]
.

Totaro
[
Tot16
]
showed that the vector space
\operatorname{Griff}^{i}(X)/\ell
can be infinite dimensional for all prime numbers
\ell
and all
2\leq i\leq\dim X-1
. This builds on earlier works of Schoen
[
Schoe02
]
and Rosenschon-Srinivas
[
RS10
]
, where there are constraints on the prime
\ell
. The result of Totaro also relies on Griffiths’ method
[
Gri69
]
, a theorem of Bloch-Esnault
[
BS83
]
and Nori’s approach
[
No93
]
of the proof of Clemens’ theorem.

Schoen
[
Schoe92
]
proved that the torsion subgroup of the Griffiths groups is in general not zero. Merkurjev-Suslin
[
MS83
]
used Bloch’s map
[
Blo79
]
to show that the
n
-torsion in
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(X)
is always finite. It was then a natural problem in the field whether
\operatorname{Griff}^{i}(X)
for
i\geq 3
can have infinite
n
-torsion, cf.
[
Schoe92
]
. This was recently solved by Schreieder
[
Sch21c
]
, who produced examples with infinite 2-torsion in their third Griffiths group. The question of infinite
\ell
-torsion for odd primes
\ell
was left open. Building on Schreieder’s approach, we solve in this paper the problem in the following strong sense:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. There is a smooth complex projective variety
X
of dimension 5 whose third Griffiths group
\operatorname{Griff}^{3}(X)
contains infinitely many torsion elements of order
n
. Moreover, these elements are linearly independent modulo
n
, i.e. in
\operatorname{Griff}^{3}(X)/n
.

The above result implies that for any finite set
P
of prime numbers, there is a smooth complex projective
5
-fold
X
, such that the vector space
\operatorname{Griff}^{3}(X)[\ell^{\infty}]/\ell
is infinite dimensional for all
\ell\in\textbf{P}
.

Taking products with projective spaces, we obtain the following generalisation in higher dimensions.

Corollary 1.2
.

For any integers
m\geq 5
and
n\geq 2
, there is a smooth complex projective
m
-fold
X
whose
j
-th Griffiths group
\operatorname{Griff}^{j}(X)
contains infinitely many torsion elements of order
n
for all
3\leq j\leq m-2
. Moreover, these elements are linearly independent in
\operatorname{Griff}^{j}(X)/n
.

The examples in Theorem
1.1
are given by a product
X:=S\times JC
, where
S
is a
\mathbb{Z}/n
-quotient of a carefully chosen smooth complete intersection of multidegree
(n,n,n)
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}_{\mathbb{C}}
(see
\lx@sectionsign
3
) and
C
is a very general genus
3
curve. The surface
S
is somewhat special as such admits an exceptional degeneration, see
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sb:degenerations}
below. For instance, if
n=2
, then
S
is an Enriques surface (as used in
[
Sch21c
]
) that degenerates to a flower pot. For
n>2
, the surface
S
is of general type; its geometric genus is positive and grows polynomially in
n
, see Proposition
3.1
.

Let
Y
be a smooth complex projective variety and let
\operatorname{NS}(Y):=\operatorname{Pic}(Y)/\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
denote its Néron Severi group. The following injectivity result is crucial for us.

be an integer. There is a smooth complex projective surface

is any generator.

Theorem

follows from Theorem

because the Jacobian of a very general genus

curve

has the property that
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)/n
has infinitely many elements of order

1.1.
Special degenerations of surfaces

Apart from a general injectivity result that works for all primes, the second main ingredient in
[
Sch21c
]
was the construction of degenerations of Enriques surfaces such that the Brauer class of the geometric generic fibre extends across the whole family, while the special fibre splits up into several components that are rational and hence the Brauer class restricts to zero on the components of the special fibre. These degenerations of Enriques surfaces are known in the literature as flower-pot, see
[
Per77
, Proposition 3.3.1(3)]
. They are exceptional as such do not admit (up to birational equivalence) an étale 2:1 cover by a Kulikov degeneration (see
[
Kul77
]
) of
K3
surfaces.

The main task within this paper is thus to generalize the above construction to other surfaces. Namely, to prove Theorem
1.3
we need to exhibit for every
n\geq 2
, a surface
S
with
\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\operatorname{tors}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n
that admits a similar degeneration, i.e. there is a Brauer class
\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(S)[n]
, whose image via the Bockstein map generates the torsion in
\bigoplus_{\ell}H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))
and extends across the whole family, while its restriction to the components of the special fibre is zero. This is achieved by the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\kappa
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Fix an algebraic closure
k
of the fraction field
\kappa((t))
. There is a regular flat projective scheme
\mathcal{S}\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa[[t]]
with geometrically connected fibres of relative dimension two, such that:

(1)

the geometric generic fibre
S_{\bar{\eta}}
is a smooth projective surface with
\operatorname{NS}(S_{\bar{\eta}})_{\operatorname{tors}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n
.

(2)

the special fibre
S_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}S_{0i}
is a reduced simple normal crossing divisor whose dual graph
\Gamma
is a chain, i.e. the double intersection
S_{0i}\cap S_{0i+1}
is a smooth and irreducible curve while all intersections
S_{0i}\cap S_{0j}
with
j\notin\{i-1,i,i+1\}
are empty.

(3)

there is a class
\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal{S})[n]
, such that

(a)

\delta(\alpha|_{S_{\bar{\eta}}})\in\bigoplus_{\ell}H^{3}(S_{\bar{\eta}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))_{\operatorname{tors}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n
is a generator, where
\delta
is the Bockstein map defined on the Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}(S_{\bar{\eta}})
and such that

(b)

for any component
S_{0i}
of the special fibre,
\alpha|_{S_{0i}}=0\in\operatorname{Br}(S_{0i})
.

The existence of the above degenerations seems to be slightly counter intuitive as one typically expects that Brauer classes ramify when the varieties break up into components. Their construction is inspired by the one of flower pot of Enriques that was given by Persson, see
[
Per77
, Appendix 2]
.

It would be interesting to know if Theorem
1.4
holds for some degenerations of some well-known surfaces e.g. Godeaux surface. For instance, the property (
3a
) completely fails (see
[
Ale23
, Lemma 5.3]
) for the degenerations of Godeaux surfaces constructed in
[
Per77
, Appendix 3]
.

1.2.
Outline of the main arguments

We briefly explain some of the main steps of the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}
be a smooth complete intersection of multidegree
(n,n,n)
that is invariant and fixed point free by the automorphism
\varphi_{n}
from (
3.1
). Then the crucial step is to degenerate
Y
to some special singular complete intersection
Y_{0}\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}_{\kappa}
(see Lemma
4.2
) that is invariant by
\varphi_{n}
. The singular locus of
Y_{0}
consists of a single point
p_{0}
of multiplicity
n
and the automorphism
\varphi_{n}
fixes exactly this point in
Y_{0}
. The total space
\mathcal{Y}
of this degeneration can be chosen to be regular and so blowing up
p_{0}
in our family we obtain
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\kappa}
with multiplicity
n
. The automorphism
\varphi_{n}
lifts naturally on the blow-up and we will see that in fact acts trivially on the exceptional divisor. Quotiening out yields the desired degeneration.

For
n>2
, the property (
3a
) is more subtle. Namely, the surface
S:=S_{\bar{\eta}}
has positive geometric genus and thus a class
\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(S)[n]
with the property that
\delta(\alpha)
generates the torsion in
\bigoplus_{\ell}H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))
is not canonically given any longer. Instead, the functoriality of the Bockstein map (see (
2.5
)) with respect to pull-backs reduces our task to showing

H^{3}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))_{\operatorname{tors}}\to H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))_{\operatorname{tors}}

is surjective for all primes
\ell
dividing
n
. One of the advantages of this reduction step is that we can apply the proper base change theorem to compute
H^{3}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))
(see Claim
3
) and in particular its torsion subgroup. The existence of a lift
\beta\in H^{3}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))
of a generator of the cyclic group
H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))_{\operatorname{tors}}
follows from the Gysin sequence. The intricate part of the proof is then to show that the class
\beta
can be chosen to be torsion, so that it comes from a Brauer class on
\mathcal{S}
.

The property (
3b
) of Theorem
1.4
is more involved than the case of flower pot of Enriques, i.e.
n=2
. This is because the special fibre
S_{0}
contains a single component (say
W
), which is not ruled if
n>2
(see Proposition
4.4
). We will be able to take care of this matter by adding some integral cohomology class to a lift
\tilde{\alpha}\in H^{2}(\mathcal{S},\mu_{n})
of the Brauer class on
\mathcal{S}
from (
3a
), i.e.
\tilde{\alpha}|_{W}=0
up to adding a cohomology class from
H^{2}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{Z}_{n}(1)):=\prod_{\ell|n}H^{2}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))
. Note that this modification on the class
\alpha
has no effect on its value via the Bockstein map (cf. (
2.5
)).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

The
n
-torsion subgroup of an abelian group
G
is denoted by
G[n]
. The subgroup
G[n^{\infty}]
consists of the elements that are annihilated by some power of
n
. We also denote by
G_{div}
the maximal divisible subgroup of
G
. If
\psi\colon H\to G
is a homomorphism of abelian groups, then we denote by slight abuse of notation
G/H:=\operatorname{coker}(\psi)
.

Let
k
be a field. An algebraic scheme
X
over
k
is a seperated scheme of finite multidegree over
k
and it is equi-dimensional if its irreducible components have all the same dimension. A variety over
k
is an integral algebraic
k
-scheme. For an equi-dimensional algebraic
k
-scheme
X
and any
0\leq i\leq\dim X
, we let
X^{(i)}:=\{x\in X|\operatorname{codim}_{X}(x):=\dim X-\dim\overline{\{x\}}=i\}
be the set of all codimension
i
points in
X
.

2.2.
Borel-Moore cohomology

Fix a field
k
and a prime
\ell
invertible in
k
. For an algebraic
k
-scheme
X
of dimension
d_{X}
and
A\in\{\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\}
, we let

H^{i}(X,A(n)):=H^{i}_{BM}(X,A(n))

be the twisted Borel-Moore pro-étale cohomology, as defined in
[
Sch21b
, (6.13)-(6.15)]
(see
[
Sch21b
, Proposition 6.6]
). The main properties of this functor are listed in
[
Sch21b
, §4]
.

If
X
is smooth and equi-dimensional, then by
[
Sch21b
, Lemma 6.5]
, there are canonical isomorphisms

(2.1)

\displaystyle H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r}(n))\simeq H^{i}(X_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n})\ \text{and}\ H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(n))\simeq H^{i}_{cont}(X_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(n)),

where
H^{i}_{cont}
denotes Jannsen’s continuous étale cohomology (see
[
Jan88
]
).

More generally, if
X
admits a closed embedding
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
into a smooth and equi-dimensional algebraic
k
-scheme
Y
of dimension
d_{Y}
, then there is a canonical identification with continuous étale cohomology with support

(2.2)

\displaystyle H^{i}(X,A(n))\simeq H^{i+2c}_{X,cont}(Y,A(n+c)),

where
c=d_{Y}-d_{X}
(see
[
AS22
, Remark 2.1]
and
[
Sch22
, Lemma A.1]
).

2.4.

2.6.
Strictly semi-stable models

Let

be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field
k
and residue field
\kappa
. A proper flat

\mathcal{X}\to\operatorname{Spec}R
is called

if the generic fibre

is smooth and the special fibre
X_{0}:=\mathcal{X}\times_{R}\kappa
is a geometrically reduced simple normal crossing divisor on
\mathcal{X}
, i.e. the irreducible components

are all smooth Cartier divisors and for every
n
, the intersection of
n
distinct components is either empty or smooth and equi-dimensional of codimension

in
\mathcal{X}
. Note that the total space
\mathcal{X}
is in fact regular (see
[
Har77
, Remark 1.1.1 and Remark 1.1.2]
). We say that the dual graph
\Gamma
of the special fibre
X_{0}
is a
chain
if the scheme-theoretic intersection of two consecutive components
X_{0i}\cap X_{0i+1}
is non-empty while all intersections
X_{0i}\cap X_{0j}
with
j\notin\{i-1,i,i+1\}
are empty.

n=\ell^{r}
, where
\ell
is a prime and
r\geq 1
an integer. By
[
Tot16
, Theorem 3.1]
, there is a smooth quartic curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, such that
\operatorname{CH}^{2}(JC)/\ell
is infinite for all prime numbers
\ell
. Recall that the group of algebraically trivial cycles modulo rational equivalence is divisible and so
A^{2}(JC)/\ell\simeq\operatorname{CH}^{2}(JC)/\ell
. In particular, we find that
A^{2}(JC)/\ell
is infinite for all primes
\ell
. The infiniteness of
A^{2}(JC)/\ell
in this case arises from pulling back the Ceresa cycle by infinitely many distinct isogenies. These cycles are known to be homologically trivial (see
[
Cer83
]
) and thus
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)/\ell
is infinite dimensional for all primes
\ell
.

It remains to treat the case
r\geq 2
. Assume that there is a prime
\ell_{0}
and an integer
r_{0}\geq 2
, such that
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)/\ell_{0}^{r_{0}}
contains only finitely many elements of order
\ell_{0}^{r_{0}}
. Fix an infinite set
\mathfrak{I}\subset\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)
whose elements are linearly independent modulo
\ell_{0}
and denote by
\overline{\mathfrak{I}}\subset\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)/\ell_{0}
its image. We claim that all but finitely many cycles in
\overline{\mathfrak{I}}
lie in the image of the map
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)[\ell_{0}^{r_{0}}]\to\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)/\ell_{0}
. Indeed, if the image of
[z]\in\mathfrak{I}
in
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)/\ell_{0}^{r_{0}}
is killed by
\ell_{0}^{r}
with
1\leq r<r_{0}
, then
\ell_{0}^{r}([z]-\ell_{0}^{r_{0}-r}[z^{\prime}])=0\in\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)
for some
[z^{\prime}]\in\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)
and in particular
[z_{0}]:=[z]-\ell_{0}^{r_{0}-r}[z^{\prime}]\in\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)[\ell_{0}^{r_{0}}]
. Since
r_{0}-r>0
, we find that the reduction of
[z_{0}]
modulo
\ell_{0}
coincides with the one of
[z]
. The claim follows as only finitely many images of elements of
\mathfrak{I}
in
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)/\ell_{0}^{r_{0}}
can have order
\ell_{0}^{r_{0}}
.

To conclude the argument recall by
[
MS83
,
\lx@sectionsign
18]
that the group
\operatorname{Griff}^{2}(JC)[\ell_{0}^{r_{0}}]
is finite. This in turn contradicts the infiniteness of
\overline{\mathfrak{I}}
. The proof is complete. ∎

3.
A cyclic quotient of a complete intersection

In this section, we introduce the surfaces that will use as a substitute of the Enriques surface used in
[
Sch21c
]
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and fix a primitive
n
-th root of unity
1\neq\omega\in k
. Consider the automorphism
\varphi_{n}
on
\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}
defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle(x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3}:x_{4}:x_{5})\longmapsto(x_{0}:\omega x_{1}:x_{2}:\omega x_{3}:x_{4}:\omega x_{5}).

Its fixed locus consists of two disjoint planes given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle P_{1}:=\{x_{0}=x_{2}=x_{4}=0\}\ \text{and}\ P_{2}:=\{x_{1}=x_{3}=x_{5}=0\}.

The complete linear system
|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}}(n)|
that parametrizes hypersurfaces
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}
of degree
n
, splits under
\varphi_{n}
into
n
disjoint eigenspaces corresponding to the eigenvalues
\omega^{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,n-1
. An easy check shows that the ones that are correlated with the eigenvalue
1
form projectively a
d
-dimensional family, where
d:=(n+1)(n+2)-1
. We denote this linear system by
{|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}}(n)|}^{\varphi_{n}}
and call its elements
invariant hypersurfaces
.

Note that the above is a base-point free linear system, since the sections
x_{0}^{n},x_{1}^{n},\ldots,x_{5}^{n}
are all invariant. It follows by Bertini’s theorem that for general choices of hypersurfaces
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\in{|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}}(n)|}^{\varphi_{n}}
, the complete intersection
Y:=H_{1}\cap H_{2}\cap H_{3}
is smooth and
\varphi_{n}
acts freely on
Y
.

A concrete example that works for any
n
is given by the
3
equations

(3.3)

\displaystyle x_{0}^{n}+x_{1}^{n}+x_{3}^{n}+x_{4}^{n}+x_{1}x_{5}^{n-1}+x_{3}x_{5}^{n-1}=0,

\displaystyle 2x_{0}^{n}+x_{1}^{n}+3x_{2}^{n}-x_{3}^{n}+5x_{4}^{n}+x_{1}x_{5}^{n-1}-x_{3}x_{5}^{n-1}=0,

\displaystyle x_{0}^{n}-3x_{1}^{n}+x_{2}^{n}+x_{3}^{n}-2x_{4}^{n}+7x_{5}^{n}=0.

We let
S:=Y/\varphi_{n}
be the
\mathbb{Z}/n
-quotient of
Y
by the automorphism
\varphi_{n}
. This is a smooth projective surface because the quotient map
\pi\colon Y\to S
is finite and étale. For instance, if
n=2
, then
S
is an Enriques surface and
Y
is its canonical double cover, which is a K3 surface.

For
n\geq 3
, the surface
S
is always of general type with
q(S):=h^{0,1}(S)=0
and topological fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n
. For the sake of completeness, in the following proposition we compute some of its invariants:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}
be a smooth complete intersection of three invariant hypersurfaces
H_{i}\in{|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}}(n)|}^{\varphi_{n}}
, such that
\varphi_{n}
from (
3.1
) acts freely on
Y
and set
S:=Y/\varphi_{n}
. Then the following assertions are true:

(1)

S
is a smooth projective surface of general type if
n\geq 3
.

(2)

The topological fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S)
is cyclic of order
n
.

(3)

The Hodge numbers of
S
are given by
h^{2,0}=\frac{n^{2}}{4}(5n^{2}-18n+17)-1
,
h^{1,1}=\frac{n^{2}}{2}(7n^{2}-18n+13),
and
h^{1,0}=0
.

Proof.

The computations are straightforward. Recall that any smooth complete intersection of dimension
2
is simply connected. Hence
\pi_{1}(S)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n
and
q(S)=\frac{1}{2}b_{1}(S)=0
.

The geometric genus of
S
is computed from the Euler characteristic formula
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y})=n\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})
, where we use
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Y})=\frac{n^{3}}{4}(5n^{2}-18n+17)
(cf.
[
BHPV04
, Proposition V.2.1]
).

The Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem yields
h^{1,1}(S)=10\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})-K_{S}^{2}
. We note that
K_{Y}^{2}=nK_{S}^{2}
, whereas
K_{Y}^{2}=9n^{3}(n-2)^{2}
(see
[
BHPV04
, Proposition V.2.1]
). An obvious substitution then gives the desired relation for
h^{1,1}(S)
.

It remains to check that
S
is indeed a surface of general type as long as
n\geq 3
. Recall that
K_{Y}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(3n-6)
is ample and
K_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{S}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n-1})
(see
[
BHPV04
, Lemma I.17.1]
) for some generator
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)[n]\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n
. We thus find by the projection formula that
\pi^{*}C.K_{Y}=\pi^{*}C.\pi^{*}(K_{S}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n-1})=nC.K_{S}>0
for all irreducible curves
C\subset S
. In particular
K_{S}
is ample. This concludes the proof.∎

Remark 3.2
.

We note that any étale
\mathbb{Z}/n
-quotient of a smooth complete intersection of multidegree
(n,n,n)
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}_{\mathbb{C}}
has the same invariants with the surface of Proposition
3.1
.

4.
Degeneration

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and fix an integer
n\geq 2
. Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}
be a smooth complete intersection of three very general invariant hypersurfaces
H_{i}\in|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}}(n)|^{\varphi_{n}}
and set
S:=Y/\varphi_{n}
.

We aim to exhibit an example of (semi-stable) degenerations for the surface

that will satisfy the properties of Theorem
1.4
. The main result of this section is as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\kappa
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and fix an integer

. Let

denote an algebraic closure of the fraction field
\kappa((t))
. There is a regular flat projective scheme
\mathcal{S}\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa[[t]]
, such that:

(1)

the geometric generic fibre

is a quotient of the form

, where

is a smooth complete intersection of three very general invariant hypersurfaces

and

is the automorphism from (

(2)

the special fibre

and consists of two irreducible components, where

and

is a surface with

for all prime factors

is a smooth plane curve of degree

We shall need the following lemma.

.

(2)

is a reduced and irreducible surface whose singular locus consists only of the point

The blow-up

(4)

, where

and

. Indeed, the tangent space of

Fock expansion for two-electron atoms. High order angular coefficients.
Evgeny Z. Liverts, Rajmund Krivec

Abstract
The Fock expansion, which describes the properties of two-electron atoms near the nucleus, is studied. The angular Fock coefficients ψ k , p ​ ( α , θ ) \psi_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta) with the maximum possible value of subscript p p are calculated on examples of the coefficients with 5 ≤ k ≤ 10 5\leq k\leq 10 . The presented technique makes it possible to calculate such angular coefficients for any arbitrarily large k k . The mentioned coefficients being leading in the logarithmic power series representing the Fock expansion may be indispensable for the development of simple methods for calculating the helium-like electronic structure. The theoretical results obtained are verified by other suitable methods. The Wolfram Mathematica is used extensively.



I
Introduction

The properties of a two-electron atomic (helium-like) system with an infinitely massive nucleus of the charge
Z
and non-relativistic energy
E
are defined by the wave function (WF)
\Psi(r_{1},r_{2},r_{12})
, where

r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are the electron-nucleus distances, and
r_{12}
is the distance between the electrons.
The behavior of the ground state WF in
the vicinity of the nucleus located at the origin is determined by the Fock expansion
FOCK

\bar{\Psi}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{12})\equiv\Psi(R,\alpha,\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}R^{k}\sum_{p=0}^{[k/2]}\psi_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)\ln^{p}R,

(1)

where the hyperspherical coordinates
R,~\alpha
and
\theta
are defined by the relations:

R=\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}},~~~~\alpha=2\arctan\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right),~~~~\theta=\arccos\left(\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-r_{12}^{2}}{2r_{1}r_{2}}\right).

(2)

The convergence of expansion (
1
) had been proven in Ref.
MORG
.
The angular Fock coefficients (AFC)
\psi_{k,p}

satisfy the Fock recurrence relation (FRR)

\left[\Lambda^{2}-k(k+4)\right]\psi_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)=h_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)

(3)

with the RHS of the form
AB1
,
LEZ4
:

h_{k,p}=2(k+2)(p+1)\psi_{k,p+1}+(p+1)(p+2)\psi_{k,p+2}-2V\psi_{k-1,p}+2E\psi_{k-2,p}.

(4)

The dimensionless Coulomb potential representing the electron-electron and electron-nucleus interactions is

V\equiv\frac{R}{r_{12}}-Z\left(\frac{R}{r_{1}}+\frac{R}{r_{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{\xi}-\frac{2Z\eta}{\sin\alpha},

(5)

where we have introduced the important (in what follows) angular quantities:

\xi=\sqrt{1-\sin\alpha\cos\theta},~~~\eta=\sqrt{1+\sin\alpha}.

(6)

The hyperspherical angular momentum operator, projected on
S
states, is defined as

\Lambda^{2}=-4\left[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}+2\cot\alpha\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}+\cot\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)\right].

(7)

It is clear that all circumnuclear features of the two-electron atoms (ions) are defined by the Fock expansion (
1
).
There are a large number of methods for calculating the electronic structure of the two-electron atomic systems.
An excellent review on this topic can be found in Refs.
NAK
;
ROD
;
FOR
;
DRK
;
AB1
.
However, we are known only one technique that correctly represents the WF,
\Psi(r_{1},r_{2},r_{12})
near the nucleus.
It is the so called correlation function hyperspherical harmonic method (CFHHM)
HM0
;
HM1
;
HM2
.
Unfortunately, the CFHHM is extremely difficult to implement.
The expansion in hyperspherical harmonics (HHs) provides the correct representation of the AFCs.
However, the HH-expansion is known to converge very slowly.
Although this method makes it possible to increase the convergence of the HH-expansion,
a sufficiently good accuracy requires a large HHs basis size, which, in turn, creates great computational difficulties.

Thus, the conclusion suggests itself that it would be extremely useful to develop a much simpler method for calculating the WF with correct behavior near the nucleus.
In this regard, we would like to emphasize the following important peculiarities of the Fock expansion (FE).
It follows from definition (
1
) that the FE can be splitted into individual power series (lines) associated with definite power of
\textrm{ln}R
. In other words, the FE can be represented in the form:

\displaystyle\Psi=(\ln R)^{0}\left(\psi_{0,0}+R\psi_{1,0}+R^{2}\psi_{2,0}+...\right)~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle+(\ln R)^{1}R^{2}\left(\psi_{2,1}+R\psi_{3,1}+R^{2}\psi_{4,1}+...\right)~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle+(\ln R)^{2}R^{4}\left(\psi_{4,2}+R\psi_{5,2}+R^{2}\psi_{6,2}+...\right)~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle+(\ln R)^{3}R^{6}\left(\psi_{6,3}+R\psi_{7,3}+R^{2}\psi_{8,3}+...\right)~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle+(\ln R)^{4}R^{8}\left(\psi_{8,4}+R\psi_{9,4}+R^{2}\psi_{10,4}+...\right)+...~~~~~~~~~

(8)

It is seen that the leading term of each line represents the product
(\ln R)^{k/2}R^{k}\psi_{k,k/2}(\alpha,\theta)
with even
k
.
The first AFCs (
\psi_{0,0}=1
) corresponding to
k=0,2,4
are well-known (see, e.g.,
AB1
;
LEZ4
):

\psi_{1,0}=\frac{1}{2}\xi-Z\eta,

(9)

\psi_{2,1}=-\frac{Z(\pi-2)}{3\pi}(1-\xi^{2}),

(10)

\psi_{3,1}=\frac{Z(\pi-2)}{36\pi}\left[6Z\eta(1-\xi^{2})+\xi(5\xi^{2}-6)\right],

(11)

\psi_{4,2}=\frac{Z^{2}(\pi-2)(5\pi-14)}{540\sqrt{\pi}}\left[Y_{40}(\alpha,\theta)+\sqrt{2}~Y_{42}(\alpha,\theta)\right].

(12)

The normalized HHs are

Y_{40}(\alpha,\theta)=\pi^{-3/2}(4\cos^{2}\alpha-1),~~~~~Y_{42}(\alpha,\theta)=2\sqrt{2}\pi^{-3/2}\sin^{2}\alpha P_{2}(\cos\theta),~~~

(13)

where
P_{n}(x)
denotes the Legendre polynomials.

In this paper, we present the theoretical calculations of the AFCs
\psi_{5,2}(\alpha,\theta)
,
\psi_{6,3}(\alpha,\theta)
,
\psi_{7,3}(\alpha,\theta)
,
\psi_{8,4}(\alpha,\theta)
and
\psi_{9,4}(\alpha,\theta)
included into the
k=4
,
k=6
and
k=8
”lines” of the expansion (
I
), and also the AFC
\psi_{10,5}(\alpha,\theta)
representing the leading term of
k=10
”line”.
It is important to note that all mentioned angular coefficients represent the AFCs
\psi_{k,p}
with the maximum possible
p
for a given
k
.

\psi_{5,2}(\alpha,\theta)

3

4
) for

and

\left(\Lambda^{2}-45\right)\psi_{5,2}(\alpha,\theta)=h_{5,2}(\alpha,\theta),

(15)

Using Eqs.(

LEZ4

II.1
Solution of the IFRR

Moving from simpler to more complex solutions, let’s start with IFRR

For convenience, we will solve the Eq.(
21

Using the method of variation of parameters, one obtains

The independent solutions of the homogeneous equation associated with Eq.(

Substitution of Eqs.(

To choose these coefficients, it is necessary to determine the behavior of all independent solutions on the boundaries of the domain

(29)

(31)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{60\pi^{3/2}}\left[11\sin\alpha+21\cos(2\alpha)+2\right]\sqrt{1+\sin\alpha}.~~~~~~

We propose the following method to find the second desired equation.
Remind that any suitable function of the angles

and

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(-24l^{3}-100l^{2}+198l+249\right)}{(2l-5)(2l-3)(2l+3)(2l+5)(2l+7)(2l+9)},~~~~~~~~

K_{v}(l)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\sin\alpha)^{2l+2}v_{5l}(\rho)d\alpha=\frac{2^{l+3/2}(2l-1)}{(2l+5)(2l+9)},~~~~~~~

is not required.

On the other hand, expanding

V
Results and Discussions

The angular Fock coefficients
\psi_{k,p}\equiv\psi_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)
with the
maximum possible
value of subscript
p
were calculated on examples of the coefficients with
5\leq k\leq 8
.
The AFCs
\psi_{9,4}
and
\psi_{10,5}
are additionally presented in the Appendix without derivations.
The presented technique makes it possible to calculate such AFCs for any arbitrarily large
k
.
These coefficients are leading in the logarithmic power series representing the Fock expansion (see Eq.(
I
)). As such, they may be indispensable for the development of simple methods for calculating the helium-like electronic structure.

The proposed technique, as well as the final results, are quite complex. Therefore, both require verification.
We are aware of two ways of the above-mentioned verification.
The first one is to use the Green’s function (GF) approach (see, Ref.
FOCK
and also Ref.
LEZ7
, Sec. 4) which enables us to calculate (at least, numerically) the AFC (or its component) by the following integral representation:

\psi_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\alpha^{\prime}\sin^{2}\alpha^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta^{\prime}\sin\theta^{\prime}~h_{k,p}(\alpha^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\cos\left[\left(\frac{k}{2}+1\right)\omega\right]}{\sin\omega}(1-\lambda)d\varphi,

(159)

where
\omega
is an angle defined by the relation

\cos\omega=\cos\alpha\cos\alpha^{\prime}+\sin\alpha\sin\alpha^{\prime}\left(\cos\theta\cos\theta^{\prime}+\sin\theta\sin\theta^{\prime}\cos\varphi\right),

(160)

whereas

\lambda=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}0~~~~~~~~~~~~~~k~~~odd\\
\omega/\pi~~~~~~~~~~~~k~~~even\\
\end{array}\right..

(161)

For even
k
and maximum value of
p=k/2
the RHS
h_{k,k/2}
of the FRR (
3
) equals zero.
This implies that the GF formula (
159
) cannot be applied in this case.
Hence, only the AFCs
\psi_{k,p}
with odd values of
k
(and maximum
p
) can be verified with the GF method.
Thus, numerically calculating (for various combinations of angles
\alpha
and
\theta
) the triple integrals (
159
) representing the AFCs
\psi_{5,2}(\alpha,\theta)
,
\psi_{7,3}(\alpha,\theta)
and
\psi_{9,4}(\alpha,\theta)
,
we have verified that the representations obtained for them in Sections
II
,
IV.1
and in the Appendix are correct.

The second method of the verification under consideration, covering all possible combinations of angles, is much more complicated. This is the CFHH method mentioned in Introduction.
It is based on decomposing the full WF into a form

\Psi^{\textrm{CFHH}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{12})=\exp\left[f(r_{1},r_{2},r_{12})\right]\Phi^{\textrm{CFHH}}(R,\alpha,\theta),

(162)

where the so called correlation function
f
can be taken in a simple linear form

f(r_{1},r_{2},r_{12})=c_{1}r_{1}+c_{2}r_{2}+c_{12}r_{12}.

(163)

The so called ”cusp parametrization”

c_{1}=c_{2}=-Z,~~~~~~~c_{12}=1/2

(164)

is used as a rule.
For small enough hyperspherical radius
R
, the function
\Phi
is represented as

\Phi^{\textrm{CFHH}}(R,\alpha,\theta)=\frac{1}{d_{0,0}(\alpha,\theta)}\sum_{k=0}^{K}(2\kappa R)^{k}\sum_{p=0}^{[k/2]}d_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)\ln^{p}(2\kappa R),

(165)

where
\kappa=\sqrt{-2E}
, and functions
d_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)
are expanded in
N
(basis size) HHs. It follows from representation (
165
) that the AFCs
\psi_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)
can be expressed in terms of the functions
d_{k^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\alpha,\theta)
calculated by the CFHHM.
For example, for the AFCs in questions one obtains:

\psi_{k,k/2}^{\textrm{CFHH}}(\alpha,\theta)=\frac{(2\kappa)^{k}d_{k,k/2}(\alpha,\theta)}{d_{0,0}(\alpha,\theta)}.

(166)

We have calculated all AFCs discussed in this article using CFHHM with
K=18
and
N=1600
.
The angles
0\leq\alpha\leq\pi
and
0\leq\theta\leq\pi
with step
\pi/6
were considered.
The relative difference
|1-\psi_{k,p}(\alpha,\theta)/\psi_{k,p}^{\textrm{CFHH}}(\alpha,\theta)|
was less than
10^{-7}
for all examined cases, including
1\leq Z\leq 5
. This indicates that all our theoretical calculations were correct.

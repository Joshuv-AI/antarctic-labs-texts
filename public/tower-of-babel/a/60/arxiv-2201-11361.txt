Gaussian heat kernel estimates of Bamler-Zhang type along super Ricci flow
Keita Kunikawa, Yohei Sakurai

Abstract
. Bamler-Zhang have developed geometric analysis on Ricci flow with scalar curvature bound. The aim of this paper is to extend their work to various geometric flows. We generalize some of their results to super Ricci flow whose Müller quantity is non-negative, and obtain Gaussian heat kernel estimates.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backgrounds

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
be a solution to the Ricci flow

(1.1)

\partial_{t}g=-2\operatorname{Ric}

on an
n
-dimensional compact smooth manifold with
T<+\infty
.
One of the common problems in the Ricci flow theory is to understand the blow-up behavior of curvature when
t\nearrow T
.
In a pioneering work by Hamilton
[
19
]
,
he has proved that the supremum of the norm of the Riemannian curvature tensor blows up at
T
;
namely,

(1.2)

\lim_{t\nearrow T}\sup_{M}|\operatorname{Rm}|=+\infty.

After that
under the same setting,
Šešum
[
34
]
has generalized (
1.2
) to the Ricci curvature;
namely, she has proved

\limsup_{t\nearrow T}\sup_{M}|\operatorname{Ric}|=+\infty.

It is natural to ask whether the same phenomenon occurs for the scalar curvature;
namely,

\limsup_{t\nearrow T}\sup_{M}R=+\infty.

This is known to occur in the case of
n=2,3
or in the Kähler case (see
[
20
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
45
]
),
but it seems to be still open in the higher dimensional and non-Kähler case.
From this point of view,
it is important to investigate the structure of Ricci flow with scalar curvature bound.

Based on the background,
geometric analysis on Ricci flow with scalar curvature bound has been developed by Cao-Tran
[
10
]
, Chen-Wang
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
, Wang
[
36
]
,
[
37
]
, Zhang
[
44
]
and so on.
Bamler-Zhang
[
7
]
have made remarkable progress in this direction establishing useful tools such as distance distortion estimates,
a nice cutoff function,
and mean value inequalities.
By using them,
they have also concluded Gaussian heat kernel estimates,
backward pseudolocality theorem,
and strong
\varepsilon
-regularity theorem.
Recently,
their estimates have yielded numerous results in the sequel
[
8
]
,
and a series of works by Bamler
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
while being improved.

1.2.
Main results

The purpose of this paper is to extend the results by Bamler-Zhang
[
7
]
to more general geometric flows.
We focus on a super solution to the Ricci flow (
1.1
).
An
n
-dimensional compact manifold
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
equipped with a time-dependent metric is called
super Ricci flow
when

\partial_{t}g\geq-2\operatorname{Ric}.

McCann-Topping
[
30
]
have introduced this notion,
and studied the relation between the Ricci flow theory and optimal transport theory.
Recently,
this notion has begun to be investigated not only from the viewpoint of the Ricci flow theory but also from that of metric measure geometry (see e.g.,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
35
]
).

We examine super Ricci flow under the assumption that
its
Müller quantity
is non-negative.
For a (time-dependent) vector field
V
,
the
Müller quantity
is defined by the following formula (see
[
31
, Definition 1.3]
):

\mathcal{D}(V):=\partial_{t}H-\Delta H-2|h|^{2}+4\operatorname{div}h(V)-2\langle\nabla H,V\rangle+2\operatorname{Ric}(V,V)-2h(V,V),

where

h:=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}g,\quad H:=\operatorname{tr}h.

It is well-known that
several results for Ricci flow can be extended to more general geometric flow under the assumption that its Müller quantity is non-negative;
for instance, the monotonicity of
\mathcal{W}
-functional and the reduced geometry (more precisely, see Subsections
2.2
,
2.3
).
Also,
there are some examples of super Ricci flow whose Müller quantity is non-negative (see
[
31
, Section 2]
,
[
17
, Section 7]
):

(1)

Ricci flow;

(2)

Ricci flow coupled with the heat equation, called
List flow
(
[
28
]
);

(3)

Ricci flow coupled with the harmonic map heat flow, called
Müller flow
(
[
32
]
);

(4)

mean curvature flow for spacelike hypersurfaces in Lorentzian manifold of non-negative sectional curvature;

(5)

(scaled) twisted Kähler-Ricci flow.

For
x,y\in M
and
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we denote by
G(x,t;y,s)
the heat kernel for the heat equation (more precisely, see Subsection
2.2
).
One of our main results is the following Gaussian heat kernel estimate,
which has been formulated by Bamler-Zhang
[
7
]
for Ricci flow (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
be an
n
-dimensional compact super Ricci flow with
T<+\infty
satisfying
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
Then for any
A>0
,
there exist positive constants
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},\mathcal{C}_{3},\mathcal{C}_{4},\mathcal{C}_{5},\mathcal{C}_{6}>0
depending only on
n,T,g(0)
and
A
such that the following holds:
We assume
H\leq H_{1}
for
H_{1}>0
.
For
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we suppose
t-s\leq AH_{1}^{-1}
and
s\geq(t-s)/A
.
Then we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle G(x,t;y,s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right),

(1.4)

\displaystyle G(x,t;y,s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{4}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right),

(1.5)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G|(x,t;y,s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{5}}{(t-s)^{(n+1)/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{6}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

Remark 1.2
.

We give a remark for the dependence of the constants on
g(0)
.
More precisely,
they depend on the volume and Sobolev constant of
M
with respect to
g(0)
,
and the minimum of
H
at
t=0
,
which is derived from the logarithmic Sobolev inequality of Fang-Zheng
[
17
]
(cf.
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
, Subsection
2.2
).
This remark can be applied throughout this paper.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall some previous works for general geometric flow.
Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
denote an
n
-dimensional compact manifold with a time-dependent metric with
T<+\infty
.

2.1.
Notations and basics

In the present subsection,
we introduce some notations,
and elementary facts that will be used frequently throughout this article.

We denote by
d_{t}
and
m_{t}
the Riemannian distance and Riemannian volume measure with respect to
g(t)
,
respectively.
When
t
is clear from the context,
we omit it,
and write them as
d
and
m
.
Notice that
the time derivative of
dm
is equal to
-H\,dm
.
For
x_{0}\in M,r_{0}>0
and
t_{0}\in[0,T)
,
we denote by
B(x_{0},r_{0},t_{0})
the open ball of radius
r_{0}
centered at
x_{0}
with respect to
g(t_{0})
.
Let
Q^{+}(x_{0},r_{0},t_{0})
stand for the
forward parabolic cube
defined as

Q^{+}(x_{0},r_{0},t_{0}):=\{(x,t)\in M\times[0,T)\mid x\in B(x_{0},r_{0},t),\,t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+r^{2}_{0}]\}.

We begin with the following elementary fact (see
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
):

Proposition 2.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

Assume that

(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfies
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
Then we have

H\geq-\frac{n}{2t}.

Remark 2.2
.

We also mention the rescaling argument.
For a positive
r_{0}>0
,
we will consider the parabolic rescaling

\bar{g}(\tau):=r^{-2}_{0}\,g(r^{2}_{0}\tau)=r^{-2}_{0}g(t)

with
\tau:=r^{-2}_{0}t
.
We see that
\bar{h}=h,\bar{H}=r^{2}_{0}H
and
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(V)=r^{4}_{0}\mathcal{D}(r^{-2}_{0}V)
,
where
\bar{h},\bar{H},\overline{\mathcal{D}}(V)
are corresponding objects for
\bar{g}(\tau)
.
In particular,
the super Ricci flow and the non-negativity of the Müller quantity are preserved under this rescaling.

2.2.
Monotonicity of entropy

The monotonicity of Perelman’s
\mathcal{W}
-functional is one of the powerful tools in the Ricci flow theory (
[
33
]
).
It is well-known that
such a monotonicity can be extended to general geometric flow under the non-negativity of the Müller quantity (see
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
18
, Theorem 5.2]
,
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
).
In this subsection,
we collect some results that can be derived from the monotonicity.

Fang-Zheng
[
17
]
obtained a logarithmic Sobolev inequality by using the monotonicity of
\mathcal{W}
-functional,
and derived the following Sobolev inequality (see
[
17
, Theorems 1.1, 1.3]
, and cf.
[
38
]
,
[
42
]
):

Proposition 2.3

(
[
17
]
)
.

Assume that

(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfies
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
Then for all
t\in[0,T)
and
u\in W^{1,2}(M)
we have

\left(\int_{M}\,|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dm\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C_{1}\int_{M}\,\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{H}{4}u^{2}\right)\,dm+C_{2}\,\int_{M}\,u^{2}\,dm,

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are positive constants depending only on
n,T
and
g(0)
.

They further concluded that
the following
\kappa
-noncollapsing estimate holds (see
[
38
, Theorem 6.1]
,
[
17
, Theorem 5.1]
):

Theorem 2.4

(
[
17
]
)
.

Assume that

(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfies
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
For
x\in M,r\in(0,\sqrt{T})
and
t\in[0,T)
,
we assume
H\leq r^{-2}
on
B(x,r,t)
.
Then we have

m(B(x,r,t))\geq\kappa\,r^{n},

where
\kappa>0
is a positive constant depending only on
n,T
and
g(0)
.

For
x,y\in M
and
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we denote by
G(x,t;y,s)
the heat kernel for the heat equation;
namely,
for a fixed
(y,s)\in M\times[0,T)
,
it solves

(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x})G(\cdot,\cdot;y,s)=0,\quad\lim_{t\searrow s}G(\cdot,t;y,s)=\delta_{y}.

Notice that

G(x,t;\cdot,\cdot)
is the kernel for the conjugate heat equation;
namely,
for any
(x,t)\in M\times[0,T)
,

(-\partial_{s}-\Delta_{y}+H)G(x,t;\cdot,\cdot)=0,\quad\lim_{s\nearrow t}G(x,t;\cdot,s)=\delta_{x}.

They also showed the following upper bound of Zhang type (see
[
44
, (1.5)]
,
[
17
, Lemma 6.3]
):

Proposition 2.5

(
[
17
]
)
.

Assume that

(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfies
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
Then for all
x,y\in M
and
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we have

G(x,t;y,s)\leq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{n/2}},

where
C>0
is a positive constant depending only on
n,T
and
g(0)
.

2.3.
Reduced geometry

Perelman’s reduced geometry is also a crucial concept in the Ricci flow theory (
[
33
]
).
It is also well-known that
the reduced geometry can be extended to general geometric flow under the assumption that the Müller quantity is non-negative (see e.g.,
[
31
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
39
]
,
[
40
]
).
Here we present some results concerning the reduced geometry.

Let us recall the notion of the reduced distance.
Let
x,y\in M
and
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
.
The
L
-distance

L_{(x,t)}(y,s)
from a space-time base point
(x,t)
to
(y,s)
is defined by

L_{(x,t)}(y,s):=\inf_{\gamma}\int^{t}_{s}\sqrt{t-\xi}\left[H+\left|\frac{d\gamma}{d\xi}\right|^{2}\right]\,d\xi,

where the infimum is taken over all curves
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
with
\gamma(s)=y
and
\gamma(t)=x
.
The
reduced distance

\ell_{(x,t)}(y,s)
, and its
modified one

\overline{L}_{(x,t)}(y,s)
are defined as

\ell_{(x,t)}(y,s):=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t-s}}L_{(x,t)}(y,s),\quad\overline{L}_{(x,t)}(y,s):=4(t-s)\,\ell_{(x,t)}(y,s).

It is well-known that
the following fundamental inequality holds under the non-negativity of the Müller quantity (see
[
31
, Lemmas 5.2, 5.3]
, and also
[
23
, Theorem 3.4]
,
[
39
, Subsection 2.3]
,
[
40
]
):

Proposition 2.6

(
[
31
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
39
]
,
[
40
]
)
.

Assume that

(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfies
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
Then we have

(2.1)

(-\partial_{s}+\Delta)\overline{L}_{(x,t)}\leq 2n

in the barrier sense.

For the Ricci flow,
this estimate (
2.1
) together with the maximum principle leads us to the uniform upper bound of the minimum of the reduced distance (see
[
33
]
,
[
15
, Lemmas 7.48, 7.50]
).
By the same argument,
Proposition
2.6
gives us the following:

Proposition 2.7
.

Assume that

(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfies
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
Then for all
x\in M
and
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
we have

\min_{y\in M}\ell_{(x,t)}(y,s)\leq\frac{n}{2}.

We also mention the following relation between the reduced distance and heat kernel (see
[
9
, Lemma 2.4]
):

Proposition 2.8

(
[
9
]
)
.

Assume that

(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfies
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
Then for all
x,y\in M
and
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
we have

G(x,t;y,s)\geq\frac{1}{(4\pi(t-s))^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\ell_{(x,t)}(y,s)\right).

2.5.
Characterization of super Ricci flow

It is well-known that
super Ricci flow can be characterized by a gradient estimate,
logarithmic Sobolev inequality,
and Poincaré inequality for the heat semigroup,
which can be regarded as a time-dependent version of the equivalence of a lower Ricci curvature bound established by Bakry-Émery
[
1
]
, Bakry-Ledoux
[
2
]
(see e.g.,
[
22
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
27
]
,
and also
[
30
]
,
[
35
]
for other characterizations).
In this subsection,
we present some results that can be deduced from such a characterization.

Let
x,y\in M
,
and let
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
.
The
heat kernel measure

\nu_{(x,t)}(y,s)
is defined by

d\nu_{(x,t)}(y,s):=G(x,t;y,s)\,dm_{s}(y).

We possess the following logarithmic Sobolev inequality (see
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
21
, Theorem 1.5]
,
[
27
, Theorem 1.3]
, and cf.
[
22
, Theorem 1.10 (2)]
):

Theorem 2.13

(
[
29
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
27
]
)
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
be a super Ricci flow.
Then for all
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
\int_{M}\,u^{2}(y)\,d\nu_{(x,t)}(y,s)=1
we have

\int_{M}\,u^{2}\log u^{2}(y)\,d\nu_{(x,t)}(y,s)\leq 4(t-s)\int_{M}\,|\nabla u|^{2}(y)\,d\nu_{(x,t)}(y,s).

Theorem
2.13
together with the method of the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 1.13]
implies the following Gaussian concentration inequality (cf.
[
22
, Theorem 1.13]
):

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
be a super Ricci flow.
Let
\nu:=\nu_{(x,t)}(\cdot,s)
.
Then for all
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset M
we have

\nu(\Omega_{1})\nu(\Omega_{2})\leq\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{s}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})}{8(t-s)}\right).

Corollary
2.14
together with the method of the proof of
[
22
, (1.20)]
tells us the following average bound for the heat kernel (cf.
[
22
, (1.20)]
):

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
be a super Ricci flow.
Let
x,y\in M
,
and let
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
.
For
c>0
we assume
m(B(y,c\sqrt{t-s},s))\geq\kappa(t-s)^{n/2}
.
Then

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\frac{1}{m(B(y,c\sqrt{t-s},s))}\int_{B(y,c\sqrt{t-s},s)}G(x,t;z,s)\,dm_{s}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{B(x,c\sqrt{t-s},s)}G(x,t;z,s)\,dm_{s}(z)\bigg)^{-1}\frac{\kappa^{-1}\,e^{c^{2}/2}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{16(t-s)}\right).

In
[
22
]
,
a similar inequality has been stated only for
c=1
.
For the sake of our argument,
we present a slightly general form.
The method is completely same as that of
[
22
, (1.20)]
.

3.
Distance distortion estimate

In what follows,
we denote by
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
an
n
-dimensional compact super Ricci flow with
T<+\infty
satisfying
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
for all vector fields
V
.
Unless otherwise stated,
positive constants which appear in the proof will depend only on
n,T
and
g(0)
(cf. Remark
1.2
).

The aim of this section is to prove a distance distortion estimate.
We start with the following key lemma,
which has been obtained by Bamler-Zhang
[
7
]
for Ricci flow (see
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
):

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C_{n}>0
depending only on
n
such that the following holds:
For
t_{0}\in[0,T)
,
let
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times(t_{0},T))
be a positive solution to the heat equation.
For
t_{1},t_{2}\in(t_{0},T)
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
,
we assume
u\leq A
for
A>0
on
M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]
.
Then for all
x\in M
and
t\in(t_{1},t_{2}]
we have

\left(|\Delta u|+\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}-AH\right)(x,t)\leq\frac{C_{n}A}{t-t_{1}}.

Proof.

We may assume
A=1
.
We first estimate

L_{1}:=-\Delta u+\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}-H.

Using the Bochner formula,
we deduce the following (cf.
[
43
, Proposition 5.1.1]
):

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\Delta u

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\Delta u-\Delta\partial_{t}u=2\langle\nabla^{2}u,h\rangle+2\operatorname{div}h(\nabla u)-\langle\nabla H,\nabla u\rangle,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{u}\left(\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{du\otimes du}{u}\right|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-h(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right),

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)H

\displaystyle=2|h|^{2}+\mathcal{D}(0).

Therefore, by
u\leq 1,\operatorname{Ric}\geq h
and
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)L_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{u}\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{du\otimes du}{u}\right|^{2}-2\langle\nabla^{2}u,h\rangle-2|h|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2\operatorname{div}h(\nabla u)+\langle\nabla H,\nabla u\rangle-\frac{2}{u}\left(\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-h(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right)-\mathcal{D}(0)

\displaystyle\leq-\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{du\otimes du}{u}\right|^{2}-2\langle\nabla^{2}u,h\rangle-2|h|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2\operatorname{div}h(\nabla u)+\langle\nabla H,\nabla u\rangle-(\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-h(\nabla u,\nabla u))-\mathcal{D}(0)

\displaystyle=-\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{du\otimes du}{u}+h\right|^{2}-\left|\frac{du\otimes du}{u}+h\right|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{D}(\nabla u)+\mathcal{D}(0))

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{n}\left(\Delta u-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}+H\right)^{2}+\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}=-\frac{1}{n}L^{2}_{1}+\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}.

Theorem
2.9
tells us that

(3.2)

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\leq\frac{u}{t-t_{1}}\log\frac{1}{u}\leq\frac{1}{e(t-t_{1})}

on
M\times(t_{1},t_{2}]
,
and hence

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)L_{1}\leq-\frac{1}{n}L^{2}_{1}+\frac{1}{e^{2}(t-t_{1})^{2}}.

Let
C_{n,1}>0
be a positive constant determined by

\frac{C_{n,1}+e^{-2}}{C_{n,1}^{2}}=\frac{1}{n}

depending only on
n
.
Note that

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\left(\frac{C_{n,1}}{t-t_{1}}\right)=-\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{C_{n,1}}{t-t_{1}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{e^{2}(t-t_{1})^{2}}.

It follows that

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\left(L_{1}-\frac{C_{n,1}}{t-t_{1}}\right)\leq-\frac{1}{n}\left(L_{1}+\frac{C_{n,1}}{t-t_{1}}\right)\left(L_{1}-\frac{C_{n,1}}{t-t_{1}}\right).

The maximum principle leads us to
L_{1}\leq C_{n,1}/(t-t_{1})
.

We next estimate

L_{2}:=\Delta u+\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}-H.

Using (
3.1
),
u\leq 1,\operatorname{Ric}\geq h
and
\mathcal{D}(V)\geq 0
again, one can calculate

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)L_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{u}\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{du\otimes du}{u}\right|^{2}+2\langle\nabla^{2}u,h\rangle-2|h|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\operatorname{div}h(\nabla u)-\langle\nabla H,\nabla u\rangle-\frac{2}{u}\left(\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-h(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right)-\mathcal{D}(0)

\displaystyle\leq-\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{du\otimes du}{u}\right|^{2}+2\langle\nabla^{2}u,h\rangle-2|h|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2\operatorname{div}h(-\nabla u)+\langle\nabla H,(-\nabla u)\rangle-\left(\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-h(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right)-\mathcal{D}(0)

\displaystyle=-\left|\nabla^{2}u-\frac{du\otimes du}{u}-h\right|^{2}-\left|\frac{du\otimes du}{u}-h\right|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{D}(-\nabla u)+\mathcal{D}(0)\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{n}\left(\Delta u-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}-H\right)^{2}+\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2n}\left(L_{2}-4\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2n}L^{2}_{2}+\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}\leq-\frac{1}{2n}L^{2}_{2}+\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)\frac{|\nabla u|^{4}}{u^{2}}.

The estimate (
3.2
) implies

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)L_{2}\leq-\frac{1}{n}L^{2}_{2}+\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)\frac{1}{e^{2}(t-t_{1})^{2}}

over
M\times(t_{1},t_{2}]
.
Let
C_{n,2}>0
be a constant determined by

C^{-1}_{n,2}+\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)\frac{1}{e^{2}}C^{-2}_{n,2}=\frac{1}{2n},

which depends only on
n
.
We notice that

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\left(\frac{C_{n,2}}{t-t_{1}}\right)=-\frac{1}{2n}\left(\frac{C_{n,2}}{t-t_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)\frac{1}{e^{2}(t-t_{1})^{2}};

in particular,

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\left(L_{2}-\frac{C_{n,2}}{t-t_{1}}\right)\leq-\frac{1}{2n}\left(L_{2}+\frac{C_{n,2}}{t-t_{1}}\right)\left(L_{2}-\frac{C_{n,2}}{t-t_{1}}\right).

Due to the maximum principle,
we obtain
L_{2}\leq C_{n,2}/(t-t_{1})
.
This completes the proof.

\Box

We are now in a position to prove the following distance distortion estimate,
which has been established by Bamler-Zhang
[
7
]
for Ricci flow (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
):

Theorem 3.2
.

There exists a positive constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending only on
n,T
and
g(0)
such that the following holds:
For
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
r_{0}\in(0,\sqrt{t_{0}}]
,
we assume
H\leq r_{0}^{-2}
.
Let
x_{0},y_{0}\in M
satisfy
d_{t_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})\geq r_{0}
.
Then for all
t\in[0,T)
with
|t-t_{0}|\leq\alpha r^{2}_{0}
,

\alpha d_{t_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq d_{t}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq\alpha^{-1}d_{t_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0}).

Proof.

By the argument of
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
,
it suffices to prove the upper bound for
d_{t}(x_{0},y_{0})
only in the case of
d_{t_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq 2r_{0}
.
By the parabolic rescaling,
it is enough to show the claim when
r_{0}=1
(see Remark
2.2
).
Then we have
t_{0}\geq 1
and
H\leq 1
.
We further possess
H\geq-n
on
M\times[t_{0}-1/2,t_{0}+1/2]
by virtue of Proposition
2.1
.
Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
be a minimal geodesic from
x_{0}
to
y_{0}
with respect to
g(t_{0})
.
By Proposition
2.7
,

(3.3)

\ell_{(x_{0},t_{0})}\left(z,t_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq\frac{n}{2}

for some
z\in M
.
We put
G(x,t):=G(x,t;z,t_{0}-1/2)
.

Due to Proposition
2.5
,
on
M\times[t_{0}-1/4,t_{0}+1/4]
it holds that

(3.4)

G(x,t)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(t-(t_{0}-1/2))^{n/2}}\leq C_{2}.

Thanks to Proposition
2.8
and (
3.3
),

(3.5)

G(x_{0},t_{0})\geq\frac{1}{(4\pi(t_{0}-(t_{0}-1/2))^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\ell_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(z,t_{0}-1/2)\right)\geq C_{3}.

By (
3.4
), Corollary
2.10
, (
3.5
) and the assumption
d_{t_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq 2
,
for all
x\in\gamma([0,1])
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle G(x,t_{0})

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}_{2}G(x_{0},t_{0})^{2}\,\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{t_{0}}(x_{0},x)}{2(t_{0}-(t_{0}-1/4))}\right)

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}_{2}\,C^{2}_{3}\,\exp\left(-2d^{2}_{t_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})\right)\geq e^{-8}\,C^{-1}_{2}\,C^{2}_{3}=:C_{4}.

For the dimensional constant
C_{n}>0
obtained in Lemma
3.1
,
we set

\alpha_{0}:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{8},\frac{C_{4}}{4C_{2}(1+8C_{n})}\right\}.

Using (
3.6
), (
3.4
), Lemma
3.1
,
H\leq 1
,
for all
x\in\gamma([0,1])
and
t\in[t_{0}-\alpha_{0},t_{0}+\alpha_{0}]
,
we see

(3.7)

\displaystyle G(x,t)

\displaystyle\geq G(x,t_{0})-\int^{t_{0}+\alpha_{0}}_{t_{0}-\alpha_{0}}\,\left|\partial_{t}G\right|(x,t)\,dt=G(x,t_{0})-\int^{t_{0}+\alpha_{0}}_{t_{0}-\alpha_{0}}\,\left|\Delta G\right|(x,t)\,dt

\displaystyle\geq C_{4}-C_{2}\int^{t_{0}+\alpha_{0}}_{t_{0}-\alpha_{0}}\,\left(H(x,t)+\frac{C_{n}}{t-(t_{0}-1/4)}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\geq C_{4}-2\alpha_{0}\,C_{2}\left(1+\frac{C_{n}}{1/4-\alpha_{0}}\right)\geq C_{4}-2\alpha_{0}\,C_{2}\left(1+8C_{n}\right)\geq\frac{C_{4}}{2}.

By (
3.4
), Corollary
2.10
, (
3.7
),
for all
s\in[0,1],t\in[t_{0}-\alpha_{0}/2,t_{0}+\alpha_{0}/2]
and
x\in B(\gamma(s),\sqrt{\alpha_{0}},t)
we have

G(x,t)\geq C^{-1}_{2}G(\gamma(s),t)^{2}\,\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{t}(x,\gamma(s))}{2(t-(t_{0}-\alpha_{0}))}\right)\geq\frac{C^{-1}_{2}C^{2}_{4}}{4}e^{-1}=:C_{5}.

Let
\{x_{i}\}^{N}_{i=1}
denote a maximal
(2\sqrt{\alpha_{0}})
-separated set in
\gamma([0,1])
with respect to
g(t)
.
The argument in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
tells us that
d_{t}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq 4N\sqrt{\alpha_{0}}
.
On
(t_{0}-1/2,t_{0}+1/2]
, we see

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\,G\,dm=\int_{M}\,\left(\Delta G-H\,G\right)\,dm\leq n\int_{M}\,G\,dm

since
H\geq-n
on
M\times[t_{0}-1/2,t_{0}+1/2]
, and hence

(3.8)

\int_{M}\,G\,dm\leq\exp\left(n(t-(t_{0}-1/2))\right)\leq e^{n}.

Combining (
3.8
) and Theorem
2.4
,
we see that
if
t\in[t_{0}-\alpha_{0}/2,t_{0}+\alpha_{0}/2]
,
then

e^{n}\geq\int_{M}\,G(\cdot,t)\,dm\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{B(x_{i},\sqrt{\alpha_{0}},t)}\,G(\cdot,t)\,dm\geq N(\kappa\alpha^{n/2}_{0})C_{5}.

We conclude that for all
t\in[t_{0}-\alpha_{0}/2,t_{0}+\alpha_{0}/2]
,

d_{t}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq\frac{4e^{n}}{\kappa\alpha^{(n-1)/2}_{0}C_{5}}<\frac{8e^{n}}{\kappa\alpha^{(n-1)/2}_{0}C_{5}}\,d_{t_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0}).

Setting

\alpha:=\min\left\{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2},\frac{\kappa\alpha^{(n-1)/2}_{0}C_{5}}{8e^{n}}\right\},

we arrive at the desired upper bound.
Thus we complete the proof.

\Box

4.
Cutoff function

Based on Theorem
3.2
,
we construct the following cutoff function,
which has been obtained by Bamler-Zhang
[
7
]
for Ricci flow (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
):

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists a constant
\rho\in(0,1)
depending only on
n,T
and
g(0)
such that the following holds:
Let
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
r_{0}\in(0,\sqrt{t_{0}}]
.
We assume
H\leq r_{0}^{-2}
.
Then for any
x_{0}\in M
and
\tau\in(0,\rho^{2}r_{0}^{2}]
,
there is
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}])
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

0\leq\phi<1
;

(2)

\phi\geq\rho
on
B(x_{0},\rho r_{0},t_{0})\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]
;

(3)

\phi=0
on
(M\setminus B(x_{0},r_{0},t_{0}))\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]
;

(4)

|\nabla\phi|\leq r_{0}^{-1}
and
|\partial_{t}\phi|+|\Delta\phi|\leq r_{0}^{-2}
.

Proof.

By the rescaling,
we may assume
r_{0}=1
(see Remark
2.2
).
We possess
t_{0}\geq 1
and
H\leq 1
.
Moreover,
H\geq-n
on
M\times[t_{0}-1/2,t_{0}]
by Proposition
2.1
.
Let
\theta\in(0,1/2),\tau\in(0,\theta/4)
, which will be determined later.

Due to Proposition
2.7
,
there exists
z\in M
such that

\ell_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(z,t_{0}-\theta)\leq\frac{n}{2}.

Set
G(x,t):=G(x,t;z,t_{0}-\theta)
.
Thanks to Proposition
2.8
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle G(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(4\pi(t_{0}-(t_{0}-\theta))^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\ell_{(x_{0},t_{0})}(z,t_{0}-\theta)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(4\pi\theta)^{n/2}}e^{-n/2}=:C_{n}\theta^{-n/2},

where
C_{n}>0
depends only on
n
.
We define

\Omega:=\left\{x\in M\mid G(x,t_{0})>C_{n}\theta^{-n/2}/2\right\}.

From (
4.1
) we derive
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega
stand for the connected component of
\Omega
containing
x_{0}
. We prove that
for any sufficiently small
\theta
,
we have
\Omega_{0}\subset B(x_{0},1,t_{0})
by contradiction.
Let us assume that

\Omega_{0}
is not contained in
B(x_{0},1,t_{0})
.
Then we can take a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega_{0}
from
x_{0}
to a point in
\partial B(x_{0},1,t_{0})\cap\Omega_{0}
.
Let
\{x_{i}\}^{N}_{i=1}
be a maximal
(2\sqrt{\theta})
-separated set in
\gamma([0,1])
with respect to
g(t_{0})
.
The argument in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
tells us that

(4.2)

N\geq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\theta}}.

Proposition
2.5
tells us that
on
M\times[t_{0}-\theta/2,t_{0}]
we see

(4.3)

G(x,t)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(t-(t_{0}-\theta))^{n/2}}\leq C_{2}\theta^{-n/2}.

Due to (
4.3
), Corollary
2.10
,
for all
y\in\Omega_{0}
and
z\in B(y,\sqrt{\theta},t_{0})
we have

(4.4)

G(z,t_{0})\geq C^{-1}_{2}\,\theta^{n/2}\,G(y,t_{0})^{2}\,\exp\left(-\frac{d_{t_{0}}(y,z)^{2}}{2\theta}\right)\geq\frac{e^{-1/2}}{2}C^{-1}_{2}C_{n}\theta^{-n/2}=:C_{3}\theta^{-n/2}.

On the other hand,
on
(t_{0}-\theta,t_{0}]
we see

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\,G\,dm=\int_{M}\,\left(\Delta G-H\,G\right)\,dm\leq n\int_{M}\,G\,dm

since
H\geq-n
on
M\times[t_{0}-\theta,t_{0}]
, and hence

(4.5)

\int_{M}\,G\,dm\leq\exp\left(n(t-(t_{0}-\theta))\right)\leq e^{n\theta}\leq e^{n/2}.

Combining (
4.2
), (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and Theorem
2.4
,
we obtain

e^{n/2}\geq\int_{M}G(\cdot,t_{0})dm\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{B(x_{i},\sqrt{\theta},t_{0})}G(\cdot,t_{0})\,dm\geq N(C_{3}\theta^{-n/2})(\kappa\theta^{n/2})=N\kappa C_{3}\geq\frac{\kappa C_{3}}{4\sqrt{\theta}}.

If
\theta<16^{-1}e^{-n}\kappa^{2}C^{2}_{3}
,
then this is a contradiction.
Hereafter,
we fix
\theta\in(0,16^{-1}e^{-n}\kappa^{2}C^{2}_{3})
such that
\Omega_{0}\subset B(x_{0},1,t_{0})
.

We now use (
4.3
) and Lemma
3.1
.
On
M\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]
it holds that

(4.6)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}G|(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\theta^{-n/2}\left(H(x,t)+\frac{C_{4}}{t-(t_{0}-\theta/2)}\right)\leq C_{2}\theta^{-n/2}(1+4C_{4}\theta^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(1+4C_{4})\theta^{-n/2-1}=:C_{5}\theta^{-n/2-1}.

Note that

C_{5}>C_{2}
.
Let
\tau\in(0,0.1C_{n}C^{-1}_{5}\theta)
.
Then on
M\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]
,

|G(x,t)-G(x,t_{0})|\leq\int^{t_{0}}_{t_{0}-\tau}\,|\partial_{t}G|(x,t)\,dt\leq C_{5}\theta^{-n/2-1}\tau<0.1C_{n}\theta^{-n/2};

in particular, on
\partial\Omega_{0}\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]
we possess

G(x,t)<G(x,t_{0})+0.1C_{n}\theta^{-n/2}=0.5C_{n}\theta^{-n/2}+0.1C_{n}\theta^{-n/2}=0.6C_{n}\theta^{-n/2}.

Now,
we can define a function
\hat{\psi}\in C^{0}(M\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}])
by

\hat{\psi}(x,t):=\begin{cases}\max\{G(x,t)-0.6C_{n}\theta^{-n/2}\}&\textrm{on}\ \Omega_{0}\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}],\\
0&\textrm{on}\ (M\setminus\Omega_{0})\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}],\end{cases}

which is compactly supported on
\Omega_{0}\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]
.
This function enjoys the following properties:

(1)

0\leq\hat{\psi}<C_{5}\theta^{-n/2}
;

(2)

\hat{\psi}(x_{0},t_{0})\geq 0.4C_{n}\theta^{-n/2}
;

(3)

|\nabla\hat{\psi}|\leq 2C_{5}\theta^{-n/2-1/2}
;

(4)

|\partial_{t}\hat{\psi}|+|\Delta\hat{\psi}|\leq 2C_{5}\theta^{-n/2-1}
.

The first inequality follows from (
4.3
) and
C_{2}<C_{5}
.
The second one is a consequence of
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
By (
4.3
) and Theorem
2.9
,
on
M\times[t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}]
,

|\nabla G|(x,t)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-(t_{0}-\theta/2)}}\sqrt{\log\frac{C_{2}\theta^{-n/2}}{G(x,t)}}G(x,t)\leq 2C_{2}\theta^{-n/2-1/2}<2C_{5}\theta^{-n/2-1/2},

and this implies the third one.
The fourth one can be derived from (
4.6
).
If we further define
\tilde{\phi}:=32^{-1}C^{-1}_{5}\theta^{n/2+1}\hat{\psi}
,
then it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

0\leq\tilde{\phi}<1
;

(2)

\tilde{\phi}(x_{0},t_{0})\geq C_{6}\,\theta
;

(3)

|\nabla\tilde{\phi}|\leq 1/8
;

(4)

|\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}|+|\Delta\tilde{\phi}|\leq 1/8
.

Once we obtain this function,
we can conclude the desired assertion by the same argument as in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
.
We complete the proof.

\Box

5.
Mean value inequality

In the present section,
we produce a mean value inequality for conjugate heat equation.
To do so,
we first yield the following integral estimate based on the Moser iteration argument,
which has been obtained by Bamler-Zhang
[
7
]
for Ricci flow (see
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
):

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
.
Then there are positive constants
\beta\in(0,1)
and
C>0
depending only on
n,T,g(0)
and
p
such that the following holds:
For
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
r_{0}\in(0,\sqrt{t_{0}}]
,
we assume
H\leq r^{-2}_{0}
.
Let
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times[t_{0},t_{0}+r_{0}^{2}])
denote a positive solution to the conjugate heat equation.
Then for all
x_{0}\in M
we have

\left(\int_{Q^{+}(x_{0},\beta r_{0},t_{0})}u^{p}\,dm\,dt\right)^{1/p}\leq\frac{C}{r_{0}^{(n+2)(p-2)/2p}}\left(\int_{Q^{+}(x_{0},r_{0},t_{0})}u^{2}\,dm\,dt\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

By the rescaling,
we may assume
r_{0}=1
(see Remark
2.2
).
It holds that
t_{0}\geq 1
and
H\leq 1
.
Moreover,

H\geq-n
on
M\times[t_{0}-1/2,t_{0}+1]
in view of Proposition
2.1
.
Let
\alpha,\rho\in(0,1)
be the constants obtained in Theorems
3.2
and
4.1
,
respectively.
We define

\theta:=\alpha\in(0,1).

Note that
(\rho\theta)^{2}\in(0,\alpha)
.
By Theorem
3.2
we have

(5.1)

B(x_{0},\theta,t_{1})\subset B(x_{0},1,t_{2})

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[t_{0},t_{0}+(\rho\theta)^{2}]
.
We further set

\sigma:=\frac{\alpha\rho\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\in(0,\rho\theta).

Notice that

2\sigma^{2}\in(0,\alpha(\rho\theta)^{2})
.
Using Theorem
3.2
again,
we obtain

(5.2)

B(x_{0},\sigma,t_{1})\subset B(x_{0},\rho\theta,t_{2})

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}]
.
With the help of Theorem
4.1
,
there is
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}])
such that the following holds:

(1)

0\leq\phi<1
;

(2)

\phi\geq\rho
on
B^{\prime}\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}]
;

(3)

\phi=0
on
(M\setminus B)\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}]
;

(4)

|\nabla\phi|\leq\theta^{-1}
and
|\partial_{t}\phi|\leq\theta^{-2}
,

where
B:=B(x_{0},\theta,t_{0}+2\sigma^{2})
and
B^{\prime}:=B(x_{0},\rho\theta,t_{0}+2\sigma^{2})
.
By (
5.1
) and (
5.2
),
we possess

(5.3)

Q^{+}(x_{0},\sigma,t_{0})\subset B^{\prime}\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}]\subset B\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}]\subset Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0}).

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}([t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}])
be a function satisfying the following properties:

(1)

0\leq\eta\leq 1
;

(2)

\eta\equiv 1
on
[t_{0},t_{0}+\sigma^{2}]
;

(3)

\eta(t_{0}+2\sigma^{2})=0
;

(4)

|\eta^{\prime}|\leq C_{1}
.

Then we define
\psi(x,t):=\eta(t)\phi(x,t)
.
Note that
\psi\geq\rho
on
Q^{+}(x_{0},\sigma,t_{0})
.

Let
p\geq 2
.
Since
u
is a solution to the conjugate heat equation,
we see

\partial_{t}u^{p/2}+\Delta u^{p/2}-\frac{p}{2}Hu^{p/2}=\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{p}{2}-1\right)u^{\frac{p}{2}-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq 0.

On
[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}]
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\,u^{p}\psi^{2}\,dm

\displaystyle=2\int_{M}(\partial_{t}u^{p/2})u^{p/2}\psi^{2}\,dm+2\int_{M}u^{p}(\partial_{t}\psi)\psi\,dm-\int_{M}\,u^{p}\psi^{2}H\,dm

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{M}\left(-\Delta u^{p/2}+\frac{p}{2}Hu^{p/2}\right)u^{p/2}\psi^{2}dm-C_{2}\int_{B}u^{p}\,dm

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{M}\,\langle\nabla u^{p/2},\psi\,\nabla(u^{p/2}\psi)+(u^{p/2}\psi)\nabla\psi\rangle\,dm-C_{3}\int_{B}u^{p}\,dm

\displaystyle=2\int_{M}\langle\nabla(u^{p/2}\psi)-u^{p/2}\nabla\psi,\nabla(u^{p/2}\psi)+u^{p/2}\nabla\psi\rangle\,dm-C_{3}\int_{B}u^{p}\,dm

\displaystyle=2\int_{M}\big|\nabla(u^{p/2}\psi)\big|^{2}dm-2\int_{M}\,u^{p}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dm-C_{3}\int_{B}u^{p}\,dm

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{M}\big|\nabla(u^{p/2}\psi)\big|^{2}\,dm-C_{3}\int_{B}u^{p}\,dm.

From Proposition
2.3
,
we conclude

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}u^{p}\psi^{2}\,dm\geq C_{4}\bigg(\int_{M}\,u^{\frac{pn}{n-2}}\psi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dm\bigg)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}-C_{5}\int_{B}\,u^{p}\,dm.

Let us integrate this inequality from a fixed
t_{1}\in[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}]
to
t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}
.
We deduce

\displaystyle\quad\,\,-\int_{B^{\prime}}\,u^{p}(\cdot,t_{1})\psi^{2}(\cdot,t_{1})\,dm_{t_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{M}u^{p}(\cdot,t_{1})\psi^{2}(\cdot,t_{1})\,dm_{t_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq C_{4}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\bigg(\int_{M}u^{\frac{pn}{n-2}}\psi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dm\bigg)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}dt-C_{5}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\int_{B}\,u^{p}\,dm\,dt.

This leads us to

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}]}\int_{B^{\prime}}u^{p}(\cdot,t)\psi^{2}(\cdot,t)\,dm_{t}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}\,\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\int_{B}\,u^{p}\,dm\,dt,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\bigg(\int_{B^{\prime}}u^{\frac{pn}{n-2}}\psi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dm\bigg)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{7}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\int_{B}\,u^{p}\,dm\,dt.

By the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\,\int_{B^{\prime}}u^{p\left(1+\frac{2}{n}\right)}\psi^{2\left(1+\frac{2}{n}\right)}\,dm\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\,\int_{B^{\prime}}\,\left(u^{p}\psi^{2}\right)\left(u^{\frac{2p}{n}}\psi^{\frac{4}{n}}\right)\,dm\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\bigg(\int_{B^{\prime}}u^{\frac{pn}{n-2}}\psi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dm\bigg)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\bigg(\int_{B^{\prime}}u^{p}\psi^{2}\,dm\bigg)^{\frac{2}{n}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\bigg(\int_{B^{\prime}}u^{\frac{pn}{n-2}}\psi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dm\bigg)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}dt\right]\left[C_{6}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\int_{B}u^{p}dm\,dt\right]^{\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(C_{8}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+2\sigma^{2}}\int_{B}u^{p}\,dm\,dt\bigg)^{1+\frac{2}{n}}.

In view of (
5.3
) and
\psi\geq\rho
on
Q^{+}(x_{0},\sigma,t_{0})
,
we arrive at

\bigg(\int_{Q^{+}(x_{0},\sigma,t_{0})}u^{p\left(1+\frac{2}{n}\right)}\,dm\,dt\bigg)^{1/p\left(1+\frac{2}{n}\right)}\leq C_{9}\bigg(\int_{Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0})}u^{p}\,dm\,dt\bigg)^{1/p}.

Once we obtain this estimate,
we can conclude the desired one by the same argument as in the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 4.2]
.

\Box

We give a proof of the following mean value inequality,
which has been formulated by Bamler-Zhang
[
7
]
for Ricci flow (see
[
7
, Lemma 4.2]
):

Theorem 5.2
.

There exist positive constants
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
C>0
depending only on
n,T
and
g(0)
such that the following holds:
For
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
r_{0}\in(0,\sqrt{t_{0}}]
,
we assume
H\leq r^{-2}_{0}
.
Let
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times[t_{0},t_{0}+r_{0}^{2}])
be a positive solution to the conjugate heat equation.
Then for all
x_{0}\in M
we have

\sup_{Q^{+}(x_{0},\gamma r_{0},t_{0})}u^{2}\leq\frac{C}{r_{0}^{n+2}}\,\int_{Q^{+}(x_{0},r_{0},t_{0})}u^{2}\,dm\,dt.

Proof.

By the rescaling,
we may assume
r_{0}=1
(see Remark
2.2
).
We have
t_{0}\geq 1
and
H\leq 1
.
Furthermore,
we have
H\geq-n
on
M\times[t_{0}-1/2,t_{0}+1]
by Proposition
2.1
.
Let
\alpha,\rho\in(0,1)
be the constants obtained in Theorems
3.2
and
4.1
,
respectively.
Let
\beta\in(0,1)
be the constant obtained in Lemma
5.1
for a fixed
p>n+2
.
We define

\theta:=\alpha\beta\in(0,1).

Note that
(\rho\theta)^{2}\in(0,\alpha\beta^{2})
.
By Theorem
3.2
,
it holds that

(5.4)

B(x_{0},\theta,t_{1})\subset B(x_{0},\beta,t_{2})

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[t_{0},t_{0}+(\rho\theta)^{2}]
.
We further define

\gamma:=\frac{\alpha\rho\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\in(0,\rho\theta).

Notice that

2\gamma^{2}\in(0,\alpha(\rho\theta)^{2})
.
In virtue of Theorem
3.2
,
we possess

(5.5)

B(x_{0},\gamma,t_{1})\subset B(x_{0},\rho\theta,t_{2})

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[t_{0},t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}]
.
By Theorem
4.1
,
there is
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}])
such that the following holds:

(1)

0\leq\phi<1
;

(2)

\phi\geq\rho
on
B^{\prime}\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}]
;

(3)

\phi=0
on
(M\setminus B)\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}]
;

(4)

|\nabla\phi|\leq\theta^{-1}
and
|\partial_{t}\phi|+|\Delta\phi|\leq\theta^{-2}
,

where
B:=B(x_{0},\theta,t_{0}+2\gamma^{2})
and
B^{\prime}:=B(x_{0},\rho\,\theta,t_{0}+2\gamma^{2})
.
From (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we deduce

(5.6)

\displaystyle Q^{+}(x_{0},\gamma,t_{0})\subset B^{\prime}\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}]\subset B\times[t_{0},t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}]\subset Q^{+}(x_{0},\beta,t_{0}).

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}([t_{0},t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}])
be a function satisfying the following properties:

(1)

0\leq\eta\leq 1
;

(2)

\eta\equiv 1
on
[t_{0},t_{0}+\gamma^{2}]
;

(3)

\eta(t_{0}+2\gamma^{2})=0
;

(4)

|\eta^{\prime}|\leq C_{1}
.

Then we define
\psi(x,t):=\eta(t)\phi(x,t)
.
In view of (
5.6
),
we notice
\psi\geq\rho
on
Q^{+}(x_{0},\gamma,t_{0})
.
For a fixed
(x,t)\in Q^{+}(x_{0},\gamma,t_{0})
, we set
G(z,l):=G(z,l;x,t)
.
Then

(u\psi)(x,t)=-\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{M}G\big(u\Delta\psi+u\partial_{l}\psi+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\psi\rangle\big)dm\,dl.

Here we used

\partial_{l}(u\psi)+\Delta(u\psi)-H(u\psi)=u\Delta\psi+u\partial_{l}\psi+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla\psi\rangle,

which is a consequence of the fact that
u
is a solution to the conjugate heat equation.
Integration by parts yields

(u\psi)(x,t)=-\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{M}Gu(-\Delta\psi+\partial_{l}\psi)+2u\langle\nabla G,\nabla\psi\rangle\,dm\,dl.

From the properties of
\psi
and (
5.6
), it follows that

(5.7)

(u\psi)(x,t)\leq C_{2}\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}\,\left(Gu+u|\nabla G|\right)\,dm\,dl=:C_{2}(I_{1}+I_{2}).

We first estimate
I_{1}
.
By the Hölder inequality, (
5.6
) and Lemma
5.1
,

(5.8)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}G^{q}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}u^{p}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}G^{q}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\int_{Q^{+}(x_{0},\beta,t_{0})}u^{p}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}G^{q}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/q}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0}))}

for
q=p/(p-1)
.
By Proposition
2.5
,
for all
l\in(t,t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}]
we have

(5.9)

G(z,l)\leq\frac{C_{4}}{(l-t)^{n/2}}.

We also see

\frac{d}{dl}\int_{M}\,G\,dm=-\int_{M}\,H\,G\,dm\leq n\int_{M}G\,dm

since
H\geq-n
on
M\times[t_{0}-1/2,t_{0}+1]
;
in particular,
for all
l\in(t,t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}]
,

(5.10)

\int_{M}G\,dm\leq\exp(n(l-t))\leq C_{5}.

Combining (
5.8
), (
5.9
) and (
5.10
),
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}G\frac{1}{(l-t)^{(q-1)n/2}}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/q}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{7}\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\frac{1}{(l-t)^{(q-1)n/2}}\,dl\bigg)^{1/q}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0}))}.

Since
p>n+2
, we have
(q-1)n/2<1
;
in particular,

(5.11)

I_{1}\leq C_{8}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0}))}.

We next derive a bound of
I_{2}
.
The estimate (
5.9
) implies that for each
l\in(t,t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}]
,

A_{l}:=\sup_{M\times\left[\frac{l+t}{2},l\right]}G\leq\frac{C_{9}}{(l-t)^{n/2}}.

Using Theorem
2.9
,
we possess

\frac{|\nabla G|^{2}}{G^{2}}(z,l)\leq\frac{2}{l-t}\log\frac{A_{l}}{G(z,l)}.

Since
G(z,l)/A_{l}\leq 1
,
we have

|\nabla G|^{q}\leq G\frac{C_{10}A_{l}^{q-1}}{(l-t)^{q/2}}\bigg(\frac{G}{A_{l}}\bigg)^{q-1}\bigg({\log\frac{A_{l}}{G}}\bigg)^{q/2}\leq G\frac{C_{11}A_{l}^{q-1}}{(l-t)^{q/2}}\leq\frac{C_{12}}{(l-t)^{((n+1)q-n)/2}}G

at
(z,l)
,
where we used a fact that a function
\varphi(\xi):=\xi^{q-1}(\log(1/\xi))^{q/2}
takes its maximum value
e^{-q/2}(q/2(q-1))^{q/2}
at
\xi=e^{-q/2(q-1)}
on
(0,1]
.
Using the Hölder inequality, this gradient bound, (
5.10
), Lemma
5.1
with (
5.6
), we conclude

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}|\nabla G|^{q}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}u^{p}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\int_{B}|\nabla G|^{q}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\int_{Q^{+}(x_{0},\beta,t_{0})}u^{p}\,dm\,dl\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{13}\,\bigg(\int_{t}^{t_{0}+2\gamma^{2}}\frac{1}{(l-t)^{((n+1)q-n)/2}}\,dl\bigg)^{1/q}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0}))}.

From
p>n+2
, we deduce
((n+1)q-n)/2<1
, and hence

(5.12)

I_{2}\leq C_{14}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0}))}.

Combining (
5.7
), (
5.11
), (
5.12
),
we arrive at

(u\psi)(x,t)\leq C_{15}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q^{+}(x_{0},1,t_{0}))}.

The desired assertion follows from
\psi(x,t)\geq\rho
.
We complete the proof.

\Box

]

6.
Gaussian heat kernel estimate

In this section,
we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.
First,
we prove the lower bound (
1.3
).
To do so,
we prepare the following lemma:

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
A>0
,
there is a constant
B>1
depending only on
n,T,g(0)
and
A
such that the following holds:
We assume
H\leq H_{1}
for
H_{1}>0
.
For
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we suppose
t-s\leq A\,H_{1}^{-1}
and
s\geq(t-s)/A
.
If
d_{t}(x,y)\geq B\sqrt{t-s}
,
then
d_{s}(x,y)\geq B^{-1}d_{t}(x,y)
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be the constant obtained in Theorem
3.2
.
We first consider the case of
A\in(0,\alpha]
.
We set

\theta:=\alpha^{-1}A\in(0,1],\quad B:=\alpha^{-1}A^{-1/2},\quad r_{0}:=A^{-1/2}\sqrt{t-s}.

It holds that

r_{0}\leq\sqrt{s}\leq\sqrt{t},\quad H\leq H_{1}\leq\frac{A}{t-s}=r^{-2}_{0},\quad t-s\leq\theta^{-1}(t-s)\leq\alpha r^{2}_{0},\quad d_{t}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{-1}r_{0}\geq r_{0},

and hence one can apply Theorem
3.2
.
It follows that

d_{s}(x,y)\geq\alpha d_{t}(x,y)\geq\alpha A^{1/2}d_{t}(x,y)=B^{-1}d_{t}(x,y),

which is a desired estimate.

We next consider the remaining case of
A\in(\alpha,\infty)
.
We put

\theta:=\alpha^{-1}A\in(1,\infty),\quad B:=\alpha^{-(\theta+2)},\quad r_{0}:=A^{-1/2}\sqrt{t-s}.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be the integer part of
\theta
,
and let
s=\tau_{N+1}\leq\tau_{N}<\dots<\tau_{1}<\tau_{0}=t
be a division of
[s,t]
such that
\tau_{i}-\tau_{i+1}=\theta^{-1}(t-s)
for
i=0,\dots,N-1
.
We see

\tau_{N}-\tau_{N+1}\in[0,\theta^{-1}(t-s))
.
We possess

r_{0}\leq\sqrt{s}\leq\sqrt{\tau_{i}}\leq\sqrt{t},\quad H\leq H_{1}\leq\frac{A}{t-s}=r^{-2}_{0},\quad\tau_{i}-\tau_{i+1}\leq\theta^{-1}(t-s)=\alpha r^{2}_{0}

for all
i=0,\dots,N
.
In view of

B\geq\alpha^{-\theta-1}A^{-1/2}\geq\alpha^{-N-1}A^{-1/2},

we see

d_{\tau_{0}}(x,y)=d_{t}(x,y)\geq B\sqrt{t-s}\geq\alpha^{-N-1}A^{-1/2}\sqrt{t-s}=\alpha^{-N-1}r_{0}\geq r_{0},

and hence Theorem
3.2
implies

d_{\tau_{1}}(x,y)\geq\alpha\,d_{\tau_{0}}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{-N}r_{0}\geq r_{0}.

Using Theorem
3.2
again,
we obtain

d_{\tau_{2}}(x,y)\geq\alpha\,d_{\tau_{1}}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{-N+1}r_{0}\geq r_{0}.

By repeating this procedure,
we arrive at

d_{\tau_{i}}(x,y)\geq\alpha d_{\tau_{i-1}}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{-N+(i-1)}r_{0}\geq r_{0}

for all
i=1,\dots,N+1
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=d_{\tau_{N+1}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha d_{\tau_{N}}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{2}d_{\tau_{N-1}}(x,y)\geq\cdots\geq\alpha^{N+1}d_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{\theta+2}d_{t}(x,y)\geq B^{-1}\sqrt{t-s}.

This proves the lemma.

\Box

Let us show the lower bound (
1.3
).

Proposition 6.2
.

For any
A>0
,
there exist positive constants
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}>0
depending only on
n,T,g(0)
and
A
such that the following holds:
We assume
H\leq H_{1}
for
H_{1}>0
.
For
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we suppose
t-s\leq A\,H_{1}^{-1}
and
s\geq(t-s)/A
.
Then we have

G(x,t;y,s)\geq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

Proof.

We possess

(6.1)

H\leq H_{1}\leq\frac{A}{t-s}.

Furthermore,
in view of Lemma
2.1
,

(6.2)

-\frac{nA}{2(t-s)}\leq-\frac{n}{2s}\leq H

on
M\times[s,t]
.
Theorem
2.11
together with (
6.1
) tells us that

(6.3)

G(x,t;y,s)\geq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{d^{2}_{t}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

Let
B>1
be the constant obtained in Lemma
6.1
.
If
d_{t}(x,y)\geq B\sqrt{t-s}
,
then
d_{s}(x,y)\geq B^{-1}\,d_{t}(x,y)
.
From (
6.3
) it follows that

G(x,t;y,s)\geq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{d^{2}_{t}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right)\geq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{B^{2}d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

On the other hand,
if
d_{t}(x,y)<B\sqrt{t-s}
,
then (
6.3
) also leads to

G(x,t;y,s)\geq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{d^{2}_{t}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right)\geq\frac{Ce^{-B^{2}}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\geq\frac{Ce^{-B^{2}}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

Thus,
we complete the proof.

\Box

We next prove the upper bound (
1.4
).
Similarly to Lemma
6.1
,
we prepare the following:

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
A>0
,
there is a constant
B>1
with
B^{-1}A^{-1/2}<1
depending only on
n,T,g(0)
and
A
such that the following holds:
Assume
H\leq H_{1}
for
H_{1}>0
.
For
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we suppose
t-s\leq A\,H_{1}^{-1}
and
s\geq(t-s)/A
.
Assume
d_{s}(x,y)\geq B\sqrt{t-s}
.
Then for all
l_{1},l_{2}\in[s,t]
we have

B^{-1}\,d_{l_{1}}(x,y)\leq d_{l_{2}}(x,y)\leq B\,d_{l_{1}}(x,y).

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be the constant obtained in Theorem
3.2
.
We begin with the case of
A\in(0,\alpha]
.
Similarly to the proof of Lemma
6.1
,
we put

\theta:=\alpha^{-1}A\in(0,1],\quad B:=\alpha^{-1}A^{-1/2},\quad r_{0}:=A^{-1/2}\sqrt{t-s}.

Note that
B>1
and
B^{-1}A^{-1/2}<1
.
For all
l_{1},l_{2}\in[s,t]
we see

\displaystyle r_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{s}\leq\sqrt{t},\quad H\leq H_{1}\leq\frac{A}{t-s}=r^{-2}_{0},

\displaystyle|l_{1}-l_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{-1}(t-s)\leq\alpha r^{2}_{0},\quad d_{s}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{-1}r_{0}\geq r_{0}.

In virtue of Theorem
3.2
,
we obtain

d_{l_{1}}(x,y)\geq\alpha d_{s}(x,y)\geq r_{0},\quad d_{l_{2}}(x,y)\geq\alpha d_{l_{1}}(x,y)\geq\alpha A^{1/2}d_{l_{1}}(x,y)=B^{-1}d_{l_{1}}(x,y).

By switching the roles of
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
,
we arrive at the desired estimate.

Next,
we study the case of
A\in(\alpha,\infty)
.
As in the proof of Lemma
6.1
,
we define

\theta:=\alpha^{-1}A\in(1,\infty),\quad B:=\alpha^{-(\theta+2)},\quad r_{0}:=A^{-1/2}\sqrt{t-s},

where
B>1
and
B^{-1}A^{-1/2}<1
.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be the integer part of
\theta
,
and let
s=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\dots<\tau_{N}\leq\tau_{N+1}=t
be a division of
[s,t]
such that
\tau_{i+1}-\tau_{i}=\theta^{-1}(t-s)
for
i=0,\dots,N-1
.
We see

\tau_{N+1}-\tau_{N}\in[0,\theta^{-1}(t-s))
.
It holds that

r_{0}\leq\sqrt{s}\leq\sqrt{\tau_{i}}\leq\sqrt{t},\quad H\leq H_{1}\leq\frac{A}{t-s}=r^{-2}_{0},\quad\tau_{i+1}-\tau_{i}\leq\theta^{-1}(t-s)=\alpha r^{2}_{0}

for every
i=0,\dots,N
.
Since

d_{\tau_{0}}(x,y)=d_{s}(x,y)\geq B\sqrt{t-s}\geq\alpha^{-N-1}A^{-1/2}\sqrt{t-s}=\alpha^{-N-1}r_{0}\geq r_{0},

Theorem
3.2
tells us that

d_{\tau_{1}}(x,y)\geq\alpha\,d_{\tau_{0}}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{-N}r_{0}\geq r_{0}.

In the same manner as in the proof of Lemma
6.1
,
we use Theorem
3.2
repeatedly.
We obtain

(6.4)

d_{\tau_{i}}(x,y)\geq\alpha d_{\tau_{i-1}}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{-N+(i-1)}r_{0}\geq r_{0}

for all
i=1,\dots,N+1
.
For
l_{1},l_{2}\in[s,t]
,
let
i_{0}\in\{0,\dots,N\}
satisfy
l_{1}\in[\tau_{i_{0}},\tau_{i_{0}+1}]
.
Theorem
3.2
together with (
6.4
) leads us to

(6.5)

d_{l_{1}}(x,y)\geq\alpha d_{\tau_{i_{0}}}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{-N+i_{0}}r_{0}\geq r_{0}.

By using Theorem
3.2
together with (
6.4
), (
6.5
) at most
N+1
times,
we conclude

\displaystyle d_{l_{2}}(x,y)\geq\alpha^{N+1}d_{l_{1}}(x,y)\geq B^{-1}d_{l_{1}}(x,y).

Switching the roles of
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
,
we complete the proof.

\Box

We now prove the upper bound (
1.4
).

Proposition 6.4
.

For any
A>0
,
there exist positive constants
\mathcal{C}_{3},\mathcal{C}_{4}>0
depending only on
n,T,g(0)
and
A
such that the following holds:
We assume
H\leq H_{1}
for
H_{1}>0
.
For
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we also suppose
t-s\leq A\,H_{1}^{-1}
and
s\geq(t-s)/A
.
Then we have

G(x,t;y,s)\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{4}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

Proof.

We again notice (
6.1
) and (
6.2
).
By Proposition
2.5
,
for
l\in[s,t)
we have

(6.6)

G(x,t;y,l)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(t-l)^{n/2}}.

Let
B>1
be the constant in Lemma
6.3
satisfying
B^{-1}A^{-1/2}<1
.
When
d_{s}(x,y)<B\sqrt{t-s}
,
(
6.6
) implies

G(x,t;y,s)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\leq\frac{C_{1}e^{B^{2}}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

This is a desired one.

We consider the case of
d_{s}(x,y)\geq B\sqrt{t-s}
.
We set

c:=B^{-1}A^{-1/2}\in(0,1).

For all
l\in[s,t)
we see

c\sqrt{t-l}<\sqrt{T},\quad H\leq\frac{A}{t-s}\leq\frac{AB^{2}}{t-l}=\frac{1}{c^{2}(t-l)},

and hence

(6.7)

m(B(x,c\sqrt{t-l},l))\geq\kappa_{1}(t-l)^{n/2}

by Theorem
2.4
.
Proposition
2.15
together with (
6.7
) implies

(6.8)

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\frac{1}{m(B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l))}\int_{B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l)}G(x,t;z,l)\,dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{B(x,c\sqrt{t-l},l)}G(x,t;z,l)\,dm(z)\bigg)^{-1}\frac{C_{2}}{(t-l)^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{l}(x,y)}{16(t-l)}\right).

By Proposition
6.2
,

(6.9)

G(x,t;z,l)\geq\frac{C_{3}}{(t-l)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{C_{4}d^{2}_{l}(x,z)}{t-l}}\right)\geq\frac{C_{5}}{(t-l)^{n/2}}

for all
z\in B(x,c\sqrt{t-l},l)
.
Combining (
6.7
), (
6.8
), (
6.9
),
we conclude

(6.10)

\frac{1}{m(B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l))}\int_{B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l)}G(x,t;z,l)\,dm(z)\leq\frac{C_{6}}{(t-l)^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}_{l}(x,y)}{16(t-l)}\right).

Now,
we possess

\displaystyle c\sqrt{t-l}

\displaystyle<A^{-1/2}\sqrt{t-l}\leq A^{-1/2}\sqrt{t-s}\leq\sqrt{s}\leq\sqrt{l},

\displaystyle H(\cdot,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A}{t-s}\leq\frac{c^{2}(t-l)}{l-\tau}\frac{A}{t-s}\leq\frac{c^{2}A}{l-\tau}

for
\tau\in[l-c^{2}(t-l),l]
,
and hence Theorem
2.12
tells us that

(6.11)

m_{l}(B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l))\leq\kappa_{2}(t-l)^{n/2}.

From (
6.10
), (
6.11
) and Lemma
6.3
, one can derive

\int_{B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l)}G(x,t;z,l)\,dm(z)\leq C_{7}\exp\left({-\frac{d^{2}_{l}(x,y)}{16(t-l)}}\right)\leq C_{7}\exp\left({-\frac{B^{-2}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{16(t-s)}}\right).

This together with (
6.6
) leads us to

\int_{B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l)}G(x,t;z,l)^{2}\,dm(z)\leq\frac{C_{8}}{(t-l)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{B^{-2}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{16(t-s)}}\right).

Integrating over
[s,(s+t)/2]
, we obtain

\int^{\frac{s+t}{2}}_{s}\int_{B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l)}\,G(x,t;z,l)^{2}\,dm(z)\,dl\leq\frac{C_{9}}{(t-s)^{n/2-1}}\exp\left({-\frac{B^{-2}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{16(t-s)}}\right).

We now set

r:=c\sqrt{\frac{t-s}{2}}.

We notice that

t-l\geq(t-s)/2
for
l\in[s,(s+t)/2]
,
and hence

Q^{+}(y,r,s)\subset\left\{(z,l)\in M\times[0,T)~~\middle|~~z\in B(y,c\sqrt{t-l},l),\,l\in\left[s,\frac{s+t}{2}\right]\right\}.

It follows that

\int_{Q^{+}(y,r,s)}G(x,t;z,l)^{2}\,dm(z)\,dl\leq\frac{C_{9}}{(t-s)^{n/2-1}}\exp\left({-\frac{B^{-2}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{16(t-s)}}\right).

By virtue of Theorem
5.2
,

G(x,t;y,s)^{2}\leq\frac{C_{10}}{r^{n+2}}\int_{Q^{+}(y,r,s)}G(x,t;z,l)^{2}\,dm(z)\,dl\leq\frac{C_{11}}{(t-s)^{n}}\exp\left({-\frac{B^{-2}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{16(t-s)}}\right).

Thus,
we complete the proof.

\Box

We finally prove the gradient estimate (
1.5
).

Proposition 6.5
.

For any
A>0
,
there exist positive constants
\mathcal{C}_{5},\mathcal{C}_{6}>0
depending only on
n,T,g(0)
and
A
such that the following holds:
We assume
H\leq H_{1}
for
H_{1}>0
.
For
s,t\in[0,T)
with
s<t
,
we also suppose
t-s\leq A\,H_{1}^{-1}
and
s\geq(t-s)/A
.
Then we have

|\nabla_{x}G|(x,t;y,s)\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{5}}{(t-s)^{(n+1)/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{6}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

Proof.

We set
G(x,t):=G(x,t;y,s)
.
Due to Theorem
2.9
,

\frac{|\nabla G|^{2}}{G^{2}}(x,t)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t-s}\log\frac{\mathcal{A}}{G(x,t)},

where

\mathcal{A}:=\sup_{M\times\left[\frac{s+t}{2},t\right]}G.

It follows that

|\nabla G|^{2}(x,t)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t-s}\mathcal{A}G(x,t)\frac{G(x,t)}{\mathcal{A}}\log\frac{\mathcal{A}}{G(x,t)}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t-s}\mathcal{A}G(x,t)

since
G(x,t)/\mathcal{A}\leq 1
.
By Propositions
2.5
and
6.4
,
we possess

\mathcal{A}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{(t-s)^{n/2}},\quad G(x,t)\leq\frac{C_{3}}{(t-s)^{n/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{C_{4}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

Combining them,
we arrive at

|\nabla G|^{2}(x,t)\leq\frac{C_{5}}{(t-s)^{n+1}}\exp\left({-\frac{C_{4}\,d^{2}_{s}(x,y)}{t-s}}\right).

This completes the proof.

\Box

.

,

,
6.5
,

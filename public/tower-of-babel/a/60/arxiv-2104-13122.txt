Why Does Propositional Quantification Make Modal and Temporal Logics on Trees Robustly Hard?
bartosz Bednarczyk ORCID:0000-0002-8267-7554, Stéphane Demri ORCID:0000-0002-3493-2610

Abstract
. Adding propositional quantification to the modal logics 𝖪 \mathsf{K} , 𝖳 \mathsf{T} or 𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{S4} is known to lead to undecidability but 𝖢𝖳𝖫 \mathsf{CTL} with propositional quantification under the tree semantics ( 𝖰𝖢𝖳𝖫 t \mathsf{QCTL}^{t} ) admits a non-elementary Tower -complete satisfiability problem. We investigate the complexity of strict fragments of 𝖰𝖢𝖳𝖫 t \mathsf{QCTL}^{t} as well as of the modal logic 𝖪 \mathsf{K} with propositional quantification under the tree semantics. More specifically, we show that 𝖰𝖢𝖳𝖫 t \mathsf{QCTL}^{t} restricted to the temporal operator 𝐄𝐗 \mathbf{EX} is already Tower -hard, which is unexpected as 𝐄𝐗 \mathbf{EX} can only enforce local properties. When 𝖰𝖢𝖳𝖫 t \mathsf{QCTL}^{t} restricted to 𝐄𝐗 \mathbf{EX} is interpreted on N N -bounded trees for some N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , we prove that the satisfiability problem is AExp pol {}_{\textsc{pol}} -complete; AExp pol {}_{\textsc{pol}} -hardness is established by reduction from a recently introduced tiling problem, instrumental for studying the model-checking problem for interval temporal logics. As consequences of our proof method, we prove Tower -hardness of 𝖰𝖢𝖳𝖫 t \mathsf{QCTL}^{t} restricted to 𝐄𝐅 \mathbf{EF} or to 𝐄𝐗𝐄𝐅 \mathbf{EX}\mathbf{EF} and of the well-known modal logics such as 𝖪 \mathsf{K} , 𝖪𝖣 \mathsf{KD} , 𝖦𝖫 \mathsf{GL} , 𝖪𝟦 \mathsf{K4} and 𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{S4} with propositional quantification under a semantics based on classes of trees.

Introduction

Propositional quantification in modal and temporal logics

A natural way to design logics that dynamically
update their models, consists in adding propositional quantification as done for example
to define QBF from SAT.
Propositional quantification is a very powerful feature to update models but this may have consequences
in terms of computability.
In the realm of modal logics
[
BdRV01
]
, the paper
[
Bul69
]
remains a quite early work adding
propositional quantification.
The undecidability of the propositional modal logic

\mathsf{K}
(resp.
\mathsf{T}
,
\mathsf{K4}
and
\mathsf{S4}
) augmented with propositional
quantification is first established in
[
Fin70
]
, and this is done thanks to a reduction
from the second-order arithmetic. By contrast, the decidability of second-order versions of the
modal logic
\mathsf{S5}
is first proved in
[
Fin69
, Chapter 3]
(see also
[
Fin70
,
Kap70
]
)
but two
\mathsf{S5}
modalities and propositional quantification already lead to
undecidability
[
AT02
,
KT96
]
.

Many subsequent works have dealt with second-order modal logics,
see
e
.
g
.
[
KT96
,
Kre97b
,
Fre01
,
tC06
]
, but in the realm of
temporal logics,
\mathsf{LTL}
with propositional quantification (written
\mathsf{QLTL}
) is
introduced in Sistla’s PhD thesis
[
Sis83
]

(see also
[
SVW87
]
) and non-elementarity of the satisfiability
problem is a consequence of
[
Mey73
]
.
So, the
\mathsf{QLTL}
satisfiability problem is decidable but with high complexity.
By contrast,
\mathsf{CTL}
with propositional
quantification (written
\mathsf{QCTL}
) already admits an undecidable satisfiability problem
by
[
Fin70
]
(as
\mathsf{CTL}
captures the modal logic
\mathsf{K}
)
but its variant under the tree semantics (written
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
) admits a non-elementary
Tower
-complete satisfiability
problem
[
LM14
,
DLM16
]
(the complexity class
Tower
is introduced in
[
Sch16
]
).
Having a tree semantics means that the formulae of

\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
are interpreted on computation trees obtained from the unfolding of
finite (total) Kripke structures, which allows us to regain decidability (see a
similar approach in
[
Zac04
]
with a quantified version of the modal logic
\mathsf{S4}
).
This is a major observation from
[
LM14
]
,
partly motivated by the design of decision procedures for
\mathsf{ATL}
with strategy
contexts
[
LM15
]
. The tree semantics, as far as
satisfiability is concerned, amounts to considering Kripke structures
that are finite-branching trees in which all the maximal branches are infinite.
This is an elegant way to regain decidability.
More generally, decidability in the presence of propositional quantification can be regained when
tree-like models are involved, see
e
.
g
.
[
AB93
,
BCZ00
,
Zac04
,
LM14
]
,
essentially by taking advantage of Rabin’s Theorem
[
Rab69
]
.

The modal logic
\mathsf{K}
with propositional quantification from
[
Fin70
]
is
interpreted under the structure semantics, as classified in
[
LM14
]
,
but many variants of propositional quantification exist in the literature
(see
e
.
g
.
[
PBD+02
,
Fre03
,
BvDP15
]

and
[
BvdH16
,
BvdHK18
]

in the context of epistemic reasoning). Sometimes, propositional quantification
is syntactically restricted in the temporal language, but its inclusion is
motivated by a gain of expressive power while preserving the decidability of the
reasoning tasks. By way of example, in
[
RP03
]
, an extension
of the modal
\mu
-calculus with partial propositional quantification is
introduced to perform control synthesis, whereas an extension for model-checking
computer systems is also presented in
[
Kup99
]
.

Interestingly enough, propositional quantification can sometimes have a more hidden
presence. For instance, hybrid logics with the down-arrow operator
\downarrow_{x}
,
see e.g
[
ABM00
,
ABM01
,
Mar02
,
Web07
,
Ker19
]
,
can be understood as a form of propositional quantification
since
\downarrow_{x}\phi
enforces that the propositional variable

x
holds true only at the current world (before evaluating the formula
\phi
).
In such logics, the companion formula
@_{x}\psi

expresses that
the unique world satisfying
x
also satisfied
\psi
;
@_{x}
is a powerful
operator to navigate in the structure
[
Mar02
]
(obviously, it is related
to the universal modality, see
e
.
g
.
[
GP92
]
).

Our motivations

As recalled above, the modal logic
\mathsf{K}
augmented with propositional quantification
is undecidable
[
Fin70
]
and a fortiori, undecidability holds for fragments
of
\mathsf{CTL}
with propositional quantification. Actually, these results hold under
the structure semantics but
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
(tree semantics) admits
Tower
-complete
satisfiability and model-checking problems
[
LM14
]
. As
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}

can express that every tree node has exactly one child,
Tower
-hardness for the
satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
, is a corollary of the
Tower
-hardness of
the satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{QLTL}
,
see
e
.
g
.
[
Mey73
,
SVW87
,
DGL16
]
.

Given the central position of the modal logic
\mathsf{K}
, surprisingly, the complexity
of the satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{K}
with propositional quantification under the
tree semantics has never been investigated
(closely related to
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
–
i
.
e
.
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
restricted to
\mathbf{EX}
– as
\mathbf{EX}
corresponds to
\Diamond
in
\mathsf{K}
but with total models).

\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
is a
natural and modest fragment of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
and we aim at
characterising its
complexity. Furthermore, the model-checking problem for

\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
is
Tower
-hard even with input Kripke structures having at most two
successor worlds per world and
Tower
-hardness of
\mathsf{QLTL}
holds with linear
structures of length
\omega
, see
e
.
g
.
[
SVW87
]
.
Thus, it is worth understanding what happens with the satisfiability problem
for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
when the tree models are

N
-bounded
(
i
.
e
. each node has at most
N
children) for some fixed
N\geq 2
.

Our contributions

Given
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
, the extension of
\mathsf{CTL}
with propositional quantification under
the tree semantics (
i
.
e
. the models are finite-branching trees where all the
maximal branches are infinite), let
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\leq N}
be its variant in which the
models are
N
-bounded, for some
N\geq 2
. We write
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
and

\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
, to denote respectively the restriction to the operator
\mathbf{EX}
,
and
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
to denote the restriction of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
to
\mathbf{EF}
.

•

We first present to the reader the toolkit of local nominals and explain the basic ideas behind the hardness results of this paper by proving, for all
N\geq 2
, that the satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
is
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
-complete (Section
3
).

AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
is the class of problems solvable by
exponential-time alternating Turing machines with a polynomial number of alternations.
By using a small model property and
the complexity of model-checking (with upper bound
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
),
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
-easiness is established.
As far as
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
-hardness is concerned,
the alternating multi-tiling problem introduced in
[
BMMP17
]

is instrumental to establish that the model-checking
problem for the interval temporal logic

B\bar{E}
with regular expressions is
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
-hard.
As a corollary, we get that the modal logic
\mathsf{K}
with propositional quantification
interpreted on finite trees of branching degree bounded by some fixed
N\geq 2
is also

AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
-complete.

•

More generally and despite the modest scope of
\mathbf{EX}
, the satisfiability problem
for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
is shown to be
Tower
-hard (Theorem
22
), by
a uniform reduction from
k\textsc{-NExpTime}
-complete tiling problems (uniformity is
with respect to
k
). The corresponding upper bound is known
from
[
LM14
]
and it is worth noting that all the
Tower
upper bound
results presented in this paper are based on translations into the satisfiability
problem for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{,}
sometimes via intermediate decision problems, which eventually
uses Rabin’s Theorem
[
Rab69
]
in some essential way.
The hardness proof is one of the main results of the paper and amounts first to
showing that one can enforce that a node has a number of children equal to some
tower of exponentials of height
k
with a formula of size exponential in
k
.
By contrast, checking the satisfiability status of
\mathsf{CTL}^{*}
formulae,
requires only to consider tree models with branching degree bounded by the size
of the formula, see
e
.
g
.
[
ES84
,
DGL16
]
.
Once this complex construction enforcing a very high number of children is
performed, the reduction from the tiling problems can be done with the help of
other properties on the number of children. Hence, even though
\mathsf{QCTL}_{\mathbf{X}}
under
the structure semantics is undecidable
[
Fin70
]
and the variant of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}

under the tree semantics (
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
) is decidable by
[
LM14
]
,
the problem admits a high complexity despite the local range of
\mathbf{EX}
.
The
Tower
lower bound for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
crucially depends on the availability of very wide trees,
which contrasts with the
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
upper bound for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
for which trees are
N
-bounded.

•

By adapting our proof method, we show that
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
and
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{F}}

(a variant of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
with the unique operator
\mathbf{EX}\mathbf{EF}
) are
Tower
-hard too
(consult Section
5.1
).

•

As
\mathbf{EX}
,
\mathbf{EF}
and
\mathbf{EX}\mathbf{EF}
correspond to the modality
\Diamond
in several
modal logics (
e
.
g
.
\mathbf{EX}\mathbf{EF}
corresponds to
\Diamond
in transitive frames), we
are able to establish
Tower
-completeness for standard modal logics with
propositional quantification when interpreted on tree-like structures (see
Section
5.2
). For instance, as the provability logic
\mathsf{GL}
(after Gödel and Löb) is complete for the class of finite transitive trees,

i
.
e
. the accessibility relation is the transitive closure of the child-relation
in the tree, see
e
.
g
.
[
BdRV01
]
, we also investigate
the satisfiability problem under the finite tree semantics.
We show that
\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}_{\mathbf{X}}
and
\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}_{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{F}}
(‘
ft
’ stands for ’finite tree
semantics’) are
Tower
-complete too. The satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{K}
(resp.
\mathsf{GL}
) with propositional quantification under the finite (resp. transitive)
tree semantics is shown to be
Tower
-complete.
Similar results are shown for
\mathsf{KD}
,
\mathsf{K4}
and
\mathsf{S4}
with propositional quantification

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Kripke structures and computation trees

Below, we recall standard definitions about Kripke structures.
Let
\mathit{AP}=\{p,q,x,y,\ldots\}

be a countably infinite set of propositional variables.
A
Kripke structure

\mathcal{K}
is a triple
\langle W,R,l\rangle
,
where
W
is a set of
worlds
,
R\subseteq W\times W
is a transition relation
and
l:W\rightarrow 2^{\mathit{AP}}
is a labelling function.
A Kripke structure
\mathcal{K}
is
total
whenever
for all
w\in W
, there is
w^{\prime}\in W
such that
(w,w^{\prime})\in R
.
Totality is a standard property for defining classes of models for temporal logics
such as
\mathsf{CTL}
. In the sequel, by a ‘Kripke structure’ we mean a structure according
to the above definition, otherwise when arbitrary (or total, finite, etc.)
Kripke structures need to be considered, we explicitly specify which classes of
structures we have in mind. For instance, a tree model (resp. finite tree model)
{\mathfrak{T}}=\langle V,E,l\rangle
is an arbitrary Kripke structure where
(V,E)
is a (resp. finite) rooted tree.
Standard definitions about trees are omitted herein. Such structures play an important role in the paper,
as the tree semantics involves specific tree models (those obtained as computation trees of
Kripke structures).

Given a Kripke structure
\mathcal{K}=\langle W,R,l\rangle
and a world
w\in W
, a
finite path

\pi
from
w
is a finite sequence
w_{0},\ldots,w_{n}
such that

w_{0}=w
and for all

i\in[0,n-1]
, we have
(w_{i},w_{i+1})\in R
.
An
infinite path
from
w
is an infinite sequence
w_{0},\ldots,w_{n},\ldots

such that
w_{0}=w
and for all
i\geq 0
, we have
(w_{i},w_{i+1})\in R
.
With
\Pi_{\mathcal{K},w}
we denote the set of all finite paths starting from a world

w\in W
on a Kripke structure
\mathcal{K}
.

For a Kripke structure
\mathcal{K}=\langle W,R,l\rangle
and
w\in W
, the

computation tree unfolding
\mathcal{K}
from
w

is the tree model
{\mathfrak{T}}_{\mathcal{K},w}=\langle V,E,l^{\prime}\rangle
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\Pi_{\mathcal{K},w}
,

(b)

\pi E\pi^{\prime}

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{\;\Leftrightarrow\;}}

\pi=w_{0},\ldots,w_{n}
,
\pi^{\prime}=\pi,w_{n+1}
for some
w_{n+1}\in W
and
(w_{n},w_{n+1})\in R
,

(c)

for all
\pi=w_{0},\ldots,w_{n}\in V
, we have
l^{\prime}(\pi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}l(w_{n})
.

Thus, when
\mathcal{K}
is finite and total,
{\mathfrak{T}}_{\mathcal{K},w}

is a finite-branching tree model in which all the maximal branches
are infinite and it is also a total Kripke structure. Below,
unless otherwise stated, the tree semantics involves such finite-branching trees with infinite
maximal branches.
In the sequel, tree-like Kripke structures are denoted by
{\mathfrak{T}}=\langle V,E,l\rangle

in order to emphasize the tree-like nature of such models.

2.2.
The logics
\mathsf{QCTL}^{s}
,
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
,

\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}
and
\mathsf{QCTL}^{gt}

In this section, we define the logics
\mathsf{QCTL}^{s}
,
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
,

\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}
and
\mathsf{QCTL}^{gt}

whose formulae are interpreted over different classes of Kripke structures.
The formulae of
\mathsf{QCTL}
-like logics are defined from the grammar below
by extending the set of formulae from the computation tree logic

\mathsf{CTL}

[
CE81
]
with propositional quantification:

\phi::=p\mid\neg\phi\mid\phi\wedge\phi\mid\mathbf{EX}\phi\mid\mathbf{E}(\phi\mathbf{U}\phi)\mid\mathbf{A}(\phi\mathbf{U}\phi)\mid\exists{p}\ \phi,

where
p\in\mathit{AP}
. We use the standard abbreviations

\vee,\rightarrow,\leftrightarrow,\perp,\top
, as well as other
operators like
\mathbf{AX}
,
\mathbf{EF}
,
\mathbf{AG}
and
\mathbf{AF}
:

•

\mathbf{AX}(\phi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\neg\mathbf{EX}(\neg\phi)
,

•

\mathbf{EF}(\phi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{E}(\top\mathbf{U}\phi)
,

\mathbf{AG}(\phi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\neg\mathbf{EF}(\neg\phi)

and
\mathbf{AF}(\phi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{A}(\top\mathbf{U}\phi)
.

•

The universal quantifier
\forall{p}\ \phi
is used as

\neg\exists{p}\ \neg\phi
.

We denote by
|\phi|
the
length
of the formula
\phi

measured in a standard way,
i
.
e
. as the number of symbols used to write

\phi
. The
modal/temporal depth
of a formula
\phi
,
written
\mathit{md}(\phi)
, is the maximal number of
nested temporal operators in
\phi
.
We stress that
\mathit{md}(\phi)
is linear in
|\phi|
.

By restricting the set of allowed temporal operators in
\mathsf{QCTL}
to the only temporal operator
\mathbf{EX}
(resp.
\mathbf{EF}
) we obtain the logic
\mathsf{QCTL}_{\mathbf{X}}
(resp.
\mathsf{QCTL}_{\mathbf{F}}
).
Note that
\mathbf{AF}
does not occur in
\mathsf{QCTL}_{\mathbf{F}}
but
\mathbf{AX}
(resp.
\mathbf{AG}
) is allowed in
\mathsf{QCTL}_{\mathbf{X}}
(resp. in
\mathsf{QCTL}_{\mathbf{F}}
) as it is the dual operator of
\mathbf{EX}
(resp.
\mathbf{EF}
).
The main object of study in the paper is the logic

\mathsf{QCTL}_{\mathbf{X}}
under the tree semantics (below, written
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
). Moreover, we write
\mathsf{QCTL}_{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{F}}

to denote the restriction of
\mathsf{QCTL}
to the (combined) temporal operator
\mathbf{EX}\mathbf{EF}
, which provides a strict version of the future-time temporal operator
\mathbf{EF}
.
In the rest of the paper, we refer to
\mathsf{QCTL}
(or to some of its fragments) to denote
a set of formulae and the notation
\mathsf{QCTL}^{\bullet}
with a superscript ‘
\bullet
’
refers to a logic based on
\mathsf{QCTL}
(or on some of its fragments) under a specific semantics and for
which the symbol ‘
\bullet
’ is just a reminder of the semantics.

To define formally the semantics for propositional quantifiers,
we introduce the notion of
X
-equivalence between two Kripke structures, with
X
being a set of propositional variables.
Intuitively, two Kripke structures are
X
-equivalent, whenever the only differences (if any) between them are restricted
to the interpretation of the propositional variables not in
X
.
Formally, we say that two Kripke structures
\mathcal{K}=\langle W,R,l\rangle
and

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\langle W^{\prime},R^{\prime},l^{\prime}\rangle
are
X
-equivalent

(written
\mathcal{K}\approx_{X}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
),
iff
W=W^{\prime}
,
R=R^{\prime}
and
l(w)\cap X=l^{\prime}(w)\cap X
for all
w\in W
.

Given a Kripke structure
\mathcal{K}=\langle W,R,l\rangle
,
a world
w\in W
and a formula

\phi
in
\mathsf{QCTL}
, the satisfaction relation

\mathcal{K},w\models\phi
is defined as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{K},w\models p&\text{iff}&p\in l(w)\\
\mathcal{K},w\models\neg\phi&\text{iff}&\mathcal{K},w\not\models\phi\\
\mathcal{K},w\models\phi\wedge\psi&\text{iff}&\mathcal{K},w\models\phi\;\text{and}\;\mathcal{K},w\models_{s}\psi\\
\mathcal{K},w\models\mathbf{EX}\phi&\text{iff}&{\rm there\ is}\ w^{\prime}\;\text{such that}\;(w,w^{\prime})\in R\;\text{and}\;\mathcal{K},w^{\prime}\models\phi\\
\mathcal{K},w\models\mathbf{E}(\phi\mathbf{U}\psi)&\text{iff}&{\rm there\ is\ a\ path}\ w_{0},\ldots,w_{n}\;\text{such that}\;w_{0}=w,\\
&&\ \mathcal{K},w_{n}\models\psi\;\text{and}\;{\rm for\ all}\ i\in[0,n-1],\ {\rm we\ have}\ \mathcal{K},w_{i}\models\phi\\
\mathcal{K},w\models\mathbf{A}(\phi\mathbf{U}\psi)&\text{iff}&{\rm for\ all\ infinite\ paths}\ w_{0},\ldots,w_{n},\ldots\;\text{such that}\;w_{0}=w,{\rm there\ is}\\
&&j\geq 0\;\text{such that}\;\mathcal{K},w_{j}\models\psi\;\text{and}\ {\rm for\ all}\ i\in[0,j-1],\mathcal{K},w_{i}\models\phi\\
\mathcal{K},w\models\exists{p}\;\phi&\text{iff}&{\rm there\ is}\ \mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;{\rm such\ that}\;\mathcal{K}\approx_{\mathit{AP}\setminus\{p\}}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;{\rm and}\;\mathcal{K}^{\prime},w\models\phi\end{array}

Note that
\mathcal{K},w\models\mathbf{EF}\phi
iff there is
w^{\prime}\in R^{*}(w)
(where
R^{*}
is the reflexive transitive closure of
R
)
such that
\mathcal{K},w^{\prime}\models\phi
. Stating that there is a unique successor of
w
satisfying
the propositional variable
x
can be expressed by the formula

\mathbf{EX}\ x\wedge\neg(\exists\ p\ \mathbf{EX}(x\wedge p)\wedge\mathbf{EX}(x\wedge\neg p))
, where
p
is
distinct from
x
.

The satisfiability problem for the logic
\mathsf{QCTL}^{s}
(under the
structure semantics
) takes as input a
formula
\phi
in
\mathsf{QCTL}
and asks whether
there is a finite and total Kripke structure
\mathcal{K}
and a world
w
such that
\mathcal{K},w\models\phi
.

The
tree semantics
introduced in
[
LM14
]
is obtained by considering as only admissible models the computation trees of
finite and total Kripke structures.
As noted in
[
LM14
, Remark 5.7]
, an equivalent formulation can be provided:
the satisfiability problem for the logic
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
(under the
tree semantics
)
takes as input a formula in
\mathsf{QCTL}

]

For proving hardness results, we make extensive use of tiling problems, see

.

3.1.
A toolkit for introducing local nominals

Below, we introduce formulae to simulate partially the use of nominals from hybrid modal logics
[
ABM01
]
.
A nominal
x
is usually understood as a propositional variable true at exactly one world of the model (a global property).
In
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
, such a property cannot be enforced but it can be done with respect to nodes at a bounded depth from the evaluation node, whence the adjective ‘local’ for the nominals.
The use of local nominals is essential in all our hardness proofs, as it allows us to simulate first-order quantification on a given set of nodes of bounded depth.

Proofs of all lemmas from this section are rather straightforward and are shown by careful inspection of the semantics. Hence, we delegate them to Appendix
A.1
–
A.3
.

{defi}

Given a tree model
{\mathfrak{T}}
and a node
v
, we say that the propositional variable
x
is a
nominal for the depth
k\geq 1
from
v

iff there is
v^{\prime}\in V
with
vE^{k}v^{\prime}
such that
{\mathfrak{T}},v^{\prime}\models x
, and for all worlds
v^{\prime\prime}\neq v^{\prime}
s.t.

vE^{k}v^{\prime\prime}
, we have
{\mathfrak{T}},v^{\prime\prime}\not\models x
(
E^{k}
is the
k
-fold composition of
E
).

The formula
\mathtt{nom}(x,k)
defined as

\mathbf{EX}^{k}x\wedge\neg\exists\ p\ (\mathbf{EX}^{k}(x\wedge p)\wedge\mathbf{EX}^{k}(x\wedge\neg p))
,
where
p
is distinct from
x
, states that
x
is a nominal for the depth
k
(
\mathbf{EX}^{k}
denotes
k
copies of
\mathbf{EX}
).

Lemma 2
.

x
is a nominal for the depth
k\geq 1
from
v
iff
{\mathfrak{T}},v\models\mathtt{nom}(x,k)
.

Let us next define
\and_{x}^{k}\phi
as the formula
\mathbf{EX}^{k}(x\wedge\phi)

(usually assuming that
\mathtt{nom}(x,k)
holds).

Lemma 3
.

Assuming that
x
is a nominal for the depth
k\geq 0
from
v
such that
vE^{k}v^{\prime}
and
{\mathfrak{T}},v^{\prime}\models x
,
we have
{\mathfrak{T}},v\models\and_{x}^{k}\phi
iff
{\mathfrak{T}},v^{\prime}\models\phi
.

Given
d\geq 1
and propositional variables
x_{1}
, …,
x_{d}
(that play the role of nominals),
we often write
\and_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}}\phi
to denote
the formula
\and_{x_{1}}^{1}\and_{x_{2}}^{1}\cdots\and_{x_{d}}^{1}\phi

(usually assuming that
\mathtt{nom}(x_{1},1)
holds and for all
i\in[2,d]
,

\and_{x_{1}}^{1}\and_{x_{2}}^{1}\cdots\and_{x_{i-1}}^{1}\mathtt{nom}(x_{i},1)
holds true).
We also use
\and_{\bar{x}}\phi
instead of
\and_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}}\phi

when
\bar{x}
is understood as
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
.
Given a node
v_{0}
such that

{\mathfrak{T}},v_{0}\models\mathtt{nom}(x_{1},1)\wedge\bigwedge_{i\in[2,d]}\and_{x_{1}}^{1}\and_{x_{2}}^{1}\cdots\and_{x_{i-1}}^{1}\mathtt{nom}(x_{i},1),

we write
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
to denote the unique sequence of nodes such that
for all
i\in[1,d]
, we have both
v_{i-1}Ev_{i}
and
{\mathfrak{T}},v_{i}\models x_{i}
.
The existence and uniqueness of the nodes
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}

follow from Lemma
2
and Lemma
3
.
Here is another useful lemma justifying the use of the introduced abbreviations.

Lemma 4
.

Assume that
{\mathfrak{T}},v_{0}\models\mathtt{nom}(x_{1},1)\wedge\bigwedge_{i\in[2,d]}\and_{x_{1}}^{1}\and_{x_{2}}^{1}\cdots\and_{x_{i-1}}^{1}\mathtt{nom}(x_{i},1)

and the sequence
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
is associated to
\bar{x}=x_{1},\ldots x_{d}
.
Then,
{\mathfrak{T}},v_{0}\models\and_{\bar{x}}\phi
iff
{\mathfrak{T}},v_{d}\models\phi
.

Let
\mathtt{diff{\mbox{-}}nom}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\alpha},k)
be an abbreviation of

\bigwedge_{i\in[1,\alpha]}\mathtt{nom}(x_{i},k)\wedge\bigwedge_{j\in[1,\alpha]}\bigwedge_{i<j}\neg\and_{x_{i}}^{k}x_{j}
.
It allows us to name
\alpha
distinct nodes at the depth
k
. Hence, the respective nodes
interpreting the nominals
x_{1}
, …,
x_{\alpha}
are pairwise distinct.
It is summarised with the lemma below, which proof is a slight variant the proof of Lemma
2
.

Lemma 5
.

Given a tree model
{\mathfrak{T}}
and a node
v
, we have

{\mathfrak{T}},v\models\mathtt{diff{\mbox{-}}nom}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\alpha},k)
iff
there are
\alpha
distinct nodes
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\alpha}

such that for all
i\in[1,\alpha]
,

is a nominal for the depth
k\geq 1
from

.

Let us illustrate the use of local nominals and
\mathtt{diff{\mbox{-}}nom}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\alpha},k)

to specify that a node has at most

with a formula of polynomial size in

.
This is exactly the type of properties that can be expressed in graded modal logics
[

,
FBDC85
,
Tob01
]
.
Given a finite set

of propositional variables,
we design a formula stating that no pair of distinct children
agree on all propositional variables from

, as done in
[

]

It is given below:

\mathrm{Uni}(X)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\ \forall x,y\ \mathtt{diff{\mbox{-}}nom}(x,y,1)\rightarrow\neg\left(\bigwedge_{p\in X}(\and_{x}^{1}p\leftrightarrow\and_{y}^{1}p)\right)

Thus, the formula
\Diamond_{\leq 2^{n}}\top
from graded modal logics can be expressed in

with

\Diamond_{\leq 2^{n}}\top\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\ \exists\ p_{0},\ldots,p_{n-1}\ \mathrm{Uni}(\{p_{0},\ldots,p_{n-1}\}).

3.2.
Beyond the
ExpSpace
upper bound:
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}

In order to solve SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
), little is needed if the
ExpSpace
upper bound is aimed.
Indeed, given a formula
\phi
in
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
, it is clear that for an
N
-bounded tree model
{\mathfrak{T}}
satisfying
\phi

at its root node
\varepsilon
, it is irrelevant what happens at nodes of depth strictly more than
\mathit{md}(\phi)
.
Hence, the formula
\phi
is satisfiable iff there is a finite
N
-bounded tree structure
{\mathfrak{T}}
with all the branches of length
exactly
\mathit{md}(\phi)
satisfying
\phi
at its root
\varepsilon
(as the branches of tree models are infinite,
we need to consider branches of length exactly
\mathit{md}(\phi)
).
Thus,
{\mathfrak{T}}
has at most
|\phi|N^{|\phi|}
nodes.
To get an algorithm working in
NExpSpace
, guess such an exponential-size finite tree structure,
and perform model-checking on it with an algorithm inherently in
PSpace
(as
model-checking finite structures with MSO is
PSpace
-complete
[
Sto74
,
Var82
]

and
\phi
can be translated
to MSO in the standard way), which leads to
NExpSpace
.
By Savitch’s Theorem
[
Sav70
]
, we get the
ExpSpace
upper bound.

This bound is not completely satisfactory as it does not use much of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
and more importantly,
Section
3.3
proves
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
-hardness of SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
) as long as
N\geq 2
.
Hence, the goal of this section is to establish an
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
upper bound.
The tight upper bound for SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
) relies on the following ingredients.

(i)

Every formula
\phi
of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
is logically equivalent to a
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
formula
\phi^{\prime}

in prenex normal form (PNF) such that
\phi^{\prime}
can be computed in polynomial-time in
|\phi|
.
Formulae in PNF are of the form
\mathcal{Q}_{1}\ p_{1}\ \cdots\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}\ p_{\beta}\ \psi
where
\{\mathcal{Q}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}\}\subseteq\{\exists,\forall\}
and

\psi
is quantifier-free.

(ii)

Existence of an
N
-bounded tree model for
\phi
is equivalent to the existence
of an
N
-bounded finite tree structure such that all branches are of length
\mathit{md}(\phi)
.
Then, we simply guess a finite tree of a small (exponential) size with the help of the
shallow model property
-
such a tree will be later unravelled to become an infinite tree model.

(iii)

Checking whether
{\mathfrak{T}},\varepsilon\models\mathcal{Q}_{1}\ p_{1}\ \cdots\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}\ p_{\beta}\ \psi
(involving an
N
-bounded finite tree with branches of length
\mathit{md}(\psi)
and the input formula in PNF)
can be done with an alternating Turing machine in time
\mathcal{O}((|\psi|+\beta)|{\mathfrak{T}}|)

and with at most
\beta
alternations.

To establish (i), we cannot rely directly on PNF for
\mathsf{QCTL}
from
[
LM14
, Prop. 3.1]
as the translation
in
[
LM14
, Prop. 3.1]
involves temporal operators beyond the language of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}.}

Lemma 6
.

For every formula
\phi
in
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
, one can compute in polynomial-time in

|\phi|
a logically equivalent formula in PNF

\mathcal{Q}_{1}\ p_{1}\ \cdots\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}\ p_{\beta}\ \psi

with
\beta\leq|\phi|
.

Proof 3.1
.

On tree models, the following formulae are tautologies,
assuming that
p
does not occur in
\psi^{\prime}
(where
\mathcal{Q}
is either
\exists
or
\forall

\mathbf{EX}\ \mathcal{Q}\ p\ \psi\leftrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\ p\ \mathbf{EX}\psi\ \ \ \ (\mathcal{Q}\ p\ \psi)\wedge\psi^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\ p\ (\psi\wedge\psi^{\prime})\ \ \ \ \neg\exists\ p\ \psi\leftrightarrow\forall\ p\ \neg\psi\ \ \ \ \neg\forall\ p\ \psi\leftrightarrow\exists\ p\ \neg\psi

.

Proof 3.2

The construction is standard and goes in exactly the same way as in the

Since we are interested in the satisfiability problem over

In order to show that the problem is

To define a grid

\varepsilon

Moreover, each node at a distance

(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{V})

in

\mathsf{h}_{n-1},\ldots,\mathsf{h}_{0}
and
\mathsf{v}_{n-1},\ldots,\mathsf{v}_{0}
to be responsible, respectively, for
the horizontal and vertical axes.
The

-th bit of
\mathfrak{H}
(resp.
\mathfrak{V}
) is taken care of by the truth value of
\mathsf{h}_{i}
(resp.
\mathsf{v}_{i}
)
and by convention
the least significant bit is encoded by
\mathsf{h}_{0}
(resp.
\mathsf{v}_{0}
).

The forthcoming formula

is dedicated to encoding such a grid.

\mathrm{grid}(2n)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\ \left(\bigwedge_{i\in[0,2n-1]}\mathbf{AX}^{i}\ \mathbf{EX}_{=2}\top)\right)\wedge\\
\forall x,y\ \mathtt{diff{\mbox{-}}nom}(x,y,2n)\rightarrow\left(\bigvee_{j\in[0,n-1]}\neg(\and_{x}^{2n}\mathsf{h}_{j}\leftrightarrow\and_{y}^{2n}\mathsf{h}_{j})\vee\neg(\and_{x}^{2n}\mathsf{v}_{j}\leftrightarrow\and_{y}^{2n}\mathsf{v}_{j})\right),

where
\mathbf{EX}_{=2}\top\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\exists\ x_{1},x_{2}\ \mathtt{diff{\mbox{-}}nom}(x_{1},x_{2},1)\wedge\mathbf{AX}(x_{1}\vee x_{2})

states that there are exactly two children.
Moreover,
\mathbf{AX}^{0}\psi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\psi
and

\mathbf{AX}^{i+1}\psi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{AX}\mathbf{AX}^{i}\psi
.

Note
that the upper part of
\mathrm{grid}(2n)
enforces that there are exactly
2^{2n}
descendants at a distance
2n
from the root, while the lower part imposes that any two such descendants differ by at least one propositional variable from
\mathsf{h}_{n-1},\ldots,\mathsf{h}_{0},\mathsf{v}_{n-1},\ldots,\mathsf{v}_{0}
.
Hence, the full grid
[0,2^{n}-1]\times[0,2^{n}-1]
is encoded with

The correctness of

Assuming that the nodes

ending at the node

and at the node

The formula

By contrast, the specific formula

In order to define

(

there is a child with number equal to zero, and if a child has number

.

The formula

(for

{\rm type}(k)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{AX}({\rm type}(k-1))\wedge\mathbf{EX}({\rm first}(k-1))\wedge{\rm uniq}(k)\wedge{\rm compl}(k).

Note that the above formula is intended to be built

p_{0},\ldots,p_{n-1},\mathit{val}

Let us first explain how we proceed to define all the mentioned formulae.
For successive values

, we define inductively the formulae:

•

{\rm type}(N)
,
{\rm first}(N)
and
{\rm last}(N)
,

•

\mathtt{nb}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=_{k}\mathtt{nb}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})
,

•

\mathtt{nb}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})<_{k}\mathtt{nb}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})

•

\mathtt{nb}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})=_{k}\mathtt{nb}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})+1

For

, only the formulae
{\rm type}(0)
,
{\rm first}(0)
and
{\rm last}(0)
make sense.
The case

is not yet an instance of the general case.
We first treat the cases for

and then we proceed with the general case

.

4.2.
Formulae for types zero and one

When

(thus

{\rm type}(0)
,
{\rm first}(0)
and
{\rm last}(0)

Let us define them, in the simplest way.

{\rm type}(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\top\ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm first}(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\neg p_{n-1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\neg p_{0}\ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm last}(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}p_{n-1}\wedge\cdots\wedge p_{0}.

Second, we turn our attention to the formula

we design a logarithmic-space many-one reduction from SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
).
A more sophisticated analysis is also possible to establish
Tower
-hardness for even smaller fragments, see
the recent work
[

]
.

Let
\phi
be a formula in
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
with modal depth
\mathit{md}(\phi)=k
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\phi
may contain occurrences
of
\mathbf{EX}
and no occurrences of
\mathbf{AX}
. Let us define
the formula
\phi^{\prime}=\textit{trans}(k,\phi)\wedge{\rm shape}(k)

in
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
, where the formula
{\rm shape}(k)
enforces a discipline for layers (explained below)
and
\textit{trans}(k,\phi)
admits a recursive definition, by relativising the occurrences of
\mathbf{EX}
.
We consider the set of propositional variables

Y_{k}=\{\mathit{layer}_{-1},\mathit{layer}_{0},\ldots,\mathit{layer}_{k}\}

with the intended meaning that a node satisfying
\mathit{layer}_{i}
is of “layer
i
”, the root node being of layer
k
.
Indeed, there is a need for such propositional variables, as unlike with
the formulae in
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
, we have to enforce that moving with
\mathbf{EF}
leads
to a lower layer.

The formula
{\rm shape}(k)
is defined as the conjunction of the following formulae.

•

Every node satisfies exactly one propositional variable from
Y_{k}
(layer unicity):

\mathbf{AG}\left((\mathit{layer}_{-1}\vee\mathit{layer}_{0}\vee\cdots\vee\mathit{layer}_{k})\wedge\bigwedge_{-1\leq i\neq j\leq k}\neg(\mathit{layer}_{i}\wedge\mathit{layer}_{j})\right)

•

When a node satisfies
\mathit{layer}_{i}
with
-1\leq i\leq k
, none of its
descendants satisfies
\mathit{layer}_{j}
with
j>i
(monotonicity of layer numbers):

\bigwedge_{-1\leq i\leq k}\mathbf{AG}(\mathit{layer}_{i}\rightarrow\mathbf{AG}(\mathit{layer}_{-1}\vee\mathit{layer}_{0}\vee\cdots\vee\mathit{layer}_{i}))

•

When a node satisfies
\mathit{layer}_{i}
with
0\leq i\leq k
, there is a descendant
satisfying
\mathit{layer}_{i-1}
(weak progress):

\bigwedge_{0\leq i\leq k}\mathbf{AG}(\mathit{layer}_{i}\rightarrow\mathbf{EF}\ \mathit{layer}_{i-1})

•

When a node satisfies
\mathit{layer}_{i}
with
0\leq i\leq k
, it has no (strict) descendant satisfying
\mathit{layer}_{i}
(no stuttering). This type of constraints does not apply to
\mathit{layer}_{-1}
.

\bigwedge_{0\leq i\leq k}\mathbf{AG}(\mathit{layer}_{i}\rightarrow\neg\exists{p}\ (p\wedge\mathbf{EF}(\mathit{layer}_{i}\wedge\neg p)))

•

The root node is at layer
k
:
\mathit{layer}_{k}
.

A tree model
{\mathfrak{T}}=\langle V,E,l\rangle
for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
with root
\varepsilon

is
k
-layered
iff the conditions below hold:

(a)

For every node
v\in V
,
{\sf card}(l(v)\cap Y_{k})=1
.

(b)

For all
v\in V
such that
\mathit{layer}_{j}\in l(v)
for some
j\in[-1,k]
,

•

if
j\geq 0
, then there is
v^{\prime}
such that
vEv^{\prime}
and
\mathit{layer}_{j-1}\in l(v^{\prime})
and,

•

for all
v^{\prime}
such that
vE^{+}v^{\prime}
and
\mathit{layer}_{j^{\prime}}\in l(v^{\prime})
, we have
j^{\prime}\leq j
.

(c)

For all
j\in[0,k]
, there are no distinct nodes
v
and
v^{\prime}

such that
vE^{+}v^{\prime}
and
\mathit{layer}_{j}\in l(v)\cap l(v^{\prime})
.

(d)

\mathit{layer}_{k}\in l(\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon
is the root of the tree model.

This means that the only propositional variable from
Y_{k}

satisfied by a node reachable in
j\in[1,k]
steps from
\varepsilon
is

\mathit{layer}_{m}
for some
m\leq k-j
, and the only propositional variable from
Y_{k}

satisfied by a node reachable in strictly more than

from

is
\mathit{layer}_{-1}
. Moreover, once
\mathit{layer}_{-1}
holds true,
it holds for all its descendants.
Actually, the formula
{\rm shape}(k)
characterises

-layered structures.

Lemma 23
.

Let
{\mathfrak{T}}=\langle V,E,l\rangle
be a tree model for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
with the root node
\varepsilon
.
We have
{\mathfrak{T}},\varepsilon\models{\rm shape}(k)
holds if and only if
{\mathfrak{T}}
is
k
-layered.

Proof 5.1
.

By careful inspection of the semantics, cf. Appendix
C.1
.

To define
\textit{trans}(k,\phi)
, we define
inductively

\textit{trans}(i,\psi)
where

\psi
is a subformula of
\phi
and
\mathit{md}(\psi)\leq i
.

•

\textit{trans}(i,p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}p
for all propositional variables
p
,

•

is homomorphic for Boolean connectives and
\textit{trans}(i,\exists\ p\ \psi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\exists\ p\ \textit{trans}(i,\psi)
,

•

\textit{trans}(i,\mathbf{EX}\psi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\mathbf{EF}\left(\mathit{layer}_{i{-}1}\wedge\textit{trans}(i{-}1,\psi)\right)
.

Note that
\textit{trans}(k,\phi)
has no occurrence of
\mathit{layer}_{-1}

since
\mathit{md}(\phi)=k
and that translating an
\mathbf{EX}
-formula decreases
the index of the layer by exactly one.
The correctness of the reduction can be now stated as follows.

A formula
\phi

\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
iff

\textit{trans}(k,\phi)\wedge{\rm shape}(k)

is satisfiable for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
.

Proof 5.2

By induction. Consult Appendix

.

Hence we conclude yet another important result.

Theorem 25
.

The satisfiability problem for

-complete.

The

in

and in particular for

As usual, we write

Theorem 26
.

The satisfiability problem for

As above, the

5.2.
Modal logics with propositional quantification on trees

Numerous well-known modal logics are complete (a.k.a. determined) for classes of tree-like structures.
A modal logic
\mathfrak{L}
(defined from the Hilbert-style system
\mathcal{H}\mathfrak{L}
)
is complete for a class of Kripke structures
\mathcal{C}
iff
the theoremhood of
\phi
in
\mathcal{H}\mathfrak{L}
is equivalent
to the validity of
\phi
in
\mathcal{C}
(i.e. for all
\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{C}
,
for all
w
, we have
\mathcal{K},w\models\phi
).
For instance, the (propositional) modal logic
\mathsf{K}
is complete for the class of finite
trees
[
Seg71
,
BdRV01
]
.
It is worth noting that a given modal logic can be complete for different classes of Kripke models
(
e
.
g
.
\mathsf{K}
is complete for the class of all the Kripke models, but also complete for the class of finite Kripke models)
and their extension to propositional quantification may lead to distinct logics.
Typically,
\mathsf{K}
with propositional
quantification under the structure semantics is undecidable
[
Fin70
]
whereas it is shown below to be

Tower
-complete under the finite tree semantics.

Below, for the propositional modal logics
\mathfrak{L}
in
\mathsf{K}
,
\mathsf{KD}
,
\mathsf{GL}
,
\mathsf{K4}
and
\mathsf{S4}
we define
an extension
\mathsf{Q}\mathfrak{L}^{t}
with propositional quantification under a class of tree-like models
that is complete for the logic
\mathfrak{L}
.

In order to avoid too many notations, the modalities for each logic
\mathsf{Q}\mathfrak{L}^{t}

are
\mathbf{EX}
and
\mathbf{AX}
(instead of the more standard modal operators
\Diamond
and
\Box
)
and therefore
\mathsf{Q}\mathfrak{L}^{t}
formulae are built from the grammar below:

\phi::=p\mid\neg\phi\mid\phi\wedge\phi\mid\mathbf{EX}\phi\mid\mathbf{AX}\phi\mid\exists{p}\ \phi
.

•

The propositional modal logic
\mathsf{K}
is complete for the class of finite trees and we define
\mathsf{QK}^{t}
as the modal logic
with propositional quantification over the class of finite trees.

•

The propositional modal logic
\mathsf{KD}
(
\mathsf{K}
with seriality, a.k.a. totality) is known to be complete for the class
of finite-branching trees for which all the maximal branches are infinite.
Indeed,
\mathsf{KD}
is complete for the class of finite total Kripke models.
Therefore by using the unfolding
construction, completeness applies also for
the class
of finite-branching trees for which all the maximal branches are infinite,
i
.
e
. the models for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
.
Let
\mathsf{QKD}^{t}
be the modal logic
with propositional quantification over the class
of finite-branching trees for which all the maximal branches are infinite.
The satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{QKD}^{t}
is exactly the problem for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
.

•

The modal logic
\mathsf{GL}
is known to be complete for the class of finite transitive trees
(
\mathsf{GL}
is complete with respect to finite irreflexive transitive Kripke models
[
Smo85
]
),
i
.
e
. the class
of Kripke structures
\langle V,E^{+},l\rangle
such that
\langle V,E,l\rangle
is a finite tree model, see
e
.
g
.
[
BdRV01
]
.
Let
\mathsf{QGL}^{t}
be the modal logic with propositional quantification over the
class of finite transitive trees, which is precisely
\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}_{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{F}}
.
It is worth noting that adding propositional quantification to
\mathsf{GL}
is studied in
[
AB93
]
,
where a fragment is shown to be decidable by translation into the weak monadic second-order logic of one successor
WS1S
[
Büc60
]
.

•

The modal logic
\mathsf{K4}
is complete for the class of Kripke structures

\langle V,E^{+},l\rangle
such that
\langle V,E,l\rangle
is a finite-branching tree
model (some branches may be infinite, some others not).
Let
\mathsf{QK4}^{t}
be the modal logic with propositional quantification over the
class of finite-branching trees.

•

The modal logic
\mathsf{S4}
is complete for the class of finite Kripke structures such that
the accessibility relation is reflexive and transitive, and therefore complete
for the class of structures

\langle V,E^{*},l\rangle
such that
\langle V,E,l\rangle
is a finite-branching tree
model in which all the branches are infinite (by unfolding).
Let
\mathsf{QS4}^{t}
be the modal logic with propositional quantification over the
class of finite-branching trees in which all the branches are infinite (precisely
the class of models for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
). The satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{QS4}^{t}
happens to be exactly the problem
for
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
, modulo the fact that
\mathbf{EX}
in
\mathsf{QS4}^{t}
corresponds to
\mathbf{EF}
in
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
.

In this section, we show that the satisfiability problem for
the logics
\mathsf{QK}^{t}
,
\mathsf{QKD}^{t}
,
\mathsf{QGL}^{t}
,
\mathsf{QK4}^{t}
and
\mathsf{QS4}^{t}
and whose models are tree-like Kripke structures is
Tower
-complete.
For instance,
\mathsf{QK}^{t}
corresponds to the modal logic
\mathsf{K}
interpreted on finite trees
with propositional quantification, which is precisely
\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}_{\mathbf{X}}
,
i
.
e
.
\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}

restricted to the
\mathbf{EX}
operator.

Theorem 27
.

SAT(
\mathsf{QK}^{t}
)
is
Tower
-complete.

Proof 5.3
.

The satisfiability problem in Theorem

6.
Conclusion

In the paper, we have developed a relatively simple proof method to show that the satisfiability problems for

\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
,
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
and
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{F}}
are
Tower
-complete, see also similar methods in
[
Sto74
,
PHST19
]
.
Our contribution is to establish
Tower
-hardness, which could be also
shown for several modal logics with propositional quantification
whose respective classes of models are tree-like structures.
Moreover, in the case of fixed degree, we have shown that for all
N\geq 2
,
the satisfiability problem for the variant
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X},\leq N}
is
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
-complete.
Whereas
AExp
{}_{\textsc{pol}}
-hardness is established by reducing the alternating multi-tiling problem
recently introduced in
[
BMMP17
]
, the
Tower
-hardness of SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
)
is essentially based on the fact that one can enforce concisely that a node has a number
of children equal to some tower of exponentials.

Section
5
deals with the
Tower
-completeness of
SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}_{\mathbf{X}}
) and SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{ft}_{\mathbf{X}\mathbf{F}}
), as well as
Tower
-completeness for
the well-known modal logics
\mathsf{K}
,
\mathsf{KD}
,
\mathsf{GL}
,
\mathsf{K4}
and
\mathsf{S4}
extended with propositional quantification
but with adequate classes of tree-like structures.
Though the
Tower
upper bound for decision problems on trees should not come as a real surprise,
all our
Tower
-hardness results significantly improve the current state-of-the-art
regarding the fragments of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}
and for the above-mentioned modal logics.
In particular, our proof technique for
Tower
-hardness of
SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
) (and therefore for
\mathsf{QK}^{t}
on finite trees)
is simple enough so that it could be further reused or adapted, see
e
.
g
. a recent refinement of the proof in
[
BDFM20
]
.

This work can be continued in several directions.
For instance,
Tower
-hardness of SAT(
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
) is recently refined
in
[
Man20b
,
Man20a
]
by establishing that already

\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
restricted to formulae of modal/temporal depth two is also

Tower
-hard.
Among the several directions, one of them would be to characterise the expressiveness
of
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{X}}
or
\mathsf{QCTL}^{t}_{\mathbf{F}}
along the lines of
[
DLM16
]
or
[
Kuu15
]
, see
also
[
AvBG18
]
.
More generally, we believe that standard modal logics with propositional quantification, but under the
tree semantics, deserve to be much better understood.

Proof of Lemma

Proof A.4
.

By way of example, we prove that

\mathbf{EX}\ \forall\ p\ \psi\leftrightarrow\forall\ p\ \mathbf{EX}\psi
is valid.
(Other formulae, e.g.
\mathbf{EX}\ \exists\ p\ \psi\leftrightarrow\exists\ p\ \mathbf{EX}\psi
can be proved to be valid in an even simpler way).
Before doing so, note that then the valid equivalences below
provide a rewriting system (by reading the equivalences from left to right) that pushes the propositional quantification
outside (in the usual way), leading to formulae in PNF in polynomial-time (
\mathcal{Q}\in\{\exists,\forall\}
).

\mathbf{EX}\ \mathcal{Q}\ p\ \psi\leftrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\ p\ \mathbf{EX}\psi\ \ \ \ (\mathcal{Q}\ p\ \psi)\wedge\psi^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\ p\ (\psi\wedge\psi^{\prime})\ \ \ \ \neg\exists\ p\ \psi\leftrightarrow\forall\ p\ \neg\psi\ \ \ \ \neg\forall\ p\ \psi\leftrightarrow\exists\ p\ \neg\psi

assuming that
p
does not occur in
\psi^{\prime}
(otherwise, rename the quantified variable).

First, assume that
{\mathfrak{T}},v\models\mathbf{EX}\ \forall\ p\ \psi
with
{\mathfrak{T}}=\langle V,E,l\rangle
.
Thus, there exists
v^{\prime}
such that
vEv^{\prime}
and
{\mathfrak{T}},v^{\prime}\models\forall\ p\ \psi
.
Hence, for all
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime}\approx_{\mathit{AP}\setminus\{p\}}{\mathfrak{T}}
, we have
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime},v^{\prime}\models\psi
. As
v^{\prime}
remains a child of
v
for all
such variants
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime}
, we have that for
all
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime}\approx_{\mathit{AP}\setminus\{p\}}{\mathfrak{T}}
,
vE^{\prime}v^{\prime}
and
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime},v^{\prime}\models\psi
.
Thus, for all
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime}\approx_{\mathit{AP}\setminus\{p\}}{\mathfrak{T}}
we have
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime},v\models\mathbf{EX}\psi
.
We conclude
{\mathfrak{T}},v\models\forall\ p\ \mathbf{EX}\psi
.

Conversely (and this is the place where the tree structure is essential),
assume
{\mathfrak{T}},v\models\forall\ p\ \mathbf{EX}\psi
.
It means that for all
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime}\approx_{\mathit{AP}\setminus\{p\}}{\mathfrak{T}}
, we have
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime},v\models\mathbf{EX}\psi
.

Ad absurdum
, suppose
{\mathfrak{T}},v\not\models\mathbf{EX}\ \forall\ p\ \psi

Equivalently, we have
{\mathfrak{T}},v\models\mathbf{AX}\ \exists\ p\ \neg\psi

Thus, for all
v^{\prime}
such that
vEv^{\prime}
, there is

{\mathfrak{T}}_{v^{\prime}}\approx_{\mathit{AP}\setminus\{p\}}{\mathfrak{T}}
(say
{\mathfrak{T}}_{v^{\prime}}=\langle V,E,l_{v^{\prime}}\rangle
)

{\mathfrak{T}}_{v^{\prime}},v^{\prime}\not\models\psi
.
Let
{\mathfrak{T}}_{v^{\prime}}^{\prime}=\langle V_{v^{\prime}},E_{v^{\prime}},l_{v^{\prime}}^{\prime}\rangle

of
{\mathfrak{T}}_{v^{\prime}}

v^{\prime}
. Obviously

{\mathfrak{T}}_{v^{\prime}}^{\prime},v^{\prime}\not\models\psi

too. Two distinct children
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
of

in
{\mathfrak{T}}
,
lead to tree models
{\mathfrak{T}}_{v_{1}}^{\prime}=\langle V_{v_{1}},E_{v_{1}},l_{v_{1}}^{\prime}\rangle
and

{\mathfrak{T}}_{v_{2}}^{\prime}=\langle V_{v_{2}},E_{v^{\prime}},l_{v_{2}}^{\prime}\rangle

Indeed,
{\mathfrak{T}}
is a tree. Let

\langle V,E,(\uplus_{v^{\prime}\in E(v)}l_{v^{\prime}}^{\prime})\uplus\{v\mapsto l(v)\}\rangle

One can show that

and for all

Ryser’s Theorem for 𝝆 \operatorname{\bm{\rho}} -latin Rectangles
Amin Bahmanian

Abstract
. Let L L be an n × n n\times n array whose top left r × s r\times s subarray is filled with k k different symbols, each occurring at most once in each row and at most once in each column. We find necessary and sufficient conditions that ensure the remaining cells of L L can be filled such that each symbol occurs at most once in each row and at most once in each column, and each symbol occurs a prescribed number of times in L L . The case where the prescribed number of times each symbol occurs is n n was solved by Ryser (Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 2 (1951), 550–552), and the case s = n s=n was settled by Goldwasser et al. (J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 130 (2015), 26–41). Our technique leads to a very short proof of the latter.

1.
Introduction

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
with
1\leq\rho_{\ell}\leq n\leq k
for
\ell\in[k]:=\{1,2,\dots,k\}
such that
\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\rho_{\ell}=n^{2}
. A
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square

L
of order
n
is an
n\times n
array filled with
k
different
symbols
,
[k]
, each occurring at most once in each row and at most once in each column, such that each symbol
\ell
occurs exactly
\rho_{\ell}
times in
L
for
\ell\in[k]
. An
r\times s

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangle
on the set
[k]
of symbols is an
r\times s
array in which each symbol in
[k]
occurs at most once in each row and in each column, and in which each symbol
\ell
occurs at most
\rho_{\ell}
times for
\ell\in[k]
. A
latin square
(or
rectangle
) is a
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square (or
rectangle
) with
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}=(n,\dots,n)
.

We are interested in conditions that ensure that an
r\times s

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangle can be extended to a
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square. The cases
(\operatorname{\bm{\rho}},s)=((n,\dots,n),n)
, and
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}=(n,\dots,n)
were settled by Hall
[
5
]
, and Ryser
[
10
]
, respectively. Most recently, the case
s=n
was solved by Goldwasser, Hilton, Hoffman, and Özkan
[
4
]
(See Theorem
1.1
). For an old yet excellent survey on embedding latin squares, we refer the reader to Lindner
[
7
]
.

We need a few pieces of notation before we can state our main result. The complement of a set
S
is denoted by
\bar{S}
,
x\dotdiv y:=\max\{0,x-y\}
, and we write
x-y\dotdiv z
for
(x-y)\dotdiv z
. If
L
is an
r\times s

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangle,
e_{\ell}
will denote the number of occurrences of symbol
\ell
in
L
. We will always assume that
n>\min\{r,s\}
, and that
[r],[s]
, and
[k]
are the set of rows, the set of columns, and the set of symbols of
L
.
Let
i
be a row in
[r]
,
j
be a column in
[s]
,
\ell
be a symbol in
[k]
, and let
I\subseteq[r],J\subseteq[s],K\subseteq[k]
. Then
\mu_{K}(i),\mu_{K}(j),\mu_{I}(\ell)
, and
\mu_{J}(\ell)
are the number of symbols in
K
that are missing in row
i
, the number of symbols in
K
that are missing in column
j
, the number of rows in
I
where symbol
\ell
is missing, and the number of columns in
J
where symbol
\ell
is missing, respectively, and

\displaystyle\mu_{K}(I)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i\in I}\mu_{K}(i),

\displaystyle\mu_{I}(K)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell\in K}\mu_{I}(\ell),

\displaystyle\mu_{K}(J)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j\in J}\mu_{K}(j),

\displaystyle\mu_{J}(K)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell\in K}\mu_{J}(\ell).

Observe that

\displaystyle\mu_{K}(I)=\mu_{I}(K),

\displaystyle\mu_{I}(\ell)+\mu_{\bar{I}}(\ell)=r-e_{\ell},

\displaystyle\mu_{K}(J)=\mu_{J}(K),

\displaystyle\mu_{J}(\ell)+\mu_{\bar{J}}(\ell)=s-e_{\ell}.

Using our notation, the main result of
[
4
]
can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
4
]

An
r\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangle
L
can be completed to an
n\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square if and only if
\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}\leq n-r
for
\ell\in[k]
, and either one of the following conditions hold.

(1)

\displaystyle|J|(n-r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\min\Big\{\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell},\mu_{J}(\ell)\Big\}

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[n],

(2)

\displaystyle|J|(n-r)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\Big(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}\dotdiv\mu_{\bar{J}}(\ell)\Big)

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[n].

Rewriting (
1
) for
\bar{J}
will result in (
2
), so conditions (
1
) and (
2
) are essentially the same (See Remark
2.4
). In
[
4
]
, (
1
) is being referred to as Hall’s refined condition, which is a special case of the more general Hall’s constrained condition. If you think of a set
J
of columns of
L
as an
r\times|J|
subrectangle of
L
, Hall’s constrained condition checks an inequality similar to that of Hall’s refined condition for all subsets of cells, rather than those subsets that form an
r\times|J|
rectangle.

We provide a short proof of Theorem
1.1
(See Theorem
3.1
). We also generalize Theorem
1.1
further by establishing an analogue of Ryser’s Theorem for
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangles.

Suppose that an
r\times s

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangle
L
is extended to an
n\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square, and let us fix a symbol
\ell\in[k]
. On the one hand, there are
\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}
occurrences of symbol
\ell
outside the top left
r\times s
subsquare. On the other hand, there are at most
n-r
occurrences of symbol
\ell
in the last
n-r
rows and at most
n-s
occurrences of symbol
\ell
in the last
n-s
columns. Therefore, we must have

(3)

\displaystyle e_{\ell}\geq r+s+\rho_{\ell}-2n\quad\forall\ell\in[k].

It turns out that condition (
3
) is a special case of a more general necessary condition. Let

\displaystyle P_{t}=\{\ell\in[k]\ |\ \rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}>n-t\}\quad\mbox{for }t\in\{r,s\}.

Note that
P_{\min\{r,s\}}\subseteq P_{\max\{r,s\}}
.

Definition 1.2
.

A sequence
\{(a_{\ell},b_{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{k}
is said to be fitting if
a_{\ell},b_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
for
\ell\in[k]
, and the following conditions are satisfied.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}=r(n-s)+|P_{r}|(n-r)-\sum_{\ell\in P_{r}}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}),\\[20.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in[k]}b_{\ell}=s(n-r)+|P_{s}|(n-s)-\sum_{\ell\in P_{s}}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}),\\[20.0pt]
a_{\ell}+b_{\ell}\leq\begin{cases}2n-r-s+e_{\ell}-\rho_{\ell}&\mbox{if }\ell\in P_{\min\{r,s\}},\\
n-\max\{r,s\}&\mbox{if }\ell\in P_{\max\{r,s\}}\backslash P_{\min\{r,s\}},\\
\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}&\mbox{if }\ell\in\bar{P}_{\max\{r,s\}}.\end{cases}\end{cases}

Here is our main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

An
r\times s

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangle
L
can be completed to an
n\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square if and only if
there exists a fitting sequence
\{(a_{\ell},b_{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{k}

such that any of the following conditions

\displaystyle|I|(n-s)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in P_{r}}\min\Big\{a_{\ell}+\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}-n+r,\mu_{I}(\ell)\Big\}+\sum_{\ell\in\bar{P}_{r}}\min\Big\{a_{\ell},\mu_{I}(\ell)\Big\}

\displaystyle\forall I\subseteq[r],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in(K\cap P_{r})}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[r]}\min\Big\{n-s,\mu_{K}(i)\Big\}-\sum_{\ell\in K}a_{\ell}+|K\cap P_{r}|(n-r)

\displaystyle\forall K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle|I|(n-s)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\ell\in P_{r}}\Big(a_{\ell}+\rho_{\ell}+\mu_{I}(\ell)\dotdiv n\Big)+\sum_{\ell\in\bar{P}_{r}}\Big(a_{\ell}\dotdiv\mu_{\bar{I}}(\ell)\Big)

\displaystyle\forall I\subseteq[r],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in(K\cap P_{r})}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in[r]}\Big(n-s\dotdiv\mu_{\bar{K}}(i)\Big)+|K\cap P_{r}|(n-r)-\sum_{\ell\in K}a_{\ell}

\displaystyle\forall K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle|I|(n-s)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in(K\cap P_{r})}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})+\sum_{\ell\in K}a_{\ell}+\mu_{I}(\bar{K})-|K\cap P_{r}|(n-r)

\displaystyle\forall I\subseteq[r],K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in(K\cap P_{r})}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq|I|(n-s)+|K\cap P_{r}|(n-r)+\mu_{K}(\bar{I})-\sum_{\ell\in K}a_{\ell}

\displaystyle\forall I\subseteq[r],K\subseteq[k],

together with any of the following conditions hold.

\displaystyle|J|(n-r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in P_{s}}\min\Big\{b_{\ell}+\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}-n+s,\mu_{J}(\ell)\Big\}+\sum_{\ell\in\bar{P}_{s}}\min\Big\{b_{\ell},\mu_{J}(\ell)\Big\}

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[s],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in(K\cap P_{s})}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in[s]}\min\Big\{n-r,\mu_{K}(j)\Big\}-\sum_{\ell\in K}b_{\ell}+|K\cap P_{s}|(n-s)

\displaystyle\forall K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle|J|(n-r)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\Big(b_{\ell}+\rho_{\ell}+\mu_{J}(\ell)\dotdiv n\Big)+\sum_{\ell\in\bar{P}_{s}}\Big(b_{\ell}\dotdiv\mu_{\bar{J}}(\ell)\Big)

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[s],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in(K\cap P_{s})}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in[s]}\Big(n-r\dotdiv\mu_{\bar{K}}(j)\Big)+|K\cap P_{s}|(n-s)-\sum_{\ell\in K}b_{\ell}

\displaystyle\forall K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle|J|(n-r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in(K\cap P_{s})}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})+\sum_{\ell\in K}b_{\ell}+\mu_{J}(\bar{K})-|K\cap P_{s}|(n-s)

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[s],K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in(K\cap P_{s})}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq|J|(n-r)+|K\cap P_{s}|(n-s)+\mu_{K}(\bar{J})-\sum_{\ell\in K}b_{\ell}

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[s],K\subseteq[k].

For a much simpler generalization of Ryser’s Theorem, see Corollary
5.3
.

2.
Tools

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\approx y
means
\lfloor y\rfloor\leq x\leq\lceil y\rceil
. For a real-valued function
f
on a domain
D
and
S\subseteq D
,
f(S):=\sum_{x\in S}f(x)
.

All graphs under consideration are loopless, but they may have parallel edges. For a graph
G=(V,E)
,
u,v\in V
and
S,T\subseteq V
with
S\cap T
,
\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{G}(u)
,
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uv)
,
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uS)
, and
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(ST)
denote the number of edges incident with
u
, the number of edges whose endpoints are
u
and
v
, the number of edges between
u
and
S
, and the number of edges between
S
and
T
, respectively. If the edges of
G
are colored with
k
colors (the set of colors is always
[k]
), then
G(\ell)
is the color class
\ell
of
G
for
\ell\in[k]
. A bigraph
G
with bipartition
\{X,Y\}
will be denoted by
G[X,Y]
, and for
S\subseteq X
,
\bar{S}:=X\backslash S
.

Let
G
be a graph whose edges are colored, and let
\alpha\in V(G)
. By
splitting

\alpha
into
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p}
, we obtain a new graph
F
whose vertex set is
\left(V(G)\backslash\{\alpha\}\right)\cup\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p}\}
so that each edge
\alpha u
in
G
becomes
\alpha_{i}u
for some
i\in[p]
in
F
. Intuitively speaking, when we
split
a vertex
\alpha
into
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p}
, we share the edges incident with
\alpha
among
\alpha_{1}\dots,\alpha_{p}
. In this manner,
F
is a
detachment
of
G
, and
G
is an
amalgamation
of
F
obtained by
identifying

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p}
by
\alpha
. The following detachment lemma will be crucial in the proof of our main result.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
2
]

Let
G
be a graph whose edges are colored with
k
colors, and let
\alpha,\beta
be two vertices of
G
. There exists a graph
F
obtained by splitting
\alpha
and
\beta
into
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p}
, and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q}
, respectively, such that the following conditions hold.

(i)

\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{F(\ell)}(\alpha_{i})\approx\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{G(\ell)}(\alpha)/p
for
i\in[p],\ell\in[k]
;

(ii)

\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{F(\ell)}(\beta_{j})\approx\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{G(\ell)}(\beta)/q
for
j\in[q],\ell\in[k]
;

(iii)

\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{F}(\alpha_{i}u)\approx\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(\alpha u)/p
for
i\in[p],u\in V(G)\backslash\{\alpha,\beta\}
;

(iv)

\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{F}(\beta_{j}u)\approx\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(\beta u)/q
for
j\in[q],u\in V(G)\backslash\{\alpha,\beta\}
;

(v)

\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{F}(\alpha_{i}\beta_{j})\approx\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(\alpha\beta)/(pq)
for
i\in[p],j\in[q]
.

For a hypergraph analogue, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
. To give the reader an idea about the usefulness of this detachment lemma, let us show how to construct
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin squares. Theorem
2.2
can be viewed as an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
(See
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

For every
n,k,\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}:=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
with
1\leq\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k}\leq n\leq k
and
\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\rho_{\ell}=n^{2}
, there exists a
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square of order
n
.

Proof.

Let
G[\{\alpha\},\{\beta\}]
be a
k
-edge-colored bigraph with
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(\alpha\beta)=n^{2}
,
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G(\ell)}(\alpha\beta)=\rho_{\ell}
for
\ell\in[k]
.
Applying the detachment lemma with
p=q=n
yields the complete bigraph
F\cong K_{n,n}
whose colored edges corresponds to symbols in the desired
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square of order
n
.
∎

Let
f,g
be integer functions on the vertex set of a graph
G
such that
0\leq g(x)\leq f(x)
for all
x
. A
(g,f)
-factor
is a spanning subgraph
F
of
G
with the property that
g(x)\leq\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{F}(x)\leq f(x)
for each
x
, and an
f
-factor
is an
(f,f)
-factor. We need the following result which is known as Ore’s Theorem. For far reaching generalizations of Ore’s Theorem, we refer the reader to Lovász’s seminal paper
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
9
]

The bipgraph
G[X,Y]
has an
f
-factor if and only if
f(X)=f(Y)
and either one of the following conditions hold.

\displaystyle f(A)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in Y}\min\Big\{f(u),\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)\Big\}

\displaystyle\forall A\subseteq X,

\displaystyle f(A)

\displaystyle\leq f(B)+\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(A\bar{B})

\displaystyle\forall A\subseteq X,B\subseteq Y.

Remark 2.4
.

Let us fix
A\subseteq X
. For
u\in Y
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa(u)

\displaystyle:=\min\Big\{f(u),\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)\Big\}+\Big(f(u)\dotdiv\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)\Big)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)+\Big(f(u)-\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)\Big)&\mbox{if }f(u)\geq\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)\\
f(u)+0&\mbox{if }f(u)<\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)\end{cases}

\displaystyle=f(u).

So,
\sum_{u\in Y}\kappa(u)=f(Y)
, and the first condition in Theorem
2.3
is equivalent to

\displaystyle f(A)

\displaystyle\leq f(Y)-\sum_{u\in Y}\Big(f(u)\dotdiv\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)\Big),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle f(\bar{A})\geq\sum_{u\in Y}\Big(f(u)\dotdiv\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(uA)\Big).

3.
Hall’s Theorem for
\rho
-latin Rectangles

Hall
[
5
]
showed that any
r\times n
latin rectangle can be extended to an
n\times n
latin square. Here we give a short proof of the main result of
[
4
]
which generalizes Hall’s theorem to
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangles.

Theorem 3.1
.

An
r\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin rectangle
L
can be completed to an
n\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-latin square if and only if
\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}\leq n-r
for
\ell\in[k]
, and any of the following conditions hold.

\displaystyle|J|(n-r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\min\Big\{\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell},\mu_{J}(\ell)\Big\}

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[n],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in K}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in[n]}\min\Big\{n-r,\mu_{K}(j)\Big\}

\displaystyle\forall K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle|J|(n-r)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\Big(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}\dotdiv\mu_{\bar{J}}(\ell)\Big)

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[n],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in K}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in[n]}\Big(n-r\dotdiv\mu_{\bar{K}}(j)\Big)

\displaystyle\forall K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle|J|(n-r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in K}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})+\mu_{J}(\bar{K})

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[n],K\subseteq[k],

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in K}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq|J|(n-r)+\mu_{K}(\bar{J})

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[n],K\subseteq[k].

Proof.

Recall that the set of symbols used is
[k]
. The necessity of

(4)

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq n-r\quad\forall\ell\in[k]

is immediate from (
3
). The necessity of the remaining conditions will be evident at the end of the proof. For now, let us assume that (
4
) is satisfied. Let
F[\tilde{X},Y]
be the complete bigraph
K_{n,n}
where
\tilde{X}:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\},Y:=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, and let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
. The edge
x_{i}y_{j}
of
F
is colored
\ell
if
L_{ij}=\ell
for
i\in[r],j\in[n]
. We have
\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{F(\ell)}(u)\leq 1
for
u\in X\cup Y,

e_{\ell}=|E(F(\ell))|\leq\rho_{\ell}
for
\ell\in[k]
, and
\sum_{\ell\in[k]}e_{\ell}=rn
.
Let
G
be the bigraph obtained from
F
by amalgamating
x_{r+1},\dots,x_{n}
into a single vertex
\alpha
, so
\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(\alpha y_{j})=n-r
for
j\in[n]
. Let
\Gamma[Y,[k]]
be the simple bigraph whose edge set is

\{u\ell\ \big|\ u\in Y,\ell\in[k],\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{F(\ell)}(u)=0\}.

For
j\in[n]
,
\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{F(\ell)}(y_{j})=r
, and for
\ell\in[k]
, there are
e_{\ell}
edges in
F(\ell)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Gamma}(y_{j})=k-r&\mbox{if }j\in[n],\\
\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Gamma}(\ell)=n-e_{\ell}&\mbox{if }\ell\in[k].\end{cases}

Observe that
L
can be completed if and only if the uncolored edges of
F
can be colored so that

(5)

\displaystyle\forall\ell\in[k],\quad\quad\begin{cases}\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{F(\ell)}(u)\leq 1&\mbox{if }u\in\tilde{X}\cup Y,\\
|E(F(\ell))|=\rho_{\ell}.\end{cases}

We show that the coloring of
F
can be completed such that (
5
) holds if and only if the coloring of
G
can be completed so that

(6)

\displaystyle\forall\ell\in[k],\quad\quad\begin{cases}\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{G(\ell)}(u)\leq 1&\mbox{if }u\in X\cup Y,\\
\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{G(\ell)}(\alpha)\leq n-r,\\
|E(G(\ell))|=\rho_{\ell}.\end{cases}

To see this, first assume that the coloring of
F
can be completed such that (
5
) holds. Identifying all the vertices in
\tilde{X}\backslash X
of
F
by
\alpha
we will get the graph
G
satisfying (
6
). Conversely, suppose that we have a coloring of
G

such that (
6
) holds. Applying Lemma
2.1
to
G
, we get a graph
F^{\prime}
obtained by splitting
\alpha
into
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-r}
such that the following hold.

(i)

\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{F^{\prime}(\ell)}(\alpha_{i})\approx\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{G(\ell)}(\alpha)/(n-r)\leq 1
for
i\in[n-r],\ell\in[k]
;

(ii)

\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{F^{\prime}}(\alpha_{i}u)=\operatorname{\textup{{mult}}}_{G}(\alpha u)/(n-r)=1
for
i\in[n-r],u\in Y
.

Since
F^{\prime}\cong F
and the coloring of
F^{\prime}
satisfies (
5
), we are done.

Now, we show that the coloring of
G
can be completed such that (
6
) is satisfied if and only if there exists a subgraph
\Theta
of
\Gamma
with
n(k-r)
edges so that

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Theta}(y_{j})=n-r&\mbox{if }j\in[n],\\
\operatorname{\textup{{deg}}}_{\Theta}(\ell)=\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}&\mbox{if }\ell\in[k].\end{cases}

To prove this, suppose that the coloring of
G
can be completed such that (
6
) holds. Let
\Theta[Y,[k]]
be the bigraph whose edge set is

Remark 4.1
.

(a)

Let us verify that Theorem
1.3
implies Theorem
3.1
. In Theorem
1.3
, let
s=n
. Recall that the existence of the fitting sequence
\{(a_{\ell},b_{\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{k}
implies that
\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}\leq n-r
for
\ell\in[k]
. Therefore,
P_{r}=\emptyset,P_{n}=\{\ell\in[k]\ |\ \rho_{\ell}>e_{\ell}\}
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}=0,\\
\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in[k]}b_{\ell}=n(n-r)-\sum_{\ell\in P_{n}}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})=n(n-r)-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell})=0,\\
a_{\ell}+b_{\ell}\leq\begin{cases}n-r+e_{\ell}-\rho_{\ell}&\mbox{if }\ell\in\emptyset,\\
0&\mbox{if }\ell\in P_{n},\\
\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}=0&\mbox{if }\ell\in\bar{P}_{n}.\end{cases}\end{cases}

Hence, there is a unique fitting sequence, namely,
a_{\ell}=b_{\ell}=0
for
\ell\in[k]
.
Consequently, the first six conditions of Theorem
1.3
will be trivial, and the remaining six conditions will be identical to those of Theorem
3.1
.

(b)

Now, we show that Ryser’s Theorem is immediate from Theorem
1.3
. In Theorem
1.3
, if we let
k=n
(so
L
is a latin rectangle), then the existence of
\Theta\subseteq\Gamma
satisfying (
10
) is trivial. In fact, in this case,
\Theta=\Gamma
.

Remark 4.2
.

We can restate Theorem
1.3
without introducing the sets
P_{r}
and
P_{s}
. To do so, let us rewrite
f_{1}(\ell)=a_{\ell}+(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}+r\dotdiv n)
, and
f_{2}(\ell)=b_{\ell}+(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}+s\dotdiv n)
. The main condition in the definition of fitting sequence can be written in the following manner.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}=r(n-s)-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}(\rho_{\ell}-e_{\ell}+r\dotdiv n),\\[20.0pt]

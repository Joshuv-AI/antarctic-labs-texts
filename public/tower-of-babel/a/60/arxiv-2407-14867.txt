1.
Introduction

For
T>0
, let
N(T)
be the number of zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
in the rectangle
0<\beta<1
,
0<\gamma\leq T
.
In particular, Davenport
[
2
, Chapter 15]
gives that the approximate formula of
N(T)
is

\displaystyle N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac{7}{8}+S(T)+O(T^{-1}),

where

S(T)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg\zeta\big(\frac{1}{2}+iT\big).

The argument of
\zeta\big(1/2+iT\big)
is defined as the continuous variation along the horizontal
segment from
\infty+iT
to
1/2+iT
starting at
\infty+iT
with the value
0
.
Understanding the complicated behavior of
S(t)
is an interesting topic in analytic number theory,
and a series of results have emerged in recent years due to Selberg’s pioneering work.

In
[
12
,
13
]
, Selberg showed that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
one has

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}S(t)^{2n}\mathrm{d}t=\frac{(2n)!}{n!(2\pi)^{2n}}T(\log\log T)^{n}+O\big(T(\log\log T)^{n-1/2}\big),

which can imply, on average sense,
|S(t)|
has order of magnitude
\sqrt{\log\log T}
.
The same method can be applied to get a similar result relating to Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)
,
where
\chi
is the primitive Dirichlet character of modulus
q
.
More precisely, for prime modulus
q
and
t>0
, Selberg
[
14
]
showed that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod\,}{q})}S(t,\chi)^{2n}=\frac{(2n)!}{n!(2\pi)^{2n}}q(\log\log q)^{n}+O\big(q(\log\log q)^{n-1/2}\big),

where
*
denotes the summation goes through the primitive characters
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod\,}{q})
,
and
S(t,\chi)
is defined by

S(t,\chi)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L\big(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi\big).

Note that the counterparts of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for the odd-order moments
can also be obtained by making a minor adaptation in Selberg’s techniques.

For higher rank
L
-functions, there are some analogues of (
1.2
) in the spectral and weight aspects, respectively.
To provide context for these results and our own work, we first recall some fundamental concepts.
The notation
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
denotes the general linear group of
n\times n
invertible matrices,
while
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
(resp.
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
) is
the special linear group of
n\times n
real matrices (resp. integer matrices) with determinant 1. For a positive integer
q
, the Hecke congruence subgroup of level
q
is defined as

\Gamma_{0}(q)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&\cdots&a_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{n1}&\cdots&a_{nn}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}):a_{ij}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod\,}{q})\text{ for all }i>j\right\}.

For
n=2
, this group acts on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}=\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:y>0\}
via Möbius transformations
z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d)
; for general
n
, the corresponding symmetric space is
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}_{n}
, with
\textrm{SO}_{n}
the group of all
n\times n
real orthogonal matrices with determinant 1.
Within this framework, and under Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), Hejhal and Luo
[
4
]
(resp. Liu and Liu
[
10
]
) obtained an asymptotic
formula for the spectral moments of
S_{F}(t)=\pi^{-1}\arg L\big(\frac{1}{2}+it,F\big)
for each fixed
t>0
, where

F
is the Hecke–Maass cusp form for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
(resp.
\rm SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
)
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{F}=1/4+t_{F}^{2}
(resp. the spectral parameter
\nu_{F}=(\nu_{F,1},\nu_{F,2},\nu_{F,3})
).
Recently, when
f
is a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with weight
k
,
Liu and Shim
[
11
]
unconditionally establish the asymptotic formula for the moments of

S(t,f)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L\big(\frac{1}{2}+it,f\big)
,
by means of the weighted version of the zero-density estimate obtained by Hough
[
5
]
in the weight aspect.
Subsequently, the authors
[
16
]
(resp.
[
17
]
) employ similar
arguments to eliminate the dependence on the GRH in
[
4
]
(resp.
[
10
]
)
and obtain unconditional asymptotic formulas for higher-order moments.

In this paper, we are devoted to get an unconditional analog for
\rm GL_{2}
in the level aspect without assuming the GRH.
Assume
q
is a fixed large prime.
Let
S_{2}(q)
be the space of holomorphic cusp forms of weight
2
and level
q

equipped with the Petersson inner product

\displaystyle\left<f,g\right>=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(q)\setminus\mathbb{H}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y}{y^{2}}.

Here
\Gamma_{0}(q)\setminus\mathbb{H}
is a fundamental domain of the action of the Hecke congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{0}(q)
on the upper-half plane
\mathbb{H}
.
Let
H_{2}(q)
be a basis of

S_{2}(q)
that are eigenfunctions of all the Hecke operators and have the first Fourier coefficient
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
.
For each
f\in H_{2}(q)
, let

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{1/2}\lambda_{f}(n)e(nz),\quad\,\,z\in\mathbb{H}

be its Fourier expansion.
The Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
are real and satisfy
the Deligne’s bound (in particular, this is due to Eichler–Shimura
[
15
]
in the case of weight
2
)

(1.3)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq\tau(n),

with
\tau(n)
being the divisor function.
For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, the Hecke
L
-function associated to
f\in H_{2}(q)
is defined by

\displaystyle L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}(1-\lambda_{f}(p)p^{-s}+\varepsilon_{q}(p)p^{-2s})^{-1},

where
\varepsilon_{q}(\cdot)
is the principal Dirichlet character modulo
q
.
It can be analytically continued to the whole complex plane and has the completed
L
-function

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,f)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{q}}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)L(s,f),

which satisfies the functional equation

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,f)=\epsilon_{f}\Lambda(1-s,f),

where
\epsilon_{f}=q^{1/2}\lambda_{f}(q)\in\{\pm 1\}
is the sign of the functional equation.
Moreover, we have the following multiplicative property of the coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum\limits_{d|(m,n)}\varepsilon_{q}(d)\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right),\quad\text{for}\,\,m,n\geq 1.

If
p
is a prime
\neq q
, we write
1-\lambda_{f}(p)Y+Y^{2}=(1-\alpha_{f}(p)Y)(1-\beta_{f}(p)Y)
,
so

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(p)=\alpha_{f}(p)+\beta_{f}(p).

The bound (
1.3
) is equivalent (for
n
coprime with the level) with the assertion that
|\alpha_{f}(p)|=1

for all
p\neq q
. For
p=q
, the
p
-factor of
L(s,f)
is of degree at most 1, and we let
\alpha_{f}(p)=\lambda_{f}(p)
,
which is shown to be of modulus at most
1
, and
\beta_{f}(p)=0
.

Now we define
S(t,f)
by

\displaystyle S(t,f):=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg L(1/2+it,f),

where the argument
\arg L(1/2+it,f)
is obtained by continuous variation along the line
\operatorname{Im}(s)=t
from

\sigma=+\infty
to
\sigma=1/2
.
Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
t>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
be given.
For sufficiently large prime number
q
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\omega_{f}\cdot S(t,f)^{n}=C_{n}(\log\log q)^{n/2}+O_{t,n}\big((\log\log q)^{(n-1)/2}\big),\end{split}

where the harmonic weight
\omega_{f}
is defined by (
2.2
) below and
C_{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle C_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{n!}{(n/2)!(2\pi)^{n}},\,&\textit{if }\,n\,\,\text{is even},\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,n\,\,\text{is odd}.\end{cases}

Remark 1
.

For a holomorphic Hecke cusp form
f
with weight
2
and level prime number
q
,
Theorem
1.1
yields the asymptotic

\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\omega_{f}\cdot S(t,f)^{2m}\sim\frac{(2m)!}{m!(2\pi)^{2m}}(\log\log q)^{m},\quad m\in\mathbb{N},

as
q\rightarrow\infty
. Together with Lemma
2.3
below, which shows that
the harmonic weight
\omega_{f}
satisfies

\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\omega_{f}=1+o(1),

we conclude that
|S(t,f)|
on average has order of magnitude
\sqrt{\log\log q}
.

It is another interesting problem to consider the following probability measure

\mu_{q}
on
\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{q}(E):=\left(\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\omega_{f}\cdot\textbf{1}_{E}\left(\frac{S(t,f)}{\sqrt{\log\log q}}\right)\right)\bigg/\left(\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\omega_{f}\right),

where
\textbf{1}_{E}
is the characteristic function on a Borel measurable set
E
in
\mathbb{R}
.
As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following weighted central limit theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

As
q\rightarrow\infty
, the probability measure
\mu_{q}
converges to the Gaussian distribution of mean
0

and variance
(2\pi^{2})^{-1}
; that is

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{q}([a,b])=\sqrt{\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\exp(-\pi^{2}\xi^{2})\mathrm{d}\xi

for any
a<b
.

Notation.
Throughout the paper,

\varepsilon
and A are arbitrarily small and arbitrarily large positive
real numbers, which may be different at each occurrence.
As usual,
e(z)=\exp(2\pi iz)=e^{2\pi iz}
.
We write
f=O(g)
or
f\ll g
to mean
|f|\leq cg
for some unspecified positive constant
c
.
The notation
f\sim g
means that
f-g=o(g)
.
The symbol
f\asymp g
means that
f=O(g)
and
g=O(f)
.

2.
Preliminaries

We firstly collect some classical results as the beginning of this section.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x\geq 2
. The following statements hold.

(1) There exists a constant
c_{1}
, such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log x+c_{1}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right).

(2) We have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log x+O(1),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p}\ll(\log x)^{2}.

Proof.

See Davenport
[
2
, pp. 56–57]
.
∎

The Petersson trace formula is given by the following basic orthogonality relation on
H_{2}(q)

(see e.g., Kowalski–Michel
[
8
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
m,n\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\omega_{f}\cdot\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\delta_{m,n}-2\pi\sum_{c\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod\,}{q})}\frac{S(m,n;c)}{c}J_{1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\right),

where
J_{1}
is the Bessel function and
S(m,n;c)
is a classical Kloosterman sum,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\omega_{f}:=\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}=\frac{\zeta(2)}{|H_{2}(q)|}\cdot\frac{1}{L(1,\mathrm{sym}^{2}f)}\cdot\left(1+O\left(\frac{(\log q)^{4}}{q}\right)\right)

is the harmonic weight of
f
.

We record here some properties of Bessel functions will be used. The Bessel function
of the first kind,
J_{\nu}(x)
with
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
, has Taylor series about zero given by (see
[
3
, pg. 918, Eq. 8.440]
)

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(x)=\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{\nu}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!\Gamma(\nu+1+m)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m}.

Specializing to
\nu=k-1
with
k\geq 1
,
we have the following bound for
x>0
(see
[
18
, Sections 2.11, 7.1]
),

\displaystyle J_{k-1}(x)\ll_{k}\begin{cases}x^{k-1},\,&\textit{ if }x\leq 1,\\
x^{-1/2},\,&\textit{ if }x>1.\end{cases}

By a general bound for Kloosterman sums (see e.g.,
[
7
, (2.13)]
)

\displaystyle|S(m,n;c)|\leq(m,n,c)\min\left\{\frac{c}{(n,c)},\frac{c}{(m,c)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau(c),

and the bound
J_{1}(x)\ll x
for
J
-Bessel function, we derive (see e.g.,
[
7
, Corollary 2.3]
)

Lemma 2.3
.

For any integers
m,n\geq 1
with
6\pi\sqrt{mn}\leq q
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\omega_{f}\cdot\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\delta_{m,n}+O\left(\frac{\tau(q)}{q^{3/2}}\frac{(m,n,q)(mn)^{1/2}}{\big((m,q)+(n,q)\big)^{1/2}}(\tau(m,n))\right).

We will need the following weighted version of zero-density estimate
which was established by Kowalski–Michel
[
9
, Theorem 4]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
q
be a prime number and

N(f;\sigma,T):=\#\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma\,|\,L(\rho,f)=0,\sigma<\beta,|\gamma|<T\}.

There exists an absolute constant
A>1
such that for any real number
T
with
T\geq\frac{1}{\log q}
,
for any
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log q}\leq\sigma\leq 1
, and for any
c
with
0<c<\frac{1}{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\omega_{f}\cdot N(f;\sigma,T)\ll(1+T)^{A}q^{-2c(\sigma-1/2)}(\log q),

where the implied constant depends only on
c
.

Finally, we need the lemma of the theory of moments in probability theory (see, for example,
[
1
, Theorem 30.2]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that the distribution of random variable
X
is determined by its moments, that the
X_{n}
have moments of all orders, and that

\lim_{n}E[X_{n}^{r}]=E[X^{r}]
for
r=1,2,\cdots
. Then
X_{n}\Rightarrow X
(i.e.,
X_{n}
converges in distribution to
X
).

3.
An approximation of
S(t,f)

In this section we will prove several lemmas and derive an approximation of
S(t,f)
.
We denote by
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
a typical zero of
L(s,f)
inside the critical strip, i.e.,
0<\beta<1
.
For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, we have the Dirichlet series expansion for the logarithmic derivative of
L(s,f)
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)C_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\Lambda(n)
denotes the von Mangoldt function, and

(3.2)

\displaystyle C_{f}(n)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{f}(p)^{m}+\beta_{f}(p)^{m},\,&\textit{ if }\,n=p^{m}\,\,\textit{for a prime}\,\,p,\\
0,\,&\textit{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x>1
. For
s\neq\rho
, and
s\neq-m-\frac{1}{2}
, we have the following identity

\begin{split}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,f)=&-\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}-\frac{1}{\log^{2}x}\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}(1-x^{\rho-s})^{2}}{(\rho-s)^{3}}\\
&-\frac{1}{\log^{2}x}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-m-\frac{1}{2}-s}(1-x^{-m-\frac{1}{2}-s})^{2}}{(-m-\frac{1}{2}-s)^{3}},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{x}(n)=\begin{cases}\Lambda(n),\,&\textit{if }\,\,n\leq x,\\
\Lambda(n)\frac{\log^{2}(x^{3}/n)-2\log^{2}(x^{2}/n)}{2\log^{2}x},\,&\textit{if }\,\,x\leq n\leq x^{2},\\
\Lambda(n)\frac{\log^{2}(x^{3}/n)}{2\log^{2}x},\,&\textit{if }\,\,x^{2}\leq n\leq x^{3},\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,\,n\geq x^{3}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall the discontinuous integral

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{y^{s}}{s^{3}}\mathrm{d}s=\begin{cases}\frac{\log^{2}y}{2},\,&\textit{if }\,\,y\geq 1,\\
0,\,&\textit{if }\,\,0<y\leq 1\end{cases}

for
\alpha>0
.
It follows from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle-\log^{2}x\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{f}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{x^{u}(1-x^{u})^{2}}{u^{3}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s+u,f)\mathrm{d}u,

where
\alpha=\max\{2,1+\operatorname{Re}(s)\}
. By moving the line of integration all way to the left, we
pick up the residues at
u=0
,
u=\rho-s
and
u=-m-\frac{1}{2}-s
(
m=0,1,2,\cdots
), and deduce that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\alpha)}\frac{x^{u}(1-x^{u})^{2}}{u^{3}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s+u,f)\mathrm{d}u\\
=&\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,f)\log^{2}x+\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}(1-x^{\rho-s})^{2}}{(\rho-s)^{3}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-m-\frac{1}{2}-s}(1-x^{-m-\frac{1}{2}-s})^{2}}{(-m-\frac{1}{2}-s)^{3}}.\end{split}

Thus we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
s=\sigma+it
,
s^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime}+it^{\prime}
such that
1/2\leq\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\leq 10
,
s\neq\rho
,
s^{\prime}\neq\rho
,
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,f)-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s^{\prime},f)\right)=\operatorname{Im}\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}-\rho}\right)+O(1),

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,f)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma-\beta}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+O(\log(|t|+q)).

Proof.

By the Hadamard factorization theorem of the entire function
\Lambda(s,f)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,f)=b(f)+\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\right)-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(s+1/2)}{\Gamma(s+1/2)}-\frac{1}{2}\log q+\log(2\pi),

for some
b(f)\in\mathbb{C}
with
\operatorname{Re}(b(f))=-\operatorname{Re}\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho}
(see
[
6
, Proposition 5.7 (3)]
).
Note that for
z\not\in-\mathbb{N}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(z)=-\gamma+\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k-1+z}\right),

where
\gamma
is the Euler constant.
Using (
3.4
), we get that for
\frac{1}{4}\leq u
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(u+iv)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{v}{(k+u-1)^{2}+v^{2}}\ll 1.

And for
|\arg z|\leq\pi-\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(z)=\log z-\frac{1}{2z}+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\right),

thus we complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Let
x\geq 4
, we define

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}=\sigma_{x,f}=\sigma_{x,f,t}:=\frac{1}{2}+2\max_{\rho}\left\{\left|\beta-\frac{1}{2}\right|,\frac{5}{\log x}\right\},

where
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
runs through the zeros of
L(s,f)
for which

(3.6)

\displaystyle|t-\gamma|\leq\frac{x^{3|\beta-1/2|}}{\log x}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x\geq 4
. For
\sigma\geq\sigma_{x}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,f)=-\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}}+O\left(x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)+O\left(x^{1/4-\sigma/2}\log(|t|+q)\right),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-1/2}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}=O\left(\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)+O\big(\log(|t|+q)\big).

Proof.

This proof follows
[
11
, Lemma 4.3]
closely, and we give the details here for completeness.
By (
3.3
), we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,f)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-\beta}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+O(\log(|t|+q)).

Moreover, if
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
is zero of
L(s,f)
, then
(1-\beta)+i\gamma
is also a zero of
L(s,f)
.
Thus we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-\beta}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-\beta}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-(1-\beta)}{(\sigma_{x}-(1-\beta))^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\right)\\
=&\left(\sigma_{x}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{\rho}\frac{(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}-(\beta-1/2)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}{((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2})((\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2})}.\end{split}

If
|\beta-1/2|\leq\frac{\sigma_{x}-1/2}{2}
, then

\begin{split}(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}-(\beta-1/2)^{2}&\geq\frac{1}{2}\big((\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}+(\beta-1/2)^{2}\big)\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}\big).\end{split}

Thus,

(3.9)

\displaystyle(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}-(\beta-1/2)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\big((\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big).

(3.10)

\begin{split}&(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}-(\beta-1/2)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\\
&=\big((\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2}+(\beta-1/2)^{2}\big)+(t-\gamma)^{2}-2(\beta-1/2)^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\big((\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}\big)+\frac{7}{9}(t-\gamma)^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{4}\big((\sigma_{x}-1+\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}\big).\end{split}

Combining (

3.10
) and (

Using this bound and (

with

Theorem 3.4

\sigma=\sigma_{x}
in Lemma
3.3

\begin{split}J_{2}\leq&\,(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,f)\right|\\

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma_{x}+it,f)-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma+it,f)\right)\\

\begin{split}\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta||t-\gamma|}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma&\leq 3(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|t-\gamma|}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma\\

\displaystyle|t-\gamma|>\frac{x^{3|\beta-1/2|}}{\log x}>3|\beta-1/2|

\begin{split}|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta|\leq|\sigma-1/2|+|\sigma_{x}-1/2|+2|\beta-1/2|\leq 6|\beta-1/2|.\end{split}

\begin{split}\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta||t-\gamma|}{(\sigma-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{d}\sigma&<\int_{1/2}^{\sigma_{x}}\frac{|\sigma+\sigma_{x}-2\beta|}{|t-\gamma|}\mathrm{d}\sigma\\

3.3

\begin{split}|J_{3}|\leq&\,10(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\sum_{\rho}\frac{\sigma_{x}-1/2}{(\sigma_{x}-\beta)^{2}+(t-\gamma)^{2}}+O(\sigma_{x}-1/2)\\
=&\,O\left((\sigma_{x}-1/2)\left|\sum_{n\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(n)\Lambda_{x}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{x}+it}}\right|\right)+O\big((\sigma_{x}-1/2)\log(|t|+q)\big).\end{split}

Thus we complete the proof of lemma.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle M(t,f)=\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}=\frac{-i}{2\pi}\left(\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}-\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{1/2-it}}\right).

Set
x=q^{\delta/3}
for a suitably small
\delta>0
(to be specified later). Thus we have

\displaystyle M(t,f)^{n}=\frac{(-i)^{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\sum_{p\leq q^{\delta}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}-\sum_{p\leq q^{\delta}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{1/2-it}}\right)^{n}.

A general term in the expansion of the above has the form

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{f}(p_{1})^{m(p_{1})+n(p_{1})}}{p_{1}^{m(p_{1})(1/2+it)}(-1)^{n(p_{1})}p_{1}^{n(p_{1})(1/2-it)}}\times\cdots\times\frac{\lambda_{f}(p_{r})^{m(p_{r})+n(p_{r})}}{p_{r}^{m(p_{r})(1/2+it)}(-1)^{n(p_{r})}p_{r}^{n(p_{r})(1/2-it)}},

where
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{r}\leq q^{\delta}
,
m(p_{j})+n(p_{j})\geq 1
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{r}(m(p_{j})+n(p_{j}))=n
.

Now we discuss the contribution from the general term (
4.3
) in the following cases.

Case (I):

In the general term (
4.3
),
m(p_{j_{0}})\not\equiv n(p_{j_{0}})\,(\mathrm{mod\,}{2})
for some
j_{0}
.

With the Hecke relation (
1.4
), the numerator of this general term (
4.3
)
can be written as a sum whose terms are all of the form
\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)
where
m\neq n
.
Since for these terms
\delta_{m,n}=0
, we see from Lemma
2.3
that the contribution from the general term
(
4.3
) to the moment (
4.1
) is

\begin{split}&\ll_{n}\sum_{p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{r}\leq q^{\delta}}p_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}(m(p_{1})+n(p_{1}))}\cdots p_{r}^{-\frac{1}{2}(m(p_{r})+n(p_{r}))}q^{-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\delta n}{2}(1+\varepsilon)}\\
&\ll_{n}\bigg(\sum_{p\leq q^{\delta}}p^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)^{r}q^{-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\delta n}{2}(1+\varepsilon)}\ll_{n}q^{-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\delta n}{2}(2+\varepsilon)},\end{split}

which is negligible by taking
0<\delta<\frac{1}{n}
.

Case (II):

In the general term (
4.3
),
m(p_{j})\equiv n(p_{j})\,(\mathrm{mod\,}{2})
for all
j
,
and
m(p_{j_{0}})+n(p_{j_{0}})\geq 4
for some
j_{0}
.

In this case,
n
is even and
n>2r
, so
r<\frac{n}{2}
.
By using Hecke relations (
1.4
),
one sees that it is possible to have resulting terms
\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)
with
m=n
.
Using Lemma
2.3
, the contribution to the moment (
4.1
) such terms is bounded by
the diagonal term of size
\asymp 1
. The contribution from the off-diagonal terms is clearly negligible compared
to
1
, given that
0<\delta<\frac{1}{n}
.
Combining the bound (
1.3
) and (
2.1
),
we have the contribution from this general term to the moment (
4.1
) is

\begin{split}&\ll_{n}\sum_{p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{r}\leq q^{\delta}}p_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}(m(p_{1})+n(p_{1}))}\cdots p_{r}^{-\frac{1}{2}(m(p_{r})+n(p_{r}))}\\
&\ll_{n}\bigg(\sum_{p\leq q^{\delta}}p^{-1}\bigg)^{r}\ll_{n}(\log\log q)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}.\end{split}

It remains to discuss the following: In the general term (
4.3
),
m(p_{j})\equiv n(p_{j})\,(\mathrm{mod\,}{2})

and
m(p_{j})+n(p_{j})=2
for all
j
. Clearly
n
must be even, say
n=2m
and so
r=m
.

Case (III):

m(p_{j_{0}})=2
or
n(p_{j_{0}})=2
for some
j_{0}
.

Under the assumption
0<\delta<\frac{1}{n}
, we deduce that the contribution from these terms to (
4.1
) is
at most
O\big((\log\log q)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\big)
by using (
1.3
)
and the convergence of
\sum_{p}p^{-1-it}
for
t\neq 0
.

Case (IV):

m(p_{j})=n(p_{j})=1
for all
j
.

The contribution from these terms to (
4.1
) is

\begin{split}\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\binom{2m}{m}(m!)(m!)\frac{(-1)^{m}(-i)^{2m}}{(2\pi)^{2m}}\sum_{p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{m}\leq q^{\delta}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p_{1})^{2}\cdots\lambda_{f}(p_{m})^{2}}{p_{1}\cdots p_{m}}.\end{split}

By the Hecke relations, we have
\lambda_{f}(p_{1})^{2}\cdots\lambda_{f}(p_{m})^{2}=\lambda_{f}(p_{1}\cdots p_{m})\lambda_{f}(p_{1}\cdots p_{m})
.
Applying Lemma
2.3
and the assumption
0<\delta<\frac{1}{n}
, we have the above equals

\begin{split}&\frac{(2m)!}{(2\pi)^{2m}}\sum_{p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{m}\leq q^{\delta}}\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{m}}\cdot\left(1+O\left(q^{-3/2}(p_{1}\cdots p_{m})(\tau(p_{1}\cdots p_{m}))\right)\right)\\
=&\frac{(2m)!}{m!(2\pi)^{2m}}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{m}\leq q^{\delta}\atop p_{j}\,\text{distinct}}\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{m}}+O\bigg(q^{-3/2}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{m}\leq q^{\delta}\atop p_{j}\,\text{distinct}}1\bigg)\\
=&\frac{(2m)!}{m!(2\pi)^{2m}}(\log\log q)^{m}+O_{m}\big((\log\log q)^{m-1}\big).\end{split}

Now the asymptotic formula (
4.1
) follows from
Cases (I)–(IV)
under the assumption
0<\delta<\frac{1}{n}
.

Next we are ready to prove (
4.2
).
Recall that
\sigma_{x}=\sigma_{x,f}
depending on
f
.
By Lemma
4.2
,

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{|R(t,f)|^{2n}}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\\
\ll&\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\left|\operatorname{Im}\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)(\Lambda_{x}(p)-\Lambda(p))}{p^{1/2+it}\log p}\right|^{2n}+\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\left|\operatorname{Im}\sum_{p\leq x^{3/2}}\frac{C_{f}(p^{2})\Lambda_{x}(p^{2})}{p^{1+2it}\log p}\right|^{2n}\\
&+\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2n}x^{2n(\sigma_{x}-1/2)}}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\left(\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}\left|\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)}{p^{\sigma+it}}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{2n}\\
&+\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2n}}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\big(\log(|t|+q)\big)^{2n}+\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}.\end{split}

Since

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{x}(p)-\Lambda(p)|=O\left(\frac{\log^{3}p}{\log^{2}x}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad C_{f}(p^{2})=\lambda_{f}(p^{2})-\varepsilon_{q}(p),

the first two terms are of
O(1)
by the argument as (
4.1
).
The last two terms are of
O(1)
by Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
2.3
.
For the third term,
it follows from Cauchy’s inequality that

\begin{split}&\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{2n}x^{2n(\sigma_{x}-1/2)}}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\left(\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}\left|\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)}{p^{\sigma+it}}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{2n}\\
&\leq\left(\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{(\sigma_{x}-1/2)^{4n}x^{4n(\sigma_{x}-1/2)}}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\times\left(\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\left(\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}\left|\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)}{p^{\sigma+it}}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{4n}\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

By Hölder’s inequality with the exponents
4n/(4n-1)
and
4n
,

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}\left|\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)}{p^{\sigma+it}}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{4n}\\
\leq&\left(\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{4n-1}\left(\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}\left|\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)}{p^{\sigma+it}}\right|^{4n}\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{(\log x)^{4n-1}}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}\left|\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)}{p^{\sigma+it}}\right|^{4n}\mathrm{d}\sigma.\end{split}

Moreover, using the same argument as in proving (
4.1
),
we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\left|\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)}{p^{\sigma+it}}\right|^{4n}\\
\ll&\left(\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{\left|\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)p^{1/2-\sigma}\right|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2n}\ll\left(\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p}(\log x)^{2}\right)^{2n}\ll(\log x)^{8n}.\end{split}

Thus,

\begin{split}&\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\left(\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}\left|\sum_{p\leq x^{3}}\frac{C_{f}(p)\Lambda_{x}(p)\log(xp)}{p^{\sigma+it}}\right|\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{4n}\\
\ll&\frac{1}{(\log x)^{4n-1}}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}x^{1/2-\sigma}(\log x)^{8n}\mathrm{d}\sigma\ll(\log x)^{4n}.\end{split}

Using this bound and Lemma
4.1
, we have the third term in (
4.4
)
is of
O(1)
.
Thus we complete the proof of proposition.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the binomial theorem, we have

\begin{split}S(t,f)^{n}=M(t,f)^{n}+O_{n}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}|M(t,f)|^{n-\ell}|R(t,f)|^{\ell}\right).\end{split}

For
1\leq\ell\leq n
, we apply the generalized Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
4.3
, and get

\begin{split}&\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}|M(t,f)|^{n-\ell}|R(t,f)|^{\ell}\\
\ll&\left(\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{1}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{|M(t,f)|^{2n}}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\right)^{\frac{n-\ell}{2n}}\left(\sum_{f\in H_{2}(q)}\frac{|R(t,f)|^{2n}}{4\pi\left<f,f\right>}\right)^{\frac{\ell}{2n}}\\
\ll&{}_{t,n}(\log\log q)^{(n-\ell)/2}\ll_{t,n}(\log\log q)^{(n-1)/2}.\end{split}

Proof of Theorem

The
n
-th moment of

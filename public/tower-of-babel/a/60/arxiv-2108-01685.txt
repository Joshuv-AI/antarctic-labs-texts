Information disclosure in the framework of Kolmogorov complexity
Nikolay Vereshchagin

Abstract
We consider the network consisting of three nodes 1, 2, 3 connected by two open channels 1 → 2 1\to 2 and 1 → 3 1\to 3 . The information present in the node 1 consists of four strings x , y , z , w x,y,z,w . The nodes 2, 3 know x , w x,w and need to know y , z y,z , respectively. We want to arrange transmission of information over the channels so that both nodes 2 and 3 learn what they need and the disclosure of information is as small as possible. By information disclosure we mean the amount of information in the strings transmitted through channels about x , y , z , w x,y,z,w (or about x , w x,w ). We are also interested in whether it is possible to minimize the disclosure of information and simultaneously minimize the length of words transferred through the channels.

Introduction

Assume that a finite directed graph is given, its arcs are called
channels
.
Assume further each node is assigned two strings, called the
input string
and the
output string
of that node.
The input string is understood as the information that is present in the node, and
the output string as the information that is needed in the node.
The goal is to arrange the information transmission so that
every node gets what it needs. By the information transmission we mean an assignment
of strings to channels in such a way that for every node its output string has low
Kolmogorov complexity relative to the tuple consisting of its input string and all the strings assigned to
its input channels. “Low” means complexity of order

o(n)
, where
n
is the maximal length of all input and output strings.

Complexity of information transmission is measured by the following quantities.
First, we consider the amount of transmitted information, that is, Kolmogorov complexity of strings assigned to channels.
The less that complexity is the better. In this setting the problem was studied (for different networks) in the papers
[
9
,
2
,
4
,
7
]
.
Second, we consider information disclosure.
That is, we assume that an eavesdropper gets the tuple
T
consisting of all the strings assigned to channels
and measure how much information has
T
about input and output strings.

We know two papers,
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
, devoted to information disclosure in the framework of Kolmogorov complexity.
The authors of
[
8
]
considered two-parties
multi-round protocols of a random key generation
in such a way that that the eavesdropper has no information about the generated key. Before communication each party
has her/his own string.
The authors of
[
8
]
showed that
the maximal length of generated key is equal to the mutual information in those strings.

In
[
5
]
, the following problem was studied: Alice has strings

x,y
and Bob has only
x
and wants to know
y
; to this end Alice is allowed to send to Bob a string
p
; they both want the
eavesdropper to obtain as little information about
y
as possible (see Fig.
1
(b)). It is not hard to show
that any such string
p
(with negligible
C(y|p,x)
) has at least

C(y)-C(x)
bits of information about
y
and its complexity is at least
C(y|x)
.
It is shown in
[
5
]
that both lower bounds can be attained simultaneously, that is, there is
a string
p
of length
C(y|x)+O(\log n)
, that has
\max\{C(y)-C(x),0\}+O(\log n)
information about
y
(where
n
denotes the maximum of lengths of

x,y
).

In this paper, we study the network shown on Fig.
1
(a),
as well as networks obtained from it by identifying some input or output strings. Those networks are shown on Fig.
1
(b), (c), (d), (e), (f).

Now we will define information disclosure for this network.
Generally, we may define information disclosure as the amount of information
in the pair
(p,q)
about any non-empty group of

To simplify things, we will consider only two groups, the group

consists of all input strings that are not output strings. Strings

.
The information in
(p,q)
about the tuple
(x,y,z,w)

(p,q)

Thus we will consider the following four parameters:

•

the amounts of transmitted information,

,

•

the total disclosure

, and

•

the private disclosure

We will denote the maximum of lengths of strings

.

It is not hard to see that the total disclosure is equal to
C(p,q)
with accuracy
2\varepsilon+O(\log n)
.
Indeed, by Commutativity of information we have

I(p,q:w,x,y,z)=C(p,q)-C(p,q|w,x,y,z)+O(\log n),

hence

C(p,q)-2\varepsilon+O(\log n)\leqslant I(p,q:w,x,y,z)\leqslant C(p,q)+O(\log n).

Besides, all lower bounds for

C(p,q)
that we are able to prove hold for
I(p,q:w,x,y,z)
as well (with accuracy
O(\log n)
). On the other hand, all upper bounds for the total disclosure hold for
C(p,q)
as well.
Therefore, in the present paper the difference between
C(p,q)
and
I(p,q:w,x,y,z)

is not important, and in the sequel we will consider
C(p,q)
instead of the total disclosure.

For given strings

,
and the private disclosure for

?

I(p,q:x,w)
for the network of Fig.

(b),
as
I(p:x)
for the network Fig.
1
(b),
as
I(p,q:x,y)
for the network Fig.
1
(d), and
as
I(p,q:x)
for the networks Fig.

(e,f).
And the private disclosure is 0 by definition for the network Fig.

The second question is:

In brief, the results of this paper are the following:

•

We show that for the network of Fig.

(a) some results from the paper
[

]

imply that the minimal possible

for

For the networks of Fig.

2
Preliminaries

We write
string
to denote a finite binary string. Other
finite objects, such as pairs of strings, may be encoded into
strings in natural ways. The set of all strings is denoted by

\{0,1\}^{*}
and the length of a string
x
is denoted by
|x|
. The empty string is denoted by
\Lambda
.

Let
\bar{x}
denote the string

\underbrace{000\dots 0}_{|x|\text{ times}}1x=0^{|x|}1x.

We shall use the string
\bar{x}y
to encode the pair

(x,y)
of strings; the notation
[x,y]
will mean the
same as
\bar{x}y
.
Let
\log n
denote the binary logarithm of
n
.

A
programming language
is a partial computable function

F
from
\{0,1\}^{*}\times\{0,1\}^{*}
to
\{0,1\}^{*}
.
The first argument of
F
is called a program,
the second argument is called the input, and

F(p,x)
is called the output of program
p
on input
x
.
A programming language
U
is called
optimal

if for any other
programming language
F
there exists a string
t_{F}

such that
U(t_{F}p,x)=F(p,x)
for all
p,x
.
By Solomonoff – Kolmogorov theorem (see e.g.
[
3
,
10
]
)
optimal programming languages
exist. We fix some optimal programming language
U
and define

•

C(x|y)=\min\{|p|\mid U(p,y)=x\}
(conditional Kolmogorov
complexity
of
x
relative to
y
),

•

C(x)=C(x|\Lambda)
(Kolmogorov complexity
of
x
),

•

I(x:y)=C(y)-C(y|x)
(information in
x
about
y
),

•

I(x:y|z)=C(y|z)-C(y|[x,z])
(information in
x
about
y
relative to
z
),

•

J(x:y)=C(x)+C(y)-C([x,y])
,
J(x:y|z)=C(x|z)+C(y|z)-C([x,y]|z)
(mutual information
between
x
and
y
).

•

We say that strings
x
and
y

are
independent
if
J(x:y)
is close to 0.

If
U(p,y)=x
, we say that
p
is
a program for
x
relative to
y
.
If
U(p,\Lambda)=x
, we say that
p
is a program for
x
.

Instead of
C([x,y])
,
I(x:[y,z])
,
C(z|[x,y])
, and
C([x,y]|z)
we shall
write
C(x,y)
,
I(x:y,z)
,
C(z|x,y)
,
and
C(x,y|z)
respectively.
We use the following well known facts
(see
[
3
,
10
]
):

•

C(x)\leqslant|x|+O(1)
;

•

C(x|y)\leqslant C(x)+O(1)
;

•

C(x|y,z)\leqslant C(x|z)+O(1)
;

•

for any partial computable function
f(x)
there is a constant
c
such that

C(f(x))\leqslant C(x)+c
for all
x
in the domain of
f
;

•

Chain rules
:

\displaystyle C(x,y)

\displaystyle=C(x)+C(y|x)+O(\log C(x,y)),

\displaystyle C(x,y|z)

\displaystyle=C(x|z)+C(y|x,z)+O(\log C(x,y|z)),

\displaystyle C(x,y:u|z)

\displaystyle=C(x:u|z)+C(y:u|x,z)+O(\log C(x,y,u|z))

for all
x,y,z,u
;

•

Commutativity of information
:

I(x:y)=J(x:y)+O(\log C(x,y))=I(y:x)+O(\log C(x,y)),

•

the upper graph

\{(x,y,i)\mid C(x|y)\leqslant i\}
of conditional Kolmogorov complexity
is computably enumerable.

3
Information transmission in networks: definitions and previous results

3.1
Main definitions

Definition 1
.

We say that a pair of strings

(p,q)
is an
\varepsilon
information transmission
in the network of Fig.
1
(a) if all the quantities

C(p|w,x,y,z),C(q|w,x,y,z),C(y|p,x),C(z|q,w)

are less than
\varepsilon
.
In a similar way we define
\varepsilon
information transmission in other networks.

Definition 2
.

Let
f(x,y,z,w)
and
g(p,q,x,y,w,z)
be some integer valued functions.
We say that
f
is an
upper bound for
g
in the network of Fig.
1
(a)
if for all strings
x,y,z,w
of length at most
n
there is a pair of strings
p,q
of length
O(n)
that is

o(n)
information transmission in the network and
g(p,q,x,y,w,z)\leqslant f(x,y,z,w)+o(n)
.
(For example,
C(y|x)

is an upper bound for
C(p)
in the network of Fig.
1
(a).)

We say that
f
is
a
lower bound for
g
in the network of Fig.
1
(a)
if
for all strings
x,y,z,w
and for all
\varepsilon
information transmission pairs
(p,q)
,
it holds

g(p,q,x,y,w,z)\geqslant f(x,y,z,w)-c\varepsilon-d\log n-e,

where
c,d
are some absolute constants,

n
is the maximal length of strings

x,y,z,w,p,q
,
and the constant
e

depends on the choice of the optimal programming language in the definition of Kolmogorov complexity.
(For instance,
C(y|x)

is a lower bound for
C(p)
in the network of Fig.
1
(a).)

If a function
f
is both an upper and lower bound
for
g
,
then we say that
the minimal possible value of

g
is

f
. (For example, the minimal possible complexity of
p

in the network of Fig.
1
(a) is
C(y|x)
.)
In a similar way we define the notions of upper and lower bounds for other networks.

Definition 3
.

The profile of a quadruple of strings
x,y,z,w

is defined as the tuple consisting of 15 numbers

C(x),C(y),C(z),C(w),C(x,y),C(x,z),C(x,w),\dots,C(x,y,z,w)

We call a function of
x,y,z,w
, a
profile function
if it is a function of the profile of
x,y,z,w
.
If there is no profile function
f
of
x,y,z,w
that is the minimal possible value
of
g
for a network, then we say that
the minimal possible value of

g
for that network is not a profile function
.

3.2
The network of Fig.
1
(a)

The minimal possible values of
C(p),C(q)
in all the networks of Fig.
1
can be found quite easily.
Namely, the minimal possible
C(p)
or
C(q)
is equal to Kolmogorov complexity of the output string
relative to the input string in the node where the corresponding arrow directs to.
For example, in the network of Fig.
1
(a), the minimal possible
C(p)
is equal to
C(y|x)
and the minimal
C(q)
is

C(z|w)
. Both minimal values can be attained simultaneously.

More specifically, if a pair
(p,q)
is
\varepsilon
information transmission, then

\displaystyle C(p)\geqslant C(y|x)-\varepsilon-O(\log n),

(1)

\displaystyle C(q)\geqslant C(z|w)-\varepsilon-O(\log n).

(2)

This can be proved via cuts. The cut technique works as follows.

We choose a set of nodes, called
a cut
. Let

A
denote the tuple consisting of all input strings of the nodes of the cut and let

B
denote the tuple consisting of all output strings of the nodes of the cut. Finally, let
P

denote the tuple consisting of labels of channels directed to the nodes of the cut.
Then
C(B|A)
is a lower bound for
C(P)
and even for
I(P:A,B)
(hence for the total disclosure).

More specifically, we have the following

Lemma 1
.

For all strings
A,B,P
of length at most
n
we have

\displaystyle C(B|A)\leqslant I(P:(A,B))+C(B|P,A)+O(\log n).

(3)

Proof.

Move
C(B|P,A)
to the left hand side of the inequality (
3
).
Then the left hand side becomes

I(P:B|A)
.
By the Chain rule the right hand side equals

I(P:A)+I(P:B|A)
(ignoring
O(\log n)
terms), and hence is larger than the left hand side.
∎

Since
C(B|P,A)=O(\varepsilon)
, Lemma
1
implies that
C(B|A)
is indeed a lower bound for
I(P:A,B)
.
Plugging in Lemma
1

A=x,B=y,P=p
and
A=w,B=z,P=q
, we get the inequalities (
1
) and (
2
), respectively.

For some networks, cuts yield even better lower bounds for
I(P:(A,B))
than
C(B|A)
.
More specifically, sometimes we can remove some input strings from

A
keeping
C(B|P,A)=O(\varepsilon)
.
This happens when some string belongs both to
A
and
B
.
For example, consider the cut consisting of both right nodes in the network shown on Fig.
1
(f). Then we may exclude
the string
y
from
A
, since it can be obtained from
p
and
x
and some extra
\varepsilon
bits. Hence we have

C(y,z|p,q,x)<2\varepsilon+O(1)
and thus
C(y,z|x)
is a lower bound for the total disclosure in that network.
Another example: consider the cut consisting of both right nodes in the network of Fig.
1
(c). Then we can exclude
x
or
y
from
A
(but cannot
exclude both
x,y
). In this way we can show
that both
C(x,y|x)
and
C(x,y|y)
are lower bounds for the total disclosure in that network.

Obviously,
C(y|x)
and
C(z|w)
are also upper bounds for
C(p)
and
C(q)
, respectively
(for the network of Fig.
1
(a), and similar upper bounds hold for other networks from Fig.
1
).
Namely, we can let
p
be the minimum length program for
y
relative to
x
(its length equals
C(y|x)
).
Similarly, we can let
q
be the minimum length program for
z
relative to
w
.

The minimal
C(p,q)
for the network Fig.
1
(a) was studied in the paper
[
6
]
. It was shown that
the minimal
C(p,q)
for this network is not a profile function of
x,y,z,w
. Hence
the minimal total disclosure is not a profile function of
x,y,z,w
.

More specifically, the following function

f(x,y,z,w)=\min\{C(p,q)\mid y=U(p,x),z=U(q,w)\}

was studied in
[
6
]
.
Here
U
is the optimal programming language.
Notice that
f(x,y,z,w)
is different from
the minimal complexity of a pair that is
\varepsilon
information transmission in this network.
The difference is in that in the definition of
f(x,y,z,w)
we do not require
C(p|x,y,z,w),C(q|x,y,z,w)
be negligible.
Another difference is that we replace the requirements of negligibility of
C(y|p,x),C(z|q,y)
by stronger requirements

y=U(p,x)
,
z=U(q,w)
. However, this second difference is not important, since the inequalities
C(y|p,x),C(z|q,y)<\varepsilon
imply
that adding to
p,q
some extra

\varepsilon
bits we can obtain
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
with
y=U(p^{\prime},x)
,
z=U(q^{\prime},w)
.
However, the first difference is important.

In
[
6
]
, for every natural number

n
two quadruples of strings

x,y,z,w
and
x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},w^{\prime}
of length
O(n)
were defined.
The profiles of those quadruples coincide with
accuracy
O(\log n)
in all components and

f(x,y,z,w)\leqslant n
but
f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},w^{\prime})\geqslant 1.5n
(with accuracy
O(\log n)
).
Besides, the profiles of those tuples has the following feature:

C(y,z|x,w)=O(\log n)
. Hence for all
p,q
the total disclosure equals the private disclosure with accuracy
O(\log n)

(for both quadruples). The second feature is that the upper bound for
C(p,q)
for the first quadruple
is attained for a pair
p,q
with
C(p,q|x,y,z,w)=O(1)
. Therefore, for the first quadruple both the total and private disclosures are at most

n
while for the second quadruple both are at least
1.5n
(with accuracy
O(\log n)
).
Hence both minimal total and private disclosures are not profile functions for this
network.

3.3
The network Fig.
1
(c) and information distance

We skip the network of Fig.
1
(b) as an obvious one and proceed to the network of Fig.
1
(c).
Private disclosure is zero for this network, independently of the choice of
p,q
, since there are no private inputs.

The minimal
C(p,q)
for this network was essentially found
in the paper
[
2
]
. It was called

the information distance between
x
and
y
.
More precisely, the information distance between
x
and
y
is defined as
\min\{C(p,q)\mid U(p,x)=y,U(q,y)=x\}
,
where
U
stands for the optimal programming language.
In
[
2
]
, it was proved that the information distance between
x
and
y
equals
\max\{C(y|x),C(x|y)\}

with accuracy
O(\log\max\{C(y|x),C(x|y)\})
.
It follows from the proof, that the pair
(p,q)
witnessing this equality has logarithmic complexity relative to
the pair
x,y
, hence
\max\{C(y|x),C(x|y)\}

is an upper bound for
C(p,q)
for this network.
On the other hand,
\max\{C(y|x),C(x|y)\}
is a lower bound as well, which can be shown using
the cut consisting of both right nodes (or with two cuts, consisting of one right nodes each).

The results of
[
2
]
leave open the following question,
can the minimum values of

C(p),C(q),C(p,q)

be attained simultaneously? The positive answer
to this question follows from An. Muchnik’s theorem from the next section,
which is devoted to the network of Fig.
1
(d). It turns out that the network of Fig.
1
(c)
is a special case of the network of Fig.
1
(d), namely,
for
z=[x,y]
the framework Fig.
1
(d) essentially turns into the network of Fig.
1
(c).

Main results

4.1
The network of Fig.
1
(d) and An. Muchnik’s theorem

Basically this network was studied in the paper
[
4
]
,

Figure 2:
The network studied in
[
4
]

along with the network shown on Fig.

(the difference is in that the input to the left node equals

, and not
(x,y,z)
).
Using cuts we can show that
C(z|x)
is a lower bound for
C(p)
and
C(z|y)
is a lower bound for
C(q)
,
and
\max\{C(z|x),C(z|y)\}

These lower bounds can be attained, and moreover, they can be attained simultaneously. More specifically,
the following holds.

Theorem 1

(
[
4
]
)
.

For all
n

for all strings
x,y,z
of length at most
n

there exist consistent strings
p,q
(which means that one of the string
is a prefix of the other one) with

\displaystyle|p|

\displaystyle=C(z|x),

\displaystyle|q|

\displaystyle=C(z|y),

\displaystyle C(z|x,p)

\displaystyle=O(\log n),

\displaystyle C(z|y,p)

\displaystyle=O(\log n),

\displaystyle C(p,q|z)

\displaystyle=O(\log n).

Note that consistency of
p,q
implies that
C(p,q)=\max\{C(z|x),C(z|y)\}+O(\log n)
,
that is,

attains its minimum.

Finally, the next theorem states the minimum private disclosure

Recall that the invariant can break less than

The next theorem claims that for some
x,y,z

it is impossible to attain the minimum of the quantities

C(p),C(q),I(p,q:x)
simultaneously.

Theorem 7
.

For all natural
\varepsilon,l

of length at most

for all strings

\displaystyle C(p)<C(y|x)+\varepsilon-O(1),\quad C(q)<C(z|y)+\varepsilon-O(1)

\displaystyle C(p,q)-C(p,q|x)

\displaystyle\geqslant l+\varepsilon-O(\log l),\qquad C(z|y)-C(y,z|x)\leqslant l+O(1)

(that is, the private disclosure is about
\varepsilon

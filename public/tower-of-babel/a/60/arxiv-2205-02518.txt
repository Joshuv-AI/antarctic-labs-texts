Abstract
. In this paper we study removable singularities for solutions of the fractional heat equation in time varying domains. We introduce associated capacities and we study some of its metric and geometric properties.



1.
Introduction

In the present paper we study removable singularities for solutions of the fractional heat equation in time varying domains. Our main motivation comes from the paper
[
MPrTo
]
where we studied removable singularities for regular
(1,1/2)-
Lipschitz solutions of the heat equation in time varying domains. Our ambient space is
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with a generic point denoted as
\bar{x}=(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, where
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For a smooth function
f
depending on
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, the heat equation is just

(1.1)

\Theta(f)=\Delta f+\partial_{t}f=0,

with fundamental solution
\displaystyle W(x,t)=(4\pi t)^{-N/2}\exp(-|x|^{2}/4t)
for
t>0
and
0
if
t\leq 0
.

We set
0<s<1
and let
\Theta^{s}
denote the fractional heat operator,

\Theta^{s}=(-\Delta)^{s}+\partial_{t}.

Then, for a smooth function
f
depending on
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,

\Theta^{s}(f)=(-\Delta)^{s}f+\partial_{t}f=0

is just the fractional heat equation. The pseudodifferential operator
(-\Delta)^{s}=(-\Delta_{x})^{s}
,
0<s<1
, is the fractional Laplacian with respect to the
x
variable. It may be defined through its Fourier transform,
\displaystyle\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}}f(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{f}(\xi)
, or by its representation

(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)=c(n,s)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy,

where
c(n,s)
is a normalization constant (see
[
La
]
and
[
S
]
for its basic properties). The standard Laplace operator,
-\Delta
, is recovered when taking the limit
s\to 1
(see
[
DPaVa
, Section 4]
), but there is a big difference between the local operator
-\Delta
, that appears in the classical heat equation and represents Brownian motion, and the non-local family
(-\Delta)^{s}
,
0<s<1
, which are generators of Lévy processes in Stochastic PDEs (see
[
A
]
i
[
Be
]
).

Given
\bar{x}=(x,t)
and
\bar{y}=(y,u)
, with
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
t,u\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
we consider the

s
-parabolic distance in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
defined by

{\rm dist}_{p}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\max\big(|x-y|,\,|t-u|^{1/{2s}}\big).

Sometimes we also write

|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|_{p}
instead of
{\rm dist}_{p}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Notice that for
s=1/2
, dist
p
is comparable to the usual distance.
We denote by
B_{p}(\bar{x},r)
an
s
-parabolic ball (i.e., in the distance
{\rm dist}_{p}
) centered at
\bar{x}
with radius
r
. By an
s
-parabolic cube
Q
of side length
\ell
, we mean a set of the form

I_{1}\times\ldots\times I_{N}\times I_{N+1},

where
I_{1},\ldots,I_{N}
are intervals in
{\mathbb{R}}
with length
\ell
, and
I_{N+1}
is another interval with
length
\ell^{2s}
. We write
\ell(Q)=\ell
.

We say that a Borel measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
has upper parabolic growth of degree
N+2s-1
,
0<s<1
, if there
exists some constant
C
such that

(1.2)

\mu(B_{p}(\bar{x},r))\leq Cr^{N+2s-1}\quad\mbox{ for all $\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},\,r>0$.}

Clearly, this is equivalent to saying that any
s
-parabolic cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
satisfies
\mu(Q)\leq C^{\prime}\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}
.
Now, let
s=1/2
.
We say that a compact set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
is removable for the
1/2-
fractional heat equation (or removable for bounded
1/2-
caloric functions) if any bounded function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.
\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}<\infty
,
satisfying the
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus E
, also satisfies the
1/2-
fractional heat equation in the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
.

Given a compact set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, we define its
1/2-
fractional caloric capacity by

(1.3)

\gamma^{1/2}_{\Theta}(E)=\sup\{|\langle\nu,1\rangle|:\nu\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\,\operatorname{supp}\nu\subset E\mbox{ and }\|P*\nu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1\},

where
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is the space of distributions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and

P(x,t)
denotes the fundamental solution of the
1/2-
fractional heat equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, that is

(1.4)

P(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\cfrac{t}{(t^{2}+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}\quad\mbox{if }\;t>0\\
\\
\quad 0\hskip 75.39963pt\mbox{if }\;t\leq 0\end{array}\right..

The fractional heat equation can be solved (by applying Fourier transform) for all
0<s\leq 1
by means of the fundamental solution
P_{s}(x,t)
, which is the inverse transform of the function
e^{-|\xi|^{2s}t}
. It is worth mentioning that only in the particular cases
s=1
and
s=1/2
the kernel is known to be explicit. In the first case we get the Gaussian kernel for the standard heat equation
\displaystyle W(x,t)=(4\pi t)^{-N/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/4t}
and in the fractional case
s=1/2
we have (
1.4
). For
0<s<1
, there is no explicit fundamental solution for the
s-
fractional heat equation. In the 1960s, the probabilists Blumenthal and Getoor
[
BG
, Theorem 2.1]
generalized in a precise way the power-like tail behaviour observed in the case
s=1/2
to the other values of
0<s<1
, that is, they established that the fundamental solution,
P_{s}(x,t)
, of the fractional heat equation, for
0<s<1
, satisfies

P_{s}(x,t)\approx\frac{t}{(t^{1/s}+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}

when
t>0
(and
0
for
t\leq 0
). Here
a\approx b
means that
a/b
is uniformly bounded above and below by a constant. Notice the marked difference with the Gaussian kernel
W(x,t)
of the heat equation (
1.1
) (case
s=1
). The behaviour as
x
tends to infinity of
P_{s}
is power-like while
W
has exponential spatial decay.

In the paper, we will also introduce and study some
s
-fractional caloric capacities
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}
,
0<s<1
, associated with the kernels
P_{s}
of the
s
-fractional heat equation.

We shall now give a brief description of the main results in the paper.
Section
2
includes some estimates of the kernels
P_{s}(x,t)
(
0<s<1
) and its derivatives, that will be needed in the rest of the paper. The four next sections deal with the case
s=1/2
. More concretely, in Section
3
we prove a localization result for
s=1/2
, that is, for a distribution
\nu
, we localize the potential
P*\nu
in the
L^{\infty}-
norm. The localization method for the Cauchy potential
\nu*1/z
in the plane is a basic tool
developed by A.G. Vitushkin in the theory of rational approximation in the plane. This was later adapted in
[
P
]
for Riesz potential
\nu*x/|x|^{N}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and used in problems of
\mathcal{C}^{1}-
harmonic approximation. These localization results have also been essential to prove the semiadditivity of analytic capacity and of Lipschitz harmonic capacity, see
[
To1
]
and
[
V
]
respectively (see also
[
Pr
]
for other related capacities).

In Section
4
we study the connection between
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional caloric removability and the capacity
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}
. In particular, we show that
a compact set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
is
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional caloric removable if and only if
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)=0
.
We also compare the capacity
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}
to the Hausdorff content
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{N}
and we prove that if
E
has zero
N-
dimensional Hausdorff measure, i.e.,
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}(E)=0
, then
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)=0
too. In the converse direction, we show that if
E
has Hausdorff dimension larger than
N
, then
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)
is positive. Hence, the critical dimension for
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional caloric capacity
(and thus for
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional caloric removability)
occurs in dimension
N
, in accordance with the classical case. Also by analogy with the classical case, one should expect that
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)>0
if and only if
\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}(E)>0
or even that both capacities are comparable (the definition of
\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}
is the same as (
1.3
) but instead of distributions one considers positive measures). However, there is a big obstacle when trying to follow this approach. Namely, the kernel

P
is not antisymmetric and thus, if
\nu
is such that
T\nu=P*\nu
is in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
, apparently one cannot get any useful information regarding
T^{*}\nu
(
T^{*}
being the dual operator to
T
). This prevents any direct application of the usual
T1
or
Tb
theorems from Calderón-Zygmund theory, which are essential tools in the case of analytic capacity and Lipschitz harmonic capacity. Hence, In Section
4
, due to the lack of antisymmetry of our kernel
P
, we also introduce a new capacity
{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}
. We set

{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{\Theta,+}(E)=\sup\mu(E),

the supremum taken over all positive measures supported on
E
with

\displaystyle\|T\mu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1
and
\|T^{*}\mu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1.
Clearly
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}\geq\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}\geq{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}
. We show that the capacity
{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}
can be characterized in terms of the
L^{2}
-norm of
T
.

In Sections
5
and
6
, we give some concrete examples of
\frac{1}{2}-
caloric removable and non-removable sets with Hausdorff dimension
N
. In particular, in the first section we construct a self similar Cantor set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with positive and finite Hausdorff
N
-dimensional measure which is
\frac{1}{2}-
caloric removable and in the second one we show that on the plane, the capacity
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}
vanishes on vertical segments and is positive on horitzontal ones. These examples allow us to deduce that, in dimension two, neither analytic capacity nor newtonian capacity are comparable to the
1/2-
fractional caloric capacity
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}
(although these three capacities have the same critical dimension).

Sections
7
and
8
are devoted to the study of the
s-
fractional capacities
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}
, when
1/2<s<1
and
0<s<1/2
respectively. In the first case, namely
1/2<s<1,
we are able to show that the critical dimension for the
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}
-capacity is
N+2s-1
. We have had several technical problems when trying to prove this statement for the case
0<s<1/2
but we can show that sets
E
with zero
(N+2s-1)-
dimensional Hausdorff measure, i.e.,
{\mathcal{H}}^{N+2s-1}(E)=0
, have
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}(E)=0
.

Some comments about the notation used in the paper: as usual, the letter
C
stands for an absolute constant which may change its value at different occurrences.
The notation
A\lesssim B
means that there is a positive absolute constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
. Also,
A\approx B
is equivalent to
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
.
The gradient symbol
\nabla
refers to
(\nabla_{x},\partial_{t})
, with
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.
Some preliminary estimates on the kernels
P
and
P_{s}

In the next two lemmas we will obtain upper bounds for the kernels
P(x,t)
,
P_{s}(x,t)
and its derivatives. We will need them later.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\bar{x}=(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the following holds:

(a)

P(\bar{x})\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|\bar{x}|^{N}}
for all
\bar{x}\neq 0
.

(b)

|\nabla_{x}P(\bar{x})|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}
for all
\bar{x}\neq 0
and

|\partial_{t}P(\bar{x})|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}
for all
\bar{x}\neq(x,0)
.

(c)

For all
\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
such that
|\bar{x}-\bar{x}^{\prime}|\leq|\bar{x}|/2
,
\bar{x}\neq 0
,

|P(\bar{x})-P(\bar{x}^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{|\bar{x}-\bar{x}^{\prime}|}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}.

Notice that the kernel P is not differentiable with respect to
t
at the points
(x,0),\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.

Proof.

The estimate in (a) comes from the explicit expression of P. For the estimates in (b) we compute

\nabla_{x}P(x,t)=-\frac{(N+1)xt}{(|x|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{N+3}{2}}},

and so we get
|\nabla_{x}P(\bar{x})|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}.
On the other hand, for
t\neq 0

\partial_{t}P(x,t)=\frac{1}{(|x|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}-\frac{(N+1)t^{2}}{(|x|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{N+3}{2}}}.

Then
|\partial_{t}P(\bar{x})|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}
for all
\bar{x}\neq(x,0),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Finally, (c) will be obtained using (b). Indeed,
given
\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
such that
|\bar{x}-\bar{x}^{\prime}|\leq|\bar{x}|/2
, write

\bar{x}=(x,t),\quad\bar{x}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}),\quad\hat{x}=(x^{\prime},t).

Then

(2.1)

|P(\bar{x})-P(\bar{x}^{\prime})|\leq|P(\bar{x})-P(\hat{x})|+|P(\hat{x})-P(\bar{x}^{\prime})|

The first term in the above inequality is bounded by

|x-x^{\prime}|\,\sup_{y\in[x,x^{\prime}]}|\nabla_{x}P(y,t)|\lesssim\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}\leq\frac{|\bar{x}-\bar{x}^{\prime}|}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}.

For the second term in (
2.1
) let
t>t^{\prime}
. If
t^{\prime}>0
, then

|t-t^{\prime}|\,\sup_{s\in[t^{\prime},t]}|\partial_{t}P(x^{\prime},s)|\lesssim\frac{|t-t^{\prime}|}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}\leq\frac{|\bar{x}-\bar{x}^{\prime}|}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}.

If
t<0
,
|P(\hat{x})-P(\bar{x}^{\prime})|=0
and if
t>0
and
t^{\prime}<0
, then

|P(x^{\prime},t)-P(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})|=|P(x^{\prime},t)-P(x^{\prime},0)|\lesssim|t|\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|\partial_{t}P(x^{\prime},s)|\lesssim\frac{|\bar{x}-\bar{x}^{\prime}|}{|\bar{x}|^{N+1}}.

∎

The following lemma shows some growth properties of our kernel
P_{s}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. For any
\bar{x}=(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the following holds:

(1)

\displaystyle|P_{s}(\bar{x})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\bar{x}|_{p}^{N}}
for all
\bar{x}\neq 0
.

(2)

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle|(-\Delta)^{\alpha}P_{s}(\bar{x})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\bar{x}|_{p}^{N+2\alpha}}
.

(3)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}P_{s}(\bar{x})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\bar{x}|_{p}^{N+2s}}
for all
\bar{x}\neq(x,0)
.

(4)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\bar{x})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\bar{x}|_{p}^{N+1}}
for all
\bar{x}\neq 0
.

(5)

For
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}(\bar{x})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{N-1}|\bar{x}|_{p}^{2s}}.

Proof.

The first property follows from the definition of the kernel. For
t\leq 0
,
P_{s}(x,t)=0
and for
t>0
,

|P_{s}(\bar{x})|=|P_{s}(x,t)|\approx\frac{t}{(t^{\frac{1}{s}}+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\leq\frac{(t^{\frac{1}{s}}+|x|^{2})^{s}}{(t^{\frac{1}{s}}+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}=\frac{1}{|\bar{x}|_{p}^{N}}.

We prove now the second one. Applying Fourier transform to the fractional heat equation with respect to the space variable
x
, and calling the new variable
\xi
, we get the equation
\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{u}=-|\xi|^{2s}\widehat{u}
, that allows to solve the initial-value
problem in Fourier space by means of the formula
\widehat{u}(\xi,t)=\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi)e^{-|\xi|^{2s}t}
.
Applying the inverse transform, the fractional heat equation can be solved for all
0<s<1

by means of the fundamental solution,
P_{s}(x,t)
, which is the inverse transform of the function

e^{-|\xi|^{2s}t}
. Hence the kernel
P_{s}
has Fourier transform
\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{s}(\xi,t)=e^{-|\xi|^{2s}t}
. It is well known that for
t>0
(recall that for
t\leq 0,\;P_{s}(x,t)=0
) it has the self-similar form

(2.2)

P_{s}(x,t)=t^{\frac{-N}{2s}}\phi\left(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\right)

for some positive
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\phi
, radially decreasing and satisfying
\phi(u)\approx(1+u^{2})^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}
(see
[
BG
]
and
[
Va
]
).

Using (
2.2
) we deduce that

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}P_{s}(x,t)=t^{-\frac{N}{2s}-\frac{\alpha}{s}}\psi\left(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\right)

where
\displaystyle\psi(z)=(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\phi(z)
. Since
\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=e^{-|\xi|^{2s}}
, then
\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2\alpha}e^{-|\xi|^{2s}}
and using the expression of the inverse Fourier transform of a radial function (see
[
G
, Section B.5]
or
[
SW
, Section IV.I]
for a proof) we obtain

\psi(|z|)=c_{N}|z|^{1-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2s}}r^{\frac{N}{2}+2\alpha}J_{\frac{N}{2}-1}(r|z|)dr,

where
J_{k}
is the classical Bessel function of order
k
.
Thus
[
PruT
, Lemma 1]
gives us the decay
\displaystyle\left|\psi(|z|)\right|=O(|z|^{-N-2\alpha})
, for
|z|
large. Since
\psi
is bounded we have

(2.3)

\left|\psi(|z|)\right|\lesssim(1+|z|^{2})^{\frac{-N-2\alpha}{2}},

which implies

\left|(-\Delta)^{\alpha}P_{s}(x,t)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N+2\alpha}{2s}}(1+|x|^{2}t^{-\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2\alpha}{2}}}=\frac{1}{|\bar{x}|_{p}^{N+2\alpha}},

the second estimate in the statement of the lemma.
Observe that for
\alpha=s
this is

(2.4)

|(-\Delta)^{s}P_{s}(\bar{x})|\lesssim|\bar{x}|_{p}^{-N-2s}.

Therefore using that
P_{s}
is the fundamental solution of the
s
-fractional heat equation

(2.5)

\partial_{t}P_{s}(x,t)=-(-\Delta)^{s}P_{s}(x,t)=-t^{-\frac{N}{2s}-1}(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(z),\quad z=|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}},\quad t>0,

and the fact that

(2.6)

|(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(|z|)|\lesssim(1+|z|^{2})^{\frac{-N-2s}{2}}

(which is estimate (
2.3
) with
\alpha=s
) we get

|\partial_{t}P_{s}(\bar{x})|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1+\frac{N}{2s}}(1+|x|^{2}t^{-\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}=\frac{1}{|\bar{x}|_{p}^{N+2s}},

which is the third estimate in the statement of the lemma.

Next we will estimate the spatial derivative
\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)
. Clearly

\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)=\nabla_{x}\left(t^{\frac{-N}{2s}}\phi(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}})\right)=t^{\frac{-N-1}{2s}}\phi^{\prime}(|x|t^{-\frac{1}{2s}}).

If we can show that

(2.7)

\phi^{\prime}(u)\lesssim(1+u^{2})^{-\frac{N+1}{2}},

then we are done because

|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x,t)|\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{-N-1}{2s}}}{(1+|x|^{2}t^{-1/s})^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{|\bar{x}|_{p}^{N+1}}.

In order to estimate
\nabla\phi(z)
, we consider the equation for
\phi
that comes from (
2.5
), that is

2s(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(z)-N\phi(z)-z\cdot\nabla\phi(z)=0.

Notice that it implies that

|\nabla\phi(z)|\lesssim\frac{|\phi(z)|+|(-\Delta)^{s}\phi(z)|}{|z|}.

Since
\phi^{\prime}(|z|)
is bounded, from
\phi(u)\approx(1+u^{2})^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}
and (
2.6
) we deduce (
2.7
).

To prove the last estimate in the statement of the lemma, that is

(2.8)

|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}(\bar{x})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{N-1}|\bar{x}|^{2s}},\quad s\in(1/2,1),

we claim that for
t>0
,

(2.9)

P_{s}(x,t)=\frac{1}{|x|^{N}}F_{s}\left(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\right)

for some function
\displaystyle F_{s}(u)\approx\frac{u}{(1+u^{1/s})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}
. (
2.9
) can be proved using (
2.2
), that is
P_{s}(x,t)=t^{\frac{-N}{2s}}\phi\left(|x|/t^{\frac{1}{2s}}\right)
for some
\phi
with
\phi(u)\approx(1+u^{2})^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}
. In fact,

P_{s}(x,t)=\frac{1}{|x|^{N}}\left(\frac{|x|}{t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\right)^{N}\phi(\left(\frac{|x|}{t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\right)=\frac{1}{|x|^{N}}\left(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2s}}\phi\left(\Big(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2s}}\right)=\frac{1}{|x|^{N}}F_{s}\left(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\right)

the last equality being a definition for
F_{s}
. Hence

F_{s}(u)=u^{\frac{-N}{2s}}\phi(u^{\frac{-1}{2s}})\approx\frac{u^{\frac{-N}{2s}}}{(1+u^{-1/s})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}=\frac{u}{u^{\frac{N+2s}{2s}}(1+u^{-1/s})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}=\frac{u}{(1+u^{1/s})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}

and claim (
2.9
) is proved.
Notice that we have

\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}(x,t)=\frac{1}{|x|^{N}}\left[\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}F_{s}\left(\frac{\cdot}{|x|^{2s}}\right)\right](t)=\frac{1}{|x|^{N+2s-1}}\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}F_{s}\left(\frac{t}{|x|^{2s}}\right).

Hence, to show (
2.8
) it is enough to see that for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(2.10)

|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}F_{s}(t)|\lesssim\min(1,|t|^{-1}).

Once (
2.10
) is available we obtain (
2.8
) easily:

|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}(x,t)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{N+2s-1}}\min\left(1,\frac{|x|^{2s}}{t}\right)=\frac{1}{\max(|x|^{2s},t)|x|^{N-1}}=\frac{1}{|x|^{N-1}|\bar{x}|_{p}^{2s}}.

To show (
2.10
), we write

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}F_{s}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{|F_{s}(r)-F_{s}(t)|}{|r-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dr=\int_{|r|\leq|t|/2}\frac{|F_{s}(r)-F_{s}(t)|}{|r-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dr

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\int_{|t|/2<|r|\leq 2|t|}\frac{|F_{s}(r)-F_{s}(t)|}{|r-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dr+\int_{|r|>2|t|}\frac{|F_{s}(r)-F_{s}(t)|}{|r-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dr

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

We begin with
I_{1}
. Notice that here
|r-t|\approx|t|
. Then,

I_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\left(\int_{|r|\leq|t|/2}|F_{s}(r)|dr+\int_{|r|\leq|t|/2}|F_{s}(t)|dr\right)=I_{11}+I_{12}.

If
|t|\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{0}^{|t|}\frac{r}{(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}dr\lesssim\frac{|t|}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{0}^{|t|}\frac{dr}{(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{\frac{1}{2s}}\leq 1.

If
|t|>1
, then we can write

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r}{(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}dr+\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{1}^{|t|}\frac{r}{(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}dr.

Since

\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{1}^{|t|}\frac{r}{(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}dr\leq\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{1}^{|t|}r^{\frac{-N}{2s}}\approx\frac{1-|t|^{1-\frac{N}{2s}}}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\lesssim\frac{|t|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}=\frac{1}{|t|},

then if
|t|>1
,

I_{11}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}+\frac{1}{|t|}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|}.

Hence
\displaystyle I_{11}\lesssim\min\left(1,\frac{1}{|t|}\right).
Since
F_{s}(t)=0
for
t\leq 0
, to estimate
I_{12}
we only need to consider positive
t
, therefore

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{|F_{s}(t)|}{|t|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}}\approx\frac{t^{2}}{t^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}(1+t^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}=\frac{t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}{(1+t^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\lesssim\min\left(1,\frac{1}{t}\right).

This finishes the estimate of
I_{1}
. To deal with
I_{2}
, we distinguish two cases, according to whether
r
has the same sign as
t
or not. In the first case we write
r\in Y
, and in the second one,
r\in N
. In the case
r\in N
, with

|t|/2\leq|r|\leq 2|t|
, it turns out that
|r-t|\approx|t|
, and thus

\displaystyle I_{2,N}

\displaystyle:=\int_{r\in N,|t|/2\leq|r|\leq 2|t|}\frac{|F_{s}(r)-F_{s}(t)|}{|r-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,dr\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{0}^{2|t|}\frac{r}{(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\,dr+\frac{|F_{s}(t)|}{|t|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}}.

Observe that this last expression is very similar to the ones in
I_{11}
and
I_{12}
.
Then, by almost the same arguments we deduce that

I_{2,N}\lesssim\min\bigg(1,\frac{1}{|t|}\bigg).

To deal with the case when the sign of
s
is the same as the one of
t
(i.e.,
s\in Y
),
we take into account that

|F_{s}(r)-F_{s}(t)|\leq\sup_{\xi\in[r,t]}|F_{s}^{\prime}(\xi)|\,|r-t|.

Notice that (
2.9
) tells us that if
|y|=1
, then
F_{s}(\xi)=P_{s}(y,\xi)
. Hence due to property (3) of this lemma
and since in this case
|t|/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2|t|
, it is immediate to check that for this
\xi
we have

|F_{s}^{\prime}(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|t|^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle I_{2,Y}

\displaystyle:=\int_{r\in Y,|t|/2\leq|r|\leq 2|t|}\frac{|F_{s}(r)-F_{s}(t)|}{|r-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|t|^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\int_{|r|\leq 2|t|}\frac{dr}{|r-t|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|t|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}{(1+|t|^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\lesssim\min\bigg(1,\frac{1}{|t|}\bigg).

Finally we will deal with
I_{3}
.

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq\int_{|r|>2|t|}\frac{|F_{s}(r)|}{|r-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dr+\int_{|r|>2|t|}\frac{|F_{s}(t)|}{|r-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dr=I_{31}+I_{32}.

Notice that
|r-t|\approx|r|>2|t|
. If
|t|\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle I_{31}\lesssim\int_{|t|}^{1}\frac{dr}{r^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}.

Since

\int_{|t|}^{1}\frac{dr}{r^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\leq\int_{|t|}^{1}\frac{dr}{r^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}}\approx 1-|t|^{\frac{1}{2s}}\leq 1

and

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\lesssim 1,

we have
\displaystyle I_{31}\lesssim 1
when
|t|\leq 1
.
If
|t|>1
, then

\displaystyle I_{31}\lesssim\int_{|t|}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(1+r^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\leq\int_{|t|}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{2+\frac{N-1}{2s}}}\approx\frac{1}{|t|^{1+\frac{N-1}{2s}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|}.

Hence
\displaystyle I_{31}\lesssim\min\left(1,\frac{1}{|t|}\right)
. Finally

\displaystyle I_{32}\lesssim\frac{t}{(1+t^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}{(1+t^{\frac{1}{s}})^{\frac{N+2s}{2}}}\leq\min\left(1,\frac{1}{t}\right).

∎

3.
Localization estimates for
s=1/2

The main objective of this section is to show the following localization result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

\nu
be a distribution in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with

\|P*\nu\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

Let
{\varphi}
be a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function supported on a cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
such that
\|\nabla{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-1}
. Then
\|P*({\varphi}\nu)\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1.

In the statement the operator
\nabla
refers to
\nabla=(\nabla_{x},\partial_{t})
. Before proving Theorem
3.1
we need several lemmas and definitions.
We say that a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function
{\varphi}
is admissible for
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
if it is supported on
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and satisfies

(3.1)

\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}\quad\mbox{ and }\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}|(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}.

Recall that

(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi},

with
R_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq N
, being the Riesz transforms with Fourier multiplier
\xi_{j}/|\xi|
. Then setting
Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}
with
Q_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
I_{Q}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, one can write

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}|(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt

\displaystyle\approx\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt.

Therefore if
{\varphi}
satisfies

\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\int_{I_{Q}}\|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}dt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N},\;\mbox{ for }1\leq j\leq N,

then
{\varphi}
is admissible for
Q
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\varphi}
be a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function supported on a cube
Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, with
Q_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
I_{Q}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, and such that

(1)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\varphi}\|_{L^{1}(Q)}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}
.

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{Q}}\|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(2Q_{1})}dt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}
, for
j=1,2,\cdots,N
.

Then
{\varphi}
is admissible for
Q
.

Proof.

Due to the above explanation, we only have to show that

\int_{I_{Q}}\|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus 2Q_{1})}dt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}.

In fact,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{Q}}\|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus 2Q_{1})}dt

\displaystyle\approx\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus 2Q_{1}}\left|\int_{Q_{1}}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(z,t)\frac{z_{j}-y_{j}}{|z-y|^{N+1}}dz\right|dydt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus 2Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{|{\varphi}(z,t)|}{|z-y|^{N+1}}dzdydt

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\ell(I_{Q})\ell(Q_{1})^{N-1}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N},

where the forelast inequality is obtained by integrating on annulus, for example.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

A
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function
{\varphi}
supported on a cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with
\|\nabla{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-1}
is admissible for
Q
.

Proof.

The first condition in lemma
3.2
is clearly fulfilled. To show the second condition in lemma
3.2
we will use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the
L^{2}-
boundedness of the Riesz transforms as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{I_{Q}}\|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(2Q_{1})}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{Q}}\ell(Q)^{N/2}\|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(2Q_{1})}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{Q}}\ell(Q)^{N/2}\|\partial_{j}{\varphi}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(2Q_{1})}dt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N},

Therefore, standard test functions supported on
Q
are admissible for
Q
.
∎

It is worth mentioning here that if
{\varphi}
is a standard
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
test function supported on
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
satisfying
\|\partial_{t}{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-1}
and
\|\Delta_{x}{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-2}
, then
{\varphi}
is admissible for
Q
. The first condition in (
3.1
) is clear and for the second one, notice that if
g=\Delta_{x}\varphi*_{x}k
, with
k(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{N-1}}
and
*_{x}
denoting the convolution on the
x
variable, then taking the Fourier transform with respect to
x
, we get:
(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}=cg
, for a suitable constant
c\neq 0
. Then, integrating on annuli and using that
\|\Delta_{x}{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-2}
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}|(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt

\displaystyle\approx\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}|\Delta_{x}\varphi*_{x}k(x,t)|dxdt

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus 2Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{|\varphi(y,t)|}{|x-y|^{N+1}}dydxdt+\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{2Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{|\Delta_{y}\varphi(y,t)|}{|x-y|^{N-1}}dydxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell(Q)^{N+1}(2^{k}\ell(Q))^{N}}{(2^{k}\ell(Q))^{N+1}}+\ell(Q)^{N}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}.

The following lemma shows an
N-
growth condition that every distribution
\nu
fulfilling the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
satisfies.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\nu
be a distribution in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with

\|P*\nu\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

If
{\varphi}
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function admissible for
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, then

|\langle\nu,{\varphi}\rangle|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}.

Proof.

Since
P
is the fundamental solution of
\Theta^{1/2}
, we can write

|\langle\nu,{\varphi}\rangle|=|\langle\nu,\Theta^{1/2}{\varphi}*P\rangle|\leq|\langle P*\nu,(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}\rangle|+|\langle P*\nu,\partial_{t}{\varphi}\rangle|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N},

because
\|P*\nu\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
{\varphi}
is admissible for
Q
, so it satisfies (
3.1
).
∎

We will say that a distribution
\nu
has
N
-growth if for any
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and any
{\varphi}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
admissible for
Q
,
|\langle\nu,{\varphi}\rangle|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\nu
be a distribution in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with
N
-growth. If
{\varphi}
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function supported on a cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-1}
, then the distribution
\displaystyle P*{\varphi}\nu
is a locally integrable function and there exists a point
\bar{x}_{0}\in\frac{1}{4}Q
such that

(3.3)

|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(\bar{x}_{0})|\lesssim 1.

Proof.

We will show that the mean of
\displaystyle f=P*{\varphi}\nu
on
\frac{1}{4}Q
is bounded by a constant. Hence at many Lebesgue points of

f
the inequality (
3.3
) holds.

We only need to show that
\displaystyle P*{\varphi}\nu
is integrable on
2Q
. In fact, we will prove a stronger statement, namely that
\displaystyle P*{\varphi}\nu
is in
L^{p}(2Q)
for each
1<p<\frac{N+1}{N}
. Indeed, fix any
q
satisfying
N+1<q<\infty
and let
p
be its dual exponent, so that
1<p<\frac{N+1}{N}
. We need to estimate the action of
\displaystyle P*{\varphi}\nu
on test functions
\psi
supported on
2Q
in terms of
\|\psi\|_{q}
. We clearly have

\langle P*{\varphi}\nu,\psi\rangle=\langle\nu,{\varphi}(P*\psi)\rangle.

We claim that the test function

(3.4)

h=\frac{{\varphi}(P*\psi)}{\ell(Q)^{(N+1)/p-N}\|\psi\|_{q}}

satisfies the inequalities in lemma
3.2
and hence it is and admissible function. Once this is proved we get

|\langle P*{\varphi}\nu,\psi\rangle|=|\langle\nu,{\varphi}(P*\psi)\rangle|=\ell(Q)^{(N+1)/p-N}\|\psi\|_{q}|\langle\nu,h\rangle|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{(N+1)/p}\|\psi\|_{q}

and so

\|P*{\varphi}\nu\|_{L^{p}(2Q)}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{(N+1)/p}.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\frac{1}{4}Q|}\int_{\frac{1}{4}Q}|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(x)|dx\leq

\displaystyle\;\frac{4^{N+1}}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;4^{N+1}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\leq C

which completes the proof of the lemma.

To prove the claim, we need to show that
h
satisfies the inequalities in lemma
3.2
, that is

\|\nabla h\|_{L^{1}(2Q)}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{I_{Q}}\|R_{j}(\partial_{j}h)\|_{L^{1}(2Q_{1})}dt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N},\;1\leq j\leq N.

Or equivalently

(3.5)

\|\nabla\left({\varphi}(P*\psi)\right)\|_{L^{1}(Q)}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}\|\psi\|_{q}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{I_{Q}}\|R_{j}\partial_{j}\big({\varphi}(P*\psi)\big)\|_{L^{1}(2Q_{1})}dt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}\|\psi\|_{q},

for
1\leq j\leq N.
For the first inequality in (
3.5
) apply Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla\left({\varphi}(P*\psi)\right)\|_{L^{1}(2Q)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}\|\nabla\left({\varphi}(P*\psi)\right)\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}\left(\|\nabla{\varphi}(P*\psi)\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}+\|{\varphi}\nabla(P*\psi)\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}(A_{1}+A_{2}).

Since
\|\nabla{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-1}
and by Hölder again,

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-1}\left(\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{2Q}\frac{|\psi(\bar{y})|}{|\bar{y}-\bar{x}|^{N}}d\bar{y}\right)^{q}d\bar{x}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\ell(Q)^{-1}\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}\left(\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{2Q}\frac{d\bar{y}}{|\bar{y}-\bar{x}|^{Np}}\right)^{q/p}d\bar{x}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-1}\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}\left(\ell(Q)^{N+1}\ell(Q)^{(N+1-Np)\frac{q}{p}}\right)^{1/q}=\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}.

Applying
[
Du
, Theorem 4.12]
, for example, we deduce that the singular integral with kernel
\partial_{t}P(x,t)
is bounded in
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
,
1<q<\infty
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\|{\varphi}\,\partial_{t}(P*\psi)\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}
. The estimate of the
L^{q}(Q)-
norm of
{\varphi}\partial_{j}(P*\psi)
in
A_{2}
is analogous. Finally
A_{2}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}
so the first inequality in (
3.5
) is proven.

For the second one write

\int_{2Q}|R_{j}\partial_{j}({\varphi}(P*\psi))|\leq\int_{2Q}|R_{j}(\partial_{j}{\varphi}(P*\psi))|+\int_{2Q}|R_{j}({\varphi}\partial_{j}(P*\psi))|=A_{3}+A_{4}.

Using Hölder, the
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})-
boundedness of the Riesz transform and arguing similar to what we have just done for the term
A_{1}
,

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}\left(\int_{2Q}\big|\partial_{j}{\varphi}(\bar{x})(P*\psi)(\bar{x})\big|^{q}d\bar{x}\right)^{1/q}\leq\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}-1}\left(\int_{2Q}\big|(P*\psi)(\bar{x})\big|^{q}d\bar{x}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}-1}\left(\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{2Q}\frac{|\psi(\bar{y})|}{|\bar{y}-\bar{x}|^{N}}d\bar{y}\right)^{q}d\bar{x}\right)^{1/q}\leq\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(2Q)}.

Analogously,

\displaystyle A_{4}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}\left(\int_{2Q}\big|{\varphi}(\bar{x})\partial_{j}(P*\psi)(\bar{x})\big|^{q}d\bar{x}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\frac{N+1}{p}}\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(2Q)},

by an argument similar to the one of
A_{2}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Take
\bar{x}\in\frac{3}{2}Q
and write
P_{\bar{x}}(\bar{y})=P(\bar{x}-\bar{y})
. We have to show that
\displaystyle\left|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(\bar{x})\right|\lesssim 1
. Write

\displaystyle\left|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(\bar{x})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(\bar{x})-\varphi(\bar{x})(P*\nu)(\bar{x})\right|+\|{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\|P*\nu\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(\bar{x})-\varphi(\bar{x})(P*\nu)(\bar{x})\right|+C.

Let
\psi
be a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function such that
\psi\equiv 1
in
2Q
,
\psi\equiv 0
in
(4Q)^{c}
and
\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\ell(Q)^{-1}
. We need to resort a standard regularization process. Take
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(B(0,1))
such that
\int\chi(x)dx=1
and set
\chi_{\varepsilon}(x)={\varepsilon}^{-n}\chi(x/{\varepsilon})
and
P^{\varepsilon}=\chi_{\varepsilon}*P
. We want to estimate

(3.6)

\left|(P^{\varepsilon}*{\varphi}\nu)(\bar{x})-\varphi(\bar{x})(P^{\varepsilon}*\nu)(\bar{x})\right|

uniformly on
\chi
and
{\varepsilon}
. Since, as
{\varepsilon}
tends to zero, (
3.6
) tends to
\displaystyle\left|(P*{\varphi}\nu)(\bar{x})-\varphi(\bar{x})(P*\nu)(\bar{x})\right|
for almost all
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, this allows the transfer of uniform estimates.

\displaystyle\left|(P^{\varepsilon}*{\varphi}\nu)(\bar{x})-\varphi(\bar{x})(P^{\varepsilon}*\nu)(\bar{x})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\nu,{\varphi}(\bar{y})P^{\varepsilon}_{\bar{x}}(\bar{y})-{\varphi}(\bar{x})P^{\varepsilon}_{\bar{x}}(\bar{y})\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\langle\nu,\psi P^{\varepsilon}_{\bar{x}}\left({\varphi}-{\varphi}(\bar{x})\right)\rangle\right|+\left|\langle\nu,(1-\psi){\varphi}(\bar{x})P^{\varepsilon}_{\bar{x}}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=A+B.

To estimate term
A
, we will show that
h(\bar{y})=\ell(Q)^{N}\psi(\bar{y})P_{\bar{x}}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})({\varphi}(\bar{y})-{\varphi}(\bar{x}))
is admissible for
4Q
and then apply lemma
3.4
. Since the function

\phi(\bar{y})=\psi(\bar{y})\left({\varphi}(\bar{y})-{\varphi}(\bar{x})\right)

satisfies
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-1}
, for

4.
Capacities and removable singularities

Given a compact set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,
we define

(4.1)

\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)=\sup|\langle\nu,1\rangle|,

where the supremum is taken over all distributions
\nu
supported on
E
such that

(4.2)

\|P*\nu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1.

We call
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)
the
\frac{1}{2}
-fractional caloric capacity of
E
.
We also define the capacity
\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}(E)
,
in the same way as in (
4.1
), but with the supremum restricted to all positive measures
\nu

supported on
E
satisfying (
4.2
).
Clearly,

\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}(E)\leq\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E).

Denote by the
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}_{\infty}(E)
the
N
-Hausdorff content of the compact set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every Borel set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,

\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}(E)\leq\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{N}_{\infty}(E),

and

\dim_{H}(E)>N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)>0.

Proof.

Just by the previous definitions,
\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}(E)\leq\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)
. To prove
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{N}_{\infty}(E)

first notice that we can assume
E
to be compact.
Let
\nu

be a distribution supported on
E
satisfying (
4.2
)
and let
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a collection of sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
which cover
E,
and such that

\sum_{i\in I}{\rm diam}(A_{i})^{N}\leq 2\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N}_{\infty}(E).

For each
i\in I
, let
B_{i}
be an open ball centered in
A_{i}
with
r(B_{i})={\rm diam}(A_{i})
, so that
E\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{i}
. By the compactness of
E
we can assume
I
to be finite.
By means of the Harvey-Polking lemma
[
HPo
, Lemma 3.1]
, we can construct
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
functions
{\varphi}_{i}
,
i\in I
, satisfying:

•

\operatorname{supp}{\varphi}_{i}\subset 2B_{i}
for each
i\in I
,

•

\|\nabla{\varphi}_{i}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1/r(B_{i})
,

•

\sum_{i\in I}{\varphi}_{i}=1
in
\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{i}
,

Hence, by corollary
3.3
and lemma
3.4

|\langle\nu,1\rangle|=\Big|\sum_{i\in I}\langle\nu,{\varphi}_{i}\rangle|\lesssim\sum_{i\in I}r(B_{i})^{N}=\sum_{i\in I}{\rm diam}(A_{i})^{N}\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{N}_{\infty}(E).

Since this holds for any distribution
\nu
supported on
E
satisfying (
4.2
),
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{N}_{\infty}(E)
.

To prove the second assertion in the lemma, let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
be a Borel set satisfying

\dim_{H}(E)=s>N
. We may assume
E
to be bounded and we can apply the well known Frostman lemma.
Then, it follows that there exists some non-zero positive measure
\mu
supported on
E
satisfying

for all
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}

Thus, by Lemma

, we deduce that for all

Therefore,

\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)\geq\frac{\mu(E)}{\|P*\mu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}}>0.

∎

We say that a compact set

is removable for bounded
\frac{1}{2}
-fractional caloric functions (or
\frac{1}{2}-

.

A compact set

is

It is clear that if

is
\frac{1}{2}-

\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E)=0
. Conversely,
suppose that
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
is not
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional caloric removable. So there exists some function

satisfying

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}<\infty,

and
\Theta^{1/2}(f)\equiv 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\setminus E
but
\Theta^{1/2}(f)\not\equiv 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
(in the distributional
sense). Since
E
is compact there exists some (open) cube such that

and
\Theta^{1/2}(f)\not\equiv 0
in

.
Consider the distribution

. Since
\nu
does not vanish

, there exists some
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}

{\varphi}
supported on

\langle\nu,{\varphi}\rangle>0
. Now take

. By theorem

,

From the preceding lemmas, it is clear that, for any compact set

.

We consider the operator

T\nu=P*\nu,

defined over distributions
\nu

{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}

\mu

For
{\varepsilon}>0

whenever the integral makes sense,
and for a function

, we write

T_{\mu}f\equiv T(f\,\mu),\qquad T_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}f\equiv T_{\varepsilon}(f\,\mu).

We also denote

T_{*}\mu(x)=\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}|T_{\varepsilon}\mu(x)|,\quad T_{*,\mu}f(x)=\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}|T_{\varepsilon}(f\,\mu)(x)|.

We say that
T_{\mu}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
if the operators
T_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
are bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)

uniformly on
{\varepsilon}>0
.

Given
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,
we define the capacity

{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{\Theta,+}^{1/2}(E)=\sup\mu(E),

where the supremum is taken over all positive measures
\mu
supported on

\|T\mu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1,\qquad\|T^{*}\mu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1.

Here

T^{*}\mu(\bar{x})=\int P(\bar{y}-\bar{x})\,d\mu(\bar{y}).

Notice that by definition,

{\widetilde{\gamma}}^{1/2}_{\Theta,+}(E)\lesssim\gamma^{1/2}_{\Theta,+}(E)

In the next theorem we characterize

in terms of the positive measures supported on

such that

is bounded in

.

Theorem 4.3
.

For any set

,

with the implicit constant in the above estimate independent of

.

Proof.

Denote

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sup\{\mu(E):\operatorname{supp}\mu\in E,\,\|T_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)}\leq 1\}.

The arguments to show that
{\widetilde{\gamma}}^{1/2}_{\Theta,+}(E)\approx S
are standard. Indeed, let
\mu
be a positive measure supported on
E
such that

{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{\Theta,+}(E)\leq 2\mu(E)
,
\|T\mu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1
and
\|T^{*}\mu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1
. By a Cotlar type inequality analogous to the one in
[
MaP
, Lemma 5.4]
, say, one deduces that

\|T_{\varepsilon}\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\lesssim 1,\qquad\|T_{\varepsilon}^{*}\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\lesssim 1,

uniformly on
{\varepsilon}>0
.

To obtain the boundedness of the operator
T_{\mu}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
we will use a suitable
T1
theorem with respect to a measure which may be non-doubling (see for example
[
To2
, Th 3.21]
). Since
T_{\mu}
and
T^{*}_{\mu}
are bounded in
L^{\infty},
as a consequence of the result in
[
HyMar
]
,
to apply the
T1
theorem in our case it is enough to check that the weak boundedness property
is satisfied for balls with thin boundaries, that is,

<T_{\mu}\chi_{B},\chi_{B}>\leq C\mu(2B)
, if
B
is a ball with thin boundary.
A ball of radius
r(B)
is said to have thin boundary if

(4.5)

\mu\{\bar{x}:\mbox{dist}(\bar{x},\partial B)\leq tr(B)\}\leq t\mu(2B).

Let’s consider a
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
function
\phi
with compact support in
2B
such that
\phi\equiv 1
on
B.
Then

|<T_{\mu}\chi_{B},\chi_{B}>|\leq\int_{B}|T_{\mu}\phi|d\mu+\int_{B}|T_{\mu}(\phi-\chi_{B})|d\mu.

Using Theorem
3.1
one can see that the first term in the right hand side is bounded by
C\mu(B)
.
To get a bound of the second term we will use that B has a thin boundary. Using the boundedness of
K
, property (a) in Lemma
2.1
,

\int_{B}|T_{\mu}(\phi-\chi_{B})|d\mu\lesssim\int_{2B\setminus B}\int_{B}\frac{d\mu(\bar{y})}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{N}}d\mu(\bar{x})\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{\{\bar{x}\notin B:\tiny\mbox{dist}(\bar{x};\partial B)\sim 2^{-j}r(B)\}}\int_{B}\frac{d\mu(\bar{y})}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{N}}d\mu(\bar{x}).

Given
j
and
x\notin B
such that
\mbox{dist}(\bar{x},\partial B)\sim 2^{-j}r(B),
since
\mu
is a measure with
N
-growth, one has

\int_{B}\frac{d\mu(\bar{y})}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{N}}\lesssim\sum_{k=-1}^{j}\int_{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|\sim 2^{-k}r(B)}\frac{d\mu(\bar{y})}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{N}}\lesssim\sum_{k=-1}^{j}\frac{\mu(B(\bar{x},2^{-k}r(B))}{(2^{-k}r(B))^{N}}\lesssim j+2.

Therefore, by
4.5

\int_{B}|T_{\mu}(\phi-\chi_{B})|d\mu\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}(j+2)\mu\{\bar{x}:\mbox{dist}(\bar{x},\partial B)\sim 2^{-j}r(B)\}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{j+2}{2^{j}}\mu(2B)\lesssim\mu(2B).

Consequently, the weak boundedness property is satified and by the
T1-
theorem it
follows that
T_{\mu}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
, with

\|T_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)}\lesssim 1.
So we deduce that
S\gtrsim{\widetilde{\gamma}}_{\Theta,+}(E)
.

To prove the converse estimate, let
\mu
be a positive measure supported on
E
be such that
\|T_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)}\leq 1

and
S\leq 2\mu(E)
. From the
L^{2}(\mu)
boundedness of
T_{\mu}
, one deduces that
T
and
T^{*}
are bounded
from the space of finite signed measures
M({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
to
L^{1,\infty}(\mu)
. That is, there exists some constant
C>0
such that for any measure

\nu\in M({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
, any
{\varepsilon}>0
, and any
\lambda>0
,

\mu\big(\big\{\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}:|T_{\varepsilon}\nu(\bar{x})|>\lambda\big\}\big)\leq C\,\frac{\|\nu\|}{\lambda},

and the same holds replacing
T_{\varepsilon}
by
T^{*}_{\varepsilon}
.
The proof of this fact is analogous to the one of Theorem 2.16 in
[
To2
]
. Then, by a well known dualization of these estimates (essentially due to Davie and Øksendal) and an application of Cotlar’s inequality, one deduces
that there exists some function
h:E\to[0,1]
such that

\mu(E)\leq C\,\int h\,d\mu,\quad\;\|T(h\,\mu)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1,\quad\;\|T^{*}(h\,\mu)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1.

See Theorem 4.6 and Lemma 4.7 from
[
To2
]
for the analogous arguments in the case of analytic capacity and also Lemma 4.2 from
[
MaP
]
for the precise vectorial version of the dualization of the weak
(1,1)
estimates required in our situation, for example. So we have

{\widetilde{\gamma}}^{1/2}_{\Theta,+}(E)\geq\int h\,d\mu\approx\mu(E)\approx S.

∎

5.
The existence of removable sets with positive Hausdorff measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}

In this section we will construct a self-similar Cantor set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with positive and finite
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}
measure which is
\frac{1}{2}-
caloric removable. To obtain this result we need the following theorem, showing that certain distributions are actually measures.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
be a compact set with
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}(E)<\infty

and let
\nu
be a distribution supported on
E
such that

\|P*\nu\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

Then
\nu
is a signed measure, absolutely continuous with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}^{N+1}|_{E}
and
there exists a Borel function
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\nu=f\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E}
and

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E})}\lesssim 1
.

In what follows, we say that a distribution
\nu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
has
N-
dimensional growth, if for any cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}

and any
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function
{\varphi}
admissible for
Q
,

\displaystyle|\langle\nu,{\varphi}\rangle|\leq\ell(Q)^{N}.

Theorem
5.1
is a consequence of Lemma
3.4
and the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
be a compact set with
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}(E)<\infty
.
Let
\nu
be a distribution supported on
E
wich has
N
-dimensional growth. Then
\nu
is a signed measure, absolutely continuous with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E}
and
there exists a Borel function
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\nu=f\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E}
and

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E})}\lesssim 1
.

Proof.

First we will show that
\nu
is a signed measure. By the Riesz representation theorem, it is enough
to show that, for any
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
function
\psi
with compact support,

(5.1)

|\langle\nu,\psi\rangle|\leq C(E)\,\|\psi\|_{\infty},

where
C(E)
is some constant depending on
E
.

To prove (
5.1
), we fix
{\varepsilon}>0
and we consider a family of open cubes
Q_{i}
,

i\in I_{\varepsilon}
, such that

•

E\subset\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}Q_{i}
,

•

\ell(Q_{i})\leq{\varepsilon}
for all
i\in I_{\varepsilon}
, and

•

\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\ell(Q_{i})^{N}\leq C\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N}(E)+{\varepsilon}
.

Since
E
is compact, we can assume that
I_{\varepsilon}
is finite.
By standard arguments, we can find a family of non-negative functions
{\varphi}_{i}
,
i\in I_{\varepsilon}
, such that

•

each
{\varphi}_{i}
is supported on
2Q_{i}
and
c{\varphi}_{i}
is admissible for
2Q_{i}
, for some
absolute constant
c>0
,

•

\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}{\varphi}_{i}=1
on
\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}Q_{i}
, and in particular on
E
.

Indeed, to construct the family of functions
{\varphi}_{i}
we can cover each cube
Q_{i}
by a bounded number (depending on
n
)
dyadic cubes
R_{i}^{1},\cdots,R_{i}^{m}
with side length
\ell(R_{i}^{j})\leq\ell(Q_{i})/8
and then apply
the usual Harvey-Polking lemma (
[
HPo
, Lemma 3.1]
to the family of cubes
\{R_{i,j}\}
.

We write

|\langle\nu,\psi\rangle|\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}|\langle\nu,{\varphi}_{i}\psi\rangle|.

For each
i\in I_{\varepsilon}
, consider the function

\eta_{i}=\frac{{\varphi}_{i}\,\psi}{\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\ell(Q_{i})\,\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}},

where
\nabla=(\nabla_{x},\partial_{t}).

We claim that
c\,\eta_{i}
is admissible for
2Q_{i}
, for some absolute constant
c>0
. To check this,
just note that
{\varphi}_{i}\,\psi
is supported on
2Q_{i}
and satisfies

\|\nabla({\varphi}_{i}\,\psi)\|_{\infty}\leq\|\nabla{\varphi}_{i}\|_{\infty}\,\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\|{\varphi}_{i}\|_{\infty}\,\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(Q_{i})}\,\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}.

Hence,

\|\nabla\eta_{i}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(Q_{i})}.

So, by corollary
3.3
, the claim above holds and, consequently, by the assumptions in the lemma,

|\langle\nu,\eta_{i}\rangle|\lesssim\ell(Q_{i})^{N}.

From the preceding estimate, we deduce that

|\langle\nu,\psi\rangle|\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}|\langle\nu,{\varphi}_{i}\psi\rangle|\lesssim\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\ell(Q_{i})^{N}\,\big(\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\ell(Q_{i})\,\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\big).

Since
\ell(Q_{i})\leq{\varepsilon}
for each
i
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\langle\nu,\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\ell(Q_{i})^{N}\,\big(\|\psi\|_{\infty}+{\varepsilon}\,\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big({\mathcal{H}}^{N}(E)+{\varepsilon}\big)\,\big(\|\psi\|_{\infty}+{\varepsilon}\,\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\big).

Letting
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, we get

|\langle\nu,\psi\rangle|\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{N}(E)\,\|\psi\|_{\infty},

which gives (
5.1
) and proves that
\nu
is a finite signed measure, as wished.

It remains to show that
there exists some Borel function
f:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\nu=f\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E}
, with

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E})}\lesssim 1
.
To this end, let
g
be the density of
\nu
with respect to its variation
|\nu|
, so that

\nu=g\,|\nu|
with
g(\bar{x})=\pm 1
for
|\nu|
-a.e.
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
.
We will show that

(5.2)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|\nu|(B(\bar{x},r))}{r^{N}}\lesssim 1\quad\mbox{ for $|\nu|$-a.e.\ $\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}$}.

This implies that
|\nu|={\widetilde{f}}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E}
for some non-negative function
{\widetilde{f}}\lesssim 1
. This fact is well known (see Theorem 6.9 from
[
Ma
]
).

So to complete the proof of the lemma it suffices to show (
5.2
) (since then we will have

\nu=g\,{\widetilde{f}}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E}
with
|g{\widetilde{f}}|\lesssim 1
). Notice that, by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem,

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{|\nu|(B(\bar{x},r))}\int_{B(\bar{x},r)}|g(\bar{y})-g(\bar{x})|\,d|\nu|(\bar{y})=0\quad\mbox{ for $|\nu|$-a.e.\ $\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}$.}

Let
\bar{x}\in E
be a Lebesgue point for
|\nu|
with
|g(\bar{x})|=1
, let
{\varepsilon}>0
to be chosen below,
and let
r_{0}>0
be small enough such that, for
0<r\leq r_{0}
,

\frac{1}{|\nu|(B(\bar{x},r))}\int_{B(\bar{x},r)}|g(\bar{y})-g(\bar{x})|\,d|\nu|(\bar{y})<{\varepsilon}.

Suppose first that

(5.3)

|\nu|(B(\bar{x},2r))\leq 2^{N+3}\,|\nu|(B(\bar{x},r)),

and let
{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}
be some non-negative
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
function supported on
B(\bar{x},2r)
which equals
1
on

B(\bar{x},r)
such that
c\,{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}
is admissible for the smallest cube
Q
containing
B(\bar{x},2r)
,
so that

\left|\int{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}\,d\nu\right|\lesssim r^{N}.

Now observe that

\displaystyle\left|\int{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}\,d\nu-g(\bar{x})\!\int{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}\,d|\nu|\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}(\bar{y})(g(\bar{y})-g(\bar{x}))\,d|\nu|(\bar{y})\right|\lesssim\int_{B(\bar{x},2r)}|g(\bar{y})-g(\bar{x})|\,d|\nu|(\bar{y})

\displaystyle\leq{\varepsilon}\,|\nu|(B(\bar{x},2r))\leq{\varepsilon}\,2^{N+3}\,|\nu|(B(\bar{x},r))\lesssim{\varepsilon}\int{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}\,d|\nu|.

Thus, if
{\varepsilon}
is chosen small enough, we deduce that

\int{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}\,d|\nu|=|g(\bar{x})|\int{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}\,d|\nu|\leq 2\left|\int{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}\,d\nu\right|\lesssim r^{N}.

Therefore, using again that
{\varphi}_{\bar{x},r}=1
on
B(\bar{x},r)
, we get

(5.4)

|\nu|(B(\bar{x},r))\lesssim r^{N}.

To get rid of the doubling assumption (
5.3
), notice that for
|\nu|
-a.e.
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
there
exists a sequence of balls
B(\bar{x},r_{k})
, with
r_{k}\to 0
, satisfying (
5.3
) (we say that the balls

B(\bar{x},r_{k})
are
|\nu|
-doubling). Further, we may assume
that
r_{k}=2^{h_{k}}
, for some
h_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The proof of this fact is analogous to the one of Lemma 2.8
in
[
To2
]
. So for such a point
\bar{x}
, by the arguments above, we know that there exists
some
k_{0}>0
such that

|\nu|(B(\bar{x},r_{k}))\lesssim r_{k}^{N}\quad\mbox{ for $k\geq k_{0}$,}

assuming also that
\bar{x}
is a
|\nu|
-Lebesgue point for the density
g
. Given an arbitrary
r\in(0,r_{k_{0}})
,
let
j
be the smallest integer
r\leq 2^{j}
, and let
2^{k}
be the smallest
j\leq k
such that
the ball
B(\bar{x},2^{k})
is
|\nu|
-doubling (i.e., (
5.3
) holds for this ball). Observe that

2^{k}\leq r_{k_{0}}
.
Then, taking into account that the balls
B(\bar{x},2^{h})
are non-doubling for
j\leq h<k
and applying (
5.4
) for

r=2^{k}
, we obtain

|\nu|(B(\bar{x},r))\leq|\nu|(B(\bar{x},2^{j}))\leq 2^{(n+3)(j-k)}\,|\nu|(B(\bar{x},2^{k}))\lesssim 2^{(n+3)(j-k)}\,2^{kn}\leq 2^{jn}\approx r^{N}.

Hence, (
5.2
) holds and we are done.
∎

Next we will construct a self-similar Cantor set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with positive and finite measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}
which is removable for the
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional heat equation. Our example is inspired by the typical planar
1/4
Cantor
set in the setting of analytic capacity (see
[
Ga
]
or
[
To2
, p. 35]
, for example).

We construct the Cantor set
E
as follows:
we let
E_{0}
be the unit cube, i.e.
E_{0}=Q^{0}=[0,1]^{N+1}
. Next we replace
Q^{0}
by
2^{N+1}
disjoint closed cubes
Q^{1}_{i}
with side length
2^{\frac{-(N+1)}{N}}
such that they are all contained in
Q^{0}
and each one contains a vertex of
Q^{0}
.

We proceed inductively:
In each generation
k
, we replace each cube
Q_{j}^{k-1}
of the previous generation by
2^{N+1}
cubes
Q_{i}^{k}
with side length
2^{(-k)\frac{N+1}{N}}
which are contained in
Q_{j}^{k-1}
and located in the same relative position to
Q_{j}^{k-1}
as the cubes
Q_{1}^{1},\ldots,Q^{1}_{2^{N+1}}
with respect to
Q_{0}
.

Notice that in each generation
k
there are
2^{k(N+1)}
closed cubes with side length
2^{\frac{-(N+1)}{N}k}
.
We denote by
E_{k}
the union of all these cubes from the
k
-th generation. By construction,
E_{k}\subset E_{k-1}
.
We let

(5.5)

E=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}E_{k}.

It is easy to check that

{\rm dist}(Q_{i}^{k},Q_{h}^{k})\geq C(N)2^{\frac{-(N+1)}{N}k}
for
i\neq h
, and if
Q_{i}^{k}
,
Q_{h}^{k}
are contained in the same cube
Q^{k-1}_{j}
, then
{\rm dist}(Q_{i}^{k},Q_{h}^{k})\lesssim 2^{\frac{-(N+1)}{N}k}
.
Taking into account that, for each
k\geq 0
,

\sum_{i=1}^{2^{k(N+1)}}\ell(Q_{i}^{k})^{N}=2^{k(N+1)}\cdot 2^{-k(N+1)}=1,

by standard arguments it follows that

0<{\mathcal{H}}^{N}(E)<\infty.

Further,
{\mathcal{H}}^{N}|_{E}
coincides, modulo a constant factor, with the probability measure
\mu
supported on
E
which gives the same measure to all the cubes
Q_{i}^{k}
of the same generation
k
, that is
\mu(Q_{i}^{k})=2^{-k(N+1)}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The Cantor set
E
defined in (
5.5
) is removable for
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional caloric functions.

Proof.

We will suppose that
E
is not removable and we will reach a contradiction.
By Theorem
4.2
, there exists a distribution
\nu
supported on
E
such that

|\langle\nu,1\rangle|>0
and

\|P*\nu\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})}\leq 1.

By Theorem
5.1
,
\nu
is a signed measure of the form

\nu=f\,\mu,\qquad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\lesssim 1,

where
\mu
is the probability measure supported on
E
such that

\mu(Q_{i}^{k})=2^{-k(N+1)}
for all
i,k
.
It is easy to check that
\mu
(and thus
|\nu|
) has upper growth of degree
N
. Recall that
T
denotes the operator
T\nu=P*\nu
, defined over distributions
\nu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
. Then,
arguing as in
[
MaP
, Lemma 5.4]
, it follows that there exists some constant
K
such that

(5.6)

T_{*}\nu(\bar{x})\leq K\,\quad\mbox{ for all $\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}$.}

For
\bar{x}\in E
, let
Q_{\bar{x}}^{k}
the cube
Q_{i}^{k}
that contains
\bar{x}
. Then we consider the auxiliary operator

{\widetilde{T}}_{*}\nu(\bar{x})=\sup_{k\geq 0}|T(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\setminus Q_{\bar{x}}^{k}}\nu)(\bar{x})|.

By the separation condition between the cubes
Q_{i}^{k}
, the upper growth of
|\nu|
, and the condition (
5.6
), it follows easily that

(5.7)

{\widetilde{T}}_{*}\nu(\bar{x})\leq K^{\prime}\,\quad\mbox{ for all $\bar{x}\in E$,}

for some fixed constant
K^{\prime}
.

We will contradict the last estimate. To this end, consider a Lebesgue point
\bar{x}_{0}\in E
(with respect to
\mu
) of the density
f=\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}
such that
f(\bar{x}_{0})>0
. The existence of this point is
guarantied by the fact that
\nu(E)>0
.
Given
{\varepsilon}>0
small enough to be chosen below, consider a cube
Q_{i}^{k}
containing
\bar{x}_{0}
such that

\frac{1}{\mu(Q_{i}^{k})}\int_{Q_{i}^{k}}|f(\bar{y})-f(\bar{x}_{0})|\,d\mu(y)\leq{\varepsilon}.

Given
m\gg 1
, to be fixed below too, it is easy to check that if
{\varepsilon}
is chosen small enough (depending on
m
and on
f(\bar{x}_{0})
), then
the above condition ensures that
every cube
Q_{j}^{h}
contained in
Q_{i}^{k}
such that
k\leq h\leq k+m
satisfies

(5.8)

\frac{1}{2}f(\bar{x}_{0})\,\mu(Q^{h}_{j})\leq\nu(Q^{h}_{j})\leq 2f(\bar{x}_{0})\,\mu(Q^{h}_{j}).

Notice also that, writing
\nu=\nu^{+}-\nu^{-}
, since
f(\bar{x}_{0})>0
,

\nu^{-}(Q_{i}^{k})=\int_{Q_{i}^{k}}f^{-}(\bar{y})\,d\mu(\bar{y})\leq\int_{Q_{i}^{k}}|f(\bar{y})-f(\bar{x}_{0})|\,d\mu(\bar{y})\leq{\varepsilon}\,\mu(Q_{i}^{k}).

Let
\bar{z}=(z,u)
be one of the upper corners of
Q^{k}_{i}
(i.e.,
u
maximal in
Q^{k}_{i}
). Since

|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu(\bar{z})|\leq 2\,{\widetilde{T}}_{*}\nu(\bar{z})
, we have

{\widetilde{T}}_{*}\nu(\bar{z})\geq\frac{1}{2}\,|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu)(\bar{z})|\geq\frac{1}{2}\,|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu^{+})(\bar{z})|-\frac{1}{2}\,|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu^{-})(\bar{z})|.

Using the fact that
{\rm dist}(\bar{z},\,Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}})\gtrsim\ell(Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}})
, we get

|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu^{-})(\bar{z})|\lesssim\frac{\nu^{-}(Q^{k}_{i})}{\ell(Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}})^{N}}\leq{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\mu(Q^{k}_{i})}{\ell(Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}})^{N}}={\varepsilon}\,\frac{\mu(Q^{k}_{i})}{2^{-m(N+1)}\ell(Q^{k}_{i})^{N}}\lesssim 2^{m(N+1)}\,{\varepsilon}.

To estimate
|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu^{+})(\bar{z})|
from below, recall
that our kernel is

P(\bar{x})=\cfrac{t}{(t^{2}+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}\,\chi_{\{t>0\}}.

Then, by the choice of
\bar{z}
, it follows that

(5.9)

P(\bar{z}-\bar{y})\geq 0\quad\mbox{ for all $\bar{y}\in Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}$.}

We write

|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu^{+})(\bar{z})|\geq\int_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}P(\bar{z}-\bar{y})\,d\nu^{+}(\bar{y})=\sum_{h=k}^{k+m-1}\int_{Q^{h}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{h+1}_{\bar{z}}}P(\bar{z}-\bar{y})\,d\nu^{+}(\bar{y}).

Taking into account (
5.9
) and the fact that, for
k\leq h\leq k+m-1
,
Q^{h}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{h+1}_{\bar{z}}
contains a cube
Q^{h+1}_{j}
such that for all
\bar{y}=(y,s)
,

0<u-s\approx|\bar{y}-\bar{z}|\approx\ell(Q^{h+1}_{j}),

using also (
5.8
),
we deduce

\int_{Q^{h}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{h+1}_{\bar{z}}}P(\bar{z}-\bar{y})\,d\nu^{+}(\bar{y})\gtrsim\frac{\nu^{+}(Q^{h+1}_{j})}{\ell(Q^{h+1}_{j})^{N}}\gtrsim f(\bar{x}_{0})\,\frac{\mu(Q^{h+1}_{j})}{\ell(Q^{h+1}_{j})^{N}}=f(\bar{x}_{0}),

Thus,

|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu^{+})(\bar{z})|\gtrsim(m-1)\,f(\bar{x}_{0}).

Together with the previous estimate for
|T(\chi_{Q^{k}_{\bar{z}}\setminus Q^{k+m}_{\bar{z}}}\nu^{-})(\bar{z})|
, this tells us that

{\widetilde{T}}_{*}\nu(\bar{z})\gtrsim(m-1)\,f(x_{0})-C\,2^{m\frac{N+1}{N}}\,{\varepsilon},

for some fixed
C>0
.
It is clear that if we choose
m
big enough and then
{\varepsilon}
small enough, depending on
m
, this
lower bound contradicts (
5.7
), as wished.
∎

6.
Neither analytic capacity nor Newtonian capacity are comparable to
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}
.

In this section we will show that, in the plane, the capacity
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}
is not comparable to analytic capacity nor to Newtonian capacity; two classical capacities related to complex analysis and potential theory with critical dimension
1
, as
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}
. More precisely, we will construct two sets, the first one will be a compact set
E_{1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
with
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E_{1})>0
but vanishing Newtonian capacity. The second example we will be a compact set
E_{2}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
with positive analytic capacity but removable for the
\frac{1}{2}-
fractional heat equation.

First we recall some definitions. For a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{C}
, the analytic capacity of
E
is defined as

\gamma(E)=\sup\{|f^{\prime}(\infty)|\},

where the supremum is taken over all analytic functions
f:\mathbb{C}\setminus E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with
|f|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus E
and

\displaystyle{f^{\prime}(\infty)=\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}z(f(z)-f(\infty))}.

The Newtonian capacity of a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{C}
can be defined as

C(E)=\sup\{\mu(E):\mbox{spt}(\mu)\subset E,\,\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{1}{|z-w|}d\mu(w)\leq 1\,\,\text{ for }z\in\mathbb{C}\}.

It is well-known that if
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a segment of length
\ell
, then
\gamma(E)=\ell/4
and
C(E)=0
(see
[
To2
, Proposition 1.4]
and
[
Ga
, Corollary 3.6]
respectively).

We will prove that if
E_{1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a horizontal segment then
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E_{1})>0
. On the other hand, if
E_{2}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a vertical segment,
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E_{2})=0
. Consequently
C(E_{1})
is not comparable to
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E_{1})
and
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E_{2})
is not comparable to
\gamma(E_{2})
. The result reads as follows.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
L
be a positive number. Set
E_{1}=\{(x,0)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:0\leq\,x\leq L\}
and
E_{2}=\{(0,t)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:0\leq t\leq L\}.
Then

a)

\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E_{1})>0.

b)

\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E_{2})=0.

Proof.

a)

Let
\chi_{E_{1}}
be the characteristic function of
E_{1}
and consider the positive measure

\mu=\chi_{E_{1}}dx

and the function

f(a,s)=\big(P\ast\chi_{E_{1}}\big)(a,s),

where
P
is the fundamental solution of the
\frac{1}{2}
-fractional heat equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, that is
\displaystyle P(x,t)=\frac{t}{x^{2}+t^{2}}\chi_{t>0}
.
Clearly
f(a,s)=0
if
s\leq 0.
For
s>0
we have

\displaystyle f(a,s)=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{s}{(a-x)^{2}+(s)^{2}}dx=\arctan(\frac{L-a}{s})-\arctan(\frac{-a}{s})\leq\pi,

so that

\gamma^{1/2}_{\Theta}(E_{1})\geq\frac{\mu(E_{1})}{\pi}=\frac{L}{\pi}>0.

b)

Assume that
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}(E_{2})>0
. Then there exists a distribution
S
supported on
E_{2}
such that
\|P\ast S\|_{\infty}\leq 1.
Approximating the distribution
S
by signed measures (take for instance the convolution of
S
with approximations of the identity in the variable
t
), we can assume that there exists a signed measure
\nu
supported on the segment

E_{\varepsilon}=\{(0,t):-\varepsilon\leq t\leq L+\varepsilon\}

for some small
\varepsilon>0
, satisfying
\|P\ast\nu\|_{\infty}\leq 2.
Since
P
is a non negative kernel, there exists a positive measure
\mu
supported on
E_{\varepsilon}
with
\mu(E_{\varepsilon})>0
such that

(6.1)

\|P\ast\mu\|_{\infty}\leq 2.

To get a contradiction we will show that (
6.1
) implies
\mu(E_{\varepsilon})=0.

Since, by lemma
3.4
, the measure
\mu
has linear growth, given
\eta>0
we can take
c\in(-{\varepsilon},L+{\varepsilon})
such that

\mu(\{(0,t):c\leq t\leq L+{\varepsilon}\})<\eta.

Set
F=\{(0,t):-{\varepsilon}\leq t\leq c\}
. By (
6.1
), for any
(0,s)\in F
there exists
\ell=\ell(0,s)>0
such that

\int_{s}^{s+\ell}\frac{t-s}{(t-s)^{2}}d\mu(t)\leq\eta.

Hence, since
F
is an interval, there exists a finite number of almost disjoint intervals
I_{n}=\{0\}\times[s_{n},s_{n}+\ell_{n}],\,1\leq n\leq N,
such that

\displaystyle{F\subset\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}}
and

\mu(I_{n})=\int_{s_{n}}^{s_{n}+\ell_{n}}d\mu(t)\leq\int_{s_{n}}^{s_{n}+\ell_{n}}\frac{\ell_{n}}{t-s_{n}}d\mu(t)\leq\eta\ell_{n}.

Consequently,

\mu(E_{\varepsilon})\leq\mu(F)+\eta\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu(I_{n})+\eta\leq\eta(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\ell_{n}+1)\leq\eta(c+{\varepsilon}+1),

which means
\mu(E_{\varepsilon})=0
and we get the desired contradiction.

∎

7.
s
-Capacities and
s
-growth for
s\in(1/2,1)
.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{*,p}
denote the norm of the parabolic BMO space:

\|f\|_{*,p}=\sup_{Q}\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{Q}|f-m_{Q}f|\,dm,

where the supremum is taken over all
s-
parabolic cubes
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,

dm
stands for the Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and
m_{Q}f
is the mean of
f
with respect to
dm
.

For a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the
\alpha-
fractional derivative with respect to
t
(recall that
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
) is defined as

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}f(x,t)=\int\frac{f(x,s)-f(x,t)}{|s-t|^{1+\alpha}}ds.

Now we introduce the
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}
capacities for
s\in(1/2,1)
. Given a compact set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, we define

(7.1)

\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}(E)=\sup\{|\langle\nu,1\rangle|\},

where the supremum is taken over all distributions
\nu
supported on
E
satisfying

(7.2)

\|(-\Delta)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\nu*P_{s})\|_{\infty}\leq 1\quad\mbox{ and}\quad\|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(\nu*P_{s})\|_{*,p}\leq 1.

Notice that for
s=1/2
we obtain
\gamma_{\Theta}^{1/2}
.

The capacity
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}
is called the
s
-fractional caloric capacity of
E
. We set
\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{s}(E)
as in (
7.1
) but with the supremum restricted to all positive measures
\nu
supported on
E
satisfying (
7.2
). Clearly

\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{s}(E)\leq\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}(E).

We are now ready to describe the basic relationship between the capacity
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}
and Hausdorff content (the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff content will be denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}_{\infty}^{d}
).

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
s\in(1/2,1)
. For every Borel set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,

\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{s}(E)\leq\gamma^{s}_{\Theta}(E)\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{N+2s-1}_{\infty}(E)

and

{\mbox{dim}}_{H,p}(E)>N+2s-1\Longrightarrow\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}(E)>0.

To prove theorem
7.1
we need to study the growth conditions satisfied by positive measures and distributions with properties (
7.2
).

7.1.
The case of positive measures.

We state first two lemmas concerning the case when the distribution
\nu
is a positive measure. But before going to the next lemma, recall that a function
f(x,t)
defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
is Lip
(\alpha)
,
0\alpha<1
, in the
t
variable if

\|f\|_{Lip(\alpha)}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},t,u\in{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{|f(x,t)-f(x,u)|}{|t-u|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
s\in(1/2,1)
and let
\mu
be a positive measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with upper parabolic growth of degree
N+2s-1
with constant
1
. Then,

\|P_{s}*\mu\|_{Lip(1-\frac{1}{2s})}\lesssim 1.

Proof.

Let
\bar{x}=(x,t)
,
\hat{x}=(x,u)
, and
\bar{x}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(\bar{x}+\hat{x})
.
Then, writing
\bar{y}=(y,s)
, we split

\displaystyle|P_{s}*\mu(\bar{x})-P_{s}*\mu(\hat{x})|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\bar{y}-\bar{x}_{0}|_{p}\geq 2|\bar{x}-\hat{x}|_{p}}|P_{s}(x-y,t-s)-P_{s}(x-y,u-s)|\,d\mu(\bar{y})

\displaystyle+\int_{|\bar{y}-\bar{x}_{0}|_{p}<2|\bar{x}-\hat{x}|_{p}}\!|P_{s}(x-y,t-s)-P_{s}(x-y,u-s)|\,d\mu(\bar{y})

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

To shorten notation, we write
d:=|\bar{x}-\hat{x}|_{p}=|t-u|^{\frac{1}{2s}}
. Then we have

I_{1}\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{2^{k}d\leq|\bar{y}-\bar{x}_{0}|_{p}<2^{k+1}d}\,\,\sup_{\xi\in[\bar{x}-\bar{y},\hat{x}-\bar{y}]}|\partial_{t}P_{s}(\xi)|\,|t-u|\,d\mu(\bar{y}).

Since by property
(3)
in lemma
2.2

|\partial_{t}P_{s}(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi|_{p}^{N+2s}}\approx\frac{1}{(2^{k}d)^{N+2s}}\quad\mbox{ if $\xi\in[\bar{x}-\bar{y},\hat{x}-\bar{y}]$, $|\bar{y}-\bar{x}_{0}|_{p}\approx 2^{k}d$,}

we deduce that

I_{1}\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mu(B_{p}(\bar{x}_{0},2^{k+1}d))}{(2^{k}d)^{N+2s}}\,|t-u|\lesssim\frac{|t-u|}{d}=|t-u|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}.

Next we deal with
I_{2}
. Writing
B_{0}=B_{p}(\bar{x}_{0},2d)
, we have

I_{2}\leq P_{s}*(\chi_{B_{0}}\mu)(\bar{x})+P_{s}*(\chi_{B_{0}}\mu)(\hat{x}).

Observe now that by lemma
2.2

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq P_{s}*(\chi_{B_{0}}\mu)(\bar{x})\lesssim\int_{\bar{y}\in B_{0}}\frac{1}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|_{p}^{N}}\,d\mu(\bar{y})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|_{p}\leq 4d}\frac{1}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|_{p}^{N}}\,d\mu(\bar{y})\lesssim d^{2s-1}=|t-u|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}.

The last estimate follows by splitting the integral into parabolic annuli and using the parabolic
growth of order
N+2s-1
of
\mu
, for example.
The same estimate holds replacing
\bar{x}
by
\hat{x}
. Then gathering all the estimates above, the lemma
follows.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
s\in(1/2,1)
and let
\mu
be a positive measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
which has upper parabolic growth
N+2s-1
, with constant
1
. Then

\|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*\mu\|_{*,p}\lesssim 1.

Proof.

Let
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a fixed

s
-parabolic cube. We have to show that there exists some constant
c_{Q}

(to be chosen below) such that

\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*\mu-c_{Q}|\,dm\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s}.

To this end consider a
C^{\infty}
test function
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q}
which equals
1
on
5Q
and vanishes in
6Q^{c}
.

We also denote
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q^{c}}=1-{\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q}
. Then we
split

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*\mu-c_{Q}|\,dm

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q}\mu)|\,dm

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q^{c}}\mu)-c_{Q}|\,dm=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

To deal with the integral
I_{1}
, we use lemma
2.2
and write

\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q}\mu)|\,dm\lesssim\int_{Q}\frac{1}{|x|^{N-1}\,|\bar{x}|_{p}^{2s}}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q}\mu)\,dm\leq\int_{6Q}\frac{1}{|y|^{N-1}\,|\bar{y}|_{p}^{2s}}*(\chi_{Q}m)\,d\mu.

Taking into account that, for
\bar{y}=(y,u)
,

\frac{1}{|y|^{N-1}\,|\bar{y}|_{p}^{2s}}\leq\frac{1}{|y|^{N-1+s/2}}\,\frac{1}{u^{3/4}},

and writing
Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}
, where
Q_{1}
is a cube with side length
\ell(Q)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
I_{Q}
is an interval of length
\ell(Q)^{2s}
,
we deduce that for
\bar{x}=(x,t)\in 6Q
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|y|^{N-1}\,|\bar{y}|_{p}^{2s}}*(\chi_{Q}m)(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{y\in Q_{1}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-1+s/2}}dy\,\int_{u\in I_{Q}}\frac{1}{|t-u|^{3/4}}\,du

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(Q)^{1-s/2}\,(\ell(Q)^{2s})^{1/4}=\ell(Q).

Thus,

\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q}\mu)|\,dm\lesssim\ell(Q)\,\mu(6Q)\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s}.

Next we will estimate the integral
I_{2}
, taking
c_{Q}:=\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q^{c}}\mu)(\bar{x}_{Q})
, where
\bar{x}_{Q}
is the center of
Q
.
To show that
I_{2}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s}
, it suffices to prove

|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q^{c}}\mu)(\bar{x})-\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q^{c}}\mu)(\bar{x}_{Q})|\lesssim 1.

In turn, to prove this it is enough to show that

(7.3)

|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q^{c}}\mu)(\bar{x})-\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*({\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q^{c}}\mu)(\bar{y})|\lesssim 1

for
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
in the following two cases:

•

Case 1:
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in Q
of the form
\bar{x}=(x,t)
,
\bar{y}=(y,t)
.

•

Case 2:
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in Q
of the form
\bar{x}=(x,t)
,
\bar{y}=(x,u)
.

Proof of (
7.3
) in Case 1.

Let
\phi={\widetilde{\chi}}_{5Q^{c}}
. We split

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)-\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int\frac{P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}du-\!\int\frac{P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,t)}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}du\right|

\displaystyle\leq\quad\quad\int_{|u-t|\leq 2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)|}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,du

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{|u-t|\leq 2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,t)|}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,du

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\!\int_{|u-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\!\!\!\!\frac{|P_{s}\!*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}\!*(\phi\mu)(x,t)-P_{s}\!*(\phi\mu)(y,u)+P_{s}\!*(\phi\mu)(y,t)|}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}du

\displaystyle=:A_{1}+A_{2}+B.

First we will estimate the term
A_{1}
. For
u,t
such that
|u-t|\leq 2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}
, we write

|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)|\leq|u-t|\,\|\partial_{t}P_{s}*(\phi\mu)\|_{\infty,(1+2^{2s})Q}.

Observe now that for each
\bar{z}=(z,v)\in(1+2^{2s})Q
(see property
(3)
in lemma
2.2
)

|\partial_{t}P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(\bar{z})|\lesssim\int_{5Q^{c}}\frac{1}{|\bar{z}-\bar{w}|_{p}^{N+2s}}\,d\mu(\bar{w})\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(Q)},

by splitting the last domain of integration into parabolic annuli and using the growth condition of order
N+2s-1
of
\mu
. Thus,

|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)|\lesssim\frac{|u-t|}{\ell(Q)}.

Plugging this into the integral that
defines
A_{1}
, we obtain

A_{1}\lesssim\int_{|u-t|\leq 2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|u-t|}{\ell(Q)\,|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,ds\lesssim\frac{(\ell(Q)^{2s})^{\frac{1}{2s}}}{\ell(Q)}=1.

By exactly the same arguments, just writing
y
in place of
x
above, we deduce also that

A_{2}\lesssim 1.

Concerning the term
B
, we write

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|u-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,t)|}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,du

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{|u-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,u)|}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,du=B_{1}+B_{2}.

By Lemma
2.2
, it follows that for
\bar{\xi}\in Q
,

|\nabla_{x}P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(\bar{\xi})|\lesssim\int_{5Q^{c}}\frac{d\mu(\bar{z})}{|\bar{\xi}-\bar{z}|^{N+1}_{p}}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{2s-2}.

Therefore,

|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,t)|\leq\|\nabla_{x}P_{s}*(\phi\mu)\|_{\infty,Q}\,|x-y|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{2s-1}.

Hence
B_{1}\lesssim 1.

For
B_{2}
, we split the integral into annulus
\mathcal{A}_{k}=\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}:|u-t|\approx(2^{k}\ell(Q))^{2s}\}
and write

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|u-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,u)|}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,du

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}\frac{|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,u)|}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,du.

For
u\in\mathcal{A}_{k},
using lemma
2.2
we get

\displaystyle|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(y,u)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\underset{5Q^{c}\cap\{|\bar{z}-(x_{Q},u)|\leq 3\ell(Q)\}}{\int}\frac{d\mu(\bar{z})}{|(x_{Q},u)-\bar{z}|_{p}^{N}}+\underset{5Q^{c}\cap\{|\bar{z}-(x_{Q},u)|>3\ell(Q)\}}{\int}\big|P_{s}((x,u)-\bar{z})-P_{s}((y,u)-\bar{z})\big|d\mu(\bar{z})

\displaystyle\lesssim\underset{5Q^{c}\cap\{|\bar{z}-(x_{Q},u)|\leq 3\ell(Q)\}}{\int}\frac{d\mu(\bar{z})}{|(x_{Q},u)-\bar{z}|_{p}^{N}}+\underset{5Q^{c}\cap\{|\bar{z}-(x_{Q},u)|>3\ell(Q)\}}{\int}\frac{|x-y|}{|(x_{Q},u)-\bar{z}|_{p}^{N+1}}d\mu(\bar{z})

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(Q)^{2s-1}+\ell(Q)\ell(Q)^{2s-2}=\ell(Q)^{2s-1}.

Therefore

B_{2}\lesssim\int_{|u-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s-1}}{|u-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,du\lesssim\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s-1}}{(\ell(Q)^{2s})^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}}\lesssim 1.

So (
7.3
) holds in this case.

Proof of (
7.3
) in Case 2.

As in Case 1, we write

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)-\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)|

\displaystyle=\biggl|\int\frac{P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,v)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)}{|v-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dv-\!\int\frac{P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,v)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)}{|v-u|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}dv\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\quad\quad\int_{|v-t|\leq 2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,v)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)|}{|v-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,dv

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{|v-t|\leq 2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,v)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)|}{|v-u|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,dv

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{|v-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\biggl|\frac{P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,v)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)}{|v-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}-\frac{P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,v)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)}{|v-u|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\biggr|dv

\displaystyle=:A_{1}^{\prime}+A_{2}^{\prime}+B^{\prime}.

The terms
A_{1}^{\prime}
and
A_{2}^{\prime}
can be estimated exactly in the same way as the terms
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
in Case 1, so that

A_{1}^{\prime}+A_{2}^{\prime}\lesssim 1.

Concerning
B^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|v-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\left|\frac{1}{|v-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}-\frac{1}{|v-u|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\right|\,\big|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,v)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)\big|\,dv

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{|v-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{1}{|v-u|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,\big|P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,t)-P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,u)\big|\,dv

Taking into account that, for
|v-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}
,

\left|\frac{1}{|v-t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}-\frac{1}{|v-u|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\right|\lesssim\frac{|t-u|}{|v-t|^{3-\frac{1}{2s}}}\lesssim\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}}{|v-t|^{3-\frac{1}{2s}}}

and that, by Lemma
7.2
,
P_{s}*(\phi\mu)(x,\cdot)
is Lip
(1-\frac{1}{2s})
in the variable
t
,
we deduce that

B^{\prime}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{2s}\int_{|v-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{|v-t|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}}{|v-t|^{3-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,dv\\
+\int_{|v-t|>2^{2s}\ell(Q)^{2s}}\frac{1}{|v-u|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}\,|t-u|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}\,dv\lesssim 1.

This concludes the proof of
\displaystyle{\|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*\mu\|_{*,p}\lesssim 1}
for
s\in(1/2,1)
.
∎

7.2.
Growth condition and admissible functions.

When
s\in(1/2,1),
we say that a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
function
{\varphi}
is admissible for an
s
-parabolic cube
Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}
,
Q_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
I_{Q}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, if it is supported on
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and satisfies

(7.4)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}|(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}

and

(7.5)

\|\partial_{t}{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\ell(I_{Q})}=\frac{1}{\ell(Q)^{2s}}.

As in the case
s=1/2
(see (
3.1
)) recall that
\displaystyle(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi},
with
R_{j}
being the Riesz transforms with Fourier multiplier
\xi_{j}/|\xi|
. Then, for an
s
-parabolic cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},

Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}
with
Q_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
I_{Q}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, one can write

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}|(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt

\displaystyle\approx\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt

Therefore if
{\varphi}
satisfies (
7.5
) and

\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|R_{j}\partial_{j}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1},\;\mbox{ for }1\leq j\leq N,

then
{\varphi}
is admissible for the
s
-parabolic cube
Q
,
s\in(1/2,1)
.

Let’s remark that the analogues of lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
also hold in this context and if
\varphi
is a standard test function supported on an
s-
parabolic cube
Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with
\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ell(I_{Q})^{-1}=\ell(Q)^{-2s}
and
\|\Delta_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-2}
, then
\varphi
is admissible for
Q
. In fact, arguing as in (
3
) we have that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}|(-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}{\varphi}(x,t)|dxdt

\displaystyle\approx\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}|\Delta_{x}\varphi*_{x}k(x,t)|dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus 2Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{|\varphi(y,t)|}{|x-y|^{N+1}}dydxdt+\int_{I_{Q}}\int_{2Q_{1}}\int_{Q_{1}}\frac{|\Delta_{y}\varphi(y,t)|}{|x-y|^{N-1}}dydxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell(Q)^{N+2s}(2^{k}\ell(Q))^{N}}{(2^{k}\ell(Q))^{N+1}}+\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

For these values of
s
, the analogous growth condition to lemma
3.4
reads as follows.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
s\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\nu
a distribution such that

\|(-\Delta)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\nu*P_{s})\|_{\infty}\leq 1\quad\mbox{ and}\quad\|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(P_{s}*\nu)\|_{*,p}\leq 1.

If
{\varphi}
is an admissible function for an
s-
parabolic cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
, then

\displaystyle\left|\langle\nu,\varphi\rangle\right|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

Proof.

\displaystyle|\langle\nu,{\varphi}\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle\nu,\Theta^{s}{\varphi}*P_{s}\rangle|\leq|\langle\nu*P_{s},(-\Delta)^{s}{\varphi}\rangle|+|\langle\nu*P_{s},\partial_{t}{\varphi}\rangle|=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Notice that since
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1
, if we consider the Fourier transform of
\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}{\varphi}
, we get

\widehat{\left((-\Delta)^{s}{\varphi}\right)}(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{\varphi}=|\xi|^{2s-1}|\xi|\hat{\varphi}.

Hence we can write
(-\Delta)^{s}{\varphi}=(-\Delta)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\varphi}
. Therefore, since
{\varphi}
is admissible for the
s
-parabolic cube
Q
, (
7.4
) is satisfied and so

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=|\langle\nu*P_{s},(-\Delta)^{s}{\varphi}\rangle|=|\langle(-\Delta)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\nu*P_{s}),(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\varphi}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|(-\Delta)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\nu*P_{s})\|_{\infty}\int_{I_{Q}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\varphi}(x,t)\big|dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

We deal now with
I_{2}
. Notice that

\partial_{t}{\varphi}=c\;\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(\partial_{t}{\varphi}*\frac{1}{|t|^{1/{2s}}}),

for some constant
c
. This identity can be easily seen by taking the Fourier transform (on the variable
t
). Set
\displaystyle g=\partial_{t}{\varphi}*\frac{1}{|t|^{1/{2s}}}
. Then

I_{2}=|\langle\nu*P_{s},\partial_{t}{\varphi}\rangle|=|c||\langle\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(\nu*P_{s}),g\rangle|.

Write
Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}
, with
Q_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
I_{Q}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, and let
c_{Q}
be the center of the interval
I_{Q}
. Because of the zero mean of
\partial_{t}{\varphi}
(integrating with respect to
t
) it is easy to check that
|g(x,t)|
decays at most like
\displaystyle|t|^{-1-\frac{1}{2s}}
at infinity. Indeed, for
t\notin 2I_{Q}
we have

(7.6)

\displaystyle|g(x,t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{I_{Q}}\frac{\partial_{u}{\varphi}(x,u)}{|t-u|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}du\right|=\left|\int_{I_{Q}}\partial_{u}{\varphi}(x,u)\left(\frac{1}{|t-u|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}-\frac{1}{|t-c_{Q}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\right)du\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ell(I_{Q})}{|t-c_{Q}|^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{I_{Q}}|\partial_{u}{\varphi}(x,u)|du\lesssim\frac{\ell(I_{Q})}{|t-c_{Q}|^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}}=\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}}{|t-c_{Q}|^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}},

where
c_{Q}
is the center of
Q
.
And for
t\in 4I_{Q}
,

(7.7)

|g(x,t)|\lesssim\|\partial_{u}{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\ell(I_{Q})^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}\lesssim\ell(I_{Q})^{-\frac{1}{2s}}=\ell(Q)^{-1}.

Since
\int g\;dm=0
, writing
f=\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(\nu*P_{s})
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=|c||\langle f,g\rangle|=\left|c\int(f-m_{Q}f)\;g\;dm\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2Q}|f-m_{Q}f|\;|g|\;dm+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\setminus 2Q}(f-m_{Q}f)\;g\;dm

\displaystyle=I_{21}+I_{22}.

Hence, due to (
7.7
),

I_{21}\lesssim\|f\|_{*,p}\,\ell(Q)^{N+2s}\ell(Q)^{-1}\leq\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

For
I_{22}
, we split the domain of integration in annuli. Write
A_{i}=2^{i}Q\setminus 2^{i-1}Q
for
i\geq 1
.
Remark that for an
s
-parabolic cube
Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}
, we denote

2^{i}Q=2^{i}Q_{1}\times 2^{2si}I_{Q},

so that
2^{i}Q
is an
s
-parabolic cube too (notice that if
Q
is centered at the origin and we consider the parabolic dilation
\delta_{\lambda}(x,t)=(\lambda x,\lambda^{2s}t)
,
\lambda>0
, we have
2^{i}Q=\delta_{2^{i}}(Q)
).
Then, using the decay of
g
given by (
7.6
), we get

(7.8)

I_{22}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}}{\ell(2^{i}Q)^{1+2s}}\left(\int_{A_{i}\cap\operatorname{supp}g}|f-m_{2^{i}Q}f|\,dm+\int_{A_{i}\cap\operatorname{supp}g}|m_{2^{i}Q}f-m_{Q}f|\,dm\right).

To estimate the first integral on the right hand side, recall that

\operatorname{supp}g\subset Q_{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
. Using Hölder’s inequality with some
exponent
q\in(0,\infty)
to be chosen in a moment and the fact that

f\in BMO_{p}
(together with John-Nirenberg), then we get:

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i}\cap\operatorname{supp}g}|f-m_{2^{i}Q}f|\,dm

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{2^{i}Q}|f-m_{2^{i}Q}f|^{q}\,dm\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,m(\operatorname{supp}g\cap 2^{i}Q)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(2^{i}Q)^{\frac{N+2s}{q}}\,(\ell(Q)^{N}\,\ell(2^{i}Q)^{2s})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\ell(2^{i}Q)^{\frac{N}{q}+2s}\,\ell(Q)^{\frac{N}{q^{\prime}}}.

For the last integral on the right hand side of (
7.8
), we write

\int_{A_{i}\cap\operatorname{supp}g}|m_{2^{i}Q}f-m_{Q}f|\,dm\lesssim i\,m(2^{i}Q\cap\operatorname{supp}g)\leq i\,\ell(Q)^{N}\,\ell(2^{i}Q)^{2s}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{22}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}}{\ell(2^{i}Q)^{1+2s}}\,\Big(\ell(2^{i}Q)^{\frac{N}{q}+2s}\,\ell(Q)^{\frac{N}{q^{\prime}}}+i\,\ell(Q)^{N}\,\ell(2^{i}Q)^{2s}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}\ell(Q)^{N+2s}}{\ell(Q)^{1+2s}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\,2^{i(\frac{N}{q}-1)}(1+\frac{i}{2^{i}}).

Choosing
q>N
, we get

I_{22}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

∎

7.3.
Proof of Theorem
7.1

The inequality
\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{s}(E)\leq\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}(E)
comes from the definition of the capacities. For the second inequality we assume
E
to be compact and let
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
be a covering of
E
by
s
-parabolic cubes
Q_{j}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
with disjoint interiors. By a parabolic version of a well known lemma of Harvey and Polking (see
[
HPo
]
), there exist functions
g_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(2Q_{j})
satisfying
\sum_{j}g_{j}=1
in a neighborhood of
\cup_{j}Q_{j}
, (
7.4
) and (
7.5
).

Let
\nu
be a distribution with compact support contained in
E
such that (
7.2
) hold. Then, using lemma
7.4

\big|\langle\nu,1\rangle\big|\leq\sum_{j}|\langle\nu,g_{j}\rangle|\leq\sum_{j}\ell(Q_{j})^{N+2s-1}.

Thus,
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}(E)\leq C{\mathcal{H}}^{N+2s-1}_{\infty}(E)
.

The second assertion in the theorem follows a standard argument that we reproduce for the reader’s convenience. Suppose
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
is a Borel set with dim
{}_{H,p}(E)=\alpha>N+2s-1
. By a parabolic version of Frostman’s Lemma (that can be proved by arguments analogous to classical ones replacing the usual dyadic lattice in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
by a parabolic dyadic lattice), there exists a non-zero positive measure
\mu
supported on
E
such that
\mu(B(\bar{x},r))\leq r^{\alpha}
for all
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and
r>0
. We have to show estimates (
7.2
) with
\nu
replaced by
\mu
.

•

The parabolic BMO-estimate in (
7.2
) is just a consequence of lemma
7.3
. Therefore
\displaystyle{\|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}*\mu\|_{*,p}\lesssim 1}
.

•

To prove
\displaystyle\|\Delta^{s-\frac{1}{2}}P_{s}*\mu\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1
, apply the second property in lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle|\Delta^{s-\frac{1}{2}}P_{s}*\mu(\bar{y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\frac{d\mu(\bar{x})}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{N+2s-1}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{\bar{x}:|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{-N-2s+1}\geq u\})du

(7.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{\bar{x}:|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|\leq u^{\frac{-1}{N+2s-1}}\})du=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(B(\bar{y},u^{\frac{-1}{N+2s-1}}))du

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(B(\bar{y},r))}{r^{N+2s}}dr\lesssim\int_{0}^{\mu(E)^{1/\alpha}}\frac{r^{\alpha}}{r^{N+2s}}dr+\int_{\mu(E)^{1/\alpha}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(E)}{r^{N+2s}}dr

\displaystyle\approx\mu(E)^{\frac{\alpha-(N+2s-1)}{\alpha}}.

∎

s-
capacities and
s
-growth in case

.

Now we introduce the
\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}

(8.1)

\gamma_{\Theta}^{s}(E)=\sup\{|\langle\nu,1\rangle|\},

where the supremum is taken over all distributions
\nu
supported on

satisfying properties (

) listed below. Before we write them, to get some intuition, let’s consider the case

Here
R_{j}^{s}
is the Calderón-Zygmund operator with kernel
\displaystyle\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{N+2s}}

*_{t}
denotes convolution with respect to the

K
is the kernel

(8.3)

K=\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{M}(M+1-\frac{1}{2s})}}*\overset{M)}{\cdots}*\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{M}(M+1-\frac{1}{2s})}},

(for a real number

Notice that for

(8.4)

(-\Delta)^{s}{\varphi}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{j}^{s}(\partial_{j}{\varphi}).

We say that a
\mathcal{C}^{M+1}
function
{\varphi}
is admissible for an
s-

Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
,
0<s<1/2

\|\nabla_{x}{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\ell(Q)}

Now the growth condition in this case reads as follows.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1/2)
,
M=\left[\frac{1}{2s}\right]
and set
\displaystyle K=\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{M}(M+1-\frac{1}{2s})}}*\overset{M)}{\cdots}*\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{M}(M+1-\frac{1}{2s})}}

Suppose that
\nu

\displaystyle\|R_{j}^{s}(\nu*P_{s})\|_{\infty}\leq 1,\quad 1\leq j\leq N\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\nu*P_{s}*_{t}\;K\|_{*,p}\leq 1.

{\varphi}

s-

|\langle\nu,{\varphi}\rangle|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

\displaystyle M=1

\displaystyle K=\frac{1}{|t|^{2-\frac{1}{2s}}}

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}(\nu*P_{s})\|_{*,p}\lesssim 1

Proof.

\displaystyle|\langle\nu,{\varphi}\rangle|

Using (
8.4
) and the
s-

{\varphi}
,

I_{1}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|R_{j}^{s}(\nu*P_{s})\|_{\infty}\int_{Q}|\partial_{j}{\varphi}|\;dm\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

To estimate
I_{2}

Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}
, with
Q_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
I_{Q}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, and let
c_{Q}

Indeed, since
\int g\;dm=0
, writing
f=\nu*P_{s}*K

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=|c||\langle f,g\rangle|=\left|c\int(f-m_{Q}f)\;g\;dm\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2Q}|f-m_{Q}f|\;|g|\;dm+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\setminus 2Q}(f-m_{Q}f)\;g\;dm

\displaystyle=I_{21}+I_{22}.

Due to (
8.7
),

I_{21}\lesssim\|f\|_{*,p}\,\ell(Q)^{N+2s}\ell(Q)^{-1}\leq\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

For
I_{22}
, we split the domain of integration in annuli. Write
A_{i}=2^{i}Q\setminus 2^{i-1}Q
for
i\geq 1
.
Remark that for an
s
-parabolic cube
Q=Q_{1}\times I_{Q}

2^{i}Q=2^{i}Q_{1}\times 2^{2si}I_{Q},

2^{i}Q

).

given by (

), we get

\operatorname{supp}g\subset Q_{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}

q\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i}\cap\operatorname{supp}g}|f-m_{2^{i}Q}f|\,dm

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{2^{i}Q}|f-m_{2^{i}Q}f|^{q}\,dm\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,m(\operatorname{supp}g\cap 2^{i}Q)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(2^{i}Q)^{\frac{N+2s}{q}}\,(\ell(Q)^{N}\,\ell(2^{i}Q)^{2s})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\ell(2^{i}Q)^{\frac{N}{q}+2s}\,\ell(Q)^{\frac{N}{q^{\prime}}}.

For the last integral on the right hand side of (
8.9
), we write

\int_{A_{i}\cap\operatorname{supp}g}|m_{2^{i}Q}f-m_{Q}f|\,dm\lesssim i\,m(2^{i}Q\cap\operatorname{supp}g)\leq i\,\ell(Q)^{N}\,\ell(2^{i}Q)^{2s}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{22}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}}{\ell(2^{i}Q)^{1+2s}}\,\Big(\ell(2^{i}Q)^{\frac{N}{q}+2s}\,\ell(Q)^{\frac{N}{q^{\prime}}}+i\,\ell(Q)^{N}\,\ell(2^{i}Q)^{2s}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}\ell(Q)^{N+2s}}{\ell(Q)^{1+2s}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\,\left(2^{i(\frac{N}{q}-1)}+\frac{i}{2^{i}}\right).

Choosing
q>N
, we get

I_{22}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{N+2s-1}.

We still have to show claims (
8.7
) and (
8.8
). Notice that (by taking Fourier transform with respect to
t
),

\partial_{t}{\varphi}=(\partial_{t}^{1/2})^{2M}\partial_{t}{\varphi}*_{t}\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M}}*_{t}K=g*_{t}K,

This identity can be understood better if we distinguish two cases.

(1)

Case
M
even. We can write

\partial_{t}{\varphi}=\partial^{M+1}_{t}{\varphi}*_{t}\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M}}*_{t}K.

Set
\displaystyle g=\partial^{M+1}_{t}{\varphi}*_{t}\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M}}
. Then
\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\varphi}=g*K
. The zero mean of
\partial_{t}{\varphi}
(integrating with respect to
t
) implies that
|g(x,t)|
decays at most like
\displaystyle|t|^{-1-\frac{1}{2s}}
at infinity. To see this notice that for
t\notin 2I_{Q}
we have

\displaystyle|g(x,t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\partial_{u}^{M+1}{\varphi}*\frac{1}{|u|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M}}\right)(x,t)\right|=\left|\int_{I_{Q}}\frac{\partial_{u}{\varphi}(x,u)}{|t-u|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}du\right|

(8.10)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{I_{Q}}\partial_{u}{\varphi}(x,u)\left(\frac{1}{|t-u|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}-\frac{1}{|t-c_{Q}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\right)du\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ell(I_{Q})}{|t-c_{Q}|^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}}\int_{I_{Q}}|\partial_{u}{\varphi}(x,u)|du\lesssim\frac{\ell(I_{Q})}{|t-c_{Q}|^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}}=\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}}{|t-c_{Q}|^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}}.

And for
t\in 4I_{Q}
,

|g(x,t)|\lesssim\|\partial^{M+1}_{u}{\varphi}\|_{\infty}\ell(I_{Q})^{1-(\frac{1}{2s}-M)}\lesssim\ell(I_{Q})^{-\frac{1}{2s}}=\ell(Q)^{-1}.

(2)

Case
M
odd. We can write

\partial_{t}{\varphi}=\partial_{t}^{\frac{1}{2s}-M}\partial_{t}^{M}{\varphi}*K=g*K,

the last equality being a definition for
g
. Notice that for
t\notin 2I_{Q}
,

\displaystyle|g(x,t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\partial_{u}^{M}\varphi(x,u)-\partial_{u}^{M}\varphi(x,t)}{|u-t|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M+1}}du\right|=\left|\int_{I_{Q}}\frac{\partial_{u}^{M}\varphi(x,u)}{|u-t|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M+1}}du\right|\approx\left|\int_{I_{Q}}\frac{\partial_{u}\varphi(x,u)}{|u-t|^{\frac{1}{2s}}}du\right|.

So arguing as in (
1
),

|g(x,t)|\lesssim\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s}}{|t-c_{Q}|^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}}.

And for
t\in 4I_{Q}
,

\displaystyle|g(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{5I_{Q}}\frac{|\partial_{u}^{M}\varphi(x,u)-\partial_{u}^{M}\varphi(x,t)|}{|u-t|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M+1}}du+\|\partial_{t}^{M}\varphi\|_{\infty}\int_{(5I_{Q})^{c}}\frac{du}{|u-s|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M+1}}=T_{1}+T_{2}.

To estimate
T_{1}
, apply the mean value theorem and (
8.6
) to get

T_{1}\leq\frac{1}{\ell(I_{Q})^{M+1}}\int_{5I_{Q}}\frac{du}{|u-t|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M}}\lesssim\ell(I_{Q})^{-\frac{1}{2s}}=\ell(Q)^{-1}.

Integrating and using (
8.6
) we get

T_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\ell(I_{Q})^{M}}\int_{(5I_{Q})^{c}}\frac{du}{|u-t|^{\frac{1}{2s}-M+1}}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-\frac{1}{2s}}=\ell(Q)^{-1}.

Therefore for
t\in 4I_{Q}
,

|g(x,t)|\lesssim\ell(Q)^{-1}.

Therefore the claims (
8.7
) and (
8.8
) are proved and also the lemma.
∎

Now we can state a result analogous to the first statement of theorem
7.1
but for
s\in(0,1/2)
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1/2)
. For every Borel set

\gamma_{\Theta,+}^{s}(E)\leq\gamma^{s}_{\Theta}(E)\lesssim{\mathcal{H}}^{N+2s-1}_{\infty}(E).

The proof is analogous to the one of theorem
7.1
but using lemma
8.1

7.4

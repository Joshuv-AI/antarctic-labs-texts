Modelling the dependence between a Wiener process and its running maxima and running minima processes
Piotr Jaworski, Karol Da̧browski

Abstract
We study a triple of stochastic processes: a Wiener process W t W_{t} , t ≥ 0 t\geq 0 , its running maxima process M t = sup { W s : s ∈ [ 0 , t ] } M_{t}=\sup\{W_{s}:s\in[0,t]\} , and its running minima process m t = inf { W s : s ∈ [ 0 , t ] } m_{t}=\inf\{W_{s}:s\in[0,t]\} . We derive the analytical formula for the corresponding copula and show that it is supported on the hemicube, a convex hexahedron with seven vertices. As an application, we draw out an analytical formula for pricing of a double barrier option.



1
Introduction

The Wiener process is one of the most ubiquitous stochastic processes
used to model complex phenomena. One of such areas is
financial mathematics, where this process is the foundation for all
models of stock, derivatives, or portfolio analysis. But with
the development of the market, more complex derivatives have shown up,
and a simple approach to the Wiener process was no longer enough. In particular, there was a
question as to what is the distribution of the Wiener process adding
boundaries on trajectories. It is especially important when one is dealing with
barrier options.

The structure of article is as follows.
In the first part, there will be provided the main results. We derive the
cumulative distribution function and copula for a triple consisting of a Wiener process
and its supremum and infimum.
The
cumulative distribution function is supported on a cone and the copula on a hemicube.
Then, we present special cases of this distribution, particularly focusing on marginals.
The next part is devoted to proofs of stated theorems.
The starting point is the
strong Markov property of Wiener processes. First, we study the simple case based on one stopping time. Finally, to the obtain desired results, we move to double stopping time. We provide a recursive dependency, which will lead to the characterization of the cumulative distribution the function and copula in terms of series.

The last parts show applications in financial mathematics, i.e., we calculate the price of a European double-barrier option in the Black–Scholes model.
Compare
[
2
,
3
,
1
]
. Our approach is based on the Girsanov theorem, which implies the existence a probabilistic measure
Q
under which the increment of the logarithm of a stock price process divided by the volatility follows the Wiener law. The double change of measure, from the risk neutral to
Q
and back after some integral calculations, significantly simplifies the proof.

3
Proofs and Auxiliary Results

3.1
Strong Markov Property

The strong Markov property plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
We recall the definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose
that
X=(X_{t}:t\geq 0)
is a stochastic process on a probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P})

with natural filtration
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}_{{t\geq 0}}
. Then, for any stopping time
\tau
on
\Omega
, we can define

{{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}=\{A\in{\mathcal{F}}:\forall t\geq 0,\{\tau\leq t\}\cap A\in{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}}.

Then,
X
is said to have the strong Markov property if, for each finite stopping time
\tau
,
we have that for each
t\geq 0
,
X_{{\tau+t}}-X_{\tau}
is independent of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}
.

It is well known that the Wiener process fulfills the above definition; see, for example,
[
13
]
(Section 37,
Theorem 37.5),
[
14
]
(Section 3.2), or
[
15
]
(Theorem 6.5). Furthermore, for a Wiener process
W_{t},t\geq 0
, we have

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\tau
be a finite stopping time and put

W^{\ast}_{t}(\omega)=W_{\tau+t}(\omega)-W_{\tau}(\omega),\;\;\;t\geq 0,\;\;\omega\in\Omega.

(3.1)

Then,
W^{*}_{t}:t\geq 0
is a Brownian motion, and it is independent of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}
.

Based on the fact that
W^{\ast}_{t}
is symmetric

\forall t>0\;\;\;W^{\ast}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}-W^{\ast}_{t}

we obtain the following version of the reflection principle.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\tau
be a finite stopping time and let a set
A
be an element of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}
of positive probability on which
\tau\leq t
,

A\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau},\;\;\mathbb{P}(A)>0,\;\;A\subset\{\omega:\tau(\omega)\leq t\},

such that under the condition
A
, the probability laws of
W_{t}
and
2W_{\tau}-W_{t}
coincide:

W_{t}\,|\,A\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}2W_{\tau}-W_{t}\,|\,A.

(3.2)

Proof.

We
observe that under the condition
\tau\leq t
,

\displaystyle W_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{t}-W_{\tau}+W_{\tau}=W^{\ast}_{t-\tau}+W_{\tau}=W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}+W_{\tau},

(3.3)

\displaystyle 2W_{\tau}-W_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(W_{t}-W_{\tau})+W_{\tau}=-W^{\ast}_{t-\tau}+W_{\tau}=-W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}+W_{\tau}.

(3.4)

Next, we compare the conditional distribution functions of the right sides of the above equations.
We apply the fact that the random variables
\tau
and
W_{\tau}
are
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
measurable and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}\leq x-W_{\tau}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{a.s.}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{W_{\tau}\leq x}&\mbox{if }\tau=t\\
\Phi\left(\frac{x-W_{\tau}}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}\right)&\mbox{other-ways }\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{W_{\tau}\leq x}&\mbox{if }\tau=t\\
1-\Phi\left(\frac{W_{\tau}-x}{\sqrt{|t-\tau|}}\right)&\mbox{other ways }\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{a.s.}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(-W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}\leq x-W_{\tau}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau})

where
\Phi
denotes the cumulative distribution function of a standard normal random variable (
N(0,1)
).

To conclude the proof, we apply the second point of the definition of the conditional expected value (see
[
13
]
§34).

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W^{\ast}_{t-\tau}+W_{\tau}\leq x\,|\,A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}+W_{\tau}\leq x}\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{A}\right)}{\mathbb{P}(A)}

(3.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)}\int_{A}\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}+W_{\tau}\leq x}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)}\int_{A}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}+W_{\tau}\leq x}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right)d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)}\int_{A}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{-W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}\leq x-W_{\tau}}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right)d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(A)}\int_{A}\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{-W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}\leq x-W_{\tau}}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{-W^{\ast}_{|t-\tau|}+W_{\tau}\leq x}\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{A}\right)}{\mathbb{P}(A)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(-W^{\ast}_{t-\tau}+W_{\tau}\leq x\,|\,A).

∎

3.2
Marginal Distributions

We consider two families of stopping times. One corresponds to an upper threshold, and the second corresponds to a lower one. In more details,
for
y>0
and
z<0
, we put

\displaystyle T_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{t>0:W_{t}=y\},

(3.12)

\displaystyle T_{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{t>0:W_{t}=z\}.

(3.13)

Note that for fixed positive
t
, the event
T_{y}\leq t
is equivalent to the event
M_{t}\geq y
:

\{\omega:T_{y}(\omega)\leq t\}=\{\omega:M_{t}(\omega)\geq y\}

(3.14)

and the event
T_{z}\leq t
is equivalent to the event
m_{t}\leq z
:

\{\omega:T_{z}(\omega)\leq t\}=\{\omega:m_{t}(\omega)\leq z\}.

(3.15)

We apply the reflection principle for
\tau=T_{y}
, where
y>0
.
Since

W_{T_{y}}=y,

the reflection principle simplifies to

W_{t}\;|T_{y}\leq t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}2y-W_{t}|T_{y}\leq t.

(3.16)

For
y,t>0
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,M_{t}\geq y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,T_{y}\leq t)

(3.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x\;|T_{y}\leq t)\mathbb{P}(T_{y}\leq t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(2y-W_{t}\leq x\;|T_{y}\leq t)\mathbb{P}(T_{y}\leq t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,M_{t}\geq y).

The further assumption
y\geq x
implies that the event
M_{t}\geq y
contains the event
W_{t}\geq 2y-x

\{\omega:W_{t}(\omega)\geq 2y-x\}\subset\{\omega:M_{t}(\omega)\geq y\}.

Indeed, for every
\omega
from the first set

M_{t}(\omega)\geq W_{t}(\omega)\geq 2y-x\geq y.

Therefore, for
y\geq x
and
y,t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,M_{t}\geq y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,M_{t}\geq y)

(3.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\geq 2y-x)=1-\Phi\left(\frac{2y-x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)=\Phi\left(\frac{x-2y}{\sqrt{t}}\right),

where
\Phi
denotes the cumulative distribution function of a standard normal random variable (
N(0,1)
).

Similarly, for
t>0
,
z<0
, and
z\leq x
, putting
\tau=T_{z}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\geq x,m_{t}\leq z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq 2z-x,m_{t}\leq z)

(3.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq 2z-x)=\Phi\left(\frac{2z-x}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

The above, together with inequalities

m_{t}\leq W_{t}\leq M_{t},\;\;m_{t}\leq 0\leq M_{t},

allows us to determine the joint distribution functions for the pairs
(W_{t},M_{t})
and
(W_{t},m_{t})
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The joint cumulative distribution function
F_{W,M,t}(x,y)
of
(W_{t},M_{t})
, where
t>0
, is of the form

F_{W,M,t}(x,y)=F_{W,M}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right),\;\;\;t>0,

where

F_{W,M}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\mbox{for }&y<0,\\
2\Phi(y)-1&\mbox{for }&0\leq y<x,\\
\Phi(x)-\Phi(x-2y)&\mbox{for }&x^{+}\leq y.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore,

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\leq y)=\max\left(0,2\Phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-1\right).

Proof.

Case
y<0
.

Since
M_{t}\geq 0
,

\forall t>0\;\;\;\mathbb{P}(M_{t}<0)=0.

Case
y\geq\max(0,x)
.

We apply Formula (
3.18
).

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,M_{t}<y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x)-\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,M_{t}\geq y)

(3.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{x-2y}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

Due to the continuity of
\Phi
, we obtain

F_{WM}(x,y)=\Phi(x)-\Phi(x-2y).

Case
x=y
.

The event
\{\omega:M_{t}(\omega)\leq y\}
is contained in the event
\{\omega:W_{t}(\omega)\leq y\}
. Indeed, for
\omega
belonging to the first set, we have

W_{t}(\omega)\leq M_{t}(\omega)\leq y.

Hence, from the previous step, we obtain

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\leq y)=\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq y,M_{t}\leq y)=\Phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{y-2y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)=2\Phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-1.

Due to the continuity of
\Phi
, we obtain

F_{WM}(x,y)=\Phi(x)-\Phi(x-2y).

Case
0\leq y<x
.

We apply the previous case and the inequality

\forall t>0\;\;\;W_{t}\leq M_{t}.

This leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,M_{t}\leq y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq y,M_{t}\leq y)

(3.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{y-2y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)=2\Phi\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-1.

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

The joint cumulative distribution function
F_{W,m,t}(x,z)
of
(W_{t},m_{t})
, where
t>0
, is of the form

F_{W,m,t}(x,z)=F_{W,m}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},\frac{z}{\sqrt{t}}\right),\;\;\;t>0,

where

F_{W,m}(x,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}2\Phi(z)-\Phi(2z-x)&\mbox{for }&z\leq min(0,x),\\
\Phi(x)&\mbox{for }&z>\min(0,x).\end{array}\right.

Furthermore,

\mathbb{P}(m_{t}\leq z)=\min\left(1,2\Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right).

Proof.

Case
x=z
.
We apply Formula (
3.19
).

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq z,m_{t}\leq z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(m_{t}\leq z)-\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\geq z,m_{t}\leq z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(m_{t}\leq z)-\Phi\left(\frac{2z-z}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

Since the event
\{W_{t}\leq z\}
is contained in the event
\{m_{t}\leq z\}
,

\mathbb{P}(m_{t}\leq z)=\Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{t}}\right)+\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq z,m_{t}\leq z)=2\Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

Case
z\leq min(0,x)
.

We apply Formula (
3.19
).

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,m_{t}\leq z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(m_{t}\leq z)-\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\geq x,m_{t}\leq z)

(3.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{2z-x}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

Case
z>x
.

The event
\{W_{t}\leq x\}
is contained in the event
\{m_{t}\leq z\}
.
Indeed, for every
\omega
from the first set,

m_{t}(\omega)\leq W_{t}(\omega)\leq x\leq z.

Thus,

\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,m_{t}\leq z)=\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x)=\Phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

Case
z>0
.

Since
m_{t}\leq 0
,

\{\omega:m_{t}(\omega)\leq z\}=\Omega.

Thus,

\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,m_{t}\leq z)=\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x)=\Phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

∎

Note that, since the distributions of running maxima and minima are closely related with stopping times

\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\leq y)=1-\mathbb{P}(T_{y}\leq t),

\mathbb{P}(m_{t}\leq z)=\mathbb{P}(T_{z}\leq t),

they might be drawn from the formulas describing exit times (from the half-line) provided, for example, in
[

]
(Theorem 4.4.5).

3.3
The Two-Barrier Stopping Time

In this section, we apply the reflection principle for the two-barrier stopping time

\tau=\min(T_{y},T_{z}),\;\;z<0<y,

and for the set
A
, which are equal, respectively, to

\{\omega:W_{\tau}(\omega)=y,\tau(\omega)\leq t\}\;\;\mbox{or}\;\;\{\omega:W_{\tau}(\omega)=z,\tau(\omega)\leq t\}.

Note that the range of
W_{\tau}
consists only of two points
y
and
z
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
any
x\leq y

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{x+2(k-1)z-2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{x+2kz-2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right).

Proof.

Applying twice the reflection principle, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(2W_{\tau}-W_{t}\leq x,\;\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)

(3.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(2y-W_{t}\leq x,\;\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,\;\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,\;\;W_{\tau}=z,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(2W_{\tau}-W_{t}\geq 2y-x,\;\;W_{\tau}=z,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq 2z-2y+x,\;\;W_{\tau}=z,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq 2z-2y+x,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle+\,\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq 2z-2y+x,\;\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\;\tau\leq t\right).

Since
x\leq y
, the event
W_{t}\geq 2y-x
is contained in the event
\tau\leq t
, and

W_{t}(\omega)\geq 2y-x\geq y\Longrightarrow M_{t}(\omega)\geq y\Longrightarrow T_{y}(\omega)\leq t\Longrightarrow\tau(\omega)=\min(T_{y}(\omega),T_{z}(\omega))\leq t.

Therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq 2y-x,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq 2y-x\right)=\Phi_{t}(x-2y),

where
\Phi_{t}
denotes the distribution function of an
N(0,t)
random variable:

\Phi_{t}(x)=\Phi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

Similarly, since
z<0<y
and
x\leq y
, the event
W_{t}\leq 2z-2y+x
is contained in the event
\tau\leq t
, so

W_{t}(\omega)\leq 2z-2y+x\leq z\Longrightarrow m_{t}(\omega)\leq z\Longrightarrow T_{z}(\omega)\leq t\Longrightarrow\tau(\omega)=\min(T_{y}(\omega),T_{z}(\omega))\leq t.

Therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq 2z-2y+x,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq 2z-2y+x\right)=\Phi_{t}(x+2z-2y).

In such a way, we obtain the recurrence for
x\leq y
:

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq 2z-2y+x,\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)+\Phi_{t}(x-2y)-\Phi_{t}(x+2z-2y).

(3.24)

Since
2z-2y<0
, we may repeat it. After
n
repetitions, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq n(2z-2y)+x,\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)

(3.25)

\displaystyle+\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\Phi_{t}(x+2(k-1)z-2ky)-\Phi_{t}(x+2kz-2ky)\right).

Since

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq n(2z-2y)+x,\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq n(2z-2y)+x\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\rightarrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

and passing to the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(x+2(k-1)z-2ky)-\Phi_{t}(x+2kz-2ky)\right).

(3.26)

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
x\geq z

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq x,\;\;W_{\tau}=z,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{x-2kz+2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{x-2kz+2(k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right).

Proof.

We base our proof on the symmetry of the Wiener process
W_{t}
.
We put
\tilde{W}_{t}=-W_{t}
. Then, we have, for
z<0<y
,

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\tilde{W}_{s}=-\inf_{0\leq s\leq t}W_{s}=-m_{t},

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{0\leq s\leq t}\tilde{W}_{s}=-\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}W_{s}=-M_{t},

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{-z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{t>0:\tilde{W}_{t}=-z\}=T_{z},

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{-y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{t>0:\tilde{W}_{t}=-y\}=T_{y},

\displaystyle\tilde{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min(\tilde{T}_{-z},\tilde{T}_{-y})=\min(T_{z},T_{y})=\tau.

Therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq x,\;\;W_{\tau}=z,\;\;\tau\leq t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{W}_{t}\leq-x,\;\;\tilde{W}_{\tilde{\tau}}=-z,\;\;\tilde{\tau}\leq t\right).

Since
\tilde{W}_{t}
is as well a Wiener process, we apply Lemma
3.5
and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{W}_{t}\leq-x,\;\;\tilde{W}_{\tilde{\tau}}=-z,\;\;\tilde{\tau}\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{-x+2kz-2(k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{-x+2kz-2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{x-2kz+2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{x-2kz+2(k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right).\;\;\;\;

∎

Together, the above lemmas give us

Corollary 3.7
.

For

z<0<y
and
z\leq x\leq y
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right)

(3.27)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{x+2(k-1)z-2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{x+2kz-2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right)+\Phi(x)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{(2k-1)z-2(k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{(2k-1)z-2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right).

(3.28)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{2(k-1)z-(2k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{2kz-(2k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right)

(3.29)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{(2k-1)z-2(k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{(2k-1)z-2ky}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right)

(3.30)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{2(k-1)z-(2k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{2kz-(2k-1)y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right).

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\geq x,\;W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right).

Thus, applying Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq x,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right)

(3.31)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(x+2(k-1)z-2ky)-\Phi_{t}(x+2kz-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(x-2kz+2(k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}(x-2kz+2ky)\right).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right)+\Phi(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(x+2(k-1)z-2ky)-\Phi_{t}(x+2kz-2ky)\right)

Since the event
W_{t}\leq z
implies the event
\tau\leq t
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq z,\;\tau\leq t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq z\right)=\Phi_{t}(z).

Therefore, substituting
x=z
in the above formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{t}\leq z,\;\tau\leq t\right)-\Phi(z)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(z+2(k-1)z-2ky)-\Phi_{t}(z+2kz-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(z)-2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2ky)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2(k-1)y)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2(k-1)y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2(k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2ky)\right).

This concludes the proof of the first two formulas of the corollary.
The third one follows from the symmetry of the Wiener process. We apply the notation from the proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Since
\tilde{W}_{t}=-W_{t}
is a Wiener process,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{W}_{\tilde{\tau}}=-y,\;\tilde{\tau}\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}((2k-1)(-y)-2(k-1)(-z))-\Phi_{t}((2k-1)(-y)-2k(-z))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(2(k-1)z-(2k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}(2kz-(2k-1)y)\right).

Since

\mathbb{P}\left(\tau\leq t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=y,\;\tau\leq t\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(W_{\tau}=z,\;\tau\leq t\right),

the last equality of the corollary is a consequence of the two previous ones.
∎

Note that, since the Formula (

) is describing an exit time from a segment, it might be obtained

]
Theorem 4.4.5).

3.4
Joint Cumulative Distribution

Theorem 3.8
.

The joint cumulative distribution function
F_{t}(x,y,z)
of
(W_{t},M_{t},m_{t})
, where
t>0
, is of the form

F_{t}(x,y,z)=F\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}},\frac{z}{\sqrt{t}}\right),\;\;\;t>0,

where

F(x,y,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mbox{for }y\leq 0,\\
\Phi(x)-\Phi(x-2y)&\mbox{for }z\geq 0,\;0<y,\;x<y,\\
2\Phi(y)-1&\mbox{for }z\geq 0,\;0<y\leq x,\\
\Phi(x)-\Phi(x-2y)&\mbox{for }x<z<0<y,\\
2\Psi(z,2y,2(y-z))-\Psi(x+2z-2y,2y,2(y-z))&\\
-\Psi(-x+2z,2y,2(y-z))&\mbox{for }z<0<y,\;z\leq x\leq y,\\
2\Psi(z,2y,2(y-z))-2\Psi(2z-y,2y,2(y-z))&\mbox{for }z<0<y<x,\end{array}\right.

(3.32)

where

\Psi(q,r,s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\Phi(q-ks)-\Phi(q-r-ks))

(3.33)

and
\Phi
denotes the distribution function of the standard normal probability law N(0, 1).

Note that the distribution of
(W_{t},M_{t},m_{t})
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure (Section
3.5
) and is supported on the set

\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\;z\leq 0\leq y,\;z\leq x\leq y\}.

Furthermore,
the marginal distributions of
F
are given by the following formulas:

\displaystyle F(x,+\infty,+\infty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{1}\leq x)=\Phi(x),

(3.34)

\displaystyle F(+\infty,y,+\infty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{1}\leq y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mbox{if }\;y\leq 0,\\
2\Phi(y)-1&\mbox{if }\;y>0,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle F(+\infty,+\infty,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(m_{1}\leq z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2\Phi(z)&\mbox{if }\;z<0,\\
1&\mbox{if }\;z\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle F(x,y,+\infty)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{1}\leq x,M_{1}\leq y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mbox{if }\;y\leq 0,\\
2\Phi(y)-1&\mbox{if }\;0<y\leq x,\\
\Phi(x)-\Phi(x-2y)&\mbox{if }\;\max(0,x)<y,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle F(x,+\infty,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{1}\leq x,m_{1}\leq z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2\Phi(z)-\Phi(2z-x)&\mbox{if }\;z\leq\min(0,x),\\
\Phi(x)&\mbox{if }\;x<z\leq 0\vee 0<z,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle F(+\infty,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{1}\leq y,m_{1}\leq z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mbox{if }\;y\leq 0,\\
2\Psi(z,2y,2(y-z))&\\
-2\Psi(2z-y,2y,2(y-z))&\mbox{if }\;z\leq 0<y,\\
2\Phi(y)-1&\mbox{if }\;0<z,0<y.\end{array}\right.

We prove Theorem
3.8
case by case.

Proof.

Case

y\leq 0
.

Since
W_{t}
is almost surely continuous, and
W_{0}=0
,
M_{t}
is almost surely positive:

F_{t}(x,y,z)=\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\leq y,\;m_{t}\leq z)\leq\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\leq y)=0.

Case
z\geq 0
and
y>0
.

Since
m_{t}
is almost surely negative,

F_{t}(x,y,z)=\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\leq y,\;m_{t}\leq z)=\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\leq y).

Following Theorem
3.3
, we obtain

F_{t}(x,y,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}2\Phi_{t}(y)-1&\mbox{for }&y<x,\\
\Phi_{t}(x)-\Phi_{t}(x-2y)&\mbox{for }&x\leq y.\end{array}\right.

Case
z<0<y
and
x\leq z
.

Since the event
W_{t}\leq x\leq z
implies the event
m_{t}\leq z
,

F_{t}(x,y,z)=\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\leq y,\;m_{t}\leq z)=\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\leq y).

As above, following Theorem
3.3
, we obtain

F_{t}(x,y,z)=\Phi_{t}(x)-\Phi_{t}(x-2y).

Case
z<0<y
and
z<x\leq y
.

We observe that

\displaystyle F_{t}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\leq y,\;m_{t}\leq z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;(M_{t}\geq y\vee m_{t}\leq z))

\displaystyle-\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\geq y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;\tau\leq t)

\displaystyle-\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x)+\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\leq y)

where
\tau
is the two-barrier stopping time discussed in Section
3.3
.
Following Corollary
3.7
and Theorem
3.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{t}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2ky)-\Phi_{t}((2k+1)z-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(x+2(k-1)z-2ky)-\Phi_{t}(x+2kz-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(-x+2kz-2(k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}(-x+2kz-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle-\Phi_{t}(x)+\Phi_{t}(x)-\Phi_{t}(x-2y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2ky)-\Phi_{t}((2k+1)z-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(x+2kz-2(k+1)y)-\Phi_{t}(x+2kz-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(-x+2kz-2(k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}(-x+2kz-2ky)\right).

Case
z<0<y
and
y\leq x
.

Since
W_{t}\leq M_{t}
,

\displaystyle F_{t}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,\;M_{t}\leq y,\;m_{t}\leq z)=\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\leq y,\;m_{t}\leq z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\geq y\vee m_{t}\leq z)+\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\leq y)-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau\leq t)+\mathbb{P}(M_{t}\leq y)-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2(k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(2(k-1)z-(2k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}(2kz-(2k-1)y)\right)

\displaystyle-2\Phi(-y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2(k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}((2k-1)z-2ky)\right)

\displaystyle-2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(2kz-(2k-1)y)-\Phi_{t}(2kz-(2k+1)y)\right)

∎

As a corollary of Theorem
3.8
, we prove the 80-year-old characterization of the Wiener process called the “Lévy Triple Law” (
[
15
]
Theorem 6.18), which deals with the trajectories lying between the upper barrier
u
and lower barrier
l
.

Corollary 3.9
.

For
l<0<u
,
l\leq x\leq u
, and
t>0
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,M_{t}\leq u,m_{t}\geq l)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(\varphi_{t}(x+2k(u-l))-\varphi_{t}(x-2l-2k(u-l)\right),

(3.53)

where
\varphi_{t}
denotes the density of
N(0,t)
.

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,M_{t}\leq u,m_{t}\geq l)=F_{t}(x,u,0)-F_{t}(x,u,l)

(3.54)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;=\Phi_{t}(x)-\Phi_{t}(x-2u)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;-\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(l-2k(u-l))-\Phi_{t}(l-2u-2k(u-l))\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(x+2l-2u-2k(u-l))-\Phi_{t}(x+2l-2u-2u-2k(u-l))\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\Phi_{t}(-x+2l-2k(u-l))-\Phi_{t}(-x+2l-2u-2k(u-l))\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbb{P}(W_{t}\leq x,M_{t}\leq u,m_{t}\geq l)

(3.55)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;=\varphi_{t}(x)-\varphi_{t}(x-2u)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\varphi_{t}(x+2l-2u-2k(u-l))-\varphi_{t}(x+2l-2u-2u-2k(u-l))\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;-\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\varphi_{t}(-x+2l-2k(u-l))-\varphi_{t}(-x+2l-2u-2k(u-l))\right).

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;=\varphi_{t}(x)-\varphi_{t}(x-2u)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\varphi_{t}(x-2(k+1)(u-l))-\varphi_{t}(x-2u-2(k+1)(u-l))\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;-\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\varphi_{t}(x-2u+2(k+1)(u-l))-\varphi_{t}(x+2(k+1)(u-l))\right).

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi_{t}(x-2k(u-l))-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi_{t}(x-2u-2k(u-l))

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi_{t}(x-2k(u-l))-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi_{t}(x-2l-2(k+1)(u-l)).

After shifting by one the parameter
k
in the second sum, we obtain the required
Formula (
3.53
)
.

∎

3.5
Properties of the Cumulative Distribution Function
F
and the Marginal Distributions

We start with the absolute continuity of the marginal distributions of random pairs
(W_{1},M_{1})
and
(W_{1},m_{1})
:

F_{W,M}(x,y)=F(x,y,\infty)\;\;\mbox{and }\;\;F_{W,m}(x,z)=F(x,\infty,z).

Proposition 3.10
.

The bivariate cumulative distributions
F_{W,M}
and
F_{W,m}
are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Proof.

We start with the function
F_{W,m}(x,z)
.
We show that a function

g(\xi,\eta)=-2(2\eta-\xi)\varphi(2\eta-\xi)\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{\eta\leq 0}\mathbf{1}{\hskip-3.5pt}\hbox{l}_{\eta\leq\xi}

is a corresponding density. Let
I(x,z)
denote an integral of
g
over the set
(-\infty,x]\times(-\infty,z]
.

\displaystyle I(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{z}\int_{-\infty}^{x}g(\xi,\eta)d\eta d\xi

(3.56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\min(z,x,0)}\int_{\eta}^{x}(-2)(2\eta-\xi)\varphi(2\eta-\xi)d\eta d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\min(z,x,0)}2\bigg(\varphi(\eta)-\varphi(2\eta-x)\bigg)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Phi(\min(z,x,0))-\Phi(2\min(z,x,0)-x)

4
Application of Results: Double-Barrier Option Pricing

In this section, we present one of the possible applications of the derived results. We consider probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and the Black–Scholes–Merton market model
[

in which the asset price

dynamic follows the geometric Brownian motion, i.e.,

Additionally, the bank account

is described with the following differential equation:

dB_{t}=rB_{t}dt,~~B_{0}=1.

The solution to this system of equations is well known in the literature, and we present only a final result without proof. The reader can check that

To valuate in this model a derivative with a payoff
X

\mathcal{F}_{T}
measurable (

is the natural filtration of

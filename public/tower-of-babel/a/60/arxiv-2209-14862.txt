Spatial analyticity and exponential decay of Fourier modes for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equation We would sincerely like to thank the referees for the many useful suggestions and corrections in the making of this manuscript.
Dan Crisan, Prince Romeo Mensah*

Abstract
. We construct a local in time spatially real-analytic solution to the 2D and 3D stochastic Navier–Stokes equation driven by a spatially real-analytic multiplicative and transport noise but emanating from an initial condition that is only required to have bounded enstrophy. Under the condition that the solution is global in time, we also establish the exponential decay of the finite-dimensional Galerkin approximation, with respect to its maximum wavenumber, to the strong pathwise solution of the stochastic Navier–Stokes equation. This decay is uniform in time, uniform with respect to the initial enstropy, and uniform in the noise coefficients.

Primary 35R60, 35B65, 35B40; Secondary 35Q35, 76D03

1.
Introduction

The Navier–Stokes equation is a widely used model to describe the evolution of incompressible viscous fluids. It represents a balance law between the forces present in the fluid and the acceleration of the fluid. Classically, the main forces taken into account will consist of the deterministic conservative pressure, non-conservative viscous term, and perhaps, all other external deterministic forces lumped up together. However, to take uncertainties in the fluid into account, it is desirable to incorporate noise into the system. This noise may be understood as a random force that is a function of the underlining deterministic unknowns.
Another form of noise is the transport noise of Stratonovich-type, that has the desirable property of preserving some of the physical laws satisfied by the deterministic system.

In this work, we are interested in the regularity of solutions to the 2D and 3D stochastic Navier–Stokes equation for incompressible fluids. In particular, we explore a specific Gevrey class regularity that renders the solution spatially real-analytic. The analysis of Gevrey class regularity for fluid-dynamic systems has a long history spanning many decades. The fundamental work by Foias and Temam
[
14
]
, in particular, bridged the gap between the functional analysis of general differential equations and the study of regularity for solutions to fluid dynamic equations. Since the work done by Foias and Temam, the treatment of Gevrey class regularity for deterministic fluid dynamic systems has gained considerable attention. We cannot give an exhaustive review but some fundamental works include
[
1
,
6
,
11
,
15
,
21
,
22
,
30
]
.
Unfortunately, very few works exist on the Gevrey class regularity for stochastic systems. From an analytic point of view, the only work we have found is by Mattingly
[
25
]
who analysed the Navier–Stokes equation with a white-in-time stochastic forcing. An analytic solution is derived from analytic data and discussions on stationary measures and ergodicity are also presented. From the numerical analysis point of view, a few more results are present
[
20
,
24
]
. In
[
20
]
, the authors show the convergence of the Galerkin approximation to the strong solution of the 2D Navier–Stokes equation defined on a bounded domain with Dirichlet boundary condition and that is driven by a multiplicative noise. However, the rate of convergence is not given and the convergence holds for the expectation of a logarithmic function of the norm of the difference of the two solutions, rather than for the norm itself. In
[
24
]
, however, the authors give an inverse polynomial rate of convergence for general (viscous) stochastic PDEs driven by additive noise whose coefficients are analytic.

In this work, we analyze a generalized Navier–Stokes equation on a 2D and 3D torus that accounts for all the deterministic and random forces discussed above. We explore its Gevrey class regularity that results in the unknowns becoming spatially real-analytic. In particular, for data consisting of just a bounded initial enstrophy and spatially real-analytic forcing terms, we construct a local in time spatially real-analytic solution to the Navier–Stokes equation. This is a regularization result in the sense that we obtain an analytic solution from an initial condition that is far less regular as observed in many deterministic dissipative systems
[
3
,
6
,
11
,
14
,
30
]
. However, a caveat is that we need more regularity for the forced data of stochastic transport type than the anticipated regularity of the solution to be constructed. As such, although the solution becomes regularized with respect to its initial condition, the trade-off is that we need more regular transport noise coefficients, in particular we assume they are constant vectors in the time variable.
However, the assumptions are optimal for noise with linear growth and Lipschitz coefficients (like additive and multiplicative noise) where we only require the same regularity for the noise coefficient as that of the anticipated solution. To our knowledge, this regularity result is a first for the stochastic Navier–Stokes equation.

Our second main result involves the analysis of the rate of decay of the finite-dimensional solution used in the approximation of the constructed solution. Here, we assume that the constructed solution is global in time. We
measure the decay rate in the space of enstrophy functionals and show that the rate of decay is exponential as a function of its Fourier modes. We refer to Theorem
3.13
for the exact statement. As a consequence, in particular, we can infer that irrespective of the addition of noise, each Fourier coefficient of our solution decays faster than exponentially (see the first estimate in (
6.3
)), uniformly in time, uniform with respect to the initial enstrophy and uniformly in the noise coefficients, with the same rate as we will expect of the corresponding deterministic system
[
9
,
15
]
. Also, see
[
10
]
for a related result for the generalized Ginzburg-Landau equation. Subsequently, the difference between the continuum and discrete velocities in the spatial
H^{1}
-norm decays exactly exponentially fast to zero.

Plan

In order to improve readability, we begin with some preliminaries covered in Section

.

Here, the

Remark 2.1

Note the

With (

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})=\bigg\{\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\in H^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\,\big|\,\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\mathbf{k}=0\bigg\}.

(2.2)

Remark 2.2
.

The condition
\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\mathbf{k}=0
corresponds to the divergence-free condition
\,\mathrm{div}\mathbf{f}=0
in frequency space.

The space

is the domain of the positive, self-adjoint Stokes operator

\displaystyle A=P(-\Delta),\qquad A:D(A)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})=:\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d});

is compactly embedded in

and where

It is well-known that

31

in

.
By using the properties

Next, by using the definition of the Stokes operator

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\qquad\mapsto\qquad A^{r}\mathbf{f}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathbf{k}|^{2r}\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}\langle\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}),e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\rangle_{L^{2}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle\|P^{N}\mathbf{f}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq N^{2(s-r)}\|P^{N}\mathbf{f}\|_{H^{r}}^{2},\qquad

3.
The main results

With our preparation in hand, we can now present the system under study. Here, we are interested in a velocity field
\mathbf{u}:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a pressure
p:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

that satisfy

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}+[(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}]\,\mathrm{d}t+\,\mathrm{d}\nabla p=\sum_{k\geq 1}\big[\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u})-(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\circ\big]\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\mathbf{u}=0,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\mathbf{x}).

Here,
\nu>0
is the viscosity coefficient and the symbol
\circ
means that the stochastic integral is understood in the Stratonovich sense. Details on the body forces
(\mathbf{g}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and the coefficients of the noise advection
(\bm{\xi}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
will be given in the next section.

The system is a generalisation of the deterministic Navier–Stokes equation that takes into account several random phenomena in the fluids. In particular, this may cover additive noise, multiplicative noise and transport noise of Stratonovich type, and a first rigorous analysis can be found in
[
28
]
.

When
\bm{\xi}_{k}\equiv 0
, the resulting stochastic partial differential equation have been studied intensively by many authors including
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
13
]
.

On the other hand, when
\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u})\equiv 0
, the system (
3.1
) becomes a so-called SALT (Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport) model introduced by Holm
[
19
]
but adjusted for viscous effects as studied in
[
12
]
in vorticity form. The (inviscid) SALT models are derived from a stochastic variational principle and their solutions follow the flow of a stochastic vector field. The viscous SALT variant above are, however, closely related to the LU (Location Uncertainty) models introduced by Mémin
[
26
]
that relies on a decomposition of the velocity fields into a random non-differentiable part and a differentiable deterministic component. In the specific case of the Stochastic Navier–Stokes under LU, one considers a random vector fields
\bm{\sigma}:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and define an elliptic
variance tensor

\mathbb{A}=(A_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
as
\mathbb{A}(\mathbf{x},t)\delta(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}t=\mathbb{E}[(\bm{\sigma}(\mathbf{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}_{t})(\bm{\sigma}(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}_{s})^{T}]
where
\mathbf{W}_{t}=(W_{t}^{k})_{k\geq 1}
. The system of equation is then given by

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-\frac{1}{2}(\,\mathrm{div}\mathbb{A}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{A}\nabla\mathbf{u})=\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}-\nabla p,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mathrm{d}\hat{p}_{t}=\nu\Delta(\bm{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}_{t})-(\bm{\sigma}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}_{t},

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\mathbf{u}=0,\qquad\,\mathrm{div}(\bm{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}_{t})=0,\qquad\,\mathrm{div}\,\mathrm{div}\mathbb{A}=0,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\mathbf{x})

where
p(\mathbf{x},t)
denotes the large-scale pressure contribution and
\hat{p}_{t}
is a zero-mean turbulent pressure related to the small-scale velocity component.

From the analytic point of view, (
3.3
) is strongly related to (
3.1
) (with
\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u})\equiv 0
) in the sense that both system preserves the deterministic energy since the noise terms and the variance tensor terms cancels out when deriving an identity for
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, assuming that
\mathbf{u}
is smooth enough.

Returning to (
3.1
), it is convenient to eliminate the pressure in (
3.1
) and only ask to find the velocity field. This is done by applying Leray’s projection
P=I-\nabla\Delta^{-1}\mathrm{div}
to the first equation in (
3.1
). Doing so and converting the Stratonovich integral to an Itô integral results in

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}+[P((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u})-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}]\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u})\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 1}P\big[\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u})-((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u})\big]\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k},

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\mathbf{u}=0,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(\mathbf{x}).

Note that by using the incompressibility condition
\,\mathrm{div}\mathbf{u}=0
, one can recover the pressure from (
3.1
)
1
by solving

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}p=(-\Delta)^{-1}\,\mathrm{div}((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u})\,\mathrm{d}t-\sum_{k\geq 1}(-\Delta)^{-1}\,\mathrm{div}\big[\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u})-(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\circ\big]\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k}.

Also note that if we consider the orthogonal complement
Q
of
P
, then for incompressible vector fields
\mathbf{u}
and
\bm{\xi}_{k}
, we have that

\displaystyle\big\langle P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u})\,,\,\mathbf{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\,,\,\mathbf{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\,,\,(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\,,\,P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\langle P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\,,\,Q(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\|P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This gives a useful cancellation property between the Stratonovich to Itô corrector and the quadratic variation term that appears when Itô’s formula is used to obtain an energy estimate from (
3.3
).

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim\|e^{\sigma_{1}A^{1/2s}}[\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}]\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{g}_{k}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=0\text{ for each }k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\qquad(Mean-free)

(3.6)

Remark 3.1
.

The assumptions imposed on the noise coefficients

cover but are not limited to the cases of additive and linear multiplicative noise.

With regards to the transport noise, we require that the sum of the coefficients

More precisely, we assume that for any

Finally, in order to treat the multiplication noise and the transport noise separately, we impose an orthogonality constraint between them. More precisely, we assume that

\displaystyle\big\langle A^{r}e^{\sigma_{1}A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{v})\,,\,A^{r}e^{\sigma_{1}A^{1/2s}}((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{w})\big\rangle_{L^{2}}=0

(3.8)

holds for any

\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in D(e^{\sigma_{1}A^{1/2s}}:\mathbb{H}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

3.2.
Concepts of solution

We now make precise the various notion of a solution that we shall refer to throughout this work.

Definition 3.2

(Local strong pathwise solution)
.

Let
\tau

Definition 3.3

(Maximal strong pathwise solution)

Remark 3.4

\displaystyle\bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\big\langle Ae^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N},\,A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\bigg|

Now use the Polarization Identity and (

\displaystyle\big\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}P^{N}P[\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})

. This yields

\displaystyle\bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}P^{N}P[(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}],\,A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

(5.10)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\bigg|\lesssim\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

By collecting the various estimates above, we obtain

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}(\sigma)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\nu\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\|\mathbf{u}^{N}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle+c(\nu)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\big(1+\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{6}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle+c(\nu)\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{\sigma}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N}),\,A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\bigg|

\displaystyle+c(\nu)\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{\sigma}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}),\,A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\bigg|.

It therefore follow that for every
\tilde{M}\in(0,M)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\bigg(\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}(\sigma)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\nu\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle>\|\mathbf{u}^{N}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+\tilde{M}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg(c(\nu)\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\big(1+\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{6}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma>\frac{\tilde{M}}{3}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\bigg(c(\nu)\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{\sigma}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N}),\,

\displaystyle A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\bigg|>\frac{\tilde{M}}{3}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\bigg(c(\nu)\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{\sigma}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}),\,

To estimate the first term on the right, we use Chebyshev’s inequality just as in

16
]

\displaystyle\leq c_{\nu,\tilde{M},M}t.

On the other hand, by Doob’s inequality and the assumption on the noise in Section
3.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\bigg(c(\nu)\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{\sigma}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N}),\,

(5.14)

\displaystyle A^{1/2}e^{sA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\bigg|>\frac{\tilde{M}}{3}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\nu,\tilde{M}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

Similarly, we obtain by using the first estimate of Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(c(\nu)\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq c_{\nu,\tilde{M},M}t.

We therefore conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{\sigma\in[0,{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}]}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}(\sigma)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle+\nu\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

(5.16)

\displaystyle>\|\mathbf{u}^{N}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\tilde{M}\bigg)\leq c_{\nu,\tilde{M},M}t

an thus, (
5.3
) immediately follows.

Our next goal is to show (
5.4
). For this, we take

with

.
We now let
\mathbf{u}^{N}
and

be the two Galerkin solutions of (

) with corresponding data

and

respectively. Set
\mathbf{u}^{Nn}=\mathbf{u}^{N}-\mathbf{u}^{n}
so that
\mathbf{u}^{Nn}
solves

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}

\displaystyle+[P^{N}P((\mathbf{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P((\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n})-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}^{Nn}]\,\mathrm{d}t

(5.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big[P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n})\big]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 1}[P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{n})]\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\geq 1}\big[P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n})\big]\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k},

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{Nn}_{0}=(P^{N}-P^{n})\mathbf{u}_{0}.

We have by the product rule,

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle(A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn})=Ae^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\,\mathrm{d}t

(5.18)

\displaystyle-A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}[P^{N}P(\mathbf{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}-P^{n}P(\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n}+\nu A\mathbf{u}^{Nn}]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\big[P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})

\displaystyle-P^{n}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n})\big]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 1}A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}[P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{n})]\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\geq 1}A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\big[P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n})\big]\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k}

and thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{u}^{Nn}_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

(5.19)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle Ae^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn},\,A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}[P^{N}P(\mathbf{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}

\displaystyle-P^{n}P(\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n}],\,A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\big[P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n})\big],\,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{g}_{k},\bm{\xi}_{k},\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{u}^{N},\mathbf{u}^{Nn})(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\|e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}[P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{n})]

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{g}_{k},\bm{\xi}_{k},\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{u}^{N},\mathbf{u}^{Nn})(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}P^{N}P[(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}],\,A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}P^{n}P[(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n}],\,A^{1/2}e^{\sigma A^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

For
\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}:=\mathcal{T}^{M}_{N}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{M}_{n}
,
we have that

\displaystyle\bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}\big\langle Ae^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn},\,A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{8}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+c(\nu)\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

In order to estimate the nonlinear convective term, we rewrite it as follows

\displaystyle\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}[P^{N}P(\mathbf{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}

\displaystyle-P^{n}P(\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n}],\,A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}[P^{N}P(\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}],\,A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle,

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}[P^{N}P(\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{Nn}],\,A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle,

, we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Ae^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{L^{2}}\|Ae^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{8}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+c(\nu)\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{4}.

(5.22)

Next,

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Ae^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{L^{2}}\|Ae^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{8}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+c(\nu)\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

as the projection onto the modes higher than

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle=\big|\big\langle e^{tA^{1/2}}Q^{n}P^{N}P(\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n},\,Ae^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle\big|

2.3
), the continuity of

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle Q^{n}P^{N}e^{tA^{1/2}}P(\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{n^{2}}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}P(\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

). Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle|I_{3}|\leq\frac{\nu}{8}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\frac{c(\nu)}{n}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

(5.26)

and as such,

\displaystyle\bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}\big\langle A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}[P^{N}P(\mathbf{u}^{N}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N}-P^{n}P(\mathbf{u}^{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n}],\,A^{1/2}e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|

(5.27)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\nu}{8}\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+c(\nu)\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}\frac{1}{n}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+c(\nu)\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big(\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{4}+\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Next, we use the Polarization Identity and (
3.8
) to obtain

\displaystyle\big\|e^{tA^{1/2}}

\displaystyle[P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{n})]

(5.28)

\displaystyle-e^{tA^{1/2}}\big[P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n})\big]\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

To estimate the first term on the right of (

), we use the triangle inequality again to obtain

\displaystyle\big\|e^{tA^{1/2}}[P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{n})]\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|P^{N}Pe^{tA^{1/2}}[\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})-\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{n})]\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\big\|Q^{n}P^{N}Pe^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{n})\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

. Thus, we obtain by using the assumptions on

in Section

, the second inequality in (

) and the continuity of

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\big\|e^{tA^{1/2}}[P^{N}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P\mathbf{g}_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{n})]\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|

(5.30)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}^{M}_{Nn}}\bigg(\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{Nn}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big(1+\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\mathbf{u}^{n}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t.

We now combine the treatment of the last term in (

) with the
\mathcal{C}_{2}
-term in (

). In particular, we note from (

) and (

) that

\displaystyle J_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle:=\big\|e^{tA^{1/2}}\big[P^{N}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N})-P^{n}P((\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{n})\big]\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

(5.31)

\displaystyle=\big\|e^{tA^{1/2}}Q^{n}P^{N}P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}^{N})\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\big\|e^{tA^{1/2}}P^{n}P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}^{Nn})\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\big\|e^{tA^{1/2}}P^{N}P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}^{N})\big\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\big\|e^{tA^{1/2}}P^{n}P(\bm{\xi}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}^{Nn})\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\|e^{\varphi(0)A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}-\|e^{\varphi(0)A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{u}^{N_{l+1}}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}<2^{-(l+2)}.

also holds on
\Omega_{p}
.
The two estimates (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) implies that
\{\tau_{l}<\tau_{l+1}\}\cap\Omega_{p}=\emptyset
which further implies that for every
l\geq p
and
\omega\in\Omega_{p}
, we have that

\displaystyle\tau_{l+1}(\omega)\leq\tau_{l}(\omega).

(7.10)

Thus, by combining (
7.4
) with the decreasing sequence (
7.10
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\tau_{l}\rightarrow\tau\qquad a.s.

with

\displaystyle\tau\leq T\qquad a.s.

following due to the definition of
\tau_{l}
. To show (
4.3
), it remains to show that
\tau>0
a.s. or equivalently that
\mathbb{P}(\tau=0)=0
. For this, we fix
\varepsilon>0
with
T>\varepsilon>0
so that

\displaystyle\liminf_{l\rightarrow\infty}\{\tau_{l}<\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle=\{\tau<\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle\subset\big\{\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}\|_{\mathcal{E}(\tau_{l}\wedge\varepsilon)}=\|e^{\varphi(0)A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}+M-1+2^{-l}\big\}

\displaystyle\subset\big\{\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}\|_{\mathcal{E}(\tau_{l}\wedge\varepsilon)}>\|e^{\varphi(0)A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}+M-1\big\}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau=0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}(\tau<\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}\Big(\liminf_{l\rightarrow\infty}\{\tau_{l}<\varepsilon\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\liminf_{l\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{l}<\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{l\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{l}<\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}\|_{\mathcal{E}(\tau_{l}\wedge\varepsilon)}>\|e^{\varphi(0)A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}+M-1\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{probZero}}{=}0.

This completes the proof of (
4.3
).

Now, to show (
4.4
), we observe from (

) that for any

, we have that
\omega\in\Omega_{p}
and

\displaystyle\tau(\omega)\leq\tau_{l+1}(\omega)\leq\tau_{l}(\omega).

(7.11)

Thus, it follows from (

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{l+1}}-\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}\|_{\mathcal{E}(\tau(\omega))}\leq\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{l+1}}-\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}\|_{\mathcal{E}(\tau_{l}(\omega)\wedge\tau_{l+1}(\omega))}<2^{-(l+2)}.

We can, therefore, find a process

Finally, to show (

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{\varphi(0)A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{u}^{N_{l+1}}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}+M-1+2^{-(l+1)}+2^{-(l+2)}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{\varphi(0)A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{u}^{N_{l+1}}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}+M

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{N}\|e^{\varphi(0)A^{1/2s}}\mathbf{u}^{N_{l}}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}+M

almost surely.

\displaystyle\|P^{N}f^{N}-P^{n}f^{n}\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(P^{N}-P^{n})f^{N}\|_{V}^{2}+\|P^{n}(f^{N}-f^{n})\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|Q^{n}P^{N}f^{N}\|_{V}^{2}+\|P^{n}(f^{N}-f^{n})\|_{V}^{2}.

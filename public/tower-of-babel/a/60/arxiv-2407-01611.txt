1.
Introduction

The question of how small the fractional part
\lVert f(n)\rVert
of a polynomial
f(n)
(with
f(0)=0
) can be made has been investigated since the early 20th century, with the current record being

(1.1)

\min_{n\leq x}\ \lVert f(n)\rVert\ll_{d}x^{-1/2d(d-1)+o(1)},

which is due to
Baker (2016)
(for
d\geq 8
). Here

\|\cdot\|
denotes the distance to the nearest integer. We impose the condition
f(0)=0
so as to avoid examples like
f(n)=n+1/2
, which clearly doesn’t attain arbitrarily small fractional parts. Note that the bound depends only on
x
and
d
, and is otherwise completely uniform over all such polynomials
f
. The exponent
1/2d(d-1)
is based on proving Vinogradov’s Mean Value Theorem for
k\geq 4
by
Bourgain et al. (2016)
. This bound is not expected to be tight, and it is conjectured
(
Baker, 1986
)
that this should be improvable to
1+o(1)
.

One of the first results of this kind is from
Heilbronn (1948)
, who obtained for any real
\alpha
,
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
x>0
,

\min_{n\leq x}\lVert\alpha n^{2}\rVert<x^{-1/2+\varepsilon}.

For the problem of a general polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}[X]
vanishing at the origin,
Selberg (1955)
obtained

\min_{n\leq x}\lVert f(n)\rVert\ll_{d}x^{-1/(8+o(1))d^{2}\log d}.

Wooley (2012)
improved the exponent to
-1/4d(d-1)
for
d\geq 2
, and as noted
Baker (2016)
improved it to
-1/2d(d-1)
for
d\geq 8
.

We can ask the same question, but instead for
k
polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
of degree at most
d
with
f_{1}(0)=\dots=f_{k}(0)=0
, the current record due to
Maynard (2021)
is for some
c_{d}>0
depending only on
d
,

(1.2)

\min_{n\leq x}\max_{i\leq k}\|f_{i}(n)\|\ll_{k,d}\frac{1}{x^{c_{d}/k}}.

This refines the work of
Baker (1980)
, where he obtained a hybrid bound

(1.3)

\min_{n\leq x}\max_{i\leq k}\|f_{i}(n)\|\ll_{k,d}\frac{1}{x^{1/(k^{2}+kc_{d})+o(1)}}.

A heuristic based on choosing the coefficients of
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
uniformly at random shows that one could only expect

(1.4)

\min_{n\leq x}\max_{i\leq k}\|f_{i}(n)\|\ll\frac{1}{x^{1/k}}.

Our main result is to establish a bound for (
1.2
) with an exponent of the form
1/10.5kd(d-1)+o(1)
. By comparing this with (
1.4
) and (
1.1
), we see that this bound is best in the
k
and
d
aspect, apart from hybrid bounds and the constant 10.5. More precisely, our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k,d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},\,\varepsilon>0,
and
M\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfy

M\geq\max\left\{4,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log\varepsilon^{-1}}{2\log 2}\right\}.

There is a constant
C_{d,k}>2
depending only on
d
,
k
(and
\varepsilon,M
) such that the following holds.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
be polynomials of degree at most
d
such that
f_{1}(0)=\cdots=

f_{k}(0)=0
. Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,1/100]
, and put
\Delta=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}
. Define

c_{2}=10.5+9k/(2k)^{M}+\varepsilon.

If
\Delta^{-1}\leq x^{1/c_{2}d(d-1)}
and
x>C_{d,k}
, then there is a positive integer
n<x
such that

\left\|f_{i}(n)\right\|\leq\varepsilon_{i}\quad\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\text{. }

By taking
\varepsilon_{1}=\cdots=\varepsilon_{k}=x^{-1/c_{2}d(d-1)k}
, and for any
\varepsilon>0
letting
M
large such that
2^{M}\geq\varepsilon^{-1}
, we arrive at the following:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
be polynomials of degree at most
d
such that
f_{1}(0)=\cdots=f_{k}(0)=0
. Then there is a positive integer
n<x
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\lVert f_{i}(n)\rVert\ll_{d,k,\varepsilon}x^{-1/(10.5+\varepsilon)kd(d-1)}

for all
i=1,2,\ldots,k
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is obtained by following the argument of
Maynard (2021)
, but making more careful choices of constants in the proofs. If we follow strictly the argument based on Weyl differencing in
Maynard (2021)
,
c_{d}
would be of the form
1/d^{d}
. To improve on the
d
-quantification, one can use arguments based on the Vinogradov Mean Value Theorem such as
Wooley (2012)
, which gives
c_{d}
to be of the form
1/2^{d}
. The main improvement made is found in Lemma
6.2
, which allows us to take
c_{d}
to be of the form
1/d^{2}
.

2.
Outline

In this section we outline the main improvement in this paper. The main method follows closely the argument of
Maynard (2021)
, where in Section
5
we prove a version of
Maynard (2021, Proposition 5.1)
, in Section
6
we prove a version of
Maynard (2021, Proposition 5.2)
, and in Section
7
we prove a version of
Maynard (2021, Proposition 5.3)
. Sections
5
and
7
are very similar to the corresponding sections in
Maynard (2021)
. To emphasize on the improvement made in Section
6
, we first review the arguments in the proof of
(
Maynard, 2021
, Lemma 7.3)
.

Given
f_{i}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}f_{i,j}X^{j}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
, Fourier analysis shows that, given any intervals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}
of length
\varepsilon>0
(for some small
\varepsilon>0
) and
x>\varepsilon^{-kC_{d}}
, either there is an
n<x
such that the vector of fractional parts
\mathbf{v}(n)=(\lVert f_{1}(n)\rVert,\ldots,\lVert f_{k}(n)\rVert)
lie in
I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{k}
, or there are at least
Q^{1/cd(d-1)}
integer tuples
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})
with
h_{i}\leq\varepsilon^{-1-o(1)}
such that

(2.1)

h_{1}f_{1,j}+\cdots+h_{k}f_{k,j}\approx\frac{a_{j}}{q_{j}}\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d,

for some
a_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
Q\leq\varepsilon^{-ckd(d-1)}
with
q_{j}<Q^{1/cd}
.
Maynard (2021)
proved that, given any
\delta>0
small,
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
set of triples
(a,q,\mathbf{h})
with
\gcd(a,q)=1
and
q\leq Q^{1/cd}
such that
\#\mathcal{S}\geq Q^{\delta}
, then one of the following holds:

(1)

At least
\#\mathcal{S}^{1/2}
of triples
(a,q,\mathbf{h})
have the same
q=q_{0}
.

(2)

There are at least
\#\mathcal{S}^{r/5}
distinct values of
a_{1}/q_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}/q_{r}
, amongst all
(a_{i},q_{i},\mathbf{h}^{(i)})\in\mathcal{S}
.

Indeed, if
(1)
is false, then one can find an integer
m_{0}
and a subset of the
q
s such that they are all multiples of
m_{0}
, and not too many of them are multiples of
m_{0}\ell
for
\ell>1
. Letting
b=q/m_{0}
for such
m_{0}
, one can prove that the size of the set of all tuples
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
with pairwise small gcd is not small. Focusing on these tuples, one can prove that there are many distinct values of
a_{1}/m_{0}b_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}/m_{0}b_{r}
.

Therefore, if we are in the case of (
2.1
), we can focus on the case
j=1
to prove there are many possible values of
\sum a_{1}/q_{1}
, hence there are at least
Q^{1/2cd(d-1)}
triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
satisfying (
2.1
), with
q_{1}
being identical amongst all triples. Doing this procedure
d
times, there are at least
Q^{1/2^{d}cd(d-1)}
triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})
satisfying (
2.1
) with
\mathbf{q}
identical amongst all triples. This eventually leads to an exponential dependence on
d
in the final exponent.

To get around this issue, we consider a multi-dimensional argument, instead of iterating a
1
-dimensional argument
d
times. We prove that given
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
set of triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})
with
\gcd(a_{i},q_{i})=1
and
q_{i}\leq Q^{1/cd}
(for
i=1,2,\ldots,d
) such that
\#\mathcal{S}\geq Q^{\delta}
, then one of the following holds:

(1)

At least
\#\mathcal{S}^{1/2}
of triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})
have the same
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}^{(0)}
.

(2)

There are at least
\#\mathcal{S}^{r/5}
distinct values of

\left(\frac{a_{1}^{(1)}}{q_{1}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{1}^{(r)}}{q_{1}^{(r)}},\ldots,\frac{a_{d}^{(1)}}{q_{d}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{d}^{(r)}}{q_{d}^{(r)}}\right),

amongst all
(\mathbf{a}^{(i)},\mathbf{q}^{(i)},\mathbf{h}^{(i)})\in\mathcal{S}
.

Indeed, if
(1)
is false, then one can find an integer tuple
\mathbf{m}^{(0)}
and a subset of the
\mathbf{q}
s such that all
q_{i}
are multiples of
m_{i}^{(0)}
, and not too many of them are multiples of
m_{i}^{(0)}\ell_{i}
, where
\bm{\ell}\neq(1,\ldots,1)
. Letting
b_{i}:=q_{i}/m_{i}^{(0)}
for such
\mathbf{m}^{(0)}
, one can prove that the size of the set of all tuples
(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)})
with pairwise small gcd (at all entries) is not small. Focusing on these tuples, we can prove that there are many distinct vectors of the aforementioned form.

Therefore, if we have (
2.1
), we can prove there are many possibilities of
(\sum a_{1}/q_{1},\ldots,\sum a_{d}/q_{d})
, hence there are at least
Q^{1/2cd(d-1)}
triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
satisfying (
2.1
), with
\mathbf{q}
identical amongst all triples. This multi-dimensional argument avoids the
2^{d}
cost in the exponent, which eventually allows a quadratic dependency on
d
in the final exponent.

4.
Notation

Throughout the paper we assume that we have polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
of degree at most
d
with
f_{1}(0)=\dots=f_{k}(0)=0
. We let these polynomials be given by
f_{i}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}f_{i,j}X^{j}
. Furthermore, we have reals
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,1/100]
, and we put
\Delta:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}
.

We often use Vinogradov’s notation, where
X\ll Y
denotes
|X|\leq CY
for some constant
C
. If the implied constant depends on
\varepsilon
say, we write
X\ll_{\varepsilon}Y
. We also use Landau’s Big O notation, where
X=O(Y)
if
X\ll Y
.

5.
Small Fractional Parts or Many Relations

Throughout this paper, we follow closely the arguments of
Maynard (2021)
. For the sake of clarity, we include the proofs for statements that are not identical to the analogous ones in
Maynard (2021)
, whereas for identical results we just state without proof.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}f_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
be a polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
with
f(0)=0
and
\varepsilon>0
. If there is some
Q\in[2,x^{1/2d(d-1)-\varepsilon}]
such that

\left|\sum_{n<x}e(f(n))\right|\geq\frac{x}{Q},

then there is an integer
q<x^{\varepsilon}Q^{d}
and integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
such that

f_{j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q}+O\left(\frac{Q^{d}}{x^{j-\varepsilon}}\right)

for
j=1,2,\ldots,d
. The implied constant only depends on
d
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Follows immediately from
Baker (2016, Theorem 4)
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0,M\geq 4
,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
be real valued functions, and
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,1/2]
be real numbers, with
\Delta:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}
. Suppose
x
is sufficiently large in terms of
\varepsilon
. Then at least one of the following holds:

(1)

We have

\#\{n\leq x:\lVert f_{i}(n)\rVert\leq\varepsilon_{i}\ \forall i\}>0.

(2)

There is a quantity
Q\geq 2
of the form
Q=2^{j}
such that there are at least
Q^{1/(1+\varepsilon)}
distinct values of
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
with
|h_{i}|<\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
such that

\frac{x}{Q}\leq\left|\sum_{n\leq x}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i}(n)\right)\right|\leq\frac{2x}{Q}.

Proof.

The proof is nearly identical to that of
Maynard (2021, Lemma 6.3)
. We fix a smooth function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
with
\phi(t)
supported on
|t|<1
, which is
1
on
|t|<1/2
. Let

\Phi_{i}(t)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi\left(\frac{t+m}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right),

which is clearly 1-periodic, smooth, and supported on
\lVert t\rVert<\varepsilon_{i}
. By Poisson summation,

\Phi_{i}(f_{i}(t))=\varepsilon_{i}\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\phi}(\varepsilon_{i}h)e(hf_{i}(t)).

Since
\phi
is fixed and smooth,
\phi^{(j)}(t)\ll_{j}1
, so
|\hat{\phi}(u)|\ll_{j}u^{-j}
for
j\geq 0
. Thus we see that the terms with
|h|\geq\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
contribute
O(\Delta^{100})
, and so

\Phi_{i}(f(t))=\varepsilon_{i}\sum_{|h|\leq\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}}\hat{\phi}(\varepsilon_{i}h)e(hf_{i}(t))+O(\Delta^{100}).

Thus we find that

\displaystyle\#\{n\leq x:\lVert f_{i}(n)\rVert\leq\varepsilon_{i}\forall i\}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n\leq x}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Phi_{i}(f_{i}(n))

\displaystyle=\Delta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\\
|h_{i}|<\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\hat{\phi}(\varepsilon_{i}h_{i})\right)\sum_{n\leq x}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i}(n)\right)+O(x\Delta^{99})

\displaystyle=x\Delta\hat{\phi}(0)^{k}+O\left(\Delta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\setminus\{0\}\\
|h_{i}|<\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{n\leq x}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i}(n)\right)\right|\right)+O(x\Delta^{99}).

For
\Delta
sufficiently small, we see that
\Delta\hat{\phi}(0)^{k}+O(\Delta^{99})\gg\Delta
, and so either

\#\{n\leq x:\lVert f_{i}(n)\rVert\leq\varepsilon_{i}\forall i\}>0,

or

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\setminus\{0\}\\
|h_{i}|<\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{n\leq x}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i}(n)\right)\right|\gg x.

In the latter case, by pigeonhole principle (or more specifically
Bloom and Sisask (2020, Lemma 2.1)
), there is some
Q=2^{j}
such that there are at least
Q^{1-\varepsilon}
choices of
\mathbf{h}
in the outer summation satisfying

\frac{x}{Q}\leq\left|\sum_{n\leq x}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i}(n)\right)\right|\leq\frac{2x}{Q}.

∎

Using this, we can prove the analogous version of
(
Maynard, 2021
, Proposition 5.1)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
be polynomials of degree at most
d
such that
f_{1}(0)=\cdots=f_{k}(0)=0
. Put
f_{i}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}f_{i,j}X^{j}
. Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,1/100]
, and put
\Delta=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}
. Let
\varepsilon>0,M\geq 4
. Define constants

c_{0}=1+\varepsilon,\quad c_{1}=1+k/(2k)^{M}+10\varepsilon,\quad c_{2}=2+1/(2k)^{M-1}+20\varepsilon.

Then provided
\Delta^{-c_{2}d(d-1)}<x
, at least one of the following holds:

(1)

We have

\#\{n\leq x:\lVert f_{i}(n)\rVert<\varepsilon_{i}\ \forall i\}>0.

(2)

There is some
Q\leq\Delta^{-c_{1}d(d-1)}
such that there are at least
Q^{1/(1+\varepsilon)c_{0}d(d-1)}
triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
satisfying:

(a)

q_{1}=\cdots=q_{d}
and
1\leq q_{j}\leq x^{\varepsilon}Q^{1/c_{0}(d-1)}
for
1\leq j\leq d
.

(b)

h_{i}\ll\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

(c)

For each
j=1,2,\ldots,d
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i,j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q_{j}}+O\left(\frac{Q^{1/d}}{x^{j-\varepsilon}}\right).

All implied constants depend only on
d,k,\varepsilon
and
M
..

Proof.

The proof is very similar to that of
Maynard (2021, Proposition 5.1)
. Suppose condition (1) does not hold. Then, by Lemma
5.2
, there is
Q_{1}\geq 2
such that there are at least
Q_{1}^{1/(1+\varepsilon)}
distinct values of
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
with
|h_{i}|<\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
such that

\left|\sum_{n\leq x}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i}(n)\right)\right|\geq\frac{x}{Q_{1}}.

Letting
Q=Q_{1}^{c_{0}d(d-1)}
, there are at least
Q^{1/(1+\varepsilon)c_{0}d(d-1)}
distinct values of
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
with
|h_{i}|<\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
such that

\left|\sum_{n\leq x}e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i}(n)\right)\right|\geq\frac{x}{Q^{1/c_{0}d(d-1)}}

Note that
Q_{1}\leq\Delta^{(-1-k/(2k)^{M})(1+\varepsilon)}
, so we have
Q_{1}^{c_{0}d(d-1)}=Q\leq\Delta^{-c_{1}d(d-1)}
since
c_{1}\geq c_{0}(1+k/(2k)^{M})(1+\varepsilon)
, and

Q_{1}=Q^{1/c_{0}d(d-1)}\leq\Delta^{-(1+k/(2k)^{M})(1+\varepsilon)}\leq\Delta^{-(c_{2}+2\varepsilon)d(d-1)/2d(d-1)}<x^{1/2d(d-1)-\varepsilon},

since
c_{2}\geq 2+1/(2k)^{M-1}+20\varepsilon
.
Therefore applying Lemma
5.1
to
Q_{1}
, there are integers
q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}<x^{\varepsilon}Q_{1}^{d}=x^{\varepsilon}Q^{1/c_{0}(d-1)}
and integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
such that
\gcd(a_{j},q_{j})=1
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,d
and

f_{j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q_{j}}+O\left(\frac{Q^{1/d}}{x^{j-\varepsilon}}\right)

for
j=1,2,\ldots,d
. ∎

6.
Many Relations Have Same Denominator

The proof of the following lemma can be found in the proof of
(
Maynard, 2021
, Lemma 7.1)
. We state it below for sake of clarity.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
B>1,\delta>0,
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. For
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

B\leq b_{i}<2B,\quad\gcd(b_{i},b_{j})<B^{\delta^{2}/r^{2}}\text{ for all }1\leq i<j\leq r,

define the set
\mathcal{R}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
consisting of
b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{r}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

(1)

B\leq b_{i}^{\prime}<2B,

\gcd(b_{i}^{\prime},b_{j}^{\prime})<B^{\delta^{2}/r^{2}}\text{ for all }1\leq i<j\leq r,

(2)

there exists
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{r}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(a)

\gcd(a_{i},b_{i}),\gcd(a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i}^{\prime})=1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
,

(b)

\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{r}}{b_{r}}=\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}}{b_{1}^{\prime}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{r}^{\prime}}{b_{r}^{\prime}}
.

Then
\#\mathcal{R}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})\leq B^{5\delta^{2}}
.

Proof.

For any choice of
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
with
\gcd(a_{i},b_{i})=1
, the denominator of
a_{1}/b_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}/b_{r}
is a multiple of
p^{\ell}
if
p^{\ell}
divides exactly one of
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
, and
p^{\ell+1}
divides none of them. Let
\gcd(b,p^{\infty})
denote the largest power of
p
dividing
b>1
, and
\gcd(b_{i},b_{j},p^{\infty})
the largest power of
p
dividing both
b_{i}
and
b_{j}
. Define

g_{p}:=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\gcd(b_{i},p^{\infty})}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\gcd(b_{i},b_{j},p^{\infty})^{2}}.

We see that if
p^{\ell}
divides exactly one of
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
, say
b_{1}
, and
p^{\ell+1}
divides none of them, then
g_{p}\leq p^{\ell}
since
\gcd(b_{1},b_{j},p^{\infty})^{2}\geq\gcd(b_{j},p^{\infty})
for all
1<j\leq r
. Similarly, if
p^{\ell}
divides at least 2 of the
b_{i}
but
p^{\ell+1}
divides none of them, then
g_{p}\leq 1
.

Therefore, for any
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
with
\gcd(a_{i},b_{i})=1
, the denominator of
a_{1}/b_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}/b_{r}
is of size at least
\prod_{p}g_{p}
by considering the prime factorisation of
b_{i}
. However,

\prod_{p}g_{p}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\gcd(b_{i},b_{j})^{2}}\geq B^{r-2\delta^{2}}.

Clearly any such denominator is of size
O(B^{r})
, so there are
O(B^{2\delta^{2}})
possible denominators of
a_{1}/b_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}/b_{r}
. Given such a denominator
q>B^{r-2\delta^{2}}
, if it is also the denominator of
a_{1}^{\prime}/b_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+a_{r}^{\prime}/b_{r}^{\prime}
, then
q
divides
\prod_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}^{\prime}
.

As
\prod_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}^{\prime}\ll B^{r}
, this implies there are
O(B^{2\delta^{2}})
choices of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}^{\prime}
, so
O(B^{2\delta^{2}+o(1)})
choices of
b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{r}^{\prime}
using the divisor bound. Therefore given
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
satisfying the above requirements, there are at most
B^{5\delta^{2}}
choices of
(b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{r}^{\prime})
in total, so
\#\mathcal{R}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})\leq B^{5\delta^{2}}
.
∎

We prove a generalisation of
(
Maynard, 2021
, Lemma 7.1)
. In
Maynard (2021)
, Lemma 7.3 was proven with induction, which introduced extra factors of
d
on the exponent. We directly prove a generalisation which avoids this issue.

Lemma 6.2
.

(Expansion or Same Denominators)
Let
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
r
a positive integer,
d\geq 2
, and
X\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
. Let
Q>0
be sufficiently large in terms of
r,\ d
,
\delta
and
\varepsilon
, and let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
be a set of triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})
with
\gcd(a_{i},q_{i})=1
and
q_{i}\leq XQ^{1/(d-1)}
(for
i=1,2,\ldots,d
) such that
\#\mathcal{S}\geq Q^{\delta}
.

Then one of the following holds:

(1)

There is a
\mathbf{q}^{(0)}
such that at least
\#\mathcal{S}^{1/2}
of the triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathcal{S}
have
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}^{(0)}
.

(2)

The set

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{\left(\frac{a_{1}^{(1)}}{q_{1}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{1}^{(r)}}{q_{1}^{(r)}},\ldots,\frac{a_{d}^{(1)}}{q_{d}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{d}^{(r)}}{q_{d}^{(r)}}\right):\exists\mathbf{h}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{h}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\text{ s.t. }(\mathbf{a}^{(i)},\mathbf{q}^{(i)},\mathbf{h}^{(i)})\in\mathcal{S}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq r\right\},

(where
\mathbf{a}^{(i)}=(a_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,a_{d}^{(i)})
and
\mathbf{q}^{(i)}=(q_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,q_{d}^{(i)}))
has cardinality at least
X^{-\varepsilon\delta dr/40-5\varepsilon\delta^{2}d/r}\#\mathcal{S}^{(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)r}
.

Proof.

The proof is a generalisation of Lemma 7.1 found in
Maynard (2021)
. Throughout the lemma we will assume that
Q
is large enough in terms of
\varepsilon,\delta
and
r
. We first restrict our attention to a suitable subset of the
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}s
appearing in
\mathcal{S}
. For
j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let

\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}=\left\{\mathbf{q}\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}[2^{j_{i}},2^{j_{i}+1}):\exists(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}\text{ with }\gcd(a_{i},q_{i})=1\ \forall i\text{ and }(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathcal{S}\right\}.

Clearly
\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}=\emptyset
if
j_{i}>2\log(XQ^{2/d})
for some
i
. Note if

\#\left\{\mathbf{q}:\exists(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}\text{ with }(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathcal{S}\right\}=\sum_{2^{j_{i}}\leq Q^{1/d}\,\forall i}\#\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}\leq\#\mathcal{S}^{1/2}

then by pigeonhole principle, there is a
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap[1,XQ^{2/d}]^{d}
such that there are at least
\#\mathcal{S}^{1/2}
choices of
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h})
with
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathcal{S}
. Thus condition (1) is satisfied in this case.

Therefore we may assume that

\sum_{j_{i}}\#\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}>\#\mathcal{S}^{1/2}\geq Q^{\delta/2},

and so there is some
j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}\leq 2\log(XQ^{2/d})
such that
\#\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}>\#\mathcal{S}^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon_{1}}
, where
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon/20
. Note we must have
2^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{d}}>Q^{\delta/3}
from the trivial bound
\#\mathcal{B}_{j}\leq 2^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{d}}
.

For convenience, we define the following terminology. We say
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
divides
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
if
m_{i}\mid q_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq d
, and define
\mathbf{m}\mathbf{q}:=(m_{1}q_{1},\ldots,m_{d}q_{d})
.

If there is a
d
-tuple
\mathbf{m}
which divides at least
\#\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}/m_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}\delta/2}\cdots m_{d}^{\varepsilon_{1}\delta/2}
elements of
\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}
, we restrict our attention to this subset. By performing this repeatedly, we may assume there is a fixed
d
-tuple
\mathbf{m}
and a set
\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}
such that
\#\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}^{\prime}\geq\#\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}/m_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}\delta/2}\cdots m_{d}^{\varepsilon_{1}\delta/2}
,
\mathbf{m}
divides all elements of
\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}^{\prime}
, and there is no
d
-tuple
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\neq(1,\ldots,1)
such that
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\mathbf{m}
divides at least
\#\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}^{\prime}/(m_{1}^{\prime}\cdots m_{d}^{\prime})^{\varepsilon_{1}\delta/2}
elements of
\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}^{\prime}
. Since
m_{i}\leq 2^{j_{i}+1}\leq 2XQ^{2/d}
, we have

\#\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}^{\prime}\geq\frac{\#\mathcal{S}^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon_{1}}}{(2X)^{\varepsilon_{1}\delta d/2}Q^{\varepsilon_{1}\delta}}\geq X^{-\varepsilon_{1}\delta d/2}\#\mathcal{S}^{\frac{1}{2}-3\varepsilon_{1}}.

Since
\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}^{\prime}\subseteq\{\mathbf{b}\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}[2^{j_{i}},2^{j_{i}+1}):m_{i}\mid b_{i}\}
is a set of size
O(2^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{d}}/m_{1}\cdots m_{d})
, we see this also implies
m_{1}\cdots m_{d}<2^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{d}}/Q^{\delta/4}
. We let

\mathcal{B}:=\left\{\mathbf{b}:(m_{1}b_{1},\ldots,m_{d}b_{d})\in\mathcal{B}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}^{\prime}\right\},

and note
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{d}[B_{i},2B_{i})
, where we set
B_{i}:=2^{j_{i}}/m_{i}
. Let
B=\max_{i}B_{i}
, and note that
\#\mathcal{B}\geq(2X)^{-\varepsilon_{1}\delta d/2}\#\mathcal{S}^{\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon_{1}}
and
B\leq XQ^{2/d}
.

Consider the graph
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, where the vertex set
\mathcal{V}
is taken to be
\mathcal{B}
, and the edge set
\mathcal{E}
is defined by

\mathcal{E}:=\left\{(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\mathbf{b}^{(2)})\in\mathcal{B}^{2}:\text{exists }1\leq i\leq d\ \text{ s.t. }\gcd(b_{i}^{(1)},b_{i}^{(2)})\geq B^{\varepsilon\delta^{2}/6r^{2}}\right\}.

We consider separately two cases.

Case 1:

\#\mathcal{E}\geq\varepsilon_{1}\#\mathcal{V}^{2}/2r^{2}
.

In this case, there are many pairs with a gcd of some size. If we pick a vertex
v
in
G
at random, then the expected number of vertices connected to
v
is at least
{\varepsilon_{1}\#\mathcal{V}}/{2r^{2}}
, and so (by the pigeonhole principle) there is some
\mathbf{b}^{(0)}\in\mathcal{B}
and
1\leq i\leq d
such that there are at least
{\varepsilon_{1}\#\mathcal{B}}/{2r^{2}d}
elements
\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{B}
with
\gcd(b_{i},b_{i}^{(0)})>B^{\varepsilon\delta^{2}/6r^{2}}
. Since there are at most
B^{o(1)}
divisors of
b_{i}^{(0)}
, there must be a divisor
m>B^{\varepsilon\delta^{2}/6r^{2}}
of
b_{i}^{(0)}
such that
m|b_{i}
for at least
{\varepsilon_{1}\#\mathcal{B}}/{2r^{2}dB^{o(1)}}>{\#\mathcal{B}}/{m^{\varepsilon_{1}\delta/2}}
elements
\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{B}
. But this contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{B}
is constructed to have no such integers. Thus we must instead have
\#\mathcal{E}<{\varepsilon_{1}\#\mathcal{V}^{2}}/{2r^{2}}
.

Case 2:

\#\mathcal{E}<\varepsilon_{1}\#\mathcal{V}^{2}/2r^{2}
.

Picking
r
distinct vertices in
G
uniformly at random, then the expected number of edges between these vertices is less than
\varepsilon_{1}
. In particular, the probability that there are no edges between any of the
r
chosen vertices is at least
1-\varepsilon_{1}
. Thus, if we define

\mathcal{C}=\left\{(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)})\in\mathcal{B}^{r}:\gcd(b^{(i)}_{s},b^{(j)}_{s})<B^{\varepsilon\delta^{2}/6r^{2}}\text{ for }1\leq i<j\leq r,\ 1\leq s\leq d\right\},

then
\#\mathcal{C}\gg_{r,\varepsilon}\#\mathcal{B}^{r}
. Given
(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)})\in\mathcal{C}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)})=

\displaystyle\Big\{(\mathbf{b}^{(1)^{\prime}},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{C}:\exists\mathbf{a}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{a}^{(r)},\mathbf{a}^{(1)^{\prime}},\ldots,\mathbf{a}^{(r)^{\prime}}\text{ s.t. }

\displaystyle\gcd(a_{i}^{(j)},m_{i}b_{i}^{(j)})=\gcd(a_{i}^{(j)^{\prime}},m_{i}b_{i}^{(j)^{\prime}})=1,\frac{a_{i}^{(1)}}{b_{i}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{i}^{(r)}}{b_{i}^{(r)}}=\frac{a_{i}^{(1)^{\prime}}}{b_{i}^{(1)^{\prime}}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{i}^{(r)^{\prime}}}{b_{i}^{(r)^{\prime}}},\forall i,j\Big\}.

Abusing notation and defining
\mathcal{R}
the same as in Lemma
6.1
, we have

\#\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)})\leq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\#\mathcal{R}(b_{i}^{(1)},\ldots,b_{i}^{(r)})\leq B^{5d\varepsilon\delta^{2}/6}.

For each
\mathbf{b}\in\mathcal{B}
, let
\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{b})
be an integer tuple where each entry is coprime to that of
\mathbf{m}\mathbf{b}
, and such that
(\mathbf{a}(\mathbf{b}),\mathbf{m}\mathbf{b},\mathbf{h})\in\mathcal{S}
for some
\mathbf{b}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\geq\#\left\{\frac{{a}(\mathbf{b}^{(1)})_{i}}{m_{i}b_{i}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{{a}(\mathbf{b}^{(r)})_{i}}{m_{i}b_{i}^{(r)}}:(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)})\in\mathcal{C}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)})\in\mathcal{C}}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{b}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\#\mathcal{C}}{B^{5d\varepsilon\delta^{2}/6}}\geq\frac{\#\mathcal{B}^{r}}{X^{5d\varepsilon\delta^{2}/6}Q^{(5+\frac{\varepsilon}{20})\varepsilon\delta^{2}/6}}.

where we recall
r\geq 1>\delta
and
\#\mathcal{B}>X^{-\varepsilon_{1}\delta d/2}\#\mathcal{S}^{\frac{1}{2}-3\varepsilon_{1}}\geq X^{-\varepsilon_{1}\delta d/2}Q^{(\frac{1}{2}-3\varepsilon_{1})\delta}
. This gives condition (2), and we are done.
∎

Using this, we obtain an improvement to
(
Maynard, 2021
, Lemma 7.3)
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
. Let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfy

M\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log\varepsilon^{-1}}{2\log 2}.

Let
k,d\geq 2
be positive integers and let
\alpha_{i,j}\in[0,1)
for
1\leq i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq d
be reals. Let
Q
be large enough in terms of
\delta,d,k,\varepsilon
, and let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,1]
be such that
\Delta=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}
satisfies
Q^{(1+\varepsilon)\delta}\leq\Delta^{-1}\leq Q^{(2k)^{M}}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
be a set of triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})
satisfying:

(1)

\gcd(a_{j},q_{j})=1
and
q_{j}\leq x^{\varepsilon}Q^{1/(d-1)}
for
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
.

(2)

h_{i}\leq\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.

(3)

\#\mathcal{S}\geq Q^{\delta}
.

(4)

For each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
, we have

\left|h_{1}\alpha_{1,j}+\cdots+h_{k}\alpha_{k,j}-\frac{a_{j}}{q_{j}}\right|\leq x^{-\varepsilon}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\right)^{(2+k/(2k)^{M}+200\varepsilon)/\delta}.

Then there is a
\mathbf{q}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that at least
\#\mathcal{S}^{1/2}
of the triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathcal{S}
have
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}_{0}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
(
Maynard, 2021
, Lemma 7.2)
. Choose an integer
r
such that
Q^{\delta r/(2+k/(2k)^{M}+10\varepsilon)}>x^{\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\geq Q^{\delta r/(2+k/(2k)^{M}+100\varepsilon)}
. A computation shows
r
must exist and satisfy

r\in[(2+k/(2k)^{M}+10\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon\log_{Q}x+\varepsilon),(2+k/(2k)^{M}+100\varepsilon)(\varepsilon\log_{Q}x+(2k)^{M})/\delta].

Therefore we may assume that
Q
is sufficiently large in terms of
r
.

If
(\mathbf{a}^{(1)},\mathbf{q}^{(1)},\mathbf{h}^{(1)}),\ldots(\mathbf{a}^{(r)},\mathbf{q}^{(r)},\mathbf{h}^{(r)})\in\mathcal{S}
, then we have for
1\leq s\leq r
such that for all
j=1,2,\ldots,d
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}^{(s)}\alpha_{i,j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q_{j}}+O\left(x^{-\varepsilon}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\right)^{(2+k/(2k)^{M}+200\varepsilon)/\delta}\right)=\frac{a_{j}^{(s)}}{q_{j}^{(s)}}+O(Q^{-(1+\varepsilon/1000)r}).

Adding these together and observing
rQ^{-\varepsilon r/1000}\leq 1
for
Q
sufficiently large gives

\frac{a_{j}^{(1)}}{q_{j}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{j}^{(r)}}{q_{j}^{(r)}}+O(Q^{-r})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i,j}\tilde{h}_{i},

where
\tilde{h}_{i}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}h_{i}^{(s)}
. Since the denominator of
\frac{a_{j}^{(1)}}{q_{j}^{(s)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{j}^{(r)}}{q_{j}^{(r)}}
(when written as a single fraction) is at most
Q^{r}
, we see that this fraction is uniquely determined by the integers
\tilde{h}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{h}_{k}
. Note
|\tilde{h}_{j}|\ll r\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
, so we have

\displaystyle\#\left\{\left(\frac{a_{1}^{(1)}}{q_{1}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{1}^{(r)}}{q_{1}^{(r)}},\ldots,\frac{a_{d}^{(1)}}{q_{d}^{(1)}}+\cdots+\frac{a_{d}^{(r)}}{q_{d}^{(r)}}\right):\exists\mathbf{h}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{h}^{(r)}\text{ s.t. }(\mathbf{a}^{(1)},\mathbf{q}^{(1)},\mathbf{h}^{(1)}),\ldots,(\mathbf{a}^{(r)},\mathbf{q}^{(r)},\mathbf{h}^{(r)})\in\mathcal{S}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\#\left\{(\tilde{h}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{h}_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:|\tilde{h}_{i}|\ll r\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots,k\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\right)^{1+k/(2k)^{M}+\varepsilon/1000}

\displaystyle\leq x^{-(1+k/(2k)^{M}+\varepsilon/1000)\varepsilon}Q^{\frac{1+k/(2k)^{M}+\varepsilon/1000}{2+k/(2k)^{M}+100\varepsilon}\delta r}\leq x^{-(1+k/(2k)^{M}+\varepsilon/1000)\varepsilon}Q^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}

Here we used the fact that
r\leq\delta^{-1}\log\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}
and
x^{-\varepsilon}Q^{\delta r/(2+k/(2k)^{M}+100\varepsilon)}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon^{-1}
, followed by
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}<x^{-\varepsilon}Q^{\delta r/(2+k/(2k)^{M}+10\varepsilon)}
.

Note our assumptions satisfy all hypotheses of Lemma
6.2
, so by the lemma and also the above bound, (1) must hold, so there must be a
\mathbf{q}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that at least
\#\mathcal{S}^{1/2}
of the triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathcal{S}
have
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}_{0}
.
∎

Therefore, we can prove a proposition akin to
(
Maynard, 2021
, Proposition 5.2)
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in

\mathbb{R}[X]
be polynomials of degree at most
d
such that
f_{1}(0)=\cdots=f_{k}(0)=0
. Put
f_{i}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}f_{i,j}X^{j}
. Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,1/100]
, and put
\Delta=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}
. Define constants

c_{0}=1+\varepsilon,\quad c_{1}=1+k/(2k)^{M}+10\varepsilon,\quad c_{2}=2.5+1/(2k)^{M-1}+500\varepsilon,

and let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfy

M\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log\varepsilon^{-1}}{2\log 2}.

Let
Q\leq\Delta^{-c_{1}d(d-1)}
be such that there are at least
Q^{1/(1+\varepsilon)c_{0}d(d-1)}
triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in

\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
satisfying:

(1)

q_{1}=\cdots=q_{d}
and
1\leq q_{j}\leq x^{\varepsilon}Q^{1/c_{0}(d-1)}
for
1\leq j\leq d
.

(2)

h_{i}\ll\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

(3)

For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i,j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q_{j}}+O\left(\frac{Q^{1/d}}{x^{j-\varepsilon}}\right).

Then provided
\Delta^{-c_{2}d(d-1)}<x
there is some positive integer
q\leq x^{\varepsilon}Q^{1/c_{0}(d-1)}
and at least
Q^{1/2(1+\varepsilon)c_{1}d(d-1)}
pairs
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h})\in

\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
such that:

(1)

h_{i}\ll\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-2/(2k)^{M}}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

(2)

For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i,j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q}+O\left(\frac{Q^{1/d}}{x^{j-\varepsilon}}\right).

All implied constants depend only on
d,k,\varepsilon
and
M
.

Proof.

The proof is nearly identical to
(
Maynard, 2021
, Proposition 5.2)
. By assumption, there is some
Q\leq(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}^{-1})^{c_{1}d(d-1)}
such that there are at least
Q^{1/2c_{1}d(d-1)}
triples
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k}
with
\gcd(a_{j},q_{j})=1
and
q_{j}\leq x^{\varepsilon}Q^{1/c_{0}(d-1)}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,d
, and with
|h_{i}|\ll\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
and with

\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}f_{i,j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q_{j}}+O\left(\frac{Q^{1/d}}{x^{j-\varepsilon}}\right).

If
Q\leq\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}
, then we just take one such triple
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{h})
. In this case the triple
(j\mathbf{a},j\mathbf{q},j\mathbf{h})
for
j=1,2,\ldots,Q
then give
Q
relations of the desired type
C_{d}^{\prime}>2d(d-1)+1
, since
jh_{i}\ll Q\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-1/(2k)^{M}}\ll\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}\Delta^{-2/(2k)^{M}}
. Thus we may assume
Q^{(2k)^{M}}>\Delta^{-1}
.

We now apply Lemma
6.3
with
\delta=((1+\varepsilon)c_{1}d(d-1))^{-1}
. Provided
c_{2}=2.5+1/(2k)^{M-1}+500\varepsilon
and
c_{1}=1+k/(2k)^{M}+10\varepsilon
, we see that the bounds
Q\leq(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}^{-1})^{c_{1}d(d-1)}
and
(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}^{-1})^{c_{2}d(d-1)}\leq x
imply

\frac{Q^{1/d}}{x^{j-\varepsilon}}<x^{-\varepsilon}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\right)^{c_{2}(1-2\varepsilon)d(d-1)-c_{1}(d-1)}<x^{-\varepsilon}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\right)^{(2+k/(2k)^{M}+200\varepsilon)/\delta}

for
1\leq i\leq d
, since

c_{2}(1-2\varepsilon)d(d-1)-c_{1}(d-1)\geq(2+k/(2k)^{M}+200\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon)c_{1}d(d-1)

is implied by the fact that

and so all hypotheses of Lemma

have

. These all give rise to a rational

Lemma 7.1

The majority of the proof is identical to the corresponding proof in

We see that

for

\prod_{i=1}^{k-r}B_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{\lVert\mathbf{z}_{1}\rVert_{\infty}\cdots\lVert\mathbf{z}_{k-r}\rVert_{\infty}\prod_{i=r+1}^{k}B_{i}}{\delta^{2(k-r)}}\ll\frac{D_{1}\prod_{i=r+1}^{k}B_{i}}{D_{2}}.

Recall that

\displaystyle\frac{y}{(\prod_{i=1}^{k-r}B_{i}^{\prime})^{C_{2}}}

\displaystyle\gg\frac{x^{1-2\varepsilon}}{(\prod_{i=r+1}^{k}B_{i})^{C_{2}}}\frac{D_{2}^{C_{2}-1}}{D_{1}^{C_{2}-1}Q^{3/d}B_{1}\cdots B_{r}}

Theorem 8.2
.

Let

and

M\geq\max\left\{4,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log\varepsilon^{-1}}{2\log 2}\right\}.

(and

The proof is essentially identical to
(

(2)
